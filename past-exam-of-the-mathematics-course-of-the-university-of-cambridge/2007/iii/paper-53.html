Adopt the phase convention CP∣K0⟩=−∣Kˉ0⟩ and CP∣Kˉ0⟩=−∣K0⟩. Changing both phases changes the convention for the mixing parameter, not physical predictions. The flavor contents are K0=dsˉ and Kˉ0=sdˉ. A pair of charged weak currents changes strangeness by two units through the following box contractions, with ui​,uj​=u,c,t summed over.
The drawing uses unitary gauge: the W boson gauge-boson propagator contains its longitudinal part. In a gauge of a renormalizable quantum field theory, charged Goldstone boson boxes must also be included to obtain the same gauge-independent result. The external lines represent the valence quarks; the meson matrix element also contains strong interaction binding effects.
Put λi​=Vis∗​Vid​, xi​=mi2​/mW2​ and PL​=(1−γ5)/2. The charged weak box contribution to kaon mixing has the effective four-fermion interaction structure
HΔS=2​=C(sˉγμ​PL​d)(sˉγμPL​d)+h.c.,C∼16π2GF2​mW2​​∑i,j​λi​λj​S(xi​,xj​).
(1)
Here GF​ is the Fermi constant, V is the CKM matrix, and S is a dimensionless loop function from a Feynman integral; the estimate suppresses convention-dependent numerical factors. Four weak interaction Feynman vertices and a one-loop Feynman integral give the coupling and loop order suppression. Unitarity of the CKM matrix gives ∑i​λi​=0, so the GIM mechanism cancels the mass-independent part. With a negligible up quark mass, the leading charm quark term has S(xc​,xc​)∼xc​. Define fK​ by ⟨0∣sˉγμ​γ5d∣K0(p)⟩=ifK​pμ​. Estimating the hadronic matrix element by fK2​mK​BK​, with the dimensionless kaon bag parameter BK​ of order unity, gives
∣H21′​∣charm​∼16π2GF2​mc2​​∣Vcs∗​Vcd​∣2fK2​mK​BK​​.
(2)
The hadronic normalization includes division by 2mK​ when relativistically normalized states are converted to the effective Hamiltonian for neutral-meson mixing. Since ∣Vcs∗​Vcd​∣≃sinθC​cosθC​, the Cabibbo suppression is explicit. Illustrative scales GF​∼10−5GeV−2, mc​∼1GeV, fK​∼0.1GeV and mK​∼0.5GeV give an order 10−16 to 10−15GeV estimate. The full sum includes charm quark-top quark and top quark-top quark terms, important for CP violation; long-distance strong interaction contributions prevent this dimensional estimate from being a precision prediction. A meson matrix element necessarily needs hadronic input in addition to electroweak parameters.
For the decaying kaon system write H′=M−iΓ/2, where M and Γ are Hermitian matrices. The entries denoted Mij​ in the question are entries of this effective Hamiltonian for neutral-meson mixing; they need not themselves form a Hermitian matrix. CPT invariance equates the diagonal masses and diagonal decay widths, giving
H11′​=H22′​​.
(3)
One can see why there is no complex conjugation on the right as follows. In the flavor subspace let J=(01​10​) and represent the antiunitary CPT operation by J followed by complex conjugation. CPT changes a projected forward propagator into its adjoint. Indeed, if P projects onto the flavor subspace and Hfull​ is the Hermitian microscopic generator, its invariance and the antiunitary reversal of i give ΘPe−iHfull​tPΘ−1=Pe+iHfull​tP=(Pe−iHfull​tP)†. In the time-independent two-state approximation, the effective generator this means JH′∗J=H′†, whose diagonal entries give (H22′​)∗=(H11′​)∗; the off-diagonal entries impose no further condition. Equivalently, apply CPT separately to the dispersive and absorptive Hermitian matrices. Treating the decaying effective Hamiltonian for neutral-meson mixing as a Hermitian matrix would incorrectly discard the absorptive part.
In the chosen flavor phase convention CP is represented by −J. Its invariance requires JH′J=H′. Thus it gives diagonal equality and additionally
H12′​=H21′​​.
(4)
The normalized CP eigenstates are
∣K10​⟩=2​∣K0⟩−∣Kˉ0⟩​,∣K20​⟩=2​∣K0⟩+∣Kˉ0⟩​​.
(5)
Applying CP directly gives eigenvalues +1 and −1 respectively.
Now use CPT to put H′=(ac​ba​) and take correlated square roots α=b​, β=c​. Its characteristic polynomial equation is (a−μ)2−bc=0, and direct multiplication shows
H′(−βα​)=(a−αβ)(−βα​),H′(βα​)=(a+αβ)(βα​).
(6)
Expressing these two eigenvectors in the CP eigenstate basis gives, respectively, coefficients proportional to (α+β,α−β) and (α−β,α+β). Therefore the kaon CP mixing parameter is
ϵ=α+βα−β​=M12​​+M21​​M12​​−M21​​​,pq​=αβ​=1+ϵ1−ϵ​,
(7)
and, because the CP eigenstate basis is orthonormal, the normalized states at production are
∣KS0​⟩=1+∣ϵ∣2​∣K10​⟩+ϵ∣K20​⟩​,∣KL0​⟩=1+∣ϵ∣2​∣K20​⟩+ϵ∣K10​⟩​​.
(8)
The kaon mixing square-root branch convention correlates the roots continuously with the limit of conserved CP b=c, where α=β and ϵ=0. Reversing one root interchanges the two eigenstates. The labels short and long are fixed by their decay widths: their complex eigenvalues are μS,L​=mS,L​−iΓS,L​/2, and subsequent evolution supplies e−iμS,L​t. The displayed formula assumes nonzero mixing and α+β=0, as in the physical kaon system; degenerate or defective matrices require a separate limiting treatment.

Use metric signature (+−−−) and average over the four initial spin states, summing over final spins and the Nc​=3 quark color charge states. The Electrons carry no color charge, so their initial color charge average is trivial. A final color charge average would not give the inclusive relativistic cross-section requested.
Photon exchange gives the tree amplitude, up to an irrelevant overall phase,
M=se2Qq​​δab​[vˉ(p1​)γμu(p2​)][uˉ(q1​)γμ​v(q2​)],e2=4πα.
(9)
Massless spin sums give ∑uuˉ=p and ∑vvˉ=p. Therefore
∣M∣2​=4s2Nc​e4Qq2​​tr(p1​γμp2​γν)tr(q1​γμ​q2​γν​).
(10)
The color charge multiplicity is ∑a,b​∣δab​∣2=Nc​, not Nc2​. Each trace equals 4(aμbν+aνbμ−gμνa⋅b). Contracting them, the terms proportional to (p1​⋅p2​)(q1​⋅q2​) cancel, leaving 32[(p1​⋅q1​)(p2​⋅q2​)+(p1​⋅q2​)(p2​⋅q1​)]. Thus
∣M∣2​=s2384π2α2Qq2​​[(p1​⋅q2​)(p2​⋅q1​)+(p2​⋅q2​)(p1​⋅q1​)]​.
(11)
In the centre-of-momentum frame every energy is s​/2. Let θ be the angle between p1​ and q2​. Then
p1​⋅q2​=p2​⋅q1​=4s​(1−cosθ),p1​⋅q1​=p2​⋅q2​=4s​(1+cosθ).
(12)
Consequently ∣M∣2​=Nc​e4Qq2​(1+cos2θ).
The supplied two-body Lorentz-invariant phase space formula has a factor-of-two error. Deriving the normalization explicitly,
dΦ2​=32π2dΩ​,incident flux=4p1​⋅p2​=2s,
(13)
so the massless two-body scattering flux normalization is
dΩdσ​=64π2s∣M∣2​​=128π2p1​⋅p2​∣M∣2​​.
(14)
For example, integrating the spatial Dirac delta in dΦ2​ leaves [4(2π)2]−1dkdΩδ(s​−2k); its radial integral supplies the factor 1/2. The printed denominator 64π2p1​⋅p2​ would double the relativistic cross-section and cannot reproduce the requested result with the standard scattering amplitude.
The correctly normalized differential and total results for electron-positron annihilation into a quark pair are
dΩdσ​=4s3α2Qq2​​(1+cos2θ),∫dΩ(1+cos2θ)=316π​,
(15)
and hence
σq​=s4πα2​Qq2​​.
(16)
At energies where photon exchange and the parton description apply, sum over kinematically accessible quark flavors. The produced quarks undergo hadronization, so the leading inclusive hadronic rate is
σhad​=s4πα2​∑q​Qq2​,R(s)=σ(e+e−→μ+μ−)σhad​​=3∑q​Qq2​.
(17)
This is the hadronic R ratio. Perturbative QCD adds a leading relative correction αs​(s)/π; thresholds, narrow resonances and, near the Z boson scale, electroweak exchange require additional treatment. The calculation is a photon-exchange, massless result from a tree-level Feynman diagram.

Quark mixing arises because the up-type and down-type quark Yukawa matrices need not be diagonal in the same weak interaction basis. In the Standard Model the three left-handed quark doublets QL′​ transform as (3,2,1/6) under SU(3)c​×SU(2)L​×U(1)Y​, whereas uR′​ and dR′​ transform as (3,1,2/3) and (3,1,−1/3). With a Higgs doublet H and its conjugate H=iσ2H∗, the gauge-invariant Yukawa interaction is
LY​=−Qˉ​L′​Yd​HdR′​−Qˉ​L′​Yu​HuR′​+h.c.
(18)
The generation indices are matrix-multiplied. Electroweak symmetry breaking, ⟨H⟩=(0,vH​/2​)T, produces Mu​=vH​Yu​/2​ and Md​=vH​Yd​/2​.
Each complex fermion mass matrix admits a biunitary diagonalization,
UuL†​Mu​UuR​=Du​,UdL†​Md​UdR​=Dd​,
(19)
where the diagonal entries are real nonnegative quark masses. Define uL′​=UuL​uL​ and dL′​=UdL​dL​, and similarly for right-handed fields. The kinetic terms retain their canonical form because the transformations are unitary matrices. However, the weak charged current becomes
Lcc​=−2​g2​​uˉL​γμVdL​Wμ+​+h.c.,V=UuL†​UdL​​.
(20)
This unitary matrix is the Cabibbo-Kobayashi-Maskawa matrix. Its entries multiply transitions between up-type and down-type mass eigenstates. The minimal Standard Model has no analogous right-handed charged weak current.
Quark mixing does not introduce tree-level flavor-changing neutral currents. Photon, gluon and weak neutral current couplings are proportional to the identity within each fixed-charge quark sector, and U†IU=I. The single Higgs doublet also has diagonal neutral scalar field couplings after the same transformations that diagonalize the masses. At higher loop orders, flavor changes are possible. For example, an s→d amplitude contains ∑i​Vis∗​Vid​f(mi2​). Unitarity of the CKM matrix sets the sum of coefficients to zero, cancelling any mass-independent part. This is the GIM mechanism; mass splittings leave a suppressed, generally nonzero amplitude.
For N generations a unitary matrix has N2 real parameters: N(N−1)/2 mixing angles and N(N+1)/2 phases. Rephasing the N up-type and N down-type quark fields removes 2N−1 phases; a common baryon number phase leaves every matrix entry unchanged. Hence the physical numbers are
Nangles​=2N(N−1)​,Nweak CP phases​=2(N−1)(N−2)​.
(21)
Two generations therefore give a real Cabibbo rotation,
V=(cosθC​−sinθC​​sinθC​cosθC​​),
(22)
with strangeness-changing charged-current transitions suppressed by sinθC​. Three generations have three mixing angles and one irreducible weak CP phase. This counting assumes nondegenerate masses; degeneracies allow additional basis rotations.
Writing sij​=sinθij​ and cij​=cosθij​, a standard exact parametrization is
V=​c12​c13​−s12​c23​−c12​s23​s13​eiδs12​s23​−c12​c23​s13​eiδ​s12​c13​c12​c23​−s12​s23​s13​eiδ−c12​s23​−s12​c23​s13​eiδ​s13​e−iδs23​c13​c23​c13​​​.
(23)
Complex entries alone do not establish CP violation, because quark quantum field rephasings can change their phases. For nondegenerate quark masses the weak mixing sector conserves CP precisely when a basis makes V real. A useful invariant test is the Jarlskog invariant,
J=Im(Vud​Vcs​Vus∗​Vcd∗​)=c12​c23​c132​s12​s23​s13​sinδ.
(24)
A nonzero J requires all three mixing angles and a nontrivial phase. If two same-charge quark masses coincide, the additional basis freedom removes the corresponding physical CP effect. The possible QCD theta angle is a separate CP parameter, not part of this weak-mixing count.
The Wolfenstein parametrization displays the hierarchy of quark mixing economically. Put λW​=s12​, s23​=AλW2​, and s13​eiδ=AλW3​(ρ+iη). Expanding the exact matrix gives
V=​1−λW2​/2−λW​AλW3​(1−ρ−iη)​λW​1−λW2​/2−AλW2​​AλW3​(ρ−iη)AλW2​1​​+O(λW4​).
(25)
The observed pattern is predominantly diagonal, with 1-2 mixing largest and 2-3, 1-3 mixing successively suppressed. The expansion implies J≃A2λW6​η. The truncated matrix must not be treated as an exactly unitary matrix.
Column orthogonality gives
Vud​Vub∗​+Vcd​Vcb∗​+Vtd​Vtb∗​=0.
(26)
The three complex numbers form a CKM unitarity triangle, with area ∣J∣/2. Dividing by −Vcd​Vcb∗​ puts its vertices at 0, 1 and ρˉ​+iηˉ​, where ρˉ​+iηˉ​=−Vud​Vub∗​/(Vcd​Vcb∗​). The barred parameters include higher-order corrections to the Wolfenstein parameters. Comparing independent measurements of its sides and angles tests both quark mixing and the absence of additional flavor dynamics.
Semileptonic decay rates determine magnitudes of CKM entries through factors ∣Vij​∣2 once hadronic matrix elements are controlled. Neutral kaon and neutral B-meson mixing constrain products of entries through weak box scattering amplitudes. CP asymmetries probe their invariant relative phases, with kaon mixing providing indirect CP violation and decay amplitudes providing direct CP violation. These processes complement tests of the unitarity constraints on rows and columns. The minimal three-generation Standard Model describes weak quark mixing by a unitary matrix with three angles and one physical CP phase; it does not predict the Yukawa couplings or their hierarchy.

There is a sign inconsistency in the printed model. For the ordinary cross product let J(a)z=a×z. The vector triple-product identity gives [J(a),J(b)]=J(a×b). Consequently the printed Dμ​=∂μ​+eJ(Aμ​) has gauge curvature
[Dμ​,Dν​]Φ=eFμν+​×Φ,Fμν+​=∂μ​Aν​−∂ν​Aμ​+eAμ​×Aν​.
(27)
Its gauge curvature cannot instead contain the printed negative quadratic term. For a noncommuting pure gauge potential for this gauge covariant derivative, F+=0 whereas F−=−2eAμ​×Aν​=0. Thus the printed gauge kinetic term assigns a nonzero value to a connection obtained by applying the gauge-field transformation law to the zero connection. Literally the printed action has global rotations but not the requested local triplet gauge symmetry. The cross-product convention for an adjoint covariant derivative makes the minimal repair explicit: change the gauge field strength quadratic term to a plus sign, retaining the printed gauge covariant derivative. Changing the sign in the gauge covariant derivative instead would be another consistent convention. The remaining calculation uses the first repair.
The corrected theory has local SO(3) gauge symmetry, equivalently the adjoint-field realization of the SU(2) Lie algebra; its adjoint scalar fields and gauge fields are insensitive to the center of a group of SU(2). Explicitly, Φ↦R(x)Φ and
eJ(Aμ​)↦ReJ(Aμ​)R−1−(∂μ​R)R−1.
(28)
Both Dμ​Φ and Fμν​ transform as triplets, so their dot products and the scalar potential are invariant. Assume λ>0, e=0 and take v≥0 without loss of generality. For v>0 the minima have Φ2=v2. Choose the scalar-field vacuum Φ0​=v(0,0,1), whose stabilizer consists of rotations around the third axis: the residual gauge symmetry is SO(2), locally U(1).
In unitary gauge write Φ=(0,0,v+h), Aμ​=Aμ3​ and Wμ±​=(Aμ1​∓iAμ2​)/2​. These are the neutral massless gauge field, two charged massive gauge fields and a real radial scalar field. Define
fμν​=∂μ​Aν​−∂ν​Aμ​,Cμν​=Wμ+​Wν−​−Wν+​Wμ−​,
(29)
Gμν±​=(∂μ​∓ieAμ​)Wν±​−(∂ν​∓ieAν​)Wμ±​.
(30)
Substitution gives Fμν3​=fμν​−ieCμν​ and (Fμν1​∓iFμν2​)/2​=Gμν±​. The entire physical-field Lagrangian density is therefore
L=−41​(fμν​−ieCμν​)(fμν−ieCμν)−21​Gμν+​G−μν+21​∂μ​h∂μh+e2(v+h)2Wμ+​W−μ−8λ​[(v+h)2−v2]2.
(31)
In particular Dμ​Φ=(e(v+h)Aμ2​,−e(v+h)Aμ1​,∂μ​h); this directly verifies the scalar field kinetic and vector mass terms. Expanding the scalar potential gives λv2h2/2+λvh3/2+λh4/8. Comparing the quadratic terms with standard real scalar field and charged-vector mass terms yields the adjoint triplet Higgs spectrum,
mW±2​=e2v2,mA​=0,mh2​=λv2​.
(32)
This also rewrites the purely vector interactions, which are already contained in the displayed gauge field strengths rather than omitted from the theory.
The original symmetry remains unbroken at v=0. The unique minimum is then Φ=0, with three massless gauge fields and three massless real scalar field components; the quartic scalar field interaction remains. The physical degrees of freedom number 3×2+3=9. At v>0 the two broken generators supply two Goldstone bosons, eaten to give longitudinal gauge-boson polarizations of the charged vectors. The physical count is 2×3+2+1=9: two massive vectors, one massless vector and one radial scalar field. The scalar components eaten in unitary gauge are not additional physical particles.
For the broken phase all interactions containing the physical scalar field are
Lscalar,int​=−2λv​h3−8λ​h4+2e2vhWμ+​W−μ+e2h2Wμ+​W−μ.
(33)
Multiplying interaction coefficients by i and differentiating with respect to the external fields includes the identical-field factorials. The scalar vertices of an adjoint triplet Higgs model are
hhh: −3iλv,hhhh: −3iλ,hWμ+​Wν−​: 2ie2vgμν​,hhWμ+​Wν−​: 2ie2gμν​​.
(34)
For example, the h3 rule contains 3!, whereas the hhW+W− rule contains only 2! from its two identical scalar fields. There are no hAA or hhAA vertices: the radial scalar field is neutral under the residual U(1). There is no derivative scalar-vector vertex in this physical spectrum in unitary gauge.
For completeness, if the unbroken phase is chosen, its physical scalar fields are the three components ϕa​, not a single radial h. Expanding its gauge-covariant kinetic term gives
LAϕϕ​=eϵiaj(∂μ​ϕi​)Aaμϕj​,LAAϕϕ​=2e2​[Aμa​Aaμϕb​ϕb​−Aμa​ϕa​Abμϕb​].
(35)
With all momenta incoming and Fourier transform convention e−ipx, the three unbroken-phase rules are
Aμa​ϕb​(p)ϕc​(q):eϵabc(q−p)μ​,
(36)
Aμa​Aνb​ϕc​ϕd​:ie2(2δabδcd−δacδbd−δadδbc)gμν​,
(37)
ϕa​ϕb​ϕc​ϕd​:−iλ(δabδcd+δacδbd+δadδbc).
(38)
The first rule follows by differentiating once on each possible scalar: for a=3,b=1,c=2, the interaction is eAμ3​(ϕ1​∂μϕ2​−ϕ2​∂μϕ1​), which fixes the momentum sign. The remaining rules follow from the quadratic gauge interaction and −λ(ϕa​ϕa​)2/8. There are no unbroken-phase cubic scalar field Feynman vertices.
