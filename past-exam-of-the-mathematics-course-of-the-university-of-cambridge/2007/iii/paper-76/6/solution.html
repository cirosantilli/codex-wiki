Write c=cos2ϕ, s=sin2ϕ and σ=(σ11​+σ22​)/2. On the fixed yield surface, ξ,l​=−c and τ,l​=−s, where l is stress-space arc length. Substituting σ11​=σ+ξ, σ22​=σ−ξ, σ12​=τ into planar force balance gives
σ,1​=cl,1​+sl,2​,σ,2​=sl,1​−cl,2​.​
(43)
Thus ∇σ=M∇l, with M=(cs​s−c​).
For a spatial curve x(s0​) and a function F(σ,l), the chain rule gives
ds0​dF​=(F,σ​ F,l​)(x1′​c+x2′​sx1′​​x1′​s−x2′​cx2′​​)(l,1​l,2​​).
(44)
Here primes refer to the spatial parameter s0​, not the yield-curve parameter l. The displayed row-matrix product being zero is therefore sufficient for F to remain constant.
The eigenvectors of M are eα​=(cosϕ,sinϕ) and eβ​=(−sinϕ,cosϕ), with eigenvalues +1 and −1. Along these slip lines, respectively, dσ=dl and dσ=−dl. This proves the stress characteristics for an anisotropic yield curve:
σ−l=constant on α,dx1​dx2​​=tanϕ;σ+l=constant on β,dx1​dx2​​=−cotϕ.​
(45)
For the velocity relations, the outward normal to the reduced yield curve is proportional to (−sin2ϕ,cos2ϕ). The associated flow rule, using the symmetric stress work pairing D:δσ, therefore gives a rate-of-strain tensor proportional to
D∝(−sin2ϕcos2ϕ​cos2ϕsin2ϕ​).
(46)
The off-diagonal stress variation contributes 2D12​δσ12​, which ensures this normality calculation uses the correct factor. Direct multiplication gives eαT​Deα​=eβT​Deβ​=0: neither slip line direction extends instantaneously.
Resolve the velocity as w=ueα​+veβ​. Since deα​=eβ​dϕ and deβ​=−eα​dϕ, its longitudinal derivative along an α curve is du−vdϕ, and along a β curve it is dv+udϕ. Setting these extensions to zero gives the Geiringer velocity relations:
du−vdϕ=0along α,dv+udϕ=0along β.​
(47)
