An integral of motion is a function of phase space that is constant along a trajectory. Put ϕ=−ψ+constant, so the stellar acceleration is v˙=∇ψ. In a time-independent spherical Newtonian gravitational potential,
dtdE​=∇ψ⋅v−v⋅v˙=0,dtdL​=v×v+r×∇ψ=0.
(1)
The second equality uses the radial direction of ∇ψ. Thus both the binding energy and the full specific angular momentum vector are integrals of motion.
The Collisionless Boltzmann equation is
∂t​f+v⋅∇x​f+∇ψ⋅∇v​f=0.
(2)
For a stationary galactic distribution function, this says df/dt=0 on every characteristic, so f is constant on each stellar orbit. Locally choose a complete set of orbit labels Ij​, themselves integrals of motion; constancy along the characteristic means f=F(I1​,…,Is​). Conversely, for any such differentiable F, the chain rule gives df/dt=∑j​F,j​I˙j​=0. This proves Jeans theorem in its orbit-invariance form. It does not claim that just two particular integrals classify every orbit in an arbitrary potential. For the specified spherical model, F(E,L) is a rotationally invariant stationary choice.
For the velocity integration, write vr​=vcosη, v⊥​=vsinη, L=rvsinη, and set k=r/ra​. At fixed position the velocity-dependent factor is
exp[−2σ2v2​(1+k2sin2η)],d3v=v2sinηdvdηdχ.
(3)
Use ∫0∞​v2e−Av2/(2σ2)dv=π/2​σ3A−3/2 and integrate over χ. The mass density is
ρ(r)=2ρ1​eψ(r)/σ2​∫0π​(1+k2sin2η)3/2sinηdη​=1+r2/ra2​ρ1​eψ(r)/σ2​.
(4)
Since ρc​=ρ(0)=ρ1​eψ(0)/σ2,
ρ(r)=1+r2/ra2​ρc​​exp[σ2ψ(r)−ψ(0)​].​
(5)
These integrals use the stated untruncated galactic distribution function over all velocities; an additional escape-energy cutoff would change the result.
In local orthogonal velocity components the same exponent is −[vr2​+(1+k2)(vθ2​+vϕ2​)]/(2σ2). Each component is a centred Gaussian distribution. The one-dimensional identity ∫v2e−Av2/(2σ2)dv/∫e−Av2/(2σ2)dv=σ2/A yields
⟨vr2​⟩=σ2,⟨vθ2​⟩=⟨vϕ2​⟩=ra2​+r2σ2ra2​​.​
(6)
In particular the velocity-anisotropy parameter is βan​=1−(σθ2​+σϕ2​)/(2σr2​)=r2/(ra2​+r2). This is Gaussian angular-momentum suppression in a stellar distribution.
The radial laws also require self-consistency through the Poisson equation for Newtonian gravity:
r21​(r2ψ′)′=−4πGρ,vc2​=−rψ′=rGM(<r)​.
(7)
For r≪ra​, outside a sufficiently small core, the model is almost isotropic and isothermal. Seek its scale-free envelope with ρ=A/r2. Its distribution-density relation gives ψ′=−2σ2/r, and the Poisson equation for Newtonian gravity gives A=σ2/(2πG). Thus the intended intermediate singular isothermal sphere behaviour is
ρ≃2πGr2σ2​,vc2​≃2σ2(rc​≪r≪ra​),​
(8)
where a core scale is rc​=σ/4πGρc​​.
The stated r−2 law cannot be the actual central limit of a regular finite-density model. For finite ρc​ and ψ(0), regularity and the Poisson equation for Newtonian gravity instead give
ψ(r)−ψ(0)=−32πGρc​​r2+O(r4),ρ(r)=ρc​[1−(ra2​1​+3σ22πGρc​​)r2+O(r4)],
(9)
and vc2​=(4πGρc​/3)r2+O(r4). A broad intermediate r−2 region exists only if rc​≪ra​; the radius ra​ alone does not ensure it.
For the outer radially anisotropic isothermal halo asymptotics, put s=log(r/ra​), y=[ψ(r)−ψ(0)]/σ2 and λ=4πGρc​ra2​/σ2. At r≫ra​ the density relation and Poisson equation become
yss​+ys​≃−λey.
(10)
The slow outer balance is ys​∼−λey, so d(e−y)/ds∼λ, giving ey∼1/(λs). Its consistency check is y∼−log(λs), for which yss​/ys​∼−1/s→0. Therefore
ρ(r)∼4πGr2log(r/r∗​)σ2​,vc2​(r)∼log(r/r∗​)σ2​.​
(11)
The reference radius r∗​ changes only subleading logarithmic terms. The circular speed falls as (logr)−1/2, rather than remaining exactly flat.
Physically, the model has an almost isotropic central region and, if the scales separate, a flat-rotation isothermal envelope. Beyond ra​, suppression of large specific angular momentum leaves predominantly radial orbits: radial velocity dispersion remains σ, while transverse dispersion falls as r−1. The outer density acquires a logarithmic suppression and the galaxy rotation curve declines slowly. The enclosed mass still grows approximately as r/logr, so this untruncated model has infinite total mass.

The divergence theorem states ∫V​∇⋅FdV=∮∂V​F⋅ndS. With the Poisson equation for Newtonian gravity, this gives
∮∂V​∇ϕ⋅ndS=4πGM(V),
(12)
or gravitational-acceleration flux ∮g⋅ndS=−4πGM(V).
Use cylindrical radius R=rsinθ, so r(1+∣cosθ∣)=r+∣z∣. An arbitrary reference length in the logarithm only adds a constant potential. Off the equatorial plane, the radial part of ∇2log[r(1+∣cosθ∣)] is 1/r2. Writing c=cosθ, its angular part is r−2d[(1−c2)dlog(1+∣c∣)/dc]/dc=−1/r2 on either open hemisphere. The two cancel: there is no volume density away from z=0.
At the plane, ∂z​ϕ∣0+​=v02​/R and ∂z​ϕ∣0−​=−v02​/R. Applying the gravitational flux law to a thin pillbox gives 4πGΣ=∂z​ϕ∣0+​−∂z​ϕ∣0−​. Consequently
Σ(R)=2πGRv02​​,ρ(R,z)=2πGRv02​​δ(z).​
(13)
This is the Mestel disk potential-density pair. There is no additional point mass at the origin: the enclosed mass tends to zero linearly as the enclosing radius shrinks.
At z=0, ϕ=v02​logR up to a constant, so radial circular balance gives
vcirc2​=R∂R​ϕ=v02​.​
(14)
On a sphere of radius r, ∂r​ϕ=v02​/r at every nonsingular angular point. Its flux is 4πv02​r, and hence
M(<r)=Gv02​r​.​
(15)
Direct integration of the surface density confirms 2π∫0r​Σ(R)RdR=v02​r/G. Thus vcirc2​=GM(<r)/r even though the source is a razor-thin disk. This equality is a special scale-free property of the Mestel disk, not evidence that the source is spherical. In a general disk, matter outside the orbit and nonspherical interior forces prevent this enclosed-mass formula for the local circular speed. The model has infinite total mass, so its potential is defined by differences rather than a zero at infinity.

Adopt the planar Fourier transform convention
g​(k)=∫e−ik⋅xg(x)d2x,g(x)=∫(2π)2d2k​eik⋅xg​(k).
(16)
Extend the disk potential to three dimensions. The Poisson equation for Newtonian gravity becomes
(∂z2​−k2)ϕ​(k,z)=4πGΣ(k)δ(z),k=∣k∣.
(17)
For k>0, decay away from an isolated disk and continuity across it give ϕ​=B(k)e−k∣z∣. Integration through the plane yields −2kB=4πGΣ, hence the razor-thin disk Poisson kernel
ϕ​(k,0)=−k2πG​Σ(k).
(18)
The given transform of K(x)=1/∣x∣ is K=2π/k. The convolution theorem therefore gives the forward potential relation
ϕ(x)=−G∫∣x−x′∣Σ(x′)​d2x′.​
(19)
For the reverse relation, Δ2​ϕ​=−k2ϕ​, so
K∗Δ2​ϕ​=k2π​(−k2ϕ​)=4π2GΣ.
(20)
Invert the transform to obtain the thin-disk potential-density inversion
Σ(x)=4π2G1​∫∣x−x′∣∂x′2​ϕ(x′)+∂y′2​ϕ(x′)​d2x′.​
(21)
An additive constant in ϕ is annihilated by Δ2​. These formulas hold for suitable isolated finite disks, and in a distributional or regularized sense for less regular profiles. The uniform zero-wavenumber sheet needs a separate vertical solution; the infinite Mestel disk requires potential differences or an infrared regularization, since its absolute forward integral diverges.

For a bound Kepler orbit, the semimajor axis is half the long diameter of the ellipse; if rp​,rmathrma​ are its apsidal distances, a=(rp​+rmathrma​)/2. The orbital eccentricity is e=(rmathrma​−rp​)/(rmathrma​+rp​), with 0≤e<1 and semiminor axis b=a1−e2​. The gravitating centre is a focus, displaced by ae from the ellipse's geometric centre.
Choose a reference plane, a positive normal and a fixed reference direction in that plane. The ascending node is where the orbit crosses toward positive normal displacement. The longitude of ascending node Ω is the reference-plane angle from the fixed direction to that node. The argument of periapsis ω is the angle from the ascending node to periapsis, measured in the orbital plane in the direction of motion. The orbital inclination i is the angle between the reference normal and the orbital angular-momentum normal, with 0≤i≤π. The diagram separates the ellipse's shape from these orientation angles.
Let μ=GM and use actions per unit test-particle mass. In the prograde spherical-action chart underlying the stated Hamiltonian, Jϕ​=Lz​ and Jθ​=L−Lz​, so Jθ​+Jϕ​=L. The radial action then satisfies Jr​+L=μa​, consistently with HK​=−μ/(2a). Define
J1​=Jr​+Jθ​+Jϕ​,J2​=Jθ​+Jϕ​,J3​=Jϕ​.​
(22)
If (wr​,wθ​,wϕ​) are the old angles, a type-two generating function is
F2​=(J1​−J2​)wr​+(J2​−J3​)wθ​+J3​wϕ​.
(23)
Differentiation with respect to the old angles recovers Jr​,Jθ​,Jϕ​, and differentiation with respect to the new actions gives the canonical transformation
w1​=wr​,w2​=wθ​−wr​,w3​=wϕ​−wθ​.​
(24)
Its triangular integer action matrix has determinant one, so it preserves the angle periodicities. In the new coordinates,
HK​=−2J12​μ2​,w˙1​=J13​μ2​=a3μ​​,w˙2​=w˙3​=0.​
(25)
These are the Delaunay variables:
J1​=L=μa​,J2​=G=μa(1−e2)​,J3​=H=Gcosi.
(26)
With the usual choices of angle origins, w1​=ℓ is mean anomaly, w2​=ω is argument of periapsis, and w3​=Ω is longitude of ascending node. The generator G=L rotates the orbit within its plane, whereas H=Lz​ rotates that plane about the reference normal; this explains the angular identification. The fast angle obeys Kepler's equation ℓ=E−esinE, where E is eccentric anomaly.
For retrograde orbits, signed Jϕ​=Lz​ gives Jθ​=L−∣Lz​∣ and the spherical Hamiltonian uses Jr​+Jθ​+∣Jϕ​∣. The stated plus-sign form is therefore a prograde chart, not a global signed-action formula. The Delaunay variables still use H=Lz​; on the retrograde branch the generating function is (L−G)wr​+(G+H)wθ​+Hwϕ​. At e=0, periapsis is undefined, and at i=0 or π the node is undefined: these are angle-chart degeneracies, not physical singularities.
For the perturbed planet orbit, the radial and vertical frequencies mean small oscillations about a circular equatorial orbit of cylindrical radius R. Write r=R2+z2​ and retain the stated quadrupole potential. On the equator,
ϕ(R,0)=−Rμ​−R3α2μ​,Ωc2​=R1​∂R​ϕ=R3μ​+R53α2μ​.
(27)
At fixed axial specific angular momentum Lz​, the radial effective potential is ϕ(R,0)+Lz2​/(2R2). Its second derivative at circular balance Lz​=R2Ωc​ gives the radial epicyclic frequency
κ2=∂R2​ϕ+3Ωc2​=RdRdΩc2​​+4Ωc2​=R3μ​−R53α2μ​.​
(28)
Reflection symmetry decouples vertical motion to first order. Expanding the potential in z gives
ϕ(R,z)=ϕ(R,0)+21​(R3μ​+R59α2μ​)z2+O(z4),
(29)
so the vertical epicyclic frequency is
ν2=R3μ​+R59α2μ​.​
(30)
The circular orbit is radially stable only for R2>3α2. For α=0 in that domain, ν>Ωc​>κ. Vertical node crossings repeat at frequency ν while azimuth advances at Ωc​, so the line of nodes has nodal precession rate Ωc​−ν<0. Radial periapsis passages repeat at κ, giving apsidal precession rate Ωc​−κ>0. To leading quadrupole order, with n=μ/R3​,
Ω˙node​≃−3nR2α2​,ϖ˙≃+3nR2α2​.​
(31)
This is oblate-quadrupole epicyclic precession. Here ϖ denotes the nearly equatorial longitude of periapsis, not ω alone.
The precession signs above have a small-eccentricity, small-inclination domain. They are not a universal claim for every inclined Kepler ellipse. To see the distinction with the same Delaunay variables, average the perturbation over an unperturbed orbit. Since z/r=sinisin(ω+f) and dt=r2df/G, the term linear in ecosf integrates to zero and the sin2(ω+f) average in the r−3 integral is 1/2. Thus
HQ​=2a3(1−e2)3/2α2μ​(1−3cos2i)=2L3G3α2μ4​(1−G23H2​).
(32)
Differentiating at fixed other actions yields the first-order secular rates
Ω˙=−a2(1−e2)23nα2cosi​,ω˙=2a2(1−e2)23nα2(5cos2i−1)​.
(33)
Their nearly equatorial sum reproduces the prograde ϖ˙. For a polar orbit, however, the node is fixed and ω˙<0. This supplies a concrete counterexample to extending the requested forward periapsis precession beyond the intended epicyclic regime.

For an isolated, virialized three-dimensional Newtonian system with negligible boundary-pressure terms, the virial theorem gives 2K+W=0. Hence its total energy is E=K+W=−K. Defining the kinetic temperature by K=3NkT/2 gives
dTdE​=−23​Nk<0.​
(34)
Thus losing energy makes the system hotter, while adding energy makes it cooler. This negative heat capacity of a virialized gravitational system uses the inverse-distance potential and the stated boundary assumptions; it is not a dimension-independent consequence of self-gravity.
A gravothermal catastrophe occurs when a stellar core loses heat to its halo through two-body relaxation, contracts and becomes hotter because of its negative heat capacity. The larger temperature gradient then drives more heat outward and accelerates contraction. In the confined three-dimensional isothermal sequence this runaway is associated with loss of a regular thermodynamic equilibrium at a finite total energy. It is different from a fast collisionless instability.
For the rods, use energies and masses per unit axial length, consistently with their logarithmic interaction. Define
q=2GMβ​,x=Re2​R2​,F(x)=1−q+qx,ψe​=−GMlog(V/V0​).
(35)
Integrating the two-dimensional Maxwell-Boltzmann velocity distribution gives ρ=Aeβψ, with a positive normalization A. The proposed potential has ψ=ψe​−2logF/β, so this requires ρ∝F−2. Direct differentiation gives
ψ′=−Re2​F2GMR​,R1​(Rψ′)′=−Re2​F24GM(1−q)​.
(36)
Consequently the Poisson equation for Newtonian gravity is satisfied precisely by
ρ(R)=πRe2​F2M(1−q)​.​
(37)
The free normalization of the velocity distribution can be chosen to give this density. Its enclosed line mass verifies total-mass normalization:
M(<R)=M(1−q)∫0x​(1−q+qu)2du​=F(x)Mx​​,M(<Re​)=M.
(38)
At the wall F(1)=1, so the required potential value is also satisfied. This establishes the two-dimensional self-gravitating rod equilibrium, including its field equation, distribution law, mass and boundary condition.
A regular positive solution needs F(0)=1−q>0. Since β=m/(kT),
T>Tmin​,kTmin​=21​GMm.​
(39)
The minimum is an infimum of regular equilibrium temperatures. At equality the central logarithm is singular; for T<Tmin​, F vanishes inside the box. Thus equality is not a regular isothermal rod configuration.
Each of the two velocity components has second moment 1/β, so the kinetic energy is K=M/β=NkT. With the given reference potential, the gravitational potential energy is W=−21​∫ρψd2R. Using dM=M(1−q)dx/F2 gives
W=−2Mψe​​+β1​∫logFdM.
(40)
The remaining integral can be evaluated explicitly:
∫logFdM​=qM(1−q)​∫1−q1​F2logF​dF=qM(1−q)​[−FlogF+1​]1−q1​=M[1+q1​log(1−q)].​
(41)
Adding K therefore yields
E=2GM2​log(V/V0​)+βM​[2+q1​log(1−q)].
(42)
Now NkTmin​=GM2/2, Θ=T/Tmin​=1/q and M/β=NkTmin​Θ. The energy-temperature curve of a two-dimensional self-gravitating rod gas is consequently
E=NkTmin​[log(V/V0​)+2Θ+Θ2log(1−Θ−1)],Θ>1.​
(43)
At fixed box area, differentiation gives
CV​=Nk[2+2Θlog(1−Θ−1)+Θ−1Θ​].
(44)
To establish its sign rather than infer it from a plot, expand the logarithm for Θ>1:
2Θ+Θ2log(1−Θ−1)=Θ−21​−∑j=3∞​jΘj−21​,NkCV​​=1+j=3∑∞​jΘj−1j−2​>1.​
(45)
The series and its derivative converge locally uniformly in Θ>1. Moreover E→−∞ as Θ↓1 and E→+∞ as Θ→∞. Thus every finite energy on this mean-field isothermal sequence has a unique regular equilibrium temperature, and there is no finite-energy termination or turning point. The central density M/[V(1−q)] diverges only in the infinite-negative-energy limit.
There is no finite-energy gravothermal catastrophe in this two-dimensional rod model. Its logarithmic interaction gives a different caloric curve from the three-dimensional inverse-distance problem. This microcanonical conclusion does not prohibit a distinct fixed-temperature collapse: in the canonical ensemble, a bath at or below Tmin​ admits no regular equilibrium of this family.
