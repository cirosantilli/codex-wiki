Past exam of the mathematics course of the University of Cambridge / 2007 / iii / Paper 65 / 3 / a / Solution

 ... Past exam of the mathematics course of the University of Cambridge 2007 iii Paper 65 3 a
OurBigBook.com Words: 264
Use a steady axisymmetric flow governed by ideal magnetohydrodynamics and take the Newtonian gravitational potential to be axisymmetric. Work locally on connected regular magnetic flux surfaces with nonzero poloidal flow. As usual for a wind without an imposed toroidal loop voltage, Eϕ​=0; regularity on an included rotation axis guarantees this condition. It is needed to exclude steady cross-field flow in an annular domain.
Since ∇ϕ=eϕ​/R, the poloidal magnetic flux function gives
Bp​=R1​∇ψ×eϕ​,BR​=−R1​∂z​ψ,Bz​=R1​∂R​ψ,Bp​⋅∇ψ=0.
(34)
The poloidal magnetic field is divergence-free. The zero toroidal electric field, with E=−u×B, gives up​∥Bp​. Define k by ρup​=kBp​. Steady mass conservation then gives
0=∇⋅(ρup​)=∇⋅(kBp​)=Bp​⋅∇k.
(35)
Thus k=k(ψ) is the magnetohydrodynamic mass loading, the mass flux per unit poloidal magnetic flux. Subtracting (k/ρ)B from the full velocity leaves only an azimuthal component. The cross product is
u×B=(uϕ​−ρkBϕ​​)R∇ψ​≡ω∇ψ.
(36)
The steady ideal magnetohydrodynamic induction equation requires 0=∇×(ω∇ψ)=∇ω×∇ψ. Consequently the field-line angular velocity is ω=ω(ψ) and
u=ρk(ψ)B​+Rω(ψ)eϕ​​.
(37)
Neither gas pressure nor axisymmetric gravity exerts an azimuthal torque. Multiplying the azimuthal momentum equation by R combines the cylindrical curvature terms into angular-momentum transport:
ρup​⋅∇(Ruϕ​)=μ0​1​Bp​⋅∇(RBϕ​).
(38)
Insert ρup​=kBp​ and use Bp​⋅∇k=0. It follows that
Bp​⋅∇[R(uϕ​−μ0​kBϕ​​)]=0,
(39)
so the magnetohydrodynamic angular-momentum invariant is
uϕ​−μ0​k(ψ)Bϕ​​=Rℓ(ψ)​​.
(40)
The two terms account respectively for matter and Maxwell stress tensor transport of angular momentum.
Finally, subtracting γ times the logarithmic mass density equation from the logarithmic pressure equation gives Dln(p/ργ)/Dt=0. The specific entropy of a fixed-composition ideal gas is a function of p/ργ. Steadiness and axisymmetry reduce its advection to up​⋅∇s=0. Hence
s=s(ψ),p=K(ψ)ργ​.
(41)
These flux-label functions are local on connected regular surfaces; disconnected components with the same numerical label need not share constants without an additional matching condition.
