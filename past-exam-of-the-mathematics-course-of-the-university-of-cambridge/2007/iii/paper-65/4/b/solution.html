To distinguish Eulerian and Lagrangian fluid perturbations, use ξ=(ξx​,ξy​,ξz​) for the fluid displacement, ϕ=Φ′ for the Eulerian potential perturbation, and δρ for the Eulerian mass density perturbation. The equilibrium mass density ρ(z) is the profile from part (a); subscript z below denotes differentiation. Linearized mass conservation and the isothermal equation of state give
δρ=−ikρξx​−(ρξz​)z​,δp=cs2​δρ.
(53)
The velocity perturbation is −iωξ. Linearized momentum initially has the form
−ω2ρξ=−∇δp−δρ∇Φ0​−ρ∇ϕ.
(54)
Since ∇Φ0​=−cs2​∇lnρ, the pressure and perturbed mass density gravity terms combine into −ρ∇(cs2​δρ/ρ). Define W=cs2​δρ/ρ+ϕ. The closed linearized system is
ω2ξx​=ikW,ω2ξz​=Wz​,ω2ξy​=0,ϕzz​−k2ϕ=4πGδρ,δρ=−ikρξx​−(ρξz​)z​​.
(55)
The y component is a neutral transverse sector; it vanishes for nonzero-frequency modes.
To prove reality rather than assume it, multiply momentum by ρξ∗ and integrate over z. With I=∫ρ∣ξ∣2dz>0, integration by parts and the conjugate continuity equation give
ω2I=[ρξz∗​W]−∞∞​+cs2​∫ρ∣δρ∣2​dz+∫δρ∗ϕdz.
(56)
Using the conjugate Poisson equation for Newtonian gravity in the last integral gives
∫δρ∗ϕdz=4πG1​[ϕϕz∗​]−∞∞​−4πG1​∫(∣ϕz​∣2+k2∣ϕ∣2)dz.
(57)
Take disturbances with finite I, finite pressure and gravitational integrals, and vanishing pressure work and gravitational boundary terms. For k=0, require the potential to decay at both infinities and the fluid displacement to make ρξz∗​W→0 as well. In a source-free outer region the decaying potential is proportional to e−∣k∣∣z∣. The isothermal displacement energy of a self-gravitating slab then gives
ω2I=cs2​∫ρ∣δρ∣2​dz−4πG1​∫(∣ϕz​∣2+k2∣ϕ∣2)dz∈R​.
(58)
Since I is strictly positive for a nonzero displacement, ω2 is real. Positive values describe oscillations; negative values give pure growth and decay. The result is the energy identity for the pressure-gravity displacement operator, a self-adjoint operator in the mass density-weighted inner product. The identity excludes an oscillatory growing eigenmode under these boundary conditions. At k=0 the same identity applies when its boundary terms vanish and its integrals remain finite, with the gravitational integral involving only ∣ϕz​∣2.
