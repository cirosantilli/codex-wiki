The Fourier sine basis of the unit square is ejℓ​(x,y)=2sin(jπx)sin(ℓπy), j,ℓ≥1, normalized in the L2 inner product. It satisfies the zero Dirichlet boundary conditions and Δejℓ​=−π2(j2+ℓ2)ejℓ​. Expand the given initial datum as u0​=∑ajℓ​ejℓ​, where ∑∣ajℓ​∣2=∥u0​∥22​. The solution is
u(x,y,t)=∑j,ℓ≥1​ajℓ​e[κ−π2(j2+ℓ2)]tejℓ​(x,y).
(21)
For κ≤2π2, every exponent is nonpositive. The series converges in L2 for t≥0, tends to u0​ in L2 as t↓0 by dominated convergence, and differentiates termwise for t>0 because the Gaussian high-frequency decay dominates every polynomial derivative factor. This constructs a classical positive-time solution and the appropriate mild solution at the possibly rough initial time.
Its norm and the difference of two such solutions satisfy
∥u(t)∥2​≤e(κ−2π2)t∥u0​∥2​,∥u(t)−v(t)∥2​≤e(κ−2π2)t∥u0​−v0​∥2​.​
(22)
Existence, uniqueness in the natural L2 solution class, and continuous dependence follow. For uniqueness one can project any weak solution onto each sine mode, obtaining the same scalar linear equation and initial coefficient. Thus this is a Hadamard well-posed problem, with a contractive strongly continuous semigroup in the stated range.
To prove pointwise decay from merely L2 data, use ∣ejℓ​∣≤2 and the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality:
supx,y​∣u(x,y,t)∣≤2∥u0​∥2​(∑j,ℓ≥1​e2[κ−π2(j2+ℓ2)]t)1/2.
(23)
For t≥1, factor out e2(κ−2π2)t; the remaining sum is bounded by ∑j,ℓ≥1​e−2π2(j2+ℓ2−2)<∞. Therefore
κ<2π2⟹(x,y)∈[0,1]2sup​∣u(x,y,t)∣⟶0.​
(24)
At equality the first sine mode is stationary, so strict inequality is essential for decay of every datum. This is the Dirichlet heat-reaction threshold on the unit square. The equation is actually well posed on every fixed finite time interval for any fixed real κ; beyond the threshold it has a growing first mode and loses long-time contractivity. The restricted well-posedness assertion in the question is therefore sufficient rather than necessary.
