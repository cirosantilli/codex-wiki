A collocation Runge-Kutta method approximates an ordinary differential equation by a polynomial whose derivative agrees with the vector field at selected times. Let s distinct nodes be ci​∈[0,1], and consider the possibly nonautonomous equation y′=f(t,y). On a step from (tn​,yn​), choose a polynomial U(τ) of degree at most s, with U(0)=yn​, such that U′(ci​)=hf(tn​+ci​h,U(ci​)). Here τ=(t−tn​)/h is scaled time; the numerical endpoint is yn+1​=U(1).
Let ℓj​ be the Lagrange interpolation polynomial equal to one at cj​ and zero at the other nodes, and set kj​=f(tn​+cj​h,U(cj​)). Interpolation of the polynomial derivative gives
U′(τ)=h∑j=1s​ℓj​(τ)kj​,U(τ)=yn​+h∑j=1s​(∫0τ​ℓj​(v)dv)kj​.
(38)
Evaluating at the nodes and endpoint derives the Runge-Kutta method coefficients:
aij​=∫0ci​​ℓj​(v)dv,bj​=∫01​ℓj​(v)dv,ki​=f(tn​+ci​h,yn​+hj∑​aij​kj​).​
(39)
Thus collocation is a specific family of generally implicit RK methods, not a separate update mechanism. The identity ∑j​ℓj​=1 gives A1=c and ∑j​bj​=1, the collocation tableau row-sum identity. For sufficiently small h, a vector field with Lipschitz constant L gives a contraction of the stage map when hLmaxi​∑j​∣aij​∣<1, on a suitable bounded neighborhood. The Banach fixed-point theorem then gives a unique nearby stage solution. Equivalently smooth stage equations have identity derivative in the stage variables at h=0, so the inverse function theorem gives a smooth nearby branch. Arbitrarily large-step solvability is a separate issue for an implicit Runge-Kutta method.
Order analysis has two levels: polynomial accuracy at stages and extra accuracy at the endpoint. Integration of interpolated powers gives the stage moment identities
∑j​aij​cjr−1​=rcir​​,1≤r≤s.

An induction in powers of h, using these identities in the stage equations, shows that the collocation polynomial agrees coefficientwise with the exact Taylor polynomial through degree s:
U(τ)=∑r=0s​r!hrτr​y(r)(tn​)+O(hs+1).

For example, the coefficient of each new stage term is integrated exactly because its node dependence is a polynomial of degree at most s−1. This proves stage order s and bounded actual-time derivatives of the collocation polynomial up to degree s. Assuming the vector field is sufficiently smooth on a fixed compact solution neighborhood, its composition with this polynomial has uniformly bounded derivatives of all finite orders needed below.
The endpoint weights define the interpolatory quadrature rule Q(v)=∑i​bi​v(ci​). Suppose it is exact through degree q−1, but not through degree q. Necessarily s≤q≤2s: interpolation integrates degrees below s exactly, while the nonzero polynomial ω(τ)2, with ω(τ)=∏i​(τ−ci​), has positive integral but quadrature value zero and degree 2s. For q=s, the O(hs) residual already gives O(hs+1). For q>s, Taylor-expand the smooth matrix weight as Ψ(tn​+hτ)=∑ℓ=0q−s−1​hℓΨℓ​τℓ+O(hq−s). Every product term with r+ℓ<q integrates to zero: its polynomial factor is ω times a polynomial of degree r−s+ℓ≤q−s−1. All remaining terms are O(hq) before the time integration, including both remainders. Hence
local endpoint error=O(hq+1),global order=q.​

This is collocation endpoint superconvergence by quadrature orthogonality. For the global assertion, the stage contraction estimate gives a one-step Lipschitz bound 1+Ch on a fixed solution neighborhood. Thus the propagated error satisfies en+1​≤(1+Ch)en​+Chq+1, and the discrete Gronwall inequality gives en​=O(hq) on a fixed time interval. The order cannot generically exceed q: for y′=tq, each collocation step reduces to the same quadrature rule, whose first nonexact moment yields a nonzero O(hq+1) local error and O(hq) composite error.
The usual node families illustrate the result. Gauss collocation methods use interior Gaussian nodes and have q=2s, the maximum possible order. Right-endpoint Radau nodes give the Radau IIA method of order 2s−1. Including both endpoints with Lobatto quadrature gives the Lobatto IIIA method of order 2s−2 for s≥2. One-stage Gaussian collocation is the second-order implicit midpoint rule, while collocation at the right endpoint is the first-order Backward Euler method. There is also a structural link with Question 2. For Gaussian nodes, let Li​(τ)=∫0τ​ℓi​(v)dv. The derivative of Li​Lj​ has degree at most 2s−1, so Gaussian quadrature is exact on it. The integral is bi​bj​, while evaluating at the nodes gives bi​aij​+bj​aji​. Thus Gaussian collocation satisfies M=0 and has Runge-Kutta conservation of quadratic invariants. For the scalar test equation, any RK method has stability function R(z)=1+zbT(I−zA)−11. For implicit midpoint this is (1+z/2)/(1−z/2); the identity ∣1−z/2∣2−∣1+z/2∣2=−2Rez proves A-stability. High order, stage solvability, invariant preservation and damping are distinct design considerations, so no stability conclusion should be inferred from collocation order alone.
