 ... Past exam of the mathematics course of the University of Cambridge 2007 iii Paper 71 5 a
OurBigBook.com Words: 406
Work with real functions and f∈L2(0,1). To give the variational problem a definite domain, choose the homogeneous Dirichlet boundary conditions used in the next part. The natural form space is the zero-trace Sobolev space H=H01​(0,1), with norm ∥v∥H​=∥v′∥2​. The Sobolev trace operator is well defined because one-dimensional H1 functions have absolutely continuous representatives. For example v(0)=∫01​v(x)dx−∫01​(1−x)v′(x)dx, so its endpoint trace is bounded in the H1 norm; the other endpoint has the analogous estimate. The zero-trace space is closed in the Hilbert space H1, and the Poincaré inequality ∥v∥2​≤π−1∥v′∥2​ makes the chosen derivative norm equivalent to its full norm. Thus H is a Hilbert space.
Differentiability of p,q implies continuity on the compact interval. Hence there are finite constants pmax​,qmax​ and a positive pmin​ with pmin​≤p≤pmax​ and 0≤q≤qmax​. Define the symmetric bilinear form
a(u,v)=∫01​[p(x)u′(x)v′(x)+q(x)u(x)v(x)]dx.
(28)
The Cauchy-Schwarz inequality and Poincaré inequality give all the estimates required by the Lax-Milgram theorem:
∣a(u,v)∣≤(pmax​+π2qmax​​)∥u∥H​∥v∥H​,a(v,v)≥pmin​∥v∥H2​,∣⟨f,v⟩∣≤π∥f∥2​​∥v∥H​.
(29)
That theorem states that a continuous coercive bilinear form on a Hilbert space and a bounded linear functional determine a unique weak solution. Completeness, continuity, coercivity and boundedness have now all been verified. Therefore there is a unique u∈H with a(u,v)=⟨f,v⟩ for every v∈H.
The energy version of the given variational problem is I(v)=a(v,v)−2⟨f,v⟩. Its exact variation is
I(v+w)−I(v)=2a(v,w)−2⟨f,w⟩+a(w,w).
(30)
Boundedness of a makes the last term quadratic in ∥w∥H​, so the Fréchet derivative is DI(v)[w]=2a(v,w)−2⟨f,w⟩. Its vanishing for every test function is precisely
∫01​[pu′v′+quv]dx=∫01​fvdxfor every v∈H01​(0,1).​
(31)
Taking compactly supported smooth tests and integrating in the distributional sense shows that this is the Euler-Lagrange equation −(pu′)′+qu=f. Conversely that weak equation gives the displayed stationary condition. The stationary point is actually the unique global minimizer, because
I(v)−I(u)=a(v−u,v−u)≥pmin​∥v−u∥H2​.​
(32)
Equality occurs only when v=u. This proves the variational equivalence, including existence and uniqueness rather than only a formal derivative calculation.
For precision about the literal L2 inner product ⟨Lv,v⟩, use the flux-domain realization of a one-dimensional elliptic operator
D(L)={v∈H01​(0,1):pv′∈H1(0,1)}.
(33)
For such v, weak integration by parts and its zero endpoint values give ⟨Lv,v⟩=a(v,v). On the larger form space H, the same energy is interpreted by the weak operator pairing. The minimizer belongs to D(L) automatically: its weak equation gives (pu′)′=qu−f∈L2, while pu′∈L2. Thus its equation also holds as an equality of L2 functions. This domain does not assume bounded p′, which mere differentiability would not supply. If p∈C1 one may equivalently use D(L)=H2∩H01​, but that stronger coefficient assumption is not needed for the proof here.
