Extend f by zero outside [1,N]∩Z. Here is a dyadic endpoint convention for small Type I and Type II sums. Write DM​=[M,2M)∩Z, with M a power of two. Type I sums are δ-small if, for every M≤N1/100, dyadic K with MK≤N, and arbitrary integer intervals Im​⊂DK​,

N1​​∑m∈DM​​∑n∈DK​​am​bn​f(mn)​≤δ.
(37)
Zero extension handles boxes crossing the endpoint mn=N. Fixed choices of open or closed dyadic endpoints do not matter, but interval uniformity and uniformity in both bounded coefficient sequences do matter. These are the estimates used in the Vinogradov Type I–II method.
An interesting technical step is Fourier separation of interval cutoffs. For an integer interval Im​, let 1Im​​​(θ)=∑r∈Im​​e(−rθ). Discrete Fourier inversion gives
1Im​​(n)=∫01​1Im​​​(θ)e(nθ)dθ.
(38)
The exponential geometric sum bound gives a common envelope HN​(θ)≪min(N,∥θ∥−1), with ∫01​HN​≪log(2N). Divide the interval transform by this envelope: at each frequency it is a bounded coefficient depending on m, and e(nθ) is a bounded coefficient depending on n. The Type II sum assumption therefore bounds the interval-cutoff sum by O(δNlogN). Choosing the outer unit phases to turn each inner sum into its modulus extends the Type I sum bound to boxes where both variables are in the Type II range.

The next two terms give Type I sums. The logarithmic term has outer coefficient μ(m), m≤U, and inner factor logn, removed by partial summation at a cost O(logN). In the other term the outer variable is m=cd≤UV with coefficient
ωm​=∑cd=mc≤U,d≤V​​μ(c)Λ(d),∣ωm​∣≤∑d∣m​Λ(d)=logm.

This logarithmic coefficient bound in Vaughan identity avoids a loss from a divisor weight. On boxes with short outer variable use the hypothesis directly; on boxes where both variables are at least N1/100 use the extended bound proved above. Boxes with inner variable less than N1/100 contain at most O(N2/3+1/100) pairs in total and are treated trivially. All remaining inner scales are at most N99/100 whenever the outer scale exceeds N1/100.
For the final convolution, group the variables as
∑m>U,n>Vmn≤N​​μ(m)b(n)f(mn),b(n)=∑d∣nd>V​​Λ(d),0≤b(n)≤logn.
(41)
Both variables lie between constant multiples of N1/3 and N2/3, well inside the Type II range. Divide b(n) by logN, and apply the Type II sum estimate. If the product cutoff is kept explicitly, Fourier separation of interval cutoffs costs only one extra logarithm; zero extension also permits its omission. There are O(log2N) boxes in the dyadic decomposition. The coefficient bound and, where needed, partial summation cost at most one more logarithm. These estimates give the convenient bound
​∑n≤N​Λ(n)f(n)​≪δNlog4N+N2/3+1/100logN+N1/3logN.
(42)
With δ=(logN)−20, each term is o(N), proving the assertion. The grouping of the long term uses the nonnegativity of the Von Mangoldt function; more usual groupings instead control a divisor coefficient by a second-moment truncation. Ben Green's notes, Sections 2.3–2.4 describe that standard alternative.

Every integer interval J⊂[0,N] is a disjoint union of aligned binary blocks with at most two blocks of each length. On such a block the digit concatenation identity and the Thue–Morse Fourier product give the same bound, up to a unit phase. Summing the geometric series proves the uniform interval cancellation for binary digit parity
supθ,J​​∑r∈J​f(r)e(rθ)​≪Nσ.

For a fixed m, the sum ∑n∈Im​​f(mn) is a sum over multiples of m in an integer interval J⊂[1,N]. The additive character orthogonality filter gives
