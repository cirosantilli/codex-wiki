Use q∈k× and the quantum plane convention yx=qxy. In this convention SLq​(2) is the coordinate Hopf algebra of quantum SL2 with the catalog parameter replaced by q−1. It is generated by a,b,c,d, subject to
ba=qab,ca=qac,db=qbd,dc=qcd,bc=cb,da−ad=(q−q−1)bc,ad−q−1bc=1.​
(1)
Equivalently the last determinant relation is da−qbc=1. The quantum determinant convention is essential: these relations use the inverse of the parameter in the catalog's ab=qba convention.
Writing T=(ac​bd​), the comultiplication is matrix multiplication, Δtij​=∑k​tik​⊗tkj​. Explicitly,
ΔaΔc​=a⊗a+b⊗c,=c⊗a+d⊗c,​ΔbΔd​=a⊗b+b⊗d,=c⊗b+d⊗d.​
(2)
The counit is ε(a)=ε(d)=1, ε(b)=ε(c)=0. Extend Δ and ε as unital algebra homomorphisms over a field. Matrix multiplication is associative, giving coassociativity; multiplication by the identity matrix gives the counit laws. The antipode is
S(T)=(d−q−1c​−qba​).​
(3)
For example (TS(T))11​=ad−q−1bc=1 and (S(T)T)11​=da−qbc=1, while the off-diagonal entries vanish by the commutation relations. Thus this is a Hopf algebra, not merely an algebra presentation.

An R-point means a unital algebra homomorphism over a field α:kq​[x,y]→R, as in points of a noncommutative algebra. It is specified by X=α(x) and Y=α(y) satisfying YX=qXY. For R=C and q not a root of unity, q=1, so scalar commutativity gives (1−q)XY=0. Hence the scalar points are the union of the two coordinate axes:
{(z,0):z∈C}∪{(0,w):w∈C}.​
(8)
For matrix-valued points of a noncommutative algebra, put H=Cn and let Hλ​(X)=ker(X−λI)n be a generalized eigenspace. From XY=q−1YX,
(X−q−1λI)nY=q−nY(X−λI)n.
(9)
Therefore YHλ​(X)⊆Hq−1λ​(X), interpreting a missing eigenvalue as the zero space. In particular the linear subspaces VX​=⨁λ=0​Hλ​(X) and H0​(X) are invariant under both matrices. If λ=0, the numbers λ,q−1λ,…,q−nλ are distinct. A nonzero vector Ynv with v∈Hλ​(X) would force all n+1 intervening generalized eigenspaces to be nonzero, impossible in dimension n. Thus YnVX​=0: Y is nilpotent on VX​, although X is invertible there.
Apply the same argument with X,Y exchanged, using XY=q−1YX. The sum VY​ of the nonzero Y generalized eigenspaces is invariant under both matrices, and X is nilpotent on it. Consequently VY​⊆H0​(X). Conversely, since Y is nilpotent on VX​, its nonzero generalized eigenspaces all lie in H0​(X). Decompose the restriction of Y to that invariant space into its nonzero and zero generalized eigenspaces:
H0​(X)=VY​⊕U,U=H0​(X)∩H0​(Y).
(10)
The zero generalized eigenspace of Y is X-invariant by the scaling identity, so U is invariant under both matrices. On U, both matrices have only zero eigenvalues, hence are nilpotent matrices. This proves the spectral decomposition of a quantum-plane point:
Cn=VX​⊕VY​⊕U.​
(11)
Here VX​,VY​ mean sums of generalized eigenspaces, not merely ordinary eigenspaces; that distinction is necessary for non-diagonalizable points.

A cobraided bialgebra is a bialgebra H equipped with a convolution-invertible bilinear form r:H⊗H→k satisfying the following identities, in Sweedler notation:
r(ab,c)r(a,bc)∑r(a(1)​,b(1)​)a(2)​b(2)​r(1,a)​=∑r(a,c(1)​)r(b,c(2)​),=∑r(a(1)​,c)r(a(2)​,b),=∑b(1)​a(1)​r(a(2)​,b(2)​),=r(a,1)=ε(a).​
(21)
Convolution invertibility means there is rˉ with ∑r(a(1)​,b(1)​)rˉ(a(2)​,b(2)​)=ε(a)ε(b), and likewise with r,rˉ interchanged. This is a coquasitriangular structure. On right comodules it induces the braiding
c(v⊗w)=∑w(0)​⊗v(0)​r(v(1)​,w(1)​).
(22)
The third identity makes this a comodule map, the first two give its two hexagon identities, and the convolution inverse gives the inverse braiding.

For an invertible braiding C satisfying the braid equation on V⊗V, begin with the free associative algebra over a field on matrix coefficients tij​, with Δtij​=∑k​tik​⊗tkj​ and ε(tij​)=δij​. Impose precisely the coefficients of
δV⊗V​C=(C⊗1)δV⊗V​,δ(ei​)=∑j​ej​⊗tji​.
(23)
This quotient is the FRT bialgebra, or Faddeev-Reshetikhin-Takhtajan construction. The relations form a coideal because composing two coactions that preserve C again preserves C; the trivial coaction also preserves it, so the counit descends. Universality follows because any such coaction must satisfy exactly these coefficient relations.
For the given C, take t11​=a, t12​=b, t21​=c, t22​=d. Thus δ(e1​)=e1​⊗a+e2​⊗c and δ(e2​)=e1​⊗b+e2​⊗d. The coefficient relations, after cancellation of q−1/2, are
ba=qab, ca=qac, db=qbd, dc=qcd, bc=cb, da−ad=(q−q−1)bc.​
(24)
These are exactly the defining relations of Mq​(2) in the inverse-parameter convention of Question 1, without setting the quantum determinant equal to one. They therefore define mutually inverse algebra homomorphisms: each named generator maps to the corresponding tij​, and conversely. The matrix comultiplication and counit are the same, so the isomorphism is also one of bialgebras.
Here is the requested check of ba=qab directly from C. Apply colinearity to e1​⊗e2​ and compare the coefficient of e1​⊗e1​. On the left, C(e1​⊗e2​)=q−1/2e2​⊗e1​, giving q−1/2ba. On the right, the corresponding term of δ(e1​⊗e2​) is e1​⊗e1​⊗ab, and C(e1​⊗e1​)=q1/2e1​⊗e1​. Hence q−1/2ba=q1/2ab, proving the relation. The other coefficient comparisons give the remaining displayed relations; no additional determinant relation is imposed in an FRT bialgebra.

The root-of-unity specialization requires q=1: we take q to be a primitive cube root of unity. The displayed presentation contains (q−q−1)−1, so substituting q=1 literally is undefined. Its classical U(sl2​) limit instead has simple finite-dimensional modules of arbitrarily large dimension. This is why the primitive-root qualification matters.
First consider simple modules occurring in the quantum plane. Here [3]=0, K3=1, and
E3(xrys)=[s][s−1][s−2]xr+3ys−3=0,
(34)
with the analogous formula F3=0. Thus the action factors through the finite-dimensional quotient with E3=F3=0, K3=1, spanned by FiKjEl with 0≤i,j,l<3. Any simple module over this quotient is finite-dimensional, since it is generated by one vector. In a nonzero simple submodule choose a K eigenvector v in kerE. This is possible because E is nilpotent and its kernel is K-invariant. Then the highest-weight string for quantum sl2 shows that
W=⟨v,Fv,F2v⟩
(35)
is invariant under E,K, and F3v=0 makes it F-invariant. Simplicity gives W=V, proving dimV≤3.
The same bound holds even for arbitrary finite-dimensional simple modules over the unrestricted quantum enveloping algebra of sl2. Indeed E3,F3,K3 are central: the string commutation identity has a factor [3]=0, and the K commutation factors satisfy q6=1. By Schur lemma, they act as scalars α,β,κ, with κ=0. The element
Ω=FE+ϕ(K),ϕ(K)=(q−q−1)2qK+q−1K−1​
(36)
is also central. For example [E,FE]=(K−K−1)E/(q−q−1), while [E,ϕ(K)]=[ϕ(q−2K)−ϕ(K)]E=−(K−K−1)E/(q−q−1); the F commutator cancels in the same way. Let Ω act as γ.
If α=0, choose the highest vector v as above. Its three-vector F string is invariant, now using F3v=βv, so again its dimension is at most three. If α=0, choose a K eigenvector v. The space W=⟨v,Ev,E2v⟩ is stable under E,K because E3=α, and it is stable under F because
F=(γ−ϕ(K))E−1,E−1=α−1E2.
(37)
It therefore equals the simple module and has dimension at most three.
Over C this also excludes infinite-dimensional simple modules of the unrestricted algebra. It is a finite module over the central finitely generated commutative algebra over a field Z0​=C[E3,F3,K3,K−3], using the same 27 ordered spanning elements. A simple module M, generated by one vector, is consequently finite over Z0​. Choose a maximal ideal m in its support. Nakayama lemma makes M/mM=0; since mM is a submodule, simplicity gives mM=0. The Hilbert Nullstellensatz gives Z0​/m=C, so M is finite-dimensional and the preceding argument applies. Thus the usual complex, primitive-root interpretation yields
every simple Uq​-module has dimension at most 3.​
(38)

For unital algebras over a field A,B, a measuring coalgebra is a coalgebra C together with a linear map p:C→Homk​(A,B) such that, in Sweedler notation,
p(c)(ab)=∑p(c(1)​)(a)p(c(2)​)(b),p(c)(1A​)=ε(c)1B​.​
(41)
Thus a group-like element acts by a unital algebra homomorphism over a field, while skew-primitive elements give twisted product rules.
The universal measuring coalgebra P(A,B) has a universal measuring map π:P(A,B)→Homk​(A,B) with this property: for every measuring map p from a coalgebra C, there is a unique coalgebra homomorphism f:C→P(A,B) satisfying πf=p. The uniqueness is among coalgebra homomorphisms; π itself need not be an injective map of vector spaces.

(c⊗d)(a)=pC​(c)(pD​(d)(a)).
(45)
Indeed applying both measuring product rules gives the product rule for the tensor comultiplication, and the unit condition follows likewise. Apply universality with C=D=P(A,A): composition induces a coalgebra homomorphism μ:P(A,A)⊗P(A,A)→P(A,A). The identity endomorphism induces the unit k→P(A,A). Associativity and the unit laws follow by uniqueness because both candidate coalgebra homomorphisms evaluate as the same compositions of endomorphisms. Thus P(A,A) is a bialgebra.
The generator actions from Question 4 satisfy all defining relations of the quantum enveloping algebra of sl2, so they define an action of Uq​ on A. Its skew product rules are exactly the rules from ΔE=E⊗K+1⊗E and ΔF=F⊗1+K−1⊗F. Products of elements satisfying the module algebra rule satisfy it as well: apply the two successive actions and use that Δ is multiplicative. Since the generators and unit satisfy it, the full action p:Uq​→Endk​(A) is a measuring of A into itself.
Universality therefore gives a unique coalgebra homomorphism ρ:Uq​→P(A,A) inducing this action. To prove multiplicativity, compare the two coalgebra homomorphisms from Uq​⊗Uq​ given by ρμUq​​ and μP​(ρ⊗ρ). Both induce (u⊗v)(a)=u(va), so uniqueness makes them equal. The unit comparison is identical. Consequently
ρ:Uq​⟶P(kq​[x,y],kq​[x,y]) is a bialgebra homomorphism.​
(46)
This avoids assuming the universal measuring map is injective: equality is justified by its universal property for coalgebra maps.

The tangle category has objects finite words in the two orientations ↑,↓, including the empty word. A morphism is an oriented compact one-dimensional manifold, embedded in a three-dimensional slab, whose boundary is the prescribed ordered set of top and bottom endpoints. Closed components are allowed. Two such embeddings represent the same morphism when they are ambient-isotopic relative to the boundary.
Read diagrams from top to bottom here, matching the maps drawn in part (d). Composition stacks diagrams with exactly matching intermediate boundary words; horizontal juxtaposition is the tensor product. The empty word is its unit, and straight oriented strands are identities. Crossings, the two oriented evaluations and the two oriented coevaluations generate all morphisms: place an embedding in generic position and slice between its crossings and local extrema. The Reidemeister moves and the cup/cap isotopies describe changes of diagrams without changing the underlying tangle. This is the ordinary unframed version; a framed tangle category instead retains framing data and has a modified first-move rule.
In this reading convention an arch ∩ is a coevaluation morphism ∅→↑↓, and a valley ∪ is an evaluation morphism ↑↓→∅. An overleft arrow reverses these strand orientations. We use X+​ for the crossing whose upper-left-to-lower-right strand passes over the other, and X−​ for its inverse; this geometric specification avoids ambiguity in sign conventions.
