The fixed-size condition cannot simply be dropped. For k=n−1 and p=1−(logn)/n2,
μn​=n(1−n2logn​)(n−1)(n−2)/2∼n​⟶∞.
(19)
However the complement graph has a binomial number J of missing edges with mean asymptotic to 21​logn and variance at most that mean. By the Chebyshev inequality, P(J≥2)→1. Conditional on J≥2, choose two distinct missing edges uniformly; their endpoints are disjoint except with probability
(2n​)−12(n−2)​=O(n−1).
(20)
Thus with probability tending to one the complement has two disjoint edges. Deleting one vertex cannot remove both, so no (n−1)-clique exists. In this example P(X≥1)→0, despite μn​→∞. This is growing clique size can invalidate a first-moment appearance criterion; the exact second-moment identity remains valid for growing k, but the stated conclusion needs the conventional fixed-k quantifier.
