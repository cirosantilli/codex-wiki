The scalar process has zero mean, because white noise has zero mean. Expanding products and retaining only matching noise indices gives its autocovariance function:
γ(h)=⎩⎨⎧​σ2(1+θ2),σ2θ,0,​h=0,h=±1,∣h∣>1.​​
(7)
The finite second moment and covariance depending only on the lag prove weak stationarity. With the two-sided angular-frequency convention, the spectral density of a stationary process is
f(λ)=2π1​h∑​γ(h)e−ihλ=2πσ2​(1+θ2+2θcosλ),−π≤λ≤π.​
(8)
It is nonnegative because the bracket is ∣1+θeiλ∣2, and it is even because the cosine is even. Hence f(−λ)=f(λ). No restriction on the real parameter θ is needed for stationarity here; invertibility is a separate condition.
For the bivariate calculation let a=σu2​, b=σv2​, D=diag(a,b), and Θ=(θij​). Uncorrelated white noise processes here means zero cross-covariances at every pair of times. Therefore the vector noise has
E[Wt​Wt+hT​]={D,0,​h=0,h=0.​​
(9)
The filtered vector is the multivariate moving-average model Zt​=Wt​+ΘWt−1​. Linearity gives EXt​=EYt​=0. Put Γ(h)=E[Zt​Zt+hT​], with exactly the orientation of the printed covariance definition. At lag zero, the present and past noise terms are uncorrelated, giving D+ΘDΘT. At lag one the only shared noise is Wt​, contributing DΘT; at lag minus one it contributes ΘD. Thus the covariance and cross-spectrum of a vector MA(1) are determined by
Γ(h)=⎩⎨⎧​D+ΘDΘT,DΘT,ΘD,0,​h=0,h=1,h=−1,∣h∣>1.​​
(10)
Explicitly,
Γ(0)Γ(1)​=(a(1+θ112​)+bθ122​aθ11​θ21​+bθ12​θ22​​aθ11​θ21​+bθ12​θ22​b(1+θ222​)+aθ212​​),=(aθ11​bθ12​​aθ21​bθ22​​),Γ(−1)=(aθ11​aθ21​​bθ12​bθ22​​).​
(11)
In particular the off-diagonal positive- and negative-lag covariances need not coincide. Reading the upper-right entries and taking their Fourier series gives the requested cross-spectrum:
fXY​(λ)=2πaθ11​θ21​+bθ12​θ22​+aθ21​e−iλ+bθ12​eiλ​.​
(12)
Every coefficient is real, so replacing λ by −λ conjugates both complex exponentials. Therefore fXY​(−λ)=fXY​(λ)​. Unlike the scalar auto-spectrum, this cross-spectrum may be complex and need not be even: its imaginary part is (bθ12​−aθ21​)sinλ/(2π).
