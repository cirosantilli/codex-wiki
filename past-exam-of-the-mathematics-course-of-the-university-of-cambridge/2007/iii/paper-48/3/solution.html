For 0<u<1 define the generalized inverse, or quantile function, by
Q(u)=inf{x∈R:F(x)≥u}.
(13)
It is finite because the cumulative distribution function tends to zero and one at the two infinities. Monotonicity and right continuity of F give the key equivalence
Q(u)≤x⟺u≤F(x).
(14)
For the forward implication, choose points in the defining set approaching its infimum from above. Right continuity gives F(Q(u))≥u, and then monotonicity gives F(x)≥u whenever Q(u)≤x. The reverse implication follows immediately because x then belongs to the defining set. Consequently, for a continuous uniform distribution draw U,
P(Q(U)≤x)=P(U≤F(x))=F(x).​
(15)
This proves inverse transform sampling for discontinuous as well as continuous distribution functions.
There is a minor endpoint defect in the printed codomain. The true quantile endpoint at probability one is Q(1)=inf{x:F(x)=1}, which equals +∞ for an unbounded-above normal distribution. Thus it need not be real at one. Use the extended-real value there, or assign an arbitrary finite value at this single probability level. Since P(U=1)=0, either choice gives the same sampled distribution; the latter supplies a real-valued version on all of (0,1]. The endpoint has no effect on the inverse-transform law.
For rejection sampling, choose a proposal probability density function g that can be sampled and a finite constant M with f(y)≤Mg(y) wherever the target has positive mass. Generate independent pairs (Yj​,Uj​), with Yj​ of density g and Uj​ uniform. Accept Yj​ if Uj​≤f(Yj​)/(Mg(Yj​)); otherwise discard the pair and repeat with fresh independent draws. At points where both densities vanish the acceptance rule may be defined arbitrarily. For any measurable set A,
P(Yj​∈A, acceptance)=∫A​g(y)Mg(y)f(y)​dy=M1​∫A​f(y)dy.
(16)
Thus one trial accepts with probability 1/M, and the conditional density given acceptance is f. More explicitly, summing over the index of the first accepted proposal gives
P(output∈A)=∑j≥1​(1−1/M)j−1M1​∫A​f(y)dy=∫A​f(y)dy.
(17)
The probability of never accepting is zero, since M<∞, and the expected number of proposals is M. The algorithm terminates almost surely and samples the target density exactly.
