Introduce a categorical latent variable Zi​ for each observation, with P(Zi​=j)=αj​, and let zij​=1{Zi​=j}​. Conditional on Zi​=j, Xi​ has a normal distribution of mean μj​ and the shared variance σ2. The complete-data log-likelihood function is
ℓc​(θ;x,z)=∑i=1N​∑j=1k​zij​logαj​−2N​log(2πσ2)−2σ21​∑i=1N​∑j=1k​zij​(xi​−μj​)2.
(44)
Initialize finite means, positive weights summing to one, and a positive common variance. At iteration r, the Bayes' theorem gives the E-step mixture responsibilities
rij​=P(Zi​=j∣xi​,θ(r))=∑ℓ=1k​αℓ(r)​exp[−(xi​−μℓ(r)​)2/(2σ2(r))]αj(r)​exp[−(xi​−μj(r)​)2/(2σ2(r))]​.​
(45)
The common normal-density factor cancels. The observations' conditional labels are independent, and replacing zij​ by their conditional expectations rij​ gives
Q(θ∣θ(r))=∑i,j​rij​logαj​−2N​logσ2−2σ21​∑i,j​rij​(xi​−μj​)2+constant.
(46)
Set nj​=∑i​rij​; because ∑j​rij​=1, ∑j​nj​=N. The constrained weight maximization uses a Lagrange multiplier η: differentiating ∑j​nj​logαj​−η(∑j​αj​−1) gives αj​=nj​/η, and summing fixes η=N. Differentiating the residual quadratic in each mean gives ∑i​rij​(xi​−μj​)=0. Hence the first two M-step updates are
αj(r+1)​=Nnj​​,μj(r+1)​=nj​∑i​rij​xi​​.​
(47)
With these new means define R=∑i,j​rij​(xi​−μj(r+1)​)2. The remaining objective in u=σ2 is −Nlogu/2−R/(2u), whose derivative is −N/(2u)+R/(2u2). For R>0 it increases up to u=R/N and decreases afterwards. Thus the shared-variance update is
σ2(r+1)=N1​i=1∑N​j=1∑k​rij​(xi​−μj(r+1)​)2.​
(48)
The denominator is N, not N−k and not a separate count for each component: this is maximum likelihood estimation with one common variance. These are the EM for Gaussian mixtures with a common variance updates. Recompute responsibilities with the new parameters and repeat until the observed-data likelihood changes sufficiently little.
The monotonicity claim also follows directly. For positive old weights, the likelihood ratio for observation i is
∑j​αj(r)​f(xi​;μj(r)​,σ2(r))∑j​αj′​f(xi​;μj′​,σ′2)​=∑j​rij​αj(r)​f(xi​;μj(r)​,σ2(r))αj′​f(xi​;μj′​,σ′2)​.
(49)
Apply the Jensen inequality to the logarithm and sum over observations. This yields
ℓ(θ′)−ℓ(θ(r))≥Q(θ′∣θ(r))−Q(θ(r)∣θ(r))≥0
(50)
for the maximizing M step. This proves likelihood increase, not convergence to the global maximum. Use several initializations to reduce sensitivity to local maxima. Compute responsibilities with a log-sum-exp function normalization to avoid numerical underflow.
If nj​=0, that component's updated weight is zero and its mean is unidentified; retain its old mean or remove the empty component. Strictly positive interior initialization avoids this case in exact arithmetic while the variance stays positive. If R=0, there is no positive finite variance maximizing this M step: the objective tends to infinity as σ2↓0. Such boundary degeneracy needs a variance constraint or regularization, as discussed in the next solution. For k=1 the formulas reduce to the ordinary sample mean and the maximum-likelihood variance N−1∑i​(xi​−x)2.
