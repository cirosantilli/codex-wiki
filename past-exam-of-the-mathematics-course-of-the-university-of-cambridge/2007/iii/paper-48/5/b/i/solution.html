Write Sm​=∑i=1m​xi​ and S=SN​. Use shape-rate notation for the Gamma distribution, as in the authoritative PDF: shape a and rate b give a density proportional to za−1e−bz. Conditional independence of the Poisson distributions gives a likelihood function proportional to

The product of factorial terms depends only on the data and can be absorbed into the normalizing constant. Multiplying by the two independent Gamma distribution prior kernels and the uniform prior on the possible indices gives the Poisson count change-point model with gamma priors:
π(λ,ϕ,m∣x)∝λα+Sm​−1e−(β+m)λϕγ+S−Sm​−1e−(δ+N−m)ϕ1{λ>0,ϕ>0, m∈{1,…,N}}​.​
(35)
Because the hyperparameters are positive, each possible index gives finite positive gamma integrals, and summing over the finitely many indices yields a proper posterior distribution. At m=N the second likelihood factor is one, so the posterior law of its unused rate ϕ remains its prior Gamma(γ,δ).
