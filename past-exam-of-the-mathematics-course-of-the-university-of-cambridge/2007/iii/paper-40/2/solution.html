An integer program for the travelling salesman problem must exclude disconnected cycle covers, not merely require one incoming and one outgoing edge. Here is a compact order formulation of the travelling salesman problem. The one-city case is trivial. For n≥2, use binary variables xij​ for directed edges, i=j, and integer variables ui​ for i=2,…,n. Minimize ∑i=j​dij​xij​ subject to
j=i∑​xij​=1,j=i∑​xji​=1(1≤i≤n),0≤xij​≤1,1≤ui​≤n−1,ui​−uj​+(n−1)xij​≤n−2(2≤i=j≤n).​
(9)
The degree equations give a collection of disjoint directed cycles. Along any selected edge between nondistinguished cities, the last inequality gives uj​≥ui​+1. Summing around a cycle avoiding city 1 would yield 0≥k for its positive length k. Thus every cycle must contain city 1, so there is exactly one tour. Conversely, number the cities after city 1 in tour order, giving ui​∈{1,…,n−1}. Selected edges satisfy the order inequalities. Unselected edges impose no additional restriction, since ui​−uj​≤n−2. This proves equivalence with tours, including symmetric-distance instances represented by directed variables.
There are O(n2) variables and rows. For the bit count, store only nonzero coefficients and their indices. The degree equations have O(n2) entries in total; each order inequality has three entries, so these too have O(n2) entries. Indices and the coefficients bounded by n need O(logn) bits each. The objective has O(n2) distance coefficients, each needing at most n+1 bits because the PDF's bound is 2n. Therefore
sparse ILP encoding length=O(n3).​
(10)
Writing an unnecessarily dense constraint matrix would give a larger encoding; it is the sparse explicit encoding that establishes the requested bound. For the decision version add ∑dij​xij​≤B. A threshold can be capped at n2n, so its relevant binary length is O(n+logn); negative thresholds can be rejected immediately for nonnegative distances.
A decision problem belongs to NP if its yes-instances have certificates of polynomial length checkable in deterministic polynomial time. It is NP-complete if it belongs to NP and every NP problem has a polynomial-time many-one reduction to it. The preceding construction is a polynomial-time reduction from travelling-salesman decision to integer-linear feasibility. If the former is NP-complete, the latter is consequently NP-hard.
For completeness we must also establish membership in NP for general integer-linear feasibility, rather than just for the binary instances produced by the reduction. An arbitrary feasible integer assignment could be numerically enormous; what is needed is the existence of a short one. We give a small integer feasibility witness by homogeneous cone decomposition.
Split each unrestricted integer variable into the difference of two nonnegative integer variables and add nonnegative integer slacks to inequalities. This gives Bz=b, z≥0, with N variables and integer data bounded in absolute value by M≥1. Both N and logM are polynomially bounded by the original input length. Consider the homogeneous cone
K={(u,t)≥0:Bu=bt}⊆RN+1.
(11)
Every vector in this cone is a nonnegative combination of at most N+1 support-minimal rays. To prove this, choose a nonnegative kernel vector h of minimal nonempty support contained in the support of a given vector, subtract λh with λ the smallest positive coordinate ratio, and repeat. Each subtraction strictly reduces support. On a minimal support the kernel is one-dimensional: an independent kernel direction would let us perturb a positive vector to the boundary of its support without making it zero, contradicting minimality. Cofactors of independent rows therefore give an integer nonnegative generator of each such ray. Every coordinate is bounded by
D=(N+1)!MN+1,
(12)
using the elementary determinant bound. This proves the needed support-minimal rays of a nonnegative kernel facts directly.
Start with any integer feasible z and decompose (z,1) in this way. A ray whose last coordinate is a positive integer q normalizes to a point v with Bv=b and 0≤vi​≤D; the coefficients of these normalized points sum to one. Rays with last coordinate zero have integer nonnegative first coordinates rj​ with Brj​=0 and coordinates at most D. Hence
z=∑k​λk​vk​+∑j​tj​rj​,λk​≥0,∑k​λk​=1,tj​≥0,
(13)
with at most N+1 ray terms. Subtract ∑j​⌊tj​⌋rj​ from z. The result z′ is still integer, nonnegative and feasible, and satisfies
0≤zi′​≤D+(N+1)D=(N+2)D.
(14)
Its binary length is polynomial, since logD=O(NlogN+NlogM). The assignment can be checked by exact integer arithmetic in polynomial time. Thus general integer-linear feasibility is in NP. Combining this with the reduction proves
TSP decision NP-complete ⟹ ILP decision NP-complete.​
(15)
