Past exam of the mathematics course of the University of Cambridge / 2007 / iii / Paper 40 / 1 / Solution

Introduce nonnegative slack variables s1​=2+x1​−x2​, s2​=8−x1​−2x2​ and s3​=8−2x1​−x2​, and write the objective as Z. With x1​,x2​ nonbasic, the initial simplex dictionary is
s1​s3​​=2+x1​−x2​,=8−2x1​−x2​,​s2​Z​=8−x1​−2x2​,=x1​+x2​.​
(1)
This is feasible at the origin. Enter x1​. The simplex ratio test selects s3​ to leave, since its bound is x1​≤4, compared with x1​≤8 from s2​. Pivoting gives
x1​s1​s2​Z​=4−21​x2​−21​s3​,=6−23​x2​−21​s3​,=4−23​x2​+21​s3​,=4+21​x2​−21​s3​.​
(2)
Now enter x2​. The ratio test chooses s2​ to leave: its bound 8/3 is smaller than 4 from s1​ and 8 from x1​. The resulting dictionary is
x1​x2​s1​Z​=38​+31​s2​−32​s3​,=38​−32​s2​+31​s3​,=2+s2​−s3​,=316​−31​s2​−31​s3​.​
(3)
All basic values are nonnegative and all nonbasic objective coefficients are nonpositive. The simplex algorithm therefore certifies
x1​=x2​=38​,ZLP​=316​.​
(4)
The negative objective coefficients also make this continuous optimum unique.
For integer x1​,x2​, all three original slacks are integers. Apply a Gomory fractional cut to the row
x1​−31​s2​+32​s3​=38​.
(5)
The fractional part of −1/3 is 2/3, so the cut is 32​s2​+32​s3​≥32​, or s2​+s3​≥1. To justify it directly, rewrite the row as
(x1​−s2​)+32​(s2​+s3​)=38​.
(6)
The parenthesized term is an integer and is at most 8/3, since the other term is nonnegative. It is therefore at most 2, proving the cut. This retains every feasible integer solution and excludes the current fractional optimum. Since s2​+s3​=16−3(x1​+x2​), the cut is equivalently x1​+x2​≤5.
Introduce the integer-valued nonnegative slack t=s2​+s3​−1. Its dictionary row is t=−1+s2​+s3​, so primal feasibility fails while the objective row remains dual feasible. In the dual simplex algorithm, t leaves; s2​,s3​ tie in the dual ratio test, each with ratio 1/3. Choose s2​ to enter. Substituting s2​=1+t−s3​ gives
x1​s1​Z​=3+31​t−s3​,=3+t−2s3​,=5−31​t.​x2​s2​​=2−32​t+s3​,=1+t−s3​,​
(7)
This dictionary is feasible at t=s3​=0 and certifies the cut relaxation's optimum Z=5, achieved at the integer point (3,2).
To find every optimal integer point, the objective row forces t=0. Nonnegativity of all the remaining rows then reduces to 0≤s3​≤1. On this optimal edge, (x1​,x2​)=(3−s3​,2+s3​). Since s3​ must be integral, only its two endpoints are possible:
(x1​,x2​)=(3,2) or (2,3),ZIP​=5.​
(8)
