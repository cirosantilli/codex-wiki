Use geometrized units with ℏ=kB​=1 initially, and write f(r)=1−2M/r, h(r)=1−Q2/(Mr). The given nonextremality assumption makes hH​=h(2M)=1−Q2/(2M2)>0. Thus the static Killing vector field has a simple zero of its norm at
rH​=2M.​
(11)
The exterior r>2M is static. For radial null geodesics, dt/dr=±1/f, independently of h. With v=t+∫dr/f, the metric is
ds2=−hf​dv2+h2​dvdr+r2dΩ2,
(12)
which is regular at rH​ since hH​>0. The outgoing null equation is dr/dv=f/2. Inside the future horizon these rays move to smaller radius, while outside they can escape to infinity. Hence the zero is the event horizon. The inner zero of h is not another regular Killing horizon: the Killing norm there diverges rather than vanishes. The strict charge bound matters when making the regular extension.
For the Euclidean black-hole regularity condition, continue t=itE​, consistent with the sign of the given quantum time-translation operator. Set x=r−2M. To leading order f=x/(2M) and h=hH​, so the radial–time part of the Euclidean metric is
dsE2​=2MhH​x​dtE2​+hH​x2M​dx2.
(13)
Introduce the radial proper length coordinate ρ by x=hH​ρ2/(8M). Then
dsE2​=dρ2+16M2ρ2​dtE2​+(2M)2dΩ2+O(ρ2).
(14)
The first two terms are a polar plane with angle tE​/(4M). Smoothness at its origin requires
ΔtE​=8πM.​
(15)
A different period would make a conical singularity. The time coordinate is normalized to proper time at infinity, so no further asymptotic redshift factor is needed. The cancellation of hH​ proves the charge independence of the Euclidean horizon period under a regular radial conformal factor; this argument would fail at hH​=0.
To justify the thermal interpretation, analytic continuation of the time-evolution operator U(t)=eiHt gives U(itE​)=e−HtE​. Its matrix element is an imaginary-time propagation kernel. Identifying the initial and final configurations and integrating over them takes its trace, giving the Euclidean path integral Z=Tre−βH. In an energy eigenbasis each state has weight e−βE, exactly the Boltzmann factor of a canonical ensemble. Thus the geometrically required period is the inverse Hawking temperature. With dimensions restored, an imaginary time period ΔtE​ gives β=ΔtE​/ℏ=1/(kB​T). Consequently
TH​=8πM1​in natural units,TH​=8πGkB​Mℏc3​(Q=0).​
(16)
Here M is mass in ordinary units; for a neutral hole M=GM/c2 and the physical imaginary-time period is 8πGM/c3. Bosonic thermal fields are periodic and fermionic fields antiperiodic over that same Euclidean period. The relation to the canonical density operator, not just dimensional reasoning, identifies the temperature.
For an evaporation estimate take a neutral, isolated Schwarzschild black hole and approximate its radiated power with the Stefan–Boltzmann law. Its area is A=16πG2M2/c4, and the photon Stefan-Boltzmann constant is σSB​=π2kB4​/(60ℏ3c2). Substituting the temperature gives
P≃AσSB​TH4​=15360πG2M2ℏc6​,dtdM​≃−15360πG2M2ℏc4​.
(17)
The black-hole evaporation speeds up as the mass decreases. Integrating, rather than dividing the initial rest energy by the initial power, gives
tevap​≃ℏc45120πG2M03​​≃2.1×1067 years(M0​=2×1030 kg).​
(18)
The corresponding initial Hawking temperature is about 6×10−8K. This is the Stefan-Boltzmann estimate of Schwarzschild evaporation time at the level of accuracy requested. Greybody factors and other emitted species alter the numerical coefficient, but not the leading cubic scaling of a large isolated hole. The estimate assumes negligible accretion or ambient radiation absorption; a stellar-mass hole in the present cosmic radiation bath is not currently losing mass at that isolated-hole rate. Finally the semiclassical formula does not describe the Planck-scale endpoint: extrapolating it to zero mass is an estimate of the macroscopic lifetime, not a proof of what the final quantum object is.
