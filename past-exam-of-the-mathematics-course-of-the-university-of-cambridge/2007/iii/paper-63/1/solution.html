An orthonormal coframe is a collection of independent differential forms Ea^=ea^μ​dxμ satisfying gμν​=ηa^b^​ea^μ​eb^ν​, with η=diag(−1,1,1,1). Locally one can diagonalize the metric tensor and rescale the resulting covectors, or use Gram-Schmidt orthogonalization for a symmetric bilinear form, choosing a timelike direction first. The coframe is not unique: a local Lorentz transformation preserves the metric. For the Levi-Civita connection, solve Cartan's first structure equation and metric compatibility together:
dEa^+ωa^b^​∧Eb^=0,ωa^b^​=ηa^c^​ωc^b^​=−ωb^a^​.
(1)
These determine the connection 1-forms. The antisymmetry applies to the lowered pair of indices; a mixed time-space pair instead has ω0i​=ωi0​. Next Cartan's second structure equation gives the curvature 2-forms, and reading off their coefficients determines the Riemann curvature tensor:
Ωa^b^​=dωa^b^​+ωa^c^​∧ωc^b^​=21​Ra^b^c^d^​Ec^∧Ed^,Rb^d^​=Ra^b^a^d^​.
(2)
This also states the curvature-sign convention. Contracting gives the Ricci tensor, and its coordinate components are Rμν​=ea^μ​eb^ν​Ra^b^​.
On a static patch with V>0, choose
E0=V​dt,E1=V​dr​,E2=rdθ,E3=rsinθdϕ.
(3)
Write h=V​, a=h′, b=h/r, and c=cotθ/r. The exterior derivative gives
dE0=−aE0∧E1,dE1=0,dE2=bE1∧E2,dE3=bE1∧E3+cE2∧E3.
(4)
The nonzero independent connection 1-forms are consequently
ω01​=aE0,ω12​=−bE2,ω13​=−bE3,ω23​=−cE3,
(5)
with ω10​=ω01​, spatial mixed pairs antisymmetric, and the remaining forms zero. For example,
Ω01​=d(aE0)=−(hh′′+h′2)E0∧E1=−2V′′​E0∧E1.
(6)
Similarly Ω02​=ω01​∧ω12​=−abE0∧E2, and the angular derivatives in dω13​ cancel against ω12​∧ω23​. The resulting Cartan curvature forms for a static spherical metric are
Ω01​Ω02​Ω12​Ω23​​=−2V′′​E0∧E1,=−2rV′​E0∧E2,=−2rV′​E1∧E2,=r21−V​E2∧E3.​Ω03​Ω13​​=−2rV′​E0∧E3,=−2rV′​E1∧E3,​​
(7)
For the last entry, d(−cE3)=E2∧E3/r2 and ω21​∧ω13​=−VE2∧E3/r2. The remaining curvature 2-forms follow from Ωab​=−Ωba​.
Put A=V′′/2, B=V′/(2r), and C=(1−V)/r2. Reading the Riemann curvature tensor from the forms and contracting gives the diagonal orthonormal Ricci tensor
Ra^b^​=diag(A+2B,−A−2B,C−2B,C−2B).​
(8)
For example R00​=R1010​+R2020​+R3030​=A+2B, while R22​=−B−B+C. All off-diagonal entries vanish because the curvature 2-forms only involve their own index planes. In the coordinate basis the nonzero components are
Rtt​Rθθ​​=V(2V′′​+rV′​),=1−V−rV′,​Rrr​Rϕϕ​​=−V1​(2V′′​+rV′​),=(1−V−rV′)sin2θ.​​
(9)
The angular Einstein field equations with the negative cosmological constant give (rV)′=1+3r2/l2. Integrating introduces one real constant, conventionally written −2m:
V(r)=1−r2m​+l2r2​.​
(10)
Substitution gives V′′/2+V′/r=3/l2, so the time and radial Einstein field equations hold as well. The solution is the Schwarzschild--anti-de Sitter black hole family, including its zero and negative mass limits.
For m>0, V increases strictly from −∞ at r=0 to +∞ at infinity. Its unique positive simple zero rh​ satisfies rh3​+l2rh​=2ml2. This zero is a coordinate singularity, not a curvature singularity: with the ingoing coordinate v=t+∫dr/V, the radial metric is −Vdv2+2dvdr and is regular there. Inside the future black-hole region, ∇r=∂v​+V∂r​ is future timelike, so every future-directed causal trajectory decreases r and cannot return to the asymptotic anti-de Sitter boundary. Conversely, every exterior radius admits an outward radial null geodesic reaching that boundary, since ∫r∞​dr/V converges. Thus m>0 gives an event horizon at the unique positive zero of V. Here the event horizon is defined relative to the asymptotic timelike boundary of Anti-de Sitter spacetime. For m=0, V=1+r2/l2>0 and the spacetime is pure Anti-de Sitter spacetime, with no black-hole event horizon. For m<0, V=1+2∣m∣/r+r2/l2>0, so there is also no event horizon; the mass term produces a naked singularity at r=0. The field equation itself does not impose a positive mass integration constant.
