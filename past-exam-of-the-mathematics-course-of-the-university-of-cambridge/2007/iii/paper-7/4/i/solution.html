Past exam of the mathematics course of the University of Cambridge / 2007 / iii / Paper 7 / 4 / i / Solution

First determine when a group automorphism can carry a transposition to another type of involution. An involution in Sn​ is a product of k disjoint transpositions, and its conjugacy class has size
2kk!(n−2k)!n!​.
(30)
An automorphism preserves element orders and conjugacy-class sizes. Equality with the transposition-class size n(n−1)/2 requires
(n−2k)!(n−2)!​=2k−1k!.
(31)
For k=2 this says (n−2)(n−3)=4, which has no integral solution n≥4. For k=3, the minimum value of the left side, at n=6, is 4!=24=22⋅3!; it increases strictly for larger n. For k≥4, even its minimum (2k−2)! exceeds 2k−1k!: at k=4 this is 720>192, and the ratio of these two quantities increases at the next step by (2k)(2k−1)/(2(k+1))>1. Thus only in degree six can the transposition class be exchanged with a different involution class, namely the triple transpositions.
We next prove that any automorphism preserving transpositions is an inner automorphism. Regard a transposition as an edge of the complete graph on the n letters. Two distinct transpositions fail to commute exactly when their edges meet. Any family of pairwise intersecting edges is contained in a vertex star or a triangle: after choosing {a,b} and {a,c}, an edge not containing a must be {b,c}, which precludes any fourth edge. For n≥5, the stars are therefore distinguished as the maximal such families of size n−1≥4. An automorphism permutes stars, inducing a permutation π of the letters; their pairwise intersections show that (i j) is sent to (π(i) π(j)). Since transpositions generate Sn​, this is conjugation by a permutation.
For n=4, stars and triangles both have three edges, but a star generates S4​ while a triangle generates a subgroup S3​. An automorphism preserves the order of the generated subgroup and hence still distinguishes stars. For n=3, every permutation of the three transpositions is induced by conjugation in S3​, since its conjugation action on them is faithful and has order six. The groups S1​,S2​ have trivial automorphism groups. This completes the small cases.
It follows that all automorphisms for n=6 are inner. In degree six, an automorphism permutes the two involution classes of size 15; the kernel of this action consists precisely of the inner automorphisms. Therefore the automorphisms of the symmetric group satisfy
Out(Sn​)=1 (n=6),∣Out(S6​)∣≤2.​
(32)
