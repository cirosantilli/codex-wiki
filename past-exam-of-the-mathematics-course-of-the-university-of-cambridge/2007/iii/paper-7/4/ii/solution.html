Past exam of the mathematics course of the University of Cambridge / 2007 / iii / Paper 7 / 4 / ii / Solution

 ... Past exam of the mathematics course of the University of Cambridge 2007 iii Paper 7 4 ii
OurBigBook.com Words: 336
A duad is an unordered pair of letters, a syntheme is a partition of the six letters into three duads, and a total of synthemes is a set of five synthemes partitioning the 15 duads. There are 6!/(23⋅3!)=15 synthemes. Fix one, M. Each of its three duads lies in three synthemes, and two distinct duads of M can occur together only in M itself. Thus precisely 1+3⋅2=7 synthemes meet M in a duad, leaving eight disjoint from it.
For any syntheme M′ disjoint from M, their union as edges in K6​ is a six-cycle: the two perfect matchings alternate, and no two-cycle is possible in a simple graph. Its complement is a triangular prism, namely two triangles joined by three corresponding edges. The prism has four perfect matchings. One consists of all three joining edges; each of the other three consists of one joining edge and the remaining edge in each triangle. To partition the prism into three perfect matchings, one cannot use the all-joining matching, since what remains is two odd triangles. The other three do partition it. Hence two disjoint synthemes extend to a unique synthematic total.
There are eight choices for M′ and four choices of M′ within each synthematic total containing M. Thus M lies in exactly two synthematic totals. Counting incidences of synthemes and synthematic totals now gives the counting synthematic totals formula
#{totals}=515⋅2​=6.​
(33)
Two distinct synthematic totals share at most one syntheme, since sharing two would force them to be the unique synthematic total extending that pair. Each syntheme gives a pair of synthematic totals containing it, and the 15 synthemes account for all (26​)=15 pairs. Therefore every pair of synthematic totals shares exactly one syntheme.
This also explains why the action on the six synthematic totals defines an automorphism of S6​. A permutation fixing all synthematic totals fixes their pairwise intersections, hence every syntheme. Each duad is the intersection of two distinct synthemes containing it, so every duad is then fixed. Intersecting two duads through a point shows that every point is fixed. The action is faithful and, by equality of orders, maps S6​ onto the full symmetric group of the synthematic totals. Part iii shows that a transposition acts as a triple transposition, establishing that this is an outer automorphism.
