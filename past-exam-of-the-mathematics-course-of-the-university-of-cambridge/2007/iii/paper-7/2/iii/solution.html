Here is an involution form of the double-coset criterion for a one-point extension. Embed G in Sym(Ω∪{∞}) by fixing ∞, choose a∈Ω, and put H=Ga​. The necessary and sufficient condition is the existence of an involution t such that
t(∞)=a,t(a)=∞,tHt=H,tgt∈GtG for every g∈G∖H.​
(18)
Products in this part are compositions of maps, with the rightmost map applied first. The criterion is a finite test on permutations of the enlarged set; it does not presuppose that a larger group has already been found.
For sufficiency, put K=G∪GtG. Multiplying a double coset by an element of G on either side leaves it unchanged. A product of two elements of GtG reduces to g1​tgtg2​. If g∈H, then tgt∈H≤G; otherwise the last condition puts it in GtG. Hence K is closed under multiplication. It contains the identity and is closed under inverses, since t−1=t, so it is a group.
Every element g1​tg2​ sends ∞ to g1​(a)∈Ω, whereas elements of G fix ∞. Thus K∞​=G. Moreover the orbit of ∞ contains a and then all of Ω, so K is transitive and is the desired one-point extension of a permutation group. For the order check, G∩tGt=H: an element of the intersection fixes both ∞ and a, and the reverse containment follows from tHt=H. Consequently ∣GtG∣=∣G∣2/∣H∣=∣Ω∣∣G∣, as required.
For necessity, let K be an extension. It is doubly transitive group action, of order ∣Ω∣(∣Ω∣+1)∣Ga​∣, which is even. By Cauchy theorem for groups it contains a nontrivial involution, and a pair interchanged by this involution can be carried to (∞,a) by double transitivity. Conjugating gives an involution t interchanging these two points. Their common point stabilizer is H, so tHt=H. Finally G has just two orbits on the enlarged set, namely {∞} and Ω, giving K=G∪GtG. More explicitly, for k∈K∖G, choose g∈G with g(a)=k(∞); then tg−1k fixes ∞ and belongs to G. If g∈/H, the element tgt moves ∞, so it belongs to GtG, completing necessity.
