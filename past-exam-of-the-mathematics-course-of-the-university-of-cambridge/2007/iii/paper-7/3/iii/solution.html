Write xij​(a)=I+aEij​ for i=j. For a=0 these are transvections, and for a=0 they are the identity. Left multiplication by xij​(a) adds a times row j to row i. We show that these elementary transvection matrices generate the special linear group.
Apply Gaussian elimination using only row additions. If a pivot is zero, invertibility ensures some lower entry in its column is nonzero; adding that row produces a nonzero pivot without interchanging or scaling rows. Clear entries below each pivot, and then above pivots in reverse order. This reduces any invertible matrix to a diagonal matrix D=diag(d1​,…,dn​), with all di​=0, preserving determinant throughout. For a determinant-one matrix, ∏i​di​=1.
A two-coordinate diagonal block is itself a product of elementary transvection matrices. On those two coordinates put
w(a)=x12​(a)x21​(−a−1)x12​(a)=(0−a−1​a0​),w(a)w(−1)=(a0​0a−1​).

This formula is valid in characteristic two as well. For n≥2, multiply the diagonal blocks with entries di​,di−1​ on coordinates (i,n) for 1≤i<n. Their product is D, because its last entry is ∏i<n​di−1​=dn​. Thus D and the original matrix lie in the subgroup generated by transvections. Conversely every transvection has determinant one by part ii. Hence
⟨T⟩=SL(V).​

For n=1, both the special linear group and the subgroup generated by the degenerate identity transvection are trivial. The argument also establishes transvections generate the special linear group over any field.
