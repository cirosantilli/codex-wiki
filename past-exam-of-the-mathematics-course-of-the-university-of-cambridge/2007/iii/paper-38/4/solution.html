On the coordinatewise ordered configuration space, μ1​ stochastically dominates μ2​ when ∫fdμ1​≥∫fdμ2​ for every increasing real function f. Equivalently, μ1​(A)≥μ2​(A) for every increasing event. The Holley condition is the sufficient cross-lattice inequality
μ1​(ω∨η)μ2​(ω∧η)≥μ1​(ω)μ2​(η)for all ω,η.​
(21)
Here join and meet are coordinatewise maximum and minimum. This condition implies μ1​≥st​μ2​ by the Holley inequality; it is not asserted to be necessary. It can be checked on unnormalized weights, since their normalization constants cancel.
Use the weights
bp​(ω)=po(ω)(1−p)∣E∣−o(ω),rp,q​(ω)=bp​(ω)qk(ω).
(22)
For the upper bound, take μ1​=ϕp,1​ and μ2​=ϕp,q​. Since open-edge count is modular, the Bernoulli factors cancel in the Holley ratio, leaving
bp​(ω)rp,q​(η)bp​(ω∨η)rp,q​(ω∧η)​=qk(ω∧η)−k(η)≥1.
(23)
The last inequality uses q≥1 and the fact that removing edges cannot reduce the number of components. Thus ϕp,q​≤st​ϕp,1​.
For the lower bound, let p′=p/[p+q(1−p)]. Its odds satisfy
1−p′p′​=q1​1−pp​.
(24)
Set a=o(ω∨η)−o(ω)=o(η)−o(ω∧η), the number of edges added to ω in the union. For μ1​=ϕp,q​ and μ2​=ϕp′,1​, the corresponding ratio is
rp,q​(ω)bp′​(η)rp,q​(ω∨η)bp′​(ω∧η)​=qa+k(ω∨η)−k(ω)≥1.
(25)
Adding one edge either merges two components or leaves the count unchanged. Therefore adding a edges reduces k by at most a, proving the exponent nonnegative. This also proves the hinted monotonicity of k(ω)+o(ω) rather than assuming it. The Holley condition now proves both Bernoulli bounds for the random-cluster model:
ϕp′,1​≤st​ϕp,q​≤st​ϕp,1​​.
(26)
The same argument holds with wired boundary conditions: identify the wired boundary vertices first and count components on that quotient graph. Taking finite-volume wired limits preserves every increasing cylinder-event inequality. Approximating {0↔∞} by the decreasing events {0↔∂[−n,n]d} consequently gives
θ(p′,1)≤θw(p,q)≤θ(p,1).
(27)
Let a=pc​(1). For p<a, the upper bound makes the wired percolation probability zero, so pc​(q)≥a. If a<1 and p′>a, the lower bound makes it positive. Solving this strict inequality,
p+q(1−p)p​>a⟺p>1+(q−1)aqa​.
(28)
Taking infima proves
pc​(1)≤pc​(q)≤1+(q−1)pc​(1)qpc​(1)​​.
(29)
For a=1, the lower bound and the trivial upper bound pc​(q)≤1 give the same conclusion. No assumption about percolation at the critical parameter is required.
