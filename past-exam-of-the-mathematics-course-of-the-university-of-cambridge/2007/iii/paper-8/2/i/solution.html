Past exam of the mathematics course of the University of Cambridge / 2007 / iii / Paper 8 / 2 / i / Solution

 ... Past exam of the mathematics course of the University of Cambridge 2007 iii Paper 8 2 i
OurBigBook.com Words: 415
Let C=∥T∥. We construct the extension by a deterministic recursion over the given dense list, using the one-dimensional dominated extension of a real linear functional. Neither Zorn's lemma nor any selection of a family of extensions is needed.
Suppose f:W→R is already a linear functional satisfying ∣f(w)∣≤C∥w∥, and v∈/W. An extension to W+Rv must have the form
fv​(w+tv)=f(w)+ta.
(16)
Define
Lv​=supw∈W​(f(w)−C∥w−v∥),Uv​=infw∈W​(C∥w+v∥−f(w)).
(17)
For any w,z∈W, the bound on f and the triangle inequality give
f(w)+f(z)=f(w+z)≤C∥w+z∥≤C∥w−v∥+C∥z+v∥.
(18)
Thus every quantity in the supremum defining Lv​ is at most every quantity in the infimum defining Uv​. Taking w=0 and z=0 gives finite bounds, specifically
−C∥v∥≤Lv​≤Uv​≤C∥v∥.
(19)
The completeness of the real numbers defines these endpoints uniquely. Set a=Lv​; this is an explicit rule, not an arbitrary choice from a nonempty interval.
For t>0, the upper-endpoint inequality with w/t yields f(w)+ta≤C∥w+tv∥. For t<0, write t=−s with s>0 and use the lower-endpoint inequality with w/s to obtain the same bound. For t=0 it is the original bound. Applying the upper bound also to the negative of the vector gives
∣fv​(w+tv)∣≤C∥w+tv∥.
(20)
The representation w+tv is unique because v∈/W, so fv​ is a well-defined bounded linear functional extending f.
Start with W0​=U and f0​=T. At step n, if yn​∈Wn−1​ leave the functional unchanged; otherwise use exactly the supremum-endpoint rule with v=yn​. Put Wn​=U+span{y1​,…,yn​}. This defines compatible functionals fn​ on Wn​ with the common bound C. Their union f∞​ on W∞​=⋃n​Wn​ is linear and bounded by C. The subspace W∞​ is dense in V because it contains every yn​.
To make the final passage to the closure equally free of the axiom of choice, for v∈V let jn​(v) be the least positive integer with ∥yjn​(v)​−v∥<2−n. Density ensures that this integer exists, and leastness specifies it uniquely. Define
T(v)=limn→∞​f∞​(yjn​(v)​).
(21)
The limit exists because
∣f∞​(yjn​(v)​)−f∞​(yjm​(v)​)∣≤C(2−n+2−m).
(22)
Any other approximating sequence in W∞​ gives the same limit, by the same norm bound. Applying this independence to sums and scalar multiples of approximating sequences proves linearity of T. It also shows T=f∞​ on W∞​ and ∣T(v)∣≤C∥v∥. Restriction to U therefore gives
T∣U​=T,∥T∥=∥T∥.​
(23)
The upper bound follows from construction and the lower bound from restriction to U. This also covers C=0, when all extensions are zero. No completeness of V or closedness of U was assumed. The argument establishes the choice-free Hahn-Banach extension in a separable space by uniquely specified operations at every stage.
