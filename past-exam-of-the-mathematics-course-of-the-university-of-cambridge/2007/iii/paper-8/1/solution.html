The Baire category theorem states that, in a nonempty complete metric space, a countable intersection of open dense sets is dense. Equivalently, a nonempty open set cannot be covered by countably many nowhere dense sets.
For the Uniform boundedness principle, let X be a Banach space, Y a normed vector space, and (Tα​) a family of bounded linear operators for which supα​∥Tα​x∥<∞ for every x∈X. Put

We next prove the Silverman-Toeplitz theorem. Write c for the real convergent sequence space with the supremum norm. It is a Banach space: the l-infinity sequence space is complete by coordinatewise passage to limits of norm-Cauchy sequences, and c is closed in it. Indeed, if x(m)→x uniformly and each x(m) converges, approximate x within ε/3 by one x(m) and make that sequence's tail oscillation smaller than ε/3. Then the tail of x is Cauchy and therefore converges in R.
