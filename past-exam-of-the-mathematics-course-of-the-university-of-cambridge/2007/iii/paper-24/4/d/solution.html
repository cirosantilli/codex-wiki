For the intended standard Hopf link, identify S3 with the unit sphere in C2 and its two components with the coordinate circles z1​=0 and z2​=0. Outside small neighborhoods of those circles, both coordinates are nonzero, and their arguments together with ∣z1​∣ give
S3∖intNL​≅T2×I.
(37)
On this product the longitude of either component is the meridian of the other. Therefore the two attaching meridians become the two coordinate curves of T2, intersecting exactly once. Adding the product collar does not change a gluing of solid tori. The meridian-intersection-one argument in Question 2(c) identifies the result with the standard genus-one splitting of the sphere. Thus zero surgery on the Hopf link gives
M≅S3.​
(38)
There is a genuine link-type qualification in the wording: merely specifying two linked but individually unknotted components does not determine the Hopf link. For example the two components of the torus link T(2,4) are unknotted but have linking number 2. Filling their preferred longitudes is zero surgery with linking matrix
(02​20​),
(39)
so the resulting first homology is its cokernel, Z/2⊕Z/2, rather than the zero group of S3. The cokernel formula follows because zero longitudes impose twice the other meridian as the homology relations. Thus the sphere answer applies to the standard once-linked Hopf interpretation; the literal unspecified link description alone is insufficient to force a single manifold.
