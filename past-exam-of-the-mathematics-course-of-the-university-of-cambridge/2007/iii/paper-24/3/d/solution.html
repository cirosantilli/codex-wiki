Past exam of the mathematics course of the University of Cambridge / 2007 / iii / Paper 24 / 3 / d / Solution

 ... Past exam of the mathematics course of the University of Cambridge 2007 iii Paper 24 3 d
OurBigBook.com Words: 205
Use the braid B=(σ1​σ2−1​)2 from part (c). Put meridians a,b,c around its three strands at one end. At a positive crossing, the Wirtinger presentation relations induce the Artin automorphism of a braid
f: a↦aba−1,b↦a,c↦c.
(26)
For the inverse crossing on strands two and three, they give
g: a↦a,b↦c,c↦c−1bc.
(27)
The first formula is the usual crossing conjugation, and the second is its inverse on the relevant pair. Following one block gives h=f∘g:
h(a)=aba−1,h(b)=c,h(c)=c−1ac.
(28)
The four-crossing braid induces h2. Closing it identifies each ending meridian with its starting meridian. Applying the Seifert-van Kampen theorem to the crossing regions and the closing arcs therefore gives
π1​(S3∖K)=⟨a,b,c∣a=h2(a), b=h2(b), c=h2(c)⟩,
(29)
where
h2(a)h2(b)h2(c)​=aba−1cab−1a−1,=c−1ac,=c−1a−1caba−1c−1ac.​
(30)
Both elementary crossing automorphisms preserve abc. Hence h2(abc)=abc, and the first two closure relations imply the third: after substitution abc=abh2(c), so cancellation gives c=h2(c).
The first remaining relation can be solved without adding any relation:
a=aba−1cab−1a−1quad⟺c=ab−1aba−1.
(31)
Substitute this expression into b=c−1ac. Free cancellation and multiplying the two sides by inverse words give
ab−1a−1ba=bab−1a−1b.
(32)
These are Tietze transformations, eliminating a generator defined by one relation and retaining the other. Thus the figure-eight knot group has the requested one-relator presentation
π1​(S3∖K)≅⟨a,b∣ab−1a−1ba=bab−1a−1b⟩.​
(33)
