Past exam of the mathematics course of the University of Cambridge / 2007 / iii / Paper 24 / 1 / b / Solution

 ... Past exam of the mathematics course of the University of Cambridge 2007 iii Paper 24 1 b
OurBigBook.com Words: 345
Take two solid tori V1​,V2​, each with a meridian-longitude basis (μi​,λi​) on its boundary. Use opposite boundary-basis orientations on the two sides, so that a determinant-one coefficient matrix describes an orientation-reversing gluing map. For A∈SL(2,Z) choose a torus homeomorphism f:∂V2​→∂V1​ inducing
f∗​μ2​=−qμ1​+pλ1​,f∗​λ2​=sμ1​+rλ1​,
(3)
and define MA​=V1​∪f​V2​. The linear map on R2/Z2 realizes the matrix. Torus homeomorphisms with the same action on first homology are isotopic, and an isotopy extends over a boundary collar, so the resulting three-manifold depends only on A. Specifying opposite boundary conventions is necessary: gluing two identically oriented boundary bases by a determinant-one map would not describe the usual oriented gluing.
We now identify this gluing directly in the quotient from part (a). Split S3 into the two invariant solid tori W1​={∣z2​∣≤1/2​} and W2​={∣z1​∣≤1/2​}. On W1​ write z1​=1−∣w∣2​e2πix, z2​=w. The quotient coordinates
(u,v)=(e2πipx,e−2πiqxw)
(4)
identify W1​/⟨g⟩ with S1×D2. They are invariant under g; choosing a pth root of u reconstructs (x,w), and different choices are exactly the same cyclic orbit. Thus this is a genuine solid-torus coordinate model.
On the common boundary, measure arguments in turns as (x,y). Its quotient lattice is
L=Z2+Z(1/p,q/p).
(5)
The meridian and longitude of the first quotient torus can be chosen as μ1​=(0,1) and λ1​=(1/p,q/p). They form a lattice basis, since (1,0)=pλ1​−qμ1​. The meridian of the second torus is precisely that (1,0) loop. Consequently
μ2​=−qμ1​+pλ1​.​
(6)
The determinant condition is qr+ps=−1. Put k=−r, so kq≡1(modp). On W2​, the analogous quotient coordinates use e2πipy and e−2πikyz1​, and a longitude is (k/p,1/p). Choose its negative for the second boundary-basis convention. Then
λ2​=(−k/p,−1/p)=sμ1​+rλ1​,
(7)
since ps+qr=−1. Thus the two quotient tori are glued by exactly A. This proves the genus-one gluing model of a lens space and
MA​≅L(p,q).​
(8)
Different choices of r,s satisfying the determinant condition differ by (s,r)↦(s,r)+j(−q,p). This changes f by a meridional Dehn twist on V2​, which extends over V2​: in solid-torus coordinates it is (z,eiθ)↦(eijθz,eiθ). Therefore there is no hidden dependence on that choice of longitude.
