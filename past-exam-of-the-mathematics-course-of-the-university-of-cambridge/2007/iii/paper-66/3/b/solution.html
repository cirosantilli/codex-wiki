To derive the nonrelativistic electron-degeneracy pressure in a hydrogen-helium mixture, take fully ionized hydrogen and helium with hydrogen mass fraction X, the Electron number density is
ne​=mp​Xρ​+24mp​(1−X)ρ​=2mp​(1+X)ρ​.
(30)
Here helium nuclei have mass 4mp​ in the approximation used in the question. Filling the two spin states of each momentum mode up to the Fermi momentum p0​ gives
ne​=∫0p0​​h38πp2​dp=3h38πp03​​,p0​=h(8π3ne​​)1/3.
(31)
The isotropic momentum flux gives pressure 31​∫pvdn. For nonrelativistic Electrons v=p/me​, so the electron degeneracy pressure is
Pe​​=31​∫0p0​​me​p2​h38πp2​dp=15me​h38πp05​​=5me​h2​(8π3​)2/3ne5/3​.​
(32)
Substitute the composition-dependent ne​ and simplify powers of two:
Pe​=Kρ5/3,K=(2π3​)2/340me​mp5/3​h2(1+X)5/3​.​
(33)
Comparing 5/3=1+1/n gives the polytropic index n=3/2. The result assumes complete Electron degeneracy, p0​≪me​c, and negligible nuclear mass corrections.
Now adopt the stated approximate partially degenerate equation of state, keeping composition and hence K,μ fixed during contraction. Set q=m/M and define the mass-averaged temperature TM​=∫01​Tdq. The stellar virial theorem from part (a) gives
3K∫01​ρ2/3dq+μ3R​TM​=−MΩ​.
(34)
For an index-3/2 stellar polytrope, the permitted gravitational-energy formula is Ω=−6GM2/(7R). Also, using ρˉ​=3M/(4πR3),
ρ2/3=R2M2/3​(4πρˉ​3ρ​)2/3.
(35)
Thus, with A=3K∫01​[3ρ/(4πρˉ​)]2/3dq,
TM​=7RR2μGM​−3RR2μAM2/3​.​
(36)
The normalized density ρ/ρˉ​ is a fixed function of q for this homologous polytropic structure. Therefore its integral and K fix A, which is independent of M and R for a common composition. The mean here is mass-weighted, rather than a volume average.
For the mass-averaged temperature maximum in a partially degenerate polytrope, put α=2μGM/(7R) and δ=μAM2/3/(3R), both positive. As a function of s=1/R the temperature is the concave quadratic
TM​=αs−δs2=4δα2​−δ(s−2δα​)2.
(37)
It reaches its maximum when R=2δ/α=7AM−1/3/(3G), with
TM,max​=4δα2​=49RA3μG2M4/3​.​
(38)
At large radius, contraction increases the temperature as in a thermally supported star. Once Electron degeneracy provides a sufficiently large fraction of the support, further contraction can lower the thermal temperature. The formal zero-temperature radius is Rdeg​=δ/α=Rmax​/2, with Rdeg​∝M−1/3. The continuation to negative temperatures would be unphysical; the adopted sequence only describes its positive-temperature part.
The contraction-limited central temperature has the same mass scaling. Indeed, if the total polytropic pressure is P=Ktot​ρ5/3, the approximate equation of state gives T=(μ/R)(Ktot​−K)ρ2/3. The fixed density profile therefore fixes Tc​/TM​. A sufficiently small mass never reaches the central temperature required for sustained hydrogen burning, producing a hydrogen-burning minimum mass. Such an object becomes a cooling, degeneracy-supported brown dwarf instead of settling onto the main sequence. More massive contracting objects can ignite hydrogen before reaching this degeneracy-limited maximum. This conclusion uses the nonrelativistic, fixed-composition model; it does not require extrapolation into the relativistic regime.
