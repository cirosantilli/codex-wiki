For the Schwarzschild criterion, displace a small gas parcel upwards. Assume its composition is unchanged, it remains in pressure balance with its surroundings, and it moves quickly enough that heat exchange is negligible. The parcel obeys the adiabatic equation of state Pρ−γ=constant. Combining this with the ideal gas equation gives T∝P(γ−1)/γ, so its adiabatic temperature gradient is ∇ad​=(γ−1)/γ=2/5.
Let the surrounding temperature gradient be ∇=dlogT/dlogP. For an upward displacement δlogP<0, the parcel's temperature relative to its new surroundings is
δlogTparcel​−δlogTenvironment​=(∇ad​−∇)δlogP.
(14)
If ∇<∇ad​, this is negative: the parcel is cooler and, at equal pressure and composition, denser than its surroundings. It therefore sinks back; a downward displacement likewise gives a restoring buoyancy force. If the inequality is reversed, the displaced parcel continues to rise. The stability condition is thus
∇=TP​dPdT​<52​.​
(15)
Equality is the neutral boundary. There is no composition-gradient term because the ideal-gas parcel and the background have the same mean molecular weight.
In the thin upper stellar atmosphere, take a plane-parallel approximation with constant g, optical depth increasing inward, and zero overlying pressure P(0)=0. Hydrostatic equilibrium in optical depth gives dP/dτ=g/κ. The ideal gas equation converts the stipulated opacity into
κ=Rκ0​μP​T4β,dτdP2​=κ0​μ2Rg​T−4β.
(16)
Define u=1+3τ/2, so the given grey atmosphere relation is T4=Te4​u/2. Integrating from the surface gives
P2​=κ0​μTe4β​2β+1Rg​∫0τ​(1+3t/2)−βdt=3κ0​(β−1)μTe4β​2β+2Rg​(1−u1−β).​
(17)
This proves the required pressure law. In particular P2∝gTe−4β​ times a function only of u and β.
For the grey-atmosphere convection onset with density-linear opacity, differentiate the two profiles to obtain the radiative temperature gradient:
dτdlogT​=8u3​,dτdlogP​=4(1−u1−β)3(β−1)u−β​,∇rad​=2(β−1)uβ−1−1​.
(18)
It increases monotonically from zero, since β>1, and first reaches the adiabatic temperature gradient at ubβ−1​=(4β+1)/5. Therefore
τb​=32​[(54β+1​)1/(β−1)−1].​
(19)
Below this point the radiative stratification is unstable and convection must transport some of the outward energy flux.
At the outer convection boundary, ub​ depends only on β, so Tb​=Te​(ub​/2)1/4∝Te​ and Pb​∝g1/2Te−2β​ for fixed μ,κ0​,β. The matching constant in P=KT5/2 is K=Pb​/Tb5/2​. Consequently
K∝g1/2Te−2β−5/2​.​
(20)
This temperature form of the polytropic relation follows from an ideal monatomic adiabat; its K is not the density-polytrope coefficient in Question 3.
For the deep radiative boundary beneath a power-law-opacity convective envelope, negligible envelope mass and absent local energy generation give m≃M and Lr​≃L. Divide the radiative temperature equation by the pressure equation to get the stellar radiative temperature gradient
∇rad​=16πacGMT43κLP​.
(21)
This is the gradient that would be needed if radiation alone carried L, and it is the quantity to compare with 2/5 even while the actual region is convective. The deeper Kramers' opacity law gives κ=κ1​μPT−9/2/R. Substituting P=KT5/2 yields
∇rad​=16πacRGMT7/23κ1​μK2L​.
(22)
As T rises inward this decreases, so the transition back to a stable radiative interior is at
16πacRGMTin7/2​3κ1​μK2L​=52​.​
(23)
Thus Tin7/2​∝K2L/M. Using the outer matching and the Stefan–Boltzmann law,
K2ML​∝gTe−4β−5​ML​,gML​=R2GM​M4πR2σTe4​​=4πGσTe4​.
(24)
The mass and radius cancel. Hence, at fixed opacity coefficients and composition,
Tin​∝Te−(8β+2)/7​.​
(25)
This uses the same adiabat through the convection zone and distinguishes its inner transition temperature from its outer onset temperature.
