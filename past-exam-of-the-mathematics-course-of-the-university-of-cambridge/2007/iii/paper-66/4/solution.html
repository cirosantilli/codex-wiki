Treat the stars as point masses for the orbital motion, neglecting their spin angular momenta. Let M=M1​+M2​. Their distances from the centre of mass are a1​=aM2​/M and a2​=aM1​/M. The sum of their orbital angular momenta is
J​=(M1​a12​+M2​a22​)Ω=MM1​M2​​a2(a3GM​)1/2.​
(39)
Thus the circular-binary orbital angular momentum is
J=M1​+M2​M1​M2​​G(M1​+M2​)a​.​
(40)
For conservative binary mass transfer, both M and J are fixed. In terms of the binary mass ratio q=M1​/M2​, M1​=Mq/(1+q) and M2​=M/(1+q), hence
J2=GM3a(1+q)4q2​,a(1+q)−4q2=C,C=GM3J2​.​
(41)
Mass conservation also gives dM2​=−dM1​, so
dlogM1​dlogM2​​=−q,dlogM1​dlogq​=1+q.
(42)
Differentiating J∝M1​M2​a1/2 at fixed M,J gives dloga/dlogM1​=2(q−1). Therefore the stipulated Roche lobe law has the Roche-lobe radius response exponent
ζL​=dlogM1​dlogRL​​=103​(1+q)+2(q−1)=1023q−17​.
(43)
Define the overfill by Δ=log(R1​/RL​). On a time scale short compared with the slow change of K, the donor's response is dlogR1​=βdlogM1​. Thus
dΔ=(β−ζL​)dlogM1​.
(44)
During mass loss dlogM1​<0. If ζL​>β, mass loss increases the overfill and therefore drives more mass loss: this is positive feedback, giving more rapid Roche-lobe overflow. If ζL​<β, mass loss decreases the overfill and can balance the slow expansion due to stellar evolution. More explicitly, with kev​=dlogK/dt>0, contact requires
0=Δ˙=kev​+(β−ζL​)M1​M˙1​​.
(45)
A slow negative transfer rate can satisfy this only on the stable side β>ζL​. The rapid-transfer condition is therefore
q>2317+10β​.​
(46)
Its time scale depends on the physical donor response represented by β; it need not be a dynamical time scale if β is a thermal-equilibrium mass-radius exponent.
For Roche-lobe overflow feedback with a constant wind torque factor, retain mass conservation to the stated approximation but allow the prescribed angular-momentum loss. Since dlogJ=fdlogM1​, differentiation gives
f=1−q+21​dlogM1​dloga​,dlogM1​dloga​=2(q−1+f).
(47)
The new Roche-lobe radius response exponent is
ζL​=103​(1+q)+2(q−1+f)=1023q−17+20f​.
(48)
The same overfill calculation gives
q>2317−20f+10β​.​
(49)
A loss of angular momentum has f>0 because M˙1​<0, and it lowers the critical ratio by making the orbit contract more strongly.
For the specified solar-mass donor exponent β=1/2, the answer is
q>2322−20f​,q>0.​
(50)
Without the wind torque this becomes q>22/23≃0.957, so donors slightly less massive than their companions can already lie on the rapid-transfer side of this model. For 0<f<11/10 the lower threshold is (22−20f)/23, and for f≥11/10 every positive q satisfies the strict inequality. There is no finite upper bound on q from this criterion. Since the paper does not specify f, the wind case has a conditional range rather than a unique numerical cutoff.
