Write [M]ω for the set of all infinite subsets of M. A family Y⊆[N]ω is a Ramsey family in the homogeneous-cone sense if there is an infinite M such that either [M]ω⊆Y or [M]ω∩Y=∅. Thus membership in Y is constant on an infinite homogeneous cone. We will prove the stronger version for open families: such a cone exists inside every prescribed infinite ground set, as in the definition of a Ramsey set of infinite subsets.
The symbols in the question refer to the ordinary topology on infinite subsets τ and the Ellentuck topology ∗. For a finite set s, let
[s]={X∈[N]ω:s is the initial segment of X}.
(26)
These sets form a topological basis for τ, equivalently the subspace topology inherited from the product topology on {0,1}N. For an infinite A with maxs<minA, the sets
[s,A]={s∪B:B∈[A]ω}
(27)
form a topological basis for ∗; take max∅=0. In particular [s]=[s,{n:n>maxs}], so ∗ is finer than τ.
For a non-Ramsey example use the finite-symmetric-difference parity colouring. On [N]ω, define the equivalence relation X∼Z if their symmetric difference X△Z is finite. By the axiom of choice, select a representative R in every equivalence class. Give X the colour
c(X)=∣X△RX​∣(mod2),
(28)
where RX​ is its class representative. Deleting one point stays in the same class and toggles the parity. Thus for every infinite M, the sets M and M∖{minM} are both in [M]ω but have opposite colours. Either colour class, regarded as a family Y, is therefore not Ramsey.
Now let U be τ-open. We prove that for every infinite A0​ there is an infinite C⊆A0​ with [C]ω⊆U or [C]ω∩U=∅. The proof uses acceptance and rejection of finite stems, which we define explicitly. For a finite stem s and an infinite tail A above maxs, say that A accepts s if [s,A]⊆U, and rejects s if no infinite subset of A accepts s. Both properties persist on taking infinite subsets. Every tail has an infinite subset that decides s: take an accepting subset if one exists, and otherwise it already rejects s.
First thin A0​ to decide the empty stem. Then construct b1​<b2​<⋯ and nested infinite tails. At step n, choose bn​ from the current tail, discard its points at most bn​, and successively thin what remains to decide every stem s⊆{b1​,…,bn​}. There are only finitely many such stems, so an infinite tail remains. Let B={b1​,b2​,…} and write B/s={b∈B:b>maxs}. For every finite s⊆B, the tail B/s decides s, since it lies in the tail obtained at the stage when the largest point of s was selected. The empty stem is also decided. This is deciding all finite stems by fusion.
If B accepts the empty stem, then [B]ω⊆U, as required. Otherwise B rejects it. We need the finitely many accepting extensions of a rejected stem observation. Suppose a tail B/s rejects s. Only finitely many a∈B/s can have B/a accepting s∪{a}. Indeed, if infinitely many such a formed a set D, every member of [s,D] would have some first new point a and all later points in B/a, hence would lie in U. This would make D an accepting subset of B/s, contradicting rejection.
Choose c1​<c2​<⋯ in B recursively so that for every finite s⊆{c1​,…,cn​}, the tail B/s rejects s. The empty stem satisfies this initially. At the next step, each of the finitely many previous stems has only finitely many accepting one-point extensions by the observation. Choose cn+1​ larger than all previous points and outside all those finite exceptional sets. The first fusion ensured that the tail for every new stem decides it; since it does not accept it, it rejects it. This maintains the recursion.
Put C={c1​,c2​,…}. If some X∈[C]ω belonged to U, ordinary openness would give a finite initial segment s of X with [s]⊆U. In particular [s,C/s]⊆U. But C/s is an infinite subset of B/s, which rejects s, a contradiction. Hence [C]ω∩U=∅. We have proved
Every τ-open family is Ramsey, even inside every infinite ground set.​
(29)
