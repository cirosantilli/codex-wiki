Van der Waerden theorem says that for every pair of positive integers r,k there is W(r,k) such that every finite colouring of [W(r,k)]={1,…,W(r,k)} using at most r colours contains a monochromatic arithmetic progression of length k and positive common difference. Consequently every finite colouring of N contains arbitrarily long monochromatic arithmetic progressions.
Here is a colour-focusing proof of Van der Waerden theorem. Induct on k, simultaneously for all r. For k=1, take W(r,1)=1. Suppose W(R,k) exists for every number of colours R, and fix r. We will construct finite bounds Ft​, 1≤t≤r, with the following property: every finite colouring of [Ft​] using at most r colours contains either a monochromatic length-(k+1) arithmetic progression, or t length-k arithmetic progressions of pairwise distinct colours, all focused at the same point f∈[Ft​]. Specifically, their terms are
f−di​,f−2di​,…,f−kdi​,di​>0,1≤i≤t.
(6)
“Focused” means that adjoining f completes the corresponding length-(k+1) arithmetic progression if f has its colour.
For t=1, put F1​=2W(r,k). Find a monochromatic length-k arithmetic progression in the first W(r,k) points. Its next point is at most 2W(r,k) and supplies the focus. For k=1, give the selected singleton common difference 1.
Suppose Ft​=N has been constructed. Set
M=W(rN,k),Ft+1​=2NM.
(7)
Divide [2NM] into 2M consecutive blocks of length N. Colour the first M block indices by their full colour profiles, that is, the ordered list of their N colours. There are at most rN profiles, so the induction hypothesis on k gives equally profiled blocks with indices
j0​,j0​+D,…,j0​+(k−1)D.
(8)
When k=1, choose D=1. Otherwise D>0 and j0​+kD≤2M, since the last selected block is at most M and D≤M.
Assume the original finite colouring contains no monochromatic length-(k+1) arithmetic progression. Applying the property of Ft​ to the common profile gives t focused length-k arithmetic progressions at a position f, with common differences di​ and distinct colours qi​. The colour q of position f differs from every qi​: otherwise the profile already contains a length-(k+1) monochromatic arithmetic progression, and so does any selected block.
Put j∗​=j0​+kD and F=N(j∗​−1)+f, a point in the original interval. For 1≤h≤k, the point
F−h(ND+di​)=N(j∗​−hD−1)+(f−hdi​)
(9)
lies in a selected block at profile position f−hdi​, so has colour qi​. Similarly F−hND has colour q. Thus t+1 length-k arithmetic progressions of distinct colours focus at F. This proves the step in t. At t=r, the focus necessarily has the colour of one of the r progressions, giving a monochromatic length-(k+1) arithmetic progression. Hence W(r,k+1) exists, completing the mathematical induction and the proof of Van der Waerden theorem.
For the partition regularity deduction we first prove a strengthened progression lemma, the Brauer progression theorem: for positive r,s,L with L≥2, there is B(r,s,L) such that every finite colouring of [B(r,s,L)] using at most r colours contains a monochromatic set
{sd,a,a+d,…,a+(L−1)d},a,d>0.
(10)
Induct on r. For r=1, take B(1,s,L)=max(s,L) and a=d=1. Given the result for r−1, put
N=B(r−1,s,L),K=(L−1)N+1,W=W(r,K),B(r,s,L)=max(W,sNW).
(11)
Van der Waerden theorem supplies a colour-q arithmetic progression a+jd0​, 0≤j<K, inside [W]. If some sjd0​, 1≤j≤N, also has colour q, take d=jd0​ and use the L terms a,a+jd0​,…,a+(L−1)jd0​. They are in the progression because (L−1)j≤K−1.
Otherwise the integers sjd0​, 1≤j≤N, use at most r−1 colours. Colour j∈[N] by the colour of sjd0​ and apply the induction hypothesis. It supplies a monochromatic set {se,b,b+e,…,b+(L−1)e}. Multiplying by sd0​ gives the required configuration with a=sd0​b and d=sd0​e: its distinguished member is s2d0​e=sd. All of these points are at most sNd0​≤sNW, so the finite bound is valid. This proves the Brauer progression theorem using only the just-proved Van der Waerden theorem.
A row is a partition regular matrix over the positive integers if every finite colouring of N admits a positive vector x whose coordinates have one colour and whose product with the row is zero. Coordinates may repeat. Since the coefficients are rational numbers, multiplying them by a common positive denominator leaves the equation and every zero subset sum unchanged, so write the row as nonzero integer coefficients b1​,…,bn​.
To prove necessity, suppose no nonempty subset of the bi​ sums to zero. Choose a prime p>∑i​∣bi​∣ and use the last nonzero digit coloring
c(x)=p−vp​(x)x(modp) ∈Fp×​,
(12)
where vp​ is the P-adic valuation. If positive xi​ all had the same colour u and satisfied ∑i​bi​xi​=0, let m=mini​vp​(xi​) and I={i:vp​(xi​)=m}. After division by pm and reduction modulo p, the equation becomes
u∑i∈I​bi​=0in Fp​.
(13)
As u=0, this says p∣∑i∈I​bi​. The latter sum is nonzero and has absolute value at most ∑i​∣bi​∣<p, a contradiction. This explicitly proves the necessary obstruction without invoking a general partition regularity criterion.
Conversely, suppose ∅=I⊆{1,…,n} and ∑i∈I​bi​=0. Select an index j∈I, and put
S=∑i∈/I​bi​,s=∣bj​∣,q=−bj​sS​∈Z,u=max(0,−q),L=max(2,∣q∣+1).
(14)
For any finite colouring, the Brauer progression theorem gives one-coloured {sd,a,a+d,…,a+(L−1)d}. The indices u and u+q both lie between 0 and L−1. Define positive, same-coloured coordinates by
xi​=⎩⎨⎧​sd,a+ud,a+(u+q)d,​i∈/I,i∈I∖{j},i=j.​
(15)
Then
∑i​bi​xi​=(a+ud)∑i∈I​bi​+d(bj​q+sS)=0.
(16)
This also covers I equal to the whole index set and q=0. Reversing the clearing of denominators proves the exact criterion:
(a1​,…,an​) is partition regular⟺∃∅=I⊆{1,…,n}: i∈I∑​ai​=0.​
