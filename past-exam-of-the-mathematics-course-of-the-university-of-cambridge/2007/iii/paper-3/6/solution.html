Choose a maximal Brauer pair (D,bD​) for B, and let NG​(D,bD​) be the subgroup normalizing D and stabilizing the local block bD​. The inertial quotient of a block and its order, the inertial index of a block, are
E(B)=NG​(D,bD​)/(DCG​(D)),e=∣E(B)∣.
(34)
The Brauer extended first main theorem says that maximal local blocks of defect D are parametrized by the normalizer orbits of defect-D blocks of k(DCG​(D)), with stabilizer quotient of order prime to p. For abelian D, conjugation embeds E(B) in Aut(D). When D=Cpn​, its prime-to-p subgroups are cyclic of order dividing p−1, so e∣p−1.
We use two precise structural results for a block with cyclic defect group, with k an algebraically closed field. First, the basic algebra is a Brauer tree algebra with e edges and exceptional multiplicity m=(pn−1)/e; its simple modules are indexed by its edges. Second, its stable Auslander–Reiten quiver has shape
ZApn−1​/⟨τe⟩.
(35)
The vertices are the isomorphism classes of nonprojective indecomposable modules. These are the cyclic-defect structure and indecomposable-classification results, rather than a claim that all Brauer trees are Morita equivalent to a star. The tube description is also recorded in Hiss–Lassueur, Section 1.
The first result gives l(B)=e. In the second, there are pn−1 positions in each slice and e slices modulo the translation. Thus there are e(pn−1) nonprojective indecomposables. A finite-dimensional split algebra has one principal indecomposable module that is a projective cover for each simple module, so there are another e projective indecomposables. Therefore
l(B)=e,#ind(B)=e(pn−1)+e=pne.​
(36)
The total includes projectives; it is different from the count of ordinary irreducible characters. This is the indecomposable count for a cyclic defect block.
Now let p=2 and D=C2​×C2​, the Klein four-group. The extended theorem makes E(B) a subgroup of order prime to 2 of Aut(D)≅GL2​(F2​)≅S3​. Its order divides 6 and is odd, so
e=1 or 3.​
(37)
For the remaining conclusion we use the central-defect quotient fact: if Z≤Z(L) is a central p-subgroup, blocks of kL correspond to blocks of k(L/Z) under the quotient, and their defect groups quotient by Z. Central block idempotents lift uniquely here because the kernel is generated by central nilpotent elements; an off-diagonal corner lies in its own product with that nilpotent ideal and is therefore zero. Defect groups quotient by Z by the relative-trace, or equivalently Brauer-pair, characterization. In particular a block with central defect group D has a defect-zero block as its quotient by J(kD).
First suppose D≤Z(G), and write A=B. Since k is an algebraically closed field, the defect-zero quotient is A≅Matr​(k) for some r. The algebra A is a direct summand of kG as a kD-module, hence is projective and therefore free over the local ring kD. Its rank is r2, since reduction modulo J(kD) gives the r2-dimensional quotient. Lift a primitive idempotent fˉ​ of A to f∈A. The corner fAf is a kD-linear direct summand of A and is free over kD. Its quotient by J(kD) is fˉ​Afˉ​≅k, so its rank is one. The central map
kD⟶fAf,z⟼zf
(38)
is surjective by Nakayama's lemma, since f generates its reduction, and is injective because both sides have dimension ∣D∣. Thus fAf≅kD as algebras. Orthogonal primitive idempotents and their equivalences in the matrix quotient lift modulo the nilpotent ideal; lifting the equivalence maps and correcting their products by units lifts the matrix units. Consequently
A≅Matr​(fAf)≅Matr​(kD).
(39)
This proves the actual algebra isomorphism of central defect blocks are matrix algebras, as an algebra.
Finally suppose D◃G but is not central. Put C=CG​(D), which is normal in G and contains D since D is abelian. The normal-subgroup argument in Question 5 puts the block idempotent eB​ in kC. It is therefore the sum of the G-conjugates of a block idempotent c of kC. The extended first main theorem chooses such a c with defect group D; since D is normal, its pair stabilizer is exactly I={g∈G:gc=c}. Now
e=[I:DC]=[I:C]=1,
(40)
so I=C. Within C, the defect group D is central, and the first case gives kCc≅Matr​(kD).
We can see the Fong–Reynolds matrix decomposition explicitly in this case. Choose representatives g1​,…,gt​ for G/C, where t=[G:C], and put
uij​=gi​cgj−1​.
(41)
Different conjugates of c are orthogonal, so uij​uℓm​=δjℓ​uim​ and ∑i​uii​=eB​. Moreover cBc=ckGc=kCc: a term ckCgc vanishes unless g stabilizes c, and that stabilizer is C. These matrix units therefore identify B with Matt​(kCc), whence
B≅Mattr​(kD).​
(42)
The inertial index condition concerns the stabilizer of the local block; it does not require every element of G to centralize D. That distinction is why the second case is necessary.
