Adjoin P1(Q) to the complex upper half-plane. Ordinary points retain their usual neighborhoods. At a rational boundary point a=σ∞, a neighborhood basis consists of a together with σ{z∈H:Imz>Y}, with Y arbitrarily large. Different choices of σ∈SL2​(Z) change it by a translation and sign, so give the same neighborhood system. Give X(1) the quotient topology. The modular group acts properly discontinuously on H; its modular cusp neighborhoods descend to punctured neighborhoods with their modular cusp point filled in. This quotient is Hausdorff: interior orbits can be separated by proper discontinuity, and a sufficiently high modular cusp neighborhood avoids any prescribed compact interior neighborhood.
Away from fixed points of the effective group PSL2​(Z), a small disk is disjoint from its nonidentity translates, so its coordinate gives a holomorphic quotient coordinate. At an elliptic fixed point τ0​, use
u=τ−τ0​​τ−τ0​​.
(10)

q=e2πiτ
(11)
is a punctured-disk coordinate; adjoining q=0 fills in the modular cusp. These charts and their holomorphic transition maps define the complex structure of the compactified modular curve.

(12)

Pass to the effective subgroup Γ⊂PSL2​(Z). Its index is μ: adjoining −I halves the original index precisely when −I∈/Γ. The natural map of compactified modular curves
π:X(Γ)⟶X(1)≅P1
(48)
has degree μ, since a generic orbit has that many lifts. It branches only over the order-two elliptic orbit, the order-three elliptic orbit, and the modular cusp. Away from these, the modular quotient charts are genuine unramified covering charts.
Over i, a lift with its own effective stabilizer of order two is unramified; there are ν2​ such lifts. Every other lift has ramification index two. As the sum of all ramification indices in that fibre is μ, their number is (μ−ν2​)/2 and their contribution to the ramification divisor is (μ−ν2​)/2. Similarly, over ρ there are ν3​ unramified elliptic lifts and (μ−ν3​)/3 lifts of ramification index three, contributing 2(μ−ν3​)/3.
At a modular cusp of width h, the local parameters are qh​=e2πiτ/h upstairs and q=e2πiτ downstairs. Thus q=qhh​ and the ramification index is h. Summing the widths gives μ, so all ν∞​ modular cusps contribute μ−ν∞​ to ramification. The Riemann-Hurwitz formula now gives
2g−2=−2μ+2μ−ν2​​+32(μ−ν3​)​+μ−ν∞​=6μ​−2ν2​​−32ν3​​−ν∞​.

For an integer weight k, a cusp form for a congruence subgroup Γ is a holomorphic function on H with f∣k​γ=f for every γ∈Γ, whose Fourier expansion of a modular form vanishes at every modular cusp. More explicitly, choose σ∈SL2​(Z) representing a modular cusp σ∞, and a genuine translation period h>0 with σThσ−1∈Γ. Then the requirement is
(f∣k​σ)(τ)=∑n≥1​aσ,n​e2πinτ/h,
(58)
convergent for sufficiently large imaginary part. Thus the slash translate extends holomorphically at qh​=0 and has value zero. The vector space of these functions is Sk​(Γ).
A genuine period is convenient when −I is absent and the weight is odd: the smaller effective cusp width may supply a negative rather than positive translation in Γ, but an actual period always exists, possibly twice that width. Using an actual period avoids imposing an incorrect periodicity. The zero-value condition is independent of the chosen representative or period. If −I∈Γ and k is odd, its slash relation forces f=(−1)kf=−f, so the space is zero.

For positive integer k, the equivalence is the bounded invariant norm characterization of cusp forms. The three conditions below work together: meromorphicity and the bound remove interior poles; the transformation law produces a periodic local function at every modular cusp; the same bound makes that function tend to zero at the modular cusp. Conversely a cusp form has exponentially decaying Fourier expansions of a modular form, and finitely many compact core regions control its invariant norm everywhere. These implications are proved under the corresponding labelled items.
Without a positive-weight convention, weight zero is a genuine exception. The function f=1 satisfies all three printed conditions at k=0 for every congruence subgroup, but is not a cusp form. Indeed a weight-zero cusp form would descend to a holomorphic function on the compact curve X(Γ) and hence be constant, and vanishing at a modular cusp would make it zero. Thus the literal unrestricted claim needs the qualification k>0.
If negative integer weights are included, both sides contain only the zero function. To check the bounded side, each modular cusp translate has at most power growth in y, hence at most a power of log(1/∣qh​∣). For a negative Laurent coefficient of order m, Cauchy's coefficient estimate bounds its absolute value by Crm(log(1/r))∣k∣/2→0, so there is no negative coefficient and every modular cusp is holomorphic. If such a nonzero negative-weight form existed, the product of its slash translates over Γ\SL2​(Z) would be a nonzero holomorphic level-one modular form of negative weight. Right multiplication permutes the translates, and all modular cusp factors are bounded, so that product really is a level-one form. Its existence contradicts the valence formula. This also excludes negative-weight modular cusp forms. The nonzero counterexample is specifically weight zero.

A cusp form is holomorphic in the upper half-plane by definition, so it is meromorphic there. In the reverse direction suppose f is meromorphic and
∣f(τ)∣yk/2≤B(τ∈H).
(61)
On a small closed disk around an interior point, y is bounded above and away from zero. Hence ∣f∣ is bounded there by a fixed constant. A pole of positive order would make ∣f∣ unbounded along points approaching its center, a contradiction. Every possible pole is therefore removable, and f is holomorphic on H. Meromorphicity alone would not establish this conclusion; the bounded invariant norm is being used here.

First suppose f is a cusp form. Choose finitely many representatives σj​ for Γ\SL2​(Z); their translates of the standard modular region cover a fundamental region for Γ. On the compact truncated part of each translate, the continuous function ∣f(τ)∣yk/2 is bounded. On its tail, the corresponding modular cusp expansion of gj​=f∣k​σj​ has zero constant term, so gj​=O(e−2πy/hj​) uniformly in the real part. Therefore yk/2∣gj​∣ is bounded there and tends to zero. Part (b) identifies this expression with ϕ(σj​τ). There are only finitely many representatives; taking the maximum of these bounds and using Γ-invariance proves that ϕ is bounded on all of H.
Conversely assume all three conditions and k>0. Items (i) and (ii) show that at each modular cusp a holomorphic periodic translate g satisfies ∣g(τ)∣≤By−k/2. In its punctured-disk coordinate,
y=2πh​log∣qh​∣1​,∣g(qh​)∣≤B(2πh​log∣qh​∣1​)−k/2⟶0.
(64)
The Riemann removable singularity theorem extends g holomorphically to zero with g(0)=0. Equivalently its Laurent expansion has no negative powers and no constant term.
