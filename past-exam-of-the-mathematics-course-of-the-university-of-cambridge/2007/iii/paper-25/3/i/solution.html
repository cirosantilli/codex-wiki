Write the square as GH=KF, with F:A→B final and G:C→D a discrete fibration. For b∈B, choose an object (a,h:b→Fa) of the nonempty comma category (b↓F). Lift K(h):Kb→GHa uniquely to an arrow
ℓh​:ch​⟶Ha
(7)
over K(h), and tentatively put L(b)=ch​.
A morphism (a,h)→(a′,h′) in this comma category is an arrow j:a→a′ with F(j)h=h′. The composite H(j)ℓh​ is a lift of K(h′) with codomain Ha′. Uniqueness of lifts gives H(j)ℓh​=ℓh′​, including equality of their source objects. Since the comma category is connected by zigzags, ch​ is independent of the choice. Thus L(b) is well defined and GL(b)=Kb.
For f:b→b′, take (a′,h′:b′→Fa′). Lift K(f) into L(b′), obtaining t:c→L(b′). Composing with ℓh′​ gives a lift of K(h′f) into Ha′, whose source must be L(b). Hence t:L(b)→L(b′). Set L(f)=t. Uniqueness of this lift makes the definition independent of the chosen comma object and proves L(1b​)=1L(b)​ and L(gf)=L(g)L(f).
At b=Fa, choose (a,1Fa​). Its lift is 1Ha​, so L(Fa)=Ha. For j:a→a′, Hj is the unique lift of K(Fj) into Ha′, so L(Fj)=Hj. We have constructed the required functor with LF=H and GL=K.
If L′ is another filler, L′(h):L′b→Ha must equal ℓh​ by unique lifting. Thus L′b=L(b) for every object. Its arrows are then forced by unique lifting into those objects. Therefore
L exists and is unique.​
(8)
This proves the orthogonality of final functors and discrete fibrations.
