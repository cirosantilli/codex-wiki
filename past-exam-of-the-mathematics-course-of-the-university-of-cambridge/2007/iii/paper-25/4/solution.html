Past exam of the mathematics course of the University of Cambridge / 2007 / iii / Paper 25 / 4 / Solution

 ... Mathematics course of the University of Cambridge Past exam of the mathematics course of the University of Cambridge 2007 iii Paper 25 4
OurBigBook.com Words: 585
Write the monad identities as
μA​ηTA​=1TA​=μA​TηA​,μA​TμA​=μA​μTA​.
(12)
If μA​ is invertible, its two right inverses agree, so ηTA​=TηA​. Conversely, suppose ηT​=Tη. Naturality of η at μA​, followed by that equality at TA, gives
ηTA​μA​=TμA​ηTTA​=TμA​TηTA​=T(μA​ηTA​)=1TTA​.
(13)
Together with μA​ηTA​=1TA​ this proves
μ is invertible ⟺ ηT​=Tη,μ−1=ηT​=Tη.​
(14)
This is the equivalence defining an idempotent monad.
For the equalizer submonad of a monad, let βA​:RA→TA equalize ηTA​ and TηA​. If f:A→B, naturality of these two transformations shows that TfβA​ equalizes the corresponding pair at B. There is therefore a unique Rf satisfying
βB​Rf=TfβA​.
(15)
Uniqueness gives R1=1 and R(gf)=RgRf, so R is a functor and β:R⇒T is a natural transformation.
Take the stipulated factorizations αA​ and θA​. Naturality of β at βA​ rewrites their defining equations as
βA​αA​=ηA​,βA​θA​=μA​βTA​RβA​=μA​TβA​βRA​.
(16)
The composites on the right are natural in A; cancellation of the monic arrow βA​ therefore makes α and θ natural. We now verify every monad law, rather than assuming that restricting the structure automatically preserves them.
For the two unit laws, naturality and the unit laws of T give
βA​θA​αRA​βA​θA​RαA​​=μA​TβA​ηRA​=μA​ηTA​βA​=βA​,=μA​TβA​TαA​βA​=μA​TηA​βA​=βA​.​
(17)
Cancelling βA​ proves θA​αRA​=1RA​=θA​RαA​. For associativity, the same naturality equations give
βA​θA​RθA​βA​θA​θRA​​=μA​TμA​TTβA​TβRA​βRRA​,=μA​μTA​TTβA​TβRA​βRRA​.​
(18)
These are equal by associativity of μ; cancelling βA​ proves associativity of θ. Thus
(R,α,θ) is a monad, and β:R⇒T is a monad morphism.​
(19)
The remaining claims use two split equalizers associated with a monad. First, ηTA​:TA→TTA is a split equalizer of ηTTA​,TηTA​. Its retraction is μA​, and the splitting of the parallel pair is TμA​, since
μA​ηTA​=1,TμA​ηTTA​=ηTA​μA​,TμA​TηTA​=1.
(20)
The equalizing equation follows from naturality of η. In general, identities re=1, sf=er, sg=1 and fe=ge prove that e is an equalizer: if fh=gh, then h=erh, with unique factorization rh. Hence ηTA​ and βTA​ are two equalizers of the same pair. Their comparison is αTA​, and its inverse is μA​βTA​. In particular
αTA​ is invertible,αTA−1​=μA​βTA​.​
(21)
Second, eA​=TηA​:TA→TTA is a split equalizer of TTηA​,TηTA​, with retraction μA​ and splitting μTA​. Indeed,
μA​TηA​=1,μTA​TTηA​=TηA​μA​,μTA​TηTA​=1.
(22)
This is precisely the parallel pair in the PDF's diagram after TβA​. The map TβA​ equalizes that pair, because applying T to the defining equalizer equation gives their equality on TβA​. Put
hA​=μA​TβA​:TRA→TA.
(23)
The split-equalizer factorization and βA​αA​=ηA​ give
eA​hA​=TβA​,hA​TαA​=1TA​.
(24)
If TαA​ is invertible, then TβA​=eA​(TαA​)−1 is a monic arrow. Conversely, if TβA​ is a monic arrow, the equation
TβA​(TαA​hA​)=eA​hA​=TβA​
(25)
forces TαA​hA​=1TRA​, so hA​ is the inverse of TαA​. Therefore
TαA​ invertible ⟺ TβA​ monic.​
(26)
Notice that, under either condition, TβA​ is actually a split monomorphism, with retraction TαA​μA​. Thus TTβA​ is a monic arrow too, since a functor preserves split monomorphisms. We have not assumed that T preserves arbitrary monomorphisms.
Now prove the specified naturality square is a pullback in a category. Let x:X→TRA and y:X→RTA satisfy TβA​x=βTA​y. Naturality of η, followed by the equalizer equation for βTA​, gives
TTβA​ηTRA​x​=ηTTA​TβA​x=ηTTA​βTA​y=TηTA​βTA​y=TηTA​TβA​x=TTβA​TηRA​x.​
(27)
Cancel the monic arrow TTβA​. Thus x equalizes ηTRA​,TηRA​ and factors uniquely as x=βRA​z with z:X→RRA. Naturality of β then gives
βTA​RβA​z=TβA​βRA​z=TβA​x=βTA​y.
(28)
Cancel βTA​ to obtain RβA​z=y. This is exactly existence and uniqueness in the pullback universal property.
Because the square is a pullback and TβA​ is a monic arrow, RβA​ is a monic arrow. Naturality of α and the formula for θ now give
RβA​αRA​θA​=αTA​βA​θA​=αTA​μA​βTA​RβA​=RβA​.
(29)
Cancelling RβA​ proves αRA​θA​=1RRA​. The other composite is already one of the unit laws. Hence θA​ is invertible. If the equivalent monicity conditions hold for every A, we conclude
(R,α,θ) is idempotent,θ−1=αR​=Rα.​
(30)
