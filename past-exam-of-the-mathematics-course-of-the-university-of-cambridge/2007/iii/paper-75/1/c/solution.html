The neutral displacement equation gives, for each nonzero-frequency root,
−iωun​=−μ(un​−ui​),ξi​=(1−μiω​)ξn​,ξi​ξn​​=μ−iωμ​.​
(11)
The vector displacements are parallel as complex polarization vectors, with this scalar amplitude and phase ratio.
For the rapidly damped root, μ−iωd​=−μ+a2/(4μ)+O(a4/μ3), and hence
ξi​ξn​​=−1−4μ2a2​+O(μ4a4​).​
(12)
The two fluids move almost equally and oppositely; collisions remove the counterflow at a rate close to 2μ.
For either weakly damped Alfvén wave, expand the ratio around unity:
ξi​ξn​​=1±i2​μa​+O(μ2a2​).​
(13)
Equivalently, the exact slippage relation is ξi​−ξn​=−(iω/μ)ξn​. Thus the relative slippage has size ∣a∣/(2​μ)≪1 and is primarily a small phase difference. The fluids almost share a common displacement, but magnetic tension acts directly on the ions and drag accelerates the neutrals. This imperfect locking causes ambipolar damping.
The sign of the damping is also checked by the perturbation energy balance. With periodic or vanishing-flux boundaries,
dtd​∫[2ρ​(∣ui​∣2+∣un​∣2)+8π∣δB∣2​]dV=−ρμ∫∣ui​−un​∣2dV≤0.
(14)
It follows by dotting each linear momentum equation with its velocity and the induction equation with δB/(4π); magnetic-tension work cancels magnetic-energy change, pressure contributes only boundary flux, and the two drag terms combine into the negative square. Even though spatial diffusion was neglected, ion-neutral friction still dissipates the wave energy.
