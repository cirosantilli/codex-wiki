δCab​=21​(□hab​+∂a​∂b​h−∂a​∂chbc​−∂b​∂chac​),δC=□h−∂a​∂b​hab.

In the transverse-traceless tensor sector h=0 and ∂chac​=0, so δC=0 and
δGαβ​=21​□hαβ​=−□Eαβ​.

The tensor part of the Linearized Einstein equations therefore gives
E¨αβ−ΔEαβ=8πGδTαβ.​

In a calculation strictly confined to the tensor sector, the source on this equation means its tensor projection; it is already trace-free there.
For a generic compact source, trace-free is not the same as transverse-traceless. For example, the static compactly supported spatial stress Tij=∂i∂jχ−γijΔχ, with smooth compactly supported χ, is divergence-free, but its trace-free part has divergence 2∂jΔχ/3, generally nonzero. There is a precise way to reproduce the unprojected trace-free formula used in the question while retaining this distinction. In Lorenz gauge in linearized gravity, the trace-reversed metric perturbation hˉab​=hab​−ηab​h/2 obeys
∂ahˉab​=0,δGab​=21​□hˉab​,□hˉab​=−16πGδTab​.
(23)
Define its spatial trace-free half-strain by
Eαβ​=−21​(hˉαβ​−31​γαβ​γμνhˉμν​).
(24)
Then its wave equation has exactly the displayed trace-free source δTαβ=δTαβ−γαβδTμμ,spatial​/3. The physical tensor radiation is obtained afterwards with the transverse-traceless projector. Without that projection, this E is a Lorenz-gauge trace-free coefficient, not necessarily a transverse tensor mode. At leading far-zone order, the same directional projector is applied to both sides of the quadrupole field formula below.
The flat contracted Bianchi identity implies stress-energy conservation, ∂b​δTab=0. Let
Iαβ(t)=∫δT00(t,x)xαxβd3x.
(25)
Compact support removes boundary terms. Differentiate and integrate by parts once:
I˙αβ​=−∫∂μ​δT0μxαxβd3x=∫(δT0αxβ+δT0βxα)d3x.​

A second time derivative, another integration by parts and symmetry of the stress-energy tensor give
I¨αβ=−∫(∂μ​δTαμxβ+∂μ​δTβμxα)d3x=2∫δTαβd3x.

Take the spatial trace-free part. The mass quadrupole moment satisfies the stress-energy quadrupole identity
Qαβ=Iαβ−31​γαβγμν​Iμν,Q¨​αβ=2∫δTαβd3x.​
(28)
Inserting this into the assumed leading retarded quadrupole field gives
Eαβ(t,x)=rG​Q¨​αβ(t−r).​
(29)
This is the requested spatial half-strain convention; the measured transverse-traceless strain is HαβTT​=2EαβTT​. The formula selects the retarded source field and does not include an independently added homogeneous gravitational wave. Dropping the source-dependent part of retardation also needs the long-wavelength source approximation, besides R/r≪1.
For a slowly moving weakly self-gravitating source, the virial theorem or the dynamical balance v2/R∼GM/R2 gives v2∼ϵ=GM/R. A varying nonspherical mass quadrupole moment has characteristic size MR2 and dynamical time τ∼R/v, so Q¨​∼MR2/τ2∼Mv2. Consequently the quadrupole strain scaling of a weak self-gravitating source is
∣Eαβ∣=O(rGM​v2)=O(ϵ2rR​).​
(30)
This is a characteristic scale, not a nonzero lower bound: a stationary or exactly spherical source does not radiate. Slow motion gives R/τ∼ϵ​≪1, justifying the long-wavelength approximation; for a radiation-zone strain one also takes r≫τ.
