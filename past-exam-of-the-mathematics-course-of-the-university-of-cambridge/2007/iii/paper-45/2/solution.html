For fixed positive initial capital, a larger adjustment coefficient reduces the exponential upper bound ψα​(u)≤e−Rα​u from the Lundberg inequality. It also makes the large-capital decay exponent in the Cramér–Lundberg ruin asymptotic larger. These are the reasons for maximizing Rα​ as a ruin-risk criterion; the coefficient alone need not order exact finite-capital ruin probabilities for arbitrary different models.
If θR​=θ, then cα​=(1+θ)λα and kα​=2(1+θ) is constant. The equal-loading quota share and vanishing retention relation becomes
Rα​=2(1+θ)α3+4θ−9+8θ​​=αR1​​.​
(25)
The numerator is positive for θ>0, so Rα​ decreases strictly with α and tends to infinity as α↓0. There is no maximizing retention in the stipulated interval 0<α≤1; the best limit is full cession. If the endpoint α=0 is admitted as an idealized contract, all claims are transferred and the net premium income is zero, leaving the direct insurer's capital constant and its ultimate ruin probability zero. At that endpoint the coefficient equation is identically zero, so it no longer defines a unique finite adjustment coefficient. Calling α=0 optimal requires this explicit extension of the original admissible set.
