Choose holomorphic coordinates centered at the point p and a polydisc U⊂Cn about the origin. The local blowup of a complex manifold at a point is the incidence manifold
U={(z,[ℓ])∈U×CPn−1:zi​ℓj​=zj​ℓi​ for all i,j},σ(z,[ℓ])=z.
(23)
Away from z=0 the direction is forced to be [ℓ]=[z], giving a biholomorphism with U∖{0}. Glue this punctured part to X∖{p}. The charts below make the result a complex manifold. The projection is proper near p, since it is the restriction of projection from a closed subset of U×CPn−1; globally the modification is proper and is an isomorphism off p. Its exceptional divisor is
E=σ−1(p)≅P(Tp​X)≅CPn−1.
(24)
On ℓi​=0, let t=zi​ and uj​=ℓj​/ℓi​ for j=i. Then
zi​=t,zj​=tuj​ (j=i).
(25)
These are coordinates on the open domain where t and all tuj​ lie in the original polydisc; at t=0 all finite uj​ are allowed. Their inverse reads the direction ratios and zi​ from the incidence point. In this chart E is the nonsingular hypersurface t=0. On overlap with chart k=i, the holomorphic coordinate changes are
t′=tuk​,ui′​=1/uk​,uj′​=uj​/uk​(j=i,k).
(26)
They are holomorphic with holomorphic inverses where uk​=0, and the n charts cover every point of E. Intrinsically, changing the original coordinates by F lifts via [ℓ]↦[A(z)ℓ], where F(z)=A(z)z, A(0)=dF0​, and A is holomorphic and invertible near zero. One can take A(z)=∫01​dFsz​ds on a sufficiently small polydisc. Away from the origin the lift is uniquely forced; this proves coordinate-independence of the gluing near E.
The canonical bundle is KX​=ΛnΩX1​. In the ith blowup chart the top-form Jacobian is
σ∗(dz1​∧⋯∧dzn​)=(−1)i−1tn−1dt∧⋀j=i​duj​,
(27)
where the uj​ occur in increasing order. Every term with a second dt vanishes in the wedge product of differential forms. Thus the differential gives a map σ∗KX​→KX​ vanishing to order n−1 along E and nowhere else. To express this through the supplied meromorphic-section hypothesis, take a nonzero meromorphic section s of KX​ and put D=div(s). Locally s=f(z)dz1​∧⋯∧dzn​, so
div(σ∗s)=σ∗D+(n−1)E.
(28)
Here σ∗D is the total divisor pullback, including any additional exceptional multiplicity from f; it is not merely the strict transform. A line bundle with a nonzero meromorphic section is isomorphic to the line bundle of that section's divisor, as its local coefficient ratios are its frame transitions. Pullback of divisor line bundles and additivity of analytic divisor line bundles therefore give the canonical bundle formula for a point blowup:
KX​≅σ∗KX​⊗OX​((n−1)E).​
(29)
For n=1 the blowup is already an isomorphism, consistently with the zero exponent.
For the projective-plane construction, an invertible linear change of homogeneous coordinates puts x=[1:0:0]. Lines through x are parametrized by [a:b]∈CP1, with equation bZ1​−aZ2​=0. Thus the required map on the punctured plane is
f([Z0​:Z1​:Z2​])=[Z1​:Z2​].
(30)
It is well-defined under rescaling and holomorphic on the covering sets Z1​=0 and Z2​=0, where its target coordinates are Z2​/Z1​ and Z1​/Z2​. These sets cover the complement of x.
The graph closure is
S={([Z0​:Z1​:Z2​],[a:b])∈CP2×CP1:Z1​b=Z2​a}.
(31)
Over the complement of x it is the graph of f. In the affine chart Z0​=0, its equation is exactly the two-dimensional local incidence model for the blowup of a complex manifold at a point, so its first projection identifies it with the required blowup globally. The second projection is a holomorphic map f​:S→CP1 extending f∘σ. Explicitly, the two blowup charts give
(z1​,z2​)=(t,tu): f​=[1:u],(z1​,z2​)=(tv,t): f​=[v:1].
(32)
These formulas agree when v=1/u and remain holomorphic at t=0. Hence the pencil extends over the exceptional divisor, where it records the projective tangent direction. This is the projective pencil resolved by a point blowup.
