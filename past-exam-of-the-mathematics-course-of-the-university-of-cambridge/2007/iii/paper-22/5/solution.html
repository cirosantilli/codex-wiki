Let n=dimC​X, and use the complex orientation. Write g for the real Riemannian metric underlying the Hermitian metric. Its fundamental form of a Hermitian manifold is
ω(u,v)=g(Ju,v),ω=2i​∑a,b​habˉ​dza∧dzˉb,
(33)
where g=Re(∑habˉ​dza⊗dzˉb) in this convention. It is real and of type (1,1), since g(Ju,Jv)=g(u,v). Choose an adapted real orthonormal frame Ej​,Fj​=JEj​ and its dual coframe ej,fj. Then ω=∑j​ej∧fj, giving the Riemannian volume form
volg​=e1∧f1∧⋯∧en∧fn=n!ωn​.​
(34)
We use the complex-linear Hodge star operator, the extension of the real metric star characterized on equal-degree forms by
α∧∗β​=⟨α,β⟩g​volg​.
(35)
It maps (p,q)-forms to (n−q,n−p)-forms and satisfies ∗∗α=(−1)p+qα. It is distinct from the conjugate-linear map α↦∗α. In the adapted coframe the star of ej∧fj is the wedge product of all the other two-dimensional factors, with positive sign. Summing proves the Hodge star of the fundamental Hermitian form formula
∗ω=(n−1)!ωn−1​.​
(36)
This computation uses no closedness assumption on ω.
Use the integrated Hermitian inner product (α,β)=∫X​α∧∗β​. The formal adjoints d∗ and ∂ˉ∗ are defined by (dα,β)=(α,d∗β) and (∂ˉα,β)=(α,∂ˉ∗β); compact manifolds here have no boundary. The Hodge Laplacian and Dolbeault Laplacian are
Δd​=dd∗+d∗d,Δ∂ˉ​=∂ˉ∂ˉ∗+∂ˉ∗∂ˉ.
(37)
The Dolbeault Hodge decomposition on a compact Hermitian manifold states that
Ap,q(X)=H∂ˉp,q​⊕∂ˉAp,q−1(X)⊕∂ˉ∗Ap,q+1(X)
(38)
is an orthogonal direct sum, with finite-dimensional H∂ˉp,q​=kerΔ∂ˉ​. Each Dolbeault cohomology class
H∂ˉp,q​(X)=ker(∂ˉ:Ap,q→Ap,q+1)/∂ˉAp,q−1
(39)
has a unique representative in H∂ˉp,q​. A form is harmonic exactly when both ∂ˉα and ∂ˉ∗α vanish, since its Laplacian inner product is the sum of their squared norms.
Now let Lα=ω∧α be the Hermitian Lefschetz operator and let Λ be its pointwise adjoint. For β of total degree r=p+q and α of degree r−2, the defining inner products give
(Lα)∧∗β​=α∧ω∧∗β​=α∧∗Λβ​.
(40)
The wedge pairing is nondegenerate, so ∗Λβ​=L∗β​. Apply another star to the degree-(r−2) form and use that the star and L commute with conjugation. This yields
Λβ=(−1)r−2∗L∗β=(−1)p+q∗L∗β.​
(41)
For degrees below two both sides vanish. The argument also identifies its bidegree as (−1,−1) and does not require the Kähler condition.
Assume now that dω=0, so the metric is a Kähler metric. Start with the supplied Kähler identities relation [Λ,∂]=i∂ˉ∗. Since Λ is real, conjugation gives [Λ,∂ˉ]=−i∂∗, hence
∂ˉ∗=−i[Λ,∂],∂∗=i[Λ,∂ˉ].
(42)
The commutators are ordinary commutators because Λ has even degree. Using ∂2=0 we obtain, by direct expansion,
∂∂ˉ∗+∂ˉ∗∂=−i(∂Λ∂−∂2Λ+Λ∂2−∂Λ∂)=0.
(43)
Conjugation gives ∂ˉ∂∗+∂∗∂ˉ=0 as well. Expanding the two unmixed Laplacians and using ∂∂ˉ=−∂ˉ∂ gives the same operator:
Δ∂​​=i(∂Λ∂ˉ−∂ˉΛ∂−∂∂ˉΛ−Λ∂∂ˉ)=Δ∂ˉ​.​
(44)
Finally expand d=∂+∂ˉ and d∗=∂∗+∂ˉ∗; the mixed terms just proved zero disappear. This derives the Kähler Laplacian identity
Δd​=Δ∂​+Δ∂ˉ​=2Δ∂ˉ​.​
(45)
The Dolbeault Laplacian preserves bidegree. Thus the complex d-harmonic forms of total degree r split into their harmonic (p,q) parts. The allowed real Hodge theorem, followed by complexification, gives
br(X)=∑p+q=r​hp,q,hp,q=dimC​H∂ˉp,q​.
(46)
The real operator Δd​ commutes with conjugation, which exchanges (p,q) and (q,p). Therefore hp,q=hq,p. For odd r, no summand has p=q, so they pair off in equal dimensions. This proves that all odd Betti numbers are even.
For 1≤k≤n, the Kähler form power ωk is closed. If it were exact, say ωk=dη, then
∫X​ωn=∫X​d(η∧ωn−k)=0
(47)
by Stokes theorem. But ωn=n!volg​ has strictly positive integral on the nonempty compact manifold. Hence the powers of a compact Kähler form have nonzero cohomology classes, and
b2k−1(X)∈2Z≥0​,b2k(X)≥1(1≤k≤n).​
(48)
For an example take the Hopf surface
H=(C2∖{0})/⟨z↦2z⟩.
(49)
The dilation action is free and properly discontinuous: on a compact subset the radii are bounded above and away from zero, so only finitely many dilations can meet it. It is holomorphic, giving a complex manifold quotient; the annulus 1≤∥z∥≤2 covers the quotient, proving compactness. The map
[z]⟼(z/∥z∥, log∥z∥modlog2)
(50)
is a diffeomorphism H≅S3×S1. The Künneth theorem gives b1(H)=1. This violates the necessary odd-degree parity, so this compact complex surface admits no Kähler metric.
