An almost complex structure is a smooth real vector bundle endomorphism J:TM→TM satisfying J2=−I. In particular, M has even real dimension 2n. Extend J complex-linearly to TC​M=TM⊗R​C. Its minimal polynomial divides (u−i)(u+i), whose roots are distinct, so the type decomposition of the complexified tangent bundle is
TC​M=T1,0M⊕T0,1M,T1,0M=ker(J−iI),T0,1M=ker(J+iI).
(1)
The smooth projections are (I−iJ)/2 and (I+iJ)/2. Complex conjugation exchanges the two rank-n eigenbundles. A (1,0)-covector annihilates T0,1M and a (0,1)-covector annihilates T1,0M. Thus the differential forms of type (p, q) are the smooth sections of
Λp,qT∗M=Λp(T1,0M)∗⊗Λq(T0,1M)∗,ΛrTC∗​M=⨁p+q=r​Λp,qT∗M.
(2)
Here the tensor product is identified with its image under the wedge product of differential forms. For a real tangent vector v, the vectors v−iJv and v+iJv exhibit the two types directly.
We now construct the almost complex structure from a decomposable volume form. Locally write Ω=θ1​∧⋯∧θn​. The nonvanishing of Ω∧Ω says that the 2n covectors θ1​,…,θn​,θ1​,…,θn​ form a complex coframe. In particular, the span of the θj​ has the intrinsic description
Wx​={α∈Tx∗​M⊗C:α∧Ωx​=0}.
(3)
Indeed, expansion in that coframe shows that every coefficient of a conjugate factor must vanish if α∧Ωx​=0. Therefore this span is independent of the chosen local factorization. The local coframes show that W is a smooth rank-n vector subbundle, and
T∗M⊗C=W⊕W.
(4)
Define JΩ∗​ to be multiplication by i on W and by −i on W. It commutes with complex conjugation, so is the complexification of a real smooth bundle endomorphism, whose dual defines JΩ​ on TM. It has square −I, and its (1,0)-covectors are exactly W. Conversely, any such almost complex structure must have this same action on the entire coframe. Thus JΩ​ exists globally and is unique, and every factor in every allowed decomposition is a (1,0)-form.
Finally suppose dΩ=0. Since θj​∧Ω=0, the exterior derivative product rule gives
0=d(θj​∧Ω)=dθj​∧Ω−θj​∧dΩ=dθj​∧Ω.
(5)
Decompose dθj​ into types (2,0), (1,1) and (0,2) using JΩ​. The first two wedge products with the (n,0)-form Ω vanish by dimension. On (0,2)-forms, wedging with Ω is injective: the monomials Ω∧θa​∧θb​ are linearly independent. For n=1 the (0,2) space is zero, so the statement still holds. Hence every dθj​ has no (0,2) component. For an arbitrary (1,0)-form α=∑j​aj​θj​, the extra terms daj​∧θj​ likewise have only types (2,0) and (1,1). The bracket and differential-form criteria for integrability proved below now give
dΩ=0 ⟹ JΩ​ is integrable.​
(6)
Local decomposability has been used essentially; the nonvanishing top-degree product alone does not provide the required coframe.
