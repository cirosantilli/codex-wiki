Let M be a compact Riemannian manifold without boundary. Let δ=d∗ be the formal L2 adjoint operator of the exterior derivative, and let ΔH​=dδ+δd be the Hodge Laplacian. Write Hr(M)=kerΔH​∩Ωr(M) for the smooth harmonic differential forms. The Hodge decomposition theorem states that this space is finite-dimensional and
Ωr(M)=Hr(M)⊕dΩr−1(M)⊕δΩr+1(M),​
(35)
an L2-orthogonal direct sum. Thus every smooth r-form is uniquely the sum of a harmonic, an exact and a coexact form; its exact and coexact components are unique, though the primitives used to write those components need not be unique. Also
Hr(M)≅HdRr​(M;R),dimHr(M)=br​(M).​
(36)
The isomorphism sends a harmonic form to its de Rham cohomology class. For example, a closed coexact component δγ must vanish because ∥δγ∥2=⟨γ,dδγ⟩=0; an exact harmonic form also vanishes by orthogonality. These facts explain uniqueness of the harmonic representative of a cohomology class.
On an oriented n-manifold the codifferential is δ=(−1)n(r+1)+1∗d∗ on r-forms. The general formulation above also holds on nonorientable Riemannian manifolds: use the globally defined Riemannian volume density for the L2 inner product and its formal adjoint, rather than require a global ordinary volume form. The closed-manifold hypothesis is essential unless suitable boundary conditions are added.
