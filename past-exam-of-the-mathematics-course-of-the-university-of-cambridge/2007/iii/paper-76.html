Use both material isotropy and material frame indifference. The polar decomposition in continuum mechanics writes F=VR, where V is the left stretch tensor. Isotropy removes the reference rotation: σ(F)=σ(V). Choose an orthonormal eigenbasis of V. A half-turn about any principal axis leaves V fixed but reverses appropriate off-diagonal entries of σ(V). Frame indifference therefore makes those entries zero. If a principal stretch is repeated, rotations in its eigenspace additionally force the stress to be isotropic there. Thus the principal stresses share the stretch axes: this proves coaxiality of isotropic elastic stress with V and with B=V2=FFT.
For simple shear, the left Cauchy-Green deformation tensor is
B=(1+γ2γ​γ1​).
(1)
Its principal axes make an angle ϑ with the coordinate axes satisfying 2cot(2ϑ)=γ when γ=0. If the two principal stresses are s1​,s2​, rotating their diagonal matrix back gives
σ11​−σ22​=(s1​−s2​)cos2ϑ,σ12​=2s1​−s2​​sin2ϑ.
(2)
Consequently the universal simple-shear normal-stress identity is
σ11​−σ22​=γσ12​.​
(3)
Equivalently, it follows immediately from the off-diagonal entry of σB−Bσ=0. At γ=0, the undeformed material has isotropic stress, so the identity holds there too. No particular elastic constitutive equation has been used.

The inextensible fibre constraint gives ∣x,1​∣=1. Write the fibre direction and its transverse unit direction as
e=(cosθ,sinθ),n=(−sinθ,cosθ).
(4)
Then x1,1​=cosθ and x2,1​=sinθ. In plane strain, incompressibility gives detF=1. Decomposing the second column of the deformation gradient along e,n therefore gives x,2​=γe+n, where γ=x,2​⋅e. Hence
F=R(θ)(10​γ1​).​
(5)
This is a local rotation followed by simple shear.
The compatibility of the deformation map requires x,12​=x,21​. Since e,j​=θ,j​n and n,j​=−θ,j​e, this is
θ,2​n=(γ,1​−θ,1​)e+γθ,1​n.
(6)
Taking components gives the compatibility relations for incompressible inextensible-fibre plane strain:
γ,1​=θ,1​,θ,2​=γθ,1​,γ=θ+f(X2​).​
(7)
Along a reference curve,
dsdθ​=θ,1​(dsdX1​​+γdsdX2​​).
(8)
Thus θ is constant when dX2​/dX1​=−1/γ. The equivalent condition dX1​+γdX2​=0 also covers γ=0. Its current tangent is proportional to F(−γ,1)T=n, so these transverse curves are straight wherever the deformation is regular.
To construct the stress with zero body force, specify the local shear stress h as the material's function or functional of γ under simple shear. The normal stresses are constraint reactions: incompressibility supplies pressure, and the inextensible fibre constraint supplies fibre tension. Write
σ=se⊗e+h(e⊗n+n⊗e)+tn⊗n.
(9)
The compatible geometry has ∇θ=κe with κ=θ,1​, so ∇⋅e=0, ∇⋅n=−κ, (e⋅∇)e=κn and (n⋅∇)e=0. Resolving ∇⋅σ=0 along the two directions gives
e⋅∇s=2κh−n⋅∇h,n⋅∇t−κt=−e⋅∇h−κs.
(10)
The triangular equilibrium construction for inextensible-fibre plane strain now determines s by integration along fibres and then t along transverse curves. Thus any regular compatible deformation has a local zero-body-force stress construction once the shear law and suitable boundary integration data are supplied. There is no additional constitutive normal-stress law to specify: the two normal components are fixed by force balance and boundary tractions. This does not assert that arbitrary separately prescribed boundary tractions can accompany an arbitrary deformation.
