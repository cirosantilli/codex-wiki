For a nonempty r-uniform set family F, write its cardinality in its unique greedy binomial representation
∣F∣=(rar​​)+(r−1ar−1​​)+⋯+(sas​​),ar​>ar−1​>⋯>as​≥s≥1.
(1)
The Kruskal-Katona theorem gives the exact minimum size of its lower shadow:
∣∂F∣≥(r−1ar​​)+(r−2ar−1​​)+⋯+(s−1as​​).​
(2)
Equality is attained by the colexicographic initial segment of the same cardinality. The empty family has empty shadow. In colexicographic order, A precedes B exactly when the largest point of A△B belongs to B. The greedy expansion exists by choosing each top argument as large as possible; Pascal's identity makes the next residual less than (j−1aj​​), forcing the next top argument below aj​. This also proves uniqueness.
Here is a proof by UV-compression of the theorem, including the step that controls the lower shadow. For disjoint nonempty sets U,V of equal size, let CU,V​ replace a member A containing V and avoiding U by A′=(A∖V)∪U only when A′ is absent. Other members stay. This UV-compression preserves cardinality. Allow only maxU<maxV, and at each step choose a changing compression with ∣U∣=∣V∣ as small as possible. Every smaller allowed UV-compression then fixes the current family.
For such a chosen UV-compression, we prove
∂(CU,V​F)⊆CU,V​(∂F).
(3)
First let S be a new shadow member, not in ∂F. It has the form A′∖{x} for a moved member A. If x∈U, choose y∈V with y=maxV when ∣V∣>1. The smaller UV-compression with sets U∖{x},V∖{y} fixes F, and therefore its target S∪{y} already belongs to F. This would put S in the old shadow, a contradiction. For ∣V∣=1 the same target is simply A, giving the same contradiction without a compression. Hence x∈/U. Now A∖{x} is an old shadow member whose compression target is S; since S was absent, it appears in the compressed shadow.
It remains to check that a surviving old shadow member is not removed on the right side. Such a removal could occur only for S containing V, avoiding U, with S′=(S∖V)∪U absent from the old shadow. A witness B=S∪{x} in the compressed family must be a retained old member, since newly moved members avoid V. If x∈/U, retention of the eligible member B forces its target S′∪{x} to be in the old family, contrary to the absence of S′ from its shadow. If x∈U, the smaller fixed compression just used forces S′∪{y} into the old family; for ∣U∣=1 this is B itself. Again there is a contradiction. This proves the containment, and hence the chosen compression cannot increase lower shadow cardinality.
Each change strictly decreases the integer potential ∑A∈F​∑a∈A​2a. The procedure therefore terminates. Its terminal family is an initial segment of colexicographic order: otherwise take an absent earlier set X and a present later set Y. The choice U=X∖Y,V=Y∖X is an allowed changing compression. Thus UV-compression transforms F into the colexicographic initial segment of the same size without increasing its lower shadow.
Finally compute that shadow. The first block comprises all r-subsets of [ar​] and contributes (r−1ar​​) shadow members. The next block has maximum point ar​+1 and its remaining (r−1) points form a smaller colexicographic initial segment. Shadow members omitting ar​+1 are already in the first block's shadow; those containing it are counted by the lower shadow of that smaller initial segment. Recursion through the binomial representation gives precisely the displayed sum. This completes the proof.
To deduce the Erdős-Ko-Rado theorem, let 1≤r≤n/2 and let F be an intersecting family. Put k=n−r and take the complement family B={[n]∖A:A∈F}. Its rank-r lower shadow, obtained by deleting k−r points, is disjoint from F: membership of A in that shadow would give A∩C=∅ for some C∈F. If ∣F∣>(r−1n−1​)=(kn−1​), iterating the proved Kruskal-Katona theorem gives this shadow at least as many members as the corresponding colex shadow. The colex family contains every k-set of [n−1] and a further k-set containing n. Its rank-r shadow therefore contains every r-set of [n−1] and at least one more r-set containing n. This applies also when k=r, with no shadow operation. The two disjoint families would consequently have total size greater than
(r−1n−1​)+(rn−1​)=(rn​),
(4)
a contradiction. Thus ∣F∣≤(r−1n−1​)​, attained by all r-sets containing a fixed point.
For nonisomorphic colex shadow minimizers of rank two, take n=4 and F={12,23,34,14}. Its shadow has four singletons, as does the initial four-set colex segment I={12,13,23,14}. The first family is the edge set of a cycle graph; its four point degrees are all two. The second has point degrees 3,2,2,1, so no permutation makes them isomorphic.
For rank three, take n=5 and
F={124,125,134,135,234,235},I={123,124,134,234,125,135}.
(5)
The first lower shadow comprises the three pairs in {1,2,3} and the six pairs joining that set to {4,5}. The second shadow comprises every pair on [5] except 45. Both therefore have size nine. Their point-degree multisets are respectively {4,4,4,3,3} and {5,4,4,3,2}, so again the two families are not isomorphic. These examples can be embedded in larger ground sets by adding unused points.
