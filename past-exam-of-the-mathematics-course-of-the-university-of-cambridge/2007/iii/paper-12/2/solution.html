There is a genuine qualification missing from the printed bound. With n=3,r=1,m=4, the family of three singletons has distinct intersections of size zero, which is not congruent to r modulo m. Nevertheless its size is three, whereas (m−1n​)=(33​)=1. Thus that bound cannot hold without a restriction on the parameters. The valid prime-power modular intersection bound is
∣F∣≤(min(r,m−1)n​).​
(6)
In particular the printed form holds when m−1≤r.
Put m=pa with p a prime number, and d=m−1. If r<d, the corrected bound is simply the total number (rn​) of r-sets. Assume henceforth d≤r. For an integer z, consider the residue detector
f(z)=(dz−r+d​)(modp).
(7)
For nonnegative upper arguments, the Lucas theorem and the fact that every base-p digit of d equals p−1 show that f(z) is one when z≡r(modm) and zero otherwise. The assertion also holds for negative upper arguments, using integer-valued generalized binomial coefficients. Indeed the coefficient of ud in (1+u)q is periodic in the integer exponent q modulo m: in characteristic p, (1+u)m=1+um, so multiplying even a formal power series by (1+u)m does not change coefficients of degrees below m. Reduce any exponent modulo m to obtain the stated detector.
For each A∈F, apply that detector to ∣A∩B∣. Its evaluations on B∈F form the identity matrix, since the diagonal intersection is r and every off-diagonal intersection has a different residue. The detector is represented on Boolean vectors by the multilinear polynomial
fA​(x)=∑S⊆A∣S∣≤d​​(d−∣S∣d−r​)∏i∈S​xi​(modp).
(8)
To check this expression, evaluate at the characteristic vector of B: summing by j=∣S∣ gives ∑j​(j∣A∩B∣​)(d−jd−r​)=(d∣A∩B∣+d−r​) by the coefficient identity for (1+u)∣A∩B∣(1+u)d−r.
Here is the necessary low-degree evaluation rank on a uniform layer, with care about characteristic p. Let M be the integer incidence matrix whose rows are subsets S of size at most d, whose columns are all r-sets B, and whose entries are 1S⊆B​. Over the rational numbers, for ∣S∣=j≤d its row satisfies
MS​=(d−jr−j​)−1∑T⊇S∣T∣=d​​MT​.
(9)
Thus its rational matrix rank is at most (dn​). Every larger square minor has integer determinant zero; reducing those determinants modulo p gives rankFp​​M≤(dn​) too. This argument does not divide by a potentially zero element of the finite field. The identity evaluation matrix (fA​(1B​))A,B∈F​ factors through these incidence rows, so its rank ∣F∣ is at most (dn​). This proves the qualified bound.
Choose the two-applications alternative. First, the polynomial method in combinatorics yields a constructive lower bound for a diagonal Ramsey number. Let p≥3 be prime, set N=p3,r=p2−1, and use all r-subsets of [N] as vertices of a complete graph. Color a pair red when its intersection size is −1 modulo p, and blue otherwise. A blue clique has size at most D=(p−1N​) by the proved bound with modulus p. In a red clique, all distinct intersections belong to
L={p−1,2p−1,…,(p−1)p−1}.
(10)
For each vertex A of that clique, the polynomial PA​(x)=∏ℓ∈L​(∑i∈A​xi​−ℓ) vanishes at all the other vertices' characteristic vectors and is nonzero at its own. Replace repeated powers xij​ by xi​ on Boolean vectors. Its degree is at most p−1, so the rational version of the same incidence-rank argument bounds a red clique by D as well. Therefore this two-coloring has neither color containing a clique of size D+1, and
R(D+1,D+1)>(p2−1p3​),D=(p−1p3​).​
(11)
The usual estimates (N/k)k≤(kN​)≤(eN/k)k give logD=Θ(plogp) and log(p2−1p3​)=Θ(p2logp). Thus along this explicit sequence the diagonal Ramsey number exceeds exp(c(logs)2/loglogs) for some absolute c>0. This is substantially larger than any fixed power of s.
Second, obtain an exponential lower bound for the chromatic number of Euclidean space. Let N=4p−1,r=2p−1, and join two r-subsets exactly when their intersection has size p−1. An independent set in this graph avoids that intersection. Among the possible distinct intersection sizes 0,…,2p−2, the only one congruent to r modulo p is p−1. Hence every independent set has size at most (p−14p−1​). Embed each vertex as vA​=1A​/2p​ in Euclidean space. Then
∥vA​−vB​∥2=2p2r−2∣A∩B∣​,
(12)
so the graph's edges have exactly Euclidean distance one. Each color class must be an independent set, giving
χ(R4p−1)≥(p−14p−1​)(2p−14p−1​)​.​
(13)
The Stirling formula makes the logarithm of this ratio N(log2−h(1/4))+o(N), where h(x)=−xlogx−(1−x)log(1−x). Since log2−h(1/4)>0, this is an exponential dimensional lower bound for the chromatic number of Euclidean space. Both applications lie in the valid parameter range of the proved modular bound.
