library(mgcv) loads the fitting package; gam fits a Gaussian generalised additive model with the printed identity link. The smooth specification s(x, bs="cr") uses a penalized cubic regression spline. This is a finite-dimensional smooth basis, rather than a full interpolating spline with a free value at all 401 observations. The fitted model is
Yi​=β0​+f(xi​)+εi​,εi​ independent N(0,σ2),i=1∑401​f(xi​)=0.​
(35)
Here f(x)=∑r=1q​θr​Br​(x) in the centred cubic regression-spline basis, with its curvature penalized through λ∫(f′′)2. The intercept is estimated separately. In the displayed fit the smooth test space has rank 9, consistent with nine smooth coefficients after the centring constraint, while penalization lowers its effective dimension.
The intercept estimate is −0.13241, with standard error 0.05426, t=−2.44 and p=0.0151. Because the smooth is centred over the observations, the intercept is the overall fitted average, not necessarily the fitted response at x=0. Its test compares this average with zero. The significance-code legend maps progressively smaller p-values to stars; stars are an annotation, not an effect-size measure.
The smooth has 8.237 effective degrees of freedom, displaying substantial nonlinearity: a centred purely linear term would use one. Est.rank=9 reports the rank used for its approximate significance calculation. The p-value below 2×10−16 is very strong evidence that the mean depends on x, against a zero smooth term. It does not mean the fitted curve is the exact generating function, or that lack of fit has been tested. The total effective dimension including the intercept is 1+8.237=9.237.
The adjusted coefficient of determination R2 of 0.983 and deviance explained of 98.4% indicate that nearly all variation about the response mean is explained. For this Gaussian model, deviance explained is 1−RSS/SST. The scale estimate 0.96737 estimates residual variance, corresponding to a residual standard deviation about 0.984. The GCV score 0.99018 is the prediction-oriented criterion used for smoothing selection, not a probability. It is consistent with the reported scale and effective dimension: 0.96737×401/(401−9.237)≃0.99018.
In the PDF's plot, the solid curve is the estimated centred smooth f(x), and the dashed curves show pointwise uncertainty around that smooth, rather than prediction intervals for new noisy responses. The label s(x,8.24) gives its rounded effective degrees of freedom. The rug marks the observed covariate locations. Reading the endpoints gives
−10≤x≤10.​

The smooth is increasing with an almost flat middle and one inflection, resembling a cubic. A simpler model to investigate is ordinary Gaussian polynomial regression
Yi​=β0​+β1​xi​+β2​xi2​+β3​xi3​+εi​.​
(37)
It uses four mean coefficients rather than about nine effective ones. The apparent near-odd shape also makes β0​+β3​x3 a plausible further reduction, but the linear and quadratic terms should be checked rather than discarded solely from the picture. The figure suggests this alternative; it does not supply coefficients or establish the true simulation model.
