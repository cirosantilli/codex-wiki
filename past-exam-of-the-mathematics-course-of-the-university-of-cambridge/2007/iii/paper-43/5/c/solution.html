With the convention that the residual term is a sum rather than an average, the penalised least squares criterion is
Jλ​(g)=i=1∑n​{yi​−g(xi​)}2+λ∫x1​xn​​{g′′(x)}2dx,λ>0.​
(31)
Minimize over functions whose first derivative is absolutely continuous and whose second derivative is square-integrable, that is, the usual Sobolev space H2[x1​,xn​]. The cubic smoothing spline g​λ​ is its minimizer, with natural boundary conditions and linear continuation outside the data range. It is a natural cubic spline with possible knots at the observed xi​, but usually does not interpolate the noisy yi​.
To see why the minimizer is a natural spline, for any g take the natural cubic spline interpolant s through its values g(xi​) and write h=g−s. Then h(xi​)=0. Integrating ∫s′′h′′ by parts on every knot interval twice gives zero: the terms involving h vanish at knots, the terms involving h′ cancel because s′′ is continuous there, and the outer terms vanish by the natural boundary conditions. Hence
∫(g′′)2=∫(s′′)2+∫(h′′)2≥∫(s′′)2,
(32)
while their residual sums of squares agree. Replacing g by s never increases the criterion. This proves the minimum roughness property of the natural cubic spline interpolant needed here.
For fitted values ui​=s(xi​), write ∫(s′′)2=uTKu, where K is positive semidefinite and has the sampled affine functions as its two-dimensional nullspace. The finite-dimensional minimization becomes ∥y−u∥2+λuTKu. Its strictly positive definite Hessian is 2(I+λK), so it has the unique solution
u=Aλ​y,Aλ​=(I+λK)−1.​
(33)
This is the roughness-matrix formula for a natural cubic smoothing spline. Larger λ penalizes curvature more strongly. As λ↓0 the fit approaches interpolation; as λ→∞ it approaches the ordinary least-squares affine line.
