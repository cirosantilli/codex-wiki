An embedding-projection pair consists of continuous maps e:D→E, p:E→D with pe=idD​ and ep≤idE​ pointwise. Thus e is an order embedding and ep approximates an element of the larger domain from below. Such maps are automatically strict: applying the inequality at ⊥E​ gives e(⊥D​)≤ep(⊥E​)≤⊥E​, and applying p then gives p(⊥E​)=⊥D​.
Suppose (ϕn−1​,ψn−1​) is such a pair. Composition with fixed continuous maps is continuous on the pointwise function space of complete partial orders, so both proposed lifted maps are continuous. Their composite on the smaller function space is
ψn​ϕn​(f)=ψn−1​ϕn−1​fψn−1​ϕn−1​=f.
(25)
Writing q=ϕn−1​ψn−1​≤id, the other composite is ϕn​ψn​(g)=qgq≤g: for every x, q(g(qx))≤g(qx)≤g(x) by monotonicity. Thus the recursion produces embedding-projection pairs at every stage.
Here is an explicit inverse-limit solution of the reflexive domain equation. Form
D∞​={(x0​,x1​,…):xn​∈Dn​, ψn​(xn+1​)=xn​ for all n},
(26)
ordered coordinatewise. The bottom sequence is coherent by strictness. A directed family has coordinatewise suprema, and continuity of each ψn​ preserves the coherence condition. Thus D∞​ is a pointed complete partial order.
Write ϕm,n​:Dm​→Dn​ for the composite embeddings when m≤n, and ψm,n​:Dn​→Dm​ for the corresponding composite projections, taking both as identities if m=n. Define Pn​:D∞​→Dn​ by Pn​(x)=xn​, and define En​:Dn​→D∞​ by
(En​a)m​={ψm,n​(a),ϕn,m​(a),​m≤n,m≥n.​
(27)
The pair identities make this sequence coherent, and its coordinates are continuous. They also give Pn​En​=idDn​​ and En​Pn​≤idD∞​​. Put Qn​=En​Pn​. Since En​=En+1​ϕn​ and Pn​=ψn​Pn+1​,
Qn​=En+1​(ϕn​ψn​)Pn+1​≤Qn+1​.
(28)
At coordinate m, Qn​x equals xm​ once n≥m, so
Qn​↑idD∞​​.​
(29)
This exhibits the required embedding-projection pair for every finite stage.
To prove the function-space isomorphism, observe that xn+1​∈Dn+1​ is itself a continuous function fn​:Dn​→Dn​. Coherence says
fn​=ψn​fn+1​ϕn​.
(30)
Conversely such a coherent function family determines an element of D∞​, with initial coordinate x0​=ψ0​(f0​). Define its action on the limit by
A(x)=supn​En​fn​Pn​ ∈[D∞​→D∞​].
(31)
The maps inside this supremum increase: substituting coherence expresses the nth one as En+1​qn​fn+1​qn​Pn+1​ with qn​=ϕn​ψn​≤id, which is below the next map. The supremum is continuous by the function-space result in Question 3.
In the other direction, for a continuous F:D∞​→D∞​, define fn​=Pn​FEn​. These satisfy the same coherence equation. Hence define B(F)∈D∞​ by
B(F)n+1​=Pn​FEn​,B(F)0​=ψ0​(P0​FE0​).
(32)
For a coherent family, restricting supn​En​fn​Pn​ back to stage m gives fm​: every term with n≥m restricts exactly to ψm,n​fn​ϕm,n​=fm​, and the earlier terms are below it. Thus BA is the identity.
Conversely,
AB(F)=supn​Qn​FQn​=F.
(33)
For the last equality, Qn​↑id and continuity give, at every x, supn​Qn​F(Qn​x)=supn,m​Qn​F(Qm​x)=F(x); the diagonal is cofinal because the double family increases in both indices. Both A and B are continuous, by coordinatewise evaluation, continuous composition and directed suprema. Therefore
D∞​≅[D∞​→D∞​]​
(34)
as complete partial orders with continuous maps, not merely as sets. This supplies the extensional reflexive object needed for a beta-eta model, together with all the stated finite-stage pairs.
