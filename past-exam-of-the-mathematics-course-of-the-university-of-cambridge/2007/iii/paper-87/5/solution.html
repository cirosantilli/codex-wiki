Write products of endomorphisms as composition, with the rightmost map applied first. Because 1 is terminal, the only hom-sets that can contain multiple arrows are Hom(1,D) and End(D). If the latter contained only the identity, any point p:1→D would have p!=idD​, where !:D→1. Then any other point q would satisfy q=p!q=p. Thus the category would be a preorder. The hypothesis therefore implies ∣End(D)∣>1.
If the product object D×D were 1, its universal property applied to arrows from D would give End(D)2≅Hom(D,1), a singleton, which is impossible. Since there are only two objects, D×D=D. Similarly, if DD=1, the exponential adjunction at 1 would give
Hom(1,1)≅Hom(1×D,D)≅End(D),
(35)
again impossible. Hence D×D=DD=D as chosen objects.
Let M=End(D), let π,π′ be the product projections, and let ε:DD×D→D be evaluation, all now endomorphisms. Pairing is ⟨−,−⟩, and for x:D×D→D write x∗ for its currying. The product identities and the two inverse exponential identities give
π⟨x,y⟩=x,π′⟨x,y⟩=y,⟨πz,π′z⟩=z,
(36)
ε⟨x∗π,π′⟩=x,(ε⟨yπ,π′⟩)∗=y.
(37)
This is the structure of a cartesian closed monoid.
For the converse, start only from the monoid and these identities. The first three make pairing a bijection from M2 to M, inverse to z↦(πz,π′z). In particular
⟨x,y⟩z=⟨xz,yz⟩,⟨π,π′⟩=1M​,
(38)
as follows by applying both projections and using uniqueness. Define U(y)=ε⟨yπ,π′⟩. The last two identities say that U and (−)∗ are inverse bijections. These facts also derive, rather than assume, the naturality law
(x⟨zπ,π′⟩)∗=x∗z.​
(39)
Indeed U(x∗z)=ε⟨x∗zπ,π′⟩=U(x∗)⟨zπ,π′⟩=x⟨zπ,π′⟩; applying the inverse bijection proves the law.
Put e=(π′)∗. The law applied to x=π′ gives, for every z∈M,
ez=(π′⟨zπ,π′⟩)∗=(π′)∗=e.
(40)
Taking z=e proves e2=e. This stronger absorption identity is what makes the split object terminal.
Assume first that M is nontrivial; the degenerate case is handled below. Construct the required category using the two idempotents d=1M​ and e, as a two-object part of the Karoubi envelope. Call their objects D and 1. A morphism from the object with idempotent a to the object with idempotent b is an element f∈M satisfying bfa=f. Composition is monoid multiplication, and the identity on each object is its idempotent. These laws are well-defined and associative. Its hom-sets are concretely
Hom(D,D)=M,Hom(D,1)={e},Hom(1,1)={e},Hom(1,D)={f:fe=f}.
(41)
For example, ef=e for every f, so the condition for D→1 forces f=e. Thus 1 is terminal, and the original monoid appears unchanged at D.
Set D×D=D with projections π,π′ and the given pairing. The product property works for arrows from either object: for global arrows x,y satisfying xe=x, ye=y, pairing also satisfies ⟨x,y⟩e=⟨x,y⟩. The original projection identities give existence and uniqueness. Products with 1 are the other object, with its identity and the unique terminal arrow as projections; 1×1=1.
For the exponential set DD=D with evaluation ε. For a parameter object D, the given (−)∗ and U already establish the universal property. We must additionally verify it for parameter object 1. An arrow f:1×D=D→D is an arbitrary element of M. Define its transpose by
κ(f)=(fπ′)∗.
(42)
Naturality gives κ(f)z=κ(f) for every z, because fπ′⟨zπ,π′⟩=fπ′. In particular κ(f)e=κ(f), so it really is an arrow 1→D.
Its uncurrying is ε⟨κ(f),1M​⟩=f. To see this, start from U(κ(f))=fπ′ and compose with ⟨e,1M​⟩. Pairing naturality, π′⟨e,1M​⟩=1M​, and the absorption property of κ(f) give precisely the claimed equality. Conversely, if g:1→D satisfies ge=g, then gz=gez=ge=g for every z. Therefore
κ(ε⟨g,1M​⟩)=(ε⟨gπ′,π′⟩)∗=(ε⟨gπ,π′⟩)∗=g.
(43)
Thus transpose and uncurrying are inverse for the terminal parameter object too. This verifies the full exponential property, rather than only its endomorphism instance.
The remaining exponentials are D1=D, 1D=1, and 11=1, by the unit product law and terminality. Hence the constructed two-object category is cartesian closed and End(D)=M.
For nontrivial M, the constructed D is nonterminal because it has more than one endomorphism, and e=1M​ by the absorption identity. This is the case corresponding to the forward non-preorder hypothesis.
If M has one element, all the algebraic identities still hold, but splitting e=1M​ alone would make the original object terminal. To satisfy the literal converse's requirement of a nonterminal object, handle this case separately: take the two-object poset category D<1. Products are meets, DD=1, 1D=1, D1=D, and 11=1, which verify the exponential adjunction on this two-element order. It is cartesian closed, D is nonterminal, and its one-element endomorphism monoid is M. It is a preorder, so it does not recover the forward non-preorder setting or the self-exponential equality. Thus the converse's stated endomorphism realization works also in the degenerate case, while the stronger non-preorder reconstruction uses nontrivial M.
