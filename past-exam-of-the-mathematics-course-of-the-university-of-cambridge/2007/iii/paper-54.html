Use the mostly-positive target Minkowski spacetime metric and a closed string coordinate 0≤σ<2π. In conformal gauge, variation of the Polyakov action gives (∂τ2​−∂σ2​)Xμ=0. The compact coordinate, denoted X25, can change by 2πRw around the closed string, with w∈Z. Write wμ=wδμ,25. Integrating the two chiral wave solutions and expanding their periodic parts gives the closed-string mode expansion
Xμ=xμ+α′pμτ+Rwμσ+i2α′​​∑n=0​n1​(αnμ​e−in(τ−σ)+αnμ​e−in(τ+σ)).
(1)
Reality imposes α−n​=(αn​)∗ and the analogous condition in the other chirality. The classical compact momentum is continuous; quantization of a wavefunction on the circle subsequently gives p25=n/R with n∈Z. The momentum and winding modes are most conveniently packaged as
pR​=Rn​−α′wR​,pL​=Rn​+α′wR​,α025​=2α′​​pR​,α025​=2α′​​pL​.
(2)
For noncompact directions both zero modes are α′/2​pμ. Thus the formula includes both independent string oscillator families and all compact topological sectors.
Define the worldsheet stress-energy tensor by θab​=−2(−γ)−1/2δI/δγab. Its normalization and components are

The target momentum includes its timelike component, so this is not a positive particle-energy formula. On the classical constraint surface the reparameterization Hamiltonian vanishes; target energy is the separate charge p0. The difference L0​−L0​=0 imposes the residual spatial constraint.
In the quantum theory the same quadratic expressions require normal ordering: [αmμ​,αnν​]=mημνδm+n,0​ makes different orderings differ by divergent constants. Their regularized finite remainder is the normal-ordering constant of a string, or string intercept. The Virasoro central extension also appears. In the critical 26-dimensional bosonic string theory, the 24 transverse string oscillators give one-unit string intercept in each chirality. For example their regulated chiral vacuum energy is 24∑k>0​k/2=−1 using zeta function regularization. The physical zero-mode constraints are (L0​−1)∣Ψ⟩=(L0​−1)∣Ψ⟩=0.
Let M be the mass seen in the remaining 25 noncompact dimensions. Substituting the compact zero modes in these two constraints gives
M2=pR2​+α′4​(N−1)=pL2​+α′4​(N−1).
(6)
Taking their mean and difference yields
M2=R2n2​+α′2w2R2​+α′2​(N+N−2),N−N+nw=0.​
(7)
This sign of compact-circle closed-string level matching follows from assigning tildes to the left-moving sector. Under T-duality, set R′=α′/R, n′=w, w′=n. Then pL′​=pL​ and pR′​=−pR​, so reversing the compact right-moving string oscillators implements the transformation while leaving N,N unchanged. Both the mass equation and closed-string level matching are invariant. This proves invariance of the full closed string spectrum, including its string oscillator degeneracies, rather than only the zero-mode contribution.
A D-brane with p spatial worldvolume directions, called a Dp-brane, is a (p+1)-dimensional timelike submanifold on which open string endpoints have Neumann boundary conditions tangentially and Dirichlet boundary conditions transversely. Apply T-duality to the compact coordinate by X=XL​−XR​. The differential relations
∂τ​X=∂σ​X,∂σ​X=∂τ​X
(8)
interchange the two endpoint conditions. Consequently a Dp-brane wrapping the original circle becomes a D(p−1)-brane localized on the dual circle. Its compact Wilson line specifies that position. This is T-duality of D-brane boundary conditions.
The separated-brane configuration must instead have the circle transverse to the D-branes. Two parallel D-branes wrapping the same circle cannot be separated along that worldvolume direction. Thus the latter part describes a new orientation, with both endpoints subject to compact Dirichlet boundary conditions. If their positions differ by L, an oriented Open string stretched between D-branes has displacement
Lw​=L+2πRw,w∈Z.
(9)
On 0≤σ≤π, its compact classical solution has a linear part y1​+Lw​σ/π, plus sine modes vanishing at the endpoints. This adds Lw2​/(4π2α′) to the Virasoro generator zero mode. Thus, for momentum p along the D-branes,
Ew2​=p2+(2πα′Lw​​)2+α′N−1​,E0,w2​=(2πα′Lw​​)2−α′1​.​
(10)
The second equation is the quantum rest-energy squared of the string oscillator ground state; the smallest one uses the shortest allowed compact separation ∣Lw​∣. Classically the straight string energy is T∣Lw​∣=∣Lw​∣/(2πα′). The quantum ground state is a tachyon if ∣Lw​∣<2πα′​, so in that regime it is an instability rather than a state with real rest energy. Dropping the string intercept would describe the classical stretched string, not its quantum ground state.
The transverse-circle T-duality changes these two D-branes to D(p+1)-branes wrapping the dual circle. Their positions become the eigenvalues Ai​=yi​/(2πα′) of a constant gauge connection. The Wilson loop phases are ei2πR′Ai​=eiyi​/R. A string connecting D-brane 1 to D-brane 2 has dual compact momentum
p25′​=R′w​+A2​−A1​=2πα′L+2πRw​.
(11)
Its momentum contribution to E2 is exactly the former stretching term, including every winding sector. Qualitatively the two geometrically coincident wrapped D-branes are distinguished by their Wilson lines, and their connecting strings have shifted momentum quantization. This is how brane separation becomes a Wilson line.

A spurious string state is a Virasoro descendant orthogonal to every physical string state: if ∣s⟩=∑n>0​L−n​∣χn​⟩, then ⟨Φ∣s⟩=0 follows from Ln​∣Φ⟩=0 and Ln†​=L−n​. A null string state is a spurious state that is itself physical. It consequently has zero norm and is removed in the null-state quotient of a string. [Lm​,Ln​]=(m−n)Lm+n​+12d​(m3−m)δm+n,0​,[Lm​,αnμ​]=−nαm+nμ​.

It is therefore physical for string intercept one and spurious by construction. In particular ∥n1​∥2=⟨χ1​∣2L0​∣χ1​⟩=0. This proves the requested level-one descendant is null.

For an oriented Euclidean string worldsheet with metric hab​, use the string nonlinear sigma model action
SE​=4πα′1​∫d2σ[h​habGμν​(X)∂a​Xμ∂b​Xν+iϵabBμν​(X)∂a​Xμ∂b​Xν+α′h​Φ(X)R(2)(h)].
(28)
Here ϵab is an antisymmetric density with ϵ12=1. The factor i in the Euclidean Kalb–Ramond field term follows from continuing the real Lorentzian two-form coupling; reversing orientation or the convention for B reverses its sign. The conventional positive Euclidean dilaton coupling is being used. With worldsheet boundaries its curvature coupling also needs ∫∂Σ​Φkds/(2π), where k is the boundary geodesic curvature, to have the proper topological normalization for constant Φ.
All three terms are invariant under worldsheet diffeomorphisms. Target-coordinate changes act on X,G,B,Φ by their ordinary scalar and tensor transformation rules, making the target description covariant as well. For the two-form there is also the target gauge symmetry B↦B+dΛ. Its change in the closed-worldsheet action is an integral of an exact two-form and hence zero. With boundaries it is instead i∫∂Σ​X∗Λ/(2πα′); an endpoint gauge field with δA=−Λ/(2πα′) cancels it.
Under a local Weyl transformation hab​↦e2ωhab​, the metric term is invariant because the two-dimensional factors h​ and hab cancel. The B term is independent of h and is separately invariant. The curvature transforms as
h​R(2)⟼h​(R(2)−2∇h2​ω).
(29)
On a closed surface the dilaton variation is consequently
δω​SΦ​=−2π1​∫h​Φ(X)∇h2​ω=−2π1​∫h​ω∇h2​Φ(X).
(30)
Thus a general nonconstant dilaton term is not separately invariant under Weyl transformations. Its variation is the Weyl variation of a nonconstant dilaton coupling; it vanishes for constant Φ after the boundary completion where necessary.
For Φ=Φ0​, the Gauss-Bonnet theorem gives SΦ​=Φ0​χ(Σ). In the Euclidean path integral the resulting weight is
e−SΦ​=gs−χ(Σ)​,gs​=eΦ0​.​
(31)
For a connected orientable genus-g surface with b boundaries, χ=2−2g−b, so the weight is gs2g+b−2​. A closed-string handle therefore adds two powers of string coupling. External vertex normalizations add their own coupling factors; they are distinct from this dilaton Euler-characteristic weighting. The constant dilaton controls the string genus expansion, rather than adding a force to the classical embedding equation.
Quantization treats the target fields as position-dependent couplings of a two-dimensional interacting quantum field theory. Expanding X around a background embedding gives fluctuation vertices involving target curvature and derivatives of B and Φ. Short-distance contractions require local counterterms, so the target fields become renormalized couplings. In particular a curvature vertex contracted once yields a Ricci tensor counterterm proportional to α′Rμν​. Each additional sigma-model loop brings another power of α′ and more target derivatives. The expansion is useful when the target variation scale is large compared with α′​; it is not the separate expansion in powers of gs​.
After gauge fixing, a worldsheet Weyl anomaly would make amplitudes depend on the discarded local conformal factor. Quantum consistency therefore requires the trace anomaly to vanish, including the reparameterization worldsheet ghost field contribution. In a conventional renormalization scheme the resulting bosonic sigma-model Weyl anomaly coefficients start as
β​μνG​β​μνB​β​Φ​=α′[Rμν​−41​Hμρσ​Hν​ρσ+2∇μ​∇ν​Φ]+O(α′2),=α′[−21​∇ρHρμν​+(∇ρΦ)Hρμν​]+O(α′2),=6d−26​+α′[(∇Φ)2−21​∇2Φ−241​Hμνρ​Hμνρ]+O(α′2),H=dB.​
(32)
The barred quantities include the dilaton stress-energy tensor improvement; ordinary coupling sigma-model beta functions can differ by field redefinitions or target diffeomorphisms. Setting these coefficients to zero gives differential equations for the background fields order by order, including the metric gravitational equation and the antisymmetric-field equation. These conventions for the leading coefficients are described in the background-field lecture notes. Higher orders supply higher-curvature and higher-derivative corrections. This explains how spacetime dynamics follows from quantum gauge consistency of the worldsheet, not from asking only for a classical stationary worldsheet in an arbitrarily chosen background.
The apparently noninvariant dilaton term is essential outside the critical dimension because its stress-energy tensor improvement can cancel the quantum central charge mismatch. A flat constant-dilaton background has matter central charge d and worldsheet ghost field central charge −26. If d=26, changing the constant value of Φ only changes gs​ and cannot cure that mismatch.
A concrete solution is the linear dilaton conformal field theory with G=η, B=0, and Φ=Φ0​+Vμ​Xμ. Its metric and antisymmetric-field anomaly coefficients vanish, while the scalar condition is
V2=6α′26−d​.​
(33)
One can also see this cancellation directly from the improved holomorphic stress-energy tensor. With the positive Euclidean curvature convention above,
T(z)=−α′1​:∂X⋅∂X:+V⋅∂2X.
(34)
The free-field contraction is Xμ(z)Xν(w)∼−α′ημνlog(z−w)/2. Differentiating it twice at each point gives a fourth-order pole 3α′V2/(z−w)4 from the improvement. Since the fourth-order stress-tensor pole is c/2, the matter central charge becomes c=d+6α′V2=26. The worldsheet ghost field contribution then cancels it. This is why a classical Weyl variation need not mean inconsistency: it can cancel the quantum variation of the other terms. For d<26 the required gradient is spacelike; for d>26 it is timelike in the Lorentzian target theory. Such backgrounds are not flat constant-dilaton Minkowski vacua with full target Lorentz symmetry, nor does a central charge cancellation by itself prove stability or unitarity of every noncritical background. If the conformal factor is instead retained dynamically, its Liouville field theory supplies the corresponding noncritical description.

MNS,02​=−2α′1​=−1(α′=1/2).​
(46)
It is a spacetime scalar tachyon. The first excitation ψ−1/2i​∣0;p⟩ has N=1/2 and is massless, with eight vector particle polarizations. Covariantly its particle polarization obeys p⋅ϵ=0 modulo the longitudinal null particle polarization, leaving the same eight degrees of freedom.
The R sector oscillator vacuum is also massless, but it is degenerate because {ψ0μ​,ψ0ν​}=ημν. The operators Γμ=2​ψ0μ​ give the target Clifford algebra, and G0​∣u;p⟩=0 becomes pμ​Γμu=0. At nonzero null momentum this Dirac equation leaves sixteen unprojected on-shell spinor states. Equivalently the eight transverse zero modes give the sixteen-dimensional spin representation 8s​⊕8c​ of SO(8). Thus the Ramond ground state describes spacetime fermions, not a single scalar oscillator vacuum.
To define the GSO projection explicitly, let fNS​ count excited NS fermions and give its vacuum even parity. Then
PNS​=21​(1−(−1)fNS​).
(47)
It retains odd fermion number, removes the tachyon, and retains the eight massless vector particle polarizations. In the Ramond sector let Γ∗​ be transverse chirality and fR​ count nonzero-mode fermions. Choose one consistent chirality sign s=±1 and use
PR​=21​(1+sΓ∗​(−1)fR​).
(48)
Each operator squares to itself. At the ground level PR​ retains one eight-dimensional spinor chirality, so the retained spectrum has eight massless bosonic and eight massless fermionic states. The parity convention can instead assign the NS vacuum odd parity and write a plus projector; the retained states are identical.
Equality continues at every mass level. Let q count α′M2, suppressing common momentum and endpoint-label factors, and set D(q)=∏n≥1​(1−qn)8. The projected string oscillator generating functions are
ZNS​(q)=2D(q)q−1/2​[∏r≥1/2​(1+qr)8−∏r≥1/2​(1−qr)8],ZR​(q)=D(q)8​∏n≥1​(1+qn)8.
(49)
The Ramond factor eight follows because either chirality projector selects eight ground states for every nonzero-oscillator parity. The Jacobi abstruse identity makes the bracket equal to 16q1/2∏n≥1​(1+qn)8, so
ZNS​(q)=ZR​(q).​
(50)
This proves level-by-level equality of bosonic and fermionic multiplicities. The appropriate spacetime supercharge from an RNS spin field relates the two sectors; the projection ensures that it acts within the retained state space. These sector and chirality conventions agree with the primary superstring lecture notes. Equal total counts alone are not the definition of the projection; they follow from the stated parity selection and the string oscillator identity.
