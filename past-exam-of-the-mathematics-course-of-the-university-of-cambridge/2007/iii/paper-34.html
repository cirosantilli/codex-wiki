Use base-two logarithms, so Von Neumann entropy is measured in bits, and set 0log0=0. Write D(ρ∥σ)=Trρ(log2​ρ−log2​σ) for the quantum relative entropy, the quantity denoted S(ρ∥σ) in the paper. It is +∞ unless the support of a positive operator ρ lies within that of σ.
We first establish nonnegativity of quantum relative entropy, including its equality condition. Let ρ=∑i:ri​>0​ri​∣ui​⟩⟨ui​∣ and σ=∑j​sj​∣vj​⟩⟨vj​∣, and suppose the support condition holds. Put qi​=⟨ui​∣σ∣ui​⟩>0. Concavity of the scalar logarithm gives
⟨ui​∣logσ∣ui​⟩=∑j​∣⟨ui​∣vj​⟩∣2logsj​≤logqi​.
(1)
Terms with sj​=0 have zero weight here. Working first with natural logarithms, the inequality −lnx≥1−x yields
(ln2)D(ρ∥σ)≥∑i:ri​>0​ri​lnqi​ri​​≥∑i:ri​>0​(ri​−qi​)≥0,
(2)
since the qi​ sum to at most Trσ=1. Equality forces qi​=ri​ for every positive-weight eigenvector, no remaining weight of σ outside their span, and equality in the logarithm's strict concavity. The latter makes each ui​ an eigenvector of σ with eigenvalue qi​. Thus D(ρ∥σ)=0 exactly when ρ=σ.
For the bipartite density operator,
suppρAB​⊆suppρA​⊗suppρB​.
(3)
Indeed, a projector onto the kernel of ρA​, tensored with IB​, has zero expectation in ρAB​. Positivity implies that ρAB​ annihilates its range: Tr(ρAB​P)=∥ρAB1/2​P∥HS2​=0. The same argument applies to B. Hence the quantum relative entropy below is finite. On this support,
log(ρA​⊗ρB​)=(logρA​)⊗IB​+IA​⊗logρB​.
(4)
Using the partial trace to evaluate the two local terms gives
D(ρAB​∥ρA​⊗ρB​)=S(ρA​)+S(ρB​)−S(ρAB​)≥0.
(5)
This proves Subadditivity of Von Neumann entropy:
S(ρAB​)≤S(ρA​)+S(ρB​).​
(6)
The proof also identifies equality exactly with the product state ρAB​=ρA​⊗ρB​.

A compression scheme consists of quantum channels
Cn​:B(H⊗n)→B(Kn​),Dn​:B(Kn​)→B(H⊗n),
(12)
where each is a CPTP map. The Hilbert space Kn​ is the compressed register. Its block rate in qubits per source qubit is Rn​=n−1log2​dimKn​, with asymptotic rate R=limsupn​Rn​ (or the limit when it exists).
Use the squared quantum fidelity convention for the ensemble overlap:
Fn​=k∑​pk(n)​⟨Ψk(n)​∣Dn​Cn​(∣Ψk(n)​⟩⟨Ψk(n)​∣)∣Ψk(n)​⟩.​
(13)
This definition tests preservation of the emitted vectors themselves, including nonorthogonal ones; preserving only their classical labels would be a different task. The scheme is reliable when Fn​→1 as n→∞. This is the ensemble-average criterion for reliable quantum source compression. A square root is not taken in this definition, even though an unsquared convention is also used for quantum fidelity.

Choose an isometry V:PH⊗n→Kn​ onto the successful code sector, extended by zero on Q, and a normalized flag ∣f⟩ orthogonal to its range. Choose any normalized vector ∣φ⟩ in the source Hilbert space. Choose 0<ε<R−S(π). The preceding construction and the typical subspace theorem give, for all sufficiently large n,
dimKn​≤2n(S(π)+ε)+1≤2⌈nR⌉.

The PDF's first Pauli term contains an undefined ρx​. For the stated depolarizing channel, use σx​ρσx​, consistently with the other two terms. This is the minimal typographical correction needed to define the intended Pauli channel.
Write ρ=21​(I+s⋅σ) using its Bloch vector. The Pauli matrices satisfy σj​σk​σj​=σk​ for j=k and −σk​ for j=k. Thus conjugating by each nonidentity Pauli matrix leaves one Bloch component unchanged and reverses the other two. Summing the three conjugates gives
∑j=x,y,z​σj​ρσj​=21​(3I−s⋅σ)=2I−ρ.
(29)
Substitution yields
Φ(ρ)=(1−34p​)ρ+32p​I,
(30)
so the Pauli-mixture parametrization of qubit depolarization, with p here the total error probability, gives
q=34p​,Φ(ρ)=(1−q)ρ+qI/2.​
(31)
There is also a genuine range error in the printed claim. For its full 0<p<1 assumption, 0<q<4/3, and 0<q<1 holds exactly when 0<p<3/4. For example p=9/10 gives q=6/5; on ∣0⟩⟨0∣ the output is diag(2/5,3/5), with negative z Bloch component. No q∈(0,1) could produce this output from that input, since its component would be 1−q>0.
The corrected channel is still completely positive in the larger range: its original Pauli probabilities are 1−p,p/3,p/3,p/3. This is depolarizing noise weight above one; the formula with q>1 is not a convex mixture with weights 1−q,q, but it has a valid random unitary channel representation. The subsequent parts can therefore be solved both in the printed q∈(0,1) regime and in the actual parameter range.

For the quantum state ensemble E={pi​,ρi​} with average ρ​=∑i​pi​ρi​, the Holevo quantity is
χ(E)=S(ρ​)−i∑​pi​S(ρi​)=i∑​pi​D(ρi​∥ρ​).​
(34)
The second equality follows by expanding the trace definition: ∑i​pi​Trρi​logρ​=Trρ​logρ​. All positive-weight states are supported within ρ​, so the expression is finite.
For a CPTP map Λ, the output average is Λ(ρ​), with unchanged probabilities pi​. The data-processing inequality for quantum relative entropy gives
χ(ΛE)​=i∑​pi​D(Λ(ρi​)∥Λ(ρ​))≤i∑​pi​D(ρi​∥ρ​)=χ(E).​
(35)
Thus the Holevo quantity cannot increase under a quantum channel.
The underlying monotonicity can also be seen directly from Strong subadditivity of Von Neumann entropy. Attach the classical label X, and realize Λ by a Stinespring representation of a completely positive map with output B and discarded environment E. The isometry preserves I(X:BE), whose input value is χ(E). The difference from the output value is
I(X:BE)−I(X:B)=S(XB)+S(BE)−S(B)−S(XBE)≥0,
(36)
which is precisely strong subadditivity. This explains why discarding an environment loses accessible ensemble information.
Here “operation” means the unconditional channel, including the classical outcome when a measurement is retained. Conditioning on a selected outcome can increase the Holevo quantity. For instance, take equally likely vectors a​∣0⟩±1−a​∣1⟩, with 1/2<a<1. Their input Holevo quantity is h2​(a)<1. The filter K=diag((1−a)/a​,1) succeeds on either state with probability 2(1−a) and produces the orthogonal states ∣+⟩,∣−⟩ after normalization. The success-conditioned quantity is 1. This does not violate the unconditional statement.

The Holevo-Schumacher-Westmoreland theorem gives the classical coding formula
C(Φ)=limm→∞​m1​χ∗(Φ⊗m),χ∗(Λ)=sup{pi​,ρi​}​χ({pi​,Λ(ρi​)}).
(37)
Its ensemble-coding statement makes every rate below a one-use output Holevo quantity achievable with product input encodings and collective output decoding. Under this product-input restriction, the capacity is
Cprod​(Φ)=sup{pi​,ρi​}​χ({pi​,Φ(ρi​)}).
(38)
For any such input ensemble, rates below its output Holevo quantity are achievable with asymptotically vanishing decoding error. Maximization gives the product-state classical capacity. The converse follows from the Holevo bound, entropy bounds on product outputs, and Fano's inequality.
Define the binary entropy h2​(x)=−xlog2​x−(1−x)log2​(1−x). For an input Bloch radius r≤1, the output eigenvalues are (1±(1−q)r)/2. Its entropy is
S(Φ(ρ))=h2​(21+∣1−q∣r​).
(39)
On [1/2,1], h2​ is decreasing, so this is minimized by pure inputs r=1. Throughout the completely positive interval 0≤q≤4/3, the minimum equals h2​(q/2), using symmetry h2​(x)=h2​(1−x). The average output entropy is at most 1, because it is a qubit. Hence every ensemble satisfies
χ({pi​,Φ(ρi​)})≤1−h2​(q/2).
(40)
Two equally likely orthogonal pure inputs attain this bound: their average output is I/2, and each individual output has entropy h2​(q/2). Therefore
Cprod​(Φ)=1−h2​(q/2)=1−h2​(2p/3)bits per channel use.​
(41)
This gives the Holevo capacity of a qubit depolarizing channel in the noise-weight convention used here.
For completeness, correlated classical code labels do not evade this upper bound. Each product input to n uses has product output entropy at least nh2​(q/2), whereas the average output entropy is at most n. Its Holevo quantity is consequently at most n[1−h2​(q/2)]. The Holevo bound and Fano's inequality rule out a reliable rate larger than this value. Combined with HSW achievability, this proves the capacity under the stated product-input restriction. It also remains valid in the extended physical parameter range arising from the original p assumption.

The unread measurement is the pinching map P(ρ)=ρ′=∑i​Pi​ρPi​, a nonselective projective measurement. Its output is block diagonal and commutes with each Pi​, so logρ′ also commutes with the projectors on its support.
First the quantum relative entropy D(ρ∥ρ′) is finite. If v lies in the kernel of ρ′, positivity gives
0=⟨v∣ρ′∣v⟩=∑i​∥ρ1/2Pi​v∥2.
(56)
Every summand is zero; summing ρ1/2Pi​v=0 gives ρ1/2v=0. Thus kerρ′⊆kerρ, equivalently suppρ⊆suppρ′.
The block-diagonal logarithm obeys ∑i​Pi​(logρ′)Pi​=logρ′ on this support. Cyclicity of the trace therefore gives
Trρlogρ′=Tr(∑i​Pi​ρPi​)logρ′=Trρ′logρ′.
(57)
Consequently the relative entropy of a pinched state is exactly the entropy increase:
D(ρ∥ρ′)=−S(ρ)−Trρlog2​ρ′=S(ρ′)−S(ρ).
(58)
By the proved nonnegativity of quantum relative entropy and its equality condition,
