For this equal-strength power-law mutual repression, the logarithmic gains and their product at equilibrium are
ϵf​=−1+yny​,ϵg​=−1+xmx​,P=ϵf​ϵg​=(1+x)(1+y)mnxy​.
(5)
The mutual repression stability criterion immediately excludes multistability if mn≤1, since P<mn at every finite positive equilibrium. The common prefactor λ gives a stronger exclusion: if either exponent is at most one, multistability is impossible for every λ, including some cases with mn>1.
To prove the additional cases, suppose m≤1<n. At equilibrium the two expressions for λ imply
(1+x)mx​=(1+y)ny​.
(6)
Put a=x/(1+x) and b=y/(1+y). Because m≤1, the left side is at least a, so a≤b(1−b)n−1. It follows that
P=mnab≤mnb2(1−b)n−1≤(n+1)n+14mn(n−1)n−1​<1.
(7)
The maximum in the second inequality occurs at b=2/(n+1). Its strict bound follows from 4n/(n+1)2≤1, m≤1, and ((n−1)/(n+1))n−1<1. Interchanging the components proves the case n≤1<m; the case with both exponents at most one was already covered by mn≤1. Every root of F(x)=f(g(x))−x therefore has F′<0. Since F begins positive and ends negative, there is exactly one root: two downward crossings would require another crossing with nonnegative derivative between them. The unique equilibrium is stable.
For completeness, neither exponent can be relaxed above one in this exclusion. If m>1 and n>1, take λ sufficiently large. On x∈[λ/2,λ], g(x)=O(λ1−m)→0, so f(g(x))=λ(1+o(1)) maps that interval into itself. It has a fixed point with
x∼λ,y∼λ1−m,P→0.
(8)
It is a stable equilibrium. By exchanging the components, there is a second stable fixed point with y∼λ and x∼λ1−n. These two regions are disjoint for large λ, proving bistability for some production strength. Hence the full answer is
No multistability for any λ>0⟺m≤1 or n≤1.​
(9)
For example, m=1/2,n=3 has mn>1 but still a unique stable equilibrium for all λ. Thus the product-only necessary condition for strong feedback is not sufficient when both production prefactors are constrained to be equal.
