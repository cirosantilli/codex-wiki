The normalized stationary fluctuation-dissipation relation for a reaction network is
Mη+ηMT=D,ηij​=μi​μj​Cov(Xi​,Xj​)​.​
(14)
Here M is the negative drift Jacobian matrix expressed in normalized population coordinates, and D is the normalized noise matrix. More explicitly, with S=diag(μi​), drift Jacobian A, stoichiometric vectors νr​, and stationary reaction propensity functions ar​, these matrices are M=−S−1AS and D=S−1BS−1, where B=∑r​ar​νr​νrT​. The linear noise approximation gives a covariance evolution η˙​=−Mη−ηMT+D; stationarity is the displayed Continuous Lyapunov equation.
In one dimension, the normalized restoring coefficient is M=H/τ=1/(2τ). Squared chemical jumps determine the noise:
B=9λ+βμ​=12λ=τ4μ​,D=μ2B​=τμ4​.
(15)
The factor nine from a birth burst is essential; its three molecules arise in one correlated reaction event. The scalar Continuous Lyapunov equation is 2Mη=D, giving
η≈μ4​,Var(X)≈4μ.​
(16)
Equivalently, the normalized variance is ⟨s⟩/(Hμ)=2/((1/2)μ). The Fano factor is approximately four. This calculation uses the prescribed mean closure and the linear noise approximation, so its small-relative-fluctuation regime is μ≫1; it is not an exact assertion for arbitrarily small molecular copy numbers.
