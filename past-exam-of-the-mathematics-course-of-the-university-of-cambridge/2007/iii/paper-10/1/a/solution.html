Use the complex inner product convention linear in its first argument. Give the exterior algebra its induced inner product, with an orthonormal wedge basis. Exterior multiplication is e(v)η=v∧η. Its adjoint is contraction:
e(b)∗(v1​∧⋯∧vr​)=∑j=1r​(−1)j−1(vj​,b)v1​∧⋯vj​⋯∧vr​.
(1)
Applying this formula to a∧η separates its first term from the remaining terms, giving e(b)∗e(a)η=(a,b)η−e(a)e(b)∗η. Hence the canonical anticommutation relations are
e(a)e(b)∗+e(b)∗e(a)=(a,b)I.​
(2)
For irreducibility, choose an orthonormal basis u1​,…,ud​. The occupancy projections are Nj​=e(uj​)e(uj​)∗, and P0​=∏j​(I−Nj​) projects onto the vacuum 1∈Λ0V. For an increasing index set J, put EJ​=e(uj1​​)⋯e(ujr​​). Then EI​P0​EJ∗​ maps the wedge basis vector uJ​ to uI​ and kills every other wedge basis vector. Thus these products are all matrix units and the generated star-algebra is End(ΛV). Any common invariant subspace for the creation and annihilation operators is therefore either zero or all of W.
