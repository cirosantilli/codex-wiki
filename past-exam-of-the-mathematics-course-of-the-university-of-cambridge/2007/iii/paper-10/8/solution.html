The loop group here is LU(1)=C∞(S1,U(1)), with pointwise multiplication. Although it is abelian, its nontrivial positive-energy quantum representations are projective: implementers can commute only up to a phase. A positive energy representation of the loop group of U(1) has rotations Rt​ whose self-adjoint generator is bounded below and which implement g(z)↦g(eitz) in the projective unitary group. The fermionic construction gives both that phase and an explicit bosonic description.
Choose the one-particle Hilbert space H=L2(S1) with modes er​(z)=zr, r∈Z, and projection P onto r≥0. Use the polarized fermionic Fock space
FP​=Λ(PH)⊗​Λ((I−P)H​),
(61)
where the exterior powers are Hilbert-completed and the tensor product is graded. Its vacuum fills all modes r<0 and leaves r≥0 empty; excitations are particles in the empty modes and holes in the filled modes. The fermionic annihilation operators cr​ and fermionic creation operators cr∗​ satisfy the canonical anticommutation relations
{cr​,cs∗​}=δrs​I,{cr​,cs​}={cr∗​,cs∗​}=0,cr​Ω=0 (r≥0),cr∗​Ω=0 (r<0).
(62)
Finite particle-hole configurations form an orthonormal basis. As in the finite-dimensional exterior algebra construction, their creation and annihilation operators act irreducibly: an operator in the commutant sends the vacuum to a scalar vacuum, since the joint kernel of its vacuum annihilators is one-dimensional, and then acts by that scalar on the dense configuration basis.
Segal's quantization criterion for fermions says that a unitary one-particle operator u has a unitary implementer Γ(u) on this Fock space, satisfying Γ(u)c∗(f)Γ(u)∗=c∗(uf), exactly when
[P,u] is Hilbert-Schmidt.​
(63)
Equivalently P−uPu∗ is Hilbert-Schmidt. Implementers are unique up to phase, hence form a projective unitary representation of the restricted unitary group. This is the fermionic, polarized criterion; it is not a condition that the entire operator u−I be Hilbert-Schmidt. The criterion and the corresponding continuity topology are stated in Theorem III.2.1.1 of the fermionic construction of loop-group representations. For the compact principal-angle part of a pair of polarizations, the threshold has a concrete interpretation. Its two-dimensional blocks are rotations through angles θj​; finitely many unmatched modes can be handled separately. Each angle θj​ rotates the vacuum into cosθj​ times its original component plus sinθj​ times a particle-hole pair. After discarding finitely many exceptional blocks, the successive tensor products are Cauchy precisely when ∏j​cosθj​ converges to a nonzero value, equivalently ∑j​sin2θj​<∞. In this block model the sum is exactly half the squared Hilbert-Schmidt norm of the projection difference; finitely many unmatched modes account for a change of charge. Infinitely many unmatched modes already violate the criterion. This explains why infinitely many arbitrarily small mixings can fail to be implementable.
For g(z)=∑n​gn​zn∈LU(1), multiplication Mg​ is a unitary one-particle operator. Its matrix entry from es​ to er​ is gr−s​. Therefore
∥[P,Mg​]∥HS2​=∑r,s​∣1r≥0​−1s≥0​∣2∣gr−s​∣2=∑n∈Z​∣n∣∣gn​∣2.
(64)
For a fixed difference n, exactly ∣n∣ pairs cross the polarization boundary. Smoothness makes the sum finite, so Segal's quantization criterion for fermions constructs a continuous projective unitary representation of every smooth loop. Smooth convergence gives operator-norm convergence of Mg​ and Hilbert-Schmidt convergence of its commutator, which is sufficient for the strong projective continuity of the implementers.
Normal ordering fixes the charge and rotation energy. On the dense finite-configuration domain put
Ers​=:cr∗​cs​:=cr∗​cs​−δrs​1r<0​I,Q=∑r​Err​,L0​=∑r​rErr​.
(65)
The sums are finite on each configuration. A particle at r≥0 adds charge one and energy r; a hole at r<0 adds charge minus one and energy −r. Consequently L0​ is a nonnegative self-adjoint diagonal operator with integer spectrum and finite-dimensional eigenspaces. The unitaries Rt​=eitL0​ are strongly continuous, have period 2π, and implement er​↦eitrer​. Conjugating multiplication by this one-particle rotation gives Mg(eit⋅)​, so their Fock implementers rotate the loop representation projectively. This is an actual positive-energy circle action.
The scalar anomaly is visible in the Heisenberg current algebra of a fermion on the circle. Define on finite-energy vectors
Jn​=∑r∈Z​Er,r+n​,Jn∗​=J−n​,J0​=Q.
(66)
For fixed n the sum has only finitely many nonzero actions on any configuration. Direct use of the canonical anticommutation relations gives
[Ers​,Etu​]=δst​Eru​−δur​Ets​+δst​δur​(1r<0​−1s<0​)I.
(67)
On summing, the two noncentral sums cancel. If m=−n the central sum is ∑r​(1r<0​−1r+n<0​)=n: for n>0 the terms are precisely r=−n,…,−1. Thus
[Jn​,Jm​]=nδn+m,0​I,[L0​,Jn​]=−nJn​.​
(68)
In particular Jn​Ω=0 for n>0. The coefficient one in the central term is the level of this basic representation.
Let h(θ)=∑n​hn​einθ be smooth and real. The normal-ordered infinitesimal implementer of multiplication by h is
A(h)=∑n​hn​J−n​.
(69)
The sign of the current index matters: multiplication by zn raises the one-particle mode by n, so it corresponds to J−n​. It follows either directly from the bilinear commutator or from the fermionic currents on the circle that [A(h),cr∗​]=∑n​hn​cr+n∗​. For trigonometric h these are oscillator fields on the finite-energy domain; their self-adjoint closures exponentiate to the implementers of eih. For general smooth h, their extensions are obtained by the oscillator-field construction below: ∑n>0​n∣hn​∣2<∞ guarantees the square-summable field coefficients, and Fourier truncation converges in the corresponding Weyl relations implementers. Thus no unjustified infinite operator product is required.
Set
ω(h,k)=2π1​∫02π​h′(θ)k(θ)dθ=i∑n​nhn​k−n​.
(70)
It is real and antisymmetric for real h,k. The Heisenberg current algebra of a fermion on the circle relations give [A(h),A(k)]=iω(h,k)I. The Weyl relations, or the finite-mode Baker-Campbell-Hausdorff computation followed by the square-summable limit, yield
eiA(h)eiA(k)=e−iω(h,k)/2eiA(h+k).
(71)
This is an explicit two-cocycle on the identity component of the loop group. A different phase choice changes it by a group coboundary, but its antisymmetric commutator remains e−iω(h,k), and hence cannot be removed.
The winding components also matter. Every smooth loop is zweih with w∈Z, real periodic h, and mean h0​ determined modulo 2π. Let S implement multiplication by z. It moves the filled boundary one place upwards, so it raises charge by one; phases of charge ground states can be chosen so that SΩq​=Ωq+1​. Conjugating the fermionic currents on the circle gives
SQS∗=Q−I,SJn​S∗=Jn​(n=0).
(72)
Choose U(w,h)=SweiA(h). Since eiA(h)Sv=Svei(A(h)+vh0​I), one obtains the full loop group two-cocycle for U(1)
U(w,h)U(v,k)=c((w,h),(v,k))U(w+v,h+k),c((w,h),(v,k))=exp(ivh0​−iω(h,k)/2).​
(73)
The exponent is bilinear in the additive winding-logarithm coordinates, so expansion immediately gives c(a,b)c(a+b,d)=c(b,d)c(a,b+d). Replacing h by h+2πℓ changes A(h) by 2πℓQ, whose exponential is identity since charges are integers; the scalar cocycle also stays unchanged since v∈Z. Thus the expression is well-defined on loops, not just on chosen logarithms. It records both the derivative anomaly and the commutator of winding with constant loops.
For the fermion-boson correspondence on the circle, decompose FP​=⨁q∈Z​Fq​ into charge eigenspaces. The charge-q ground state Ωq​ fills all integer modes r<q. Its energy is
L0​Ωq​=2q(q−1)​Ωq​.
(74)
For q>0, sum the energies of the added modes 0,…,q−1; for q<0, sum the hole energies of the removed modes q,…,−1. Every other charge-q configuration is obtained by moving finitely many occupied levels upwards, so this is the lowest energy. Since Jn​ preserves charge and lowers energy by n>0, Jn​Ωq​=0 for n>0.
Define bosonic annihilation operators bn​=Jn​/n​ and bn∗​=J−n​/n​, n>0. They obey [bn​,bm∗​]=δnm​I. Their occupation vectors
∏n>0​nmn​mn​!​(J−n​)mn​​Ωq​,mn​∈N0​ with finite support,
(75)
are orthonormal by commuting positive fermionic currents on the circle through negative fermionic currents on the circle. Their energies are q(q−1)/2+∑n>0​nmn​. It remains to prove that they span, rather than assume the boson correspondence. Count configurations by formal charge u and energy t. Empty modes r≥0 can contain one extra particle, while filled modes −r, r≥1, can contain one hole. Therefore the fermionic character is
∏r≥0​(1+utr)∏r≥1​(1+u−1tr).
(76)
The charge form of the Jacobi triple product proved in Question 4 identifies this with
∑q∈Z​∏n≥1​(1−tn)uqtq(q−1)/2​.
(77)
The reciprocal product counts bosonic occupations, or partitions of an integer. For fixed charge and energy, the finite-dimensional fermion space has exactly as many basis configurations as the independent fermionic current on the circle monomials already constructed. Hence those monomials exhaust every such space, proving their dense completeness. This establishes a unitary identification of every charge sector with a bosonic Fock space, and on that identification
L0​=2Q(Q−I)​+n>0∑​nbn∗​bn​.​
(78)
The usual half-integer fermion convention instead gives ground energy q2/2 and requires the corresponding adjustment of rotations. Here integer modes were deliberately chosen to obtain a genuine circle action with integral nonnegative energies. The two ground states of energy zero, at charges zero and one, are therefore consistent, not a negative-energy defect.
This is also a constructive bosonic realization of the loop representation: the mean of h acts by h0​Q, nonzero modes of h act as the square-summable oscillator field ∑n>0​n​(hn​bn∗​+hn​​bn​), and winding shifts the charge sectors. Taking k tensor copies adds the fermionic current on the circle central terms and gives level k∈N. Positivity constrains their sign, since in a vacuum sector ∥J−n​Ω∥2=kn for n>0. The level-one construction, its explicit loop cocycle, and its complete bosonic occupation basis show how an abelian classical loop group acquires noncommuting quantum currents while retaining positive rotation energy.
