For finitely many disjoint bounded intervals J1​,…,Jr​, all dyadic cells used in their level-n sums are disjoint. The independent occupied indicators proved in part (a) form independent groups, so Mn​(J1​),…,Mn​(Jr​) are independent. Their almost-sure limits are also independent: for 0≤zj​≤1, bounded convergence gives
E∏j​zjM(Jj​)​=limn​∏j​EzjMn​(Jj​)​=∏j​exp(λ(Jj​)(zj​−1)).
(57)
This joint probability generating function determines the joint law as that of independent Poisson counts. If an interval is unbounded, truncate all intervals by [0,R) and let R↑∞ in the joint Laplace transforms. An unbounded interval has infinite count almost surely, since its Poisson means tend to infinity; this extends the independence assertion to such intervals as well.
For an interval-step function s=∑j​aj​1Jj​​ with aj​≥0 and disjoint bounded intervals, independent Poisson distributions yield
Ee−M(s)=∏j​exp(λ(Jj​)(e−aj​−1))=exp(−∫0∞​(1−e−s(x))dx).
(58)
We now extend this to arbitrary nonnegative Borel functions, rather than stopping at interval-step functions. The mean measure m(A)=EM(A) is countably additive by Tonelli theorem, is finite on bounded intervals, and agrees with λ on intervals by the preceding Poisson count calculation. Uniqueness of finite measures on each bounded Borel space, then exhaustion by bounded spaces, gives m=λ on every Borel set. Simple-function approximation therefore gives
EM(g)=∫gdλ(g≥0 Borel).
(59)
For bounded f supported in a bounded interval, choose nonnegative interval-step functions sk​ with ∫∣sk​−f∣dλ→0. Their existence follows by approximating measurable level sets in finite Lebesgue measure by finite unions of intervals, then approximating f by simple functions. The mean-measure identity implies
E∣M(sk​)−M(f)∣≤∫∣sk​−f∣dλ⟶0.
(60)
The function e−u is one-Lipschitz on [0,∞), so the expectations of the exponentials converge. The deterministic integrals of 1−e−sk​ converge by the same Lipschitz bound. Hence the Laplace formula holds for this bounded Borel f.
For general f≥0, put fk​=(f∧k)1[0,k)​. These functions increase to f, giving M(fk​)↑M(f) and ∫(1−e−fk​)dλ↑∫(1−e−f)dλ. Bounded convergence for the exponentials and monotone convergence for the integrals prove the Laplace functional of a Poisson random measure:
E[e−M(f)]=exp(−∫0∞​(1−e−f(x))dx),​
(61)
with e−∞=0.
Finally take f=∑j​uj​1Aj​​ for disjoint Borel sets of finite Lebesgue measure. The formula gives the product joint Laplace transform of independent Poisson counts with means λ(Aj​). Uniqueness of that transform proves the required count laws on all Borel sets. For infinite-measure sets the formula gives Ee−uM(A)=0 for u>0, so their counts are infinite almost surely. Together with the given local finiteness, these facts show that M is a Poisson random measure with intensity λ. This completes the exponential void probabilities characterize a Poisson random measure argument; simplicity was used to recover counts from fine-cell occupancy.
