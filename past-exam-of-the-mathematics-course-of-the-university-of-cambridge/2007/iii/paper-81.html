Take x tailward, so forward swimming has laboratory velocity −Ui. For an inextensible travelling waveform, material motion in the swimmer frame is Vi−ct: a material point passes through the wave along its tangent while the wave pattern translates at V. Thus the relative velocity of otherwise stationary fluid is
w=ct−(V−U)i.
(1)
Put q=V−U and choose t=(Xs​,Ys​), n=(−Ys​,Xs​), with Xs2​+Ys2​=1. Then
wt​=c−qXs​,wn​=qYs​.
(2)
On the forward-swimming branch 0<U<V<c and Xs​>0, the tangential slip is positive. A law for reversed tangential slip would also need its sign, not just its absolute magnitude.
The quadratic normal law represents inertial cross-flow drag: dynamic pressure scales as fluid density times wn2​, while ∣wn​∣wn​ chooses the force direction. Its coefficient contains the cross-sectional size and an empirical drag coefficient. The ∣wt​∣3/2 exponent is characteristic of an attached laminar boundary layer: its thickness scales as δ∼(νs/∣wt​∣)1/2 and its shear as μ∣wt​∣/δ. However, this gives local shear proportional to ∣wt​∣3/2s−1/2, not s1/2. The positive power of s is genuinely printed in this paper and repeated in its target formulas. Below it is treated as the prescribed phenomenological weight, rather than misidentified as standard local laminar skin-friction scaling along a slender swimmer.
The normal axial force on an element is
dFN,x​=KN​∣qYs​∣qYs​(−Ys​)ds=−KN​q2∣Ys​∣3ds.
(3)
The prescribed tangential force gives dFT,x​=KT​(c−qXs​)3/2Xs​s1/2ds. Zero mean axial force therefore gives the nonlinear resistive-force balance for a travelling filament
KN​(V−U)2∫0L​∣Ys​∣3ds=KT​∫0L​∣c−(V−U)Xs​∣3/2Xs​s1/2ds.​
(4)
The absolute values are present in the original PDF. In a periodically beating, constant-mean-speed model, phase-average the integrals where necessary; the leading approximation below is phase independent. Local force laws neglect nonlocal hydrodynamic interactions and assume nearly steady local response.
For the sinusoidal displacement, let ε=βk≪1. Then Ys​=εcosks and Xs​=(1−Ys2​)1/2=1+O(ε2). Over one wavelength,
∫0L​∣Ys​∣3ds=kβ3k3​∫02π​∣cosz∣3dz=38​β3k2,∫0L​s1/2ds=32​L3/2.
(5)
Replace Xs​ by one in the leading tangential term and balance the forces. After multiplication by 3/2,
4β3k2KN​(V−U)2≃KT​L3/2(c−V+U)3/2.​
(6)
This replacement also requires cε2≪c−V+U for a uniformly small relative error inside the fractional power. If the tangential slip is itself O(cε2), its variation along the body must be retained. Periodicity gives V/c=⟨Xs​⟩=1−ε2/4+O(ε4), so small geometric slope alone does not guarantee that additional slip condition.
For Lighthill elongated-body theory, put h(x,t)≃βsin[k(x−Vt)] and w⊥​=ht​+Uhx​. The transverse fluid kinetic energy per unit length is MA​w⊥2​/2. In the usual tail-flux approximation, with negligible leading-end contribution, the lateral work flux is MA​U⟨ht​w⊥​⟩, whereas kinetic energy carried into the wake is MA​U⟨w⊥2​⟩/2. Their difference is the useful propulsive power TU. Consequently
T=MA​⟨ht​w⊥​−21​w⊥2​⟩=2MA​​⟨ht2​−U2hx2​⟩=4MA​​β2k2(V2−U2).
(7)
Balancing this against the drag of the printed tangential model, D=(2/3)KT​L3/2(c−V+U)3/2, gives
83​MA​β2k2(V2−U2)=KT​L3/2(c−V+U)3/2.​
(8)
For a genuine local laminar skin-friction estimate, write dD=CT​ut3/2​s−1/2ds, where CT​ has the appropriate different dimensions. Its integrated drag is 2CT​ut3/2​L​, and the corresponding elongated-body balance is instead MA​β2k2(V2−U2)/8=CT​L​(c−V+U)3/2. The printed L3/2 expression is therefore a result of its stated force model, not of that standard boundary-layer law.
The two thrust models describe different mechanisms: cross-flow resistive-force theory uses dissipative local drag, while elongated-body theory uses reactive acceleration of added mass. Both suppress details of the wake, body-body interactions, finite slenderness, skin-friction unsteadiness, separation and possible turbulent transition. The tail-only reactive result also requires a negligible leading-end flux, usually supplied by a nose taper or small leading-end motion; a blunt constant-cross-section body waving equally at both ends cannot automatically omit its leading-end contribution. Speed fluctuations and recoil may matter, and a planar constant-amplitude waveform is an idealization of a real sea snake.

Choose the helix handedness so its unit tangent is t=cosαi+sinαeϕ​. Let q=Ω0​−Ω; the body rotates at +Ωi, while the flagellum rotates at −qi in the laboratory, since its relative motor rotation is −Ω0​i. Both translate at −Ui. Thus the relative fluid velocity on a flagellar element is w=Ui+bqeϕ​.
Low-Reynolds-number resistive-force theory gives force per unit length on the flagellum
f=KN​w+(KT​−KN​)(w⋅t)t.
(9)
Its axial and azimuthal components are
fx​=(KN​sin2α+KT​cos2α)U−(KN​−KT​)bqsinαcosα,
(10)
fϕ​=(KN​cos2α+KT​sin2α)bq−(KN​−KT​)Usinαcosα.
(11)
Define the integrated axial resistance matrix of a slender helix coefficients
A=L(KN​sin2α+KT​cos2α),C=bL(KN​−KT​)sinαcosα,H=b2L(KN​cos2α+KT​sin2α).
(12)
The sphere has translational resistance Ab​=6πμa by the Stokes drag law and rotational resistance Hb​=8πμa3. Its hydrodynamic force is +Ab​Ui and its torque −Hb​Ωi. Integration of fx​ and bfϕ​ gives the zero total force and torque conditions
(A+Ab​)U=Cq,CU=Hq−Hb​Ω.​
(13)
Dividing the first by L and expanding the coefficients gives exactly the two requested balances. The internal motor torque cancels in the total torque balance; it is not an external applied torque.
Eliminate U and use q+Ω=Ω0​. The effective flagellar rotational resistance is He​=H−C2/(A+Ab​), so
q=Hb​+He​Hb​Ω0​​,Ω=Hb​+He​He​Ω0​​,U=(A+Ab​)(H+Hb​)−C2CHb​Ω0​​.​
(14)
The determinant of helical resistance in resistive-force theory is AH−C2=b2L2KN​KT​>0, hence the denominator is positive. Opposite handedness changes the sign of the translation-rotation coupling and reverses the swimming direction.
For KT​=K, KN​=2K, α=π/4 and a=b, the coefficients are A=3KL/2, C=KaL/2, H=3Ka2L/2. Therefore
U=2K2L2+21πμaKL+48π2μ2a24πμKLa2Ω0​​.​
(15)
Equivalently, with h=KL/(πμa), U=aΩ0​4h/(2h2+21h+48). These are positive speed magnitudes for the selected handedness; the translational velocity is −Ui.
This helical microswimmer with a spherical head approximation neglects head-tail hydrodynamic interactions as permitted, treats the flagellum as slender and rigid, and averages away transverse forces associated with finite helical ends. The flow is quasistatic Stokes flow, so neither body inertia nor fluid inertia enters the balance.

Physically, downward shear tilts upward swimmers toward the axis, concentrating excess weight there and enhancing the downward flow. Positive S in the specified +Snpp stress convention reinforces this effect; sufficiently negative S opposes it. A positive w means reduced downward velocity, not necessarily reversal of the total pipe flow. The deterministic orientation closure fails at excessive shear, and scalar diffusivity neglects directional and shear-dependent dispersion.

From part (i), the steady mean has cothΛ−1/Λ=1−1/Λ+O(e−2Λ). Hence BDr​=Λ−1→0 gives Vc​→Vs​k. Part (ii) gives the same mean as t→∞. Thus

Both limiting distributions concentrate weakly at the upward pole. A common limiting mean does not identify their finite-time distributions or the order in which the limits are taken.
Time scales must be compared at a specified accuracy. The exact deterministic solution approaches the limiting mean with deficit (4t/B−2)e−2t/B: its alignment scale is B, with exponential late scale B/2. For weak but nonzero diffusion, linearize about the upward direction using the two transverse tilt components q. They obey the Ornstein-Uhlenbeck process
dq=−Bq​dt+2Dr​​dW,dtd​⟨∣q∣2⟩=−B2​⟨∣q∣2⟩+4Dr​.
(47)
The stationary angular variance is 2BDr​, and nearby axisymmetric variance perturbations decay on B/2 to leading weak-noise order. This is weak-noise relaxation of gyrotactic alignment: the aligning torque, not the pure rotational-diffusion time Dr−1​, controls the weak-noise alignment rate.
Nevertheless the full relaxation times are not identical. Finite diffusion leaves mean deficit approximately BDr​, whereas deterministic alignment has no such floor. Starting from isotropy, reducing the transient deficit to that tiny floor requires (4t/B−2)e−2t/B∼BDr​, or
t∼2B​logBDr​1​
(48)
with smaller logarithmic corrections. The two limits have the same mean and the same order-B weak-noise alignment mechanism, but not identical transients or accuracy-dependent settling times. No finite time achieves the exact deterministic point-mass limit; positive diffusion never gives that exact point mass at all.
