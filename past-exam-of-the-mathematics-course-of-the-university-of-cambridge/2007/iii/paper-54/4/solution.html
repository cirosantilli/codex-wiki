Take ∂±​=(∂τ​±∂σ​)/2 on the strip 0≤σ≤π. Varying the Grassmann-odd worldsheet Majorana fermions gives ∂−​ψ1​=0, ∂+​ψ2​=0 and, up to the orientation of the strip boundary, the surface term
δIbdry​=2πi​∫dτ(ψ2​⋅δψ2​−ψ1​⋅δψ1​)​0π​.
(35)
At each endpoint it vanishes if ψ1​=ηψ2​ with η=±1, with the same relation on variations. A common sign can be absorbed by redefining one chiral field, so set η0​=+1. Since ψ1​=f(τ+σ) and ψ2​=f(τ−σ), the second endpoint imposes f(u+2π)=ηπ​f(u). Equal endpoint signs give the periodic Ramond sector, and opposite endpoint signs give the antiperiodic Neveu–Schwarz sector:
ψ1​(0)=ψ2​(0),ψ1​(π)=+ψ2​(π) (R),ψ1​(π)=−ψ2​(π) (NS).​
(36)
These gluing choices preserve the Neumann-endpoint worldsheet supersymmetry conditions and give integer or half-integer worldsheet Majorana fermion modes, respectively.
First fix a normalization issue in the original PDF. With its stated canonical string oscillator algebra, the standard superconformal current mode is
Gr​=∑m∈Z​:αm​⋅ψr−m​:,
(37)
without the printed factor 1/2. To check the Ramond supercurrent zero-mode square directly, pair positive and negative integer modes:
G0​=α0​⋅ψ0​+∑m>0​Qm​,Qm​=α−m​⋅ψm​+αm​⋅ψ−m​.
(38)
The zero-mode Clifford relations give (α0​⋅ψ0​)2=α02​/2. Different Qm​ anticommute, and so do Qm​ with the zero-mode term. The two same-sign terms in Qm2​ vanish by antisymmetry of the fermions and symmetry of the bosonic product. The cross terms give
Qm2​=α−m​⋅αm​+mψ−m​⋅ψm​.
(39)
The second term comes from commuting αm​ through α−m​, so the frequency factor m is essential. Therefore
G02​=21​α02​+∑m>0​(α−m​⋅αm​+mψ−m​⋅ψm​)=L0R​,{G0​,G0​}=2L0R​.​
(40)
Here L0R​ uses vacuum-annihilating Ramond normal ordering; its string intercept is zero. A plane-to-cylinder central shift must be included consistently if a different zero-mode convention is used.
For the mode Fr​=Gr​/2 actually printed, the algebra instead gives {F0​,F0​}=L0R​/2. Already the oscillator-free term gives {F0​,F0​}=α02​/4, whereas 2L0R​=α02​. Thus the requested identity is not valid with both that prefactor and the supplied string oscillator algebra. Removing the prefactor gives the identity just derived. Renaming L0R​/4 as L0​ would fix this isolated equality but would spoil the usual Virasoro normalization and the subsequent mass formula. We use the standard normalized modes Gr​ in the remaining physical constraints; their vanishing is unaffected by a common nonzero rescaling.
The RNS string physical-state conditions in the two sectors are
NS:R:​(L0​−21​)∣Φ⟩=0,Ln>0​∣Φ⟩=0,Gr>0​∣Φ⟩=0,r∈Z+21​,L0R​∣Φ⟩=0,Ln>0​∣Φ⟩=0,Gn>0​∣Φ⟩=0,G0​∣Φ⟩=0.​
(41)
The last condition is the fermionic zero-mode constraint; its square implies the Ramond mass-shell condition. Physical null string states are quotiented. Matter central charge is 3d/2, while the reparameterization worldsheet ghost fields and superconformal ghosts contribute −26+11=−15. Thus d=10 is the critical dimension of the RNS superstring, with aNS​=1/2 and aR​=0.
To solve the constraints, choose X±=(X0±X9)/2​, so η+−​=−1, and work on a patch with α0+​=2α′​p+=0. In light-cone gauge in string theory fix all nonzero αn+​ and all ψr+​ to zero. For the Ramond sector, the printed restriction to nonzero fermion modes is not by itself complete: one must also reduce the longitudinal fermionic zero modes using the zero-mode supersymmetry constraint. The usual reduced light-cone description sets the remaining ψ0+​ to zero as well and retains the transverse Clifford algebra. Covariantly this reduction is the Dirac equation on the ground-state particle polarization; it is not an extra unconstrained canonical string oscillator.
With this reduction the string oscillator generators have the form
Ln​Ln⊥​Gr​​=−α0+​αn−​+Ln⊥​,=21​m∈Z∑​:αn−mi​αmi​:+21​r∑​(r−2n​):ψn−ri​ψri​:,=−α0+​ψr−​+Gr⊥​,Gr⊥​=m∑​αmi​ψr−mi​.​
(42)
Repeated i runs over the eight transverse directions. The coefficient in Ln⊥​ comes from the fermion worldsheet stress tensor −ψ∂ψ/2; for n=0, positive and negative r combine to give the occupation energy rψ−r​ψr​. Thus the light-cone super-Virasoro constraints are explicitly solved by
αn−​=α0+​1​[21​m∑​:αn−mi​αmi​:+21​r∑​(r−2n​):ψn−ri​ψri​:−aδn0​],ψr−​=α0+​1​m∑​αmi​ψr−mi​.​
(43)
Here a is 1/2 in the NS sector and zero in the R sector. The zero-mode equation says 2p+p−=pi2​+(N−a)/α′. All longitudinal excitations are consequently determined by transverse bilinears. The critical dimension and string intercepts are necessary for the resulting quantum theory to preserve the target Lorentz algebra.
The frequency-weighted Neveu–Schwarz level operator is
NNS​=∑n=1∞​α−ni​αni​+∑r=1/2,3/2,…​rψ−ri​ψri​.
(44)
The PDF's displayed number operator omits the fermion frequency weights and the r=1/2 term. With the stated anticommutators that cannot be a level operator: it would assign no level to the first fermionic excitation. The bosonic term already includes its frequency through [αn​,α−n​]=n, whereas the fermionic term must include its frequency explicitly.
One independent way to obtain the string intercept is the transverse zero-point energy. Each periodic boson contributes 21​∑n≥1​n=−1/24. Each antiperiodic real fermion contributes −21​∑r≥1/2​r=−1/48, with the minus sign due to fermionic normal ordering. For example a common exponential cutoff gives
∑n≥1​ne−εn=ε21​−121​+O(ε2),∑r≥1/2​re−εr=ε21​+241​+O(ε2).
(45)
Subtracting the local divergent terms leaves 8(−1/24−1/48)=−1/2, so the NS string intercept is a=1/2. In the Ramond sector the integer fermions instead contribute +1/24 per component and cancel the bosonic contribution, giving aR​=0.
The unprojected NS sector oscillator vacuum has N=0 and
MNS,02​=−2α′1​=−1(α′=1/2).​
(46)
It is a spacetime scalar tachyon. The first excitation ψ−1/2i​∣0;p⟩ has N=1/2 and is massless, with eight vector particle polarizations. Covariantly its particle polarization obeys p⋅ϵ=0 modulo the longitudinal null particle polarization, leaving the same eight degrees of freedom.
The R sector oscillator vacuum is also massless, but it is degenerate because {ψ0μ​,ψ0ν​}=ημν. The operators Γμ=2​ψ0μ​ give the target Clifford algebra, and G0​∣u;p⟩=0 becomes pμ​Γμu=0. At nonzero null momentum this Dirac equation leaves sixteen unprojected on-shell spinor states. Equivalently the eight transverse zero modes give the sixteen-dimensional spin representation 8s​⊕8c​ of SO(8). Thus the Ramond ground state describes spacetime fermions, not a single scalar oscillator vacuum.
To define the GSO projection explicitly, let fNS​ count excited NS fermions and give its vacuum even parity. Then
PNS​=21​(1−(−1)fNS​).
(47)
It retains odd fermion number, removes the tachyon, and retains the eight massless vector particle polarizations. In the Ramond sector let Γ∗​ be transverse chirality and fR​ count nonzero-mode fermions. Choose one consistent chirality sign s=±1 and use
PR​=21​(1+sΓ∗​(−1)fR​).
(48)
Each operator squares to itself. At the ground level PR​ retains one eight-dimensional spinor chirality, so the retained spectrum has eight massless bosonic and eight massless fermionic states. The parity convention can instead assign the NS vacuum odd parity and write a plus projector; the retained states are identical.
Equality continues at every mass level. Let q count α′M2, suppressing common momentum and endpoint-label factors, and set D(q)=∏n≥1​(1−qn)8. The projected string oscillator generating functions are
ZNS​(q)=2D(q)q−1/2​[∏r≥1/2​(1+qr)8−∏r≥1/2​(1−qr)8],ZR​(q)=D(q)8​∏n≥1​(1+qn)8.
(49)
The Ramond factor eight follows because either chirality projector selects eight ground states for every nonzero-oscillator parity. The Jacobi abstruse identity makes the bracket equal to 16q1/2∏n≥1​(1+qn)8, so
ZNS​(q)=ZR​(q).​
(50)
This proves level-by-level equality of bosonic and fermionic multiplicities. The appropriate spacetime supercharge from an RNS spin field relates the two sectors; the projection ensures that it acts within the retained state space. These sector and chirality conventions agree with the primary superstring lecture notes. Equal total counts alone are not the definition of the projection; they follow from the stated parity selection and the string oscillator identity.
