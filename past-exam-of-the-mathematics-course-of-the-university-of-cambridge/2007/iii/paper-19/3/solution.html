Use the metric on M induced by its given Euclidean embedding. For two smooth maps define the C1 metric on a smooth mapping space by
d1​(f,g)=supx∈M​∣f(x)−g(x)∣+supx∈M​∥Dfx​−Dgx​∥op​,
(13)
where the differential has domain Tx​M with the induced inner product. Compactness makes both suprema finite. The norm properties give symmetry and the triangle inequality, and the first term makes distance zero imply equality of maps. Thus convergence in d1​ is uniform convergence of both maps and differentials.
Because i is an immersion, Dix​ is injective at every point. The continuous function ∣Dix​v∣ on the compact unit tangent bundle has a positive minimum a. Therefore
∣Dix​v∣≥a∣v∣(x∈M, v∈Tx​M).
(14)
For all sufficiently large n, supx​∥Dfn,x​−Dix​∥<a/2, and then
∣Dfn,x​v∣≥∣Dix​v∣−∣(Dfn,x​−Dix​)v∣≥2a​∣v∣.
(15)
Consequently every sufficiently late fn​ is an immersion. If M has dimension zero it is a finite set, the immersion assertion is automatic, and eventual injectivity follows directly from separation of its finitely many image points; we henceforth treat positive dimension.
For injectivity, choose finitely many coordinate maps ϕb​ on convex balls, with smaller coordinate neighborhoods covering M and closures lying inside the larger balls. Write Ib​=i∘ϕb​ and Fn,b​=fn​∘ϕb​. On the finitely many relevant compact coordinate closures, the differentials of the Ib​ have a common positive lower bound s, and convergence in d1​ gives
∣DFn,b​(u)v∣≥2s​∣v∣
(16)
for all late n. The assumed uniform second-derivative bound gives a common bound L for D2Fn,b​ on those larger coordinate neighborhoods. If the bound is expressed using an intrinsic Hessian, the coordinate chain rule also involves first derivatives and derivatives of the fixed charts; their bounds are uniform by C1 convergence and compactness. Thus L can be larger than the printed constant C, but is independent of n.
For u,v whose segment lies in one such coordinate ball, Taylor formula with integral remainder gives
Fn,b​(v)−Fn,b​(u)=DFn,b​(u)(v−u)+R,∣R∣≤2L​∣v−u∣2.
(17)
Choose δ>0 with Lδ≤s/2. Then, whenever 0<∣u−v∣<δ,
∣Fn,b​(v)−Fn,b​(u)∣≥2s​∣u−v∣−2L​∣u−v∣2≥4s​∣u−v∣>0.
(18)
The Lebesgue number lemma applied to the finite smaller-chart cover, together with uniform continuity of its coordinate inverses on the relevant compact sets, gives ρ>0 such that every pair x,y∈M with 0<∣x−y∣<ρ lies in one such chart with coordinate distance below δ. The displayed estimate therefore excludes all sufficiently close pairs of equal images, uniformly in late n.
The remaining pairs form the compact set
Kρ​={(x,y)∈M×M:∣x−y∣≥ρ}.
(19)
If it is nonempty, injectivity of the smooth embedding i gives a positive minimum b of ∣i(x)−i(y)∣ on it. Uniform convergence makes supx​∣fn​(x)−i(x)∣<b/3 for all sufficiently large n, and hence on Kρ​,
∣fn​(x)−fn​(y)∣≥∣i(x)−i(y)∣−2supz​∣fn​(z)−i(z)∣>b/3.
(20)
If Kρ​ is empty there is nothing left to check. Combining the near-pair and distant-pair bounds makes fn​ injective for every sufficiently late n. It is already an immersion, and a continuous injection from compact M into a Hausdorff space is a homeomorphism onto its image. By compact injective immersion is an embedding,
fn​:M↪RN is a smooth embedding for all sufficiently large n.​
(21)
This proves the requested stability of compact smooth embeddings using the stated second-derivative assumption. That assumption supplies a convenient Taylor constant; the stronger C1 openness result can replace it by the uniform continuity of the limiting differential on finitely many small coordinate balls.
