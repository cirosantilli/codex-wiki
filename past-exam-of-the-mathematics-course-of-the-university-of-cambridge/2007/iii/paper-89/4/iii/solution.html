The overlap of the two charts is obtained by inverting c=t3. Since c2=b3, this also inverts b=t2; then t=c/b and t−1=b/c. Thus its coordinate ring is R[t,t−1]. The other chart has coordinate s=t−1. A global section is therefore an element in the intersection
Γ(X,OX​)=R[πt,t2,t3]∩R[t−1]=R
(24)
inside R[t,t−1]: a Laurent polynomial with both only nonnegative and only nonpositive exponents is constant.
Let k=R/(π). Reducing the presentation of B gives
Bs​=k[a,b,c]/(a2,ab,ac,c2−b3).
(25)
As a vector space and as a ring extension, this is
Bs​=k[b,c]/(c2−b3)⊕ka,
(26)
where a2=0 and a is annihilated by b,c. In particular a=0: the free basis of B in part ii remains a basis after tensoring with k. Although a mapped to πt before reduction, reducing the subring is not the same operation as taking its image inside k[t]; that distinction is exactly where the extra section arises.
On the overlap, c is invertible and ac=0, so a restricts to zero. Therefore the section a on the first chart glues to zero on the second chart and defines a nonzero global nilpotent section η on Xs​. More precisely the reduced first-chart ring is k[t2,t3], and
k[t2,t3]∩k[t−1]=k
(27)
in k[t,t−1]. The only extra compatible sections are multiples of a. Hence
Γ(Xs​,OXs​​)≅k[η]/(η2),Γ(X,OX​)=R.​
(28)
The map printed in the question reduces R modulo π and includes its image as constants. Its kernel is πR and its cokernel is kη. More strongly, even the usual base-change map after tensoring,
Γ(X,OX​)⊗R​k=k⟶k[η]/(η2),​
(29)
is not surjective. This is global-section base-change failure in a flat projective family, despite the properness and flatness proved above.
