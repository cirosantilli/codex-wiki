Use the polynomial graded ring R[x0​,x1​,x2​,x3​], as in the PDF; the doubled brackets in the converted TeX are an OCR error. Let I denote the homogeneous defining ideal. The four cubic coordinates in the proposed map never vanish simultaneously on PF1​, since y03​ and y13​ already have that property. Substitution makes each generator of I zero, so they define a morphism into the generic fibre.
The two standard opens D+​(x0​) and D+​(x3​) cover X. Indeed, a homogeneous prime containing x0​,x3​ contains x2​ by x0​x32​=x23​, and then contains x1​ by x12​=π2x0​x2​. Such a prime contains the irrelevant ideal and gives no point of Proj.
On D+​(x0​) put a=x1​/x0​, b=x2​/x0​, c=x3​/x0​. Over F, where π is invertible, the equations give
a=πt,b=t2,c=t3,t=a/π.
(17)
Thus the generic-fibre chart is exactly SpecF[t]. The proposed map restricts to this isomorphism with t=y1​/y0​. On D+​(x3​) put s=x2​/x3​. The equations give
x0​/x3​=s3,x1​/x3​=πs2,
(18)
so this chart is SpecF[s], and the map has s=y0​/y1​. On the overlap s=t−1. These are the standard two charts of the projective line, proving the scheme-level isomorphism
PF1​∼​XF​.​
(19)
Equivalently, rescaling x1​ by π−1 gives the usual twisted cubic. The chart proof also shows that the extra cubic relation introduces no additional generic-fibre scheme structure.
