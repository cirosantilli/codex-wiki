We give the bounds for an alphabet of size q≥2, including the binary case q=2. Let C be a length-n code with M codewords and minimum Hamming distance at least d, where 1≤d≤n. Write Aq​(n,d) for the maximum code size at a given distance and
Vq​(n,t)=∑j=0t​(jn​)(q−1)j
(20)
for the number of words in a Hamming ball of radius t. The jth term chooses the changed coordinates and the q−1 alternative symbols in each.
For the Hamming bound, put t=⌊(d−1)/2⌋. Balls of radius t around distinct codewords are disjoint: a common point would, by the triangle inequality, make their distance at most 2t<d. Counting those balls inside the qn ambient words proves
MVq​(n,⌊2d−1​⌋)≤qn,Aq​(n,d)≤Vq​(n,⌊(d−1)/2⌋)qn​.​
(21)
No linearity is needed. For a linear code of dimension k over Fq​, replace M by qk.
For the Gilbert–Varshamov bound, construct a code greedily, adding any word at distance at least d from all previously selected words, until none remains. The process terminates because the space is finite. Its maximality implies that the radius-(d−1) balls around its codewords cover the whole space: an uncovered word could have been added. These balls may overlap, but the size of their union is at most the sum of their sizes. Hence the constructed code has MVq​(n,d−1)≥qn, proving the existence bound
Aq​(n,d)≥Vq​(n,d−1)qn​.​
(22)
One may take the ceiling on the right because code size is integral.
For completeness, the sharper linear version is the Varshamov bound for linear codes. When q is a prime power and d≥2, an [n,k,≥d] linear code exists provided
j=0∑d−2​(jn−1​)(q−1)j<qn−k.​
(23)
Set r=n−k and choose n columns of an r×n parity-check matrix in order. When choosing column l, exclude all combinations of at most d−2 earlier columns, including the zero combination. There are at most ∑j=0d−2​(jl−1​)(q−1)j forbidden vectors, smaller than qr by the assumed inequality. A column can therefore be chosen. Inductively every set of at most d−1 chosen columns is independent: a relation involving the newest one would express it as a combination of at most d−2 earlier columns. The kernel has dimension at least n−r=k and minimum Hamming distance at least d, since a low-weight kernel vector would be such a column relation. If its dimension is larger than k, take a k-dimensional subspace. This proves the stated linear existence claim; it does not assume the greedy nonlinear code was linear.
To derive the asymptotic bounds, define the q-ary entropy
Hq​(x)=xlogq​(q−1)−xlogq​x−(1−x)logq​(1−x),
(24)
with the continuous endpoint conventions. Its derivative is logq​((q−1)(1−x)/x), so it is increasing on [0,1−1/q] and has maximum one at 1−1/q. Stirling's formula gives, uniformly for integer 0≤j≤n,
logq​[(jn​)(q−1)j]=nHq​(j/n)+O(log(n+1)).
(25)
The sum defining Vq​ is between its largest summand and (n+1) times that summand. For radius fractions tending to ρ≤1−1/q, monotonicity of Hq​ makes the largest exponent tend to Hq​(ρ). Consequently the Hamming ball volume exponent is
n1​logq​Vq​(n,tn​)⟶Hq​(ρ)if tn​/n→ρ∈[0,1−1/q].​
(26)
For ρ>1−1/q, the exponent is one: use a radius fraction just below 1−1/q for a lower bound and Vq​≤qn for an upper bound.
Set dn​=max{1,⌈δn⌉} and define the asymptotic rate-distance function by
αq​(δ)=limsupn→∞​n1​logq​Aq​(n,dn​),0≤δ≤1.
(27)
The code rate is n−1logq​M and the relative minimum distance is d/n. A limsup suffices; no unproved existence of a limit for optimal code sizes is required. The packing radii have fraction tending to δ/2, so the finite Hamming bound and the volume exponent give the asymptotic Hamming bound
αq​(δ)≤1−Hq​(δ/2)(0≤δ≤1).​
(28)
The covering radii have fraction tending to δ, giving the asymptotic Gilbert–Varshamov bound
αq​(δ)≥1−Hq​(δ)(0≤δ≤1−1/q).​
(29)
For larger δ, this covering argument gives only the trivial lower bound zero, not the expression obtained by extending the decreasing information entropy function beyond its maximum.
The asymptotic lower bound is also achievable by linear codes over a finite field. For δ>0 and sufficiently large n, take redundancy rn​=⌊logq​Vq​(n−1,dn​−2)⌋+1, so qrn​>Vq​(n−1,dn​−2). The linear existence condition produces dimension at least n−rn​; its rate has liminf at least 1−Hq​(δ). At δ=0, use the whole space, of rate one. In particular, for binary codes the bounds reduce to
1−h2​(δ)≤α2​(δ)≤1−h2​(δ/2)(0≤δ≤1/2),​
(30)
where h2​ is the binary entropy function.
