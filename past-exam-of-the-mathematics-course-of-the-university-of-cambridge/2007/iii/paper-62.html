a OurBigBook.com
The degeneracy factor gA​ counts internal states with the same energy, including the allowed spin states. The chemical potential μA​ measures the free-energy cost of changing the number of particles of species A; in this convention the rest energy remains in the exponential of the nonrelativistic Maxwell--Boltzmann number density.
The chemical potential is fixed by the densities of conserved charges and by chemical equilibrium between reactions. For a reaction ∑A​νA​A=0, chemical equilibrium requires ∑A​νA​μA​=0. Equivalently, when these are the only constraints, μA​ is a linear combination of the chemical potentials of the conserved charges carried by A. An unconstrained species has zero chemical potential. In particular, the photon chemical potential vanishes in thermal equilibrium when photon-number-changing reactions are effective.

For p+e⇌H+γ, chemical equilibrium and the vanishing photon chemical potential imply μH​=μp​+μe​. Taking the ratio of the three nonrelativistic Maxwell--Boltzmann number densities cancels these chemical potentials:

Write nb​=np​+nH​ for the cosmological baryon density in this hydrogen-only model, and xe​=np​/nb​ for the hydrogen ionization fraction. By charge neutrality, ne​=np​, so the Saha ionization equation becomes
xe2​1−xe​​=nb​(Me​T2π​)3/2eB/T.
(14)
The baryon-to-photon ratio is η=nb​/nγ​, and the equilibrium photon number density is nγ​=2ζ(3)T3/π2. At an order-one hydrogen ionization fraction, neglecting factors of order one therefore gives
1≃η(Me​T​)3/2eB/T,T≃log[η−1(Me​/T)3/2]B​.
(15)
Replacing T inside the slowly varying logarithm by B gives
T≃log[η−1(Me​/B)3/2]B​≪B.​
(16)
With B=13.6eV, Me​=5×105eV and η=10−10, this is T≃0.351eV≃4.1×103K. Solving the preceding implicit approximation instead gives T≃0.306eV≃3.5×103K. Both are reasonable estimates of the recombination temperature at the accuracy requested. The small baryon abundance delays hydrogen recombination: even when T≪B, the high-energy tail of the abundant photons can still ionize the much rarer hydrogen atoms.

A perturbation of the inflaton shifts its local clock: δϕ≃ϕ˙​δt. Regions with different clock shifts reach the end of inflation at different times and hence undergo different amounts of expansion. The inflaton clock-shift origin of curvature perturbations gives a local expansion difference of magnitude Hδt=Hδϕ/ϕ˙​. The sign depends on whether δt denotes a clock advance or a delay to a fixed end value.
The comoving curvature perturbation has amplitude R≃Hδϕ/ϕ˙​. For adiabatic evolution, superhorizon conservation of single-field comoving curvature carries this amplitude into the later growing density contrast, with the numerical conversion depending on the epoch and the density slice. Thus the approximate density-amplitude statement in the question follows; it does not identify the density contrast and R in every gauge.
The frozen scalar power per dlogk is (H/2π)2, so the slow-roll curvature power spectrum is
ΔR2​(k)=(2πϕ˙​H2​)k at horizon exit2​.​
(27)
Consequently ⟨R2⟩=∫(dk/k)ΔR2​(k), giving the requested approximate growing-density spectrum. Both H and ϕ˙​ are evaluated separately when each Fourier mode undergoes cosmological horizon exit.

After cosmological horizon entry in radiation domination, the oscillating radiation source averages away. Neglecting both that source and CDM self-gravity in the CDM density equation in a matter-radiation universe gives
δC′′​+τ1​δC′​=0,(τδC′​)′=0,
(36)
so δC​=C1​(k)+C2​(k)logτ. The superhorizon adiabatic CDM mode in radiation domination has amplitude AR​(k)τh2​∼AR​(k)k−2 at entry τh​∼k−1, fixing this scale for the subsequent logarithmic CDM growth after radiation-era horizon entry.
More explicitly, in a sharp-transition approximation with τh​=1/k, continuity of δC​ and δC′​ gives
δC​=k2AR​(k)​[1+2log(kτ)].
(37)
The matching the CDM growing mode at horizon entry therefore yields the late subhorizon scaling
δC​(τ,k)≃AR​(k)k−2log(kτ).​
(38)
Here ≃ suppresses order-one coefficients and the nonlogarithmic term, as in the question. A smooth treatment of the radiation forcing through entry changes those coefficients; the factor two is exact only for the sharp matching model just used. This logarithmic growth, rather than growth proportional to a, is the Mészáros effect.
