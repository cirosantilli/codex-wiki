Set d=−7. The affine rational pair (u,v)=(2,−1) gives P=(84,756) on the elliptic curve y2=x3−21168. It is a point of infinite order: in the rational torsion of a diagonal cubic classification, neither −7 nor −7/2 is a rational cube, so this curve has trivial rational torsion subgroup.
There is also a short direct torsion check specific to this curve. The primes 5 and 11 are good and have point counts 6 and 12, respectively, because cubing is bijective in their finite fields. Reduction at these two primes bounds the entire rational torsion by order dividing 6, including its possible 5- and 11-primary parts by reduction at the other prime. The flex calculation excludes order 3, and the inversion calculation excludes order 2. Hence P is indeed nontorsion.
Every nonzero multiple nP is distinct, and its inverse projective image has Z=0: the only rational point at infinity on the diagonal cubic is its identity. Thus each yields a distinct rational pair. More explicitly, if nP=(xn​,yn​) on the Weierstrass model, then
un​=6xn​yn​+252​,vn​=6xn​252−yn​​,un3​+vn3​=7(n≥1).​
