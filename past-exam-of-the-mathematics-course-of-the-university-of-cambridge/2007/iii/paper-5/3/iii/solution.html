Suppose first that G is uniform and H is a powerful open subgroup. The torsion criterion stated above makes G a torsion-free group; so H is a torsion-free group too. It is finitely generated, for example by the Schreier lemma, and hence is uniform by the same criterion. Put d=d(G) and e=d(H), and choose k with Pk​(G)≤H. For every n≥0,
Pn+k​(G)≤Hpn≤Gpn=Pn+1​(G).
(17)
Uniformity of the two groups gives
pnd≤[G:Hpn]=[G:H]pne≤p(n+k−1)d.
(18)
Taking logarithms and dividing by n, then letting n→∞, proves e=d. This proves (i)⇒(iii) without assuming that H is a power subgroup. Conversely every Pi​(G) is powerful and open, so (iii) applied to these subgroups gives (ii). Therefore all three conditions are equivalent.
