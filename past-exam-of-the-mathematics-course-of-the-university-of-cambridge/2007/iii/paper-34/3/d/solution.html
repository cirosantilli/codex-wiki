The Holevo-Schumacher-Westmoreland theorem gives the classical coding formula
C(Φ)=limm→∞​m1​χ∗(Φ⊗m),χ∗(Λ)=sup{pi​,ρi​}​χ({pi​,Λ(ρi​)}).
(37)
Its ensemble-coding statement makes every rate below a one-use output Holevo quantity achievable with product input encodings and collective output decoding. Under this product-input restriction, the capacity is
Cprod​(Φ)=sup{pi​,ρi​}​χ({pi​,Φ(ρi​)}).
(38)
For any such input ensemble, rates below its output Holevo quantity are achievable with asymptotically vanishing decoding error. Maximization gives the product-state classical capacity. The converse follows from the Holevo bound, entropy bounds on product outputs, and Fano's inequality.
Define the binary entropy h2​(x)=−xlog2​x−(1−x)log2​(1−x). For an input Bloch radius r≤1, the output eigenvalues are (1±(1−q)r)/2. Its entropy is
S(Φ(ρ))=h2​(21+∣1−q∣r​).
(39)
On [1/2,1], h2​ is decreasing, so this is minimized by pure inputs r=1. Throughout the completely positive interval 0≤q≤4/3, the minimum equals h2​(q/2), using symmetry h2​(x)=h2​(1−x). The average output entropy is at most 1, because it is a qubit. Hence every ensemble satisfies
χ({pi​,Φ(ρi​)})≤1−h2​(q/2).
(40)
Two equally likely orthogonal pure inputs attain this bound: their average output is I/2, and each individual output has entropy h2​(q/2). Therefore
Cprod​(Φ)=1−h2​(q/2)=1−h2​(2p/3)bits per channel use.​
(41)
This gives the Holevo capacity of a qubit depolarizing channel in the noise-weight convention used here.
For completeness, correlated classical code labels do not evade this upper bound. Each product input to n uses has product output entropy at least nh2​(q/2), whereas the average output entropy is at most n. Its Holevo quantity is consequently at most n[1−h2​(q/2)]. The Holevo bound and Fano's inequality rule out a reliable rate larger than this value. Combined with HSW achievability, this proves the capacity under the stated product-input restriction. It also remains valid in the extended physical parameter range arising from the original p assumption.
