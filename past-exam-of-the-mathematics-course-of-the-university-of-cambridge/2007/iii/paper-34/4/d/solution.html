Use the unnormalized Choi matrix
JΦ​=(ΦA​⊗idB​)(∣ΨAB​⟩⟨ΨAB​∣).
(46)
It is positive because ΦA​ is a completely positive map. Take its spectral decomposition, absorbing square roots of nonzero eigenvalues into the vectors:
JΦ​=∑k=1r​∣vk​⟩⟨vk​∣,r=rankJΦ​≤d2.
(47)
Expand ∣vk​⟩=∑i,j​vk,ij​∣i⟩A​∣j⟩B​ and define the Kraus operator Ak​ by (Ak​)ij​=vk,ij​. Equivalently,
∣vk​⟩=(Ak​⊗IB​)∣ΨAB​⟩.
(48)
The preceding relative state identity then gives (I⊗⟨ϕB∗​∣)vk​=Ak​ϕA​. Inserting the spectral decomposition into the stated reconstruction identity yields
ΦA​(∣ϕ⟩⟨ϕ∣)=∑k​Ak​∣ϕ⟩⟨ϕ∣Ak†​.
(49)
The conjugated index vector is required here; it is present in the PDF but missing in the converted TeX version of this identity.
Rank-one projectors span all operators by polarization. Explicitly, putting P(w)=∣w⟩⟨w∣ gives
4∣u⟩⟨v∣=P(u+v)−P(u−v)+iP(u+iv)−iP(u−iv).
(50)
Thus linearity extends the equality to every X∈B(HA​):
ΦA​(X)=k∑​Ak​XAk†​.​
(51)
Finally trace preservation and cyclicity of the trace give
TrX=TrΦA​(X)=Tr[X∑k​Ak†​Ak​].
(52)
For pure-projector inputs this says the Hermitian operator ∑k​Ak†​Ak​−I has zero expectation on every vector, so it vanishes. Therefore
k∑​Ak†​Ak​=IA​.​
(53)
This is the spectral Kraus decomposition. Using a normalized Choi state JΦ​/d instead would require the extra factor d​ when reshaping its eigenvectors into Kraus operators.
