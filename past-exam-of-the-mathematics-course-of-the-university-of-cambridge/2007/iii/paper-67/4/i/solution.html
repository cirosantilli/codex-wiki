A complete Einstein ring requires an axisymmetric gravitational lens and exact alignment of the source with the lens centre and observer: β=0. A finite aligned circular source gives a ring of finite width. The background source must lie behind the lens, so the lensing distance factor is positive.
With β=0 the thin gravitational lens equation gives
θE2​=c24G​Ds​Dd​Dds​​M(<Dd​θE​).​
(26)
The Einstein radius here is angular, and the mass is projected inside the corresponding aperture. For an extended mass distribution the equation is implicit because the enclosed mass depends on θE​.
Using the supplied approximations, Dds​/(Ds​Dd​)=1/(3×1025m) and θE​=3×10−4, the inferred mass is
M=4GθE2​c2​Dds​Ds​Dd​​=4(7×10−11)(3×10−4)2(3×108)2(3×1025)​kg≃8.7×1044kg.
(27)
Thus
M(<θE​)≃4.3×1014M⊙​.​
(28)
This measures the projected aperture mass without requiring the singular isothermal sphere model used subsequently.
