Write A=σσT and let V(x,t) denote the optimal remaining expected reward from positive portfolio wealth x at time t. The Hamilton-Jacobi-Bellman equation and terminal condition are
Vt​+θ∈Rd, c≥0sup​{(θTμ−c)Vx​+21​θTAθVxx​+Uconsumption​(c)}=0,V(x,T)=Uwealth​(x).​
(15)
The controls are progressively measurable, the investment integrals exist, consumption is nonnegative and locally integrable, and the resulting portfolio wealth stays in the utility domain. At zero consumption use the right limit of the utility function if needed. This is the usual admissible trading strategy formulation; without restrictions preserving the domain the reward need not be defined.
For the verification bound, assume V is a nonnegative classical solution, continuous through the terminal time, with one time derivative and two wealth derivatives in the interior. Fix any admissible controls and put
Rt​=V(Xt​,t)+∫0t​Uconsumption​(Cs​)ds.
(16)
The Itô formula gives
dRt​=[Vt​+(θtT​μ−Ct​)Vx​+21​θtT​Aθt​Vxx​+Uconsumption​(Ct​)]dt+Vx​θtT​σdWt​.
(17)
The drift is nonpositive because the actual controls cannot exceed the supremum in the Hamilton-Jacobi-Bellman equation. Localize by stopping times on which the stochastic integral is a true martingale, the derivatives and wealth stay in their interior domain, and the accumulated reward is finite. For each such stop τn​,
ERT∧τn​​≤V(X0​,0).
(18)
Both utilities and V are nonnegative. Thus the Fatou lemma, continuity, and the terminal condition give
E[Uwealth​(XT​)+∫0T​Uconsumption​(Cs​)ds]≤V(X0​,0).​
(19)
For admissible paths strictly positive on the closed horizon, the domain-localizing stops eventually reach T. If zero wealth is allowed, the same proof uses the continuous boundary extension of V and the utilities there. Nonnegativity is what permits removing the stops without assuming the uncontrolled stochastic term already has zero expectation. This is verification by a nonnegative control supermartingale.
The square-root case needs an additional no-arbitrage qualification because the printed volatility matrix need not be invertible. If there is v∈kerσT with vTμ>0, investment θ=Kv has zero volatility and arbitrarily large positive drift. With any fixed positive consumption, sufficiently large K preserves positive wealth and makes terminal square-root utility tend to infinity. For example, in one dimension σ=0, μ=1, θ=K, C=1 gives Xs​=x+(K−1)(s−t), so the value is infinite when T>t. A finite separated solution does not exist for all matrices allowed by the printed hypotheses.
The needed condition is μ∈RangeA, equivalent to μ being orthogonal to kerσT, because A is a symmetric matrix and kerA=kerσT. Assume this condition and let B=A+ be its Moore-Penrose inverse; if A is invertible, simply use B=A−1. Put q=μTBμ≥0. Seek the square-root investment-consumption value in the form
V(x,t)=2x​g(t),g(T)=1,g(t)>0.
(20)
Then Vx​=g/x​ and Vxx​=−g/(2x3/2). Completing the quadratic investment expression, using ABμ=μ, shows that its maximum is attained at θ∗=2xBμ and has value qgx​:
x​g​θTμ−4x3/2g​θTAθ=qgx​−4x3/2g​(θ−2xBμ)TA(θ−2xBμ).
(21)
Any investment component in kerA has no effect. For consumption, set z=c​. Its contribution is the concave quadratic 2z−(g/x​)z2, whose maximum is x​/g at c∗=x/g2. Substituting both maxima into the Hamilton-Jacobi-Bellman equation gives
2g′+qg+g1​=0.
(22)
With h=g2, this becomes h′+qh+1=0, h(T)=1. Solving this linear ordinary differential equation gives
h(t)=⎩⎨⎧​eq(T−t)+qeq(T−t)−1​,1+T−t,​q>0,q=0,​V(x,t)=2x​h(t)​.​
(23)
In particular h≥1, so g=h​ stays positive, and the two controls are
θt∗​=2Xt​Bμ,Ct∗​=h(t)Xt​​.​
(24)
These controls are admissible and actually attain the value. Indeed their portfolio wealth satisfies
Xs​dXs​​=(2q−h(s)1​)ds+2μTBσdWs​.
(25)
Since ∥2μTBσ∥2=4q, the explicit positive solution starting at (x,t) is
Xs​=xexp[−∫ts​h(r)dr​+2μTBσ(Ws​−Wt​)].
(26)
Its finite-horizon moments of every positive order are finite. More directly,
EXs​​=x​exp[2q​(s−t)−21​∫ts​h(r)dr​].
(27)
Differentiating g(s)EXs​​ and using the equation for g gives dsd​(g(s)EXs​​)=−EXs​​/g(s). Integrating from t to T therefore yields
E[2XT​​+∫tT​2Cs∗​​ds]=2g(t)x​.
(28)
This proves attainment without relying on an unjustified expectation of a local martingale. When μ is outside RangeA, the singular-volatility drift arbitrage counterexample applies instead.
