Fix t>0. For 0≤s≤t, the residual observed path is
Ys​−ts​Yt​=Ws​−ts​Wt​.
(29)
This Brownian bridge is independent of (λ,Yt​). Indeed it is independent of λ, while its covariance with Wt​ is sI−(s/t)tI=0; zero covariance implies independence in the joint Gaussian law. The same argument for every finite collection of bridge times, followed by continuity and generation from rational times, gives independence of the entire bridge. Since the full observed path is determined by its endpoint and this independent bridge, Brownian endpoint sufficiency for a constant drift gives
L(λ∣Gt​)=L(λ∣Yt​).
(30)
First suppose the prior covariance matrix V0​ is positive definite. The Bayes' theorem multiplies the prior Gaussian density by the conditional observation density, whose dependence on λ is exp(λTYt​−t∣λ∣2/2). Thus the posterior exponent, up to terms not depending on λ, is
−21​λT(V0−1​+tI)λ+λT(V0−1​λ0​+Yt​).
(31)
Completing the square gives the Gaussian Brownian drift filter:
λt​=(I+tV0​)−1(λ0​+V0​Yt​),Vt​=V0​(I+tV0​)−1,L(λ∣Gt​)=N(λt​,Vt​).​
(32)
For invertible V0​ these are equivalently Vt​=(V0−1​+tI)−1 and λt​=Vt​(V0−1​λ0​+Yt​). At t=0 they reduce to the prior parameters.
The boxed formulas remain valid for a singular positive semidefinite matrix V0​. To see this directly without inverse prior matrices, the joint Gaussian random vector (λ,Yt​) has
Cov(λ,Yt​)=tV0​,Cov(Yt​)=t(I+tV0​).
(33)
The latter matrix is invertible for t>0. The conditional multivariate normal distribution therefore has mean
λ0​+V0​(I+tV0​)−1(Yt​−tλ0​)=λt​
(34)
and covariance matrix
V0​−tV0​(I+tV0​)−1V0​=V0​(I+tV0​)−1=Vt​.
(35)
Alternatively, subtract this mean from λ; the resulting Gaussian random vector has zero covariance with Yt​ and is therefore independent of it, which proves these conditional parameters.
Write the proposed innovation process in the observable form
Wt​=Yt​−∫0t​λs​ds.
(36)
It is adapted to the observation filtration and continuous. To prove the martingale property, take 0≤u<v. Future increments of W are independent of the joint information generated by λ and the past of W, so
E[Yv​−Yu​∣Gu​]=(v−u)E[λ∣Gu​]=(v−u)λu​.
(37)
Also the tower property of conditional expectation gives E[λs​∣Gu​]=λu​ for s≥u. Conditional integration is legitimate because E∣λs​∣2≤E∣λ∣2<∞. Subtracting the integral proves E[Wv​−Wu​∣Gu​]=0. Removing a continuous finite variation process does not change quadratic covariation, hence
[Wi,Wj]t​=[Wi,Wj]t​=δij​t.
(38)
The Lévy characterization of multidimensional Brownian motion now applies: a continuous zero-start martingale with this bracket matrix is a standard d-dimensional Brownian motion relative to its given filtration. The observable innovation is a Wiener process in the observation filtration.
Differentiating the posterior formulas yields Vt′​=−Vt2​ and
dλt​=Vt​(dYt​−λt​dt)=Vt​dWt​.
(39)
For the final expression, the printed inverse matrices require the extra assumption that V0​ is positive definite. Under that assumption put At​=Vt−1​=V0−1​+tI. The Itô formula gives
d(λtT​At​λt​)=2λtT​dWt​+(∣λt​∣2+trVt​)dt,
(40)
since At​Vt​=I and tr(At​Vt2​)=trVt​. On the other hand,
dlogdet(I+tV0​)=trVt​dt.
(41)
The trace terms cancel in the innovation exponential for Gaussian drift filtering, leaving
dlogZt​=−λtT​dWt​−21​∣λt​∣2dt,dZt​=−Zt​λtT​dWt​.​
(42)
Thus Z0​=1 and Z is a positive stochastic exponential, hence a nonnegative local martingale. To obtain the requested supermartingale conclusion explicitly, choose localizing stopping times τn​↑∞ for which Zτn​ is a true martingale. The Conditional Fatou lemma gives
E[Zv​∣Gu​]≤liminfn​E[Zv∧τn​​∣Gu​]=liminfn​Zu∧τn​​=Zu​.
(43)
The same argument at u=0 gives EZv​≤1, supplying integrability. The process is a supermartingale in the observation filtration. No unproved exponential-integrability condition on the random drift is required.
For a singular prior the literal inverse-matrix expression in the PDF is undefined, but there is a natural extension with the same conclusion. Define, for fixed y,
L(t,y)=Ea∼N(λ0​,V0​)​exp(aTy−2t​∣a∣2),Zt​=L(t,Yt​)−1.
(44)
For positive definite V0​, a Gaussian integral shows this reciprocal is exactly the printed expression. For any positive semidefinite V0​, the integral is positive and finite, Lt​=−Δy​L/2, and ∇y​logL(t,Yt​)=λt​, by the same posterior weighting. The Itô formula consequently gives dlogL(t,Yt​)=λtT​dYt​−∣λt​∣2dt/2=λtT​dWt​+∣λt​∣2dt/2. Its reciprocal satisfies the boxed stochastic differential equation and the same supermartingale proof. This completes the singular-covariance extension while explicitly separating it from the undefined printed formula.
