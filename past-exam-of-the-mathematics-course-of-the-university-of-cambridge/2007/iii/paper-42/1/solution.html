Let I=(U′)−1 be the inverse marginal utility. The derivative U′ is continuous and strictly decreasing: concavity makes it nonincreasing, and equality at two distinct points would make U affine between them, contradicting strict concavity. The Inada conditions therefore make U′ a bijection from (0,∞) onto (0,∞). For each y>0, the derivative of U(x)−xy is positive below I(y) and negative above it. Consequently its unique global maximum is finite and
V(y)=U(I(y))−yI(y).
(1)
There is no need to differentiate I to find the first derivative of this utility conjugate. Comparing the maxima at y and y+h gives, for h>0,
−hI(y)≤V(y+h)−V(y)≤−hI(y+h).
(2)
Continuity of I, together with the corresponding inequalities for h<0, gives
V′(y)=−I(y),V′(0+)=−∞,V′(∞)=0.​
(3)
Thus V is strictly decreasing and strictly convex, since its derivative is strictly increasing. This proves all the asserted properties except the second differentiability, which does not follow from the printed hypotheses.

Near zero w(s) is asymptotic to s−2, and at infinity it is asymptotic to s−2; thus p(0+)=∞ and p(∞)=0. This smooth utility function satisfies every printed positive-domain assumption, but U′′(1)=0. For the financial assertions, use the positive-wealth admissible trading strategy class appropriate to the positive-domain utility function, and expectations for which the expressions are defined. A normalized terminal state-price density satisfies ZN​>0, EZN​=1, and defines an equivalent martingale measure Q by dQ=ZN​dP. A self-financing portfolio obeys
Xn​=x+∑k=0n−1​πk​⋅(Sk+1​−Sk​),

where the holdings are measurable before the next price increment. Under Q the gains are a martingale when integrable; for the usual lower-bounded admissible class, localization gives a supermartingale. In particular the state-price budget constraint is E[ZN​XN​]≤x. The positive-domain qualification matters: assumptions only on U at positive arguments do not control its values at nonpositive wealth.
The definition of the utility conjugate gives the pointwise inequality
U(XN​)≤V(yZN​)+yZN​XN​.

u(x)≤y>0inf​{v(y)+xy}.​

A complete market is one in which every terminal contingent claim in the specified claim class is attainable by a self-financing portfolio. Bounded terminal claims suffice for the uniqueness argument. In the standard finite-horizon discrete-time market, absence of arbitrage gives existence of an equivalent martingale measure by the fundamental theorem of asset pricing. To prove uniqueness, let ZN​ and ZN​ be two normalized state-price densities. Every bounded claim H has a replicating price p(H), and its replication has the same price under either equivalent martingale measure:
E[ZN​H]=p(H)=E[ZN​H].
(10)
Take H=1A​ for every terminal event A. The integrable difference ZN​−ZN​ has zero integral over every event, hence vanishes almost surely: its positive and negative sets would otherwise give a nonzero integral. The normalized terminal state-price density is unique up to null sets. Its density process is then uniquely Zn​=E[ZN​∣Fn​]. This uses the usual pricing replication class, where bounded claims have bounded replicating value processes; arbitrary doubling strategies are not an admissible definition of completeness.
For logarithmic utility, direct maximization gives V(y)=−logy−1. In the complete market, write Z=ZN​. The unique-density utility conjugate bound is minimized at y=1/x, giving
u(x)≤logx−ElogZ.
(11)
The candidate terminal portfolio wealth is H∗=x/Z, whose cost is exactly E[ZH∗]=x. If completeness includes nonnegative Q-integrable claims, it is replicable and attains the bound. There is also an approximation proof using only bounded-claim completeness. Define
bn​=EZ∨n−1Z​,Hn​=bn​(Z∨n−1)x​.
(12)
Then 0<bn​≤1, bn​↑1, Hn​ is positive and bounded, and E[ZHn​]=x. Replicating Hn​ therefore gives an admissible terminal claim of initial cost x. Since E(logZ)+≤EZ=1, the negative parts of −log(Z∨n−1) have an integrable common bound. Monotone convergence after adding this lower bound gives
ElogHn​=logx−logbn​−Elog(Z∨n−1)⟶logx−ElogZ.
(13)
Consequently the logarithmic terminal wealth in a complete market is
u(x)=logx−ElogZN​,H∗=ZN​x​.​
(14)
