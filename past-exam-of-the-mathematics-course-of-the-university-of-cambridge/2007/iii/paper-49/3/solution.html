A nondegenerate distribution function G is a max-stable distribution if, for every integer r≥1, there are ar​>0 and br​∈R such that
G(ar​x+br​)r=G(x)(x∈R).
(21)
Equivalently, the suitably centered and positively scaled sample maximum of r independent and identically distributed random variables with law G again has law G. The extremal types theorem states that every nondegenerate limiting law of normalized sample maxima is a max-stable distribution. Thus this property identifies all possible limiting types, although a particular starting distribution function need not admit any such limiting normalization.
The three standard types are the Gumbel distribution
Λ(x)=exp(−e−x),x∈R,
(22)
the Fréchet distribution, with α>0,
Φα​(x)={0,exp(−x−α),​x≤0,x>0,​
(23)
and the negative Weibull distribution, with α>0,
Ψα​(x)={exp(−(−x)α),1,​x<0,x≥0.​
(24)
They are max-stable distributions, as follows respectively by taking (ar​,br​)=(1,logr), (r1/α,0), and (r−1/α,0). The necessary and sufficient classification is: G is max-stable exactly when G(x)=H((x−b)/a) for a>0, b∈R, and one of these three types H. A positive affine map sends a max-stable distribution to another max-stable distribution.
