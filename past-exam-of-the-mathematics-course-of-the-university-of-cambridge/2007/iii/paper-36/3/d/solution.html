The stochastic integral is a true martingale on finite horizons, since the required 2k−2 moment is integrable in time. The continuous finite-variation term is not identically zero. If it were, its continuous derivative would vanish at every time, whereas at a fixed t>0, the nondegenerate Gaussian Bt​ does not almost surely satisfy the polynomial equation Btk−2​=βk−2​(t). Uniqueness of continuous semimartingale decomposition therefore rules out even the local martingale property. Hence
Btk​−βk​(t) is a martingale precisely for k=1,2.​
