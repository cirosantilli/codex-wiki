ε=P(∣G∣>C+2)>0,G∼N(0,1).

On this event at time one, the drift has absolute size at most C, while ∣Y0​∣<1, so ∣Y1​∣≥∣M1​∣−∣Y0​∣−C>1. Continuity forces exit by time one. Therefore

The same estimate applies conditionally at each integer time without assuming anything about future drift independence. On {T>n}, ∣Yn​∣<1; if ∣Mn+1​−Mn​∣>C+2, the drift bound forces exit by n+1. That Brownian increment is independent of Fn​. Hence

On intervals where K is constant, both M and D are constant. Thus du​=Dτu​​ is a continuous adapted function of clock time satisfying ∣dv​−du​∣≤C(v−u). The time-changed process is
Yu​=Yτu​​=Y0​+Wu​+du​.

The unit-clock argument uses only this increment bound on the drift, so its first exit time S obeys P(S>n)≤(1−ε)n. It is finite almost surely, and a corresponding finite ordinary time has ∣Y∣≥1. This proves the exit with drift controlled by quadratic variation result
T<∞almost surely.​
(46)
This proof needs only a nonnegative measurable bracket density, a continuous divergent integral clock, and the drift increment bound; pointwise continuity of the density is unnecessary. This observation permits the application below with measurable SDE coefficients.
For the multidimensional SDE, put aij​(x)=∑k=1m​σki​(x)σkj​(x). The generator of an SDE diffusion is
Lf(x)=i∑​bi​(x)∂i​f(x)+21​i,j∑​aij​(x)∂ij​f(x).​

For every f∈Cb2​(Rd), the Itô formula gives
f(Xtx​)−f(x)−∫0t​Lf(Xsx​)ds=∑k​∫0t​∇f(Xsx​)⋅σk​(Xsx​)dBsk​.

Bounded derivatives and bounded noise fields make the right side square-integrable on each finite horizon, hence a true martingale. This is precisely the defining test-function property of an L-diffusion, in the martingale-problem sense.
Now fix x and ξ=0. Its scalar projection has martingale part Mtξ​=∑k​∫0t​⟨ξ,σk​(Xsx​)⟩dBsk​ and drift ⟨ξ,b(Xsx​)⟩. The uniform ellipticity assumption gives

Let B∗​=supz​∣b(z)∣ and take R>∣⟨ξ,x⟩∣. Rescale the projection by R. Its bracket density is at least λ∣ξ∣2/R2, its absolute drift is at most B∗​∣ξ∣/R, and hence that drift is at most CR​ times its bracket density, with CR​=B∗​R/(λ∣ξ∣). Its clock diverges, so the exit lemma forces it out of (−1,1) in finite time. Equivalently it leaves the slab ∣⟨ξ,Xtx​⟩∣<R. Taking a countable sequence of integer R tending to infinity proves the projection unboundedness of a uniformly elliptic diffusion:
t≥0sup​∣⟨ξ,Xtx​⟩∣=∞almost surely, for each fixed x, ξ=0.​

For the final uniqueness result, let τD​=inf{t:Xtx​∈/D}, with x∈D. Since D is bounded, it lies in a finite slab; the preceding finite slab-exit time bounds τD​ from above. Thus τD​<∞ almost surely. Continuity gives XτD​x​∈∂D.
Set h=u−v. It is bounded, has bounded derivatives, is zero on the boundary, and satisfies Lh=0 in D. The stopped test-function identity says h(Xt∧τD​x​) is a martingale; equivalently it is a bounded local martingale, which suffices. Taking expectations and then using bounded convergence as t→∞ gives

Therefore the bounded-domain harmonic uniqueness for a diffusion conclusion is
u=v throughout D.​
(52)
In particular the argument supplies the representation u(x)=Ex​u(XτD​​) and establishes uniqueness without selecting a unique diffusion law.
