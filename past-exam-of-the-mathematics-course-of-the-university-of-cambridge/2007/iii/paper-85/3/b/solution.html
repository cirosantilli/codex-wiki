Use the natural dihedral group generators ρz=e2πi/nz and mz=zˉ, and orient the unfolding parameter so the trivial equilibrium loses stability as μ increases through zero. Rotation equivariance requires a monomial zpzˉq in z˙ to have p−q≡1(modn), while reflection makes its coefficient real. These constraints give the dihedral steady-state normal form. The reflection axes have angles θ=kπ/n and one-dimensional fixed-point subspaces of a group action; their equilibria are the branches guaranteed by the equivariant branching lemma.
For n=3, a generic quadratic anisotropy dominates:
z˙=μz+bzˉ2+az∣z∣2+⋯,b=0.
(32)
In polar coordinates, r˙=μr+br2cos3θ+O(r3) and θ˙=−brsin3θ+O(r2). At a nonzero equilibrium, χ=cos3θ=±1 and r=−μ/(bχ)+O(μ2)>0. There are three symmetry-related equilibria on each parameter side, on opposite half-axes as the sign of μ changes. The radial and angular eigenvalues are −μ+O(μ2) and 3μ+O(μ2), respectively. Hence all three nonzero branches are saddles in the full plane, even though radial stability exchanges at the crossing. The origin is a sink for μ<0 and a source for μ>0. The reflection normaliser has no residual sign reversal in this odd dihedral case, so these are transcritical-like branches, not symmetry-forced pitchforks.
For n=4, both relevant nonlinearities are cubic:
z˙=μz+az∣z∣2+bzˉ3+O(∣z∣5),r˙=r(μ+(a+bcos4θ)r2),θ˙=−br2sin4θ.
(33)
There are two conjugacy classes of reflection isotropy: the coordinate axes, with χ=cos4θ=1, and the diagonals, with χ=−1. Generically b=0 and a±b=0. Each class has a pitchfork bifurcation, with four symmetry-related equilibria when its squared radius is positive:
rχ2​=−a+bχμ​,ρrad​=−2μ,ρang​=−4bχrχ2​.​
(34)
These formulas give all generic existence and stability cases. If a<−∣b∣, both classes emerge for μ>0; the class with bχ>0 consists of stable nodes and the other of saddles. If a>∣b∣, both classes exist for μ<0; the class with bχ>0 consists of saddles and the other of sources. If ∣a∣<∣b∣, one class exists on each side and both consist of saddles. Equality a=±b is nongeneric and requires higher-order terms. Unlike the odd dihedral cases, the residual normaliser quotient contains a sign reversal, enforcing exact paired pitchfork branches.
For n=5, cubic radial saturation occurs before the first angular anisotropy:
z˙=μz+az∣z∣2+bzˉ4+O(∣z∣5),r˙=r(μ+ar2+br3cos5θ+⋯),θ˙=−br3sin5θ+⋯.
(35)
For generic a,b=0, ten equilibria lie on the reflection half-axes whenever −μ/a>0, divided into two distinct five-point group orbits. Their radii and eigenvalues are
rχ​=−μ/a​+O(μ),ρrad​=−2μ+O(∣μ∣3/2),ρang​=−5bχrχ3​+O(rχ4​),χ=±1.
(36)
If a<0, they emerge for μ>0: the five with bχ>0 are stable nodes and the other five are saddles. If a>0, they exist for μ<0: the five with bχ>0 are saddles and the others are sources. The leading amplitude scaling is pitchfork-like, but the quartic term distinguishes opposite half-axes and generally gives different corrections to their radii; there is no exact sign-reversal symmetry on an individual fixed line. Thus D3​ has quadratic saddle branches, D4​ has two cubic axial branch types, and D5​ has cubic radial branches whose angular stability splits at quartic order.
