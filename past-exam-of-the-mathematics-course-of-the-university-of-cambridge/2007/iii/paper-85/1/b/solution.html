It is invariant under (q,p)↦(−q,−p). Thus the large-s case has σ=1 and the small-s case σ=−1. At the origin the Jacobian matrix has determinant λ and trace κ: it is a saddle for λ<0, stable for λ>0,κ<0, and unstable for λ>0,κ>0. In the latter two cases the discriminant κ2−4λ distinguishes nodes from foci; its zero is not an equilibrium-creation bifurcation.
The nonzero equilibria satisfy p=0, q2=λ/σ. At either of them the determinant is −2λ and the trace is κ−2λ. Therefore for s≫1, a pair of saddles exists when λ>0, while for s≪1, a pair of attracting equilibria exists when λ<0,κ<2λ, and a repelling pair when λ<0,κ>2λ. The repelling or attracting points may be nodes or foci. Their origin collision occurs on the pitchfork bifurcation line λ=0, away from κ=0. For fixed κ=0, the scalar centre equation there begins
q˙​=κλ​q−κσ​q3+⋯.
(7)
For κ<0, the large-s case is a subcritical pitchfork and the small-s case a supercritical pitchfork. For κ>0, the scalar criticality reverses, but there is already an unstable transverse eigenvalue, so the full-plane branches have the stability just listed.
At the origin, κ=0, λ>0 is a Hopf bifurcation with frequency λ​. Averaging the leading weakly nonlinear oscillator gives the amplitude equation
r˙=2κ​r−4σ​r3+⋯.
(8)
Consequently
σ=1: stable cycle for κ>0, r2∼2κ;σ=−1: unstable cycle for κ<0, r2∼−2κ.​
(9)
These are respectively a supercritical Hopf bifurcation and a subcritical Hopf bifurcation, with λ>0 fixed and the cycle sufficiently small. The portraits change from a stable focus to an unstable focus surrounded by a stable cycle in the first case; in the second, a stable focus is surrounded by an unstable cycle before the focus becomes unstable and that cycle disappears.

Thus both nonzero Hopf bifurcations are supercritical: for small δ>0, each newly unstable well equilibrium is surrounded by a stable small cycle with r2∼δ.
To constrain periodic orbits, use either the divergence or the energy
H=2p2​+2λ​q2−4σ​q4,H˙=(κ−2σq2)p2,divF=κ−2σq2.
(12)
For σ=1,κ≤0, the divergence has one strict sign except on a line, and the Bendixson-Dulac criterion excludes every nonconstant periodic orbit. For σ=−1,κ≥0, the same reasoning excludes every periodic orbit. When the divergence changes sign, this criterion makes no such exclusion; the Hopf cycles above are consistent with that restriction. In the large-s case a periodic orbit also requires λ>0: if λ≤0, a positive maximum of q would have q¨​=−λq+q3>0, or a negative minimum would have negative acceleration, both impossible. For λ>0 the same extremum argument confines a cycle to ∣q∣<λ​, between the two saddle locations.
