Local equilibrium and Hopf bifurcations do not locate the larger cycles' endpoints. In the large-s case, the stable origin cycle is confined between two symmetry-related saddles. A natural global completion is a heteroclinic cycle formed by the two separatrix connections between those saddles. The stable periodic orbit expands into that connecting cycle and disappears; the two connections occur together because (q,p)↦(−q,−p) exchanges them. This is a global transition in the positive-κ, positive-λ region where the Bendixson-Dulac criterion permits cycles. Beyond it, the cubic inverted potential allows trajectories to leave the local region; the local reduction does not specify their ultimate fate in the original four-dimensional system.
In the small-s case, the two stable cycles born around the nonzero well equilibria can encounter the central saddle simultaneously. Their limiting orbits form a symmetric figure-eight pair of homoclinic orbits. A symmetric homoclinic gluing bifurcation can then replace the two one-well cycles by one larger cycle visiting both wells. There is also an outer unstable cycle associated with the subcritical origin Hopf branch, continued into the double-well region. A saddle-node bifurcation of periodic orbits between the large stable and unstable cycles provides a possible later termination. Thus the global curves needed for the usual completion are a symmetric heteroclinic connection in the large-s case, and paired homoclinic gluing together with a fold of cycles in the small-s case.
These connections, and any resulting basin changes, are not detected by the equilibrium Jacobians. Their precise parameter curves require a global return-map or separatrix calculation, which is not part of this requested qualitative description. Symmetry forces paired connections where indicated, but the local centre manifold and Hopf calculations alone should not be presented as numerical locations or a proof of the full large-amplitude diagram.
