An orthonormal coframe is a collection of independent differential forms Ea^=ea^μ​dxμ satisfying gμν​=ηa^b^​ea^μ​eb^ν​, with η=diag(−1,1,1,1). Locally one can diagonalize the metric tensor and rescale the resulting covectors, or use Gram-Schmidt orthogonalization for a symmetric bilinear form, choosing a timelike direction first. The coframe is not unique: a local Lorentz transformation preserves the metric. For the Levi-Civita connection, solve Cartan's first structure equation and metric compatibility together:
dEa^+ωa^b^​∧Eb^=0,ωa^b^​=ηa^c^​ωc^b^​=−ωb^a^​.
(1)
These determine the connection 1-forms. The antisymmetry applies to the lowered pair of indices; a mixed time-space pair instead has ω0i​=ωi0​. Next Cartan's second structure equation gives the curvature 2-forms, and reading off their coefficients determines the Riemann curvature tensor:
Ωa^b^​=dωa^b^​+ωa^c^​∧ωc^b^​=21​Ra^b^c^d^​Ec^∧Ed^,Rb^d^​=Ra^b^a^d^​.
(2)
This also states the curvature-sign convention. Contracting gives the Ricci tensor, and its coordinate components are Rμν​=ea^μ​eb^ν​Ra^b^​.
On a static patch with V>0, choose
E0=V​dt,E1=V​dr​,E2=rdθ,E3=rsinθdϕ.
(3)
Write h=V​, a=h′, b=h/r, and c=cotθ/r. The exterior derivative gives
dE0=−aE0∧E1,dE1=0,dE2=bE1∧E2,dE3=bE1∧E3+cE2∧E3.
(4)
The nonzero independent connection 1-forms are consequently
ω01​=aE0,ω12​=−bE2,ω13​=−bE3,ω23​=−cE3,
(5)
with ω10​=ω01​, spatial mixed pairs antisymmetric, and the remaining forms zero. For example,
Ω01​=d(aE0)=−(hh′′+h′2)E0∧E1=−2V′′​E0∧E1.
(6)
Similarly Ω02​=ω01​∧ω12​=−abE0∧E2, and the angular derivatives in dω13​ cancel against ω12​∧ω23​. The resulting Cartan curvature forms for a static spherical metric are
Ω01​Ω02​Ω12​Ω23​​=−2V′′​E0∧E1,=−2rV′​E0∧E2,=−2rV′​E1∧E2,=r21−V​E2∧E3.​Ω03​Ω13​​=−2rV′​E0∧E3,=−2rV′​E1∧E3,​​
(7)
For the last entry, d(−cE3)=E2∧E3/r2 and ω21​∧ω13​=−VE2∧E3/r2. The remaining curvature 2-forms follow from Ωab​=−Ωba​.
Put A=V′′/2, B=V′/(2r), and C=(1−V)/r2. Reading the Riemann curvature tensor from the forms and contracting gives the diagonal orthonormal Ricci tensor
Ra^b^​=diag(A+2B,−A−2B,C−2B,C−2B).​
(8)
For example R00​=R1010​+R2020​+R3030​=A+2B, while R22​=−B−B+C. All off-diagonal entries vanish because the curvature 2-forms only involve their own index planes. In the coordinate basis the nonzero components are
Rtt​Rθθ​​=V(2V′′​+rV′​),=1−V−rV′,​Rrr​Rϕϕ​​=−V1​(2V′′​+rV′​),=(1−V−rV′)sin2θ.​​
(9)
The angular Einstein field equations with the negative cosmological constant give (rV)′=1+3r2/l2. Integrating introduces one real constant, conventionally written −2m:
V(r)=1−r2m​+l2r2​.​
(10)
Substitution gives V′′/2+V′/r=3/l2, so the time and radial Einstein field equations hold as well. The solution is the Schwarzschild--anti-de Sitter black hole family, including its zero and negative mass limits.
For m>0, V increases strictly from −∞ at r=0 to +∞ at infinity. Its unique positive simple zero rh​ satisfies rh3​+l2rh​=2ml2. This zero is a coordinate singularity, not a curvature singularity: with the ingoing coordinate v=t+∫dr/V, the radial metric is −Vdv2+2dvdr and is regular there. Inside the future black-hole region, ∇r=∂v​+V∂r​ is future timelike, so every future-directed causal trajectory decreases r and cannot return to the asymptotic anti-de Sitter boundary. Conversely, every exterior radius admits an outward radial null geodesic reaching that boundary, since ∫r∞​dr/V converges. Thus m>0 gives an event horizon at the unique positive zero of V. Here the event horizon is defined relative to the asymptotic timelike boundary of Anti-de Sitter spacetime. For m=0, V=1+r2/l2>0 and the spacetime is pure Anti-de Sitter spacetime, with no black-hole event horizon. For m<0, V=1+2∣m∣/r+r2/l2>0, so there is also no event horizon; the mass term produces a naked singularity at r=0. The field equation itself does not impose a positive mass integration constant.

Use geometrized units with ℏ=kB​=1 initially, and write f(r)=1−2M/r, h(r)=1−Q2/(Mr). The given nonextremality assumption makes hH​=h(2M)=1−Q2/(2M2)>0. Thus the static Killing vector field has a simple zero of its norm at
rH​=2M.​
(11)
The exterior r>2M is static. For radial null geodesics, dt/dr=±1/f, independently of h. With v=t+∫dr/f, the metric is
ds2=−hf​dv2+h2​dvdr+r2dΩ2,
(12)
which is regular at rH​ since hH​>0. The outgoing null equation is dr/dv=f/2. Inside the future horizon these rays move to smaller radius, while outside they can escape to infinity. Hence the zero is the event horizon. The inner zero of h is not another regular Killing horizon: the Killing norm there diverges rather than vanishes. The strict charge bound matters when making the regular extension.
For the Euclidean black-hole regularity condition, continue t=itE​, consistent with the sign of the given quantum time-translation operator. Set x=r−2M. To leading order f=x/(2M) and h=hH​, so the radial–time part of the Euclidean metric is
dsE2​=2MhH​x​dtE2​+hH​x2M​dx2.
(13)
Introduce the radial proper length coordinate ρ by x=hH​ρ2/(8M). Then
dsE2​=dρ2+16M2ρ2​dtE2​+(2M)2dΩ2+O(ρ2).
(14)
The first two terms are a polar plane with angle tE​/(4M). Smoothness at its origin requires
ΔtE​=8πM.​
(15)
A different period would make a conical singularity. The time coordinate is normalized to proper time at infinity, so no further asymptotic redshift factor is needed. The cancellation of hH​ proves the charge independence of the Euclidean horizon period under a regular radial conformal factor; this argument would fail at hH​=0.
To justify the thermal interpretation, analytic continuation of the time-evolution operator U(t)=eiHt gives U(itE​)=e−HtE​. Its matrix element is an imaginary-time propagation kernel. Identifying the initial and final configurations and integrating over them takes its trace, giving the Euclidean path integral Z=Tre−βH. In an energy eigenbasis each state has weight e−βE, exactly the Boltzmann factor of a canonical ensemble. Thus the geometrically required period is the inverse Hawking temperature. With dimensions restored, an imaginary time period ΔtE​ gives β=ΔtE​/ℏ=1/(kB​T). Consequently
TH​=8πM1​in natural units,TH​=8πGkB​Mℏc3​(Q=0).​
(16)
Here M is mass in ordinary units; for a neutral hole M=GM/c2 and the physical imaginary-time period is 8πGM/c3. Bosonic thermal fields are periodic and fermionic fields antiperiodic over that same Euclidean period. The relation to the canonical density operator, not just dimensional reasoning, identifies the temperature.
For an evaporation estimate take a neutral, isolated Schwarzschild black hole and approximate its radiated power with the Stefan–Boltzmann law. Its area is A=16πG2M2/c4, and the photon Stefan-Boltzmann constant is σSB​=π2kB4​/(60ℏ3c2). Substituting the temperature gives
P≃AσSB​TH4​=15360πG2M2ℏc6​,dtdM​≃−15360πG2M2ℏc4​.
(17)
The black-hole evaporation speeds up as the mass decreases. Integrating, rather than dividing the initial rest energy by the initial power, gives
tevap​≃ℏc45120πG2M03​​≃2.1×1067 years(M0​=2×1030 kg).​
(18)
The corresponding initial Hawking temperature is about 6×10−8K. This is the Stefan-Boltzmann estimate of Schwarzschild evaporation time at the level of accuracy requested. Greybody factors and other emitted species alter the numerical coefficient, but not the leading cubic scaling of a large isolated hole. The estimate assumes negligible accretion or ambient radiation absorption; a stellar-mass hole in the present cosmic radiation bath is not currently losing mass at that isolated-hole rate. Finally the semiclassical formula does not describe the Planck-scale endpoint: extrapolating it to zero mass is an estimate of the macroscopic lifetime, not a proof of what the final quantum object is.

Use geometrized units, and denote the beacon's proper time by s to distinguish it from the short emitted duration τ. Take s=0 at release. The period M, the duration τ, and the wavelength λ are measured by the beacon's own clock. Its radial timelike geodesic has conserved Killing energy per unit rest mass E=fdt/ds, where f=1−2M/r. Initially dr/ds=0 at r=8M, so E=f(8M)​=3​/2. Normalization of the four-velocity gives
−1=−f(dsdt​)2+f1​(dsdr​)2,dsdr​=−E2−f​=−r2M​−41​​.
(19)
For the Schwarzschild radial fall from rest at a finite radius, introduce
r=4M(1+cosη)=8Mcos22η​,s=8M(η+sinη),0≤η≤32π​.
(20)
Indeed ds/dη=8M(1+cosη) and dr/dη=−4Msinη reproduce the inward radial equation. At the Schwarzschild event horizon, r=2M and cosηH​=−1/2. Hence
sH​=8M(32π​+23​​)=(316π​+43​)M≃23.6834M.​
(21)
This is the finite falling-clock time to the event horizon. By contrast, Schwarzschild time obeys dt/ds=E/f and diverges at crossing. Near the horizon r−2M≃E(sH​−s), so dt/ds≃2M/(sH​−s) and t≃−2Mlog((sH​−s)/M)+constant.
Every outward optical pulse emitted while r>2M can reach a distant observer, and no pulse emitted after entry into the future black-hole region can do so. The infinite coordinate crossing time does not produce infinite emissions, because the pulse clock runs in proper time. If there is a pulse at release, the escape times are sn​=nM for n=0,…,23. Thus 24 pulses are received, counting the pulse at release; 23 are emitted subsequently during the fall. More generally the PDF does not specify the phase of the pulse clock. For a first emission at s=δ with 0≤δ<M, the finite pulse count for a falling Schwarzschild beacon is
N(δ)=#{n≥0:δ+nM<sH​}=⌈MsH​−δ​⌉∈{23,24}.​
(22)
The ceiling handles a pulse scheduled exactly at crossing correctly: it cannot escape. This count concerns pulses associated with the fall, not any pulses emitted before release, and assumes an ideal observer able to detect arbitrarily redshifted signals.
To find the stretching of an optical pulse, use the Schwarzschild tortoise coordinate r∗​=r+2Mlog(r/(2M)−1). An outward radial null geodesic has constant Schwarzschild retarded null coordinate u=t−r∗​. At an observer's fixed large radius R, its arrival time is tR​=u+r∗​(R); therefore only the varying emission value of u contributes to the interval between arriving wavefronts. Differentiating along the falling source gives
D(r)=dsdu​=dsdt​−f1​dsdr​=f(r)E+E2−f(r)​​=E−E2−f(r)​1​.
(23)
The outgoing pulse redshift from a radial Schwarzschild infaller includes both gravitational redshift and the emitter's inward Doppler shift. A short pulse emitted at radius re​ has, to leading order in its duration,
τobs​≃D(re​)τ,νobs​≃D(re​)λ1​.​
(24)
For a finite receiver radius, replace D by f(R)​D to measure the receiver's proper time and frequency; this correction is negligible for R≫M. The inverse scaling of frequency follows because the number of carrier cycles is unchanged when the pulse is stretched. Here ν=1/λ is ordinary frequency, not angular frequency, in units with light speed one.
Under the release-phase convention, the last emission is at se​=23M. Solve 8(ηe​+sinηe​)=23 to obtain ηe​≃1.94374184, re​≃2.54255983M, and D(re​)≃7.49122492. Because τ≪M and this pulse begins 0.683364M before crossing, the local short-pulse approximation applies. Thus its approximate observed duration and frequency are
τlast​≃7.49τ,νlast​≃λ0.1335​if a pulse starts at release.​
(25)
Without fixing that phase, these numerical coefficients are not unique: put se​=δ+(N(δ)−1)M, find re​ from the parameterization, and use the boxed general formulas. For a last pulse close to crossing, with Δ=sH​−se​≪M but τ≪Δ, the horizon redshift of a Schwarzschild infaller gives the useful estimates
τlast​≃Δ4M​τ,νlast​≃4MλΔ​.​
(26)
If a phase instead places the pulse within one pulse-duration of crossing, Δ≲τ, a single constant stretching factor is inappropriate. The received extent of a complete escaping pulse is exactly u(se​+τ)−u(se​); a pulse straddling crossing has an escaping tail extending to arbitrarily late arrival times, whose instantaneous frequency tends to zero. The finite count remains valid, while its last-pulse duration then has no finite constant multiple of τ. This identifies precisely what the unspecified pulse phase can change.

Use geometrized units and assume M>0, ∣Q∣≤M, so the Reissner-Nordstrom spacetime contains a black hole. Its outer event horizon is r+​=M+M2−Q2​. The classical absorption cross-section is obtained in geometrical optics, by counting incident null geodesics captured from infinity. Spherical symmetry lets us use the equatorial plane. Write f(r)=1−2M/r+Q2/r2, and let a dot denote differentiation with respect to an affine parameter. The conserved Killing energy and angular momentum are E=ft˙ and L=r2ϕ˙​. Null normalization gives
0=−ft˙2+f−1r˙2+r2ϕ˙​2,r˙2=E2−L2r2f(r)​=E2(1−b2W(r)),
(27)
where the impact parameter is b=L/E and W=f/r2. An incoming ray turns around if the angular-momentum barrier reaches E2 before the ray reaches the event horizon. The limiting captured ray approaches the exterior unstable circular null geodesic, at the maximum of W.
The stationary-point equation is
W′(r)=−r52​(r2−3Mr+2Q2)=0.
(28)
The outer root is the photon sphere radius
rph​=23M+9M2−8Q2​​.​
(29)
It lies outside r+​: for ∣Q∣≤M it ranges from 3M down to 2M, whereas r+​ ranges from 2M down to M. More precisely rph​>2M except in the extremal case, and r+​≤2M; even at extremality rph​=2M>r+​=M. At this root,
W′′(rph​)=−rph5​2(2rph​−3M)​<0,
(30)
so the circular null geodesic is unstable. The smaller stationary root lies at or inside the outer horizon and does not set capture from infinity. For b<bc​, the radial equation has no exterior turning point and the ray enters the event horizon; for b>bc​, it turns around. The critical impact parameter is therefore
bc2​=W(rph​)1​=f(rph​)rph2​​.
(31)
The stationary-point equation gives Q2=(3Mrph​−rph2​)/2, hence f(rph​)=(rph​−M)/(2rph​). Since a parallel incident beam is captured over a disk of radius bc​, the photon capture cross-section of a Reissner-Nordstrom black hole is
σabs​=πbc2​=rph​−M2πrph3​​=2(M+9M2−8Q2​)π(3M+9M2−8Q2​)3​.​
(32)
The equality case approaches the unstable orbit indefinitely, but it is a measure-zero boundary of the captured disk and does not change the cross-section.
For the suggested parameterization, choose ψ with Q=3Msinψ/8​ and cosψ≥0. Then 9M2−8Q2​=3Mcosψ, so the same cross-section is
σabs​=227πM2​1+3cosψ(1+cosψ)3​.​
(33)
The black-hole range has ∣sinψ∣≤8​/3, hence cosψ≥1/3. For Q=0, rph​=3M, f(rph​)=1/3, and bc​=33​M, giving
σabs​(Q=0)=27πM2,​
(34)
the classical Schwarzschild black hole light-capture result. As an additional limit check, ∣Q∣=M gives rph​=2M, bc​=4M, and σabs​=16πM2. These are geometric capture areas, larger than the corresponding horizon areas; the relevant barrier is the photon sphere, not the horizon's apparent disk. The result applies to light in the short-wavelength limit, while a finite-frequency wave absorption cross-section depends on the greybody factors.
