First derive the triangle removal lemma from the Szemerédi regularity lemma. Given an allowed deletion fraction 0<η<1, take d=η/10, m0​=⌈10/η⌉, and 0<ε≤min(d/4,η/100,1/8). Apply part (i), with upper class bound M. Delete edges incident with V0​, inside each nonexceptional class, between irregular pairs, and between regular pairs of subset density less than d. Their total number is at most
(2ε+2m0​1​+2d​)n2<ηn2.
(35)
These four terms are bounded respectively by εn2, n2/(2m0​), εn2, and dn2/2.
If a triangle in a graph remains, its three vertices lie in three distinct equal classes, all three regular pairs with subset density at least d. The permitted regular triangle counting lemma gives at least (d3/4)t3 triangles in a graph in the original graph, where t is the class size. This lower bound can also be checked directly: all but 2εt vertices of the first class have at least (d−ε)t neighbors in each of the other two. Those neighborhoods are large enough to use the regular pair condition on the remaining pair, giving at least (d−ε)3t2 edges between them. Hence the total is at least (1−2ε)(d−ε)3t3≥(d3/4)t3.
Since t≥n/(2M), a surviving triangle in a graph forces at least d3n3/(32M3) original triangles in a graph. Thus a graph with fewer than ξn3 triangles in a graph, where ξ=d3/(64M3)>0, can be made triangle-free by deleting fewer than ηn2 edges, for all sufficiently large n. This proves the removal lemma needed here.
Now use the triangle-removal proof of Roth theorem. Fix a subset density lower bound δ>0 and suppose A⊆[N], ∣A∣≥δN, has no nonconstant three-term arithmetic progression. Set m=2N+1 and G=Zm​. Use three disjoint copies X,Y,Z of G and put edges according to
xy∈E⟺y−x∈A,yz∈E⟺z−y∈A,xz∈E⟺(z−x)/2∈A.
(36)
Division by two is valid because m is odd. A triangle in a graph corresponds to a=y−x, b=z−y, c=(z−x)/2 with a,b,c∈A and a+b=2c. Since A⊆[N] and ∣a+b−2c∣<m, this congruence is an integer equality. Progression-freeness therefore forces a=b=c.
Every triangle in a graph is consequently of the form (x,x+a,x+2a) with x∈G,a∈A, so there are exactly m∣A∣ triangles in a graph. These triangles in a graph are edge-disjoint: an XY edge determines (x,a); a YZ edge determines a and then x; and an XZ edge determines a=(z−x)/2 and x. Eliminating them all requires at least m∣A∣ edge deletions.
The graph has 3m vertices but only m∣A∣≤mN=O(m2) triangles in a graph. Take η=δ/100. For large enough N its triangle in a graph count is below ξ(3m)3, so removal requires fewer than η(3m)2=9δm2/100 deletions. Yet
m∣A∣≥δmN≥3δm2​>1009δm2​,
(37)
a contradiction. Therefore every fixed positive-density subset of a sufficiently long interval contains a nonconstant three-term arithmetic progression.
This proves the qualitative Roth theorem on three-term arithmetic progressions. The quantitative bound from part 2(i) came from the explicit Fourier iteration; the regularity-removal argument gives a density-dependent threshold without that double-logarithmic estimate.
