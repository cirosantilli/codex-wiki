A quantitative form of Roth theorem on three-term arithmetic progressions is: there is an absolute constant CR​ such that, for N≥3, every A⊆[N] with no nonconstant three-term arithmetic progression satisfies
∣A∣≤CR​loglogNN​.​
(8)
All logarithms below are natural. We prove the classical Roth bound for three-term progressions with an unoptimized CR​=100. The empty set is immediate; write δ=∣A∣/N>0.
First establish a quantitative density increment. Suppose N≥4⋅106δ−4. Put M=2N+1, embed I=[N] in G=ZM​, and extend all interval functions by zero. Let a=1A​, u=δ1I​ and f=a−u, the balanced subset indicator. Define
g​(r)=∑x∈G​g(x)e−2πirx/M,Λ(g1​,g2​,g3​)=∑x+z=2y​g1​(x)g2​(y)g3​(z).
(9)
The relation in the sum is modulo M. For interval elements its integer discrepancy has absolute value less than M, so it is precisely the integer relation. Thus Λ(a,a,a)=∣A∣=δN. The full interval count is the number of endpoint pairs of the same parity:
Λ(1I​,1I​,1I​)=⌈N/2⌉2+⌊N/2⌋2≥N2/2.
(10)
Consequently, since N≥4δ−2,
Λ(u,u,u)−Λ(a,a,a)≥4δ3N2​.
(11)
Orthogonality of the additive characters gives
Λ(g1​,g2​,g3​)=M1​∑r​g​1​(r)g​2​(−2r)g​3​(r),M1​∑r​∣g​(r)∣2=∑x​∣g(x)∣2.
(12)
Both identities follow by expanding the sums and using ∑r​e2πirt/M=M for t=0 in G, and zero otherwise; the latter follows from a finite geometric series. Since M is odd, r↦−2r permutes the frequencies. Telescope the difference as
Λ(a,a,a)−Λ(u,u,u)=Λ(f,a,a)+Λ(u,f,a)+Λ(u,u,f).
(13)
The Cauchy-Schwarz inequality and the displayed Parseval identity on a finite group bound each term by δNmaxr​∣f​(r)∣. Indeed, ∥a∥22​=δN and ∥u∥22​=δ2N, so every product of the two remaining L2 norms is at most δN. Hence
maxr​∣f​(r)∣≥12δ2N​.
(14)
The zero coefficient vanishes because ∑f=0, so choose a nonzero frequency θ=r/M giving this bound.
Let Q=⌊N​⌋. The pigeonhole principle applied to the Q+1 fractional parts 0,θ,…,Qθ in Q intervals gives an integer 1≤q≤Q with ∥qθ∥R/Z​≤1/Q. Partition each residue class modulo q in [N] into consecutive arithmetic progressions of exactly
L=⌊1000δ2N​​⌋
(15)
elements, leaving fewer than L remainder points per residue. The total remainder R0​ has size at most qL≤δ2N/1000. The size hypothesis gives L≥δ2N​/2000. On a full arithmetic progression P, the phase e−2πiθx varies from its value at the first point by at most 2πL/Q≤4πδ2/1000.
Let FP​=∑x∈P​f(x). Replacing the phase by its first value on each full arithmetic progression and estimating the remainder using ∣f∣≤1 gives
∑P​∣FP​∣≥12δ2N​−1000(4π+1)δ2N​>16δ2N​.
(16)
Also ∣∑P​FP​∣=∣∑R0​​f∣≤δ2N/1000. Therefore the sum of the positive FP​ is
21​(∑P​∣FP​∣+∑P​FP​)>64δ2N​.
(17)
Since the total length of these arithmetic progressions is at most N, at least one has FP​/∣P∣≥δ2/64. Thus the step just proved is
∣P∣≥2000δ2N​​,∣P∣∣A∩P∣​≥δ+64δ2​.​
(18)
An affine map taking this arithmetic progression to [∣P∣] preserves the absence of nonconstant three-term arithmetic progressions.
Now iterate. Write Nj​,δj​ for successive lengths and subset densities, with N0​=N and δ0​=δ. Since every subset density is at most one,
δj+1​1​≤δj​1​−64+δj​1​≤δj​1​−651​.
(19)
Consequently T=⌈65/δ⌉ successful steps are impossible. On the other hand, subset densities never decrease, so with B=log(2000/δ2),
logNj+1​≥21​logNj​−B,logNj​≥2−jlogN−2B.
(20)
Suppose δloglogN>100. Since T≤66/δ,
2−TlogN>exp(δ100−66log2​)>e54/δ.
(21)
Let D=log(4⋅106δ−4). We have 2B+D<31+8log(1/δ)≤39/δ<e54/δ. Therefore every length through step T satisfies logNj​>D, and hence Nj​≥4⋅106δ−4≥4⋅106δj−4​. The density increment lemma remains applicable for all T steps, contradicting the reciprocal-density bound.
Thus δloglogN≤100, proving the boxed theorem. The proof includes the Fourier analysis on a finite abelian group detection, conversion to an integer arithmetic progression, and the length bookkeeping that produces the double logarithm.
