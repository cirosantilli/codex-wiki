Fix C and write m=∣A∣. Choose the permitted prime N with Cm<N<2Cm. Part (ii) gives a subset A′ of size at least m/2 and its cyclic group model B⊆ZN​. Thus
N∣B∣​>4C1​.
(27)
To apply the interval form of Roth theorem on three-term arithmetic progressions, represent B in {0,…,N−1} and split this interval into two consecutive intervals of length at most ⌈N/2⌉. One has at least ∣B∣/2≥m/4 points. After translation, this is an integer subset B0​ of an interval of length at most Cm+1 and subset density bounded below in terms of C only. Pad the interval to length L=⌈N/2⌉ if necessary. If B0​ had no nonconstant three-term arithmetic progression, part (i) would give
4m​≤∣B0​∣≤loglogLC0​L​≤loglogLC0​(Cm+1)​.
(28)
Since L→∞ with m, this is impossible once m is sufficiently large depending only on C.
Thus B contains distinct elements b1​,b2​,b3​ with b1​+b3​=2b2​ modulo N. Their distinct preimages a1​,a2​,a3​∈A′ satisfy a1​+a3​=2a2​ in the integers by the Freiman 2-isomorphism. Reordering the endpoints if necessary gives
a1​,a2​,a3​ form a nonconstant three-term arithmetic progression in A.​
(29)
The required size threshold depends on the fixed constant C; it is not a claim uniform in an arbitrarily growing C.
