Assume A is finite and nonempty; the empty case is immediate. Here 2A=A+A. Choose a nonempty subset X⊆A minimizing
K=∣X∣∣X+A∣​.
(1)
Taking X=A as a competitor gives K≤C, and every subset Y⊆X satisfies ∣Y+A∣≥K∣Y∣, including Y=∅.
We first prove the Petridis minimal-growth lemma in the particular form needed: for every finite set S,
∣X+A+S∣≤K∣X+S∣.
(2)
List S={s1​,…,st​} and let Xj​ consist of those x∈X for which x+sj​ does not belong to an earlier translate X+sh​, h<j. The sets Xj​+sj​ partition X+S, so ∣X+S∣=∑j​∣Xj​∣.
If x∈X∖Xj​, then x+sj​=x′+sh​ for some earlier h and x′∈X. Hence every point of x+A+sj​ already belongs to X+A+sh​. Therefore the new points contributed by (X+A)+sj​ are contained in
[(X+A)∖((X∖Xj​)+A)]+sj​.
(3)
Their number is at most
∣X+A∣−∣(X∖Xj​)+A∣≤K∣X∣−K∣X∖Xj​∣=K∣Xj​∣.
(4)
Summing over j proves the lemma. With S=A, it gives
∣X+2A∣≤K∣X+A∣=K2∣X∣.
(5)
We also prove the Ruzsa triangle inequality directly. For each d∈U−V, choose one representation d=ud​−vd​. The map
(d,x)⟼(x+ud​,x+vd​)
(6)
from (U−V)×X into (X+U)×(X+V) is injective: subtracting the output coordinates recovers d, after which either coordinate recovers x. Thus ∣U−V∣∣X∣≤∣X+U∣∣X+V∣. Taking U=V=2A now yields
∣2A−2A∣≤∣X∣∣X+2A∣2​≤K4∣X∣≤C4∣A∣.​
(7)
This proves the required growth and difference set estimates directly.
