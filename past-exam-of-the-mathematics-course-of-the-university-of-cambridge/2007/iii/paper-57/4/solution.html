r(3+r2)=∣B∣,θ=3arg(−B)+2πk​,k=0,1,2.​

These also give V=0, and on each branch W=3B/(3+r2)=0. Consequently FC=0 but FT=0, so these are also supersymmetry breaking no-scale vacua with a cubic matter superpotential. A supersymmetric finite vacuum would require DT​W=0, hence W=0, and then DC​W=3C2=0; this is possible only at C=0 with B=0.
The scalar potential is independent of ImT everywhere. On every zero-energy vacuum branch it is also independent of ReT, so both real components of T are flat directions of a scalar potential at the vacuum. Away from DC​W=0, the real direction is not flat: ∂t​V=−3V/t, giving a runaway towards t→∞ rather than a finite positive-energy stationary point. The zero-energy branches have no continuous flat direction of a scalar potential in C; their allowed C values are isolated. This is why the vacuum does not select a unique numerical gravitino mass: the modulus t is unfixed.
The gravitino mass from a superpotential is m3/2​=eK/2∣W∣. On the conventional C=0 branch,
m3/2​=(T+Tˉ)3/2∣B∣​,Vmin​=0.​

When B=0, the only finite zero-energy matter solution is C=0 and m3/2​=0, with unbroken supersymmetry. These results exhibit the distinction between vanishing vacuum energy and vanishing supersymmetry breaking auxiliary fields in a no-scale supergravity model.
