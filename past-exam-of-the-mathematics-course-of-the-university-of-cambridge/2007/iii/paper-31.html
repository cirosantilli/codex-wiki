Solution

a OurBigBook.com
Take m≥2, as required for the lower bound in this part. A binary Huffman code has a full binary tree: every merge has two children, and all codewords are leaves. More generally an optimal prefix code for positive-probability symbols cannot have an internal vertex with only one child, since suppressing that vertex shortens its descendant codewords and reduces the expected length.
Choose a leaf of maximum depth s. Its sibling must also be a leaf, since an internal sibling would have a descendant deeper than s. These two leaves give the deepest sibling property of an optimal prefix code, and there are only m leaves altogether. Therefore
2≤ns​≤m.​
(1)
For the degenerate singleton alphabet, an empty codeword suffices and ns​=1; the stated lower bound consequently excludes m=1.

b OurBigBook.com
If all leaves of a full binary tree have depth s, all binary paths of length s end in leaves. Thus it has exactly 2s leaves and m=2s. Equivalently, equality in the Kraft inequality gives m2−s=1.
Conversely, when m=2k a complete depth-k tree is a prefix code with mean length k. For any prefix lengths si​, convexity gives
2−L≤m1​∑i=1m​2−si​≤m1​,L=m1​∑i​si​,
(2)
so L≥log2​m=k. The complete tree is optimal. A Huffman code attains this optimum, and equality in the strictly convex first inequality forces all its lengths to equal k. Hence, for m≥2,
ns​=m⟺m is a power of two.​
(3)
This also proves that ties in the Huffman construction do not change the conclusion.

c OurBigBook.com
Use the balancing fact proved by the exchange argument in part d: every leaf of an optimal tree has depth s−1 or s. A full binary tree with this property has all 2s−1 possible vertices at level s−1; at least one of these is split into two deepest leaves. Thus
2s−1<m≤2s.
(4)
These inequalities characterize the maximum codeword length exactly:
s=⌈log2​m⌉.​
(5)
In the suggested notation m=a2k, 1≤a<2, this is s=k for a=1 and s=k+1 for 1<a<2. The distinction at a power of two is important.

d OurBigBook.com
Suppose a leaf has depth t≤s−2. By part a there is a sibling pair of leaves at depth s. These leaves are disjoint from the shallow leaf and its descendants, since a leaf has no descendants. Modify the prefix tree of a code by splitting the shallow leaf into two leaves at depth t+1 and replacing the deepest sibling pair by their parent as one leaf at depth s−1. The number of leaves remains m, so assign the equiprobable source letters bijectively to the new leaves.
The sum of depths for the three affected leaves changes from t+2s to 2(t+1)+(s−1). The change is
2(t+1)+(s−1)−(t+2s)=t−s+1≤−1.
(6)
All other depths are unchanged. Since probabilities are equal, this strictly decreases the expected codeword length, contradicting the optimality of the Huffman code. This is the balancing exchange for a uniform optimal prefix code. There is therefore no leaf of depth at most s−2, proving
si​∈{s−1,s} for every i,ns−1​+ns​=m.​
(7)
The argument applies to every optimal binary prefix tree for the uniform source, independently of how Huffman ties are resolved.

e OurBigBook.com
A full binary tree has Kraft sum one. For example, place mass one at the root and divide each internal vertex's mass equally between its children; a leaf at depth l receives 2−l, and the total mass on leaves remains one. Using part d gives the two equations
ns−1​+ns​=m,2s−1ns−1​​+2sns​​=1.
(8)
Multiplying the second equation by 2s and subtracting the first gives the uniform-source Huffman length distribution
ns−1​=2s−m,ns​=2m−2s,s=⌈log2​m⌉.​
(9)
If m=2s, the first number is zero and every codeword has length s. Otherwise both levels occur. The corresponding mean length is L=s−(2s−m)/m.

For the uniform 27-symbol model, s=⌈log2​27⌉=5. The uniform-source Huffman length distribution gives
n4​=32−27=5,n5​=54−32=22.​
(10)
Thus five symbols receive four-bit codewords and the other 22 receive five-bit codewords. To realize these lengths, start with all 32 binary words of length five and collapse any five disjoint sibling pairs into their four-bit parents. This leaves exactly five leaves at depth four and 22 at depth five. All source letters have the same probability, so which five letters receive the shorter codewords is arbitrary.
The expected codeword length is
L=275⋅4+22⋅5​=27130​≈4.815 bits per symbol.​
(11)
The 27 symbols may represent 26 letters and a space, but this idealization keeps them equiprobable; it is not an empirical frequency model of English.

Use base-two logarithms and put H=H(U1​)=−∑i​pi​log2​pi​, with 0log2​0=0. Zero-probability letters can be omitted. The random variables Ij​=−log2​pUj​​ are self-information values, are IID, and have finite mean H because the alphabet is finite. Independence gives
−log2​P(U1​=u1​,…,Un​=un​)=∑j=1n​−log2​puj​​.
(12)
For an arbitrary δ>0, define the typical set
Tn,δ​={u(n):P(u(n))>0, ​−n1​log2​P(u(n))−H​≤δ}.
(13)
The weak law of large numbers implies P(Tn,δ​)→1. Every string in this set satisfies
2−n(H+δ)≤P(u(n))≤2−n(H−δ).
(14)
This is the asymptotic equipartition property for the given finite IID source, here obtained directly from the weak law of large numbers.
For the upper bound, summing the lower bound on typical-string probabilities yields ∣Tn,δ​∣≤2n(H+δ). For sufficiently large n, its probability is at least 1−ε, so it is an admissible set in the definition of M(n,ε). Hence
limsupn→∞​n1​log2​M(n,ε)≤H+δ.
(15)
For the lower bound, let A be any admissible set and write ηn​=P(Tn,δc​)→0. Then
P(A∩Tn,δ​)≥1−ε−ηn​.
(16)
Each member of this intersection has probability at most 2−n(H−δ). Therefore, for all sufficiently large n,
∣A∣≥(1−ε−ηn​)2n(H−δ)≥21−ε​2n(H−δ).
(17)
This applies in particular to a minimum-size admissible set, giving a liminf at least H−δ. Letting δ↓0 proves both existence and the value of the minimal high-probability source-set exponent:
n→∞lim​n1​log2​M(n,ε)=H(U1​)(0<ε<1).​
(18)
For a deterministic source, H=0 and M(n,ε)=1, consistent with the formula.
For coding, label the strings in an admissible set with distinct binary indices of length ⌈log2​M(n,ε)⌉. A decoder reconstructs those strings exactly and may fail outside the set. Thus asymptotically H bits per source symbol describe any fixed fraction 1−ε of the probability mass. More strongly, every rate R>H permits block codes with error tending to zero: choose δ<R−H, encode the typical strings individually and reserve one index for all other strings. The required number of indices is at most 2n(H+δ)+1≤2nR for large n.
Conversely, a block encoder with at most 2nR outputs can reconstruct at most that many distinct input strings correctly. If R<H, choose 0<δ<H−R. Its success probability is at most
ηn​+2nR2−n(H−δ)⟶0.
(19)
This gives the strong converse for fixed-rate source coding. It is the operational significance of the information entropy limit for reliable source encoding at a rate. The allowance of a small reconstruction error matters: a fixed-length code that must encode every possible string without error generally needs the larger rate log2​∣{i:pi​>0}∣.

We give the bounds for an alphabet of size q≥2, including the binary case q=2. Let C be a length-n code with M codewords and minimum Hamming distance at least d, where 1≤d≤n. Write Aq​(n,d) for the maximum code size at a given distance and
Vq​(n,t)=∑j=0t​(jn​)(q−1)j
(20)
for the number of words in a Hamming ball of radius t. The jth term chooses the changed coordinates and the q−1 alternative symbols in each.
For the Hamming bound, put t=⌊(d−1)/2⌋. Balls of radius t around distinct codewords are disjoint: a common point would, by the triangle inequality, make their distance at most 2t<d. Counting those balls inside the qn ambient words proves
MVq​(n,⌊2d−1​⌋)≤qn,Aq​(n,d)≤Vq​(n,⌊(d−1)/2⌋)qn​.​
(21)
No linearity is needed. For a linear code of dimension k over Fq​, replace M by qk.
For the Gilbert–Varshamov bound, construct a code greedily, adding any word at distance at least d from all previously selected words, until none remains. The process terminates because the space is finite. Its maximality implies that the radius-(d−1) balls around its codewords cover the whole space: an uncovered word could have been added. These balls may overlap, but the size of their union is at most the sum of their sizes. Hence the constructed code has MVq​(n,d−1)≥qn, proving the existence bound
Aq​(n,d)≥Vq​(n,d−1)qn​.​
(22)
One may take the ceiling on the right because code size is integral.
For completeness, the sharper linear version is the Varshamov bound for linear codes. When q is a prime power and d≥2, an [n,k,≥d] linear code exists provided
j=0∑d−2​(jn−1​)(q−1)j<qn−k.​
(23)
Set r=n−k and choose n columns of an r×n parity-check matrix in order. When choosing column l, exclude all combinations of at most d−2 earlier columns, including the zero combination. There are at most ∑j=0d−2​(jl−1​)(q−1)j forbidden vectors, smaller than qr by the assumed inequality. A column can therefore be chosen. Inductively every set of at most d−1 chosen columns is independent: a relation involving the newest one would express it as a combination of at most d−2 earlier columns. The kernel has dimension at least n−r=k and minimum Hamming distance at least d, since a low-weight kernel vector would be such a column relation. If its dimension is larger than k, take a k-dimensional subspace. This proves the stated linear existence claim; it does not assume the greedy nonlinear code was linear.
To derive the asymptotic bounds, define the q-ary entropy
Hq​(x)=xlogq​(q−1)−xlogq​x−(1−x)logq​(1−x),
(24)
with the continuous endpoint conventions. Its derivative is logq​((q−1)(1−x)/x), so it is increasing on [0,1−1/q] and has maximum one at 1−1/q. Stirling's formula gives, uniformly for integer 0≤j≤n,
logq​[(jn​)(q−1)j]=nHq​(j/n)+O(log(n+1)).
(25)
The sum defining Vq​ is between its largest summand and (n+1) times that summand. For radius fractions tending to ρ≤1−1/q, monotonicity of Hq​ makes the largest exponent tend to Hq​(ρ). Consequently the Hamming ball volume exponent is
n1​logq​Vq​(n,tn​)⟶Hq​(ρ)if tn​/n→ρ∈[0,1−1/q].​
(26)
For ρ>1−1/q, the exponent is one: use a radius fraction just below 1−1/q for a lower bound and Vq​≤qn for an upper bound.
Set dn​=max{1,⌈δn⌉} and define the asymptotic rate-distance function by
αq​(δ)=limsupn→∞​n1​logq​Aq​(n,dn​),0≤δ≤1.
(27)
The code rate is n−1logq​M and the relative minimum distance is d/n. A limsup suffices; no unproved existence of a limit for optimal code sizes is required. The packing radii have fraction tending to δ/2, so the finite Hamming bound and the volume exponent give the asymptotic Hamming bound
αq​(δ)≤1−Hq​(δ/2)(0≤δ≤1).​
(28)
The covering radii have fraction tending to δ, giving the asymptotic Gilbert–Varshamov bound
αq​(δ)≥1−Hq​(δ)(0≤δ≤1−1/q).​
(29)
For larger δ, this covering argument gives only the trivial lower bound zero, not the expression obtained by extending the decreasing information entropy function beyond its maximum.
The asymptotic lower bound is also achievable by linear codes over a finite field. For δ>0 and sufficiently large n, take redundancy rn​=⌊logq​Vq​(n−1,dn​−2)⌋+1, so qrn​>Vq​(n−1,dn​−2). The linear existence condition produces dimension at least n−rn​; its rate has liminf at least 1−Hq​(δ). At δ=0, use the whole space, of rate one. In particular, for binary codes the bounds reduce to
1−h2​(δ)≤α2​(δ)≤1−h2​(δ/2)(0≤δ≤1/2),​
(30)
where h2​ is the binary entropy function.

Use binary codes, so the polynomial modulus is f(X)=XN+1=XN−1 in F2​[X]. A cyclic code C is a linear subspace of F2N​ invariant under the cyclic shift (a0​,…,aN−1​)↦(aN−1​,a0​,…,aN−2​). Identify a word with a(X)=∑i=0N−1​ai​Xi. Addition is coefficientwise, and multiplication is polynomial multiplication reduced modulo f. In the quotient ring
R=F2​[X]/(f),
(31)
multiplication by X is exactly that cyclic shift. Closure under this map and linear combinations gives closure under multiplication by every polynomial, so a cyclic code is an ideal of R. Conversely, an ideal is a linear subspace closed under multiplication by X, proving the correspondence.
Take the inverse image of C in F2​[X]. This is an ideal containing (f). The polynomial division algorithm shows directly that it has a unique monic generator g: choose a nonzero polynomial of least polynomial degree and divide any other member by it; the remainder is in the ideal and has smaller polynomial degree, so must vanish. If two monic generators existed, mutual divisibility would make them equal. Because f belongs to the ideal, g∣f. Conversely every monic divisor of f defines an ideal of R. Thus the generator polynomial of a cyclic code satisfies
C=(g)⊆R,g∣XN+1.​
(32)
For a nonzero code, g is its nonzero polynomial representative of least polynomial degree. For the zero code, use g=f, whose residue in R is zero; this standard convention covers that endpoint despite its having no nonzero codeword.
Let r=degg and k=N−r, and put h=f/g. Every codeword has a unique representation bg with degb<k: dividing an arbitrary multiplier by h does not change its product with g modulo f. The words g,Xg,…,Xk−1g have distinct leading degrees below N and are independent. Consequently
dimC=N−degg=k.​
(33)
The statement remains valid for the zero code, with an empty basis, and the full code, with g=1.
For length 16, characteristic two gives X16+1=(X+1)16. Its only monic divisors are (X+1)j for 0≤j≤16. Hence all binary cyclic codes of length a power of two in this case are
Cj​=((X+1)j),dimCj​=16−j,0≤j≤16.​
(34)
There are exactly 17 codes, forming the chain C16​⊂C15​⊂⋯⊂C0​. This is a repeated-root cyclic code situation, so it would be incorrect to count just square-free factors. Their canonical check polynomials of a cyclic code are (X+1)16−j. An explicit expansion of each generator is ∏b:bit b of j is 1​(1+X2b).
The canonical check polynomial of a cyclic code is
h(X)=g(X)XN+1​,g(X)h(X)=XN+1.​
(35)
Indeed ah≡0(modf) means f∣ah. Since f=gh, cancellation in the polynomial ring gives g∣a, exactly code membership. No coprimality of g and h is needed, including for length 16.
There is a normalization qualification in the printed definition. The kernel condition alone does not force the supplied polynomial h to equal this canonical h. If d=gcd(f,h), write f=df1​ and h=dh1​ with gcd(f1​,h1​)=1. Then f∣ah holds exactly when f1​∣a, so its kernel is the ideal generated by f/d. Therefore
ker(a↦ahmodf)=C⟺gcd(f,h)=f/g.​
(36)
Equivalently h=hu with gcd(u,g)=1. Requiring the monic divisor of f selects the canonical check polynomial of a cyclic code. For example Xh gives the same kernel as h, since X is a unit modulo f, but is generally a different polynomial. Given any permitted h, taking gcd(f,h) recovers the canonical h.
To construct a parity-check matrix, write the canonical h=∑l=0k​hl​Xl and its reciprocal polynomial h∗(X)=Xkh(X−1). Take coefficient vectors, padded to length N, of
h∗, Xh∗,…,Xr−1h∗​
(37)
as the r rows of H. Here h0​=1 because h∣f, so these rows have successive leading degrees k,k+1,…,N−1 and are independent. For a word a, row j has dot product ∑l=0k​hk−l​aj+l​, the coefficient of Xk+j in the ordinary product ah. If a∈C, then ah=bf with degb<k. This product has no terms in degrees k,…,N−1, so HaT=0. The kernel of H has dimension N−r=k, the same as C, proving kerH=C. This proves the reciprocal-polynomial parity-check matrix construction and identifies its rows as a basis for the dual of a cyclic code.
Alternatively, a supplied check polynomial of a cyclic code reduced modulo f, h=∑l=0N−1​hl​Xl, gives the polynomial multiplication parity-check matrix
Bti​=ht−imodN​(0≤t,i<N).
(38)
This circulant matrix represents multiplication by h modulo f, so its kernel is C and its matrix rank is r. Selecting independent rows yields a rank-r parity-check matrix even without initially normalizing the supplied polynomial.
The syndrome s(y)=HyT labels the coset C+y. Indeed s(y)=s(z) if and only if H(y−z)T=0, equivalently y−z∈C. There are 2r cosets, each with 2k words, and the syndrome classification of code cosets gives a bijection between them and F2r​. If y=c+e is a transmitted codeword plus an error, then s(y)=s(e): the syndrome determines the error coset. Choosing a minimum-weight coset leader and subtracting it performs minimum-distance decoding. The error itself need not be uniquely identified, but it is unique when its weight is at most ⌊(d−1)/2⌋, since the corresponding error-correction balls are disjoint.
For the repetition code, the nonzero codeword is the all-ones word, so
grep​(X)=1+X+⋯+XN−1,hrep​(X)=X+1.​
(39)
Their product is XN+1, and the generator polynomial degree N−1 gives dimension one. For the single parity-check code, membership is a(1)=0, equivalent to divisibility by X+1. Thus
gpar​(X)=X+1,hpar​(X)=1+X+⋯+XN−1.​
(40)
Its dimension is N−1; it is the even-weight code.
Finally, factor the binary length-seven modulus:
X7+1=(X+1)(X3+X+1)(X3+X2+1).
(41)
A standard cyclic realization of the Hamming code of length seven uses
g(X)=X3+X+1,h(X)=(X+1)(X3+X2+1)=X4+X2+X+1.​
(42)
Its dimension is four. The reciprocal-check construction gives
H=​100​010​101​110​111​011​001​​.
(43)
Its columns are exactly the seven distinct nonzero vectors of F23​, so no word of weight one or two is in its kernel. The generator itself has weight three, so the minimum Hamming distance is exactly three; this is the [7,4,3] Hamming code. Reversing the cyclic coordinate order gives the equally valid reciprocal choice
g(X)=X3+X2+1,h(X)=X4+X3+X2+1.​
(44)
The polynomial answer therefore depends on the cyclic coordinate convention, while the resulting codes are equivalent.
