For positive frequency take p0=E=∣p∣2+m2​>0, with m≥0. Lowering the index in pμ​σμ is important: put
A=p⋅σ=EI2​−p⋅σ,B=p⋅σˉ=EI2​+p⋅σ.

p=(0B​A0​).

A qualification is essential if “spin along the first axis” is interpreted literally at zero mass. The fixed transverse rotation generator is Σ1​/2, with Σ1​=diag(σ1​,σ1​), and the massless Hamiltonian at this momentum is H=Eγ0γ3. They anticommute. If Hu=Eu and Σ1​u=u both held, then HΣ1​u=−Σ1​Hu=−Eu, whereas HΣ1​u=Hu=Eu, forcing u=0. Thus there is no nonzero positive-energy massless solution with that literal transverse-spin eigenvalue. The displayed nonzero spinor is the transported rest-spin label requested by the massive-to-massless construction, rather than a transverse-spin eigenstate.

The Yukawa interaction has one real-scalar leg and an oriented fermion line at each vertex. At lowest connected order, two vertices exchange one scalar, so the diagrams are of order λ2.
For two incoming fermions, there are two pairings of the outgoing fermions: the t channel joins p to p′ and q to q′, with scalar momentum p−p′; the u channel joins p to q′ and q to p′, with scalar momentum p−q′. Fermion number conservation excludes an annihilation channel for two particles carrying the same charge.
For an incoming fermion and antiparticle, there is a t-channel scalar exchange with momentum p−p′=q′−q and an s-channel annihilation and recreation diagram with momentum p+q=p′+q′. Arrows on antiparticle lines run opposite to the direction of physical propagation. These are the four tree scattering in Yukawa theory diagrams shown below; all external momenta are labelled.
The crossing in the u-channel drawing is not a vertex. Dashed internal lines are the real scalar field; solid arrows show fermion-number flow.

b OurBigBook.com
A gauge redundancy relates different field representatives of the same physical configuration. For example, the transformation just exhibited changes Aμ​ and the scalar phase but leaves Fμν​, ∣φ∣2 and all other gauge-invariant operators unchanged. Fixing a gauge removes this freedom; it does not remove a physical symmetry-related state. In canonical quantization, local transformations trivial at the boundary are generated by the Gauss law constraint in gauge theory, and physical states satisfy that constraint.
A global symmetry in field theory instead has a spacetime-independent parameter and can act nontrivially on physical states. For a continuous global symmetry in field theory, Noether theorem gives a conserved charge that generates the transformation and labels the states; discrete global symmetries do not acquire a Noether current from the same infinitesimal theorem. These are statements about physical transformations, rather than merely whether a parameter is written as a constant or a function.
Boundary conditions matter in gauge theory. gauge transformations that vanish at spatial infinity are redundancies, while transformations nontrivial at infinity may act through a physical electric charge. In particular a constant phase transformation can survive as a global charge symmetry rather than being quotiented out with the local gauge redundancy. Thus local gauge redundancy is a redundancy of description; genuine global symmetry in field theory can distinguish physical states and charges, with the boundary qualification made explicit.

At order e2, scalar particle–antiparticle scattering has a t-channel photon exchange and an s-channel annihilation and recreation diagram. They are shown below, with incoming momenta p,q and outgoing momenta p′,q′. The seagull vertex has only two scalar legs and does not yield a four-scalar tree diagram at this order.
For the exchange graph, let k=p−p′=q′−q. Apart from overall charge signs, the two scalar currents are J=p+p′ and K=q+q′. The external mass shell conditions give
k⋅J=p2−p′2=0,k⋅K=q′2−q2=0.
(27)
For the annihilation graph, k=p+q=p′+q′, with currents J=p−q and K=p′−q′, again up to vertex signs. Their contractions are p2−q2=0 and p′2−q′2=0. Thus each graph couples the photon to conserved currents, the relevant scalar quantum electrodynamics Ward identity.
Write k=(ω,k) and κ2=∣k∣2. conserved current means k⋅J=ωJ0 and k⋅K=ωK0. For κ=0, contraction with the Coulomb-gauge photon propagator gives, displaying the rational identity before the pole boundary limit,
JμDμνC​Kν​=κ2iJ0K0​+k2i​(J⋅K−κ2(J⋅k)(K⋅k)​)=k2iJ⋅K​+κ2iJ0K0​(1−k2ω2​)=k2i​(J⋅K−J0K0)=−k2i​J⋅K.​
(28)
We used k2=ω2−κ2. Restoring the common Feynman i-epsilon prescription yields the Coulomb-gauge propagator between conserved currents result
JμDμνC​Kν=Jμ(k2+i0−iημν​​)Kν.​
(29)
Hence each on-shell tree amplitude can use the Lorentz-invariant propagator. This is equality after contraction, not equality of the gauge-dependent tensors. At k=0, the separate Coulomb-gauge terms are ill-defined; combine them first and take the conserved-current limit. The combined expression has only the physical photon pole.

Antiparticles are positive-energy quanta of relativistic quantum fields, not physical particles with negative energies. The negative-frequency part of a quantum field creates these quanta; the distinction between its classical frequency and a state's excitation energy is central to their interpretation.
For a real scalar field, Hermiticity identifies the two frequency branches:
ϕ(x)=∫(2π)32Ep​​d3p​(ap​e−ipx+ap†​eipx).
(30)
There is one species of excitation. Its particle is its own antiparticle; a single Hermitian scalar cannot carry the nonzero continuous phase charge of a complex field. This does not prevent self-conjugate particles from having other quantum numbers.
A complex scalar field is not Hermitian. Its two independent sets of oscillators give
Φ(x)=∫(2π)32Ep​​d3p​(ap​e−ipx+bp†​eipx),Φ†(x)=∫(2π)32Ep​​d3p​(ap†​eipx+bp​e−ipx).
(31)
The canonical commutation relations make a† and b† create different positive-energy species. With charge convention fixed by [Q,Φ]=−qΦ, normal ordering gives
H=∫(2π)3d3p​Ep​(ap†​ap​+bp†​bp​),Q=q∫(2π)3d3p​(ap†​ap​−bp†​bp​).
(32)
Thus a†∣0⟩ and b†∣0⟩ have the same mass and spin but opposite charge. Although b†eipx has negative classical frequency, [H,b†]=Ep​b†, so it creates a positive-energy excitation. Two real scalar fields can be combined into one complex field; the particle–antiparticle basis diagonalizes the conserved phase charge. Neutrality alone does not require a particle to be self-conjugate.
Locality explains why both branches occur. For the complex scalar field,
[Φ(x),Φ†(y)]=∫(2π)32Ep​d3p​(e−ip⋅(x−y)−eip⋅(x−y)).
(33)
At spacelike separation, Lorentz invariance of the mass shell measure permits a frame with equal times; reversing p makes the two terms cancel. This is microcausality. If the antiparticle creation term were omitted, the commutator would be just a Wightman function, generally nonzero at spacelike separation. For example, for a massless scalar at equal times and separation r>0, it is 1/(4π2r2). The cancellation is the role of antiparticle modes and spacelike commutativity. In a real scalar field its self-conjugate creation modes supply the same cancellation.
A nonrelativistic particle field obeys a first-order time equation and need not contain an antiparticle-creation branch. For example,
L=iΨ†∂t​Ψ−2m∣∇Ψ∣2​,i∂t​Ψ=−2m∇2​Ψ,
(34)
allows an expansion using only ap​e−ip2t/(2m)+ip⋅x in Ψ; its adjoint creates those particles. The basic theory conserves particle number and does not impose a Lorentz-invariant light cone locality condition. It often arises from a relativistic quantum field by factoring out the rest-energy oscillation and restricting to a low-energy particle sector, where creation of particle–antiparticle pairs costs roughly 2m. An antiparticle species can certainly also be treated nonrelativistically with its own field; antiparticles are simply not forced by this nonrelativistic quantum field equation.
Historically, the Dirac equation has one-particle energy branches ±p2+m2​. Treating both as ordinary electron energies would leave no lowest-energy one-particle vacuum: an electron could fall through arbitrarily negative levels. The Dirac sea interpretation fills every negative-energy electron level. The Pauli exclusion principle then blocks further occupation. A missing negative-energy electron is a hole: removing energy −E increases energy by E, and removing charge −e leaves charge +e. This predicts the positron's mass, spin and opposite electric charge.
The quantum field theory account quantizes a Dirac field instead:
ψ(x)=∑r​∫(2π)32Ep​​d3p​(ar​(p)ur​(p)e−ipx+br†​(p)vr​(p)eipx).
(35)
The independent particle and antiparticle operators obey canonical anticommutation relations. Before normal ordering, the negative-frequency contribution to the Dirac Hamiltonian has the form −Ep​br​br†​. Anticommutation rewrites it as +Ep​br†​br​ plus a vacuum constant. Removing the constant leaves positive particle and antiparticle energies. The charge has opposite signs on the two occupation numbers, and interactions permit pair creation and annihilation while conserving the relevant charges.
There is therefore no need for a literal infinitely populated material sea. The hole picture is a useful fermionic reinterpretation, but it cannot explain bosonic antiparticles through filled-level exclusion, since bosons can multiply occupy a state. Canonical quantization of a complex scalar field supplies them just as consistently. More generally charge conjugation relates field representations with opposite internal charges; real scalar fields and Majorana fermions are self-conjugate exceptions. The relativistic Fock space description handles these cases, statistics and changing particle number within one framework.
