Use the Wirtinger derivatives ∂z​=(∂x​−i∂y​)/2 and ∂zˉ​=(∂x​+i∂y​)/2. In particular qzzˉ​=Δq/4. Let E=e−ikz−λzˉ/(ik), A=E(qz​+ikq) and B=E(qzˉ​+λq/(ik)), so the printed differential form is W=Adz−Bdzˉ. Since Ez​=−ikE and Ezˉ​=−λE/(ik),
Azˉ​Bz​​=E[qzzˉ​+ikqzˉ​−ikλ​qz​−λq],=E[qzzˉ​+ikλ​qz​−ikqzˉ​−λq].​
(14)
Thus the exterior derivative is
dW=−2E(qzzˉ​−λq)dz∧dzˉ.​
(15)
Because E never vanishes and k=0, this proves both directions: W is a closed differential form if and only if qzzˉ​=λq. The latter is the modified Helmholtz equation Δq−4λq=0 for positive λ. The associated global relations on a bounded region Ω are, for every nonzero spectral parameter,
∫∂Ω​e−ikz−λzˉ/(ik)[(qz​+ikq)dz−(qzˉ​+λq/(ik))dzˉ]=0.​
(16)
The boundary orientation is positive, by Stokes theorem. Each admissible spectral parameter gives a separate identity between the traces. Interchanging z and zˉ gives the dual family, equivalently the same family at k↦−λ/k with an overall minus sign. No assumption that q is real is needed.
For later use with λ>0, as in the remaining parts, make the global relation explicit in the first quadrant. Put
a(k)=k−kλ​,b(k)=k+kλ​,E=e−iax+by.
(17)
Let h2​(x)=q(x,0), n2​(x)=qy​(x,0) and h1​(y)=q(0,y), n1​(y)=qx​(0,y). These normals are inward-coordinate derivatives. The bottom is traversed from zero to infinity and the left side from infinity to zero. Their oriented spectral integrals are
ρx​(k)ρy​(k)​=i∫0∞​e−ia(k)x[b(k)h2​(x)−n2​(x)]dx,=∫0∞​eb(k)y[a(k)h1​(y)−in1​(y)]dy.​
(18)
Here ρx​ is analytic for Imk<0 and ρy​ for Rek<0, with suitable boundary limits. Indeed Ima=(r+λ/r)sinθ and Reb=(r+λ/r)cosθ for k=reiθ. On their common third quadrant, both exponential weights decay. Apply the finite-domain relation to expanding rectangles; sufficient decay of the solution and traces eliminates the distant edges. This derives
ρx​(k)+ρy​(k)=0,π<argk<3π/2.​
(19)
The limiting relations on the negative real and negative imaginary axes will remove the unknown derivative traces.
