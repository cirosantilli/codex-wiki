Use the finite map f:C→P1 from the preceding solution. The inverse images of the two standard affine charts and of their intersection are affine, because f is finite. The vanishing of quasi-coherent cohomology on an affine scheme therefore lets their two-term Čech cochain complex compute Hi(C,E). It has terms only in degrees zero and one, proving
Hi(C,E)=0(i≥2).​
(29)
This actually applies to every quasi-coherent sheaf on C.
For a smooth projective curve with H0(C,OC​)=k, define its genus of a smooth projective curve by
g=dimk​H1(C,OC​).​
(30)
The preceding Serre duality also gives g=dimk​H0(C,ΩC1​). The condition on constants holds in particular for a geometrically connected smooth projective curve. It makes g equal to the arithmetic genus 1−χ(OC​). We state the theorem with this usual convention and then give the formula valid without that condition.
For a divisor on an algebraic curve D and a canonical divisor K with O(K)≅ΩC1​, the Riemann-Roch theorem is
ℓ(D)−ℓ(K−D)=degk​D+1−g,ℓ(D)=h0(C,O(D)).​
(31)
The degree over an arbitrary field is weighted by residue degrees: degk​D=∑p​np​[k(p):k] when D=∑p​np​p. Equivalently, for a line bundle L,
h0(L)−h0(ΩC1​⊗L−1)=degk​L+1−g.
(32)
We now prove it via Riemann-Roch via elementary modifications.
For any closed point p and any line bundle L, the exact sequence
0⟶L(−p)⟶L⟶L∣p​⟶0
(33)
has a skyscraper sheaf as its quotient, with k-dimension [k(p):k]. This follows locally from a uniformizer of the discrete valuation ring at p: a local line-bundle generator modulo that uniformizer spans one copy of k(p). The quotient has zero higher cohomology, as it is the direct image from a finite affine scheme. The long exact sequence in sheaf cohomology and the proved higher vanishing give
χ(L)−χ(L(−p))=[k(p):k],χ(L)=h0(L)−h1(L).
(34)
Every line bundle has a nonzero rational section of a line bundle, whose local orders give a divisor on an algebraic curve D with L≅O(D). Iterating the displayed identity, adding or subtracting closed points with their multiplicities, gives
χ(O(D))=degk​D+χ(OC​).
(35)
For the constants convention above, χ(OC​)=1−g. The dualizing sheaf on a smooth projective curve gives h1(O(D))=h0(O(K−D)), which converts this Euler-characteristic identity into the stated Riemann-Roch theorem. This proves the formula over arbitrary k, without assuming the closed points are k-rational.
For vector bundles, the corresponding formula is
χ(E)=degk​(detE)+rank(E)(1−g).​
(36)
To see it, saturate a rational rank-one subspace to a line subbundle and induct on rank. Both Euler characteristic of a coherent sheaf and the degree of the determinant are additive in the resulting short exact sequence. The line-bundle formula just proved starts the induction.
Taking L=ΩC1​ and using duality gives h0(ΩC1​)=g, h1(ΩC1​)=1, and hence degk​ΩC1​=2g−2. In particular a line bundle of degree greater than 2g−2 has zero first cohomology: its dual twisted by ΩC1​ has negative degree, and a negative-degree line bundle has no nonzero section, since any section would have an effective divisor of zeros.
If the term “curve over k” is used without requiring H0(OC​)=k, the unconditional formula we proved is
χ(L)=degk​L+χ(OC​).​
(37)
Writing g=h1(OC​) then replaces 1−g by h0(OC​)−g. For example, Pk′1​ viewed as a k-curve for a finite separable extension k′/k has h0(OC​)=[k′:k], so a literal constant term 1 would be incorrect. Alternatively the arithmetic genus pa​=1−χ(OC​) gives the formula with constant 1−pa​. The constants hypothesis for the usual genus is recorded in Stacks Project, genus of a curve.
