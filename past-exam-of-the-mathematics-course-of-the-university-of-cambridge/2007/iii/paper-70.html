For an equatorial circular orbit in the Paczyński-Wiita potential, radial force balance gives
RΩ2=dRdΦ​=(R−RS​)2GM​,Ω2=R(R−RS​)2GM​.
(1)
Its specific angular momentum is j=R2Ω=GM​R3/2/(R−RS​). Perturbing the circular radius at fixed j gives the radial epicyclic frequency squared κ2=R−3d(j2)/dR. Therefore
κ2(R)=R(R−RS​)3GM(R−3RS​)​=Ω2R−RS​R−3RS​​.​
(2)
Circular motion is radially stable for R>3RS​, marginal at 3RS​, and unstable between RS​ and 3RS​. Thus the inner edge of the nearly circular thin accretion disk is Rin​=3RS​, joining a plunging region of a black-hole accretion disk inside the innermost stable circular orbit. This conclusion concerns the circular thin-disk model, not an assertion that all gas disappears inside this radius.
Take the inward mass accretion rate M˙ as positive, and write the positive outward viscous torque in an accretion disk as
G=−2πνΣR3Ω′(R).
(3)
Steady conservation of angular momentum makes the net inward flux C=M˙j−G independent of radius. Impose a zero-torque inner boundary condition at 3RS​, appropriate to an approximately stress-free transition to plunging motion. Then
C=M˙jin​=233​​M˙GMRS​​,G=M˙(j−jin​).​
(4)
This is the steady accretion disk with arbitrary rotation law angular-momentum balance.
The local viscous dissipation integrated through the full disk thickness is D=νΣ(RΩ′)2=−GΩ′/(2πR). It is the power per unit planar area summed over both radiating faces. Since
−Ω′=2R3/2(R−RS​)2GM​(3R−RS​)​,jjin​​=233​​(RRS​​)1/2RR−RS​​,
(5)
substitution gives the two-face dissipation of a Paczyński-Wiita disk:
D(R)=4πR(R−RS​)3GMM˙(3R−RS​)​[1−233​​(RRS​​)1/2RR−RS​​].​
(6)
In particular D vanishes at the stress-free inner edge and is nonnegative outside it because j increases there. A one-face radiative flux is D/2; the total accretion luminosity is therefore L=∫3RS​∞​2πRDdR, with no additional factor of two.
For a direct evaluation put x=R/RS​. Then
L=2RS​GMM˙​{∫3∞​(x−1)33x−1​dx−233​​∫3∞​x3/2(x−1)23x−1​dx}.
(7)
The first integral is 7/4, using (3x−1)/(x−1)3=3/(x−1)2+2/(x−1)3. For the second,
dxd​x​(x−1)1​=−2x3/2(x−1)23x−1​,
(8)
so it equals 2/[3​(3−1)]=1/3​. Hence
L=8RS​GMM˙​=161​M˙c2,ϵ=161​.​
(9)
This is the radiative efficiency of black-hole accretion within this pseudo-Newtonian model.
Independently, the specific orbital energy of a circular particle is
E(R)=21​R2Ω2+Φ(R)=2(R−RS​)2GM(2RS​−R)​.
(10)
Thus E(∞)=0 and E(3RS​)=−GM/(8RS​)=−c2/16. The energy released per unit accreted mass is exactly c2/16, confirming the integrated accretion luminosity. The Paczyński-Wiita circular orbit reproduces the Schwarzschild inner radius but gives an approximate binding energy; the efficiency derived here is for the stated potential.

Far from the inner edge of a steady Keplerian accretion disk, the inner angular-momentum flux is negligible compared with M˙j. Since Ω∝R−3/2, the viscous torque in an accretion disk becomes G=3πνΣj. The steady balance G≃M˙j therefore gives νΣ=M˙/(3π). With the stated surface density of a disk,
ν=ν0​R2,ν0​Σ0​=3πM˙​.​
(11)
Steady conservation of mass gives M˙=−2πRΣvR​, and consequently
vR​=−2πRΣM˙​=−2R3ν​=−23​ν0​R.​
(12)
Both relations use the far-inner-edge approximation, rather than extrapolating the stress-free boundary profile to the inner edge.
For the trace passive scalar, put σ=ΣC. Substitute this into the contaminant conservation equation and expand its advective flux:
0=∂t​(ΣC)+∇⋅(ΣCu)−∇⋅(kΣ∇C)=C{∂t​Σ+∇⋅(Σu)}+Σ{∂t​C+u⋅∇C}−∇⋅(kΣ∇C).
(13)
The braces multiplying C vanish by background conservation of mass. Hence the concentration satisfies
Σ(Ct​+u⋅∇C)=∇⋅(kΣ∇C).​
(14)
The small contaminant fraction ensures it does not alter the ambient disk dynamics. The surface-density model already averages vertically; an axisymmetric background and ring-like injection give C=C(R,t). More generally, rapid differential orbital motion winds azimuthal structure and facilitates mixing, but radial-only dependence remains a symmetry or mixing assumption, not a property of every initial injection.
For the axisymmetric advection-diffusion equation, cylindrical geometry gives

Here k=ζν and kΣ=ζν0​Σ0​ are independent of radius. Thus

Let Y=ln(R/R∗​), with any fixed reference length R∗​. Since CY​=RCR​ and CYY​=R2CRR​+RCR​, this is the tracer diffusion in a power-law accretion disk equation
ν0​1​Ct​−23​CY​=ζCYY​.​

Assume ζ>0, ν0​>0, and write v=3ν0​/2, D=ζν0​. The change X=Y+vt turns this into the heat equation Ct​=DCXX​. The radial Dirac delta function must be transformed with its Jacobian:

This full-line logarithmic solution extends the far-edge power-law model to 0<R<∞. An actual finite inner boundary would require its own boundary condition and modified kernel.
The fraction must be computed with the contaminant mass measure, rather than with CdR. Indeed

The complementary error function accounts for the one-time mass fraction outside the specified radius.
For an outward target r1​>1, put ℓ=lnr1​>0. Since erfc is decreasing, maximize F by minimizing z(t)=(ℓ+vt)/4Dt​. Its derivative has the sign of vt−ℓ, so the unique maximum occurs at t∗​=ℓ/v=2lnr1​/(3ν0​). At this time z(t∗​)=vℓ/D​. It is not, however, the probability of ever reaching the radius. A tracer can pass through the target and later return; its history is not counted by a one-time exterior fraction. The underlying logarithmic diffusion process is
Yt​−Y0​=−vt+2D​Bt​,
(23)
where Bt​ is standard Brownian motion. To compute its actual hitting probability, first put absorbing levels at −A and ℓ relative to Y0​. Conditioning on the first small time step gives the backward equation Dh′′−vh′=0 for the probability of reaching the upper level first, with h(−A)=0, h(ℓ)=1. Solving gives

Reflection symmetry gives Φz​(R,0)=0. In a vertically stable thin disk, expand the vertical force as Φz​(R,z)=Ωz2​z+O(z3), where Ωz2​=Φzz​(R,0) is the vertical epicyclic frequency squared. For local hydrostatic equilibrium and height-independent isothermal sound speed, p=cs2​ρ and
cs2​ρz​=−ρΩz2​z.
(26)
Thus ρ=ρ0​exp[−z2/(2H2)] with H=cs​/Ωz​. Integrating the pressure and mass density through the disk gives the vertically integrated pressure of an isothermal disk:
P=cs2​Σ=Ωz2​ΣH2.​
(27)
The force expansion is approximate in H/R, while P=cs2​Σ is exact for a height-independent sound speed.
In a nearly Keplerian disk, the vertical oscillation and radial epicyclic motion frequencies are close to the orbital frequency. The exact leading orbital differences are Ω−Ωz​ and Ω−κ, so
Ω−Ωz​=Ω+Ωz​Ω2−Ωz2​​≃ω1​,Ω−κ=Ω+κΩ2−κ2​≃ω2​.
(28)
Thus ω1​ is the nodal precession rate of a slightly tilted orbit, and ω2​ is the apsidal precession rate of a slightly eccentric orbit. Their signs distinguish prograde from retrograde precession. This also agrees with the sign convention in the complex tilt: a freely precessing ring obeys Wt​=iω1​W.
For exactly Keplerian motion, Ωz​=κ=Ω, so ω1​=ω2​=0 and P/Σ=cs2​. In the local approximation, freeze the slowly varying coefficients and take W,G∝ei(kR−ωt). The inviscid warp equations of a nearly Keplerian disk become
−iωΣR2ΩW=Rik​G,−iωG=4ik​PR3ΩW.
(29)
Eliminating G gives ω2=k2P/(4Σ)=k2cs2​/4. Hence
ω=±21​cs​k,vgroup​=±21​cs​.​
(30)
These are bending waves of a Keplerian disk, carrying changes in orbital tilt. The local approximation needs wavelength small compared with R; the secular thin-disk warp equations also describe the long-vertical-wavelength regime H≪λ≪R.
For a nonspinning hole, a=0 gives ω1​=0. A steady disk warp then satisfies GR​=0. The zero inner torque forces G=0 throughout, and the second warp equation, with positive P, forces WR​=0. The outer orientation fixes W(R)=W∞​ everywhere. An apsidal precession term by itself therefore does not require a tilt warp.
For nonzero spin, set the time derivatives to zero. The two equations give
GR​=−iω1​ΣR3ΩW,G=i4ω2​PR3Ω​WR​.
(31)

Use ω2​=3Ω/r, ω1​=2aΩr−3/2, Ω2R3=GM, and P≃Ω2ΣH2. The first coefficient becomes GMΣH2r/12 and the second becomes 2aGMΣr−3/2. Therefore

drd​(r9/4Wr​)+ϵ224a​r−9/4W=0,

The tilt magnitude is β(r)=∣W∞​∣∣cosx∣. Far out it tends to the imposed tilt. Moving inward, it decreases to zero at
