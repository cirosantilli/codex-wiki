An ℓ-group here means a locally profinite group, so identity neighbourhoods contain compact-open subgroups. The right action is a group representation because ρ(g)ρ(h)f(x)=f(xgh)=ρ(gh)f(x).
Let f be a compactly supported locally constant function with support S. For every x∈S, local constancy supplies a compact-open subgroup Kx​ with f constant on xKx​, and this coset lies in S. Compactness gives a finite cover S=⋃j=1r​xj​Kj​. Put K=⋂j​Kj​. Each coset in this cover is right K-invariant, so S is right K-invariant. Within the support, xk and x lie in the same chosen xj​Kj​ and have the same function value; outside the support, right K-invariance of S ensures that both values remain zero. Hence ρ(k)f=f for all k∈K. The zero function is fixed by every subgroup. This proves that the smooth right regular representation is smooth.
For a fixed compact-open subgroup K, right-invariant functions are functions on the discrete coset space G/K. Compact support becomes finite support there, since the image of a compact set in a discrete space is finite. Conversely every finitely supported function on G/K lifts to a compactly supported locally constant function on G. Thus

where the superscript indicates finite support. Its basis is the set of characteristic functions 1gK​, and its dimension is finite exactly when [G:K] is finite.
If G is compact, the discrete quotient G/K is compact and therefore finite for every such K; hence the representation is admissible. Conversely, admissibility gives finite index for any one compact-open subgroup. Then G is a finite union of compact cosets, so is compact. Therefore the representation is admissible if and only if G is compact.
