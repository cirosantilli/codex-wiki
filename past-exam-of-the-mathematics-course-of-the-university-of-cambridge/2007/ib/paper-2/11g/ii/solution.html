Hilbert's basis theorem: if R is a commutative Noetherian ring with identity, then the polynomial ring R[x] is Noetherian. Iteration gives the same assertion for every finite number of variables.
Here is a bounded-degree coefficient proof of Hilbert basis theorem. Let I be any ideal of R[x]. For n≥0, define Jn​⊆R to be the coefficients of xn in the polynomials of I of degree at most n, including the zero coefficient. Closure under addition and scalar multiplication makes Jn​ an ideal of R. Multiplication of a polynomial by x shows Jn​⊆Jn+1​. By the ascending chain condition, there is N such that JN​=JN+1​=⋯.
Each Jn​ for 0≤n≤N is finitely generated. Choose generators an,1​,…,an,rn​​ and corresponding polynomials fn,j​∈I of degree at most n whose coefficient of xn is an,j​. There are only finitely many chosen polynomials. We prove they generate I by induction on degree.
Take a nonzero f∈I of degree d and leading coefficient a. If d≤N, express a=∑j​cj​ad,j​ and subtract ∑j​cj​fd,j​. If d>N, use a∈Jd​=JN​, express a=∑j​cj​aN,j​, and subtract ∑j​cj​xd−NfN,j​. In both cases the leading coefficient cancels, leaving a polynomial in I of smaller degree. For degree zero the remainder is zero, and the induction completes the claim.
Thus every ideal of R[x] is finitely generated, proving the Hilbert basis theorem. The proof uses no cancellation of nonzero elements of R, so it remains valid when R has zero divisors.
