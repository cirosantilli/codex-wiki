In SI units, the two curl Maxwell equations are
∇×B=μ0​j+μ0​ϵ0​Et​,∇×E=−Bt​.
(55)
Use the vector identity ∇⋅(E×B)=B⋅(∇×E)−E⋅(∇×B) to obtain
∂t​(2ϵ0​E2​+2μ0​B2​)+∇⋅μ0​E×B​=−j⋅E.
(56)
Integration over the fixed volume and the divergence theorem give Poynting's theorem:
dtd​∫V​(2ϵ0​E2​+2μ0​B2​)dV+∫S​P⋅ndS=−∫V​j⋅EdV,P=μ0​E×B​.​
(57)
The first term is the rate of change of stored electromagnetic energy. The surface integral is the outward energy flux carried by the Poynting vector. The right side is minus the power delivered by the field to matter; for an Ohmic conductor it is minus the Ohmic heating rate. Thus energy lost from the field either flows out or does work on charges.
For the specified transverse plane wave, both divergence Maxwell equations hold because Ey​ depends only on x,t. Faraday's equation requires
B=ωkE0​​(0,0,1)cos(kx−ωt).​
(58)
Here we take the magnetic wave associated with the electric wave, without an additional static background field. Ampère's vacuum equation then gives k2=μ0​ϵ0​ω2, equivalently
ω2=c2k2,c=(μ0​ϵ0​)−1/2.​
(59)
Thus all four vacuum Maxwell equations are satisfied. The Poynting vector is P=(kE02​/(μ0​ω))cos2(kx−ωt)ex​, and averaging over a period gives
⟨P⟩=2μ0​ωkE02​​ex​.​
(60)
For propagation in the positive x direction, ω=ck and this is E02​ex​/(2μ0​c). It represents the mean energy transmitted per unit area per unit time, the wave's time-averaged energy flux per unit area, directed along its propagation.
