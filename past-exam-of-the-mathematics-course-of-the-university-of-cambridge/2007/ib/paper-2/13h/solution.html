For the first assertion, fix x0​∈[0,1] and ε>0. By uniform convergence, choose N such that ∣fN​(x)−f(x)∣<ε/3 for every x. By continuity of fN​ at x0​, there is δ>0 such that ∣fN​(x)−fN​(x0​)∣<ε/3 whenever ∣x−x0​∣<δ. The triangle inequality then gives
∣f(x)−f(x0​)∣≤∣f(x)−fN​(x)∣+∣fN​(x)−fN​(x0​)∣+∣fN​(x0​)−f(x0​)∣<ε.
(36)
Thus a uniform limit of continuous functions is continuous, including at the endpoints with their relative neighborhoods.
The converse implication involving integrals is false. Set f1​=0 and, for n≥2, use the shrinking continuous spikes with vanishing integral
fn​(x)=max{1−n2∣x−1/n∣,0}.
(37)
Their supports are [1/n−1/n2,1/n+1/n2]. For each x>0 they eventually vanish at x, and they vanish at zero for every n. Hence fn​→0 pointwise, with continuous limit. Each triangular area is 1/n2, so ∫01​fn​→0=∫01​0. Yet sup∣fn​∣=1, excluding uniform convergence.
The final assertion is also false. Take fn​(x)=xn. Then the pointwise limit is
f(x)={0,1,​0≤x<1,x=1.​
(38)
This limit is discontinuous at one but is Riemann integrable, with integral zero. Each fn​ is continuous and ∫01​fn​=1/(n+1)→0. Thus convergence of the integrals to the integral of an integrable pointwise limit does not enforce its continuity.
