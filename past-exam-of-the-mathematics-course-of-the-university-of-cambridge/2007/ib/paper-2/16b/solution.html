The orbital angular momentum operators are L=x×p, so
L1​=x2​p3​−x3​p2​,L2​=x3​p1​−x1​p3​,L3​=x1​p2​−x2​p1​.
(49)
The canonical commutation relations are [xi​,xj​]=[pi​,pj​]=0 and [xi​,pj​]=iℏδij​. On a common domain of smooth wavefunctions, the product commutator identity gives
[xa​pb​,xc​pd​]=−iℏδbc​xa​pd​+iℏδad​xc​pb​.
(50)
Contract this with ϵiab​ϵjcd​. The first term contributes iℏ(δij​x⋅p−xj​pi​) and the second contributes iℏ(xi​pj​−δij​x⋅p). Their sum is
[Li​,Lj​]=iℏ(xi​pj​−xj​pi​)=iℏϵijk​Lk​.​
(51)
This verifies the angular momentum commutation relations explicitly. Similarly,
[Li​,pj​]=ϵiab​[xa​pb​,pj​]=iℏϵijb​pb​,[Li​,pj​]=iℏϵijk​pk​.​
(52)
In the position representation p=−iℏ∇. For a radial wavefunction, ∇Ψ0​=Ψ0′​(r)x/r, parallel to x, so
LΨ0​=−iℏx×∇Ψ0​=0.​
(53)
Write Φi​=h(r)xi​ with h(r)=Ψ0′​(r)/r. The operator L3​=−iℏ(x1​∂2​−x2​∂1​) annihilates the radial factor. It gives L3​x1​=iℏx2​, L3​x2​=−iℏx1​, and L3​x3​=0, hence
L3​Φ3​=0,L3​(Φ1​±iΦ2​)=±ℏ(Φ1​±iΦ2​).​
(54)
Thus these components, when nonzero, are the specified eigenfunctions with eigenvalues 0,+ℏ,−ℏ. This is an example of angular momentum of a radial function times a linear polynomial.
