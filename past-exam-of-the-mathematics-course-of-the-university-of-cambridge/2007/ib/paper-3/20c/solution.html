For the Lagrangian sufficiency theorem, write a maximization problem as maxx∈D​f(x) subject to gi​(x)≤ci​. Here D may already incorporate simple constraints such as nonnegativity. Define the Lagrangian in optimization
L(x,λ)=f(x)−∑i​λi​(gi​(x)−ci​),λi​≥0.
(77)
The theorem states that a feasible point x∗​ is a global maximizer if there are such multipliers for which x∗​ globally maximizes L(⋅,λ) on D and complementary slackness holds: λi​(gi​(x∗​)−ci​)=0 for every i. To prove it, for any feasible x observe that
f(x)≤L(x,λ)≤L(x∗​,λ)=f(x∗​).
(78)
The first inequality uses feasibility and nonnegative multipliers, the second uses global maximization of the Lagrangian in optimization, and the last uses complementary slackness. Thus x∗​ is globally optimal. No convexity hypothesis is needed for this implication; concavity is useful when establishing the required global maximization of L from its gradient.
For the particular problem, put d=ec2​−1≥0 and R=c1​−2d≥0. The logarithmic constraint is equivalent to x1​≥d. The objective increases strictly with either variable, so at a maximum the resource constraint is tight: otherwise x1​ could be increased. Hence
x1​=2c1​−3x2​​,0≤x2​≤3R​.
(79)
On this interval the objective becomes F(x2​)=(c1​−3x2​)/2+3log(1+x2​), with

It increases up to x2​=1 and decreases thereafter, so the unique feasible maximizer is
x2∗​=min{1,3c1​−2(ec2​−1)​},x1∗​=2c1​−3x2∗​​.​

The two expressions agree at R=3. This includes the degenerate feasible set R=0, where x1∗​=d and x2∗​=0.
To certify the result using the Lagrangian sufficiency theorem, take D=[0,∞)2 and
L=x1​+3log(1+x2​)−λ(2x1​+3x2​−c1​)+μ(log(1+x1​)−c2​),

where λ,μ≥0. Set
λ=1+x2∗​1​,μ=(2λ−1)(1+x1∗​).

Since x2∗​≤1, λ≥1/2 and μ≥0. These choices make both components of ∇x​L vanish at x∗​: 1−2λ+μ/(1+x1∗​)=0 and 3/(1+x2∗​)−3λ=0. The Lagrangian in optimization is concave on D, so the zero gradient proves global maximization there. The resource constraint is tight. When R<3 the logarithmic constraint is tight; when R≥3, μ=0. Thus complementary slackness holds in every case, including both endpoints, and the theorem establishes the claimed optimum.
