(k+2)(k+1)ak+2​+(n(n+1)−k(k+1))ak​=0,

ak+2​=(k+2)(k+1)(k−n)(k+n+1)​ak​.​

The even and odd coefficients form separate chains. Choose a nonzero seed of the same parity as n and set the other seed to zero. In the chosen chain, none of the factors vanishes before k=n, and the factor k−n vanishes at k=n. Therefore this chain terminates with a nonzero an​: it gives a polynomial of degree exactly n.
To impose the normalization, its value at x=1 must be nonzero. Suppose a nonzero polynomial solution were y=(x−1)mq(x) with m≥1 and q(1)=0. The lowest-order coefficient in (1−x2)y′′−2xy′ would be −2m2q(1)(x−1)m−1, whereas n(n+1)y has order m. This cannot satisfy the equation. Thus divide by y(1) to obtain the Legendre polynomial Pn​ with Pn​(1)=1. Substitution or the coefficient recurrence gives
P0​(x)=1,P1​(x)=x,P2​(x)=21​(3x2−1).​

For an axisymmetric solution of the Laplace equation regular at the two polar axes, the separated modes and their superposition in spherical polar coordinates are
ϕn​(r,θ)=(An​rn+Bn​r−n−1)Pn​(cosθ),ϕ(r,θ)=∑n=0∞​(An​rn+Bn​r−n−1)Pn​(cosθ).

This is the usual separation of variables expansion; the singular angular solutions are excluded by regularity at the axes.
Here sin2θ=32​(P0​(cosθ)−P2​(cosθ)), so only degrees 0 and 2 are required. The degree-zero radial function A0​+B0​/r takes the same value 2/3 at r=a,b; since a<b, this forces B0​=0, A0​=2/3. Write the degree-two coefficient as −(2/3)T(r), where T=Ar2+Br−3 and T(a)=T(b)=1. Multiplying these boundary equations by a3,b3 and subtracting gives
A=b5−a5b3−a3​,B=b5−a5a3b3(b2−a2)​.

Each term is harmonic in the shell, and the radial factor is 1 at both boundaries, so this satisfies both Dirichlet boundary conditions. The maximum principle for harmonic functions, applied to the difference of two solutions, proves uniqueness among solutions continuous on the closed shell.
