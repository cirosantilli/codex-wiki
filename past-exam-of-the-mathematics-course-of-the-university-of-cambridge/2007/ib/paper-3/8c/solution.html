Under the null hypothesis, the defect count in each packet has a binomial distribution with parameters 3,θ. The likelihood, apart from factors independent of θ, is
L(θ)∝θ94+2(40)+3(6)(1−θ)3(116)+2(94)+40=θ192(1−θ)576.

The fitted binomial distribution probabilities for counts 0,1,2,3 are 27/64,27/64,9/64,1/64, yielding expected packet counts 108,108,36,4. The Pearson chi-squared goodness-of-fit test uses
X2=108(116−108)2​+108(94−108)2​+36(40−36)2​+4(6−4)2​=27104​≃3.852​.
(21)
For binomial goodness-of-fit with an estimated parameter, there are 4−1−1=2 statistical degrees of freedom: the category counts have a fixed total, and one parameter was estimated. At the conventional 5% level the chi-squared distribution with two statistical degrees of freedom has upper critical value 5.99, so the data do not reject the independent common-defect-probability model. The approximate p-value is e−X2/2≃0.146; even at 10%, the critical value 4.61 exceeds the observed statistic.
This is a goodness-of-fit conclusion rather than proof that individual bulbs are independent. The last expected count is only 4, so the chi-squared asymptotic approximation is somewhat marginal in that cell. The stated calibration is the usual asymptotic test with all four cells retained; if more accurate small-count inference is required, calibrate the fitted statistic under the binomial distribution model. Pooling cells would require a corresponding treatment of parameter fitting and test calibration.
