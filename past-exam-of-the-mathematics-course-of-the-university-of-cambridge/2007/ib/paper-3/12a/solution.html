For a regular parametrized surface σ(u,v), the first fundamental form is the induced metric
I=Edu2+2Fdudv+Gdv2,E=σu​⋅σu​,F=σu​⋅σv​,G=σv​⋅σv​.
(30)
Choose the unit normal N=(σu​×σv​)/∣σu​×σv​∣. With the convention IIij​=N⋅σij​, the second fundamental form is
II=edu2+2fdudv+gdv2,e=N⋅σuu​,f=N⋅σuv​,g=N⋅σvv​.
(31)
The Gaussian curvature, the product of the principal curvatures, is
K=EG−F2eg−f2​.​
(32)
For a compact smooth embedded surface without boundary, the Gauss-Bonnet theorem gives
∫S​KdA=2πe(S),​
(33)
where e(S) is the Euler characteristic. The no-boundary condition is needed for this form; a smooth embedded surface with boundary has the additional boundary geodesic curvature integral.
For the surface of revolution, use
σ(u,v)=((r+asinu)cosv,(r+asinu)sinv,bcosu).
(34)
The parameters are periodic, with local open parameter rectangles providing the coordinate charts. Put R=r+asinu and D=a2cos2u+b2sin2u​. Since r>a>0 and b>0, both R and D are positive. The tangent vectors are
σu​=(acosucosv,acosusinv,−bsinu),σv​=(−Rsinv,Rcosv,0).
(35)
Their inner products are E=D2, F=0, and G=R2. Thus
I=(a2cos2u+b2sin2u)du2+(r+asinu)2dv2.​
(36)
Their cross product is R(bsinucosv,bsinusinv,acosu), so its normalization gives the outward unit normal
N=D1​(bsinucosv,bsinusinv,acosu).
(37)
Next,
σuu​σuv​σvv​​=(−asinucosv,−asinusinv,−bcosu),=(−acosusinv,acosucosv,0),=(−Rcosv,−Rsinv,0).​
(38)
Taking their inner products with N yields e=−ab/D, f=0, and g=−bRsinu/D. Hence the fundamental forms of an elliptic ring torus give
II=−a2cos2u+b2sin2u​ab​du2−a2cos2u+b2sin2u​b(r+asinu)sinu​dv2.​
(39)
Choosing the inward unit normal changes both displayed coefficients of II to their negatives and leaves I and K unchanged. As a consistency check, K=ab2sinu/(RD4); its integral over the torus is zero, since KdA=ab2sinududv/D3 cancels under u↦u+π. This agrees with e(S)=0 in the Gauss-Bonnet theorem.
