Write X(φ)=φK. Since K2=I, the Exponential map of a Lie group gives
Λ(φ)=eφK=coshφ,I+sinhφ,K=(coshφsinhφ​sinhφcoshφ​).

For the null coordinates in two-dimensional Minkowski spacetime, direct substitution gives
u′=x′+y′=eφu,v′=x′−y′=e−φv.
(4)
Thus a boost dilates one null direction and contracts the other by the reciprocal factor. It preserves
uv=x2−y2.
(5)
The invariant curves are therefore the level sets x2−y2=c: the branches of hyperbolas for c=0, together with the two null lines when c=0. Each connected branch is preserved by the identity component.

Solving ΛTηΛ=η with detΛ=1 shows that every element is either Λ(φ) or −Λ(φ). Hence
SO(1,1)={Λ(φ):φ∈R}⊔{−Λ(φ):φ∈R}.
(6)
Each set is connected, but the sign of the (1,1) entry cannot change continuously because its absolute value is at least one. Thus SO(1,1) has two connected components. The matrix −I lies in the component disjoint from the identity.

For φ=±1, the Cayley transform is
C(φK)=1−φ21​(1+φ22φ​2φ1+φ2​).
(7)
Writing its diagonal and off-diagonal entries as a,b, one has a2−b2=1, so CTηC=η and detC=1. For ∣φ∣<1, put φ=tanh(t/2) to obtain C(φK)=Λ(t), so this interval covers the identity component. For ∣φ∣>1 the image lies in the other component and covers it except for −I, approached only as ∣φ∣→∞. The Exponential map of a Lie group reaches only the identity component, whereas the Cayley transform also reaches nonidentity-component elements but omits −I and is undefined at φ=±1.

Differentiating ΛTηΛ=η at the identity gives XTη+ηX=0. The six matrices
(Mμν)αβ​=ημαδβν​−ηναδβμ​,Mμν=−Mνμ,
(8)

J1​=M23,J2​=M31,J3​=M12,Ki​=M0i.
(9)
The Ji​ mix the two spatial coordinates perpendicular to xi and therefore generate rotations about that axis; Ki​ mixes x0 with xi and generates a Lorentz boost in the xi direction.

Matrix multiplication gives
[Ji​,Jj​]=ϵijk​Jk​,[Ji​,Kj​]=ϵijk​Kk​,[Ki​,Kj​]=−ϵijk​Jk​.
(10)
For example, [J1​,J2​]=J3​, [K1​,K2​]=−J3​, and [J1​,K2​]=K3​. Over C, define

Then [Ai​,Aj​]=ϵijk​Ak​, [Bi​,Bj​]=ϵijk​Bk​, and [Ai​,Bj​]=0. This proves the chiral decomposition of the complex Lorentz algebra. Its finite-dimensional irreducible representations are tensor products of irreducible representations of the two factors and are labelled by pairs (jL​,jR​) of nonnegative half-integers.

A coordinate transformation x↦Px conjugates an infinitesimal generator, hence Mμν↦PMμνP. It fixes the rotation generators Ji​ and negates the boosts Ki​. Therefore it exchanges Ai​ and Bi​, and the parity action on a Lorentz representation is
(jL​,jR​)⟼(jR​,jL​).
(12)
An irreducible representation is parity invariant precisely when jL​=jR​. If jL​=jR​, parity invariance requires the reducible sum (jL​,jR​)⊕(jR​,jL​). Thus the two Weyl-spinor representations (1/2,0) and (0,1/2) are exchanged, while their direct sum is the parity-invariant Dirac spinor.

Let W⊆g be invariant under the Adjoint representation of a Lie algebra. Then [g,W]⊆W, so W is an ideal. If g is a Simple Lie algebra, its only ideals are 0 and g. Hence the adjoint representation is irreducible. Compact type is compatible with the anti-Hermitian realization used below, although simplicity alone proves this adjoint irreducibility of a simple Lie algebra.

The Trace form of a Lie algebra representation is invariant because
H([X,Y],Z)​=Tr([d(X),d(Y)]d(Z))=Tr(d(X)[d(Y),d(Z)])=H(X,[Y,Z]).​
(19)
Choose a basis orthonormal for the positive-definite form −κ of the compact simple algebra. Any invariant bilinear form determines an endomorphism commuting with the irreducible adjoint action; Schur lemma makes it scalar. Thus H(Ta​,Tb​)=cδab​. Since d(Ta​) is anti-Hermitian,
H(Ta​,Ta​)=−Tr(d(Ta​)†d(Ta​))<0.
(20)
The inequality is strict because the kernel of the nontrivial irreducible representation is an ideal and hence zero. Therefore c=−μ with μ>0.
