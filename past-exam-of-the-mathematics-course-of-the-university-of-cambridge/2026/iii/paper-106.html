A Schauder basis of a real Banach space X is a sequence (en​) for which every x∈X has a unique norm-convergent expansion x=∑n​an​en​. Its basis projection is
Pn​x=∑k=1n​ak​ek​,
(1)
and its basis constant is Kb​=supn​∥Pn​∥<∞.
For the sequence space Y in the question, define
J:Y⟶X,J(a)=∑n=1∞​an​en​.
(2)
Convergence in the definition of Y makes J well defined, and uniqueness of basis coefficients makes it a linear bijection. Moreover,
∥J(a)∥=limn​∥∑k=1n​ak​ek​∥≤∥a∥Y​,
(3)
whereas
∥a∥Y​=supn​∥Pn​J(a)∥≤Kb​∥J(a)∥.
(4)
Thus J is an isomorphism and, in particular, the displayed supremum really is a complete norm on Y.
The coordinate functional of a Schauder basis is
en∗​(∑k​ak​ek​)=an​.
(5)
It is bounded because an​en​=(Pn​−Pn−1​)x. On finite linear combinations of the en∗​, the nth partial-sum operator is the restriction of Pn∗​, since
Pn∗​ej∗​={ej∗​,0,​j≤n,j>n.​
(6)
The operators have norms at most Kb​, so the standard basis criterion shows that the dual sequence of a Schauder basis (en∗​) is a basic sequence in X∗. For every x∗∈X∗ and x∈X,
⟨Pn∗​x∗,x⟩=⟨x∗,Pn​x⟩⟶⟨x∗,x⟩,
(7)
which is precisely Pn∗​x∗→x∗ in the weak-star topology.
Suppose now that X is a reflexive Banach space. If ∥x∗−Pn∗​x∗∥ did not tend to zero, approximation by finite basis blocks would give a bounded block sequence (uj​) and an ε>0 such that ∣x∗(uj​)∣≥ε. Reflexivity gives a weakly convergent subsequence. Every fixed coordinate functional is eventually zero on a block sequence, so its weak limit has every basis coordinate zero and is therefore zero. This contradicts ∣x∗(uj​)∣≥ε. Hence Pn∗​x∗→x∗ in norm for every x∗, so the basis is shrinking and (en∗​) is a basis of X∗.
The converse fails. The standard basis of c0​ is shrinking because its dual sequence is the standard basis of ℓ1=(c0​)∗, but c0​ is not reflexive.
Finally assume (en∗​) is a basis of X∗. Map x∗∗∈X∗∗ to
an​=x∗∗(en∗​).
(8)
For sn​=∑k=1n​ak​ek​,
∥sn​∥=sup∥x∗∥≤1​∣x∗∗(Pn∗​x∗)∣≤Kb​∥x∗∗∥,
(9)
so (an​)∈Z. Conversely, if (an​)∈Z and M=supn​∥sn​∥, define
F(x∗)=limn​x∗(sn​).
(10)
The limit exists: for m>n,
∣x∗(sm​−sn​)∣≤2M∥x∗−Pn∗​x∗∥⟶0
(11)
because (en∗​) is a basis. Also ∣F(x∗)∣≤M∥x∗∥, so F∈X∗∗ and F(en∗​)=an​. These two constructions are inverse and satisfy
∥F∥≤∥(an​)∥Z​≤Kb​∥F∥.
(12)
Thus X∗∗ and Z are isomorphic.

For x in a unital complex algebra A, the spectrum of an element is
σA​(x)={λ∈C:x−λ1 is not invertible}.
(13)
For a nonunital algebra one uses its unitization.
Now let A be a Banach algebra. The invertible group is open, so the resolvent set is open and the spectrum is closed. If ∣λ∣>∥x∥, the Neumann series
(λ1−x)−1=λ−1∑n=0∞​(x/λ)n
(14)
converges, so σA​(x) is contained in the closed disc of radius ∥x∥ and is therefore compact.
If the spectrum were empty, R(λ)=(λ1−x)−1 would be an entire A-valued function. For each f∈A∗, the scalar function f(R(λ)) is bounded: it tends to zero at infinity by the Neumann series and is bounded on every compact disc. The Liouville theorem makes it identically zero. Since the Hahn-Banach theorem separates points, this would give R(λ)=0, contradicting its invertibility. Hence the spectrum is nonempty.

A character of an algebra is a nonzero multiplicative complex-linear functional φ:A→C. The character space of an algebra is
ΦA​={φ:A→C:φ is a character}.
(15)
If A is unital, multiplicativity and nonzeroness force φ(1)=1.

For a unital Banach algebra, φ(a) belongs to σA​(a): otherwise a−φ(a)1 would be invertible, while applying φ to its inverse identity would give 0=1. Therefore
∣φ(a)∣≤r(a)≤∥a∥,
(16)
so every character of an algebra is continuous and has norm one. The nonunital case follows by extending the character to the unitization of an algebra.
The Gelfand topology on ΦA​ is the weak-star topology inherited from A∗: a net φi​ converges to φ exactly when φi​(a)→φ(a) for every a∈A. If A is unital, ΦA​ lies in the weak-star compact dual unit ball by the Banach-Alaoglu theorem. The equations
φ(1)=1,φ(ab)=φ(a)φ(b)
(17)
define a weak-star closed subset, so ΦA​ is compact.

Suppose an algebra norm made C(R) a Banach algebra. Its unital character space of an algebra would be compact in the Gelfand topology. By part i it consists of the evaluations δx​. The map
x⟼δx​
(26)
is continuous from the usual topology because every h∈C(R) is continuous, and its inverse is the continuous map φ↦φ(t) defined by the coordinate function t. Thus the character space is homeomorphic to the noncompact space R, a contradiction. No complete algebra norm exists.

Let B be the completion under the assumed algebra norm. Restriction sends every ψ∈ΦB​ to a character of the dense subalgebra C(R), hence by part i to δx​ for some x. Continuity of the restriction says exactly that x∈K. Conversely, every δx​ continuous for the algebra norm extends uniquely through the completion, and continuity of multiplication makes the extension a character of B. Restriction and extension therefore give a bijection
ΦB​⟷K.
(27)
For b∈B, choose hn​∈C(R) converging to b in the algebra norm. Characters on the unital Banach algebra B are uniformly norm bounded, so hn​∣K​ converges uniformly to the function x↦δx​(b). This function is continuous. Hence x↦δx​ is continuous into the Gelfand topology. Its inverse is again ψ↦ψ(t), so the bijection is a homeomorphism. In particular, K is compact.

Let
d=infx∈K​∥x∥>0,M=supx∈K​∥x∥<∞.
(30)
Choose positive εn​ with ∏n​(1+εn​)<∞. We construct xn​∈K inductively. Once En​=span{x1​,…,xn​} has been chosen, compactness of its unit sphere gives finitely many members of X∗ that almost norm every vector of En​. Because 0∈Kw, the next xn+1​ may be chosen so that all those functionals are as small on it as required. Choosing the error relative to d gives
​∑j=1n​aj​xj​​≤(1+εn​)​∑j=1n+1​aj​xj​​
(31)
for all scalars a1​,…,an+1​. Indeed, if the last coefficient could threaten this estimate, ∥xn+1​∥≥d first bounds that coefficient by a fixed multiple of the norm of the preceding sum; the selected norming functional then gives the displayed inequality. Iteration and the finite product bound satisfy the standard basis criterion, so (xn​) is a basic sequence contained in K.
The same proof works for any Hausdorff locally convex vector topology τ weaker than the weak topology: on each finite-dimensional En​, the τ-continuous linear functionals still norm the space, and 0∈Kτ supplies the next point. A canonical strictly weaker example arises on X∗ when X is not reflexive: the weak-star topology σ(X∗,X) is then strictly weaker than the weak topology σ(X∗,X∗∗).
We next prove the Eberlein-Smulian theorem. If the weak closure of a bounded set K is weakly compact, take any sequence in K and let Y be its closed linear span. The relevant weak closure lies in the separable space Y. A countable weak-star dense subset of the dual unit ball separates points of this compact set, so its weak topology is metrizable. Compact metrizability gives a weakly convergent subsequence.
For the converse, suppose K is not relatively weakly compact. In the canonical embedding into X∗∗, choose
x∗∗∈JX​K​w∗∖JX​(X).
(32)
The Hahn-Banach theorem gives x∗∗∗∈X∗∗∗ that vanishes on JX​(X) but satisfies x∗∗∗(x∗∗)=1. Alternating Goldstine approximation with the fact that x∗∗ lies in the weak-star closure of JX​K constructs bounded xn∗​∈X∗ and xn​∈K such that, up to errors tending to zero,
xi∗​(xj​)=0(j≤i),xi∗​(xj​)=1(i<j).
(33)
If a subsequence xnj​​ converged weakly to x, then for each fixed i the second relation would give xi∗​(x)=1. A weak-star cluster point x∗ of the bounded sequence (xi∗​) satisfies x∗(xnj​​)=0 by the first relation, and hence x∗(x)=0 by weak convergence; but passing to the same cluster point in xi∗​(x)=1 gives x∗(x)=1. This contradiction produces a sequence in K with no weakly convergent subsequence. Relative weak compactness is therefore equivalent to the subsequence condition.
Finally suppose K is weakly sequentially compact and x∈Kw. If x lies in the norm closure of K, a norm-convergent sequence suffices. Otherwise apply the first part to K−x and obtain a basic sequence (yn​). A subsequence converges weakly by hypothesis. Its weak limit lies in the closed span of the basic sequence, while every coordinate functional is eventually zero on that subsequence. The limit is consequently zero, so the corresponding sequence from K converges weakly to x.
This also shows that a weakly sequentially compact K is weakly closed: any point of its weak closure is the limit of a sequence in K, and a weakly convergent subsequence has its limit in K. The relative form of the Eberlein-Smulian theorem then makes K weakly compact. The reverse implication follows from the first direction of that theorem. Thus weak compactness and weak sequential compactness are equivalent.

A linear map T:X→Y is a weakly compact operator when T(BX​) is relatively weakly compact. Every weakly compact subset of a Banach space is norm bounded, so
sup∥x∥≤1​∥Tx∥<∞.
(34)
Thus even without assuming continuity initially, a weakly compact linear map is bounded.
The key bidual criterion is
T is weakly compact⟺T∗∗(X∗∗)⊆JY​(Y).
(35)
For the forward implication, approximate x∗∗∈BX∗∗​ weak-star by points JX​xα​ using the Goldstine theorem. Weak compactness supplies a subnet for which Txα​ converges weakly to some y∈Y, while weak-star continuity of T∗∗ makes JY​Txα​ converge to T∗∗x∗∗. Hence T∗∗x∗∗=JY​y. Conversely, if the displayed inclusion holds, the weak-star compact set T∗∗(BX∗∗​) lies in JY​(Y), where the inherited weak-star topology is the weak topology of Y. It is a weakly compact set containing JY​T(BX​).
If T is weakly compact and y∗∗∗∈Y∗∗∗, restriction of y∗∗∗ to JY​(Y) defines y∗∈Y∗. For x∗∗∈X∗∗,
(T∗∗∗y∗∗∗)(x∗∗)=y∗∗∗(T∗∗x∗∗)=x∗∗(T∗y∗),
(36)
so T∗∗∗y∗∗∗=JX∗​(T∗y∗). The bidual criterion makes T∗ weakly compact. Conversely, if T∗ is weakly compact, then T∗∗∗(Y∗∗∗)⊆JX∗​(X∗). Every y∗∗∗ annihilating JY​(Y) is sent to zero, so every T∗∗x∗∗ lies in
(JY​(Y)⊥)⊥​=JY​(Y).
(37)
The criterion makes T weakly compact. Hence T is weakly compact exactly when T∗ is.
The bidual criterion also proves the structure of W(X,Y). It is closed under linear combinations. If Tn​→T in operator norm and every Tn​ is weakly compact, then Tn∗∗​x∗∗→T∗∗x∗∗ in norm; the canonical copy JY​(Y) is norm closed, so T is weakly compact. For bounded composable maps A and B, the image condition for (ATB)∗∗=A∗∗T∗∗B∗∗ proves the ideal property. Thus the weakly compact operators form a norm-closed operator ideal.
The Krein-Smulian theorem states that the norm-closed convex hull coK of a weakly compact set K is weakly compact. To prove it, view K as a compact Hausdorff space in its weak topology. The probability measures on K form a weak-star compact subset of C(K)∗ by the Banach-Alaoglu theorem. The barycentre map
μ⟼bμ​,x∗(bμ​)=∫K​x∗(x)dμ(x)
(38)
is weak-star-to-weak continuous; scalar integration and weak compactness ensure that bμ​∈X. Finitely supported probability measures are weak-star dense and their barycentres are precisely coK. The image of all probability measures is therefore the weak closure of coK, and it is weakly compact. A convex set has the same weak and norm closures by the Hahn-Banach separation theorem, proving the claim.
If X is reflexive, BX​ is weakly compact and every bounded T:X→Y is weak-to-weak continuous, so T is weakly compact. If Y is reflexive, every bounded subset of Y is relatively weakly compact, with the same conclusion. Thus
B(X,Y)=W(X,Y)
(39)
whenever either space is reflexive.
If X=ℓ1 and Y is nonreflexive, the Eberlein-Smulian theorem supplies a bounded sequence (yn​) in Y with no weakly convergent subsequence. The formula
T(a)=∑n=1∞​an​yn​
(40)
defines a bounded operator T:ℓ1→Y. Since Ten​=yn​, it cannot be weakly compact. Therefore B(ℓ1,Y)=W(ℓ1,Y).
This conclusion does not hold for every pair of nonreflexive spaces. Both c0​ and ℓ1 are nonreflexive, while the Pitt theorem says every bounded operator c0​→ℓ1 is compact and hence weakly compact. Thus
B(c0​,ℓ1)=W(c0​,ℓ1).​
(41)
