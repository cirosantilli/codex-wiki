Take the weighted projective plane X=P(1,1,2). Its fan in N=Z2 has rays generated by
v0​=(−1,−2),v1​=(1,0),v2​=(0,1),
(1)
and all three two-dimensional cones spanned by adjacent rays. Their union is NR​, so the fan is complete and the toric variety X is proper.
The determinants of the cones ⟨v1​,v2​⟩ and ⟨v2​,v0​⟩ have absolute value one, while
∣det(v0​,v1​)∣=2.
(2)
The smoothness criterion for a toric variety therefore shows that the affine chart for ⟨v0​,v1​⟩ is singular; it is the cyclic quotient singularity of type 21​(1,1). Thus X is a singular proper toric surface.

Insert the primitive ray
v3​=(0,−1)=21​(v0​+v1​)
(3)
inside the singular cone. The corresponding star subdivision replaces ⟨v0​,v1​⟩ by ⟨v0​,v3​⟩ and ⟨v3​,v1​⟩. Both new determinants have absolute value one, as do the two unchanged cones, so the subdivided fan is smooth. The induced proper birational toric morphism X→X is therefore a toric resolution of singularities. Its exceptional invariant curve has self-intersection −2, and X is the Hirzebruch surface F2​.

On F2​, let S be the negative section of a Hirzebruch surface, so S2=−2, and let F be the fiber class of a Hirzebruch surface, with F2=0 and S⋅F=1. The line bundle O(S+3F) is very ample. Indeed, the toric ampleness criterion for aS+bF is
a>0,b−2a>0,
(4)
and on a smooth complete toric variety every ample line bundle is very ample. These inequalities hold for (a,b)=(1,3).
Explicitly, after choosing the standard lattice coordinates for F2​, its lattice polytope of a toric divisor is
PS+3F​=conv{(0,0),(3,0),(1,1),(0,1)}.
(5)
The monomials indexed by the lattice points of this polygon separate torus orbits and tangent directions, so their Kodaira map is a closed embedding. This directly verifies that the resolved proper toric surface is projective.

Write the invariant prime divisors in the order of the rays
v1​=(1,0),v2​=(0,1),v3​=(−1,k),v4​=(0,−1)
(6)
as D1​,…,D4​. The toric divisor class sequence is
0⟶M⟶Z4⟶Cl(Fk​)⟶0,
(7)
and the two characters in the standard basis of M give
D1​−D3​=0,D2​+kD3​−D4​=0.
(8)

Cl(Fk​)≅Z[S]⊕Z[F],
(9)
where S=D2​, F=D1​=D3​, and D4​=S+kF. Since the fan is smooth, this is also the Picard group.
The orbit-cone correspondence decomposes Fk​ into one two-dimensional torus, four one-dimensional torus orbits, and four fixed points. Only the zero-dimensional orbits contribute to the compactly supported Euler characteristic, so
χtop​(Fk​)=4.​
(10)

The Cox construction uses coordinates x1​,…,x4​ of degrees
degx1​=degx3​=F,degx2​=S,degx4​=S+kF.

V(x1​,x3​)∪V(x2​,x4​),

(13)

The toric morphism Ax,y2​→At1​ with t=xy is induced on cocharacter lattices by
φ:N=Z2⟶Z,(a,b)⟼a+b.
(16)
The fan of A2 is the cone ⟨e1​,e2​⟩ and its faces, while the fan of A1 is R≥0​ and its zero face. The blowup of the affine plane at the origin is the star subdivision obtained by inserting the ray through e1​+e2​; its maximal cones are
⟨e1​,e1​+e2​⟩,⟨e1​+e2​,e2​⟩.
(17)
Because φ(e1​)=φ(e2​)=1 and φ(e1​+e2​)=2, every cone maps into R≥0​, giving the composite toric morphism f:X→A1.

The two standard affine charts of the blowup restrict to an affine open cover of X0​. On the x-chart write y=xu. Since xy=x2u, the fiber is
Ux​∩X0​=Speck[x,u]/(x2u).

Let q:NR​→N(τ)R​=NR​/span(τ) be the quotient map. The images q(σ) for τ⪯σ satisfy the face and intersection axioms, so Star(τ) is a fan.
To prove completeness, take vˉ∈N(τ)R​, choose a lift v, and choose w in the relative interior of τ. Since Σ is complete, each v+mw lies in some cone of Σ. There are finitely many cones, so one cone σ contains v+mj​w for an unbounded sequence mj​. Closedness gives w∈σ. Because w lies in the relative interior of the cone τ and σ∩τ is a face of τ, this forces τ⪯σ. Finally,
vˉ=q(v+mj​w)∈q(σ).
(22)
Thus every point of the quotient lies in the support of Star(τ), proving that it is a complete fan.

b OurBigBook.com
Let a complete fan in a d-dimensional real vector space have ray generators v1​,…,vr​. Every cone of the fan lies in the positive hull cone(v1​,…,vr​). If r<d, these vectors do not span the space. If r=d, either they still fail to span, or they are linearly independent and their positive hull is a proper strictly convex cone. In either case their positive hull cannot be the whole vector space, contrary to completeness. Hence r≥d+1.
The bound is attained: take rays through
e1​,…,ed​,−(e1​+⋯+ed​)
(23)
and cones generated by every proper subset of these rays. This is the simplex fan, which is complete and has d+1 rays. Therefore the minimal number is d+1=dimNR​+1.
