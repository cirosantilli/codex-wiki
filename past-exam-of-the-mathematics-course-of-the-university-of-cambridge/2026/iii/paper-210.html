The pushforward measure of μ under g is g∗​μ, defined for B∈B by
(g∗​μ)(B)=μ(g−1(B)).
(31)
The Lebesgue decomposition theorem says that if ν and μ are sigma-finite measures on the same measurable space, then uniquely
ν=νac​+νs​,νac​≪μ,νs​⊥μ.
(32)
Let f:[0,∞)→R∪+∞ be convex with f(1)=0. If λ dominates the probability distributions P,Q, with densities p,q, their f-divergence is
Df​(P∥Q)=∫q,f(p/q)dλ,
(33)
using the lower-semicontinuous perspective convention where q=0. This definition is independent of the dominating measure.
The data processing inequality for f-divergences states
Df​(g∗​P∥g∗​Q)≤Df​(P∥Q).
(34)
To prove it, take λ=P+Q and let G=g−1(B). If p=dP/dλ and q=dQ/dλ, then the pullbacks of the densities of g∗​P and g∗​Q with respect to g∗​λ are respectively Eλ​[p∣G] and Eλ​[q∣G]. The perspective F(a,b)=bf(a/b) of a convex function is jointly convex. Conditional Jensen inequality therefore gives
F(E[p∣G],E[q∣G])≤E[F(p,q)∣G].
(35)

The Squared Hellinger distance is
H2(P,Q)=∫(dP/dλ​−dQ/dλ​)2dλ.
