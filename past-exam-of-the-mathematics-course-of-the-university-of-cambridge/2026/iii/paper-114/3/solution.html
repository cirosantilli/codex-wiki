A rank-d vector bundle π:E→B is R-oriented when its fiber groups Hd(Eb​,Eb​∖{0};R) admit a locally coherent choice of generator. Equivalently, it has a Thom class uE​∈Hd(D(E),S(E);R) restricting to that generator on every fiber. The Thom isomorphism theorem states that
Hq(B;R) ≅ ​Hq+d(D(E),S(E);R),a⟼π∗a⌣uE​.
(13)
The Euler class of a vector bundle is e(E)=s∗uE​∈Hd(B;R), where s is the zero section. The Gysin sequence of a sphere bundle is
⋯→Hq−d(B)⌣e(E)​Hq(B)→Hq(S(E))→Hq−d+1(B)⌣e(E)​Hq+1(B)→⋯.
(14)
Over F2​=Z/2, the required rings are
H∗(CPn;F2​)=F2​[x]/(xn+1),∣x∣=2,H∗(RPm;F2​)=F2​[y]/(ym+1),∣y∣=1,
(15)
as in the cohomology ring of complex projective space and the mod-two cohomology ring of real projective space. By the Künneth theorem, the base has ring F2​[x,y]/(xn+1,ym+1).
Let L be the underlying real plane bundle of the complex tautological bundle on CPn, and let λ be the real tautological line bundle on RPm. Under the splitting principle, write the formal Stiefel-Whitney roots of L as a,b, so a+b=w1​(L)=0 and ab=w2​(L)=x. Tensoring with λ adds y=w1​(λ) to each root. Thus the mod-two Euler class of L⊗R​λ is
e=w2​(L⊗λ)=(a+y)(b+y)=x+y2.
(16)
For n=m=2, put B=CP2×RP2. Multiplication by e=x+y2 on
H∗(B;F2​)=F2​[x,y]/(x3,y3)
(17)
has ranks 1,1,2,1,1,0,0 from degrees 0 through 6. More explicitly, it is injective through degree three; in degree four its kernel is generated by x2+xy2, and all of degrees five and six lie in its kernel. The Gysin sequence therefore yields
Hq(X;F2​)≅{F2​,0,​q=0,1,2,5,6,7,q=3,4,​
(18)
for the unit sphere bundle X=S(L⊗λ).
