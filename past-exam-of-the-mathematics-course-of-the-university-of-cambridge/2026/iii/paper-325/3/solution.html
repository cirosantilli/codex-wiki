For equal masses with positions r1​,r2​, the two-body Schrodinger equation is
iℏ∂t∂Ψ​=[−2mℏ2​(∇12​+∇22​)−∣r1​−r2​∣Gm2​]Ψ​.
(16)
Neglecting wave-packet spreading and overlap, write ∣a⟩i​ for the packet ψai​. The initial state and its branchwise gravitational evolution are
∣Ψ(0)⟩=n1​∑a,b=1n​∣a⟩1​∣b⟩2​,∣Ψ(t)⟩≃n1​∑a,b=1n​eiGm2t/(ℏdab​)∣a⟩1​∣b⟩2​,
(17)
up to local kinetic phases. The branch dependence of the Newtonian gravitational potential energy is the source of gravitationally induced entanglement.
Under the approximation stated in the question, only the matching pairs a=b acquire an appreciable common phase
ϕ=ℏdGm2t​.
(18)
The coefficient matrix of the resulting bipartite state is
C=n1​[J+(eiϕ−1)I],
(19)
where J is the all-ones matrix. Therefore
ρ1​=CC†=n21​([n+2(cosϕ−1)]J+2(1−cosϕ)I).
(20)
A maximally entangled state would require ρ1​=I/n. This demands
n+2(cosϕ−1)=0,orcosϕ=1−2n​.
(21)
That equation has no solution for n>4. Thus the requested large-n conclusion does not follow from the assumptions printed in the paper: within the stated approximation, the system never becomes maximally entangled in the large-n limit. For completeness, maximal entanglement is possible for n=3 at ϕ=2π/3 and for n=4 at ϕ=π; for n=2 it occurs at ϕ=π/2 or 3π/2. The n=4 time would be
t=Gm2πℏd​=2Gm2hd​≃9.9 s,
(22)
but it is not a large-n answer.
The system also does not remain entangled for every t>0. Whenever ϕ=2πk, the phase matrix again factorizes and the state returns to its initial product state. The revival period is
T=Gm22πℏd​=Gm2hd​≃19.7 s​.
(23)
For n>2 it is entangled at all intervening times; for n=2 there is the additional product-state revival at ϕ=π.
