The Schwartz space is
S(Rn)={φ∈C∞(Rn):pα,β​(φ)<∞ for all multi-indices α,β},
(1)
where
pα,β​(φ)=supx∈Rn​∣xαDβφ(x)∣.
(2)
A sequence φj​ converges to φ in S exactly when every one of these seminorms of φj​−φ tends to zero.
The space of tempered distributions S′(Rn) is the continuous dual of S(Rn). Its standard weak convergence is
uj​⟶u⟺⟨uj​,φ⟩⟶⟨u,φ⟩for every φ∈S.​
(3)

Continuity immediately implies sequential continuity. Conversely, suppose a linear form u is sequentially continuous but not continuous at zero. Enumerate an increasing family of seminorms that generates the Schwartz space topology, and let
Uk​={φ:pj​(φ)<1/k for 1≤j≤k}.
(4)
Since u is unbounded on every neighborhood of zero, choose φk​∈Uk​ with ∣u(φk​)∣≥1. For each fixed j, pj​(φk​)<1/k once k≥j, so φk​→0 in S. Sequential continuity would imply u(φk​)→0, a contradiction. Hence u is continuous and belongs to S′.

A parametrix for P(D) is a distribution E for which
P(D)E=δ0​+r
(27)
with r∈C∞. Choose a smooth cutoff χ that vanishes on a large ball containing every real zero of P and equals one outside a slightly larger ball. Ellipticity makes

The symbol class Sym(X,Rn;N)=S1,0N​ consists of c∈C∞(X×Rn) such that, for every compact K⊂X and all multi-indices α,β,
supx∈K​∣Dxα​Dλβ​c(x,λ)∣≤CK,α,β​⟨λ⟩N−∣β∣.
(30)
The defining estimate gives
Dxα​Dλβ​ci​∈Sym(X,Rn;Ni​−∣β∣).
(31)
The Leibniz rule gives
c1​c2​∈Sym(X,Rn;N1​+N2​),
(32)
and the triangle inequality gives
c1​+c2​∈Sym(X,Rn;max{N1​,N2​}).
(33)
These are the basic rules of symbol calculus.
