For the elementary complex with differential m, reduction modulo p is acyclic if p∤m. If p∣m, its mod-p homology has one Fp​ in each of degrees i+1 and i, and the Bockstein from the upper group to the lower group is multiplication by m/p modulo p. It is an isomorphism when p2∤m, so its Bockstein homology vanishes. It is zero when p2∣m, so both classes survive and contribute Z/p to βHi+1​ and to βHi​.

Give the closed oriented genus-g surface Σg​ its usual CW structure with one vertex, 2g one-cells a1​,b1​,…,ag​,bg​, and one two-cell attached along ∏i​[ai​,bi​]. The cellular boundary maps vanish after abelianization, so
Hq(Σg​;Z)≅⎩⎨⎧​Z,Z2g,0,​q=0,2,q=1,otherwise.​
(9)
Choose degree-one classes αi​,βi​ dual to ai​,bi​ and orient Σg​ by ω∈H2. The intersection pairing, equivalently the cellular diagonal approximation, gives the cohomology ring of a closed oriented surface:
αi​⌣βj​=δij​ω,βj​⌣αi​=−δij​ω,
(10)
and all αi​⌣αj​ and βi​⌣βj​ vanish.
For the space X, use the genus-two CW structure and attach an additional two-cell ev2​ along [b1​,b2​]. Both two-cell attaching words have zero exponent sum in every one-cell, so the cellular boundary C2​(X)→C1​(X) is zero. Hence
H0(X;Z)=Z,qquadH1(X;Z)=Z4,qquadH2(X;Z)=Z2.
(11)
Let u,v be the degree-two classes dual respectively to the original surface cell and the new cell. The original relator [a1​,b1​][a2​,b2​] and the new relator [b1​,b2​] give
α1​⌣β1​=alpha2​⌣β2​=u,β1​⌣β2​=v,
(12)
together with the products forced by graded commutativity. Every other product of degree-one basis classes is zero, and every product of total degree greater than two is zero. These relations completely determine the cohomology ring of X.

A rank-d vector bundle π:E→B is R-oriented when its fiber groups Hd(Eb​,Eb​∖{0};R) admit a locally coherent choice of generator. Equivalently, it has a Thom class uE​∈Hd(D(E),S(E);R) restricting to that generator on every fiber. The Thom isomorphism theorem states that
Hq(B;R) ≅ ​Hq+d(D(E),S(E);R),a⟼π∗a⌣uE​.
(13)
The Euler class of a vector bundle is e(E)=s∗uE​∈Hd(B;R), where s is the zero section. The Gysin sequence of a sphere bundle is
⋯→Hq−d(B)⌣e(E)​Hq(B)→Hq(S(E))→Hq−d+1(B)⌣e(E)​Hq+1(B)→⋯.
(14)
Over F2​=Z/2, the required rings are
H∗(CPn;F2​)=F2​[x]/(xn+1),∣x∣=2,H∗(RPm;F2​)=F2​[y]/(ym+1),∣y∣=1,
(15)
as in the cohomology ring of complex projective space and the mod-two cohomology ring of real projective space. By the Künneth theorem, the base has ring F2​[x,y]/(xn+1,ym+1).
Let L be the underlying real plane bundle of the complex tautological bundle on CPn, and let λ be the real tautological line bundle on RPm. Under the splitting principle, write the formal Stiefel-Whitney roots of L as a,b, so a+b=w1​(L)=0 and ab=w2​(L)=x. Tensoring with λ adds y=w1​(λ) to each root. Thus the mod-two Euler class of L⊗R​λ is
e=w2​(L⊗λ)=(a+y)(b+y)=x+y2.
(16)
For n=m=2, put B=CP2×RP2. Multiplication by e=x+y2 on
H∗(B;F2​)=F2​[x,y]/(x3,y3)
(17)
has ranks 1,1,2,1,1,0,0 from degrees 0 through 6. More explicitly, it is injective through degree three; in degree four its kernel is generated by x2+xy2, and all of degrees five and six lie in its kernel. The Gysin sequence therefore yields
Hq(X;F2​)≅{F2​,0,​q=0,1,2,5,6,7,q=3,4,​
(18)
for the unit sphere bundle X=S(L⊗λ).

The Lefschetz fixed-point theorem says that a continuous self-map f of a compact triangulable space has a fixed point whenever its Lefschetz number
L(f)=∑i​(−1)itr(f∗​:Hi​(X;Q)→Hi​(X;Q))
(19)
is nonzero. In the smooth nondegenerate case, the Lefschetz-Hopf fixed-point theorem expresses this number as the sum of the local fixed-point indices.
Identify H1​(R2/Z2;Z) with Z2 by sending v∈Z2 to the class of the loop t↦tvmodZ2. The image of this loop under f lifts from 0 to the path t↦f​(tv), whose endpoint is f​(v)=A(v). Its homology class is therefore A(v). Thus A=f∗​ under this identification, and in particular A is a homomorphism represented by an integer matrix.
The expansion inequality implies, after taking a derivative, that
∣Df​x​(v)∣≥μ∣v∣
(20)
for every tangent vector. The supplied linear-algebra fact shows that every eigenvalue of Df​x​ has modulus at least μ>1, so 1 is not an eigenvalue. Every fixed point of f is consequently nondegenerate and contributes local index +1 or −1.
On the torus, the induced maps on H0​,H1​,H2​ have traces 1,trA,detA. Hence
L(f)=1−trA+detA=det(I−A).
(21)
The original expansion inequality applied to lattice vectors gives ∣Av∣≥μ∣v∣ for v∈Z2, and homogeneity and density extend it to all v∈R2. If λ1​,λ2​ are the possibly complex eigenvalues of A, then ∣λj​∣≥μ, so
∣L(f)∣=∣1−λ1​∣∣1−λ2​∣≥(μ−1)2.
(22)
Since every fixed point contributes an index of absolute value one, the Lefschetz-Hopf formula gives
#Fix(f)≥∣L(f)∣≥(μ−1)2.​
