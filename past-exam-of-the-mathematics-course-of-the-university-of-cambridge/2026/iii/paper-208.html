Disintegrate Q successively as
Q(dy)=Q1​(dy1​)Q2​(dy2​∣y1​)⋯QN​(dyN​∣y<N​).
(1)
For each history y<i​, choose an optimal coupling of Pi​ and Qi​(⋅∣y<i​). Sampling these couplings recursively produces a joint law of (X,Y) with Y∼Q. Its conditional Xi​-marginal is always Pi​ and is independent of the past, so X∼P1​⊗⋯⊗PN​=P. It is therefore a coupling π∈Π(P,Q).
Write ci​(y<i​) for the conditional expected cost w(Xi​,Yi​) in the chosen coordinate coupling. The assumed one-coordinate transport-entropy inequality gives
ϕ(ci​(y<i​))≤D(Qi​(⋅∣y<i​)∥Pi​).
(2)
By Jensen inequality and the chain rule for relative entropy,
∑i=1N​ϕ(Eπ​w(Xi​,Yi​))≤∑i=1N​EQ​ϕ(ci​(Y<i​))≤∑i=1N​EQ​D(Qi​(⋅∣Y<i​)∥Pi​)=D(Q∥P).
(3)
Taking the infimum over all couplings proves the tensorization of a transport-entropy inequality.

Put μ=EZ and h(u)=(1+u)log(1+u)−u. For λ>0, the Chernoff bound and the assumed cumulant-generating-function estimate give
P(Z−μ≥t)≤infλ>0​exp{−λt+μ(eλ−λ−1)}.
(4)
The optimizer satisfies eλ=1+t/μ, and hence
P(Z−μ≥t)≤e−μh(t/μ)≤exp(−2μ+2t/3t2​).
(5)
For the left tail, apply the same argument at a negative parameter. If 0<t<μ the optimizer satisfies e−λ=1−t/μ, giving
P(Z−μ≤−t)≤exp{−μ[(1−t/μ)log(1−t/μ)+t/μ]}≤e−t2/(2μ).
(6)
For t≥μ, nonnegativity of Z makes the strict lower-tail event empty, with the boundary handled directly.

Let di​=f(X)−fi​(X(i)). The self-bounding function assumptions say 0≤di​≤1 and ∑i​di​≤Z. Apply tensorization of entropy to eλZ and the one-coordinate entropy inequality. Since φ(u)=eu−u−1 is convex and φ(td)≤dφ(t) for 0≤d≤1, the resulting bound is
Ent(eλZ)≤φ(−λ)E[ZeλZ].
(7)
Writing ψ(λ)=logEeλ(Z−μ) and dividing by the moment-generating function reduces this to
(eλ−1ψ(λ)​)′≤μ(eλ−1−λ​)′.
(8)
Both sides have finite limits at zero and ψ(0)=ψ′(0)=0. Integrating from zero to λ, with the direction interpreted correctly when λ<0, yields
ψ(λ)≤μ(eλ−λ−1)=μφ(λ),
(9)
which is the required inequality.

logEe−λ(Z−EZ)≤2λ2​E[g(Z)](λ≥0).

Bi​=(Xij​:j>i),1≤i<n.

e OurBigBook.com
As a function of the independent edge indicators, the maximum matching number is unit-Lipschitz. The event f(G)≥k is certified by exhibiting the k present edges of a matching, so f is g(k)=k certifiable. The Talagrand concentration inequality for certifiable functions around a median M gives, for universal constants in the displayed standard version,
P(f(G)≤M−t)≤2e−t2/(4M),P(f(G)≥M+t)≤2e−t2/[4(M+t)].
(18)
Here M≍n​. If t/n1/4→∞, then t2/(M+t)→∞, as does t2/M whenever the corresponding event is possible. Both tail probabilities therefore tend to zero. Thus deviations of any order larger than n1/4 are unlikely.
