A linear map T:X→Y is a weakly compact operator when T(BX​) is relatively weakly compact. Every weakly compact subset of a Banach space is norm bounded, so
sup∥x∥≤1​∥Tx∥<∞.
(34)
Thus even without assuming continuity initially, a weakly compact linear map is bounded.
The key bidual criterion is
T is weakly compact⟺T∗∗(X∗∗)⊆JY​(Y).
(35)
For the forward implication, approximate x∗∗∈BX∗∗​ weak-star by points JX​xα​ using the Goldstine theorem. Weak compactness supplies a subnet for which Txα​ converges weakly to some y∈Y, while weak-star continuity of T∗∗ makes JY​Txα​ converge to T∗∗x∗∗. Hence T∗∗x∗∗=JY​y. Conversely, if the displayed inclusion holds, the weak-star compact set T∗∗(BX∗∗​) lies in JY​(Y), where the inherited weak-star topology is the weak topology of Y. It is a weakly compact set containing JY​T(BX​).
If T is weakly compact and y∗∗∗∈Y∗∗∗, restriction of y∗∗∗ to JY​(Y) defines y∗∈Y∗. For x∗∗∈X∗∗,
(T∗∗∗y∗∗∗)(x∗∗)=y∗∗∗(T∗∗x∗∗)=x∗∗(T∗y∗),
(36)
so T∗∗∗y∗∗∗=JX∗​(T∗y∗). The bidual criterion makes T∗ weakly compact. Conversely, if T∗ is weakly compact, then T∗∗∗(Y∗∗∗)⊆JX∗​(X∗). Every y∗∗∗ annihilating JY​(Y) is sent to zero, so every T∗∗x∗∗ lies in
(JY​(Y)⊥)⊥​=JY​(Y).
(37)
The criterion makes T weakly compact. Hence T is weakly compact exactly when T∗ is.
The bidual criterion also proves the structure of W(X,Y). It is closed under linear combinations. If Tn​→T in operator norm and every Tn​ is weakly compact, then Tn∗∗​x∗∗→T∗∗x∗∗ in norm; the canonical copy JY​(Y) is norm closed, so T is weakly compact. For bounded composable maps A and B, the image condition for (ATB)∗∗=A∗∗T∗∗B∗∗ proves the ideal property. Thus the weakly compact operators form a norm-closed operator ideal.
The Krein-Šmulian theorem states that the norm-closed convex hull coK of a weakly compact set K is weakly compact. To prove it, view K as a compact Hausdorff space in its weak topology. The probability measures on K form a weak-star compact subset of C(K)∗ by the Banach-Alaoglu theorem. The barycentre map
μ⟼bμ​,x∗(bμ​)=∫K​x∗(x)dμ(x)
(38)
is weak-star-to-weak continuous; scalar integration and weak compactness ensure that bμ​∈X. Finitely supported probability measures are weak-star dense and their barycentres are precisely coK. The image of all probability measures is therefore the weak closure of coK, and it is weakly compact. A convex set has the same weak and norm closures by the Hahn-Banach separation theorem, proving the claim.
If X is reflexive, BX​ is weakly compact and every bounded T:X→Y is weak-to-weak continuous, so T is weakly compact. If Y is reflexive, every bounded subset of Y is relatively weakly compact, with the same conclusion. Thus
B(X,Y)=W(X,Y)
(39)
whenever either space is reflexive.
If X=ℓ1 and Y is nonreflexive, the Eberlein-Šmulian theorem supplies a bounded sequence (yn​) in Y with no weakly convergent subsequence. The formula
T(a)=∑n=1∞​an​yn​
(40)
defines a bounded operator T:ℓ1→Y. Since Ten​=yn​, it cannot be weakly compact. Therefore B(ℓ1,Y)=W(ℓ1,Y).
This conclusion does not hold for every pair of nonreflexive spaces. Both c0​ and ℓ1 are nonreflexive, while the Pitt theorem says every bounded operator c0​→ℓ1 is compact and hence weakly compact. Thus
B(c0​,ℓ1)=W(c0​,ℓ1).​
(41)
