A quantum channel T:B(H)→B(K) is a linear, completely positive, trace-preserving map. Complete positivity means T⊗idn​ is positive for every ancillary dimension n.

Words: 146 Articles: 1
c OurBigBook.com
Solution
Words: 146
iii OurBigBook.com
The normalized Choi matrix of the trace-to-identity map is I⊗I/d, while that of transposition is the flip operator F/d. Hence the Werner–Holevo channel has
CT​=d(d−1)I−F​=d(d−1)2​P−​,
(8)
the normalized projector onto the antisymmetric subspace.
If the channel were random unitary, part (i) would express CT​ as a mixture of maximally entangled vectors. Every vector in such a mixture must lie in the support of CT​, hence in the antisymmetric subspace. Under vectorization, an antisymmetric vector corresponds to a skew-symmetric matrix A, while maximal entanglement requires AA† to be proportional to I. In odd dimension, detA=det(−AT)=−detA, so detA=0; such an A cannot be proportional to a unitary. Thus for odd d≥3, and in particular d=3, this unital channel is not random unitary, disproving the converse. The odd-dimensional qualification matters because antisymmetric maximally entangled vectors can exist in even dimension.

ρAB​=∑j​pj​ρjA​⊗ρjB​

For a bipartite input,
(T⊗id)(ρAB​)=∑k​σkA​⊗TrA​[(Mk​⊗I)ρAB​].

Each second factor is positive and has trace equal to the probability of measurement outcome k. Dividing nonzero factors by their traces therefore writes the output as a convex combination of product states. Hence every measure-and-prepare channel is an entanglement-breaking channel.

In quantum binary hypothesis testing, hypothesis zero supplies ρ0​ with prior p and hypothesis one supplies ρ1​ with prior 1−p. A two-outcome POVM {Q,I−Q} decides zero on outcome Q. The conditional errors are
α=Tr[(I−Q)ρ0​],β=Tr[Qρ1​].
(18)
Symmetric testing minimizes the prior-weighted average error pα+(1−p)β, equivalently maximizing the average success probability.

Let Δ=pρ0​−(1−p)ρ1​. The success probability of Q is
Psucc​(Q)=pTr(Qρ0​)+(1−p)Tr[(I−Q)ρ1​]=1−p+Tr(QΔ).

Write the spectral decomposition Δ=Δ+​−Δ−​. For every effect 0≤Q≤I,
Tr(QΔ)≤TrΔ+​,

with equality when Q projects onto the positive spectral subspace, with arbitrary action on the kernel. Since TrΔ=2p−1 and ∥Δ∥1​=TrΔ+​+TrΔ−​, the Holevo–Helstrom theorem follows:
Psucc∗​=21​(1+∥Δ∥1​)​,Perr∗​=21​(1−∥Δ∥1​)​.

For a density operator ρ on a finite-dimensional Hilbert space, the Von Neumann entropy is
S(ρ)=−Tr(ρlogρ)​,
(28)
with 0log0=0. Its concavity says that, for 0≤p≤1,
S(pρ1​+(1−p)ρ2​)≥pS(ρ1​)+(1−p)S(ρ2​)​.
(29)

Words: 65 Articles: 1
4 OurBigBook.com

Solution

Words: 65
b OurBigBook.com
Put ρ=pρ1​+(1−p)ρ2​. The operator inequality ρ≥pρ1​ and the operator monotonicity of logarithm give, on the support of ρ1​,
logρ≥log(pρ1​)=(logp)I+logρ1​.
(30)
Consequently,
−pTr(ρ1​logρ)≤pS(ρ1​)−plogp.
(31)
The corresponding inequality from ρ≥(1−p)ρ2​ yields
−(1−p)Tr(ρ2​logρ)≤(1−p)S(ρ2​)−(1−p)log(1−p).
(32)
Adding these inequalities and using S(ρ)=−Tr(ρlogρ) proves the entropy bound for a binary mixture:
S(ρ)≤pS(ρ1​)+(1−p)S(ρ2​)+H(p)​.
(33)
Singular states follow by adding a positive multiple of the identity and taking a limit; the endpoint cases use 0log0=0.

If ε=0, then ρAB​=σAB​ and the desired continuity bound is immediate, so assume ε>0. Apply the positive-negative decomposition of a Hermitian operator to
X=ρAB​−σAB​=X+​−X−​.
(34)
Because TrX=0 and ∥X∥1​=2ε, one has
TrX+​=TrX−​=ε.
(35)
Thus ΔAB​=X+​/ε is positive with trace one, hence is a density operator. Define
ωAB​=1+εσAB​+εΔAB​​=1+εσAB​+X+​​=1+ερAB​+X−​​.
(36)
It is a convex combination of states. The equation εΔAB′​=(1+ε)ωAB​−ρAB​ gives
ΔAB′​=X−​/ε,
(37)
which is likewise positive and has trace one.

D(ωAB​∥IA​⊗ξB​)​=−S(ωAB​)−Tr(ωB​logξB​)≥qD(ρAB​∥IA​⊗ξB​)+(1−q)D(ΔAB′​∥IA​⊗ξB​)−H(q).​

−H(A∣B)ω​≥−qH(A∣B)ρ​−(1−q)H(A∣B)Δ′​−H(q).

H(A∣B)ω​≤1+εH(A∣B)ρ​+εH(A∣B)Δ′​​+H(1+εε​)​.

H(A∣B)σ​−H(A∣B)ρ​≤ε(H(A∣B)Δ′​−H(A∣B)Δ​)+(1+ε)H(1+εε​).
