Center the board at the origin and map each white square (i,j) to
(u,v)=(2i+j​,2i−j​).
(1)
The white squares become the integer points of the diamond
Dn​={(u,v)∈Z2:∣u∣+∣v∣≤n},
(2)
and a bishop move changes exactly one coordinate. From (u,v) the chain can move first to (u,0) and then to (0,0), since both points lie in Dn​. Reversing such paths connects any two states, so the bishop random walk is an irreducible Markov chain.

In the diamond coordinates, the horizontal line through (u,v) has 2(n−∣u∣)+1 points and the vertical line has 2(n−∣v∣)+1 points. There is a numerical constant a>0, independent of n and of the starting point, such that the walk enters the middle diamond Dn/2​ within two steps with probability at least a. To see this, suppose ∣u∣≤∣v∣, so ∣u∣≤n/2. With probability at least 1/2 the first move uses the longer horizontal line, and a fixed positive fraction of its choices have ∣v′∣≤n/4. From (u,v′), the vertical line has length at least 3n/2, is chosen with probability bounded below, and a fixed positive fraction of its points satisfy ∣u′∣+∣v′∣≤n/2. The case ∣v∣≤∣u∣ is symmetric.
Now couple two copies. Independently use the preceding construction until both lie in Dn/2​, an event having probability at least a2 in two steps. From (u,v) and (u′,v′) in that diamond, their horizontal line ranges overlap in at least n+1 values. A maximal coupling of the next moves can therefore, with probability bounded below, send them to (u,w) and (u′,w) for the same ∣w∣≤n/2. Their vertical lines are then identical, and another maximal coupling makes the two states equal with probability bounded below. Thus there are constants k and b>0, independent of n, for which the two copies coalesce during every block of k steps with conditional probability at least b.
The coupling time consequently has a geometric tail. By the coupling inequality for total variation, for each fixed 0<ε<1,
tmix​(ε)≤k⌈−log(1−b)log(1/ε)​⌉=Oε​(1).
(3)
Since a nontrivial chain has mixing time bounded below by a positive constant, the mixing time has constant order.

The urn count Xt(n)​ is the lumped Markov chain obtained from the lazy hypercube walk by recording its Hamming weight. Starting from 0n, the hypercube law is uniform on every Hamming sphere, as is its stationary law conditional on the sphere. Consequently the total variation distance of the full walk from stationarity equals that of its Hamming-weight projection.
Parts (a) and (b) place every fixed-ε mixing time at
21​nlogn+Oε​(n).
(14)

Under the standard intended reading that the added edges form a perfect matching between Gn​ and Hn​, the claim follows as follows. Degrees in Mn​ remain bounded in terms of Δ. For A⊆V(Mn​), write AG​=A∩V(Gn​) and AH​=A∩V(Hn​). The matching contributes at least ​∣AG​∣−∣AH​∣​ boundary edges, while expansion inside Gn​ contributes a constant multiple of min{∣AG​∣,n−∣AG​∣}. A case split according as ∣AG​∣≤n/2 or ∣AG​∣>n/2 shows
∣∂Mn​​A∣≥cα,Δ​min{∣A∣,2n−∣A∣}.
(22)
Thus Mn​ has a uniform Cheeger constant. Cheeger inequality gives a uniformly bounded relaxation time, while πmin​≍1/n. The usual spectral mixing estimate, or part (b), then gives tmix​≲logn.
If “adding n edges” permits all Gn​ vertices to attach to the same vertex of Hn​, the assertion is false as written. Take Hn​ to be a path and attach every vertex of the expander to one endpoint. A walk started at the other endpoint needs order n2 time to reach the attachment endpoint, so its mixing time is not O(logn). The perfect-matching interpretation is therefore necessary.

i OurBigBook.com
For any two states X,Y, the closed neighbourhood of X∪Y has at most 2s(Δ+1)≤2n/3 vertices. The remaining induced graph therefore has at least n/3 vertices and maximum degree at most Δ, so the greedy independent-set bound supplies an independent set Z of size s there. No vertex of Z is adjacent to a vertex of X∪Y. Replace the elements of X one at a time by the elements of Z, and then replace the elements of Z one at a time by those of Y. Every intermediate set is independent, and every prescribed swap has positive transition probability. Hence the chain is irreducible.

Pair the choice x in the first chain with y in the second, and pair every a∈A with itself; use the same proposed vertex v in both chains. When the pair (x,y) is removed, the chains coalesce whenever v is not in the closed neighbourhood of A. This has probability at least
C=snn−(s−1)(Δ+1)​.
(28)
When a common a∈A is removed, the distance can increase from one to at most two only if v lies in one of the closed neighbourhoods of x and y, an event of probability at most

E[ρ(X1​,Y1​)]≤1−C+B≤1−sn1​,
