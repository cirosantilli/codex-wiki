A Seifert surface for an oriented knot K is a compact connected oriented surface F⊂S3 whose oriented boundary is K. For homology classes represented by oriented curves x,y⊂F, the Seifert form is
θF​([x],[y])=lk(x+,y),
(1)
where x+ is the positive normal push-off. Choosing a basis of H1​(F;Z) gives a Seifert matrix A.
For ω∈S1∖{1}, the Levine-Tristram signature is
σω​(K)=sign((1−ω)A+(1−ω)AT).
(2)
The determinant of this Hermitian matrix vanishes away from ω=1 exactly at the unit roots of the Alexander polynomial of a knot ΔK​. Consequently the signature is locally constant on their complement.
For ω=eiθ near 1,
(1−ω)A+(1−ω)AT=iθ(AT−A)+O(θ2).
(3)
The real skew-symmetric unimodular matrix AT−A has standard symplectic blocks, so the Hermitian matrix i(AT−A) has its positive and negative eigenvalues in opposite pairs and has signature zero. Thus σω​(K)=0 near 1. If ΔK​ has no unit roots, then S1∖{1} contains no singular point of the signature form and is connected, so local constancy gives σω​(K)=0 everywhere. With the usual convention σ1​(K)=0, the signature vanishes identically.

Let V=S1×D2 contain the pattern of a satellite knot P. Its class in H1​(V;Z)≅Z is the winding number of a satellite pattern. Winding number zero therefore makes P null-homologous in V, so P bounds an oriented embedded surface FP​⊂V. Let
gP​=g(FP​).
(4)
For any companion knot K, an embedding V↪S3 as a tubular neighborhood of K carries FP​ to a Seifert surface for the satellite knot P(K). Hence
gs​(P(K))≤g(FP​)=gP​,
(5)
and the bound depends only on the pattern.

Boundary-connected-summing minimal Seifert surfaces for K and K′ gives
gs​(K#K′)≤gs​(K)+gs​(K′).
(6)
For the reverse inequality, let F be a minimal-genus Seifert surface for the connected sum of knots and let S be its standard splitting sphere. A minimal-genus Seifert surface is incompressible in the knot exterior: a compression either lowers its genus or separates off a closed component that can be discarded. Put F and S in transverse position and minimize the number of intersection circles. An innermost circle on S either gives a compression of F or bounds a disk on F across which it can be removed. Both alternatives contradict minimality, so F∩S consists only of the single arc joining the two points of S∩∂F.
Cutting F along this arc gives Seifert surfaces F1​,F2​ for K,K′. Their Euler characteristics satisfy
χ(F)=χ(F1​)+χ(F2​)−1,
(7)
which, since all three surfaces have one boundary component, is equivalent to
g(F)=g(F1​)+g(F2​)≥gs​(K)+gs​(K′).
(8)
This proves additivity.
The torus knot T2,3​ bounds a once-punctured torus, and its degree-two Alexander polynomial of a knot forces every Seifert surface to have genus at least one. Thus gs​(T2,3​)=1. If it were a composite knot, both nontrivial summands would have positive Seifert genus, and additivity would give genus at least two. Hence T2,3​ is a prime knot.

Induct on the Seifert genus gs​(K). A genus-zero knot is the unknot. If K is prime there is nothing to prove. Otherwise write K=K1​#K2​ with both summands nontrivial. Part c gives
gs​(K)=gs​(K1​)+gs​(K2​),
(9)
so each summand has strictly smaller genus than K. Apply the induction hypothesis to both. Since the genus drops at every nontrivial split, the process terminates after finitely many steps and expresses K as a finite connected sum of prime knots.

If K=K1​#K2​ with both summands nontrivial, the sphere separating the two punctured three-balls in the connected-sum construction is a splitting sphere of a knot. If it were trivial, the corresponding one-string tangle would be boundary-parallel and one of K1​,K2​ would be the unknot. The sphere is therefore nontrivial.
Conversely, a splitting sphere S divides S3 into three-balls B1​,B2​, and K∩Bi​ is a properly embedded arc. Join its endpoints by a fixed arc on S and push that joining arc slightly into Bi​; this closes the two tangles to knots K1​,K2​. Reversing the construction shows
K=K1​#K2​.
(10)
If either Ki​ were unknotted, an innermost-disk argument for a spanning disk of Ki​ would make the corresponding tangle boundary-parallel, which is exactly the stated triviality condition for S. A nontrivial splitting sphere therefore makes both summands nontrivial, so K is composite.

After replacing a class by a nonsingular representative, let A represent a Seifert form over a field F of characteristic different from two. Set
Q=A+AT,T=A−1AT.
(11)
A direct calculation gives TTQT=Q, so (F2g,Q,T) is an isometric structure. A metabolizer for A corresponds to a T-invariant metabolizer for Q, and stabilization gives the canonical homomorphism
ACF​⟶WF​.
(12)
Conversely, for an isometric structure with I+T invertible, define
A=Q(I+T)−1.
(13)
Then A+AT=Q and A−1AT=T. These constructions respect orthogonal sums and metabolic structures and are inverse on Witt classes, proving ACF​≅WF​.
For an irreducible symmetric Laurent polynomial δ, the primary component of an isometric structure is
Vδ​=kerδ(T)N
(14)
for large N. The primary decomposition is orthogonal, so restriction of Q and T to Vδ​ defines the projection
WF​⟶WFδ​.
(15)
Now take F=R and let δ have roots ω,ω on the unit circle, with ω=eiθ in the upper half-plane. The isomorphism WRδ​≅2Z sends a class to the even signature jump
Jω​=limε↓0​(σei(θ+ε)​−σei(θ−ε)​).
(16)
For the class of a knot, this is precisely the jump of its Levine-Tristram signature at the root ω; reversing the choice of side changes the overall sign convention.

(H1​(Y),λY​)≅(H1​(Y),−λY​).
(20)

PTAP=−A.
(21)

(detP)2=(−1)d.
(22)

νp​∣H1​(Y)∣=νp​∣ΔK​(−1)∣.
(23)
It is therefore even, as required.

For the supplied Seifert matrix A,
ΔK​(t)=det(tA−AT)=−t4+3t3−3t2+3t−1,

and the symmetric form Q=A+AT has signature −2. Thus
σ−1​(K)=−2=0.

Since the Levine-Tristram signature is an additive homomorphism on the algebraic concordance group, K has infinite algebraic-concordance order.
Over Q3​, reduction of the Alexander polynomial gives
−ΔK​(t)≡(t2+t−1)(t2−t−1)(mod3).
(26)
The factors are coprime, nonsymmetric, and exchanged by reciprocity. Hensel lifting therefore decomposes the local isometric structure into a reciprocal pair, which is metabolic. Its class in WQ3​​ is zero and in particular does not have order four.
For p=11, diagonalization gives
Q∼⟨−2,−23​,2,−611​⟩.

A slice knot is a knot K⊂S3=∂B4 that bounds a smooth properly embedded slice disk in B4. A doubly slice knot is a transverse equatorial cross-section of an unknotted smooth two-sphere in S4.
The slice genus g4​(K) is the minimum genus of a smooth compact connected oriented surface properly embedded in B4 with boundary K. The double slice genus gds​(K) is the minimum genus of an unknotted closed connected oriented surface in S4 whose transverse intersection with an equatorial S3 is K. Thus slice and doubly slice mean respectively g4​(K)=0 and gds​(K)=0.

Remove a small ball meeting K in an unknotted arc. The remaining knotted arc lies in a three-ball. Rotate that ball once around its boundary two-sphere in S4 and rotate the arc through m additional full twists during the revolution. The trace, capped along the fixed endpoints, is the twist-spun knot Twm​(K).
The Zeeman theorem on twist-spun knots says that the complement fibers over S1 with fiber the punctured m-fold cyclic branched cover of S3 over K. For m=1 or −1 this cover is S3, so Tw±1​(K) is unknotted.
A projection-independent specification of the requested banded-unlink diagram for Tw0​(T2,3​) is as follows. Draw a reflection-symmetric diagram of T2,3​#(−T2,3​) with the connected-sum neck on the symmetry axis. Cut at the neck, perform the oriented smoothing in each reflected crossing pair to obtain the lower unlink, and retain the two dual rectangular bands in each pair, one above and one below the projection plane. Simultaneous surgery on all these paired bands gives the reflected upper unlink. Capping the two unlinks produces exactly the spinning movie: the lower half rotates the cut trefoil through one semicircle and the upper half supplies its mirror semicircle. The paired placement of the bands records zero twisting; adding one full relative twist to every band pair gives the corresponding m-twist-spun diagram.

A genus-one Seifert matrix for the Stevedore knot is
A=(10​1−2​).
(32)
Its Alexander polynomial is
Δ(t)=det(tA−AT)=−2t2+5t−2,
(33)
whose roots are 2 and 1/2. There are no unit roots, so Question 1(a) proves that every Levine-Tristram signature of the Stevedore knot vanishes.
On the other hand,
A+AT=(21​1−4​)
(34)
has Smith normal form diag(1,9). Therefore
H1​(Σ2​(61​))≅Z/9.
(35)
If a knot is doubly slice, the linking form on the first homology of its two-fold branched cover of a knot is hyperbolic: it has two complementary metabolizers, arising from the two sides of the unknotted sphere. A cyclic group of order nine has a unique subgroup of order three, so its linking form of a branched cover cannot have two complementary metabolizers. The Stevedore knot is consequently not doubly slice, despite its identically vanishing signature function.

In either diagram of the Stevedore knot, the evident ribbon band can be cut to leave a two-component unlink. Cap those components by disjoint disks in B4 and restore the band. This constructs a ribbon disk, hence proves that the Stevedore knot is slice.
The two displayed Stevedore diagrams give two distinct ribbon-band presentations. Use one below the equator and the reverse of the other above it. In banded-unlink diagram language, draw their common unlink and include the two ribbon bands, one from each presentation. Reading the movie from bottom to top gives the indicated birth level, the two saddle bands, and the death level. The resulting surface is knotted: the two-band movie is the standard presentation obtained by gluing the two Stevedore ribbon disks, and a van Kampen calculation retains a noncyclic quotient coming from the trefoil group, whereas the complement of the unknotted model has cyclic fundamental group.
There is a terminology issue in the question. If “2-knot” means an embedded S2, as it normally does, one minimum, two index-one saddles, and one maximum have Euler characteristic
1−2+1=0,
(36)
so the resulting orientable surface knot is a torus, not a two-sphere. The construction above answers the question under the broader usage in which “2-knot” means a connected knotted surface. Under the standard narrow definition, the requested critical-point data are impossible.

Let D⊂B4 be a slice disk for K. Its normal bundle is trivial. A small normal push-off D′ is disjoint from D, and its boundary is the zero-framed Seifert longitude νK​. Hence the two-component link K2,0​=K∪νK​ bounds the pair of disjoint disks D∪D′.
Orient D′ oppositely to D. In the other hemisphere of S4, join their boundary circles by the product annulus supplied by the zero framing. The union
D∪(K×[0,1])∪D′
(37)
is a two-sphere. More concretely, it is the rounded boundary of the three-ball D×[−ε,ε], so it is unknotted. Its equatorial intersection is K∪νK​, and both link components lie on the same sphere. Thus K2,0​ is doubly slice as a colored link with the trivial coloring.
