On p-forms in dimension n, the defining identity for the Hodge star operator gives
∗2=(−1)p(n−p).
(27)
For n=4 and p=2, therefore, ∗2=1. For every α∈Ω2(N) define
α+​=21​(α+∗α),α−​=21​(α−∗α).
(28)
Then ∗α+​=α+​, ∗α−​=−α−​, and α=α+​+α−​. The two eigenspaces of the involution ∗ have zero intersection, which proves uniqueness. They are respectively the spaces of self-dual and anti-self-dual two-forms.
Now suppose N is compact and let β be an exact three-form, say β=dθ. Apply the Hodge decomposition theorem to the two-form θ:
θ=h+dφ+δψ.
(29)
Set a=δψ. Then da=dθ=β and δa=δ2ψ=0. For a two-form in dimension four, δ=−∗d∗, so d∗a=0. The self-dual form
η=a+∗a
(30)
satisfies
∗η=∗a+∗2a=η,dη=da+d∗a=β.
(31)
Thus every exact three-form is the exterior derivative of a self-dual two-form.
