Suppose for a contradiction that Y×R carries a complete Ricci-flat metric. Since Y is closed, the two subsets Y×(A,∞) and Y×(−∞,−A) are different unbounded components outside the compact set Y×[−A,A]. Thus Y×R is disconnected at infinity and, by part (a), contains a line in a Riemannian manifold.
Its Ricci curvature is zero, so the Cheeger-Gromoll splitting theorem gives an isometry
Y×R≅N3×R.
(37)
The product Ricci tensor shows that N is a complete three-dimensional Ricci-flat manifold. By the allowed fact, N is flat, and hence so is Y×R.
The universal cover of a complete flat manifold is complete, simply connected, and has zero sectional curvature. The Hadamard-Cartan theorem therefore identifies it diffeomorphically with R4, so it is contractible. On the other hand, the universal cover of the product is
Y×R​=Y×R,
(38)
which deformation retracts onto Y. It is contractible only if Y is contractible, contrary to the hypothesis. Hence no such complete Ricci-flat metric exists.
