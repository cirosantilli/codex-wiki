Use the Polynomial representation of the Heisenberg Lie algebra on the infinite-dimensional polynomial ring C[x]:
a⋅f=f′,b⋅f=xf,c⋅f=f.
(6)
The product rule gives [d/dx,x]=1, so this is a Lie algebra representation. It is a Faithful Lie algebra representation: if α(d/dx)+βx+γ is the zero operator, applying it first to 1 gives βx+γ=0, and then applying the remaining operator to x gives α=0.
To prove irreducibility, let W be a nonzero invariant subspace and choose a nonzero polynomial in W of least degree. If its degree were positive, repeated differentiation would produce a nonzero element of smaller degree, so W contains a nonzero constant. Invariance under multiplication by x then puts every monomial xn in W, and hence W=C[x].
