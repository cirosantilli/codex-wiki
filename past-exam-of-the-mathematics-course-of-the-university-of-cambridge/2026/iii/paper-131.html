For a connected Riemannian manifold (M,g), its Riemannian distance is
dg​(p,q)=infγ​Lg​(γ),Lg​(γ)=∫ab​∣γ˙​(t)∣g​dt,
(1)
where the infimum is over the piecewise smooth curves from p to q. Connectedness of a smooth manifold implies path connectedness, so this set of curves is nonempty.
The Gauss lemma says that the differential of expp​ preserves the radial inner product: for v,w∈Tp​M,
gexpp​(v)​((dexpp​)v​v,(dexpp​)v​w)=gp​(v,w).
(2)
Consequently radial geodesics from p are orthogonal to the images of tangent vectors to spheres centred at the origin in Tp​M. In a sufficiently small normal neighbourhood of p, this implies
dg​(p,expp​v)=∣v∣g​:
(3)
every competing curve has length at least the total variation of its radial coordinate, and the radial geodesic has that length.
The axioms dg​(p,q)≥0, symmetry, and the triangle inequality follow directly from length and concatenation. Certainly dg​(p,p)=0. If q=p, choose a normal ball Bg​(p,r) that does not contain q. Every curve from p to q first meets its boundary, and its initial part has length at least r by the Gauss lemma. Hence dg​(p,q)≥r>0. Thus dg​(p,q)=0 if and only if p=q.

The metric g is geodesically complete when every maximal affinely parametrized geodesic is defined on all of R; equivalently, expp​ is defined on every Tp​M for every p∈M.
The Hopf-Rinow theorem says that for a connected Riemannian manifold, the following are equivalent: geodesic completeness; completeness of the Riemannian distance dg​; compactness of every closed bounded subset; and the existence, between every two points, of a length-minimizing geodesic. It is enough in the exponential-map formulation that expp​ be defined on all of Tp​M for one point p.

The Bonnet-Myers theorem states that if a complete connected n-dimensional Riemannian manifold satisfies
Ric≥(n−1)kg
(13)
for some k>0, then
diam(M)≤k​π​.
(14)
In particular, M is compact and has finite fundamental group.
By the Hopf-Rinow theorem, points p,q∈M are joined by a unit-speed length-minimizing geodesic γ:[0,ℓ]→M. Choose a parallel orthonormal frame E1​,…,En−1​ normal to T=γ˙​ and set
Vi​(t)=sin(ℓπt​)Ei​(t).
(15)
The endpoint-vanishing fields Vi​ arise from fixed-endpoint variations. Since γ minimizes length, its Riemannian index form is nonnegative on each Vi​. Summing the second variation of Riemannian arc length gives
0≤∑i=1n−1​I(Vi​,Vi​)=∫0ℓ​[(n−1)ℓ2π2​cos2(ℓπt​)−Ric(T,T)sin2(ℓπt​)]dt.
(16)
Using the Ricci curvature bound and integrating sin2 and cos2 yields
0≤2(n−1)ℓ​(ℓ2π2​−k),
(17)
so ℓ≤π/k​. Taking the supremum over p,q proves the diameter bound. Hopf-Rinow now makes the closed bounded space M compact. Finally, the same bound applies to the complete universal cover; a compact universal cover has finite fibres over M, so π1​(M) is finite.

Give S2×R the Riemannian product of the unit round metric and the Euclidean metric. It is complete and has infinite diameter. The round sphere has scalar curvature 2, while the line has scalar curvature 0; scalar curvature is additive under Riemannian products, so
ScalS2×R​=2.
(18)
Thus this manifold has a strictly positive uniform lower bound on scalar curvature but violates the conclusion of the Bonnet-Myers theorem. Its Ricci curvature vanishes in the R direction, showing precisely why a scalar-curvature bound is insufficient.

An orientation selects the positive ordered bases in each tangent space. On an oriented n-dimensional Riemannian manifold, the Riemannian volume form ωg​ is the unique smooth n-form satisfying
ωg​(e1​,…,en​)=1
(19)
for every positively oriented orthonormal frame. In positively oriented local coordinates,
ωg​=det(gij​)​dx1∧⋯∧dxn.
(20)
The metric induces an inner product on the bundle ΛpT∗M of p-forms. The Hodge star operator is the unique linear map
∗:Ωp(M)⟶Ωn−p(M)
(21)
such that
α∧∗β=⟨α,β⟩g​ωg​
(22)
for all p-forms α,β. With the codifferential δ, the Laplace-Beltrami operator on differential forms is
Δ=dδ+δd.
(23)
The Hodge decomposition theorem says that on a compact oriented Riemannian manifold,
Ωp(M)=Hp(M)⊕dΩp−1(M)⊕δΩp+1(M),
(24)
an L2-orthogonal direct sum, where Hp(M)=kerΔ is the finite-dimensional space of harmonic p-forms. Every de Rham cohomology class has exactly one harmonic representative.

A line in a Riemannian manifold is a unit-speed geodesic γ:R→M that minimizes globally:
dg​(γ(s),γ(t))=∣s−t∣
(32)
for all s,t∈R. A connected noncompact manifold is disconnected at infinity if some compact set K has a complement with at least two unbounded connected components.
Choose points pj​ and qj​ in two such components with
dg​(pj​,K)→∞,dg​(qj​,K)→∞.
(33)
The Hopf-Rinow theorem supplies a length-minimizing geodesic γj​ from pj​ to qj​. Its image must meet K, since otherwise it would connect the two different components of M∖K. Reparametrize so that γj​(0)=xj​∈K. After taking a subsequence, compactness gives xj​→x∈K and the unit tangent vectors γ˙​j​(0) converge to some unit v∈Tx​M.
Both endpoint parameters tend to infinity because their distances from K do. Smooth dependence of geodesics on initial data therefore makes γj​ converge on every compact parameter interval to the complete geodesic
γ(t)=expx​(tv).
(34)
Every finite segment of every γj​ minimizes length. Passing to the limit gives dg​(γ(s),γ(t))=∣s−t∣, so γ is a line.

The Cheeger-Gromoll splitting theorem states that a complete connected Riemannian manifold with nonnegative Ricci curvature that contains a line in a Riemannian manifold is isometric to a Riemannian product
N×R.
(35)
The Hadamard-Cartan theorem states that if a complete simply connected Riemannian manifold has nonpositive sectional curvature, then for every point p its exponential map
expp​:Tp​M⟶M
(36)
is a diffeomorphism. In particular, the manifold is diffeomorphic to Euclidean space and is contractible.

Suppose for a contradiction that Y×R carries a complete Ricci-flat metric. Since Y is closed, the two subsets Y×(A,∞) and Y×(−∞,−A) are different unbounded components outside the compact set Y×[−A,A]. Thus Y×R is disconnected at infinity and, by part (a), contains a line in a Riemannian manifold.
Its Ricci curvature is zero, so the Cheeger-Gromoll splitting theorem gives an isometry
Y×R≅N3×R.
(37)
The product Ricci tensor shows that N is a complete three-dimensional Ricci-flat manifold. By the allowed fact, N is flat, and hence so is Y×R.
The universal cover of a complete flat manifold is complete, simply connected, and has zero sectional curvature. The Hadamard-Cartan theorem therefore identifies it diffeomorphically with R4, so it is contractible. On the other hand, the universal cover of the product is
Y×R​=Y×R,
(38)
which deformation retracts onto Y. It is contractible only if Y is contractible, contrary to the hypothesis. Hence no such complete Ricci-flat metric exists.
