Integrate each conservative ideal magnetohydrodynamics law through a thin pillbox around the stationary shock. Mass conservation gives
[ρux​]=0.
(47)
The MHD momentum-flux tensor is
ρuu+(p+2μ0​B2​)I−μ0​BB​.
(48)
Its normal and tangential components give
[ρux2​+Π−μ0​Bx2​​]=0,[ρux​uy​−μ0​Bx​By​​]=0,
(49)
where Π=p+B2/(2μ0​) is total pressure. The solenoidal condition gives [Bx​]=0, while steady Faraday's law gives continuity of tangential electric field,
[−Ez​]=[ux​By​−uy​Bx​]=0.
(50)
Finally, material energy flux plus the normal Poynting vector component gives
[ρux​(21​u2+h)−μ0​Ez​By​​]=0,
(51)
where h is specific enthalpy per unit mass.
Gravity is a bounded volume force, so its integral across a shock whose thickness tends to zero vanishes. Viscosity and resistivity inside the layer may produce entropy; consequently entropy flux need not be equal on both sides, although the second law requires nonnegative net entropy production.

The flux-function form solves ∇⋅B=0 identically. With k=k(ψ), the poloidal relation ρup​=kBp​ and axisymmetry solve steady mass conservation. The ideal induction equation is solved by the velocity representation with the field-line angular velocity ω=ω(ψ). The azimuthal momentum equation integrates once to the magnetohydrodynamic angular-momentum invariant ℓ=ℓ(ψ).
What remains is the pressure or entropy equation and the two poloidal components of momentum. Projecting poloidal momentum along a field line produces the Bernoulli invariant in part (c); projecting across magnetic surfaces produces the transfield or Grad-Shafranov equation that determines their shape. An equation of state and boundary conditions complete the problem. The stellar gravitational potential is prescribed, so no self-gravity Poisson equation remains to solve.
