The spectral characterization of the Reproducing-kernel Hilbert space of a stationary Gaussian process says that its elements are precisely the functions whose Fourier transforms satisfy
∥f∥HK​2​=c∫R​K(u)∣f​(u)∣2​du<∞,

with c determined only by the Fourier transform convention. Here K(u)=(1+u2)−1, so
∥f∥HK​2​=c∫R​(1+u2)∣f​(u)∣2du.

This is an equivalent norm for the Sobolev space H1(R), and hence the RKHS equals H1(R) as a set.
Since K is integrable, K is continuous and the process has a jointly measurable separable version. For every finite Borel measure ν on R, Tonelli theorem gives
E∥X∥L2(ν)2​=∫R​E∣X(t)∣2dν(t)=K(0)ν(R)<∞.
(14)
Thus X∈L2(R,ν) almost surely and is a Borel random variable there. Every continuous linear functional of X is a centered normal random variable: approximate its L2(ν) integral by finite linear combinations of process values and pass to the L2 limit. Therefore the induced law is a Gaussian Borel measure on L2(R,ν).
