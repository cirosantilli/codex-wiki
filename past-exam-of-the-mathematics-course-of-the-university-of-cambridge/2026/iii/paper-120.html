A Kripke model for intuitionistic propositional logic for a propositional language is a poset (S,≤) together with a persistent forcing relation on atoms: if w⊩p and w≤v, then v⊩p. Extend forcing by
w⊩α∧β⟺w⊩α and w⊩β,
(1)
w⊩α∨β⟺w⊩α or w⊩β,
(2)
and
w⊩α→β⟺for every v≥w,v⊩α⟹v⊩β.
(3)
No world forces ⊥. Induction proves persistence for every proposition.

The Kripke completeness theorem for intuitionistic propositional logic says
⊢IPC​φ⟺w⊩φ
(4)
for every world w in every intuitionistic Kripke model.
Take a root r with two incomparable successors u,v. Force p but not q at u, force q but not p at v, and force neither at r. Then r⊮p→q because of u, and r⊮q→p because of v. Hence
r⊮(p→q)∨(q→p),
(5)
so completeness shows that this proposition is not intuitionistically valid.

On equivalence classes define
[w]⪯[v]iffThφ​(w)⊆Thφ​(v),
(6)
and, for every atomic proposition p∈Φ, put [w]⊩p exactly when w⊩p. This is well-defined, is a partial order, and makes atomic forcing persistent.
The filtration of a Kripke model truth lemma states
[w]⊩ψ⟺w⊩ψ(ψ∈Φ).
(7)
Conjunction and disjunction are immediate by induction. For implication, if w⊩α→β and [w]⪯[v], then α→β belongs to Thφ​(v); if [v]⊩α, induction gives v⊩α, hence v⊩β and [v]⊩β. Conversely, if w⊮α→β, some actual v≥w forces α but not β; persistence gives [w]⪯[v], which witnesses failure in the quotient. Thus every formula in Φ is preserved.

Let T consist of all finite nondecreasing paths
(s0​,s1​,…,sk​),qquads0​≤S​s1​≤S​⋯≤S​sk​,
(8)
ordered by initial-segment extension. The one-point path (s0​) is least, and the predecessors of any path are its initial segments, hence linearly ordered. Force an atom at a path exactly when it is forced at the path's endpoint.
The endpoint map e:T→S is monotone and has the back property: if e(t)≤S​s, append s to t. Induction on propositions therefore gives
t⊩T​ψ⟺e(t)⊩S​ψ.
(9)
In particular the roots force exactly the same propositions. This is the unravelling of a Kripke model.

Assume ⊬IPC​φ. By Kripke completeness there is a rooted countermodel. Unravel it into a tree and retain only the subformulas Φ of φ. Whenever a retained node fails an implication α→β∈Φ, retain one successor witnessing α and the failure of β. Along a branch, passing to a genuinely new witness strictly enlarges the finite theory of subformulas, so at most n witness levels are needed. Identifying repeated equal theories and retaining at most one witness for each failed implication leaves at most n representatives for each of the at most 2n theories. The resulting pruned filtration has at most n2n worlds and still refutes φ by the truth lemma.
Thus every underivable formula with n subformulas has a countermodel of size at most n2n. The contrapositive proves the claim and is the quantitative Finite model property of intuitionistic propositional logic.

The crude incompleteness theorem says that every consistent recursively axiomatized extension T of PA− is incomplete.
Suppose instead that T were complete. Enumerating proofs until either σ or ¬σ appears would decide theoremhood, so its characteristic function χT​ would be total recursive. By the assumed representation theorem, choose a formula R(x) such that PA− proves R(nˉ) when χT​(n)=1 and proves ¬R(nˉ) when χT​(n)=0. The diagonal lemma supplies γ with
PA−⊢γ↔¬R(┌γ┐).
(12)
If T⊢γ, then χT​(┌γ┐)=1, so T⊢R(┌γ┐) and is inconsistent. If T⊢¬γ, then the characteristic value is zero, so T⊢¬R(┌γ┐) and hence T⊢γ, again a contradiction. Completeness must therefore fail.

The recursive theory T=PA−+φ is consistent because N⊨T. By the Gödel-Rosser theorem it has an undecidable sentence ρ, so both T+ρ and T+¬ρ are consistent. Exactly one of ρ,¬ρ is false in N; add that one to T. The first-order completeness theorem gives a model, and the Downward Lowenheim-Skolem theorem gives a countable model M. Then M⊨PA−+φ, but M disagrees with N on the chosen sentence and is therefore not elementarily equivalent to it.

Consistency of PA− makes A and B disjoint. Suppose a recursive set C separated them, and let R(x) represent its total characteristic function in PA−. By the diagonal lemma, choose a sentence σ satisfying
PA−⊢σ↔¬R(┌σ┐).
(14)
Put n=┌σ┐. If n∈C, representability gives PA−⊢R(nˉ) and hence PA−⊢¬σ, so n∈B, contradicting B∩C=∅. If n∈/C, representability gives PA−⊢¬R(nˉ) and hence PA−⊢σ, so n∈A⊆C, again a contradiction. Therefore A and B are recursively inseparable.

The Strong normalization theorem for simply typed lambda calculus says that every well-typed term has no infinite beta-reduction sequence. In the untyped calculus,
Ω=(λx.xx)(λx.xx)
(16)
reduces to itself and is therefore not strongly normalizing.

The nowhere-defined partial function is represented by
F=λn.Ω.
(17)
For every Church numeral cm​, the term Fcm​ reduces to Ω and hence to no numeral. If F had type Nat→Nat, the strong normalization theorem would make every reduction sequence from F finite, contradicting the visible infinite reduction inside Ω. Thus this partial function is lambda-definable by an untypable term of the required kind.

A closed beta-eta-long normal term of type σ→σ→σ must have the form λx:σ.λy:σ.t, where the normal term t:σ can only be x or y: the pure calculus has no constants or other closed source of a value of the atomic type σ. Hence the only two beta-eta-equivalence classes are the Church Booleans
⊤=λx.λy.x,⊥=λx.λy.y.​
(18)

Given a finite input word w, construct the typed term wq0​ effectively and beta-normalize it. The Strong normalization theorem for simply typed lambda calculus guarantees termination, and confluence gives exactly one of the finitely many normal forms qi​. Compare that normal form syntactically with the listed accepting states in F. This algorithm accepts exactly when δ∗(q0​,w)∈F, so L is recursive. Equivalently, this is the standard theorem that every language recognized by a deterministic finite automaton is a regular language and hence decidable.
