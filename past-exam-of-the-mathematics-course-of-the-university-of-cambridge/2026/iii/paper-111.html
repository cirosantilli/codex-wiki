A fundamental system of a root system is a subset Δ⊂Φ which is a basis of V and for which every β∈Φ has an expansion
β=∑α∈Δ​cα​α
(1)
whose coefficients are either all nonnegative or all nonpositive. Its associated positive system of a root system is
Π={β∈Φ:cα​≥0 for every α∈Δ}.
(2)
Thus Φ=Π⊔(−Π), and the elements of Δ are the simple roots.

Let C be the fundamental chamber of a root system. The chambers wC and wsα​C are adjacent across the reflecting hyperplane orthogonal to w(α). The chamber wC lies on the side on which w(α) is positive. If w(α)∈Π, then C lies on that same side, so crossing this wall moves one step farther from C; if w(α)∈−Π, it moves one step nearer. The gallery distance from C to wC is the Coxeter length ℓ(w), and adjacent chamber distances differ by one. Consequently
w(α)∈Π⟺ℓ(wsα​)=ℓ(w)+1⟺ℓ(wsα​)>ℓ(w).​
(6)

Write N(w) for the inversion set of a Weyl-group element. Part b shows that sα​ permutes Π∖{α}. It follows that right multiplication by sα​ changes the size of the inversion set by
∣N(wsα​)∣={∣N(w)∣+1,∣N(w)∣−1,​w(α)∈Π,w(α)∈−Π.​
(7)

ℓ(w)=∣N(w)∣=∣{β∈Π:w(β)∈−Π}∣.​

By the assumed transitivity on fundamental systems, some w0​∈W sends Δ to −Δ. It therefore sends the entire positive system of a root system Π to −Π. Part d then gives
ℓ(w0​)=∣Π∣=2∣Φ∣​.
(9)
For every w∈W, its inversion set is contained in Π, so ℓ(w)≤∣Π∣ and w0​ has maximal length.
If u also has maximal length, then N(u)=Π, so u(Π)=−Π. Hence w0−1​u preserves Π and has no inversions. Part d makes its Coxeter length zero, so it is the identity. Thus u=w0​, proving that the longest element of a finite Coxeter group is unique and has length ∣Φ∣/2.

Let V be the real vector space with basis (ei​)i∈I​. The Coxeter Gram matrix defines the symmetric bilinear form
⟨ei​,ej​⟩=G(W)ij​=−2cos(mij​π​).
(10)
Its diagonal entries are 2. The Geometric representation of a Coxeter group is generated by the reflections
σ(xi​)(v)=v−⟨v,ei​⟩ei​.
(11)
Each has square one, and on span{ei​,ej​} the product σ(xi​)σ(xj​) has order mij​. The reflections therefore satisfy the Coxeter relations and define a group representation σ:W→GL(V).

The Coxeter graph has vertex set I. Distinct vertices i,j are joined precisely when mij​≥3, and the edge is labelled mij​ when mij​>3; the customary unlabelled edge therefore means mij​=3. The Coxeter system is an Irreducible Coxeter system precisely when this graph is connected.

All edges in the Type E(p,q,k) Coxeter graph are unlabelled, so the factor a2 in part c is one. Separate the arm of length p from the central vertex. The remaining two arms form a type Aq+k+1​ chain, while deleting the central vertex leaves the disjoint type Aq​ and type Ak​ chains. Using detG(Ar​)=r+1 in the formula from part c yields
detG(E(p,q,k))​=(p+1)(q+k+2)−p(q+1)(k+1)=(p+1)(q+1)(k+1)(p+11​+q+11​+k+11​−1).​
(15)
The associated bilinear form is degenerate exactly when
p+11​+q+11​+k+11​=1.
(16)
The positive-integer solutions of 1/a+1/b+1/c=1, up to permutation, are (3,3,3), (2,4,4), and (2,3,6). Consequently the degenerate arm-length triples are the permutations of
(2,2,2),(1,3,3),(1,2,5).
(17)
For every other allowed (p,q,k) the determinant is nonzero, so the form is nondegenerate.

The Generic Hecke algebra of a Coxeter system is the free module with basis (Tw​)w∈W​ over the polynomial ring in parameters ai​, subject to ai​=aj​ whenever xi​ and xj​ are conjugate, and with multiplication
Ti​Tw​={Txi​w​,ai​Txi​w​+(ai​−1)Tw​,​ℓ(xi​w)>ℓ(w),ℓ(xi​w)<ℓ(w).​
(18)
Equivalently, its generators satisfy the Coxeter braid relations and
(Ti​−ai​)(Ti​+1)=0.
(19)
In type An​, all simple generators are conjugate, so there is one parameter.

A BN-pair consists of subgroups B,N≤G for which G=⟨B,N⟩, the subgroup H=B∩N is normal in N, the quotient W=N/H is generated by a distinguished set S of involutions, and the Bruhat multiplication and nondegeneracy axioms hold. The quotient is the associated Weyl group, and the axioms give the Bruhat decomposition of a BN-pair
G=⨆w∈W​Bw˙B.
(20)
The Iwahori-Hecke algebra of a BN-pair may be defined, up to the usual opposite-algebra convention, by
Hk​(G,B)=EndkG​(k[G/B]).
(21)
Its standard basis (Tw​)w∈W​ is indexed by the Bruhat double cosets. For a simple generator xi​ represented by x˙i​∈N, set
qi​=[B:B∩x˙i​Bx˙i−1​].
(22)
The double-coset multiplication rule is the generic rule from part a with ai​ specialized to qi​⋅1k​. Thus Hk​(G,B) is a specialization of the generic algebra.

Every Hecke parameter of a BN-pair qi​ divides ∣B∣ and, because B is a q-group, is a power of q. If q≡1(modp), then qi​=1 in the field k of characteristic p. The specialized quadratic relation becomes
Ti2​=1,
(23)
while the braid relations are unchanged. These are the defining relations of the Coxeter group W, so xi​↦Ti​ induces a surjective homomorphism
k[W]⟶Hk​(G,B).
(24)
Both algebras have bases indexed by W, hence the homomorphism is an isomorphism of group algebras.

There is a missing hypothesis in the printed claim: it is false when every irreducible component of W has type A1​. The intended statement holds as soon as W has an irreducible component of rank at least two, which we now assume.
Since q=p, every Hecke parameter of a BN-pair vanishes in k, and H is the 0-Hecke algebra with
Ti2​=−Ti​.
(25)
Let w0​ be the longest element of a finite Coxeter group. Choose a simple generator s in a component of rank at least two, put
r=w0​sw0​,qquadv=w0​s,qquadX=Tv​+Tw0​​.
(26)
The element r is again a simple generator. The identities rv=w0​ and ℓ(w0​)=ℓ(v)+1 give
Tr​X=Tw0​​−Tw0​​=0.
(27)
If t=r is simple, then t is a left descent of both v and w0​: using ℓ(w0​u)=ℓ(w0​)−ℓ(u), one gets ℓ(tv)=ℓ(v)−1. Hence
Tt​X=−Tv​−Tw0​​=−X.
(28)
The one-dimensional subspace kX is therefore a left ideal. It is nonzero because Tv​ and Tw0​​ are distinct basis elements.
Every reduced expression in a Coxeter group for v=w0​s contains r: in an irreducible finite component of rank at least two, deleting one final generator from w0​ does not remove any vertex from its support. A reduced expression for w0​ contains r as well. Since Tr​X=0, associativity now gives
Tv​X=Tw0​​X=0,X2=(Tv​+Tw0​​)X=0.
(29)
If H were a semisimple algebra, the left ideal kX would be a direct summand of the regular module. The corresponding projection would produce a nonzero idempotent in kX, impossible because (kX)2=0. Thus H is not semisimple.
For completeness, if W≅(A1​)m, then
H≅k[T1​,…,Tm​]/(Ti​(Ti​+1))≅(k×k)⊗m,
(30)
which is semisimple. This is the counterexample showing why the omitted rank condition is necessary.

At t=1, the diagonal entries of U−L are 2, while every off-diagonal (i,j) entry is
−aij​=−2cos(mij​π​).
(40)
Hence U−L is exactly the Coxeter Gram matrix G(W), and part c gives

For a Finite Coxeter group the Gram matrix is positive definite, and for a Hyperbolic Coxeter group it is nondegenerate with Lorentzian signature. In either case det(I−C)=0, so 1 is not an eigenvalue of C and the Coxeter element fixes no nonzero vector.
For an Affine Coxeter group, the Gram form has a nonzero radical. If 0=v∈radG(W), then ⟨v,ei​⟩=0 for every i, and every generating reflection satisfies

(42)
Their product σ(c) therefore fixes v. Thus every affine Coxeter element has a nonzero fixed vector.
