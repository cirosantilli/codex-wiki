Suppose X→Y→Z is a Markov chain, so I(X;Z∣Y)=0. The chain rule for mutual information gives
I(X;Y,Z)=I(X;Y)+I(X;Z∣Y)=I(X;Y)
(1)
and also
I(X;Y,Z)=I(X;Z)+I(X;Y∣Z)≥I(X;Z),
(2)
because conditional mutual information is nonnegative. Therefore I(X;Z)≤I(X;Y). Similarly,
I(X,Y;Z)=I(Y;Z)+I(X;Z∣Y)=I(Y;Z)
(3)
while I(X,Y;Z)=I(X;Z)+I(Y;Z∣X)≥I(X;Z), so I(X;Z)≤I(Y;Z). These are the two data processing inequalities. In particular, applying any deterministic function or Markov kernel to either argument cannot increase mutual information.

Independence makes
X⟶X+Y⟶X+Y+Z
(4)
a Markov chain. The data processing inequality for mutual information therefore gives I(X;X+Y+Z)≤I(X;X+Y). Translation by the known value of X is a bijection, so conditional entropy and independence give
I(X;X+Y+Z)=H(X+Y+Z)−H(Y+Z),
(5)
and I(X;X+Y)=H(X+Y)−H(Y). Rearranging proves the stated entropy submodularity for three independent sums:
H(X+Y+Z)+H(Y)≤H(X+Y)+H(Y+Z).
(6)

Because the Entropic Ruzsa distance depends only on marginal distributions, take X,Y,Z independent with the required marginals. Since X−Z=(X−Y)+(Y−Z) is a function of (X−Y,Y−Z), the data processing inequality for mutual information yields
I(X;(X−Y,Y−Z))≥I(X;X−Z).
(7)
The map (X,X−Y,Y−Z)↦(X,Y,Z) is a bijection. Using independence and the chain rule for information entropy, the left side is
H(X−Y,Y−Z)−H(Y)−H(Z),
(8)
whereas the right side is H(X−Z)−H(Z). Hence
H(X−Z)+H(Y)≤H(X−Y,Y−Z)≤H(X−Y)+H(Y−Z),
(9)
where the final step is subadditivity of information entropy. Substituting this inequality into the definition of dR​ gives the Entropic Ruzsa triangle inequality
dR​(X,Z)≤dR​(X,Y)+dR​(Y,Z).​
(10)

Let P=Q be probability mass functions on a finite alphabet, with P absolutely continuous with respect to Q. A decision region Bn​ accepts the null law P⊗n. Write
αn​=P⊗n(Bnc​),βn​=Q⊗n(Bn​)
(17)
for its type-I and type-II errors. Stein's lemma states that for every fixed 0<ε<1,
limn→∞​−n1​loginfBn​:αn​≤ε​βn​=D(P∥Q),
(18)
where logarithms and relative entropy use base two.
For achievability, let
Bn​={x1n​:n1​logQ⊗n(x1n​)P⊗n(x1n​)​≥D(P∥Q)−η}.
(19)
Under P⊗n, the normalized log-likelihood ratio converges in probability to D(P∥Q) by the weak law of large numbers, so alphan​→0. On Bn​, Q⊗n≤2−n(D(P∥Q)−η)P⊗n, whence βn​≤2−n(D(P∥Q)−η).
For the converse, let
An​={x1n​:n1​logQ⊗n(x1n​)P⊗n(x1n​)​≤D(P∥Q)+η}.
(20)
Again P⊗n(An​)→1. If alphan​≤ε, then
βn​≥Q⊗n(Bn​∩An​)≥2−n(D(P∥Q)+η)P⊗n(Bn​∩An​).
(21)
The final factor has positive lower limit at least 1−ε. Taking exponential rates and then eta↓0 proves the converse and the lemma.

For μ>0, let Q be the geometric distribution
Q(k)=1+μ1​(1+μμ​)k,k≥0.
(40)
If P is the law of X, Gibbs inequality gives
0≤D(P∥Q)=−H(X)+log(1+μ)+μlogμ1+μ​.
(41)

H(X)≤log(1+μ)+μlogμ1+μ​=(1+μ)h(1+μ1​).
(42)
For μ=0, the nonnegative random variable X is zero almost surely and both sides vanish. This proves that the maximum entropy distribution on the nonnegative integers with fixed expected value is geometric.
