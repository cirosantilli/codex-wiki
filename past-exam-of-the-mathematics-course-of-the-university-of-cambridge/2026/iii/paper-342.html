The Knill--Laflamme condition for code projector P and errors {Ea​} is
PEa†​Eb​P=Cab​P​.
(23)
For Pauli errors of weight at most t, the product Ea†​Eb​ has weight at most 2t. If d≥2t+1, such a product cannot be a nontrivial logical Pauli, because every such operator has weight at least the distance of a stabilizer code d. It is therefore either a stabilizer, acting as a scalar on the code, or it anticommutes with some stabilizer and maps the code to an orthogonal syndrome space. These are exactly the two possibilities required by the displayed condition, so every error on at most t qubits is correctable.

The six displayed generators of the Steane code are independent and commuting, so the common positive eigenspace has dimension
27−6=2;
(24)
it encodes one logical qubit. The binary columns of the underlying Hamming parity-check matrix are all nonzero and distinct. Hence no weight-one or weight-two Pauli lies in the stabilizer normalizer outside the stabilizer. On the other hand,
X=X1​X2​⋯X7​
(25)
is logical, and multiplying it by X1​X2​X3​X4​ gives the weight-three representative
X∼X5​X6​X7​.
(26)
The analogous statement holds for Z, so
d=3​.
(27)
Now include both E1​=X5​ and E2​=X6​X7​ in the proposed error set. Their product is
E1†​E2​=X5​X6​X7​∼X,
(28)
which is non-scalar on the code and violates the Knill--Laflamme condition. Since multiplying by a logical operator does not change commutation with stabilizers, X5​ and X6​X7​ have the same error syndrome. A decoder cannot know which occurred and may apply a correction that leaves a logical X error.

All plaquette stabilizers commute and square to one. Every term in
H=−∑P​(SP(X)​+SP(Z)​)
(29)
is minimized by eigenvalue +1, so any ground state satisfies
SP(X)​∣ψ⟩=SP(Z)​∣ψ⟩=∣ψ⟩​
(30)
for every plaquette.
The R-edge operator XeR​​ commutes with every X-type stabilizer. It overlaps the two endpoint R plaquettes in an odd number of vertices and every other plaquette evenly, so it anticommutes only with the two endpoint Z-type stabilizers. It flips those two eigenvalues to −1, creating two rx​ particles. Multiplying by an adjacent R-edge operator toggles the shared endpoint twice, annihilating that excitation there while creating one at the new endpoint. Repetition forms a string operator in a topological code whose two excitations move farther apart.

An rx​ string is a product of X operators, while a gz​ string is a product of Z operators. A process that carries rx​ once around gz​ can be represented by a closed red X string crossing the green Z string once. At the crossing qubit,
XZ=−ZX,
(31)
while all other factors commute. Reversing the order of the two string operators therefore multiplies the state by −1. The full braid phase is
−1​,
(32)
so rx​ and gz​ are mutual semions.

A green X, Y, or Z string may end on the bottom green boundary without leaving a violated green plaquette beyond the lattice. The top vertex is likewise a zero-length green boundary where such strings can terminate. Thus both locations absorb gx​,gy​,gz​: they exhibit anyon condensation at a boundary.
A string joining these two green condensers preserves every stabilizer but cannot be reduced to stabilizers, so it is logical. In the pictured lattice the shortest such path contains nine qubits. No shorter nontrivial string connects equivalent condensing boundaries, hence
d=9​.
(33)
For example, the product of X operators along any shortest green path from the bottom boundary to the top corner is a logical X, and the product of Z operators along a corresponding path is a logical Z. They may be chosen to overlap on an odd number of vertices, so they anticommute as required for one encoded qubit.

Two local gapped Hamiltonians are topologically equivalent when a continuous path of local Hamiltonians joins them without closing the bulk gap. The Bogoliubov--de Gennes Hamiltonian has energies
E±​(k)=±(2tcosk+μ)2+4Δ2sin2k​.
(45)
For t,Δ>0, this gap can close only at
μ=−2t(k=0),μ=2t(k=π).
(46)
The whole region −2t<μ<2t is connected and gapped, so its parameters can be continuously deformed to t=Δ,μ=0. Therefore
H≃H0​for −2t<μ<2t​.
(47)
Writing
HBdG​(k)=dy​(k)σy​+dz​(k)σz​,dy​=−2Δsink,dz​=−2tcosk−μ,
(48)
shows the topological distinction. As k crosses the Brillouin zone, (dy​,dz​) traces an ellipse. It encloses the origin once when ∣μ∣<2t, giving nonzero winding number of a one-dimensional Bogoliubov--de Gennes Hamiltonian. For μ<−2t it does not enclose the origin and has winding zero. Changing this integer requires the ellipse to pass through the origin, exactly the bulk gap closing.

A Majorana zero mode is a normalized self-adjoint fermion operator localized near a boundary and commuting with the Hamiltonian. With open boundaries,
H0​=it∑j=1M−1​c2j​c2j+1​.
(49)
Neither c1​ nor c2M​ appears. Each anticommutes with both factors in every displayed bilinear and consequently commutes with their product. Thus
[H0​,c1​]=[H0​,c2M​]=0​,
(50)
and c1​,c2M​ are Majorana zero modes localized exactly at the two end sites.

Let U be the quasi-local unitary carrying the ground space of H0​ to that of a topologically equivalent H. Define
γ1​=Uc1​U†,γ2M​=Uc2M​U†.
(51)
Unitary conjugation preserves self-adjointness and the Majorana algebra. Quasi-locality spreads each endpoint operator only into an exponentially decaying tail, and the two operators preserve the ground space because c1​,c2M​ preserve the ground space of H0​.
Diagonalize the quadratic Hamiltonian as
H=21​∑j​ϵj​iχ2j−1​χ2j​.
(52)
The hint gives a real expansion γ=∑j​gj​χj​. Any coefficient along a pair with ϵj​>0 would create or remove a positive-energy quasiparticle and send some ground state outside the two-lowest-state subspace. Ground-space preservation therefore forces γ1​ and γ2M​ to have support only in the zero-energy Majorana subspace. Hence
[H,γ1​]=[H,γ2M​]=0​
(53)
in the ideal infinite-chain limit, with only exponentially small finite-size corrections. They are the exponentially localized endpoint Majorana zero modes throughout the topological phase.
