Let R be a valuation ring with fraction field K. For every commutative square
SpecK↓SpecR​⟶⟶​X↓fY,​
(1)
the valuative criterion for separatedness says that a finite type morphism f between Noetherian schemes is separated exactly when there is at most one dotted lift SpecR→X completing the diagram.
Under the same finiteness hypotheses, the valuative criterion for properness says that f is proper exactly when every such square has a unique lift. Thus separatedness supplies uniqueness, while properness supplies existence as well.

The composite g∘f is proper, hence separated. If two lifts SpecR→X solve a valuation-ring lifting problem for f, they also solve the corresponding problem for g∘f. The valuative criterion for separatedness for g∘f makes them equal, so f is separated.
It remains to prove existence. Start with a square
SpecK↓SpecR​u​v​​X↓fY.​
(2)
After composing the lower map with g, properness of g∘f gives a lift a:SpecR→X over Z whose generic restriction is u. The two maps f∘a and v from SpecR to Y agree on SpecK and have the same composite with g. Since g is separated, its valuative uniqueness criterion gives f∘a=v. Hence a is the required lift for f.
The morphism f is of finite type by hypothesis, and it is separated and satisfies valuative existence. The valuative criterion for properness therefore proves that f is proper.

Let R be a discrete valuation ring with uniformizer π, fraction field K, and normalized discrete valuation ν:K×→Z. A map SpecK→PZn​ is a projective point
[a0​:⋯:an​],ai​∈K,
(3)
with at least one nonzero coordinate. Put m=minai​=0​ν(ai​) and set bi​=π−mai​. Then every bi​ lies in R, and at least one bj​ is a unit.
On the standard affine chart Uj​ of projective space, the ratios bi​/bj​ all lie in R. They therefore define a map SpecR→Uj​⊆PZn​ whose generic restriction is the original point. This proves existence in the DVR case. The assumed separatedness of PZn​→SpecZ, through the valuative criterion for separatedness, gives uniqueness. Thus the morphism satisfies the requested DVR form of the valuative criterion for properness.

Write A=k[x,y] and m=(x,y). Since f(0,0)=0, the principal open subscheme D(f)=SpecAf​ contains m, and
V=D(f)∖{m}.
(14)
Cover V by the two affine opens D(xf) and D(yf), whose intersection is D(xyf). The degree-zero part of the resulting Čech cohomology complex gives
H0(V,OV​)=Axf​∩Ayf​=Af​
(15)
inside the fraction field of A. The last equality follows because Af​ is a unique factorization domain and a rational function regular after localizing at both x and y has no possible prime factor left in its denominator.
The same affine cover is acyclic, so its degree-one Čech group computes sheaf cohomology and gives
H1(V,OV​)≅Axyf​/(Axf​+Ayf​).
(16)
Before localizing at f, the quotient
Q=Axy​/(Ax​+Ay​)
(17)

{x−ay−b:a,b≥1}.
(18)

H1(V,OV​)≅Qf​≅Q.

For the cover U={X1​,X2​} of the affine plane with doubled origin, the overlap is the punctured affine plane U. Since H0(U,OU​)=k[x,y], the Čech complex begins
k[x,y]⊕k[x,y]⟶k[x,y],(a,b)⟼b−a.
(20)
This map is surjective, and the normalized complex has no terms in degrees at least two. Consequently
Hˇp(U,OY​)=0(p>0).
(21)
The Mayer-Vietoris sequence for sheaf cohomology also gives H1(Y,OY​)=0, but its next part gives
H2(Y,OY​)≅H1(U,OU​).
(22)
Part b with f=1 shows that the group on the right is infinite-dimensional. Thus
Hˇ2(U,OY​)=0=H2(Y,OY​).
(23)
This does not contradict the acyclic cover theorem. Although X1​ and X2​ are affine, their intersection U is not acyclic: it has nonzero first structure-sheaf cohomology. Equivalently, this affine cover does not satisfy the theorem's hypotheses; the doubled-origin plane is not a semi-separated scheme.

A morphism of schemes i:Z→X is a closed immersion if it identifies Z homeomorphically with a closed subset of X and the morphism of sheaves
OX​⟶i∗​OZ​
(24)
is surjective. Equivalently, every point of X has an affine neighborhood SpecA on which i is isomorphic to Spec(A/I)→SpecA for some ideal I⊆A.
A closed subscheme of X is a scheme Z supplied with a closed immersion Z↪X, considered up to isomorphism over X.

On an affine open W=SpecA⊆X, write Z∩W=V(I). Give Z∩W the reduced induced subscheme structure Spec(A/I​). These constructions agree under localization and therefore glue to a reduced closed subscheme Z↪X.
Let Z′↪X be another closed immersion with the same underlying closed set. Affine-locally write Z′=Spec(A/J). Since V(J)=Z∩W, its vanishing ideal is J​=I​, and the inclusion J⊆J​ induces a quotient homomorphism
A/J⟶A/J​.
(25)
Contravariance of the spectrum of a commutative ring gives a factorization
Z∩W⟶Z′∩W⟶W.
(26)
The quotient maps force these local factorizations to agree on overlaps, so they glue. They are also the only possible maps over X, which proves uniqueness.

Write X=SpecB, Y=SpecA, and let the morphism correspond to a homomorphism φ:A→B. Put I=kerφ and
Z=Spec(A/I).
(27)
The injection A/I↪B gives X→Z, and the quotient A→A/I gives a closed immersion Z↪Y, so f factors through Z.
If f factors through another closed subscheme Z′=Spec(A/J), then J⊆kerφ=I. The resulting quotient A/J→A/I induces the unique factorization Z→Z′→Y. Thus Z is the scheme-theoretic image.
It remains to identify its underlying set. A principal open subscheme D(a)⊆SpecA misses f(X) exactly when D(φ(a)) is empty, equivalently when φ(a) is a nilpotent element. Hence the ideal of functions vanishing set-theoretically on f(X) has radical kerφ​. The closure is therefore
V(kerφ​)=V(kerφ)=∣Z∣,
(28)
as required.
