The first two terms are the kinetic term and mass term of a real scalar field; together they determine the free quantum field theory propagator. The term −λϕ4/4! is its quartic field interaction term. The remaining three terms are counterterms: δZ​ renormalizes the field normalization, δm​ renormalizes the mass, and δλ​ renormalizes the quartic coupling. Their regulator dependence cancels the ultraviolet divergences of loop diagrams, while their finite parts implement the chosen renormalization conditions.

Through order λ2, the quartic one-particle-irreducible Feynman diagrams are the tree-level four-point vertex, the local δλ​ counterterm vertex, and three one-loop bubble diagrams. The bubbles have two quartic vertices and differ by whether the momentum through the loop is the s, t, or u Mandelstam variables; each has symmetry factor 1/2. External-leg self-energies are not part of the 1PI four-point vertex, and δZ​ begins beyond the required order in this theory.

i OurBigBook.com
The leading contributions to δ2​ and δm​ come from the one-loop electron self-energy: one internal electron line and one internal photon line join two electron-photon vertices. Its coefficients of γμpμ​ and m determine the field-strength and mass counterterms. The leading contribution to δ1​ comes from the one-loop electron-photon vertex correction, with two internal electron propagators and one internal photon propagator, together with the corresponding local counterterm vertices. The Ward identity gives δ1​=δ2​ in a gauge-invariant scheme.

The leading two-point diagram has one sextic vertex, two external legs, and its remaining four legs paired into two tadpole loops; it determines the mass counterterm. The leading six-point diagram has two sextic vertices joined by three internal lines, leaving three external legs at each vertex; it is a two-loop diagram and determines the coupling counterterm. The corresponding local δm​ and δλ​ vertices complete the counterterm calculation.

For the two-point graph, L=2 and I=2, so its superficial degree of divergence in three dimensions is
D=3L−2I=2.
(12)
Its additive mass-squared counterterm is therefore proportional to λΛ2; writing that counterterm as m2δm​ gives the dimensionless scaling δm​∼λΛ2/m2, or Λ2/m2 when coupling factors are suppressed as in the question. For the six-point graph, L=2 and I=3, so D=0 and its ultraviolet divergence is logarithmic:
δλ​∼λ2log(Λ/m).
(13)
Suppressing powers of λ yields the two stated estimates.

The only two-point diagram through second loop order is the double tadpole from one sextic vertex. It has no route by which the external momentum can pass through an internal propagator, so its value is independent of p and renormalizes only the mass. A field-strength counterterm is determined by the coefficient of p2 in the two-point 1PI function, hence δZ​=0 at one and two loops. The first momentum-dependent two-point topology uses two sextic vertices joined by five internal lines and has four loops.

Changing nμ to uμ changes the gauge-fixing fermion by
Ψu​−Ψn​=∫d4xcˉa(u−n)μAμa​.
(30)
The corresponding change of the action is the BRST-exact term s(Ψu​−Ψn​). For a BRST-closed observable O, invariance of the functional measure implies that the variation of its expectation value is the expectation of a total BRST variation and vanishes:
δ⟨O⟩∝⟨s[O(Ψu​−Ψn​)]⟩=0.
(31)
