The definable power set of a set X is
D(X)={{x∈X:(X,∈)⊨φ(x,a1​,…,an​)}:φ is a formula and a1​,…,an​∈X}.
(1)
Thus definability is evaluated internally in the structure (X,∈) and parameters from X are allowed.

A condensation sentence for the constructible hierarchy may be obtained by taking a single conjunction σ that expresses a sufficiently strong finite fragment of set theory, the assertion V=L, and that the ordinals have no largest member. The finite fragment is chosen strong enough to define the satisfaction relation needed for the L-construction and to prove its absoluteness for transitive sets.
If a transitive set X satisfies σ, let λ=X∩Ord. The absence of a largest ordinal makes λ a limit ordinal. Internal V=L says every x∈X belongs to some internally constructed Lβ​, while transitivity and absoluteness identify that level with the actual Lβ​. Conversely the finite closure axioms ensure that every Lβ​, β<λ, belongs to X. Hence X=Lλ​.

Choose a successor ordinal β=δ+1>α, and then choose a limit ordinal γ>β. The level Lγ​ satisfies the condensation sentence for the constructible hierarchy. The level Lβ​ cannot satisfy it: otherwise condensation would give Lβ​=Lλ​ for a limit λ, but
Lξ​∩Ord=ξ
(3)
would imply the impossible equality β=λ. Thus the condensation sentence belongs to Tγ​ but not to Tβ​, and Tβ​=Tγ​.

The language of set theory has only countably many sentences, so there are at most 2ℵ0​ possible complete sets of sentences Tξ​. Choose (2ℵ0​)++1 ordinals above α. By cardinal pigeonhole, two of their theories agree. Hence some γ>β>α satisfy Tβ​=Tγ​.

With the convention that p≤q means that p is stronger, a set G⊆P is a generic filter over M when it is a filter and meets every dense set D⊆P with D∈M. Explicitly, G is upward closed toward weaker conditions, every two members have a common stronger member in G, and G∩D=∅ for every such D.

For g∈NN∩M and n∈N, the set
Dg,n​={s:some k≥n below ∣s∣ satisfies s(k)=g(k)}
(4)
is dense in Q0​: extend any finite sequence at one fresh coordinate with the corresponding value of g. The generic union x0​ meets every Dg,n​, so it agrees infinitely often with every ground-model g. Thus x0​ is infinitely equal over M and is not eventually different over M.
For g∈NN∩M, conditions of Q1​ whose side set contains g form a dense set. Once such a condition enters G1​, every later coordinate added to its stem must avoid g. Hence the generic union x1​ is eventually different from every ground-model g, and consequently is not infinitely equal over M.
The four answers are therefore
x0​x1​​eventually differentnoyes​infinitely equalyesno.​​

The Fn forcing Fn(X,Y,κ) consists of partial functions p:X⇀Y such that
∣domp∣<κ.
(8)
It is ordered by reverse inclusion: p≤q exactly when p⊇q, so a stronger condition supplies more values.

If M regards P as satisfying the κ-chain condition, then forcing with P preserves every cardinal and cofinality at least κ. If M regards ∣P∣ as μ, then every antichain has size at most μ, so P has the μ+-chain condition. It follows that every cardinal and cofinality at least μ+ is preserved in a P-generic extension.

If P is ℵ1​-closed in M, a descending sequence deciding successively all entries of a proposed function N→M has a common lower bound. Thus the extension contains no new countable sequences of ground-model elements and in particular
℘(N)M[G]=℘(N)M.
(9)
It follows that ℵ1M​ remains uncountable and hence is preserved. More generally such closure preserves cardinals at most ℵ1​, but closure alone need not preserve larger cardinals.

The forcing
P=Fn(ℵ1M​×N,2,ℵ1M​)
(10)
is ℵ1M​-closed, so by closed forcing it adds no new real numbers. Its generic union can be viewed as a sequence
⟨rξ​:ξ<ℵ1M​⟩,rξ​(n)=⋃G(ξ,n),
(11)
of old reals. For every r∈℘(N)M, conditions asserting that some unused row equals r are dense: assigning all countably many values of that row is a legitimate condition. Thus the generic sequence surjects ℵ1M​ onto the old set of reals.
In M, that set had cardinality ℵ2M​. The forcing therefore collapses ℵ2M​ to ℵ1M​, while adding no reals and preserving ℵ1M​. Consequently
M[G]⊨2ℵ0​=ℵ1​.​
(12)

The forcing P=Fn(ℵ1N​,2,ℵ1N​) is ℵ1N​-closed, so it adds no reals:
℘(N)∩N[G]=℘(N)∩N.
(13)
By contrast, ℵ1M​ is countable in N. The union of the Q-generic filter is a total binary function
h:ℵ1M​⟶2.
(14)
Fix in N a bijection e:N→ℵ1M​. Then r(n)=h(e(n)) is a real in N[H]. It is not in N: for any ground-model real r0​ and any p∈Q, some coordinate of ℵ1M​ is outside domp, and extending there forces r to differ from r0​. Hence Q adds a new real, and
℘(N)∩N[G]=℘(N)∩N[H].​
(15)
