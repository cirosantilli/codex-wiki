The mean value property for harmonic functions says that whenever Br​(x0​)​⊂Ω,
u(x0​)=∣Br​∣1​∫Br​(x0​)​u=∣∂Br​∣1​∫∂Br​(x0​)​u.
(1)
If u attains its maximum M at an interior point, the average of the nonnegative function M−u over every sufficiently small centred ball is zero. Continuity makes u=M on each such ball, and connectedness propagates this equality throughout the domain. Applying the same argument to −u proves the strong maximum principle for harmonic functions.

A minimizing sequence in Lipg​(Ω,k) is uniformly bounded and equi-Lipschitz. The Arzelà-Ascoli theorem gives a uniformly convergent subsequence with limit u having the same boundary data and Lipschitz constant at most k. Its gradients have a weak-star convergent subsequence in L∞, with limit Du. Convexity of F makes the integral functional weak-star lower semicontinuous, so
F[u]≤liminfj​F[uj​].
(14)
Thus u attains the infimum by the direct method in the calculus of variations.
