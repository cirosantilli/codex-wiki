Noetherian means every ideal is finitely generated, equivalently every ascending ideal chain stabilises. Quotients preserve this; for example Z[−5​] is Noetherian as a quotient of Z[X] but not a UFD. A UFD need not be Noetherian (a polynomial ring in infinitely many variables is a counterexample). Stabilisation of kernels proves every surjective endomorphism of a Noetherian ring injective. The shift k[x1​,x2​,…]→k[x1​,x2​,…], x1​↦0, xi+1​↦xi​, is surjective noninjective; x↦x2 on k[x] shows injective need not mean surjective. Finally fn​(X)=(nX​) is integer-valued. Finite differences prove uniquely that every integer-valued polynomial is an integral linear combination of the fn​, so they are a Z-basis. Int(Z) is not Noetherian: the denominators in fp​ yield an ideal chain requiring new prime denominators.

10F OurBigBook.com
Bounded means finite diameter; sequential compactness means every sequence has a convergent subsequence. A failure of uniform continuity supplies two close sequences whose image distances stay apart; a convergent subsequence contradicts continuity. Into a discrete metric, continuity is local constancy, and compactness upgrades the radii uniformly. The Cantor set is closed in compact [0,1], hence sequentially compact. Uniform local constancy gives a positive separation scale, so finitely many initial Cantor-code (binary) digits determine f. Removing zero destroys compactness: the hinted function reading the digit after the first 1 is continuous at every remaining sequence but depends on arbitrarily late digits.

An extreme point is not a nontrivial convex combination of two distinct points of the set. Write x=x+−x− with nonnegative parts and set y=(x+,x−), c=(1,1), M=(A,−A), a=b. An extreme feasible point of this standard-form LP has at most m positive coordinates (otherwise the corresponding columns are dependent and permit a two-sided feasible perturbation); an optimum may be chosen extreme, and cancelling simultaneous positive pairs gives an x with at most m nonzeros. For the final problem, fix b=Ax∗ for any optimum and replace x∗ by such a sparse minimum-ℓ1 solution of Ax=b; f(Ax) is unchanged and the penalty cannot increase.
