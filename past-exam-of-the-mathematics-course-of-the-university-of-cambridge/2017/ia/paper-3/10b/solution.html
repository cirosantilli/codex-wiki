Let a be any constant vector and take u=ϕa. The product rule for divergence gives ∇⋅u=a⋅∇ϕ. The divergence theorem on a bounded region with piecewise smooth boundary, oriented outward, therefore yields
a⋅∫V​∇ϕdV=a⋅∫S​ϕdS.
(38)
Since a is arbitrary, equality of all components proves
∫V​∇ϕdV=∫S​ϕdS.​
(39)
Here ϕ is continuously differentiable on a neighbourhood of the closed region.
For the side of this right circular cone, the parameter tangents are xr​=(cosθ,sinθ,3​) and xθ​=(−rsinθ,rcosθ,0). Their cross product in the outward order is
dS=(xθ​×xr​)drdθ=(3​cosθ,3​sinθ,−1)rdrdθ.​
(40)
The sign is outward because the solid right circular cone lies at smaller cylindrical radius for fixed height. Reversing the parameter order reverses the oriented surface element.
To check the integral identity, the closed boundary must include the top Euclidean disk z=1, radius 1/3​, as well as the curved side. For ϕ=z2, horizontal components cancel on integrating θ. The side contribution is
∫side​ϕdS=−2π∫01/3​​3r3drez​=−6π​ez​.
(41)
On the top Euclidean disk ϕ=1 and dS=ez​dA, so its contribution is (π/3)ez​. The total is (π/6)ez​. Independently, the cross-section of the solid at height z has area πz2/3, and ∇ϕ=2zez​, giving
∫V​∇ϕdV=∫01​2z3πz2​dzez​=6π​ez​​.
(42)
Thus the two sides agree. The curved side alone is not a closed surface and does not satisfy this volume identity. The apex has zero area; alternatively one can truncate at height ε and let ε↓0, with the extra boundary contribution vanishing.
