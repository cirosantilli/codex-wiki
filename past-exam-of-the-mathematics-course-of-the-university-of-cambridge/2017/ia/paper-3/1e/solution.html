Past exam of the mathematics course of the University of Cambridge / 2017 / ia / Paper 3 / 1E / Solution

Use the Riemann sphere convention for evaluating a Möbius transformation at a pole and at infinity. When the three prescribed points are finite, take
f(z)=(w3​−w2​)(z−w1​)(w3​−w1​)(z−w2​)​.
(1)
Its zero is w2​, its pole is w1​, and substitution at w3​ gives one. The remaining three cases are
point at infinityw1​=∞w2​=∞w3​=∞​f(z)(z−w2​)/(w3​−w2​)(w3​−w1​)/(z−w1​)(z−w2​)/(z−w1​).​​​
(2)
All coefficient determinants are nonzero because the points are distinct. There is only one such Möbius transformation: the composite of any two candidate maps, one inverted, fixes ∞,0,1; fixing infinity makes it affine, and fixing zero and one makes it the identity.
For this question use the cross-ratio normalization
[w1​,w2​,w3​,w4​]=f(w4​).​
(3)
In the finite case this is (w3​−w1​)(w4​−w2​)/[(w3​−w2​)(w4​−w1​)], with the corresponding limits if a point is infinite. It is finite and distinct from zero and one, since w4​ is distinct from the other three points and f is a bijection. Ordering conventions for the cross-ratio differ; the definition here is fixed by the specified images, rather than by importing another ordering formula.
A generalized circle means either a circle or a straight line completed by infinity. Let K be the unique generalized circle through the first three points. Its image under f is the unique generalized circle through ∞,0,1, namely R∪{∞}. Therefore w4​∈K exactly when f(w4​) is real. Conversely a real f(w4​) lies on that extended real line, whose inverse image is K. This proves the real cross-ratio criterion for a generalized circle in both directions.
