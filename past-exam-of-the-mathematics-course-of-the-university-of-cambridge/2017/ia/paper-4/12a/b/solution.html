For 0≤k<1, the first burn reduces the total mass from M+m0​ to M+(1−k)m0​. The rocket equation therefore gives
v1​=ulogM+(1−k)m0​M+m0​​.
(62)
Detachment exerts negligible impulse, so the surviving second stage keeps velocity v1​. Its initial and final masses are (1−k)(M+m0​) and (1−k)M, respectively. A second application of the rocket equation gives
v2​=ulogM+(1−k)m0​M+m0​​+ulog(1+Mm0​​).​
(63)
For the single-stage comparison from rest, vsingle​=ulog(1+m0​/M). Hence
v2​−vsingle​=ulogM+(1−k)m0​M+m0​​≥0.​
(64)
Equality holds at k=0; for positive fuel and exhaust speed it is strict when 0<k<1. This two-stage rocket with proportional fuel and body masses gains speed by discarding inert body mass before the last burn.
As k→1− the expression tends to 2ulog(1+m0​/M), but the surviving second-stage mass also tends to zero. At exactly k=1 there is no second stage, so its final speed is undefined and that limiting formula cannot be assigned to it. The first stage alone reaches the single-stage burnout speed before detachment. If m0​=0, neither design gains speed.
