 ... Past exam of the mathematics course of the University of Cambridge 2017 ia Paper 4 11A a
OurBigBook.com Words: 107
The rotating-frame derivative formula is (dA/dt)S​=(dA/dt)S′​+ω×A. Applying it twice to the position vector, with constant angular velocity, gives
aS​=aS′​+2ω×vS′​+ω×(ω×r).
(51)
Solving for aS′​ identifies the displayed terms: its left side is the acceleration relative to the rotating reference frame, the first right-hand term is the inertial acceleration, −2ω×vS′​ is the Coriolis acceleration, and −ω×(ω×r) is the outward centrifugal acceleration.
For a particle of mass m, replace aS​ by the actual force divided by m using Newton's second law. The remaining terms then appear as apparent forces in S′. There is no Euler acceleration because ω is constant and no translational acceleration term because the common origin is fixed in the inertial frame.
