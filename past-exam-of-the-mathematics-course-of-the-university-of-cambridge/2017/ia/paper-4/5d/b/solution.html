Suppose X2≡−1(modp). Then X is a unit modulo n for modulus p, and Fermat's little theorem implies
1≡Xp−1=(X2)(p−1)/2≡(−1)(p−1)/2(modp).
(15)
Since p is an odd prime number, 1 and −1 are distinct residue classes. Therefore (p−1)/2 is even, and p≡1(mod4).
Conversely, put q=(p−1)/2. Pair each integer j∈{1,…,q} with p−j in the factorial. Wilson theorem yields
−1≡(p−1)!=∏j=1q​j(p−j)≡(−1)q(q!)2(modp).
(16)
When p≡1(mod4), q is even, so X=q! satisfies X2≡−1(modp). This proves the first supplementary law for quadratic reciprocity without assuming a primitive root. Hence
X2≡−1(modp) is solvable exactly when p≡1(mod4).​
(17)
The two solutions are q! and −q! modulo p: their difference is nonzero, and factoring X2−Y2 shows that a degree-two polynomial over the field of residues modulo p has no further roots.
