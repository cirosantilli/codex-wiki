The triple bond in the original Dynkin diagram gives the Cartan integers
⟨β,α∨⟩=−1,⟨α,β∨⟩=−3,
(20)
where α is the long root and β the short root. Their product is 4cos2θ=3, and the inner product of distinct simple roots is nonpositive. The ratio of the two integers gives the squared-length ratio. Therefore
θ=65π​=150∘,∥β∥∥α∥​=3​.​
(21)
It is convenient to normalize (α,α)=6, (β,β)=2 and (α,β)=−3. Scaling the inner product does not affect the root system or the fundamental-weight relations.
For nonproportional roots γ,δ, the root-string theorem states that the root string is consecutive:
Sγ,δ​={δ−pγ,…,δ+qγ},p,q∈Z≥0​,p−q=(γ,γ)2(δ,γ)​.
(22)
The endpoints are maximal, and reflection in γ reverses the string. The Cartan integer determines p−q, not in general the total p+q+1 by itself. This result follows by restricting the Adjoint representation to the sl2 subalgebra associated with a root. If the roots are distinct simple roots, δ−γ cannot be a root: its simple-root coefficients have opposite signs. Thus p=0 and
∣Sγ,δ​∣=1−⟨δ,γ∨⟩.​
(23)
Here length means number of roots; the number of intervals between them is one less. Distinctness matters. If γ=δ, a reduced root system gives the set {γ,−γ}, with a missing zero between them, so the consecutive-string theorem and the displayed simple-root formula do not apply.
In the G2 root system, the initial strings are
Sα,β​={β,α+β},Sβ,α​={α,α+β,α+2β,α+3β}.​
(24)
For δ=α+3β, ⟨δ,α∨⟩=−1. Since δ−α=3β is not a root in a reduced root system, p=0 and q=1: this generates 2α+3β. The remaining strings explain why the construction stops. The α-strings through β and α+β are the same two-element string; the one through α+2β is a singleton since subtracting α gives 2β, and its Cartan integer is zero; the one through 2α+3β is the string {α+3β,2α+3β}. The β-strings through α, α+β, α+2β and α+3β are the initial four-element string. Finally, 2α+3β is orthogonal to β; its β-string is a singleton because 2α+2β=2(α+β) is not a root. Apply the same reasoning to negatives. Using the permitted completeness of this procedure gives
Φ=±{β,α,α+β,α+2β,α+3β,2α+3β}.​
(25)
The short positive roots are β,α+β,α+2β, of squared length two; the other three are long, of squared length six. Each root space is one-dimensional and the Cartan subalgebra has dimension two, so
dimG2​=2+12=14.​
(26)
Here the dimension refers to the Lie algebra, with one Cartan generator per rank, not just the number of roots.
Write a prospective weight as w=aα+bβ. The pairings with simple coroots are
⟨w,α∨⟩=2a−b,⟨w,β∨⟩=−3a+2b.
(27)
The fundamental weights are dual to those coroots. Solving the two linear systems gives, in the long-root-first numbering of this paper,
ω1​=2α+3β,ω2​=α+2β.​
(28)
The representation with Dynkin labels (0,1) has highest weight λ=ω2​, a short root. Numbering the short root first, as some references do, would call this the (1,0) representation instead; the representation itself is unchanged.
The weight set of a finite-dimensional irreducible highest-weight representation is invariant under the Weyl group and lies in the convex hull of the orbit of its highest weight. All weights also differ from the highest weight by an element of the root lattice. The orbit of λ comprises the six short roots, so all six are weights. The lowering operators give the chain
α+2β −β​ α+β −α​ β −β​ 0 −β​ −β −α​ −α−β −β​ −α−2β.
(29)
In particular zero occurs: at weight β, its pairing with β∨ is two, so the lowering operator is nonzero by the finite-dimensional sl2 Lie algebra representation theory. The β-string through it is the usual three-weight string β,0,−β; higher weight 2β would lie outside the highest-weight convex hull.
There can be no further weights. Every point of that convex hull has squared norm at most ∥λ∥2=2, while a root-lattice point has
∥aα+bβ∥2=6a2−6ab+2b2=23​a2+2(b−23​a)2.
(30)
The integer solutions of the bound ∥w∥2≤2 are precisely zero and the six short roots: a=0 gives b=−1,0,1; a=1 gives b=1,2; and a=−1 gives b=−1,−2. Thus
WtV(0,1)={0,±β,±(α+β),±(α+2β)},dimV(0,1)=7.​
(31)
The final dimension uses the stipulated nondegeneracy of the weights. It also agrees with the Weyl dimension formula without that stipulation. The zero weight is not in the Weyl orbit of the nonzero weights, so this is not a minuscule representation.
