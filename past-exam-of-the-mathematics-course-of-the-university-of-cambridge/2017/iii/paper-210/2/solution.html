Choose a Borel measurable function version of the posterior success probability η:Rd→[0,1]. Conditional on X=x, predicting zero has error probability η(x) and predicting one has error probability 1−η(x). A Bayes classifier for zero-one loss is therefore
CBayes(x)=1{η(x)≥1/2}​,R∗=Emin{η(X),1−η(X)}.​
(12)
Either decision is optimal when η(x)=1/2. The displayed Bayes risk also equals ∫min{η(x),1−η(x)}dPX​(x) and is unaffected by changing η on a PX​-null set.
For any measurable set A⊆Rd, independence and the uniform distribution of U1​ give
P(X1​∈A,Y1​=1)​=∫A​P(U1​≤η(x))dPX​(x)=∫A​η(x)dPX​(x)=P(X∈A,Y=1).​
(13)
Likewise the probability with label zero is ∫A​(1−η(x))dPX​(x). These sets determine the joint probability distribution, so
(X1​,Y1​) =d (X,Y).​
(14)
The same construction makes all training pairs independent and identically distributed random variables.
Fix a feature-measurable nearest-neighbour tie-breaking rule, for example increasing original index for observations at equal Euclidean norm distance from the query. This is important: the ordering may depend on the features, but not on the Ui​ or labels. Let Y(j)​(x) be the label attached to the jth ordered feature. The classifier from the K-nearest neighbors algorithm is
Cnknn​(x)=1{k−1∑j=1k​Y(j)​(x)≥1/2}​.​
(15)
The displayed rule resolves a tied vote in favour of one; any fixed vote convention would also define the method. When k=1, this is the one-nearest-neighbour classifier Cn1nn​(x)=Y(1)​(x).
Write In​(x) for the selected original index. Conditional on all features X1​,…,Xn​, it is fixed, and UIn​(x)​ is still uniform on [0,1]. The two decisions being compared are
Cn1nn​(x)=1{UIn​(x)​≤η(XIn​(x)​)}​,Cn1nn​(x)=1{UIn​(x)​≤η(x)}​.
(16)
The latter is an oracle comparison rule: its relabeling is recomputed at each query x, rather than being a fixed relabeling of the training data for all queries. For a variable U with the uniform distribution on [0,1], the two threshold indicators at r,s∈[0,1] differ exactly when U lies between them, an interval of length ∣r−s∣. This Bernoulli coupling by a shared uniform random variable proves
P{Cn1nn​(x)=Cn1nn​(x)}=E∣η(X(1)​(x))−η(x)∣.​
(17)
Take the test pair (X,Y) independent of the training data and its auxiliary uniforms. Let Tn​=σ((Xi​,Yi​,Ui​):1≤i≤n), and interpret L(C) as conditional misclassification risk given Tn​. The tower property of conditional expectation gives EL(C)=P(C(X)=Y). Conditional on the test feature X=x and all training features, Cn1nn​(x) and Y are independent variables with a Bernoulli distribution of parameter η(x). Consequently, for every n,
EL(Cn1nn​)=E[2η(X){1−η(X)}].​
(18)
This is an exact finite-sample equality, not merely a limit.
The two error indicators can differ only if the two classifiers disagree, so
​EL(Cn1nn​)−EL(Cn1nn​)​​≤P{Cn1nn​(X)=Cn1nn​(X)}=E∣η(X(1)​(X))−η(X)∣.​
(19)
The permitted nearest-neighbour approximation theorem states that the last expected value tends to zero for the feature-based selection rule. This establishes the one-nearest-neighbour asymptotic risk
n→∞lim​EL(Cn1nn​)=n→∞lim​EL(Cn1nn​)=E[2η(X){1−η(X)}].​
(20)
For r∈[0,1], put a=min(r,1−r)∈[0,1/2]. Then 2r(1−r)=2a(1−a), and a≤2a(1−a)≤2a. Integrating gives the Bayes risk bound for one-nearest-neighbour classification
R∗≤n→∞lim​EL(Cn1nn​)≤2R∗.​
(21)
Thus a one-nearest-neighbour classifier need not achieve the Bayes risk: for constant η=1/4, its limiting misclassification risk is 3/8, while R∗=1/4.
Feature-based neighbour tie-breaking is required. The convention cannot be dropped for arbitrary PX​. For a counterexample, take Xi​=X=0 almost surely and constant η=1/4, and choose among the coincident neighbours the one with the smallest Ui​. Then the predicted label is one with probability 1−(3/4)n, so its misclassification risk tends to 3/4, exceeding the claimed upper bound 2R∗=1/2. The corrected theorem uses ties determined independently of the uniforms and labels, as above.
