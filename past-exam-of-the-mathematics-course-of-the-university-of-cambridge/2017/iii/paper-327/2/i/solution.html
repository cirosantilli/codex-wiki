For multi-indices α,β, define
pα,β​(φ)=supx∈Rn​∣xα∂βφ(x)∣,S(Rn)={φ∈C∞:pα,β​(φ)<∞ for all α,β}.
(13)
These seminorms define the Fréchet space topology of the Schwartz space: φj​→φ exactly when every pα,β​(φj​−φ) tends to zero. An equivalent increasing family is
qm​(φ)=max∣β∣≤m​supx​(1+∣x∣)m∣∂βφ(x)∣.
(14)
A tempered distribution is a continuous linear functional on this space. Equivalently, ∣⟨T,φ⟩∣≤Cqm​(φ) for some C,m. The usual weak convergence of tempered distributions means ⟨Tj​,φ⟩→⟨T,φ⟩ for every fixed Schwartz function. The strong dual topology instead requires uniform convergence on every subset of the Schwartz space that is a bounded set in a topological vector space; the Fourier maps below are continuous in both topologies.
Using φ​(ξ)=∫e−ix⋅ξφ(x)dx, differentiation under the integral and integration by parts give
​ξα∂ξβ​φ​(ξ)​≤​∂xα​(xβφ)​L1​.
(15)
The omitted coefficient has modulus one. By the Leibniz rule, every integrand is a finite sum of a polynomial times a derivative of φ. Inserting the integrable weight (1+∣x∣)−n−1 bounds its L1 norm by finitely many Schwartz space seminorms. In particular qm​(φ​)≤Cm​qm+n+1​(φ). Thus the Fourier transform maps S continuously into itself.
For completeness, the Fourier inversion theorem follows here by Gaussian regularization. The inverse transform of e−t∣ξ∣2 is gt​(x)=(4πt)−n/2e−∣x∣2/(4t). The Fourier transform of a Gaussian and Fubini's theorem show
(2π)−n∫eix⋅ξe−t∣ξ∣2φ​(ξ)dξ=(gt​∗φ)(x).
(16)
As t↓0, the right side tends to φ(x) by the approximate identity property, while the left side tends to the undamped inverse integral by dominated convergence theorem, since φ​∈L1. Hence
F−1ψ(x)=(2π)−n∫eix⋅ξψ(ξ)dξ,F2φ(x)=(2π)nφ(−x).​
(17)
The inverse is (2π)−n times reflection composed with the continuous Fourier transform, and is therefore continuous on the Schwartz space. This proves a continuous linear isomorphism with continuous inverse.
Define the Fourier transform of a tempered distribution by
⟨T,φ⟩=⟨T,φ​⟩.
(18)
The continuous map on Schwartz space makes this a tempered distribution; the transpose of F−1 supplies its inverse. Pointwise convergence of pairings proves weak continuity. For the strong dual topology, the transform of a bounded set of Schwartz functions is bounded, so uniform convergence of pairings on bounded sets proves continuity of both Fourier maps there too.
Now let R∈SO(n) be a rotation matrix in the special orthogonal group and write ρR​T=T∘R. A change of variables with unit Jacobian gives
φ∘Rt​(ξ)=φ​(Rtξ).
(19)
The rotation equivariance of the Fourier transform on tempered distributions follows by duality:
⟨ρR​T​,φ⟩​=⟨T,φ​∘Rt⟩=⟨T,φ∘Rt​⟩=⟨T,φ∘Rt⟩=⟨ρR​T,φ⟩.​
(20)
Therefore ρR​T​=ρR​T. Applying this identity and the inverse Fourier transform gives the two directions:
T∘R=T for every R∈SO(n) ⟺ T∘R=T for every R∈SO(n).​
(21)
This is precisely preservation of radial tempered distributions. For n>1, SO(n) acts transitively on spheres, so an invariant smooth function is an ordinary radial function.
