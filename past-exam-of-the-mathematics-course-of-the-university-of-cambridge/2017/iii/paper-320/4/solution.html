For a regular Lagrangian define the canonical momentum components pj​=∂L/∂q˙​j​. Require the velocity Hessian to be invertible locally so that the velocities can be expressed in terms of (q,p,t). The Legendre transform in mechanics defines
H(q,p,t)=j∑​pj​q˙​j​−L(q,q˙​,t).​
(32)
The explicit time argument cannot generally be omitted when the given Lagrangian depends on time. Its differential is dH=∑j​q˙​j​dpj​−∑j​(∂L/∂qj​)dqj​−(∂L/∂t)dt, since the dq˙​j​ terms cancel. The Euler-Lagrange equations give ∂L/∂qj​=p˙​j​, and comparison yields Hamilton's equations:
q˙​j​=∂pj​∂H​,p˙​j​=−∂qj​∂H​,∂t​H=−∂t​L.​
(33)
For the time-independent stellar gravitational potential below, H is conserved. Its sign convention differs from the binding energy in question 2: here E=H=v2/2−ψ is the ordinary specific orbital energy.
The prescribed nondegenerate prolate spheroidal coordinates require 0<α<β; equality collapses the coordinate family. Their physical velocity components are vj​=hj​q˙​j​, and hϕ​=R. Per unit stellar mass the Lagrangian is L=21​∑j​hj2​q˙​j2​+ψ, so pj​=hj2​q˙​j​=hj​vj​. Its Hamiltonian is
H=21​(hλ2​pλ2​​+hμ2​pμ2​​+hϕ2​pϕ2​​)−ψ.​
(34)
This uses acceleration +∇ψ, as specified, rather than importing the opposite gravitational potential convention.
For the Stäckel potential, use the Hamilton-Jacobi equation with principal function S=Wλ​(λ)+Wμ​(μ)+lϕ−Et. Axisymmetry makes pϕ​=l=Rvϕ​ constant. Write d=λ−μ and A(τ)=(τ+α)(τ+β). Multiplying H=E by d and using
(λ+α)(μ+α)d​=μ+α1​−λ+α1​
(35)
separates it as F(λ)−Eλ=F(μ)−Eμ, where F(τ)=2A(τ)(Wτ′​)2−(α−β)l2/[2(τ+α)]−G(τ). Each side depends on its own coordinate only and therefore equals a constant, chosen as −I. Here G(τ) is the generating function of the gravitational potential, not the gravitational constant. Hence
2(τ+α)(τ+β)pτ2​−2(τ+α)(α−β)l2​−G(τ)−Eτ=−I,τ=λ,μ.
(36)
This proves the conserved axisymmetric Stäckel third integral. Eliminating E between the two expressions and using pj​=hj​vj​ gives
I=21​[μvλ2​+λvμ2​+(λ+μ+α)vϕ2​]−λ−μμG(λ)−λG(μ)​.​
(37)
At regular generic phase-space points the three separation constants E,l,I are independent; special circular or degenerate orbits can reduce their local independence. Thus the model is an integrable Hamiltonian system.
For the intended regular, phase-mixed steady model, Jeans theorem permits a galactic distribution function f=F(E,l,I). At any fixed point, E and I depend on vλ​ and vμ​ only through their squares, and l depends only on vϕ​. Thus f is even separately in vλ​ and vμ​, whether or not it is even in vϕ​. Odd integration proves velocity-ellipsoid alignment in a Stäckel potential:
⟨vλ​vμ​⟩=⟨vλ​vϕ​⟩=⟨vμ​vϕ​⟩=0.​
(38)
The argument allows azimuthal streaming: it does not require zero mean vϕ​. The means in the two meridional directions vanish, so the off-diagonal velocity covariance also vanishes. Second moments must exist for this conclusion.
There is a hypothesis behind the printed universal wording. On a generic nonresonant regular invariant torus, a continuous steady distribution is constant along a dense orbit and hence depends only on the torus integrals; this justifies the phase-mixed form. Bare stationarity alone is not sufficient in a degenerate resonant gravitational potential with additional integrals. For example H=21​(v2+ω2r2) is a Stäckel Hamiltonian: choose G(τ)=−21​ω2[τ2+(α+β)τ], up to a constant vacuum shift. The additional conserved quantity Kxy​=vx​vy​+ω2xy gives a positive normalizable steady tracer f∝exp(−aH+ϵKxy​) for a>∣ϵ∣>0. Its local ⟨vx​vy​⟩=ϵ/(a2−ϵ2)=0. At azimuth zero and a regular point where the λ direction has a radial component, this gives nonzero ⟨vλ​vϕ​⟩. This tracer need not itself source the harmonic gravitational potential. It exhibits why the stronger, ordinary phase-mixed three-integral assumption is necessary instead of claiming that every imaginable steady distribution has the asserted alignment.
The geometry follows by multiplying the coordinate-defining equation and evaluating at τ=−α,−β:
R2=α−β(λ+α)(μ+α)​,z2=β−α(λ+β)(μ+β)​.
(39)
Constant λ surfaces are prolate spheroids, with equatorial semiaxis λ+α​ and polar semiaxis λ+β​. Constant interior μ surfaces are two-sheeted hyperboloids. Their common foci lie at z=±β−α​. The orbit volume in an axisymmetric Stäckel potential is determined by
pτ2​=2(τ+α)(τ+β)Eτ+G(τ)−I+(α−β)l2/[2(τ+α)]​≥0.
(40)
Its allowed coordinate intervals have turning boundaries or coordinate endpoints. A typical bound orbit with l=0 oscillates between inner and outer spheroids, oscillates vertically between hyperboloidal limits, and circulates about the symmetry axis with ϕ˙​=l/R2. The equatorial fold μ=−β joins the positive and negative z branches; it is not necessarily a physical turning point in z.
For three incommensurate frequencies, the regular trajectory densely samples its projected allowed volume, with nonuniform occupation determined by its speeds. It does not follow one coordinate surface, and it need not close. Resonant orbits may be closed or lie in a smaller projected set; l=0 confines azimuthal motion to a meridional plane (with the usual axis coordinate qualification), and circular or equatorial limits are also lower-dimensional. Smooth Stäckel separation removes the generic chaotic behavior of nonintegrable potentials; it does not assert that every orbit fills a three-dimensional volume.
