To check that this really separates growing modes, put c=ω/k in the upper half-plane. Residue integration, or integration by parts followed by the Cauchy resolvent, gives ∫F′(u)/(u−c)du=ρ0​/(c+ib)2. The upper-half-plane solution has ω=i(4πGρ0​​−kb), which grows exactly for 0<k<kJ​. At the threshold it approaches the marginal mode continuously. Although b plays the threshold role of a velocity scale, this distribution's second moment diverges: the radial integrand for ∫v2f0​d3v tends to a nonzero constant at large speed. It is therefore incorrect to identify b2 with a finite velocity dispersion or to infer this result by substituting an rms speed into a fluid formula.
