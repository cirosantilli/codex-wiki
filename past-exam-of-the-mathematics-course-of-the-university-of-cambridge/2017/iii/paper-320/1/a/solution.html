The stellar relaxation time is the time on which accumulated discrete gravitational encounters change a typical star's velocity by an amount comparable to its original velocity. It concerns two-body relaxation, rather than the much faster orbital evolution in a smooth gravitational potential. A collisionless stellar system requires this time to greatly exceed the duration being studied.
In the straight-line impulse approximation, an encounter at relative speed u has transverse acceleration Gmb/(b2+u2t2)3/2. Thus
Δv⊥​=∫−∞∞​(b2+u2t2)3/2Gmbdt​=bu2Gm​.
(1)
A small-angle gravitational encounter needs Δv⊥​≪u. The transition to order-one deflections is therefore b∼Gm/u2, ignoring factors such as the equal-mass relative deflection. For the spherical estimate u∼v,
bmin​∼v2Gm​.​
(2)
This is the lower cutoff of the weak-scattering estimate; closer encounters actually occur and require strong-scattering treatment.
The number of encounters in time T with impact parameters in [b,b+db] is 2πbdbnuT. Independent random transverse directions make mean kicks cancel while their variances add. Consequently
T⟨∣Δv∣2⟩​=∫bmin​R​(bu2Gm​)22πbnudb=u8πG2m2n​logΛ,Λ=bmin​R​.
(3)
Each logarithmic interval contributes equally, giving the Coulomb logarithm in stellar dynamics. Defining a deflection time by variance v2 and setting u=v gives v3/(8πG2m2nlogΛ). The paper instead uses an order-one normalization three quarters of this estimate:
trel​≃32π3​G2m2nlogΛv3​.​
(4)
Both have the same physical scaling. The specified characteristic speed, the word “comparable”, the velocity-distribution average and strong-encounter cutoff do not fix that numerical coefficient uniquely. The crude equal-speed impulse calculation must not be claimed to determine 3/(32π) exactly.
For a self-gravitating, approximately virialized system, the virial theorem gives v2∼GNm/R. With n=3N/(4πR3), Λ∼N and the stellar crossing time tdyn​=R/v, substitution into the paper's convention gives
trel​≃8logNN​tdyn​.​
(5)
An externally dominated gravitational potential or a different structural constant changes this substitution. For an N-body simulation lasting T=Ktdyn​, a tolerable fractional velocity-squared diffusion ε≪1 requires T/trel​≲ε, hence
8logNN​≳εK​.​
(6)
There is no unique smallest N without K, the error tolerance and the force prescription. For scale, equality at trel​/tdyn​=100 occurs near N=7.1×103; N=105 gives about 1.1×103 crossing times. A calculation lasting 100 crossing times therefore needs substantially more than the first threshold to have negligible relaxation. Gravitational softening can raise the effective cutoff and reduce artificial scattering, but it also sets the spatial force resolution.
