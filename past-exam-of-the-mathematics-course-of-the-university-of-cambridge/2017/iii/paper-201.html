Relative to a probability space (Ω,F,P) and a filtration (Fn​), the real stochastic process (Mn​) is a martingale if it is adapted, each Mn​ is an integrable random variable, and
E[Mn+1​∣Fn​]=Mn​almost surely for every n≥0.​
(1)
Here adapted means Mn​ is Fn​-measurable, and integrability means E∣Mn​∣<∞. By the tower property of conditional expectation, the equivalent multi-step condition is E[Mj​∣Fi​]=Mi​ for all i≤j. The filtration is part of the definition; specifying only the marginal means is insufficient.

The martingale convergence theorem in its L1-bounded form says that a martingale with supn​E∣Mn​∣<∞ has an integrable limit M∞​ and
Mn​⟶M∞​almost surely,E∣M∞​∣≤nsup​E∣Mn​∣.​
(23)
The norm bound on the limit follows from Fatou lemma. Boundedness in L1 by itself does not imply convergence in L1. The uniformly integrable martingale convergence theorem gives the stronger conclusion: if (Mn​) is uniformly integrable, then Mn​→M∞​ both almost surely and in L1 norm, and Mn​=E[M∞​∣Fn​]. Conversely, convergence in L1 implies uniform integrability.
For the distinction, the fair-coin doubling martingale Mn​=2n1{first n tosses are heads}​ has expectation one for every n but converges almost surely to zero. Its L1 norm remains one, so its convergence is not in L1 norm. These two formulations specify exactly which hypothesis is needed in part (d).
