Construct a mean-square derivative of a Gaussian process on the original probability space before choosing its continuous modification. The real covariance function satisfies K(h)=K(−h), because covariance is symmetric. Three-times differentiability implies that K′′ is continuous everywhere and differentiable at zero. Since K′′ is an even function,
K′′′(0)=0,K′′(h)−K′′(0)=o(∣h∣)(h→0).
(10)
This uses differentiability of K′′ at zero, not a bound on K′′′ throughout a neighborhood.
Let Dh​X(t)=(X(t+h)−X(t))/h for h=0. Twice applying the fundamental theorem of calculus to the covariance function gives
E[Dh​X(t)Dk​X(s)]=−hk1​∫0h​∫0k​K′′(t−s+u−v)dvdu.
(11)
Oriented integrals make this identity valid for negative as well as positive h,k. For a fixed t, the right side tends to −K′′(0) as h,k→0. In particular,
E∣Dh​X(t)−Dk​X(t)∣2⟶0.
(12)
By completeness of L2 space, the mean-square derivative of a Gaussian process
Y(t)=L2-limh→0​Dh​X(t)
(13)
exists. Finite vectors consisting of values of X and Y have a centered multivariate normal distribution: approximate the Y coordinates by difference quotients and use mean-square convergence and characteristic functions. Thus Y is a centered Gaussian process jointly constructed with X, with
E[Y(t)Y(s)]=−K′′(t−s),E[X(t)Y(s)]=−K′(t−s).
(14)
In particular −K′′(0)≥0, and −K′′ is a positive-semidefinite kernel because it is an actual covariance function. Merely constructing an unrelated Gaussian process with that covariance function would not yet construct a modification of a stochastic process of X.
The increment variance of Y is
E∣Y(t)−Y(s)∣2=2(K′′(t−s)−K′′(0)).
(15)
For small ∣t−s∣, differentiability of K′′ at zero bounds this by C∣t−s∣. For the remaining distances in [0,1], continuity of K′′ enlarges C to give the same bound. The fourth moment of a centered normal distribution is three times its variance squared, so
E∣Y(t)−Y(s)∣4=3(E∣Y(t)−Y(s)∣2)2≤C4​∣t−s∣2.
(16)
The one-dimensional Kolmogorov continuity theorem states that E∣U(t)−U(s)∣p≤C∣t−s∣1+η with p,η>0 gives a continuous modification, with sample-path Hölder continuity of every order less than η/p. Apply it here with p=4,η=1. We obtain a continuous modification Y on [0,1]. On its null exceptional event, set the entire path equal to zero.
Define, on this same probability space,
X(t)=X(0)+∫0t​Y(s)ds(0≤t≤1).
(17)
The path integral exists because its integrand is a continuous function. It agrees in L2 space with the integral of Y: Riemann sums converge in L2 space because Y is mean-square continuous; sums of Y have the same values almost surely at each finite collection of mesh points and converge pathwise to the integral. These two limits therefore agree almost surely for each fixed t. Equivalently, the integral is the Bochner integral of the L2 space-valued map s↦Y(s).
To verify the modification of a stochastic process property directly, put At​=X(t)−X(0) and It​=∫0t​Y(s)ds. The preceding covariance identities and the fundamental theorem of calculus give
EAt2​E[At​It​]EIt2​​=2(K(0)−K(t)),=∫0t​[−K′(t−s)−K′(s)]ds=2(K(0)−K(t)),=−∫0t​∫0t​K′′(s−u)dsdu=2(K(0)−K(t)).​
(18)
Therefore E(At​−It​)2=0 and X(t)=X(t) almost surely for every fixed t. This is also the mean-square fundamental theorem of calculus in this stationary setting. If a version on all of R is desired, retain the original X(t) outside [0,1]; equality at every fixed parameter still holds.
The ordinary fundamental theorem of calculus now gives the simultaneous pathwise conclusion
X∈C1([0,1]) almost surely,X′(t)=Y(t)(0<t<1).​
(19)
Thus the requested differentiable modification of a stationary Gaussian process exists. Integrability of K is compatible with the hypotheses but is unnecessary for this direct argument. It also avoids any assumption that a third absolute moment of a spectral density follows from three-times differentiability.
