Let X be a coarse-grained configuration, with restricted partition function ZX​ and free energy F(X)=−kB​TlogZX​. Its Boltzmann distribution is π(X)∝e−βF(X). Microscopic reversibility pairs every equilibrium microscopic history with a history read backwards, including reversal of all time-odd variables. Summing this identity over the microscopic histories representing a given coarse-grained path preserves it; a Markov assumption is not needed for this argument.
Write PF​ for the probability density of a specified history conditional on its starting configuration X1​, and PB​ for its physically reversed history conditional on starting at ΘX2​, where Θ reverses time-odd variables. Equilibrium time-reversal symmetry gives
π(X1​)PF​=π(ΘX2​)PB​.
(1)
Since F(ΘX)=F(X), the desired conditional path relation is
PB​PF​​=π(X1​)π(X2​)​=e−β(F2​−F1​).​
(2)
For a reversible Markov chain, this also follows by multiplying detailed balance ratios Pij​/Pji​=πj​/πi​ along the history: the intermediate equilibrium weights telescope.
The conditioning is essential. If the starting equilibrium weights are included in the path probabilities, the forward and reversed probabilities are equal, rather than differing by the displayed exponential. For example, a two-state continuous-time Markov chain with F1​=0, F2​=Δ>0, rates k12​=e−βΔ and k21​=1 has equal equilibrium-weighted probabilities for the two reversed single-jump histories. Their conditional rate ratio is instead e−βΔ. Normalized bridges conditioned on both endpoints also have a different normalization from the path weights used above. The PDF prints FB​ in the denominator while its explanatory text defines PB​; the latter is the intended notation. For the scalar order parameter used subsequently, Θϕ=ϕ.

Here Γ>0 is a kinetic coefficient, and the additive Gaussian white noise must be centered. A rigorous starting point is a finite spatial discretization or finite set of modes of the coarse-grained field. For each field coordinate, the Fokker-Planck probability current has the continuum notation
J(r;[ϕ])=−Γδϕ(r)δF​P[ϕ]−2σ2​δϕ(r)δP​.
(3)
The equilibrium Boltzmann distribution Peq​∝e−βF has δPeq​/δϕ=−β(δF/δϕ)Peq​. Detailed balance for this time-even field requires zero configuration-space current. Therefore
Jeq​=(−Γ+2βσ2​)δϕδF​Peq​=0,σ2=2ΓkB​T.​
(4)
This is the Model A fluctuation-dissipation relation. Conversely, that noise strength makes the equilibrium current vanish and gives reversible relaxational dynamics when the equilibrium measure is normalizable.
Alternatively, the Onsager--Machlup path probability for Model A dynamics compares the squared required noise histories ϕ˙​+ΓδF/δϕ and −ϕ˙​+ΓδF/δϕ. Their difference gives log(PF​/PB​)=−2ΓΔF/σ2, using the functional chain rule. The midpoint convention and time-reversal invariance of a path Jacobian justify cancellation of the common Jacobian. Comparing with the conditional path ratio gives the same result. The spatial white-noise continuum is a coarse-grained idealization: retain a microscopic cutoff when pointwise fluctuations or ultraviolet divergences matter.

With periodic or sufficiently decaying boundary conditions, integration by parts gives
δϕδF​=aϕ−κ∇2ϕ.

Assume κ≥0 for a stable quadratic free energy; the diffusive smoothing discussed below requires κ>0. In a periodic box of volume V, use the unitary convention ϕq​=V−1/2∫V​ϕ(r)e−iq⋅rdr. A real field has ϕ−q​=ϕq∗​. Since the Fourier transform of a derivative converts ∇2 to −q2, each Fourier mode obeys
ϕ˙​q​=−r(q)ϕq​+ηq​,r(q)=Γ(a+κq2)>0.​

ϕq​(t)=e−r(q)tϕq​(0)+∫0t​e−r(q)(t−s)ηq​(s)ds.​

Unlike a conserved order parameter, the zero wavenumber mode decays at the nonzero rate Γa.

The post-quench Ornstein-Uhlenbeck process has r1​(q)=Γ(a1​+κq2). Its future noise is independent of the initial field. Consequently the cross term between the initial Fourier mode and its future noise integral has zero expectation. The Itô isometry, or the given white-noise covariance, then yields
Sq​(t)​=e−2r1​(q)tSq​(0)+2ΓkB​T∫0t​e−2r1​(q)(t−s)ds=e−2r1​(q)tSq​(0)+a1​+κq2kB​T​[1−e−2r1​(q)t].​
(8)
The initial Gaussian Boltzmann distribution, with a=a0​, gives Sq​(0)=kB​T/(a0​+κq2) in the same unitary normalization. Thus the Gaussian Model A quench has
Sq​(t)=a1​+κq2kB​T​+[a0​+κq2kB​T​−a1​+κq2kB​T​]e−2Γ(a1​+κq2)t.​
(9)
The static structure factor interpolates monotonically mode by mode between its two positive equilibrium values. The covariance relaxes at twice the amplitude decay rate. These statements assume that T, Γ and κ are unchanged by the quench; both a0​,a1​>0 exclude a spinodal instability.

For a deterministic localized initial peak in an unbounded d-dimensional sample, the mean obeys ∂t​⟨ϕ⟩=Γκ∇2⟨ϕ⟩−Γa⟨ϕ⟩. The Gaussian Model A impulse response is therefore the heat kernel multiplied by the local relaxation factor:
⟨ϕ(r,t)⟩=(4πΓκt)d/2Ae−Γat​exp[−4Γκt∣r−r′∣2​],t>0.​
(10)
The peak spreads over a distance of order Γκt​ and its central height falls both by spreading and by exponential relaxation. Its integrated mean is Ae−Γat, rather than A: the equation describes nonconserved order-parameter dynamics, despite the use of the word “density” in this part.
An individual realization also develops thermal fluctuations; it does not tend pointwise to zero. For an initially deterministic field, the connected Fourier mode variance grows as [kB​T/(a+κq2)](1−e−2r(q)t), while the deterministic mean decays. It approaches the zero-mean Gaussian equilibrium field at the coarse-graining cutoff. On a periodic or bounded sample use the corresponding boundary-adapted heat kernel; at κ=0 there is no spatial spreading. The Dirac delta distribution is an ideal initial profile, recovered as a distributional limit; a finite-energy physical peak has a microscopic width.

a OurBigBook.com
The field ϕ is a compositional order parameter in a binary fluid mixture. Its spatial integral is conserved in a closed sample, so advection and diffusion enter a continuity equation; the material derivative follows the incompressible fluid. The diffusive current J=−M∇μ, with positive order-parameter mobility M, moves material down the chemical potential gradient. The chemical potential of a composition field is the functional derivative of the symmetric quartic Landau free energy
F=∫[2a​ϕ2+4b​ϕ4+2κ​∣∇ϕ∣2]dr,
(11)
so μ=aϕ+bϕ3−κ∇2ϕ. Positive b,κ stabilize large amplitudes and penalize sharp interfaces; a<0 permits phase separation.
The momentum equation is the Navier-Stokes equation with dynamic viscosity η, constant mass density ρ, and the Korteweg force −ϕ∇μ arising from composition stresses. The pressure enforces incompressible flow, ∇⋅v=0, and can absorb pure gradient contributions to that thermodynamic force. For example, −ϕ∇μ=μ∇ϕ−∇(ϕμ) gives an equivalent force after redefining the pressure. These equations constitute deterministic Model H dynamics.
Their dissipative signs can be checked by the energy dissipation identity for Model H. Under periodic or no-flux, no-work boundary conditions, composition advection and capillary work cancel between F and the kinetic energy, giving
dtd​[F+2ρ​∫∣v∣2dr]=−∫[M∣∇μ∣2+η∣∇v∣2]dr≤0.​
(12)
Thus diffusion and viscosity dissipate energy, while the reversible advective coupling transfers it between composition and motion. Thermal noises are omitted at this deterministic hydrodynamic level.

The dynamical scaling of binary-fluid coarsening assumes a statistically self-similar bicontinuous morphology with one growing length L, thin interfaces, curvature of order 1/L, and typical fluid speed of order L˙. Inertial acceleration then scales as L¨ and L˙2/L, viscous force density as ηL˙/L2, and capillary force density as σ/L2. The pressure is eliminated or estimated together with the interfacial force. The numerical coefficients summarize geometry and projected force directions; this is a scaling closure, not a spatially exact momentum equation.
The approximation further neglects diffusive growth relative to advective growth, assumes fixed volume fraction and material parameters, no external forcing, and no additional relevant macroscopic length. A finite initial domain size, interface width or finite container would introduce further ratios outside the asymptotic scaling regime. Velocity gradients need not stay on the domain scale in a genuinely multiscale turbulent flow, so this single-length argument is not a proof for every hydrodynamic morphology.
With [ρ]=ML−3, [η]=ML−1T−1 and [σ]=MT−2, dimensional analysis gives the unique scales
L0​=ρση2​,t0​=ρσ2η3​,t0​L0​​=ησ​.
(13)
Thus, within the stated asymptotic single-scale hypothesis, the hydrodynamic coarsening crossover scales give L=L0​f(u), u=t/t0​. All four force-density terms have the same dimensional prefactor ρ2σ3/η4. Dividing by it yields
αf′′+βff′2​=γf2f′​+f2δ​.​
(14)
Primes denote d/du. The PDF includes ηγL˙/L2; the TeX drops η. The PDF expression is dimensionally consistent. For a positive growth branch, a resisting viscous term and a driving capillary term have opposite signed coefficients; one may set γ=−cv​<0, δ=cs​>0. The question calls these coefficients order one, not positive. Treating every term on the printed right-hand side as positive would lose the viscous-capillary balance.

For a power law f(u)=Cup, restoring dimensions gives
L(t)=Cη2−3pρp−1σ2p−1tp.
(15)
Independence of mass density requires p−1=0. Hence viscous hydrodynamic coarsening has
f(u)∼Cv​u,L(t)∼Cv​ησ​t.​
(16)
At small u, the capillary and viscous terms are both of order u−2, while the convective inertial term is of order u−1 and the acceleration of the leading linear law is zero. Thus the leading force balance is viscous resistance against capillary driving, giving Cv​=cs​/cv​=−δ/γ>0 in the signed convention of the preceding part.
Equivalently, the domain-scale Reynolds number is ρLL˙/η∼Cv2​u, so inertia is negligible as u→0. “Small” here refers to time compared with t0​ within a late-stage hydrodynamic window: it does not extend the model through the microscopic initial stage, where diffusion or interface formation can dominate. An initial-time offset changes the intercept, not this asymptotic growth exponent.

Use the intended regular scaling ansatz: g(u)∼Cuy, g′(u)∼Cyuy−1 and g′′(u)=Cy(y−1)uy−2+o(uy−2), with C>0, y>0. Relative to the drag magnitude g′, the two inertial contributions obey
g′g′′​∼uy−1​,g′g′2/g​=gg′​∼uy​.
(22)
Thus both inertial-to-drag ratios tend to zero at large u, whatever the positive growth exponent; for y=1 the leading acceleration term vanishes identically. The long-time drag-limited hydrodynamic coarsening balance is therefore
cd​g′∼g2cs​​,g3(u)∼3cd​cs​​u,
(23)
so
g(u)∼(3cs​u/cd​)1/3,L(t)∼(3cd​cs​​ηˉ​σ​t)1/3.​
(24)
The leading law is independent of mass density. Within the drag-capillary reduction, finite initial data give L3(t)−L3(t∗​)=3(cs​/cd​)(σ/ηˉ​)(t−t∗​).
The derivative hypotheses are important: bare g(u)∼uy does not justify differentiating an asymptotic equivalence. For instance, g(u)=u+21​∫1u​sin(s3)ds is smooth and increasing, with g(u)/u→1, but g′′=23​u2cos(u3) is not small compared with g′=1+21​sin(u3). This is a counterexample to an inference from the bare asymptotic relation, not a solution of the coarsening equation. The physical claim uses the regular self-similar power law, for which the ratio calculation above applies.
This conclusion concerns the model's advective drag-capillary channel. Diffusive Ostwald ripening can also have a 1/3 growth exponent; equality of exponents does not establish that diffusion is negligible. If it is retained, the prefactor and crossover function may also depend on order-parameter mobility, and one must compare transport mechanisms rather than infer the mechanism from the exponent alone.

A nematic director is an unoriented axis: n and −n describe the same molecular alignment. A vector average cannot represent this head-tail symmetry. The orientational second moment ⟨ni​nj​⟩ is a symmetric second-rank tensor, and removing its isotropic part gives the traceless second-rank tensor Qij​∝⟨ni​nj​−δij​/2⟩. It vanishes for an isotropic angular distribution and transforms correctly under rotation matrices.
Every nonzero real symmetric traceless 2×2 matrix has eigenvalues λ/2,−λ/2 and can be written Q=λ(nnT−I/2) by choosing the eigenvector of the positive eigenvalue as director and λ≥0. Consequently TrQ2=λ2/2. The bulk Landau-de Gennes free energy becomes
fbulk​=2a​λ2+4b​λ4=4b​(λ2+ba​)2−4ba2​.
(25)
For a<0, its minimum occurs at λ2=−a/b. The elastic density K∣∇⋅Q∣2/2 is nonnegative and vanishes for a uniform field. Thus, with no boundary condition imposing a texture, the pointwise lower bound is attained by
λ0​=−a/b​,Qij​=λ0​(ni​nj​−δij​/2),fmin​=−a2/(4b).​
(26)
The negative-amplitude representation is equivalent in two dimensions to rotating the director by π/2, so the positive choice loses no tensor minima. This is minimization of the specified coarse-grained functional, not a claim of true thermodynamic long-range nematic order at every temperature in an infinite two-dimensional system. The PDF explicitly uses the divergence elastic term; the TeX's ∣∇Q∣2 loses the dot and would give different elastic coefficients.

Outside a core of radius r0​, set λ≃λ0​ to leading order. The uniform-state bulk energy cancels on subtraction, while the divergence elastic density is Kλ02​/(2r2). Its two-dimensional area integral is
ΔFouter​=2π∫r0​L​2r2Kλ02​​rdr=πKλ02​log(L/r0​).
(30)
Thus the logarithmic energy of a planar nematic disclination gives
ΔF=πKλ02​log(L/r0​)+O(Kλ02​),L≫r0​.​
(31)
The core suppresses the nematic amplitude to regularize the singular director texture. Balancing lost bulk ordering energy against elasticity gives r0​ of order K/∣a∣​. The exact core radius and additive core energy depend on the amplitude profile.
The “local” amplitude disturbance need not be literally compactly supported. The radial Euler-Lagrange equation following from the PDF density is
aλ+bλ3−4K​(λ′′+rλ′​−r24λ​)=0.
(32)
Its far-field expansion is λ=λ0​[1−K/(2∣a∣r2)+O(r−4)]. These algebraic tails change only finite contributions, not the logarithm. A regular core has λ=O(r2) for this integer winding.
The large cutoff L is set by the sample size for an isolated defect with corresponding imposed far-field winding, or by the distance to compensating defects or other texture-screening boundaries. An isolated nonzero winding in an infinite ordered plane has a logarithmically divergent excess energy; in a neutral pair, far-field distortions cancel beyond the separation. If L is not much larger than r0​, the leading logarithm is not a dominant approximation.

For a strictly planar nematic director, the order-parameter manifold at fixed nonzero amplitude is RP1, homeomorphic to a circle. Its fundamental group is Z. The topological charge of a two-dimensional nematic disclination is q=Δχ/(2π) for director angle χ, defined modulo π; the integer winding of the physical doubled angle 2χ is m=2q. Thus a q=+1 texture has m=2=0 and cannot be continuously replaced by a uniform nonvanishing planar texture while the surrounding loop is held fixed:
Topologically nontrivial in a strictly planar nematic.​
(33)
It can nevertheless split into two q=+1/2 defects, because their charges add to one. Such splitting preserves the total winding and is not topological unwinding. Allowing escape into the third dimension changes the manifold to RP2, with fundamental group Z/2Z; the doubled winding of this integer defect is then trivial. That escape is excluded by the stated two-dimensional director model.
Energetically, the defect is not the preferred isolated charge-one configuration at large separation of scales. At constant amplitude, the planar nematic divergence-elasticity identity gives Fel​=(Kλ02​/2)∫∣∇χ∣2, so a charge q has self-energy πKλ02​q2log(L/r0​). Separating two +1/2 defects by d, with r0​≪d≪L, makes the far field still charge one but reduces the near-field logarithm:
ΔFsplit​−ΔFq=1​=−2πKλ02​​log(d/r0​)+finite core-energy difference.
(34)
The logarithmic gain eventually dominates the finite core cost. Hence
Energetically favorable to split into two +21​ defects.​
(35)
This is energetic fission of an integer nematic disclination. It proves failure of global energetic stability, not the absence of every microscopic kinetic barrier or a full linear stability spectrum of the radial core. Core structure, boundaries or pinning can make a charge-one texture metastable over a finite time or length scale.

The total charge of the widely separated +1,−1 pair is zero, so a uniform planar nematic director is compatible with the far boundary if it imposes no additional winding. Each integer defect is susceptible to energetic fission of an integer nematic disclination: the positive defect can split into two +1/2 defects and the negative defect into two −1/2 defects. Like-sign elementary defects repel, whereas opposite signs attract through their elastic distortion. This commonly produces subsequent encounters and annihilations of opposite half-integer defects.
The attraction can also be seen before fission. In the fixed-amplitude outer theory, a neutral pair of charges q,−q separated by R has
Fpair​=2πKλ02​q2log(R/r0​)+core terms,−dRdFpair​​=−R2πKλ02​q2​.
(36)
Its energy therefore falls as the separation decreases. In nematic defect-antidefect annihilation, opposite defects meet, the amplitude can vanish in their merging cores, and the local winding disappears; the remaining smooth director distortion relaxes towards the uniform energy minimum.
Expected route: integer-defect fission and attraction of opposite elementary defects, followed by annihilation and relaxation of the remaining texture.
Direct approach and annihilation of the unsplit integer pair is also topologically allowed. Whether fission happens first, the paths of the four elementary defects, and the annihilation time require a specified dynamical law and core mobilities; the free energy alone does not fix them. Pinning, imposed boundary textures, or sustained thermal production of defect pairs can prevent a strictly defect-free final state. The stated final state presumes dissipative relaxation with no such obstruction.
