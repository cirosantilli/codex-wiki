Use the paper's classical norm convention: ∣u∣2;U​ is the derivative supremum norm through order two, not an L2 norm. Write H(u;U)=[D2u]α;U​ and Q(u,f)=∣u∣2;B1​​+∣f∣0,α;B1​​, where the latter is the full Hölder norm of the forcing.
Fix 0<δ<1. If the asserted estimate failed, rescaling the functions by their global Hessian matrix Hölder seminorm would produce uj​,fj​ with
H(uj​;B1​)=1,H(uj​;B1/2​)>δ+jQ(uj​,fj​).
(26)
In particular Q(uj​,fj​)→0. Choose xj​,yj​∈B1/2​ for which
∣D2uj​(yj​)−D2uj​(xj​)∣>2δ​∣yj​−xj​∣α.
(27)
The supremum norm of D2uj​ tends to zero, so rj​=∣yj​−xj​∣→0. Let Pj​ be the quadratic Taylor polynomial of uj​ at xj​, and define
Uj​(z)=rj2+α​uj​(xj​+rj​z)−Pj​(xj​+rj​z)​.
(28)
The domains contain B1/(2rj​)​, and Uj​,DUj​,D2Uj​ vanish at zero. Moreover
[D2Uj​]α​≤1,ΔUj​(z)=rj−α​(fj​(xj​+rj​z)−fj​(xj​)).
(29)
The right side is bounded by [fj​]α​∣z∣α and tends to zero locally uniformly. The normalized Hessian matrix bound controls ∣D2Uj​(z)∣≤∣z∣α; integration along line segments then bounds DUj​,Uj​ on every compact Euclidean ball. Arzelà-Ascoli theorem and a diagonal subsequence argument give convergence in C2 on compact subsets to an entire harmonic function U, with [D2U]α;Rn​≤1 and D2U(0)=0.
After a further subsequence zj​=(yj​−xj​)/rj​→z∗​ with ∣z∗​∣=1. The normalization ensures ∣D2U(z∗​)∣≥δ/2. But each second derivative of U is harmonic and has finite global Hölder seminorm with exponent below one. The supplied Liouville theorem makes each derivative constant; it is the globally Hölder case of the polynomial-growth Liouville theorem for harmonic functions. Its value at zero makes it zero, a contradiction. This blow-up compactness proof of an interior Schauder estimate establishes
H(u;B1/2​)≤δH(u;B1​)+Cn,α,δ​Q(u,f).​
(30)
To absorb the term on the larger Euclidean ball, rescale this estimate to Euclidean balls contained in Bs​. For 1/2≤r<s<1, pairs separated by less than (s−r)/2 are controlled in a Euclidean ball centred at their first point; farther pairs are controlled directly by ∥D2u∥∞​. Thus
H(u;Br​)≤δH(u;Bs​)+Cδ​(s−r)−2−αQ(u,f).
(31)
This is the covering step in the Simon absorption lemma. Take rj​=1−2−j−1 and fix δ<2−2−α. Iterating makes the remainder δjH(u;Brj​​) tend to zero, since the global seminorm is finite; the error terms form a convergent geometric series. Consequently
[D2u]α;B1/2​​≤Cn,α​(∣u∣2;B1​​+∣f∣0,α;B1​​).​
(32)
For the requested failure of the weaker estimate, take um​=m−2sin(mx1​) and fm​=−sin(mx1​). The derivative supremum norm ∣um​∣2​ and ∥fm​∥∞​ remain bounded, but comparison at 0 and πe1​/(2m), both in B1/2​ for large m, gives
[D2um​]α;B1/2​​≥(2m/π)α⟶∞.​
(33)
This proves the necessity of Hölder forcing for Schauder estimates. Every member of the sequence is smooth; failure comes from the increasing frequency, not from any lack of individual regularity.
