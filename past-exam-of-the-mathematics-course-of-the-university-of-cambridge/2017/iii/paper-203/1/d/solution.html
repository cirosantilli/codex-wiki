For the vertical slit, choose the branch of z2+1​ asymptotic to z at infinity. It is the mapping-out function of a compact H-hull for (0,i], and
z2+1​=z+2z1​+O(z−3).
(10)
Thus half-plane capacity of a vertical slit gives
hcap([0,i])=21​.​
(11)
The endpoint 0 is merely part of the boundary closure convention.
For the filled upper half-disc K={z∈H:∣z∣≤1}, the conformal map gK​(z)=z+1/z maps the exterior half-disc onto H. Indeed, it sends the semicircle to [−2,2], the remaining real boundary to the complementary intervals, and its inverse is the branch of (w+w2−4​)/2 asymptotic to w. Hence half-plane capacity of a half-disc gives
hcap(K)=1.​
(12)
If D denotes the open unit disc, the printed H∩D is not relatively closed and is literally not a compact H-hull. The intended value is the one for its filled relative closure K. With a closed-disc convention the displayed notation already represents that hull.
For a nonempty compact H-hull A, its closure meets R: otherwise a nonempty compact set strictly inside H would be separated from the lower half-plane in the complement, contradicting the simply connected domain condition for H∖A. Choose x∈A∩R and put d=diam(A). Taking limits in the definition of diameter shows ∣z−x∣≤d for every z∈A. Thus A lies in the filled half-disc of radius d centered at x. Its half-plane capacity is d2 by scaling and translation of half-plane capacity. Using monotonicity of half-plane capacity,
hcap(A)≤diam(A)2.​
(13)
For the empty compact H-hull, use diameter zero. This proves the unit-constant version of half-plane capacity is bounded by squared diameter; no claim of optimality of the constant is needed.
