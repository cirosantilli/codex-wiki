Take P0​>0, real α, and the real branch T≥T0​, with physical temperature T>0. In hydrostatic equilibrium the pressure decreases with height z:
dzdP​=−ρg<0.
(1)
Thus an atmospheric thermal inversion means dT/dz>0, equivalently dT/dP<0. For α=0, the logarithm of the given relation yields
T(P)=T0​+α2log2(P/P0​)​,αlog(P/P0​)​=T−T0​​≥0.
(2)
The sign restriction must be retained after squaring. In particular, the inverted branch has 0<P≤P0​, whereas the non-inverted branch has P≥P0​. By differentiation, on the interior of either branch,
dlogPdT​=α22log(P/P0​)​=α2T−T0​​​,dzdT​=−αP2ρgT−T0​​​.
(3)
Consequently,
α<0: thermal inversion;α>0: temperature decreases outward.​
(4)
At T=T0​, the inverse temperature gradient vanishes at the branch endpoint, rather than producing a finite isothermal interval. If α=0, the relation becomes P=P0​ independently of T; it is not a nontrivial vertical atmospheric pressure-temperature profile under hydrostatic equilibrium. These distinctions prevent the two sides of the squared parabola from being interpreted as one permissible atmosphere.
