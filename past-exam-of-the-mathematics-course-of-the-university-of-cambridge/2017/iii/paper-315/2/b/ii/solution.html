Assume a single-phase ideal gas mixture in a closed parcel, with common temperature T, total pressure P, and molar species amounts Ni​. Define N=∑i​Ni​ and volume mixing ratios xi​=Ni​/N, so ∑i​xi​=1. Ideal mixing makes partial pressure Pi​=xi​P.
For a pure ideal species, dμi​=−Sˉi​dT+Vˉi​dPi​, with molar volume Vˉi​=RT/Pi​. At fixed temperature, integration from standard pressure P∘ gives
μi​(T,Pi​)=μi∘​(T)+RTlog(Pi​/P∘).
(27)
The same logarithmic composition term follows from the ideal mixing entropy ΔSmix​=−R∑i​Ni​logxi​. Extensivity gives G=∑i​Ni​μi​. Hence the Gibbs free energy of an ideal-gas mixture is
G(T,P,{Ni​})=Ni∑​xi​μi∘​(T)+NRT[log(P/P∘)+i∑​xi​logxi​].​
(28)
Equivalently, write each standard chemical term as μi∘​=hi∘​−Tsi∘​, using molar enthalpy and entropy. For zero abundance the limit xlogx→0 defines the extensive expression continuously.
The fractions alone do not specify the extensive Gibbs free energy: the total amount N or an equivalent elemental inventory must also be fixed. Moreover, in a chemically closed reacting system N need not remain constant; for example, hydrogenation of carbon monoxide changes the total molecular count while conserving atoms. Minimization is therefore over species amounts subject to elemental constraints, rather than over fractions with an artificially fixed molecular count. Nonideal gases require activities or fugacities; condensates contribute separate phase terms.
