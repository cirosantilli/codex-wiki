Let an isothermal non-scattering atmospheric layer of temperature Ta​ lie above an optically thick continuum source with brightness temperature Tb​. Assume local thermodynamic equilibrium, so the layer emits with Planck function Bλ​(Ta​). Its vertical optical depth is τλ​; a ray with direction cosine μ has slant depth tλ​=τλ​/μ.
The formal solution of the radiative transfer equation gives
Iλ​=Bλ​(Tb​)e−tλ​+Bλ​(Ta​)(1−e−tλ​).​
(52)
The two terms are attenuated background and layer emission. Compare a line and nearby continuum at the same wavelength to adequate approximation, with tline​>tcont​. The one-layer spectral contrast is
Iline​−Icont​=[Bλ​(Ta​)−Bλ​(Tb​)][e−tcont​−e−tline​].
(53)
The second bracket is positive, and Bλ​(T) increases with temperature. Therefore a cooler upper layer produces absorption, a hotter upper layer produces emission, and an isothermal source-plus-layer produces no line contrast:
Ta​<Tb​: absorption;Ta​>Tb​: emission;Ta​=Tb​: no contrast.​
(54)
This is why higher opacity sampling a cooler altitude creates absorption in an outward-cooling atmosphere, whereas an atmospheric thermal inversion can create emission. If both line and continuum are very optically thick in the same layer, their contrast tends to vanish even with a different deeper temperature. Scattering, non-LTE excitation, and nonuniform layers require a more general radiative transfer treatment.
