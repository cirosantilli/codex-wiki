The weak-star topology σ(X∗,X) is the coarsest topology making f↦f(x) continuous for every x∈X. Its basic neighbourhoods of zero impose finitely many inequalities ∣f(xj​)∣<ϵ.
Suppose first that zero had a countable neighbourhood base (Un​) in the entire dual. Choose a basic neighbourhood Vn​⊂Un​ controlled by a finite set Fn​⊂X, and let En​=span(F1​∪⋯∪Fn​). Given x∈X, the neighbourhood Wx​={f:∣f(x)∣<1} contains some Un​, hence Vn​. If x∈/spanFn​, the Hahn-Banach theorem supplies a bounded linear functional h which vanishes on Fn​ but has h(x)=0. Indeed, the finite-dimensional span is closed, and the functional taking e+tx to t on its sum with Fx is bounded because dist(x,spanFn​)>0. Every scalar multiple of h lies in Vn​, contradicting Vn​⊂Wx​. Thus x∈En​, and X=⋃n​En​ has countable Hamel dimension.
If X is infinite-dimensional, every finite-dimensional En​ is closed and has empty interior. Completeness rules out their union. For clarity, the Baire category theorem needed here has a direct nested-ball proof: inside a starting open ball choose a closed ball missing E1​, then inside its interior choose a closed ball missing E2​, and continue with positive radii tending to zero. The centres are Cauchy; their limit belongs to every ball and to none of the En​, a contradiction. A metric topology would have a countable ball base at zero. Hence
σ(X∗,X) is not metrizable on X∗.​
(11)
This is the weak-star topology on an entire infinite-dimensional Banach dual is not metrizable result. It does not contradict weak-star metrizability of the dual ball for a separable predual: that assertion concerns a bounded subset.
The Banach-Alaoglu theorem states that BX∗​ is compact for σ(X∗,X) for every normed vector space X, whether complete or not. Embed it in the product ∏x∈X​{a∈F:∣a∣≤∥x∥} by its evaluations. Each factor is compact, so Tychonoff theorem makes the product compact Hausdorff. The equations expressing additivity and scalar homogeneity define a closed subset of the product. Its elements are exactly the bounded linear functionals of norm at most one, since the coordinate bounds give ∣f(x)∣≤∥x∥. The induced product topology is precisely weak-star, proving the theorem.
The theorem printed as “Goldstein” is the standard Goldstine theorem: J(BX​) is weak-star dense in BX∗∗​ for the canonical embedding into the bidual. To prove it, fix Φ∈BX∗∗​ and f1​,…,fm​∈X∗. The set S={(f1​(x),…,fm​(x)):x∈BX​} is convex. If b=(Φ(f1​),…,Φ(fm​)) were outside its closure, finite-dimensional Hahn-Banach separation theorem would supply coefficients aj​ such that, for f=∑j​aj​fj​,
ReΦ(f)>supx∈BX​​Ref(x)=∥f∥.
(12)
The real case omits real parts; in the complex case every real-linear separating functional on Cm has the displayed form. The supremum equals the dual norm by multiplying vectors by signs or unimodular scalars. The strict inequality contradicts ∥Φ∥≤1. Thus b∈S, which is exactly approximation on every prescribed finite family of weak-star coordinates.
Now take K=BX∗​ with its compact Hausdorff weak-star topology. For each x∈X, the evaluation x:f↦f(x) is continuous on K, and Hahn-Banach theorem gives ∥x∥∞​=∥x∥. Therefore
X⟶C(K),x⟼x​
(13)
is the isometric evaluation embedding into continuous functions on a compact dual ball, over the same scalar field as X.
For the final sequential assertions, choose a norm-dense sequence (fj​) in the separable dual X∗ and suppose ∥xn​∥≤M. Each bounded scalar sequence fj​(xn​) has a convergent subsequence: divide a bounding interval or square into finitely many smaller closed pieces, repeatedly retain one containing infinitely many terms, and let the diameters tend to zero. Successive extraction for j=1,2,…, followed by a diagonal subsequence (yn​), makes every fj​(yn​) converge.
If M=0 the conclusion is immediate. Otherwise, for any f∈X∗ and ϵ>0, choose j with ∥f−fj​∥<ϵ/(4M). For sufficiently large n,m,
∣f(yn​)−f(ym​)∣≤2M∥f−fj​∥+∣fj​(yn​)−fj​(ym​)∣<ϵ.
(14)
Thus φ(f)=limn​f(yn​) exists for every f. Taking limits proves linearity, and ∣φ(f)∣≤M∥f∥ proves
φ∈X∗∗,∥φ∥≤M.​
(15)
This diagonal argument includes the continuity estimate rather than appealing to a sequential compactness theorem.
If X is not reflexive, choose Φ∈BX∗∗​∖JX, scaling a bidual element outside JX if necessary. The Goldstine theorem proved above supplies xn​∈BX​ with ∣fj​(xn​)−Φ(fj​)∣<1/n for j≤n. Norm density and the bound ∥Jxn​−Φ∥≤2 extend this convergence to every f∈X∗. Hence Jxn​→Φ weak-star. Every subsequence has the same coordinate limits, so a weakly convergent subsequence with limit x∈X would give Jx=Φ, a contradiction. This sequential Goldstine approximation for a separable dual proves
∥xn​∥≤1,(xn​) has no weakly convergent subsequence in X.​
(16)
The construction uses the Goldstine proof already given, not an unproved weak sequential compactness result.
