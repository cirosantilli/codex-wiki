The prescribed-pole form of Runge theorem is this: let K⊂C be compact, and let E⊂C∖K meet every connected component of the complement in the Riemann sphere. If f is holomorphic on a neighbourhood of K, then for every ϵ>0 there is a rational function with all its poles in E whose uniform distance from f on K is less than ϵ. A polynomial is regarded as having its only possible pole at infinity. The empty compact set is trivial, so assume K=∅.
First suppose ∞∈E. Let A⊂C(K) be the uniform rational approximation algebra with prescribed poles, the uniform closure of rational functions whose finite poles lie in E. It is a commutative unital Banach algebra, and contains u(z)=z. Set
S={λ∈C∖K:(u−λ1)−1∈A},
(25)
where the inverse in this definition is the pointwise continuous function on K. The set S is relatively open: if the inverse at λ belongs to A, the Neumann series gives inverses at nearby points. It is relatively closed: when λn​→λ∈/K, the corresponding scalar functions converge uniformly on K, and A is closed.
Each bounded complementary component meets E in a finite point a, and (u−a1)−1 is an allowed rational function. The unbounded component meets S because for ∣λ∣>∥u∥ the geometric series for (u−λ1)−1 belongs to A. Being both open and closed in the complement, S therefore contains every component, so it is the entire complement. Conversely, evaluation at any z∈K prevents u−z1 from being invertible. Hence
σA​(u)=K.​
(26)
Apply the holomorphic functional calculus in A to f near K. For every z∈K, the evaluation character of an algebra and the contour formula from (b) give f(u)(z)=f(z). Thus f∣K​∈A. By the definition of this uniform closure, the required rational approximations exist.
If ∞∈/E, choose a finite point a∈E in the component containing infinity. The Möbius transformation w=(z−a)−1 sends K to a compact subset K′ of the plane and sends E to a set E′ containing infinity. It preserves complementary components, so E′ meets every component of the complement of K′. The transformed function F(w)=f(a+1/w) is holomorphic near K′, since 0∈/K′. The case already proved approximates F by rational functions with poles in E′. Pulling them back gives rational approximations to f on K with poles only in E: a pole at infinity in the w-plane becomes a pole at a, and every other pole has its prescribed preimage. In particular the pullbacks do not acquire a pole at the original infinity, since 0∈/E′.
This proves the full prescribed-pole theorem, including the case where a pole at infinity is forbidden. If C∖K is connected, take E={∞} to obtain the polynomial Runge theorem as a corollary.
