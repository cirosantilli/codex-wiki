A character of an algebra is a nonzero complex-linear multiplicative map φ:A→C. The character space of an algebra ΦA​ carries the Gelfand topology, namely pointwise convergence on A, equivalently the subspace weak-star topology in A∗.
For a unital complex Banach algebra, φ(1)=1: multiplicativity and nonzeroness force this. If a−φ(a)1 were invertible, applying φ to its product with its inverse would give 0=1. Thus φ(a)∈σA​(a). The Neumann series shows σA​(a)⊂{∣z∣≤∥a∥}, so ∣φ(a)∣≤∥a∥ and the character of an algebra is automatically bounded. The same conclusion for a nonunital algebra follows by extending φ to its unitization of an algebra via φ​(a,t)=φ(a)+t and using the standard norm ∥(a,t)∥=∥a∥+∣t∣.
In the algebra R(K) let u(z)=z and fix φ∈ΦR(K)​. Write λ=φ(u). If λ∈/K, the rational function (u−λ)−1 belongs to R(K), which is impossible because a character of an algebra cannot vanish on an invertible element. Thus λ∈K. For a rational function r=p/q without poles in K, choose a reduced denominator having no zeros in K. Multiplicativity gives φ(r)=p(λ)/q(λ)=r(λ). Density of these rational functions and boundedness of φ then yield φ(h)=h(λ) for every h∈R(K).
Conversely, evaluation at each λ∈K is a character of an algebra. The map λ↦δλ​ is continuous in the Gelfand topology because every h∈R(K) is continuous; its inverse is φ↦φ(u), also continuous. Hence the character space of R(K) is
ΦR(K)​≅K,φ=δλ​.​

The identification includes the topology, not merely a bijection of sets.
