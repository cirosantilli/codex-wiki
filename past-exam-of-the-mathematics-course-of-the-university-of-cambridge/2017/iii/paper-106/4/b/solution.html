Work over the complex scalar field. If λ∈σA​(x), the ideal generated by x−λ1 is proper: otherwise commutativity would make x−λ1 invertible. By Zorn lemma extend it to a maximal ideal M. This ideal is closed. Indeed, if its closure were all of A, it would contain an element of M sufficiently close to 1 to be invertible, since the invertible group is open; a proper ideal cannot contain such an element. Its closure is therefore proper, and maximality gives M=M.
The quotient A/M is a complex unital Banach algebra and a division algebra. The Gelfand-Mazur theorem identifies it with C, giving a character of an algebra φ with φ(x)=λ. Conversely, the argument in (a) shows that every φ(x) is in the spectrum. Thus
σA​(x)={φ(x):φ∈ΦA​}.​

Here, as usual, a unital Banach algebra has a nonzero identity; the zero algebra is excluded.
The holomorphic functional calculus assigns to every function holomorphic on an open neighbourhood U of σA​(x) an element f(x)∈A. It is a unital algebra homomorphism, sends the coordinate function to x, agrees with evaluation of rational functions having no poles on the spectrum, depends only on the germ near the spectrum, and is continuous for uniform convergence on a fixed surrounding contour. Its formula is
f(x)=2πi1​∫Γ​f(z)(z1−x)−1dz.​

Here Γ is a finite oriented contour cycle in U∖σA​(x), with winding number one on the spectrum and zero off U. A finite union of suitable polygonal boundary cycles suffices; no connectedness of U is required. Such cycles exist by compactness of the spectrum, and the calculus is independent of the choice. The inverse is the resolvent of an element.
