The Hahn-Banach theorem for bounded linear functionals says that a bounded linear functional on any linear subspace of a normed vector space extends to the whole space with its norm unchanged. No closedness or completeness of the subspace is required. Question 1 uses real-valued ℓ∞​(Γ), so its operator assertions are read over the real scalar field. The analogous complex statements use complex-valued indexed functions.
For x=0, define g(tx)=t∥x∥ on its one-dimensional span. This is a norm-one functional, so Hahn-Banach theorem supplies an extension f∈X∗ with
∥f∥=1,f(x)=∥x∥.​
(1)
The canonical embedding into the bidual is Jx(f)=f(x). It is linear and ∥Jx∥≤∥x∥. The bounded linear functional just constructed gives the reverse inequality for nonzero x, and the zero case is immediate. Thus ∥Jx∥=∥x∥​ and J is injective.
For the coordinate functional representation of an operator into bounded indexed functions, let eγ​ evaluate a coordinate and put fγ​=eγ​T. If T is bounded and linear, then fγ​∈X∗ and ∥fγ​∥≤∥T∥. Conversely, if M=supγ​∥fγ​∥<∞, the formula (Tx)(γ)=fγ​(x) defines a bounded scalar function for each x, is linear, and satisfies ∥Tx∥∞​≤M∥x∥. Combining the two estimates gives
T∈B(X,ℓ∞​(Γ))⟺γsup​∥fγ​∥<∞,∥T∥=γsup​∥fγ​∥.​
(2)
For an empty index set both spaces/families have norm bound zero; the supremum of the empty nonnegative family is taken as zero.
Choose Γ=BX∗​ and Tx=(f(x))f∈BX∗​​. The Hahn-Banach theorem makes ∥Tx∥∞​=∥x∥, so this is a linear isometric embedding into bounded scalar functions on an index set.
If X is nonzero and separable, choose a dense sequence un​ in its unit sphere and supporting functionals fn​ with ∥fn​∥=fn​(un​)=1. For a unit vector u and ϵ>0, some un​ satisfies ∥u−un​∥<ϵ, whence ∣fn​(u)∣≥1−ϵ. Thus (fn​) is a countable norming family and x↦(fn​(x)) is an isometry into the l-infinity sequence space. Completeness of X is not needed.
If instead X=E∗ for a separable normed vector space E, choose a dense sequence vn​ in BE​. The evaluations fn​(x)=x(vn​) lie in BX∗​ and continuity of x∈E∗ gives supn​∣x(vn​)∣=∥x∥. This again gives Γ=N, even when E∗ is not separable. If X={0}, use zero coordinates throughout.
To prove 1-injectivity, take T:Y→ℓ∞​(Γ) on a subspace of Z. Extend every coordinate fγ​∈Y∗ to f​γ​∈Z∗ by Hahn-Banach theorem, keeping its norm. Their uniform bound defines Tz=(f​γ​(z)), and the coordinate norm identity yields
T∣Y​=T,∥T∥=∥T∥.​
(3)
Selection of these extensions uses the usual choice convention for arbitrary index sets. This verifies the lambda-injective normed space definition with λ=1.
For the final retraction characterization of lambda-injectivity, suppose first that X is lambda-injective and i:X→Z is a linear isometry. The inverse i(X)→X has norm one when X=0; extend it to P:Z→X with ∥P∥≤λ. Then Pi=IX​, and iP is a bounded projection onto i(X). For the zero space take P=0.
Conversely, suppose every linear isometry out of X has such a left inverse. Fix an isometry j:X→ℓ∞​(Γ) as above and a left inverse P with ∥P∥≤λ. For any T:Y→X, extend jT to S:Z→ℓ∞​(Γ) by 1-injectivity. Then T=PS extends T, and ∥T∥≤λ∥S∥=λ∥T∥. Therefore X is lambda-injective exactly when every isometric embedding i:X→Z admits a bounded map P:Z→X satisfying
Pi=IX​,∥P∥≤λ.​
(4)
