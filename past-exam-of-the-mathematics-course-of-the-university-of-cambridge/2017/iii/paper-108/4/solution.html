For a probability measure-preserving system, the hypothesis is completely positive entropy: every finite measurable partition with positive static entropy of a finite measurable partition has positive entropy rate of a measurable partition. Fix a finite partition ξ and put
FN​=σ(⋁j≥N​T−jξ),T(ξ)=⋂N≥0​FN​.
(21)
All sigma-algebras are interpreted modulo null sets. We will prove that every finite partition α measurable with respect to this tail sigma-algebra of a measurable partition has hμ​(T,α)=0. Applying this to a binary partition will force the required triviality. This proves the needed direction of the Tail characterization of the Pinsker sigma-algebra directly, including noninvertible transformations.
Write h=hμ​(T,ξ). The infinite-future entropy formula and the backwards chain rule for information entropy give, for every L≥1, the block conditional entropy given the infinite future identity
Hμ​(ξ0L−1​∣FL​)=j=0∑L−1​Hμ​(T−jξ∣Fj+1​)=Lh​.
(22)
Each term equals h by invariance of the joint probabilities under a common pullback and continuity of conditional entropy under increasing finite future blocks. Invertibility is not needed for this identity.
Fix ε>0. Since α is F0​-measurable and finite, the martingale convergence theorem and continuity of finite-partition conditional entropy allow an r≥0 with
Hμ​(α∣ξ0r​)<ε.
(23)
To see the continuity explicitly, for each partition atom A of α the conditional probabilities E[1A​∣σ(ξ0r​)] tend to 1A​ almost everywhere; apply dominated convergence theorem to the bounded continuous function −tlogt on [0,1] and sum over the finitely many partition atoms.
For n≥1 set γ=α0n−1​, L=n+r, and β=ξ0L−1​. The conditional entropy of finite measurable partitions satisfies
H(γ∣β)≤∑j=0n−1​H(T−jα∣β)≤∑j=0n−1​H(T−jα∣T−jξ0r​)=nH(α∣ξ0r​)<nε.
(24)
The second inequality uses conditioning reduces entropy, since β refines each block T−jξ0r​; the final equality uses measure preservation.
Moreover γ is FL​-measurable. For each j≥0, tail measurability gives α measurable with respect to FL​, hence T−jα measurable with respect to T−jFL​=FL+j​⊆FL​. Thus conditioning reduces entropy and the displayed block identity imply
H(β∣γ)≥H(β∣FL​)=Lh.
(25)
Use the symmetric entropy identity H(γ)=H(β)−H(β∣γ)+H(γ∣β) to obtain
0≤n1​H(α0n−1​)≤nH(ξ0n+r−1​)−(n+r)h​+ε.
(26)
The fraction on the right tends to zero: r is fixed and H(ξ0n+r−1​)/(n+r)→h by the definition of entropy rate of a measurable partition. Taking n→∞ and then ε↓0 gives
hμ​(T,α)=0for every finite T(ξ)-measurable partition α​.
(27)
Now let A∈T(ξ) and take α={A,X∖A}. If 0<μ(A)<1, its binary entropy is −μ(A)logμ(A)−(1−μ(A))log(1−μ(A))>0, while its entropy rate is zero, contradicting completely positive entropy. Therefore
μ(A)∈{0,1}for every A∈T(ξ)​.
(28)
Since ξ was arbitrary, every finite-partition tail is trivial. The argument needs only that ξ is finite and the measure is a probability; it does not assume a finite generator, finite total system entropy, or invertibility.
