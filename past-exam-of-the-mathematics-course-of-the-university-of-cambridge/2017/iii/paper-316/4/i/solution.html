Let r0​ be the separation of the two centres at release. The two-body problem has relative specific orbital energy ε=v2/2−G(Mp​+M)/r. Marginal escape has ε=0, so
vesc​(r0​)=r0​2G(Mp​+M)​​≃r0​2GMp​​​​.
(50)
This follows equally by equating the relative kinetic energy 21​mred​v2 to the magnitude of the Newtonian gravitational potential energy GMp​M/r0​, where mred​=MMp​/(M+Mp​).
For a pointlike comet released at the planet's surface, r0​=Rp​, giving the usual vesc​=2GMp​/Rp​​​. If the finite comet touches that surface, its centre is at r0​=Rp​+R; the same formula uses this larger separation. The assumption Mp​≫M by itself does not imply R≪Rp​. Subsequent formulas written in terms of the planet-only escape velocity use the additional small-comet approximation.
This is local escape from an isolated planet. In a stellar Newtonian gravitational field, reaching the edge of the Hill sphere and escaping the stellar system are different energy thresholds.
