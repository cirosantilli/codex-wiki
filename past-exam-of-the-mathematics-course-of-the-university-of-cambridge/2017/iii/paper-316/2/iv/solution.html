Let μp​=GMp​, A=βGM⋆​/c and κ=A/ap2​. A tightly bound circumplanetary orbit lies well inside the Hill sphere, so a/ap​≪1 and its orbital period is short compared with the planet's year. Treat the stellar flux and stellar direction R as constant during one dust orbit. With stellar-frame velocity Vp​+u, the velocity-dependent acceleration is
aPR​=−κ[Vp​+u+((Vp​+u)⋅R)R].
(28)
The terms independent of u, including the leading static radiation pressure, do no net work on an unperturbed closed circular orbit. Hence
⟨ε˙p​⟩=−κ⟨u2+(u⋅R)2⟩.
(29)
For a coplanar circular orbit, u2=μp​/a and the component along the stellar direction has mean square u2/2. Thus a˙=2a2ε˙p​/μp​ yields the Poynting–Robertson decay of a circumplanetary orbit
⟨a˙⟩cp​=−3cβμ​ap2​a​​.
(30)
The coefficient three assumes coplanarity, which the PDF does not state. For orbital normal k, the general circular covariance is ⟨ui​uj​⟩=(u2/2)(δij​−ki​kj​), giving
⟨a˙⟩cp​=−κa[3−(k⋅R)2]​.
(31)
An orbital plane normal to the stellar radial direction has coefficient two during that orbit, a counterexample to an orientation-independent coefficient three. Averaging also over the planet's circular stellar orbit, with fixed dust-plane orbital inclination I to it, gives coefficient 3−21​sin2I. The conservative forces must be weak enough for the assumed approximately circular, planet-bound orbit to persist; the small planet-to-star mass ratio alone does not ensure this for arbitrary grain β.
