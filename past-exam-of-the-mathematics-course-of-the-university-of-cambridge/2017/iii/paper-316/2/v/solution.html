Keep A=βGM⋆​/c constant. A circular circumstellar orbit stays circular in the secular drag approximation, and da/dt=−2A/a integrates to a2=ap2​−4At. The inspiral time under Poynting–Robertson drag to the stellar surface is
tcs​=4Aap2​−R⋆2​​≃4Aap2​​​.
(32)
The final expression treats the star as a point, or assumes R⋆​≪ap​.
For the coplanar circumplanetary orbit used in the preceding result, da/dt=−3Aa/ap2​, so a=a0​exp(−3At/ap2​). The inspiral time of circumplanetary dust to the planet's surface, for a0​>Rp​, is
tcp​=3Aap2​​lnRp​a0​​​,tcs​tcp​​=3[1−(R⋆​/ap​)2]4ln(a0​/Rp​)​​.
(33)
Thus the timescales have the same dependence on stellar flux and β, but planetary arrival contains a logarithm of the initial planetocentric radius. It is not always shorter: for a point star, tcp​<tcs​ only if a0​/Rp​<e3/4. A constant tilted-orbit average replaces three by its appropriate orientation coefficient. Sublimation or other removal can terminate the evolution before either idealized arrival time.
