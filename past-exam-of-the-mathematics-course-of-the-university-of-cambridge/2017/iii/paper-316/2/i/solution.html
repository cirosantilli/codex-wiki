Treat a,e as instantaneous osculating orbital elements of the fixed-central-parameter Kepler orbit. The perturbing work changes the specific orbital energy according to
ε˙=v⋅(Rˉr+Tˉθ)=Rˉr˙+Tˉrh​,a˙=μ2a2​ε˙.
(19)
The specific angular momentum and conic equation give
r˙=hμ​esinf,rh​=hμ​(1+ecosf),h=μa(1−e2)​.
(20)
Substituting proves the Gauss planetary equation for the semi-major axis:
a˙=2μ(1−e2)a3​​[Rˉesinf+Tˉ(1+ecosf)]​.
(21)
Here f denotes true anomaly, not the fractional luminosity used in Question 1. The equation follows from work alone; a normal perturbing acceleration has no instantaneous contribution because the velocity lies in the orbital plane. For slowly averaged evolution, the perturbation must be weak over one orbital period.
