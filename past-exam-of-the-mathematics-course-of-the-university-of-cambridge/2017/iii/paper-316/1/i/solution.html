Use the separation vector r from the star to the comet, and let mred​=mM⋆​/(m+M⋆​). The two-body problem separates into uniform centre of mass motion and
r¨=−r3μ​r,μ=G(M⋆​+m).
(1)
Taking a cross product with r proves conservation of angular momentum; taking the scalar product with r˙ proves conservation of energy. The relative constants are
h=r×r˙,ε=2v2​−rμ​.
(2)
The total relative angular momentum and energy are mred​h and mred​ε, respectively. The conserved eccentricity vector is e=r˙×h/μ−r. Its scalar product with r gives r=h2/[μ(1+ecosf)], with f the true anomaly. The periapsis and apoapsis distances are therefore h2/[μ(1+e)] and h2/[μ(1−e)]. Their sum is twice the semi-major axis, so
h=μa(1−e2)​​.
(3)
At either apsis v=h/r; substituting an apsidal radius gives ε=−μ/(2a). Thus the specific orbital energy implies the vis-viva equation
v=μ(r2​−a1​)​​.
(4)
These are exact relative-coordinate formulas for an elliptic orbit, 0≤e<1. In the barycentric frame the comet's speed is M⋆​/(M⋆​+m) times the relative speed; the printed identification with the comet's stellar orbital speed uses M⋆​≫m.
