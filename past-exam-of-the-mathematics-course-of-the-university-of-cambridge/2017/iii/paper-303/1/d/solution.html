For A2​=0, rescale the order parameter by m=(∣A2​∣/A6​)1/4ψ. Write σ=sgnA2​ and introduce dimensionless scaling variables
X=∣A2​∣1/2A61/2​A4​​,Y=∣A2​∣5/4hA61/4​​.
(11)
Every term then has the common energy-density factor ∣A2​∣3/2/A61/2​:
A=A61/2​∣A2​∣3/2​(2σ​ψ2+4X​ψ4+61​ψ6−Yψ).
(12)
Minimizing over ψ defines the tricritical crossover scaling functions
Φσ​(X,Y)=minψ∈R​(2σ​ψ2+4X​ψ4+61​ψ6−Yψ).
(13)
For the minimized potential, following the paper's notation F, this proves
(u,v,w,x,y,z)=(23​,21​,21​,21​,41​,45​).​
(14)
The exponent names x,y here are unrelated to the couplings in question 2. The expression concerns the specified order-parameter polynomial; a general material can also have a smooth, field-independent background free-energy density. Such a background should be separated before writing a homogeneous singular scaling form. A potential at prescribed magnetization, instead of prescribed field, is a different Legendre transform and is not obtained by this minimization.
There is a needed qualification to the printed single Φ and nonzero-value condition. At (X,Y)=(0,0), the ordered branch has minimizers ψ=±1 and Φ−​(0,0)=−1/3, whereas the disordered branch has minimizer zero and Φ+​(0,0)=0. Thus
Φ−​(0,0)=−31​,Φ+​(0,0)=0.​
(15)
For example, A2​>0, A4​=h=0 gives an identically zero minimized polynomial, contradicting a nonzero Φ(0,0) on that side. The valid scaling statement uses separate sign branches, or restricts the asserted nonzero value to approach from the ordered side. At A2​=0 the displayed coordinates are singular; the critical isotherm is obtained as a limit or directly from the equation of state.
