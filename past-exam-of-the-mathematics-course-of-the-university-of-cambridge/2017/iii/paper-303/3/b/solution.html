Compute the coupling flow at zero magnetic field, or assume the source has support only in retained Fourier modes, so that the shell Gaussian measure is centered. First justify the shell propagator. Changing p to −p gives the negative-exponent convention as well. The dependence is on the separation, as required by translation invariance. The printed numerator uses x alone: it is correct only if that symbol means the separation or if y=0. For example at x=y=0, the covariance must equal the constant coincident value G>​(0), while the literal printed integral generally depends on x. Thus the missing separation is a real source qualification, not a change of Fourier-sign convention.

A negative slow-mode mass may be stabilized by the retained quartic term and does not require pretending that the full unconstrained quadratic measure at negative mass is normalizable.
Linearizing about (r0​,u0​)=(0,0) gives
(r0′​u0′​​)=(b20​21​b2I1​(0)b4−D​)(r0​u0​​)+O(r0​u0​,u02​).
(40)
Its mass and quartic eigenvalues are b2 and b4−D. The off-diagonal term is an additive critical-mass shift: setting the bare r0​=0 is not generally the critical tuning when u0​>0. The mass remains relevant for every D; at D=2 the two linear eigenvalues coincide and the matrix can have a Jordan block, but both perturbations are still relevant.
For D>4, the quartic interaction is an irrelevant operator, and the Gaussian fixed point attracts weak quartic perturbations after the mass and magnetic field have been tuned. The quartic can nevertheless be a dangerously irrelevant coupling: its positive value stabilizes the ordered phase. For D<4, it is a relevant operator, so the Gaussian description is unstable toward interactions. At D=4 it is a marginal operator; first-order perturbation theory alone does not decide its fate.
To settle that borderline case, retain the leading quartic contribution from the second cumulant, at zero external momentum in the local expansion. The two-fast-field part of V is (u0​/4)∫ϕ<2​ϕ>2​. Since ⟨ϕ>2​(x)ϕ>2​(y)⟩c​=2G>​(x−y)2, its connected second cumulant contributes

This convention has the opposite scale direction to a renormalization-group beta function defined using increasing momentum. On the tuned critical surface at D=4, R=O(g) and dg/dℓ=−3g2/2+O(g3), so weak positive g is marginally irrelevant, tending to zero with logarithmic corrections rather than remaining an exactly marginal parameter. Just below four dimensions, D=4−ε with 0<ε≪1, the same calculation yields the Wilson-Fisher fixed point g∗​=2ε/3+O(ε2), R∗​=−ε/6+O(ε2).
