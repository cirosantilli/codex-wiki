i OurBigBook.com
The Nakai–Moishezon criterion says that a Cartier divisor D on a projective scheme is ample exactly when
DdimV⋅[V]>0for every positive-dimensional integral closed subvariety V.​
(1)
In particular the test includes each positive-dimensional irreducible component. Here and below, “proper” means proper over the ground field, not necessarily a strict subset of X. Interpreting it as a strict subset would make the later criteria false even for an integral projective curve, whose strict closed subvarieties have dimension zero.
The intersection product of Cartier divisors with cycles depends only on their numerical equivalence of divisors classes. One way to see this is to intersect all but one factor first, obtaining a one-cycle; replacing the remaining factor by a numerically equivalent divisor does not change its pairing with that cycle. Multilinearity then handles replacement of every factor. Consequently D≡D′ makes all the displayed numbers equal. Thus
D≡D′⟹(D ample⟺D′ ample).​
(2)
The criterion applies to the integral reduced subvarieties of a possibly nonreduced scheme; ampleness on reduced components explains why the nilpotent structure does not change this condition.

Put L=OX​(D) and let sD​ be the canonical global section cutting out the effective Cartier divisor D. The divisor restriction exact sequence gives
0⟶Lm−1sD​​Lm⟶Lm∣D​⟶0.
(3)
The hypothesis says L∣D​ is an ample line bundle. By Serre vanishing, H1(D,Lm∣D​)=0 for all sufficiently large m, so the long exact sequence in sheaf cohomology makes
H1(X,Lm−1)⟶H1(X,Lm)
(4)
surjective for all such m. These are finite-dimensional vector spaces. Their dimensions form a nonincreasing sequence of nonnegative integers, so the maps are isomorphisms from some point on. Exactness then implies that the restriction
H0(X,Lm)⟶H0(D,Lm∣D​)
(5)
is surjective for all sufficiently large m.
Choose such an m for which Lm∣D​ is also a globally generated line bundle. Lift a generating collection of its global sections to X. At each point of D, one lift has nonzero image in the one-dimensional residue-field fibre, hence generates the stalk of Lm by Nakayama lemma. Outside D, sDm​ is nowhere zero and generates Lm. Together these sections generate it everywhere. Therefore
OX​(mD) is globally generated for m≫0;D is semiample.​
(6)
This proof works on an arbitrary projective scheme because the defining section of an effective Cartier divisor is a non-zero-divisor. If D is empty, L≅OX​ and the conclusion is immediate. Semiampleness is the conclusion: the pullback of a line avoiding the centre of a blowup of a smooth algebraic surface of P2 satisfies the hypothesis on its support but has zero intersection with the exceptional curve, and therefore is not ample.

Assume D is ample. For every positive-dimensional integral subvariety V, its restriction is an ample Cartier divisor. The asymptotic Riemann–Roch polynomial has leading term
χ(V,OV​(mD))=d!Dd⋅[V]​md+O(md−1),d=dimV.
(7)
By the Nakai–Moishezon criterion, the coefficient is positive, so this proves implication (a)⇒(b).
For (a)⇒(c), choose m>0 such that OV​(mD) is very ample. Fix a closed point p∈V. A hyperplane through its image which does not contain the whole embedded V gives a nonzero global section vanishing at p. Such a hyperplane exists since dimV>0. Its vanishing is therefore nonempty but not all of V. This proves both required forward implications:
(a)⟹(b) and (c).​
(8)
The proofs in the next two sections establish the converse implications. Reduction to integral components is legitimate by ampleness on reduced components; in dimension zero every line bundle on a projective scheme is ample and there is no positive-dimensional subvariety to test.

We prove (b)⇒(a) by induction on dimension, using the independently proved (c)⇒(a) argument in the next section. Work on an integral projective variety V of dimension d>0. The hypothesis is inherited by all its integral closed subvarieties. Induction therefore makes D ample on every strictly lower-dimensional closed reduced subvariety, and hence on every proper closed subscheme of dimension less than d, by ampleness on reduced components.
Choose an effective Cartier divisor H which is a very ample hyperplane section of V. Then OH​(D) is ample. We need a vanishing statement uniform in extra positive H-twists:
Hj(H,OH​(mD+tH))=0(j>0, m≥M, t≥0).
(9)
Here is a justification using Castelnuovo–Mumford regularity. Embed H by OH​(H). For each of the finitely many positive cohomology degrees j, Serre vanishing for the ample bundle OH​(D) makes Hj(H,OH​(mD−jH)) vanish for m≥M. Thus the pushed-forward sheaf OH​(mD) is zero-regular. Persistence of regularity makes it t+j-regular for every t≥0, giving exactly the displayed vanishing. This is the uniform Serre vanishing for two ample twists lemma. It does not assume that D is ample on V.
Apply the divisor restriction exact sequence to H with twists mD+tH. For i≥2, both neighbouring cohomology groups on H vanish, so
Hi(V,OV​(mD+tH))≅Hi(V,OV​(mD+(t+1)H))(t≥0).
(10)
For a fixed m≥M, sufficiently large t kills the right-hand cohomology by Serre vanishing for H on V. The higher cohomology vanishing from an ample hyperplane restriction argument gives Hi(V,OV​(mD))=0 for all i≥2. Consequently
h0(V,mD)=χ(V,mD)+h1(V,mD)≥χ(V,mD)⟶∞.
(11)
This step is essential: divergence of a polynomial does not by itself prove that its top-degree coefficient is positive.
For m large enough, h0(V,mD)>1. Evaluation at a closed point has a one-dimensional target, so its kernel contains a nonzero section vanishing there. We have obtained condition (c) on V. Lower-dimensional subvarieties already have the same property by induction, so the next section's implication (c)⇒(a) applies. Thus
(b)⟹(a)​.
(12)
For d=1, higher groups with i≥2 vanish automatically and the same evaluation argument starts the induction. The regularity facts used above are stated in the Stacks Project, regularity lemmas.

We prove (c)⇒(a) by induction on dimension. It suffices to work on an integral projective variety V. By induction, D is ample on every lower-dimensional integral subvariety, and hence on every lower-dimensional closed subscheme by ampleness on reduced components.
Apply (c) to V itself. The nonzero section of OV​(mD) has a nonempty zero divisor E. Because V is integral, this is an effective Cartier divisor and OV​(E)≅OV​(mD). Its support has dimension less than dimV, so D∣E​, and therefore OE​(E), is ample. Part (ii) makes E semiample, hence some positive multiple of D is basepoint-free.
Let f:V→Y⊂PN be the resulting Kodaira map, with OV​(qD)=f∗OY​(1). No fibre can have positive dimension: such a projective fibre contains an integral projective curve C, on which D has degree zero. But the assumed nonzero section of some OC​(rD) cannot vanish anywhere, since its nonempty effective divisor would have positive degree. This contradicts (c).
Thus f has zero-dimensional fibres. A proper quasi-finite morphism is a finite morphism. The finite pullback of an ample line bundle is ample, so qD and then D are ample. This proves
(c)⟹(a);(a), (b), (c) are equivalent.​
(13)
The fibre argument proves the semiample and curve-positive ampleness criterion. It also explains why testing only existence of a nonzero section, without requiring a zero, would be insufficient: the trivial bundle on a positive-dimensional projective variety has a nowhere-vanishing section.

On a smooth algebraic surface, distinct integral curves have nonnegative intersection numbers, because their intersection multiplicities are nonnegative. For any member E∈∣D∣ of the complete linear system of a divisor, E⋅C=D⋅C<0. Hence C must be a component of E; otherwise the intersection would be nonnegative.
Subtracting one copy of C from every effective member gives precisely the effective members linearly equivalent to D−C. Conversely adding C gives a member of ∣D∣. Thus C is a fixed component and
∣D∣=∣D−C∣+C.​
(14)
Equivalently, multiplication by its defining section induces an isomorphism H0(X,OX​(D−C))∼​H0(X,OX​(D)). The original PDF has this linear-system equality; the TeX loses the bars and reduces it to an uninformative divisor identity.

Part (i) first shows that D−C is effective. Also C2<0: writing D=aC+E with a≥1 and E effective without C as a component gives D⋅C=aC2+E⋅C, where E⋅C≥0.
For 0≤j<m,
(mD−jC)⋅C=m(D−C)⋅C+(m−j)C2<0.
(15)
Every effective member of ∣mD∣ therefore contains C; after subtracting it, the same reasoning applies again, until m copies have been subtracted. Conversely adding mC to an effective member of ∣m(D−C)∣ is allowed. Hence
∣mD∣=∣m(D−C)∣+mC(m≥0).​
(16)
For m=0 this is just the identity system ∣0∣. The extra intersection hypothesis is needed: on the Hirzebruch surface F2​, with negative section S and fibre F, take D=S+F. Then D⋅S=−1 but (D−S)⋅S=1. Here h0(2D)=4 while h0(2F)=3, so removing 2S loses a section.
For the point blowup of a smooth algebraic surface ψ:X→X with exceptional curve E, the canonical divisor formula for a surface blowup and the intersection formula for blowing up a surface give
KX​=ψ∗KX​+E,E2=−1,ψ∗KX​⋅E=0.
(17)
One must not assume KX​ itself is effective. Instead, if an effective member of ∣mKX​∣ exists, then for each 0≤j<m its class after removing jE has intersection −m+j<0 with E. The same fixed-component argument removes exactly the required mE, giving
H0(X,O(mψ∗KX​)) ⋅sEm​ ​H0(X,O(mKX​))
(18)
as an isomorphism, also when both sides are zero. Since X is normal and ψ is proper and birational, ψ∗​OX​=OX​. The projection formula for sheaves therefore identifies the space on the left with H0(X,OX​(mKX​)). Consequently
H0(X,mKX​)≅H0(X,mKX​)(m≥0).​
(19)
The PDF writes equality of spaces; this is the canonical identification just described, rather than literal equality of spaces on different schemes. This proves blowup invariance of plurigenera in arbitrary characteristic.

Because C≅P1 and degOC​(C)=C2=0, its normal line bundle is OC​(C)≅OP1​. Thus every OC​(mC) is trivial and has H1=0. The divisor restriction exact sequence gives, for every m≥1,
0→OX​((m−1)C)→OX​(mC)→OC​→0.
(20)
The maps H1(X,(m−1)C)→H1(X,mC) are surjective. Their nonnegative finite dimensions eventually stabilize, so for every sufficiently large m restriction H0(X,mC)→H0(C,OC​)=k is surjective. A lift of 1 is nowhere zero along C, and the canonical section of mC is nowhere zero outside C. Together they generate OX​(mC). Therefore C is semiample.
For those same large m, the exact sequence of global sections gives
h0(X,mC)−h0(X,(m−1)C)=1.
(21)
Thus h0(X,mC)=m+O(1), and the Iitaka dimension is exactly one:
C is semiample,κ(C)=1.​
(22)
Equivalently, its basepoint-free multiple defines a morphism to a curve: it is nonconstant because its sections grow, and cannot have two-dimensional image because (mC)2=0. This is the semiampleness of a square-zero rational curve; it uses no characteristic-zero vanishing theorem.

By Kodaira's lemma for the big divisor KX​, choose an integer q>0, an ample Cartier divisor A, and an effective divisor E with
qKX​∼A+E.
(23)
If an integral curve C is not a component of E, nonnegative intersections of distinct curves give
qKX​⋅C=A⋅C+E⋅C>0.
(24)
Thus every curve with KX​⋅C<0 is one of the finitely many components of E. In particular,
Neg(KX​)⊆Suppcomponents​(E),Neg(KX​) is finite.​
(25)
This is the surface case of negative curves of a big real divisor lie in finitely many divisors. The proof does not assert that every negative curve on X belongs to this finite set; it concerns curves negative against this particular big canonical class.

Let C∈Neg(KX​). The arithmetic adjunction formula on a smooth surface gives
2pa​(C)−2=C2+KX​⋅C,pa​(C)≥0.
(26)
We first exclude C2≥0, following the PDF's hint and the finiteness proved in (a).
If C2=0, adjunction and KX​⋅C<0 force pa​(C)=0 and KX​⋅C=−2. An integral projective curve of arithmetic genus zero is a smooth rational curve: normalization and the nonnegative singularity-length correction show both normalization genus and singularity correction vanish. Part (iii) makes C semiample. Choose a basepoint-free ∣mC∣. Over the infinite algebraically closed field, a general section avoids containing any of the finitely many curves of Neg(KX​) as a component. Its effective divisor G then has KX​⋅G≥0, but KX​⋅G=mKX​⋅C<0, a contradiction.
If C2>0, Riemann–Roch theorem for algebraic surfaces and Serre duality show h0(X,mC) is unbounded. Indeed h2(X,mC)=h0(X,KX​−mC)=0 for m≫0, because the latter divisor has negative intersection with a fixed ample divisor. Hence
h0(X,mC)≥χ(X,OX​)+21​(m2C2−mKX​⋅C)⟶∞.
(27)
There is therefore some m with h0(X,mC)>h0(X,(m−1)C), giving a section whose divisor does not contain C. The canonical section of mC does not vanish identically along any other curve. A general linear combination of these two sections consequently contains none of the finite set Neg(KX​), again contradicting its negative canonical intersection. Thus C2<0.
Now put a=−C2≥1 and b=−KX​⋅C≥1. Adjunction gives
−a−b=2pa​(C)−2≥−2.
(28)
It follows that a=b=1 and pa​(C)=0. Hence
C≅P1,C2=−1,KX​⋅C=−1.​
(29)
The general-section argument only avoids finitely many proper linear subspaces, so it works over any algebraically closed field, including positive characteristic.

The Castelnuovo contraction criterion says that a smooth rational curve C on a smooth projective surface with C2=−1 can be contracted by a birational morphism to a smooth point of a smooth projective surface. The contraction is an isomorphism away from C, and its inverse is the blowup of a smooth algebraic surface at that point. This is the algebraic contraction criterion, valid over the algebraically closed field here; it is not the rationality criterion bearing Castelnuovo's name.
By (b), every C∈Neg(KX​) satisfies the hypothesis. Therefore the required morphism exists:
f1​:X→X1​,f1​(C)={p},X1​ smooth and projective,X∖C≅X1​∖{p}.​
(30)
The PDF calls f1​ a birational map; the conclusion is stronger, since this map is everywhere defined and is a birational morphism.

An ample real divisor is a finite positive real combination of ample Cartier divisors:
D=∑j=1r​aj​Aj​,aj​>0.
(31)
Equivalently its numerical class lies in the ample cone. This is a numerical condition even when the coefficients are irrational; it does not mean that some integer multiple of D must be an integral divisor.
On an integral projective variety, a big real divisor is a finite positive real combination of big Cartier divisors. Equivalently, by the real form of Kodaira's lemma,
D∼R​B+E,B ample real,E effective real Cartier.​
(32)
For an integral Cartier divisor, bigness means maximal section-growth order h0(X,mD)≥cmdimX along sufficiently divisible positive m, or Iitaka dimension dimX. The real linear equivalence of divisors formulation permits finite positive combinations of effective Cartier divisors, whose supports are codimension one. The definitions and the ample-plus-effective formulation on integral varieties are discussed in Fujino's notes on big real divisors.
For the paper's assertions on a general projective scheme, use componentwise bigness on a projective scheme: require bigness on every reduced irreducible component. All arguments below can then be carried out on those finitely many integral components; ampleness is also detected there. On a reducible scheme, merely asking for maximal total section growth on one component is insufficient. For example X=P2⨿P2, with D restricting to O(1) on the first component and O(−1) on the second, has quadratic total section growth, but negative intersection with every line in the second component. No finite collection of codimension-one subvarieties can contain all those lines. Thus that weaker meaning would make part (ii) false. In dimension zero the positivity statements are vacuous and every line bundle is ample; the compatible bigness convention also regards it as big.

First work on an integral component. Write the big real divisor as D∼R​B+E with B ample real divisor and E effective real Cartier, using Kodaira's lemma. Let E1​,…,Ek​ be the finitely many integral components of its support. For an integral projective curve C not contained in this support, restriction of each effective Cartier summand to C is effective, so
D⋅C=B⋅C+E⋅C>0.
(33)
Therefore
D⋅C<0⟹C⊆Ei​ for some i.​
(34)
This proves negative curves of a big real divisor lie in finitely many divisors.
Now let A be the given ample divisor. Openness of the ample cone gives a δ>0 such that B−ϵA is ample for 0<ϵ≤δ. For each of the finitely many Ei​, the assumption that D∣Ei​​ is ample similarly gives a δi​>0 such that (D−ϵA)∣Ei​​ is ample for 0<ϵ≤δi​. Choose a single positive ϵ smaller than all these bounds.
If C is contained in some Ei​, its intersection with D−ϵA is positive by that restriction. Otherwise
(D−ϵA)⋅C=(B−ϵA)⋅C+E⋅C>0.
(35)
In particular D−ϵA is nef:
D−ϵA is nef for all sufficiently small ϵ>0.​
(36)
Only the finitely many exceptional support components are needed for the restriction test; no uniform bound over all subvarieties was assumed.
For a reducible projective scheme, use componentwise bigness on a projective scheme and repeat this argument on each reduced irreducible component. Collect their exceptional supports and take the minimum of all the finitely many positive bounds. Codimension one here is measured in the relevant irreducible component. Every integral curve lies in a component, so the same conclusion holds on X. Nilpotent structure does not affect these curve intersection numbers.

Choose an ample Cartier divisor H. Put initially A=δH, A′=tH for small positive real δ,t. Since D is nef, the nef-plus-ample ampleness lemma makes D+tH ample. The polynomial
P(δ,t)=(D+tH)n−n(D+tH)n−1⋅((δ+t)H)
(38)
has P(0,0)=Dn⋅[X]>0. Thus the desired strict inequality holds when δ,t are sufficiently small and positive.
We must also arrange rationality of the two specified classes; D itself need not be rational. Choose rational ample classes B and C sufficiently near D+tH and (δ+t)H, and define
A′=B−D,A=C−A′=C−B+D.
(39)
Then A′ is close to tH and A is close to δH, so both are ample real divisors by openness of the ample cone. Also D+A′=B and A+A′=C are rational and ample. Continuity preserves the strict inequality, giving
Bn>nBn−1⋅C,D−A=B−C.​
(40)
Here is the needed algebraic Morse inequality for ample divisors, with its section-count proof. Choose rational Cartier divisor representatives of B,C and a common positive integer q making qB,qC very ample integral Cartier divisors. For the section-count argument rename these scaled representatives B,C; undoing this scaling restricts section indices to sufficiently divisible multiples and leaves bigness unchanged. Choose an effective Cartier divisor G∈∣C∣ by taking a defining section that avoids the associated points of OX​. Repeated divisor restriction exact sequences give
h0(X,m(B−C))≥h0(X,mB)−∑j=0m−1​h0(G,OG​(mB−jC)).
(41)
Because C is very ample, for each j a section of jC avoiding the finitely many associated points of OG​ gives an injection into OG​(mB). Thus every summand is at most h0(G,OG​(mB)). By Serre vanishing and asymptotic Riemann–Roch for the ample B,
h0(X,m(B−C))≥n!Bn−nBn−1⋅C​mn+O(mn−1).
(42)
For n=1, the restriction term is the constant length of G, giving the same formula directly. The positive coefficient proves that B−C, hence D−A, is big. Scaling back preserves bigness, so
D−A is big.​
(43)
For a general projective scheme, enforce the same strict inequality separately on each positive-dimensional reduced irreducible component Vj​, using its own dimension nj​. At (δ,t)=(0,0) every such expression equals the positive number Dnj​⋅[Vj​]. Finitely many conditions are preserved by one sufficiently small choice and one sufficiently close rational approximation on N1(X)R​. The top-dimensional inequalities imply the printed inequality for [X] with its positive generic multiplicities; the section proof on each component makes D−A componentwise big. This avoids inferring bigness on every component from just a positive sum. The displayed inequality is used for n≥1. For n=0, its literal n−1 intersection power is undefined; handle this vacuous positivity case separately. Every line bundle is ample, all numerical classes are zero, and the componentwise bigness convention makes the conclusions automatic.

Choose 0<ϵ≪1 as in (c). Then
D=(D−ϵA)+ϵA
(45)
is the sum of a nef divisor and an ample real divisor. The nef-plus-ample ampleness lemma gives

For clarity, this last lemma follows from Kleiman's criterion and the convex cone property: if x is an interior point of the nef cone and y lies in that cone, translating a small neighbourhood of x by y stays in the cone. Thus x+y remains in its interior, which is the ample cone on a projective scheme.
The complete argument proves the real Nakai–Moishezon criterion rather than assuming it: curve positivity gives nefness, rational approximation and a proved section-count inequality give bigness, induction and the finite-support argument give a uniform ample subtraction, and the nef-plus-ample lemma concludes ampleness. The zero-dimensional case is automatic, and ampleness on reduced components handles reducibility and nilpotents.
