For this stellar population, let m=M/M⊙​ denote initial mass in units of the solar mass, and let τ=t/Gyr denote present stellar age. Constant formation of equal numbers of stars per unit time makes the stellar age a uniform distribution on [0,10] Gyr, so Y=τ/10 is uniform on [0,1]. The normalized initial mass function has probability density function f(m)=0.1m−2 for m>0.1, since ∫0.1∞​m−2dm=10. Consequently
Y(t)=10Gyrt​,X(M)=∫0.1∞​u−2du∫m∞​u−2du​=m0.1​.​
(1)
These formulae have the stated age and mass domains; outside them the relevant cumulative fractions saturate at zero or one. The probability integral transform makes X uniform on [0,1]: m=0.1/X gives f(m)∣dm/dX∣=1. The time-independent initial mass function and constant number formation rate give independence of X and Y. All fractions here count objects, including white dwarfs, using the stipulated stellar evolution law.
A red giant has
m9​<τ<m10​,0≤τ≤10.
(2)
Thus the mass boundaries are m=9/τ and m=10/τ for positive τ. Equivalently, for 0.9<m<1 the red giant region runs from 9/m to the age cap 10, and for m≥1 it runs from 9/m to 10/m. There are no red giants with m≤0.9. Boundaries have zero probability and their endpoint convention does not affect the fractions. In the uniform (X,Y) square the red giant region is 9X<Y<10X, with triangle vertices (0,0), (1/10,1) and (1/9,1). The white dwarf region is the triangle 0<X<Y/10.
At fixed Y=y, define the conditional probabilities of a red giant, white dwarf and main sequence star by g(y), w(y) and s(y). Their interval widths are
g(y)=90y​,w(y)=10y​=9g(y),s(y)=1−9y​.
(3)

The systems form a coeval binary population: the two binary star components have the same age. Their masses are independent, so (X1​,X2​,Y) has uniform probability density function on [0,1]3 and their states have conditional independence given Y.
