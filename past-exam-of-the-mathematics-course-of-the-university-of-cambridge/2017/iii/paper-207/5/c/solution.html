The ordinary Nelson–Aalen estimator adds dj​/rj​ at event time tj​. Under a fitted proportional hazards model, replace the number at risk by the sum of fitted hazard multipliers:
H0​(t)=∑tj​≤t​∑i∈R(tj​)​ϕ​i​dj​​.
(44)
This is the Breslow estimator of the integrated baseline hazard, using fitted regression coefficients obtained by maximizing the Cox partial likelihood. The denominator arises because the total event intensity is h0​(t)∑i∈R(t)​ϕi​; with every multiplier equal to one it reduces to the Nelson–Aalen estimator.
The estimator is right-continuous, so H0​(ta​) already includes the event at ta​. Subtracting it leaves only the events at tc​ and td​, whose risk sets are {c,d} and {d}. Hence
H0​(td​)−H0​(ta​)=ϕ​c​+ϕ​d​1​+ϕ​d​1​.​
(45)
There is no increment at the censoring time tb​. If the lower endpoint had instead been ta​−, an additional 1/(ϕ​a​+ϕ​b​+ϕ​c​+ϕ​d​) would be present. The final singleton event can increase the baseline hazard estimate even though its partial likelihood contribution is one.
