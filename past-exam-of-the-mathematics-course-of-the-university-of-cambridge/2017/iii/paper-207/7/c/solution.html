Let population 1 have baseline θt and population 2 baseline λθt, with θ>0 and λ>1, and identical unit-rate exponential frailty distributions initially. Their population hazard functions have ratio, for t>0,
R(t)=h1​(t)h2​(t)​=λ1+λθt2/21+θt2/2​.​
(61)
At exactly t=0, both population hazards are zero, so their literal ratio is 0/0 and is undefined. The intended initial comparison is the right-hand limit,
R(0+)=λ,t→∞lim​R(t)=1.​
(62)
With z=θt2/2, dR/dz=λ(1−λ)/(1+λz)2<0, so the marginal hazard ratio decreases from λ toward one. The faster-hazard population loses highly frail individuals sooner; its survivors become less frail than the first population's survivors.
Conditional on the same fixed frailty u>0, the individual hazard ratio is the constant λ. Marginalizing over frailty therefore destroys proportional hazards, even though they hold at the individual level. A population-level Cox proportional-hazards model that ignores frailty would impose an incorrect constant hazard ratio here. Convergence of the ratio to one does not mean identical survival: S2​(t)/S1​(t)→1/λ.
