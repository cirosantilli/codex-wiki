In a competing risks model, the first event has time T and type J; observing one type prevents another type from being the first event for that individual. Its cause-specific hazard is
hj​(t)=limΔ↓0​ΔP(t≤T<t+Δ,J=j∣T≥t)​.
(46)
For absolutely continuous event times the total hazard function is ∑j​hj​(t), giving the event-free survivor function S(t)=exp[−∫0t​∑j​hj​(u)du]. Since an event of type j near t has unconditional density S(t)hj​(t), its cumulative incidence function satisfies
Fj​(t)=P(T≤t,J=j)=∫0t​S(u)hj​(u)du.​
(47)
This cause-specific hazard to cumulative incidence formula does not require independent latent times for the competing causes. In general Fj​(t) is not 1−e−∫0t​hj​, since that expression ignores removal by other causes.
Put a=θA​≥0, b=θB​≥0 and q=a+b, with τ≥0. Here
S(t)=e−at−bmin(t,τ).
(48)
Integrating aS(u) separately before and after τ gives, for q>0,
FA​(t)=⎩⎨⎧​qa​(1−e−qt),qa​(1−e−qτ)+e−qτ(1−e−a(t−τ)),​0≤t≤τ,t>τ.​​
(49)
After τ, only type A can occur, so the second term is the fraction event-free at τ multiplied by their subsequent event probability. The expressions agree at τ. If a=b=0, both incidence functions are identically zero; if a=0<b, this formula gives FA​=0.
