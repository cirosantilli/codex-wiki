Let δD​ and δI​ be independent, approximately unbiased estimates of one consistent treatment effect, with variances vD​,vI​>0. The inverse-variance weighted mean gives
δ=1/vD​+1/vI​δD​/vD​+δI​/vI​​,Var(δ)=(vD​1​+vI​1​)−1.​
(3)
Indeed, weights w,1−w give variance w2vD​+(1−w)2vI​; differentiating gives w=vI​/(vD​+vI​), the weight on the direct estimate. Here the three displayed trials give δD​=−1.33, vD​=0.04, and hence δ≃−1.3203 with variance 0.03448. This numerical pooling uses the displayed evidence, not the unavailable data from all twelve trials. A shared study or shared control would introduce covariance, requiring the corresponding correlated-estimator formula; inconsistency would invalidate the common-effect interpretation.
