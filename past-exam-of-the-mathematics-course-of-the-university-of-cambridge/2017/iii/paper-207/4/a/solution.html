For a nonnegative continuous event time, the survivor function is F(t)=P(T>t); here the printed F denotes survival, not the usual cumulative distribution function. A lower median is
m=inf{t≥0:F(t)≤1/2}.​
(33)
For a continuous strictly decreasing survivor function this is the unique solution of F(m)=1/2. If the curve has a flat stretch at 1/2, the distribution has a nonunique median and the infimum specifies the lower endpoint.
The tail integral formula for moments follows from T=∫0∞​1{T>t}dt and Tonelli theorem. Therefore, when survival vanishes beyond t∗,
ET=∫0t∗​F(t)dt.​
(34)
If F(t)>0 at every finite time, the mean is finite precisely when ∫0∞​F(t)dt<∞. For a concrete sufficient condition, require F(t)≤Ct−1−ε for all t≥M>0, with C<∞ and ε>0. The integral over [0,M] is at most M, and the tail integral is at most CM−ε/ε. An exponential tail bound also suffices. Merely having F(t)→0, or even tF(t)→0, does not suffice: a properly completed decreasing tail proportional to 1/(tlogt) for t≥e has infinite mean.
