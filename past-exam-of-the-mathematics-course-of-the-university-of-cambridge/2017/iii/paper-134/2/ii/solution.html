Part (i) first shows that D−C is effective. Also C2<0: writing D=aC+E with a≥1 and E effective without C as a component gives D⋅C=aC2+E⋅C, where E⋅C≥0.
For 0≤j<m,
(mD−jC)⋅C=m(D−C)⋅C+(m−j)C2<0.
(15)
Every effective member of ∣mD∣ therefore contains C; after subtracting it, the same reasoning applies again, until m copies have been subtracted. Conversely adding mC to an effective member of ∣m(D−C)∣ is allowed. Hence
∣mD∣=∣m(D−C)∣+mC(m≥0).​
(16)
For m=0 this is just the identity system ∣0∣. The extra intersection hypothesis is needed: on the Hirzebruch surface F2​, with negative section S and fibre F, take D=S+F. Then D⋅S=−1 but (D−S)⋅S=1. Here h0(2D)=4 while h0(2F)=3, so removing 2S loses a section.
For the point blowup of a smooth algebraic surface ψ:X→X with exceptional curve E, the canonical divisor formula for a surface blowup and the intersection formula for blowing up a surface give
KX​=ψ∗KX​+E,E2=−1,ψ∗KX​⋅E=0.
(17)
One must not assume KX​ itself is effective. Instead, if an effective member of ∣mKX​∣ exists, then for each 0≤j<m its class after removing jE has intersection −m+j<0 with E. The same fixed-component argument removes exactly the required mE, giving
H0(X,O(mψ∗KX​)) ⋅sEm​ ​H0(X,O(mKX​))
(18)
as an isomorphism, also when both sides are zero. Since X is normal and ψ is proper and birational, ψ∗​OX​=OX​. The projection formula for sheaves therefore identifies the space on the left with H0(X,OX​(mKX​)). Consequently
H0(X,mKX​)≅H0(X,mKX​)(m≥0).​
(19)
The PDF writes equality of spaces; this is the canonical identification just described, rather than literal equality of spaces on different schemes. This proves blowup invariance of plurigenera in arbitrary characteristic.
