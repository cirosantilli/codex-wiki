Let C∈Neg(KX​). The arithmetic adjunction formula on a smooth surface gives
2pa​(C)−2=C2+KX​⋅C,pa​(C)≥0.
(26)
We first exclude C2≥0, following the PDF's hint and the finiteness proved in (a).
If C2=0, adjunction and KX​⋅C<0 force pa​(C)=0 and KX​⋅C=−2. An integral projective curve of arithmetic genus zero is a smooth rational curve: normalization and the nonnegative singularity-length correction show both normalization genus and singularity correction vanish. Part (iii) makes C semiample. Choose a basepoint-free ∣mC∣. Over the infinite algebraically closed field, a general section avoids containing any of the finitely many curves of Neg(KX​) as a component. Its effective divisor G then has KX​⋅G≥0, but KX​⋅G=mKX​⋅C<0, a contradiction.
If C2>0, Riemann–Roch theorem for algebraic surfaces and Serre duality show h0(X,mC) is unbounded. Indeed h2(X,mC)=h0(X,KX​−mC)=0 for m≫0, because the latter divisor has negative intersection with a fixed ample divisor. Hence
h0(X,mC)≥χ(X,OX​)+21​(m2C2−mKX​⋅C)⟶∞.
(27)
There is therefore some m with h0(X,mC)>h0(X,(m−1)C), giving a section whose divisor does not contain C. The canonical section of mC does not vanish identically along any other curve. A general linear combination of these two sections consequently contains none of the finite set Neg(KX​), again contradicting its negative canonical intersection. Thus C2<0.
Now put a=−C2≥1 and b=−KX​⋅C≥1. Adjunction gives
−a−b=2pa​(C)−2≥−2.
(28)
It follows that a=b=1 and pa​(C)=0. Hence
C≅P1,C2=−1,KX​⋅C=−1.​
(29)
The general-section argument only avoids finitely many proper linear subspaces, so it works over any algebraically closed field, including positive characteristic.
