Past exam of the mathematics course of the University of Cambridge / 2017 / iii / Paper 134 / 3 / ii / Solution

 ... Past exam of the mathematics course of the University of Cambridge 2017 iii Paper 134 3 ii
OurBigBook.com Words: 244
First work on an integral component. Write the big real divisor as D∼R​B+E with B ample real divisor and E effective real Cartier, using Kodaira's lemma. Let E1​,…,Ek​ be the finitely many integral components of its support. For an integral projective curve C not contained in this support, restriction of each effective Cartier summand to C is effective, so
D⋅C=B⋅C+E⋅C>0.
(33)
Therefore
D⋅C<0⟹C⊆Ei​ for some i.​
(34)
This proves negative curves of a big real divisor lie in finitely many divisors.
Now let A be the given ample divisor. Openness of the ample cone gives a δ>0 such that B−ϵA is ample for 0<ϵ≤δ. For each of the finitely many Ei​, the assumption that D∣Ei​​ is ample similarly gives a δi​>0 such that (D−ϵA)∣Ei​​ is ample for 0<ϵ≤δi​. Choose a single positive ϵ smaller than all these bounds.
If C is contained in some Ei​, its intersection with D−ϵA is positive by that restriction. Otherwise
(D−ϵA)⋅C=(B−ϵA)⋅C+E⋅C>0.
(35)
In particular D−ϵA is nef:
D−ϵA is nef for all sufficiently small ϵ>0.​
(36)
Only the finitely many exceptional support components are needed for the restriction test; no uniform bound over all subvarieties was assumed.
For a reducible projective scheme, use componentwise bigness on a projective scheme and repeat this argument on each reduced irreducible component. Collect their exceptional supports and take the minimum of all the finitely many positive bounds. Codimension one here is measured in the relevant irreducible component. Every integral curve lies in a component, so the same conclusion holds on X. Nilpotent structure does not affect these curve intersection numbers.
