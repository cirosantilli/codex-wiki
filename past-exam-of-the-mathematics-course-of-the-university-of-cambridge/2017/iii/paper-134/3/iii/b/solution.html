Choose an ample Cartier divisor H. Put initially A=δH, A′=tH for small positive real δ,t. Since D is nef, the nef-plus-ample ampleness lemma makes D+tH ample. The polynomial
P(δ,t)=(D+tH)n−n(D+tH)n−1⋅((δ+t)H)
(38)
has P(0,0)=Dn⋅[X]>0. Thus the desired strict inequality holds when δ,t are sufficiently small and positive.
We must also arrange rationality of the two specified classes; D itself need not be rational. Choose rational ample classes B and C sufficiently near D+tH and (δ+t)H, and define
A′=B−D,A=C−A′=C−B+D.
(39)
Then A′ is close to tH and A is close to δH, so both are ample real divisors by openness of the ample cone. Also D+A′=B and A+A′=C are rational and ample. Continuity preserves the strict inequality, giving
Bn>nBn−1⋅C,D−A=B−C.​
(40)
Here is the needed algebraic Morse inequality for ample divisors, with its section-count proof. Choose rational Cartier divisor representatives of B,C and a common positive integer q making qB,qC very ample integral Cartier divisors. For the section-count argument rename these scaled representatives B,C; undoing this scaling restricts section indices to sufficiently divisible multiples and leaves bigness unchanged. Choose an effective Cartier divisor G∈∣C∣ by taking a defining section that avoids the associated points of OX​. Repeated divisor restriction exact sequences give
h0(X,m(B−C))≥h0(X,mB)−∑j=0m−1​h0(G,OG​(mB−jC)).
(41)
Because C is very ample, for each j a section of jC avoiding the finitely many associated points of OG​ gives an injection into OG​(mB). Thus every summand is at most h0(G,OG​(mB)). By Serre vanishing and asymptotic Riemann–Roch for the ample B,
h0(X,m(B−C))≥n!Bn−nBn−1⋅C​mn+O(mn−1).
(42)
For n=1, the restriction term is the constant length of G, giving the same formula directly. The positive coefficient proves that B−C, hence D−A, is big. Scaling back preserves bigness, so
D−A is big.​
(43)
For a general projective scheme, enforce the same strict inequality separately on each positive-dimensional reduced irreducible component Vj​, using its own dimension nj​. At (δ,t)=(0,0) every such expression equals the positive number Dnj​⋅[Vj​]. Finitely many conditions are preserved by one sufficiently small choice and one sufficiently close rational approximation on N1(X)R​. The top-dimensional inequalities imply the printed inequality for [X] with its positive generic multiplicities; the section proof on each component makes D−A componentwise big. This avoids inferring bigness on every component from just a positive sum. The displayed inequality is used for n≥1. For n=0, its literal n−1 intersection power is undefined; handle this vacuous positivity case separately. Every line bundle is ample, all numerical classes are zero, and the componentwise bigness convention makes the conclusions automatic.
