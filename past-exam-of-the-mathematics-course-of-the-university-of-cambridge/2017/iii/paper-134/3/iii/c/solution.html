Part (b) supplies an ample A with D−A big. Therefore D=(D−A)+A is big as well, since adding an ample class to a big class preserves the ample-plus-effective decomposition. The permitted induction hypothesis makes D ample on every proper positive-dimensional closed subscheme of lower dimension, in particular on every codimension-one integral subvariety. Zero-dimensional restrictions are ample automatically.
Apply part (ii) to this big D and the ample A chosen in (b). The result is
D−ϵA is nef for 0<ϵ≪1.​
(44)
For a reducible scheme the same argument is componentwise. If a positive-dimensional irreducible component itself is a strict closed subscheme of X, the permitted induction assumption already makes D ample on that component; otherwise its codimension-one subvarieties are lower-dimensional and the same proof applies. Only one finite minimum of bounds is required.
