OurBigBook.com Words: 183
Use azimuth measured from north toward west, and positive hour angle westward. These conventions are forced by the signs in the coordinate formulae. In the diagram the observer is at O, the local vertical meets the celestial sphere at the zenith Z, and the north celestial pole is P. The astronomical horizon is perpendicular to OZ; the celestial equator is perpendicular to OP. Their intersections with the celestial meridian give the north horizon point and the upper equatorial meridian point.
The north celestial pole has altitude θ; thus the angle between OP and OZ is 90∘−θ. Project X along its vertical great circle onto the astronomical horizon at H: the arc HX is the altitude γ, and the westward horizon arc from north to H is the azimuth η. Project X along its hour circle onto the celestial equator at Q: the arc QX is the declination δ, and the westward equatorial arc from the upper celestial meridian to Q is the hour angle h. Equivalently the triangle on the celestial sphere PZX has sides PZ=90∘−θ, PX=90∘−δ and ZX=90∘−γ. All five angles refer to arcs on their specified reference circles, not arbitrary angles in the projected drawing.
