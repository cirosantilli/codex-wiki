Use a two-sided top-hat line plume with full width w(z), upward speed U(z), and reduced gravity b(z) uniform across the plume. Fluxes are measured per unit span along the line source: the volume flux is V=wU, the kinematic momentum flux is M=wU2, and the buoyancy flux is B=wUb. If the inward edge speed is eU, where e>0 is the entrainment coefficient, the two exposed edges give
dzdV​=2eU,dzdM​=wb=UB​,dzdB​=0.
(13)
These are the volume conservation, momentum conservation, and buoyancy flux balances for a Boussinesq approximation plume in an unstratified ambient. Entrained ambient fluid supplies neither vertical momentum nor reference buoyancy.
A pure plume has no persistent source length scale. Since a line-source B has dimensions L3T−3, dimensional analysis gives constant U∝B1/3, w∝z, and V∝z. Substituting constant U into M=UV and the momentum balance determines the coefficients:
2eU2=UB​,U=(2e)−1/3B1/3,w=2ez,V=(2e)2/3B1/3z.​
(14)
Thus λ=(2e)2/3 and μ=2e when width is full width and velocity and buoyancy have top-hat profiles. If width means half-width, μ=e instead. Other prescribed profile shapes change these numerical factors; the linear growth laws remain the same. The point-source B in Question 1 has different dimensions, so its z5/3 law must not be used here.
Let C(x,z,t) be local contaminant concentration and let c(z,t)=∫C(x,z,t)dx be horizontally integrated plume concentration. Its conserved amount per unit span is ∫0∞​cdz=Vc​. The dilute contaminant is a passive scalar; the maintained plume is unaffected by its impulsive release. A one-dimensional effective transport closure takes the integrated scalar flux to be
J=ac−K(z)cz​,a=αB1/3,K(z)=Dz,D=βB1/3.
(15)
The constant advective speed and the eddy diffusivity scaling K∼Uw∝B1/3z follow from the line-plume scales. The entrainment coefficient alone does not determine the scalar dispersion coefficient: this Fickian closure for the integrated variable is an additional modelling assumption. Mass conservation ct​+Jz​=0 then gives the displayed transport equation in the PDF. With this effective closure and advective speed chosen as U, α=(2e)−1/3; if K=κUw, then β=κ(2e)2/3, with independent dimensionless mixing coefficient κ.
There is a second possible closure convention. If one instead applies local diffusive flux to horizontally averaged plume concentration C=c/w, its integrated diffusive flux is −KwCz​=−K(cz​−c/z). For K=Dz, the total scalar flux is then (U+D)c−Dzcz​. The same printed equation is obtained with a=U+D, rather than a=U. Thus the printed α and β should be regarded as effective coefficients unless the averaging and closure conventions are specified; no equality between α and the velocity prefactor is universal.
Assume a,D>0, z>0, an initial impulse Vc​δ0​ at the origin, no further contaminant input, and zero endpoint scalar flux for t>0. For a similarity solution, put
r=Da​=βα​,η=Dtz​,c=DtVc​​f(η).
(16)
Substituting into the advection-diffusion equation gives
ηf′′+(1−r+η)f′+f=0,[ηf′+(η−r)f]′=0.
(17)
Decay at infinity makes the integrated constant zero. Hence f′/f=r/η−1, and normalization with the gamma function gives f(η)=ηre−η/Γ(1+r). The resulting gamma impulse solution for linearly increasing diffusivity is
c(z,t)=βB1/3tΓ(1+α/β)Vc​​(βB1/3tz​)α/βexp(−βB1/3tz​).​
(18)
Its integral over z>0 is Vc​. Its flux is J=(z/t)c, which vanishes at both endpoints for t>0. The scale Dt shrinks to zero as t↓0, so the normalized solution has weak convergence of probability measures to the required unit source impulse. The mass at the boundary is a full unit impulse on the half-line, not half of a whole-line impulse.
For the integrated quantity, ∂z​logc=r/z−1/(Dt), and consequently
zmax,c​=rDt=αB1/3t.​
(19)
This verifies the printed location for horizontally integrated plume concentration. It is a maximum because the derivative changes from positive to negative there. The normalized profile is a gamma distribution with shape 1+r: its expected value is (a+D)t and its variance is (a+D)Dt, so neither the mean position nor the spread should be mistaken for the modal position.
The PDF changes from “integral” to “averaged” concentration in its final request. A genuine horizontally averaged plume concentration divides by the growing width w=μz, and is therefore
C(z,t)=μ(Dt)2Γ(1+r)Vc​​(Dtz​)r−1e−z/(Dt).​
(20)
It is normalized by ∫0∞​wCdz=Vc​, not by ∫Cdz. When a>D, its interior maximum is
zmax,C​=(a−D)t=(α−β)B1/3t.​
(21)
When a=D it decreases from a finite boundary supremum; when 0<a<D it is singular at the ideal point source and has no positive interior maximum. A finite source regularizes that singularity. Thus the final printed maximum is correct for the integrated variable c, or for an “average” using a fixed reference width, but is not generally the maximum of the local mean c/w(z). Both quantities have been given rather than silently identifying them. If D=0, the smooth similarity formula is replaced by the advected impulse Vc​δ(z−at); positive eddy diffusivity is essential to the gamma profile.
