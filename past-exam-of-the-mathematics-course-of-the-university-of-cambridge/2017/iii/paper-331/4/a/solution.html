Use ν=Re−1>0, κ=Pe−1>0, U=u+u, and Θ=θ+θ. Work along a sufficiently regular direct trajectory satisfying the given constraints. The zero spatial mean of the scalar field is conserved by incompressible flow, impermeable walls and zero scalar flux, so minimizing J=(Θ(T),Θ(T)) is equivalent to minimizing scalar variance, up to the fixed domain volume.
The printed functional fixes the initial state to a candidate u0​; it contains no term that enforces its kinetic energy. For the optimization over that candidate, add the real Lagrange multiplier constraint
LE​=L−λ[21​(u0​,u0​)−E0​].
(41)
Equivalently, one can restrict all control variations to the sphere in a normed vector space of fixed kinetic energy. This term changes the initial-control optimality condition, not the interior adjoint equations.
Let v=δu, σ=δθ and π=δp. Linearization of the momentum and scalar transport residuals gives
δFu​=∂t​v+U⋅∇v+v⋅∇U+RiB​σy^​+∇π−νΔv,
(42)
δFθ​=∂t​σ+U⋅∇σ+v⋅∇Θ−κΔσ.
(43)
Both appearances of the perturbation velocity in the nonlinear momentum term have been differentiated. In particular, the coefficient is the gradient of the total velocity, not just the base shear.
For the negative-constraint convention of the functional, integration by parts gives the interior coefficients of v,σ,π as
Au​Aθ​Ap​​=∂t​u†+U⋅∇u†−(∇U)Tu†+∇p†+νΔu†−θ†∇Θ,=∂t​θ†+U⋅∇θ†+κΔθ†−RiB​uy†​,=∇⋅u†.​
(44)
Thus the adjoint equations for Boussinesq scalar mixing are
Au​=0,Aθ​=0,∇⋅u†=0.​
(45)
The transpose is essential: the jth component of (∇U)Tu† is ∑i​ui†​∂j​Ui​. The coupling −θ†∇Θ transposes advection of the scalar by a velocity perturbation; −RiB​uy†​ transposes buoyancy feedback. Dropping the latter would give a passive scalar adjoint, not the active scalar problem.
These equations are integrated backward, not forward. If τ=T−t, they read
∂τ​u†=U⋅∇u†−(∇U)Tu†+∇p†+νΔu†−θ†∇Θ,
(46)
∂τ​θ†=U⋅∇θ†+κΔθ†−RiB​uy†​,
(47)
with direct coefficients evaluated at t=T−τ. Both terms from the diffusion equation now have the usual forward sign in τ. A direct-adjoint looping method stores or reconstructs the forward trajectory, solves these equations backward, and uses the initial adjoint as the control gradient. The endpoint and fixed-energy conditions below give necessary conditions for a local optimizer, not a global optimality theorem.
