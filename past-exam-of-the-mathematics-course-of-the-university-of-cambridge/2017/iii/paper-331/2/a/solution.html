Let D=U−c and F=w/D1/2. For ci​>0, D never vanishes, so an analytic branch of a square root exists along the real flow domain. Take real smooth velocity and buoyancy frequency coefficients, nonzero k (chosen positive for the growing-wave convention), a nontrivial regular normal mode, and boundary decay strong enough to remove the integration-by-parts term.
Substitution into the Taylor–Goldstein equation gives the half-power case of the power-transformed Taylor–Goldstein energy identity:
(DF′)′−k2DF−21​U′′F+DS​F=0,S=N2−41​(U′)2.
(19)
For completeness, differentiating w=D1/2F yields w′′=D1/2F′′+U′D−1/2F′+[21​U′′D−1/2−41​(U′)2D−3/2]F, which explains the coefficient 1/4.
Multiply by F∗ and integrate over z. The boundary term [F∗DF′] is zero for the given homogeneous endpoint conditions, or for sufficiently decaying finite-energy modes at infinity. Therefore
∫[D(∣F′∣2+k2∣F∣2)+21​U′′∣F∣2−DS​∣F∣2]dz=0.
(20)
Since ImD=−ci​, Im(1/D)=ci​/∣D∣2, and S,U′′ are real, taking the imaginary part and dividing by −ci​ gives
∫[∣F′∣2+k2∣F∣2+∣D∣2S​∣F∣2]dz=0.
(21)
The first two terms have strictly positive integral for a nonzero mode. Thus S cannot be nonnegative everywhere:
N2−41​(U′)2<0somewhere.​
(22)
This proves the Miles–Howard theorem in contrapositive form. Where U′=0, the corresponding gradient Richardson number must fall below 1/4 somewhere. Failure of this sufficient-stability criterion does not prove instability. The derivation uses ci​>0; it cannot be applied unchanged to a neutral singular critical layer.
