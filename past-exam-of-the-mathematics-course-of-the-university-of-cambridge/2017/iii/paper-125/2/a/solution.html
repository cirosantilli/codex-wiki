An elliptic curve has good reduction over K if it admits an integral Weierstrass equation of an elliptic curve with unit elliptic-curve discriminant. Equivalently, the reduction of its Minimal Weierstrass equation is a nonsingular cubic over the residue field k. Its smooth integral projective model is a group scheme with the point O as identity.
Choose homogeneous coordinates in the valuation ring OK​ with at least one coordinate a unit, and reduce them modulo its maximal ideal mK​. This defines red:E(K)→E(k). The chord-and-tangent group law extends over the smooth integral model, so reduction is a group homomorphism. For any point of E(k), choose an affine projective chart containing it. Smoothness means some partial derivative of its equation is nonzero in k. Lift the other coordinate arbitrarily and apply the Hensel lemma to the coordinate with unit derivative. This constructs a point of E(K) above the prescribed point, proving the surjectivity of good reduction over a local field.
The kernel of reduction of an elliptic curve consists precisely of points reducing to O. The parameter t=−x/y at O gives a bijection between this kernel and mK​: the integral formal-coordinate series solve the equation uniquely near O. Addition in these coordinates is the formal group of an elliptic curve, not ordinary addition in mK​. Thus
