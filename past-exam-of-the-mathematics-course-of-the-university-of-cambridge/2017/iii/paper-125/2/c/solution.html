Past exam of the mathematics course of the University of Cambridge / 2017 / iii / Paper 125 / 2 / c / Solution

 ... Past exam of the mathematics course of the University of Cambridge 2017 iii Paper 125 2 c
OurBigBook.com Words: 266
Let Kur be the maximal unramified extension of K, with residue field k. First show that every geometric solution of nQ=P lies in E(Kur).
The multiplication-by-n morphism on the reduced elliptic curve is surjective over k. Choose Q​ with nQ​=P, defined over some finite finite field extension k′/k. Let L/K be the corresponding finite unramified extension. By the surjectivity of good reduction over a local field, lift Q​ to Q0​∈E(L). Then P−nQ0​∈E1​(L). The multiplication isomorphism of a formal group law gives a unique R∈E1​(L) with nR=P−nQ0​, so Q0​+R is an n-division point of P defined over L.
Taking P=O gives every element of E[n] over Kur: reduction is injective on this torsion point of an elliptic curve group because [n] is injective on the formal kernel, and each reduced n-torsion point has the unique corrected lift just constructed. Every other division point differs from one lift by an element of E[n]. Hence the entire field K([n]−1P) is unramified, not merely a field containing one choice of Q.
To bound the composite uniformly in P, put f=[K(E[n]):K]. This division field of an elliptic curve is unramified. The Galois group of Kur/K is procyclic, generated topologically by its Frobenius element σ, and σf fixes every element of E[n]. If nQ=P∈E(K), then
T=σfQ−Q∈E[n],σfT=T.
(16)
Iteration gives σjfQ=Q+jT. In particular σnfQ=Q. Thus every such Q, for every P together, lies in the unique unramified extension of degree nf. If M is the composite of all these fields, the uniform unramified division field over a local field gives
M/K is finite unramified,[M:K]≤n[K(E[n]):K].​
(17)
The prime-to-p hypothesis is essential to the inverse-series argument; no assertion about arbitrary p-division is being made.
