Take the odd congruence class p≡3(mod8). The congruent number elliptic curve in the standard scaled coordinates is
Ep​:y2=x(x2−p2),Ep′​:y2=x(x2+4p2).
(50)
These are the two curves in two-isogeny descent. On Ep​, the prime-support bound in two-isogeny descent restricts the first image to {1,−1,p,−p}. All four classes occur: the 2-torsion points give α((0,0))=[−p2]=[−1], α((p,0))=[p], and α((−p,0))=[−p]. Thus #α(Ep​)=4.
On Ep′​, the equation forces x>0 whenever x=0. Its exceptional value is [4p2]=1. The second image is therefore contained in {1,2,p,2p}. Use the quartic covering in a two-isogeny descent to exclude its three nontrivial candidates.
For d=p, a rational solution can be written with coprime integral U,V, and must satisfy
W2=p(U4+4V4).
(51)
Then p∣W, so U4+(2V2)2≡0(modp). Since p≡3(mod4), −1 is not a quadratic residue; a sum of two squares is zero only if both are zero. This forces p∣U,V, contradicting coprimality. For d=2p, the same reasoning applies to W2=2p(U4+V4) and again forces p∣U,V.
For d=2, the equation is
W2=2U4+2p2V4.
(52)
It implies W=2Z, whence 2Z2=U4+p2V4. Exactly one of U,V odd is impossible by parity, while both even contradict coprimality. Thus both are odd. Since p≡3(mod8) implies p2≡9(mod16), reduction modulo sixteen gives 2Z2≡1+9=10. But the possible residues of 2Z2 modulo sixteen are 0,2,8. This contradiction eliminates d=2. Consequently α′(Ep′​)={1} and the square-class index formula for two-isogeny descent gives
2r=44⋅1​=1,r=0.
(53)
The reduction argument for y2=x3+kx used earlier bounds rational torsion by four for every nonzero integer k: its point-count cancellation at q≡3(mod4) does not depend on the sign of k, and the same prime choices apply. Here k=−p2 and all four 2-torsion points are rational. Hence Ep​(Q) consists exactly of O,(0,0),(p,0),(−p,0).
To relate this calculation to congruent numbers, a rational point (x,y) with y=0 produces the right triangle with side lengths
A=​yx2−p2​​,B=​y2px​​,C=​yx2+p2​​.
(54)
The identities A2+B2=C2 and AB/2=p follow from y2=x(x2−p2). Conversely, a positive rational right triangle of area p with legs A,B and hypotenuse C gives
x=Bp(A+C)​,y=B22p2(A+C)​=0.
(55)
Direct substitution uses AB=2p and C2=A2+B2. No such point exists here. This proves the noncongruent primes congruent to three modulo eight:
p≡3(mod8) prime ⟹ p is not a congruent number.​
(56)
