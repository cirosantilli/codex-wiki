Past exam of the mathematics course of the University of Cambridge / 2017 / iii / Paper 125 / 3 / b / Solution

OurBigBook.com Words: 227
For this integral Weierstrass equation of an elliptic curve, compute
Δ=16m4(m2−1)2,c4​=16(m4−m2+1).
(24)
If an odd prime divides m or m2−1, then c4​ is a unit there and Δ is not. The j-invariant of an elliptic curve consequently has negative valuation. Since good reduction implies an integral j-invariant, such a prime cannot be a prime of good reduction, regardless of whether the displayed equation is minimal. Conversely, if it divides neither factor, this equation already has unit discriminant.
At five the good possibilities have m≡±2, so m2≡4. At seven they have m2≡2 or 4. Direct elliptic-curve point count over a finite field gives the following nonzero ordinates, together with the three zero-ordinate points and O:
p577​m2modp424​points with y=0(2,±1),(3,±2)(3,±2),(4,±1)(2,±1),(5,±2)​#E(Fp​)888​​
(25)
At either prime, torsion-freeness of the formal group over Qp for odd p makes reduction injective on all of E(Q)tors​, so its order is at most eight. Using only prime-to-p injectivity here would leave an unjustified possible p-primary component.
All three nonzero 2-torsion points (0,0),(−1,0),(−m2,0) are rational. The rational point P=(m,m(m+1)) lies on the curve, since m(m+1)(m+m2)=m2(m+1)2. For an equation y2=x(x2+ax+b) the elliptic-curve addition formula gives
x(2P)=4x(P)(x(P)2+ax(P)+b)(x(P)2−b)2​.
(26)
Here a=1+m2, b=m2, so 2P=(0,0) and P has order four. The point (−1,0) is not in ⟨P⟩, whose only nonzero point of order two is (0,0). Therefore P and (−1,0) generate a subgroup of order eight isomorphic to Z/4Z×Z/2Z. The upper bound proves the rational torsion in the family x times x plus one times x plus m squared:
E(Q)tors​≅Z/2Z×Z/4Z.​
(27)
