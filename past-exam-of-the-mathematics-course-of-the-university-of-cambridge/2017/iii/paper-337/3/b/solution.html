For homogeneous corrections at the square boundary, integration by parts gives ⟨ψ0​Δψ1​⟩=⟨ψ1​Δψ0​⟩ and ⟨θ0​Δθ1​⟩=⟨θ1​Δθ0​⟩. The first-derivative cross terms give ⟨ψ0​θ1x​⟩=−⟨ψ0x​θ1​⟩ and ⟨θ0​ψ1x​⟩=−⟨θ0x​ψ1​⟩. Using Δψ0​=qc2​θ0x​ and Δθ0​=−ψ0x​, these four contributions cancel in pairs. This proves exactly the supplied solvability condition, including the weight qc2​ on its second row. Equivalently (ψ0​,qc2​θ0​) is the relevant adjoint eigenfunction for this coupled system.
At first order in the detuning, the inhomogeneous equations are
Δψ1​−qc2​θ1x​=2qc​q1​θ0x​,ψ1x​+Δθ1​=λθ0​.
(23)
Insert these right-hand sides into the verified solvability condition. One obtains
2qc​q1​⟨ψ0​θ0x​⟩+qc2​λ⟨θ02​⟩=0.
(24)
A further integration by parts, using the leading heat equation, gives
⟨ψ0​θ0x​⟩=−⟨ψ0x​θ0​⟩=⟨θ0​Δθ0​⟩=−⟨∣∇θ0​∣2⟩.
(25)
Hence the growth-rate solvability for conducting-square Darcy convection yields
λ=qc​2q1​​⟨θ02​⟩⟨∣∇θ0​∣2⟩​.​
(26)
The PDF prints the reciprocal of the required integral ratio. With its declared θt​=ϵλθ, the displayed reciprocal does not follow and is false for q1​=0. Both integrals are positive for these nonzero modes. The square's Poincaré inequality gives their ratio at least 2π2, so it cannot equal its reciprocal. For q1​=1, the two explicit parities give approximately λ=11.34770 for odd θ and λ=7.30164 for even θ; the printed expression instead gives about 0.00446439 and 0.00693825. These values provide direct mode-based counterexamples.
The corrected growth rate is positive for q1​>0 and negative for q1​<0, as expected at a convection threshold. Because the critical eigenspace is two-dimensional, an arbitrary superposition generally splits into two different first-order growth rates. The specified parity modes remain independent under the detuning: the temperature linear operator preserves reflection parity, so the cross-parity projection vanishes. This justifies applying the scalar solvability condition to either listed eigenfunction; it should not be applied as a single common eigenvalue to an arbitrary mixture.
