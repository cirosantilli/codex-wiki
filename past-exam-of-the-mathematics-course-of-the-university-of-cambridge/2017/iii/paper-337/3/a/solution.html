For Darcy-Bénard convection, the dimensionless Darcy law is u=−∇p+Rθez​, with incompressibility and a heat equation about T0​=1−z. Here R is a Darcy thermal-time Rayleigh number, with permeability replacing the squared-depth dependence in the fluid-layer Rayleigh number. The stated stream function convention gives u=(−ψz​,0,ψx​), so w=ψx​. The vertical-plane curl of Darcy law gives ∇2ψ=Rθx​; omitting the quadratic perturbation advection from the heat equation gives θt​=ψx​+∇2θ.
On the impermeable connected square boundary, zero normal velocity makes ψ constant along the boundary; choose that constant as zero. The prescribed conductive boundary temperature fixes θ=0 on all four sides. Thus the perfect-conductor wording is interpreted through the supplied homogeneous perturbation data: the side temperatures retain the background vertical profile, rather than imposing a different isothermal side state.
At marginal stability put q=R​ and P=ψ+iqθ. The two coupled equations imply
∇2P+iqPx​=0,P=0 on the boundary.
(19)
The gauge transformation for conducting-square Darcy onset P=e−iq(x−1/2)/2F removes the first derivative:
∇2F+4q2​F=0,F=0 on the boundary.
(20)
The square's Dirichlet Laplacian eigenfunctions are sin(mπx)sin(nπz), with eigenvalues of −∇2 equal to π2(m2+n2), m,n≥1. The first marginal value is therefore
qc​=22​π,Rc​=8π2.​
(21)
It is genuinely the first instability threshold, not just an isolated neutral value. Eliminating ψ gives the temperature linear operator LR​=ΔD​+R∂x​ΔD−1​∂x​, where ΔD​ is the Dirichlet realization of an elliptic operator for the Laplacian. Integration by parts shows the second term is self-adjoint and nonnegative, since its quadratic form is −⟨θx​,ΔD−1​θx​⟩≥0. Thus the spectrum is real, starts negative at R=0, and its leading eigenvalue cannot cross into growth without a zero eigenvalue. The smallest zero value is the one found above.
Let S=sinπxsinπz, ξ=x−1/2 and a=qc​/2=2​π. Multiplying the lowest real F=S by independent real and imaginary constants gives two real physical eigenfunctions:
(ψe​,θo​)(ψo​,θe​)​=(Scos(aξ),−Ssin(aξ)/qc​),=(Ssin(aξ),Scos(aξ)/qc​).​​
(22)
The subscripts indicate even and odd parity about x=1/2. Because S is even in ξ, the first has even ψ and odd θ, while the second reverses those parities. They are linearly independent and satisfy all four Dirichlet boundary conditions. The lowest complex F eigenfunction is simple over the complex numbers, but its arbitrary complex multiplier supplies two independent real pairs; this explains the physical degeneracy.
