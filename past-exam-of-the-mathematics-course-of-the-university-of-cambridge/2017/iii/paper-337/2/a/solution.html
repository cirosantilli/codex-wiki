Nonzero arbitrarily long-wave modes remain allowed on a sufficiently large domain. For fixed r>0, the two roots tending to zero have λ=l2ν+O(l4) at simple limiting roots. Repeated limiting roots may require a further expansion, but do not affect the strict instability criterion. If (μ−σ)r+2σq2>0, the constant term is negative and the two real roots have opposite signs. The positive root gives a growing long-wave sideband instability. Using r=1−q2 gives

This is sufficient for instability when a sufficiently small nonzero allowed l is available. It is not an unconditional instability theorem for every fixed finite period. For example q=0, μ=2, σ=1, L=1 satisfy the inequality, but the nonzero allowed l obey l2≥4π2. Their roots are −l2 and −l2−1±1+4l2​, all negative. The prescribed zero mean removes the uniform flux root and the remaining uniform phase is merely neutral. The corrected conclusion is the long-wave instability with a conserved mean field, with domain size and mode availability stated.
