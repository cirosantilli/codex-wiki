B−1γ5∗B=−i(B−1γ0∗B)(B−1γ1∗B)(B−1γ2∗B)(B−1γ3∗B)=(−i)(−1)3γ0γ1γ2γ3.

b OurBigBook.com
Assume a complete relativistically normalized basis of scalar momentum eigenstates transforms as
T^∣p⟩=eiχ(p)∣−p⟩,χ(p)∈R.
(3)
This states both reversal of spatial momentum and preservation of the normalization of basis quantum states. Antilinearity alone would not suffice: multiplying a conjugation operator by two gives an antilinear operator that multiplies squared norms by four.
Expand ∣ϕ⟩=∫dΠp​ϕ(p)∣p⟩ and similarly for ∣ψ⟩, with dΠp​=d3p/((2π)32Ep​). This Lorentz-invariant phase-space measure is unchanged under p↦−p. The antilinearity of the quantum time-reversal operator gives conjugated expansion coefficients. Orthogonality of the momentum eigenstates and cancellation of their unit-modulus phases yield
⟨T^ϕ∣T^ψ⟩=∫dΠp​ϕ(p)ψ(p)∗=⟨ϕ∣ψ⟩∗.
(4)
Since momentum reversal is a bijection of the complete basis, T^ is also onto. Thus
⟨T^ϕ∣T^ψ⟩=⟨ϕ∣ψ⟩∗,T^ is antiunitary.​
(5)
This proves antiunitarity, with the normalization hypothesis explicitly included. For scalar multiparticle quantum states the same argument uses the complete occupation-state basis and reverses all momenta; it is not restricted to a single-particle wave packet.

Write fs​=(−1)1/2−s for s=±1/2, so f−s​=−fs​. Under the antiunitary operator T^, the coefficients and exponentials in the mode expansion of a Dirac field are conjugated as well as the creation and annihilation operators being transformed. Thus
T^ψ(x)T^−1=∑p,s​fs​[b−s(pT​)us∗(p)eip⋅x+d−s†(pT​)vs∗(p)e−ip⋅x].
(6)
Relabel q=pT​ and r=−s. The stated Dirac spinor identities imply
f−r​u−r∗(qT​)=−γ5Cur(q),f−r​v−r∗(qT​)=−γ5Cvr(q).
(7)
Also qT​⋅x=−q⋅xT​. Substitution reconstructs the original Dirac field at the reflected time:
T^ψ(x)T^−1=Bψ(xT​),B=−γ5C.​
(8)
The minus sign comes from reversing the spin label, not from anticommuting field operators. This is the relation between the time-reversal matrix and the charge-conjugation matrix in the supplied spin phases and intrinsic phase convention. Rephasing the charge-conjugation matrix or the intrinsic time-reversal phase can change its displayed form. It proves time reversal of a Dirac field without identifying an antiunitary operator with its finite-dimensional spinor matrix.

Use tμ​=(1,−1,−1,−1)μ​ without summing in individual component transformations. The Dirac current transforms as
T^(ψˉ​γμψ)(x)T^−1=tμ​(ψˉ​γμψ)(xT​).
(9)
Since the coupling is real, invariance of its contraction fixes the time reversal of an electromagnetic gauge field:
T^A0​(x)T^−1=A0​(xT​),T^Ai​(x)T^−1=−Ai​(xT​).​
(10)
These equations use a compatible gauge; an additional pure gauge transformation would not change the gauge field strength.
Coordinate differentiation at xT​ introduces rμ​=−tμ​, because the time coordinate is reversed and the spatial coordinates are unchanged. Hence
T^Fμν​(x)T^−1=−tμ​tν​Fμν​(xT​),B−1σμν∗B=−tμ​tν​σμν.
(11)
Thus the electric field is even and the magnetic field is odd. The chirality matrix is even by part (a). The two tensor signs cancel in the dipole contraction, but antiunitarity conjugates its explicit i. Therefore the time-reversal parity of a fermion electric dipole operator is
T^LEDM​(x)T^−1=−LEDM​(xT​).​
(12)
It is a time-reversal symmetry violating interaction, and, under the usual local relativistic quantum field theory hypotheses of the CPT theorem, it violates CP symmetry. A nonzero coefficient is absent from the renormalizable tree-level Standard Model Lagrangian: the broken-phase fermion electric dipole moment operator has mass dimension five, and its electroweak-invariant completion requires a Higgs field and has dimension six. However, it can arise as a radiatively induced effective interaction in the Standard Model, whose CKM matrix contains a CP-violating phase. It is not forbidden to all orders. An explicit primary calculation of quark dipoles is Czarnecki and Krause's Standard Model calculation.

e OurBigBook.com
Starting from a matter-antimatter symmetric ensemble, exact charge conjugation would pair every baryon-producing process with an equally probable antibaryon-producing process. Their contributions to the net baryon number cancel. Thus C violation is necessary. Violating charge conjugation alone is insufficient: if CP symmetry is exact, the parity-reflected conjugate channels still have equal probabilities and cancel in a CP symmetry invariant ensemble. One needs CP violation as well.
The remaining Sakharov conditions are baryon number violation and departure from thermal equilibrium. Conserving baryon number cannot turn an initially zero value into a nonzero one. In thermal equilibrium, under the usual CPT theorem hypotheses and with no imposed chemical potentials for charges odd under CPT symmetry, states and their conjugates have equal thermal weights, so the equilibrium expectation of net baryon number vanishes and thermal stationarity prevents its sustained production. A nonequilibrium history allows different forward and reverse populations to generate an asymmetry. These are the standard necessary conditions for dynamical baryogenesis from symmetric initial conditions, not a guarantee of a sufficiently large asymmetry.

Take τa=σa/2, Yϕ​=1/2, metric gμν​=diag(1,−1,−1,−1), and a Higgs potential with μh2​>0, λ>0. The gauge-scalar electroweak interaction is
Lbos​=−41​Wμνa​Waμν−41​Bμν​Bμν+(Dμ​ϕ)†Dμϕ−V(ϕ),V(ϕ)=−μh2​ϕ†ϕ+λ(ϕ†ϕ)2.
(13)
Here Bμν​=∂μ​Bν​−∂ν​Bμ​. With the printed plus-sign convention for Dμ​, define [Dμ​,Dν​]=igWμνa​τa+ig′Yϕ​Bμν​; thus Wμνa​=∂μ​Wνa​−∂ν​Wμa​−gϵabcWμb​Wνc​. The minus sign in the nonlinear gauge field strength follows from this convention.
The minima have ϕ†ϕ=v2/2, with v2=μh2​/λ. A gauge transformation rotates the vacuum to (0,v)T/2​. In unitary gauge, the three angular Goldstone bosons are removed, leaving ϕ=(0,v+H)T/2​ and one real Higgs boson. The generator Q=T3+Y annihilates the vacuum, because its lower component has T3=−1/2 and hypercharge 1/2. This identifies the unbroken electromagnetic gauge group.
The quadratic terms from the Higgs field kinetic term are
21​(∂H)2+8g2v2​[(W1)2+(W2)2]+8v2​(gW3−g′B)2.
(14)
Define the Weinberg angle and physical fields by
tanθW​=gg′​​,Wμ±​=2​Wμ1​∓iWμ2​​​.
(15)
The neutral combinations are
Zμ​Aμ​​=cosθW​Wμ3​−sinθW​Bμ​,=sinθW​Wμ3​+cosθW​Bμ​.​​
(16)
The neutral gauge-boson mass matrix is (v2/4)(g2−gg′​−gg′g′2​). It has eigenvalues zero and (g2+g′2)v2/4, with the zero eigenvector giving Aμ​. Expanding the Higgs potential about its minimum gives V=constant+λv2H2+λvH3+λH4/4. Therefore the tree-level electroweak gauge-boson masses and scalar mass are
mA​=0,mW+​=mW−​=2gv​,mZ​=2v​g2+g′2​,mH​=2λ​v.​
(17)
Also mW​=mZ​cosθW​ and e=gsinθW​=g′cosθW​. The three removed Goldstone bosons supply the longitudinal polarization vector degrees of freedom of the massive electroweak gauge bosons.
Replacing v2 by (v+H)2 in the neutral mass term gives all the Higgs boson couplings to Z bosons in unitary gauge:
LHZ​=vmZ2​​HZμ​Zμ+2v2mZ2​​H2Zμ​Zμ.
(18)
There are exactly the cubic HZZ and quartic HHZZ elementary tree-level Feynman diagrams. Differentiating with respect to the identical fields gives Feynman rules 2imZ2​gμν​/v and 2imZ2​gμν​/v2, respectively. There is no elementary ZHH vertex for the real neutral radial field.

M=v2mZ2​​ϵ1∗​⋅ϵ2∗​,

up to an irrelevant overall phase. Apply the massive vector polarization sum, identifying the hint's MZ​ with mZ​:
∑s1​,s2​​∣ϵ1​⋅ϵ2​∣2=(−gμν​+mZ2​k1μ​k1ν​​)(−gμν+mZ2​k2μ​k2ν​​)=2+mZ4​(k1​⋅k2​)2​.

Since k1​⋅k2​=(mH2​−2mZ2​)/2, putting x=mZ2​/mH2​ gives
∑spins​∣M∣2=v2mH4​​(1−4x+12x2).
(21)
No initial-spin average is needed for a Higgs boson. In its rest frame the two-body momentum is ∣k∣=(mH​/2)1−4x​. Integrating the energy and momentum delta functions in the Lorentz-invariant phase-space measure gives dΦ2​=1−4x​dΩ/(32π2), hence ∫dΦ2​=1−4x​/(8π).
The printed generic width formula treats the daughters as labelled. Here the identical final-state symmetry factor is 1/2!, without which the same physical configuration is counted twice. Thus the Higgs decay to two Z bosons has
Γ(H→ZZ)=32πv2mH3​​1−4x​(1−4x+12x2),mH​>2mZ​.​
(22)
Equivalently, 1/v2=2​GF​ gives the prefactor GF​mH3​/(162​π). The threshold limit is zero and the large-mass limit is mH3​/(32πv2). The additional factor for identical daughters is a required specialization of the PDF's generic phase-space formula, not an extra factor in the vertex.

Allowed through an s-channel W boson. The initial up quark and down antiquark annihilate into a virtual W+, which produces the final charm quark and strange antiquark. The two weak charged current vertices contain the nonzero CKM matrix elements Vud​ and Vcs​, with complex conjugations determined by fermion-flow conventions.
The left panel shows the unique physical-particle tree-level Feynman diagram. A neutral exchanged gauge boson cannot connect these charged annihilation currents, and flavour-diagonal neutral vertices cannot turn an up quark into a charm quark. There is no additional elementary charged scalar in the minimal Standard Model.

Allowed through a t-channel W boson. Along one fermion line the up quark becomes a down quark by emitting a virtual W+; along the other the charm antiquark absorbs it and becomes a strange antiquark. Allowed through a virtual W boson. The charm quark emits a W+ and becomes a down quark through the nonzero CKM matrix element Vcd​. Use metric (+−−−), ϵ0123=+1 and relativistically normalized external spinors. The Fermi interaction gives
M=2​GF​​[vˉ(p2​)γα(P+Qγ5)u(p1​)][uˉ(k1​)γα​(P+Qγ5)v(k2​)],
(23)
up to an irrelevant phase. The fermion spin sums replace uuˉ and vvˉ by the slashed massless momenta. Write C=P2+Q2. The gamma matrix trace identities yield
Tαβ(a,b)=Tr[aγα(P+Qγ5)bγβ(P+Qγ5)]=4C(aαbβ+aβbα−gαβa⋅b)+8iPQϵαβρσaρ​bσ​.
(24)
The symmetric-antisymmetric mixed contractions vanish. The symmetric contraction is 2[(a⋅c)(b⋅d)+(a⋅d)(b⋅c)], while the two epsilon tensors contract to −2[(a⋅c)(b⋅d)−(a⋅d)(b⋅c)]. Including GF2​/2 and the 1/4 initial-spin average therefore gives
∣M∣2​=4GF2​[(C2+4P2Q2)(p2​⋅k1​)(p1​⋅k2​)+(C2−4P2Q2)(p2​⋅k2​)(p1​⋅k1​)].
(25)
The singlet color charge contractions δij​δkl​ have net factor one after summing final colours and averaging initial color charge states. Thus this is also the colour-averaged partonic result; no extra factor three is required.
In the centre-of-momentum frame, let cθ​=cosθ. The Mandelstam variables satisfy t=−s(1−cθ​)/2 and u=−s(1+cθ​)/2, giving (p2​⋅k1​)(p1​⋅k2​)=s2(1+cθ​)2/16 and (p2​⋅k2​)(p1​⋅k1​)=s2(1−cθ​)2/16. Hence
∣M∣2​=2GF2​s2​[C2(1+cθ2​)+8P2Q2cθ​].
(26)
The massless invariant flux factor is 2s and dΦ2​=dΩ/(32π2), so the weak charged-current quark scattering cross-section is
F(s)=128π2GF2​s​,H1​=(P2+Q2)2,H2​=8P2Q2.​
(27)
For a purely left-handed weak charged current, P=1, Q=−1, this reduces to dσ/dΩ=GF2​s(1+cosθ)2/(32π2), fixing the sign of the forward term. Purely right-handed weak charged currents at both vertices have the same unpolarized angular law. The factorization convention for F,H1​,H2​ could be rescaled by a common numerical factor; the displayed product fixes the normalization.

For fixed (P,Q)=(0,0) the effective Fermi interaction predicts dσ/dΩ∝GF2​s. The angular odd term integrates to zero, while ∫dΩ(1+cos2θ)=16π/3, so
σeff​(s)=24πGF2​s​(P2+Q2)2.​
(28)
The corresponding fixed-spin scattering amplitudes grow as GF​s, eventually conflicting with partial-wave unitarity. This is a failure of extrapolating the effective field theory, whose contact approximation requires s≪MW2​, not a failure of the Standard Model.
Restoring the virtual W boson Feynman propagator replaces its constant low-energy denominator by MW2​/(MW2​−s), away from the resonance. The massless external Dirac equations give zero contraction of each current with the exchanged momentum, so the longitudinal numerator does not contribute. Its squared modulus multiplies the effective cross-section. For s≫MW2​ the resulting tree-level rate decreases as 1/s rather than growing as s. Near the pole one must include the W boson decay width. These statements assume the same massless conserved external currents; the contact expression alone is not valid there.

Opposite directions replace cosθ by −cosθ. The even term cancels from the numerator and the odd term from the denominator. For (P,Q)=(0,0), the angular asymmetry of a chiral charged-current interaction is
A(θ)=H1​(1+cos2θ)H2​cosθ​=(P2+Q2)28P2Q2​1+cos2θcosθ​.​
(29)
Here θ+π denotes the antipodal direction in the stated angular law. If P=Q=0, both rates vanish and the asymmetry is undefined.
The overall Fermi constant, energy-dependent prefactor and common normalization cancel. Thus this observable constrains the relative vector current and axial current couplings of the weak charged current rather than only their total strength. Pure vector currents or pure axial currents give zero asymmetry; P=±Q gives the maximal coefficient, A=2cosθ/(1+cos2θ). The inequality 4P2Q2≤(P2+Q2)2 ensures ∣A∣≤1.
It does not uniquely identify the handedness: the formula is insensitive to P↦−P, Q↦−Q, and interchanging P,Q. In particular equally strong purely left- and right-handed weak charged currents at both vertices are indistinguishable in this unpolarized measurement. Additional spin-sensitive observables would be needed to remove that degeneracy. The cancellation and constraints are within the massless parton approximation specified for the calculation.

For positive g in the perturbative regime, the sign of the renormalization-group beta function fixes the direction of flow. If β0​>0, then g decreases when the renormalization scale increases: the theory exhibits asymptotic freedom. Toward low scales the interaction grows stronger, and its one-loop approximation reaches an infrared strong-coupling scale. This perturbative divergence does not by itself prove confinement.
If β0​<0, g increases toward the ultraviolet and decreases toward the infrared: the one-loop flow is infrared free and has an ultraviolet Landau pole. Indeed
g2(μ)1​=g2(μ0​)1​+8π2β0​​lnμ0​μ​,
(30)
whose right side reaches zero at a finite ultraviolet scale for negative β0​. Beyond strong coupling this formula is not controlled. If β0​=0, the displayed one-loop term gives no running and higher orders decide the behavior; g=0 is a fixed point in all three cases.

Differentiating the strong coupling constant αs​=g2/(4π) gives
dlnμdαs​​=−2πβ0​​αs2​,dlnμd​αs​1​=2πβ0​​.
(31)
Integrate this constant derivative and express the integration constant through the strong-coupling scale Λ at which the one-loop inverse coupling vanishes. The one-loop running of the strong coupling is
αs​(μ)=β0​ln(μ/Λ)2π​=β0​ln(μ2/Λ2)4π​.​
(32)
The positive-coupling branch has μ>Λ and is perturbatively reliable only when the logarithm is sufficiently large. The pole at Λ signals failure of the one-loop expansion, not a prediction of a physically infinite observable. Equivalently, Λ=μ0​exp[−2π/(β0​αs​(μ0​))] is scale-independent to this order; this is dimensional transmutation.

For the Quantum chromodynamics gauge group SU(3), the coefficients on the two sides of the bottom threshold are β0(4)​=25/3 and β0(5)​=23/3. Continuity of the one-loop running of the strong coupling at mb​ gives
325​lnΛ42​mb2​​=323​lnΛ52​mb2​​,lnΛ5​mb​​=2325​lnΛ4​mb​​.

Set a=αs​(μ2)/π, b=β0​/4, C=1.99−0.11nf​ and L=lnμ2, treating nf​ as fixed. The notation αs​(μ2) means the coupling evaluated at energy scale μ, using the squared-scale label. The one-loop running of the strong coupling gives da/dL=−ba2. Write ℓ=ln(q2/μ2), so dℓ/dL=−1 and
K=1+a+a2(C−bℓ).
(35)
Differentiating both explicit and implicit scale dependence gives
dLdK​=−ba2−2ba3(C−bℓ)+ba2=−2ba3(C−bℓ).
(36)
For nonzero coupling and β0​=0, the principle of minimal sensitivity applied to this truncated expression with one-loop running therefore selects
μ∗2​=q2exp[−β0​4(1.99−0.11nf​)​]=q2exp[−33−2nf​12(1.99−0.11nf​)​]for QCD.​
(37)
This is the one-loop stationary scale for the hadronic annihilation correction. For five active flavours it is approximately μ∗2​=0.472q2, or μ∗​=0.687q2​. It is meaningful within a fixed-flavour perturbative region; crossing a threshold requires the appropriate matched coupling.
The derivative cancels identically through order a2. The prescription retains the residual order-a3 term generated by differentiating the truncated observable with the one-loop running. If one instead discards every order-a3 contribution, no unique scale is determined: every scale is stationary to the retained accuracy. Two-loop running and uncomputed higher observable coefficients can shift the optimum. For β0​=0, or the trivial a=0 limit, this leading approximation likewise selects no unique scale.
