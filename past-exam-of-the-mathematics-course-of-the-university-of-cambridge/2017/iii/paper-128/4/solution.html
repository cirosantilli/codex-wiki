Choose a finite-dimensional generating vector subspace V⊆A containing 1, and write Vn for the linear span of products of n elements of V. The Gelfand–Kirillov dimension is
GKdimA=n→∞limsup​lognlogdimk​Vn​.​
(14)
For a nonzero finitely generated module M considered as a right module, choose a finite-dimensional generating vector subspace W⊆M and define the Gelfand–Kirillov dimension of a module by
GKdimA​M=n→∞limsup​lognlogdimk​(WVn)​.​
(15)
For left modules, replace WVn by VnW. These values are independent of the choices: two algebra-generating vector subspaces satisfy V⊆(V′)c and V′⊆Vd for some positive integers c,d, and two module-generating vector subspaces are contained in bounded-degree translates of one another. The corresponding growth bounds differ only by a constant rescaling and shift of n, which do not change the limit superior.
Let S be a nonzero standard graded algebra, generated by r elements of degree one. It is a homogeneous quotient ring of the polynomial ring k[z1​,…,zr​]. By the Hilbert-Serre theorem, its Hilbert series is a rational function whose only possible pole is at t=1. After canceling, write it as P(t)/(1−t)d with P(1)=0, where 0≤d≤r. The cumulative dimensions are the coefficients of P(t)/(1−t)d+1 and are eventually a polynomial in n of degree d, with positive leading coefficient. With V=S0​⊕S1​, these cumulative dimensions equal dimVn. Consequently
GKdimS=d∈{0,1,…,r}.​
(16)
For d=0 the algebra is finite-dimensional and nonzero, so the cumulative dimension is eventually a positive constant. If the zero algebra is allowed, the common convention GKdim0=−∞ is an exception to the printed assertion; the integer conclusion concerns nonzero unital algebras.
For the Weyl algebra A=A1​(k)=k⟨X,D⟩/(DX−XD−1), the total-degree filtered algebra structure has associated graded ring grA=k[x,ξ]. Its ordered monomials XiDj form a basis. Every finitely generated module has a good filtration of a module, and its associated graded module is finitely generated over k[x,ξ]. The Hilbert-Serre theorem therefore shows that its Gelfand–Kirillov dimension is an integer in {0,1,2}.
A nonzero module of Gelfand–Kirillov dimension zero here would have eventually constant cumulative dimension, hence be finite-dimensional. This is impossible in characteristic zero: taking the trace of the endomorphisms representing DX−XD=1 gives 0=dimk​M. For right modules the right-action operators reverse composition and give the negative identity instead, with the same contradiction. Thus
GKdimA​M∈{1,2}.​
(17)
Both occur. The regular module A has dimVn=(2n+2​), hence Gelfand–Kirillov dimension two. For a right module of Gelfand–Kirillov dimension one, take M=k[x] with f⋅X=xf and f⋅D=−f′. These actions satisfy the defining relation because (f⋅D)⋅X−(f⋅X)⋅D=f. The module is a cyclic module generated by 1, and dim(1⋅Vn)=n+1. This also gives the lower bound for this particular Weyl algebra without invoking a general inequality for higher Weyl algebras.
