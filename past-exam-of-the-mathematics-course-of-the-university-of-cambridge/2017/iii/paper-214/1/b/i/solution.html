Put qk​(n)=Pp​((0,0)↔(n,0) inside Tk​), with qk​(0)=1. The connection event is increasing in the open edges. Apply the Harris-FKG inequality to connection from (0,0) to (n,0) and connection from (n,0) to (n+m,0), both constrained to the strip. Their intersection implies connection between the outer endpoints, while horizontal translation invariance makes the second event's probability qk​(m). Hence
qk​(n+m)≥qk​(n)qk​(m).
(3)
These events may depend on infinitely many strip edges. To justify the inequality directly, first restrict each connecting graph path to a finite box, apply Harris-FKG inequality in the finite independent edge family, and then let the box grow. Every connection has a finite witness graph path, so continuity from below gives the stated inequality.
The direct horizontal graph path supplies qk​(n)≥pn>0, and probabilities are at most one. Thus ak​(n)=−logqk​(n) is a finite nonnegative subadditive sequence with ak​(n)≤−nlogp. The Fekete lemma now gives a finite connection decay rate in a percolation strip:
fk​(p)=n→∞lim​nak​(n)​=n≥1inf​nak​(n)​,0≤fk​(p)≤−logp.​
(4)
Each individual ratio is at least its infimum, so −logqk​(n)≥nfk​(p) and therefore
qk​(n)≤e−nfk​(p).​
(5)
At n=0 this reads 1≤1. Only nonnegative separations are intended by this notation; horizontal reflection supplies the analogous bound with ∣n∣ for negative separations.
