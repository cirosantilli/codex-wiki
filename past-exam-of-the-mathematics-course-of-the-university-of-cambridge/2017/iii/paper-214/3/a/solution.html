Past exam of the mathematics course of the University of Cambridge / 2017 / iii / Paper 214 / 3 / a / Solution

 ... Past exam of the mathematics course of the University of Cambridge 2017 iii Paper 214 3 a
OurBigBook.com Words: 299
Fix an edge e={u,v} and orient it from u to v. For each spanning tree T, let γT​ be its unique graph path from u to v. Assign a signed unit graph path flow jT​ to oriented edges: value +1 in the direction used by γT​, value −1 on the reversed orientation, and zero otherwise. Define its mean
J(x,y)=EjT​(x,y).
(18)
Each jT​ has net divergence one at u, minus one at v and zero elsewhere, so J is a unit flow. The mean spanning-tree path current also satisfies the Kirchhoff cycle law. Although its verification is permitted to be omitted, a short counting argument makes it explicit. Let Fuv​ be the two-component spanning forests separating u and v, write AF​ for the component containing u, and let NT​ be the number of spanning trees. Deleting an edge used by the oriented tree graph path gives one such forest. Conversely adjoining an edge across its two components gives a unique tree with that edge on its u-to-v graph path. Consequently
J(x,y)=NT​1​∑F∈Fuv​​(1AF​​(x)−1AF​​(y))=h(x)−h(y),h(x)=NT​1​∑F∈Fuv​​1AF​​(x).
(19)
The gradient form proves the Kirchhoff cycle law directly and is Ohm's law for unit edge resistance. The Kirchhoff node law then gives Lh=δu​−δv​, and the electrical unit flow is unique: the difference between two such voltages is a harmonic function on a graph and hence constant on the connected finite graph.
If e∈T, its single-edge route is the unique tree graph path from u to v, so jT​(u,v)=1. If e∈/T, that tree graph path does not use e, so jT​(u,v)=0. Therefore
J(u,v)=P(e∈T).
(20)
By Ohm's law, J(u,v)=h(u)−h(v); for a unit terminal current, this voltage difference is exactly the effective resistance between the terminals. We have proved the requested edge-inclusion formula for a uniform spanning tree:
P(e∈T)=Reff​(u,v).​
(21)
Now use linearity of expectation and the fact that every spanning tree has exactly n−1 edges. This gives Foster's theorem:
e∈E∑​Reff​(e)=e∈E∑​P(e∈T)=E∣T∣=n−1.​
(22)
The sums here use unoriented edges exactly once. For unit conductance this is the displayed identity; with conductances ce​ the inclusion probability and summand become ce​Reff​(e).
