Under the corrected orthogonality hypothesis, the preceding quadratic variation calculation gives [X]t​=At​=∫0t​e2Bs​ds. We justify the needed infinite occupation time of one-dimensional Brownian motion, rather than inferring an infinite integral from recurrent visits alone.
Let T0​=0, and recursively let Sn​ be the first exit of B from (−1,1) after Tn​, and Tn+1​ its first return to zero after Sn​. Recurrence of one-dimensional Brownian motion makes these stopping times finite almost surely. The Strong Markov property shows that Dn​=Sn​−Tn​ are independent and identically distributed random variables, each strictly positive. Choose ε>0 with P(Dn​≥ε)>0. The Borel-Cantelli lemma gives infinitely many such durations and hence ∑n​Dn​=∞ almost surely. These intervals are disjoint, and ∣Bs​∣≤1 on each, so
[X]∞​=∫0∞​e2Bs​ds≥e−2n=0∑∞​Dn​=∞almost surely.​

This proves the intended conclusion. Without the orthogonality correction, the printed setup gives instead the more general quadratic variation in the previous part; it does not justify identifying that quantity with A. The proof here is explicitly for the corrected setup.
