Parameterize the initial product normal distribution by η=(m,ρ), using
Xη(0)​=m+diag(eρ1​,…,eρp​)ε,ε∼N(0,Ip​).
(31)
Run the given recursion with independent random variables with uniform distributions to obtain Xη​=Ft​(Xη(0)​,Z1:t​). Because the noise laws do not depend on η, the chain rule and automatic differentiation give reparameterization gradients through the entire simulated trajectory. The variational objective is J(η)=KL(qη​∥π), where qη​=Ktνη​ and π=μ(⋅∣y).
One must account for the output differential entropy, not just optimize E[logπ(Xη​)]. Simulation and differentiability of f do not themselves supply an evaluable qη​. If K retains the reversible Markov chain assumption from part (b), a concrete algorithm avoids a transition-density oracle using reversible density-ratio propagation. Write π=h/Zπ​, with h an evaluable unnormalized posterior density, and define
Aη​(x)=EB∼Kt(x,⋅)​[h(B)νη​(B)​].
(32)
Detailed balance implies
qη​(x)=h(x)Aη​(x),J(η)=logZπ​+E[logAη​(Xη​)].​
(33)
For example, integrate νη​(b)Kt(b,dx) and replace h(b)Kt(b,dx) by h(x)Kt(x,db) to obtain the first identity. This also proves absolute continuity of measures of Ktνη​ with respect to π when νη​≪π. An everywhere finite differentiable log-posterior has positive density, so an ordinary product normal distribution has this domination.
For each outer sample Xη​, independently run M trajectories of length t starting at Xη​, with endpoints Bη,j​=Ft​(Xη​,Z1:t(j)​). These are reverse trajectories because of detailed balance; the same recursion simulates them. Estimate Aη​(Xη​) by
Aη​(Xη​)=M1​∑j=1M​h(Bη,j​)νη​(Bη,j​)​.
(34)
Average logA over an outer batch. Compute it stably by a logarithmic sum of exponentials. Differentiate through both outer and reverse trajectories and through the explicit product-normal probability density function νη​. Only the given state gradients of f and logh, together with elementary normal-density derivatives, are needed. Apply stochastic gradient descent to m and ρ, refreshing the independent noise batches. The logarithmic scale parameters keep all variances positive.
This is nested Monte Carlo optimization of the exact objective in the limit of adequately increasing inner and outer sample sizes. For fixed M, logA is generally biased, even though A is an unbiased estimator. Finite logarithmic moments, suitable uniform integrability, and justified differentiation under the integral sign are needed for consistency and gradient interchange. Under those conditions increasing M and controlling the optimization error yields a justified numerical approximation; nonconvexity prevents a generic guarantee of a global optimum.
There is a real assumption issue: part (c) redefines K without explicitly restating reversibility. The algorithm above uses the reversible interpretation inherited from part (b). For an arbitrary differentiable recursion, one additionally needs access to output densities or a simulatable reverse kernel and regularity of the objective. Differentiability alone is insufficient: f(x,z)=0 is differentiable and, with a standard-normal target, makes Ktν a point mass for t≥1, so every candidate has infinite Kullback-Leibler divergence. Thus a universal finite-objective algorithm cannot be justified solely from the literal differentiability assumptions. An invertible recursion with computable density transformations offers a different sufficient route, but invertibility is not stated here.
