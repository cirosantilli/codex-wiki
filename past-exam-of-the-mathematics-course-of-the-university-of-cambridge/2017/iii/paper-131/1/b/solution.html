A local isometry here is a local diffeomorphism whose pullback metric is the source metric. Use the usual convention that a covering map is surjective. Assume the target is connected and the source is nonempty; this is the connected-manifold setting of the intended assertion. For a disconnected target the precise result is a covering of the union of target components met by the map. For example, inclusion R↪R⊔R is a local isometry from a complete manifold but is not a surjective covering of the whole target.
The correct Hopf-Rinow theorem says that on a connected finite-dimensional Riemannian manifold, completeness for the induced distance is equivalent to geodesic completeness, and to compactness of every closed bounded subset. These conditions imply that every two points are joined by a minimizing geodesic. The last property by itself does not imply completeness: an open Euclidean ball has minimizing segments between every pair but is incomplete.
A local isometry preserves the Levi-Civita connection, by uniqueness of the torsion-free metric connection on each local isometry chart. Hence it preserves affinely parametrized geodesics, and

Suppose M is complete. Given y∈N, join f(x) to y by a minimizing target geodesic using target completeness. The source geodesic whose initial velocity is its inverse image under dfx​ exists for its full parameter interval by source completeness; its image is the target geodesic by uniqueness. Thus f is onto.
Choose a normal ball U=expy​(Br​(0)) with expy​ a diffeomorphism on Br​(0)⊂Ty​N. For every x∈f−1(y) put Ux​=expx​(Br​(0)). Source completeness defines these exponential maps throughout the tangent balls. The displayed exponential identity makes f:Ux​→U a diffeomorphism: injectivity follows from injectivity of the target exponential map, and differentiating the identity shows the source exponential has invertible derivative on the ball, so its image is open.
These sheets are disjoint. If z lay in two, lift the reversed radial target geodesic from f(z) to y, starting at z. Its initial velocity is uniquely fixed by dfz​; uniqueness of the source geodesic forces the two sheet centres to be equal. They exhaust f−1(U): for any point over U, lift that reversed radial geodesic using source completeness, obtaining a centre over y, and then reverse it. Thus U is evenly covered. This proves the complete local isometry is a covering assertion.
Conversely suppose f is a covering. Given any initial tangent vector in M, the corresponding target geodesic extends over all R by target completeness. The path lifting theorem for coverings lifts it over every compact time interval, with uniqueness gluing the lifts to a curve on R. In local covering charts the lift is a geodesic, because the charts are local isometries. It extends the source initial-value geodesic. Therefore M is geodesically complete, and Hopf-Rinow yields metric completeness on each source component. In the stated connected-target setting,
