Let M bound the piecewise Hölder norms, and let B=∥f∥∞​. At scale j, classify a wavelet as bad when its localized support of a function meets a discontinuity, and as good otherwise. Compact support and bounded overlap imply at most C0​K bad wavelets per scale and at most C1​2j total wavelets. A good support lies in a single smooth piece, so the preceding wavelet coefficient decay for Hölder functions gives ∣cj,n​∣≤CM2−j(α+1/2). The permitted bounded-function estimate gives ∣cj,n​∣≤CB2−j/2 for bad coefficients.
Consider a specific N-term approximation: retain all coarse scaling functions and all wavelets through scale J, and also retain the bad coefficients at scales J<j≤L, where L=⌈2αJ⌉. Since α>1/2, L≥J for large J. Its term count is bounded by
NJ​≤C2​2J+C3​K(L−J)+C4​=O(2J+KJ).

∑j>L​∑n bad​∣cj,n​∣2≤CKB2∑j>L​2−j≤C′KB22−L≤C′KB22−2αJ.

Choose J=⌊log2​(N/(2C2′​))⌋ with a fixed sufficiently large C2′​; then the O(KJ) extra terms fit the remaining budget for all sufficiently large N, while 2J is comparable to N. Padding to N terms does not increase the error. The best N-term approximation is at least as accurate as this constructed selection, so
ϵn​(N,f)=∥f−fNnonlin​∥22​=O(N−2α).​

The constant may depend on the finite number of jumps, the piecewise Hölder norms, B and the fixed wavelet family. Values exactly at the jumps are immaterial in L2. The improvement comes from retaining only a bounded number of jump-crossing coefficients at each finer scale, as in best N-term wavelet approximation of piecewise Hölder functions.
