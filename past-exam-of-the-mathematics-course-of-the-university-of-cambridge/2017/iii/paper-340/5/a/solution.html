Write D=∇ and use the real pixelwise inner products. Let D∗ be the adjoint operator, defined by ⟨Du,p⟩=⟨u,D∗p⟩. For each nontrivial grid direction its one-dimensional formula is
(Dx+∗​p1)i,j​=⎩⎨⎧​−p1,j1​,pi−1,j1​−pi,j1​,pN−1,j1​,​i=1,1<i<N,i=N,​

with the analogous formula in j for Dy+∗​p2. Their sum is D∗p; unused components pN,j1​ and pi,N2​ do not contribute. For N=1, D=D∗=0. If discrete divergence is defined, its sign convention is div=−D∗, as in the adjoint of a discrete forward gradient.
Define the compact convex set Pλ​={p∈X2:∣pi,j​∣2​≤λ} and its image C=D∗Pλ​. The image is compact and convex, hence closed, and contains zero. The finite-dimensional Euclidean duality formula λ∣a∣2​=max∣p∣2​≤λ​a⋅p, applied independently at every pixel, gives
λ∥Du∥2,1​=maxp∈Pλ​​⟨Du,p⟩=maxw∈C​⟨u,w⟩=σC​(u).
(37)
Thus the penalty is the support function σC​. Its convex conjugate is the indicator functional of a constraint set ιC​, equal to zero on C and infinity elsewhere. For w∈C, the conjugate supremum is zero; for w∈/C, the separation theorem supplies a direction u with ⟨u,w⟩>σC​(u), and scaling that direction makes the supremum infinite.
The proximal operator is proxσC​​(g)=argminu​{σC​(u)+21​∥u−g∥22​}. It exists uniquely because the objective is continuous, coercive and strictly convex. The Moreau decomposition for a proper closed convex function gives proxσC​​(g)+proxιC​​(g)=g. The second term is the Euclidean projection onto a convex set, so the projection residual for discrete total variation is
u=g−PC​g,C=D∗{p:∣pi,j​∣2​≤λ}.​
(38)
Alternatively, the variational characterization of convex projection says w=PC​g exactly when ⟨g−w,z−w⟩≤0 for every z∈C. Hence w attains the support function at u=g−w, giving w∈∂σC​(u) and the same optimality condition. This supplies a direct verification of the Moreau decomposition step in this instance.
The printed wording needs “the residual after a projection”, rather than “the projection itself”. In general this denoising map is not a projection onto any fixed closed convex set. A metric projection onto a closed convex set is idempotent, while denoising a sufficiently large jump twice shrinks it twice. Concretely, for N=2 and data with rows (0,0) and (6λ,6λ), the minimizer has rows (λ,λ) and (5λ,5λ). Reapplying the map gives rows (2λ,2λ) and (4λ,4λ), so it is not idempotent. These formulas have a global certificate: take p1,j1​=λ, all other dual entries zero, giving D∗p with rows (−λ,−λ) and (λ,λ); it saturates every positive row difference and yields the primal optimality condition. The qualification is therefore not merely a consequence of a restricted two-level ansatz.
