Let B=B(0,R), with R>0. Its indicator function has L2 norm squared πR2 and total variation seminorm on a domain 2πR. On the family u=cχB​, the objective is 2παR∣c∣+21​πR2(c−1)2, whose minimizer is c=(1−2α/R)+​. To prove global optimality, a total variation calibration is needed.
Define the bounded radial vector field
z(x)={x/R,Rx/∣x∣2,​∣x∣≤R,∣x∣>R.​
(34)
It satisfies ∣z∣≤1 and has a continuous normal component across ∂B. Its distributional divergence therefore has no boundary measure, and direct differentiation yields q=divz=(2/R)χB​∈L2. Although z is not compactly supported, multiply it by a smooth radial cutoff equal to one through radius T and zero beyond 2T. The extra divergence has magnitude O(R/T2) on an annulus of area O(T2), hence L2 norm O(R/T). Smoothing the continuous piecewise field gives compactly supported smooth admissible test vector fields with divergences tending to q in L2, preserving the bound ∣z∣≤1. The dual definition therefore gives ⟨q,v⟩≤J(v) for every finite-penalty v, and trivially for all other v.
Moreover ⟨q,χB​⟩=(2/R)πR2=2πR=J(χB​). By the subgradient characterization of an absolutely one-homogeneous functional, q∈∂J(cχB​) for every c>0, and q∈∂J(0). If 0<α<R/2, choose c=1−2α/R; then (g−cχB​)/α=q. If α≥R/2, choose u=0; then g/α=(R/(2α))q belongs to ∂J(0), since multiplying the dual bound by a number in [0,1] preserves it. The previous optimality criterion proves

The quadratic fidelity is strictly convex, so this minimizer is unique, including the threshold α=R/2. The disk retains its radius on the positive branch and disappears on the zero branch; this is total variation denoising of a disk.
