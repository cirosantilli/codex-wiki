We prove mutual genericity for product forcing. Let D∈M[G0​] be a dense subset of Q, and take a forcing name D˙∈M evaluating to D. By the forcing theorem, some p0​∈G0​ forces that D˙ is a dense subset of the canonical forcing name for Q.
In M define
E={(p,q):p⊥p0​} ∪ {(p,q):p≤p0​ and p⊩qˇ​∈D˙}.
(32)
This set is dense in the product forcing order. Given (p,q), the incompatible case is immediate. Otherwise first strengthen p below p0​. The forced density assertion and the existential clause of syntactic forcing supply a further r≤p,p0​ and a ground-model q′≤q with r⊩qˇ​′∈D˙. To justify choosing a ground-model q′, a name forced to lie in Qˇ​ can densely be made equal to some qˇ​′ by the atomic membership clause; strengthen to that equality and use the forced order comparison. Thus (r,q′)∈E lies below (p,q).
The generic filter H meets E. It cannot meet the first part, because its first projection contains p0​ and is directed. Hence there is (p,q)∈H with p∈G0​ and p⊩qˇ​∈D˙. Soundness of the forcing theorem gives q∈D, and the projection gives q∈G1​. Since every such D is met,
G1​ is Q-generic over M[G0​].​
(33)
This establishes the stronger property, rather than merely meeting dense ground-model subsets.
