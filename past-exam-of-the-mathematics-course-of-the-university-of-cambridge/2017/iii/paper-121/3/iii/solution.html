Past exam of the mathematics course of the University of Cambridge / 2017 / iii / Paper 121 / 3 / iii / Solution

 ... Past exam of the mathematics course of the University of Cambridge 2017 iii Paper 121 3 iii
OurBigBook.com Words: 209
We prove power set in a generic extension by bounding possible subnames in the ground model, rather than presupposing the desired power set in M[G]. Let x=τG, and let S={ρ:∃r((ρ,r)∈τ)}. In M form
U={(ρ,p)∈S×P:∃r((ρ,r)∈τ∧p≤r)},B=PM(U).
(25)
Every ν∈B is a forcing name. Its value is a subset of x: an active pair (ρ,p)∈ν has p≤r for some (ρ,r)∈τ, so p∈G implies r∈G and ρG∈x.
Now take any y∈M[G] with y⊆x, and choose a forcing name σ∈M with σG=y. By axiom schema of separation and definability of the syntactic forcing relation,
νσ​={(ρ,p)∈U:p⊩∗ρ∈σ}∈B.
(26)
The atomic membership truth lemma for forcing shows νσG​=y. One inclusion follows immediately from its soundness direction. For the other, if a∈y⊆x, choose (ρ,r)∈τ with r∈G and ρG=a. The truth direction supplies p∈G forcing ρ∈σ; strengthen within G below p and r to obtain an active pair in νσ​ representing a.
Finally the ground-model forcing name
Πτ​={(ν,p):ν∈B, p∈P}
(27)
exists by Axiom schema of replacement and the ground-model power set axiom. Since G is nonempty, all ν∈B contribute their values, and
ΠτG​={y∈M[G]:y⊆τG}=PM[G](x).​
(28)
This set belongs to M[G] by the definition of a generic extension. The construction uses all conditions in the outer pairs and therefore does not require a greatest condition in P.
