Take z upwards and write ζ=z+Wt, so that the unperturbed interface is ζ=0, with carbon dioxide above and water below. Use the sharp-interface, incompressible flow version of Darcy's law, with constant porosity and permeability of a porous medium, no capillary pressure, and effectively unbounded layers. The distinction between Darcy flux and interface speed matters: the base flux is qz​=−ϕW, since the printed W is the speed of the interface. Thus the base pressure gradients satisfy
pj,z0​=kμj​ϕW​−ρj​g,j=c,w.
(1)
The mass density ordering is stabilizing, whereas injection of the less viscous fluid into the more viscous fluid produces the Saffman–Taylor instability.
For linear stability analysis, displace the interface by η=η​eσt+iαx. Taking the divergence of Darcy's law shows that the perturbation pressure satisfies the Laplace equation. Decay away from the interface gives, for aα​=∣α∣>0,
pc′​=Ae−aα​ζeσt+iαx,pw′​=Beaα​ζeσt+iαx.
(2)
The kinematic boundary condition and continuity of normal Darcy flux give
ϕση​=μc​kaα​A​=−μw​kaα​B​.
(3)
Meanwhile pressure continuity, evaluated on the displaced interface, gives
A−B=−η​(pc,z0​−pw,z0​)=η​[kϕW(μw​−μc​)​−g(ρw​−ρc​)].
(4)
Eliminating A,B yields the buoyancy-modified Darcy fingering dispersion relation:
σ(α)=ϕ(μw​+μc​)∣α∣​[ϕW(μw​−μc​)−kg(ρw​−ρc​)].​
(5)
Every nonzero wavenumber decays when W<Wc​, grows when W>Wc​, and is neutral at equality, where
Wc​=ϕ(μw​−μc​)kg(ρw​−ρc​)​.​
(6)
The zero wavenumber is a uniform displacement and is neutral. Without a short-scale regularization such as surface tension, this model has no fastest-growing finite wavenumber: on the unstable side its growth rate increases without bound as ∣α∣ increases. Finite layer depths would change the dispersion relation; none are specified here.
Taking g=9.81ms−2 and a year of 365.25 days, the data give
ϕWc​=3.27×10−6ms−1≃103.2myr−1,Wc​≃ϕ103.2​myr−1.​
(7)
The numerical paragraph supplies no porosity, so it cannot determine a unique numerical interface speed. If W were instead intended to mean Darcy velocity, the numerical critical value would be 103.2myr−1, and the interface would move at W/ϕ. These two velocity conventions must not be conflated.
