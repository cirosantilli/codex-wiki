Let z increase upwards, so the bed is at z=−b(x), the surface is at z=h(x,t), and H=h+b. In the shallow-shelf approximation the incompressible flow has nearly depth-uniform horizontal speed u and vertical strain wz​=−ux​. The Newtonian fluid stress tensor, together with the hydrostatic approximation, then gives
σzz​=−ρg(h−z),σxx​=−ρg(h−z)+4μux​.
(27)
The factor four includes both horizontal extension and the pressure correction required by vertical compression. Integrating horizontal force balance over depth, using zero surface shear and the bed traction, gives the membrane-stress derivative ∂x​(4μHux​) and the gravitational driving force −ρgHhx​. The thin subglacial till layer is idealized by Newtonian till lubrication and undergoes Couette flow, with resisting basal stress λμu/l. Hence force balance and mass conservation yield
4μ∂x​(Hux​)−ρgHhx​−λμlu​=0,Ht​+∂x​(Hu)=0.​
(28)
Since b is fixed, Ht​=ht​. No accumulation or ablation is included.
At the grounding line, the first boundary condition is ice-sheet flotation:
ρHG​=ρw​bG​,HG​=ρρw​​αxG​.​
(29)
For an unbuttressed ice shelf, the second is the extensional stress required to balance the difference between the integrated ice and seawater hydrostatic pressures. The ice contribution is ρgHG2​/2, while the water contribution is ρw​gbG2​/2. On using ice-sheet flotation, their difference is ρg′HG2​/2, with g′=g(1−ρ/ρw​)>0. Equivalently, shelf force balance integrates to 4μHux​−ρg′H2/2=constant; absence of a buttressing force sets this constant to zero. Thus the unbuttressed Newtonian grounding-line stress condition is
4μHG​ux,G​=21​ρg′HG2​,ux,G​=8νg′HG​​,ν=μ/ρ.​
(30)
Here ν is kinematic viscosity; it appears in the printed target formula without an explicit definition.
The ratio of longitudinal stress divergence to till drag is of order 4lH/(λL2). The given small-parameter limit therefore yields the friction-dominated bulk relation
u=−λl​νg​Hhx​,ht​=λl​νg​∂x​(H2hx​).​
(31)
The coefficient depends on the basal lubrication, and hx​<0 gives seaward motion. This is a bulk reduction: longitudinal stress has been neglected in the differential equation but its boundary traction remains specified. A complete uniformly valid approximation close to the grounding line could require a membrane-stress boundary layer. In the remainder, use the pointwise friction closure and retain the shelf stress condition as the boundary data, as requested in the paper.
Differentiate the ice-sheet flotation condition along the moving grounding line:
Ht​+Hx​x˙G​=αρρw​​x˙G​,(αρρw​​−Hx​)x˙G​=Ht​=−uHx​−Hux​.
(32)
Now Hx​=hx​+α. Substitution of the friction-dominated speed and the shelf stress boundary condition gives the friction-dominated grounding-line evolution law:
(αρρw​​−Hx​)x˙G​=λl​νg​Hhx​(hx​+α)−8νg′​H2at x=xG​(t).​
(33)
The coefficient of x˙G​ is the spatial derivative of the flotation deficit, up to sign. If it vanishes, the implicit equation remains the correct compatibility condition but division to determine a finite speed is invalid; tangential contact needs separate analysis.
In a steady state, volume flux per unit width is constant: Hu=q0​. The friction relation gives
hx​=−lgH2λνq0​​,Hx​=α−lgH2λνq0​​.
(34)
Differentiating Hu=q0​ and using ux​=g′H/(8ν) at the grounding line gives
Hq0​​Hx​+8νg′H2​=0,8νg′H5​=q0​(lgλνq0​​−αH2).
(35)
Put β=l/λ, H=H/β and Q=νq0​/(gβ3). Substitution and cancellation of gβ5/ν yield the steady friction-dominated grounding-line thickness relation:
8gg′​H5=Q(Q−αH2).​
(36)
For q0​>0 and a bed deepening seaward (α≥0), the polynomial (g′/8g)H5+αQH2−Q2 is strictly increasing for H>0, starts negative and tends to infinity, so it has exactly one positive root. At that root Q>αH2 and Hx​<0, as required by steady outward extension. The relation belongs to the specified friction closure; it is not a flux law for arbitrary till rheology or arbitrary membrane-stress matching.
