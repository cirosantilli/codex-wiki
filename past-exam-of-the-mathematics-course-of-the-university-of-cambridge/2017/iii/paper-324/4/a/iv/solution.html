Let the ideal two-qubit output before final measurement in quantum measurements be
∣Ω⟩=E23​J(α)2​∣+⟩2​∣+⟩3​.
(28)
The PDF places J(α) only on the upper wire, followed by the Controlled-Z gate and a quantum measurement in the computational basis on each output wire. Measure vertex 0 of the square in the computational basis, obtaining r, then vertex 1 of the surviving path in the fixed equatorial qubit measurement basis at angle α, obtaining s. Put t=r⊕s. The previous part gives E23​X2t​J(α)2​Z3r​∣++⟩.
Commute its Pauli frame through the Controlled-Z gate. Since E23​X2t​=X2t​Z3t​E23​ and E23​ commutes with Z3r​, the actual output is
X2r⊕s​Z3s​∣Ω⟩.
(29)
Finally measure vertices 2,3 in the computational basis, with raw results d2​,d3​. A Pauli X gate flips a computational basis result, whereas a Pauli Z gate changes only its phase. Thus the purely classical correction is
k=d2​⊕r⊕s,l=d3​.​
(30)
This four-cycle graph-state simulation of an entangle-and-measure circuit reproduces the full joint output distribution, not just each marginal. All measurement bases are fixed beforehand, and no physical byproduct correction is necessary. As a check, the ideal Controlled-Z gate is diagonal in the output basis, so P(k,l)=21​cos2(α/2) for k=0 and 21​sin2(α/2) for k=1, independently of l. The measurement procedure gives exactly these probabilities after its classical relabelling.
