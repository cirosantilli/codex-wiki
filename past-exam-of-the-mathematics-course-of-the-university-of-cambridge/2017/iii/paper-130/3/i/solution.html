Past exam of the mathematics course of the University of Cambridge / 2017 / iii / Paper 130 / 3 / i / Solution

Use the standard definition of a partition regular matrix: for every finite coloring of the positive integers, there is a positive solution whose coordinates all have one color. Coordinates may repeat. Clearing denominators lets us assume ai​∈Z∖{0}; multiplication of the row by a nonzero rational number changes neither its zero-sum subsets nor its solutions. We prove both directions of the Rado theorem for one equation directly, without invoking any form of Rado's theorem.
For necessity, choose a prime number p>∑i​∣ai​∣ and use the last nonzero digit coloring: write x=pvp​(x)u, with p∤u, and assign color umodp. Given a monochromatic solution, let v=mini​vp​(xi​), let I={i:vp​(xi​)=v}, and let r=0 be the common color. Divide ∑i​ai​xi​=0 by pv and reduce modulo p. Terms outside I vanish; the others give
r∑i∈I​ai​≡0(modp).
(14)
The nonzero residue r is invertible modulo the prime number p, so p divides ∑i∈I​ai​. This sum has absolute value less than p, forcing it to be zero. The set I is nonempty by its definition.
For sufficiency we first derive the needed Brauer progression theorem from the permitted Van der Waerden theorem. Fix positive integers s,ℓ. We claim that for every number of colors r, a finite integer interval [B(r,s,ℓ)] forces a monochromatic set
{sd, a,a+d,…,a+(ℓ−1)d},a,d>0.
(15)
The case ℓ=1 is immediate by taking a=s and d=1. For r=1 and ℓ≥2, take a=d=1 in an interval of length at least max(s,ℓ). Suppose the claim holds for r−1 and write M=B(r−1,s,ℓ). By the Van der Waerden theorem, some R forces an arithmetic progression of length (ℓ−1)M+1 in [R] with one color q. Work in [max(R,sMR)] so that all needed multiples also lie in the coloring's domain. If a subprogression of length ℓ and step td has std of color q, we are done. Otherwise, for every 1≤t≤M, the initial long arithmetic progression contains such a subprogression, and std avoids color q. The induced finite coloring t↦χ(sdt) of [M] therefore uses at most r−1 colors. The induction hypothesis gives a monochromatic set {se,b,b+e,…,b+(ℓ−1)e} for this induced finite coloring. Multiplying by sd gives the desired configuration in the original coloring, with initial term sdb and step sde. This proves the claim for ℓ≥2; the only use below has ℓ≥3.
Now suppose ∑i∈I​ai​=0 for a nonempty I. Choose i0​∈I, put B=∑i∈/I​ai​, and put s=∣ai0​​∣. If B=0, then ∑i​ai​=0 and any constant positive vector is already a monochromatic solution. Otherwise take L=∣B∣ and apply the proved Brauer progression theorem with ℓ=2L+1. All of sd and a+jd for 0≤j≤2L have one color. Define
xi​=⎩⎨⎧​sd,a+Ld,a+(L−sgn(ai0​​)B)d,​i∈/I,i∈I∖{i0​},i=i0​.​
(16)
Every coordinate is a positive integer in that monochromatic set, since L−sgn(ai0​​)B is either 0 or 2L. Using the zero sum on I gives
∑i​ai​xi​=sBd−ai0​​sgn(ai0​​)Bd=0.
(17)
Thus both directions are proved:
(a1​,…,an​) is partition regular⟺∃∅=I⊆[n]: i∈I∑​ai​=0.​
(18)
For n=1 the right side is impossible, agreeing with the absence of positive solutions for a single nonzero coefficient.
