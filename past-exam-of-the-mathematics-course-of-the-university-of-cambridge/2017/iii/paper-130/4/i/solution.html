Past exam of the mathematics course of the University of Cambridge / 2017 / iii / Paper 130 / 4 / i / Solution

OurBigBook.com Words: 425
Use the product topology on C=[k]Z, with [k] discrete, and the left shift (Lc)(j)=c(j+1). A basic cylinder set specifies finitely many coordinates. This is a compact metric space, and L is a homeomorphism. Write Y={Lnc:n≥0}​ for the forward orbit closure. A minimal point means that Y is a minimal dynamical system, equivalently that every point of Y has a dense forward orbit in Y; it need not be a fixed point.
There is a convention needed in the source: the bounded-gaps property concerns every finite integer interval I, hence every finite word over an alphabet. Literally allowing I=Z would make the displayed condition impossible for finite U, although a constant coloring is a minimal point. Under the standard finite-word interpretation, the property is precisely uniform recurrence.
Suppose first that Y is a minimal dynamical system. Its nonempty compact forward-invariant subset L(Y) must equal Y. As the ambient left shift is invertible, all integer translates of c belong to Y. Let I=[a,b]∩Z have length ℓ=b−a+1, and let V={z∈Y:z∣I​=c∣I​}. This is a nonempty clopen set. Each forward orbit in Y meets V, so {L−nV:n≥0} covers Y. By compactness, finitely many suffice; let D be the largest index in this finite cover. For any integer u, the point Lu−ac lies in Y, so some 0≤n≤D satisfies Lu−a+nc∈V. The prescribed word therefore occurs at positions [u+n,u+n+ℓ−1], entirely inside [u,u+D+ℓ−1]. Taking M=D+ℓ proves the bounded-gaps property for every interval of length at least M.
Conversely suppose c is uniformly recurrent. Every finite word from any integer translate of c occurs arbitrarily far to the right, so every such translate belongs to Y. Moreover, the property that every length-M block contains a specified word of c passes to every z∈Y: a finite block of z is a limit of blocks of forward translates of c, and the finite discrete alphabet forces eventual exact agreement on that block. Given I=[a,b]∩Z, look for its word inside z([a,a+M−1]). An occurrence starts at a+n for some n≥0, so Lnz agrees with c on I. Taking I=[−r,r]∩Z for arbitrarily large r proves that c belongs to the forward orbit closure of every z∈Y. That closure is a closed forward-invariant subset of Y, so it also contains every forward translate of c and hence all of Y. Every forward orbit is therefore dense in Y, proving
c is a minimal point⟺c is uniformly recurrent.​
(21)
The same conclusion holds if orbit closure is defined using all integer iterates: the bounded-gaps condition makes the forward and two-sided closures equal.
