Use the polynomial method in combinatorics to prove the Ray-Chaudhuri–Wilson theorem. For each Ai​ define an intersection polynomial over R,
pi​(x1​,…,xn​)=∏ℓ∈L​(∑a∈Ai​​xa​−ℓ).
(23)
At the characteristic vector of a set Aj​, the inner sum is ∣Ai​∩Aj​∣. Therefore
pi​(χAj​​)=0(i=j),pi​(χAi​​)=∏ℓ∈L​(r−ℓ)=0.
(24)
It follows that the restrictions of p1​,…,pm​ to the uniform layer of the Boolean cube
Ωr​={χA​:A⊆[n], ∣A∣=r}
(25)
have linear independence: evaluating any vanishing linear combination at each χAj​​ forces its jth coefficient to be zero.
Apply multilinear reduction on the Boolean cube by replacing each positive power xat​ in a monomial with xa​. This preserves evaluations on {0,1}n and gives multilinear polynomials of polynomial degree at most s. Counting all monomials of degrees up to s would give only ∑j=0s​(jn​), which is too weak. Instead use homogenisation on a uniform layer.
Because L has s distinct integers in {0,…,r−1}, we have s≤r. For T⊆[n] with ∣T∣=j≤s, write xT​=∏a∈T​xa​, with x∅​=1. On Ωr​,
xT​=(s−jr−j​)1​∑S⊇T∣S∣=s​​xS​.
(26)
Indeed, at χA​ both sides vanish if T⊈A; otherwise exactly (s−jr−j​) summands equal 1. The denominator is positive since j≤s≤r. Thus every monomial of degree at most s, restricted to Ωr​, lies in the span of the (sn​) degree-s monomials. This vector space consequently has dimension at most (sn​). The linear independence already proved now gives
m≤(sn​).​
(27)
No additional condition such as r+s≤n is needed: only the spanning upper bound is used, not independence of all degree-s monomials. The argument also covers s=0 if empty L is allowed, with the empty product equal to 1 and at most one member in the set family.
