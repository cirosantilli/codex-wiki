Choose disjoint subsets K1​,K2​⊆[n] of size k and let R=[n]∖(K1​∪K2​). The two-block extremisers for the cross-Sperner inequality are
A={S⊆[n]:K1​⊆S, S∩K2​=∅},B={S⊆[n]:K1​⊈S, S∩K2​=∅}.
(20)
For A∈A and B∈B, some point of K1​ belongs to A but not B, so A⊈B. Some point of K2​ belongs to B but not A, so B⊈A. Thus these form a cross-Sperner family pair.
An element of A fixes its intersections with both blocks and freely chooses its subset of R. An element of B chooses any proper subset of K1​, any nonempty subset of K2​, and any subset of R. Hence
∣A∣=2n−2k=2−2k2n,∣B∣=(2k−1)22n−2k=(1−2−k)22n.​
(21)
The hypotheses ensure that the blocks exist and both set families are nonempty, including when n=2k. Moreover,
∣A∣​+∣B∣​=(1+(2k−1))2(n−2k)/2=2n/2,
(22)
so this construction attains equality in the Cross-Sperner inequality.
