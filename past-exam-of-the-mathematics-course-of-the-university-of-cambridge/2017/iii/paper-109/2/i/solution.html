The sharp bound form of the Erdős-Ko-Rado theorem is as follows. If 1≤r≤n/2 and F⊆(r[n]​) is an intersecting family, then
∣F∣≤(r−1n−1​).​
(6)
The uniform set family of all r-element subsets containing one fixed point attains the bound. We prove it by the Katona circle method.
First establish the cyclic interval intersection bound. In any fixed cyclic ordering of [n], at most r cyclic intervals of length r can form an intersecting family. If there is a selected interval, rotate the ordering so that it ends at position n and consists of positions n−r+1,…,n. Intervals ending at positions r,…,n−r are disjoint from it and cannot be selected. Of the remaining intervals, one ends at n; the others have endpoints paired as
(j, j+n−r),1≤j≤r−1.
(7)
The two intervals in each pair are disjoint: one wraps around position n and the other lies in the intervening gap, since n≥2r. At most one of each pair belongs to the intersecting family, giving at most 1+(r−1)=r intervals.
Now count pairs (σ,A) where σ is a permutation of [n] placed on labelled cyclic positions and A∈F occupies r consecutive positions. Each permutation contributes at most r pairs by the cyclic interval intersection bound. Each fixed A contributes nr!(n−r)! pairs: choose its starting position and order the elements inside and outside its interval. Since r<n, the starting position is unique for each such placement. Consequently
∣F∣nr!(n−r)!≤rn!,∣F∣≤nr​(rn​)=(r−1n−1​).
(8)
This completes the Erdős-Ko-Rado theorem proof. At n=2r, complementary r-element subsets are the only disjoint pairs, so choosing one member of every complementary pair gives an extremiser. This describes all equality cases at that boundary. The range n≥2r is essential: for n<2r, the entire uniform set family is an intersecting family.
