For a known mean zero, a common sample autocovariance function convention is
γ​n​(h)=n1​∑t=1n−h​Yt​Yt+h​,0≤h<n.
(51)
The weakly stationary process property gives E[Yt​Yt+h​]=γ(h), so
Eγ​n​(h)=(1−nh​)γ(h).​
(52)
The lag-zero estimate is unbiased, but at nonzero lags the divisor n causes a finite-sample multiplicative bias. An alternative convention uses (n−h)−1 instead; for that convention the expectation is exactly γ(h). These are two normalizations of the sample autocovariance function, so the divisor must be specified. Here no estimated-mean centering term occurs because zero is known. Replacing zero by a sample mean would introduce additional bias terms. Extend to negative lags by symmetry; there are no observed lagged pairs for h≥n.
