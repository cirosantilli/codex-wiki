There is a literal domain error in the printed formula. A semivariogram indexed by a signed displacement must satisfy γ(h)=γ(−h), since reversing the two observations leaves the squared increment unchanged. For positive σ2 or τ2, the displayed positive-lag value is nonzero whereas the displayed negative-lag value is zero. Thus the claim on all of R is false as written.
Interpret the argument as a nonnegative distance, or use the even extension of the Gaussian semivariogram. For σ2,τ2≥0 and ϕ>0, the intended model is
γ(h)=τ21{h=0}​+σ2(1−e−ϕ2h2).
(62)
It admits the covariogram
C(h)=σ2e−ϕ2h2+τ21{h=0}​.​
(63)
The Gaussian kernel is a positive-definite kernel, as can be seen from the Fourier transform of a Gaussian: in one dimension
e−ϕ2h2=∫R​eiωh2ϕπ​e−ω2/(4ϕ2)​dω,
(64)
and the nonnegative spectral density makes every finite covariance quadratic form nonnegative. In higher dimensions use the product Gaussian density. Adding independent Gaussian white noise of variance τ2 adds the diagonal nugget covariance. Thus a zero-mean stationary Gaussian random field exists with this covariance, and C(0)−C(h) gives the corrected semivariogram.
For nonzero structured variance, the nugget effect is the right limit τ2 at zero, the sill of a semivariogram is τ2+σ2, and the range of a semivariogram is infinite if defined as the distance at which the sill is exactly reached. A 95-percent practical range solves 1−e−ϕ2h2=0.95:
h0.95​=log20​/ϕ.​
(65)
The scale 1/ϕ is sometimes called the range parameter; it is neither the exact range nor the 95-percent practical range. If σ2=0, there is no nontrivial structured range.
The fact that a semivariogram does not determine stationarity is a further distinction: a semivariogram specifies increment variation, not a unique process or absolute covariance. Adding an independent random constant adds a constant to C without changing γ. More strongly, subtracting W(0) from a stationary field W(s) preserves its semivariogram but generally makes its variance depend on s. The corrected statement is that this semivariogram admits a second-order stationary model, with the displayed covariance using the usual convention C(h)→0 at large distance; it does not force every compatible process to be stationary.
