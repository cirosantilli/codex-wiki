Let K=ρ0​∣u∣2/2 be the kinetic energy per unit volume. Dot the momentum equation with ρ0​u. The Coriolis acceleration does no work because u⋅(ez​×u)=0. Incompressibility turns the advection and pressure terms into divergences, yielding
∂t​K+∇⋅[(K+P)u]=ρ0​Ω2(3x−2qz)ux​.​
(12)
Thus F=(K+P)u is the energy flux and S=ρ0​Ω2(3x−2qz)ux​ is the source in the requested kinetic-energy form.
To decide about conservation of energy, absorb the ordinary radial shearing-sheet tidal potential Φt​=−3Ω2x2/2 into the density. Its material derivative cancels the 3x work term:
∂t​(K+ρ0​Φt​)+∇⋅[(K+P+ρ0​Φt​)u]=−2ρ0​Ω2qzux​.​
(13)
For q=0 this is the usual conservative Jacobi energy in a shearing sheet, subject to vanishing boundary energy flux. For q=0, the extra body force has nonzero curl, ∇×[−2Ω2qzex​]=−2Ω2qey​, so no scalar potential can absorb it. A potential 2Ω2qxz would necessarily introduce a vertical force −2Ω2qxez​, absent from the model. Therefore this vertical-shear model does not generically conserve the standard total mechanical energy: its maintained shear/irradiation background can supply or remove energy. This is the energy balance of the incompressible vertical-shear model.
