Take 0<r1​<r2​ and positive mean kinematic viscosity νˉ. The steady state of the supplied viscous evolution of an accretion disk equation satisfies F′=−2πrS(r). The outer no-flux condition therefore gives
F(r)=2π∫rr2​​vS(v)dv.
(1)
Positive S produces positive inward accretion rate in this sign convention. Set Y=r1/2νˉΣ. In a Keplerian disk, the viscous torque in an accretion disk is proportional to Y, so the zero-torque inner boundary condition gives Y(r1​)=0. Since Y′=F/(6πr1/2), a second integration yields
Σ(r)=3νˉ(r)r1/21​∫r1​r​u−1/2[∫ur2​​vS(v)dv]du.​
(2)
This is the steady viscous disk supplied by a radial source. It does not require νˉ to be independent of radius: the equation directly determines νˉΣ. If viscosity depends on the local thermodynamic state, the displayed formula is an implicit relation completed by a separate closure. For nonnegative integrable S it gives nonnegative surface density of a disk, and the inner accretion rate equals the total supplied mass per unit time, 2π∫r1​r2​​rS(r)dr. Differentiating both nested integrals recovers the flux and the differential equation.
