For parameters a1​,…,an​∈M, set-theoretic absoluteness means agreement of the two membership first-order structures:
(M,∈)⊨φ(a1​,…,an​) ⟺ (N,∈)⊨φ(a1​,…,an​).​
(1)
This must hold for every such parameter tuple. Equivalently, formula relativization to a class produces two formulas φM and φN whose truth values agree on tuples from M. Agreement for parameters only in N∖M is not part of the definition. For a proper class in set theory, this notation is understood separately for each fixed first-order formula, rather than as an unrestricted truth predicate for the entire universe.

A syntactically bounded formula in set theory is built from the atomic first-order formulas u=v and u∈v using Boolean operations and bounded quantifiers ∃u∈v and ∀u∈v. These abbreviate ∃u(u∈v∧⋯) and ∀u(u∈v⇒⋯), with u different from the bounding variable v.
The superscript in the question allows provable equivalence: a ZF-equivalent bounded formula is any first-order formula φ(v) for which some syntactically bounded formula in set theory ψ(v) satisfies
ZF⊢∀v(φ(v)↔ψ(v)).​
(2)
Thus Δ0ZF​ is the class of first-order formulas equivalent over ZF to a syntactic Δ0​ first-order formula. If a convention uses Δ0ZF​ just for syntactically bounded set-theoretic first-order formulas, it is the smaller syntactic class; the subsequent set-theoretic absoluteness proof works for both conventions.

The Von Neumann hierarchy is the following transfinite recursion on the ordinals:
V0​=∅,Vα+1​=P(Vα​),Vλ​=β<λ⋃​Vβ​ for nonzero limit λ.​
(3)
Here P denotes the full power set. The levels are increasing transitive sets, and the construction is continuous at limit ordinals. Transfinite induction proves these properties: transitivity at a successor follows because a member of a subset of Vα​ already belongs to Vα​⊆P(Vα​); at a limit it follows from the set union of increasing transitive sets. In ZF, Axiom of foundation and Axiom schema of replacement ensure that every set belongs to some Vα​. In terms of the rank of a set, x∈Vα​ exactly when rank(x)<α.

First prove set-theoretic absoluteness for syntactically bounded formulas in set theory by induction on their construction. Atomic equality and membership use the actual equality and membership relations in both transitive classes. Boolean operations preserve agreement.
For the bounded existential step, take a∈M and suppose N⊨∃u∈aψ(u,b). Its witness c is an actual member of a. Since M is a transitive class, c∈M; the induction hypothesis then gives M⊨ψ(c,b). The converse uses the same witness in N. The bounded universal step follows similarly because both structures quantify over exactly the actual members of a, or follows by negation from the existential step. Hence
ψM(b)↔ψN(b)for every syntactic Δ0​ formula and b∈M.​
(4)
No ZF axioms are needed for this syntactic statement beyond the ambient meaning of a transitive class. For a ZF-equivalent bounded formula φ, choose a bounded equivalent ψ. The assumption that both structures model ZF gives φM↔ψM and φN↔ψN. Combining these with the displayed equivalence proves the asserted set-theoretic absoluteness of Δ0ZF​.

The essential fact is absoluteness of cardinalhood in limit ranks. Let α≤β be limit ordinals and x∈Vα​. Being an ordinal is absolute between these transitive sets, so a non-ordinal is a cardinal number in neither structure. For an ordinal δ∈Vα​, we have δ<α.
If δ is not an ambient cardinal number, there is an ordinal γ<δ and a bijection f:γ→δ. With Kuratowski ordered pairs, its graph has rank of a set at most δ+3. Since α is a limit ordinal, δ+3<α, so f∈Vα​. The assertion that f is such a bijection is a bounded formula in set theory and is therefore evaluated correctly by each rank. Conversely, any internal bijection witnessing failure of cardinal number status is an actual one by the same set-theoretic absoluteness. Consequently
Vα​⊨“δ is a cardinal” ⟺ δ is an ambient cardinal ⟺ Vβ​⊨“δ is a cardinal”.​
(5)
Unlike arbitrary transitive models, these rank-initial structures contain every sufficiently low-rank witness. That witness availability is what upgrades the usual downward absoluteness of cardinalhood to agreement in both directions.

Use the standard convention that a strongly inaccessible cardinal is uncountable, regular and strong limit. The printed explanatory definition omits uncountability: literally it also admits κ=ω, for which no finite cardinal is a worldly cardinal. Thus that omission makes the requested conclusion false under the literal abbreviated definition. The proof below applies to the usual, intended strongly inaccessible cardinal convention κ>ω.
First ∣Vγ​∣<κ for every γ<κ. At successor stages this follows from the strong limit cardinal property, and at limits from the regular cardinal property. Consequently Vκ​⊨ZFC. In particular, for Axiom schema of replacement, a domain a∈Vκ​ has size less than κ, so its functional image has fewer than κ elements, so the supremum of the rank of a set over its elements is below κ by regular cardinal structure. All other axioms, including axiom of choice, have their witnesses at bounded ranks below κ; uncountability supplies axiom of infinity.
Enumerate the first-order formulas as φ0​,φ1​,…. For each finite collection, reflection inside the set structure Vκ​ gives a closed unbounded subset of κ of agreeing ranks. To see why the witness bounds stay below κ, there are fewer than κ parameter tuples at each Vγ​, and the supremum of their least witness ranks remains below κ by regular cardinal structure. Closing under these bounds and taking increasing countable limits gives the usual reflection theorem for definable hierarchies argument. The countable intersection of these closed unbounded subsets is still closed unbounded because κ is regular and uncountable. At each resulting α,
(Vα​,∈)≺(Vκ​,∈),Vα​⊨ZFC.
(6)
The infinite cardinal numbers below κ also form a closed unbounded subset: they are unbounded because λ+≤2λ<κ for infinite λ<κ, and a supremum of increasing cardinal numbers is a cardinal number. Intersect the two closed unbounded subsets. Every member of the intersection is a worldly cardinal, and an unbounded subset of a regular cardinal κ has size κ. Hence
∣{α<κ:α is a cardinal and Vα​⊨ZFC}∣=κ.​
(7)
In fact this proves the stronger worldly cardinals below an inaccessible cardinal result that these worldly cardinals contain a closed unbounded subset of κ.

A definable continuous hierarchy is a definable class function α↦Hα​ on the ordinals, with set-valued levels, such that Hα​⊆Hβ​ for α<β and
Hλ​=⋃α<λ​Hα​
(8)
for nonzero limit ordinals. Its union is a definable class in set theory H. Often the definition additionally requires every level to be a transitive set; the following statement also works without that requirement.
The reflection theorem for definable hierarchies says that for any finite collection F of first-order formulas, there is a closed unbounded class of ordinals α such that, for every φ∈F and every parameter tuple from Hα​,
(Hα​,∈)⊨φ(a) ⟺ (H,∈)⊨φ(a).​
(9)
A useful justification is the witness-closure proof. Close F under subformulas. At each level and for each existential subformula, bound the least level containing a witness for each parameter tuple for which a witness exists in H. Axiom schema of replacement bounds these indices. Iterating the finitely many bounds through ω produces a limit level containing all required witnesses. Induction on the first-order formulas, equivalently the finite-formula version of the Tarski-Vaught test, yields agreement. Continuity gives closedness of the reflecting class, and starting above any prescribed ordinal gives unboundedness. This is finite reflection, not a claim that all first-order formulas reflect simultaneously in an arbitrary hierarchy.

Use relative constructible level recognition. The finite coding conditions matter here: merely writing “every set is relatively constructible” inside an arbitrary transitive set does not ensure that its computations are correct.
Let A be a single finite conjunction expressing finite relation closure for set-theoretic coding. Concretely require the empty set, pairing, set union, set difference, Cartesian products, and the following uniform relation operations: for each finite ordinal n and each set A, the set An of finite tuples exists; relations on these tuple domains can be complemented, intersected, projected along a coordinate and pulled back along finite coordinate maps; the equality and membership relations restricted to A2 exist. These are finitely many first-order closure assertions with n quantified, not an axiom schema. Each operation is specified by its usual elementwise membership equivalence. Transitivity makes the operations correct externally. Finite tuple domains are also correct: every individual finite tuple is already present by pairing and union.
These conditions make satisfaction for a set structure absolute. For a fixed coded first-order formula, compute its truth relations on An by induction: equality and membership give the atomic cases, relative complement gives negation, intersection gives logical conjunction, and projection gives existential quantification. All required truth tables exist by A and agree with the actual ones. Thus the first-order assertion B=Def(A) is absolute whenever A,B belong to a transitive structure satisfying A: require that each member of B has such a finite formula-and-parameter definition, and that each coded definition contributes a member of B. Codes are finite objects; no truth predicate for the ambient universe is being used.
Write Sx​(ξ,B) for the coded relative constructible stage assertion: ξ is an ordinal and there is a function f with domain ξ+1, with f(0)=x, f(η+1)=Def(f(η)), f(λ)=⋃η<λ​f(η) at nonzero limit ordinals, and f(ξ)=B. Under A, any such internal code is correct by transfinite induction. Restrictions of a code to shorter domains exist by the relation operations. Define the one-free-variable first-order formula
Φ(x):=​A ∧ x is transitive∧ ∀y∃ξ∃B(Sx​(ξ,B)∧y∈B)∧ ∀ξ∀B(Sx​(ξ,B)⇒∃η∃C(ξ<η∧Sx​(η,C))).​​
(12)
All displayed abbreviations expand into first-order formulas of the membership language.
Suppose M is transitive, X∈M, and M⊨Φ(X). Let I be the externally defined set of indices of stage codes in M. It contains 0, is downward closed by restricting codes, and has no largest member by the last conjunct. Thus I is a nonzero limit ordinal α. Correctness of stage codes gives Lξ​(X)∈M for ξ<α, hence Lξ​(X)⊆M by transitivity. Conversely the exhaustion conjunct puts every y∈M in some such level. Therefore
M=⋃ξ<α​Lξ​(X)=Lα​(X).
(13)
For the converse, every Lα​(X) with nonzero limit α satisfies A: each listed operation on parameters from one level is definable at finitely many later levels, still below α. Moreover stage histories appear below every limit constructible level. Here is the essential limit step of that lemma. If the histories fη​ for η<λ are available in Lλ​(X), the correct predicate SX​ defines their graph of endpoints as a subset of Lλ​(X). No endpoint Lη​(X) with η≥λ can belong to Lλ​(X), because Lλ​(X)⊆Lη​(X) would then give Lη​(X)∈Lη​(X), contradicting Axiom of foundation. The defined graph therefore has domain exactly λ. Adjoining its final pair (λ,Lλ​(X)) takes finitely many more stages. Together with finite successor extensions, this proves by transfinite induction that every fξ​ belongs to Lξ+k​(X) for some finite k.
Thus all histories for ξ<α belong to Lα​(X). Exhaustion follows from continuity, and ξ+1<α supplies a larger represented stage. Hence Lα​(X)⊨Φ(X), proving both directions without assuming ZF for the arbitrary input structure M.

A suitable relative condensation lemma fixes the base pointwise. Let α be a nonzero limit ordinal, and let Y≺(Lα​(X),∈) be an elementary substructure with
X∪{X}⊆Y.
(14)
Then the Mostowski collapse theorem gives an isomorphism π:Y→T onto a transitive set, and
T=Lβ​(X)for a nonzero limit ordinal β≤α,π↾X=idX​.​
(15)
For the collapse to apply, the restricted membership relation is well-founded because it is actual membership. It is extensional: if two members of Y differ, an element distinguishing them in Lα​(X) can be chosen in Y by elementary substructure structure. Since X is transitive and all its members are in Y, transfinite induction on their rank of a set shows that π fixes every member of X, and then π(X)=X.
By the preceding relative constructible level recognition, Lα​(X)⊨Φ(X). Elementary substructure agreement transfers this to Y, and the collapse isomorphism transfers it to T. Thus T⊨Φ(X) and T=Lβ​(X) for a nonzero limit ordinal β.
For the bound, let ρ=X∩Ord. The relative constructible hierarchy has ordinal height Lγ​(X)∩Ord=ρ+γ: at a successor the definable power set adds exactly the previous ordinal height as a new ordinal, and at limits take unions. The order type of Y∩Ord is at most ρ+α, so ρ+β≤ρ+α; strict increase of ordinal addition in the right argument gives β≤α. The requirement X⊆Y is essential to this version: merely having X∈Y need not make the collapse fix the base.

The assumed beth number equality ℶ1​=ℵ2​ means 2ℵ0​=ℵ2​ in the ambient universe. Put W=L(X) with X=P(ω), using the relative constructible hierarchy defined above.
The base X is transitive because every member of a member of X is a finite ordinal, hence itself a subset of ω. Moreover X⊆W and X∈W, and W is an inner model of ZF. The latter standard fact follows from the hierarchy and reflection: bounded-rank witnesses give pairing and union; reflection gives separation and replacement; ambient replacement bounds the stages of all relatively constructible subsets of any fixed set, giving power set. It does not require axiom of choice inside W. Since no new subsets of ω can appear in an inner class,
PW(ω)=X=P(ω).
(16)
Since inner models with all reals preserve omega-one, it suffices to check that their real-code argument applies to W. Every ambient countably infinite ordinal has a well-order code on ω, coded by a subset of ω in X. Finite ordinals and ω are already shared. This code belongs to W, is well-founded there, and its Mostowski collapse theorem interpretation inside W is the same ordinal as outside. Hence the ordinal is countable in W. Conversely, any countability witness in W remains a witness in the ambient universe. Thus ω1W​=ω1​.
If W satisfied the Continuum hypothesis in its usual well-orderable formulation, it would contain a bijection from ω1W​ onto its power set of ω. The same bijection would exist externally from ω1​ onto X, contradicting ∣X∣=ℵ2​. Therefore
L(P(ω))⊨¬CH.​
(17)
This proof uses agreement on all reals and on ω1​, not an unwarranted assertion that L(X) satisfies axiom of choice or computes every higher cardinal number correctly.

Use standard notation for forcing: q≤p means that q is stronger. A generic filter is nonempty, upward closed and downward directed, and meets every dense subset of a forcing order in the ground model. Names below are forcing names in M, with pairs ordered as (name,condition).
The semantic forcing relation is
p⊩φ(τ1​,…,τn​) ⟺ for every M-generic K∋p, M[K]⊨φ(τ1K​,…,τnK​).​
(18)
Here τK=val(τ,K) is the evaluation of a forcing name. Countability of M supplies such generic filters through every condition by the Rasiowa–Sikorski lemma.
Define the syntactic forcing relation by mutual well-founded recursion on the forcing names for the atomic cases, then induction on the first-order formula. Its membership clause is
p⊩∗σ∈τ ⟺ ∀q≤p ∃s≤q ∃(ρ,r)∈τ (s≤r ∧ s⊩∗σ=ρ).
(19)
For equality, first abbreviate
p⊩∗σ⊆τ ⟺ ∀(ρ,r)∈σ ∀q(q≤p∧q≤r⇒q⊩∗ρ∈τ),
(20)
and set p⊩∗σ=τ exactly when both inclusions hold. Each recursive call lowers the rank of at least one forcing name without raising the other. This makes the mutual recursion well-founded.
For a basis of connectives consisting of logical conjunction, negation and existential quantification, the remaining clauses are
p⊩∗(φ∧ψ)p⊩∗¬φp⊩∗∃vφ(v)​⟺(p⊩∗φ ∧ p⊩∗ψ),⟺no q≤p satisfies q⊩∗φ,⟺∀q≤p ∃s≤q ∃ρ∈MP (s⊩∗φ(ρ)).​
(21)
The last line is the existential clause of syntactic forcing; witnesses need only occur densely, rather than be forced by p itself with one preselected name. Other connectives are defined by logical abbreviations. The recursion gives a definable relation inside M for each fixed first-order formula, with quantification over the class of its names. If P has no greatest element, use canonical forcing names aˇ={(bˇ,p):b∈a, p∈P}, which still evaluate to a. It also proves monotonicity: strengthening a condition preserves what it forces. The forcing theorem identifies the two relations and supplies the truth lemma.

A forcing atom is a condition p∈P such that any two strengthenings of p are compatible forcing conditions:
p is an atom ⟺ ∀q,r≤p ∃s∈P (s≤q,r).
(34)

∀p∈P ∃q,r≤p (q⊥r).​
(35)
The meaning of q⊥r is that there is no common stronger condition. For an arbitrary partial order, an atom need not be a minimal element: the definition concerns compatibility below it. This distinction prevents a minimal-element definition from misclassifying an order with descending but mutually compatible conditions.

The two-argument version of Fn forcing consists of finite partial functions:
Fn(I,J)={p:p is a function, domp⊆I, ranp⊆J, ∣domp∣<ω},q≤p ⟺ q⊇p.​
(36)
Thus a stronger condition specifies more values. The empty partial function is the greatest condition. Two conditions are compatible forcing conditions exactly when they agree on their common domain; if they do, their set union is a common strengthening. This is Fn(I,J,ω) in the three-argument convention for Fn forcing. If I or J is empty, only the empty condition exists. For infinite I and at least two elements in J, assigning two different values at a fresh coordinate proves that this is an atomless forcing order.

Suppose, towards a contradiction, that G∈M. Then D=P∖G is a ground-model set by axiom schema of separation. It is dense. If p∈/G, it already lies in D. If p∈G, atomless forcing order structure gives incompatible forcing conditions q,r≤p. Both cannot belong to the directed filter G, so at least one is a strengthening of p in D.
All compatibility and extension quantifiers range over the same ground-model set P, so this density argument is also valid internally in the transitive model M. Genericity requires G∩D=∅, contradicting the definition of D. Therefore a generic filter for an atomless order is new:
G∈/M.​
(37)
The assumption is essential: a forcing atom determines a ground-model generic filter. For an atom p, the conditions compatible with p form a generic filter Gp​∈M: two such conditions have strengthenings below p, which have a common strengthening by the atom property; every dense set has a member below p. In a general partial order, Gp​ need not be the principal filter above p.

Dξ,n​={p:(ξ,n)∈domp}

is dense: add a value at that coordinate if necessary. The generic filter meets all these sets, making F:κ×ω→2 total.
For distinct ξ,η<κ, the ground-model set
Dξ,η​={p:∃n ((ξ,n),(η,n)∈domp and p(ξ,n)=p(η,n))}
(39)
is dense. Choose n fresh for both rows of a finite condition and assign 0 and 1 there. Genericity therefore makes the rows pairwise different. The generic coordinate reals for finite-function forcing are cξ​={n∈ω:F(ξ,n)=1}, and in the generic extension
ξ⟼cξ​ is an injection ℵ2M​↪PM[G](ω).​

The Rasiowa–Sikorski lemma constructs a generic filter Gi​ over each Mi​. Every resulting generic extension remains a countable transitive model of ZFC: there are only countably many ground-model names externally. The order P has the same elements and ordering at every stage, and atomless forcing order structure is absolute because all its quantifiers are bounded to P. Hence the generic filter for an atomless order is new result gives Gi​∈/Mi​ for every i.
The increasing union N=⋃i<ω​Mi​ is transitive and contains P. If it satisfied Axiom of power set for P, there would be a set A∈N with
N⊨∀z(z⊆P↔z∈A).
(41)
Choose i with A∈Mi​. The next-stage generic filter Gi​ belongs to Mi+1​⊆N and is an actual subset of P. This subset assertion is absolute for the transitive set N, so Gi​∈A. Transitivity of Mi​ and A∈Mi​ then give Gi​∈Mi​, a contradiction. Thus the power-set failure in an increasing union of generic extensions occurs already at the fixed ground-model order:
N⊨PowerSetbecause no PN(P) belongs to N.​
(42)
The stages form an increasing chain, not an elementary chain, so the elementary chain theorem does not assert ZFC for their union.
