The printed formula is not a consistent spatial discretization of the stated PDE. The original PDF genuinely has 1/Δx, rather than 1/(Δx)2. Writing h=Δx, the Taylor expansion gives
h−1(u(x−h)−2u(x)+u(x+h))=huxx​(x)+O(h3),
(21)
so it approximates a diffusion coefficient tending to zero. Moreover, m=1,…,M and h=1/(M+1) cover (0,1), whereas the given Dirichlet boundary conditions belong to (−1,1). No value at the interior location x=0 was prescribed. These are actual source defects, not repairs justified by the TeX conversion.
A conditional analysis of the literal ordinary differential equation system is still possible. If it is closed by imposing u0​=uM+1​=0 on the displayed grid, the negative Dirichlet discrete Laplacian has positive frequencies squared
κj​(h)=h4​sin22(M+1)jπ​,j=1,…,M,
(22)
and each normal mode obeys qj′′​=(α−κj​)qj​. For a fixed mesh, every solution is bounded for all time exactly when
α<κ1​(h)=h4​sin22πh​​.
(23)
Equality produces a linearly growing normal mode for nonzero modal initial velocity; above the threshold there is an exponentially growing normal mode. Since κ1​(h)∼π2h→0, any 0<α<π2/4 gives bounded continuous solutions but unbounded literal discrete solutions on sufficiently fine meshes. For example, α=1, M=99 gives κ1​<1, and a growing first sine normal mode. At α=0, every fixed mesh is bounded for all time, but unit first-mode initial velocity has maximal displacement 1/κ1​(h)​, which diverges on mesh refinement. For uniform-in-mesh, all-time displacement bounds for arbitrary bounded displacement/velocity data in the mesh-weighted L2 norm, the literal closed system therefore requires α<0.
This differs from finite-time stability of a numerical method, governed here by displacement stability of a symmetric semidiscrete wave equation. For the conditional closure above, diagonalization of a matrix gives, with α+​=max(α,0),
∥U(t)∥h​≤eα+​​t(∥U(0)∥h​+t∥U′(0)∥h​),∥U∥h2​=h∑m​∣Um​∣2.
(24)
Oscillatory modes use ∣sin(ωt)/ω∣≤t, including its value t at ω=0; growing modes use sinh(βt)/β≤teβt. Thus the displacement map is mesh-uniformly bounded on each fixed finite time interval for every fixed real α. This is a legitimate finite-time displacement stability estimate, but neither consistency of a numerical method nor the all-time conclusion of part (i). It does not assert a velocity bound in the unweighted displacement norm.
If the intended method instead uses h−2 and the full interval, take interior indices m=−M,…,M, h=1/(M+1), with zeros at m=±(M+1). Its first positive discrete eigenvalue is
