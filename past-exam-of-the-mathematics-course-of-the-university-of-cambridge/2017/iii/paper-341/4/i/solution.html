The original PDF has a spatial shift um+2n+2​, so the printed scheme is not the usual second-order BDF discretization. The PDF does have −2umn+2​ on the right; the TeX aid loses that factor two, and that transcription must not be used.
Let h=Δx, k=Δt, and expand an exact smooth heat equation solution about (xm​,tn+2​). Relative to the standard unshifted backward differentiation formula, the printed newest value adds
u(xm​+2h,t)−u(xm​,t)=2hux​+2h2uxx​+O(h3).
(26)
Dividing the raw residual by 2k/3, its expansion is
ut​−uxx​+k3h​ux​+k3h2​uxx​−3k2​uttt​−12h2​uxxxx​+O(k3+h4+h3/k).
(27)
In particular, for the usual refinement k=μh2 with fixed μ>0, the term 3ux​/(μh) generally diverges. A concrete smooth counterexample is u=e−π2t/4cos(πx/2), which satisfies the Dirichlet boundary conditions and has ux​=0 at x=1/2. Thus the printed scheme has no consistent order under fixed-μ refinement. Its normalized shift error is O(h/k), so one could only investigate a special coupled limit such as h=o(k) after specifying a valid boundary closure; it has no standard joint second-order accuracy.
For the likely intended correction umn+2​, the extra shift terms disappear. The normalized local truncation error is
−3k2​uttt​−12h2​uxxxx​+O(k3+h4).
(28)
With a stable, suitably accurate starter, the corrected backward differentiation formula combined with the second-order central difference therefore has
order two in time and order two in space​.
(29)
This is a correction to the printed method, not a claim about it.
