Put h=∣u−v∣, q=Q(u,v) and h0​=∣u0​−v0​∣. The mean value theorem gives ∣q∣≤Mh on the bounded state range. Fix a final time t and let L(τ)=a−M(t−τ) and R(τ)=b+M(t−τ). Choose a smooth increasing function ρ equal to zero on (−∞,0] and one on [1,∞). For small δ>0, define
Wδ​(τ,x)=ρ(δx−L(τ)​)ρ(δR(τ)−x​).
(55)
Its two nonnegative derivative contributions imply (Wδ​)τ​+M∣(Wδ​)x​∣≤0. Therefore h(Wδ​)τ​+q(Wδ​)x​≤0.
Use the nonnegative test φ(τ,x)=ηϵ​(τ)Wδ​(τ,x), where ηϵ​(0)=1, it is one up to just before s, decreases smoothly to zero near s, and remains zero afterward. The Kato inequality for scalar conservation laws gives
−∫ηϵ′​(τ)∫hWδ​dxdτ≤∫h0​(x)Wδ​(0,x)dx,
(56)
because the other interior contribution is nonpositive. At Lebesgue times s, the temporal approximate identity tends to ∫h(s)Wδ​(s); take a countable sequence δ↓0 and use dominated convergence on the bounded cone. This yields
∫a−M(t−s)b+M(t−s)​∣u(s,x)−v(s,x)∣dx≤∫a−Mtb+Mt​∣u0​(x)−v0​(x)∣dx​
(57)
for almost every s∈[0,t]. This is local L1 contraction for scalar conservation laws. It uses only local integrability, so the bounded data need not have finite global L1 norm. If M=0, the flux difference is zero and the same proof uses a stationary interval.
