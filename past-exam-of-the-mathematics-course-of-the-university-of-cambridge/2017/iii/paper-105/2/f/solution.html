Here is a direct use of the spherical representation that also avoids estimating separate coordinate derivatives at the poles. Put A=ΔS2​, T=∂r2​+2r−1∂r​, and write dω for unit-sphere area. Then f=Tu+r−2Au. Expanding its squared norm with the physical measure gives
∥f∥22​=∥Tu∥22​+∥r−2Au∥22​+2∫1/22​∫S2​(Tu)(Au)dωdr.
(27)
On each boundary sphere u=0, so every angular derivative, including Au and ∇S​u, is zero there. Radial and angular integration by parts consequently give
∫(Tu)(Au)=∫∣∇S​ur​∣2−∫r−2∣∇S​u∣2.
(28)
Indeed the urr​ term gives ∫∣∇S​ur​∣2 with boundary remainder [∫ur​Au]=0, while the 2ur​/r term is −∫r−1∂r​∣∇S​u∣2=−∫r−2∣∇S​u∣2, its boundary remainder also being zero. Thus
∥Tu∥22​+∥r−2Au∥22​+2∫∣∇S​ur​∣2=∥f∥22​+2∫r−2∣∇S​u∣2≤C∥f∥22​,
(29)
where the last step uses the preceding H1 energy estimate and r≥1/2. All unmarked integrals in these two identities are with drdω.
To control every angular second derivative, the spherical Hessian identity is
∫S2​∣∇S2​w∣2=∫S2​(Aw)2−∫S2​∣∇S​w∣2.
(30)
It follows by integrating the derivative-commutation identity ∇a∇a​∇b​w=∇b​Aw+Ricb​c∇c​w and using Ric=h on the unit sphere. There is no sphere boundary. Hence the preceding bound controls ∇S2​u, ∇S​ur​, and urr​=Tu−2ur​/r in their appropriate weighted norms.
In an orthonormal polar frame the Cartesian Hessian matrix has components
D2u(er​,er​)=urr​,D2u(er​,eA​)=r−1(∇S​ur​)A​−r−2(∇S​u)A​,
(31)
and D2u(eA​,eB​)=r−2(∇S2​u)AB​+r−1ur​δAB​. Since r is bounded above and below, these bounds plus the first-order estimate prove
∥u∥H2(Ω)​≤C∥f∥2​.​
(32)
This is the direct spherical-shell H2 estimate. Intrinsic angular integration combines the suggested angular tests, with no spurious boundary at the polar coordinate singularities.
