Fix the coefficient normal form and a positive analyticity exponent ζ0​ once and for all. If g is a polynomial, its pullback g~​ε​=g(x~−y~​2+ε,y~​) is again a polynomial, with degree bounded independently of ε. On a fixed closed coordinate ball, its finitely many nonzero derivatives are uniformly bounded as the parameter ranges over a fixed compact small interval. Hence there is a finite Ag​≥1 such that
∥∂β(g~​ε​/Ag​)∥∞​≤β!ζ0∣β∣​for every β and every small ε.
(15)
All higher derivatives vanish, so arbitrarily high polynomial degree causes no failure of this bound; it only changes Ag​.
The normalized forcing belongs to one common majorant class. The Cauchy-Kovalevskaya theorem therefore gives a radius r∗​>0 independent of g. The problem is linear with zero data, so multiplying the normalized solution by Ag​ solves the original forcing without changing its radius. Choose one ε with the cap inside this radius. Thus
one ε>0 works for every polynomial g.​
(16)
This is the uniform Cauchy radius for polynomial forcing. It asserts a common domain, not a uniform solution-amplitude bound over all polynomials.
