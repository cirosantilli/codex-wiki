At the stellar surface boundary condition, match the interior to a stellar atmosphere, rather than setting the temperature and pressure to zero at an arbitrary radius. At a photosphere of radius R, the enclosed mass is M, the luminosity is L, and the outward radiative flux defines the effective temperature through F=L/(4πR2)=σSB​Teff4​. With no external illumination the incoming specific intensity satisfies Iν​(0,μ)=0 for μ<0. A deeper boundary is matched to the nearly isotropic radiative diffusion field. A grey atmosphere commonly matches near τ=2/3; under the Eddington surface boundary condition this gives T=Teff​. For roughly constant gravity and opacity, the total pressure rises by gτ/κ relative to its outer boundary value; if radiation supplies appreciable support, the gas-pressure gradient uses the effective gravity g−κF/c instead.
Take z increasing outward and μ the outward direction cosine. If jν​ is emission per unit path length and solid angle, the radiative transfer equation is
μ∂z∂Iν​​=jν​−ρκν​Iν​.
(1)
Define inward-increasing optical depth by dτν​=−ρκν​dz and the source function by Sν​=jν​/(ρκν​). Then μ∂τν​​Iν​=Iν​−Sν​. This states the emission-coefficient convention; if jν​ is defined per unit mass instead, the emission term is ρjν​.
For frequency-integrated intensity, introduce the radiation-field moments
J=21​∫−11​Idμ,H=21​∫−11​μIdμ,K=21​∫−11​μ2Idμ,F=4πH,cPrad​=4πK.
(2)
The first angular approximation writes I≃J+3μH. Its odd part does not contribute to K, so
K=3J​,cPrad​=34π​J.​
(3)
This is the Eddington closure approximation, not an exact description of the escaping angular distribution. An approximate surface condition takes the outgoing hemisphere to have direction-independent intensity I0​ and the incoming hemisphere to be dark. Its moments give F=πI0​ and J(0)=I0​/2=F/(2π). Integrating the moment equations with the closure then gives J=3F(τ+2/3)/(4π); in thermal equilibrium, J=arad​cT4/(4π) gives T4=(3/4)Teff4​(τ+2/3). This derives the usual approximate photospheric matching condition rather than imposing a zero surface temperature. For conservative coherent isotropic scattering, S=J. Integrating the transfer equation gives dH/dτ=0 and dK/dτ=H, the moment conditions for radiative equilibrium.
The exponential ansatz in the PDF has a genuine consistency problem. Compute its moments before making any approximation. For a nonsingular real intensity on all −1≤μ≤1, take ∣ϵ∣<1 and define
A0​=21​∫−11​1+ϵμdμ​=ϵatanhϵ​,A1​=ϵ1−A0​​,A2​=ϵ2A0​−1​.
(4)
The continuous zero limits are A0​=1, A1​=0, A2​=1/3. Direct integration yields
J=α(β+τ+γA0​e−δτ),F(τ)=4πα(31​+γA1​e−δτ),​
(5)
cPrad​=4πα(3β+τ​+γA2​e−δτ).​
(6)
For a decaying nontrivial correction, δ>0 and γ=0. Exact constant flux then requires A1​=0. But atanhϵ/ϵ>1 for every nonzero real ∣ϵ∣<1, so A1​=0 forces ϵ=0. Thus exact radiative equilibrium cannot imply a small nonzero ϵ for this ansatz.
Substitution into the full transfer equation makes the obstruction sharper. The exponential coefficient must satisfy, for every μ,
−δμ=1−A0​(1+ϵμ).
(7)
Comparing the constant and linear coefficients gives A0​=1 and δ=A0​ϵ=ϵ. Hence regular real coefficients require δ=ϵ=0, eliminating any decaying correction. This is the obstruction to a single smooth exponential mode in conservative grey transfer. There is no exact nontrivial real solution of the stipulated form with a small nonzero decay parameter. Choosing, for example, ϵ=δ=0.1 and γ=0 supplies an explicit counterexample to the claimed exact flux constancy, because A1​=0.
Several requested conclusions remain useful as formal approximations, provided they are identified as such. If δ>0, the large-depth exponential vanishes, giving F→4πα/3, hence α=3F/(4π) for the asymptotic physical flux. It also gives cPrad​→(4π/3)J. Since A0​=1+ϵ2/3+O(ϵ4), neglecting second-order angular errors yields the formal relation δ≃ϵ. However A1​=−ϵ/3+O(ϵ3), so the finite-depth flux still varies at first order. The formal relation is not an exact transfer or constant-flux solution.
There is also a flux-normalization mismatch in the PDF's last displayed relation. With the same physical F used above, the Hopf function of a grey atmosphere is defined by
J=4π3F​[τ+q(τ)].
(8)
The PDF's coefficient 3F/4 would require redefining its F as physical flux divided by π; it cannot simultaneously use the earlier α=3F/(4π) for the same physical flux. With the consistent physical-flux normalization, formal moment matching gives
β=q(∞)=0.71,γA0​=q(0)−q(∞)=−0.14.
(9)
In the intended small-ϵ approximation this is
β≃0.71,γ≃−0.14.​
(10)
The approximate outgoing surface ansatz then gives the limb darkening ratio
I(0,0)I(1,0)​=β+γβ+1+γ/(1+ϵ)​ ⟶ 0.571.57​≃2.75.​
(11)
For finite ϵ, γ=−0.14/A0​ gives a parameter-dependent ratio, not a unique exact value. The two Hopf endpoint values alone do not determine the exact emergent intensity: the formal solution of the radiative transfer equation uses the entire source profile, I(0,μ)=∫0∞​J(t)e−t/μdt/μ for μ>0. Nor does the simple linear-plus-exponential ansatz satisfy the exact no-incoming-radiation condition for every negative μ. Thus 2.75 is a clearly labelled approximate disk-centre-to-limb result, not an exact consequence of inconsistent premises.
