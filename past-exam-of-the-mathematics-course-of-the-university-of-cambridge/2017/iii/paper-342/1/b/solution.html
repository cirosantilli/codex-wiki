In particular the free-end displacement is δ=hs​(L)=wL4/(8B) and the total buoyancy-corrected gravitational load is F=wL. Thus the tip stiffness of a uniformly loaded cantilever is
F=keff​δ,keff​=L38B​=L32πAa4​.​

The last expression uses A as Young's modulus; if A itself is flexural rigidity, the same result reads keff​=8A/L3. This is Hooke's law for the specified loading pattern. It is not the stiffness for a point force at the tip, which would be 3B/L3; replacing a distributed force by its resultant does not preserve its bending moment distribution.
For dimensional analysis, [A]= force/length squared, [I]= length to the fourth power, hence [B]= force times length squared. Consequently [B/L3]= force/length, as required for a spring constant. Also [ζ⊥​]= force times time/length squared makes ζ⊥​ht​, Bhxxxx​ and w all forces per unit length. Neither viscosity nor density enters the stiffness, although viscous drag controls the rate of approach to the steady shape. The small-slope approximation requires wL3/B≪1, since hs′​(L)=wL3/(6B).
