Fix a positive-mass unitary irreducible representation at rest, Pμ=(M,0,0,0) with M>0. Use the central charge in supersymmetry convention
{QαA​,Qˉ​β˙​B​}=2δBA​σαβ˙​μ​Pμ​,{QαA​,QβB​}=ϵαβ​ZAB,ZAB=−ZBA,
(1)
The conjugate relation is {Qˉ​α˙A​,Qˉ​β˙​B​}=ϵα˙β˙​​ZAB, with the dotted epsilon tensor the conjugate of the undotted one. The supercharges commute with Pμ​ and the central charges. Their Lorentz transformation is that of a Weyl spinor; together with the ordinary Poincare algebra, these relations specify the Super-Poincaré algebra. Take ϵ12​=1 and write Z12=2z. This makes the normalization of the mass bound explicit: a convention that writes 2ϵαβ​ZAB instead has z=Z12.
When Z=0, define four fermionic annihilation operators and their fermionic creation operators by
aαA​=2M​QαA​​,(aαA​)†=2M​Qˉ​α˙A​​.
(2)
In the rest frame they obey the canonical anticommutation relations {ai​,aj†​}=δij​ and {ai​,aj​}=0. Choose a spin-zero Clifford vacuum annihilated by all four ai​. Acting with each distinct creator at most once constructs the fermionic Fock space
ai1​†​⋯aik​†​∣Ω⟩,i1​<⋯<ik​,k=0,1,2,3,4.
(3)
The five levels have the binomial coefficient multiplicities 1,4,6,4,1, so the massive supermultiplet contains 24=16 states. The four creators are two copies of the spin-one-half SU(2) representation. Their second exterior power is
⋀2(2⊗2)=(Sym22⊗⋀22)⊕(⋀22⊗Sym22).
(4)
Here the first factor carries physical spin and the second counts the two supersymmetries. Thus level two contains one spin-one triplet and three spin-zero singlets; levels one and three each contain two spin-one-half doublets. The final spin content is
5 spin-0 states+4 spin-21​ doublets+1 spin-1 triplet=16 states.​
(5)
Even levels give eight boson states and odd levels eight fermion states, exhibiting boson-fermion degeneracy in a supermultiplet.
For nonzero z=∣z∣eiφ, pair the two supersymmetries before normalizing. Define
qα​=Qα1​,rα​=ϵαβ​(Qβ2​)†,bα±​=2​qα​±eiφrα​​.
(6)
The rest-frame algebra gives {qα​,rβ†​}=2zδαβ​ and consequently
{bα±​,(bβ±​)†}=2(M±∣z∣)δαβ​,{bα+​,(bβ−​)†}=0.
(7)
For M>∣z∣, divide b± by 2(M±∣z∣)​ to recover four normalized oscillators. Positivity of the Hilbert space norm proves the BPS bound in supersymmetry, since
∥bα−​∣v⟩∥2+∥(bα−​)†∣v⟩∥2=2(M−∣z∣)∥v∥2≥0.
(8)
Therefore
M≥∣z∣=21​∣Z12∣.​
(9)
For a BPS state saturating this bound, both bα−​ and their adjoints annihilate every state of the representation. Four of the eight real supercharges act trivially. Only the two b+ oscillators remain, so a scalar Clifford vacuum gives a shortened massive supermultiplet with two spin-zero states and one spin-one-half doublet:
M=∣z∣:4 states=2 bosons+2 fermions,one-half of the supersymmetry preserved.​
(10)
This is the irreducible count at fixed central charge. A CPT completion of a supermultiplet can require the charge-conjugate multiplet as well, doubling it to eight states; the full hypermultiplet count must not be confused with this four-state count. Saturation is a representation-theoretic relation between mass and conserved charge: shortening persists while the state remains BPS, although its existence can change across parameter space.
For even N=2n, unitary skew-diagonalization of an antisymmetric matrix brings the central-charge matrix to blocks 2zr​(0−1​10​), r=1,…,n. Repeat the oscillator construction separately for each block. Every block supplies two oscillators with norm 2(M+∣zr​∣) and two with norm 2(M−∣zr​∣), giving
M≥rmax​∣zr​∣.​
(11)
If k blocks saturate, 2k complex oscillators disappear, leaving 2N−2k. Starting with spin j0​, the irreducible state count and preserved fraction are
dimH=(2j0​+1)22N−2k,4Npreserved real supercharges​=Nk​.​
(12)
The largest massive shortening, k=n, preserves one-half. The ordinary massive representation has k=0. This rest-frame discussion excludes M=0, where the massless supermultiplet construction uses a null momentum instead.

Use left Grassmann derivatives for the Grassmann variables. A chiral superfield satisfies Dˉα˙​Φ=0. In the derivative convention printed in the PDF, differentiating θσμθˉ with respect to a barred coordinate brings a minus sign from moving the odd derivative through θ. Consequently Dˉα˙​yμ=0, so the general solution depends only on y and θ:
Φ(y,θ)=ϕ(y)+2​θψ(y)+θ2F(y).​
(13)
Here ϕ is a complex scalar field, ψ a Weyl spinor and F a complex auxiliary field. This is a finite Grassmann algebra expansion, not an assumption that the spacetime fields are constant.
For a completely convention-independent way to organize the ordinary-coordinate expansion, let vμ=θσμθˉ. Translation by iv gives
Φ=ϕ+2​θψ+θ2F+ivμ∂μ​ϕ+i2​vμθ∂μ​ψ−21​vμvν∂μ​∂ν​ϕ.
(14)
All higher terms vanish because there are only two unbarred and two barred odd coordinates. For example take σμ=(I,σ), signature (+−−−), θ2=θαθα​ and θˉ2=θˉα˙​θˉα˙, with both lower epsilon tensors having ϵ12​=1. Then vμvν=21​θ2θˉ2ημν and the chiral-superfield component expansion reads
Φ=ϕ+2​θψ+θ2F+iθσμθˉ∂μ​ϕ−2​i​θ2∂μ​ψσμθˉ−41​θ2θˉ2□ϕ.​
(15)
The frequently written positive quarter coefficient corresponds to the opposite convention for □ or the spinor contractions; the preceding shift formula fixes the sign without ambiguity. The local TeX's barred derivative is damaged: the original PDF has ∂μ​, not a barred-coordinate factor at its end.
Write X=x+2​θG+θ2FX​ in chiral coordinates. Multiplication gives the three component constraints
x2=0,xGα​=0,2xFX​−GG=0.
(16)
On the regular branch where the ordinary commuting part of FX​ is nonzero, the nilpotent chiral superfield is therefore
X=2FX​GG​+2​θG+θ2FX​.​
(17)
The goldstino G and auxiliary field FX​ are independent; the scalar is a composite, not another independent degree of freedom. Its first two constraints follow because any product of three identical two-component odd spinor entries vanishes. It is essential not to divide by FX​ on a branch with zero commuting part: for example X=0 satisfies the nilpotency condition without breaking supersymmetry.
For a regular local action with no superspace derivatives in K or W, nilpotency truncates the most general superpotential and Kähler potential to
W=w0​+fX,K=k0​+k1​X+k1​​Xˉ+κXXˉ,k0​∈R,κ>0.​
(18)
The last inequality makes the kinetic Kähler metric healthy. In global four-dimensional N=1 supersymmetry, the constant and holomorphic pieces of K integrate to zero, and w0​ does not affect the action. On the bosonic background G=0, the constraint sets x=0 and the auxiliary terms are κFX​FˉX​+fFX​+fˉ​FˉX​. Eliminating them gives
FX​=−κfˉ​​,V=κ∣f∣2​.​
(19)
A nonzero f is necessary for this regular constrained branch to be an on-shell vacuum. It gives nonzero FX​ and supersymmetry breaking, with G the goldstino. Nilpotency alone is not a proof that every possible branch breaks supersymmetry. With f=0 the above composite parametrization is singular on shell, rather than evidence for a regular supersymmetric vacuum containing an independent Goldstino.
If these data are instead embedded in supergravity, the constant and linear terms cannot simply be discarded independently: a Kähler transformation also transforms W. Before such a transformation, at x=0 the supergravity F-term potential is
V=ek0​(κ∣f+k1​w0​∣2​−3∣w0​∣2),FX=−ek0​/2κf+k1​w0​​​.
(20)
Thus nonzero FX still breaks supersymmetry, but the vacuum energy need not be positive. This distinguishes the global answer from the local theory explicitly introduced in the next question.
Now write Y=y+2​θχ+θ2FY​. The condition XY=0 becomes
xy=0,xχα​+yGα​=0,xFY​+yFX​−Gχ=0.
(21)
Using the same nonzero-FX​ branch gives the chiral superfield constrained by a nilpotent superfield
y=FX​Gχ​−2FX2​GG​FY​,Y=y+2​θχ+θ2FY​.​
(22)
The spinor χ and the auxiliary FY​ remain independent; the scalar is removed. Substituting this expression verifies the other two product constraints using two-component spinor identities. It does not impose Y2=0. It does imply cubic nilpotency from a mixed chiral constraint: Y3=0. Indeed (Gχ)2=−21​(GG)(χχ), so
y2=−2FX2​(GG)(χχ)​,y3=0,y2χ=0,y2FY​−yχχ=0.
(23)
These are exactly the three components needed for Y3=0. The generic nonzero expression for y2 shows why imposing quadratic nilpotency on Y would lose allowed interactions.
The analytic chiral monomials consequently reduce to 1,X,Y,Y2. The most general superpotential is
W=w0​+fX+gY+2m​Y2.​
(24)
For the Kähler potential, put U=(X,Y,Y2)T. Every allowed mixed monomial is included in
K=k0​+ℓTU+ℓTU+a,b=1∑3​habˉ​Ua​Uˉb​,k0​∈R,habˉ​=hbaˉ​​.​
(25)
This includes XXˉ, XYˉ, XYˉ2, YYˉ, YYˉ2, Y2Yˉ2 and the necessary conjugates; products containing XY or XˉYˉ vanish. In a global theory the holomorphic ℓ terms again integrate to zero; in supergravity they can be changed by a Kähler transformation. Positivity requires the kinetic metric on the independent multiplets to be positive definite at the chosen background, not necessarily the entire coefficient matrix on the redundant composite list U. The most general claim here concerns regular analytic two-derivative actions; singular functions of the constrained fields or higher-derivative operators are outside it.

The exponent in the original PDF is e−αS with real α>0, not the e−aS produced by the local TeX. Use that authoritative expression. Put
s=S+Sˉ>0,t=T+Tˉ,q=t−∣C∣2>0,w(S)=ae−αS+b,W=C3+w(S).
(26)
The domain conditions ensure a real Kähler potential and positive Kähler metric. There are no gauge multiplets specified, so the scalar potential is the supergravity F-term potential
V=eK(Kijˉ​Di​WDj​W​−3∣W∣2),Di​W=Wi​+Ki​W,eK=sq31​.
(27)
The Kähler covariant derivative of a superpotential and metric entries are
DS​W=wS​−sW​,DT​W=−q3W​,DC​W=3C2+q3CˉW​,KSSˉ​=s−2,
(28)
(Kijˉ​​)i,j=T,C​=q23​(1−Cˉ​−Ct​).
(29)
State the inverse with its indices explicitly, since transposing the off-diagonal complex entries would change the answer:
KSSˉ=s2,KTTˉ=3qt​,KTCˉ=3qCˉ​,KCTˉ=3qC​,KCCˉ=3q​.
(30)
Here ∑j​Kijˉ​​Kkjˉ​=δik​, so the displayed upper-index array is the transpose of the ordinary matrix inverse of the displayed lower-index array.
Substitution shows that the cross terms involving W cancel and that the T,C sector gives
Kijˉ​Di​WDj​W​=3∣W∣2+3q​∣WC​∣2(i,j=T,C).
(31)
The first term cancels the universal negative term. Thus the no-scale supergravity potential is
V=sq3∣swS​−W∣2+3q∣C∣4​=sq3∣C3+b+(1+αs)ae−αS∣2+3q∣C∣4​≥0.​
(32)
This positivity is an algebraic cancellation, not an assumption that the individual Kähler derivatives vanish.
Choose the supergravity auxiliary field convention Fi=−eK/2Kijˉ​Dj​W​. Direct multiplication gives
FS=eK/2s(Wˉ−swˉS​),FT=eK/2qwˉ,FC=−eK/2qCˉ2.​
(33)
A conventional common phase or overall sign on the auxiliary fields changes none of the vanishing conditions. A supersymmetric configuration requires every Fi to vanish. First FC=0 implies C=0, then FT=0 implies w=0, and FS=0 implies wS​=0. Since wS​=−αae−αS, at a finite point of the physical domain this is possible only when a=0, followed by b=0. Consequently If (a,b)=(0,0), every finite configuration has a nonzero auxiliary field, so any finite vacuum breaks supersymmetry. The printed request cannot hold for arbitrary parameters without this exception: a=b=0, C=0 is a zero-energy supersymmetric family with both S and T unfixed.
With the stipulated vanishing vacuum expectation value of C, define A(S)=b+(1+αs)ae−αS. The potential reduces to ∣A∣2/(st3). Its derivative along t is −3V/t, so a finite stationary point must have A=0. Such a point is a global minimum of the full nonnegative potential, since both squares vanish at C=0. The finite zero-energy vacuum for a single exponential superpotential therefore satisfies
C=0,b=−(1+αs)ae−αS,V=0.​
(34)
For nonzero a,b, write S=σ+iχ and x=ασ>0. The modulus and phase conditions are
ρ≡​ab​​=(1+2x)e−x,e−iαχ=−ρb/a​.
(35)
The function (1+2x)e−x increases up to x=1/2 and then decreases to zero; its maximum is 2e−1/2. Hence a finite minimum exists exactly when
ab=0,0<∣b/a∣≤2e−1/2.​
(36)
There is one positive solution for 0<ρ≤1, two for 1<ρ<2e−1/2, and one coalesced solution at the upper bound. The formal x=0 solution at ρ=1 is outside s>0. At each allowed x, the phase fixes χ modulo 2π/α. Equivalently x=−21​−Wk​(−ρ/(2e​)), using the real branches of the Lambert W function and retaining only x>0.
At any of these nontrivial minima, w=−αsae−αS=0. The auxiliary fields become
FS=FC=0,FT=eK/2twˉ=0.​
(37)
This is a supersymmetry breaking Minkowski minimum in which the nonzero auxiliary field belongs to T. Both real components of T are exact flat directions of a scalar potential at the minimum. They change the metric and auxiliary-field magnitudes but not the zero potential. Generically the two real components of S are fixed. At the coalesced solution x=1/2, the radial quadratic restoring term vanishes, but the leading restoring term is quartic; this is not an additional exact flat direction of a scalar potential. The matter field C likewise has a positive leading quartic potential, not an exact flat direction of a scalar potential, despite its zero quadratic mass here.
The exceptional parameter cases must also be stated. If a=0,b=0 or a=0,b=0, no finite zero-energy minimum with C=0 exists. The same holds when ∣b/a∣>2e−1/2. Because the t derivative is nonzero at any positive-energy point on C=0, none is a finite minimum there; the energy approaches zero along the runaway t→∞. If a=b=0, all physical S,T at C=0 give supersymmetric zero-energy minima. Thus an unconditional finite minimum or unconditional breaking would be a false claim for the printed arbitrary parameters.
For the homogeneous model, assume Γ>0 is twice differentiable, of degree one in the real moduli τi​, and that its Hessian matrix for K is invertible on the sector considered. Differentiate K=−3logΓ:
Ki​=−Γ3Γi​​,Kij​=Γ23Γi​Γj​​−Γ3Γij​​.
(38)
The two Euler theorem for homogeneous functions identities in the question imply
τi​Kij​=Γ3Γj​​.​
(39)
Multiplying by the inverse gives Kij−1​Γj​/Γ=τi​/3, so contracting once more yields
Γ2Γi​Kij−1​Γj​​=31​,Ki​Kij−1​Kj​=3.​
(40)
This is the no-scale identity from degree-one homogeneity. It does not assert that every degree-one function gives a positive or invertible metric: for example Γ=τ1​+τ2​ has a rank-one Kähler Hessian, and its inverse is undefined. The inverse hypothesis is necessary.
For complex moduli with τi​=Ti​+Tˉi​, the same derivatives are the mixed Kähler metric; choosing real parts with a factor of two only introduces factors that cancel in the contraction. If W is independent of these moduli, Di​W=Ki​W, and their contribution is 3eK∣W∣2. It cancels the universal −3eK∣W∣2, leaving no scalar potential from this isolated no-scale sector. Other chiral sectors can still contribute positive terms. For this conclusion in a larger theory, the displayed metric must be the appropriate decoupled no-scale block, or the full inverse metric must itself satisfy the corresponding identity; arbitrary mixed additions to K do not inherit the cancellation automatically.
