Write D/Dt=∂t​+u⋅∇ for the material derivative and q=u⋅B for the cross-helicity density, so Hc​=∫V​qdV. The ideal magnetohydrodynamic induction equation of ideal magnetohydrodynamics, with ∇⋅B=0, gives
DtDB​=(B⋅∇)u−B∇⋅u.
(1)
The dot product of the Euler momentum equation with the magnetic field has no Lorentz force contribution, because B⋅[(∇×B)×B]=0. Consequently,
DtDq​=B⋅∇(2u2​−Φ)−ρB⋅∇p​−q∇⋅u.
(2)
Here h=e+p/ρ is the specific enthalpy, T the temperature, and s the specific entropy; e is the internal energy per unit mass. The first law of thermodynamics in this convention is de=Tds−pd(1/ρ), so dh=Tds+dp/ρ. Substitution, followed by ∇⋅B=0, yields the cross-helicity conservation law
∂t​q+∇⋅[uq+(h+Φ−2u2​)B]=TB⋅∇s.
(3)
The vector triple product identity u×(u×B)=uq−u2B puts this in the equivalent form
∂t​(u⋅B)+∇⋅[u×(u×B)+(2u2​+Φ+h)B]=TB⋅∇s.​
(4)
For the fixed volume, the divergence theorem gives the exact cross-helicity balance
dtdHc​​=∫V​TB⋅∇sdV−∫∂V​[uq+(h+Φ−2u2​)B]⋅ndS.
(5)
Thus cross-helicity is constant when the net source equals the outward flux. Simple sufficient hypotheses are B⋅∇s=0 throughout the volume and u⋅n=B⋅n=0 on its boundary. The first condition includes a homentropic flow; the second prevents both relevant boundary fluxes. Periodic boundary conditions, or sufficiently rapid decay at infinity, provide alternatives. These statements concern smooth ideal magnetohydrodynamics: material specific entropy conservation alone, Ds/Dt=0, does not eliminate spatial specific entropy gradients along the magnetic field lines.

Let the constant mass density be ρ>0 and put va​=B/μ0​ρ​, the Alfvén velocity. Since both the velocity and the magnetic field have zero divergence, the magnetic tension and magnetic pressure decomposition of the Lorentz force gives
∂t​u+(u⋅∇)u=−∇ψ+(va​⋅∇)va​,∂t​va​+(u⋅∇)va​=(va​⋅∇)u,ψ=ρp​+Φ+2∣va​∣2​.​
(6)
The Newtonian gravitational potential Φ remains in ψ; uniform mass density does not justify dropping a prescribed gravitational acceleration. Adding and subtracting the two equations proves the Elsässer variable equations
∂t​z±+(z∓⋅∇)z±=−∇ψ,∇⋅z±=0.​
(7)
Here z±=u±va​; each Elsässer variable is transported by the other.
Define I±​=∫V​∣z±∣2dV. Taking the dot product with 2z± gives the Elsässer energy invariant balance
∂t​∣z±∣2+∇⋅(∣z±∣2z∓+2ψz±)=0.
(8)
The divergence theorem proves dI±​/dt=0 whenever the net boundary flux vanishes. In particular, u⋅n=B⋅n=0 makes z±⋅n=0, so both fluxes vanish pointwise. Periodic boundary conditions or decay at infinity are also sufficient. These boundary conditions matter: fixed volume alone gives no conservation.
The kinetic energy plus magnetic energy is
E=2ρ​∫V​(u2+va2​)dV.
(9)
Expanding ∣u±va​∣2 and using the cross-helicity definition yields
E=4ρ​(I+​+I−​),Hc​=4μ0​ρ​​(I+​−I−​),I±​=ρ2E​±μ0​ρ​2Hc​​.​
(10)
Both energy and cross-helicity therefore follow from the two Elsässer energy invariants. The quantity E here is exactly the requested kinetic energy plus magnetic energy, without an additional gravitational term.

Use v=∣ur​∣>0 for the radial speed and distinguish the central mass M from the Mach number M=v/c. In each smooth nonzero-flow region, mass conservation, the adiabatic equation of state, and the Bernoulli equation give
j=r2ρv=4π∣M˙∣​>0,p=Kρ3/2,c2=23​Kρ1/2,E=2v2​+2c2−rGM​.
(11)
Here K>0 is constant on a smooth isentropic flow branch, c is the adiabatic sound speed, and E is the Bernoulli function. Either sign of ur​ is possible. The positive-radius nondimensionalization in the question requires E>0; flows with E≤0 do not have these positive rs​,x,λ and require another scaling.
Indeed, the supplied adiabatic process equations give D(pρ−3/2)/Dt=0, so K is spatially constant along a steady nonzero radial fluid flow. The adiabatic sound speed is c2=(∂p/∂ρ)s​, and the specific enthalpy is h=c2/(γ−1)=2c2. The radial Euler momentum equation, vdv/dr=−ρ−1dp/dr−GM/r2, integrates to E because dh=dp/ρ on this isentropic flow branch.
Put D=[j(3K/2)2]2/5. Eliminating the mass density from the continuity equation gives
c2=Dr−4/5M−2/5.
(12)
With y=M2/5, the Bernoulli equation becomes
E+rGM​=2D​r−4/5(y4+4/y).
(13)
Therefore the physical constants of the spherical polytropic flow with adiabatic exponent three halves are
rs​=4EGM​,λ=2Ers4/5​[j(3K/2)2]2/5​,λF(y)=F(x),F(t)=t4+t4​,x=(r/rs​)1/5.​
(14)
For t>0, F′(t)=4(t5−1)/t2, so F decreases from infinity to its unique minimum F(1)=5, then increases to infinity. This completely determines the algebraic curves. For 0<λ<1, there are two disconnected branches at every x>0, one subsonic flow with y<1 and one supersonic flow with y>1. For λ=1, the line y=x and a decreasing branch meet at (1,1). Their slopes are +1 and −1, since the leading expansion is F(1+η)=5+10η2+O(η3). For λ>1, solutions exist only where F(x)≥5λ: if x−​<1<x+​ solve F(x±​)=5λ, there is a forbidden interval x−​<x<x+​. At either endpoint the two branches turn at y=1 and have an infinite slope.
A smooth sonic point needs F′(x)=F′(y)=0; hence a regular sonic transition requires λ=1 and r=rs​. The critical speed of a polytropic flow is then cs2​=GM/(2rs​)=2E, and the allowed mass flux is j=(2E)5/2rs2​/(3K/2)2. Here the subscript on cs​ denotes evaluation at the sonic point, rather than the isothermal sound speed convention used in the next solution.
The simple transonic branch is y=x. Direct substitution gives
M=(r/rs​)1/2,v=2E​,c2=2rGM​,ρ=(3KrGM​)2,p=K(3KrGM​)3.​
(15)
Thus the radial velocity has constant magnitude, while the mass density and pressure scale as r−2 and r−3. For an outward fluid flow, this is a subsonic flow becoming a supersonic flow as its adiabatic sound speed decreases.
On the other transonic branch, y decreases as x increases. At large r, the terms 4/y and x4 dominate, giving y∼4x−4. At small r, the terms y4 and 4/x dominate, giving y∼41/4x−1/4. The resulting asymptotic expansions are
r≫rs​:r≪rs​:​M∼32(rs​/r)2,c2⟶E/2,ρ⟶(3KE​)2,v∼32E/2​(rs​/r)2,M∼25/4(rs​/r)1/8,c2∼2​E(rs​/r)3/4,v∼r2GM​​,ρ∼2GM​j​r−3/2,p∼K(2GM​j​)3/2r−9/4.​
(16)
For inward fluid flow, these are the nearly static reservoir at infinity and the inner free fall region of Bondi accretion. The specific enthalpy is smaller than GM/r in the inner limit, which explains free fall. Reversing the radial velocity gives a decelerating outflow with the same profiles. The formal small-r limit describes the point-mass model: if the spherical gravitating body has a finite surface, the exterior solution stops there.
A stationary, nonradiative normal shock wave conserves the mass flux and the sum v2/2+h, while the Newtonian gravitational potential is continuous across its thin layer. Hence E and rs​ are unchanged. The entropy production in a perfect-gas shock increases K=p/ρ3/2, so
λ1​λ2​​=(K1​K2​​)4/5>1.​
(17)
More explicitly, the Rankine-Hugoniot condition for γ=3/2 gives, with upstream m=M12​>1,
ρ1​ρ2​​=m+45m​,p1​p2​​=56m−1​,M22​=6m−1m+4​<1.
(18)
Thus the shock wave jumps vertically, at the same x, from the supersonic flow to a subsonic flow on a larger-λ curve. The figure illustrates an outward flow on y=x with a shock wave at x=1.4; the downstream subsonic flow has λ≃1.224 and continues to larger x. The shock wave radius is an additional boundary-data choice, rather than being determined by the smooth equations alone.

Write cs2​=p/ρ for the constant isothermal sound speed squared and va2​=B2/(μ0​ρ) for the constant Alfvén speed squared. The horizontal magnetic field has no vertical magnetic tension; magnetostatic equilibrium is
dzdΠ​=−gρ,Π=p+2μ0​B2​=(cs2​+2va2​​)ρ.
(19)
Consequently the magnetized isothermal atmosphere has
H=gcs2​+va2​/2​,ρ=ρ0​e−z/H,p=cs2​ρ0​e−z/H,B=B0​e−z/(2H),B02​=μ0​ρ0​va2​.​
(20)
The sign of B0​ specifies the orientation of the magnetic field. The adiabatic sound speed is vs2​=γp/ρ=γcs2​, also constant.
For the proposed normal mode, let A=exp(ik⋅x+z/(2H)−iωt), a=1/(2H), and temporarily write ξ for its constant complex amplitude. The divergence amplitude is Δ=ikx​ξx​+iky​ξy​+(ikz​+a)ξz​. Using ρ′/ρ=−1/H and B′/B=−a, the Eulerian and Lagrangian fluid perturbations give
ρAδρ​=−Δ+Hξz​​,BAδB​=iky​ξ−ey​Δ+aey​ξz​.
(21)
Since Π′/ρ=−g, the magnetohydrodynamic total pressure amplitude is
ψ=ρAδΠ​=−(vs2​+va2​)Δ+va2​iky​ξy​+gξz​.
(22)
The vertical derivative of δΠ=ρAψ is ρA(ikz​−a)ψ. For the magnetic tension perturbation, the terms proportional to aiky​ξz​ey​ from δB⋅∇B and B⋅∇δB cancel, leaving
μ0​ρAδB⋅∇B+B⋅∇δB​=−va2​ky2​ξ−va2​iky​Δey​.
(23)
Thus the linearization really reduces to the constant-coefficient eigenvalue problem
−ω2ξx​−ω2ξy​−ω2ξz​​=−ikx​ψ−ky2​va2​ξx​,=−iky​(ψ+va2​Δ)−ky2​va2​ξy​,=g(Δ−ξz​/H)−(ikz​−a)ψ−ky2​va2​ξz​.​
(24)
For a static ideal magnetohydrodynamics equilibrium with conservative gravitational acceleration, the magnetohydrodynamic energy principle gives a Hermitian operator for the restoring force in the mass density weighted inner product, provided the surface terms vanish. The factor ez/(2H) removes the exponential mass density weight. This can be checked directly, without assuming stability: set s=vs2​, b=va2​, and q=s+b. Then ω2ξ=Kξ, where
K=​qkx2​+bky2​skx​ky​qkx​kz​−ikx​(g−qa)​skx​ky​sky2​sky​kz​−iky​(g−sa)​qkx​kz​+ikx​(g−qa)sky​kz​+iky​(g−sa)q(kz2​+a2)+bky2​​​.
(25)
This is a Hermitian matrix for real k and real positive s,b. Therefore every squared frequency ω2 is real; negative values give exponential growth, rather than an oscillation with a complex squared frequency. In an infinite atmosphere these are generalized plane waves, not finite-total-energy global modes.
At ω2=0 and ky​=0, the y equation, after cancelling its magnetic terms, gives sΔ=gξz​. For nonzero magnetic field, the x equation also gives ikx​ξx​=kx2​ψ/(ky2​b). Substituting ψ=−b[Δ−iky​ξy​]+gξz​−sΔ and then sΔ=gξz​ yields
sΔ=gξz​,−(kx2​+ky2​)ψ=ky2​b(ikz​+a)ξz​.​
(26)
For a nontrivial mode ξz​=0; otherwise these equations and ky​b=0 force every component to vanish. Substitution in the vertical equation gives
bky2​(∣k∣2+4H21​)+Hg​(1−sgH​)(kx2​+ky2​)=0.​
(27)
For example, the determinant provides an independent check:
detK=sbky2​[bky2​(∣k∣2+a2)+(Hg​−sg2​)(kx2​+ky2​)].
(28)
To prove the claimed “if and only if”, rather than merely locating a zero eigenvalue, use the magnetic buoyancy energy criterion
ξ†Kξ=​s​Δ−sg​ξz​​2+b∣ikx​ξx​+(ikz​+a)ξz​∣2+bky2​(∣ξx​∣2+∣ξz​∣2)+(Hg​−sg2​)∣ξz​∣2.​
(29)
If gH≤s, every term is nonnegative, so all eigenvalues are nonnegative for every wavevector, including ky​=0. If gH>s, choose kz​=0, any fixed kx​=0, and ξz​=1, ξx​=−a/(ikx​), ξy​=g/(siky​). The first two squares vanish; the remaining value is
bky2​(1+kx2​a2​)+Hg​−sg2​,
(30)
which is negative for sufficiently small nonzero ky​. The Rayleigh quotient of this displacement is therefore negative, proving a negative eigenvalue and an unstable normal mode. This uses precisely the assumption that boundary conditions do not exclude the chosen wavelengths. Equality is a neutral threshold, not exponential growth.
Finally the plasma beta is β=p/[B2/(2μ0​)]=2cs2​/va2​. Hence
instability ⟺ gH>vs2​ ⟺ 2va2​​>(γ−1)cs2​ ⟺ β(γ−1)<1.​
(31)
The magnetic field is assumed nonzero in the marginal-mode elimination and in the finite β expression. The positive-sound-speed, nonmagnetic limit remains covered by the energy identity; for the usual γ>1 it is stable. Parker instability occurs because magnetic pressure supports part of the atmosphere's weight while the gas can drain along gently bent magnetic field lines. The resulting buoyant displacement can release Newtonian gravitational potential energy; choosing long wavelengths along the magnetic field makes the stabilizing magnetic tension small. The completed-square calculation quantifies this competition, including the adiabatic process restoring force.

Let the free surface be at r=R1​ and take the Newtonian gravitational potential to vanish at infinity. Mass conservation fixes R1​=(3M1​/(4πρ1​))1/3. The enclosed mass is m(r)=4πρ1​r3/3, and hydrostatic equilibrium requires dp0​/dr=−ρ1​Gm(r)/r2. With vacuum outside, p0​(R1​)=0, giving
p0​(r)=32πGρ12​​(R12​−r2),Φ0​(r)=⎩⎨⎧​−2R1​GM1​​(3−R12​r2​),−rGM1​​,​r≤R1​,r≥R1​.​​
(32)
The pressure is zero outside. A prescribed constant external pressure would simply add that constant to p0​.
The bulk Eulerian and Lagrangian fluid perturbations obey δρ=0 because the mass density is uniform and the Lagrangian displacement has zero divergence. The linearized Euler momentum equation in the interior is
−ω2ξ=−∇(ρ1​δp​+δΦ).
(33)
Taking the curl gives ω2∇×ξ=0. Thus every nonzero-frequency normal mode has an irrotational flow displacement; the simply connected space formed by the interior admits ξ=∇U and incompressibility gives the Laplace equation ∇2U=0. For a regular center, one spherical harmonic component is U=Al​rlYlm​, with ∣m∣≤l.
It is essential to retain the surface gravity perturbation of a uniform-density sphere. Although the bulk mass density perturbation is zero, the displaced density discontinuity gives
δρ=ρ1​ξr​(R1​)δ(r−R1​),ξr​(R1​)=lAl​R1l−1​Ylm​.
(34)
The Poisson equation implies that δΦ is continuous and its outward radial derivative has the jump
[∂r​δΦ]out−in​=4πGρ1​ξr​(R1​).
(35)
Using regularity at the center and decay at infinity, write
δΦin​=Cl​rlYlm​,δΦout​=Cl​R12l+1​r−l−1Ylm​.
(36)
The derivative jump is −(2l+1)Cl​R1l−1​Ylm​, so Cl​=−4πGρ1​lAl​/(2l+1).
Integrating the bulk Euler momentum equation gives δp/ρ1​=ω2U−δΦ for l≥1. At the free surface the Lagrangian pressure perturbation vanishes:
0=δp+ξr​p0′​(R1​),ρ1​δp​=R12​GM1​​ξr​.
(37)
Combining these relations yields the Kelvin stellar mode frequency
ωl2​=lR13​GM1​​−2l+14πGρ1​l​=2l+12l(l−1)​R13​GM1​​​,l≥1.
(38)
The 2l+1 choices of m are degenerate because of spherical symmetry. For l=1, rY1m​ is a linear combination of coordinates in a Cartesian coordinate system, so ∇U is a constant displacement: the zero frequency is rigid translation of the whole isolated body. Such a translation has no restoring force. The constant l=0 potential generates no displacement; a radial breathing motion is excluded by incompressibility and a regular center.
The printed irrotational assertion needs the nonzero-frequency qualification, or restriction to the potential incompressible stellar surface modes. It is false for all neutral displacements: ξ=Ω×r has zero divergence, zero normal displacement at the surface, and δp=δΦ=0, so it is a zero-frequency displacement, but ∇×ξ=2Ω=0. This does not change the Kelvin stellar mode spectrum just derived.

The separation of the two bodies is a, as is fixed by the supplied Kepler third law ωo2​=G(M1​+M2​)/a3. The phrase “orbit of radius a” cannot denote the first body's distance from the center of mass in that formula.
Expand the companion's Newtonian potential of a point mass about the first body's center. The constant term produces no force; the linear term produces the uniform acceleration of the center and disappears in the translating frame. The leading differential acceleration therefore comes from the quadrupolar tidal forcing
Φtide​=−2a3GM2​​[3(n(t)⋅r)2−r2],
(39)
where n(t) points toward the companion. It is a degree-two solid spherical harmonic. For a circular nonrotating-frame orbit, its time-dependent components have m=±2 and frequency 2ωo​, because the squared direction cosines repeat twice per orbit. There is also a static m=0 component. The real part of one complex harmonic represents the pair of time-dependent components; its normalization and orientation are absorbed into the order-one coefficient ψ. This gives Ψ=ψ(GM2​/a3)r2Y2m​ and ω=2ωo​. The expansion is the leading term for R1​/a small; spin is being neglected.
For a forced potential flow with the same temporal frequency, the Euler momentum equation becomes
−ω2∇U=−∇(ρ1​δp​+δΦ+Ψ).
(40)
Use U=Ar2Y2m​. The surface gravity perturbation of a uniform-density sphere is unchanged, as is the vanishing Lagrangian pressure perturbation boundary condition. Integrating the equation and applying that condition gives
(ω2−ω22​)A=ψa3GM2​​,ω22​=54​R13​GM1​​.
(41)
Since ξr​(R1​)=2AR1​Y2m​, the real surface displacement is
ξr​(R1​,t)=ϵR1​ω2−ω22​ω22​​Re(ψY2m​e−iωt),ϵ=25​M1​M2​​(aR1​​)3.​
(42)
Indeed ϵω22​=2GM2​/a3, so the normalization is consistent. This is a particular forced response away from the resonance; arbitrary free normal modes can be added.
Put μ=M2​/M1​ and q=(R1​/a)3. The tidal resonance of a stellar oscillation condition is 4ωo2​=ω22​, hence
q=5(1+μ)1​,ϵ=2(1+μ)μ​,(aR1​​)3=51​(1−2ϵ).​
(43)
The resonance gives 0≤ϵ<1/2 for finite nonnegative μ, with actual forcing requiring M2​>0. At exact resonance the undamped linearization has no bounded response at the forcing frequency: the time-domain particular solution grows proportionally to t times a sinusoid. Thus the frequency-response formula has a pole, rather than describing a finite resonant displacement. Away from that pole, linearization requires the displayed surface displacement to be small compared with R1​; the leading quadrupolar tidal forcing approximation separately requires R1​/a small.
