Use the standard inverse-problem setting of a bounded linear K:ℓ2→ℓ2 and exact data having a finite-penalty solution. Let u† denote the unique J-minimizing exact solution for J(u)=∥u∥1​+β∥u∥22​, β>0. This is the limiting target of elastic net regularization in Hilbert sequence space; it need not be the Moore–Penrose minimum-norm solution unless additional hypotheses identify them, for example injectivity of K.
For each α>0, the reconstruction objective is weakly lower semicontinuous, coercive by αβ∥u∥22​, and strictly convex. The direct method on the reflexive l2 sequence space gives existence, and strict convexity gives uniqueness. For two data sets, monotonicity of the one-norm subgradients and the quadratic penalty give, with d=u1​−u2​ and g=f1​−f2​,
∥Kd∥2+2αβ∥d∥2≤⟨g,Kd⟩≤∥g∥∥Kd∥.
(55)
Completing the square yields ∥d∥≤∥g∥/(22αβ​), so each reconstruction map is continuous.
Minimality against u† gives
21​∥Kuαδ​−fδ∥2+αJ(uαδ​)≤2δ2​+αJ(u†).
(56)
Choose α→0 and δ2/α→0, for example α=δ. This bounds the Hilbert norms, drives the data residual to zero and gives limsupJ(uαδ​)≤J(u†). Every weakly convergent subsequence has an exact solution as its limit, by boundedness of K; weak lower semicontinuity and uniqueness of the penalty minimizer identify that limit with u†. Separately applying weak lower semicontinuity to the one-norm gives
limsupβ∥uαδ​∥22​≤J(u†)−∥u†∥1​=β∥u†∥22​.
(57)
Weak convergence and convergence of Hilbert norms imply strong convergence by the Radon-Riesz theorem. This proves norm convergence of elastic net regularization:
uαδ​→u† in ℓ2,α→0,δ2/α→0.​
(58)
The argument applies to every sequence of noise levels and admissible data, hence gives the usual uniform noise-ball convergence for a fixed admissible exact datum.
For nonlinearity of elastic net reconstruction, zero is the minimizer exactly when ∥K∗fδ∥∞​≤α. This includes a neighborhood of zero. If K=0, choose ej​ with Kej​=0 and fδ=tKej​ for t∥Kej​∥2>α; zero then fails its optimality condition. A linear map vanishing on a neighborhood would vanish everywhere, so the elastic-net reconstruction is nonlinear whenever K=0. For K=I the explicit formula is [Rα​f]j​=sign(fj​)(∣fj​∣−α)+​/(1+2αβ), exhibiting soft thresholding.
A concrete distinction from Moore–Penrose convergence is K(u)=u1​+2u2​ (embedded in the first output coordinate of ℓ2), exact datum one, and β=1. Its minimum-norm solution is (1/5,2/5,0,…), with penalty 4/5, while the unique penalty-minimizing exact solution is (0,1/2,0,…), with penalty 3/4. For 0<α<2 the exact-data elastic-net reconstruction is (0,(2−α)/(4+2α),0,…), converging to the latter. Thus it is not a Moore–Penrose-consistent regularization for every noninjective operator under the earlier definition.
The PDF supplies neither a nonzero assumption on K nor a target/domain qualification here. Its unconditional nonlinearity claim is false for K=0, whose reconstruction is the zero linear map. The convergence proof above states the finite-penalty exact-solution hypotheses explicitly; it establishes convergence to the penalty-minimizing solution, not an automatically identical minimum-norm least-squares solution.
