Write D=Re​−Ri​, S=Re​+Ri​, A=Re2​−Ri2​=DS, and Δp=pi​−pe​. Incompressible flow with axial strain rate E gives
ur​=−2E​r+rC(t)​.
(16)
Its radial Stokes equation gives pr​=0, because ur,rr​+ur,r​/r−ur​/r2=0. The radial Newtonian fluid stress tensor component is σrr​=−p−μE−2μC/r2. The outer and inner Young–Laplace equation conditions have opposite curvature signs:
σrr​(Re​)=−pe​−Re​γ​,σrr​(Ri​)=−pi​+Ri​γ​.
(17)
Subtracting and eliminating C gives
C=2μARi2​Re2​​[Δp−γ(Ri​1​+Re​1​)],p=pe​−AΔpRi2​​+AγS​−μE.​
(18)
In particular, the boxed pressure is equivalent to the printed identity for (−p−μE)A. For equal gas pressures, C=−γRi​Re​/(2μD), so surface tension draws both interfaces inward in addition to the imposed extension.
The kinematic boundary condition is R˙j​=−ERj​/2+C/Rj​ at either interface. Taking the difference, and then the difference of squared radii, gives
D˙=−2E​D+2μγ​−2μSΔpRi​Re​​,A˙=−EA,A(t)=A(0)e−Et.​
(19)
The cancellation of C in the area equation is mass conservation: axial stretching reduces the liquid area while radial redistribution changes the hole size. For E=0, equal pressures, and Ri​(0)=b, Re​(0)=2b, one has A=3b2 and D=b+γt/(2μ). Since S=A/D,
Ri​=21​(DA​−D),Re​=21​(DA​+D).
(20)
Closure occurs at D=A​=3​b, so
t∗​=γ2μb​(3​−1).​
(21)
This capillary collapse of an annular viscous cylinder has finite closure time for γ>0 and b>0. For γ=0 it does not close in this case. The annular formula is used only up to closure; the singular cylindrical inner curvature is not a boundary condition on the subsequent solid cylinder.
