First supply the introductory scaling and geometry. The leading tangential Stokes equation in a thin gap balances P/L against μU/H2. The shear stress scale is τ∼μU/H, so lubrication pressure dominates shear stress:
P∼H2μUL​,Pτ​∼LH​≪1.​
(28)
The outer circle, viewed along a ray r(cosθ,sinθ) from the inner centre, satisfies r2−2αΔrsinθ+α2Δ2=(a+Δ)2. Expanding its positive root to first order in Δ/a gives r=a+Δ+αΔsinθ+O(Δ2/a), hence h=Δ(1+αsinθ).
Take positive Ω to mean increasing θ, and put s=aθ, y=r−a, U=Ωa. With no-slip boundary conditions at y=0,h, lubrication theory gives
uθ​=U(1−hy​)+2μps​​y(y−h),q=2Uh​−12μh3​ps​.
(29)
Steady mass conservation makes q independent of θ. Single-valued pressure requires ∫02π​pθ​dθ=0. Write In​=∫02π​(1+αsinθ)−ndθ and Dα​=1+α2/2. It follows that
q=2ΩaΔ​I3​I2​​=2ΩaΔ​Dα​1−α2​,ps​=h26μΩa​−h312μq​.​
(30)
The tangential fluid tractions on the inner and outer solids, measured in the same increasing-θ direction, are respectively
τi​=μuθ,y​(0)=h26μq​−h4μΩa​,τo​=−μuθ,y​(h)=h26μq​−h2μΩa​.​
(31)
This sign convention distinguishes the traction on a wall from the corresponding stress component in the fluid.
To leading order the holding force balances the pressure traction on the inner circle. Integration by parts gives
Fx​=a∫02π​pcosθdθ=−a∫02π​pθ​sinθdθ.
(32)
Use pθ​=aps​ and ∫sinθ/(1+αsinθ)ndθ=(In−1​−In​)/α, with the continuous limit at α=0. Substituting the boxed flux yields
Fx​=−Δ26πμΩa3​1−α2​Dα​α​,Fz​=0.​
(33)
The zero vertical component also follows from reflection in mathematics in the vertical line: this leaves the geometry and vertical force unchanged, reverses Ω, and Linearity of Stokes flow then requires Fz​=0.
Pressure gives no moment about either circular wall's own centre. Integrating the viscous shear torque with lever arm a to leading order gives the fluid couples
Ci​=−Δ1−α2​2πμΩa3​Dα​1+2α2​,Co​=ΔDα​2πμΩa31−α2​​.​
(34)
Indeed Ci​=a2∫τi​dθ and Co​=a2∫τo​dθ at this order. Applied holding or driving couples are the negatives of these fluid couples. They need not cancel because they are moments about different axes. The fluid force on the outer solid equals the holding force Fx​ on the inner one. Transferring its moment to the inner axis adds −αΔFx​, so angular momentum balance about a common axis is
Ci​+Co​=αΔFx​.​
(35)
For α=0 the usual equal and opposite concentric couples are recovered. All these formulas are leading thin-gap expressions at fixed ∣α∣<1; taking the contact limit requires maintaining the local slender-gap assumptions.
