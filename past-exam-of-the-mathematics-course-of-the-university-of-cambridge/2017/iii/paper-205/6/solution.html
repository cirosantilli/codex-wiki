Put Σ=XTX/n. The Debiased Lasso correction is
b=β​+ΘXT(Y−Xβ​)/n.​
(32)
For the Nodewise Lasso, let rj​=Xj​−X−j​γ​(j). Its Karush-Kuhn-Tucker conditions are
X−jT​rj​/n=λj​zj​,zj​∈∂∥γ​(j)∥1​,
(33)
so zjk​=sgn(γ​k(j)​) at a nonzero coefficient and ∣zjk​∣≤1 at a zero coefficient. Multiplying by γ​(j) gives the nodewise-Lasso residual identity
nXjT​rj​​=n∥rj​∥22​​+n(γ​(j))TX−jT​rj​​=n∥rj​∥22​​+λj​∥γ​(j)∥1​=τj2​.
(34)
We require nonzero design columns, a necessary qualification missing from the unrestricted printed formulation. Because λj​>0, τj2​=0 would force both rj​=0 and γ​(j)=0, and hence Xj​=0. Thus nonzero columns suffice for τj2​>0 even when p>n or the full Gram matrix is singular. A zero column instead gives τj2​=0 and its coefficient is unidentifiable from Y; the proposed normalization and confidence interval do not exist there.
For each j, define cj​∈Rp by (cj​)j​=1 and (cj​)−j​=−γ​(j). Set row j of Θ to cjT​/τj2​. Since Xcj​=rj​,
(ΘΣ)jk​=nτj2​rjT​Xk​​,(ΘΣ)jj​=1,∣(ΘΣ)jk​∣≤τj2​λj​​(k=j).
(35)
This row construction is the nodewise normalization used by van de Geer and coauthors. The matrix Θ need not be symmetric or be an actual inverse.
Substitute the linear model into the correction. The exact decomposition is
n​(b−β0)WΩΔ​=W+Δ,=ΘXTε/n​,=ΘΣΘT,=n​(ΘΣ−I)(β0−β​).​
(36)
In the fixed-design normal linear model, or with ε∣X∼Nn​(0,σ2I) and nodewise estimates and tuning chosen from X alone, a linear transformation of a multivariate normal distribution gives W∣X∼Np​(0,σ2Ω). Marginal normality of ε without this conditional assumption would not suffice for a response-dependent random design. No independence between W and Δ is asserted. The diagonal cancellation and the Holder inequality give the debiased-Lasso remainder bound
∥Δ∥∞​≤n​∥β0−β​∥1​jmax​τj2​λj​​.​
(37)
For a coordinate variance, Θj​XT=rjT​/τj2​, whence
Ωjj​=n(τj2​)2∥rj​∥22​​=(τj2​)2τj2​−λj​∥γ​(j)∥1​​.
(38)
Writing z1−α/2​ for the corresponding standard normal quantile, an approximate confidence interval is
​bj​ ± z1−α/2​n​σ​τj2​τj2​−λj​∥γ​(j)∥1​​​​.​
(39)
Equivalently its half-width is z1−α/2​σ∥Xj​−X−j​γ​(j)∥2​/(nτj2​). We assume 0<α<1 and σ>0. For asymptotic coverage, the standardized remainder Δj​/(σΩjj​​) must have convergence in probability to zero and the coordinate variance must be nondegenerate. Exact conditional normality of W alone does not give finite-sample exact coverage for b.
One sufficient random-design regime is the Gaussian identity design for debiased Lasso: take independent rows Xi​∼Np​(0,Ip​), independently of ε, fixed σ>0, p=pn​→∞, s=sn​≥1, and
logp=o(n),slogp=o(n​).
(40)
Choose λ=Cσlogp/n​ and all λj​=Dlogp/n​ for sufficiently large fixed C,D. Here is why this regime has the requested remainder bound. Uniformly over columns, their squared norms divided by n tend to one, while all off-diagonal sample cross-correlations have magnitude at most a constant times logp/n​ with probability tending to one. Taking D larger makes the zero vector satisfy all nodewise Karush-Kuhn-Tucker conditions strictly, so γ​(j)=0 for every j on that event, and τj2​=∥Xj​∥22​/n≥1/2 eventually. This also gives Ωjj​=1/τj2​→1 uniformly.
In this regime, the entrywise entrywise concentration of a Gaussian Gram matrix and Lasso cone condition argument in Gaussian identity design for debiased Lasso gives a Compatibility condition for the Lasso bounded away from zero with probability tending to one. Together with the noise-score event and the Basic inequality for the Lasso, this yields, for a fixed C1​,
Pr(∥β​−β0∥1​>C1​slogp/n​)⟶0.
(41)
For example, the cone inequality and compatibility bound give ∥β​−β0∥1​≤12λs/ϕ2 on their joint event. Therefore, with a fixed A≥2DC1​,
Pr(∥Δ∥∞​>Aslogp/n​)⟶0.​
(42)
The stated sparsity condition makes this remainder vanish and supplies the preceding confidence-interval requirement. Both dimensions may grow: p=n2 and s=⌊n1/4⌋ satisfy these assumptions. The concentration and Gaussian-design compatibility results are sufficient standard conditions here; the question does not require their proofs. The displayed rate is a high-probability fixed-constant bound, rather than only a claim of boundedness in probability.
