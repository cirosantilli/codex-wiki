It suffices to handle one single-box up-move, and then use the chain already constructed. Suppose the affected row sizes are a=λi​ and b=λj​, i<j. Since λ is a partition of an integer, a≥b≥1. Put N=a+b. Over the complex numbers, the two-row Young permutation module decomposition gives
M(a,b)≅⨁r=0b​V(N−r,r),M(a+1,b−1)≅⨁r=0b−1​V(N−r,r).
(51)
A zero second row is omitted. Therefore
M(a,b)≅M(a+1,b−1)⊕V(a,b).
(52)
Let K be the product of the symmetric groups of the unaffected rows and the symmetric group SN​ on the union of the affected rows. The two relevant Young subgroups lie in K. Transitivity of induced representations expresses Mλ and Mν by inducing the preceding two-row modules, tensored with the trivial representations of the unaffected factors, from K to Sn​. Induction preserves this direct sum, so
Mλ≅Mν⊕Lλ,ν​
(53)
for an actual group representation Lλ,ν​, not merely a difference of characters. Iterate along the chain and take the direct sum of the induced complements. The resulting complement can be realized as an invariant subspace of Mλ, either through these isomorphisms or by Maschke's theorem. Thus
Mλ≅Mμ⊕Lλ,μ​.​
(54)
If λ=μ, use the zero complement. The characteristic-zero hypothesis is essential to this use of the irreducible two-row decomposition.
