Suppose λ⊴μ in the dominance order on partitions and λ=μ. Let i be the first unequal row. Then λi​<μi​. Put
Dh​=∑r=1h​(μr​−λr​).
(49)
We have Di​>0, and eventually Dh​=0. Let j>i be its first return to zero. Then Dh​≥1 for i≤h<j and λj​>μj​.
Move one box from row j to row i. Increasing row i is legal: if i>1, then λi−1​=μi−1​≥μi​>λi​. Decreasing row j is also legal. If λj​=λj+1​, then μj+1​≤μj​<λj​=λj+1​, forcing Dj+1​<0, a contradiction. Also λj​>0. Thus the new list ν is a partition of an integer.
The new deficits equal Dh​−1 on i≤h<j and Dh​ elsewhere, so λ⊴ν⊴μ. The quantity ∑r​∣λr​−μr​∣ decreases by two, since the changed rows were a deficit and a surplus. Repeating must terminate at μ. Hence
λ⊴μ⟺μ is reachable from λ by single-box up-moves.​
(50)
The construction proves both termination and preservation of the intermediate dominance inequalities.
