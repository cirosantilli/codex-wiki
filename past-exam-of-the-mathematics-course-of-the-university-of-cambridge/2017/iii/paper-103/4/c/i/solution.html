Let P be the averaging linear map
P=∣G∣1​∑h∈G​ρ(h).
(42)
For a unitary representation of a finite group, P is the orthogonal projection onto the invariant subspace VG, and Pρ(h)=ρ(h)P=P. Applying this to ρ(g)v=λv gives Pv=λPv. Since λ=1, Pv=0. The vector v is a cyclic vector for a group representation, so its translates span V, and every translate also has projection zero. Thus P=0 and
VG={0}.​
(43)
This is the cyclic eigenvector obstruction to invariant vectors. Unitarity also gives a direct proof by pairing v with an invariant vector and then pairing every translate; the averaging proof makes the role of cyclicity especially transparent.
