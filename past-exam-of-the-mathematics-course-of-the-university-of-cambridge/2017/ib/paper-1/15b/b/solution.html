The wavefunction is continuous at each delta: a jump would produce a delta derivative in ψ′′ with no matching term in the equation. The symmetry of the symmetric double-delta potential permits even and odd solutions. Up to a nonzero normalization factor, the even candidate is cosh(κx) for ∣x∣≤a, and cosh(κa)e−κ(∣x∣−a) outside. Its derivative jump at a gives
κ(1+tanhκa)=ℏ22mλ​.
(57)
The odd candidate is sinh(κx) inside and sgn(x)sinh(κa)e−κ(∣x∣−a) outside. Its jump gives
κ(1+cothκa)=ℏ22mλ​.
(58)
Parity ensures the jump at −a as well. Put t=κa and L=2mλa/ℏ2. The continuous strictly increasing function t(1+tanht) ranges from 0 to infinity, so there is exactly one even bound state for every L>0. The continuous strictly increasing function t(1+cotht) ranges from 1 to infinity, so the odd bound state of a symmetric double-delta potential exists if and only if L>1. Strict increase follows from the allowed monotonicity facts plus the strictly increasing term t. Therefore
even: every λ>0;odd: λ>2maℏ2​​,E=−2mℏ2κ2​.

At equality the odd limiting solution has κ=0 and is not square integrable, so it is not a bound state. No nonnegative energy can supply another full-line square-integrable state, since its exterior solutions are oscillatory or affine.
