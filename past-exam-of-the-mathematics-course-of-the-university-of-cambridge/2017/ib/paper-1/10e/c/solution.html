For order 48=24⋅3, the Sylow theorems give n2​∣3 and n2​≡1(mod2), hence n2​=1 or 3. If n2​=1, the Sylow subgroup of order 16 is a nontrivial proper normal subgroup. If G were simple in the other case, it would be nonabelian (a finite abelian simple group has prime order), and the preceding result would force 48∣3!/2=3, impossible. Thus every group of order 48 is not simple.
An example with no normal Sylow subgroup for p=2 is
G=S4​×C2​​.
(35)
In S4​, P=⟨(1234),(13)⟩ is a dihedral group of order 8: conjugation by (13) inverts (1234). Thus P×C2​ has order 16 and is a Sylow subgroup of G. The only transpositions in P are (13) and (24); conjugating (13) by (23) gives (12)∈/P. Therefore P and P×C2​ are not normal. By conjugacy, no other Sylow subgroup for p=2 is normal either.
