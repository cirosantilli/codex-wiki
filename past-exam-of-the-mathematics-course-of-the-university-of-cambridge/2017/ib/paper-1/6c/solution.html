The Lagrange cardinal polynomials are
ℓi​(x)=∏0≤j≤nj=i​​xi​−xj​x−xj​​,ℓi​(xj​)=δij​.
(17)
Thus the Lagrange interpolation polynomial is
p(x)=i=0∑n​fi​ℓi​(x)​.
(18)
It has degree at most n and takes the required values. Uniqueness follows because the difference of two such polynomials has n+1 distinct roots and degree at most n, so is zero. The source's phrase “degree n” must allow a smaller degree, for example when f is constant.
Define the divided difference recursively by f[xi​]=fi​ and
f[x0​,…,xn​]=xn​−x0​f[x1​,…,xn​]−f[x0​,…,xn−1​]​.
(19)
Induction on this recursion, or comparison of the leading coefficient in the Newton interpolation polynomial, gives
f[x0​,…,xn​]=i=0∑n​∏j=i​(xi​−xj​)fi​​​.
(20)
For completeness the induction works term by term: an interior term gets the coefficient (1/(xi​−xn​)−1/(xi​−x0​))/(xn​−x0​)=1/((xi​−xn​)(xi​−x0​)), multiplying the product over the other interior nodes; the two endpoint terms give the same formula. This is also the leading coefficient of p directly from the cardinal formula, so p(n)=n!f[x0​,…,xn​].
The function f−p∈Cn[x0​,xn​] has n+1 distinct zeros. Repeated application of Rolle theorem gives ξ∈(x0​,xn​) with (f−p)(n)(ξ)=0 when n≥1. Hence
f[x0​,…,xn​]=n!f(n)(ξ)​​.
(21)
For n=0, this is simply f[x0​]=f(x0​) and we take ξ=x0​.
