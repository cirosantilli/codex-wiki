Since P(X1​=0)=e−λ, the indicator Y is an unbiased estimator of θ. The joint probability mass function is
pλ​(x1​,…,xn​)=e−nλ∏i​xi​!λ∑i​xi​​.

The Fisher-Neyman factorization theorem therefore makes T=∑i​Xi​ a sufficient statistic. More explicitly, conditioning on T=t gives a multinomial distribution with t trials and cell probabilities 1/n, so X1​∣T=t has the binomial distribution Bin(t,1/n). Consequently conditioning by the Rao-Blackwell theorem gives
E[Y∣T]=(1−n1​)T​.
(25)
For n=1, interpret the value at T=0 as 1 and at T>0 as 0; thus the same expression uses 00=1 in this probability convention. For λ=0 all observations and T are zero almost surely. For n>1 and λ>0, the reduction in variance is strict: given T=1, the event X1​=0 still has a nondegenerate conditional probability. Directly, using the probability generating function of T∼Poisson(nλ), the new variance is e−2λ(eλ/n−1).
