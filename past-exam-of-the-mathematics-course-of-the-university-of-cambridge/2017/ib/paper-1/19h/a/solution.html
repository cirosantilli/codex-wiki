A sufficient statistic has a conditional distribution of the full data given its value that is independent of the unknown parameter. A minimal sufficient statistic is sufficient and is a function of every other sufficient statistic, up to null sets. For a dominated family with common positive support, the likelihood-ratio criterion for minimal sufficiency states that T is minimal sufficient if T(x)=T(y) holds exactly when pθ​(x)/pθ​(y) is independent of θ.
For S=∑i​Xi​, the joint probability mass function is
pθ​(x)=θ−n(1−1/θ)S−n,xi​≥1.
(75)
The Fisher-Neyman factorization theorem proves sufficiency of S. For two samples, the likelihood ratio is (1−1/θ)S(x)−S(y), independent of θ>1 exactly when their sums agree. Thus S is minimal sufficient. Any one-to-one transformation of it is also minimal sufficient, so choose
T=X=nS​,Eθ​T=θ​.
(76)
This chosen estimator is an unbiased estimator, since each observation has mean θ. If instead the statistic is reported as S, its expectation is nθ, and as an estimator of θ it has bias (n−1)θ (zero only when n=1). Being a minimal sufficient statistic is unchanged by this rescaling; being an unbiased estimator is not.
