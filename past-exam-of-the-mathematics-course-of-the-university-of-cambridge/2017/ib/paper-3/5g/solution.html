Write w for the complex coordinate. The inverse stereographic projection is
π−1(w)=1+∣w∣2(2Rew,2Imw,∣w∣2−1)​,
(11)
with ∞ mapped to the north pole. A rotation Rz​(θ) about the third axis sends w to eiθw, represented by
Uz​(θ)=(eiθ/20​0e−iθ/2​)∈SU(2).
(12)
For the rotation Ry​(θ):(x,y,z)↦(xcosθ+zsinθ,y,−xsinθ+zcosθ), substitution in the stereographic projection gives
w⟼sw+ccw−s​,Uy​(θ)=(cs​−sc​)∈SU(2),c=cos(θ/2), s=sin(θ/2).
(13)
Both displayed matrices are unitary with determinant one, hence belong to the special unitary group.
Use the rotation-generation result that every R∈SO(3) can be written Rz​(α)Ry​(β)Rz​(γ), with angles chosen suitably. This is the Euler-angle decomposition, which is allowed here. Composition of Möbius transformations corresponds to multiplication of their matrix representatives, so
πRπ−1 is represented by Uz​(α)Uy​(β)Uz​(γ)∈SU(2).​
(14)
The formulas extend at poles in the Riemann sphere. This realizes sphere rotations as special-unitary Möbius transformations. The two representatives U and −U define the same Möbius transformation, as expected for the double covering of the special orthogonal group.
