Use the radial exponential e−αr shown in the original PDF; the TeX's e−αx is a transcription defect. Every orbital angular momentum operator annihilates a radial function, so it acts only on P=x1​+x2​+2x3​. The angular momentum of a radial function times a linear polynomial is determined by its degree-one angular factor: direct application of the rotation derivatives gives
L^2P=2ℏ2P.
(58)
Equivalently its restriction to the sphere is a degree-one spherical harmonic. Thus
L^2ψ=2ℏ2ψ,l(l+1)=2,l=1.​
(59)
Furthermore L^3​ψ=−iℏK(x1​−x2​)e−αr. Its expected value is proportional to the integral of (x1​+x2​+2x3​)(x1​−x2​)e−2αr. The cross terms are odd in a coordinate, and the integrals of x12​ and x22​ agree by rotational symmetry. Hence
⟨L^3​⟩ψ​=0.​
(60)
The state need not be an eigenstate of L^3​. If normalization is desired, ∫P2e−2αrd3x=6π/α5, so K=α5/2/6π​; the zero expectation holds after normalization for any nonzero initial K.
