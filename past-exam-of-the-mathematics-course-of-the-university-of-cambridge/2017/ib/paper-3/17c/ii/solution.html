Write R=∣p∣>a. In SI units, the exterior electrostatic potential solves
ΔΦ=−ε0​q​δ(x−p)(∣x∣>a),Φ=0(∣x∣=a),Φ→0(∣x∣→∞),
(62)
with the point-charge singularity q/(4πε0​∣x−p∣). The image charge for a grounded conducting sphere choices
p′=R2a2​p,q′=−Ra​q​
(63)
lie inside the sphere. On ∣x∣=a, direct squaring gives ∣x−p′∣=(a/R)∣x−p∣. Therefore
Φ(x)=4πε0​1​(∣x−p∣q​+∣x−p′∣q′​)
(64)
satisfies the source equation, grounded boundary value and decay at infinity. The Uniqueness of the Dirichlet problem, applied to the harmonic difference with its singularity removed, identifies this with the physical exterior potential. Thus its gradient gives the required exterior electric field.
The charge feels the induced field, equivalently the field of the image charge, excluding its own singular field. Their separation is (R2−a2)/R, so Coulomb's law gives
F=−4πε0​(R2−a2)2q2aR​Rp​.​
(65)
Its magnitude is q2aR/[4πε0​(R2−a2)2] and its direction is toward the sphere's center, independent of the sign of q. No factor one-half belongs in this force; that factor occurs in induced electrostatic energy instead.
