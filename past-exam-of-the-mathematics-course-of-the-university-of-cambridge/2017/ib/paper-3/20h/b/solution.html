Write the one-column design as x∈Rn∖{0} and S=xTx. Maximizing the normal linear model likelihood first gives β​=xTY/S. Its minimized residual sum of squares is R=∥Y−xβ​∥2. For variance v>0, the remaining log likelihood is −2n​logv−R/(2v) plus a constant, whose maximum is at
σ2=nR​.​
(75)
Here R>0 almost surely because n>1 and the true variance is positive. In the exceptional data case R=0, the likelihood is unbounded as v↓0, so there is no positive-variance maximum.
Let P=xxT/S, the orthogonal projection matrix onto the design column. Then β​−β=xTε/S and Y−xβ​=(I−P)ε. These are orthogonal linear projections of an isotropic multivariate normal distribution, hence independent. An orthonormal coordinate system adapted to x makes the residual squared length a sum of n−1 independent squared standard normals. Thus the joint distribution of least-squares and variance estimators is the product law
β​∼N(β,Sσ2​),σ2nσ2​∼χn−12​,β​ and σ2 are independent.​
(76)
In particular the maximum likelihood variance estimator is biased: Eσ2=(n−1)σ2/n; the unbiased residual estimator uses denominator n−1.
