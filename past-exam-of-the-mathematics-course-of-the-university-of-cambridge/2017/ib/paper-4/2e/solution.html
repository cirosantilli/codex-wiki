Use the class equation for the action of G on itself by conjugation:
∣G∣=∣Z(G)∣+∑j​[G:CG​(gj​)],
(4)
where the sum runs over noncentral conjugacy classes. By Lagrange's theorem, each noncentral class has size a positive power of p greater than one. Thus ∣Z(G)∣≡∣G∣≡0(modp). Since the identity element belongs to the center of a group,
∣Z(G)∣≥p>1​.
(5)
For ∣G∣=p3, the possible orders of the center of a group are p,p2,p3. Order p3 would make G an abelian group. Order p2 would make the quotient group G/Z(G) have prime order and hence be a cyclic group. But a cyclic quotient by the center forces G to be Abelian: if gZ(G) generates the quotient, any two elements are gaz and gbw with z,w∈Z(G), and their products commute. Both larger orders contradict the hypothesis. Consequently
∣Z(G)∣=p​.
(6)
