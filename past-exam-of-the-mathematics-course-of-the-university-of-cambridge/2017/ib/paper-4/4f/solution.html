OurBigBook.com Words: 156
Choose a star centre a of the open star-shaped set D and define the candidate antiderivative by a straight-segment contour integral:
F(z)=∫[a,z]​f(ζ)dζ.
(10)
For each fixed z∈D and all sufficiently small h, the filled triangle with vertices a,z,z+h lies in D. Indeed, the compact segment [a,z] has a neighbourhood of some positive radius contained in the open set D, and every point of this triangle lies within ∣h∣ of that segment. The zero triangle integral consequently gives
F(z+h)−F(z)=∫[z,z+h]​f(ζ)dζ=h∫01​f(z+th)dt.
(11)
By continuity, the last integral tends to f(z) as h→0. Hence
F′(z)=f(z)(z∈D)​.
(12)
In particular, this proves the existence of the antiderivative without incorrectly assuming the star-shaped set is convex.
On a general domain, the conclusion can fail. Take D=C∖{0} and f(z)=1/z. Every filled triangle contained in D has zero boundary integral by Cauchy integral theorem, but the unit circle has integral 2πi. An antiderivative would make every closed contour integral zero. This is the period obstruction to a holomorphic antiderivative: the local conclusion of Morera's theorem does not remove the global obstruction.
