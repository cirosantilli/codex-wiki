Write ζ=(∇×u)⋅ez​ and q=ζ−fη/h0​, so the PDF's vector potential vorticity is Q=qez​. The local TeX loses some of this boldface. Taking the vertical curl of the momentum equation gives ζt​=−f∇⋅u, while mass conservation gives ηt​=−h0​∇⋅u. Thus
qt​=ζt​−h0​f​ηt​=0,Qt​=0​.
(72)
This is the linear perturbation of shallow-water potential vorticity, scaled by h0​; it should not be confused with the full nonlinear conserved ratio (f+ζ)/h.
Since ∇⋅(ez​×u)=−ζ, taking the divergence of momentum gives (∇⋅u)t​=fζ−g∇2η. Differentiate mass conservation and use ζ=q+fη/h0​ to obtain
ηtt​−gh0​∇2η+f2η=−h0​fq=−h0​f⋅Q​.
(73)
For Q=0, substitution of the stated plane wave gives the linear rotating shallow-water dispersion relation, with c=gh0​​:
ω2=f2+c2k2,ω±​(k)=±f2+c2k2​​.
(74)
The two even branches approach ±c∣k∣ at large ∣k∣ and have values ±∣f∣ at k=0. The original dispersion diagram below also compares the speed magnitudes.
For k=0, the phase velocity and group velocity magnitudes are
∣cp​∣=∣k∣∣ω∣​=c2+k2f2​​,∣cg​∣=​dkdω​​=f2+c2k2​c2∣k∣​,∣cp​∣∣cg​∣=c2​.
(75)
The printed convention ei(kx+ωt) means the signed phase velocity is −ω/k and the signed group velocity is −dω/dk. The speed magnitudes above are independent of that sign convention.
For f=0, longer wavelengths have greater phase velocity but smaller group velocity: individual crests and wave packets therefore answer “faster or slower” differently. At long wavelength, ∣k∣≪∣f∣/c, the frequency is nearly the inertial frequency ∣f∣, ∣cp​∣∼∣f∣/∣k∣, and ∣cg​∣∼c2∣k∣/∣f∣. Rotation has its largest relative effect here, and the waves are strongly dispersive. At short wavelength, ∣k∣≫∣f∣/c, both speeds tend to c and rotation gives only a small, weakly dispersive correction. If f=0, the gravity waves are nondispersive, with speed c; at k=0 the phase velocity formula is undefined.
