The chain rule for Fréchet derivatives states that if f is differentiable at a and g is differentiable at f(a), then
D(g∘f)a​=Dgf(a)​∘Dfa​​.
(7)
The composition on the right is a composition of linear maps, equivalently a product of Jacobian matrices in coordinates.
Apply the chain rule to the affine function x↦(x,c−x). The resulting derivative is
gc′​(x)=fx​(x,c−x)−fy​(x,c−x)​.
(8)
If the two partial derivatives agree everywhere, gc′​=0. The mean value theorem makes gc​ constant on R. Define h(c)=f(c,0), which is itself differentiable by the chain rule. Taking c=x+y gives gc​(x)=gc​(c)=h(c), and therefore
f(x,y)=h(x+y)​.
(9)
No continuity of the partial derivatives beyond the assumed differentiability is needed.
