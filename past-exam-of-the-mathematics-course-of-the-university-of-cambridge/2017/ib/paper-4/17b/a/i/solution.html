Take K>0. For t>0, the diffusion fundamental solution of a linear differential operator F satisfies (∂t​−K∂x2​)F=0 and has initial value δ(x) in the sense of distributions:
t↓0lim​∫R​F(x,t)ψ(x)dx=ψ(0)​
(64)
for every smooth compactly supported test function ψ. With the usual spatial decay it has unit mass. Equivalently the causal extension Θ(t)F(x,t) is a retarded fundamental solution satisfying (∂t​−K∂x2​)(ΘF)=δ(x)δ(t).
The causal Green function is zero for t<τ and satisfies
(∂t​−K∂x2​)G(x,t;y,τ)=δ(x−y)δ(t−τ)​.
(65)
For t>τ it solves the homogeneous diffusion equation, and its initial jump is G(x,τ+;y,τ)=δ(x−y), again distributionally. The natural spatial decay and a standard bounded or integrable solution class specify the usual kernel, avoiding unrestricted growing solutions. The PDE acts on x,t; y,τ label the location and time of the unit impulse. Values of the Heaviside step function exactly at the jump do not affect these distribution identities.
