For each injection time τ, let vτ​ solve the homogeneous Cauchy problem for a partial differential equation with initial profile vτ​(x,τ)=h(x,τ). Its representation by the fundamental solution of a linear differential operator is
vτ​(x,t)=∫R​F(x−y,t−τ)h(y,τ)dy(t>τ).
(66)
The causal Green function and Duhamel principle give
ϕ(x,t)=∫0t​vτ​(x,t)dτ=∫0t​∫R​F(x−y,t−τ)h(y,τ)dydτ​.
(67)
For smooth forcing with sufficient decay, differentiating this formula gives
ϕt​=h(x,t)+∫0t​∂t​vτ​dτ=h(x,t)+Kϕxx​,ϕ(x,0)=0.
(68)
The boundary term uses the Dirac delta function limit of F, not a finite pointwise value of F(x,0). The same formula extends to weaker forcing in an appropriate integrable or distribution sense. Physically, each short time interval adds h(⋅,τ)dτ to the temperature profile; that addition subsequently undergoes homogeneous diffusion equation. The total solution is the superposition of all such diffusing injections.
