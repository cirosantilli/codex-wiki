For the same pair, S=Z[X]⊂Q[X]​. Since Z is a unique factorization domain, Gauss lemma for polynomials proves that S is a unique factorization domain.
To prove that S is not a principal ideal domain, consider the ideal I=(2,X). A generator g would divide 2 and X. Divisibility of 2 forces g to be a nonzero constant integer; divisibility of X then forces that integer to divide its coefficient 1, so g=±1. This would make I=S. But evaluation at X=0 followed by reduction modulo 2 maps every element of I to zero and maps 1 to one. Hence 1∈/I, a contradiction. This is the integer polynomial ring is not a principal ideal domain example, with both clauses satisfied by a single inclusion.
