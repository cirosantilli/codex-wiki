The Baire category theorem says that in a nonempty complete metric space, every countable intersection of open dense sets is dense. To prove it, start in an arbitrary nonempty open set O and let Gn​ be open dense. Inductively choose closed balls B(xn​,rn​) with 0<rn​≤2−n, contained in Gn​ and in the interior of the previous ball, with the first also contained in O. Density provides a point at each stage, and openness permits the smaller closed ball. The centers are a Cauchy sequence; completeness supplies a limit lying in every closed ball, hence in O∩⋂n​Gn​. The proof also works at isolated points, where sufficiently small balls are singletons.
The incomplete metric space Q is a counterexample: it is the countable union of its closed nowhere dense singletons, so the complements are open dense with empty intersection.
For the sequence in the problem, put
EN​=⋂n,m≥N​{x∈[0,1]:∣fn​(x)−fm​(x)∣≤ϵ}.
(30)
Each EN​ is closed by continuity. Pointwise convergence makes each point's values a Cauchy sequence, so [0,1]=⋃N​EN​. By the Baire category theorem, one EN​ has nonempty relative interior, which contains a nonempty open interval J in (0,1). This proves the required common tail bound.
If continuous gn​ converged pointwise to the rationality indicator, take ϵ=1/4 and let m→∞ in this bound. On J, ∣gn​−g∣≤1/4 for n≥N. Hence a fixed continuous gN​ is at least 3/4 at every rational point and at most 1/4 at every irrational point of J. The density of both sets contradicts continuity. Thus the rationality indicator is not a pointwise limit of continuous functions.
Nevertheless hn​(x)=xn are continuous and converge pointwise to h(x)=0 for x<1, h(1)=1. This limit is discontinuous at 1: a pointwise limit of continuous functions can be discontinuous.
