The Jacobian criterion on an affine chart xj​=0 says that a point of the projective hypersurface is singular exactly when all derivatives of its dehomogenized equation vanish there. The Euler homogeneous function theorem identity ∑i​xi​fxi​​=df then recovers the omitted jth derivative on X, without dividing by d. Thus the correct statement in every characteristic is
Sing(X)=X∩V(fx0​​,…,fxn​​).​
(63)
If the printed Z(I) denotes a zero locus inside X, this proves it as written. If it means the ambient projective zero locus, the assertion is false when the characteristic divides d. When the characteristic does not divide d, the Euler homogeneous function theorem forces f=0 at every common zero of the derivatives, and the intersection with X is redundant.
For a genuine counterexample to the unrestricted ambient interpretation, in characteristic p take
f=X0p​+X1p−1​X2​.
(64)
This is an irreducible homogeneous polynomial: it is primitive and linear in X2​ over k[X0​,X1​], with coprime coefficients. At [1:0:0] all partial derivatives vanish, including when p=2, but f=1. This point is outside X. Hence the ambient-locus version needs the displayed intersection or a restriction on the degree.
