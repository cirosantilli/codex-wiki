Choose a branch of t=1−z with ∣argt∣<π and write d=c−a−b∈/Z. The Gauss hypergeometric equation is
z(1−z)y′′+[c−(a+b+1)z]y′−aby=0.
(36)
Changing to t turns this into the same equation with third parameter a+b+1−c=1−d. Its solution regular at t=0 is y1​=F(a,b,1−d;t). Substitution of y=tdv gives the Gauss hypergeometric equation for v with parameters c−a,c−b,1+d. Thus y2​=tdF(c−a,c−b,1+d;t) is also a solution. Their distinct nonintegrally separated characteristic exponents at a regular singular point 0,d imply linear independence near z=1, so
F(a,b,c;z)=Ay1​(z)+By2​(z).
(37)
This also follows from the exponent transformation in the Papperitz symbol.
Initially assume ℜc>ℜb>0, so the Euler integral for the hypergeometric function applies. If ℜd>0, taking z→1 gives an integral evaluated by the beta function and determines
A=Γ(c−a)Γ(c−b)Γ(c)Γ(d)​.
(38)
Indeed y1​→1 and y2​→0 there. To find B, instead work in the nonempty parameter region ℜd<0, put z=1−ϵ and scale the integration variable by 1−s=ϵu. The singular contribution is
Γ(b)Γ(c−b)Γ(c)​ϵd∫0∞​uc−b−1(1+u)−adu.
(39)
The defining integral of the beta function evaluates this integral to Γ(c−b)Γ(−d)/Γ(a). Since ϵ−dy1​→0 and ϵ−dy2​→1, it determines B. Analytic continuation in the parameters then yields the hypergeometric connection formula
A=Γ(c−a)Γ(c−b)Γ(c)Γ(c−a−b)​,B=Γ(a)Γ(b)Γ(c)Γ(a+b−c)​.​
(40)
The usual power-series definition requires c∈/{0,−1,−2,…}; exceptional parameters need a separately specified analytic limit. Reciprocal gamma functions make vanishing coefficients at denominator poles meaningful. The branch must remain fixed when continuing in z.
