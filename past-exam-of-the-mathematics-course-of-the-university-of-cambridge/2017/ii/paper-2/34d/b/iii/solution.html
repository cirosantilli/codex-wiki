Differentiating the grand potential gives
N=kVTbf′(y),S=kVTb[(b+1)f(y)−yf′(y)],NS​=(b+1)f′(y)f(y)​−y.
(122)
A reversible adiabatic change in an isolated gas keeps S and N fixed. To infer constant y, one must check that this ratio is one-to-one, rather than merely assuming it.
Put q=y/k, n(x)=1/(1+ex−q), Fj​=∫0∞​xjn(x)dx and Mj​=∫0∞​xa+jn(x)(1−n(x))dx for j=0,1,2. Integration by parts gives M1​=bFa​ and M2​=(b+1)Fa+1​, and
kNS​=bb+1​Fa​Fa+1​​−q,dqd​kNS​=b(1−M12​M0​M2​​)<0.
(123)
The strict inequality is the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality for the positive weight xan(x)(1−n(x)), since x is not constant on its support. This proves strict monotonicity of Fermi gas entropy per particle and hence μ/T is constant. Then N=kVTbf′(y) gives VTb constant, and P=kTb+1f(y) gives
VTc=constant,PVd=constant,c=a+1,d=a+1a+2​.​
(124)
This adiabatic scaling of a Fermi gas with power-law density of states concerns positive temperature; the zero-temperature limit is a separate limiting case.
