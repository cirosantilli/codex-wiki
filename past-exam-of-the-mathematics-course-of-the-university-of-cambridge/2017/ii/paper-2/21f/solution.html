By the argument principle, the number of zeros minus poles, counted with multiplicity, in the fundamental parallelogram is (2πi)−1∮f′/fdz. The logarithmic derivative is periodic with the same lattice, so the integrals over opposite sides cancel. Thus the numbers of zeros and poles are equal.
The Weierstrass elliptic function gives a degree-two map ℘:C/Λ→P1 whose fibers identify z and −z. An even elliptic function is constant on these fibers, so it descends to a single-valued function Q on the sphere. This is meromorphic even at a branch value: near a half-period the involution is t↦−t, the Laurent series of f has only even powers, and ℘−℘0​ has a double zero and provides a local coordinate in t2. At the image of 0, ℘∼t−2 gives the same conclusion at infinity. A meromorphic function on the Riemann sphere is a rational function, so f=Q(℘)​.
A meromorphic antiderivative need not be elliptic. Take f=℘. Its double poles have zero residue, so integrating on the punctured plane gives a single-valued primitive, since every closed-contour integral is a sum of zero residues. The primitive extends meromorphically over each pole, with local principal part −1/z. If F were elliptic, it would have precisely one pole modulo the lattice, with residue −1, contradicting the zero sum of residues in a fundamental parallelogram. This is a nonconstant counterexample satisfying the stated premise; the preceding descent proves that even elliptic functions are rational in the Weierstrass function.
