For a regular parameter t, the curvature and torsion of a space curve are
κ=∣α′∣3∣α′×α′′∣​,τ=∣α′×α′′∣2det(α′,α′′,α′′′)​.
(68)
The torsion of a space curve requires κ=0. Reparameterize by arc length s and set T=αs​, N=Ts​/κ, B=T×N. Differentiating the pairwise inner products of this orthonormal Frenet frame shows that its derivative matrix is skew symmetric. Since Ts​=κN, defining τ=Ns​⋅B gives the Frenet-Serret formulas
Ts​=κN,Ns​=−κT+τB,Bs​=−τN.​
(69)
The fundamental theorem of regular space curves says that prescribed smooth κ>0 and smooth τ on an interval determine a unit-speed curve uniquely up to an orientation-preserving Euclidean rigid motion. Solve this frame's linear differential equation with an initial oriented orthonormal frame, then integrate αs​=T; uniqueness proves the assertion.
Under the intended convention that the shared parameter s is arc length, equality on an open interval gives equal position and equal Frenet frames at one point. Equal κ,τ give identical differential equations, so uniqueness gives α=α on I​. The printed “regular smooth” hypothesis alone does not establish this convention. Without common arc length, an orientation-preserving smooth change of speed that is the identity on J but not elsewhere reparameterizes a circle into a counterexample: both curves have κ=1, τ=0 and agree on J, but differ elsewhere as parameterized curves.
For an oriented surface with unit normal n, define normal curvature κn​=Ts​⋅n and geodesic curvature κg​=Ts​⋅(n×T). Thus
Ts​=κg​(n×T)+κn​n.
(70)
A unit-speed curve is a geodesic exactly when κg​=0. If also κn​≡0, then Ts​=0, so it is a straight line. A vertical ruling of a circular cylinder is such a geodesic, although the cylinder contains no open piece of a plane. A nonconstant regular straight line cannot be a simple closed curve. Hence no planar patch is required, and such a geodesic cannot be simple and closed.
Finally let the principal curvatures be κ1​,κ2​. If the Gaussian curvature K=κ1​κ2​≥0, they have the same sign or one vanishes. Euler formula for normal curvature makes κn​ a weighted average of them. For a geodesic, κ=∣κn​∣, so
κ≤max(∣κ1​∣,∣κ2​∣)≤∣κ1​+κ2​∣=2∣H∣.​
(71)
A horizontal circle on a cylinder of radius R is a geodesic with κ=1/R, K=0, ∣H∣=1/(2R), attaining equality.
