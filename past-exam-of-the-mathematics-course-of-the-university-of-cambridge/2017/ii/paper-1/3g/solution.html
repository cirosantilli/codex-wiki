The ideal observer performs maximum a posteriori decoding: for received word y, it maximizes Pr(X=c∣Y=y), equivalently Pr(X=c)Pr(Y=y∣X=c). Maximum-likelihood decoding maximizes the latter conditional likelihood alone. Minimum-distance decoding minimizes the Hamming distance d(c,y). Ties may be resolved arbitrarily among maximizers.
For independent errors in a binary symmetric channel,
Pr(Y=y∣X=c)=pd(c,y)(1−p)n−d(c,y).
(5)
For 0<p<1/2, its ratio on increasing the distance by one is p/(1−p)<1. Thus maximum-likelihood decoding and minimum-distance decoding select exactly the same codewords. At the allowed endpoint p=0, they agree on a possible observation y∈C; impossible observations have zero likelihood for every codeword, so an arbitrary maximum-likelihood tie rule need not agree with minimum distance. This qualification is needed for the literal assumption p<1/2.
For the specified observation, the Hamming distances from 000 and 111 are two and one. Their likelihoods are 3/64 and 9/64, respectively. After multiplying by the priors, their joint probabilities are 12/320 and 9/320, so their posterior probabilities are 4/7 and 3/7. Therefore
ideal observer: 000,maximum likelihood: 111,minimum distance: 111​.
