The microcanonical ensemble assigns equal probability to accessible quantum states in a narrow energy shell of an isolated system with fixed energy, particle number and external parameters. To derive the canonical ensemble, put a small system in weak thermal contact with a much larger isolated bath. If the small-system state has energy Ei​, its probability is proportional to the number Ωb​(Etotal​−Ei​) of bath states. Expanding the bath entropy gives Sb​(Etotal​−Ei​)=Sb​(Etotal​)−Ei​/T+⋯, since ∂Sb​/∂E=1/T. Negligible interaction energy and an effectively constant bath temperature therefore give pi​=e−Ei​/(kB​T)/Z. The microcanonical ensemble suits an isolated fixed-energy system; the canonical ensemble suits a system exchanging energy with a thermostat at fixed temperature, with particle number still fixed.
For one atom, summing the finite geometric series over m=−J,…,J gives the partition function
Z1​=m=−J∑J​exm=sinh(x/2)sinh((J+1/2)x)​,x=kB​TμB​B​​.
(130)
At x=0 the ratio is understood by continuity as 2J+1. Logarithmic differentiation gives the mean magnetic dipole moment
⟨μz​⟩=μB​[(J+21​)coth((J+21​)x)−21​coth(x/2)]​.
(131)
This is odd in x, has small-x expansion μB​J(J+1)x/3+O(x3), and saturates at ±JμB​. The requested common graph is plotted in units of μB​: its ordinate is ⟨μz​⟩/(JμB​), so multiplication by μB​ gives ⟨μz​⟩/J. The normalized slopes at zero are (J+1)/3 and the limits are ±1.
The magnetic susceptibility is
χ=kB​TNμB2​​[41​csch2(x/2)−(J+21​)2csch2((J+21​)x)]​.
(132)
Equivalently it is NμB2​Var(m)/(kB​T)≥0. For kB​T≫μB​∣B∣, the apparent singular terms cancel, yielding Curie law
χ∼3kB​TNμB2​J(J+1)​​.
(133)
For fixed nonzero B, T→0 and integer J≥1, the first excited magnetic level is separated from the aligned ground state by μB​∣B∣. Its exponentially small occupation gives
χ∼kB​TNμB2​​e−μB​∣B∣/(kB​T)⟶0​.
(134)
The moments are saturated, so changing the magnetic field hardly changes their alignment. This is a fixed-nonzero-field low-temperature limit. At exactly B=0, differentiating first gives the Curie expression for every T>0, which diverges as T→0; the two limits do not commute in this noninteracting model. For J=0 both moment and susceptibility vanish identically.
