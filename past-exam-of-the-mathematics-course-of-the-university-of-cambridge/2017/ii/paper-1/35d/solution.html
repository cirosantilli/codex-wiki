Use a Lorentz boost along positive x with v=E/B. Since E<cB, its dimensionless speed β=E/(cB) is less than one. The given transformation of the electric field and magnetic field yields Ey′​=γ(β)(E−vB)=0 and
β=cBE​,B′=B1−β2​=B2−E2/c2​​.
(135)
All other electric components vanish. In this frame a magnetic field does no work, so the particle's speed u<c and Lorentz factor γu​=(1−u2/c2)−1/2 are constant. In proper time, the Lorentz force equations are d(γu​ux′​)/dτ=(qB′/m)γu​uy′​ and d(γu​uy′​)/dτ=−(qB′/m)γu​ux′​. Thus, choosing the phase and origin,
ct′=γu​cτ,x′=ωγu​u​sinωτ,y′=ωγu​u​cosωτ,ω=mqB′​​.
(136)
This is the signed proper-time cyclotron frequency; the frequency with respect to t′ is ω/γu​. The inverse Lorentz boost gives
ctxy​=γu​γ(β)(cτ+ωβu​sinωτ),=γu​γ(β)(βcτ+ωu​sinωτ),=ωγu​u​cosωτ.​​
(137)
In particular the average drift speed is βc=E/B along E×B.
For u>0, q=0, set s=ωτ and d=βc/u. The dimensionless spatial curve is
x=γ(β)(ds+sins),y​=coss​.
(138)
The drift between successive cycles is 2πγ(β)d. When 2πβ≪u/c, this is small compared with the oscillation size: the curve consists of nearly closed loops with slow rightward drift. When 2πβ≫u/c, the longitudinal drift is large, producing a progressing oscillatory curve. More precisely dx/ds=γ(β)(d+coss): loops occur for d<1, cusps at d=1, and monotone progression for d>1. The stated large-drift inequality compares the drift over a full cycle, so an example of that regime with d>1 is shown; that comparison alone does not replace the exact loop criterion.
Reversing q reverses the cyclotron orientation; the drift remains along positive x. If u=0 the trajectory is pure straight drift and the dimensionless coordinates are undefined. If q=0 the particle is inertial and the displayed 1/ω parametrization must be replaced by its inertial limit.
