Consider the singularity of the injective entire function f at infinity. If removable, it is bounded outside a disk and therefore bounded globally; Liouville theorem makes it constant, a contradiction. If essential, the Casorati-Weierstrass theorem says its values outside every large disk are dense. Choose a small neighbourhood U of a finite point; the open mapping theorem puts a nonempty open disk inside f(U). A point outside a disk containing U would map into that open disk, duplicating a value already attained in U. This contradicts injectivity. Thus infinity is a pole, so f is a polynomial. If its degree were greater than one, its derivative would have a root; the local power-series form at that point has local degree at least two and cannot be injective. Hence
f(z)=az+b,a=0​.

For a nonconstant holomorphic map of connected compact Riemann surfaces of degree d, the Riemann-Hurwitz formula is
2gR​−2=d(2gS​−2)+∑p∈R​(ep​−1),
(82)
where gR​,gS​ are genera and ep​ is the local mapping degree, the multiplicity in the target fibre. There are finitely many points with ep​>1.
For a degree-two sphere map, the formula gives total ramification two. Since ep​≤2, there are exactly two distinct ramification points, each of local degree two. Their images are distinct, since a fibre has total multiplicity two. Choose a Möbius transformation T taking 0,∞ to these domain points and S0​ taking their images to 0,∞. The resulting rational map has its only zero at zero of order two and its only pole at infinity of order two, hence is cz2 with c=0. Replace S0​ by S(z)=c−1S0​(z), obtaining
