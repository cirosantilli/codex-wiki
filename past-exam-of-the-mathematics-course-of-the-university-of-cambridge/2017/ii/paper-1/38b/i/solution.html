At l2​=0 use the preceding velocity equations, or take their limit, rather than substituting an infinite δ. For real l2​>0, the reflected energy fraction is ∣R∣2, the transmitted fraction is ∣T∣2/δ, and these sum to one. An evanescent transmitted wave carries no mean normal energy flux.
When the gases match, δ=1, so R=−iβ/(2−iβ) and T=2/(2−iβ). For a nongrazing incident wave of nonzero frequency, perfect transmission means R=0, equivalent to
β=0⟺mω2=BkI4​sin4θ⟺mc02​=BkI2​sin4θ​.

The plate's inertial and bending terms cancel: the incident frequency and tangential wavevector coincide with its free flexural wave dispersion relation. Then T=1, including its phase. With positive m,B, this condition is possible only at angles and frequencies satisfying that relation; for normal incidence at nonzero frequency it cannot occur unless the mass is zero.
