The Liouville approximation theorem states that an irrational algebraic number α of degree d≥2 satisfies ∣α−a/b∣≥Cα​b−d for all integers a and b≥1, for some Cα​>0. The constant is independent of the rational approximation.
The required condition is infinitely many of the coefficients equal one. If only finitely many do, the series is a finite sum of rational numbers. Conversely let α be the sum with infinitely many nonzero terms. Its truncation sN​ has denominator dividing qN​=10N!. For N≥2, its strictly positive tail satisfies
0<α−sN​≤∑j=N+1∞​10−j!<210−(N+1)!=2qN−(N+1)​.
(3)
If α=a/b were rational, the distinct fractions α,sN​ would have distance at least 1/(bqN​), contradicting the tail bound for large N. Thus α is irrational. If it were algebraic of fixed degree d, apply the Liouville approximation theorem to sN​. Even if its reduced denominator is smaller than qN​, the lower bound is at least Cα​qN−d​. The displayed upper bound contradicts this as N→∞. Hence
α is transcendental⟺#{n:ζn​=1}=∞​.
(4)
The coincidence 0!=1! changes only the first rational contribution and has no effect on this criterion.
