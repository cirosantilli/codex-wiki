An embedded smooth d-dimensional manifold X⊂RN is locally carried by a smooth ambient coordinate change to Rd×{0}. A map between such manifolds is a smooth map between manifolds if its coordinate representatives are smooth. Its differential dfx​:Tx​X→Tf(x)​Y sends the tangent vector represented by a curve γ to (f∘γ)′(0); equivalently it is the derivative in charts.
A regular value y is one for which dfx​ is surjective at every x∈f−1(y), including vacuous regularity when the fibre is empty. For example zero is not a regular value of f:R→R, f(x)=x2, because df0​=0.
For an invertible matrix A, the derivative of the determinant is
(ddet)A​(H)=det(A)Tr(A−1H).
(90)
At a determinant-one matrix it is nonzero, since taking H=A/n gives value one. The regular level set theorem therefore makes SLn​(R)=det−1(1) an embedded manifold of dimension n2−1, also for n=1 when it is a single point. Matrix multiplication has polynomial ambient coordinates and restricts to the group, so it is smooth. At the identity,
TI​SLn​(R)={H:TrH=0},dimSLn​(R)=n2−1​.
(91)
Finally suppose the singular-matrix set were an embedded smooth manifold near zero for n≥2. Every curve t↦tEij​ lies in it, since its rank is at most one. Thus every matrix unit belongs to its tangent space at zero. These span Rn2, forcing its local dimension to be n2. An embedded manifold of full ambient dimension is locally open, but arbitrarily small scalar multiples of the identity are invertible. This contradiction proves the singular-matrix set is not an embedded smooth manifold. The argument addresses the subset-manifold convention specified at the start.
