On the normal subgroup N=⟨a,b⟩, the first diagonal entry is the linear character χ(aibj)=εiηj. Its image has order at most three, although ∣N∣=9, so its kernel of a group homomorphism has order at least three. The second entry is the inverse character and has the same kernel of a group homomorphism. Thus
ρ is never faithful​.
(52)
Elements of the other coset have off-diagonal image and cannot be the identity. If (ε,η)=(1,1), the kernel of a group homomorphism has order exactly three; otherwise it is all of N.
When both roots equal one, only C acts nontrivially and its eigenlines spanned by (1,1) and (1,−1) are invariant, so the representation is reducible. Otherwise one of A,B has two distinct eigenvalues. Any invariant line would be one of its coordinate eigenlines, but C interchanges these two lines. Hence no invariant line exists, and
ρ is irreducible⟺(ε,η)=(1,1)​.
(53)
