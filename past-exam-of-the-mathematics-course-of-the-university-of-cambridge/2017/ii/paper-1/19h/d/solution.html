We prove the conjugate product of a quadratic ideal identity aa=(N(a)), which applies to the given two-generator ideal without assuming its displayed generators form an integral basis.
The quadratic ring of integers of a number field has a basis 1,ω over Z: the vector 1 is primitive, since a rational algebraic integer must be an integer, and extends to an integral basis. Write ω2−Tω+U=0, with T,U∈Z. Let B be the least positive integer in a and let s>0 generate the set of coefficients of ω in elements of a. Its lattice basis can be chosen as B,r+sω, with r∈Z. Since Bω∈a, comparison of coefficients shows s∣B and s∣r. Write B=sd, r=se and θ=e+ω. Then
a=s⟨d,θ⟩Z​,θ+θˉ=T′=T+2e,θθˉ=U′=e2+eT+U.
(60)
The scaled ideal j=s−1a remains an OL​-ideal. From θ2−T′θ+U′=0 we get U′∈j∩Z=dZ, so d∣U′. The product jjˉ is generated as an ideal by d2,dθ,dθˉ,U′ and is contained in (d).
Moreover gcd(d,T′,U′/d)=1. If a prime ℓ divided all three, then ℓ∣T′ and ℓ2∣U′, so θ/ℓ would satisfy the monic integer polynomial z2−(T′/ℓ)z+U′/ℓ2. It would lie in OL​, impossible because its ω coefficient in the integral basis is 1/ℓ. Bezout identity now expresses one as an integer combination of d,T′,U′/d. Multiplying by d, the terms are d2, dT′=dθ+dθˉ, and U′, all in the product. Hence d belongs to it, proving jjˉ=(d).
The determinant of the lattice basis sd,s(e+ω) gives the ideal norm N(a)=s2d. Consequently
aaˉ=(s2d)=(N(a))​.
(61)
In particular ⟨b,α⟩⟨b,αˉ⟩ is principal. This does not require the original b to be nonzero or the ideal itself to be principal.
