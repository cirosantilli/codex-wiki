The equilibrium equations are x(−1+x2+βy2)=y(−1+βx2+y2)=0. For β=1 the fixed points are the origin, four axis points (±1,0),(0,±1), and four diagonal points (±s,±s) with independent signs and s=(1+β)−1/2. The hypothesis β>−1 ensures that s is real. The Jacobian matrix has diagonal entries −1+3x2+βy2, −1+βx2+3y2 and off-diagonal entries 2βxy.
At the origin both eigenvalues are −1, so it is a stable node. At each axis point the eigenvalues are 2,β−1. At each diagonal point they are 2,2(1−β)/(1+β), along the radial and transverse diagonal directions respectively. Thus

The axes and diagonals are invariant lines. For β=1/2 the axis saddle points have stable directions transverse to their axes and unstable directions along them; the diagonal points are unstable nodes. For β=2 these roles switch: the diagonal saddle points have stable transverse directions and unstable radial directions, and the axis points are unstable nodes. Their stable manifolds separate trajectories attracted to the origin from outward-escaping trajectories. Symmetry gives the other quadrants.
For β=1, the entire unit circle consists of equilibria. In polar coordinates r˙=r(r2−1) and θ˙=0: every ray inside the circle moves toward the origin, and every ray outside moves outward. The circle has neutral tangential and unstable radial directions; its points are not isolated saddle points or unstable nodes.
