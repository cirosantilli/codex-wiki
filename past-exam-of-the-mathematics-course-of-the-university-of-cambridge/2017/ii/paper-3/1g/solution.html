An Euler pseudoprime is an odd composite number N with gcd(b,N)=1 satisfying b(N−1)/2≡(Nb​)(modN), where the right side is the Jacobi symbol. A weaker convention requires only b(N−1)/2≡±1; it is distinguished below. A strong pseudoprime, with N−1=2sd and d odd, satisfies bd≡1 or b2jd≡−1 for some 0≤j<s.
By the Chinese remainder theorem for unit groups, the unit group modulo 65 is C4​×C12​. Modulo 5, b32=1. Thus the only possible sign in b32≡±1(mod65) is positive. Modulo 13, writing b=ge for a primitive root, the condition is 12∣32e, equivalently 3∣e. Consequently b has multiplicative order dividing 4 in each factor. Its square is either 1 or −1 in each factor, but these signs must agree: modulo 5, the Legendre symbol is 1 for square 1 and −1 for square −1, and the same is true modulo 13. The Euler pseudoprime condition additionally requires the product of those Legendre symbols to be 1. Hence
65 is an Euler pseudoprime to b⟺b2≡±1(mod65).​
(1)
Each sign has two roots modulo each prime, hence four roots modulo 65. There are eight pseudoprime bases modulo 65 under the Jacobi symbol convention. Under the weaker sign-only convention there are instead sixteen pseudoprime bases, since the two signs of the squares need not agree; the stated equivalence therefore specifically uses the Jacobi symbol convention.
For the strong pseudoprime condition, s=6,d=1. Since the unit group has exponent of a finite group 12, the only possibilities are b=±1 or b2=−1. The six pseudoprime bases are 1,8,18,47,57,64. For example 82=182≡−1, while 8⋅18≡14 has 142≡1 but 14≡±1. Thus the strong-pseudoprime pseudoprime bases are not a subgroup.
