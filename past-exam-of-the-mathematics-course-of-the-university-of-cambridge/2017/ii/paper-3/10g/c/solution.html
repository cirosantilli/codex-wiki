Periodicity says that M fixes θ1​ as a Möbius transformation, while B takes θ1​ to d​. Thus N=(AC​B1​D​) has integer entries and fixes d​. Equating rational and irrational parts in Ad​+B1​=d​(Cd​+D) gives A=D,B1​=dC. Consequently
A2−dC2=detN=(−1)m.

The eigenvalue of M on (θ1​,1)T is its denominator M21​θ1​+M22​>1; this uses ai​≥1 and θ1​>1, including the one-term period. The corresponding eigenvalue of N is A+Cd​>1. If the algebraic norm is −1, square this unit; otherwise keep it. In either case there is a unit u=X+Yd​>1 of algebraic norm 1 with integer X,Y. Its powers have integer coefficients and algebraic norm 1, and are distinct since uj strictly increases. Hence Pell's equation has infinitely many integer solutions.
