Context-free languages are closed under union: use a new start symbol with alternatives for two renamed context-free grammars. If they were closed under complement in Σ∗, De Morgan's laws would give
L1​∩L2​=Σ∗∖((Σ∗∖L1​)∪(Σ∗∖L2​)),
(6)
contradicting the preceding intersection example. Therefore some CFL has a non-context-free complement. This is an existence argument; it does not assert that both complements of the particular L1​,L2​ are non-context-free.
A concrete choice is L={a,b,c}∗∖{anbncn:n≥0}. Words outside a∗b∗c∗ form a regular language. Among words inside it, membership in L means the first two counts or the last two counts differ. Each unequal-count comparison has a context-free grammar: for aibj with i>j, use T→aTb∣A, A→aA∣a; for i<j, replace A by B→bB∣b. Append any number of c's, or prefix any number of a's for the analogous b,c comparison. Taking these unions gives a context-free language whose complement is the non-context-free equal-three-count language. These productions are definitions of the example, not a restriction to strict Chomsky normal form.
