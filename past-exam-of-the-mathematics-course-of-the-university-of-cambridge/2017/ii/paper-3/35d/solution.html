The Maxwell equations with vacuum permittivity and permeability give ρ=ϵ0​∇⋅E and J=μ0−1​∇×B−ϵ0​∂t​E. The current identity is the Ampère-Maxwell equation, and Faraday's law gives ∂t​B=−∇×E. With g=ϵ0​E×B,
f+∂t​g=ϵ0​[(∇⋅E)E−E×(∇×E)]+μ0−1​(∇×B)×B.
(90)
The identity (∇×B)×B=(B⋅∇)B−21​∇B2, and its electric analogue, turn this into the divergence of the Maxwell stress tensor T. Thus the tensor in the question is its negative:
σij​=ϵ0​(21​E2δij​−Ei​Ej​)+μ0​1​(21​B2δij​−Bi​Bj​),∂t​gi​+∂j​σij​=−fi​.​

The vector g is electromagnetic momentum density, and σij​ is the outward flux of its ith component across a surface normal in the j direction. In static conditions the total force on matter is Fi​=−∮σij​nj​dS; generally subtract the time derivative of the enclosed electromagnetic field momentum as well.
For the two infinite sheets, superposition and the electromagnetic field jumps give E=(σ/ϵ0​)ex​, B=μ0​Key​ between them and zero outside. Substitution yields exactly
σij​=2ϵ0​σ2​diag(−1,1,1)+2μ0​K2​diag(1,−1,1).
(92)
A thin pillbox enclosing the x=0 sheet has only its +x face in the interior electromagnetic field. Therefore
AF​=(2ϵ0​σ2​−2μ0​K2​)ex​.​
(93)
Directly, omit the sheet's self-electromagnetic field: the other sheet supplies Eother​=σex​/(2ϵ0​) and Bother​=μ0​Key​/2. The Lorentz force per area is σEother​+Kez​×Bother​, giving the same result. The electric contribution is attractive and the magnetic contribution repulsive.
If K=σv for a single moving charge population, use μ0​ϵ0​=c−2 to obtain F/A=σ2(1−v2/c2)ex​/(2ϵ0​). For physical ∣v∣<c this is attractive, so motion of these charges cannot make the force repulsive. Independently imposed currents need not satisfy that restriction on K/σ.
