For a simple homogeneous thermodynamic system, internal energy is extensive: E(λS,λV,λN)=λE(S,V,N). Euler theorem for homogeneous functions and dE=TdS−pdV+μdN give E=TS−pV+μN, hence
G=E+pV−TS=μN.​
(143)
Also dG=−SdT+Vdp+μdN, so at fixed T,N,
(∂p∂μ​)T,N​=NV​=v.​
(144)
At coexistence gas and liquid have the same temperature, pressure and chemical potential. Integrating dμ=vdp along the intervening isotherm gives ∫vdp=0. Integration by parts, with endpoints at the same coexistence pressure p∗​, gives the Maxwell construction
∫vl​vg​​[p(v,T)−p∗​]dv=0.​
(145)
The horizontal line at p∗​ replaces the mechanically unstable loop of the van der Waals isotherm. The extensivity argument presupposes the ordinary thermodynamic limit and neglects macroscopic interface contributions.
