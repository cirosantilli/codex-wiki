Assume a finite optimum with X>0 and continuous price near its completion time. With costate p∗ for the remaining-file state, the minimization Hamiltonian of an optimal-control problem is H=u(p(t)−p∗). It is independent of x, so p∗ is constant. By the Pontryagin maximum principle, pointwise minimization over [0,1] gives
u(t)=1 if p(t)<p∗,u(t)=0 if p(t)>p∗.​
(117)
This is a bang-bang control away from equality. At equality partial transmission or either endpoint rate is allowed. This also follows by exchanging a small amount of transmission from a more expensive time to a cheaper unused time. The free-terminal-time transversality condition is minu​H(T,u)+2γT=0. Since T>0, it forces p∗>p(T) and terminal transmission at rate one, yielding
p∗=p(T)+2γT.​
(118)
Without regularity/existence hypotheses an arbitrary known price need not have a finite differentiable optimum; the equality is the interior terminal-time condition used here.
For p(t)=t+1/t, X=1, feasible completion requires T>1 for finite cost. The price decreases to its minimum at one and then increases. Its cheapest unrestricted interval of length one has endpoints a,a+1 of equal price, giving a(a+1)=1 and a+1=φ=(1+5​)/2. For 1<T≤φ, the cheapest length-one subset of [0,T] is the terminal interval [T−1,T]: its left endpoint has at least the price of its right endpoint, and the earlier times are still more expensive. For T≥φ the transmission minimum is unchanged from that unrestricted interval, while the delay cost increases, so no optimum lies beyond φ.
For the terminal-interval policy the total cost is
J(T)=T−21​+logT−1T​+γT2,J′(T)=1−T(T−1)1​+2γT.
(119)
Its derivative is strictly increasing on T>1, since J′′(T)=(2T−1)/[T2(T−1)2]+2γ>0. It is negative near one and positive at φ, so the unique global optimum is
u=1 on [T−1,T],u=0 earlier,(T−1)T1​=1+2γT.​
(120)
There is exactly one positive root: none lies in (0,1) because the left side is negative, and the strictly monotone comparison gives exactly one in (1,φ). Its threshold is p(T−1)=p(T)+2γT.
