The fixed point zero has multiplier F′(0)=μ, so it is attracting for ∣μ∣<1 and repelling for ∣μ∣>1. Other real fixed points satisfy
ax2+bx+μ−1=0,x=2a−b±b2+4a(1−μ)​​.​
(125)
Their multiplier is M=1+bx+2ax2, and they are attracting exactly when −2<bx+2ax2<0. At equality M=±1 the linear test is inconclusive. For b=0 two branches meet at xs​=−b/(2a), μs​=1+b2/(4a), a generic saddle-node bifurcation. At μ=1, the nonzero branch crossing zero gives a transcritical bifurcation when b=0.
The three sketches use (a,b)=(1,0),(1,1/2),(−1,−1/2) respectively; case (iii) therefore illustrates the subcritical-flip choice.
