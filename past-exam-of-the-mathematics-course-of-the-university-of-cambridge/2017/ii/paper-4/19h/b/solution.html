Here OK​=Z[s] with s=−14​ and field discriminant −56. The imaginary-quadratic Minkowski bound for ideal classes is (2/π)56​<5, so each ideal class has an integral representative of ideal norm at most four. Representatives therefore use prime ideals above two and three. Put
p=(2,s),q=(3,s+1).
(69)
The prime two ramifies, with p2=(2), and three splits into qq​=(3). No element has field norm two, because N(a+bs)=a2+14b2; thus [p] is nontrivial of order two. Norm-four ideals represent [p]2=1 and add no generator, so the class group is generated by [p],[q].
The element 2−s has field norm 18. It belongs to p and q, but not q​, since reduction there gives s≡1(mod3). Its ideal therefore factors as
(2−s)=pq2.
(70)
The exponents follow from the ideal norm 18, or from the root s≡2(mod9) defining q2. Hence [q]2=[p], so [q] has order four and already generates all possible classes. Therefore
Cl(Q(−14​))≅Z/4Z.​
(71)
As a separate check, the reduced forms of discriminant of a binary quadratic form −56 are [1,0,14],[2,0,7],[3,2,5],[3,−2,5], giving exactly four classes.
