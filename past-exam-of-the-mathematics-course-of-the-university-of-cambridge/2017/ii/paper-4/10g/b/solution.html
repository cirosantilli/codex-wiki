Write an integral binary quadratic form as Q(u,v)=Au2+Buv+Cv2, with discriminant of a binary quadratic form d=B2−4AC. If Q(u,v)=p is prime, then gcd(u,v)=1: any common divisor would have its square dividing p. Complete (u,v) to a matrix in SL2​(Z) using Bezout identity. After the associated integral change of variables, the properly equivalent form has leading coefficient p, say [p,b,c]. Its discriminant of a binary quadratic form gives d=b2−4pc, hence b2≡d(modp).
Conversely suppose b02​≡d(modp). Since d is an integral-form discriminant of a binary quadratic form, d≡0 or 1(mod4). Choose b≡b0​(modp) with even integer parity for d≡0(mod4) and odd integer parity for d≡1(mod4). This is possible because p is odd. Then b2−d is divisible by 4p, and
Q(u,v)=pu2+buv+4pb2−d​v2​
(26)
is an integral form of discriminant of a binary quadratic form d representing p=Q(1,0). This proves the discriminant criterion for prime representation by a binary quadratic form, including the case p∣d; no assumption that d is a fundamental discriminant was used.
