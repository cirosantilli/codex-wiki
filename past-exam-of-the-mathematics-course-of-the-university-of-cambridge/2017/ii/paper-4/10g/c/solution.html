Both forms have discriminant of a binary quadratic form −60. The reduction algorithm for a positive definite binary quadratic form gives ∣b∣≤a≤c and a≤60/3​<5 for a reduced positive definite representative. Enumerating a=1,2,3,4 gives
[1,0,15],[2,2,8],[3,0,5],[4,2,4].
(27)
The second and fourth forms are imprimitive and represent only even integers. Every odd prime represented by a form of discriminant of a binary quadratic form −60 must therefore be represented by f or g.
For a prime p∤30, quadratic reciprocity gives
(p−60​)=(p−3​)(p5​)=(3p​)(5p​).
(28)
Every prime p≡1(mod15) has this symbol equal to one and hence is represented by one of the two primitive forms, by part (b). But an odd prime represented by g and different from three is 2(mod3), so these primes must be represented by f. Similarly, primes p≡2(mod15) have both symbols equal to −1, and are represented by g, since a prime represented by f is 1(mod3). Both residue classes are coprime to 15, so Dirichlet theorem on primes in arithmetic progressions proves each form represents infinitely many primes.
The same modulo-three distinction excludes every prime other than three from being represented by both. The form f does not represent three; g represents three and five but neither is represented by f, and two is represented by neither. Therefore there are no primes represented by both forms.
