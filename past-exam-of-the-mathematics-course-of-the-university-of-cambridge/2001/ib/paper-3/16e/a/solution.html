The monic orthogonal polynomial pn​ is orthogonal to every polynomial of degree less than n, since p0​,…,pn−1​ form a basis of that polynomial space. Let r1​,…,rk​ be its distinct interior roots at which its sign changes, and put q(x)=∏j=1k​(x−rj​), with q=1 if k=0. The real polynomial pn​q has constant sign throughout (a,b): multiplication by each linear factor removes its corresponding sign change, while even-multiplicity roots do not change sign. It is not identically zero. Since w>0, its weighted integral is nonzero.
If k<n, orthogonality instead gives ∫ab​pn​qwdx=0, a contradiction. Thus k≥n. Degree n also gives k≤n, so k=n and all roots have multiplicity one, with no roots left outside the interval. Therefore there are exactly n distinct simple zeros in (a,b). The weighted inner products are understood to exist, as required by the stipulated orthogonality.
