For a spherical triangle on a sphere of radius R, bounded by shorter great circle arcs and with interior angles α,β,γ, the spherical excess formula, the triangular form of the Gauss-Bonnet theorem, is
area=R2(α+β+γ−π).​
(14)
Here is an area proof. On the unit sphere, a spherical lune of angle α has area 2α: rotating around its vertices shows it occupies a fraction α/(2π) of the sphere when one uses the full angular width, or directly integrates ∫0α​∫0π​sinθdθdφ. The three side great circles of the triangle divide the sphere into eight regions, including the triangle T and its antipodal triangle −T. Choose, at each vertex, the lune containing T, and also its antipodal lune. Each of T and −T is covered three times by these six lunes; each other region is covered once. Thus, with A the area of T,
4(α+β+γ)=4π+4A.
(15)
This proves A=α+β+γ−π; scaling lengths by R scales area by R2.
Radially project each face of the regular dodecahedron from its centre onto the unit sphere. Each edge and the origin lie in a plane, so its image is a shorter great circle arc. The twelve faces give congruent convex regular spherical polygons partitioning the sphere. At each projected vertex three congruent face angles fill the tangent plane. Hence each spherical pentagon has angle 2π/3 at each of its five vertices. Splitting a pentagon into three spherical triangles and summing the spherical excess formula gives
area=532π​−3π=3π​​,each angle=32π​​.
(16)
This also agrees with 4π/12, as required by the spherical partition.
