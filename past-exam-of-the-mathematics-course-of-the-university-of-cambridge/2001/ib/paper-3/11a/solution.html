For the differentiability from continuous partial derivatives argument, choose a small box around zero inside the given open set. For h=(h1​,…,hp​) in that box, telescope f(h)−f(0) by changing one coordinate at a time. The one-dimensional mean value theorem on the jth coordinate segment gives
f(h)−f(0)=∑j=1p​hj​fj​(ξj​),
(34)
where ξj​ lies on that segment, and therefore ∥ξj​∥2​≤∥h∥2​. A zero-length segment contributes zero and needs no choice of intermediate point. Set L(h)=∑j​fj​(0)hj​. For every ε>0, continuity of the finitely many partial derivatives at zero gives ∣fj​(ξj​)−fj​(0)∣<ε/p​ when h is sufficiently small. Then the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality gives
∣f(h)−f(0)−L(h)∣≤p​ε​∑j​∣hj​∣≤ε∥h∥2​.
(35)
Thus f is Fréchet differentiable at zero, with derivative L. No continuity of the partial derivatives away from zero was used.
For the supplied one-dimensional function,
g′(s)={2ssin(1/s)−cos(1/s),0,​s=0,s=0.​
(36)
The derivative at zero follows from g(s)/s=ssin(1/s)→0. For nonzero s the derivative is continuous. At zero, the sequences sk​=1/(2πk) and tk​=1/((2k+1)π) give derivative values −1 and 1. Since fx​(x,y)=g′(x), the partial derivative is continuous exactly where x=0, irrespective of y.
Nevertheless f is differentiable at every point of the plane. The one-dimensional function g is differentiable at every real number. At a fixed (x0​,y0​),
g(x0​+h)−g(x0​)=g′(x0​)h+o(∣h∣),g(y0​+k)−g(y0​)=g′(y0​)k+o(∣k∣).
(37)
Their sum has remainder o(h2+k2​), which proves Fréchet differentiability with derivative (h,k)↦g′(x0​)h+g′(y0​)k. In particular, at the origin ∣f(h,k)∣≤h2+k2, an immediate remainder bound. Continuity of all partial derivatives is sufficient, not necessary, for differentiability.
