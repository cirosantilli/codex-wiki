The Fisher-Neyman factorization theorem says that, for a family dominated by a common measure, a statistic T is a sufficient statistic when its densities have the form gμ​(T(x))h(x) with h independent of the parameter. The displayed factorization therefore shows that (S1​,S2​) is a two-dimensional sufficient statistic.
For maximum likelihood estimation, discard parameter-independent terms from the log-likelihood:
ℓ(μ)=−nlogμ−2μ2S2​​+μS1​​,ℓ′(μ)=μ3S2​−S1​μ−nμ2​.

If S2​>0, the quadratic equation has exactly one positive root. The numerator is positive before that root and negative after it, while the likelihood tends to zero at both ends of μ>0. Hence the normal likelihood with variance equal to squared mean gives the global maximizer
μ​=2nS12​+4nS2​​−S1​​​.

This holds almost surely. The exceptional all-zero sample has S2​=0 and likelihood proportional to μ−n, so its supremum is approached as μ↓0 and there is no positive maximizer.
