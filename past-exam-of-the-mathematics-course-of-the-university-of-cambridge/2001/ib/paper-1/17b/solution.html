First work over a field of characteristic different from two. If a nonzero symmetric bilinear form had ϕ(v,v)=0 for every vector, polarization would give
2ϕ(u,v)=ϕ(u+v,u+v)−ϕ(u,u)−ϕ(v,v)=0,
(72)
a contradiction. Hence choose e with ϕ(e,e)=0. Every v decomposes uniquely as
v=ϕ(e,e)ϕ(v,e)​e+(v−ϕ(e,e)ϕ(v,e)​e),
(73)
with the second summand orthogonal to e. Induct on the orthogonal complement to obtain a diagonal basis. If the restricted form becomes zero, any basis of that remaining space completes the diagonalization. In this basis the rank of a quadratic form is exactly the number of nonzero diagonal coefficients; the remaining basis vectors span its radical of a bilinear form.
Over the reals, rescale the nonzero basis vectors so the diagonal entries are p copies of +1, q copies of −1 and z=n−p−q zeros. Define the signature of a quadratic form by σ=p−q, so r=p+q. To prove it is basis independent, note that p is the largest dimension of a linear subspace on which the form is positive-definite. The positive coordinate linear subspace attains dimension p; on any other positive linear subspace, projection to those positive coordinates is injective, since its kernel has nonpositive quadratic value. Thus its dimension is at most p. The same argument for the negative form characterizes q. This proves Sylvester's law of inertia and that the signature is well-defined.
Let ei​ and fi​ be positive and negative normalized basis vectors, and let Z be the radical of a bilinear form. The span of Z and ei​+fi​ for 1≤i≤min(p,q) is a totally isotropic subspace: all mutual bilinear pairings vanish. Its dimension is z+min(p,q). Conversely, project any totally isotropic subspace U to the nondegenerate positive-plus-negative quotient. The projected linear subspace is still null, and its projections to both the positive and negative coordinate spaces are injective: a vector with one component zero would have strictly signed quadratic value unless it vanished. Its dimension is therefore at most min(p,q), while the kernel of the quotient map on U has dimension at most z. Hence
maxdimU=z+min(p,q)=n−2r+∣σ∣​​.
(74)
This proves both construction and sharpness of the maximum dimension of a totally isotropic subspace.
For the five-variable example, the symmetric coefficient matrix is
A=​01001​10100​01010​00101​10010​​.
(75)
Its cyclic structure makes the vectors (1,ζk,ζ2k,ζ3k,ζ4k), ζ=e2πi/5, eigenvectors with eigenvalues ζk+ζ−k=2cos(2πk/5). Thus the eigenvalues are
2,25​−1​ (twice),−25​+1​ (twice).
(76)
Their product is 2, and three are positive and two negative. Therefore
detA=2,r=5,σ=1​.
(77)
The real and imaginary parts of each conjugate pair of eigenvectors give the corresponding real two-dimensional eigenspaces, consistent with the real inertia calculation.
