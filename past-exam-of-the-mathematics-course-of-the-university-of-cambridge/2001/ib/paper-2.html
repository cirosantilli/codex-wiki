The contraction mapping theorem states that a self-map f of a nonempty complete metric space (X,d) satisfying d(fu,fv)≤qd(u,v) for a fixed 0≤q<1 has a unique fixed point. Its iterates converge to that point from every starting point.
To prove it, set un+1​=f(un​). Induction gives d(un+1​,un​)≤qnd(u1​,u0​). Hence for m>n, the triangle inequality yields
d(um​,un​)≤∑j=nm−1​qjd(u1​,u0​)≤1−qqn​d(u1​,u0​).
(1)
Thus the iterates are a Cauchy sequence, with limit u by completeness. The contraction inequality gives d(fu,u)≤qd(u,un​)+d(un+1​,u)→0, so f(u)=u. For any two fixed points u,v, d(u,v)≤qd(u,v) forces equality of the points. This proves existence, uniqueness and convergence; passing m→∞ also gives the displayed error bound.
For the three-point example, positivity and symmetry of d′ are immediate. For three distinct points, the largest side has length two, no more than the sum of the other two; repeated-point triangle inequalities are trivial. Thus d′ is a metric. The discrete metric satisfies
d≤d′≤2d,​
(2)
which proves bilipschitz equivalence of the two metrics. Define
f(x)=y,f(y)=f(z)=z.​
(3)
The ratios of output to input distances for the three distinct pairs are 1/2,1/2,0 in d′, so it is a contraction with q=1/2. In d, the pair (x,y) has input and output distance one, precluding any q<1. This illustrates the contraction property under equivalent metrics.

A connected space admits no separation into two disjoint nonempty open subsets whose union is the space. A path-connected space has, for every pair of points, a continuous path from [0,1] joining them. If a path-connected space had a separation U,V, choose its endpoints in different pieces. Their inverse images under the path would separate the connected interval [0,1], a contradiction. Thus path connectedness implies connectedness.
For the specified subspace, first consider Y=I∪⋃n≥1​Jn​. Every point of a tooth Jn​ can move vertically to its base in I, then horizontally to the origin; points already on I use only the horizontal segment. Concatenating these paths shows that Y is path-connected and hence connected.
Every point (0,t) of A is a limit of (1/n,t)∈Y. Consequently Y is dense in X=Y∪A in the subspace topology. To prove X connected, suppose X=U∪V were a separation. Restricting to the connected Y puts all of Y in one piece, say U. But the other nonempty relatively open piece V must meet dense Y, a contradiction. Therefore X is connected​. This uses density, not an unproved path from the added vertical segment to the teeth.

For a bilinear form ϕ, define Fϕ​:V→V∗ by Fϕ​(v)(u)=ϕ(u,v). Bilinearity makes Fϕ​(v) a linear functional and makes Fϕ​ linear in v. Conversely, every linear F:V→V∗ defines a bilinear form by ϕF​(u,v)=F(v)(u). These constructions undo each other, giving the desired bijective correspondence.
For a nondegenerate bilinear form, Fϕ​ has zero kernel and is an isomorphism because V and its dual space have the same finite dimension. For the two forms, put
α=Fϕ1​−1​Fϕ2​​.​
(15)
It is an isomorphism and satisfies ϕ1​(u,αv)=Fϕ1​​(αv)(u)=Fϕ2​​(v)(u)=ϕ2​(u,v) for all u,v. This is the representation of a bilinear form relative to a nondegenerate bilinear form.

ϕ1​(u,αv)=ϕ2​(u,v)=ϕ2​(v,u)=ϕ1​(v,αu)=ϕ1​(αu,v).
(16)
Nondegeneracy makes the adjoint with respect to ϕ1​ unique; this equality proves α†=α​ in that bilinear-form sense. No positivity or Hermitian conjugation is assumed over the arbitrary field.

A metric space is totally bounded if, for every ϵ>0, finitely many open balls of radius ϵ cover it. The Bolzano-Weierstrass property here means that every sequence has a subsequence converging to a point of the space, namely sequential compactness.
First suppose that property holds. A Cauchy sequence has a convergent subsequence with limit x in the space. Given ϵ>0, choose an index after which all sequence terms lie within ϵ/2 of each other, and a subsequence term beyond that index within ϵ/2 of x. The triangle inequality then puts every subsequent term within ϵ of x. Thus the whole Cauchy sequence converges, proving completeness.
If total boundedness failed, some ϵ>0 would admit no finite ball cover. Choose points inductively outside the union of the ϵ-balls about previously chosen points. Distinct sequence terms then have distance at least ϵ. No subsequence can be Cauchy, and therefore none can converge, a contradiction. This proves total boundedness.
Conversely, suppose the space is complete and totally bounded. For any sequence, cover the space by finitely many radius-2−1 balls and retain the infinitely many indices in one ball. Within those indices, a finite cover by radius-2−2 balls retains an infinite subset in one smaller ball. Repeat with radii 2−k to obtain nested infinite index sets Ik​. Choose increasing indices nk​∈Ik​. For l,j≥k, both terms lie in the ball selected at step k, so
d(xnl​​,xnj​​)<21−k.
(21)
The subsequence is Cauchy and completeness makes it converge within the space. Hence the Bolzano-Weierstrass property is equivalent to completeness plus total boundedness.

On smooth wavefunctions, [xi​,pj​]=iℏδij​, while position operators commute with each other and momentum operators commute with each other. Applying the product rule for commutators gives
[xi​pj​,xk​pl​]=−iℏδjk​xi​pl​+iℏδil​xk​pj​.
(47)
Use it on the four terms in [L1​,L2​]: only [x2​p3​,x3​p1​]=−iℏx2​p1​ and [x3​p2​,x1​p3​]=iℏx1​p2​ survive, with the latter entering positively from the two minus signs. Therefore
[L1​,L2​]=iℏL3​.​
(48)
The cyclic versions similarly give [L3​,L1​]=iℏL2​ and [L3​,L2​]=−iℏL1​. Thus, for L±​=L1​±iL2​,
[L3​,L±​]=±ℏL±​.​
(49)
Now define L2=L12​+L22​+L32​. For each i,
[L2,Li​]=iℏ∑j,k​ϵjik​(Lj​Lk​+Lk​Lj​)=0,
(50)
because the Levi-Civita symbol is antisymmetric in j,k and the operator pair is symmetric. Linearity then gives [L2,L±​]=0​. The PDF superscript here is the squared total angular momentum, not the component L2​.

Every orbital angular momentum operator annihilates a radial function: substituting ∂i​f(r)=f′(r)xi​/r makes the two terms cancel. It also annihilates r2. Thus the radial factor can be carried through the angular operators. Since x32​ is independent of x1​,x2​, L3​ψ=0 for every value of a.
Direct differentiation gives
L12​x32​=2ℏ2(x32​−x22​),L22​x32​=2ℏ2(x32​−x12​),L32​x32​=0.
(51)
Consequently L2x32​=ℏ2(6x32​−2r2) and L2r2=0. For a nonzero admissible radial factor, comparison with λ(x32​+ar2)f(r) forces λ=6ℏ2 and 6a=−2. Therefore the simultaneous eigenfunction occurs at
a=−31​,L2ψ=6ℏ2ψ,L3​ψ=0.​
(52)
It is the axially symmetric quadratic orbital eigenfunction, with quantum numbers l=2, m=0 and angular dependence proportional to the spherical harmonic Y20​. The subtraction of r2/3 removes its rotationally invariant scalar component.
