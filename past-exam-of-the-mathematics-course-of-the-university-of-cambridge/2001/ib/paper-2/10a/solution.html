A metric space is totally bounded if, for every ϵ>0, finitely many open balls of radius ϵ cover it. The Bolzano-Weierstrass property here means that every sequence has a subsequence converging to a point of the space, namely sequential compactness.
First suppose that property holds. A Cauchy sequence has a convergent subsequence with limit x in the space. Given ϵ>0, choose an index after which all sequence terms lie within ϵ/2 of each other, and a subsequence term beyond that index within ϵ/2 of x. The triangle inequality then puts every subsequent term within ϵ of x. Thus the whole Cauchy sequence converges, proving completeness.
If total boundedness failed, some ϵ>0 would admit no finite ball cover. Choose points inductively outside the union of the ϵ-balls about previously chosen points. Distinct sequence terms then have distance at least ϵ. No subsequence can be Cauchy, and therefore none can converge, a contradiction. This proves total boundedness.
Conversely, suppose the space is complete and totally bounded. For any sequence, cover the space by finitely many radius-2−1 balls and retain the infinitely many indices in one ball. Within those indices, a finite cover by radius-2−2 balls retains an infinite subset in one smaller ball. Repeat with radii 2−k to obtain nested infinite index sets Ik​.
