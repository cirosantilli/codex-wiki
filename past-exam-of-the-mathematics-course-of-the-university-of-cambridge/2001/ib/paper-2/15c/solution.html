The algebraic dual space is V∗=Homk​(V,k), the vector space of linear functionals. For an ordered basis v1​,…,vn​, define the dual basis by vi(vj​)=δij​, equivalently vi(∑j​xj​vj​)=xi​. Every functional satisfies ℓ=∑i​ℓ(vi​)vi, proving spanning. Evaluating a zero linear combination on each vj​ forces every coefficient to vanish, proving independence.
For a linear map α:V→W, its dual map is α∗:W∗→V∗, α∗(ℓ)=ℓ∘α. Suppose the matrix of α satisfies α(vi​)=∑j​Aji​wj​. Then
α∗(wj)(vi​)=wj(α(vi​))=Aji​.
(34)
These are the ith coordinates of α∗(wj) in the dual basis, so the dual matrix is AT​. For complex vector spaces this is still an ordinary transpose, because the algebraic dual uses linear functionals, not conjugate-linear ones.
To prove equality of ranks directly, kerα∗ consists precisely of functionals on W vanishing on imα, its annihilator. A basis of the image extends to a basis of W; such functionals have zero values on the image basis and arbitrary values on the remaining dimW−rankα vectors. Thus dimkerα∗=dimW−rankα. The rank-nullity theorem gives
rankα∗=rankα.​
(35)
Finally, if α is invertible, for every ℓ∈V∗ the two compositions give (ℓ∘α−1)∘α=ℓ and (ℓ∘α)∘α−1=ℓ. Hence the two dual maps are mutual inverses and
(α∗)−1=(α−1)∗.​
(36)
