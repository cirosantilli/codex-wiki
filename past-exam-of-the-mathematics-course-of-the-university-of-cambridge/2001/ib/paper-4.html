For constant density ρ, the Euler equations for an inviscid fluid with a conservative force per unit mass are
∂t​u+(u⋅∇)u=−∇(p/ρ+Φ),∇⋅u=0.
(20)
Set ω=∇×u. The vector identity (u⋅∇)u=∇(∣u∣2/2)−u×ω and vanishing curl of a gradient give
∂t​ω=∇×(u×ω)=(ω⋅∇)u−(u⋅∇)ω,
(21)
where both divergence terms vanish. Thus the vorticity equation is
DtDω​=(ω⋅∇)u.​
(22)
The partial time derivative measures Eulerian change; (u⋅∇)ω is advection; the right-hand side describes stretching and tilting of vortex lines by velocity gradients. A conservative body force supplies no curl source, and constant density removes any baroclinic pressure source.
For planar velocity independent of z, vorticity is perpendicular to the plane, ω=(0,0,ω), and (ω⋅∇)u=ω∂z​u=0. Hence each material particle conserves its vorticity, Dω/Dt=0. Choose the stream function convention u=ψy​, v=−ψx​. Then
ω=vx​−uy​=−Δψ.​
(23)
In a steady flow, ψy​ωx​−ψx​ωy​=0, so vorticity is constant along streamlines. Where ∇ψ=0, use ψ as a local transverse coordinate: this equality makes ω independent of the coordinate along a streamline. Consequently
Δψ=F(ψ)locally on a regular streamline patch.​
(24)
This is steady planar vorticity as a local function of stream function.
A global single-valued F additionally requires compatible vorticity values on all components of each stream-function level set. That condition is not supplied by steady Euler flow alone. For example, ψ=x3−x gives (u,v)=(0,1−3x2), a smooth steady incompressible shear with zero advective acceleration and constant pressure. Yet ψ(0,y)=ψ(1,y)=0 while Δψ(0,y)=0 and Δψ(1,y)=6. Thus the unrestricted global deduction is false; the local result, or a suitable connected-level-set hypothesis, is the valid conclusion.

Write the Laurent series on the punctured disc as f(z)=∑j=−∞∞​aj​zj. A removable singularity means that f extends holomorphically to the origin; exactly all negative-index coefficients vanish. A pole of order k≥1 means that zkf(z) extends with nonzero value at zero; exactly a−k​=0 and every coefficient below −k vanishes. An essential singularity is neither of these cases; exactly infinitely many negative-index coefficients are nonzero.
Here is a proof of the required Casorati-Weierstrass theorem consequence. If some disc of values around w were missed in a punctured neighbourhood, then ∣f(z)−w∣≥ε>0 there. The holomorphic function q(z)=1/(f(z)−w) would be bounded, and hence have a removable singularity. If its extension had q(0)=0, then f=w+1/q would extend holomorphically. If q(0)=0, its zero has a finite positive order, since q is not identically zero; thus f would have a pole. Both contradict an essential singularity. Therefore every sufficiently small punctured neighbourhood has values arbitrarily close to every w. Choosing 0<∣zn​∣<min(r/2,1/n) with ∣f(zn​)−w∣<1/n proves
zn​→0,f(zn​)→w.​

The sequence just constructed has zn​→0 outside U, but eventually f(zn​)∈f(U). Thus some yn​∈U has f(yn​)=f(zn​) although yn​=zn​, contradicting injectivity. Assume nonzero upstream speed, so F>0. For steady constant-density flow, mass conservation gives the constant volume flux
whu=WHU.
(54)
The Bernoulli equation states that u2/2+p/ρ+gz is constant along each streamline of a steady inviscid constant-density flow under gravity. It need not have the same value across unrelated streamlines in a general rotational flow. Here uniform upstream velocity supplies the same head, and the slowly varying, nearly horizontal channel permits hydrostatic pressure p=patm​+ρg(h−z). Neglecting losses, the Bernoulli equation therefore gives
2u2​+gh=2U2​+gH.
(55)
Substitute u=UWH/(wh) and set s=h/H, F=U2/(gH), the square of the upstream Froude number. Rearrangement gives
(wW​)2=G(s),G(s)=(1+F2​)s2−F2​s3.​
(56)
For positive depth, this cubic increases to its maximum at s∗​=(F+2)/3 and then decreases until it reaches zero at (F+2)/2. Its maximum is G(s∗​)=(F+2)3/(27F). Consequently the critical width of a rectangular open-channel contraction is
wc​=W(F+2)327F​​,w(x)≥wc​.​
(57)
At equality the two positive-depth branches coalesce and the local Froude number equals one.
When w>wc​ everywhere, a smooth solution connected to the upstream state cannot change branch without passing through that coalescence. At w=W the upstream depth is s=1, with G′(1)=2(F−1)/F. If F<1, this point is on the descending, subcritical branch. At a wider location w>W, the left-hand side falls below 1=G(1), so the depth on that branch must increase: h>H for subcritical upstream flow F<1. If F>1, the upstream state is on the ascending, supercritical branch and decreasing the left-hand side decreases s: h<H for supercritical upstream flow F>1.
For F=1, wc​=W and the upstream state is critical. A literally constant-width upstream region then conflicts with the strict assumption w>wc​ everywhere. If the upstream state is interpreted only as an asymptotic critical limit, a widened channel has one solution with h<H and one with h>H; a further branch condition is required. Merely solving the cubic at one location, without retaining the upstream-connected branch, would not determine the physically continued depth.
