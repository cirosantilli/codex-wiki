Complete the square in the real-valued complex plane equation:
α​z−αβ​​2=α∣β∣2−αγ​.
(19)
Dividing by α gives center and radius
c=αβ​,r=∣α∣∣β∣2−αγ​​.​
(20)
If the radicand is zero, the locus is the single point c, a degenerate circle. If it is negative, the locus is empty. The absolute value of α is essential even when α<0.
For the requested sketch, the five centers and radii are respectively (−1/2,3​/2), (−1,2​), (−2,6​), (2,2​) and (1,0), all centers on the real axis.
The original PDF's map is f(z)=(β/βˉ​)zˉ; the converted TeX drops the conjugate on its denominator. Write β=∣β∣eiθ, so f(z)=e2iθzˉ. This is the reflection symmetry of a complex circle equation. It fixes c=β/α and satisfies
∣f(z)−c∣=∣f(z)−f(c)∣=∣z−c∣,f(f(z))=z.
(21)
Thus it maps every point of the circle into the same circle. Conversely, for any point w on that circle, z=f(w) also lies on it and f(z)=w. This proves f(C)=C, including the surjectivity required by the question.
For any three nonempty circles, fix u∈C3​. The triangle inequality gives, for every z∈C1​,w∈C2​,
∣z−w∣≤∣z−u∣+∣u−w∣≤m(C1​,C3​)+m(C2​,C3​).
(22)
Taking the maximum over z,w proves the requested inequality.
For centers c1​,c2​ and radii r1​,r2​, the maximum separation of two circles is bounded above by ∣c1​−c2​∣+r1​+r2​. If the centers differ, choose z=c1​+r1​v and w=c2​−r2​v, where v=(c1​−c2​)/∣c1​−c2​∣, to attain the bound. If the centers coincide, any unit v gives opposite points attaining r1​+r2​. Therefore
m(Cα1​β1​γ1​​,Cα2​β2​γ2​​)=∣α1​α2​∣∣α1​β2​−α2​β1​∣​+∣α1​∣∣β1​∣2−α1​γ1​​​+∣α2​∣∣β2​∣2−α2​γ2​​​.​
(23)
The function m is not a metric on positive-radius circles, since m(C,C)=2r, even though it satisfies this triangle-type inequality.
