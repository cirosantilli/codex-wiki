Compose permutations from right to left. The indicated cycle lengths immediately give X3=P2=Q2=e. Under conjugation, X−1(ab)X=(X−1a X−1b). Since X−1 sends 1,2,3 to 3,1,2 and fixes 4,
X−1PX=(31)(24)=Q,X−1QX=(32)(14)=(14)(23)=PQ.​
(18)
Direct composition also gives PQ=QP=(14)(23). Thus V={e,P,Q,PQ} is a Klein four-group. Conjugation by X cycles its three nonidentity elements, so X normalizes V.
The cosets V,VX,VX2 have four elements each and are distinct. If two coincided, a nontrivial power of X would belong to V, impossible because it has order three whereas every nonidentity element of V has order two. Hence the subgroup generated by X,P,Q contains at least twelve elements. These generators are even permutations, so the subgroup lies in the alternating group A4​, whose order is 4!/2=12; multiplying by one fixed transposition pairs even and odd permutations to justify this count. Therefore
A4​=⟨X,P,Q⟩.​
(19)
The calculation also describes A4​ as the semidirect product V⋊⟨X⟩, with the order-three factor permuting the three involutions.
