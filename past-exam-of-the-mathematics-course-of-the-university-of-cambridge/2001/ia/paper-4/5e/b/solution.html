Write r=(p−1)/2, O=1⋅3⋯(p−2) and E=2⋅4⋯(p−1). The residues p−2,p−4,…,p−2r are exactly the odd factors of O, in reverse order. Hence
O≡(−1)rE(modp),OE=(p−1)!≡−1(modp)
(11)
by Wilson's theorem. It follows that the odd-residue squared product modulo a prime is
12⋅32⋯(p−2)2=O2≡(−1)r+1=(−1)(p+1)/2(modp).​
(12)
Thus it is −1 for p≡1(mod4) and 1 for p≡3(mod4).
