proves continuity of inversion. Inversion is its own inverse, hence it is a homeomorphism of G(A) onto itself.
The spectrum of an element is σA​(x)={λ:λ1−x∈/G(A)}, and its complement is the algebra resolvent set ρA​(x). Openness of G(A) makes the algebra spectrum closed. For ∣λ∣>∥x∥,
R(λ)=(λ1−x)−1=λ1​∑n=0∞​(x/λ)n,

so it is bounded and thus compact. For nonemptiness, suppose R existed everywhere. The local Neumann expansion around each point makes R a Banach-space-valued entire function. For every bounded linear functional ℓ on A, ℓ(R(λ)) is entire, bounded on compact discs, and tends to zero at infinity by the displayed series. The Liouville theorem makes it identically zero. The Hahn-Banach theorem separates points, so R(λ)=0, contradicting (λ1−x)R(λ)=1. Hence the algebra spectrum is nonempty and compact.
If B is a closed unital subalgebra with the same identity, then ρB​(x)⊆ρA​(x). It is relatively open by the Neumann series in B. If λj​∈ρB​(x) tends to λ∈ρA​(x), continuity of inversion in A gives (λj​1−x)−1→(λ1−x)−1. Closedness puts this last inverse in B, so λ∈ρB​(x). Therefore ρB​(x) is relatively open and closed in ρA​(x). This proves the required resolvent components in a closed unital subalgebra directly, with no spectral-boundary assumption.
