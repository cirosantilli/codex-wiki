Let A,b be the coefficients of the ν-stage Runge-Kutta method. On the Dahlquist test equation y′=λy, its stability function is
R(z)=1+zbT(I−zA)−11=det(I−zA)det(I−zA+z1bT)​,z=hλ.
(1)
Both polynomials have degree at most ν, with denominator normalized to one at zero. Order 2ν implies R(z)−ez=O(z2ν+1). A rational function with these degree bounds and this accuracy is uniquely the diagonal Padé approximant [ν/ν]ez​. To see uniqueness directly, if P/Q and P/Q​ have the required accuracy, then PQ​−PQ is a polynomial of degree at most 2ν vanishing to order at least 2ν+1 at zero, hence identically zero.
The diagonal exponential Padé approximant can be written
R(z)=Pν​(−z)Pν​(z)​,Pν​(z)=∑k=0ν​(2ν)!k!(ν−k)!(2ν−k)!ν!​zk.
(2)
Use the permitted Padé property that the zeros of Pν​(−z) lie strictly in the right half-plane and numerator and denominator are coprime. Thus R has no poles in the closed left half-plane. Its real coefficients give Pν​(−iy)=Pν​(iy)​, so ∣R(iy)∣=1. Also R(z)→(−1)ν at infinity. Applying the maximum modulus principle on left half-disks and letting their radii increase proves ∣R(z)∣≤1 for Rez≤0. There are no hidden stage poles there either: the irreducible Padé denominator has degree ν, exhausting the possible degree of det(I−zA), so the latter cannot contain an additional canceled factor. Every such maximal-order Runge-Kutta method is A-stable. This is maximal-order Runge-Kutta methods are A-stable; it does not assert L-stability, since the stability function does not tend to zero at infinity.
