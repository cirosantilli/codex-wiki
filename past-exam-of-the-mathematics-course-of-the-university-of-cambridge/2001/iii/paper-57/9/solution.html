Let s∗​ be the unique sonic point, f′(s∗​)=0. For a differentiable convex function, f′ is nondecreasing, negative below s∗​ and positive above it. Split the flux into the parts propagating right and left:
f+(u)=∫s∗​u​max(f′(v),0)dv,f−(u)=∫s∗​u​min(f′(v),0)dv.
(34)
Thus f(u)=f(s∗​)+f+(u)+f−(u); explicitly, f+(u)=f(u)−f(s∗​) for u>s∗​ and zero below, while f−(u)=f(u)−f(s∗​) for u<s∗​ and zero above. The Engquist-Osher flux takes the right-going contribution from the left state and the left-going contribution from the right state:
F(a,b)=f(s∗​)+f+(a)+f−(b).
(35)
It is consistent, F(u,u)=f(u). For a transonic rarefaction wave with a≤s∗​≤b it gives f(s∗​); reversed transonic states give f(a)+f(b)−f(s∗​). This is sonic-point splitting of a convex Engquist-Osher flux.
On cells of width h let Ujn​ approximate the cell average and λ=Δt/h. The explicit Engquist-Osher method is the finite volume method
Ujn+1​=H(Uj−1n​,Ujn​,Uj+1n​)=Ujn​−λ[F(Ujn​,Uj+1n​)−F(Uj−1n​,Ujn​)].
(36)
The shared interface flux makes it conservative: sums telescope for periodic data, and the same applies on the infinite grid when boundary contributions vanish. To prove stability, choose an interval [m,M] containing the initial states and impose the CFL condition
λu∈[m,M]max​∣f′(u)∣≤1.​
(37)
The partial derivatives of the update are
Ha​=λ(f+)′(a)≥0,Hc​=−λ(f−)′(c)≥0,Hb​=1−λ[(f+)′(b)−(f−)′(b)]=1−λ∣f′(b)∣≥0.
(38)
Therefore the update is a monotone conservative scheme. Because H(q,q,q)=q, componentwise comparison with the constant states m,M proves m≤Ujn+1​≤M. Induction gives a mesh-independent maximum bound for every time level, and ensures the same CFL speed bound remains valid.
There is also a stronger L1 contraction of a monotone conservative scheme. Let T denote one whole-grid update, and let W=min(U,V) and Z=max(U,V) componentwise. Monotonicity gives TW≤TU,TV≤TZ. Hence ∣TU−TV∣≤TZ−TW componentwise, and conservation yields
h∑j​∣(TU)j​−(TV)j​∣≤h∑j​[(TZ)j​−(TW)j​]=h∑j​(Zj​−Wj​)=h∑j​∣Uj​−Vj​∣.
(39)
This applies to periodic grids or summable perturbations with vanishing end fluxes. Apply the same result to U and its one-cell translate; translation commutes with T, so
TV(TU)=∑j​∣(TU)j+1​−(TU)j​∣≤∑j​∣Uj+1​−Uj​∣=TV(U).
(40)
Thus the method is a total variation diminishing scheme. Under the stated CFL condition it preserves the initial range, contracts differences in discrete L1, and cannot increase total variation. These bounds give a precise nonlinear stability result, including across the sonic point.
Convexity alone does not remove the time-step restriction omitted in the question. For the Inviscid Burgers equation f(u)=u2/2, linearize about a positive constant U. The update becomes vjn+1​=(1−λU)vjn​+λUvj−1n​. Its alternating Fourier mode has multiplier 1−2λU, whose modulus exceeds one if λU>1. Hence unrestricted explicit time steps are unstable even for a convex flux with one sonic point. This is CFL necessity for explicit Engquist-Osher stability.
