Take the diagonal Cartan subalgebra of sl3​ and let εi​ select its ith diagonal entry, so ε1​+ε2​+ε3​=0 on it. Use simple roots α1​=ε1​−ε2​, α2​=ε2​−ε3​. The fundamental weights are ω1​=ε1​ and ω2​=−ε3​=ε1​+ε2​. The defining representation V has highest vector e1​ and the dual has highest vector e3∗​. Thus
hw(V)=ω1​,hw(V∗)=ω2​.​
(29)
Their crystal basis diagrams are B:11​22​3 and B∨:3ˉ2​2ˉ1​1ˉ, with weights εi​ and −εi​ respectively. Use the same crystal tensor-product rule as in2(d): lowering acts on the first factor when φi​(u)>εi​(v); otherwise on the second. Raising uses the first factor when φi​(u)≥εi​(v), so it reverses each lowering arrow. An edge is absent if the selected factor operation is zero.
For B⊗B, abbreviate i⊗j by ij. The complete color-one edge list is 11→21→22, 31→32, 13→23; the complete color-two list is 21→31, 22→32→33, 12→13. There are two connected components:

At the representation level, the two components are the six-dimensional symmetric square and the three-dimensional exterior square:
V⊗V≅L(2ω1​)⊕L(ω2​).​
(31)
For B⊗B∨, abbreviate i⊗jˉ​ by ijˉ​. The complete color-one edge list is 13ˉ→23ˉ, 12ˉ→22ˉ→21ˉ, 32ˉ→31ˉ; the complete color-two list is 13ˉ→12ˉ, 23ˉ→33ˉ→32ˉ, 21ˉ→31ˉ. The isolated vertex is 11ˉ. Thus the two highest vertices are 13ˉ of weight ε1​−ε3​=ω1​+ω2​, and 11ˉ of weight zero. Lowering from the former visits all eight other vertices. Consequently
V⊗V∗≅L(ω1​+ω2​)⊕L(0).​
(32)
This also follows concretely from V⊗V∗≅End(V)=sl3​⊕CI: the first term is the Adjoint representation, with highest weight the highest root, and the second is the trivial Lie algebra representation. The ordinary invariant vector is ∑i​ei​⊗ei∗​, not the bare tensor suggested by the singleton's crystal label. The explicit edge lists and the diagrams exhibit all eighteen tensor vertices and justify the four highest weights.
