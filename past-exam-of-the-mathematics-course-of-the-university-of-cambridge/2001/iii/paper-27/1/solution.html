Where the moment-generating function MY​(t)=EetY is finite in a neighborhood of zero, define the cumulant-generating function κY​(t)=logMY​(t) and the rth cumulant by κr​(Y)=κY(r)​(0). Differentiation gives
κY′​(0)=MY​(0)MY′​(0)​=EY,κY′′​(0)=MY′′​(0)−MY′​(0)2=VarY,
(1)
since MY​(0)=1. Thus the first two cumulants are the mean and variance.
Conditional on N=n, independence gives E(etT∣N=n)=MV​(t)n. Summing over the Poisson distribution yields
MT​(t)=e−ν∑n=0∞​n!(νMV​(t))n​=exp{ν(MV​(t)−1)}.
(2)
Hence the compound Poisson cumulants give
κT​(t)=ν(MV​(t)−1),ET=νEV,VarT=νEV2.​
(3)
For the moment statements, finite second moments suffice even when a moment-generating function does not exist near zero: conditioning gives VarT=ENVarV+VarN(EV)2=νEV2. This distinguishes the raw second moment from the variance of an individual claim.
For a single flood, the law of total expectation and law of total variance similarly give
ESi​=λμ,VarSi​=λ(σ2+μ2).
(4)
Condition on the number of floods and apply the same laws once more. All floods have these moments and are independent of M, so the annual total satisfies
ES=νλμ,VarS=ν[λ(σ2+μ2)+λ2μ2].​
(5)
The additional νλ2μ2 term is the variation in the number of entire flood clusters; it would be lost by treating individual claims as an ordinary Poisson stream.
For the annual count N=∑i=1M​Ni​, its probability generating function is
GN​(z)=E(eλ(z−1)M)=exp{ν(eλ(z−1)−1)}.​
(6)
This is the nested Poisson flood count. Differentiating, or conditioning on M, gives EN=νλ and VarN=νλ(1+λ). Thus N is not Poisson when ν,λ>0, since its variance exceeds its mean. If either parameter is zero it is identically zero, a degenerate Poisson distribution.
Let Ki​ be the number of valid claims in flood i. Independent validity marking gives Ki​∣Ni​∼Binomial(Ni​,1−p). Its generating function is
EzKi​=exp{λ[p+(1−p)z−1]}=exp{λ(1−p)(z−1)},
(7)
so Poisson thinning gives Ki​∼Poisson(λ(1−p))​. With validity independent also of claim amount, each claim contributes expected paid amount (1−p)μ. Therefore the annual expected payment is νλ(1−p)μ​. The independent-mark assumption is needed for this multiplication; a size-dependent invalidity rule would require the joint expected value of amount and validity instead.
