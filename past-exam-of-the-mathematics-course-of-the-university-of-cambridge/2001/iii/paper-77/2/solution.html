For integer partitions μ⊆λ, set λ/μ={(i,j):μi​<j≤λi​}. A semistandard Young tableau on it has weakly increasing rows, strictly increasing columns and positive integer entries. Its weight is xT=∏(i,j)​xT(i,j)​; their sum is the Skew Schur function sλ/μ​. Define
hr​(x)=∑i1​≤⋯≤ir​​xi1​​⋯xir​​(r>0),h0​=1,hr​=0(r<0).
(6)
The Jacobi–Trudi identity, including its skew form, is
sλ/μ​=det[hλi​−μj​−i+j​]1≤i,j≤ℓ​,​
(7)
where ℓ is any common padded length at least ℓ(λ). Taking μ empty gives the ordinary Schur function formula.
For a proof, first use finitely many variables x1​,…,xN​. A directed northeast path from Aj​=(μj​−j,1) to Bi​=(λi​−i,N) has one horizontal step for each unit of the horizontal displacement. Give such a step at height r weight xr​, and vertical steps weight one. The weakly increasing sequence of step heights shows that the sum of its weights is hλi​−μj​−i+j​.
The determinant result needed is the nonintersecting lattice-path determinant: in an acyclic weighted network, the determinant of the single-path sums is the signed sum over vertex-disjoint path families, with sign the permutation of the paired endpoints. To prove it, expand the determinant and its products of path sums. An intersecting family has a first intersection in a fixed topological order. Choose the first two indexed paths there and swap their tails. Their total weight stays the same, their endpoint permutation changes by one transposition, and the operation is its own inverse. All intersecting families cancel. Only the disjoint families remain.
In this planar network the starting and finishing points occur in the same strict left-to-right order, so disjoint paths have identity endpoint pairing. For the path in row i, associate its horizontal step from coordinate j−i−1 to j−i with cell (i,j). Reading its height gives the tableau entry. Weak increase follows from northward movement; nonintersection with the path for the next row is exactly the strict column condition. Conversely a tableau constructs the paths, and the strict column inequalities keep them disjoint. This weight-preserving bijection proves the determinant formula. Arbitrary N gives its stable symmetric function version.
The dual determinant will also be useful below. Allow steps (0,1) or (1,1), assigning weight xr​ to a diagonal step reaching level r. A single path uses distinct levels, so its sum is the elementary symmetric function er​, rather than hr​. The same cancellation proof leaves tableaux with strict rows and weak columns. Transposing them gives
sλ′/μ′​=det[eλi​−μj​−i+j​],sλ/μ​=det[eλi′​−μj′​−i+j​],
(8)
with e0​=1 and er​=0 for r<0.
Put n=∣λ∣−∣μ∣. A squarefree weight x1​⋯xn​ in sλ/μ​ uses every label once, so it is counted by the number fλ/μ of standard skew Young tableaux. In one determinant product ∏i​hri​​, the signed permutation gives ∑i​ri​=n. If any ri​<0 the product is zero; otherwise the squarefree coefficient is the number of ways to distribute the distinct labels into groups of sizes ri​, namely n!/∏i​ri​!. Coefficient extraction therefore gives the standard skew tableau determinant
fλ/μ=n!det[(λi​−μj​−i+j)!1​]1≤i,j≤ℓ​.​
(9)
Negative factorial arguments mean zero entries, and 0!=1. The empty skew shape has count one, as this convention requires.
