A skew Young diagram is connected when any two of its cells can be joined by a sequence of cells sharing edges; touching only at a corner does not connect them. A nonempty border strip, or rim hook, is a connected skew diagram containing no 2×2 square. Its height is its number of occupied rows minus one.
For a weak composition α=(α1​,…,αℓ​), a border-strip tableau of shape λ/μ and type α is a chain
μ=λ(0)⊆λ(1)⊆⋯⊆λ(ℓ)=λ,
(55)
whose ith difference is a border strip of size αi​. Filling that strip with i gives the equivalent weakly increasing row-and-column filling. A zero part corresponds to an empty difference and has height zero. Put ht(T)=∑i​ht(λ(i)/λ(i−1)). For positive r, pr​=∑j​xjr​; write pα​=∏αi​>0​pαi​​. Thus zero type parts are ignored: one does not multiply by the finite-variable value p0​=N.
We first prove the one-strip multiplication formula using alternants. Choose N sufficiently large and let βi​=μi​+N−i be the decreasing beta numbers of a partition. Expanding the determinant by permutations shows
pr​aβ​=∑i=1N​a(β1​,…,βi​+r,…,βN​)​.
(56)
Indeed, in each determinant monomial every variable occurs in exactly one row factor; multiplying that variable by its rth power is equivalent to raising that row exponent.
A term vanishes if the new exponent equals another exponent. Otherwise suppose the changed exponent moves from position i to position j≤i when restored to decreasing order. Its determinant sign is (−1)i−j. The resulting partition λ has
λj​=μi​+r−i+j,λt​=μt−1​+1(j<t≤i),
(57)
and all other rows unchanged. The new diagram contains μ, and its size increases by r. Between successive added rows, the overlap is exactly the single column μt−1​+1. Thus the added cells form a connected strip without a 2×2 square, of height i−j. Conversely every added border strip has consecutive occupied rows and exactly this single-column overlap, forcing these formulas and the corresponding exponent shift. The correspondence is therefore bijective. Divide by the unchanged Vandermonde determinant and use the bialternant formula to get the power-sum border-strip multiplication identity
pr​sμ​=λ⊇μλ/μ border strip, ∣λ∣−∣μ∣=r​∑​(−1)ht(λ/μ)sλ​.​
(58)
Apply this formula successively to each positive part of α. Each sequence of choices is exactly a border-strip tableau, and its product of signs is (−1)ht(T). Therefore
sμ​pα​=λ∑​χλ/μ(α)sλ​,χλ/μ(α)=T∑​(−1)ht(T).​
(59)
Commutativity of the power sums also shows that the resulting coefficient does not depend on the order of the positive parts of the type.
Take μ empty and restrict to N≥ℓ(λ) variables. Multiply by aδ​, with δ=(N−1,…,0):
pα​aδ​=∑ν​χν(α)aν+δ​.
(60)
In an alternant aν+δ​, the exponents are distinct and their decreasing arrangement is ν+δ. Consequently the monomial xλ+δ occurs only for ν=λ, and in that determinant its coefficient is one. This proves the Frobenius alternant character formula
χλ(α)=[xλ+δ]pα​aδ​.​
(61)
To identify these coefficients with symmetric-group characters, recall the Frobenius characteristic map
ch(ψ)=∑ρ⊢d​zρ​ψ(ρ)​pρ​,d=∣α∣.
(62)
The representation-theoretic identification ch(χλ)=sλ​ can be justified by Young's rule: the permutation character on ordered blocks of sizes ν is ∑λ​Kλν​χλ. Its characteristic is hν​, because its fixed block decompositions allocate whole cycles to blocks, and the exponential generating series for those allocations is ∏j​H(tj​). On the symmetric-function side, Hall duality and Schur orthonormality give hν​=∑λ​Kλν​sλ​. Inverting the unitriangular Kostka matrix establishes the identification. Thus the coefficient of sλ​ in pρ​ is the irreducible character value on cycle type ρ. We have supplied the border-strip computation rather than assuming the character rule.
Grouping border-strip chains by their first removal of a cycle length r now yields the Murnaghan–Nakayama rule
χλ(r,ρ)=ν⊆λλ/ν border strip of size r​∑​(−1)ht(λ/ν)χν(ρ).​
(63)
One may choose the r-cycle to be last in a strip-building chain because the power sums commute. The empty-shape value on the empty type is one, providing the recursion's initial condition.
For the staircase δ=(m−1,m−2,…,1), use m beta numbers including its padded zero row. Its beta set is
{0,2,4,…,2m−2}.
(64)
Removing a rim hook of length r lowers one beta number by r into an unoccupied nonnegative position, reversing the exponent-shift description above. For a positive even r, this move either hits another occupied even position or goes below zero. Therefore there are no removable even-size border strips.
Under the Hall inner product of symmetric functions, the adjoint of multiplication by pr​ is r∂/∂pr​: this follows on the power-sum basis from zν​/zν∖r​=rmr​(ν). Taking adjoints of the one-strip multiplication rule expresses this derivative on sδ​ as the signed sum over removable size-r strips. That sum is empty for even r, so
∂p2r​∂sδ​​=0(r≥1).
(65)
The rational symmetric-function algebra is freely generated by the power sums, and its characteristic is zero. Vanishing of each of these derivatives therefore proves the staircase Schur functions use only odd power sums statement:
s(m−1,m−2,…,1)​∈Q[p1​,p3​,p5​,…].​
(66)
Finally, the cycle type of an n-cycle has one strip of size n. A straight diagram is a border strip exactly when its second row has length at most one: otherwise it contains the upper-left 2×2 square; conversely the first row with a single-column tail is connected and contains none. These diagrams are exactly the hook partitions (n−s,1s), of height s. There is one strip filling in this case and none otherwise, giving
χλ(w)={(−1)s,0,​λ=(n−s,1s),otherwise.​​
(67)
