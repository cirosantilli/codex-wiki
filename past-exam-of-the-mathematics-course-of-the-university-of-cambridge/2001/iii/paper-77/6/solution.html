In N variables, pad λ to length N and set δ=(N−1,N−2,…,0). Define the alternant
aγ​(x)=det[xjγi​​]i,j=1N​,aδ​=∏i<j​(xi​−xj​).
(35)
The last equality is the Vandermonde determinant with the indicated descending-exponent convention.
We use the Jacobi–Trudi identity proved above and give the determinant step explicitly. Let
Jij​=hλi​−i+j​(X),Bjk​=(−1)N−jeN−j​(X∖xk​).
(36)
Put bi​=λi​+N−i≥0. The generating-function identity
HX​(t)∏r=k​(1−xr​t)=1−xk​t1​
(37)
shows, by extracting the coefficient of tbi​, that
(JB)ik​=∑j=1N​hbi​−N+j​(X)(−1)N−jeN−j​(X∖xk​)=xkbi​​.
(38)
When λ is empty, Jij​=h−i+j​ is upper triangular with diagonal one, and the product matrix is [xkN−i​]. Thus detB=aδ​. Taking determinants for general λ gives sλ​aδ​=aλ+δ​ and proves the bialternant formula
sλ​(x1​,…,xN​)=aδ​aλ+δ​​.​
(39)
The equality before division is polynomial. Consequently the quotient extends at coincident variables as the Schur polynomial.
For the Fourier minors, choose a square submatrix of size d≤p, with distinct row residues r1​,…,rd​ and column residues. Sort the columns into decreasing exponents b1​>⋯>bd​ in {0,…,p−1}; this changes a determinant only by sign. Put
λi​=bi​−(d−i).
(40)
Because the bi​ are distinct nonnegative integers, bi​≥d−i, and bi​−bi+1​≥1. Hence λ is a partition. With xj​=ζrj​, transposition of the submatrix and the bialternant formula give
detB=±ab​(x)=±aδ​(x)sλ​(x),δ=(d−1,…,0).
(41)
The xj​ are distinct, so the Vandermonde factor does not vanish.
To prove that the Schur factor cannot vanish, work in Z[ζ] and reduce modulo 1−ζ. Since the prime cyclotomic polynomial is Φp​(t)=1+t+⋯+tp−1 and Φp​(1)=p,
Z[ζ]/(1−ζ)≅Fp​.
(42)
All xj​ reduce to one, and the Schur factor reduces to sλ​(1d). We need its value, not an assumption that the specialization is nonzero. The Schur evaluation at all ones formula is
sλ​(1d)=∏i<j​j−ibi​−bj​​.
(43)
For completeness, substitute xj​=etyj​ with distinct yj​ into the bialternant. In the exponential expansions, the first nonzero determinant coefficient uses exactly the distinct powers 0,1,…,d−1 and has degree d(d−1)/2 in t. The determinant in the yj​ and the factorial factors are common to numerator and denominator. The remaining ratio is the Vandermonde product in bi​ divided by that in d−i, giving the formula as t→0. Its value is an integer because sλ​ has integer tableau coefficients.
Every positive difference bi​−bj​ and j−i is strictly less than p. Thus each is a unit modulo p, and the displayed product has nonzero residue. A zero Schur factor would have zero residue, a contradiction. This proves the prime Fourier matrix minor theorem:
Every square minor of [ζjk]j,k=0p−1​ is nonzero.​
(44)
Finally use the one-row shape λ=(r), for which s(r)​=hr​. Its numerator determinant has first-row exponents N−1+r and subsequent exponents N−2,…,0. Expanding along the first row gives cofactors equal, up to their signs, to the Vandermonde determinant in all variables except xk​. Since
aδ​(X∖xk​)aδ​(X)​=(−1)k−1∏i=k​(xk​−xi​),
(45)
the cofactor sign (−1)1+k cancels the indicated sign in the quotient. Therefore
hr​(x1​,…,xN​)=k=1∑N​xkN−1+r​i=k∏​(xk​−xi​)−1.​
(46)
This rational expression is first computed for pairwise distinct variables; its sum is the polynomial hr​, giving the continuous polynomial extension when variables coincide.
