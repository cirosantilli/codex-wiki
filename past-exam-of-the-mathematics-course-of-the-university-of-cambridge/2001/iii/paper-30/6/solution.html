For the general normal linear model, minimizing ∥Y−Xβ∥2 gives the least-squares normal equations XTXβ​=XTY. Full column rank makes XTX invertible, so
β​=(XTX)−1XTY,Eβ​=β,Cov(β​)=σ2(XTX)−1.​
(30)
The mean and covariance follow by substituting Y=Xβ+ϵ and using Eϵ=0, Cov(ϵ)=σ2I. Normal errors also give the exact multivariate normal distribution of this estimator.
Use coded experimental variables
x1​=5ξ1​−95​,x2​=2.5ξ2​−37.5​.​
(31)
Their origin is the middle of the allowed rectangle, and the stated endpoints become ±1. The four corners and n0​ center runs give mutually orthogonal columns 1,x1​,x2​, with
XTX=diag(4+n0​,4,4).
(32)
Writing Yˉ0​=n0−1​∑i=54+n0​​Yi​ when n0​>0, the ordinary least squares estimates are
β​0​β​1​β​2​​=4+n0​∑i=14+n0​​Yi​​,=4Y1​+Y2​−Y3​−Y4​​,=4Y1​−Y2​−Y3​+Y4​​.​​
(33)
Their variances are σ2/(4+n0​), σ2/4, σ2/4, with zero covariances.
The repeated center runs estimate pure error without requiring the fitted mean surface to be correct:
sPE2​=n0​−1∑i=54+n0​​(Yi​−Yˉ0​)2​(n0​≥2).
(34)
They also permit a center-point curvature contrast. Let YˉF​=(Y1​+Y2​+Y3​+Y4​)/4 and γ​=YˉF​−Yˉ0​. Under the first-order model, Eγ​=0 and Var(γ​)=σ2(1/4+1/n0​). Under a quadratic surface, its expectation is β11​+β22​, since the linear and x1​x2​ terms average to zero. Thus
sPE2​(1/4+1/n0​)γ​2​∼F1,n0​−1​
(35)
under the first-order normal model. With only one center run there is no pure-error degree of freedom. A zero center contrast does not exclude all curvature, because the two quadratic coefficients can cancel. The corners also estimate an interaction contrast; the full Lack-of-fit F-test compares the first-order fit with the five setting means, with two lack-of-fit and n0​−1 pure-error degrees of freedom.
If the first-order model is adequate and b=(β​1​,β​2​)T=0, steepest ascent in response surface methodology uses the unit coded direction d=b/∥b∥. The Cauchy-Schwarz inequality proves that it maximizes the predicted directional derivative. Run sequential settings x=hd for increasing feasible h, corresponding to physical increments (5hd1​,2.5hd2​) from the center. Stop or reduce the steps when measured yield ceases to improve, and refit locally near promising settings. Respect the allowed rectangle; if a boundary is reached, investigate feasible boundary directions rather than extrapolating outside it. A near-zero gradient offers no first-order ascent direction and calls for further modeling.
If curvature is indicated, add the four axial settings (1,0),(−1,0),(0,1),(0,−1), together with further center replication. This face-centered central composite design stays inside the allowed physical ranges and separates the two squared coordinates, which the corners alone cannot do. Fit the full quadratic regression surface
m(x)=β0​+β1​x1​+β2​x2​+β11​x12​+β12​x1​x2​+β22​x22​.
(36)
Write its fitted quadratic part as xTBx, where B11​=β​11​, B22​=β​22​ and B12​=B21​=β​12​/2. Its gradient and Hessian are b+2Bx and 2B. If B is nonsingular, the stationary setting is
x∗​=−21​B−1b.​
(37)
If B is negative definite and x∗​ is feasible, it is the fitted maximum. Otherwise compare feasible stationary settings, optima on each edge obtained from the restricted one-variable quadratic, and the corners. A singular or poorly determined B calls for attention to ridge directions and more runs. Convert any chosen coded setting back using ξ1​=95+5x1​, ξ2​=37.5+2.5x2​, and confirm its yield experimentally. The fitted local surface guides the search; it does not establish an untested physical optimum.
