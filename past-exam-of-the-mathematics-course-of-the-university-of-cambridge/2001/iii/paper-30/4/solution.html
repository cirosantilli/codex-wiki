A balanced incomplete block design has t treatments and b blocks, each containing k distinct treatments with 1<k<t. Every treatment occurs in r blocks and every distinct treatment pair occurs together in λ>0 blocks. A symmetric balanced incomplete block design has b=t, which implies r=k by the first counting identity below.
For the separate even-order condition, let N be the square treatment-by-block incidence matrix of a set system of a symmetric design. Its diagonal overlap counts are r and its off-diagonal counts are λ, giving
NNT=(r−λ)I+λJ.
(18)
The counting identities proved in the next slots show that r=k and r+(t−1)λ=r2. Consequently the eigenvalues of NNT are r2 on the constant vector and r−λ on its (t−1)-dimensional orthogonal complement. Taking determinants gives
det(N)2=r2(r−λ)t−1.
(19)
If b=t is even, the exponent t−1 is odd. The exponent of every prime in det(N)2 and in r2 is even; therefore its exponent in the positive integer r−λ must be even. This proves the even-order symmetric design square obstruction:
r−λ is a perfect square.​
(20)
It is a necessary condition, not a sufficiency claim.
