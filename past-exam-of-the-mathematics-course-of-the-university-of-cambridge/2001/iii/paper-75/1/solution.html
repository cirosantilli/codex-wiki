The closed standard fundamental domain of the modular group is
F={z=x+iy∈H:∣x∣≤21​, ∣z∣≥1}.​
(1)
Its vertical sides are paired by T:z↦z+1, and its circular sides by S:z↦−1/z. It is a fundamental region with boundary identifications. To obtain exactly one representative of each orbit, use −1/2<x≤1/2 and, on ∣z∣=1, retain the half with x≥0. We will include both vertices when discussing the closure.
To prove existence of a representative, fix z∈H. Among primitive integer pairs (c,d) choose one minimizing ∣cz+d∣. The minimum exists: below any fixed bound, ∣c∣y≤∣cz+d∣ bounds c, and then the real part bounds d, so only finitely many pairs occur. Extend this primitive pair to the bottom row of a determinant-one integer matrix γ. Since
Im(γz)=∣cz+d∣2y​,
(2)
this makes the imaginary part maximal over the orbit. Translate by an integer to put the real part in [−1/2,1/2]. If the resulting point had modulus below one, inversion by S would increase its imaginary part, a contradiction. Thus every orbit meets F.
For uniqueness in the interior, note that y≥3​/2 throughout F and that ∣cz+d∣≥1 for every primitive pair. If ∣c∣≥2, then ∣cz+d∣≥∣c∣y≥3​>1. For ∣c∣=1, the minimum over integer d is attained at d=0 or, at an endpoint, at a neighboring integer: ∣z∣≥1 and ∣z±1∣2=∣z∣2±2x+1≥1. All larger ∣d∣ give a strict inequality. If c=0, primitivity gives d=±1.
If two points in F are equivalent, applying this inequality to the transformation and its inverse shows their imaginary parts are equal. For two interior points equality forces c=0,d=±1, hence an integer translation. Their real parts lie in an interval of length one, so the translation is zero. The only matrices acting trivially are ±I. On the boundary, the equality cases give precisely the stated vertical and circular identifications. This establishes the fundamental-domain claim, including its boundary convention.
An elliptic point of a modular curve means a fixed point of a nonidentity element of the effective modular group PSL2​(Z); the central matrix −I is not counted, since it acts trivially everywhere. The equality cases above show that the only such points in the closed region are
i,ρ=−21​+23​​i,ρ+​=21​+23​​i=ρ+1.​
(3)
Their stabilizer subgroups in SL2​(Z) are
Stab(i)=⟨S⟩≅C4​,Stab(ρ)=⟨ST⟩≅C6​,Stab(ρ+​)=⟨TS⟩≅C6​,​
(4)
where
S=(01​−10​),ST=(01​−11​),TS=(11​−10​).
(5)
Indeed S2=−I, (ST)3=(TS)3=−I, and their fractional-linear fixed-point equations give the listed points. To see that these are the full stabilizer subgroups, equality in ∣cz+d∣≥1 leaves only bottom rows (0,±1),(±1,0), with the extra neighboring rows at the vertices. The fixed-point equation then fixes the top row. This yields exactly four matrices at i, six at each vertex, and just {±I} elsewhere. These are the elliptic stabilizers of the modular group. After passing to PSL2​(Z) their effective orders are two and three. The two vertices represent the same elliptic orbit.
