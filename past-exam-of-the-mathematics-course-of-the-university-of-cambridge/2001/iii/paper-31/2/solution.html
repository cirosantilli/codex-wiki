Choose the sign convention
Lb​(x,λ)=f(x)−λT(h(x)−b),x∈X,λ∈Rm.
(4)
This is the optimization Lagrangian; equality-constraint Lagrange multipliers are unrestricted in sign. The Lagrangian sufficiency theorem for minimization says: if x∗​∈X satisfies h(x∗​)=b and globally minimizes Lb​(⋅,λ∗​) on X for some λ∗​, then it globally minimizes the constrained objective function. Indeed, for every feasible x,
f(x)=Lb​(x,λ∗​)≥Lb​(x∗​,λ∗​)=f(x∗​).
(5)
This is a full global certificate, not merely stationarity. No convexity is needed for the implication, although convexity commonly supplies the required global optimization Lagrangian minimum.
Let ϕ(u)=inf{f(x):x∈X, h(x)=u}, setting inf∅=+∞, and assume ϕ(b) is finite. The Strong Lagrangian property at b means that a finite multiplier attains the constrained value as an unconstrained lower bound:
x∈Xinf​Lb​(x,λ∗​)=ϕ(b).​
(6)
This includes dual attainment, but need not include primal attainment. A non-vertical supporting hyperplane of a value function at b has a finite slope λ∗​ and supports the epigraph from below:
ϕ(u)≥ϕ(b)+λ∗T​(u−b)for every u.
(7)
If this inequality holds, then for every x∈X,
f(x)≥ϕ(h(x))≥ϕ(b)+λ∗T​(h(x)−b),
(8)
so infX​Lb​(x,λ∗​)≥ϕ(b). Conversely restricting the infimum to h(x)=b gives infX​Lb​≤ϕ(b), even if the primal infimum is approached only by a sequence. Thus equality holds. In the reverse direction, the Strong optimization Lagrangian identity implies f(x)−λ∗T​(h(x)−b)≥ϕ(b) for every x. Taking the infimum over h(x)=u yields the supporting inequality, with empty fibres causing no problem. This proves both implications without a convexity or attainment assumption beyond finite ϕ(b).
For the advertising calculation, measure the budget in thousands of pounds by B=a/1000. Requiring the full budget to be spent gives 3x+y=B, with x,y≥0. Substitute y=B−3x to reduce the maximization problem to
FB​(x)=−14x2+(7B−1)x+3B−B2,0≤x≤B/3.

Its second derivative is −28, so the unique constrained maximum is obtained by clipping the stationary point:
(x∗​,y∗​)={(0,B),(B/4−1/28, B/4+3/28),​0≤B≤1/7,B≥1/7.​​
(10)
The upper constraint never clips the interior formula because B/4−1/28<B/3 for B≥0. The maximal revenue in thousands of pounds is
R(B)={3B−B2,−B2/8+11B/4+1/56,​0≤B≤1/7,B≥1/7.​

Equivalently the revenue Hessian is (−41​1−2​), negative definite since its leading minor is negative and its determinant is seven. Thus the full-budget affine feasible set has a unique global maximum.

An additional advertising pound therefore increases optimized revenue locally by £2.50 at the smaller budget and £0.25 at the larger budget. These are marginal rates, not exact finite-difference increments: on the interior branch G(a+1)−G(a)=G′(a)−1/8000.
The maximization multiplier in f(x,y)−μ(3x+y−B) equals R′(B)=(11−B)/4 on the interior branch. The sign is consistent with the minimization convention above applied to −f, whose supporting slope is −μ. If spending is only capped by the budget, rather than required to equal it, these same answers apply at the two budgets. Beyond £11,000 the unconstrained maximum (x,y)=(19/7,20/7) is affordable, so further allowed spending is unused and the capped-budget value is constant; forced full spending would instead lower revenue.
