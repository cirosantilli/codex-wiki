A simple zero eigenvalue occurs on c=d, except at (1,1). The odd symmetry of the vector field gives a pitchfork bifurcation. Near this curve the critical eigenvalue is
m=2(1−d)d−c​+O((c−d)2).
(7)
For 0<d<1 the other two eigenvalues are stable: this is a supercritical pitchfork bifurcation as c decreases through d. For d>1 it is a subcritical pitchfork bifurcation in the center direction, with an already unstable transverse direction.
For a Hopf bifurcation, factor the characteristic polynomial as (m+τ)(m2+ω2). Comparing coefficients gives
τ=2−d/2,ω2=1−d,2c−d​=(2−d/2)(1−d).
(8)
Thus the trivial branch has the local bifurcation curves
c=d(d>0),c=(d−2)2(0<d<1).​
(9)
The second curve is a Hopf bifurcation with a stable third eigenvalue. The Routh-Hurwitz stability criterion gives the stable trivial region 0<d<1, d<c<(d−2)2. Its Hopf bifurcation is subcritical. To check the sign, normalize the critical right eigenvector by qx​=1 and put
G=2iω(τ+iω)iω−d/2​.
(10)
The second component of the normalized left projection is G. There is no quadratic term at the origin, and the cubic derivative in the second component is C2​(q,q,qˉ​)=2. The cubic Hopf coefficient is therefore l1​=ReG/ω=4/[ω((4−d)2+4ω2)]>0. A small repelling periodic orbit lies on the stable-origin side. The specified coordinate change gives the exact equations
z˙=v/2,v˙=−3v/2+3w,w˙=−(z−v)3/9.
(16)
At linear order v=2w on the center manifold. More precisely the stable coordinate η=v−2w obeys η˙​=−3η/2 at linear order, while z˙=w, w˙=0. Thus v relaxes towards its center-manifold value and can be eliminated by center manifold reduction or a properly ordered adiabatic elimination.
Write v=2w+h2​(z,w)+h3​(z,w)+⋯, where hj​ is homogeneous of degree j. At degree two the graph invariance equation is w∂z​h2​=−3h2​/2. On homogeneous quadratic polynomials the operator w∂z​ is nilpotent, so adding 3/2 makes it invertible. Hence h2​=0. Equivalently, the odd vector field permits a symmetry-compatible odd graph: odd symmetry removes even center-manifold jets. No quadratic forcing is present for adiabatic elimination either. However, setting v˙=0 alone is insufficient at cubic order, because the evolving graph has a nonzero derivative.

L=​−σ11​σr−10​−σζq0−ζ​​.
(24)
Its characteristic polynomial is m3+Am2+Bm+C, where
A=σ+1+ζ,B=σ(1−r)+σζ(1+q)+ζ,C=σζ(1+q−r).
(25)
Consequently the steady threshold is rs​=1+q​. The symmetry (a,b,d)↦(−a,−b,−d), with (c,e) unchanged, gives a generic pitchfork bifurcation, whose forward or backward direction is determined below. At threshold the other two critical-block eigenvalues satisfy m2+Am+Bs​=0, where Bs​=ζ(σ+1)−σ(1−ζ)q. They are stable if Bs​>0; if Bs​<0 there is already one unstable transverse direction. Degenerate zero cubic slope requires higher-order analysis and is not a generic pitchfork.
At a Hopf bifurcation, AB=C, B=ω2>0. Solving the coefficient equation gives
rH​=σ(σ+ζ)(1+ζ)​+σ+1ζ(σ+ζ)​q,ω2=σ+1σζ(1−ζ)​q−ζ2.​
(26)
For physical q≥0 the oscillatory threshold therefore exists exactly when 0<ζ<1 and q>q∗​. The conduction double-zero criterion for five-mode magnetoconvection gives
q∗​=σ(1−ζ)ζ(σ+1)​,r∗​=1+q∗​,0<ζ<1.​
(27)
At this Bogdanov–Takens bifurcation, the polynomial is m2(m+A) and the zero eigenspace is one-dimensional. For q<q∗​ conduction first loses stability at the steady threshold; for q>q∗​ it first loses stability at rH​<rs​. For ζ≥1 no positive-q double-zero or oscillatory threshold exists. These conclusions also follow from the Routh-Hurwitz stability criterion, B,C>0 and AB>C. r(s)=(1+s)[1+(m+s)2qm[m+(4−ϖ)s]​].​
(29)
Expanding at s=0 gives
r=1+q+r2​a2+O(a4),r2​=1+q+(4−ϖ)ζ2qϖ(2−ϖ)​.​
(30)
Near ϖ=2 the required amplitude restriction a2≪ζ2 ensures s≪m. For ϖ≤2, r2​>0, so the paired equilibrium points emerge in the forward direction r>1+q. For fixed q>0 and small ζ, the pitchfork reversal near geometric ratio two in magnetoconvection occurs at
ϖc​=2+q1+q​ζ2+O(ζ4).​
(31)
This follows by expanding the exact zero-slope equation qϖ(2−ϖ)+(4−ϖ)ζ2(1+q)=0. Above this value the branch initially runs backwards into r<1+q; at q=0 there is no reversal. This is a degenerate pitchfork bifurcation at r2​=0, not a conclusion that every emerging state is stable. If Bs​>0, the center-direction cubic term has the opposite sign to r2​: the forward branch is locally attracting and the backward branch repelling. If Bs​<0, an unstable transverse mode remains.
For the nearby geometry, the next coefficient is r4​=q[(2ϖ−5)/m2+(2−ϖ)/m], which is negative near the reversal. The exact formula eventually grows like r∼s, so a backward branch must turn at a finite amplitude, potentially outside the small-amplitude expansion.

The equilibrium points satisfy y=0, x2=λ. For λ<0 there are none. For λ>0 set a=λ​. The Jacobian matrix is
L(x∗​)=(02x∗​​1μ+x∗​​),detL=−2x∗​,trL=μ+x∗​.
(32)
The positive equilibrium point (a,0) is always a saddle equilibrium. At (−a,0) the determinant is 2a, so it is stable for μ<a and unstable for μ>a. The discriminant (μ−a)2−8a distinguishes a node from a focus; its zero does not destroy hyperbolicity and is not another local bifurcation curve.
The local bifurcation curves are
λ=0 (saddle-node, μ=0),μ=λ​ (Hopf, λ>0).​
(33)
At (0,0) the linearization has a double zero eigenvalue and is nilpotent, giving a Bogdanov–Takens bifurcation. For μ=0 on λ=0, solving the local center manifold gives x˙=(λ−x2)/μ+⋯, displaying the saddle-node bifurcation. For μ<0 the nonsaddle branch born at positive λ is attracting; for μ>0 it is repelling. On the Hopf curve the angular frequency is 2a​. The permitted subcritical Hopf bifurcation creates a repelling periodic orbit on the stable-focus side μ<a.
A local analysis alone does not say how far that periodic orbit extends. The global calculation below supplies the additional division of the stable-focus region. The complete nearby phase portraits and the leading global curve are shown together here. The lambda<0 flow has no closed periodic orbit, since a closed orbit would have index one and hence enclose an equilibrium point.

These homoclinic integrals for a quadratic-force oscillator give the simple first-order splitting zero β=5a/7, since ∂β​(βI0​+I1​)=I0​>0. The Melnikov energy-balance method therefore yields the leading global bifurcation curve
β∼75​α​,μ∼75​λ​.​
(40)
This is an asymptotic curve near the double-zero point, not an exact finite-parameter identity. More precisely, time reversal (τ,v,ε)↦(−τ,−v,−ε) preserves the loop condition. The smooth curve selected by its simple splitting zero is even in ε, giving β=5α​/7+O(ε2) and hence μ=5λ​/7+O(λ) for fixed scaled α>0.
Between the saddle-loop curve and μ=λ​, a stable focus is surrounded by the repelling periodic orbit created at the subcritical Hopf bifurcation. As μ decreases, this orbit grows until it reaches the saddle. Below the saddle-loop curve there is a stable focus and a saddle, but no nearby cycle. Above the Hopf curve there is an unstable focus and a saddle, with no nearby cycle. At the global curve the saddle separatrices form the repelling loop. Under the stipulated absence of further bifurcations, these are the distinct nearby regions in the earlier sketch; no extra remote phase structure is being inferred from the leading-order calculation.
