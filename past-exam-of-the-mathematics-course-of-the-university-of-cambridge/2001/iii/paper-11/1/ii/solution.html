For the upper bound, an explicit greedy exposure rule suffices; the sharper factor two is not needed here. Set

While at least m0​ vertices remain, construct a colour class by choosing a candidate, retaining its non-neighbours as candidates, and repeating for r choices. Expose only edges incident to chosen vertices. They will all be removed with this colour class. By deferred edge exposure in greedy independent-set colouring, the remaining internal edges are still independent Bernoulli edges conditional on the history. This assertion depends on this particular rule; it is not true for an arbitrary adaptively selected remainder.
If Mj​ candidates remain after j choices, then conditionally
Mj+1​∼Bin(Mj​−1,q).

(8)
On the event that these lower bounds hold, a class started from m≥m0​ has
Mj​≥m[q(1−2η)]j≥cp​(logn)6(0≤j≤r).

Indeed qr≥n−1(logn)8, while (1−2/logn)r is bounded below by a positive constant depending on p. Thus each conditional failure probability is at most exp[−cp​(logn)4]. A union bound over at most nr candidate steps shows that all these classes can be formed with probability tending to one. Colour the final fewer than m0​ vertices individually. 2logb​nn​≤χ(G(n,p))≤logb​nn​(1+o(1)).​

The failure estimates are summable in n. Thus they give both the usual asymptotically-almost-sure assertion and, on any common probability space carrying the sequence, eventual almost-sure validity by the first Borel-Cantelli lemma.
