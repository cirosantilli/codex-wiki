We need simultaneous concentration, not just a positive expectation. A single tentative-colour change can affect Av​ or Bv​ only in the old and new colour categories, so each function changes by at most two. A witness for Av​≥s fixes two vertices for each of s colours. A witness for Bv​≥s fixes such a nonadjacent pair and an erased neighbour together with a same-coloured adjacent vertex, at most four vertices per colour. Thus both are certifiable functions with Lipschitz constant 2 and certificate parameter at most 4. Both are bounded by C≤Δ.
To see exactly what concentration follows, let an integer-valued Z change by at most L per coordinate and have certificates of size at most rb. For an input with Z≥b, fix such a certificate J. If y has Z(y)≤a<b, form a hybrid agreeing with that input on J and with y outside J. The hybrid has value at least b, so at least (b−a)/L certificate positions differ from y. Equal weights on J show that its Talagrand convex distance from {Z≤a} is at least (b−a)/(Lrb​). A bad event at v depends only on tentative colours at distance at most two. It is independent of the entire collection of bad events at distance greater than four. Its dependency graph of events consequently has maximum degree at most 2Δ4 for large Δ. The Lovász local lemma now makes all high-degree vertices good simultaneously: exponential decay beats this polynomial dependency bound.
The needed local-lemma step can be checked directly. If every bad-event probability is at most x(1−x)D, induction on conditioning sets gives Pr(E∣avoiding specified other events)≤x: split those events into non-neighbours of E, independent of it, and at most D neighbours, whose avoidance probability is at least (1−x)D by the induction. The constants are deliberately conservative; existence of a fixed positive improvement is the point. The same proof uses an external maximum degree bound, so if infinite locally finite graphs are allowed, every finite subgraph has this uniform colouring bound and compactness extends the colouring to the whole graph.
For the stronger triangle-free result, the appropriate brief route is semi-random palette refinement for triangle-free colouring. Start with K=C0​Δ/logΔ colours and repeatedly make small tentative random assignments, retain conflict-free choices, and update the uncoloured vertices' available lists. Triangle-freeness makes each graph neighbourhood independent. Track list size and average residual neighbour congestion per colour, prune unusually congested colours, and use concentration inequalities and the Lovász local lemma to preserve these invariants through the rounds. The ratio of residual congestion to list size decreases until a final completion using the Lovász local lemma or greedy coloring succeeds. Average control and pruning are important: four-cycles are allowed, so assuming tight uniform concentration for every individual colour load would be unjustified. With a sufficiently large absolute C0​, this programme gives χ(H)=O(Δ/logΔ). The one-round fixed-fraction saving proved above alone does not establish that logarithmic improvement.
To show the order is best possible, let d→∞ through integers and sample G(n,d/n) with n=d7. A Chernoff bound and union bound give maximum degree at most 2d with probability tending to one. The expected triangle count is at most d3/6=o(n), so fewer than n/2 vertices need be deleted to remove all triangles with high probability.
