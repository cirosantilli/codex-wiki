Take ω>0, with time convention e−iωt, and use the Fourier transform pair h(k)=∫h(x)e−ikxdx, h(x)=(2π)−1∫h(k)eikxdk. For an acoustic velocity potential, u=∇ϕ and p=−ρ0​ϕt​. Its harmonic amplitude solves the Helmholtz equation. The outgoing Fourier component has the form
ϕ​(k,y)=A(k)e−γy,γ2=k2−k02​,k0​=ω/c0​.
(1)
The outgoing acoustic square-root branch is fixed by decay for evanescent components and upward radiation for propagating components:
γ(k)={k2−k02​​>0,−ik02​−k2​,​∣k∣>k0​,∣k∣<k0​.​
(2)
A precise causal prescription is to replace ω by ω+iσ, σ>0, select Reγ>0 for real k, invert on the real k axis, and take σ↓0. It corresponds to switching on the forcing from the remote past. The limiting inversion contour C passes below the positive outgoing surface-wave pole and above the negative one. The branch points are approached with the same causal prescription; analytic continuation during contour deformation must remain on this sheet. A principal real square root with positive imaginary part inside the acoustic interval would produce incoming sound and the wrong solution.
The elastic membrane's normal velocity equals the fluid velocity at the boundary, so −γA=−iωη​. Consequently
A=γiω​η​,p​(k,0)=−γρ0​ω2​η​.
(3)
The elastic membrane balance therefore gives
(Tk2−mω2−γρ0​ω2​)η​=F.
(4)
Writing D=(Tk2−mω2)γ−ρ0​ω2, the one-sided fluid-loaded membrane radiation formulas are
η(x,t)=2πF​∫C​D(k,ω)γeikx−iωt​dk,ϕ(x,y,t)=2πiωF​∫C​D(k,ω)eikx−γy−iωt​dk.​
(5)
The sign of the fluid term describes positive added mass of an evanescent fluid layer, not negative inertia.
For 0<θ<π, the acoustic saddle point is ks​=k0​cosθ, with γs​=−ik0​sinθ. Applying the stated method of steepest descent result gives
ϕrad​∼iωF2πrk0​​​Ds​sinθ​eik0​r−iωt−iπ/4,Ds​=−ρ0​ω2−ik0​sinθ(Tk02​cos2θ−mω2).
(6)
Since p=iρ0​ωϕ in amplitude notation, the angular acoustic directivity of the pressure, apart from angle-independent factors, is
D(θ)=ρ02​+k02​sin2θ[m−(T/c02​)cos2θ]2​sinθ​.​
(7)
The intensity directivity is proportional to D2. This cylindrical radiating field has amplitude of order r−1/2. The formula is for fixed interior angles; grazing-angle limits require a separate uniform approximation.
For real k>k0​, γ is positive, D→−ρ0​ω2 as k↓k0​, and D→+∞ as k→∞. A root exists. A root also requires Tk2−mω2>0. In that range,
Dk​=2Tkγ+(Tk2−mω2)γk​>0,
(8)
so the root is unique. Evenness supplies the second root, −k∗​. There are no real roots inside ∣k∣<k0​: the fluid-loading term is real and nonzero while the other term is purely imaginary. Thus there are exactly two real physical-sheet roots, ±k∗​, for nonzero forcing frequency, positive elastic-sheet tension and positive fluid mass density. The zero-frequency static limit is not a two-distinct-root statement.
These roots are counterpropagating evanescent acoustic surface waves coupled to the membrane:
ω2(m+γ∗​ρ0​​)=Tk∗2​,γ∗​=k∗2​−k02​​>0.
(9)
Their normal profiles decay as e−γ∗​y, and their phase speed is below both c0​ and the vacuum membrane speed. They matter for the elastic membrane motion and for observations near its surface, but are not automatically part of the leading far-field radiation. A residue is picked up only when the causal contour deformation crosses its pole; at fixed y/r>0 its magnitude is exponentially small, e−γ∗​rsinθ.
For example, in the right half-plane put k=k0​cosζ, γ=−ik0​sinζ and k∗​=k0​cosha. The pole is at ζ=ia and the saddle at ζ=θ. The steepest-descent path has Recos(ζ−θ)=1. At the pole height its left branch has real coordinate θ−arccos(secha); comparing it with the original contour just to the right of the pole shows that its residue is crossed for
0<θ<θs​,θs​=arccos(k0​/k∗​).
(10)
The analogous sector lies near θ=π for the negative pole. This is why the existence of real dispersion zeros alone does not make their residues relevant in every observation direction.

Let the perturbation velocity and pressure be (u,w,p)(z)eik(x−ct), with real k>0. Linearizing the Euler equations about the inviscid parallel shear flow gives
ik(U−c)u+U′w=−ikp/ρ0​,ik(U−c)w=−p′/ρ0​,iku+w′=0.
(11)
Use u=ϕ′, w=−ikϕ to satisfy incompressibility. The first momentum equation gives p/ρ0​=−(U−c)ϕ′+U′ϕ; differentiate it and use the second equation. The Rayleigh equation for inviscid shear flow follows:
(U−c)(ϕ′′−k2ϕ)−U′′ϕ=0,ϕ→0(z→±∞).​
(12)
A growing temporal normal mode has ci​=Imc>0 and growth rate kci​.
Rayleigh's inflection-point theorem states that such an instability of a smooth parallel profile requires U′′ to change sign somewhere. To prove it, divide by U−c, multiply by ϕ∗, integrate over z and integrate the second derivative by parts. Decay removes the endpoint terms, giving
∫−∞∞​(∣ϕ′∣2+k2∣ϕ∣2)dz+∫−∞∞​U−cU′′​∣ϕ∣2dz=0.
(13)
Taking the imaginary part yields
ci​∫−∞∞​(U−cr​)2+ci2​U′′∣ϕ∣2​dz=0.
(14)
The weighting is nonnegative. A one-signed U′′ that is not identically zero cannot satisfy this identity for a nontrivial mode; otherwise the mode vanishes on an interval and uniqueness of the differential equation forces it to vanish throughout. If U′′≡0, the real part of the identity is already a strictly positive integral for a nontrivial decaying mode. This proves the necessary sign-change condition.
Fjørtoft's theorem strengthens this: if Us​ is the velocity at an inflection point, instability requires U′′(U−Us​)<0 somewhere. This is a necessary condition, not a sufficient criterion. For U=tanhz, Us​=0 and
U′′(U−Us​)=−2tanh2zsech2z<0(z=0).
(15)
The possibility of instability is therefore consistent with Fjørtoft's theorem.
For the piecewise-linear approximation, U′′=0 within each of the three regions. The decaying solutions outside and the two independent interior solutions can be written
ϕ=⎩⎨⎧​A−​ek(z+1),Cekz+Ee−kz,A+​e−k(z−1),​z<−1,−1<z<1,z>1.​
(16)
At each corner, the normal velocity is continuous, so ϕ is continuous. Pressure matching at a piecewise-linear shear interface additionally gives [(U−c)ϕ′−U′ϕ]=0, hence (U−c)[ϕ′]=[U′]ϕ. At z=1 the slope jumps from 1 to 0; at z=−1 it jumps from 0 to 1. Substituting the decaying outer solutions gives
(1−2k+2kce−2k​e−2k1−2k−2kc​)(CE​)=0.
(17)
Thus the inviscid instability of a piecewise-linear mixing layer has dispersion relation
4k2c2=(2k−1)2−e−4k.​
(18)
At k=1/2, c=±i/e, so the root with positive imaginary part is an explicitly growing mode, with growth rate 1/(2e). More generally the unstable interval is 0<k<kc​, where the unique kc​>1/2 solves 2kc​−1=e−2kc​. This direct calculation establishes instability of the approximation; passing a necessary inflection test alone would not have established it.

A singular perturbation is one for which setting the parameter to zero does not give a regular approximation uniformly over the region of interest. The limiting equation may lose a derivative or boundary condition, or a formally small correction may accumulate over a long time. Here the last mechanism occurs.
At fixed t, substitute a regular asymptotic expansion. The leading equations and initial conditions give y0​=3 and x0​=sint. At first order,
x1′′​+x1​=−9cost,y1′​=sint−2,
(19)
with zero initial corrections. Therefore
x1​=−29​tsint,y1​=1−cost−2t.
(20)
The secular terms become comparable with the leading terms at t=O(ϵ−1). The fixed-time expansion is not uniform on the slow relaxation time.
Introduce τ=ϵt through the method of multiple scales, and write x=A(τ)sint+O(ϵ), y=Y(τ)+O(ϵ). Average the second equation over the rapid oscillation. Since the sine has zero mean, Y′=1−Y, where the prime now denotes d/dτ. With Y(0)=3, Y=1+2e−τ.
In the oscillator equation, 2(y′)2 is of order ϵ2. The order-ϵ resonant cosine forcing is −(2A′+3YA)cost. A bounded first correction requires its coefficient to vanish, so
2A′+3YA=0,A(0)=1.
(21)
Integration gives the slow oscillator damping by a relaxing auxiliary variable:
A(τ)=exp[−23​τ−3(1−e−τ)].​
(22)
Hence the uniform leading solution is
x(t)=e−3ϵt/2−3(1−e−ϵt)sint+O(ϵ),y(t)=1+2e−ϵt+O(ϵ).​
(23)
To check the uniform ordering, the instantaneous sine forcing in the auxiliary equation is absorbed by a bounded first correction −ϵA(τ)cost, plus a bounded slow correction chosen to match the initial data. The resonant first-order oscillator forcing has already been removed. Remaining order-ϵ2 terms accumulate by at most O(ϵ) over 0≤t≤K/ϵ for fixed K. The slow coefficients stay bounded on this interval, and variation of constants for the oscillator and the auxiliary first-order equation gives the displayed uniform errors. Expanding the amplitude near τ=0 gives A=1−9τ/2+⋯, recovering the secular term of the failed regular expansion.

y′(1)=−ϵ2​+4+O(ϵ).​
(31)
The order-one term cannot be obtained by differentiating the leading inner solution alone.

qZ​−qqθ​−ϵqθθ​=2ϵ∂θ​{LZ​−ϵ(Lθθ​+Lθ2​)}.

ψ(θ,0)={1,eUθ/(2ϵ),​θ<0,θ>0.​

I−​=∫θ∞​e−s2/(4ϵZ)ds,I+​=∫−(θ+UZ)∞​e−s2/(4ϵZ)ds,E=eU(θ+UZ/2)/(2ϵ).

Differentiating the moving endpoints gives ∂θ​I−​=−e−θ2/(4ϵZ) and E∂θ​I+​=e−θ2/(4ϵZ). These terms cancel. Hence ψθ​=(4πϵZ)−1/2UEI+​/(2ϵ), and the viscous Burgers step solution with negative flux is

The lower limit of I−​ is θ, as printed in the original PDF. The converted TeX's −θ does not give the correct convolution or endpoint cancellation.
For the vanishing-viscosity limit, the conservative flux is F(q)=−q2/2 and the characteristic speed is F′(q)=−q. If U>0, the characteristics from the right move left and collide with those on the left. The Rankine-Hugoniot condition gives shock speed [F]/[q]=−U/2. Thus
q⟶{0,U,​θ<−UZ/2,θ>−UZ/2.​
(39)
The viscous transition has width O(ϵ/U), and its centre value is exactly U/2 by the midpoint symmetry of a viscous Burgers step.
If U<0, the right characteristics move right and separate from the left ones. The limit is a rarefaction wave,
q(θ,Z)⟶⎩⎨⎧​0,−θ/Z,U,​θ≤0,0<θ<−UZ,θ≥−UZ.​​
(40)
This follows either from the Burgers Riemann problem with negative flux or the large-argument Gaussian-tail asymptotics of the exact formula. The two signs therefore give a shock and a spreading rarefaction respectively, not two shocks.

Use the conservative continuity equation and momentum balance, with σij​ the viscous stress:
ρt​+∂i​(ρui​)=0,∂t​(ρui​)+∂j​(ρui​uj​)=−∂i​p+∂j​σij​.
(46)
Differentiate the first equation in time and take the divergence of the second. Eliminating momentum gives
ρtt​=∂i​∂j​(ρui​uj​+pδij​−σij​).
(47)
Subtract c02​Δρ, and define the Lighthill stress tensor by
Tij​=ρui​uj​+[(p−p0​)−c02​(ρ−ρ0​)]δij​−σij​.​
(48)
The constant reference subtraction has no double divergence. The exact Lighthill acoustic analogy is
(∂t2​−c02​Δ)ρ′=∂i​∂j​Tij​,ρ′=ρ−ρ0​.​
(49)
This is an exact rearrangement; linearization is not used to eliminate the nonlinear source tensor.
Convolving with the retarded acoustic Green function and transferring the source derivatives by integration by parts gives
ρ′(x,t)=4πc02​1​∂xi​​∂xj​​∫∣x−y∣Tij​(y,t−∣x−y∣/c0​)​d3y.
(50)
The acoustic compact-source approximation requires source size ℓ to satisfy Ωℓ/c0​≪1 for its characteristic frequency Ω; the acoustic far field additionally requires r≫ℓ and Ωr/c0​≫1. Internal retardation is then negligible at leading order, and the radiating parts of the two observer derivatives act on the common retarded time. With ni​=xi​/r and Sij​=∫Tij​d3y, the far-field acoustic force and stress moments give
ρ′(x,t)∼4πc04​rni​nj​​S¨ij​(t−r/c0​).​
(51)
The two spatial derivatives supply two factors of 1/c0​, in addition to the 1/c02​ in the Green function.
For a subsonic fluctuating source of velocity scale V, size ℓ and turnover time ℓ/V, suppose its stress scale is Tij​=O(ρ0​V2). Then Sij​=O(ρ0​V2ℓ3) and S¨ij​=O(ρ0​V4ℓ). The compact acoustic quadrupole Mach-number scaling is therefore
ρ0​ρ′​=O(rℓ​(c0​V​)4)=O((ℓ/r)m4).​
(52)
This fourth-power statement concerns the acoustic density amplitude under the specified eddy-time and stress scaling. With an independently imposed frequency, the general estimate instead retains V2Ω2ℓ3/c04​r.

With a mass source, the continuity equation becomes ρt​+∂i​(ρui​)=M. If the conservative momentum equation has no added source, repeating the elimination gives the mass-injection term in the acoustic analogy:
(∂t2​−c02​Δ)ρ′=∂i​∂j​Tij​+∂t​M.​
(53)
More generally, injection can carry momentum. If its momentum source is Si​, the additional term is ∂t​M−∂i​Si​; prescribing M alone does not determine this dipole contribution. The displayed monopole formula assumes no separately imposed momentum source.
Let M(t)=∫M(y,t)d3y be the total mass rate. Convolution with the retarded acoustic Green function, followed by the acoustic compact-source approximation, gives the extra acoustic monopole
ρM′​(x,t)∼4πc02​rM˙(t−r/c0​)​.​
(54)
The stress quadrupole from the preceding part remains present. If required, a compact injected momentum rate Si​=∫Si​d3y adds the dipole ni​S˙i​/(4πc03​r).
A small pulsating bubble displaces liquid with volume flux V˙(t) and acts acoustically like a mass rate M=ρ0​V˙. Spherical symmetry makes this a monopole, with
ρ′∼4πc02​rρ0​​V¨(t−r/c0​).​
(55)
For volume oscillation V=V0​+ΔVcosωt, the density amplitude is ρ0​ω2∣ΔV∣/(4πc02​r). At fixed volume-displacement amplitude, it scales as ω2. Holding volume-flux amplitude fixed would instead give an ω scaling. The bubble must remain small compared with the wavelength, and a frequency-dependent dynamical bubble amplitude is a separate effect.

On a region with smooth positive area, set q=G(Z)Q(θ,ζ), ζ=h(Z). Substitution into the simple wave equation gives
Gh′Qζ​−G2QQθ​+(G′+2G​AA′​)Q=0.
(56)
Remove the last term by G′/G=−A′/(2A), and choose h′=G. Starting at a regular section Z0​, one convenient normalized area transformation of a nonlinear acoustic simple wave is
G(Z)=A(Z)A(Z0​)​​,ζ=∫Z0​Z​A(s)A(Z0​)​​ds.​
(57)
In the notation g(ζ), set g(ζ)=G(h−1(ζ)). The remaining equation is Qζ​−QQθ​=0. With initial value f at Z0​, the method of characteristics gives
Q=f(ξ),θ=ξ−f(ξ)ζ,q=G(Z)f(ξ).
(58)
The map from the initial label loses invertibility when 1−f′(ξ)ζ=0. For smooth data with bounded derivative and M=supf′>0, the first forward characteristic crossing is at ζs​=1/M; if M≤0, no forward shock occurs.
For spherical spreading, write physical radius as R>0 and specify data at an emitting radius R0​>0. Since A(R)=R2,
G=RR0​​,ζ=R0​log(R/R0​).
(59)
The shock distance for a spherical simple wave launched at finite radius is
Rs​=R0​exp(R0​M1​),M=ξsup​f′(ξ)>0.​
(60)
If the axial distance is measured from the emitting surface, Z=R−R0​, then A(Z)=(R0​+Z)2 and
Zs​=R0​[exp(R0​M1​)−1].​
(61)
No finite shock distance exists in this model when M≤0.
The printed combination A=Z2 with general bounded data at Z=0 is singular and does not define the intended outgoing spherical initial-value problem. Along a spherical characteristic, dθ/dR=−q and dq/dR=−q/R, so Rq is constant. A bounded trace at R=0 forces that constant to be zero for every characteristic curve emanating from the origin; arbitrary nonzero bounded f cannot be launched there. For example, the ostensibly admissible data f≡1 would demand both Rq=0 and a nonzero trace, a contradiction. The logarithmic transformed distance is also divergent at the origin. Thus an unqualified numerical shock formula from those literal data would be unjustified. The finite-radius formulas above give the physical interpretation once the launching radius, or the shifted area law, is supplied.
