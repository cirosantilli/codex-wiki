The resource here is one copy of a two-qubit Werner state, with the physical range 1/2<F≤1. The impossibility must include successful conditional branches of local operations and classical communication; average monotonicity alone would not exclude probabilistic enhancement.
For a normalized two-qubit pure state ∣χ⟩=∑i,j​Mij​∣ij⟩, define its concurrence as C(χ)=2∣detM∣. Its mixed-state concurrence of two qubits is the convex roof extension
C(ρ)=infρ=∑j​rj​∣χj​⟩⟨χj​∣​∑j​rj​C(χj​).
(11)
Combining near-optimal decompositions proves convexity. A separable quantum state has zero concurrence, because it has a decomposition into product vectors with zero coefficient determinant.
First establish the concurrence of a two-qubit Werner state. A pure state with squared Schmidt coefficients λ,1−λ has C=2λ(1−λ)​. In this Schmidt basis a maximally entangled state has coefficient matrix V/2​ with V unitary. Its overlap magnitude is at most (λ​+1−λ​)/2​, because ∣Vii​∣≤1. Thus the singlet overlap satisfies
∣⟨Ψ−​∣χ⟩∣2≤21+C(χ)​.
(12)
Averaging any decomposition of WF​ gives C(WF​)≥2F−1.
For the reverse bound use the orthonormal triplet vectors
t1​=Ψ+​,t2​=Φ−​,t3​=iΦ+​,∣χj,±​⟩=F​∣Ψ−​⟩±1−F​∣tj​⟩.
(13)
Each is normalized, and direct evaluation of its two-by-two coefficient determinant gives C(χj,±​)=2F−1. Their equally weighted six-state mixture has cancelled singlet–triplet coherences and triplet weight (1−F)/3, so it is exactly WF​. Hence
C(WF​)=2F−1.​
(14)
Every fully specified record of an LOCC protocol gives a product Kraus operator A⊗B on the original pair. Local ancillas and discarded local outcomes can be included by refining the record. Write its success probability and normalized output as
p=Tr[(A⊗B)WF​(A†⊗B†)],ρ′=p(A⊗B)WF​(A†⊗B†)​.
(15)
For invertible A,B, the concurrence scaling under invertible local filters is
C(ρ′)=p∣detAdetB∣​C(WF​).
(16)
Here is a derivation of that scaling. Each pure coefficient matrix transforms to AMBT, so its unnormalized determinant is multiplied by detAdetB. If that pure component has success probability pj​, its normalized concurrence is multiplied by ∣detAdetB∣/pj​, while its mixture weight becomes rj​pj​/p. Every ensemble average therefore scales by ∣detAdetB∣/p. Invertibility provides the reverse correspondence for every decomposition of ρ′, so taking both infima proves equality. If either local filter has rank one, the output is a separable quantum state and the desired bound is immediate.
It remains to show that the filter normalization cannot make this ratio exceed one. Use the Pauli matrices representation
WF​=41​(I⊗I−w∑j=13​σj​⊗σj​),w=34F−1​∈(1/3,1].
(17)
Write the positive matrices X=A†A=a0​I+a⋅σ and Y=B†B=b0​I+b⋅σ, so a0​≥∣a∣, b0​≥∣b∣. Taking traces gives
p=a0​b0​−wa⋅b≥a0​b0​−∣a∣∣b∣≥(a02​−∣a∣2)(b02​−∣b∣2)​=∣detAdetB∣.
(18)
For completeness, squaring the middle comparison leaves (a0​∣b∣−b0​∣a∣)2≥0, and both sides are nonnegative. This proves the single-copy Werner filtering normalization bound, and hence
C(ρ′)≤C(WF​)for every nonzero-probability branch.​
(19)
Forgetting some record information or grouping several successful records mixes these branch outputs; convexity preserves the same bound. Any alleged final WF′​ would therefore obey 2F′−1≤2F−1, contradicting F′>F. Thus even heralded single-copy LOCC cannot produce the specified improved Werner state. This proves that single-copy Werner filtering cannot increase concurrence. It does not forbid collective entanglement distillation from several noisy pairs; allowing additional shared entangled input copies would change the resource assumption.
