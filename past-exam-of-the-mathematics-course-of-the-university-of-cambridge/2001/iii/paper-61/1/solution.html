Choose a real unitary matrix and undo it at the second stage:
U=(q​p​​−p​q​​),U′=U†,0<p<1,q=1−p.
(1)
After the first rotation the photon qubit is q​∣0⟩+p​∣1⟩. A dud leaves this quantum superposition unchanged, so the inverse rotation restores ∣0⟩. Its final projective measurement can never return 1.
For a live bomb the absorbing component explodes with probability p. If the photon survives, the normalized state is ∣0⟩, and the second rotation makes it q​∣0⟩−p​∣1⟩. The unconditional probabilities of the three possible live-bomb outcomes are consequently
P(explosion)=p,P(safe certification)=qp,P(inconclusive)=q2.
(2)
They sum to one. The nonexplosive result 1 certifies a live bomb because its dud probability is exactly zero. The inconclusive result 0 resets the qubit to its original state, allowing another identical trial.
The repeated weak-rotation bomb-test efficiency follows by summing the geometric series over the first successful trial:
PN​=pq∑j=0N−1​q2j=1+qq​(1−q2N),P∞​=1+qq​=2−p1−p​⟶21​as p↓0.​
(3)
This is conditional on testing a live bomb, not an average over an unspecified prior fraction of duds. To make the finite-run success probability exceed 1/2−ε, choose positive p so that p/[2(2−p)]<ε/2, then choose N so that q2N<ε/2. The first condition controls the limiting deficit and the second controls the remaining tail. Exactly p=0 yields no explosion but also no information, so the limit must be approached with nonzero weak rotations.
The half-efficiency limit is consistent with the structure of this Elitzur-Vaidman bomb tester. Suppose generally U∣0⟩=a∣0⟩+b∣1⟩. A final outcome that certifies a live bomb must be orthogonal to the dud output U′U∣0⟩. Pulling this outcome backwards through U′ gives the normalized vector orthogonal to (a,b). Its squared overlap with ∣0⟩ is ∣b∣2. Thus the safe-certification probability is ∣a∣2∣b∣2, whereas the explosion probability is ∣b∣2. The former never exceeds the latter. Summing these mutually exclusive terminal events over repeated, possibly adaptive trials gives Psafe​≤Pexplosion​ and hence Psafe​≤1/2. This concerns the specified rotate–interact–rotate–measure protocol.
