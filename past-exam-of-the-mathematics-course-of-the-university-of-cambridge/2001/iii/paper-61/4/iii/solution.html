A guaranteed exact overlap device would also distinguish two remotely prepared ensembles and permit a forbidden signal. Feed it a known reference ∣ψ1​⟩=∣0⟩ and Bob's unknown state ∣ψ2​⟩, obtained from Alice's projective measurement on a shared Bell state. A device promised to work for arbitrary unknown inputs must work on this fixed-reference subset.
Alice chooses either the computational basis or the diagonal basis ∣+⟩,∣−⟩. By the same remote preparation in conjugate bases, Bob receives a uniformly random member of the chosen basis. The possible exact squared overlap outputs are
∣⟨0∣ψ2​⟩∣2={0 or 1,1/2,​ψ2​=∣1⟩ or ∣0⟩,ψ2​=∣+⟩ or ∣−⟩.​
(24)
These output sets are disjoint. Bob would infer Alice's basis choice from one run, without her communicating the random measurement result. Spacelike operation would contradict special relativity. Thus exact pure-state overlap readout would allow superluminal signalling, and no guaranteed exact overlap device is compatible with the stated assumptions.
This argument rules out even the phase-invariant magnitude squared. The raw complex inner product has an additional issue: replacing either pure state representative by a global phase changes that complex number without changing the physical input. The no-signalling contradiction therefore remains valid even if some phase convention is supplied. Statistical estimation from many identically prepared copies is not the promised exact single-pair readout.
