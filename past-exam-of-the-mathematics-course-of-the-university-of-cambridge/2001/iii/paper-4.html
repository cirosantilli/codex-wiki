Let U=(U0​,…,Um​) be a finite affine open cover of the projective scheme X. For a coherent sheaf F, form the Čech cochain complex
Cˇp(U,F)=∏i0​<⋯<ip​​Γ(Ui0​​∩⋯∩Uip​​,F),(δc)i0​…ip+1​​=∑j=0p+1​(−1)jci0​…ij​​…ip+1​​​Ui0​​∩⋯∩Uip+1​​​.
(1)
The two ways to omit any pair of indices have opposite signs, giving δ2=0. Its cohomology groups are
Hˇp(U,F)=kerδp/imδp−1.
(2)
A projective scheme over a field is a separated scheme. Finite intersections of its affine opens are affine, and a coherent sheaf is quasi-coherent. Higher sheaf cohomology of a quasi-coherent sheaf on an affine scheme vanishes. Thus this is an acyclic cover and the acyclic cover theorem identifies the displayed Čech cohomology with Hp(X,F). In particular its degree-zero kernel glues compatible local sections to Γ(X,F). A cover by m+1 opens also gives vanishing for p>m.
For the cohomology of twists on projective space, take S=k[X0​,…,Xr​] with the usual grading and Ui​=D+​(Xi​). On an intersection indexed by the nonempty set J,
Γ(UJ​,O(n))=(S[Xj−1​:j∈J])n​.
(3)
This follows directly from the construction of the twisting sheaf on projective space: sections of the twist are the degree-n homogeneous fractions in the corresponding localization. The Čech differential preserves each Laurent monomial Xa=X0a0​​⋯Xrar​​, where ai​∈Z and ∑i​ai​=n. Such a monomial appears precisely in summands with
N(a):={i:ai​<0}⊆J.
(4)
The complex therefore decomposes as the direct sum of finite-dimensional cochain complexes indexed by these exponent vectors. Their differentials have only the alternating signs of the simplex incidence maps.
If N(a)=∅, this is the ordinary unaugmented simplex cochain complex: its degree-zero kernel consists of the common value on every vertex and has dimension one, while all higher cohomology vanishes. If ∅=N(a)={0,…,r}, choose v∈/N(a). A cochain homotopy inserting v into the ordered index list, with the sign of that insertion, contracts this subcomplex. Insertion or deletion of v preserves the condition N(a)⊆J; in δh+hδ, terms inserting and deleting different vertices cancel in pairs, while the term inserting then deleting v is the identity. Thus this entire monomial subcomplex is acyclic. Finally, if all ai​<0, the monomial occurs only in the full intersection, in degree r, and contributes one copy of k there.
For r≥1, these three cases give the complete answer:
Hi(Pkr​,O(n))≅⎩⎨⎧​Sn​,a0​,…,ar​<0a0​+⋯+ar​=n​⨁​kX0a0​​⋯Xrar​​,0,​i=0, n≥0,i=r, n≤−r−1,otherwise.​​
(5)
In the first case counting nonnegative exponent vectors gives h0=(rn+r​). In the top case write ai​=−1−bi​ with bi​≥0; then ∑bi​=−n−r−1, giving
hr(Pkr​,O(n))=(r−n−1​)(n≤−r−1).​
(6)
This is the Laurent-monomial description of top cohomology on projective space. All intermediate degrees vanish for every twist, and the top degree vanishes when n≥−r. If r=0, then Pk0​=Speck and every twist is trivial, so H0(Pk0​,O(n))=k for every integer n, with all higher groups zero.

Use homogeneous coordinates X0​,…,Xn​. Multiplication by these degree-one sections defines the sheaf morphism
ϵ:O(−1)⊕(n+1)⟶O,(s0​,…,sn​)⟼∑j=0n​Xj​sj​.
(7)
On Ui​=D+​(Xi​), write tj(i)​=Xj​/Xi​, with ti(i)​=1, and let Ej(i)​ be the standard vector in component j using the local line bundle frame Xi−1​ of O(−1). Then ϵ(Ej(i)​)=tj(i)​, so ϵ is surjective since ϵ(Ei(i)​)=1. Its kernel is a locally free sheaf with basis
Fj(i)​=Ej(i)​−tj(i)​Ei(i)​(j=i).
(8)
Indeed any vector in the kernel is uniquely a sum of these vectors, by solving for its i-th component.
The Kähler differential sheaf ΩPn/k1​ has basis dtj(i)​ on Ui​, since this chart is a polynomial affine scheme. Define the local map dtj(i)​↦Fj(i)​. To check that these isomorphisms glue, on Ui​∩Uℓ​ put u=ti(ℓ)​ and v=tj(ℓ)​. Then
tj(i)​=uv​,dtj(i)​=u−1dtj(ℓ)​−vu−2dti(ℓ)​.
(9)
The twisting sheaf on projective space frame changes by Ej(i)​=u−1Ej(ℓ)​, hence
Fj(i)​=u−1Fj(ℓ)​−vu−2Fi(ℓ)​.
(10)
For j=ℓ, use tℓ(ℓ)​=1, dtℓ(ℓ)​=0 and Fℓ(ℓ)​=0; the same formula applies. Thus the differential and kernel frames have identical transition matrices. We obtain the cotangent form of the Euler sequence:
0⟶ΩPn/k1​⟶O(−1)⊕(n+1)ϵ​O⟶0.​
(11)
This construction works over any field, including positive characteristic.
The Kähler differential sheaf has rank n. Taking determinant line bundles in this locally split short exact sequence of sheaves gives
det(O(−1)⊕(n+1))≅det(ΩPn/k1​)⊗det(O).
(12)
The left side is the tensor product of n+1 copies of O(−1), and det(O)=O. Consequently
⋀n​ΩPn/k1​≅O(−n−1).​
(13)
It is the canonical bundle of projective space. The determinant identity can also be seen directly by taking the wedge of the kernel basis followed by a lift of the quotient basis; changing that lift by a kernel vector leaves the wedge unchanged.

The identification with P1 means that C is a smooth plane conic. An arbitrary singular or nonreduced conic over an algebraically closed field cannot be identified with the projective line, so this is a necessary qualification implicit in the requested description.
Choose an equation ℓ=0 for L⊂P3 and a degree-two equation q=0 whose restriction to L defines C. Its homogeneous ideal in P3 is (ℓ,q). These equations form a regular sequence: ℓ is a non-zero-divisor in the polynomial ring, and modulo ℓ the ring is a polynomial ring in three variables, in which the nonzero quadratic q is again a non-zero-divisor. The generator classes therefore form a basis of the conormal sheaf, with their grading shifts:
IC​/IC2​≅OC​(−1)⊕OC​(−2).
(14)
One can verify the absence of relations from the two-generator Koszul complex: a relation between ℓ,q is a multiple of (q,−ℓ), whose coefficients become zero modulo IC​. This is the normal sheaf of a projective complete intersection calculation. Dualizing the conormal sheaf gives
NC/P3​≅OC​(1)⊕OC​(2).
(15)
These twists are restrictions from P3, not intrinsic degree-one twists on P1. A line in the plane cuts the degree-two curve in two points counted with multiplicity, so the degree of a line bundle OC​(1) is two. The Picard group of the projective line then gives
OC​(1)≅OP1​(2),OC​(2)≅OP1​(4).
(16)
Equivalently, the degree-two Veronese embedding [s:t]↦[s2:st:t2] pulls each linear coordinate back to a quadratic. Thus the normal bundle of a smooth plane conic is
NC/P3​≅OP1​(4)⊕OP1​(2),{a,b}={4,2}.​
(17)
The same splitting follows from the normal sequence for the two embeddings:
0⟶NC/L​⟶NC/P3​⟶NL/P3​∣C​⟶0.
(18)
Its outer terms are OP1​(4) and OP1​(2). The extension group class lies in Ext1(O(2),O(4))=H1(P1,O(2))=0, by the preceding Čech cohomology calculation, so the sequence splits. Both methods distinguish the ambient twist from the intrinsic degree on the curve.

For a projective scheme X over k, define its Hilbert functor on k-schemes by assigning to T the set of closed subschemes W⊆X×k​T such that W→T is a flat morphism of finite presentation and is proper. For projective X, the properness is automatic for these closed families. A morphism T′→T sends a family to W×T​T′⊆X×k​T′; base change preserves the required properties, making this a contravariant functor. The embedding in X×T is part of the data, so these are embedded families rather than families modulo automorphisms of X.
Choose a very ample line bundle on X. In a flat projective family of finite presentation the fibre Hilbert polynomial is locally constant. Requiring polynomial P gives a subfunctor represented by HilbP(X); the unrestricted Hilbert functor is represented by the disjoint union over P. For the specified Z, use its polynomial PZ​ and let h=[Z] be the resulting k-point of the Hilbert scheme H.
A Zariski tangent space vector at h is a k-morphism
v:SpecD⟶H,D=k[ε]/(ε2),
(19)
whose restriction to Speck is h. Here D is the ring of dual numbers. Locally such a morphism sends a to a(h)+εd(a), where d(ab)=a(h)d(b)+b(h)d(a), so these morphisms form the vector space Homk​(mh​/mh2​,k). By the representing property of the Hilbert scheme, the same tangent vector is exactly a first-order embedded deformation
Zε​⊆X×k​SpecD,Zε​ flat over D,Zε​×D​k=Z
(20)
with the last equality an equality of embedded closed subschemes.
Let I be the ideal sheaf of i:Z↪X, and define its normal sheaf by
NZ/X​=HomOZ​​(I/I2,OZ​).
(21)
This definition is valid even when X or Z is singular; it need not give a locally free sheaf. We now derive the tangent-space identification by constructing inverse maps, rather than merely invoking the normal sheaf's name.
Work on an affine open subscheme SpecA⊆X, put B=A/I, and let J⊆A⊕εA define a flat deformation with special fibre B. The flatness criterion over dual numbers says that a D-module M is flat exactly when
ker(ε:M→M)=εM.
(22)
Flatness implies this by tensoring 0→(ε)→D→k→0. Conversely lift a k-basis of M/εM to M. It generates M over D: after removing the linear combination representing an element modulo ε, the remainder is ε times another element, whose residue is another finite basis combination. For independence, reduce a relation modulo ε to remove its constant coefficients. The remaining relation says that a linear combination of the lifts belongs to the kernel of ε, hence to εM, so its remaining coefficients vanish modulo ε too. The lifts are a free D-basis, establishing flatness.
Applying this criterion to M=(A⊕εA)/J gives
J∩εA=εI.
(23)
Indeed εI⊆J follows by lifting each f∈I to f+εg∈J and multiplying by ε. Conversely, if εa∈J, then the class of a is in the kernel of ε on M, hence in εM; reducing modulo ε gives a∈I.
For f∈I, choose a lift f+εg∈J and define
ϕ(f)=gmodI∈B.
(24)
Two lifts differ by an element of J∩εA=εI, so this is well defined. Addition and multiplication of lifts by elements of A prove that ϕ is A-linear. For f1​,f2​∈I, one has ϕ(f1​f2​)=f1​ϕ(f2​)=0 in B, so it factors through an element of HomB​(I/I2,B).
Conversely, for such a homomorphism define
Jϕ​={f+εg:f∈I,gmodI=ϕ(f)}⊆A⊕εA.
(25)
This is an ideal: multiplying by a+εb gives af+ε(ag+bf), and ag+bfmodI=aϕ(f)=ϕ(af). Its reduction is I. To prove that its quotient is flat, suppose ε[a+εb]=0. The condition εa∈Jϕ​ says a∈I. Choose g lifting ϕ(a); then a+εg∈Jϕ​, so
[a+εb]=ε[b−g].
(26)
Thus the kernel of ε is its image, and the criterion proves flatness. These constructions are inverse: the graph condition reconstructs every lift in J, and changes by εI account for all choices.
Localization respects both constructions. The local maps I/I2→B therefore glue exactly to global sections of the internal Hom sheaf NZ/X​; conversely such a section gives compatible local ideals that glue to Zε​. Hence
T[Z]​Hilb(X)≅HomOX​​(I,i∗​OZ​)≅H0(Z,NZ/X​).​
(27)
The middle identification uses that every map to OZ​ kills I2. The zero map gives the product deformation Z×SpecD; addition and scalar multiplication of the maps ϕ give the canonical vector-space operations on the tangent space. This proves the Zariski tangent space of a Hilbert scheme formula without a smoothness or regular-embedding hypothesis.
