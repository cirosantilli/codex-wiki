An isotropic source's bolometric luminosity is distributed over a wavefront of physical area 4πa02​r2. Each photon loses an energy factor (1+z)−1 through cosmological redshift, and successive photons arrive with a further rate factor (1+z)−1 through cosmological time dilation. Therefore the radiative flux and luminosity distance are
F=4πa02​r2(1+z)2L​,dL​=a0​r(1+z),1+z=a(t)a0​​.
(4)
These are bolometric formulas; a fixed observed frequency would require the shifted emission spectrum as well.
Normalize the scale factor by a0​=1. Summing the radiative flux over the source counts on an FLRW past light cone makes the transverse shell-area factors cancel:
dF0​=FdN=Ln(t)a(t)4dt,F0​=L∫0t0​​n(t)a(t)4dt.​
(5)
Thus F0​ is power per effective detector area, summed over the whole sky; an all-sky detector of effective area Adet​ receives Adet​F0​. The corresponding intensity per solid angle is F0​/(4π). Restoring the speed of light multiplies these flux integrals by c.
Spatially uniform n(t) by itself does not specify its time evolution. For a persistent population comoving with the expansion, with neither creation nor destruction, the cosmological continuity equation for source number is n˙+3Hn=0. Its conserved comoving number density is n0​=a3n, so the cosmological bolometric background from conserved sources becomes Ln0​∫adt. For the Einstein-de Sitter universe,
Fflat​=Ln0​∫0t0​​(t0​t​)2/3dt=53​Ln0​t0​=5H0​2Ln0​​.​
(6)
The last equality uses H0​=2/(3t0​). The early-time improper integral converges despite the diverging physical source density. For a completely unspecified source history the general integral above is the determined answer; constancy of individual luminosity alone does not imply number conservation.

At a fixed temperature, the deuterium equilibrium abundance contains an explicit factor nB​ through η. Two nucleons have to encounter each other to make one bound nucleus. Their capture rate per volume is proportional to nn​np​, whereas nuclear photodisintegration competes through the very numerous thermal photons. The small baryon-to-photon ratio therefore delays stable deuterium formation until T≪εD​, producing the deuterium bottleneck. Changing the cosmological baryon density changes both its release time and the subsequent efficiency of nuclear burning. The residual primordial deuterium abundance is particularly sensitive to these rates: on the usual nucleosynthesis branch, increasing nB​ burns more deuterium into heavier nuclei and lowers the remaining D/H. The positive dependence of the instantaneous equilibrium expression and the negative dependence of the final relic D/H are different statements.
By contrast, once nuclear reactions are efficient, nearly every surviving neutron ends in helium-4. Two neutrons make each helium-4 nucleus, so nHe​≃nn​/2 and
YP​≃nB​4nHe​​=nn​+np​2nn​​=1+r2r​,r=np​nn​​.​
(20)
This primordial helium mass fraction is mainly neutron limited: r is set by weak conversion, cosmological weak freeze-out and free-neutron decay after freeze-out, rather than by the absolute baryon density. There is a weaker dependence because a higher η releases the deuterium bottleneck earlier and allows fewer neutrons to decay. Thus YP​ is comparatively insensitive, not exactly independent, of nB​. At such exceptionally small baryon densities that nuclei never form efficiently, the neutron-limited approximation would fail.

The stochastic scale dependence of A(k) is not specified by the deterministic metric solution. If one additionally assumes a scale-invariant primordial spectrum, k3PA​(k)=constant, then
Cℓ​ ∝ ∫0∞​kdk​jℓ​(kD)2=2ℓ(ℓ+1)1​
(59)
for ℓ≥1, giving the familiar large-angle Sachs-Wolfe plateau, ℓ(ℓ+1)Cℓ​≃constant, for the measured ℓ≥2 multipoles. A tilted primordial spectrum changes that trend. The full numerical temperature transfer also includes intrinsic last-scattering and Doppler terms, omitted from the stipulated metric-only integral.
