In deep radiation domination, a∝τ and a′/a=1/τ. Neglect Ωc​δc​ against the radiation gravity source, so the supplied CDM density equation in a matter-radiation universe becomes
δc′′​+τ1​δc′​=τ23​δr​.
(40)
On superhorizon scales omit the k2δr​/3 pressure-gradient term. Under adiabatic initial conditions, δr​=(4/3)δc​ then gives
δc′′​+τ1​δc′​−τ24​δc​=0.
(41)
The Euler-Cauchy equation characteristic polynomial is p2−4, so
δc​=Ar​(k)(τ/τi​)2+Br​(k)(τ/τi​)−2,δr​=34​δc​.​
(42)
These are the general growing and decaying adiabatic solutions in this approximation. A regular primordial growing mode discards the divergent τ−2 solution. Although its conformal-time growth is again quadratic, now δc​∝a2, rather than δc​∝a in matter domination.
If “general” is taken without imposing adiabaticity, both independent relative-entropy integration constants must also be retained. The gradient-free radiation equation gives
S=δr​−34​δc​=S0​+S1​τ.
(43)
Substitution into the matter equation produces
δc​=Cg​τ2+Cd​τ−2−43​S0​−S1​τ,δr​=34​Cg​τ2+34​Cd​τ−2−31​S1​τ.
(44)
This is the general solution of the leading supplied coupled system, described by the superhorizon radiation-era entropy integration constants. Setting both S0​ and S1​ to zero recovers the adiabatic result. Regularity and the full initial Einstein field equations constraints can further restrict which of these leading solutions represents a prescribed physical initial mode.
On scales well inside the horizon, radiation pressure drives oscillations at frequency approximately k/3​. Their amplitude is bounded at leading order, and their gravitational forcing of a slowly varying matter contrast can be averaged away. With this approximation, as well as negligible matter self-gravity,
δc′′​+τ1​δc′​≃0,(τδc′​)′≃0,
(45)
hence the logarithmic CDM growth after radiation-era horizon entry is
δc​=C0​(k)+Clog​(k)log(τ/τh​),kτ≫1,τ≪τeq​.​
(46)
This is the Mészáros effect. It does not mean that δr​ itself vanishes. For a rapidly oscillating source Rγ​cos(ωτ+φ), the matter equation has a particular response
δc,osc​≃−ω2τ23Rγ​​cos(ωτ+φ).
(47)
Its leading second derivative reproduces 3δr​/τ2; the remaining terms are smaller by powers of (kτ)−1. Thus the suppressed cold-matter forcing by rapid radiation oscillations is only order δr​/(kτ)2 and does not change the secular logarithm. Radiation forcing near horizon entry determines C0​,Clog​ and cannot be averaged away there. Neither this smoothing approximation nor the neglect of matter gravity remains uniformly valid near equality.
