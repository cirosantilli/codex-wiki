Past exam of the mathematics course of the University of Cambridge / 2001 / iii / Paper 6 / 5 / Solution

For a bounded linear operator T:E→F, write the weak square norm of a finite family as
w2​(x1​,…,xm​)=supϕ∈E∗, ∥ϕ∥≤1​(∑j=1m​∣ϕ(xj​)∣2)1/2.
(23)
It is a 2-summing operator if (∑j​∥Txj​∥2)1/2≤Cw2​(x1​,…,xm​) for all finite families, and π2​(T) is the least such C. This specializes the definition of an absolutely p-summing operator.
First let E=H1​, F=H2​ be Hilbert spaces. For the column operator A:ℓ2m​→H1​, Aej​=xj​, the Riesz representation theorem gives w2​(x1​,…,xm​)=∥A∗∥=∥A∥. If T is a Hilbert-Schmidt operator, the Parseval identity applied to orthonormal bases gives
∑j=1m​∥Txj​∥2=∥TA∥HS2​=∑α​∥A∗T∗fα​∥2≤∥A∥2∑α​∥T∗fα​∥2=∥A∥2∥T∥HS2​.
(24)
Thus π2​(T)≤∥T∥HS​. Conversely test the summing inequality on any finite orthonormal family in H1​. Its weak square norm is 1, by the Bessel inequality, with equality by choosing one of its vectors. Hence ∑j​∥Tej​∥2≤π2​(T)2. Taking the supremum over finite subsets of an orthonormal basis proves the Hilbert-Schmidt operator condition and the reverse norm inequality. These basis sums work even for nonseparable Hilbert spaces, being understood as suprema of finite subsums. Therefore
T is Hilbert-Schmidt⟺T is 2-summing,π2​(T)=∥T∥HS​.​
(25)
For an n-dimensional normed vector space E, again associate a family (xj​) with A:ℓ2m​→E. By the Hahn-Banach theorem and scalar Euclidean duality,
∥A∥=sup∥ϕ∥≤1​(∑j​∣ϕ(xj​)∣2)1/2=w2​(x1​,…,xm​).
(26)
Let Q be the orthogonal projection onto (kerA)⊥, whose dimension is at most n. Since A=AQ, the trace of Q gives
∑j​∥xj​∥E2​≤∥A∥2∑j​∥Qej​∥22​=∥A∥2rankA≤nw2​(x1​,…,xm​)2.
(27)
This proves π2​(IE​)≤n​ for every norm, without comparing that norm to an arbitrary Euclidean norm.
To obtain equality and the projection bound, choose a maximum-volume centered ellipsoid inside the unit ball BE​ and use coordinates in which it is the Euclidean ball B2​. In the real case this is the John ellipsoid; in the complex case maximize among Hermitian ellipsoids. There are unit contact vectors ur​∈∂BE​∩∂B2​ and positive weights cr​ with the John contact decomposition
∑r​cr​ur​ur∗​=IE​,∑r​cr​=n.
(28)
For completeness, the maximum-volume argument gives this identity. If IE​ were outside the closed convex cone of the contact matrices, finite-dimensional separation would supply a symmetric, or Hermitian, matrix D with trD>0 and u∗Du≤0 at every contact. The cone is closed because its compact generators have trace one. Subtract a sufficiently small multiple of the identity to make all contact inequalities strict while retaining positive trace. The support function of (I+tD)B2​ decreases near the contact directions for small t>0; away from them the strict gap between the support functions of BE​ and B2​ permits the same small deformation. Thus this deformed ball still lies in BE​. Its volume increases, since the real determinant is 1+ttrD+O(t2) in the real case and ∣detC​(I+tD)∣2=1+2ttrD+O(t2) in the complex case. This contradicts maximality. The finite-dimensional convex-cone representation gives finitely many contacts, and taking traces gives the sum of the weights.
At a contact ur​, a supporting hyperplane to BE​ also supports B2​. The Euclidean ball has a unique supporting direction there, so the corresponding functional is ϕr​(x)=ur∗​x and has dual norm one on E. In the complex case balancedness of BE​ turns the real support bound into ∣ϕr​(x)∣≤∥x∥E​. Also B2​⊆BE​ gives ∥x∥E​≤∣x∣2​. For any dual-unit functional ϕ, this containment implies that its Euclidean norm is at most one. Apply the summing definition to xr​=cr​​ur​:
∑r​∥xr​∥E2​=∑r​cr​=n,∑r​∣ϕ(xr​)∣2=∣ϕ∣22​≤1.
(29)
The weak square norm is exactly one, since any contact functional attains that Euclidean norm. Hence π2​(IE​)≥n​. Together with the upper estimate, the norm is
π2​(IE​)=n​.​
(30)
This proves the 2-summing norm of a finite-dimensional identity formula.
Finally retain the preceding finite-dimensional hypothesis when E⊆X. Extend each ϕr​ to a dual-unit functional ϕ​r​ on X using the real or complex Hahn-Banach theorem. Define
P:X→E,Px=∑r​cr​ϕ​r​(x)ur​.
(31)
The contact identity gives P∣E​=IE​, hence P2=P and its range is E. The synthesis operator (ar​)↦∑r​cr​​ar​ur​ has Euclidean operator norm one, because its product with its adjoint is the identity. Therefore
∥Px∥E​≤∣Px∣2​≤(∑r​cr​∣ϕ​r​(x)∣2)1/2≤∑r​cr​​∥x∥X​=n​∥x∥X​.
(32)
Thus the linear projection satisfies ∥P∥≤n​​, the Kadec-Snobar projection bound. If n=0, the zero projection gives the same conclusion. No assertion about arbitrary infinite-dimensional subspaces is required.
