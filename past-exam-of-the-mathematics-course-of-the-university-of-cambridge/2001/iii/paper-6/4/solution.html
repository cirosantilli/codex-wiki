The weak topology σ(E,E∗) is the coarsest topology making every continuous linear functional in E∗ continuous. A basic neighborhood of x imposes finitely many inequalities ∣fj​(z−x)∣<ε, and a sequence converges weakly precisely when each f(xn​) converges to f(x). The Hahn-Banach theorem makes the dual separate points, so the weak topology is Hausdorff.
Here is a proof of the requested weak subsequence property, the compact-to-sequential direction of the Eberlein-Šmulian theorem. Let Y be the closed linear span of the countable set {xn​:n≥1}. It is a separable Banach space. The Hahn-Banach theorem separates points outside a closed vector subspace from that subspace, so Y is weakly closed in E. Therefore C=K∩Y is weakly compact.
Choose a countable dense set (uj​) in the unit sphere of Y, and choose norming functionals fj​∈Y∗ with ∥fj​∥=1 and fj​(uj​)=1. They separate points: for y=0, a sufficiently close uj​ to y/∥y∥ gives ∣fj​(y)∣>0. This is a countable norming family. Extend the functionals to E by the Hahn-Banach theorem. The evaluation map

is weakly continuous and injective. Its domain is compact and its target Hausdorff, so it is a homeomorphism onto its image: images of closed subsets are compact, hence closed. A countable product of real or complex lines is metrizable, for example by ∑j​2−jmin(1,∣aj​−bj​∣). Thus C has a compact metrizable weak topology. Compact metric spaces are sequentially compact, giving a subsequence with xnj​​⇀x∈C⊆K​. If Y={0} the result is immediate. We did not assert that a separable Banach space has a separable dual or that its weak topology is generally metrizable.
