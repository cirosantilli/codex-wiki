We prove the convex domination form of the Hahn-Banach theorem by extending across one vector. Suppose a linear functional f is already dominated on a vector subspace W and choose v∈/W. For w∈W and positive t,s, the proposed extension g(w+τv)=f(w)+τc must satisfy the bounds
L=supw∈W, t>0​tf(w)−ϕ(w−tv)​≤c≤U=infw∈W, s>0​sϕ(w+sv)−f(w)​.
(9)
To prove compatibility, take w1​,w2​∈W and t,s>0. The convex combination of w1​−tv and w2​+sv with weights s/(s+t) and t/(s+t) lies in W. Domination there and convexity give
sf(w1​)+tf(w2​)≤sϕ(w1​−tv)+tϕ(w2​+sv).
(10)
Rearranging proves every lower candidate is at most every upper candidate. Also the candidates w=0,t=s=1 show
−ϕ(−v)≤L≤U≤ϕ(v).
(11)
Thus both endpoints are finite, and some c∈[L,U] exists. For τ>0 the upper bound gives domination of f(w)+τc; for τ<0 the lower bound gives it; and for τ=0 domination was assumed. This defines a dominated linear functional on W+Rv extending f.
Order all dominated extensions by inclusion of their domains and agreement of values. A chain has the union extension, still linear and dominated. By Zorn's lemma there is a maximal extension. The one-vector construction would enlarge any proper domain, so its domain is V. Hence there is a full linear extension with g∣W​=f,g≤ϕ​. A convex function here need not be positively homogeneous; this is why the bounds included all positive s,t.
Now fix y∈V. The translated convex function φy​(z)=ϕ(y+z)−ϕ(y) vanishes at zero. Apply the just-proved extension theorem to the zero linear functional on {0} to obtain a linear functional ℓy​ with ℓy​(z)≤φy​(z) for every z. Set
ay​(x)=ϕ(y)+ℓy​(x−y).
(12)
This is an affine function, satisfies ay​(x)≤ϕ(x) for every x, and has ay​(y)=ϕ(y). Let A={ay​:y∈V}. Every member is an affine minorant, while the member indexed by x attains ϕ(x) at x. Thus the exact representation is
ϕ(x)=a∈Asup​a(x)(x∈V).​
(13)
This is the finite convex function as supremum of affine minorants property. The linear functionals are algebraic; without a topology no assertion of continuous affine minorants is intended.
