For the upward incident internal gravity wave, P=ρ0​ωm0​W/k2. Using real physical fields, its time-averaged vertical energy flux per unit area is
I=21​Re(PW∗)=2k2ρ0​ωm0​∣w0​∣2​.
(18)
The assumed mixing power is I/4. A deepening law also requires the gravitational potential energy of the mixed layer. The wave problem specifies a well-mixed half-space above the interface, but supplies neither a finite upper mixed-layer depth nor a replacement buoyancy/energy boundary condition. There is therefore no unique finite-layer d(t) from the printed information alone.
The missing parameter can be displayed explicitly. Let the initially mixed upper region have finite depth a, with a fixed upper boundary at z=a, and conserve its integrated buoyancy while homogenizing down to z=−d. Its new buoyancy is
bm​=b1​−a+dΔbd+N02​d2/2​.
(19)
Integrate −ρ0​zb over the initial and final profiles in −d<z<a. The potential energy of mixed-layer deepening with an initial buoyancy jump is
ρ0​ΔP​=2Δbad​+4N02​ad2​+12N02​d3​.
(20)
With d(0)=0 and a maintained constant incident flux, the resulting law is
2Δbad​+4N02​ad2​+12N02​d3​=8k2ωm0​∣w0​∣2​t,d˙=Δba/2+N02​ad/2+N02​d2/4ωm0​∣w0​∣2/(8k2)​.​
(21)
The right-hand side is positive and determines a unique depth once a is supplied. Initially d˙=ωm0​∣w0​∣2/(4k2Δba) for a>0, already showing why the upper depth matters. If the added assumptions instead specify a zero initial mixed depth, the cubic term gives d=[3ωm0​∣w0​∣2t/(2k2N02​)]1/3; the initial jump then has no finite volume above it to mix with. Neither assumption is contained in the original half-space wave statement. Literal homogenization of the entire upper half-space is the a→∞ limit and costs infinite energy for any fixed positive d. This limitation should not be concealed by a guessed entrainment depth or by keeping the upper buoyancy fixed while claiming conservation.
