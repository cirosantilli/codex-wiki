Let T0​=Ti​−T∞​>0, S0​=Si​−S∞​>0 and R0​=βS0​/(αT0​). At steady volume, the weir outflow equals the inflow Q. The well-mixed heat and salt balances give
Q(Ti​−T)=AFT​,Q(Si​−S)=AFS​.
(41)
With θ=(Ti​−T)/T0​ and η=(Si​−S)/S0​, the flux ratio implies ηs​=qθs​, where q=RF​/R0​. The actual interface ratios are ΔT=T0​(1−θ), ΔS=S0​(1−η) and Rρ​=R0​(1−η)/(1−θ).
The original PDF uses Rρ−2​ in the heat-flux law; the converted TeX's exponent −3 is a transcription error. Using the PDF yields
FT​=R02​bα1/3T04/3​​(1−η)2(1−θ)10/3​.
(42)
Substitute this into the heat balance to obtain the double-diffusive overflow reservoir relations
ηs​=R0​RF​​θs​,θs​=C(1−ηs​)2(1−θs​)10/3​,C=QR02​Ab(αT0​)1/3​.​
(43)
For the usual heat-dominated positive upward buoyancy flux, 0≤RF​<1 and R0​>1 give 0≤q<1. Then the right-hand side after substituting ηs​=qθs​ decreases from C to zero as θs​ runs from zero to one; there is a unique physical steady root. The net upward buoyancy flux is B0​=gαFT​(1−RF​).
