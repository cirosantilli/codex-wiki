The Lighthill elongated-body theory is a reactive, nearly inviscid flow description of a long slender swimmer, appropriate when the longitudinal Reynolds number is large but its lateral displacement and slope are small. To leading order, crossflow past each section is the two-dimensional potential flow generated by lateral motion of that section. Longitudinal variation is slow on the cross-sectional scale, and axial speed U is prescribed at this order. With x increasing from nose to tail, fluid passes sections in increasing x, and the linearized relative lateral velocity is
w=ht​+Uhx​,D=∂t​+U∂x​.
(1)
The section's added mass per unit length m(x) is defined by its crossflow kinetic energy 21​m(x)w2. Equivalently, if Φ is the velocity potential for unit lateral section motion, then m=ρ∫∣∇⊥​Φ∣2dA over the exterior crossflow plane. It has units of mass per length and depends on cross-sectional shape and density. For a circular section of radius a, Φ=−a2cosφ/r gives m=ρπa2. It is not the body's mass per unit length.
The transverse fluid impulse attached to a section is m(x)w. A control segment has both changing impulse and an impulse flux Umw through its ends. Taking their sum per unit length gives the reaction on the fluid:
Fz​=∂t​(mw)+U∂x​(mw)=(∂t​+U∂x​)[m(x)(ht​+Uhx​)].​
(2)
The force on the fish is −Fz​. In particular, m is inside the convective derivative; dropping its spatial derivative or the endpoint fluxes is not justified merely because the fish is slender.
The recoil correction in elongated-body theory adds unknown whole-body translation and yaw to a prescribed active bending shape:
h=h0​+Zr​(t)+(x−xˉ)θ(t).
(3)
They are determined by total lateral linear momentum and yaw angular momentum balance, rather than by imposing that the deforming swimmer remains on its original axis. Body inertia and hydrodynamic added inertia have the same sign. If the translation variable is required to be the literal centre of mass, the active deformation must first have its mass-weighted mean removed: the reference displacement Zr​ is otherwise not the centre of mass displacement during the stroke. The corresponding deformation contribution to angular momentum must also be retained when it is significant.

Write r=x−xˉ, A(t)=∫0t​α(s)ds, and use the usual reduced rigid-recoil representation h=Z+rθ−αr2, with zero initial recoil and a smooth stroke returning α and α˙ to zero. For this representation,
γ=Z+U∫0t​θ(s)ds,w=γ˙​+rθ˙−α˙r2−2Uαr.
(4)
Define the fluid impulse and its first moment by
Q=M0​γ˙​+M1​θ˙−M2​α˙−2UM1​α,P=M1​γ˙​+M2​θ˙−M3​α˙−2UM2​α.
(5)
The force law from part (i) gives, by integration by parts,
∫0L​Fz​dx=Q˙​+U[mw]0L​,∫0L​rFz​dx=P˙+U[rmw]0L​−UQ.
(6)
These are the endpoint momentum flux in elongated-body recoil. Put
Bj​=[m(x)rj]0L​,j=0,1,2,3.
(7)
The endpoint quantities are needed in addition to the integrals Mj​ unless further end-shape assumptions are made. Lateral force and yaw torque balance are
Mb​Z¨=−Q˙​−U[mw]0L​,Ib​θ¨=−P˙−U[rmw]0L​+UQ.
(8)
Expanding gives the requested simultaneous second-order equations, with the retained endpoint fluxes:
(Mb​+M0​)Z¨+M1​θ¨+UB0​Z˙+U(M0​+B1​)θ˙+U2B0​θM1​Z¨+(Ib​+M2​)θ¨+U(B1​−M0​)Z˙+UB2​θ˙+U2(B1​−M0​)θ​=M2​α¨+U(2M1​+B2​)α˙+2U2B1​α,=M3​α¨+U(M2​+B3​)α˙+2U2(B2​−M1​)α.​​
(9)
Substituting Z¨=γ¨​−Uθ˙ and integrating once from the initially straight, unrecoiling state yields
(Mb​+M0​)γ˙​+M1​θ˙+UB0​γ+U(B1​−Mb​)θM1​γ˙​+(Ib​+M2​)θ˙+U(B1​−M0​)γ+U(B2​−M1​)θ​=M2​α˙+U(2M1​+B2​)α+2U2B1​A,=M3​α˙+U(M2​+B3​)α+2U2(B2​−M1​)A.​​
(10)
These are first-order coupled equations for γ,θ with known forcing.
The claimed mass-independent final angle requires a qualification. Let
M=(Mb​+M0​M1​​M1​Ib​+M2​​),K=(B0​B1​−M0​​B1​−Mb​B2​−M1​​).
(11)
After the stroke, α=α˙=0 and A=AT​=∫0T​αdt. If the post-stroke dynamics are stable, the first-order system relaxes to
K(γ∞​θ∞​​)=2UAT​(B1​B2​−M1​​).
(12)
Let C=B0​(B2​−M1​)−B1​(B1​−M0​). Solving this two-by-two system gives
θ∞​=2UAT​C+Mb​(B1​−M0​)C​,γ∞​=2UAT​C+Mb​(B1​−M0​)Mb​(B2​−M1​)​.​
(13)
This is quadratic-bend turning with finite body mass. At the limiting state θ˙=γ˙​=0, so Z˙=−Uθ∞​. The forward velocity is −U in the chosen x direction, and its lateral component has precisely the body's slope: the fish glides parallel to itself. The angle is generally reached after a relaxation transient, not necessarily immediately at t=T.
The advertised result is obtained in the thin-body approximation that neglects Mb​ relative to fluid added mass, or in the special geometry B1​=M0​:
θ∞​=2U∫0T​α(t)dt.​
(14)
For example, take negligible nose added mass and nonzero tail added mass mT​, so Bj​=mT​(L−xˉ)j. In the zero-body-mass approximation, the post-stroke equations for (γ,θ−2UAT​) are homogeneous. The inertia matrix is positive definite by Cauchy-Schwarz inequality, and
detK=mT​∫0L​m(x)(L−x)dx>0,tr(M−1K)=detMmT​[Ib​+∫0L​m(x)(L−x)2dx]​>0.
(15)
The two eigenvalues of M−1K therefore have positive real parts: their sum and product are positive, whether they are real or conjugate. For U>0 the transient decays, proving parallel gliding and the displayed turn in this approximation.
With finite Mb​, the angle is not a consequence of the printed force law for arbitrary m(x). A concrete stable counterexample uses L=1, xˉ=1/2, and m(x)=m∗​x2. Then M0​=m∗​/3, M1​=m∗​/12, B0​=m∗​, B1​=m∗​/2, B2​=m∗​/4, and C=m∗2​/12. Hence
θ∞​=1+2Mb​/m∗​2UAT​​,
(16)
which differs from 2UAT​ for positive body mass. This example has positive determinant and positive matrix trace of the relaxation matrix. Another immediate check is that if both endpoint added masses vanish, integrating the force law conserves total lateral momentum. A finite-mass fish cannot then end with w=0 and a nonzero new transverse velocity. Thus endpoint and inertia assumptions cannot silently be omitted.
If Z is literally the centre of mass position of a deforming body, use a mass-centered deformation rather than the raw quadratic. For body mass density μb​(x), its mean is −αJ2​/Mb​, with J2​=∫μb​r2dx; in the slender approximation J2​=Ib​. The reference translation above is then Zr​=Z+αJ2​/Mb​. Likewise the active angular momentum contains −α˙J3​, where J3​=∫μb​r3dx, which vanishes for symmetric mass distribution. These corrections modify derivative forcing during the stroke. Since the shape and its time derivative return to zero, they do not remove the finite-body-mass qualification of the final impulse relation.
The principal physical limitations are small slopes and small total turning angle, prescribed approximately constant forward speed, slowly varying slender geometry, and neglected viscosity, separation and detailed three-dimensional wake interactions. Fast large-amplitude C-starts need more than this linear theory. Neglecting body mass is most reasonable for a laterally thin body whose crossflow added mass greatly exceeds its displaced mass. Stable relaxation also requires an appropriate tail/end geometry; it is not guaranteed for every added-mass profile.

Choose an explicit orientation convention, since the supplied PDF does not display the referenced diagram. Let ez​ point upward and e2​ complete a right-handed frame with the horizontal flow direction e1​. Use
i=sinθe1​+cosθez​,j=−cosθe1​+sinθez​,
(17)
where i points from the head towards the tail. A tail point has position r=(a+s)i. Its rotational velocity is Ω(a+s)j, where the scalar Ω is about −e2​ and therefore θ˙=−Ω. Keeping that sign is essential for stability.
The point lies at height z=(a+s)cosθ, so the local imposed fluid velocity is α(a+s)cosθe1​. Its velocity relative to that fluid has components
vj​=Uj​+(Ω+αcos2θ)(a+s),vi​=Ui​−αsinθcosθ(a+s),​
(18)
where Ui​=U⋅i and Uj​=U⋅j. These follow by projection; rigid rotation has no tangential component.
Write P=m′g, H=6πμa, T=8πμa3/3, K=KN​L, and
I1​=∫0L​(a+s)ds=aL+2L2​,I2​=∫0L​(a+s)2ds=a2L+aL2+3L3​.
(19)
Let q=Ω+αcos2θ. The resistive-force theory force per length on the tail is −KN​vj​j−γKN​vi​i. Head drag and excess weight give
(H+K)Uj​=−Psinθ−KN​I1​q,(H+γK)Ui​=−Pcosθ+γKN​I1​αsinθcosθ.
(20)
The fluid vorticity is αe2​, so its local angular velocity has scalar component −α/2 about our axis. Sphere rotation resistance therefore contributes −T(Ω+α/2). Torque balance about C, including gravity acting at hi, is
0=−Phsinθ−KN​(I1​Uj​+I2​q)−T(Ω+α/2).
(21)
Eliminating Uj​ gives the unapproximated local-resistance relation
[KN​I2​−H+KKN2​I12​​+T]q=P[H+KKN​I1​​−h]sinθ−Tα(21​−cos2θ).
(22)
Now retain head and tail translation resistance at the same order, as intended by σ=O(L/a), but use a/L≪1 in the lever-arm integrals. Thus I1​≃L2/2, I2​≃L3/3. Sphere rotation resistance is smaller than tail rotation resistance by order (a/L)2, so omit T at fixed scaled shear. Also neglect h relative to the tail's hydrodynamic force-centre distance KN​I1​/(H+K)=O(L). Uniform density gives
Lh​=L(4πa3/3+πb2L)πb2L(a+L/2)​;
(23)
hence this step requires b2L/a3≪1, or head-dominated volume. It is consistent with the distinguished logarithmic ordering, but b≪a≪L by itself would not ensure it. The model further neglects hydrodynamic interaction between head and tail, and uses low-Reynolds-number local drag.
Under these approximations,
q=(H+K)L3/3−KN​L4/4(L2/2)Psinθ​=L(K+4H)6P​sinθ=2βsinθ,
(24)
so
Ω≃2βsinθ−αcos2θ,β=KN​L2/3+8πμaLP​=L(K+4H)3P​.​
(25)
Because θ˙=−Ω, its equilibria satisfy α(1−sin2θ)=2βsinθ. Define
s∗​=β+β2+α2​α​,
(26)
with s∗​=0 at zero shear. The two orientations modulo 2π are θs​=arcsins∗​ and θu​=π−θs​. The derivative of θ˙=αcos2θ−2βsinθ at an equilibrium is
−2cosθ(β+αsinθ).
(27)
The second factor is positive, so the branch cosθs​>0 is stable and the other is unstable. On the stable branch the tail tip is above C: the head is lower than the far tail end.
The corresponding leading translation velocities are
Ui​≃H+γK−Pcosθ+21​γKN​L2αsinθcosθ​,Uj​≃−K+4H4P​sinθ.​
(28)
The second formula uses q=2βsinθ in the normal force balance. In the equilibrium orientation αcos2θ=2βsinθ, and Uz​=Ui​cosθ+Uj​sinθ simplifies to
Uz​=−H+γKP​+(H+γK)(K+4H)PK(1−γ)​s∗2​.​
(29)
This is shear-dependent settling of a sphere-and-tail body. Since 0<γ<1 for a slender filament, s∗2​ increases with the magnitude of shear and the signed upward-coordinate velocity Uz​ increases towards zero. Equivalently, increasing shear decreases the downward sedimentation speed. The formula remains negative throughout this leading model. If γ=1 there is no shear dependence; reversing the drag anisotropy would reverse the trend. At very large shear, the omitted sphere rotation term proportional to Tα can become important, so the approximation is not uniform in arbitrarily large α.

The cell conservation equation has the form nˉtˉ​=−∇ˉ⋅Jˉ, with
Jˉ=nˉuˉ+Vc​nˉp−D∇ˉnˉ.
(30)
The first term is advection by the suspension's fluid velocity, the second is active swimming at fixed speed Vc​ in direction p, and the last is an isotropic diffusive flux due to random swimming. Here D has units length squared per time, nˉ is cell number per volume, and the divergence measures local loss by transport. No birth or death term is included.
For a spherical cell of radius ac​, its excess buoyancy force is (4πac3​/3)Δρg. The Stokes drag law gives passive settling speed Vset​=2ac2​Δρg/(9μ). Neglecting individual settling requires Vset​≪Vc​, not exact neutral buoyancy. A dilute suspension can still generate appreciable collective buoyancy because many small excess masses contribute to the bulk density.
Use the half-rate convention for the gyrotactic reorientation time B, required by the coefficients printed in the question:
0=2Bez​−pz​p​+21​ωˉ×p,∣p∣=1.​
(31)
The first torque tends to point the bottom-heavy cell upward; the second rotates a sphere with half the fluid vorticity. This is a quasistatic torque balance, assuming orientation relaxation is fast enough to follow the local flow. Keeping orientation dynamics instead would put dp/dtˉ on the left. The convention with aligning rate 1/B uses a reorientation parameter twice this B. Thus the factor of two is a definition, not a physical discrepancy. Close to the upward state the quasistatic gyrotactic balance with half-rate reorientation gives p⊥​=Bωˉ×ez​.
For a dilute Newtonian suspension with the Boussinesq approximation, the other equations are
∇ˉ⋅uˉ=0,∂tˉ∂uˉ​+uˉ⋅∇ˉuˉ=−ρ1​∇ˉpˉ​+ν∇ˉ2uˉ−ρυΔρg​nˉez​.
(32)
The background-water hydrostatic pressure is absorbed in pˉ​. The cell volume is υ, density is ρ, cell density is ρ+Δρ, dynamic viscosity is μ=ρν, and υnˉ≪1 is the volume fraction. Extra cell mass produces the downward buoyancy term. Constant viscosity and no explicit swimming stress are part of this closure. Rigid impermeable plates impose no-slip boundary condition and zero normal total cell flux.
At rest, the stable cell direction is upward and the steady vertical flux is Vc​nˉ−Dnˉzˉ​. It is constant in depth by conservation and zero at an impermeable plate, so
nˉ0​(zˉ)=Nˉ0​eVc​zˉ/D,Nˉ00​=Nˉ0​βeβ−1​,Nˉ0​=Nˉ00​eβ−1β​,β=DhVc​​.​
(33)
The pressure gradient balances its excess weight. The mean-concentration formula follows by averaging over 0≤zˉ≤h and is continuous at β=0.
Use x=xˉ/h, t=Dtˉ/h2, u=huˉ/D, n=nˉ/Nˉ0​, and p=h2pˉ​/(ρνD). Then
nt​+∇⋅[n(u+βp)−∇n]=0,∇⋅u=0,
(34)
Sc−1(ut​+u⋅∇u)=−∇p+∇2u−βR​nez​,
(35)
where
G=h2BD​,Sc=Dν​,R=ρνDβh3υΔρgNˉ0​​.
(36)
The dimensionless base concentration is n0​=eβz.
Linearize with the horizontal Fourier mode eσt+ilx+imy and k2=l2+m2. Write the first-order orientation perturbation as p=ez​+ϵp1​+⋯. Unit length gives p1z​=0, while torque balance gives p1⊥​=Gω1​×ez​. Incompressibility implies
∇⋅p1​=G(∂x​ω1y​−∂y​ω1x​)=−G∇2w1​.
(37)
Let L=d2/dz2−k2. The perturbation of fluid advection is n0′​W=βn0​W, and swimming contributes βN′+βn0​∇⋅p1​. Cell conservation therefore gives
σN=LN−βN′−βn0​W+βGn0​LW,
(38)
or
(d2/dz2−βd/dz−k2−σ)N=−βeβz[G(W′′−k2W)−W].​
(39)
This explains the destabilizing concentration-advection term and the gyrotactic redistribution term with their signs.
To eliminate pressure from the fluid equations, take their divergence, giving Lp1​=−(R/β)N′. Apply L to the vertical equation Sc−1σW=−p1′​+LW−(R/β)N. The concentration second derivatives cancel, leaving
(L−σ/Sc)LW=−βR​k2N.​
(40)
No-slip conditions give W=0 and W′=0 at each plate; for nonzero k, the latter follows from the vanishing horizontal velocity and incompressibility. Zero perturbed normal cell flux gives
W=W′=0,N′−βN=0at z=0,1.​
(41)
The normal orientation correction is zero at this order, and the fluid velocity is zero at the plate, so neither adds another term to this concentration condition.
Now use the stated distinguished scalings k=βα, σ=β2σ2​, G=β−1G−1​, N=N0​+βN1​+β2N2​+⋯, W=βW1​+β2W2​+⋯, and R=R0​+βR1​+⋯. Here α is a scaled wavenumber, unrelated to Question 2's shear rate. At order one, the concentration equation and boundary conditions give N0′′​=0, N0′​=0 at both endpoints, so N0​ is constant. Absorb its nonzero value into the perturbation amplitude and choose
N0​=1.​
(42)
At order β, the fluid equation is W1(4)​=−R0​α2 with W1​=W1′​=0 at both ends. Its unique solution is
W1​=−24R0​α2​z2(1−z)2.​
(43)
A homogeneous difference is cubic and the four clamped conditions make it zero.
The concentration equation at order β gives
N1′′​=−G−1​W1′′​,N1′​(0)=N1′​(1)=1,
(44)
so N1​=z−G−1​W1​+C1​, where C1​ fixes the amplitude normalization. At order β2 it gives
N2′′​−N1′​−(α2+σ2​)=−G−1​W2′′​+W1​−zG−1​W1′′​.
(45)
The boundary conditions expand to N2′​=N1​ at each endpoint. Integrating this equation from zero to one cancels [N2′​]01​ against [N1​]01​. Also [W2′​]01​=0 and
∫01​zW1′′​dz=[zW1′​]01​−[W1​]01​=0.
(46)
Thus the required solvability condition is
α2+σ2​=−∫01​W1​dz.​
(47)
Since ∫01​z2(1−z)2dz=1/30, the clamped-quartic solvability in shallow bioconvection gives
σ2​=α2(720R0​​−1).​
(48)
For nonzero long horizontal waves, R0​<720 gives decay and R0​>720 gives growth; the leading neutral threshold is R0​=720. The leading instability is stationary and independent of G−1​ and Sc. The exactly uniform horizontal mode α=0 has zero growth at this order because total cell number is conserved, so it does not determine the threshold. Upward swimming produces a dense upper layer; the unstable disturbance is its buoyancy-driven overturning, or bioconvection. Higher orders are needed for wavelength selection and finite-depth threshold corrections.
With fixed material parameters and mean concentration, R∼h4Vc​υΔρgNˉ00​/(ρνD2) for small β, so a sufficiently thin chamber is stable to this branch. Formally its threshold corresponds to hc​∼[720ρνD2/(Vc​υΔρgNˉ00​)]1/4 when the weak-stratification and distinguished-gyrotaxis assumptions apply. In particular, the assumed G=O(β−1) is a specified asymptotic analysis; at fixed B,D,Vc​ and decreasing h, one instead has G=O(β−2). The leading result should not be asserted unchanged in that different ordering without redoing the expansion.
