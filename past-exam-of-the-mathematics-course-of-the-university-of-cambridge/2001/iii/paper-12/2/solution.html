Let i:M↪N be an embedded submanifold. In the general terminology, a vector field along a map i is a smooth section X∈Γ(i∗TN), so Xp​∈Tp​N. A local extension of a vector field along a submanifold near p is a vector field X on an open neighbourhood in N with X∣M​=X on that neighbourhood. Choose adapted coordinates (x1,…,xm,ym+1,…,yn) in which M is given by y=0. Writing X=Xa(x)∂a​∣y=0​, extend the coefficient functions independently of y:
X(x,y)=∑a​Xa(x)∂a​.
(5)
This proves local existence, including for fields with normal components.
For the Lie bracket assertion, the fields must be tangent to M, namely sections of TM viewed inside i∗TN. In this case the useful criterion for a local extension of a vector field along a submanifold is
(XF)∣M​=X(F∣M​)for every local smooth function F on N.​
(6)
If X extends X, the chain rule gives this identity because their tangent values agree. Conversely, applying it to the adapted coordinate functions forces the tangent components to equal those of X and all normal components on M to vanish, so X∣M​=X. The criterion also shows that functions vanishing on M are differentiated to functions vanishing on M by X.
For tangent vector fields X,Y and local extensions X,Y, apply the criterion twice:
([X,Y]F)∣M​​=X((YF)∣M​)−Y((XF)∣M​)=X(Y(F∣M​))−Y(X(F∣M​))=[X,Y](F∣M​).​
(7)
Hence the ambient bracket restricts to the intrinsic bracket, independently of the chosen extensions. Tangency is essential. For example, on the x-axis in R2, let X=∂y​∣M​ and Y=0∣M​. The two extensions Y=0 and Y=y∂x​, with X=∂y​, give bracket restrictions 0 and ∂x​∣M​. Thus general sections with normal components do not possess the extension-independent Lie bracket asserted for tangent fields.
For the hypersurface Mn⊂Rn+1, choose a smooth unit normal ν locally. A global choice is needed only if one wants a globally signed shape operator; an arbitrary hypersurface need not come equipped with such a choice. With D the Euclidean Levi-Civita connection, define the shape operator, also called the Weingarten map, by
Sp​:Tp​M→Tp​M,Sp​(v)=−Dv​ν.​
(8)
The derivative is defined along M and is linear in v. Since v⟨ν,ν⟩=2⟨Dv​ν,ν⟩=0, its value is tangent to M, so this is an endomorphism of the tangent space. Differentiating ⟨ν,Y⟩=0 gives, for tangent vector fields X,Y,
h(X,Y):=⟨SX,Y⟩=⟨ν,DX​Y⟩.
(9)
This is the scalar second fundamental form for the chosen normal. The Euclidean connection on a vector bundle has zero torsion tensor, giving DX​Y−DY​X=[X,Y]. The bracket is tangent to M by the previous argument; therefore
h(X,Y)−h(Y,X)=⟨ν,[X,Y]⟩=0.
(10)
Consequently ⟨Sp​v,w⟩=⟨v,Sp​w⟩​ for every v,w∈Tp​M: the shape operator is self-adjoint. Reversing ν reverses S and h but preserves this self-adjointness.
