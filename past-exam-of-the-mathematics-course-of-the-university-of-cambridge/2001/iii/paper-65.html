i OurBigBook.com
Three distinct motivations are stabilizing the weak scale, improving gauge-coupling unification, and supplying dark matter. Supersymmetric cancellation of quadratic divergences protects the Higgs field from the hierarchy problem; soft supersymmetry breaking near the weak scale keeps the residual corrections modest. The MSSM particle content improves gauge coupling unification under the usual assumptions about intermediate thresholds. Conserved R-parity can make a neutral lightest supersymmetric particle, such as a neutralino, stable and a possible weak-scale dark matter candidate. These are motivations, not guaranteed predictions. If supersymmetry is instead broken at much higher energies, it can still organize string theory and supergravity, and it remains a powerful tool for nonperturbative quantum field theory, as illustrated by Seiberg–Witten theory.

A BPS state saturates a supersymmetry energy bound and is annihilated by some supercharges. For a massive state with one relevant central charge in supersymmetry, normalize the supersymmetry algebra by {QαA​,QβB​}=2ϵαβ​ϵABZ. In its rest frame, appropriate linear combinations of supercharges have anticommutators 2(M+∣Z∣) and 2(M−∣Z∣). Positivity therefore gives the BPS bound in supersymmetry M≥∣Z∣. At equality, the latter combinations have zero norm and annihilate the state, producing a shortened supermultiplet and preserved supersymmetry. With the alternative convention omitting the factor 2 in the central-charge anticommutator, the bound reads M≥∣Z∣/2. The mass is tied to the charge, but this does not imply unconditional stability against decay as parameters vary.

The super-Higgs mechanism is the absorption of the goldstino by the gravitino when local supersymmetry is spontaneously broken. In rigid supersymmetry breaking, the goldstino is a physical massless fermion. In supergravity, its transformation permits a gauge choice removing it; its two degrees of freedom become the longitudinal helicity ±21​ states of the massive gravitino. The massless gravitino has helicities ±23​, whereas the massive gravitino has four spin states. This is distinct from the supersymmetric Higgs mechanism, which gives an internal gauge vector a mass and need not break supersymmetry.

Anticommuting-variable dependence alone is not the defining condition: a superfield must carry a specified Super-Poincaré group transformation law. Any appropriately graded function on superspace becomes an unconstrained scalar superfield under coordinate pullback, but an arbitrary collection of coefficients in a Grassmann variable polynomial is not automatically a prescribed supermultiplet. For example, a chiral superfield has Φ(y,θ)=A(y)+2​θψ(y)+θ2F(y), with yμ=xμ+iθσμθˉ and Dˉα˙​Φ=0; a vector superfield obeys V=V† and contains a gauge vector, gaugino and auxiliary field in Wess-Zumino gauge. A polynomial with constant component fields can be a special chiral superfield; lack of explicit spacetime dependence alone does not disqualify it. This is superfield covariance is not Grassmann dependence alone.

Specify the gauge group, its gauge group representations and gaugings, a real Kähler potential K, a holomorphic superpotential W, and a symmetric holomorphic gauge kinetic function fab​, with positive kinetic matrices. The latter includes the gauge couplings and theta angles; allowed Abelian Fayet–Iliopoulos terms require constants ξa​. For local supersymmetry, also specify the Planck mass; constant Fayet–Iliopoulos terms in conventional N=1 supergravity require a compatible gauged R-symmetry and transformation of W. W and f are holomorphic; K generally is not. Radiative corrections generally change K. The Wilsonian effective action obeys superpotential non-renormalization perturbatively, although physical couplings run through field normalization and nonperturbative corrections can change W. The gauge kinetic function does receive corrections while remaining holomorphic; its perturbative Wilsonian running has one-loop exactness of the holomorphic gauge kinetic function, not all-order constancy of the physical gauge coupling.

The irreducible massive N=1 vector multiplet consists of one massive vector, one real scalar, and two massive spin-21​ fermions. Thus its on-shell boson and fermion counts are
nB​=3+1=4,nF​=2+2=4.
(1)
The two fermions can be written as two Majorana spinors or one Dirac spinor. All these states have the same mass when supersymmetry is unbroken; auxiliary fields are not extra propagating states.
To obtain rather than assume this spectrum, use the rest-frame supersymmetry algebra {Qα​,Qβ†​}=2Mδαβ​. The two normalized fermionic creation operators form a spin-21​ doublet. Start with a spin-21​ fermionic Clifford vacuum annihilated by the two lowering operators. The zero- and two-creation-operator levels each have spin 21​, whereas the one-creation-operator level has
21​⊗21​=0⊕1.
(2)
It is bosonic, giving exactly the scalar and vector above. A higher-spin Clifford vacuum would introduce spin above 1, while a spin-zero Clifford vacuum gives maximum spin only 21​.

For a massless supermultiplet, put the momentum along the third axis. The matrix 2σ⋅p in the supersymmetry algebra has rank one, so only one fermionic oscillator acts nontrivially. It relates states whose helicities differ by 21​. Including the antiparticles required by CPT completion of a supermultiplet gives the following complete spectra.
A chiral multiplet contains a complex scalar A and a Weyl spinor ψ. Its scalar particle and antiparticle have helicity 0; its fermion particle and antiparticle have helicities −21​ and +21​. Therefore nB​=2=nF​, with maximum absolute helicity 21​.
A supersymmetric vector multiplet contains a massless gauge vector Aμ​, with helicities +1,−1, and a Majorana spinor gaugino λ, with helicities +21​,−21​. Again nB​=2=nF​, now with maximum absolute helicity 1. The chiral F and vector D auxiliary fields contribute no on-shell states.
The Higgs mechanism is compatible with unbroken supersymmetry. A supersymmetric vacuum may have nonzero scalar vacuum expectation values that break the internal gauge symmetry while obeying both F-flatness and D-flatness. For example, oppositely charged chiral superfields with W=0, no Fayet–Iliopoulos term and equal nonzero scalar magnitudes have F=D=0 but break the Abelian gauge group. In the supersymmetric Higgs mechanism, a massless vector multiplet combines with the chiral multiplet along the broken gauge direction. One real scalar becomes the longitudinal vector polarization; the other remains a physical real scalar, and the gaugino combines with the Higgsino into a Dirac spinor. The resulting massive vector multiplet has 3+1=4 bosonic and 4 fermionic states with a common mass, as in part (i). Any other uneaten chiral multiplets remain separately in the spectrum.

Take canonical kinetic terms, gauge coupling g>0, and define the Fayet–Iliopoulos term by +ξD. If ϕ is the scalar component of the charge-q chiral superfield, the relevant auxiliary field Lagrangian is
LD​=21​D2+D(gq∣ϕ∣2+ξ),D=−(gq∣ϕ∣2+ξ).
(3)
The algebraic equation eliminates D and gives the D-term scalar potential
VD​=21​(ξ+gq∣ϕ∣2)2.​
(4)
Since the superpotential vanishes, F=0. In a homogeneous vacuum, the supersymmetry transformation of the gaugino includes
δλα​=iϵα​D+(σμνϵ)α​Fμν​=iϵα​D0​.
(5)
Thus D0​=0 gives an inhomogeneous shift: no nonzero constant supersymmetry parameter leaves this vacuum invariant. The gaugino λ is the goldstino, because it is the field shifting under the broken supercharge; there is no F contribution here.
For q=0, minimizing over x=∣ϕ∣2≥0 separates three cases. If qξ<0, the allowed value x=−ξ/(gq) gives D0​=V0​=0: supersymmetry is unbroken while the internal gauge symmetry is Higgsed. If qξ>0, the unconstrained zero of D would have negative x, so the minimum is ϕ=0 with D0​=−ξ and V0​=ξ2/2. If ξ=0, the minimum ϕ=0 is supersymmetric. Consequently, with this explicitly stated sign convention,
qξ>0⟺supersymmetry breaking(q=0).​
(6)
Reversing the sign used to define the Fayet–Iliopoulos term reverses the printed sign criterion, not the physics. If q=0, the scalar is decoupled and flat: any ξ=0 still breaks supersymmetry through D0​=−ξ, but the charged-field criterion does not apply.
On the broken branch, expand about ϕ=0:
V=21​ξ2+gqξ∣ϕ∣2+21​g2q2∣ϕ∣4.
(7)
Writing ϕ=(ϕ1​+iϕ2​)/2​ gives both real scalar modes the squared mass gqξ. There is no superpotential fermion mass and no vacuum expectation value to induce gaugino–matter-fermion mixing. The Abelian gauge symmetry is unbroken, so its vector is also massless. The complete mass spectrum of single charged-field D-term breaking is
mϕ1​2​=mϕ2​2​=gqξ,mψ​=mλ​=mA​=0.​
(8)
The original chiral multiplet therefore has the squared-mass splitting
Δmchiral2​=mϕ2​−mψ2​=gqξ=−gqD0​.​
(9)
The original vector multiplet has zero mass splitting, although the nonzero D0​ means it is not an unbroken-supersymmetry representation. As a check, the tree-level supertrace mass sum rule gives StrM2=2gqξ when the two real scalar degrees of freedom are counted. On the q=0 broken branch all the propagating fields are massless.
There is a necessary scope qualification: one nonzero-charge Weyl fermion has an Abelian cubic gauge anomaly proportional to q3 and a mixed gravitational gauge anomaly proportional to q. These are the classical vacuum and spectrum calculations requested by the model; a consistent quantum gauge theory needs an anomaly-cancelling completion, whose additional matter must also be included in its vacuum analysis.

Keep the PDF's hypercharge signs, which are the negatives of the frequently used convention. Write U,D,E,N for the displayed UR,DR,ER,NR chiral superfields. Their gauge group representations are conjugate right-handed-matter representations; their Weyl spinor components are left-chiral charge conjugates. Thus assign baryon number B(Q)=31​, B(U)=B(D)=−31​, and lepton number L(L)=1, L(E)=L(N)=−1, with both Higgs fields neutral under B,L. The corresponding electromagnetic convention is Qem​=T3​−Y.
Let X⋅Y=ϵab​XaYb contract the two weak doublets, and use the color Levi-Civita symbol for three color antitriplets. The most general homogeneous cubic superpotential with right-handed-neutrino superfields separates as W3​=WB,L​+WBorL​, where
WB,L​=​(Yu​)ij​(Qi​⋅H2​)Uj​+(Yd​)ij​(Qi​⋅H1​)Dj​+(Ye​)ij​(Li​⋅H1​)Ej​+(Yν​)ij​(Li​⋅H2​)Nj​,​
(10)
and
WBorL​=​21​λijk​(Li​⋅Lj​)Ek​+λijk′​(Li​⋅Qj​)Dk​+21​λijk′′​ϵrst​Uir​Djs​Dkt​+κi​Ni​(H1​⋅H2​)+61​ρijk​Ni​Nj​Nk​.​
(11)
Repeated generation and color indices are summed. The four Yukawa coupling matrices are arbitrary. The weak and color contractions imply λijk​=−λjik​ and λijk′′​=−λikj′′​, while ρijk​ is completely symmetric. The λ,λ′ terms have ΔL=1; λ′′ has ΔB=−1; κ and ρ have respectively ΔL=−1,−3. Their conjugate interactions have the opposite changes. All four Yukawa terms preserve both numbers; all five remaining types violate at least one. In particular, the neutral N fields printed in the PDF require both NH1​H2​ and NNN terms.
For completeness of this list, a cubic color gauge-invariant can contain a Q–U or Q–D pair and one colorless doublet, or three color antitriplets. Vanishing hypercharge gives QH2​U, QH1​D, LQD and UDD. Three Q doublets have no weak singlet. Among colorless terms, two doublets and a singlet give LLE, LH1​E, LH2​N and H1​H2​N; the apparent H1​H1​E contraction vanishes since the identical bosonic superfields commute. Three singlets yield only NNN. This also explains why no omitted cubic monomial is available. The word cubic excludes the linear N term and the quadratic H1​H2​, Li​H2​, Ni​Nj​ terms; these are separate possible terms in the full renormalizable superpotential.
To demonstrate squark-mediated proton decay, take λ11k′​ and λ11k′′​ with k=2 or 3. Identical down-flavor indices would make λ111′′​ vanish. Both interactions couple to the same scalar Dk​ of mass M. Suppressing overall convention-dependent vertex signs, their relevant two-component fermion bilinears are
Lint​=−Dk​A−Dk∗​A†,A=λ11k′​(νe​dL​−eL​uL​)+λ11k′′​ϵrst​ucrdcs.
(12)
The color index on A is contracted with that on Dk​; Lorentz spinor indices within each bilinear are contracted too. Together with −M2∣Dk​∣2, its algebraic equation at momenta small compared with M gives Dk​=−A†/M2. Substitution gives A†A/M2, whose cross term is the four-fermion matching for squark-mediated proton decay
Leff​⊃M2λ11k′​λ11k′′​∗​ϵrst​(νe​dLt​−eL​uLt​)(ucr†dcs†)+h.c.
(13)
Its electron term contains three quark fields of flavors uud and a lepton, and changes baryon number and lepton number together. The Hermitian-conjugate terms describe both orientations of the process. The hadronic matrix element of the three-quark proton-decay operator between a proton and a neutral pion has the right quantum numbers and is not forbidden by a remaining symmetry. Hence the operator permits p→e+π0: the lepton field creates the positron and the three-quark matrix element connects the proton to the pion. This supplies the mechanism rather than merely naming the two violating interactions.
Each fermion field has mass dimension 23​, so this effective operator has dimension six and coefficient C6​=λ′λ′′∗/M2. By dimensional analysis its contribution to the decay rate has the form
Γ(p→e+π0)∼ch​M4∣λ11k′​λ11k′′​∣2mp5​​.​
(14)
Here ch​ collects phase space, running and the dimensionless hadronic factor; a dimensional estimate takes it of order unity rather than pretending to calculate a strong-interaction matrix element. Assuming this exchange dominates and there is no tuned cancellation, the supplied lifetime gives the proton-lifetime bound on a product of R-parity-violating couplings
∣λ11k′​λ11k′′​∣≲ch​τmin​mp5​​M2​≃6.5×10−27ch−1/2​(1TeVM​)2.​
(15)
The last estimate uses mp​≃1GeV and τmin​=2.4×1064GeV−1. Keeping mp​=0.938GeV instead changes 6.5 to about 7.6. A numerical limit independent of the squark mass or hadronic factor is not determined by the supplied data.
Finally, matter parity is PM​=(−1)3(B−L): all matter chiral superfields, including N, are odd, and the Higgs chiral superfields are even. R-parity of a component field is Rp​=PM​(−1)2s. In a superpotential, the spin factor is irrelevant to the allowed monomials: each of QH2​U,QH1​D,LH1​E,LH2​N has two odd matter factors and is even. Each of LLE,LQD,UDD,NNN has three odd matter factors, and NH1​H2​ has one, so all are odd and forbidden. Higgs vacuum expectation values are even; the allowed Yukawa interactions therefore still generate ordinary fermion masses.
R-parity forbids all the baryon/lepton-number-violating cubic terms above, not every possible violating operator. In particular, 21​Mij​Ni​Nj​ is even under matter parity but changes lepton number by two: matter parity allows Majorana neutrino masses. Thus the literal unrestricted claim would be false for these very fields; its valid cubic interpretation gives precisely the requested exclusion while preserving the Yukawa coupling mass terms.
