For 1<s≤2​, use centered tent-map period-doubling renormalization. Let
c=s−1,Js​=[−c,c],hs​(y)=−cy.
(6)
The image Ts​(Js​)=[1+s−s2,1] lies to the right of Js​, with disjoint interiors, because 1+s−s2≥s−1. It lies in the positive branch. Consequently,
Ts2​(x)=1−s+s2∣x∣=−c+s2∣x∣(x∈Js​),
(7)
and its range [−c,(s2−1)c] is contained in Js​. The orientation-reversing affine map hs​ gives the exact topological conjugacy
hs−1​∘Ts2​∘hs​(y)=1−s2∣y∣=Ts2​(y).​
(8)
Thus renormalization squares the slope and halves the return period. Since s>1, there is a least n≥0 for which s2n>2​. Minimality implies s2n≤2, and all previous slopes are in the renormalizable range. Successive affine maps therefore conjugate the restriction of Ts2n​ on a nested central interval to Ts2n​.
By the preceding part, an iterate Ts2nm​ has a horseshoe for an interval map. Pull its two branch intervals and its range interval back through the composed conjugacy. They give a horseshoe for Ts2nm​. Therefore
Ts​ has an iterate with a horseshoe for every 1<s≤2.​
(9)
At the endpoint s=2​, one renormalization gives the full slope-two tent map, so the argument includes that endpoint without assuming the strict inequality from part (a).
