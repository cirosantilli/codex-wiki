At a current approximation p, Taylor's theorem replaces Φ(p+h)=0 by the affine equation Φ(p)+DΦ(p)h=0. Solving this equation gives the Newton iteration in a Banach space
N(p)=p−[DΦ(p)]−1Φ(p),pn+1​=N(pn​).​

The displayed operator is defined where the derivative is invertible, in particular on the specified open domain; the hypotheses do not supply a globally defined Newton operator on all of X. If Φ(pˉ​)=0 with pˉ​ in that domain, then N(pˉ​)=pˉ​.

For a fixed-point equation, apply the same method to F(p)=Φ(p)−p. Wherever DΦ(p)−I is invertible, the exact adapted map is
A(p)=p−[DΦ(p)−I]−1[Φ(p)−p].​
(31)
Every fixed point of Φ is fixed by A, and conversely on this domain. This additional invertibility condition is distinct from invertibility of DΦ; for example, DΦ=I is invertible but DΦ−I=0 is not.
For certification, a frozen Newton correction for a fixed-point equation is often more useful. Choose a bounded, injective approximate inverse J of DΦ(p0​)−I and set
AJ​(p)=p−J[Φ(p)−p],DAJ​(p)=I−J[DΦ(p)−I].
(32)
Again AJ​(p)=p if and only if Φ(p)=p. A well-chosen J makes the corrected operator contract even if the original operator has an expanding direction. The subsequent contraction mapping argument requires the stated derivative and residual bounds; it does not assume that arbitrary inverse approximations provide them.
