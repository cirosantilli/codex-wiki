Let qj​ denote the four critical wavevectors, and write Aj​=Rj​eiθj​. A spatial translation by (s,t) acts on the amplitudes as
(A1​,A2​,A3​,A4​)⟼(ei(2s+t)A1​,ei(2s−t)A2​,ei(s+2t)A3​,ei(s−2t)A4​).
(1)
The two independent translation directions in phase space are (2,2,1,1) and (1,−1,2,−2). Their common orthogonal complement is spanned by (2,−2,−1,1) and (1,1,−2,−2). Hence translation invariants of Fourier-mode phases can be chosen as
χ1​=2θ1​−2θ2​−θ3​+θ4​,χ2​=θ1​+θ2​−2θ3​−2θ4​.​
(2)
Away from zero amplitudes, a translation-invariant function of the phases is a function of these two invariant angles. The four magnitudes are themselves invariant. Thus an equivariant dynamical system on the eight-real-dimensional centre manifold descends, after quotienting translation symmetry, to four magnitude equations and two invariant-phase equations. The phase coordinates are singular where an amplitude vanishes; the original complex-amplitude equations remain smooth there.
The wavevector selection rule for equivariant monomials proves the cubic regularity. For a monomial ∏j​Ajpj​​Ajqj​​ in the first equation, put dj​=pj​−qj​. Translation covariance requires
2d1​+2d2​+d3​+d4​=2,d1​−d2​+2d3​−2d4​=1.
(3)
At total degree N, additionally ∑∣dj​∣≤N and N−∑∣dj​∣ is even. Conversely, any integer vector satisfying these conditions yields a monomial, with any remaining even degree supplied by factors ∣Aj​∣2. This makes the degree test exhaustive. For example, eliminate the first two entries:
d1​=44−5d3​+3d4​​,d2​=43d3​−5d4​​.
(4)
Checking the integer pairs ∣d3​∣+∣d4​∣≤N, retaining only integral d1​,d2​ with the norm and parity conditions, gives
N135​admissible d(1,0,0,0)(1,0,0,0)(1,0,0,0), (−1,2,1,−1), (0,−1,2,2).​​
(5)
At degree three the only possibility is therefore A1​∣Aj​∣2, j=1,…,4. Square symmetry gives the same conclusion for every other equation. All cubic terms are regular.
Two generators of the square dihedral group act as follows. Reflection in the x axis gives (A1​,A2​,A3​,A4​)↦(A2​,A1​,A4​,A3​), while interchange of x,y gives (A3​,A4​,A1​,A2​). Rotation by π conjugates every amplitude, forcing the coefficients in a steady normal form of a dynamical system to be real. With real unfolding parameter σ and real coefficients c1​,c2​,c3​,c4​, define
G1​G2​G3​G4​​=σ+c1​∣A1​∣2+c2​∣A2​∣2+c3​∣A3​∣2+c4​∣A4​∣2,=σ+c1​∣A2​∣2+c2​∣A1​∣2+c3​∣A4​∣2+c4​∣A3​∣2,=σ+c1​∣A3​∣2+c2​∣A4​∣2+c3​∣A1​∣2+c4​∣A2​∣2,=σ+c1​∣A4​∣2+c2​∣A3​∣2+c3​∣A2​∣2+c4​∣A1​∣2.​
(6)
The most general cubic normal form is A˙j​=Aj​Gj​+O(∣A∣5). There are no further coefficient identifications: the square-group stabilizer of one of these oblique wavevectors is trivial, so its three couplings to the other magnitudes are independent. At cubic order R˙j​=Rj​Gj​ and all phases, including χ1​,χ2​, are constant.
