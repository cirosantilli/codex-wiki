Past exam of the mathematics course of the University of Cambridge / 2001 / iii / Paper 54 / 2 / a / Solution

The spatial mean is conserved: averaging every term in the partial differential equation over the periodic cell gives ∂t​⟨T⟩=0. Adding a constant to T also preserves the equation. We therefore fix the mean, conveniently at zero. This qualification is essential: without it the constant Fourier mode contributes an additional neutral direction, so the centre subspace would have five real dimensions rather than four.
On a Fourier mode ei(mx+ny), the linearized linear operator has eigenvalue
λm,n​=μ(m2+n2)−(m2+n2)2.
(13)
On the fixed-mean subspace, the first eigenvalues to reach zero are those with m2+n2=1. At μ=1 these are the modes e±ix,e±iy; every other nonconstant Fourier mode is strictly damped. The reality condition leaves two complex amplitudes, hence a four-real-dimensional centre manifold on the fixed-mean subspace. The trivial state loses linear stability as μ increases through one.
Use slow time τ=ε2t, and write T=εW+ε3T3​+⋯, where
W=A(τ)eix+A(τ)e−ix+B(τ)eiy+B(τ)e−iy.
(14)
There is no quadratic nonlinearity, so no forced second-order correction is needed. At order ε3, with μ=1+ε2ν, projection onto the critical Fourier modes gives
Wτ​=−νΔW+(−Δ−Δ2)T3​+∇⋅(∣∇W∣2∇W).
(15)
The correction T3​ does not contribute to the critical projection because the linear operator vanishes on those modes. To find the nonlinear coefficient of eix, split the divergence term into
∂x​(Wx3​)+∂x​(Wy2​Wx​)+∂y​(Wx2​Wy​)+∂y​(Wy3​).
(16)
The first term contributes −3∣A∣2A. In the second, only the zero-y Fourier coefficient of Wy2​ contributes; it is 2∣B∣2, and differentiating Wx​ once more gives −2∣B∣2A. The third term has zero y-average, and the fourth has no x-dependent critical component. Interchanging x,y gives the other amplitude equation. Thus the conserved-mean convection amplitude equations are
Aτ​=νA−3∣A∣2A−2∣B∣2A,Bτ​=νB−3∣B∣2B−2∣A∣2B,α=3, β=2.​
(17)
To establish the selected planform, put A=reiθ, B=seiψ. The phases are constant and the magnitudes obey
r˙=r(ν−3r2−2s2),s˙=s(ν−3s2−2r2).
(18)
For ν>0, the square state has r=s=ν/5​. Its magnitude Jacobian matrix is −(2ν/5)(32​23​), with eigenvalues −2ν and −2ν/5. Its two neutral phases correspond to translation symmetry. The pure roll has r2=ν/3,s=0; an infinitesimal B disturbance grows at rate ν−2ν/3=ν/3>0. The other pure roll is equivalent, while the trivial state is unstable for ν>0. Consequently squares, with ∣A∣2=∣B∣2=ν/5, are the stable small-amplitude planform, modulo translations. For ν<0 the trivial state is stable.
