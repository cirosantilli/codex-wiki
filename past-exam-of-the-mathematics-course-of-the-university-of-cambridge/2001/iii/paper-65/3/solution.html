Take canonical kinetic terms, gauge coupling g>0, and define the Fayet–Iliopoulos term by +ξD. If ϕ is the scalar component of the charge-q chiral superfield, the relevant auxiliary field Lagrangian is
LD​=21​D2+D(gq∣ϕ∣2+ξ),D=−(gq∣ϕ∣2+ξ).
(3)
The algebraic equation eliminates D and gives the D-term scalar potential
VD​=21​(ξ+gq∣ϕ∣2)2.​
(4)
Since the superpotential vanishes, F=0. In a homogeneous vacuum, the supersymmetry transformation of the gaugino includes
δλα​=iϵα​D+(σμνϵ)α​Fμν​=iϵα​D0​.
(5)
Thus D0​=0 gives an inhomogeneous shift: no nonzero constant supersymmetry parameter leaves this vacuum invariant. The gaugino λ is the goldstino, because it is the field shifting under the broken supercharge; there is no F contribution here.
For q=0, minimizing over x=∣ϕ∣2≥0 separates three cases. If qξ<0, the allowed value x=−ξ/(gq) gives D0​=V0​=0: supersymmetry is unbroken while the internal gauge symmetry is Higgsed. If qξ>0, the unconstrained zero of D would have negative x, so the minimum is ϕ=0 with D0​=−ξ and V0​=ξ2/2. If ξ=0, the minimum ϕ=0 is supersymmetric. Consequently, with this explicitly stated sign convention,
qξ>0⟺supersymmetry breaking(q=0).​
(6)
Reversing the sign used to define the Fayet–Iliopoulos term reverses the printed sign criterion, not the physics. If q=0, the scalar is decoupled and flat: any ξ=0 still breaks supersymmetry through D0​=−ξ, but the charged-field criterion does not apply.
On the broken branch, expand about ϕ=0:
V=21​ξ2+gqξ∣ϕ∣2+21​g2q2∣ϕ∣4.
(7)
Writing ϕ=(ϕ1​+iϕ2​)/2​ gives both real scalar modes the squared mass gqξ. There is no superpotential fermion mass and no vacuum expectation value to induce gaugino–matter-fermion mixing. The Abelian gauge symmetry is unbroken, so its vector is also massless. The complete mass spectrum of single charged-field D-term breaking is
mϕ1​2​=mϕ2​2​=gqξ,mψ​=mλ​=mA​=0.​
(8)
The original chiral multiplet therefore has the squared-mass splitting
Δmchiral2​=mϕ2​−mψ2​=gqξ=−gqD0​.​
(9)
The original vector multiplet has zero mass splitting, although the nonzero D0​ means it is not an unbroken-supersymmetry representation. As a check, the tree-level supertrace mass sum rule gives StrM2=2gqξ when the two real scalar degrees of freedom are counted. On the q=0 broken branch all the propagating fields are massless.
There is a necessary scope qualification: one nonzero-charge Weyl fermion has an Abelian cubic gauge anomaly proportional to q3 and a mixed gravitational gauge anomaly proportional to q. These are the classical vacuum and spectrum calculations requested by the model; a consistent quantum gauge theory needs an anomaly-cancelling completion, whose additional matter must also be included in its vacuum analysis.
