Work in a regular statistical model, with fixed support, differentiability under the integral, and nonsingular Fisher information. Let U=(Uψ​,Uλ​) be the score function. The blocks are orthogonal statistical parameters at θ when
Iψλ​(θ)=Eθ​[Uψ​UλT​]=−Eθ​[ℓψλ​]=0.​
(1)
This is global parameter orthogonality if it holds throughout the parameter domain; otherwise it is an assertion at a specified point.
For an independent size-n sample, the regular maximum-likelihood estimator satisfies
n​(ψ​−ψλ−λ​) d​ N(0,(Iψψ−1​0​0Iλλ−1​​)),
(2)
where I is the information in one observation. Thus the fitted blocks are independent to first order in their limiting normal distribution. The efficient information for ψ is generally the Schur complement Iψψ​−Iψλ​Iλλ−1​Iλψ​; under orthogonality it is just Iψψ​. Estimating the nuisance block therefore has no first-order variance cost for the interest block, compared with knowing the nuisance parameter.
This also reduces local movement of the constrained nuisance fit. If j=−ℓ′′ is the observed information, differentiating its nuisance score equation gives
∂ψT∂λψ​​=−jλλ−1​jλψ​.
(3)
Under regular independent sampling and orthogonality, jλλ​=Op​(n) and the centered mixed block is Op​(n​). Thus this derivative is Op​(n−1/2) locally, and an Op​(n−1/2) change in ψ changes the nuisance fit by only Op​(n−1). Neither exact finite-sample independence nor vanishing observed cross-information follows from expected-information orthogonality.
For the displayed density family, λ is scalar and
ℓ(ψ,λ;y)=loga(λ,y)+λt(y;ψ),Uψ​=λ∇ψ​t(y;ψ).

The mean-zero score identity gives Eθ​∇ψ​t=0 for λ=0. But
ℓψλ​=∇ψ​t,

so
Iψλ​=0.​
(6)
This is score factorization implies parameter orthogonality. If zero belongs to the parameter domain, the conclusion extends there by continuity of the regular expected derivatives. At zero the density itself has no dependence on ψ, so identifiability and nonsingular information for that block must be treated separately.
