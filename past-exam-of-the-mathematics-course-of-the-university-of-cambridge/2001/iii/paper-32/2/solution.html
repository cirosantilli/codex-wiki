Partition the statistical parameter as θ=(ψ,λ), with ψ of interest. For each fixed ψ, maximize over the nuisance parameter to obtain λψ​. The profile likelihood and profile log-likelihood are
Lp​(ψ)=L(ψ,λψ​),ℓp​(ψ)=ℓ(ψ,λψ​).
(7)
Their maximizer is the interest component of the joint maximum-likelihood estimator. At a regular nuisance maximum, ℓp′​(ψ)=ℓψ​(ψ,λψ​) and its negative Hessian matrix is the Schur complement
jp​=jψψ​−jψλ​jλλ−1​jλψ​.
(8)
The profile likelihood ratio is unchanged by a one-to-one nuisance reparametrization, and transforms naturally under a one-to-one interest reparametrization. Under the regular conditions of Wilks theorem, 2{ℓp​(ψ​)−ℓp​(ψ0​)} has a limiting chi-squared distribution with dimension of ψ degrees of freedom. Profiling is maximization, rather than integration or conditioning; Lp​ need not be a normalized data density, and nuisance fitting can cause appreciable higher-order distortion.
The modified profile likelihood corrects for nuisance curvature and sample-coordinate distortion. In suitable coordinates (ψ​,λ,a), with a an ancillary statistic, set
Jψ​=​det∂λ∂λψ​​​,
(9)
holding ψ​,a fixed when differentiating the data coordinates. Up to an interest-independent factor, the Barndorff-Nielsen modified profile likelihood is
Lm​(ψ)=Lp​(ψ)∣jλλ​(ψ,λψ​)∣−1/2Jψ−1​.​
(10)
Equivalently, implicit differentiation of the nuisance score equation gives Jψ​=∣jλλ​∣−1∣ℓλ;λ​∣, and
ℓm​=ℓp​+21​log∣jλλ​∣−log∣ℓλ;λ​∣.
(11)
The semicolon denotes sample-coordinate differentiation, not a second parameter derivative. In orthogonal coordinates, the Cox-Reid adjusted profile likelihood uses the information adjustment alone; it agrees with this modification when Jψ​=1, as happens below.
For the Inverse Gaussian distribution, write s=∑i​yi​, r=∑i​yi−1​ and yˉ​=s/n. Terms independent of both parameters can be absorbed into c(y), giving
ℓ(ψ,λ)=c(y)+2n​logψ−2ψ​(r−λ2n​+λ2s​).
(12)
The nuisance score function is
ℓλ​=λ3ψ(s−nλ)​.
(13)
It is positive below yˉ​ and negative above it. Thus its global maximum is
λψ​=λ=yˉ​,
(14)
independent of ψ. Put D=r−n/yˉ​. Then
ℓp​(ψ)=2n​logψ−2ψ​D+constant.​
(15)
The nuisance observed information at this fit is
jλλ​(ψ,yˉ​)=yˉ​3nψ​.
(16)
Since the constrained nuisance estimator is literally the full nuisance estimator as a function of the data, Jψ​=1 in the specified sample coordinates. Hence the modified profile likelihood for inverse Gaussian shape is
ℓm​(ψ)=2n−1​logψ−2ψ​D+constant.​
(17)
For n≥2 and a nonconstant positive sample, the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality gives D>0. The corresponding fits are ψ​p​=n/D and ψ​m​=(n−1)/D. A constant sample has D=0 and no finite profile maximum; under continuous independent sampling this event has probability zero when n≥2. In particular, the modified coefficient accounts for the fitted mean nuisance parameter.
