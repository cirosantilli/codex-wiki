Use the usual two mutually independent samples, and rank their N=n+m pooled observations increasingly. Continuity gives no ties with probability one. Let Ri​ be the pooled rank of Xi​ and define the Wilcoxon rank sum test statistic
WX​=∑i=1n​Ri​,UX​=WX​−2n(n+1)​=∑i=1n​∑j=1m​1{Yj​<Xi​}​.
(44)
The last identity counts the ranks contributed by the other sample after subtracting the within-X ranks.
When FX​≥FY​, the X observations are stochastically smaller, so reject for small WX​, equivalently small UX​. For instance,
EUX​=nmPr(Y<X)=nm∫{1−FX​(y)}dFY​(y)≤2nm​.
(45)
The inequality follows from FX​≥FY​ and ∫FY​dFY​=1/2 for continuous FY​. A common-uniform quantile coupling also makes this direction transparent: FX−1​(u)≤FY−1​(u), and UX​ is nondecreasing in each Xi​.
Under the common-distribution null hypothesis, the pooled observations are exchangeable. Every selection of n ranks for the X labels has probability (nN​)−1. Thus the exact null law is
Pr0​(WX​=w)=(nN​)#{A⊆{1,…,N}:∣A∣=n, ∑r∈A​r=w}​.
(46)
Choose a lower-tail critical value with probability at most the desired size, optionally randomizing at its boundary to attain the size exactly. This law is independent of the unknown common distribution. Common increasing transformations leave the pooled ranks of observations unchanged, so the statistic is a function of the maximal invariant from part (i). Although a rank sum is not itself maximal, it is an invariant test statistic. These facts justify the test within nonparametric statistics.
Sampling ranks without replacement gives
E0​WX​=2n(N+1)​,var0​WX​=12nm(N+1)​,
(47)
so E0​UX​=nm/2 with the same variance. For n,m→∞ with n/N→ρ∈(0,1), the normal approximation for a rank sum states
nm(N+1)/12​WX​−n(N+1)/2​ d​ N(0,1).​
(48)
Equivalently replace WX​−n(N+1)/2 by UX​−nm/2. The large-sample one-sided test rejects when this standardized statistic is below the lower α normal distribution quantile. This is the two-sample rank-sum test, rather than the paired Wilcoxon signed-rank test.
