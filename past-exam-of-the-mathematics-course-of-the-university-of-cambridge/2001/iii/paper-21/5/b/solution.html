For t>0, put Mt​=sup0≤s≤t​Bs​. For λ>0, reflect the Brownian path after its first hit of λ. The Strong Markov property and symmetry of subsequent Brownian increments make this reflection preserve the path distribution. It exchanges the events {Mt​≥λ,Bt​<λ} and {Bt​>λ}. Since Bt​ has no atom at λ, the Brownian reflection principle gives
P(Mt​>λ)=2P(Bt​>λ)=2(1−Φ(λ/t​)),t>0, λ≥0.​
(36)
The case λ=0 follows by decreasing positive levels to zero: the probability is one. At t=0, M0​=0 and the probability is zero for every λ≥0.
The displayed tail probabilities are exactly those of ∣Bt​∣, so Mt​ has the half-normal distribution of t​∣Z∣ with Z∼N(0,1). Thus
∥Mt​∥2​=EBt2​​=t​,t≥0.​
(37)
For At​=sups≤t​∣Bs​∣, the pointwise inequality At​≥Mt​ gives the lower non-strict bound. To prove strictness when t>0, fix λ>0. Reflection at λ also gives
P(Bt​<−λ,Mt​≥λ)=P(Bt​>3λ).
(38)
Subtracting this from P(Bt​<−λ)=P(Bt​>λ) gives
P(Bt​<−λ,Mt​<λ)=P(Bt​>λ)−P(Bt​>3λ)>0.
(39)
On this event At​≥∣Bt​∣>λ>Mt​, so At2​−Mt2​ is strictly positive with positive probability. Finally, ∣Bt​∣ is a continuous nonnegative submartingale by the conditional Jensen inequality, and part (a) gives ∥At​∥2​≤2∥Bt​∥2​=2t​. Its square is therefore integrable, and the strict pointwise comparison on a positive-probability event gives
t​<∥At​∥2​≤2t​(t>0).​
(40)
This is the strict comparison of one-sided and absolute Brownian maxima. The strict lower inequality printed with t≥0 needs the qualification t>0: at t=0 both maxima vanish and all three quantities are zero.
