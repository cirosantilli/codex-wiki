The holding move contributes zero. Thus the period-two martingale corrector for a birth-death chain exists precisely when
θ=s0​d0​​=−s1​d1​​,s0​d0​​+s1​d1​​=0.​

This is the condition that hθ​ be a harmonic function for a Markov chain. The process has integrable values from a fixed initial state because ∣Xn​∣≤∣X0​∣+n; the Markov property therefore turns the zero conditional increments into the required martingale property. Conversely, both parities are reached with positive probability because all right and left probabilities are positive, so the two conditional equations are necessary. Expanding their numerator also gives the equivalent balance condition p0​p1​=q0​q1​. Positivity makes ∣θ∣<1.
Put L=−aN, U=bN. The exit time TN​ is almost surely finite. Indeed, set m=U−L and p∗​=min(p0​,p1​)>0. From any interior point, consecutive right steps reach U in at most m moves, with probability at least p∗m​. The Markov property gives P(TN​>km)≤(1−p∗m​)k. This is the finite-interval exit times have geometric tails argument. The walk cannot skip a boundary because its jumps have size at most one.
Under the balance condition, the stopped martingale hθ​(Xn∧TN​​) is bounded, because the stopped position stays in [L,U]. Apply the optional stopping theorem at n∧TN​, then dominated convergence as n→∞. Starting from zero gives
0=πN​hθ​(L)+(1−πN​)hθ​(U).

With εa​=1{aN odd}​ and εb​=1{bN odd}​,
πN​=(a+b)N+θεa​−θεb​bN−θεb​​,N→∞lim​πN​=a+bb​.​

The bounded parity corrections disappear after division by N; the holding probability of a Markov chain may be positive or zero and may differ between parities.
For failure of balance, use scale increments for a birth-death chain rather than the nonexistent bounded correction. Let u(k) be the upper exit probability from k for boundaries L,U. The Markov property yields
pk​(u(k+1)−u(k))=qk​(u(k)−u(k−1)),u(L)=0,u(U)=1,
