The physical angle takes values on a circle, so it is defined modulo 2π. Around a closed curve enclosing a phase vortex,
∮∇θ⋅dl=2πn,n∈Z.
(12)
The integer winding number cannot change through a smooth single-valued deformation unless a defect crosses the curve. Thus a globally smooth Gaussian spin wave field omits whole topological sectors of the XY model.
Outside a vortex core of radius a, an isolated charge-n phase vortex has θ=nφ and ∣∇θ∣=∣n∣/r. Its dimensionless energy in a sample of size L is
βEn​=2Kˉ​∫aL​2πrdrr2n2​=πKˉn2log(L/a)+βEcore​.
(13)
There are of order (L/a)2 positions for its core, so the positional entropy contributes 2log(L/a). The competition for unit charge is therefore (πKˉ−2)log(L/a), apart from core terms. Opposite charges have a logarithmic attraction and form a vortex Coulomb gas.
At low temperature, vortex-antivortex binding leaves no free charges on large scales. Spin waves still destroy true long-range order, but the correlation function decays algebraically with the renormalized phase stiffness. At the Berezinskii–Kosterlitz–Thouless transition, pairs unbind; free vortices screen the interaction and produce a finite correlation length and exponential decay above the transition. The energy-entropy estimate identifies the threshold stiffness KR​=2/π, where KR​ is the long-distance dimensionless stiffness, not necessarily the bare Kˉ. It agrees with the universal stiffness jump and the limiting algebraic exponent 1/(2πKR​)=1/4. The two-dimensional transition is vortex unbinding between quasi-long-range order and disorder, rather than the onset of nonzero spontaneous magnetization.
