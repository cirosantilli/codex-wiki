Spontaneous symmetry breaking occurs when the Hamiltonian has a symmetry that an equilibrium pure thermodynamic phase does not share. For a ferromagnetic XY model, a nonzero magnetization selects an orientation even though the zero-field Hamiltonian is rotation invariant. The order of limits matters: with V the volume and h a field selecting the orientation, the spontaneous magnetization is
m0​=limh→0+​limV→∞​⟨m⟩V,h​.
(1)
A finite system at zero field instead averages over symmetry-related orientations and has zero vector magnetization. Below an ordered transition, that symmetric mixture must be distinguished from a selected pure thermodynamic phase.
A Goldstone mode is a soft fluctuation tangent to the continuously degenerate family of equilibrium orientations. For the XY model, the uniform change θ↦θ+ϵ costs no energy. Local changes cost only gradients, so the Goldstone-mode effective free energy starts with 21​ρs​∫(∇θ)2, with no restoring term proportional to θ2. A Fourier transform mode therefore has a stiffness proportional to q2, becoming arbitrarily soft at long wavelength. The dimensionless phase stiffness here is Kˉ=βρs​. These are the spin waves of the angle field. Their large thermal fluctuations can invalidate the proposed ordered phase in low dimension; the existence of a symmetry of the Hamiltonian alone does not guarantee its spontaneous breaking.
