The transformed values are Cox–Snell residuals. Under a correct common survival function and a good fitted cumulative hazard function, the complete transformed failure times should have unit exponential distribution. Transform the observed times and retain their original censoring indicators, then estimate residual survival with a Kaplan–Meier estimator. Its target is
Su​(u)=e−u,u≥0,​
(4)
so a plot of its logarithm against u should lie approximately on a line of slope −1. Censored residuals must not be treated as fully observed failure times.
There is a useful caution for this particular common nonparametric fit. The shared-fit Cox–Snell residuals reproduce the fitted survival curve property means that the pooled residual plot is largely built into the transformation. With event ordering and compatible tie processing preserved, at a transformed failure time uj​=H(tj​) its residual Kaplan–Meier estimator equals S(tj​)=e−uj​. Finite steps, coalesced censoring times and estimation uncertainty still matter, and an infinite final residual arises if the fitted survivor becomes zero. Separate group plots are more informative about a common-distribution assumption than this pooled fit alone.
