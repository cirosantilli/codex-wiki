Work over C with a finite-dimensional semisimple Lie algebra g. Choose a Cartan subalgebra h, its root system R⊂h∗, a positive system R+ and its simple roots. Let W be the Weyl group, generated by the corresponding root reflections, and put ρ=21​∑α∈R+​α, the Weyl vector. The finite-dimensional irreducible highest-weight representation L(λ) is indexed by a dominant integral weight λ.
If P is the weight lattice, its group ring has formal symbols eμ with multiplication eμeν=eμ+ν. The formal character is
chL(λ)=∑μ∈P​(dimL(λ)μ​)eμ,
(1)
where L(λ)μ​={v:Hv=μ(H)v for all H∈h} is a weight space. For a weight ν, define the Weyl alternant Aν​=∑w∈W​det(w)ewν; the sign det(w)=(−1)ℓ(w) is the determinant of w on the real root span, equivalently the parity of its expression in simple reflections.
The Weyl character formula is
chL(λ)=Aρ​Aλ+ρ​​=eρ∏α∈R+​(1−e−α)∑w∈W​det(w)ew(λ+ρ)​.​
(2)
The second denominator is the Weyl denominator formula. The quotient can initially be taken in the fraction field of the group ring; the formula says it is the finite character on the left, so its apparent singularities are removable. The shifts by ρ make λ+ρ strictly dominant.

Let N=∣R+∣ and identify the real weight and Cartan spaces using a Weyl group-invariant positive inner product. For a regular H, meaning (α,H)=0 for every root, specialize eμ to et(μ,H). Then the value of the formal character at t=0 is dimL(λ), but both Weyl alternants in the Weyl character formula vanish there. We compute their leading terms instead of substituting into that zero-over-zero expression.
Expand
Aν​(tH)=∑m≥0​tmPm​(ν,H),Pm​(ν,H)=m!1​∑w∈W​det(w)(wν,H)m.
(3)
For every root reflection s, replacing ν by sν changes the sign of Pm​, by replacing w with ws in the sum. Replacing H by sH also changes its sign, by replacing w with sw. Thus the polynomial vanishes whenever ν or H lies on any reflecting hyperplane. It is divisible by ∏α>0​(ν,α) in its first variables and by ∏α>0​(H,α) in its second variables. Distinct positive roots give distinct linear factors; each product has degree N.
The polynomial Pm​ has degree m in each group of variables. Therefore Pm​=0 for m<N, and for m=N it has the form
PN​(ν,H)=C∏α>0​(ν,α)∏α>0​(H,α)
(4)
with a constant C independent of both variables. At ν=ρ, the Weyl denominator formula gives
Aρ​(tH)=et(ρ,H)∏α>0​(1−e−t(α,H))=tN∏α>0​(α,H)+O(tN+1).
(5)
Consequently C∏α>0​(ρ,α)=1. For strictly dominant ν=λ+ρ, its leading coefficient is nonzero, and the ratio of the two Weyl alternants tends to
dimL(λ)=α>0∏​(ρ,α)(λ+ρ,α)​=α>0∏​⟨ρ,α∨⟩⟨λ+ρ,α∨⟩​.​
(6)
The coroot is α∨=2α/(α,α), so the root-length factors cancel in each ratio. This proves the Weyl dimension formula by the lowest-degree alternant proof of the Weyl dimension formula, including the limiting step.

Apply the Weyl character formula with λ=ρ. In the Weyl denominator formula, replace every formal exponential eμ by e2μ. This gives
A2ρ​=e2ρ∏α>0​(1−e−2α).
(7)
Dividing by Aρ​=eρ∏α>0​(1−e−α) and cancelling each factor yields
chL(ρ)=eρα>0∏​(1+e−α)=α>0∏​(eα/2+e−α/2).​
(8)
The last expression is a compact way of writing the same character; individual half-root exponentials may need a larger formal lattice, but their full product lies in the group ring of the weight lattice. Expanding the first expression, a subset S⊆R+ contributes the weight ρ−∑α∈S​α. Different subsets with the same sum give that weight's multiplicity. In particular dimL(ρ)=2∣R+∣, consistent with the Weyl dimension formula. This is the case k=1 of the character of a Weyl-vector multiple.

The complex Special orthogonal Lie algebra so5​ consists of endomorphisms preserving a nondegenerate symmetric bilinear form infinitesimally:
so(V,B)={X:B(Xu,v)+B(u,Xv)=0}.
(9)
In an orthonormal basis this is {X∈M5​(C):XT+X=0}, with the commutator bracket and dimension (25​)=10. Its compact real form is obtained by taking real skew-symmetric matrices. For diagonal root calculations it is more convenient to use an isotropic basis v1​,v2​,v0​,v−2​,v−1​ with B(vi​,vj​)=δi,−j​, including B(v0​,v0​)=1.
A maximal torus of the complex special orthogonal group is
T={diag(t1​,t2​,1,t2−1​,t1−1​):t1​,t2​∈C×}.
(10)
Its Lie algebra is the Cartan subalgebra
h={H(h1​,h2​)=diag(h1​,h2​,0,−h2​,−h1​)}.
(11)
In the compact real form the corresponding torus consists of independent rotations in two orthogonal coordinate planes, fixing the remaining direction. Define εi​(H)=hi​ for i=1,2, and ε−i​=−εi​, ε0​=0.
Writing Eij​ for the matrix unit in the isotropic basis, Fij​=Eij​−E−j,−i​ lies in so5​ and satisfies
[H,Fij​]=(εi​−εj​)(H)Fij​.
(12)
Thus the nonzero root spaces are one-dimensional and the root-space decomposition is
so5​=h⊕α∈R⨁​gα​,R={±ε1​,±ε2​,±ε1​±ε2​}.​
(13)
Explicit generators for four positive root spaces are
αε1​−ε2​ε1​+ε2​ε1​ε2​​Xα​E12​−E−2,−1​E1,−2​−E2,−1​E10​−E0,−1​E20​−E0,−2​​​
(14)
The transposes of these matrices generate the negative root spaces. Each transpose still satisfies the bilinear-form condition and reverses the displayed diagonal weight. These eight root vectors and the two independent Cartan matrices exhaust the dimension ten; a matrix commuting with every H is diagonal and the form condition makes it lie in h. This also proves that the torus has maximal dimension.

Give the real root span an inner product with ε1​,ε2​ orthonormal. The B2 root system consists of four short roots on the coordinate axes and four long roots on the diagonals. Choose the long-first simple roots
α1​=ε1​−ε2​,α2​=ε2​.​
(15)
The positive roots are α1​,α2​,α1​+α2​=ε1​, and α1​+2α2​=ε1​+ε2​. The corresponding simple reflections are
s1​(x,y)=(y,x),s2​(x,y)=(x,−y).
(16)
They generate every signed permutation of the two coordinates. Thus the Weyl group has order eight and is the symmetry group of a square; every element sends (x,y) to (±x,±y) or (±y,±x), with independent signs.
Since α1​,α2​ form an integral basis of Z2, the root lattice is Q=Zε1​⊕Zε2​. The simple coroots are α1∨​=ε1​−ε2​ and α2∨​=2ε2​. Therefore the weight lattice is
P={(x,y):x−y∈Z, 2y∈Z}=Z2 ∪ ((Z+21​)×(Z+21​)).​
(17)
Solving ⟨Λi​,αj∨​⟩=δij​ gives the fundamental weights
Λ1​=ε1​,Λ2​=21​(ε1​+ε2​).​
(18)
The cone of dominant weights is
P+={aΛ1​+bΛ2​:a,b∈Z≥0​}={(x,y)∈P:x≥y≥0}.​
(19)
Indeed a=x−y and b=2y are exactly the two simple-coroot pairings. This describes representations of the Lie algebra, or of its simply connected Spin group; half-integral weights are allowed. For representations descending to SO(5) itself, the weight must lie in Z2, equivalently b must be even.

Every finite-dimensional irreducible Lie algebra representation has highest weight λ=aΛ1​+bΛ2​ for unique integers a,b≥0. The Weyl vector is
ρ=21​((ε1​−ε2​)+ε2​+ε1​+(ε1​+ε2​))=23​ε1​+21​ε2​,
(20)
so λ+ρ=(a+b/2+3/2,b/2+1/2). In the Weyl dimension formula, the factors for the four positive roots, ordered as ε1​−ε2​,ε2​,ε1​,ε1​+ε2​, are respectively
a+1,b+1,32a+b+3​,2a+b+2​.
(21)
Hence
dimL(aΛ1​+bΛ2​)=6(a+1)(b+1)(a+b+2)(2a+b+3)​.​
(22)
In particular the two fundamental representations have dimensions five and four. The highest root is θ=ε1​+ε2​=2Λ2​, so the Adjoint representation has dimension ten. These also fix the dimensions of the crystals below. Our numbering places the long root first; interchanging a B2/C2 numbering without interchanging the associated fundamental-weight labels would give the wrong dimension formula.

Use directed color-i edges ui​v for the Kashiwara operator f​i​, so every edge subtracts αi​ from the weight. The defining crystal of the defining odd-orthogonal representation is
ε1​1​ε2​2​02​−ε2​1​−ε1​.​
(23)
The defining module has highest weight Λ1​ and precisely these five weights, all of multiplicity one. The color-two string through zero has length two, since ⟨ε2​,α2∨​⟩=2. The other indicated strings have length one. Restriction to each simple-root sl2 Lie algebra and the root-string property of a crystal justify the edges; no other weight difference is the relevant simple root.
For the B2 spin crystal B(Λ2​), denote the four weights 21​(±ε1​±ε2​) by their sign pairs. Its graph is
++2​+−1​−+2​−−.​
(24)
The fundamental weight Λ2​ is a minuscule weight: its pairings with all positive coroots are zero or one. Its Weyl orbit has four weights, and the dimension formula gives four, so each occurs once and there are no additional weights. All simple-root strings have length one. Subtracting α1​ or α2​ where another weight is present gives exactly this graph.
For the B2 adjoint crystal, write xβ​ for the vertex of root weight β, and use two distinct zero-weight vertices z1​,z2​. There are eight root vertices and two zero vertices because the zero-weight space is the two-dimensional Cartan subalgebra. The complete lists of colored strings are
12​xε1​​→xε2​​,xα1​​→z1​→x−α1​​,x−ε2​​→x−ε1​​xε1​+ε2​​→xε1​​→xε1​−ε2​​,xε2​​→z2​→x−ε2​​,x−ε1​+ε2​​→x−ε1​​→x−ε1​−ε2​​.​
(25)
Every arrow in a given row has that row's color; the reverse arrows are the raising operators, and all unlisted lowering operations vanish. In particular the two zero vertices must not be merged.
For a constructive justification, let V=C5 with the invariant symmetric form from part(a). The map
v∧w⟼[u↦B(w,u)v−B(v,u)w]
(26)
is an equivariant isomorphism Λ2V≅so5​, identifying the highest weight as ε1​+ε2​. Compute its crystal as the highest-weight component starting at 1⊗2 in B(Λ1​)⊗B(Λ1​), with labels 1,2,0,2ˉ,1ˉ for the defining chain. The crystal tensor-product rule is
f​i​(u⊗v)={f​i​u⊗v,u⊗f​i​v,​φi​(u)>εi​(v),φi​(u)≤εi​(v),​
(27)
where εi​ counts available raising steps and φi​ counts lowering steps in the color-i string. It generates the following ten vertices:
weight/vertexε1​+ε2​ε1​α1​ε2​z1​z2​−α1​−ε2​−ε1​−ε1​−ε2​​tensor label1⊗21⊗01⊗2ˉ2⊗02⊗2ˉ0⊗02⊗1ˉ0⊗2ˉ0⊗1ˉ2ˉ⊗1ˉ​​
(28)
Applying the displayed rule to these labels gives precisely the listed strings, proving the entire diagram rather than only its weight multiplicities. Tensor labels here are crystal vertices in the combinatorial limit, not an assertion that a bare ordinary tensor such as v0​⊗v0​ lies in Λ2V.

Take the diagonal Cartan subalgebra of sl3​ and let εi​ select its ith diagonal entry, so ε1​+ε2​+ε3​=0 on it. Use simple roots α1​=ε1​−ε2​, α2​=ε2​−ε3​. The fundamental weights are ω1​=ε1​ and ω2​=−ε3​=ε1​+ε2​. The defining representation V has highest vector e1​ and the dual has highest vector e3∗​. Thus
hw(V)=ω1​,hw(V∗)=ω2​.​
(29)
Their crystal basis diagrams are B:11​22​3 and B∨:3ˉ2​2ˉ1​1ˉ, with weights εi​ and −εi​ respectively. Use the same crystal tensor-product rule as in2(d): lowering acts on the first factor when φi​(u)>εi​(v); otherwise on the second. Raising uses the first factor when φi​(u)≥εi​(v), so it reverses each lowering arrow. An edge is absent if the selected factor operation is zero.
For B⊗B, abbreviate i⊗j by ij. The complete color-one edge list is 11→21→22, 31→32, 13→23; the complete color-two list is 21→31, 22→32→33, 12→13. There are two connected components:

Assume 0≤k≤n. Let V=Ln−1​ be the n-dimensional irreducible sl2 Lie algebra representation. Choose a weight basis v0​,…,vn−1​ with weights 2j−(n−1); this lists the usual irreducible weight string in increasing order. Its exterior power ΛkV has basis vj1​​∧⋯∧vjk​​ for 0≤j1​<⋯<jk​<n, so its formal character is
chΛkV=∑j1​<⋯<jk​​q2(j1​+⋯+jk​)−k(n−1)=q−N∑j1​<⋯<jk​​(q2)j1​+⋯+jk​−k(k−1)/2,N=k(n−k).
(33)
The last sum is the Gaussian binomial coefficient Gn,k​(q2). To verify the product identity, splitting the subsets according to whether they contain n−1 gives
Gn,k​(t)=Gn−1,k​(t)+tn−kGn−1,k−1​(t),Gn,0​=Gn,n​=1.
(34)
The product ∏j=1k​(1−tn−k+j)/(1−tj) satisfies the same recurrence, after putting both terms over their common denominator. Thus induction identifies it with the subset sum, proving it is a polynomial with nonnegative integer coefficients.
The quantum integer convention in the PDF has
[m]q​=q−q−1qm−q−m​=q1−m1−q21−q2m​.
(35)
Multiplying these factors in the factorial quotient gives
[nk​]q​=q−NGn,k​(q2)=chΛkLn−1​.​
(36)
This establishes the required representation-theoretic interpretation.
By the Weyl complete reducibility theorem, write ΛkV≅⨁m≥0​rm​Lm​, where rm​ are nonnegative integers. The classification of finite-dimensional sl2 representations gives
chLm​=qm+qm−2+⋯+q−m.
(37)
Every exterior-power weight has parity N and lies between −N and N. Therefore only m≤N with m≡N(mod2) occur. For j≥0 with that parity, the coefficient cj​ of qj is
cj​=∑m≥jm≡N (2)​​rm​,cj​=c−j​,cj​−cj+2​=rj​≥0.
(38)
The coefficients consequently increase toward the middle of the occupied weight string and decrease afterward. If Gn,k​(t)=∑s=0N​as​ts, then as​=c−N+2s​. Hence
a0​≤a1​≤⋯≤a⌊N/2⌋​,as​=aN−s​.​
(39)
This is the unimodality of Gaussian binomial coefficients, proved through parity unimodality of an sl2 character. It includes k=0,n, when the character is one.
There is a normalization qualification in the printed wording. The symmetric quantum-binomial quotient is a Laurent polynomial in q, with exponents spaced by two; the unimodality just proved is for that occupied parity, or equivalently for the ordinary polynomial Gn,k​(t) after setting t=q2 and removing the monomial shift. If one instead includes every integer exponent of q, the literal coefficient claim is false: [21​]q​=q+q−1 has coefficients 1,0,1. The representation-theoretic conclusion is unimodality on the parity string, and ordinary unimodality of the Gaussian polynomial.
