Far downslope let the typical height be H(X) and the cross-slope half-width be W(X). Slenderness gives W≪X and slowly varying longitudinal thickness. In a steady porous gravity current, the longitudinal spreading term is smaller than advection by the ratio H/X, while longitudinal diffusion relative to transverse diffusion is of order (W/X)2. Thus the leading balance is
∂X​hβ=∂Y​(hβhY​).
(52)
Integrating across the finite current, with zero flux through its dry side edges, makes the leading downslope volume flux constant:
∫hβdY=constant,HβW∼constant.
(53)
Balancing the two differential terms gives
XHβ​∼W2Hβ+1​,W2∼HX.
(54)
Eliminate H∝W−1/β to find the far-downslope width of a constant-flux porous current:
YN​(X)∝Xγ,γ=2β+1β​.​
(55)
The typical height is correspondingly H∝X−1/(2β+1). For every β>0, the width exponent is less than one half, so W/X→0 and H/X→0, consistent with the discarded longitudinal terms. The porosity exponent α drops out because it enters storage, which has zero time derivative in this steady problem.
