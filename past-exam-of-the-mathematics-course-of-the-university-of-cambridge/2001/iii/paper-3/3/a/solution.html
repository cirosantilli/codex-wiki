 ... Past exam of the mathematics course of the University of Cambridge 2001 iii Paper 3 3 a
OurBigBook.com Words: 367
An group automorphism preserves element orders and sizes of conjugacy classes. A nonidentity involution in Sn​ has cycle type 2k1n−2k and class size
2kk!(n−2k)!n!​,1≤k≤⌊n/2⌋.
(6)
For its class to have the same size as the transposition class, it must satisfy
(n−2k)!(n−2)!​=2k−1k!.
(7)
For fixed k≥2 the left side grows strictly with n. When k=2, it is (n−2)(n−3), which is two at n=4 and at least six at n=5, never the required four. For k=3 equality first occurs at n=6, since 4!=22⋅3!, and thereafter the left side is larger. For k≥4 it is already larger at n=2k: the ratio (2k−2)!/[2k−1k!] is 15/4 at k=4, and increases with k, its successive ratio being k(2k−1)/(k+1)>1. This proves the symmetric-group involution class-size collision result. Therefore, for n=6, every group automorphism maps transpositions to transpositions.
Now recover the underlying points from transpositions. Two distinct transpositions fail to commute exactly when their two-element supports meet. A maximal family of pairwise intersecting edges of a complete graph is either a vertex star or a triangle. Indeed, after selecting edges {1,2} and {1,3}, every edge either contains 1 or is {2,3}; if the latter occurs, all edges lie in that triangle. For n≥5, the stars have size n−1≥4 and triangles size three, so stars are recognized purely by the multiplication structure of Sn​.
An group automorphism must thus permute the stars, giving a permutation τ of the points. The unique transposition shared by stars i,j is (i j), so its image is (τ(i) τ(j)). Since transpositions generate Sn​, the whole group automorphism is conjugation by τ.
At n=4, stars and triangles both have three elements, but their generated subgroups distinguish them: the transpositions in a star generate all of S4​ (since (i j)=(a i)(a j)(a i) for its center a), while a triangle generates the S3​ fixing the fourth point. The same reconstruction follows. At n=3, conjugation by S3​ induces every permutation of its three transpositions (a transposition swaps the other two, and a three-cycle rotates all three), so a transposition-preserving group automorphism is again inner. At n=2 the only group automorphism of C2​ is the identity, and n=0,1 are trivial (if the empty-set degree is included). Thus
Out(Sn​)=1for n=6.​
(8)
The small-degree adjustments are necessary: a cardinality-only star argument would not cover n=4.
