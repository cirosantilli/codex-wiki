The orbits of a normal subgroup form a block system: g(Nω)=N(gω), so every group element permutes these orbits. By primitivity the orbits are either all singletons or one whole orbit. In the singleton case N fixes every point, giving N≤G[Ω]​.
In the other case N is transitive. For each g∈G choose n∈N with nα=gα. Then n−1g∈Gα​, so G=NGα​. Work in the quotient G/N. If g=nh with h∈Gα​, the image of Ag=g−1Ag is the image of Ah, which equals the image A of A because A◃Gα​. All conjugates of A thus have the same image in G/N.
By hypothesis those conjugates generate G, so the quotient is generated by the single subgroup A, and equals it. Since A is abelian, G/N is abelian. The kernel of a group homomorphism of a group homomorphism to an abelian group contains the commutator subgroup, yielding
G′≤NorN≤G[Ω]​.​
(17)
This is the normal-subgroup argument behind the Iwasawa simplicity lemma. Faithfulness is not needed for this version: retaining the pointwise kernel of a group homomorphism as the second alternative is essential. In a faithful primitive group action it says every nontrivial normal subgroup contains G′.
