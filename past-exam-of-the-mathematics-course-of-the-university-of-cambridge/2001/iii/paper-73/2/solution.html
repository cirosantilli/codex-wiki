For fixed routing, let each route r require one unit on every resource in its fixed set, let Cj​≥1 be integer resource capacities, and let αr​ be the offered load, arrival rate times mean holding time. The Erlang fixed point approximation treats distinct resource availability events as independent. If Bj​ is the approximate blocking probability at resource j, the reduced-load approximation gives
aj​(B)=∑r:j∈r​αr​∏k∈r∖{j}​(1−Bk​),Bj​=E(aj​(B),Cj​),
(8)
where the Erlang loss formula is
E(a,C)=∑k=0C​ak/k!aC/C!​.
(9)
Screening excludes resource j itself: it is the load offered to that resource, not merely the load ultimately admitted there. Approximate route acceptance is ∏j∈r​(1−Bj​). This specifies the approximation, not an assertion of exact independence in the actual loss network.
Here is a constructive variational proof of both existence and uniqueness. For C≥1 write eC​(a)=E(a,C) and mC​(a)=a[1−eC​(a)], the carried load of an Erlang loss resource. Under its upper-truncated Poisson occupancy law, differentiating the finite sums gives
mC′​(a)=aVara​(K)​>0,eC′​(a)=aeC​(a)​[C−mC​(a)]>0(a>0).
(10)
Consequently eC​ increases continuously from 0 to 1, and mC​ increases from 0 to C. Put yj​=−log(1−Bj​) and
gj​(y)=e−yeCj​−1​(1−e−y)=mCj​​(eCj​−1​(1−e−y)),gj​(0)=0.
(11)
Each gj​ is continuous and strictly increasing, tending to Cj​. Consider the convex potential for the Erlang fixed point
F(y)=∑r​αr​exp(−∑j∈r​yj​)+∑j​∫0yj​​gj​(z)dz,y≥0.
(12)
The first sum is convex, and each integral is strictly convex because its derivative gj​ strictly increases. Hence F is strictly convex. Also each integral grows at least linearly for sufficiently large yj​, since gj​(yj​)→Cj​>0. Thus F is coercive, its bounded sublevel sets are compact, and it attains a unique minimum y∗.
Its derivative is
∂j​F=gj​(yj​)−∑r:j∈r​αr​exp(−∑k∈r​yk​)=gj​(yj​)−e−yj​aj​(B).
(13)
For a resource used by some positive-load route, this derivative is negative at yj​=0 for every finite choice of the other coordinates, so its minimizing coordinate is positive and satisfies ∂j​F=0. Cancelling e−yj​ gives eCj​−1​(Bj​)=aj​(B), exactly the required fixed-point equation. An unused resource minimizes its integral at yj​=0, giving Bj​=0=E(0,Cj​). Conversely any fixed point has Bj​<1, since the total offered load is finite; its logarithmic coordinates satisfy these same minimizing conditions. Strict convexity therefore proves one and only one fixed-routing Erlang fixed point. Zero-capacity resources can be removed with their blocked routes, whose acceptance probabilities are already zero.
For a concrete alternative routing counterexample, take three nodes forming a triangle, each link of capacity 1000, with offered traffic 950 for each endpoint pair. Try the direct link first; if it is blocked, try the other two links together. In a symmetric reduced-load approximation, a link has its direct load plus two overflow streams, each of load 950B(1−B): the preferred link is blocked with probability B, and the other link of the alternative path is available with probability 1−B. The link itself is excluded from screening. Thus a common link blocking probability must satisfy
B=E(950[1+2B(1−B)],1000).
(14)
Define the right side minus B as G(B). The stable Erlang loss formula recursion b0​=1, bk​=abk−1​/(k+abk−1​) gives
G(0)G(0.1)​≃0.0036493>0,≃0.0144598>0,​G(0.01)G(0.4)​≃−0.0009231<0,≃−0.1095146<0.​
(15)
All four arguments are rational, so the same recursion can certify these signs with rational arithmetic, without trusting rounded values. Continuity and the intermediate value theorem give at least three distinct symmetric fixed points, one in each intervening interval. This is nonuniqueness of the approximation; the exact finite irreducible loss network still has a unique stationary distribution.
