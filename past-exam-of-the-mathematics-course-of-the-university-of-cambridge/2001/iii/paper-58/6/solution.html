The Weierstrass approximation theorem asserts that every real continuous function on a compact interval is a uniform limit of polynomials. An affine change of variable reduces the interval to [0,1]. There are two complementary routes: construct positive approximation operators, or construct an algebra dense enough to approximate absolute values and hence pointwise maxima and minima.
For the first route, the Korovkin theorem reduces convergence of positive linear operators on continuous functions Ln​ to the three tests 1,t,t2. Here is its proof. Given f∈C[0,1] and ε>0, uniform continuity supplies a δ>0 such that ∣f(t)−f(x)∣<ε for ∣t−x∣<δ. For all t,x one then has
∣f(t)−f(x)∣≤ε+M(t−x)2,M=2∥f∥∞​/δ2.

The same criterion gives the Schoenberg spline operator convergence in Solution 3. For periodic functions its counterpart uses 1,cost,sint: the nonnegative barrier 1−cos(t−x) is bounded away from zero outside any fixed small arc. This periodic Korovkin test set gives convergence of the normalized Fejér sums and density of trigonometric polynomials.
The Lebesgue convergence criterion for approximation operators addresses stability. Suppose bounded linear operators Ln​:C[a,b]→C[a,b] converge to the identity on every polynomial. Then they converge on every continuous function if and only if supn​∥Ln​∥<∞. Sufficiency is the extension of approximation operators from a dense subspace: for a polynomial p with ∥f−p∥<η and a common bound M,
∥Ln​f−f∥≤(M+1)η+∥Ln​p−p∥.

First let n→∞, then η↓0. Necessity follows from the Uniform boundedness principle, since a convergent Ln​f is bounded for every f. Applied to approximation projectors, the Lebesgue inequality for approximation projectors is
∥f−Pn​f∥∞​≤(1+∥Pn​∥)EVn​​(f),EVn​​(f)=infv∈Vn​​∥f−v∥∞​.

There is also a kernel version of this stability criterion, useful for trigonometric polynomial approximation. If integrable periodic kernels Kn​ have uniformly bounded L1 norms, their integrals tend to one, and ∫∣t∣≥δ​∣Kn​(t)∣dt→0 for every δ>0, then convolution with Kn​ converges uniformly to every continuous periodic function. Subtract f(x)∫Kn​ and split the error into a small arc, bounded by ω(f,δ)supn​∥Kn​∥1​, and its complement, bounded by 2∥f∥∞​∫∣t∣≥δ​∣Kn​∣. The remaining mass error tends to zero. This approximate identity argument shows directly why nonnegative unit-mass Fejér kernels work even though ordinary Fourier partial sums need not converge uniformly for every continuous target.

Approximating f first by L and then by a polynomial is another complete proof of the Weierstrass approximation theorem.
The Stone-Weierstrass theorem generalizes from intervals to a compact Hausdorff space K: a real subalgebra A⊆C(K,R) containing the constants and separating points is uniformly dense. Its proof uses the same absolute-value mechanism. The uniform closure of a function algebra is an algebra. If a belongs to this closure, approximation of ∣t∣ by polynomials on a bounded interval containing its range proves that ∣a∣ also belongs to the closure. Therefore the closure contains
max(a,b)=2a+b+∣a−b∣​,min(a,b)=2a+b−∣a−b∣​.
(32)
Point separation and constants allow exact interpolation of any two real values: choose h∈A with h(s)=h(t) and solve for α,β in αh+β. The lattice approximation from two-point approximation proved in Solution 4 now makes the closure all of C(K,R).
The complex version requires a unital, point-separating subalgebra closed under complex conjugation. Its real-valued members separate points using real or imaginary parts, so the real result approximates both parts of every complex target. Conjugation closure is essential: polynomials in z alone on the closed unit disk cannot approximate z uniformly, because ∫∣z∣=1​p(z)dz=0 whereas ∫∣z∣=1​zdz=2πi.
Among the corollaries are density of multivariate polynomials on compact subsets of real Euclidean space, since coordinates separate points, and density of trigonometric polynomials on the circle, since sine and cosine separate points there. On a compact interval, polynomials with rational coefficients are a countable dense family, giving separability of C[a,b]. The real and complex approximation results above concern uniform approximation of continuous targets; neither asserts convergence of arbitrary interpolation schemes or arbitrary Fourier series.
