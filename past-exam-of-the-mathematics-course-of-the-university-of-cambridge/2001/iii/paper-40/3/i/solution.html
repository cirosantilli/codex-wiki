Use gravitational acceleration g=∇ψ. Through the plane with upward unit normal, the signed gravitational flux per unit area of the point mass is
gz​(R,0)=∂z​ψ∣z=0​=−(R2+b2)3/2Gmb​.
(21)
Thus its downward flux magnitude is Gmb/(R2+b2)3/2. Subtracting a constant from the gravitational potential does not change this flux.
The Kuzmin reflection method gives the even relative potential ψ=Gm/R2+(∣z∣+b)2​. It is harmonic above and below the plane because the corresponding point masses lie outside the respective half-spaces. At the plane the derivative jump is
∂z​ψ(R,0+)−∂z​ψ(R,0−)=−(R2+b2)3/22Gmb​.
(22)
Integrating the Poisson equation for Newtonian gravity ∇2ψ=−4πGΣ(R)δ(z) through a thin layer, with δ the Dirac delta, equates this jump to −4πGΣ. Therefore
Σ(R)=2π(R2+b2)3/2mb​.​
(23)
This is a Kuzmin disk. As a check, its total mass is 2π∫0∞​RΣ(R)dR=m.
Replace the point mass by dm=μ(b)db and superpose the Kuzmin disks. Linearity of the Poisson equation for Newtonian gravity gives, whenever the force integral converges,
Σ(R)=2π1​∫0∞​(R2+b2)3/2bμ(b)​db.​
(24)
Both choices of potential reference give this same surface density. If the unreferenced potential diverges, perform the reference subtraction in each component before taking the integral limit; the force and the surface density may still be finite.
