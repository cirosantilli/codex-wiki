If η decreases outwards on an interval, then it increases as a surface ray travels inward through that interval. Such a ray cannot turn there, since turning requires the decreasing inward value of η to reach p. Rays that penetrate this interval can traverse it and turn deeper, but the map η↦r now has several branches. The angular data constrain combinations of those branch contributions, rather than the single function h(η) used above. This is a hidden turning-ray zone from nonmonotone spherical slowness. The stated single-valued inversion fails and does not uniquely locate the speed profile in that interval without additional information. Missing turning rays and possible multiple travel-time branches are the obstruction, not a failure of the algebraic Abel-kernel identity.
