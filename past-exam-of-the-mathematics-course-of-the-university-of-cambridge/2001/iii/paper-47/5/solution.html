Write n=(x−ξ)/R for the receiver direction. With the printed time convention eiωt, an outgoing spherical wave has phase e−iωR/v. The leading terms of the elastodynamic Green tensor are of order 1/(α2R) and 1/(β2R), while the remaining terms have orders 1/(ωvR2) and 1/(ω2R3). Thus the far-field approximation requires
ωR/α≫1,ωR/β≫1.​
(41)
This is an absolute asymptotic comparison of the tensor terms. At an angular node of a leading radiation component, the smaller near field can still dominate that component. The PDF prints an opposite phase sign in the near-field shear exponential; that would be incoming with this time convention. The outgoing leading terms, which determine all the requested far-field patterns, are unambiguous.
For a unit time-harmonic point force in direction d, the far-field P and S radiation from a point force is
uP​=4πα2Re−iωR/α​n(n⋅d),uS​=4πβ2Re−iωR/β​{d−n(n⋅d)}.
(42)
The normalization here follows the given elastodynamic Green tensor; a separate force-density convention would include its corresponding density factor. If θ is the angle between n and d, the signed radial P wave amplitude is cosθ, and the magnitude of the S wave amplitude is sinθ. The P wave has two oppositely signed lobes and a nodal plane perpendicular to d. The S wave is polarized in the projection of d onto the receiver's tangent plane, has nodes along ±d, and has its maximum on the perpendicular great circle. Rotating its planar section about d gives the full toroidal S wave magnitude pattern.
For a point source with seismic moment tensor M, convolution with the derivatives of the Dirac delta function gives ui​=Mjk​∂ξk​​Gij​. In the far field, differentiating the outgoing phase contributes iωnk​/v; differentiating R−1 or n is a lower-order effect. Thus the point-moment elastodynamic displacement is
uP​=4πα3Riωe−iωR/α​n(nTMn),uS​=4πβ3Riωe−iωR/β​(I−nnT)Mn.​
(43)
The double couple has M=e1​e2T​+e2​e1T​. Its double-couple radiation pattern therefore has signed radial P wave factor and tangential S wave vector
AP​=2n1​n2​,AS​=(n2​,n1​,0)−2n1​n2​n,∣AS​∣2=n12​+n22​−4n12​n22​.
(44)
In the x1​x2​ plane, n=(cosϕ,sinϕ,0), giving AP​=sin2ϕ and AS​=cos2ϕ, where the signed S wave component is along (−sinϕ,cosϕ,0). The P wave nodal planes are n1​=0 and n2​=0, and the alternating lobes peak on their bisectors. The planar S wave lobes peak along the coordinate axes. For the complete three-dimensional pattern, write n=(sinθcosϕ,sinθsinϕ,cosθ):
AP​=sin2θsin2ϕ,ASV​=sinθcosθsin2ϕ,ASH​=sinθcos2ϕ.
(45)
The full S wave has additional zeros at ±e3​ as well as at the planar bisectors.
For the explosion source, M=I. Then nTMn=1 and (I−nnT)Mn=0. The explosion radiation in an isotropic elastic solid is therefore an isotropic radial P wave, with no S wave radiation. A dilatational point source is a gradient source; its far-field force is parallel to the propagation direction, so the transverse projection vanishes.
Each radius in the sketch is the magnitude of the angular factor, separately normalized for each mode. Blue and orange distinguish signs of the indicated radial or tangential component. The point-force sections contain its axis; the double-couple sections are in the x1​x2​ plane. The formulas above specify the full three-dimensional patterns.
