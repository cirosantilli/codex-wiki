Only L⇒A remains. Assume U preserves small categorical limits, and choose a small cogenerating family (Qi​)i∈I​ in C. Fix a set X. The comma category (X↓U) is a complete category: underlying limits in C, together with limit preservation by U, lift compatible maps from X. It is locally small, since its arrows form subsets of the hom-sets in C.
We construct a weakly initial set in this comma category. Given f:X→U(C), intersect all subobjects m:B↪C through which f lifts under U. There are only a set of subobjects because C is well-powered; the family includes 1C​. Their intersection exists by completeness. Also U preserves monomorphisms, since a map is monic precisely when its self-pullback diagonal is invertible, and U preserves such pullbacks.
Thus all lifts of f are unique and compatible, and preservation of the intersection gives
f=U(m0​)f0​,m0​:C0​↪C.
(53)
No proper subobject of C0​ supports a lift of f0​: its composite into C would belong to the intersected family, forcing it to contain C0​. This is a minimal supported subobject.
For each i, the map
C(C0​,Qi​)⟶Set(X,UQi​),h⟼U(h)f0​
(54)
is injective. Indeed, equal images mean f0​ lifts to the equalizer of the two maps, because U preserves that equalizer. Minimality forces the equalizer to be invertible, so the maps agree.
The cogenerator property makes the evaluation map
C0​⟶∏i∈I, h:C0​→Qi​​Qi​
(55)
a monomorphism: any two arrows into C0​ that agree after all these projections are equal. The indexing pairs (i,h) inject into the fixed set
BX​=∐i∈I​Set(X,UQi​).
(56)
Their image is some subset J⊆BX​. Consequently C0​ is isomorphic to a subobject of a product PJ​ of the Qi​ indexed by J. There are only a set of such subsets J, only a set of subobjects of each PJ​, and only a set of maps X→U(B) for each representative subobject B.
Collect all these pairs (B,x:X→U(B)). Every (C,f) receives a comma arrow from one of them, via the isomorphic copy of (C0​,f0​) and m0​. Hence they form a weakly initial set. The initial-object lemma for complete categories with a weakly initial set, proved explicitly in Question 8, gives an initial object of (X↓U). Its map from X is a universal arrow from an object to a functor. Choosing these universal arrows for all X defines a left adjoint to U.
Thus
A⟺R⟺L.​
(57)
The products indexed by realized subsets J are important: one cannot assume that C0​ has a map to every cogenerator and simply add arbitrary missing coordinates.
