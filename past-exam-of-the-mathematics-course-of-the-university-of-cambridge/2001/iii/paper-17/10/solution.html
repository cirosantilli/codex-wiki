A form of Beck's monadicity theorem compatible with equivalence-based monadic adjunctions is this. A functor U:D→C is monadic exactly when it has a left adjoint, is a conservative functor, and D has coequalizers of all pairs whose images under U admit a split coequalizer, with U preserving these coequalizers. In the strict lifting formulation, one states creation of these coequalizers. Such a pair is a functor-split coequalizer pair; the splitting is required in the base category, not necessarily upstairs.
A split coequalizer fork Ar​s​​Bq​Q has maps u:B→A and t:Q→B such that
qr=qs,qt=1Q​,ru=1B​,su=tq.
(77)
Every functor preserves this coequalizer: if hr=hs, then h=hru=hsu=htq, and q is split epic, so the factor through q is unique. The same equations remain true after applying any functor.
For necessity, let UT:CT→C be the monad algebra forgetful functor. It has the free-algebra left adjoint. It reflects isomorphisms, because if an algebra map f is invertible in C, the equation fa=bTf rearranges to f−1b=aT(f−1).
If two algebra maps r,s:(A,a)⇉(B,b) have an underlying split coequalizer q:B→Q, then Tq and T2q are also coequalizers. Since qb equalizes Tr,Ts, there is a unique c:TQ→Q satisfying
cTq=qb.
(78)
After composing with the epimorphism q, the equation cηQ​=1Q​ reduces to the unit law for b. After composing with the epimorphism T2q, the equation cT(c)=cμQ​ reduces to bT(b)=bμB​. Thus (Q,c) is an algebra and q an algebra map. If an algebra map h:(B,b)→(Z,z) equalizes r,s, its unique underlying factor k:Q→Z satisfies kc=zTk: this follows by precomposing with the epimorphism Tq. Therefore this is a coequalizer upstairs as well. This proves that the monad algebra forgetful functor creates split coequalizers. The same properties transport through the comparison equivalence of categories, with the object-lifting convention distinguished in Question 9.
For sufficiency, suppose the three conditions hold, choose F⊣U, and let T=UF with comparison K. For each algebra (X,a), consider the pair
FTXF(a)​εFX​​​FX.
(79)
Its image has coequalizer a:TX→X. It is split: take r=μX​, s=T(a), q=a, u=ηTX​ and t=ηX​. The monad and algebra laws give
aμX​=aT(a),aηX​=1X​,μX​ηTX​=1TX​,T(a)ηTX​=ηX​a.
(80)
By hypothesis the pair has a coequalizer q:FX→DX​ and Uq is also a coequalizer. Identify UDX​ with X using the unique coequalizer isomorphism, so Uq=a. Since q is carried by K to an algebra map from the free algebra, the transported action b:TX→X satisfies
bT(a)=aμX​=aT(a).
(81)
But T(a) is a split epimorphism, with right inverse T(ηX​), so b=a. Hence (X,a)≅K(DX​): the comparison is essentially surjective.
Next, for every D∈D, put aD​=UεD​. The counit of an adjunction coequalizes
FTUDF(aD​)​εFUD​​​FUD.
(82)
This follows from naturality of the counit. The image pair has the split coequalizer aD​, as above. If q:FUD→Q is the hypothesized preserved coequalizer, the induced map h:Q→D has Uh invertible because both Uq and aD​ coequalize the same pair. Conservativity makes h invertible. Therefore εD​ itself is a coequalizer.
Let f:K(D)→K(E) be an algebra map, so faD​=aE​Tf. Set g=εE​F(f):FUD→E. Under the adjunction transpose bijection, the two composites of g with the displayed presentation pair correspond respectively to faD​ and aE​Tf. They are equal, so g factors uniquely through εD​, giving h:D→E with hεD​=g. Applying U yields
UhaD​=aE​Tf=faD​.
(83)
Since aD​ is split epic, Uh=f. Thus K is full. If Uh=Uk, the transposes of hεD​ and kεD​ are equal, so those composites agree. The counit is epic, hence h=k; thus K is faithful.
The comparison is full, faithful and essentially surjective, so it is an equivalence of categories. This proves monadicity. The argument also explains the role of each hypothesis: split base presentations construct all algebras, and reflection of isomorphisms turns the counit presentations into actual coequalizers upstairs.
