The general adjoint functor theorem says that, for a functor U:C→D with C a complete category and both categories locally small, U has a left adjoint exactly when it preserves small categorical limits and satisfies the solution-set condition. The latter means that for each D∈D there is a set of arrows fi​:D→U(Ci​) such that every f:D→U(C) factors as
f=U(h)fi​for some i and h:Ci​→C.
(58)
Equivalently, (D↓U) has a weakly initial set.
The limit form of the special adjoint functor theorem says that, if in addition C is well-powered and has a small cogenerating family, then U has a left adjoint exactly when it preserves small limits. These are the limit/left-adjoint forms; their duals interchange limits with colimits, left with right adjoints, and cogenerators with generators.
For the first route, let E be a complete category that is locally small and has a weakly initial set (Wi​). Put P=∏i​Wi​. It is weakly initial: for any X, choose an arrow Wi​→X and precompose with the corresponding product projection. The set E(P,P) indexes a simultaneous equalizer
e:E⟶P,ue=efor every u:P→P.
(59)
It exists by completeness, for example as the equalizer of the two maps P⇉∏u:P→P​P with components u and 1P​. Since E→P→X exists for each X, E is also weakly initial.
Choose r:P→E. Applying the defining equation to er:P→P gives ere=e, whence re=1E​ because e is a monomorphism. If v:E→E is any endomorphism, apply it to evr:P→P to obtain evre=e, hence ev=e and v=1E​. Thus E has only its identity endomorphism.
For two arrows a,b:E⇉X, take their equalizer j:Y→E. Weak initiality of E gives t:E→Y. Since jt is an endomorphism of E, jt=1E​. The monic arrow j with this right inverse is invertible; therefore a=b. Existence of an arrow E→X and this uniqueness show that E is initial.
For the general adjoint functor theorem, limit preservation makes each comma category (D↓U) complete, and the solution-set condition makes it weakly initially generated by a set. The lemma provides an initial object (FD,ηD​). For v:D→D′, the universal property uniquely defines Fv:FD→FD′ by
U(Fv)ηD​=ηD′​v.
(60)
Uniqueness gives the functor laws. The same property gives natural bijections
C(FD,C)≅D(D,UC),
(61)
so F⊣U. Conversely a right adjoint preserves limits, by applying its hom-set bijections to cones, and its unit (FD,ηD​) is already an initial object of each comma category, hence a singleton solution set. This proves necessity as well as sufficiency.
For the second route, assume the general adjoint functor theorem and the additional hypotheses of the Special adjoint functor theorem. Let (Qi​)i∈I​ be a small cogenerating family. Fix D∈D and an arrow f:D→UC. A limit-preserving functor preserves monomorphisms, by the self-pullback criterion. Intersect the set of all subobjects B↪C supporting f. Preservation of this categorical limit gives a minimal supporting pair (C0​,f0​:D→UC0​) and a monomorphism m0​:C0​→C with U(m0​)f0​=f.
If g,h:C0​⇉Qi​ have Ugf0​=Uhf0​, their equalizer supports f0​ and must be invertible by minimality. Thus
C(C0​,Qi​)↪D(D,UQi​),h⟼Uhf0​.
(62)
The evaluation map into the product of all these cogenerator targets is monic, because the family coseparates arrows. Its coordinates inject into the fixed set
BD​=∐i∈I​D(D,UQi​).
(63)
For every subset J⊆BD​, form the product PJ​ whose coordinate (i,z) has target Qi​. Each minimal C0​ is isomorphic to a subobject of one such product, using precisely its realized coordinates. There is a set of subsets J; the well-powered category condition gives a set of representative subobjects of each product. For each representative B, include every arrow D→UB, a set by local smallness of D.
This is a solution-set condition: the minimal pair is isomorphic to one of these representatives, and its inclusion into C supplies the required factorization of f. The general adjoint functor theorem now gives the left adjoint. This is the cogenerator bound for comma-category solution sets proof of the special theorem. Necessity follows again because a right adjoint preserves limits. Both alternative proof routes are therefore supplied.
