 ... Past exam of the mathematics course of the University of Cambridge 2001 iii Paper 17 2 iii
OurBigBook.com Words: 161
Use the preservation obstructions to extending an adjoint chain: a functor having a further left adjoint must preserve categorical limits, while one having a further right adjoint must preserve colimits.
At the left end of the presheaf category chain, Λ does not preserve the terminal object. The terminal categorical presheaf has singleton value everywhere, whereas (Λ1)(S)=∅ because S=∅. Thus Λ cannot itself be a right adjoint. At the right end, ∇ does not preserve the initial object: (∇∅)(∅)={∗} since the empty open set is proper. Thus ∇ cannot itself be a left adjoint.
For the category of small categories chain, take two functors from the terminal category 1 to the two-object indiscrete category I{0,1}, selecting its distinct objects. Their equalizer is the empty category. But their images under C=π0​ are the same function between singleton sets, whose equalizer is a singleton. Hence C does not preserve equalizers and has no further left adjoint. Finally, I1⨿I1 is the two-object discrete category, while I(1⨿1) has arrows between its distinct objects. The canonical comparison is not an isomorphism of categories, so I fails to preserve this coproduct and has no further right adjoint.
Neither chain extends in either direction.
