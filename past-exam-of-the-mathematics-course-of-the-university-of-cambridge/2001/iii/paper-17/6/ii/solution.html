A Cartesian closed category has finite products in a category and, for all X,Y, an exponential object YX with a natural bijection
E(Z,YX)≅E(Z×X,Y).
(38)
Equivalently the functor −×X has a right adjoint. Its counit of an adjunction is the evaluation map of an exponential object YX×X→Y.
For the presheaf category, define the exponential in a presheaf category by
(YX)(C)=Nat(hC​×X,Y).​
(39)
For f:D→C, its restriction sends α to α∘(hf​×1X​). Composition of these restriction maps follows from composition of the hf​, so this is a categorical presheaf. Define evaluation map of an exponential object by
eC​(α,x)=αC​(1C​,x).
(40)
For f:D→C, naturality of α gives
Y(f)αC​(1C​,x)=αD​(f,X(f)x)=eD​((YX)(f)α,X(f)x).
(41)
Thus e is a natural transformation.
Given a natural transformation β:Z×X→Y, define its currying by
β​C​(z)D​(g,x)=βD​(Z(g)z,x),g:D→C,x∈X(D).
(42)
Naturality of β proves that β​C​(z) is a natural transformation hC​×X→Y. For f:C′→C, the same formula proves that β​:Z→YX is natural. Evaluating at g=1C​ gives e(β​×1X​)=β.
Conversely, if γ:Z→YX and β=e(γ×1X​), then
β​C​(z)D​(g,x)=γD​(Z(g)z)D​(1D​,x)=γC​(z)D​(g,x),
(43)
by naturality of γ. Hence the two constructions are inverse and natural in Z,Y. Products are available pointwise, including the terminal singleton presheaf, so the presheaf category is Cartesian closed. The exponential uses natural transformations out of hC​×X; simply exponentiating the individual values generally does not give this object.
