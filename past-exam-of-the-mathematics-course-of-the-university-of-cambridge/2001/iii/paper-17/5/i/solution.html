Let D:J→[C,S] be a small diagram in a category. Since S is a complete category, choose at each C∈C a categorical limit
L(C)=limj∈J​Dj​(C),pj,C​:L(C)→Dj​(C).
(31)
For f:C→C′, the arrows Dj​(f)pj,C​ form a cone over a diagram at C′, because the diagram arrows Dj​→Dk​ are natural transformations. There is therefore a unique arrow L(f):L(C)→L(C′) with
pj,C′​L(f)=Dj​(f)pj,C​.
(32)
The universal property immediately gives L(1C​)=1L(C)​ and L(gf)=L(g)L(f): both sides have the same composites with every limit projection. Thus L is a functor, and the displayed equations make the pj​ natural transformations.
Given any categorical cone qj​:X→Dj​ in the functor category, its components induce unique qC​:X(C)→L(C). For f:C→C′, compare L(f)qC​ and qC′​X(f) after each pj,C′​; naturality of qj​ makes their composites equal. Hence q is a natural transformation, and uniqueness is componentwise. This proves that L is a categorical limit in [C,S].
The functor category is complete, and every evaluation functor preserves limits, since evaluating the constructed limit at C gives exactly the chosen limit in S. This is the pointwise limits in a functor category construction; it also covers the empty diagram and its terminal object.
