The reduced denominator of k!a/q is between q/k! and q. Its approximation error is bounded by a constant depending on k times the inverse square of that denominator. For k≥2, the following precise cardinality-controlled cyclic Freiman model suffices. If A⊆Z is nonempty, ∣A∣=n and D=kA−kA has ∣D∣≤Cn, there is A′⊆A with
∣A′∣≥k+1n​,A′≅k​B⊆ZN​,N=4∣D∣+1≤4Cn+1.​

Here ≅k​ means a bijection preserving and reflecting all equalities of k-term sums, a Freiman s-isomorphism of order s=k. The same construction works for k=1 if only injectivity is needed.
Choose an auxiliary prime P larger than N and every nonzero absolute value in D, and large enough to embed the finite set A injectively modulo P. Multiply its residues by a uniformly chosen nonzero residue u. For a fixed d∈D∖{0}, ud is uniform among the P−1 nonzero residues. The forbidden residues are
F={tN(modP):0<∣tN∣<P},
(30)
of number at most 2(P−1)/N. The union bound says that the probability some nonzero d∈D enters F is at most 2(∣D∣−1)/N<1. Fix a multiplier avoiding every forbidden event.
Let a∈{0,…,P−1} represent ua(modP). Partition that range into k+1 half-open intervals of length P/(k+1) and retain the most populated interval; it supplies A′ with ∣A′∣≥n/(k+1). Define the model map by θ(a)=a(modN). For two k-term sums of retained representatives, their difference T has ∣T∣<P. If the original sums are equal, then T≡0(modP), hence T=0, so the model preserves the equality.
Conversely, if the model sums are equal, then T is a multiple of N. If their original difference d∈D were nonzero, it would satisfy ud≡T(modP) with 0<∣T∣<P, contradicting the forbidden-event choice. If T=0 directly, ud≡0 implies d=0 too by the choice of P. Thus the equality is reflected. Padding equal one-term images with k−1 copies of a fixed retained element shows that the map is injective. This proves the Freiman s-isomorphism and the claimed bound on the cyclic modulus, independently of the diameter of A.

If m(t,r) counts the pairs in the displayed coefficient, then ∣γ(t,r)∣≤m(t,r). Thus ∑∣γ∣2≤∑m2=E(Γ), the additive energy of a frequency graph Γ={(k,ϕ(k)):k∈B}. Equality of two pair differences is equivalent, by permuting the four labels, to equality of the two requested sums in each coordinate.
For every t, Parseval identity on a finite group in normalized Fourier convention gives
∑r​∣C(t,r)∣2=Ey​∣f(y+t)∣2∣f(y)∣2≤1.
(50)
There are N shifts, so T4≤NE(Γ). Since T≥αN, we conclude
E(Γ)≥α4N3.​
(51)
This is precisely the number of ordered (a,b,c,d)∈B4 satisfying both a+b=c+d and ϕ(a)+ϕ(b)=ϕ(c)+ϕ(d), all modulo N. It proves squared derivative correlations force frequency-graph energy with the constant and exact equalities stated, rather than approximate frequency matching.
