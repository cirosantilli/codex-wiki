The hypotheses make K a CM-field, with nontrivial automorphism c over the totally real number field k. In every complex embedding of K, this automorphism acts as complex conjugation. Write EK​=OK×​, Ek​=Ok×​ and μK​ for the finite cyclic group of roots of unity in K.
For any u∈EK​, the element u/c(u) is an algebraic integer, indeed a unit, and every conjugate has absolute value one. The Kronecker theorem on algebraic integers in the unit disk makes it a root of unity. For clarity, the essential proof is finite: the conjugates of all positive powers have modulus one, so the integer coefficients of their monic minimal polynomials are bounded by fixed binomial coefficients. There are only finitely many such polynomials and roots; two powers coincide, forcing a root of unity.
Thus
ϕ:EK​⟶μK​,ϕ(u)=u/c(u)
(42)
is a homomorphism. Its kernel is exactly Ek​, since a conjugation-fixed element lies in k and both it and its inverse are integral there. For ζ∈μK​, c(ζ)=ζ−1 and ϕ(ζ)=ζ2. Consequently ϕ induces an injection
EK​/(Ek​μK​)↪μK​/μK2​.
(43)
To check injectivity explicitly, if ϕ(u)=ζ2, then ϕ(u/ζ)=1, so u/ζ∈Ek​. The cyclic group μK​ has even order because it contains −1, and its quotient by squares has order two. Therefore the unit index of a CM-field satisfies
[EK​:Ek​μK​]∈{1,2}.​
(44)
