A Dedekind domain is a Noetherian integrally closed domain in which every nonzero prime ideal is maximal. The equivalent local characterization is that its localizations at nonzero prime ideals are discrete valuation rings. A field is harmless as the dimension-zero case.
Write R for the domain, F for its fraction field and I for the nonzero fractional ideal. Its proposed inverse is an R-submodule of F. Choose 0=d∈R with dI⊆R, so d∈I−1 and the inverse is nonzero. Choose also 0=a∈I. Every x∈I−1 satisfies xa∈R, hence I−1⊆a−1R. Writing a=r/s with r,s∈R gives rI−1⊆sR⊆R. Thus I−1 is a fractional ideal; the Noetherian hypothesis also makes it finitely generated.
For a nonzero maximal prime ideal p, the discrete valuation ring Rp​ makes Ip​ principal, say Ip​=ap​Rp​. We need to justify that inversion commutes with this localization. One inclusion is immediate. Conversely, if xIp​⊆Rp​, finite generation of I supplies a common s∈/p such that sxI⊆R. Therefore sx∈I−1, proving
(I−1)p​=(Ip​)−1=ap−1​Rp​.
(1)
It follows that (II−1)p​=Rp​ at every such prime ideal. Globally II−1⊆R. If this were a proper ideal, it would be contained in a maximal ideal and would remain proper after localizing there, a contradiction. In the field case every nonzero fractional ideal is the whole field. Hence in all cases
II−1=R.​
(2)
This proves that every nonzero fractional ideal of a Dedekind domain is an invertible fractional ideal, rather than presupposing its inverse exists.
