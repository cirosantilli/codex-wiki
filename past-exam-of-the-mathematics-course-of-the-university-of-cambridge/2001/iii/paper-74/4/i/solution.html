Use R=Okp​​, the valuation ring of the completion of a number field at a prime ideal, with uniformizer π and residue field κ. This completeness is essential for coprime-factor Hensel lifting. Normalize the two residue factors to be monic first; if the original factors have reciprocal leading units, undo the normalization by multiplying the eventual factors by reciprocal lifts of those units.
Let the two monic residue degrees be d1​,d2​. Start with monic lifts g1​,h1​ satisfying f≡g1​h1​(modπ). Suppose monic lifts gr​,hr​ of these degrees satisfy f−gr​hr​∈πrR[X]. Put Er​=(f−gr​hr​)/πr, of degree less than d1​+d2​. Over κ, the linear map
κ[X]<d1​​⊕κ[X]<d2​​⟶κ[X]<d1​+d2​​,(u,v)⟼uϕ2​+vϕ1​
(10)
is injective: a zero image makes ϕ1​ divide u, by coprimality, forcing u=0 by its degree bound, and then v=0. The dimensions agree, so this map is a bijection. Choose lifts ur​,vr​ whose image is Er​, and set
gr+1​=gr​+πrur​,hr+1​=hr​+πrvr​.
(11)
Their product agrees with f modulo πr+1; the new cross term has factor π2r and 2r≥r+1. The degree bounds preserve monicity. Each coefficient is a Cauchy sequence in the complete discrete valuation ring R, so the limits f1​,f2​ satisfy
f=f1​f2​,f​1​=ϕ1​,f​2​=ϕ2​.​
(12)
If op​ were interpreted only as an ordinary localization rather than the completed valuation ring, the assertion would be false. For example X2−2 splits modulo seven but is irreducible over Q. The completion notation in the next part provides the intended local-field interpretation.
