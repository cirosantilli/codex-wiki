A (p+1)-form gauge potential Ap+1​ couples electrically to a spatial p-brane through the Wess-Zumino brane coupling
Scharge​=e∫Σp+1​​X∗Ap+1​.
(11)
The worldvolume dimension supplies exactly the degree required for integration. Under A↦A+dΛ, the change is e∫∂Σ​X∗Λ, so a closed worldvolume is gauge invariant; an open one needs suitable endpoint couplings. If Fp+2​=dAp+1​ has an ordinary dual potential, ∗F has degree d−p−2 and the dual potential degree d−p−3. Thus the electric and magnetic dimensions of a form-coupled brane are
pelectric​=p,pmagnetic​=d−p−4.​
(12)
Electric charge is measured by ∫Sd−p−2​∗F, magnetic charge by ∫Sp+2​F, on spheres linking the corresponding objects. Flux normalization, and possible Chern-Simons modifications of charge definitions, must be fixed in interacting backgrounds; this is the basic linear form-field dictionary.
In ten dimensions the common NS-NS fields are the graviton gμν​, Kalb–Ramond field B2​, and dilaton Φ. Their on-shell bosonic polarizations are 35,28,1, respectively. The graviton count is 10(10−3)/2=35, and a massless k-form has (k8​) transverse polarizations. The RR fields distinguish the two theories:
TheoryRR potentials and physical polarizationsTotal bosonsFermions and chiralities
Type IIA superstring theoryC1​:8, C3​:5635+28+1+8+56=128Two opposite-chirality Majorana-Weyl gravitini; two opposite-chirality Majorana-Weyl dilatini
Type IIB superstring theoryC0​:1, C2​:28, C4​:35 with self-dual F5​35+28+1+1+28+35=128Two same-chirality Majorana-Weyl gravitini; two same-chirality Majorana-Weyl dilatini of the opposite chirality to the gravitini
Each gravitino has 56 physical polarizations, and each dilatino 8, giving 128 fermions in either theory. In IIA the two supersymmetry sectors have opposite ten-dimensional chiralities, and the dilatino in each sector has chirality opposite to its gravitino. In IIB both gravitini can be designated positive-chirality and both dilatini negative-chirality. The self-duality of the IIB five-form halves the unconstrained four-form count (48​)=70 to 35. These are the massless type IIA spectrum and massless type IIB spectrum, not raw component counts.
The Neveu–Schwarz two-form couples electrically to the fundamental string and magnetically to the NS5-brane in both theories. IIA C1​ couples electrically to a D0-brane and magnetically to a D6-brane; C3​ couples electrically to a D2-brane and magnetically to a D4-brane. The magnetic descriptions use dual seven-form and five-form potentials, not extra independent polarizations. In IIB, C2​ gives D1-brane and D5-brane charges, while self-dual C4​ gives the self-dual D3-brane. The scalar C0​ couples to a Euclidean D-instanton, whose magnetic partner is the D7-brane. Momentum and, after circle compactification, Kaluza-Klein monopole charge come from the metric sector.
The full brane inventory also involves nonpropagating top-form potentials. A D8-brane is an IIA domain-wall source for Romans mass F0​, described dually by a nine-form potential; it is not generated by the propagating C1​,C3​ massless spectrum alone. A space-filling IIB D9-brane has a ten-form coupling and requires appropriate global RR-charge consistency. Such fields add no ordinary massless polarizations. This distinction avoids confusing the list of propagating particles with all possible charged extended objects.
For T-duality, compactify a spatial coordinate with X9∼X9+2πR. Closed strings have integer momentum n and winding w, with
pL​=Rn​+α′wR​,pR​=Rn​−α′wR​.
(13)
The map
R′=Rα′​,n′=w,w′=n​
(14)
leaves pL​ unchanged and reverses pR​. Equivalently, it reverses XR9​ while keeping XL9​, and reverses the corresponding right-moving worldsheet fermion to preserve worldsheet supersymmetry. Multiplication of the right Ramond ground-state spinor by Γ9 reverses its ten-dimensional chirality. Left and right Ramond projections therefore change from opposite chirality to equal chirality, or conversely:
IIA on SR1​⟷IIB on Sα′/R1​.​
(15)
The zero-mode mass contribution (n/R)2+(wR/α′)2 and the oscillator spectrum are unchanged. The Buscher dilaton shift gives gs′​=gs​α′​/R, which preserves the nine-dimensional gravitational coupling proportional to gs2​/R. The mixed metric and two-form components along the circle are exchanged; RR potentials with a circle leg map to one lower degree and those without it to one higher degree, in the corresponding gauge-covariant description.
For open strings, the derivative relations ∂τ​X=∂σ​X and ∂σ​X=∂τ​X exchange Neumann boundary conditions with Dirichlet boundary conditions. Consequently a wrapped Dp-brane becomes a D(p−1)-brane, while a transverse one becomes a wrapped D(p+1)-brane. The even-dimensional IIA branes and odd-dimensional IIB branes therefore transform consistently with the closed-string chirality flip; transverse positions and compact gauge Wilson lines are exchanged.
