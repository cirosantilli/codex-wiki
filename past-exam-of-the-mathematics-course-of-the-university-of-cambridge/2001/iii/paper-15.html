At a point [X]∈Pn, the complex tautological line bundle has fiber CX⊆Cn+1. The hyperplane line bundle is its dual, [H]=O(1), and O(m)=O(1)⊗m. On Ui​={Xi​=0} let ti​=X/Xi​ be the tautological frame and ei​ its dual. On overlaps ej​=(Xj​/Xi​)ei​. The sheaf of holomorphic sections of a vector bundle of this dual bundle is the sheaf OPn​(1).
If a nonzero linear form ℓ cuts out the projective hyperplane H, it gives a holomorphic section sH​ of O(1) with a simple zero along H. Dividing a local section s by sH​ gives a meromorphic function with no poles away from H and at most a simple pole on H. Conversely multiplication by sH​ turns such a function into a holomorphic section. These mutually inverse local constructions agree on overlaps and prove the holomorphic line bundle associated to a divisor identification
O(1)≅O(H).​
(1)
The nonnegative hyperplane twist cohomology by restriction calculation gives
hr(Pn,O(m))={(nn+m​),0,​r=0,r>0.​​
(2)
To derive it from the permitted vanishing, use the hyperplane exact sequence for twisting sheaves
0⟶OPn​(m−1)sH​​OPn​(m)⟶i∗​OH​(m)⟶0,
(3)
where i:H↪Pn and H≅Pn−1. Local multiplication by a defining equation is injective, and restriction gives exactly its cokernel. Induct on n, starting from a point, and then on m. For m=0, higher sheaf cohomology vanishes by assumption and global holomorphic functions are constant by compactness and the maximum principle. For m>0, the long exact sequence in sheaf cohomology, the inductive vanishing for O(m−1) and the dimension induction on H give vanishing in every positive degree and
h0(Pn,O(m))−h0(Pn,O(m−1))=(n−1n−1+m​).
(4)
Summing this recurrence gives (nn+m​). Homogeneous degree-m monomials give that many independent sections, so they form a basis. The printed extra equality to zero cannot hold in degree zero, already because h0(Pn,O)=1; the boxed calculation gives the intended dimensions.
For the differential of the projective quotient map, the differential of a smooth map is computed by applying a tangent vector to coordinate functions. Since xj​=Xj​/X0​,
∂Xi​∂xj​​=a0​δij​​(i>0),∂X0​∂xj​​=−a02​aj​​.
(5)
Thus
π∗​(∂Xi​∂​)=a0​1​∂xi​∂​ (i>0),π∗​(∂X0​∂​)=−j=1∑n​a02​aj​​∂xj​∂​.​
(6)
Under replacing X by λX, dπ on a constant ambient tangent vector scales by λ−1, while a homogeneous linear form L scales by λ. Therefore dπX​(L(X)∂Xj​​) is independent of the representative. Its chart coefficients are holomorphic, so it is a global holomorphic vector field. The radial Euler vector field lies in the kernel of dπ, either from the same formulas or because it differentiates a scaling orbit. Hence
π∗​(i=0∑n​Xi​∂Xi​​)=0.​
(7)
Describe the Euler sequence on complex projective space fiberwise as well as in coordinates. At a line ℓ=CX, the middle bundle has fiber Hom(ℓ,Cn+1) and the holomorphic tangent bundle has fiber Hom(ℓ,Cn+1/ℓ). Send a homomorphism to its composition with the quotient. The map from O sends a scalar to that scalar times the inclusion ℓ↪Cn+1. In homogeneous notation these maps are
f⟼(fX0​,…,fXn​),(s0​,…,sn​)⟼dπX​(∑j​sj​(X)∂Xj​​).
(8)
A local section of O(1) is a local degree-one homogeneous function on the punctured cone, so the second expression is representative-independent. Quotienting is surjective, and its kernel consists exactly of scalar inclusion maps. This proves the short exact sequence of locally free sheaves
0→O→O(1)⊕(n+1)→O(T′)→0.​
(9)
Since it is locally split, dualizing and tensoring by O(1) preserves exactness and gives the cotangent Euler sequence in homogeneous coordinates
0→Ω1(1)→O⊕(n+1)(ci​)↦∑i​ci​Xi​​O(1)→0.
(10)
On global sections the last map identifies Cn+1 with the basis of linear forms in H0(O(1)). Its kernel is zero. This proves the vanishing of global sections of the once-twisted projective cotangent bundle; left exactness gives h0(Pn,Ω1(1))=0​.

An almost complex structure is a smooth endomorphism J:TM→TM with J2=−I; in particular the real dimension is even. Complexifying the tangent bundle splits it into the i and −i eigensubbundles
TC​M=T1,0M⊕T0,1M,
(11)
interchanged by conjugation. On a complex manifold, these are locally spanned by ∂zj​ and ∂zˉj​. An integrable almost complex structure is one arising from a holomorphic coordinate atlas. Its T0,1 sections are closed under the Lie bracket. The obstruction is the Nijenhuis tensor
NJ​(X,Y)=[JX,JY]−J[JX,Y]−J[X,JY]−[X,Y].
(12)
Expanding this expression in the two eigentypes shows that NJ​=0 is equivalent to involutivity of T0,1. Holomorphic coordinates make this condition necessary; the Newlander-Nirenberg theorem gives its sufficiency for smooth J. Integrability also gives d=∂+∂ˉ, with ∂2=∂ˉ2=0 and ∂∂ˉ+∂ˉ∂=0. The Dolbeault operator on T1,0M=TM′​ defines its holomorphic vector bundle structure.
A real connection on a vector bundle ∇ on TM preserves J when ∇J=0. Its complexification then preserves both eigensubbundles. Restricting to TM′​ gives a complex connection D. Conversely any complex connection on TM′​ gives such a real connection by adjoining its conjugate on T0,1 and restricting to the conjugation-fixed real tangent bundle. Equivalently, transfer D through the real isomorphism v↦(v−iJv)/2. These operations are inverse. Preserving J alone does not impose the holomorphic compatibility condition D0,1=∂ˉT′​.
For integrable J, the latter condition has a precise real-connection interpretation: the mixed-type torsion tensor vanishes. In holomorphic coordinates it says D∂zˉi​​∂zj​=0. Reality also gives ∇∂zj​​∂zˉi​=0. Since these coordinate fields commute, their torsion is zero. Conversely a J-preserving real connection with zero mixed torsion has these two derivatives equal; they belong to opposite eigentypes, so both vanish, proving D0,1=∂ˉT′​. This is the holomorphic tangent connection and mixed torsion criterion.
Full torsion-freeness imposes the additional symmetry Γijk​=Γjik​ in D∂zi​​∂zj​=Γijk​∂zk​, and its conjugate. Such a J-preserving torsion-free connection exists on any complex manifold: local holomorphic-coordinate flat connections have these properties, and a real partition of unity combines them globally. Preservation of J and zero torsion persist because both conditions are affine in the connection. Conversely if J is merely almost complex, a torsion-free connection preserving it forces NJ​=0: replace brackets by ∇X​Y−∇Y​X in the displayed tensor and use ∇J=0; all terms cancel. Thus this connection condition detects integrability, without imposing any metric condition.
A Hermitian metric is a real Riemannian metric g with g(JX,JY)=g(X,Y). It induces a Hermitian metric h on TM′​ and the fundamental two-form ω(X,Y)=g(JX,Y). The unique Chern connection satisfies both D0,1=∂ˉT′​ and metric compatibility. In a holomorphic frame with metric matrix H and coefficient-column convention, its matrix is H−1∂H. It need not be torsion-free when transferred to TM.
In holomorphic coordinates its coefficients are Γijk​=hklˉ∂i​hjlˉ​. Their symmetry in i,j is equivalent to ∂i​hjlˉ​=∂j​hilˉ​, which says ∂ω=0. The conjugate gives ∂ˉω=0. The result that a torsion-free Chern tangent connection characterizes a Kähler metric follows: the transferred Chern connection is torsion-free exactly when dω=0​, the Kähler metric condition. In that case it equals the Levi-Civita connection, by uniqueness of the metric-compatible torsion-free connection. Equivalently a Hermitian metric is Kähler exactly when its Levi-Civita connection preserves J. In the reverse direction ∇J=0 implies ∇ω=0, and zero torsion gives dω=0.
This explains the role of Kähler metrics: they make the real Riemannian and holomorphic connection theories coincide. A general complex manifold has torsion-free J-preserving connections and has Hermitian metrics, but need not have a connection satisfying both requirements simultaneously. On a Kähler manifold, the Kähler identities then link the Dolbeault Laplacian and Hodge Laplacian, giving Δd​=2Δ∂ˉ​=2Δ∂​ and the resulting harmonic Hodge decomposition theorem for compact Kähler manifolds.

For a smooth complex vector bundle E, a connection is a complex-linear map D:Γ(E)→A1(M;E) satisfying D(fs)=df⊗s+fDs for smooth complex functions f. Local trivializations supply flat local connections. A locally finite smooth partition of unity (ρi​) subordinate to them gives D=∑i​ρi​Di​; local finiteness makes the sum smooth and ∑i​ρi​=1 gives the Leibniz rule. Thus connections on a vector bundle always exist on the usual paracompact smooth manifold.
Extend D to bundle-valued forms by D(α⊗s)=dα⊗s+(−1)degαα∧Ds. Squaring shows that D2(fs)=fD2s, so the curvature form is the endomorphism-valued two-form Θ=D2. With a row frame e=(e1​,…,er​), write De=eA; for coefficient columns s=ev, Ds=e(dv+Av). Applying D again and using the graded Leibniz rule gives
D2(ev)=e(dA+A∧A)v.
(13)
The cross terms −A∧dv and A∧dv cancel. Hence the Cartan curvature matrix equation in this convention is
Θ=dA+A∧A.​
(14)
For a frame change e′=eg, the connection matrix and curvature transform as A′=g−1Ag+g−1dg and Θ′=g−1Θg. Trace is invariant under conjugation, so the local forms TrΘ patch into the global trace of vector-bundle curvature.
The induced determinant connection on detE=ΛrE is
Dr​(s1​∧⋯∧sr​)=∑j=1r​s1​∧⋯∧Dsj​∧⋯∧sr​,
(15)
where the one-form factor of each Dsj​ is placed in front of the section factors. In the local determinant frame e1​∧⋯∧er​, only the diagonal terms survive, so its connection form is TrA. Its curvature is dTrA. Since
Tr(A∧A)=∑i,j​Aij​∧Aji​=0
(16)
by pairing off-diagonal terms and using Aii​∧Aii​=0, we obtain
ΘDr​​=dTrA=TrΘD​.​
(17)
It is closed locally by d2=0, and thus closed globally.
For two connections, B=D1​−D0​ is a global endomorphism-valued one-form: the derivative terms cancel in their Leibniz rules. In a local frame A1​=A0​+B, so
Θ1​−Θ0​=dB+A0​∧B+B∧A0​+B∧B.
(18)
Taking traces cancels the mixed terms, because both factors are one-forms, and cancels Tr(B∧B). Therefore the trace curvature transgression formula is
TrΘ1​−TrΘ0​=dTrB.​
(19)
The right-hand side is globally exact, proving independence of the de Rham cohomology class without needing a sheaf-cohomology identification.
Finally let e be a holomorphic frame for the holomorphic line bundle, with h=∥e∥2>0. Define De=e∂logh, extending by the Leibniz rule. If e′=eg for a nowhere-zero holomorphic function g, then h′=∣g∣2h and
∂logh′=∂logh+g−1dg.
(20)
This is exactly the line-bundle frame-change law for a connection, so the local definitions glue globally. Its (0,1) part is the Dolbeault operator. Metric compatibility follows from dlogh=∂logh+∂ˉlogh, or dh=h(A+A) for A=∂logh. These conditions uniquely force this A, identifying the Chern connection. Since a scalar one-form wedges with itself to zero,
Θ=d(∂logh)=∂ˉ∂logh=−∂∂ˉlogh.​
(21)
This agrees with the local formula for the Chern connection on a line bundle; keeping the order of the two Dolbeault differentials fixes the sign.

The Hermitian metric induces pointwise Hermitian inner products on the exterior powers of the complex cotangent bundle, with different bidegrees orthogonal, and a positive volume form dVg​. Thus the global L2 inner product on smooth differential forms of type (p,q) is
⟨α,β⟩L2​=∫M​⟨α(x),β(x)⟩g​dVg​.
(22)
Let ∂ˉ∗ be the formal adjoint of the Dolbeault operator, defined by integration by parts, and let Δ∂ˉ​=∂ˉ∂ˉ∗+∂ˉ∗∂ˉ. This Dolbeault Laplacian preserves bidegree, is elliptic and nonnegative, and has
⟨Δ∂ˉ​α,α⟩=∥∂ˉα∥2+∥∂ˉ∗α∥2.
(23)
On a compact Hermitian manifold without boundary, the Dolbeault Hodge decomposition on a compact Hermitian manifold says that the smooth harmonic space H∂ˉp,q​=kerΔ∂ˉ​ is finite-dimensional and
Ap,q=H∂ˉp,q​⊕⊥Δ∂ˉ​Ap,q.
(24)
More precisely, there are the orthogonal harmonic projection H and a Dolbeault Green operator G taking smooth forms to smooth forms, with G=0 on harmonics and Δ∂ˉ​G=GΔ∂ˉ​=I−H. Elliptic regularity ensures these are statements about smooth forms, not only L2 completions. The Green operator commutes with ∂ˉ and ∂ˉ∗, since the Laplacian does and its inverse is unique on the orthogonal complement of the kernel.
Expanding α=Hα+Δ∂ˉ​Gα gives
Ap,q=H∂ˉp,q​⊕∂ˉAp,q−1⊕∂ˉ∗Ap,q+1.​
(25)
The three summands are orthogonal: harmonic forms are killed by both adjoints, and ⟨∂ˉu,∂ˉ∗v⟩=⟨∂ˉ2u,v⟩=0. If α is ∂ˉ-closed, its coexact component is orthogonal to every closed form; taking its inner product with α shows that component has norm zero. Conversely the first two components are closed. Thus ker∂ˉ=H∂ˉp,q​⊕∂ˉAp,q−1​, with out-of-range form spaces taken to be zero.

On the Kähler manifold define Lα=ω∧α using the Kähler form; the operator Λ=L∗ is the adjoint Lefschetz operator, the pointwise contraction adjoint to wedging with ω. It lowers bidegree by (1,1). Set
Δd​=dd∗+d∗d,Δ∂​=∂∂∗+∂∗∂,
(26)
and keep Δ∂ˉ​ as above. These are the Hodge Laplacian and the holomorphic and antiholomorphic Laplacians respectively. The permitted identity among the Kähler identities [Λ,∂]=i∂ˉ∗ gives ∂ˉ∗=−i[Λ,∂]. Expanding rather than assuming the desired anticommutation,
∂∂ˉ∗+∂ˉ∗∂=−i(∂Λ∂−∂2Λ+Λ∂2−∂Λ∂)=0,
(27)
because ∂2=0. Hence ∂∂ˉ∗+∂ˉ∗∂=0​.

Use both Kähler identities from the preceding parts, so that ∂∗=i[Λ,∂ˉ] and ∂ˉ∗=−i[Λ,∂]. For odd operators a,b, write {a,b}=ab+ba. Direct expansion gives
{∂,[Λ,∂ˉ]}+{∂ˉ,[Λ,∂]}=[Λ,{∂,∂ˉ}]=0.
(29)
Since ∂∂ˉ+∂ˉ∂=0, we obtain
Δ∂​=i{∂,[Λ,∂ˉ]}=−i{∂ˉ,[Λ,∂]}=Δ∂ˉ​.
(30)
Together with part (b), this proves the full Kähler Laplacian identity
Δd​=2Δ∂​=2Δ∂ˉ​.​
(31)
Now take the exact form η. Being ∂ˉ-exact, it is orthogonal to the harmonic space, so Hη=0, and it is also ∂ˉ-closed. Choose α=Gη for the Dolbeault Green operator of the Dolbeault Laplacian. Commutation gives ∂ˉα=G∂ˉη=0. Therefore
η=Δ∂ˉ​α=∂ˉ∂ˉ∗α.​
(32)
Suppose also ∂η=0, and put v=∂ˉ∗α, u=∂v. The mixed identities and (∂ˉ∗)2=0 give
∂ˉu=−∂∂ˉv=−∂η=0,∂ˉ∗u=−∂∂ˉ∗v=0.
(33)
Thus u=∂∂ˉ∗α is harmonic for the Dolbeault Laplacian. By the Kähler Laplacian identity it is also ∂-harmonic and hence ∂∗u=0. But it is ∂-exact, so
∥u∥2=⟨∂v,u⟩=⟨v,∂∗u⟩=0.
(34)
Consequently ∂∂ˉ∗α=0​: the form v is ∂-closed.
For every harmonic form h of the same bidegree as v, ⟨v,h⟩=⟨α,∂ˉh⟩=0. Harmonic spaces for the two Dolbeault Laplacians coincide, so v has no ∂-harmonic component. Apply the ∂ version of the Dolbeault Hodge decomposition. Since v is ∂-closed, it has no ∂∗-coexact component either. Thus v=∂ϕ, with ϕ∈Ap−1,q−1. We conclude the requested ddbar lemma:
η=∂ˉ∂ϕ.​
(35)
For example ϕ=∂∗Gv is a choice, since the same Green operator serves Δ∂​=Δ∂ˉ​. If p=0 or q=0, the relevant negative-bidegree space is zero and the argument forces η=0. Compactness, the absence of boundary and the Kähler condition are essential to this global harmonic argument.
