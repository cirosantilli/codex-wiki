For fixed Imτ>0, Gaussian decay of ∣q∣n2 gives locally uniform convergence of the theta function series and all its z-derivatives. It is therefore entire. Shifting the series argument and reindexing gives
θ3​(z+π)=θ3​(z),θ3​(z+πτ)=q−1e−2izθ3​(z).
(28)
Since θ4​(z)=θ3​(z+π/2), the corresponding second multiplier for θ4​ has the opposite sign:
θ4​(z+π)=θ4​(z),θ4​(z+πτ)=−q−1e−2izθ4​(z).
(29)
Thus h=θ3​/θ4​ obeys h(z+π)=h(z) and h(z+πτ)=−h(z). These give candidate period lattices; to show they are exact, first locate every zero.
At z∗​=(π+πτ)/2,
θ3​(z∗​)=∑n∈Z​(−1)nqn(n+1)=0,
(30)
because the terms with indices n and −n−1 cancel. The absolute convergence justifies that pairing. Quasi-periodicity propagates this zero to every z∗​+mπ+nπτ.
For exhaustiveness and simplicity, let v=θ3′​/θ3​. It satisfies v(z+π)=v(z) and v(z+πτ)=v(z)−2i. Take a fundamental parallelogram for Λ0​=πZ+πτZ, translated so its boundary has no zero. The two sloping edges cancel by the first identity; the bottom and reversed top give
∮v(z)dz=∫z0​z0​+π​(v(z)−v(z+πτ))dz=2πi.
(31)
The argument principle therefore counts one zero with multiplicity in every cell. Since a known zero class already exists, all zeros are simple and exactly
z=2π​+2πτ​+mπ+nπτ,m,n∈Z​.
(32)
The denominator's simple zeros are instead πτ/2+Λ0​. These two cosets are disjoint: π/2 cannot lie in Λ0​ because Imτ>0. Thus there are no cancelled zeros or poles in the quotient.
Any period of h or h2 must translate its zero set onto itself, so must belong to Λ0​. A translation mπ+nπτ multiplies h by (−1)n; the quotient is nonconstant, so an odd n cannot be a period of h. Squaring removes precisely that sign. This proves the period lattice of theta3 over theta4 and its squared counterpart:
Per(θ4​θ3​​)=πZ+2πτZ​,Per[(θ4​θ3​​)2]=πZ+πτZ​.
(33)
The second lattice is for the squared function; no additional translation can preserve its zero set.
