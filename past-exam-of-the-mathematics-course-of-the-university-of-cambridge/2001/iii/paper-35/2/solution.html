The energy-conserving self-similar blast wave requires a stage in which the original stellar radius, ejecta mass, ambient pressure and cooling have ceased to supply important scales. In particular, the swept-up mass must greatly exceed the ejecta mass; R≫R∗​ alone need not ensure this in a very dilute interstellar medium. For fixed explosion energy E, uniform ambient mass density ρ0​ and elapsed time t, dimensional analysis gives R=CEaρ0b​td. Matching mass, length and time gives a=1/5, b=−1/5, d=2/5. Equivalently, a self-similar strong shock wave has E∼ρ0​R3R˙2, whose constant-energy solution has the same exponents:
R=ξ0​(E/ρ0​)1/5t2/5.​
(15)
The dimensionless coefficient depends on γ and the post-shock profiles; dimensional reasoning cannot fix it.
For completeness, obtain the Strong-shock Rankine-Hugoniot conditions in the shock frame. Let v1​=V−u denote downstream speed relative to the outward-moving front. With negligible upstream pressure and enthalpy, conservation of mass, momentum and energy gives
ρ0​V=ρ1​v1​,ρ0​V2=p1​+ρ1​v12​,2V2​=2v12​​+(γ−1)ρ1​γp1​​.
(16)
Put q=ρ1​/ρ0​. Substituting v1​=V/q into the last two equations and selecting the compressive, nontrivial branch yields q=(γ+1)/(γ−1). Hence
ρ1​=γ−1γ+1​ρ0​,p1​=γ+12​ρ0​V2,u=γ+12​V.​
(17)
Here the symbol u, written as u=2V/(γ+1) in what follows, is the laboratory-frame radial velocity; it is not the relative speed v1​.
Use the thin-shell approximation for a spherical blast wave. Set W=4πR3/3 and Ms​=ρ0​W. Equating the swept-up mass to 4πR2dρ1​ gives d/R=(γ−1)/[3(γ+1)]. The growing shell's momentum is Ms​u. Since newly swept gas has zero laboratory momentum, the net force is the cavity pressure acting on the inner shell, to thin-shell accuracy:
dtd​(Ms​u)=4πR2pc​,pc​=αp1​.
(18)
The pressure at the shock is already accounted for by the jump conditions and mass loading; subtracting p1​ as an additional external pressure would count the shock reaction twice. With u=2V/(γ+1), the last equation becomes
RV˙+3V2=3αV2,dlnRdlnV​=3(α−1).
(19)
Therefore, for constant α,
R˙=KR3(α−1).​
(20)
The shell volume is Ms​/ρ1​=W/q. Its internal energy and kinetic energy are
Es,int​=q(γ−1)p1​W​=(γ+1)22ρ0​WV2​,Es,kin​=21​Ms​u2=(γ+1)22ρ0​WV2​.
(21)
Treat the cavity volume as W at the same thin-shell level, so its internal energy is Ec,int​=αp1​W/(γ−1). Their sum is proportional to R3V2 for constant α and γ. Constant total energy thus requires 3+6(α−1)=0, giving α=1/2. Consequently
E=ρ0​WV2(γ−1)(γ+1)25γ−3​.
(22)
Using V=2R/(5t), this becomes
ξ05​=16π75​5γ−3(γ−1)(γ+1)2​.​
(23)
For γ=5/3, d/R=1/12 and the approximate energy fractions are Es,kin​/E=1/4, Es,int​/E=1/4, Ec,int​/E=1/2.
This is an approximate normalization, not an exact treatment of finite shell thickness. If instead one subtracts the shell volume and uses Wc​=W(1−1/q) for the cavity, while retaining the other shell assumptions, the energy coefficient becomes 2(2γ−1)/[(γ−1)(γ+1)2] and ξ05​=75(γ−1)(γ+1)2/[32π(2γ−1)]. The difference is an order-d/R geometric correction of this crude shell model; a consistent exact coefficient requires the full self-similar profiles.
In the idealized mass budget all swept gas is assigned to the shell, so the cavity inertia is neglected. The hot cavity's residual mass density is not specified, so its kinetic energy cannot be assigned a unique numerical fraction from the stated data. If its low mass is Mc​ and its velocity is of order u, then Ec,kin​∼Mc​u2/2 and
EEc,kin​​∼Ms​Mc​​5γ−32(γ−1)​≪1when Mc​≪Ms​.
(24)
For an illustrative uniform-density cavity with homologous velocity v(r)=ur/R, direct radial integration gives Ec,kin​=3Mc​u2/10, so the preceding fraction has an extra factor 3/5. The cavity can carry substantial internal energy despite negligible kinetic energy because it is hot. Neglect of its inertia is justified in the swept-mass-dominated stage, not merely by its small initial radius.
The calculation describes the adiabatic expansion stage of a supernova remnant, giving an estimate of its age or explosion energy from its radius, expansion speed and ambient mass density. For example t≃2R/(5V), and E∝ρ0​R3V2. Such blasts inject energy and momentum into the interstellar medium and redistribute stellar ejecta. The early ejecta-dominated stage, radiatively cooled late shell, nonuniform ambient medium, finite ambient sound speed, magnetic fields, nonspherical geometry and energy transfer to cosmic rays violate different assumptions. Even in the adiabatic spherical stage, the real profiles are not a uniform thin shell and a uniform-pressure cavity, so ξ0​ here is an estimate.
