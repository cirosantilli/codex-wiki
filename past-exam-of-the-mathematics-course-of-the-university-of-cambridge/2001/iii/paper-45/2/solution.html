Choose an explicit orientation convention, since the supplied PDF does not display the referenced diagram. Let ez​ point upward and e2​ complete a right-handed frame with the horizontal flow direction e1​. Use
i=sinθe1​+cosθez​,j=−cosθe1​+sinθez​,
(17)
where i points from the head towards the tail. A tail point has position r=(a+s)i. Its rotational velocity is Ω(a+s)j, where the scalar Ω is about −e2​ and therefore θ˙=−Ω. Keeping that sign is essential for stability.
The point lies at height z=(a+s)cosθ, so the local imposed fluid velocity is α(a+s)cosθe1​. Its velocity relative to that fluid has components
vj​=Uj​+(Ω+αcos2θ)(a+s),vi​=Ui​−αsinθcosθ(a+s),​
(18)
where Ui​=U⋅i and Uj​=U⋅j. These follow by projection; rigid rotation has no tangential component.
Write P=m′g, H=6πμa, T=8πμa3/3, K=KN​L, and
I1​=∫0L​(a+s)ds=aL+2L2​,I2​=∫0L​(a+s)2ds=a2L+aL2+3L3​.
(19)
Let q=Ω+αcos2θ. The resistive-force theory force per length on the tail is −KN​vj​j−γKN​vi​i. Head drag and excess weight give
(H+K)Uj​=−Psinθ−KN​I1​q,(H+γK)Ui​=−Pcosθ+γKN​I1​αsinθcosθ.
(20)
The fluid vorticity is αe2​, so its local angular velocity has scalar component −α/2 about our axis. Sphere rotation resistance therefore contributes −T(Ω+α/2). Torque balance about C, including gravity acting at hi, is
0=−Phsinθ−KN​(I1​Uj​+I2​q)−T(Ω+α/2).
(21)
Eliminating Uj​ gives the unapproximated local-resistance relation
[KN​I2​−H+KKN2​I12​​+T]q=P[H+KKN​I1​​−h]sinθ−Tα(21​−cos2θ).
(22)
Now retain head and tail translation resistance at the same order, as intended by σ=O(L/a), but use a/L≪1 in the lever-arm integrals. Thus I1​≃L2/2, I2​≃L3/3. Sphere rotation resistance is smaller than tail rotation resistance by order (a/L)2, so omit T at fixed scaled shear. Also neglect h relative to the tail's hydrodynamic force-centre distance KN​I1​/(H+K)=O(L). Uniform density gives
Lh​=L(4πa3/3+πb2L)πb2L(a+L/2)​;
(23)
hence this step requires b2L/a3≪1, or head-dominated volume. It is consistent with the distinguished logarithmic ordering, but b≪a≪L by itself would not ensure it. The model further neglects hydrodynamic interaction between head and tail, and uses low-Reynolds-number local drag.
Under these approximations,
q=(H+K)L3/3−KN​L4/4(L2/2)Psinθ​=L(K+4H)6P​sinθ=2βsinθ,
(24)
so
Ω≃2βsinθ−αcos2θ,β=KN​L2/3+8πμaLP​=L(K+4H)3P​.​
(25)
Because θ˙=−Ω, its equilibria satisfy α(1−sin2θ)=2βsinθ. Define
s∗​=β+β2+α2​α​,
(26)
with s∗​=0 at zero shear. The two orientations modulo 2π are θs​=arcsins∗​ and θu​=π−θs​. The derivative of θ˙=αcos2θ−2βsinθ at an equilibrium is
−2cosθ(β+αsinθ).
(27)
The second factor is positive, so the branch cosθs​>0 is stable and the other is unstable. On the stable branch the tail tip is above C: the head is lower than the far tail end.
The corresponding leading translation velocities are
Ui​≃H+γK−Pcosθ+21​γKN​L2αsinθcosθ​,Uj​≃−K+4H4P​sinθ.​
(28)
The second formula uses q=2βsinθ in the normal force balance. In the equilibrium orientation αcos2θ=2βsinθ, and Uz​=Ui​cosθ+Uj​sinθ simplifies to
Uz​=−H+γKP​+(H+γK)(K+4H)PK(1−γ)​s∗2​.​
(29)
This is shear-dependent settling of a sphere-and-tail body. Since 0<γ<1 for a slender filament, s∗2​ increases with the magnitude of shear and the signed upward-coordinate velocity Uz​ increases towards zero. Equivalently, increasing shear decreases the downward sedimentation speed. The formula remains negative throughout this leading model. If γ=1 there is no shear dependence; reversing the drag anisotropy would reverse the trend. At very large shear, the omitted sphere rotation term proportional to Tα can become important, so the approximation is not uniform in arbitrarily large α.
