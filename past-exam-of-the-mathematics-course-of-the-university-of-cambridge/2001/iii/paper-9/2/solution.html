Boundedness of the Nevanlinna characteristic refers to r↑1, equivalently to a uniform bound on r0​≤r<1 for any fixed r0​>0. This avoids the harmless origin normalization when ν0​>0.
The Nevanlinna first main theorem states, for every fixed finite value a and every nonconstant meromorphic function on the disk,
N(r,1/(f−a))+m(r,1/(f−a))=T(r,f)+O(1).​
(13)
For a=∞, this is the definition of T. The error is bounded independently of r. Translation changes T by O(1), while Jensen's formula gives T(r,h)−T(r,1/h)=log∣c∣ when h(z)=czν(1+O(z)) near zero; these observations explain the first theorem.
The Nevanlinna second main theorem in the unit disc states that for q≥3 distinct values aj​ on the Riemann sphere,
(q−2)T(r,f)≤j=1∑q​N(r;aj​)+O(log+T(r,f)+log1−r1​)​
(14)
as r↑1 outside an exceptional set E satisfying ∫E​dr/(1−r)<∞. The truncated Nevanlinna counting function N counts each distinct preimage once. The boundary-distance error cannot simply be suppressed: on the disk it need not be o(T). The disk error and exceptional-set estimates follow from the logarithmic-derivative estimates in mathweb.tifr.res.in/Documents/Publications/Lectures/tifr17.pdf .
Suppose first that T(r,f) is bounded. Since m(r,f)≥0, its poles satisfy the Blaschke condition, counting their orders: let r↑1 in their integrated count and use 1−∣b∣≤−log∣b∣. Let B be their Blaschke product, including the origin factor if necessary. Then g=Bf extends to a holomorphic function on the whole disk, and ∣B∣≤1 gives
2π1​∫02π​log+∣g(reiθ)∣dθ≤m(r,f)≤C.
(15)
Here the last bound follows from bounded T and the uniform lower bound N(r,f)≥ν0​logr0​ on r≥r0​.
The nonnegative subharmonic function log+∣g∣ has a finite harmonic majorant. Indeed its Poisson integrals HR​ on expanding disks dominate it by the maximum principle for subharmonic functions, increase with R, and have HR​(0)≤C. The Harnack inequality for harmonic functions bounds them on every compact subdisk, so their increasing limit is a finite harmonic function H≥log+∣g∣. Since the disk is simply connected, H has a harmonic conjugate, giving a holomorphic function A with ReA=H. Consequently
u=ge−A,v=Be−A,∣u∣≤1,∣v∣≤1,v≡0,f=u/v.
(16)
This proves the constructive half of the quotient characterization of bounded characteristic.
Conversely, if f=u/v with u,v bounded holomorphic functions and v≡0, each pole of f is a zero of v of at least the same order. The elementary inequality log+∣u/v∣≤log+∣u∣+log+∣1/v∣ and the Nevanlinna first main theorem give
T(r,f)≤m(r,u)+T(r,1/v)+O(1)≤log+∥u∥∞​+T(r,v)+O(1)=O(1).
(17)
Any cancellation at an origin zero contributes only a bounded multiple of logr on r≥r0​, which is included in this O(1). The zero function is immediate; constant nonzero v is handled directly. Thus bounded characteristic is exactly a quotient of two bounded analytic functions.
An unbounded example with bounded characteristic is
g(z)=1−z1​.​
(18)
It is a ratio of the bounded holomorphic functions 1 and 1−z. More explicitly, Jensen's formula gives the mean of log∣1−reiθ∣ as zero, so m(r,1/(1−z))=m(r,1−z)≤log2, although g(r)→∞.
Finally, set Y=(1+∣g∣2)p/2≥1. Pointwise log+∣g∣≤p−1logY. Jensen's inequality for the concave logarithm gives
T(r,g)=m(r,g)≤p1​∫logY2πdθ​≤p1​log∫Y2πdθ​<plogC​.

There is no pole term because g is holomorphic. Hence the stated integral bound implies bounded characteristic. This argument in fact works for every p>0.
