The Blaschke condition therefore makes the infinite product converge uniformly on every compact subdisk. Away from the prescribed zeros its tail factors tend uniformly to one with summable errors, so their logarithms converge and the product is nonzero. It has exactly the prescribed zeros, and the maximum modulus principle bounds every finite partial product by one, hence ∣B∣≤1. Thus f=B∘ϕ is the required bounded analytic function, with no extra zeros.

The hyperbolic metric on the punctured disk is ∣dz∣/(∣z∣log(1/∣z∣)). Its universal covering map τ↦eiτ pulls it back to the curvature-minus-one upper-half-plane metric. The imaginary axis is a geodesic; the lift from i to in2 has length logn2, and any other lift differs by a real deck translation and has at least that distance. Therefore
ρ(w,zn​)=2logn,∑n≥2​e−ρ(w,zn​)=∑n≥2​n21​<∞.

The triangle inequality again makes the sum finite for every basepoint. However, any bounded holomorphic function on the punctured disk extends over zero by the Riemann removable singularity theorem. These zeros accumulate at zero, so the identity theorem makes the extension identically zero. It cannot have exactly the prescribed zero set. This example of hyperbolic zero sequences on a punctured disk satisfies the distance condition but not the analytic zero-set condition.
