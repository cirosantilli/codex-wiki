The unbroken group is the stabilizer subgroup of the chosen vacuum expectation value:
H={h∈G:hϕ0​=ϕ0​}.​
(1)
Its Lie algebra consists of generators T with Tϕ0​=0. The vacuum manifold is the orbit Gϕ0​≃G/H under the stated transitivity assumption. Its tangent space is the image of T↦Tϕ0​, so its dimension is r=dimG−dimH.
Let Kij​=∂i​∂j​V(ϕ0​) be the scalar mass matrix, with canonical scalar kinetic terms. Invariance of the scalar potential gives (∂i​V)(Ta​ϕ)i​=0 for every ϕ. Differentiate with respect to ϕj​ and evaluate at the minimum, where ∂i​V=0:
Kji​(Ta​ϕ0​)i​=0.
(2)
Thus every independent tangent direction is a zero eigenvector of K. These are the Goldstone directions in the scalar mass matrix; for a global symmetry they are r physical massless Goldstone bosons. For a generic nondegenerate minimum transverse to the orbit, they are precisely all the zero eigenvalues. Without that nondegeneracy assumption, additional massless scalars are possible.
For a local gauge group, use the orthogonal unitary-gauge slice near a scalar vacuum. Since ϕ0T​Ta​ϕ0​=0 by antisymmetry, the proposed condition is equivalent to
(ϕ−ϕ0​)TTa​ϕ0​=0.
(3)
It sets the gauge-orbit components of the scalar fluctuation to zero. This is an admissible local unitary gauge: on a basis of independent broken tangent vectors va​=Ta​ϕ0​, the variation of these conditions under the broken gauge parameters is vaT​vb​. This Gram matrix is invertible on that basis, so the implicit function theorem selects the required gauge transformation near the vacuum. Unbroken transformations remain as a residual H gauge symmetry; no global gauge slice through zeros of the scalar field is being asserted.
Write ϕ=ϕ0​+h. To quadratic order, the covariant kinetic term contains
21​(∂μ​h)T∂μh+gAμa​(Ta​ϕ0​)T∂μh+21​g2Aμa​Abμ(Ta​ϕ0​)TTb​ϕ0​.
(4)
The middle term vanishes in this unitary gauge. The gauge-boson mass rank from a real scalar vacuum is therefore determined by
(MA2​)ab​=g2(Ta​ϕ0​)TTb​ϕ0​.​
(5)
These are squared masses: the physical gauge boson masses are square roots of the matrix's nonzero eigenvalues after diagonalization. For real ca​,
ca​(MA2​)ab​cb​=g2∣∑a​ca​Ta​ϕ0​∣2≥0.
(6)
Its kernel consists exactly of the unbroken generators. With g=0, the rank is r, so exactly dimG−dimH gauge-field combinations become massive. Each removed scalar Goldstone mode supplies the extra longitudinal polarization of a massive gauge boson.
The remaining physical scalar fluctuations lie in the orthogonal complement of the orbit tangent space. They have no massless modes if K is positive definite on that complement. The unconditional absence of massless scalars does not follow from the printed assumptions. For a concrete counterexample, take a real doublet, G=SO(2) and
V(ϕ)=λ(ϕ12​+ϕ22​−v2)4,λ>0,v>0,ϕ0​=(v,0).
(7)
Its entire minimum set is one circle orbit, as required, but its Hessian matrix vanishes at the minimum. Gauging the rotation gives one vector of squared mass g2v2 and removes the angular scalar. The radial scalar remains massless at quadratic order, since its potential starts at fourth order in the radial fluctuation. This is an accidental massless radial mode beyond Goldstone modes. Thus the intended no-massless-scalar conclusion needs nonzero transverse curvature.
In the Standard Model, the Higgs doublet contains four real components and breaks SU(2)L​×U(1)Y​ to U(1)em​. Three Goldstone bosons become the longitudinal components of W± and Z, while the photon remains massless. The remaining radial Higgs boson is massive for the ordinary quartic potential with positive curvature. With ⟨H⟩=(0,vEW​/2​)T,
mW​=gvEW​/2,mZ​=vEW​g2+g′2​/2,mh2​=2λvEW2​.
(8)
The colour gauge group is unbroken. This is the Higgs mechanism, with the generic curvature condition realized by the standard Higgs potential.

Use the current convention in the question, whose vertex prefactor is g/(2cosθW​). The neutral-current vector and axial couplings are then
cV​=T3​−2Qsin2θW​,cA​=T3​.
(9)
For a charged lepton, T3​=−1/2, Q=−1, giving
cV​=2sin2θW​−21​,cA​=−21​.​
(10)
For an active neutrino, T3​=1/2, Q=0, so cV​=cA​=1/2. The right-handed weak singlet contribution is purely vectorlike in the charge term; combining it with the left-handed contribution gives precisely this Dirac-current form. These coefficients would be halved if the vertex prefactor were g/cosθW​ instead.
Let p=k+k′ be the Z boson momentum and k,k′ the outgoing massless lepton momenta. The decay amplitude is
M=2cosθW​g​ϵμ​(p)uˉ(k)γμ(cV​−cA​γ5​)v(k′).
(11)
Use the fermion spin sums ∑uuˉ=k and ∑vvˉ=k′. Anticommutation of γ5​ with the Dirac gamma matrices gives the symmetric part of the trace,
Lsymμν​=4(cV2​+cA2​)[kμk′ν+kνk′μ−gμνk⋅k′].
(12)
The vector-axial cross term is antisymmetric in μ,ν, and hence drops out of the massive vector polarization sum. The massless equations also give pμ​Lμν=0: in the symmetric part, the first two contractions sum to pνk⋅k′, canceling the last; the antisymmetric term vanishes because p=k+k′. The longitudinal pμ​pν​/mZ2​ term therefore makes no contribution.
Since 2k⋅k′=mZ2​, contraction with −gμν​ gives 4(cV2​+cA2​)mZ2​. Thus the massless fermion Z decay spin sum, including all three initial polarizations, is
Z,ℓ,ℓˉ mspins∑​∣M∣2=cos2θW​g2mZ2​​(cV2​+cA2​).​
(13)
To use this fully summed expression for the decay width of one unpolarized Z boson, apply the massive-vector spin average, dividing by three. The width of each fixed polarization is the same after integration over all directions, so that procedure also gives the physical single-particle width. Omitting the division would sum the widths of three initial states.
For two massless distinguishable final particles, the two-body Lorentz-invariant phase space is
dΦ2​=32π2dΩ​,∫dΦ2​=8π1​.
(14)
Consequently
Γ=2mZ​1​31​∑∣M∣28π1​=48πcos2θW​g2mZ​​(cV2​+cA2​).
(15)
The tree-level electroweak symmetry breaking relation is mW​=mZ​cosθW​. Combining it with the Fermi constant normalization gives
ΓZ→ℓℓˉ​=62​πGF​mZ3​​(cV2​+cA2​).​
(16)
For an active neutrino, the vertex itself projects out inactive chiralities, so no additional helicity factor is needed. This is the tree-level massless leptonic Z decay width; lepton masses and radiative corrections have been excluded.

First fix the photon momentum convention. With Q2=−q2>0, ν=P⋅q>0 and x=Q2/(2ν)>0, an absorbed photon gives final momentum pX​=P+q. This is the physical momentum support of a deep-inelastic hadronic tensor. The original PDF instead prints δ(4)(P−pX​−q) while keeping that positive-x convention. Taken literally, it would require
pX2​=(P−q)2=M2−Q2(1+1/x)<0
(17)
in the Bjorken scaling limit, leaving no physical final states. The sign is therefore inconsistent with the requested nonzero deep-inelastic structure functions. Below, use the physically consistent absorption delta function δ(4)(P+q−pX​). Alternatively all photon-momentum and scaling-variable conventions would have to be reversed together.
Work in the leading parton model: the target is unpolarized; scattering is incoherent from massless collinear quarks and antiquarks; intrinsic transverse momentum and target-mass effects are neglected. The parton distribution functions count constituents and include their colour multiplicity. At this order, neglect strong radiative corrections and the resulting logarithmic scale evolution.
Let a quark carry momentum p=ξP, using a lightlike reference P at leading order, and let r=p+q. The spin-averaged electromagnetic trace is
21​tr(pγμrγν)=2[pμrν+pνrμ−gμνp⋅r].
(18)
Integrating its one-particle final Lorentz-invariant phase space, with the 1/(4π) tensor normalization, supplies δ+​(r2)/2. Thus the spin-averaged electromagnetic parton tensor is
wfμν​=Qf2​δ+​(r2)[pμrν+pνrμ−gμνp⋅r].
(19)
On physical support, r2=0 gives ξ=x and p⋅q=Q2/2. Define the transverse tensors
tμν=−gμν+qμqν/q2,Pμ=Pμ−(ν/q2)qμ.
(20)
The bracket in wf​ becomes
2Q2​tμν+2ξ2PμPν.
(21)
This follows by writing p=ξP−q/2 on shell and expanding; it also verifies transversality to q.
The correct parton convolution normalization for a hadronic tensor is
Wμν=∑f​∫01​ξdξ​[qf​(ξ)+qˉ​f​(ξ)]wfμν​(ξP,q).
(22)
The 1/ξ converts number weighting into covariant target-state normalization: a parton response per constituent is normalized by 2p0, whereas the hadron tensor uses 2P0, and P0/p0=1/ξ. Antiquark charge changes sign but its square and symmetric spin trace are the same.
Use the parton on-shell delta identity,
δ(r2)=δ(2ν(ξ−x))=2νδ(ξ−x)​.
(23)
Matching the two tensor coefficients now gives
F1​=W1​=21​f∑​Qf2​[qf​(x)+qˉ​f​(x)],F2​=νW2​=xf∑​Qf2​[qf​(x)+qˉ​f​(x)].​
(24)
In particular F2​=2xF1​, the Callan-Gross relation for spin-one-half partons. Scaling here means the naive leading approximation; full Quantum chromodynamics produces scale-dependent distributions and corrections.
Each quark contributes +1 and each antiquark contributes −1 to its flavour-vector charge. Sea pairs therefore cancel in the quark number sum rule:
∫01​[qf​(x)−qˉ​f​(x)]dx=Nf​.​
(25)
Here Nf​ is the net flavour number, not the total number including sea pairs. This condition by itself fixes no antiquark distribution.
Under the separately imposed valence-only approximation, qˉ​f​=0 and only up/down distributions remain. The proton has (Nu​,Nd​)=(2,1) and the neutron has (1,2). With Qu​=2/3, Qd​=−1/3, the valence-only proton and neutron structure-function moments are
∫01​xF2p​(x)​dx=94​(2)+91​(1)=1,∫01​xF2n​(x)​dx=94​(1)+91​(2)=32​.​
(26)
No extra factor of three belongs here: colour is already included in the constituent distributions and the flavour counts. These are conditional valence-model moments, not unrestricted asymptotic statements in a theory whose evolution generates a sea.

Use the electroweak hypercharge normalization Q=T3​+Y, rather than Q=T3​+Y/2. In the latter convention all tabulated hypercharges would be doubled. The electroweak representation and hypercharge table for one minimal Standard Model generation is
fieldQL​=(uL​,dL​)TuR​dR​LL​=(νL​,eL​)TeR​​SU(3)colour​33311​SU(2)T​21121​Y1/62/3−1/3−1/2−1​Q(2/3,−1/3)2/3−1/3(0,−1)−1​​
(27)
The quarks are colour triplets, the leptons are colour singlets, and all listed right-handed fields are weak singlets. A sterile right-handed neutrino, if added, has representation (1,1)0​ and changes none of the anomaly traces.
On each left weak doublet, T3​=diag(1/2,−1/2). The electric charges are therefore 1/6±1/2=(2/3,−1/3) for QL​ and −1/2±1/2=(0,−1) for LL​. On each right singlet T3​=0, so its electric charge equals its hypercharge. These are the observed quark and lepton charge assignments.
For Standard Model anomaly cancellation, traces must include both components of each doublet and all three colours. Every right weak singlet has T32​=0, giving
trR​(T32​Y)=0.​
(28)
On the left doublets, T32​=I/4, while trL​T3​=0 separately in each doublet. Consequently
trL​(T32​Y)=41​trL​Y=41​trL​Q.​
(29)
The linear trace is
trL​Y=3⋅2⋅61​+2(−21​)=1−1=0.
(30)
Equivalently, trL​Q=3(2/3−1/3)+(0−1)=0. This proves cancellation of the mixed weak-hypercharge gauge anomaly within one generation.
The one-generation hypercharge anomaly traces for the cubic condition are
trL​Y3trR​Y3​=6(61​)3+2(−21​)3=361​−41​=−92​,=3(32​)3+3(−31​)3+(−1)3=98​−91​−1=−92​.​
(31)
Thus both required anomaly differences vanish generation by generation. The right-handed subtraction is important: counting all physical fields with the same chirality sign would not give the cubic cancellation.

For massless up and down quarks, the Quantum chromodynamics kinetic term splits into two independent chirality sectors,
Lq​=iqˉ​L​γμDμ​qL​+iqˉ​R​γμDμ​qR​.
(32)
The colour covariant derivative acts identically on both flavours and commutes with constant flavour matrices. Independent transformations qR​↦AqR​, qL​↦BqL​, with A,B∈SU(2), therefore leave the action invariant. This is chiral symmetry. The non-singlet axial transformations have traceless flavour generators, so they are not broken by the colour axial anomaly; the anomalous axial U(1) is not being included in this symmetry.
For Vij​=qˉ​Lj​qRi​, transforming each index gives
V↦AVB†=AVB−1.​
(33)
The nonzero condensate ⟨V⟩=−vI is unchanged precisely when A=B. Thus chiral symmetry breaking leaves the diagonal vector subgroup SU(2)V​. Three of the six generators are broken, producing three massless Goldstone bosons in the exact massless limit. They are the pions, represented at low energy by the coset coordinate U∈SU(2).
The uniqueness of the two-derivative two-flavour chiral action follows from the symmetry, rather than merely from guessing an invariant. The chiral group acts transitively on U, so every derivative-free invariant is constant. At U=I, the stabilizer acts on the three tangent directions by conjugation, the irreducible rotation representation. Its invariant symmetric quadratic form is a single multiple of δab​. Transporting that form around the group gives a unique invariant two-derivative metric. Equivalently, the Maurer-Cartan form U†∂μ​U is traceless, so an independent double-trace term vanishes; the remaining contracted single trace is the same metric. Lorentz invariance supplies the spacetime contraction. Up to total derivatives, a constant term and its overall coefficient, the massless action is therefore
Lπ​=4F2​tr(∂μU†∂μ​U).​
(34)
The positive coefficient is conveniently expressed through the pion decay constant F.
Use the Pauli matrices with tr(τa​τb​)=2δab​. Expanding the matrix exponential gives ∂μ​U=iτa​∂μ​πa​/F+⋯. Hence
Lπ​=21​∂μπ⋅∂μ​π+⋯,
(35)
so the three fields have canonical kinetic terms. There is no nonconstant invariant potential in the exact chiral theory, consistent with their masslessness.
For real diagonal M, the two terms in the mass trace are
tr(MV)=mu​uˉL​uR​+md​dˉL​dR​,tr(MV†)=mu​uˉR​uL​+md​dˉR​dL​.
(36)
Their sum therefore gives the usual Dirac mass term,
Lm​=−mu​uˉu−md​dˉd.
(37)
These terms explicitly break chiral symmetry. In the low-energy chiral perturbation theory prescription, V↦−vU yields
Lπ,m​=vtr[M(U+U†)].
(38)
The Pauli matrix algebra implies (π⋅τ)2=∣π∣2I. Thus
U+U†=2I−F2∣π∣2​I+O(π4),
(39)
and
Lπ,m​=2v(mu​+md​)−F2v(mu​+md​)​∣π∣2+O(π4).
(40)
Comparing with the canonically normalized scalar mass term gives the leading pion mass from a two-flavour condensate:
mπ1​2​=mπ2​2​=mπ3​2​=F22v(mu​+md​)​.​
(41)
Positive quark masses therefore give positive pion squared masses. Even if mu​=md​, this leading action depends only on their sum and gives equal masses for all three pions. Strong isospin-breaking effects beyond this order and electromagnetic effects can split them. In the condensate convention used here, ⟨uˉu⟩=⟨dˉd⟩=−2v, which also explains the factor of two in the mass relation. The massive pions are pseudo-Goldstone bosons, becoming massless as both quark masses tend to zero.
