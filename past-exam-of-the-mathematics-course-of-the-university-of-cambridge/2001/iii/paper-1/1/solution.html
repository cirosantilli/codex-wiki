Identify the Lie algebra g with the tangent space Te​G. For X∈g, its left-invariant vector field is XL(g)=(dLg​)e​X. Let γX​ be its integral curve with γX​(0)=e. Uniqueness of integral curves and left invariance give γX​(s+t)=γX​(s)γX​(t) wherever initially defined. Repetition of a local curve extends it to all real times. Thus γX​ is the unique one-parameter subgroup with derivative X at zero, and the Exponential map of a Lie group is defined by
expX=γX​(1),exp(tX)=γX​(t).​
(1)
Smooth dependence of solutions of differential equations on their initial data and parameters makes this map smooth.
The derivative at zero is particularly simple:
(dexp)0​(X)=dtd​​t=0​exp(tX)=X.
(2)
It is the identity linear map from g to Te​G. The inverse function theorem therefore proves that the Exponential map of a Lie group is a local diffeomorphism at zero, producing a local exponential chart. This proves the requested local assertion.
There is a necessary qualification to the other assertion: the image of every one-parameter subgroup lies in the identity component of a Lie group G∘. The correct statement without a connectedness hypothesis is
exp:(g,+)→G is a homomorphism⟺G∘ is abelian.​
(3)
Indeed, if exp is a group homomorphism, its image is an abelian subgroup. It contains an identity neighborhood by the local result. An open subgroup of a connected group is the entire group: every coset is open, so its complement is also open. Thus exp(g)=G∘, proving that this component is abelian.
Conversely, if G∘ is abelian, then exp(tX)exp(tY) is a one-parameter subgroup: its group law follows by commuting the factors. Its derivative at zero is X+Y. Uniqueness gives exp(t(X+Y))=exp(tX)exp(tY) and, at t=1, additivity. This proves the additivity of the Lie exponential map criterion. For connected G it is exactly the stated equivalence with an abelian group. For disconnected G the unrestricted claim is false: S3​×R is a nonabelian Lie group, but its exponential is the homomorphism x↦(e,x). The original PDF does not impose connectedness in that sentence, so that qualification cannot be omitted.
Now suppose G is connected and abelian, of dimension n. The exponential is a surjective group homomorphism, and its kernel Λ is discrete by local injectivity at zero. The induced map
Rn/Λ⟶G
(4)
is a bijective Lie group homomorphism and a local diffeomorphism, hence a Lie group isomorphism. To identify the quotient, take linearly independent elements λ1​,…,λb​∈Λ spanning the real linear span of Λ, and let Λ0​=∑i​Zλi​. Every coset of Λ0​ in Λ has a representative in the bounded fundamental parallelepiped of these vectors. A discrete additive subgroup has finite intersection with a compact set: otherwise differences of arbitrarily close elements would approach zero, contradicting discreteness at zero. Thus Λ/Λ0​ is finite.
It follows that Λ is a finitely generated torsion-free abelian group of rank b, hence has a Z-basis of b elements. These basis elements also span its b-dimensional real span and are linearly independent over R. Extending them to a real basis identifies Λ with Zb×{0}n−b. Consequently the classification of connected abelian Lie groups is
G≅Rn−b×(R/Z)b=Ra×Tb,a+b=n.​
(5)
The compact circle factors record the periods of the one-parameter subgroups.
