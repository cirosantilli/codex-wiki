Use coordinates 1,2,3 for paper, scissors and stone. Let XN take values in the lattice simplex SN​={(n1​,n2​,n3​)/N:ni​≥0, ∑i​ni​=N}. where J={e1​−e3​,e2​−e1​,e3​−e2​} and the three β functions are, in that order, x1​x3​,x2​x1​,x3​x2​. Its drift is precisely
F(x)=∑ℓ​ℓβℓ​(x)=(x1​(x3​−x2​), x2​(x1​−x3​), x3​(x2​−x1​)).

The polynomial drift is Lipschitz on the compact simplex, with some finite constant L. It preserves the sum of the coordinates, and each coordinate solves an equation of the form x˙i​=xi​hi​(x), so nonnegativity is preserved. Thus the ordinary differential equation stays in the simplex and exists for all time. The Gronwall inequality gives
supt≤T​∥XtN​−xt​∥≤eLT(∥X0N​−x0​∥+supt≤T​∥MtN​∥),

which proves the claim by Markov's inequality. This proves the required version directly, rather than invoking an unspecified approximation theorem.
For an interior trajectory, differentiating the logarithm of the product of numbers gives
dtd​log(x1​x2​x3​)=(x3​−x2​)+(x1​−x3​)+(x2​−x1​)=0.
(36)
The same identity for the product itself holds on the boundary, so x1​(t)x2​(t)x3​(t) is conserved. A strictly positive initial product keeps the deterministic trajectory away from the boundary. Except at (1/3,1/3,1/3) these interior trajectories are periodic: the strictly concave function ∑i​logxi​ has smooth compact level curves surrounding its unique maximum, and the vector field is nonzero and tangent to each such curve.
The finite Markov chain behaves differently at long times. For P(x)=x1​x2​x3​, the change in P during the first jump is
P(x+Ne1​−e3​​)−P(x)=x2​(Nx3​−x1​​−N21​).
(37)
The other two terms are cyclic permutations. Their first-order contributions cancel, leaving the exact product decay in a cyclic imitation chain identity
LN​P=−N3​P,EP(XtN​)=e−3t/NEP(X0N​).​
(38)
For N≥3, while all three counts are positive, their product is at least N−2, so P(XtN​)≥(N−2)/N3. A missing type cannot reappear. If τ is the first loss of a type, then
P(τ>t)≤N−2N3​e−3t/NEP(X0N​)⟶0.
(39)
Once only two types remain, one always wins, so its count increases until an absorbing monochromatic vertex is reached, almost surely in finite time. The cases of one or two initially present types follow the same argument without the first stage. Every finite chain eventually becomes monochromatic, whereas an interior deterministic solution retains its positive product. The fluid limit holds on each fixed finite interval; it does not justify exchanging the limits N→∞ and t→∞.
