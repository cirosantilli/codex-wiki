For a rational ℓ×n matrix A, write its columns as c1​,…,cn​. It is partition regular if every finite colouring of N admits positive integers x1​,…,xn​, all of one colour, with Ax=0. Repeated coordinates are allowed. The columns condition is an ordered partition
[n]=B1​⊔⋯⊔Bs​
(16)
into nonempty blocks such that
∑i∈B1​​ci​=0,∑i∈Bj​​ci​∈spanQ​{ci​:i∈B1​∪⋯∪Bj−1​}(j>1).
(17)
Rado's theorem states that a rational matrix is partition regular if and only if it satisfies the columns condition. We prove both implications.
For necessity, clear denominators so the columns are integral. For every disjoint pair of index sets I,B with B=∅ and
vB​=∑i∈B​ci​∈/spanQ​{ci​:i∈I},
(18)
choose a rational linear functional annihilating all columns in I but not vB​. Such a functional exists by extending a basis of the indicated linear span to include vB​, prescribing zero on the former and one on the latter. Clearing its denominators produces an integer-coefficient functional fI,B​ with fI,B​(vB​) a nonzero integer. There are only finitely many pairs I,B. Choose a prime number p dividing none of these nonzero evaluations; if there are no bad pairs, choose any prime.
Use the last nonzero digit coloring for this prime. Namely, write x=pνp​(x)u with p∤u and colour x by umodp∈{1,…,p−1}. By partition regularity, obtain a positive monochromatic solution. Let the distinct P-adic valuations of its coordinates be
v1​<⋯<vs​,
(19)
and put Bj​={i:νp​(xi​)=vj​}. All unit parts ui​ have a common nonzero residue t modulo p.
For a block Bj​, let I=B1​∪⋯∪Bj−1​. If its column sum were outside the earlier linear span, apply fI,Bj​​ to the equation ∑i​ci​xi​=0. Earlier-block contributions are exactly zero. Divide the remaining equation by pvj​ and reduce modulo p. Higher-valuation terms vanish, leaving
0≡tfI,Bj​​(∑i∈Bj​​ci​)(modp).
(20)
This contradicts the choice of p and t≡0. Consequently every block sum lies in the earlier linear span. For j=1 that span is {0}, so its sum is zero. This proves the columns condition by the finite separating-functional proof of the columns condition.
For sufficiency, suppose the blocks satisfy the columns condition. Choose rational coefficients αij​, for i∈B1​∪⋯∪Bj−1​, such that
∑i∈Bj​​ci​+∑i∈B1​∪⋯∪Bj−1​​αij​ci​=0(j>1).
(21)
Take a positive integer c clearing all their denominators and an integer p≥1 at least as large as every ∣cαij​∣. An M-p-c set with generators z1​,…,zs​ consists of all values
czh​+∑j>h​λj​zj​,λj​∈Z,∣λj​∣≤p,
(22)
with every such value positive. The generators are therefore chosen with czh​>p∑j>h​zj​. Positivity is part of the configuration, not achieved by deleting inconvenient values.
Use the permitted monochromatic m-p-c set theorem to obtain one such monochromatic set. For each i∈Bh​, put
xi​=czh​+∑j>h​cαij​zj​.
(23)
Every xi​ is a positive element of that set, because its non-leading coefficients are integers of magnitude at most p. In the sum ∑i​ci​xi​, the coefficient of z1​ is c∑i∈B1​​ci​=0, and the coefficient of each zj​ with j>1 is
c(∑i∈Bj​​ci​+∑i∈B1​∪⋯∪Bj−1​​αij​ci​)=0.
(24)
Thus Ax=0. This is an explicit Rado solution inside an m-p-c set and proves sufficiency.
For the Finite sums theorem, define
FS(x1​,…,xk​)={∑i∈I​xi​:∅=I⊆[k]}.
(25)
To deduce its monochromaticity from Rado's theorem, introduce variables zI​ for all nonempty I⊆[k] and equations
zI​−∑i∈I​z{i}​=0(∣I∣≥2).
(26)
Let Ak​ be their coefficient matrix, and partition its columns by Bj​={I:minI=j}. For a fixed j, the vector with I-coordinate 1 when j∈I, and 0 otherwise, solves this homogeneous system: in every row both sides count whether j belongs to its indexing set. Hence
∑I∈Bj​​cI​+∑minI<jj∈I​​cI​=0.
(27)
The first block sum is zero, and each later block sum lies in the linear span of earlier columns. This proves the columns partition for finite-sums systems. The positive monochromatic solution supplied by Rado's theorem has xi​=z{i}​>0, and its other coordinates are exactly all the nonempty finite sums. Therefore FS(x1​,…,xk​) is monochromatic. For k=1 a singleton is immediate. Equivalently, the full positive M-p-c set with m=k,p=c=1 directly contains every such sum, by taking its leading index to be the least index in I.
