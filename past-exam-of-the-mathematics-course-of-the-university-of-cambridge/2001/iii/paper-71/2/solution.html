The displayed matrices describe the orientation-preserving component SE(2) of the full Euclidean group E(2)=O(2)⋉R2. Both have the same Lie algebra, so this naming convention does not affect the calculation. Choose the translation generators P1​,P2​ and rotation generator J in the Lie algebra of this Matrix Lie group as
P1​=​000​000​100​​,P2​=​000​000​010​​,J=​010​−100​000​​.
(11)
Taking matrix commutators gives
[P1​,P2​]=0,[J,P1​]=P2​,[J,P2​]=−P1​.​
(12)
All remaining brackets follow from antisymmetry. The corresponding left-invariant vector fields in coordinates (x,y,ψ) are
L1​=cosψ∂x​+sinψ∂y​,L2​=−sinψ∂x​+cosψ∂y​,L3​=∂ψ​.
(13)
Their Lie brackets of vector fields obey the same relations. This also fixes which sign of the rotation generator is being used.
There is an important qualification to the printed connection prescription. For an arbitrary vector field Z and smooth function f, its literal right-hand side obeys
λ[Li​,fZ]=λLi​(f)Z+λf[Li​,Z].
(14)
An affine connection instead requires ∇Li​​(fZ)=Li​(f)Z+f∇Li​​Z. Thus the formula for arbitrary Z defines a connection only when λ=1. For example, at ψ=0, take i=1, f=x and Z=L1​: the erroneous formula gives λL1​, whereas the Leibniz rule requires L1​.
The intended one-parameter family is the bracket connection on a Lie group: prescribe the rule for left-invariant Z, then extend it by the Leibniz rule. If [Li​,Lj​]=cij​kLk​, this means
∇Li​​Lj​=λcij​kLk​,∇Li​​(zjLj​)=Li​(zj)Lj​+λzjcij​kLk​.
(15)
Extend linearly over functions in the first slot. This is a well-defined affine connection for every real λ. The following Ricci tensor calculation applies to this intended family; under the literal arbitrary-Z reading only its λ=1 member exists.
Use the curvature convention
R(X,Y)Z=∇X​∇Y​Z−∇Y​∇X​Z−∇[X,Y]​Z,Ric(X,Y)=tr(Z↦R(Z,X)Y).
(16)
For left-invariant vector fields the structure constants of a Lie algebra are constant. The Jacobi identity gives
R(X,Y)Z​=λ2([X,[Y,Z]]−[Y,[X,Z]])−λ[[X,Y],Z]=λ(λ−1)[[X,Y],Z].​
(17)
Writing A=λ(λ−1), the only nonzero curvature actions, apart from antisymmetry in the first two slots, are
R(L3​,L1​)L3​=AL1​,R(L3​,L2​)L3​=AL2​.
(18)
For instance, [[J,P1​],J]=[P2​,J]=P1​. Contracting the first and output slots gives Ric(L3​,L3​)=−2A, while every other component vanishes. Equivalently, [[Z,X],Y]=adY​adX​Z, so
Ric(X,Y)=AB(X,Y),B(X,Y)=tr(adX​adY​).
(19)
This is the Killing form. Here adJ​ rotates the two-dimensional translation space and vanishes on J, so B(J,J)=−2; translation generators give all other components zero. Therefore
(Ricij​)i,j=13​=​000​000​002λ(1−λ)​​.​
(20)
Since the coframe component dual to L3​ is dψ, the same Ricci tensor is 2λ(1−λ)dψ⊗dψ in these coordinates. Reversing the curvature convention reverses this sign.
At both λ=0 and λ=1, the full curvature vanishes. At λ=0, the left-invariant vector fields form a global parallel frame. At λ=1, every right-invariant vector field is parallel because left- and right-invariant vector fields commute; the connection is flat in that global parallel frame. Flatness does not imply zero torsion: for left-invariant arguments the torsion tensor is
T(X,Y)=∇X​Y−∇Y​X−[X,Y]=(2λ−1)[X,Y].
(21)
Thus the two endpoint connections are flat, with opposite nonzero torsion. For comparison, λ=1/2 is torsion-free but has nonzero curvature, with Ric33​=1/2. No metric has been specified, so these connections should not automatically be identified with a Levi-Civita connection.
