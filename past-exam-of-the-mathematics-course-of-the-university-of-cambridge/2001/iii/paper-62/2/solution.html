Use Minkowski spacetime with signature (+,−,…,−) and ℏ=1, with a vacuum time-ordering prescription. At a regulator, define the normalized generating functional
Z[J]=∫DϕeiS[ϕ]∫Dϕei(S[ϕ]+∫Jϕ)​,Z[0]=1.
(8)
The normalized vacuum correlation functions of a time-ordered product are
Gn​(x1​,…,xn​)=in1​δJ(x1​)⋯δJ(xn​)δnZ​​J=0​.
(9)
The connected generating functional is
W[J]=−ilogZ[J],Cn​=i1−nδJ(x1​)⋯δJ(xn​)δnW​​J=0​.​
(10)
The logarithm selects connected source diagrams, or equivalently cumulants. With the assumed vanishing one-point function, derivatives of Z=eiW give the centered four-point cumulant decomposition:
G1234​=C1234​+C12​C34​+C13​C24​+C14​C23​.​
(11)
In detail, four differentiations either act on one connected block or form one of three pair partitions. Contributions with blocks of size one vanish. If Wij​ and W1234​ denote source derivatives at zero, the same relation is G1234​=iW1234​−W12​W34​−W13​W24​−W14​W23​, since C2​=−iW2​ and C4​=iW4​.
Let φ(x)=δW/δJ(x) be the source-dependent mean field. On a locally invertible source-to-field branch, define the quantum effective action by the Minkowski Legendre transform
Γ[φ]=W[J]−∫ddxJ(x)φ(x).​
(12)
Its variation is δΓ=−∫Jδφ, so δΓ/δφ=−J. Differentiating the two inverse source-field maps yields the Minkowski inverse-Hessian relation for an effective action:
∫ddzδφ(x)δφ(z)δ2Γ​δJ(z)δJ(y)δ2W​=−δ(d)(x−y).​
(13)
Thus Γ(2)=−(W(2))−1. The inverse is understood at the regulator and on a nonsingular fluctuation sector; it is not an assertion that every source-field map is globally invertible.
For a free real scalar field, integrate by parts to write
S[ϕ]=21​ϕAϕ,A=−□−m2,Aϵ​=A+iϵ,
(14)
where repeated spacetime variables are integrated. Specify the propagator convention explicitly:
ΔF​(x−y)=∫(2π)dddp​p2−m2+i0ie−ip(x−y)​,ΔF​=iAϵ−1​.
(15)
Completing the Gaussian path integral around ϕ=−Aϵ−1​J cancels the source-independent determinant between numerator and denominator. Therefore
Z[J]=exp[−2i​JAϵ−1​J],W[J]=−21​JAϵ−1​J=2i​JΔF​J.​
(16)
The positive imaginary term in Aϵ​ damps the oscillatory Gaussian. Together with vacuum projection at the time boundaries, it selects the Feynman poles and the time-ordered, in-out correlation function rather than a retarded inverse. It can also be obtained by continuation from the Euclidean vacuum integral. A denominator convention without the numerator i redistributes the displayed factors of i.
Finally φ=−Aϵ−1​J, hence J=−Aϵ​φ. Substitution into the Legendre transform gives the vacuum-normalized effective action of a free scalar field:
Γ[φ]=21​φAϵ​φ⟶S[φ](ϵ↓0).​
(17)
All connected functions beyond order two vanish and all proper vertices beyond the classical quadratic kernel vanish. Vacuum normalization fixes the additive constant; without it, the quantum effective action equals the classical action up to a field-independent determinant contribution.
