Write the Möbius transformation as
f(z)=cz+daz+b​,ad−bc=0.
(21)
A finite fixed point of a Möbius transformation satisfies
cz2+(d−a)z−b=0.
(22)
If three distinct points of the Riemann sphere are fixed, this quadratic is identically zero, with the usual interpretation when one point is infinity. Hence c=b=0 and d=a, so f(z)=z. A nonidentity transformation's fixed-point equation is a nonzero polynomial of degree at most two on the sphere, and therefore it has one repeated fixed point or two distinct fixed points.

A set is countable when it is finite or admits a bijection with a subset of N; equivalently, its elements can be listed in a sequence. The integers are countable, for example in the order 0,1,−1,2,−2,…. Enumerating pairs by diagonals, or applying the Cantor pairing function, then shows that N×Z is countable.
Suppose instead that the power set P(N) had an enumeration A1​,A2​,…. The Cantor diagonal argument forms
D={n∈N:n∈/An​}.
(41)
For every n, the set D differs from An​ at the element n, so it is absent from the alleged enumeration. Therefore
P(N) is uncountable​.
(42)

The set T is uncountable. Partition N into countably many infinite sets B1​,B2​,…, and choose bijections hj​:Bj​→Z. For every subset A⊆N, define a permutation fA​ separately on each block by
