For subgroups H,K≤G and an H-representation W, Mackey restriction formula is
ResKG​IndHG​W≅⨁x∈K\G/H​IndK∩xHx−1K​ResK∩xHx−1xHx−1​(xW).
(88)
Frobenius reciprocity states
⟨IndHG​χ,ψ⟩G​=⟨χ,ResHG​ψ⟩H​.
(89)
Applying these with K=H gives
⟨IndHG​χ,IndHG​χ⟩G​=∑x∈H\G/H​⟨ResH∩xHx−1H​χ,ResH∩xHx−1xHx−1​(xχ)⟩.
(90)
The identity double coset contributes one exactly when W is irreducible, and every summand is a nonnegative integer. This proves Mackey irreducibility criterion: the induced representation is irreducible exactly when W is irreducible and every nonidentity double-coset summand vanishes.
Now take G=Sn​, H=Sn−1​, and x=(n−1 n)∈/H. Then
H∩xHx−1=Sn−2​,
(91)
the subgroup fixing both n−1 and n. Conjugation by x fixes every element of this subgroup, so the two representations appearing in the corresponding Mackey summand are both ResSn−2​Sn−1​​W. Their inner product is positive because this restriction is nonzero. Hence the Mackey irreducibility criterion fails whenever W is irreducible. If W is reducible, induction preserves its direct-sum decomposition, so the induced representation is again reducible. Therefore IndSn−1​Sn​​W is never irreducible for n≥2.
