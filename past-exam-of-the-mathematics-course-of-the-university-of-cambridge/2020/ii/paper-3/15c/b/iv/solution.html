Now let z=0n and choose one representative x from each pair {x,x⊕z}. Since f(x)=f(x⊕z) and
(−1)(x⊕z)⋅y=(−1)x⋅y(−1)z⋅y,
(72)
the unnormalized second-register amplitude for outcome y is
∑pairs [x]​(−1)x⋅y(1+(−1)z⋅y)∣f(x)⟩.
(73)
If y⋅z=1, every paired contribution cancels, so the probability is zero. If y⋅z=0, every coefficient has magnitude two. The 2n−1 distinct pair values give orthogonal states, so the squared norm before the overall 2−n factor is 4⋅2n−1=2n+1. The probability is therefore
2−2n2n+1=2−(n−1).
(74)
Thus one run samples uniformly from the 2n−1 strings in the orthogonal complement over the binary field {y:y⋅z=0}, which is the sampling step in Simon's algorithm.
