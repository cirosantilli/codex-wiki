Write ξ=V1​, η=ϕ, and uj​=dju/dxj. On the jet space of a scalar ordinary differential equation, define
η(0)=η,η(j)=Dx​η(j−1)−uj​Dx​ξ,
(212)
where the total derivative operator is
Dx​=∂x​+u1​∂u​+u2​∂u1​​+⋯.

The order-N prolongation of a vector field is
pr(N)V=ξ∂x​+η∂u​+j=1∑N​η(j)∂uj​​​.

In the coordinates (x,u), the given action of the special orthogonal group is
x=xcoss+usins,u=ucoss−xsins.
(215)
Differentiating at s=0 gives the infinitesimal generator of a Lie point symmetry
V=u∂x​−x∂u​,
(216)
so ξ=u and η=−x. The recursive formula gives
η(1)=Dx​(−x)−ux​Dx​u=−1−ux2​
(217)

η(2)=Dx​(−1−ux2​)−uxx​Dx​u=−3ux​uxx​.
(218)
Therefore the second prolongation of the rotation generator for plane graphs is
pr(2)V=u∂x​−x∂u​−(1+ux2​)∂ux​​−3ux​uxx​∂uxx​​​.
(219)
For Δ0​=uxx​,
pr(2)V(Δ0​)=−3ux​uxx​,
(220)
which vanishes when Δ0​=0. For
Δ1​=uxx​−(1+ux2​)3/2,
(221)
we obtain
pr(2)V(Δ1​)​=−3ux​uxx​−3ux​(1+ux2​)1/2[−(1+ux2​)]=−3ux​Δ1​,​
(222)
which likewise vanishes on the equation. This is the infinitesimal invariance criterion for both equations.
Geometrically, uxx​=0 describes straight lines. The signed curvature of a plane graph is
κ=(1+ux2​)3/2uxx​​,
(223)
so the second equation says κ=1 and describes consistently oriented arcs of unit circles. Rotation preserves straight lines, circles, and signed curvature, explaining both invariances.
