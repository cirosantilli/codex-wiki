For nonadjacent vertices a,b, the local connectivity κ(a,b;G) is the minimum cardinality of an a-b vertex separator: a set S⊆V(G)∖{a,b} such that a and b lie in different components of G−S.
To prove the vertex form of the Menger theorem, replace every vertex v∈/{a,b} by an arc vin​→vout​ of capacity one. Replace every undirected edge uv by directed arcs from uout​ to vin​ and conversely, each of capacity larger than ∣V(G)∣; leave a,b unsplit. An integral a-b flow of value k decomposes into k paths, and the unit-capacity vertex arcs force the corresponding graph paths to be internally vertex-disjoint paths. Conversely, k such paths give an integral flow of value k.
Because a and b are nonadjacent, any finite minimum cut consists entirely of unit-capacity vertex arcs. These arcs correspond exactly to an a-b vertex separator of the same size. The max-flow min-cut theorem, whose augmenting-path proof also guarantees an integral maximum flow for integral capacities, therefore gives
max{number of internally vertex-disjoint a-b paths}=κ(a,b;G).
(106)
In particular, G contains the required set of κ(a,b;G) paths.
