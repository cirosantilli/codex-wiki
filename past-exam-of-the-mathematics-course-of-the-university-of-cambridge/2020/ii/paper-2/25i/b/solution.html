The map α:R→α(R) is a local arc-length coordinate and, when the image is closed, a covering parametrization. The restriction of ϕ to the one-dimensional submanifold α(R) therefore lifts to a map h:R→R satisfying
ϕ(α(s))=α(h(s)).
(177)
Both curves have unit speed and ϕ is an isometry, so ∣h′(s)∣=1. Continuity makes the sign constant on the connected domain, hence h′(s)=±1 throughout and
ϕ(α(s))=α(±s+σ0​)​
(178)
for some constant σ0​.
A proper Euclidean motion of Euclidean three-space preserves both curvature and torsion. Reversal of an arc-length parameter also preserves them: in the determinant formula for torsion, both the first and third derivatives change sign. Comparing the two expressions for the transformed curve gives
κ(±s+σ0​)=κ(s)​,
(179)
and, wherever κ(s)>0,
τ(±s+σ0​)=τ(s)​.
(180)
If the sign is plus and σ0​=0, then ϕ fixes every point of the curve. A nonidentity proper Euclidean motion with any fixed points is a rotation, whose fixed set is its rotation axis. Thus α(R) lies on that axis and is a straight line.
