Introduce a propositional variable Px,i​ for every x∈X and i∈{1,…,100}. Form a theory containing, for every x, clauses saying that exactly one of
Px,1​,…,Px,100​
(103)
is true, and, for every y∈Lx​ and every i, the clause
¬(Px,i​∧Py,i​).
(104)
Every finite subset of these clauses mentions only finitely many elements, forming a finite set Y⊂X. The assumed coloring fY​ gives that finite subset a model. The propositional compactness theorem, equivalently the propositional consequence of first-order compactness, therefore gives a model of all the clauses.
For each x, let F(x) be the unique i for which Px,i​ is true. The edge clauses ensure that F(x)=F(y) whenever y∈Lx​. Hence
F:X⟶{1,…,100}​
(105)
has the required property.
