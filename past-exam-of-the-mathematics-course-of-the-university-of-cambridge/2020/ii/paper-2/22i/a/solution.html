The Baire category theorem says that a complete metric space cannot be a countable union of nowhere dense sets, equivalently that every countable intersection of open dense sets is dense. To prove it, let G1​,G2​,… be open dense subsets and let V be nonempty and open. Choose nested closed balls
B(xn​,rn​)⊂B(xn−1​,rn−1​)∩Gn​,0<rn​<2−n,
(153)
starting inside V∩G1​. The centres are a Cauchy sequence; completeness gives a limit lying in every ball, hence in V∩⋂n​Gn​. Thus the intersection is dense.
Choose a countable sequence qj​↑p with 1≤qj​<p. Since ℓq⊂ℓr for q<r, every ℓq with q<p lies in some ℓqj​. For integers m≥1, set
Ej,m​={x∈ℓp:∥x∥qj​​≤m}.
(154)
Pointwise convergence and Fatou lemma for series show that Ej,m​ is closed in ℓp. It has empty interior: inside any ℓp ball, add a sufficiently long finite block whose ℓp norm is tiny but whose ℓqj​ norm is larger than m. Therefore every Ej,m​ is nowhere dense. Since ℓp is a Banach space, Baire gives
ℓp=⋃j,m​Ej,m​=⋃1≤q<p​ℓq.
(155)
An explicit element of the difference is
xn​=n1/p{log(n+1)}2/p1​​.
(156)
Its pth powers form a convergent logarithmic series, whereas for every q<p the powers contain n−q/p with exponent below one and their series diverges. Hence x∈ℓp but x∈/ℓq for every q<p.
