Yes. The regularity of Lebesgue measure says that for every Lebesgue measurable set A⊆[0,1] and every ϵ>0, there is an open set O⊇A with
m(O∖A)<2ϵ​.
(192)
Write O∩[0,1] as a countable disjoint union of intervals. Since its total measure is at most one, a finite subunion B can be chosen with
m(O∖B)<2ϵ​.
(193)
Then B∈B and m(A△B)<ϵ. Hence every Lebesgue measurable set lies in C, while C⊆L[0,1]​ by definition. Therefore
C=L[0,1]​​.
(194)
