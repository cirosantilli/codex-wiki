The first Born approximation for a one-dimensional reflection coefficient follows by replacing the Jost solution inside its Volterra scattering equation by the free wave eikx:
R(k)=2ikϵ​∫−∞∞​e−2ikzq(z)dz+O(ϵ2)​.
(265)
To check the inverse problem, any shallow discrete state contributes only at O(ϵ2) pointwise, so to first order the Marchenko input is
F(s)=2π1​∫−∞∞​R(k)eiksdk.
(266)
Differentiating and inserting the Born approximation gives
F′(s)​=4πϵ​∫R​∫R​eik(s−2z)q(z)dzdk+O(ϵ2)=4ϵ​q(s/2)+O(ϵ2),​
(267)
where the second line is the Fourier inversion theorem. At first order the quadratic integral term in the Marchenko equation may be dropped, so
K(x,y)=−F(x+y)+O(ϵ2).
(268)
The reconstruction formula therefore returns
u(x)=−2dxd​K(x,x)=4F′(2x)+O(ϵ2)=ϵq(x)+O(ϵ2)​.
(269)
Thus direct and inverse scattering agree to first order.
