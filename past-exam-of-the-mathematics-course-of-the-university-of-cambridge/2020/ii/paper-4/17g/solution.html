Vizing theorem states that every finite simple graph of maximum degree Δ has
Δ≤χ′(G)≤Δ+1,
(73)
where χ′(G) is its edge chromatic number. The lower bound follows because all edges incident with a vertex of degree Δ need different colours.
For the upper bound, induct on the number of edges. Delete an edge xy and colour the remaining graph with the palette {1,…,Δ+1}. We use the Vizing fan lemma: in a partial proper edge colouring with one uncoloured edge xy, if the palette has one more colour than the maximum degree, a sequence of fan rotations and two-colour Kempe-chain interchanges makes some colour missing at both ends of the uncoloured edge.
Here is the fan argument. Build a maximal sequence y0​=y,y1​,…,yk​ of distinct neighbours of x such that, for i≥1, the colour on xyi​ is missing at an earlier fan vertex. Rotating an initial fan segment means moving each colour on xyi​ to the preceding edge where it is missing; this preserves properness and moves the uncoloured edge to the end of that segment. Choose a colour α missing at x and a colour β missing at yk​. On the maximal α-β alternating path from yk​, interchange α and β. If the path does not reach x, α is then missing at both x and yk​, so rotate the fan and colour its final uncoloured edge α. If it reaches x, its last edge at x has colour β. Maximality of the fan forces the other endpoint of that edge to be a fan vertex; rotate up to that vertex first. This breaks the alternating path before x, reducing to the preceding case. This proves the fan lemma and extends the colouring to xy, completing the induction.
For Kn,n​, label both vertex classes by Z/nZ and colour edge (i,j) by i+j. Each colour is a perfect matching, so this is an n-edge-colouring. Since Δ(Kn,n​)=n, χ′(Kn,n​)=n. If r=max{m,n}, embed Km,n​ into Kr,r​ and restrict its r-edge-colouring. The degree lower bound then gives
χ′(Km,n​)=max{m,n}​.
(74)
Finally, subdividing one edge of Kn,n​ produces n2+1 edges on 2n+1 vertices while keeping maximum degree n. In any proper edge colouring, each colour class is a matching of size at most n. An n-edge-colouring could therefore colour at most n2 edges, a contradiction. Thus χ′(G)>n, while Vizing theorem gives χ′(G)≤n+1, so
χ′(G)=Δ(G)+1=n+1​.
(75)
