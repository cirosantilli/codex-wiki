OurBigBook.com Words: 244
A family S⊆C(K) is equicontinuous if for every x∈K and ε>0 there is a neighborhood U of x such that
∣f(y)−f(x)∣<ε
(106)
for every y∈U and every f∈S. The Arzelà-Ascoli theorem says that, for a compact Hausdorff space K, a subset of C(K) has compact closure in the uniform norm if and only if it is equicontinuous and pointwise bounded. On compact K, equicontinuity and pointwise boundedness together imply uniform boundedness.
For necessity, a compact closure is totally bounded. Given ε>0, choose a finite ε/3-net f1​,…,fm​. The finitely many continuous functions fj​ are simultaneously continuous near each point, and comparison with a nearby fj​ proves equicontinuity of the whole family. Evaluation at any fixed point is a continuous map C(K)→R or C, so compactness also gives pointwise boundedness.
Conversely, equicontinuity gives, around each x∈K, a neighborhood on which every f∈S oscillates by less than ε/3. Compactness supplies a finite such cover with selected points x1​,…,xm​. Pointwise boundedness makes the set of vectors
{(f(x1​),…,f(xm​)):f∈S}
(107)
a bounded subset of a finite-dimensional normed vector space, so it has a finite ε/3-net. Choose one function of S for every nonempty cell of this net. If two functions have evaluation vectors in the same cell, then comparison at a selected xj​ and the two oscillation bounds show that their uniform distance is less than ε. Thus S is totally bounded. Since C(K) is a Banach space, its closure is complete; a complete totally bounded metric space is compact, proving the theorem.
