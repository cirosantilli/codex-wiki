Choose sets K,L,M of cardinalities κ,λ,μ. Their cardinal arithmetic operations are
κ+λ=∣K⊔L∣,κλ=∣K×L∣,κλ=∣KL∣,
(137)
where KL is the set of functions L→K. The relation κ≤λ means that there is an injective function K→L.
The currying law for cardinal exponentiation follows from the explicit bijection
(KL)M⟷KL×M,f⟼((l,m)↦f(m)(l)),
(138)
and proves
(κλ)μ=κλμ​.
(139)
Moreover, 2κ is the cardinality of the power set of a set of size κ, so Cantor theorem gives
2κ>κ​.
(140)
For completeness, identify each cardinal with its initial ordinal. Suppose that some infinite κ violates κ2=κ, and choose the least such cardinal. Well-order the pairs (α,β)∈κ2 first by max{α,β} and then lexicographically. Every proper initial segment is contained in γ2 together with finitely many boundary pieces for some γ<κ, and has cardinality below κ by minimality. The resulting well-order therefore has cardinality at most κ. The reverse inequality is immediate from α↦(α,0), contradicting the choice of κ. Thus the square of an infinite cardinal satisfies κ2=κ.
If ν=max{κ,λ}, monotonicity now gives
ν≤κ+λ≤ν+ν=ν,ν≤κλ≤ν2=ν.
(141)
Hence the sum and product of two infinite cardinals obey
κ+λ=κλ=max{κ,λ}​.
(142)
Assertion (i) can be false. For any infinite λ, take κ=(2λ)+. Then κλ≥κ>2λ, so
κλ=2λ is not always true​.
(143)
