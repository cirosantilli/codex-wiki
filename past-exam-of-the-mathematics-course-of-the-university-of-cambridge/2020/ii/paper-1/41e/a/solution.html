For a fixed shift s that is not an eigenvalue, inverse iteration is the power method applied to (A−sI)−1. Before normalization, the kth vector is proportional to
(A−sI)−kx(0)=∑i=1n​ci​(λi​−s)−kwi​.
(497)
If s<λ1​, factor out the first coefficient:
(A−sI)−kx(0)=c1​(λ1​−s)−k[w1​+∑i=2n​c1​ci​​(λi​−sλ1​−s​)kwi​].
(498)
Every ratio in parentheses lies strictly between zero and one. The bracket therefore tends to w1​, and normalization in the Euclidean norm gives
x(k)⟶sgn(c1​)w1​.​
(499)
This proves the stated convergence to w1​ or −w1​ and exhibits the convergence of fixed-shift inverse iteration rate.
Now suppose λm​<s<λm+1​. The closest eigenvalue to s is one of λm​ and λm+1​.
  • If s−λm​<λm+1​−s, the direction approaches wm​, but (λm​−s)−1<0, so consecutive iterates alternate signs.
  • If s−λm​>λm+1​−s, the iterates approach one fixed sign of wm+1​ because (λm+1​−s)−1>0.
  • At the midpoint, the two dominant inverse eigenvalues have equal modulus and opposite signs. Generic iterates alternate between the two normalized combinations proportional to cm​wm​+cm+1​wm+1​ and −cm​wm​+cm+1​wm+1​.
Finally, if λn​<s, the closest eigenvalue is λn​ and its inverse eigenvalue is negative. Thus the direction approaches the line spanned by wn​, while normalized vectors alternate between the two signs. These cases are the sign behavior of fixed-shift inverse iteration.
