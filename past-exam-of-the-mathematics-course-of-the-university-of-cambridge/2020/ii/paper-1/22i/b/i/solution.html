Write h(t)=t/(1+t) for t≥0. Every summand in d is nonnegative and at most 2−m, so the series converges. Symmetry is immediate. If d(x,y)=0, then ϕm​(x−y)=0 for every m. For arbitrary ϕ∈(ℓp)∗, choose a sequence of ϕm​ converging to ϕ in operator norm. Since x−y is fixed, ϕ(x−y)=0. The continuous dual space separates points, so x=y.
The function h is increasing and subadditive:
h(a+b)≤h(a)+h(b)(a,b≥0).
(216)
Combining this with the triangle inequality for each ∣ϕm​(x−y)∣ and summing proves the triangle inequality for d. Hence d is a metric on B.
Suppose first that x(n) converges weakly to x, meaning that
ϕ(x(n))→ϕ(x)for every ϕ∈(ℓp)∗.
(217)
For each fixed m, the mth summand of d(x(n),x) tends to zero. A finite-head and geometric-tail estimate, using that every summand is bounded by 2−m, then gives d(x(n),x)→0.
Conversely, if d(x(n),x)→0, each nonnegative summand tends to zero, and therefore
ϕm​(x(n)−x)→0
(218)
for every m. Given ϕ∈(ℓp)∗ and ε>0, choose m with ∥ϕ−ϕm​∥<ε. Since x(n),x∈B,
∣ϕ(x(n)−x)∣≤∣ϕm​(x(n)−x)∣+2ε.
(219)
Taking the limit superior and then letting ε↓0 proves weak convergence. Thus
ϕ(x(n))→ϕ(x) ∀ϕ∈(ℓp)∗⟺d(x(n),x)→0​.
(220)
The same approximation argument for neighbourhoods shows that d induces the weak topology on B, as in the metrization of the weak topology on a bounded set.
If q is conjugate to p, the given duality of sequence spaces applied twice gives
(ℓp)∗∗≅(ℓq)∗≅ℓp,
(221)
so ℓp is a reflexive Banach space. Its closed unit ball is weakly compact by the weak compactness characterization of reflexivity. Since d induces that topology on B,
(B,d) is a compact metric space​.
(222)
