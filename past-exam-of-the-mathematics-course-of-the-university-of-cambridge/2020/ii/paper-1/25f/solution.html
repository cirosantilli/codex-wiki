Suppose first that the affine variety V is irreducible. If fg∈I(V), then
V=(V∩V(f))∪(V∩V(g)).
(251)
Both pieces are Zariski closed in V. Irreducibility forces one to equal V, so either f∈I(V) or g∈I(V). Thus I(V) is a prime ideal.
Conversely, suppose that I(V) is prime and that V=A∪B with A,B closed in V. If both were proper, the Hilbert Nullstellensatz would give
I(V)⊊I(A),I(V)⊊I(B).
(252)
Choose f∈I(A)∖I(V) and g∈I(B)∖I(V). Their product vanishes on A∪B=V, so fg∈I(V), contradicting primality. Hence one piece equals V. This proves the criterion of irreducibility and prime vanishing ideal:
V is irreducible⟺I(V) is prime​.
(253)
For the semicubical parabola
C=V(y2−x3),
(254)
consider
θ:k[x,y]→k[t],θ(x)=t2,θ(y)=t3.
(255)
Certainly (y2−x3)⊆kerθ. Divide any polynomial by the monic polynomial y2−x3 as a polynomial in y; its remainder is a(x)y+b(x). If this remainder lies in kerθ, then
a(t2)t3+b(t2)=0.
(256)
The first summand contains only odd powers of t and the second only even powers, so both vanish and a=b=0. Therefore
kerθ=(y2−x3),k[C]≅k[t2,t3].
(257)
The latter is a subring of the integral domain k[t], hence is a domain. Thus (y2−x3) is prime and
C is irreducible​.
(258)
An affine variety of dimension d is smooth at p when its Zariski tangent space has dimension d; equivalently, the Jacobian criterion requires the Jacobian of its defining ideal to have rank n−d. For this plane curve, put F(x,y)=y2−x3. Its gradient is
∇F=(−3x2,2y).
(259)
At (0,0)∈C the gradient vanishes, so the tangent space is all of k2 and has dimension two rather than the curve dimension one. Hence the result on the coordinate ring and singularity of the semicubical parabola gives
C is not smooth; its origin is singular​.
(260)
