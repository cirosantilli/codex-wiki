The equilibrium points of a dynamical system form the three branch families
x=0,qquadx=±2μ​(μ≥0),qquadx=±μ−a​(μ≥a).
(351)
For a simple equilibrium, fixed point stability for an autonomous differential equation is determined by the sign of fx​. On the three families,
branchx=0x2=2μx2=μ−a​fx​−2μ(μ−a)−4μ(μ+a)2(μ−a)(μ+a).​​
(352)
Thus solid branches in the figure are stable and dashed branches are unstable.
If a<0, the pair x=±μ−a​ emerges stably from x=0 at μ=a in a pitchfork bifurcation. At μ=0, the pair x=±2μ​ meets x=0 with unstable nonzero branches; after reversing the parameter orientation this is a subcritical pitchfork bifurcation. At μ=−a, the two positive branches cross and exchange stability, as do the two negative branches, giving two simultaneous transcritical bifurcations at x=±−2a​.
If a=0, every branch meets at (μ,x)=(0,0). For μ<0 only the stable trivial branch exists. For μ>0, x=0 is stable, the inner branches x=±μ​ are unstable, and the outer branches x=±2μ​ are stable. This is a degenerate fifth-order bifurcation rather than a generic pitchfork.
If a>0, a supercritical pitchfork bifurcation occurs at μ=0: the trivial branch loses stability and the stable branches x=±2μ​ emerge. At μ=a, the unstable branches x=±μ−a​ meet the trivial branch, which regains stability; this is the parameter-reversed form of a subcritical pitchfork bifurcation.
These cases comprise the bifurcations of the 2020 Cambridge quintic system.
