Let X have regular density pθ​, let
Sθ​(X)=∂θ∂​logpθ​(X)
(306)
be its score function, and let
I(θ)=Eθ​[Sθ​(X)2]
(307)
be its Fisher information. Regularity gives the mean-zero score identity Eθ​Sθ​=0.
Suppose an estimator T has mean Eθ​T=ψ(θ). Differentiating under the integral gives
ψ′(θ)=Eθ​[TSθ​]=Covθ​(T,Sθ​).
(308)
The Cauchy-Schwarz inequality therefore yields
ψ′(θ)2≤Varθ​(T)I(θ),
(309)
and hence the Cramer-Rao bound
Varθ​(T)≥I(θ)ψ′(θ)2​​.
(310)
In particular, an unbiased estimator of θ has variance at least 1/I(θ). For an independent sample, the information is the sum of the individual informations.
In a decision problem with risk of a decision rule R(θ,δ), a rule δ∗ is minimax when
θ∈Θsup​R(θ,δ∗)=δinf​θ∈Θsup​R(θ,δ).​
(311)
Now let X1​,…,Xn​ be independent N(θ,1) variables with θ≥0, under squared-error loss. The sample mean is unbiased with variance 1/n, so
R(θ,Xn​)=Eθ​(Xn​−θ)2=n1​
(312)
for every θ≥0. Thus the minimax value is at most 1/n.
For the matching lower bound, choose a continuously differentiable density π on (0,1) that vanishes at both endpoints and has finite prior information
J=∫01​π(u)π′(u)2​du;
(313)
for example, π(u)=30u2(1−u)2. For L>0, define the prior
πL​(θ)=L1​π(θ/L),0<θ<L.
(314)
It is supported inside the parameter space, and its information is J/L2.
Here the likelihood score is
Sθ​(X)=∑i=1n​(Xi​−θ),
(315)
whose Fisher information is n. For any decision rule δ(X), combine it with the prior score to form the joint score
U=Sθ​(X)+πL​(θ)πL′​(θ)​.
(316)
Integration by parts in θ, with no boundary term because πL​ vanishes there, gives
EπL​​[(δ(X)−θ)U]=1.
(317)
The likelihood score has conditional mean zero, so its cross term with the prior score vanishes and
EπL​​U2=n+L2J​.
(318)
Cauchy--Schwarz now proves the Van Trees inequality in this case:
∫0L​R(θ,δ)πL​(θ)dθ≥n+J/L21​.
(319)
The worst-case risk dominates every integrated risk, and therefore
supθ≥0​R(θ,δ)≥n+J/L21​.
(320)
Letting L→∞ shows that every rule has worst-case risk at least 1/n. Since Xn​ attains that value, the result on the minimax sample mean for a nonnegative normal location is
Xn​ is minimax on [0,∞)​.
