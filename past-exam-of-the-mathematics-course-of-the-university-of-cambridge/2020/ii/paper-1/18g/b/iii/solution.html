Let R be the set of these roots. The Frobenius endomorphism and the identities
(x+y)qn=xqn+yqn,(xy)qn=xqnyqn
(169)
show that R is closed under addition and multiplication. It is also closed under additive inverses and, for x=0, inversion. Thus R is a finite field. Since h splits into qn distinct linear factors, ∣R∣=qn. The field generated by all roots is therefore R itself, so L=R and
q[L:K]=∣L∣=qn.

Hence the polynomial characterization of a finite field gives
[L:K]=n​.

[K(α):K]=n,

so K(α) has qn elements. Its nonzero elements form a finite group under multiplication, of order qn−1; hence αqn=α. Therefore α is a root of Xqn−X, and its minimal polynomial f divides every polynomial over K that vanishes at α. Thus the irreducible factors of a finite-field Frobenius polynomial satisfy
