An alphabet Σ is a finite nonempty set of symbols. A word over an alphabet is a finite sequence of symbols from Σ, including the empty word ϵ; the set of all words is Σ∗. A formal language over Σ is any subset L⊆Σ∗.
A regular expression is defined recursively from ∅, ϵ, and the individual symbols a∈Σ by the operations of finite union, concatenation, and Kleene star. Its language is defined by
L(∅)=∅,L(ϵ)={ϵ},L(a)={a},

L(R∣S)=L(R)∪L(S),L(RS)=L(R)L(S),L(R∗)=L(R)∗.
(30)
There are only countably many regular expressions: each is a finite word over a finite collection of symbols and punctuation. On the other hand, for every nonempty finite alphabet, Σ∗ is countably infinite, so its power set P(Σ∗) is uncountable by the Cantor theorem. Thus there are uncountably many languages but only countably many languages denoted by regular expressions. Consequently
some languages over Σ are not regular​.
