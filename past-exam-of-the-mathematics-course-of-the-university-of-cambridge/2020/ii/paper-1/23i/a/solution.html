For f∈L∞(Rn) and g∈L1(Rn), define their convolution by
(f∗g)(x)=∫Rn​f(y)g(x−y)dy.
(225)
It is bounded because
∣(f∗g)(x)∣≤∥f∥∞​∥g∥1​.
(226)
If τh​g(z)=g(z+h), then
∣(f∗g)(x+h)−(f∗g)(x)∣≤∥f∥∞​∥τh​g−g∥1​.
(227)
Continuity of translation in L1 makes the right-hand side tend to zero with h, uniformly in x. Thus the convolution of L infinity and L1 functions is bounded and continuous.
Now let f=1A​ and g(x)=1A​(−x). Then
(f∗g)(z)=∫Rn​1A​(y)1A​(y−z)dy.
(228)
At zero this equals ∣A∣>0. By continuity it remains positive throughout some open neighbourhood U of zero. Positivity at z means that some y satisfies y∈A and y−z∈A, so z∈A−A. Hence
U⊆A−A​.
(229)
This is the Steinhaus theorem.
The conclusion also holds when ∣A∣=∞. Since Rn is the union of bounded balls, some measurable subset A∩B(0,R) has finite positive measure. Its difference set lies inside A−A and already contains a neighbourhood of zero.
