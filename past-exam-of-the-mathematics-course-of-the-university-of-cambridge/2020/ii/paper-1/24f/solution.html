Let F:X→Y be a nonconstant holomorphic map of degree d between compact connected Riemann surfaces. At p∈X, let ep​ be the ramification index of a holomorphic map. Choose a triangulation of Y containing every branch value among its vertices, and lift it to X.
If the target triangulation has V vertices, E edges, and T triangles, then the lifted triangulation has dE edges and dT triangles, because no branch value lies in an edge or face interior. For a target vertex v, local degree counting gives
∑p∈F−1(v)​ep​=d.
(238)
Consequently the number of vertices above v is
∣F−1(v)∣=d−∑p∈F−1(v)​(ep​−1).
(239)
Writing R=∑p∈X​(ep​−1), the Euler characteristic is therefore
χ(X)=(dV−R)−dE+dT=dχ(Y)−R.
(240)
Since a compact orientable surface of genus g has Euler characteristic 2−2g, this Triangulation proof of the Riemann-Hurwitz formula gives
2−2gX​=d(2−2gY​)−∑p∈X​(ep​−1),
(241)
or equivalently
2gX​−2=d(2gY​−2)+p∈X∑​(ep​−1)​.
(242)
Now extend a cubic polynomial f:C→C to a degree-three holomorphic map
F:P1(C)→P1(C)
(243)
of the Riemann sphere. Both genera are zero, so Riemann--Hurwitz gives
∑p​(ep​−1)=2⋅3−2=4.
(244)
The point at infinity is totally ramified with e∞​=3, contributing two. Thus the finite points contribute exactly two. There are consequently two cases.
If there is one finite ramification point α, it has index three. Choose an affine source map h sending zero to α. Then
(f∘h)′(z)=cz2
(245)
for some c=0, and integration gives
f∘h(z)=3c​z3+d.
(246)
An affine target map subtracts d and rescales by 3/c, yielding z3.
Otherwise there are two distinct finite ramification points, each of index two. Choose an affine source map h sending −1 and 1 to them. The derivative of f∘h is then a nonzero multiple of z2−1:
(f∘h)′(z)=c(z2−1).
(247)
Hence
f∘h(z)=c(3z3​−z)+d.
(248)
The affine target map g(w)=(w−d)/c gives
g∘f∘h(z)=3z3​−z=z(3z2​−1).
(249)
Therefore the affine normal forms of a complex cubic polynomial are precisely
z3andz(z2/3−1)​.
(250)
