The Minkowski convex body theorem states that if Λ⊂Rn is a full lattice and C is a measurable, convex, centrally symmetric set with
vol(C)>2ncovol(Λ),

Here d≡3(mod4), so the ring of integers of a quadratic field is
OK​=Z[−d​].

Under its complex embedding it has real-lattice covolume d​. An integral ideal I has index N(I) in OK​, so its covolume is d​N(I). Apply Minkowski's theorem to the disk ∣z∣≤R, whose area is πR2. Taking R2 down to 4d​N(I)/π gives a nonzero α∈I with

Thus every ideal class has a bounded-norm representative. Only finitely many integral ideals have bounded norm, proving the finiteness of the ideal class group.
Now take K=Q(−22​). Its ring of integers is Z[−22​], its discriminant is −88, and

The polynomial X2+22 is irreducible modulo three and modulo five, so both primes are inert and there are no ideals of norm three or five. Suppose finally that y3=x2+22. If x were even, the right-hand side would be 2 modulo four, which is not a cube modulo four. Hence x and y are odd. In OK​,
(x+−22​)(x−−22​)=(y)3.

By unique factorization of ideals in a number field, coprimality implies
