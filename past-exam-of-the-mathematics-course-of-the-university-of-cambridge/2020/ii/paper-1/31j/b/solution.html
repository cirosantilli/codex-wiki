For z=(x,y) define the misclassification loss
fh​(z)=1{h(x)=y}​,
(338)
and take the loss class
F={fh​:h∈H}.​
(339)
Write Pf=Ef(Z) and Pn​f=n−1∑i=1n​f(Zi​), so the misclassification risk and empirical misclassification risk are R(h)=Pfh​ and R(h)=Pn​fh​.
Because h is an empirical risk minimizer, Pn​(fh​−fh∗​)≤0. Hence its excess risk obeys
R(h)−R(h∗)≤G(Z1:n​),G(Z1:n​)=suph∈H​(P−Pn​)(fh​−fh∗​).
(340)
Introduce an independent ghost sample and perform Rademacher symmetrization. The contribution involving the fixed function fh∗​ has zero expectation over the Rademacher signs, so each of the two independent sample terms has supremum expectation Rn​(F). Therefore
EG≤2Rn​(F).
(341)
Every difference fh​−fh∗​ takes values in [−1,1]. Replacing one observation can consequently change G by at most 2/n. Applying the Bounded differences inequality with ci​=2/n gives, except on an event of probability at most δ/2,
G≤EG+n2log(2/δ)​​.
(342)
Combining these inequalities proves, with probability at least 1−δ,
R(h)−R(h∗)≤2Rn​(F)+n2log(2/δ)​​.​
(343)
