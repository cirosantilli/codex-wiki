Take rightward velocities as positive just before the right-wall collision. The incoming particle velocity is v and the wall velocity is −V. Their total momentum divided by total mass gives the centre of mass velocity
vcm​=m+Mmv−MV​.​
(51)
In an elastic collision, relative velocity reverses, so the particle's signed outgoing velocity is vout​=2vcm​−v. For M>m it is negative, and its speed is
v′=M+m(M−m)v+2MV​.​
(52)
Consequently the exact change in speed is
v′−v=M+m2(MV−mv)​=2V−M+m2m​(v+V).
(53)
For m/M→0 with v,V fixed, this proves the requested leading increment v′−v≃2V. Reflection gives the same increment at the left wall.
This approximation is not uniform when v/V is allowed to become arbitrarily large. To neglect recoil relative to the increment 2V requires
Mm​Vv​≪1.
(54)
The inequalities m≪M and v≫V alone do not imply it: for example, m/M=10−3 and v/V=103 give an exact zero speed increment. The subsequent slowly moving-wall approximation therefore uses the consistent joint range 1≪v/V≪M/m, or treats the walls as effectively prescribed massive reflectors. The assumed slow change of wall speed over many impacts embodies the same negligible-recoil requirement.
Within this approximation, the separation derivative is the right-wall velocity minus the left-wall velocity:
ℓ˙=−2V,Δℓ≃−2VΔt.
(55)
For a time window containing many crossings but little separation change, ℓ/v≪Δt≪ℓ/V, the flight time between successive wall collisions is approximately ℓ/v. Thus their number and the cumulative speed change are
Ncoll​≃ℓvΔt​,Δv≃2VNcoll​=ℓ2VvΔt​=−ℓΔℓ​v.
(56)
Passing to the averaged differential equation gives
vv˙​=−ℓℓ˙​,dtd​log(vℓ)≃0.
(57)
Hence the particle-speed invariant between slowly approaching walls is
v(t)ℓ(t)≃v(t0​)ℓ(t0​),v∝ℓ−1.​
(58)
It is an adiabatic invariant for the rapid elastic bouncing motion while the separation and wall velocities vary slowly on the flight-time scale.
