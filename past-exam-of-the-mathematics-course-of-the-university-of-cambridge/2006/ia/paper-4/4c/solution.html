Write v=x˙. The simple pendulum becomes x˙=v, v˙=−sinx. Its equilibria are (kπ,0). The linearization is (0−cos(kπ)​10​). For even k its eigenvalues are ±i, and for odd k they are ±1. The conserved energy
H=2v2​+1−cosx
(13)
has a strict local minimum at each even multiple of π, proving nonlinear Lyapunov stability there. The odd multiples are saddles and are unstable. Therefore
(2kπ,0) are stable centres;((2k+1)π,0) are unstable saddles.​
(14)
The stable centres are not asymptotically stable because energy is conserved.
The saddle energy is H=2, so the separatrix is determined by
2x˙2​+1−cosx=2,x˙=±2∣cos(x/2)∣.​
(15)
Energy levels 0<H<2 form closed libration curves around the centres; levels H>2 have velocity of constant sign and describe rotations. The separatrices join neighboring saddles in the unwrapped (x,v) plane. On the angle cylinder, where x is identified modulo 2π, they are homoclinic to the single saddle.
At x=0 the positive separatrix has x˙=2. Before reaching the neighboring saddle at x=π, the velocity is 2cos(x/2)>0, so the time to angle a<π is
t(a)=∫0a​2cos(x/2)dx​=log(sec(a/2)+tan(a/2)).
(16)
As a↑π, cos(x/2)∼(π−x)/2. The integrand is therefore asymptotic to 1/(π−x), whose improper integral diverges logarithmically. Thus the pendulum separatrix approach takes infinite time:
T=∫0π​2cos(x/2)dx​=∞.​
(17)
Starting with velocity −2 gives the reflected approach to x=−π and the same infinite time. The velocity tends to zero but never reaches zero at a finite time.
