The series ∑n≥1​an​ converges to A when its partial sums sn​ have limit of a sequence A: for every ε>0, there is N such that ∣sn​−A∣<ε for all n≥N.
Its averages are the Cesaro means of the sequence of partial sums. To prove their convergence, choose N with ∣sk​−A∣<ε/2 for k≥N. Then
​n1​∑k=1n​sk​−A​≤n1​∑k=1N−1​∣sk​−A∣+nn−N+1​2ε​.
(8)
The finite first sum divided by n tends to zero. This proves the Cesaro theorem for convergent sequences in this setting, and
A=A.​
(9)

Use these standard facts about a power series: it is absolutely convergent at every point strictly inside its radius of convergence and divergent at every point strictly outside it. Also, the triangle inequality implies that a sum or difference of two absolutely convergent series is absolutely convergent.
Let T be the radius of convergence of the coefficientwise sum. For ∣z∣<min(R,S),
∑n≥0​∣(an​+bn​)zn∣≤∑n≥0​∣an​zn∣+∑n≥0​∣bn​zn∣<∞.
(11)
Hence T≥min(R,S).
Suppose R<S. If T>R, choose a finite ρ with R<ρ<min(T,S) and choose z of complex modulus ρ. The sum series and the bn​ series both converge absolutely there, so
∑n≥0​∣an​zn∣≤∑n≥0​∣(an​+bn​)zn∣+∑n≥0​∣bn​zn∣<∞,
(12)
contradicting ρ>R. Thus T=R. Interchanging the series covers S<R, proving the sum of power series with unequal radii of convergence rule:
T=min(R,S).​
(13)
The argument also covers a zero radius or an infinite larger radius. When the two radii are equal, cancellation can enlarge the radius, so the inequality of the radii matters.

A positively oriented planar rotation matrix acts by
(x1′​x2′​​)=(cosθsinθ​−sinθcosθ​)(x1​x2​​).​
(19)
For a unit vector n in three dimensions, the parallel and perpendicular orthogonal projections are
x∥​=(x⋅n)n,x⊥​=x−(x⋅n)n.
(20)
On the plane perpendicular to n, the map v↦n×v preserves lengths and turns vectors through a positive right angle about n. It squares to minus the identity there. Thus cosθx⊥​+sinθ(n×x⊥​) rotates that component, while the parallel component remains fixed. This proves the Rodrigues rotation formula
x′=(x⋅n)n+cosθ[x−(x⋅n)n]+sinθ(n×x).
(21)
Its component form uses the Levi-Civita symbol:
Rij​=cosθδij​+(1−cosθ)ni​nj​−sinθϵijk​nk​.​
(22)
Since ni​ni​=1 and ϵiik​=0, its matrix trace is 3cosθ+1−cosθ=1+2cosθ.
For the given matrix, the matrix trace is 1+2r with r=1/2​, so cosθ=1/2​. In the stated angular range, θ=±π/4. The antisymmetric entries give

(4π​,2​(1,1,0)​),(−4π​,−2​(1,1,0)​).​
(24)
Reversing the axis and the angle leaves the Rodrigues rotation formula unchanged, so these describe the same rotation.

The intermediate value theorem states that a continuous real-valued function on [a,b] takes every value between f(a) and f(b). To prove it, suppose f(a)<k<f(b) and repeatedly bisect the interval, keeping endpoints whose function values bracket k. The resulting nested closed intervals have lengths tending to zero and a common point c by completeness of the real numbers. Both endpoint sequences tend to c, so continuity implies f(c)=k. Endpoint values are immediate, and reversing the inequalities handles f(a)>f(b).
For the derivative assertion, put m=(f(b)−f(a))/(b−a). The intermediate secant slope from endpoint derivatives can be constructed in three cases. If m=k, take [a′,b′]=[a,b]. If m>k, the function
g(x)=x−af(x)−f(a)​,g(a)=f′(a),
(40)
is continuous on [a,b]: differentiability at a supplies the endpoint limit, and differentiability elsewhere implies continuity. Since g(a)<k<g(b), the intermediate value theorem gives x∈(a,b) with g(x)=k. Take [a′,b′]=[a,x].
If m<k, use instead
h(x)=b−xf(b)−f(x)​,h(b)=f′(b).
(41)
Its continuous endpoint extension satisfies h(a)<k<h(b), so there is y∈(a,b) with h(y)=k. Take [a′,b′]=[y,b]. In every case,
b′−a′f(b′)−f(a′)​=k.
(42)
The mean value theorem on this subinterval now gives
some c∈(a,b) satisfies f′(c)=k.​
(43)
This proves the Darboux theorem for derivatives without assuming that f′ is continuous.

A real sequence has limit a∈R if, for every ε>0, there is N such that ∣an​−a∣<ε for all n≥N. It tends to +∞ if, for every real M, eventually an​>M; it tends to −∞ if eventually an​<M for every real M.
The Bolzano-Weierstrass theorem states that every bounded sequence of real numbers has a real convergent subsequence. This proves the claim for bounded sequences. If the sequence is unbounded above, every tail remains unbounded above because a finite prefix is bounded. Choose indices nj​ increasing with anj​​>j. This subsequence tends to +∞. If it is unbounded below, choose anj​​<−j to obtain a subsequence tending to −∞. This proves the extended real subsequence theorem.
For the final example, a bounded sequence with vanishing increments need not converge. Take
an​=sinn​.​
(50)
The mean value theorem gives
∣an+1​−an​∣≤n+1​−n​=n+1​+n​1​⟶0.
(51)
Nevertheless, along nj​=⌊(π/2+2πj)2⌋, the square-root rounding error tends to zero, so anj​​→1. Along mj​=⌊(3π/2+2πj)2⌋, it tends to −1. These two subsequences have different limits, proving nonconvergence.

For a bounded function on an interval I, write ωI​(f)=supI​f−infI​f for its oscillation of a function on an interval. For a partition P, the difference between its upper Darboux sum and lower Darboux sum is ∑I∈P​∣I∣ωI​(f). The Riemann integrability criterion says that this can be made arbitrarily small.
Since ωI​(f1​+f2​)≤ωI​(f1​)+ωI​(f2​), choose good partitions for the two functions and take their common refinement. Darboux sum refinement monotonicity makes both oscillation sums small on this refinement, so their sum is Riemann integrable. The same argument handles differences and scalar multiples.
Both t↦max(t,0) and t↦∣t∣ are Lipschitz continuous with constant one. Thus ωI​(f+)≤ωI​(f) and ωI​(∣f∣)≤ωI​(f). This calculation shows that Lipschitz composition preserves Riemann integrability, so the positive part and the absolute value are integrable.
The converse for the absolute value is false. Define f=1 on rational numbers and f=−1 on irrational numbers. Then ∣f∣=1 is integrable, but every interval has supremum one and infimum minus one, so every Darboux difference for f is 2(b−a). Hence f is not Riemann integrable.
Finally,
max(f1​,f2​)=2f1​+f2​+∣f1​−f2​∣​​
(52)
is integrable by the closure properties just proved. This establishes that Riemann integrability is closed under addition and maximum.
A countable supremum can fail, even for a uniformly bounded family. Enumerate the rational points of [a,b] as q1​,q2​,… and set fn​=1{qn​}​. Each singleton indicator function is Riemann integrable with integral zero: isolate its single nonzero point in intervals of arbitrarily small total length. But
nsup​fn​=1Q∩[a,b]​​
(53)
is the nonintegrable Dirichlet function. This proves that a pointwise supremum need not preserve Riemann integrability.
