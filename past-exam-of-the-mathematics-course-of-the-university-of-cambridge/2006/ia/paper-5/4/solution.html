OurBigBook.com Words: 241
A prime number is an integer greater than one whose only positive divisors are one and itself. In particular, one is not a prime number. Every integer greater than one has a prime number divisor, by taking its smallest divisor greater than one.
Suppose there were only finitely many prime numbers congruent to −1 modulo six, and let their product be P, with empty product one. The integer N=6P−1>1 has no divisor two or three and is divisible by none of the listed prime numbers, since N≡−1(modpj​). Every prime number divisor of N is therefore 1 or −1 modulo six. If all these divisors were 1 modulo six, their product, with multiplicities, would also be 1 modulo six. But N≡−1(mod6). Hence at least one divisor is an unlisted prime number congruent to −1 modulo six, a contradiction. There are infinitely many primes of the form 6k−1.
For the other class, suppose its prime numbers were a finite list with product P. None is divisible by three. The integer M=4P2+3 is odd and is 1 modulo three, since P2≡1(mod3). Thus a prime number divisor q of M satisfies q>3. The residue x=2P solves x2≡−3(modq), so the allowed quadratic-congruence fact gives q≡1(mod6). However, for every listed divisor pj​, M≡3(modpj​), and pj​>3, so none divides M. We have again found an unlisted prime number in the supposedly complete list. There are infinitely many primes of the form 6k+1. Both arguments remain valid for an empty starting list.
