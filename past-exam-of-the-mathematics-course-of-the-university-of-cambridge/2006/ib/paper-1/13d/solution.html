Let F(z)=eaz/(1+ez) and integrate counterclockwise around the rectangle with vertices −R,R,R+2πi,−R+2πi. It contains a single simple pole at z=πi, with residue

On the top edge F(x+2πi)=e2πiaF(x), and its orientation reverses that of the bottom edge. On the right edge ∣F∣≤eaR/(eR−1), so its integral tends to zero for a<1. On the left ∣F∣≤e−aR/(1−e−R), which tends to zero for a>0. The residue theorem therefore gives

The restriction is also necessary for ordinary convergence: at −∞ the integrand behaves as eax, requiring a>0, while at +∞ it behaves as e(a−1)x, requiring a<1. At either endpoint one tail approaches a nonzero constant; outside this range one tail grows. There is no cancellation because the real integrand is positive.
