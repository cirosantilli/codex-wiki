Define source strength m here as the volume flux through a small sphere around the source. For incompressible potential flow, u=∇ϕ and the governing conditions are
Δϕ=mδ(x−xs​),∂z​ϕ=0 on z=0,ϕ→0 at infinity.
(52)
Equivalently the Laplace equation holds away from the source, with local behavior ϕ∼−m/(4π∣x−xs​∣). The boundary condition expresses impermeability. The method of images uses an equal source below the plane:
ϕ(x)=−4πm​(x2+y2+(z−a)2​1​+x2+y2+(z+a)2​1​).​
(53)
The image singularity lies outside the fluid, the two vertical derivatives cancel on the wall, and the source has the specified outward flux.
Let s=x2+y2​. On the wall uz​=0 and the tangential radial velocity is us​=ms/[2π(s2+a2)3/2]. With ambient pressure p∞​, the steady Bernoulli equation in this irrotational flow gives
p(s,0)=p∞​−8π2(s2+a2)3ρm2s2​.​
(54)
The fluid pressure is reduced everywhere on the wall except at its axis and in the infinite-distance limit. The finite hydrodynamic force is the excess over the uniform ambient-pressure force. Integrating its magnitude over the plane yields
F=2π∫0∞​[p∞​−p(s,0)]sds=4πρm2​∫0∞​(s2+a2)3s3ds​.
(55)
Putting u=s2 gives the last integral as 21​∫0∞​u(u+a2)−3du=1/(4a2). Hence
F=16πa2ρm2​,F=Fez​.​
(56)
Relative to ambient pressure on the other side, the boundary is attracted toward the source. This is the hydrodynamic attraction of a plane wall to a point source.
There is an alternative source-strength convention: if m denotes the coefficient in ur​=m/r2 rather than total volume flux, replace the flux above by 4πm. Then ϕ=−m(1/r+​+1/r−​), p−p∞​=−2ρm2s2/(s2+a2)3, and F=πρm2/a2. The physical result is identical once the normalization is fixed.
