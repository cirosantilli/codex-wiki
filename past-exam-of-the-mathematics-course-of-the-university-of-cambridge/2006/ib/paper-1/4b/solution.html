Interpret m as the rest mass of each initial ball and assume that no energy or momentum escapes the collision. Let γ=(1−v2/c2)−1/2 be its Lorentz factor. The total initial energy and momentum are
E=mc2(γ+1),P=γmv.
(7)
Their conservation determines the final four-momentum. For a single lump, P=γ′m′v′ and E=γ′m′c2, hence
v′=EPc2​=γ+1γv​.​
(8)
The final invariant mass follows from m′2c4=E2−c2P2:
m′2=m2[(γ+1)2−γ2c2v2​]=m2(2+2γ),m′=m2(1+γ)​.​
(9)
This is the rest mass of a coalescing relativistic collision. For v>0, m′>2m: some kinetic energy becomes internal energy in the final rest frame. Setting m′=2m would discard that energy and contradict conservation. At small speeds v′∼v/2, while v=0 gives m′=2m.
