1H OurBigBook.com
The minimal polynomial mA​ is the monic polynomial of smallest degree satisfying mA​(A)=0. An annihilating polynomial exists, for example because I,A,…,An2 are linearly dependent in the n2-dimensional space of matrices. A nonzero constant cannot annihilate A, so the minimal degree is positive.
If p and q are two such monic polynomials of minimal degree d, then (p−q)(A)=0 and deg(p−q)<d unless p=q. Minimality therefore forces p=q. Polynomial division also shows that mA​ divides every annihilating polynomial: the remainder after division would otherwise be an annihilator of smaller degree. This proves uniqueness rather than merely choosing one of several minimal-degree polynomials.
If A is real, coefficientwise complex conjugation of mA​(A)=0 gives mA​​(A)=0. The conjugated polynomial is monic of the same degree, hence uniqueness gives mA​​=mA​. The minimal polynomial of a real matrix has real coefficients.
For n≥3, use the block diagonal matrix
A=(10​11​)⊕(−1)⊕In−3​.​
(1)
The two-dimensional Jordan block is annihilated by (t−1)2 but not by t−1, while the scalar block requires the factor t+1. A polynomial annihilates the direct sum exactly when it annihilates every block. Thus its minimal polynomial is the least common multiple (t−1)2(t+1). For n=3 the final identity block is omitted.

Interpret m as the rest mass of each initial ball and assume that no energy or momentum escapes the collision. Let γ=(1−v2/c2)−1/2 be its Lorentz factor. The total initial energy and momentum are

The displayed evolution law uses the usual assumptions of incompressible flow, constant density and conservative body force. With ω=∇×u, the Euler equation is
∂t​u+(u⋅∇)u=−∇(p/ρ+Φ).
(10)
Use (u⋅∇)u=∇(∣u∣2/2)−u×ω and take a curl. Gradients have zero curl, and
∂t​ω=∇×(u×ω)=(ω⋅∇)u−(u⋅∇)ω+u∇⋅ω−ω∇⋅u.
(11)
Here ∇⋅ω=0 because it is a curl, and ∇⋅u=0 by incompressibility. The vorticity equation is therefore
DtDω​=(ω⋅∇)u.​
(12)
The material derivative combines local change with advection by fluid particles. The right-hand side is vortex stretching and tilting: spatial variation of velocity along a vortex line changes its strength and direction. Along each particle path this is a homogeneous linear equation for vorticity. For a smooth velocity field its solution with zero initial vorticity remains zero by uniqueness. Thus an initially irrotational flow remains irrotational while these assumptions and smoothness hold.
For a plane flow u=(u(x,y,t),v(x,y,t),0), the vorticity is ω=(0,0,ζ) and (ω⋅∇)u=ζ∂z​u=0. Hence Dζ/Dt=0. Every particle retains its initial value, so an initially uniform value gives
ω(x,y,t)=ω0​for all later times.​
(13)
Inviscid motion alone is insufficient for the printed simplified law: compressible variable-density motion generally adds −ω∇⋅u+∇ρ×∇p/ρ2. Those terms vanish under the assumptions used above.

At the first step of Gaussian elimination, the first column of a nonsingular matrix cannot be zero. Choose a nonzero pivot there using a row interchange and eliminate below it. The resulting matrix has block form
(p0​rB​),p=0.
(17)

The recorded elimination multipliers, with previous entries permuted when necessary, form the unit lower-triangular factor. Every nonsingular square matrix admits PA=LU under columnwise partial pivoting.

For a linear map θ:U→V, its rank of a linear map is r(θ)=dimimθ, and its nullity is n(θ)=dimkerθ. Choose a basis e1​,…,es​ of its kernel and extend it to a basis e1​,…,ed​ of U. Then θes+1​,…,θed​ span its image. They are linearly independent: a linear combination mapped to zero would belong to the kernel, contradicting independence of the extended basis unless all its coefficients vanish. Hence r(θ)=d−s, proving
r(θ)+n(θ)=dimU.​
(27)
For endomorphisms of U, define (θ+ϕ)(u)=θ(u)+ϕ(u) and (θϕ)(u)=θ(ϕ(u)). The image of the sum lies in the sum of the two images. Thus
r(θ+ϕ)≤dim(imθ+imϕ)≤r(θ)+r(ϕ).
(28)
To prove the product inequality, restrict ϕ to K=ker(θϕ). This map has kernel kerϕ and image imϕ∩kerθ: every vector in the intersection has a preimage in K. Applying the rank-nullity theorem to the restricted map gives
n(θϕ)=n(ϕ)+dim(imϕ∩kerθ)≤n(ϕ)+n(θ).
(29)
Now put d=dimU and s=r(θ)+r(ϕ). If both equalities hold, r(θ+ϕ)=s≤d, while n(θϕ)=2d−s≤d forces s≥d. Therefore s=d, the sum has full rank, and the product has nullity d. The sum is an isomorphism and the product is zero.
Conversely, if θϕ=0, then imϕ⊆kerθ, giving s≤d. If the sum is also an isomorphism, its rank is d≤s. Thus s=d, and the two inequalities become equalities. This proves the equality in the rank-sum and nullity-product inequalities in both directions.

Use the following standard facts: A5​ has order 60 and is a nonabelian simple group; the coset action on the cosets of an index-j subgroup is a transitive homomorphism into Sj​. If H<A5​ has index j>1, that action is nontrivial. Its kernel is a normal subgroup, hence is trivial by simplicity. It would therefore embed A5​ in Sj​. For j=2,3,4 this is impossible since j!<60. There are no subgroups of indices 2, 3 or 4.
An index-5 subgroup has order 12 by Lagrange's theorem. Consider its natural action on the five letters. It cannot be transitive, since an orbit of size 5 would divide its order by the orbit-stabilizer theorem. If it had no fixed letter, its orbits would have sizes 2 and 3. It would then be a subgroup of the permutations preserving that partition, namely S2​×S3​. Exactly half of those 12 permutations are even, so (S2​×S3​)∩A5​ has order 6, too small to contain H.
Thus H fixes a letter i. The stabilizer of i in A5​ is the copy of A4​ consisting of even permutations of the other four letters; it has order 12. Containment and equality of orders force H to equal that stabilizer. Conversely each such stabilizer has index 5. Consequently
The index-5 subgroups are precisely StabA5​​(i),i=1,…,5.​
(30)
These five subgroups are distinct: for i=j, a three-cycle on letters other than i can move j, so it belongs to the stabilizer of i but not that of j.

i OurBigBook.com
The product topology on X×Y consists of arbitrary unions of rectangles U×V, with U open in X and V open in Y. Equivalently, these rectangles form a basis.
The empty set is the empty union, and X×Y is itself an allowed rectangle. An arbitrary union of sets of this form is still a union of allowed rectangles. Finally,
(U1​×V1​)∩(U2​×V2​)=(U1​∩U2​)×(V1​∩V2​),
(34)
which is an allowed rectangle because the factor topologies are closed under finite intersections. Distributing intersection over unions shows that the intersection of any two product-open sets is product-open; induction handles all finite intersections. These verify every topology axiom. Rectangles alone are generally not a topology, which is why their arbitrary unions are included.

A sufficient statistic T(X) for θ is a function of the observations whose conditional distribution of X given T(X) does not depend on θ, whenever that conditioning event is possible. It retains all parameter dependence of the likelihood.
In the discrete case the Fisher-Neyman factorization theorem provides a direct criterion: f(x∣θ)=g(T(x),θ)h(x), where h is independent of the parameter. To justify sufficiency, for a value t with positive probability, sum this expression over its fiber and divide:
Pθ​(X=x∣T=t)=∑y:T(y)=t​h(y)h(x)​,T(x)=t.
(57)
The parameter-dependent factor cancels. Conversely, parameter-independent conditional probabilities ht​(x) give the factorization f(x∣θ)=Pθ​(T=t)ht​(x) with t=T(x). Thus the criterion is both necessary and sufficient, with conditional laws only required on fibers of positive probability.
For the shifted exponential sample the likelihood is
f(x∣θ)=λne−λ∑i​xi​enλθ1{θ≤mini​xi​}​,θ≥0.
(58)
It factors through T=X(1)​=mini​Xi​, so the sample minimum is a sufficient statistic. Writing Yi​=Xi​−θ, independence gives for t≥0

For the natural filtration Fn​=σ(X0​,…,Xn​), a stopping time T takes values in {0,1,…}∪{∞} and satisfies {T≤n}∈Fn​ for each n. Whether it has occurred by time n can be decided from the observations through that time.
The Strong Markov property for a time-homogeneous Markov chain says that, on {T<∞}, conditional on FT​, the process (XT+r​)r≥0​ has the law of a fresh chain started at XT​. In particular its future conditional law depends on the past only through XT​. For example P(XT+r​=j∣FT​)=(Pr)XT​j​ on that event, and the corresponding statement holds for every finite future path.
Start at i and let T0​=0, Tr+1​=inf{n>Tr​:Xn​=i}, with inf∅=∞. These are stopping times. Put f=Pi​(T1​<∞). Each finite return restarts the chain at i, so the Strong Markov property gives inductively
Pi​(Tr​<∞)=fr.
(61)
The events decrease as r increases; infinitely many visits occur exactly when every return time is finite. Continuity of probability therefore gives
Pi​(infinitely many visits to i)=r→∞lim​fr={0,1,​f<1,f=1.​​
(62)
No irreducibility assumption is needed for this zero-one conclusion at the starting state.
Let N=∑n≥0​1{Xn​=i}​, counting the initial visit. Summing nonnegative random variables and then using the tail-sum identity gives
∑n≥0​Pi​(Xn​=i)=Ei​N=∑r≥0​Pi​(N≥r+1)=∑r≥0​fr.
(63)
If f<1, this is 1/(1−f)<∞. Thus divergence of the given sum forces f=1, and infinitely many visits then occur with probability one. This proves the recurrence criterion by return probabilities directly from the regeneration at return times; divergent probability sums alone would not suffice for arbitrary dependent events.
