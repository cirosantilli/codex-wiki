At a constrained stationary point where ∇g=0, every tangent direction to g=0 is orthogonal to ∇g. Vanishing of the directional derivative of f in all such directions forces ∇f=λ∇g for a Lagrange multiplier λ. Solve these equations together with the constraint, then check the candidates and any singular or limiting cases for minima or maxima.
Let a closed cylinder have radius r>0 and height h>0, with fixed V>0. Its area is A=2πr2+2πrh and the constraint is πr2h=V. The Lagrange multiplier equations give
4πr+2πh=λ2πrh,2πr=λπr2.
(6)
The second yields λ=2/r; substituting into the first gives h=2r. Thus the minimum-area closed cylinder at fixed volume has
r=(2πV​)1/3,h=2(2πV​)1/3,Amin​=3(2π)1/3V2/3.​
(7)
To prove it is the global minimum, eliminate h to obtain A(r)=2πr2+2V/r. Its derivative is 4πr−2V/r2, negative before the displayed radius and positive afterwards; the area tends to infinity both as r↓0 and as r→∞. This establishes uniqueness and rules out limiting smaller areas.
