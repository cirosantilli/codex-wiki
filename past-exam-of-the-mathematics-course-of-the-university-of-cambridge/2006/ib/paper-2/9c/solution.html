A cut of a flow network of a directed flow network is a partition (S,N∖S) with the source in S and the sink outside it. Its cut capacity is c(S)=∑i∈S,j∈/S​cij​. Summing flow conservation over all vertices of S cancels every internal edge and gives the flow value as outgoing flow minus incoming flow across the cut. Incoming flow is nonnegative and outgoing flow is bounded by capacity, so
∣f∣≤c(S)for every cut S,max∣f∣≤Smin​c(S).​
(17)
This is weak duality for the maximum-flow problem; no converse theorem is needed for the upper bound.
For this grid, use S={(i1​,i2​):i1​<i2​}. It contains (0,3) but not (3,0). An adjacent edge can leave S only by reaching the diagonal. Each of the three interior diagonal vertices has two incoming crossing edges and each endpoint has one, making eight edges. Every crossing edge has capacity one. Thus the diagonal cut in a nearest-neighbour lattice flow has capacity eight.
Here is a feasible flow of value eight. Send one unit on each of the following directed paths, and zero on every edge not used. In this list ij abbreviates the vertex (i,j):
​03→02→01→00→10→20→30,03→02→01→11→10→20→30,03→02→12→11→21→20→30,03→13→12→22→21→31→30,03→13→23→22→32→31→30,03→13→23→33→32→31→30,03→04→14→24→34→33→43→42→41→40→30,03→04→14→24→34→44→43→42→41→40→30.​
(18)
Every path conserves flow at its intermediate vertices. To check the capacities without hiding a feasibility assumption, group used edges by capacity: the capacity-one edges are traversed at most once; the capacity-two edges at most twice; the capacity-three edges at most three times; the capacity-four edges at most twice. Shared source edges 03→02, 03→13, 03→04 carry 3,3,2 units, and the corresponding three sink edges carry 3,3,2. The diagram displays every positive edge flow beside its capacity. Thus all constraints hold and the matching cut proves
maximum flow value=8.​
