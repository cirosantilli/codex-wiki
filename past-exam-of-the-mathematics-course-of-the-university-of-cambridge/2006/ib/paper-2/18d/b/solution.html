For a continuous f and ϵ>0, the Weierstrass approximation theorem gives a fixed polynomial Q of degree m with ∥f−Q∥∞​<ϵ. For every n≥m, polynomial exactness gives In​(Q)=I(Q). Thus
∣I(f)−In​(f)∣​≤∣I(f−Q)∣+∣In​(f−Q)∣≤2W∥f−Q∥∞​<2Wϵ.​
(45)
Since ϵ is arbitrary, In​(f)→I(f)​ for every continuous f. This is convergence of positive quadrature on continuous functions. The argument requires nodes in [−1,1], implicit in a rule defined on all continuous functions there; otherwise their function values would not even be specified by the stated domain. Exactness alone would not provide the norm bound if arbitrarily signed weights were allowed.
