In SI units, the source-free Maxwell equations in vacuum are
∇⋅E=0,∇⋅B=0,∇×E=−∂t​B,∇×B=μ0​ϵ0​∂t​E.
(68)
Use complex amplitudes with phase ei(ωt−k⋅x) and take real parts at the end. The electric divergence equation gives k⋅E0​=0. Faraday's equation gives k×E0​=ωB0​, so
B(x,t)=ℜ[ωk×E0​​ei(ωt−k⋅x)].​
(69)
The magnetic divergence equation follows because k⋅(k×E0​)=0. Ampere's equation becomes k×B0​=−μ0​ϵ0​ωE0​. Since k×(k×E0​)=−∣k∣2E0​, a nonzero wave satisfies
ω2=c2∣k∣2,c=(μ0​ϵ0​)−1/2.​
(70)
Thus both fields are transverse, mutually perpendicular for linear polarization, and obey the vacuum plane-wave dispersion relation.
For the interface let n=ez​ point from the minus side to the plus side and write [E]=E+​−E−​, similarly for B. Applying the integral Maxwell equations to a thin pillbox and a thin loop gives the electromagnetic boundary conditions
n⋅[E]=σ/ϵ0​,n×[E]=0,n⋅[B]=0,n×[B]=μ0​j.​
(71)
Here j is the tangential surface current. The jump notation fixes the signs, including the direction of the normal.
For the perfect conductor, take the time-varying interior fields to vanish. Choose positive k with ω=ck. The incident electric field has complex form E0​ex​eiωt+ikz. To cancel its tangential value at the surface, use the reflected field −E0​ex​eiωt−ikz, whose wavevector points upward. The total vacuum fields are therefore
EB​=ℜ[2iE0​eiωtsin(kz)]ex​,=ℜ[−(2E0​/c)eiωtcos(kz)]ey​.​
(72)
The sign of each magnetic contribution follows from B0​=k×E0​/ω. At z=0, the electric field is zero and the magnetic field is tangential. The jump conditions yield
σ=0,j(t)=μ0​c2​ℜ(E0​eiωt)ex​.​
(73)
For a real incident amplitude this is j=(2E0​/μ0​c)cos(ωt)ex​. It supplies the reflected wave while satisfying the normal-field and tangential-electric conditions. Any independently imposed static interior field is separate from this oscillatory response.
