Use the permitted triangularization to write A=TUT−1 with U upper triangular and diagonal λ1​,…,λn​. Products of upper-triangular matrices are upper triangular, and their diagonal entries multiply, so
0=tr(Ak)=tr(Uk)=∑j=1n​λjk​(1≤k≤n).
(35)
The result of part b makes every λj​ zero. Thus U is strictly upper triangular. To see directly that Un=0, a potentially nonzero entry in a product of n factors requires a chain
i=i0​<i1​<⋯<in​=j
(36)
of n+1 indices in {1,…,n}, which is impossible. Hence
An=TUnT−1=0.​
(37)
This is nilpotence from vanishing traces of powers. It proves nilpotence without substituting an unproved diagonalization for the allowed triangularization.
