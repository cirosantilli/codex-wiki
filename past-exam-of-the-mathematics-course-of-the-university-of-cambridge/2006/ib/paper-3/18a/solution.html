For an inviscid flow of constant density ρ, driven by a conservative body force −∇Φ, the Euler equation is

The vector identity (u⋅∇)u=∇(∣u∣2/2)−u×(∇×u) and irrotationality give the Unsteady Bernoulli equation. Specifically, wherever a single-valued potential u=∇ϕ is available,
∇(ϕt​+21​∣u∣2+ρp​+Φ)=0.
(75)
On a connected potential-flow region this means
ϕt​+21​∣u∣2+ρp​+Φ=C(t).​

The integration function is spatially constant but need not be constant in time; it can be removed by changing the potential's time-dependent gauge. A simply connected irrotational region provides the requisite global potential. For a barotropic fluid the pressure term is replaced by ∫dp/ρ(p); here the liquid density is constant.
For the tube, let U(t) be its approximately uniform axial velocity. The large reservoir's surface velocity is negligible and gravity is neglected as stipulated. Entrance-region inertia contributes an effective length of order a, negligible against L. The potential difference between the outlet and the reservoir is consequently LU to leading order. Subtract Bernoulli at the reservoir surface, where pressure is P+pa​, from its value at the outlet, where pressure is pa​ and speed is U. This gives
