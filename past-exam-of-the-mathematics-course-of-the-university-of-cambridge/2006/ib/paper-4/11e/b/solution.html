A finite basis is a generating set, so the preceding determinant trick applies with R=k and M=V. If p(t)=det(A−tI), the map k[t]→k[ϕ] sending t to ϕ is a ring homomorphism. Since a determinant is a polynomial expression in its entries, it sends p(t) to det(A−ϕI)=0. Thus
p(ϕ)=0.​
(32)
With the more usual monic characteristic polynomial χA​(t)=det(tI−A)=(−1)dimVp(t), this reads χA​(ϕ)=0, the Cayley-Hamilton theorem. If the matrix convention uses column coordinates instead of the row convention above, transpose A; its characteristic polynomial is unchanged.
