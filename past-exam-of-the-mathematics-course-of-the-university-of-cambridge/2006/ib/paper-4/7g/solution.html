Orient the moving boundary C(t) consistently with the normal on a spanning surface S(t). The relevant Maxwell equations are ∇⋅B=0 and ∇×E=−∂t​B.
First hold the magnetic field fixed at time t and compare the fluxes through the old and new surfaces. The closed surface consists of the new surface, the reversed old surface, and the joining ribbon. A parametrization of the ribbon by boundary arclength and time gives its outward oriented area vector as dr×vδt. Thus its flux is
∫S′​B⋅dS=−δt∮C​B⋅(v×dr).
(15)
By the divergence theorem, the total flux through the closed surface is zero. Adding the field's explicit change in time consequently gives the moving-surface flux derivative
dtdΦ​=∫S(t)​∂t​B⋅dS+∮C​B⋅(v×dr).
(16)
Now apply Stokes theorem and B⋅(v×dr)=−(v×B)⋅dr:
dtdΦ​=−∮C​E⋅dr−∮C​(v×B)⋅dr.
(17)
Consequently the electromotive force, including the motional electromotive force, satisfies
dtdΦ​=−ε.​
(18)
This derives the moving-circuit form of Faraday's law, including both changing fields and changing circuit geometry.
