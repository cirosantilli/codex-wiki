Use two-phase simplex to handle the constraint of the opposite inequality direction. Introduce nonnegative slack variables s1​,s2​, a surplus variable s3​, and an artificial variable a. The equality constraints are
x1​+x2​+x3​+s1​=30,2x1​−x2​+s2​=35,x1​+2x2​−x3​−s3​+a=40.
(76)
For Phase I maximize w=−a, initially with basis (s1​,s2​,a). Its initial simplex dictionary is
s1​=30−x1​−x2​−x3​,s2​=35−2x1​+x2​,a=40−x1​−2x2​+x3​+s3​,
(77)
w=−40+x1​+2x2​−x3​−s3​.
(78)
Choose x1​ to enter. The simplex ratio test compares 30, 35/2 and 40, so s2​ leaves. The new dictionary is
x1​=235​+2x2​​−2s2​​,s1​=225​−23​x2​−x3​+21​s2​,a=245​−25​x2​+x3​+21​s2​+s3​.
(79)
Since w=−45/2+(5/2)x2​−x3​−s2​/2−s3​, choose x2​ to enter. Its limiting ratios are 25/3 from s1​ and 9 from a, so s1​ leaves. This gives
x2​=325​−32​x3​+31​s2​−32​s1​,
(80)
x1​=365​−31​x3​−31​s2​−31​s1​,a=35​+38​x3​−31​s2​+35​s1​+s3​.
(81)
Now w=−5/3−(8/3)x3​+s2​/3−(5/3)s1​−s3​, so let s2​ enter. Its limiting ratios are 65 from x1​ and 5 from a; the artificial variable leaves. The simplex dictionary becomes
s2​=5+8x3​+5s1​+3s3​−3a,
(82)
x1​=20−3x3​−2s1​−s3​+a,x2​=10+2x3​+s1​+s3​−a.
(83)
Setting all nonbasic variables to zero gives a=0, a feasible original basis. Since −a≤0, Phase I is optimal with value zero; delete a.
For Phase II the original objective in this basis is
z=50x1​−30x2​+x3​=700−209x3​−130s1​−80s3​.
(84)
All nonbasic reduced costs are strictly negative, so no further improving simplex pivot exists. As the nonbasic variables are nonnegative, the dictionary also directly certifies z≤700, with equality only when x3​=s1​=s3​=0. Therefore
(x1​,x2​,x3​)=(20,10,0),zmax​=700.​
(85)
The second original constraint has slack five and the other two are tight. As an independent linear programming optimality certificate, multiply the first constraint by 130 and the third, after reversing it, by 80. This gives 50x1​−30x2​+210x3​≤700, and subtracting 209x3​≥0 proves the same upper bound for the objective.
