Write X=ϵx, k=∇X​θ, and denote the slow density and velocity field amplitudes by aρ​ and au​. Acting on the rapid phase, ∂t​+U⋅∇ gives iΩ, where Ω=ω−U⋅k. Spatial differentiation gives −ik, while derivatives of the amplitudes and the mean shear flow are O(ϵ). The leading continuity equation and linearized Euler equations therefore become
Ωaρ​=ρ0​k⋅au​,ρ0​Ωau​=c02​kaρ​.
(38)
Eliminating au​ on the nonzero acoustic branch gives the acoustic eikonal equation in a shear flow:
(ω−U⋅∇θ)2=c02​∣∇θ∣2.​
(39)
The mean-shear term is small here because ∇U=O(ϵ); retaining advection does not require retaining that gradient in the leading phase equation. The positive intrinsic-frequency acoustic dispersion relation is ω=Ukx​+c0​∣k∣, with group velocity vg​=Uex​+c0​k/∣k∣.
In a stationary wall boundary layer, horizontal homogeneity preserves ω and kx​ along a Hamiltonian ray-tracing equations trajectory. For a two-dimensional ray,
ky2​=c02​(ω−Ukx​)2​−kx2​.
(40)
Suppose U increases from zero at the wall to a positive free-stream value. For a downstream-directed wave normal, kx​>0, increasing U decreases Ω and ky​. The wave normal bends towards the wall, and the ray becomes more nearly parallel to it. If U reaches ω/kx​−c0​, then ky​=0: the ray turns and returns towards the wall rather than reaching the free stream. With launch angle β0​ above the wall and free-stream Mach number M∞​=U∞​/c0​, the escape condition is
cosβ0​≤1+M∞​1​for downstream launch.​
(41)
For upstream-directed wave normals, kx​<0, Ω and ∣ky​∣ increase through the layer; there is no such downstream turning barrier on this branch. A wavefront normal is parallel to k, while the energy ray follows the group velocity: advection makes their directions different. In particular, a wall-normal wave normal with kx​=0 can still have a ray drifting downstream. The ordinary WKB method needs a local turning-point continuation at ky​=0; the ray sketch depicts that qualitative continuation.
