For a density pattern rotating steadily about its third principal axis, choose t=0 when its in-plane axes are principal axes. Then I(0)=diag(I1​,I2​,I3​) and
I(t)=R(Ωt)I(0)R(Ωt)T,R(θ)=​cosθsinθ0​−sinθcosθ0​001​​.

In particular, I11​=I1​cos2Ωt+I2​sin2Ωt, I22​=I1​sin2Ωt+I2​cos2Ωt, and I12​=(I1​−I2​)sinΩtcosΩt, while I33​=I3​ and I13​=I23​=0. Two differentiations at t=0 prove the inertia-tensor acceleration of a steadily rotating stellar pattern:
21​I¨=Ω2diag(I22​−I11​, I11​−I22​, 0).​

Both the off-diagonal acceleration and the in-plane trace acceleration vanish at this instant.
Write u=v for the local mean streaming. The stellar kinetic-energy tensor, stellar pressure-energy tensor, and stellar potential-energy tensor have the conventions

−2Ω2(I11​−I22​)=(W11​−W22​)+2(T11​−T22​)+(Π11​−Π22​),

so
Ω2=−2(I11​−I22​)(W11​−W22​)+2(T11​−T22​)+(Π11​−Π22​)​.​

This quotient requires I11​=I22​. In an axisymmetric density distribution it becomes 0/0: rotating the pattern about its symmetry axis does not change the density, and this equation cannot determine a pattern speed. Stellar streaming need not be rigid rotation with angular frequency Ω.
Next add the first two diagonal virial equations and use the third. Their acceleration terms vanish, so
W11​+W22​+2(T11​+T22​)+Π11​+Π22​=0,W33​+2T33​+Π33​=0.
(34)
For T33​=0, Π33​=−W33​. With the planar virial trace Π⊥​=Π11​+Π22​ and W⊥​=W11​+W22​, the prescribed mass-weighted quantities satisfy Π⊥​=2Mσ02​, 2(T11​+T22​)=MV02​, and 1−δ=2Π33​/Π⊥​. Therefore W33​=−(1−δ)Mσ02​ and the planar equation becomes
W⊥​+MV02​+2Mσ02​=0.
(35)
Eliminating Mσ02​ gives
σ02​V02​​=(1−δ)W33​W11​+W22​​−2.​
(36)
The ν0​ in the numerator of the PDF's displayed relation is a notation slip: the velocity subsequently defined is V0​. The relation follows from the traces, not from replacing a galaxy by a solid rotating body.
For the axisymmetric oblate spheroid, rotational symmetry gives Π11​=Π22​=Mσ02​, Π33​=Mσ02​(1−δ) and zero integrated off-diagonal random stresses. A line-of-sight unit vector can be chosen as n=(sini,0,cosi), with i=0 face-on. Contracting the random stress tensor with this vector gives the inclination projection of an axisymmetric stellar velocity tensor:
σ2=Mni​Πij​nj​​=σ02​sin2i+σ02​(1−δ)cos2i=σ02​(1−δcos2i).​
(37)
This is a mass-weighted random velocity dispersion after subtracting streaming, with consistent aperture weights. Along the projected major axis, azimuthal streaming lies along the direction whose line-of-sight projection is sini; a rotation amplitude V0​ consequently projects as
Vr​=V0​sini.​
(38)
There is a necessary observational convention here. The globally defined V02​=2(T11​+T22​)/M is a streaming mean square. The displayed amplitude relation holds if the rotation curve is constant, or if the measured rotation-amplitude statistic is weighted consistently to represent this intrinsic quantity. Axisymmetry alone does not equate an arbitrary observed peak rotation to the global V0​. If Vr​ instead means the full-aperture line-of-sight streaming root-mean-square, azimuthal averaging yields
⟨ulos2​⟩=sin2iM2T11​​=21​V02​sin2i,
(39)
and the corresponding root-mean-square is V0​sini/2​. Keeping amplitude and root-mean-square distinct prevents a spurious factor of two in the kinetic energy.
Finally, let the intrinsic equatorial and polar semiaxes be a and c, and put qt​=c/a=1−ϵt​. An intrinsic boundary has (x2+y2)/a2+z2/c2=1. Its sky minor-axis coordinate is Y=ycosi−zsini. Eliminating the line-of-sight coordinate, or maximizing Y on this ellipse, gives the projected minor semiaxis a2cos2i+c2sin2i​; the major semiaxis remains a. Thus the projected axis ratio of an oblate spheroid is
qa2​=cos2i+qt2​sin2i,qa​=1−ϵa​.
(40)
Subtracting from unity proves
ϵa​(2−ϵa​)=ϵt​(2−ϵt​)sin2i.​
(41)
The face-on limit is round with zero projected rotation amplitude; the edge-on limit reveals the intrinsic flattening and in-plane random velocity dispersion. These limits check both the geometry and the convention for inclination.
