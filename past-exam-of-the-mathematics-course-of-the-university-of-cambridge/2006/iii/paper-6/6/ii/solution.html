Let h=h∗. The exponential ut​=eith is defined by its norm-convergent power series. Continuity and conjugate linearity of the involution give ut∗​=e−ith, and multiplication of the commuting exponential series gives ut∗​ut​=ut​ut∗​=1. Thus ut​ is a Unitary element of a C-star algebra and, by the C-star identity, ∥ut​∥=1.
A character of an algebra is automatically continuous with ∣φ(a)∣≤∥a∥, as proved in Question 5. Passing it through the exponential series gives
∣eitφ(h)∣=∣φ(ut​)∣≤1(t∈R).

The left side is e−tℑφ(h). Considering both signs of t forces ℑφ(h)=0. So characters take real values on self-adjoint C-star elements.
Now write x=h+ik with
h=2x+x∗​,k=2ix−x∗​,
(38)
both self-adjoint C-star elements. Then
φ(x∗)=φ(h)−iφ(k)=φ(h)+iφ(k)​=φ(x)​.​
(39)
This proves that characters of a C-star algebra respect the involution. The argument itself does not require commutativity, although a noncommutative algebra need not have any characters.
