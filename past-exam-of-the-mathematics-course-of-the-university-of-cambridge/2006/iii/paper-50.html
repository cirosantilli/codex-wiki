For X∈L, define the Adjoint representation of a Lie algebra by adX​(Y)=[X,Y]. The Jacobi identity gives, for every Z,
[adX​,adY​]Z=[X,[Y,Z]]−[Y,[X,Z]]=[[X,Y],Z]=ad[X,Y]​Z.
(1)
Thus ad preserves brackets and is a Lie algebra representation. Its Killing form is
κ(X,Y)=Tr(adX​adY​).
(2)
Using the representation identity and the cyclic property of the trace,
κ([X,Y],Z)​=Tr([adX​,adY​]adZ​)=Tr(adX​[adY​,adZ​])=κ(X,[Y,Z]).​
(3)
The Killing form is also symmetric by cyclicity.
In a basis Ta​, write [Ta​,Tb​]=cab​dTd​. The lowered structure constants satisfy
cabc​=κ([Ta​,Tb​],Tc​)=κ(Ta​,[Tb​,Tc​]).
(4)
The first expression is antisymmetric in a,b and the second in b,c. These transpositions generate all permutations, so cabc​ is totally antisymmetric.
An invariant subspace of L under its Adjoint representation of a Lie algebra is exactly an ideal of a Lie algebra: [L,I]⊆I. A simple Lie algebra has no nonzero proper ideal, proving the adjoint representation is irreducible.
Put DX​=d(X). The same trace calculation proves invariance of the Trace form of a Lie algebra representation:
H([X,Y],Z)=Tr([DX​,DY​]DZ​)=Tr(DX​[DY​,DZ​])=H(X,[Y,Z]).
(5)
Because the DX​ are anti-Hermitian, H is a real symmetric bilinear form. The Killing form of a simple compact Lie algebra is negative definite. Define a real linear map T by H(X,Y)=κ(TX,Y). Invariance of both forms implies that T commutes with every adZ​, while symmetry makes T self-adjoint for the positive inner product −κ. By the spectral theorem, its real eigenspaces are invariant under the Adjoint representation of a Lie algebra. Irreducibility forces a single eigenvalue, so T=μI and H=μκ. This also avoids the extra care needed when applying the complex form of the Schur lemma to a real vector space.
The kernel of d is an ideal of a Lie algebra. It cannot be all of L: a trivial representation of dimension greater than one is reducible. Simplicity therefore makes d faithful. For every nonzero X,
H(X,X)=Tr(DX2​)=−Tr(DX†​DX​)<0.
(6)
Hence the positive trace index for a compact simple Lie algebra gives
Hab​=−μδab​,μ>0​
(7)
in the adapted basis.
For the cubic trace tensor of a Lie algebra representation, the identity
Tr([DY​,DX​DZ​DW​])=0
(8)
expands to
B([Y,X],Z,W)+B(X,[Y,Z],W)+B(X,Z,[Y,W])=0.
(9)
Set Y=Td​, X=Ta​, Z=Tb​, W=Tc​. Cyclicity of B converts Bℓbc​ to Bbcℓ​ and Baℓc​ to Bcaℓ​. Thus the invariance identity for a cubic trace tensor is
cda​ℓBbcℓ​+cdb​ℓBcaℓ​+cdc​ℓBabℓ​=0.​
(10)
For the final contraction, it is important to keep the specified index-raising convention. In the adapted basis write Cab​c=cab​c. Then cabc​=−Cab​c, while raising the last two indices with κab=−δab gives ca​mn=−Cam​n. Total antisymmetry and the definition of the Killing form imply
∑m,n​Cam​nCrm​n=−κar​=δar​,ca​mncmn​r=−δa​r.
(11)
Only the antisymmetric part of B in m,n contributes, so
ca​mnBmnb​​=21​ca​mnTr([d(Tm​),d(Tn​)]d(Tb​))=21​ca​mncmn​rHrb​=−21​Hab​.​
(12)
Consequently the Killing-normalized contraction of a cubic trace tensor is
ca​mnBmnb​=+2μ​δab​.​
(13)
The negative sign printed in the last requested identity is incompatible with raising indices by the inverse Killing form. The opposite sign is obtained if one instead defines the lowered structure constants using the positive Euclidean metric, a different convention from the one specified.
For a concrete check, take the two-dimensional representation of su(2) with Ta​=−iσa​/(22​), where the σa​ are Pauli matrices. Then
κab​=−δab​,Hab​=−41​δab​,ca​mn=−2​1​ϵamn​,Bmnb​=−82​1​ϵmnb​.
(14)
Their contraction is +δab​/8, confirming the positive sign with μ=1/4 and providing a counterexample to the printed sign.

The anti-Hermitian operators represent the compact real form; its complexification also contains the Hermitian Hi​ and the raising operators and lowering operators E±r​, with (E+r​)†=E−r​. Define the roots in the two-dimensional Cartan plane by
α1​=(1,0),α2​=(−1/2,3​/2),α3​=α1​+α2​=(1/2,3​/2).
(15)
Each has squared length one. For each r=1,2,3, set
J3(r)​=αr​⋅(H1​,H2​),J±(r)​=2​E±r​.
(16)
The displayed commutators give [J3(r)​,J±(r)​]=±J±(r)​ and [J+(r)​,J−(r)​]=2J3(r)​. Thus these are the three root SU(2) subalgebras of SU(3).
The commuting Hermitian operators H1​,H2​ can be simultaneously diagonalized. A weight of a representation is a pair λ=(h1​,h2​) for which
Hi​∣λ,a⟩=hi​∣λ,a⟩,
(17)
where a labels independent states of that representation weight. The weight multiplicity is the dimension of this simultaneous eigenspace. Commuting Hi​ past a ladder operator proves
E±r​:Vλ​⟶Vλ±αr​​,​
(18)
with zero image allowed when the raised or lowered representation weight is absent.
Choose α1​,α2​ as positive simple roots. A highest-weight vector is a nonzero simultaneous eigenvector annihilated by E+1​,E+2​,E+3​; the third condition follows from the first two and their commutator. In an irreducible representation, its highest weight Λ has multiplicity one and repeated lowering generates the representation. The three root SU(2) subalgebras of SU(3) give nonnegative integer Dynkin labels
p=2Λ⋅α1​=2h1​,q=2Λ⋅α2​=−h1​+3​h2​.
(19)
Equivalently, with ω1​=(1/2,1/(23​)) and ω2​=(0,1/3​),
Λ=pω1​+qω2​=(2p​,23​p+2q​),p,q∈Z≥0​.​
(20)
These are the SU(3) highest-weight coordinates in the normalization used here.
The Weyl group is generated by the reflections sr​(λ)=λ−2(λ⋅αr​)αr​. The SU(2) Lie algebra root-string symmetry makes these reflections preserve both representation weights and their weight multiplicities. They generate the symmetries of an equilateral triangle: rotations through 120∘ and three reflections. The outer polygon is the convex hull of the Weyl group orbit of Λ. When p,q>0 it is a hexagon with alternating side lengths p,q in root-step units; when exactly one is zero it is a triangle; when both vanish it is a point. The representation conjugate to (p,q) has labels (q,p) and negative representation weights. For p=q, this gives an additional inversion symmetry and a regular hexagon with 60∘ rotations. A general unequal-sided hexagon need not have that extra symmetry.
The shell multiplicities in an SU(3) weight diagram can be stated precisely. Let Pa,b​ be the convex hull of the Weyl group orbit of aω1​+bω2​. For λ in the root lattice coset Λ+Q,
mp,q​(λ)=k=0∑min(p,q)​1Pp−k,q−k​​(λ).​
(21)
representation weights outside this coset or outside the outer polygon are absent. This states that boundary representation weights have multiplicity one, each inward shell adds one, and the innermost triangular region has constant multiplicity min(p,q)+1. When p=q the innermost region is a single point. All representation weights of a triangular diagram have multiplicity one. Counting the diagram gives dim(p,q)=(p+1)(q+1)(p+q+2)/2. The examples below display both triangular orientations, a regular hexagon, an unequal-sided hexagon, a central multiplicity three, and the trivial diagram.

A fundamental matrix realization compatible with the root normalization is
H1​=21​diag(1,−1,0),H2​=23​1​diag(1,1,−2).
(22)
The weights of a representation on the coordinate states u,d,s are therefore
u=(21​,23​1​),d=(−21​,23​1​),s=(0,−3​1​).​
(23)
Each has weight multiplicity one. The weight diagram of the defining SU(3) representation is the corresponding equilateral triangle. These are (H1​,H2​) coordinates; the familiar vertical flavor hypercharge coordinate is Y=2H2​/3​.
For the adjoint representation of SU(3), the states are elements of the Lie algebra. The two Cartan generators commute with both Hi​, giving representation weight (0,0) with multiplicity two. Each root generator E±r​ is an eigenvector of the Cartan adjoint action with representation weight ±αr​. Consequently
adjoint weights: ±(1,0),±(−1/2,3​/2),±(1/2,3​/2),(0,0) twice.​
(24)
The six nonzero representation weights form a regular hexagon, with a doubly occupied centre. Its highest weight is α3​=ω1​+ω2​, so its Dynkin labels are (1,1) and its dimension is eight. The triplet and octet panels in the preceding figure give these two weight diagrams explicitly.

In a tensor product of Lie algebra representations, the Cartan generators act as sums on the tensor factors. Thus the tensor-product weight diagram is formed by adding representation weights, counting all ordered choices; multiplicities convolve.
For three triplets, the three vertices 3u,3d,3s each arise once. Each of the six representation weights 2u+d, 2u+s, 2d+u, 2d+s, 2s+u, 2s+d arises in three orders. The central representation weight u+d+s=0 arises in six orders. The resulting weight diagram has total dimension 3+6⋅3+6=27.
Its maximal highest weight is 3u=3ω1​, with Dynkin labels (3,0). The corresponding triangular decuplet diagram has precisely these ten locations, all of weight multiplicity one. Subtract it by highest-weight character subtraction. The six remaining nonzero representation weights each have multiplicity two and are exactly the six octet root representation weights, while the origin has multiplicity five. Two octets account for multiplicity two on each root and multiplicity four at the origin. The remaining origin is one singlet. Therefore the tensor cube of the defining SU(3) representation decomposes as
3⊗3⊗3=10(3,0)​⊕8(1,1)​⊕8(1,1)​⊕1(0,0)​.​
(25)
The dimension check is 10+8+8+1=27. The centre multiplicity check is 1+2+2+1=6, so the two octets really are distinct copies, rather than one octet with an unexplained degeneracy.

Take a local gauge transformation g(x) acting on the adjoint field by Φ′=gΦg−1. The connection convention Dμ​=∂μ​+[Aμ​,⋅] is compatible with
Aμ′​=gAμ​g−1−(∂μ​g)g−1,Dμ′​Φ′=g(Dμ​Φ)g−1.​
(29)
Indeed, differentiating Φ′ gives the extra commutator [(∂μ​g)g−1,Φ′], exactly cancelled by the inhomogeneous term in the transformed gauge potential.
For a direct proof of the Yang-Mills gauge transformation of curvature, put Ωμ​=(∂μ​g)g−1 and Bμ​=gAμ​g−1. Differentiation gives
∂μ​Bν​=g(∂μ​Aν​)g−1+[Ωμ​,Bν​],∂μ​Ων​−∂ν​Ωμ​=[Ωμ​,Ων​].
(30)
Substituting Aμ′​=Bμ​−Ωμ​ into the Yang-Mills field strength cancels all terms involving Ω and leaves
Fμν′​=gFμν​g−1.​
(31)
This direct calculation also applies when the adjoint action has a kernel.
Expand Dμ​Fνρ​+Dν​Fρμ​+Dρ​Fμν​. The mixed second derivatives of A cancel in pairs. The first-derivative terms from differentiating [Aν​,Aρ​] cancel the terms from [Aμ​,∂ν​Aρ​−∂ρ​Aν​]. The remaining sum is
[Aμ​,[Aν​,Aρ​]]+[Aν​,[Aρ​,Aμ​]]+[Aρ​,[Aμ​,Aν​]]=0
(32)
by the Jacobi identity. Thus the gauge-theory Bianchi identity is
Dμ​Fνρ​+Dν​Fρμ​+Dρ​Fμν​=0.​
(33)
For the variation of the Yang-Mills action, write aμ​=δAμ​. Differentiating the curvature gives δFμν​=Dμ​aν​−Dν​aμ​. Invariance of the Killing form implies
∂μ​κ(X,Y)=κ(Dμ​X,Y)+κ(X,Dμ​Y).
(34)
This is invariant integration by parts for a gauge covariant derivative. Using symmetry of the Killing form, antisymmetry of F, and compactly supported variations,
δSYM​​=2e21​∫d4xκ(δFμν​,Fμν)=e21​∫d4xκ(Dμ​aν​,Fμν)=−e21​∫d4xκ(aν​,Dμ​Fμν).​
(35)
Since the Killing form of a semisimple Lie algebra is nondegenerate and the variations are arbitrary, the Yang-Mills equations are
Dμ​Fμν=0.​
(36)
For the constant Yang-Mills theta term, symmetry under exchanging the two antisymmetric index pairs gives
δSθ​​=2θ∫d4xϵμνρσκ(δFμν​,Fρσ​)=4θ∫d4xϵμνρσκ(Dμ​aν​,Fρσ​)=−4θ∫d4xϵμνρσκ(aν​,Dμ​Fρσ​),​
(37)
up to the boundary variation of the Yang-Mills theta term. Contracting the gauge-theory Bianchi identity with ϵμνρσ makes the last integrand vanish. Therefore a constant theta term does not alter the bulk Yang-Mills equations: Dμ​Fμν=0. Its variation is a boundary term, so this conclusion uses compact support or boundary conditions that remove that term.
Finally apply Dρ to the gauge-theory Bianchi identity, obtaining
DρDρ​Fμν​=−DρDμ​Fνρ​−DρDν​Fρμ​.
(38)
The adjoint covariant derivative obeys [Dα​,Dβ​]X=[Fαβ​,X]. Commute Dρ past the other derivative. The differentiated divergences vanish by the Yang-Mills equations, leaving
DρDρ​Fμν​​=−[Fρμ​,Fνρ​]−[Fρν​,Fρμ​]=[Fμ​ρ,Fνρ​]+[Fμρ​,Fν​ρ].​
(39)
In the last line the second commutator needs its sign tracked carefully: directly,
−[Fρν​,Fρμ​]=−[Fν​ρ,Fμρ​]=[Fμρ​,Fν​ρ]=[Fμ​ρ,Fνρ​].
(40)
Thus the simplified sum gives the covariant wave equation for the Yang-Mills field strength
DρDρ​Fμν​=2[Fμ​ρ,Fνρ​].​
(41)
All spacetime derivatives and index manipulations here use the fixed flat metric.

Use the metric η=diag(1,−1,−1,−1) and first consider the connected Poincaré group, R1,3⋊SO+(1,3). Physical half-integer spins require unitary representations of its double cover R1,3⋊SL(2,C); genuine representations of the group without this cover permit only integer spin. Write Pμ=d(Pμ) and Mμν=d(Mμν). Strong continuity gives self-adjoint generators, with commutators understood on a common invariant dense domain.
The commuting translation generators have a joint spectral resolution. Lorentz transformations carry the spectral four-momentum p to Λp, so an irreducible representation has its momentum spectral measure concentrated on a single Lorentz orbit. For positive-energy particles the orbits of interest are the future mass shells p2=m2>0, or p2=0 with p=0 and p0>0. The zero-momentum orbit and spacelike or negative-energy orbits are outside these two particle cases.
Choose ϵ0123=1 and define the Pauli-Lubanski pseudovector with the overall sign convention
Wμ=−21​ϵμνρσPν​Mρσ​.
(42)
This sign makes the spatial rest-frame vector equal to mJ. The generators' Hermiticity and the antisymmetric epsilon contraction make Wμ Hermitian: the ordering correction from commuting P through M contracts metric tensors into epsilon and vanishes. The same antisymmetry proves Pauli-Lubanski orthogonality, WμPμ​=0.
The assumed commutators say that Pμ and Wμ transform as four-vectors and that W commutes with all translations. Consequently
C1​=Pμ​Pμ,C2​=Wμ​Wμ
(43)
commute with the entire Poincare algebra. For instance the two terms in the Lorentz commutator with a contracted vector square cancel. The Casimir operators therefore have fixed spectral values in a unitary irreducible representation, by the Schur lemma applied to their spectral projections. A second ingredient, the little group, distinguishes the internal states.
Fix a standard momentum k on the orbit. Its little group is the subgroup of Lorentz transformations leaving k fixed. Choose L(p) with L(p)k=p. A Lorentz transformation acts on the internal fibre by the Wigner rotation
R(Λ,p)=L(Λp)−1ΛL(p),R(Λ,p)k=k.
(44)
If dK​ is a unitary irreducible representation of this stabilizer, the induced representation acts on wavefunctions as
(U(a,Λ)ψ)(p)=e−ia⋅pdK​(R(Λ,Λ−1p))ψ(Λ−1p).
(45)
The measure d3p/(2p0) on either future mass shell is Lorentz invariant, so this action is unitary. The identity
R(Λ1​Λ2​,p)=R(Λ1​,Λ2​p)R(Λ2​,p)
(46)
proves the group multiplication law. Conversely, covariance of the translation spectral resolution supplies precisely these fibres and their stabilizer action. A commuting operator acts fibrewise; transitivity relates its fibre values, and irreducibility of dK​ makes it scalar. This explains why the construction gives, and classifies, the unitary irreducible representations on each orbit.
For a timelike momentum choose k=(m,0,0,0). A Lorentz transformation fixing it acts only as a rotation of its spatial orthogonal complement, so the massive particle little group is SO(3), or SU(2) in the double cover. Put
Ji​=21​ϵijk​Mjk,Ki​=M0i.
(47)
The Poincare algebra gives [Ji​,Jj​]=iϵijk​Jk​. The unitary irreducible representations of SU(2) are labelled by j=0,21​,1,…, with J2=j(j+1) and J3​ eigenvalues −j,−j+1,…,j. Evaluation of the Pauli-Lubanski pseudovector at k gives W0=0, W=mJ. Thus the massive Pauli-Lubanski Casimir and particle labels are
P2=m2,W2=−m2j(j+1),(m,j),2j+1 spin states.​
(48)
The full massive induced representation of the Poincare double cover is infinite-dimensional because momentum ranges continuously over the orbit; its finite internal multiplicity is 2j+1.
For a null momentum choose k=(E,0,0,E) with E>0. The infinitesimal stabilizer generators are
R=J3​,N1​=K1​−J2​,N2​=K2​+J1​.
(49)
Their commutators with momentum, evaluated at k, vanish. From the rotation and boost commutators of the Poincare algebra one obtains
[R,N1​]=iN2​,[R,N2​]=−iN1​,[N1​,N2​]=0.
(50)
These are the massless little-group generators of ISO(2): a planar rotation and two commuting null rotations, which act like translations in the little-group algebra. Hence the massless particle little group is the Euclidean plane group, with the appropriate double cover for half-integer spin.
The definition of W gives W0=P⋅J and W=P0J−P×K. At the standard null momentum,
Wμ=(ER,EN2​,−EN1​,ER),W2=−E2(N12​+N22​).
(51)
Suppose the little-group representation has finite dimension, as for particles with finitely many polarizations. The commuting Hermitian N1​,N2​ have a finite joint spectrum. Conjugation by e−iϕR rotates this spectrum through every planar angle. The only finite set invariant under all these rotations is the origin. Thus both Ni​ vanish, proving that finite-dimensional unitary representations of the massless little group have trivial translations. Irreducibility then leaves a one-dimensional rotation character, R=hI. The massless longitudinal Pauli-Lubanski eigenvalues are
P2=0,Wμ=hPμ,W2=0,h∈21​Z​
(52)
for the double cover, with h∈Z for a genuine SO(2) character. The number h is helicity. Different helicities have the same two Casimir eigenvalues, so the little group is indispensable for the classification. A connected-group irreducible representation has one helicity; if parity is also implemented, it exchanges h and −h and normally requires their pair.
Unitarity alone also permits nontrivial null-rotation generators. The rotation-invariant operator N12​+N22​ is scalar in an irreducible little-group representation; if it has value (ρ/E)2>0, one obtains a continuous-spin representation. A concrete realization on angular wavefunctions is
N1​=Eρ​cosϕ,N2​=Eρ​sinϕ,R=−idϕd​.
(53)
These operators satisfy the same commutators. Periodic or antiperiodic angular wavefunctions give integer or half-integer rotation modes, and the translation generators connect infinitely many such modes. The invariant labels are
P2=0,W2=−ρ2,ρ>0.​
(54)
These are the additional infinite-polarization massless unitary irreducible representations. Restricting to finitely many particle polarizations excludes them; they must not be ruled out merely by assuming unitarity. This completes the timelike and null cases of the Wigner classification.
