For N∼Pois(λ), the Poisson distribution gives
H(N)=λlog2​e−λlog2​λ+Elog2​(N!).
(8)
This information entropy is finite: log2​(n!)≤n2 for integer n≥0, while EN2=λ+λ2.
Fix 0<λ1​<λ2​. Take independent random variables U∼Pois(λ1​) and V∼Pois(λ2​−λ1​). Their convolution is
P(U+V=n)=e−λ2​∑j=0n​j!(n−j)!λ1j​(λ2​−λ1​)n−j​=e−λ2​n!λ2n​​,
(9)
by the binomial theorem. Thus U+V has Poisson distribution with parameter λ2​. Conditioning on V=v only translates U, so H(U+V∣V)=H(U)=F(λ1​). The entropy monotonicity under independent addition yields F(λ2​)≥F(λ1​).
In fact the inequality is strict. The covariance Cov(U+V,V)=Var(V)=λ2​−λ1​ is positive, so U+V and V are not independent random variables. Their information entropies are finite, and the equality criterion from part (a) excludes equality. Therefore the Poisson entropy monotonicity gives the stronger conclusion
λ2​>λ1​>0 ⟹ F(λ2​)>F(λ1​).​
