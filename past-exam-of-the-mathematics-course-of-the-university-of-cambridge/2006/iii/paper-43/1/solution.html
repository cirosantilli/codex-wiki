G(z)=∑n≥1​pn​F(z)n=P(F(z)).
(1)
The crucial point is that the count recurrence begins at n=2. Multiplying it by nzn−1 and summing, we obtain
P′(z)−p1​​=n≥2∑​(an+b)pn−1​zn−1=m≥1∑​(a(m+1)+b)pm​zm=azP′(z)+(a+b)P(z).​

Consequently (1−az)P′(z)=(a+b)P(z)+p1​. The chain rule applied to the aggregate probability generating function therefore gives
(1−aF(z))G′(z)=((a+b)G(z)+p1​)F′(z).
(3)
These identities hold inside the unit disk, or as identities of formal power series. Since F(0)=0, every coefficient of the composition depends on only finitely many count probabilities.

The required aggregate recursion for zero-truncated Panjer counts is initialized by g0​=0 and g1​=p1​f1​, and for every k≥1 it is

The empty sum at k=1 is zero. Every term on the right is known or has a smaller aggregate index. Omitting p1​fk​ would incorrectly apply the usual Panjer recursion with a zero initial value, producing zero for every aggregate probability.
For the zero-truncated Poisson distribution, pn​/pn−1​=λ/n for n≥2, so a=0, b=λ and p1​=λ/(eλ−1), with λ>0. Thus
