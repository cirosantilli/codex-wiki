Put r=∣x∣, n=x/r, and take a compactly supported Lighthill stress tensor. The isotropic term in that tensor is (p−c02​ρ)δij​: the Kronecker delta is implicit in the printed shorthand. Convolving the Lighthill acoustic analogy with the retarded acoustic Green function and integrating twice by parts in the source coordinates gives
ρ′(x,t)=∂i​∂j​∫4πc02​∣x−y∣Tij​(y,t−∣x−y∣/c0​)​d3y.
(1)
The boundary terms vanish because of compact support. For source size ℓ, the acoustic compact-source approximation requires ωℓ/c0​≪1. At r≫ℓ, we may therefore replace the denominator by r and the retarded argument by t−r/c0​ in the leading source integral. Define Sij​(t)=∫Tij​(y,t)d3y. In the radiation region ωr/c0​≫1, the leading two spatial derivatives act on the retarded time rather than on 1/r or n:
∂i​∂j​rSij​(t−r/c0​)​∼c02​rni​nj​​S¨ij​(t−r/c0​).
(2)
Thus the leading acoustic quadrupole density is
ρ′(x,t)∼4πc04​r3xi​xj​​S¨ij​(t−r/c0​).​
(3)
For the compact acoustic quadrupole Mach-number scaling, let U be the source fluctuation speed, m=U/c0​≪1, and use the advective source time ℓ/U. With Tij​=O(ρ0​U2), one has Sij​=O(ρ0​U2ℓ3) and S¨ij​=O(ρ0​U4ℓ). Consequently
ρ0​ρ′​=O(m4rℓ​).​
(4)
The fourth power refers to the radiation coefficient with geometric spreading separated. It assumes that the source frequency scales as U/ℓ; an independently imposed frequency or an independently strong thermal source would change that Mach number estimate.

Let Dij​=Tij+​−Tij−​, so the Lighthill stress tensor is Tij−​+Dij​H(S). Write Si​=∂i​S and Sij​=∂i​∂j​S. The distributional derivative of the Heaviside step function gives ∂i​H(S)=Si​δ(S). Applying the product rule a second time yields the surface sources of a discontinuous Lighthill stress tensor:
∂i​∂j​Tij​=​H(S)∂i​∂j​Tij+​+H(−S)∂i​∂j​Tij−​+(Dij,i​Sj​+Dij,j​Si​+Dij​Sij​)δ(S)+Dij​Si​Sj​δ′(S).​
(8)
Repeated indices are summed. The first line gives the two bulk acoustic quadrupole distributions. The second line consists of additional surface delta distributions and their derivatives, all supported on the shock wave S=0. For a symmetric Lighthill stress tensor, the first two coefficients of δ(S) combine to 2Dij,i​Sj​ after relabelling indices.
A discontinuity therefore adds both a surface-delta source and a surface-delta-derivative source. The level function need not be a signed distance: retaining its derivatives makes the expression invariant under a smooth reparametrization of the same surface. If the two bulk fields are only continuously differentiable, their second derivatives in the first line are read weakly; no classical second derivative is being assumed. The coefficients multiplying δ′(S) are kept as extensions, not replaced prematurely by traces.

A rigid zero-thickness screen imposes the same normal-derivative cancellation on both faces; the scattered velocity potential is consequently odd in y. Across the open part x>0 it is continuous, so its trace there is zero. These complementary half-line data are what make the Wiener-Hopf method applicable.
Use the Fourier transform ϕ​(κ,y)=∫R​ϕ(x,y)eiκxdx, with inverse exponential e−iκx. The outgoing Helmholtz equation solution is ϕ​=F−(κ)e−γy for y>0, where γ=κ2−k02​​ has γ=ik02​−κ2​ on the propagating interval and positive real part on evanescent components. The limiting absorption principle supplies the contour prescription. The transform F− of the trace supported on x<0 is analytic below the contour. Let G+ be the analytic upper transform of the unknown derivative on x>0. The prescribed derivative on x<0 has transform
∫−∞0​(−ib)ei(κ+a)xdx=−κ+ab​.
(11)
Since the upper normal derivative is −γF−, the Wiener-Hopf equation is
γF−+G+=κ+ab​.
(12)
For the Wiener-Hopf factorization, choose γ+​=(κ−k0​)1/2 analytic above and γ−​=(κ+k0​)1/2 analytic below. Their branch cuts go into the opposite half-planes, with γ+​(−a)=ik0​+a​. Dividing by γ+​ and performing pole subtraction in a Wiener-Hopf equation gives
(κ+a)γ+​(κ)b​=(κ+a)γ+​(−a)b​+κ+ab​[γ+​(κ)1​−γ+​(−a)1​].
(13)
The forcing pole is allocated to the lower analytic part; the second quotient has a removable pole and is upper analytic. Subtracting these parts leaves a common entire function. The finite-energy edge condition excludes an inverse-square-root singularity in the potential: its odd scattered trace is O(∣x∣​), so F−=O(∣κ∣−3/2). The normal derivative on the open side can be O(x−1/2), giving G+=O(∣κ∣−1/2). Both sides of the entire remainder tend to zero. The Liouville theorem sets that remainder to zero, giving the explicit Wiener-Hopf solution of rigid half-plane diffraction:
F−(κ)=(κ+a)γ+​(−a)γ−​(κ)b​=(κ+a)κ+k0​​−i2k0​​sin(θ0​/2)​.​
(14)
The scattered field on either side of the screen is therefore
ϕ(x,y)=2πsgny​∫F−(κ)e−iκx−γ∣y∣dκ.
(15)
Its forcing-pole residue at κ=−a is −i. Deforming to the outgoing steepest descent contour crosses this pole precisely when ∣θ∣>π−θ0​, where x=rcosθ, y=rsinθ and −π<θ<π. The residue contributes sgn(y)eiax−ib∣y∣. Thus, away from transition boundaries, the scattered geometrical optics field is
ϕGO​=⎩⎨⎧​eiax−iby,−eiax+iby,0,​π−θ0​<θ<π,−π<θ<θ0​−π,otherwise.​
(16)
Adding the incident acoustic plane wave gives incident plus unit specular reflection in the upper reflected sector, just the incident wave in the remaining illuminated sectors, and zero in the lower shadow sector. The geometrical shadow boundaries also follow directly by tracing a straight ray to y=0: the reflected ray hits the screen at x+ycotθ0​<0, and the transmitted incident ray crosses the open part at x−ycotθ0​>0.
For the diffracted contribution, the outgoing phase −iκrcosθ−γr∣sinθ∣ has saddle κs​=k0​cosθ, value −ik0​r, and second derivative ir/(k0​sin2θ). Stationary phase therefore supplies 2πk0​/r​∣sinθ∣e−ik0​r+iπ/4. Substituting F−(κs​), and using sgn(y)∣sinθ∣/1+cosθ​=2​sin(θ/2), gives

There is also a sign inconsistency in the supplied saddle formula: with its printed inverse exponential e+iκx, the outgoing saddle is −k0​cosθ, not +k0​cosθ. The consistent inverse convention used above gives the requested outgoing field without that ambiguity.

For n>0, k0​hsinα=nπ. Hence the numerator and denominator of the combined independent-edge radiation from an open planar duct both vanish at θ=±α. At the positive angle, the ratio has the removable limit
limθ→α​cosθ−cosαsin(k0​hsinθ)​=−(−1)nk0​hcotα.
(21)
At the negative angle the ratio changes sign, as does sin(θ/2), so both directions give the same total velocity potential. Since 2sin(α/2)cos(α/2)=sinα, the result is
ϕd​(±α)∼2ikh​πk0​r2​​e−ik0​r−iπ/4(n>0).​
(22)
For the zero mode, use α=0 directly: sin(θ/2)sin(k0​hsinθ)/(cosθ−1)→−k0​h. Its two directions coincide and
ϕd​(0)∼ik0​hD(r)(n=0).​
(23)
The two-edge field is finite in the constituent propagation directions. Its magnitude there is enhanced by a factor of order kh over an ordinary isolated-edge diffracted amplitude away from a shadow boundary. An isolated-edge angular formula has a pole in these directions, because its geometrical optics and edge-wave split is nonuniform; the exact single-edge field is not infinite. In the combined field the equal and opposite pole residues cancel, and the difference of endpoint propagation phases produces the finite limit above. Taking this combined limit describes the leading far-field beam; it does not justify evaluating either nonuniform single-edge expression separately at its pole.

q∼s24δθ​∑j=0∞​(2j+1)2+(θ/s)21​.
(48)
For the shock-layer scaling θ=sη, the passage to this sum follows from dominated convergence: coshz−1≥z2/2 and 1−cosθ≥cθ2 for small ∣θ∣ provide a summable bound proportional to [(2j+1)2+η2]−1. The same leading expression matches the outer small-angle range s≪∣θ∣≪1. More generally, split the exact sum at (2j+1)s=b with max(s,∣θ∣)≪b≪1: the low terms have vanishing relative Taylor error, while the discarded exact and approximate tails are smaller than the leading expression by O(max(s,∣θ∣)/b).
Now apply the partial-fraction identity for the hyperbolic tangent with η=θ/s:
∑j=0∞​(2j+1)2+η21​=4ηπ​tanh(πη/2).
(49)
The Fay shock-layer asymptotics are therefore
q(θ,Z)∼Zπ​tanh(2δZπθ​),∣θ∣≪1, δZ≪1.​
(50)
The angular shock thickness is O(δZ), and its matched states are ±π/Z. At θ=0, both formulas vanish; the limiting slopes agree. The denominator throughout this derivation is sinh(nδZ) as printed in the PDF, not the erroneous sin(nδZ) in the converted TeX.
