The mean-field approximation replaces each site's fluctuating surroundings by a self-consistent average. On a cubic lattice each spin has coordination number of a lattice z=2D, even though summing only over positive basis directions counts each bond once. Write σn​=M+δσn​ and drop products of deviations. Each Ising bond becomes σn​σm​≃Mσn​+Mσm​−M2, so the approximate statistical Hamiltonian is
HMF​=−zJM∑n​σn​+2NzJ​M2.
(9)
The constant corrects counting each bond twice. The resulting independent-spin partition function is ZMF​=e−βT​NzJM2/2[2cosh(βT​zJM)]N, where βT​=1/(kB​T). The one-spin probabilities give
⟨σ⟩=eβT​zJM+e−βT​zJMeβT​zJM−e−βT​zJM​.
(10)
Equating this average with the assumed M gives
M=tanh(kB​T2DJM​),Tc​=kB​2DJ​.​
(11)
Here J>0 is assumed; a ferromagnetic order parameter is not the appropriate choice for an antiferromagnetic coupling. Put A=Tc​/T. For A≤1, tanh(AM)<AM≤M at any M>0, so only M=0 is possible. For A>1, the function tanh(AM)−M has positive slope at zero, is strictly concave on M>0 and is negative at M=1. It therefore has exactly one positive zero and a spin-reversed negative zero. Expanding at the bifurcation gives
0=(A−1)M−3A3M3​+O(M5),M2=A33(A−1)​+O((A−1)2)∼3Tc​Tc​−T​.
(12)
The transition is continuous, with order-parameter critical exponent β=1/2. Stability can also be checked from the Bragg-Williams free energy of the Ising model per Ising spin:
fBW​(M)=−2zJ​M2+kB​T{21+M​log21+M​+21−M​log21−M​}.
(13)
Its derivative is −zJM+kB​TarctanhM, giving the same self-consistency equation. Near zero,
fBW​(M)=−kB​Tlog2+2kB​T−zJ​M2+12kB​T​M4+O(M6).
(14)
The quartic coefficient is positive at Tc​, the stable minima approach zero continuously, and the minimized free energy has no first-derivative jump. Thus mean-field approximation predicts a second-order transition at Tc​=2DJ/kB​. This is the mean-field Ising critical bifurcation.
For short-range interactions, large dimension gives many neighbours and suppresses the relative fluctuations of their average. More quantitatively, Gaussian field theory fluctuations averaged over a correlation volume have variance of order ξ2−D, while the squared ordinary mean-field order parameter is of order ∣t∣∼ξ−2. Their ratio is proportional to ξ4−D. The Ginzburg criterion therefore makes neglect of critical fluctuations asymptotically consistent above four dimensions, inconsistent below four, and marginal at four, where logarithms occur. Large dimension is a route to validity; sufficiently long-range or infinite-range interactions can also produce mean-field behaviour. In low-dimensional short-range systems even the existence and location of a transition can differ from this approximation.
