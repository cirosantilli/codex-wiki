Assume X is a connected compact Riemann surface of geometric genus g≥1. Work first in Picg−1(X), translating back to the Jacobian variety by tensoring with OX​(−(g−1)p0​) when desired. Put
Θ=Wg−1​={L:degL=g−1, h0(X,L)>0}.
(26)
This is the image of the Abelian sum map from X(g−1). The Riemann singularity theorem asserts that this image is a reduced Cartier divisor and that
multL​Θ=h0(X,L).​
(27)
Here multiplicity means the order of a local defining equation in the maximal ideal of the smooth ambient Jacobian variety. Consequently the singular points are precisely the classes with at least two independent sections. We will prove the multiplicity equality, including the nonvanishing of its proposed leading term.
Choose an effective divisor E of degree n≥g, and a local holomorphic family L of degree-g−1 line bundles about L on X×U, where U is a small neighborhood in Picg−1(X). Such a family is obtained by varying the Čech cocycles of a line bundle. Since degL′(E)>2g−2 for all L′∈U, Serre duality gives H1(L′(E))=0; the Riemann-Roch theorem gives h0(L′(E))=n. The cohomology and base change for line bundles on a curve then identifies
A=π∗​L(E),B=π∗​(L(E)∣E​)
(28)
as vector bundles of rank n. Evaluation defines a matrix M:A→B. For each L′, its exact sequence is
0⟶H0(L′)⟶AL′​M(L′)​BL′​⟶H1(L′)⟶0.
(29)
Thus f=detM vanishes exactly on Θ.
The function f is not identically zero. To exhibit a degree-g−1 line bundle with no section, choose g distinct points p1​,…,pg​ giving independent evaluation functionals on H0(KX​). Such points can be chosen successively because a nonzero holomorphic differential form cannot vanish everywhere. For F=∑pi​, the Riemann-Roch theorem gives h0(OX​(F))=1. Choose q outside F. The unique section of OX​(F) does not vanish at q, so OX​(F−q) has no section. Therefore the evaluation determinant genuinely cuts out a hypersurface. Its support is irreducible, since it is the image of the irreducible symmetric product of a curve X(g−1).
Fix now L∈Θ, and put r=h0(L)=h1(L), the equality following from the Riemann-Roch theorem. The matrix M(L) has an invertible (n−r) by (n−r) block. After invertible holomorphic row and column operations, its local form is
(In−r​0​0N(x)​),N(0)=0,
(30)
where N is r by r. More explicitly, eliminate the invertible block by its Schur complement; the discarded determinant is a holomorphic unit. Hence f is a unit times detN. Every entry of N lies in the maximal ideal, so its order is at least r.
The linear term of N is the cup-product map
N1​(ξ):H0(L)⟶H1(L),s⟼ξ⌣s,ξ∈H1(OX​).
(31)
To verify this, deform the transition functions as gij​(1+εξij​) and try to lift a section s to s+εs′. Its first-order gluing equation has right side ξij​sj​, whose Čech cohomology class is exactly the obstruction to the lift. The kernel-to-cokernel derivative of the evaluation matrix gives that same obstruction. Under Serre duality, if s1​,…,sr​ and t1​,…,tr​ are bases of H0(L) and H0(KX​⊗L−1), then
(N1​(ξ))ji​=⟨ξ,si​tj​⟩.
(32)
We must show that this matrix is invertible for some ξ; otherwise the lower bound on multiplicity would not be an equality.
For each of these two r-dimensional section spaces, there are r distinct points with independent evaluations. Choose them successively, using a section in the remaining kernel to find the next point. The set of such r-tuples is a nonempty open subset of Xr. The two open sets intersect because Xr is irreducible, and we may avoid their diagonals. Choose q1​,…,qr​ in that intersection and trivialize L and KX​ there. Both matrices
Ski​=si​(qk​),Tkj​=tj​(qk​)
(33)
are invertible. Let ξ∈H0(KX​)∗=H1(OX​) be the sum of the evaluation functionals at these points, with any nonzero weights ck​. Then
N1​(ξ)=Ttdiag(c1​,…,cr​)S
(34)
is invertible. It follows that the degree-r polynomial detN1​ is nonzero, so ordL​f=r. This proves the needed invertible cup-product direction for a line bundle on a curve by an explicit construction.
We still need to identify this determinant hypersurface with the reduced image, rather than a multiple of it. Choose g−1 distinct points imposing independent conditions on H0(KX​). For their sum D, the Riemann-Roch theorem gives h0(D)=h0(KX​−D)=1. At its class, the calculation above gives order one. The determinant hypersurface has irreducible support, so its sole divisor on an algebraic curve coefficient is one everywhere. A local hypersurface in a smooth space with these divisor on an algebraic curve coefficients is reduced. Thus it is exactly the reduced image Wg−1​, and the multiplicity formula holds for that image itself.
The argument gives the useful additional description
the tangent cone at L is defined by det(ξ⌣−)=0.​
(35)
For r=1, its nonzero linear equation is ⟨ξ,st⟩=0. This agrees with the derivative of the Abelian sum map: if div(s)=D, then st is the unique holomorphic differential form vanishing along D. For r≥2, the local equation has no linear term and the point is singular. When g=1, W0​ is the single trivial line bundle in Pic0(X), with multiplicity one; the argument includes this case using the empty effective divisor. In genus zero there is no Wg−1​ of effective divisors on an algebraic curve, so that hypothesis is necessary.
