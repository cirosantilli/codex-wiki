Every nonzero P-adic number has a unique decomposition z=pmu with m∈Z and u∈Zp×​. The group homomorphism kernel of reduction from the unit group onto Fp×​ is 1+pZp​, the group of principal units. Each nonzero residue class has a unique lift ω satisfying ωp−1=1, by the simple-root Hensel lemma proved in question 3. Uniqueness makes these lifts multiplicative. They form the cyclic group μp−1​, and every unit has a unique factorization
u=ωv,ω∈μp−1​,v∈1+pZp​.
(1)
Thus Qp×​=pZ×μp−1​×(1+pZp​).
For odd p, the p-adic logarithm and p-adic exponential function give inverse group isomorphisms 1+pZp​↔pZp​. To verify the convergence domain, for t∈pZp​ the p-adic logarithm terms have valuations nvp​(t)−vp​(n)→∞, and the exponential terms have valuations nvp​(t)−vp​(n!)→∞, since vp​(n!)≤(n−1)/(p−1) and p>2. In both series every term after the linear one has strictly larger valuation than the linear term. Consequently log(1+t)∈pZp​ and exp(t)∈1+pZp​. The formal identities log(vw)=logv+logw and exp(logv)=v, log(expt)=t hold on these convergent domains, as follows by multiplying the convergent series or passing to their formal identities termwise.
Choose a generator ζ of μp−1​. An explicit isomorphism is
Z/(p−1)Z×Zp​×Z⟶Qp×​,(a,b,m)⟼ζaexp(pb)pm.​
(2)
The inverse reads off the valuation, the root-of-unity unit factor, and p−1log(v). Both directions are continuous with the standard product topology, so this is also a topological group isomorphism. The odd-prime condition is essential for using all of 1+pZp​ as the p-adic logarithm-isomorphism domain.

For a finite extension of fields complete for a Non-Archimedean absolute value, use the unique extended field absolute value. The extension is unramified when its residue extension is a separable field extension and
[L:K]=[kL​:kK​].
(3)
Equivalently, it has ramification index one, a separable field extension of residue fields, and no defect. For general nondiscrete valued fields, merely saying that the ramification index is one is insufficient; the displayed degree equality is part of the unramified condition.
Put n=[L:K]=[kL​:kK​]. Since xˉ generates the residue extension, 1,xˉ,…,xˉn−1 are linearly independent over kK​. Their lifts 1,x,…,xn−1 are linearly independent over K: a nonzero relation could be divided by a coefficient of largest field absolute value, giving an integral relation whose reduction has at least one nonzero coefficient, contrary to residue independence. They therefore form a K-basis of L.
For any y=∑j=0n−1​aj​xj, choose ar​ of largest field absolute value. All coefficients of y/ar​ belong to OK​, and at least one is a unit. Its reduction is a nonzero linear combination of the residue basis, so y/ar​ is a unit of OL​. This proves the residue-basis norm formula for an unramified extension
∣y∣=maxj​∣aj​∣.
(4)
Hence y∈OL​ exactly when every aj​∈OK​. We conclude
OL​=j=0⨁n−1​OK​xj=OK​[x].​
(5)
The last equality also uses the fact that every polynomial in the integral element x is integral. The proof requires neither a uniformizer nor discrete valuation, and applies to every residue generator specified in the question.

The Mahler theorem states that a function f:Zp​→Qp​ is continuous if and only if it has a unique expansion
f(x)=∑n≥0​cn​(nx​),cn​∈Qp​,cn​⟶0,
(6)
and the expansion converges uniformly. Moreover ∥f∥∞​=supn​∣cn​∣p​; in particular f takes values in Zp​ exactly when all cn​ belong to Zp​. Here (0x​)=1 and (nx​)=x(x−1)⋯(x−n+1)/n!. These binomial polynomials are continuous and integer-valued on Zp​, since they are integer-valued on the dense nonnegative integers. The coefficient condition cn​→0 therefore makes the series uniformly convergent.
Evaluate at a nonnegative integer m. Terms with n>m vanish, giving the finite triangular relation f(m)=∑n=0m​(nm​)cn​. Binomial inversion gives the Mahler coefficients
cn​=j=0∑n​(−1)n−j(jn​)f(j)=(Δnf)(0),Δf(x)=f(x+1)−f(x).​
(7)
To obtain the generating function, multiply the last finite identity by Tn/n! and compare coefficients. Setting n=j+k yields
∑n≥0​n!cn​​Tn=∑j,k≥0​j!k!(−1)kf(j)​Tj+k=e−Tj≥0∑​j!f(j)​Tj.​
(8)
The Mahler coefficient exponential generating function is an identity in Qp​[[T]]: each coefficient is a finite sum. It does not assert convergence for every p-adic substitution for T.

Let cn​ be the Mahler coefficients of f. Define its discrete antiderivative by
g(x)=n≥0∑​cn​(n+1x​).​
(9)
Because cn​→0 and the binomial polynomials have field absolute value at most one on Zp​, this series converges uniformly to a continuous Zp​-valued function. Every summand vanishes at zero. Pascal's identity gives (n+1x+1​)−(n+1x​)=(nx​), so taking differences through the uniformly convergent series proves g(x+1)−g(x)=f(x). For nonnegative integers, induction from g(0)=0 gives precisely g(m)=∑j=0m−1​f(j). Thus this is the required continuous extension, unique because the nonnegative integers are dense in Zp​.
The Mahler expansion of g has coefficient zero in degree zero and coefficient cm−1​ in degree m≥1. This discrete antidifferentiation on the p-adic integers is the mechanism behind the next part.

Take an arbitrary f∈C(Zp​,Qp​). Compactness of Zp​ bounds its field absolute value, so some pmf takes values in Zp​. Apply part (ii) to obtain a continuous h with h(x+1)−h(x)=pmf(x). Then g=p−mh belongs to the same continuous-function space and satisfies f=g(⋅+1)−g.
Translation invariance by one alone now gives L(f)=L(g(⋅+1))−L(g)=0. Since f was arbitrary,
L=0.​
(10)
This proves translation-invariant linear forms on p-adic continuous functions vanish without assuming that the linear form itself is continuous. A proof using only density of polynomials would not suffice for an arbitrary, potentially discontinuous linear form; surjectivity of the forward difference operator on the entire function space is what is needed.

We first prove the simple-root Hensel lemma directly. Put x1​=x. Suppose xn​≡x(modπ) and f(xn​)≡0(modπn). The derivative remains a unit since its reduction agrees with f′(x). Choose tn​∈OK​ whose residue solves
πnf(xn​)​+tn​f′(xn​)≡0(modπ),
(11)
and set xn+1​=xn​+πntn​. Polynomial expansion gives f(xn+1​)=f(xn​)+πntn​f′(xn​)+π2nB with B∈OK​. Since 2n≥n+1, the new value vanishes modulo πn+1. The sequence is Cauchy because v(xn+1​−xn​)≥n, and completeness supplies y∈OK​ with y≡x(modπ). Continuity gives f(y)=0.
For uniqueness, if y,z are two roots in that residue class, then
0=f(y)−f(z)=(y−z)(f′(z)+(y−z)B1​),B1​∈OK​.
(12)
The factor in parentheses is a unit, since f′(z) is a unit and y−z∈πOK​. Thus y=z. This proves existence and uniqueness of the specified lifted root.
Now suppose the residue field has q=pr elements and put e=v(p). Every element of kK×​ is a simple root of Tq−1−1. The Hensel lemma therefore lifts its q−1 roots uniquely to a subgroup of the roots of unity in K. These are the Teichmuller representatives and give all the prime-to-p torsion. To verify the latter assertion, if a root of unity u≡1 has order m prime to p, write u=1+t. In um−1=mt+(2m​)t2+⋯, the first term has valuation v(t) and all later terms have greater valuation, so the sum cannot vanish unless t=0. Reduction is therefore injective on prime-to-p torsion, whose order divides q−1.
Any root of unity has a unique decomposition into a p-power-order factor and a prime-to-p factor, by splitting its finite order using coprime powers. For a primitive path root ζ, a≥1, reduction in characteristic p gives ζˉ​=1. The cyclotomic identity
p=Φpa​(1)=∏1≤j≤pap∤j​​(1−ζj)
(13)
shows that all factors have the same valuation: (ζj−1)/(ζ−1)=1+ζ+⋯+ζj−1 reduces to j=0. Consequently the valuation of a primitive p-power root of unity is
v(ζ−1)=pa−1(p−1)e​.​
(14)
The left side is a positive integer. Hence pa−1(p−1)≤e, so the possible p-power orders are bounded. Combining this bound with the prime-to-p result bounds all root orders by one integer, proving finiteness. A finite multiplicative subgroup of a field is cyclic, and it contains exactly the full prime-to-p subgroup of order q−1. Thus
∣μ(K)∣=ps(q−1)for some s≥0.​
(15)
If e<p−1, even a primitive pth root would violate the valuation formula, so s=0.
At the boundary e=p−1, assume first that p is odd. Take K+​=Qp​(ζp​). The polynomial Φp​(1+T)=p+(2p​)T+⋯+Tp−1 is Eisenstein, so K+​/Qp​ is totally ramified of degree p−1 and vK+​​(p)=p−1. It contains a primitive pth root, while the valuation formula excludes primitive p2th roots. Thus s=1.
For the other example, take K0​=Qp​(π) with πp−1=p. The polynomial Tp−1−p is Eisenstein, giving the same ramification index and residue field Fp​. If a primitive pth root belonged to K0​, the valuation formula would give ζp​−1=uπ with u a unit. Substitute this into Φp​(1+(ζp​−1))=0 and divide by p. All intermediate terms reduce to zero, leaving 1+uˉp−1=0 in Fp​. But uˉp−1=1, contradicting 2=0. Thus s=0 in K0​. This proves both cases of roots of unity at the tame ramification boundary for odd primes.
The printed boundary claim needs the qualification p>2. When p=2, every characteristic-zero field already contains the root −1 of order two, so s=0 is impossible. If v(2)=1, the valuation formula excludes order four and higher, forcing s=1. For example K=Q2​ has exactly this behavior. This is a genuine exception to the final request as printed, rather than an omitted construction.

The valuation ring is O={x∈K:∣x∣≤1}, and m={x∈K:∣x∣<1} is an ideal by the ultrametric inequality. Its complement inside O consists exactly of elements of field absolute value one, whose inverses also lie in O. Every proper ideal must avoid units and hence lie in m. Therefore m is the unique maximal ideal: O is a local ring. Also its fraction field is K, since every nonzero element outside the ring has its inverse inside it.
If z∈K is integral over O, it satisfies a monic equation zn+an−1​zn−1+⋯+a0​=0 with ∣ai​∣≤1. Were ∣z∣>1, the leading term would have strictly larger field absolute value than every other term, so the ultrametric inequality would prevent cancellation to zero. Thus z∈O, proving the valuation ring is integrally closed.
For the ideal criterion, assume the valuation is nontrivial and write v=−log∣⋅∣. If its value group is discrete, normalize it to Z and choose a uniformizer π of valuation one. In any nonzero ideal I, the nonnegative integer valuations have a least value m. Choose a∈I of that value. For every b∈I, v(b/a)≥0, so b∈aO. Hence I=(a)=(πm) and O is a principal ideal domain.
Conversely, if O is a principal ideal domain, its nonzero maximal ideal is (π). For every element x of positive valuation, x∈m implies x=πy with y∈O, so v(x)≥v(π)>0. Thus γ=v(π) is the least positive value. For any value t, subtract ⌊t/γ⌋γ. The remainder belongs to the value group and lies in [0,γ), so is zero. The value group is therefore γZ, and the valuation is discrete. We have proved the principal ideal criterion for a nontrivial rank-one valuation ring.
The nontriviality qualification matters under the standard definition of discrete valuation, which requires value group isomorphic to Z. With the trivial field absolute value, O=K is still a principal ideal domain, but the value group is zero and there is no uniformizer. Thus the literal unrestricted equivalence has a field exception. If “discrete” instead includes the trivial value group as a discrete subgroup of R, that exceptional case satisfies the equivalence too.

Krasner's lemma states: let K be complete for a Non-Archimedean absolute value, let α be a separable algebraic element over K, and let β be algebraic over K, all in a fixed algebraic closure with the extended field absolute value. If
∣β−α∣<∣α′−α∣for every K-conjugate α′=α,
(16)
then K(α)⊆K(β). If α∈K, the conclusion is automatic and there is no conjugate-distance condition to check.
To prove it, put F=K(β) and take a finite splitting field N/F for the minimal polynomial of α over F. That is a separable polynomial, so N/F is Galois. The finite extension F is complete, and uniqueness of extensions of an field absolute value from a complete non-Archimedean field makes every σ∈Gal(N/F) an isometry. It fixes β, so
∣σ(α)−α∣≤max{∣σ(α)−β∣,∣β−α∣}=∣β−α∣.
(17)
If σ(α)=α, it is another K-conjugate and this inequality contradicts the strict hypothesis. Every such automorphism therefore fixes α. The Fundamental theorem of Galois theory gives α∈F, proving
K(α)⊆K(β).​
(18)
Only α needs to be a separable algebraic element: the proof works even when β is inseparable, because the splitting field is taken over K(β).

Choose compatible primitive roots of unity ζpn+1​, with ζpn+2p​=ζpn+1​. Use the slightly faster-converging series permitted by “or otherwise”:
AN​=∑n=0N​p2nζpn+1​.
(19)
The terms have field absolute value p−2n, so the partial sums are a Cauchy sequence in Q​p​. We show that an algebraic limit would contradict the growth of cyclotomic extension degrees. The faster weights make the strict conjugate-distance estimate work uniformly also at p=2.
Put LN​=Qp​(ζpN+1​). Its degree is pN(p−1): the translated cyclotomic polynomial ΦpN+1​(1+T) has constant term p and reduces modulo p to TpN(p−1), so is Eisenstein. To verify the reduction, write it as ∑j=0p−1​(1+T)jpN and use characteristic p to reduce this to ∑j=0p−1​(1+TpN)j=TpN(p−1). All roots lie in LN​, and its Galois automorphisms are σa​(ζ)=ζa for units a modulo pN+1.
For a nonidentity automorphism, put r=vp​(a−1), choosing a representative with 0≤r≤N. It fixes the terms with n<r. For n≥r, the root ζpn+1a−1​ has order pn+1−r, so the root-of-unity valuation formula, with vp​(p)=1, gives
vp​(p2n(σa​ζpn+1​−ζpn+1​))=2n+pn−r(p−1)1​.
(20)
This is strictly increasing as n increases: successive differences are 2−p−(n−r+1)>0. Thus the first changed term is the unique term of smallest valuation and cannot cancel. Consequently
vp​(σa​AN​−AN​)=2r+p−11​≤2N+p−11​.
(21)
In particular no nonidentity automorphism fixes AN​, so Qp​(AN​)=LN​. Also the distance from AN​ to every distinct conjugate is at least p−2N−1/(p−1).
Suppose the Cauchy sequence converged to A∈Q​p​. The tail estimate gives
∣A−AN​∣p​≤p−2N−2<p−2N−1/(p−1).
(22)
Apply Krasner's lemma with α=AN​, β=A. It forces LN​=Qp​(AN​)⊆Qp​(A) for every N. The right-hand side has finite degree, while [LN​:Qp​]=pN(p−1) is unbounded, a contradiction. Therefore
Q​p​ is not complete.​
(23)
This proves algebraic closure of the p-adic numbers is not complete by an explicit algebraic Cauchy sequence and a fully quantified conjugate-separation argument.
