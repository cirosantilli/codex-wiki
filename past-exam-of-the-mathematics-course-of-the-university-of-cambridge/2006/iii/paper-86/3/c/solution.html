First resolve the orientation sign in quadrant harmonic spectral inversion. The printed spectral definitions traverse the physical boundary clockwise, whereas both reconstruction rays run outward from zero. Integrating first in k gives the kernel i/(z−w). The resulting clockwise Cauchy integral is the negative of the desired holomorphic function. Thus, with exactly the spectral definitions used above, the consistent reconstruction is
qz​(z)=−2π1​[∫0∞​eikzq​2​(k)dk+∫0i∞​eikzq​1​(k)dk].
(34)
The printed plus sign would reconstruct −qz​. For a concrete nonzero example, q(z)=−2Re[(1+i)/(z+1+i)] is real, harmonic, smooth on the closed quadrant and decays along both axes. With β1​=β2​=0, it has h1​(0)=h2​(0)=1 and qz​=(1+i)/(z+1+i)2. The printed orientation returns the negative of this derivative, so the issue persists even under the stated corner condition.
Let a=21​e−iβ1​−iγ. In the expressions from the preceding part, the coefficient of Φ on the imaginary ray is a, and the coefficient on the real ray is −ae4iγ. For each of the three allowed sums, e4iγ=1. The unknown part is therefore proportional to
∫0i∞​eikzΦ(k)dk−∫0∞​eikzΦ(k)dk=0.
(35)
Indeed, close the first spectral quadrant. For z=x+iy with x,y>0, ∣eikz∣=e−(xImk+yRek), so the large-arc contribution vanishes for bounded Φ. The Cauchy integral theorem proves the equality and eliminates the single unknown function.
Substituting only the known pieces leaves
qz​(z)=2π1​[eiβ2​∫0∞​eikzH2​(k)dk−eiβ2​+iγ∫0∞​eikzH1​(−k)dk−e−iβ1​∫0i∞​eikzH1​(k)dk+e−iβ1​−iγ∫0i∞​eikzH2​(−k)dk].
(36)
This already determines the answer from h1​,h2​ alone. Evaluating the elementary ray kernels gives the more direct rational-angle oblique derivative problem on a quadrant formula
qz​(z)=2πi​[​eiβ2​∫0∞​z−xh2​(x)​dx+e−iβ1​−iγ∫0∞​z+xh2​(x)​dx−e−iβ1​∫0∞​z−iyh1​(y)​dy−eiβ2​+iγ∫0∞​z+iyh1​(y)​dy].​​
(37)
No denominator vanishes for an interior point of the first quadrant.
For explicit formulas in the three cases, define
I2​(z)=∫0∞​z2−x2h2​(x)​dx,I1​(z)=∫0∞​z2+y2h1​(y)​dy,
(38)
K2​(z)=∫0∞​z2−x2xh2​(x)​dx,K1​(z)=∫0∞​z2+y2yh1​(y)​dy.
(39)
Combining the paired fractions and using β2​=γ−β1​ yields
qz​(z)=πe−iβ1​​⎩⎨⎧​iz[I2​(z)−I1​(z)],K1​(z)−K2​(z),−iz[I2​(z)+I1​(z)],​γ=0,γ=π/2,γ=π.​​
(40)
There is also a necessary corner compatibility for collinear oblique derivative data. When γ=π/2, the two prescribed derivative directions are opposites, so a continuous corner gradient requires h2​(0)=−h1​(0). Together with the stipulated equality, this forces h1​(0)=h2​(0)=0 in that case. Existence was assumed, so admissible data must satisfy this additional consequence. Smoothness at the corner and decay at infinity also exclude the homogeneous corner ambiguity in a quadrant Laplace problem; no extra singular or growing term is permitted in the boxed reconstruction.
