Let G0​(k),Gl​(k) denote the Half-range Fourier transforms of g0​,gl​, and let N0​(k),Nl​(k) denote those of the upward derivatives n0​,nl​. Also set
H(k)=∫0l​ekyh(y)dy,W(k)=∫0l​ekyv(y)dy.
(16)
Integrate the tangential derivatives in the three spectral functions by parts. For example, ∫0∞​e−ikxg0′​=−h(0)+ikG0​, and ∫0l​ekyh′=eklh(l)−h(0)−kH. All corner values cancel in ρL​+ρB​+ρT​=0. The result is the semistrip Laplace spectral global relation
eklNl​(k)−N0​(k)+k[G0​(k)−eklGl​(k)]−W(k)−ikH(k)=0.
(17)
For κ>0, use the sine transforms
Gjs​(κ)=∫0∞​sin(κx)gj​(x)dx,Sj​(κ)=∫0∞​sin(κx)nj​(x)dx.
(18)
Reality of q makes W(κ) and W(−κ) real, so taking imaginary parts removes the unknown left-side normal derivative. At k=κ and k=−κ the two consequences of the global relation are
eκlSl​−S0​e−κlSl​−S0​​=κ[eκlGls​−G0s​−H(κ)],=κ[G0s​−e−κlGls​−H(−κ)].​
(19)
Subtracting these equations and then substituting back gives the semistrip Dirichlet-to-Neumann sine transforms entirely in terms of the prescribed Dirichlet boundary data:
S0​(κ)=sinh(κl)κ​[−cosh(κl)G0s​(κ)+Gls​(κ)+∫0l​sinh(κ(l−y))h(y)dy],​
(20)
Sl​(κ)=sinh(κl)κ​[−G0s​(κ)+cosh(κl)Gls​(κ)−∫0l​sinh(κy)h(y)dy].​
(21)
These are the requested transforms of qy​ itself. The bottom outward Neumann boundary data have the opposite sign. The apparent division at κ=0 is handled by a continuous limit whenever the corresponding moments exist; there is no singularity for any κ>0.
