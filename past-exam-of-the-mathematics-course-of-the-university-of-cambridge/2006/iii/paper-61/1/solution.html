Past exam of the mathematics course of the University of Cambridge / 2006 / iii / Paper 61 / 1 / Solution

 ... Mathematics course of the University of Cambridge Past exam of the mathematics course of the University of Cambridge 2006 iii Paper 61 1
OurBigBook.com Words: 474
Use the paper's curvature convention throughout, and define the Ricci tensor and scalar curvature by Rkn​=Rikin​ and R=gknRkn​. In particular, if semicolons are read from left to right, Xi;km​=∇m​(∇k​X)i, including the connection on the derivative index. This ordering matters for the Ricci identity sign.
Choose Riemann normal coordinates at an arbitrary point P. The Christoffel symbols vanish there, and the Levi-Civita connection formula gives
Rikmn​(P)=21​(gim,kn​+gkn,im​−gkm,in​−gin,km​)(P).
(1)
Metric symmetry and commuting partial derivatives show directly that exchanging m,n or i,k changes the sign, while interchanging the pairs (i,k) and (m,n) leaves the expression unchanged. Adding its three cyclic versions over k,m,n cancels every second derivative. These are tensorial statements and P was arbitrary, so
Rik(mn)​=0,Ri[kmn]​=0,R(ik)mn​=0,Rikmn​=Rmnik​.​
(2)
The cyclic relation is the first Bianchi identity; its reduction to the total antisymmetrization uses last-pair antisymmetry. These arguments use zero torsion tensor and metric compatibility, not a field equation.
At P, the covariant derivative of curvature equals its partial derivative. Differentiating the connection expression for curvature gives derivatives of ∂n​Γikm​−∂m​Γikn​; derivatives of the quadratic connection terms vanish because Γ(P)=0. In the cyclic sum over m,n,p, every second derivative of a connection coefficient occurs twice with opposite signs. Thus
Rikmn;p​+Riknp;m​+Rikpm;n​=0,Rik[mn;p]​=0.​
(3)
This is the second Bianchi identity, valid everywhere by tensoriality.
Lower the first curvature index and contract the differential identity with gim. Metric compatibility allows the metric to pass through the covariant derivatives. Using the pair antisymmetries yields
∇iRikmn​=∇m​Rkn​−∇n​Rkm​.​
(4)
Contract again with gkm. The left side is −∇iRin​, while the right side is ∇kRkn​−∇n​R. Hence the contracted Bianchi identity is
∇iRin​=21​∇n​R,∇i(Rin​−21​gin​R)=0.​
(5)
The Ricci tensor is symmetric, as follows by contracting the pair-exchange symmetry. The final divergence is that of the Einstein tensor.
For the vector commutator, at the same normal-coordinate point,
Xi;km​=∂m​∂k​Xi+(∂m​Γijk​)Xj.
(6)
The terms involving undifferentiated connection coefficients vanish at P. Subtracting the reversed expression leaves (Γijk,m​−Γijm,k​)Xj, exactly the stated curvature convention at P. Therefore
Xi;km​−Xi;mk​=Rijkm​Xj.​
(7)
For a covector field, its connection term has the opposite sign: ωi;k​=∂k​ωi​−Γjik​ωj​. The same calculation gives
ωi;km​−ωi;mk​=−Rjikm​ωj​.​
(8)
Equivalently, apply the commutator to the scalar ωi​Xi, whose two covariant derivatives commute, and cancel the vector contribution. Each covariant index contributes a negative curvature action and each contravariant index a positive one in this semicolon ordering.
To prove the requested double divergence of the Riemann tensor, let
Dmn​=Rikmn;ik​=∇k​∇i​Rikmn​.
(9)
The once-contracted identity gives
Dmn​=∇k​∇m​Rkn​−∇k​∇n​Rkm​.
(10)
Commuting the outer derivative past ∇m​ and ∇n​ leaves a difference of scalar Hessians, 21​(∇m​∇n​−∇n​∇m​)R=0, plus curvature terms. The operator commutator [∇k​,∇m​] has the negative of the paper's curvature sign, since it reverses the semicolon order. On the mixed Ricci tensor it gives
[∇k​,∇m​]Rkn​=−Ram​Ran​+Rankm​Rka​.
(11)
The first term is symmetric in m,n: it is the metric contraction of two symmetric Ricci tensors. The second is Smn​=RakRankm​ and is also symmetric, since
Smn​=RakRkman​=RakRamkn​=Snm​,
(12)
where the first equality uses curvature pair exchange and the second swaps the dummy indices a,k. Thus the two commutator terms cancel on antisymmetrization in m,n, proving
Rikmn;ik​=0.​
(13)
No assumption such as vacuum, constant curvature or vanishing Ricci tensor was used.
