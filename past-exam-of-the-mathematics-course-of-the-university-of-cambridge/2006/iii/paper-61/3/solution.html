Raise perturbation indices with the background Minkowski metric. To first order, the inverse metric is gik=ηik−ϵhik+O(ϵ2) and the Levi-Civita connection is
Γikm​=2ϵ​ηij(hjk,m​+hjm,k​−hkm,j​)+O(ϵ2).
(20)
Quadratic connection products in the Riemann curvature tensor are of order ϵ2. Substitution into the paper's curvature convention gives
Rikmn​=2ϵ​(him,kn​+hnk,im​−hmk,in​−hin,km​)+O(ϵ2).​
(21)
The signs follow the convention on the cover sheet; reversing the curvature definition would reverse this expression.
The gauge freedom is the freedom to identify points of the perturbed spacetime with points of the flat background in slightly different coordinates. Under the passive infinitesimal change x′i=xi+ϵξi(x), the metric transformation law gives the linearized coordinate gauge transformation
hik′​=hik​−∂i​ξk​−∂k​ξi​.
(22)
Thus a nonzero perturbation can partly represent a coordinate change rather than physical curvature. Substituting this variation into the displayed curvature formula gives
δRikmn​=−2ϵ​(ξi,mkn​+ξm,ikn​+ξn,kim​+ξk,nim​−ξm,kin​−ξk,min​−ξi,nkm​−ξn,ikm​)=0.
(23)
Each third derivative has a partner differing only by the order of its commuting partial derivatives. This explicitly proves gauge invariance of the linearized Riemann tensor. The restriction to first order matters: a coordinate change acts on nonzero background curvature as well, but the background here is flat.
Define the trace-reversed metric perturbation and d'Alembert operator by
h=ηikhik​,hˉik​=hik​−21​ηik​h,□=ηik∂i​∂k​=∂t2​−∂x2​−∂y2​−∂z2​.
(24)
In four dimensions hˉ=−h and hik​=hˉik​−21​ηik​hˉ. Contract the curvature formula using Rik​=Rmimk​:
Rik​=2ϵ​(□hik​+∂i​∂k​h−∂i​∂ahak​−∂k​∂ahai​),R=ϵ(□h−∂a∂bhab​),
(25)
up to O(ϵ2). Consequently the Einstein tensor is
Gik​=2ϵ​(□hˉik​+ηik​∂a∂bhˉab​−∂i​∂ahˉak​−∂k​∂ahˉai​).
(26)
Choose Lorenz gauge in linearized gravity, ∂khˉik​=0. It can be imposed locally by solving □ξi​=∂khˉik​, since the gauge transformation changes this divergence by −□ξi​. The remaining coordinate freedom satisfies □ξi​=0. In this gauge,
Gik​=2ϵ​□hˉik​,□hˉik​=−16πGϵ−1Tik​.​
(27)
This is the requested Linearized Einstein equations in the stated sign convention. Their divergence requires ∂kTik​=0 to this order.
For the photon beam, introduce u=t−x and the constant null covector ℓi​=(1,−1,0,0), so ℓi=(1,1,0,0). The stress-energy tensor is
Tik​=ϵρ(u)ℓi​ℓk​.
(28)
It is trace-free and conserved: ℓi∂i​ρ(u)=0. A beam-adapted particular solution, with no independently added homogeneous gravitational field, is
hˉik​=hik​=H(u,y,z)ℓi​ℓk​.
(29)
The equality follows because its trace is zero, and the Lorenz condition holds because ℓk∂k​H=0. The field equation becomes a transverse Poisson equation:
□H=−Δ⊥​H=−16πGρ(u),Δ⊥​H=16πGρ(u).​
(30)
For the transversely uniform source in the question, one particular solution is H=4πGρ(u)(y2+z2). Thus the sourced components can be chosen as
h00​=h11​=−h01​=−h10​=H,all other components zero.​
(31)
Boundary conditions and homogeneous solutions determine additional freedom; the source does not uniquely specify every component in every gauge. In particular, taking H to depend only on t−x would give □H=0 and cannot solve the equation for nonzero ρ. The uniform idealization is not an asymptotically flat finite beam, and its quadratic particular solution is a weak-field description only where ∣ϵH∣≪1.
Finally consider another photon with tangent proportional to ℓi. Its tangent remains null because hik​ℓiℓk=0. Moreover hik​ℓk=0, and since ℓ is constant, the connection contraction is
Γijk​ℓjℓk=2ϵ​ηia(2ℓj∂j​(hak​ℓk)−∂a​(hjk​ℓjℓk))=0.
(32)
The geodesic equation is therefore satisfied by a straight affinely parametrized ray along the beam. Hence co-propagating photons experience no deflection from the beam's gravitational field. This is parallel photon beams in linearized gravity; it does not assert absence of forces on counter-propagating photons or effects from unrelated homogeneous gravitational perturbations.
