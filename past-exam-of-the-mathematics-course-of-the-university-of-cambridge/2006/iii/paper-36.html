In the SI model, one infection reduces the susceptible count by one, and the total event rate is λX(t)Y(t)/n=nb(Xn​(t)), where b(u)=λu(1−u). The Poisson time-change representation of a Markov chain therefore gives
Xn​(t)=a−n1​Nn​(n∫0t​b(Xn​(s))ds),
(1)
where Nn​ is a unit-rate Poisson process. Subtracting its clock from its count gives
εn​(t)=n1​[Nn​(n∫0t​b(Xn​(s))ds)−n∫0t​b(Xn​(s))ds]​.
(2)
Thus Xn​(t)=a−∫0t​b(Xn​(s))ds−εn​(t). The clock stops when the susceptible count reaches zero, so the representation also includes the absorbing state.

Fix T>0 and put K=λT/4. Since 0≤Xn​≤1, the clock in part (a) is at most nK on [0,T]. The Poisson maximal concentration bound gives, for every δ>0,
P(supt≤T​∣εn​(t)∣>δ)≤2exp[−nKh(δ/K)],h(u)=(1+u)log(1+u)−u.
(3)
Here h(u)>0 for u>0, so the probabilities are summable in n. The Borel-Cantelli lemma, first for positive rational δ and then for all δ, yields supt≤T​∣εn​(t)∣→0 almost surely. No independence between the processes for different population sizes is required.
The deterministic SI model solves the logistic equation x′=−λx(1−x) with x(0)=a, giving
x(t)=1−a+ae−λtae−λt​​.
(4)
On [0,1], b is Lipschitz continuous with constant λ. Subtracting the two integral equations gives
∣Xn​(t)−x(t)∣≤sups≤T​∣εn​(s)∣+λ∫0t​∣Xn​(s)−x(s)∣ds.
(5)
The Gronwall inequality implies
supt≤T​∣Xn​(t)−x(t)∣≤eλTsupt≤T​∣εn​(t)∣⟶0almost surely.
(6)
This is the uniform almost-sure fluid limit of the SI model. One can take the intersection of the probability-one events for integer T to obtain the conclusion on every finite interval. Use population sizes with an integral; if the initial susceptible count is rounded, the extra initial error is at most 1/n and the same Gronwall inequality proof applies.

Write q=1−p. A disconnected graph has a connected component of a graph of size 1≤s≤⌊n/2⌋. For a fixed s-element vertex set S, all s(n−s) edges between S and its complement must be absent. Their independent absence has probability qs(n−s). The union bound gives
P(G(n,p) disconnected)≤∑s=1⌊n/2⌋​(sn​)qs(n−s)≤s=1∑⌊n/2⌋​(nqn/2)s​.
(7)
Set rn​=nqn/2. Since p is fixed in (0,1), rn​→0. For large n, the final sum is at most the geometric series rn​/(1−rn​), which tends to zero. Hence the binomial random graph is connected with probability tending to one, as in connectivity of a fixed-density binomial random graph.

Let Iij​ be the indicator random variable that the unordered vertex pair {i,j} has no common neighbour. For each of the other n−2 vertices, the two required edges are both present with probability p2. The edge pairs for different candidate neighbours are disjoint sets of independent random choices. EX=(2n​)(1−p2)n−2​,

R=(I−βA)−1=∑k=0∞​(βA)k,∥R∥2​≤1−βρ1​.
