Use the paper's curvature convention throughout, and define the Ricci tensor and scalar curvature by Rkn​=Rikin​ and R=gknRkn​. In particular, if semicolons are read from left to right, Xi;km​=∇m​(∇k​X)i, including the connection on the derivative index. This ordering matters for the Ricci identity sign.
Choose Riemann normal coordinates at an arbitrary point P. The Christoffel symbols vanish there, and the Levi-Civita connection formula gives
Rikmn​(P)=21​(gim,kn​+gkn,im​−gkm,in​−gin,km​)(P).
(1)
Metric symmetry and commuting partial derivatives show directly that exchanging m,n or i,k changes the sign, while interchanging the pairs (i,k) and (m,n) leaves the expression unchanged. Adding its three cyclic versions over k,m,n cancels every second derivative. These are tensorial statements and P was arbitrary, so
Rik(mn)​=0,Ri[kmn]​=0,R(ik)mn​=0,Rikmn​=Rmnik​.​
(2)
The cyclic relation is the first Bianchi identity; its reduction to the total antisymmetrization uses last-pair antisymmetry. These arguments use zero torsion tensor and metric compatibility, not a field equation.
At P, the covariant derivative of curvature equals its partial derivative. Differentiating the connection expression for curvature gives derivatives of ∂n​Γikm​−∂m​Γikn​; derivatives of the quadratic connection terms vanish because Γ(P)=0. In the cyclic sum over m,n,p, every second derivative of a connection coefficient occurs twice with opposite signs. Thus
Rikmn;p​+Riknp;m​+Rikpm;n​=0,Rik[mn;p]​=0.​
(3)
This is the second Bianchi identity, valid everywhere by tensoriality.
Lower the first curvature index and contract the differential identity with gim. Metric compatibility allows the metric to pass through the covariant derivatives. Using the pair antisymmetries yields
∇iRikmn​=∇m​Rkn​−∇n​Rkm​.​
(4)
Contract again with gkm. The left side is −∇iRin​, while the right side is ∇kRkn​−∇n​R. Hence the contracted Bianchi identity is
∇iRin​=21​∇n​R,∇i(Rin​−21​gin​R)=0.​
(5)
The Ricci tensor is symmetric, as follows by contracting the pair-exchange symmetry. The final divergence is that of the Einstein tensor.
For the vector commutator, at the same normal-coordinate point,
Xi;km​=∂m​∂k​Xi+(∂m​Γijk​)Xj.
(6)
The terms involving undifferentiated connection coefficients vanish at P. Subtracting the reversed expression leaves (Γijk,m​−Γijm,k​)Xj, exactly the stated curvature convention at P. Therefore
Xi;km​−Xi;mk​=Rijkm​Xj.​
(7)
For a covector field, its connection term has the opposite sign: ωi;k​=∂k​ωi​−Γjik​ωj​. The same calculation gives
ωi;km​−ωi;mk​=−Rjikm​ωj​.​
(8)
Equivalently, apply the commutator to the scalar ωi​Xi, whose two covariant derivatives commute, and cancel the vector contribution. Each covariant index contributes a negative curvature action and each contravariant index a positive one in this semicolon ordering.
To prove the requested double divergence of the Riemann tensor, let
Dmn​=Rikmn;ik​=∇k​∇i​Rikmn​.
(9)
The once-contracted identity gives
Dmn​=∇k​∇m​Rkn​−∇k​∇n​Rkm​.
(10)
Commuting the outer derivative past ∇m​ and ∇n​ leaves a difference of scalar Hessians, 21​(∇m​∇n​−∇n​∇m​)R=0, plus curvature terms. The operator commutator [∇k​,∇m​] has the negative of the paper's curvature sign, since it reverses the semicolon order. On the mixed Ricci tensor it gives
[∇k​,∇m​]Rkn​=−Ram​Ran​+Rankm​Rka​.
(11)
The first term is symmetric in m,n: it is the metric contraction of two symmetric Ricci tensors. The second is Smn​=RakRankm​ and is also symmetric, since
Smn​=RakRkman​=RakRamkn​=Snm​,
(12)
where the first equality uses curvature pair exchange and the second swaps the dummy indices a,k. Thus the two commutator terms cancel on antisymmetrization in m,n, proving
Rikmn;ik​=0.​
(13)
No assumption such as vacuum, constant curvature or vanishing Ricci tensor was used.

a OurBigBook.com
Use units c=1 and signature (+−−−). For a massive test particle, write its four-momentum as pi=mui, where ui=dxi/dτ, τ is Minkowski proper time, and uiui​=1. The stated flat-background scalar-force theory of gravity then gives
dτdpi​=m(∂iΦ−uiuj∂j​Φ).
(14)
Contract with pi​=mui​. The two terms cancel:
dτd(m2)​=2pi​dτdpi​=2m2(uj∂j​Φ−uiui​uj∂j​Φ)=0.
(15)
Thus rest mass is conserved and the acceleration law is
dτdui​=∂iΦ−ui(u⋅∂Φ).​
(16)
There is no mass or composition parameter in this equation. Particles released at the same event with the same velocity consequently have identical trajectories. In a static weak field at small speed, its spatial part becomes d2x/dt2=−∇Φ, since raising a spatial index introduces a minus sign. The field equation likewise reduces to ∇2Φ=4πGρ for nonrelativistic matter, because Tii​≃ρ and □=−∇2 in the static limit.
Therefore yes: the model agrees with the Eötvös test of universal free fall. In the point-particle/test-body approximation, inertial and passive gravitational masses have the same universal ratio. This is the weak equivalence principle tested by the Eötvös experiment; it is not a claim about strongly self-gravitating bodies or a proof of all aspects of the Einstein equivalence principle.

b OurBigBook.com
For a photon, pipi​=0 and the null displacement along the ray is parallel to pi. Consequently pj​dxj=0, and the first term of the momentum law vanishes. Write dxi=pidλ with an appropriate path parameter. Then
dpi=−(pj∂j​Φ)pidλ=−pidΦ,d(eΦpi)=0.​
(17)
For a static potential, a photon emitted and received by laboratories at rest in the background frame has energy proportional to p0. Using E=hP​ν, the scalar-force gravitational redshift is
νe​νr​​=eΦe​−Φr​=1−(Φr​−Φe​)+O((ΔΦ)2).​
(18)
Restoring c, the leading fractional shift is Δν/ν=−ΔΦN​/c2, where ΦN​ is the dimensional Newtonian gravitational potential. Near Earth's surface, an upward displacement H has ΔΦN​=gH>0, hence Δν/ν=−gH/c2: the receiver sees a redshift.
Thus yes: the predicted weak-field frequency shift agrees with the Pound-Rebka experiment. The calculation concerns stationary emitter and receiver, with identical local transition-energy standards; it does not add a Doppler shift due to their relative motion. Agreement with this gravitational redshift measurement alone does not establish the tensor theory of general relativity.

c OurBigBook.com
The null momentum equation gives pi=Cie−Φ, with a constant null vector Ci. Since the tangent is parallel to pi, every spatial direction ratio is constant:
dx0dxa​=p0pa​=C0Ca​,a=1,2,3.
(19)
The changing potential changes the scale of the four-momentum, not its direction. Photon paths are therefore straight lines in the background Minkowski spacetime, up to reparametrization. This is also consistent with conformal preservation of null geodesic paths: the particle motion law can be expressed through a conformally flat metric, which cannot change the unparametrized null trajectories of the background.
Hence no: the theory predicts zero solar light deflection, in disagreement with the observed nonzero bending of light. For comparison, the leading Schwarzschild light deflection is 4GM/(bc2) at impact parameter b. The scalar theory's nonzero gravitational redshift does not rescue this failed directional prediction.

Raise perturbation indices with the background Minkowski metric. To first order, the inverse metric is gik=ηik−ϵhik+O(ϵ2) and the Levi-Civita connection is
Γikm​=2ϵ​ηij(hjk,m​+hjm,k​−hkm,j​)+O(ϵ2).
(20)
Quadratic connection products in the Riemann curvature tensor are of order ϵ2. Substitution into the paper's curvature convention gives
Rikmn​=2ϵ​(him,kn​+hnk,im​−hmk,in​−hin,km​)+O(ϵ2).​
(21)
The signs follow the convention on the cover sheet; reversing the curvature definition would reverse this expression.
The gauge freedom is the freedom to identify points of the perturbed spacetime with points of the flat background in slightly different coordinates. Under the passive infinitesimal change x′i=xi+ϵξi(x), the metric transformation law gives the linearized coordinate gauge transformation
hik′​=hik​−∂i​ξk​−∂k​ξi​.
(22)
Thus a nonzero perturbation can partly represent a coordinate change rather than physical curvature. Substituting this variation into the displayed curvature formula gives
δRikmn​=−2ϵ​(ξi,mkn​+ξm,ikn​+ξn,kim​+ξk,nim​−ξm,kin​−ξk,min​−ξi,nkm​−ξn,ikm​)=0.
(23)
Each third derivative has a partner differing only by the order of its commuting partial derivatives. This explicitly proves gauge invariance of the linearized Riemann tensor. The restriction to first order matters: a coordinate change acts on nonzero background curvature as well, but the background here is flat.
Define the trace-reversed metric perturbation and d'Alembert operator by
h=ηikhik​,hˉik​=hik​−21​ηik​h,□=ηik∂i​∂k​=∂t2​−∂x2​−∂y2​−∂z2​.
(24)
In four dimensions hˉ=−h and hik​=hˉik​−21​ηik​hˉ. Contract the curvature formula using Rik​=Rmimk​:
Rik​=2ϵ​(□hik​+∂i​∂k​h−∂i​∂ahak​−∂k​∂ahai​),R=ϵ(□h−∂a∂bhab​),
(25)
up to O(ϵ2). Consequently the Einstein tensor is
Gik​=2ϵ​(□hˉik​+ηik​∂a∂bhˉab​−∂i​∂ahˉak​−∂k​∂ahˉai​).
(26)
Choose Lorenz gauge in linearized gravity, ∂khˉik​=0. It can be imposed locally by solving □ξi​=∂khˉik​, since the gauge transformation changes this divergence by −□ξi​. The remaining coordinate freedom satisfies □ξi​=0. In this gauge,
Gik​=2ϵ​□hˉik​,□hˉik​=−16πGϵ−1Tik​.​
(27)
This is the requested Linearized Einstein equations in the stated sign convention. Their divergence requires ∂kTik​=0 to this order.
For the photon beam, introduce u=t−x and the constant null covector ℓi​=(1,−1,0,0), so ℓi=(1,1,0,0). The stress-energy tensor is
Tik​=ϵρ(u)ℓi​ℓk​.
(28)
It is trace-free and conserved: ℓi∂i​ρ(u)=0. A beam-adapted particular solution, with no independently added homogeneous gravitational field, is
hˉik​=hik​=H(u,y,z)ℓi​ℓk​.
(29)
The equality follows because its trace is zero, and the Lorenz condition holds because ℓk∂k​H=0. The field equation becomes a transverse Poisson equation:
□H=−Δ⊥​H=−16πGρ(u),Δ⊥​H=16πGρ(u).​
(30)
For the transversely uniform source in the question, one particular solution is H=4πGρ(u)(y2+z2). Thus the sourced components can be chosen as
h00​=h11​=−h01​=−h10​=H,all other components zero.​
(31)
Boundary conditions and homogeneous solutions determine additional freedom; the source does not uniquely specify every component in every gauge. In particular, taking H to depend only on t−x would give □H=0 and cannot solve the equation for nonzero ρ. The uniform idealization is not an asymptotically flat finite beam, and its quadratic particular solution is a weak-field description only where ∣ϵH∣≪1.
Finally consider another photon with tangent proportional to ℓi. Its tangent remains null because hik​ℓiℓk=0. Moreover hik​ℓk=0, and since ℓ is constant, the connection contraction is
Γijk​ℓjℓk=2ϵ​ηia(2ℓj∂j​(hak​ℓk)−∂a​(hjk​ℓjℓk))=0.
(32)
The geodesic equation is therefore satisfied by a straight affinely parametrized ray along the beam. Hence co-propagating photons experience no deflection from the beam's gravitational field. This is parallel photon beams in linearized gravity; it does not assert absence of forces on counter-propagating photons or effects from unrelated homogeneous gravitational perturbations.

Take both curvature radii to be one. Coordinate ranges depend on the global identification, which a line element alone cannot fix. For the ordinary two-dimensional de Sitter spacetime,
t∈R,χ∈R/(2πZ).​
(33)
This is the unit hyperboloid X02​−X12​−X22​=−1 in ambient signature (+−−), parametrized by X0​=sinht, X1​=coshtcosχ, X2​=coshtsinχ. Unwrapping χ instead gives its universal cover.
For two-dimensional anti-de Sitter spacetime, the embedding X02​+X12​−X22​=1 in ambient signature (++−) is parametrized by X0​=coshrcost, X1​=coshrsint, X2​=sinhr. On this hyperboloid r∈R and t is periodic modulo 2π, creating closed timelike circles. The physically usual universal cover removes that periodicity:
r∈R,t∈Ron the covering AdS spacetime.​
(34)
Both signs of r are needed for the full two-dimensional spatial line. Taking r≥0 without an additional construction would omit one of its two ends. The following causal comparison uses the AdS universal cover.
Both models are maximally symmetric Lorentzian manifolds, with constant sectional curvature and no curvature singularities. In the paper's convention their Ricci scalars are respectively +2 and −2. The de Sitter spatial scale factor a(t)=cosht contracts to a nonzero minimum and then expands; t=0 is not a big-bang singularity. The AdS metric is static, and ∂t​ is timelike everywhere because gtt​=cosh2r>0.
The embeddings give a concise classification of all affinely parametrized geodesics. Geodesic acceleration in the ambient space is normal to the hyperboloid, hence proportional to X. Differentiating the constraint and setting X′2=κ, with κ=1,0,−1 for timelike, null and spacelike curves, gives
X′′=κXin de Sitter,X′′=−κXin anti-de Sitter.
(35)
Every solution stays in the two-plane spanned by its initial point P and tangent V, so every geodesic is a plane section through the ambient origin. For de Sitter, these are Pcoshs+Vsinhs for timelike curves, P+sV for null curves, and Pcoss+Vsins for spacelike curves. Thus timelike geodesics and null geodesics are complete and nonclosed, while spacelike geodesics are closed circles on the ordinary hyperboloid. These are the geodesics of two-dimensional de Sitter spacetime.
For AdS the roles of the circular and hyperbolic solutions are reversed: timelike curves are Pcoss+Vsins, null curves are P+sV, and spacelike curves are Pcoshs+Vsinhs. Timelike curves close after proper time 2π on the hyperboloid, but their lifts on the universal cover continue to ever later time rather than forming closed curves. All three kinds extend for arbitrary affine parameter. This is geodesics of two-dimensional anti-de Sitter spacetime, not an assertion that geodesics reach the conformal boundary in finite physical affine length.
The coordinate first integrals make the behavior concrete. In de Sitter, spatial rotational symmetry gives a conserved Pχ​=cosh2tdχ/ds, and normalization gives
(dsdt​)2=κ+cosh2tPχ2​​,dsdχ​=cosh2tPχ​​.
(36)
Constant-χ observers are timelike geodesics. Nontrivial null geodesics have affine parameter proportional to sinht, which diverges at both t→±∞. In AdS the conserved static energy is E=cosh2rdt/ds, with
(dsdr​)2=cosh2rE2​−κ.
(37)
For timelike curves, E≥1 and
sinhr=E2−1​sin(s−s0​).
(38)
They oscillate through the center and never reach spatial infinity; r=0 is the E=1 geodesic. For null curves, λ=±sinhr/E+constant, so both ends are at infinite affine parameter. Spacelike AdS geodesics likewise extend to the two spatial ends.
For the conformal structure, introduce de Sitter conformal time
η=arctan(sinht),−2π​<η<2π​,ds2=sec2η(dη2−dχ2).​
(39)
Multiplying by cos2η gives the conformal cylinder of two-dimensional de Sitter spacetime. The past and future conformal infinities are spacelike circles at η=∓π/2. Null curves are χ=±η+constant modulo 2π. Although the conformal-time interval is finite, their physical affine parameters and timelike observers' proper times are infinite at its ends. Constant-η circles are Cauchy hypersurfaces; global de Sitter is globally hyperbolic.
For AdS put
ψ=arctan(sinhr),−2π​<ψ<2π​,ds2=sec2ψ(dt2−dψ2).​
(40)
The conformal strip of two-dimensional anti-de Sitter spacetime has unbounded time and timelike conformal boundaries at ψ=±π/2. Null curves have t±ψ=constant. A signal from any finite r reaches the center in coordinate time ∣ψ∣<π/2, and a null curve crosses the entire conformal strip in time π. This finite coordinate travel time coexists with infinite physical affine length to the boundary. The covering spacetime has no closed timelike curves, but is not globally hyperbolic: causal curves can arrive from timelike infinity without meeting a proposed initial slice. Field evolution consequently requires boundary conditions as well as initial data. Unwrapping time removes closed timelike curves, not the timelike boundary.
The contrast in observer event horizons follows directly from these null curves. For the complete de Sitter observer χ=0, let d(χ,0)∈[0,π] be shortest angular distance. An event can send a signal to this observer before its future endpoint precisely when
d(χ,0)<2π​−η.
(41)
Equality is its future observer event horizon. It can receive a signal emitted by the observer after its past endpoint precisely when d(χ,0)<η+π/2, whose equality is the past horizon. In the observer's fundamental domain these horizons are null lines
χ=±(π/2−η),χ=±(η+π/2).
(42)
Their intersection encloses the observer's static patch of de Sitter spacetime, the diamond d(χ,0)<π/2−∣η∣. They are observer-dependent cosmological horizons, not curvature singularities or Cauchy horizons. Every inertial observer has the corresponding horizons by de Sitter symmetry.
For the complete AdS static observer at r=0, every event at finite r can send a signal to the observer, and can receive one, because time is unbounded and the coordinate distance ∣ψ∣ is finite. Thus there is no corresponding global static-observer event horizon. The lapse never vanishes in these coordinates. Restricted accelerated-observer patches can have observer horizons, but those are distinct from the global static model. On the original periodic-time AdS hyperboloid, the more serious issue is closed timelike curves.
The figure identifies the de Sitter spatial edges and displays only a finite time window of the AdS covering strip, whose time continues indefinitely. Solid red curves are null geodesics; the oscillating AdS curve is a timelike geodesic. The shaded de Sitter diamond is the static observer's two-way communication region, not the entire spacetime.
The concise comparison is de Sitter has spacelike conformal infinities and cosmological observer horizons; covering AdS has timelike conformal boundaries and no horizon for an eternal global static observer. Both are constant-curvature and geodesically complete, but their geodesic recurrence, global topology, causal boundaries and initial-value properties differ.
