For n≥1, the Bernstein polynomial is
Bn​(f;x)=∑j=0n​f(j/n)(jn​)xj(1−x)n−j.
(1)
The weights sum to one by the binomial theorem. Equivalently, if K has the binomial distribution with parameters n,x, then Bn​(f;x)=Ef(K/n). This interpretation makes the cancellation of apparent high-degree terms transparent.
For the falling factorial ur​=u(u−1)⋯(u−r+1), with u0​=1, direct cancellation in the binomial weights gives
EKr​​=j=r∑n​jr​(jn​)xj(1−x)n−j=nr​xrj=r∑n​(j−rn−r​)xj−r(1−x)n−j=nr​xr.​
(2)
The polynomials u0​,…,um​ form a monic triangular basis of the degree-at-most-m polynomials. Hence um=um​+∑r<m​cr​ur​, and
Bn​(xm;x)=nmnm​​xm+∑r<m​cr​nmnr​​xr.
(3)
For m≤n, its leading coefficient is nm​/nm=0. If p has actual degree m and leading coefficient am​=0, linearity shows that the leading coefficient of Bn​p is am​nm​/nm; lower-degree terms cannot cancel it. Thus the Bernstein polynomial degree preservation property gives
degBn​p=mwhenever degp=m≤n.​
(4)
This includes the requested m<n, and also explains why the property cannot simply be extended to m>n.
The same moments give EK=nx and EK2=n(n−1)x2+nx. Therefore
Bn​1=1,Bn​x=x,Bn​(x2)=x2+nx(1−x)​.​
(5)
The first two approximation errors vanish identically, while
∥Bn​(x2)−x2∥∞​=4n1​⟶0.​
(6)
All three convergences are consequently uniform convergence on [0,1].

ω(f,δ)=sup∣h∣≤δ​supx​∣f(x+h)−f(x)∣.

For real-valued f∈C[−1,1], there is a unique best uniform approximation p∗​∈Pn​, where Pn​ means degree at most n. If E=∥f−p∗​∥∞​>0, the Chebyshev alternation theorem characterizes it by the existence of n+2 ordered points
−1≤x0​<x1​<⋯<xn+1​≤1
(24)
and a sign ε∈{−1,1} such that
f(xj​)−p∗​(xj​)=ε(−1)jE,j=0,…,n+1.​
(25)
Conversely, a polynomial in Pn​ whose error has this alternation is the best one. If the best error is zero, f itself is the exact polynomial approximant. The real-valued qualification matters: this sign-alternation formulation is not the corresponding criterion for arbitrary complex-valued approximation.
The obstruction to a strict improvement is concrete. If q had smaller error at every alternation point, q−p∗​ would have alternating strict signs there. The intermediate value theorem would give at least n+1 distinct zeros, impossible for a nonzero polynomial of degree at most n.

Since ∣Tj​(x)∣≤1 on [−1,1] and the coefficients are summable, the Weierstrass M-test gives uniform convergence of this positive lacunary Chebyshev series and makes its sum continuous.
Let r be the largest integer with 3r≤n, and set r=−1 when n=0. The candidate and its tail size are
pn​(x)=k=0∑r​ak​T3k​(x),En​(f)=k=r+1∑∞​ak​.​
(26)
The empty sum for n=0 is zero. The candidate has degree at most n. The triangle inequality bounds its error by the proposed tail sum, and equality holds at x=1, since every Chebyshev polynomial has Tj​(1)=1 and all tail coefficients are positive. It remains to prove that another polynomial cannot improve this error.
Put L=3r+1, the first omitted degree, so L>n. At the points yj​=cos(jπ/L) for j=0,…,L, every omitted term has
T3k​(yj​)=cos(3k−r−1jπ)=(−1)j,k≥r+1,
(27)
because 3k−r−1 is odd. Uniform convergence therefore gives
f(yj​)−pn​(yj​)=(−1)j∑k=r+1∞​ak​.
(28)
These L+1 distinct points run in decreasing order; reversing their order still gives alternating signs. Since L+1≥n+2, select n+2 consecutive ones and apply the Chebyshev alternation theorem. It proves both optimality and uniqueness of the displayed candidate. Thus the best polynomial and the error stay unchanged between successive powers of three; this exact truncation property depends on the synchronized tail signs, not on a general rule that truncating a Chebyshev expansion is optimal.

Px​f=∑j=1n​cj​Nj​,Ax​c=Rf,Rf=(f(x1​),…,f(xn​))T.

The stipulated uniquely defined spline interpolation operator presupposes invertibility of the B-spline collocation matrix. For the standard increasing distinct sites, the conditions Ni​(xi​)>0 give it by the Schoenberg–Whitney theorem. Thus c=Ax−1​Rf.
Use the standard partition-normalized B-splines of the question: they are nonnegative and satisfy the subpartition of unity for B-splines, ∑j​Nj​(t)≤1. This also holds outside the basic knot interval; one may extend the knots and regard the finite family as a subfamily of a full partition of unity. The recurrence proof of the full partition is given in Question 6(c). ​∑j​cj​Nj​(t)​≤∑j​∣cj​∣Nj​(t)≤∥c∥ℓ∞​.

We first derive the recurrence needed for the partition of unity. Denote an order-r partition-normalized spline by Ni,r​. For r≥2, set ht​(u)=(u−t)+r−2​, so (u−t)ht​(u)=(u−t)+r−1​. The elementary product identities for divided differences are
[u0​,…,us​](uh(u))​=u0​[u0​,…,us​]h+[u1​,…,us​]h=us​[u0​,…,us​]h+[u0​,…,us−1​]h.​
(48)
They follow from the Leibniz rule for divided differences because the divided differences of the linear factor vanish above first order. Use the first identity on ti​,…,ti+r−1​ and the second on ti+1​,…,ti+r​. Subtracting cancels the common middle-block term. The defining divided-difference recurrence for Ni,r​ then gives
Ni,r​(t)=ti+r−1​−ti​t−ti​​Ni,r−1​(t)+ti+r​−ti+1​ti+r​−t​Ni+1,r−1​(t).​
(49)
This is the Cox-de Boor recurrence, derived here from the specified normalization rather than assumed as a partition theorem.
Extend the distinct knot sequence in both directions to a strictly increasing sequence (ti​)i∈Z​ tending to ±∞. For order one, Ni,1​=1[ti​,ti+1​)​, so the full sum equals one. All higher-order splines are supported on [ti​,ti+r​], making the following sums locally finite. Reindexing the second term of the recurrence yields
i∈Z∑​Ni,r​(t)​=j∈Z∑​[tj+r−1​−tj​t−tj​​+tj+r−1​−tj​tj+r−1​−t​]Nj,r−1​(t)=j∈Z∑​Nj,r−1​(t).​
(50)
Induction proves that the full sum is one for every order. The same recurrence gives nonnegativity: each coefficient is nonnegative wherever its associated lower-order spline is nonzero. Removing splines therefore gives the subpartition of unity for B-splines on the whole line.
For tk​<t<tn+1​, a spline with index i≤0 ends no later than tk​, and one with i≥n+1 starts no earlier than tn+1​. They all vanish, leaving exactly the indices 1,…,n. For k≥2 the splines are continuous and vanish at their support endpoints, so the equality extends to the closed basic knot interval:
i=1∑n​Ni​(t)=1,tk​≤t≤tn+1​.​
(51)
If order one is allowed, the standard half-open indicator convention gives equality on [t1​,tn+1​); to include its final endpoint literally, assign the left-limit value there. This harmless endpoint convention does not change integrals or L∞ norms. For the continuous splines of order at least two no such convention is necessary.
