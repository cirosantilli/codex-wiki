In the truncated tensor algebra T(N)(Rd), multiplication is
(a⊗b)(k)=∑i+j=k​a(i)⊗b(j).
(11)
Let gN(Rd) be the Lie algebra generated by the degree-one space, with commutator [u,v]=u⊗v−v⊗u and every term of degree greater than N set to zero. The free step-N nilpotent Lie group is
GN(Rd)=expN​(gN(Rd)),expN​(ℓ)=∑k=0N​k!ℓ⊗k​.
(12)
Its operation is the truncated tensor product, its identity is e=(1,0,…,0), and expN​(ℓ)−1=expN​(−ℓ). Truncated multiplication is associative; the finite Lie exponential and logarithm identify this group with the free nilpotent Lie algebra as a manifold.
The Chow theorem says that on a connected manifold a smooth bracket-generating family of vector fields joins every pair of points by a piecewise smooth horizontal path. Applied to the degree-one left-invariant fields on this group, it says that every g∈GN(Rd) is SN​(x)0,1​ for a piecewise smooth, and hence suitably parametrized Lipschitz continuous, control path x starting at zero.
For N=d=2, every Lie element is v+a[e1​,e2​], with v∈R2 and a∈R. The step-2 planar group coordinates give
exp(v+a[e1​,e2​])=1+v+21​v⊗v+a(e1​⊗e2​−e2​⊗e1​).
(13)
We realize this element explicitly. First traverse the rectangle
(0,0)→(r,0)→(r,σr)→(0,σr)→(0,0),r=∣a∣​,σ=sgn(a).
(14)
If a=0, omit the loop. Its displacement is zero, and its second-level entries are
∫x1dx2=σr2=a,∫x2dx1=−a,∫xidxi=0.
(15)
Thus the loop signature is exp(a[e1​,e2​]). Follow it by the straight segment from zero to v, whose signature is exp(v)=1+v+21​v⊗v. By the Chen identity, concatenation has signature
exp(a[e1​,e2​])⊗exp(v)=exp(v+a[e1​,e2​]),
(16)
because the commutator lies in the central second layer. Every group element has therefore been realized, proving this case of Chow's theorem.
The Carnot-Carathéodory distance is the infimum of lengths of horizontal controls realizing the relative group element. The construction gives the upper bound ∥exp(v+a[e1​,e2​])∥CC​≤∣v∣+4∣a∣​. Conversely, a control of length L has displacement at most L, and its antisymmetric area satisfies
∣a∣=​21​∫(x1dx2−x2dx1)​≤21​L2.
(17)
Here the control starts at zero, so its maximum Euclidean distance from zero is at most L. These estimates show that the homogeneous group norm is comparable to ∣v∣+∣a∣1/2.
The given path is the pure-area rough path yt​=exp(t[e1​,e2​]). Centrality gives
ys−1​⊗yt​=exp((t−s)[e1​,e2​])=(1,0,(0−(t−s)​t−s0​)).​
(18)
The graded dilations δr​(v,a)=(rv,r2a) scale horizontal lengths by r. Inversion preserves the norm. Hence, with c=dCC​(e,exp([e1​,e2​]))>0,
dCC​(ys​,yt​)=c∣t−s∣1/2.​
(19)
Thus the path is exactly 1/2-Hölder continuous: on a compact time interval it has every exponent at most 1/2, and none greater.
Its p-variation is finite precisely when p≥2. Indeed, the pth power of the variation sum is cp∑j​(tj+1​−tj​)p/2. For p≥2 it is bounded by cpTp/2; for p<2, the partition into m equal intervals gives cpTp/2m1−p/2→∞. In the usual step-two rough-path range it is weak geometric for 2≤p<3, including p=2. For p≥3, the same formula exp(t[e1​,e2​]) in G⌊p⌋ gives the additional levels and a weak geometric p-rough path there. The original G2 data alone are a truncated object when those additional levels are required.
