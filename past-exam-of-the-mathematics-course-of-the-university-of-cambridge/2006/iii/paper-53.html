Use target-space signature (−,+,…,+) and let a dot between target vectors denote contraction with the Minkowski metric. Varying the string embedding map in the Polyakov action, with periodic variations around the closed string, gives
δX​I=2πα′1​∫d2σδXμ​∂a​(−γ​γab∂b​Xμ).
(1)
Thus the embedding equation of motion is
∂a​(−γ​γab∂b​Xμ)=0.​
(2)
The independent worldsheet metric variation uses δ−γ​=−21​−γ​γab​δγab. It sets the worldsheet stress tensor to zero:
∂a​X⋅∂b​X−21​γab​γcd∂c​X⋅∂d​X=0.​
(3)
In conformal gauge, γab​=e2ωdiag(−1,1), the Weyl factor cancels. Define worldsheet derivatives ∂±​=(∂τ​±∂σ​)/2. The equations and Virasoro constraints reduce to
∂+​∂−​Xμ=0,(∂+​X)2=(∂−​X)2=0.​
(4)
Equivalently, X¨−X′′=0, X˙⋅X′=0 and X˙2+X′2=0. The free wave equation permits independent periodic left- and right-moving fields.
Take 0≤σ<2π and the uncompactified closed-string mode expansion
Xμ=xμ+α′pμτ+i2α′​​∑n=0​n1​(αnμ​e−in(τ−σ)+αnμ​e−in(τ+σ)),α0μ​=α0μ​=2α′​​pμ.
(5)
The Virasoro generators are the Fourier coefficients of the two quadratic worldsheet stress tensors. Their quantum expressions are
Ln​=21​∑m​:αn−m​⋅αm​:,Ln​=21​∑m​:αn−m​⋅αm​:.
(6)
The canonical commutation relations are [αmμ​,αnν​]=mημνδm+n,0​, with an identical tilded algebra and commuting chiral sectors. Normal ordering places annihilation modes to the right. It gives L0​=α′p2/4+N, where N=∑n>0​α−n​⋅αn​, and similarly on the other side. In old covariant string quantization, physical string states obey
Ln>0​∣Ψ⟩=Ln>0​∣Ψ⟩=0,(L0​−a)∣Ψ⟩=(L0​−a)∣Ψ⟩=0,
(7)
modulo physical null string states. Negative generators are adjoint constraints on bras, not further annihilation conditions on the same ket. In critical bosonic string theory, d=26 and the string intercept is a=1. These closed-string physical-state Virasoro conditions imply
N=N,M2=−p2=α′4​(N−1).
(8)
Thus the massless first closed-string level has N=N=1, and its general state is
∣ζ,p⟩=ζμν​α−1μ​α−1ν​∣0,p⟩.
(9)
To derive the polarization tensor constraints, the oscillator algebra gives [Ln​,αmμ​]=−mαn+mμ​. Applying L1​ to this state replaces α−1μ​ by α0μ​; the independent tilded condition replaces the other oscillator by α0ν​. All higher positive generators annihilate the state automatically. Therefore
p2=0,pμζμν​=0,pνζμν​=0.​
(10)
There is no condition that the tensor trace vanish: each chiral side has only one oscillator, so neither L2​ nor L2​ imposes a trace condition. The null-state quotient of a string also identifies
ζμν​∼ζμν​+pμ​ξν​+pν​ξ​μ​,p⋅ξ=p⋅ξ​=0.
(11)
For example, L−1​(ξν​α−1ν​∣0,p⟩) is a momentum-longitudinal state with zero inner product against every transverse physical polarization. This explains the string-state gauge redundancy, rather than merely counting covariant components. For an ordinary nonzero null momentum, choose a frame adapted to p. The constraints remove one null direction in each index, and these null-state identifications remove the remaining longitudinal directions. The representatives are then ζij​, i,j=1,…,24.
Decompose that transverse matrix into its symmetric trace-free part, antisymmetric part and trace. They describe the graviton, Kalb–Ramond field and dilaton, respectively. Their particle polarization counts are
(224⋅25​−1)+224⋅23​+1=299+276+1=576.​
(12)
These are representations of the rotational part SO(24) of the massless little group. The dilaton corresponds to the transverse trace; a covariant scalar polarization can be written proportional to ημν​−pμ​ℓν​−ℓμ​pν​, with ℓ2=0 and p⋅ℓ=1. It is transverse in both indices and has trace 24. The nonzero-momentum assumption is appropriate for propagating particle states; zero momentum requires separate treatment of gauge and background zero modes.
In a general background, these fields replace the flat-space free action by the string nonlinear sigma model. A convenient Euclidean convention is
SE​[X,γ;G,B,Φ]=​4πα′1​∫d2σγ​γabGμν​(X)∂a​Xμ∂b​Xν+4πα′i​∫d2σϵabBμν​(X)∂a​Xμ∂b​Xν+4π1​∫d2σγ​Φ(X)R(2).​
(13)
Here ϵab is the orientation density; reversing its convention changes the displayed sign of the two-form coupling. The metric tensor is Gμν​=ημν​+hμν​+⋯, B is the antisymmetric Kalb–Ramond field, and Φ is the dilaton. Their worldsheet couplings reproduce the corresponding massless closed-string vertex operators when expanded about flat space. The Polyakov path integral integrates e−SE​ over embedding fields and metrics modulo worldsheet diffeomorphisms and Weyl transformations; gauge fixing supplies ghost determinants and moduli integrations. Quantum consistency of a general background additionally requires vanishing sigma-model beta functions, not just insertion of arbitrary backgrounds into a free theory.
Finally, separate the constant dilaton mode, Φ=Φ0​+Φ. By the Gauss-Bonnet theorem, its action is
SΦ0​​=4πΦ0​​∫γ​R(2)=Φ0​χ(Σ).
(14)
For a connected oriented surface of genus g, its Euler characteristic is χ=2−2g. Defining the string coupling by gs​=eΦ0​ gives
e−SΦ0​​=gs−χ​=gs2g−2​.​
(15)
Thus the string genus expansion is a topology sum with two extra powers of gs​ per handle. An amplitude with n canonically normalized external closed-string vertices has the additional vertex normalization gsn​, so its overall factor is gs2g−2+n​. The curvature term, rather than a guessed coupling inserted at each internal interaction, supplies this dependence directly from the functional integral.

Distinguish target-space light-cone coordinates from the worldsheet ± labels. In target space take X±=(X0±X25)/2​, so the Minkowski metric is ds2=−2dX+dX−+∑i​(dXi)2. After conformal gauge has been chosen, the residual transformations of τ+σ and τ−σ may be used, on a patch with p+=0, to make

−2∂±​X+∂±​X−+∑i​(∂±​Xi)2=0.

∂±​X−=κ1​i∑​(∂±​Xi)2.​
(18)
Its oscillator content is fixed by the transverse coordinates, leaving d−2 independent fields. This is the light-cone gauge in string theory; it solves the constraints rather than imposing them afresh on each transverse state.
For Neumann boundary conditions at both endpoints, the open-string expansion is
Xμ=xμ+2α′pμτ+i2α′​∑n=0​nαnμ​​e−inτcos(nσ).

All spatial directions have free endpoints, so this string ends on a space-filling D25-brane. More generally, a D-brane has Neumann boundary conditions along its worldvolume and Dirichlet boundary conditions in its transverse directions; a Dp-brane has p spatial worldvolume directions. A stack may additionally supply Chan-Paton factors, without changing the oscillator construction above.
To see T-duality of D-brane boundary conditions directly, decompose the compact coordinate as X=XL​(τ+σ)+XR​(τ−σ) and define its dual by X=XL​−XR​. Differentiation gives
∂τ​X=∂σ​X,∂σ​X=∂τ​X.
(23)
Therefore the original Neumann boundary condition ∂σ​X=0 becomes ∂τ​X=0 at each endpoint. The dual endpoint coordinate is time-independent: the dual condition is Dirichlet. Conversely a fixed endpoint in X becomes Neumann in the dual coordinate. For a circle, the T-duality radius is R=α′/R. A wrapped Neumann brane loses one spatial dimension in the dual picture, for example D25 becomes D24. The two dual endpoint constants need not be identical; their positions encode the original Wilson-line data.
For the separated-brane problem, use two static parallel Dp-branes and no relative angle or background field. Let their displacement in transverse directions be dI, with ∑I​(dI)2=d2. A Dirichlet coordinate contains the classical term
XI(σ,τ)=y1I​+πdI​σ+integer-moded sine oscillators.
(24)
The spatial gradient contributes to the worldsheet Hamiltonian
4πα′1​∫0π​dσ∑I​(X′I)2=4π2α′d2​.
(25)
Parallel branes have integer modes in their NN and DD directions, so their oscillator vacuum energy is still −1. The zero-mode Virasoro constraint is consequently
α′(−E2+k∥2​)+4π2α′d2​+N−1=0.
(26)
With string tension T=1/(2πα′), the Open string stretched between D-branes has
MN2​=(Td)2+α′N−1​.​
(27)
The classical stretching energy is Td, while the quantum ground-state energy, when real, is
E0​(k∥​)=k∥2​+4π2α′2d2​−α′1​​,E0​(0)=(Td)2−α′1​​.​
(28)
The vacuum correction is an additive term in energy squared, not a constant subtraction from the classical stretching energy. It follows that the bosonic stretched-string tachyon threshold is
d0​=2πα′​,M02​<0 ⟺ d<d0​.​
(29)
Below this threshold the lowest state is a tachyon: small-momentum modes have imaginary frequency and the background is unstable, rather than having an ordinary real negative ground-state energy. It is massless at the threshold. The assumption of parallel branes is needed for the integer moding used here; relative angles change the vacuum contribution.

The Polyakov action is invariant under worldsheet diffeomorphisms and Weyl transformations, γab​↦e2ωγab​. A two-dimensional metric has three local components: two coordinate transformations and one Weyl function remove these locally. In conformal gauge the factor −γ​γab is independent of the conformal scale, and on a flat coordinate patch the matter action is
IX​=4πα′1​∫dσdτ(X˙2−X′2)=πα′1​∫dσdτ∂+​X⋅∂−​X.
(30)
Thus it is the action of d free scalar fields, with their target-space contractions using the Minkowski metric. On a Euclidean sphere there are no unpunctured conformal moduli: a metric can be put into the conformal class of the round metric, and in stereographic patches the scalar action has this free-field form. This does not mean that the sphere has a globally flat metric. A spherical worldsheet here is understood after Wick rotation; it has no nonsingular global Lorentzian metric. The residual Möbius transformations and their zero modes still have to be divided out or fixed by vertex positions.
To derive the worldsheet ghost action, consider an infinitesimal diffeomorphism generated by va and a Weyl variation ω. The metric variation separates as
δγab​=(2ω+∇⋅v)γab​+(P1​v)ab​,(P1​v)ab​=∇a​vb​+∇b​va​−γab​∇c​vc.
(31)
Integration over the Weyl parameter removes the trace gauge condition. The trace-free part produces the Faddeev-Popov determinant of an operator mapping vectors to symmetric traceless tensors. This is trace elimination in string Faddeev-Popov gauge fixing. The stated Grassmann Gaussian integral exponentiates this determinant with an odd vector ca and an odd symmetric trace-free antighost bab​.
The normalization of the operator and antighost can be chosen to match the printed convention. For example, in a flat patch take P=−P1​/2. It differs from the orbit operator only by a field-independent determinant factor. Tracelessness and symmetry give bab​(Pc)ab=−bab​∂acb. Integration by parts, followed by exchanging two odd factors, gives ∫bab​∂acb=∫ca∂bbab​. Consequently the determinant exponent is +(2π)−1∫ca∂bbab​=iIgh​ with
Igh​=−2πi​∫dσdτca∂bbab​.​
(32)
Overall constant normalization does not affect the determinant's dependence on the fields. The Faddeev-Popov ghost fields are vectors, while the antighost fields are symmetric trace-free tensors; they are not extra target-space string excitations.
Now use σ±=τ±σ, ∂±​=(∂τ​±∂σ​)/2 and c±=c0±c1. In these coordinates η+−​=η−+​=−1/2 and ∂±=−2∂∓​. Tracelessness implies b+−​=0, so directly
ca∂bbab​=−2c+∂−​b++​−2c−∂+​b−−​.
(33)
Therefore
Igh​=πi​∫dσdτ(c+∂−​b++​+c−∂+​b−−​).​
(34)
For a Cartesian check, b11​=b00​ and b++​=(b00​+b01​)/2, b−−​=(b00​−b01​)/2 reproduce the same result.
The original PDF repeats δη​c+ for its final ghost transformation. The second occurrence must be δη​c−; otherwise the displayed rules give two incompatible transformations for one ghost and none for the other. Write the corrected transformation as δη​Φ=ηsΦ, with η on the left. Then the odd left-acting BRST differential satisfies the graded Leibniz rule s(AB)=(sA)B+(−1)∣A∣A(sB). Its local rules are
sXsb++​sb−−​​=c+∂+​X+c−∂−​X,=α′i​(∂+​X)2+c+∂+​b++​+2(∂+​c+)b++​,=α′i​(∂−​X)2+c−∂−​b−−​+2(∂−​c−)b−−​.​sc±=c±∂±​c±,
(35)
The last two expressions are sb±±​=iθ±±​/α′ with the matter-plus-ghost worldsheet stress tensor in the question.
Here is an off-shell check of the action variation, including the Grassmann signs. Temporarily set c=c+, f=c−, b=b++​, h=b−−​, u=∂+​X and v=∂−​X. Apart from the common factor 1/π, the total Lagrangian is L=u⋅v/α′+i(c∂−​b+f∂+​h). Its matter variation is

The matter terms in sb and sh give the ghost-action contribution α′−1[c∂−​(u2)+f∂+​(v2)]. Combining these terms with the last two matter terms produces α′−1[∂−​(cu2)+∂+​(fv2)].

where subscripts denote derivatives. In the last equality the terms c−​c+​b and c+​c−​b cancel by anticommutation. The f,h sector has the same identity with +↔−. Thus

where

Hence the total action is BRST invariant up to a boundary term, which vanishes on a closed worldsheet or under the usual compatible boundary and time-end conditions. This calculation requires no equations of motion.
Nilpotence needs a qualification for these particular chiral rules. For either ghost, s(c∂c)=(c∂c)∂c−c∂(c∂c)=0, because c2=(∂c)2=0. Thus s2c±=0 algebraically. For the embedding, however, the graded calculation gives

(40)
Mixed second derivatives of X cancel because c+c−=−c−c+. The remaining terms vanish on the ghost equations of motion, ∂−​c+=∂+​c−=0, which follow by varying the antighosts in the chiral ghost action. They need not vanish off shell. An explicit counterexample uses independent Grassmann variables ξ,ρ: take c+=ξ, c−=ρσ+ and one coordinate X=σ−. Then s2X=−ξρ=0.
For the antighosts, use the permitted stress-tensor identity sθab​=0 on the classical solution space. Then s2b±±​=(i/α′)sθ±±​=0. Indeed direct variation of the displayed stress tensors gives
sθ++​sθ−−​​=2(∂+​c−)∂+​X⋅∂−​X+2c−∂+​X⋅∂+​∂−​X,=2(∂−​c+)∂+​X⋅∂−​X+2c+∂−​X⋅∂+​∂−​X,​
(41)
which vanish using the ghost equations and the free matter wave equation. Therefore two successive BRST transformations annihilate every field on shell. This is chiral conformal-gauge BRST nilpotence is on shell, not an unrestricted off-shell identity for arbitrary ghosts. Quantum BRST nilpotence additionally requires cancellation of the worldsheet anomaly, giving d=26 in the critical bosonic theory.

Use ∂±​=(∂τ​±∂σ​)/2 and keep each constant odd supersymmetry parameter on the left. The transformation of a fermion may be written δψ1μ​=ϵ1​∂+​Xμ or ∂+​Xμϵ1​, since the bosonic derivative commutes with the parameter. The variation δ itself is even, so it obeys the ordinary product rule; the signs below arise when moving an odd parameter past an odd field.
First consider the ϵ1​ transformation. Suppress target indices and put L=2X+​⋅X−​+iψ1​⋅∂−​ψ1​+iψ2​⋅∂+​ψ2​. Its bosonic part varies as
δ1​LX​=−iϵ1​(∂+​ψ1​⋅X−​+X+​⋅∂−​ψ1​).
(42)

δ1​Lψ1​​​=i((ϵ1​X+​)⋅∂−​ψ1​+ψ1​⋅∂−​(ϵ1​X+​))=iϵ1​(X+​⋅∂−​ψ1​−ψ1​⋅∂−​X+​).​

This proves worldsheet supersymmetry invariance up to the rigid worldsheet supersymmetry boundary term, without using field equations.
For an open string, the boundary term is part of the invariance statement. For example, a Neumann endpoint has ∂+​X=∂−​X and compatible fermion gluing ψ1​=κψ2​, κ=±1. The spatial boundary flux vanishes when ϵ2​=κϵ1​. Both endpoints must admit the same preserved parameter. Opposite gluing signs, giving the Neveu–Schwarz sector, do not preserve a nonzero constant parameter at both ends. Thus the local bulk identity and the existence of a global rigid charge on a particular open-string sector are distinct. The local superconformal symmetry remains the basis of the string constraints; this is consistent with the spin-structure obstruction to constant worldsheet supersymmetry.
Now write A±μ​=∂±​Xμ and J+1​=ψ1​⋅A+​, J−2​=ψ2​⋅A−​. The other two components vanish. The action implies ∂−​ψ1​=0, ∂+​ψ2​=0 and ∂+​∂−​X=0, so these supercurrents are chiral and conserved locally. At equal time, the given canonical commutation relations imply

[ϵi​ψiν​A±ν​,ψjμ​]=ϵi​{ψiν​,ψjμ​}A±ν​.
(47)
Using the PDF's normalization {ψiμ​(σ),ψjν​(σ′)}=πημνδij​δ(σ−σ′) yields

To calculate the supercurrent bracket, put A=∂+​X and J=ψ1​⋅A. The equal-time bosonic relations give
[Aμ(σ),Aν(σ′)]​=41​([X˙μ(σ),X′ν(σ′)]+[X′μ(σ),X˙ν(σ′)])=2iπ​ημν∂σ​δ(σ−σ′).​

The printed current identity is the classical, or noncentral, part of the algebra. Fully quantized coincident products require normal ordering; the matter algebra then also has a central contact term proportional to δ′′ in the current anticommutator. The 10 bosons and 10 real fermions contribute matter central charge c=10+10/2=15. The central term cannot be discarded in a literal quantum operator identity, although it is irrelevant to identifying the local noncentral stress tensor above.
The other brackets close on these same currents. The stress-tensor commutator contains θδ′ and θ′δ and, quantum mechanically, a central δ′′′ term. The stress-tensor–supercurrent commutator contains Jδ′ and J′δ, expressing the conformal weight 3/2 of J. The supercurrent anticommutator contains θδ and its central δ′′ term. In a conventional normalization of Fourier modes these form the N=1 super-Virasoro algebra:
[Lm​,Ln​][Lm​,Gr​]{Gr​,Gs​}​=(m−n)Lm+n​+Cmn​1,=(2m​−r)Gm+r​,=2Lr+s​+Drs​1.​
(53)
Here Cmn​ and Drs​ are central terms supported at zero total mode number; their detailed coefficients are not needed for this request. The indices r are half-integers in the Neveu–Schwarz sector and integers in the Ramond sector. Opposite chiralities commute or anticommute as appropriate.
For the closed RNS string, impose a copy of the constraints in each chirality. In the Neveu–Schwarz sector, the physical conditions are
Ln>0​∣Ψ⟩=0,Gr>0​∣Ψ⟩=0,(L0​−aNS​)∣Ψ⟩=0,aNS​=21​.

In the Ramond sector, use vacuum-normal-ordered generators with zero Ramond intercept. Then

The Ramond zero-mode equation is essential: its square gives the mass-shell condition in this convention, G02​=L0​, and on the oscillator ground sector it is the spacetime Dirac constraint. In a convention whose unshifted plane generator has G02​=L0​−c/24, the zero-mode equation instead sets that shifted combination to zero; these are the same condition, not competing intercepts.
For either choice, the corresponding tilded constraints hold on the other side. Both chiral mass-shell conditions and closed-string level matching are required:
NL​−aL​=NR​−aR​,M2=α′4​(NL​−aL​),aNS​=21​,aR​=0.​
(56)
The state sectors are NS–NS, NS–R, R–NS and R–R. Quotienting physical null states removes unphysical longitudinal polarizations; equivalently, use the appropriate BRST cohomology including the superconformal ghosts. A consistent superstring also specifies the GSO projection. It is an additional sector selection, not a replacement for these local super-Virasoro constraints. Physical states satisfy both chiral positive-mode and zero-mode constraints, level matching, and the null-state quotient; Ramond states additionally obey the fermionic zero-mode constraint.
