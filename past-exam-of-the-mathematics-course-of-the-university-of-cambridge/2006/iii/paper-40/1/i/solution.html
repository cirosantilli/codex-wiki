Take St​ to be the ex-dividend stock price: the date-t output has already been paid when trading occurs. Receiving θt​dt​ units of fruit and selling θt​−θt+1​ shares gives the discrete dividend budget equation
Ct​=θt​dt​+(θt​−θt+1​)St​,Ct​+St​θt+1​=(St​+dt​)θt​.​
(1)
The entering holding θt​ is chosen with date-(t−1) information, while Ct​ and the next holding θt+1​ may use date-t information. The output is the consumption good and price numéraire; its stochastic law is exogenous to the individual's price-taking choice.
For the interior differentiable case, attach an adapted Lagrange multiplier Λt​ to each budget constraint. On a finite horizon, with the terminal holding fixed or its continuation value included, the optimization Lagrangian is
L=E∑t​{βtU(Ct​)+Λt​[(St​+dt​)θt​−St​θt+1​−Ct​]}.
(2)
An adapted variation in Ct​ gives Λt​=βtU′(Ct​). An arbitrary date-t measurable variation in θt+1​ affects two adjacent terms. Its coefficient must have zero conditional mean, so
Λt​St​=E[Λt+1​(St+1​+dt+1​)∣Ft​],Λt​=βtU′(Ct​).​
(3)
These are the Euler equations of marginal utility pricing in a dividend economy. If the utility function is merely concave, a supporting slope replaces U′; binding consumption or trading restrictions give the corresponding inequalities rather than unconstrained equalities.
With known initial information and Λ0​>0, normalize ζt​=Λt​/Λ0​. This is a state-price density for the traded asset. In particular,
St​=Et​[ζt​ζt+1​​(St+1​+dt+1​)],ζt​ζt+1​​=βU′(Ct​)U′(Ct+1​)​.
(4)
The ratio is the stochastic discount factor: payment in a state with greater marginal utility has a higher marginal value. For a priced contingent payoff H at t+1, its price is Et​[(ζt+1​/ζt​)H]; pricing arbitrary claims uniquely also requires market completeness. The deflated cumulative gains ζt​St​+∑s=1t​ζs​ds​ form a martingale whenever the displayed terms are integrable.
Iterating the Euler equation gives
ζt​St​=Et​[∑s=t+1N​ζs​ds​+ζN​SN​].
(5)
Under the dividend-price transversality condition and the needed integrability, this becomes the fundamental price
St​=ζt​1​Et​s>t∑​ζs​ds​.​
(6)
Thus an infinite-horizon price is not automatically just a dividend sum: its terminal term must vanish. Telescoping the individual budget constraints similarly gives
E∑t=0N​ζt​Ct​=ζ0​θ0​(S0​+d0​)−E[ζN​SN​θN+1​].
(7)
A no-Ponzi lower restriction on this final term gives the consumption budget inequality; its vanishing gives equality. Together with the supporting-line inequality for a concave utility function, the Euler equations and these terminal conditions supply the usual sufficiency argument for an admissible candidate. In an incomplete market, each optimal agent's marginal utility can supply a different state-price density pricing the same traded tree.
