For a competitive equilibrium with one productive asset, the primitives are the probability law and information filtration of total output d, each agent's utility function and discount factor, the initial ownership shares θ0j​ summing to one, and the permitted consumption and trading strategies. Prices are not primitives of the equilibrium problem. The derived objects are the ex-dividend price S, each agent's consumption Cj and holdings θj, and their marginal-utility pricing processes. At a common price, every agent solves the individual problem, and resource and share clearing require
∑j=1J​Ctj​=dt​,∑j=1J​θtj​=1.

The individual discrete dividend budget equations, optimality Euler equations and terminal conditions from part (i), together with these clearing equations, define equilibrium even when the market is incomplete. Summing the individual budget constraints is consistent with total fruit consumption.
For the given constant absolute risk aversion utility, Uj′​(c)=e−γj​c. Hence each interior optimum satisfies

A single traded tree need not span the contingent transfers in the general risk-sharing formula. A genuine equilibrium example within the literal one-tree market is obtained by taking all βj​=β and initial shares θ0j​=Γ/γj​. Then
θtj​=Γ/γj​,Ctj​=(Γ/γj​)dt​,ζt​=βte−Γ(dt​−d0​).​
(16)
Every agent simply keeps the initial shares, all budget constraints and clearing conditions hold, and their discounted marginal utilities coincide. The fundamental price above satisfies every Euler equation. For example, nonnegative bounded output ensures the required price and utility integrability and the terminal terms vanish; the supporting-line argument from part (i) then verifies individual optimality. For heterogeneous discount factors or arbitrary initial ownership, the displayed coupled Euler-budget-clearing system remains the correct one-tree formulation; using the unconstrained Pareto allocation as a traded equilibrium requires an additional financing check.
