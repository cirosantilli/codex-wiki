Assume, as in the Bayesian model, that the unknown drift α is independent of the driving Brownian motion, with known positive prior precision τ0​. Recover the observed signal from the positive stock price:
Yt​=σ1​log(St​/S0​)+2σ​t=αt+Wt​.
(26)
For a fixed time t, the centered observed path Ys​−(s/t)Yt​ is a Brownian bridge independent of (α,Yt​): it is independent of the prior and has zero covariance with Wt​ in their joint Gaussian law. Thus the whole observed history has the same posterior information about α as its endpoint. This proves Brownian endpoint sufficiency for a constant drift rather than assuming it.
The likelihood of that endpoint, as a function of α, is proportional to exp(αYt​−α2t/2). Multiplying by the normal distribution prior and completing the square gives the Gaussian Brownian drift filter
α∣FtS​∼N(αt​,τt−1​),τt​=τ0​+t,αt​=τ0​+tτ0​α0​+Yt​​.​
(27)
Define the observed innovation process Wt​=Yt​−∫0t​αs​ds. It is a continuous observed-filtration martingale: for s<u, independence of future noise and the tower property of conditional expectation give E[α−αu​∣FsS​]=0, so the conditional mean of its increment vanishes. Its quadratic variation is t, since subtracting the finite-variation drift from Y does not change quadratic variation. The Lévy characterization of Brownian motion makes W an observed-filtration Brownian motion. Differentiating the posterior mean gives
dαt​=τt−1​(dYt​−αt​dt)=τt−1​dWt​.​
(28)
Conversely, the last equation determines α from W with deterministic coefficients and known initial value, and Y=W+∫αdt. Thus the observed and innovation filtrations coincide, supplying the natural Brownian information needed for replication. Consequently the observed stock dynamics are dSt​/St​=σdWt​+σαt​dt. Its observed market price of risk is κt​=αt​−r/σ, and its normalized state-price density solves
dζt​=−ζt​(rdt+κt​dWt​),ζt​=exp(−rt−∫0t​κs​dWs​−21​∫0t​κs2​ds).
(29)
The posterior mean is unbounded, so bounded-coefficient Novikov condition reasoning from Question 2 is not available automatically. A direct finite-horizon argument supplies true pricing. Relative to driftless Wiener measure for Y, the Bayesian mixture has observation density
Lt​(Yt​)=τ0​+tτ0​​​exp(2(τ0​+t)(τ0​α0​+Yt​)2​−2τ0​α02​​).
(30)
A law with observation drift k=r/σ instead has density ekYt​−k2t/2. Therefore
Dt​=Lt​(Yt​)ekYt​−k2t/2​,ζt​=e−rtDt​.​
(31)
This is the finite-horizon pricing density for Gaussian drift learning. The ratio changes the physical observed law into a genuine probability law, hence has mean one. Indeed, differentiation of the explicit likelihood, using dYt​=dWt​+αt​dt, gives
dlogLt​=αt​dYt​−21​αt2​dt,dlogDt​=(k−αt​)dWt​−21​(αt​−k)2dt.
(32)
The Itô formula therefore gives dDt​=−Dt​κt​dWt​, agreeing with the stochastic exponential above. Under this pricing law the stock drift is σk=r.
For logarithmic utility, terminal marginal utility is 1/wT​. The state-price budget constraint and the marginal condition imply wT∗​=1/(yζT​) and 1/y=w0​. Since ζT​wT∗​=w0​ is constant, its conditional-expectation pricing process is constant as well. Thus the entire optimal wealth process is
wt∗​=ζt​w0​​.​
(33)
Indeed, for any other nonnegative admissible terminal wealth X, the elementary inequality log(X/wT∗​)≤X/wT∗​−1 gives
Elog(X/wT∗​)≤E[ζT​X/w0​]−1≤0.
(34)
This directly verifies optimality. Expected candidate log wealth is finite because Eαt2​=α02​+τ0−1​−(τ0​+t)−1 and its time integral is finite on every fixed horizon. Applying the Itô formula to 1/ζ gives
wt∗​dwt∗​​=(r+κt2​)dt+κt​dWt​.
(35)
The observed self-financing portfolio with risky dollar fraction πt​ has diffusion coefficient πt​σ. Matching the coefficients proves the log-optimal investment with Gaussian drift learning rule
πt∗​=σκt​​=σ2σαt​−r​,θt∗​=πt∗​wt∗​.​
(36)
The remaining dollars are held in the bank account; the fraction may be negative or exceed one because the question imposes neither short-sale nor borrowing restrictions.
