Use θt​ for the dollar amount invested in the stock; the number of shares is θt​/St​. The bank account contains wt​−θt​ dollars. With no intermediate consumption, the self-financing portfolio equation is
dwt​=rt​(wt​−θt​)dt+θt​St​dSt​​=[rt​wt​+θt​(μt​−rt​)]dt+θt​σt​dWt​.​

If instead θ denotes share holdings, replace each dollar θt​ here by St​θt​. The equation says that trading only reallocates existing portfolio wealth; gains come from cash interest and stock returns.
For the standard unconstrained complete-diffusion formulation, assume the completed natural Brownian filtration, initial wealth w0​>0, and nonnegative admissible wealth. Define the market price of risk κt​=(μt​−rt​)/σt​. The boundedness assumptions make κ bounded. The state-price density normalized at zero is
ζt​=exp(−∫0t​rs​ds−∫0t​κs​dWs​−21​∫0t​κs2​ds),dζt​=−ζt​(rt​dt+κt​dWt​).​
(18)
Its money-market-adjusted process Zt​=e∫0t​rs​dsζt​ is a true martingale by the Novikov condition. Under dQ=ZT​dP, the Girsanov theorem gives WtQ​=Wt​+∫0t​κs​ds as a Brownian motion, and the discounted stock has zero drift.
The Itô product rule applied to the wealth equation gives
d(ζt​wt​)=ζt​(σt​θt​−κt​wt​)dWt​.
(19)
For nonnegative admissible wealth this is a nonnegative local martingale, hence a supermartingale. Therefore every affordable terminal payoff X=wT​ satisfies the state-price budget constraint E[ζT​X]≤w0​. Nonzero volatility and the Brownian martingale representation theorem allow replication of nonnegative claims with finite state-price cost.
For a differentiable strictly concave utility function with the usual conditions ensuring an interior integrable optimizer, put I=(U′)−1, the inverse marginal utility. Pointwise maximization of U(x)−yζT​x gives
wT∗​=I(yζT​),E[ζT​I(yζT​)]=w0​.​
(20)
The second equation determines the positive constant y. To prove optimality, use the concave supporting-tangent inequality:
U(X)−U(wT∗​)≤U′(wT∗​)(X−wT∗​)=yζT​(X−wT∗​).
(21)
Taking expectations and using the budget bound proves that the candidate dominates every admissible affordable payoff, whenever the utility expectations are well defined. This is the complete-market terminal utility optimizer.
The actual wealth and dollar portfolio are as explicit as general adapted coefficients permit. Let
Nt​=E[ζT​I(yζT​)∣Ft​],dNt​=ht​dWt​.
(22)
The Brownian martingale representation theorem, with localization if only L1 integrability is initially known, gives h. The dollar portfolio from a deflated wealth martingale is
wt∗​=ζt​Nt​​,θt∗​=σt​ht​/ζt​+κt​wt∗​​.​

The bank holds wt∗​−θt∗​ dollars. Equivalently, with Bt​=e∫0t​rs​ds, wt∗​=Bt​EQ[wT∗​/BT​∣Ft​]. These formulas need no unjustified differentiability of a value function with random coefficients.
The printed assumption that U is only increasing and concave does not guarantee that the interior formula exists, or even that the optimal value is finite. In general replace the inverse formula by an affordable selection
X∗(ω)∈argmaxx∈D​{U(x)−yζT​(ω)x},
(24)
where D is the wealth domain; boundary choices or supporting slopes handle nondifferentiability. Existence, finite cost and replication still have to hold. A concrete obstruction is the unbounded linear terminal-wealth utility: take U(x)=x, r=0, μ=σ=1, and the Brownian filtration. Then ζT​=e−WT​−T/2. For An​={ζT​<1/n}, the bounded nonnegative claim
Xn​=E[ζT​1An​​]w0​1An​​​
(25)
is replicable, costs w0​, and has EXn​>nw0​. Thus there is no finite optimal expected utility under the literal utility hypotheses alone. Also, bounded coefficients do not ensure market completeness in an arbitrarily enlarged filtration containing untraded randomness; the full replication formula uses the Brownian market information assumption stated above.
