The first principal component has equal positive coefficients on the three academic subjects and zero coefficient on physical education. It measures overall academic performance in those subjects and explains 71.16% of the variance. The large positive academic covariances explain why these subjects vary predominantly together.
The second principal component has normalized coefficients approximately (0.099,0,−0.099,0.990). It is mainly a physical-education score, with a small adjustment for the physics-versus-mathematics contrast, and explains 12.90% of the variance. The small physical-education covariances with the academic subjects make this direction close to a separate physical-education axis. This is an interpretation of the observed covariance matrix, not proof of distinct causal abilities or statistical independence.
The third principal component compares physics and mathematics together against biology: its coefficient vector is (1,−2,1,0)/6​. It captures subject balance rather than overall academic performance, accounting for 9.49% of the variance. The fourth principal component primarily contrasts physics with mathematics, with a smaller physical-education correction; its normalized coefficients are approximately (0.700,0,−0.700,−0.140), and it accounts for the remaining 6.45%. Changing all signs in any component reverses its score without changing the interpretation of its contrast or its explained variance of a principal component.
A two-component summary separates broad academic performance from predominantly physical-education performance and retains about 84.06% of the variation. It still discards about 15.94%, including differences between academic subjects. Whether that loss is acceptable depends on the purpose: an overall summary may use two principal components, whereas studying subject-specific strengths requires the later principal components too. A single principal component would lose most of the physical-education information.
