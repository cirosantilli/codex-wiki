Assume an independent Gaussian sample, positive-definite population covariance, and n>p. Define the sample mean and unbiased sample covariance matrix by
Xˉ=n1​∑i=1n​Xi​,S=n−11​∑i=1n​(Xi​−Xˉ)(Xi​−Xˉ)T.
(7)
The standard test is based on Hotelling's T-squared statistic
T2=n(Xˉ−μ0​)TS−1(Xˉ−μ0​).​
(8)
Reject the null hypothesis for large values. Under the null its exact distribution is
p(n−1)n−p​T2∼Fp,n−p​.​
(9)
The factor uses the unbiased, divisor-n−1 covariance convention. Normal-sample theory makes Xˉ independent of the residual covariance, whose whitened version has a Wishart distribution; this yields the stated F-distribution. The condition n>p gives invertibility of S almost surely and positive denominator degrees of freedom.
This is also the likelihood-ratio test. To see the connection, put W=(n−1)S and δ=Xˉ−μ0​. The maximized covariance estimates under the unrestricted and null models are W/n and W/n+δδT, respectively. The determinant identity for a rank-one update gives
∣W/n∣∣W/n+δδT∣​=1+nδTW−1δ=1+n−1T2​.
(10)
Thus the likelihood ratio is (1+T2/(n−1))−n/2, decreasing in T2. The term best is understood here as this standard likelihood-ratio, affine-invariant procedure; a two-sided multidimensional alternative does not specify a single direction to optimize power against.
For the transformed sample Yi​=AXi​+b, with A the prescribed nonsingular coordinate transformation, we have
Yˉ−(Aμ0​+b)=A(Xˉ−μ0​),SY​=ASAT,SY−1​=A−TS−1A−1.
(11)
Consequently
TY2​=nδTAT(A−TS−1A−1)Aδ=nδTS−1δ=TX2​.
(12)
This proves the affine invariance of Hotelling's statistic. The statistic, its p-value and its test decision are unchanged by a nonsingular affine transformation, provided the null mean is transformed with the data. Changes of units and rotations are included.
