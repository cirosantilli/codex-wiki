Apply the chain rule to the squared loss, using the same pre-update weights throughout a forward and backward pass. At the output, ∂E/∂zk​=zk​−tk​ and ∂zk​/∂xk​=gk′​(xk​). Therefore
∂wjk​∂E​=(zk​−tk​)gk′​(xk​)zj​.
(11)
For a gradient descent step of size η>0, define δk​=η(tk​−zk​)gk′​(xk​). This gives Δwjk​=δk​zj​​.
A hidden activation affects all output units to which it connects. Thus
∂xj​∂E​=gj′​(xj​)∑k​wjk​(zk​−tk​)gk′​(xk​),
(12)
and
δj​=gj′​(xj​)k∑​wjk​δk​,Δwij​=δj​zi​​.
(13)
The learning rate is already contained in δk​ and hence in δj​; do not multiply by it a second time. For a sigmoid function, g′(x)=g(x)[1−g(x)]. Thresholds can be treated as negative biases with an extra constant input and updated by the same derivative rule.
This is squared-error backpropagation. The forward pass computes activations, while the backward pass transports output-loss derivatives through the outgoing weights and the derivatives of the activation functions, yielding an error signal for every hidden unit. That reverse flow of derivatives explains the name backpropagation.
