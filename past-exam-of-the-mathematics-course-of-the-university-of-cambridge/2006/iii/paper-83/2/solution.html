Use constant porosity ϕ, isotropic rock permeability of a porous medium K, common liquid dynamic viscosity μ and gravitational acceleration g. Treat the two liquids as separated by a sharp interface, with negligible capillary pressure, molecular mixing, ambient-current resistance and inertia. At distances beyond the initial source scale the current is shallow relative to its horizontal length, so its pressure is hydrostatic. The initial release is symmetric about x=0 and has actual liquid volume V per unit length of source. Consequently its geometric thickness integral is V/ϕ, not V.
Below the interface the excess pressure relative to the ambient hydrostatic field is p′=Δρg(h−z), so Darcy law gives horizontal discharge qx​=−KΔρghx​/μ. The vertically integrated discharge is Qx​=−KΔρghhx​/μ. Take the specified downward leakage v=γh to mean Darcy flux per bulk horizontal area. Mass conservation then gives
ϕht​+∂x​Qx​=−γh,ht​=β(hhx​)x​−λh,β=μϕKΔρg​,λ=ϕγ​.​
(8)
If v is instead defined as interstitial seepage speed, the actual leakage discharge is ϕv and the corresponding coefficient is λ=γ. Stating the velocity convention removes that otherwise ambiguous porosity factor. For a shale of thickness ds​ and permeability Ks​, a simple hydrostatic leakage model has γ=Ks​Δρg/(μds​), if the reservoir below it has the same ambient pressure reference and no capillary entry threshold.
Let R(t) be the half-width of the support; the full end-to-end length will be L(t)=2R(t). Appropriate conditions are symmetry hx​(0,t)=0, h(±R,t)=0, no flux beyond the support, and the moving-front condition R˙=−βhx​(R−,t). Initially ϕh(x,t)dx converges weakly to Vδ0​, expressing the concentrated line release. The equations describe the spreading porous gravity current, not the singular immediate release itself.
With λ=0, conserved volume and balance of ht​ against β(hhx​)x​ give width proportional to t1/3 and height proportional to t−1/3. Write h=t−1/3f(η), η=x/t1/3. Substitution gives
−31​(f+ηf′)=β(ff′)′.
(9)
Integrating from the symmetry axis gives βff′=−ηf/3. Inside the support f>0, so f′=−η/(3β) and f=A−η2/(6β). Thus the compact Barenblatt solution is
h(x,t)=6βt[R(t)2−x2]+​​.​
(10)
The positive-part notation makes h=0 outside the current. Volume fixes its remaining constant:
ϕV​=∫−RR​hdx=9βt2R3​,R(t)=(2ϕ9βVt​)1/3,L(t)=2(2ϕ9βVt​)1/3.​
(11)
If “length” denotes the distance from the source to one front, it is R rather than L; both conventions are given explicitly. Differentiating gives R˙=R/(3t)=−βhx​(R−,t), so the front condition holds. Using V/2 on each reflecting half-line gives the same normalization.
For positive leakage define actual retained volume V(t)=ϕ∫hdx. Since the edge thickness and boundary flux vanish, integration of the equation yields
dtdV​=−λV,V(t)=Ve−λt.​
(12)
Put h=e−λtH(x,τ), where τ=(1−e−λt)/λ. Because τ˙=e−λt, substitution cancels the leakage term and leaves Hτ​=β(HHx​)x​. This derives the exponential drainage transform for porous-medium diffusion. Its conserved geometric volume is still V/ϕ, so
h(x,t)=e−λt6βτ(t)[R(t)2−x2]+​​,R(t)=[2ϕλ9βV​(1−e−λt)]1/3.​
(13)
The full length is again L(t)=2R(t). In particular,
R∞​=(2ϕλ9βV​)1/3,L∞​=2R∞​.​
(14)
At early times τ∼t, recovering impermeable-bed spreading. At late times, drainage reduces the current thickness and therefore its hydrostatic driving pressure; the effective spreading time saturates at 1/λ. The current approaches a finite horizontal reach while its retained volume and thickness vanish exponentially. This does not mean a positive-volume pool remains at a finite final depth. For small positive λ the length scale diverges as λ−1/3, consistently with unbounded spreading when leakage is absent.
