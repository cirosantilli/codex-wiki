Model the post-eruption flow as an axisymmetric, shallow, hydrostatic, turbulently mixed layer on a horizontal bed. Neglect entrainment, erosion, heat exchange, rotation and large-scale viscous drag. Particles are dilute, with density ρp​, uniform-in-depth particle volume fraction c(r,t) and constant downward settling speed ws​>0 relative to the carrier gas. Deposited particles are not resuspended. Their volume is small enough that settling does not significantly change the bulk carrier volume. Let ρa​ be ambient air density and ρg​ carrier-gas density at its local temperature, and use a leading Boussinesq approximation for the mixture motion. Define
g′=bp​c+bT​,bp​=ρa​g(ρp​−ρa​)​,bT​=ρa​g(ρg​−ρa​)​<0.
(15)
The hot gas reduces the density excess; the ground-hugging current model requires g′>0. If density contrasts are too large for Boussinesq dynamics, density-weighted balances would be needed instead. The equations below are a stated shallow-layer model, not a description of the initial explosive vent jet.

With no heat exchange or entrainment, a carrier-temperature anomaly Θ is advected:

Axis regularity requires u(0,t)=0 and smooth scalar fields, with no further source after the initial pulse. The initial height, particle inventory and temperature anomaly specify the released volume and ash content. Outside the front r=R(t), the current layer is absent. At a resolved advancing head, use the material-front condition and the gravity-current front condition R˙=Fgf′​hf​​, where F is an order-one front Froude number and hf​,gf′​ refer to the fluid feeding the head. The front closure replaces unresolved head dynamics; simply prescribing both a zero-depth mathematical edge and a finite-depth box-head speed would be inconsistent.

All these predictions depend on the mixing and constant-Froude head closure; they do not claim to resolve the initial blast or the ultimate inertia-dominated motion of almost particle-free gas.
