Let z>0 measure height from the lower edge and y>0 distance from one face of the vertical plate. Use the steady Boussinesq approximation, homogeneous isotropic permeability of a porous medium K, reference fluid mass density ρ0​, dynamic viscosity μ, thermal expansion coefficient α, effective thermal conductivity ke​ and liquid volumetric heat capacity Cf​=ρ0​cf​. Define B=ρ0​gαK/μ and κ=ke​/Cf​, consistently with the thermal convention used above. Write θ=T−Ta​, where Ta​ is the uniform ambient temperature.
Interpret F as uniform wall heat-flux density: a plate segment of height dz inputs heat Fdz per unit horizontal span. The cumulative heat input up to height z is therefore Fz. If F instead denotes the sum of flux densities into two identically heated faces, replace it by F/2 in each face formula before summing. A specification only of total power per span, without its distribution in height, would not determine this uniformly heated-wall problem. We calculate one heated face; identical heating on both faces doubles the integrated fluxes.
Away from the leading edge, suppose the boundary layer is slender, δ(z)≪z, and porous inertia and longitudinal heat conduction are negligible. Lateral pressure adjustment leaves the vertical Darcy velocity proportional to buoyancy:
w=Bθ,vy​+wz​=0,vθy​+wθz​=κθyy​.
(34)
At the plate, v=0 and −ke​θy​=F; in the far field w,θ→0. Darcy law permits tangential discharge at an impermeable plate, so imposing a further no-slip condition w=0 would be inappropriate in this leading porous model.
Balance θ∼Fδ/ke​, w∼Bθ and wθ/z∼κθ/δ2 to obtain
δ(z)=(BFκke​z​)1/3,θ∼ke​Fδ​∝z1/3,w∝z1/3.​
(35)
Thus the warmed layer thickens and accelerates upward. Define a streamfunction by w=ψy​, v=−ψz​ and set
η=δy​,ψ=δκz​f(η),w=δ2κz​f′(η),θ=ke​Fδ​f′(η).
(36)
The last two expressions agree because δ3=κke​z/(BF). Differentiation gives v=−(κ/3δ)(2f−ηf′). Substituting in the thermal equation leaves the single nonlinear equation for the uniformly heated vertical plate in a porous medium:
f′′′+32​ff′′−31​(f′)2=0,f(0)=0,f′′(0)=−1,f′(∞)=0.​
(37)
The first condition is impermeability; the second fixes the wall heat flux; the third fixes ambient temperature and vertical velocity. Seek the positive-temperature branch with f′≥0 and finite f(∞). Decay also gives f′′(∞)=0. The far-field transverse inflow need not vanish: v∞​=−(2κ/3δ)f(∞) supplies the increasing discharge.
An approximate solution can be obtained without a numerical shooting calculation. Integrate the differential equation over 0<η<∞. The boundary conditions and integration by parts give
0=[f′′]0∞​+32​[ff′]0∞​−∫0∞​(f′)2dη=1−∫0∞​(f′)2dη.
(38)
Thus the exact thermal budget is ∫0∞​(f′)2dη=1. Assume an exponential thermal shape, f=C(1−e−η/d). The wall derivative requires C/d2=1, and the integral condition requires C2/(2d)=1. Hence d=21/3, C=22/3 and the integral exponential profile for porous wall convection is
f(η)≃22/3(1−e−η/21/3),θ(y,z)≃ke​21/3Fδ​e−y/(21/3δ).​
(39)
It satisfies all boundary conditions, has positive decaying temperature, and matches the exact integrated heat budget. It is an integral approximation, not an exact pointwise solution of the differential equation; its mass-flux coefficient should be read accordingly.
Define the volume discharge, kinematic Darcy momentum flux and buoyancy flux per horizontal span by
Q(z)=∫0∞​wdy,M(z)=∫0∞​w2dy,B(z)=gα∫0∞​wθdy.
(40)
With these standard discharge conventions the mass flux is m˙=ρ0​Q. Their similarity expressions are
Q=δκz​f(∞),M=δ3κ2z2​∫0∞​(f′)2dη,B=Bgα​M.
(41)
The integral identity evaluates two of them exactly:
M(z)=ke​BκFz​=Cf​BFz​,B(z)=ke​gακFz​=Cf​gαFz​.​
(42)
Equivalently Cf​∫wθdy=Fz: all the heat supplied below height z is carried upward through that section. Although these fluxes are exact within the boundary-layer equations, porous drag means that the momentum flux itself is not conserved with height.
The approximate mass flux is
m˙(z)≃22/3ρ0​δ(z)κz​=22/3ρ0​κ2/3(ke​BF​)1/3z2/3.​
(43)
For clarity, if “momentum flux” means actual pore-fluid momentum rather than the commonly quoted kinematic Darcy diagnostic, constant porosity ϕ and pore speed w/ϕ give P˙=(ρ0​/ϕ)M. The mass flux remains ρ0​Q because w already denotes discharge per bulk area. Stating both prevents an implicit porosity error. Validity also requires αθ≪1 and a sufficiently slender, low-inertia porous boundary layer; the approximation is not the immediate leading-edge conduction field.
