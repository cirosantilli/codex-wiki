Relative to a filtration satisfying the usual conditions, a local martingale is an adapted càdlàg process X for which there exist increasing stopping times Sn​↑∞ almost surely such that every stopped process XtSn​​=Xt∧Sn​​ is an integrable martingale. Thus, for s≤t,
E[Xt∧Sn​​∣Fs​]=Xs∧Sn​​.
(1)
The sequence is a localizing sequence. A continuous local martingale additionally has continuous sample paths. The usual definition entails integrability of the initial value; local martingales need not be integrable at later times without stopping.

Let Vt​ be the total variation of X on [0,t], and set τn​=inf{t:Vt​≥n}. Adaptation and continuity of V make these stopping times; finite variation on every compact interval gives τn​↑∞. The Dambis-Dubins-Schwarz theorem states: let M be a continuous local martingale with M0​=0 and [M]∞​=∞ almost surely. Set
τu​=inf{t≥0:[M]t​>u},Gu​=Fτu​​.
(14)
Then Wu​=Mτu​​ is standard Brownian motion relative to (Gu​) and
Mt​=W[M]t​​.​
(15)
Strict increase of the bracket is not required in the general theorem: M is constant on intervals on which its bracket is constant. Continuity of M is required. A nonzero initial value is handled by applying the theorem to M−M0​ and adding it back. If the terminal bracket is finite, the finite-lifetime extension of the Dambis-Dubins-Schwarz theorem supplies the Brownian motion up to that bracket time and, if necessary, an independent continuation on an enlarged probability space.

Uniqueness in law means that, for a fixed initial value, every two weak stochastic solutions of the stochastic differential equation have the same distribution as entire solution paths, even if their filtered probability spaces and driving Brownian motions differ. For an equation on (0,∞), initially consider the stopped path and lifetime at the boundary.
We first justify that the boundary cannot intervene when γ≥2. For any solution of the displayed Bessel equation, let τε,L​ be its first exit from (ε,L), where 0<ε<r<L. Applying Itô to R2 gives
ERt∧τε,L​2​=r2+γE(t∧τε,L​)≤L2.
(27)
Thus the annulus exit has finite expectation and is almost surely finite. The one-dimensional generator is 21​∂rr​+2rγ−1​∂r​. Its harmonic scale functions are logr for γ=2 and r2−γ for γ>2, as direct differentiation verifies. They are bounded on the stopped annulus, so optional sampling gives
Pr​(τε​<τL​)=⎩⎨⎧​log(L/ε)log(L/r)​,ε2−γ−L2−γr2−γ−L2−γ​,​γ=2,γ>2.​
(28)
Both tend to zero as ε↓0. A continuous path hitting zero before τL​ would hit every inner level first, so that event has probability zero. Exhausting the upper levels proves that zero is not hit at any finite time. Nor can an upper explosion occur: localization of R2 gives P(τL​≤t)≤(r2+γt)/L2→0. These are the Hitting-zero classification for a Bessel process arguments needed for the dimension-at-least-two case.
Now suppose γ is an integer and take a γ-dimensional Brownian motion U from any deterministic vector u with ∣u∣=r. For ρ=∣U∣>0, differentiation of the Euclidean norm gives
∂i​∣u∣=ui​/∣u∣,Δ∣u∣=(γ−1)/∣u∣.
(29)
Define the radial noise β=∑i​∫Ui​/ρdWi​, filling the integrand by a fixed unit vector on the zero set, as in part (b). It has bracket t and is a Brownian motion. the Itô formula, initially before a possible zero, gives
dρt​=dβt​+2ρt​γ−1​dt,ρ0​=r.
(30)
The boundary argument just given makes this a global positive weak stochastic solution. The assumed uniqueness in law therefore identifies every solution's path law with that of ∣U∣. Orthogonal invariance of Brownian motion makes this radial law independent of the selected point u on the sphere. Consequently
(Rt​)t≥0​ =law (∣u+Wt​∣)t≥0​,∣u∣=r.​
(31)
This proves equality of process laws, not merely equality of one-time marginal distributions.

Let X,Y be two solutions with the same Brownian driver and the same initial value. Write D=X−Y, and take a common Lipschitz constant L for both coefficients. Stop when ∣X∣+∣Y∣ reaches n. Before this stopping time τn​, all difference integrands below are bounded. The Itô formula gives
d(D2)=2D[σ(X)−σ(Y)]dB+([σ(X)−σ(Y)]2+2D[b(X)−b(Y)])dt.
(32)
The stopped stochastic integral is a true martingale on every finite horizon. Lipschitz continuity therefore yields
EDt∧τn​2​≤(L2+2L)∫0t​E[1{s<τn​}​Ds2​]ds≤(L2+2L)∫0t​EDs∧τn​2​ds.
(33)
Since D0​=0, the Gronwall inequality makes the left side zero. Let n→∞; the continuous global solutions are bounded on each compact time interval, so their stopping times exhaust it. Equality first at every rational time and then by continuity gives indistinguishability. This proves pathwise uniqueness for globally Lipschitz drift and diffusion coefficients. The argument does not require the initial value to have a global second moment: after stopping, the difference is bounded and initially zero.

A Markov jump process, considered up to its explosion time, is an adapted càdlàg piecewise-constant process whose conditional future law given Ft​ depends only on its current state. Describe its rates by an off-diagonal kernel q(x,dy) with finite total rate q(x,E). In state x the holding time has the exponential distribution with this rate, followed by a destination distributed as q(x,dy)/q(x,E); a zero-rate state is absorbing. Its Markov jump-process generator acts by
Qf(x)=∫E​[f(y)−f(x)]q(x,dy).
(48)
The Markov property must hold relative to the specified filtration, not merely the natural filtration: future-revealing information could change the jump compensator.
Let μ count jumps, marked by their destination states. For this jump measure of a Markov jump process, the predictable compensator is
ν(ds,dy)=q(Xs−​,dy)ds.
(49)
The compensated random-measure theorem says that a predictable H(s,y) with E∫0t​∫∣H∣dν<∞ gives a true martingale
(H∗(μ−ν))t​=∫(0,t]×E​H(s,y)(μ−ν)(ds,dy).
(50)
The same statement holds locally when that expected integrability is obtained after localization. The compensator identity applied to 1A​1(s,t]​H, for A∈Fs​, shows that each increment has zero conditional expectation. Taking H(s,y)=f(y)−f(Xs−​) gives the generator local martingale f(Xt​)−f(X0​)−∫0t​Qf(Xs​)ds. This states the integrability conditions in the compensated jump-measure local martingale result explicitly.
For the birth-process claim first take the customary fixed initial state X0​=i. Write Nt​=Xt​−i, Λt​=∫0t​λ(Xs​)ds and a=eθ−1. The compensator of N is Λ, whose integrand may equivalently use Xs−​ because jump times have zero Lebesgue measure. A birth multiplies eθX by eθ, so the jump product rule gives
dMt​=aMt−​(dNt​−λ(Xt−​)dt),M0​=eθi.​
(51)
For an explicit localization, stop at τn​=inf{t:Xt​≥n}∧n with n>i. Up to that time the statewise rates are bounded by the maximum of the finitely many rates below n, the jump count is bounded, and M and its compensated-integral integrand are bounded on the finite stopped horizon. The compensated-measure theorem therefore makes each stopped process a true martingale. The times increase to the explosion lifetime (or infinity if there is no explosion), proving M is a local martingale on [0,ζ) for every real θ.
Now suppose λ(j)≤C uniformly. For C>0, propose candidate births at the times of a rate-C Poisson process, accepting each with probability λ(Xs−​)/C using fresh independent uniform marks. While the state is fixed, Poisson thinning gives a holding time with the required exponential distribution of rate λ; accepted births have exactly the prescribed law. Thus there can be no explosion, and the true birth count NT​ is stochastically dominated by a Poisson random variable of mean CT. In particular,
EebNT​≤exp{CT(eb−1)}<∞(b≥0).
(52)
The case C=0 is constant and immediate. For every fixed horizon T and all s≤T, including localization times,
0≤Ms∧τn​​≤exp{θi+θ+​NT​+(1−eθ)+​CT}.
(53)
This is one integrable dominating variable for the entire stopped family. Conditional dominated convergence removes the localization and proves
E[Mt​∣Fs​]=Ms​(s≤t),​
(54)
so the exponential martingale of a pure birth process is a genuine martingale for uniformly bounded rates. Only uniform integrability on each finite horizon is asserted; it need not be uniformly integrable over infinite time.
For a random initial state, the same proof requires EeθX0​<∞. The source does not explicitly specify the initial distribution, so that qualification cannot be dropped: take constant rate one and an independent initial law P(X0​=n)=6/(π2n2). For θ>0, already EM0​=∞, precluding even the integrability in the definition of a martingale. The fixed-initial-state interpretation above supplies the intended complete proof; bounded rates alone do not remedy an arbitrary heavy-tailed initial law.
