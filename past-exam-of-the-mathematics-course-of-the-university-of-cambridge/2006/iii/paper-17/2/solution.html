The affine module sheaf construction makes the remaining statements explicit. For A=k[V] and an A-module M, define
M(D(f))=Mf​
(11)
on the basis of principal open subsets. Restrictions are the natural localization maps. Equivalently, a section on an arbitrary open set is a family sP​∈MmP​​ which near each point is represented by a single fraction m/fr, with f nonvanishing throughout that neighbourhood. This locally representable family construction gives a sheaf; the usual localization gluing verifies its quasi-coherence. Its stalk is MmP​​ and
Γ(V,M)=M​.
(12)
This last identification is precisely the standard module-localization gluing on the affine basis, not an assertion that arbitrary sheaves are determined by their global sections.
Now write A=k[X], B=k[Y] and α=ϕ#:A→B. Let G=M, H=N for A-modules M,N, and let F=L for a B-module L. The affine versions of the three constructions are
G⊗OX​​H≅M⊗A​N​,ϕ∗​F≅A​L​,ϕ∗H≅B⊗A​N​​.
(13)
Here A​L denotes restriction of scalars along α, whereas the last associated sheaf lives on Y. To verify the tensor-product assertion, localize at every prime: both stalks are Mm​⊗Am​​Nm​. For the direct image, ϕ−1D(a)=D(α(a)), and
Γ(D(a),ϕ∗​L)=Lα(a)​=(A​L)a​.
(14)
These equalities identify the sheaves on a basis and respect restrictions. For the pullback of a sheaf of modules, its stalk at Q is BmQ​​⊗A​N, exactly the stalk of B⊗A​N​. The local tensor maps are compatible, so these stalk identifications come from an actual sheaf isomorphism. Thus all three constructions are quasi-coherent in the stated affine setting.
Finally construct the natural projection formula for sheaves map by sending a local tensor m⊗b to b(1⊗m) in the pulled-back module, followed by direct image. In the affine description, the target ϕ∗​ϕ∗M is the sheaf associated with the A-module A​(B⊗A​M), while M⊗OX​​ϕ∗​OY​ corresponds to M⊗A​B. The maps
m⊗b⟼b⊗m,b⊗m⟼m⊗b
(15)
are inverse A-linear maps. Localizing them commutes with every restriction, proving
ϕ∗​ϕ∗M≅M⊗OX​​ϕ∗​OY​​.
(16)
No flatness assumption on A→B or finiteness assumption on M is needed.
