Choose the maximal torus of SO(2n) consisting of rotations in n orthogonal planes, and write zi​=eiθi​. In the complexified standard representation E=C2n the torus eigenvalues are z1​,z1−1​,…,zn​,zn−1​, with weights ei​,−ei​. For n≥2 the complexified Lie algebra has the Dn root system {±ei​±ej​:i<j}. The root-space decomposition theorem splits this Lie algebra into its Cartan subalgebra and one-dimensional root spaces. The Cartan subalgebra contributes the zero weight with multiplicity n. Therefore its character is
χad​(z)=n+i<j∑​(zi​zj​+zj​zi​​+zi​zj​​+zi​zj​1​).​
(32)
Take positive roots ei​−ej​,ei​+ej​ for i<j, so ρ=(n−1,n−2,…,0). For n>2 the root θ=e1​+e2​ is a highest weight: its root vector is killed by every positive root vector, since θ+α is never a root for positive α. To verify irreducibility without relying on an unstated simplicity theorem, apply complete reducibility and compare dimensions. For the Dn root system the Weyl dimension formula reads
dimVλ​=∏i<j​ρi2​−ρj2​(λi​+ρi​)2−(λj​+ρj​)2​.
(33)
For λ=(1,1,0,…,0) only pairs involving the first two indices change. With s=0,…,n−3, cancellation gives
dimVθ​=2n−32n−1​∏s=0n−3​(n−2)2−s2n2−s2​=2n−32n−1​(2n(n−1)​)(n−12(2n−3)​)=n(2n−1).
(34)
This is exactly dimso2n​(C), so the irreducible summand containing the highest-weight vector exhausts the Adjoint representation. Thus for n>2 the complexified Adjoint representation is irreducible, with highest weight e1​+e2​.
For n=2, the roots split into the two independent systems {±(e1​+e2​)} and {±(e1​−e2​)}. The Adjoint representation splits into two irreducibles of dimension three, with respective highest weights (1,1) and (1,−1). Equivalently these are the self-dual and anti-self-dual exterior two-forms in four dimensions. Their characters are
1+z1​z2​+(z1​z2​)−1,1+z1​/z2​+z2​/z1​.
(35)
They add to the displayed adjoint character. This agrees with the Chiral decomposition of the complexified so4 Lie algebra.
For a direct character comparison, put s(z)=∑i​(zi​+zi−1​). The eigenvalue formula for an exterior square gives
χΛ2E​(z)=21​(s(z)2−∑i​(zi2​+zi−2​))=n+∑i<j​(zi​zj​+zi​/zj​+zj​/zi​+(zi​zj​)−1).
(36)
This is precisely χad​. More intrinsically, let B be the nondegenerate symmetric bilinear form preserved by SO(2n) and define
Ψ(u∧v)(x)=B(v,x)u−B(u,x)v.
(37)
This endomorphism is skew with respect to B, so Ψ maps Λ2E to so(E,B). In an orthonormal basis the bivectors ei​∧ej​ map to a basis of skew skew-symmetric matrices, proving that it is an isomorphism. Since B is invariant,
Ψ(gu∧gv)=gΨ(u∧v)g−1.
(38)
Thus the exterior square and the complexified Adjoint representation are naturally isomorphic, explaining the character equality without a weight calculation.
The symmetric square is not irreducible. The inverse metric tensor Ω is a nonzero invariant vector, and contraction with B splits off its trivial line:
Sym2E=CΩ⊕Sym02​E.
(39)
The contraction sends Ω to 2n, so this is indeed a direct sum. A highest-weight vector f1​⊗f1​, where f1​ has torus weight e1​, lies in the traceless part because B(f1​,f1​)=0, and has highest weight 2e1​. The same dimension formula gives, for n≥2,
dimV2e1​​=∏s=0n−2​(n−1)2−s2(n+1)2−s2​=(2n(n+1)​)(n2(2n−1)​)=(n+1)(2n−1).
(40)
This equals dimSym2E−1=n(2n+1)−1. Complete reducibility therefore proves that the traceless part is irreducible, including the dimension-nine case for SO(4). In particular,
Sym2(C2n)≅1⊕V2e1​​(n≥2),so it is reducible.​
(41)
If the circle case n=1 is included, the adjoint character is 1 and the symmetric square instead has three one-dimensional weights 2,0,−2.
