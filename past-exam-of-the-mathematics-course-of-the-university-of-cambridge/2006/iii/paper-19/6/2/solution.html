 ... Past exam of the mathematics course of the University of Cambridge 2006 iii Paper 19 6 2
OurBigBook.com Words: 182
Use the corrected generating series of the Kostant partition function and the permitted Weyl character formula:
χλ​=(∑w∈W​ε(w)ew(λ+ρ)−ρ)(∑ν​p(ν)e−ν).
(45)
For a fixed w, the coefficient of eμ arises exactly when
w(λ+ρ)−ρ−ν=μ,that is,ν=w(λ+ρ)−(μ+ρ).
(46)
There are only finitely many Weyl-group terms, and the partition function vanishes outside the cone of nonnegative root combinations. Coefficient extraction is therefore legitimate in the formal completion used in the preceding part. Summing the signed contributions proves
mλ​(μ)=w∈W∑​ε(w)p(w(λ+ρ)−(μ+ρ)).​
(47)
This proves the Kostant multiplicity formula for every compact connected Lie group, not just the unitary groups. In the presence of a central torus, roots have zero central component, so the partition function automatically gives zero for any incompatible central weight. Also ρ need not itself be an integral weight for the given global group: wρ−ρ always belongs to the root lattice, so every argument in the formula is nevertheless integral whenever λ and μ are integral group weights.
As a normalization check, for SU(2) write a highest weight as the nonnegative integer ℓ, with positive root 2 and ρ=1. The two Weyl-group terms give
mℓ​(μ)=p(ℓ−μ)−p(−ℓ−μ−2).
(48)
Since p(a)=1 precisely when a is even and nonnegative, this is one for μ=ℓ,ℓ−2,…,−ℓ and zero otherwise, including the cancellation below the lowest weight. This recovers the full familiar weight string.
