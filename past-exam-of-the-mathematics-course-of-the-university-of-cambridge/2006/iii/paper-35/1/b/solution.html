Let Vt​ be the total variation of X on [0,t], and set τn​=inf{t:Vt​≥n}. Adaptation and continuity of V make these stopping times; finite variation on every compact interval gives τn​↑∞. The stopped process U=Xτn​ satisfies ∣Ut​∣≤n and has total variation at most n. It is a martingale by the bounded local martingale criterion, and is square-integrable.
Fix t and take deterministic partitions 0=t0​<⋯<tm​=t with mesh tending to zero. The martingale-difference orthogonality of the increments gives
E[Ut2​]=∑j​E[(Utj+1​​−Utj​​)2].
(2)
Pathwise, however,
∑j​(Utj+1​​−Utj​​)2≤maxj​∣Utj+1​​−Utj​​∣∑j​∣Utj+1​​−Utj​​∣≤nmaxj​∣Utj+1​​−Utj​​∣⟶0,
(3)
by uniform continuity on [0,t]. These sums are bounded by n2, so the dominated convergence theorem gives EUt2​=0. Hence Ut​=0 almost surely. Take all rational t on a common probability-one event and use continuity to obtain U≡0. Then let n→∞. This proves the continuous finite-variation local martingale is constant result in its zero-starting form:
Xt​=0 for every t≥0 almost surely.​
(4)
The proof does not assume the uniqueness of quadratic variation that is to be established next.
