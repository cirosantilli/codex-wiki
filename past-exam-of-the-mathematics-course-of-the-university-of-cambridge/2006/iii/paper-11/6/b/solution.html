Let
h(M)=xM∖{minM}∗​(xminM​).
(31)
This scalar function is continuous. The first element is locally constant; deleting it is continuous on increasing sequences; and evaluation on a fixed xm​ is continuous in the weak-star topology. Thus U={M:∣h(M)∣<δ} is open.
By part (a), choose an infinite L0​ homogeneous for U. If all its infinite subsets are in U, the required conclusion follows. Suppose instead every one has ∣h(M)∣≥δ. Choose finitely many unit scalars ζ1​,…,ζq​ such that every scalar z with ∣z∣≥δ has Re(ζj​z)>δ/2 for some j; for real scalars the two signs suffice. The open sets
Uj​={M:Re(ζj​h(M))>δ/2}
(32)
cover [L0​]ω. Apply the open Ramsey set of infinite subsets result successively to them. After finitely many refinements, at least one, say Uj​, contains every infinite subset of a remaining infinite L; otherwise their cover would be violated.
For any finite F⊆L, choose an infinite N⊆L after maxF. For each m∈F, apply this homogeneity to M={m}∪N. Its deleted tail is always the same N, so the same functional satisfies
Re(ζj​xN∗​(xm​))>δ/2(m∈F).
(33)
But (xm​)m∈L​ is a weakly null sequence. The Mazur lemma gives a finite convex combination z=∑m∈F​λm​xm​ with ∥z∥<δ/2. The displayed inequalities give Re(ζj​xN∗​(z))>δ/2, contradicting ∥xN∗​∥≤1. The bad homogeneous case is impossible. Therefore
∣xM′∗​(xm​)∣<δfor every M∈[L]ω,m=minM, M′=M∖{m}.​
(34)
Continuity is used to make the scalar tests open; weak nullity is used only for the finite convex-combination contradiction. The argument works over both real and complex scalars.
