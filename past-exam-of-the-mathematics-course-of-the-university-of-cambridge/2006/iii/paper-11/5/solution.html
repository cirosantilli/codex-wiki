OurBigBook.com Words: 1k
The norm comparison in bounded distortion of a Banach space is understood up to a positive rescaling of the new norm. Thus, after replacing D by D2 if one's convention uses symmetric comparison constants, we may use the following form: for every equivalent norm q on a block subspace, a further block subspace Z and a number a>0 satisfy
a∥z∥≤q(z)≤aD∥z∥(z∈Z).
(20)
The number a may depend on the norm and the subspace; D is fixed. Without this rescaling convention the premise would be impossible, since arbitrarily large scalar multiples of the original norm are equivalent norms. We prove the substantive, scale-invariant assertion for an infinite Schauder basis.
Let K be the basis constant of the original basis. Every block sequence has basis constant at most K, so its initial, tail and finite interval projections have norms at most K, 1+K and 2K, respectively. We shall construct increasingly complicated finite unconditional configurations while keeping their unconditional constant fixed at C=2D.
First construct the Maurey hierarchy of finite sets. Write M0​={∅}, and define
Mα+1​=Mα​∪{{n}∪F:F∈Mα​, n<minF},
(21)
where prepending to the empty set is permitted. For each nonzero countable limit ordinal λ, fix positive ordinals λj​↑λ and put
Mλ​={∅}∪{F∈Mλj​​:j≤minF for some j}.
(22)
Induction shows that every family is hereditary and spreading: subsets remain in the family, and increasing any of the coordinates preserves membership. Every nonzero stage contains all singletons. These families are also well-founded as extension trees. At a successor stage, an infinite branch would yield an infinite branch at the previous stage after deleting its first integer. At a limit stage, a hypothetical branch with first integer m has every initial segment in the finite union ⋃j≤m​Mλj​​. One family then contains arbitrarily long initial segments, and heredity would give it an infinite branch.
For every infinite set L, the extension tree Mα​∩[L]<∞ has empty-root rank of a well-founded tree at least α. Here is the induction proving this lower bound. At a successor stage fix n∈L; the tree above the node {n} contains a copy of Mα​ restricted to the infinite tail of L after n, and hence has rank at least α. The root therefore has rank at least α+1. At a limit stage, for every j the restriction of Mλj​​ to a sufficiently late infinite tail of L is contained in Mλ​. The root rank is consequently at least supj​λj​=λ.
The crucial elementary estimate concerns signed interval projection norms. Given a hereditary family F containing all singletons and a basis (wi​), define on finitely supported vectors
qF(w)​(x)=sup{​∑i=1r​εi​PEi​(w)​x​:E1​<⋯<Er​ finite intervals, (minEi​)i=1r​∈F, εi​∈{−1,1}}.
(23)
Choosing one interval containing the support gives qF(w)​(x)≥∥x∥. Whenever this supremum is bounded by a multiple of ∥x∥, it extends to a norm on the closed span giving equivalent norms with the original norm.
If S=∑i​εi​PEi​​ is one of the admitted signed interval projections, then
qF(w)​(Sx)≤2qF(w)​(x).
(24)
To prove this, test Sx with another admitted operator R=∑j​δj​PFj​​. The composition is the signed sum of projections onto the nonempty intervals Ei​∩Fj​. Colour each intersection according to which of Ei​,Fj​ supplies its left endpoint, breaking ties in favour of Ei​. In the first colour the list of minima is a subset of (minEi​), with no repetitions; in the second it is a subset of (minFj​). Both lists belong to F by heredity. Each colour therefore contributes an admitted signed interval projection, and the triangle inequality gives ∥RSx∥≤2qF(w)​(x). Taking the supremum over R proves the estimate.
Now suppose qF(w)​ is an equivalent norm on a block subspace W. Apply bounded distortion to obtain a further block basis (zi​) spanning Z, with a∥z∥≤qF(w)​(z)≤aD∥z∥. A signed interval projection relative to (zi​), with an admissible list of minima, is the restriction of an admitted signed interval projection relative to (wi​). Indeed, replace each interval of block indices by the interval from the first to the last original coordinate of its blocks; its minimum only increases, so the spreading property preserves admissibility. The elementary estimate and the norm comparison therefore give
∥Sz∥≤a−1qF(w)​(Sz)≤2a−1qF(w)​(z)≤2D∥z∥.
(25)
In particular qF(z)​≤C∥⋅∥, with the same C=2D for every family to which we apply this procedure.
We prove by transfinite induction that, in every block subspace and for every 1≤α<ω1​, there is a normalized block basis (zi​) for which
qMα​(z)​(x)≤C∥x∥.
(26)
For α=1 only one interval is permitted, so the initial signed interval projection norm is bounded by 2K∥x∥. Apply the preceding renorming procedure.
For a successor stage, first choose (wi​) satisfying the induction hypothesis for Mα​. Delete the first interval from any Mα+1​-admissible family. The remaining minima lie in Mα​, so
qMα+1​(w)​(x)≤(2K+C)∥x∥.
(27)
This is an equivalent norm. The renorming procedure produces a further block basis with the constant reset to C.
At a limit stage λ, choose decreasing block subspaces Wj​, each with a block basis satisfying the induction hypothesis for Mλj​​. Choose successive normalized zj​∈Wj​. Make each zj​ sufficiently far out that its first coordinate in the basis of every Wk​, k≤j, is at least j; only finitely many restrictions are imposed at each choice. An admissible interval family for Mλ​ belongs to Mλj​​ for some j≤minE1​. All blocks on which it acts lie in the tail [zi​:i≥j]⊂Wj​. Transferring its intervals to the basis of Wj​ increases their minima, so the induction hypothesis gives
​∑i​εi​PEi​(z)​x​≤C∥P[j,∞)(z)​x∥≤C(1+K)∥x∥.
(28)
Thus qMλ​(z)​ is again an equivalent norm, and one more application of bounded distortion resets its bound to C. This completes the transfinite induction. The tail-projection factor in this step is important; the diagonal tail need not have the same norm as the original vector.
Finally let TC​(X) be the tree of finite normalized sequences (x1​,…,xn​) satisfying
∥∑i=1n​εi​ai​xi​∥≤C∥∑i=1n​ai​xi​∥
(29)
for all scalars and all signs. Each finite level is closed in the corresponding power of the unit sphere, since the norm inequalities are closed conditions. The unit sphere of the separable Banach space X is a Polish space. An infinite branch is an unconditional basic sequence: sign changes give coordinate projections with norm at most (1+C)/2, imply linear independence, and give uniformly bounded initial projections, hence the basis property on the closed span. These projection bounds also give unconditional convergence, including over complex scalars.
For each countable α, the block basis constructed above maps the extension tree Mα​ into TC​(X) by {i1​<⋯<ir​}↦(zi1​​,…,zir​​). To check the sign inequality, use the admissible singleton intervals {i1​},…,{ir​} on the vector ∑ak​zik​​. Thus the unconditional tree index is at least α for every countable ordinal. If TC​(X) were well-founded, the closed-tree result would give it countable height, contradicting these lower bounds. It has an infinite branch. Therefore X contains an unconditional basic sequence.
