Past exam of the mathematics course of the University of Cambridge / 2006 / iii / Paper 11 / 4 / a / Solution

OurBigBook.com Words: 311
Use a compatible complete metric on the Polish space P. For a tree on a Polish space T⊆⋃n​Pn, let
rT​(t)=sup{rT​(t⌢x)+1:t⌢x∈T}
(14)
be its rank of a well-founded tree, with terminal rank zero. We bound this rank using a countable approximation tree, rather than assuming that the original tree has countably many nodes.
Choose a countable base of open balls. A node of the approximation tree S at depth n records, for every coordinate i≤j≤n, a basic ball Uj,i​. Require its diameter to be less than 2−j, require Uj+1,i​​⊆Uj,i​, and require
Tj​∩∏i=1j​Uj,i​=∅(1≤j≤n).
(15)
Initial truncation removes the last row of balls. There are only countably many such finite records, so S is countable.
An infinite branch in S would give, by completeness and shrinking nested balls, a limit pi​∈P for each coordinate. For fixed j, witnesses from later levels of T have their first j coordinates converging to (p1​,…,pj​). Initial-segment closure puts those witnesses in Tj​, and closedness of Tj​ puts the limit there too. Hence (pi​) would be an infinite branch of T. Thus S is well-founded.
Every tuple t=(p1​,…,pn​)∈Tn​ can be fitted inside such a record with each pi​∈Un,i​. Every extension t⌢p can be fitted in a child record: shrink the old balls around their actual points and choose a small ball around the new point. A well-founded induction therefore gives rT​(t)≤rS​(s) whenever the record s fits t. At the empty record this bounds the whole tree's rank by rS​(∅).
The rank of a countable well-founded tree is countable: at each node it is a supremum of countably many successors of countable ordinals, by well-founded recursion. Consequently a closed well-founded tree on a Polish space has countable height. The completeness and closedness assumptions are precisely what turn a branch of approximation records into an actual branch.
