For independent observations in a natural exponential family, the log likelihood is
ℓn​(θ)=n{θTYˉ−κ(θ)}+constant,Un​(θ)=n{Yˉ−∇κ(θ)},jn​(θ)=n∇2κ(θ).
(31)
In a minimal exponential family the Observed Fisher information matrix is positive definite, so the log likelihood is strictly concave. Any finite solution of the likelihood equation ∇κ(θ)=Yˉ is therefore the unique global maximum likelihood estimator. Existence is a separate issue: the observed sufficient-statistic mean must belong to the image of the mean parameter map ∇κ.
For a full regular minimal natural exponential family this image is the interior of the convex support of an exponential family, C=conv(supp(hν))​. The necessity follows from a supporting hyperplane argument. If a finite parameter had its mean on a boundary face, some nonzero v would satisfy vTY≤b almost surely and E(vTY)=b. Equality of the expectation forces vTY=b almost surely. Exponential tilting preserves the base-measure null sets, so this contradicts minimality.
For sufficiency, let Yˉ be an interior point of C. Choose finitely many points y1​,…,ym​ of the support whose convex hull contains a ball about Yˉ, say of radius 2ε. Such points exist because Yˉ is interior to the closed convex hull; one may approximate the vertices of a small simplex surrounding it by finite convex combinations of support points. Take support neighbourhoods of radius at most ε with positive finite base-measure masses aj​. These masses can be taken finite by restricting to bounded neighbourhoods and using finiteness of the normalizing integral at any fixed interior parameter. For every vector θ, at least one j has θT(yj​−Yˉ)≥2ε∥θ∥. Integrating over that neighbourhood yields
κ(θ)−θTYˉ≥ε∥θ∥+minj​logaj​.
(32)
Thus the negative log likelihood grows to infinity as ∥θ∥→∞. At a finite boundary point of the full open natural parameter space, the normalizing integral is infinite, since otherwise that point would itself belong to the full domain. Fatou's lemma makes κ(θ) diverge on approach to such a boundary. A minimizing sequence for κ(θ)−θTYˉ therefore stays in a bounded subset away from the boundary; it has an interior limit where the minimum is attained. Its gradient vanishes, giving the required likelihood equation. Strict convexity gives uniqueness.
Hence, under these full, regular and minimal conventions,
a finite unique MLE exists ⟺ Yˉ∈intC.​
(33)
This is maximum likelihood existence in a full regular natural exponential family. Regularity alone does not make every sample admissible: in a Bernoulli distribution an all-zero or all-one sample has its sample mean on the boundary of C=[0,1], and the canonical maximum is approached only as θ→−∞ or +∞. The usual probability parameter then has a boundary maximum in an enlarged model, but no finite canonical maximizer. A regular restriction of the natural parameter space requires membership in its actual mean-map image, rather than merely in intC. Without minimality, affine redundancies may prevent uniqueness of the canonical parameter even when the fitted probability distribution is unique.
