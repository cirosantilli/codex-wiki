A general exponential family of order p has the representation
f(x;α)=h(x)exp{η(α)TT(x)−κ(η(α))},T(x)=(T1​(x),…,Tp​(x))T.
(28)
The canonical sufficient statistics Tj​ are independent of the parameter, the support is fixed, and the cumulant function normalizes the probability density function. Minimality rules out a redundant affine relation among the sufficient statistics. If α ranges over an open subset of Rq, q<p, and η is a smooth embedding with derivative of rank q, its image is a q-dimensional parameter surface in the p-dimensional natural parameter space: this is a (p,q) curved exponential family. In a genuinely curved example the surface is nonaffine.
Take the normal distribution N(μ,μ2) with μ>0. Its log density expands as
logf(y;μ)=−21​log(2πμ2)−21​+μy​−2μ2y2​.
(29)
It is a (2,1) curved exponential family with sufficient statistics (y,y2) and natural parameters η1​=1/μ, η2​=−1/(2μ2). The ambient full normal distribution family has η1​∈R, η2​<0 and
κ(η)=−4η2​η12​​+21​log−η2​π​.
(30)
The curve is η2​=−η12​/2 with η1​>0, and its derivative with respect to μ has rank one. Thus the mean and variance are constrained by Var(Y)=(EY)2; the two canonical parameters cannot vary independently. Concavity of an ambient canonical log likelihood need not persist along a curved parameter surface.
