The printed local assumptions alone do not guarantee localization. A useful localization condition for Laplace's method is that g(y)≥g(y​)+δε​ outside each sufficiently small fixed neighbourhood of y​, for some δε​>0, and that ∫ab​e−n∗​g(y)dy<∞ for some n∗​>0. The Laplace formula therefore has its usual implicit tail assumption, rather than following from uniqueness of the interior minimum alone.

The first term accounts for the curvature of the amplitude and the second for posterior skewness. This is posterior expectation by Laplace approximation. If there are several modes, boundary modes, or nonconcentrating tails, the appropriate separate contributions must be included; a single interior Gaussian approximation cannot then be assumed.
