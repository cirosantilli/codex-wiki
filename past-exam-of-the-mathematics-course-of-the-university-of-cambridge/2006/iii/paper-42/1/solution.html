The empirical distribution function gives each observation mass 1/n:
Fn​(x)=n1​∑i=1n​1{Xi​≤x}.
(1)
The Glivenko-Cantelli theorem asserts the uniform, almost sure convergence
x∈Rsup​∣Fn​(x)−F(x)∣⟶0almost surely.​
(2)
No continuity of the distribution function is required. Here is a proof that also handles its jumps. Let Ui​ be independent uniform random variables on (0,1), and write Xi​=F−1(Ui​), where F−1(u)=inf{x:F(x)≥u}. The generalized inverse property gives 1{Xi​≤x}=1{Ui​≤F(x)}, so, if Gn​ is the empirical distribution function of the Ui​, then Fn​(x)=Gn​(F(x)).
For a fixed positive integer m, the strong law of large numbers applied at each of the finitely many grid points j/m shows that Δn,m​=max0≤j≤m​∣Gn​(j/m)−j/m∣→0 almost surely. If j/m≤t≤(j+1)/m, monotonicity sandwiches Gn​(t) between its endpoint values, whence
sup0≤t≤1​∣Gn​(t)−t∣≤Δn,m​+m1​.
(3)
Take the probability-one intersection of these events over all m. On that event the limit superior is at most 1/m for every m, hence is zero. Therefore supx​∣Gn​(F(x))−F(x)∣→0. The coupled sequence has the same joint law as any independent sample from F, which proves the asserted almost sure conclusion for the original sample.
For 0<p<1, the sample quantile is
Fn−1​(p)=inf{x:Fn​(x)≥p}=X(⌈np⌉)​,
(4)
where X(j)​ denotes the jth order statistic, with repeated observations retained. Suppose the population median m=F−1(1/2) is unique and F is continuously differentiable in a neighbourhood of m, with probability density function f(m)>0. Then
n​(Fn−1​(1/2)−m) d​ N(0,4f(m)21​).​
(5)
To see the variance directly, fix t and count observations below m+t/n​. This count has a binomial distribution with success probability qn​=1/2+f(m)t/n​+o(n−1/2). The event that the sample median is below this point is that the count is at least ⌈n/2⌉. The central limit theorem for this binomial triangular array gives a limiting probability Φ(2f(m)t), exactly the displayed normal distribution. Centering is necessary for stating the limiting distribution; subtracting the deterministic population median has no effect on the asymptotic variance requested in the comparisons.
