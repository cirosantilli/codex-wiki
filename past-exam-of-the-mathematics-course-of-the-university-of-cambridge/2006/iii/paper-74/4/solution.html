Write cT2​=RT0​ for the squared isothermal sound speed, distinguishing the gas constant R from a Rayleigh number, and put H=cT2​/g. A uniform magnetic field has no bulk Lorentz force, so hydrostatic equilibrium and the ideal gas equation give
dzdpi​​=−ρi​g,pi​=cT2​ρi​,pi​(z)=pi​(0)e−z/H.
(44)
At the interface, it is magnetohydrodynamic total pressure, not gas pressure alone, that is continuous:
p1​(0)+2μ0​B12​​=p2​(0)+2μ0​B22​​.
(45)
Define ΔB​=B12​−B22​>0. The magnetic pressure discontinuity in an isothermal atmosphere therefore produces
ρ1​(z)ρ2​(z)​=ρe−z/H,=(ρ+2μ0​cT2​ΔB​​)e−z/H,​p1​(z)p2​(z)​=ρcT2​e−z/H(z<0),=(ρcT2​+2μ0​ΔB​​)e−z/H(z>0).​​
(46)
In particular, the upper gas is denser at the interface. Both halves have the same scale height H, despite their different pressure normalizations.
For the disturbance calculation, write ρ−​=ρ, ρ+​=ρ+δρ, where δρ=ΔB​/(2μ0​cT2​), and Σρ​=ρ−​+ρ+​. Work to leading order in the prescribed short-vertical-scale approximation: the bulk motion is locally incompressible potential flow, while the isothermal equation determines the equilibrium density jump. Its scale separation and small compressive correction will be checked at the maximizing mode.
Let the interface displacement be hest+ikx+imy and K=k2+m2​>0. Using displacement rather than velocity potentials, write ξi​=∇χi​ and ui​=sξi​. Incompressibility and irrotational flow give Δχi​=0. Decay away from the interface and continuity of its normal displacement fix
χ1​=Kh​eKz,χ2​=−Kh​e−Kz,
(47)
with the common horizontal and temporal exponential understood. Both satisfy ∂z​χi​(0)=h. Thus the disturbance has vertical extent of order K−1.
In ideal magnetohydrodynamics, magnetic flux freezing gives bi​=(Bi​⋅∇)ξi​ for these locally incompressible displacements. If πi​=pi′​+Bi​⋅bi​/μ0​ is the total-pressure perturbation, linear momentum is
ρi​s2ξi​=−∇πi​+μ0​1​(Bi​⋅∇)bi​.
(48)
Since (Bi​⋅∇)2=−(kh​⋅Bi​)2 on the mode, integration in each half-space yields
πi​=−[ρi​s2+μ0​(kh​⋅Bi​)2​]χi​,kh​=(k,m,0).
(49)
At the displaced interface, continuity of Lagrangian magnetohydrodynamic total pressure requires π1​−ρ−​gh=π2​−ρ+​gh. Inserting the two potentials gives the orthogonal-field magnetic Rayleigh-Taylor dispersion relation:
s2=Σρ​gδρk2+m2​−(B22​k2+B12​m2)/μ0​​.​
(50)
The first term is the destabilizing Rayleigh-Taylor instability of the heavier upper gas; the second is stabilizing magnetic tension. A negative right-hand side gives an oscillatory mode rather than growth.
For fixed K, the magnetic penalty is
B22​k2+B12​m2=B22​K2+ΔB​m2.
(51)
It is minimized by m=0: the wavevector is along the weaker upper magnetic field and perpendicular to the stronger lower field. If B2​=0, define A=gδρ/Σρ​ and C=B22​/(μ0​Σρ​). The growth law for this orientation is s2=AK−CK2, a concave quadratic with maximum at K∗​=A/(2C). Hence
m∗​=0,∣k∗​∣=K∗​=4B22​HΔB​​,smax​=2∣B2​∣Σρ​​gδρμ0​​​=4cT2​∣B2​∣μ0​Σρ​​gΔB​​.​
(52)
For this orientation the unstable interval is 0<K<2K∗​; the maximum lies halfway along it.
The localization condition for the fastest orthogonal-field interface mode follows directly from the vertical decay length:
ℓ∗​=K∗​1​=ΔB​4B22​​H,4B22​≪B12​−B22​.​
(53)
This is the condition for ℓ∗​≪H on both sides. It also controls the locally incompressible approximation, since
(cT​K∗​smax​​)2=μ0​Σρ​cT2​B22​​=2μ0​ρcT2​+ΔB​/2B22​​≤ΔB​2B22​​≪1.
(54)
Acoustic adjustment is then fast compared with growth, so bulk density perturbations are higher-order corrections. If this localization condition fails, the constant-background approximation cannot be used to identify the physical fastest mode.
Finally, the printed assumptions allow B2​=0. In that case m=0 eliminates magnetic tension on both sides and gives s2=gδρ∣k∣/Σρ​. There is no finite maximum growth rate or maximizing wavenumber in the ideal sharp-interface model: growth is unbounded as ∣k∣→∞. The finite maximum and localization formula above therefore require 0<∣B2​∣<∣B1​∣.
In the growth curves, θ is the angle of the horizontal wavevector from the x-axis. The illustrated ratio B12​/B22​=101 gives K∗​H=25, and shows how even a small rotation toward the stronger magnetic field lowers the maximum growth.
