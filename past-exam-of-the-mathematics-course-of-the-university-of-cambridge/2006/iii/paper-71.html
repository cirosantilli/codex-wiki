Consider a small outward displacement δr of a fluid element. It adjusts to the ambient pressure while retaining its specific entropy and stellar composition, so an adiabatic process gives dlogρparcel​=dlogP/γ. Its excess mass density over the new surroundings is, to first order,
ρparcel​−ρambient​=[γPρ​drdP​−drdρ​]δr.
(12)
Restoring buoyancy requires this bracket to be positive. With γ=5/3, the Schwarzschild criterion is therefore
drdP​>3ρ5P​drdρ​.​
(13)
Equality is neutral stability. For uniform mean molecular weight, the ideal gas law gives dlogρ=dlogP−dlogT. Since hydrostatic equilibrium pressure decreases outward, the inequality is equivalent to ∇=dlogT/dlogP<∇ad​=2/5. Dividing the radiative diffusion in a star and hydrostatic equilibrium stellar structure equations gives
∇rad​=16πacGmT43κLr​P​,16πacGmT43κLr​P​<52​.​
(14)
This is the stellar radiative temperature gradient test for stellar convective stability.
In the thin upper stellar atmosphere take m≃M, Lr​≃L and use gas pressure dominance. The prescribed opacity becomes κ=κ0​μPT12/R. Dividing the two structure equations then yields
PdTdP​=3κ0​Lμ16πacGMR​T−9.

At zero optical depth, the atmospheric boundary has Ts4​=Te4​/2 and negligible gas pressure. Integrating from that boundary, with Ts−8​=4Te−8​, gives

To locate the onset, put y=Te8​/T8. Logarithmic derivative gives dlogP/dlogT=4y/(4−y), hence
∇rad​=4y4−y​=Te8​T8​−41​.
(17)
This starts at zero at the outer boundary and increases inward. It first reaches 2/5 at
Tb​=(13/20)1/8Te​.​
(18)
The corresponding optical depth is τb​=34​(13/20​−21​)>0, so the onset lies inside the stellar atmosphere. This is grey-atmosphere convection onset with thirteenth-power opacity.
Let the interior of the fully convective star have P=KT​T5/2, with KT​ spatially constant. At its boundary Tb​/Te​ is fixed, so the atmospheric pressure formula gives
KT2​=Tb5​Pb2​​∝LTe13​M​.

The Stefan–Boltzmann law, L=4πR2σTe4​ with σ=ac/4, consequently implies
KT2​∝MR13/2L−17/4.

The ideal gas equation transforms the interior relation into
P=Kρ​ρ5/3,Kρ​=(R/μ)5/3KT−2/3​.

For completeness, the mass-radius scaling follows by taking ρ=ρc​θ3/2 and r=αξ with α2=5Kρ​ρc−1/3​/(8πG). Hydrostatic equilibrium and mass conservation reduce to the Lane-Emden equation (ξ2θ′)′=−ξ2θ3/2, with θ(0)=1, θ′(0)=0. Its fixed dimensionless profile gives R∝α and M∝α3ρc​. Eliminating ρc​ gives R∝Kρ​G−1M−1/3, or Kρ​∝GM1/3R. Thus KT2​∝M−1R−3 at fixed stellar composition. Equating this with the atmospheric expression gives L17/4∝M2R19/2, and hence

(22)
This Hayashi relation with thirteenth-power opacity concerns the interior of a fully convective star beneath a thin stellar atmosphere. The coefficient KT​ may vary between stars; spatially constant specific entropy does not mean equal specific entropy across the whole sequence.

Outside the emitting layer, take m≃Mc​, Lr​≃L and P=RρT/μ. The Kramers' opacity law then reads κ=(κ0​μ/R)PT−9/2. Division of the radiative diffusion in a star equation by the stellar hydrostatic equation gives

For consistency, Mc​/Rc​ must provide a hot hydrogen burning shell while the stellar core remains inert to core helium burning; the radiative envelope mass must be much less than Mc​, and Rc​ must be well inside an extended radiative envelope. The assumed stellar core sequence must genuinely have nearly fixed Rc​ over the mass interval considered. A cold helium stellar core supported by a nonrelativistic degenerate electron gas instead has the mass-radius law from part (a), so substituting that law would describe a different model and change the luminosity exponent.

Let M=M1​+M2​. The centre of mass condition gives a1​=aM2​/M, a2​=aM1​/M. For circular motion the component angular momenta are Ji​=Mi​ai2​Ω, so
J=(M1​a12​+M2​a22​)Ω=MM1​M2​​a2Ω.
(38)
Using Kepler's third law, a3Ω2=GM, and Ω=2π/Porb​ gives
J=MM1​M2​​a2Ω=(2π)1/3M1/3G2/3Porb1/3​M1​M2​​.​
(39)
This is the circular-binary orbital angular momentum. Mass received by the companion redistributes angular momentum within the binary star; only the escaping stellar wind removes it under the prescribed model.
The stellar wind from the donor star has specific angular momentum
jw​=M1​J1​​=a12​Ω,Jjw​​=M1​MM2​​.
(40)
Here M˙1​<0, M˙2​=−fM˙1​ and M˙=(1−f)M˙1​. Thus donor-wind angular-momentum loss gives
JJ˙​=(1−f)M˙1​M1​MM2​​.
(41)
Taking the logarithmic derivative of the period expression for J gives
Porb​P˙orb​​=3JJ˙​−3M1​M˙1​​−3M2​M˙2​​+MM˙​.
(42)
Use M2​/(M1​M)=1/M1​−1/M to simplify this to
Porb​P˙orb​​=−3fM1​M˙1​​−3M2​M˙2​​−2MM˙​.
(43)
For constant f, integration therefore gives
Porb​∝M1−3f​M2−3​(M1​+M2​)−2.​
(44)
This is the donor-wind period invariant with fixed retention fraction. If f changes during the evolution, the differential equation remains valid but the integrated invariant is instead Porb​M23​M2exp(3∫fdlogM1​)=constant. The fixed-power expression cannot be used with a time-dependent exponent without this modification.
For the Roche lobe, write q=M1​/M2​. The equivalent separation expression J=M1​M2​Ga/M​ gives
aa˙​=2JJ˙​−2M1​M˙1​​−2M2​M˙2​​+MM˙​=M1​M˙1​​[2f(q−1)−(1−f)1+qq​].
(45)
Taking the logarithmic derivative of RL​=0.46a(M1​/M)1/3 then adds M˙1​/(3M1​)−M˙/(3M), yielding
RL​R˙L​​=M1​M˙1​​[f(2q+3(1+q)4q​−2)+31​−3(1+q)4q​].​
(46)
This donor-wind Roche-lobe response uses only the instantaneous value of f and thus still holds if it varies.
Define A(q)=2q+4q/[3(1+q)]−2 and B(q)=1/3−4q/[3(1+q)]. The Roche-lobe radius response exponent is ζL​=B+Af, whereas the donor star's stellar radius response exponent is ζ∗​=−n. Keeping R1​=RL​ requires the two logarithmic derivatives of radius to agree, so
f(2q+3(1+q)4q​−2)=3(1+q)4q​−n−31​.​
(47)
For A=0, this determines
freq​=2q+4q/[3(1+q)]−24q/[3(1+q)]−n−1/3​.​
(48)
A physical mixture of Roche-lobe overflow and stellar wind from the donor star needs 0<f<1. Equivalently, −n must lie strictly between the pure-wind response B and the conservative response B+A=2q−5/3. The values f=0 and 1 are valid limiting prescriptions, respectively pure stellar wind and conservative mass transfer, rather than mixtures. If freq​ lies outside [0,1], no allowed division of the lost mass can maintain contact under this radius law and angular-momentum-loss prescription. This is feasibility of donor-wind binary contact.
The likely direction away from contact follows without assigning a nonphysical fraction. For any actual f, let Δ=log(R1​/RL​). Then
Δ˙=(−n−B−Af)M1​M˙1​​=A(freq​−f)M1​M˙1​​.
(49)
Since M˙1​<0, a positive value of A(freq​−f) makes the donor star move inside its lobe and detach; a negative value increases overfill and promotes stronger transfer. Thus if A>0, freq​<0 gives increasing overfill and freq​>1 gives detachment; if A<0, these outcomes are reversed. A runaway or a new equilibrium would require the donor star's response or the angular-momentum-loss model to change, possibly with additional driving. The contact calculation alone does not prove a particular nonlinear endpoint.
There is also a genuine exceptional denominator: A=0 at q=(10​−1)/3. At this ratio both limiting lobe responses coincide. Contact is possible only if −n=B, namely n=(10​−2)/(10​+2); then every f gives the same instantaneous radius response. Otherwise no fraction works, and the sign of (−n−B)M˙1​/M1​ determines detachment or increasing overfill.
