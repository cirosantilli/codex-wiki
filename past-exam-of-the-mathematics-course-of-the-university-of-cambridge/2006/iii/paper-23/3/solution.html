Past exam of the mathematics course of the University of Cambridge / 2006 / iii / Paper 23 / 3 / Solution

OurBigBook.com Words: 712
Use the canonical nonnegative Laplace-Beltrami operator and its Riemannian heat kernel for t>0. For the spectral conclusion we use closed compact manifolds, so the operator has a discrete spectrum and a finite heat trace. The kernel identity itself can also be formulated for the canonical Friedrichs extension on a noncompact manifold; this specifies the heat operator rather than assuming uniqueness among unrestricted solutions of the heat equation.
An isometry h preserves the Laplacian, its defining quadratic form and therefore its heat semigroup. Consequently
KN​(hz,hw,t)=KN​(z,w,t).
(13)
Set H(x,y,t)=∑g∈U​KN​(x,gy​,t). A different choice of lifts has the form hx,ky​ with h,k∈U, because the covering is normal. The resulting sum is
∑g∈U​KN​(hx,gky​,t)=∑g∈U​KN​(x,h−1gky​,t)=H(x,y,t),
(14)
since g↦h−1gk permutes U. This proves independence of both lifts. Local smooth sections of the covering map then show that H is smooth for positive time. There are finitely many summands, so spatial and time differentiation commute with the sum, and local isometry gives (∂t​+Δx​)H=0.
For the integral and initial condition, use the sheet-counting formula
∫N​ψ(z)dVN​(z)=∫M​∑p(z)=y​ψ(z)dVM​(y).
(15)
It follows by dividing the base into measurable pieces lying in evenly covered charts and using the locally isometric inverse branches. For a smooth test function ϕ on the base, this formula gives
∫M​H(x,y,t)ϕ(y)dVM​(y)=∫N​KN​(x,z,t)ϕ(pz)dVN​(z).
(16)
On a closed manifold these integrals are finite, and the finite sheet sum entails no convergence exchange. The right side tends to ϕ(px)=ϕ(x) as t↓0 by the initial property of the Riemannian heat kernel. Thus H has the correct delta initial condition.
One can identify the operator exactly, also avoiding a noncompact uniqueness issue, by heat semigroup descent through a finite normal covering. If d=∣U∣, the map Iϕ=d−1/2ϕ∘p is unitary onto the U-invariant subspace of L2(N), because
∥Iϕ∥L2(N)2​=∥ϕ∥L2(M)2​,∫N​∣∇Iϕ∣2dVN​=∫M​∣∇ϕ∣2dVM​.
(17)
Locally isometric pullback proves the second equality. Smooth compactly supported invariant functions descend, and averaging over the finite group gives the corresponding equality of the closed quadratic-form domains. Hence the Friedrichs extensions and their heat semigroups intertwine. The preceding integral is precisely that descended semigroup, proving
KM​(x,y,t)=g∈U∑​KN​(x,gy​,t).​
(18)
For the noncompact version, finite covering maps are proper, so compactly supported test functions have compactly supported lifts; the same form argument is valid. The canonical heat kernels are sub-Markovian, which bounds integrals against bounded test functions. All operations used above are therefore defined. In particular there is no factor 1/∣U∣ in the kernel formula: integrating over the sheets already accounts for its normalization.
Now take a finite ambient group T of isometries of a closed N, and free Gassmann equivalent subgroups U1​,U2​. Define the orbital integral
Φt​(g)=∫N​KN​(z,gz,t)dVN​(z).
(19)
It is finite, and the conjugacy-invariance identity makes it a class function on T. The heat kernel on a finite isometric quotient and the sheet-counting formula give the quotient heat traces
ZMi​​(t)=∣Ui​∣1​∑g∈Ui​​Φt​(g).
(20)
The averaging factor belongs here, since the cover has ∣Ui​∣ sheets. By Gassmann equivalence, the two subgroups meet each conjugacy class in the same number of elements, and summing these numbers also gives ∣U1​∣=∣U2​∣. Since Φt​ is constant on each class, the two heat traces are equal for every t>0.
Finally, a discrete nonnegative eigenvalue multiset is determined by its heat trace. Its zero multiplicity is limt→∞​Z(t). After subtracting the zero terms, the smallest remaining eigenvalue and its multiplicity are recovered from the leading exponential decay; subtract those terms and repeat. Compactness ensures only finitely many eigenvalues in any bounded interval, so this procedure recovers the entire multiset. We have proved the Sunada theorem:
Spec(M1​)=Spec(M2​) with multiplicities.​
(21)
The closedness assumption is used for this heat-trace deduction; on a general noncompact manifold one cannot replace it by a possibly infinite trace without further analysis.
If the initial manifold is not compact, the same isospectral conclusion can be interpreted for the canonical self-adjoint operators without taking an infinite trace. To see this, decompose the unitary representation of the finite group T as L2(N)=⨁ρ​Vρ​⊗Hρ​, with Vρ​ irreducible and Hρ​ its multiplicity space. Commutation with the group makes the Laplacian act on each summand as I⊗Dρ​, by the Schur lemma applied to its bounded resolvents. The projection onto the Ui​-invariant factor is ∣Ui​∣−1∑u∈Ui​​ρ(u), and its trace is therefore dimVρUi​​=∣Ui​∣−1∑u∈Ui​​χρ​(u). Gassmann equivalence makes these dimensions equal. Unitary identifications VρU1​​→VρU2​​, tensored with the identity on each multiplicity space, intertwine the restricted Laplacians. The normalized pullbacks identify those restrictions with the two quotient operators. This Sunada unitary equivalence without a finite heat trace proves equality of their full spectra, including continuous spectrum when present.
