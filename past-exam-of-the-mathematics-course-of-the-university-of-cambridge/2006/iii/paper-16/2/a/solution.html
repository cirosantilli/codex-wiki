Work first over F2​. The mod-two cohomology ring of real projective space is H∗(RPn;F2​)=F2​[a]/(an+1), with ∣a∣=1. A map missing a point has zero mod-two degree of a map between closed manifolds, so f∗(an)=0. The group in degree one has only two elements, so f∗a is either zero or a. The latter would imply f∗(an)=(f∗a)n=an=0, a contradiction. Therefore f∗a=0, and multiplicativity makes f∗ zero in every positive cohomological degree. Duality of homology and cohomology over a field consequently makes f∗​ zero in every positive degree with mod-two coefficients.
For integral coefficients, cellular homology of real projective space gives Hk​≅Z/2 in odd degrees 0<k<n, zero in even degrees 0<k<n, and a top group Z if n is odd and zero if n is even. For each torsion group, the natural reduction map to mod-two homology is injective by the universal coefficient theorem for homology; naturality and the preceding vanishing imply that its integral induced map is zero. When n is odd, the integral top-degree map is also zero because the nonsurjective map has integer degree of a map between oriented manifolds zero. All groups above dimension n vanish. Hence f∗​ is zero on all positive integral homology groups as well.
For n≥2, π1​(RPn)=Z/2, and its abelianization is already H1​(RPn;Z). The vanishing just proved therefore implies f∗​π1​=0. By the lifting criterion for a covering space, f lifts through the double covering space q:Sn→RPn to a map f​:RPn→Sn with qf​=f.
Choose a point y missed by f. Both points of q−1(y) are missed by f​. In particular the lift has image in Sn∖{z}≅Rn, for either such point z. This punctured sphere is contractible, so contraction there gives a homotopy from f​ to a constant map into Sn. Composing with q contracts f. Thus
f≃constant.​

This proves the nonsurjective self-map of real projective space is null-homotopic conclusion, and also shows that all positive-degree induced maps vanish with any coefficients.
