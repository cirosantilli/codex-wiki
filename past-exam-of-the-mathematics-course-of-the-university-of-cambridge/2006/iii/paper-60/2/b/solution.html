The stated relation places every generator, and therefore the whole Dynamical Lie algebra, inside the standard compact symplectic Lie algebra sp(ℓ)⊂su(2ℓ). Indeed the relation is closed under linear combinations and commutators, and differentiating evolution gives
dtd​(UTJU)=0,UTJU=J.
(6)
Thus it supplies a symplectic dynamical symmetry in quantum control, rather than a claim that J commutes with every Quantum Hamiltonian.
For ℓ≥2, b1 is false: even the full compact symplectic group does not act transitively on every mixed-state spectral orbit. Its algebra has dimension ℓ(2ℓ+1)<(2ℓ)2−1. An explicit obstruction is the symplectic invariant of a density operator
IJ​(ρ)=Tr(ρJρTJ†),
(7)
which is unchanged by a symplectic conjugation. For N=4 and the printed J, let a,b>0, a=b, 2a+2b=1. The isospectral operators diag(a,a,b,b) and diag(a,b,a,b) have invariants 4ab and 2(a2+b2), respectively, and cannot be interconverted. For the exceptional case ℓ=1, sp(1)=su(2), so b1 cannot be decided from inclusion alone; equality gives density-state controllability.
b2 is not implied by the displayed relation: more information is needed. It permits the full symplectic algebra but also a single commuting generator, or even zero generators. The full Sp(ℓ) is pure-state controllable: identify C2ℓ with Hℓ, extend any unit vector to a quaternionic orthonormal basis, and map one such basis to another. A one-axis diagonal subgroup, in contrast, cannot change level populations.
The required extra information is the generated algebra, obtained by computing its real span of iterated commutators. Within this specified symplectic inclusion, equality g=sp(ℓ) gives pure-state controllability; its dimension is ℓ(2ℓ+1). The mere inclusion does not establish equality. For N=2, equality also answers b1 affirmatively. None of these strictly symplectic generators supplies arbitrary global phase.
