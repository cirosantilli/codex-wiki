Use geometric quantum state synthesis by adjacent rotations, rather than three full level population swaps. Each resonant pulse implements a complex Givens rotation on one allowed two-state plane. Start with the target amplitude vector, use such rotations to eliminate entries back to the first basis vector, and reverse that sequence. Its angles are fixed by the ratios of target amplitudes, and optical phases supply their relative complex phases.

The pulse areas equal these rotation parameters in the direct Hamiltonian-coefficient convention of quantum pulse area. A given sign or phase choice for a physical transition depends on its dipole and basis conventions; calibration realizes the specified rotation without assuming every adjacent transition has the same energy ordering.
If the requested amplitudes refer to the laboratory frame at tF​, the target for this construction is U0​(tF​,0)†∣Ψ⟩, not simply ∣Ψ⟩. Set the rotation phases accordingly, or add the equivalent accessible diagonal phase corrections. This compensates the known drift phases and gives the stated laboratory superposition, not merely the correct level populations. Strong regularity and connected allowed transitions guarantee the required controllability; bandwidth restrictions set how slowly the selective approximation must be implemented.
