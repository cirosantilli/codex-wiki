With negligible cold-dark-matter velocity, the synchronous perfect-fluid density equation gives δC′​=−h′/2. Differentiate and use the trace Einstein equation, taking the cold matter sound speed to vanish and its density to dominate:
δC′′​+HδC′​=4πGa2ρC​δC​.
(40)
In a spatially flat matter-dominated universe, a∝τ2, H=2/τ, and the conformal Friedmann equation gives 4πGa2ρC​=3H2/2=6/τ2. Hence
δC′′​+τ2​δC′​−τ26​δC​=0.
(41)
Put δC​∝τs. The indicial equation is s(s−1)+2s−6=(s−2)(s+3)=0. These two independent modes span the solution space, so
δC​(τ,x)=A(x)τ2+B(x)τ−3.​
(42)
Equivalently the growing mode is proportional to a and the decaying mode to a−3/2. The coefficients are set by initial data. Since synchronous gauge has residual coordinate modes, interpreting the density contrast physically requires fixing its residual freedom consistently, for example by the cold-matter rest frame and metric tensor integration convention; the mode equation alone is not a gauge-invariant observable.
