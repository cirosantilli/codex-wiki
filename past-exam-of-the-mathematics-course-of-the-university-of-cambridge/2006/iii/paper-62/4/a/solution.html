At linear order a coordinate displacement ξμ changes a metric perturbation by qμν​↦qμν​−Lξ​gˉ​μν​. Work in conformal time with gˉ​μν​=a2diag(−1,1,1,1), write ξ0=α and lower spatial indices with δij​. Directly evaluating the Lie derivative of a covariant tensor field gives
q00′​=q00​+2a2(α′+Hα),q0i′​=q0i​−a2(ξi′​−∂i​α),H=aa′​.
(36)
Here primes on α,ξi​ mean conformal-time derivatives, while primes on q0μ′​ label transformed components. Setting the transformed lapse and shift perturbations to zero requires
α′+Hα=−2a2q00​​,ξi′​=a2q0i​​+∂i​α.
(37)
These are first-order time equations. Their explicit local solutions are
α(τ,x)=a(τ)1​[C(x)−∫τ2a(s)q00​(s,x)​ds],ξi​(τ,x)=Di​(x)+∫τ[a(s)2q0i​(s,x)​+∂i​α(s,x)]ds.
(38)
Thus four coordinate functions can impose the four synchronous conditions in any regular perturbative patch. This is synchronous gauge fixing by coordinate displacement. The arbitrary spatial functions C,Di​ remain as residual gauge freedom; the conditions alone do not fix the coordinate system completely.
Geometrically, synchronous coordinates follow a congruence of freely falling observers launched normally from a spatial slice, using their proper time. This gives unit lapse and zero shift in cosmic time; conversion to background conformal time gives the displayed convention. “Always possible” is local: geodesic caustics or singularities can prevent a global synchronous chart. Such global obstructions do not invalidate local linear cosmological perturbation theory.
