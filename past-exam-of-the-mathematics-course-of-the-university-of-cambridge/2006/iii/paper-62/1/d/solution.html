To express it in today's convention a0​=1, multiply L∗​ by 1+z∗​. This is the accumulated free-streaming distance in a radiation-dominated universe.

The printed CMB temperature contains a factor-of-ten error. Its 2.35meV means 2.35×10−3eV, whereas the cosmic microwave background temperature in energy units is about 2.35×10−4eV=0.235meV, corresponding to 2.725K. The temperature is supported by NASA's CMB measurements. Using the printed value gives yeq​≃0.5106/ωM​; using the corrected value gives yeq​≃5.106/ωM​. For the illustrative choice ΩM​=1,h=0.5, so ωM​=0.25, these give respectively Teq​≃14.7 and 1.47eV, and distance-to-Hubble-radius ratios about 0.716 and 0.182. This benchmark is an assumption for the estimate, not an extra parameter supplied by the paper.
