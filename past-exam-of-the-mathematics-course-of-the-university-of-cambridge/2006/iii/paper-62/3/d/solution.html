 ... Past exam of the mathematics course of the University of Cambridge 2006 iii Paper 62 3 d
OurBigBook.com Words: 149
Use the flat matter-only Einstein-de Sitter approximation for the distance-redshift relation, neglecting radiation corrections at cosmological recombination. Let z∗​ be the cosmological recombination redshift. Adiabatic photon cooling gives 1+z∗​=T∗​/T0​. The Hubble rate is H(z)=H0​(1+z)3/2, so the comoving radial distance to last scattering is
χ∗​=∫0z∗​​H(z)dz​=H0​2​(1−1+z∗​​1​).
(33)
The angular diameter distance is DA​=χ∗​/(1+z∗​). A proper Hubble radius H∗−1​=H0−1​(1+z∗​)−3/2 therefore subtends the small angle
θH​=DA​H∗−1​​=2(1+z∗​​−1)1​radians.​
(34)
This is the angular Hubble-radius scale in an Einstein-de Sitter universe; the present Hubble constant cancels.
Taking T∗​=0.3eV and the literal printed T0​=2.35meV gives 1+z∗​=127.7 and θH​≃0.0486 radians, or 2.8 degrees. As noted above, that present CMB energy is too large by ten. With the corrected T0​=0.235meV, 1+z∗​=1277 and
θH​≃0.0144 rad≃0.83∘,​
(35)
which gives the familiar order-one-degree scale within the assumed cosmology. Using 0.28eV to mark a few-per-cent ionization fraction changes this only modestly. These numbers refer to the Hubble-radius scale, not its diameter. In a matter-only universe the particle-horizon radius is 2H−1 and would give twice the angle; the acoustic sound horizon is a different length again.
