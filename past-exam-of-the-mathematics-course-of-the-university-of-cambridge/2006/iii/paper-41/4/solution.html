For independent binomial distributions, the log-likelihood, up to known binomial coefficients, is
ℓ(p)=∑i=1m​{yi​logpi​+(ni​−yi​)log(1−pi​)}.
(17)
The saturated statistical model maximizes each term at p​i​=yi​/ni​. Under the logit link, the fitted probabilities are p​i​=(1+e−xiT​β​)−1. Subtracting the fitted log-likelihood from the saturated log-likelihood gives the binomial deviance
D1​=2i=1∑m​[yi​logni​p​i​yi​​+(ni​−yi​)logni​(1−p​i​)ni​−yi​​].​
(18)
A zero-count summand uses the continuous convention 0log0=0. For the restricted model, replace p​i​ by p​i(0)​=(1+e−xiT​β​​)−1 to obtain D0​. Since the saturated likelihood cancels, D0​−D1​=2(ℓ1​−ℓ0​) is the likelihood-ratio test statistic. For the nested restriction setting p−k coefficients to zero, Wilks theorem gives the asymptotic chi-squared distribution χp−k2​ under regularity and an interior true parameter; reject for a large difference.
For the six anther cells, ω0​ gives a common success probability, ω1​ gives a separate probability for each storage condition, ω2​ gives a common intercept and a log-force slope, and ω3​ gives storage-specific intercepts with a common log-force slope. Their parameter counts are 1,2,2,3, so their residual statistical degrees of freedom are 5,4,4,3. In ω3​, storage has a constant effect on log odds; multiplying force by r multiplies the odds by rβ in either storage group.
The relevant nested likelihood-ratio tests at the 5% level are:
  • Adding storage to ω0​ gives D0​−D1​=5.279>3.84 on one statistical degree of freedom, with p≃0.0216.
  • Adding log-force to ω0​ gives D0​−D2​=2.360<3.84 on one statistical degree of freedom, with p≃0.1245.
  • Adding log-force to the storage model gives D1​−D3​=2.554<3.84 on one statistical degree of freedom, with p≃0.1100.
  • Adding storage to the log-force model gives D2​−D3​=5.473>3.84 on one statistical degree of freedom, with p≃0.0193.
  • Adding both terms to ω0​ gives D0​−D3​=7.833>5.99 on two statistical degrees of freedom, with p≃0.0199.
The two two-parameter models ω1​ and ω2​ are not nested, so their deviance difference has no ordinary nested chi-squared distribution calibration. The preferred parsimonious model is ω1​, with a storage effect and no log-force effect. Its deviance goodness-of-fit test is acceptable: D1​=5.173<9.49 on four statistical degrees of freedom. The common-probability model also passes its separate goodness-of-fit comparison, 10.452<11.07, but this does not contradict the relative evidence for adding storage: the two tests assess different null hypotheses. These are large-sample approximations; sufficiently large expected successes and failures, not merely the number of anthers, underpin their calibration.
