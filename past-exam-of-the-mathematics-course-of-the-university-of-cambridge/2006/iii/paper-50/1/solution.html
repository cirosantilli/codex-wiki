For X∈L, define the Adjoint representation of a Lie algebra by adX​(Y)=[X,Y]. The Jacobi identity gives, for every Z,
[adX​,adY​]Z=[X,[Y,Z]]−[Y,[X,Z]]=[[X,Y],Z]=ad[X,Y]​Z.
(1)
Thus ad preserves brackets and is a Lie algebra representation. Its Killing form is
κ(X,Y)=Tr(adX​adY​).
(2)
Using the representation identity and the cyclic property of the trace,
κ([X,Y],Z)​=Tr([adX​,adY​]adZ​)=Tr(adX​[adY​,adZ​])=κ(X,[Y,Z]).​
(3)
The Killing form is also symmetric by cyclicity.
In a basis Ta​, write [Ta​,Tb​]=cab​dTd​. The lowered structure constants satisfy
cabc​=κ([Ta​,Tb​],Tc​)=κ(Ta​,[Tb​,Tc​]).
(4)
The first expression is antisymmetric in a,b and the second in b,c. These transpositions generate all permutations, so cabc​ is totally antisymmetric.
An invariant subspace of L under its Adjoint representation of a Lie algebra is exactly an ideal of a Lie algebra: [L,I]⊆I. A simple Lie algebra has no nonzero proper ideal, proving the adjoint representation is irreducible.
Put DX​=d(X). The same trace calculation proves invariance of the Trace form of a Lie algebra representation:
H([X,Y],Z)=Tr([DX​,DY​]DZ​)=Tr(DX​[DY​,DZ​])=H(X,[Y,Z]).
(5)
Because the DX​ are anti-Hermitian, H is a real symmetric bilinear form. The Killing form of a simple compact Lie algebra is negative definite. Define a real linear map T by H(X,Y)=κ(TX,Y). Invariance of both forms implies that T commutes with every adZ​, while symmetry makes T self-adjoint for the positive inner product −κ. By the spectral theorem, its real eigenspaces are invariant under the Adjoint representation of a Lie algebra. Irreducibility forces a single eigenvalue, so T=μI and H=μκ. This also avoids the extra care needed when applying the complex form of the Schur lemma to a real vector space.
The kernel of d is an ideal of a Lie algebra. It cannot be all of L: a trivial representation of dimension greater than one is reducible. Simplicity therefore makes d faithful. For every nonzero X,
H(X,X)=Tr(DX2​)=−Tr(DX†​DX​)<0.
(6)
Hence the positive trace index for a compact simple Lie algebra gives
Hab​=−μδab​,μ>0​
(7)
in the adapted basis.
For the cubic trace tensor of a Lie algebra representation, the identity
Tr([DY​,DX​DZ​DW​])=0
(8)
expands to
B([Y,X],Z,W)+B(X,[Y,Z],W)+B(X,Z,[Y,W])=0.
(9)
Set Y=Td​, X=Ta​, Z=Tb​, W=Tc​. Cyclicity of B converts Bℓbc​ to Bbcℓ​ and Baℓc​ to Bcaℓ​. Thus the invariance identity for a cubic trace tensor is
cda​ℓBbcℓ​+cdb​ℓBcaℓ​+cdc​ℓBabℓ​=0.​
(10)
For the final contraction, it is important to keep the specified index-raising convention. In the adapted basis write Cab​c=cab​c. Then cabc​=−Cab​c, while raising the last two indices with κab=−δab gives ca​mn=−Cam​n. Total antisymmetry and the definition of the Killing form imply
∑m,n​Cam​nCrm​n=−κar​=δar​,ca​mncmn​r=−δa​r.
(11)
Only the antisymmetric part of B in m,n contributes, so
ca​mnBmnb​​=21​ca​mnTr([d(Tm​),d(Tn​)]d(Tb​))=21​ca​mncmn​rHrb​=−21​Hab​.​
(12)
Consequently the Killing-normalized contraction of a cubic trace tensor is
ca​mnBmnb​=+2μ​δab​.​
(13)
The negative sign printed in the last requested identity is incompatible with raising indices by the inverse Killing form. The opposite sign is obtained if one instead defines the lowered structure constants using the positive Euclidean metric, a different convention from the one specified.
For a concrete check, take the two-dimensional representation of su(2) with Ta​=−iσa​/(22​), where the σa​ are Pauli matrices. Then
κab​=−δab​,Hab​=−41​δab​,ca​mn=−2​1​ϵamn​,Bmnb​=−82​1​ϵmnb​.
(14)
Their contraction is +δab​/8, confirming the positive sign with μ=1/4 and providing a counterexample to the printed sign.
