The anti-Hermitian operators represent the compact real form; its complexification also contains the Hermitian Hi​ and the raising operators and lowering operators E±r​, with (E+r​)†=E−r​. Define the roots in the two-dimensional Cartan plane by
α1​=(1,0),α2​=(−1/2,3​/2),α3​=α1​+α2​=(1/2,3​/2).
(15)
Each has squared length one. For each r=1,2,3, set
J3(r)​=αr​⋅(H1​,H2​),J±(r)​=2​E±r​.
(16)
The displayed commutators give [J3(r)​,J±(r)​]=±J±(r)​ and [J+(r)​,J−(r)​]=2J3(r)​. Thus these are the three root SU(2) subalgebras of SU(3).
The commuting Hermitian operators H1​,H2​ can be simultaneously diagonalized. A weight of a representation is a pair λ=(h1​,h2​) for which
Hi​∣λ,a⟩=hi​∣λ,a⟩,
(17)
where a labels independent states of that representation weight. The weight multiplicity is the dimension of this simultaneous eigenspace. Commuting Hi​ past a ladder operator proves
E±r​:Vλ​⟶Vλ±αr​​,​
(18)
with zero image allowed when the raised or lowered representation weight is absent.
Choose α1​,α2​ as positive simple roots. A highest-weight vector is a nonzero simultaneous eigenvector annihilated by E+1​,E+2​,E+3​; the third condition follows from the first two and their commutator. In an irreducible representation, its highest weight Λ has multiplicity one and repeated lowering generates the representation. The three root SU(2) subalgebras of SU(3) give nonnegative integer Dynkin labels
p=2Λ⋅α1​=2h1​,q=2Λ⋅α2​=−h1​+3​h2​.
(19)
Equivalently, with ω1​=(1/2,1/(23​)) and ω2​=(0,1/3​),
Λ=pω1​+qω2​=(2p​,23​p+2q​),p,q∈Z≥0​.​
(20)
These are the SU(3) highest-weight coordinates in the normalization used here.
The Weyl group is generated by the reflections sr​(λ)=λ−2(λ⋅αr​)αr​. The SU(2) Lie algebra root-string symmetry makes these reflections preserve both representation weights and their weight multiplicities. They generate the symmetries of an equilateral triangle: rotations through 120∘ and three reflections. The outer polygon is the convex hull of the Weyl group orbit of Λ. When p,q>0 it is a hexagon with alternating side lengths p,q in root-step units; when exactly one is zero it is a triangle; when both vanish it is a point. The representation conjugate to (p,q) has labels (q,p) and negative representation weights. For p=q, this gives an additional inversion symmetry and a regular hexagon with 60∘ rotations. A general unequal-sided hexagon need not have that extra symmetry.
The shell multiplicities in an SU(3) weight diagram can be stated precisely. Let Pa,b​ be the convex hull of the Weyl group orbit of aω1​+bω2​. For λ in the root lattice coset Λ+Q,
mp,q​(λ)=k=0∑min(p,q)​1Pp−k,q−k​​(λ).​
(21)
representation weights outside this coset or outside the outer polygon are absent. This states that boundary representation weights have multiplicity one, each inward shell adds one, and the innermost triangular region has constant multiplicity min(p,q)+1. When p=q the innermost region is a single point. All representation weights of a triangular diagram have multiplicity one. Counting the diagram gives dim(p,q)=(p+1)(q+1)(p+q+2)/2.
