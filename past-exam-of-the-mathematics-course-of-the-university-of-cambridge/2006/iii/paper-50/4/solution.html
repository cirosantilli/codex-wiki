Use the metric η=diag(1,−1,−1,−1) and first consider the connected Poincaré group, R1,3⋊SO+(1,3). Physical half-integer spins require unitary representations of its double cover R1,3⋊SL(2,C); genuine representations of the group without this cover permit only integer spin. Write Pμ=d(Pμ) and Mμν=d(Mμν). Strong continuity gives self-adjoint generators, with commutators understood on a common invariant dense domain.
The commuting translation generators have a joint spectral resolution. Lorentz transformations carry the spectral four-momentum p to Λp, so an irreducible representation has its momentum spectral measure concentrated on a single Lorentz orbit. For positive-energy particles the orbits of interest are the future mass shells p2=m2>0, or p2=0 with p=0 and p0>0. The zero-momentum orbit and spacelike or negative-energy orbits are outside these two particle cases.
Choose ϵ0123=1 and define the Pauli-Lubanski pseudovector with the overall sign convention
Wμ=−21​ϵμνρσPν​Mρσ​.
(42)
This sign makes the spatial rest-frame vector equal to mJ. The generators' Hermiticity and the antisymmetric epsilon contraction make Wμ Hermitian: the ordering correction from commuting P through M contracts metric tensors into epsilon and vanishes. The same antisymmetry proves Pauli-Lubanski orthogonality, WμPμ​=0.
The assumed commutators say that Pμ and Wμ transform as four-vectors and that W commutes with all translations. Consequently
C1​=Pμ​Pμ,C2​=Wμ​Wμ
(43)
commute with the entire Poincare algebra. For instance the two terms in the Lorentz commutator with a contracted vector square cancel. The Casimir operators therefore have fixed spectral values in a unitary irreducible representation, by the Schur lemma applied to their spectral projections. A second ingredient, the little group, distinguishes the internal states.
Fix a standard momentum k on the orbit. Its little group is the subgroup of Lorentz transformations leaving k fixed. Choose L(p) with L(p)k=p. A Lorentz transformation acts on the internal fibre by the Wigner rotation
R(Λ,p)=L(Λp)−1ΛL(p),R(Λ,p)k=k.
(44)
If dK​ is a unitary irreducible representation of this stabilizer, the induced representation acts on wavefunctions as
(U(a,Λ)ψ)(p)=e−ia⋅pdK​(R(Λ,Λ−1p))ψ(Λ−1p).
(45)
The measure d3p/(2p0) on either future mass shell is Lorentz invariant, so this action is unitary. The identity
R(Λ1​Λ2​,p)=R(Λ1​,Λ2​p)R(Λ2​,p)
(46)
proves the group multiplication law. Conversely, covariance of the translation spectral resolution supplies precisely these fibres and their stabilizer action. A commuting operator acts fibrewise; transitivity relates its fibre values, and irreducibility of dK​ makes it scalar. This explains why the construction gives, and classifies, the unitary irreducible representations on each orbit.
For a timelike momentum choose k=(m,0,0,0). A Lorentz transformation fixing it acts only as a rotation of its spatial orthogonal complement, so the massive particle little group is SO(3), or SU(2) in the double cover. Put
Ji​=21​ϵijk​Mjk,Ki​=M0i.
(47)
The Poincare algebra gives [Ji​,Jj​]=iϵijk​Jk​. The unitary irreducible representations of SU(2) are labelled by j=0,21​,1,…, with J2=j(j+1) and J3​ eigenvalues −j,−j+1,…,j. Evaluation of the Pauli-Lubanski pseudovector at k gives W0=0, W=mJ. Thus the massive Pauli-Lubanski Casimir and particle labels are
P2=m2,W2=−m2j(j+1),(m,j),2j+1 spin states.​
(48)
The full massive induced representation of the Poincare double cover is infinite-dimensional because momentum ranges continuously over the orbit; its finite internal multiplicity is 2j+1.
For a null momentum choose k=(E,0,0,E) with E>0. The infinitesimal stabilizer generators are
R=J3​,N1​=K1​−J2​,N2​=K2​+J1​.
(49)
Their commutators with momentum, evaluated at k, vanish. From the rotation and boost commutators of the Poincare algebra one obtains
[R,N1​]=iN2​,[R,N2​]=−iN1​,[N1​,N2​]=0.
(50)
These are the massless little-group generators of ISO(2): a planar rotation and two commuting null rotations, which act like translations in the little-group algebra. Hence the massless particle little group is the Euclidean plane group, with the appropriate double cover for half-integer spin.
The definition of W gives W0=P⋅J and W=P0J−P×K. At the standard null momentum,
Wμ=(ER,EN2​,−EN1​,ER),W2=−E2(N12​+N22​).
(51)
Suppose the little-group representation has finite dimension, as for particles with finitely many polarizations. The commuting Hermitian N1​,N2​ have a finite joint spectrum. Conjugation by e−iϕR rotates this spectrum through every planar angle. The only finite set invariant under all these rotations is the origin. Thus both Ni​ vanish, proving that finite-dimensional unitary representations of the massless little group have trivial translations. Irreducibility then leaves a one-dimensional rotation character, R=hI. The massless longitudinal Pauli-Lubanski eigenvalues are
P2=0,Wμ=hPμ,W2=0,h∈21​Z​
(52)
for the double cover, with h∈Z for a genuine SO(2) character. The number h is helicity. Different helicities have the same two Casimir eigenvalues, so the little group is indispensable for the classification. A connected-group irreducible representation has one helicity; if parity is also implemented, it exchanges h and −h and normally requires their pair.
Unitarity alone also permits nontrivial null-rotation generators. The rotation-invariant operator N12​+N22​ is scalar in an irreducible little-group representation; if it has value (ρ/E)2>0, one obtains a continuous-spin representation. A concrete realization on angular wavefunctions is
N1​=Eρ​cosϕ,N2​=Eρ​sinϕ,R=−idϕd​.
(53)
These operators satisfy the same commutators. Periodic or antiperiodic angular wavefunctions give integer or half-integer rotation modes, and the translation generators connect infinitely many such modes. The invariant labels are
P2=0,W2=−ρ2,ρ>0.​
(54)
These are the additional infinite-polarization massless unitary irreducible representations. Restricting to finitely many particle polarizations excludes them; they must not be ruled out merely by assuming unitarity. This completes the timelike and null cases of the Wigner classification.
