The uniform modular Frankl-Wilson theorem states the following. Let p be prime, let L⊆Fp​ have size s, and let F be a rank-k family on [N], with 0≤s≤min{k,N−k}. Assume kmodp∈/L and ∣A∩B∣modp∈L for every distinct A,B∈F. Then
∣F∣≤(sN​).​
(29)
The restrictions concern distinct pairs; the diagonal size residue is explicitly excluded from L.
For each member A, over Fp​ form the intersection polynomial
fA​(z1​,…,zN​)=∏ℓ∈L​(∑i∈A​zi​−ℓ).
(30)
At the characteristic vector 1B​, its value vanishes when B=A and is nonzero when B=A. Thus these functions are linearly independent on the whole rank-k layer: evaluate any linear relation at each family member in turn. Replacing every positive power of zi​ by zi​ preserves all Boolean evaluations, so the functions are restrictions of multilinear polynomials of degree at most s.
We need the sharp low-degree evaluation rank on a uniform layer, not the larger whole-cube dimension ∑j≤s​(jN​). Let M be the integer incidence matrix with rows indexed by subsets S of size at most s, columns by rank-k sets B, and entries 1S⊆B​. Over the rationals, if ∣S∣=j≤s, then
∑S⊆T⊆[N]∣T∣=s​​MT,⋅​=(s−jk−j​)MS,⋅​.
(31)
This coefficient counts the possible T inside each containing column set and is positive because s≤k. Consequently the degree-s rows span all rows over Q, giving rank at most (sN​). Every larger square minor is therefore the integer zero. Reducing those minors modulo p shows the rank over Fp​ is also at most (sN​). This transfer avoids dividing by a binomial coefficient that might vanish modulo p. The independent functions fA​ lie in this row space, so their number is at most its rank. This proves the theorem in full.
For the requested forbidden-intersection application, split A into sets containing coordinate one and sets avoiding it. In the first part, delete one. Distinct original sets have intersection size between one and 2p−1, excluding p. Their reduced intersections therefore lie between zero and 2p−2, excluding p−1, so their residues belong to L={0,…,p−2}. The reduced set size is 2p−1, whose residue is p−1∈/L. Frankl-Wilson on the ground set of size 4p−1 bounds this part by (p−14p−1​).
For the avoiding part, first complement every set. The complements contain one, retain size 2p, and satisfy
∣Ac∩Bc∣=4p−∣A∪B∣=∣A∩B∣.
(32)
Delete one and apply the same argument. The complement splitting for forbidden midpoint intersections yields the stronger bound
∣A∣≤2(p−14p−1​)≤2(p−14p​).​
(33)
The split is essential: a family may contain complementary pairs with intersection zero, preventing direct use of L={1,…,p−1} on the original layer.
The Borsuk conjecture asserted that a bounded subset of Rd of positive diameter can be partitioned into d+1 subsets of strictly smaller diameter. Here is the Kahn-Kalai counterexample to the Borsuk conjecture, implemented by balanced cut vectors. For each X∈[4p](2p), define vX​∈R(24p​) whose coordinate indexed by an unordered pair {i,j} is one when exactly one endpoint lies in X, and zero otherwise. Complementary sets give the same cut. Choose the representative with 1∈X, obtaining exactly
M=(2p−14p−1​)
(34)
distinct points: a cut determines its two parts, and fixing the side containing one removes its only ambiguity. Every cut has (2p)2=4p2 occupied coordinates, so all points lie in the affine hyperplane with coordinate sum 4p2. Its dimension is
d=(24p​)−1.
(35)
We can identify this hyperplane isometrically with Rd; no claim of full affine span is needed.
For two side sets with t=∣X∩Y∣, their symmetric difference has 4p−2t points. The two cuts disagree exactly on pairs with one endpoint in that symmetric difference, giving the balanced cut-vector distance formula
∥vX​−vY​∥22​=(4p−2t)(2t)=4t(2p−t)=4(p2−(t−p)2).​
(36)
The diameter is 2p, attained exactly when t=p; such pairs exist even among representatives containing one. Thus a smaller-diameter part contains no pair with side intersection p. Since all representative sets already contain one, deleting that common coordinate and applying the preceding Frankl-Wilson argument bounds each part by (p−14p−1​). There is no extra factor two at this step. This is the cut construction described in the original Kahn-Kalai paper; the distance and part-size calculations above give the required justification directly.
An explicit choice is p=13. The point set lies in dimension
d=(252​)−1=1325.​
(37)
The exact counts are
M=(2551​)=247959266474052,B=(1251​)=158753389900.
(38)
Their ratio requires at least ⌈M/B⌉=1562 smaller-diameter parts, exceeding d+1=1326. In particular,
M−1326B=37452271466652>0.
(39)
Rescaling the point set to diameter one changes none of these partition counts. Therefore the Borsuk counterexample in dimension 1325 is fully explicit. This is a concrete dimension, not a claim that it is the smallest possible counterexample dimension.
