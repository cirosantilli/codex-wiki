The relevant notion is a root-of-unity Euler system, which contains auxiliary-prime data as well as the p-power tower. Fix a finite set S of primes containing two but not p, and let WS​ consist of all roots of unity whose orders are prime to every member of S. It is a map Φ:WS​→Q​× satisfying these three axioms:
  • Evenness and Galois equivariance: Φ(ζ−1)=Φ(ζ) and Φ(σζ)=σΦ(ζ) for every σ∈Gal(Q​/Q).
  • Distribution: for every prime q∈/S, ∏ρ∈μq​​Φ(ρζ)=Φ(ζq).
  • Auxiliary-prime congruence: if q∈/S and the order of ζ is prime to q, then Φ(ρζ)≡Φ(ζ)(modq) for every ρ∈μq​ and every prime q above q; the values are integral at the primes used in the congruence.
The first axiom puts Φ(ζ) in the maximal real subfield of Q(ζ). For ζ=ζn​, the second axiom is exactly norm compatibility from Kn+​ to Kn−1+​ for n≥1. Auxiliary roots with prime orders different from p are essential: mere norm compatibility of a sequence in the tower is not a verification of all three axioms.
The real cyclotomic unit group at a p-power conductor is generated by −1 and the symmetric cyclotomic units cn​(a,b) with p∤ab. Galois conjugates are included, since a conjugate of cn​(a,b) is cn​(ua,ub) for an integer u prime to p. Thus a prescribed unit can be expressed as
ε=η∏j=1r​daj​​(ζn​)ej​,η∈{±1},p∤aj​,ej​∈Z,∑j​ej​=0,

where da​(z)=z−a/2−za/2, with half exponents interpreted in the odd-order root group. Choose S to contain two and every prime divisor of the aj​. For z=1 in WS​ put
Φ(z)=η∏j​daj​​(z)ej​,Φ(1)=η∏j​ajej​​.

Each factor at z=1 is nonzero because raising z to aj​ preserves its order. At z=1 each daj​​ has a simple zero with first coefficient −aj​; the total exponent zero cancels the zeros and signs, giving the stated removable value. Consequently Φ(ζn​)=ε.
We verify the axioms individually. First, da​(z−1)=−da​(z); the product's sign is (−1)∑ej​=1, so Φ is even. The unique half-root operation commutes with every field automorphism, all exponents are integers and η is rational. Hence Φ(σz)=σΦ(z), including at z=1.
Second, q∈/S is odd and does not divide any aj​. For a variable x, choose half powers consistently and use
∏ρ∈μq​​da​(ρx)=x−qa/2∏ρ∈μq​​(1−ρaxa)=x−qa/2(1−xaq)=da​(xq).
(17)
Here ρ↦ρa permutes μq​, and the product of its half powers is one. Raising these identities to ej​ and multiplying gives the distribution identity, since ηq=η. If x=1 or xq=1, take the removable values after cancellation; the rational-function identity remains valid there. Thus the distribution axiom holds also at the special roots where the uncancelled factors vanish.
Third, fix q∈/S, a root z of order prime to q, and a prime q above q. Every ρ∈μq​ reduces to one modulo q. If z=1, its reduction retains its exact order: prime-to-q roots have distinct reductions because their order polynomial is separable in characteristic q. Therefore 1−zaj​ is a local unit, and all the factors and inverse factors are regular at that reduction. Reduction of the expression for Φ(ρz) then gives Φ(z), since the half roots reduce compatibly. This proves the congruence and local integrality for nontrivial z.
At z=1, write ρ=1+T. The cancelled expression
η(1+T)−21​∑j​aj​ej​∏j​(T1−(1+T)aj​​)ej​
(18)
is a unit formal power series over Zq​, because every aj​ and two are q-adic units. Its constant term is η∏j​ajej​​. Evaluating at T=ρ−1 proves the remaining congruence modulo every prime over q. This also covers ρ=1. Hence every real cyclotomic unit at any layer is a value of an Cyclotomic Euler system satisfying all three axioms.
For completeness, at a nontrivial root z every ratio da​(z)/db​(z) is a global unit. After removing a root-of-unity factor, both it and its inverse are geometric sums because a,b are invertible modulo the order of z. Thus the values used at nontrivial roots really are units, not just nonzero algebraic numbers. Values at one may be rational S-units, which is compatible with the definition.
