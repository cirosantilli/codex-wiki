A smooth vector field on a smooth manifold M is a smooth section of its tangent bundle: it assigns v(p)∈Tp​M smoothly to each point. On a Lie group, it is a left-invariant vector field when
v(gp)=(dLg​)p​v(p),Lg​(p)=gp.
(26)
For X∈Te​G, define vX​(g)=(dLg​)e​X. Smoothness of multiplication makes this a smooth vector field, and Lg​Lp​=Lgp​ plus the chain rule proves left invariance. Every left-invariant vector field arises this way from its value at the identity.
For a matrix Lie group, vX​(g)=gX, because the derivative at zero of gγ(t) is gX. The printed group name in this subpart must be read as SL2​, rather than the additive vector space sl2​: it is the matrix-group calculation consistent with the preceding subparts. At the specified matrix,
vX​((0−1​10​))=(0−1​10​)(00​10​)=(00​0−1​).​
(27)
A tangent vector at a nonidentity group element need not itself be trace-free; here g−1vX​(g)=X is trace-free, as required. If one instead interpreted the printed sl2​ as its additive Lie group, the field would be the constant field X, a different problem.
