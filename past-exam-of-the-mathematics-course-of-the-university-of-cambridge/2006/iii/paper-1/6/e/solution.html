The single bonds give equal simple-root lengths and inner products, after normalization,
(α,α)=(β,β)=(γ,γ)=2,(α,β)=(β,γ)=−1,(α,γ)=0.
(53)
The positive roots of this A3 root system are
α, β, γ, α+β, β+γ, α+β+γ.​
(54)
The first two sums are roots by the qualified obtuse-root lemma. The last is a root because (α+β,γ)=−1. All have nonnegative simple-root coordinates, hence are positive.
To prove completeness, first note that every root is Weyl-conjugate to a simple root. For a nonsimple positive root δ=∑ci​αi​, some (δ,αi​)>0, since (δ,δ)=∑ci​(δ,αi​)>0. Reflection subtracts a positive integer multiple of αi​, decreasing its integer height. The reflected root stays positive: it leaves the coefficients at all other simple roots unchanged, at least one of which is positive; a root has coordinates all of one sign. Repeating this height decrease reaches a simple root. Thus every root has squared length two in this connected single-bond system.
Write an arbitrary positive root as aα+bβ+cγ, with nonnegative integers a,b,c. Its squared length condition is
a2+b2+c2−ab−bc=1,2=a2+c2+(a−b)2+(b−c)2.
(55)
Thus a,c∈{0,1}. For (a,c)=(0,0) the equation gives b=1; for (1,0) or (0,1) it gives b=0 or 1; for (1,1) it gives b=1. These are exactly the six coefficient triples in the displayed list. This proves completeness from the axioms, not merely from recognizing the Dynkin diagram.
