Let f1​,f2​,f3​ be the dual basis of W∗, and use L1​+L2​+L3​=0. The elementary tensors ei​⊗ej​⊗fk​ have weight Li​+Lj​−Lk​. Grouping them gives every weight space explicitly:

The counts are 6+3⋅2+3⋅5=27, so this table exhausts the tensor product. The diagrams below use equilateral coordinates L1​=(1,0), L2​=(−1/2,3​/2), L3​=(−1/2,−3​/2); numbers at points are weight multiplicities.
To decompose the module, first split the first two factors into their symmetric and alternating parts. Put tij​=(ei​⊗ej​+ej​⊗ei​)/2, including tii​=ei​⊗ei​. The equivariant contraction
C:S2W⊗W∗→W,C(tij​⊗fk​)=δik​ej​+δjk​ei​
(9)
is onto, since C(tii​⊗fi​)=2ei​. Its kernel K has dimension fifteen and contains the highest-weight vector t11​⊗f3​ of weight 2L1​−L3​.
Here is an explicit weight basis of the trace-free symmetric-square dual tensor module, and thus the requested fifteen-vector basis:
tii​⊗fk​,tij​⊗fk​,tij​⊗fj​−21​tii​⊗fi​,​i=k(6 vectors),i<j, {i,j,k}={1,2,3}(3 vectors),i=j(6 vectors).​​
(10)
Every displayed vector contracts to zero. The first two families occupy distinct one-dimensional weights; at each weight Li​ the two vectors in the last family are independent because their respective tij​⊗fj​ terms are distinct. Thus all fifteen are independent and span K.
For completeness, K is irreducible, not just an invariant kernel of the right dimension. On elementary tensors the raising action is
Eab​(ei​⊗ej​⊗fk​)=δbi​ea​⊗ej​⊗fk​+δbj​ei​⊗ea​⊗fk​−δak​ei​⊗ej​⊗fb​.
(11)
At the six weights 2Li​−Lk​, both E12​ and E23​ kill the vector only for (i,k)=(1,3). At each weight −2Lk​, at least one of those operators is nonzero. At weight L1​, write a vector as a(t12​⊗f2​−t11​⊗f1​/2)+b(t13​⊗f3​−t11​⊗f1​/2). The two raising equations are 3a+b=0 and −a+b=0, forcing zero. On the two-dimensional weight-L2​ space E12​ is injective, and on the weight-L3​ space E23​ is injective, as substitution in the same action formula shows. Therefore the common raising kernel in K is the single line C(t11​⊗f3​). By Weyl complete reducibility theorem, every irreducible summand supplies a highest-weight line, so K has only one summand and is irreducible.
The symmetric-factor contraction splits off a defining copy W; explicitly ei​↦∑j​tij​⊗fj​ is equivariant and its contraction is 4ei​. For the alternating factor, the invariant volume form identifies Λ2W≅W∗, sending e1​∧e2​ to f3​, e2​∧e3​ to f1​ and e3​∧e1​ to f2​. Therefore
Λ2W⊗W∗≅W∗⊗W∗=S2W∗⊕Λ2W∗≅S2W∗⊕W.
(12)
The symmetric dual module is irreducible of dimension six and highest weight −2L3​; its symmetric-monomial basis has weights −2Li​ and −Li​−Lj​=Lk​, all of multiplicity one. In the original tensor product its highest-weight vector is
(e1​⊗e2​−e2​⊗e1​)⊗f3​.​
(13)
The raising action above annihilates it, and its weight is L1​+L2​−L3​=−2L3​.
Altogether the Triple tensor decomposition for the defining sl3 representation is
Z≅V(2ω1​+ω2​)⊕V(2ω2​)⊕W⊕W,27=15+6+3+3.​
(14)
The fifteen-dimensional module has multiplicity one at each 2Li​−Lk​ and −2Li​, and multiplicity two at each Li​. The six-dimensional module has multiplicity one at −2Li​ and Li​. Each defining module has the three weights Li​, once each. These add to the original table of multiplicities.
The two defining summands are not intrinsically distinguished: other diagonal copies inside their isotypic component have the same three-point weight diagram. Thus the displayed diagrams account for every irreducible submodule type, as well as showing both copies in this decomposition.
