Choose simple roots α1​,…,αr​. For the orbit statement one must use the Closed dominant Weyl chamber
W={λ:⟨λ,αi∨​⟩≥0 for every i}.​
(43)
Its interior, defined using strict inequalities, is the open Weyl chamber. Weights on reflecting walls, including zero, cannot be moved into that interior, so closure is necessary here.
Choose ρ strictly inside the chamber, for example the sum of the fundamental weights. The finite Weyl group orbit of β contains an element λ maximizing (λ,ρ). If (λ,αi​)<0 for some simple root, then
(si​λ,ρ)−(λ,ρ)=−(αi​,αi​)2(λ,αi​)​(αi​,ρ)>0,
(44)
contradicting the maximum. Thus every simple-root pairing of λ is nonnegative, and λ∈W. Consequently some w∈W sends every integral weight β into the closed fundamental chamber. The argument in fact works for every vector in the real weight space.
