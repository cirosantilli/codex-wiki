Work in finite dimension over C; the characteristic-zero real version follows by complexification. The Cartan solvability criterion is
g is solvable⟺B(g,[g,g])=0.​
(15)
Its semisimplicity consequence, also often called Cartan's criterion, is g is semisimple if and only if its Killing form is nondegenerate. We prove both forms.
The auxiliary results used are as follows. The Lie theorem simultaneously triangularizes every finite-dimensional representation of a complex solvable Lie algebra. The Engel theorem says that a matrix Lie algebra consisting entirely of nilpotent endomorphisms has a common annihilated nonzero vector and a basis making all its matrices strictly upper triangular; in particular it is a nilpotent Lie algebra. Nilpotent means that the lower central series terminates, which implies that the derived series terminates as well. Finally, the Additive Jordan decomposition writes any complex endomorphism uniquely as x=xs​+xn​, where xs​ is diagonalizable, xn​ is nilpotent and they commute. On a generalized eigenspace Vλ​, the two parts are λI and x−λI, and both are polynomials in x. On endomorphisms, adxs​ is diagonalizable, adxn​ is nilpotent, and these are the Jordan parts of adx: the first assertion follows by splitting into maps between eigenspaces, and the second by expanding repeated commutators with the nilpotent xn​.
First prove the linear trace version: if L⊆gl(V) and
tr(ab)=0(a∈L, b∈[L,L]),
(16)
then L is solvable. Fix x∈[L,L] and let V=⨁Vλ​ be its generalized eigenspace decomposition. Define y to act on Vλ​ by the scalar λ. We do not assume that y, xs​ or xn​ belongs to L.
On Hom(Vμ​,Vλ​), adx has the form (λ−μ)I+N with N nilpotent, whereas ady is multiplication by λ−μ​. Choose a polynomial q with q(d)=dˉ for every occurring eigenvalue difference d, and with all its derivatives of positive order up to the relevant nilpotency index zero at d. Such a polynomial exists by Hermite interpolation, or the Chinese remainder theorem for the pairwise coprime powers of (t−d). At d=0 its value is zero, so q(0)=0. Expanding q(dI+N) now gives
ady=q(adx).
(17)
Because x∈L and [L,L] is an ideal, every positive power of adx sends L into [L,L]. The zero constant term therefore gives [y,L]⊆[L,L].
Express x as a sum of commutators ∑j​[aj​,bj​] with aj​,bj​∈L. Cyclicity of trace and the hypothesis imply
tr(xy)=∑j​tr([aj​,bj​]y)=∑j​tr(aj​[bj​,y])=0.
(18)
But on Vλ​, xy has trace dim(Vλ​)∣λ∣2, since the nilpotent part has trace zero. Hence
0=tr(xy)=∑λ​dim(Vλ​)∣λ∣2,
(19)
forcing every eigenvalue of x to vanish. Thus every member of the derived algebra [L,L] is a nilpotent endomorphism. By the Engel theorem, [L,L] is nilpotent and therefore solvable; its derived series terminates, and adjoining the initial term L shows that L is solvable.
Conversely, if L is solvable, the Lie theorem makes it upper triangular. Commutators of upper-triangular matrices have zero diagonal, and multiplying such a commutator by an upper-triangular matrix still has zero diagonal. Therefore the trace condition holds. Apply this equivalence to L=adg. Its trace pairing is the Killing form, and solvability of its image is equivalent to solvability of g: the kernel is its abelian centre, so a terminating derived series in the quotient terminates after at most one more step in g. This proves the solvability criterion.
For the semisimplicity criterion, let R be the radical of the Killing form. Invariance makes R a Lie algebra ideal. For x,y∈R, their adjoint actions on g/R vanish, so computing trace in a basis adapted to R gives BR​(x,y)=Bg​(x,y)=0. The solvability criterion applied to R shows it is a solvable ideal. Thus a semisimple Lie algebra has R=0 and a nondegenerate Killing form.
Conversely, any nonzero solvable ideal has a last nonzero term A in its derived series; this is an abelian ideal of g. For x∈A, y∈g, the map adxady sends g into A and vanishes on A, so has trace zero. Hence A⊆R. A nondegenerate Killing form therefore rules out nonzero solvable ideals, exactly the definition of semisimplicity.
For classification, nondegeneracy of the Killing form provides the duality and root-space pairings used to extract a reduced crystallographic root system from a semisimple Lie algebra. Classification then reduces to the finite Dynkin diagrams and reconstruction of the Lie algebra from its root data.
