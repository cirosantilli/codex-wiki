Put S=u2+v2. At a nonzero equilibrium point the two equations form the homogeneous linear system
(μ−S/21+σ−S/2​1−σ+S/2μ−S/2​)(vu​)=0.
(1)
Its determinant must vanish, giving S2/2−(μ+σ)S+μ2+σ2−1=0. Thus
S=μ+σ±2−(μ−σ)2​,S>0.​
(2)
Each positive real polynomial root gives an antipodal equilibrium point pair; a negative polynomial root does not describe a real equilibrium point. A direction is specified by sin2θ=S/2−μ, cos2θ=S/2−σ, where u=S​cosθ, v=S​sinθ. This is the polar form of the cubic confinement model. The origin is always an equilibrium point.
The origin's eigenvalues are λ±​=μ±1−σ2​. A simple zero eigenvalue occurs on μ2+σ2=1, except at μ=0. The symmetry of an odd function (u,v)↦(−u,−v) makes the generic steady bifurcation a pitchfork bifurcation. At a point (σ,μ0​) on this circle with μ0​=0, vary μ=μ0​+δ. Expanding the radius equation gives S∼2μ0​δ/(μ0​+σ). In a signed, unit-eigenvector centre manifold coordinate, the cubic coefficient therefore has sign −(μ0​+σ)/(2μ0​).
On the lower semicircle μ0​=−1−σ2​ the transverse eigenvalue is negative: the pitchfork bifurcation is supercritical for σ<1/2​ and subcritical for σ>1/2​. On the upper semicircle it is supercritical for σ>−1/2​ and subcritical for σ<−1/2​ in the centre manifold direction, but its transverse eigenvalue is positive, so the bifurcating equilibrium points are not fully attracting. At (σ,μ0​)=(±1/2​,∓1/2​) the cubic vanishes. The radius equation instead gives δ=−S2/(4μ0​)+o(S2), a nonzero quintic degeneracy with amplitude proportional to ∣δ∣1/4: these are degenerate pitchforks in the cubic confinement model.
A Hopf bifurcation occurs at μ=0, ∣σ∣>1, with frequency σ2−1​. To determine its direction, first note that the divergence is 2μ−2S. The Bendixson-Dulac criterion excludes every nonconstant periodic orbit for μ≤0, since this divergence is negative except possibly at the origin. We can also calculate a nonzero saturation coefficient. Set a=∣σ+1∣, b=∣σ−1∣ and V=au2+bv2. At μ=0 the linear orbits are ellipses of constant V. Direct differentiation gives
V˙=2μV−S[V+(b−a)uv].
(3)
On a linear ellipse, write u=V/a​cosϕ, v=V/b​sinϕ. The angular speed is constant; the averages of u3v and uv3 vanish, while ⟨S⟩=V∣σ∣/(σ2−1). Averaging the cubic terms, or removing their oscillatory parts by a periodic change of coordinates in its normal form, gives for ϱ=V​
ϱ˙​=μϱ−2(σ2−1)∣σ∣​ϱ3+O(ϱ5+∣μ∣ϱ3).
(4)
The cubic coefficient is strictly negative. Hence a small attracting limit cycle exists for μ>0, and
the Hopf bifurcation is supercritical.​
(5)
Finally (σ,μ)=(±1,0) have a nonzero nilpotent matrix as their linearization with two zero eigenvalues. They are reflection-symmetric double-zero bifurcations where the Hopf bifurcation and steady thresholds meet; an ordinary nonzero-frequency Hopf bifurcation calculation does not apply there. These, the two degenerate pitchfork bifurcation points, and the generic pitchfork bifurcation/Hopf bifurcation loci above exhaust the origin's local loss-of-hyperbolicity possibilities.
