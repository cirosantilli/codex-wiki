The linearized complex equation couples a perturbation and its conjugate, so a single complex Fourier mode by itself is not a closed perturbation ansatz. Equivalently use real amplitude and wave phase perturbations, with Fourier modes paired to make each real field. Writing A=A0​+α+iβ and B=A02​ gives
αT​=4αXX​+(μ+9s^B−50B2)α,βT​=4βXX​+(μ+3s^B−10B2)β.
(41)
A real amplitude mode of slow wavenumber ℓ>0 therefore becomes neutral when
μ−4ℓ2+9s^A02​−50A04​=0.​
(42)
For a nonzero uniform state the equilibrium point condition eliminates μ, leaving amplitude growth 6s^B−40B2−4ℓ2. The wave phase mode growth is simply −4ℓ2, so it cannot give a nonzero-wavenumber bifurcation from these undetuned states.
The concave quadratic has maximum
maxB≥0​(6s^B−40B2)=409s^2​,B=403s^​.
(43)
Thus a nonzero uniform state needs ℓ≤3s^/(410​) to have a modulation bifurcation. The slow domain length is ε2L, so ℓn​=2πn/(ε2L). If its smallest positive wavenumber already exceeds this bound, none can become neutral. Since s=ε2s^, the conclusion is
L<3s8π10​​ ⟹ no modulation bifurcation of a nonzero uniform state.​
(44)
This is the modulation cutoff of a cubic-quintic uniform pattern. If the bound is reversed, candidate squared amplitudes are B=(3s^±9s^2−160ℓ2​)/40. They lie on the lower branch; the upper branch is already damped in amplitude and cannot undergo this stationary modulation bifurcation.
The word “nonzero” is necessary in the final claim. At A0​=0, the equilibrium point exists independently of μ, and the mode growth is μ−4ℓ2. It becomes neutral at μ=4ℓn2​ for every finite domain. For example, take s^=1, ε2L=20: then L=20/ε2<8π10​/(3s), but the zero state has a first-sideband threshold at μ=π2/25. Therefore the printed assertion, if read to include the trivial state, is false. The derived domain-size bound is the correct statement for nonzero pattern branches.
