A filtration of a ring is an exhaustive increasing family of additive subgroups Fi​R, indexed by the integers, with 1∈F0​R and Fi​RFj​R⊆Fi+j​R. A compatible filtration of a module on a right module satisfies Fi​MFj​R⊆Fi+j​M. The associated graded module has components Fi​M/Fi−1​M, and its action by the associated graded ring is
(m+Fi−1​M)(r+Fj−1​R)=mr+Fi+j−1​M.
(12)
Changing either representative changes the product only by an element of Fi+j−1​M, so the action is well-defined.
The subspace filtration on N is Fi​N=N∩Fi​M. The quotient filtration is Fi​(M/N)=(Fi​M+N)/N. Inclusion induces an injective map grN→grM, and the quotient induces a surjective map to gr(M/N). If m∈Fi​M maps to zero in its degree-i quotient, write m=n+m′ with n∈N and m′∈Fi−1​M. Then n∈N∩Fi​M, so the kernel is exactly the image of grN. We have the short exact sequence
0⟶grN⟶grM⟶gr(M/N)⟶0​,
(13)
which is exactness of associated graded modules for induced filtrations.
For the negative case, Fi​R=R for i≥0. Completeness means that R→lim​q​R/F−q​R is an isomorphism; in particular the filtration of a ring is separated. If x∈F−1​R and r∈R, then (xr)q∈F−q​R. The geometric series
∑q=0∞​(xr)q
(14)
converges in the complete negative filtration and is a two-sided inverse of 1−xr, by the finite geometric identity and continuity of multiplication. The unit criterion for the Jacobson radical now gives
F−1​R⊆J(R)​.
(15)
Let I be any right ideal. The associated graded module grI for its subspace filtration is a homogeneous ideal on the right in grR. If grR is a right Noetherian ring, choose homogeneous generators that lift to x1​,…,xs​∈I, with degrees d1​,…,ds​≤0.
For x∈I∩Fd​R, cancel its degree-d symbol by a sum ∑i​xi​ai,0​ with ai,0​∈Fd−di​​R. The remainder belongs to I∩Fd−1​R. Repeat the cancellation in each succeeding degree. After q steps,
x−∑i​xi​∑j=0q−1​ai,j​∈Fd−q​R,ai,j​∈Fd−j−di​​R.
(16)
For every i, the coefficient series converges to some ai​∈R by completeness. Passing to the limit gives x=∑i​xi​ai​. Thus these finitely many xi​ generate the actual right ideal I; no assumption that I is closed is needed. Since every right ideal is finitely generated, R is right Noetherian, by complete negative filtered-graded transfer of Noetherianity.
