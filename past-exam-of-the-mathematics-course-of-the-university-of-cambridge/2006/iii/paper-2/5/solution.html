The prime radical of a noncommutative ring is N(R)=⋂P​P, with P ranging over all prime ideals of a noncommutative ring. Here primeness means that for two-sided ideals A,B, AB⊆P implies A⊆P or B⊆P. A minimal prime over an ideal I is a prime ideal containing I with no strictly smaller prime ideal containing I.
The ascending chain condition on left ideals also applies to two-sided ideals. We first prove by Noetherian induction that every proper I contains a finite product of prime ideals, each containing I. If there were counterexamples, choose a maximal one. It is not prime, so there are strictly larger two-sided ideals A,B with AB⊆I. Neither is a counterexample; products of prime ideals contained in A and in B then concatenate to a product contained in I, a contradiction.
Write P1​⋯Pt​⊆I⊆Pj​. Every prime ideal P over I contains at least one Pj​, by primeness. The inclusion-minimal members of the finite list Pj​ are therefore precisely the minimal primes over an ideal I: any smaller prime over I would contain a member of that same list. Consequently there are finitely many. Replacing each factor Pj​ by a minimal prime contained in it gives
Q1​Q2​⋯Qt​⊆I
(17)
with each Qj​ minimal over I. Repeated factors may be necessary; in k[ε]/(εm) the sole minimal prime (ε) requires its mth power to reach zero.
Apply this to I=0. Since N(R)⊆Qj​ for every j,
N(R)t⊆Q1​⋯Qt​=0​.
(18)
Thus the prime radical is a nilpotent ideal, as in finite minimal primes and nilpotent prime radical.
Now assume R is commutative. A prime ideal P is an associated prime of a module M when P=AnnR​(m) for some 0=m∈M. Every nonzero module over R has an associated prime: the ascending chain condition gives a maximal annihilator of a nonzero element, and maximal annihilator of a module element is prime.
Since the given M is a Noetherian module, repeatedly choosing such a cyclic submodule in the next quotient yields a finite prime filtration
0=M0​⊊M1​⊊⋯⊊Ms​=M,Mi​/Mi−1​≅R/Pi​.
(19)
The process terminates by the ascending chain condition. In a short exact sequence 0→U→M→W→0, one has Ass(M)⊆Ass(U)∪Ass(W). Indeed, if P=Ann(m) and Rm∩U=0, the image of m in W still has annihilator P. Otherwise choose 0=am∈U; then a∈/P, and primeness gives Ann(am)=P. As Ass(R/Pi​)={Pi​}, induction along the prime filtration proves
AssR​(M)⊆{P1​,…,Ps​}​.
(20)
In particular there are only finitely many associated primes of a module.
Finally let P be a minimal prime ideal of R. Its localization at a prime ideal RP​ has exactly one prime, its maximal ideal PRP​. The preceding nilpotence result makes this maximal ideal nilpotent. Choose 0=y∈RP​ annihilated by PRP​, for example a nonzero element in its last nonzero power, or 1 if PRP​=0. Since elements outside the maximal ideal are units, AnnRP​​(y)=PRP​.
Write y=r/s. The element r/1 is nonzero and also has annihilator PRP​. Generate P by p1​,…,pℓ​. For each i, choose ui​∈/P with ui​pi​r=0, and put u=∏i​ui​. Then m=ur remains nonzero after localization, while Pm=0. If am=0, localization forces a/1∈PRP​, and hence a∈P. Therefore AnnR​(m)=P. This proves that minimal primes are associated primes.
