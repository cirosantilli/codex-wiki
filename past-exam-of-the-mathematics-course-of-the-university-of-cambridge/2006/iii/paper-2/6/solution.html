Fix a finite-dimensional nonnegative filtration of a ring giving R its almost commutative algebra structure, and choose a good filtration of a module on M. When grR is a standard graded algebra, the Hilbert-Serre theorem makes the cumulative Hilbert function hM​(j)=dimk​Fj​M eventually a polynomial. For M=0, define the dimension of a filtered module and multiplicity of a filtered module by
d(M)=deghM​,m(M)=d(M)![jd(M)]hM​(j)​.
(21)
Use d(0)=−∞ and m(0)=0. With positive weighted generators instead of a standard grading, the cumulative Hilbert function is eventually a quasipolynomial with a common positive leading coefficient across residue classes. Equivalently, hM​(j)=cjd+O(jd−1), and the definitions are d(M)=d and m(M)=d!c. The common leading coefficient follows from monotonicity: interlacing consecutive residue classes forces their leading coefficients to agree. For a standard grading, multiplicity is a positive integer, since an integer-valued polynomial has an integral expansion in the binomial polynomials; weighted normalization can instead give a positive rational number.
Two good filtrations of a module for this fixed algebra filtration bound each other after fixed index shifts. The corresponding Hilbert functions therefore satisfy hF​(j−a)≤hG​(j)≤hF​(j+b) for fixed a,b. Comparing their leading growth shows that the degree and leading coefficient agree. Thus d(M) and m(M) are independent of the module filtration; the fixed algebra filtration is part of the multiplicity convention.
For a submodule N, use its subspace filtration and the quotient filtration on M/N. These are good because grR is Noetherian. The short exact sequence of filtered pieces gives
hM​(j)=hN​(j)+hM/N​(j).
(22)
Nonzero leading coefficients are positive, so
d(M)=max{d(N),d(M/N)}​.
(23)
If d(N)=d(M/N), those leading coefficients add and
m(M)=m(N)+m(M/N)​.
(24)
This is dimension and multiplicity in a filtered exact sequence.
For the Weyl algebra An​(k) in characteristic zero, Bernstein inequality for Weyl algebra modules asserts d(M)≥n for every nonzero finitely generated module M. Write its generators as xi​,Di​, with [Di​,xj​]=δij​ and all other generator commutators zero. The ordered monomials xαDβ give the Bernstein filtration
Aj​=spank​{xαDβ:∣α∣+∣β∣≤j},dimk​Aj​=(2nj+2n​).
(25)
Take a finite-dimensional generating subspace V=0 of M and put Mj​=Aj​V.
To prove faithful finite-step action of a Weyl algebra, consider 0=a∈Aj​ and choose a nonzero coefficient cαβ​ of a monomial of maximal total degree d≤j. Applying ∏i​(adDi​)αi​(adxi​)βi​ to a gives
λ=cαβ​α!β!(−1)∣β∣=0.
(26)
Indeed, these commutators differentiate the ordered coefficients: lower-degree monomials vanish, and another degree-d monomial can survive only if all its exponents dominate (α,β), which forces equality. This is scalar extraction by iterated Weyl commutators.
Expanding these iterated commutators writes λ=∑ℓ​uℓ​avℓ​ with uℓ​,vℓ​∈Aj​. If a annihilated Mj​, every summand would annihilate V, contrary to λV=0. Therefore the action map is injective:
Aj​↪Homk​(Mj​,M2j​).
(27)
Taking dimensions and comparing polynomial degrees gives
(2nj+2n​)≤hM​(j)hM​(2j),2n≤2d(M),
(28)
which proves Bernstein inequality for Weyl algebra modules. The polynomial representation k[x1​,…,xn​] has dimension n, so the bound is sharp.
The associated graded ring of the Weyl algebra is a polynomial ring, so ascending filtered-graded transfer of Noetherianity makes An​(k) a left Noetherian ring. Thus every submodule of a finitely generated module is finitely generated. If d(M)=n, every nonzero subquotient has dimension at most n by the exact-sequence formula, and at least n by Bernstein inequality for Weyl algebra modules. Every such subquotient therefore has dimension n and positive integral multiplicity.
In a strictly descending chain M=M0​⊋M1​⊋⋯, each nonzero quotient Mi​/Mi+1​ consumes at least one unit of multiplicity, by dimension and multiplicity in a filtered exact sequence. Hence there are at most m(M) strict steps. This proves the descending chain condition, so M is Artinian. In fact this holonomic Weyl algebra module has module length at most m(M), as in multiplicity bounds the length of a holonomic Weyl module.
