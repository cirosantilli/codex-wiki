The Jacobson radical is J(R)=⋂L​L, where L runs over the maximal right ideals. Every proper right ideal I lies in such an L: the union of a chain of proper right ideals containing I is a right ideal and remains proper because it omits 1. Thus Zorn lemma applies.
The right-module form of Nakayama lemma says that a finitely generated module M with MJ=M is zero. More generally, if N is a submodule and M=N+MJ, then M=N. To prove the first statement, suppose M=0 and take a generating set m1​,…,ms​ of minimal size. Since MJ=M, there are ai​∈J with

The unit criterion for the Jacobson radical makes 1−as​ a unit. Hence ms​ is generated by the other mi​, a contradiction. Apply this result to the quotient module M/N to obtain the general form.
For an injective module endomorphism f of an Artinian module M, the descending chain condition gives
fn(M)=fn+1(M)
(5)
for some n. Given x∈M, write fn(x)=fn+1(y); injectivity of fn gives x=f(y). Thus f is surjective, as asserted by Artinian modules are co-Hopfian.
Now R is a semilocal ring and V=V/VJ is a finitely generated module over the right Artinian ring R=R/J. A finite direct sum of copies of R, and hence its quotient module V, is an Artinian module. The module endomorphism α induces α on V, and the given inverse-image condition says precisely that kerα=0. The preceding argument makes α surjective, so
V=α(V)+VJ.
(6)
The quotient module W=V/α(V) is finitely generated and satisfies WJ=W. Applying Nakayama lemma yields W=0. Therefore α is surjective, by surjectivity from injectivity modulo the Jacobson radical.
