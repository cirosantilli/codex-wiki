The Selberg upper-bound sieve replaces a difficult coprimality indicator by a nonnegative square whose coefficients can be optimized. Let P(z)=∏p≤z​p, and suppose a finite sequence has divisibility counts

This proves the basic Selberg sieve diagonalization and the useful bound on the weights, rather than merely asserting an optimized density.
Apply it to the polynomial F(n)=n(n+2) for 1≤n≤N. If ω(d) is the number of roots of F modulo squarefree d, the Chinese remainder theorem gives
ω(2)=1,ω(p)=2 (p>2),Ad​=dω(d)​N+O(ω(d)).

Use g(d)=ω(d)/d, R=z=N1/8, and sieve the indices for which F(n) has no prime factor at most z. The local weights are
