Use static gauge for a D3-brane in flat space and let X(x) be one physical transverse displacement. Put E=2πα′F0​ and Bi​=πα′εijk​Fjk​, so the fields entering the DBI action are dimensionless. With no magnetic field, writing v=∇X gives
L=−T3​1+∣v∣2−∣E∣2−∣E×v∣2​.
(24)
Introduce the dimensionless electric displacement in DBI theory, d=T3−1​∂L/∂E. Its Hamiltonian density obeys the DBI Hamiltonian bound for an electric spike:
(T3​H​)2=1+∣v∣2+∣d∣2+(d⋅v)2=(1+d⋅v)2+∣d−v∣2.
(25)
The electric bound is saturated when d=±∇X. On that locus the constitutive relation also gives E=d. With the Gauss law constraint in gauge theory away from charges, the electric BPS equations for a one-scalar D3-brane are therefore
E=±∇X,B=0,ΔX=0.​
(26)
The magnetic version is B=±∇X, E=0. More generally, one constant charge angle gives the aligned dyonic branch E=cosϑ∇X, B=sinϑ∇X with X harmonic. A single aligned electric, magnetic or dyonic spike preserves eight of the D3 vacuum's sixteen supersymmetry generators. Thus “one-half BPS” here means one-half of the linearly realized worldvolume supersymmetry; the string–D3 configuration preserves one-quarter of the thirty-two spacetime supersymmetries.
The electric BIon is the single-centre harmonic solution
X=X∞​+rc​,E=±∇X,c=πgs​∣N∣α′.​
(27)
The sign fixes its electric charge and spike orientation. The scalar is an actual transverse coordinate, so X→∞ as r→0 describes a long narrow spike emerging from an asymptotically planar D3-brane. It carries ∣N∣ fundamental strings ending on the brane. A magnetic BIon similarly represents D1-branes, and an aligned dyonic BIon represents a (p,q) string.
The string-tension interpretation of BIon energy follows explicitly. With T3​=[(2π)3gs​α′2]−1, the energy above the planar-brane vacuum outside a small radius r0​ is
ΔE=T3​∫r≥r0​​∣∇X∣2d3x=r0​4πT3​c2​=∣N∣TF1​(X(r0​)−X∞​),TF1​=2πα′1​.
(28)
Its divergence is the energy of a semi-infinite string, with the correct finite tension. For a magnetic spike, c=π∣N∣α′ instead, and the same calculation gives the D1-brane tension 1/(2πgs​α′). The solution solves the full nonlinear DBI action, not just its weak-field expansion.
The validity of the abelian BIon approximation concerns its derivative expansion, string loops and gravitational backreaction. Large field strengths are included by the DBI action, but rapidly varying field strengths and embedding curvature require corrections. Far outside the singular core these corrections can be small. The transition region has r∼c​; an electric throat can be parametrically controlled with ∣N∣gs​≫1 while keeping gravitational backreaction small with gs2​∣N∣≪1. For a magnetic throat the corresponding conditions are ∣N∣≫1 and gs​∣N∣≪1. The weak-coupling and large-charge windows allow both requirements. The ideal r→0 tip is not uniformly controlled by the single abelian effective action, so one should not infer exact microscopic core physics solely from its formal singularity.
Less supersymmetry survives when independent, compatible geometric or charge projectors must hold simultaneously. A useful purely geometric family is a D3-brane whose spatial worldvolume is a special Lagrangian submanifold of C3. In a graph zi=xi+iYi(x), the conditions are
ω∣Σ​=0,ImΩ∣Σ​=0,ReΩ∣Σ​=volΣ​,Ω=dz1∧dz2∧dz3.
(29)
The first condition makes ∂i​Yj symmetric, so locally Y=∇f. Pulling back Ω gives det(I+iD2f)dx1∧dx2∧dx3. Hence the phase-zero special Lagrangian graph equation is
Δf=det(D2f),1−σ2​(D2f)>0,​
(30)
where σ2​ is the sum of the pairwise products of the three Hessian eigenvalues. The positive real branch fixes the orientation. These curved D3 geometries minimize volume by their calibration ReΩ and generically preserve four spacetime supersymmetries, one-quarter of the D3 worldvolume supersymmetry. Special flat graphs can preserve more.
The generic count follows from the common tangent-plane projectors. With complex coordinate pairs (1,4),(2,5),(3,6), the phase-preserving rotations require (Γ14​−Γ25​)ϵ=0 and (Γ14​−Γ36​)ϵ=0. Multiplication by Γ14​ turns these into two independent commuting involution conditions with eigenvalue −1. Each halves the sixteen-dimensional planar D3 eigenspace, leaving 16/22=4. This is an intersection of compatible kappa symmetry projectors, rather than a count based on the rank of a single projector at one point.
Charge-carrying examples include compatible networks of differently oriented (p,q) strings ending on D3-branes. At each string junction, charge vectors add to zero and tension vectors balance; their directions and charge phases must also give common kappa symmetry projectors. They can impose further independent conditions beyond the planar D3 projector. Geometrically, several transverse scalar profiles describe the corresponding spikes and intersections. Merely replacing one electric spike by an aligned dyonic spike does not lower its preserved fraction; additional independent conditions are what reduce the common Killing spinor space.
