External intermittency is the alternation of turbulent and non-turbulent fluid at a point as a fluctuating interface passes it. Internal intermittency in the equilibrium range instead describes uneven, bursty concentrations of small-scale velocity gradients and viscous dissipation within turbulent fluid. Integral-scale intermittency is modulation of the energy-containing motions and their energy supply. It is non-universal because it retains information about forcing, boundary conditions and the history of the large-scale flow.
The Landau intermittency counterexample challenges the unconditional universality asserted by Kolmogorov 1941 theory. Suppose turbulent populations have different conditional mean viscous dissipation rates ϵa​. Even if each obeys the same conditional scaling, averaging over populations gives
SP​=βP​rP/3⟨ϵaP/3​⟩,

The Kolmogorov refined similarity hypothesis replaces global mean viscous dissipation by coarse-grained energy dissipation ϵAV​(r). It assumes that
V=[rϵAV​(r)]1/3Δv​
(7)
has universal conditional statistics at sufficiently large local Reynolds number. If the conditional Pth moment is βP​, the law of total expectation gives
⟨(Δv)P⟩=βP​⟨ϵAV​(r)P/3⟩rP/3​.
(8)
The refined similarity hypothesis does not itself prescribe the distribution of coarse-grained energy dissipation. Scale dependence of those moments can change the predicted exponents. As before, noninteger orders require absolute velocity increments.
