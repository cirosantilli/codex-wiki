The zeroth law of turbulence states that at fixed outer velocity u and integral scale of turbulence ℓ, the mean viscous dissipation per unit mass approaches a nonzero finite value as the Reynolds number Re=uℓ/ν becomes large. In an established forward energy cascade, this gives ϵ∼Cϵ​u3/ℓ, with Cϵ​ of order one. The small-scale velocity gradients become large enough to offset decreasing kinematic viscosity; this is the turbulent dissipation anomaly.
At the Kolmogorov microscales, the local Reynolds number is of order one, vη/ν∼1, and the viscous dissipation is ϵ∼ν(v/η)2. Solving these balances gives
η=(ν3/ϵ)1/4,v=(νϵ)1/4​.
(1)
Substituting the outer-scale estimate, and omitting order-one constants, gives
η∼ℓRe−3/4,v∼uRe−1/4​.
(2)
The associated eddy turnover time is η/v=(ν/ϵ)1/2.
