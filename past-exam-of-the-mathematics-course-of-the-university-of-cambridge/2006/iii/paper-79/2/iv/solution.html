For the equality, expand the two squares using ωz​=uθ′​+uθ​/r; their integrated difference is proportional to [uθ2​]0∞​, which vanishes. Substitution of the Gaussian vorticity profile gives
Dθ​=πδ42νΓ2​∫0∞​re−2r2/δ2dr=2πδ2νΓ2​=8παΓ2​​.

This excess dissipation of a Burgers vortex is independent of kinematic viscosity at fixed strain α and circulation Γ. Multiply by the mass density if a dimensional power per length is wanted.
The qualification "excess" matters. The imposed uniform strain itself has viscous dissipation density 3να2 per unit mass, so its integral over an infinite cross-section diverges. One must subtract that background or restrict to a finite cross-section. In a core-sized area, its contribution is of order ν2α, smaller than the swirl contribution by order (ν/Γ)2 as Γ/ν→∞.
Meanwhile δ∝ν1/2 shrinks and the central vorticity Γ/(πδ2) grows. The finite dissipation becomes concentrated in a narrow tube: a useful local model of internal intermittency and the turbulent dissipation anomaly, though not a proof that an entire turbulent flow consists of such vortices.
