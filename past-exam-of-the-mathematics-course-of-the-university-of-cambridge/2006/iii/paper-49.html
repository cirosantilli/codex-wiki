e−iH^T=limN→∞​(e−iΔp^​2/2e−iΔV(q^​))N.

At conjugate times, the fluctuation operator has a zero eigenvalue and the ordinary finite-prefactor expression fails; the kernel is obtained by distributional continuation. Away from these times the classical boundary problem is unique and the stated factorization applies directly.
For the infinite-time oscillator with an external source, use vacuum boundary conditions and the Feynman i-epsilon prescription. For a smooth source of compact support, define

The shifted stationary path is qc​=−GJ and obeys q¨​c​+m2qc​=J with the same vacuum prescription. A retarded inverse would define a different boundary problem. Dividing by the zero-source integral removes N and gives the vacuum source functional of a quantum oscillator. At zero mass an additional infrared prescription is needed for the inverse on constant modes.

Use the mostly-plus Minkowski metric, so p2=−p02​+p2, and the source term +∫Jϕ. Define the vacuum-normalized generating functional, connected generating functional, and quantum effective action by
Z[J]=∫DϕeiS[ϕ]∫DϕeiS[ϕ]+i∫Jϕ​,W[J]=−ilogZ[J],φ=δJδW​,Γ[φ]=∫Jφ−W[J].
(12)
The Legendre transform is taken after expressing J in terms of φ, locally where this relation is invertible. Varying it gives δΓ/δφ=J and Γ(2)=(W(2))−1. The logarithm generates connected graphs; the Legendre transform removes graphs joined by a single propagator, leaving one-particle-irreducible vertices. This sign convention is important: its classical limit is Γ=−S, consistent with the positive vertices in this question.
For the free real scalar field, integration by parts puts the action into the form S=−21​ϕAϕ, where A=−□+m2 has Fourier symbol p2+m2. Include the Feynman i-epsilon prescription A↦A−i0. Completing the square yields
Z0​[J]=exp(2i​JA−1J),W0​[J]=21​JA−1J,φ=A−1J,
(13)
and consequently
Γ0​[φ]=21​φAφ=∫ddx(21​(∂φ)2+21​m2φ2).
(14)
The physical scalar propagator is −iA−1, whereas the inverse quadratic kernel appearing in W0​ is A−1. Differentiating this quadratic functional and Fourier transforming gives
τ2​(p,−p)=p2+m2,τn​=0(n≥3).​
(15)
The infinitesimal prescription specifies propagators and is dropped when quoting the local vertex kernel.
At the order represented by tree-level Feynman diagrams, stationary phase gives Wmtree​=S[φ]+∫Jφ, with the classical field equation δS/δφ+J=0. The Legendre transform cancels the source term, so
Γmtree​[φ]=−S[φ]=∫ddx(21​(∂φ)2+21​m2φ2+4!λ​φ4).
(16)
Thus
τ2tree​=p2+m2,τ4tree​=λ.​
(17)
Other connected tree graphs with several quartic vertices are one-particle reducible and belong to W, not to the proper vertices of Γ. This explains why no extra tree correction is hidden in these formulas.
The one-loop proper two-point graph is the tadpole diagram. The one-loop proper four-point graphs are the three bubble diagrams, with pairings (12∣34), (13∣24) and (14∣23) of the external legs. Each has Feynman-diagram symmetry factor 1/2. The figure shows the topologies and every four-point channel; external momenta are incoming.
The tadpole factor can also be counted directly: there are 4⋅3=12 assignments of two labeled external legs to one quartic vertex, while its 4! normalization leaves 12/24=1/2. For a fixed four-point partition, the interchange of the two internal lines gives the same factor 1/2; there are three distinct partitions. Graphs with an external self-energy insertion are one-particle reducible and are not additional contributions to τ4​.
For the two-point divergence, perform a Wick rotation. The Euclidean tadpole contribution to the proper inverse kernel is
δτ2(1)​=2λ​Id​(m),Id​(m)=∫(2π)dddkE​​kE2​+m21​.
(18)
Assume m2>0 to separate the ultraviolet calculation from infrared issues. A Schwinger parameterization gives 1/(kE2​+m2)=∫0∞​dse−s(kE2​+m2). Using the supplied Gaussian integral, initially in the convergence region, gives
Id​(m)​=(4π)−d/2∫0∞​dss−d/2e−sm2=(4π)d/2(m2)d/2−1​Γ(1−2d​).​
(19)
The last expression provides the meromorphic dimensional regularization continuation. Set d=4−ϵ. Since Γ(−1+ϵ/2)=−2/ϵ+O(1),
I4−ϵ​(m)=−8π2ϵm2​+O(1),δτ2,div(1)​=−16π2ϵλm2​.​
(20)
If the coupling is written as μϵλ, that factor multiplies the intermediate integral but does not change the displayed pole residue. The negative pole is a property of analytic continuation; it must not be inferred from the positive cutoff integrand without carrying out the continuation.
The added mass counterterm contributes +B to τ2​, because Γmtree​=−S and its Lagrangian term is −Bϕ2/2. Hence cancellation requires
B=16π2(4−d)λm2​+O(λ2),​
(21)
up to an arbitrary finite subtraction specifying the renormalization scheme. No external momentum occurs in the tadpole, so no one-loop wave-function renormalization is needed. At m=0 the unregulated tadpole is scaleless and vanishes in dimensional regularization; the massive calculation identifies the mass-dependent ultraviolet pole without conflating it with a massless infrared limit.
For completeness, the signs of the four-point graphs in this effective-action convention can be read from the Euclidean one-loop functional 21​Trlog(AE​+λφ2/2). Its expansion has terms λTr(AE−1​φ2)/4 and −λ2Tr(AE−1​φ2AE−1​φ2)/16. Differentiating four times gives
δτ4(1)​=−2λ2​∑(ij)=(12),(13),(14)​∫(2π)dddkE​​(kE2​+m2)((kE​+pi​+pj​)2+m2)1​.
(22)
This is the Euclidean form, analytically continued to the physical external momenta. It confirms the three channel diagrams and their symmetry factors; evaluating its divergence is not needed for the requested mass counterterm.

In units ℏ=c=1, the action is dimensionless and a Lagrangian density has mass dimension d. The kinetic term therefore gives 2+2[ϕ]=d. Requiring each interaction density gn​ϕn to have the same dimension gives
[ϕ]=2d​−1,[gn​]=dn​=d−n(2d​−1)=n−d(2n​−1).​
(23)
This is engineering dimension; quantum scaling can acquire an anomalous dimension.
For a connected Feynman diagram with no derivative interactions, each independent loop integration contributes d powers of momentum, and each internal scalar propagator contributes −2. Thus its superficial degree of divergence is D=dL−2I. Let V=∑n​Vn​. A connected graph has L=I−V+1 independent cycles, and counting the half-edges incident on vertices gives 2I+E=∑n​nVn​. Substitution proves the scalar graph power-counting identity:
D​=d+(d−2)I−dn∑​Vn​=d−(2d​−1)E+n∑​[n(2d​−1)−d]Vn​=d−(2d​−1)E−n∑​dn​Vn​.​​
(24)
For a graph with C connected components, the first d is instead dC, since L=I−V+C. The printed expression therefore uses the usual connected-graph convention. This is the overall degree under simultaneous rescaling of all loop momenta. Subgraphs can still have ultraviolet divergences when the whole graph has D<0, so their counterterms must be subtracted before declaring an integral finite.
When all retained couplings have nonnegative dimension, only finitely many external-leg/derivative structures can have nonnegative degree. Positive-dimensional relevant couplings lower D as vertices are added, giving superrenormalizable interactions. Zero-dimensional marginal couplings give power-counting renormalizable interactions. Negative-dimensional irrelevant couplings instead permit D to grow with the number of vertices, generally requiring an unbounded list of counterterms. Such nonrenormalizable interactions can still define an effective field theory to any fixed order in a low-energy expansion.
In d=4, dn​=4−n: cubic interactions are superrenormalizable, quartic interactions are marginal and renormalizable, and powers n≥5 are nonrenormalizable. In d=3, dn​=3−n/2: cubic, quartic and quintic interactions are superrenormalizable, sixth-order interactions are marginal and renormalizable, and n≥7 are nonrenormalizable. This is a power-counting classification, not a guarantee that an odd highest-power potential is bounded below. All lower-dimensional operators allowed by the symmetries must be included when required by renormalization; for example, odd interactions can require a linear counterterm, whereas the quartic model's ϕ↦−ϕ symmetry excludes it.
Dimensional regularization defines convergent Euclidean loop integrals in a suitable domain of Red, then continues their parameter formulas meromorphically to complex d. The Gaussian identity supplies (4π)−d/2 and Schwinger parameterization supplies Gamma functions such as Γ(n−d/2). Their poles encode the ultraviolet singularities near physical dimension. Noninteger complex d is an analytic regulator of amplitudes, not a literal space with a noninteger number of coordinates. Masses or nonexceptional external momenta distinguish ultraviolet poles from infrared poles; scaleless integrals vanish in this convention and can hide that distinction.
For the quartic model let d=4−ϵ, ϕ0​=Z1/2ϕ, m02​=Zm2​m2 and λ0​=μϵZλ​λ. In renormalized fields, the bare Lagrangian is
L0​=−21​Z(∂ϕ)2−21​ZZm2​m2ϕ2−4!μϵλ​Z2Zλ​ϕ4.
(25)
Separate the terms with each Z equal to one from their differences. The differences are field, mass and quartic counterterms. After subtracting divergent subgraphs, the remaining ultraviolet pole of a proper graph is a local polynomial in its external momenta. In four-dimensional phi-fourth theory, power counting and reflection symmetry restrict these local terms to (∂ϕ)2, ϕ2, ϕ4 and a field-independent constant. Choosing the Laurent coefficients of the three renormalization factors recursively cancels these poles at every perturbative order. The constant vacuum term is removed by the zero-source normalization. This is an order-by-order finite renormalized expansion, not a claim of convergence of the perturbation series.
The source renormalization of a scalar generating functional follows directly from rewriting the source coupling in bare variables:
μ−ϵ/2hϕ=h0​ϕ0​,h0​=μ−ϵ/2Z−1/2h.​
(26)
A general source attached to a single elementary field needs this field renormalization; its connected correlations are the ordinary renormalized Green functions. Normalizing the path integral at h=0 cancels source-independent vacuum diagrams. Subject to a separate infrared/volume prescription, their source-dependent generating functional F is consequently ultraviolet finite order by order. For a constant source, this statement is interpreted as a formal connected expansion or a vacuum density, not as an automatically finite total logarithm in infinite spacetime.
Changing integration variables from ϕ to ϕ0​ introduces a source-independent constant Jacobian that cancels between the numerator and its normalization. The resulting normalized integral depends only on λ0​,m02​,h0​ and fixed physical boundary/volume data. Thus
μdμdF​​λ0​,m02​,h0​​=0.
(27)
Define all scale derivatives at these fixed bare quantities:
β​(λ)=μdμdλ​,γm2​(λ)=μdμdlogm2​,γ​h​(λ)=μdμdlogh​.
(28)
In a mass-independent subtraction scheme, logarithmic differentiation of the bare relations gives
β​=−1+λ∂λ​logZλ​ϵλ​,γm2​=−β​∂λ​logZm2​,γ​h​=2ϵ​+2β​​∂λ​logZ.​
(29)
The factors contain regulator poles, but the combinations determining the physical renormalization group functions have finite limits. In particular β​=−ϵλ+β(λ) and γ​h​=ϵ/2+γϕ​(λ). The chain rule now gives the requested equation:
(μ∂μ​+β​∂λ​+γm2​m2∂m2​+γ​h​h∂h​)F=0.​
(30)
As a sign check, minimal subtraction gives Zm2​=1+λ/(16π2ϵ)+O(λ2) and Zλ​=1+3λ/(16π2ϵ)+O(λ2), with Z=1+O(λ2). Hence β=3λ2/(16π2)+O(λ3), γm2​=λ/(16π2)+O(λ2), and γϕ​ starts at two loops. The positive signs agree with the tadpole subtraction and the three four-point channels.
The dimensional rewriting needs care. The chosen source normalization gives [h]=3, [m2]=2, and [λ]=0, so m2/μ2 and h/μ3 are the natural dimensionless parameters. For the full source functional, spacetime arguments must also be rescaled: with y=μx, write ϕ(x)=μ(d−2)/2ψ(y) and h(y)=μ−3h(y/μ). The dimensionless functional then depends on λ,m2/μ2, the rescaled source profile, and the rescaled integration domain.
For a literal uniform source in a fixed physical spacetime volume Vd​, the uniform-source vacuum functional and spacetime volume relation is instead
F=Vd​μdf(λ,μ2m2​,μ3h​).​
(31)
For example, even at λ=0 and m2>0, completing the square for the constant mode gives
F0​=2m2Vd​μ−ϵh2​=2Vd​μd​m2/μ2(h/μ3)2​.
(32)
Thus the printed three-argument rewriting of the total F suppresses necessary volume/source-profile data. It is valid as a dimensional shorthand only with those data understood; a dimensionless density has the three arguments, while its scale equation includes its engineering dimension. At infinite volume, the total constant-source logarithm is generally extensive and infinite even after ultraviolet renormalization.
The massless renormalization-group characteristics with a source solve the requested flow directly, independently of this notational issue. Since the mass flow is proportional to m2, its zero value remains zero. Starting at a reference scale μ∗​, solve
dlogμdλ(μ)​=β​(λ(μ)),dlogμdh(μ)​=γ​h​(λ(μ))h(μ).
(33)
Then
F(λ(μ),0,h(μ);μ)=F(λ(μ∗​),0,h(μ∗​);μ∗​),​
(34)
with any physical volume or source-shape data retained. Where β​=0, the running quantities are determined by
logμ∗​μ​=∫λref​λ(μ)​β​(g)dg​,h(μ)=href​exp(∫λref​λ(μ)​β​(g)γ​h​(g)​dg).
(35)
These equations transport matching data at one scale to any other scale on the same flow trajectory. At a zero of the beta function use the differential equations themselves, rather than divide by zero.
For the uniform-source density, set y=h/μ3 and m2=0. At d=4 the corrected dimensionless equation and its local characteristic solution are
[β(λ)∂λ​+(γh​(λ)−3)y∂y​+4]f(λ,y)=0,
(36)
f(λ,y)=exp[−∫λβ(g)4dg​]Ψ(yexp[−∫λβ(g)γh​(g)−3​dg]).
(37)
The arbitrary function Ψ is fixed by boundary/matching data. If the volume is retained as a dimensionless argument v=μ4V4​, the full dimensionless functional obeys the same transport operator with 4v∂v​ in place of the additive 4 term; extensivity makes the two statements equivalent. Massless zero-momentum source expansions can also have infrared singularities, so this RG solution is formal until an infrared prescription or a suitable background expansion is chosen.
An ultraviolet fixed point is a zero of the beta functions approached by the dimensionless couplings as μ→∞. Near a single-coupling fixed point λ∗​, perturbations obey δλ∝μβ′(λ∗​), so β′<0 gives attraction in that direction. In four-dimensional quartic scalar theory, the positive one-loop beta function makes positive small coupling grow toward the ultraviolet: 1/λ(μ)=1/λ(μ∗​)−[3/(16π2)]log(μ/μ∗​). This exhibits a perturbative Landau pole, not an interacting ultraviolet fixed point. A perturbative result does not exclude hypothetical strong-coupling behavior beyond its regime.

The gauge-invariant Yang-Mills action integrates over physically equivalent configurations along each gauge orbit. At the quadratic level its gauge-field kernel has longitudinal zero directions: in momentum space Kμν​(k)=−k2ημν​+kμ​kν​ satisfies Kμν​kν=0. It therefore has no inverse from which to construct a gauge-boson propagator. Gauge fixing both removes the redundant orbit integration and supplies an invertible quadratic operator away from the ordinary physical propagator poles.
For the covariant condition ∂μAμ​=0, the Faddeev-Popov determinant is that of ∂μDμ​. Its representation by odd Faddeev-Popov ghost fields and antighost fields produces the displayed ghost term. In a nonabelian theory Dμ​ depends on the gauge field, so the determinant is not an irrelevant constant: ghost interactions must be retained. The even Nakanishi-Lautrup field b writes gauge fixing in a form with off-shell BRS symmetry. For ξ=0, its algebraic equation is b=−∂⋅A/ξ, giving −(∂⋅A)2/(2ξ) after elimination. At ξ=0 it imposes the gauge condition as a Lagrange multiplier.
For the nilpotence proof, write the adjoint bracket as [X,Y]a=fabcXbYc, with coefficients in a Grassmann algebra. If ∣X∣,∣Y∣ are their parities, this is a graded Lie bracket:
[X,Y]=−(−1)∣X∣∣Y∣[Y,X].
(38)
Thus two odd fields have a symmetric bracket, in particular [c,c] need not vanish. The transformations become sAμ​=Dμ​c, sc=−[c,c]/2, scˉ=−b, sb=0, and s obeys the left graded Leibniz rule.
Because Dμ​ is even, Dμ​[c,c]=[Dμ​c,c]+[c,Dμ​c]=2[Dμ​c,c]. Varying both the field and the connection in Dμ​c now gives
s2Aμ​=s(Dμ​c)=Dμ​(sc)+[sAμ​,c]=−21​Dμ​[c,c]+[Dμ​c,c]=0.
(39)
For the ghost,
s2c=−21​{[sc,c]−[c,sc]}=−[sc,c]=21​[[c,c],c]=0.
(40)
The last equality is the graded Jacobi identity applied to three copies of the odd field c. In components the remaining expression is 21​fabcfbdecdcecc; its coefficient is antisymmetrized over c,d,e, and the ordinary Jacobi identity for the structure constants makes that antisymmetrization zero. Finally s2cˉ=−sb=0 and s2b=0. Hence
s2=0on all four fields, without field equations.​
(41)
This is off-shell nilpotence of the Yang-Mills BRST quartet.
To verify invariance of the complete Lagrangian, sFμν​=[Fμν​,c], obtained from [Dμ​,Dν​]c=[Fμν​,c]. Thus s(Fμνa​Fμνa)=0 because the product of the two curvature components is symmetric in their color labels while fabc is antisymmetric. The remaining terms vary as
s(ba∂μAμa​)s(ξbaba/2)s(cˉa∂μ(Dμ​c)a)​=ba∂μ(Dμ​c)a,=0,=−ba∂μ(Dμ​c)a−cˉa∂μs(Dμ​c)a.​
(42)
The last term vanishes by the already proved s2A=0, and the first and third terms cancel pointwise. Therefore
sL=0.​
(43)
Equivalently, the covariant gauge-fixing density as a BRST variation is sΨ with Ψ=−cˉa(∂⋅Aa+ξba/2). The graded sign from moving s past cˉ produces the positive ghost term, and s2Ψ=0 proves its invariance. No integration-by-parts qualification is needed for the density as written.
Let Q=QBRS​ be the BRS charge. The physical state space is its BRST cohomology, usually at ghost number zero:
Hphys​=kerQ/imQ.​
(44)
Nilpotence ensures imQ⊆kerQ. A state Q∣χ⟩ is orthogonal to every closed state because ⟨ψ∣Q∣χ⟩=⟨Qψ∣χ⟩=0, and its norm is ⟨χ∣Q2∣χ⟩=0. Thus adding an exact state changes neither physical inner products nor amplitudes. Self-adjointness here is relative to the stated indefinite Hermitian form; a nonzero self-adjoint nilpotent operator could not exist on a positive-definite state space. Positivity must be proved on the quotient, not assumed on the gauge/ghost space.
Fix a color label and a nonzero momentum k, and take the usual nontrivial asymptotic charge, α=0. Nilpotence on a vector state gives Q∣c⟩=0, because at least one component of k is nonzero. Nilpotence on the antighost state then gives
0=Q2∣cˉ⟩=αˉαkμkμ​∣c⟩,k2=0.​
(45)
The two coefficients are complex conjugates as required by self-adjointness: using the given ghost pairing,
⟨Aν​∣Q∣cˉ⟩=αˉkν​=⟨QAν​∣cˉ⟩=α∗kν​.
(46)
Thus the antighost-to-vector coefficient is α∗, as the printed bar notation indicates. These conditions are the massless shell condition and the consistency of the charge with the inner products.
A general one-particle vector representative is ∣ε⟩=εμ∣Aμ​⟩. It is closed precisely when kμ​εμ=0. The vector state kμ∣Aμ​⟩=Q∣cˉ⟩/αˉ is exact, so representatives obey
ε∼ε+zk.
(47)
The ghost state is also exact: choose vμ with αv⋅k=1, then Q(vμ∣Aμ​⟩)=∣c⟩. The antighost state is not closed because its image is a nonzero longitudinal vector. More explicitly, closure of a general sum of vector, ghost and antighost states forces the antighost coefficient to vanish and the vector polarization to be orthogonal to k; its ghost component disappears in the quotient. Thus the full one-particle cohomology already reduces to
{ε:k⋅ε=0}/span{k}.​
(48)
With the mostly-plus Minkowski metric, choose a Lorentz frame with kμ=(ω,0,0,ω), ω>0. Closure gives ε3=ε0, and the norm is
⟨ε∣ε⟩=−∣ε0∣2+∣ε1∣2+∣ε2∣2+∣ε3∣2=∣ε1∣2+∣ε2∣2.
(49)
Subtracting a multiple of k sets ε0=ε3=0. Consequently positive one-particle BRST cohomology consists of two transverse positive-norm polarizations per color, represented by ∣A1​⟩,∣A2​⟩. A nonzero class has strictly positive norm; the zero-norm longitudinal direction was precisely the exact direction removed.
The qualifications k=0 and α=0 are essential to this one-particle argument. The zero-momentum sector requires separate treatment, and the trivial-charge case cannot yield the asserted removal of negative-norm vector states from the stated relations alone. Abstract nilpotence and self-adjointness by themselves do not guarantee positive cohomology.
