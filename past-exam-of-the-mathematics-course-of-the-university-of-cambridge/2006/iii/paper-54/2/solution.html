Use ημν​=diag(1,−1,−1,−1), σμ=(I,σ) and σˉμ=(I,−σ), where σ are the Pauli matrices. The translation and Lorentz generators obey the Poincare algebra
[Pμ​,Pν​][Mμν​,Pρ​][Mμν​,Mρσ​]​=0,=i(ηνρ​Pμ​−ημρ​Pν​),=i(ηνρ​Mμσ​−ημρ​Mνσ​−ηνσ​Mμρ​+ημσ​Mνρ​).​
(9)
One can derive the additional relations by realizing the supercharges as differential operators on superspace. With left Grassmann derivatives and Pμ​=i∂μ​, take
Qα​=∂θα​−iσαβ˙​μ​θˉβ˙​∂μ​,Qˉ​β˙​​=−∂θˉβ˙​​+iθασαβ˙​μ​∂μ​.
(10)
Using {∂θα​,θβ}=δα​β, the two mixed derivative-coordinate terms each give iσαβ˙​μ​∂μ​. The remaining terms cancel by Grassmann anticommutation. Equal-chirality terms cancel in the same way. Hence
{Qα​,Qˉ​β˙​​}=2σαβ˙​μ​Pμ​,{Qα​,Qβ​}={Qˉ​α˙​,Qˉ​β˙​​}=0.​
(11)
All coefficients are independent of x, so direct differentiation gives [Pμ​,Qα​]=[Pμ​,Qˉ​α˙​]=0. This also shows that supercharges commute with translations and preserve the four-momentum of a state.
For the Lorentz generators, define the left-handed spinor matrices
Sμν​=4i​(σμ​σˉν​−σν​σˉμ​).
(12)
The identity σμσˉν+σνσˉμ=2ημνI gives their Lorentz commutation relations. Acting on the spinor superspace coordinates and their dual derivatives then gives, in a column-component convention,
[Mμν​,Qα​]=−(Sμν​)α​βQβ​,[Mμν​,Qˉ​α˙​]=(Sμν∗​)α˙​β˙​Qˉ​β˙​​.​
(13)
Here dotted index values are identified with the conjugate undotted values, and Qˉ​α˙​=Qα†​. The second formula also follows directly by taking the adjoint of the first, using Hermitian Mμν​. For example, S12​=σ3/2, so [J3​,Q1​]=−Q1​/2 and [J3​,Q2​]=Q2​/2 for J3​=M12​. This explicit component convention avoids reversing signs by mixing state matrices with operator-component matrices. Covariance of the mixed anticommutator follows by applying these matrices to σρPρ​.
These are the minimal Super-Poincaré algebra relations. In the ordinary particle algebra a possible scalar term in {Qα​,Qβ​} would be proportional to ϵαβ​, but an anticommutator is symmetric in its two labels; with only one supersymmetry such a central term vanishes. Extended algebras can instead use an antisymmetric internal index tensor. Tensor charges associated with extended objects are not part of this minimal particle algebra.
For a positive-energy massless supermultiplet, choose pμ=(E,0,0,E) with E>0, so pμ​=(E,0,0,−E) and
{Qα​,Qβ†​}=2σαβ˙​μ​pμ​=(00​04E​).
(14)
Positivity of the Hilbert-space norm implies that both Q1​ and Q1†​ annihilate every state: their squared norms sum to zero. Set
a=4E​Q2​​,a†=4E​Q2†​​,{a,a†}=1,a2=(a†)2=0.
(15)
For momentum along the positive third axis, helicity is h=J3​. The Lorentz commutators give [h,a]=a/2 and [h,a†]=−a†/2. Choose a highest-helicity Clifford vacuum ∣λ⟩ with a∣λ⟩=0. Its partner a†∣λ⟩ has unit norm if the first state does and has helicity λ−1/2. No further state is generated because (a†)2=0. Therefore
an irreducible finite-helicity massless N=1 multiplet has helicities (λ,λ−21​).​
(16)
The two states have opposite fermion parity. A CPT completion of a supermultiplet adds helicities (−λ,−λ+1/2) and conjugate internal charges when necessary. Taking λ=1/2 produces a complex scalar and a Weyl spinor after CPT completion; taking λ=1 produces the massless vector multiplet. This construction assumes ordinary finite-helicity particle representations, rather than continuous-spin representations.
