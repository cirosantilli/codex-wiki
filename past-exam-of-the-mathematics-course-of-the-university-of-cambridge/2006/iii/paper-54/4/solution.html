First complexify the Clifford algebra in D=2n spacetime dimensions. Multiplying appropriate generators by i converts the signature to the Euclidean form {Γa​,Γb​}=2δab​ without changing the complex module dimension. Pair the generators into
bj​=21​(Γ2j−1​+iΓ2j​),bj†​=21​(Γ2j−1​−iΓ2j​),j=1,…,n.
(30)
The Clifford algebra relations give
{bi​,bj​}={bi†​,bj†​}=0,{bi​,bj†​}=δij​.
(31)
The commuting occupation operators Nj​=bj†​bj​ have eigenvalues zero or one. Choose a nonzero state killed by every bj​; repeated annihilation reaches such a state in any finite irreducible module. Acting with distinct creation operators produces the 2n occupation states
(b1†​)r1​⋯(bn†​)rn​∣0⟩,rj​∈{0,1}.
(32)
Their distinct occupation patterns make them linearly independent, and their span is invariant under every Clifford generator. Irreducibility therefore makes it the entire Dirac module. This derives the even-dimensional Clifford spinor dimension:
dimC​SDirac​=2n=2D/2.​
(33)
The chirality matrix is a phase-adjusted product of all 2n generators. It squares to one, anticommutes with each generator and acts as occupation parity up to an overall sign. Its two eigenspaces each have 2n−1 states. The even Clifford algebra, and hence the connected spin group, preserves them, giving irreducible Weyl spinors of complex dimension 2D/2−1. A Majorana spinor imposes a reality condition only in signatures where it exists; simultaneous Majorana and Weyl conditions are not available in every even dimension.
In Lorentzian ten dimensions, the complex Dirac module has dimension 32, and a Weyl module dimension 16. A Majorana-Weyl spinor is allowed and has 16 real components before its equation of motion. The massless Dirac equation leaves eight physical gaugino polarizations. A ten-dimensional massless gauge field also has 10−2=8 physical polarizations. Thus ten-dimensional super Yang-Mills theory has eight bosonic and eight fermionic states per gauge generator, with sixteen real supercharges.
For dimensional reduction, take a flat six-torus with periodic fermions and retain fields independent of its coordinates. Split AM​ into Aμ​ and six internal components Am​, m=4,…,9. The latter are four-dimensional real adjoint scalar fields. Under the spin groups of the two factors, a ten-dimensional chiral spinor decomposes as
16C​⟶(2,4)⊕(2,4)
(34)
for Spin(1,3)×Spin(6), with Spin(6)≃SU(4). The Majorana condition relates these conjugate pieces, leaving four independent four-dimensional Weyl spinors. The supersymmetry parameters reduce in exactly the same fashion: sixteen real charges are four four-dimensional supersymmetries. The resulting field content is
Aμ​,4 adjoint Weyl fermions,6 real adjoint scalars:N=4 Yang-Mills.​
(35)
The on-shell counts are 2+6=8 bosonic and 4×2=8 fermionic states, as required. The internal spin group supplies the SU(4) R-symmetry of the reduced action.
To derive its scalar potential, use Hermitian gauge matrices and a positive trace inner product, with
S10​=−4g102​1​∫d10xtr(FMN​FMN),FMN​=∂M​AN​−∂N​AM​−i[AM​,AN​].
(36)
If the internal volume is V6​, integration gives g42​=g102​/V6​. Since internal derivatives vanish,
Fμm​=∂μ​Am​−i[Aμ​,Am​],Fmn​=−i[Am​,An​].
(37)
With mostly-minus spacetime signature, Fμm​Fμm has an extra minus sign and the double sum over mixed indices gives a factor two. Rescale Aμ​=g4​aμ​ and Am​=g4​Xm​ to canonical fields. The resulting bosonic Lagrangian is
L4​=−41​tr(fμν​fμν)+21​∑m​tr(Dμ​Xm​DμXm​)+4g42​​∑m,n​tr[Xm​,Xn​]2,
(38)
where Dμ​Xm​=∂μ​Xm​−ig4​[aμ​,Xm​]. A commutator of Hermitian matrices is anti-Hermitian, so the last term is minus a nonnegative scalar potential:
V=4g42​​m,n∑​tr([Xm​,Xn​]†[Xm​,Xn​])≥0.​
(39)
The sum is over ordered internal pairs; a sum over m<n would instead have coefficient g42​/2. Trace normalization merely changes the matched gauge-coupling convention.
The origin attains zero potential, so the global minima are exactly
[Xm​,Xn​]=0for every m,n.​
(40)
For a compact connected gauge group, a commuting tuple is simultaneously conjugate into a Cartan subalgebra. Write Xm​=∑a=1r​xma​Ha​, where r is the rank. Every choice of the 6r real numbers xma​ has zero potential. After the residual Weyl group identifications, the classical commuting-scalar vacua of four-dimensional N=4 Yang-Mills theory form (R6)r/W in the four-dimensional theory. For SU(N) one may describe them as six simultaneous diagonal matrices, with tracelessness and permutations of their common eigenvalue labels. Varying these eigenvalues changes gauge-invariant configurations while leaving V=0, proving the existence of genuine flat directions of a scalar potential, not just gauge-equivalent directions. Generic values break the gauge group to its maximal torus; coincident eigenvalue vectors produce enhanced unbroken gauge symmetry. If one keeps a finite compact torus rather than the four-dimensional limit, large gauge transformations add periodic identifications of the Wilson-line coordinates.
