For an arbitrary subgroup A, the full inverse image of its image is AK, not generally A. In fact, θ(g)∈θ(A) exactly when g=ak for some a∈A,k∈K. Since K is normal, AK is a subgroup. By the subgroup correspondence for a surjective group homomorphism,
[H:θ(A)]=[G:AK].
(43)
Multiplicativity of finite index gives
[G:A]=[G:AK][AK:A].
(44)
The map k(K∩A)↦kA bijects the left cosets of K∩A in K with those of A in AK, since AK=KA. Consequently
[H:θ(A)]=[K:K∩A]i​.​
(45)
This need not equal i. For example, take θ:Z→Z/2Z to be reduction modulo 2 and A=3Z. Then [G:A]=3, but θ(A)=H has index one. If A contains K, however, the denominator is one and
K≤A ⟹ [H:θ(A)]=i.​
(46)
For finite i, equality holds exactly when K≤A.
