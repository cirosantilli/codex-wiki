A precise sufficient interpretation of the regularity assumption is f=0, f∈H1(R) in the first-order Sobolev space and xf∈L2(R); in particular, nonzero Schwartz functions are admissible. We use the Plancherel theorem, in the normalization ∥f​∥22​=2π∥f∥22​, together with its derivative identity f′​(λ)=iλf​(λ). Both identities hold in L2 for f∈H1, by extending their identities for Schwartz functions. Thus the frequency factor is
∫∣f​(λ)∣2dλ∫λ2∣f​(λ)∣2dλ​=∥f∥22​∥f′∥22​​.

For a Schwartz function, integration by parts gives ∥f∥22​=−2Re∫xf′(x)f(x)​dx. This remains true under the stated assumptions: insert a smooth cutoff χR​(x)=χ(x/R) into the derivative of x∣f∣2, where χ is one near zero and compactly supported. Integration gives
∫(χR​+xχR′​)∣f∣2dx=−2Re∫χR​xf′f​dx.

The cutoff-derivative term tends to zero, since xχR′​ is bounded independently of R and supported in a tail. The other terms converge by dominated convergence and the integrability of ∣f′∣∣xf∣, furnished by the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality. Hence
∥f∥22​=−2Re⟨f′,xf⟩≤2∣⟨f′,xf⟩∣≤2∥f′∥2​∥xf∥2​.

After squaring and using the Plancherel theorem, we obtain the uncentred Fourier uncertainty principle:
∫∣f(x)∣2dx∫x2∣f(x)∣2dx​∫∣f​(λ)∣2dλ∫λ2∣f​(λ)∣2dλ​≥41​.​

If equality holds, both displayed inequalities must be equalities. The equality condition in the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality gives f′=cxf almost everywhere for a constant c∈C; the real-part inequality and the identity for ∥f∥22​ then force c=−a with a>0 real. The weak derivative equation has the solution f(x)=Ce−ax2/2: locally multiply by eax2/2 to obtain a function whose weak derivative is zero, hence a constant. Conversely, for this Gaussian function the normalized second moments are 1/(2a) in position and a/2 in angular frequency, whose product is 1/4. Therefore
Equality holds exactly for f(x)=Ce−ax2/2,a>0, C∈C∖{0}.​
(25)
These are raw second moments, rather than variances about their respective means. Consequently translations and nonzero frequency modulations of the Gaussian function are not equality cases here; the equality case of the Heisenberg uncertainty relation for centred variances has those additional freedoms.
