Maximizing the posterior density itself gives a posterior mode, not generally a maximum-likelihood estimate; the two coincide for a flat prior on a region containing the maximum. A prior excluding candidate maximizers cannot recover them through its posterior output. Boundary behavior also matters: for k=0, the likelihood is decreasing in θ, so the unrestricted maximum is at θ=0.
