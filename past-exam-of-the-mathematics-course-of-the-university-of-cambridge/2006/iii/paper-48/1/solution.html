Use Minkowski spacetime with gμν​=diag(1,−1,−1,−1) and units ℏ=c=1. The transformation rotates the two components in their internal plane. In particular,
δ(ϕ12​+ϕ22​)=2α(ϕ1​ϕ2​−ϕ2​ϕ1​)=0,δ(∂μ​ϕ1​∂μϕ1​+∂μ​ϕ2​∂μϕ2​)=0.
(1)
Thus both terms in the Lagrangian density are invariant: this is an internal rotation symmetry of two real scalar fields, rather than a transformation of spacetime. The equal masses are essential to this symmetry.
The Noether theorem gives the Noether current
jμ=∑k=12​∂(∂μ​ϕk​)∂L​αδϕk​​=ϕ2​∂μϕ1​−ϕ1​∂μϕ2​.
(2)
Indeed, the cross terms in its divergence cancel, and the two Klein-Gordon equations imply
∂μ​jμ=ϕ2​□ϕ1​−ϕ1​□ϕ2​=−m2ϕ2​ϕ1​+m2ϕ1​ϕ2​=0.
(3)
For fields decaying sufficiently at spatial infinity, there is no outward current flux. The conserved Noether charge is therefore
Q=∫d3x(ϕ2​ϕ˙​1​−ϕ1​ϕ˙​2​).​
(4)
In canonical quantization, the canonical momenta are πk​=ϕ˙​k​, with equal-time canonical commutation relations
[ϕk​(x),πl​(y)]=iδkl​δ3(x−y),[ϕk​,ϕl​]=[πk​,πl​]=0.
(5)
Consequently Q=∫d3x(π1​ϕ2​−π2​ϕ1​). Operators of different components commute, so this expression is Hermitian without an ordering correction. Write ∫p​=∫d3p/(2π)3, Ep​=p2+m2​ and akp​=apk​. Substituting the oscillator expansions and using the Dirac delta function from the spatial integral gives
Q=−2i​∫p​[(a1p​−a1,−p†​)(a2,−p​+a2p†​)−(a2p​−a2,−p†​)(a1,−p​+a1p†​)].
(6)
The terms with two annihilation operators cancel after p↦−p; the terms with two creation operators cancel in the same way. In the remaining terms use
[akp​,alq†​]=(2π)3δkl​δ3(p−q).
(7)
There is no commutator constant between the distinct components. The result is already in normal ordering:
Q=−i∫p​(a2p†​a1p​−a1p†​a2p​).​
(8)
For the charged oscillator basis of a scalar doublet, compute
[Q,a1p†​]=−ia2p†​,[Q,a2p†​]=ia1p†​,b±p†​=2​a1p†​∓ia2p†​​.
(9)
It follows that [Q,b±p†​]=±b±p†​. The Fock vacuum satisfies Q∣0⟩=0, so b+p†​∣0⟩ has charge +1, and b−p†​∣0⟩ has charge −1. To obtain a normalized one-particle state, replace the sharp momentum by ∫p​f(p)b±p†​∣0⟩, where ∫p​∣f(p)∣2=1. The charge is unchanged. Equivalently,
Q=∫p​(b+p†​b+p​−b−p†​b−p​).
(10)
The generator convention is δϕk​=iα[Q,ϕk​], which reproduces the original rotation signs.
For stability of a two-scalar quartic potential, the relevant potential energy density is
V=2m2​(ϕ12​+ϕ22​)+V4​,V4​=λ(ϕ12​−ϕ22​)2+2(λ+μ)ϕ12​ϕ22​.
(11)
Along either axis boundedness requires λ≥0; along ϕ1​=ϕ2​ it requires λ+μ≥0. Conversely the displayed sum is nonnegative whenever both conditions hold. With m2>0 the origin is then the unique global minimum, including the equality cases. Hence the stable-vacuum conditions are
λ≥0,μ≥−λ.​
(12)
Strict positivity of the quartic term away from the origin would instead require λ>0 and μ>−λ. That stronger condition is unnecessary for a positive mass term. If m=0, the same non-strict conditions give boundedness, but the equality cases have flat directions and do not give an isolated classical vacuum.
Finally, the interaction changes the variation of the potential energy by
δV4​=4α(λ−μ)ϕ1​ϕ2​(ϕ12​−ϕ22​).
(13)
The interacting Euler-Lagrange equations consequently give
∂μ​jμ=−4(λ−μ)ϕ1​ϕ2​(ϕ12​−ϕ22​).
(14)
Thus the same Noether charge is conserved for all field configurations precisely when μ=λ. Then V4​=λ(ϕ12​+ϕ22​)2 preserves the internal rotation symmetry of two real scalar fields; imposing stability additionally requires λ≥0.
