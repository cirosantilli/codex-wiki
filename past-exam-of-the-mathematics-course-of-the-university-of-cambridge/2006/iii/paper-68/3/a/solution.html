The first characteristic polynomial factors as
ρ(w)=(w−1)(w2−2αw+1).
(11)
Also ρ(1)=0 and ρ′(1)=σ(1)=2(1−α), so the order conditions for a linear multistep method hold for every α. Convergence of the full linear multistep method recurrence requires, in addition, the root condition for a multistep method: all polynomial roots of ρ lie in the closed unit disk and its polynomial roots on the unit circle are simple.
For −1<α<1, write α=cosθ with 0<θ<π. The other polynomial roots are e±iθ, distinct from each other and from 1, so the root condition for a multistep method holds. For ∣α∣>1, the two quadratic polynomial roots are real and reciprocal, and one has modulus greater than one. At α=1, ρ=(w−1)3; at α=−1, ρ=(w−1)(w+1)2. Both endpoints violate simplicity. Consistency of a numerical method plus zero-stability therefore gives
−1<α<1.​
(12)
The conclusion applies to the recurrence as printed, including its starting data. Canceling common factors is not innocuous: at α=1 the polynomials contain (w−1)2, and at α=−13/5 they contain w+5. The reduced recurrences exclude parasitic solutions of the original one. This is the common-factor cancellation defect in a multistep recurrence, so those parameter values are not added to the interval.
