Put h=Δx, k=Δt, with fixed positive μ=k/h. Insert a smooth exact solution and divide the residual by 2k:
2ku(t+k)−u(t−k)​−2hu(x+h,y)−u(x−h,y)+u(x,y+h)−u(x,y−h)​.
(7)
Taylor expansion gives
ut​−ux​−uy​+6k2​uttt​−6h2​(uxxx​+uyyy​)+O(k4+h4).
(8)
The differential equation cancels the leading part, leaving normalized local truncation error O(k2+h2). Since uttt​=(∂x​+∂y​)3u contains mixed derivatives, there is no fixed positive Courant number canceling this leading error for every smooth solution. Thus the method is second order in time and space. Global second-order convergence also requires stability and initial values at both time levels accurate enough to retain this order.
