A frailty model represents unobserved individual heterogeneity by a random effect in the hazard function. In a proportional frailty model, an individual with frailty random variable U=u has hazard uh0​(t). The normalization EU=1 identifies the scale of the baseline hazard: without it, multiplying all frailties by a constant and dividing h0​ by that constant would leave all individual hazards unchanged.
Let H0​(t)=∫0t​h0​(v)dv. Conditional survival follows by integrating the hazard function:
S(t∣U=u)=exp(−∫0t​uh0​(v)dv)=e−uH0​(t).
(15)
Taking the expectation over the frailty law yields
S(t)=∫0∞​e−uH0​(t)g(u)du=g​(H0​(t)),g​(s)=∫0∞​e−sug(u)du.​
(16)
This is the Laplace transform of the frailty density evaluated at the cumulative hazard. More generally, the same calculation integrates against a probability measure, allowing atoms. It averages conditional survival rather than substituting EU into its exponent. Indeed, the frailty distribution among survivors is proportional to e−uH0​(t)g(u), giving population hazard
hpop​(t)=h0​(t)E[e−UH0​(t)]E[Ue−UH0​(t)]​.
(17)
Survival progressively selects smaller frailties, so the population hazard need not be the baseline or even retain the individual's proportional structure.
For the cure model, first take 0≤π<1. The necessary mean-one frailty law and baseline hazard are
Pr(U=0)=π,Pr(U=1−π1​)=1−π,h0​(t)=(1−π)h∗​(t).​
(18)
Then EU=π⋅0+(1−π)/(1−π)=1. A cured person's conditional hazard is zero, while a susceptible person's conditional hazard is h0​/(1−π)=h∗​. Taking U to be zero or one while leaving h0​=h∗​ would instead have mean frailty 1−π and violate the required normalization.
The frailty law is the measure
g(du)=πδ0​(du)+(1−π)δ1/(1−π)​(du),
(19)
where the δ terms are Dirac measures. This is not an ordinary Lebesgue density; the cure construction requires permitting a discrete frailty law. Its Laplace transform is
g​(s)=π+(1−π)e−s/(1−π).
(20)
Writing H∗​(t)=∫0t​h∗​(v)dv, we have H0​=(1−π)H∗​, and therefore
S(t)=g​(H0​(t))=π+(1−π)e−H∗​(t).​
(21)
If H∗​(t)→∞, the susceptible class eventually experiences the event and S(t)→π. If H∗​(∞)<∞, the limiting survival is larger than π because some susceptible subjects also never experience the event. At π=1, everyone is cured: set h0​≡0 and, for example, U≡1 to retain mean one, giving S≡1 without using the singular formula 1/(1−π).
