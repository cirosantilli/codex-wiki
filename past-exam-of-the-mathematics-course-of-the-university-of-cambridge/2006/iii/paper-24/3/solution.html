For a locally small category C, the covariant Yoneda lemma gives a bijection
Nat(C(c,−),H)≅H(c),τ⟼τc​(1c​),​
(9)
natural in c and the functor H:C→Set. The inverse sends x∈H(c) to the natural transformation whose component at d maps u:c→d to H(u)x. Naturality of τ at u forces this formula, proving both inverseness and uniqueness.
Write E=∫F for the category of elements of F. Its objects are (c,x) with x∈F(c), and an arrow (c,x)→(d,y) is an arrow u:c→d satisfying F(u)x=y.
Given an object α:P→F of the slice category [C,Set]/F, define
Φ(α)(c,x)={p∈P(c):αc​(p)=x}.
(10)
An arrow u:(c,x)→(d,y) acts by the restriction of P(u); naturality of α makes this restriction well-defined. A slice morphism v:P→Q restricts to a map between the corresponding fibers and thus gives a natural transformation on E.
Conversely, given H:E→Set, set
PH​(c)=∐x∈F(c)​H(c,x),PH​(u)(x,h)=(F(u)x,H(u)h),
(11)
and let αH​:PH​→F be projection to x. The functor laws and naturality follow from those for F and H. On morphisms use the maps on each summand. Taking a fiber of this disjoint union recovers H(c,x) canonically. Taking the disjoint union of the fibers of α recovers P(c) by (x,p)↦p, naturally in c and in the slice object. These are inverse natural isomorphisms, proving
[C,Set]/F≃[E,Set].​
(12)
This is the slice of a set-valued functor category construction.
Now assume C is a small category, so E is small. Define a diagram in a category
D:Eop⟶[C,Set],D(c,x)=C(c,−).
(13)
For u:(c,x)→(d,y) in E, its reverse arrow acts by precomposition C(d,−)→C(c,−). There is a cocone to F with component g↦F(g)x. To verify its colimit property directly, use pointwise colimits of sets. At b, a colimit element has a representative (c,x,g:c→b), and the induced map to F(b) sends it to F(g)x. This map is surjective, since z∈F(b) is represented by (b,z,1b​). Moreover, the element-category arrow g:(c,x)→(b,F(g)x) gives precisely the colimit relation
[(c,x,g)]=[(b,F(g)x,1b​)].
(14)
Thus all representatives with the same image are equal in the colimit, proving injectivity. The resulting bijections are natural in b. We have proved the canonical colimit presentation of a covariant set-valued functor:
F≅colim(c,x)∈Eop​C(c,−).​
(15)
A Cartesian closed category has finite products in a category and an exponential object HG for each pair, representing maps from a product with G. In [C,Set], the terminal functor and binary products are computed pointwise. Define the exponential of covariant set-valued functors by
(HG)(c)=Nat(C(c,−)×G,H).
(16)
Smallness makes this a set. If u:c→d, precomposition with C(d,−)×G→C(c,−)×G defines (HG)(u). Functoriality is immediate from associativity of composition. Its evaluation map of an exponential object is
evc​(τ,z)=τc​(1c​,z).
(17)
For u:c→d, naturality of τ shows H(u)τc​(1c​,z)=τd​(u,G(u)z), which is exactly the naturality equation for evaluation.
Given θ:P×G→H, define its currying at p∈P(c) by
(θc​(p))b​(f,z)=θb​(P(f)p,z),f:c→b,z∈G(b).
(18)
Naturality of θ proves that this is a natural transformation in b, and its compatibility with arrows c→d proves that θ:P→HG is natural. Evaluation recovers θ by setting f=1c​. Conversely, for σ:P→HG, its naturality implies
(σc​(p))b​(f,z)=(σb​(P(f)p))b​(1b​,z),
(19)
so currying the evaluation composite recovers σ. These formulas give the required natural bijection, hence [C,Set] is Cartesian closed.
Finally, for every F the small category E=∫F has a Cartesian closed functor category by the same construction. The slice equivalence above transports its terminal object, products and exponentials to [C,Set]/F. The original functor category also has pointwise finite limits. Therefore it is locally Cartesian closed. Every construction here is explicit; no adjoint functor theorem is used.
