The Bloch vector representation of a qubit density matrix is ρ=21​(I+rx​X+ry​Y+rz​Z), where rj​=Tr(ρσj​) and ∣r∣≤1. Pure states have ∣r∣=1, while the origin represents the maximally mixed state I/2, not a pure state.
For the two preparations, direct evaluation of the Pauli matrices gives
r1​=(0,0,1),r2​=(−3​/2,0,−1/2).​
(1)
Their dot product is −1/2, so their Bloch vectors meet at angle 2π/3​, or 120 degrees. This is the angle between Bloch vectors; the angle determined by the modulus of the Hilbert-space overlap is different.
Completeness of the POVM requires E3​=I−E1​−E2​. In the computational basis, subtraction gives
E3​=(21​−63​​​−63​​61​​)=32​∣ϕ3​⟩⟨ϕ3​∣,∣ϕ3​⟩=23​​∣0⟩−21​∣1⟩.​
(2)
An overall phase of ϕ3​ is immaterial. The matrix has eigenvalues 2/3 and zero, so it is positive; together with E1​,E2​ it is a valid trine qubit POVM.
The Born rule gives Pr(j∣ρi​)=Tr(Ej​ρi​)=32​∣⟨ϕj​∣ψi​⟩∣2. The conditional probabilities are
ρ1​ρ2​​j=101/2​j=21/20​j=31/21/2​​.
(3)
Outcome one identifies ρ2​, since ϕ1​ is orthogonal to ψ1​; outcome two identifies ρ1​, since ϕ2​ is orthogonal to ψ2​. Outcome three is inconclusive. If Alice's prior probability for ρ1​ is q, the unconditional outcome probabilities are (1−q)/2, q/2, and 1/2 respectively. Because outcome three has the same likelihood for both preparations, it leaves the prior odds unchanged. Bob's conclusive identification succeeds with probability one half and is never wrong; he does not identify the state on the inconclusive trials. This is unambiguous quantum state discrimination.

A linear map Φ is a completely positive map if idR​⊗Φ sends every positive operator to a positive operator for every finite-dimensional auxiliary system R. Positivity of Φ alone tests only inputs without an auxiliary system and is weaker.
For the Kraus representation, let Ω≥0 be any operator on the auxiliary system and the input system. Then
(idR​⊗Φ)(Ω)=∑k​(IR​⊗Ak​)Ω(IR​⊗Ak†​).
(4)
For any vector v, its expectation is ∑k​⟨(IR​⊗Ak†​)v,Ω(IR​⊗Ak†​)v⟩≥0. Every amplification is therefore positive, proving the Kraus-form map is completely positive. No normalization condition on the Ak​ is needed for this positivity proof. To make it a deterministic quantum channel, one additionally requires ∑k​Ak†​Ak​=I; a physical outcome branch instead obeys ∑k​Ak†​Ak​≤I.
Transposition is positive on one system: for any positive ρ and any v, v†ρTv is the complex conjugate of vˉ†ρvˉ, hence is real and nonnegative. It fails complete positivity already for a qubit. Apply partial transposition to the second subsystem of the Bell state ∣Φ+⟩=(∣00⟩+∣11⟩)/2​. The resulting operator is
(id⊗T)(∣Φ+⟩⟨Φ+∣)=21​(∣00⟩⟨00∣+∣01⟩⟨10∣+∣10⟩⟨01∣+∣11⟩⟨11∣).
(5)
On the antisymmetric vector (∣01⟩−∣10⟩)/2​, its eigenvalue is −1/2. Thus the partial transpose is not positive on this entangled input, and
T is positive but not completely positive in dimension at least two.​
(6)
The same witness embeds into any larger input dimension; in the exceptional one-dimensional case transposition is simply the identity.

Choose ∣0⟩ as the ground state and ∣1⟩ as the excited state. The relevant quantum channel is the amplitude damping channel. Its Kraus operators are
K0​=(10​01−p​​),K1​=(00​p​0​)=p​∣0⟩⟨1∣.​
(7)
They satisfy K0†​K0​+K1†​K1​=I. The K0​ branch corresponds to no emitted photon: the ground-state amplitude is unchanged and the excited-state amplitude is attenuated. The K1​ branch corresponds to photon emission and transition to the ground state. These are unnormalized branch states; the squared norm or matrix trace of each branch gives its probability.

Model the environment's vacuum and one-photon states by ∣0E​⟩,∣1E​⟩. A unitary dilation of amplitude damping acts on the initially vacant environment as
U∣0,0E​⟩=∣0,0E​⟩,U∣1,0E​⟩=1−p​∣1,0E​⟩+p​∣0,1E​⟩.
(8)
These images are orthonormal. One unitary completion takes U∣0,1E​⟩=−p​∣1,0E​⟩+1−p​∣0,1E​⟩ and fixes ∣1,1E​⟩. Taking environment matrix elements ⟨kE​∣U∣0E​⟩ gives K0​,K1​.
Evolve ρ⊗∣0E​⟩⟨0E​∣ by U and take the partial trace over the environment. Orthogonality of the two environment records removes the cross-branch terms, giving Ap​(ρ)=K0​ρK0†​+K1​ρK1†​. Entrywise,
Ap​(ρ)=(ρ00​+pρ11​1−p​ρ10​​1−p​ρ01​(1−p)ρ11​​).​
(9)
Excited population is transferred to the ground state, while coherence is reduced by the square root of the survival probability.

Successive uses of the same quantum channel mean coupling to a fresh vacuum environment each time, or resetting the environment between uses. They do not mean repeatedly applying the dilation unitary to one unreinitialized two-state environment. Direct composition gives
Ap2​(ρ)=(ρ00​+(2p−p2)ρ11​(1−p)ρ10​​(1−p)ρ01​(1−p)2ρ11​​).
(10)
Inductively, with qn​=1−(1−p)n,
Apn​(ρ)=(ρ00​+qn​ρ11​(1−p)n/2ρ10​​(1−p)n/2ρ01​(1−p)nρ11​​).​
(11)
For 0<p≤1, this tends to the pure ground state ∣0⟩⟨0∣, whose Von Neumann entropy is zero. For p=0, the channel is the identity: the state remains ρ and its Von Neumann entropy remains S(ρ). Thus the repeated amplitude damping limit must include this no-decay exception.
A zero-Von Neumann entropy output means there is no uncertainty about the final atomic state. It does not mean that the receiver has gained information about the initial preparation: all initial states have the same limiting output, so their distinguishability has been erased from the atom. Under the global unitary evolution, information is carried into the environment. Without observing an emission record, this is a nonselective dissipative process, not an inference about which input was sent. Indeed, starting from the excited pure state, one intermediate output has Von Neumann entropy equal to the binary entropy of p, which is positive for 0<p<1; the atom's Von Neumann entropy need not decrease monotonically during the approach to the final ground state.

A unital quantum channel preserves the identity operator: Φ(I)=I. In equal input and output dimensions, it therefore preserves the maximally mixed state. For amplitude damping channel,
Ap​(I)=K0​K0†​+K1​K1†​=(1+p0​01−p​)=I+pZ.
(12)
Hence the channel is not unital for p>0; it is unital only in the identity case p=0. Trace preservation involves the opposite products Ki†​Ki​ and does not imply unitality.

Let ej​ be the bit string with one in position j and zero elsewhere, and use the standard convention Y=iXZ. On the computational basis, the single-site Pauli operators act as
Xj​∣x⟩=∣x⊕ej​⟩,Zj​∣x⟩=(−1)xj​∣x⟩,Yj​∣x⟩=i(−1)xj​∣x⊕ej​⟩.​
(13)
The identity leaves the basis state unchanged. A tensor-product Pauli operator applies these actions at each occupied site; operators at different sites commute. Their linear span is the full operator algebra, so correcting their span handles coherent combinations as well as random Pauli errors.
Let P project onto the code C, with orthonormal logical basis ∣iL​⟩. A set E={Ea​} is correctable exactly when the Knill--Laflamme condition holds:
PEa†​Eb​P=Cab​P,equivalently ⟨iL​∣Ea†​Eb​∣jL​⟩=Cab​δij​,​
(14)
for a matrix C independent of the logical indices. A set D is detectable when
PDP=cD​Pfor every D∈D.​
(15)
These conditions extend by linearity to the corresponding operator spans. Detection allows an error to act as a harmless scalar on the code; it need not assign a distinct syndrome to such an error.
For distinct Pauli representatives modulo phase, a nondegenerate quantum error-correcting code has orthogonal error-image subspaces, giving Cab​=δab​ because each Pauli is unitary. In the usual nondegenerate, or pure, detection convention, every nonidentity detectable Pauli has PDP=0, while PIP=P. More generally a nonsingular error-overlap matrix can be diagonalized and the resulting error operators rescaled to obtain orthogonal syndrome spaces; a singular overlap matrix expresses degeneracy of the specified independent error family.
For an [[n,k,d]] code, the quantum code distance is the smallest Pauli weight for which scalar compression fails. Hence every Pauli of weight at most d−1 is detectable. If Ea​,Eb​ each have weight at most t, their product Ea†​Eb​ has weight at most 2t. When 2t<d, its compression is scalar, so the Knill--Laflamme condition proves simultaneous correction of all such errors. Therefore
detectable weight: d−1,correctable unknown-location weight: ⌊2d−1​⌋.​
(16)
The integer part is understood when the printed half-distance is not an integer. Arbitrary operators on such subsets are covered by their Pauli expansion.
For errors confined to a known set of m positions, every pairwise product is still supported on that same set and has weight at most m, rather than 2m. Thus m<d supplies quantum erasure correction, with maximum guaranteed number
m=d−1.​
(17)
This guarantee cannot hold for every set of d positions: a minimum-weight undetectable Pauli has support in such a set, and the pair consisting of it and the identity violates the Knill--Laflamme condition. Particular larger location sets can sometimes be correctable; d−1 is the maximum uniform guarantee over all possible known sets.
For the Shor code, write ∣G±​⟩=(∣000⟩±∣111⟩)/2​. The logical codewords are ∣G+​⟩⊗3 and ∣G−​⟩⊗3. A phase flip Zj​ in one block interchanges G+​ and G−​ in that block, independently of its position within the block. Let B1​=X1​X2​X3​, B2​=X4​X5​X6​, B3​=X7​X8​X9​. Measure the two commuting stabilizer generators S1​=B1​B2​ and S2​=B2​B3​. Both are +1 on the entire logical code space. The Shor-code phase-flip syndrome is
affected blocknone123​S1​+1−1−1+1​S2​+1+1−1−1​correctionIZ1​Z4​Z7​​​.
(18)
These parity measurements reveal the erroneous block without revealing the logical amplitudes, so they preserve arbitrary superpositions of the two codewords. Applying the listed representative phase flip restores the state.
For any two physical positions i,j in the same block, Zi​Zj​ acts as +1 on both ∣000⟩ and ∣111⟩, and hence on the code. Therefore Zi​P=Zj​P and PZi†​Zj​P=P even when i=j. Their error spaces coincide, and the error-overlap matrix has a nonzero off-diagonal entry. The correction is degenerate because different within-block phase errors have exactly the same action on every logical state.

Use base-two logarithms, so the Von Neumann entropy is S(ρ)=−Tr(ρlog2​ρ), with 0log0=0. The quantum mutual information is I(A:B)=S(A)+S(B)−S(AB); this is the quantity denoted by S(ρA​:ρB​) in the question, using the given joint state.
Strong subadditivity of Von Neumann entropy states that for every tripartite density operator,
S(AB)+S(BC)≥S(B)+S(ABC).​
(19)
Equivalently the conditional quantum mutual information I(A:C∣B) is nonnegative. We apply this property to the original state or to a dilation of the relevant quantum channel.

Expand the difference between the two quantum mutual informations:
I(A:BC)−I(A:B)=S(BC)−S(ABC)−S(B)+S(AB).
(20)
This is precisely the nonnegative expression in Strong subadditivity of Von Neumann entropy. Thus
I(A:B)≤I(A:BC).​
(21)
The reduced state on AB is obtained by the partial trace over C, so this proves that discarding a system cannot increase its quantum mutual information with the retained reference.

Here a quantum operation is interpreted as a deterministic quantum channel, so it is completely positive and matrix trace preserving. Write its Kraus representation with ∑k​Ak†​Ak​=I and define the isometry in its Stinespring dilation
V∣ψ⟩=∑k​Ak​∣ψ⟩⊗∣k⟩E​.
(22)
Then V†V=I and Φ(ρ)=TrE​(VρV†). Acting on B gives σAB′E​=(IA​⊗V)ρAB​(IA​⊗V†). An isometry preserves the nonzero eigenvalues of a density operator. Hence S(AB′E)=S(AB), S(B′E)=S(B), and S(A) is unchanged. It follows that I(A:B′E)σ​=I(A:B)ρ​.
Now discard E and apply part 1. This proves data processing for quantum mutual information:
I(A:B′)≤I(A:B′E)=I(A:B).​
(23)
The matrix trace-preserving convention matters. Postselection can increase conditional quantum mutual information: take a uniform classical bit A, copy it to B with probability ε, and otherwise put B in an erasure state independent of A. Initially I(A:B)=ε bits. Projecting onto the nonerased sector and conditioning on success gives a perfectly correlated bit pair with quantum mutual information one. That success branch is matrix trace decreasing, and its renormalized action is not a deterministic channel. Retaining the full outcome flag and averaging all branches restores the ordinary channel inequality.

Let A be a register with orthonormal basis {∣x⟩} for Alice's classical symbol, Q the transmitted system, and B a register initially in a fixed blank state. The initial classical-quantum state is
ρAQB​=x∑​p(x)∣x⟩⟨x∣A​⊗ρx​⊗∣0⟩⟨0∣B​.​
(24)
A POVM fixes probabilities but not a unique conditional state of Q. Choose the Lüders rule instrument My​=Ey​​, which satisfies ∑y​My†​My​=I. Record outcome y in orthogonal states of B′ and retain the corresponding quantum output Q′. The final nonselective state is
ρA′Q′B′​=x,y∑​p(x)∣x⟩⟨x∣A′​⊗My​ρx​My†​⊗∣y⟩⟨y∣B′​.​
(25)
The positive operators in this sum are unnormalized: their traces already include the outcome probabilities. If Tr(Ey​ρx​)>0, the normalized conditional state of Q′ is My​ρx​My†​/Tr(Ey​ρx​).
More generally, an instrument may use operators Myμ​ with ∑μ​Myμ†​Myμ​=Ey​, replacing My​ρx​My†​ by ∑μ​Myμ​ρx​Myμ†​. All subsequent classical information bounds are unchanged. This explicitly accounts for the fact that a POVM does not determine the post-measurement state of a retained quantum system.

The initially blank register B is pure and independent, so adding it changes neither relevant Von Neumann entropy difference: I(A:QB)=I(A:Q). Bob's measurement and recording procedure, with all outcomes retained, is a matrix trace-preserving quantum channel from QB to Q′B′. Part (a) therefore gives I(A′:Q′B′)≤I(A:QB). Discarding Q′ gives I(A′:B′)≤I(A′:Q′B′). Combining the two inequalities proves
I(A′:B′)≤I(A:Q).​
(26)
Alice's register is not changed by Bob's local operation; the primes label its place in the final joint state rather than a change in its classical symbol.

iii OurBigBook.com
Trace out Q′ in the final state. With q(x,y)=p(x)Tr(Ey​ρx​), the remaining state is diagonal in the product classical basis:
ρA′B′​=∑x,y​q(x,y)∣x⟩⟨x∣⊗∣y⟩⟨y∣.
(27)
The Von Neumann entropy of a classical diagonal density operator equals the Shannon entropy of its diagonal probabilities, by its spectrum. Therefore I(A′:B′)=H(X)+H(Y)−H(X,Y)=I(X:Y), the mutual information between source symbol and measurement outcome.
For the input, put ρˉ​=∑x​p(x)ρx​. The marginal on Q is ρˉ​, the marginal on A has Von Neumann entropy H(X), and the entropy of a classical-quantum state is
S(AQ)=H(X)+∑x​p(x)S(ρx​).
(28)
To justify the last formula, diagonalize each ρx​ with eigenvalues λxj​. The joint block-diagonal state has eigenvalues p(x)λxj​; expanding −∑x,j​p(x)λxj​log2​[p(x)λxj​] yields exactly the displayed Von Neumann entropy. Thus I(A:Q)=S(ρˉ​)−∑x​p(x)S(ρx​), the Holevo quantity of the ensemble. Part (ii) becomes the Holevo bound
I(X:Y)≤χ=S(x∑​p(x)ρx​)−x∑​p(x)S(ρx​).​
(29)
It holds for every POVM, and hence also for the accessible information obtained by maximizing the mutual information over all measurements. The only extra Von Neumann entropy identities used were the classical-diagonal and classical–quantum block-spectrum formulas, both justified above; the information inequality itself followed from the requested strong-subadditivity argument.
