Let A=ρˉ​+Pˉ, and assume A=0 so that the density-defined curvature is meaningful. Under the cosmological gauge transformation in part ii,
Ψ=Ψ+NˉHT,δρ​=δρ+3NˉHAT.
(44)
Therefore the two time-shift terms cancel:
ζ​=Ψ−3Aδρ​​=Ψ−3Aδρ​=ζ.​
(45)
This is the uniform-density curvature perturbation with the paper's overall sign. On a uniform-density slice it is the spatial curvature potential Ψ. Its opposite overall sign is also a common convention, so the sign must be kept consistent rather than imported from another metric convention.
To establish time conservation, write Dt​=Nˉ−1∂t​ and ca2​=Pˉ˙/ρˉ​˙​. Background stress-energy conservation implies
Dt​A=−3H(1+ca2​)A.
(46)
For constant w in the given barotropic equation of state, ca2​=w. Differentiate ζ=Ψ−δρ/(3A):
Dt​ζ=Dt​Ψ−3ADt​δρ​+3A2δρ​Dt​A.
(47)
Insert both perturbed evolution equations from the hint, keeping the spatial Laplacian Δ=∇2/a2:
Dt​ζ​=−HΦ+3κ​+3Δχ​+AH(δρ+δP)​−3κ​+HΦ+3AΔu​−AH(1+ca2​)δρ​=AH​(δP−ca2​δρ)+31​Δ(χ+Au​).​
(48)
Thus the lapse perturbation Φ and the trace perturbation κ cancel exactly. The remaining pressure term is the non-adiabatic pressure perturbation δPnad​=δP−ca2​δρ. In the assumed barotropic fluid, the perturbed equation of state gives δP=wδρ and ca2​=w, so it vanishes. For a Fourier mode,
Nˉζ˙​​=−3a2k2​(χ+Au​).​
(49)
For regular long-wavelength perturbations, the velocity and shear potentials in this combination do not grow as k−2, so the right-hand side is gradient-suppressed when k≪aH. Therefore ζ is conserved to leading superhorizon order, which is the superhorizon conservation of uniform-density curvature requested.
The statement is not exact for arbitrary finite k: the displayed gradient term gives its correction. It also requires adiabatic cosmological perturbations; entropy perturbations in a multicomponent system or a nonbarotropic fluid can give δPnad​=0 and evolve ζ even at zero gradient order. At exact w=−1, A=0 and the question's variable is undefined, rather than a well-defined conserved density-slicing perturbation.
