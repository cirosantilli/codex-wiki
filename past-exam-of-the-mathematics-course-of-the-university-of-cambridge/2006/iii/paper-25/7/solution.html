For nonempty first-order structures (Mi​)i∈I​ in a common language and an ultrafilter D on I, identify product functions by
f∼D​g⟺{i:f(i)=g(i)}∈D.
(15)
The ultraproduct ∏i​Mi​/D has these equivalence classes as elements. Functions are interpreted coordinatewise, and a relation holds of classes exactly when its coordinatewise truth set belongs to D. Closure under finite intersections makes these interpretations independent of representatives.
The Łoś theorem asserts
∏i​Mi​/D⊨φ([f1​],…,[fn​])⟺{i:Mi​⊨φ(f1​(i),…,fn​(i))}∈D.
(16)
Prove it by induction on first-order formulas. Atomic first-order formulas hold by the definitions and induction on terms. Conjunction uses intersections, and negation uses the fact that an ultrafilter contains exactly one of a set and its complement. For an existential first-order formula, a product witness gives a coordinate witness on a D-large set. Conversely, on the D-large set where a coordinate witness exists, choose one in each factor, and choose arbitrary values elsewhere. Its class is a product witness by the induction hypothesis. This is the only witness-selection step; the surrounding argument is made in a choice metatheory. The special case with all factors equal is an ultrapower, and constant functions give an elementary embedding.
Here is a direct compactness theorem proof. Suppose every finite subset of a theory T has a model. Index factors by the finite subsets i⊆T, choosing Mi​⊨i. For finite F⊆T, the cone CF​={i:F⊆i} is nonempty, and CF​∩CH​=CF∪H​. Extend the generated proper filter to an ultrafilter D. For every sentence σ∈T, its truth set contains C{σ}​, so the Łoś theorem gives ∏i​Mi​/D⊨T. Finite satisfiability therefore implies satisfiability, directly from the ultraproduct construction rather than from a syntactic completeness argument.
For saturated models, a structure is κ-saturated if every complete type over fewer than κ parameters is realized. A useful concrete case is countable saturation of a nonprincipal ultraproduct over omega. In a countable language, enumerate any finitely satisfiable type over countably many parameters as φ1​(x),φ2​(x),…, and represent its parameters by product functions. By the Łoś theorem, the set An​ of coordinates where the first n first-order formulas have a simultaneous witness belongs to the nonprincipal ultrafilter D. Put
Bn​={i:i≥n}∩⋂j≤n​Aj​.
(17)
These are decreasing D-large sets. At coordinate i, let h(i) be the largest n≤i with i∈Bn​, or zero if none exists, and choose a witness for the first h(i) first-order formulas. For fixed n, every coordinate in Bn​ has h(i)≥n, so the resulting product function satisfies φn​ on a D-large set. Its class realizes the whole type. Thus such an ultraproduct is ℵ1​-saturated.
There is also an existence construction at every prescribed degree κ. Given an infinite model M, choose a regular cardinal λ≥κ and construct an elementary chain (Mα​)α≤λ​. At a successor stage, add a new constant for a realization of every type over every subset of Mα​ of size below κ, together with the elementary diagram of Mα​. Every finite part is satisfiable: only finitely many types and finitely many first-order formulas from each are involved, and each finite type fragment has a witness in Mα​. Compactness therefore supplies a simultaneous elementary extension. At limits take unions. To justify elementarity of a union, any existential first-order formula with parameters in an earlier stage has a witness in that stage whenever it has one at a later stage, by elementarity; this is the Tarski-Vaught test.
Any fewer-than-κ parameters of Mλ​ occur together in some Mα​, by regularity of λ. A type over them which is finitely satisfiable in Mλ​ is finitely satisfiable in Mα​, again by elementarity, and was realized in Mα+1​. Hence every infinite structure has a κ-saturated elementary extension for any prescribed cardinal κ. No bound on the size of this extension is asserted; full saturation at the model's own cardinality has additional cardinal-arithmetic issues.
