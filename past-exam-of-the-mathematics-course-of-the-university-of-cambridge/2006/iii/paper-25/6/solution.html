Past exam of the mathematics course of the University of Cambridge / 2006 / iii / Paper 25 / 6 / Solution

Let M be an externally countable model of ZFC, and let κ=(ω1​)M. In M form the forcing order
P={p:p is a finite partial function ω⟶κ},q≤p⟺q⊇p.
(12)
This is the finite-function collapse to countable size. For every n∈ωM and α∈κM, the internal sets
Dn​={p:n∈dom(p)},Eα​={p:α∈ran(p)}
(13)
are dense: extend a finite function by assigning a missing input, and use a fresh input to put any required value in its range.
Externally enumerate all the dense subsets of P which M recognizes as dense. There are only countably many, because M is countable. Recursively choose pi+1​≤pi​ in the ith such dense set and let G be the upward closure of this descending chain. This produces a generic filter over M. By the forcing theorem, its generic extension M[G] satisfies ZFC. The canonical forcing name g˙​=⋃G˙ is forced to be a function with domain ω and range κ: the dense sets Dn​ and Eα​ ensure exactly these assertions. Therefore
M[G]⊨“the old ω1​ is countable.”​
(14)
Its new first uncountable ordinal is accordingly different from κ.
For a transitive M, ordinary evaluation of names gives the familiar literal extension. The question only assumes a countable model, so one must also cover externally ill-founded models. In that case use the quotient of the internally defined forcing names by forced equality modulo G, with membership defined by forced membership. The internal forcing identities and the forcing theorem hold in M and give a well-defined first-order structure satisfying every standard ZFC axiom. Check names embed the original membership structure, so identify their images with M. Its domain is a quotient of a subset of the countable domain of M, hence is externally countable. Thus the required extension exists even without assuming transitivity. It is a membership extension, not an elementary extension: the assertion that the parameter κ is uncountable changes truth value.
