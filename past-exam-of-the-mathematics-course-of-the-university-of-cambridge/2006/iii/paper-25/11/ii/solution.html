OurBigBook.com Words: 229
Use the bounded successor characterization of finite ordinals. Let Ord(x) assert that x is transitive, each of its members is transitive, and any two members are equal or comparable by membership. This is a bounded first-order formula; foundation makes its linear membership order well-founded, so it is precisely the Von Neumann ordinal predicate. Define
Succ0​(x)⟺x=∅ ∨ (∃y∈x)(∀z∈x)(z=y ∨ z∈y),
(30)
and put
Nat(x)⟺Ord(x) ∧ Succ0​(x) ∧ (∀y∈x)Succ0​(y).​
(31)
For an ordinal, having a greatest member y says exactly x=y∪{y}. The displayed definition makes no reference to an infinite inductive set or to ω; all quantifiers are bounded within the candidate and its members. Occurrences of x=∅ can also be expressed as (∀u∈x)u=u, so no unbounded quantifier is hidden in empty-set notation.
Every usual natural number satisfies this predicate, by induction: it is a finite ordinal, and every nonzero initial segment is a successor. Conversely, suppose an ordinal x is not a usual natural number. Ordinal comparability gives x≥ω. If x=ω, it has no greatest member, contradicting Succ0​(x). If x>ω, then ω∈x has no greatest member, contradicting the corresponding bounded requirement on members of x. Hence Nat(x) holds exactly for the usual natural numbers. The axiom of infinity and separation therefore collect this class as the usual set ω. This is a bounded definition of membership in the natural-number class; it does not claim that the infinite set ω itself can be produced without the infinity axiom.
