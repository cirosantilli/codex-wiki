Use finitely many colonies and let ni​ be the population of colony i. An open migration process has independent external Poisson processes of rates νi​, local total departure rates ϕi​(ni​), and fixed routing probabilities pij​ to other colonies and pi0​ out of the system. Here ϕi​(0)=0, 0<ϕi​(k)<∞ for k≥1, pii​=0 and pi0​+∑j​pij​=1. The continuous-time Markov chain has intensities
q(n,n+ei​)=νi​,q(n,n−ei​)=ϕi​(ni​)pi0​,q(n,n−ei​+ej​)=ϕi​(ni​)pij​.
(1)
Require the routing matrix P to be transient: every individual eventually exits. For finitely many colonies this is equivalent to every colony having a directed path to an exit. Restrict to colonies reachable from external arrivals, so their traffic rates are positive and the relevant chain is irreducible. The total population cannot exceed its initial value plus the number of external arrivals, so on any finite time interval it is bounded by a finite random number. There are only finitely many states below that bound and finite rates in each, proving nonexplosion even when the departure functions grow rapidly.
The traffic equations of an open migration process, in row-vector form, are
α=ν+αP,α=ν(I−P)−1.
(2)
Transience makes the inverse ∑k≥0​Pk finite. Define
bi​(k)=∏h=1k​ϕi​(h)αik​​,Zi​=∑k≥0​bi​(k),bi​(0)=1.
(3)
Provided every Zi​ is finite, the candidate product-form stationary distribution of an open migration process is
π(n)=i∏​Zi−1​∏h=1ni​​ϕi​(h)αini​​​.​
(4)
The normalizability condition is exactly Zi​<∞ for every active colony. For example, constant total service ϕi​(k)=μi​ for k>0 requires αi​<μi​ and gives a geometric distribution; independent individual departures ϕi​(k)=kμi​ give a Poisson distribution of mean αi​/μi​. In the irreducible case the normalizable invariant law is the unique equilibrium. If the weights cannot be normalized, no stationary probability law exists, by uniqueness up to scale of invariant measures for an irreducible recurrent chain.
We establish both stationarity and the time reversal of an open migration process assertion by adjacent-weight calculations. Define
q∗(n,m)=π(n)π(m)q(m,n)​(m=n).
(5)
The identity bi​(k)ϕi​(k)=αi​bi​(k−1) gives
q∗(n,n+ei​)q∗(n,n−ei​)q∗(n,n−ei​+ej​)​=αi​pi0​,=ϕi​(ni​)αi​νi​​,=ϕi​(ni​)αi​αj​pji​​.​
(6)
Thus the proposed time reversal of an open migration process retains the local service functions and has
νi∗​=αi​pi0​,pi0∗​=νi​/αi​,pij∗​=αj​pji​/αi​.​
(7)
The traffic equation for colony i implies pi0∗​+∑j​pij∗​=1. Summing all traffic equations also gives ∑i​νi∗​=∑i​νi​. Therefore the total outgoing rate from every state is the same for q∗ and q:
∑m=n​q∗(n,m)=∑i​νi​+∑i​ϕi​(ni​)=∑m=n​q(n,m).
(8)
Consequently ∑m=n​π(m)q(m,n)=π(n)∑m=n​q(n,m), proving global balance for a continuous-time Markov chain. The same weight recurrence gives Eπ​ϕi​(ni​)=αi​, so the stationary mean total jump rate is finite. The candidate is a stationary distribution, and the displayed intensities are indeed its stationary time reversal of a continuous-time Markov chain. Reverse routing is also open: reverse an original arrival-to-colony path to obtain a path to a colony with pi0∗​>0.
For the last request, let S be all colonies reachable by routing from k, including k itself. The no-return-path hypothesis gives j∈/S. By construction no routing edge leads from S to its complement C. The populations in C therefore evolve autonomously as an open migration process, with moves into S treated as exits. Its stationary marginal is the corresponding product of the factors above. Retain the original destination as a mark on each such exit.
In this autonomous process, a marked exit j→k has intensity ϕj​(nj​)pjk​. The same reversed-weight calculation makes its reverse a marked external immigration channel of constant rate αj​pjk​. External immigration channels can be constructed as independent Poisson processes. Stationary reversal of a two-sided Poisson process preserves its law, so reversing this immigration channel gives
The stationary j→k stream is Poisson with rate αj​pjk​.​
(9)
This Poisson migration stream without a return path argument is needed because a general internal migration stream in a network with feedback is not automatically Poisson. A zero routing probability gives the empty Poisson stream of rate zero.

For a standard loss network, let type-r calls arrive as independent external Poisson processes of rates νr​. Each accepted call holds Ajr​ units of resource j for a holding time with an independent exponential distribution of parameter μr​, then releases them. Capacities Cj​ and requirements are nonnegative integers. Rejected calls leave immediately and do not queue. With fixed routes, the feasible count set is
F(C)={n∈Z+R​:An≤C}.
(10)
For a call of each type using at least one finite-capacity resource, this set is finite. The count continuous-time Markov chain has accepted birth rate νr​1{n+er​∈F(C)}​ and death rate μr​nr​. Put ρr​=νr​/μr​ and
w(n)=∏r​nr​!ρrnr​​​,Z(C)=∑n∈F(C)​w(n).
(11)
On every admitted edge, w(n+er​)/w(n)=ρr​/(nr​+1), so w(n)νr​=w(n+er​)μr​(nr​+1). This proves detailed balance and the product-form stationary distribution of a loss network:
π(n)=Z(C)−1w(n),n∈F(C).
(12)
It is unique when all relevant arrival rates are positive, since departures lead to zero and every feasible state can be built from zero through feasible births.
An external Poisson process arrival sees this stationary prearrival law by Poisson arrivals see time averages. Indeed its constant conditional arrival rate makes the expected count of arrivals in any state set equal to νr​ times the time spent there. A type-r arrival is accepted exactly in F(C−Ar​), where Ar​ is its resource column. Thus the blocking partition ratio for a loss network is
Br​=n∈F(C)n+er​∈/F(C)​∑​π(n)=1−Z(C)Z(C−Ar​)​.​
(13)
Set Z to zero when reduced capacities have an empty feasible set.
For the triangle, index call types by their endpoint pairs and give physical links integer capacities C12​,C23​,C31​. We assume each call needs one circuit on every traversed link; arrivals of the three types are independent Poisson processes, and holding times have independent exponential distributions with parameters μ12​,μ23​,μ31​ determined by endpoints alone. Calls may be rearranged instantaneously and without cost among their direct and two-link paths, without restarting or changing their holding times. Admit a call if any simultaneous arrangement of all calls is feasible. These assumptions make the three type counts Markovian even though the physical route assignments may change.
The cut feasibility for a rearrangeable triangle gives the exact feasible count set. Define virtual endpoint capacities
K1​=C12​+C31​,K2​=C12​+C23​,K3​=C23​+C31​.
(14)
Every endpoint call must use at least one of the two links incident to that endpoint, so feasibility requires
n12​+n31​≤K1​,n12​+n23​≤K2​,n23​+n31​≤K3​.
(15)
These cut inequalities are also sufficient. If each count fits its direct-link capacity, route every call directly. Otherwise at most one count exceeds its own direct capacity: two excesses would violate the cut inequality at their shared endpoint. If, for example, n12​>C12​, route C12​ such calls directly and the excess d=n12​−C12​ through node three. The other link usages are n31​+d and n23​+d, and the first two cut inequalities bound them by C31​ and C23​. The other cases are cyclic permutations. Thus the count process is exactly a virtual fixed routing loss network in which a pair call consumes one unit of each endpoint resource.
Write
Z(K1​,K2​,K3​)=∑n12​,n23​,n31​≥0n12​+n31​≤K1​n12​+n23​≤K2​n23​+n31​≤K3​​​n12​!n23​!n31​!ρ12n12​​ρ23n23​​ρ31n31​​​.
(16)
The exact stationary distribution count probabilities are these weights divided by Z. The three arriving-call blocking probabilities are therefore
B12​B23​B31​​=1−Z(K1​,K2​,K3​)Z(K1​−1,K2​−1,K3​)​,=1−Z(K1​,K2​,K3​)Z(K1​,K2​−1,K3​−1)​,=1−Z(K1​,K2​,K3​)Z(K1​−1,K2​,K3​−1)​.​​
(17)
Equivalently, a type-12 call is blocked on the union of the events n12​+n31​=K1​ and n12​+n23​=K2​, with the other pairs treated cyclically. The partition ratios count their union without double-counting intersections. These expressions are exact for the stated rearrangeable triangular loss network; a restricted rearrangement policy would require a different admission model.

Use finite sets of links J, source-sink pairs S, and admissible routes R, with at least one route for each pair. Route r carries aggregate flow xr​. The link-route incidence matrix has Ajr​=1 if route r uses link j and zero otherwise; repeated traversals, if allowed, are counted with their multiplicity. The source-sink route incidence matrix has Hsr​=1 if r serves pair s. Thus y=Ax gives link throughputs and f=Hx gives source-sink demands. Write Rs​ for the routes serving s and
Lr​(x)=∑j​Ajr​Dj​(yj​),λs​(x)=minr∈Rs​​Lr​(x).
(18)
A Wardrop equilibrium routes positive flow only on minimum-delay routes:
xr​>0⟹Lr​(x)=λs​(x)(r∈Rs​),Lr​(x)≥λs​(x) for every r∈Rs​.
(19)
This is a nonatomic equilibrium: an individual infinitesimal user's route change does not alter the aggregate link delays.
For fixed nonnegative demand f, the feasible route-flow set {x≥0:Hx=f} is a nonempty product of finite-dimensional simplexes and hence a compact set. The Beckmann potential
V(y)=∑j∈J​∫0yj​​Dj​(u)du
(20)
is continuous, so it attains a minimum over this feasible set. Each primitive is a strictly convex function, since its derivative Dj​ is continuous and strictly increasing. Its route-flow derivative is exactly the corresponding route delay:
∂xr​∂​V(Ax)=∑j​Ajr​Dj​((Ax)j​)=Lr​(x).
(21)
If a minimizer had positive flow on r and a lower-delay alternative q∈Rs​, transferring a small amount from r to q would give directional derivative Lq​−Lr​<0, contradicting minimality. Hence every minimizer is a Wardrop equilibrium.
Conversely, let x be a Wardrop equilibrium and z any feasible competitor. Convexity gives
V(Az)−V(Ax)≥∑r​Lr​(x)(zr​−xr​).
(22)
For each s, subtract λs​ inside this sum. The demand constraint makes ∑r∈Rs​​λs​(zr​−xr​)=0, and the remaining terms are nonnegative: used routes have Lr​−λs​=0, while unused routes have xr​=0, zr​≥0 and Lr​−λs​≥0. Thus V(Az)≥V(Ax). We have proved both existence and the optimization characterization.
Strict convexity in the link vector makes y unique: two different minimizing link vectors would have a feasible midpoint of strictly smaller potential. Thus link delays and the minimum route delays at the Wardrop equilibrium are also unique. The route vector need not be unique, because different route decompositions may have the same Ax and Hx. For example, two successive stages with two parallel links at each stage have four routes. With unit demand and link delay D(u)=u, the vectors
(1/4+t,1/4−t,1/4−t,1/4+t),∣t∣≤1/4,
(23)
all give throughput 1/2 on each of the four links and delay one on every route. This exhibits route-flow nonuniqueness at a Wardrop equilibrium. The equilibrium link throughputs are unique; the individual route flows need not be.
For elastic demand, make the usual physical assumptions explicit: delays are nonnegative on [0,∞), every route is nonempty, and Bs​:[0,∞)→[0,∞) is finite, continuous and strictly decreasing. Put
Ms​=Bs​(0),ℓs​=limλ→∞​Bs​(λ),Ps​(v)=Bs−1​(v)(ℓs​<v≤Ms​).
(24)
The limit exists by monotonicity; it need not be zero. The inverse demand function Ps​ is continuous, strictly decreasing, satisfies Ps​(Ms​)=0, and tends to infinity as v↓ℓs​.
Choose any reference bs​∈(ℓs​,Ms​) and use elastic-demand utility with an interior reference:
G(f)=s∑​Gs​(fs​),Gs​(v)=∫bs​v​Ps​(u)du.​
(25)
Each utility is strictly concave, with derivative Ps​. A reference inside the range avoids a potentially divergent integral from zero. For instance, B(λ)=(1+λ)−1/2 has inverse v−2−1, whose integral from zero is infinite, although the displayed reference-point primitive is finite at every interior demand.
Give Gs​ its limiting value at ℓs​, which may be −∞, and extend it by −∞ outside [ℓs​,Ms​]. This encodes the physical demand range in the objective. After substituting y=Ax, f=Hx, minimize
V(Ax)−G(Hx),x≥0.
(26)
Its effective feasible set is contained in {x≥0:ℓs​≤(Hx)s​≤Ms​}, a nonempty compact set. The objective is lower semicontinuous, including a possible value +∞ at a lower endpoint, and is finite when all demands equal their interior reference values. It therefore has a finite-valued minimizer.
The boundary exclusion for elastic Wardrop demand shows that every minimizing demand is interior. If fs​=ℓs​ and the utility there is finite, adding a small flow h on any route serving s costs at most a bounded constant times h, while the utility gain is at least hPs​(ℓs​+h), whose ratio to h tends to infinity. This decreases the objective. If that endpoint utility is −∞, a finite minimizer cannot occur there in the first place. At fs​=Ms​>0, choose a used route. Its delay is strictly positive, since its nonempty links have positive throughput and strictly increasing nonnegative delays. Removing a small amount of its flow saves a positive first-order cost, whereas the utility loss is o(h) because Ps​(Ms​)=0. This also decreases the objective. Hence ℓs​<fs​<Ms​ for every pair.
The interior-demand route derivatives are now
∂xr​∂​{V(Ax)−G(Hx)}=Lr​(x)−Ps​(fs​),r∈Rs​.
(27)
At a minimizer they are zero on positive route flows and nonnegative on zero route flows, by increasing or decreasing a single route flow. Since fs​>0, some route is used, so
λs​=Ps​(fs​),fs​=Bs​(λs​).​
(28)
These are precisely the elastic-demand Wardrop equilibrium conditions. Conversely, those conditions give the same nonnegative directional derivative for every competitor; convexity then proves global minimality. The equivalent constrained form is the requested minimization of V(y)−G(f) with Hx=f, Ax=y, x≥0. Strict convexity determines both y and f uniquely, while their route decomposition can still be nonunique.

Only active routes, those with nr​>0, enter the proportional fairness inequality and the weighted logarithmic utility. Their rates must be positive; inactive routes have no per-flow rate to determine and may be assigned zero. This convention removes otherwise meaningless terms 0log0 and 0/0.
For positive active rates, concavity of the logarithm gives, for any positive feasible competitor y,
∑r​nr​(logyr​−logxr​)≤∑r​nr​xr​yr​−xr​​.
(29)
Thus the proportional fairness inequality proves that x maximizes the log-utility objective. A competitor with an active zero rate has utility −∞ and cannot improve it. Conversely, if x maximizes utility, the right directional derivative along x+ε(y−x) at ε=0 is nonpositive, giving exactly that inequality. Positive resource capacities and finitely many active routes allow a feasible allocation with every active rate positive, so an optimizer cannot have an active zero rate. Strict concavity gives uniqueness of the active rates. Hence proportional fairness is equivalent to the stated log-utility maximization.
Index the four adjacent-pair routes in cyclic order and put zi​=ni​xi​, the aggregate service on route i. The resource constraints are
z1​+z4​≤1,z1​+z2​≤1,z2​+z3​≤1,z3​+z4​≤1.
(30)
They involve every odd-even route pair, so they are equivalent to
p+q≤1,p=max(z1​,z3​),q=max(z2​,z4​).
(31)
For given maxima, increasing each active odd service to p and each active even service to q remains feasible and improves utility. Apart from the constant −∑i​ni​logni​, the objective therefore reduces to
Plogp+Qlogq,P=n1​+n3​,Q=n2​+n4​,p+q≤1.
(32)
When both groups are present the constraint is tight, and differentiation gives P/p=Q/(1−p). If one group is empty, the other receives unit aggregate service on each of its active routes. Thus the opposite-route reduction for a proportionally fair four-cycle yields, with T=P+Q>0,
xi​={P/(Tni​),Q/(Tni​),​i∈{1,3}, ni​>0,i∈{2,4}, ni​>0.​​
(33)
Inactive rates can be set to zero, including every rate in the empty state. In particular n{1,2}​x{1,2}​=(n{1,2}​+n{3,4}​)/∑r​nr​ whenever that route is active. This proves the required formula even when other routes are empty.
For the flow-level network model, an active document's residual size retains an exponential distribution with parameter μi​. Receiving service at rate xi​(n) gives completion intensity μi​xi​(n), so ni​ independent active documents give total completion intensity μi​ni​xi​(n). Consequently the population continuous-time Markov chain has
q(n,n+ei​)=νi​,q(n,n−ei​)=⎩⎨⎧​μi​P/T,μi​Q/T,0,​i odd, ni​>0,i even, ni​>0,ni​=0.​​
(34)
All death intensities are zero at T=0. Their total is bounded by ∑i​μi​, and the total arrival rate is constant, proving nonexplosion.
Put ρi​=νi​/μi​. The required stationary weight uses a binomial coefficient, as printed in the PDF; the converted TeX incorrectly rendered it as an ordinary fraction. Define
w(n)=(PT​)∏i=14​ρini​​,w(0)=1.
(35)
For an odd route, the neighboring binomial coefficient ratio is
w(n)w(n+ei​)​=ρi​P+1T+1​,
(36)
while its death intensity at the neighboring state is μi​(P+1)/(T+1). Their product is νi​, so w(n)q(n,n+ei​)=w(n+ei​)q(n+ei​,n). For an even route, replace P by Q in the same calculation. This proves detailed balance and the stationary law of a proportionally fair four-cycle:
π(n)=B−1(n1​+n3​∑i​ni​​)i=1∏4​(μi​νi​​)ni​,B=n∈Z+4​∑​w(n).​
(37)
A finite B is essential for this expression to be a probability distribution. We determine its exact convergence condition rather than asserting stationarity for arbitrary arrival rates. Let
hp​(a,c)=∑i=0p​aicp−i,a=ρ1​, c=ρ3​, b=ρ2​, d=ρ4​.
(38)
Grouping the states by odd and even population gives
B=∑p,q≥0​(pp+q​)hp​(a,c)hq​(b,d).
(39)
With A=max(a,c) and E=max(b,d), the bounds Ap≤hp​(a,c)≤(p+1)Ap and Eq≤hq​(b,d)≤(q+1)Eq show that convergence holds exactly when A+E<1. For necessity, the lower bound sums to ∑k≥0​(A+E)k, which diverges at or above one. For sufficiency, choose slightly larger A′,E′ with A′+E′<1; the polynomial factors are absorbed into constants times (A′)p(E′)q, giving a convergent geometric generating series. Thus the stability region for the reversible four-cycle flow network is
max(ρ1​,ρ3​)+max(ρ2​,ρ4​)<1.​
(40)
Equivalently, the offered load on each resource, ρ1​+ρ4​, ρ1​+ρ2​, ρ2​+ρ3​, or ρ3​+ρ4​, is strictly below its unit capacity. For positive arrival rates this finite normalizing constant gives the unique stationary distribution of the irreducible process. At or beyond the boundary, the displayed invariant weights are not summable and do not define a stationary probability law.
If an explicit normalizing constant is desired, hp​(a,c)=(ap+1−cp+1)/(a−c) for a=c, and ∑p,q​(pp+q​)upvq=(1−u−v)−1. Hence for a=c, b=d in the stable region,
B=(a−c)(b−d)1​(1−a−bab​−1−a−dad​−1−c−bcb​+1−c−dcd​).
(41)
