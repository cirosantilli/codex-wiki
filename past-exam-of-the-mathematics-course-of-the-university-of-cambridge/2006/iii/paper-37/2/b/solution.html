We use the Van den Berg-Kesten inequality: for increasing events A,B under independent bond percolation, their disjoint occurrence A□B, meaning that they have disjoint sets of open edges witnessing the two events, satisfies Pp​(A□B)≤Pp​(A)Pp​(B). The finite-edge statement applies here; connection to a finite-box boundary can always be witnessed before the first exit from that box.
On the event 0↔∂Λm+n​ choose a simple open path to that boundary, and let y be its first point on ∂Λm​. Its initial segment witnesses 0↔∂Λm​ through y, inside Λm​. The remaining segment eventually reaches maximum-norm distance at least n from y, because its endpoint has norm m+n while ∥y∥∞​=m. Stop that segment when it first reaches y+∂Λn​. These two segments use disjoint edges.
For fixed y, let Ay​ be connection from zero to y inside Λm​, and By​ be connection from y to y+∂Λn​ inside y+Λn​. Then Pp​(Ay​)≤βm​ and, by translation invariance, Pp​(By​)=βn​. The union bound and the stated disjoint-occurrence inequality give
βm+n​≤y∈∂Λm​∑​Pp​(Ay​□By​)≤∣∂Λm​∣βm​βn​.​
(13)
Set xn​=logβn​ and αn​=log∣∂Λn​∣. The previous part applies, since
∣∂Λn​∣=(2n+1)d−(2n−1)d=Od​(nd−1),αn​/n⟶0.
(14)
Moreover a straight path of n open bonds has probability pn, so pn≤βn​≤1. Thus the limit is finite and
γ=n→∞lim​nlogβn​​∈[logp,0],βn​≥∣∂Λn​∣−1enγ.​
(15)
Equivalently, for the nonnegative decay rate κ=−γ, the bound is βn​≥∣∂Λn​∣−1e−nκ.
The printed final exponent has the opposite sign while retaining the definition of γ as the limit of n−1logβn​. With that definition it cannot be correct in general. Indeed, if (2d−1)p<1, count self-avoiding paths of length ℓ≥n to get
βn​≤∑ℓ≥n​2d(2d−1)ℓ−1pℓ=2d−12d​1−(2d−1)p((2d−1)p)n​.
(16)
Hence γ≤log((2d−1)p)<0. A lower bound with e−nγ would eventually exceed one, because the boundary factor grows only polynomially. The valid bound is the boxed expression with enγ, or the equivalent expression using κ=−γ.
