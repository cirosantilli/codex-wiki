Take z upward from the unperturbed interface in the apparatus frame, with liquid at z>0 and pulling velocity −Vez​. The primary ice contains no salt. We use the dilute, equal-density approximation and neglect fluid motion relative to the mould. The initial salt inventory per unit horizontal area, with a common density factor suppressed, is N=hC0​. Its conservation is essential: the far liquid has zero concentration, so an imposed nonzero far-field composition would describe a different experiment.
The steady advection-diffusion equation is −VC′=DC′′. Decay into the pure water and interfacial salt rejection give
C(z)=CI​e−Vz/D,DC′(0)+VCI​=0.
(1)
Since ∫0∞​C(z)dz=DCI​/V=N, the finite-inventory directional salt rejection solution is
C(z)=DVhC0​​e−Vz/D(z>0),C=0in the primary ice.​
(2)
The decay length is D/V. Local liquidus equilibrium gives
TI​=−mDVhC0​​.​
(3)
Thus the thermal apparatus selects the interface position at this isotherm. The stated speed inequality is precisely CI​<CE​, so the interface is warmer than the eutectic temperature, TI​>TE​=−mCE​. These are steady values after the initial finite saline layer has rearranged; the final exponential distribution is not confined to the original depth h.
For VhC0​/D>CE​, a steady pure-ice front would require an interfacial composition beyond the eutectic composition. That branch is unavailable. During the transient, rejected brine concentrates until it reaches CE​; secondary salt-bearing crystals can then form and the residual brine freezes as an eutectic system. Salt is incorporated into the product rather than remaining indefinitely in a super-eutectic exponential layer. The detailed pattern can include brine pockets and a nonplanar freezing region; it depends on the thermal history and the morphological stability. The speed inequality alone does not decide that latter stability.
For the stability calculation use an interface displacement η=η​eikx+st and a liquid perturbation c=ceikx+st−qz. Here k>0 is the wavenumber, s is the temporal rate and ℜq>0 gives spatial decay. The frozen thermal field is TI​+Gz. Define Γ=γsl​Tm​/(ρL)>0, where γsl​ is solid-liquid surface energy, Tm​ is absolute pure-solvent melting temperature, ρ is mass density and L is specific latent heat. For a solid protrusion into liquid the curvature is K=−ηxx​=k2η, and the Gibbs-Thomson effect gives local equilibrium T=−mC−ΓK. We assume isotropic surface energy and instantaneous interface kinetics, so no kinetic undercooling term is present.
Linearizing the equilibrium condition at the displaced interface gives
Gη=−m[c(0)+ηC′(0)]−Γk2η,c=[DVCI​​−mG+Γk2​]η​.
(4)
The perturbation advection-diffusion equation gives
s=Dq2−Vq−Dk2.
(5)
The local solute balance at a moving graph, to first order, is
D∂n​C+(V+ηt​)C=0.
(6)
Its perturbation is (V−Dq)c+sCI​η​=0, because the terms DηC′′(0) and VηC′(0) cancel. Consequently the full frozen-temperature solutal interface dispersion is
s=(Dq−V)[DV​−mCI​G+Γk2​],s=Dq2−Vq−Dk2.​
(7)
No quasistatic approximation to solute diffusion was made here. For a growing mode the unique admissible liquid root is
q=2DV+V2+4D(s+Dk2)​​.
(8)
Writing b=(G+Γk2)/(mCI​) and eliminating s gives (q−V/D)(q−V/D+b)=k2. The root continuously connected to the growing interface branch is therefore
q=DV​+2b2+4k2​−b​,s(k)=2D​(DV​−b)(b2+4k2​−b).​
(9)
In particular, unstable wavelengths exist exactly when mVCI​/D>G, and their band is
0<k2<ΓmVCI​/D−G​.
(10)
This is the constitutional supercooling criterion: immediately above the plane, the local liquidus rises at rate −mC′(0)=mVCI​/D, so it overtakes the imposed thermal gradient when that rate exceeds G. The Gibbs-Thomson effect cuts off short waves. At long wavelength s∼D(V/D−b)k2/b for positive G. The exactly uniform inventory-changing displacement is not an instability of a fixed-inventory experiment. On stable branches the full diffusion problem also admits relaxation transients; the root selection above is sufficient to identify every exponentially growing interface mode.
If one additionally neglects the perturbation time derivative in the liquid diffusion equation, the alternative reduced rate is
sqs​(k)=21​[V2+4D2k2​−V][DV​−mCI​G+Γk2​].
(11)
It has the same onset and cutoff, but need not have the same rate away from the slow-growth regime. The frozen-temperature approximation by itself does not justify this extra reduction.
After onset, protrusions reach more weakly saline liquid and advance faster, while grooves accumulate rejected brine. The planar interface develops cells and, as growth becomes sufficiently strong, crystal dendrites with concentrated liquid between them. Nonlinear competition changes the selected spacing; linear rates alone cannot fix the final geometry. The interdendritic liquid forms a mushy layer, concentrates toward the eutectic composition, and ultimately freezes into a salt-bearing eutectic product when it reaches TE​. This replaces the pure planar salt-rejection picture with trapping and freezing of brine between crystals.
