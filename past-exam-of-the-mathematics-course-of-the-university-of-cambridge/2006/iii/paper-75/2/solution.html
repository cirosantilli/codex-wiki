Work in the frame of the steadily advancing mush, so the rigid primary-crystal skeleton moves with −Vez​. Let ϕ be solid fraction, C the pore-liquid concentration, q=(u,w) the Darcy flux relative to that skeleton, and K the common thermal conductivity. Set κ=K/(ρcp​), the thermal diffusivity. The actual pore velocity is q/(1−ϕ)−Vez​. Primary crystals are taken to exclude solute; the residual pore liquid freezes into a two-solid eutectic product at the bottom. These are the standard binary mushy layer assumptions implicit in using a single liquidus and an eutectic floor.
It is convenient to use hydraulic pressure P=p+ρgz, absorbing the constant-density gravitational term. With equal phase properties, no solute diffusion, negligible fluid inertia and local liquidus equilibrium, a complete mush model is
∇⋅q=0,q=−μΠ​∇P,T=−mC,
(12)
−V∂z​[(1−ϕ)C]+∇⋅(qC)=0,
(13)
(q−Vez​)⋅∇T+cp​VL​∂z​ϕ=κ∇2T.
(14)
The last equation follows from conservation of energy with mixture specific enthalpy cp​T−Lϕ. Sensible heat is transported by the total volume flux q−Vez​; the primary solid carries the latent-enthalpy flux VLϕez​. In particular the sign of the latent-heat term is fixed by the downward-moving skeleton.
At the liquid roof, impose
z=h:T=T0​=−mC0​,C=C0​,ϕ=0,Tz​=G,P=P0​−21​E2x2.
(15)
Here P0​=p0​+ρgh. The gradient condition matches heat flux into the imposed liquid field, since there is no phase-fraction jump at this roof and the phase conductivities are equal. The pore velocity there equals the relative Darcy velocity. At the floor,
z=0:T=TE​,C=CE​=−TE​/m,w=0.
(16)
There is no liquid penetration into fully solid material. The unspecified solid fraction at the floor is determined by the solute balance, not set to one: remaining eutectic liquid freezes as it crosses the floor. If Cs​ denotes the bulk concentration of the final solid product, the floor solute balance and Stefan condition are
Cs​=(1−ϕ(0))CE​,K[Tz​(0−)−Tz​(0+)]=ρLV[1−ϕ(0)].
(17)
Maintaining the floor at TE​ permits the solid-side bath to remove this final latent heat. Equivalently its required gradient is Tz​(0−)=Tz​(0+)+ρLV[1−ϕ(0)]/K. No additional imposed solid-side gradient is specified. Laterally we take the quadratic-pressure strain solution, without sidewalls; finite sidewalls would require additional flow conditions and generally break this horizontal invariance.
For uniform permeability of a porous medium, incompressible flow gives ∇2P=0. The roof pressure and zero normal flow at the floor are satisfied by
P=P0​−2E2​(x2+h2−z2),u=αx,w=−αz,α=μΠE2​.​
(18)
Horizontal outflow is balanced by downward inflow. The parameter α is an inverse time; E2 in the printed pressure condition already has dimensions of pressure divided by length squared, so no extra density factor belongs in this definition.
With T=T(z), C=C(z) and ϕ=ϕ(z), the x-dependence disappears from both scalar balances. Although u is nonzero, its horizontal divergence cancels wz​ in ∇⋅(qC). The exact strain-driven steady mush equations become
κT′′+(V+αz)T′−cp​VL​ϕ′=0,[V(1−ϕ)C]′=−αzC′,C=−T/m.​
(19)
Together with T(0)=TE​, T(h)=T0​, T′(h)=G and ϕ(h)=0, they determine the scalar fields and the unknown thickness. This reduction proves that the equations admit horizontally invariant scalar solutions despite the imposed straining flow. An equivalent useful form, obtained by differentiating the solute equation, is
ϕ′=C1−ϕ+αz/V​C′,κT′′+[V+αz+cp​mCL​{V(1−ϕ)+αz}]T′=0.
(20)
This exact form also shows why one must not silently discard latent heat in an arbitrary strong-flow calculation.
Define ΔT=T0​−TE​>0, R=αh/V and S=L/(cp​ΔT). For the stated regular ordering R≫S≫1, strain transport dominates skeleton transport and the bounded phase-change enthalpy contribution. More precisely, at fixed finite thermal strain number αh2/κ, the dimensionless energy equation is
Tζζ​+κVh​(1+Rζ)Tζ​−κcp​VhL​ϕζ​=0,ζ=z/h,
(21)
so its skeleton and integrated latent-heat corrections are small in 1/R and S/R respectively. The leading strain-dominated mush temperature profile obeys κT′′+αzT′=0. Put δ=2κ/α​ and H=h/δ. Integrating first for T′ and then for T gives
T′=Be−z2/δ2,T(z)≃TE​+ΔTerfHerf(z/δ)​.​
(22)
The error function profile follows from integrating a Gaussian, with erfx=(2/π​)∫0x​e−s2ds. Matching the roof gradient gives the requested transcendental thickness relation
G=π​δ2ΔT​erfHe−H2​,h=δH,δ=ΠE22κμ​​.​
(23)
For prescribed G>0 and ΔT>0 the right side decreases continuously from infinity to zero as H increases, so it fixes one positive leading-order thickness. The resulting h must still satisfy the assumed ordering and the phase-fraction constraints.
To find the product composition, integrate the exact solute balance from the roof to an arbitrary depth. With f=1−ϕ,
f(z)C(z)=C0​−Vα​[z(C(z)−C0​)+∫zh​(C(s)−C0​)ds].
(24)
At z=0, the exact floor composition is therefore
Cs​=C0​−Vα​∫0h​(C−C0​)dz=C0​−mVα​∫0h​(T0​−T)dz.​
(25)
The minus sign has a direct physical meaning: the horizontal outflow removes more salt than arrives at the same depth from a uniform-C0​ liquid, leaving a depleted solid product. Using the leading error function profile and
∫0h​erf(z/δ)dz=herfH+π​δ​(e−H2−1)
(26)
yields
Cs​≃C0​−mVπ​αΔTδ​erfH1−e−H2​.​
(27)
The full solid fraction follows by substituting this same profile into the preceding integral formula for f(z). It has ϕ(h)=0 and 1−ϕ(0)=Cs​/CE​. A physical branch requires 0≤Cs​≤CE​ and 0≤ϕ≤1. In this monotone profile, Cs​≥0 ensures a nonnegative liquid fraction everywhere; it is not permissible to interpret a negative formal result as a material composition.
The error-function reduction is a regular leading approximation, not a uniform statement for every possible scaling of the thermal strain number. If αh2/κ becomes singularly large along the ordering limit, a thin basal layer can amplify the discarded terms. The printed two inequalities alone do not specify that extra scaling. In such a regime the displayed exact scalar equations and boundary conditions, rather than an unqualified zero-latent-heat profile, provide the appropriate thickness and composition calculation.
