Use Minkowski spacetime with gμν​=diag(1,−1,−1,−1) and units ℏ=c=1. The transformation rotates the two components in their internal plane. In particular,
δ(ϕ12​+ϕ22​)=2α(ϕ1​ϕ2​−ϕ2​ϕ1​)=0,δ(∂μ​ϕ1​∂μϕ1​+∂μ​ϕ2​∂μϕ2​)=0.
(1)
Thus both terms in the Lagrangian density are invariant: this is an internal rotation symmetry of two real scalar fields, rather than a transformation of spacetime. The equal masses are essential to this symmetry.
The Noether theorem gives the Noether current
jμ=∑k=12​∂(∂μ​ϕk​)∂L​αδϕk​​=ϕ2​∂μϕ1​−ϕ1​∂μϕ2​.
(2)
Indeed, the cross terms in its divergence cancel, and the two Klein-Gordon equations imply
∂μ​jμ=ϕ2​□ϕ1​−ϕ1​□ϕ2​=−m2ϕ2​ϕ1​+m2ϕ1​ϕ2​=0.
(3)
For fields decaying sufficiently at spatial infinity, there is no outward current flux. The conserved Noether charge is therefore
Q=∫d3x(ϕ2​ϕ˙​1​−ϕ1​ϕ˙​2​).​
(4)
In canonical quantization, the canonical momenta are πk​=ϕ˙​k​, with equal-time canonical commutation relations
[ϕk​(x),πl​(y)]=iδkl​δ3(x−y),[ϕk​,ϕl​]=[πk​,πl​]=0.
(5)
Consequently Q=∫d3x(π1​ϕ2​−π2​ϕ1​). Operators of different components commute, so this expression is Hermitian without an ordering correction. Write ∫p​=∫d3p/(2π)3, Ep​=p2+m2​ and akp​=apk​. Substituting the oscillator expansions and using the Dirac delta function from the spatial integral gives
Q=−2i​∫p​[(a1p​−a1,−p†​)(a2,−p​+a2p†​)−(a2p​−a2,−p†​)(a1,−p​+a1p†​)].
(6)
The terms with two annihilation operators cancel after p↦−p; the terms with two creation operators cancel in the same way. In the remaining terms use
[akp​,alq†​]=(2π)3δkl​δ3(p−q).
(7)
There is no commutator constant between the distinct components. The result is already in normal ordering:
Q=−i∫p​(a2p†​a1p​−a1p†​a2p​).​
(8)
For the charged oscillator basis of a scalar doublet, compute
[Q,a1p†​]=−ia2p†​,[Q,a2p†​]=ia1p†​,b±p†​=2​a1p†​∓ia2p†​​.
(9)
It follows that [Q,b±p†​]=±b±p†​. The Fock vacuum satisfies Q∣0⟩=0, so b+p†​∣0⟩ has charge +1, and b−p†​∣0⟩ has charge −1. To obtain a normalized one-particle state, replace the sharp momentum by ∫p​f(p)b±p†​∣0⟩, where ∫p​∣f(p)∣2=1. The charge is unchanged. Equivalently,
Q=∫p​(b+p†​b+p​−b−p†​b−p​).
(10)
The generator convention is δϕk​=iα[Q,ϕk​], which reproduces the original rotation signs.
For stability of a two-scalar quartic potential, the relevant potential energy density is
V=2m2​(ϕ12​+ϕ22​)+V4​,V4​=λ(ϕ12​−ϕ22​)2+2(λ+μ)ϕ12​ϕ22​.
(11)
Along either axis boundedness requires λ≥0; along ϕ1​=ϕ2​ it requires λ+μ≥0. Conversely the displayed sum is nonnegative whenever both conditions hold. With m2>0 the origin is then the unique global minimum, including the equality cases. Hence the stable-vacuum conditions are
λ≥0,μ≥−λ.​
(12)
Strict positivity of the quartic term away from the origin would instead require λ>0 and μ>−λ. That stronger condition is unnecessary for a positive mass term. If m=0, the same non-strict conditions give boundedness, but the equality cases have flat directions and do not give an isolated classical vacuum.
Finally, the interaction changes the variation of the potential energy by
δV4​=4α(λ−μ)ϕ1​ϕ2​(ϕ12​−ϕ22​).
(13)
The interacting Euler-Lagrange equations consequently give
∂μ​jμ=−4(λ−μ)ϕ1​ϕ2​(ϕ12​−ϕ22​).
(14)
Thus the same Noether charge is conserved for all field configurations precisely when μ=λ. Then V4​=λ(ϕ12​+ϕ22​)2 preserves the internal rotation symmetry of two real scalar fields; imposing stability additionally requires λ≥0.

Fix the phi-fourth theory convention Lint​=−λϕ4/4!, with mostly-minus Minkowski spacetime and ℏ=c=1. Define the scattering amplitude by stripping the overall momentum-conserving delta function from S−1=iM. If instead the coupling is defined by Lint​=−λϕ4, every vertex coupling below must be replaced by 24λ; the question does not specify this factorial convention.
The momentum-space Feynman rules are a Feynman propagator i/(k2−m2+i0) for each internal scalar line, a Feynman vertex −iλ with four scalar legs, and four-momentum conservation at each vertex. Integrate each independent loop momentum with d4ℓ/(2π)4 and divide by the Feynman-diagram symmetry factor. For an amputated scattering amplitude there are no external propagators; external states have the usual relativistic normalization. Sum the connected diagrams and all inequivalent assignments of the labelled external momenta. The overall delta function is (2π)4δ4(∑pin​−∑pout​).
These Feynman rules follow by expanding the Dyson series Texp(i∫d4xLint​) and applying the Wick theorem to the free-field correlation functions. A Wick contraction gives the free Feynman propagator; the 4! ways of contracting a vertex cancel its factorial denominator. The expansion's 1/V! cancels permutations of identical interaction insertions, while any remaining automorphisms give the Feynman-diagram symmetry factor. Fourier transformation produces momentum conservation, and the LSZ reduction formula amputates external propagators to obtain the scattering amplitude.
For six-point amplitudes in phi-fourth theory, an original pair of diagrams is:
In the tree-level Feynman diagram, let the internal four-momentum be r=p1​+p2​−q1​=q2​+q3​+q4​. The labelled diagram has symmetry factor one, so its contribution is
iMtree​=(−iλ)2r2−m2+i0i​,Mtree​=−r2−m2+i0λ2​.​
(15)
This is one channel. The full tree scattering amplitude sums the ten unordered partitions of the six labelled external legs into two groups of three, since (36​)/2=10.
For the one-loop triangle Feynman diagram, set P=p1​+p2​, Q12​=q1​+q2​, Q34​=q3​+q4​, so P=Q12​+Q34​. A consistent routing has denominators
D0​=ℓ2−m2+i0,D1​=(ℓ−P)2−m2+i0,D2​=(ℓ−Q34​)2−m2+i0.
(16)
With the external labels held fixed, no nontrivial graph automorphism remains, so again the symmetry factor is one. The contribution is
iM△​=(−iλ)3∫(2π)4d4ℓ​D0​D1​D2​i3​=λ3∫(2π)4d4ℓ​D0​D1​D2​1​.​
(17)
There are other labelled assignments and other one-loop topologies in the full scattering amplitude; the displayed expression belongs to the particular drawn diagram.
One can also express this scalar triangle Feynman integral using Feynman parameters. With x+y+z=1, completing the square in xD0​+yD1​+zD2​ gives
Δ=m2−xyP2−xzQ342​−yzQ122​.
(18)
The identity 1/(D0​D1​D2​)=2∫x,y,z≥0​dxdydzδ(1−x−y−z)/(xD0​+yD1​+zD2​)3 and the shifted momentum integral yield
∫(2π)4d4ℓ​D0​D1​D2​1​=−16π2i​∫x,y,z≥0​Δ−i0dxdydzδ(1−x−y−z)​.
(19)
Thus M△​=−λ3/(16π2) times the displayed parameter integral. It is ultraviolet finite in four dimensions, although physical threshold singularities must retain the Feynman i-epsilon prescription.
For the fixed-target production threshold, the incoming four-momenta are (E,p) and (m,0), with E=m2+∣p∣2​. Their invariant mass obeys
s=(p1​+p2​)2=2m2+2mE.
(20)
In the centre-of-momentum frame, four final particles have total energy at least 4m, attained when all four are at rest in that frame. Therefore s≥16m2, equivalently E≥7m, and
∣p∣≥43​m.​
(21)
This is the kinematic threshold for m>0. Exactly at equality the final phase space has zero volume; a nonzero production rate requires a strict inequality.

Let the masses of Φ and ϕ be M and μ, respectively. Write their external four-momenta as P=k1​+k2​, with P2=M2 and k12​=k22​=μ2. The condition M>2μ opens the two-body decay phase space. Use mostly-minus Minkowski spacetime, the Dirac propagator i(k+m)/(k2−m2+i0), and the convention that an amputated connected diagram contributes iM.
There is no tree-level three-scalar Feynman vertex in the displayed interaction. The leading decay amplitude has one Φ Yukawa interaction and two ϕ Yukawa interactions, so is of order Gg2. The Yukawa fermion triangle is:
The two inequivalent cyclic orderings around the fermion loop are related by reversal and interchange of k1​,k2​. They give equal integrals: reversing all internal four-momenta leaves the masses and denominators unchanged, and the scalar gamma matrix trace identities below give the same numerator. Both must be included. The closed fermion loop sign supplies −1, and each ordering has the product
(−1)(−iG)(−ig)2i3=−Gg2.
(22)
There is no extra 1/2! in the decay amplitude. Such a factor belongs to integration over the identical-particle final phase space when calculating a decay rate.
Route a=ℓ, b=ℓ+k1​, c=ℓ−k2​ and define Da​=a2−m2+i0, and similarly for Db​,Dc​. A regulated leading decay amplitude is
iMloop​=−2Gg2μR2ϵ​∫(2π)4−2ϵd4−2ϵℓ​Da​Db​Dc​tr[(a+m)(b+m)(c+m)]​.​
(23)
Here dimensional regularization preserves translation of the loop momentum, and μR​ is its reference mass scale. Factors μR2ϵ​ account for the loop's dimension; dimensionally continued couplings can equivalently carry these powers. No γ5 occurs in this scalar loop, so there is no γ5 regularization ambiguity.
Using gamma matrix trace identities, traces with an odd number of gamma matrices vanish, while tr1=4 and tr(γμγν)=4gμν. Thus the numerator simplifies completely to
N​=4m[m2+a⋅b+a⋅c+b⋅c]=4m[m2+3ℓ2+2ℓ⋅(k1​−k2​)−k1​⋅k2​],k1​⋅k2​=2M2−2μ2​.​
(24)
For a reduction to standard scalar integrals, disregard the vanishing infinitesimals only in the numerator. Since Da​+Db​+Dc​=3ℓ2+2ℓ⋅(k1​−k2​)+2μ2−3m2 up to those infinitesimals,
N=4m[Da​+Db​+Dc​+4m2−μ2−2M2​].
(25)
Define the scalar bubble and scalar triangle Feynman integral by
B(s)=μR2ϵ​∫(2π)4−2ϵd4−2ϵℓ​(ℓ2−m2+i0)((ℓ+q)2−m2+i0)1​,q2=s,C=μR2ϵ​∫(2π)4−2ϵd4−2ϵℓ​Da​Db​Dc​1​.
(26)
Cancelling each denominator in turn gives two bubbles with external invariant μ2 and one with invariant M2. Therefore
iMloop​=−8mGg2[2B(μ2)+B(M2)+(4m2−μ2−2M2​)C].​
(27)
This explicitly displays the complete gamma matrix trace simplification, the two loop orientations and the overall phase convention.
There is a renormalization qualification to this answer. For m=0 the loop is logarithmically divergent; retaining just the unregulated four-dimensional integral would not specify a finite physical decay amplitude. To see the divergence, a Feynman parameter and a shifted loop momentum give
B(s)=16π2i​[ϵˉ1​−∫01​dxlogμR2​m2−x(1−x)s−i0​]+O(ϵ),ϵˉ1​=ϵ1​−γE​+log(4π).
(28)
The scalar triangle Feynman integral is ultraviolet finite. Hence
Mloop,div​=−16π2ϵˉ24mGg2​.
(29)
A cubic counterterm from a Yukawa fermion triangle, Lct​=−δhΦϕ2/2, contributes −δh to M and cancels the pole with δhdiv​=−24mGg2/(16π2ϵˉ). This is an instance of counterterm closure of a massive Yukawa theory. After subtraction the physical answer is M=−hR​+Mloop,ren​, where a renormalization condition fixes the finite cubic coupling hR​. The displayed interaction alone does not supply that condition. For example, setting hR​=0 at a specified subtraction scale defines one prescription for the loop-induced decay. The regulated expressions above remain the lowest-order loop answer without such extra data. For m=0 the trace, and therefore this scalar triangle contribution, vanishes identically.
Now take the Hermitian pseudoscalar Yukawa interaction with the sign printed in the PDF. Varying with respect to the Dirac adjoint gives the Dirac equation
(iγμ∂μ​−m−gϕ+iGΦγ5)ψ=0.​
(30)
Varying with respect to ψ and integrating by parts gives the adjoint Dirac equation
i(∂μ​ψˉ​)γμ+(m+gϕ)ψˉ​−iGΦψˉ​γ5=0.
(31)
The factor i in the coupling matters: ψˉ​γ5ψ is anti-Hermitian, so this factor makes the interaction Hermitian for real G.
For the axial-current divergence for scalar and pseudoscalar backgrounds, set S=m+gϕ and H=GΦ. The two equations are equivalently
γμ∂μ​ψ=−iSψ−Hγ5ψ,(∂μ​ψˉ​)γμ=iSψˉ​+Hψˉ​γ5.
(32)
In the divergence of the axial current, use {γ5,γμ}=0 and (γ5)2=1:
∂μ​(ψˉ​γμγ5ψ)​=(∂μ​ψˉ​)γμγ5ψ−ψˉ​γ5γμ∂μ​ψ=(iSψˉ​+Hψˉ​γ5)γ5ψ−ψˉ​γ5(−iSψ−Hγ5ψ).​
(33)
Thus
∂μ​j5μ​=2i(m+gϕ)ψˉ​γ5ψ+2GΦψˉ​ψ.​
(34)
This is the classical field-equation identity requested here. It includes explicit breaking by both backgrounds; it is not a claim that a renormalized quantum axial current has no chiral anomaly when gauge interactions are present.

In quantum electrodynamics, gauge invariance connects local phase freedom, electric-charge conservation and the restrictions on photon interactions. For definiteness take
L=−41​Fμν​Fμν+ψˉ​(iγμDμ​−m)ψ,Dμ​=∂μ​+ieAμ​,Fμν​=∂μ​Aν​−∂ν​Aμ​.
(35)
The gauge transformation
ψ↦e−ieα(x)ψ,ψˉ​↦ψˉ​eieα(x),Aμ​↦Aμ​+∂μ​α
(36)
has Dμ​ψ↦e−ieαDμ​ψ: the derivative of the local phase cancels the added gauge potential. Also Fμν​ is unchanged because mixed partial derivatives commute. Consequently every term in the Lagrangian density is invariant. This derives the minimal electromagnetic coupling of a Dirac field, including the interaction −eψˉ​γμψAμ​. A fermion mass is compatible with the symmetry, whereas a local Proca photon mass mγ2​Aμ​Aμ/2 is not.
The classical Maxwell equations are ∂μ​Fμν=jν, with jν=eψˉ​γνψ. Taking a divergence gives ∂ν​jν=0 because Fμν is antisymmetric. The same conservation law follows directly from the charged Dirac equation and its adjoint. For vanishing flux at spatial infinity, Q=∫d3xj0 is conserved. Gauss law identifies this charge with the electric flux through a surrounding surface. Particle-antiparticle creation is allowed, but its net electric charge is zero.
Local gauge invariance is also a redundancy in the potentials, rather than an extra propagating degree of freedom. The canonical momentum of A0​ vanishes, and its field equation enforces the Gauss law constraint in gauge theory, ∇⋅E=j0. After imposing the constraint and quotienting gauge freedom, a massless photon has two transverse physical polarizations. Gauge transformations vanishing at the boundary identify equivalent descriptions; constant phase transformations at infinity can act on charged states and generate their conserved charge. This distinction explains why gauge redundancy and electric charge are compatible.
In perturbative QED, the quadratic photon operator cannot be inverted before gauge fixing. Adding −(∂μ​Aμ)2/(2ξ) gives the covariant gauge propagator
Dμν​(k)=k2+i0−i​[gμν​−(1−ξ)k2+i0kμ​kν​​].
(37)
This propagator contains longitudinal and timelike components, but these are not external physical photon states. A Gupta-Bleuler null-state quotient, or the equivalent BRST construction, removes them. In linear Lorenz gauge, δ(∂μ​Aμ)=□α, so the Faddeev-Popov determinant is independent of Aμ​ and the Faddeev-Popov ghost fields decouple. This last statement concerns the linear gauge; Abelian gauge groups alone do not guarantee decoupling in a nonlinear gauge condition.
At the quantum level, the vector symmetry gives a Ward identity. One way to obtain it is to change variables by a localized phase in a time-ordered correlation function containing ψ(y) and ψˉ​(z). The action variation becomes a current divergence; the two field insertions contribute opposite contact terms, since their charges are opposite. Fourier transformation turns the divergence into the photon momentum, and amputating the external fermion propagators turns the two contact terms into the difference of their inverse propagators. With the charge removed from the proper vertex and the overall propagator factor i removed from its inverse, the result is the proper-vertex Ward-Takahashi identity in QED
kμ​Γμ(p+k,p)=S−1(p+k)−S−1(p).​
(38)
At tree level this is simply kμ​γμ=(p+k−m)−(p−m). Between on-shell external spinors, the inverse propagators vanish, so the full physical scattering amplitude satisfies kμ​Mμ=0. Therefore replacing a photon polarization vector by εμ​+ckμ​ leaves the amplitude unchanged. The identity applies to the complete required sum of diagrams, not necessarily each diagram separately. It also ensures cancellation of the gauge parameter from physical observables when all terms at the chosen perturbative order are included.
For the photon two-point function, the Ward identity implies transverse photon vacuum polarization:
kμ​Πμν(k)=0,Πμν(k)=(k2gμν−kμkν)Π(k2).
(39)
Thus ultraviolet subtraction renormalizes the gauge-invariant kinetic term, rather than requiring a local photon-mass counterterm. Perturbative unbroken QED retains a massless photon. Transversality by itself would not exclude a nonlocal massless pole in a different theory; the assertion here includes the perturbative QED setting.
Taking k→0 in the proper-vertex identity gives Γμ(p,p)=∂S−1(p)/∂pμ​. Matching the divergent coefficients of the derivative and vertex terms yields the Ward identity for QED renormalization constants, Z1​=Z2​, in a gauge-preserving prescription. If ψ0​=Z2​​ψ and A0​=Z3​​A, comparison of the interaction coefficients gives
e0​=μRϵ​Z2​Z3​​Z1​​e=μRϵ​Z3−1/2​e(d=4−2ϵ).
(40)
Hence charge renormalization is fixed by photon wave-function renormalization. Gauge invariance constrains counterterms and relates renormalizations; it does not make the electric charge independent of scale.
The Soft photon theorem gives another physical manifestation. Attaching a photon of small momentum k to the external charged legs gives the leading factor
Msoft​=eMhard​∑i​ηi​Qi​pi​⋅kpi​⋅ε​+O(k0),
(41)
where Qi​ is charge in units of e and ηi​=+1 for outgoing and −1 for incoming legs. Replacing ε by k makes this factor proportional to ∑i​ηi​Qi​, which vanishes by electric charge conservation. Massless photons also cause infrared divergences; physical inclusive measurements combine unresolved real emission with virtual corrections, rather than treating either contribution alone as an observable.
Finally, the vector gauge symmetry of a Dirac fermion in QED is anomaly-free, so it can be maintained by regularization and renormalization. This must be distinguished from the axial current: its divergence has explicit mass terms, as in Question 3, and can additionally have a quantum chiral anomaly. Conservation of the gauge current does not imply axial-current conservation. The resulting picture is that gauge invariance simultaneously organizes the physical photon states, enforces electric-charge conservation and ties together amplitudes and ultraviolet subtractions.
