Here write σ for the buoyancy perturbation about the resting constant-N stratification; the total buoyancy also includes its background gradient N2. Use kinematic perturbation pressure p. The weak-forcing Linearized Boussinesq equations are
ut​=−px​+F,wt​=−pz​+σ,σt​=−N2w,ux​+wz​=0.
(18)
Weakness means that perturbation advection is small compared with the retained linear terms. With the specified streamfunction convention, the transverse vorticity is
η=uz​−wx​=ψzz​+ψxx​=∇2ψ.
(19)
Differentiate the horizontal momentum equation in z and subtract the x derivative of vertical momentum. The pressure derivatives cancel, giving
∇2ψt​=Fz​−σx​,∇2ψtt​+N2ψxx​=Fzt​.​
(20)
The second equation uses σt​=N2ψx​ and is the forced internal-wave equation.
For free waves at frequency ∣ω∣≪N, the internal gravity wave dispersion relation gives ∣k∣/∣m∣≃∣ω∣/N≪1. Thus the horizontal response scale is much larger than the vertical scale. Vertical acceleration is small in the vertical momentum equation, leaving the hydrostatic approximation pz​=σ. Correspondingly the transverse vorticity is predominantly uz​: neglect −wx​=ψxx​ relative to ψzz​ in the inertial vorticity term. The restoring term N2ψxx​ must still be retained, since it balances the small time derivatives.
For m=0 put ψ=eimzP(x,t), σ=eimzB(x,t) and interpret physical fields as real parts. The reduced equation is
Ptt​−c2Pxx​=−mi​ft​,c=∣m∣N​.​
(21)
One can solve it while obtaining the buoyancy simultaneously. Define U=imP and H=iB/(cm). The first-order hydrostatic equations become
Ut​=f+cHx​,Ht​=cUx​.
(22)
Hence R+​=U+H and R−​=U−H obey
(∂t​−c∂x​)R+​=f,(∂t​+c∂x​)R−​=f.
(23)
Apply the method of characteristics with zero fields in the past. With
I±​(x,t)=∫−∞t​f(x∓c(s−t),s)ds,
(24)
the solution is R±​=I±​. Consequently the hydrostatic response to a localized horizontal force is
ψ=−2mieimz​(I+​+I−​),σ=−2icmeimz​(I+​−I−​).​
(25)
These are exactly the required two characteristic integrals, derived with their relative sign fixed by the buoyancy equation. Quiet-past initial conditions exclude additional freely propagating solutions.
For the consistency check, set τ=s−t, so both integrals run over the fixed range (−∞,0). Differentiation at fixed x,z now gives
wt​=2mieimz​∫−∞0​[fxt​(x−cτ,t+τ)+fxt​(x+cτ,t+τ)]dτ.​
(26)
This change of variable is important: it includes the time dependence of both the sampled forcing and the moving integration geometry without omitting a term. Each straight path intersects ∣x∣≤a for a time interval of length at most 2a/c. If L=sup∣fxt​∣, then
∣wt​∣≤∣m∣c2a​L=N2a​L.
(27)
The characteristic size of the hydrostatic buoyancy and pressure-gradient balance is ∣m∣afmax​, as follows from the analogous bound on B in its integral formula. Thus the omitted acceleration has relative scale at most a constant times
∣m∣Nfmax​L​≪1.
(28)
Together with the low-frequency assumption, this verifies the requested hydrostatic consistency. It is a comparison of characteristic magnitudes, rather than division by a buoyancy value at a node where both terms may vanish. The capital N here is the buoyancy frequency, as printed in the PDF.
For t≥0, only 0≤s≤t contributes. If ∣x∣>ct+a, every point x∓c(s−t) lies outside the source interval, so both integrals vanish:
ψ=σ=0for ∣x∣>ct+a.​
(29)
This finite propagation cone belongs to the reduced hydrostatic wave equation.
The hypotheses do not require an even forcing, so no single symmetric graph follows for every permitted f. For an illustrative smooth even nonnegative profile, U is even and H is odd. At late time and fixed x, if Af​=∫−aa​f(r,∞)dr, then
U⟶2cAf​​,H⟶2c1​[∫x∞​f(r,∞)dr−∫−∞x​f(r,∞)dr].
(30)
Thus the streamfunction develops a broad nearly level interior response with outgoing fronts, while the buoyancy changes sign across the source and approaches opposite interior plateaus to its left and right. Both vanish beyond the fronts. For m>0, a vertical phase with sin(mz)=1 gives real fields ψ=U/m and σ=cmH. The figure plots those profiles up to positive normalization factors, for an even compact forcing increasing slowly from zero.
