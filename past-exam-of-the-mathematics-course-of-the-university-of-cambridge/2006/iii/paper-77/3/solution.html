Choose a fixed vertical datum so the physical free surface is at H+ζ, with H constant; its depth above the bottom is h=H+ζ−b. The gradient of the constant datum does not enter the pressure force. With DH​=∂t​+u∂x​+v∂y​, the rotating shallow water equations are
DH​u−fv=−gζx​,DH​v+fu=−gζy​,ht​+(hu)x​+(hv)y​=0.​
(31)
The fluid is hydrostatic with depth-independent horizontal velocity at this order. The pressure-gradient force uses the free-surface slope, not the layer-depth slope: ∇ζ=∇h+∇b.
Let q=vx​−uy​ be relative vertical vorticity, qa​=f+q the absolute vorticity, and δ=ux​+vy​. Taking the horizontal curl of the two momentum equations and expanding the advective derivatives gives
DH​q=−(f+q)δ,DH​qa​=−qa​δ.​
(32)
Mass conservation is DH​h=−hδ. The two stretching factors cancel, proving the exact shallow-water potential vorticity conservation law
DH​(hqa​​)=0.​
(33)
The denominator is h, the actual column thickness that changes under horizontal convergence; an arbitrary surface elevation ζ does not obey that column mass equation. There is no w∂z​ term because the reduced layer quantities have no vertical dependence. The vertical motion has already supplied the layer-thickness equation, rather than remaining an independent coordinate of this model.
Now take H=h00​ and assume ∣q∣≪∣f∣, ∣ζ∣,∣b∣≪H. To first order,
hqa​​=Hf​+Hq​−H2fζ​+H2fb​+second-order terms.​
(34)
For a small Rossby number, leading geostrophic flow satisfies fv=gζx​ and fu=−gζy​. Thus
ψ=fgζ​,u=−ψy​,v=ψx​,q=∇H2​ψ,∇H2​=∂x2​+∂y2​.
(35)
For f=0, the barotropic deformation radius is LR​=gH​/∣f∣. Multiplication of the first-order PV by H gives
Q=Hhqa​​≃f+Hf​b+∇H2​ψ−LR2​ψ​.​
(36)
This is the required shallow-water quasi-geostrophic potential vorticity, including the bottom contribution.
Write S=fϵ/H and L=LR​. The initial state has ψ=0, so Qi​=f+(f/H)b. The specified homogenization sets Qf​=f in ∣y∣<a and leaves it unchanged outside. Hence the requested change is
ΔQ(y)={−Sy,0,​∣y∣<a,∣y∣>a.​​
(37)
For f>0 this is a descending straight segment from +Sa to −Sa, with zero exterior values and jumps at the two edges. Endpoint values do not affect the inversion. It is the anomaly, not the full final PV profile, that appears as the source below.
For the zonal mean, v=0, u=−ψ′(y), and potential-vorticity inversion requires
ψ′′−L−2ψ=ΔQ,
(38)
with decaying disturbance at both infinities. The odd source makes the unique decaying solution odd. A particular interior solution is SL2y, so write
ψin​=SL2y+Csinh(y/L),ψout​=sgn(y)Dexp[−(∣y∣−a)/L].
(39)
Both ψ and ψ′ must be continuous at y=±a: a jump would introduce a delta function or its derivative into a source that has only finite jumps. Matching at a gives
D=SL2a+Csinh(a/L),−D/L=SL2+(C/L)cosh(a/L),
(40)
and therefore
C=−SL2(a+L)e−a/L,D=2SL2​[a−L+(a+L)e−2a/L].
(41)
Differentiate the interior streamfunction to obtain
u(y)=HϵfLR2​​[−1+(1+LR​a​)e−a/LR​coshLR​y​],∣y∣<a.​
(42)
The exterior profiles needed for the sketches are u=(D/L)e−(∣y∣−a)/L and ζ=(f/g)sgn(y)De−(∣y∣−a)/L.
For L≫a, put A=a/L, Y=y/L, so ∣Y∣≤A. Uniform Taylor expansion gives
(1+A)e−AcoshY=1+2Y2−A2​+O(A3).
(43)
Consequently
u=HϵfLR2​​[2LR2​y2−a2​+O(LR3​a3​)],∣y∣<a.​
(44)
For f>0 the leading interior current is westward and approximately parabolic.
For L≪a and away from the edges, u≃−SL2 and ζ=(f/g)ψ≃ϵy: the surface follows the bottom slope centrally. Near ∣y∣=a, the exponentially thin corrections give positive edge jets of width L and peak scale SLa/2 when f>0. Those jets decay in the exterior. The elevation is odd, has opposite-sign extrema just inside the edges, and decays to zero outside; at the positive edge ζ(a)≃ϵ(a−L)/2.
