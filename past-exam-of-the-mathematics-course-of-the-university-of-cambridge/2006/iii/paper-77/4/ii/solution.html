For the Boussinesq approximation, subtract the resting background buoyancy N2z and denote its perturbation by b. With constant nonzero f and constant N>0, the leading geostrophic flow and hydrostatic balance define the quasi-geostrophic streamfunction
p′=fψ,ug​=(−ψy​,ψx​),b=fψz​.
(51)
The leading horizontal flow is nondivergent. At the next order, its relative vertical vorticity qg​=∇H2​ψ is stretched by the small vertical velocity, while the background buoyancy is advected vertically. Thus the curl and buoyancy equations give
Dg​∇H2​ψ=fwz​,Dg​b+N2w=0,Dg​=∂t​−ψy​∂x​+ψx​∂y​.
(52)
These follow from Dqa​/Dt=−qa​∇H​⋅u with qa​≃f and ∇H​⋅ua​=−wz​, retaining the geostrophic advection of the first-order anomalies. Vertical advection of those anomalies is of higher quasi-geostrophic order.
Differentiate the buoyancy equation in z. It is important not to assume without justification that ∂z​ commutes with Dg​. Their commutator on b is
[∂z​,Dg​]b=ug,z​⋅∇H​b=f1​(−by​,bx​)⋅(bx​,by​)=0,
(53)
by hydrostatic balance and thermal wind. Hence Dg​bz​=−N2wz​. Adding f/N2 times this equation to the vorticity equation eliminates wz​, yielding
Dg​Q=0,Q=f+∇H2​ψ+N2f2​ψzz​.​
(54)
This is the quasi-geostrophic potential-vorticity equation on an f-plane. The additive f makes Q equal to the first-order normalized Ertel PV of total buoyancy N2z+b; using a PV anomaly instead removes that constant and does not change the dynamics.
For stretched coordinates for quasi-geostrophic inversion, put
X=x,Y=y,Z=∣f∣N​z.
(55)
Then (f2/N2)∂z2​=∂Z2​, and the potential-vorticity relation becomes
Q=f+∇∗2​ψ,∇∗2​=∂X2​+∂Y2​+∂Z2​.​
(56)
Stable stratification makes this operator elliptic. Uniform N and nonzero f are essential to this simple constant-coefficient stretching.
