A statistic is sufficient when the conditional distribution of the observation given it does not depend on the parameter. It is minimal sufficient when it is, up to null sets, a measurable function of every sufficient statistic. For a dominated family, the likelihood-ratio criterion identifies two observations precisely when their likelihood ratio is independent of the parameter.
The Rao–Blackwell theorem says that conditioning a square-integrable unbiased estimator on a sufficient statistic preserves unbiasedness and cannot increase variance. The Cramér–Rao bound for an unbiased estimator of scalar θ is Varθ​θ≥1/I(θ). It requires parameter-independent support, differentiability, interchange of differentiation and integration, and finite positive Fisher information. More generally the numerator is the square of the derivative of the estimand. The bound follows from Eθ​[θ∂θ​logf]=1 and Cauchy–Schwarz.
