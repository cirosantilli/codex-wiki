The coefficient of u is nonnegative, so the maximum over [0,x] occurs at u=x and equals V(x). Therefore spending all income at every step is optimal.
If 1/(1+θ)<β<1 and x>0, invest all income for T steps and thereafter spend everything. Its expected return is x[β(1+θ)]T/(1−β), unbounded as T→∞. In fact a sufficiently small fixed positive consumption fraction gives infinite expected discounted reward if β[1+θ(1−c)]≥1. Thus the value is infinite and the problem has no finite-valued optimum. For β=1, spending everything already gives infinite value when x>0. If x=0, the value is zero. A mean condition alone, without nonnegative income or equivalent admissibility restrictions, would not make the model well-defined.
