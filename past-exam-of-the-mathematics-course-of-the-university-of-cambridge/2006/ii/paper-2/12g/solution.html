A cyclic binary code is a linear subspace of F2n​ invariant under cyclic shifts. Identify a word with its coefficient polynomial in F2​[X]/(Xn−1). Multiplication by X is the shift, so the code is precisely an ideal of that quotient ring. Its inverse image in the polynomial ring is a principal ideal (g) containing (Xn−1); its unique monic generator divides Xn−1. Conversely each monic divisor generates such an ideal. Thus the bijection is with monic divisors; g=1 gives the full code and g=Xn−1 the zero code.
For odd n, some m has n∣2m−1, so the cyclic multiplicative group of F2m​ contains a primitive nth root α. If a nonzero word with the specified roots had weight w<δ, write its nonzero positions as r1​,…,rw​. The root equations give
∑j=1w​cj​(αrj​)i=0(1≤i≤w).
(22)
Their matrix has determinant ∏j​αrj​∏j<l​(αrl​−αrj​)=0, by the Vandermonde formula and distinctness of the positions. Thus all coefficients would vanish, a contradiction. Hence minimum distance is at least δ.
For n=7, choose α with minimal polynomial X3+X+1. The roots α,α2 force also the conjugate α4, giving this cubic as generator. Its degree gives dimension four and its weight gives distance exactly three. Expressing c(α)=0 in a basis of F8​ gives a parity-check matrix with the seven distinct nonzero vectors of F23​ as columns. This is the binary [7,4,3] Hamming code. Its sixteen disjoint radius-one balls have 16(1+7)=128 words, so one-error correction is perfect. The other primitive cubic produces an equivalent coordinate-permuted code.
