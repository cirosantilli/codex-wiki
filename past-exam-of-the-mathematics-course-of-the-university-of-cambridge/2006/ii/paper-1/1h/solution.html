For every odd prime p and n≥1, the group of units modulo an integer (Z/pnZ)× is a cyclic group, of order φ(pn)=pn−1(p−1); a generator is a primitive root.
Modulo seven, 3,32,…,36 are 3,2,6,4,5,1, so three has order six. Moreover 36−1=728=7⋅104 is divisible by seven exactly once. We prove the lifting needed here. If u=1+7rv with r≥1 and 7∤v, the binomial expansion gives u7=1+7r+1v+O(7r+2), so v7​(u7−1)=r+1. Induction therefore gives
v7​(36⋅7j−1)=j+1.

Any exponent giving one modulo 7n is divisible by six. The order also divides 6⋅7n−1 by Lagrange theorem, while the displayed valuation rules out each proper divisor of this form. Consequently three is a primitive root modulo every 7n.
Since 23=1(mod7) and two is not one, its order modulo seven is three. A generator modulo 7n would reduce to a generator modulo seven, because reduction is a surjective group homomorphism. Thus two is never a primitive root modulo 7n. Finally every unit modulo eight is odd, and (2k+1)2=1+4k(k+1)≡1(mod8). All unit orders are at most two, whereas the unit group has four elements. There is no primitive root modulo eight.
