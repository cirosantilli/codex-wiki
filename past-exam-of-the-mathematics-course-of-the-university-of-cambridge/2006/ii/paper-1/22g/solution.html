Two norms are Lipschitz equivalent norms if constants c,C>0 satisfy c∥u∥1​≤∥u∥2​≤C∥u∥1​ for every u. On the vector space of finitely supported sequences, the norms ∥x∥1​=∑∣xi​∣ and ∥x∥∞​=max∣xi​∣ are not equivalent: a vector with N entries equal to one has the two norms N and one.
For dimension d<∞, choose a basis and write u=∑xi​ei​. Any norm satisfies ∥u∥≤∑∣xi​∣∥ei​∥≤C∣x∣2​. This also implies its continuity in the Euclidean coordinates, by ∣∥u∥−∥v∥∣≤∥u−v∥≤C∣x−y∣2​. Its positive continuous values on the compact Euclidean unit sphere have a positive minimum c. Homogeneity gives c∣x∣2​≤∥u∥, proving equivalence with the Euclidean norm and hence between any two norms.
If a finite-dimensional subspace U of a normed space contains a sequence converging in the ambient norm, its coefficient vectors are Cauchy by this equivalence. The coefficients converge in Rd or Cd, and the reconstructed vector lies in U and is the ambient limit. Thus finite-dimensional subspaces are closed.
A finite-dimensional normed space has nonempty open balls with compact closures by equivalence and Heine-Borel. Conversely let an open set O have compact closure. Choose w∈O and r>0 such that B(w,r)⊂O. Its closed ball of radius r/2 is a closed subset of the compact closure of O and is therefore compact. Translation and scaling make the closed unit ball compact.
If the space were infinite-dimensional, construct unit vectors xj​ with dist(xj​,span(x1​,…,xj−1​))>1/2. Here is the needed Riesz lemma: for a proper closed subspace M, choose z∈/M, put d=dist(z,M)>0, and choose m∈M with ∥z−m∥<2d; then (z−m)/∥z−m∥ has distance greater than 1/2 from M. The finite spans are closed by the earlier argument, so this constructs the sequence. Its members are separated by more than 1/2, and have no convergent subsequence, contradicting compactness. Therefore the space is finite-dimensional exactly when it has a nonempty open set with compact closure.
