Let L be the splitting field of the separable polynomial. Its Galois group is G=AutK​(L), acting faithfully on its n distinct roots, hence as a subgroup of Sn​. An automorphism preserves each root's minimal polynomial. Conversely an isomorphism K(α)→K(β) for roots of the same irreducible factor extends to an embedding of L in an algebraic closure, and normality of the splitting field makes the image L itself. Thus the root orbits are precisely the irreducible factors' root sets. The action is transitive exactly when the polynomial is irreducible.
If n=p is prime, a transitive action has p∣∣G∣ by orbit-stabilizer, so Cauchy theorem for groups supplies an element of order p. Its permutation must be a p-cycle. Conversely a p-cycle already acts transitively on all p roots. This proves the stated equivalence for prime degree.
The factorization theorem modulo a good prime says that if an integer polynomial retains its degree and has square-free reduction modulo p, and that reduction's irreducible factor degrees are d1​,…,dr​, its Galois group over Q contains a permutation with disjoint cycle lengths d1​,…,dr​. Equivalently the finite-field Galois group, generated by Frobenius on the roots, occurs as an unramified decomposition subgroup. The degree-retention qualification matters for nonmonic polynomials.
For the given quintic, Eisenstein criterion at three proves irreducibility. Modulo two it factors as
x5+x+1=(x2+x+1)(x3+x2+1).
(48)
Neither factor has a root in F2​, so both are irreducible, and they are distinct; the reduction is separable. Thus G contains an element of cycle type (2)(3), whose cube is a transposition. It is also transitive of degree five.
To prove that this forces the full symmetric group here, form the graph on the five roots with an edge for each conjugate in G of that transposition. The graph is G-invariant, and transitivity makes its connected components all the same size. That size divides five; there is an edge, so the size cannot be one. Hence the graph is connected. Transpositions along the edges of a connected graph generate all transpositions (move along a path and conjugate successive swaps), and hence generate S5​. They all belong to G, giving
G=S5​.​
(49)
