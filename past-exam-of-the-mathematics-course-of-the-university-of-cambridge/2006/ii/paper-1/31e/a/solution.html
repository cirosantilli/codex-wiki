Let E=e−ikx+k2t. Expanding ∂t​(Eq)+∂x​(EX) shows that the required flux is
X=−qx​−ikq.​
(86)
Indeed Xx​−ikX=−qxx​−k2q, cancelling the k2q from the time derivative. Introduce a potential φ by φx​=Eq, φt​=E(qx​+ikq). Equality of its mixed derivatives is precisely the conservation law. With φ=Eμ, these equations become the linear auxiliary system
μx​−ikμ=q,μt​+k2μ=qx​+ikq.​
(87)
This is a Lax pair in the affine-potential form used for linear evolution equations. If a homogeneous matrix pair is preferred, set Ψ=(μ,1)T and use Ψx​=(ik0​q0​)Ψ, Ψt​=(−k20​qx​+ikq0​)Ψ. Its zero-curvature condition has upper-right entry qt​−qxx​ and all other entries zero.
