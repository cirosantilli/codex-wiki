Past exam of the mathematics course of the University of Cambridge / 2006 / ii / Paper 1 / 17F / Solution

For a finite connected plane graph, with v vertices, e edges and f faces including the unbounded face, Euler formula for a connected planar graph is
v−e+f=2.​
(46)
If the graph is a tree, e=v−1 and f=1. Otherwise remove an edge on a cycle. This preserves connectivity and joins the two faces on its sides, so both e and f decrease by one. Repeating reaches a tree, preserving v−e+f throughout and proving the formula.
For a simple connected graph with v≥3, each face boundary walk has length at least three, with bridges counted twice in the walk; the sum of lengths is 2e. Hence 3f≤2e, which with Euler gives e≤3v−6. If it has no triangles in a graph, no face boundary walk can have length three, and every boundary length is at least four. Thus 4f≤2e, giving
e≤2v−4for a triangle-free planar graph with v≥3.​
(47)
For a disconnected graph, add noncrossing bridges between its components; this preserves simplicity and creates no triangles, reducing to the connected bound.
Every triangle-free planar graph with at least three vertices has average degree 2e/v<4, and therefore a vertex of degree at most three. Remove one such vertex. Its remaining graph is again triangle-free and planar. Induction, with graphs on at most two vertices as the base cases, provides a graph colouring with four colours of the remainder. At most three colours appear on the removed vertex's neighbours, so a fourth colour extends the graph colouring. This proves four-colourability without using the four-colour theorem.
