A bounded operator on a complex Hilbert space is self-adjoint when T=T∗, equivalently ⟨Tx,y⟩=⟨x,Ty⟩. The spectral theorem for compact self-adjoint operators says its nonzero spectrum consists of real eigenvalues of finite multiplicity, with at most countably many and with accumulation possible only at zero. Their eigenspaces are mutually orthogonal; their closed span is the orthogonal complement of the kernel. On that span the operator has the norm-convergent spectral action Tx=∑j​λj​⟨x,ej​⟩ej​.
Take H=ℓ2(N) and Ten​=en​/n. It is self-adjoint and has infinite-dimensional range. Truncation to the first N coordinates is a finite-rank operator and differs from T in norm by 1/(N+1), so T is compact.
The resolvent set comprises those λ for which T−λI has a bounded everywhere-defined inverse; its complement is the spectrum, and the point spectrum consists of eigenvalues. In this example
σp​(T)={1/n:n≥1},σ(T)={0}∪{1/n:n≥1},ρ(T)=C∖σ(T)​.
(60)
For λ outside the displayed closed set the inverse is diagonal with entries (1/n−λ)−1 uniformly bounded, proving membership of the resolvent. Each 1/n has eigenvector en​. Zero is not an eigenvalue, but ∥Ten​∥→0 prevents a bounded inverse; also (1/n)n​∈ℓ2 has no preimage since that would be the non-square-summable constant sequence. This justifies every asserted spectral case.
