OurBigBook.com Words: 112
For the plain pseudoprime assertion use the Fermat primality test. In the finite group of units modulo an integer, let
L={b∈(Z/NZ)×:bN−1=1}.
(30)
It is the kernel of a group homomorphism, because the unit group is an abelian group. The given witness makes L a proper subgroup. By Lagrange's theorem for finite groups, ∣L∣≤φ(N)/2. Thus at least half of all units fail the test. Removing base one removes a passing base, so among the permitted 1<b<N there remain φ(N)−∣L∣ failures out of φ(N)−1 bases, still at least half.
The same argument works for the Euler–Jacobi test: its passing bases form the kernel of b↦b(N−1)/2(b/N)−1. One must not assert that strong-test passing bases form a subgroup; the kernel argument above concerns the Fermat statement requested here.
