OurBigBook.com Words: 134
Identify the pointed torus with R2/Z2 with p at the origin. Its fundamental group is Z2, generated by the two coordinate loops. A homeomorphism fixing p induces an automorphism of this group, giving
φ(f)=f∗​∈GL(2,Z)​.
(58)
Functoriality makes it a homomorphism. It is nontrivial, indeed onto: every integer invertible matrix defines a pointed torus homeomorphism by x↦Ax modulo the euclidean lattice.
If f∗​=I, lift f to a continuous F:R2→R2 with F(0)=0. Its deck-transformation law is F(x+n)=F(x)+n for every n∈Z2, because the induced map on coordinate loops is identity. Then
Ht​([x])=[(1−t)F(x)+tx]
(59)
is well defined: adding n changes the bracketed expression by exactly n. It is a continuous pointed homotopy from f to identity. Hence every kernel element is homotopic to the identity, although the intermediate maps need not be homeomorphisms.
