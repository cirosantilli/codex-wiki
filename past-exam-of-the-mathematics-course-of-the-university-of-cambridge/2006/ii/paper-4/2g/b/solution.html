Suppose the continuous functions converged pointwise to the rationality indicator. Each FN​ is closed, since
FN​=⋂n≥N​(fn−1​((−∞,1/3])∪fn−1​([2/3,∞))).
(3)
Pointwise convergence implies that every point belongs to some FN​: its eventual values lie near either zero or one. Thus R=⋃N​FN​. By the Baire category theorem, some FN​ contains a nonempty open interval I. Choose a rational r∈I and an irrational s∈I. For sufficiently large n≥N, convergence gives fn​(r)>2/3 and fn​(s)<1/3. The intermediate value theorem then forces fn​ to take the value 1/2 between them, at a point in I⊆FN​. This contradicts the defining condition of FN​. Therefore the rationality indicator is not a pointwise limit of continuous functions, the rationality indicator is not Baire class one result.
