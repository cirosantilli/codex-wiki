The Lie group PSL(2,C) is a second-countable space. For every point of its discrete subgroup, choose the first basic neighbourhood isolating that point; different elements require different such neighbourhoods. This injects G into a countable set, proving countability.
A discrete subgroup is closed and meets compact sets in finitely many points. Indeed, distinct subgroup elements accumulating anywhere would give quotients converging to identity, contradicting an identity neighbourhood containing no other subgroup element.
