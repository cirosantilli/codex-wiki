Normal coordinates at an event have gab​=ηab​ and first metric derivatives, equivalently connection coefficients, zero there. They describe freely falling frames in which nongravitational laws take their special-relativistic form to first order, expressing the Equivalence principle. Curvature and tidal effects cannot generally be removed throughout a neighbourhood.

The first term vanishes by antisymmetry and the second by the geodesic equation, proving conservation in every coordinate system. A stationary metric requires the Killing vector to be timelike in the region concerned. For particle energy, the tangent should be future timelike for a massive particle, with affine parameter proportional to proper time, or future null with appropriate momentum normalization for a photon; normalize the future timelike Killing vector relative to the chosen stationary observers or at infinity. Then the conserved quantity is proportional to their Killing energy. A spacelike Killing vector instead represents momentum, not stationary time translation.
