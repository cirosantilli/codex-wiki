A plane crystallographic group is a discrete subgroup of the Euclidean plane's isometry group with compact quotient; equivalently a compact set has translates covering the plane. We prove the crystallographic restriction theorem without assuming its conclusion. The orientation-preserving subgroup G+ has index at most two and still has a cocompact group action. Its subgroup T of translations is discrete as an additive subgroup of R2.
Suppose T were trivial. Linear parts of orientation-preserving plane isometries commute, so every group commutator in G+ is a translation. Thus G+ would be an abelian group. Every member would then commute with the given nontrivial rotation g and preserve its unique fixed point. A discrete subgroup of the compact rotation group about that point is finite. Then G would be finite, contradicting cocompactness on the unbounded plane. Hence T contains a nonzero vector v.
If g is a half-turn its order is already two. Otherwise its linear part R rotates v to a linearly independent vector Rv, and conjugating translations shows that Rv∈T. Thus T spans the plane and is a euclidean lattice. A discrete additive subgroup meets every bounded closed set in finitely many points: an accumulating sequence would give nonzero differences tending to zero, contradicting isolation of the identity. To see the euclidean lattice assertion directly, choose a shortest nonzero translation v. Translations on its line are integer multiples of v. Reducing all parallel components modulo v shows that nonzero perpendicular components are bounded away from zero, since otherwise infinitely many translation vectors would accumulate in a compact rectangle. Choose a vector w with the smallest positive perpendicular component; reducing by w and then v proves T=Zv+Zw.
Conjugation by g preserves this euclidean lattice. In its euclidean lattice basis, R is therefore an invertible integer matrix with determinant one. Its trace is an integer and is also 2cosθ, where θ is the rotation angle. Since g is nontrivial,
2cosθ∈{−2,−1,0,1}.
