A divisor on a complex manifold, specialized to a compact Riemann surface, is a finite sum D=∑p​np​p with integer coefficients, and degD=∑p​np​. Two divisors are linearly equivalent when D′=D+(h) for a nonzero meromorphic function h, whose principal divisor on a complex manifold records zero orders minus pole orders. Define
L(D)={0}∪{f meromorphic:(f)+D≥0},ℓ(D)=dimC​L(D).
(61)
Multiplication by 1/h gives an isomorphism L(D)→L(D′), because (f/h)+D′=(f)+D. Its inverse is multiplication by h, proving ℓ(D′)=ℓ(D).
On the Riemann sphere move P to infinity by a Möbius coordinate. Meromorphic functions on the sphere are rational; those with no finite poles and a pole of order at most one at infinity are exactly az+b. Thus ℓ(P)=2, without using Riemann–Roch.
The Riemann-Roch theorem for a compact connected genus-g surface is ℓ(D)−ℓ(K−D)=degD+1−g, where K is a canonical divisor. Choose D=(g+1)P. Since the second dimension is nonnegative, ℓ(D)≥2. Constants span only one dimension, so a nonconstant f exists whose only possible pole is at P with order at most g+1. The degree of its holomorphic map to the sphere is the total pole order. Therefore
deg(f:C→S2)≤g+1​.
(62)
