Statistical temperature is defined by 1/T=(∂S/∂U)V,N​, with entropy S=kB​logΩ. Maximizing total entropy when two systems exchange energy makes their derivatives equal, explaining why equal temperature characterizes thermal equilibrium and why a thermometer can measure another system.
For a dilute monatomic ideal gas, Gaussian integration of momenta gives the classical partition function ZN​=VN/(N!λT3N​), with λT​=h/2πmkB​T​. Differentiating its logarithm gives
PV=NkB​T,U=23​NkB​T​.
(101)
At fixed volume, gas pressure is proportional to absolute temperature; at fixed pressure its volume is proportional to temperature. A low-density gas thermometer therefore supplies an absolute calibration independent of the gas species in the ideal limit. A liquid-in-glass column responds to the difference between liquid and glass thermal expansion, which need not be exactly linear. Calibration against gas temperatures and correction for expansion nonlinearities improve a simple two-point column scale; an ideal-gas law is not a law of the liquid itself.
For blackbody radiation, the electromagnetic mode density is 8πν2/c3 per volume and frequency interval, including two polarizations. For one photon mode, summing its occupation numbers gives Zν​=∑j≥0​e−jhν/(kB​T)=(1−e−hν/(kB​T))−1. Differentiation of logZν​ gives mean energy hν/(ehν/kB​T−1), so the Planck distribution follows. Converting to wavelength gives spectral radiance
Bλ​(T)=λ52hc2​ehc/(λkB​T)−11​.
(102)
At its maximum, z=hc/(λkB​T) satisfies 5(1−e−z)=z, whose positive root is 4.9651…. Thus the Wien displacement law is
λmax​T=4.9651…kB​hc​≃2.898×10−3 mK​.
(103)
This calibrates a wavelength-based radiation thermometer and avoids identifying a subjective colour with a universal temperature. A peak per frequency interval has a different numerical location, so the spectral convention must be stated. Real objects require emissivity and atmospheric corrections; fitting a measured spectrum is more informative than choosing its visually brightest colour. Integrating the Planck distribution also gives energy density proportional to T4 and radiative flux proportional to T4, supplying the complementary Stefan–Boltzmann law calibration.
