A Fourier mode with q=k2≥0 evolves under J−qdiag(κ1​,κ2​). Its trace remains negative for a homogeneously stable positive branch. Its determinant is
D(q)=κ1​κ2​q2+(sκ1​−u0​κ2​)q+s(2u02​−u0​).
(21)
If u0​κ2​≤sκ1​, this is positive for every q≥0. Otherwise its minimum is attained at q∗​=(u0​κ2​−sκ1​)/(2κ1​κ2​). Stability requires D(q∗​)>0, or
u0​κ2​−sκ1​<2κ1​κ2​s(2u02​−u0​)​.
(22)
In terms of d=κ2​/κ1​​, the boundary is the positive root of u0​d2−s=2ds(2u02​−u0​)​:
dc​(u0​)=2s​+u0​s(2u02​−u0​)​​.​
(23)
Within 1/2<u0​<s, all modes decay for d<dc​; a finite band grows for d>dc​, giving a Turing instability. Equality is neutral at one nonzero wavenumber.
For s=2, the positive lower branch 0<u0​<1/2 is unstable for every d. The upper branch is homogeneously stable for 1/2<u0​<2: it is stable to all modes to the left of dc​=2+4−2/u0​​ and unstable to spatial modes to the right. For u0​>2 it is already homogeneously unstable. The zero branch is stable to every spatial mode when r<0, because its mode eigenvalues are r−κ1​k2 and −s−κ2​k2.
