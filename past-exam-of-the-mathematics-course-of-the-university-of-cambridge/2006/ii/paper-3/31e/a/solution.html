The upper analytic function is M−1 and the lower analytic function is N(−k)−1. The Cauchy integral formula and the Sokhotski–Plemelj formula solve this jump as the upper and lower boundary values of (2πi)−1∫R​G(s)/(s−z)ds. At the lower point z=−k, with ℑk>0, this gives N(k)−1.
The rational part can be evaluated by closing the contour upward: ∫R​[(s−ip)(s+k)]−1ds=2πi/(k+ip). Thus the required linear integral equation is
N(k)=1−k+ipce−2px+8p3tN(ip)​+2πi1​∫R​s+kR(s)e2isx+8is3tN(s)​ds.​

It is supplemented by its value at k=ip, and follows under the usual decay or principal-value hypotheses of the stated Riemann-Hilbert problem.
