A binary block code is a subset C⊆{0,1}n; its length is the common word length n, its size is m=∣C∣, and its minimum distance is the least Hamming distance between distinct words. There are V(n,r)=∑j=0r​(jn​) words in a radius-r Hamming ball, since one chooses the positions to change.
Put t=⌊(d−1)/2⌋. Two radius-t Hamming balls about different codewords cannot meet: otherwise the triangle inequality would make their centres at distance at most 2t<d. Comparing their combined size with 2n gives the Hamming bound
A(n,d)V(n,t)≤2n.
(4)
For the other direction, start with one word and repeatedly add any word at distance at least d from every word already selected. Finiteness ensures that this process stops. At that point the radius-(d−1) Hamming balls cover the entire cube; an uncovered word could otherwise be added. These balls may overlap, but their total size is at least 2n. This gives the Gilbert–Varshamov bound
V(n,d−1)2n​≤A(n,d)≤V(n,⌊(d−1)/2⌋)2n​.​
(5)
