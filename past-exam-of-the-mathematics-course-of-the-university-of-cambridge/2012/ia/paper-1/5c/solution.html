For a point x on the plane, the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality gives ∣d∣=∣x⋅n∣≤∣x∣, because n is a unit vector. Equality is achieved at x=dn. Thus the distance from the origin is ∣d∣.
Let δ=p⋅n−d and let q=p−δn be the perpendicular projection of the sphere's center onto the plane. Every point of the plane has form x=q+u with u⋅n=0. The Pythagorean theorem gives
∣x−p∣2=∣u−δn∣2=∣u∣2+δ2.
(11)
If ∣δ∣=r, the sphere equation therefore forces u=0. There is exactly one contact point, q; it lies on both surfaces.
Write τ=a⋅(b×c)>0. The nonzero scalar triple product proves that a,b,c are linearly independent, so they form a positively oriented basis of R3 and define a nondegenerate tetrahedron.
For the three faces through the origin, choose the inward unit vectors
na​=∣b×c∣b×c​,nb​=∣c×a∣c×a​,nc​=∣a×b∣a×b​.
(12)
Their signs are correct because each has positive inner product with the opposite vertex vector. Write the center as p=αa+βb+γc. It lies on the interior side of every face. Tangency of the interior sphere therefore means signed distance r, not −r, from each of those three planes. Taking inner products gives
α∣b×c∣τ​=r,β∣c×a∣τ​=r,γ∣a×b∣τ​=r.
(13)
Solving proves the center formula
p=τr​(∣b×c∣a+∣c×a∣b+∣a×b∣c).​
(14)
This is the sphere center from three tetrahedron face distances; the interior hypothesis selects these signs rather than an exterior center.
Finally set N=a×b+b×c+c×a. The scalar triple product identities give N⋅a=N⋅b=N⋅c=τ. In particular N=0, and the plane through the three vertices is
Ψ: N⋅x=τ,dist(O,Ψ)=∣N∣τ​.​
(15)
The second formula follows by normalizing N to a unit vector and applying the first distance result.
