The three handle attachments give H2​(W;Z)≅Z3: there are no one-handles or three-handles. Use the capped core disks of handles as the homology basis, ordered by the framing labels −2,−1,+1. The surgery linking matrix gives the intersection form. Orient the three components so that the outer components have linking number 3 and the middle component has linking number 1 with each outer component. There are six crossings between the outer components, and two crossings between the middle component and each outer component; counting their signs gives these linking numbers. Reversing a component changes the corresponding row and column signs and does not change the invariants.
Thus the intersection form is
QW​=​−213​1−11​311​​.​
(1)
An exact matrix congruence diagonalizes this intersection form to
diag(−2,−21​,18).
(2)
Consequently its signature is σ(W)=−1​, and detQW​=18.
The long exact sequence in relative homology and Poincare-Lefschetz duality identify
H2​(W)⟶H2​(W,∂W)
(3)

The Smith normal form has first invariant factor 1, because the entries have greatest common divisor 1, and second invariant factor 1, because the upper-left two-by-two matrix minor has determinant 1. The last invariant factor is 18. Hence

Use generators a,b,c for the three one-handles in the generalized Heegaard diagram. Read a downward crossing of the cut from a left-hand hole to its right-hand partner as the corresponding generator. Traverse the attaching curve around the left-hand A hole, starting at its outgoing downward strand, and then traverse the attaching curve around the left-hand B hole in the same way. Their cut-crossing words are
r1​=abcab−1a−1c−1b−1,r2​=bcabc−1b−1a−1c−1.
(5)
These are the two relators imposed by the two handle attachments. The remaining boundary is a torus; no third relator should be added. The fundamental group presentation is therefore
π1​(Y)≅⟨a,b,c∣r1​,r2​⟩.​
(6)
The exponent-sum vectors are (1,−1,0) and (0,1,−1). Thus the abelianization is Z, with a,b,c all mapping to its positive generator t.
Apply the Fox derivatives and then this abelianization. The resulting Alexander matrix is
F(t)=(1−t2+t3t2−t​−1+t−t31−t2+t3​t2−t−1+t−t3​).
(7)
For example, the three occurrences of a in r1​ contribute 1,t3,−t2 to the first entry. Every row sums to zero, as the Fox calculus identity predicts.
The cellular boundary in degree one for the infinite cyclic cover is (t−1,t−1,t−1). Its kernel is freely generated by ea​−ec​,eb​−ec​. Expressing the two relation boundaries in this basis gives the two-by-two matrix obtained by deleting column c of F(t). Its determinant is the order of the Alexander module of a space. Equivalently, all three maximal matrix minors of F(t) agree up to sign. The Alexander polynomial is
ΔY​(t)≐(t2−t+1)(t4−t2+1),​
(8)
where ≐ allows multiplication by a unit ±tm in the Laurent polynomial ring. Its value at 1 is 1.

Number the seven crossings from top to bottom. Orient the strand entering the top-left arch downward. With the usual positive braid crossing convention, the crossing signs are
(+,+,+,−,−,+,+).
(9)
To make the diagram calculation reproducible, its oriented Gauss code is
1o​2u​3o​6u​7o​1u​2o​4u​5o​7u​6o​5u​4o​3u​.
(10)
Subscripts record overpassing and underpassing. In this convention a positive trefoil knot has knot signature −2.
Label the seven Wirtinger generators x0​,…,x6​ successively between undercrossings, beginning just before the encounter 2u​. The Alexander matrix, with rows ordered by crossing number, is
M(t)=​1−tt−10000​0−11−t00t0​t0000−11−t​−11−t01000​000−tt−10t​000011−t−1​00tt−1−t00​​.
(11)
At a positive crossing the Fox derivative entries at the overpassing, incoming and outgoing arcs are 1−t,t,−1; at a negative crossing a unit multiple of the row has entries t−1,1,−t. Deleting the last row and column gives determinant t(t4−5t3+7t2−5t+1). Hence a symmetric normalization of the Alexander polynomial of a knot is
ΔK​(t)=t2−5t+7−5t−1+t−2.​
(12)
In particular, ΔK​(1)=−1 in this displayed normalization; multiplying by −1 gives normalization 1 and does not change any conclusion.
The Seifert algorithm produces four Seifert circles and seven bands. Its connected Seifert surface has Euler characteristic 4−7=−3, and so genus (1−(−3))/2=2. Conversely, the Alexander breadth bound on Seifert genus gives 2gs​(K)≥4. Therefore
gs​(K)=2.​
(13)
For the knot signature, use the alternating diagram signature formula: for a reduced alternating knot diagram,
σ(K)=sA​(D)−c+​(D)−1,
(14)
where sA​ counts circles in the all-A bracket smoothing and c+​ counts positive crossings. Here the all-A smoothing has four circles, the all-B smoothing has five, and c+​=5. Thus
σ(K)=−2.​
(15)
The opposite global knot signature convention gives +2 instead.
For the slice genus, the Levine-Tristram signature bound on the slice genus at −1 gives g4​(K)≥1. There is also an explicit unknotting crossing: switch crossing 3. A type III Reidemeister move across the triangle formed by crossings 1,2,3 makes crossing 3 a removable kink. Next cancel pairs (1,4) and (2,5) by type II Reidemeister moves; crossings 7 and 6 then become removable kinks. This leaves the unknot. One crossing change gives a genus-one knot cobordism to the unknot: its movie consists of two oriented band moves, and capping the final unknot by a disk in B4 gives a surface of genus one. Consequently
g4​(K)=1.​
(16)

All homology and cohomology in this solution use F2​ coefficients, so no orientation hypothesis is necessary. The Poincare-Lefschetz duality degrees are complementary:
Hk​(W;F2​)≅Hn−k(W,∂W;F2​).​
(17)
The starred notation in the question must be understood with this degree reversal.
Turn the handle decomposition upside down. An absolute k-handle becomes a relative (n−k)-handle based on ∂W. The absolute cellular boundary coefficient counts intersections of an attaching sphere with a belt sphere. In the reversed handle decomposition, the same intersections are counted in the opposite order. Over F2​ the two incidence matrices are transposes, with no sign ambiguity. Thus the absolute chain complex is the complementary-degree dual of the relative chain complex. Taking homology and using the universal coefficient theorem for cohomology over a field proves the displayed isomorphism. This is mod-two handle duality.
To obtain a perfect pairing from the assumed closed case, form the double of a manifold D(W) from two copies of W. The fold map D(W)→W is a retraction, so the inclusion of either copy induces an injection on homology. If a=0 in Hk​(W), its image in Hk​(D(W)) is nonzero. Closed-manifold mod-two Poincare duality supplies a class z∈Hn−k​(D(W)) with intersection 1 against that image. Cut a representative of z along the boundary and retain its part in the first copy; it defines b∈Hn−k​(W,∂W). Equivalently, apply the quotient map D(W)→D(W)/W−​≅W/∂W and excision. Intersections with a representative of a in the interior are unchanged, so a⋅b=1. This proves nondegeneracy in the first variable. Mod-two handle duality gives equal finite dimensions for the two spaces, hence nondegeneracy in the second variable as well.
For the three-dimensional conclusion, set
V=H1​(∂W),L=ker(H1​(∂W)→H1​(W)).
(18)
The long exact sequence in relative homology says L=im∂, where ∂:H2​(W,∂W)→V. The closed-surface intersection pairing on V is nondegenerate. A collar calculation gives the adjoint identity
(∂b)⋅x=b⋅i∗​x.
(19)
Indeed, push x slightly into the collar: intersections with the relative surface representing b correspond to its boundary intersections with x. By the absolute-relative perfect pairing already proved, x is orthogonal to every ∂b precisely when i∗​x=0. Therefore L⊥=L.
For any finite-dimensional space with a perfect pairing, dimL+dimL⊥=dimV. Hence the half-lives-half-dies theorem gives
dimkeri∗​=21​dimH1​(∂W;F2​).​
(20)
But H1​(RP2;F2​)≅F2​ has dimension one. It cannot be the entire boundary of a compact three-manifold, because the displayed dimension would be 1/2. This excludes nonorientable three-manifolds as well as orientable ones.

A closed connected three-manifold has a handle decomposition with one zero-handle and one three-handle. To arrange this, cancel the zero-handles along a spanning tree of connecting one-handles; perform the corresponding construction in the dual handle decomposition to consolidate the three-handles. This does not require the three-manifold to be orientable.
If there are r one-handles and s two-handles, the Euler characteristic is
χ(Y)=1−r+s−1=s−r.
(21)
Mod-two Poincare duality pairs the Betti numbers of a closed odd-dimensional manifold, giving χ(Y)=0. Thus r=s. The one-handles give r generators of a group of the fundamental group, the attaching circles of the two-handles give r relators, and the three-handle does not change the fundamental group. Therefore π1​(Y) admits a balanced presentation.
Now let
G=⟨x1​,…,xr​∣R1​,…,Rs​⟩
(22)
be any finite group presentation. Start with a four-dimensional zero-handle and r one-handles. Its fundamental group is the free group on the xi​. Represent the finitely many relators by disjoint embedded circles in its three-dimensional boundary; a small perturbation makes the circles disjoint. Choose framings of an embedded sphere and attach s two-handles. The resulting compact connected oriented four-manifold W has π1​(W)≅G by the Seifert-van Kampen theorem.
The map π1​(∂W)→π1​(W) is surjective. One way to see this is to turn the handle decomposition upside down: the relative handles based on ∂W have indices 2,3,4, and so add no fundamental group generators. Also ∂W is connected, since surgery on embedded circles in a connected three-manifold preserves connectedness.
Take the double of a manifold
M=W∪∂W​(−W).
(23)
It is a closed connected smooth oriented four-manifold. The Seifert-van Kampen theorem gives
π1​(M)≅G∗π1​(∂W)​G.
(24)
Both maps from π1​(∂W) are the same surjection, under the natural identification of the two copies. The pushout identifies the two copies of every element of G and imposes no new relations: the fold homomorphism is inverse to either inclusion. Thus
π1​(M)≅G.​
(25)
This is four-manifold realization of finitely presented groups.

Replace the open knot complement by the compact knot exterior EK​; they have the same homotopy type. The abelianization
π1​(EK​)⟶H1​(EK​;Z)≅Z
(26)
sends an oriented meridian of a knot to 1. The connected covering space corresponding to its kernel is the infinite cyclic cover of a knot exterior EK​. Choose a generator t of its deck transformation group. Then
AK​=H1​(EK​;Z)
(27)
is the Alexander module of a knot over Λ=Z[t,t−1]. It is a finitely presented torsion module, and its order, equivalently the greatest common divisor of the appropriate maximal matrix minors of an Alexander matrix, is the Alexander polynomial of a knot, up to a unit ±tm.
For a fibered knot, the infinite cyclic cover of a knot exterior is Σ×R. Write the mapping torus with the gluing convention in the question. The deck transformation
D(x,s)=(ϕ(x),s−1)
(28)
has exactly that quotient: (x,1) and (ϕ(x),0) are identified. Choose t=D. Its action on H1​(Σ;Z) is ϕ∗​. If one instead chooses the generator projecting in the positive circle direction, its action is ϕ∗−1​; this replaces t by t−1 and produces an associate for a knot.
Choose an integral basis for H1​(Σ)≅Z2g and let Φ be the matrix of ϕ∗​. The Alexander module of a knot has the finite free module presentation
Λ2gtI−Φ​Λ2g⟶AK​⟶0.
(29)
There are no further relations: using tv=Φv and the invertibility of Φ, every Laurent combination reduces uniquely to an integral combination of the chosen basis elements. Taking the order of this square presentation gives the monodromy formula
ΔK​(t)≐det(tI−ϕ∗​).​
(30)
For a disk fiber both sides are 1, using the empty determinant convention.

Choose an oriented Seifert surface F of genus g and an integral basis a1​,…,a2g​ of H1​(F;Z). Push ai​ in the positive normal direction to F. The Seifert matrix is
Vij​=lk(ai+​,aj​).​
(31)
It represents the Seifert form. Its skew-symmetrization V−VT is the integral intersection form of F. The relation to the Alexander polynomial of a knot is
ΔK​(t)≐det(tV−VT).​
(32)
This follows from the presentation of the Alexander module of a knot obtained by cutting the knot exterior along F and stacking the resulting copies; a proof is not needed here.
The printed genus inequality is false. The useful consequence of the determinant relation is the Alexander breadth bound on Seifert genus:
brΔK​≤2gs​(K).​
(33)
Here the breadth of a Laurent polynomial is its largest exponent minus its smallest exponent, so it is unchanged by multiplying by ±tm. Indeed, for a minimal-genus Seifert surface, the matrix has size 2gs​(K) and each entry of tV−VT has degree at most one. Its nonzero determinant is an ordinary polynomial of degree at most 2gs​(K); its breadth is no larger than that degree. Equivalently, the highest exponent of a symmetrically normalized Alexander polynomial of a knot is at most gs​(K). An unnormalized degree is not invariant under Laurent units.
For a counterexample to the printed inequality and the requested example, take the untwisted Whitehead double of a trefoil knot. The picture specifies the two-strand tangle in the zero Seifert framing; the two exterior caps form a Whitehead double clasp. The inset identifies the trefoil knot used as companion.
Its usual genus-one Seifert surface is a zero-framed annulus following the trefoil knot, joined by the clasp band. Choose the annulus core and a curve traversing the clasp band as the basis of its first homology. Zero annulus twisting gives the first self-linking number 0. The clasp contributes a Hopf band with self-linking number −1 in the positive-clasp convention used here. Plumbing the bands contributes one linking in one push-off direction and none in the other. Orienting the basis suitably gives
V=(00​1−1​),tV−VT=(0−1​t1−t​).
(34)
Tying the annulus into the trefoil knot changes neither these local linking counts nor its prescribed zero Seifert framing. Therefore
ΔK​(t)≐t≐1.​
(35)
This Whitehead double is not the unknot, as allowed without proof in the question. Its Seifert genus is consequently at least one and at most one, hence exactly one. Thus gs​(K)=1 while every normalized constant Alexander polynomial of a knot has degree zero: a direct counterexample to the printed inequality.

Orient S3 as the boundary of B4. Push the interior of an oriented Seifert surface F slightly into B4, and let XF​ be the double branched covering of B4 over this pushed-in surface. Its boundary is the two-fold branched cover of a knot. Define the knot signature by
σ(K)=signQXF​​.​
(36)
The signature of a possibly degenerate intersection form means the number of positive eigenvalues minus the number of negative eigenvalues; its radical contributes zero.
Here is why the definition is independent of F. Given two choices F0​,F1​, glue their branched covers along the common boundary to obtain
X=XF0​​∪Σ2​(K)​(−XF1​​).
(37)
This is the double branched covering of S4 over the closed oriented surface obtained by gluing the pushed-in F0​ to −F1​. Every closed oriented surface in S4 admits a Seifert hypersurface: its complement has an integral meridional class, represent it by a map to the circle, and take a regular value. Near the surface choose the angular map in its trivial normal disk bundle. The closure of a suitable regular fiber is a compact oriented three-manifold bounding the surface. Its trivial normal framing can be chosen to match this construction.
Push the interior of this Seifert hypersurface into B5. The double branched covering of B5 over the resulting properly embedded three-manifold has boundary X. The existence of the cover follows from the meridian homomorphism to Z/2; smoothness near the branch set is the local map (z,u)↦(z2,u). By the hypothesis about boundaries of five-manifolds, signX=0. Novikov additivity for gluing along an entire closed three-dimensional boundary gives
0=signX=signXF0​​−signXF1​​.
(38)
Thus the knot signature is independent of the Seifert surface. An isotopy of the knot carries the construction to an equivalent one, so it is also a knot invariant.
To calculate its intersection form, express F as a disk with 2g bands. The double branched covering of B4 over the pushed-in disk is again B4. Each band lifts to a two-handle. The capped lifted cores give a basis of H2​(XF​;Z) corresponding to the band-core basis of H1​(F;Z). The framing of the ith lifted attaching circle is 2lk(ai+​,ai​)=2Vii​. For distinct bands, the two sheets contribute the two push-off linkings Vij​ and Vji​, so the mutual linking number is Vij​+Vji​. Hence
QXF​​=V+VT,σ(K)=sign(V+VT).​
(39)
A different integral basis changes this matrix by matrix congruence and leaves its signature unchanged.
In Figure 7 the upper knot is a connected sum of knots T#T, while the lower one is T#T, where T is the positive trefoil knot. Both knot groups have the same presentation
⟨a,b,c∣aba=bab, aca=cac⟩.​
(40)
To see this, the Seifert-van Kampen theorem expresses the knot group of a connected sum of knots as the amalgamated free product of the two summand knot groups, amalgamating their meridians of a knot. A trefoil knot has presentation ⟨a,b∣aba=bab⟩ with a a meridian. Reflection gives the same abstract group for the mirror. If it reverses the chosen meridian, send both generators to their inverses; the inverse braid relation is again the same relation. Thus the meridian amalgamation yields the displayed group presentation in both cases.
A band basis for the positive trefoil knot gives
VT​=(−10​1−1​),
(41)
whose symmetrization is negative definite. Therefore σ(T)=−2 and σ(T)=2. Boundary-connected-summing Seifert surfaces makes the Seifert matrix block diagonal, so the knot signature is additive under connected sum of knots. Consequently
σ(K1​)=−4,σ(K2​)=0.​
(42)
The upper knot and lower knot are therefore not isotopic, even though their knot groups are isomorphic. With the opposite global knot signature convention the first value is +4, and the distinction is unchanged.

There are two common normalizations. I will define both explicitly, because the numerical conclusion in the question uses the unreduced one.
Set δ=−A2−A−2. The reduced Kauffman bracket of a nonempty link diagram is
⟨D⟩red​=∑s​Aa(s)−b(s)δ∣s∣−1,
(43)
where s runs over all bracket smoothings, a(s),b(s) count A- and B-smoothings, and ∣s∣ is the number of resulting circles. Locally,
⟨crossing⟩=A⟨A-smoothing⟩+A−1⟨B-smoothing⟩.
(44)
Fix the usual Kauffman bracket convention in which the A-smoothing at a positive braid crossing is its oriented smoothing; reflecting a crossing exchanges A and A−1. The unknot has reduced bracket 1, and adjoining another circle multiplies the bracket by δ. The unreduced Kauffman bracket uses δ∣s∣ instead and assigns the empty diagram 1. Thus for a nonempty diagram
⟨D⟩un​=δ⟨D⟩red​.
(45)
For completeness, the bracket calculations for all three Reidemeister moves can be carried out in the Temperley-Lieb diagram algebra. Let I be the identity two-strand tangle, and let e be the cap-cup tangle. Composition gives e2=δe. The two crossings correspond to
R=AI+A−1e,R−1=A−1I+Ae.
(46)
Consequently
RR−1=I+(A2+A−2+δ)e=I,
(47)
which proves invariance under the second Reidemeister move. On three strands, the cap-cup diagrams satisfy e1​e2​e1​=e1​, e2​e1​e2​=e2​, and ei2​=δei​. Expand the two sides of the third move. Their difference is
R1​R2​R1​−R2​R1​R2​=(A+A−1δ+A−3)(e1​−e2​)=0.
(48)
Thus the third Reidemeister move also preserves either bracket normalization. Reflected forms of these moves follow by replacing A with A−1.
A positive curl contributes Aδ+A−1=−A3, and a negative curl contributes A−1δ+A=−A−3. The writhe of a link diagram w(D) changes by +1 or −1 in exactly these cases. Therefore the corrected bracket
fD​(A)=(−A3)−w(D)⟨D⟩
(49)
is invariant under the first Reidemeister move as well; the second and third moves leave the writhe of a link diagram unchanged. Substituting t=A−4 defines a Jones polynomial. For multiple components half-integer powers of t may occur. The reduced version VLred​ has VUred​=1, and the Unreduced Jones polynomial is
VLun​(t)=−(t1/2+t−1/2)VLred​(t).
(50)
At A=1, switching a crossing does not change the bracket: both smoothing coefficients are 1. It changes the writhe of a link diagram by 2, so the correction factor (−1)−w is unchanged. Hence either Jones polynomial at t=1 is unchanged by a crossing change. By switching crossings, any l-component link can be made an unlink. Its crossing-free diagram has l circles and zero writhe of a link diagram, giving
VLred​(1)=(−2)l−1,VLun​(1)=(−2)l.​
(51)
Thus the printed conclusion ∣VL​(1)∣=2l holds for the unreduced normalization. For the reduced Jones polynomial, the correct conclusion is ∣VLred​(1)∣=2l−1​; the unknot alone already rules out 2l in that convention.
