Fix a smaller angular strip and a momentum ball B′⋐B; the canonical transformation will be defined there and map into the original domain for small ϵ. This domain restriction is natural for a near-identity change of coordinates with a nonzero momentum shift.
First solve the scalar cohomological equation on a Diophantine torus
Dω​u=⟨V⟩−V,⟨u⟩=0.
(43)
The preceding result gives a real-analytic periodic u on every smaller strip. We need a near-identity angular diffeomorphism χ and a constant vector a such that
Dχ(θ)ω=ω+ϵ∇u(χ(θ))+a,χ=id+O(ϵ),a=O(ϵ2).
(44)
Here is a complete analytic construction of this translated conjugacy of a Diophantine vector field.
Write w=ϵ∇u and, for a current approximation, put
A=Dχ,e=Dω​χ−ω−w∘χ−a.
(45)
Assume A is close to the identity. For a correction Δχ=Av, the linearized defect is
Dω​(Av)−Dw(χ)Av−Δa=ADω​v+(De)v−Δa.

The identity follows by differentiating the definition of e, so the term (De)v already contains the current error. Choose

The matrix average is invertible near the identity, and the right-hand side has zero mean. The preceding torus small-divisor estimate therefore solves this vector equation componentwise. Update χ+​=χ+Av, a+​=a+Δa. The exact new error is

For convergence, reserve a fixed outer strip on which w is analytic and work on shrinking inner strips whose total loss is less than half the starting width. Let s=n+τ and choose loss parameters δj​=d2−j with 0<d<1 small enough that the fixed multiples of these losses used at each stage fit in the reserved total width. Taking a slightly larger exponent μ covers all these losses. Start with χ0​=id and a0​=0, so e0​=−w=O(ϵ). If a small constant K satisfies Cd−μK22μ≤1 and ∥e0​∥≤K, induction gives ∥ej​∥≤K2−2μj. The estimates on Δχj​ and Δaj​ are summable. Choosing ϵ still smaller keeps the derivatives close to the identity and the images inside the reserved strip, closing the induction. The real periodic analytic limits χ,a solve the conjugacy equation. Since ⟨w⟩=0, the very first constant correction is zero; the first new defect is O(ϵ2), and the remaining summed constant corrections are O(ϵ2). Also the summed change of χ is O(ϵ). This proves the claimed estimates as well as existence, without leaving an unsolved linear remainder.
Now set η(x)=ϵ∇u(x)+a and define the symplectic cotangent lift with a closed momentum shift
x=χ(x′),y=η(χ(x′))+Dχ(x′)−Ty′.​
(51)
The cotangent lift of a diffeomorphism is symplectic. Translation by η is also symplectic because the one-form η⋅dx=ϵdu+a⋅dx is closed. The constant term need not be exact on the flat torus; closedness is sufficient. Thus their composition is the required near-identity canonical transformation.
Expanding the quadratic kinetic term gives a transformed linear coefficient Dχ−1(ω+η∘χ), which is exactly ω by the conjugacy equation. The constant-in-y′ part is
ω⋅η+ϵV+21​∣η∣2=ϵ⟨V⟩+ω⋅a+21​∣η∣2,
