Retain the factor ϵ in the last temporal phase, as printed in the original PDF. Let a=U/(2γ) and put η=eaxb(ξ)est with s=−iω0​−iϵω1​. The drift-removing substitution for a Dirichlet Sturm-Liouville problem cancels the first derivative:
sb=γbxx​+(μ0​−4γU2​−νϵ2x2)b.
(38)
With ξ=ϵ​fx, this becomes
bξξ​+[γϵf2μ0​−U2/(4γ)+iω0​+iϵω1​​−γf4ν​ξ2]b=0.
(39)
Choose the positive scale f=(ν/γ)1/4. To leave an order-one spectral parameter, cancel the numerator's order-one term. Then
ω0​=i(μ0​−4γU2​),f=(ν/γ)1/4,λ=γν​iω1​​.​
(40)
The assumed decaying solutions of the Hermite differential equation require λ=2j+1, j=0,1,…. They are proportional to e−ξ2/2Hj​(ξ), with physicists' Hermite polynomials. Therefore the global modes of a quadratically confined Ginzburg-Landau equation have
ω1​=−i(2j+1)γν​,sj​=μ0​−4γU2​−ϵ(2j+1)γν​.​
(41)
For every fixed positive ϵ, multiplying the Hermite-Gaussian by eUx/(2γ) still gives decay at both infinities. The strongest mode is j=0, so the whole-line system is globally unstable precisely when
μ0​>4γU2​+ϵγν​.​
(42)
Equality is a neutral ground mode. This spectrum is exact for the stated quadratic profile, even though the notation organizes it as an expansion in ϵ.
The homogeneous absolute-instability threshold in part (a) is μ=U2/(4γ). The global threshold approaches that value as the profile varies increasingly slowly, but at finite ϵ the quadratic confinement adds the loss ϵγν​. A locally absolutely unstable patch at the profile maximum is necessary but must be strong and broad enough to overcome that loss. Merely having positive local temporal growth μ0​>0, which can be only convective wave-packet instability, is insufficient for a growing decaying global normal mode. This connects the local impulse classification to the discrete global spectrum.
