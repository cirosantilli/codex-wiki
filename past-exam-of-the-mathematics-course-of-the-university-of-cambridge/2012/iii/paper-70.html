First prove necessity. If polynomials with integer coefficients converge to f in the supremum norm, their values at 0 and 1 are convergent sequences of integers. Each such sequence is eventually constant, since a convergent sequence is Cauchy and two distinct integers are at distance at least one. Its limit is an integer. Thus f(0),f(1)∈Z.
For sufficiency we also need uniform convergence of the Bernstein polynomial to f. Here is a direct argument. For X∼Bin(n,x), write Bn​(f,x)=Ef(X/n). The binomial distribution has E(X/n)=x and Var(X/n)=x(1−x)/n≤1/(4n). For every δ>0, uniform continuity of f and the Chebyshev inequality give
∣Bn​(f,x)−f(x)∣≤ω(f,δ)+2∥f∥∞​P(∣X/n−x∣>δ)≤ω(f,δ)+2nδ2∥f∥∞​​.
(12)
First make δ small and then n large; the bound is uniform in x. Hence ∥Bn​(f)−f∥∞​→0.
If the endpoints are integers, part (b) gives ∥Bn∗​(f)−Bn​(f)∥∞​→0. Each coefficient multiplying xk(1−x)n−k in Bn∗​ is an integer. Expanding (1−x)n−k by the binomial theorem therefore gives integer coefficients in the ordinary monomial basis. The triangle inequality proves ∥Bn∗​(f)−f∥∞​→0. Thus the integer endpoint condition is necessary and sufficient for integer-coefficient polynomial approximation.

Use real-valued continuous functions and real trigonometric polynomials. The trigonometric Chebyshev alternation theorem says that p∈Tn​ is the unique best uniform approximation to f if and only if there are distinct points, ordered within one period,
x0​<x1​<⋯<x2n+1​<x0​+2π,f(xj​)−p(xj​)=ε(−1)j∥f−p∥∞​,ε∈{−1,1}.
(13)
Thus the required number of alternating extrema is 2n+2. When the error is zero, the assertion is interpreted trivially and p=f is unique. The count reflects dimTn​=2n+1 and the fact that a nonzero degree-n trigonometric polynomial has at most 2n zeros in one period. In particular a strictly better candidate would force its difference from p to have alternating signs at these points and hence at least 2n+2 zeros, counting the closing interval around the circle. The trigonometric Chebyshev alternation theorem also gives necessity and uniqueness.

Use the usual strictly increasing spline knot sequence, and initially assume k≥2. The explicit formula for the order-k divided difference gives
Mi​(t)=k∑j=ii+k​∏ℓ=iℓ=j​i+k​(tj​−tℓ​)(tj​−t)+k−1​​.
(18)
Each summand is a truncated power function in t, with its junction at tj​. It is a piecewise polynomial function of degree at most k−1 and has k−2 continuous derivatives. Finite summation preserves these properties.
For t≥ti+k​ all summands vanish. For t≤ti​, all spline knot data come from the polynomial u↦(u−t)k−1. An order-k divided difference annihilates a polynomial of smaller degree, so Mi​(t)=0 there too. Thus the support is contained in [ti​,ti+k​].
On the last open spline knot interval only the j=i+k term survives; it is a nonzero multiple of (ti+k​−t)k−1. On the first open spline knot interval, compare the truncated spline knot data with the untruncated polynomial data: only the j=i value differs. Since the untruncated divided difference is zero, the result is a nonzero multiple of (ti​−t)k−1. Consequently both outer pieces are nonzero, giving exact closed support. The Cox-de Boor recurrence used below also proves positivity throughout the open support. Therefore
Mi​∈Ck−2(R),suppMi​=[ti​,ti+k​],deg each piece≤k−1.​
(19)
This is simple-knot B-spline regularity. For k=1 the B-splines are interval indicators; they are piecewise constant and need not be continuous. The notation Ck−2 should then be read as no continuity requirement, not as a classical negative-order differentiability space.

The divided difference is a finite linear combination of spline knot values, so it commutes with the finite integral. For every spline knot tj​ in the defining interval,
∫ti​ti+k​​(tj​−t)+k−1​dt=k(tj​−ti​)k​.

(21)
because the order-k divided difference of a monic degree-k polynomial is its leading coefficient. This proves the unit-integral normalization of a B-spline.
For the partition of unity, let Ni,r​ denote the partition-normalized order-r B-spline. The Cox-de Boor recurrence is
Ni,r​(t)=ti+r−1​−ti​t−ti​​Ni,r−1​(t)+ti+r​−ti+1​ti+r​−t​Ni+1,r−1​(t).
(22)
It follows by substituting the explicit divided difference formula into both sides and collecting each truncated power function. The starting case is Ni,1​=1[ti​,ti+1​)​, with endpoint conventions irrelevant when r≥2. The two weights are nonnegative wherever their respective lower-order B-splines are nonzero. Induction therefore gives Ni,r​≥0 and strict positivity in its open support.
Extend the finite spline knot sequence strictly in both directions, with no finite accumulation. At each point only finitely many B-splines are nonzero. For the full extended family the sum is one for order one. Summing the Cox-de Boor recurrence, the combined coefficient of Nj,r−1​ is
tj+r−1​−tj​t−tj​​+tj+r−1​−tj​tj+r−1​−t​=1.
(23)
Induction gives a full partition of unity at every order. On tk​<t<tn+1​, all extended order-k functions with index i≤0 or i≥n+1 vanish by their supports. Hence

(24)
The same argument gives the useful global subpartition of unity for B-splines, 0≤∑i=1n​Ni​(t)≤1, including outside the basic spline knot interval.

The orthogonal projection has range S, not all of C[0,1]: the latter is an infinite-dimensional vector space whereas S is a finite-dimensional vector space. For a concrete counterexample, ex cannot belong to any finite-degree piecewise polynomial space, since all its derivatives are nonzero on every open interval. For k≥2, the B-splines are continuous, so the orthogonal projection is a map C[0,1]→S⊂C[0,1]. For order one it instead has a generally discontinuous piecewise constant range. For example, a single interval indicator on [1/4,3/4) is the projection of the constant function one onto its span, and is not continuous.
Write s∗=∑j​aj​Nj​. Since Mi​ is a positive scalar multiple of Ni​, the operator normal equations ⟨f−s∗,Ni​⟩=0 are equivalent to
Ga=b,bi​=⟨Mi​,f⟩.
(25)
The mixed-normalization spline Gram matrix is invertible: G=DH where Dii​=k/(ti+k​−ti​)>0 and Hij​=⟨Ni​,Nj​⟩ is the ordinary positive-definite matrix of inner products of a linearly independent basis. The positivity and unit-integral normalization of a B-spline give ∣bi​∣≤∥f∥∞​. The subpartition of unity for B-splines gives

An orthonormal multiresolution analysis consists of closed subspaces of a Hilbert space (Vj​)j∈Z​ of the Hilbert space L2(R) such that
Vj​⊂Vj+1​,⋃j​Vj​​=L2(R),⋂j​Vj​={0},g∈Vj​ ⟺ g(2⋅)∈Vj+1​.
(33)
There is a scaling function ϕ whose integer function translations form an orthonormal basis of V0​. Consequently ϕj,k​(x)=2j/2ϕ(2jx−k) form an orthonormal basis of Vj​; in particular V0​ is invariant under integer function translations.
Let Wj​=Vj+1​⊖Vj​ be the orthogonal complement of Vj​ in Vj+1​. Nesting, density and the trivial intersection imply the orthogonal direct sum
L2(R)=⨁j∈Z​Wj​.
(34)
For example, telescoping VJ+1​=VL​⊕⨁j=LJ​Wj​ and letting L→−∞, J→∞ gives this decomposition; the corresponding orthogonal projections converge strongly to zero and the identity by the intersection and density properties.
To construct the orthonormal wavelet, use ϕ∈V1​ to write the scaling refinement equation ϕ=∑n​an​ϕ(2⋅−n), with a∈ℓ2 and ∑n​∣an​∣2=2. Put m(t)=21​∑n​an​e−int. The orthonormal translates and Fourier periodization criterion proved in part (b), applied to the scaling refinement equation, yields
1=∑k​∣ϕ​(2t+2πk)∣2=∣m(t)∣2∑ℓ​∣ϕ​(t+2πℓ)∣2+∣m(t+π)∣2∑ℓ​∣ϕ​(t+π+2πℓ)∣2=∣m(t)∣2+∣m(t+π)∣2.
(35)
This is the quadrature mirror filter identity. Define
q(t)=−e−itm(t+π)​,bn​=(−1)na1−n​​,ψ(x)=∑n​bn​ϕ(2x−n).
(36)
The Fourier series of q is 21​∑n​bn​e−int, and ψ​(2t)=q(t)ϕ​(t). The two rows (m(t),m(t+π)) and (q(t),q(t+π)) form a unitary 2×2 matrix almost everywhere: both have squared norm one and their inner product is zero. This wavelet completion of a multiresolution filter proves not just orthogonality but completeness. Splitting a finer-scale coefficient Fourier series into its values at t and t+π, this invertible two-channel matrix resolves it into a coarse-scale channel and its complementary channel. Thus the integer function translations of ψ form an orthonormal basis of W0​.
The dilated functions ψj,k​(x)=2j/2ψ(2jx−k) therefore form an orthonormal basis of each Wj​. The orthogonal direct sum proves the conclusion: every orthonormal multiresolution analysis produces an orthonormal wavelet basis {ψj,k​:j,k∈Z} of L2(R).
