We give a quantitative construction for the whole open set, including the case of infinitely many component intervals. Pointwise divergence at a single point is insufficient here. Write z=e2πit and use normalized measure on T.
First let K be a finite union of closed arcs with ∣K∣≤3ε, where ε is small. Choose a smooth function nonnegative function u, equal to one on K, with u≤1 and η:=∫u≤6ε. G(z)=2ηη+∫T​e2πis−ze2πis+z​u(s)ds​.

The complex logarithm has no branch problem because G lies in the right half-plane. Its smooth function boundary values give absolutely summable Taylor coefficients. Use the density form of the Szemerédi theorem: for fixed α>0 and length r, every sufficiently large subset of {1,…,N} of size at least αN contains a nonconstant arithmetic progression of length r, as established in Szemerédi's density theorem. This theorem supplies a progression, not the desired averaging configuration; we now select the actual indices.
Take r=2k+1. Write the resulting progression as
a,a+d,…,a+2kd∈A,d≥1,
(21)
and put y=a+kd∈A. If k=2m is even, select y−d,y+d,…,y−md,y+md. These are 2m=k distinct members of the progression, and each opposite pair sums to 2y. If k=2m+1 is odd, select those 2m opposite points together with y itself. All k selected points are distinct, and their sum is again ky. The case k=1 simply selects y.
In both cases the indices stay between 0 and 2k and the difference d is nonzero. Thus the precise answer is
x1​,…,xk​∈A distinct,kx1​+⋯+xk​​=y∈A.​
(22)
No deletion of diagonal solutions and no multidimensional density theorem is needed. The averaging point is allowed to be one of the selected points, as it is in the odd case.

We first construct a genuinely compact Besicovitch set; a sequence of unrelated small-area sets would not suffice. The compact Kakeya construction from small projections below gives the limiting compact set together with its segments.
Let X consist of nonempty compact sets K⊂[0,1]×R whose horizontal projection is exactly [0,1]. With the Hausdorff metric this is a complete metric space. Indeed a Hausdorff-Cauchy sequence is eventually contained in one bounded closed rectangle and converges there to a nonempty compact set; horizontal projection is Lipschitz for Hausdorff distance, so its value [0,1] is preserved in the limit.
For t∈[0,1] put Lt​(x,y)=y+tx and mt​(K)=∣Lt​(K)∣. The function (K,t)↦mt​(K) is upper semicontinuous. To verify this, cover the compact set Lt​(K) by finitely many open intervals with total length less than mt​(K)+ε. Joint continuity of the compact images in the Hausdorff metric puts every sufficiently nearby image inside the same cover. This proves the required upper bound, uniformly over a compact parameter interval when a strict bound holds throughout it.
Fix q∈[0,1] and ε>0. The set
U(q,ε)={K∈X:supt∈[0,1],∣t−q∣≤ε​mt​(K)<2ε}
(33)
is open. It is also dense, by the following explicit finite approximation. Given K and tolerance h>0, partition [0,1] into N equal subintervals and choose (j/N,yj​)∈K at each left endpoint. Above that subinterval put the segment

The disk has a boundary normal in every direction; a fixed polygon does not.
The geometric input for this overview is the standard finite Perron tree packing used in Fefferman's original proof: for arbitrarily small η>0 one obtains long thin rectangles Rj​ with A=∑j​∣Rj​∣ and ∣⋃j​Rj​∣≤ηA, while fixed longitudinal translates Rj′​ are pairwise disjoint. Use a Boolean circuit of unbounded-fan-in AND and OR gates, with negations pushed to the input Boolean literals. Its circuit depth is the largest number of AND/OR gates on an input-to-output path, and its circuit size M counts these gates. Constants and Boolean literals may be supplied as inputs. Equivalent standard size conventions only enlarge the absolute constants. One can merge adjacent gates of the same type; the depth-reduction argument below keeps the two possible normal forms available at each gate.
A random restriction with parameter p independently leaves each variable live with probability p and otherwise fixes it to zero or one with probability (1−p)/2 each. The Håstad switching lemma states that for a width-w DNF or conjunctive normal form F,
Pρ∼Rp​​(DTdepth(Fρ​)>s)≤(5pw)s.
(43)
Width bounds Boolean literals in a term or clause, not the number of terms. A decision tree of depth at most s has both a DNF and a conjunctive normal form of width at most s and has real multilinear degree at most s. Indeed every leaf condition is a product of at most s factors xi​ or 1−xi​; sum the accepting leaves. The switching lemma controls the canonical decision tree, hence implies this representation bound.
We detail the Boolean circuit simplification parameters. First use a 1/10-random restriction. For a bottom AND gate, let X count its live Boolean literals on the event that no fixed Boolean literal kills it. For w distinct Boolean literals,
E[5X1{not killed}​]=(9/20+5/10)w=(19/20)w≤1.
(44)
Thus the probability of retaining more than s live Boolean literals without becoming constant is at most 5−s. The same calculation applies to bottom OR gates, with the opposite killing value. Repeated Boolean literals are removed and contradictory Boolean literals make a constant gate. A union bound makes all nonconstant bottom gates width at most s, except with probability at most M0​5−s.
Now apply d−1 successive restrictions, each retaining probability 1/(10s) of the remaining variables. At a two-layer gate, the current bottom forms are width-s DNFs or conjunctive normal forms. The Håstad switching lemma bounds its probability of failing to reduce to decision-tree depth at most s by 2−s. Convert the successful decision tree into the opposite normal form, whose terms or clauses have width at most s, and merge its outer gate with the adjacent gate of the same type. This removes one layer. Repeat upwards until the output has a depth-s decision tree.
The bookkeeping matters: although a decision tree may give 2s clauses, each subsequent switching event is charged to the corresponding original Boolean circuit gate, not to each newly created clause. The lemma permits any number of terms. Each original gate is charged once, so the sum of the failure probabilities is at most M2−s, without an extra exponential size factor. Conditioning on successful preceding stages is harmless because each next restriction is fresh and the width bound holds for every preceding success. For a general Boolean circuit the conjunctive normal form or DNF representation at a shared gate can be chosen separately for each outgoing use; no extra gates need to be counted as switching targets.

(51)
Thus the exact expectation identity bounds at least half the original tail above t, and
∑∣S∣>t​∣f​(S)∣2≤2M2−s≤4M2−t1/d/40.
(52)
When u<40 the same bound follows from the trivial total-weight bound ∑S​∣f​(S)∣2≤1. We obtain explicit absolute constants,
∣r∣>t∑​∣f​(r)∣2≤4M2−t1/d/40.​
(53)
This supplies the requested overview of the Linial-Mansour-Nisan theorem with the actual switching scale, gate-count bookkeeping and restriction-to-Fourier calculation. The argument uses the unbounded-fan-in AND/OR Boolean circuit basis; allowing arbitrary gates would make the theorem false, since one parity gate could have all its nonconstant Fourier weight at level n. The original theorem is Linial, Mansour and Nisan's circuit Fourier bound.
