The particle horizon is the greatest distance from which a light signal could have reached the observer since the initial cosmic time ti​. Its comoving radius and its radial proper distance at time t are

(1)
Here proper distance is measured along the spatial slice, rather than by the transverse area of a sphere. The Hubble parameter, deceleration parameter and cosmological density parameter are
H=aa˙​,q=−a˙2aa¨​,Ω=ρcr​ρ​,ρcr​=κ3H2​.​
(2)
The last expression defines the critical density. For blackbody radiation, p=ρ/3, so the Friedmann acceleration equation gives a¨/a=−κρ/3. Consequently
q=3H2κρ​=Ω.​
(3)
This is the radiation relation between deceleration and density. It does not require spatial flatness.
For the open radiation universe particle horizon, write x=a/a0​. The cosmological continuity equation gives ρ=ρ0​x−4, while the present Friedmann equation gives −k/a02​=H02​(1−Ω0​). Thus
H2=H02​[Ω0​x−4+(1−Ω0​)x−2],x˙=xH0​​Ω0​+(1−Ω0​)x2​.
(4)
Taking the big-bang endpoint x=0 and using dt=dx/x˙, the present radial proper distance becomes
dh0​​=∫01​xx˙dx​=H0​1​∫01​Ω0​+(1−Ω0​)x2​dx​=H0​1−Ω0​​arsinh(1−Ω0​)/Ω0​​​.​
(5)
Therefore
dh0​=H0​1−Ω0​​1​ln[Ω0​1−Ω0​​​+1+Ω0​1−Ω0​​​].​
(6)
The formula applies to 0<Ω0​<1. Its flat-radiation limit is H0​dh0​→1, agreeing with a∝t1/2 and dh​=2t=H−1. The divergence as Ω0​→0 reflects the unbounded past conformal interval of the limiting empty open model; that endpoint is not a radiation-filled universe.
The horizon problem concerns the nearly uniform temperature of widely separated parts of the cosmic microwave background. In a purely decelerating Hot Big Bang model, their past light cones at last scattering do not overlap far enough to explain this agreement by thermal contact. During cosmic inflation, accelerated expansion shrinks the comoving Hubble radius. A patch initially small enough for causal communication can be stretched to encompass the later observable universe. Reheating converts the inflationary energy into a hot plasma with correlated initial conditions across that patch. Enough inflation must occur before the observable scales leave the Hubble radius; inflation cannot establish contact between regions that were never initially causally related.

For a quantitative thermal-relic prediction, assume one ordinary weakly interacting neutrino flavor: one populated neutrino helicity and one antineutrino helicity, negligible chemical potential, early thermal equilibrium, and relativistic decoupling around the MeV epoch. Equal neutrino and antineutrino populations imply no new lepton asymmetry. The Fermi-Dirac distribution is maintained while the weak interaction rate exceeds the expansion rate. After neutrino decoupling, the distribution free streams, its momentum scale falls as a−1, and its comoving number is conserved. A mass of 1keV is negligible at Big Bang nucleosynthesis temperatures, but the relic later becomes nonrelativistic. Its energy density then changes from radiation-like a−4 scaling to matter-like a−3 scaling. Stability prevents its removal by decay; decoupling while relativistic prevents a subsequent equilibrium Boltzmann suppression of its number.

The density bound on a relativistically decoupled massive relic is already decisive for a conventional near-critical cosmological density budget: the thermal pair alone gives Ω≃22.4. The same additional relativistic pair increases the effective number of neutrino species by one during nucleosynthesis and raises the primordial helium mass fraction, so its expansion effect supplies another constraint. Thus a stable, fully thermalized MeV-decoupling neutrino pair of this mass is incompatible with the usual cosmological density budget.
This conclusion is not a prohibition on every particle with that mass. Earlier decoupling followed by entropy dilution, a reduced production abundance, or interactions that change the assumed thermal history can alter both tests. At fixed mass, requiring this pair to contribute no more than the supplied critical density would demand a number density at most 1/22.4 of the standard value, or a thermal temperature at most (1/22.4)1/3≃0.355 of the standard neutrino temperature. No numerical abundance or exclusion independent of these assumptions follows from the mass and equal particle-antiparticle counts alone.

A general scalar cosmological perturbation has four scalar metric functions: a lapse perturbation, a scalar shift, a spatial curvature perturbation and a scalar spatial shear. The two scalar functions in a cosmological gauge transformation can set the shift and shear to zero. For nondegenerate spatial modes this gives conformal Newtonian gauge, leaving the two potentials ψ and ϕ. The scalar traceless spatial Einstein equation makes their difference proportional to scalar anisotropic stress. In general relativity, with no scalar anisotropic stress and the usual boundary conditions, ψ=ϕ. Pressure alone does not spoil equality; free-streaming relativistic species can do so through their anisotropic stress.
Write Ψ=(ϕ+ψ)/2, the Weyl lensing potential. A conformal rescaling leaves the unparameterized null geodesics unchanged, although the affine parameter changes. To first order, the conformal optical metric for scalar perturbations has
g​00​=1+4Ψ,g​0i​=0,g​ij​=−δij​.
(14)
The only nonzero connection coefficients at this order are
Γ000​=2∂η​Ψ,Γ0i0​=2∂i​Ψ,Γ00i​=2∂iΨ.
(15)
Here spatial indices are raised with δij. An affine parameter λ for this rescaled metric therefore obeys
η,λλ​+2∂η​Ψη,λ2​+4∂i​Ψx,λi​η,λ​x,λλi​+2∂iΨη,λ2​​=0,=0.​
(16)
The first line is also η,λλ​+2η,λ​dΨ/dλ+2x,λi​η,λ​∂i​Ψ=0, since the derivative of the potential is along the ray.
Put vi=dxi/dη. The chain rule gives x,λλi​=η,λ2​dvi/dη+viη,λλ​. Eliminating the affine acceleration gives
dηdvi​−2vi(dηdΨ​+vj∂j​Ψ)+2∂iΨ=0,dηdΨ​=∂η​Ψ+vj∂j​Ψ.​
(17)
The extra spatial-gradient term inside the parentheses does not replace the spatial term already contained in the total derivative.
At reception, the null condition is ∣v∣2=1+4Ψ0​. With the specified unit propagation vector e, this implies
vi(η0​)=(1+2Ψ0​)ei.​

The zero-order trajectory is xi=ei(η−η0​). In every term already containing a first-order potential, we may substitute v=e. Defining the transverse projector P⊥ij​=δij−eiej, the first-order geodesic equation becomes
dη2d2xi​=2eidηdΨ​−2P⊥ij​∂j​Ψ.
(19)
Integrating with the reception position and velocity gives the first-order photon trajectory in scalar perturbations:
xi(η)=ei(η−η0​)+2ei∫η0​η​Ψ(η′)dη′−2∫η0​η​(η−η′)P⊥ij​∂j​Ψ(η′)dη′.​

For a first-order calculation, evaluating the integrals on the unperturbed ray is sufficient; shifting their evaluation points by the first-order displacement changes them only at second order. This is the Born approximation in cosmological lensing.
To fix the angular-deflection sign, define χs​=η0​−ηs​>0, nobs​=−e, and nsrc​=xs​/∣xs​∣. The latter is the unlensed coordinate direction to the source, while the former is its apparent direction measured by the observer. The longitudinal integral changes the radial distance, not the direction at this order. The transverse displacement is
xs⊥i​=−2∫0χs​​(χs​−χ)∂⊥i​Ψ(η0​−χ,−eχ)dχ.
(21)
Dividing by χs​ yields
αi≡nobsi​−nsrci​=2∫0χs​​χs​χs​−χ​∂⊥i​Ψ(η0​−χ,−eχ)dχ.​
(22)
This is the angular deflection kernel for cosmological lensing. It depends on the sum of the two metric potentials; setting them equal reduces it to the familiar twice-the-Newtonian-potential expression. With angular derivatives, the lensing potential is 2∫0χs​​[(χs​−χ)/(χs​χ)]Ψdχ, and its angular gradient is α.
Gravitational lensing displaces apparent source positions and produces magnification and shear through angular derivatives of the deflection. Weak gravitational lensing of galaxy shapes probes the foreground mass distribution, including dark matter. Lensing also remaps the cosmic microwave background, smoothing its acoustic features and converting part of its E-mode polarization into B modes. Strong deflections can produce multiple images, though a single first-order ray calculation does not describe that regime completely.

The broad turnover near k∼10−2Mpc−1 marks the matter-radiation equality scale. Modes entering the Hubble radius after matter-radiation equality grow efficiently during matter domination. Modes entering earlier have their cold-matter growth delayed by radiation domination: their subhorizon growth is logarithmic rather than proportional to a. This is the Mészáros effect. The smaller undulations on the declining side are baryon acoustic oscillations inherited from the photon-baryon fluid, with an oscillatory wavenumber spacing set approximately by π divided by the sound horizon. They are damped at sufficiently short scales by photon diffusion and the transfer of the baryon perturbation into the total matter field. The plotted spectrum is a linear prediction; nonlinear evolution changes the actual sufficiently small-scale spectrum at redshift zero.
For an adiabatic growing mode, the comoving curvature perturbation transfers to the matter overdensity as
Δm​(k,a)=52​Ωm0​H02​k2​T(k)D(a)R(k),
(23)
up to an irrelevant overall sign convention for R. Normalize the cosmological transfer function by T→1 at small k and the linear growth factor by D→a in matter domination. The relation distinguishes a dimensional matter power spectrum from a dimensionless cosmological power spectrum: the dimensional curvature spectrum is proportional to k−3PR​(k). Therefore
Pm​(k,a)∝D(a)2kT(k)2PR​(k).
(24)

Pm​(k)∝{kns​,kns​−4ln2(k/keq​),​k≪keq​,k≫keq​,​​

Take the usual smooth late dark energy, in particular a cosmological constant, and hold the primordial perturbations fixed. Increasing its physical density while holding the matter and radiation densities fixed raises H0​ in a flat universe. It lowers Ωm0​, but leaves the physical product Ωm0​H02​=κρm0​/3 fixed. It also leaves the early equality ratio aeq​=ρr0​/ρm0​ and the equality wavenumber in Mpc−1 essentially unchanged, provided dark energy remains negligible at equality. The early cosmological transfer function, including the acoustic scale, is then unchanged.
More dark energy causes accelerated expansion to start earlier, reducing the time for matter growth. The suppression of matter growth by smooth accelerated expansion lowers D(1) and hence the present spectrum. For the subhorizon scales shown,
Pmold​(k,0)Pmnew​(k,0)​≃[Dold​(1)Dnew​(1)​]2<1.​
(26)
This is approximately a downward amplitude change with the same turnover and wiggle locations in physical inverse-megaparsec units. For matter plus a cosmological constant, an explicit early-normalized growth solution is
D(a)=25Ωm0​H02​​H(a)∫0a​a′3H(a′)3da′​,
(27)
with radiation neglected in this late-time growth equation. A numerical factor needs the old and new dark-energy densities. A change to axes expressed using h would additionally relabel wavenumbers and volumes; the printed axes use Mpc−1 and Mpc3.
