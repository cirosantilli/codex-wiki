Write μ=1+ε, p=μ/n, q=1−p, and let T be the total progeny of a binomial branching process. The preceding binomial branching survival correction gives ρ=(2+o(1))ε. For a branching process conditioned on extinction, the offspring probability generating function is f((1−ρ)s)/(1−ρ), with mean
f′(1−ρ)=(1+ε)(1−pρ)n−1=1−ε+O(ε2+ε/n)≤1−ε/2

for sufficiently large n. Summing expected generation sizes therefore gives E[T;T<∞]=(1−ρ)/(1−f′(1−ρ))=O(1/ε).

No graph component finishes there: all these explored vertices belong to one graph component, of order at least (2−2h)εn−O(1), with high probability. Its expected value is therefore at least (2−o(1))εn. Combining both inequalities proves
