Assume 0<λ=1−ε<1, as required for this nontrivial subcritical binomial random graph. Put δ=λ−1−logλ>0. A breadth-first exploration of a binomial random graph is dominated by a Galton-Watson process whose offspring counts are independent random variables with law Bin(n,λ/n). If its total progeny T is at least j, the first j−1 offspring counts sum to at least j−1. Their moment-generating function is bounded by that of a Poisson distribution of mean λ(j−1). The exponential Markov inequality, optimized at t=−logλ, yields
P(T≥j)≤e−δ(j−1).
(14)
Taking j=⌈(1+η)logn/δ⌉ for any fixed η>0, a union bound over all starting vertices gives P(L1​≥j)=o(1).
For the matching lower bound, let Xj​ count tree components of order j. The tree-component expectation in the Erdős-Rényi model is exactly
EXj​=(jn​)jj−2pj−1(1−p)j(n−j)+(2j​)−j+1.
(15)
For j=O(logn), the Stirling formula gives
EXj​=(1+o(1))λ2π​j5/2n​e−δj.

Choose j=⌊(1−η)logn/δ⌋, with 0<η<1. Then EXj​→∞. Distinct overlapping vertex sets cannot both be graph components. For disjoint sets, the joint probability differs from the product only because the j2 between-set edges were counted twice as absent. Thus
(EXj​)2E[Xj​(Xj​−1)]​=(jn​)(jn−j​)​(1−p)−j2=1+O(j2/n).

It follows that varXj​/(EXj​)2=o(1). The second moment method gives Xj​>0 with high probability. Combining both bounds, and then taking arbitrarily small fixed η, proves the sharp subcritical largest-component scale
L1​=(1+o(1))δlogn​=(1+o(1))ℓ0​with high probability.​

Finally the Taylor expansion of −log(1−ε) gives δ=ε2/2+ε3/3+⋯. The endpoint λ=0 has no edges and is excluded from this logarithmic asymptotic.
