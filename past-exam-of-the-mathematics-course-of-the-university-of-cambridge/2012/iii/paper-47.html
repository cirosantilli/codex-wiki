Use ℏ=c=1 in the field-theory calculations. Work with signature (+,−) and a=0. Since x=(x+​+x−​) and t=(x+​−x−​), ∂+​=∂x​+∂t​, ∂−​=∂x​−∂t​, and the Sine-Gordon equation is ϕ+−​=sinϕ. Introduce w=(ρ+κ)/2 and d=(ρ−κ)/2. The specified Sine-Gordon Bäcklund transformation becomes d+​=asinw, w−​=a−1sind. Its compatibility gives
d+−​=coswsind,w+−​=cosdsinw.
(1)
Adding and subtracting these equations establishes
ρ+−​=sin(w+d)=sinρ,κ+−​=sin(w−d)=sinκ.​
(2)
Thus a compatible transformed field satisfies the same Sine-Gordon equation. Smoothness is needed to interchange mixed derivatives. The statement cannot literally include a=0, since one defining equation contains 1/a.
Generating the kink. With zero seed, the two first-order equations give ρ+​=2asin(ρ/2) and ρ−​=2a−1sin(ρ/2). On a nonconstant branch put p=tan(ρ/4); then p+​=ap, p−​=a−1p. Integration gives
ρ=4arctan[Cexp(21​(a+a−1)x+21​(a−a−1)t)].​
(3)
For a>0,C>0, this is the Sine-Gordon kink, after absorbing C in the center position. Its speed and Lorentz factor obey
v=1+a21−a2​,(1−v2)−1/2=21​(a+a−1),a=e−η, v=tanhη.​
(4)
Changing the sign of a or of the exponential coefficient selects the corresponding antikink orientation; ∣v∣<1 whenever a is finite and nonzero. Constant vacuum branches, omitted by division by sin(ρ/2), can be added separately.
The continuously parametrized Bäcklund transformation does more than produce this one solution. Successive compatible transformations and Bianchi permutability for sine-Gordon Bäcklund transformations construct multisoliton solutions; expansion of the generating relations gives the local conserved-charge hierarchy of sine-Gordon theory. Together with its Lax pair and inverse scattering transform, this is the structure behind classical integrability, elastic scattering and the absence of generic radiative energy loss in soliton collisions. Existence of a transformation alone is not a proof that every generated conserved charge is independent and in involution; those are additional properties of the integrable system.
Transforming the static kink at a=1. The seed obeys κx​=2sechx, sin(κ/2)=sechx, cos(κ/2)=−tanhx. Adding and subtracting the two Sine-Gordon Bäcklund transformation equations yields
ρx​=−2tanhxsin(ρ/2),ρt​=2sechx[1+cos(ρ/2)].
(5)
Again set p=tan(ρ/4). These simplify to px​=−ptanhx, pt​=sechx. The first gives p=f(t)/coshx, and the second gives f′=1. Therefore
ρ(x,t)=4arctancoshxt−t0​​.​
(6)
Continuous inverse-tangent branches may be chosen without creating artificial jumps. At spatial infinity the field approaches the same vacuum, so its net topological charge is zero. For large ∣t−t0​∣ there are two well-separated transitions near x±​=±arcosh∣t−t0​∣, with speeds tending to zero. The two transitions exchange their kink/antikink orientations as they pass through the collision, while the field remains smooth.
This is the Sine-Gordon threshold kink-antikink solution, a separatrix between finite-speed scattering and the bounded Sine-Gordon breather motion, rather than a finite-period breather. In fact it is the v→0 limit of the scattering field in question 3. At t=t0​, ρ=0 and ρt​=4sechx, so the reduced energy is ∫ρt2​dx/2=16, exactly twice the reduced single-kink energy 8.
For mass comparisons below, the original PDF really prints m2/β. Restoring physical coordinates X=x/m, T=t/m gives the angular-field action β−1∫dtdx[21​(∂ϕ)2+cosϕ−1] and literal kink mass 8m/β, for β>0. The usual Sine-Gordon theory instead has prefactor m2/β2 and mass 8m/β2. The equation of motion and every field above are unchanged by this overall normalization; the quantum scattering comparison in question 3 is not.

Take λ=0 and use the complex sine-Gordon theory reduced density ℓ=L/M2, with physical lengths and times x/M,t/M. Physical rest energy is M∫dxHℓ​, and the dimensionless Noether charge is obtained from the reduced action. Put c=cosα, s=sinα, f(x)=csech(cx)/λ, so ψ=feist. Initially take 0<∣α∣<π/2, avoiding the singular target-space boundary.
The canonical Hamiltonian density is
Hℓ​=1−λ2∣ψ∣2∣ψ˙​∣2+∣ψx​∣2​+∣ψ∣2.
(7)
Here ∣ψ˙​∣2=s2f2, ∣ψx​∣2=c2f2tanh2(cx), and D=1−c2sech2(cx). Since s2+c2tanh2(cx)=D, the kinetic contribution is simply f2. Consequently
M(α)=2M∫f2dx=λ24M​cosα.​
(8)
To fix the charge sign, take the Noether current directly from δψ=iεψ, δψ∗=−iεψ∗:
jμ=1−λ2∣ψ∣2i(ψ∂μψ∗−ψ∗∂μψ)​,j0=D2sf2​.
(9)
Thus positive internal rotation has positive charge in this convention. With X=cx and the supplied integral,
Q=λ22sc​∫−∞∞​cosh2X−c2dX​,Q(α)=λ24​[sgn(α)2π​−α].​
(10)
Reversing the definition of the generator would reverse all charge labels but not any mass. The limit α→π/2 is the vacuum with zero mass and charge. At α=0 the field reaches ∣ψ∣=1/∣λ∣ at its center and the current's coordinate expression becomes singular. The mass still has the finite limiting value 4M/λ2, but
α→0±lim​Q=±2π/λ2.​
(11)
The displayed local density does not assign a unique endpoint charge merely by substituting α=0.
The four collective parameters. Translation, a Lorentz boost of rapidity b, and the global phase symmetry produce
ψ(x,t)=λc​cosh(cξ)eiχ+isη​,ξ=coshb(x−X0​)−sinhbt,η=coshbt−sinhb(x−X0​).​
(12)
The independent real parameters are α,b,X0​,χ, with χ periodic modulo 2π. A further time translation is absorbed into these parameters. The energy and momentum are Mcoshb and Msinhb, while Q is invariant. The internal angle is the collective coordinate whose canonical momentum is this Noether charge.
Semiclassical quantization. For one rest-frame internal rotation, the period in dimensionless time is T=2π/∣s∣. The action around the orbit is
I=∫0T​dt∫dx(πψ​ψ˙​+πψ∗​ψ˙​∗)=∫0T​dt∫dxD2∣ψ˙​∣2​=TsQ=2π∣Q∣.
(13)
Equivalently this is S+ET, where E is the reduced rest energy. Bohr-Sommerfeld quantization with ℏ=1 gives I=2πn. Because the internal orbit is a cyclic angle, not a one-dimensional oscillator between turning points, there is no half-integer Maslov index shift. Hence
Q=±n,n=1,2,…,∣α∣=2π​−4λ2n​,Mn​=λ24M​sin4λ2n​.​
(14)
On the nonsingular branch 0<n<2π/λ2. The elementary charge-one mass approaches M at weak coupling. For positive a,b with (a+b)λ2/4≤π/2, sina′+sinb′>sin(a′+b′), where a′=aλ2/4, b′=bλ2/4. Thus Ma+b​<Ma​+Mb​: the charge-n soliton cannot fragment into smaller like-charge particles. For a general charge-conserving partition, a constituent with ∣qi​∣≥n already has mass at least Mn​; if every ∣qi​∣<n, the decreasing ratio Mq​/q gives ∑i​M∣qi​∣​≥(Mn​/n)∑i​∣qi​∣≥Mn​, strictly for nontrivial fragmentation. Thus opposite-charge constituents cannot open a lower-energy decay either. This is charged rather than topological stability, and the semiclassical spectrum is reliable at weak coupling.
Counting states and the endpoint. Write L=2π/λ2. If L is not an integer, the regular branch gives
Q=±1,…,±⌊L⌋,number of charge states=2⌊L⌋.​
(15)
If L is an integer, the local nonsingular calculation gives only 2(L−1) regular states. The formal closed-branch list has 2L charge labels, but its last two labels are limits of the same singular static solution. Counting them needs a global completion; it cannot be decided from a regular current integral at α=0.
In the additional integer-level completion, λ2=4π/k at leading semiclassical order and charges are identified modulo integer k>1. There are then k−1 nonzero charge sectors. For odd k, positive and negative labels are distinct; for even k, the endpoint labels ±k/2 describe one self-conjugate state. This supplies 2L−1 states when k=2L, rather than 2L endpoint labels. This global interpretation, and the distinction between classical and renormalized couplings, are discussed in Dorey and Hollowood, section 2. A generic classical λ does not automatically define an integer k or specify this quantum completion.

Take 0<v<1 and Γ=(1−v2)−1/2. The Sine-Gordon kink-antikink scattering solution tends to the same vacuum at both spatial ends and hence has zero net topological charge. At large ∣t∣, the two transition centers, found by setting the magnitude of the inverse-tangent argument to one, obey
Γ∣x∣=Γv∣t∣−logv+o(1).
(16)
They are a kink and an antikink moving with asymptotic speeds ±v, with no outgoing radiation. Their left-right orientations interchange at the collision. To define the soliton time delay, label a transmitted trajectory by its preserved rapidity rather than its left-right position. The right-moving asymptotes are
xin​=vt+Γlogv​,xout​=vt−Γlogv​.
(17)
After restoring physical lengths, the forward shift is ΔX=−2logv/(mΓ)>0. The arrival-time difference is therefore
ΔT=−ΔX/v=mΓv2logv​=msinh(θ/2)2logtanh(θ/2)​<0,v=tanh(θ/2).​
(18)
This is a time advance relative to the extrapolated incoming free motion.
The semiclassical scattering phase. For an outgoing energy wave packet with full S-matrix phase S=eiδ(E), stationary phase in e−iET+iδ(E) shifts its arrival time by dδ/dE. The full S-matrix phase from a classical soliton delay uses the paper's δ, twice the phase in a convention S=e2iδpw​. In the center-of-momentum frame, E=2MK​cosh(θ/2), so dE/dθ=MK​sinh(θ/2). It follows that
dθdδsc​​=m2MK​​logtanh(θ/2).​
(19)
The literal PDF normalization gives MK​=8m/β and consequently
δliteral​(θ)=δliteral​(0)+β16​∫0θ​logtanh(u/2)du.​
(20)
The integrable logarithmic singularity at zero causes no divergence of this phase difference. With the intended standard angular-field prefactor m2/β2, the result is instead
δstandard​(θ)=δstandard​(0)+β216​∫0θ​logtanh(u/2)du.​
(21)
The soliton time delay determines only phase differences, not an energy-independent constant or a choice of 2π branch. Keeping this distinction avoids an arbitrary high-energy subtraction.
Bound-state poles. Set aj​=πj/N. Each factor of the exact amplitude can be written cosh[(θ−iaj​)/2]/cosh[(θ+iaj​)/2]. Its denominator vanishes in the physical rapidity strip at
θ=iun​,un​=π(1−n/N),n=1,…,N−1.
(22)
The corresponding numerator is nonzero, and no other numerator cancels the pole. In the direct kink–antikink fusion interpretation, analytically continue the constituent rapidities to ±iun​/2. Their momenta sum to (2MK​cos(un​/2),0), so the relativistic bound-state mass from a rapidity pole gives
Mn​=2MK​sin2Nnπ​,n=1,…,N−1.​
(23)
These neutral particles form the Sine-Gordon breather spectrum at reflectionless couplings: there are N−1 breathers, none for N=1, and the putative n=N state is at the unbound 2MK​ threshold. The amplitude fixes these ratios to the physical kink mass; its rapidity dependence alone cannot fix the overall mass scale or a mass-renormalization prescription relating that mass to m.
It is important to distinguish direct and crossed interpretations rather than count every occurrence of a pole twice. Crossing symmetry sends iun​ to i(π−un​)=iuN−n​. At the original angle the momentum-difference invariant is 4MK2​sin2(un​/2)=MN−n2​, so the crossed interpretation exchanges the complementary member of the same tower. In particular the transmission residue alternates sign: direct evaluation of the remaining factors gives Resiun​​ST​=i(−1)nRn​, Rn​>0. Thus one must retain charge-channel/crossed-channel information, not reject every negative-imaginary transmission residue or declare an additional particle for a crossed pole. Direct and crossed locations coincide in the reflectionless pole set. The familiar N=2 case has one breather of mass 2​MK​ despite the negative-imaginary transmission residue; this diagonal reflectionless example is also displayed in Castro-Alvaredo, Chen, Doyon and Hoogeveen, section 4.2.
Matching the exact phase. For the unwrapped reflectionless sine-Gordon transmission phase, at real θ≥0 choose the continuous unwrapped branch with δN​(0)=πN. Then
δN​(θ)=πN−2j=1∑N−1​arctan[tanh(θ/2)tan2Nπj​].​
(24)
Every factor has unit modulus, consistent with purely transmitting elastic scattering. Differentiating is simpler than integrating its complex logarithm:
δN′​(θ)=−∑j=1N−1​coshθ+cos(πj/N)sin(πj/N)​.
(25)
For fixed θ>0 the sum becomes a Riemann sum, and the elementary integral yields
δN′​(θ)​∼−πN​∫0π​coshθ+cosasina​da=−πN​logcoshθ−1coshθ+1​=π2N​logtanh(θ/2)=γ16​logtanh(θ/2).​
(26)
Since β2=8π/(N+1) and γ=8π/N, replacing γ by β2 changes this only at subleading order. The leading phase difference agrees with the standard semiclassical result. The derivative approximation is not uniform as θ→0 at fixed N; integrating its logarithm gives a finite leading phase difference nevertheless. A compatible constant is the given branch πN, whose choice is supplied by the exact amplitude rather than the classical trajectory.
As a useful endpoint check, ∫0∞​logtanh(u/2)du=−π2/4, obtained by expanding the two logarithms log(1−e−u)−log(1+e−u). Thus the leading phase with coefficient 2N/π tends to πN/2. The exact unwrapped endpoints are
δN​(0)=πN,δN​(+∞)=2π(N+1)​,​
(27)
where the extra π/2 is subleading. Neither should be replaced by zero by an unannounced branch convention. Finally, the literal m2/β action gives a phase derivative of order N​, whereas the printed exact amplitude gives order N. The requested exact/semiclassical matching requires correcting the overall action normalization to m2/β2. This is an actual inconsistency between pages 2 and 4, not a change to the sine-Gordon equation or the classical scattering field.

An instanton describes a finite-action trajectory in imaginary time that crosses between classical vacua. It accounts for quantum tunnelling effects exponentially small in ℏ, beyond an ordinary power series around one minimum. A symmetric double well makes the connection between the Euclidean path integral, fluctuation theory and the low-energy spectrum particularly explicit.
For a particle of mass μ, use a Wick rotation t=−iτ in the kernel. The Euclidean action and its classical equation are
SE​[q]=∫dτ[21​μq˙​2+V(q)],μq¨​=V′(q).
(28)
The latter is Newtonian motion in the inverted potential −V. Subtract the common minimum energy so V(±a)=0. A finite-action path approaching ±a at infinite Euclidean times has conserved Euclidean energy μq˙​2/2−V=0. Therefore
q˙​=±2V(q)/μ​,SI​=∫−aa​2μV(q)​dq.
(29)
This gives both the classical trajectory by a quadrature and its tunnelling exponent. The plus solution is the instanton; reversal is the anti-instanton. A single crossing has action SI​, not 2SI​, although a periodic closed path necessarily has equal numbers of crossings in both directions.
An explicit double well. Take V(q)=μω2(q2−a2)2/(8a2), whose local harmonic frequency at either minimum is ω. Integrating the zero-energy equation gives the quartic double-well instanton
qI​(τ)=atanh[ω(τ−τ0​)/2],SI​=2μωa2/3.​
(30)
The arbitrary center τ0​ is a collective coordinate. The tunnelling regime is SI​/ℏ≫1, so the crossing width ω−1 is short compared with the typical separation between rare crossings.
The exponential alone does not give a dimensionally complete amplitude. Expand q=qI​+η; the quadratic Euclidean action is μ∫ηLI​ηdτ/2, where
LI​=−∂τ2​+ω2[1−23​sech22ω(τ−τ0​)​],L0​=−∂τ2​+ω2.
(31)
The derivative q˙​I​ is a normalizable translation instanton translation zero mode. The factorization of this one-dimensional fluctuation operator shows it has no negative modes. In z=ω(τ−τ0​)/2, its two discrete eigenfunctions are proportional to sech2z and sechztanhz, with eigenvalues zero and 3ω2/4; the continuum begins at ω2. Treating its zero eigenvalue as an ordinary Gaussian integral would give a divergent determinant. Instead integrate over τ0​: since μ∫q˙​I2​dτ=SI​, the Jacobian supplies SI​/(2πℏ)​dτ0​. The nonzero fluctuations supply the determinant ratio. With common endpoint regularization,
K=2πℏSI​​​[det′LI​detL0​​]1/2,κ=Ke−SI​/ℏ.​
(32)
This is the instanton fluctuation prefactor, with K and κ having frequency units. The prime omits precisely the translation mode.
For this quartic potential the determinant can be evaluated explicitly. Set z=ω(τ−τ0​)/2 and add a small positive spectral regulator ε to both operators. With r=21+ε/ω2​, the dimensionless operator is −∂z2​+r2−6sech2z. Applying successively −∂z​+tanhz and −∂z​+2tanhz to the free decaying solution e−rz, and normalizing at z→+∞, gives a growing coefficient at z→−∞ equal to (r−1)(r−2)/[(r+1)(r+2)]. The common Dirichlet functional determinant ratio has this coefficient. Hence the quartic double-well fluctuation determinant is
det(L0​+ε)det(LI​+ε)​=(r+1)(r+2)(r−1)(r−2)​=12ω2ε​+O(ε2),detL0​det′LI​​=12ω21​,K=ωπℏ6SI​​​.​
(33)
The frequency factor cannot be discarded when deleting the zero eigenvalue.
Summing rare crossings. Consider Euclidean duration T much longer than a crossing width. In the dilute instanton gas, n ordered centers have integration volume Tn/n!, and the amplitude per crossing is κ. Starting in the left well, directions alternate: an even number returns to the left well, and an odd number ends in the right well. If Ewell​=ℏω/2+ local anharmonic corrections, the leading kernels, with a common endpoint normalization A, are
GLL​(T)≃Ae−Ewell​T/ℏcosh(κT),GRL​(T)≃Ae−Ewell​T/ℏsinh(κT).​
(34)
The even/odd sums must not be replaced by counting all possible crossing directions independently. Comparing their exponential components with the spectral representation gives
Eeven​=Ewell​−ℏκ,Eodd​=Ewell​+ℏκ,ΔE=2ℏKe−SI​/ℏ.​
(35)
For the quartic example, ΔE=2ℏω6SI​/(πℏ)​e−SI​/ℏ to leading semiclassical order. This is the double-well tunneling splitting: the even state is lower because the symmetric wavefunction has no node. The equivalent low-energy Hamiltonian in localized left/right states is Ewell​I−ℏκσx​. An initially localized state tunnels coherently, with probability sin2(ΔEt/(2ℏ)) of occupying the other well. Tunnelling does not imply irreversible decay in this closed symmetric system.
The instanton approach thus yields the same leading forbidden-region action as the WKB approximation, while identifying the zero-mode measure, fluctuation normalization and multiple tunnelling events systematically. Perturbation theory about either separate minimum cannot generate e−SI​/ℏ. Multi-instanton interactions and higher-loop fluctuations correct the leading dilute-gas result; at finite temperature one instead uses periodic Euclidean boundary conditions, whose saddles may differ when the period is comparable to the crossing width. A false-vacuum bounce returning to the same metastable minimum has a negative fluctuation mode and describes a decay rate. It should not be confused with the stable-vacuum connecting instanton above, which has a translation zero mode but no negative mode.
