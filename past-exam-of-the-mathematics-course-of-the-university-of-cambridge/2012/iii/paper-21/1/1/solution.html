Write d=degA for the degree of a central simple algebra, so dimK​A=d2. Choose a finite Galois splitting field of a central simple algebra E/K for A and an isomorphism ϕ:A⊗K​E→Md​(E). The reduced norm is
NrdA/K​(a)=detϕ(a⊗1).
(1)
The existence of a finite separable splitting field is a standard structural property of a central simple algebra; passing to its Galois closure supplies E.
Every E-algebra automorphism of Md​(E) is inner. Here is the matrix-unit argument for the inner automorphisms of a matrix algebra result. For an automorphism h, choose w=0 in the image of h(E11​), and set wi​=h(Ei1​)w. Then h(Eij​)wk​=δjk​wi​. The wi​ are nonzero and independent, and their number is d, so they form a basis. Relative to that basis h acts on each matrix unit in the usual way. Thus h(T)=PTP−1 for some P∈GLd​(E). Determinants are unchanged by conjugation, proving independence of ϕ.
For σ∈Gal(E/K), compare ϕ with the isomorphism obtained by applying σ to matrix entries and σ−1 to the scalar factor of A⊗E. Their difference is again inner. For a∈A, this shows σ(detϕ(a))=detϕ(a). The determinant therefore belongs to K. In fact, for a K-basis a1​,…,ad2​, the same comparison shows that all coefficients of
det(∑i​Xi​ϕ(ai​))
(2)
are fixed by the Galois group, so the reduced norm is a homogeneous polynomial of degree d over K. This coefficient argument also applies over finite fields, where equality merely as functions would not identify polynomials.
Finally, two finite splitting fields embed into a common finite splitting extension. Determinant commutes with scalar extension, and over that common extension the two matrix identifications differ by an inner automorphism. Hence the resulting polynomials agree over K. The reduced norm is independent of both the splitting field and the matrix identification. It is multiplicative, and Nrd(a)=0 exactly when a is not invertible: a matrix with nonzero determinant is invertible after scalar extension, and invertibility descends by the invertibility of the K-linear multiplication map. In a division algebra the only element of reduced norm zero is zero.
