Let G=GK​ and choose a profinite Sylow subgroup P at the prime p. Its image in every finite quotient of G is a Sylow p-subgroup. Put F=KsP​. Every finite intermediate field K⊆L⊆F has degree prime to p, because its corresponding open subgroup contains P. Condition (iii), together with the preceding Kummer reduction, gives Hn+1(L,μp​)=0 for each such field. The cohomology continuity at closed subgroups property says
Hq(P,M)=lim​P⊆U⊆G, U open​Hq(U,M)
(28)
for a discrete G-module M. Applying it to M=μp​ gives Hn+1(P,μp​)=0.
The action of the pro-p group P on μp​ is trivial. Indeed it maps continuously to Aut(μp​)≅Fp×​, whose order p−1 is prime to p; every finite image of P is a p-group, so that image is trivial. Choosing a primitive root identifies this module with the trivial module Fp​. Thus Hn+1(P,Fp​)=0.
We spell out why this controls arbitrary p-primary coefficients. A finite discrete p-primary P-module has a composition series with trivial Fp​ factors: its action factors through a finite p-group, whose only simple module in characteristic p is the trivial one. Induction through the long exact cohomology sequence therefore gives vanishing of Hn+1 for every such finite module. Any discrete p-primary P-module is the filtered union of finite P-stable submodules. The orbit of an element is finite by continuity, and its orbit generates a finite abelian p-group. Continuous cohomology commutes with these filtered unions, so
Hn+1(P,M)=0
(29)
for every discrete p-primary module M. This is the mechanism behind trivial coefficients detect the cohomological dimension of a pro-p group.
Now let M be any discrete p-primary G-module and a∈Hn+1(G,M). Its restriction to P is zero. Continuity at the closed subgroup P makes its restriction zero in some open U⊇P. The index m=[G:U] is prime to p, and the restriction-corestriction identity in group cohomology for the corestriction map in group cohomology gives
ma=CorUG​ResUG​a=0.
(30)
But a has p-power order, so multiplication by m is invertible on the cyclic group it generates. Hence a=0. This proves Hn+1(G,M)=0 for all discrete p-primary M. Dimension shifting through an acyclic coinduced module gives vanishing in every higher degree, which is exactly cdp​(K)≤n.
Therefore (iii) implies (i), completing the equivalence of all three conditions. The key reason prime-to-p extensions suffice is that they approximate a pro-p Sylow subgroup, where the cyclotomic module becomes trivial; prime-to-p transfer then detects every p-primary cohomology class.
