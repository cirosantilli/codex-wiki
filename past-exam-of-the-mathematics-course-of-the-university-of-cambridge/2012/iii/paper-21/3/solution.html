Let G=Gal(L/K). A semilinear action on an L-vector space V is an additive action with σ(av)=σ(a)σ(v). The Galois descent of vector spaces theorem says that VG is a K-vector space and the natural map
L⊗K​VG⟶V,a⊗v⟼av
(16)
is an isomorphism. In particular dimK​VG=dimL​V. Morphisms also descend: an L-linear equivariant map is the scalar extension of its restriction to invariant subspaces.
We prove that the invariant vectors span V over L, without dividing by ∣G∣. For a∈L and v∈V, the vector
Ta​(v)=∑σ∈G​σ(a)σ(v)
(17)
is invariant, because every τ∈G merely permutes the terms. Suppose their L-span were proper. There would be a nonzero L-linear functional λ annihilating that span. For each fixed v, we would have
∑σ∈G​λ(σ(v))σ(a)=0for every a∈L.
(18)
The Artin independence theorem makes all coefficients zero, in particular λ(v)=0 for every v, a contradiction. To recall its elementary proof, take a nontrivial linear relation between distinct field automorphisms with the fewest nonzero coefficients. Replace the input a by ab and subtract one automorphism's value on b times the original relation. Choose b on which two participating automorphisms differ. The resulting nonzero relation has fewer terms, contradicting minimality.
Choose an L-basis v1​,…,vm​ consisting of invariant vectors. If v=∑i​ai​vi​ is invariant, uniqueness of coordinates gives σ(ai​)=ai​ for every σ, so every ai​ lies in K. These vectors are therefore a K-basis of VG, proving the displayed map is an isomorphism. Restriction and scalar extension are visibly inverse on equivariant morphisms, completing the descent theorem. The argument remains valid when the characteristic divides ∣G∣.
Now put B=Mn​(L), and let σ0​ act on B by applying σ to each entry. The group AutL​(B) is a G-group under σ(h)=σ0​hσ0−1​. Its nonabelian first cohomology consists of cocycles cσ​ satisfying
cστ​=cσ​σ(cτ​),
(19)
modulo cσ′​=bcσ​σ(b)−1 for b∈AutL​(B). This convention is equivalent to the usual one after replacing b by its inverse. The matrix-automorphism calculation above identifies this coefficient group with PGLn​(L).
For a central simple algebra A of degree n split by L, choose ϕ:A⊗K​L→B. Transport the natural semilinear action to Tσ​=ϕ(1⊗σ)ϕ−1 and set
cσ​=Tσ​σ0−1​.
(20)
It is L-linear and multiplicative, and Tσ​Tτ​=Tστ​ gives the cocycle identity. Replacing ϕ by bϕ changes c by the displayed coboundary equivalence. Isomorphic K-algebras give the same class, so this defines the descent classification of central simple algebras map naturally.
Conversely, a cocycle defines a semilinear algebra action Tσ​=cσ​σ0​. Let
Ac​=BT(G).
(21)
The vector-space descent theorem gives Ac​⊗K​L≅B, with the natural map also respecting multiplication and the identity. Hence dimK​Ac​=n2. If I is a nonzero two-sided ideal of Ac​, then I⊗K​L is a nonzero ideal of B, so it is all of B. Dimensions force I=Ac​, proving simplicity. If z is central in Ac​, it commutes with its L-span B, and thus is a scalar matrix with scalar a∈L. Twisted invariance of a scalar matrix means σ(a)=a, so a∈K. The center is exactly K. Thus Ac​ is central simple, of degree n, and split by L.
Equivalent cocycles have conjugate semilinear actions: Tσ′​=bTσ​b−1, so b restricts to a K-algebra isomorphism between the fixed algebras. In the other direction, an isomorphism of fixed algebras extends to an L-algebra automorphism of B and intertwines the two actions, giving exactly that equivalence relation. Starting from A recovers the fixed algebra of A⊗L, namely A; starting from a cocycle recovers its action. Therefore
CSAn​(L/K) ≅ H1(G,AutL​(Mn​(L))) = H1(G,PGLn​(L))​.
(22)
This is a bijection of pointed sets, with the split algebra corresponding to the trivial cocycle. Fixed-degree algebra classes do not carry the Brauer group's tensor-product group law, so a pointed-set interpretation is the appropriate meaning of the isomorphism here.
