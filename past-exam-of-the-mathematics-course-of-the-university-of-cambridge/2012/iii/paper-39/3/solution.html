Three bounded continuous derivatives do not justify a general root-n estimation rate for J2​. For a smooth perturbation a with compact support, the directional derivative is

For a more quantitative heuristic, impose the additional assumption f∈Hβ, use a smooth Fourier cutoff of width 1/b, and estimate the quadratic functional by its off-diagonal U-statistic. The tail bias of an estimator is O(b2(β−2)) because the Plancherel theorem weights this functional by frequency to the fourth power. The degenerate part of its standard deviation is O(n−1b−9/2): the squared L2 norm of the quadratic kernel scales as b−9. At β=3, these two terms balance at b≍n−2/13 and have size n−4/13, already slower than n−1/2; any additional first-order variance cannot improve that balance. To make both terms O(n−1/2) requires b2(β−2)≲n−1/2 and b−9/2≲n1/2, which are compatible only when β≥17/4. This is an illustrative smoothness calculation under extra Sobolev and moment assumptions, not a rate theorem asserted solely from bounded third derivatives. Particular much smoother probability density functions may of course permit root-n estimation.
