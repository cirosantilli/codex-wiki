Take N={1,2,…}. We first prove the Ellis–Numakura lemma in the one-sided continuity convention needed here. Let S be a nonempty compact Hausdorff space with an associative multiplication for which each map x↦xa is continuous. Among its nonempty closed subsemigroups, there is an inclusion-minimal one, M: a decreasing chain has nonempty intersection by compactness, and that intersection is a closed subsemigroup, so Zorn's lemma applies.
Fix a∈M. The set Ma is nonempty and compact, hence closed in the Hausdorff space, and is a subsemigroup because
(xa)(ya)=(x(ay))a∈Ma.
(12)
Minimality gives Ma=M. Thus the set T={x∈M:xa=a} is nonempty. It is closed by the stated continuity, and it is a subsemigroup, since (xy)a=x(ya)=xa=a for x,y∈T. Minimality again gives T=M, so a∈T and a2=a​, giving a semigroup idempotent. If one instead uses the opposite one-sided continuity convention, the same proof uses aM and {x:ax=a}; no joint continuity is required.
Apply this lemma to the Stone-Čech compactification of the natural numbers with its given addition. In the addition on the Stone-Čech compactification of the natural numbers convention,
A∈p+q⟺{n:A−n∈q}∈p,A−n={t∈N:n+t∈A}.
(13)
The continuous variable is p when q is fixed. The granted compactness, Hausdorff property and associativity therefore imply there exists an idempotent ultrafilter p with p+p=p.
On the positive integers such a p is nonprincipal: a principal ultrafilter at n adds to itself to give the one at 2n, which is different. Hence every cofinite set belongs to p. If zero is included in one's convention for N, use the same compact semigroup argument on the closed space of nonprincipal ultrafilters: it is nonempty by compactness of the infinite discrete set's compactification, and the displayed addition sends two free ultrafilters to a free ultrafilter. This avoids the trivial principal idempotent at zero.
To deduce Hindman's theorem, let A be the unique colour class belonging to p. Define
A∗=A∩{n:A−n∈p}.
(14)
Idempotence gives A∗∈p. The idempotent-ultrafilter star-set lemma also gives A∗−n∈p whenever n∈A∗. Here is its proof: A−n∈p, and applying idempotence to A−n gives
{t:(A−n)−t∈p}={t:A−(n+t)∈p}∈p.
(15)
Intersecting this set with A−n produces exactly A∗−n.
Choose x1​∈A∗. Suppose x1​<⋯<xr​ have been chosen with every nonempty finite sum in A∗. The finite intersection
A∗∩⋂s∈FS(x1​,…,xr​)​(A∗−s)∩{n:n>xr​}
(16)
belongs to p, and is therefore nonempty. Choose xr+1​ from it. The new sums are xr+1​ and s+xr+1​, and they all lie in A∗. Induction yields
FS(x1​,x2​,…)={i∈F∑​xi​: ∅=F⊆N finite}⊆A.​
(17)
This proves every finite colouring of the positive integers admits a monochromatic finite-sums set generated by a strictly increasing infinite sequence, the required Hindman theorem.
