The first equation has solutions e±kz; with u=kR, the second is the order-zero Bessel differential equation. Regularity at the axis selects the Bessel function of the first kind J0​(kR), rather than the singular Bessel function of the second kind Y0​. Thus
Φk​(R,z)=e±kzJ0​(kR).​

There are also zero-separation-constant modes, and other boundary conditions can select other separated families. For a localized reflection-symmetric thin disk, decay away from the disk selects e−k∣z∣ on the respective half-spaces.

The order-zero Hankel transform and its inversion now determine the Hankel representation of thin-disk gravity:
S(k)=−2πG∫0∞​Σ(R)J0​(kR)RdR.​
(24)
The sign is fixed by the positive derivative of an attractive Newtonian gravitational potential above the disk. For ordinary finite disks the representation uses the isolated boundary condition, fixing any extra vacuum field or additive constant. Infinite scale-free disks require suitable regularization of the potential itself.
Equatorial circular orbit balance is vc2​=RΦR​(R,0). Using J0′​(u)=−J1​(u) in the Hankel representation of thin-disk gravity gives

Differentiation under the integral is justified by convergence for regular models, or by first working off the disk and taking the limiting radial field.

The Mestel disk has a flat galaxy rotation curve, despite its nonspherical geometry.
Its infinite total mass prevents setting the Newtonian gravitational potential to zero at infinity. Indeed the integral for Φ diverges at k=0 because S(k)∼1/k. Subtracting the potential at a reference point removes this additive divergence. As an explicit check, the Mestel disk potential-density pair is
Φ=2πGClog(R∗​R2+z2​+∣z∣​).
(28)
It is harmonic for z=0, its vertical derivative jump is 4πGC/R, and RΦR​(R,0)=2πGC. Equivalently, an Abel regularization of an oscillatory integral with damping factor e−ak in the radial-field integral gives R∫0∞​e−akJ1​(kR)dk=1−a/a2+R2​, which tends to one as a↓0. The force is well-defined although the unreferenced potential is not.
The equality with GM(R)/R is special to the Mestel disk, not a thin-disk shell theorem. The spherical shell theorem normally makes that formula possible, but does not apply to an astrophysical disk. Exterior annuli exert outward radial gravitational acceleration inside their radii, while interior annuli do not act exactly as point masses at the origin. For this particular scale-free surface density, their combined contributions happen to produce the same result as the interior mass expression. Changing the radial surface density or truncating the disk generally destroys the equality: enclosed mass does not determine a disc rotation curve.
