For lower-boundary heating, forced convection means that mean shear supplies most of the turbulence even though buoyancy assists it, approximately z≪∣ℓO​∣. Free convection means buoyancy dominates, approximately z≫∣ℓO​∣ within the constant-flux region. In this limit the local convective speed is
wc​∼(B0​z)1/3.​
(32)
It is the velocity whose eddy transport supplies the imposed vertical turbulent buoyancy flux. The associated diffusivity is Kb​=CK​wc​z=CK​B01/3​z4/3, with a dimensionless coefficient fixed by a closure. From B0​=−Kb​N2,
N2=−CK−1​B02/3​z−4/3,∣N∣=CK−1/2​B01/3​z−2/3.​
(33)
Here N2<0: there is no real restoring buoyancy frequency. The displayed magnitude is a local overturning/growth rate, or one may write N=i∣N∣. The Reynolds analogy makes Km​=Kb​ and hence Uz​∼u∗2​B0−1/3​z−4/3; the remaining mean shear is weak compared with the convective motion.
The turnover dissipation estimate gives
ϵ∼zwc3​​∼B0​,​
(34)
also consistent with the local energy equation ϵ=P+B0​ when P is negligible. Thus free-convection dissipation is height independent to leading scaling order, in contrast with neutral u∗3​/(κz). The constants and detailed profiles are not determined by dimensional analysis. These are local free-convection surface-layer scalings, not a claim that an entire deep convecting layer has a uniform unstable mean buoyancy gradient.
