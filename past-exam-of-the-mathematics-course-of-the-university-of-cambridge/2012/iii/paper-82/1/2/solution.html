We give a quantitative construction for the whole open set, including the case of infinitely many component intervals. Pointwise divergence at a single point is insufficient here. Write z=e2πit and use normalized measure on T.
First let K be a finite union of closed arcs with ∣K∣≤3ε, where ε is small. Choose a smooth function nonnegative function u, equal to one on K, with u≤1 and η:=∫u≤6ε. This follows by making the transition regions around the finitely many arc endpoints sufficiently short. Define the analytic Herglotz integral
G(z)=2ηη+∫T​e2πis−ze2πis+z​u(s)ds​.
(3)
Its real part is (η+P[u](z))/(2η)>0, where P[u] is the disk Poisson integral, and G(0)=1. Smoothness of u makes G continuous and smooth function up to the circle; on K its boundary real part is at least 1/(2η). The branch H=logG therefore has H(0)=0 and
∣ImH∣<π/2,ReH≥log12ε1​on K.
(4)
The complex logarithm has no branch problem because G lies in the right half-plane. Its smooth function boundary values give absolutely summable Taylor coefficients. Choose an analytic polynomial A, with zero constant coefficient, uniformly within 1/4 of H on the closed disk. Put P(t)=ImA(e2πit)/π. Then ∥P∥∞​≤1/2+1/(4π)<1.
If D=degA and m>D, the modulated trigonometric polynomial Q(t)=e2πimtP(t) has spectrum in [m−D,m+D]. A sharp Fourier partial sum at m selects exactly the negative-frequency half of P, since A(0)=0:
Sm​(Q,t)=−2πie2πimtA(e2πit)​​.
(5)
Consequently, for every t∈K,
∣Sm​(Q,t)∣≥B(ε):=2πlog(1/(12ε))−1/4​,∥Q∥∞​≤1.
(6)
The carrier frequency m can be arbitrarily large. This compact-set Fourier amplification lemma uses a bounded imaginary part to obtain a large one-sided Fourier multiplier; no assertion about pointwise divergence is used.
Now let E be any open set of measure δ. Define
ψ(δ)=max{0,B(δ)/4−1}.
(7)
If this is zero take f=0. Otherwise choose a decreasing sequence δj​↓0, with δ1​=δ, so small for j≥2 that
2−jB(δj​)≥ψ(δ)+2.
(8)
For j=1 this inequality also holds: B(δ)>4 and B(δ)/2≥B(δ)/4+1. Let Lj​ be an increasing compact exhaustion of E.
Using compact batching of a small open set, we recursively choose finite closed-arc unions Kj​⊂E, of measure at most 3δj​, such that Lj​ is covered by the interiors of K1​,…,Kj​ and
∣E∖int(K1​∪⋯∪Kj​)∣≤δj+1​.
(9)
Here is the measure-theoretic justification. At step j, the uncovered portion has measure at most δj​. Its intersection with Lj​ after removing the previously covered interiors is compact, and can be covered by finitely many closed arcs lying in E with total length at most δj​ plus an arbitrarily small excess. By inner regularity of Lebesgue measure, a further compact subset of the uncovered open portion captures all but δj+1​ of its measure; cover it similarly. The combined finite arc union has length at most 3δj​. This both covers the outstanding part of Lj​ and ensures the next remainder estimate. In particular E⊂⋃j​Kj​.
Apply the finite-arc construction to Kj​ to get Pj​ of degree Dj​. Choose carriers mj​ recursively so that the bands [mj​−Dj​,mj​+Dj​] are positive and strictly ordered with no overlap. Set
f(t)=∑j=1∞​2−je2πimj​tPj​(t).
(10)
The frequency-separated Fourier block series and the Weierstrass M-test give a continuous complex-valued function with ∥f∥∞​≤∑j​2−j=1. At the cutoff mj​, all earlier bands have been included in full and every later band is absent. Thus on Kj​,
∣Smj​​(f,t)∣≥2−jB(δj​)−​∑i<j​2−ie2πimi​tPi​(t)​≥ψ(δ)+2−1>ψ(δ).
(11)
Every point of E lies in some Kj​, so
S∗(f,t)≥ψ(δ)(t∈E),ψ(δ)∼8π1​logδ1​⟶∞.​
(12)
Values of ψ outside 0<δ≤1 may be defined arbitrarily. The construction handles an arbitrary open set, not just one point or a finite collection of intervals. It also prevents cancellation explicitly: earlier bands contribute a bounded complete sum, and later bands contribute nothing at the chosen cutoff.
