The printed interval has a typo: its right endpoint must be 2/3+δ, rather than 1/3+δ. Keep ϕ as an unnormalized indicator function; only ψ is normalized to have integral one. Thus use
ϕ=1[1/3−δ,2/3+δ]​,ψ=(2δ)−11[−δ,δ]​,f=ϕ∗ψ.
(13)
With the Boolean literal printed interval, the convolution vanishes at 2/3, so a sequence consisting entirely of 2/3 is already a counterexample to the proposed majorization. For the corrected interval and every t∈[1/3,2/3], all t−s with ∣s∣≤δ belong to the support of ϕ. Hence f(t)=1 there. Everywhere 0≤f≤1, giving SN​≤∑n≤N​f(un​), including interval endpoints.

∣f​(k)∣≤min{L,1/(π∣k∣)}min{1,1/(2πδ∣k∣)}≤2π2δk21​.

This also supplies the required justification of the Fourier series. The convolution f is continuous: it is the overlap length of two moving intervals, divided by 2δ. Its Fourier coefficients are absolutely summable, so the corresponding Fourier series converges uniformly to a continuous function g. Termwise integration gives g​(k)=f​(k); the permitted uniqueness assertion applied to f−g yields g=f.

This proves the requested interval discrepancy estimate without an unjustified sharp truncation of the Fourier series.
