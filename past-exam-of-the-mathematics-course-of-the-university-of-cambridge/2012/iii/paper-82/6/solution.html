Use a Boolean circuit of unbounded-fan-in AND and OR gates, with negations pushed to the input Boolean literals. Its circuit depth is the largest number of AND/OR gates on an input-to-output path, and its circuit size M counts these gates. Constants and Boolean literals may be supplied as inputs. Equivalent standard size conventions only enlarge the absolute constants. One can merge adjacent gates of the same type; the depth-reduction argument below keeps the two possible normal forms available at each gate.
A random restriction with parameter p independently leaves each variable live with probability p and otherwise fixes it to zero or one with probability (1−p)/2 each. The Håstad switching lemma states that for a width-w DNF or conjunctive normal form F,
Pρ∼Rp​​(DTdepth(Fρ​)>s)≤(5pw)s.
(43)
Width bounds Boolean literals in a term or clause, not the number of terms. A decision tree of depth at most s has both a DNF and a conjunctive normal form of width at most s and has real multilinear degree at most s. Indeed every leaf condition is a product of at most s factors xi​ or 1−xi​; sum the accepting leaves. The switching lemma controls the canonical decision tree, hence implies this representation bound.
We detail the Boolean circuit simplification parameters. First use a 1/10-random restriction. For a bottom AND gate, let X count its live Boolean literals on the event that no fixed Boolean literal kills it. For w distinct Boolean literals,
E[5X1{not killed}​]=(9/20+5/10)w=(19/20)w≤1.
(44)
Thus the probability of retaining more than s live Boolean literals without becoming constant is at most 5−s. The same calculation applies to bottom OR gates, with the opposite killing value. Repeated Boolean literals are removed and contradictory Boolean literals make a constant gate. A union bound makes all nonconstant bottom gates width at most s, except with probability at most M0​5−s.
Now apply d−1 successive restrictions, each retaining probability 1/(10s) of the remaining variables. At a two-layer gate, the current bottom forms are width-s DNFs or conjunctive normal forms. The Håstad switching lemma bounds its probability of failing to reduce to decision-tree depth at most s by 2−s. Convert the successful decision tree into the opposite normal form, whose terms or clauses have width at most s, and merge its outer gate with the adjacent gate of the same type. This removes one layer. Repeat upwards until the output has a depth-s decision tree.
The bookkeeping matters: although a decision tree may give 2s clauses, each subsequent switching event is charged to the corresponding original Boolean circuit gate, not to each newly created clause. The lemma permits any number of terms. Each original gate is charged once, so the sum of the failure probabilities is at most M2−s, without an extra exponential size factor. Conditioning on successful preceding stages is harmless because each next restriction is fresh and the width bound holds for every preceding success. For a general Boolean circuit the conjunctive normal form or DNF representation at a shared gate can be chosen separately for each outgoing use; no extra gates need to be counted as switching targets.

This supplies the requested overview of the Linial-Mansour-Nisan theorem with the actual switching scale, gate-count bookkeeping and restriction-to-Fourier calculation. The argument uses the unbounded-fan-in AND/OR Boolean circuit basis; allowing arbitrary gates would make the theorem false, since one parity gate could have all its nonconstant Fourier weight at level n. The original theorem is Linial, Mansour and Nisan's circuit Fourier bound.
