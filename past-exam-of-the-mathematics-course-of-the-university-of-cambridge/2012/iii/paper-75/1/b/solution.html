Use the Papkovich–Neuber representation in the convention
u=Φ−21​∇(x⋅Φ+χ),p=−μ∇⋅Φ,∇2Φ=0,∇2χ=0.
(4)
It gives ∇⋅u=0 and μ∇2u=−μ∇(∇⋅Φ)=∇p. Additive pressure constants are immaterial.
Let q=x⋅Ex, with E symmetric and traceless. The harmonic vector Ex/r3 is a derivative of 1/r. The scalar q/r5 is a decaying degree-two harmonic because q is a harmonic homogeneous quadratic. These Papkovich potentials for a strained sphere have precisely the strain symmetry and decay required for the disturbance, so try
Φ=Ar3Ex​,χ=Br5q​.
(5)
Substitution gives
u′=23A​r5qx​−Br5Ex​+25B​r7qx​.
(6)
At r=a, require u′=−Ex. The independent vector structures give B=a5 and A=−5a3/3. The disturbance for a sphere in a uniform straining Stokes flow is
u′=−r5a5​Ex−2r55a3​(1−r2a2​)qx,p′=−5μa3r5q​.​
(7)
It decays at infinity and satisfies no slip exactly at the sphere. Its leading far field is a stresslet, of velocity scale Ea3/r2.
A force-free sphere in a linear background translates with U0​, by Faxén's first law, and a couple-free sphere rotates with the background rigid-body rotation Ω, by Faxén's rotational law. Uniform translation and solid-body rotation can then be matched without disturbance. The remaining relative boundary velocity is exactly −Ex. The Linearity of Stokes flow and Uniqueness of Stokes flow therefore give the same disturbance. The pure strain produces no resultant force or couple, by its symmetric degree-two angular structure.
