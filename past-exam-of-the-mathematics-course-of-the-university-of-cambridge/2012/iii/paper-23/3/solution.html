Write C=[Cop,Set] and similarly for D. The induced inverse-image functor is precomposition:
u∗:D→C,u∗(Q)=Q∘fop.
(17)
All categorical limits in a presheaf category are computed pointwise. Thus u∗ preserves every limit, in particular finite limits.
Because the indexing categories are small and Set is complete and cocomplete, both Kan extensions along fop exist. Their universal properties give
u!​=Lanfop​ ⊣ u∗=(−)∘fop ⊣ u∗​=Ranfop​.​
(18)
For example their values are the comma-category formulas
(u!​P)(d)=colim(fop↓d)​P(c),(u∗​P)(d)=lim(d↓fop)​P(c).
(19)
The comma categories here are formed in Dop: an object of the first involves f(c)→d there, equivalently d→f(c) in D. An object of the second involves d→f(c) there, equivalently f(c)→d in D. Keeping the opposite category explicit prevents reversal of the two constructions.
The right-hand adjunction and finite-limit preservation are exactly the axioms for a geometric morphism u:C→D. The extra left adjoint makes it an essential geometric morphism. On representable presheaves, its left adjoint satisfies u!​(yC​c)≅yD​(fc), by the Yoneda lemma and the left adjunction. This identifies the induced morphism directly with the original functor f.
