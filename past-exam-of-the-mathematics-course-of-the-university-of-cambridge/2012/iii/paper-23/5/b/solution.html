For the covariant category in this part, put E=[C,Set] and rc​=C(c,−). Its global-sections functor is Γ=Hom(1,−), the limit of the diagram. If Γ is also an inverse image, it is a left adjoint and preserves all small colimits.
Consider the canonical pointwise-surjective map
q:∐c∈C​rc​⟶1.
(29)
At d, the summand rd​(d) contains 1d​, so q is an epimorphism. In a presheaf topos epimorphisms are regular, and a colimit-preserving functor preserves their coequalizer presentations. Thus Γq is surjective. Since Γ preserves coproducts, some summand has a global element, yielding a section s:1→rc​ of its unique map rc​→1. The constant singleton functor is a retract of a covariant representable.
Conversely, if 1 is such a retract in a category, Hom(1,−) is a retract of Hom(rc​,−), which is evaluation at c by the Yoneda lemma. Evaluation preserves colimits pointwise. The colimit comparison for a retract functor is a retract of the evaluation comparison; a retract of an isomorphism is an isomorphism, so Γ preserves all small colimits. It also preserves finite limits because it is representable. A right adjoint can be constructed by
R(S)(d)=Set(Γrd​,S).
(30)
For h:d→d′, precomposition gives rd′​→rd​, and applying Γ and then Set(−,S) gives R(S)(d)→R(S)(d′). The presentation of every functor as a colimit of representables gives Hom(F,R(S))≅Set(ΓF,S). Hence Γ⊣R, so Γ is an inverse image and the topos is local.
The retract condition has a precise interpretation as the initial object criterion for a covariant local topos. Write the section as a natural family ud​:c→d. Naturality says hud​=ud′​ for every h:d→d′. Put e=uc​. Then e2=e and every f:c→d satisfies fe=ud​. In the idempotent completion of C, the object (c,e) is initial: an arrow from it to (d,k) must satisfy v=kve, and the identities force v=ud​; this arrow exists because kud​=ud​ and ud​e=ud​.
Conversely an initial object (c,e) in the idempotent completion gives that natural family and the retract. Therefore
[C,Set] is local ⟺ Kar(C) has an initial object.​
(31)
If C is already idempotent-complete, this is equivalent to an initial object in C itself, and Γ is evaluation there. The word is initial, not terminal, because this question uses covariant functors.
One cannot omit idempotent completion for a general small category. Take the one-object category of the monoid {1,0} with absorbing zero. It has no initial object because its endomorphism set has two elements, but the natural family u=0 satisfies m0=0 for every m. Its covariant functor category is local. Concretely, a functor is a set with an idempotent operator, and its global sections are the fixed points of that operator.
For a topological space X, there is an equally explicit criterion. If Sh(X) is local, apply Γ to the epimorphism ∐i​Ui​→1 associated with any open cover X=⋃i​Ui​, regarding the opens as subterminal sheaves. Since ΓUi​ is a singleton exactly when Ui​=X, and otherwise empty, coproduct and epimorphism preservation force some Ui​=X.
Thus every open cover of X contains X itself. Conversely this open cover criterion for a local sheaf topos condition is equivalent to existence of a point x whose only open neighborhood is X: the union of all proper open sets cannot be all of X, so choose x outside it. Such a point plainly forces any cover to contain X. Empty spaces fail the condition.
At this point, the stalk functor F↦Fx​ is simply F(X)=ΓF, since there is only one open neighborhood. Its right adjoint is the skyscraper sheaf of sets
R(S)(V)={S,{∗},​x∈V,x∈/V.​
(32)
A morphism F→R(S) is determined exactly by its map F(X)→S, because all proper opens omit x. Hence Γ⊣R, and Γ preserves finite limits, proving locality. Consequently
Sh(X) is local ⟺ ∃x∈X with no proper open neighborhood.​
(33)
In a T0​ space this distinguished point is unique and closed. In a T1​ space the condition forces X to be a singleton. Indistinguishable points in a non-T0​ space can all have this property; uniqueness of an actual point is not part of the general criterion.
