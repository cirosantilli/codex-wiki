For the covariant version of the Yoneda embedding, put Y(A)=C(A,−). A morphism u:A→B induces the natural transformation Y(u):Y(B)→Y(A) whose component sends v:B→X to vu. Thus Y is a functor from the opposite category Cop to the functor category [C,Set]. The locally small category hypothesis makes each value a set.
The Yoneda lemma says that, for a functor F:C→Set, evaluation at the identity morphism gives a natural bijection
Nat(C(A,−),F)≅F(A),θ⟼θA​(1A​).​
(1)
Given a∈F(A), define θXa​(v)=F(v)(a). For w:X→X′, the functor law gives F(w)θXa​(v)=F(wv)(a)=θX′a​(wv), proving naturality. Conversely, naturality of θ at v:A→X gives θX​(v)=F(v)θA​(1A​). These two constructions are inverse, because F(1A​)(a)=a.
The bijection is natural in both variables. A natural transformation β:F→H sends the element a to βA​(a), matching postcomposition by β. For u:A→B, precomposing with Y(u) sends θ to a transformation from Y(B) and its distinguished element is θB​(u)=F(u)(a). This is exactly the required dependence on A.
Taking F=Y(B) gives Nat(Y(A),Y(B))≅C(B,A). Consequently the covariant Yoneda embedding is full and faithful, with the reversal of arrows accounted for by its opposite category domain.

Suppose every component αA​ is a monomorphism in the Category of sets, hence an injective function. If β,γ:H→F are natural transformations with αβ=αγ, then αA​βA​=αA​γA​ at every object. Componentwise cancellation gives βA​=γA​, so β=γ. Thus α is a monomorphism in the functor category.
Conversely, suppose α is a monomorphism and x,y∈F(A) satisfy αA​(x)=αA​(y). The Yoneda lemma supplies natural transformations θx,θy:Y(A)→F. Its naturality in F identifies αθx and αθy with the equal elements αA​(x) and αA​(y). Cancellation by α gives θx=θy; evaluating at 1A​ gives x=y. Therefore a natural transformation is a monomorphism exactly when all its components are injective functions. This is the pointwise monomorphism in a set-valued functor category criterion.

An object of the slice category C/A is a morphism x:X→A; a map from x to y:Y→A is u:X→Y with yu=x. The identity morphism 1A​:A→A is a terminal object of this slice category, even when C itself has no terminal object.

This common map equips P with its slice structure. An underlying mediating map into P is automatically over A, because its composite with xp is the prescribed structure map. A monomorphism m:A→B satisfies mu=mv⇒u=v for every parallel pair into A. An epimorphism e:X→Y satisfies ue=ve⇒u=v for every parallel pair out of Y. A strong monomorphism is a monomorphism with the following lifting property: whenever e:X→Y is an epimorphism and mu=ve, with u:X→A and v:Y→B, there is a unique d:Y→A with de=u and md=v. A regular monomorphism is a morphism occurring as the equalizer of two parallel maps. A balanced category is one in which every morphism that is both a monomorphism and an epimorphism is an isomorphism.
Suppose m:A→B equalizes p,q:B⇉D. If mu=mv, the common composite has two factorizations through the equalizer, so its uniqueness property gives u=v. Hence every regular monomorphism is indeed a monomorphism.
Now take the lifting square just described. The equations pmu=qmu imply pve=qve. Cancel the epimorphism e to obtain pv=qv. The equalizer gives a unique d with md=v. Since mde=ve=mu and m is a monomorphism, de=u. Thus every regular monomorphism is a strong monomorphism; the existence of the lift uses epic cancellation, and its uniqueness uses the equalizer property.

(3)

The limit form of the Special adjoint functor theorem is as follows. Let C be a locally small category, a complete category, and a well-powered category, and suppose it has a small cogenerating family (Qi​)i∈I​. Let D be a locally small category. Then a functor G:C→D has a left adjoint if and only if it preserves all small categorical limits. The dual exchanges completeness, well-poweredness and cogenerators for cocompleteness, well-copoweredness and generators, and characterizes functors with a right adjoint.
Here a cogenerating set distinguishes unequal parallel maps by postcomposition into one of the Qi​. A well-powered category has a set of subobjects of each fixed object. Completeness means existence of all small categorical limits. We use the initial-object lemma for complete categories with a weakly initial set: a complete category that is locally small has an initial object exactly when it has a weakly initial set. A proof is given in Question 5(c). We also use the fact that when G preserves small categorical limits, the comma category (B↓G) is complete, with its limits constructed in C; local smallness is inherited from C. Finally, having an initial object in (B↓G) for each B is the universal arrow from an object to a functor characterization of a left adjoint.
Assume G preserves small categorical limits, and fix B∈D. We will construct a weakly initial set in (B↓G), rather than assume a solution set. Start with an arbitrary object (C,x), where x:B→GC. Call a subobject m:S↪C supporting if x=G(m)xS​ for some xS​:B→GS. There is at least one, namely 1C​. Because G preserves pullbacks in a category, it preserves monomorphisms: the self-pullback characterization of a monic map is preserved. Consequently each xS​ is unique.
There is a set of supporting subobjects by well-poweredness. Their intersection of subobjects exists by completeness; write it m0​:C0​↪C. Applying G to this intersection categorical limit gives a unique x0​:B→GC0​ lifting all the xS​, and hence x=G(m0​)x0​. Minimality says that any subobject n:R↪C0​ supporting x0​ is invertible: m0​n supports x, so m0​ factors through m0​n, giving a right inverse to the monic n.
If u,v:C0​→Qi​ satisfy G(u)x0​=G(v)x0​, their equalizer supports x0​, since G preserves that equalizer. It is therefore an isomorphism, and u=v. We have obtained an injective function
C(C0​,Qi​)⟶D(B,GQi​),u⟼G(u)x0​.
(4)
Let Ji​ be its image, a subset of the fixed set Si​=D(B,GQi​). The evaluation embedding into cogenerator products is a monomorphism
C0​↪PJ​:=∏i∈I​∏s∈Ji​​Qi​.
(5)
Indeed each s∈Ji​ corresponds to a unique map C0​→Qi​, and the cogenerating set property makes the resulting family jointly monic. Notice that we use the realized subsets Ji​, not all of Si​: there need not be a map C0​→Qi​ available for an unused index.
There is a set of tuples J=(Ji​), since I and every Si​ are sets. For each resulting PJ​, choose representatives of its subobjects; these form a set by well-poweredness. For every representative R, include every map y:B→GR, a set by local smallness of D. All resulting pairs (R,y) form a set. Our given (C,x) receives a map from such a pair: transport x0​ along the isomorphism between C0​ and the chosen representative, then compose its inverse with m0​. Thus these pairs form a weakly initial set in (B↓G). This is the cogenerator bound for comma-category solution sets.
The initial-object lemma for complete categories with a weakly initial set gives an initial object (LB,ηB​). For h:B→B′, define Lh by the unique equation
G(Lh)ηB​=ηB′​h.
(6)
Uniqueness proves the functor laws and gives the natural bijections
C(LB,C)≅D(B,GC),f⟼G(f)ηB​.​
(7)
Thus L⊣G. Conversely, a right adjoint preserves small categorical limits: its adjunction identifies maps into the proposed limiting object with compatible families of maps into the diagram. This proves both directions of the limit form of the special adjoint functor theorem. Applying the argument to the opposite categories proves the dual statement. Choices of representatives and adjoint objects are understood in the usual ambient-universe convention for large categories.

The comma category (X↓G) has objects (A,x) with A∈C and a function x:X→G(A). Its morphisms (A,x)→(B,y) are maps f:A→B in C satisfying G(f)x=y. The identity morphism and composition in a category are inherited from C; the functor laws make the defining equations stable under both.
For a singleton 1, its map 1→G(A) is just an element a∈G(A). Thus (1↓G) is the covariant category of elements of G, with arrows f:(A,a)→(B,b) satisfying G(f)(a)=b.

Conversely, if (R,r) is an initial object, then

A weakly initial set is a set of objects (Wi​)i∈I​ such that every object receives at least one map from some Wi​. An initial object is itself a singleton weakly initial set. We prove the converse without replacing uniqueness by weak initiality.
Let E be a complete category that is locally small. Form W=∏i​Wi​. For every object A, choose Wi​→A and compose with the projection W→Wi​, so W is weakly initial. Completeness supplies a terminal object, so the given weakly initial set cannot be empty.
The endomorphisms of W form a set. Take a simultaneous equalizer e:I→W of all of them with 1W​; equivalently, equalize the two maps from W into ∏s∈E(W,W)​W with components s and 1W​. Thus se=e for every endomorphism s. The object I is weakly initial, since Ie​W→A exists for every A. Weak initiality of W gives a map u:W→I. Applying the equalizing identity to eu yields eue=e, so monicity of e gives ue=1I​.
For any endomorphism v:I→I, the map evu is an endomorphism of W. Therefore evue=e. Since ue=1I​, this becomes ev=e, giving v=1I​. Now let f,g:I⇉A and form their equalizer j:E→I. Weak initiality of I gives h:I→E. The composite jh is an endomorphism of I, hence the identity. Consequently f=fjh=gjh=g.
There is at least one map from I to every object, and the argument proves that there is at most one. The object I is an initial object. This establishes the initial-object lemma for complete categories with a weakly initial set using only small products in a category and equalizers.

A semi-additive category has commutative-monoid enrichment: each hom-set is a commutative monoid, and composition in a category preserves addition and zero in each variable. In the finite-biproduct convention, which we use here, it also has finite products in a category and coproducts in a category. A preadditive category has abelian groups as its hom-sets, with composition additive in each variable; this definition alone does not require finite products in a category or a zero object.
Let P=∏i=1n​Ai​ with projections pi​. Define ij​:Aj​→P by pi​ij​=1Aj​​ for i=j and pi​ij​=0 otherwise. Bilinearity gives
pk​(∑j​ij​pj​)=pk​,
(17)
so product uniqueness gives ∑j​ij​pj​=1P​. For a family fj​:Aj​→X, set f=∑j​fj​pj​. Then fij​=fj​. If h:P→X has the same restrictions, h=h∑j​ij​pj​=∑j​fj​pj​=f. Thus the product in a category is also the coproduct in a category, with its canonical injections. Conversely, the analogous construction from a finite coproduct in a category gives projections and the same identities, proving that it is a product in a category.
For the empty family, a terminal object T has a singleton endomorphism monoid, so 1T​=0. Every map T→X equals its composite with 1T​=0, hence equals the zero map; this map exists by enrichment. Therefore T is also an initial object, a zero object. The dual argument applies to an initial object. Consequently finite products in a category and coproducts in a category coincide canonically as biproducts, including the nullary case.

(24)
The functoriality of abelian image factorization supplies their vertical outer maps. We show that both are isomorphisms.
First, L≅cokerd1​ and L′≅cokerd1′​: exactness at A2​ identifies imd1​ with kerd2​, and the image-coimage isomorphism for d2​ gives the claimed cokernel. Write q:A2​→L and q′:B2​→L′ for these categorical cokernels. To construct an inverse to the induced map ℓ:L→L′, observe
(qv2−1​d1′​)v1​=qv2−1​v2​d1​=0.
(25)
Cancellation of the epimorphism v1​ gives qv2−1​d1′​=0, so qv2−1​ factors uniquely as ℓˉq′ with ℓˉ:L′→L. From ℓq=q′v2​, composing with the epic q,q′ gives ℓˉℓ=1L​ and ℓℓˉ=1L′​.
Second, R≅kerd4​ and R′≅kerd4′​ by exactness at the fourth objects. Write their inclusions as j:R→A4​ and j′:R′→B4​. The equality d4′​v4​=v5​d4​ gives the induced map ρ:R→R′. Since
v5​d4​v4−1​j′=d4′​j′=0
(26)
and v5​ is a monomorphism, d4​v4−1​j′=0. Kernel universality gives ρˉ​:R′→R with jρˉ​=v4−1​j′. Cancelling the monic j,j′ proves ρˉ​ρ=1R​ and ρρˉ​=1R′​.
Apply the short five lemma to the two short exact sequences: their outer maps ℓ,ρ are invertible, so v3​ is an isomorphism. This five lemma via image factorization argument uses only universal properties and therefore works in any abelian category, without treating its objects as literal sets of elements.
