 ... Past exam of the mathematics course of the University of Cambridge 2012 iii Paper 78 4 a
OurBigBook.com Words: 150
Use the inner product convention linear in its first argument. A singular value system consists of positive singular values σn​ and orthonormal bases un​ of (kerA)⊥⊂X and vn​ of ranA⊂Y, with
Aun​=σn​vn​,A∗vn​=σn​un​.
(45)
For an infinite-rank compact operator, σn​→0; a finite-rank operator has a finite system. Applying the spectral theorem for compact self-adjoint operators to A∗A constructs un​, with eigenvalues σn2​, and then vn​=Aun​/σn​. This solution uses the input/output naming of this problem, which is the reverse of another common singular-vector convention.
The Moore–Penrose inverse of an operator is
x†=A†y=n∑​σn​⟨y,vn​⟩​un​.​
(46)
Its domain is ranA⊕(ranA)⊥, equivalently the data satisfying the Picard criterion
∑n​σn2​∣⟨y,vn​⟩∣2​<∞.
(47)
It ignores the component in kerA∗ and lies in (kerA)⊥. The series then converges in X by orthonormality and is the minimum-norm least-squares solution. If y∈ranA, it is an exact solution; all other exact solutions are x†+z with z∈kerA. Compactness does not make the inverse bounded when there are infinitely many positive singular values.
