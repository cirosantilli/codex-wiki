Let M×X have the diagonal right monoid action (g,x)m=(gm,xm), and put E=HomM​(M×X,Y). Define the right action on this set of equivariant maps of monoid sets by
(ek)(g,x)=e(kg,x).
(1)
It really stays in E: (ek)(gm,xm)=e(kgm,xm)=e(kg,x)m. Also e1=e and ((ek)l)(g,x)=e(klg,x)=(e(kl))(g,x), so it satisfies the right-action law. Left multiplication in the first argument is intentional; no commutativity of M has been assumed.
Define the evaluation map of an exponential object
ev:E×X→Y,ev(e,x)=e(1,x).
(2)
It is equivariant, because
ev(ek,xk)=e(k,xk)=e((1,x)k)=e(1,x)k.
(3)
For any right M-set Z and equivariant map h:Z×X→Y, define its currying by
h(z)(g,x)=h(zg,x).
(4)
For fixed z, this is equivariant in the diagonal variables: h(zgm,xm)=h(zg,x)m. The map z↦h(z) is also equivariant, since
h(zk)(g,x)=h(zkg,x)=(h(z)k)(g,x).
(5)
It satisfies ev(h(z),x)=h(z,x).
Conversely, given an equivariant u:Z→E, set h(z,x)=u(z)(1,x). Evaluation makes this equivariant, and currying recovers u:
h(z)(g,x)=u(zg)(1,x)=(u(z)g)(1,x)=u(z)(g,x).
(6)
These constructions are inverse and natural in Z. Therefore they establish the exponential object universal property
HomM​(Z,E)≅HomM​(Z×X,Y),YX=E.​
(7)
This is the exponential of right monoid actions, not the set of ordinary maps X→Y with an arbitrarily guessed action.

When M=G is a group, evaluation at its identity gives a bijection from the previous equivariant-map set to all functions X→Y. For an arbitrary function f, the inverse construction is
ef​(g,x)=f(xg−1)g.
(8)
Indeed,
ef​(gk,xk)=f(xk(gk)−1)gk=f(xg−1)gk=ef​(g,x)k,
(9)
so ef​ is an equivariant map. Conversely, if e is equivariant, (g,x)=(1,xg−1)g implies e(g,x)=e(1,xg−1)g, so e=ef​ for f(x)=e(1,x).
Transport the right action from part (a) through this bijection. At x its value is
(ek)(1,x)=e(k,x)=f(xk−1)k.
(10)
Thus the exponential of right group actions is
YX=Set(X,Y),(fk)(x)=f(xk−1)k.​
(11)
For clarity, the right-action law holds even in a nonabelian group:
((fk)l)(x)=f(xl−1k−1)kl=f(x(kl)−1)kl=(f(kl))(x).
(12)
Evaluation is equivariant because (fk)(xk)=f(x)k. The underlying functions need not be equivariant; the fixed points of this exponential action are exactly the equivariant functions.

At a point x∈X, all restrictions of the constant presheaf of sets are identities. Two representatives (U,t) and (V,t′) give the same germ precisely when t=t′, so its stalk is canonically T. The disjoint union of the stalks is therefore the set X×T.
The topology of the associated étale space of a presheaf has basic opens obtained from sections over open U. For the constant section t, that basic open is U×{t}. These are exactly the basic opens of the product topology with T discrete. Consequently the germ-space identification is a homeomorphism over X, and the projection
p:X×Tdisc​⟶X​
(13)
is a local homeomorphism.
A continuous section over V has the form x↦(x,f(x)), where f:V→Tdisc​ is continuous, equivalently locally constant. Its sections form the constant sheaf of sets:
ΔT(V)={f:V→T:f is locally constant}.​
(14)
The sheaf gluing axiom follows by gluing the functions; continuity is local. This includes ΔT(∅) being a singleton, even when the constant presheaf's value on the empty open set was T. The construction is its sheafification.
A function u:T→T′ acts by postcomposition on locally constant functions, defining a functor Δ:Set→Sh(X). It preserves identities and composition.
To prove the adjunction with the global sections functor, let F be a sheaf. A sheaf morphism λ:ΔT→F gives a function
T⟶F(X),t⟼λX​(constant function t).
(15)
Conversely, suppose global sections st​∈F(X) are prescribed for every t∈T. For a locally constant f:V→T, its fibres Vt​=f−1(t) are disjoint open sets covering V. Restrict st​ to Vt​ and glue these sections. They agree on intersections, which are empty, so unique gluing defines λV​(f). Restriction to smaller opens commutes with this construction, making λ a sheaf morphism.
Applying the two constructions successively returns the original data: a locally constant function is locally one of the constant functions, and sheaf morphisms respect those restrictions and unique gluing. Naturality in T and F follows from postcomposition and restriction. Hence
HomSh(X)​(ΔT,F)≅Set(T,ΓF),Δ⊣Γ.​
(16)
For an empty space the same proof works: all global sections sets are singletons, and the sheaf category is degenerate. Constant sheaves are generally locally constant rather than globally constant on disconnected opens.

Write C=[Cop,Set] and similarly for D. The induced inverse-image functor is precomposition:
u∗:D→C,u∗(Q)=Q∘fop.
(17)
All categorical limits in a presheaf category are computed pointwise. Thus u∗ preserves every limit, in particular finite limits.
Because the indexing categories are small and Set is complete and cocomplete, both Kan extensions along fop exist. Their universal properties give
u!​=Lanfop​ ⊣ u∗=(−)∘fop ⊣ u∗​=Ranfop​.​
(18)
For example their values are the comma-category formulas
(u!​P)(d)=colim(fop↓d)​P(c),(u∗​P)(d)=lim(d↓fop)​P(c).
(19)
The comma categories here are formed in Dop: an object of the first involves f(c)→d there, equivalently d→f(c) in D. An object of the second involves d→f(c) there, equivalently f(c)→d in D. Keeping the opposite category explicit prevents reversal of the two constructions.
The right-hand adjunction and finite-limit preservation are exactly the axioms for a geometric morphism u:C→D. The extra left adjoint makes it an essential geometric morphism. On representable presheaves, its left adjoint satisfies u!​(yC​c)≅yD​(fc), by the Yoneda lemma and the left adjunction. This identifies the induced morphism directly with the original functor f.

Present the Grothendieck topos as E=Sh(C,J) for a small site. Let a:C→E be sheafification and i the inclusion. The standard sheafification construction supplies a⊣i, with a preserving finite limits. Concretely, matching-family constructions commute with finite limits, and their directed refinement over covering sieves does so as well. Limits of sheaves are computed in the presheaf category, so E has finite limits; its colimits are sheafifications of presheaf colimits.
For sheaves A,B, form the presheaf exponential
E(c)=Nat(yc×A,B).
(20)
It is a sheaf. Indeed, for a covering sieve S↪yc, one has aS≅a(yc). Exponential adjunction, sheafification adjunction and left exactness give
Nat(S,E)≅Nat(S×A,B)≅HomE​(aS×A,B)≅HomE​(a(yc)×A,B)≅Nat(yc,E).
(21)
These are the restriction comparisons, proving the sheaf condition. Restricting the presheaf exponential adjunction to sheaves now makes E=BA in E.
The subobject classifier is the sheaf ΩJ​ of J-closed sieves. A sieve S on c is J-closed when, for every f:d→c, f∗S∈J(d) implies f∈S. Pullback of sieves defines its restrictions, and the maximal sieve defines truth. Local sieve data glue by taking the J-closure of their compatible generated sieve; uniqueness follows because membership in a J-closed sieve is local. This proves that ΩJ​ is a sheaf.
For a subsheaf A↪B, the characteristic morphism sends b∈B(c) to
χA​(b)={f:d→c:B(f)(b)∈A(d)}.
(22)
This sieve is J-closed because local membership in a subsheaf descends by its gluing axiom. Pulling truth back recovers exactly A. Conversely, pulling back truth along any morphism into ΩJ​ gives a subsheaf, and the same formula recovers that morphism. Finite limits, exponentials and this classifier make every Grothendieck topos an elementary topos.
Here are the explicit Heyting operations on subsheaves of an object B. Intersections give meets:
(A∧D)(c)=A(c)∩D(c),(⋀i​Ai​)(c)=⋂i​Ai​(c).
(23)
The top is B. Arbitrary joins are local unions:
b∈(i⋁​Ai​)(c) ⟺ {f:d→c:B(f)b∈Ai​(d) for some i}∈J(c).​
(24)
Equivalently, b locally lies in one of the Ai​, with the index allowed to vary across the covering arrows. This is the J-closure of the pointwise union, or its sheafification. It is the smallest subsheaf containing every Ai​, since any such subsheaf must contain sections locally in it.
The bottom is the empty join, namely the initial subobject. Its value at c consists of all b∈B(c) if the empty sieve covers c, and is empty otherwise. This detail matters on sites with empty covers; it is not safe to use an always-empty presheaf as the initial sheaf.
Implication is described without any pointwise-complement assumption:
(A⇒D)(c)={b∈B(c): for every f:d→c, B(f)b∈A(d)⟹B(f)b∈D(d)}.​
(25)
Restrictions preserve this condition. It is also local: pull a covering sieve back along an arbitrary f, use membership in A on those restrictions, then descend their membership in D. Thus it is a subsheaf. It satisfies the defining Heyting algebra adjunction
C≤(A⇒D)⟺C∩A≤D.
(26)
For the forward direction take the identity restriction. For the reverse direction every restriction of a section of C remains in C, so membership in A forces membership in D. Negation is ¬A=A⇒0. These formulas give the complete Heyting algebra structure on SubE​(B); in general negation is a pseudocomplement rather than a set-theoretic complement.

In a Grothendieck topos define
ΔS=∐s∈S​1,ΓA=HomE​(1,A).
(27)
The coproduct universal property gives HomE​(ΔS,A)≅Set(S,ΓA), naturally. Thus Δ⊣Γ. On any sheaf presentation, Δ is the composite of the constant-presheaf functor with sheafification. The former preserves finite limits pointwise, and the latter is left exact, so Δ preserves finite limits. Therefore these functors define a geometric morphism γ:E→Set.
For uniqueness, let v be any such geometric morphism. Its inverse image is a left adjoint and preserves the terminal object. Every set has the canonical coproduct decomposition S=∐s∈S​{∗}, so
v∗S≅∐s∈S​v∗{∗}≅∐s∈S​1=ΔS.
(28)
These isomorphisms respect all functions between sets, giving a natural isomorphism v∗≅Δ. The right adjoint is then unique up to the corresponding natural isomorphism, so v∗​≅Γ. There is a unique geometric morphism to sets, up to isomorphism, called the global sections geometric morphism. This also applies to the degenerate topos.

For the covariant category in this part, put E=[C,Set] and rc​=C(c,−). Its global-sections functor is Γ=Hom(1,−), the limit of the diagram. If Γ is also an inverse image, it is a left adjoint and preserves all small colimits.
Consider the canonical pointwise-surjective map
q:∐c∈C​rc​⟶1.
(29)
At d, the summand rd​(d) contains 1d​, so q is an epimorphism. In a presheaf topos epimorphisms are regular, and a colimit-preserving functor preserves their coequalizer presentations. Thus Γq is surjective. Since Γ preserves coproducts, some summand has a global element, yielding a section s:1→rc​ of its unique map rc​→1. The constant singleton functor is a retract of a covariant representable.
Conversely, if 1 is such a retract in a category, Hom(1,−) is a retract of Hom(rc​,−), which is evaluation at c by the Yoneda lemma. Evaluation preserves colimits pointwise. The colimit comparison for a retract functor is a retract of the evaluation comparison; a retract of an isomorphism is an isomorphism, so Γ preserves all small colimits. It also preserves finite limits because it is representable. A right adjoint can be constructed by
R(S)(d)=Set(Γrd​,S).
(30)
For h:d→d′, precomposition gives rd′​→rd​, and applying Γ and then Set(−,S) gives R(S)(d)→R(S)(d′). The presentation of every functor as a colimit of representables gives Hom(F,R(S))≅Set(ΓF,S). Hence Γ⊣R, so Γ is an inverse image and the topos is local.
The retract condition has a precise interpretation as the initial object criterion for a covariant local topos. Write the section as a natural family ud​:c→d. Naturality says hud​=ud′​ for every h:d→d′. Put e=uc​. Then e2=e and every f:c→d satisfies fe=ud​. In the idempotent completion of C, the object (c,e) is initial: an arrow from it to (d,k) must satisfy v=kve, and the identities force v=ud​; this arrow exists because kud​=ud​ and ud​e=ud​.
Conversely an initial object (c,e) in the idempotent completion gives that natural family and the retract. Therefore
[C,Set] is local ⟺ Kar(C) has an initial object.​
(31)
If C is already idempotent-complete, this is equivalent to an initial object in C itself, and Γ is evaluation there. The word is initial, not terminal, because this question uses covariant functors.
One cannot omit idempotent completion for a general small category. Take the one-object category of the monoid {1,0} with absorbing zero. It has no initial object because its endomorphism set has two elements, but the natural family u=0 satisfies m0=0 for every m. Its covariant functor category is local. Concretely, a functor is a set with an idempotent operator, and its global sections are the fixed points of that operator.
For a topological space X, there is an equally explicit criterion. If Sh(X) is local, apply Γ to the epimorphism ∐i​Ui​→1 associated with any open cover X=⋃i​Ui​, regarding the opens as subterminal sheaves. Since ΓUi​ is a singleton exactly when Ui​=X, and otherwise empty, coproduct and epimorphism preservation force some Ui​=X.
Thus every open cover of X contains X itself. Conversely this open cover criterion for a local sheaf topos condition is equivalent to existence of a point x whose only open neighborhood is X: the union of all proper open sets cannot be all of X, so choose x outside it. Such a point plainly forces any cover to contain X. Empty spaces fail the condition.
At this point, the stalk functor F↦Fx​ is simply F(X)=ΓF, since there is only one open neighborhood. Its right adjoint is the skyscraper sheaf of sets
R(S)(V)={S,{∗},​x∈V,x∈/V.​
(32)
A morphism F→R(S) is determined exactly by its map F(X)→S, because all proper opens omit x. Hence Γ⊣R, and Γ preserves finite limits, proving locality. Consequently
Sh(X) is local ⟺ ∃x∈X with no proper open neighborhood.​
(33)
In a T0​ space this distinguished point is unique and closed. In a T1​ space the condition forces X to be a singleton. Indistinguishable points in a non-T0​ space can all have this property; uniqueness of an actual point is not part of the general criterion.

A geometric formula is built from atomic formulas, including equality, using finite conjunctions, arbitrary set-indexed disjunctions and existential quantification in a finite variable context. Truth is the empty conjunction and falsity the empty disjunction. A geometric theory is a set of sequents ϕ⊢x​ψ between such formulas over a many-sorted signature. The context supplies the universal force of an axiom; unrestricted universal quantification, implication and negation are not formula constructors in this fragment.
These choices are exactly suited to inverse image functors of geometric morphisms. Finite-limit preservation handles equality and conjunction; colimit and image preservation handle disjunction and existential quantification. Therefore an inverse image carries an internal model of a geometric theory to another model.
Construct the geometric syntactic category CT​ as follows. Objects are formulas in context {x.ϕ}, modulo provable renaming and equivalence. A morphism to {y​.ψ} is an equivalence class of formulas θ(x,y​) that are provably total and single-valued:
θ⊢ϕ∧ψ,ϕ⊢x​∃y​θ,θ(x,y​)∧θ(x,y​′)⊢x,y​,y​′​y​=y​′.
(34)
The last notation abbreviates componentwise equality. Identity is equality of the context variables; composition is existential conjunction over the intermediate tuple. The category has finite limits, formed through conjunction and equality.
Give it the geometric syntactic topology JT​. A family of arrows represented by θi​(y​i​,x) into {x.ϕ} covers when
T⊢x​ϕ ⟹ ⋁i​∃y​i​θi​(y​i​,x).
(35)
Pullback stability is substitution, and transitivity comes from distributing existential conjunction through the covering disjunctions. Thus the generated sieves define a Grothendieck topology. Empty covers impose the interpretation of falsity as the initial object.
The classifying topos is
Set[T]=Sh(CT​,JT​).​
(36)
Its universal model UT​ assigns a sort A the sheafified representable of {xA.⊤}, functions their definable graph morphisms and relations their definable subobjects. The syntactic covering conditions make the axioms valid in this model.
For the universal property, interpreting formulas in a model M in a Grothendieck topos F gives a finite-limit-preserving, JT​-continuous functor CT​→F: covers go to jointly epimorphic families precisely because the relevant sequents hold. The Diaconescu equivalence for geometric morphisms associates to this functor a geometric morphism F→Set[T]. Conversely, pulling UT​ back along a geometric morphism gives a model. The constructions on models and their homomorphisms are inverse up to natural isomorphism, yielding
Geom(F,Set[T])≃T-Mod(F)​
(37)
naturally in F. This proves the required classifying property, not merely classification of set-valued models.

Yes, with the Horn/Cartesian-fragment convention. A Horn theory uses positive finite-conjunction sequents and has a Cartesian syntactic category CTcart​ with finite limits. Provably uniquely witnessed existential formulas can also be admitted without changing the finite-limit nature of its semantics. Its internal models are precisely finite-limit-preserving functors from that category.
For a category with finite limits, flat functors into a Grothendieck topos are exactly finite-limit-preserving functors. In sets, the reason is concrete: the category of elements of a covariant left-exact functor is cofiltered. Its terminal object supplies nonemptiness, products supply cones over pairs of elements, and equalizers supply equalizing cones over parallel arrows. Conversely a cofiltered category of elements expresses the functor as a filtered colimit of covariant representables; filtered colimits of sets commute with finite limits. The internal version uses the same cone conditions locally.
The presheaf form of the Diaconescu equivalence for geometric morphisms therefore identifies geometric morphisms into
[(CTcart​)op,Set]​
(38)
with internal Horn models, naturally in the domain topos. This is the presheaf classifier of a Horn theory.
Equivalently, using a small skeleton of the category A of finitely presented set-based Horn models, the classifier is [A,Set], with covariant functors. The duality A≃(CTcart​)op sends a formula to the model generated by its tuple subject to its finite constraints. The presheaf claim does not extend merely because a general geometric or regular theory has no written disjunctions: arbitrary existential witnesses are not uniquely witnessed Horn data.

Morita equivalence of geometric theories means equivalence of their classifying toposes. Equivalently, their categories of models in every Grothendieck topos are equivalent pseudonaturally with respect to inverse image. Agreement only of their set-based model categories, without this natural internal-model structure, is not the definition.
Suppose Set[T]≃Set[S]=E. A property expressed intrinsically in terms of the topos is the same under either presentation. One can therefore translate a site or logical characterization of that property from T into one for S. Examples include Booleanity, connectedness, atomicity and the structure of the subtopos lattice. The bridge is the common invariant E, rather than an assumed literal identification of the two signatures.
The duality between geometric quotients and subtoposes makes this precise. A geometric quotient theory T′ of T adds geometric axioms over the same signature. Quotients are identified when they prove the same geometric sequents. The theorem gives
{geometric quotients of T}/provable equivalence ⟷ {subtoposes of Set[T]}.​
(39)
The subtopos corresponding to T′ is its classifying topos. Stronger axioms correspond to smaller subtoposes under inclusion.
The site mechanism explains the correspondence. On CT​, an additional sequent requires the associated family of definable images to cover its antecedent. Adding these covering sieves produces a topology J′⊇JT​ and a geometric embedding
Sh(CT​,J′)↪Sh(CT​,JT​).
(40)
Conversely, subtoposes correspond to such larger topologies; requiring their extra definable covers gives the corresponding deductively closed quotient theory. Pulling the universal model into the subtopos supplies the universal model of the quotient.
Given a quotient T′, transport its subtopos along the chosen equivalence of classifying toposes. Apply the duality again to obtain a quotient S′ of S. Their classifying toposes are equivalent, so
T′ and S′ are Morita-equivalent.​
(41)
This is an explicit transfer principle for whole families of theory extensions and their order relations. Intrinsic constructions such as open, closed or Boolean subtoposes can likewise be described in each syntax. Different descriptions may look unrelated, but their equality is explained by the common subtopos. No universal translation of individual formulas is asserted without choosing the relevant equivalence and interpretations.
