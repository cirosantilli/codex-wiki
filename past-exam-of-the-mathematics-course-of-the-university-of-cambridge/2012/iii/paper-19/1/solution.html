Choose a generator [Sn] of Hn​(Sn;Z)≅Z. The mapping degree of a continuous map is the integer determined by
f∗​[Sn]=(degf)[Sn].​
(1)
For n≥1 this is ordinary top-dimensional homology; using reduced homology also gives a convention for n=0. Domain and target use the same orientation, so reversing the chosen generator in both leaves the integer unchanged. Homotopy invariance of homology makes the mapping degree invariant under homotopy.
We interpret the manifold criterion for a suspension in the category of nonempty topological manifolds without boundary, and “sphere” as homeomorphic to the standard sphere. Let X have dimension d. If X≅Sd, an explicit homeomorphism from its suspension to Sd+1 is
[x,t]⟼(sin(πt)x,cos(πt)).
(2)
It is a continuous map that is a bijection: the two collapsed ends become the poles, and every other point determines t and x uniquely. The compact-to-Hausdorff continuous bijection theorem makes it a homeomorphism.
Conversely suppose SX is a topological manifold. Its open part X×(0,1) shows its dimension is d+1. At a suspension vertex v there is an open cone neighbourhood U on X. It is contractible, while U∖{v} admits a deformation retraction onto X. The long exact sequence in relative homology and excision give the local homology from a link calculation
Hi​(SX,SX∖{v};Z)≅Hi−1​(X;Z).
(3)
A point of a (d+1)-dimensional topological manifold without boundary has local homology Z in degree d+1 and zero in all other degrees. Consequently
Hj​(X;Z)≅{Z,0,​j=d,j=d.​
(4)
Thus X is an integral homology sphere. This conclusion alone does not identify its homeomorphism type.
If d≥2, it is also simply connected. Choose a cone neighbourhood U of the vertex and an open coordinate ball B around the vertex inside U. By compactness of X, sufficiently short entire cone neighbourhoods fit inside any neighbourhood of v: the inverse image of that neighbourhood contains X×{0}, and a finite subcover supplies one uniform collar length. Choose such a shorter cone V⊂B. The inclusions of punctured neighbourhoods factor as
V∖{v}⟶B∖{v}⟶U∖{v}.
(5)
The composite induces an isomorphism of fundamental groups, since both cone punctures are products of X with intervals and their inclusion is a homotopy equivalence. The middle punctured ball has the homotopy type of Sd and is simply connected for d≥2. Hence the composite is also zero on the fundamental group, forcing π1​(X)=0. This uses the nested neighbourhoods, not an unsupported claim that an arbitrary punctured neighbourhood is a sphere.
The Hurewicz theorem, applied successively to the vanishing lower homology groups, now gives πj​(X)=0 for j<d and πd​(X)≅Hd​(X)≅Z. A representative of its generator is a continuous map Sd→X inducing an isomorphism in every integral homology degree. Topological manifolds have the homotopy type of CW complexes, so the homological Whitehead theorem makes it a homotopy equivalence. We have proved that X is a homotopy sphere.
At this point the topological classification theorem is essential: the topological generalized Poincare theorem says that a closed homotopy sphere is homeomorphic to the standard sphere. Applying it completes the converse for d≥2. For d=1, the local calculation makes X a connected compact one-dimensional manifold without boundary, hence a circle. For d=0, a compact zero-dimensional manifold is a finite set, and H0​(X)≅Z forces exactly two points. Thus it is S0. Altogether,
SX is a topological manifold without boundary⟺X≅Sd.​
(6)
The last classification step is not a consequence of homology alone, and we make no assertion of diffeomorphism with a prescribed smooth structure. If the word manifold allows boundary on both sides, the printed assertion needs this correction: the suspension of the interval is a closed disk. For instance [x,t]↦((2x−1)(1−∣2t−1∣),2t−1) identifies it with the filled diamond ∣u∣+∣v∣≤1, although the interval is not a sphere.
To construct sphere maps of arbitrary integer degree, first use hm​:S1→S1, hm​(z)=zm for any integer m, including the constant map when m=0. Lifting its argument to the real line gives θ↦mθ, so the fundamental one-cycle is sent to m times itself. Therefore deghm​=m.
Two cone neighbourhoods cover a suspension; their intersection admits a deformation retraction onto the original space. The reduced Mayer–Vietoris theorem gives a natural suspension isomorphism
Hq+1​(SX)≅Hq​(X).
(7)
Naturality means that the suspension of a continuous map induces the same integer multiplier. This proves degree under suspension. Using the standard identification S(Sk)≅Sk+1, set
fn,m​=Sn−1hm​:Sn→Sn,degfn,m​=m(n≥1).​
(8)
Thus every integer occurs in every positive sphere dimension, with negative degrees handled just as well as positive ones.
If “every n” includes zero, that final printed assertion is false. The generator of H0​(S0) is the difference of its two points. A map of a two-point set is the identity, the transposition, or one of two constant maps, giving precisely
degf∈{−1,0,1}on S0.​
(9)
These degrees of maps of the zero-sphere are the explicit exception to the arbitrary-degree construction.
