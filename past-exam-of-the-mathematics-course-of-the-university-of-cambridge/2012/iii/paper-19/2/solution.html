Use integral homology throughout. The singular chain group Ck​(X) is free on the continuous maps from the standard k-simplex to X. Since the boundary operator preserves chains lying in A, these form a chain subcomplex. The relative chain complex and its relative homology are
Ck​(X,A)=Ck​(X)/Ck​(A),Hk​(X,A)=ker(∂:Ck​(X,A)→Ck−1​(X,A))/im(∂:Ck+1​(X,A)→Ck​(X,A)).​
(10)
The short exact sequence of chain complexes gives the long exact sequence in relative homology.
A sufficient hypothesis for the quotient comparison is a nonempty good pair: A is closed and has an open neighbourhood U which admits a deformation retraction onto A while fixing A throughout. A CW pair is another standard sufficient setting. These hypotheses hold for a simple closed curve in the surface here, using its annular collar neighbourhood. The induced quotient map q:X→Y=X/A collapses A to a point a.
Here is the proof of the collapsing a pair theorem. Put V=U/A⊂Y. The deformation retraction makes H∗​(U,A)=0, so the long exact sequence of the triple gives H∗​(X,A)≅H∗​(X,U). It also contracts V onto a, giving H∗​(Y,{a})≅H∗​(Y,V). Because A is closed inside the open neighbourhood U, excision gives
H∗​(X,U)≅H∗​(X∖A,U∖A).
(11)
Similarly, excision of the closed point a in the open set V gives
H∗​(Y,V)≅H∗​(Y∖{a},V∖{a}).
(12)
The quotient map restricts to a homeomorphism between the two punctured pairs, so these are the same groups and the identifications commute with q∗​. Finally H∗​(Y,{a})≅H∗​(Y), including degree zero. Thus
q∗​:Hk​(X,A)∼​Hk​(X/A).​
(13)
The neighbourhood condition is part of the result; the quotient assertion is not made for arbitrary bad pairs.
For the genus-two closed orientable surface with its chosen orientation, H0​(X)=Z, H1​(X)=Z4, H2​(X)=Z, with higher groups zero. The curve has H0​(A)=H1​(A)=Z. Its relevant long exact sequence in relative homology is
0⟶Z⟶H2​(X,A)⟶Zi∗​​Z4⟶H1​(X,A)⟶Z∼​Z⟶0.
(14)
The final map is an isomorphism because both spaces are connected. Consequently H1​(X,A)=cokeri∗​ and H0​(X,A)=0; all relative groups above degree two vanish. The quotient topological space is connected, so its unreduced H0​ is Z.
In the separating case, the curve is the oriented boundary of one of the two subsurfaces. Its homology class in X is therefore zero, so i∗​=0. The exact sequence becomes
0→Z→H2​(X,A)→Z→0,H1​(X,A)=Z4.
(15)
The first sequence splits since its quotient is a free abelian group. Hence the homology after collapsing a separating surface curve is
Hk​(X/A;Z)≅⎩⎨⎧​Z,Z4,Z2,0,​k=0,k=1,k=2,k≥3.​​
(16)
Geometrically, collapsing the boundary of each once-punctured torus fills its puncture with a cone on the circle, which is a disk. The quotient is a wedge sum of two tori at the collapsed point. Their two independent fundamental classes explain the extra second-homology generator.
In the nonseparating case, join the two new boundary components of the cut surface by an arc. Upon regluing, this supplies a closed curve meeting A once transversely. The signed intersection pairing on an oriented surface therefore provides an integer homomorphism H1​(X)→Z taking [A] to ±1. Thus [A] is a nonzero primitive homology class, and i∗​:Z→Z4 is an injection onto a direct summand. Its kernel is zero and its cokernel is Z3. The same exact sequence gives H2​(X,A)≅H2​(X)≅Z. Therefore the homology after collapsing a nonseparating surface curve is
Hk​(X/A;Z)≅⎩⎨⎧​Z,Z3,0,​k=0,2,k=1,k≥3.​​
(17)
Another description starts from the genus-one surface with two boundary circles. Collapse those two circles separately to obtain a closed torus with two marked points, then identify the two points. Identifying two distinct points in a connected CW complex adds a loop up to homotopy, so this quotient has the homotopy type of T2∨S1. The preceding relative homology calculation proves the groups without requiring that homotopy description.
