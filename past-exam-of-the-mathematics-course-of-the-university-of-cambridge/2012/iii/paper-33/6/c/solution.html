For the simple symmetric random walk, the increments have mean 0 and variance 1. Use the polygonal interpolation from (b). The maximum functional is Lipschitz in the supremum norm, since
∣max0≤s≤1​g(s)−max0≤s≤1​h(s)∣≤∥g−h∥∞​.
(46)
Each interpolating segment is linear, so its maximum occurs at a grid endpoint. The continuous mapping theorem applied to the Donsker invariance principle gives
n​1​max0≤j≤n​Sj​ ⇒ max0≤s≤1​Bs​.
(47)
The limiting maximum has no atom at a>0 by the Brownian reflection principle, so the closed-tail probabilities converge at this threshold. Including j=0 does not change the event because a>0. Therefore the random-walk maximum limit from Donsker invariance is
n→∞lim​P(j≤nmax​Sj​≥an​)=2(1−Φ(a)),a>0.​
(48)
Here Φ is the standard normal distribution function. The normalization and the positive-threshold condition are those in the PDF; the TeX aid's t​ is not the printed expression.
