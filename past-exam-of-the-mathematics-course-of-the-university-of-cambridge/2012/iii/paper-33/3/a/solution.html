On a measurable space (E,E) with a sigma-finite measure μ, a Poisson random measure N is a random counting measure such that N(A) has the Poisson distribution with parameter μ(A) for every measurable A of finite intensity, and the counts on finitely many pairwise disjoint measurable sets are independent. The sample measures are countably additive; repeated atoms are allowed if the intensity has atoms.
When μ(A)=∞, exhaustion by finite-intensity subsets gives N(A)=∞ almost surely. Indeed their counts increase, and the Poisson distribution probability of a count at most any fixed integer tends to zero as the parameter tends to infinity. Independent Poisson counts on disjoint sets, with means prescribed by μ, characterize the Poisson random measure.
