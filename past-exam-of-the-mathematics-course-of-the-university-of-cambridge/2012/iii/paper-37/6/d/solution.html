Keep baseline covariates fixed and put q=1−π, μj​=eβ0​+βTxi​+αj​, and r=1/τ for τ>0. Integration of a Poisson count over the Gamma component gives
gj​(y)=Γ(r)y!Γ(r+y)​(r+μj​r​)r(r+μj​μj​​)y,y=0,1,….
(30)
This negative binomial distribution has mean μj​ and variance μj​+τμj2​. Therefore the unconditional one-term distribution is the zero-inflated negative binomial distribution
P(Yij​=y)=π1{y=0}​+qgj​(y).​
(31)
In particular its zero probability is π+q(1+τμj​)−1/τ, not simply π. As τ↓0 this becomes a Zero-inflated Poisson distribution.
The mixing effect has Ebi​=q, Ebi2​=q(1+τ) and Var(bi​)=q(τ+π). The law of total variance and law of total covariance now give
EYij​Var(Yij​)Cov(Yij​,Yik​)​=qμj​,=qμj​+q(τ+π)μj2​,=q(τ+π)μj​μk​,j=k.​​
(32)
Equivalently, for μ=(μ1​,μ2​,μ3​)T, EYi​=qμ and Cov(Yi​)=diag(qμ)+q(τ+π)μμT. This also supplies raw second moments by adding (EYi​)(EYi​)T. Dependence arises from both shared heterogeneity and shared structural zeros, even when the positive random effect is constant.
The printed moment variance matches the hierarchical variance at the same parameter values, but its printed off-diagonal covariance is τμj​μk​. Equality would require τ=q(τ+π), or π(τ−q)=0. It is therefore false to claim that the two approaches consistently estimate all the same parameters in general.
The precise distinction is between consistency of mean ratios and identification of structural parameters. In the hierarchical model, with mj​=qμj​, both the quadratic variance coefficient and the covariance coefficient in terms of the marginal mean are
κ=qτ+π​,Var(Yj​)=mj​+κmj2​,Cov(Yj​,Yk​)=κmj​mk​.
(33)
For the moment parameterization, writing its parameters as πQ​,τQ​,qQ​=1−πQ​, these coefficients are AQ​=(πQ​+τQ​)/qQ​ and BQ​=τQ​/qQ2​. Its intercept is identifiable from the mean only as γ0Q​=β0Q​+logqQ​. Matching the true first two moments demands AQ​=BQ​=κ. One solution always is
πQ​=0,τQ​=κ,β0Q​=β0​+logq.
(34)
For κ>1 there is also a solution qQ​=1/κ, πQ​=1−1/κ, τQ​=1/κ, with the corresponding shifted intercept. Indeed eliminating τQ​ gives πQ​(τQ​−qQ​)=0. This is moment aliasing in a shared zero-inflated count model: matching the moments does not identify the actual structural-zero probability.
For a concrete counterexample take π=0.2,τ=0.3. Then q=0.8 and κ=0.625. The only admissible moment-matching choice is πQ​=0,τQ​=0.625, with an intercept shifted by log0.8; it cannot recover the true structural-zero proportion 0.2. Correct marginal means can give consistent term and baseline-covariate slopes with sandwich standard errors; they do not justify consistent estimation of the intended π,τ, or latent intercept. Nor does a mean-only estimating equation identify π at all. Full-distribution inference provides information beyond these first two moments.
