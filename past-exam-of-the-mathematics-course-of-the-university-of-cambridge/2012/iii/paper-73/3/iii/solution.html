A large spherical region contains N∼VR​/ℓ3 energy-containing eddies. If their hydrodynamic impulses have zero mean, finite variance and sufficiently short-range correlations, the mean-square sum grows as N, rather than N2. In the ideal independent case,
⟨​∑a=1N​Ia​​2⟩=N⟨∣Ia​∣2⟩.
(41)
The spherical impulse-momentum relation makes the integrated velocity proportional to this sum for isolated enclosed structures. In a full field, external dipolar velocities and boundary effects can change the coefficient; they do not alter the volume scaling under these random-impulse statistics. Thus
⟨∣PR​∣2⟩∝VR​​,∣PR​∣rms​∝VR1/2​.
(42)
It is a random walk law for momentum fluctuations, not an extensive mean flow. Although the mean impulse is zero by isotropy, its variance need not vanish. The Saffman integral measures this large-volume momentum-fluctuation density. If every eddy had exactly zero impulse or correlations enforced systematic cancellation, this leading density could vanish and a different small-wavenumber spectrum could result.
