In the inertial frame the steady convective acceleration is −RΩ2eR​. For a barotropic fluid, ∇p/ρ=∇h, where h is the barotropic enthalpy function. The equilibrium momentum equation therefore gives
∇(h+Φ−21​Ω2R2)=0,H=h+Φ−21​Ω2R2=constant​
(28)
on each connected fluid region. Uniform rotation is essential: it makes the centrifugal acceleration derivable from the centrifugal potential −Ω2R2/2.
The Cowling approximation sets the gravitational-potential perturbation to zero while retaining background gravity. The barotropic pressure force linearizes as δ(∇p/ρ)=∇δh, with δh=p′/ρ=cs2​ρ′/ρ. Denote its mode amplitude by W. The advective time derivative on a scalar mode is ∂t​+Ω∂ϕ​, so the stated positive-frequency exponential produces iσ with σ=ω+mΩ. Linearizing the radial centrifugal term produces −2Ωvϕ​, while linearizing the azimuthal convective term produces 2ΩvR​. Thus
iσvR​−2Ωvϕ​=−WR​,iσvϕ​+2ΩvR​=−imW/R,iσvz​=−Wz​.
(29)
These are the pressure-gradient and Coriolis acceleration terms in the corotating-frame form. Independently, linearizing continuity equation gives
iσcs2​ρW​=−R1​∂R​(RρvR​)−Rimρvϕ​​−∂z​(ρvz​).​
(30)
The equilibrium mass density is axisymmetric, so no additional azimuthal background-density derivative appears.
For σ=0 and D=4Ω2−σ2=0, invert the horizontal momentum system:
vR​=−Di​(σWR​+R2mΩW​),vϕ​=D1​(2ΩWR​+RmσW​),vz​=σi​Wz​.
(31)
Inserting these into the continuity equation, dividing by iσ and multiplying by D gives
Dcs2​ρW​=R1​∂R​(RρWR​)−R2m2ρW​−σ2D​∂z​(ρWz​)+Rσ2mΩ​ρR​W.
(32)
The two terms proportional to ρWR​ cancel; this cancellation leaves precisely the background-density derivative in the last term. Since −D/σ2=1−4Ω2/σ2, this is exactly
Dcs2​ρW​=R1​∂R​(RρWR​)−R2m2ρW​+(1−σ24Ω2​)∂z​(ρWz​)+Rσ2mΩ​ρR​W.​
(33)
The singular frequency cases require the original velocity equations rather than this inversion.
In the low-frequency anelastic approximation for a rotating barotropic star, neglect the left side. Spherical background mass density satisfies ρR​/R=ρz​/z, understood by its smooth limiting form on the equatorial plane. For W=zRm,
R1​∂R​(RρWR​)−R2m2ρW​=mzRm−1ρR​,∂z​(ρWz​)=Rmρz​.
(34)
The remaining equation reduces to
Rmρz​[m+1+σ2mΩ​−σ24Ω2​]=0.
(35)
Writing q=2Ω/σ, the bracket factors as (m+1−q)(q+1). The regular desired branch is therefore
σ=m+12Ω​,W=zRm.​
(36)
The other algebraic factor corresponds to σ=−2Ω, where the eliminated horizontal system is singular; it is not classified by this pressure-equation inversion. A constant-density background makes the bulk factor identically zero but does not invalidate the displayed solution.
For the regular branch, an explicit velocity check is particularly informative. Up to a common mode normalization,
(vR​,vϕ​,vz​)=2Ωm+1​(−izRm−1, zRm−1, iRm).
(37)
It satisfies RvR​+zvz​=0 and ∇⋅v=0. Thus ∇⋅(ρv)=0 for every spherical mass density profile, directly verifying the anelastic continuity condition. The motion is tangential to spherical shells and is the sectoral inertial mode of a slowly rotating barotropic star. For single-valued azimuthal modes, m is a positive integer. With the source's exponential convention,
ω=σ−mΩ=−m+1(m−1)(m+2)​Ω.
(38)
For m≥2 the pattern is retrograde relative to the star but prograde in the inertial frame. This is a leading slow-rotation anelastic mode, not an exact solution of the compressible equations whose left-hand term was discarded.
