Write u=ux​ and B=Bz​. In this geometry the Lorentz force density is purely longitudinal and equals −∂x​(B2/2μ0​)ex​. The ideal magnetohydrodynamic induction equation becomes Bt​+∂x​(uB)=0. Together with the continuity equation and adiabatic pressure evolution, the four primitive-variable equations are
ρt​+uρx​+ρux​=0,pt​+upx​+γpux​=0,ut​+uux​+ρ−1px​+μ0​ρB​Bx​=0,Bt​+uBx​+Bux​=0.
(1)
They have the required quasilinear form Ut​+AUx​=0 with
A=​u000​0u1/ρ0​ργpuB​00B/(μ0​ρ)u​​.
(2)
Here the notation ux​ in derivatives means ∂x​u, whereas u denotes the longitudinal velocity. Spatial dependence is one-dimensional; time dependence is allowed in the wave problem.
Put cf2​=γp/ρ+B2/(μ0​ρ), and assume ρ>0 and cf​>0. Expansion of the characteristic polynomial gives
det(A−λI)=(u−λ)2[(u−λ)2−cf2​],λ0​=u twice,λ±​=u±cf​.​
(3)
Two independent eigenvectors for λ0​ can be chosen as (1,0,0,0)T and (0,−B/μ0​,0,1)T. For the two propagating branches, useful eigenvectors are
r±​=(1,ργp​,ρ±cf​​,ρB​)T.
(4)
The repeated branch advects an entropy disturbance and a disturbance with unchanged total gas-plus-magnetic pressure. The ± branches are perpendicular fast magnetosonic waves, with speed cf​ relative to the fluid.
On a uniform background, a Fourier amplitude Uei(kx−ωt) obeys (kA−ωI)U=0. Thus the eigenvalues are the phase velocities ω/k for k=0, and the four dispersion branches are ω=ku twice and ω=k(u±cf​). The reduced geometry retains neither an independent transverse velocity nor an additional propagating Alfvén wave.
To construct each finite-amplitude simple wave in magnetohydrodynamics, trace a state-space curve with tangent r±​, using ρ as its parameter. Integrating the pressure and magnetic components gives
p=Kργ,B=bρ,dρdu​=±ρcf​(ρ)​,cf2​=γKργ−1+μ0​b2ρ​.
(5)
The constants K and b are the entropy and transverse magnetic flux freezing invariants along this state curve. Hence
u(ρ)=u∗​±∫ρ∗​ρ​rcf​(r)​dr.
(6)
Since AUρ​=λ±​Uρ​, substituting this curve into the original system reduces it to ρt​+λ±​(ρ)ρx​=0. By the chain rule,
∂t​λ±​+λ±​∂x​λ±​=0.​
(7)
Thus the characteristic speed itself satisfies the Inviscid Burgers equation. For initial speed L(a), its smooth solution is λ±​=L(a) along x=a+tL(a), until the characteristic curves intersect. This derivation supplies genuine state curves for simple waves, not merely a linear dispersion relation.
