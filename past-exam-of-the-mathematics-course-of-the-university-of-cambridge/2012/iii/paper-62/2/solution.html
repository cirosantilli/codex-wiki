For a barotropic fluid, dp/dr=cs2​dρ/dr. Dividing continuity equation by r2ρu gives ρ′/ρ=−2/r−u′/u. Eliminating this derivative yields
uu2−cs2​​drdu​=r2cs2​​−Φ′(r).​

At a smooth transonic critical point, the coefficient of du/dr vanishes. A finite derivative then requires the right side to vanish simultaneously:
uc​=−cs,c​,Φ′(rc​)=rc​2cs,c2​​.​
(10)
These are the sonic and regularity conditions. Otherwise the derivative is singular and the proposed smooth transonic passage fails. The local slope must also be a real root compatible with the desired branch. For example, defining C=ρd(cs2​)/dρ at the point and differentiating both sides gives
(2+cs2​C​)(uc′​)2+rc​uc​4C​uc′​+rc2​4C+2cs2​​+Φ′′(rc​)=0.

This explains why simultaneous vanishing is a necessary regularity condition, rather than an automatic proof that every potential admits a critical crossing.
Define the barotropic enthalpy function by dh=dp/ρ. The momentum equation integrates to the Bernoulli equation
21​u2+h(ρ)+Φ=B.​
(12)
Changing the reference mass density in h changes only the constant B. For an isothermal closure this logarithmic function is a barotropic barotropic pressure potential; it should not be confused with the constant thermodynamic specific enthalpy of an ideal gas held at fixed temperature.
For isothermal transonic accretion in the Paczyński-Wiita potential, assume M>0 and cs​>0, and define rB​=GM/(2cs2​) and β=16cs2​/c2. The critical equation is
(rc​−rG​)2=rB​rc​,
(13)
whose two roots are rG​+21​rB​(1±1+β​). Only the plus root lies outside rG​. Therefore
rc​=rG​+4cs2​GM​(1+1+β​)=4cs2​GM​(1+β/2+1+β​)>rG​.​
(14)
For this isothermal case C=0, and the slope relation reduces to
(uc′​)2=(rc​−rG​)3GM​−rc2​cs2​​=rc2​(rc​−rG​)cs2​(rc​+rG​)​>0.
(15)
The positive slope is the inward-accretion solution connecting a small inward speed at large radius to the supersonic inward branch. Thus the exterior critical point is a genuine nondegenerate transonic point.
Choose the reference mass density as ρ0​, so h=cs2​log(ρ/ρ0​) and B=0 from the conditions at infinity. At uc​=−cs​,
ρc​=ρ0​exp[cs2​(rc​−rG​)GM​−21​]=ρ0​exp[1+1+β​4​−21​].
(16)
Substitution into M˙=4πrc2​ρc​cs​ gives
M˙=4cs3​πρ0​(GM)2​(1+β/2+1+β​)2exp[1+1+β​4​−21​].​

This is the rate selected by the smooth transonic accretion solution. Arbitrary static or subsonic formal solutions are not assigned this rate merely by specifying conditions at infinity. As rG​/rB​→0, the result tends to the isothermal Bondi accretion rate πe3/2ρ0​(GM)2/cs3​, a useful normalization check.
