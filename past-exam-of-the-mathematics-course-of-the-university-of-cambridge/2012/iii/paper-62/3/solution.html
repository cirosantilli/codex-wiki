Thus the steady continuity equation is satisfied.
The cross product in the ideal magnetohydrodynamic induction equation simplifies to
u×B=veϕ​×B=Rv​∇ψ,∇×(u×B)=∇(v/R)×∇ψ=Reϕ​B⋅∇(v/R).

Hence steady induction requires B⋅∇(v/R)=0. On a regular connected poloidal flux surface, this integrates locally to
Rv​=ω(ψ).​
(20)
This is the field-line angular velocity of an axisymmetric wind. It need not equal the gas angular velocity when Bϕ​=0, because uϕ​=Rω+kBϕ​/ρ. The regular-surface qualification matters: if ψ is constant and the field is purely toroidal, induction alone cannot turn an arbitrary v(R,z)/R into a function of ψ.
Put F=(∇×B)×B and E=∣u∣2/2+Φ. Dot the pressure-free momentum equation with u. Since B⋅F=0, axisymmetry gives
ρkB​⋅∇E=μ0​ρRω​Fϕ​.
(21)
The azimuthal momentum equation in cylindrical coordinates similarly gives
uR​∂R​(Ruϕ​)+uz​∂z​(Ruϕ​)=μ0​ρR​Fϕ​,orρkB​⋅∇(Ruϕ​)=μ0​ρR​Fϕ​.
(22)
Subtracting ω times the second relation from the first, and using B⋅∇ω=0, proves that E−ωRuϕ​ is constant along a flowing regular poloidal flux surface. For nonzero mass loading its corotating energy invariant of an axisymmetric magnetic wind is
21​(uR2​+uz2​+(uϕ​−Rω)2)+Φ−21​R2ω2=ϵJ​(ψ).​
(23)
This is the invariant labeled ϵ in the question. It is a corotating mechanical energy, distinct from the total magnetohydrodynamic Bernoulli invariant, which includes electromagnetic energy flux. In standard notation it equals total specific energy minus ω times the total specific angular-momentum invariant.
Near the footpoint, negligible kBϕ​/ρ gives uϕ​≃Rω, while the poloidal velocity is parallel to the poloidal magnetic field. The gas approximately corotates and slides along a rotating field line as a bead on a wire, with effective potential
Φeff​=Φ−21​ω2R2.
(24)
A decreasing effective potential converts corotating potential energy into poloidal kinetic energy. This is the mechanism of magnetocentrifugal acceleration; local launching does not alone guarantee global escape.
For an attractive point mass, take Φ=−GM/(R2+z2)1/2 and ω2=GM/R03​. Parameterize the outward straight line by R=R0​+ssinα, z=scosα. The first derivative of Φeff​ vanishes at s=0 because the footpoint is in circular radial balance. The meridional Hessian there is ω2diag(−3,1), so
ds2d2Φeff​​​0​=ω2(cos2α−3sin2α)=ω2(1−4sin2α).
(25)
Thus the thirty-degree magnetocentrifugal launching criterion in its strict negative-curvature form is
α>π/6,0≤α≤π/2.​
(26)
Above this angle the equilibrium is unstable to an outward displacement; below it the near-footpoint potential initially rises. The source's proportionality constant must be negative to describe attraction.
The marginal straight-line launch at thirty degrees has zero quadratic curvature. For precisely the straight line and point-mass potential here, let x=s/R0​ and a=sinα. Direct expansion gives
GM/R0​Φeff​(s)−Φeff​(0)​=(21​−2a2)x2+(25​a3−23​a)x3+O(x4).
(27)
At α=π/6, this is −7x3/16+O(x4), so the outward direction is downhill at cubic order. Consequently the strict inequality is the nondegenerate quadratic criterion; it should not be interpreted as excluding every one-sided nonlinear marginal launch at equality. An exactly resting bead remains an equilibrium until displaced.
