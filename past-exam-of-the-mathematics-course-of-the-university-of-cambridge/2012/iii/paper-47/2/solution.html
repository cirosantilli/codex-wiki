The four collective parameters. Translation, a Lorentz boost of rapidity b, and the global phase symmetry produce
ψ(x,t)=λc​cosh(cξ)eiχ+isη​,ξ=coshb(x−X0​)−sinhbt,η=coshbt−sinhb(x−X0​).​

The independent real parameters are α,b,X0​,χ, with χ periodic modulo 2π. A further time translation is absorbed into these parameters. The energy and momentum are Mcoshb and Msinhb, while Q is invariant. The internal angle is the collective coordinate whose canonical momentum is this Noether charge.

Q=±n,n=1,2,…,∣α∣=2π​−4λ2n​,Mn​=λ24M​sin4λ2n​.​

On the nonsingular branch 0<n<2π/λ2. The elementary charge-one mass approaches M at weak coupling. For positive a,b with (a+b)λ2/4≤π/2, sina′+sinb′>sin(a′+b′), where a′=aλ2/4, b′=bλ2/4. Thus Ma+b​<Ma​+Mb​: the charge-n soliton cannot fragment into smaller like-charge particles. For a general charge-conserving partition, a constituent with ∣qi​∣≥n already has mass at least Mn​; if every ∣qi​∣<n, the decreasing ratio Mq​/q gives ∑i​M∣qi​∣​≥(Mn​/n)∑i​∣qi​∣≥Mn​, strictly for nontrivial fragmentation. Thus opposite-charge constituents cannot open a lower-energy decay either. This is charged rather than topological stability, and the semiclassical spectrum is reliable at weak coupling.
Counting states and the endpoint. Write L=2π/λ2. If L is not an integer, the regular branch gives
Q=±1,…,±⌊L⌋,number of charge states=2⌊L⌋.​
(15)
If L is an integer, the local nonsingular calculation gives only 2(L−1) regular states. The formal closed-branch list has 2L charge labels, but its last two labels are limits of the same singular static solution. Counting them needs a global completion; it cannot be decided from a regular current integral at α=0.
In the additional integer-level completion, λ2=4π/k at leading semiclassical order and charges are identified modulo integer k>1. There are then k−1 nonzero charge sectors. For odd k, positive and negative labels are distinct; for even k, the endpoint labels ±k/2 describe one self-conjugate state. This supplies 2L−1 states when k=2L, rather than 2L endpoint labels. This global interpretation, and the distinction between classical and renormalized couplings, are discussed in Dorey and Hollowood, section 2. A generic classical λ does not automatically define an integer k or specify this quantum completion.
