We prove the Dirichlet density form of the Chebotarev density theorem. Let L/K be a finite Galois extension with Galois group G, let C be a conjugacy class in G, and let SC​ consist of the unramified prime ideals of K whose Frobenius conjugacy class is C. The assertion is
δ(SC​)=s↓1lim​log(1/(s−1))p∈SC​∑​(Np)−s​=∣G∣∣C∣​.​
(20)
The same result holds for any union of conjugacy classes, by addition. The following proof uses exactly the allowed one-dimensional Artin L-function behavior at 1 and the permitted induction and Euler-product properties.
First determine the order of vanishing at 1 for an arbitrary character of a representation χ of G. The Brauer induction theorem supplies subgroups Hj​≤G, linear characters ψj​ of Hj​, and integers nj​, possibly negative, such that
χ=∑j​nj​IndHj​G​ψj​.
(21)
Put Kj​=LHj​. Compatibility of Artin L-functions with induced representations and direct sums gives, initially for Res>1,
LK​(χ,s)=∏j​LKj​​(ψj​,s)nj​.
(22)
Each ψj​ is a one-dimensional Galois representation for L/Kj​, so the stated analytic assumptions apply to every factor. A trivial ψj​ contributes a simple pole; a nontrivial ψj​ is holomorphic and nonzero at one. Thus the product is meromorphic near one and
ords=1​LK​(χ,s)=−∑j​nj​⟨ψj​,1Hj​​⟩Hj​​.
(23)
The character inner product is ⟨α,β⟩G​=∣G∣−1∑g∈G​α(g)β(g)​. Applying Frobenius reciprocity to each induced term gives
ords=1​LK​(χ,s)=−j∑​nj​⟨IndHj​G​ψj​,1G​⟩G​=−⟨χ,1G​⟩G​.​
(24)
In particular, a nontrivial irreducible character has order zero, so its Artin L-function is holomorphic and nonzero near one. For the trivial character, LK​(1G​,s)=ζK​(s), the Dedekind zeta function of the base field K, and its pole is simple. This argument establishes the behavior needed at one, not holomorphy everywhere for arbitrary Artin L-functions.
Next convert that order calculation into a prime sum. For an irreducible character χ, choose a unitary representation of dimension dχ​ affording it, and choose a Frobenius automorphism gp​ at each unramified prime. The logarithm specified by the absolutely convergent Euler product gives, for real s>1,
logLK​(χ,s)=∑p unramified​∑m≥1​m(Np)msχ(gpm​)​+Rχ​(s).
(25)
Only finitely many ramified primes occur. Their Euler-factor logarithms Rχ​(s) stay bounded near one, since the relevant eigenvalues have modulus one and (Np)−1<1. Also ∣χ(g)∣≤dχ​, so the contribution with m≥2 is uniformly bounded for s≥1 by
∑p​∑m≥2​m(Np)msdχ​​≤2dχ​∑p​(Np)−2<∞.
(26)
Convergence follows, for example, from the convergent ideal series defining ζK​(2). Therefore
∑p unramified​(Np)sχ(gp​)​=logLK​(χ,s)+O(1).
(27)
Writing aχ​=⟨χ,1G​⟩G​, the order computation means LK​(χ,s)=(s−1)−aχ​Uχ​(s), where Uχ​ is holomorphic and nonzero near one. Choose its local logarithm. Along the real interval approaching one, this and the Euler-product logarithm differ only by a fixed multiple of 2πi. Consequently
p unramified∑​(Np)sχ(gp​)​=aχ​logs−11​+O(1).​
(28)
Only the trivial irreducible character has aχ​=1; all the others have aχ​=0.
Finally, character orthogonality makes the irreducible characters an orthonormal basis of class functions. For c∈C, the coefficient of χ in the class indicator is ⟨1C​,χ⟩G​=(∣C∣/∣G∣)χ(c)​. Hence
1C​(g)=∣G∣∣C∣​∑χ∈Irr(G)​χ(c)​χ(g).
(29)
Sum this identity over the unramified primes with weight (Np)−s. The finite character sum and the boxed estimates give
p∈SC​∑​(Np)−s=∣G∣∣C∣​logs−11​+O(1).​
(30)
Dividing by log(1/(s−1)) proves the asserted Dirichlet density. The same Euler-product calculation with ζK​ gives ∑p​(Np)−s=log(1/(s−1))+O(1), so using all primes as the denominator is equivalent. Finitely many ramified primes do not affect either limit. No prime-counting asymptotic or natural-density Tauberian step is required by this proof.
