There is no infinite component in the modulus (5). The ordinary ideal class group of Q is trivial, and its unit group is {±1}. The ray class exact sequence therefore gives
Cl(5)​(Q)≃(Z/5Z)×/{±1}≃C2​.​
(13)
Concretely, a fractional ideal prime to 5 has a rational generator r=a/b with 5∤ab. Reducing r modulo 5 is well-defined up to multiplication by the units ±1. Its ray class is trivial precisely when one of the two generators r,−r is congruent to one. Thus the quotient has the two classes {1,4} and {2,3}. No positivity restriction is imposed on a generator.
To identify the corresponding field, put E=Q(ζ5​+ζ5−1​)=Q(5​). The maximal real subfield of the fifth cyclotomic field is fixed by complex conjugation, the element −1 of (Z/5Z)×. For a prime ℓ=5, the Frobenius automorphism of Q(ζ5​)/Q is ζ5​↦ζ5ℓ​. Its restriction to E thus corresponds exactly to the residue class of ℓ modulo {±1}. Multiplicativity extends this to the Artin reciprocity map on all fractional ideals prime to 5, whose kernel is exactly PQ,1​((5)). Its field discriminant 5 also confirms that E is unramified outside 5; moreover E is a totally real number field. The uniqueness in the Main theorem of class field theory now gives
Q(5)​=Q(5​).​
(14)
Including the real place changes the ray class group: a principal generator must then be positive as well as congruent to one. The residue and signature group of a modulus gives
Cl(5)∞​(Q)≃((Z/5Z)××{±1})/⟨(−1,−1)⟩≃(Z/5Z)×.
(15)
Here the isomorphism sends (u,ε) to εu. Consequently Q(5)∞​=Q(ζ5​), of degree four. The finite modulus gives the real quadratic field; the modulus with the real place gives the full cyclotomic field. The printed finite-modulus assertion needs no correction.
