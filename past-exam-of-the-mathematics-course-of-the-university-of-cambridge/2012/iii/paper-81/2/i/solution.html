Past exam of the mathematics course of the University of Cambridge / 2012 / iii / Paper 81 / 2 / i / Solution

Write e=e(q/p), f=f(q/p) and g for the number of primes above p. The Galois group acts transitively on those primes, with stabilizer Dq​. The fundamental identity for prime decomposition and the orbit-stabilizer theorem therefore give
[F:K]=efg,∣Dq​∣=[F:K]/g=ef.
(7)
It remains to establish that the residue action has image of order f, rather than assume this in the desired equality.
Use the decomposition group of a prime and local Galois group identification Dq​=Gal(Fq​/Kp​). A generator of the finite field extension kq​/kp​ has a separable monic minimal polynomial of an algebraic element. Lift its coefficients to OKp​​. The Hensel lemma lifts each of its distinct residue roots to Fq​. One such lift has field degree at most f, while its residue generates the degree-f finite field extension, so its field degree is exactly f and its ramification index of a prime ideal is one. It therefore generates an unramified extension of degree f, and sending it to each of the other lifts gives its f embeddings. Because Fq​/Kp​ is a Galois extension, these embeddings extend to its automorphisms. Their reductions realize every automorphism of kq​/kp​. Thus the residue action is surjective, and
∣Iq​∣=∣Dq​∣/f=e.​
(8)
Now let G be cyclic of order ℓ2, and let H=Gal(F/L) be its unique subgroup of order ℓ. In a tower of Galois extensions, inertia maps onto inertia in the intermediate extension. This also follows from the just-proved order formula: its image has order e(F/K)/e(F/L)=e(L/K), using multiplicativity of the ramification index of a prime ideal in a tower. If p ramifies in L/K, this image in G/H is nontrivial. Every proper subgroup of G is contained in H, so inertia upstairs must be G. Hence
e(F/K)=ℓ2,p is totally ramified in F/K.​
(9)
The fundamental identity for prime decomposition then also forces a single prime above p and residue-field degree one.
