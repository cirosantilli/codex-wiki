First prove necessity. If polynomials with integer coefficients converge to f in the supremum norm, their values at 0 and 1 are convergent sequences of integers. Each such sequence is eventually constant, since a convergent sequence is Cauchy and two distinct integers are at distance at least one. Its limit is an integer. Thus f(0),f(1)∈Z.
For sufficiency we also need uniform convergence of the Bernstein polynomial to f. Here is a direct argument. For X∼Bin(n,x), write Bn​(f,x)=Ef(X/n). The binomial distribution has E(X/n)=x and Var(X/n)=x(1−x)/n≤1/(4n). For every δ>0, uniform continuity of f and the Chebyshev inequality give
∣Bn​(f,x)−f(x)∣≤ω(f,δ)+2∥f∥∞​P(∣X/n−x∣>δ)≤ω(f,δ)+2nδ2∥f∥∞​​.

First make δ small and then n large; the bound is uniform in x. Hence ∥Bn​(f)−f∥∞​→0.
If the endpoints are integers, part (b) gives ∥Bn∗​(f)−Bn​(f)∥∞​→0. Each coefficient multiplying xk(1−x)n−k in Bn∗​ is an integer. Expanding (1−x)n−k by the binomial theorem therefore gives integer coefficients in the ordinary monomial basis. The triangle inequality proves ∥Bn∗​(f)−f∥∞​→0. Thus the integer endpoint condition is necessary and sufficient for integer-coefficient polynomial approximation.
