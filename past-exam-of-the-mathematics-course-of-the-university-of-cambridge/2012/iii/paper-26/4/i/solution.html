Let Fn​=Q(μpn+1​) and F0​=Q(μp​). The cyclotomic character identifies
G=Gal(F∞​/Q)=Δ×Γ,Δ≅(Z/pZ)×,Γ≅1+pZp​.
(17)
Choose γ with cyclotomic value u=1+p, and put
Λ=Zp​[[Γ]]=Zp​[[T]],T=γ−1.
(18)
The Iwasawa algebra of G is Λ[Δ]. Conjugation, independent of lift as in the preceding solution, makes X a compact Galois module over this ring. It is the p-ramified Iwasawa module, not the unramified class-group module.
Because p−1 is a unit in Zp​, the Teichmüller character ω gives orthogonal character idempotents
eχ​=p−11​∑δ∈Δ​χ(δ)−1δ,χ=ωi,0≤i≤p−2.
(19)
They sum to one, so X=⨁χ​Xχ​, where Xχ​=eχ​X. Each is a module over the one-variable ring Λ. Complex conjugation is the element −1 of Δ; characters with χ(−1)=1 are even, and those with χ(−1)=−1 are odd.
The basic Iwasawa-module rank theorem in this tower gives
rankΛ​Xχ​={0,1,​χ even,χ odd.​​
(20)
In particular,
rankΛ​X=2p−1​,X+ is torsion,rankΛ​X−=2p−1​.​
(21)
Thus it would be incorrect to describe all of X as a torsion module.
Here is the class-field unit sequence explaining these ranks. Let U∞​ be the inverse limit, under local norms, of the pro-p completions of the unit groups at the unique prime over p in Fn​. Let E∞​ be the inverse limit of the closures of the global units in these local unit groups, and let
Y=lim​n​Cl(Fn​)(p)
(22)
under ideal norms. The Artin reciprocity maps yield
0⟶E∞​⟶U∞​⟶X⟶Y⟶0.
(23)
The final module is the unramified Iwasawa module and is finitely generated torsion, as proved in the other essay. Local-unit theory gives U∞​ Iwasawa-module rank p−1, with rank one in each Δ-character. The Leopoldt theorem for abelian number fields gives E∞​ rank (p−1)/2, with rank one in each even character and zero in each odd character. In odd characters the possible norm-compatible roots of unity contribute a rank-zero term, not a free summand. Subtracting ranks in the exact sequence gives the displayed result and also finite generation.
For any character, the Iwasawa module structure theorem describes Xχ​ up to pseudo-isomorphism:
Xχ​∼Λrχ​⊕⨁j​Λ/(paj​)⊕⨁k​Λ/(fk​(T)bk​),
(24)
where the fk​ are irreducible distinguished polynomials and the kernel and cokernel of a pseudo-isomorphism are finite. For a torsion component, its characteristic ideal is generated by
p∑j​aj​∏k​fk​(T)bk​.
(25)
The Iwasawa invariants are μ=∑aj​ and λ=∑bk​degfk​. A characteristic ideal describes the elementary divisors only in aggregate; it is not an assertion that the module is cyclic or that an odd component is actually free.
The trivial character component X1​ is zero. Indeed, the maximal abelian pro-p extension of Q unramified outside p is precisely the rational cyclotomic Zp-extension, by the Kronecker–Weber theorem. Coprime descent using the character idempotent e1​, followed by the abelianization over a Zp-extension formula, identifies (X1​)Γ​ with the additional abelian pro-p quotient over Q. There is no such extension beyond the cyclotomic one, so (X1​)Γ​=0, and the Compact Nakayama lemma gives X1​=0.
The central arithmetic description concerns the nontrivial even characters. Let gχ​(T) be the integral Kubota-Leopoldt p-adic L-function power series with convention
gχ​(u1−s−1)=Lp​(χ,s).
(26)
For positive integers k of the matching parity, the interpolation is
Lp​(χ,1−k)=−(1−(χω−k)(p)pk−1)kBk,χω−k​​,
(27)
where the Dirichlet character in the Euler factor is its primitive associate, and Bk,η​ is a generalized Bernoulli number. The Iwasawa main conjecture, a theorem here, says
charΛ​Xχ​=(gχ​(T))(χ=1, χ even).​
(28)
The trivial character is excluded from this displayed analytic normalization: its p-adic zeta function has a pole and must be treated separately.
The proof mechanism starts with norm-compatible cyclotomic units, for instance ratios
cn​(a)=1−ζpn+1​1−ζpn+1a​​(p∤a),
(29)
and their character projections. A Coleman power series encodes a norm-compatible local unit by a single power series. Applying its logarithmic derivative to these cyclotomic units produces the p-adic L-function above. If C∞​ denotes their closed norm-limit module, this calculation determines
charΛ​(U∞,χ​/C∞,χ​)=(gχ​).
(30)
The remaining global input is
charΛ​Yχ​=charΛ​(E∞,χ​/C∞,χ​)
(31)
for nontrivial even χ. In the class-field unit sequence modulo C∞,χ​, multiplicativity of characteristic ideals cancels these two terms and gives the boxed formula for Xχ​.
There are two established routes to this global input. The Mazur-Wiles theorem constructs suitable abelian extensions from Galois representations associated with modular forms and the Eisenstein ideal; their sizes supply the missing divisibility. The cyclotomic Euler system route uses norm relations for cyclotomic units at auxiliary primes and descent to bound the class-group module. The analytic class number formula and the cyclotomic-unit index formula then supply the equality of characteristic ideals. These are substantial arithmetic theorems, rather than consequences of the abstract structure theorem alone.
A useful further result is the Ferrero-Washington theorem: μ(Y)=0 for this cyclotomic tower, more generally for cyclotomic towers of abelian number fields. Its proof rules out an identically zero reduction modulo p of the relevant p-adic L-series, using distribution of p-adic digits. Together with the main conjecture, it gives μ(Xχ​)=0 for each nontrivial even χ. Therefore these torsion components are finitely generated over Zp​ up to finite error.
Finally, Kummer reflection in Iwasawa theory relates the even p-ramified module to an odd unramified component:
Xχ​∼Yωχ−1ι​(1),ι(γ)=γ−1,
(32)
where (1) is the Tate twist by the cyclotomic character. The corresponding power-series substitution is
T⟼u(1+T)−1−1.
(33)
This explains why the p-ramified interpolation involves u1−s while the reflected class-group interpolation involves us. Both the involution and the twist matter. The rank theorem, the class-field sequence, the structure theorem and the explicit even-character characteristic ideals together give the known compact Galois module structure; none justifies replacing every odd component by a free module without further argument.
