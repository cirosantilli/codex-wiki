Past exam of the mathematics course of the University of Cambridge / 2012 / iii / Paper 13 / 2 / i / Solution

 ... Past exam of the mathematics course of the University of Cambridge 2012 iii Paper 13 2 i
OurBigBook.com Words: 334
A blowup of an algebraic variety supplies a reduced special fibre with all three requested defects. Let B=A3 have coordinates (x,y,z), let p=(0,1,0), and let π:X=Blp​B→B. Take Y=A1 and the regular map
f:X⟶Y,f=(xy)∘π.
(14)
For an explicit construction, X is the closed subvariety of A3×P2, with projective coordinates [u:v:w], defined by
xv=(y−1)u,xw=zu,(y−1)w=zv.
(15)
These equations say that (x,y−1,z) is proportional to (u,v,w). The three standard projective charts are affine spaces A3, so X is a smooth variety which is an irreducible variety embedded in a quasi-projective algebraic set. Its algebraic exceptional divisor is E=π−1(p)≅P2.
If t=0, the surface xy=t misses p, and the blowup of an algebraic variety is an isomorphism over it. Hence
Xt​≅Speck[x,x−1,z]≅Gm​×A1.​
(16)
It is a smooth variety, an affine variety, an irreducible variety, and has algebraic dimension 2.
For t=0, the fibre of a morphism has three irreducible components: the strict transform of an algebraic subvariety V(x)​, the unchanged plane V(y), and the algebraic exceptional divisor E≅P2. The centre p lies on V(x) but not on V(y); this is why all three components have multiplicity one. To verify reducedness and the crossing directly near E, use the chart v=0, putting
a=x/(y−1),b=y−1,c=z/(y−1).
(17)
Here x=ab, y=1+b, z=bc, and f=ab(1+b). Near b=0, the factor 1+b is a unit, so the fibre of a morphism is ab=0, a reduced union of two crossing planes. The u-chart gives f=x(1+xv), whose second factor is a unit along E; the w-chart gives f=zu(1+zv) and the same reduced crossing. Away from E, reducedness follows from xy=0 in B. Thus the scheme-theoretic fibre of a morphism is itself reduced.
It has singular points of an algebraic variety already along V(x,y), which misses p: locally its equation is xy=0, with Zariski tangent space of dimension of a vector space 3 but local dimension of an algebraic variety 2. Finally, E is a closed subvariety of X0​. A closed subvariety of an affine variety is an affine variety, whereas P2 is not: all its global regular functions are constant, which cannot be the coordinate ring of a positive-dimensional affine variety. Consequently X0​ is singular, reducible, and nonaffine, as required. Choosing the centre off the intersection of the original planes avoids introducing a nonreduced special fibre of a morphism.
