For a smooth projective curve C, let g=h1(C,OC​) be its geometric genus, let KC​ be a canonical divisor, and write ℓ(D)=h0(C,OC​(D)) for the dimension of a vector space of the Riemann-Roch space. The Riemann-Roch theorem says
ℓ(D)−ℓ(KC​−D)=degD+1−g​
(37)
for every divisor on an algebraic curve D on C.
Here is the cohomological proof of Riemann-Roch for curves. At a closed point P, the local ring is a discrete valuation ring. A local uniformizer shows that increasing the allowed pole order by one gives the short exact sequence of sheaves
0⟶OC​(D−P)⟶OC​(D)⟶k(P)⟶0.
(38)
The last term is a length-one skyscraper sheaf; its precise identification with k is noncanonical, but its sheaf cohomology has h0=1 and h1=0. All the relevant groups of sheaf cohomology are finite-dimensional because C is a projective variety, and coherent sheaf cohomology above degree one vanishes on a algebraic curve. The long exact sequence in sheaf cohomology therefore gives
χ(OC​(D))=χ(OC​(D−P))+1,χ(F)=h0(C,F)−h1(C,F).
(39)
Since k is an algebraically closed field, every closed point has degree one. Iterating this identity for both positive and negative coefficients of D proves
χ(OC​(D))=degD+χ(OC​).
(40)
Every global regular function on a projective variety which is an irreducible variety is constant; hence h0(C,OC​)=1 and χ(OC​)=1−g.
Finally apply Serre duality for a smooth projective curve:
H1(C,OC​(D))∗≅H0(C,ωC​⊗OC​(−D))=H0(C,OC​(KC​−D)).
(41)
Taking dimensions of a vector space and inserting this in the Euler characteristic of a coherent sheaf identity proves the displayed Riemann-Roch theorem. The substantial inputs from sheaf cohomology are finiteness and vanishing for coherent sheaves on a projective curve, and Serre duality; the change of Euler characteristic of a coherent sheaf is proved directly by the point exact sequence. In particular the argument handles negative as well as effective divisors on an algebraic curve.
