Write a=t1​, b=t2​, c=t3​, and A=C[a,b,c]/(ac−b2). It is the quadratic cone invariant ring
A≅C[u2,uv,v2]=C[u,v]{±1},
(34)
where the involution negates both u and v. The invariant monomials have even total degree and are generated by u2,uv,v2; reduction by ac=b2 proves the asserted presentation.
To verify the normal variety property, suppose q∈Frac(A) is integral over A. The same monic equation makes q integral over C[u,v]. This polynomial ring is an integrally closed domain, so q∈C[u,v]. Since q belongs to the fraction field of A, it is invariant under the involution, and hence belongs to A. Thus X is normal. The gradient of its equation is (c,−2b,a), so the Jacobian criterion shows that the origin o is its only singular point of an algebraic variety. In particular X is not smooth.
Take the prime Weil divisor D=VX​(a,b) and x=o. We show that D is not a Cartier divisor at o. In the local ring
R=A(a,b,c)​,m=(a,b,c)R,
(35)
its prime ideal is I=(a,b)R. The quotient I/mI has dimension of a vector space 2 over C: a,b generate it, and they are independent since mI⊂m2 while the relation ac−b2 has no linear term. By Nakayama lemma, I cannot be generated by one element.
On a normal variety, the ideal of an effective prime Weil divisor is the divisorial ideal of functions with order at least one along that divisor and order at least zero along every other prime Weil divisor. If D were a Cartier divisor at o, a local defining rational function h would identify this ideal with hR, because an integrally closed domain is the intersection of its height-one localizations at a prime ideal inside its fraction field. That would make I principal, contradicting the calculation. Therefore D has a nonzero class in the local divisor class group.
Now let D′=D+div(q) be any linearly equivalent Weil divisor. If o were outside its support of a Weil divisor, there would be an open subset containing o on which D′ is zero. On that neighbourhood D=−div(q) would be a principal Weil divisor, hence a Cartier divisor, which is impossible. Therefore this point is unavoidable in every representative:
o∈Supp(D′)for every D′∼D.​
(36)
The argument does not assume that D′ is effective. For comparison, div(a)=2D because on the chart c=0, a=b2/c; the obstruction has order two, as in the Divisor class group of an A-type surface singularity.
