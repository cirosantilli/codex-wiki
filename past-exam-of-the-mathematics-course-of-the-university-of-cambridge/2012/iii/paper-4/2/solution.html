Use y on the target and x on the source, so the map of coordinate rings sends y to x4. A closed point of the real affine line corresponds to a maximal ideal generated either by y−a, a∈R, or by a monic irreducible quadratic q(y). The scheme-theoretic fibre over that point has coordinate algebra
Bp​=R[x]⊗R[y]​κ(p)=R[x]/(qp​(x4)).
(5)
Here κ(p) is the residue field, and qp​=y−a in the real-point case. The fibre is a scheme, so its nilpotents must be retained.
For a>0, put r=a1/4. The factors x−r, x+r, x2+r2 are pairwise coprime, with the last one irreducible over R. The Chinese remainder theorem identifies Bp​ with R×R×C, giving three irreducible components.
For a<0, put r=(−a)1/4. The exact factorization is
x4+r4=(x2+2​rx+r2)(x2−2​rx+r2).
(6)
Each quadratic has discriminant −2r2<0, and they are distinct and coprime. Thus Bp​≅C×C, giving two irreducible components.
For a=0, Bp​=R[x]/(x4). It has one prime ideal, generated by the class of x, so one irreducible component. It is not the reduced point SpecR: its class of x is a nonzero nilpotent element with nilpotency index four.
Finally write the irreducible quadratic as qp​(y)=(y−z)(y−z), with z∈/R. Over C, qp​(x4) has four roots above z and four above z. They are all simple, because z=0, and the two sets are disjoint. None is real. Conjugation pairs them into four distinct irreducible real quadratics. Hence Bp​≅C4 and this fibre has four irreducible components.

These are ordinary scheme components, not geometric components counted after complexifying the base. Counting only real roots would miss both the complex-residue factors and the nonreal closed points.
