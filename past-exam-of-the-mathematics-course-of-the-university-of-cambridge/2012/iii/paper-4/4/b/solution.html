A generic linear projection over an infinite field is not enough for the stated arbitrary field k. Instead use Noether normalization by weighted substitutions to make the defining polynomial monic in one coordinate.
For n>1, choose an integer M at least every exponent occurring in a nonzero monomial of f, put B=M+1, and use the triangular polynomial change of variables
xi​=yi​+ynBi​(1≤i<n),xn​=yn​.
(19)
Here M≥1 because the irreducible polynomial is nonconstant. This is an automorphism, with inverse yi​=xi​−xnBi​, yn​=xn​. A monomial x1α1​​⋯xnαn​​ contributes a highest yn​ power
w(α)=αn​+∑i=1n−1​αi​Bi.
(20)
These weights are all distinct: the exponents are base-B digits bounded by M. All other terms in the expansion have smaller yn​ degree than that monomial's weight. Therefore the highest-degree yn​ term in the transformed f comes from exactly one original monomial and has a nonzero coefficient in k, independent of the other yi​. No assumption about the cardinality or characteristic of k enters this argument.
Multiply by the inverse of that coefficient to obtain a monic polynomial F∈A[yn​] of some degree D>0, where A=k[y1​,…,yn−1​]. The same construction for n=1 simply rescales f to be monic over A=k.
The monic polynomial quotient is finite free because division by F gives a unique representative of degree less than D. Thus
k[x1​,…,xn​]/(f)≅A[yn​]/(F)≅A⊕Ayn​⊕⋯⊕AynD−1​​
(21)
as A-modules. Uniqueness follows because any nonzero multiple of a monic F has yn​ degree at least D. In particular the map A→A[yn​]/(F) is injective.
The induced morphism
X⟶SpecA=Akn−1​​
(22)
is finite, since its coordinate algebra is a finite module, and flat, since tensoring with a finite free module is a finite direct sum of copies of the original module and preserves exactness. This is a finite flat Noether normalization of an affine hypersurface, with the explicit projection coordinates yi​=xi​−xnBi​. The construction actually applies to any nonconstant polynomial, not only an irreducible one.
