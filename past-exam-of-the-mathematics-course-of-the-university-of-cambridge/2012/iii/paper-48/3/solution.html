Use a real Lie algebra convention [Ta​,Tb​]=fab​cTc​. Its Adjoint representation of a Lie algebra acts on g itself:
adX​(Y)=[X,Y].​
(28)
Linearity is immediate. The Jacobi identity gives
[adX​,adY​]Z=[X,[Y,Z]]−[Y,[X,Z]]=[[X,Y],Z],
(29)
so [adX​,adY​]=ad[X,Y]​, the defining Lie algebra representation condition. In the basis Tb​, its matrix entries are
(Taad​)cb​=fab​c.​
(30)
Thus the adjoint generators are the structure constants arranged as matrices. The representation has kernel equal to the center of a Lie algebra; it need not be faithful for an arbitrary algebra.
The Adjoint representation of a Lie group is Adg​(Y)=gYg−1. It satisfies Adgh​=Adg​Adh​, preserves the identity, and sends inverses to inverse matrices. Differentiating etXYe−tX gives [X,etXYe−tX], so
AdeX​=eadX​,e−XYeX=e−adX​Y.​
(31)
Consequently the positive adjoint exponentials furnish the representation on elements g=eX and their products, and the intrinsic conjugation action defines it globally.
The inverse-conjugation formula in the PDF needs a minus adjoint exponent with this standard definition. Its first-order term is Y−[X,Y], whereas e+adX​Y has first-order term Y+[X,Y]. For example X=T1​,Y=T2​ in a nonabelian algebra with [T1​,T2​]=0 already distinguishes the two. The inverse conjugation and adjoint antirepresentations identity explains the group-order issue as well: F(g)=Adg−1​ satisfies F(gh)=F(h)F(g). Retaining the printed inverse conjugation as a left action without this order reversal would not give an ordinary group representation.
The Killing form is the symmetric bilinear form
B(X,Y)=tr(adX​adY​).​
(32)
To prove degeneracy for a non-semisimple Lie algebra, take its nonzero solvable radical and the last nonzero member a of its derived series of a Lie algebra. This is a nonzero abelian ideal of a Lie algebra. For x∈a, adx​ maps g into a and kills a. Every ady​ preserves a. Hence adx​ady​ maps the full space into a and has zero restriction there, so its trace is zero. Thus B(x,y)=0 for every y. This is the abelian ideals lie in the radical of the Killing form argument, and establishes a nonzero kernel and vanishing determinant.
For a compact real semisimple Lie algebra, the adjoint action is unitary in an invariant positive inner product. Its infinitesimal generators are skew-Hermitian, giving
B(X,X)=−tr(adX†​adX​)<0(X=0).
(33)
Strictness follows because the adjoint kernel is the zero center. This explains the compactness criterion from the Killing form: “strictly negative” means negative-definite, not that every entry of its matrix is negative.
In the first three-generator example, take columns to be images of the basis (X,Y,H). Direct use of the brackets gives
adX​=​000​002​020​​,adY​=​00−2​000​200​​,adH​=​0−20​−200​000​​.​
(34)
Taking traces of products yields the Killing form for cyclic three-generator brackets
[B](X,Y,H)​=diag(8,−8,8).​
(35)
It is nondegenerate, hence semisimple by the preceding degeneracy result, but it has mixed signature and is not compact. An explicit realization is
X=(01​10​),Y=(0−1​10​),H=(−10​01​),
(36)
which span the real traceless two-by-two matrices and satisfy exactly these brackets.
Changing the sign of [X,H] changes the first and third adjoint matrices to
adX​=​000​002​0−20​​,adH​=​020​−200​000​​,
(37)
while adY​ is unchanged. Now
[B]=−8I3​,​
(38)
the negative-definite compact case. The basis X=−iσ1​, Y=−iσ2​, H=−iσ3​ realizes it as the real special unitary Lie algebra su(2).
