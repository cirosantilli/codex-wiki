Use signature η=diag(1,−1,−1,−1), the numerical matrices σμ​=(I,σi​) and σˉμ​=(I,−σi​) given in the question, and raise genuine tensor indices with η. A real Minkowski spacetime vector corresponds to the Hermitian matrix
X=x0I+x⋅σ,detX=(x0)2−∣x∣2.
(39)
For SL(2,C) matrices, X′=AXA† is Hermitian and has the same determinant. It therefore induces a real linear Lorentz transformation; composition of the congruence actions agrees with matrix multiplication. The kernel consists of ±I: preservation of X=I makes a kernel element unitary, and preservation of every Hermitian X makes it scalar.
Polar decomposition of an invertible complex matrix connects every determinant-one complex matrix to its unitary factor, so the group is connected. The action gives the Lorentz spinor double cover of the Proper orthochronous Lorentz group. It covers rotations via AR​=e−iϑn⋅σ/2 and boosts via the positive Hermitian matrices below. Every proper orthochronous transformation is a boost followed by a rotation, since one can first match its image of the future unit time vector and then use its rotation stabilizer. The congruence construction does not cover spatial parity or time reversal. In particular parity has determinant −1 as a four-vector transformation; all transformations continuously produced from SL(2,C) have determinant +1.
Set cθ​=cosh(θ/2), sθ​=sinh(θ/2) and N=n⋅σ. The Pauli matrix multiplication law gives N2=I. The proposed boost matrix is AB​=cθ​I+sθ​N, with eigenvalues e±θ/2, determinant one and AB†​=AB​. Split x=x∥​n+x⊥​. The perpendicular Pauli part anticommutes with N, so multiplication gives
x′0=coshθx0+sinhθx∥​,x∥′​=sinhθx0+coshθx∥​,x⊥′​=x⊥​.​
(40)
This is an active Lorentz boost. The image of a rest worldline has velocity v=tanhθn, fixing the velocity sign convention.
For two nonzero boosts, write A′=c′I+s′n′⋅σ and A=cI+sn⋅σ. Their product is
A′A=(c′c+s′sn′⋅n)I+(c′sn+s′cn′)⋅σ+is′s(n′×n)⋅σ.
(41)
The last term is anti-Hermitian. Thus A′A is not Hermitian unless the axes are collinear; a pure boost has Hermitian lifts ±AB​, so it cannot give the same Lorentz transformation. The noncollinear boost obstruction from Pauli products is therefore
two nonzero noncollinear boosts require an accompanying rotation.​
(42)
That rotation is a Wigner rotation. A zero-rapidity factor is the trivial exception, regardless of the arbitrary axis assigned to it.
Define Ji​=21​ϵijk​Mjk​ and Ki​=M0i​. The printed Lorentz algebra brackets give
[Ji​,Jj​]=iϵijk​Jk​,[Ji​,Kj​]=iϵijk​Kk​,[Ki​,Kj​]=−iϵijk​Jk​.
(43)
Set Li​=(Ji​+iKi​)/2, Ri​=(Ji​−iKi​)/2. Then
[Li​,Lj​]=iϵijk​Lk​,[Ri​,Rj​]=iϵijk​Rk​,[Li​,Rj​]=0.​
(44)
This is the chiral decomposition of the complex Lorentz algebra. The two copies are the complexifications of the SU(2) algebras conventionally labelled left and right. They are not two independent compact real subalgebras of the real Lorentz algebra: L,R are complex linear combinations of the real generators. The distinction is needed for noncompact boosts.
To verify the infinitesimal two-component action, put
a=41​ωμνσμ​σˉν​,A=I+a+O(ω2).
(45)
Its trace vanishes by antisymmetry, so detA=1+O(ω2). The Pauli matrix multiplication law implies
σμ​σˉν​σρ​+σρ​σˉν​σμ​=2(ημν​σρ​+ηνρ​σμ​−ημρ​σν​).
(46)
Since a†=41​ωμνσˉν​σμ​, antisymmetrizing this identity yields
aσρ​+σρ​a†=ωμρ​σμ​.
(47)
Consequently AXA†=X+σμ​ωμν​xν+O(ω2), exactly the claimed infinitesimal coordinate transformation with ωμν​=ωμρηρν​.
The two Weyl spinor representations transform as
ψL​↦AψL​,ψR​↦(A†)−1ψR​.​
(48)
Both maps preserve group multiplication. For Λ=eω, choose its spin lift and write the finite matrices as
A=exp(41​ωμνσμ​σˉν​),(A†)−1=exp(41​ωμνσˉμ​σν​).
(49)
For rotations these coincide as the SU(2) doublet; for boosts their generator signs are opposite. Any complex-linear intertwiner would commute with all rotations and hence be scalar by the Schur lemma, but a nonzero scalar cannot intertwine the opposite boost matrices. They are therefore inequivalent. Infinitesimally their Ki​ matrices are −iσi​/2 and +iσi​/2, while their Ji​ matrices are both σi​/2. Thus they have chiral labels (1/2,0) and (0,1/2). The finite matrices are representations of the spin cover; choosing A or −A matters for spinors even though their four-vector transformations coincide.
Using the supplied block gamma matrices, the generator of the Dirac spinor is
Ω=41​ωμνγμν​=(41​ωμνσμ​σˉν​0​041​ωμνσˉμ​σν​​).
(50)
The antisymmetry of ω removes the symmetric Clifford part. Hence
ψ↦Sψ,S=eΩ=diag(A,(A†)−1).​
(51)
There is a conjugation-order error in the last displayed gamma identity in the PDF. The Clifford algebra gives, with γρ=ηρσγσ​,
[γμν​,γρ]=2(δνρ​γμ​−δμρ​γν​),[Ω,γρ]=−ωρμ​γμ.
(52)
Exponentiating this linear commutator action proves the inverse Lorentz action on gamma matrices
SγρS−1=(Λ−1)ρμ​γμ,S−1γρS=Λρμ​γμ.​
(53)
The second identity is the order required for the requested bilinears when ψ↦Sψ. An explicit countercheck to the printed order is a positive boost along the third axis: ω03=−θ gives
Sγ0S−1=coshθγ0−sinhθγ3,
(54)
whereas the printed right side has a plus sign. This cannot be repaired by dropping index raising; the same raising convention is needed in the preceding coordinate transformation.
The adjoint relation supplied in the question gives Ω†γ0=−γ0Ω, hence the Dirac spinor pseudo-unitarity identity S†γ0S=γ0. The Dirac adjoint therefore transforms as ψˉ​↦ψˉ​S−1. It now follows that the Dirac scalar bilinear is
ψˉ​′ψ′=ψˉ​S−1Sψ=ψˉ​ψ,​
(55)
the vector is
ψˉ​′γρψ′=Λρμ​ψˉ​γμψ,​
(56)
and the antisymmetric second-rank tensor is
ψˉ​′i[γρ,γσ]ψ′=Λρμ​Λσν​ψˉ​i[γμ,γν]ψ.​
(57)
These are respectively a Lorentz scalar, Lorentz four-vector and Lorentz tensor. The source's inconsistent gamma identity is replaced by its correct inverse/order pair; all three transformation laws then follow with the stated spinor transformation.
