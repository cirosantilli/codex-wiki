Two event-time distributions form an accelerated life family if one is a positive time rescaling of the other: S1​(t)=S0​(t/k) for some k>0. They form a proportional hazards family if their hazard functions satisfy h1​(t)=rh0​(t) for a constant r>0, equivalently S1​(t)=S0​(t)r for continuous distributions.
First take the standard positive-scale convention c>0. Write SU​(u)=FU​(u)=P(U>u), retaining the question's survivor notation rather than interpreting FU​ as a cumulative distribution. Since Tz​=ea+bzUc,
Sz​(t)=SU​(t1/ce−(a+bz)/c),t>0.​
(8)
It follows that Sz​(t)=S0​(te−bz), proving the accelerated life family property with time multiplier ebz. In fact the scaling identity Tz​=debzT0​ holds for any fixed real c.
The given density integrates to P(X≤x)=1−e−ex, so U=eX has the unit exponential distribution. Its survivor is e−u. Consequently
Sz​(t)=exp[−e−(a+bz)/ct1/c],hz​(t)=c1​e−(a+bz)/ct1/c−1,
(9)
and
hz​(t)/h0​(t)=e−bz/c.​
(10)
These are the Weibull accelerated-life and proportional-hazards families, with common shape 1/c>0.
The positive-scale assumption is necessary for this last conclusion and is not explicit in the printed description of the constants. If c<0, the transformation reverses the inequality, giving Sz​(t)=1−SU​(r−) with r=t1/ce−(a+bz)/c; for continuous U this is 1−SU​(r). For the given density these are scaled Fréchet distributions, generally not proportional hazards. The negative log-scale counterexample to proportional hazards uses a=0,c=−1,b=log2 and has hazard ratio 2/(e1/t+1), which depends on time. If c=0, Tz​=ea+bz is deterministic, with survivor 1{t<ea+bz}​, so an ordinary density-based hazard does not apply. These cases preserve the accelerated-life identity but do not prove the asserted proportional-hazards conclusion.
