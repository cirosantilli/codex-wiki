Use the predictable sigma-algebra generated by A×{0}, A∈F0​, and A×(s,t], A∈Fs​. A previsible process is a measurable function for this sigma-algebra. Set Jt​=1{S<t≤T}​, with J0​=0. The events {S<t} and {T<t} are in Ft​, because they are countable unions of events {S≤q} and {T≤q} for rational q<t. Thus J is an adapted process. Each path is left-continuous, including at the two endpoints, and is zero if S=T.
For a direct measurability argument, let
Jt(n)​=∑k≥0​Jk2−n​1(k2−n,(k+1)2−n]​(t).

1(S,T]​ is previsible.​
