Use just the parameters 1 and −1. Positivity and adaptation of their local martingales give
Xt​=21​(logZt(1)​−logZt(−1)​),At​=−logZt(1)​−logZt(−1)​.
(13)
Thus both processes are adapted. A positive continuous local martingale has a semimartingale logarithm, by Itô formula after localization away from zero. Since X=logZ(1)+A/2 and A has finite variation, X is a continuous semimartingale. Write X=N+V, where N is a zero-starting continuous local martingale and V is continuous finite variation, also starting from zero.
Apply Itô formula to each exponential:
dZ(θ)=θZ(θ)dN+Z(θ)(θdV+21​θ2(d[N]−dA)).
(14)
Because Z(θ) is itself a local martingale, the finite-variation term is zero by part (a). Divide its finite signed measure by the strictly positive Z(θ). For θ=1,−1, respectively,
dV+21​(d[N]−dA)=0,−dV+21​(d[N]−dA)=0.
(15)
Adding and subtracting show V=0 and [N]=A. Therefore the exponential criterion for a continuous local martingale and its bracket gives
X is a continuous local martingale,[X]=A indistinguishably.​
(16)
The semimartingale property was established before applying Itô's formula to X; it was not assumed from the outset.
