The Riemannian volume form of an oriented n-dimensional Riemannian manifold is the unique positive n-form taking value one on every positively oriented orthonormal frame. In positive coordinates,
ωg​=det(gij​)​dx1∧⋯∧dxn.
(17)
The metric induces an inner product on exterior powers of the cotangent bundle, with wedges of distinct orthonormal covectors forming an orthonormal basis. The Hodge star is the pointwise linear map ∗:ΛpT∗M→Λn−pT∗M specified by
α∧∗β=⟨α,β⟩g​ωg​.
(18)
It is an isometry and satisfies ∗∗α=(−1)p(n−p)α. With the positive-sign convention, the Hodge Laplacian on differential forms is
Δ=dδ+δd,δ∣Ωp​=(−1)n(p−1)+1∗d∗.
(19)
Here d is the exterior derivative and δ its formal adjoint; δ on functions is zero. On functions this is the Laplace-Beltrami operator
Δf=−div(gradf)=−detg​1​∂i​(detg​gij∂j​f).
(20)
The convention divgrad gives the negative of this operator. All subsequent Laplacians use the nonnegative convention above.
On a compact oriented manifold without boundary, the Hodge decomposition theorem gives an orthogonal decomposition of smooth real forms,
Ωp(M)=Hp(M)⊕dΩp−1(M)⊕δΩp+1(M),Hp=kerΔ.
(21)
The three component forms are uniquely determined, although the potentials in the last two summands need not be unique. The space Hp is finite-dimensional, and each de Rham cohomology class has a unique harmonic representative. Integration by parts gives ⟨Δα,α⟩L2​=∥dα∥L22​+∥δα∥L22​, so harmonic forms are exactly the closed and coclosed forms. Set Ωk=0 outside 0≤k≤n. Compactness without boundary is a hypothesis of this version of the theorem; orientation alone does not supply it.
A connection on TM induces a dual connection on T∗M and then connections on its tensor and exterior powers by the Leibniz rule. Explicitly, for a p-form α,
(∇X​α)(Y1​,…,Yp​)=X(α(Y1​,…,Yp​))−∑j=1p​α(Y1​,…,∇X​Yj​,…,Yp​).
(22)
To prove the parallelism of the Riemannian volume form, use a local positively oriented orthonormal frame Ei​, its dual coframe θi, and write ∇X​Ej​=∑i​aij​(X)Ei​. Metric compatibility makes the matrix a(X) skew-symmetric. Since ωg​=θ1∧⋯∧θn, differentiating the wedge yields
∇X​ωg​=−∑i​aii​(X)ωg​=0.
(23)
Off-diagonal terms vanish because they repeat a coframe factor. Thus ∇ωg​=0; equivalently parallel transport preserves both the metric and the continuously chosen orientation.
Now let M be compact, oriented and without boundary. For representing functionals on harmonic forms by wedge pairing, choose an L2-orthonormal basis h1​,…,hb​ of Hp and define
hf​=∑a=1b​f(ha​)ha​,ψ0​=∗hf​.
(24)
If φ=∑a​ca​ha​, linearity gives
f(φ)=⟨φ,hf​⟩L2​=∫M​φ∧ψ0​​.
(25)
This also works when b=0, using the empty sum. Since the Hodge star commutes with the Hodge Laplacian, ψ0​ is harmonic of degree n−p.
For the full ambiguity of harmonic wedge-pairing representatives, write another representative as ψ0​+χ. Because the star is an isometry,
∫M​φ∧χ=⟨∗φ,χ⟩L2​.
(26)
The forms ∗φ range over all of Hn−p. Hence χ gives zero functional exactly when its harmonic projection is zero. Hodge decomposition proves that the complete answer is
ψ=∗hf​+dα+δβ,α∈Ωn−p−1(M),β∈Ωn−p+1(M)​.
(27)
Both summands in this ambiguity are permitted; it is not only an exact-form ambiguity. The exact and coexact component forms are orthogonal to every harmonic form, so every displayed choice works. Conversely a nonzero harmonic component would pair nontrivially with some ∗φ, and therefore cannot occur. The harmonic representative ∗hf​ is unique; the arbitrary smooth representative generally is not. The formula also covers the endpoint degrees and any exceptional case where the ambiguity space is zero.
For a concrete coexact ambiguity, take the torus (R/2πZ)2 with metric dx2+dy2. The form χ=δ(sinydx∧dy)=cosydx annihilates every harmonic one-form by orthogonality, yet dχ=sinydx∧dy is nonzero. Thus an annihilating representative need not even be closed, let alone exact.
