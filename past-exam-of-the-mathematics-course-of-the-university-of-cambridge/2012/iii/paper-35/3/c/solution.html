One precise relation is to radial SLE with parameter 2. Its orientation is from the boundary point 1 to the interior target 0. With conformal-radius time it is defined by
∂t​gt​(z)=gt​(z)eiθt​−gt​(z)eiθt​+gt​(z)​,θt​=2​βt​,gt′​(0)=et.
(45)
There is a coupling of the conditioned Brownian path B with this simple radial curve η such that, for each initial curve segment, the first point at which B encounters it is its tip. More precisely, if st​=inf{s:Bs​∈η[0,t]}, then Bst​​=η(t). Reversing the Brownian path turns these first-hit times into the last-visit times that characterize loop-erasure of planar Brownian motion. Thus
η is a loop-erasure of the time reversal of B,η∼radial SLE2​(D;1→0).​
(46)
This is an existence statement for a coupling, not a pointwise equality of the two processes or a claim that a naive finite-loop deletion algorithm applies to Brownian motion.
Here is the martingale mechanism behind the coupling. Put at​=eiθt​ and
Qt​(z)=Reat​−gt​(z)at​+gt​(z)​.
(47)
This is the positive slit-domain Poisson kernel at the tip, normalized by Qt​(0)=1. Writing qt​=gt​(z)/at​, the radial equation gives
dqt​=−iκ​qt​dβt​+[qt​1−qt​1+qt​​−2κ​qt​]dt.
(48)
The drift of (1+qt​)/(1−qt​) is (2−κ)qt​(1+qt​)/(1−qt​)3, so Qt​(z) is a local martingale at κ=2.
Start independent stopped B and radial SLE2, and weight their joint laws, before meeting, by
L(s,t)=h(Bs​)Qt​(Bs​)​.
(49)
For fixed s this is an SLE local martingale. For fixed t it is a Brownian-transform local martingale, because Lh(Qt​/h)=(2h)−1ΔQt​=0. Since L(0,t)=L(s,0)=1, localized two-parameter weighting preserves both original marginals. For a fixed slit, the changed Brownian law is the transform by Qt​, so it hits that slit at the pole, its tip. Compatible stopped couplings, followed by exhaustion, give the first-hit-at-tip property. This completion is the substantive continuum coupling step. Discrete loop-erased random walks and their radial-SLE2 limit give a parallel approximation picture.
For example, in this coupling avoidance by B implies avoidance by η, since the radial curve is contained in the Brownian trace. The probability from part (b) therefore supplies a lower bound ∣ϕ′(1)∣≤P(η⊂U). The two avoidance laws are not identical: unweighted simple radial SLE2​ does not have the Brownian radial-restriction property. This is a way to transfer path-containment information while retaining the distinct geometry and orientation of the two random curves.
