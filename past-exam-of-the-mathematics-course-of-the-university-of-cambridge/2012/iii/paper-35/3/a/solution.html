Let D be the unit disc and normalize its positive Poisson kernel at the boundary point 1 by
h(z)=∣1−z∣21−∣z∣2​,h(0)=1.
(39)
It is harmonic in D. Let pD​(t,z,w) be the killed Brownian transition density, for planar Brownian motion with generator 21​Δ. The Doob h-transform has sub-Markov transition density
pDh​(t,z,w)=pD​(t,z,w)h(z)h(w)​.​
(40)
Its missing mass represents paths already absorbed at the distinguished boundary point. Equivalently, for a stopping time σ before exit from a compact subdomain, its law is weighted relative to ordinary killed Brownian motion by h(Bσ​)/h(B0​). These stopped laws are consistent and define a diffusion up to its lifetime. Its generator is
Lhf=h1​21​Δ(hf)=21​Δf+∇logh⋅∇f.
(41)
This is a rigorous Brownian motion conditioned to exit at a boundary point, not conditioning on an event of positive probability. Exhaustion of D shows that its terminal boundary limit is 1; its lifetime is finite. For example its expected lifetime from 0 is ∫D​GDBM​(0,w)h(w)dw, which is finite: the Green function vanishes linearly near the smooth boundary and cancels the Poisson-kernel singularity there, while its logarithmic singularity at 0 is integrable.
One can also condition ordinary Brownian motion on exiting through an arc Iϵ​ about 1, and then let its length decrease to zero. The conditional harmonic functions ωD​(z,Iϵ​)/ωD​(0,Iϵ​) converge locally uniformly to h(z). Thus their stopped conditional laws converge to this same diffusion. Both constructions specify the conditioning unambiguously.
