The time-changed diffusion has generator A=(κ/2)∂zz​+2(1/z+1/(z−1))∂z​. For 1<z<R, its boundary hitting probability from a diffusion scale function is
Pz​(τR​<τ1​)=sκ​(R)sκ​(z)​.
(20)
To see this directly, stop the scale local martingale on [1+ϵ,R] and pass to ϵ↓0. Exit from (1,R) occurs in finite clock time: the scale density behaves as (z−1)−4/κ and the speed density as (z−1)4/κ, so the usual scale-times-speed exit integral is finite at 1. More explicitly, with m(v)=2/(κsκ′​(v)) the finite-interval diffusion exit Green kernel gives
Ez​τ1,R​=∫1R​sκ​(R)sκ​(z∧v)[sκ​(R)−sκ​(z∨v)]​m(v)dv.
(21)
The equation Au=−1 follows by differentiation and the derivative jump at v=z, so stopping the corresponding Itô identity gives this exit bound. The integrand near 1 is O(v−1); elsewhere it is bounded. At large z the drift is bounded, excluding explosion at infinity in finite clock time.
Here is why these exits describe the original swallowing event. In clock time,
D(u)=D(0)exp(−2∫0u​Zv​(Zv​−1)dv​).
(22)
If Z hits 1 at finite u, its integrated stochastic differential equation shows ∫0τ1​​(Zv​−1)−1dv<∞: the Brownian term has a finite limit and both positive drift integrals must then have finite limits. Consequently D(τ1​)>0, X hits zero and Y stays positive. This gives T(x)<T(y).
Conversely T(x) is finite by part (b). If its clock ends at a finite value, the diffusion must hit 1; an interior endpoint with positive D would leave X positive. If the clock runs forever without hitting 1, the standard one-dimensional scale classification gives escape to infinity whenever Sκ​<∞. This cannot represent a strict swallowing event, which has D→YT(x)​>0 and Z→1. Hence the surviving event represents T(x)=T(y). This reasoning also explains why a simultaneous collision must be treated separately rather than assigned the boundary value at 1.
For κ≥8, the scale is unbounded. The maximal inequality for a nonnegative supermartingale gives P(supsκ​(Z)≥sκ​(R))≤sκ​(z)/sκ​(R); letting R→∞ rules out escape. Finite-interval exit then forces a hit at 1. For 4<κ<8, taking R→∞ gives
F(x,y)=1−Sκ​sκ​(y/(y−x))​,P(T(x)=T(y))=Sκ​sκ​(y/(y−x))​>0.​
(23)
The strict-event probability is also positive, since 0<sκ​(z)<Sκ​ for finite z.
For κ≥8, first intersect the probability-one strict-order events over rational 0<a<b. The real boundary flow gives nondecreasing swallowing times on (0,∞). For any 0<x<y, choose rational x<a<b<y; then T(x)≤T(a)<T(b)≤T(y). This proves the simultaneous assertion
almost surely T(x)<T(y) for every 0<x<y,κ≥8.​
(24)
The range is positive points, as in the definition of F; reflection reverses the ordering on the negative axis. At the printed endpoint κ=4, each fixed positive point has T=∞. Thus P(T(x)=T(y))=1 if equality of extended times is allowed, but there is no finite simultaneous-swallowing assertion there. The preceding formulas assume κ>4.
