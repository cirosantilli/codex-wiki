For Res>1, define the Riemann zeta function by the absolutely convergent Dirichlet series
ζ(s)=∑n≥1​n−s=∏p​(1−p−s)−1.
(21)
The Euler product follows from unique prime factorization and absolute convergence. The counting-function integral gives
ζ(s)=s∫1∞​⌊u⌋u−s−1du=s−1s​−s∫1∞​{u}u−s−1du.
(22)
Because the fractional part is bounded, the last integral, and its derivatives with respect to s on compact subsets, converge uniformly for Res>0. It is a holomorphic function there. This proves the Meromorphic continuation of the Riemann zeta function to the right half-plane, with a simple pole at one of residue one and no other singularity in that half-plane.
The Gamma function is
Γ(z)=∫0∞​e−uuz−1du(Rez>0).
(23)
The Gamma function recurrence extends it meromorphically; its poles are at the nonpositive integers and it has no zeros. The functional equation of the Riemann zeta function can be stated as
π−s/2Γ(s/2)ζ(s)=π−(1−s)/2Γ((1−s)/2)ζ(1−s),​
(24)
or, equivalently,
ζ(s)=2sπs−1sin(πs/2)Γ(1−s)ζ(1−s).​
(25)
These are identities of meromorphic functions; the second also extends the Riemann zeta function to the remaining half-plane. The critical strip is 0<Res<1; its boundary lines will also be treated below, rather than included among possible exceptional zeros.
For Res>1, the absolutely convergent reciprocal Euler product gives ζ(s)=0. For Res<0, the factors 2s, πs−1, Γ(1−s) and ζ(1−s) in the second functional equation of the Riemann zeta function are finite and nonzero. Hence the only zeros there are the zeros of the sine factor:
s=−2,−4,−6,….​
(26)
Each is simple. At zero, the sine zero cancels the pole of ζ(1−s); using its residue one gives ζ(0)=−1/2, not zero. Nonvanishing on the rest of Res=0 follows from the boundary-line proof below and the functional equation of the Riemann zeta function. Together these facts prove that the trivial zeros of the Riemann zeta function are the only zeros outside the open critical strip.
Here is a Jensen disk proof of the zeta zero-count bound which avoids any unproved left-half-plane growth estimate. Set F(s)=(s−1)ζ(s), a holomorphic function throughout Res>0, including at one. The integral continuation formula gives
∣F(s)∣≪(∣s∣+1)2(Res≥1/4).
(27)
For each integer j, apply Jensen's formula in the disk with centre 2+ij, outer radius R=7/4 and inner radius r=8/5. The outer disk stays in Res≥1/4, so its maximum modulus is O((∣j∣+2)2). At its centre,
∣ζ(2+ij)∣≥ζ(2)−1,∣F(2+ij)∣≥∣1+ij∣/ζ(2),
(28)
since ∣1/ζ(2+ij)∣≤∑n​∣μ(n)∣n−2≤ζ(2). Therefore the number of zeros in its inner disk, counted with multiplicity, is at most
log(R/r)log(max∣s−(2+ij)∣≤R​∣F(s)∣/∣F(2+ij)∣)​≪log(∣j∣+2).
(29)
The rectangle 1/2≤Res≤1, ∣Ims−j∣≤1/2 fits in the inner disk because its farthest point has distance (3/2)2+(1/2)2​=5/2​<8/5. Summing over O(T) such disks counts O(TlogT) zeros in the right half of the critical strip up to height T. The first functional equation of the Riemann zeta function bijects zeros, with multiplicities, in the left half with their reflections s↦1−s in the right half; its Gamma function factors are finite and nonzero in the strip. Thus
#{ρ:ζ(ρ)=0, 0<Reρ<1, ∣Imρ∣≤T}≪TlogT.​
(30)
The pole of zeta function at one does not count as a zero: F(1)=1.
Finally, for real σ>1 and t=0, the Euler-product logarithms and the nonnegative trigonometric polynomial
3+4cosθ+cos(2θ)=2(1+cosθ)2≥0
(31)
give the three-four-one product proof of zeta boundary nonvanishing:
log(ζ(σ)3∣ζ(σ+it)∣4∣ζ(σ+2it)∣)=∑p,k≥1​kpkσ3+4cos(ktlogp)+cos(2ktlogp)​≥0.
(32)
If ζ(1+it) had a zero of order a≥1, its factor would be O((σ−1)4a) as σ↓1. The real zeta function factor has pole order three, and the factor at 1+2it stays bounded because t=0. The product would tend to zero as O((σ−1)4a−3), contradicting that it is at least one. Therefore
ζ(1+it)=0(t=0).​
(33)
At t=0 there is a pole, not a zero. Applying the sine-form functional equation of the Riemann zeta function at s=it=0 now proves nonvanishing on the remaining imaginary axis, completing the earlier assertion about all zeros outside the critical strip.
