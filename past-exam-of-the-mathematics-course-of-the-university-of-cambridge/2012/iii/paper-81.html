Let L/K be an extension of number fields, let α∈OL​ be a primitive element of a field extension, and let f∈OK​[X] be its monic minimal polynomial of an algebraic element. Put R=OK​[α] and S=OL​. The Kummer-Dedekind theorem applies at a nonzero prime ideal p of OK​ when Rp​=Sp​: equivalently, p is outside the support of the finite-index quotient S/R. Write the factorization over the residue field k=OK​/p as
f​=∏j=1r​g​jmj​​,
(1)
with distinct monic irreducible polynomials g​j​, and choose monic lifts gj​∈OK​[X]. Then
pS=j=1∏r​Pjmj​​,Pj​=(p,gj​(α)),f(Pj​/p)=degg​j​.​
(2)
Here f(Pj​/p) denotes the residue-field degree, and the ramification index of a prime ideal is mj​.
To prove this, the local index condition gives
S/pS≃R/pR≃k[X]/(f​)≃∏j=1r​k[X]/(g​jmj​​).
(3)
The final isomorphism is the Chinese remainder theorem. Each factor is a local ring with unique maximal ideal generated by g​j​, so its inverse image in S is exactly Pj​, and its residue field is k[X]/(g​j​). This proves the list of primes and the residue-field degrees. For the exponents, localize at Pj​. The ring of integers of a number field is a Dedekind domain, so SPj​​ is a discrete valuation ring. If pSPj​​=Pjej​​SPj​​, the quotient has ej​ successive layers isomorphic to S/Pj​, and hence k-dimension ej​degg​j​. The corresponding polynomial factor has k-dimension mj​degg​j​. Thus ej​=mj​, completing the proof rather than merely identifying the radicals.
For the calculation, set K=Q(5​) and t=(5​−1)/2. The ring of integers of a quadratic field gives OK​=Z[t], with t2+t−1=0. The algebraic norm of 4+5​ is 11, so its principal ideal has ideal norm 11. Its quotient ring has prime order and is therefore the finite field F11​; consequently (4+5​) is a prime ideal. In that residue field, 5​=−4=7 and t=3.
Let L=Q(ζ5​). Since t=ζ5​+ζ5−1​ lies in Z[ζ5​], the permitted identity OL​=Z[ζ5​] gives OL​=OK​[ζ5​]. Thus the local index condition holds at every prime. The relative minimal polynomial of an algebraic element is X2−tX+1, whose reduction is
X2−3X+1=(X−5)(X−9)in F11​[X].
(4)
The roots are distinct. Applying the Kummer-Dedekind theorem gives
(4+5​)OL​=(4+5​,ζ5​−5)(4+5​,ζ5​−9)=(11,ζ5​−5)(11,ζ5​−9).​
(5)
For the last equality, evaluation at 5 or 9 in F11​ gives ζ5​+ζ5−1​=3 and therefore 4+5​=5+2t=0. Both descriptions are the same kernels of these evaluation maps. Each prime has ideal norm 11, residue-field degree one and ramification index of a prime ideal one.

Let F/K be a finite Galois extension, choose a prime ideal q above p, and write kq​ and kp​ for the residue fields. The decomposition group and inertia group are
Dq​={σ∈Gal(F/K):σq=q},Iq​=ker(Dq​⟶Gal(kq​/kp​)).​
(6)
The Frobenius automorphism on the finite field kq​ is x↦xNp. Its inverse image defines a distinguished coset in Dq​/Iq​; a Frobenius element is a lift of that coset. At an unramified prime, Iq​=1 and the lift is unique. Changing the prime above p conjugates it, so a Frobenius conjugacy class is intrinsically attached to p. These are arithmetic Frobenius conventions.

Write e=e(q/p), f=f(q/p) and g for the number of primes above p. The Galois group acts transitively on those primes, with stabilizer Dq​. The fundamental identity for prime decomposition and the orbit-stabilizer theorem therefore give
[F:K]=efg,∣Dq​∣=[F:K]/g=ef.
(7)
It remains to establish that the residue action has image of order f, rather than assume this in the desired equality.
Use the decomposition group of a prime and local Galois group identification Dq​=Gal(Fq​/Kp​). A generator of the finite field extension kq​/kp​ has a separable monic minimal polynomial of an algebraic element. Lift its coefficients to OKp​​. The Hensel lemma lifts each of its distinct residue roots to Fq​. One such lift has field degree at most f, while its residue generates the degree-f finite field extension, so its field degree is exactly f and its ramification index of a prime ideal is one. It therefore generates an unramified extension of degree f, and sending it to each of the other lifts gives its f embeddings. Because Fq​/Kp​ is a Galois extension, these embeddings extend to its automorphisms. Their reductions realize every automorphism of kq​/kp​. Thus the residue action is surjective, and
∣Iq​∣=∣Dq​∣/f=e.​
(8)
Now let G be cyclic of order ℓ2, and let H=Gal(F/L) be its unique subgroup of order ℓ. In a tower of Galois extensions, inertia maps onto inertia in the intermediate extension. This also follows from the just-proved order formula: its image has order e(F/K)/e(F/L)=e(L/K), using multiplicativity of the ramification index of a prime ideal in a tower. If p ramifies in L/K, this image in G/H is nontrivial. Every proper subgroup of G is contained in H, so inertia upstairs must be G. Hence
e(F/K)=ℓ2,p is totally ramified in F/K.​
(9)
The fundamental identity for prime decomposition then also forces a single prime above p and residue-field degree one.

For a direct check of unramified behavior, the cyclotomic polynomial Φ25​ divides X25−1. Modulo 7, the latter has derivative 25X24=4X24 and has no common root with that derivative. Thus Φ25​ is separable, so 7 does not divide its polynomial discriminant. The field discriminant divides this polynomial discriminant, and therefore 7 is unramified in Q(ζ25​). The Frobenius automorphism satisfies
Frob7​(ζ25​)=ζ257​.​
(10)
Indeed its residue action is seventh powering. The reduced 25th roots of unity are distinct because 7∤25, so the residue congruence identifies the exponent uniquely modulo 25.
The Galois group is (Z/25Z)×, of order φ(25)=20. Since 72=49≡−1(mod25), 74≡1 but 72≡1. Consequently the Frobenius automorphism has order four. An unramified decomposition group is generated by that element, and the orbit-stabilizer theorem gives
g=20/4=5,f=4,e=1.​
(11)
There are therefore five primes above 7, each with residue field F74​.

For a number field K and a modulus of a number field m=m0​m∞​, let IK​(m) be the group of fractional ideals prime to m0​, and let PK,1​(m) consist of principal fractional ideals (a) with a≡1 at the finite modulus and a>0 at the real places in m∞​. The ray class group is Clm​(K)=IK​(m)/PK,1​(m).
The Main theorem of class field theory states that there is a unique finite abelian ray class field Km​/K for which the Artin reciprocity map induces
Clm​(K)≃Gal(Km​/K).​
(12)
It is unramified outside the finite modulus and has no ramification at real places omitted from m∞​. More generally, subgroups H with PK,1​(m)⊆H⊆IK​(m) correspond, with inclusions reversed, to intermediate abelian fields, with Galois group IK​(m)/H. Every finite abelian extension occurs for some modulus. Equivalently, Km​ is the maximal abelian extension with conductor of an abelian extension dividing m. This states both reciprocity and the existence theorem of global class field theory.

There is no infinite component in the modulus (5). The ordinary ideal class group of Q is trivial, and its unit group is {±1}. The ray class exact sequence therefore gives
Cl(5)​(Q)≃(Z/5Z)×/{±1}≃C2​.​
(13)
Concretely, a fractional ideal prime to 5 has a rational generator r=a/b with 5∤ab. Reducing r modulo 5 is well-defined up to multiplication by the units ±1. Its ray class is trivial precisely when one of the two generators r,−r is congruent to one. Thus the quotient has the two classes {1,4} and {2,3}. No positivity restriction is imposed on a generator.
To identify the corresponding field, put E=Q(ζ5​+ζ5−1​)=Q(5​). The maximal real subfield of the fifth cyclotomic field is fixed by complex conjugation, the element −1 of (Z/5Z)×. For a prime ℓ=5, the Frobenius automorphism of Q(ζ5​)/Q is ζ5​↦ζ5ℓ​. Its restriction to E thus corresponds exactly to the residue class of ℓ modulo {±1}. Multiplicativity extends this to the Artin reciprocity map on all fractional ideals prime to 5, whose kernel is exactly PQ,1​((5)). Its field discriminant 5 also confirms that E is unramified outside 5; moreover E is a totally real number field. The uniqueness in the Main theorem of class field theory now gives
Q(5)​=Q(5​).​
(14)
Including the real place changes the ray class group: a principal generator must then be positive as well as congruent to one. The residue and signature group of a modulus gives
Cl(5)∞​(Q)≃((Z/5Z)××{±1})/⟨(−1,−1)⟩≃(Z/5Z)×.
(15)
Here the isomorphism sends (u,ε) to εu. Consequently Q(5)∞​=Q(ζ5​), of degree four. The finite modulus gives the real quadratic field; the modulus with the real place gives the full cyclotomic field. The printed finite-modulus assertion needs no correction.

Set K=Q(pq​) and E=Q(p​,q​). The distinct primes give independent square classes, so [E:Q]=4 and [E:K]=2. In particular K(p​)=K(q​)=E: these are the same quadratic extension of K. We prove it is unramified by an explicit field discriminant calculation.
Since p,q≡1(mod4), put a=(1+p​)/2, b=(1+q​)/2. Both are algebraic integers. The ring R=Z[a,b]⊆OE​ has basis 1,a,b,ab. Up to the ordering of the basis, its trace pairing matrix is the tensor product of the quadratic trace matrices, because the four embeddings of E choose the two radical signs independently. The two quadratic matrices are
Tp​=(21​1(p+1)/2​),Tq​=(21​1(q+1)/2​),detTp​=p,detTq​=q.
(16)
Thus
disc(R)=det(Tp​⊗Tq​)=p2q2.
(17)
On the other hand, pq is squarefree and congruent to one modulo four, so dK​=pq. Let j=[OE​:R]. The discriminant-index formula for an integral lattice and the relative discriminant tower formula give
j2p2q2​=dE​=dK[E:K]​NK/Q​(dE/K​)=p2q2NK/Q​(dE/K​).
(18)
The ideal norm on the right is a positive integer, while j is a positive integer. Hence N(dE/K​)=1/j2 forces j=1 and dE/K​=OK​. Its prime divisors are exactly the ramified finite primes, so E/K is unramified at every finite prime, including primes above 2,p,q. This also proves dE​=p2q2, agreeing with the discriminant of a biquadratic field formula.
Every embedding of E is real, so the real places of K split as well. The extension is quadratic, hence abelian. The ordinary Hilbert class field characterization therefore implies
Q(p​,q​)⊆HK​.​
(19)
Finally, the Artin reciprocity map identifies Cl(K) with Gal(HK​/K). Restriction onto Gal(E/K)≃C2​ is surjective, so the ideal class group has a quotient of order two. Therefore ∣Cl(K)∣ is even​.

We prove the Dirichlet density form of the Chebotarev density theorem. Let L/K be a finite Galois extension with Galois group G, let C be a conjugacy class in G, and let SC​ consist of the unramified prime ideals of K whose Frobenius conjugacy class is C. The assertion is
δ(SC​)=s↓1lim​log(1/(s−1))p∈SC​∑​(Np)−s​=∣G∣∣C∣​.​
(20)
The same result holds for any union of conjugacy classes, by addition. The following proof uses exactly the allowed one-dimensional Artin L-function behavior at 1 and the permitted induction and Euler-product properties.
First determine the order of vanishing at 1 for an arbitrary character of a representation χ of G. The Brauer induction theorem supplies subgroups Hj​≤G, linear characters ψj​ of Hj​, and integers nj​, possibly negative, such that
χ=∑j​nj​IndHj​G​ψj​.
(21)
Put Kj​=LHj​. Compatibility of Artin L-functions with induced representations and direct sums gives, initially for Res>1,
LK​(χ,s)=∏j​LKj​​(ψj​,s)nj​.
(22)
Each ψj​ is a one-dimensional Galois representation for L/Kj​, so the stated analytic assumptions apply to every factor. A trivial ψj​ contributes a simple pole; a nontrivial ψj​ is holomorphic and nonzero at one. Thus the product is meromorphic near one and
ords=1​LK​(χ,s)=−∑j​nj​⟨ψj​,1Hj​​⟩Hj​​.
(23)
The character inner product is ⟨α,β⟩G​=∣G∣−1∑g∈G​α(g)β(g)​. Applying Frobenius reciprocity to each induced term gives
ords=1​LK​(χ,s)=−j∑​nj​⟨IndHj​G​ψj​,1G​⟩G​=−⟨χ,1G​⟩G​.​
(24)
In particular, a nontrivial irreducible character has order zero, so its Artin L-function is holomorphic and nonzero near one. For the trivial character, LK​(1G​,s)=ζK​(s), the Dedekind zeta function of the base field K, and its pole is simple. This argument establishes the behavior needed at one, not holomorphy everywhere for arbitrary Artin L-functions.
Next convert that order calculation into a prime sum. For an irreducible character χ, choose a unitary representation of dimension dχ​ affording it, and choose a Frobenius automorphism gp​ at each unramified prime. The logarithm specified by the absolutely convergent Euler product gives, for real s>1,
logLK​(χ,s)=∑p unramified​∑m≥1​m(Np)msχ(gpm​)​+Rχ​(s).
(25)
Only finitely many ramified primes occur. Their Euler-factor logarithms Rχ​(s) stay bounded near one, since the relevant eigenvalues have modulus one and (Np)−1<1. Also ∣χ(g)∣≤dχ​, so the contribution with m≥2 is uniformly bounded for s≥1 by
∑p​∑m≥2​m(Np)msdχ​​≤2dχ​∑p​(Np)−2<∞.
(26)
Convergence follows, for example, from the convergent ideal series defining ζK​(2). Therefore
∑p unramified​(Np)sχ(gp​)​=logLK​(χ,s)+O(1).
(27)
Writing aχ​=⟨χ,1G​⟩G​, the order computation means LK​(χ,s)=(s−1)−aχ​Uχ​(s), where Uχ​ is holomorphic and nonzero near one. Choose its local logarithm. Along the real interval approaching one, this and the Euler-product logarithm differ only by a fixed multiple of 2πi. Consequently
p unramified∑​(Np)sχ(gp​)​=aχ​logs−11​+O(1).​
(28)
Only the trivial irreducible character has aχ​=1; all the others have aχ​=0.
Finally, character orthogonality makes the irreducible characters an orthonormal basis of class functions. For c∈C, the coefficient of χ in the class indicator is ⟨1C​,χ⟩G​=(∣C∣/∣G∣)χ(c)​. Hence
1C​(g)=∣G∣∣C∣​∑χ∈Irr(G)​χ(c)​χ(g).
(29)
Sum this identity over the unramified primes with weight (Np)−s. The finite character sum and the boxed estimates give
p∈SC​∑​(Np)−s=∣G∣∣C∣​logs−11​+O(1).​
(30)
Dividing by log(1/(s−1)) proves the asserted Dirichlet density. The same Euler-product calculation with ζK​ gives ∑p​(Np)−s=log(1/(s−1))+O(1), so using all primes as the denominator is equivalent. Finitely many ramified primes do not affect either limit. No prime-counting asymptotic or natural-density Tauberian step is required by this proof.
