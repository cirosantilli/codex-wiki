First, equality of two U0​ values forces equality of the corresponding V0​ values: neither strict comparison holds for U0​, and the same is then true for V0​ in both directions. Thus V0​=g∘U0​ for a well-defined strictly increasing function g on J=U0​(P). Since P is a convex set and U0​,V0​ are affine functions, J is a real interval and
g(pu+(1−p)v)=pg(u)+(1−p)g(v),u,v∈J,0≤p≤1.
(4)
If J contains u<v, let a=[g(v)−g(u)]/(v−u)>0 and b=g(u)−au. The mixture identity gives g(w)=aw+b for w∈[u,v]. For w>v, express v as a convex combination of u and w and solve the same identity for g(w); for w<u, express u as a mixture of w and v. Hence the formula holds on all of J, not merely between the chosen anchors. We obtain positive affine uniqueness of affine preference representations:
V0​(λ)=aU0​(λ)+bfor every λ∈P,a>0.​
(5)
If U0​ is constant, the same comparisons force V0​ to be constant; choose a=1 and the appropriate b. If the probability-measure set is empty, the assertion is vacuous. These degenerate cases do not require distinct anchors.
