Past exam of the mathematics course of the University of Cambridge / 2012 / iii / Paper 25 / 2 / a / Solution

 ... Past exam of the mathematics course of the University of Cambridge 2012 iii Paper 25 2 a
OurBigBook.com Words: 120
Let D:J→C be a finite diagram in a category. Form the finite products in a category
P=∏j∈ObJ​D(j),Q=∏a:j→k in J​D(k).
(2)
Define s,t:P⇉Q by πa​s=D(a)πj​ and πa​t=πk​. Take their equalizer e:L→P. The maps λj​=πj​e satisfy D(a)λj​=λk​, so they constitute a categorical cone.
Any other categorical cone xj​:X→D(j) gives a unique x:X→P by the product in a category property. Its cone equations say sx=tx, so the equalizer gives a unique xˉ:X→L with exˉ=x. The projections then give λj​xˉ=xj​, and uniqueness follows from both universal properties. Thus this equalizer of two product maps is the finite limit of the diagram in a category.
The empty indexing category is included: its empty product in a category is a terminal object, which is the empty categorical limit. Hence no nonemptiness restriction has entered the construction.
