For the covariant version of the Yoneda embedding, put Y(A)=C(A,−). A morphism u:A→B induces the natural transformation Y(u):Y(B)→Y(A) whose component sends v:B→X to vu. Thus Y is a functor from the opposite category Cop to the functor category [C,Set]. The locally small category hypothesis makes each value a set.
The Yoneda lemma says that, for a functor F:C→Set, evaluation at the identity morphism gives a natural bijection
Nat(C(A,−),F)≅F(A),θ⟼θA​(1A​).​
(1)
Given a∈F(A), define θXa​(v)=F(v)(a). For w:X→X′, the functor law gives F(w)θXa​(v)=F(wv)(a)=θX′a​(wv), proving naturality. Conversely, naturality of θ at v:A→X gives θX​(v)=F(v)θA​(1A​). These two constructions are inverse, because F(1A​)(a)=a.
The bijection is natural in both variables. A natural transformation β:F→H sends the element a to βA​(a), matching postcomposition by β. For u:A→B, precomposing with Y(u) sends θ to a transformation from Y(B) and its distinguished element is θB​(u)=F(u)(a). This is exactly the required dependence on A.
Taking F=Y(B) gives Nat(Y(A),Y(B))≅C(B,A). Consequently the covariant Yoneda embedding is full and faithful, with the reversal of arrows accounted for by its opposite category domain.
