Suppose every component αA​ is a monomorphism in the Category of sets, hence an injective function. If β,γ:H→F are natural transformations with αβ=αγ, then αA​βA​=αA​γA​ at every object. Componentwise cancellation gives βA​=γA​, so β=γ. Thus α is a monomorphism in the functor category.
Conversely, suppose α is a monomorphism and x,y∈F(A) satisfy αA​(x)=αA​(y). The Yoneda lemma supplies natural transformations θx,θy:Y(A)→F. Its naturality in F identifies αθx and αθy with the equal elements αA​(x) and αA​(y). Cancellation by α gives θx=θy; evaluating at 1A​ gives x=y. Therefore a natural transformation is a monomorphism exactly when all its components are injective functions. This is the pointwise monomorphism in a set-valued functor category criterion.
