Past exam of the mathematics course of the University of Cambridge / 2012 / iii / Paper 25 / 8 / a / Solution

 ... Past exam of the mathematics course of the University of Cambridge 2012 iii Paper 25 8 a
OurBigBook.com Words: 202
Take any morphism h:A→B and let c:B→C be its categorical cokernel. Let i:I→B be the categorical kernel of c. Since ch=0, kernel universality gives e:A→I with h=ie. The map i is a monomorphism. We must establish the pseudo-epimorphism property of e, rather than merely name I an image.
Suppose t:I→Z satisfies te=0. Let k:K→I be its categorical kernel. Then e=ka for some a:A→K. The composite ik is a monomorphism, so the hypothesis that every monomorphism is normal provides a map q:B→D of which ik is a categorical kernel, up to its unique compatible isomorphism. Now qh=qika=0. The categorical cokernel property of c gives q=qˉ​c, and therefore qi=qˉ​ci=0.
Since ik is the categorical kernel of q, the map i factors through it: i=ikℓ for some ℓ:I→K. Cancel the monomorphism i to get kℓ=1I​. As k is also a monomorphism, ℓk=1K​, so k is an isomorphism. Finally t=tkℓ=0, proving that e is a pseudo-epimorphism.
Thus
h=ie,i=ker(cokerh),e pseudo-epic,i monic.​
(21)
The pseudo-epimorphism factorization through the kernel of a cokernel proof uses a pointed category, existence of categorical kernels and categorical cokernels, and normality of every monic map. It does not silently replace pseudo-epic cancellation against zero by the stronger epimorphism condition.
