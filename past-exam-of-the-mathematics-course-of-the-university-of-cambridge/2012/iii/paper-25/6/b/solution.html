Past exam of the mathematics course of the University of Cambridge / 2012 / iii / Paper 25 / 6 / b / Solution

 ... Past exam of the mathematics course of the University of Cambridge 2012 iii Paper 25 6 b
OurBigBook.com Words: 151
Define the forgetful functor GT(A,a)=A, acting as the identity on underlying morphisms. Define the free algebra functor by
FT(X)=(TX,μX​),FT(h)=T(h).
(13)
The monad laws make μX​ an algebra for a monad structure, and naturality of μ makes T(h) an monad algebra morphism.
For (A,a), define the transpose maps
Φ(h)=hηX​,Ψ(k)=aT(k),Φ:CT(FTX,(A,a))⟶C(X,A).
(14)
The map Ψ(k) is an monad algebra morphism, because naturality of μ and the algebra associativity law give
aT(k)μX​=aμA​T2(k)=aT(a)T2(k)=aT(aT(k)).
(15)
For k:X→A, naturality of η gives ΦΨ(k)=aT(k)ηX​=aηA​k=k. Conversely, if h is an monad algebra morphism, then
ΨΦ(h)=aT(h)TηX​=hμX​TηX​=h.
(16)
Precomposing by a map into X or postcomposing by an monad algebra morphism respects both formulas. Thus the bijection is natural and FT⊣GT. This is the free-forgetful Eilenberg-Moore adjunction.
Its adjunction unit is the given ηX​; its adjunction counit at (A,a) is the monad algebra morphism a:(TA,μA​)→(A,a). Hence GTFT=T, and the induced multiplication is the underlying counit at (TX,μX​), namely μX​. Therefore this adjunction induces exactly the original monad, including its unit and multiplication of a monad.
