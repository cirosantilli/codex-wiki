A weakly initial set is a set of objects (Wi​)i∈I​ such that every object receives at least one map from some Wi​. An initial object is itself a singleton weakly initial set. We prove the converse without replacing uniqueness by weak initiality.
Let E be a complete category that is locally small. Form W=∏i​Wi​. For every object A, choose Wi​→A and compose with the projection W→Wi​, so W is weakly initial. Completeness supplies a terminal object, so the given weakly initial set cannot be empty.
The endomorphisms of W form a set. Take a simultaneous equalizer e:I→W of all of them with 1W​; equivalently, equalize the two maps from W into ∏s∈E(W,W)​W with components s and 1W​. Thus se=e for every endomorphism s. The object I is weakly initial, since Ie​W→A exists for every A. Weak initiality of W gives a map u:W→I. Applying the equalizing identity to eu yields eue=e, so monicity of e gives ue=1I​.
For any endomorphism v:I→I, the map evu is an endomorphism of W. Therefore evue=e. Since ue=1I​, this becomes ev=e, giving v=1I​. Now let f,g:I⇉A and form their equalizer j:E→I. Weak initiality of I gives h:I→E. The composite jh is an endomorphism of I, hence the identity. Consequently f=fjh=gjh=g.
There is at least one map from I to every object, and the argument proves that there is at most one. The object I is an initial object. This establishes the initial-object lemma for complete categories with a weakly initial set using only small products in a category and equalizers.
