Past exam of the mathematics course of the University of Cambridge / 2012 / iii / Paper 25 / 3 / a / Solution

 ... Past exam of the mathematics course of the University of Cambridge 2012 iii Paper 25 3 a
OurBigBook.com Words: 175
A monomorphism m:A→B satisfies mu=mv⇒u=v for every parallel pair into A. An epimorphism e:X→Y satisfies ue=ve⇒u=v for every parallel pair out of Y. A strong monomorphism is a monomorphism with the following lifting property: whenever e:X→Y is an epimorphism and mu=ve, with u:X→A and v:Y→B, there is a unique d:Y→A with de=u and md=v. A regular monomorphism is a morphism occurring as the equalizer of two parallel maps. A balanced category is one in which every morphism that is both a monomorphism and an epimorphism is an isomorphism.
Suppose m:A→B equalizes p,q:B⇉D. If mu=mv, the common composite has two factorizations through the equalizer, so its uniqueness property gives u=v. Hence every regular monomorphism is indeed a monomorphism.
Now take the lifting square just described. The equations pmu=qmu imply pve=qve. Cancel the epimorphism e to obtain pv=qv. The equalizer gives a unique d with md=v. Since mde=ve=mu and m is a monomorphism, de=u. Thus every regular monomorphism is a strong monomorphism; the existence of the lift uses epic cancellation, and its uniqueness uses the equalizer property.
