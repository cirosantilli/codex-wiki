Identify a configuration with its set S of open edges. Let k(S) count the connected components of a graph on the full graph vertex set, including isolated graph vertices. The random-cluster measure is
μp,q​(S)=Zp,q​1​p∣S∣(1−p)∣E∣−∣S∣qk(S).
(26)
For p∈(0,1) and finite q≥1 all its weights are positive. We compare 1≤q1​≤q2​, taking μ1​=μp,q1​​ and μ2​=μp,q2​​.
The key fact is supermodularity of graph component count:
k(S∩T)+k(S∪T)≥k(S)+k(T).
(27)
To prove it, orient the graph edges arbitrarily and let US​ be the real span of their incidence vectors for edges in S. Its dimension of a vector space is r(S)=∣V∣−k(S): on each connected component of a graph these vectors span the vectors whose coordinates sum to zero. Since US∪T​=US​+UT​ and US∩T​⊂US​∩UT​, the dimension formula for a sum of subspaces gives
r(S∪T)+r(S∩T)≤r(S)+r(T),
(28)
which is exactly the component-count inequality.
Put a=k(S)−k(S∪T)≥0 and b=k(S∩T)−k(T)≥0. The preceding inequality gives b≥a. In the ratio of the two sides of Holley's condition, the normalization constants and Bernoulli edge factors cancel, because ∣S∪T∣+∣S∩T∣=∣S∣+∣T∣. The ratio is therefore
μ1​(S)μ2​(T)μ1​(S∪T)μ2​(S∩T)​=q1−a​q2b​=(q1​q2​​)aq2b−a​≥1.
(29)
By the Holley inequality, μp,q1​​ stochastically dominates μp,q2​​. Thus increasing cluster weight suppresses every increasing event:
μp,q1​​(A)≥μp,q2​​(A)(q1​≤q2​).​
(30)
The random-cluster single-edge conditional probability confirms the mechanism: it is p when the endpoints are already connected, and p/(p+q(1−p)) otherwise. Larger q favours configurations with more separate components. The condition q≥1 enters explicitly in the last factor q2b−a​.
