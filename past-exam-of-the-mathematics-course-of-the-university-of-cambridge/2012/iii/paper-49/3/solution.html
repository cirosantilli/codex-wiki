For a uniform source h, perform a momentum-shell renormalization group step: separate slow modes ∣p∣<Λ/b and fast modes Λ/b<∣p∣<Λ. In a Gaussian field theory they decouple; the uniform source couples only to the retained zero mode. Integrating fast modes contributes only a field-independent determinant to the free energy. The change of variables p=κt​q proves this power because the rescaled integral converges at large q exactly when D<4. The two-dimensional free energy itself has a tlogt resonance, while its second derivative still has the indicated t−1 power.
The printed magnetization exponent is a formal Gaussian scaling index, not a proved ordered-phase law of the purely quadratic model. The ordered-phase obstruction in a Gaussian scalar model is explicit: the uniform field has energy density m2M2/2−hM. At h=0,m2<0 it tends to −∞ as ∣M∣→∞, and the partition function diverges. For m2>0, the mean is M=h/m2 and tends to zero as h→0, so there is no spontaneously ordered branch. Thus no literal M∼∣t∣β for t<0 can be established from this Statistical Hamiltonian alone. The value (D−2)/4 follows from the scaling dimension of the field if one assumes such a branch; for D≤2 its nonpositive value further warns against that interpretation. Adding a stabilizing quartic interaction defines an ordered phase, but below four dimensions that interaction is relevant and generally leads to the Wilson-Fisher fixed point, not these Gaussian thermodynamic powers.
For the Gaussian specific-heat infrared threshold, at D=4 the same determinant integral grows like log(Λ2/t), so α=0 as a power exponent, with a logarithmic divergence. For D>4, it has a finite cutoff-dependent limit; the leading heat capacity is a regular constant and has no divergent power, again described by α=0 in the requested convention. After subtraction of this background the Gaussian singular term can still have the negative power index 2−D/2; it is not correct to erase that distinction. In a stable quartic Landau-Ginzburg theory above four dimensions, the quartic coefficient is a dangerously irrelevant coupling: it is needed for the ordered phase and contributes fs​∝−t2/u, giving the mean-field specific-heat jump and α=0 while invalidating naive hyperscaling relation. At four dimensions, marginal interactions can add further logarithmic corrections. The agreement with Landau theory is in the leading heat-capacity power; it does not assert the absence of logarithms or supply a missing stable Gaussian ordered phase.
