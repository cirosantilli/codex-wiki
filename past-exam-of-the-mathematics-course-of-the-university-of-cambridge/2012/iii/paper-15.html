In cotangent bundle coordinates (q1,…,qn,p1​,…,pn​), the Liouville one-form and the canonical symplectic form are
α=∑i​pi​dqi,ω=−dα=∑i​dqi∧dpi​.
(1)
The twisted cotangent symplectic form is closed: pullback of a differential form commutes with the exterior derivative, so
dωσ​=dω+π∗(dσ)=0.
(2)
To prove the form is nondegenerate, write a tangent vector in these coordinates as (u,v) and test its pairing with an arbitrary vertical vector (0,w). The base differential two-form has zero pairing with vertical vectors, hence
ωσ​((u,v),(0,w))=uiwi​.
(3)
A vector in the kernel of a linear map therefore has u=0. Pairing it next with every (z,0) gives −zivi​=0, so v=0. Thus the kernel is zero at every point, independently of the size or rank of σ. Together with closedness, this proves ωσ​ is a symplectic form. The argument is local only for convenience; the nondegenerate property is intrinsic and the coordinate charts cover the whole cotangent bundle.

The differential one-form θ defines a smooth section of a vector bundle, and its one-form graph is an embedded copy of X because π∘θ=idX​. The defining property of the Liouville one-form gives θ∗α=θ. Consequently
θ∗ωσ​=−dθ+(π∘θ)∗σ=σ−dθ.
(4)
The one-form graph has dimension n, half the dimension of the cotangent bundle. It is therefore a Lagrangian submanifold precisely when this pullback of a differential form vanishes:
θ(X) is Lagrangian in (T∗X,ωσ​)⟺dθ=σ.​
(5)
This is the twisted Lagrangian graph criterion.
Now suppose the projection restricts to a diffeomorphism on a Lagrangian submanifold L. Its inverse followed by the inclusion defines a smooth section s:X→T∗X, hence a global differential one-form θ with image L. The criterion forces σ=dθ, so its class in de Rham cohomology is zero. Thus [σ]=0 rules out every such Lagrangian section. This cohomological obstruction to a Lagrangian section concerns graphs over the entire base; it does not claim that all Lagrangian submanifolds are absent.

Tβ∗​ωσ​=ω−π∗(dβ)+π∗σ=ω.​

Put ξt​=kerαt​, the contact distribution. If dimM=2r+1, the contact form condition is αt​∧(dαt​)r=0. It implies that dαt​∣ξt​​ is a symplectic form on each contact hyperplane: inserting a vector transverse to ξt​ in that volume form leaves the nonzero top exterior power (dαt​∣ξt​​)r. Hence there is a unique smooth time-dependent vector field Yt​∈ξt​ satisfying
(ιYt​​dαt​)∣ξt​​=−α˙t​∣ξt​​.
(9)
Its smoothness follows by inverting the smoothly varying nondegenerate matrix of this contact distribution symplectic form. This is the horizontal generator of contact stability.
Let Rt​ be the Reeb vector field of αt​. The one-form α˙t​+ιYt​​dαt​ vanishes on ξt​, so it is ht​αt​. Evaluating on Rt​, using αt​(Rt​)=1 and ιRt​​dαt​=0, identifies the coefficient:
ht​=α˙t​(Rt​),α˙t​+ιYt​​dαt​=ht​αt​.
(10)
By Cartan's magic formula, the Lie derivative of a differential form is
LYt​​αt​=ιYt​​dαt​+d(αt​(Yt​))=ιYt​​dαt​,
(11)
because Yt​ lies in the contact hyperplane.
Let ρt​ solve ∂t​ρt​=Yt​∘ρt​, with ρ0​=idM​. Since M is compact without boundary and Yt​ is smooth on the closed time interval, its solutions cannot escape and exist for the entire interval. Reversing the time-dependent equation supplies a smooth inverse for each ρt​. Thus these flow maps give a smooth isotopy. Differentiating the pullback of a differential form along this isotopy yields
dtd​(ρt∗​αt​)=ρt∗​(α˙t​+LYt​​αt​)=(ht​∘ρt​)ρt∗​αt​.
(12)
For each base point this is a scalar linear equation for a covector, initially α0​. Its solution is
ρt∗​αt​=ut​α0​,ut​(x)=exp(∫0t​hs​(ρs​(x))ds)>0.​
(13)
By finite-time flow completeness on a compact manifold, this construction gives the whole time interval. The contact conformal factor along an isotopy is smooth in (t,x) and nowhere zero, proving Gray stability theorem. The precise domains here are ρt​:M→M and ρ:M×[0,1]→M; the extra time factor attached to the already indexed ρt​ in the source is a notational slip. The proof produces the required family on its whole specified interval. If a parameter domain of all R is intended, extend the smooth field slightly beyond [0,1], multiply it by a time cutoff, and use compactness to obtain the extended isotopy ρ:M×R→M. Its restriction to [0,1] is unchanged.

We prove the Weinstein neighborhood theorem with the canonical sign convention ω0​=−dα. The essential first step is to match the two symplectic forms as bilinear forms on the entire tangent space along X, not only after pulling them back to TX.
Here is the needed symplectic splitting along a Lagrangian submanifold. Write E=TM∣X​ and L=TX. Choose a smooth vector bundle complement N of L, for example using a Riemannian metric. Since L is a Lagrangian subspace in every fiber, the pairing map
N⟶L∗,v⟼(u⟼ω(u,v))
(14)
is an isomorphism: its kernel is N∩Lω=N∩L=0, and both bundles have rank n. Let s:L∗→N be its inverse and put b(η,ζ)=ω(sη,sζ). Define a:L∗→L by
ζ(aη)=−21​b(η,ζ),
(15)
and set s=s+a. Nondegeneracy of the dual pairing gives a unique smooth a. Since b is alternating,
ω(sη,sζ)=b(η,ζ)−η(aζ)+ζ(aη)=0.
(16)
Thus K=s(L∗) is a smooth Lagrangian complement to L. The map
A:TX⊕T∗X⟶TM∣X​,A(u,η)=u+sη
(17)
is a vector bundle isomorphism, is the identity on TX, and satisfies
ω(A(u,η),A(v,ζ))=ζ(u)−η(v)=ω0​((u,η),(v,ζ)).
(18)
The last equality uses the canonical splitting of the tangent bundle of T∗X along its zero section.
Choose a Riemannian metric near X for which K is orthogonal to TX. The tubular neighborhood construction using its Riemannian exponential map gives a diffeomorphism
ψ:W⊂T∗X⟶W′⊂M,ψ(q,p)=expq​(sq​p),
(19)
after shrinking around the zero section. It restricts to the given inclusion of X and has differential A along it. Consequently β=ψ∗ω and ω0​ agree pointwise as ambient bilinear forms at every point of the zero section.
We use the following precise relative Moser theorem. If two closed symplectic forms γ0​,γ1​ near a compact embedded submanifold S agree as ambient bilinear forms along S, then, on smaller neighborhoods, there is a diffeomorphism κ fixing S pointwise with κ∗γ1​=γ0​. One sufficient version allows a symplectic path γt​ with ∂t​γt​=dηt​ and ηt​=0 as ambient covectors along S: solve ιZt​​γt​=−ηt​ and use its flow. The vector field vanishes on S, so the flow fixes S, and compactness permits a common smaller neighborhood for the full time interval.
For completeness, all hypotheses can be checked directly here. Put δ=β−ω0​ and γt​=ω0​+tδ. At the zero section, every γt​ equals ω0​. The nondegenerate property is open, and compactness of X×[0,1] allows a single smaller neighborhood on which all γt​ are symplectic forms. Choose it fiberwise star-shaped. Write Hs​(q,p)=(q,sp) and let E be the vertical radial vector field. The radial homotopy primitive near a zero section
η=∫01​s−1Hs∗​(ιE​δ)ds
(20)
is smooth, is zero as an ambient covector along the zero section, and satisfies
dη=δ−H0∗​δ=δ.
(21)
The radial homotopy operator gives this identity, using dδ=0 and H0∗​δ=π∗i0∗​δ=0. Smoothness at s=0 follows because contraction with E supplies a factor of s after evaluation at (q,sp); the vanishing of δ along X supplies additional vanishing. Therefore solve ιZt​​γt​=−η. By Cartan's magic formula, its flow maps κt​ satisfy
dtd​(κt∗​γt​)=κt∗​(δ+d(ιZt​​γt​))=0.
(22)
After shrinking the starting neighborhood, these flows exist for 0≤t≤1 and fix X. This is a relative local construction, not an assertion of completeness throughout the noncompact cotangent bundle.
Finally set φ=ψ∘κ1​, let U0​ be its sufficiently small domain, and let U=φ(U0​). Then
φ∗ω=κ1∗​β=ω0​,φ∘i0​=i.​
(23)
Thus the map is a symplectomorphism of neighborhoods and agrees with the specified inclusion at every point of X.

First observe that the Liouville one-form is zero as an ambient covector exactly on the zero section. Indeed, α(x,p)​=p∘dπ, and dπ is surjective. Since dg is invertible, g∗α=α therefore implies that g maps the zero section onto itself. It induces a diffeomorphism f:X→X defined by g(x,0)=(f(x),0).
The map g is a symplectomorphism, since it preserves −dα. Let E=∑i​pi​∂pi​​ be the vertical Liouville vector field. With the chosen sign convention,
ιE​ω=−α.
(26)
Preservation of both ω and α forces g∗​E=E because ω is nondegenerate. Its complete cotangent fiber-dilation flow is
Ds​(x,p)=(x,esp),s∈R.
(27)
Uniqueness of integral curves gives g∘Ds​=Ds​∘g. If g(x,p)=(y,P), let s→−∞. Continuity and this commutation yield
(f(x),0)=g(x,0)=lims→−∞​g(x,esp)=lims→−∞​(y,esP)=(y,0).
(28)
Thus y=f(x) for every p: the whole cotangent fiber over x maps into the fiber over f(x). This step proves that g covers f; it was not assumed.
Now write g(x,p)=(f(x),P(x,p)). For any u∈Tx​X, choose a tangent vector to T∗X projecting to u. Evaluating g∗α=α on it gives
P(x,p)(dfx​u)=p(u).
(29)
Since dfx​ is invertible, P(x,p)=p∘(dfx​)−1. Therefore
g=f#​,f=π∘g∘i0​,​
(30)
and f is unique. This rigidity of cotangent Liouville-form preservation uses smoothness at the zero section; it follows from the canonical form and its dilation dynamics on the entire cotangent bundle.

For any positive-dimensional X, choose a smooth function h with dh not identically zero. A nonconstant smooth bump in a coordinate chart supplies one on an arbitrary smooth manifold. Translate by this exact differential form:
Tdh​(x,p)=(x,p+dhx​).
(31)
The cotangent fiber translation identities give
Tdh∗​ω=ω,Tdh∗​α=α+π∗(dh)=α.​
(32)
Thus a nontrivial exact fiber translation is a symplectomorphism that fails to preserve the Liouville one-form. For example, on T∗R one can take T(q,p)=(q,p+1): it preserves dq∧dp but changes pdq to (p+1)dq.
If dimension zero is admitted, T∗X=X and α=0, so every diffeomorphism preserves α and this requested distinction is impossible. The example therefore applies in positive dimension, as required for a nonzero differential one-form.
