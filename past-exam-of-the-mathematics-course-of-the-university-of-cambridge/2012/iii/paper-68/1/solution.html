Use Dj​=−i∂xj​​ and ⟨ξ⟩=(1+∣ξ∣2)1/2, so the Fourier transform of P(D)u is P(ξ)u(ξ). Write PN​ for the homogeneous degree-N part. An elliptic differential operator has PN​(ξ)=0 for every real ξ=0. By continuity on the unit sphere, m=min∣ω∣=1​∣PN​(ω)∣>0. The lower-degree terms are bounded by C∣ξ∣N−1 for ∣ξ∣≥1, whence
∣P(ξ)∣≥m∣ξ∣N−C∣ξ∣N−1≥2m​∣ξ∣N≥c⟨ξ⟩N
(1)
for sufficiently large ∣ξ∣. This proves the high-frequency lower bound for an elliptic polynomial. Degree zero just means a nonzero constant and has no derivative gain.
With the Fourier convention in Question 3, the Sobolev space is
Hs(Rn)={u∈S′:⟨ξ⟩su∈L2},∥u∥Hs2​=(2π)−n∫⟨ξ⟩2s∣u(ξ)∣2dξ.​
(2)
The condition includes that the weighted transform is represented by an L2 function. For open X, the Local Sobolev space consists of u∈D′(X) for which χu, extended by zero, belongs to Hs(Rn) for every χ∈Cc∞​(X). We use the elementary Sobolev multiplication by a smooth cutoff fact for every real s. It follows from Fourier convolution with the rapidly decreasing χ​, the weighted inequality ⟨ξ⟩s≲⟨ξ−η⟩∣s∣⟨η⟩s, and the L1∗L2→L2 convolution bound.
For a compactly supported distribution, continuity on test functions supported in a fixed compact neighborhood gives finite order: for some integer M,
∣⟨u,φ⟩∣≤Cmax∣α∣≤M​supK​∣∂αφ∣.
(3)
Insert a compact smooth cutoff function equal to one near the support, times e−ix⋅ξ. The resulting Fourier transform is smooth and satisfies ∣u(ξ)∣≤C′⟨ξ⟩M. The distribution is also tempered by this same finite-order bound. Thus
u∈Ht(Rn)whenever t<−M−n/2.​
(4)
Indeed the square of the weighted bound is integrable exactly when 2(t+M)<−n. This is negative Sobolev regularity of a compactly supported distribution.
We next prove local regularity by a high-frequency reciprocal parametrix kernel, including its off-diagonal smoothing property. Choose ψ∈Cc∞​(Rn) equal to one on a ball containing all real zeros of P, and put b(ξ)=(1−ψ(ξ))/P(ξ), defining it smoothly as zero in the inner ball. Differentiation of the reciprocal and the elliptic lower bound give
∣∂ξα​b(ξ)∣≤Cα​⟨ξ⟩−N−∣α∣.
(5)
The Fourier multiplier E=b(D) maps Hs to Hs+N by its zeroth-order bound, and
EP(D)=P(D)E=1−R,R=ψ(D).
(6)
The kernel K=F−1b is smooth away from the origin: for x=0, repeatedly integrate by parts using eix⋅ξ=(i∣x∣2)−1x⋅∂ξ​eix⋅ξ. After sufficiently many integrations, the differentiated symbol is integrable. For any desired x derivative, repeat the argument with the additional polynomial ξβ. A large-radius cutoff function justifies each step and its removal uniformly on compact sets away from zero. Thus convolution by K carries a compactly supported distribution to a smooth function at points separated from its support. Also R carries a compactly supported distribution to a Schwartz function, because ψu is smooth with compact support.
For a target compact set in X, choose χ∈Cc∞​(X) equal to one on a neighborhood of it. Then χu and [P(D),χ]u have compact support, and the commutator is supported where derivatives of χ occur, away from the target. The parametrix identity gives
χu=E[χP(D)u]+E[[P(D),χ]u]+R(χu).
(7)
The first term is in Hs+N since χP(D)u∈Hs. The other two terms are smooth near the target by the proved kernel property. Since the target was arbitrary,
P(D)u∈Hlocs​(X)⟹u∈Hlocs+N​(X).​
(8)
This proof does not discard the cutoff function commutator; it places its support away from the set where regularity is sought.
For the final polynomial, select a multi-index α0​ with ∣α0​∣=N and ∂α0​Q a nonzero constant. The derivative-ratio hypothesis immediately gives ∣Q(ξ)∣≥c∣ξ∣δN at large frequency. In particular Q has no real zero there. For N>0, its degree bound also forces δ≤1. Differentiating 1/Q gives products of ratios ∂βQ/Q, whose total derivative order is ∣α∣. Hence the corresponding high-frequency reciprocal satisfies
∣∂ξα​bQ​(ξ)∣≤Cα​⟨ξ⟩−δN−δ∣α∣.
(9)
Its multiplier maps Hs to Hs+δN. Its kernel is still smooth off zero: each frequency integration by parts now lowers the order by δ, so more iterations may be needed, but δ>0 supplies arbitrarily much decay. The same separated-support commutator identity applies without a change. The derivative-ratio Sobolev gain for a polynomial operator is therefore
Q(D)u∈Hlocs​(X)⟹u∈Hlocs+δN​(X).​
(10)
Simply estimating the commutator by its differential order would lose this sharper gain; the off-diagonal kernel argument uses the full derivative-ratio hypothesis.
