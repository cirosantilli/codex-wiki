The singular support of a distribution is the complement of the largest open set on which it is represented by a smooth function. For the support-sensitive bound one must use conic support of an oscillatory amplitude: a closed set of limiting high-frequency directions, locally in the base variable. If suppa(x,⋅) literally means the support of the restricted function, the printed inclusion can fail. There is a slice-support obstruction for oscillatory singularities. In one dimension, take Φ=xθ and a=xη(θ)/∣θ∣, where η is smooth and even, zero for ∣θ∣≤1, and one for ∣θ∣≥2. This is a symbol of order −1, and the full phase differential is nonzero for θ=0. Its regularized integral near x=0 is
I(x)=2x∫0∞​η(θ)θcos(xθ)​dθ=−2xlog∣x∣+a smooth function.
(17)
Indeed substitute u=∣x∣θ in the tail, split at u=1, and write cosu=1+(cosu−1) below one. The first term gives −log∣x∣, while the remainder has an even convergent power series in x plus a constant; the bounded-frequency correction is smooth. Thus 0 is in the singular support, but a(0,⋅)=0 has empty slice support. The joint closed conic support retains this limiting base point and makes the proved theorem valid. No nonstationarity conclusion is inferred just from the amplitude vanishing at one base point.
