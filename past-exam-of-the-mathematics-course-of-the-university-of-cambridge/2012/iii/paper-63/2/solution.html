Write u=cosθ, S=j/κ and use axial symmetry. The radiative flux is F=2π∫−11​uIdu. Integrating the radiative transfer equation over directions gives
dτdF​=4π(J−S).
(15)
There are no energy sources in the atmosphere, so radiative equilibrium makes F constant. Consequently
J=S=κj​=πσT4​.​
(16)
For the linear angular approximation I=A+Cu, the radiation-field moments are
J=A,F=34π​C,cPr​=2π∫−11​u2(A+Cu)du=34π​A.
(17)
Thus it satisfies the Eddington closure approximation, and
cPr​=34π​J,C=4π3F​.​
(18)
Since F is constant, C′=0. Substitution in the radiative transfer equation, using S=A, gives uA′=Cu, hence A′=C​.
In this moment approximation, the inward hemispheric flux at the surface is
Fin​=−2π∫−10​u(A0​+Cu)du=πA0​−32π​C.
(19)
Setting this boundary moment to zero gives A0​=2C/3. Therefore
A(τ)=4π3F​(τ+32​).
(20)
Combining it with the thermal source function and F=σTe4​ gives the grey atmosphere temperature law:
T4=43​Te4​(τ+32​),T0​=2−1/4Te​.​
(21)
The surface condition is an approximate angular-moment condition. If the affine intensity were interpreted as an exact nonnegative boundary radiation field, no incident radiation would require I(0,u)=0 for every u<0; a linear polynomial vanishing on that interval would have A0​=C=0 and no outward flux either. The derived formal profile indeed has I(0,−1)=−F/(4π)<0. This positivity limitation of a linear Eddington intensity is why one uses the Eddington surface boundary condition as a moment closure, not the affine ansatz as an exact emergent angular intensity. An outward direction-independent intensity with no inward intensity gives the same boundary moments.
For the gas-pressure-dominated atmosphere, combine dP/dz=−ρg with dτ/dz=−κρ. Thus the hydrostatic equilibrium in optical depth is dP/dτ=g/κ. Let w=T4. The grey atmosphere law implies dw/dτ=3T04​/2. With the specified power-law opacity,
dwd(Pα)​=3κ0​T04​2αg​wβ−1.
(22)
At the outer vacuum limit take P=0 and T=T0​. Since α,β>0, integration gives
Pα=3βκ0​T04​2αg​(T4β−T04β​).​
(23)
This is the grey-atmosphere pressure with power-law opacity relation.
At the matching radius the local radiative flux is Lr​/(4πr2)=σT4. In the thin plane-parallel atmosphere approximation this equals F=σTe4​, so T=Te​ and T4=2T04​. Multiplying the pressure relation by κ0​T4−4β/g yields the required surface pressure-opacity relation
gPκ​=3β2α​T04​T4​[1−(TT0​​)4β]=3β4α​(1−2−β).​
(24)
Here Pκ is a product, not a pressure subscript.
