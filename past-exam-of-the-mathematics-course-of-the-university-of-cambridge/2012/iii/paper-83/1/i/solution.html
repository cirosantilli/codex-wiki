Here the power set notation gives xEy⟺P(x)⊆y. Suppose a nonempty set A has no E-minimal member. Form its intersection b=⋂A. This is a set: for any one a0​∈A, use the axiom schema of separation inside a0​ to define the elements common to all members of A. Selecting a single witness to nonemptiness is not an application of the axiom of choice.
For an arbitrary y∈A, lack of minimality supplies some x∈A with P(x)⊆y. Since b⊆x, every subset of b is a subset of x, so
P(b)⊆P(x)⊆y.
(1)
This conclusion holds for each y∈A, without choosing all the corresponding x simultaneously. Intersecting over y gives P(b)⊆b.
For completeness, the contradiction in Cantor's theorem is completely explicit here. By the axiom schema of separation put d={u∈b:u∈/u}. Then d∈P(b)⊆b, and consequently
d∈d⟺d∈/d,
(2)
which is impossible. Thus no such A exists:
E is well-founded, without Choice or Foundation.​
(3)
This power-set-predecessor relation proof uses neither a descending sequence nor a comparison of possibly non-well-orderable cardinalities. It proves well-foundedness directly from the minimal-member definition.
