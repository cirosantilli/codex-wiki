Past exam of the mathematics course of the University of Cambridge / 2012 / iii / Paper 83 / 1 / ii / Solution

 ... Past exam of the mathematics course of the University of Cambridge 2012 iii Paper 83 1 ii
OurBigBook.com Words: 204
Order the collection Ch(P) of every chain in a partial order, including the empty chain, by inclusion. Suppose that j:Ch(P)→P is an order-preserving function and an injection. Let θ=h(P) be the Hartogs ordinal: by Hartogs theorem, no injection from θ to P exists.
Use transfinite recursion to define, for α<θ,
Cα​={pβ​:β<α},pα​=j(Cα​).
(4)
We verify both that j(Cα​) is defined and that the values are strictly increasing. Assume inductively that the earlier values are strictly increasing. Then Cα​ is a chain in a partial order. For any β<α, Cβ​⊆Cα​, so the fact that j is order-preserving gives pβ​≤pα​. Furthermore pβ​∈Cα​, whereas pβ​∈/Cβ​ by distinctness of the earlier values. Thus Cβ​=Cα​, and injectivity gives pβ​=pα​. We have proved
β<α⟹pβ​<pα​.
(5)
The empty initial chain starts the induction, and the same argument applies at every limit ordinal.
If a globally defined recursion rule is desired, apply j when the earlier range is a chain and otherwise use the fixed value j(∅). The induction shows that the fallback case never occurs. Thus no choices of new points are being made: the function j uniquely determines every stage.
The resulting function α↦pα​ is an injection from θ to P, contrary to its defining Hartogs ordinal property. Hence the chain-poset nonembedding lemma gives
No order-preserving injection Ch(P)⟶P exists.​
(6)
