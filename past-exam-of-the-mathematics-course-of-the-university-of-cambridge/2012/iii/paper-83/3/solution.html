Past exam of the mathematics course of the University of Cambridge / 2012 / iii / Paper 83 / 3 / Solution

Put κ=∣X∣, an infinite cardinal number. The original PDF has the double exponent 22∣X∣; the single-exponent expression in the TeX aid is a transcription error.
We explicitly construct an independent family of subsets with 2κ members on a set of size κ. Let
D={(s,H):s∈[κ]<ω, H⊆P(s)}.
(16)
For each finite s there are finitely many possibilities for H. The collection of finite subsets of an infinite cardinal has size κ, so ∣D∣≤κ; the points ({ξ},∅) give the reverse bound. Thus ∣D∣=κ.
For every B⊆κ define
AB​={(s,H)∈D:B∩s∈H}.
(17)
To verify independence, take distinct B1​,…,Bn​ and bits ε1​,…,εn​. For each pair i<j, choose a point of Bi​△Bj​ and let s contain all these finitely many points. The traces Bi​∩s are pairwise distinct. Set
H={Bi​∩s:εi​=1}.
(18)
Then (s,H)∈ABi​​ exactly for those i with εi​=1. Every finite Boolean pattern is therefore realized. The cases n=0,1 work by the same definition, allowing s=∅. In particular the sets AB​ are all distinct, so the finite-trace independent family construction gives exactly 2κ independent members.
For each function ε:P(κ)→{0,1}, prescribe AB​ when ε(B)=1 and D∖AB​ when ε(B)=0. Independence gives the finite intersection property. Their finite intersections generate a proper filter on a set: its members are the subsets of D containing one of those intersections.
To extend this to an ultrafilter, order its proper filter extensions by inclusion. The union of a chain is again a proper filter, so Zorn's lemma supplies a maximal one, Uε​. If neither a subset Y nor its complement belonged to this filter, adjoining Y would still generate a proper filter: otherwise some existing member would be disjoint from Y and force its complement into the filter already. Maximality therefore gives the ultrafilter dichotomy. Using the ambient axiom of choice, choose such an extension for every ε.
Distinct functions differ at some B. Their ultrafilters respectively contain AB​ and its complement, and cannot be equal. There are 22κ such functions. Transporting the ultrafilters along a bijection D→X proves the lower bound. The upper bound is immediate because every ultrafilter is a subset of P(X):
22κ≤∣Ult(X)∣≤∣P(P(X))∣=22κ.
(19)
Thus the number of ultrafilters on an infinite set is
∣Ult(X)∣=22∣X∣.​
(20)
