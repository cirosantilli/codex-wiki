Past exam of the mathematics course of the University of Cambridge / 2012 / iii / Paper 83 / 4 / Solution

The maximum is 2ℵ1​. We construct that many aleph-one-like linear orders, whose proper initial segments are countable, and prove that they are nonisomorphic. As usual the rational-type requirement concerns nonempty proper initial segments; the empty segment occurs in every order.
Let D be the club set of nonzero limit ordinals below ω1​. For S⊆D, form the order sum
LS​=∑α<ω1​​Bα​,Bα​={1+Q,Q,​α∈S,α∈/S.​
(21)
Here 1+Q means a new least point followed by a copy of the rational numbers; distinct blocks are disjoint, and all points of an earlier block precede every point of a later one. Every block is countable, dense and has no greatest point. Since 0∈/S, the full total order has neither a least nor a greatest point. It has size ℵ1​.
The sum is dense: within a block use density, and for points in different blocks choose a larger point in the earlier block. If I is a proper initial segment and y∈/I lies in block α, then I is contained in the union of the blocks through α. There are only countably many such blocks, each countable. Thus I is countable, rather than an uncountable interval in an aleph-one-dense order.
Every nonempty I is dense and has no least point. If it has no greatest point either, the back-and-forth method gives I≅Q: enumerate both orders, alternately include the least unused element from either enumeration in a finite partial order isomorphism, and choose its partner in the appropriate open interval. Density and absence of endpoints always supply a new partner. The union is a bijective order isomorphism. If I has a greatest point, remove it, apply the same argument, and reattach it. Hence every nonempty proper initial segment is isomorphic to Q or Q+1.
The stationary information is recorded at the limit cuts. Write
FαS​=⋃β<α​Bβ​.
(22)
This is a continuous increasing kappa-filtration by countable initial segments. At a limit δ, the complement LS​∖FδS​ has a least point exactly when δ∈S.
Suppose f:LS​→LT​ is an order isomorphism. Since each filtration stage is countable, its image under f and the corresponding image under f−1 are bounded in block index: the supremum of their countable collection of countable ordinals remains below ω1​. Choose g(α)<ω1​ so that
f[FαS​]⊆Fg(α)T​,f−1[FαT​]⊆Fg(α)S​.
(23)
The set
C={δ∈D:(∀α<δ) g(α)<δ}
(24)
is a club set. To see unboundedness, start above any given ordinal and recursively choose γn+1​>γn​ larger than every g(α) for α≤γn​. The supremum of the countable sequence remains below ω1​ and belongs to C. Closure follows because below a limit of members of C, each fixed α lies below one of those members.
For δ∈C, continuity of the filtrations gives both inclusions needed for
f[FδS​]=FδT​.
(25)
The isomorphism therefore preserves whether the complementary final segment has a least point. Consequently
δ∈S⟺δ∈T(δ∈C).
(26)
This club agreement of countable filtrations proves the invariant: if S△T is stationary, the orders cannot be isomorphic.
We also construct enough pairwise different stationary encodings. For each β<ω1​, fix an injection eβ​:β→ω, and set
Aξ,n​={β∈D:ξ<β, eβ​(ξ)=n}.
(27)
For fixed ξ, these sets partition the stationary tail D∖(ξ+1). At least one Aξ,n​ is stationary: otherwise choose a club avoiding each one and intersect the countably many clubs to obtain a club avoiding the entire tail. The countable-intersection fact follows directly: above any starting point choose an increasing sequence, visiting each club infinitely often; its countable supremum belongs to every club by closure. For each ξ let n(ξ) be the least such n. One value n∗​ occurs for uncountably many ξ, since a countable union of countable sets is countable. For that fixed n∗​ the cells are pairwise disjoint, by injectivity of eβ​. Enumerating those cells gives
(Sη​:η<ω1​),
(28)
a family of pairwise disjoint stationary subsets of D. This is the explicit Ulam matrix on omega-one proof of disjoint stationary subsets of omega-one.
For A⊆ω1​ put S(A)=⋃η∈A​Sη​. If A=A′, their symmetric difference contains an entire Sη​ and is stationary. The stationary encoding in an aleph-one-like dense order therefore produces 2ℵ1​ pairwise nonisomorphic orders LS(A)​.
Finally, on a fixed carrier of size ℵ1​, every total order is a subset of its Cartesian square. That square has size ℵ1​, so there are at most 2ℵ1​ possible relations. Thus
2ℵ1​ pairwise nonisomorphic orders, the maximum possible.​
(29)
