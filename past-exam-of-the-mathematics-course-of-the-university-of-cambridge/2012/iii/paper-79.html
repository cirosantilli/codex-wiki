Use the horizontal streamfunction convention (u,v)=(−ψy​,ψx​), and set a=π/L, kd2​=f02​/(gH0​). Geostrophic balance gives f0​uˉ=−gηˉ​y​, hence
ηˉ​(y)=ηc​+gaf0​U0​​cosay,ψˉ​=f0​gηˉ​​=ψc​+aU0​​cosay.​
(1)
The constant is fixed by the total volume or the reference surface height. To obtain shallow-water quasi-geostrophic potential vorticity, require a small Rossby number, nearly geostrophic horizontal motion and ∣η∣/H0​≪1. Expanding the exact shallow-water potential vorticity gives
H0​+ηζ+f0​​=H0​f0​​+H0​1​(ζ−H0​f0​η​)+higher orders.
(2)
At leading nontrivial order ζ=∇h2​ψ, so after removing the constant background and multiplying by H0​,
q=∇h2​ψ−kd2​ψ,qt​+J(ψ,q)=0,J(A,B)=Ax​By​−Ay​Bx​.​
(3)
The mean potential vorticity and its gradient are
qˉ​=−aU0​​(a2+kd2​)cosay−kd2​ψc​,qˉ​y​=(a2+kd2​)uˉ.​
(4)
With ψ=ψˉ​+ψ′ and q=qˉ​+q′, expand the Jacobian. Since both mean fields depend only on y, their Jacobian vanishes. The two mixed terms are uˉqx′​ and v′qˉ​y​, giving
qt′​+J(ψ′,q′)+uˉqx′​+v′qˉ​y​=0,q′=(∇h2​−kd2​)ψ′.​
(5)
The PDF has this correct mean-advection term; the TeX aid incorrectly inserts a division by ∂x.
Drop the quadratic perturbation Jacobian. Freeze uˉ and qˉ​y​ locally for a short-wave plane wave, with K2=k2+l2 and D=K2+kd2​. Then q′=−Dψ′, v′=ikψ′, and the local Rossby-wave dispersion relation in a zonal jet is
ω=kuˉ−Dkqˉ​y​​=kuˉK2+kd2​K2−a2​.​
(6)
The intrinsic phase moves against an eastward mean flow because ω−kuˉ=−kqˉ​y​/D.
The printed wavelength bound is not a consequence of these equations. The local short-wave assumption is K/a≫1, equivalently wavelength 2π/K≪2L; a particular cutoff L/2 is an optional scale-separation criterion, not a universal derived threshold. If one requires the ground-frame phase to follow the mean current, the dispersion relation only gives K>a, or wavelength less than 2L. For example K=2a,l=0 has wavelength L and a phase moving with the mean. More decisively, the stationary condition requested next fixes wavelength 2L, contradicting a bound L/2 if both are imposed on the same waves.
For nonzero zonal k and mean velocity, a ground-stationary wave satisfies
K=a=π/L.​
(7)
It has the background length scale, so its existence should not be justified solely by the short-wave approximation. It is nevertheless an exact stationary linear solution of the Rossby-wave equation for a sheared zonal current: writing ψ′=F(y)eikx gives uˉ[F′′−(k2+kd2​)F]+qˉ​y​F=0, which reduces to F′′+(a2−k2)F=0. Thus F=eily with k2+l2=a2 works globally. Indeed the total field then has q=−(a2+kd2​)ψ+ a constant, so its full Jacobian vanishes as well.
Differentiating the local dispersion relation gives
cg​=(uˉ[1−Da2+kd2​​+D22(a2+kd2​)k2​],D22uˉ(a2+kd2​)kl​).
(8)
On the stationary circle this becomes
cg​=K2+kd2​2uˉk​(k,l).​
(9)
For a purely zonal wavevector, l=0, this is the printed expression cg​=2uˉK2x^/(K2+kd2​). With l=0 the printed expression needs a directional qualification; the general vector is the one above. Here stationarity means ω=0 in the ground frame, not vanishing intrinsic frequency in the moving fluid.
A stationary obstacle can therefore anchor the phase pattern while wave energy travels away from it. The zonal group component has the mean-flow sign, so an eastward jet carries the stationary wave response downstream; tilted wavevectors also carry energy meridionally. This is the usual stationary Rossby wave wake distinction between fixed phase crests and propagating wave energy.

Let f(y) denote the signed vertical component of the Coriolis parameter; it is negative in the southern hemisphere. In the steady, linear, inviscid interior, depth integration of horizontal momentum gives
fz^×U=−∇h​Π+τw/ρ0​,
(10)
where U=∫−H0​(u,v)dz and Π=∫−H0​pdz/ρ0​. Horizontal curl eliminates the pressure. Its left side is f∇h​⋅U+βV. Incompressibility and impermeable flat top and bottom imply ∇h​⋅U=w(−H)−w(0)=0, giving
βV=(∇h​×τw)z​/ρ0​.​
(11)
This is Sverdrup balance. It assumes steady small-amplitude motion, a Boussinesq approximation, negligible nonlinear advection, negligible Rayleigh and bottom/lateral drag in the interior, a flat fixed-depth domain and no normal top/bottom flux. The applied surface wind stress remains a forcing, even though interior friction is neglected. With cooling absent, the steady buoyancy equation additionally requires Bz​=0, if that equation is imposed; the curl balance itself does not depend on the cooling coefficient. In the closed, x-independent channel below, depth-integrated V=0, so a nonzero wind-stress curl cannot be balanced by an inviscid interior alone.
Steady forced channel. Now take r>0,α>0. Put a=π/L, s=(z+h)+​=max(z+h,0) and write τ0​ for the positive wind-stress amplitude. The prescribed profiles give
b=αBz​​=αhB0​​cosay(−h<z<0),b=0(−H<z<−h),
(12)
F=τx,z​/ρ0​=ρ0​h22τ0​s​sinay.
(13)
The buoyancy jumps across the sharp forcing interface in this simplified model; pressure and the circulation below are continuous. Let P=py​/ρ0​, D=f2+r2, and bytop​=−aB0​sinay/(αh). Hydrostatic balance gives P=C(y)+bytop​s. The horizontal momentum equations and their solution are
ru−fv=F,fu+rv=−P,u=DrF−fP​,v=D−fF−rP​.
(14)
The no-normal-flow conditions imply ∫−H0​vdz=0: this integral is independent of y by incompressibility and is zero at the meridional walls. It determines the otherwise free depth-uniform pressure gradient,
C(y)=−ρ0​rHfτ0​​sinay−2Hh2​bytop​.
(15)
Define
A(y)=f(y)2+r2−f(y)τ0​/ρ0​+raB0​h/(2α)​,G(z)=Hz+H​−h2s2​.
(16)
A meridional overturning streamfunction satisfying v=−Ψz​, w=Ψy​ is
Ψ(y,z)=A(y)sinayG(z),v=A(y)sinay(h22s​−H1​),w=[A(y)sinay]y​G(z).​
(17)
It vanishes on all four boundaries. Together with the expressions for b,P,u, this solves all the steady equations. The formula permits variable f(y); freezing it at a negative f0​ gives the simple schematic w=aAcosayG(z). No unspecified latitude dependence is silently needed for the sketch.
Since A>0, upper-layer meridional transport is northward and the deep return is southward. At constant f, the circulation rises on the southern side and sinks on the northern side. The meridional velocity changes sign at z=−h+h2/(2H), slightly above the forcing interface, not exactly at −h. The required schematic follows from this derived field:
Forcing comparison. The buoyancy and wind contributions have the same vertical shape and, with these signs, the same circulation sense. Their ratio is
AW​AB​​=αr​2Lπh​∣f∣τ0​/ρ0​B0​​≃2π​10−4≃1.6×10−4.​
(18)
The printed scaling fτ0​/ρ0​≃B0​ must be understood in magnitude, since f<0 and both forcing amplitudes are positive. Surface confinement supplies the small factor h/L, making direct buoyancy forcing negligible in this circulation model.
The missing physical mechanism in the stratified Southern Ocean is eddy-induced overturning. The wind-driven mean Deacon cell tilts isopycnals and builds available potential energy; baroclinic instability produces mesoscale eddies whose buoyancy transport largely opposes the mean overturning. The residual circulation depends on the small net balance and the diabatic buoyancy transformation. Wind energy also enters mean kinetic energy; eddies redistribute momentum through form stress and transfer energy toward dissipative scales and bottom friction. The displayed linear model already balances its wind input by imposed drag, but omits this mean-advection/eddy buoyancy budget, so the necessity of an opposing eddy circulation is a physical extension, not another mathematical constraint on the four given equations.

Define the positive total shear stress by τ=ρ[νUz​−u′w′], so the physical wall traction on the fluid opposes the positive flow. The steady mean momentum equation gives
0=−Px​+τz​,τ(z)=τS​+Px​z.​
(19)
For a driving pressure gradient Px​<0, the stress decreases upward. It is approximately constant while
z≪δτ​=τS​/∣Px​∣,u∗​=τS​/ρ​.​
(20)
An outer boundary-layer height can impose a stricter limit. In the turbulent region outside the viscous or roughness sublayer, the only local neutral scales are u∗​ and z, so Uz​=Cu∗​/z. Conventionally write C=1/κ, where κ is the von Kármán constant. Equivalently, a mixing length ℓ=κz and stress closure u∗2​=ℓ2Uz2​ give the same result. Integration yields the law of the wall
U(z)=κu∗​​logz0​z​.​
(21)
The roughness length z0​ is an extrapolation scale, not a statement that the logarithm is valid at the actual solid surface. For a smooth boundary, the viscous inner scales are z+=zu∗​/ν, U+=U/u∗​. The viscous sublayer has U+≃z+; farther out U+=κ−1logz++Cs​, corresponding to z0​=(ν/u∗​)e−κCs​. For a fully rough boundary, z0​ is proportional to a geometric roughness height and viscosity no longer determines the leading profile. The logarithmic region still needs roughness height ≪z≪δτ​.
In local equilibrium, production of turbulent kinetic energy by the shear is P=−u′w′Uz​≃u∗2​Uz​. Neglecting its transport relative to production and viscous dissipation,
ϵ≃κzu∗3​​.​
(22)
Dimensional similarity alone gives ϵ∝u∗3​/z; the displayed coefficient additionally uses the local energy balance and the logarithmic shear.

Let B0​=w′b′ be the vertical turbulent buoyancy flux, positive for heating from below. Its dimensions are length squared per time cubed. The stress velocity and height therefore admit a new dimensionless group B0​z/u∗3​. Define the signed Obukhov length
ℓO​=−κB0​u∗3​​,ζ=z/ℓO​.​
(23)
Stable cooling has B0​<0,ℓO​>0; unstable heating has B0​>0,ℓO​<0. Monin-Obukhov similarity theory expresses the mean shear as
dzdU​=κzu∗​​ϕm​(ζ),ϕm​(0)=1.​
(24)
The similarity hypothesis assumes approximately steady, horizontally homogeneous, constant-stress and constant-flux conditions, with the observation level outside the inner sublayer but below the outer-layer scales. Dimensional analysis fixes the arguments and prefactor, not the entire function ϕm​. A measured or modeled turbulence closure supplies its form.

For a rough boundary, integrate the similarity shear from the roughness length. A usual moderate-stability closure is
ϕm​(ζ)=1+cs​ζ(ζ≥0),ϕm​(ζ)=(1−cu​ζ)−1/4(ζ≤0),
(25)
with positive dimensionless coefficients. These are empirical closures, not quantities determined by similarity alone. An often-used choice is cs​=5,cu​=16. The unstable quarter-power relation is tested over approximately −1≲z/ℓO​≲−0.01 in Dyer's flux-gradient study; the stable coefficient convention is recorded in the surface-flux formulation. Near neutrality one takes their smooth limits.
For moderate stable conditions, integration gives
U(z)=κu∗​​[logz0​z​+cs​ℓO​z−z0​​].​
(26)
For the unstable closure set x(ζ)=(1−cu​ζ)1/4 and define
ψm​(ζ)=2log21+x​+log21+x2​−2arctanx+2π​.
(27)
Then 1−ζψm′​(ζ)=ϕm​(ζ) and
U(z)=κu∗​​[logz0​z​−ψm​(z/ℓO​)+ψm​(z0​/ℓO​)].​
(28)
For weak unstable departure, ∣cu​z/ℓO​∣≪1, this reduces to the stable-form expression with cs​ replaced by cu​/4 and a negative ℓO​. The full quarter-power expression is useful for moderately unstable conditions with ∣z/ℓO​∣ of order one; it must not be extrapolated automatically to the distinct asymptotic free-convection regime. The stable linear expression similarly describes a continuously turbulent surface layer, rather than guaranteeing validity after strong stratification destroys the constant-flux state. Both profiles require z0​≪z≪δτ​ and approximately uniform fluxes.

c OurBigBook.com
At fixed friction velocity, unstable heating gives B0​>0 and ζ<0. The usual similarity closure has ϕm​<1, so it reduces the mean velocity gradient relative to the neutral law of the wall. Rising warm and descending cool parcels mix momentum efficiently; less mean shear is needed to transport the same stress. Relative to a fixed near-wall reference, the speed difference aloft is reduced.
Stable cooling gives B0​<0,ζ>0 and ϕm​>1. The restoring buoyancy suppresses vertical parcel excursions and reduces the effective momentum diffusivity, requiring a steeper shear for the same stress. The speed difference from the wall reference is correspondingly larger. These are comparisons at prescribed stress; fixing an outer wind instead would allow u∗​ and the stress to change, so it would not be the same comparison.

Write S=Uz​ and N2=dbˉ/dz. The gradient Richardson number and Flux Richardson number are
Rig​=S2N2​,Rif​=−−u′w′Sw′b′​=−PB0​​.​
(29)
The first compares the restoring buoyancy gradient with mean shear; the second is the fraction of shear production consumed by stable buoyancy work. With this convention, both are positive for stable mixing and negative for unstable stratification. In local steady equilibrium, ϵ=P+B0​=P(1−Rif​), so a positive dissipation requires Rif​<1. The inviscid Miles–Howard theorem concerning Rig​≥1/4 is a linear stability criterion, not a universal equality for turbulent fluxes.
Use the gradient-diffusion closures −u′w′=Km​S and B0​=−Kb​N2. The turbulent Prandtl number is Prt​=Km​/Kb​, giving
Rif​=Km​Kb​​Rig​=Prt​Rig​​.​
(30)
Under the ideal Reynolds analogy Km​=Kb​, the two Richardson numbers coincide. It is an additional closure, not an automatic consequence of the definitions. If the buoyancy-gradient similarity function is defined by N2=−B0​ϕh​/(κzu∗​), then
Rig​=ζϕm2​ϕh​​,Rif​=ϕm​ζ​,Prt​=ϕm​ϕh​​.​

For lower-boundary heating, forced convection means that mean shear supplies most of the turbulence even though buoyancy assists it, approximately z≪∣ℓO​∣. Free convection means buoyancy dominates, approximately z≫∣ℓO​∣ within the constant-flux region. In this limit the local convective speed is
wc​∼(B0​z)1/3.​
(32)
It is the velocity whose eddy transport supplies the imposed vertical turbulent buoyancy flux. The associated diffusivity is Kb​=CK​wc​z=CK​B01/3​z4/3, with a dimensionless coefficient fixed by a closure. From B0​=−Kb​N2,
N2=−CK−1​B02/3​z−4/3,∣N∣=CK−1/2​B01/3​z−2/3.​
(33)
Here N2<0: there is no real restoring buoyancy frequency. The displayed magnitude is a local overturning/growth rate, or one may write N=i∣N∣. The Reynolds analogy makes Km​=Kb​ and hence Uz​∼u∗2​B0−1/3​z−4/3; the remaining mean shear is weak compared with the convective motion.
The turnover dissipation estimate gives
ϵ∼zwc3​​∼B0​,​
(34)
also consistent with the local energy equation ϵ=P+B0​ when P is negligible. Thus free-convection dissipation is height independent to leading scaling order, in contrast with neutral u∗3​/(κz). The constants and detailed profiles are not determined by dimensional analysis. These are local free-convection surface-layer scalings, not a claim that an entire deep convecting layer has a uniform unstable mean buoyancy gradient.

For very stable continuously turbulent conditions, z≫ℓO​>0, local eddies lose leading dependence on their distance from the wall. In very stable surface-layer similarity, the dimensionless functions must therefore scale as ϕm​∼cm​ζ and ϕh​∼ch​ζ, with positive closure constants. Using ℓO​=u∗3​/(κ∣B0​∣) gives
Uz​∼cm​u∗2​∣B0​∣​,U(z)−U(zr​)∼cm​u∗2​∣B0​∣​(z−zr​),N2∼ch​u∗4​∣B0​∣2​.​
(35)
Thus the mean velocity is approximately linear in height and the real buoyancy frequency is approximately constant, N∼ch​​∣B0​∣/u∗2​. Under the Reynolds analogy, ch​=cm​.
The local turbulent kinetic energy balance gives
ϵ=P−∣B0​∣∼(cm​−1)∣B0​∣,ℓϵ​∼ϵu∗3​​=(cm​−1)∣B0​∣u∗3​​.​
(36)
Positive dissipation requires cm​>1. The turbulent energy-turnover length is of order u∗3​/∣B0​∣, hence of order the Obukhov length, independent of observation height. A related buoyancy-limited length is u∗​/N. The Ozmidov length follows by equating an eddy turnover time ℓ2/3ϵ−1/3 with N−1:
ℓOz​=(ϵ/N3)1/2∼ch3/4​cm​−1​​∣B0​∣u∗3​​.
(37)
These are turbulent turnover or buoyancy-transition scales. If “the scale at which dissipation occurs” means the actual viscous cutoff, molecular viscosity is also required: the Kolmogorov microscales give
ηK​=(ν3/ϵ)1/4∼[(cm​−1)∣B0​∣ν3​]1/4.​
(38)
Without ν, the viscous length cannot be fixed by the supplied stress and flux alone. Separating it from the turbulent turnover length avoids confusing an energy-budget estimate with the molecular cutoff.
Finally,
Rif​⟶cm​1​,Rig​⟶cm2​ch​​.​
(39)
They become height-independent constants. The commonly used continuation cm​=ch​=5 gives Rif​=Rig​≃0.2 and ϵ≃4∣B0​∣. This numerical value is a closure choice, not a deduction from units; setting cm​=1 would incorrectly leave zero dissipation. Extremely stable intermittent or collapsed turbulence need not satisfy this local-equilibrium model. The relation between the Obukhov and buoyancy-dissipation lengths in the local regime is also analyzed in Grachev and colleagues' similarity study.

Use a top-hat plume model per unit span. Define volume and kinematic momentum fluxes Q=bw and J=bw2, and set the pure-ice reduced gravity g′=g(ρ0​−ρs​)/ρ0​>0. The suspension buoyancy is g′ϕ. The supplied equations express vertical momentum conservation, conservation of relative enthalpy including latent heat, and conservation of excess salt, respectively. At the release height, prescribe compatible source volume/momentum, salt and enthalpy fluxes, or equivalent source width, velocity, composition and ice fraction; impose ambient entrained water at (T0​,C0​) with no vertical momentum. A pure far-field similarity solution may instead be described using a virtual origin, but its singular near-origin values are not physical inlet conditions.
The missing balance is total mass, equivalently volume under the Boussinesq approximation. In a wall plume, only the exposed outer edge entrains, so the Batchelor entrainment hypothesis ve​=ew gives
Q′=ew.​
(40)
If two free edges are intended, replace e by 2e everywhere; the numerical coefficient is a geometry convention. The glacier boundary itself has no normal flow. Local phase conversion does not add volume at retained Boussinesq order, and crystals are assumed to share the liquid velocity.
For a box of height dz, the upward momentum flux changes by ρ0​[J(z+dz)−J(z)]. The ambient hydrostatic pressure has been subtracted; the remaining force is the suspension buoyancy ρ0​bg′ϕdz. Entrained ambient water carries no vertical momentum, and drag is neglected, giving J′=bg′ϕ. Salt entering through the exposed edge is C0​Q′dz, so (CˉQ)′=C0​Q′ and therefore
J′=bg′ϕ,[(Cˉ−C0​)Q]′=0.​
(41)
These control-volume derivations account for entrainment rather than treating Q as constant.
Thermodynamic elimination. Let the conserved excess enthalpy and salt fluxes be E and S:
E=[cp​(T−T0​)−Lϕ]Q,S=(Cˉ−C0​)Q.
(42)
The ambient satisfies T0​=−mC0​, while equilibrium in the ice-bearing plume gives
Cˉ=C0​+S/Q,T=−m1−ϕC0​+S/Q​.
(43)
Substitute this temperature in the enthalpy invariant. Exact rearrangement gives
ϕQ[L+cp​mC0​−Lϕ]=−E−cp​mS+Eϕ.​
(44)
Thus, at dilute-crystal order,
ϕQ≃I0​=L+cp​mC0​−E−cp​mS​,F=g′I0​.​
(45)
The ice-bearing wall-plume thermodynamic invariant fixes the leading advected ice volume and buoyancy flux from the source deficits. A rising ice-dominated branch requires I0​>0. It is not determined from reduced gravity alone. The small-ϕ expansion also presumes finite source fluxes and no cancellation making the discarded terms comparable to −E−cp​mS.
The three remaining leading equations are Q′=ew, (Qw)′=F/w, and ϕ=I0​/Q. Seek a similarity form in Z=z−zv​>0 with Q∝Zp, w∝Zq. Continuity gives p=q+1, while momentum gives p+2q=1. Therefore q=0,p=1. The coefficients obey ew3=F, giving the self-similar ice-bearing wall plume
b=eZ,w=(eg′I0​​)1/3,Q=e(eg′I0​​)1/3Z,ϕ=g′Zw2​=e2/3g′1/3ZI02/3​​.​
(46)
The width grows linearly, the speed approaches a constant, and the crystals dilute inversely with height. This is a buoyancy-conserving wall line plume, with buoyancy supplied mainly by the ice fraction rather than a temperature anomaly. The dilute-crystal regime requires Z≫w2/g′ and lies away from the formal virtual-origin singularity. If all source flux deficits vanish, I0​=0 and this nontrivial rising branch does not exist. The exact thermodynamic invariants above, rather than their truncated version, are needed near a concentrated source.
