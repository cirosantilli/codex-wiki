For adjoint-valued quantities write (X×Y)a​=εabc​Xb​Yc​. Substituting the infinitesimal transformation into the gauge field strength and using the Jacobi identity gives
δFμνa​=gεabc​Fμνb​λc​.​
(33)
A useful way to organize the calculation is [Dμ​,Dν​]X=gFμν​×X: the gauge field strength transforms covariantly because the gauge covariant derivative does. Thus δ(Faμν​Fμνa​)=2gFaμν​εabc​Fμνb​λc​=0 by antisymmetry. This proves gauge invariance of the Yang-Mills action.
The Yang-Mills equations are Dμ​Fμν=0. They determine physical evolution only modulo gauge redundancy: gauge transformations with arbitrary spacetime-dependent parameters take solutions to equivalent solutions. In Hamiltonian mechanics, A0​ has no independent quadratic time-derivative term, and the corresponding equation is a Gauss law constraint in gauge theory. A gauge parameter can vanish with its necessary derivatives on the initial slice yet change the later potential energy, so the potential energy itself is not uniquely fixed by initial physical data.
In the uncorrected path integral, integration along gauge orbits overcounts equivalent fields. At the perturbative level the quadratic operator of the kinetic term has gauge zero modes and no inverse, so it does not supply a quantum field theory propagator. Gauge fixing together with the Faddeev-Popov determinant removes this obstruction in the local perturbative construction.
For the odd BRST transformation, products obey the graded Leibniz rule. The adjoint product with Grassmann variables satisfies
X×Y=−(−1)∣X∣∣Y∣Y×X.
(34)
In particular, c×c is not zero, and c×Dc=Dc×c. The supplied transformations give
s2Aμ​=Dμ​(sc)+g(sAμ​)×c=−2g​Dμ​(c×c)+g(Dμ​c)×c=0,
(35)
since the even covariant derivative obeys D(c×c)=2(Dc)×c. For the ghost,
s2c=−2g​((sc)×c−c×(sc))=−g(sc)×c=2g2​(c×c)×c=0.
(36)
The final equality is the graded Jacobi identity; in components it is the Jacobi identity for εabc​ contracted with the totally antisymmetric product of three ghosts. Also s2cˉ=−sb=0 and s2b=0. The square of an odd derivation is an even derivation, because its two mixed product terms cancel. Hence s2=0 on every polynomial in the fields, off shell, without imposing field equations.
Let Ψ=∫ddx[∂μcˉa​Aμa​+ξcˉa​ba​/2] be the gauge-fixing fermion. Since sF=gF×c, sLYM​=0. The BRST-exact operator in Lq​=LYM​−sψ has zero variation by BRST nilpotence, so
sLq​=0.​
(37)
To see the resulting kinetic terms and signs, expand the exact term with scˉ=−b:
−sψ=(∂μba​)Aμa​+(∂μcˉa​)(Dμ​c)a​+2ξ​ba2​.
(38)
After integration by parts,
Lq​=LYM​−ba​∂μAμa​+2ξ​ba2​−cˉa​∂μ(Dμ​c)a​.
(39)
The auxiliary Nakanishi-Lautrup field b can be completed into a square and integrated out, giving the gauge-fixing term −(∂⋅Aa​)2/(2ξ) for ξ=0. At ξ=0, retain b as the multiplier enforcing the gauge condition rather than divide by ξ. The Grassmann Gaussian integral gives the determinant of the gauge-condition operator −∂⋅D. Field-independent normalizations cancel in the normalized integral; insertions involving b can be handled before integration or through its source terms.
At g=0 the gauge-fixed quadratic operator is invertible with the usual prescription, and the ghosts have a quadratic kinetic term. The remaining terms are cubic gauge interactions of order g, quartic gauge interactions of order g2, and a ghost-gauge interaction of order g. Expanding them and using Wick contractions gives a perturbative expansion for any defined polynomial insertion X of the fields. The exponent is the gauge-fixed action Sq​, as printed in the PDF; the converted TeX incorrectly substitutes SA​.
At ξ=1, the free gauge action, up to a boundary term, is 21​∫Aμa​□Aaμ​. Its Fourier kernel is −p2ημν​δab​. With the mostly-plus convention used above, the Feynman-gauge adjoint propagator is
⟨TAμa​(x)Aνb​(y)⟩0​=∫(2π)dddp​eip⋅(x−y)p2−iϵ−iημν​δab​​.​
(40)
This is consistent with the scalar-line convention in Question 2.
Finally define the odd functional B=21​∫ddxcˉa​ba​. Then ∂ξ​Sq​=−sB. Differentiating the normalized expectation, including its denominator, gives
∂ξ​⟨X⟩=−i[⟨XsB⟩−⟨X⟩⟨sB⟩]
(41)
for an insertion with no explicit ξ dependence. A physical gauge-invariant insertion X[A] is BRST-closed, since its infinitesimal gauge variation vanishes also when the parameter is replaced by c. The assumed BRST Ward identity gives ⟨sB⟩=0 and ⟨s(XB)⟩=0. For an even X with sX=0, the latter says ⟨XsB⟩=0. Therefore
∂ξ​⟨X⟩=0.​
(42)
The same conclusion holds for any BRST-closed insertion. Mere color-singlet invariance of an arbitrary ghost-dependent expression need not imply sX=0; the physical-observable qualification is essential. The proof uses the invariant-measure assumptions encoded in the stated Ward identity.
