Use the metric signature (−,+,…,+), so p2=−p02​+p2, and the weight eiS. The momentum-space Feynman rules for the massless Phi-fourth theory are: an internal scalar line contributes −i/(k2−iϵ); a quartic interaction vertex contributes −iλ with its momentum-conservation delta function; every independent loop is integrated with ddk/(2π)d; and each graph is multiplied by its Feynman-diagram symmetry factor. All external momenta can be taken incoming. These rules follow by expanding the interaction exponential and contracting free fields with the Wick theorem.
For a connected graph with I internal lines, V interaction vertices and L loops,
4V=2I+n,L=I−V+1,D=dL−2I=d+(d−4)V−2d−2​n.
(10)
Here D is the superficial degree of divergence. In four dimensions,
D=4−n.​
(11)
Odd n vanish by Z2 symmetry. Thus only the nonvacuum two-point and four-point one-particle-irreducible Feynman diagrams can have overall ultraviolet divergences: degree two for n=2, degree zero for n=4. Vacuum graphs with n=0 may also diverge but cancel from normalized correlators. This is an overall power-counting statement; subgraphs must be subtracted before using it to conclude finiteness for n≥6.
For the full one-particle-irreducible vertices, the tree-level quadratic kernel and quartic interaction give
τ2(0)​(p,−p)=−p2,τ4(0)​=−λ.​
(12)
If τ2​ is instead reserved for interaction-generated self-energy insertions, its tree value is zero and −p2 is displayed separately as the free inverse kernel. The distinction is only that convention; the loop insertions below are unchanged.
The requested one-loop diagrams are the two-point tadpole diagram and the three four-point bubble diagrams. Each has symmetry factor 1/2. The three bubble channels correspond to Ps​=p1​+p2​, Pt​=p1​+p3​, Pu​=p1​+p4​.
The tadpole insertion obeys
iτ2(1)​=2−iλ​∫(2π)dddk​k2−iϵ−i​.
(13)
In massless dimensional regularization this scaleless integral is zero. A mass or cutoff regulator exposes the ultraviolet divergence allowed by power counting; its vanishing in the scaleless convention does not remove the need to include this diagram.
For a bubble, the vertex and quantum field theory propagator factors give (−iλ)2(−i)2/2=λ2/2. The PDF defines f with the factor 1/i outside the Minkowski integral, so the integral itself is if(P2). Therefore
τ4​=−λ+2λ2​[f(Ps2​)+f(Pt2​)+f(Pu2​)]+O(λ3).​
(14)
The converted TeX misplaces this i as though it were part of the exponent of (2π); keeping the printed 1/i is essential to this real pole coefficient.

For example, represent the inverse square as ∫0∞​dααe−α(ℓ2+Δ), do the Gaussian momentum integral, and then use the Gamma function. This also derives the formula rather than assuming a loop-integration table. At d=4−ε, Γ(ε/2)=2/ε+O(1) and the parameter integral tends to one, giving

Take P2>0 before continuation back to Minkowski momenta. At P=0, a massless scaleless integral mixes ultraviolet and infrared issues, so it cannot be used to read off this pole.
Introduce a renormalization scale μ and a quartic counterterm. With λ denoting a dimensionless renormalized coupling constant,
λB​=με(λ+δλ),δλ=16π2ε3λ2​+O(λ3).
(17)
After factoring out με from the vertex, each loop contains μεfd​. The counterterm contributes −δλ to τ4​ and cancels the pole from all three channels. More explicitly,
μεfd​(P2)=16π21​[ε2​−γE​+log4π+2−logμ2P2​]+O(ε).
(18)
In modified minimal subtraction, replace the pole-only counterterm by
δλMS​=32π23λ2​(ε2​−γE​+log4π)+O(λ3),
(19)
which subtracts the first three terms inside the brackets. The finite renormalized quartic scalar vertex is then
τ4,R​=−λ+32π2λ2​r=s,t,u∑​[2−logμ2Pr2​​]+O(λ3).​
(20)
Another renormalization condition can change the finite constant, but not the pole cancellation or the momentum-dependent logarithms. The corresponding timelike expressions follow by the same Feynman continuation.
