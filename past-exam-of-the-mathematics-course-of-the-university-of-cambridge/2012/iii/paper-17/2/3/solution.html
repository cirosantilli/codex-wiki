Define the pointwise representation of one-forms by vector-field functionals by
θ(ω)(X)=ω(X).​
(16)
It is R-linear, its value is a smooth function, and Xp​=0 implies θ(ω)(X)(p)=0. Moreover
θ(gω)=gθ(ω),
(17)
so it is a map of C∞(M)-modules. This is the properly typed scalar-multiplication property: the same symbol for a form and its associated functional is understood through θ.
To construct its inverse, let α∈W and v∈Tp​M. Choose a global smooth vector field X with Xp​=v; a local coordinate field multiplied by a smooth bump function supplies such an extension. Set
ωp​(v)=α(X)(p).
(18)
This is well-defined: if Xp​=Yp​, then X−Y vanishes at p, so α(X−Y)(p)=0. Real linearity of α makes ωp​ a covector. In fact the pointwise vanishing assumption also forces
α(fX)(p)=f(p)α(X)(p),
(19)
because (f−f(p))X vanishes at p. Thus α(fX)=fα(X) for every smooth f, even though only real linearity was initially imposed.
It remains to prove smoothness, rather than assume continuity of an abstract linear map. Near a fixed point, choose global fields E1​,…,En​ equal to the coordinate basis on a smaller neighborhood, using a bump function equal to one there. On that neighborhood,
ω=∑i​α(Ei​)dxi.
(20)
All coefficients are smooth by the definition of W, so ω is a smooth differential one-form. The construction holds near every point, hence gives ω∈Ω1(M) with α(X)=ω(X) globally.
Every tangent vector can be realized by a global field, so θ(ω)=0 forces ω=0. The inverse just constructed gives surjectivity. The evaluation map is a natural C∞(M)-module isomorphism Ω1(M)≅W. No auxiliary metric or connection was chosen.
