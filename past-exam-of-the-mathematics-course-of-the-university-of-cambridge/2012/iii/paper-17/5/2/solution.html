The Levi-Civita connection is the unique connection on a vector bundle TM that is torsion-free and metric-compatible. These conditions are
∇X​Y−∇Y​X=[X,Y],X(g(Y,Z))=g(∇X​Y,Z)+g(Y,∇X​Z).
(49)
Existence and uniqueness follow from the Koszul formula; equivalently, these are the defining properties that characterize the connection.
Define the correction tensor
A(X,Y)=X(σ)Y+Y(σ)X−g(X,Y)Vσ​
(50)
and set DX​Y=∇X​Y+A(X,Y). All terms of A are C∞(M)-linear in both X and Y, so D is a connection. The symmetry A(X,Y)=A(Y,X) preserves vanishing of the torsion tensor.
To check compatibility with g​, compute
X(g​(Y,Z))=e2σ(2X(σ)g(Y,Z)+g(∇X​Y,Z)+g(Y,∇X​Z)).
(51)
On the other hand,
g(A(X,Y),Z)g(Y,A(X,Z))​=X(σ)g(Y,Z)+Y(σ)g(X,Z)−g(X,Y)Z(σ),=X(σ)g(Y,Z)+Z(σ)g(X,Y)−g(X,Z)Y(σ).​
(52)
The cross terms cancel, so their sum is 2X(σ)g(Y,Z). Multiplying by e2σ proves X(g​(Y,Z))=g​(DX​Y,Z)+g​(Y,DX​Z). Hence D is torsion-free and compatible with g​, so uniqueness of the Levi-Civita connection gives
∇X​Y=∇X​Y+X(σ)Y+Y(σ)X−g(X,Y)gradg​σ.​
(53)
This is the Levi-Civita connection under conformal rescaling.
On a nonempty compact manifold without boundary, σ attains a maximum at some p. The derivative of a smooth function vanishes at an interior extremum, so (dσ)p​=0 and (Vσ​)p​=0. The correction tensor therefore vanishes at p, giving
(∇X​Y)p​=(∇X​Y)p​for every X,Y.​
(54)
This is equality for all vector fields at the same point, not an assertion that either connection vanishes there. More generally, the two connections agree at any critical point of σ. Conversely, if their correction vanishes for all X,Y at a point of positive dimension, evaluating g(A(v,v),v)=g(v,v)dσ(v) for every v shows that the point is critical. This is the critical-point criterion for equal conformal connections.
The boundaryless hypothesis is the usual convention for “manifold” here. If manifolds with boundary were included, compactness alone would not suffice: on [0,1] with g=dx2 and σ=x, one has ∇∂x​​∂x​=∂x​ everywhere while ∇∂x​​∂x​=0. An extremum at the boundary need not be a critical point.
