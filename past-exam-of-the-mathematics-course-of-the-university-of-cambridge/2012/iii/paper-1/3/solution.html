A finite-dimensional complex Lie algebra is a semisimple Lie algebra when its solvable radical is zero, equivalently when it has no nonzero solvable ideals. Its Killing form is the symmetric bilinear form
BL​(x,y)=trL​(adx​ady​).
(9)
The cyclic trace identity and ad[z,x]​=[adz​,adx​] give
BL​([z,x],y)+BL​(x,[z,y])=0.
(10)
Thus the Killing form is an invariant bilinear form on a Lie algebra. In particular its radical R={x:BL​(x,L)=0} is an ideal.
We supply the trace argument needed for nondegeneracy rather than assuming the Cartan criterion for semisimplicity. The matrix form of the Cartan solvability criterion says: if A⊆EndC​(V) and tr(uv)=0 for every u∈[A,A], v∈A, then A is solvable. To prove this direction, fix u∈[A,A]. Let V=⨁λ​Vλ​ be its generalized eigenspace decomposition, and define s to act on Vλ​ by the scalar λ. On Hom(Vμ​,Vλ​), the semisimple part of adu​ has eigenvalue λ−μ, whereas ads​ acts by λ−μ​=λ−μ​. Polynomial interpolation on the finitely many eigenvalues, with all derivatives through the sizes of the nilpotent blocks set to zero, therefore gives
ads​=p(adu​),p(0)=0.
(11)
Here the same difference always has the same conjugate, so the interpolation is consistent; the prescribed zero derivatives remove every nilpotent block. Since adu​(A)⊆[A,A], it follows that [s,A]⊆[A,A].
Write u=∑j​[aj​,bj​]. By cyclicity and the trace hypothesis,
tr(us)=∑j​tr(aj​[bj​,s])=0.
(12)
On the other hand, tr(us)=∑λ​dim(Vλ​)∣λ∣2. Thus all eigenvalues of u vanish, and every element of [A,A] is nilpotent. The Engel theorem makes [A,A] nilpotent as a Lie algebra, hence solvable; A/[A,A] is abelian, so A is solvable. This is the Conjugate-spectrum proof of Cartan solvability.
Apply this to the Killing radical R. For x,y∈R, the adjoint actions preserve R and act as zero on L/R, because R is an ideal. Computing traces in a basis adapted to R gives
BR​(x,y)=BL​(x,y)=0.
(13)
The matrix Lie algebra adR​(R) consequently satisfies the trace criterion and is solvable. Its kernel is Z(R), an abelian ideal; a central extension of a solvable algebra is solvable. Thus R is solvable. This proves the reusable assertion that the Killing radical is a solvable ideal. Semisimplicity forces R=0, and hence BL​ is nondegenerate.
A Cartan subalgebra is a nilpotent subalgebra H which is self-normalizing:
NL​(H)={x∈L:[x,H]⊆H}=H.
(14)
For a complex semisimple algebra this is equivalently a maximal toral subalgebra. The nilpotent, self-normalizing definition permits a proof of the restricted nondegeneracy without first assuming the toral characterization.
Use the generalized-weight decomposition for a nilpotent Lie algebra for the adjoint action of H:
L=⨁λ​Lλ,Lλ={v:(adh​−λ(h)I)dimLv=0 for all h∈H}.
(15)
For completeness, the stability underlying this decomposition follows directly from nilpotence of H. For fixed h, every adh​ on H is nilpotent. If T=ρ(h) and S=ρ(y) in a finite-dimensional representation, then (adT​)rS=0 for sufficiently large r. The identity
(T−aI)NS=∑j=0N​(jN​)(adT​)j(S)(T−aI)N−j
(16)
shows that S preserves each generalized eigenspace of T. Starting with a basis of H, refine these primary decompositions successively; all summands remain H-invariant. Each resulting summand has only one eigenvalue for each basis element. The Lie theorem triangularizes the action on that summand, so those eigenvalues extend to a single linear character λ on all of H. This proves the displayed decomposition and nilpotence of all shifted operators there.
Since H is nilpotent, H⊆L0. The space L0 is a subalgebra: repeated use of the derivation rule for adh​ shows that the bracket of two generalized zero-eigenvectors is another such vector. If L0/H=0, the adjoint action of H on this quotient consists entirely of nilpotent maps. The Engel theorem supplies a nonzero coset x+H with [H,x]⊆H, contradicting self-normalization. Therefore the zero generalized weight space of a Cartan subalgebra is exactly H.
Finally, BL​(H,Lλ)=0 when λ=0. Choose h∈H with λ(h)=0 and put T=adh​. On H, a power Tm vanishes. On Lλ, T is invertible. For u∈H and v∈Lλ, write v=Tmw; invariance gives
BL​(u,v)=BL​(u,Tmw)=(−1)mBL​(Tmu,w)=0.
(17)
If u∈H is orthogonal to H, it is now orthogonal to every summand of L, so nondegeneracy of BL​ implies u=0. The restriction BL​∣H×H​ is nondegenerate. This establishes the nondegeneracy of the Killing form on a Cartan subalgebra for every Cartan subalgebra, without requiring a chosen root basis.
