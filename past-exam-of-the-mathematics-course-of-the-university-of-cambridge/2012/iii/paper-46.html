Use units ℏ=c=1, metric gμν​=diag(1,−1,−1,−1), and p⋅x=Ep​t−p⋅x. Treat the complex scalar field and its adjoint as independent variables when varying the action. Their canonical momenta are
π=∂ϕ˙​∂L​=ϕ˙​†,π†=∂ϕ˙​†∂L​=ϕ˙​.​
(1)
Canonical quantization in the Heisenberg picture imposes, at the same time t,
[ϕ(t,x),π(t,y)]=iδ(3)(x−y),[ϕ†(t,x),π†(t,y)]=iδ(3)(x−y).
(2)
All other independent equal-time commutators vanish: in particular [ϕ,ϕ†]=[π,π†]=[ϕ,π†]=[ϕ†,π]=0, together with the same-type field and momentum commutators. The Legendre transform gives the Hamiltonian density
H=πϕ˙​+π†ϕ˙​†−L=ππ†+∇ϕ†⋅∇ϕ+m2ϕ†ϕ,H=∫d3xH.​
(3)
The Euler-Lagrange equations are (□+m2)ϕ=(□+m2)ϕ†=0. The positive- and negative-frequency plane waves form a complete basis of solutions of the Klein-Gordon equation. Since the field is complex, their operator coefficients are independent rather than being adjoints of each other. Define the Lorentz-invariant phase space measure dΠp​=d3p/[(2π)32Ep​]. A convenient expansion is
ϕ(x)=∫dΠp​[a(p)e−ip⋅x−b†(p)eip⋅x],ϕ†(x)=∫dΠp​[a†(p)eip⋅x−b(p)e−ip⋅x].
(4)
The minus sign is a phase convention: replacing b by −b produces the usual plus-sign expansion without changing its commutators or number operator. The normalization is fixed by the canonical commutation relations. For example,
π(x)[ϕ(t,x),π(t,y)]​=i∫dΠp​Ep​[a†(p)eip⋅x+b(p)e−ip⋅x],=2i​∫(2π)3d3p​[eip⋅(x−y)+e−ip⋅(x−y)]=iδ(3)(x−y),​
(5)
when [a(p),a†(q)]=[b(p),b†(q)]=(2π)32Ep​δ(3)(p−q). The cross commutators vanish and the other canonical relation follows by taking adjoints with the operator order reversed. Conversely these mode commutators can be obtained by Fourier inversion of the equal-time relations. This is canonical quantization of a complex scalar field.
Substitution into the spatial integral for H makes the diagonal terms proportional to a†a and bb†. Terms creating or annihilating a pair have q=−p and a coefficient Ep2​−p2−m2=0, so they cancel. The result before vacuum subtraction is
Hraw​=∫dΠp​Ep​[a†(p)a(p)+b(p)b†(p)].
(6)
Commuting b past b† leaves a divergent field-independent vacuum energy. The displayed zero-vacuum-energy form must therefore be understood as a normal-ordered Hamiltonian:
H=:Hraw​:=∫dΠp​Ep​(a†a+b†b)=∫(2π)3d3p​21​(a†a+b†b).​
(7)
A regulator can be introduced before subtracting the constant. This is the normal-ordered Hamiltonian of a free complex scalar field; the unrenormalized Hamiltonian is not literally equal to this expression without that convention.
The scalar-field vacuum is annihilated by both annihilation operators. Their adjoints create two species of spin-zero bosons with the same positive energy Ep​ and mass m, because
[H,a†(p)]=Ep​a†(p),[H,b†(p)]=Ep​b†(p).
(8)
Repeated creation operators give the bosonic Fock space. The negative-frequency part does not create a negative-energy physical state; it creates the antiparticle of positive energy. The charge distinguishes the two species.
For the global phase transformation δϕ=−iαϕ, δϕ†=iαϕ†, Noether theorem gives jμ=i(ϕ†∂μϕ−∂μϕ†ϕ). Its divergence is
∂μ​jμ=i(ϕ†□ϕ−□ϕ†ϕ)=i(−m2ϕ†ϕ+m2ϕ†ϕ)=0.
(9)
With vanishing boundary flux, the integral of j0 is a conserved charge. Substituting the modes gives, before charge normal ordering, Qraw​=∫dΠp​(a†a−bb†). The pair terms cancel because their frequency difference is zero when their spatial momenta sum to zero. Taking the vacuum charge to vanish gives the complex scalar charge operator
Q=∫dΠp​(a†a−b†b).​
(10)
The mode commutators imply [Q,a†]=a† and [Q,b†]=−b†, so
Qa†=a†(Q+1),Qb†=b†(Q−1).​
(11)
Thus the particle carries charge +1, the antiparticle carries charge −1, and a state with occupation numbers na​,nb​ has charge na​−nb​. Also [Q,ϕ]=−ϕ and [Q,H]=0, agreeing with the phase transformation and charge conservation.

Continue with metric signature +−−−. The Pauli matrices obey {σi,σj}=2δijI2​. Block multiplication in the chiral gamma-matrix representation gives
(γ0)2=I4​,γ0γi=(−σi0​0σi​),γiγ0=−γ0γi,
(12)
and
γiγj=−(σiσj0​0σiσj​).
(13)
Hence {γμ,γν}=2gμνI4​, the required Clifford algebra. Multiplying the Dirac equation by iγν∂ν​+m yields
(iγν∂ν​+m)(iγμ∂μ​−m)ψ=−(□+m2)ψ=0.
(14)
The antisymmetric part of γνγμ drops out because partial derivatives commute. Therefore every component of the Dirac spinor satisfies the Klein-Gordon equation. The converse is not true: four arbitrary scalar solutions need not obey the first-order Dirac equation.
The Lorentz group consists of real invertible matrices Λ preserving the Minkowski metric: ΛTgΛ=g. Writing Λ=I+ω+O(ω2) gives ωμν​=−ωνμ​, so there are six continuous parameters: three spatial rotation angles and three boost rapidities. The Proper orthochronous Lorentz group is the connected component with determinant +1 that preserves time orientation. Parity and time reversal are additional discrete operations and are not encoded by the six real parameters in an exponential near the identity.
For the real-generator convention used here, vector generators can be written
(Mvecρσ​)μν​=gρμδσν​−gσμδρν​.
(15)
Take Ωρσ​=−Ωσρ​ and use the same parameters in the spinor representation. The Lorentz-spinor generators from a Clifford algebra are
Mspρσ​=41​[γρ,γσ],S(Λ)=exp(21​Ωρσ​Mspρσ​).​
(16)
To verify the Lorentz algebra, first commute a generator with one gamma matrix:
[Mspρσ​,γτ]=gστγρ−gρτγσ.
(17)
For example, this follows by moving γτ through each product using the Clifford anticommutator. Applying the commutator derivation rule to 41​[γτ,γν] then gives
[Mspρσ​,Mspτν​]=gστMspρν​−gρτMspσν​+gρνMspστ​−gσνMspρτ​.
(18)
No extra factor of i belongs in these generators with this commutator convention. Hermitian-generator conventions shift the factors of i into the brackets and the exponential instead.
The same gamma commutator proves
S−1γμS=Λμν​γν.
(19)
Indeed, to first order S−1γμS=γμ+21​Ωρσ​[γμ,Mspρσ​], exactly the infinitesimal vector action defined above. Exponentiating proves the finite relation. The spinor field transforms as ψ′(x′)=S(Λ)ψ(x) with x′=Λx; combining this relation with ∂μ′​=(Λ−1)νμ​∂ν​ preserves the Dirac equation. A 2π rotation gives S=−I4​, so the spinor matrices give the double-cover spin representation, rather than a single-valued ordinary representation of the Lorentz group itself.
For a boost in direction i, Msp0i​=21​γ0γi is Hermitian, with eigenvalues ±21​. A real rapidity η therefore produces eigenvalues e±η/2 in S, whose moduli are not one. The finite-component spinor representation is not unitary for boosts, although the rotation matrices are unitary. The Lorentz group is noncompact; this does not forbid the unitary infinite-dimensional action on the physical Hilbert space of states.
Nevertheless, since (γμ)†=γ0γμγ0,
(Mspρσ​)†γ0+γ0Mspρσ​=0,S†γ0S=γ0.
(20)
This Dirac spinor pseudo-unitarity implies ψˉ​′(x′)=ψˉ​(x)S−1, and consequently
ψˉ​′(x′)ψ′(x′)=ψˉ​(x)ψ(x).​
(21)
The Dirac adjoint is precisely the adjoint needed to form this Lorentz scalar; ordinary ψ†ψ alone is not a scalar.
Let γ5=iγ0γ1γ2γ3=diag(−I2​,I2​). It anticommutes with every gamma matrix and commutes with every Mspρσ​, so S−1γ5S=γ5. Thus the axial current transforms as a four-vector under proper Lorentz transformations:
j5′​μ(x′)=Λμν​j5ν​(x).
(22)
Under parity it is an axial vector, acquiring the additional pseudovector sign. Contracting two axial currents cancels that sign and uses the invariant metric. Therefore j5μ​j5μ​ is compatible with Lorentz invariance, including parity invariance of this contraction. Lorentz invariance of the interaction does not require the axial current to be conserved.

For fields Φa​ with a first-derivative Lagrangian density, suppose an infinitesimal global transformation at fixed coordinates is δΦa​=αΔa​ and δL=α∂μ​Kμ, with constant infinitesimal α. Noether theorem gives the on shell conserved current
Jμ=a∑​∂(∂μ​Φa​)∂L​Δa​−Kμ,∂μ​Jμ=0.​
(23)
This follows by varying the Lagrangian, integrating the terms with ∂μ​Δa​ by parts, and setting the Euler-Lagrange expressions to zero. If spatial boundary flux vanishes, ∫d3xJ0 is a conserved Noether charge. Spacetime symmetries are covered by including the induced field variation at fixed coordinates and the corresponding total derivative Kμ.
An example of a theory with a Lorentz four-vector is the source-free Maxwell field with electromagnetic four-potential Aμ​. Under an Abelian gauge transformation,
Aμ​⟼Aμ​+∂μ​χ,Fμν​=∂μ​Aν​−∂ν​Aμ​.
(24)
The change in Fμν​ is ∂μ​∂ν​χ−∂ν​∂μ​χ=0, so the Maxwell Lagrangian is gauge-invariant. For Aμ​=(Φ,−A) define Ei​=F0i​ and Fij​=−ϵijk​Bk​. With signature +−−−,
Fμν​Fμν=2(B2−E2),L=21​(E2−B2).
(25)
The minus sign in −F2/4 gives the positive electric kinetic term and positive physical field energy; reversing it would reverse the Hamiltonian's sign for the physical modes. The normalization 1/4 accounts for antisymmetry and yields the conventional equations. Varying Aν​ gives ∂μ​Fμν=0.
For time translation at fixed coordinates take Δν​=∂0​Aν​. Since α is constant, δFμν​=α∂0​Fμν​, and hence δL=α∂0​L. Thus Kμ=δμ0​L. The momentum derivative is
∂(∂μ​Aν​)∂L​=−Fμν.
(26)
Noether theorem therefore gives the canonical stress-energy tensor's time-translation current
Jcanμ​=Tcanμ​0​=−Fμν∂0​Aν​−δμ0​L,Hcan​=∫d3x[21​(E2+B2)+E⋅∇A0​].​
(27)
Here A˙i​=Ei​+∂i​A0​ was used. The canonical momentum of A0​ vanishes, while that of the lower spatial component Ai​ is Ei​: A0​ acts as a multiplier for Gauss law, not a propagating degree of freedom. The density above is not manifestly gauge-invariant, but its charge agrees with the physical energy on the constraint surface and with appropriate boundary conditions.
The gauge-covariant time translation instead has Δν​=∂0​Aν​−∂ν​A0​=F0ν​. It is time translation combined with a gauge transformation of parameter χ=−αA0​. Directly,
δFμν​=α(∂μ​F0ν​−∂ν​F0μ​)=α∂0​Fμν​,
(28)
using the definition of F, equivalently its Bianchi identity. The total derivative in δL is unchanged. The improved current is
Jimpμ​=−FμνF0ν​−δμ0​L=Θμ0​,Θμν​=−FμρFνρ​+41​δμν​Fρσ​Fρσ.
(29)
It is the gauge-invariant Maxwell stress-energy tensor. In particular,
u=Jimp0​=21​(E2+B2),H=∫d3xu.​
(30)
The spatial current is the Poynting vector E×B, so conservation gives ∂t​u+∇⋅(E×B)=0. This is the familiar positive local electromagnetic field energy density, expressed entirely in measurable fields.
To compare the currents,
Jcanμ​−Jimpμ​=−Fμν∂ν​A0​=−∂ν​(A0​Fμν)+A0​∂ν​Fμν.
(31)
The last term vanishes on the source-free Maxwell equations, leaving a stress-energy tensor improvement generated by a stress-energy superpotential. At the charge level,
Hcan​−H=∫d3xE⋅∇A0​=∮A0​E⋅dS−∫d3xA0​∇⋅E=0
(32)
when the surface term vanishes and Gauss law holds. Thus the symmetries yield the same conserved energy under these conditions while the second supplies a gauge-invariant local density. Boundary terms would have to be retained if those boundary conditions were not imposed.

Take the muon mass to be mμ​, and use the quantum electrodynamics convention Lint​=−eψˉ​γμψAμ​ with e>0. Let p1​,p2​ be the incoming muon and antimuon momenta and k1​,k2​ the outgoing photon momenta, so p1​+p2​=k1​+k2​. External particles are on shell: p12​=p22​=mμ2​ and k12​=k22​=0. The two tree-level Feynman diagrams are the two orders in which the photon legs attach to the muon line. There is no three-photon QED vertex, and therefore no single-photon annihilation diagram into two photons at this order.
The QED Feynman rules needed here are the vertex −ieγμ, the Dirac propagator
q2−mμ2​+i0i(q+mμ​)​,q=γμqμ​,
(33)
an incoming muon spinor u(p1​), an incoming antimuon adjoint vˉ(p2​), and an outgoing photon polarization ϵμ∗​(k). At a vertex enforce four-momentum conservation. Spinor products are ordered along the fermion line; exchanging the external photons introduces no relative minus sign. Define R(q)=(q+mμ​)/(q2−mμ2​+i0) and ϵ=γμϵμ​.
With the convention that a diagram contributes iM, the two muon-antimuon annihilation amplitudes are
Mt​Mu​Mann​​​=−e2vˉ(p2​)ϵ2∗​R(p1​−k1​)ϵ1∗​u(p1​),=−e2vˉ(p2​)ϵ1∗​R(p1​−k2​)ϵ2∗​u(p1​),=Mt​+Mu​.​​
(34)
Their denominators are respectively t−mμ2​+i0 and u−mμ2​+i0, where t=(p1​−k1​)2, u=(p1​−k2​)2. A different consistent overall amplitude-phase convention has no physical effect.
A physical photon polarization vector obeys k⋅ϵ=0, and represents an equivalence class ϵ∼ϵ+αk. For a real null momentum, adding αk preserves transversality. One can additionally choose transverse spatial vectors with ϵ0=0 and ϵ∗⋅ϵ=−1; there are two independent physical polarizations. The equivalence class removes the unphysical longitudinal direction, rather than imposing four independent physical polarization states.
The invariance of the total amplitude is a two-photon fermion Ward identity. Replace ϵ1∗​ by k1​ and put qt​=p1​−k1​, qu​=p1​−k2​=k1​−p2​. The external Dirac equations give
(p1​−mμ​)u(p1​)=0,vˉ(p2​)(p2​+mμ​)=0.
(35)
Away from propagator poles, R(q)(q−mμ​)=(q−mμ​)R(q)=I; the identity extends with the common Feynman prescription. Therefore
R(qt​)k1​u(p1​)vˉ(p2​)k1​R(qu​)​=R(qt​)(p1​−qt​)u(p1​)=−u(p1​),=vˉ(p2​)(qu​+p2​)R(qu​)=vˉ(p2​).​
(36)
The two terms in the bracket then give −vˉϵ2∗​u and +vˉϵ2∗​u, which cancel. Exchanging photon labels proves the second Ward identity. Thus the sum is unchanged under ϵi​↦ϵi​+αki​ for either photon. Neither diagram is generally gauge-invariant separately. Physically, longitudinal pure-gauge polarization does not couple to the observable scattering amplitude; only the two transverse photon degrees of freedom contribute.
For Compton scattering write the incoming momenta as p,k and outgoing momenta as p′,k′, with p+k=p′+k′. The outgoing muon contributes uˉ(p′), the incoming photon contributes ϵ(k) without conjugation, and the outgoing photon contributes ϵ′∗(k′). The two tree-level Compton amplitudes are
Ms​Mu​MC​​​=−e2uˉ(p′)ϵ′∗R(p+k)ϵu(p),=−e2uˉ(p′)ϵR(p−k′)ϵ′∗u(p),=Ms​+Mu​.​​
(37)
These are the lower two diagrams. Their intermediate muon momenta are p+k and p−k′, with denominators s−mμ2​+i0 and u−mμ2​+i0. The first attaches the incoming photon before the outgoing photon along fermion flow; the second reverses that attachment order. Both contribute at order e2 in the amplitude.
Compton scattering has one incoming and one outgoing muon and one photon on each side; annihilation has an incoming particle-antiparticle pair and two outgoing photons. Accordingly the external spinors/polarizations and physical Mandelstam channels differ, although both arise from the same two-vertex QED matrix element. Crossing symmetry relates them by continuing p2​↦−p′ and one outgoing photon momentum k1​↦−k, with the corresponding external wavefunction replacements. The annihilation t channel becomes the Compton s channel and the other channel becomes its u channel. Identical final photons require a factor 1/2! in an annihilation phase-space integral over an otherwise double-counted full final-state region; there is no analogous final-state identical-particle factor for the muon-photon Compton final state. Gauge cancellation holds for the summed Compton amplitude as well.
