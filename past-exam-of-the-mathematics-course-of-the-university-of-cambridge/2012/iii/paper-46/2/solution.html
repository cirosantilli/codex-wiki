Continue with metric signature +−−−. The Pauli matrices obey {σi,σj}=2δijI2​. Block multiplication in the chiral gamma-matrix representation gives
(γ0)2=I4​,γ0γi=(−σi0​0σi​),γiγ0=−γ0γi,
(12)
and
γiγj=−(σiσj0​0σiσj​).
(13)
Hence {γμ,γν}=2gμνI4​, the required Clifford algebra. Multiplying the Dirac equation by iγν∂ν​+m yields
(iγν∂ν​+m)(iγμ∂μ​−m)ψ=−(□+m2)ψ=0.
(14)
The antisymmetric part of γνγμ drops out because partial derivatives commute. Therefore every component of the Dirac spinor satisfies the Klein-Gordon equation. The converse is not true: four arbitrary scalar solutions need not obey the first-order Dirac equation.
The Lorentz group consists of real invertible matrices Λ preserving the Minkowski metric: ΛTgΛ=g. Writing Λ=I+ω+O(ω2) gives ωμν​=−ωνμ​, so there are six continuous parameters: three spatial rotation angles and three boost rapidities. The Proper orthochronous Lorentz group is the connected component with determinant +1 that preserves time orientation. Parity and time reversal are additional discrete operations and are not encoded by the six real parameters in an exponential near the identity.
For the real-generator convention used here, vector generators can be written
(Mvecρσ​)μν​=gρμδσν​−gσμδρν​.
(15)
Take Ωρσ​=−Ωσρ​ and use the same parameters in the spinor representation. The Lorentz-spinor generators from a Clifford algebra are
Mspρσ​=41​[γρ,γσ],S(Λ)=exp(21​Ωρσ​Mspρσ​).​
(16)
To verify the Lorentz algebra, first commute a generator with one gamma matrix:
[Mspρσ​,γτ]=gστγρ−gρτγσ.
(17)
For example, this follows by moving γτ through each product using the Clifford anticommutator. Applying the commutator derivation rule to 41​[γτ,γν] then gives
[Mspρσ​,Mspτν​]=gστMspρν​−gρτMspσν​+gρνMspστ​−gσνMspρτ​.
(18)
No extra factor of i belongs in these generators with this commutator convention. Hermitian-generator conventions shift the factors of i into the brackets and the exponential instead.
The same gamma commutator proves
S−1γμS=Λμν​γν.
(19)
Indeed, to first order S−1γμS=γμ+21​Ωρσ​[γμ,Mspρσ​], exactly the infinitesimal vector action defined above. Exponentiating proves the finite relation. The spinor field transforms as ψ′(x′)=S(Λ)ψ(x) with x′=Λx; combining this relation with ∂μ′​=(Λ−1)νμ​∂ν​ preserves the Dirac equation. A 2π rotation gives S=−I4​, so the spinor matrices give the double-cover spin representation, rather than a single-valued ordinary representation of the Lorentz group itself.
For a boost in direction i, Msp0i​=21​γ0γi is Hermitian, with eigenvalues ±21​. A real rapidity η therefore produces eigenvalues e±η/2 in S, whose moduli are not one. The finite-component spinor representation is not unitary for boosts, although the rotation matrices are unitary. The Lorentz group is noncompact; this does not forbid the unitary infinite-dimensional action on the physical Hilbert space of states.
Nevertheless, since (γμ)†=γ0γμγ0,
(Mspρσ​)†γ0+γ0Mspρσ​=0,S†γ0S=γ0.
(20)
This Dirac spinor pseudo-unitarity implies ψˉ​′(x′)=ψˉ​(x)S−1, and consequently
ψˉ​′(x′)ψ′(x′)=ψˉ​(x)ψ(x).​
(21)
The Dirac adjoint is precisely the adjoint needed to form this Lorentz scalar; ordinary ψ†ψ alone is not a scalar.
Let γ5=iγ0γ1γ2γ3=diag(−I2​,I2​). It anticommutes with every gamma matrix and commutes with every Mspρσ​, so S−1γ5S=γ5. Thus the axial current transforms as a four-vector under proper Lorentz transformations:
j5′​μ(x′)=Λμν​j5ν​(x).
(22)
Under parity it is an axial vector, acquiring the additional pseudovector sign. Contracting two axial currents cancels that sign and uses the invariant metric. Therefore j5μ​j5μ​ is compatible with Lorentz invariance, including parity invariance of this contraction. Lorentz invariance of the interaction does not require the axial current to be conserved.
