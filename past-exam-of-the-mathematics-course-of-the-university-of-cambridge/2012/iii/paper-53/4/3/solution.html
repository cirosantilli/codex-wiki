Derive both cases from V=Cp​(Φ/MPl​)p, using the positive-field branch. The potential slow-roll parameter and second potential slow-roll parameter are
ϵV​=2Φ2p2MPl2​​,ηV​=Φ2p(p−1)MPl2​​.
(30)
Taking the leading inflation-end estimate ϵV​=1, the remaining number of e-folds is
N≃MPl2​1​∫Φe​ΦN​​V′V​dΦ=2pMPl2​ΦN2​−Φe2​​,MPl2​ΦN2​​=2pN+2p2​.
(31)
The corresponding monomial-inflation curvature amplitude and tilt are
PR​(N)=24π2MPl4​Cp​​(2pN+2p2​)p/2p4N+p​,ns​−1=−4N+p2(p+2)​.
(32)
In particular,
ns(2)​=1−2N+14​≃0.9669,ns(4)​=1−N+13​≃0.9508(N=60).​
(33)
The quartic case is redder. Its small-k matter envelope is k0.9508 instead of k0.9669; its high-k envelope is k−3.0492ln2k instead of k−3.0331ln2k. With the late cosmology fixed, the turnover and baryonic acoustic scale do not move. If the spectra are matched in amplitude at a pivot k∗​, their ratio is approximately
Pm(2)​(k)Pm(4)​(k)​≃(k∗​k​)−0.0161.​
(34)
This gives more large-scale power and less small-scale power relative to the pivot, with about a 7% relative tilt across two decades in wavenumber.
The quartic coefficient is not specified, so changing the power of the potential does not by itself specify the overall amplitude. If C2​=V0​ and the quartic coefficient is C4​, the formula above gives, at the same N,
PR(2)​PR(4)​​=32V0​C4​​(2N+1)2(N+1)3​≃496V0​C4​​(N=60).​
(35)
Consequently choosing the same dimensionful coefficient would give a very different normalization, whereas choosing C4​/V0​≃1/496 matches the pivot amplitude. These are different normalization choices, not a universal amplitude prediction from a quartic shape alone.
