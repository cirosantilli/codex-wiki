Put C=⋃r≥1​Q(μpr​), where μpr​ is the group of prth roots of unity. The compatible Galois groups of the cyclotomic fields give
Gal(C/Q)≅lim​r​(Z/prZ)×=Zp×​.
(1)
For odd p, the Teichmüller character gives
Zp×​=μp−1​×(1+pZp​).
(2)
The p-adic logarithm identifies 1+pZp​ with pZp​, which is topologically isomorphic to Zp​. More explicitly, a↦(1+p)a is an isomorphism from the additive p-adic integers onto 1+pZp​: the logarithm of 1+p has valuation one and generates pZp​. Take the fixed field of μp−1​ in C.
For p=2, use instead
Z2×​={±1}×(1+4Z2​).
(3)
The p-adic logarithm identifies the second factor with 4Z2​, and a↦5a identifies it with Z2​. The fixed field of {±1} in C is the union of the maximal real subfields of the cyclotomic fields of 2-power conductor. Thus in both cases the cyclotomic Zp-extension exists:
Q[p∞]=CΔp​,Gal(Q[p∞]/Q)≅Zp​,​
(4)
where Δp​=μp−1​ for odd p and Δ2​={±1}.
The closed subgroup pnZp​ is the unique subgroup of index pn, so the Galois correspondence supplies the unique degree-pn field Q[pn]. In the odd case it is the Δp​-fixed field in Q(μpn+1​); in the even case it is the real subfield of Q(μ2n+2​). These finite layers are all totally real number fields.
Now set A=Q[pn], B=Q[pm], and let HA​ be the ordinary Hilbert class field of A. Thus HA​/A is abelian, unramified at finite primes, and split at every real place, with degree h(A). If m=n there is nothing to prove. Otherwise the unique prime over p is totally ramified in B/A, by the permitted total-ramification fact and multiplicativity of the ramification index. Any nontrivial intermediate field of B/A is ramified at that prime. Hence
HA​∩B=A.
(5)
It follows that the two extensions are linearly disjoint field extensions, and
[BHA​:B]=[HA​:A]=h(A).
(6)
Unramified extensions remain unramified after base change. The real places also remain split: HA​ and B are totally real, so their compositum is totally real. Therefore BHA​/B lies inside the ordinary Hilbert class field HB​/B. Its degree divides [HB​:B]=h(B), proving
h(pn)∣h(pm).​
(7)
This is class number divisibility with split real places; using a class-field statement restricted to imaginary fields would unnecessarily omit the present real layers.

Write Γ=Gal(K∞​/K)≅Zp​. Its only finite subgroup is trivial. In particular, a real place cannot acquire complex inertia in this Zp-extension: the possible nontrivial inertia at a real place has order two. Thus every infinite place splits in the tower.
Suppose that no finite prime ramified. Every finite layer would then be an abelian everywhere unramified extension of number fields, including splitting at real places. Every layer would lie in the ordinary Hilbert class field of K, a finite extension. Their degrees are unbounded, a contradiction. Therefore some finite prime must ramify.
Let v have residue characteristic ℓ=p. Apply local class field theory to the corresponding decomposition group, a closed subgroup of Γ. The image of the unit group OKv​×​ is the inertia group. Its first principal-unit subgroup is a pro-l group, so its continuous image in the pro-p group Γ is trivial. The residue-unit quotient is the finite group Fqv​×​. Its image is finite, and is therefore also trivial in the torsion-free group Zp​. The entire inertia group is trivial. Hence
v ramifies in K∞​/K ⟹ v∣p.​
(8)
Equivalently, the tame ramification relation with a Frobenius element would force a tame inertia generator to satisfy τqv​−1=1, which is impossible nontrivially in Zp​.
For the final assertion, the cyclotomic Zp-extension of K is KQ[p∞]. Fix v∣p and put E=Kv​, a finite extension of Qp​. Let Cn​ be the completion of Q[pn] at its unique prime over p. This is a totally ramified extension of Qp​ of degree pn. In a compatible local algebraic closure,
e(ECn​/E)=e(E/Qp​)e(ECn​/Qp​)​≥e(E/Qp​)pn​.
(9)
Here the inequality follows from the tower through Cn​. The right side is unbounded. These composita occur among the completions of the cyclotomic tower over K, so v has unbounded ramification index, in particular nontrivial inertia group. Thus
Every prime v∣p ramifies in the cyclotomic Zp-extension.​
(10)
This argument allows an arbitrary finite intersection between K and the rational cyclotomic tower; it does not assume disjointness.

Let G=Gal(M∞​/F). The Galois group sequence is
1⟶X⟶G⟶Γ⟶1.
(11)
For g∈Γ and x∈X, choose a lift g​∈G and define
g⋅x=g​xg​−1.
(12)
This is independent of the lift because X is abelian. Write X additively. As an abelian pro-p group, it is a compact Zp​-module, with ax defined by the limit of integer powers, and the conjugation action is continuous and Zp​-linear. It extends to the Iwasawa algebra
Λ=Zp​[[Γ]]=lim​r,n​(Z/prZ)[Γ/Γpn].
(13)
Indeed, use the finite quotients of X obtained by intersecting it with open normal subgroups of G; on each quotient the action factors through one of these finite group rings. This defines the natural compact Galois module structure, even when X is not finitely generated. Choosing γ identifies Λ with Zp​[[T]] through T=γ−1.
The subgroup (γ−1)X is closed: it is the image of the compact group X under a continuous map. Every element (γ−1)x is a commutator of a lift of γ with x. Consequently
(γ−1)X⊆[G,G]​.
(14)
Conversely, in the quotient by (γ−1)X, the lift of γ centralizes the image of X. Its powers are dense in the procyclic subgroup they generate. Since these powers map densely onto Γ, compactness shows that G is generated by X and that procyclic subgroup. The quotient is therefore abelian. Hence
[G,G]​=(γ−1)X.​
(15)
One can also see this by choosing a lift of γ. Since G is a pro-p group, the closed subgroup generated by this lift is Zp​, and its map to Γ is an isomorphism. This gives a semidirect product splitting.
The maximal abelian subextension M/F is the fixed field of the closed commutator subgroup. Because F∞​/F is abelian, F∞​⊆M. Restriction of the Galois correspondence to X now gives the coinvariant formula
Gal(M/F∞​)≅X/(γ−1)X=XΓ​.​
(16)
The closed image above is important: the formula is a quotient of compact groups, not a quotient by an unclosed abstract subgroup. This is abelianization over a Zp-extension.

Let Fn​=Q(μpn+1​) and F0​=Q(μp​). The cyclotomic character identifies
G=Gal(F∞​/Q)=Δ×Γ,Δ≅(Z/pZ)×,Γ≅1+pZp​.
(17)
Choose γ with cyclotomic value u=1+p, and put
Λ=Zp​[[Γ]]=Zp​[[T]],T=γ−1.
(18)
The Iwasawa algebra of G is Λ[Δ]. Conjugation, independent of lift as in the preceding solution, makes X a compact Galois module over this ring. It is the p-ramified Iwasawa module, not the unramified class-group module.
Because p−1 is a unit in Zp​, the Teichmüller character ω gives orthogonal character idempotents
eχ​=p−11​∑δ∈Δ​χ(δ)−1δ,χ=ωi,0≤i≤p−2.
(19)
They sum to one, so X=⨁χ​Xχ​, where Xχ​=eχ​X. Each is a module over the one-variable ring Λ. Complex conjugation is the element −1 of Δ; characters with χ(−1)=1 are even, and those with χ(−1)=−1 are odd.
The basic Iwasawa-module rank theorem in this tower gives
rankΛ​Xχ​={0,1,​χ even,χ odd.​​
(20)
In particular,
rankΛ​X=2p−1​,X+ is torsion,rankΛ​X−=2p−1​.​
(21)
Thus it would be incorrect to describe all of X as a torsion module.
Here is the class-field unit sequence explaining these ranks. Let U∞​ be the inverse limit, under local norms, of the pro-p completions of the unit groups at the unique prime over p in Fn​. Let E∞​ be the inverse limit of the closures of the global units in these local unit groups, and let
Y=lim​n​Cl(Fn​)(p)
(22)
under ideal norms. The Artin reciprocity maps yield
0⟶E∞​⟶U∞​⟶X⟶Y⟶0.
(23)
The final module is the unramified Iwasawa module and is finitely generated torsion, as proved in the other essay. Local-unit theory gives U∞​ Iwasawa-module rank p−1, with rank one in each Δ-character. The Leopoldt theorem for abelian number fields gives E∞​ rank (p−1)/2, with rank one in each even character and zero in each odd character. In odd characters the possible norm-compatible roots of unity contribute a rank-zero term, not a free summand. Subtracting ranks in the exact sequence gives the displayed result and also finite generation.
For any character, the Iwasawa module structure theorem describes Xχ​ up to pseudo-isomorphism:
Xχ​∼Λrχ​⊕⨁j​Λ/(paj​)⊕⨁k​Λ/(fk​(T)bk​),
(24)
where the fk​ are irreducible distinguished polynomials and the kernel and cokernel of a pseudo-isomorphism are finite. For a torsion component, its characteristic ideal is generated by
p∑j​aj​∏k​fk​(T)bk​.
(25)
The Iwasawa invariants are μ=∑aj​ and λ=∑bk​degfk​. A characteristic ideal describes the elementary divisors only in aggregate; it is not an assertion that the module is cyclic or that an odd component is actually free.
The trivial character component X1​ is zero. Indeed, the maximal abelian pro-p extension of Q unramified outside p is precisely the rational cyclotomic Zp-extension, by the Kronecker–Weber theorem. Coprime descent using the character idempotent e1​, followed by the abelianization over a Zp-extension formula, identifies (X1​)Γ​ with the additional abelian pro-p quotient over Q. There is no such extension beyond the cyclotomic one, so (X1​)Γ​=0, and the Compact Nakayama lemma gives X1​=0.
The central arithmetic description concerns the nontrivial even characters. Let gχ​(T) be the integral Kubota-Leopoldt p-adic L-function power series with convention
gχ​(u1−s−1)=Lp​(χ,s).
(26)
For positive integers k of the matching parity, the interpolation is
Lp​(χ,1−k)=−(1−(χω−k)(p)pk−1)kBk,χω−k​​,
(27)
where the Dirichlet character in the Euler factor is its primitive associate, and Bk,η​ is a generalized Bernoulli number. The Iwasawa main conjecture, a theorem here, says
charΛ​Xχ​=(gχ​(T))(χ=1, χ even).​
(28)
The trivial character is excluded from this displayed analytic normalization: its p-adic zeta function has a pole and must be treated separately.
The proof mechanism starts with norm-compatible cyclotomic units, for instance ratios
cn​(a)=1−ζpn+1​1−ζpn+1a​​(p∤a),
(29)
and their character projections. A Coleman power series encodes a norm-compatible local unit by a single power series. Applying its logarithmic derivative to these cyclotomic units produces the p-adic L-function above. If C∞​ denotes their closed norm-limit module, this calculation determines
charΛ​(U∞,χ​/C∞,χ​)=(gχ​).
(30)
The remaining global input is
charΛ​Yχ​=charΛ​(E∞,χ​/C∞,χ​)
(31)
for nontrivial even χ. In the class-field unit sequence modulo C∞,χ​, multiplicativity of characteristic ideals cancels these two terms and gives the boxed formula for Xχ​.
There are two established routes to this global input. The Mazur-Wiles theorem constructs suitable abelian extensions from Galois representations associated with modular forms and the Eisenstein ideal; their sizes supply the missing divisibility. The cyclotomic Euler system route uses norm relations for cyclotomic units at auxiliary primes and descent to bound the class-group module. The analytic class number formula and the cyclotomic-unit index formula then supply the equality of characteristic ideals. These are substantial arithmetic theorems, rather than consequences of the abstract structure theorem alone.
A useful further result is the Ferrero-Washington theorem: μ(Y)=0 for this cyclotomic tower, more generally for cyclotomic towers of abelian number fields. Its proof rules out an identically zero reduction modulo p of the relevant p-adic L-series, using distribution of p-adic digits. Together with the main conjecture, it gives μ(Xχ​)=0 for each nontrivial even χ. Therefore these torsion components are finitely generated over Zp​ up to finite error.
Finally, Kummer reflection in Iwasawa theory relates the even p-ramified module to an odd unramified component:
Xχ​∼Yωχ−1ι​(1),ι(γ)=γ−1,
(32)
where (1) is the Tate twist by the cyclotomic character. The corresponding power-series substitution is
T⟼u(1+T)−1−1.
(33)
This explains why the p-ramified interpolation involves u1−s while the reflected class-group interpolation involves us. Both the involution and the twist matter. The rank theorem, the class-field sequence, the structure theorem and the explicit even-character characteristic ideals together give the known compact Galois module structure; none justifies replacing every odd component by a free module without further argument.

Let Fn​ be the degree-pn layer, Γn​=Γpn, and
Λ=Zp​[[Γ]]≅Zp​[[T]],ωn​(T)=(1+T)pn−1.
(34)
Conjugation makes Y a compact Λ-module. The goal is to show that it is finitely generated and has Iwasawa-module rank zero. This proof applies to every Zp-extension, not only a cyclotomic one, and makes no Leopoldt conjecture assumption.
By the ramification argument above, only primes over p ramify in F∞​/F, and at least one does. Every nonzero closed inertia group in Γ≅Zp​ is open. After replacing F by a finite layer, every prime which ramifies is totally ramified in the remaining tower. The number s≥1 of such primes then stays constant. This replacement does not affect whether Y is a torsion module: Zp​[[Γ]] is finite free over the Iwasawa algebra of an open subgroup, and the two module ranks vanish together.
Let Gn​=Gal(L∞​/Fn​), and let Dn​ be its maximal abelian quotient. The abelianization over a Zp-extension formula gives an exact sequence
0⟶YΓn​​⟶Dn​⟶Γn​⟶0,YΓn​​=Y/ωn​Y.
(35)
Choose a lift of a generator of Γn​ if a splitting is desired. We will bound the Zp​-rank of the middle term independently of n.
At each of the s ramified primes, inertia in L∞​/Fn​ maps isomorphically onto Γn​: its kernel is inertia in the unramified extension L∞​/F∞​, hence trivial, and the map onto the totally ramified base inertia is surjective. Thus each image in Dn​ is procyclic and has Zp​-rank at most one. No other finite prime contributes inertia. Quotienting Dn​ by the closed subgroup generated by these s images gives an abelian extension of Fn​ unramified at all finite primes. Its Galois group is finite by class field theory, using the ordinary or narrow ideal class group according to the treatment of real places. Any infinite-place inertia is finite and does not affect the rank bound. Therefore Dn​ is finitely generated over Zp​ and
rankZp​​Dn​≤s.
(36)
The preceding exact sequence now yields the uniform bound
rankZp​​(Y/ωn​Y)≤s−1.​
(37)
It does not assert that these coinvariant modules are always finite when several primes ramify.
At n=0, Y/TY is finitely generated over Zp​, so Y/(p,T)Y is finite. Lift a finite basis of this quotient to Y. The Compact Nakayama lemma shows that these lifts generate Y over Λ. Briefly, the quotient by their compact generated image satisfies Q=(p,T)Q. Every finite continuous Λ-quotient of Q has nilpotent action by (p,T): p is nilpotent, and modulo p a finite pro-p action makes γ−1 nilpotent. Such a quotient must be zero. Finite quotients separate points of a compact pro-p group, so Q=0.
Suppose now that Y had positive Λ-rank r. The Iwasawa module structure theorem provides a pseudo-isomorphism to
Λr⊕E
(38)
with E a finitely generated torsion module. Passing to Γn​-coinvariant modules leaves a finite cokernel. But
Λ/ωn​Λ≅Zp​[Γ/Γn​]
(39)
is free of rank pn over Zp​. Thus the coinvariant modules would have rank at least rpn, contradicting the uniform bound s−1. Equivalently their ranks have asymptotic form rpn+O(1), with the torsion elementary divisors contributing only a bounded rank.
It follows that r=0. Since Λ is an integral domain, rank zero means every element is killed by a nonzero scalar. Therefore
Y is a finitely generated torsion Zp​[[Γ]]-module.​
(40)
This is the unramified Iwasawa torsion theorem. The crucial arithmetic inputs are finite class fields and the bounded number of ramified primes; neither vanishing of a μ-invariant nor a cyclotomic main conjecture is required.
