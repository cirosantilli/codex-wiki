Start with a finite subcover of the compact set K. Among its remaining open balls, retain one of greatest radius and discard every ball intersecting it. Repeat until no balls remain. The retained family F is finite and pairwise disjoint. If a discarded ball B(c,r) met the retained ball B(c0​,R), then r≤R and ∣c−c0​∣<r+R. Therefore, for any y∈B(c,r),
∣y−c0​∣≤∣y−c∣+∣c−c0​∣<2r+R≤3R.
(29)
Every original ball lies in the concentric triple of one retained ball. This proves the Wiener covering lemma for the finite subcover. By Lebesgue measure scaling and subadditivity,
λd​(K)≤∑B∈F​λd​(3B)=3d∑B∈F​λd​(B).
(30)
Disjointness gives
λd​(B∈F⋃​B)≥3−dλd​(K).​
(31)
The empty compact set is covered by the empty selection.

Ds​μ(x)=limr↓0​λd​(B(x,r))μ(B(x,r))​=limr↓0​vd​rdμ(B(x,r))​,

when it exists, where vd​ is the unit-ball volume. We prove that this limit is zero outside a Lebesgue-null set for the singular measure.
First derive the needed maximal estimate from the selection lemma. For a finite positive Borel measure η, put
Mu​η(x)=supB an open ballx∈B​​λd​(B)η(B)​.

This is the uncentered maximal function of a finite measure. For α>0, its strict superlevel set is the union of all balls with η(B)>αλd​(B), so it is open. Cover any compact subset H of this set by such balls and apply the finite Wiener covering lemma. The selected disjoint balls give
λd​(H)≤3d∑B∈F​λd​(B)≤α3d​∑B∈F​η(B)≤α3d​η(Rd).
(34)
By inner regularity of Lebesgue measure, this proves the uncentered maximal weak-type inequality
λd​{Mu​η>α}≤α3d​η(Rd).​
(35)
Since μ and λd​ are mutually singular measures, there is a Borel set N with λd​(N)=0 and μ(Rd∖N)=0. By regularity of finite Borel measures on Euclidean space, for each ε>0 choose compact L⊂N with μ(Rd∖L)<ε. Define the remainder measure ηε​(A)=μ(A∖L).
For x∈/L, distance from x to this compact set is positive. Thus sufficiently small centered balls avoid L, and
μ(B(x,r))=ηε​(B(x,r))for all sufficiently small r.
(36)
Write Ds​μ(x) for the upper limit of the ratios. If it exceeds α, then either x∈L or Mu​ηε​(x)>α. Hence
{Ds​μ>α}⊂L∪{Mu​ηε​>α}.
(37)
The right side has Lebesgue measure at most 3dε/α, since λd​(L)=0. This even bounds the outer measure of the left side, without a separate measurability argument for the upper density. Let ε↓0, and then take the countable union over positive rational α. We obtain Ds​μ=0 Lebesgue almost everywhere. The ratios are nonnegative, so their lower limit is also zero and
Ds​μ(x)=0λd​-almost everywhere.​
(38)
This proves that the spherical derivative of a singular measure vanishes. It is important to approximate the null carrier by compact subsets: a null carrier can be dense, so points outside it need not have any neighbourhood avoiding it.
In one dimension, consider the cumulative distribution function F. For either sign of h=0, monotonicity gives a nonnegative difference quotient, and the relevant half-open interval lies inside B(x,2∣h∣). In detail, the numerator is μ((x,x+h]) for h>0, while after reversing both signs it is μ((x+h,x]) for h<0. Thus
0≤hF(x+h)−F(x)​≤∣h∣μ(B(x,2∣h∣))​=4λ1​(B(x,2∣h∣))μ(B(x,2∣h∣))​.
(39)
At each point where the spherical derivative of a measure is zero, this bound tends to zero from both sides. The enlarged open interval avoids any endpoint ambiguity from atoms. Therefore the ordinary two-sided derivative exists and
F′(x)=0λ1​-almost everywhere.​
(40)
This is why singular distribution functions have zero derivative almost everywhere without having to be constant: almost-everywhere differentiation recovers increments only under extra hypotheses such as absolute continuity of a function.
