Choose an oriented Seifert surface F of genus g and an integral basis a1​,…,a2g​ of H1​(F;Z). Push ai​ in the positive normal direction to F. The Seifert matrix is
Vij​=lk(ai+​,aj​).​
(31)
It represents the Seifert form. Its skew-symmetrization V−VT is the integral intersection form of F. The relation to the Alexander polynomial of a knot is
ΔK​(t)≐det(tV−VT).​
(32)
This follows from the presentation of the Alexander module of a knot obtained by cutting the knot exterior along F and stacking the resulting copies; a proof is not needed here.
The printed genus inequality is false. The useful consequence of the determinant relation is the Alexander breadth bound on Seifert genus:
brΔK​≤2gs​(K).​
(33)
Here the breadth of a Laurent polynomial is its largest exponent minus its smallest exponent, so it is unchanged by multiplying by ±tm. Indeed, for a minimal-genus Seifert surface, the matrix has size 2gs​(K) and each entry of tV−VT has degree at most one. Its nonzero determinant is an ordinary polynomial of degree at most 2gs​(K); its breadth is no larger than that degree. Equivalently, the highest exponent of a symmetrically normalized Alexander polynomial of a knot is at most gs​(K). An unnormalized degree is not invariant under Laurent units.
For a counterexample to the printed inequality and the requested example, take the untwisted Whitehead double of a trefoil knot. The picture specifies the two-strand tangle in the zero Seifert framing; the two exterior caps form a Whitehead double clasp. The inset identifies the trefoil knot used as companion.
Its usual genus-one Seifert surface is a zero-framed annulus following the trefoil knot, joined by the clasp band. Choose the annulus core and a curve traversing the clasp band as the basis of its first homology. Zero annulus twisting gives the first self-linking number 0. The clasp contributes a Hopf band with self-linking number −1 in the positive-clasp convention used here. Plumbing the bands contributes one linking in one push-off direction and none in the other. Orienting the basis suitably gives
V=(00​1−1​),tV−VT=(0−1​t1−t​).
(34)
Tying the annulus into the trefoil knot changes neither these local linking counts nor its prescribed zero Seifert framing. Therefore
ΔK​(t)≐t≐1.​
(35)
This Whitehead double is not the unknot, as allowed without proof in the question. Its Seifert genus is consequently at least one and at most one, hence exactly one. Thus gs​(K)=1 while every normalized constant Alexander polynomial of a knot has degree zero: a direct counterexample to the printed inequality.
