There are two common normalizations. I will define both explicitly, because the numerical conclusion in the question uses the unreduced one.
Set δ=−A2−A−2. The reduced Kauffman bracket of a nonempty link diagram is
⟨D⟩red​=∑s​Aa(s)−b(s)δ∣s∣−1,
(43)
where s runs over all bracket smoothings, a(s),b(s) count A- and B-smoothings, and ∣s∣ is the number of resulting circles. Locally,
⟨crossing⟩=A⟨A-smoothing⟩+A−1⟨B-smoothing⟩.
(44)
Fix the usual Kauffman bracket convention in which the A-smoothing at a positive braid crossing is its oriented smoothing; reflecting a crossing exchanges A and A−1. The unknot has reduced bracket 1, and adjoining another circle multiplies the bracket by δ. The unreduced Kauffman bracket uses δ∣s∣ instead and assigns the empty diagram 1. Thus for a nonempty diagram
⟨D⟩un​=δ⟨D⟩red​.
(45)
For completeness, the bracket calculations for all three Reidemeister moves can be carried out in the Temperley-Lieb diagram algebra. Let I be the identity two-strand tangle, and let e be the cap-cup tangle. Composition gives e2=δe. The two crossings correspond to
R=AI+A−1e,R−1=A−1I+Ae.
(46)
Consequently
RR−1=I+(A2+A−2+δ)e=I,
(47)
which proves invariance under the second Reidemeister move. On three strands, the cap-cup diagrams satisfy e1​e2​e1​=e1​, e2​e1​e2​=e2​, and ei2​=δei​. Expand the two sides of the third move. Their difference is
R1​R2​R1​−R2​R1​R2​=(A+A−1δ+A−3)(e1​−e2​)=0.
(48)
Thus the third Reidemeister move also preserves either bracket normalization. Reflected forms of these moves follow by replacing A with A−1.
A positive curl contributes Aδ+A−1=−A3, and a negative curl contributes A−1δ+A=−A−3. The writhe of a link diagram w(D) changes by +1 or −1 in exactly these cases. Therefore the corrected bracket
fD​(A)=(−A3)−w(D)⟨D⟩
(49)
is invariant under the first Reidemeister move as well; the second and third moves leave the writhe of a link diagram unchanged. Substituting t=A−4 defines a Jones polynomial. For multiple components half-integer powers of t may occur. The reduced version VLred​ has VUred​=1, and the Unreduced Jones polynomial is
VLun​(t)=−(t1/2+t−1/2)VLred​(t).
(50)
At A=1, switching a crossing does not change the bracket: both smoothing coefficients are 1. It changes the writhe of a link diagram by 2, so the correction factor (−1)−w is unchanged. Hence either Jones polynomial at t=1 is unchanged by a crossing change. By switching crossings, any l-component link can be made an unlink. Its crossing-free diagram has l circles and zero writhe of a link diagram, giving
VLred​(1)=(−2)l−1,VLun​(1)=(−2)l.​
(51)
Thus the printed conclusion ∣VL​(1)∣=2l holds for the unreduced normalization. For the reduced Jones polynomial, the correct conclusion is ∣VLred​(1)∣=2l−1​; the unknot alone already rules out 2l in that convention.
