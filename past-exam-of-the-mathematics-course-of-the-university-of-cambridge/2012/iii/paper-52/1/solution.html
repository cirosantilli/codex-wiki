Use metric signature +−−− and the parity matrix Pμν​=diag(1,−1,−1,−1). If a Dirac field transforms as ψ′(x)=ηP​SP​ψ(xP​), the chain rule changes the spatial derivatives' signs. Covariance of its Dirac equation therefore requires
SP−1​γ0SP​=γ0,SP−1​γiSP​=−γi.
(1)
The Clifford algebra gives precisely these identities for SP​=γ0. In an irreducible Dirac representation any other solution differs by a scalar phase, because its ratio with γ0 commutes with all gamma matrices. With ∣ηP​∣=1, the Dirac mass bilinear and kinetic term consequently obey
ψˉ​′(x)ψ′(x)=ψˉ​(xP​)ψ(xP​),ψˉ​′(x)iγμ∂μ​ψ′(x)=ψˉ​(xP​)iγμ∂μ​ψ(xP​).
(2)
The factor γ0 compensates the sign reversal of spatial derivatives. A transformation containing only the argument change would not preserve the massive Dirac equation. This establishes parity symmetry of the free or vector-coupled Dirac theory; it does not make a chiral weak interaction parity symmetric.
For charge conjugation, transpose the adjoint Dirac equation to obtain (iγμT∂μ​+m)ψˉ​T=0. Multiplying by C gives the original Dirac equation for ψc=Cψˉ​T if
CγμTC−1=−γμ,C−1γμC=−γμT.​
(3)
One may choose C unitary; in four dimensions a conventional choice in a standard Dirac or chiral basis is C=iγ2γ0, with CT=−C. Its overall phase and ηC​ do not affect the following bilinear transformation. Reordering the Grassmann fermion fields gives, for a color matrix X commuting with the gamma matrices,
ψc​γμXψc=−ψˉ​γμXTψ.
(4)
Thus a vector color current changes sign and transposes its color generator under charge conjugation.
Write the Hermitian color connection as Aμ​=Aμa​Ta. The interaction coming from Dμ​=∂μ​+igAμ​ is −gψˉ​γμAμ​ψ. Its charge-conjugation symmetry requires AμC​=−AμT​. Parity acts on the Lorentz index as on a vector connection, so combining the two gives
AμCP​(x)=−Pμ​νAνT​(xP​).​
(5)
In components, A0​↦−A0T​(xP​) and Ai​↦+AiT​(xP​). The transpose is a color-space transpose, independent of the spinor matrix C. Intrinsic fermion phases cancel between a field and its adjoint.
Define the matrix gauge field strength unambiguously by
Fμν​=∂μ​Aν​−∂ν​Aμ​+ig[Aμ​,Aν​].
(6)
The derivative terms transform with the expected parity factors and a minus transpose. For the commutator, the crucial identity is [XT,YT]=−[X,Y]T, so the nonlinear term has the same transformation as the derivative terms. This is the CP transformation of non-Abelian field strength:
FμνCP​(x)=−Pμ​αPν​βFαβT​(xP​).​
(7)
For example, F0i​↦+F0iT​(xP​) while Fij​↦−FijT​(xP​). With tr(TaTb)=21​δab, the color sum in the theta operator is twice a trace of two field strengths. The two charge-conjugation minus signs cancel, and transposition reverses the trace factors without changing their trace. The four parity matrices contracted with the Levi-Civita symbol contribute detP=−1. Hence
[ϵμνρσFμνa​Fρσa​]CP(x)=−ϵμνρσFμνa​Fρσa​(xP​).​
(8)
The theta operator is CP odd; a generic fixed nonzero coefficient violates CP. Setting the coefficient to zero removes this term. Although the density is locally a total derivative, nontrivial gauge topology means that it need not be irrelevant to the quantum theory. If a properly normalized theta angle is identified periodically, invariance at special values equivalent to their negatives is a separate global question; it does not change the CP odd transformation of the local operator.
