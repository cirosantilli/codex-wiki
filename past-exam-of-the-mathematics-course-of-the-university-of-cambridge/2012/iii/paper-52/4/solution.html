The flavor content is K0=dsˉ and Kˉ0=sdˉ. A charged-current box diagram changes strangeness by two units. The two internal quark lines can contain any up-type flavors ui​,uj​∈{u,c,t}, connected by two charged W propagators. One allowed topology is shown below; its crossed counterpart also contributes to the full mixing amplitude.
Each charged-current vertex contains the appropriate CKM matrix element. The flavor sum has combinations λi​λj​, with λi​=Vis∗​Vid​, multiplying mass-dependent loop functions. CKM matrix unitarity gives ∑i​λi​=0, illustrating the GIM cancellation of flavor-independent loop terms. The diagram is at order g4 and second order in the weak interaction.
Let Θ be the antiunitary CPT operator. It interchanges the neutral-kaon flavor states up to phases. For a Hermitian Hamiltonian invariant under CPT,
⟨ΘK0∣H′∣ΘK0⟩=⟨K0∣Θ−1H′Θ∣K0⟩∗=⟨K0∣H′∣K0⟩,
(25)
since the diagonal expectation is real. Therefore CPT gives R11​=R22​. More generally an effective decay Hamiltonian has R=M−iΓ/2, where both M and Γ are Hermitian. CPT gives M11​=M22​ and Γ11​=Γ22​, hence the same equality of the complex diagonal entries. It does not require R12​=R21​; that is a CP condition. Nor is R21​=R12∗​ true for the full decay matrix in general.
Choose the CP convention CP∣K0⟩=−∣Kˉ0⟩ and CP∣Kˉ0⟩=−∣K0⟩. Then CP acts as −σx​ on the flavor basis. Invariance of H′ means R=σx​Rσx​, and therefore
CP invariance: R12​=R21​.​
(26)
With a different flavor-state phase convention this relation carries the corresponding phase factors; equality is the relation in the convention adopted here. For a Hermitian mass matrix it makes the off-diagonal element real.
For the requested production-time mass eigenstates, discard the absorptive part. From now on R denotes the Hermitian part (Roriginal​+Roriginal†​)/2, so its entries have the form
R=(r0​z∗​zr0​​),r0​∈R,z=0.
(27)
If the original matrix already was Hermitian no replacement is needed. The neutral-kaon mass matrix diagonalization gives eigenvalues r0​±∣z∣, since (r0​−λ)2−∣z∣2=0. Write z=reiφ, r=∣z∣>0. Use the kaon mixing square-root branch convention
a=R12​​=r​eiφ/2,b=R21​​=r​e−iφ/2,ab=r,
(28)
continuously from the CP-conserving convention in which z>0. Independent unrelated root signs would interchange the eigenvalue labels. Direct multiplication gives R(a,−b)T=(r0​−r)(a,−b)T and R(a,b)T=(r0​+r)(a,b)T. Assigning the larger mass to KL0​ gives
∣KS0​⟩=2r​a∣K0⟩−b∣Kˉ0⟩​,∣KL0​⟩=2r​a∣K0⟩+b∣Kˉ0⟩​,​
(29)
with mass shifts r0​−r and r0​+r respectively. A common strong-interaction mass may simply be added to both. Their norms are one and their inner product is zero. A Hermitian mass-only calculation determines the lighter and heavier combinations; it does not determine their different lifetimes.
In the CP-conserving limit, the chosen lighter and heavier combinations are
∣K10​⟩=2​∣K0⟩−∣Kˉ0⟩​,∣K20​⟩=2​∣K0⟩+∣Kˉ0⟩​.​
(30)
They are CP eigenstates with eigenvalues +1 and −1 in the convention above. The sign of the real off-diagonal entry and the flavor-state convention are chosen together so that the stated K10​=KS0​ limit is the lower-mass state.
Changing to this CP basis gives
a∣K0⟩−b∣Kˉ0⟩a∣K0⟩+b∣Kˉ0⟩​=2​a+b​∣K10​⟩+2​a−b​∣K20​⟩,=2​a+b​∣K20​⟩+2​a−b​∣K10​⟩.​
(31)
Consequently, provided a+b=0, define
ϵ=a+ba−b​=R12​​+R21​​R12​​−R21​​​.​
(32)
Absorbing the common phase of a+b into the definitions of the mass eigenstates and normalizing now yields
∣KS0​⟩=1+∣ϵ∣2​∣K10​⟩+ϵ∣K20​⟩​,∣KL0​⟩=1+∣ϵ∣2​∣K20​⟩+ϵ∣K10​⟩​.​
(33)
For this Hermitian approximation, ϵ=itan(φ/2) is purely imaginary, so ⟨KS0​∣KL0​⟩=(ϵ+ϵ∗)/(1+∣ϵ∣2)=0. The phase convention matters for the kaon CP mixing parameter. Physical kaon decay eigenstates instead diagonalize the generally non-Hermitian matrix M−iΓ/2, for which the mixing parameter can have a real part and the eigenstates need not be orthogonal. The formula here is the requested mass-only result, not a calculation of that full decay dynamics.
