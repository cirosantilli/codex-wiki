A stable symmetry-breaking minimum requires λ>0 and m2<0. Put v2=−m2/λ>0. The common scalar potential can then be written, up to a constant, as
V=4λ​(Φ⋅Φ−v2)2.
(9)
Its minima form the sphere Φ⋅Φ=v2. Choose the vacuum direction Φ0​=(0,0,v)T. If instead m2>0 with positive λ, the minimum is at the origin and the assumed nonzero vacuum expectation value is not a stable vacuum of these models.
In the ungauged theory, the internal symmetry is global O(3); its connected part is SO(3). Constant orthogonal transformations preserve both the kinetic term and the potential. The chosen vacuum is invariant under O(2) acting on its first two components, so the continuous breaking is SO(3)→SO(2) with two broken generators. The vacuum orientations are genuinely different degenerate global vacua.
The global triplet scalar symmetry breaking is visible directly in the fluctuation masses. Write Φ=(π1​,π2​,v+h)T. The potential Hessian is
∂Φa​∂Φb​∂2V​​Φ0​​=(m2+λv2)δab​+2λΦ0a​Φ0b​=diag(0,0,2λv2).
(10)
The quadratic Lagrangian is therefore
L1(2)​=21​(∂h)2+21​(∂π1​)2+21​(∂π2​)2−21​(2λv2)h2.
(11)
There is one radial scalar of mass mh2​=2λv2=−2m2 and two massless Goldstone bosons. The two Goldstone modes describe motion tangential to the vacuum sphere. They remain physical, since a global rotation has only constant parameters and cannot remove arbitrary spacetime-dependent fluctuations. All three original real-scalar degrees of freedom remain. Interactions persist: the shifted potential is V−Vmin​=4λ​(2vh+h2+π12​+π22​)2, containing cubic and quartic terms. Masslessness of the angular modes is protected by the exact continuous symmetry through Goldstone's theorem.
In the gauged theory, the three-component real field is the vector representation of local SO(3), equivalently the Adjoint representation of SU(2). The center of SU(2) acts trivially on this field, so both descriptions have the same local field content and perturbative spectrum. Take (ta)bc​=−iϵabc, for which [ta,tb]=iϵabctc. The covariant kinetic term and the gauge kinetic term are invariant under local transformations with the associated connection transformation. The scalar inversion Φ↦−Φ, with the gauge field unchanged, is also a discrete invariance of the displayed action.
The vacuum leaves rotations about its third axis unbroken. The residual gauge group is SO(2), or the corresponding U(1) subgroup in the SU(2) description. Expanding the covariant kinetic term about Φ0​ gives
21​(Dμ​Φ0​)⋅(DμΦ0​)=21​g2v2(Bμ1​B1μ+Bμ2​B2μ).
(12)
Thus the gauge-boson mass matrix is g2v2diag(1,1,0). The third generator annihilates the vacuum, while the first two do not. The masses are mB1​=mB2​=gv, mB3​=0, and mh​=2λ​v.
The quadratic derivative mixing displays what happens to the Goldstone fields. With the generator convention just chosen,
(Dμ​Φ)1​=∂μ​π1​−gvBμ2​+⋯,(Dμ​Φ)2​=∂μ​π2​+gvBμ1​+⋯,(Dμ​Φ)3​=∂μ​h+⋯.
(13)
Local rotations can set the two angular fields to zero in unitary gauge. Then Φ=(0,0,v+h)T and
Lscalar​=21​(∂h)2+21​g2(v+h)2[(B1)2+(B2)2]−V(v+h).
(14)
The two Goldstone modes supply the longitudinal polarizations of the two massive vector bosons. They do not survive as additional physical massless scalars. The radial scalar is still physical, and B3 retains only two transverse polarizations. This adjoint triplet Higgs spectrum realizes the Higgs mechanism; the gauge group is only partially broken, so one massless gauge boson remains.
The degree-of-freedom check is
before: 3+3×2=9,after: 1+2×3+1×2=9.​
(15)
In contrast, the global theory has just the three scalar modes, including its two physical Goldstone bosons. A vacuum expectation value in the local theory is a choice of gauge-fixed description: the direction can be changed by gauge transformations and is not itself an observable. Gauge-independent masses and the physical degree count express the actual effect. Both theories retain Lorentz symmetry in the constant scalar vacuum.
