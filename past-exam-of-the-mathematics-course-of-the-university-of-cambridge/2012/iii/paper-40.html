Condition on N. Since the amounts are independent and identically distributed random variables and are independent of N, their moment-generating functions multiply:
E[etS∣N=n]=MX​(t)n.
(1)
Summing against the positive-support geometric distribution gives the geometric-sum moment-generating function
MS​(t)=n≥1∑​p(1−p)n−1MX​(t)n=1−(1−p)MX​(t)pMX​(t)​.​
(2)
The finite-transform domain is MX​(t)<∞ and (1−p)MX​(t)<1. The terms are nonnegative for real t, so if the latter condition fails the sum diverges. For t≤0 both conditions always hold, since the claims are positive; thus the same calculation always provides the Laplace transform of a nonnegative random variable. Positivity alone does not guarantee a finite moment-generating function on a positive neighbourhood of zero.
There is at least one strictly positive amount in the portfolio. Hence P(S=0)=0; zero carries no atom of a measure to add to the continuous laws in the next two parts.

For the exponential distribution of mean μ, MX​(t)=(1−μt)−1. Substitution into the geometric-sum moment-generating function gives
MS​(t)=p−μtp​(t<p/μ).
(3)
This is the moment-generating function of an exponential distribution with rate p/μ. By the uniqueness theorem for moment-generating functions, the geometric sum of exponential variables therefore has probability density function
fS​(s)=μp​e−ps/μ(s>0),fS​(s)=0(s<0).​
(4)
Its expected value is μ/p, agreeing with E[N]E[X]=μ/p in the random sum of independent claims formula.

MS​(t)=(β−t)2−(1−p)β2pβ2​.
(5)
Put r0​=1−p​, a=β(1−r0​) and b=β(1+r0​). Then 0<a<b, ab=pβ2, and
MS​(t)=a−ta​b−tb​(t<a).
(6)

For a single claim x, quota share reinsurance with retained fraction α∈[0,1] makes the direct insurer pay αx and the reinsurer pay (1−α)x. Under excess of loss reinsurance with retention M≥0, the direct insurer pays min(x,M) and the reinsurer pays the positive part (x−M)+​. Thus the concise payout pairs are
(xI​,xR​)=(αx,(1−α)x)quador(min(x,M),(x−M)+​).​
(10)
The cap in excess of loss reinsurance applies separately to every claim; it is not a cap on the entire annual aggregate.
For the following variance calculations take λ>0 and E[X2]<∞, so the displayed variances are finite. For any per-claim payout Y, the law of total variance in a compound Poisson distribution gives
Var(∑j=1N​Yj​)=E[N]Var(Y)+Var(N)(E[Y])2=λE[Y2].
(11)
The final term is the raw second moment, not the single-claim variance. Both parties' totals are retained compound Poisson aggregates, with different payout functions of the same claims; they are generally dependent.

A credibility estimate blends the risk's own sample mean with a population or prior expected value. In the basic equal-exposure form it is ZX+(1−Z)μ, where the credibility factor Z∈[0,1] is the weight attached to the individual experience. More data ordinarily increase that weight; greater within-risk variation relative to between-risk variation reduces it.
Write m(θ)=μ(θ) for the conditional claim expected value, keeping μ for the prior-level parameter. Normalization of the probability density function gives
q(θ)=∫0∞​p(x)e−θxdx.
(34)
At interior points of its finite domain, differentiation under the integral sign yields
q′(θ)=−∫0∞​xp(x)e−θxdx,m(θ)=−q(θ)q′(θ)​.​
(35)
This is an instance of the exponential-family derivative identities for natural parameter −θ. The minus sign is present in the PDF and is lost in the converted TeX. The conditional variance is likewise (logq)′′(θ).
Differentiate the log of the natural conjugate prior:
π(θ)π′(θ)​=−kq(θ)q′(θ)​−kμ=k(m(θ)−μ).
(36)
Integrate on a compact subinterval and then let its endpoints approach those of the parameter interval. The prescribed vanishing of the prior density eliminates the boundary term. Since m(θ)≥0, monotone convergence theorem justifies the limit of its expectation, and the identity gives
Eπ​[m(Θ)]=μ.​
(37)
In particular the assumed proper prior and boundary conditions themselves establish this first-moment integrability.
For T=∑i=1n​xi​ and x=T/n, conditional independence makes the likelihood function proportional to q(θ)−ne−θT. Multiplication by the prior distribution gives the Bayesian posterior
π(θ∣x)∝q(θ)−(k+n)e−θ(kμ+T).
(38)
Thus this is a conjugate prior family, with updated parameters
kn​=k+n,μn​=k+nkμ+T​.​
(39)
The integration-by-parts calculation for the posterior mean also needs vanishing posterior boundary values. Here that property can be checked, rather than assumed. Let a,b be the essential lower and upper endpoints of the claim support. Every nondegenerate tilted claim law has a<m(θ)<b, so the established prior mean satisfies a<μ<b. Supported observations have x∈[a,b]; hence a<μn​<b. At a finite parameter endpoint the prior's vanishing forces q(θ)→∞, because its exponential factor has a finite positive limit; the increased power q−kn​ makes the posterior vanish there too. At θ→+∞, choose a<d<μn​ with positive base-measure mass below d. Then q(θ)≥Ce−dθ and the unnormalized posterior is at most C−kn​e−kn​(μn​−d)θ→0. At θ→−∞, choose μn​<d<b with positive mass above d to obtain the corresponding bound C−kn​ekn​(d−μn​)θ→0. These bounds also ensure posterior propriety at infinite endpoints. This is the endpoint control for a Laplace-family conjugate posterior.
Apply the previous score integration to the Bayesian posterior. The natural conjugate credibility identity is
E[m(Θ)∣x]=μn​=n+kn​x+n+kk​μ.​
(40)
Thus Z=n/(n+k) is the credibility factor. This is an exact posterior mean, rather than an affine approximation to one.
For the specific shape-two gamma distribution, take
p(x)=x,q(θ)=θ−2,θ>0.​
(41)
Then m(θ)=2/θ and Var(X∣θ)=2/θ2. The gamma rate gamma conjugacy prior is
π(θ)=Γ(2k+1)(kμ)2k+1​θ2ke−kμθ,θ>0,​
(42)
that is, Θ∼Gamma(2k+1,rate kμ). Its normalizing constant in the notation of the question is c(μ,k)=Γ(2k+1)/(kμ)2k+1. The gamma distribution density vanishes at both zero and infinity for k>0. The posterior has shape 2k+2n+1 and rate kμ+T, so its conditional-mean posterior mean is again (kμ+T)/(k+n).
For the last calculation, gamma distribution integration gives
E[Θ−1]=2kkμ​=2μ​,E[Θ−2]=2k(2k−1)(kμ)2​.
(43)
The second identity requires the printed k>1/2. Therefore the expected process variance and variance of hypothetical means, denoted by a,b in this question, are

(44)
Consequently a/b=k, and the Bühlmann credibility factor agrees with the exact Bayesian weight:
Z=n+kn​=n+a/bn​.​
(45)
This is the exact Bühlmann credibility for gamma claims, expressed using the reciprocal rate as the random scale. The symbols a,b here are the process and between-risk variances, respectively; their roles should not be interchanged when comparing with other notation for the Bühlmann model.
