Fix the standard Jacobian matrix convention (Da)ij​=∂vj​​ai​. With column gradients, the commuting velocity derivative for kinetic transport is
G=∇v​f+t(Da)T∇x​f.​
(15)
If the symbol Ja​ is instead defined with derivative indices as rows, the printed expression is this same formula. Under the standard convention it needs a transpose.
To prove the claim, put D=∂t​+a(v)⋅∇x​. Differentiating Df=0 in vj​ gives
D(∂vj​​f)=−∑i​(∂vj​​ai​)∂xi​​f,D(t∑i​(∂vj​​ai​)∂xi​​f)=∑i​(∂vj​​ai​)∂xi​​f.
(16)
The terms cancel, so each component of G satisfies the transport equation. Multiplication by the smooth coefficient Da and differentiation preserve the same uniform compact support, hence G∈CT​ componentwise. At t=0, G(0)=∇v​fin​. The L2 norm calculation in part (d), summed over components, gives
∫∣T(Da)T∇x​f(T)+∇v​f(T)∣2dxdv=∫∣∇v​fin​∣2dxdv.​
(17)
One can also differentiate the explicit solution: the t(Da)T∇x​f term cancels the derivative of x−ta(v), leaving the initial velocity derivative transported along the characteristic curves.
The transpose matters. For the shear a(v)=(v1​+v2​,v2​), detDa=1 but Da=(Da)T. The printed field satisfies Dg=(Da−(Da)T)∇x​f, which is nonzero for a generic compactly supported datum. The untransposed formula is false with the standard convention.
