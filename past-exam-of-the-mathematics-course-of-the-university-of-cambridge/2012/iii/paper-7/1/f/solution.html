At t=1, choose nonnegative smooth functions ψ,χ with compact support and prescribe f(1,x,v)=ψ(x)χ(v). This is a legitimate transported solution with fin​(x,v)=ψ(x+v)χ(v). There is a second issue after replacing f(t) by fin​: the Jacobian determinant bounds give a local diffeomorphism, not necessarily a one-to-one map. Suppose additionally that a is injective. With y=x−ta(v), the change of variables formula and ∣detDa∣≥α1​ give

Surjectivity is not needed because the domain of integration can be enlarged. With injectivity, the corrected estimate has Ca​=1/α1​. The upper bound α2​ is unnecessary.
More generally, dispersion with a nonlinear velocity map uses the area formula to sum over inverse branches. Define the weighted inverse multiplicity
ma​(w)=∑v:a(v)=w​∣detDa(v)∣1​.
(11)
If esssupw​ma​(w)<∞, the same argument gives the corrected estimate with Ca​=∥ma​∥∞​. At most N inverse branches give Ca​≤N/α1​. Thus the missing global assumption concerns multiplicity, not merely local volume distortion.
For a noninjective map with constant Jacobian determinant giving a counterexample in two dimensions, write v=(s,r) and set
a(s,r)=es(cos(re−2s),sin(re−2s)).
(12)
The polar coordinates calculation gives detDa=e2se−2s=1, yet a(0,2πk)=(1,0) for every integer k. To turn this into a failure of the estimate, take a small open disk W about (1,0) that avoids the origin. For each of N inverse branches over W, choose a smooth cutoff χk​(v)=χ(a(v)) supported on that branch, where χ has support strictly inside W and is extended by zero. These velocity supports are disjoint. Set
fin​(x,v)=ψ(x)∑k=1N​χk​(v).
