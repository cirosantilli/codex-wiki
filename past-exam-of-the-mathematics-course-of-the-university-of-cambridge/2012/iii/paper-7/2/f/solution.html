Literally, the printed hypothesis with positive C is impossible for pairs at distance less than one, even when U is constant. Use the corrected log-Lipschitz modulus μ from part (e). Local existence for the continuous finite-dimensional vector field follows from the Peano existence theorem. Boundedness gives ∣X(t)−X(s)∣≤∥U∥∞​∣t−s∣. Thus a trajectory cannot escape to infinity in finite time; at a finite endpoint it has a limit, and local existence there extends it. Solutions exist globally.
For uniqueness, let X,Y have the same initial point and set δ(t)=∣X(t)−Y(t)∣. This absolutely continuous function satisfies, almost everywhere,
δ′(t)≤Cμ(δ(t)),δ(0)=0.
(30)
This is the Osgood uniqueness criterion, rather than the usual linear Gronwall inequality, because the velocity need not be Lipschitz continuous. To see the argument directly, put wε​=δ+ε. As long as wε​≤1, monotonicity of μ gives
wε′​≤Cwε​(1−logwε​).
(31)
For zε​=1−logwε​, this becomes zε′​≥−Czε​. Integration gives
wε​(t)≤exp(1−(1−logε)e−Ct).
(32)
For any fixed finite interval, this upper bound tends uniformly to zero as ε↓0. Taking ε small prevents a first exit through wε​=1, so the bound remains valid throughout that interval. Thus δ=0. Backward time has the same proof. Every initial point has exactly one global trajectory.

∣X(t)−Y(t)∣≤e1−e−Ct∣X(0)−Y(0)∣e−Ct

until the separation reaches one. This quantitative continuous dependence makes the flow a homeomorphism, although it need not be a smooth diffeomorphism.
