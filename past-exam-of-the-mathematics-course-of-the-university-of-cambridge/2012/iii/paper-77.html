Use the layer depth d, the thermal diffusion time d2/κ, velocity κ/d, and the imposed temperature difference ΔT as scales. Let θ be the temperature departure from the conductive profile, u the velocity, w=u⋅z^, and let pressure absorb hydrostatic terms. The dimensionless Boussinesq equations for Rayleigh-Bénard convection are
∇⋅u=0,σ1​(∂t​u+u⋅∇u)=−∇p+Rθz^+∇2u,∂t​θ+u⋅∇θ=w+∇2θ.
(1)
The Prandtl number and Rayleigh number are σ=ν/κ and R=gαT​ΔTd3/(νκ), with kinematic viscosity ν, thermal diffusivity κ and coefficient of thermal expansion αT​. At z=0,1, the stress-free boundary condition and perfectly conducting thermal boundary condition give w=0, ∂z​ux​=∂z​uy​=0, and θ=0.
For the fundamental vertical normal mode, take w=Wsin(πz)eik⋅x+λt and θ=ϑsin(πz)eik⋅x+λt. Here k=∣k∣>0 is the horizontal wavenumber. With s=k2+π2, projection onto incompressible flow eliminates pressure and gives
(λ/σ+s)sW=Rk2ϑ,(λ+s)ϑ=W.
(2)
Thus the stress-free convection growth-rate polynomial is (λ+σs)(λ+s)=σRk2/s.
For the infinite-Prandtl number limit, the finite thermal growth rate is
λ∞​(k)=(k2+π2)2Rk2​−(k2+π2).​
(3)
The other, viscously damped root tends to −σs. For σ=1, both roots are explicit:
λ±​(k)=−s±sRk2​​.​
(4)
The plus root determines instability. A higher vertical harmonic replaces π2 by n2π2; the fundamental is the most favorable one.
For the large-R maximum in the infinite-Prandtl number limit, put x=k2 and p=π2. Differentiating gives dλ∞​/dx=R(p−x)/(p+x)3−1. The unique maximum for sufficiently large R therefore satisfies R(p−x)=(p+x)3, whence
kmax2​=p−R8p3​+O(R−2)≃π2,λmax​=4π2R​−2π2+O(R−1)≃4π2R​.​
(5)
This is fastest growth in infinite-Prandtl convection. The large-R expansion is taken after the infinite-Prandtl number limit; at finite σ, neglecting inertia additionally requires ∣λ∣≪σs. It selects the fastest growing mode far above onset, rather than the neutral-curve minimum k2=π2/2.

The Lorenz model of thermosolutal convection is a Galerkin method truncation retaining a roll velocity and the leading temperature and concentration modes. The variable a measures the roll velocity; b is the temperature mode correlated with the vertical motion, while c is the horizontally averaged temperature-profile distortion generated by that motion. The corresponding concentration modes are d and e. The symbol d in this model is a solute amplitude, not the dimensional layer depth. The terms ab and ad change the mean scalar profiles; a(1−c) and a(1−e) express advection of the remaining background gradients.
The positive parameters are the Prandtl number σ=ν/κ, the ratio τ=κs​/κ of solutal diffusivity to thermal diffusivity, and the geometric vertical-mode decay ratio ϖ. In the usual fundamental-roll truncation with time normalized to the thermal roll-mode decay rate, ϖ=4π2/(k2+π2); treating it as an independently specified positive truncation parameter leaves the arguments below unchanged.
The parameter r is the destabilizing thermal Rayleigh number divided by the free-slip critical-mode value, and rs​>0 measures stabilizing solutal buoyancy in the same thermal-diffusive normalization. For example, using a positive bottom-to-top stabilizing solute difference ΔS and density coefficient βS​,
r=νκR0​(k)gαT​ΔTdlayer3​​,rs​=νκR0​(k)gβS​ΔSdlayer3​​,R0​(k)=k2(k2+π2)3​.
(6)
If a solutal Rayleigh number is instead defined with κs​ in its denominator, the coefficient rs​ here is τ times that normalized number. Thermal buoyancy drives the roll; solutal buoyancy opposes it and diffuses at a different rate. This is the essential mechanism of thermosolutal convection.

Take β≥0 without loss of generality, since its definition fixes only β2. With rs​=β2τ, the steady-branch minimum expands as rmin​=1+2βτ+O(τ2). For fixed positive σ, the source's supplied approximation therefore gives
rsupplied(0)​−rmin​=τ(δ+δβ2​−2β)+O(τ2)=δτ​(β−δ)2+O(τ2)≥0to the retained order.

Thus the requested inequality follows from the supplied approximation, with equality at first order when β=δ. If negative β is retained, replace it by ∣β∣ throughout.
There is an independent source-consistency issue with calling that supplied expression the oscillatory threshold of the displayed model. Linearizing about conduction, the c,e modes decay separately and the a,b,d characteristic polynomial is
(λ+σ)(λ+1)(λ+τ)−σr(λ+τ)+σrs​(λ+1)=0.
(13)
Writing it as λ3+Aλ2+Bλ+C, a nonzero imaginary pair requires C=AB and B>0, by the Routh-Hurwitz stability criterion. Direct algebra gives the oscillatory threshold of a thermosolutal Lorenz model
rH​=1+στ(σ+1+τ)​+σ+1σ+τ​rs​,ωH2​=σ+1σ​rs​(1−τ)−τ2.

Consequently, with δ=σ/(1+σ),
rH​=1+δτ​+β2τδ+O(τ2).​
(15)
The two factors of δ in the supplied threshold are interchanged. For example σ=1, β=2, τ=10−3 gives a genuine marginal imaginary pair at rH​=1.004003, whereas the supplied first-order expression gives 1.0085. The intended comparison remains true after correction:
rH​−rmin​=δτ​(βδ−1)2+O(τ2)≥0 to first order.​
(16)
This comparison applies to a physical Hopf bifurcation when ωH2​>0 and to an interior steady fold when a∗2​>0. For fixed nonzero β and fixed σ>0, both conditions hold for sufficiently small positive τ. At a vanishing squared difference, neglected terms must be examined before drawing a higher-order conclusion.

For the real-coefficient cubic-quintic amplitude equation, the trivial pattern A=0 exists for every μ. A nonzero spatially uniform pattern has arbitrary constant phase and intensity q=∣A∣2>0 satisfying μ+αq−q2=0. Thus
q±​=2α±α2+4μ​​.​
(17)
Nonzero patterns exist for μ≥−α2/4. The upper branch q+​ exists throughout that range; a distinct positive lower branch q−​ exists only for −α2/4<μ<0 and reaches zero at μ=0.
For a homogeneous perturbation, write A=Reiϕ. Then R˙=R(μ+αR2−R4) and ϕ˙​=0. The radial eigenvalue at a nonzero pattern is
λR​=μ+3αq−5q2=2q(α−2q),
(18)
while the phase eigenvalue is zero. Therefore the upper branch is stable modulo its arbitrary phase for μ>−α2/4; the lower branch is unstable. The phase-neutral direction prevents asymptotic attraction to one particular complex amplitude, but permits orbital stability of the circle of equivalent patterns.
Conduction is asymptotically stable for μ<0 and unstable for μ>0. At μ=0, the positive cubic term destabilizes arbitrarily small nonzero amplitudes, even though its linear eigenvalue vanishes. At μ=−α2/4, the merged nonzero branch is semistable from above only: R˙=−R(R2−α/2)2, so amplitudes just below it eventually decrease toward zero. Thus the bistable interval is −α2/4<μ<0, with the endpoints treated separately rather than declared stable merely because an eigenvalue is zero.

Assume α>0, so a finite nonzero critical wavenumber exists. The linear growth rate of a Fourier mode is λ(k)=−α+μk2−k4. Maximizing over k and setting that maximum to zero gives
μc​=2α​,kc​=α1/4.​
(23)
Set K=kc2​=α​. The resonant three-wave triad has wavevectors satisfy k1​+k2​+k3​=0 and ki​⋅kj​=−K/2 for i=j. With the slow time and amplitude scaling, every growth, quadratic and cubic term first enters at order ϵ3. Projection onto each critical mode removes the nonresonant correction Θ3​ by the method of multiple scales solvability condition.
The parameter displacement contributes μ2​KA to the first mode. For the quadratic term, use ∇⋅(Θ1​∇Θ1​)=21​∇2(Θ12​). The resonant coefficient of Θ12​ at k1​ is 2BˉCˉ, so its contribution is +γKBˉCˉ.
For the cubic gradient term, an ordered triple of modes with wavevectors p,q,r summing to k1​ contributes (p⋅q)(k1​⋅r) times its amplitude product. The three self-interaction permutations yield −3K2∣A∣2A. The six permutations of (k1​,k2​,−k2​) yield −2K2−4(k1​⋅k2​)2=−3K2, and likewise for the third mode. Therefore the hexagonal convection amplitude equations are
AT​BT​CT​​=μ2​KA+γKBˉCˉ−3K2A(∣A∣2+∣B∣2+∣C∣2),=μ2​KB+γKCˉAˉ−3K2B(∣A∣2+∣B∣2+∣C∣2),=μ2​KC+γKAˉBˉ−3K2C(∣A∣2+∣B∣2+∣C∣2).​​
(24)
For a nonzero convection roll, B=C=0 and ∣A∣2=μ2​/(3K), requiring μ2​>0. Choose its phase so A=a>0. The radial perturbation decays, but a perturbation in the two other orientations gives
∂T​(BCˉ​)=(0γKa​γKa0​)(BCˉ​).
(25)
The eigenvalues are ±∣γ∣Ka. Thus
γ=0⟹every nonzero roll is unstable.​
(26)
This is roll instability from broken up-down symmetry. A growing homogeneous amplitude perturbation already proves instability, without a sideband calculation. The source's slightly asymmetric setting means nonzero quadratic coupling. If γ=0 is allowed literally, the displayed leading equations instead have a continuous sphere of constant-total-intensity states and neutral orientation perturbations. They then establish marginality, not strict growing-mode instability; higher-order effects would decide further selection. Likewise α=0 would invalidate the assumed finite-kc​ triad scaling.

Use the same length, thermal-time, velocity and temperature scales as in thermal convection. Write the imposed magnetic field as B0​z^ and measure its perturbation in units of B0​. The physical linear magnetohydrodynamic momentum equation contains magnetic tension (B02​/μ0​)∂z​b; the gradient part of the Lorentz force density is absorbed into magnetohydrodynamic total pressure.
Divide the momentum equation by the viscous force scale ρ0​νκ/d3. Its acceleration coefficient is κ/ν=1/σ and its magnetic coefficient is
μ0​ρ0​νκB02​d2​=Qζ,ζ=κη​,Q=μ0​ρ0​ηνB02​d2​.
(27)
Here Q is the Chandrasekhar number, comparing magnetic and viscous-diffusive effects, and ζ is the ratio of magnetic diffusivity to thermal diffusivity. The induction scale is B0​κ/d2, so linearization gives
σ1​u˙=−∇P+Qζ∂z​b+Rθz^+∇2u,b˙=∂z​u+ζ∇2b.​
(28)
Complete the linear system with θ˙=w+∇2θ, ∇⋅u=0 and the solenoidal magnetic-field constraint ∇⋅b=0. The omitted advective and perturbed-field tension terms are quadratic in the disturbances. Magnetic tension resists bending of the vertical field, while diffusion allows that bending to relax.

Interpret perfect conductivity here as the perfectly conducting thermal boundary condition θ=0; impose the stated magnetic verticality separately. Together with the stress-free boundary condition, admissible vertical profiles are
w=Wsinπz,θ=ϑsinπz,uh​=Uh​cosπz,bz​=Zcosπz,bh​=Bh​sinπz,

each multiplied by eik⋅x+λt. Solenoidality requires ik⋅Uh​=−πW and ik⋅Bh​=πZ. The resistive induction equation preserves both profile choices. In particular bh​=0 at each boundary and ∂z​bz​=0 there.
