For three players, the predecessor-set weights are 1/3 for sizes zero and two, and 1/6 for size one. Thus
ϕ1​ϕ2​ϕ3​​=31​(4)+61​(10−3)+61​(11−2)+31​(12−5)=319​,=31​(3)+61​(10−4)+61​(5−2)+31​(12−11)=617​,=31​(2)+61​(11−4)+61​(5−3)+31​(12−10)=617​.​
(30)
Therefore the Shapley value is (19/3,17/6,17/6)​, whose coordinates sum to 12.
For the nucleolus, use imputations x1​+x2​+x3​=12, x1​≥4, x2​≥3, x3​≥2. The excess of {1,3} is 11−(12−x2​)=x2​−1≥2. Thus the smallest possible largest excess is at least 2. It is attained when x2​=3, x3​=9−x1​, and 5≤x1​≤7: the remaining proper-coalition excesses then are
e1​=4−x1​,e2​=0,e3​=x1​−7,e12​=7−x1​,e23​=x1​−7,
(31)
all at most 2, while e13​=2. Conversely, a largest excess of 2 forces x2​=3 and precisely this interval for x1​.
On this first-stage face the top excess 2 is fixed. The next largest excess is 7−x1​≥0, because the other varying excesses are nonpositive and e2​=0 is fixed. Its unique minimum is zero at x1​=7. No further lexicographic tie remains. Hence the nucleolus is (7,3,2)​. Its proper-coalition excesses, sorted decreasingly, are (2,0,0,0,0,−3).
Finally, a core allocation would require x2​≥3 and x1​+x3​≥11, whose sum contradicts the efficient total 12. Thus the core of a cooperative game is empty. The game is not superadditive, so neither core nonemptiness nor individual rationality of the Shapley value should be presumed. Under the distinct prenucleolus convention the answer would be (15/2,2,5/2). Balancing e2​=3−x2​ and e13​=x2​−1 gives a first-stage maximum of 1 and forces x2​=2. The remaining first-stage constraints restrict x1​ to [7,8]. The next largest complaints are e12​=8−x1​ and e23​=x1​−7, balanced at x1​=15/2, with x3​=5/2. This is not an imputation and is not the nucleolus under the definition in (a).
