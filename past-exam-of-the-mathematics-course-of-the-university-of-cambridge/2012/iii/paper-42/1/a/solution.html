For a minimization problem on a domain D, write the equality constraints as h(x)=0 and the inequality constraints as g(x)≤0. Define the optimization Lagrangian
L(x,λ,μ)=f(x)+λTh(x)+μTg(x),μ≥0.
(1)
The Lagrangian sufficiency theorem states that a feasible x∗ is globally optimal if it globally minimizes L(⋅,λ∗,μ∗) on D for some multipliers with μ∗≥0 and satisfies complementary slackness, μj∗​gj​(x∗)=0 for every j. Equality multipliers have unrestricted signs.
For any feasible x, the multiplier sign gives L(x,λ∗,μ∗)≤f(x). Feasibility and complementary slackness at x∗ give L(x∗,λ∗,μ∗)=f(x∗). Hence
f(x)≥L(x,λ∗,μ∗)≥L(x∗,λ∗,μ∗)=f(x∗).​
(2)
This proves sufficiency. Global Lagrangian minimization, rather than stationarity alone, is the certificate. No convexity or constraint qualification is needed for this implication; convexity is one way to verify the required global minimum.
