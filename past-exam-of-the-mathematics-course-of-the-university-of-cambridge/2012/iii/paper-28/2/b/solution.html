For p=2, the even residue class contains exactly one root, by the simple-root form of Hensel lemma at a=0: f(0)=20 and f′(0)=−5 is a unit. For the odd classes use the strong form of Hensel lemma:
f(1)=16,f′(1)=−2;f(3)=32,f′(3)=22.

In both cases v2​(f(a))>2v2​(f′(a))=2. Each gives exactly one root in a+4Z2​. These two balls partition the odd integers, so there are no further roots.
For p=3, reduction gives f​=X3+X+2, whose sole root is 2. The derivative 3X2−5 is always a unit modulo 3, so that root lifts uniquely. For p=5, the only possible residue is zero. But if x∈5Z5​, then x3−5x∈25Z5​ while 20 has valuation one, so f(x) cannot vanish. The exact root counts are
#{f=0 in Z2​}=3,#{f=0 in Z3​}=1,#{f=0 in Z5​}=0.​
