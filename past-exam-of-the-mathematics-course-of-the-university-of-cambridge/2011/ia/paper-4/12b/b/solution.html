The incident four-momentum has energy E and spatial momentum p=γmv, while the stationary target has energy mc2 and zero spatial momentum. By the relativistic energy-momentum relation, c2p2=E2−m2c4. For the fixed-target threshold for production of equal-mass particles, the squared total centre-of-momentum energy is
W2=(E+mc2)2−c2p2=2mc2(E+mc2).
(76)
In the centre-of-momentum frame, each of the final N particles has energy at least its rest energy mc2. Four-momentum conservation therefore requires W≥Nmc2, and thus
E≥2N2−2​mc2.​
(77)
For the physically relevant N≥2, this is equivalent to γ≥(N2−2)/2. The speed is an increasing function of γ, so
cv​=1−γ−2​≥1−(N2−2)24​​=N2−2NN2−4​​.
(78)
Consequently
v≥N2−2NN2−4​​c.​
(79)
Equality is the kinematic threshold at which all final particles are at rest in their common centre-of-momentum frame. It shows why a collision with a stationary target needs more incident energy than merely the additional rest energy: some laboratory energy must remain as collective motion. A one-particle final state of the specified mass, with no other outgoing energy, is impossible because its invariant energy would be mc2, whereas the two-particle initial state has invariant energy at least 2mc2.
