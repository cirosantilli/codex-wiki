In the parametric description, a is a point of the straight line and the nonzero vector b gives its direction. The real parameter runs through every point of that line. For the cross product description, take another cross product with c and use the vector triple product identity:
c×(x×c)=∣c∣2x−(c⋅x)c=c×d.
(11)
Hence every solution has the representation
x=a+λ(x)b,a=∣c∣2c×d​,b=c,λ(x)=∣c∣2c⋅x​.​
(12)
Here a⋅b=0 and ∣b∣=∣c∣, as required. Conversely,
(a+λc)×c=∣c∣2(c×d)×c​=d−∣c∣2(c⋅d)c​=d.
(13)
Thus the perpendicularity assumption is sufficient, and the solutions form a straight line parallel to c. When d=0, the vector d is perpendicular both to c and to every position vector on the line; it is the normal to the plane through the origin containing that line. The point a=c×d/∣c∣2 is the perpendicular foot from the origin. Its distance from the origin is ∣d∣/∣c∣, since c⋅d=0.
For a general parametric straight line, minimize the squared Euclidean distance
∣a+λb−y∣2=∣a−y∣2+2λb⋅(a−y)+λ2∣b∣2.
(14)
This strictly convex quadratic function has its unique minimum at λ=b⋅(y−a)/∣b∣2, giving the orthogonal projection
xnearest​=a+∣b∣2b⋅(y−a)​b.​
(15)
For the cross product description, a⋅c=0, so this becomes
xnearest​=∣c∣2c×d​+∣c∣2c⋅y​c.​
(16)
For the two planes, put s=m⋅n and c=m×n. Nonparallel unit normals give ∣s∣<1 and ∣c∣2=1−s2. Seek the perpendicular foot in their span, a=um+vn. Its two dot products impose u+sv=μ and su+v=ν. Solving gives both requested descriptions of the intersection:
x=1−s2(μ−sν)m+(ν−sμ)n​+λ(m×n),λ∈R,​
(17)
and
x×(m×n)=νm−μn.​
(18)
Indeed the vector triple product identity gives x×(m×n)=m(x⋅n)−n(x⋅m). Conversely, since m,n are linearly independent, equality to νm−μn forces both plane equations. Thus neither description introduces extra points.
