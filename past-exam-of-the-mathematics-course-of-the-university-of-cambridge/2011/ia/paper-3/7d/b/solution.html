By Lagrange's theorem, a stabilizer subgroup of G has order pj for some 0≤j≤n. The orbit-stabilizer theorem therefore gives
∣Y∣=pn−j.
(39)
Thus an orbit of a group action either has one element or has size divisible by p, even if the acted-on set is infinite.
Apply this to the conjugation action. Its singleton orbits of a group action are the elements of the centre of a group, while every other orbit has size divisible by p. Partitioning the finite group into these orbits gives the class equation, and hence
∣Z(G)∣≡∣G∣≡0(modp).
(40)
Since the identity belongs to Z(G), its order is positive and divisible by p. Consequently
∣Z(G)∣≥p>1.​
(41)
Now suppose that ∣Z(G)∣=pn−1. It is then proper, so choose x∈/Z(G). The centralizer
CG​(x)={g∈G:gx=xg}
(42)
is a subgroup: commuting with x is preserved by multiplication and inverses. It contains Z(G) and also x, so ∣CG​(x)∣>∣Z(G)∣=pn−1. By Lagrange's theorem, its order is a power of p dividing pn, forcing CG​(x)=G. But that would make x central, a contradiction. Therefore
∣Z(G)∣=pn−1.​
(43)
