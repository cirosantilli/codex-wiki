With t=1−x2 the chain rule gives y′=−2xu′ and y′′=−2u′+4x2u′′. Substitution, initially for x>0, gives
t(1−t)u′′+(1−t)u′+41​u=0.​
(34)
The transformed coefficients are P(t)=1/t and Q(t)=1/[4t(1−t)]. At t=0, both tP and t2Q are analytic; at t=1, both (t−1)P and (t−1)2Q are analytic. Each point is singular because a normalized coefficient has a pole, but satisfies the regular singular point criterion for a second-order equation. Thus both points are regular singular points.

For a second independent solution, normalize u1​(0)=1. Reduction of order and P=1/t give

Since u1​(s)−2=1+O(s), this has the Logarithmic Frobenius solution form
