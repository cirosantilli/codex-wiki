For an open cover X=A∪B, the Mayer–Vietoris theorem gives the long exact sequence
⋯→Hj​(A∩B)(i∗​,−j∗​)​Hj​(A)⊕Hj​(B)→Hj​(X)→Hj−1​(A∩B)→⋯.
(60)
The same sequence holds for a union of simplicial subcomplexes. All homology groups here have integer coefficients.
An explicit triangulation of Xq​ is as follows. Take vertices b0​,b1​,b2​ forming a target circle, a circle of 3q vertices a0​,…,a3q−1​, and a cone vertex c. With subscripts on a interpreted modulo 3q and on b modulo three, include triangles
[aj​,aj+1​,bj+1​],[aj​,bj+1​,bj​],[c,aj​,aj+1​]
(61)
for every j, together with all their faces. The first two types triangulate the mapping cylinder of the degree-q covering aj​↦bj​; the last type fills the inner circle by a disc. A filled disc with an attached annular collar is still a disc, and identifying the outer collar boundary by this covering gives exactly the source quotient. Distinct inner vertices ensure this is a genuine simplicial complex, not just a one-cell presentation.
Let A be the mapping cylinder and B the coned disc. Then A retracts to the target circle, B is contractible and A∩B is the inner circle. Its inclusion into A induces multiplication by q on H1​. The exact sequence therefore reduces to
0→H2​(Xq​)→Zq​Z→H1​(Xq​)→0.
(62)
Connectedness and the absence of higher-dimensional simplices give
H0​(Xq​)=Z,H1​(Xq​)=Z/qZ,Hj​(Xq​)=0(j≥2).​
(63)
This is a two-dimensional Moore space with torsion in degree one.
The suspension of a connected complex is the union of two cones whose intersection retracts to the original complex. Reduced Mayer-Vietoris gives
Hj​(SK)≅Hj−1​(K)(j≥1),H0​(SK)=Z.​
(64)
In particular its H1​ vanishes. For the wedge of two connected polyhedra, choose neighborhoods retracting onto the two summands with contractible intersection at the wedge point. Then
Hj​(X∨Y)≅Hj​(X)⊕Hj​(Y).​
(65)
Decompose each prescribed Gi​ into cyclic summands. Realize each infinite cyclic summand in degree i by Si, and each finite nontrivial summand Z/q by Si−1Xq​, meaning i−1 successive suspensions. Take their finite wedge, or a point if there are no summands. The suspension formula shifts the torsion to degree i and creates no other positive-degree homology, while the wedge formula adds summands. Thus the resulting connected polyhedron satisfies H0​(X)=Z, Hi​(X)=Gi​ (1≤i≤n), Hi​(X)=0 (i>n)​. The finite triangulations above, their simplicial suspensions and wedges provide an actual polyhedron.
