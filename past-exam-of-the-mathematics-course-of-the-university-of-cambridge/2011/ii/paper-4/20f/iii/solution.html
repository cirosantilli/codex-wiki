Since 7≡3(mod4), the ring of integers is Z[7​]. A unit a+b7​ has norm a2−7b2=±1. Norm minus one is impossible: it would give a2≡−1(mod7), whereas the squares modulo seven are 0,1,2,4. The element ε=8+37​ has norm one and exceeds one.
Suppose a positive unit u in the chosen real embedding satisfies 1<u<ε. Its conjugate is 1/u>0, so
b=27​u−u−1​>0.
(58)
This expression is strictly increasing in u, and at u=ε it equals three. Thus the integer b is one or two. But then a2=7b2+1 is eight or twenty-nine, neither a square. Therefore there is no positive unit strictly between one and ε.
For any positive unit u, choose an integer k such that 1≤uε−k<ε. The remaining unit must be one, so u=εk. Multiplication by minus one handles negative units. Hence
OK×​={±(8+37​)k:k∈Z},​
(59)
which proves that 8+37​ is fundamental, using no continued fractions.
