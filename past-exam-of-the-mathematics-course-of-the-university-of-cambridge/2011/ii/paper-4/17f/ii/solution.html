Past exam of the mathematics course of the University of Cambridge / 2011 / ii / Paper 4 / 17F / ii / Solution

 ... Past exam of the mathematics course of the University of Cambridge 2011 ii Paper 4 17F ii
OurBigBook.com Words: 270
In any two-colouring of K7​, there is a monochromatic triangle because f(2)=6. Call its colour blue. If any edge from one of its vertices to an outside vertex is blue, the triangle and that edge give the required copy of H. Otherwise all edges from that triangle to the four outside vertices are yellow. If any edge among those four is yellow, it makes a yellow triangle with a vertex of the original triangle, and another incident yellow edge supplies the pendant edge. If none is yellow, the four outside vertices form a blue K4​, which also contains H. Hence g(2)≤7.
For the lower bound, divide six vertices into two triples. Colour edges inside each triple blue and all cross-edges yellow. Each blue component is only a triangle, and the yellow graph is bipartite, so neither colour contains H. Therefore g(2)=7​. Copies are not required to be induced: an additional edge in a monochromatic K4​ does not invalidate the copy of H.
For three colours, f(3)=17 supplies a triangle in K17​. If it has no pendant edge of the same colour, each of its vertices has fourteen edges to outside vertices in the other two colours. Choose one vertex v and a colour occurring on at least seven of those edges, with endpoint set S. If an internal edge of S has this colour, it creates a triangle with v and a fourth neighbour supplies the pendant edge. Otherwise S is internally two-coloured, and g(2)=7 supplies H. Thus g(3)≤17. Conversely the definition f(3)=17 ensures a three-colouring of K16​ without any monochromatic triangle, and therefore without H. Hence g(3)=17​.
