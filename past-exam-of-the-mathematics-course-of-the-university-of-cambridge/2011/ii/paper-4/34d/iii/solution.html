At a fixed subcritical temperature, coexistence requires equal pressure and equal chemical potential of the liquid and gas. The Maxwell equal-area construction chooses p0​ and the two outer intersection volumes Vl​,Vg​ so that
p(Vl​)=p(Vg​)=p0​,∫Vl​Vg​​[p(V)−p0​]dV=0.​
(116)
Indeed dμ=(V/N)dp, and integration by parts gives N(μg​−μl​)=p0​(Vg​−Vl​)−∫Vl​Vg​​p(V)dV, so the equal-area condition is exactly equality of chemical potentials.
Let Vs,l​<Vs,g​ be the two spinodal volumes where p′(V)=0. A homogeneous phase is mechanically unstable on Vs,l​<V<Vs,g​, since p′>0 gives negative compressibility. The negative-slope branches Vl​<V<Vs,l​ and Vs,g​<V<Vg​ are metastable homogeneous liquid and gas respectively: they resist small perturbations but a two-phase state has lower free energy. The single-phase stable branches are V<Vl​ and V>Vg​. Throughout the coexistence interval [Vl​,Vg​], the equilibrium state is a mixture at pressure p0​ with its phase fractions chosen to produce the total volume.
