A geodesic is a curve with zero covariant acceleration in an affine parameter. For a unit-speed curve on an oriented surface with unit normal N, its geodesic curvature is kg​=⟨γ′′,N×γ′⟩: it measures the tangent-plane component of acceleration perpendicular to the tangent. A unit-speed curve is geodesic exactly when this component vanishes.
The exponential map expp​(v) follows for unit time the geodesic starting at p with initial velocity v, whenever it is defined. Since expp​(tv)=γv​(t), its derivative at zero is d(expp​)0​=idTp​S​​. Choose an oriented orthonormal basis of Tp​S and let v(θ) be its unit circle. Geodesic polar coordinates are F(r,θ)=expp​(rv(θ)) in a normal neighborhood, with the usual angular singularity at r=0.
Put T=Fr​ and J=Fθ​. Radial curves are unit-speed geodesics, so E=⟨T,T⟩=1 and ∇r​T=0. The torsion-free connection gives ∇r​J=∇θ​T, and therefore
∂r​⟨T,J⟩=⟨T,∇θ​T⟩=21​∂θ​⟨T,T⟩=0.
(69)
Since J(0,θ)=0, this proves F=⟨T,J⟩=0, the radial form of the Gauss lemma. Moreover J(r,θ)=rv′(θ)+O(r2), with ∣v′∣=1, by the derivative of the exponential map. Hence G=∣J∣2=r2+O(r3), giving
E=1,F=0,G(0,θ)=0,(G​)r​(0,θ)=1.​

The last derivative is the one-sided radial limit.
The global Gauss-Bonnet theorem for a compact oriented surface with piecewise smooth boundary is
∫S​KdA+∫∂S​kg​ds+j∑​εj​=2πχ(S).​
(71)
Here K is Gaussian curvature, the boundary is positively oriented with the surface on its left, εj​ are signed exterior turning angles at corners, and χ is the Euler characteristic. Smooth geodesic boundary components contribute neither curvature nor corner terms.
A contractible simple closed geodesic on the cylinder would bound a disc, giving ∫KdA=2π, impossible when K<0. Thus any such geodesic is an essential loop. If two distinct essential simple loops on an annulus intersect, the elementary bigon criterion gives two subarcs bounding an innermost disc: lifting the homotopic loops to the covering strip and choosing an innermost region supplies such a bigon. Distinct geodesics cannot have tangential intersections by uniqueness of the geodesic initial-value problem, so its two interior angles α,β are positive. Gauss-Bonnet on this geodesic bigon gives ∫KdA=α+β>0, another contradiction. Therefore two distinct simple closed geodesics would be disjoint. They would then bound a compact annulus with geodesic boundary and Euler characteristic zero, forcing ∫KdA=0, again impossible. There is at most one simple closed geodesic. Completeness of the noncompact surface is not needed for these compact-region arguments.
