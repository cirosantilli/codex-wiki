With s=r2, its nonzero stationary amplitudes solve βs2−2αs+8=0. If α2>8β, both roots are positive and simple:
r±2​=βα±α2−8β​​.​
(13)
The averaged drift is positive below r−​, negative between r−​ and r+​, and positive above r+​. Thus the inner orbit is attracting and the outer orbit repelling, with leading displacement amplitudes r−​ and r+​ and period 2π+O(ϵ). The simple zeros of the Poincaré return map persist for sufficiently small ϵ.
If α2<8β, the leading quadratic is strictly positive for all s≥0; the energy increases on every nonzero unperturbed orbit, so the energy balance method yields no periodic orbits in any fixed compact range of nonzero amplitudes for sufficiently small ϵ. This perturbative calculation does not itself establish a uniform global exclusion at amplitudes growing as ϵ tends to zero. At equality, the leading drift has a double zero at r2=α/β. It is positive on both sides, with a semistable candidate at that amplitude. First-order averaging alone cannot decide whether the exact system has zero, one, or two nearby periodic orbits at equality: higher-order drift determines the unfolding of this degenerate zero. The original PDF has α2=8β; the extra exponent on β in the converted TeX is an OCR error.
