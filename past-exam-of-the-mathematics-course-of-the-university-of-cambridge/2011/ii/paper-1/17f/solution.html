Past exam of the mathematics course of the University of Cambridge / 2011 / ii / Paper 1 / 17F / Solution

 ... Past exam of the mathematics course of the University of Cambridge 2011 ii Paper 1 Section II 17F
OurBigBook.com Words: 281
A matching in a graph is a set of edges with no shared endpoints. A matching from X to Y here covers every vertex of X. Necessity of the Hall marriage theorem condition is immediate: the partners of vertices of A⊆X are distinct members of its neighbour set Γ(A).
For sufficiency, take a matching of maximum size. If it leaves x∈X unmatched, follow alternating unmatched and matched edges from x. Let A⊆X and B⊆Y be the reachable vertices. No vertex of B can be unmatched, because the resulting augmenting path in a matching would increase the matching. Every neighbour of A is in B, and the matching pairs B bijectively with A∖{x}. Thus ∣Γ(A)∣=∣B∣=∣A∣−1, contradicting Hall's condition. The maximum matching therefore covers X.
For the deficiency bound, adjoin d new vertices to Y, each adjacent to all of X. Every nonempty A then has ∣Γ(A)∣+d≥∣A∣ neighbours. Apply the Hall marriage theorem and remove the at most d edges using new vertices. This leaves at least ∣X∣−d independent edges. The lower bound is vacuous if d>∣X∣.
Write the adjacency matrix of a graph in bipartite block form (0CT​C0​). If Hall's condition failed for A⊆X, the linear map from vectors supported on A to their neighbour coordinates in Y would have fewer output than input coordinates. It therefore has a nonzero kernel, giving a nonzero vector v supported on A with CTv=0. The vector (v,0) would be a zero eigenvector of the adjacency matrix. Consequently absence of the zero eigenvalue implies a matching covering X.
The converse is false, even with equal part sizes. The four-cycle K2,2​ has a perfect matching, but its matrix C=(11​11​) is singular. Its adjacency matrix has eigenvalues 2,−2,0,0.
