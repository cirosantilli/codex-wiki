For a character ψ of a subgroup H≤G, the induced character is
(IndHG​ψ)(g)=∣H∣1​∑x∈Gx−1gx∈H​​ψ(x−1gx).
(92)
It is the character of the induced representation C[G]⊗C[H]​Vψ​, and has degree [G:H]ψ(1). The character inner product is ⟨α,β⟩G​=∣G∣−1∑g​α(g)β(g)​. Substituting the displayed formula and putting g=xhx−1 yields
⟨IndHG​ψ,χ⟩G​=∣G∣∣H∣1​∑x∈G​∑h∈H​ψ(h)χ(xhx−1)​=∣H∣1​∑h∈H​ψ(h)χ(h)​.
(93)
Thus Frobenius reciprocity is
⟨IndHG​ψ,χ⟩G​=⟨ψ,ResHG​χ⟩H​.​
(94)
Now assume index two, so H is normal. Choose g∈/H and write ψg(h)=ψ(g−1hg). Directly from the induction formula,
ResHG​IndHG​ψ=ψ+ψg,⟨Indψ,Indψ⟩G​=1+⟨ψ,ψg⟩H​.
(95)
If ψg=ψ, this norm is one, so induction is irreducible of degree 2d: ψ is monogamous. If ψg=ψ, the norm is two. Since a norm is the sum of squared nonnegative integer multiplicities, induction is the sum of two distinct irreducibles, each once. Each is related to ψ, so its degree is at least d by restriction and reciprocity; their degrees sum to 2d, forcing both to have degree d. Thus ψ is bigamous.
Every irreducible χ of G restricts to a nonzero sum of irreducibles. Choose a constituent ψ. If ψ is bigamous, χ is one of its two degree-d partners; since its restriction already contains a degree-d constituent, Resχ=ψ. If ψ is monogamous, χ=Indψ and Resχ=ψ+ψg, giving precisely two distinct monogamous partners of equal degree.
The complex irreducible character degrees of A5​ are 1,3,3,4,5, with squared sum 60. Conjugation by the outside coset preserves degrees, so it fixes the unique degree-1, degree-4 and degree-5 characters and either fixes or swaps the two degree-3 characters. The two possibilities for G are
1,1,3,3,3,3,4,4,5,5or1,1,4,4,5,5,6.​
(96)
Both lists have squared sum 120=∣G∣. They occur for A5​×C2​ and S5​ respectively; in S5​, odd conjugation swaps the two classes of 5-cycles and hence the two degree-3 characters.
