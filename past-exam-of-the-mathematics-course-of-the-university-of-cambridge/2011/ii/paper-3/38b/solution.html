A plane wave in the rest frame of the medium has phase k⋅x−Ω0​(k)t. Under the Galilean transformation x′=x−Ut, this becomes k⋅x′−[Ω0​(k)−U⋅k]t. Therefore
ω=Ω0​(k)−U⋅k,cg​=∇k​Ω0​−U.​
(203)
Here cg​ is the group velocity in the moving frame.
For acoustic waves, Ω0​=c0​∣k∣ and take U=Mc0​ex​. A steady wave has ω=0, requiring cosβ=1/M, where β is the angle of k to the flight direction. Its ray velocity is c0​k−Mc0​ex​, with backward component −c0​(M2−1)/M and transverse magnitude c0​M2−1​/M. Thus the angle θ of the ray to the backward flight direction obeys
tanθ=M2−1​1​,θ=sin−1(1/M).​
(204)
The axial symmetry gives the Mach cone behind the aircraft.
For unsteady emitted disturbances, all ray velocities lie on the sphere cg​=−U+c0​k. Since M>1, the origin lies outside this velocity sphere and its tangent cone has exactly the same semi-angle. All other rays are strictly inside the cone. Equivalently a signal emitted τ time units ago has a wavefront sphere centered at −Uτ with radius c0​τ; these spheres are enclosed by their Mach-cone envelope. Consequently the unsteady wavefield is confined to the cone's interior, with the steady envelope on its boundary.
For capillary waves put a=T/ρ​, so Ω0​=ak3/2. The positive-frequency steady condition is ak​=Ucosβ, requiring cosβ>0. Then
∇k​Ω0​=23​ak​(cosβ,sinβ),
(205)
and
cg​=U(23​cos2β−1, 23​cosβsinβ).​
(206)
Its endpoint traces the circle
(Ucgx​​+41​)2+(Ucgy​​)2=(43​)2,
(207)
since cgx​/U=−1/4+(3/4)cos2β and cgy​/U=(3/4)sin2β. As β runs through (−π/2,π/2) this traces the circle except its leftmost limiting point, corresponding to the zero-wavenumber limit. The circle surrounds the origin, so every direction except the precisely backward axis is attained by a nonzero-wavenumber steady ray; the backward direction is approached as k↓0. Thus the closure of the steady ray field extends all around the insect, with no excluded angular sector such as the acoustic Mach-cone exterior.
