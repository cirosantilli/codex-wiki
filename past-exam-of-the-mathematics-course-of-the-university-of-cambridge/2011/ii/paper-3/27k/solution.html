Given the complete stopped sample (x1​,…,xn​), the likelihood function is
L(θ;x)=θne−θSα(xn​)∏i=1n−1​[1−α(xi​)],S=∑i=1n​xi​.

The stopping factor is positive and independent of θ. The Fisher-Neyman factorization theorem makes (N,S) sufficient. For two possible stopped samples of lengths n,m and sums S,S′, their likelihood ratio is a parameter-free factor times θn−me−θ(S−S′). This is independent of θ exactly when n=m and S=S′: differentiating its logarithm gives (n−m)/θ−(S−S′)=0 for all θ>0. By the likelihood-ratio criterion,
(N,i=1∑N​Xi​) is a minimal sufficient statistic.​
(135)
The likelihood principle says proportional likelihoods give the same evidence about the parameter. Thus with the full sample observed, this parameter-free stopping rule adds no likelihood evidence beyond the realized values and length; it gives the same likelihood-based inference as a fixed-length sample with those values. This statement does not assert that frequentist sampling distributions are unchanged.
