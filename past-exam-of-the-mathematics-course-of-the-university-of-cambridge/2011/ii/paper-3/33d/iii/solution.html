A putative simultaneous assignment of classical values sx(i)​,sy(i)​∈{±a} reproducing the first three certain quantum outcomes must obey, almost surely,
sx(1)​sy(2)​sy(3)​=sy(1)​sx(2)​sy(3)​=sy(1)​sy(2)​sx(3)​=a3.
(178)
Multiply the three equalities. Each y value occurs twice and its square is a2, leaving a6sx(1)​sx(2)​sx(3)​=a9. Thus
sx(1)​sx(2)​sx(3)​=+a3,​
(179)
contrary to the quantum prediction −a3.
This is the GHZ theorem against predetermined local measurement values. Experimentally prepare many copies of the three-spin state and choose independently among the four measurement settings on separated subsystems. The three mixed correlations force the classical prediction for the fourth, whereas quantum mechanics predicts the opposite sign. Since sx​,sy​ on the same subsystem do not commute, the four settings are measured on separate copies; the classical contradiction relies precisely on the common assignment of values across settings.
