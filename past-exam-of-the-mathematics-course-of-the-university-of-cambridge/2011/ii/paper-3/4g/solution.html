For a binary linear code C≤F2n​, its rank is its dimension of a vector space r. Use the weight enumerator convention
WC​(s,t)=∑c∈C​sw(c)tn−w(c),
(13)
where w(c) is the Hamming weight. This convention is essential here: WC​(1,0) counts words of weight n, rather than zero words. There are 2r codewords, so WC​(1,1)=2r​.
There is only one binary word of weight n, namely 1=(1,…,1). Thus WC​(1,0)=1 precisely when 1∈C. If this holds, the linear map c↦c+1 is a bijection of C and replaces weight w by n−w; hence WC​(s,t)=WC​(t,s). Conversely this symmetry gives WC​(1,0)=WC​(0,1)=1, because the zero word is the unique word of weight zero.
The repetition code consists of 0,1, giving
Wrep​(s,t)=tn+sn.​
(14)
The single parity-check code consists of all words of even weight. Exactly (jn​) words have weight j, so the binomial theorem gives
Wparity​(s,t)=j even∑​(jn​)sjtn−j=2(t+s)n+(t−s)n​.​
(15)
