Differentiating the two equations for a fundamental matrix of solutions gives
ψρτ​=(Uτ​+UV)ψ,ψτρ​=(Vρ​+VU)ψ.
(165)
Equality for all independent initial vectors is equivalent to the zero-curvature condition
Uτ​−Vρ​+[U,V]=0.​
(166)
A single possibly zero solution vector alone would not imply a matrix identity; compatibility is understood for the complete linear system.
Write q=ϕρ​, s=sinϕ, c=cosϕ and use Pauli matrices:
U=iλσ3​+2iq​σ1​,V=4iλ1​(cσ3​+sσ2​),λ=0.
(167)
The matrix products give
[U,V]=−2is​σ1​−4λiqc​σ2​+4λiqs​σ3​,
(168)
whereas Vρ​=−(iqc/(4λ))σ2​+(iqs/(4λ))σ3​. Thus these last two components cancel, and the remaining curvature is (i/2)(ϕρτ​−sinϕ)σ1​. Therefore
ϕρτ​=sinϕ,​
(169)
the Sine-Gordon equation in light-cone coordinates.
