Let the torsion submodule of the module over a ring M be
T(M)={m∈M:rm=0 for some 0=r∈R}.
(6)
This is a submodule. Indeed, if rm=0 and sn=0 with r,s=0, then rs=0 because the scalar ring is an integral domain, and rs(m+n)=0; scalar multiples and additive inverses are handled similarly.
Take the quotient module M0​=M/T(M) and let q:M→M0​ be the quotient R-module homomorphism. It is torsion-free: if 0=r∈R and r(m+T(M))=0, then rm∈T(M), so srm=0 for some s=0. Since sr=0, this puts m in T(M) and therefore m+T(M)=0.
Now let N be torsion-free and let f:M→N be an R-module homomorphism. If m∈T(M) and rm=0 for r=0, then rf(m)=f(rm)=0, so torsion-freeness gives f(m)=0. Hence T(M)⊆kerf, and the universal property of a quotient module gives a unique homomorphism
f0​:M/T(M)⟶N,f0​(m+T(M))=f(m),
(7)
with f=f0​∘q​. Thus M/T(M) is the maximal torsion-free quotient of M.
