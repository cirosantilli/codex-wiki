For an independent second sample, let
SXX​=∑j=1n​(Xj​−X)2.
(117)
Under H0​:σ2=σ2, independence and the two chi-squared laws give the F-distribution
F=SXX​/(n−1)SXX​/(n−1)​∼Fn−1,n−1​​.
(118)
For the one-sided alternative σ2>σ2, reject for large F, using the upper α critical value.
No values of the unknown means enter this statistic: centering by the sample means removes them. If both means are known, one may instead use sums about the known means; the corresponding degrees of freedom are n and n, rather than n−1 and n−1. Thus knowledge of the means changes the degrees of freedom and critical value, while the variance-ratio principle is unchanged.
