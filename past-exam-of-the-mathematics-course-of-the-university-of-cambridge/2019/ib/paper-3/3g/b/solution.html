First, compactness makes X totally bounded: for every positive integer k, the open cover by balls of radius 1/k has a finite subcover. Given a sequence, repeatedly choose a ball of radius 1/k containing infinitely many terms of the subsequence retained at the preceding stage. Taking a diagonal subsequence (xnk​​), any two terms with indices at least k lie in one ball of radius 1/k, so their distance is below 2/k. The diagonal subsequence is therefore a Cauchy sequence.
A compact metric space is complete. To see this without assuming sequential compactness, let (yn​) be Cauchy and put Fn​={ym​:m≥n}​. These nonempty closed sets are nested and have the finite intersection property; compactness gives a point y∈⋂n​Fn​. Given ε>0, a sufficiently late tail has diameter below ε/2, and because y lies in its closure, every point of that tail lies within ε of y. Thus yn​→y.
The Cauchy subsequence constructed above consequently converges in X. Hence every compact metric space is sequentially compact.
