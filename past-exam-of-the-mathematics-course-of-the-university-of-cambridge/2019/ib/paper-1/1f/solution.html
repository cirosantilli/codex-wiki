A basis of a vector space V is a family of vectors that is both linearly independent and a spanning set of V.
Write the given finite basis as B={v1​,…,vn​}. First, any other basis B′ must also be finite. Each vi​ is a finite linear combination of elements of B′, so the union S of the finitely many elements of B′ occurring in these n expressions is finite. Since S spans every vi​, it spans V. If some w∈B′∖S existed, then w would lie in the span of S, contradicting the linear independence of B′. Hence B′=S; write ∣B′∣=m.
It remains to prove the elementary Steinitz exchange lemma directly. If independent vectors w1​,…,wr​ lie in the span of v1​,…,vn​, express w1​ in terms of the vi​. Some coefficient is nonzero, so the corresponding vi​ can be solved for in terms of w1​ and the other vi​; replacing it by w1​ preserves the span. Inductively, after replacing k of the vi​ by w1​,…,wk​, the expression for wk+1​ must have a nonzero coefficient on one of the unreplaced vi​, since otherwise wk+1​ would be a linear combination of w1​,…,wk​. That vi​ can again be replaced. There are only n original vectors to replace, so r≤n.
Apply this argument first to the independent family B′ and the spanning family B to obtain m≤n, and then with the two bases interchanged to obtain n≤m. Therefore
∣B′∣=∣B∣​.
