Let the common initial state be x and the robber's final state be y. Part (a) gives their joint distribution as
π(x)P∗(x,y)=π(y)P(y,x).
(125)
Let τy​=min{n≥0:Xn​=y} be the cop's hitting time of y when starting from x. For a chain started from y, condition on its first step to obtain
Ey​Ty+​=1+∑x​P(y,x)Ex​τy​.
(126)
Hence the expected catch time, averaged over the displayed joint law, is
∑x,y​π(y)P(y,x)Ex​τy​=∑y​π(y)(Ey​Ty+​−1).
(127)
Using Kac's lemma once more gives
E[catch time]=y∑​π(y)(π(y)1​−1)=N−1​.
(128)
The convention τy​=0 correctly counts an immediate catch when the robber's reverse step leaves it at the common starting state.
