Jordan lemma states that if a>0 and g is analytic in the upper half-plane outside a fixed circle, with
MR​=max0≤θ≤π​∣g(Reiθ)∣⟶0,

∫CR​​eiazg(z)dz⟶0.

Indeed, ∣eiaReiθ∣=e−aRsinθ. Using symmetry and sinθ≥2θ/π on 0≤θ≤π/2 gives
​∫CR​​eiazg(z)dz​≤2RMR​∫0π/2​e−2aRθ/πdθ≤aπMR​​⟶0.
(70)
The residue of f at an isolated singularity a is the coefficient of (z−a)−1 in its Laurent series, equivalently
Res(f,a)=2πi1​∮f(z)dz.
(71)
If f(z)=g(z)/(z−a)k, the Taylor expansion of the analytic function g shows that
Res(f,a)=(k−1)!g(k−1)(a)​​.
(72)
For the real integral, apply contour integration in the upper half-plane to

Jordan's lemma removes the semicircular arc. The only enclosed singularity is the double pole at z=i, whose residue is
Res(F,i)=dzd​((z+i)2z3eiz​)​z=i​=4e1​.
(74)
