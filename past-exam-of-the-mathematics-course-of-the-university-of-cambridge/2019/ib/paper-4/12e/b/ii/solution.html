Let (xn​) be any sequence in a complete, totally bounded metric space X. Cover X by finitely many balls of radius 2−1; one contains infinitely many terms. From those terms choose an infinite subsequence lying in one ball of radius 2−2, and continue. The diagonal argument produces a subsequence (xnj​​) whose tail from its jth term onward lies in a ball of radius 2−j. Hence
d(xnk​​,xnℓ​​)<21−j
(55)
whenever k,ℓ≥j, so the subsequence is Cauchy. Completeness makes it converge in X.
Every sequence in X therefore has a convergent subsequence, so X is sequentially compact. Sequential compactness is equivalent to compactness for metric spaces, and thus X is compact. This proves that a complete totally bounded metric space is compact.
