The space is not compact because it is not complete. For n≥2, define the continuous function
fn​(t)=⎩⎨⎧​0,2n​(t−21​+n1​),1,​0≤t≤21​−n1​,21​−n1​<t<21​+n1​,21​+n1​≤t≤1.​
(56)
These functions converge in the L1 norm to the step function h=1(1/2,1]​, because fn​−h is supported on an interval of length 2/n and bounded in absolute value by one. They are consequently Cauchy for the metric d, since for sufficiently close pairs the outer minimum with 1 does nothing.
If (fn​) converged in this metric to some f∈C[0,1], it would also converge to f in L1. Uniqueness of an L1 limit would give f=h almost everywhere. Continuity would then force f=0 on [0,1/2) and f=1 on (1/2,1], which is impossible at 1/2. Thus this Cauchy sequence has no limit in C[0,1], while every compact metric space is complete.
