For p=0 and λ=1, the linear ordinary differential equation is y′′′′=y, whose general solution is
y=Acosx+Bsinx+Ccoshx+Dsinhx.
(110)
By the stated parity reduction, an even eigenfunction has the form y=Acosx+Ccoshx. The conditions y(c)=y′(c)=0 have a nonzero solution exactly when
det(cosc−sinc​coshcsinhc​)=0,
(111)
that is,
coscsinhc+sinccoshc=0.
(112)
An odd eigenfunction has the form y=Bsinx+Dsinhx, and the corresponding determinant gives
coscsinhc−sinccoshc=0.
(113)
Thus λ=1 is an eigenvalue precisely under one of the two stated conditions.
For the minus sign, division by cosccoshc away from c=π/2 gives tanc=tanhc. On (0,π/2), tanc>tanhc for c>0, while on (π/2,π) their signs differ, so there is no root. For the plus sign the equation is
tanc=−tanhc.
(114)
There is no root in (0,π/2). On (π/2,π), the function tanc+tanhc increases strictly from −∞ to tanhπ>0, so it has exactly one root by the intermediate value theorem. Numerically,
c≈2.365020372​.
(115)
Hence the combined ± condition has exactly one solution in 0<c<π.
