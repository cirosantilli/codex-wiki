Represent a Möbius transformation by a matrix, with nonzero scalar multiples representing the same map. Matrix multiplication reproduces composition, so θ is a group homomorphism. Every Möbius map has a representing matrix A∈GL2​(C); multiplying A by either square root of (detA)−1 gives a matrix in SL2​(C) representing the same map. Thus θ is surjective. A matrix in its kernel represents the identity and is therefore scalar; determinant one leaves
kerθ={I,−I}​.

If a nonidentity Möbius map T has two distinct fixed points, conjugate those points to 0 and ∞. The conjugated map fixes both and hence has the form S(z)=μz with μ=0,1. If T has one fixed point, conjugate it to ∞. The resulting affine map has the form az+b; having no finite fixed point forces a=1 and b=0, and conjugating by a scaling makes it S(z)=z+1.
Directly, the finite fixed points satisfy
cz2+(d−a)z−b=0.
(30)
Including ∞ when appropriate, a nonidentity Möbius map consequently has one or two distinct fixed points.
