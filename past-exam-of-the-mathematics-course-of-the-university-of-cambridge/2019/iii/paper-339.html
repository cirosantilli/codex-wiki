Suppose y in the capped simplex has a coordinate strictly between zero and one. Since ∑i​yi​=k is an integer, it cannot have exactly one such coordinate: the other coordinates would contribute an integer sum. Hence there are distinct i,j with 0<yi​,yj​<1.
Choose 0<ε<min{yi​,1−yi​,yj​,1−yj​} and set y±=y±ε(ei​−ej​), where ei​,ej​ are standard basis vectors. Both perturbed vectors satisfy the coordinate bounds and have the same coordinate sum. They are distinct and y=(y++y−)/2, so y is not an extreme point.
Conversely, every zero-one vector in this convex polytope is an extreme point. If it were a nontrivial convex combination of two feasible vectors, each coordinate equal to zero would force both corresponding coordinates to be zero, and each coordinate equal to one would force both to be one. Thus the two vectors would equal the original one. The extreme points are therefore exactly the indicators of k-element subsets.

The required linear program is
fk​(x)=y∈Rnmax​{xTy:yi​≥0, yi​≤1 (1≤i≤n), i∑​yi​=k}.​
(1)
There are 2n inequality constraints and one equality constraint, all independent of x.
The feasible set is the capped simplex, a nonempty compact set. A linear function attains its maximum at an extreme point; the previous part identifies these as the indicators of k-element subsets. At such a vector, the objective is the sum of the selected coordinates. Maximizing selects the k largest coordinates, including when some are negative or tied. Equivalently, fk​ is the support function called the sum of the largest components.

Keep y≥0 as the domain restriction, introduce nonnegative Lagrange multipliers si​ for yi​≤1, and introduce a free multiplier t∈R for ∑i​yi​=k. The maximizing Lagrangian is
L(y,s,t)=xTy+sT(1−y)+t(k−1Ty)=1Ts+kt+(x−s−t1)Ty.
(2)
Its supremum over y≥0 is finite exactly when s+t1≥x, and then equals kt+∑i​si​. By linear programming duality, since the primal is feasible and bounded,
fk​(x)=t∈R, s∈Rnmin​{kt+i∑​si​:si​≥0, si​+t≥xi​}.​
(3)
Minimizing over s for fixed t gives si​=(xi​−t)+​, the positive part of a real-valued function. Thus the threshold formula for the sum of the largest components is
fk​(x)=t∈Rmin​[kt+i∑​(xi​−t)+​].​
(4)
For a direct attainment check, order the coordinates x[1]​≥⋯≥x[n]​. Any t∈[x[k+1]​,x[k]​] is optimal for k<n, while any t≤x[n]​ is optimal for k=n.

Replace coordinate inequalities by the Loewner order and coordinate sums by matrix trace. The maximizing semidefinite program is
Fk​(X)=Y∈Snmax​{tr(XY):Y⪰0, I−Y⪰0, trY=k}.​
(6)
Its feasible set is the fantope. In an orthonormal eigenbasis of X, its objective is ∑i​λi​(X)Yii​, and the diagonal entries of Y belong to the capped simplex. Choosing Y as the orthogonal projection matrix onto the largest k eigenvectors attains the sum of the largest eigenvalues, as in the Ky Fan maximum principle.
The minimizing semidefinite program is
Fk​(X)=t∈R, S∈Snmin​{kt+trS:S⪰0, S+tI−X⪰0}.​
(7)
For example, its Lagrangian arises by assigning S⪰0 to I−Y⪰0, keeping Y⪰0 in the domain, and using free t for trY=k. Finiteness of the supremum over Y requires S+tI−X⪰0.
Equality follows directly by choosing S to have eigenvalues (λi​(X)−t)+​ in the same orthonormal eigenbasis, with t between the kth and (k+1)st eigenvalues, or below the smallest one for k=n. This is the threshold semidefinite program for the largest eigenvalues, and it also verifies the endpoint case k=n.

Let J=11T be the all-ones matrix. Every feasible block matrix in the first formulation has t≥1, because its principal 2×2 submatrix on indices 0,i is (t1​11​) and is a positive semidefinite matrix. In particular, t>0.
By the Schur complement,
(t1​1TZ​)⪰0⟺Z−t1​J⪰0.
(8)
Set U=tZ−J. Then U⪰0, Uii​=t−1, and Uij​=−1 on every edge. Conversely, for a feasible (t,U) in the second formulation, its nonnegative diagonal gives t≥1, and Z=(U+J)/t has Zii​=1, Zij​=0 on edges, and Z−J/t=U/t⪰0.
Both transformations preserve the objective t. Hence the two semidefinite programs defining the complement theta number have the same feasible objective values and the same minimum.

For an edge ij, put ρ=⟨vi​,vj​⟩ and θ=arccosρ. Since ρ≤−1/2, we have θ≥2π/3.
The random normal a has the isotropic Gaussian distribution N(0,Ip​). Its distribution is invariant under orthogonal transformations; if vi​,vj​ are linearly independent, its projection onto their plane has a uniformly distributed direction. The signs of its inner products with vi​,vj​ differ in two sectors of total angle 2θ, out of 2π. Thus random hyperplane rounding gives
P(H cuts ij)=πθ​≥32​.​
(10)
If vj​=−vi​, the signs differ with probability one and the same formula holds with θ=π. Zero inner products have probability zero, since each vi​ is a unit vector.

A fixed edge is monochromatic exactly when none of the r hyperplanes separates its endpoints. Each hyperplane fails to cut it with probability at most 1/3, and the hyperplanes are independent. Therefore
P{c(i)=c(j)}≤3−r.
(11)
Let B=∑ij∈E​1{c(i)=c(j)}​ count monochromatic edges. Applying linearity of expectation to these indicator random variables gives
EB≤m3−r.​
(12)
Independence between different edges is unnecessary; only the independently sampled hyperplanes are used. The r signs provide at most 2r colors, whether or not every sign region is nonempty.

If m=0, use a single color. Otherwise choose
r=max{0,⌈log3​n4m​⌉}.
(13)
The previous bound gives EB≤n/4, including the case r=0. By Markov inequality, P(B>n/2)≤1/2. Sample the hyperplanes, count the monochromatic edges, and repeat if B>n/2. The success probability is at least 1/2, so at most two draws are needed on average.
For a successful draw, select one endpoint of each monochromatic edge and let D be the union of the selected vertices. Then ∣D∣≤B≤n/2. Every monochromatic edge meets D, so the original colors give a proper graph colouring on the induced subgraph with vertex set W=V∖D. Since ∣W∣≥n/2, the coloring of all vertices is a semicoloring. This is a random alteration method; no additional colors for D are required.
The number of available colors satisfies 2r≤2max{1,(4m/n)log3​2}. A simple graph has m≤n(n−1)/2, hence semicoloring by independent hyperplanes gives
k=O(nγ),γ=log3​2=log3log2​≈0.63093<1.​

The Horn copositive matrix is invariant under cyclic permutation of its five coordinates: its negative entries correspond precisely to neighboring indices on the five-cycle. For any x≥0, cyclically relabel the coordinates so that x5​ is a smallest coordinate. In particular, x4​−x5​≥0.
Using the stated identity in this coordinate order,
xTHx=(x1​−x2​+x3​−x4​+x5​)2+4x2​x5​+4x1​(x4​−x5​)≥0.
(20)
The square is nonnegative, and both remaining terms are nonnegative since xi​≥0 and x4​≥x5​. Cyclic invariance means the relabeling has not changed the quadratic form. Thus H is a copositive matrix for every original ordering of x.

Let w=(1,2,1,0,0)T. Direct multiplication gives Hw=(0,0,0,2,2)T, so wTHw=0.
Suppose H=P+N, where P is a positive semidefinite matrix and N is a symmetric nonnegative matrix. Since w≥0, both wTPw and wTNw are nonnegative. Their sum is zero, so both vanish. In particular,
0=wTNw=∑i,j=13​wi​wj​Nij​.
(21)
Every coefficient wi​wj​ in this sum is strictly positive and every Nij​ is nonnegative, forcing Nij​=0 for 1≤i,j≤3.
Now apply the same argument to all five cyclic shifts of w. The Horn copositive matrix is cyclically invariant, so every shifted vector also has zero quadratic form. It follows that the entries of N vanish on every cyclic block of three consecutive indices. Every pair of indices on a five-cycle lies in such a block, hence N=0.
This would imply P=H. But the zero quadratic form of a positive semidefinite matrix would then force Hw=0, contradicting Hw=(0,0,0,2,2)T. Therefore H lies outside the positive-semidefinite-plus-nonnegative cone. By the previous equivalence, its quartic form is a nonnegative polynomial that is not a sum of squares polynomial.
