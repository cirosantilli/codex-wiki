For admissible data, the Moore–Penrose inverse of an operator selects the minimum-norm least-squares solution:
x†∈(kerA)⊥,∥Ax†−y∥=x∈Xinf​∥Ax−y∥,A†y=x†.​
(19)
Its domain is ranA⊕(ranA)⊥. When the equation is consistent it is the solution of Ax=y orthogonal to the kernel; for inconsistent admissible data it solves the normal equation for a linear inverse problem and projects away the component orthogonal to the range.
Take a singular system of a compact operator satisfying Avj​=σj​uj​, A∗uj​=σj​vj​. The generalized inverse is
x†=j∑​σj​⟨y,uj​⟩​vj​.​
(20)
The Picard criterion requires the squared coefficients to be summable. For an infinite-rank compact operator, σj​→0, so ∥A†uj​∥=1/σj​→∞ even though ∥uj​∥=1. Hence A† is unbounded. Finite-rank compact operators do not have this obstruction. Data failing the Picard condition need not have a least-squares minimizer at all, even when the infimum residual is zero.
