Give X×Y the uniform probability measure and let f be the indicator function of the edge set. For partitions P={Xi​} and Q={Yj​}, define the energy
E(P,Q)=∥E(f∣P⊗Q)∥22​=∑i,j​∣X∣∣Y∣∣Xi​∣∣Yj​∣​d(Xi​,Yj​)2.

Because conditional expectation is an orthogonal projection in L2 space, 0≤E≤∥f∥22​≤1.
Suppose the pair of partitions is not ε-regular. For each irregular (Xi​,Yj​) choose witnesses Aij​⊆Xi​ and Bij​⊆Yj​ with
∣Aij​∣≥ε∣Xi​∣,qquad∣Bij​∣≥ε∣Yj​∣,qquad∣d(Aij​,Bij​)−d(Xi​,Yj​)∣>ε.
(28)
Refine each Xi​ by all sets Aij​ and each Yj​ by all sets Bij​. If the old partitions have r,s cells, the refined ones have at most r2s,s2r cells.
The Pythagorean theorem for the two nested conditional-expectation projections gives
E(P′,Q′)−E(P,Q)=∥E(f∣P′⊗Q′)−E(f∣P⊗Q)∥22​.
(29)
Inside an irregular Xi​×Yj​, the witness rectangle occupies at least an ε2 proportion and the mean of the displayed difference over it has magnitude greater than ε. The Cauchy-Schwarz inequality therefore gives an energy gain greater than
ε4∣X∣∣Y∣∣Xi​∣∣Yj​∣​
(30)
from that pair. Since irregular pairs have total weight greater than ε, the complete refinement raises the energy by more than ε5.
Energy is at most one, so after at most ⌈ε−5⌉ refinements the process stops at an ε-regular pair of partitions. Iterating the cell-count bounds r↦r2s and s↦s2r from r=s=1 a bounded number of times produces a finite K(ε) independent of G. This proves the Bipartite Szemerédi regularity lemma.
