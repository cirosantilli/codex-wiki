Assume first that A⊆F3n​ is a cap set. Over the finite field F3​, define
T(x,y,z)=∏i=1n​(1−(xi​+yi​+zi​)2).
(31)
Since 1−t2 is one at t=0 and zero at t=±1, T is the indicator function of x+y+z=0. If x,y,z∈A satisfy this equation, then either they are all equal or they are three distinct points; the latter is excluded. Thus T∣A3​ is a diagonal tensor with every diagonal entry equal to one, and the slice rank of a diagonal tensor gives
slice rank(T∣A3​)=∣A∣.
(32)
Expand T as a polynomial. Every variable has exponent at most two, and every monomial has total degree at most 2n. Splitting a monomial's degree among its x-, y-, and z-blocks, at least one block has degree at most 2n/3. Assign each monomial to one such block and group together terms with the same low-degree block monomial. Each group is one slice, so
∣A∣≤3mn​,
(33)
where
mn​=​{α∈{0,1,2}n:∑i​αi​≤32n​}​.
(34)
If Xi​=1−αi​, then the Xi​ are independent random variables uniformly distributed on {−1,0,1} and
3nmn​​=P(∑i​Xi​≥3n​)≤e−n/12
(35)
by the given tail probability bound. Consequently every cap set satisfies
∣A∣≤3n+1e−n/12.
(36)
Put C0​=3e−1/24<3. For n≥27, the preceding bound is at most C0n​. For each of the finitely many 1≤n<27, a cap set is a proper subset of F3n​, so its size is at most 3n−1. We may therefore choose
max{C0​,max1≤n<27​(3n−1)1/n}<C<3.
(37)
Every cap set then has size strictly below Cn. Equivalently,
∣A∣≥Cn⟹A contains distinct x,y,z with x+y+z=0.​
(38)
