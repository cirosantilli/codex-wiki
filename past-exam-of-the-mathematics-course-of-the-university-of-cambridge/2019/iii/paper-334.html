a OurBigBook.com
Use the Cartesian streamfunction convention u=ψy​, v=−ψx​. Taking the curl of the incompressible Stokes equation eliminates pressure and gives the biharmonic stream function for planar Stokes flow equation
∇4ψ=0for y>ϵsinθ,θ=x−t.​
(1)
The material points have no horizontal velocity in the swimming frame and have vertical velocity −ϵcosθ. The no-slip boundary condition is therefore
ψy​(x,ϵsinθ,t)=0,ψx​(x,ϵsinθ,t)=ϵcosθ.​
(2)
At infinity the laboratory fluid is at rest, so in this translating frame it moves with Uex​:
ψy​→U,ψx​→0(y→∞).​
(3)
Velocity and its perturbations are periodic in x, with period 2π, and the pressure has no imposed mean gradient. The Taylor swimming sheet is force-free; the unbounded problem's bounded far-field velocity enforces the absence of mean shear. An additive constant in the streamfunction has no physical effect.

Expand ψ=ϵψ1​+ϵ2ψ2​+⋯ and U=ϵU1​+ϵ2U2​+⋯. A Taylor expansion of the sheet boundary conditions about y=0 gives
∇4ψ1​=0,ψ1y​(0)=0,ψ1x​(0)=cosθ.
(4)
The first-order Fourier mode is ψ1​=f(y)sinθ, with (∂y2​−1)2f=0, f(0)=1, and f′(0)=0. Decay excludes the growing exponentials, leaving f=(a+by)e−y. The two boundary values give a=b=1:
ψ1​=(1+y)e−ysinθ,u1​=−ye−ysinθ,v1​=−(1+y)e−ycosθ.​
(5)
There is no first-order mean boundary motion. Boundedness excludes mean shear, so U1​=0​.

The wall is stationary in the laboratory and moves with velocity +Uex​ in the sheet frame. Its no-slip boundary condition is therefore
ψy​(x,d,t)=U,ψx​(x,d,t)=0.​
(9)
The first equation prescribes wall tangential velocity; the second imposes zero normal velocity. The streamfunction may have a constant mean value on the wall, set by the fluid transport, and that constant need not equal its value at the sheet.

The wall removes the far-field decay condition and instead imposes ψ1x​(d)=0, ψ1y​(d)=U1​. The force-free condition eliminates any first-order mean shear; since mean sheet tangential velocity is zero, U1​=0. Thus ψ1​=fd​(y)sinθ, where
(∂y2​−1)2fd​=0,fd​(0)=1,fd′​(0)=0,fd​(d)=fd′​(d)=0.
(10)
Start with the hyperbolic function form fd​=(a+by)coshy+(c+ey)sinhy. The lower conditions give a=1, c=−b. Defining Δ=sinh2d−d2, the wall conditions give
b=Δd+sinhdcoshd​,e=−Δsinh2d​.
(11)
Consequently the first-order flow for Taylor-sheet swimming next to a rigid wall is
ψ1​=[coshy+Δd+sinhdcoshd​(ycoshy−sinhy)−Δsinh2d​ysinhy]sinθ.​
(12)
For d>0, sinhd>d ensures a nonzero denominator; as d→∞, this reduces to the unbounded first-order field on every fixed height interval.

At the sheet, the second-order conditions are ψ2x​(0)=0 and ψ2y​(0)=−fd′′​(0)sin2θ. At the wall, ψ2x​(d)=0 and ψ2y​(d)=U2​. The general solution has a polynomial mean mode and a second harmonic with exponentials e±2y, as in the unbounded calculation.
Let u2​(y)=⟨ψ2y​⟩. The averaged Stokes equation, with periodic pressure and no imposed mean pressure gradient, gives u2′′​=0. The total horizontal hydrodynamic force on the sheet is opposite to the mean shear force on the flat wall. Since the swimmer is force-free, u2′​(d)=0, making the mean velocity constant. Matching its values at the sheet and wall therefore gives
U2​=−21​fd′′​(0).
(13)
The previous part gives fd′′​(0)=1−2sinh2d/Δ=−(sinh2d+d2)/Δ, hence
U2​=2(sinh2d−d2)sinh2d+d2​.​
(14)
This determines the speed without solving the second-harmonic flow. The force constraint matters: prescribing a translating sheet without imposing zero net force would allow a mean shear and would not determine a unique swimming speed.

The first term is the local viscous force per unit length from resistive-force theory. With unit tangent vector t=rs​, the drag tensor gives −c∥​v∥​−c⊥​v⊥​, where v=rt​ is velocity relative to the fluid.
The second term is the bending force of an inextensible filament. Varying its energy Eb​=(B/2)∫∣rss​∣2ds gives bulk force density −Brssss​. The final term, ∂s​(Trs​), is the force density from filament tension; T is the Lagrange multiplier enforcing ∣rs​∣=1. At low Reynolds number, these forces balance without a filament acceleration term.
With mass, length, and time dimensions M,L,T, the coefficients have
[c⊥​]=[c∥​]=ML−1T−1,[B]=ML3T−2,[T]=MLT−2.​
(16)
Thus the drag coefficients have the dimensions of dynamic viscosity, the filament bending modulus has dimensions force times length squared, and tension has dimensions force.

b OurBigBook.com
In the small-slope Monge representation, s=x+O(yx2​), t=(1,yx​)+O(yx2​), and the leading transverse velocity is yt​. With no imposed axial prestress, the induced filament tension is second order in transverse amplitude, so its transverse contribution is higher order. The transverse component of force balance reduces to the small-slope elastohydrodynamic filament equation
c⊥​yt​=−Byxxxx​.​
(17)
An externally imposed zeroth-order tension would instead add Tyxx​.
For the transverse motion, the off-diagonal part of the resistive-force theory tensor gives axial propulsive force density (c⊥​−c∥​)yx​yt​ to second order. Its integral is
F=(c⊥​−c∥​)∫0L​yx​yt​dx.​
(18)
Substitute the bending equation and integrate by parts:
∫0L​yx​yxxxx​dx=[yx​yxxx​]0L​−∫0L​yxx​yxxx​dx=[yx​yxxx​−21​yxx2​]0L​.
(19)
The boundary expression for transverse filament thrust is therefore
F=B(1−c⊥​c∥​​)[21​yxx2​−yx​yxxx​]0L​.​
(20)
Only endpoint slope, bending moment, and shear enter this expression. Exact inextensibility also generates second-order longitudinal material motion; if that motion is retained in instantaneous total axial drag, it adds −c∥​∫vx​dx. For a periodic deformation with fixed axial base position, that additional term has zero cycle average. Thus the displayed formula is the transverse propulsive contribution and also gives the cycle-averaged propulsive force.

For the long filament, take the time-periodic solution of the dimensionless bending equation, after transients have decayed. Write y=Re[Y(x)eit]. Then Y(4)=−iY, so the spatial exponents satisfy λ4=−i. Define C=cos(π/8) and S=sin(π/8). The two roots with negative real part are λ1​=−C+iS and λ2​=−S−iC; the other roots violate decay at infinity.
Hence Y=Aeλ1​x+Beλ2​x. The driven displacement gives A+B=y0​, while the zero bending moment gives λ12​A+λ22​B=0. Since λ22​=−λ12​, A=B=y0​/2. The oscillatory bending of a moment-free semi-infinite filament is
y(x,t)=2y0​​[e−Cxcos(t+Sx)+e−Sxcos(t−Cx)].​
(23)
Holding each wave phase constant gives its dimensionless phase velocity:
v1​=−S1​≃−2.6131,v2​=C1​≃1.0824.​
(24)
The first travels toward the actuator and attenuates over length 1/C; the second travels away and attenuates over the longer length 1/S. These are spatially damped phase patterns in an overdamped bending equation, not two undamped inertial beam waves. Their combination satisfies both displacement and zero-moment conditions at the driven end.
