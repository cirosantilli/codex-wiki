An Ideal of a Lie algebra is a vector subspace I⊆g such that [g,I]⊆I. The derived series of a Lie algebra is defined by g(0)=g and g(r+1)=[g(r),g(r)].
Suppose that I is an ideal and x∈g, u,v∈I. The Jacobi identity gives
[x,[u,v]]=[[x,u],v]+[u,[x,v]].
(1)
Both terms on the right lie in [I,I], because [x,u],[x,v]∈I. Thus [I,I] is again an ideal. Starting from the ideal g and applying this observation inductively proves that every term g(r) of the derived series is an ideal of g.

A Simple Lie algebra is a nonabelian Lie algebra whose only Ideals of a Lie algebra are 0 and the whole algebra.
Use the standard basis e,f,h of the sl2 Lie algebra, with
[h,e]=2e,[h,f]=−2f,[e,f]=h.
(2)
Let I=0 be an ideal and choose 0=x=ae+bf+ch∈I. Since I is invariant under the Adjoint representation, it is invariant under the linear operator adh. The three basis vectors are eigenvectors of adh with distinct eigenvalues 2,−2,0. Applying the corresponding polynomial spectral projections to x shows that I contains at least one nonzero multiple of e, f, or h.
If e∈I, then [f,e]=−h∈I and [f,h]=2f∈I; the cases f∈I and h∈I are identical after taking brackets with the other basis vectors. Hence e,f,h∈I, so I=sl2​(C). Therefore sl2​(C) is simple.

The Killing form of a finite-dimensional Lie algebra g is the symmetric bilinear form
κ(x,y)=tr(adxady).
(3)
The cyclicity of the trace makes it invariant:
κ([x,y],z)=κ(x,[y,z]).
(4)
Consequently its radical of a bilinear form R={x:κ(x,g)=0} is an ideal, since x∈R implies κ([y,x],z)=κ(x,[z,y])=0 for all y,z∈g.
If g is simple, then R is either 0 or g. In the second case the Killing form vanishes identically, so the stated solvability criterion makes g a Solvable Lie algebra. A nonabelian simple Lie algebra cannot be solvable: its first derived algebra is a nonzero ideal and hence equals g, after which the derived series never reaches zero. Thus R=0, and the Killing form of a simple Lie algebra is nondegenerate.

Put h0​=ψ(h) and e0​=ψ(e)=∑i=1ℓ​ei​, where 0=ei​∈gαi​​. Since [h0​,e0​]=2e0​ and the root spaces are a direct sum, αi​(h0​)=2 for every simple root. Every root has simple-root coefficients of one sign, so no root vanishes on h0​. The Regular element criterion in a Cartan subalgebra therefore shows that h0​ is regular.
Restrict the Adjoint representation of g along ψ. By Complete reducibility of semisimple Lie algebra representations, it is a direct sum of finite-dimensional sl2 Lie algebra modules. The h0​-eigenvalues on a root space are twice the heights of the roots, so they are all even. Each irreducible summand consequently has even highest weight, contains exactly one zero-weight vector, and has a one-dimensional kernel for the raising operator e0​ by the Classification of finite-dimensional sl2 representations.
Because h0​ is regular, its zero-weight space in g is precisely t and has dimension ℓ. There are therefore exactly ℓ irreducible summands, whence
dimker(ade0​)=ℓ.
(15)
Thus e0​ is regular; equivalently it is a principal nilpotent element in the given Principal sl2 subalgebra.

V⊗V=S2V⊕Λ2V.

Let C(Δ) be the fundamental chamber of a root system determined by Δ. Every positive root is a nonnegative linear combination of the simple roots, so every point in the interior of C(Δ) pairs strictly positively with every positive root. In particular, the interior meets none of the reflecting hyperplanes belonging to the subsystem Φ0​ of roots of maximal length.
The connected set C(Δ) therefore lies in one chamber of the long-root subsystem. Let Δ0​ be the unique fundamental system of a root system defining that chamber. Taking closures gives C(Δ)⊆C(Δ0​). Uniqueness follows because the interiors of two distinct chambers are disjoint. Thus there is a unique such Δ0​.

The short-root Weyl reflection sα​ interchanges ω1​ and ω2​, because α=ω2​−ω1​ and α∨=δ2∨​−δ1∨​. Hence
sα​λ=bω1​+aω2​.
(29)
The Weyl group orbit of the highest weight occurs in U with a one-dimensional extremal weight space. Let v be a nonzero vector of weight sα​λ. The same reflection exchanges the long simple roots: sα​δ1​=δ2​ and sα​δ2​=δ1​. If an A2​ raising operator did not annihilate v, then sα​λ+δi​ would be a weight of U; applying sα​ would make λ+δj​ a weight, contradicting the fact that λ is the highest weight.
Thus v is an A2​ highest-weight vector of weight bω1​+aω2​. The Complete reducibility of semisimple Lie algebra representations then supplies the corresponding irreducible summand, so
V(bω1​+aω2​)⊆U↓sl3​​.​
