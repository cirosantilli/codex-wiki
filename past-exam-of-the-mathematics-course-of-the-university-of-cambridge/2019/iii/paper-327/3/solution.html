The Paley–Wiener–Schwartz theorem in its closed-ball form says that a tempered distribution u is supported in BR​(0) if and only if its Fourier transform is the real restriction of an entire function F on Cn satisfying, for some C>0 and integer N≥0,
∣F(ζ)∣≤C(1+∣ζ∣)NeR∣Imζ∣.​
(20)
The distribution is unique by Fourier inversion. More generally a compact convex support set K replaces R∣Imζ∣ by its support function HK​(Imζ).
For the forward implication, a compactly supported distribution has finite order of a distribution, say m, on a fixed neighborhood of its support. Define
F(ζ)=⟨u,e−ix⋅ζ⟩,
(21)
using a cutoff equal to one near the support. Differentiation with respect to each complex coordinate is allowed in this pairing and gives ∂ζj​​F=⟨u,−ixj​e−ix⋅ζ⟩, so F is entire.
To obtain the exact radius R, rather than an enlarged radius, choose a cutoff function χε​ equal to one near BR​ and supported in BR+ε​, with derivatives through order m bounded by Cγ​ε−∣γ∣. Set ε=(1+∣ζ∣)−1. The finite-order bound and the Leibniz rule give
∣F(ζ)∣≤C(1+∣ζ∣)me(R+ε)∣Imζ∣≤Ce(1+∣ζ∣)meR∣Imζ∣.
(22)
This proves the required growth estimate.
For the converse, F has at most polynomial growth on real frequencies, so define its inverse tempered distribution by
⟨u,φ⟩=(2π)−n∫Rn​F(ξ)φ​(−ξ)dξ.
(23)
Let a compactly supported test function lie in a half-space x⋅ω≥R+η, with ∣ω∣=1 and η>0. By contour shifting,
⟨u,φ⟩=(2π)−n∫Rn​F(ξ+itω)φ​(−ξ−itω)dξ,t≥0.
(24)
Here is the decay needed to justify the shift and then let t grow. Repeated integration by parts, applying 1−Δx​ to e−tx⋅ωφ(x), gives for every integer M,
∣φ​(−ξ−itω)∣≤CM​(1+t)2Me−(R+η)t(1+∣ξ∣2)−M.
(25)
Choose 2M>N+n. For each fixed t, this decay makes the vertical sides of a large rectangle vanish in the one complex coordinate parallel to ω; Cauchy integral theorem then supplies the contour identity. Combining the two bounds yields
∣⟨u,φ⟩∣≤C′(1+t)N+2Me−ηt⟶0.
(26)
Thus u vanishes on such test functions. Every point outside BR​ has a neighborhood of this form. A partition of unity therefore proves suppu⊆BR​, completing the converse. The same argument with separating half-spaces proves the compact-convex-set form. This is the contour-shift proof of the Paley–Wiener–Schwartz theorem.
For a regular function u, the change of variables y=tx gives
∫u(tx)φ(x)dx=t−n∫u(y)φ(y/t)dy.
(27)
The map φ↦φ(⋅/t) is continuous on the Schwartz space, so duality extends the dilation of a distribution to
⟨δt​u,φ⟩=t−n⟨u,δ1/t​φ⟩.​
(28)
Its Fourier transform obeys δt​u​(ζ)=t−nF(ζ/t). If the original support is in the unit ball, the theorem bounds F by C(1+∣ζ∣)Ne∣Imζ∣. Hence
∣δt​u​(ζ)∣≤Ct​(1+∣ζ∣)Ne∣Imζ∣/t.
(29)
The converse theorem gives the support law for distributional dilation
supp(δt​u)⊆{x:∣x∣≤1/t}={x:∣tx∣≤1}.​
(30)
