An isolated collapse emits transient matter and gravitational waves. The exterior is expected to settle to an asymptotically flat stationary spacetime. Because the star is uncharged, the black-hole uniqueness theorem identifies the regular final state with a Kerr black hole. Its higher multipole moments are fixed by its conserved charges: the black-hole no-hair theorem leaves only the total mass M and angular momentum J. Thus the late-time spacetime is characterized by M and J.

The zeros of f give three causal structures.
  • If M>Q, there are two simple horizons r+​>r−​>0. The maximally extended Penrose diagram is the repeating subextremal charged-black-hole diagram: r=r+​ is an event horizon, r=r−​ is a Cauchy horizon, and r=0 is a timelike singularity.
  • If M=Q, the roots coincide. The extremal diagram has one degenerate horizon, no bifurcation surface, and an infinite throat between the exterior and interior.
  • If M<Q, f has no real zero. The timelike r=0 singularity is visible from infinity, so there is a naked singularity and no black-hole region.
Thus the cases are subextremal, extremal, and overextremal according as M>Q, M=Q, and M<Q.

The relevant implications in the causal hierarchy are
stable causality⟹strong causality⟹future-distinguishing spacetime.
(21)
The first follows from strict cone widening: failure of strong causality would give causal curves leaving and re-entering arbitrarily small neighborhoods, and the wider metric turns the limiting recurrence into a closed timelike curve. For the second, if distinct p,q had I+(p)=I+(q), arbitrarily short timelike segments near either point and this equality would produce a causal curve leaving and re-entering a sufficiently small causally convex neighborhood. Therefore
I+(p)=I+(q)⟹p=q​.
(22)
The stated null geodesic completeness is stronger than needed: stable causality already gives the conclusion.

For a real free scalar field on a globally hyperbolic spacetime, take
S=−21​∫d4x−g​(∇a​ϕ∇aϕ+(m2+ξR)ϕ2).

The Klein-Gordon equation has a well-posed initial-value problem on every Cauchy hypersurface. Its complex solution space carries the conserved Klein-Gordon inner product. Choose modes with
(ui​,uj​)KG​=δij​,(ui∗​,uj∗​)KG​=−δij​,(ui​,uj∗​)KG​=0,
(25)
and expand
ϕ​=∑i​(ai​ui​+ai†​ui∗​),[ai​,aj†​]=δij​.
(26)
The canonical commutation relations produce the bosonic Fock space, and ai​∣0⟩=0 defines the chosen vacuum.
The choice of positive-frequency solutions is extra structure. In a time-dependent geometry there is no preferred time translation, so different choices mix ui​ with ui∗​ and give the vacuum ambiguity in a nonstationary spacetime. In a stationary spacetime, a timelike Killing vector field defines positive frequency by iLK​ui​=ωi​ui​, ωi​>0, and hence the preferred vacuum state in a stationary spacetime.
If the spacetime is stationary in the remote past and future, define normalized in- and out-modes there. Their Bogoliubov transformation is
uiout​=∑j​(αij​ujin​+βij​ujin∗​).
(27)

(28)
Thus nonzero beta coefficients measure particle creation by the intervening time dependence.

Classically, the laws of black-hole mechanics have the same form as the laws of thermodynamics, but this could have been only a formal analogy. Hawking radiation makes it physical: a stationary black hole emits approximately thermal radiation with
TH​=2πkB​cℏκ​​,
(29)
so surface gravity is proportional to genuine temperature.
The correspondence is:
  • constancy of κ on a stationary horizon becomes constancy of equilibrium temperature;
  • the First law of black-hole mechanics
dM=8πGκ​dA+ΩH​dJ+ΦH​dQ
(30)
matches the first law of thermodynamics and fixes the Bekenstein-Hawking entropy
SBH​=4GℏkB​c3A​​;
(31)
  • Hawking's area theorem becomes a second law, replaced in the quantum theory by the generalized second law for SBH​+Soutside​;
  • the impossibility of reaching κ=0 by a finite process parallels the unattainability of absolute zero.
A real Hawking temperature therefore identifies horizon area as thermodynamic entropy and turns the geometric laws into thermodynamic laws.
