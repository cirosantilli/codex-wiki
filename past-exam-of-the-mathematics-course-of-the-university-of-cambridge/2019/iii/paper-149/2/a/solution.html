Let π:G→G/[G,G] be the abelianization map. Its image π(A) is again a K-approximate group. Apply the large-progression form of the Freiman-Green-Ruzsa theorem to π(A). It gives a finite subgroup H, elements x1​,…,xr​, and lengths L1​,…,Lr​, with
r≤KO(1),HP(x1​,…,xr​;L1​,…,Lr​)⊆π(A4),
(19)
and
∣HP∣≥exp(−KO(1))∣π(A)∣.
(20)
The large lifted product from a coset progression applied to this progression gives
​(A16∩π−1(H))∏i=1r​(A22∩π−1(⟨xi​⟩))​≥exp(−KO(1))∣A∣.
(21)
Briefly, choose a section of π on π(A6). Multiplication by that section is multiplicative up to A12∩[G,G]; lifting successively the subgroup part and each progression direction therefore places every element of A6∩π−1(HP) in the displayed product. The fiber-counting lemma for a quotient map gives ∣A6∩π−1(HP)∣≥exp(−KO(1))∣A∣, which proves the estimate.
Set
A0​=A16∩π−1(H),Ai​=A22∩π−1(⟨xi​⟩)(1≤i≤r).
(22)
The intersection of an approximate group power with a subgroup shows that each Ai​ is a KO(1)-approximate group contained in AO(1). The preimage of a cyclic subgroup of G/[G,G] has step less than s. The same is true of the preimage of the finite subgroup H because G is a torsion-free group. Consequently each ⟨Ai​⟩ has step less than s, and the displayed estimate is the required conclusion.
