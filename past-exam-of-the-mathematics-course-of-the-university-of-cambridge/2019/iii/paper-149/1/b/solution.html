The Noncommutative Ruzsa triangle inequality says that nonempty finite subsets B,C,D of a group satisfy
∣B∣∣CD−1∣≤∣CB−1∣∣BD−1∣.
(2)
The Ruzsa covering lemma says that if ∣CD∣≤K∣D∣, then some X⊆C with ∣X∣≤K satisfies
C⊆XDD−1.
(3)
Now let A be a symmetric subset of a group, so A−1=A and 1∈A. Apply the triangle inequality with the middle set A to obtain, for m≥4,
∣A∣∣Am∣≤∣Am−1∣∣A3∣.
(4)
The hypothesis therefore gives ∣Am∣≤K∣Am−1∣. Starting from ∣A3∣≤K∣A∣ proves
∣Am∣≤Km−2∣A∣(m≥3).​
(5)
In particular ∣A5∣≤K3∣A∣. Apply the covering lemma to C=A4 and D=A. There is X⊆A4 with ∣X∣≤K3 such that
A4⊆XAA−1=XA2.
(6)
The set A2 is symmetric and contains the identity element, so this inclusion is exactly the covering condition showing that
A2 is a K3-approximate group.​
(7)
Small doubling alone is insufficient in a noncommutative group. Let H be a finite group, let G=H∗⟨x⟩ be its free product with an infinite cyclic group, and put
A=H∪{x,x−1}.
(8)
Then A is symmetric and ∣A2∣≤5∣H∣+4=O(∣A∣), while A3 contains the double coset HxH. Distinct pairs (h1​,h2​)∈H2 give distinct reduced words h1​xh2​, so ∣HxH∣=∣H∣2. Letting ∣H∣→∞ proves the small doubling does not control tripling in a noncommutative group phenomenon.
