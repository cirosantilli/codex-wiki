On every open set U, define FU#​(a)=ap. In characteristic of a ring p, the binomial theorem gives (a+b)p=ap+bp, so these are ring homomorphisms; they commute with restrictions and hence define a morphism of sheaves of rings.
On an affine chart SpecA, the inverse image of a prime ideal p under the Frobenius endomorphism is
{a:ap∈p}=p,
(10)
by primality. The induced continuous map is therefore the identity. These local morphisms agree on overlaps, giving the Absolute Frobenius morphism FX​:X→X. Its action on the underlying space and on every local section was prescribed, so the morphism is unique.

Put R=k[x,y,z]/(xy−z3) and let X=SpecR. The ideal P=(x,z) is a height-one prime ideal, because R/P≅k[y] and dimR=2. It therefore defines a prime Weil divisor D=V(x,z).
Localizing at x eliminates y, giving
Rx​≅k[x,x−1,z],
(16)
a unique factorization domain. The Nagata theorem for divisor class groups says that Cl(X) is generated by the height-one primes containing x. Since
R/(x)≅k[y,z]/(z3),
(17)
the only such prime is P, and hence [D] generates. At the generic point of D, y is a unit in a ring and x=z3/y, so the order of vanishing is three:
div(x)=3D.
(18)
Thus 3[D]=0.
This relation has exact order three. Indeed, if a principal divisor were supported on D, its defining rational function would be a unit on D(x). The units of Rx​=k[z][x,x−1] are precisely cxm with c∈k× and m∈Z, whose divisors are 3mD. Consequently
Cl(k[x,y,z]/(xy−z3))≅Z/3Z,​
(19)
generated by [V(x,z)]. This is the n=3 case of the Divisor class group of an A-type surface singularity.

First prove that A is an integrally closed domain. If u∈FracA is integral over A, then it is integral over Ax​. Since the unique factorization domain Ax​ is integrally closed, write u=a/xn with a∈A and n≥0 minimal. If n>0, an integral equation for u gives, after multiplication by a suitable power of x,
am+c1​am−1xn+⋯+cm​xnm=0.
(20)
Hence am∈(x). The ideal (x) is prime, so a∈(x), contradicting minimality of n. Therefore n=0 and u∈A.
Now apply the Nagata theorem for divisor class groups. The class group of Ax​ vanishes by the divisor-class criterion for unique factorization. Hence Cl(A) is generated by height-one primes that meet {1,x,x2,…}. Such a prime contains x and must equal (x), because the Krull principal ideal theorem makes the nonzero prime (x) itself height one. Its divisor class is principal, so Cl(A)=0. A second application of the divisor-class criterion yields
A is a unique factorization domain.​
(21)
This argument is the Nagata criterion for unique factorization domains.
