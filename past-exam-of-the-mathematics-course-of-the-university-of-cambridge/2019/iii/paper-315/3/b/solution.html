At 10au from a four-solar-luminosity star, the incident flux is proportional to 4/102=1/25, approximately the same as for Jupiter at 5au around the Sun. Thus Jupiter supplies a possible old comparison at roughly the same mass and irradiation.
Assume the radius law holds from the young epoch to an old age of 4.5Gyr, that the irradiation history can be represented by the same fixed value, and that the present old radius is RJ​. If t is approximated by stellar age, then
RJ​2RJ​​=(4500Myr5Myr​)−α,α=log900log2​≃0.102.​
(21)
If t instead starts at completion of formation, the model's formation delay of up to 3Myr leaves the young planet with thermal age between 2 and 5Myr. Taking the old thermal age as approximately 4500Myr then gives α≃0.090–0.102.
Thus an exponent of order 0.1 is a reasonable conditional estimate. A single radius at one age cannot determine both the exponent and normalization of a power law; the old Jupiter comparison and the assumptions about formation time and irradiation are essential.
