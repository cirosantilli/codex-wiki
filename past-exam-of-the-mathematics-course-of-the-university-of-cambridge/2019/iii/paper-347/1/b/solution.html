Use a geometrically thin spherical shell with constant mass Msh​ and assume that its dust transfers momentum efficiently to its gas. Define the ultraviolet and infrared opacities per unit total shell mass by κUV​ and κIR​. Its dust optical depths are
τj​(R)=4πR2κj​Msh​​,j∈{UV,IR}.
(7)
For a central ultraviolet luminosity L, the usual dusty radiation-pressure-driven shell model absorbs a fraction 1−e−τUV​ of the incident momentum and adds an infrared trapping contribution τIR​L/c. Neglecting swept-up mass, shell self-gravity, and other gravitating matter, its thin-shell momentum equation is
Msh​dtdv​=cL​(1+τIR​−e−τUV​)−R2GMBH​Msh​​.
(8)
Balancing these terms gives the critical luminosity of a dusty shell:
LEdd,sh​(R)=R2(1+τIR​−e−τUV​)GMBH​Msh​c​.​
(9)
This is a local force-balance luminosity, so it can depend on radius even when L is constant.
The three useful limiting Eddington ratios are
LEdd,IR​LEdd,ss​(R)LEdd,UV​​=κIR​4πGMBH​c​,=R2GMBH​Msh​c​,=κUV​4πGMBH​c​,​ΓIR​Γss​(R)ΓUV​​=4πGMBH​cκIR​L​,=GMBH​Msh​cLR2​,=4πGMBH​cκUV​L​.​​
(10)
The infrared limit assumes τIR​≫1 and ultraviolet absorption; the single-scattering limit assumes τUV​≫1≫τIR​; the ultraviolet-thin limit assumes τUV​≪1 with infrared opacity negligible compared with ultraviolet opacity. In the ultraviolet-opaque region the two radiative contributions add, and the effective ratio is ΓIR​+Γss​(R).
The ultraviolet dust transparency radius for this constant-mass shell is
RUV​=(4πκUV​Msh​​)1/2.
(11)
For the terminal speed of a constant-mass dusty shell, approximate the ultraviolet absorption factor by 1 inside RUV​ and by τUV​ outside. The infrared contribution outside that radius is neglected; it is small there if κIR​≪κUV​. Define Γss​=Γss​(R0​), so this otherwise radius-dependent ratio in the paper's final formula is an initial value.
Inside RUV​ the acceleration is
vdRdv​=Msh​cL​+R2GMBH​​(ΓIR​−1).
(12)
Integrating from R0​ to RUV​ gives
vUV2​=v02​+Msh​c2L​(RUV​−R0​)+2GMBH​(ΓIR​−1)(R0​1​−RUV​1​).
(13)
Outside RUV​, vdv/dR=GMBH​(ΓUV​−1)/R2. Its integral to infinity supplies 2GMBH​(ΓUV​−1)/RUV​. Combining the two integrals yields
v∞2​=​v02​+R0​2GMBH​​[Γss​R0​RUV​​+ΓIR​−1](1−RUV​R0​​)+RUV​2GMBH​​(ΓUV​−1).​​
(14)
This assumes that the outward trajectory actually reaches the transparency radius; a positive formal endpoint energy alone does not rule out an intermediate turning point.
Put X=RUV​/R0​=τUV​(R0​)​ and vesc,02​=2GMBH​/R0​. The ratios obey ΓUV​=Γss​X2. The preceding expression becomes
vesc,02​v∞2​−v02​​=Γss​(2X−1)+ΓIR​(1−X−1)−1.
(15)
If X≫1 and infrared work is subdominant,
vesc,02​v∞2​−v02​​≃2Γss​τUV​(R0​)​−1.​
(16)
For a luminosity of order the initial single-scattering Eddington luminosity, Γss​∼1, this implies v∞​/vesc,0​∼2​τUV​(R0​)1/4≫1. The shell continues intercepting approximately L/c of momentum per unit time over a large distance while gravity weakens as R−2. The resulting radiative work is not restricted to its initial gravitational binding energy: an Eddington luminosity is a local force threshold, not a universal cap on outflow speed.
If the unqualified LEdd​ instead means the electron-scattering value, the statement needs an additional condition. For L=LEdd,es​, ΓUV​=κUV​/κes​, and
vesc,02​v∞2​−v02​​≃κes​τUV​(R0​)​2κUV​​−1.
(17)
Thus speeds greatly above escape velocity require κUV​/κes​≫τUV​(R0​)​, together with a viable launch. Large ultraviolet optical depth by itself does not prove the conclusion at fixed electron-scattering luminosity and opacity. The two interpretations must not be conflated.
