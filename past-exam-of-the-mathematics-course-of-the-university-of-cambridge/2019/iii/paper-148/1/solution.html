An element t∈T is an integral element over R when it satisfies a monic equation
tn+rn−1​tn−1+⋯+r0​=0,ri​∈R.
(1)
The extension R⊆T is an integral extension when every t∈T is integral over R.
Let P∈SpecR and localize both rings at S=R∖P. The extension RP​⊆S−1T remains integral. Choose a maximal ideal n of S−1T. The contraction of a maximal ideal under an integral extension is maximal, and the local ring RP​ has unique maximal ideal PRP​, so n∩RP​=PRP​. Contracting n back to T produces Q∈SpecT with Q∩R=P. This proves the Lying-over theorem and hence the surjectivity of
π:SpecT⟶SpecR.
(2)
Suppose Q1​⊆Q2​ and both contract to P. Quotient by Q1​ and localize the resulting integral domain at the nonzero elements of R/P. The localized ring is an integral domain integral over the field Frac(R/P), and is therefore itself a field. The localization of Q2​/Q1​ must consequently be zero; since the localized ring is a domain, Q2​/Q1​=0. Thus the incomparability theorem for integral extensions gives
Q1​=Q2​.​
(3)
The Krull dimension of a ring is the supremum of the lengths of its strict chains of prime ideals. Incomparability makes the contraction of every strict prime chain in T strict, so dimT≤dimR. Conversely, start over the bottom prime of any chain in R using lying over and lift each subsequent inclusion using the going-up theorem. Hence
dimT=dimR.​
(4)
For the concrete surface, write x,y,z for the residue classes of X,Y,Z and put
u=x−z,v=y−z.
(5)
Then
xy+yz+zx=uv+2(u+v)z+3z2,
(6)
and characteristic zero allows division by three. Therefore
T≅k[u,v][z]/(z2+32​(u+v)z+31​uv).
(7)
Division by the monic quadratic shows that T is free over k[u,v] with basis 1,z. In particular, u,v are algebraically independent and the requested Noether normalization of the quadratic surface xy plus yz plus zx is
R=k[x−z,y−z]≅k[U,V].​
