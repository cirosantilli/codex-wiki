For the R-R-bimodule M, the Hochschild chain complex is
Cn​(R,M)=M⊗k​R⊗k​n,
(29)
with boundary
b(m⊗r1​⊗⋯⊗rn​)=​mr1​⊗r2​⊗⋯⊗rn​+i=1∑n−1​(−1)im⊗r1​⊗⋯⊗ri​ri+1​⊗⋯⊗rn​+(−1)nrn​m⊗r1​⊗⋯⊗rn−1​.​
(30)
Then
HHn​(R,M)=Hn​(C∙​(R,M),b).
(31)
The Hochschild cochain complex is Cn(R,M)=Homk​(R⊗k​n,M) with
(δf)(r1​,…,rn+1​)=​r1​f(r2​,…,rn+1​)+i=1∑n​(−1)if(r1​,…,ri​ri+1​,…,rn+1​)+(−1)n+1f(r1​,…,rn​)rn+1​,​
(32)
and HHn(R,M)=Hn(C∙(R,M),δ).
A derivation into a bimodule is a k-linear map d:R→M satisfying
d(rs)=rd(s)+d(r)s.
(33)
It is an inner derivation when d=dm​ for some m∈M, where dm​(r)=rm−mr. The degree-one cocycle equation is precisely the Leibniz rule, and the degree-one coboundaries are the inner derivations. Therefore the First Hochschild cohomology as outer derivations is
HH1(R,M)≅Derk​(R,M)/Inn(R,M).​
(34)
Let μ:R⊗k​R→R be multiplication and Ω=kerμ, equipped with the outer bimodule structure. The universal bimodule derivation
D:R⟶Ω,D(r)=r⊗1−1⊗r
(35)
satisfies D(rs)=rD(s)+D(r)s. Composition with D defines
HomR−R​(Ω,M)⟶Derk​(R,M).
(36)
For a derivation d, its inverse image under this map is
θd​(∑i​ri​⊗si​)=∑i​d(ri​)si​.
(37)
If ∑i​ri​si​=0, the Leibniz rule shows that this formula is both left and right R-linear. Moreover every element of Ω is ∑i​D(ri​)si​, proving existence and uniqueness.
For dm​(r)=rm−mr, the corresponding map is
θm​(i∑​ri​⊗si​)=i∑​ri​msi​.​
(38)
These are exactly the maps Ω→M which extend to bimodule maps R⊗k​R→M.
