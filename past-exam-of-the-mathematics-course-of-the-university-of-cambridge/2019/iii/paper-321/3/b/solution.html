Write H=z+ and B+​=Bx+​, and assume Bz​=0 and q>0. In a steady state, the horizontally invariant magnetized shearing-sheet equations give
vx′​=0,By′​=Bz​μ0​ρ​(2−q)Ωvx​.
(31)
Since By​ vanishes at both boundaries, vx​=0 for q=2, and then By​=0 throughout. The other two equations are
vy​=−2μ0​ρΩBz​​Bx′​,vy′​=Bz​qΩ​Bx​.
(32)
Eliminating vy​ gives the harmonic oscillator equation
Bx′′​+K2Bx​=0,K2=Bz2​2qμ0​ρΩ2​.
(33)
The midplane-symmetric magnetic bending in an incompressible disk has odd Bx​ and even vy​. Applying Bx​(±H)=±B+​ gives
Bx​=B+​sin(KH)sin(Kz)​,By​=0,vx​=0​,
(34)
vy​=−2μ0​ρΩsin(KH)Bz​KB+​​cos(Kz)​.
(35)
For nonzero imposed inclination B+​, this equilibrium exists only when sin(KH)=0. More generally, Bx​=Asin(Kz)+Dcos(Kz); the boundary conditions require Asin(KH)=B+​ and Dcos(KH)=0. Thus the displayed solution is unique away from these resonances. At cos(KH)=0, an additional even homogeneous solution is possible unless midplane symmetry is imposed. At q=2, an arbitrary constant vx​ is also allowed because its coefficient in the azimuthal equation vanishes; choosing vx​=0 gives the same symmetric equilibrium.
