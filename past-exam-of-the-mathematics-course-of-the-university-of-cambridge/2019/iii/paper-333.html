Work on a beta plane with f=f0​+βy and constant depth H. Assume a steady, small-Rossby number interior, negligible friction there, small sea-surface displacements, and negligible depth gradients. Taking the vertical curl of momentum gives
∂t​ζ+u⋅∇ζ+(ζ+f)∇⋅u+βv=(∇×W)z​+(∇×D)z​.
(1)
The constant-depth continuity equation gives ∇⋅u=0. Neglecting relative-vorticity tendency and advection, and the interior dissipation, yields Sverdrup balance:
βv=(∇×W)z​.​
(2)
Introduce the ocean transport streamfunction by Hu=−ψy​, Hv=ψx​. Then βψx​=H(∇×W)z​. Here W has units of acceleration, as in the given momentum equation; for a physical surface wind stress τ, it would be τ/(ρH).
To obtain the circulation law, use the vector identity u⋅∇u=∇(∣u∣2/2)+ζk×u. Its gradient term and the sea-surface pressure gradient integrate to zero around a closed contour. Thus
∂t​∮C​u⋅dl=−∮C​(ζ+f)(k×u)⋅dl+∮C​(W+D)⋅dl.​
(3)
On an impermeable island boundary, velocity is tangent to the coast, so (k×u)⋅dl=0 pointwise. Consequently its circulation obeys
∂t​∮CI​​u⋅dl=∮CI​​(W+D)⋅dl.​
(4)
For the steady island problem, choose a counterclockwise contour that goes east from the southern tip to the basin's eastern wall, north along that wall, west to the northern tip, and south down the western coast of the island. It avoids the eastern-island and western-basin dissipative layers. In the leading momentum balance, k×u=−∇ψ/H. Integration by parts gives
∮C​f(k×u)⋅dl=H1​∮C​ψdf=−Hf(yN​)−f(yS​)​ψI​.
(5)
Here ψ=0 on the basin wall and ψ=ψI​ on the island, with constant latitude on the two connecting legs. Equating this integral to the wind forcing gives the Godfrey island rule
ψI​=−β(yN​−yS​)H​∮C​W⋅dl.​
(6)
For the square island, yN​=R/2, yS​=−R/2, and its west coast is x=−R/2. Both horizontal contour legs have length S+R/2. With W=sin(πy)ex​,
∮C​W⋅dl=−2(S+R/2)sin(πR/2),
(7)
so
ψI​=βR2H(S+R/2)​sin(πR/2),Teast​=∫R/2S​Hvdx=−ψI​.​
(8)
The sign convention makes Teast​ positive northward. For 0<R<2 it is southward. The total transport includes the island's thin eastern boundary current: omitting that current would leave a latitude-dependent interior transport rather than this constant passage transport.
To quantify the island's effect on the western boundary current, compare interior streamfunctions west of the island. In its latitude band, the inviscid solution is
ψwest​(x,y)=ψI​+βHπ​cos(πy)(−R/2−x).
(9)
Without an island it would be Hπcos(πy)(S−x)/β. Since the western basin wall has zero streamfunction, the difference in its required northward boundary-current transport is
δTW​(y)=ψI​−βHπ​(S+R/2)cos(πy).​
(10)
Thus the island changes the current's strength by redistributing transport across its latitude band. For a square contained in the central positive-cosine band, it weakens the northward current near y=0 and strengthens it near the island's northern and southern edges. The change averages to zero over −R/2<y<R/2; its local sign follows the formula for other island sizes.

Take f0​>0 for definiteness and define K=(k2+l2)1/2>0. Thermal-wind balance gives Uz​=−by​/f0​=Λ, and the lower boundary condition fixes U=Λz. The basic interior quasi-geostrophic potential vorticity has no gradient, so a boundary-supported normal mode ψ′=Re[ϕ(z)ei(kx+ly−ωt)] has zero interior potential-vorticity perturbation. Its vertical equation is
ϕ′′−μ02​ϕ=0,μ0​=f0​N0​K​.
(11)
Decay upward selects ϕ=Ae−μ0​z.
The buoyancy perturbation is b′=f0​ψz′​. At a rigid boundary w′=0, linearized material conservation of buoyancy gives
(∂t​+U∂x​)ψz′​−Λψx′​=0.
(12)
At z=0, this is cϕ′(0)+Λϕ(0)=0, where c=ω/k for k=0. The Eady edge wave therefore has
c=μ0​Λ​=N0​KΛf0​​,ω=N0​k2+l2​Λf0​k​.​
(13)
It is an exponentially trapped boundary-buoyancy wave, not a vertically propagating interior mode. The formula for ω extends to k=0, where the perturbation is stationary.
For the two-region problem, absence of a density jump at the common material interface requires continuity of buoyancy:
(N12​−N22​)h(y)=f0​(Λ1​−Λ2​)y.
(14)
Thus its basic slope is a=hy​=f0​(Λ1​−Λ2​)/(N12​−N22​). For a nondegenerate stratification contrast, its variation over horizontal scale L is aL/D=O(Ro/Bu), which is small in the usual quasi-geostrophic approximation with Burgers number of order one. The matching conditions can therefore be applied at the constant reference level z=0 to leading order. An exceptionally small stratification contrast would require checking this flat-interface ordering separately.
Pressure and normal velocity must be continuous because there is no membrane or singular interfacial force. Since base buoyancy is continuous, expanding pressure continuity at the displaced interface gives simply ϕ1​(0)=ϕ2​(0). The base horizontal velocity is also continuous there; call it UI​. Decay away from the interface then gives
ϕ1​=Aeμ1​z(z<0),ϕ2​=Ae−μ2​z(z>0),μj​=f0​Nj​K​.
(15)
Linearized buoyancy conservation in each region gives
wj′​=Nj2​ikf0​​[(c−UI​)ϕj′​+Λj​ϕj​].
(16)
Matching normal velocity gives w1′​=w2′​: the common slope correction to normal velocity cancels because pressure continuity also makes the horizontal velocity perturbation continuous. Hence
(c−UI​)(N12​ϕ1′​​−N22​ϕ2′​​)+(N12​Λ1​​−N22​Λ2​​)A=0.
(17)
The quasi-geostrophic wave at a stratification interface has intrinsic phase velocity
c−UI​=μ1​/N12​+μ2​/N22​Λ2​/N22​−Λ1​/N12​​=Kf0​​N1​N2​(N1​+N2​)Λ2​N12​−Λ1​N22​​.​
(18)
As N1​/N2​→∞ with N2​ and the shears bounded, the lower region becomes effectively rigid and
c−UI​⟶N2​KΛ2​f0​​,​
(19)
recovering the upper-fluid Eady edge wave. The matching calculation describes regular interface-supported modes; arbitrary interior potential-vorticity initial disturbances can additionally be advected by the shear.

Let Dg​=∂t​+ug​⋅∇h​, ψ=Φ/f0​, and ug​=(−ψy​,ψx​,0). The hydrostatic and thermal equations combine to give
Dg​ψz​=−f0​N2​w,N2=HRS(z)​.​
(20)
Taking the vertical curl of the horizontal momentum equations to leading quasi-geostrophic order gives
Dg​∇h2​ψ+βψx​+f0​∇h​⋅ua,h​=0.
(21)
The log-pressure coordinate continuity equation gives ∇h​⋅ua,h​=−ez/H∂z​(e−z/Hw). Substitute w=−f0​Dg​ψz​/N2. In commuting the vertical derivative with Dg​, the extra term is ug,z​⋅∇h​ψz​=(−ψyz​,ψxz​)⋅(ψxz​,ψyz​)=0. Therefore the log-pressure quasi-geostrophic potential vorticity equation is
Dg​[ψxx​+ψyy​+ez/H∂z​(e−z/HN2f02​​ψz​)]+βψx​=0.​
(22)
The required small parameters have distinct roles. The Rossby number ϵR​=U/(∣f0​∣L) makes inertial acceleration small relative to the leading geostrophic balance. The parameter ϵβ​=βL/∣f0​∣ lets that leading balance use a single f0​ while retaining the beta effect in the slower vorticity evolution. Finally, ψ∼UL and the thermal equation imply w∼∣f0​∣U2/(N2D). Density-weighted continuity has vertical derivative scale 1/δ, where δ=min(D,H), so ∣ua,h​∣∼Lw/δ. The vertical-advection consistency criterion for log-pressure quasi-geostrophy is thus
U∣ua,h​∣​∼ϵa​=RSDmin(D,H)f02​L2H​∣f0​∣LU​≪1.​
(23)
It also controls the omitted vertical-advection ratio wL/(UD)∼ϵa​δ/D. Small Rossby number alone would not ensure small ageostrophic flow for every choice of stratification and vertical scales.
For constant N, linearization about rest gives
∂t​[∇h2​ψ+N2f02​​(ψzz​−H1​ψz​)]+βψx​=0.
(24)
Putting ψ=Re[ψ​c​ez/(2H)ei(kx+mz−ωt)] replaces the vertical operator by −m2−1/(4H2). Consequently the Rossby wave in a log-pressure atmosphere has
ω=−k2+(f02​/N2)[m2+1/(4H2)]βk​.​
(25)
For the forced half-space, substitution of the specified ansatz gives
χ​′′+m2χ​=0,m2=−4H21​−f02​N2​(k2+ωβk​),χ​(0)=Ψb​.
(26)
Assume β>0 and k>0, and define ω∗​=−βk/[k2+f02​/(4N2H2)]<0.
If ω>0 or ω<ω∗​, then m2<0. Boundedness excludes the growing solution and uniquely selects χ​=Ψb​e−−m2​z​. If ω=ω∗​, boundedness excludes the linear-in-z solution, leaving χ​=Ψb​; this is the zero-vertical-wavenumber threshold.
If ω∗​<ω<0, both oscillatory solutions are bounded. Boundedness alone does not give a unique propagating solution. With forcing at the bottom and no incident wave from above, impose an upward radiation condition. Since
∂m∂ω​=[k2+(f02​/N2)(m2+1/(4H2))]22βk(f02​/N2)m​,
(27)
positive m has upward group velocity. The outgoing solution is χ​=Ψb​eimz,m=m2​>0​. The physical streamfunction includes the prescribed ez/(2H) density-weighting factor; it is the amplitude χ​ that must remain bounded.
Thus the strict range for vertically propagating waves is
−k2+f02​/(4N2H2)βk​<ω<0.​
(28)
At ω=0, the unforced steady interior equation requires βψx​=0, so a nonzero k bottom streamfunction cannot have a time-independent solution of this ideal rest-state problem without additional dynamics.

Assume β>0, c=gH​, and use complex normal mode amplitudes. The zonal momentum and continuity equations give
ωu−kgη​=iβyv,ωη​−kHu=−iHv′.
(29)
When ω2=k2c2, solving these two linear equations gives
u=ω2−k2c2i(ωβyv−kc2v′)​,η​=ω2−k2c2iH(kβyv−ωv′)​.​
(30)
For v=0, a nonzero solution requires ω=±kc and η​=±cu/g. The remaining momentum equation gives u′=∓βyu/c. Only the positive sign decays at both infinities, so the equatorial Kelvin wave is
ω=kc,u=U0​e−βy2/(2c),η​=gc​u,v=0.​
(31)
Its phase and group velocities are both eastward with speed c.
For nonzero meridional velocity, substitute the eliminated amplitudes into −iωv+βyu=−gη​′. The result is
v′′+(c2ω2​−k2−ωβk​−c2β2y2​)v=0.
(32)
Set Y=yβ/c​. The decaying Hermite polynomial modes have v=Vn​Hn​(Y)e−Y2/2, up to normalization, and their eigenvalue gives the equatorial shallow-water dispersion relation
ω2−c2k2−ωβkc2​=(2n+1)βc.​
(33)
For n=0, the quadratic in k factors as
(k+ω/c)(k−ω/c+β/ω)=0.
(34)
The admissible Yanai wave root is k=ω/c−β/ω. With v(0)=1, substitution and cancellation give
v=e−βy2/(2c),u=icωy​v,η​=igωy​v.​
(35)
These expressions satisfy the original equations even at the isolated point where the two algebraic roots coincide and the earlier elimination formula has a zero denominator. The other root is not an independent admissible branch: its generic singular reconstruction fails the trapping conditions.
In the zonal momentum equation, the acceleration term is −iωu=(ω2y/c)v, the Coriolis term is −βyv, and the pressure term is −ikgη​=kωyv. When ω2≪βc, acceleration is small and the leading balance is between Coriolis acceleration and the pressure gradient. When ω2≫βc, acceleration balances pressure and the Coriolis term is small; the branch tends to an eastward gravity wave.
For n≥1, the fixed-frequency zonal roots are
k=−2ωβ​±4ω2β2​+c2ω2​−c(2n+1)β​​.
(36)
The radicand is nonnegative exactly when s=ω2/(βc) satisfies s2−(2n+1)s+1/4≥0. This gives the frequency gap for higher equatorial wave modes:
s≤n+21​−n(n+1)​ors≥n+21​+n(n+1)​.​
(37)
For the forcing frequency ω=21​βc​, we have s=1/4. Already the n=1 low-frequency upper bound is 3/2−2​<1/4, and that bound decreases with n, while the high-frequency bound increases. Hence no n≥1 mode propagates. The available real-wavenumber branches are
kK​=21​cβ​​,kY​=−23​cβ​​.​
(38)
For the equatorial Kelvin wave, cg,K​=c. For the Yanai wave, differentiating k=ω/c−β/ω gives
cg,Y​=ω2+βccω2​=5c​>0.​
(39)
Thus both propagating responses are detected east of the localized forcing, provided it has nonzero projection onto their meridional structures. The Yanai phase travels westward at this frequency, but its wave packet and energy travel eastward. Higher modes contribute only an evanescent response near the forcing.
