Assume stellar homology: fixed dimensionless profiles, uniform fixed composition, negligible radiation pressure, ideal gas support, and radiative transport throughout the model. Mass conservation gives ρc​∝M/R3. The hydrostatic pressure support equation gives Pc​∝GM2/R4; combining with P∝ρT gives Tc​∝M/R.
Integrating specific proton–proton chain energy generation over mass gives
Lnuc​∝Mρc​Tc4​∝M6R−7.
(1)

Lrad​∝ρc2​RTc15/2​​∝M11/2R−1/2.
(2)
Equating generated and transported luminosity gives M1/2∝R13/2, so the radiative homology with proton-proton burning and Kramers opacity relation is
M∝R13.​
(3)
The proportionality constants are fixed only within the adopted homologous family, not for arbitrary evolving stars.

Consider an element displaced upward by ξ in hydrostatic equilibrium. Assume it quickly reaches the ambient pressure, but moves adiabatically and retains its composition. Put ∇=dlogT/dlogP, ∇μ​=dlogμ/dlogP, and let ∇ad​ be the adiabatic temperature gradient. The equation of state has differential
dlogρ=αP​dlogP−δdlogT+ϕdlogμ,
(13)
where δ=−(∂logρ/∂logT)P,μ​ and ϕ=(∂logρ/∂logμ)P,T​.
At the new pressure, the parcel-to-environment density difference is
ρρparcel​−ρenv​​=[δ(∇−∇ad​)−ϕ∇μ​]ΔlogP.
(14)
With positive pressure scale height HP​=−(dlogP/dr)−1, ΔlogP=−ξ/HP​. The buoyancy acceleration is therefore ξ¨​=−N2ξ, with stellar buoyancy frequency
N2=HP​g​[δ(∇ad​−∇)+ϕ∇μ​].
(15)
A negative N2 amplifies the displacement. For a radiative background, ∇=∇rad​, so the Ledoux criterion for instability is
∇rad​>∇ad​+δϕ​∇μ​.​
(16)
For uniform composition, ∇μ​=0, giving the Schwarzschild criterion
∇rad​>∇ad​.​
(17)
Equality is marginal stability. A mean molecular weight increasing inward has ∇μ​>0 and supplies a stabilizing composition term when ϕ,δ>0.

The Eddington closure approximation with the standard surface boundary condition gives the grey atmosphere profile
T4=43​Teff4​(τ+32​).

Assume zero temperature, complete ionization, constant composition, noninteracting electrons, negligible ion thermal pressure, and Newtonian stellar gravity. Ions supply almost all the mass, while electron degeneracy pressure supplies support. By the Pauli exclusion principle, the two electron spin states fill a momentum sphere up to Fermi momentum pF​. Counting states gives
ne​=(2πℏ)32​34πpF3​​=3π2ℏ3pF3​​,ρ=μe​mu​ne​,
(22)
where μe​ is mean molecular weight per electron. The momentum flux of this isotropic Fermi gas is
P=3(2πℏ)32​∫p≤pF​​pv(p)d3p=3π2ℏ31​∫0pF​​me2​c4+p2c2​p4c2​dp.
(23)
Writing x=pF​/(me​c) and performing the integral gives the equation of state of a cold electron gas
P=24π2ℏ3me4​c5​[x(2x2−3)1+x2​+3arsinhx],x=me​cℏ​(μe​mu​3π2ρ​)1/3.​
(24)
In the nonrelativistic limit pF​≪me​c, v(p)≃p/me​, so
P=KNR​ρ5/3,KNR​=5me​(μe​mu​)5/3ℏ2(3π2)2/3​.​
(25)
In the ultrarelativistic limit pF​≫me​c, v(p)≃c, so
P=KR​ρ4/3,KR​=4(μe​mu​)4/3ℏc(3π2)1/3​.​
(26)
These are polytropic equations of state with indices 3/2 and 3, respectively. The relativistic softening underlies the Chandrasekhar mass limit; Coulomb corrections, thermal effects, rotation, and general relativity are excluded from this idealized derivation.

The same polytrope of index one interior equation is ∇2ρ+(2πG/K)ρ=0. A positive separated solution with zero density on all six cube faces is the cubic polytropic interior
ρ(x,y,z)=ρc​sinLπx​sinLπy​sinLπz​.​
(39)
Its Laplacian is −3π2ρ/L2, so the required side length is
L=2G3πK​​.​
(40)
With Φ=C−2Kρ and P=Kρ2, it satisfies the interior hydrostatic equilibrium and Poisson equation for Newtonian gravity. Integrating each sine factor yields
M=ρc​(π2L​)3,ρc​ρˉ​​=π38​.​
(41)
This formal interior solution is not an isolated physical cubic star. The cubic polytrope fails isolated gravitational matching: at a vertex, the product of sines has ∇ρ=0, so the interior potential predicts zero gravitational acceleration. At the vertex (0,0,0), however, the field generated by its own positive mass is
g(0)=G∫[0,L]3​ρ(r′)∣r′∣3r′​d3r′,
(42)
and each component is strictly positive. There can be no continuous matching to the isolated external field without additional forces or mass sources. Thus solving the interior density equation and imposing zero face values is insufficient. Fluid stars also have no rigid structure to maintain sharp cubic faces, and observed stellar shapes are approximately spherical or rotationally flattened rather than cubic.
