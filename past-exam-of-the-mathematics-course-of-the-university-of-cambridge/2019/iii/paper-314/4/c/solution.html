A regular sonic point must make both sides of the flow equation vanish:
vs​=cs​,rs​=3cs2​GM⋆​​.
(40)
Substituting into the Bernoulli equation yields
cs2​(γ−11​−25​)=γ−1c02​​,cs2​=7−5γ2c02​​.
(41)
For γ>1, positive finite sound speed therefore requires the critical adiabatic index for dipolar accretion
1<γ<γcrit​=57​.​
(42)
To check that this gives real regular crossings, differentiate the flow equation at the sonic point and put x=rs​vs′​/cs​. The transonic accretion in a power-law tube calculation gives
(γ+1)x2+6(γ−1)x+9γ−12=0,x=γ+1−3(γ−1)±3(7−5γ)​​.
(43)
The minus sign gives the transonic branch whose Mach number increases inward. At γ=7/5 the positive-energy reservoir cannot match a finite sonic point; for larger γ the required cs2​ is negative. A physical surface-crossing solution also requires rs​>R⋆​ and validity of the narrow flux tube approximation up to the sonic region.
