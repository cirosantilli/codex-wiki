The decomposition matrix separates into two connected components. The first contains ϕ1​,ϕ2​ and χ1​,χ3A​,χ3B​,χ4​; it is the principal block B0​. The second contains only ϕ3​ and χ5​; since ϕ3​(1)=5 contains the full 5-part of ∣A5​∣=60, this is a defect-zero representation and its block B1​ has defect group 1.
For D=1, the normalizer is NG​(1)=G, so the Brauer correspondence is the identity and B1​ corresponds to itself.
The defect group of the principal block is a Sylow 5-subgroup P≅C5​. There are six Sylow 5-subgroups in A5​, so the orbit-stabilizer theorem gives
∣NA5​​(P)∣=660​=10.
(99)
The centralizer of a 5-cycle in A5​ is P, and an involution in the normalizer acts on P by inversion. Hence
N=NA5​​(P)≅C5​⋊C2​≅D10​.
(100)
In characteristic five the simple kN-modules are inflated from N/P≅C2​: their Brauer characters are ψ+​=(1,1) and ψ−​=(1,−1) on the identity and involution classes. If 1,ε,ρ1​,ρ2​ are the two one-dimensional and two two-dimensional ordinary characters of D10​, their reductions are
1↦ψ+​,ε↦ψ−​,ρ1​,ρ2​↦ψ+​+ψ−​.
(101)
The resulting decomposition matrix is connected, so these characters form the unique 5-block b0​ of N, with defect group P. By the Brauer first main theorem,
B0​⟷b0​ for D=C5​,B1​⟷B1​ for D=1.​
(102)
This is the complete 5-modular blocks of A5 correspondence.
