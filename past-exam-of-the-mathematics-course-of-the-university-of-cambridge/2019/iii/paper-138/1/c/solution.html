The radical of a module is the smallest submodule N⊆M for which M/N is a semisimple module. Consequently, if
M=M0​⊇M1​⊇M2​⊇⋯
(11)
has semisimple successive quotients, then J(Mi​)⊆Mi+1​. Induction gives
Ji(M)⊆Mi​,
(12)
so the radical series of a module descends at least as fast as every such series.
Dually, the socle is the largest semisimple submodule. If
0=N0​⊆N1​⊆N2​⊆⋯
(13)
has semisimple successive quotients, induction in M/Ni​ gives
Ni​⊆Soci(M),
(14)
so the socle series of a module ascends at least as fast as every such series.
Both series terminate because M has finite composition length. More precisely,
Ji(M)=J(A)iM,Soci(M)={x∈M:J(A)ix=0}.
(15)
Thus Jr(M)=0 exactly when J(A)r annihilates all of M, which is exactly when Socr(M)=M. The two least terminating indices therefore coincide:
m=n=the Loewy length of M.​
(16)
