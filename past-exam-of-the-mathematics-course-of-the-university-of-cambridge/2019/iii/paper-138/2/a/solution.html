Let g be a p-regular element. Its eigenvalues on M are roots of unity of order prime to p. If λ1​,…,λd​ are their Teichmuller lifts to characteristic-zero roots of unity, the Brauer character is
χM​(g)=r=1∑d​λr​.​
(24)
It depends only on the conjugacy class of g, is additive in short exact sequences, and equals the restriction of an ordinary character whenever the representation lifts.
For linear independence, choose a splitting p-modular system and let Pi​ be the projective cover of the simple module Si​. A projective lattice lifting Pi​ has an ordinary character Φi​ that vanishes on p-singular elements. Reduction and ordinary character orthogonality give
∣G∣1​∑g p-regular​Φi​(g−1)χSj​​(g)=dimHomkG​(Pi​,Sj​)=δij​,
(25)
because Si​ is the head of Pi​. Pairing a relation ∑j​cj​χSj​​=0 with every Φi​ yields ci​=0 for every i. Hence
{χSi​​} is linearly independent over C.​
(26)
This is the linear-independence part of the Brauer–Nesbitt theorem.
