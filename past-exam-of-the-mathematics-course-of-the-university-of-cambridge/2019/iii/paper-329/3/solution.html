Reflection in y=0 preserves the prescribed translational velocity and axial angular velocity. A force transforms as a polar vector, whereas a torque transforms as an axial vector; consequently Fy​=Gx​=Gz​=0. Reflection in x=0 reverses both imposed motions but preserves the normal force component. The linearity of Stokes flow then gives Fz​=0. Thus only Fx​ and Gy​ can be nonzero.
There is a sign inconsistency in the printed question. In right-handed coordinates, Ω=Ωey​ gives a rotational velocity −Ωaex​ at the sphere's lowest point. The printed shear and force formulas instead use the opposite rotational sense. Below, let ω be the right-handed component along +ey​; the paper's displayed formulas are recovered by setting ω=−Ω.
In the translating frame, the parabolic gap and leading boundary velocities are
h=aϵ+2ax2+y2​,v∥​(0)=−Uex​,v∥​(h)=−ωaex​.
(24)
The no-slip boundary condition and lubrication theory give
v∥​(z)=2μz(z−h)​∇∥​p−Uex​+(U−ωa)hz​ex​,q=−12μh3​∇∥​p−2h​(U+ωa)ex​.
(25)
The gap is stationary in this frame, so ∇∥​⋅q=0. The Reynolds lubrication equation is therefore
∇∥​⋅(h3∇∥​p)=−6μ(U+ωa)hx​.
(26)
Since hx​=x/a and ∇∥2​h=2/a,
∇∥​⋅[h3∇∥​(x/h2)]=−5x/a,p=56μ​(U+ωa)h2x​.​
(27)
Differentiating the velocity profile gives the leading shear stresses
μσxz​(0)​μσxz​(h)​μσyz​(0)​μσyz​(h)​​=5ah26(U+ωa)x2​+5h2U−8ωa​,=−5ah26(U+ωa)x2​+5h8U−2ωa​,=5ah26(U+ωa)xy​,=−5ah26(U+ωa)xy​.​
(28)
The first expression becomes the printed formula after ω=−Ω.
For the logarithmic lubrication resistance of a sphere near a wall, write ℓ=log(1/ϵ). The logarithmic region is aϵ​≪r≪a, where h∼r2/(2a). Consequently
∫hdxdy​=2πaℓ+O(a),∫ah2x2dxdy​=2πaℓ+O(a).
(29)
These follow from ∫r/hdr=aℓ+O(a) and ∫r3/h2dr=2a2ℓ+O(a2), with a fixed small outer cutoff. The general logarithmic radial numerator is r2n−1 over hn; the numerator in the printed integration hint appears to have a typographical error.
The force exerted on the fluid through the upper gap boundary includes pressure acting on its slope:
Fx​=∫[σxz​(h)+phx​]dxdy.
(30)
The pressure cancels the x2/h2 term, giving
Fx​=54πμa​(4U−ωa)ℓ+O(μa(∣U∣+a∣ω∣)).​
(31)
Pressure exerts no torque about the centre of a sphere because its traction is radial. To logarithmic order, the shear has lever arm −aez​, so
Gy​=−a∫σxz​(h)dxdy=54πμa2​(4ωa−U)ℓ+O(μa2(∣U∣+a∣ω∣)).
(32)
The other components vanish by parity: Gx​ involves an integral of the xy term, and Gz​ has an integrand odd in y. The combined hydrodynamic resistance matrix is
(Fx​Gy​/a​)∼54πμa​ℓ(4−1​−14​)(Uaω​).​
(33)
Its equal off-diagonal coefficients are required by the Lorentz reciprocal theorem for Stokes flow. It is a positive-definite matrix, as required by viscous dissipation.
In the rotational convention of the printed force formula, set Ω=−ω and measure the scalar couple along −ey​, so G=−Gy​. Then
F∼54πμa​(4U+aΩ)ℓ,G∼54πμa2​(U+4aΩ)ℓ.​
(34)
For sedimentation under the buoyancy-adjusted weight F, a uniform sphere is torque-free. Thus ωa=U/4, and the force balance gives
U∼3πμalog(1/ϵ)F​,ω∼12πμa2log(1/ϵ)F​.​
(35)
The rotational scalar in the paper's displayed-force convention is Ω=−U/(4a). The sphere rolls in the right-handed +ey​ sense but still slips: its lowest point has laboratory velocity U−aω=3U/4. The signs and coefficients of the right-handed resistance agree with the rigid-wall terms of Bertin et al., equations (4.4)–(4.5), after reversing the forces and torques there from fluid-on-sphere to sphere-on-fluid.
