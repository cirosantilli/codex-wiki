Take inward fluid entrainment speed to be positive. The non-Boussinesq form of the Batchelor entrainment hypothesis is
ve​=αwρ/ρ0​​,​
(23)
where α is the entrainment coefficient. Define physical, cross-section-integrated fluxes for the top-hat plume model by
Q=πb2ρw,M=πb2ρw2,F=πb2wg(ρ0​−ρ).​
(24)
Thus Q is mass flux, M is momentum flux, and F is a density-weighted buoyancy flux. The kinematic buoyancy flux in the Boussinesq approximation is F/ρ0​. These definitions keep the factor π; conventions suppressing it simply rescale all three fluxes consistently.
For a homogeneous ambient, the sectional volume, mass and momentum balances are
∂t​(πb2)+∂z​(πb2w)=2πbve​,
(25)
∂t​(πb2ρ)+∂z​Q=2πbρ0​ve​,∂t​Q+∂z​M=πb2g(ρ0​−ρ).
(26)
The momentum source is the net upward buoyancy force; entrained ambient fluid initially supplies no vertical momentum. Subtracting the mass balance from ρ0​ times the volume balance gives conservation of the density deficit:
∂t​[πb2g(ρ0​−ρ)]+∂z​F=0.
(27)
Now w=M/Q, πb2ρ=Q2/M, and πb2g(ρ0​−ρ)=QF/M. Substitution produces the non-Boussinesq top-hat plume equations
∂t​(MQ2​)+∂z​Q=2απρ0​M​,∂t​Q+∂z​M=MQF​,∂t​(MQF​)+∂z​F=0.​
(28)
For reconstructing the fields without a Boussinesq approximation,
ρ=gQ+Fρ0​gQ​,b2=πρMQ2​,ρg(ρ0​−ρ)​=QF​.
(29)
The last expression uses the plume density in the acceleration denominator; it agrees with the ambient-density definition of reduced gravity to Boussinesq order.
