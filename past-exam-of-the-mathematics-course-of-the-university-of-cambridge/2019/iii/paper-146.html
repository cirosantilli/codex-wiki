The symplectic neighborhood theorem says that a symplectomorphism between closed symplectic submanifolds which lifts to an isomorphism of their symplectic normal bundles extends to a symplectomorphism of neighborhoods.
Let CX​⊂X and CY​⊂Y be the given copies of C. Their self-intersection numbers are the Euler classes of their oriented normal bundles, so square zero makes both normal bundles trivial. The neighborhood theorem identifies neighborhoods with C×D2. Remove their interiors and identify the boundary circle bundles by a map covering the chosen identification CX​≅CY​ and reversing the normal-circle orientation. On collars, the two forms have the model
ωC​+d(r2dθ),
(8)
and the radial coordinate can be reversed while the circle coordinate is reversed so that the forms glue. A collar application of Moser's trick removes any discrepancy. This proves that the symplectic fiber sum along a square-zero surface X#C​Y has a natural symplectic form.
The displayed relation is an ordinary product relation, so
Γ=⟨a,b,c∣ba=ac⟩≅⟨a,c⟩=F2​,
(9)
where the relation eliminates b=aca−1. The Gompf realization theorem constructs a closed symplectic four-manifold MΓ​ with this fundamental group. Concretely, its construction starts from a product of a sufficiently high-genus surface and a torus, represents the two surviving generators and the relations by loops, and crosses the relevant loops with circle factors to obtain square-zero tori. Symplectic sums with copies of the rational elliptic surface kill the unwanted generators and impose the relations: the complement of a regular elliptic fiber is simply connected, so the Seifert-van Kampen theorem gives exactly π1​(MΓ​)=Γ≅F2​.
Finally, CP48 is simply connected and has real dimension 96. With the product symplectic form,
MΓ​×CP48​
(10)
is a closed symplectic manifold of real dimension 100 and has fundamental group Γ.

On the affine chart [1:z1​:⋯:zn​] of CPn, the normalized Fubini-Study form is
ωFS​=2i​∂∂ˉlog(1+∑j=1n​∣zj​∣2).
(11)
Its integral over a projective line is π. The Darboux theorem says that every point of a symplectic 2n-manifold has local coordinates in which the form is ∑j​dxj​∧dyj​.
To form the blowup Xλ​, choose a symplectic embedding of the closed standard ball B2n(λ) centered at p, remove its interior, and collapse each characteristic Hopf circle of its boundary S2n−1 to a point. The boundary becomes the exceptional divisor E≅CPn−1, and the reduced form extends the old form outside the ball with every projective line in E having area πλ2. This is the symplectic blowup of size λ. In real dimension four its volume is
Vol(Xλ​)=Vol(X)−2π2λ4​.
(12)
Thus, whenever two different sizes are allowed, different λ give different total symplectic volumes and hence nonsymplectomorphic blowups.
The punctured area-π sphere CP1∖{∞} is symplectomorphic to the open unit disc. Consequently
B4(r)⊂D2(1)×D2(1)↪CP1×CP1
(13)
for every r<1. The bound is sharp. Given a hypothetical larger ball, choose a compatible almost complex structure agreeing with the pushed-forward standard structure on the ball. A standard J-holomorphic-curve result supplies a sphere in one ruling class through the ball center. Its total area is π, while monotonicity inside the ball requires at least πr2. Hence r≤1. Equivalently, the Gromov width of the monotone product of projective lines is
cG​(CP1×CP1)=π.​
(14)

Fix p∈X. A linear change of coordinates first identifies ωp​ with the standard symplectic form ω0​. After shrinking to a star-shaped neighborhood, every
ωt​=(1−t)ω0​+tω
(15)
is nondegenerate. The Poincare lemma gives ω−ω0​=dσ, with σ(p)=0. Define Xt​ by ιXt​​ωt​=−σ and let ϕt​ be its local flow. Cartan's magic formula gives
dtd​ϕt∗​ωt​=ϕt∗​(dσ+dιXt​​ωt​)=0.
(16)
Thus ϕ1∗​ω=ω0​, proving the Darboux theorem.
For a smooth function H on a closed symplectic manifold, its Hamiltonian vector field is defined by ιXH​​ω=−dH. The same formula gives LXH​​ω=−d2H=0, so the Hamiltonian flow preserves the symplectic form. To move one point to another in a connected X, join them by a path, cover the path by finitely many Darboux charts, and in each chart use a cutoff linear Hamiltonian to perform a small translation. Composing these compactly supported Hamiltonian diffeomorphisms proves that symplectomorphisms act transitively on each connected component.
In R2, every embedded curve is a Lagrangian submanifold. Let L1​,L2​ be circles enclosing different Euclidean areas. A plane symplectomorphism preserves area and carries the bounded complementary component of one circle to that of its image, so no symplectomorphism maps L1​ to L2​.
The same phenomenon exists in every R2n. With
λ=21​∑j=1n​(xj​dyj​−yj​dxj​),
(17)
take the product tori
L1​=S1(1)n,L2​=S1(2​)n.
(18)
The Liouville class of a Lagrangian submanifold has respective period vectors π(1,…,1) and 2π(1,…,1) on these tori. Every symplectomorphism of R2n preserves the Liouville class up to the induced integral change of basis on H1​(Tn), because its pullback changes λ only by an exact form. An integral automorphism sends a primitive vector to a primitive vector and therefore cannot send the first period vector to the second. Hence
L1​ and L2​ are compact connected Lagrangians not related by any symplectomorphism.​
(19)
