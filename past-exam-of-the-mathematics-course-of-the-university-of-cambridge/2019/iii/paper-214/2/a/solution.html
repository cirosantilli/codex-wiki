Because p<pc​=p​c​, choose a finite set S∋0 with ϕp​(S)=ρ<1. Let L exceed the ℓ∞-distance from 0 to every endpoint of an edge in ∂S, and put
qn​=Pp​(0↔∂B(n)).
(16)
On the one-arm event to distance n>L, take the first oriented boundary edge (x,y)∈∂S used by an open self-avoiding path. The connection 0S​x, the open edge (x,y), and the remaining connection from y to ∂B(n) occur disjointly. The Van den Berg-Kesten inequality and translation invariance therefore give
qn​​≤p(x,y)∈∂S∑​Pp​(0S​x)Pp​(y↔∂B(n))≤ϕp​(S)qn−L​=ρqn−L​.​
(17)
Iteration yields qn​≤ρ⌊n/L⌋ up to an inessential finite-scale adjustment. Since p<1, each of the finitely many remaining qn​ is strictly below one, so reducing the exponent if necessary produces a constant c>0 valid for every n≥1:
Pp​(0↔∂B(n))≤e−cn.​
(18)
This is the finite-size proof of exponential decay of subcritical percolation.
