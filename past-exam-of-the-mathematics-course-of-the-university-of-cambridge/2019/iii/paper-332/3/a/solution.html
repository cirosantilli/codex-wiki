OurBigBook.com Words: 163
Use the common-density, equal-volume-heat-capacity model implicit in the stated speed law. Let the liquid fraction be ϕ ahead of the melting front and ϕ+Δϕ behind it. Melting adds exactly the liquid needed to fill the newly created pores, so total mass conservation makes Darcy flux continuous. Consequently,
uahead​=U,vahead​=U/ϕ,vbehind​=U/(ϕ+Δϕ).​
(31)
Here u denotes Darcy velocity and v the pore-liquid velocity in the stationary rock frame. Treating the pore-space increase as storage without its simultaneous melting source would incorrectly change the Darcy flux.
Relative to the cold unmelted material, the bulk enthalpy increase behind the front is ρcp​ΔT+ρLΔϕ: all phases gain sensible heat and the melted ice consumes latent heat. The advective heat-flux difference is ρcp​UΔT. Applying the Rankine-Hugoniot condition to this energy balance therefore gives
V(ρcp​ΔT+ρLΔϕ)=ρcp​UΔT,
(32)
so the advection-driven melting front in a porous matrix moves at
V=1+SΔϕU​,S=cp​ΔTL​.​
(33)
The advected latent contribution of the liquid is the same on both sides and cancels; the denominator measures sensible heating plus phase-change energy per unit bulk volume.
