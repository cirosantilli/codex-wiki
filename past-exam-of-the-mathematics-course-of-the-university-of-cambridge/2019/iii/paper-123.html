The prime ideal factorization of pB is
pB=∏q∣p​qeq/p​,
(1)
which defines the ramification index of a prime ideal eq/p​=vq​(pB). The residue-field degree is
fq/p​=[B/q:R/p].
(2)
Localizing at p makes Bp​ a free Rp​-module of rank [L:K], so B/pB has dimension [L:K] over the residue field R/p. The Chinese remainder theorem and the filtration by powers of each q decompose this vector space into eq/p​ successive quotients isomorphic to B/q, each of dimension fq/p​. Therefore the fundamental identity for prime decomposition gives
[L:K]=q∣p∑​eq/p​fq/p​.​
(3)

The completion of a number field at a prime ideal does not change either invariant. More precisely, Bq​ is the valuation ring of Lq​, its maximal ideal is qBq​, and
e(Lq​/Kp​)=eq/p​,Bq​/qBq​≅B/q.​
(4)
Thus the completed extension has the same ramification index and the same residue-field extension, hence the same residue-field degree fq/p​. This is completion preserves ramification and residue data.

Write G=Gal(L/K). The decomposition group and inertia group at q are
Dq/p​={σ∈G:σ(q)=q},Iq/p​=ker(Dq/p​⟶Gal(B/q/R/p)).
(5)
The transitivity of the Galois action on primes gives G/Dq/p​ as the set of primes above p. Completion induces the canonical isomorphism
Dq/p​≅Gal(Lq​/Kp​),
(6)
and reduction gives the exact sequence
1⟶Iq/p​⟶Dq/p​⟶Gal(B/q/R/p)⟶1.
(7)
Consequently ∣Iq/p​∣=eq/p​, ∣Dq/p​/Iq/p​∣=fq/p​ and, if g is the number of primes above p,
∣G∣=geq/p​fq/p​.​

Let
F(X)=X3+25X2−50X+40.
(9)
Over Q2​, the Newton polygon has three length-one segments of slopes −2,−1,0. The resulting roots can also be obtained directly from Hensel lemma. There is one unit root because F(1) is even and F′(1) is odd. For the two remaining roots, put
F(2Y)=4(2Y3+25Y2−25Y+10),
(10)
whose parenthesized polynomial has a simple root Y≡1(mod2), and
F(4Z)=8(8Z3+50Z2−25Z+5),
(11)
whose parenthesized polynomial has a simple root Z≡1(mod2). Thus F splits completely over Q2​, with roots of valuations 0,1,2. There are three primes q above 2, and for each one
Lq​=Q2​,πq​=2,kq​=F2​.​
(12)
Over Q5​, F is an Eisenstein polynomial. Hence there is one prime q above 5, and if α is the chosen root then
Lq​=Q5​(α),πq​=α,kq​=F5​,​
(13)
with e=3 and f=1.
The polynomial is irreducible over Q by the Eisenstein criterion at 5. Its polynomial discriminant is
Δ=−4⋅25⋅13807,
(14)
which is not a square number, so the Galois group of an irreducible cubic is
Gal(E/Q)≅S3​.​
(15)
At 2, all three roots already lie in Q2​, so the local splitting field is trivial and
D2​=I2​=1.​
(16)
At 5, the cubic is totally and tamely ramified. The square class of its discriminant is represented by −13807≡3(mod5), a nonsquare unit, so the quadratic resolvent field of a cubic is the unramified quadratic extension of Q5​. The local splitting field therefore has degree six, with
D5​≅S3​,I5​≅A3​≅C3​.​
(17)
These calculations are summarized by local factorization of X3 plus 25X2 minus 50X plus 40.

Krasner's lemma states that if K is complete, α is separable over K, and an algebraic element β satisfies
∣β−α∣<∣α′−α∣
(18)
for every other K-conjugate α′ of α, then K(α)⊆K(β).
Let f∈Cp​[X] be nonconstant and let α be a root in an algebraic closure of Cp​. Because the characteristic is zero, replace f by the separable minimal polynomial of α. Approximate its coefficients arbitrarily closely by elements of Q​p​. By continuity of roots over a non-Archimedean field, the approximating polynomial has a root β arbitrarily close to α. Since Q​p​ is algebraically closed, β∈Q​p​⊂Cp​.
Choose the approximation so that β is closer to α than every other Cp​-conjugate of α. Krasner's lemma gives
Cp​(α)⊆Cp​(β)=Cp​,
(19)
so α∈Cp​. Hence completion of an algebraic closure of a p-adic field is algebraically closed proves that Cp​ is algebraically closed.

vp​(n!)=∑j≥1​⌊pjn​⌋=p−1n−sp​(n)​,

i OurBigBook.com
A finite extension L/K of non-Archimedean local fields is an unramified extension when its ramification index is one and its residue-field degree equals [L:K]. Equivalently, its maximal ideal is generated by a uniformizer of K and its residue-field extension is separable.
A finite extension of number fields is an everywhere unramified extension of number fields when every nonzero prime ideal of K is unramified in L. Under the convention that includes infinite places, one also requires every real embedding of K to remain real.

The Hilbert class field K is the maximal everywhere unramified abelian extension of the number field K, with complete splitting at real places if infinite places are included. Global class field theory gives the canonical Artin reciprocity map
Cl(K)≅Gal(K/K),​
(23)
so [K:K] is the class number of K.

Put K=Q(−6​). Its fundamental discriminant is −24. The Minkowski bound for ideal classes is
π2​24​<4,
(24)
so every ideal class contains an integral ideal of norm 1, 2 or 3. The primes above 2 and 3 are ramified, their classes have order at most two, and
(−6​)=p2​p3​
(25)
shows that they represent the same class. This class is nontrivial because the norm form a2+6b2 does not represent 2. Hence
Cl(K)≅C2​.
(26)
Now let H=K(2​)=Q(2​,−3​). The discriminant of a biquadratic field is the product of the discriminants of its three quadratic subfields, so
dH​=8⋅(−3)⋅(−24)=576=dK2​.
(27)
The relative discriminant DH/K​ therefore has norm one, proving that H/K is unramified at every finite prime. Since [H:K]=2=∣Cl(K)∣, the Hilbert class field of Q of square root minus six is
K=Q(2​,−3​).​
(28)

The discrete valuation gives a split exact sequence
1⟶O×⟶K×vK​​Z⟶0,
(29)
split by n↦πn. Reduction gives a second exact sequence
1⟶1+m⟶O×⟶Fq×​⟶1.
(30)
The Teichmuller lift identifies the cyclic group Fq×​ with the group μq−1​ of prime-to-p roots of unity in K, where p is the residue characteristic. Consequently the multiplicative group of a non-Archimedean local field decomposes as
K×≅Z×μq−1​×(1+m).​
(31)
If d=[K:Qp​], the p-adic logarithm identifies a sufficiently deep higher principal-unit group with the additive group of a rank-d Zp​-lattice. The remaining kernel is precisely the finite group μpa​(K) of p-power roots of unity in K. Thus the principal-unit group of a mixed-characteristic local field has the topological group structure
1+m≅μpa​(K)×Zpd​.​
(32)

The main theorem of local class field theory consists of Local Artin reciprocity and its existence theorem. There is a canonical continuous reciprocity map
ArtK​:K×⟶Gal(Kab/K)
(33)
with dense image, normalized so that a uniformizer maps to a chosen Frobenius. For every finite abelian extension L/K, it induces an isomorphism
K×/NL/K​(L×)≅Gal(L/K).​
(34)
Moreover, the existence theorem of local class field theory says that L↦NL/K​(L×) is an inclusion-reversing bijection between finite abelian extensions of K in Kab and finite-index open subgroups of K×.

Let Ln​=Q2​(ξ2n​). The extension is a cyclotomic extension of a p-adic field and is totally ramified. Under Local Artin reciprocity, the action on ξ2n​ is the reduction of a 2-adic unit modulo 2n, up to the harmless inverse determined by the Frobenius convention. A uniformizer acts trivially because Ln​/Q2​ has no unramified part. The kernel is therefore
2Zker(Z2×​⟶(Z/2nZ)×)=2Z(1+2nZ2​).
(35)
By the norm subgroup of a local field extension, this kernel is exactly the required norm group. Hence, for every n>0,
NQ2​(ξ2n​)/Q2​​(Q2​(ξ2n​)×)=2Z(1+2nZ2​).​
(36)
For n=1, the factor 1+2Z2​ is all of Z2×​, consistently with Q2​(ξ2​)=Q2​.
