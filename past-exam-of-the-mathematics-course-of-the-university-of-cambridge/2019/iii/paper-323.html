A bipartite density operator ρAB​ has positive partial transpose when ρABTB​​≥0 in a product basis. Positivity is independent of the choice of local bases, although the matrix representing the partial transpose changes.
If the state is a separable quantum state, write ρAB​=∑j​pj​ρjA​⊗ρjB​. Taking its partial transpose gives
ρABTB​​=∑j​pj​ρjA​⊗(ρjB​)T≥0.
(1)
Indeed, the matrix transpose preserves the nonnegative eigenvalues of each local density operator; a tensor product of positive semidefinite operators is positive, and so is their nonnegative sum. This proves the necessary direction of the positive partial transpose criterion.

Use two precise finite-dimensional results. The positive-map separability criterion says that a state on C2⊗C3 is separable exactly when (id2​⊗Φ)(σ)≥0 for every positive linear map Φ:M3​(C)→M2​(C). The Størmer-Woronowicz decomposability theorem says that every such map is a decomposable positive map, so
Φ=Φ1​+Φ2​∘T,
(2)
with Φ1​,Φ2​ completely positive maps and T the matrix transpose on M3​.
If σ has positive partial transpose, both σ and σTB​ are positive. Hence
(id2​⊗Φ)(σ)=(id2​⊗Φ1​)(σ)+(id2​⊗Φ2​)(σTB​)≥0.
(3)
The positive-map separability criterion now proves that σ is a separable quantum state. The dimension-specific decomposability theorem is essential: the conclusion does not extend to arbitrary bipartite dimensions.

The partial transpose of the spin singlet state projector is the Hermitian operator
W=21​(∣01⟩⟨01∣+∣10⟩⟨10∣−∣00⟩⟨11∣−∣11⟩⟨00∣)=2I​−∣Φ+⟩⟨Φ+∣.
(4)
For every separable quantum state τ, the positive partial transpose criterion and the trace-adjoint identity for partial transpose give
Tr(Wτ)=Tr(Ψ−​τTB​)=⟨Ψ−∣τTB​∣Ψ−⟩≥0.
(5)
For the specified Bell state, however,
Tr(W∣Φ+⟩⟨Φ+∣)=−21​​.
(6)
Thus W is an entanglement witness detecting ∣Φ+⟩. In fact, it detects any state whose overlap with that Bell state exceeds 1/2.

Use the normalized Choi state CΛ​=(Λ⊗id)(∣Φd​⟩⟨Φd​∣), with ∣Φd​⟩=d−1/2∑i​∣ii⟩. If Λ is an entanglement-breaking channel, its action on this particular bipartite input makes CΛ​ a separable quantum state.
Conversely, suppose CΛ​=∑a​pa​σa​⊗τa​, with local density operators σa​,τa​. The Choi reconstruction formula gives
Λ(X)=dTrB​[CΛ​(I⊗XT)]=∑a​Tr(Ea​X)σa​,Ea​=dpa​τaT​≥0.
(7)
Because Λ is trace preserving, TrA​CΛ​=I/d, so ∑a​Ea​=I. Thus the Ea​ form a POVM and the channel is a measure-and-prepare channel.
For any bipartite input ρAR​, define the positive, possibly unnormalized reference operators
Ra​=TrA​[(Ea​​⊗I)ρAR​(Ea​​⊗I)].
(8)
Its output is ∑a​σa​⊗Ra​. Since ∑a​TrRa​=1, normalizing each nonzero Ra​ expresses this as a convex combination of product states. Hence every output is separable, proving the separable Choi-state criterion for entanglement breaking.

Apply the measure-and-prepare channel locally to an arbitrary bipartite density operator ρAR​. The resulting state is
(Λ⊗idR​)(ρAR​)=∑a​σa​⊗Ra​,
(9)
where
Ra​=TrA​[(Ea​​⊗I)ρAR​(Ea​​⊗I)]≥0.
(10)
To see that this is the correct output, the partial trace is cyclic for operators acting only on the traced subsystem, so the displayed expression equals TrA​[(Ea​⊗I)ρAR​]. Put qa​=TrRa​. The POVM completeness relation implies ∑a​qa​=1, and terms with qa​=0 vanish. Therefore
(Λ⊗idR​)(ρAR​)=∑a:qa​>0​qa​σa​⊗qa​Ra​​
(11)
is a separable quantum state. Thus the channel is an entanglement-breaking channel, for every reference-system dimension.

Expanding the normalized maximally entangled state in the definition of the Choi state gives
CΛ​=d1​∑k,ℓ​Λ(∣k⟩⟨ℓ∣)⊗∣k⟩⟨ℓ∣=j∑​pj​∣aj​⟩⟨aj​∣⊗∣bj​​⟩⟨bj​​∣​.
(12)
Here the bar denotes componentwise complex conjugation in the basis defining the Choi state. Every term is a positive tensor-product operator, and the whole operator has trace one because Λ is assumed to be a quantum channel. It is therefore a separable quantum state, proving entanglement breaking by the separable Choi-state criterion for entanglement breaking.
If the vectors are unit vectors, the displayed pj​ are already the product-state weights. If they are not normalized, absorb their squared norms into the weights and normalize the nonzero vectors. Trace preservation requires
d∑j​pj​∥aj​∥2∣bj​⟩⟨bj​∣=I;
(13)
the probability-distribution condition alone would not guarantee this. Equivalently, the Kraus operators are Kj​=dpj​​∣aj​⟩⟨bj​∣, exhibiting the rank-one Kraus representation of an entanglement-breaking channel.

Use base-two logarithms. The weakly typical sequence definition is
​−n1​log2​p(xn)−H(X)​≤ε​,p(xn)=∏j=1n​p(xj​).
(14)
Equivalently, 2−n(H(X)+ε)≤p(xn)≤2−n(H(X)−ε). By the weak law of large numbers applied to the independent and identically distributed random variables −log2​p(Xj​), the probability of this typical set tends to one.
This captures the usual probability scale of a long sample, but it need not make every symbol frequency representative. For a fair binary source, every word has probability 2−n and H(X)=1, so even the all-zero word is weakly typical. The strongly typical sequence definition additionally controls empirical symbol frequencies and excludes this word for small tolerance. Thus weak typicality agrees with the high-probability-set intuition, while allowing individually unrepresentative members.

Every member of the typical set has probability at least 2−n(H(X)+ε). Summing these probabilities gives
1≥∑xn∈Tε(n)​​p(xn)≥∣Tε(n)​∣2−n(H(X)+ε),
(15)
so
∣Tε(n)​∣≤2n(H(X)+ε)​.
(16)
The upper bound itself holds at every block length. For sufficiently large n, the high-probability property also gives the companion lower bound
1−δ≤P(Tε(n)​)≤∣Tε(n)​∣2−n(H(X)−ε),
(17)
hence ∣Tε(n)​∣≥(1−δ)2n(H(X)−ε). Together these are the typical-set cardinality bounds underlying Shannon source coding theorem.

Memorylessness means that the ensemble's average density operator is ρ(n)=π⊗n. Its spectral decomposition is
ρ(n)=∑in​qi1​​⋯qin​​∣ϕin​⟩⟨ϕin​∣,∣ϕin​⟩=∣ϕi1​​⟩⊗⋯⊗∣ϕin​​⟩.
(18)
The product eigenvalues give
S(ρ(n))=−∑in​(∏j​qij​​)log2​(∏j​qij​​)=nH(q)=nS(π).
(19)
Zero eigenvalues contribute zero to the Von Neumann entropy and are excluded from logarithmic typicality tests.
The quantum typical subspace is the span of eigenvectors whose eigenvalues satisfy
​−n1​log2​(qi1​​⋯qin​​)−S(π)​≤ε.
(20)
Its orthogonal projection Pε(n)​ selects precisely the classical typical set for the eigenvalue distribution q. The typical-set cardinality bounds and the weak law of large numbers therefore give
dimTε(n)​≤2n(S(π)+ε)​,Tr(ρ(n)Pε(n)​)≥1−δ​
(21)
for any fixed δ>0 and all sufficiently large n.

A compression-decompression scheme consists of quantum channels En​:D(H⊗n)→D(Kn​) and Dn​:D(Kn​)→D(H⊗n), where limsupn​n−1log2​dimKn​≤R. For the emitted pure-state ensemble, reliability means that the mean squared quantum fidelity tends to one:
k∑​pk​⟨Ψk(n)​∣(Dn​∘En​)(∣Ψk(n)​⟩⟨Ψk(n)​∣)∣Ψk(n)​⟩⟶1​.
(22)
The average is taken over the actual source distribution, rather than the worst possible input vector.
A standard stronger formulation of reliable quantum source compression requires
Fe​(π⊗n,Dn​∘En​)⟶1,
(23)
where entanglement fidelity tests preservation of a purification and its reference-system correlations. It implies the mean-fidelity condition for every pure-state ensemble of π⊗n. Schumacher compression achieves this at any rate R>S(π): choose 0<ε<R−S(π), encode the quantum typical subspace, and map atypical outcomes to a fallback state. The composed channel has a Kraus term Pε(n)​, so its entanglement fidelity is at least [Tr(π⊗nPε(n)​)]2→1.

Let Z indicate whether the outcome differs from x1​. Then P(Z=1)=q. The chain rule for information entropy gives
H(X)=H(Z)+qH(X∣Z=1),
(24)
since X=x1​ is determined when Z=0. The remaining conditional distribution has at most m−1 outcomes, so the maximum entropy on a finite alphabet is log2​(m−1). Therefore the Shannon source coding theorem limit obeys
H(X)≤h2​(q)+qlog2​(m−1)​.
(25)
Here h2​ is the binary entropy function; equality holds when the rare outcomes are equiprobable. A convenient explicit small-q bound is
H(X)≤qlog2​qe(m−1)​​,
(26)
because −(1−q)log2​(1−q)≤qlog2​e. For a fixed alphabet, this upper bound tends to zero as q→0.

Assume no previously shared entanglement. Alice's encoded ensemble {px​,ρx​} lives in a Hilbert space of dimension 2n. Its Holevo quantity satisfies
χin​=S(∑x​px​ρx​)−∑x​px​S(ρx​)≤log2​(2n)=n,
(27)
using nonnegativity of Von Neumann entropy and the maximum entropy of a quantum state. The data-processing inequality for quantum relative entropy implies χout​≤χin​ for the transmission quantum channel. Finally, the Holevo bound applied to Bob's measurement in quantum mechanics gives
I(X:Y)≤χout​≤n bits​.
(28)
This argument also works when the output system has larger dimension than the input. Prior shared entanglement changes the communication resource: superdense coding can transmit two classical bits per sent qubit, so the absence of that resource is part of this bound's setting.

Assume a finite-dimensional memoryless quantum channel, no shared entanglement, and a code of Mn​ messages with n−1log2​Mn​→R. Use a uniform message M to relate average and maximum error. Each permitted input is a product state ρm(n)​=⨂i=1n​ρm,i​, giving output σm(n)​=⨂i​Λ(ρm,i​). The factors may depend on both the message and the channel use.
Let χ∗(Λ) denote the one-use Holevo capacity in the question. The Subadditivity of Von Neumann entropy for the average output, and entropy additivity for each product output, imply
χn​​=S(Mn​1​m∑​σm(n)​)−Mn​1​m∑​i∑​S(Λ(ρm,i​))≤i∑​[S(Mn​1​m∑​Λ(ρm,i​))−Mn​1​m∑​S(Λ(ρm,i​))]≤nχ∗(Λ).​
(29)
This is the key step of the product-input classical-capacity converse; it does not assume that the average output itself is a product.
Bob may measure all outputs jointly. The Holevo bound still gives I(M:M)≤χn​. Let Pe​ be his average message-error probability. Fano's inequality gives
log2​Mn​=I(M:M)+H(M∣M)≤nχ∗(Λ)+1+Pe​log2​Mn​.
(30)
Since the maximum error Pmax​ is at least Pe​,
n→∞liminf​Pmax​≥1−Rχ∗(Λ)​>0​
(31)
when R>χ∗(Λ). Thus the maximum error cannot vanish asymptotically.

Let Δ=ρ−σ=Δ+​−Δ−​ be its positive-negative decomposition. Since TrΔ=0, the positive and negative parts have equal trace, namely D(ρ,σ). For every positive contraction M,
Tr(MΔ)=Tr(MΔ+​)−Tr(MΔ−​)≤TrΔ+​=D(ρ,σ).
(34)
Here both products have nonnegative trace, and M≤I bounds the first term. Equality is attained by the orthogonal projection P+​ onto the positive spectral subspace of Δ, since P+​Δ+​=Δ+​ and P+​Δ−​=0. Thus the variational characterization of trace distance is
D(ρ,σ)=0≤M≤Imax​Tr[M(ρ−σ)]​.
(35)

Let P+​ be the maximizing projection in the variational characterization of trace distance. Use the binary POVM {P+​,I−P+​} and its measurement channel. The difference between its two output probability vectors is
(p1​−q1​,p2​−q2​)=(Tr[P+​(ρ−σ)],Tr[(I−P+​)(ρ−σ)])=(D,−D),

Choose the trace-distance-preserving binary measurement from the previous part. Let p,q be its output probability distributions, so ∥p−q∥1​=2D(ρ,σ). The data-processing inequality for quantum relative entropy gives
D(ρ∥σ)≥D(Φ(ρ)∥Φ(σ))=D(p∥q).
(41)
For diagonal density operators, the quantum expression is the classical Kullback-Leibler divergence. Applying the supplied Pinsker's inequality gives
D(p∥q)≥2ln21​∥p−q∥12​=ln22​D(ρ,σ)2.

Consequently the quantum Pinsker inequality is
D(ρ∥σ)≥ln22​D(ρ,σ)2​.
(43)
The ln2 factor converts natural-logarithm relative entropy to bits. If the relative entropy is infinite because its support condition fails, the inequality holds automatically.

First prove monotonicity for a partial trace. Let r=dimHB​ and define the Heisenberg-Weyl twirling channel
TB​(X)=r21​∑k,m​(IA​⊗Wk,m​)X(IA​⊗Wk,m​)†=(TrB​X)⊗rIB​​.
(44)
The supplied identity on individual operators extends to bipartite operators by expanding them in a tensor-product basis. The joint convexity of quantum relative entropy and its unitary invariance imply
D(ρA​⊗rIB​​​σA​⊗rIB​​)≤r21​∑k,m​D(Uk,m​ρAB​Uk,m†​∥Uk,m​σAB​Uk,m†​)=D(ρAB​∥σAB​).
(45)
The additivity of quantum relative entropy makes the left side D(ρA​∥σA​), proving the partial-trace case.
For a general deterministic quantum operation, meaning a quantum channel, use a Stinespring dilation Λ(X)=TrE​(VXV†) with V†V=I. An isometric embedding preserves quantum relative entropy, because restricting the output to the common image of V preserves the eigenvalues and trace formula. Applying the partial-trace result gives
D(Λ(ρ)∥Λ(σ))≤D(VρV†∥VσV†)=D(ρ∥σ)​.
(46)
The other properties used are unitary invariance and invariance under adjoining an identical ancillary state; both follow directly from the relative-entropy trace formula. This is the Lindblad-Uhlmann monotonicity theorem. The proof applies to deterministic channels; normalized postselection is not such a linear channel.

Write a qubit density operator in Bloch vector form, ρ=(I+r⋅σ)/2, with ∣r∣≤1. The quantum depolarizing channel maps r to pr, so its output eigenvalues are (1±p∣r∣)/2. The output Von Neumann entropy is minimized on pure inputs, where it equals h2​((1+p)/2).
For any input ensemble, its output Holevo quantity is at most the maximum qubit entropy minus this minimum output entropy:
χ≤1−h2​(21+p​).
(47)
Equality is attained by two equiprobable orthogonal pure inputs: their average output is I/2, while each output has the minimum entropy. Thus
χ∗(Λdep​)=1−h2​(21+p​)​.
(48)
This uses the question's retention parameter p; in the usual convex-mixture range, 0≤p≤1. The expression also holds throughout the full completely positive qubit range −1/3≤p≤1, since h2​((1+p)/2)=h2​((1+∣p∣)/2).
The supplied additivity of Holevo capacity gives χ∗(Λdep⊗n​)=nχ∗(Λdep​). The Holevo-Schumacher-Westmoreland theorem expresses the unassisted classical capacity of a quantum channel as the regularized Holevo capacity, so
C(Λdep​)=χ∗(Λdep​)​.
(49)
Therefore entangled inputs across channel uses cannot increase this classical capacity.

Let ω=∑j​pj​ωj​. When the relative entropies are finite, expanding their trace definitions gives
j∑​pj​D(ωj​∥ρ)−j∑​pj​D(ωj​∥ω)​=j∑​pj​Tr[ωj​(log2​ω−log2​ρ)]=Tr[ω(log2​ω−log2​ρ)]=D(ω∥ρ).​
(50)
Thus Donald's identity is
j∑​pj​D(ωj​∥ρ)=j∑​pj​D(ωj​∥ω)+D(ω∥ρ)​.
(51)
For every pj​>0, the support of a positive operator ωj​ lies in that of ω, so the first sum on the right is finite in finite dimension. If the support of ω is not contained in the support of ρ, at least one positive-weight ωj​ also violates the support condition. Both sides of the identity are then +∞. This extends the conclusion without subtracting infinite quantities.

By Donald's identity, the objective equals
∑j​pj​D(ωj​∥ρ)=∑j​pj​D(ωj​∥ω)+D(ω∥ρ).
(52)
The nonnegativity of quantum relative entropy makes the last term at least zero, with equality exactly when ρ=ω. Hence the unique minimizing density operator is the ensemble average and
minρ​∑j​pj​D(ωj​∥ρ)=∑j​pj​D(ωj​∥ω)=S(ω)−∑j​pj​S(ωj​).

For the classical-quantum state σXB​, its marginal on X has entropy H(p), its marginal on B is ω, and its joint entropy is H(p)+∑j​pj​S(ωj​). Substituting in the definition of quantum mutual information gives
ρmin​j∑​pj​D(ωj​∥ρ)=χ({pj​,ωj​})=I(X:B)σ​,ρmin​=ω​.
(54)
This is the relative-entropy barycenter of a quantum ensemble: the average state minimizes mean distinguishability from the ensemble. Its minimal value is exactly the Holevo quantity.
