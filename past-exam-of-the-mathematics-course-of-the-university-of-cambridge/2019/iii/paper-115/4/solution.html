The Levi-Civita connection of a Riemannian manifold (M,g) is the unique connection on a vector bundle on TM that is torsion-free and compatible with the Riemannian metric. Any such connection must satisfy the Koszul formula
2g(∇X​Y,Z)=​Xg(Y,Z)+Yg(Z,X)−Zg(X,Y)−g(X,[Y,Z])+g(Y,[Z,X])+g(Z,[X,Y]).​
(20)
Nondegeneracy of g determines ∇X​Y uniquely from the right-hand side. Conversely, define ∇ by this formula. Direct substitution shows that it is C∞-linear in X, satisfies the Leibniz rule in Y, preserves g, and obeys ∇X​Y−∇Y​X=[X,Y]. It is therefore a torsion-free metric connection. This proves the Existence and uniqueness of the Levi-Civita connection.
For the metric vector bundle E, choose the stated orthonormal local frame (e1​,…,em​) and write dA​ej​=Aij​ei​. Since ⟨ei​,ej​⟩=δij​, metric compatibility gives
0=d⟨ei​,ej​⟩=⟨dA​ei​,ej​⟩+⟨ei​,dA​ej​⟩=Aji​+Aij​.
(21)
Hence the connection matrix in an orthonormal frame is skew-symmetric:
Aij​=−Aji​.​
(22)
On an oriented Riemannian d-manifold, the Hodge star operator is defined by
α∧∗β=⟨α,β⟩dvolg​.
(23)
It is an orthogonal map ∗:ΛrTx∗​M→Λd−rTx∗​M and satisfies ∗2=(−1)r(d−r). In dimension d=2n on middle-degree forms, the Adjoint of the Hodge star on middle-degree forms is
∗†=∗−1=(−1)n2∗.
(24)
Thus it is self-adjoint when n is even, but it is not always self-adjoint. For n=1 on the oriented Euclidean plane,
∗dx=dy,∗dy=−dx,
(25)
so its matrix in the orthonormal basis (dx,dy) is skew-adjoint.
The Laplace-Beltrami operator on differential forms is the Hodge Laplacian
Δ=dδ+δd,
(26)
where the codifferential δ is the formal L2 adjoint of the exterior derivative. On a compact manifold without boundary, if Δω=λω for a nonzero differential form ω, then integration by parts gives
λ∥ω∥L22​=⟨Δω,ω⟩L2​=∥dω∥L22​+∥δω∥L22​≥0.
(27)
Therefore the nonnegativity of the Hodge Laplacian yields
λ≥0.​
(28)
