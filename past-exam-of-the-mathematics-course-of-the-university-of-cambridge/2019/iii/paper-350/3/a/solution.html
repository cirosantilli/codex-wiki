The Cameron-Martin space of a Gaussian measure μ=N(0,Σ) is
E=RanΣ1/2,⟨h,g⟩E​=⟨Σ−1/2h,Σ−1/2g⟩H​.​
(32)
The inverse is defined on the range of Σ1/2. In this infinite-dimensional setting “positive definite” must mean ⟨Σh,h⟩>0 for every nonzero h, rather than a uniform lower bound: a trace-class operator cannot be uniformly positive on an infinite-dimensional Hilbert space.
By the spectral theorem for compact Hermitian operators, choose an orthonormal basis (ej​) with Σej​=λj​ej​, λj​>0, and ∑j​λj​<∞. (A strictly positive trace-class operator also forces the ambient Hilbert space to be separable.) In coordinates hj​=⟨h,ej​⟩,
E={h∈H:∑j​λj​hj2​​<∞},∥h∥E2​=∑j​λj​hj2​​.
(33)
This is a Hilbert space with its indicated norm, even though its range need not be closed in the ambient norm.
The Cameron-Martin theorem for a Gaussian measure states that the translated law μh​(B)=μ(B−h)=N(h,Σ)(B) is an equivalent probability measure to μ exactly when h∈E. For such h, its Radon-Nikodym derivative is
dμdμh​​(x)=exp(ℓh​(x)−21​∥h∥E2​),ℓh​(x)=N→∞lim​j=1∑N​λj​hj​xj​​.​
(34)
The series has mean-square convergence and converges almost surely under μ, since its independent summands have total variance ∑j​hj2​/λj​. Its law is N(0,∥h∥E2​), so the density has expected value one. It is sometimes formally written ℓh​(x)=⟨h,x⟩E​, but a typical infinite-dimensional Gaussian sample is not in E; the series interpretation is essential. If h∈/E, the two laws are mutually singular measures.
