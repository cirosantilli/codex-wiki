Use the shape-rate convention for the Gamma distribution. Define
B=ATA,u∗​=B−1ATm,r=m−Au∗​,a=α+2k​,b(u)=β+21​∥m−Au∥2.
(23)
The zero null space assumption makes B a positive-definite matrix, and u∗​ is the unique ordinary least squares solution. The Gaussian likelihood contributes γk/2, which must be retained because γ is unknown. Multiplying it by the improper prior and the Gamma distribution gives the normal-gamma posterior with a flat prior:
πm(u,γ)=Z1​γa−1e−γb(u),u∈Rd,γ>0.​
(24)
Here the constant density of the improper prior cancels on normalization. Put
b0​=β+21​∥r∥2>0,s=a−2d​=α+2k−d​>0.
(25)
The normal equation ATr=0 makes the residual orthogonal to the range of A. The orthogonal decomposition by a closed subspace into that range and its orthogonal complement gives
b(u)=b0​+21​(u−u∗​)TB(u−u∗​).
(26)
Integration over u by the Gaussian integral, followed by integration over γ using the Gamma distribution, yields
Z=detB​(2π)d/2​b0s​Γ(s)​<∞.
(27)
Thus the posterior distribution is proper, despite the improper prior; here k≥d follows from the zero null space assumption.
Completing the square in u and collecting the powers of γ give the two conditional distributions:
u∣γ,m∼N(u∗​,γ−1B−1),γ∣u,m∼Gamma(a,b(u)).​
(28)
Explicitly their probability density functions are
π(u∣γ,m)=(2π)d/2γd/2detB​​e−2γ​(u−u∗​)TB(u−u∗​),π(γ∣u,m)=Γ(a)b(u)a​γa−1e−b(u)γ.
(29)
For a joint maximum a posteriori estimate relative to dudγ, first fix γ>0. The unique maximizing u is u∗​. The remaining log density is (a−1)logγ−b0​γ plus a constant. Consequently, if a>1,
uMAP​=u∗​,γ​MAP​=β+∥r∥2/2α+k/2−1​.​
(30)
The stated assumptions do not always imply a>1. If a=1, the supremum occurs as γ↓0 but is not attained on γ>0; if a<1, the density is unbounded there. In either case there is no joint maximum a posteriori estimate in the stated parameter space. Calling zero a boundary mode does not make it an admissible positive precision.
If “MAP estimators” instead means separate modes of the marginal distributions, integration gives
π(u∣m)∝[b0​+21​(u−u∗​)TB(u−u∗​)]−a,γ∣m∼Gamma(s,b0​).
(31)
Thus the marginal maximum a posteriori estimate of u is always u∗​, and that of γ is (s−1)/b0​ when s>1, with the same nonattainment at zero when s≤1. Joint and marginal maximization are different operations; the usual joint interpretation gives the preceding boxed pair.
