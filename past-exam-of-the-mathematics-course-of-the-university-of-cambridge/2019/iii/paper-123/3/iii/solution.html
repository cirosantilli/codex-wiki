Put K=Q(−6​). Its fundamental discriminant is −24. The Minkowski bound for ideal classes is
π2​24​<4,
(24)
so every ideal class contains an integral ideal of norm 1, 2 or 3. The primes above 2 and 3 are ramified, their classes have order at most two, and
(−6​)=p2​p3​
(25)
shows that they represent the same class. This class is nontrivial because the norm form a2+6b2 does not represent 2. Hence
Cl(K)≅C2​.
(26)
Now let H=K(2​)=Q(2​,−3​). The discriminant of a biquadratic field is the product of the discriminants of its three quadratic subfields, so
dH​=8⋅(−3)⋅(−24)=576=dK2​.
(27)
The relative discriminant DH/K​ therefore has norm one, proving that H/K is unramified at every finite prime. Since [H:K]=2=∣Cl(K)∣, the Hilbert class field of Q of square root minus six is
K=Q(2​,−3​).​
