Let
F(X)=X3+25X2−50X+40.
(9)
Over Q2​, the Newton polygon has three length-one segments of slopes −2,−1,0. The resulting roots can also be obtained directly from Hensel lemma. There is one unit root because F(1) is even and F′(1) is odd. For the two remaining roots, put
F(2Y)=4(2Y3+25Y2−25Y+10),
(10)
whose parenthesized polynomial has a simple root Y≡1(mod2), and
F(4Z)=8(8Z3+50Z2−25Z+5),
(11)
whose parenthesized polynomial has a simple root Z≡1(mod2). Thus F splits completely over Q2​, with roots of valuations 0,1,2. There are three primes q above 2, and for each one
Lq​=Q2​,πq​=2,kq​=F2​.​
(12)
Over Q5​, F is an Eisenstein polynomial. Hence there is one prime q above 5, and if α is the chosen root then
Lq​=Q5​(α),πq​=α,kq​=F5​,​
(13)
with e=3 and f=1.
The polynomial is irreducible over Q by the Eisenstein criterion at 5. Its polynomial discriminant is
Δ=−4⋅25⋅13807,
(14)
which is not a square number, so the Galois group of an irreducible cubic is
Gal(E/Q)≅S3​.​
(15)
At 2, all three roots already lie in Q2​, so the local splitting field is trivial and
D2​=I2​=1.​
(16)
At 5, the cubic is totally and tamely ramified. The square class of its discriminant is represented by −13807≡3(mod5), a nonsquare unit, so the quadratic resolvent field of a cubic is the unramified quadratic extension of Q5​. The local splitting field therefore has degree six, with
D5​≅S3​,I5​≅A3​≅C3​.​
(17)
These calculations are summarized by local factorization of X3 plus 25X2 minus 50X plus 40.
