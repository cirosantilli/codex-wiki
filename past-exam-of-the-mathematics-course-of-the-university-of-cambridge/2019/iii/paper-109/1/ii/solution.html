Let A⊆[n](r) be an intersecting family, with n≥2r. By the Iterated local LYM inequality, its upper shadow in level n−r satisfies
∣∇,n−2rA∣≥(rn​)(n−rn​)​∣A∣=∣A∣.

The family of complements Ac={[n]∖A:A∈A} also lies in level n−r and has cardinality ∣A∣. It is disjoint from the upper shadow: if A⊆[n]∖B for A,B∈A, then A∩B=∅, contradicting intersection. Both families fit inside the (n−r)th level, so
2∣A∣≤(n−rn​)=(rn​).
(6)
Thus replacing the Kruskal-Katona theorem by Local LYM gives only
∣A∣≤21​(rn​).​
(7)
This agrees with the Erdős-Ko-Rado theorem when n=2r but is weaker when n>2r.
