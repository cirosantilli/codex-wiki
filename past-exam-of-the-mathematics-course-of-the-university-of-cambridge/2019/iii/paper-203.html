Let D be simply connected domain with distinct marked boundary points a,b, and choose a conformal map f:D→H with f(a)=0 and f(b)=∞. Chordal SLEκ​ from a to b is the unparameterized curve f−1(γ), where γ is chordal SLE in (H,0,∞).
Any other such map is f​=rf for some r>0. The Scaling invariance of SLE says that r−1γ has the same unparameterized law as γ; only its capacity clock changes. Hence the pullback law is independent of f. This proves the Conformal invariance of SLE definition is well-defined.

For a real boundary point x=0, set
Xt​=κ​gt​(x)−Ut​​.
(29)
After a deterministic rescaling of time, the Boundary-point Bessel flow for SLE says that X is a Bessel process of dimension
δ=1+κ4​.
(30)
When 0<κ≤4, one has δ≥2, and the Hitting-zero classification for a Bessel process says that X never reaches zero. Thus no nonzero real boundary point is swallowed. The standard Loewner trace criterion then implies that each new tip is attached only to the preceding tip and the trace never intersects its past, so it is simple. For κ=0, the equation is driven by zero and generates a vertical slit. Hence SLEκ​ is simple for 0≤κ≤4.
