Assume first that μ(A∩T−nA)=0 for every n≥1. For n>m the measure-preserving transformation property gives
μ(T−mA∩T−nA)=μ(A∩T−(n−m)A)=0.
(1)
Thus the sets T−nA, n≥0, are pairwise disjoint and all have measure μ(A)>0, impossible in a probability space. Hence
μ(A∩T−nA)>0 for some n>0.​
(2)
The Poincare recurrence theorem states that almost every x∈A returns to A infinitely often. Let
E={x∈A:Tnx∈/A for every n≥1}.
(3)
The sets T−nE are pairwise disjoint: if x belonged to the mth and nth inverse images with m<n, then Tmx∈E would return to E⊆A after n−m steps. Invariance and finiteness therefore give μ(E)=0. A point of A with only finitely many returns belongs, after its last return, to some T−kE. The countable union of these null sets is null, proving the theorem.

The statement D-liman​=a means convergence in density of a sequence: for every ε>0, the proportion of n≤N with ∣an​−a∣≥ε tends to zero. The statement C-liman​=a means Cesaro convergence of a sequence, N−1∑n=1N​an​→a.

The product characterization of weak mixing says that if T is weakly mixing, then T×S is ergodic exactly when S is ergodic; in particular, ergodicity of T×T characterizes weak mixing.
Suppose UT​f=λf. Unitarity of the Koopman operator gives ∣λ∣=1. Weak mixing implies ergodicity, so ∣f∣ is almost everywhere constant. On the product,
F(x,y)=f(x)f(y)​
(9)
is invariant under T×T. Since the square is ergodic, F is constant, which forces f to be constant almost everywhere. Thus there are no nonconstant Koopman eigenfunctions.
Finally use the density-one correlation characterization of a weakly mixing measure-preserving transformation. For each positive-measure pair (A,B), the integers n for which μ(T−nA∩B)>0 form a density-one set after discarding finitely many terms; the corresponding sets for (A,B) and (A,C) therefore intersect, proving simultaneous hitting. Conversely, the simultaneous-hitting property is precisely the simultaneous hitting characterization of weak mixing, so it implies weak mixing.
