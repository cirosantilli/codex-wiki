Put R=k[x,y,z]/(xy−z3) and let X=SpecR. The ideal P=(x,z) is a height-one prime ideal, because R/P≅k[y] and dimR=2. It therefore defines a prime Weil divisor D=V(x,z).
Localizing at x eliminates y, giving
Rx​≅k[x,x−1,z],
(16)
a unique factorization domain. The Nagata theorem for divisor class groups says that Cl(X) is generated by the height-one primes containing x. Since
R/(x)≅k[y,z]/(z3),
(17)
the only such prime is P, and hence [D] generates. At the generic point of D, y is a unit in a ring and x=z3/y, so the order of vanishing is three:
div(x)=3D.
(18)
Thus 3[D]=0.
This relation has exact order three. Indeed, if a principal divisor were supported on D, its defining rational function would be a unit on D(x). The units of Rx​=k[z][x,x−1] are precisely cxm with c∈k× and m∈Z, whose divisors are 3mD. Consequently
Cl(k[x,y,z]/(xy−z3))≅Z/3Z,​
(19)
generated by [V(x,z)]. This is the n=3 case of the Divisor class group of an A-type surface singularity.
