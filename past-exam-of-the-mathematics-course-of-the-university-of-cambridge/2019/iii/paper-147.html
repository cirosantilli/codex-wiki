We prove the polynomial nonvanishing below the field size by mathematical induction on n. The case n=1 is the Lagrange root bound over a field: a nonzero polynomial of degree below p cannot have all p elements of Fp​ as roots.
For the induction step, suppose that P vanishes on all of Fpn​ and write
P(x1​,…,xn​)=∑j=0p−1​Pj​(x1​,…,xn−1​)xnj​.
(10)
The upper limit is valid because the total degree of a polynomial is less than p. Fixing the first n−1 variables produces a univariate polynomial of degree below p which vanishes at every xn​∈Fp​. It is therefore the zero polynomial, so every Pj​ vanishes on all of Fpn−1​. Each Pj​ also has total degree below p, and the induction hypothesis gives Pj​=0 for every j. Thus P=0.
Taking the contrapositive, every nonzero such P has some a∈Fpn​ for which
P(a)=0.​
