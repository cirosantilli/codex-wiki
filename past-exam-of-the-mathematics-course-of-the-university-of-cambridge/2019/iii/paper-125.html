The Hasse theorem for elliptic curves states that every elliptic curve E/Fq​ satisfies
∣#E(Fq​)−(q+1)∣≤2q​.​
(1)
Let π be the Frobenius isogeny and put a=tr(π). Its fixed points are exactly E(Fq​), so separability of 1−π gives
#E(Fq​)=deg(1−π)=1+q−a.
(2)
The degree of an isogeny is a nonnegative quadratic form on the endomorphism ring, and for all integers m,n,
deg([m]+[n]π)=m2+amn+qn2≥0.
(3)
If a2>4q, this quadratic polynomial has two real roots and takes a negative value at some rational m/n between them, hence after clearing denominators at some integer pair (m,n). Therefore a2≤4q, and substituting a=q+1−#E(Fq​) proves the bound. This is the degree-form proof of the Hasse bound.

If p∣D, square-freeness gives the cuspidal reduction y2=x3. Its nonsingular group is isomorphic to the additive group Fp​, so it is cyclic of order p.
Suppose p∤D. The reduction is an elliptic curve. Since p≡3(mod4), −1 is a quadratic nonresidue. Pairing x with −x in the quadratic-character sum and using
(−x)3−D2(−x)=−(x3−D2x)
(8)
shows that the two contributions cancel. Therefore #E(Fp​)=p+1. The three roots 0,D,−D are distinct, so the full 2-torsion is rational. A cyclic group has at most two elements killed by 2, hence E(Fp​) is noncyclic. Thus
Ens​(Fp​) is cyclic of order p if p∣D, and noncyclic of order p+1 otherwise.​
(9)

A one-dimensional commutative formal group law over a ring R is a series F(X,Y)∈R[[X,Y]] satisfying
F(X,0)=X,F(X,Y)=F(Y,X),F(F(X,Y),Z)=F(X,F(Y,Z)).
(10)
An isomorphism u:F→G is a series u(T)∈TR[[T]] with a compositional inverse and
u(F(X,Y))=G(u(X),u(Y)).
(11)
The multiplication series is defined recursively by [0]F​=0, [1]F​=T and [n+1]F​=F([n]F​,T), with the formal inverse handling negative n. Its linear term is
[n]F​(T)=nT+O(T2).
(12)
By the invertible morphism criterion for formal group laws, it is an isomorphism exactly when its linear coefficient n is a unit of R. Indeed, when n∈R×, recursive coefficient comparison constructs a unique compositional inverse; applying the morphism identity for [n]F​ shows that the inverse also respects F. Conversely, an invertible series must have a unit linear coefficient. Therefore
[n]F​ is an isomorphism⟺n∈R×.​
(13)

A positive integer D is a congruent number when it is the area of a right triangle with positive rational side lengths. For a point P=(x,y) with y=0 on
ED​:y2=x3−D2x,
(14)
the formulas
a=yx2−D2​,b=y2Dx​,c=yx2+D2​
(15)
give a2+b2=c2 and ab/2=D, after changing signs if necessary. Conversely, a rational right triangle of area D gives
x=bD(a+c)​,y=b22D2(a+c)​,
(16)
so these constructions are inverse up to the usual sign choices.
It remains to distinguish torsion. If an odd prime ℓ divided the order of a rational torsion point, choose by the Dirichlet theorem on primes in arithmetic progressions a good prime p≡3(mod4) for which p≡−1(modℓ). Part (a) gives #ED​(Fp​)=p+1, while part (b), applied to the formal group of an elliptic curve, makes reduction injective on ℓ-power torsion because ℓ is a unit in Zp​. This is impossible. Similarly, a good prime p≡3(mod8) shows that the rational 2-primary torsion has order at most four. Since
O,(0,0),(D,0),(−D,0)
(17)
already form the full rational 2-torsion,
ED​(Q)tors​≅(Z/2Z)2.
(18)

D is congruent⟺rankED​(Q)≥1.​

Kummer theory begins with the exact sequence
1⟶μn​⟶K×(cdot)n​K×⟶1.
(32)
Galois cohomology and Hilbert theorem 90 identify
H1(K,μn​)≅K×/K×n,
(33)
so cyclic extensions of exponent dividing n are described by adjoining nth roots when K contains μn​.
For an elliptic curve E/K, multiplication by n gives the Kummer exact sequence of an elliptic curve
0⟶E[n]⟶E(K)[n]​E(K)⟶0.
(34)
Its connecting homomorphism is the injective Kummer map of an elliptic curve
E(K)/nE(K)↪H1(K,E[n]),P⟼(σ↦σQ−Q),
(35)
where nQ=P. Passing to the finite division field of an elliptic curve K(E[n]) makes E[n] constant. Rational functions whose divisors are n(T)−n(O) then express the classes through finitely many elements of K(E[n])×/K(E[n])×n.
Let S contain the primes above n, the primes of bad reduction of an elliptic curve and the finitely many primes introduced by these functions. The local theory of good reduction shows that every Kummer class coming from E(K) is unramified outside S, so it lies in an S-unramified power class group. Such a group is finite: valuations outside S vanish modulo n, the ideal class group is finite, and the Dirichlet unit theorem makes the group of S-units modulo nth powers finite. The kernel created by passing to K(E[n]) is finite by finite-group Galois cohomology. Hence
E(K)/nE(K) is finite.​
(36)
This is the Kummer-theoretic proof of the weak Mordell-Weil theorem. Combining it with the height descent lemma proves the Mordell-Weil theorem: E(K) is a finitely generated abelian group.
