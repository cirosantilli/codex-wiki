Let Δ={(x,y):x=y}. For any transport plan π and any Borel set A,
μ(A)−ν(A)=π(A×Ac)−π(Ac×A).
(63)
Both rectangles are contained in Δc, so
∣μ(A)−ν(A)∣≤π(Δc)=K(π).
(64)

To attain this bound, use the dominating measure ρ=μ+ν and Radon-Nikodym derivatives f=dμ/dρ, g=dν/dρ. Define the common measure and residual mass by
dα=min(f,g)dρ,r=1−α(Rd).
(65)
Since ∫(f−g)dρ=0, the positive and negative parts have equal mass. Taking A={f>g} gives
r=∫(f−g)+​dρ=21​∫∣f−g∣dρ=supA​∣μ(A)−ν(A)∣.
(66)
If r=0, then μ=ν and the diagonal transport plan has zero cost. If r>0, put β=μ−α, γ=ν−α and define
π†=(Id,Id)#​α+r1​β⊗γ.
(67)
The residual measures each have mass r, so the marginal distributions of π† are α+β=μ and α+γ=ν, and its total mass is 1−r+r=1. They are mutually singular measures, concentrated respectively on {f>g} and {g>f}. Their product measure therefore gives no mass to Δ, and K(π†)=r. This is a maximal coupling.
