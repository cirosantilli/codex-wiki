The linear dispersion relation for a mode ei(kx−ωt) is 5ω2=k4+4. The proposed modes share phase velocity c, so 5c2kj2​=kj4​+4. A quadratic wave interaction generates the second harmonic and the difference harmonic. With only two positive wavenumbers, phase matching for a quadratic wave interaction requires k2​=2k1​. Equating their phase velocities gives
k12​+k12​4​=4k12​+k12​1​,
(30)
and therefore
k1​=1,k2​=2,c=1.​
(31)
This is a two-to-one resonance of dispersive waves. Its amplitude changes accumulate on t=O(ϵ−1). Choose the slow time T=ϵt/5; this harmless constant rescaling makes the later amplitude formulas simple. With ξ=x−t, the leading real field is A0​eiξ+B0​e2iξ+A0​e−iξ+B0​e−2iξ.
For the fundamental, the order-ϵ cross derivative in 5ψtt​ is −2i(A0​)T​eiξ, while the fundamental coefficient in ψ0​ψ0x​ is iA0​B0​. For the second harmonic the corresponding terms are −4i(B0​)T​e2iξ and iA02​. The solvability condition in the method of multiple scales removes the resonant forcing and gives
(A0​)T​=−21​A0​B0​,(B0​)T​=−41​A02​.​
(32)
The nonresonant third and fourth harmonics enter the correction. They do not change these leading amplitude equations.
Write A0​=Reiθ, B0​=Peiϕ and δ=ϕ−2θ. Taking real and imaginary parts gives the explosive two-to-one amplitude system in polar form:
RT​=−2RP​cosδ,θT​=−2P​sinδ,PT​=−4R2​cosδ,ϕT​=4PR2​sinδ.​
(33)
The polar phases are used where their corresponding amplitudes are nonzero. These equations imply
(R2−2P2)T​=0,(R2Psinδ)T​=0.
(34)
For the second identity, differentiate using δT​=(R2/(4P)+P)sinδ: the terms proportional to sinδcosδ cancel. Therefore
R2θT​=−21​R2Psinδ,P2ϕT​=41​R2Psinδ
(35)
are first integrals, and in particular
(R2θT​)T​=0,(P2ϕT​)T​=0.​
(36)
For constant phases with ϕ=2θ+π, these real equations reduce to RT​=RP/2 and PT​=R2/4. The initial data give R2−2P2=8, so the Riccati equation for P is 2PT​=P2+4. Integrating and using P(0)=2 gives

This is formal finite-time blowup of the reduced amplitude equations; the weakly nonlinear expansion loses validity as the amplitudes become large, so it cannot establish a singularity of the full partial differential equation.
