Let P be a Sylow p-subgroup. Since [G:P] is invertible in k, every kG-module is relatively P-projective.
Suppose first that kP has only finitely many indecomposable modules U1​,…,Ut​. For every indecomposable kG-module M, decompose ResPG​M into the Ui​. Relative projectivity makes M a summand of the corresponding finite direct sum of the IndPG​Ui​. The Krull–Schmidt theorem leaves only finitely many possible indecomposable summands, so kG has finite representation type.
Conversely, suppose kG has finitely many indecomposables. For an indecomposable kP-module U, the identity double coset in the Mackey restriction formula shows that U is a direct summand of
ResPG​IndPG​U.
(63)
Decomposing the induced module into the finitely many kG-indecomposables and restricting them shows, again by Krull–Schmidt, that only finitely many U can occur. Thus
kG has finite representation type⟺kP has finite representation type.​
(64)
If P is cyclic, the indecomposable modules of a cyclic p-group in characteristic p form a finite list. If P is noncyclic, its Frattini quotient has rank at least two and therefore has a quotient Cp​×Cp​. Inflation preserves indecomposability and nonisomorphism, while k(Cp​×Cp​) has infinitely many indecomposable modules. The Higman criterion for finite representation type of a group algebra now gives
kG has finite representation type⟺P is cyclic.​

Existence of a minimal subgroup follows because G has only finitely many subgroups and every module is relatively G-projective. Suppose an indecomposable module M is relatively projective for both H and K. Then M is a summand of IndHG​U and of IndKG​V for suitable modules U,V. Applying the Mackey restriction formula and the Krull–Schmidt theorem shows that M is relatively projective for some subgroup
H∩gK.
(66)
If H and K are minimal, this forces H⊆gK. Reversing their roles gives the reverse containment after conjugacy; since the groups are finite, H and K are conjugate. Thus vertices form a unique conjugacy class.
Let Q be a vertex and let P be a Sylow p-subgroup of Q. Since [Q:P] is invertible in k, every kQ-module is relatively P-projective. Transitivity of relative projectivity makes M relatively P-projective, so minimality forces Q=P. Hence every vertex is a p-group.
For the trivial module k, every kH-endomorphism is scalar, and its relative trace to G is multiplication by [G:H]. The D. Higman criterion says that k is relatively H-projective exactly when p∤[G:H]. The minimal such subgroups are precisely the Sylow p-subgroups. Therefore the vertex of an indecomposable module gives

Assume (iii). Reduction of the projective lattice W is projective, because a direct-summand decomposition of a free OG-module remains one after tensoring with k. Completeness of O and idempotent lifting show that W is indecomposable: otherwise a nontrivial idempotent of EndkG​(W) would lift and split W, hence split the simple KG-module M.
Let χ be the ordinary irreducible character of M. Since W is projective, χ vanishes on p-singular elements and restricts to the projective character Φ of W. Thus
dimk​EndkG​(W)=⟨Φ,Φ⟩p′​=⟨χ,χ⟩=1.

(iii)⟹(v).​

The decomposition matrix separates into two connected components. The first contains ϕ1​,ϕ2​ and χ1​,χ3A​,χ3B​,χ4​; it is the principal block B0​. The second contains only ϕ3​ and χ5​; since ϕ3​(1)=5 contains the full 5-part of ∣A5​∣=60, this is a defect-zero representation and its block B1​ has defect group 1.
For D=1, the normalizer is NG​(1)=G, so the Brauer correspondence is the identity and B1​ corresponds to itself.
The defect group of the principal block is a Sylow 5-subgroup P≅C5​. There are six Sylow 5-subgroups in A5​, so the orbit-stabilizer theorem gives
∣NA5​​(P)∣=660​=10.
(99)
The centralizer of a 5-cycle in A5​ is P, and an involution in the normalizer acts on P by inversion. Hence
N=NA5​​(P)≅C5​⋊C2​≅D10​.

In characteristic five the simple kN-modules are inflated from N/P≅C2​: their Brauer characters are ψ+​=(1,1) and ψ−​=(1,−1) on the identity and involution classes. If 1,ε,ρ1​,ρ2​ are the two one-dimensional and two two-dimensional ordinary characters of D10​, their reductions are
