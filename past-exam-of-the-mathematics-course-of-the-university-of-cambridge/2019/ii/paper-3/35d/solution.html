The chemical potential is the change in internal energy when one particle is added at fixed entropy and volume:
μ=(∂N∂E​)S,V​.
(340)
Equivalently, μ=(∂F/∂N)T,V​ for the Helmholtz free energy.
Let a microstate s have energy Es​ and particle number Ns​. Maximizing the Gibbs entropy subject to normalization and fixed mean values of E and N is the maximum-entropy derivation of equilibrium ensembles. With Lagrange multipliers α, β, and −βμ, vary
−∑s​ps​logps​−α(∑s​ps​−1)−β∑s​ps​(Es​−μNs​).
(341)
The stationarity equation is −logps​−1−α−β(Es​−μNs​)=0. Normalization therefore gives the grand canonical ensemble
ps​=Ze−β(Es​−μNs​)​,Z=s∑​e−β(Es​−μNs​),β=kB​T1​.​
(342)
Here Z is the grand canonical partition function and kB​ is the Boltzmann constant.
For one fermionic quantum state of energy ε, the Pauli exclusion principle permits occupation numbers only n=0,1. Its two grand-canonical weights are 1 and e−β(ε−μ), so its mean occupation is the Fermi-Dirac distribution
nF​(ε)=1+e−β(ε−μ)e−β(ε−μ)​=eβ(ε−μ)+11​.​
(343)
For a free nonrelativistic particle, ε=ℏ2k2/(2m). In a region of area A, the number of wave-vector states in the annulus from k to k+dk, including the two spin angular momentum states, is
2(2π)2A​2πkdk.
(344)
Since kdk=(m/ℏ2)dε, the two-dimensional free-electron density of states is constant:
g(ε)=πℏ2Am​.​
(345)
We now use units in which kB​=1, as in the question. At zero temperature, the Fermi-Dirac distribution is a step function, and hence
N=g∫0εF​​dε=gεF​.
(346)
At positive temperature the same fixed particle number satisfies the exact relation
N=g∫0∞​e(ε−μ)/T+1dε​=gTlog(1+eμ/T).
(347)
Thus
μ=Tlog(eεF​/T−1)=εF​+Tlog(1−e−εF​/T)=εF​+O(Te−εF​/T).
(348)
This is the low-temperature particle-number cancellation for constant density of states. Therefore, for T≪εF​, the mean number of particles in the energy interval [ε,ε+dε] is
g(ε)nF​(ε)dε≃εF​N​e(ε−εF​)/T+1dε​.​
(349)
Finally, compare the internal energy with its zero-temperature value. The thermally excited particles above εF​ and holes below εF​ have equal leading particle numbers, so their terms proportional to εF​ cancel. With x=∣ε−εF​∣/T,
U(T)−U(0)=2gT2∫0∞​ex+1xdx​=6π2​gT2,
(350)
where the integral follows from the fermion-to-boson thermal integral ratio. Differentiation gives the linear low-temperature heat capacity of a two-dimensional Fermi gas:
CA​=(∂T∂U​)N,A​=3π2​gT=3εF​π2N​T.​
(351)
The requested power law is therefore linear in T, with exponent one.
