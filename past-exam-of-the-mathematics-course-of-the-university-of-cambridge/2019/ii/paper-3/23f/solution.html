Let π:C→C/Λ be the universal covering map. Since the complex plane is a simply connected domain, the lifting criterion for a covering space gives a holomorphic lift between one-dimensional complex tori F:C→C such that
π∘F=f∘π.
(194)
For every λ∈Λ, the difference F(z+λ)−F(z) belongs to the discrete period lattice Λ. It depends continuously on z, so it is constant. Differentiating shows that the derivative F′ is Λ-periodic. It is bounded on the compact closure of a fundamental parallelogram, and periodicity makes it bounded on all of C. The Liouville theorem therefore makes F′ constant, and hence
F(z)=az+b.
(195)
Thus every holomorphic map has an affine lift of a holomorphic map between one-dimensional complex tori. If “map of complex tori” means an identity-preserving map, choose F(0)=0; then b=0, so F is the required linear map. Without that convention the statement must say affine, since a nonzero translation in a group of the torus lifts to z↦z+b.
The Weierstrass elliptic function of Λ is
℘Λ​(z)=z21​+∑ω∈Λ∖{0}​((z−ω)21​−ω21​).

The subtracted term gives the Normal convergence of the Weierstrass elliptic-function series away from Λ. Put
G2r​=∑ω∈Λ∖{0}​ω−2r.
(197)
The Laurent coefficients of the Weierstrass elliptic function at zero give
℘(z)=z−2+3G4​z2+5G6​z4+O(z6),℘′(z)=−2z−3+6G4​z+20G6​z3+O(z5).
(198)
Set g2​=60G4​ and g3​=140G6​. Direct substitution shows that the principal part and constant term of
H(z)=℘′(z)2−4℘(z)3+g2​℘(z)+g3​

vanish at zero. Since H is an elliptic function, translation gives the same cancellation at every point of the period lattice. Every apparent isolated singularity is therefore a removable singularity, so H is an entire function. It is periodic and hence bounded on the translates of a compact fundamental parallelogram. The Liouville theorem gives H=0, which proves the Weierstrass elliptic differential equation
℘′(z)2=4℘(z)3−g2​℘(z)−g3​.​
(200)
Finally suppose that f is a biholomorphic group homomorphism. Its identity-preserving lift has the form F(z)=ζz by the first part. Since the inverse map also lifts linearly,
ζΛ=Λ.
(201)
Choose a Z-basis of Λ. Multiplication by ζ is then represented by a unimodular matrix A∈GL2​(Z). As a real-linear transformation of C, it has determinant ∣ζ∣2>0; hence detA=1 and ∣ζ∣=1. The characteristic polynomial of A and the Cayley-Hamilton theorem give
ζ2−tr(A)ζ+1=0.
(202)
This already has the required form with m=−tr(A)∈Z and n=1. Moreover 2Reζ=tr(A), so ∣ζ∣=1 forces tr(A)∈{−2,−1,0,1,2}. Thus ζ is a root of unity, of order 1, 2, 3, 4, or 6, completing the description of an automorphism of a one-dimensional complex torus.
