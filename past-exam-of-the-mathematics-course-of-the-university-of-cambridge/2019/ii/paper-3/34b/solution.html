Bloch theorem states that the energy eigenstates of a Hamiltonian invariant under translations by a Bravais lattice can be chosen as Bloch states
Tr​∣ψk​⟩=e−ik⋅r∣ψk​⟩,ψk​(x)=eik⋅xuk​(x),uk​(x+r)=uk​(x).​
(329)
Indeed, the unitary operators Tr​ commute with one another because Tr​Ts​=Tr+s​=Ts​Tr​, and they commute with H by hypothesis. The simultaneous diagonalization theorem therefore lets us diagonalize all translations within each energy eigenspace. Their eigenvalues form a unitary character χ of the additive lattice:
χ(r+s)=χ(r)χ(s),∣χ(r)∣=1.
(330)
Writing χ(aj​)=e−ik⋅aj​ on a primitive basis gives χ(r)=e−ik⋅r. In position space, with (Tr​ψ)(x)=ψ(x−r), this implies ψk​(x+r)=eik⋅rψk​(x). Hence uk​(x)=e−ik⋅xψk​(x) is lattice-periodic. Adding a reciprocal lattice vector to k leaves the character unchanged, so the crystal momentum lies in a Brillouin zone.
For
a1​=2a​(3​,1),a2​=2a​(3​,−1),
(331)
the equations ai​⋅bj​=2πδij​ give the reciprocal basis
b1​=(3​a2π​,a2π​),b2​=(3​a2π​,−a2π​).​
(332)
The reciprocal lattice is triangular, so the first Brillouin zone is its Wigner-Seitz cell, a regular hexagon. Its six corners are
±32b1​+b2​​,±3b1​+2b2​​,±3b1​−b2​​.
(333)
Reciprocal-lattice translations identify these corners in two classes of three. Representatives are
K=32b1​+b2​​=(3​a2π​,3a2π​),K′=3b1​+2b2​​=(3​a2π​,−3a2π​).​
(334)
For example, (b1​−b2​)/3=K′−b2​, while the other equivalences follow by symmetry and reciprocal translations. This gives the requested sketch: a regular hexagon with the vertical edge from K′ to K at kx​=2π/(3​a) and alternating K,K′ corner classes.
For the tight-binding model, introduce the normalized Bloch sum
∣k⟩=Ns​​1​∑r∈Λ​eik⋅r∣r⟩.
(335)
Each hop by ±aj​ multiplies this state by e∓ik⋅aj​. Thus the two-direction nearest-neighbour tight-binding dispersion is
E(k)​=E0​−2λ{cos(k⋅a1​)+cos(k⋅a2​)}=E0​−4λcos(23​akx​​)cos(2aky​​).​​
(336)
Along the boundary edge from K′ to K, kx​=2π/(3​a) and ∣ky​∣≤2π/(3a), so
E(k)=E0​+4λcos(2aky​​).
(337)
For λ>0, it rises from E0​+2λ at either corner to E0​+4λ at the midpoint; for λ<0 the ordering reverses. Globally the two cosines in the first expression can simultaneously equal 1 or −1, and therefore
Emin​=E0​−4∣λ∣,Emax​=E0​+4∣λ∣,band width=8∣λ∣.​
(338)
Each orbital band contains two one-electron states per lattice site because an electron has two spin states. By band filling, a valency of one leaves this band half-filled and the material conducts, whereas a valency of two fills it. A filled band can be insulating only if it is separated from every empty band by a positive band gap.
The first band's maximum is E0​+4∣λ∣. If the second band's minimum is E0​+Δ, a gap exists precisely when Δ>4∣λ∣. Consequently, among the nontrivial fillings described here,
the material is a band insulator when the valency is 2 and Δ>4∣λ∣.​
(339)
For valency one the first band is partially filled. If Δ≤4∣λ∣, the bands overlap or touch, so even at valency two the overlapping energy bands prevent a band insulator.
