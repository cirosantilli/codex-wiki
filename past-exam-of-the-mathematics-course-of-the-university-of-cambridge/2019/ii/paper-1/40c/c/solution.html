The Householder-John theorem states: if A=M−N is Hermitian positive definite and M∗+N is also Hermitian positive definite, then M is invertible and
ρ(M−1N)<1.
(417)
Now let A=D+L+LT be real symmetric positive definite and tridiagonal. For the Jacobi splitting, M=D and N=−(L+LT). Positive definiteness of A implies every diagonal entry is positive, so D is positive definite. Introduce the alternating-sign diagonal matrix
S=diag(1,−1,1,−1,…).
(418)
Tridiagonality gives
SAS=D−L−LT=M∗+N.
(419)
Since S is orthogonal, SAS is positive definite whenever A is. Both hypotheses of the Householder-John theorem hold, and hence the Jacobi iteration matrix satisfies ρ(M−1N)<1. Therefore the Jacobi convergence for a symmetric positive-definite tridiagonal matrix follows.
