In this discrete age-structured population model, individuals of age a≥1 present during the breeding season each produce ba​ newborns, so the number entering age zero is
n0,t​=∑a=1∞​na,t​ba​.

An age-a individual survives the following winter with probability 1−μa​ and is then age a+1 in the next year, giving
na+1,t+1​=(1−μa​)na,t​.
(42)
Together with the initial age distribution, these equations determine every later cohort.

ra+1​=γ1−μa​​ra​,

r0​=∑a=1∞​ra​ba​=r0​∑a=1∞​[∏i=0a−1​(1−μi​)]γ−aba​.
(45)
A nonzero population mode therefore requires the Discrete Euler-Lotka equation
ϕ(γ)=1​,ϕ(γ)=∑a=1∞​[∏i=0a−1​(1−μi​)]γ−aba​.
(46)
For nonnegative vital rates with at least one surviving reproductive age and enough decay to make the series finite, ϕ is continuous and strictly decreasing on γ>0, tends to infinity as γ↓0, and tends to zero as γ→∞. The intermediate value theorem gives one positive solution, and strict monotonicity makes it unique.
The value ϕ(1) is the expected lifetime offspring count under these fixed rates. Since ϕ decreases, ϕ(1)>1 places the root at γ>1 and the population grows geometrically; ϕ(1)<1 places it at γ<1 and the population shrinks. Equality gives replacement, γ=1.
