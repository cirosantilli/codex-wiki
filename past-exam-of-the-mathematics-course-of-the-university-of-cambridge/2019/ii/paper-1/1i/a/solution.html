The Chinese remainder theorem says that if the positive integers m1​,…,mr​ are pairwise coprime and M=∏i​mi​, then for arbitrary residue classes ai​(modmi​) there is a unique residue class x(modM) satisfying
x≡ai​(modmi​)(1≤i≤r).
(1)
Equivalently, reduction is a ring isomorphism
Z/MZ⟶∏i=1r​Z/mi​Z.
(2)
For existence, put Mi​=M/mi​. Pairwise coprimality gives gcd(Mi​,mi​)=1, so Bézout's identity supplies an integer ui​ with ui​Mi​≡1(modmi​). Then
x=∑i=1r​ai​ui​Mi​
(3)
has the required residues, because every term except the ith is divisible by mi​. If x and y are two solutions, each mi​ divides x−y. Pairwise coprimality then implies that their product M divides x−y, proving uniqueness modulo M.
