Let
F(x,y)=x3y+y3+x.
(213)
Its complex partial derivatives are
Fx​=3x2y+1,Fy​=x3+3y2.
(214)
There is no point of X′ at which both vanish. Indeed, if F=Fx​=Fy​=0, then Fy​=0 and F=0 give
x3=−3y2,x=2y3.
(215)
Neither x nor y can be zero because Fx​=0. Substitution into Fx​=0 gives 12y7+1=0, whereas substitution into Fy​=0 gives 8y7+3=0; these equations are incompatible.
For every p=(x0​,y0​)∈X′, at least one partial derivative is nonzero. If Fy​(p)=0, the holomorphic implicit function theorem expresses the curve near p as y=g(x), and the restriction of the x-projection is a holomorphic coordinate chart. If Fx​(p)=0, use the y-projection instead. On overlaps, the transition functions are restrictions of holomorphic coordinate functions and are holomorphic. These charts define an atlas making X′ a one-dimensional complex manifold, hence a Riemann surface.
