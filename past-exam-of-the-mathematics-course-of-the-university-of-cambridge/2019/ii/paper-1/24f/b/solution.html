Past exam of the mathematics course of the University of Cambridge / 2019 / ii / Paper 1 / 24F / b / Solution

OurBigBook.com Words: 248
Homogenizing the affine equation gives the projective closure
C={[X:Y:Z]∈P2:X3Y+Y3Z+Z3X=0}.
(216)
This is the Klein quartic. It is smooth: if all three projective partial derivatives vanished, then
3X2Y=−Z3,3Y2Z=−X3,3Z2X=−Y3.
(217)
Multiplying them gives 28X3Y3Z3=0. If one coordinate is zero, the displayed equations force all three to vanish, which is impossible in projective space. Thus C is a smooth compactification of X′. The smooth compactification compatible with the extended coordinate functions is its normalization, and since C is already smooth it is biholomorphic to C.
For a direct genus computation, consider the extended coordinate map πx​:X→C∞​. A generic x leaves the cubic equation
y3+x3y+x=0,
(218)
so degπx​=3. In the affine part, ramification occurs where Fy​=0. Solving F=Fy​=0 gives (0,0) and the seven points
y7=−83​,x=2y3.
(219)
At the origin, x=−y3+O(y6), so the ramification index of a holomorphic map is three and the contribution is two. At each of the other seven points, Fx​=−7/2=0, Fy​=0, and Fyy​=6y=0, so the ramification index is two and each contributes one.
There are two points at infinity. Near P=[1:0:0], in coordinates u=Y/X, v=Z/X, the equation is
u+u3v+v3=0,
(220)
and 1/x=v, so P is unramified. Near Q=[0:1:0], in coordinates r=X/Y, s=Z/Y, the equation is
r3+s+rs3=0.
(221)
Thus s=−r3+O(r10) and 1/x=s/r=−r2+O(r9), so Q has ramification index two and contributes one. The total ramification is therefore
2+7+1=10.
(222)
The Riemann-Hurwitz formula for the degree-three map to the sphere gives
2g(X)−2=3(2⋅0−2)+10=4,
(223)
and hence
g(X)=3​.
(224)
This also agrees with the genus of a smooth plane curve of degree four.
