For integer k≥1, define fk​(n)=f(k,n). The assumed monotonicity in t gives
fk​(n)↑limt→∞​f(t,n)
(209)
for every n. Apply the monotone convergence theorem on N with counting measure:
limk→∞​∑n≥1​f(k,n)=∑n≥1​limk→∞​f(k,n)=∑n≥1​limt→∞​f(t,n).
(210)
The function t↦∑n​f(t,n) is itself nondecreasing, and the positive integers are cofinal in R as t→∞, so its limit over real t equals its limit over integer k. Therefore
t→∞lim​n≥1∑​f(t,n)=n≥1∑​t→∞lim​f(t,n)​.
(211)
Without monotonicity, take
f(t,n)=1[n,n+1)​(t)(t≥1).
(212)
For each fixed n, f(t,n)→0, so the sum of the pointwise limits is zero. At every t≥1, however, exactly one term is one, so ∑n​f(t,n)=1 and its limit is one. This moving-spike obstruction to interchanging a limit and an infinite sum gives the required counterexample.
