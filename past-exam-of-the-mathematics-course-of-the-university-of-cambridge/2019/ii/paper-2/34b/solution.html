For a normalized trial state ψ, expand it in an orthonormal basis of energy eigenstates, ψ=∑n​cn​ψn​. The Rayleigh quotient is then
⟨H⟩ψ​=∑n​∣cn​∣2En​≥E0​∑n​∣cn​∣2=E0​.

This is the quantum variational principle: minimizing the expectation of the Hamiltonian operator over any trial family gives an upper bound on the ground-state energy.
Let ψ be the normalized ground-state wavefunction and form the normalized dilation ψλ​(x)=λ1/2ψ(λx). Its kinetic and potential expectations are
T(λ)=λ2T,V(λ)=λ−nV.
(201)
The true ground state makes the energy stationary at λ=1, so
0=dλd​(λ2T+λ−nV)​λ=1​=2T−nV.
(202)
Thus the power-law quantum virial theorem gives
2⟨T⟩0​=n⟨V⟩0​.​
(203)
If n≤−3 and A>0, the two sides have opposite signs, because T>0 and V>0. If A<0, the scaled energy λ2T+λ−nV tends to −∞ as λ→∞, since −n>2, so the Hamiltonian is not bounded below. If A=0, the free particle has no normalizable ground state. Hence there is no normalizable ground state for n≤−3.
