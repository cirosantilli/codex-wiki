Let ζ be a primitive fifth root of unity. Over Fp​, the Frobenius automorphism acts by ζ↦ζp, so the splitting field of X5−1 has degree equal to the multiplicative order of p modulo 5. Its Galois group is generated by Frobenius and is cyclic. The possible orders are therefore 1 for p≡1, 4 for p≡2,3, and 2 for p≡4(mod5).
Put α=51/5. The polynomial X5−5 satisfies the Eisenstein criterion at 5, so [Q(α):Q]=5. Also [Q(ζ):Q]=φ(5)=4. The intersection of these two fields has degree dividing both 5 and 4, hence is Q. Since the splitting field is Q(α,ζ),
[Q(α,ζ):Q]=20.​

Label the five roots by αζi, i∈F5​. The Galois group acts through the affine group over a finite field:
i⟼ai+b,a∈F5×​,b∈F5​.
(86)
Its possible nontrivial cycle types are 5, 1+2+2, and 1+4; in particular, no element has a three-cycle. By the Dedekind factorization cycle test, the reduction of X5−5 modulo any prime p=5 therefore has no irreducible cubic factor.
The multiplier of the Frobenius element is a=p(mod5). If p≡2 or 3, then a has order four; every affine map with this multiplier has one fixed point and one four-cycle. Hence X5−5 is a product of a linear factor and an irreducible quartic. If p≡1, Frobenius is a translation i↦i+b: it is the identity when b=0, giving complete splitting, and is a five-cycle when b=0, giving an irreducible polynomial.
