Let nℓ​ be the number of graduates in college ℓ. In model 1, every edge from a person in college k to a person in college ℓ has probability
pkℓ(1)​=logit−1(βkℓ​),
(114)
so
Mi​,Mj​∼iidBin(nℓ​,pkℓ(1)​).
(115)
In model 2 the corresponding probability is
pkℓ(2)​=logit−1(βk​+βℓ​).
(116)
Because k=ℓ, the within-college indicator in model 3 vanishes, so pkℓ(3)​=pkℓ(2)​. Consequently models 2 and 3 both give
Mi​,Mj​∼iidBin(nℓ​,pkℓ(2)​).
(117)
The distinct edge indicators used in Mi​ and Mj​ are independent under all three models, so every model also forces Mi​ and Mj​ to be independent. Thus all members of one college have the same cross-college friendship propensity, with no person-level heterogeneity or dependence between their friendship counts. Real friendship networks can have unusually sociable individuals and correlated choices of friends, producing overdispersion and dependence that these models cannot fit.
