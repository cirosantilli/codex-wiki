A regular surface is a minimal surface when its mean curvature vanishes identically, equivalently when its two principal curvatures sum to zero everywhere.
The catenoid
ϕ(u,v)=(coshvcosu,coshvsinu,v)
(272)
is an example. In part (a), take f(v)=coshv and g(v)=v, so q=coshv. The formula there gives
H=21​(sech2v−sech2v)=0,K=−sech4v<0.
(273)
A plane and a circular cylinder have zero Gaussian curvature, as does a flat cone away from its vertex. Because a local isometry preserves Gaussian curvature, the catenoid is minimal and is not locally isometric to any of those surfaces; this is the catenoid is a minimal surface.
