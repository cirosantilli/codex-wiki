The source-free Maxwell equations in a linear medium are
∇⋅D=0,∇⋅B=0,∇×E=−∂t∂B​,∇×H=∂t∂D​.​
(395)
Here the constitutive relations are D=εE and B=μH.
Substitution of the sinusoidal plane waves into the two divergence equations gives the transversality of an electromagnetic plane wave,
k⋅E0​=0,k⋅B0​=0.
(396)
Faraday's law and the Ampère-Maxwell equation give, respectively,
k×E0​=ωB0​,k×B0​=−μεωE0​.
(397)
Taking k× the first equation and using transversality yields
−k2E0​=−μεω2E0​.
(398)
Thus a nonzero plane electromagnetic wave in a linear medium must satisfy
k2=μεω2,B0​=ωk×E0​​,vph​=kω​=με​1​.​
(399)
In particular, E0​, B0​, and k are mutually orthogonal, with ∣B0​∣=με​∣E0​∣.
Take the interface to be x=0 and the plane of incidence to be the xy-plane. The incident, reflected, and transmitted wavevectors may be written
kI​kR​kT​​=k−​(cosθI​,sinθI​,0),=k−​(−cosθR​,sinθR​,0),=k+​(cosθT​,sinθT​,0).​
(400)
The phase matching at a planar wave interface requires one common angular frequency and one common tangential wavevector:
k−​sinθI​=k−​sinθR​=k+​sinθT​.
(401)
Since k±​=ωε±​μ​=n±​ω/c, this gives the Snell law for electromagnetic waves,
θR​=θI​,n−​sinθI​=n+​sinθT​.​
(402)
For the final polarization, take the signed electric amplitudes along z. The dielectric-interface boundary conditions require continuity of tangential E and H. Since
H=μωk×E​,
(403)
their z- and y-components give
EI​+ER​=ET​,
(404)
and
k−​cosθI​(EI​−ER​)=k+​cosθT​ET​.
(405)
Eliminating EI​ yields
ET​ER​​=21​(1−k−​cosθI​k+​cosθT​​).
(406)
Using θR​=θI​ and Snell's law,
k−​cosθI​k+​cosθT​​=sinθT​cosθR​sinθR​cosθT​​=tanθT​tanθR​​.
(407)
Therefore the reflected-to-transmitted transverse-electric amplitude ratio is
∣ET​∣∣ER​∣​=21​(1−tanθT​tanθR​​),​
(408)
with the polarization directions chosen so that the displayed signed factor is nonnegative; otherwise its absolute value gives the magnitude ratio.
