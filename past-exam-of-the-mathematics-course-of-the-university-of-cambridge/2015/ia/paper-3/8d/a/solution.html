The identity belongs to the center of a group. If z,w commute with every g, then zw does too; rearranging gz=zg also shows that z−1 commutes with g. Hence Z(G) is a subgroup. Moreover gzg−1=z for z∈Z(G), so it is a normal subgroup.
Suppose ∣G∣=pn>1. The conjugation action partitions G into conjugacy classes. The orbit-stabilizer theorem gives class size [G:CG​(x)], where CG​(x) is the centralizer of x. By Lagrange's theorem, this size is a power of p; it is one exactly when x is central. Thus the class equation has the form
∣G∣=∣Z(G)∣+∑noncentral classes​pmi​,mi​≥1.
(34)
It follows that p∣∣Z(G)∣. Since the identity is central, ∣Z(G)∣≥1, and therefore
∣Z(G)∣≥p,Z(G)={e}.​
(35)
This is the nontrivial-centre property of a finite p-group.
