Write both nonnegative integers n,k in base-p expansions, padding with zero digits to a common length L. By the preceding polynomial identity,
(1+x)n=∏i=0L​((1+x)pi)ni​≡∏i=0L​(1+xpi)ni​(modp).
(15)
Expanding the last product with the binomial theorem, each term is obtained by choosing an integer ji​ with 0≤ji​≤ni​<p. Its exponent is ∑i​ji​pi and its coefficient is ∏i​(ji​ni​​). Uniqueness of base-p expansions says that the only way this exponent can equal k is to choose ji​=ki​ for every i.
If some ki​>ni​, no such term exists and the coefficient is zero. Otherwise its coefficient is precisely the product below. Comparing the coefficient of xk proves Lucas theorem:
(kn​)≡i=0∏L​(ki​ni​​)(modp),​
(16)
with the convention (ba​)=0 for b>a. This also covers k>n, and padding either expansion by further zero digits multiplies the product only by (00​)=1.
