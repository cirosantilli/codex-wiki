In plane polar coordinates, use er​=(cosθ,sinθ) and eθ​=(−sinθ,cosθ). Their derivatives are e˙r​=θ˙eθ​ and e˙θ​=−θ˙er​. Differentiating r=rer​ gives the velocity and acceleration in polar coordinates:
v=r˙er​+rθ˙eθ​,a=(r¨−rθ˙2)er​+(rθ¨+2r˙θ˙)eθ​.​
(37)
Both the electrostatic inverse-square force and the drag point radially, so the torque about the origin is zero even though the force depends on radial velocity. The vector angular momentum h=r×r˙ is constant. For h=0, every position lies in the fixed plane perpendicular to it; if h=0, the trajectory is radial and still lies in a plane. In the nonzero case the transverse equation is rθ¨+2r˙θ˙=0, giving
h=r2θ˙=constant.​
(38)
The signed h depends on the plane orientation. The stipulated ratio k/∣h∣ requires h=0.
Set u=1/r and use primes for differentiation with respect to θ. Then θ˙=hu2, r˙=−hu′ and r¨=−h2u2u′′. The radial equation is r¨−rθ˙2=−p/r2−kr˙/r2, so
−h2u2(u′′+u)=−pu2+khu2u′,u′′+hk​u′+u=h2p​.​
(39)
This damped Binet equation has the general solution, for k/∣h∣<2,
u(θ)=h2p​+e−k(θ−θ0​)/(2h)[Ccos(ω(θ−θ0​))+Dsin(ω(θ−θ0​))],ω=1−4h2k2​​.​
(40)
It is used on the physical interval where u>0. For either sign of h, introduce the forward angular distance ϕ=sgn(h)(θ−θ0​); then the exponential is e−kϕ/(2∣h∣). Thus damping acts forward in time for both orientations, not just when h>0.
For unit mass, the kinetic energy is 21​(r˙2+r2θ˙2) and the electrostatic potential energy is −p/r. Therefore
E=2h2​(u′2+u2)−pu.​
(41)
Using the damped Binet equation,
dθdE​=h2u′(u′′+u−h2p​)=−khu′2,
(42)
and multiplication by θ˙=hu2 gives
dtdE​=−kh2u2u′2=−r2kr˙2​≤0.​
(43)
This is also the drag force times the radial speed. Energy is nonincreasing in time, strictly decreasing while radial motion occurs; a circular trajectory already has constant energy. The sign of dE/dθ alone would be misleading for negative h.
To justify circularization under radial inverse-square drag, suppose the forward trajectory remains bounded, r≤R∗​. Since E≤E(0) and the effective radial potential energy h2/(2r2)−p/r tends to +∞ as r→0, the radius is also bounded away from zero. Velocity then stays bounded, so the solution continues for all forward time without collision. Moreover ϕ˙​=∣h∣/r2≥∣h∣/R∗2​, so ϕ→∞. The decaying general solution gives u→p/h2 and u′→0. Consequently
r→ph2​,r˙→0,E→−2h2p2​.​
(44)
The tangential motion remains, so the limiting orbit is circular. The convergence is asymptotic; boundedness is needed because a physical solution could otherwise reach u=0 and escape rather than complete indefinitely many turns.
The example uses p=h=1, k=0.6 and u(θ)=1+0.35e−0.3θcos(0.91​θ), which stays positive. Radial oscillations decay while angular momentum stays fixed.
