The series with a bounded monotone multiplier converges. Here is a proof that also permits conditional convergence. Choose M>0 with ∣bj​∣≤M. The Cauchy sequence criterion for the convergent series ∑aj​ ensures that, for any η>0 and sufficiently large m, all the partial tails Tj​=∑k=mj​ak​ satisfy ∣Tj​∣<η. Apply summation by parts to these partial tails:
∑j=mn​aj​bj​=Tn​bn​+∑j=mn−1​Tj​(bj​−bj+1​).
(43)
Since the multiplier is a monotone sequence, ∑j=mn−1​∣bj​−bj+1​∣=∣bm​−bn​∣. Consequently
​∑j=mn​aj​bj​​≤η(∣bn​∣+∣bm​−bn​∣)≤3Mη.

Taking η=ε/(3M) proves that the weighted partial sums form a Cauchy sequence, and hence converge. This is a special case of bounded-variation multipliers of a convergent series.
The series ∑n1/nan​ also converges. The multiplier need only be monotone eventually, since a finite initial segment cannot affect convergence. For h(x)=logx/x,
