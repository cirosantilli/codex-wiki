In the state-picture convention, a Stinespring representation of a completely positive map N:L(HQ​)→L(HQ′​) consists of an auxiliary Hilbert space HE​ and a linear map V:HQ​→HQ′​⊗HE​ such that
N(X)=TrE​(VXV†)for all X.​
(1)
The partial trace discards the environment. For example, from a Kraus representation N(X)=∑j​Kj​XKj†​, take V=∑j​Kj​⊗∣j⟩E​. The adjoint, or observable-picture, form is N†(Y)=V†(Y⊗IE​)V.
A general completely positive map does not require V to be an linear isometry of Hilbert spaces. If N is trace preserving, then V†V=∑j​Kj†​Kj​=I, so V is an linear isometry of Hilbert spaces. This is the Stinespring dilation of a quantum channel, which will be used in the data-processing proof.

For a bipartite density operator ρAB​, let S(A)ρ​=S(ρA​), with ρA​=TrB​ρAB​, and similarly for other systems. The quantum conditional entropy and coherent information are
H(A∣B)ρ​=S(AB)ρ​−S(B)ρ​,I(A⟩B)ρ​=S(B)ρ​−S(AB)ρ​=−H(A∣B)ρ​.​
(3)
Unlike classical conditional entropy, quantum conditional entropy can be negative. For a Bell state, S(AB)=0 and S(B)=1, giving H(A∣B)=−1 and coherent information equal to one bit.

Use the Schmidt decomposition of the bipartite pure state:
∣ψ⟩QR​=∑j=1r​pj​​∣uj​⟩Q​∣vj​⟩R​,pj​>0,∑j​pj​=1.
(4)
The two reduced density matrices, obtained by partial trace, are
ρQ​=∑j​pj​∣uj​⟩⟨uj​∣,ρR​=∑j​pj​∣vj​⟩⟨vj​∣.
(5)
Their nonzero eigenvalues are identical, even if the two ambient dimensions differ. Zero eigenvalues contribute no Von Neumann entropy, so
H(Q)ψ​=H(R)ψ​=−j∑​pj​log2​pj​.​
(6)
This common Von Neumann entropy is the entanglement entropy of the bipartite pure state.

Use two results: the isometric Stinespring dilation of a quantum channel, and Strong subadditivity of Von Neumann entropy. Let V:B→B′E dilate the given operation, and define
ωAB′E​=(IA​⊗V)ρAB​(IA​⊗V†),σAB′​=TrE​ω.
(7)
An linear isometry of Hilbert spaces preserves the nonzero eigenvalues, so S(B′E)ω​=S(B)ρ​ and S(AB′E)ω​=S(AB)ρ​. Subtracting the two coherent information expressions gives
I(A⟩B)ρ​−I(A⟩B′)σ​​=S(B′E)ω​−S(AB′E)ω​−S(B′)ω​+S(AB′)ω​=I(A:E∣B′)ω​≥0.​
(8)
The last inequality is Strong subadditivity of Von Neumann entropy, in the form S(AB′)+S(B′E)≥S(B′)+S(AB′E). Thus the data-processing inequality for coherent information is
I(A⟩B)ρ​≥I(A⟩B′)σ​.​
(9)
The lost coherent information is precisely the quantum conditional mutual information between the reference A and discarded environment E, conditional on the retained output B′.

Take a purification of a density operator ∣Ψ⟩ABCD​ of ρABC​. Apply Strong subadditivity of Von Neumann entropy to the reduced state on ACD, with A the conditioning system:
S(AC)+S(AD)≥S(A)+S(ACD).
(10)
Complementary subsystems of a pure state have the same Von Neumann entropy, by the Schmidt decomposition. Therefore S(AD)=S(BC) and S(ACD)=S(B). Substitution gives
S(A)+S(B)≤S(AC)+S(BC).​
(11)
This is weak monotonicity of quantum entropy. The purification of a density operator is only a proof device; no purity assumption is imposed on ρABC​.

Use superdense coding. For the two-bit message (a,b)∈{0,1}2, Alice applies ZaXb to her half of the shared Bell state, where X,Z are the Pauli X gate and Pauli Z gate. The encoded states are
(a,b)(0,0)(0,1)(1,0)(1,1)​(ZaXb⊗I)∣Φ+⟩(∣00⟩+∣11⟩)/2​(∣01⟩+∣10⟩)/2​(∣00⟩−∣11⟩)/2​(∣01⟩−∣10⟩)/2​.​​
(12)
These four Bell states are orthonormal. Alice then transmits her one qubit to Bob, who now holds both qubits. A Bell-basis measurement identifies the message with probability one.
More explicitly, Bob can apply a CNOT gate with the received qubit as control and his original qubit as target, followed by a Hadamard gate on the control. The CNOT gate sends the encoded state to
2​∣0⟩+(−1)a∣1⟩​⊗∣b⟩,
(13)
and the Hadamard gate sends this to ∣a⟩⊗∣b⟩. Computational-basis projective measurements therefore read out a,b exactly. One transmitted qubit, assisted by one previously shared Bell pair, communicates two classical bits.

Use one-bit remote preparation of real qubit states. Alice measures her qubit in the orthonormal basis
∣αθ​⟩=cosθ∣0⟩+sinθ∣1⟩,∣βθ​⟩=−sinθ∣0⟩+cosθ∣1⟩.
(14)
Because this change of basis is real and orthogonal, the shared Bell state has the expansion
∣Φ+⟩=2​∣αθ​⟩A​∣αθ​⟩B​+∣βθ​⟩A​∣βθ​⟩B​​.
(15)
Her projective measurement gives each outcome with probability one half. She sends m=0 for the first outcome and m=1 for the second. Bob's conditional pure state is respectively ∣αθ​⟩ or ∣βθ​⟩.
For m=0 Bob does nothing. For m=1 he applies the fixed unitary operator
U=iY=(0−1​10​),U∣βθ​⟩=∣αθ​⟩,
(16)
where Y is the Pauli Y gate. Bob need not know θ, because this correction is the same for all real θ. Both outcomes produce the exact density operator ∣αθ​⟩⟨αθ​∣, using one classical bit and only local operations.
This remote state preparation consumes the shared entangled state. Before receiving the bit, Bob has the mixture (∣αθ​⟩⟨αθ​∣+∣βθ​⟩⟨βθ​∣)/2=I/2, independent of θ, as required by quantum no-signalling. The real-state restriction is what permits a fixed unitary correction of the orthogonal outcome.

The positive part of a Hermitian operator and negative part of a Hermitian operator are the positive operators
X+​=i∑​max(λi​,0)∣αi​⟩⟨αi​∣,X−​=i∑​max(−λi​,0)∣αi​⟩⟨αi​∣.​

Thus X=X+​−X−​ and X+​X−​=0. In this convention the negative part itself is nonnegative. This is the positive-negative decomposition of the Hermitian operator.
The operator absolute value is defined by the unique positive operator square root
∣X∣=X†X​=X2​=∑i​∣λi​∣∣αi​⟩⟨αi​∣.
(18)
The scalar identity ∣λ∣=max(λ,0)+max(−λ,0) applied in the spectral decomposition proves
∣X∣=X+​+X−​.​
(19)

For any linear operator, the trace norm is ∥X∥1​=TrX†X​, the sum of its singular values. For a Hermitian operator,
∥X∥1​=Tr∣X∣=i∑​∣λi​∣.​
(20)
The Holevo–Helstrom theorem concerns minimum-error discrimination of two density operators ρ,σ. If their prior probabilities are p and 1−p, the maximum success probability over all measurement in quantum measurements is
Psucc∗​=21​(1+∥pρ−(1−p)σ∥1​).​
(21)
An optimal binary POVM declares ρ on the positive spectral subspace of pρ−(1−p)σ and declares σ on its negative spectral subspace; zero-eigenvalue vectors can be assigned either way. For equal priors the formula becomes Psucc∗​=21​+41​∥ρ−σ∥1​.

The constraints −I≤T≤I are in the order of Hermitian operators. They imply −1≤⟨αi​∣T∣αi​⟩≤1. Using the spectral decomposition of X,
Tr(XT)=∑i​λi​⟨αi​∣T∣αi​⟩≤∑i​∣λi​∣=∥X∥1​.
(22)
Choose T=sgn(X), with eigenvalues +1 on the positive spectral subspace, −1 on the negative spectral subspace, and zero on the kernel. It satisfies the constraints and attains equality. The trace-norm variational principle for Hermitian operators is therefore
∥X∥1​=−I≤T≤Imax​Tr(XT).​
(23)
To prove the Holevo–Helstrom theorem, write a binary POVM as {E,I−E}, where 0≤E≤I, and associate the first outcome with ρ. Any larger outcome set followed by a binary decision can be grouped into this form. Put Δ=pρ−(1−p)σ. Its success probability is
Psucc​=pTr(ρE)+(1−p)Tr(σ(I−E))=(1−p)+Tr(ΔE).
(24)
The substitution T=2E−I bijects the allowed effects with the interval −I≤T≤I. Since TrΔ=2p−1,
Psucc​=21​+21​Tr(ΔT)≤21​(1+∥Δ∥1​).
(25)
Choosing E as the positive spectral projection of Δ attains this value, with an arbitrary decision on its kernel. This proves the optimal success probability and constructs an optimal measurement.

Write xi​=⟨ψi​∣X∣ψi​⟩, which is real because X is a Hermitian operator. Set
T=∑i​sgn(xi​)∣ψi​⟩⟨ψi​∣,
(28)
with sgn(0)=0. This Hermitian operator satisfies −I≤T≤I. The trace-norm variational principle for Hermitian operators gives the diagonal absolute-sum bound for the trace norm:
∥X∥1​≥Tr(XT)=i∑​∣⟨ψi​∣X∣ψi​⟩∣.​
(29)
For X=ρ−P, with P=∣ψ⟩⟨ψ∣, extend ∣ψ⟩ to an orthonormal basis ∣ψ1​⟩=∣ψ⟩,∣ψ2​⟩,…. Put r=⟨ψ∣ρ∣ψ⟩. The first diagonal entry is r−1≤0, and all the others are nonnegative. Their sum is 1−r, because Trρ=1. The bound therefore yields ∥ρ−P∥1​≥2(1−r). Using the definitions of trace distance and quantum fidelity,
D(ρ,P)≥1−r=1−F(ρ,P)2.​
(30)
This pure-target lower bound on trace distance is attained whenever ρ has no coherence between ∣ψ⟩ and its orthogonal complement. The proof used the trace-norm variational principle for Hermitian operators, together with positivity and normalization of a density operator.

The Shannon entropy of a finite-valued random variable is
H(X)=−x∈AX​∑​PX​(x)log2​PX​(x),0log2​0=0.​
(31)
An epsilon-sufficient set is a subset S⊆AX​ retaining all but at most ϵ of the probability:
Pr(X∈S)=x∈S∑​PX​(x)≥1−ϵ.​
(32)
Usually 0≤ϵ≤1. Sufficiency here concerns retained probability mass, rather than a sufficient statistic.

Use the weak notion of a typical set. For a word zn=(z1​,…,zn​) write PZn​(zn)=∏i=1n​PZ​(zi​). The definition is
Tδn​(PZ​)={zn:PZn​(zn)>0, ​−n1​log2​PZn​(zn)−H(Z)​≤δ}.​
(33)
A weakly typical sequence therefore has probability between 2−n(H(Z)+δ) and 2−n(H(Z)−δ). Words containing zero-probability symbols are excluded. These two bounds and the weak law of large numbers provide the three conclusions below.

Let Yi​=−log2​PZ​(Zi​). Since the alphabet is finite, these independent and identically distributed random variables are finite almost surely, have mean H(Z), and have finite variance v. Also
−n1​log2​PZn​(Z(n))=n1​∑i=1n​Yi​.
(34)
The weak law of large numbers says that this average converges in probability to H(Z). Thus
n→∞lim​Pr(Z(n)∈Tδn​(PZ​))=1(δ>0).​
(35)
One can also state the quantitative Chebyshev inequality bound Pr(Z(n)∈/Tδn​)≤v/(nδ2). If v=0, every word of positive probability is in the typical set. This is the finite-alphabet asymptotic equipartition property.

Fix 0≤ϵ<1 and δ>0. By the asymptotic equipartition property, all sufficiently large n satisfy Pr(Z(n)∈/Tδn​)≤(1−ϵ)/2. For any epsilon-sufficient set Sn​,
Pr(Z(n)∈Sn​∩Tδn​)≥Pr(Z(n)∈Sn​)−Pr(Z(n)∈/Tδn​)≥21−ϵ​.

∣Sn​∣≥21−ϵ​2n(H(Z)−δ).​
(40)
The Chebyshev inequality used above supplies the sufficient quantitative condition n≥2v/[δ2(1−ϵ)]. No assumption that Sn​ itself is a typical set is needed; its intersection with one provides the bound.

Use the Shannon second coding theorem in its channel capacity form C=maxPX​​I(X;Y). The finite-alphabet erasure channel erases independently of the input. On an unerased output Bob knows X exactly; on the erasure output his posterior distribution is still PX​. Its classical conditional entropy is therefore
H(X∣Y)=fH(X),I(X;Y)=H(X)−H(X∣Y)=(1−f)H(X).
(41)
Equivalently, H(Y)=h(f)+(1−f)H(X) and H(Y∣X)=h(f), so the binary entropy terms cancel in the mutual information.
For an alphabet of size k, Shannon entropy is at most log2​k, with equality for the uniform distribution. Maximizing the displayed mutual information proves the capacity of a finite-alphabet erasure channel:
C=(1−f)log2​kbits per channel use.​
(42)
This includes f=0, which transmits the whole symbol, and f=1, which transmits no information.

For t<1, normalize the remaining probabilities by qj​=PX​(j)/(1−t), j=2,…,k. Directly splitting the Shannon entropy sum gives
H(X)=−tlog2​t−(1−t)log2​(1−t)+(1−t)H(q)=h(t)+(1−t)H(q).
(43)
Here h is the binary entropy. The Shannon entropy of a distribution on k−1 points is at most log2​(k−1). For example, nonnegativity of its Kullback-Leibler divergence from the uniform distribution gives log2​(k−1)−H(q)≥0. Thus the entropy bound with one prescribed probability is
H(X)≤h(t)+(1−t)log2​(k−1).​
(44)
For t<1, equality holds precisely when the remaining probabilities are all (1−t)/(k−1). For t=1, the distribution is deterministic and both sides are zero. The displayed formula is for k≥2; a one-point alphabet simply has zero Shannon entropy.

Put pi​=⟨ψi​∣ρ∣ψi​⟩. The rank-one dephasing is ρ′=∑i​pi​∣ψi​⟩⟨ψi​∣. If pi​=0, positivity gives
0=pi​=∥ρ​∣ψi​⟩∥2,
(45)
so ρ∣ψi​⟩=0. The kernel of ρ′ is exactly the span of these zero-probability basis vectors and is therefore contained in the kernel of ρ. Taking orthogonal complements proves support inclusion under rank-one dephasing:
suppρ⊆suppρ′.​
(46)
Here the support of a positive operator is the orthogonal complement of its kernel.
On that support, log2​ρ′ is diagonal in the measurement basis, giving
Tr(ρlog2​ρ′)=∑i:pi​>0​pi​log2​pi​=Tr(ρ′log2​ρ′).
(47)
Consequently the relative-entropy identity for rank-one dephasing is
Drel​(ρ∥ρ′)=Tr[ρ(log2​ρ−log2​ρ′)]=S(ρ′)−S(ρ).
(48)
Klein's inequality gives Drel​(ρ∥ρ′)≥0, since both density operators have trace one. For singular ρ, first restrict to suppρ′ where ρ′ is positive definite, replace ρ by (ρ+ηI)/(1+ηdimsuppρ′), and let η↓0. The support inclusion ensures that the limit is finite. Thus
S(ρ′)≥S(ρ).​
(49)
This proves entropy increase under nonselective projective measurement using Klein's inequality. Equality holds exactly when ρ=ρ′, so the original density operator was already diagonal in the chosen basis.

Choose an orthonormal basis of the joint d2-dimensional Hilbert space whose first vector is the given purification of a density operator ∣ρ⟩QR​. Apply rank-one dephasing to σQR​ in this basis, and denote the resulting diagonal probabilities by p1​,…,pd2​. Their first entry is
p1​=⟨ρ∣σQR​∣ρ⟩=f2.
(50)
The preceding entropy increase under nonselective projective measurement and entropy bound with one prescribed probability yield
S(σQR​)≤H(p)≤h(f2)+(1−f2)log2​(d2−1).
(51)
Therefore the quantum Fano inequality is
S(σQR​)≤h(f2)+(1−f2)log2​(d2−1).​
(52)
The quantity f2 is the entanglement fidelity of N on ρQ​; it is already a squared overlap, so it is f2, rather than f, that enters the binary entropy. The argument is an instance of the entropy bound from overlap with a pure state in dimension d2. At f2=1 the output is the original pure state and its Von Neumann entropy is zero. For d=1 the system is trivial and the same zero-entropy conclusion holds without evaluating log2​(d2−1).
