The terms are free bimodules with the formal symbols commuting with coefficients. Centrality makes d a bimodule map, and the terms in d2 cancel in pairs because the ti​ commute. This defines the Koszul complex over a possibly noncommutative ring without using an undefined exterior algebra of arbitrary one-sided modules. In particular K(t)⊗R​M is a well-defined chain complex of left modules.
A regular sequence on a module means that multiplication by ti​ is injective on M/(t1​,…,ti−1​)M for each i, usually with the additional convention that the final quotient is nonzero. The homology vanishing below uses only the injectivity conditions. For one element, the chain complex is Mt1​​M, with zero first homology and zeroth homology M/t1​M.
For the induction let C=K(t1​,…,tn−1​)⊗R​M. Adjoining the last generator identifies the new Koszul complex with the mapping cone of multiplication by tn​ on C. In the convention Cone(tn​)k​=Ck​⊕Ck−1​ its differential is (a,b)↦(da+tn​b,−db); the identification is a+en​∧b. The long exact sequence in homology of this mapping cone gives
⋯⟶Hk​(C)tn​​Hk​(C)⟶Hk​(Cone(tn​))⟶Hk−1​(C)tn​​Hk−1​(C)⟶⋯.
(8)
The induction hypothesis is Hk​(C)=0 for k>0 and H0​(C)=N=M/(t1​,…,tn−1​)M. Regularity makes tn​:N→N injective. Hence the new positive homology is zero and its zeroth homology is N/tn​N. The augmentation to this quotient induces these homology isomorphisms, giving the quasi-isomorphism
K(t)⊗R​M≃M/(t1​,…,tn​)M,​
(9)
where the right side is placed in degree zero. The results used are the long exact sequence in homology of a degreewise short exact sequence of complexes and its mapping cone form, together with the stated induction; no unmentioned acyclicity criterion is needed.
For R=Z[X], (p,X) is a regular sequence: p is a non-zero-divisor in R, and multiplication by X is injective in (Z/pZ)[X], even when p is composite. The Koszul resolution of R/(p,X) is
0⟶R(p−X​)​R2(p  X)​R⟶Z/pZ⟶0.

ExtR0​=ker(p:N→N),ExtR1​=(N/pN)⊕ker(p:N→N),ExtR2​=N/pN,

These are R-modules through X=0 and reduction modulo p. If the multiplicative self-Ext functor is desired, the Koszul self-Ext algebra is the exterior algebra on two degree-one generators over Z/pZ. The two contractions on the Koszul resolution lift these classes, square to zero and anticommute, so this description also holds for composite p and characteristic two.
