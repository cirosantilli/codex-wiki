For a cohomological spectral sequence, dr​:Erp,q​→Erp+r,q−r+1​ and the next page is its cohomology. In the bounded setting, convergence of a spectral sequence to H∗ means that each Hn has a finite decreasing, exhaustive and separated filtration with
E∞p,q​≅FpHp+q/Fp+1Hp+q.​
(15)
Here E∞p,q​ is the eventual stable value. This determines the associated graded module of Hn, rather than automatically a canonical direct-sum decomposition of Hn. For unbounded filtrations additional completeness/convergence conditions are necessary; boundedness removes those issues here.
The bounded filtered-complex convergence theorem states the following. If FpC is a decreasing filtration by cochain subcomplexes, preserved by the differential, finite in each cochain degree (uniform bounds FaC=C, FbC=0 are sufficient), then there is a spectral sequence
E0p,q​=FpCp+q/Fp+1Cp+q,E1p,q​=Hp+q(FpC/Fp+1C),Erp,q​⇒Hp+q(C).
(16)
The abutment filtration is FpHn(C)=im[Hn(FpC)→Hn(C)]. Its finite length ensures stabilization and the displayed limiting-page identification. The filtered cochain complex need not itself be bounded in cochain degree. In the degreewise finite version, the bounds in degrees n−1,n,n+1 suffice to stabilize the terms of total degree n.
For a double cochain complex Dp,q bounded in both indices, form the total cochain complex TotnD=⨁p+q=n​Dp,q with anticommuting differentials dh​,dv​ and total differential dh​+dv​. If the original differentials commute, inserting the usual sign in one of them gives this convention. Filtering by the first index gives E1p,q​=Hvq​(Dp,∗) and then E2p,q​=Hhp​(Hvq​D). Filtering by the second index gives the spectral sequence taking horizontal cohomology first and vertical cohomology next. Both filtrations are finite, so both converge to H∗(TotD). This is the two spectral sequences of a bounded double complex construction.
The printed left/left tensor expression needs a handedness repair. Over an arbitrary ring, a tensor product of modules over R pairs a right module with a left module. To retain the order of the printed formula, take P to be a bounded cochain complex of projective right R-modules and M a left R-module. Alternatively, keep P left, take M right, and write M⊗R​P and Tor−pR​(M,HqP). For a commutative ring no repair is needed. We prove the first, correctly typed formulation.
Finite projective dimension gives a finite projective resolution Q∙​→M by left modules. Form Dp,q=Pq⊗R​Q−p​ for −d≤p≤0. On x⊗y take dv​=dP​⊗1 and dh​=(−1)q1⊗dQ​. These anticommute, and the double cochain complex is bounded in both directions. Projective modules are flat modules, so taking vertical cohomology first gives
E1p,q​=Hq(P)⊗R​Q−p​,E2p,q​=Tor−pR​(HqP,M).​
(17)
The first identification uses flatness of Q−p​; the second is the Tor functor computed by resolving its left-module argument M. Balancedness of the Tor functor allows either correctly sided projective resolution to compute it, by the double-resolution argument.
Taking horizontal cohomology first instead uses flatness of Pq. The resolution Q∙​ then leaves only Pq⊗R​M in column p=0. Its remaining differential is dP​⊗1, so the augmentation TotD→P⊗R​M is a quasi-isomorphism. Apply the convergence theorem to this underlying abelian-group double complex. The two spectral sequences therefore prove the Künneth spectral sequence:
E2p,q​=Tor−pR​(HqP,M)⇒Hp+q(P⊗R​M).​
(18)
These are abelian groups in general; an extra module structure requires appropriate bimodule hypotheses. The index p is nonpositive, so −p is the nonnegative homological index of the Tor functor.
Finally write the cycle modules as Zq=kerdq and the boundary modules as Bq=imdq−1. If every boundary module Bq is projective, the short exact sequence 0→Zq→Pq→Bq+1→0 splits. Thus Zq is projective. The short exact sequence 0→Bq→Zq→HqP→0 is now a length-one projective resolution, so ToriR​(HqP,M)=0 for i≥2. The E2​ page occupies only columns p=−1,0. Every dr​ for r≥2 goes r columns to the right, hence has zero source or zero target. Therefore
E2​=E∞​.​
(19)
This two-column degeneration of a Künneth spectral sequence gives the edge short exact sequences
0⟶HnP⊗R​M⟶Hn(P⊗R​M)⟶Tor1R​(Hn+1P,M)⟶0.
(20)
Degeneration itself does not supply a canonical splitting. Projective boundaries also do not force HqP to be projective: a complex with differential multiplication by 2 over Z has projective boundary but a Z/2Z cohomology group.
