Put p=dP/dμ and q=dQ/dμ. Using natural logarithms, the Kullback-Leibler divergence is
K(P,Q)=∫Ω​plogqp​dμ.​
(1)
The integrand is zero where p=0, including where both densities vanish, and is +∞ where p>0=q. Equivalently, K(P,Q)=∫log(dP/dQ)dP when P is absolutely continuous with respect to Q, and it is +∞ otherwise. This is a well-defined extended integral: on p<q, writing t=p/q gives −plog(p/q)=−qtlogt≤q/e, so its negative part is integrable.
For Pinsker's inequality, an infinite Kullback-Leibler divergence makes the conclusion immediate. Assume it is finite. Set
A={p≥q},a=P(A),b=Q(A),t=a−b.
(2)
Since ∫(p−q)dμ=0, the positive and negative parts of p−q have equal integrals. Thus
∫∣p−q∣dμ=2t,t≥0.
(3)
This t is the total variation distance with the convention supE​∣P(E)−Q(E)∣.
We first prove the binary partition bound for relative entropy. For any measurable E with Q(E)>0, apply Jensen inequality to the convex function u↦ulogu under the conditional probability measure Q(⋅∣E). Absolute continuity of measures gives
∫E​plogqp​dμ≥P(E)logQ(E)P(E)​.
(4)
If P(E)=0, both sides are zero; if Q(E)=0, finiteness forces P(E)=0 as well. Apply this bound to A and its complement to obtain
K(P,Q)≥d(a,b):=alogba​+(1−a)log1−b1−a​.
(5)
For 0<b<1, regarding d(a,b) as a function of a gives
d(b,b)=0,∂a​d(b,b)=0,∂a2​d(a,b)=a(1−a)1​≥4.
(6)
Consequently d(a,b)−2(a−b)2 is a convex function and has its minimum zero at a=b. By continuity this also holds at a=0,1. For b=0,1, the finite case has a=b and the remaining cases have infinite divergence. This proves the Bernoulli relative entropy lower bound in every case. Combining the bounds yields
K(P,Q)≥2t2=21​(∫∣p−q∣dμ)2,∫∣p−q∣dμ≤2K(P,Q)​.​

For the Hellinger distance, retain the unnormalized convention in which
H2(P,Q)=2−2∫pq​dμ.
(8)
The integral is the Hellinger affinity. In the finite-divergence case, q/p>0 for P-almost every point. Apply −logu≥1−u with u=q/p​ to get
K(P,Q)​=∫{p>0}​−2logq/p​pdμ≥2∫{p>0}​(1−q/p​)pdμ=2−2∫pq​dμ=H2(P,Q).​
(9)
The affinity is finite by Cauchy-Schwarz inequality. Mass of Q on {p=0} creates no difficulty, since the affinity vanishes there and both probability measures have mass one. The infinite-divergence case is again immediate. We obtain the relative entropy bound for the unnormalized Hellinger distance:
H(P,Q)≤K(P,Q)​.​

In the Gaussian white noise model, observe the entire path
Y(t)=∫0t​f(s)ds+n−1/2W(t),0≤t≤1,​
(11)
where W is standard Brownian motion and the deterministic drift belongs to L2 space on [0,1]. Equivalently, dY(t)=f(t)dt+n−1/2dW(t). For every deterministic h∈L2[0,1], the observed stochastic integral satisfies
Y(h):=∫01​h(t)dY(t)=⟨h,f⟩+n−1/2W(h),Cov(W(h),W(g))=⟨h,g⟩.

The Hoeffding inequality states that if X1​,…,Xn​ are independent random variables with ai​≤Xi​≤bi​ almost surely, then for t>0
P(∑i=1n​(Xi​−EXi​)≥t)≤exp(−∑i​(bi​−ai​)22t2​).
(23)
The same bound holds for the lower tail. Consequently the two-sided tail is at most twice this bound. When all ranges have length zero, the sum is deterministic and every positive tail probability is zero.
We construct an exponential packing of a Hamming cube. Choose an inclusion-maximal separated set S in {−1,1}n with separation at least r=n/8 for the Hamming distance. It exists by a finite greedy procedure: keep adding any vertex at distance at least r from all selected vertices until none remains. By maximality, every vertex is within distance strictly less than r of some member of S. This is the principle that maximal separated sets give covers.
For a uniformly random vertex U and any fixed vertex s, the mismatch indicators in different coordinates are independent Bernoulli random variables with success probability 1/2. Hence ρ(U,s) has the binomial distribution with parameters n,1/2. The lower-tail Hoeffding inequality gives
P(ρ(U,s)≤8n​)=P(ρ(U,s)−2n​≤−83n​)≤e−9n/32.
(24)
Thus the Hoeffding lower-tail bound for Hamming balls shows that every strict-radius ball B<r​(s) has at most 2ne−9n/32 vertices. Using a strict ball handles noninteger n/8 without changing the required separation.
The strict balls centered at S cover the entire Hamming cube. Counting their union by the sum of their sizes yields
2n≤∑s∈S​∣B<r​(s)∣≤∣S∣2ne−9n/32,∣S∣≥e9n/32≥en/4.
(25)
Therefore
∣S∣≥en/4,s=s′∈Smin​ρ(s,s′)≥n/8.​
(26)
In particular this proves the assertion for every n≥8. Maximality here means that no further vertex can be added; finding a largest separated set is unnecessary.
