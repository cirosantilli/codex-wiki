a OurBigBook.com
Each bond of the classical Heisenberg antiferromagnet is minimized when its two spins are antiparallel. Since the periodic chain has an even number of sites, all bonds can be minimized simultaneously:
Sn​=(−1)nSn,∣n∣=1,Ecl​=−NJS2​.
(1)
The ground-state degeneracy is a continuous sphere of orientations n∈S2. Choose the Néel state with even sites in ∣S,S⟩ and odd sites in ∣S,−S⟩.
This product state is not an energy eigenstate. The spin ladder operators in
Sn​⋅Sn+1​=Snz​Sn+1z​+21​(Sn+​Sn+1−​+Sn−​Sn+1+​)
(2)
produce states with both neighboring magnetic quantum numbers changed. On an up-down bond, Sn−​Sn+1+​ has a nonzero matrix element 2S, hence coefficient JS in the Hamiltonian. These orthogonal spin configurations show explicitly why the classical minimum is not an exact quantum eigenstate for S>0.

b OurBigBook.com
Make a bipartite spin rotation: rotate every odd site's spin through π about the x axis, using U=∏n odd​e−iπSnx​. This unitary conjugation preserves the spin commutation relations. At an odd site it sends (Sx,Sy,Sz) to (Sx,−Sy,−Sz) and exchanges the spin raising operator and spin lowering operator.
Each bond joins one rotated and one unrotated site. In the transformed operators,
Sn​⋅Sn+1​⟼Snx​Sn+1x​−Sny​Sn+1y​−Snz​Sn+1z​=−Snz​Sn+1z​+21​(Sn+​Sn+1+​+Sn−​Sn+1−​).
(3)
Thus
H=−Jn∑​[Snz​Sn+1z​−21​(Sn+​Sn+1+​+Sn−​Sn+1−​)]​.
(4)
The selected Néel state becomes an all-up reference state, so a single Holstein–Primakoff transformation convention works on both sublattices.

Use the Holstein–Primakoff transformation on the physical occupation number subspace 0≤n≤2S. With the canonical commutation relation [a,a†]=1, the ordered square root gives
S+∣n⟩=n(2S−n+1)​∣n−1⟩,S−∣n⟩=(n+1)(2S−n)​∣n+1⟩.
(5)
Both endpoints are respected: S+∣0⟩=0 and S−∣2S⟩=0. Consequently,
[S+,S−]∣n⟩=[(n+1)(2S−n)−n(2S−n+1)]∣n⟩=2(S−n)∣n⟩=2Sz∣n⟩.
(6)
Since these Fock states form a basis of the physical spin space, [S+,S−]=2Sz there. Similarly [Sz,S±]=±S±. The Holstein–Primakoff occupation constraint is essential: unrestricted bosonic occupation would not represent a spin-S Hilbert space.

The linear spin-wave approximation expands about the rotated all-up state, with nn​=an†​an​:
Snz​=S−nn​,Sn+​=2S​an​+O(S−1/2),Sn−​=2S​an†​+O(S−1/2).
(7)

H2​=−NJS2+JSn∑​[2an†​an​+an​an+1​+an†​an+1†​]​.
(8)
The omitted terms are of order S0 at fixed small occupation number. The pair terms describe the quantum fluctuations that were missing from the classical Néel state.

Apply a discrete Fourier transform, with lattice spacing one:
an​=N​1​∑k​ak​eikn,k=N2πm​(mod2π).
(9)
The wavevectors lie in a Brillouin zone, which we choose as [−π,π). Pairing k with −k makes the quadratic Hamiltonian
H2​=−NJS2+∑k​[Aak†​ak​+2Bk​​(ak​a−k​+ak†​a−k†​)],A=2JS,Bk​=2JScosk.
(10)
A bosonic Bogoliubov diagonalization uses ak​=coshθk​αk​−sinhθk​α−k†​. The canonical commutation relations hold because cosh2θk​−sinh2θk​=1. The anomalous terms vanish when tanh2θk​=Bk​/A=cosk, giving
H2​=E0​+∑k​ωk​αk†​αk​,E0​=−NJS2+21​∑k​(ωk​−A),ωk​=A2−Bk2​​=2JS∣sink∣​.
(11)
Near k=0 and k=π, the dispersion relation is linear: ωk​∼2JS∣k−k0​∣. These are the low-energy antiferromagnetic spin waves; the two zeroes are related by the two-sublattice description. The transformation is singular at the exact zero modes, which require an infrared regulator or separate treatment of the collective rotation.

Under the bipartite spin rotation, the staggered magnetization becomes the uniform transformed Sz. In the Bogoliubov transformation vacuum αk​∣0α​⟩=0,
⟨ak†​ak​⟩=sinh2θk​=21​(ωk​A​−1)=21​(∣sink∣1​−1).
(12)
The quantum depletion of Néel order therefore gives, with the zero modes regulated,
Ms​=S−N1​k∑​sinh2θk​ ⟶ S−∫−ππ​2πdk​21​(∣sink∣1​−1)​.
(13)
Near each zero of sink, the integrand behaves as 1/(2∣k−k0​∣), producing a logarithmic divergence. With a finite-size cutoff of order N−1, the depletion grows as π−1logN+O(1). Thus the large-S expansion about a state with finite Néel order is not self-consistent in the infinite one-dimensional chain. The divergent expression is not a negative physical magnetization; it signals breakdown of that ordered approximation. It does not determine whether the exact excitation spectrum is gapped.

The canonical anticommutation relations are preserved precisely when the real matrix acting on (γk↑​,γ−k↓†​) is orthogonal. Directly,
{ck↑​,ck↑†​}=uk2​+vk2​,{ck↑​,c−k↓​}=−uk​vk​+vk​uk​=0.
(16)
The other relations are identical or vanish between disjoint pairs. Hence uk2​+vk2​=1, and one may write uk​=cosθk​, vk​=sinθk​. This fermionic Bogoliubov transformation uses an ordinary rotation; bosonic mixing instead preserves a difference of squared coefficients.

For real Δk​ and ξ−k​=ξk​, introduce the particle-hole column Ψk​=(ck↑​,c−k↓†​)T. Its quadratic block is the Bogoliubov--de Gennes Hamiltonian
Kk​=Ψk†​(ξk​−Δk​​−Δk​−ξk​​)Ψk​+ξk​.
(17)
The BCS coherence factors can be chosen to obey
uk2​=21​(1+Ek​ξk​​),vk2​=21​(1−Ek​ξk​​),2uk​vk​=Ek​Δk​​,Ek​=ξk2​+Δk2​​.
(18)
With uk​≥0, choose the sign of vk​ to match Δk​. The rotation diagonalizes the matrix to diag(Ek​,−Ek​). Reordering γ−k↓​γ−k↓†​=1−γ−k↓†​γ−k↓​ and restoring the mean-field constant gives
K=k,σ∑​Ek​γkσ†​γkσ​+k∑​(ξk​−Ek​+Δk​⟨bk†​⟩)​.
(19)
The inverse transformation is γk↑​=uk​ck↑​−vk​c−k↓†​ and γ−k↓​=vk​ck↑†​+uk​c−k↓​. Both annihilate (uk​+vk​bk†​)∣0⟩. Since every positive-energy quasiparticle mode is empty, the normalized BCS ground state is
∣g.s.⟩=k∏​(cosθk​+sinθk​ck↑†​c−k↓†​)∣0⟩​.
(20)
Each momentum label refers to the pair (k↑,−k↓) once; distinct labels use disjoint single-particle spin states.

In each factor of the BCS ground state, the BCS anomalous average is ⟨bk​⟩=uk​vk​=Δk​/(2Ek​). Self-consistency therefore gives the zero-temperature BCS gap equation
Δk​=−k′∑​Vk,k′​2ξk′2​+∣Δk′​∣2​Δk′​​​.
(21)
Put ΩD​=ℏωD​. The specified constant attractive interaction makes Δk​ independent of k inside the energy shell and zero outside it:
Δk​=Δ1{∣ξk​∣<ΩD​}​​.
(22)
The printed δk,0​ is incompatible with this interaction: k labels relative pair momentum, not the total momentum of a Cooper pair. Every pair here has zero total momentum, while many relative momenta contribute.
For the nonzero solution, the constant-shell BCS gap equation becomes 1=(V/L3)∑∣ξk​∣<ΩD​​(2ξk2​+Δ2​)−1. Let νs​ denote the approximately constant single-spin density of states per unit volume at the Fermi level. Then
1=νs​V∫0ΩD​​ξ2+Δ2​dξ​=νs​VarsinhΔΩD​​,Δ=sinh(1/(νs​V))ℏωD​​​.
(23)
In weak coupling, Δ≃2ℏωD​e−1/(νs​V). The displayed answer in the question uses ν=νs​. If “total electronic density of states” includes both spin species, νtot​=2νs​, the argument instead reads 2/(νtot​V). If the density counts the whole box rather than unit volume, divide it by L3 before using this formula.

Use unfolding a reflected interval trajectory: reflect the interval across each wall so that a bouncing trajectory becomes a free trajectory in an image interval. A return path starting at q ends at q+2rL after an even number of reflections, or at −q+2rL after an odd number. Each hard-wall reflection contributes phase −1, giving the positive direct images and negative reflected images of the Dirichlet heat kernel on an interval.
The method of images therefore gives
Z=∫0L​dq∑r∈Z​[∫q(0)=qq(β)=q+2rL​Dqe−A[q]−∫q(0)=qq(β)=−q+2rL​Dqe−A[q]],A[q]=2ℏ2m​∫0β​(∂τ​q)2dτ.
(27)
Here τ has inverse-energy units, since β=1/(kB​T). In physical imaginary time u=ℏτ, the same dimensionless action is A=SE​/ℏ, with SE​=∫0ℏβ​(m/2)(dq/du)2du. Thus the question's convention S=(m/(2ℏ))∫0β​(∂τ​q)2dτ has exactly the weight e−S/ℏ.

C∑r​∫0L​e−2m(rL−q)2/(ℏ2β)dq=C∫−∞∞​e−2mx2/(ℏ2β)dx=21​.

The exponential acts on the Fock vacuum: ∣ψ⟩=eζ∑n​ψn​an†​∣0⟩. For bosons, ψn​ are commuting complex numbers. Since [an​,∑m​ψm​am†​]=ψn​, commuting an​ through the exponential gives an​∣ψ⟩=ψn​∣ψ⟩. These are unnormalized bosonic coherent states.
For fermions, the labels are independent odd Grassmann variables, which anticommute with one another and with the fermionic operators. For one mode, the fermionic coherent state is ∣ψ⟩=(1−ψa†)∣0⟩. The canonical anticommutation relations give a(−ψa†)∣0⟩=ψ∣0⟩=ψ∣ψ⟩, using ψ2=0. The even factors 1−ψn​an†​ commute between modes, so the same argument applies to every n. A Grassmann eigenvalue is a formal extension of the state space, not an ordinary complex eigenvalue of the nilpotent annihilation operator.
With dual state ⟨ψ∣=⟨0∣eζ∑n​an​ψˉ​n​, both cases obey ⟨ψ∣ψ′⟩=e∑n​ψˉ​n​ψn′​.

Write O=∫d[ψˉ​,ψ]e−∑ψˉ​ψ∣ψ⟩⟨ψ∣. For bosons the measure is ∏n​dReψn​dImψn​/π over C in each mode, with ψˉ​n​=ψn∗​. For fermions it is an ordered Berezin integral over independent ψˉ​n​,ψn​; choose ∫dψˉ​dψψˉ​ψ=−1, so ∫dψˉ​dψe−ψˉ​ψ=1.
For one bosonic mode, a∣ψ⟩=ψ∣ψ⟩ and ⟨ψ∣a=∂ψˉ​​⟨ψ∣. Integration by parts in the Gaussian measure gives aO=Oa; the conjugate argument gives a†O=Oa†. Boundary terms vanish because of the Gaussian weight.
For one fermionic mode, put n=a†a and move Grassmann coefficients to the left. The weighted projector is
W=e−ψˉ​ψ∣ψ⟩⟨ψ∣=(1−n)−ψa†+ψˉ​a−ψˉ​ψI.
(33)
Its ordinary commutators are [a,W]=−a+ψI and [a†,W]=a†−ψˉ​I. Their Berezin integrals vanish, so again [a,O]=[a†,O]=0. Equivalently the sole surviving coefficient in ∫W is −ψˉ​ψI, giving I directly.
The modes factorize. In the irreducible Fock space representation, commuting with every creation and annihilation operator makes O a scalar multiple of the identity. Its vacuum matrix element is the normalized Gaussian integral, equal to one. Thus the coherent-state resolution of identity is
∫d[ψˉ​,ψ]e−∑n​ψˉ​n​ψn​∣ψ⟩⟨ψ∣=I​.
(34)
For infinitely many modes, this argument first uses a finite-mode regulator.

⟨ψj+1​∣e−ϵK∣ψj​⟩=exp[∑n​ψˉ​n,j+1​ψn,j​−ϵK(ψˉ​j+1​,ψj​)+O(ϵ2)].

For the quantum harmonic oscillator, set μ=0 and Ω=ℏω. The normal symbol is HN​(ψˉ​,ψ)=Ω(ψˉ​ψ+1/2), so the thermal coherent-state time slicing gives
Z=∫periodic​D(ψˉ​,ψ)exp[−∫0β​(ψˉ​∂τ​ψ+Ωψˉ​ψ+Ω/2)dτ].
(39)
The connection with a phase-space path integral uses the canonical real coordinates
ψ=2ℏ​mω​q+ip/mω​​,ψˉ​=2ℏ​mω​q−ip/mω​​.
(40)
Their derivative term satisfies ψˉ​ψ˙​=−ipq˙​/ℏ up to total derivatives that vanish for periodic paths. The normal and Weyl symbols of a harmonic oscillator must be distinguished: in midpoint phase-space time slicing, the Weyl ordering symbol of a†a is ψˉ​ψ−1/2. Thus the appropriate midpoint Hamiltonian is HW​=p2/(2m)+mω2q2/2, with no additional constant. The change from the adjacent-label normal prescription to the midpoint prescription includes this ordering correction.
In physical imaginary time u=ℏτ, the resulting phase-space path integral is
Z=∫periodic​DqDpexp[−ℏ1​∫0ℏβ​(−ipdudq​+2mp2​+2mω2q2​)du].
(41)
Gaussian momentum integration in a phase-space path integral produces the usual oscillator configuration-space path integral. An exact check follows directly from the coherent kernel ⟨ψ′∣e−βΩa†a∣ψ⟩=exp(e−βΩψˉ​′ψ):
Z=e−βΩ/2∫C​πd2ψ​e−(1−e−βΩ)∣ψ∣2=1−e−βℏωe−βℏω/2​=2sinh(βℏω/2)1​​.
(42)
This is the thermal partition function of a quantum harmonic oscillator. Keeping the Ω/2 normal-ordering constant a second time after switching to the Weyl symbol would double count the zero-point energy.
