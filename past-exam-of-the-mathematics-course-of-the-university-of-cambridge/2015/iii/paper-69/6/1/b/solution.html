Use the convention that the inner product is conjugate-linear in its first argument; the real case is the same without conjugates. Linear independence makes the Gram matrix G positive definite, so B=G−1 exists and is Hermitian. Define the Riesz dual basis in an inner product space within Un​ by
uk​=∑l=1n​blk​ul​.
(34)
Then (ui​,uk​)=∑l​Gil​blk​=δik​. Its own Gram entries are
(uj​,uk​)=∑l,m​blj​​Glm​bmk​=(B∗GB)jk​=Bjk​.
(35)
Hence the inverse Gram matrix is the Gram matrix of the dual vectors:
bjk​=(uj​,uk​).​
(36)
The dual vectors are required to lie in Un​; adding arbitrary vectors orthogonal to that subspace would preserve the displayed biorthogonality but would destroy this conclusion.
