Put e=x−u∗. If u∗ minimizes distance, then for every v∈Un​ and every real t,
∥e−tv∥2=∥e∥2−2tRe(e,v)+t2∥v∥2≥∥e∥2.
(31)
Both signs of arbitrarily small t force Re(e,v)=0. In a complex inner product space, apply the same argument to iv to obtain the imaginary part too. Conversely, if e is orthogonal to the subspace, the Pythagorean theorem for inner product spaces gives
∥x−u∥2=∥x−u∗∥2+∥u−u∗∥2(u∈Un​).
(32)
Thus orthogonality characterizes the unique best approximation:
u∗ is best⟺(x−u∗,v)=0(v∈Un​).​
(33)
Finite dimension also guarantees existence by solving the invertible Gram matrix normal equations; completeness of the ambient inner product space is unnecessary.
