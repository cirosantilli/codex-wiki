Introduce the slow time T=ϵt and use the method of multiple scales with x0​=A(T)cosψ, ψ=t+θ(T). The initial data give A(0)=2​ and θ(0)=−π/4. At first order the equation for the correction is
x1,tt​+x1​=2A′sinψ+2Aθ′cosψ+Af(Acosψ)sinψ.
(9)
The averaged amplitude for position-dependent damping follows from the solvability condition in the method of multiple scales, which removes the resonant sine and cosine terms. With brackets denoting a full-period average, this gives
A′=−A⟨f(Acosψ)sin2ψ⟩,θ′=0.
(10)
The wave phase average vanishes because f(Acosψ)sinψcosψ changes sign under ψ↦2π−ψ.
For the quadratic position-dependent damping, ⟨cos2ψsin2ψ⟩=1/8 and ⟨sin2ψ⟩=1/2, so
A′=2A​−8A3​,(A2)′=A2−4A4​.
(11)
Solving this logistic equation with A2(0)=2 gives A(T)=2/1+e−T​. Thus the uniformly valid leading approximation is
x(t;ϵ)=1+e−ϵt​2cos(t−π/4)​+O(ϵ),0≤t≤T∗​/ϵ.​
(12)
Here T∗​ is any fixed finite positive constant. Bounded nonresonant corrections and the averaging estimate make the error uniform on this slow-time interval. The leading expression meets the position datum exactly and the velocity datum to leading order; the first-order correction supplies its O(ϵ) adjustment.
