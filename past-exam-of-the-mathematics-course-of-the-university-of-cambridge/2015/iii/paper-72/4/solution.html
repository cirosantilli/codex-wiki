Take α>0; a real disturbance with negative wavenumber is recovered by complex conjugation. To obtain the pressure equation for a shear-flow normal mode, apply iα to the streamwise momentum equation and differentiate the normal momentum equation. The mass conservation equation cancels the viscous divergence and the terms proportional to U−c, leaving 2iαU′v=α2p−p′′. Thus the fluid pressure equation and wall value are
p′′−α2p=−2iαU′v,p(0)=iαRu′′(0)​=α2Rv(3)(0)​.​
(26)
The wall value follows from streamwise momentum with no-slip boundary condition. Normal momentum also gives p′(0)=v′′(0)/R.
In the lower viscous boundary layer, mass conservation gives v=O(αδ) if u=O(1). Since U∼y, convection and shear have size αδ when c=O(δ); matching these with transverse viscous diffusion gives R−1δ−2∼αδ. The pressure gradient balance then gives p=O(δ). Thus the lower scalings are
δ=(αR)−1/3,c=δC,v=αδV,p=δP.​
(27)
The neglected streamwise viscous term is smaller by (αδ)2. In these variables the leading equations are
VY​=−iu,i(Y−C)u+V=−iP+uYY​,PY​=0.
(28)
Differentiating the second and using the first yields the Airy reduction uYYY​=i(Y−C)uY​​.
Put q=eiπ/6, so q3=i. The bounded matching branch of the Airy function gives uY​=AAi(q(Y−C)); the other independent Airy solution grows along the ray and is excluded. Integrating with the two wall conditions gives
u(Y)=A∫0Y​Ai(q(s−C))ds,V(Y)=−iA∫0Y​(Y−s)Ai(q(s−C))ds,
(29)
while the wall momentum equation gives the constant fluid pressure
P0​=−iAqAi′(−qC).
(30)
Define the convergent Airy displacement integrals
I0​(C)=∫0∞​Ai(q(s−C))ds,I1​(C)=∫0∞​sAi(q(s−C))ds.
(31)
The Airy function equation and its decaying derivative imply I1​−CI0​=iqAi′(−qC). Consequently, as Y→∞,
u→B=AI0​,P→P0​=A(CI0​−I1​),V=−iBY+iAI1​+o(1).​
(32)
Both differentiated lower momentum and the constant of integration are satisfied; retaining that constant is essential for the final matching.
For the middle region introduce a perturbation stream function Φ with u=Φ′ and v=−iαΦ. The leading inviscid flow streamwise equation is
(U−c)Φ′−U′Φ=−p,(U−cΦ​)′=−(U−c)2p​.
(33)
For a shear flow, this is the integrated form of the Rayleigh equation for inviscid shear flow. The leading matched solution for fixed y=O(1) is Φ∼BU, so u∼BU′ and v∼−iαBU. In the overlap with the lower viscous boundary layer, the first constant correction is
Φ∼B(y−c)+δP0​=By−δAI1​,
(34)
which agrees with the integrated lower solution. For a regular smooth profile, the normal momentum equation gives p′∼−α2(U−c)Φ. Thus fluid pressure variation across a fixed-width middle region is O(α2B); in the final distinguished scaling this is smaller than its O(δ) value, so p∼δP0​ throughout that region. Since U→1, the middle velocity tends to v∼−iαB​, which does not decay and requires another region.
The fluid pressure equation becomes p′′−α2p=0 when U∼1. Therefore the upper scale is Δ=1/α, with z=αy. The inviscid decaying upper solution is
p=P∞​e−z,v=−1−ciP∞​​e−z,u=−1−cP∞​​e−z.​
(35)
Matching velocity as z↓0 gives P∞​∼αB; matching fluid pressure to the middle region gives δP0​∼αB. This is three-layer long-wave shear-flow matching, expressed as a matched asymptotic expansion.
Under α=kR−1/4 with fixed positive k, δ=k−1/3R−1/4, and α/δ=k4/3. Eliminating the nonzero amplitude A gives the Airy wall-layer matching determinant and dispersion relation
k4/3I0​(C)+iqAi′(−qC)=0.​
(36)
Equivalently, with T(C)=∫−qC∞eiπ/6​Ai(ζ)dζ=qI0​(C),
k4/3T(C)=e−iπ/6Ai′(−qC).​
(37)
The determinant form does not divide by a possibly vanishing I0​. The wavespeed is c=δC; temporal growth has sign ImC for positive α.
There is a genuine conflict in the printed assumptions: fixed-k α=kR−1/4 cannot satisfy R−1/7≪α as R→∞. For order-one lower matching amplitudes, constant leading middle fluid pressure requires α2≪δ, equivalently α≪R−1/7; this is the reverse inequality and is satisfied by the final distinguished scaling. Thinness also requires αR≫1. The dispersion relation above is therefore derived under the final distinguished limit, for which the complete consistent window is R−1≪α≪R−1/7. Both printed limits cannot be imposed simultaneously.
