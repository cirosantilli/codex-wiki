We establish a quantitative almost global existence for wave equations estimate. Take 0<ε≤1, fix an integer m≥6, and use the commutation vector fields for the wave equation from the preceding part. Put ∂=(∂t​,∇) and define the commuted wave energy
Am​(t)=∑∣I∣≤m​∥∂ZIϕ(t)∥2​.
(21)
All these L2 norms are finite on any smooth existence interval by finite propagation speed. At t=0, the polynomial coefficients of the vector fields are bounded on the fixed compact support of the Cauchy data. Whenever a higher time derivative occurs, use ϕtt​=Δϕ−ϕt2​ and its differentiated versions to express it in terms of initial spatial derivatives. Every term contains at least one factor of ε; consequently
Am​(0)≤C0​ε
(22)
for a constant depending only on finitely many derivatives and the support radius of ϕ0​,ϕ1​.
The commutators [Z,∂μ​] are constant linear combinations of translations. Together with [□,S]=2□ and the Leibniz rule, this shows that each commuted source is a finite linear combination of products
∂μ​ZJϕ∂ν​ZKϕ,∣J∣+∣K∣≤∣I∣.
(23)
This statement includes the extra copies of the original source produced by the scaling vector field. In each product put the factor with fewer commutations in the Lp norm L∞ and the other in the L2 norm. The lower order is at most ⌊m/2⌋. Applying the Klainerman-Sobolev inequality to ∂μ​ZJϕ costs at most two additional commutations; commuting those past ∂μ​ introduces only lower-order translations. Since ⌊m/2⌋+2≤m,
∑∣I∣≤m​∥□ZIϕ(t)∥2​≤1+tCm​​Am​(t)2.
(24)
The inhomogeneous wave energy estimate now gives
Am​(t)≤C0​ε+Cm​∫0t​1+sAm​(s)2​ds.
(25)
Let Tε​=ε−N. Use a bootstrap argument with Am​(t)≤2C0​ε up to the smaller of Tε​ and the maximal existence time. The energy estimate improves this to
Am​(t)≤C0​ε+4Cm​C02​ε2log(1+Tε​).
(26)
For each fixed N,
εlog(1+ε−N)⟶0asε↓0.
(27)
Choose εN​≤1 so that 4Cm​C0​εlog(1+ε−N)≤1/2 for every 0<ε≤εN​. Then Am​(t)≤(3/2)C0​ε, a strict improvement. A continuity argument closes the bootstrap argument.
Finally, the translation terms in Am​ control ordinary spatial Sobolev norms of ∂ϕ. The missing L2 norm of ϕ satisfies
∥ϕ(t)∥2​≤ε∥ϕ0​∥2​+∫0t​∥ϕt​(s)∥2​ds.
(28)
Thus the full local-existence Sobolev norms remain bounded on this finite interval. The smooth continuation criterion for semilinear wave equations extends the solution past any finite endpoint before Tε​. To see smooth persistence explicitly, the tame Sobolev product estimate gives ∥ϕt2​∥Hk​≤Ck​∥ϕt​∥∞​∥ϕt​∥Hk​. Ordinary differentiated wave energy estimates therefore bound each higher derivative energy by its initial value times exp(Ck​∫0t​∥ϕt​(s)∥∞​ds). This is finite on the interval already controlled by the base commuted wave energy; no separate εN​ is needed for each derivative order. Therefore
the solution is smooth on [0,ε−N] for 0<ε≤εN​.​
(29)
For ε=0 the zero solution is global. The same energy estimate in fact permits an exponential lower bound for the lifespan, which is stronger than any fixed inverse power.
