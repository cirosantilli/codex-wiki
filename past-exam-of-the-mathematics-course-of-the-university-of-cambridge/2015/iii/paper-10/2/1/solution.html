One version of the Klainerman-Sobolev inequality, for a sufficiently decaying smooth function ψ on R1+3, is
∣ψ(t,x)∣≤(1+t+∣x∣)(1+∣t−∣x∣∣)1/2C​∣I∣≤2∑​∥ZIψ(t,⋅)∥L2(R3)​​,t≥0.
(17)
Here I denotes a word of length ∣I∣ in the following eleven commutation vector fields for the wave equation:
​∂t​, ∂1​, ∂2​, ∂3​,Ωij​=xi​∂j​−xj​∂i​(1≤i<j≤3),Li​=t∂i​+xi​∂t​(1≤i≤3),S=t∂t​+i=1∑3​xi​∂i​,​​spacetime translations,spatial rotations,Lorentz boosts,scaling.​
(18)
The first four are spacetime translation vector fields; the next three are spatial rotation vector fields; the next three are Lorentz boost vector fields; and S is the scaling vector field. The vector field method for wave equations uses these vector fields because their commutators with the d'Alembert operator obey
[□,Z]=0(Z=S),[□,S]=2□.
(19)
In particular the Klainerman-Sobolev inequality implies
∥ψ(t,⋅)∥∞​≤1+tC​∑∣I∣≤2​∥ZIψ(t,⋅)∥2​.
(20)
The displayed L2 norms are spatial norms at fixed time; the spacetime vector fields can contain time derivatives. No wave equation assumption is needed for the Klainerman-Sobolev inequality itself.
