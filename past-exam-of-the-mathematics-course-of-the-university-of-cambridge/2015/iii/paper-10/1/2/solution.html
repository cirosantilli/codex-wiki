We prove finite propagation speed using a shrinking cone energy argument. Fix T>0 and x∗​, and suppose the Cauchy data vanish on B(x∗​,T). For 0≤s<T, define the local wave energy
e=21​(ϕt2​+∣∇ϕ∣2),E(s)=∫B(x∗​,T−s)​e(s,x)dx.

The homogeneous wave equation gives the local conservation law
∂t​e=∇⋅(ϕt​∇ϕ).

Differentiate the integral over the moving ball. Its boundary moves inward with speed one, so the divergence theorem gives
E′(s)=∫∂B(x∗​,T−s)​(ϕt​∂n​ϕ−e)dS=−21​∫∂B(x∗​,T−s)​((ϕt​−∂n​ϕ)2+∣∇tan​ϕ∣2)dS≤0.

Here n is the outward unit normal, ∂n​ the normal derivative, and ∇tan​ the component of the gradient tangent to the boundary. Since E(0)=0 and e≥0, we have E(s)=0. Hence both ϕt​ and ∇ϕ vanish inside the backward light cone. Integrating ϕt​(s,x∗​)=0 from the zero initial displacement gives ϕ(s,x∗​)=0; continuity then gives ϕ(T,x∗​)=0.
Applying the same energy estimate to the difference of two solutions proves the domain of dependence assertion. If dist(x∗​,K)>T, the initial ball misses K, and the preceding argument proves the stated support bound. No disturbance propagates faster than one.
