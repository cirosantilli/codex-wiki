For the wave equation with d'Alembert operator □=−∂t2​+Δ, the wave speed is one. The domain of dependence of (t,x) is the initial ball B(x,t). More precisely, two solutions whose Cauchy data agree on a neighbourhood of that ball have the same value at (t,x). Thus, if both Cauchy data have support in K, finite propagation speed gives
suppϕ(t,⋅)⊆{x:dist(x,K)≤t},t≥0.
(1)
The Strong Huygens principle is sharper in three spatial dimensions: only the initial data on the sphere ∣y−x∣=t contribute, including the first spatial derivatives of the initial displacement there. The Kirchhoff formula makes this precise:
ϕ(t,x)=∂t​(4πt​∫S2​ϕ0​(x+tω)dω)+4πt​∫S2​ϕ1​(x+tω)dω.
(2)
In particular, if the Cauchy data have compact support in B(0,R), then
ϕ(t,x)=0whenever​∣x∣−t​>R.​
(3)
For t>R, the solution therefore vanishes behind the inward edge ∣x∣=t−R as well as outside ∣x∣=t+R. The absence of an interior tail is the additional content of the Strong Huygens principle.
