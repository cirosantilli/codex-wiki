The Inviscid Burgers equation here has the negative quadratic flux F(f)=−f2/2. By the method of characteristics, df/dZ=0 and dθ/dZ=−f, so a characteristic beginning at θ0​ satisfies
θ=θ0​−f0​(θ0​)Z,f(Z,θ)=f0​(θ0​).​
(28)
This is a classical solution until characteristic curves intersect. Across a shock wave, the Rankine-Hugoniot condition gives
θs′​=fR​−fL​F(fR​)−F(fL​)​=−2fR​+fL​​.​
(29)
It uses the one-sided limiting values and agrees with the weak-shock expression.
For U>0, the right-state characteristic curves travel left with speed −U while the left-state characteristic curves have speed zero. They converge into a compressive shock wave with speed −U/2. The entropy solution is
f(Z,θ)={0,U,​θ<−UZ/2,θ>−UZ/2.​(U>0).​
(30)
The characteristic speeds satisfy 0>−U/2>−U, so both sides enter the shock.
For U<0, right-state characteristic curves travel right with speed −U>0, leaving a gap. A steep continuous transition fills that gap with a rarefaction wave. The entropy solution is
f(Z,θ)=⎩⎨⎧​0,−θ/Z,U,​θ<0,0<θ<−UZ,θ>−UZ.​(U<0).​
(31)
The middle value is fixed by θ/Z=F′(f)=−f. A discontinuity joining the two states would satisfy the jump equation but violate entropy admissibility. If U=0, the solution is identically zero.
