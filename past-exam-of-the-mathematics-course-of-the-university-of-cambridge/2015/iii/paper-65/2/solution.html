For the ellipse, the orbital eccentricity satisfies 0≤e<1. Its extreme separations are rp​=l/(1+e) and ra​=l/(1−e). The semi-major axis is half their sum, giving a=l/(1−e2) and thus l=a(1−e2). The true anomaly θ runs through a full 2π; the endpoints are the same position, and for a circular Kepler orbit the angular origin is arbitrary.
The radial and transverse relative speeds are r˙=(GM/h)esinθ and rθ˙=h/r. Substituting these into the specific orbital energy gives

The negative specific orbital energy and fixed angular momentum characterize the bound Kepler orbit; neither should be assumed unchanged through a mass-ejecting explosion.
Initially the circular Kepler orbit has r=a and v2=GM/a. Treat the supernova kick in a binary star as impulsive: the relative position does not change, the companion's velocity is unchanged during the impulse, and the new neutron star receives u with ∣u∣=αv. Thus v′=v+u, and with ψ the angle between these vectors before the kick,
∣v′∣2=v2S,S=1+2αcosψ+α2.
(17)
Write M′=M1′​+M2​, β=M′/M and μ′=M1′​M2​/M′. The post-explosion specific orbital energy is
ε′=21​v2S−aGM′​=2aGM​(S−2β).

Using ε′=−GM′/(2a′) gives
a′M′​=a2M′​−aM​(1+2αcosψ+α2).​
(19)
Here a′>0 for a bound Kepler orbit. For a hyperbolic Kepler orbit, this formula uses the signed energy parameter a′<0, rather than the positive geometric magnitude of the hyperbola's semi-major axis. At the parabolic Kepler orbit boundary, 1/a′=0.
The kick-direction binary survival criterion is bound if S<2β and unbound with positive asymptotic speed if S>2β. To prove the printed sufficient disruption condition, choose a perpendicular kick, cosψ=0: then S=1+α2, so M′<21​(1+α2)M produces positive specific orbital energy. This is sufficient, not the sharp existence threshold. Since
(1−α)2≤S≤(1+α)2,

the complete kick-direction binary survival criterion is
some direction gives ε′>0some direction remains bound​⟺β<21​(1+α)2,⟺β>21​(1−α)2.​​
(21)
The second inequality follows by taking a kick directly opposite to v. All directions remain bound if 2β>(1+α)2, while every direction has positive escape energy if 2β<(1−α)2. For α>0, the marginal direction obeys cosψ=(2β−1−α2)/(2α) when the right-hand side lies in [−1,1]. Equalities give marginal parabolic Kepler orbits for the relevant extreme direction. With zero kick, losing more than half the original total mass unbinds the circular Kepler orbit.
For an escaping pair, the Newtonian gravitational potential energy approaches zero at infinite separation. Conservation of the post-explosion two-body orbital energy then gives 21​μ′V2=μ′ε′, or the asymptotic relative speed of a disrupted binary
V=v1+2αcosψ+α2−M2M′​​.​
(22)
This is the relative recession speed, not the velocity of the new center of mass or either star's individual velocity in the original inertial frame. A marginal parabolic Kepler orbit separates with V=0 at infinity.
