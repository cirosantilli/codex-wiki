Let r=∣x∣, ni​=xi​/r, and let the source have size ℓ. The retarded acoustic Green function gives the outgoing solution of Lighthill acoustic analogy as
ρ′(x,t)=4πc02​1​∂xi​​∂xj​​∫∣x−y∣Tij​(y,t−∣x−y∣/c0​)​d3y.
(1)
This follows by integrating the source derivatives by parts in the retarded convolution. Assume the source is localized and the boundary terms vanish. In the acoustic compact-source approximation, k0​ℓ≪1, the retardation across the source can be neglected, while r≫ℓ permits replacement of the denominator by r. The integral becomes Sij​(t−r/c0​)/r.
In the radiation region k0​r≫1, derivatives of the retarded argument dominate derivatives of the spreading factor. Since ∂xi​​(t−r/c0​)=−ni​/c0​, the leading acoustic quadrupole field is
ρ′(x,t)∼4πc04​rni​nj​S¨ij​(t−r/c0​)​=4πc04​r3xi​xj​S¨ij​(t−r/c0​)​.​
(2)
The two negative retardation derivatives give a positive sign. This is a far-field approximation, with smaller near-field terms omitted.
For low-Mach number aerodynamic fluctuations of speed U and advective time ℓ/U, take Tij​=O(ρ0​U2), Sij​=O(ρ0​U2ℓ3), and two time derivatives of order (U/ℓ)2. Therefore
ρ0​ρ′​=O[(c0​U​)4rℓ​]=O(m4ℓ/r).​
(3)
The quoted fourth power is the compact acoustic quadrupole Mach-number scaling, with geometric spreading shown explicitly. It assumes the source strength and time scale just stated; the source must be acoustically compact, and the observation point must remain in the radiation region.

Choose the shock wave normal to point from S<0 to S>0:
n=∣∇S∣∇S​,vn​=v⋅n=−∣∇S∣St​​.
(6)
The latter identity follows by differentiating S(xs​(t),t)=0 along the moving surface. Write [a]=a+−a− and [b]=b+−b−. The distributional derivative of the Heaviside step function is ∂t​H(S)=St​δ(S) and ∇H(S)=∇Sδ(S). The regular terms cancel using the conservation equations on each side, leaving
∂t​a+∇⋅b=([a]St​+[b]⋅∇S)δ(S)=([b]−v[a])⋅n∣∇S∣δ(S).
(7)
Thus the explicitly requested vector is
f=[b]−v[a].​
(8)

Apply the moving-interface conservation jump identity first to mass, then to each momentum component. Write ji​=ρui​ and Πij​=ρui​uj​+pδij​−σij​, with all quantities understood piecewise on the two sides. Define the jumps of flux relative to the moving shock wave by
Q=[ρ(u−v)⋅n],Li​=[ρui​(u−v)⋅n+pni​−σij​nj​].
(9)
The global distributional conservation equations are
∂t​ρ+∂i​ji​=Qδs​,∂t​ji​+∂j​Πij​=Li​δs​.
(10)
Differentiate the first in time and subtract the divergence of the second. With ρ′=ρ−ρ0​ and the piecewise Lighthill stress tensor Tij​=ρui​uj​+(p′−c02​ρ′)δij​−σij​, this gives
(∂t2​−c02​∇2)ρ′=∂i​∂j​Tij​+∂t​(Qδs​)−∂i​(Li​δs​).​
(11)
Derivatives act on the complete distributions, including their moving support. The acoustic quadrupole term represents momentum-stress fluctuations throughout the volume. The time derivative of the surface mass-flux defect is an acoustic monopole, representing injection or removal of mass/volume. The divergence of the surface momentum-flux defect is an acoustic dipole, representing a force sheet. This is the distributional acoustic analogy across a moving interface.
For an actual freely propagating fluid shock wave with no singular mass or momentum supply, the Rankine-Hugoniot conditions give Q=0 and Li​=0. Such a shock does not acquire independent monopole and force-sheet sources merely because it is discontinuous. Its effects remain in the distributional derivatives of Tij​, including singular derivatives of its jump. Nonzero surface sources are appropriate for an interface with exchange/forcing or for a formulation that omits one side of the fluid.
A shock does not, by itself, justify retaining the m4 scaling. That estimate required a low-Mach number stress O(ρ0​U2) varying on the slow time ℓ/U. Fast shock motion, short time scales, or thermodynamic deviations can invalidate that estimate. If these same compact, slow-source assumptions remain valid for the integrated Lighthill stress tensor, its quadrupole estimate still follows, even distributionally. There is no universal replacement power deducible from the mere presence of a shock; nor should vanished physical flux defects be treated as additional independent radiation sources.

Q=−P,P=−γρ0​ω2​η​,[p​]=−γ2ρ0​ω2​η​.

Linearity permits subtraction of the incident acoustic membrane wave. The scattered pressure solves the homogeneous Helmholtz equation, and the kinematic relation defines its line displacement η′ on both halves. On x<0, subtracting the incident elastic membrane equation gives the scattered dynamic equation. On x>0, there is no elastic membrane, so the total pressure jump must vanish, giving [p′]=−[pI​]. The pinned endpoint has η(0)=0, hence η′(0)=−1, rather than zero.
Use full and Half-range Fourier transforms with the same eikx convention as part (a), and put
β=ρ0​ω2,a(k)=mω2−Tk2,A=ηx​(0),κ=k−kI​.
(15)
Write P− for the left transform of the upper scattered pressure, P+ for its right transform, and η± for the corresponding transforms of η′. Outgoing antisymmetry in y implies
η−+η+=−βγ​(P−+P+),P+=κγI​iβ​.
(16)
The second relation comes from the prescribed pressure cancellation on x>0.
The endpoint terms in the left Fourier transform of ηxx′​ are crucial:
∫−∞0​eikxηxx′​dx=ηx′​(0)−ikη′(0)−k2η−=A+i(k+kI​)−k2η−.
(17)
Thus 2P−=aη−+TA+iT(k+kI​). Eliminating η− gives the Wiener-Hopf equation
K(k)P−(k)+η+(k)=a(k)TA​+a(k)iT(k+kI​)​−(k−kI​)γI​iγ(k)​,K(k)=a(k)2​+βγ(k)​.​
(18)
There is a defect in the printed right side: with the stated incident exponential, pressure jump, and pinned total displacement, it omits the incident endpoint term and has the opposite sign on the incident-pressure term. The displayed corrected equation follows directly from both boundary traces. The given right side cannot be derived with these definitions. Using a(kI​)=−2β/γI​, an especially useful equivalent form is
KP−+η+=aTA​−κi​−κγI​iβK​.
(19)
The correction is necessary for the reconstructed pressure to satisfy the original physical boundary conditions.
For Wiener-Hopf factorization, take K=K+K− with the plus factor analytic and nonzero in the upper half-plane and the minus factor analytic and nonzero in the lower half-plane. Singularities listed next refer to continuation out of each factor's own analytic half-plane. Let b=ωm/T​, so a=−T(k−b)(k+b) and b lies below the real axis. Generically K+ inherits the pole at b and the square-root branch point at k0​, while K− inherits the pole at −b and the branch point at −k0​. The kernel is finite but nonanalytic at the acoustic branch points: its local behavior is a constant plus a square-root term, not an inverse-square-root divergence. Coincident branch points and poles require a limiting treatment.
Zeros of K are the fluid-loaded dispersion relation roots. Lower-half-plane roots, including the incoming guided root kI​, belong to the continued K+; upper-half-plane roots, including the reflected root −kI​ for the even kernel, belong to the continued K−. The roots represent membrane-guided modes; the branch cuts represent radiated acoustic waves. Only roots on the selected outgoing sheet are included, not spurious roots created by squaring the dispersion relation. No explicit factorization is required.
Here is an explicit solution in terms of those factors. Define
B(k)=a(k)K+(k)T​,C=2bK+(−b)1​,B−(k)=k+bC​,B+(k)=B(k)−k+bC​.
(20)
The pole of B at −b has residue C; the pole at b is canceled by the pole of K+. Thus B± have the required respective analyticity. Divide the corrected Wiener-Hopf equation by K+ and split its right side as F++F−, where
F−F+​=Ak+bC​−γI​iβ​k−kI​K−(k)−K−(kI​)​,=AB+(k)−(k−kI​)K+(k)i​−(k−kI​)γI​iβK−(kI​)​.​
(21)
The difference quotient in F− is removable at kI​ and is analytic below. Moving the plus and minus terms to opposite sides yields the common entire function. With the assumed E(k)=0, the solution is
P−=K−F−​,η+=K+F+.​
(22)
Combining P− with the known P+ gives the full scattered pressure transform
P−+P+=K−(k)1​[k+bAC​+(k−kI​)γI​iβK−(kI​)​].
(23)
The requested pressure integral, for y=0, is consequently
p(x,y)=pI​(x,y)+2πsgn(y)​∫C​e−ikx−γ(k)∣y∣K−(k)1​[k+bAC​+(k−kI​)γI​iβK−(kI​)​]dk.​
(24)
The real contour C uses the causal continuation Imω<0; its undamped limit retains the induced pole and branch-cut prescriptions. The residue theorem shows that upper-half-plane zeros of K− yield left-going scattered elastic membrane modes. On the right, the lower incident-pole residue of the scattered integral cancels pI​ at the open line, as required.
Finally reconstruct the left scattered displacement:
η−=a(k)2P−−TA−iT(k+kI​)​.​
(25)
For a generic unspecified A, this has a lower-half-plane pole at the bare elastic membrane wavenumber b. Such a pole is incompatible with analyticity of an outgoing left-supported scattered displacement: it would represent an additional right-going incoming elastic membrane contribution. It must be removed. Its residue is −[2P−(b)−TA−iT(b+kI​)]/(2Tb), so the incoming-pole cancellation at a pinned membrane edge condition is
2P−(b)−TA−iT(b+kI​)=0.​
(26)
Since P− is linear in A, this fixes the endpoint slope generically. Explicitly it is
A[T−bK−(b)C​]=(b−kI​)γI​K−(b)2iβK−(kI​)​.
(27)
The physical lower incident pole of the total displacement is already prescribed by ηI​; it must not be confused with this removable spurious pole of the scattered field.

The Inviscid Burgers equation here has the negative quadratic flux F(f)=−f2/2. By the method of characteristics, df/dZ=0 and dθ/dZ=−f, so a characteristic beginning at θ0​ satisfies
θ=θ0​−f0​(θ0​)Z,f(Z,θ)=f0​(θ0​).​
(28)
This is a classical solution until characteristic curves intersect. Across a shock wave, the Rankine-Hugoniot condition gives
θs′​=fR​−fL​F(fR​)−F(fL​)​=−2fR​+fL​​.​
(29)
It uses the one-sided limiting values and agrees with the weak-shock expression.
For U>0, the right-state characteristic curves travel left with speed −U while the left-state characteristic curves have speed zero. They converge into a compressive shock wave with speed −U/2. The entropy solution is
f(Z,θ)={0,U,​θ<−UZ/2,θ>−UZ/2.​(U>0).​
(30)
The characteristic speeds satisfy 0>−U/2>−U, so both sides enter the shock.
For U<0, right-state characteristic curves travel right with speed −U>0, leaving a gap. A steep continuous transition fills that gap with a rarefaction wave. The entropy solution is
f(Z,θ)=⎩⎨⎧​0,−θ/Z,U,​θ<0,0<θ<−UZ,θ>−UZ.​(U<0).​
(31)
The middle value is fixed by θ/Z=F′(f)=−f. A discontinuity joining the two states would satisfy the jump equation but violate entropy admissibility. If U=0, the solution is identically zero.

Let h=logψ and f=2αhθ​. Direct substitution into the viscous Burgers equation gives
fZ​−ffθ​−αfθθ​=2α∂θ​(hZ​−αhθθ​−αhθ2​).
(32)
If ψZ​=αψθθ​, then hZ​=α(hθθ​+hθ2​), proving the Cole-Hopf transformation. A function of Z inside the parentheses can be removed by rescaling ψ by a time-dependent factor, which leaves f unchanged.
For the forward heat equation and the displayed Gaussian kernel we require α>0 and Z>0. The algebraic transformation works for nonzero α, but the printed convolution is not a forward solution for negative α: its Gaussian then grows and the step-data integral diverges.
Integrating the initial logarithmic derivative fixes a convenient positive initial heat datum,
ψ(0,ϕ)={1,eUϕ/(2α),​ϕ<0,ϕ>0.​
(33)
Split the heat kernel convolution at zero and complete the square in the positive half. With h∗​=U(2θ+UZ)/(4α), put
A0​=∫θ∞​e−y2/(4αZ)dy,C0​=∫−(θ+UZ)∞​e−y2/(4αZ)dy.
(34)
Then ψ=(A0​+eh∗​C0​)/4παZ​. On differentiation, the moving-limit terms cancel because eh∗​e−(θ+UZ)2/(4αZ)=e−θ2/(4αZ). Hence
f(Z,θ)=A0​+eh∗​C0​Ueh∗​C0​​=1+Je−U(2θ+UZ)/(4α)U​,J=C0​A0​​.​
(35)
This is the viscous Burgers step solution with negative flux. Both integrals can be written as παZ​ times a complementary error function.
For fixed Z>0, the Gaussian-tail asymptotics give f→U as θ→+∞ and f→0 as θ→−∞. For example Je−h∗​ tends to zero on the right with a Gaussian factor e−(θ+UZ)2/(4αZ), while on the left it diverges with a Gaussian factor eθ2/(4αZ). This holds for either sign of U; for U=0 the solution is already zero.
The Gaussian tail is strictly decreasing in its lower limit, so J=1 occurs exactly at θ=−UZ/2. There h∗​=0, and f=U/2. For U>0, the two lower limits are both far into the negative tail in the mature shock region, so J≃1 throughout its thin transition. The solution is then approximately the traveling viscous front
f≃2U​[1+tanh4αU(θ+UZ/2)​],
(36)
centred at the inviscid shock position, with thickness of order α/U.
In the vanishing-viscosity limit, for U>0 the solution tends to the compressive entropy solution of part (a), away from its shock. For U<0, it tends instead to the rarefaction wave. To see the latter explicitly inside 0<θ<−UZ, both tails have positive lower limits, and their leading asymptotics give Je−h∗​→(−θ−UZ)/θ. Thus f→−θ/Z in the fan, with the constant states outside. Although J=1 still marks the fan midpoint, it is not approximately one throughout the expanding fan; replacing it by one there would create an inadmissible compressive-front approximation.
