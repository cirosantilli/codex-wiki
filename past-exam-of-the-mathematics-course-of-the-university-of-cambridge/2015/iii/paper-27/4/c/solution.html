Take sieve level D=x1/3 and coefficient cutoff L=D​=x1/6. Let P be the finite set of odd primes at most L. Give n=p+2 one unit of weight for each prime p≤x. For an odd squarefree integer d on these primes, the sieve distribution is

The residue −2 is coprime to every such d, and g(p)=1/(p−1)∈(0,1) for all permitted primes. Values of g outside the permitted set can be chosen arbitrarily in (0,1) at primes and extended multiplicatively on squarefree integers.

Here is a direct proof of this lower bound. Restrict the Euler product to 3≤p≤y=L1/4. The Mertens first theorem gives ∑3≤p≤y​logp/(p−1)=logy+O(1), which is at most 21​logL for large L. The Mertens second theorem gives

The truncated Euler-product lower bound, with θ=1/2, now gives J≥W/2≫logL.

The first factor uses the Bombieri–Vinogradov theorem; D=x1/3 is below x1/2/logBx for all sufficiently large x. The second uses the permitted ∑q≤D​9ω(q)/ϕ(q)≪log9D. The PDF has the exponent nine, which the TeX transcribes incorrectly. Choose A=14 to obtain O(x/log3x).
Every twin prime pair with p+2>L survives the finite sieve, while the pairs with p+2≤L number at most L. The Selberg upper-bound sieve thus gives
#{p≤x:p,p+2 prime}≤L+JX​+O(log3xx​)≪log2xx​.
(37)
Therefore the twin-prime count is O(x/log2x). The finite cutoff on the forbidden primes is essential: sieving by all odd primes would also discard the large prime values p+2 that we are trying to count.
