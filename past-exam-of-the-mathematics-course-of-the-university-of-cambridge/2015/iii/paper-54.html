A regular central expansion of the TOV equations requires m(0)=0. Once ρc​ is chosen, the barotropic stellar equation of state fixes pc​=p(ρc​). Smoothness at the centre forces the radial first derivatives of p and ρ to vanish. Expanding the Tolman–Oppenheimer–Volkoff equation gives
m(r)p(r)Φ(r)​=34π​ρc​r3+O(r5),=pc​−2π(ρc​+pc​)(3ρc​​+pc​)r2+O(r4),=Φc​+2π(3ρc​​+pc​)r2+O(r4).​
(3)
Since dp/dρ>0, the equation of state is locally invertible, and these coefficients determine the corresponding energy density expansion as well. Integrating the regular solution outward gives the surface at the first zero of the pressure, when such a finite surface exists. The ordinary differential equation uniqueness theorem then fixes the interior and its exterior mass from ρc​.
The additive constant Φc​ is a time-coordinate normalization, not a second physical parameter. Matching to the Schwarzschild metric with time normalized at infinity fixes it. Therefore the regular stellar solutions, modulo time rescaling, have one physical parameter: ρc​. This statement uses a suitably smooth fixed equation of state and refers to central densities for which the solution actually forms a regular star; not every central energy density need give a finite-radius configuration.

The useful high-density core bound from TOV compactness follows without choosing the unknown high-density equation of state. Let a be the radius at which the decreasing energy density first reaches ρ0​, and put μ=m(a), p0​=p(ρ0​). The Tolman–Oppenheimer–Volkoff equation gives p′<0 away from a nonempty centre; since dp/dρ>0, energy density decreases outward. Hence
μ=4π∫0a​ρ(r)r2dr≥34π​ρ0​a3.
(4)
For the local stellar compactness, if z≥0, 1−z+1+z​≤2, since its derivative is −1+(21+z​)−1<0. Applying the supplied compactness inequality at the core boundary therefore gives μ/a<4/9. Combining the two bounds yields
a<3πρ0​​1​,μ<93πρ0​​4​.​
(5)
The pressure-dependent form further restricts the allowed core-boundary data:
aμ​<92​(1−6πa2p0​+1+6πa2p0​​).
(6)
In particular, positivity of its right side gives 6πa2p0​<3, so a2<1/(2πp0​) when p0​>0. These restrictions depend only on known quantities, not on the equation of state above ρ0​.
The intended stellar-mass argument now attaches the known low-density envelope to each admissible pair (a,μ) with pressure p0​. Its Tolman–Oppenheimer–Volkoff equation involves only the known equation of state. For a physical low-density envelope with a uniformly bounded mass on this bounded set of core data, taking the supremum of the envelope-plus-core masses gives an upper mass limit independent of any high-density continuation. Stars with ρc​≤ρ0​ are also determined entirely by the known equation of state. Unknown dense-core physics cannot evade the displayed core bounds.
A qualification is necessary for a claim about the entire star: the printed monotonicity assumptions alone do not guarantee a finite maximum total mass for an arbitrary known low-density equation of state. An explicit low-density polytropic mass divergence occurs for p=Kρ5/4 with fixed K>0. In the low-density Newtonian limit, this is a stellar polytrope of index n=4, whose Lane-Emden equation has a finite first zero. With ρ=ρc​ϑ4 and r=ℓξ, hydrostatic balance fixes
ℓ2=4π5K​ρc−3/4​,M=4πρc​ℓ3μ4​∝ρc−1/8​,R∝ρc−3/8​,
(7)
where μ4​>0 is the dimensionless Lane-Emden surface mass constant. Thus M→∞ as ρc​→0, although M/R∝ρc1/4​→0. The first Lane-Emden zero is simple. The continuous dependence of an ODE solution on parameters therefore preserves this finite surface for sufficiently small relativistic parameter. The relativistic correction is controlled by pc​/ρc​=Kρc1/4​→0, so the corresponding regular finite-radius Tolman–Oppenheimer–Volkoff equation solutions have the same limiting scalings. They never sample ρ>ρ0​, and satisfy the local positivity and monotonicity assumptions in their nonvacuum interiors. This shows exactly why a suitable bounded-envelope hypothesis, or an appropriate restriction to physically stable stellar models, must accompany the intended total-mass conclusion. The compactness inequality supplies a bound on the unknown core, not a bound on the mass of every conceivable dilute envelope.

A null geodesic congruence is a smooth family of null geodesics filling a region without intersections there, with one tangent direction assigned smoothly at every point. Choose an affine parameter λ and tangent Ua, so UaUa​=0 and Ub∇b​Ua=0. Choose a second null vector Na with U⋅N=−1. The screen-space projector is
qab​=gab​+Ua​Nb​+Na​Ub​.
(15)
It annihilates U,N and restricts to a positive-definite metric on the two-dimensional transverse screen. Project the covariant derivative of the tangent to obtain the optical tensor
Bab​=qa​cqb​d∇d​Uc​.
(16)
Its decomposition defines the three optical quantities:
θ=qabBab​,ωab​=B[ab]​,σab​=B(ab)​−21​θqab​.​
(17)
The null expansion is the fractional rate of change of a transverse area, θ=A−1dA/dλ. The null shear changes a beam's shape without changing its area to first order. The null twist is its antisymmetric transverse deformation. The factor 1/2 uses four spacetime dimensions.

For an affinely parametrized null geodesic congruence, put Bab​=∇b​Ua. Differentiating along a ray and commuting covariant derivatives gives
Uc∇c​Bab​=−Bac​Bcb​+Radcb​UdUc,
(18)
using [∇c​,∇b​]Va=Radcb​Vd. The geodesic acceleration term vanishes. Since U is null and affine, Bab​Ub=0 and Ua​Bab​=0. Its trace is therefore the screen trace θ, and the trace of its square equals that of the optical tensor squared. Taking the trace, with Rdb​=Radab​, yields
dλdθ​=−Bab​Bba−Rab​UaUb.
(19)
Now decompose the optical tensor into null expansion, null shear and null twist. The symmetric trace-free part is orthogonal to the trace and to the antisymmetric part, while ωab​ωba=−ωab​ωab. Hence
Bab​Bba=21​θ2+σab​σab−ωab​ωab.
(20)
Substitution proves the Null Raychaudhuri equation:
dλdθ​=−21​θ2−σab​σab+ωab​ωab−Rab​UaUb.​
(21)
The squares use the positive-definite screen metric. Thus null shear contributes to focusing, whereas nonzero null twist has the opposite sign.

For the spherical null expansions in double-null coordinates, the cross-sectional area of a symmetry sphere is A=4πr2. For the two affine null geodesic congruences, the definition of null expansion therefore gives
θk​=fr2r,V​​,θℓ​=fr2r,U​​.​
(24)
There is no extra derivative of f in these formulas: the null expansion differentiates the transverse area, not the normalization of the tangent. Equivalently, the projected metric is qAB​=r2γAB​ and its optical tensor is (k(r)/r)qAB​, or (ℓ(r)/r)qAB​ for the other congruence. It is purely trace, so both null shears vanish.
The null twist vanishes because the covector tangents are gradients of −U and −V, and hence their exterior derivatives vanish. Each congruence is orthogonal to a family of null hypersurfaces. This also identifies the Frobenius theorem reason for zero null twist.

Use the Null Raychaudhuri equation for ℓ=f−1∂U​. Its null shear and null twist vanish, and Rab​ℓaℓb=f−2RUU​. Substitution of θℓ​=2r,U​/(fr) gives

For the Komar integral of a five-dimensional Reissner--Nordstrom metric, use the one-form metrically dual to the stationary Killing vector field: k♭=−f(r)dt. Its exterior derivative is dk♭=f′(r)dt∧dr. The stated orientation gives volume form
ϵ=−r3sin2χsinθdt∧dr∧dχ∧dθ∧dϕ.
(29)
Because the squared norm of dt∧dr is gttgrr=−1, the defining wedge identity for the Hodge star operator gives
⋆(dt∧dr)=r3sin2χsinθdχ∧dθ∧dϕ.
(30)
Thus the Komar integral over the positively oriented angular three-sphere is
∫S3​⋆dk♭=2π2r3f′(r),
(31)
since the unit three-sphere has volume 2π2. Write s=r+2​+r−2​, q=r+2​r−2​. Then f=1−s/r2+q/r4 and r3f′=2s−4q/r2. With the normalization specified for the Komar mass,
M=4π​(r+2​+r−2​).​
(32)
The orientation makes this positive. The problem's coefficient 1/(16π) must be retained: it is not the usual dimension-adjusted five-dimensional ADM-normalized Komar coefficient. With that conventional normalization the ADM mass would instead be 3πs/8.

For an asymptotically flat spacetime with a chosen exterior component of future null infinity, the black-hole region is
B=M∖J−(I+),​
(37)
namely the events from which no future-directed causal curve can reach that infinity. Here I+ is the infinity of the original r>r+​ exterior.
Let a future causal tangent in the Ingoing Eddington-Finkelstein coordinates have components (v˙,r˙,Ω˙). Its inner product with the future null field n=−∂r​ is −v˙, so v˙≥0. The causal inequality is
−fv˙2+2v˙r˙+r2∣Ω˙∣2≤0.
(38)
If v˙>0, this implies
r˙≤2f​v˙−2v˙r2∣Ω˙∣2​.
(39)
In the band r−​<r<r+​, f<0, so r˙<0. If v˙=0, the causal inequality forces Ω˙=0, and future direction means the remaining radial tangent is a nonnegative multiple of n, again giving r˙≤0. This is causal trapping between two spherical horizons: r is nonincreasing along every future causal curve in the between-horizon band. No event there can escape outward. An event with 0<r≤r−​ would also have to cross this band outward to reach the original exterior, which is impossible. Thus all 0<r<r+​ events in this extension lie in the black-hole region.
Conversely, from any r>r+​ point, the outgoing radial null ray u=constant satisfies dr/dv=f/2>0. It reaches arbitrarily large r and then the original future null infinity. Therefore the exterior does not intersect the black-hole region, and r=r+​ is its event horizon. This conclusion is relative to the selected asymptotic end; the maximal charged-black-hole extension can have other asymptotic ends.

The inner horizon in the nonextreme Reissner-Nordstrom spacetime diagram is a Cauchy horizon. Radiation from exterior perturbations can approach it at arbitrarily late advanced time and undergo unbounded blueshift. Here its positive blueshift scale is κ−​=(r+2​−r−2​)/r−3​. The inner-horizon blueshift coordinate behaves as V−​∝−e−κ−​v, so
TV−​V−​​=(dV−​dv​)2Tvv​∝e2κ−​vTvv​.
(42)
Even a decaying power-law tail can therefore generate divergent local flux. With counterstreaming radiation, backreaction produces mass inflation and curvature growth, rather than the smooth inner horizon of the exact solution.
This supports the strong cosmic censorship conjecture: a generically perturbed maximal Cauchy development is expected to lose the smooth extension across its Cauchy horizon, restoring predictability in the appropriate regularity class. The exact smooth inner horizon is unstable, not a robust failure of deterministic evolution. Curvature divergence supports an obstruction to a C2 metric extension; it does not by itself exclude every continuous metric extension. The regularity class is part of the conjecture, and the blueshift argument is evidence, not a general theorem proving it.

The Physical-process first law for a rotating black hole, in four-dimensional general relativity with G=1, is
8πκ​δA=δE−ΩH​δJ.​
(43)
It applies to a small neutral matter influx into an initially stationary, nonextremal black hole that settles to another stationary state. The background surface gravity and angular velocity are κ and ΩH​, and δE,δJ are the Killing energy and angular momentum delivered through the event horizon. Other conserved charges are held fixed. The perturbation must be small enough that its horizon generators do not develop caustics, and all equalities below are to first order.
Let ta be the stationary Killing vector field normalized at infinity, and ψa the axial Killing vector field with 2π-periodic orbits. The horizon generator is ξa=ta+ΩH​ψa. Choose an affine tangent ka and parameter λ with zero at the past stationary limiting section, so affine horizon-generator scaling gives, on the background horizon
ξa=κλka.
(44)
The background null expansion and null shear vanish, and null twist vanishes for the hypersurface-orthogonal horizon generators. Linearizing the Null Raychaudhuri equation and using the Einstein field equations gives
dλdδθ​=−8πδTab​kakb.
(45)
The quadratic null expansion and null shear terms are second order. With background cross-sectional measure dA0​, the first-order area change is δA=∫H​δθdλdA0​. Multiplying the evolution equation by λ and integrating by parts gives the first-order horizon-area response to matter flux
δA=8π∫H​λδTab​kakbdλdA0​.
(46)
The boundary term [λδθ] vanishes: λ=0 at the past limiting section, and the final stationary boundary condition gives zero expansion in the settled future. Equivalently, assume a sufficiently localized influx with the required late-time decay.
The energy and angular-momentum fluxes have signs
δE=∫H​δTab​takbdλdA0​,δJ=−∫H​δTab​ψakbdλdA0​.
(47)
These correspond to the particle conventions E=−p⋅t and J=p⋅ψ. Their combination is
δE−ΩH​δJ=∫H​δTab​ξakbdλdA0​=κ∫H​λδTab​kakbdλdA0​=8πκ​δA.
(48)
This proves the requested Physical-process first law of black-hole mechanics. For charged infall the corresponding law contains an additional horizon-potential term ΦH​δQ; the energy-minus-angular-momentum version assumes that work term is absent.
