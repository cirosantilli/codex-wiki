Here a divisor is a modulus of a number field, rather than an arbitrary real-weighted divisor. Write
c=c0​c∞​,c0​=∏p​pmp​,
(25)
where c0​ is a nonzero integral ideal, the finite multiplicities mp​ are nonnegative integers, and c∞​ is a set of real Archimedean places. The multiplicity of a place in a modulus is mv​(c)=mp​ at the finite place corresponding to p, is 1 at a real place in c∞​, and is 0 at every other infinite place. In particular complex places have multiplicity 0.
Let Ic​ be the group of nonzero fractional ideals prime to c0​. Put
Kc,1×​={a∈K×:vp​(a−1)≥mp​ for p∣c0​,σv​(a)>0 for v∈c∞​}.
(26)
Let Pc​ consist of the principal ideals (a) with a∈Kc,1×​. The generalized ideal class group is the ray class group
Hc​=Ic​/Pc​.​
(27)
Let U=OK×​ be the unit group, let Uc​=U∩Kc,1×​ be the group of ray units, and define the residue and signature group of a modulus
Gc​=(OK​/c0​)××{±1}c∞​.
(28)
The first factor is omitted when c0​=OK​. There is a group homomorphism ρ:U→Gc​ recording the unit's finite residues and its signs. Its kernel is Uc​.
Let Pc consist of all principal fractional ideals prime to c0​, and put Rc​=Pc/Pc​. The two short exact sequences are
1⟶U/Uc​ρ​Gc​⟶Rc​⟶1,​
(29)
and
1⟶Rc​⟶Hc​⟶Cl(OK​)⟶1.​
(30)
For the first short exact sequence, weak approximation for number fields realizes every choice of finite unit residues and real signs by some a∈K× prime to c0​. Send that data to the class of (a) in Rc​. Changing a without changing its residues and signs multiplies it by an element of Kc,1×​, so the map is well-defined. Its kernel consists exactly of data arising from units: if (a)=(b) with b∈Kc,1×​, then a/b∈U. This gives Rc​≅Gc​/ρ(U). For the second short exact sequence, forget the ray conditions. Its kernel is Rc​, and weak approximation for number fields gives a representative prime to c0​ for every ideal class. These are the two parts of the ray class exact sequence.
For K=Q(15​), the ring of integers of a quadratic field is OK​=Z[15​]. The finite modulus is trivial and both real places occur, so Hc​ is the narrow ideal class group and Gc​={±1}2. The two field embeddings send 15​ to 15​ and −15​. The given unit ϵ=4+15​ is positive at both places, since 4−15​>0; the unit −1 is negative at both. Thus the unit signature map has image
ρ(U)={(+,+),(−,−)},∣Rc​∣=24​=2.
(31)
The given ideal class group has order 2. The second short exact sequence therefore gives
∣Hc​∣=2⋅2=4.​
(32)
To determine the group structure, retain the ideal p=(2,1+15​) that generates the ordinary ideal class group. We have p2=(2): all generators 4, 2(1+15​) and (1+15​)2 lie in (2), while
(1+15​)2−2(1+15​)−3⋅4=2
(33)
puts 2 in p2. Since 2 is totally positive, [p]2=1 in the narrow ideal class group. Its image in the ordinary ideal class group is nontrivial, so [p] has order exactly 2.
The class of the principal ideal (15​) is a nontrivial element of Rc​. Its two signs are (+,−), and multiplying by a unit can only reverse both signs or neither, so no generator of this ideal is totally positive. Its square is (15), which does have a totally positive generator. Thus [(15​)] is another element of order 2, distinct from [p] because its ordinary ideal class is trivial. These two elements are independent and generate all four classes. Consequently
Hc​≅(Z/2Z)2.​
(34)
The nontrivial ordinary ideal class already has a lift of order 2, so the extension in the second short exact sequence splits; it cannot be cyclic of order 4.
