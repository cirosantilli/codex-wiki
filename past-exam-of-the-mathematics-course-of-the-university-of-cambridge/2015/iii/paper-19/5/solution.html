Write u=un​∈Hn(K(Z/2,n);F2​) for the fundamental class. We use the path-loop Serre spectral sequence, its multiplicative structure, and the Kudo transgression theorem: transgression of the fundamental classes and their compatible Steenrod squares is natural, equivalently cohomology suspension commutes with these stable operations. The initial ring is H∗(K(Z/2,1);F2​)=F2​[t1​].
Here is the low-degree calculation. In the first path fibration, t, t2 and t4 transgress respectively to u2​, Sq1u2​ and Sq2Sq1u2​, in degrees 2,3,5. These supply all indecomposable base classes through degree five; the other classes there are products. Repeating the path-loop calculation raises the degree of the transgressive classes by one. For n=3, the class Sq2u3​ transgresses from u22​, and Sq2Sq1u3​ from the degree-five generator. For n=4, Sq3u4​ transgresses from u32​. For larger n all four displayed low-degree operations are transgressive indecomposables. Acyclicity of the path-space total cohomology forces these transgressions and excludes additional classes in this range.
More systematically, this is the range up to n+3 of the Serre polynomial generators for mod-two Eilenberg–MacLane cohomology:
H∗(K(Z/2,n);F2​)=F2​[SqIun​:I admissible, e(I)<n].
(31)
For an admissible sequence of Steenrod squares, I=(i1​,…,ir​) has ij​≥2ij+1​; its degree increment is ∑ij​, and its Steenrod excess is e(I)=i1​−i2​−⋯−ir​. Include the empty sequence. Up to increment three the nonempty possibilities are (1),(2),(3),(2,1); the strict excess bound and possible products explain precisely the small-n exceptions.
The low-degree mod-two cohomology of an Eilenberg–MacLane space is
Hi=⎩⎨⎧​F2​{1},0,F2​{u},F2​{Sq1u},F2​{Sq2u},F2​{uSq1u,Sq2Sq1u},F2​{Sq3u,Sq2Sq1u},​i=0,0<i<n,i=n,i=n+1,i=n+2,i=n+3, n=2,i=n+3, n≥3.​​
(32)
Negative-degree cohomology is zero. For n=2, Sq2u=u2 and Sq3u=0 by instability, so the product in degree five must not be omitted. For n=3, Sq3u=u2 is a product, still independent from Sq2Sq1u. For n≥4 the listed classes are indecomposable. The Adem relations include Sq1Sq1=0 and Sq1Sq2=Sq3, so no further increment-three class comes from reversing the two squares.
Now use the space actually printed in the PDF,
Y=RP10/RP6;
(33)
the converted TeX dropped the projective-space P's. This stunted real projective space has one cell in dimensions 7,8,9,10, besides its basepoint, and is 6-connected. The integral cellular boundary is 2 in even dimensions and zero in odd dimensions. Hence
Hi​(Y;Z)={Z/2,0,​i=7,9,otherwise.​
(34)
The Hurewicz theorem gives π7​(Y)=Z/2.
For the next two groups, let K=K(Z/2,7) and choose f:Y→K representing the generator of H7(Y;F2​). It induces an isomorphism on π7​, and the target's higher homotopy groups vanish.
We need the low-degree integral homology of a mod-two Eilenberg–MacLane space. Here it is
H7​(K;Z)=Z/2,H8​(K;Z)=0,H9​(K;Z)=Z/2,H10​(K;Z)=Z/2.
(35)
To justify the torsion orders, the Serre class theorem first makes every positive-degree integral homology group of K a finite 2-group. The universal coefficient theorem for cohomology with F2​ and the low-degree dimensions give one cyclic summand in degrees seven, nine and ten and none in degree eight. The mod-two Bockstein homomorphism is Sq1. Its relevant nonzero actions are
u↦Sq1u,Sq2u↦Sq3u,Sq2Sq1u↦Sq3Sq1u.
(36)
The last target is nonzero: (3,1) is admissible with excess two, below seven. It also follows by transgressing the nonzero square (Sq1u2​)2 through successive path fibrations. Each nonzero Bockstein pairs the mod-two classes associated with a cyclic integral summand of order exactly two; for a summand of order 2r with r>1, this first Bockstein would be zero. In degree ten, Sq3u is already the Ext class from H9​, so the independent class Sq2Sq1u detects H10​. This proves all four integral groups without confusing them with mod-two Betti numbers.
Let x∈H1(RP10;F2​) be its usual generator. The quotient classes in degrees 7 through 10 identify with x7,…,x10 via the pair's cohomology. The Steenrod squares on real projective space satisfy
Sqj(xr)=(jr​)xr+j.
(37)
Thus
f∗u=x7,f∗Sq1u=x8,f∗Sq2u=x9,f∗Sq3u=x10,f∗Sq2Sq1u=0,
(38)
since (27​) and (37​) are odd, while (28​) is even. In degree nine, the nonzero cohomology map detects the map on H9​, as H8​=0 on both sides. Consequently f∗​:H9​(Y;Z)→H9​(K;Z) is an isomorphism.
Treat f as a mapping-cylinder pair (K,Y). It is 8-connected: both spaces are 6-connected, π7​ is an isomorphism, and π8​(K)=0. The relative homology sequence gives H9​(K,Y)=0, since H9​(Y)→H9​(K) is an isomorphism and H8​(Y)=0. The Relative Hurewicz theorem then gives π9​(K,Y)=0, and the relative homotopy sequence identifies this group with π8​(Y). Thus π8​(Y)=0 and the pair is now 9-connected.
Next,
H10​(K,Y)≅H10​(K)≅Z/2,
(39)
because H10​(Y)=0 and the degree-nine map is an isomorphism. Apply the Relative Hurewicz theorem again and use π10​(K)=π9​(K)=0:
π9​(Y)≅π10​(K,Y)≅H10​(K,Y)≅Z/2.
(40)
Therefore the homotopy groups of the stunted projective space through degree nine are
πi​(RP10/RP6)=⎩⎨⎧​0,Z/2,0,Z/2,​1≤i≤6,i=7,i=8,i=9.​​
(41)
