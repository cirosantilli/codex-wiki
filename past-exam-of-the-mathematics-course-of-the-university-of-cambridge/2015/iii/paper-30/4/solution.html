Write δn​=2−n and let Qn​(U) denote the printed squared-increment sum for a process U, on grid times kδn​. We construct its limit first for bounded martingales, then use localizing sequences and the finite variation part of a semimartingale.
Continuity and monotonicity of the bounded-martingale limit. Let U be a uniformly bounded continuous martingale. The given result supplies a limit RU in uniform convergence on compacts in probability. Each Qn​(U) is continuous and adapted. A subsequence converges almost surely uniformly on each compact time interval, by choosing summable error probabilities and diagonalizing. Its limit therefore has a continuous version; in the usual completed filtration this version is adapted.
The sums Qn​(U) themselves can decrease between grid points, because the last partial increment is being squared. To establish monotonicity, use instead the quadratic variation from completed grid increments
Sn​(U)t​=∑kδn​≤t​(Ukδn​​−U(k−1)δn​​)2.
(42)
These step processes are nondecreasing, and pathwise uniform continuity gives
supt≤T​∣Qn​(U)t​−Sn​(U)t​∣≤ωU​(δn​;T)2⟶0,
(43)
where ωU​(δ;T)=sup{∣Ut​−Us​∣:s,t≤T, ∣t−s∣≤δ}. Thus the same almost surely uniform subsequence of Sn​ converges to RU, proving that RU is nondecreasing. Also R0U​=0.
Localization of a continuous local martingale. Subtract the initial value, which does not affect increments, so that M0​=0. Set
τm​=inf{t:∣Mt​∣≥m}∧m.
(44)
Continuity gives τm​↑∞ almost surely and makes Mτm​ bounded. The bounded local martingale criterion makes it a true martingale. Let Rm be its continuous, adapted, nondecreasing limit.
The discrete sums commute exactly with stopping:
Qn​(Mτm​)t​=Qn​(M)t∧τm​​.
(45)
For ℓ≤m, uniqueness of the probability limit and stability of uniform convergence on compacts in probability under stopping give
Rt∧τℓ​m​=Rtℓ​
(46)
up to indistinguishability. Taking a common null set for the countably many pairs, patch these processes into RM by setting RtM​=Rtm​ when t≤τm​. Compatibility makes this definition independent of m. It is continuous, adapted, and nondecreasing. For each T,ε>0,
P(supt≤T​∣Qn​(M)t​−RtM​∣>ε)≤P(τm​<T)+P(supt≤T​∣Qn​(Mτm​)t​−Rtm​∣>ε).
(47)
Let n→∞ and then m→∞. This proves the localization and patching of quadratic variation.
Adding finite variation. Decompose the continuous semimartingale as X=X0​+M+A, where M is a continuous local martingale starting at zero and A is continuous, adapted, and locally of finite variation. For every compact interval, the sum of the absolute increments of A is at most its total variation of a function. Hence, pathwise,
supt≤T​Qn​(A)t​≤ωA​(δn​;T)Var[0,T]​(A)⟶0.
(48)
The Cauchy-Schwarz inequality bounds the mixed increment sum by
supt≤T​∣∑k​Δk​M(t)Δk​A(t)∣≤(supt≤T​Qn​(M)t​)1/2(supt≤T​Qn​(A)t​)1/2.
(49)
The first factor is bounded in probability, since Qn​(M)→RM uniformly on compacts in probability; the second tends to zero almost surely. Thus the mixed term tends to zero in probability. Expanding the square proves
Qn​(X)⟶RMuniformly on compacts in probability.​
(50)
The constructed R=RM is continuous, nondecreasing, and adapted. This is the quadratic variation of X, and expresses the fact that finite-variation terms do not change quadratic variation.
