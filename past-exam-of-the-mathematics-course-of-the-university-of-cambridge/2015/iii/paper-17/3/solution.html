Let E→M have rank r. A vector bundle covariant derivative is a real-linear operator
∇:Γ(E)⟶Ω1(M;E),∇(fs)=df⊗s+f∇s.​
(13)
Pairing its one-form slot with a vector field X gives ∇X​s, with ∇fX​s=f∇X​s and ∇X​(fs)=X(f)s+f∇X​s.
In the horizontal description, a connection on a vector bundle A is a smooth splitting Ta​E=Va​E⊕Ha​E, where Va​E=ker(dπ)a​, and the horizontal choice is compatible with fibre addition and scalar multiplication. This linearity is essential; an arbitrary nonlinear horizontal splitting is not a vector-bundle connection. Identify a vertical vector at a∈Ex​ with the element of Ex​ obtained by differentiating the curve a+tv. Let KA​:Ta​E→Ex​ be vertical projection followed by this identification. The induced vector bundle covariant derivative is
∇XA​s=KA​(ds(X)).​
(14)
In a local frame of a vector bundle e=(e1​,…,er​), a linear horizontal splitting has the form
H(x,v)​E={(ξ,−Γx​(ξ)v):ξ∈Tx​M},KA​(ξ,w)=w+Γx​(ξ)v,
(15)
for a matrix-valued connection one-form Γ. Thus for s=ev, ∇As=e(dv+Γv), which directly verifies the Leibniz rule.
Conversely, the Leibniz rule makes ∇ local: if a section vanishes near a point, multiply it by a smooth cutoff function supported in that neighborhood and equal to one near the point to see that its derivative vanishes there too. Local sections can therefore be differentiated by any extension agreeing near the point. Given ∇, define its local connection one-form by ∇eb​=∑a​ea​Γba​. The Leibniz rule gives ∇(ev)=e(dv+Γv). Under e′=eg, v′=g−1v, one has
Γ′=g−1Γg+g−1dg.
(16)
The tangent fibre coordinate transforms by w′=g−1w−g−1(dg)(ξ)g−1v, so w=−Γ(ξ)v transforms precisely to w′=−Γ′(ξ)v′. Consequently these local horizontal subspaces of a vector bundle connection glue to a smooth linear splitting inducing the given ∇. Its local formula also shows uniqueness. This proves the horizontal connection associated to a covariant derivative construction.
A horizontal lift γ​ of γ is a smooth curve in E with π∘γ​=γ and γ​˙​(t)∈Hγ​(t)​E. In a local frame write γ​(t)=e(γ(t))v(t). Horizontality is exactly the linear ordinary differential equation
v˙(t)+Γγ(t)​(γ˙​(t))v(t)=0.​
(17)
We use existence and uniqueness for an ODE with smooth coefficients, and the continuation theorem that a solution extends across a finite interval when it remains bounded inside the coordinate domain. For a linear system v˙=−C(t)v with continuous coefficients bounded by L on a compact time interval, the Gronwall inequality bounds ∣v(t)∣ by its initial norm times eL∣t−t0​∣, preventing finite-time fibre escape. Cover a compact subinterval of I by finitely many bundle charts and partition it so each piece lies in one chart. Solve successively, changing fibre frames at the partition points. Coordinate invariance of the horizontal equation and uniqueness make the pieces agree. Exhausting I by compact intervals containing 0 produces a unique horizontal lift on all of I for every prescribed a0​. This is global continuation of linear parallel transport.
The lift equation is linear in its initial value. Hence the endpoint map Pγ​:Eγ(0)​→Eγ(1)​ is linear. Solving the same equation backwards, or transporting along t↦γ(1−t), gives its inverse, so
Pγ​ is a linear isomorphism.​
(18)
This is parallel transport. For two parallel lifts s1​(t),s2​(t), the assumed metric connection identity extends along the embedded curve and becomes
dtd​⟨s1​(t),s2​(t)⟩=⟨Dt​s1​,s2​⟩+⟨s1​,Dt​s2​⟩=0.
(19)
Here Dt​ is the covariant derivative along a curve. The permitted extension of sections and the tangent vector along the embedded curve justifies applying the bundle identity; equivalently it follows in each local frame. Therefore
⟨Pγ​a,Pγ​b⟩γ(1)​=⟨a,b⟩γ(0)​,​
(20)
so parallel transport is an isometry. This is the principle that parallel transport preserves a fibre metric.
