The height of a prime ideal is the supremum of the lengths of strict chains of prime ideals ending at P:
htP=sup{d:P0​⊊P1​⊊⋯⊊Pd​=P}=dimRP​.
(25)
The last equality follows from the prime ideal correspondence for localization. Here is a length-growth proof of the Krull height theorem that makes the bound explicit.
We first establish a prime-chain lower bound for local length. For a Noetherian local ring (B,m) admitting a prime chain of length d, put HB​(t)=length(B/mt). Then
HB​(t)≥ctd(t≫0)
(26)
for some c>0. For d=0, use HB​(t)≥1. For d>0, quotient by the first prime ideal of the chain; this only decreases HB​ and reduces us to a local integral domain B with
0⊊P1​⊊⋯⊊Pd​=m.
(27)
Take 0=a∈P1​ and put C=B/(a). It has a prime chain of length d−1, so induction bounds HC​(t) below by a positive multiple of td−1.
The Artin-Rees lemma applied to (a)⊆B gives, for a fixed integer c0​≥1,
(a)∩mt⊆amt−c0​(t≥c0​).
(28)
Because a is a non-zero-divisor, the first term in the exact sequence
0⟶(a)/((a)∩mt)⟶B/mt⟶B/((a)+mt)⟶0
(29)
has length at least HB​(t−c0​). Hence
HB​(t)≥HB​(t−c0​)+HC​(t).
(30)
Iterate this inequality about t/(2c0​) times, while its arguments remain at least t/2. This gives the desired positive multiple of td. The particular Artin-Rees lemma inclusion used here follows directly from finite generation: the Rees algebra ⨁j≥0​mjtj is Noetherian, by the Hilbert basis theorem, and its graded submodule ⨁j​((a)∩mj)tj has finitely many homogeneous generators. A bound on their degrees gives c0​.
Now localize at the given minimal prime ideal P. Write B=RP​, m=PRP​, and J=IRP​. The only prime ideal of B containing J is m, so J​=m. Since m is finitely generated, some mq⊆J. Thus B/J has finite module length, say L. The ideal J is generated by at most n elements.
For n>0, products of these generators give a surjection from (n−1j+n−1​) copies of B/J onto Jj/Jj+1. Summing the lengths gives the generator bound for primary-ideal length
length(B/Jt)≤L∑j=0t−1​(n−1j+n−1​)=L(nt+n−1​)=O(tn).
(31)
Since J⊆m, we have Jt⊆mt, and therefore
HB​(t)≤length(B/Jt)=O(tn).
(32)
The prime-chain lower bound for local length now excludes any chain of length greater than n. Consequently
htP=dimRP​≤n.​
(33)
For n=0, minimality over the zero ideal says that P is a minimal prime ideal, so its height is zero. This also handles that boundary case.
