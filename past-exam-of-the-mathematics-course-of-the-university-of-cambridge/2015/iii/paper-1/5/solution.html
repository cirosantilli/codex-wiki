A local ring is a nonzero ring with one maximal ideal m. Write κ=R/m for its residue field. Its Krull dimension dimR is the supremum of lengths of strict chains of prime ideals; in a local ring chains may be extended to end at m.
For the local Hilbert-function convention, define the Hilbert–Samuel growth dimension
d(R)=deg(the eventual polynomial HR​(t)=length(R/mt)).​
(34)
Equivalently it is the order of the pole at z=1 of the Hilbert series of the associated graded ring
grm​R=⨁j≥0​mj/mj+1.
(35)
This finite standard graded κ-algebra is generated by m/m2. The Hilbert-Serre theorem makes the cumulative Hilbert function eventually polynomial, which establishes the definition. For an Artinian local ring, the polynomial is constant and nonzero, so d(R)=0.
For every prime chain of length s, the prime-chain lower bound for local length proved in Question 4 gives HR​(t)≥cts. A polynomial of degree d(R) cannot satisfy this when s>d(R). Thus the requested inequality is
dimR≤d(R).​
(36)
For clarity, the embedding dimension is the different invariant
e(R)=dimκ​(m/m2)=the minimal number of generators of m.
(37)
The equality follows from the Nakayama lemma. Applying Question 4 to I=m gives dimR≤e(R), while the polynomial-algebra surjection below also gives d(R)≤e(R). In particular, growth dimension and embedding dimension should be kept distinct.
A regular local ring is a Noetherian local ring with e(R)=dimR. Let this common value be e and choose a minimal generating set x1​,…,xe​ of m. The initial forms give a surjective graded ring homomorphism
κ[T1​,…,Te​]↠grm​R.
(38)
For e=0, the Nakayama lemma gives m=0, so R is a field. Assume e>0. If the kernel contained a nonzero homogeneous polynomial F of degree q>0, the cumulative Hilbert function of the target would be bounded by that of κ[T1​,…,Te​]/(F). Multiplication by F is injective in the polynomial integral domain, so this bound is
(et+e−1​)−(et−q+e−1​)=O(te−1)(t>q).
(39)
But dimR=e provides a prime chain of length e, and Question 4 gives HR​(t)≥cte. This is a contradiction. Hence the associated graded ring of a regular local ring is
grm​R≅κ[T1​,…,Te​],​
(40)
an integral domain.
Finally the Krull intersection theorem gives ⋂j​mj=0. One can see the needed separatedness directly: for the finitely generated ideal K=⋂j​mj, the Artin-Rees lemma gives K=mK, and the Nakayama lemma gives K=0. Therefore each nonzero a∈R has finite m-adic order r, with nonzero initial form in mr/mr+1. For nonzero a,b, their initial forms have nonzero product in the graded integral domain. It follows that ab=0, and indeed its order is the sum of their orders. Thus R is an integral domain.
