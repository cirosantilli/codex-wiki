A Noetherian ring is a ring whose ideals satisfy the ascending chain condition; equivalently every ideal is finitely generated. For the surjective ring endomorphism, put Kj​=kerfj. The ascending chain of ideals K1​⊆K2​⊆⋯ stabilizes, say Km​=Km+1​. If a∈kerf, surjectivity of fm gives b with fm(b)=a. Then fm+1(b)=0, so b∈Km+1​=Km​ and a=0. The endomorphism is therefore injective, hence a ring automorphism.
The nilradical is the ideal
N(R)=(0)​={a∈R:aj=0 for some j≥1}.
(1)
For example, if au=bv=0, the binomial expansion gives (a+b)u+v−1=0; multiplication by any ring element also preserves nilpotence. Every prime ideal contains each nilpotent element, since aj∈P implies a∈P. Conversely, if a is not nilpotent, the multiplicative subset {1,a,a2,…} avoids (0). By Zorn's lemma, choose an ideal maximal among those disjoint from this subset. It is a prime ideal: if xy∈J but neither factor is in J, then J+(x) and J+(y) both meet the subset, and multiplying their two witnesses puts a power of a in J, a contradiction. This prime ideal excludes a. Hence
N(R)=P∈SpecR⋂​P.​
(2)
For the zero ring the empty intersection is the whole ring, as required.
Here is a finite prime-intersection representation of a radical argument that does not assume the result. In a Noetherian ring, suppose some ideal J has ideal radical not expressible as a finite intersection of prime ideals, and choose such a J maximal by the ascending chain condition. It is proper and cannot be prime. Choose a,b∈/J with ab∈J. The two larger ideals J+(a) and J+(b) have finite prime-intersection radicals, and
J​=J+(a)​∩J+(b)​.
(3)
Indeed, if xu∈J+(a) and xv∈J+(b), then xu+v∈J+(ab)=J. This contradicts the choice of J. Applying the result to (0) proves that N(R) is the intersection of finitely many prime ideals. Empty intersections handle the unit ideal.
For reducedness of a formal power series ring, the inclusion of constants gives one implication: a nonzero nilpotent element of R remains nonzero and nilpotent in R[[X]]. For the other, let R be a reduced ring and take a nonzero formal power series F=∑j≥r​aj​Xj with ar​=0. The coefficient of Xmr in Fm is arm​=0, because R is reduced. Thus F is not nilpotent. Therefore
N(R)=0⟺N(R[[X]])=0.​
(4)
No Noetherian ring hypothesis is needed for this argument.
For a nilradical that is not nilpotent, take
R=k[t1​,t2​,…]/(t12​,t22​,…),N(R)=(tˉ1​,tˉ2​,…).​
(5)
Every element of this ideal uses finitely many variables and is nilpotent: an element using s variables lies in an ideal whose (s+1)st power is zero. The quotient by this ideal is the field k, so it is exactly the nilradical. Nevertheless tˉ1​⋯tˉm​=0 for every m, because the square-free monomials form a vector space basis. Thus N(R)m=0 for all m.
