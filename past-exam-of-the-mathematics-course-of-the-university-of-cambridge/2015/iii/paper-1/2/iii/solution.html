Suppose S is a saturated multiplicative subset, and take a∈/S. The principal ideal (a) is disjoint from S: otherwise ra∈S would force a∈S. The prime ideal avoiding a multiplicative subset argument from Question 1 extends (a) to a prime ideal Pa​ disjoint from S. Thus every point of the complement belongs to such a prime ideal, and
R∖S=P∩S=∅⋃​P.​
(14)
If S=R, this is the empty union.
Conversely suppose R∖S is a union of prime ideals. If xy∈S and x∈/S, some prime ideal in that union contains x, and hence xy, a contradiction. The same holds for y. Thus The saturated complement as a union of prime ideals characterization holds.
For units with no finite prime-union complement, take
R=Z,S=Z×={−1,1}.​
(15)
Products of units are units, and a product is a unit only if both factors are units, so S is a saturated multiplicative subset. Every nonunit integer lies in some (p) with p a positive prime, giving Z∖S=⋃p​(p). If finitely many prime ideals sufficed, discard any (0) and write the others as (p1​),…,(pr​). A prime ℓ outside this finite list belongs to none of them but is a nonunit, a contradiction. There is always such an ℓ: a prime divisor of p1​⋯pr​+1 is outside the list.
