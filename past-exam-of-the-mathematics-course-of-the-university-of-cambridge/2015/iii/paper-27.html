∑p≤Q​p∑amodp​∣Bp​(a)−B/p∣2≪(H+Q2)∑∣bn​∣2.

The constant is absolute. One may take the explicit right side (Q2+2πH)∑∣bn​∣2, by orthogonality of roots of unity and the exponential-sum large sieve proved in Question 2.
Take H=⌊N2⌋, Q=N, and bn​ the indicator function of the Nε-smooth numbers up to N2. Applying the given smooth-number density with parameter ε/2 gives
B≥κ(ε/2)N2,
(7)
with a harmless adjustment of the constant for integer endpoints. If an odd prime p≤N has least quadratic nonresidue n(p)>Nε, then p>Nε: a quadratic nonresidue always occurs among 1,…,p−1. Every prime factor of every selected smooth number is thus a nonzero quadratic residue modulo p. By the multiplicativity of the Legendre symbol, every selected number is a nonzero quadratic residue modulo p.

The points are δ-spaced if their circular spacing satisfies ∥θr​−θs​∥≥δ for r=s, where ∥t∥ is distance to the nearest integer. Ordinary distance on the real line would be insufficient because the complex exponential is periodic.
Let S(t)=∑M<n≤M+N​an​e(nt) and F(t)=e(−Mt)S(t). Multiplication by this unit-modulus factor leaves ∣S(t)∣ unchanged and places the frequencies of F in 1,…,N. Put E=∑∣an​∣2. The permitted Sobolev–Gallagher inequality, in the form needed here, is
∣F(t)∣2≤δ−1∫t−δ/2t+δ/2​∣F(u)∣2du+∫t−δ/2t+δ/2​∣F(u)F′(u)∣du.

∑r​∣S(θr​)∣2≤δ−1∫01​∣F∣2+(∫01​∣F∣2)1/2(∫01​∣F′∣2)1/2.

For a fixed prime p∈[P,2P], the grid a/p has circular spacing 1/p≥1/(2P). Consequently an arc of length Δ=1/P contains at most three points of this grid, including endpoints. The standard Chebyshev estimate π(t)≪t/logt for the prime-counting function gives
K(1/P)≤3#{p∈[P,2P]:p prime}≪logPP​.

P≤p≤2Pp prime​∑​a=1∑p−1​∣S(a/p)∣2≪logPP2​E.​

Let 1(n)=1 and ϵ(n)=1n=1​, the identity for Dirichlet convolution. The elementary identities needed are
μ∗1=ϵ,Λ∗1=log,μ∗log=Λ.
(25)
For the first, prime factorization gives ∑d∣n​μ(d)=∏p∣n​(1−1) when n>1, and one when n=1. For the second, if n=∏pap​, then ∑d∣n​Λ(d)=∑p​ap​logp=logn, the Von Mangoldt divisor identity. Convolving the second identity with μ proves the third.
For U,V≥1, let the subscripts ≤U, >U, ≤V, >V denote truncations. The Vaughan identity is
Λ=Λ≤V​+μ≤U​∗log−μ≤U​∗1∗Λ≤V​+μ>U​∗1∗Λ>V​.​
(26)
Its pointwise form is
Λ(n)=Λ(n)1n≤V​+∑d∣nd≤U​​μ(d)log(n/d)−∑dc∣nd≤U, c≤V​​μ(d)Λ(c)+∑dcm=nd>U, c>V​​μ(d)Λ(c).
(27)
To prove it, use μ>U​∗1=ϵ−μ≤U​∗1 and 1∗Λ>V​=log−1∗Λ≤V​. This gives
μ>U​∗1∗Λ>V​=Λ>V​−μ≤U​∗log+μ≤U​∗1∗Λ≤V​,
(28)
and rearrangement proves the formula, as in the Vaughan identity proof.
The Bombieri–Vinogradov theorem states that for every A>0 there is B>0 such that
∑q≤Q​max(a,q)=1​max2≤y≤x​​ψ(y;q,a)−ϕ(q)y​​≪A​logAxx​,Q≤logBxx1/2​.
(29)
Here ψ(y;q,a)=∑n≤yn≡a(modq)​​Λ(n) is the Chebyshev function in an arithmetic progression, and ϕ is the Euler totient function. In particular, partial summation gives
∑q≤Q​max(a,q)=1​​π(x;q,a)−ϕ(q)Li(x)​​≪A​logAxx​,Li(x)=∫2x​logtdt​,
(30)
after choosing the logarithmic saving in the weighted theorem sufficiently large and absorbing the prime power terms. This is the offset logarithmic integral function.
For the proof strategy, Orthogonality of Dirichlet characters converts errors in arithmetic progressions into character sums of Dirichlet characters weighted by Λ. Reduction to primitive Dirichlet characters is followed by the character large sieve, which bounds their mean square by O((N+Q2)∑∣an​∣2) with the usual q/ϕ(q) weights. The Vaughan identity separates the weighted sums into short terms, Type I sums and Type II sums. Direct inner-sum estimates handle the Type I sums; the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality and the large sieve control the Type II sums. The Siegel–Walfisz theorem supplies arbitrarily strong logarithmic savings for small moduli, where an average estimate alone would not suffice. A dyadic decomposition, suitable U,V and a sufficiently large B absorb the divisor and logarithmic losses. This explains why the range is essentially Q2≤x, with logarithmic room to spare.
