The filtered decomposition is not necessarily frustration-free. Commuting with P0​ makes ∣ϕ0​⟩ an eigenvector of each term; it does not make that eigenvector a lowest-energy state of each term. This is the distinction expressed by commuting with a ground projector does not imply frustration freeness.
For an explicit positive, two-local counterexample on three qubits, let P0(i)​=∣0⟩⟨0∣i​ and P1(i)​=∣1⟩⟨1∣i​, and use the two distinct interaction sets {1,2} and {2,3}:
h12​=P1(1)​+P0(2)​,h23​=P1(3)​+2P1(2)​.
(49)
Both are positive semidefinite and commute with their sum. The total energy on a computational basis vector is
E(b1​,b2​,b3​)=b1​+b3​+1+b2​.
(50)
Thus H=h12​+h23​ has unique ground state ∣000⟩, energy 1, and spectral gap 1. Since [H,h12​]=[H,h23​]=0, spectral filtering of Hamiltonian terms leaves both terms unchanged for every normalized filter:
g(12)=h12​,g(23)=h23​.
(51)
But h12​∣000⟩=∣000⟩, whereas h12​∣010⟩=0. The global ground state therefore does not minimize g(12), proving that the resulting decomposition is not a frustration-free Hamiltonian.
There is a useful positive result if the original decomposition already is a frustration-free Hamiltonian. If aZ​=λmin​(hZ​) and hZ​∣ϕ0​⟩=aZ​∣ϕ0​⟩, then hZ​−aZ​I≥0. Averaging its unitary conjugates with nonnegative w gives g(Z)−aZ​I≥0, while g(Z)∣ϕ0​⟩=aZ​∣ϕ0​⟩. Thus filtering preserves existing frustration freeness; it does not create it for an arbitrary gapped Hamiltonian.
