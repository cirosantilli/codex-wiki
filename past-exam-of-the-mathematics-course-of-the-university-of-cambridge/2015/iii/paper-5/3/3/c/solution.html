The original PDF assumes u≥0 at the beginning of this question; the TeX conversion omits that hypothesis. First replace a smooth nonnegative solution by v=u+ε>0, which solves the same homogeneous divergence-form elliptic equation. For w=vq/2, the chain rule gives the favorable term

in the distributional sense. Thus w is a weak subsolution. Testing its subsolution inequality with ζ2w yields the same Caccioppoli inequality as before. More precisely, testing the equation for v with ζ2vq−1 gives
∫ζ2∣∇vq/2∣2≤(q−1)2q2​R2∫vq∣∇ζ∣2≤4R2∫vq∣∇ζ∣2.

where Dℓ​≥1 is independent of q. This is uniform power gain for elliptic solutions. The exponent is qα, not the TeX conversion's qα. The PDF's weaker prefactor Cℓ​ outside the 2/q power follows from this stronger estimate, but that weaker form alone cannot be iterated infinitely.
For completeness, the estimate also extends to nonnegative locally H1 weak solutions without assuming they are already bounded. For s≥0 define FM​(s)=sq/2 up to M and continue it linearly with slope (q/2)Mq/2−1 above M; set ΨM​(s)=∫0s​FM′​(r)2dr. Both have bounded derivatives for fixed M.
