The filtration and its meaning. Let L/K be a finite Galois extension of non-Archimedean local fields, with group G, normalized valuation vL​, and residue fields kL​/kK​ of characteristic ℓ. The lower ramification numbering is
G−1​=G,Gj​={σ∈G:vL​(σa−a)≥j+1 for every a∈OL​}(j≥0).
(39)
The higher ramification groups are normal, decrease with j, and eventually become trivial: for each nonidentity automorphism some integral element is moved by a nonzero amount. The first group G0​ is the inertia group, with
G/G0​≅Gal(kL​/kK​).
(40)
Its fixed field is the maximal unramified extension inside L. The wild inertia group is G1​. For a uniformizer πL​, there are injective homomorphisms
G0​/G1​↪kL×​,σ⟼σ(πL​)/πL​​,Gj​/Gj+1​↪(kL​,+),σ⟼πLj+1​σ(πL​)−πL​​​(j≥1).
(41)
The kernels are the indicated next groups; the additive homomorphism assertion follows by expanding a product of automorphisms modulo πLj+2​. These maps show that G0​/G1​ is cyclic of order prime to ℓ, while G1​ is an ℓ-group. Thus tame ramification is exactly the case G1​=1.
To calculate the groups, the uniformizer criterion for lower ramification groups says
Gj​={σ∈G0​:vL​(σπL​−πL​)≥j+1}.​
(42)
Indeed, OL​ is generated by πL​ over the integers of the maximal unramified subextension, which G0​ fixes. Differences of powers of πL​ are divisible by σπL​−πL​, so the one test implies all the defining inequalities.
Eisenstein facts used in the calculations. For a complete discrete valuation ring A with uniformizer ϖ, a monic polynomial
f(T)=Td+ad−1​Td−1+⋯+a0​
(43)
is an Eisenstein polynomial if ϖ∣aj​ for every j<d and ϖ2∤a0​. The Eisenstein criterion makes it irreducible. If θ is a root, its valuation relative to the base normalization is 1/d, the extension has degree and ramification index d, its residue field is unchanged, and
OK(θ)​=A[θ],θ is a uniformizer.​
(44)
The root valuation follows by comparing the terms of its equation. The ramification index must then be at least d, hence exactly d. In the basis 1,θ,…,θd−1, the valuations of bj​θj are distinct modulo d, so an integral linear combination has every bj​∈A. This proves the integer-ring assertion. Conversely, a uniformizer in a totally ramified extension generates the field, and its minimal polynomial is Eisenstein; this follows from its value 1/d, equal valuations of its conjugates, and the valuation one of its norm.
For a monogenic separable integer ring A[θ], the different ideal is generated by f′(θ). For a Galois extension, the different exponent from ramification groups is
vL​(DL/K​)=j≥0∑​(∣Gj​∣−1).​
(45)
In the totally ramified case these agree directly: f′(πL​)=∏σ=1​(πL​−σπL​), and an automorphism with displacement valuation h contributes once to each of G0​,…,Gh−1​.
Upper numbering. Extend the lower indexing to real s≥0 by Gs​=G⌈s⌉​, and set
φL/K​(s)=∫0s​[G0​:Gt​]dt​,ψL/K​=φL/K−1​,Gu=GψL/K​(u)​.
(46)
On [−1,0] take φ(s)=s. This Herbrand function slows the indexing as the groups shrink. Lower numbering is compatible with subgroups, while upper ramification numbering is compatible with quotients: for normal H, (G/H)u=GuH/H. This is the fact that upper ramification groups commute with quotients. Thus upper breaks are particularly useful when comparing intermediate Galois extensions.
The eighth-root cyclotomic extension. Put ζ=ζ8​ and π=ζ−1. The shifted cyclotomic polynomial
(1+T)4+1=T4+4T3+6T2+4T+2
(47)
is Eisenstein at two. Hence L=Q2​(ζ) is totally ramified of degree four, vL​(2)=4, and π is a uniformizer. Its Galois group consists of σa​(ζ)=ζa for a=1,3,5,7, and is C2​×C2​.
Since ζ is a unit,
vL​(σa​π−π)=vL​(ζa−1−1).
(48)
For a=3,7, this is the valuation of i−1 or −i−1, namely two, because their squares are units times two. For a=5, it is vL​(−2)=4. The ramification groups of the eighth-root cyclotomic extension of the 2-adic field are therefore
G−1​=G0​=G1​=C2​×C2​,G2​=G3​={1,σ5​}≅C2​,Gj​=1 (j≥4).​
(49)
The lower breaks are one and three, and
φ(s)=⎩⎨⎧​s,1+(s−1)/2,2+(s−3)/4,​0≤s≤1,1≤s≤3,s≥3.​
(50)
Thus the upper breaks are one and two:
Gu=⎩⎨⎧​C2​×C2​,{1,σ5​},1,​−1≤u≤1,1<u≤2,u>2.​​
(51)
The different exponent from ramification groups is 3+3+1+1=8, agreeing with vL​(4ζ3)=8 from the derivative of T4+1.
The cubic splitting field at three. Let α3=2, ζ=ζ3​, and put
β=α+1,λ=ζ−1.
(52)
The polynomials
β3−3β2+3β−3=0,λ2+3λ+3=0
(53)
are Eisenstein at three. They give totally ramified subextensions of degrees three and two. Their intersection is the base field, so L=Q3​(α,ζ) has degree six. Its ramification index is divisible by both three and two, hence equals six; therefore L/Q3​ is totally ramified. It is the splitting field of T3−2, with Galois group S3​.
Normalize vL​ so that vL​(3)=6. Then vL​(β)=2 and vL​(λ)=3, making
π=βλ​​
(54)
a uniformizer. Let σ(α)=ζα, σ(ζ)=ζ and τ(α)=α, τ(ζ)=ζ2. They generate S3​. Since τ(λ)=(ζ+1)λ,
τ(π)−π=ζπ,vL​(τ(π)−π)=1.
(55)
For the order-three automorphism,
σ(π)−π=−(α+1)(ζα+1)αλ2​,vL​(σ(π)−π)=6−2−2=2.
(56)
Here α is a unit and vL​(ζα+1)=vL​(σβ)=2. The same calculation gives valuation two for σ2. The three transpositions are conjugate and the defining filtration is normal, so they all have displacement valuation one. The ramification groups of the splitting field of T3 minus 2 over Q3 are
G−1​=G0​=S3​,G1​=⟨σ⟩≅C3​,Gj​=1 (j≥2).​
(57)
The lower breaks are zero and one. Since [G0​:Gs​]=2 for 0<s≤1,
φ(s)={s/2,1/2+(s−1)/6,​0≤s≤1,s≥1,​
(58)
and the upper groups are
Gu=⎩⎨⎧​S3​,C3​,1,​−1≤u≤0,0<u≤1/2,u>1/2.​​
(59)
The different exponent from ramification groups is 5+2=7. As an independent check, the cubic subfield has different exponent vQ3​(α)​(3α2)=3, and the quadratic extension above it is tame with different exponent one. Transitivity of the different ideal gives 1+2⋅3=7, as required.
