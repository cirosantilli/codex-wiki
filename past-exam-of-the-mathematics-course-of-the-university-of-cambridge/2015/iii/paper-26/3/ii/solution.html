Logarithms on sufficiently deep units. Suppose charK=0, let ℓ be the residue characteristic, and put e=v(ℓ). For every integer r>e/(ℓ−1) the p-adic logarithm and p-adic exponential function, evaluated in K, converge on the required domains:
log(1+x)=∑m≥1​m(−1)m+1xm​,exp(y)=∑m≥0​m!ym​.
(30)
The bounds v(m!)=evℓ​(m!)≤e(m−1)/(ℓ−1) and v(m)≤e(m−1)/(ℓ−1) show that, for x,y∈πrOK​, every term beyond the linear one has valuation strictly greater than v(x) or v(y). The series therefore converge and preserve these lattices; in particular exp(y)∈1+πrOK​. Their formal composition and addition identities are valid by convergence, giving inverse continuous group homomorphisms
log:1+πrOK​≅​πrOK​,exp:πrOK​≅​1+πrOK​.
(31)
Thus the logarithm isomorphism on deep principal units is
1+πrOK​≅(OK​,+),u⟼π−rlogu,r>ℓ−1e​.​
(32)
The right-hand group is torsion-free.
Roots of unity in Qp​. For odd p, take ℓ=p,e=1,r=1. The principal unit group 1+pZp​ is torsion-free by the logarithm isomorphism on deep principal units. The Teichmuller representative splitting leaves precisely the p−1 roots in μp−1​.
For p=2, take r=2. Each odd unit is uniquely ±u with u∈1+4Z2​, since its residue modulo four is either one or minus one. This latter group is torsion-free. Consequently
∣μ(Qp​)∣={p−1,2,​p odd,p=2,​μ(Q2​)={1,−1}.​
(33)
This computes the roots of unity in the p-adic numbers.
The units of Q2​(i). Set π=1−i. Its polynomial T2−2T+2 is Eisenstein, so
OK​=Z2​[i],v(π)=1,v(2)=2,k=F2​.
(34)
The Eisenstein polynomial facts justifying this are stated in the next solution. Write Us​=1+πsOK​. Reduction gives OK×​=U1​, and the successive quotients of principal-unit groups
Us​/Us+1​≅(k,+),1+πsb⟼bˉ
(35)
have order two for s≥1. Hence [U1​:U3​]=4. The four roots in μ4​={1,i,−1,−i} have distinct images modulo U3​, since
v(i−1)=v(−i−1)=1,v(−1−1)=2.
(36)
They exhaust the quotient and intersect U3​ trivially. Since r=3>2/(2−1), the logarithm isomorphism on deep principal units gives U3​≅(OK​,+). Therefore the unit decomposition of the 2-adic Gaussian field is
OK×​=μ4​×U3​≅μ4​×(OK​,+).​
(37)
In particular these four elements are all its roots of unity.
Quadratic extensions. A nonzero element of K is uniquely a power of π times a unit, so K×≅πZ×OK×​. Squaring on the decomposition above acts as squaring on μ4​ and multiplication by two on the additive ring OK​=Z2​⊕Z2​i. Thus the square-class group of the 2-adic Gaussian field is
K×/(K×)2≅C2​×(μ4​/μ42​)×(OK​/2OK​)≅C24​.​
(38)
By quadratic extensions from square classes, in characteristic different from two the nontrivial square classes classify quadratic extensions K(a​): two such extensions are K-isomorphic exactly when a/b is a square. There are therefore 24−1=15​ quadratic extensions up to K-isomorphism.
