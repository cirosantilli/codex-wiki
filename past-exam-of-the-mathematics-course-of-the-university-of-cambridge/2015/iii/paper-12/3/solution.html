An additive form of the Plünnecke inequality is the following. If A,B are nonempty finite subsets of an abelian group and ∣A+B∣≤K∣A∣, then there is one nonempty X⊆A such that, simultaneously for every integer m≥0,
∣X+mB∣≤Km∣X∣.​
(20)
Here mB is an iterated sumset and 0B={0}. In particular, its Plünnecke-Ruzsa inequality consequence is
∣kB−ℓB∣≤Kk+ℓ∣A∣(k,ℓ≥0).​
(21)
We prove both statements, so that the sumset and difference set formulations are covered.
Choose a nonempty X⊆A minimizing the ratio ∣X+B∣/∣X∣, and denote this minimum by κ. Such a minimizer exists because A is finite, and κ≤K. Every Z⊆X satisfies ∣Z+B∣≥κ∣Z∣, with the empty case also valid. We first prove the Petridis minimal-growth lemma
∣X+B+C∣≤κ∣X+C∣
(22)
for every finite nonempty C.
List C={c1​,…,ct​}. Let Ui​=X+{c1​,…,ci​} and U0​=∅. Define
Xi​={x∈X:x+ci​∈/Ui−1​},Zi​=X∖Xi​.
(23)
The new points of Ui​ are exactly Xi​+ci​, so ∣X+C∣=∑i​∣Xi​∣. Since Zi​+ci​⊆Ui−1​, the sumset Zi​+B+ci​ is already contained in Ui−1​+B. It follows that the new points introduced into Ui​+B are contained in
((X+B)∖(Zi​+B))+ci​.
(24)
As Zi​+B⊆X+B, their number is at most
∣X+B∣−∣Zi​+B∣≤κ∣X∣−κ∣Zi​∣=κ∣Xi​∣.
(25)
Summing these increments proves the Petridis minimal-growth lemma. Taking C=(m−1)B for m≥1 and iterating now gives
∣X+mB∣≤κ∣X+(m−1)B∣≤κm∣X∣≤Km∣X∣.
(26)
This proves the Plünnecke inequality with the same minimizing set X for every m.
To obtain the difference set bound, we also prove the required Ruzsa triangle inequality. For finite U,V,Z with Z=∅, choose one representation d=ud​−vd​ of each d∈U−V. The map
(U−V)×Z⟶(U−Z)×(Z−V),(d,z)⟼(ud​−z,z−vd​)
(27)
is an injection: the sum of the output coordinates recovers d, and the first coordinate then recovers z because ud​ was fixed. Thus
∣U−V∣∣Z∣≤∣U−Z∣∣Z−V∣.
(28)
Use U=kB, V=ℓB, and Z=−X. The already proved Plünnecke inequality yields
∣kB−ℓB∣≤∣X∣∣X+kB∣∣X+ℓB∣​≤κk+ℓ∣X∣≤Kk+ℓ∣A∣.
(29)
This finishes the proof of the stated Plünnecke-Ruzsa inequality. The use of an abelian group is essential in commuting the translates and sumsets in the minimal-growth argument.
