We derive the triangle removal lemma from the permitted Szemerédi regularity lemma, then turn a corner in an integer grid into a triangle in a graph.
The triangle removal lemma says that for every η>0 there are ρ>0 and N0​ such that a graph on N≥N0​ vertices with fewer than ρN3 triangles in a graph can be made triangle-free by deleting at most ηN2 edges. Here triangles in a graph are unordered triples of distinct vertices. We prove this consequence with the necessary quantitative dependence.
We may assume 0<η<1. Put d=η/4, choose an integer m0​≥4/η, and choose
0<ε≤min{η/8,d/4,1/4}.
(30)
Apply the Szemerédi regularity lemma to obtain an exceptional class V0​ of size at most εN and equal-sized classes V1​,…,Vk​ of size L, where m0​≤k≤M, and at most εk2 pairs are not regular pairs of vertex sets. The constant M depends only on these chosen parameters. Delete all edges incident with V0​, all edges within a class, all edges between irregular pairs, and all edges between pairs whose edge density of a bipartite graph is less than d. The respective costs are at most
εN2,2m0​N2​,εN2,2d​N2.
(31)
Their sum is at most ηN2/2, hence certainly at most ηN2.
If a triangle in a graph survives, it lies in three distinct classes whose three pairs are ε-regular pairs of vertex sets of edge density of a bipartite graph at least d in the original graph. We now check the needed regular triangle counting lemma. In the first class all but at most 2εL vertices have at least (d−ε)L neighbours in each of the other two classes; otherwise the definition of a regular pair of vertex sets would be violated. For each such vertex v, its two neighbour sets each have size at least εL, so their mutual edge density of a bipartite graph is at least d−ε. It follows that the original three classes contain at least
(1−2ε)(d−ε)3L3≥8d3​L3
(32)
triangles in a graph. The elementary lower bound holds because ε≤1/4 and ε≤d/4. Also L=(N−∣V0​∣)/k≥N/(2M). Thus a surviving triangle in a graph implies at least d3N3/(64M3) original triangles in a graph. Set ρ=d3/(128M3) and take N0​ large enough for the Szemerédi regularity lemma. Fewer than ρN3 original triangles in a graph force the cleaned graph to be triangle-free, proving the triangle removal lemma.
Now suppose 0<δ≤1 and A⊆[n]2. Use the tripartite graph encoding of a grid to construct a tripartite graph with disjoint labelled classes
X=[n],Y=[n],Z=[2n].
(33)
For each (a,b)∈A, put in the three edges joining a∈X, b∈Y, and a+b∈Z. These yield ∣A∣ canonical triangles in a graph. They are pairwise edge-disjoint triangles: an XY edge determines (a,b) directly, an XZ edge determines b=z−a, and a YZ edge determines a=z−b. Any deletion making the graph triangle-free must therefore remove at least ∣A∣ edges.
On the other hand, an arbitrary triangle in a graph with labels (x,y,z) gives three points of A:
(x,y),(x,z−x),(z−y,y).
(34)
Writing d=z−x−y, these are (x,y),(x,y+d),(x+d,y). If d=0, they form the required corner in an integer grid. If there is no such corner in an integer grid, every triangle in a graph is canonical, and the total number is precisely ∣A∣≤n2.
Apply the triangle removal lemma with η=δ/32. The constructed graph has N=4n vertices. For sufficiently large n, N≥N0​ and
n2<ρ(4n)3.
(35)
In the absence of a corner in an integer grid, the triangle removal lemma would destroy all triangles in a graph by deleting at most
η(4n)2=2δ​n2<δn2≤∣A∣
(36)
edges, contradicting the pairwise edge-disjoint triangles. Every sufficiently large grid therefore has the asserted corner in an integer grid in every subset of positive fixed density of a finite subset. For δ>1 there is no eligible subset, so the assertion is vacuous. This proves the corners theorem, including the stipulated nonzero displacement; no positivity of d was required.
