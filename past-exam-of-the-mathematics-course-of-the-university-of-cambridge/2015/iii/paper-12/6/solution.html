Use the part-preserving bipartite four-cycle density convention
t4​(G)=n41​∑x,x′∈X​∑y,y′∈Y​G(x,y)G(x,y′)G(x′,y)G(x′,y′).
(42)
Here G(x,y) is the indicator function of adjacency, and repeated labels are allowed. This is the homomorphism density convention appropriate to the four-cycle norm. We derive the needed counting and operator norm facts directly.
Let M=(G(x,y)) be the n-by-n biadjacency matrix, let 1 be the all-ones vector, let J=11T, and put H=M−δJ. The degree hypothesis says
M1=δn1,MT1=δn1,
(43)
so the centered matrix H annihilates 1 on both sides. In particular, HJ=JHT=0, and
MMT=δ2nJ+HHT.
(44)
The two terms have zero products in either order. Expanding the matrix trace directly counts four adjacency factors:
tr((MMT)2)=∑x,x′,y,y′​Mxy​Mx′y​Mx′y′​Mxy′​=n4t4​(G).
(45)
Since J2=nJ and trJ=n, we obtain
n4t4​(G)=δ4n4+tr((HHT)2).
(46)
The given upper bound therefore implies
tr((HHT)2)≤c4δ4n4.
(47)
This computation proves the centered four-cycle identity for a biregular graph, rather than assuming a four-cycle norm identity.
The matrix Q=HHT is a positive semidefinite matrix. By the spectral theorem for real symmetric matrices it has nonnegative eigenvalues λj​, and
λmax2​≤∑j​λj2​=tr(Q2).
(48)
Moreover, for the Euclidean norm,
∥H∥op2​=λmax​(HHT).
(49)
Indeed ∥HTv∥22​=vTQv, whose maximum on unit vectors is λmax​; the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality characterization ∥H∥op​=sup∥u∥2​=∥v∥2​=1​∣uTHv∣ also shows that H and HT have the same operator norm. Taking fourth roots thus gives
∥H∥op​≤cδn,
(50)
where the error parameter c is nonnegative, as in the claimed bound.
Finally use the indicator vectors a=1A​, b=1B​. Their Euclidean norms are ∣A∣​ and ∣B∣​, so the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality and the operator norm estimate yield
∣e(A,B)−δ∣A∣∣B∣∣​=∣aTHb∣≤∥a∥2​∥H∥op​∥b∥2​≤cδn∣A∣∣B∣​=cδαβ​n2.​
(51)
This is the required discrepancy bound. It also holds when either subset is empty or δ=0. In fact, because H annihilates constants, replacing a,b by a−α1,b−β1 gives the stronger bound cδn2α(1−α)β(1−β)​. This is the spectral discrepancy bound for a biregular graph.
