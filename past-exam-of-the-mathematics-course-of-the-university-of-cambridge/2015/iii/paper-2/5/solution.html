For a complex semisimple Lie algebra g, a Cartan subalgebra h is a maximal abelian subalgebra consisting of elements whose Adjoint representation matrices are diagonalizable. Equivalently it is a nilpotent self-normalizing Lie subalgebra. The root-space decomposition is
g=h⊕⨁α∈R​gα​,gα​={x:[h,x]=α(h)x for every h∈h},
(18)
where the roots are the nonzero weights of that Adjoint representation.
We use these properties of the Killing form B: it is nondegenerate on g and on h, it is invariant, distinct root spaces are orthogonal unless their roots sum to zero, and B pairs gα​ nondegenerately with g−α​. Define tα​ by B(tα​,h)=α(h), and choose e∈gα​, f∈g−α​ with B(e,f)=1. Since weights add, [e,f]∈h, and invariance gives
B([e,f],h)=B(e,[f,h])=α(h),[e,f]=tα​.
(19)
The nonisotropic root lemma shows α(tα​)=0. Here is its short proof: if this number were zero, the span of e,f,tα​ would be a solvable Lie algebra with tα​ central. Apply the Lie theorem to its action on g. The commutator adtα​=[ade,adf] would be strictly upper triangular and hence nilpotent. But tα​∈h makes it diagonalizable. It would therefore be zero, putting tα​ in the zero center of a Lie algebra of g, contrary to its definition. Set
Hα​=α(tα​)2tα​​,Xα​=e,Yα​=α(tα​)2f​.
(20)
Then
[Hα​,Xα​]=2Xα​,[Hα​,Yα​]=−2Yα​,[Xα​,Yα​]=Hα​,​
(21)
so their span is the sl2 subalgebra associated with a root.
The abstract reduced crystallographic root system axioms are as follows. In a finite-dimensional real inner product space E, the set R is finite, consists of nonzero vectors and spans E; for every α∈R, one has R∩Rα={α,−α}; the root reflection sα​(β)=β−2(β,α)α/(α,α) preserves R; and 2(β,α)/(α,α) is an integer for every pair of roots. We verify the positive-definite real form as well as these axioms, rather than assuming the complex Killing form is already positive.
First R is finite and has no zero element by its definition. The roots span h∗ over C: any h∈h annihilated by all roots commutes with the whole root-space decomposition and is central, hence zero. Therefore the tα​, and also the Hα​, span h over C. For every root β, the classification of finite-dimensional sl2 representations applied to the Adjoint representation of the root subalgebra gives β(Hα​)∈Z. Put hR​=spanR​{Hα​:α∈R}. Every root takes real values on this space, and
B(h,h)=tr((adh)2)=∑β∈R​(dimgβ​)β(h)2>0(0=h∈hR​).
(22)
The strict inequality follows because the roots span h∗. This also shows that the complexification of hR​ injects into h: an equality h1​+ih2​=0 with real hj​ would contradict positivity of B(h1​,h1​) and B(h2​,h2​). Since its complex span is all of h, it is a real form. Moreover B(Hα​,Hα​)=4/α(tα​)>0, so tα​ is a real scalar multiple of Hα​. The dual inner product thus makes E=spanR​R a Euclidean space, as in the Euclidean subspace of a Cartan subalgebra.
To prove reducedness without assuming it, consider the root-subalgebra module
Mα​=h⊕⨁j∈Z∖{0}​gjα​,
(23)
with absent root spaces understood to be zero. Its Hα​ weights are even, so every nontrivial Irreducible Lie algebra representation in it has even positive highest weight and one-dimensional weight-zero space. The action of Xα​ on its weight-zero space h has image exactly CXα​, of dimension one. Therefore there is precisely one nontrivial irreducible summand, the already embedded adjoint sl2​ module of highest weight 2. Thus dimgα​=1 and 2α∈/R. If β=cα is any root on the same real line, the integral numbers β(Hα​)=2c and α(Hβ​)=2/c have product 4. Hence c is one of ±21​,±1,±2; the half and double cases are excluded by applying the preceding argument to the appropriate root. This proves the root-space reducedness lemma and R∩Rα={±α}.
For a root β not parallel to α, the sum of root spaces ⨁j∈Z​gβ+jα​ is stable under the root sl2​. Its integer Hα​ weights are symmetric under sign in each irreducible summand. Therefore the weight −β(Hα​) also occurs, at the root β−β(Hα​)α. The Killing form normalization gives
β(Hα​)=(α,α)2(β,α)​,
(24)
so this root is exactly sα​(β). For β=±α the reflection just swaps the two roots. This proves reflection invariance and the Cartan integer condition. All axioms of the reduced crystallographic root system have now been verified.
