Work over C. Decompose V as the direct sum of the generalized eigenspaces Vλ​ of X. Define Xs​ to be λI on Vλ​ and set Xn​=X−Xs​. Then Xs​ is a diagonalisable endomorphism, Xn​ is a nilpotent endomorphism, and they commute. For uniqueness, any commuting decomposition X=S+N has S and N commuting with X, hence preserving each Vλ​. Decompose Vλ​ further into eigenspaces of S. On a nonzero such space with eigenvalue σ, the operator X=σI+N has only the eigenvalue σ, so σ=λ. Since S is diagonalizable, S=λI throughout Vλ​. Thus S=Xs​ and N=Xn​. This is the Additive Jordan decomposition.
The Chinese remainder theorem for the pairwise coprime polynomials (t−λ)rλ​ also gives a polynomial p with p(t)≡λ(mod(t−λ)rλ​), where rλ​ is the largest Jordan block size. Consequently Xs​=p(X) and Xn​=X−p(X). This polynomial description shows that both parts preserve every X-invariant subspace.
On Hom(Vλ​,Vμ​) the operator adXs​ is the scalar μ−λ, so it is diagonalizable. The nilpotence of commutation by a nilpotent endomorphism makes adXn​ nilpotent. They commute, since [adXs​,adXn​]=ad[Xs​,Xn​]=0. Uniqueness of the Additive Jordan decomposition therefore gives the adjoint compatibility of additive Jordan decomposition:
(adX)s​=adXs​,(adX)n​=adXn​.​

Now let g⊆gl(V) be a complex semisimple Lie algebra and X∈g. Since the semisimple part of adX is a polynomial in adX, it preserves g. Thus [Xs​,g]⊆g. By the Weyl complete reducibility theorem, the Adjoint representation of g on End(V) has a decomposition End(V)=g⊕M into invariant subspaces. Write Xs​=y+z with y∈g and z∈M. For a∈g, the vector [z,a]=[Xs​,a]−[y,a] lies in g, and invariance of M puts it in M as well. Hence [z,a]=0.
Decompose V=⨁j​Uj​ into Irreducible Lie algebra representations. Each Uj​ is preserved by Xs​=p(X) and by y, hence by z. The Schur lemma makes z∣Uj​​=cj​I. A semisimple Lie algebra is a perfect Lie algebra, so every representing element of g has zero trace on every Uj​. Also tr(Xs​∣Uj​​)=tr(X∣Uj​​), because Xn​ is nilpotent there. It follows that cj​dimUj​=tr(z∣Uj​​)=0. In characteristic zero, cj​=0, so z=0. Therefore Xs​∈g and Xn​=X−Xs​∈g. This proves that semisimple matrix Lie algebras are closed under additive Jordan decomposition, including representations with repeated isomorphic irreducible summands.
