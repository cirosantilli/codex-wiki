On a sphere of radius R rotating at angular speed Ω, the Coriolis parameter is f=2Ωsinϕ. Near latitude ϕ0​, write y=R(ϕ−ϕ0​), use locally eastward and northward Cartesian coordinates, and expand
f=f0​+βy,f0​=2Ωsinϕ0​,β=R2Ωcosϕ0​​.​
(11)
The beta plane retains the first northward variation of planetary vorticity while neglecting higher latitude dependence and metric curvature. A midlatitude local calculation assumes L/R≪1 and ∣β∣L/∣f0​∣≪1; near the equator the distinct equatorial beta plane has f0​=0, so the midlatitude low-frequency reduction used below does not apply.
For a homogeneous shallow layer, the hydrostatic approximation gives p=patm​+ρg(η−z) and hence px​/ρ=gηx​, py​/ρ=gηy​, independently of depth. The approximation follows from the small aspect ratio H/L and neglect of vertical acceleration. Differentiate the linear horizontal momentum equations in z. Their depth derivatives obey
(uz​)t​−fvz​=0,(vz​)t​+fuz​=0.
(12)
Starting from rest gives uz​=vz​=0 initially, and the unique solution remains zero. This establishes depth independence of hydrostatic shallow-water flow; an arbitrary pre-existing shear would not be removed merely by taking the hydrostatic approximation. Integrating the continuity equation between the rigid bottom and the moving surface gives ηt​+H(ux​+vy​)=0 at linear order. With the depth-integrated shallow-water transports U=Hu, V=Hv, one obtains
Ut​−fV=−c2ηx​,Vt​+fU=−c2ηy​,ηt​+Ux​+Vy​=0,c2=gH.​
(13)
It is useful to make the coefficient approximation in the height reduction explicit. Put Fy​=∂t2​+f(y)2. Differentiating the two momentum equations in time and eliminating the other transport gives
Fy​U=−c2(ηxt​+fηy​),Fy​V=−c2(ηyt​−fηx​).
(14)
Since ∂y​(Fy​V)=Fy​Vy​+2fβV, taking their horizontal divergence and using the continuity equation gives
[∇h2​η−c2Fy​η​]t​=βηx​−c22fβ​V.
(15)
Applying Fy​ proves the exact variable-Coriolis shallow-water height equation
Fy​[∇h2​η−c2Fy​η​]t​=β(ηxtt​+2fηyt​−f2ηx​).​
(16)
At the reference latitude, or after the usual local freezing of undifferentiated f factors to f0​, this gives the height relation written with F0​=∂t2​+f02​. With f=f0​+βy over a finite region, that constant-coefficient version is a local approximation, not an exact identity. Keeping Fy​ as above avoids silently commuting a variable Coriolis parameter through a spatial derivative.
For the slow Rossby wave branch, take ∣ω∣/∣f0​∣≪1, approximate F0​ by f02​, and discard the two time-derivative terms on the right compared with −f02​ηx​. This is the regular midlatitude long-time ordering, with nondegenerate zonal variation. If a=f0​/c, the result is
[∇h2​η−a2η]t​=−βηx​.​
(17)
The printed low-frequency equation has the opposite right-hand sign. The minus sign follows directly from the preceding eliminated equation and is also the sign needed for the printed isofrequency-circle centre. An independent check uses geostrophic balance: ψ=gη/f0​, (u,v)=(−ψy​,ψx​), and the shallow-water quasi-geostrophic potential vorticity is ∇h2​ψ−a2ψ+βy. Linear potential-vorticity conservation gives (∇h2​ψ−a2ψ)t​+βψx​=0.
For η=Aei(kx+ly−ωt), the shallow-water Rossby-wave dispersion relation is
ω=−k2+l2+a2βk​.​
(18)
For fixed l and β>0, it is odd in k, zero at k=0, negative for k>0, and tends to zero from below as k→+∞. Its minimum occurs at k=l2+a2​ with ωmin​=−β/[2l2+a2​]. Thus both long and short waves have small frequency. The zonal phase velocity is westward, ω/k<0, whereas the zonal group velocity is
cgx​=∂k∂ω​=(k2+l2+a2)2β(k2−l2−a2)​.
(19)
It changes sign at the frequency minimum.
At fixed nonzero frequency, completing the square yields the Rossby-wave isofrequency circle
(k+2ωβ​)2+l2=4ω2β2​−a2.​
(20)
The radius is real only if ∣ω∣≤∣β∣/(2∣a∣). For β>0, the branch with k>0 has ω<0 and a circle centred on the positive k-axis. If instead the printed plus-sign wave equation is taken literally with this same Fourier convention, its dispersion is ω=+βk/(k2+l2+a2) and its circle is centred at (+β/(2ω),0). These two conventions cannot be mixed.
For reflection of a Rossby wave at a meridional wall, the stationary wall preserves frequency, and its translation invariance in y preserves the tangential wavenumber. Thus ωr​=ωi​, lr​=li​. Both k values solve
ωi​k2+βk+ωi​(li2​+a2)=0.
(21)
Their sum and product give
kr​=−ωi​β​−ki​=ki​li2​+a2​.​
(22)
An incident wave in x>0 travels toward the wall in group velocity, not necessarily in phase velocity. For β>0, 0<ki​<li2​+a2​ has cgx,i​<0, while its partner kr​>li2​+a2​ has cgx,r​>0. The double-root case has zero normal group velocity and does not describe a wave packet incident on the wall.
At leading quasi-geostrophic approximation, the impermeability condition is u=−gηy​/f0​=0. For li​=0, the boundary condition gives
−if0​gli​​(Ai​+Ar​)ei(li​y−ωi​t)=0,Ar​=−Ai​,∣Ar​∣=∣Ai​∣.​
(23)
The reflected height therefore has equal amplitude and a phase change of π. Equality here concerns the height or quasi-geostrophic streamfunction amplitudes in the reduced model. At the degenerate li​=0, geostrophic no-normal-flow is automatically satisfied and alone does not determine their ratio; the usual homogeneous wall-streamfunction condition supplies Ar​=−Ai​ if imposed. Retaining the small ageostrophic transport changes the boundary condition to
Ai​Ar​​=−ωi​kr​+if0​li​ωi​ki​+if0​li​​,
(24)
which tends to −1 in the regular low-frequency ordering but is not generally of unit modulus. In particular, li​=0 gives Ar​/Ai​=−ki​/kr​ in that more complete boundary relation. Thus the printed equal-amplitude assertion requires the nondegenerate leading quasi-geostrophic approximation, or an explicit homogeneous wall condition; it is not a general exact shallow-water reflection law.
