For k=kz^, scalar axisymmetry leaves only m=0 in the photon quadrupole: Θ20​=−4π/5​Θ2​ with the normalization used above. The two spin-weighted spherical harmonics satisfy +2​Y20​=−2​Y20​∝sin2θ. Translation from last scattering supplies e−ikχ∗​μ. Thus the two helicity combinations have the same scalar source:
Q±iU=CΘ2​(η∗​,kz^)e−ixμ(1−μ2),x=kχ∗​,μ=cosθ,​

where C is a common normalization constant independent of μ. In this meridian polarization basis the scalar contribution has U=0; this does not mean that every rotated polarization basis has zero U.
Apply the spin-raising and lowering operators to an axisymmetric scalar potential f. The first action is −∂θ​f. In the second action, the intermediate spin is respectively +1 or −1; both give
ð2f=ð2f=∂θ2​f−cotθ∂θ​f=(1−μ2)dμ2d2f​.
(52)
It follows that
dμ2d2​(PE​±iPB​)=CΘ2​e−ixμ.​
(53)
Subtracting the two equations gives PB′′​=0. The possible affine PB​=A+Bμ consists solely of the unobservable ℓ=0,1 kernel of the twice-applied spin operators. Defining polarization potentials to have no such components sets the physical scalar B mode to zero, PB​=0​. This is the low-multipole kernel of polarization potentials, not a physical dipole polarization.
For x=0 the remaining equation integrates to PE​=−CΘ2​e−ixμ/x2+AE​+BE​μ. Remove the same monopole/dipole kernel and use the plane-wave expansion. Thus the scalar E-mode radial projection is
PE​∝ℓ≥2∑​(−i)ℓ(2ℓ+1)Θ2​(η∗​,k)(kχ∗​)2jℓ​(kχ∗​)​Pℓ​(k^⋅e).​
(54)
The overall minus sign is absorbed in the stated proportionality constant. Although the unprojected antiderivative has x−2, its divergent monopole and dipole pieces are discarded; the physical ℓ≥2 limit is finite because jℓ​(x)=O(xℓ).
The temperature-only tight-coupling quadrupole is suppressed by k/ΓT​. For regular superhorizon scalar modes, the photon dipole is itself proportional to k, so the recombination quadrupole and the resulting polarization are especially small at large angular scales. Last-scattering polarization is generated by the small departure from perfect tight coupling, not by an isotropic monopole. Reionization can generate a separate large-angle signal after recombination and is absent from the instantaneous-last-scattering approximation here. The no-B-mode conclusion applies to linear scalar sources; it does not include tensor sources or conversion by lensing.
