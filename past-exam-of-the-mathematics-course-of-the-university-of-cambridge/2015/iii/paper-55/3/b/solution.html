Along the zeroth-order photon path, set Y=Θ+ψ and S=(3/4)∫dm^Θ(m^)[1+(e⋅m^)2]/(4π). The scalar photon energy redshift and the Photon Boltzmann equation with Thomson scattering give
dηdY​=ϕ′+ψ′+τ′[Y−ψ−S−e⋅vb​].
(35)
The integrating factor is e−τ, because τ′<0 in this convention. Hence
dηd​(e−τY)=e−τ(ϕ′+ψ′)+g(η)(ψ+S+e⋅vb​),g=−τ′e−τ.
(36)
Tight coupling before decoupling makes the incident distribution nearly isotropic in the baryon frame. Neglecting its quadrupole, the angular kernel integrates to S=Θ0​, since (3/4)⟨1+(e⋅m^)2⟩=1. The primordial term at the optically thick lower endpoint is erased. With instantaneous last scattering, no later scattering and g=δ(η−η∗​), the line-of-sight solution is
Θ(η0​,x0​,e)+ψ(η0​,x0​)=(Θ0​+ψ+e⋅vb​)(η∗​,x0​−χ∗​e)+∫η∗​η0​​dη(ϕ′+ψ′)(η,x0​−(η0​−η)e).​
(37)
The approximation is linear, uses an unperturbed ray, neglects the scattering quadrupole and polarization feedback, and idealizes the cosmological visibility function as a delta function. It also excludes reionization or any other post-recombination opacity.
The photon monopole Θ0​ is the intrinsic emission-temperature perturbation. The emission potential ψ∗​ gives gravitational redshift on leaving the potential well; together they form the Sachs-Wolfe combination. The baryon peculiar velocity supplies the Doppler CMB anisotropy. The integral is the Integrated Sachs-Wolfe effect, from changes in gravitational potentials during travel. The observer potential is a direction-independent endpoint shift absorbed into the observed monopole. An observer peculiar velocity, if included, contributes an additional kinematic dipole; the stated observer is coordinate-comoving.
