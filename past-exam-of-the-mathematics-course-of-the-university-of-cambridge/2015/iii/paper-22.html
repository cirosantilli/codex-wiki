ΦC,X​:Nat(C(−,C),X)≅X(C).​
(1)

ΦC,X​(α)=αC​(1C​),ΨC,X​(x)A​(f)=X(f)(x)(f:A→C).
(2)

αA​(f)=X(f)(αC​(1C​)),

For the categorical presheaf X, its category of elements has objects (C,x) with x∈X(C). A morphism (C,x)→(D,y) is a morphism f:C→D satisfying X(f)(y)=x. Composition in a category is inherited from C: if also X(g)(z)=y, then X(gf)(z)=X(f)X(g)(z)=x. The identity morphisms are inherited as well. The forgetful functor sends (C,x) to C and f to f.
A universal element is a pair (R,r) for which each x∈X(C) is uniquely of the form X(f)(r) for f:C→R. Thus (R,r) is a terminal object of the category of elements, with the variance appropriate to a categorical presheaf.
Given a universal element, define
ψC​:C(C,R)⟶X(C),f⟼X(f)(r).
(5)
The defining uniqueness makes each map a bijection; X(u)ψC​(f)=ψC′​(fu) gives naturality for u:C′→C. Hence ψ is a natural isomorphism and X is a representable presheaf. Conversely, from a natural isomorphism ψ:C(−,R)≅X, take r=ψR​(1R​). The Yoneda lemma gives ψC​(f)=X(f)(r); its bijectivity makes (R,r) a universal element. Therefore the two descriptions coincide:
X is representable⟺Elts(X) has a terminal object.​

For a morphism f:(C,x)→(D,y) of the category of elements, the defining equation X(f)(y)=x says that the family of distinguished elements (x)(C,x)​ is compatible. Preservation gives a unique r∈X(R) with
X(uC,x​)(r)=xfor every (C,x).
(23)
In particular each uC,x​ is now a morphism (C,x)→(R,r) in the category of elements. Naturality of the colimit cocone gives
uR,r​uC,x​=uC,x​.
(24)
The universal property of the colimit forces uR,r​=1R​, since it and 1R​ agree after every cocone leg. If f:C→R also satisfies X(f)(r)=x, it is a morphism (C,x)→(R,r), and cocone compatibility now yields uC,x​=uR,r​f=f. Thus (R,r) is a universal element. By the preceding representability criterion, the presheaf is represented by the colimit object:
C(−,R)≅X,f⟼X(f)(r).​
(25)
The size qualification matters. If preservation means only small categorical limits, the implication as printed is false for general locally small categories. For an explicit counterexample, let C be the ordered category of all ordinals with an extra greatest object ∞. Put X(α)={∗} for every ordinal α and X(∞)=∅, with the forced restriction maps. Every small colimit in C is the supremum of its object values: it is an ordinal unless the diagram contains ∞. Applying X therefore gives the appropriate small categorical limit of singletons, or the empty set when a value is empty; an empty diagram gives X(0)={∗}. Hence X preserves all small categorical limits.
Its category of elements consists of all ordinals, whose projection has the large colimit ∞ in C. Nevertheless, no ordinal represents X, because its representable vanishes on larger ordinals; ∞ does not represent it either, since C(∞,∞) is nonempty while X(∞) is empty. The valid proof consequently uses preservation of the displayed possibly large limit, or a smallness hypothesis making that diagram small.

UA​:(F↓A)→X,(X,f:FX→A)⟼X
(26)
has a colimit in X. The right adjoint is obtained from these comma-category colimits. Thus the objects to construct are
GA=colim(X,f)∈(F↓A)​X.​

For sufficiency, choose such a colimit L with legs uX,f​:X→L. The morphisms f:FX→A are a cocone on FUA​. Since F preserves this colimit, there is a unique εA​:FL→A satisfying
εA​F(uX,f​)=f.
(28)
Each uX,f​ is thus a morphism (X,f)→(L,εA​) in the comma category. The original colimit cocone gives uL,εA​​uX,f​=uX,f​ for every object, hence uL,εA​​=1L​ by the universal property. For any other morphism h:(X,f)→(L,εA​), cocone compatibility gives
uX,f​=uL,εA​​h=h.

X(X,GA)≅A(FX,A),F⊣G.​

The general adjoint functor theorem has the following limit form. Let U:C→D be a functor, where C is a complete category and both categories are locally small. Then U has a left adjoint exactly when it preserves small limits and satisfies the solution-set condition.
The solution-set condition requires that, for each D∈D, there be a set of pairs (Ci​,di​:D→UCi​) such that every d:D→UC factors as
d=U(h)di​for some i and h:Ci​→C.​
(31)
Equivalently, each comma category (D↓U) has a weakly initial set. All completeness and preservation requirements here concern small categorical limits. Dually, a small-colimit-preserving functor from a cocomplete category has a right adjoint precisely when each (U↓D) has a weakly terminal set.

The Special adjoint functor theorem says: if C is a locally small category, a complete category, a well-powered category, and has a small cogenerating family, then a functor U:C→D into any locally small category has a left adjoint if and only if it preserves small categorical limits.
A small cogenerating family (Qi​)i∈I​, with I a set, distinguishes unequal parallel morphisms by postcomposition: for f=g:A→B, some q:B→Qi​ has qf=qg. Being well-powered means that the subobjects of each object form a set up to the usual equivalence of monomorphisms. These hypotheses make the solution-set condition automatic.
The dual formulation uses a cocomplete category, being a well-copowered category and having a small generating family, and concludes that every small-colimit-preserving functor has a right adjoint.

It is enough to prove the solution-set condition and then apply the general adjoint functor theorem. Fix D∈D and a morphism d:D→UC. We will factor it through one object in a set depending only on D.
First construct a minimal supported subobject. Among the subobjects m:M↪C for which d=U(m)dM​ for some dM​:D→UM, include 1C​ and take their intersection m0​:C0​↪C. This is a small intersection, by well-poweredness. It exists by completeness as the categorical limit of the diagram consisting of these monomorphisms into C. Its map to C is a monomorphism: two maps with the same composite to C have equal projections to every M, since each m is monic, and are then equal by the categorical limit property. One can equivalently construct these intersections by pullbacks in a category and small products in a category, using stability of monomorphisms under pullback in a category.
Choose the factorizations dM​. They form a compatible categorical cone into the image diagram under U, all with common composite d to UC. Preservation of small categorical limits yields d0​:D→UC0​ with U(m0​)d0​=d. If n:N↪C0​ is another subobject through which d0​ factors after applying U, then m0​n is among the original supported subobjects. The intersection property gives r:C0​→N with m0​nr=m0​. Since m0​ is monic, nr=1C0​​; since n is monic, also rn=1N​. Thus every supported subobject of C0​ is invertible.
For each member Qi​ of the small cogenerating family, consider
θi​:C(C0​,Qi​)⟶D(D,UQi​),q⟼U(q)d0​.
(32)
This map is injective. If U(q)d0​=U(q′)d0​, preservation of the equalizer of q,q′ makes d0​ factor through the image of that equalizer. Minimality makes its inclusion an isomorphism, so q=q′.
Write Ri​=D(D,UQi​); it is a set by local smallness of D. Let Si​⊆Ri​ be the image of θi​. For each s∈Si​, there is exactly one corresponding qi,s​:C0​→Qi​. These maps define the evaluation embedding into cogenerator products
e:C0​⟶PS​:=∏i∈I​∏s∈Si​​Qi​.
(33)
It is a monomorphism: if eg=eh and g=h, cogeneration supplies some q:C0​→Qi​ distinguishing g,h; that q is one of the projections of e, a contradiction. The product is small. It is important to use the subfamilies Si​, since some missing coordinate in Ri​ need not correspond to a morphism out of C0​.
There are only a set of possible families (Si​⊆Ri​)i∈I​. For each such family form PS​, choose a set of representatives N↪PS​ of its subobjects, and take all pairs (N,dN​:D→UN). Local smallness of D and well-poweredness make their union a set. In the case just constructed, e identifies C0​ with one chosen representative N. Transporting d0​ to UN and composing the inverse identification with m0​ gives a factorization of the original d through that pair. Therefore these pairs form a weakly initial set in (D↓U).
The general adjoint functor theorem now supplies a left adjoint to U. Conversely, a right adjoint preserves small categorical limits, either by the adjunction hom-set bijections and the hom-set detection of categorical limits, or directly from their universal properties. Hence the stated special theorem is proved in both directions.

If GT is fully faithful, the criterion proved for a right adjoint says that the adjunction counit of FT⊣GT is a natural isomorphism. Its component at (A,a) is precisely the algebra action a. In particular, its component at the free algebra for a monad (TX,μX​) has underlying morphism μX​:T2X→TX. Thus every μX​ is an isomorphism, and the natural transformation μ is invertible.
