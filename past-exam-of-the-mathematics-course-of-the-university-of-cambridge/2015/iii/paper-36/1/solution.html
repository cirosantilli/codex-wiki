Put p=dP/dμ and q=dQ/dμ. Using natural logarithms, the Kullback-Leibler divergence is
K(P,Q)=∫Ω​plogqp​dμ.​
(1)
The integrand is zero where p=0, including where both densities vanish, and is +∞ where p>0=q. Equivalently, K(P,Q)=∫log(dP/dQ)dP when P is absolutely continuous with respect to Q, and it is +∞ otherwise. This is a well-defined extended integral: on p<q, writing t=p/q gives −plog(p/q)=−qtlogt≤q/e, so its negative part is integrable.
For Pinsker's inequality, an infinite Kullback-Leibler divergence makes the conclusion immediate. Assume it is finite. Set
A={p≥q},a=P(A),b=Q(A),t=a−b.
(2)
Since ∫(p−q)dμ=0, the positive and negative parts of p−q have equal integrals. Thus
∫∣p−q∣dμ=2t,t≥0.
(3)
This t is the total variation distance with the convention supE​∣P(E)−Q(E)∣.
