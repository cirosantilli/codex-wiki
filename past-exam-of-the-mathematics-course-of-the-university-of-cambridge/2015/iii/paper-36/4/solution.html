We first construct a unit sphere net from ball covering. Enlarge the numerical covering constant, if necessary, to A0​≥1. Cover the unit ball in Rk with at most (2A0​/δ)k balls of radius at most δ/2. For each such ball meeting the unit sphere, choose a point of the sphere in it and discard the others. These selected points form a metric net N of the unit sphere with radius δ: any two points in one covering ball have distance at most δ. This argument ensures that the net points have unit length even if the original covering centers did not.
