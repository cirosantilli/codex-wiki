We prove the required Hahn-Banach theorem for a seminorm directly. First suppose the vector space is real. At an intermediate extension stage, let g:E→R be a linear functional with ∣g(y)∣≤p(y), and let z∈/E. Extending to E+Rz amounts to choosing c=f(z). The upper domination f≤p requires
supy∈E​{g(y)−p(y−z)} ≤c≤ infw∈E​{p(w+z)−g(w)}.
(22)
The interval is nonempty: for all y,w∈E,
g(y)+g(w)=g(y+w)≤p(y+w)≤p(y−z)+p(w+z).
(23)
Its endpoints are finite since inserting zero gives bounds −p(z) and p(z), while every lower candidate is below every upper candidate. Choose c in the interval and put f(y+tz)=g(y)+tc. For t>0, the upper bound with w=y/t proves f(y+tz)≤p(y+tz). For t<0, writing s=−t>0 and applying the lower bound with y/s proves the same inequality. The case t=0 is already known. Applying this domination to −x and using p(−x)=p(x) gives ∣f(x)∣≤p(x).
Partially order all dominated linear functional extensions of the original g by extension of their domains and values. They form a nonempty set. The union along any chain is a well-defined dominated linear functional on a vector subspace, so every chain has an upper bound. The Zorn lemma gives a maximal extension. If its domain were not X, the preceding one-dimensional construction would enlarge it, a contradiction. Thus the required extension exists on X.
For a complex vector space, apply the proved real result to Reg on the underlying real vector subspace. Let U:X→R be the resulting real linear functional, with ∣U(x)∣≤p(x), and set
f(x)=U(x)−iU(ix).
(24)
It is additive and real-linear, and f(ix)=if(x), so it is complex-linear. On Y, the identity Reg(iy)=−Img(y) proves f(y)=g(y). Choose α with ∣α∣=1 and αf(x)=∣f(x)∣ when f(x)=0. Then
∣f(x)∣=Ref(αx)=U(αx)≤p(αx)=p(x).
(25)
The bound is immediate if f(x)=0. This proves the real and complex seminorm forms of Hahn-Banach theorem without invoking any version of that theorem.
For the distance to a set assertion, let d=d(z,Y)>0. Define on Y+Kz the linear functional g(y+αz)=αd. The decomposition is unique because z∈/Y, and for α=0,
∣g(y+αz)∣=∣α∣d≤∣α∣∥z+y/α∥=∥y+αz∥.
(26)
For α=0 the bound is immediate. Apply the just-proved Hahn-Banach theorem with p(x)=∥x∥ to get f∈X∗, f∣Y​=0, f(z)=d, and ∥f∥≤1. Choose yn​∈Y with ∥z−yn​∥→d. Since
d=∣f(z−yn​)∣≤∥f∥∥z−yn​∥,
(27)
and d>0, passage to the limit gives ∥f∥≥1. Thus f∣Y​=0,f(z)=d(z,Y),∥f∥=1​. This version of the Hahn-Banach distance formula needs neither a closed Y nor an attained distance to a set.
For the Banach limit construction below, take the real l-infinity sequence space, let S(x1​,x2​,…)=(x2​,x3​,…) be its left shift on bounded sequences, put Y=(I−S)ℓ∞, and let 1=(1,1,…). This is a vector subspace and its distance to a set from 1 determines the construction.
