Conditioning on the intensity in the Poisson mixture gives
GN​(z)=E[eΛ(z−1)]=(1−qzp​)2.
(10)
Thus N has the negative binomial distribution with two successes and success probability p, counting failures; explicitly P(N=j)=(j+1)p2qj for j≥0. The probability generating function is finite for real z<1/q.
The claim-size moment-generating function is MX​(t)=(1−μt)−1. Substituting into the aggregate moment-generating function and using p+q=1 gives
MS​(t)=(p−μtp(1−μt)​)2​,t<μp​.
(11)
Put L(t)=p/(p−μt), the moment-generating function of an exponential distribution with rate p/μ. The identity
p−μtp(1−μt)​=p+qL(t)
(12)
then yields
MS​(t)=p2+2pqL(t)+q2L(t)2.
(13)
The gamma-mixed Poisson aggregate with exponential claims has three nonnegative mixture weights summing to one. By uniqueness of the moment-generating function near zero, the aggregate distribution is
L(S)=p2δ0​+2pqExp(p/μ)+q2Gamma(2,rate p/μ).​
(14)
Here δ0​ is the Dirac measure at zero. In particular P(S=0)=p2, consistently with P(N=0). The positive components have respective expected values μ/p and 2μ/p; their mixture distribution accounts for the possibility of no aggregate payout.
