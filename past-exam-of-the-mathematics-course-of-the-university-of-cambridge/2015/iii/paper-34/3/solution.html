Use cprem​ for the premium income rate, reserving c later for the smaller exponential decay rate. In the classical risk model, the surplus is
Ut​=u+cprem​t−∑j=1Nt​​Xj​,cprem​=(1+ρ)λμ.
(24)
Here ρ is the relative safety loading. The aggregate claims form a Compound Poisson process. Define Lt​=∑j=1Nt​​Xj​−cprem​t, so ruin occurs when Lt​>u. By independent increments and the exponential formula for a marked Poisson sum,
E[eR(Lt​−Ls​)]=exp{(t−s)[λ(M(R)−1)−cprem​R]}=1.
(25)
Thus Zt​=eRLt​ is a nonnegative continuous-time martingale with Z0​=1, because the adjustment coefficient makes the exponent vanish.
Let τ=inf{t:Ut​<0}. Apply the optional stopping theorem at the bounded stopping time τ∧T. On {τ≤T}, Lτ​>u, so
1=E[Zτ∧T​]≥eRuP(τ≤T).
(26)
Letting T increase proves the Lundberg inequality and the unscaled limit:
ψ(u)≤e−Ru,u→∞lim​ψ(u)=0.​
(27)
For the precise asymptotic, put
h=cprem​λμ​=1+ρ1​,z(u)=eRuψ(u),k(x)=heRxfI​(x),g(u)=heRu∫u∞​fI​(x)dx.
(28)
The given exponential integral identity makes k a probability density. Multiplying the given defective renewal equation by eRu turns it into the ordinary renewal equation
z(u)=g(u)+∫0u​z(u−x)k(x)dx.
(29)
For clarity, the version of the key renewal theorem used here is: if the interarrival law is nonarithmetic, has mean mk​∈(0,∞), and g is directly Riemann integrable, the locally bounded solution of this renewal equation satisfies z(u)→mk−1​∫0∞​g(v)dv. The infinite-mean version gives zero for nonnegative directly Riemann integrable g.
All the hypotheses can be checked here. The density k gives a nonarithmetic distribution. The Tonelli theorem gives
∫0∞​g(u)du=h∫0∞​fI​(x)ReRx−1​dx=R1−h​.
(30)
Furthermore
g(u)=∫u∞​e−R(x−u)k(x)dx,g′(u)=Rg(u)−k(u)almost everywhere.
(31)
Thus g is continuous and integrable, and ∫0∞​∣g′(u)∣du≤R∫g+1<∞. On a mesh of width δ, the difference between its upper and lower sums is at most δ∫∣g′∣; its upper sum is at most ∫g+δ∫∣g′∣. This proves direct Riemann integrability rather than assuming it. Also 0≤z(u)≤1 by the Lundberg inequality, so the solution is locally bounded. Its renewal representation is z=g∗Uk​, where Uk​=∑j≥0​K∗j and K(dx)=k(x)dx; the residual after iteration tends to zero on compact intervals because sums of positive interarrivals tend to infinity.
Writing J=∫0∞​xeRxfI​(x)dx, the tilted interarrival expected value is mk​=hJ. The key renewal theorem gives the Cramér–Lundberg ruin asymptotic
u→∞lim​eRuψ(u)=RhJ1−h​=R∫0∞​xeRxfI​(x)dxρ​=A.​
(32)
If J=∞, the same formula is interpreted as A=0. A positive finite asymptotic constant requires J<∞; this extra integrability is not explicitly stated in the paper.
For the final two-exponential case, evaluate the defective renewal equation at zero:
h=ψ(0)=a+b,ρ=a+b1−a−b​​.
(33)
One can identify the adjustment coefficient without silently assuming A>0. For 0<r<c, set
P(r)=∫0∞​eruψ(u)du=c−ra​+d−rb​.
(34)
It is finite and positive. Integrating the nonnegative terms of the defective renewal equation, using the Tonelli theorem, first shows that FI​(r)=∫0∞​erxfI​(x)dx is finite and then gives
P(r)=rh​[FI​(r)−1]+hP(r)FI​(r),hFI​(r)=1+rP(r)h+rP(r)​.
(35)
As r↑c, P(r)→∞ because a>0. By monotone convergence theorem, hFI​(c)=1. The integrated tail distribution in the classical risk model has density fI​(x)=P(X1​>x)/μ, so FI​(r)=[M(r)−1]/(μr) by the tail integral formula for moments. Hence c solves the adjustment equation, and its stipulated uniqueness implies R=c. Finally the displayed form of ψ gives the remaining constants
R=c,A=a,ρ=a+b1−a−b​.​
(36)
In particular the decay exponent d and the coefficient b do not affect R or A. The c in these final answers is the printed decay rate, not cprem​.
