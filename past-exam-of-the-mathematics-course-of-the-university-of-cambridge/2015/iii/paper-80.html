Use the Cartesian streamfunction convention ux​=∂y​Ψ, uy​=−∂x​Ψ. In the creeping-flow regime implicit in the question's biharmonic equation hint, the Stokes equations are μ∇2u=∇p, ∇⋅u=0. Taking their curl eliminates pressure and gives
∇4Ψ=0.​
(1)
The fluid occupies the region above the actual sheet; the perturbation calculation expands its boundary about y=0. In the swimming frame the material velocity is (0,−ωy0​cos(kx−ωt)). The vertical kinematic condition and the supplied Navier slip boundary condition therefore give, at y=y0​sin(kx−ωt),
Ψx​=ωy0​cos(kx−ωt),Ψy​=γ(Ψyy​−Ψxx​).
(2)
At infinity, Ψy​→U and Ψx​→0, with bounded velocity and no imposed shear; all fields are periodic in x with period 2π/k. The sign is important: a sheet translating at −Uex​ sees the remote fluid translating at +Uex​.
Set X=kx, Y=ky, τ=ωt, ψ=k2Ψ/ω, and U∗=kU/ω. The two dimensionless parameters are the small-slope approximation parameter ϵ=ky0​ and dimensionless slip length δ=kγ. With θ=X−τ, the dimensionless biharmonic stream function for planar Stokes flow satisfies
(∂X2​+∂Y2​)2ψψX​ψY​​=0,=ϵcosθ,ψY​=δ(ψYY​−ψXX​)⟶U∗,ψX​⟶0​​at Y=ϵsinθ,as Y⟶∞.​
(3)
Expand ψ=ϵψ1​+ϵ2ψ2​+⋯ and U∗=ϵU1​+ϵ2U2​+⋯. At first order,
(ψ1​)X​(X,0,τ)=cosθ,(ψ1​)Y​(X,0,τ)=δ[(ψ1​)YY​−(ψ1​)XX​](X,0,τ).
(4)
The decaying first harmonic of the biharmonic equation has the form (A+BY)e−Ysinθ. The vertical condition fixes A=1 and excludes a cosine component. The horizontal condition gives
B−1=δ[(1−2B)−(−1)]=−2δ(B−1).
(5)
Thus (1+2δ)(B−1)=0 and B=1 for every δ≥0. The spatially averaged first-order streamfunction is affine in Y; its Navier slip boundary condition forces U1​=0. Therefore
ψ1​=(1+Y)e−Ysin(X−τ),U1​=0.​
(6)
This first-order slip independence of a transverse sheet is independent of slip length. Its surface tangential velocity and surface shear are both zero: (ψ1​)Y​(0)=0 and (ψ1​)YY​(0)−(ψ1​)XX​(0)=0. This explains why the first-order streamfunction agrees with the no-slip boundary condition.
To find the slip-enhanced swimming speed of a transverse sheet, expand only the Navier slip boundary condition through second order. Put Qj​=(ψj​)YY​−(ψj​)XX​. Taylor expansion at the displaced boundary yields
(ψ2​)Y​−δQ2​=−sinθ(ψ1​)YY​+δsinθ∂Y​Q1​at Y=0.
(7)
Direct differentiation gives
(ψ1​)YY​(0)=−sinθ,Q1​=2Ye−Ysinθ,∂Y​Q1​(0)=2sinθ.
(8)
Hence
(ψ2​)Y​(X,0,τ)−δQ2​(X,0,τ)=(1+2δ)sin2θ.
(9)
Average over X. The zero Fourier series mode of a biharmonic streamfunction is a cubic polynomial in Y; bounded velocity at infinity removes the quadratic and cubic terms. Thus its velocity is the constant U2​, and its averaged shear ⟨Q2​⟩X​ is zero. The mean boundary velocity determines Taylor-sheet swimming speed principle then gives
U2​=21+2δ​,U∗=2ϵ2​(1+2δ)+O(ϵ4).​
(10)
The even remainder follows because changing the sign of the amplitude is equivalent to a half-period phase shift. In dimensional form,
Vsheet​=−2ωky02​​(1+2kγ)ex​+O(kω​ϵ4).​
(11)
For the Navier-slip Taylor swimming sheet, the ratio of speed to the no-slip value is 1+2δ>1 whenever δ>0: slip increases the swimming speed. The expansion is for fixed δ as ϵ→0; it is not a uniform prediction of arbitrarily large speed if the slip length is allowed to diverge with the inverse amplitude.

Let s be arclength, t=∂s​r the unit tangent, and v the local filament velocity relative to the fluid. The resistive-force theory force per unit arclength, exerted by the fluid on the filament, is
f=−ξ∥​(v⋅t)t−ξ⊥​[v−(v⋅t)t]=−ξ⊥​v+(ξ⊥​−ξ∥​)(v⋅t)t.​
(12)
The parallel and perpendicular drag coefficients of a slender filament are proportional to dynamic viscosity. For radius a and a relevant axial cutoff length ℓ≫a, the anisotropic slender-filament drag from Stokeslet integration gives the leading logarithmic forms
ξ∥​∼ln(ℓ/a)2πμ​,ξ⊥​∼ln(ℓ/a)4πμ​.
(13)
The cutoff may be set by filament length or deformation wavelength. End shape and the choice of cutoff change order-one constants inside the logarithms. Slender-body theory also includes nonlocal interactions that the local resistive-force theory approximation omits. Typically ξ⊥​>ξ∥​, with ratio tending to two in the slender limit; this drag anisotropy enables propulsion.
Write C=ξ⊥​−ξ∥​>0. For a small-amplitude planar deformation, t=(1,yx​)+O(yx2​) and the leading transverse velocity is yt​. Exact inextensibility requires the quadratic longitudinal displacement of an inextensible planar filament: if X(s,t)=s+X2​(s,t) and Y(s,t)=y(s,t), then X2,s​=−ys2​/2 to this order. Its velocity is quadratic and has zero time average for a periodic material cycle without mean axial drift. Thus the fixed material abscissa in the question is leading-order notation; this correction does not change the averaged propulsive term below. An imposed mean translation would contribute its separate ordinary axial drag.
To leading nonzero order,
fx​=−ξ∥​vx​+Cyx​yt​,−f⋅v=ξ⊥​yt2​+higher-order terms,ds=dx+higher-order terms.
(14)
The time-averaged deformation-induced propulsive force of a periodic planar filament and power of a periodic planar filament are consequently
Fx​=TC​∫0T​∫0λ​yx​yt​dxdt,W˙=Tξ⊥​​∫0T​∫0λ​yt2​dxdt.​
(15)
These are the leading quadratic functionals. Their signs refer to force on the filament. For example, y=asin(kx−Ωt) gives negative Fx​, opposing the direction of wave propagation.
Let η(x,t) be an arbitrary smooth variation periodic in both variables. The first variation of the constrained functional is
δJ=T1​∫0T​∫0λ​[C(yt​ηx​+yx​ηt​)+2Γξ⊥​yt​ηt​]dxdt.
(16)
Using integration by parts in x and t, every boundary term cancels by periodic boundary conditions. Therefore
δJ=−T2​∫0T​∫0λ​(Cyxt​+Γξ⊥​ytt​)ηdxdt.
(17)
The Euler-Lagrange equation is
Cyxt​+Γξ⊥​ytt​=0.​
(18)
For a nontrivial stroke with nonzero thrust, Γ=0. Set c=C/(Γξ⊥​) and v=yt​. Then vt​+cvx​=0, whose method of characteristics solution is v=V(x−ct). Integrating in time gives the travelling-wave stationarity of planar filament propulsion:
y(x,t)=f(x−ct)+g(x),c=Γξ⊥​ξ⊥​−ξ∥​​.​
(19)
Here g is an arbitrary static periodic component. It contributes neither power nor mean propulsion because the time average of yt​ vanishes. It can be set to zero when describing the active stroke. The wave must be compatible with both specified periods: f(x−cT)=f(x).
For a pure travelling wave, its leading force and power are
Fx​=−Cc∫0λ​[f′(x)]2dx,W˙=ξ⊥​c2∫0λ​[f′(x)]2dx,W˙Fx​​=−ξ⊥​cC​.​
(20)
Thus positive x propulsion uses a wave travelling in the negative x direction. Any smooth interior maximizer satisfying only the stated power constraint must have this travelling active deformation. If the drag were isotropic, C=0 and there would be no leading propulsion.
There is a qualification to the printed maximization claim: the variation proves stationarity, not the existence of an unrestricted global maximum. The bandwidth limitation in fixed-power filament optimization can be seen directly. Set k=2π/λ, Ω=2π/T, and consider ym​=asin(mkx+Ωt). The leading expressions give
W˙=21​ξ⊥​λa2Ω2,Fx​=21​Cλa2mkΩ.
(21)
At fixed power the formal quadratic objective increases with m. Eventually that sequence violates the small-slope approximation, but the approximation supplies no numerical slope or wavelength cutoff with which to define the global optimization. Even within a strict small-slope neighbourhood, a sufficiently small admixture of a higher spatial harmonic can improve a candidate interior maximum while keeping power fixed. For example, y=a[1−η2​sin(kx+Ωt)+ηsin(mkx+Ωt)] retains the fundamental periods and exactly the same leading power, while its force is multiplied by 1+(m−1)η2. For fixed m>1, taking a and η small keeps every slope small.
With an explicit admissible Fourier bandwidth or an additional geometric constraint, the intended conclusion can be made precise. For a finite set of nonstatic Fourier series modes proportional to ei(kj​x−Ωj​t), with wavenumbers kj​ and frequencies Ωj​,
W˙Fx​​=−ξ⊥​C​∑j​Ωj2​∣aj​∣2∑j​kj​Ωj​∣aj​∣2​.
(22)
This is a weighted average of −Ckj​/(ξ⊥​Ωj​). A largest allowed ratio is attained by a single travelling mode, or by modes sharing the same phase speed; their superposition is still a travelling wave. Thus the Euler-Lagrange calculation yields the requested travelling-wave form, while a global maximum requires a specified admissible shape class.

A freely swimming body has no net external force or torque in Stokes flow. Its force-free condition removes the r−1 Stokeslet monopole, and its torque-free condition removes the antisymmetric force-dipole contribution. The leading generic far field is therefore a symmetric traceless rank-two tensor stresslet, decaying as r−2. Its axial strength may vanish for special swimmers, in which case higher-order singularities dominate.
The axisymmetric stresslet from a Stokeslet pair provides an explicit construction and fixes the sign convention. Place forces +Fe and −Fe on the fluid at +(a/2)e and −(a/2)e. The Stokeslet tensor is
Jij​(r)=8πμ1​(rδij​​+r3ri​rj​​).
(23)
Expanding their sum for r≫a gives
u=FJ(r−2a​e)e−FJ(r+2a​e)e=−Fa(e⋅∇)[J(r)e]+⋯.
(24)
Since
(e⋅∇)[J(r)e]=8πμ1​[r3r​−r53(e⋅r)2r​],
(25)
the dipole strength is S=Fa and the resulting stresslet is the given one. More general surface-traction distributions produce the same leading form through their symmetric traceless first force moment.
Writing cosα=e⋅r, its radial velocity is
ur​=8πμr2S​(3cos2α−1).
(26)
Consequently
S>0: pusher, axial outflow and equatorial inflow;S<0: puller, axial inflow and equatorial outflow.​
(27)
This classifies pusher microswimmers and puller microswimmers in the force-on-fluid convention used by the given formula.
For the circular swimmer let φ=ωt, p=(cosφ,sinφ,0), x′=Rp, and choose the tangent director e=(−sinφ,cosφ,0). Its sign is immaterial to the stresslet. Write r=x for the observation vector from the circle centre, and assume r≫R. The leading far-field orbit average of a tangent stresslet is obtained by replacing x−x′ by r while retaining the time-dependent director.
The orientation averaging of an axisymmetric stresslet over a full revolution gives
⟨ei​ej​⟩=21​(δij​−zi​zj​),⟨(e⋅r)2⟩=21​(r2−z2).
(28)
Substitution gives
⟨u(r)⟩​=8πμS​[−r3r​+23​r5(r2−z2)r​]+higher multipoles=−8πμS/2​[−r3r​+r53z2r​]+higher multipoles.​
(29)
Thus
S=−2S​,e=z​
(30)
with e equivalently −z. The effective axis is normal to the orbit plane. A positive original strength becomes negative, and a negative strength becomes positive: a pusher averages to a puller, and a puller averages to a pusher, at leading far-field order. The result is independent of R and ω at this order.
The accuracy of the centre replacement can be made explicit. Half a revolution changes p and e to their negatives; the stresslet is even in e. Pairing those times replaces the displaced field by
21​[G(r−Rp,e)+G(r+Rp,e)]=G(r,e)+O(μr4∣S∣R2​).
(31)
Hence
⟨u⟩=G(r;S,z)+O(μr4∣S∣R2​).​
(32)
The finite-radius average is not exactly a point stresslet at every location. For example, on the positive z axis the exact average of the supplied model is
⟨u(0,0,z)⟩=−8πμS​(z2+R2)3/2z​z,
(33)
which has the derived point-stresslet limit for z≫R. This even displacement correction in an orbit-averaged stresslet is consistent with the far-field restriction.
