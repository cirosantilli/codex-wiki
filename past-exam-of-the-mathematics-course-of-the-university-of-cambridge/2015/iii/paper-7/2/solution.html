On a compact metric space X, a conservative Feller semigroup is a strongly continuous semigroup of positive linear maps on C(X) satisfying Pt​1=1. Positivity means f≥0⟹Pt​f≥0. These conditions imply sup-norm contraction. Conversely, for a contraction semigroup with 1∈D(L) and L1=0, differentiation on the generator domain gives dtd​Pt​1=Pt​L1=0, so Pt​1=1. On the real space C(X), if 0≤f≤1, then ∥1−Pt​f∥∞​≤∥1−f∥∞​≤1, implying Pt​f≥0. Scaling proves positivity for every nonnegative f. This is the unital contraction positivity criterion. For complex C(X) the same conclusion follows because each functional f↦Pt​f(x) has norm one and value one on 1, hence is a positive linear functional.
For each t,x, the Riesz-Markov-Kakutani representation theorem represents that functional by a unique Borel probability measure pt​(x,dy):
Pt​f(x)=∫X​f(y)pt​(x,dy).​
(5)
Continuity of Pt​f gives weak continuity of x↦pt​(x,⋅). Approximating indicators of open sets increasingly by continuous functions proves Borel measurability in x; a monotone-class argument then gives a Markov kernel. The semigroup property and uniqueness of representing measures yield p0​(x,⋅)=δx​ and the Chapman-Kolmogorov equation
ps+t​(x,A)=∫X​pt​(y,A)ps​(x,dy).
(6)
These are the required transition probabilities.
An invariant probability measure for a semigroup satisfies ∫X​Pt​fdμ=∫X​fdμ for every t≥0 and f∈C(X). Equivalently its distribution is preserved by the Markov kernel. For 1≤p<∞, Jensen inequality gives ∣Pt​f∣p≤Pt​(∣f∣p) pointwise. Integrating and using invariance proves
∥Pt​f∥Lp(μ)​≤∥f∥Lp(μ)​.​
(7)
Also ∥Pt​f−f∥Lp(μ)​≤∥Pt​f−f∥∞​→0. Continuous functions are dense in these Lp spaces for a finite Borel measure on a compact metric space, so the contraction extends to a strongly continuous semigroup on Lp(μ).
For real f with f,f2∈D(L), the kernel form of Jensen inequality gives Pt​(f2)−(Pt​f)2≥0, with equality at t=0. Take its right derivative to obtain the generator square inequality
L(f2)≥2fLf.​
(8)
This is nonnegativity of the associated carré du champ.
Each time-average functional ϕn​ is positive with ϕn​(1)=1, hence represents a Borel probability measure μn​. The compactness of probability measures on a compact metric space gives a subsequence converging weakly to a probability measure μ. Explicitly, C(X) is a separable Banach space, so Banach-Alaoglu theorem and a countable dense family give a subsequence converging on every continuous function, rather than merely a net. For fixed t≥0, the semigroup property gives the Krylov-Bogolyubov time-average argument:
ϕn​(Pt​f)−ϕn​(f)=n1​[∫nn+t​∫X​Ps​fdνds−∫0t​∫X​Ps​fdνds].
(9)
Its absolute value is at most 2t∥f∥∞​/n. Pass to the subsequential limit, observing that Pt​f∈C(X), to get ∫X​Pt​fdμ=∫X​fdμ. Thus the limiting measure is invariant.
