A standard form of the Kakutani solution of the Dirichlet problem uses a bounded domain D⊂Rd, continuous Dirichlet boundary data f∈C(∂D), and regularity of every boundary point for the Dirichlet problem. The boundedness of D makes ∂D a compact set, so f is a bounded function and a uniformly continuous function. A bounded domain with C2 boundary is a sufficient geometric case; one must not omit boundary regularity for an arbitrary bounded domain.
One standard analytic characterization of a regular boundary point on a bounded domain is the existence of a positive harmonic barrier: for each ξ∈∂D there is a harmonic function hξ​∈C(D) with hξ​(ξ)=0 and hξ​(x)>0 for x∈D∖{ξ}. This is the harmonic form of a barrier for the Dirichlet problem; the barrier characterization of regularity is a standard fact of potential theory.
Let B be d-dimensional Brownian motion started at x∈D and let τD​=inf{t≥0:Bt​∈/D} be its Brownian exit time. Then the unique solution in C(D)∩C2(D) is
u(x)=Ex​[f(BτD​​)](x∈D),u(ξ)=f(ξ)(ξ∈∂D).​
(17)
Equivalently, u(x)=∫∂D​f(ξ)ωD​(x,dξ), where ωD​ is harmonic measure, the exit probability distribution of Brownian motion. The harmonic function equation is Δu=0, with the Laplace operator convention Δ=∑j​∂j2​.
