For fixed rational 0≤t1​<t2​<t3​<t4​, write
maxt3​≤t≤t4​​Bt​−maxt1​≤t≤t2​​Bt​=ZBt3​​−Bt2​​​​+Vt3​≤t≤t4​max​(Bt​−Bt3​​)​​+WBt2​​−t1​≤t≤t2​max​Bt​​​.
(16)
By independent increments, Z is independent of (V,W) and has normal distribution N(0,t3​−t2​). Conditional on (V,W), the displayed difference has a continuous probability density function; in particular it equals zero with probability zero. Taking the countable intersection over all such rational quadruples proves that, on one event of probability one, the maxima over any two separated rational closed intervals are distinct.
Suppose a local maximum at t were not a strict local maximum. There is a neighbourhood in which every value is at most Bt​, and, arbitrarily close to t, some other time u strictly inside that neighbourhood has Bu​=Bt​. Choose separated rational closed intervals containing t and u, both lying inside that neighbourhood. When one time is zero, use a first interval with left endpoint zero. Both interval maxima equal Bt​, contrary to the preceding event.
Therefore every local maximum of Brownian motion is a strict local maximum almost surely, simultaneously over all times. This countable-interval argument avoids an invalid intersection of probability-one events over uncountably many candidate times.
