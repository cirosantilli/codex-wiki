Write z−=max(−z,0). For a<b, let UN​[a,b] be the upcrossing count obtained by successively buying at an observation at or below a and selling at an observation at or above b, with the first purchase allowed at time zero. The Doob upcrossings lemma for an integrable discrete-time martingale is
(b−a)EUN​[a,b]≤E(XN​−a)−−E(X0​−a)−≤E(XN​−a)−.​
(1)
The weaker last bound is also the usual Doob upcrossing inequality.
To prove it, hold one unit between each purchase and sale, and zero units otherwise. If Hk​ records the holding over (k,k+1], the decision is Fk​-measurable, with Hk​∈{0,1}. The resulting martingale transform has gain
GN​=∑k=0N−1​Hk​(Xk+1​−Xk​),EGN​=0.
(2)
Each completed upcrossing earns at least b−a. An unfinished holding loses at most (XN​−a)−. If a purchase occurs at zero, its price is X0​≤a, giving the additional discount (X0​−a)−, whether that holding has been sold or remains open. If no purchase occurs at zero, this extra term is zero. Therefore, path by path,
GN​≥(b−a)UN​[a,b]−(XN​−a)−+(X0​−a)−.
(3)
Taking expectations proves the bound.
The martingale convergence theorem, stated without proof, says that a discrete-time martingale satisfying supn​E∣Xn​∣<∞ converges almost surely to a finite integrable random variable X∞​. In particular every nonnegative martingale converges almost surely to a finite limit. Under uniform integrability, the uniformly integrable martingale convergence theorem additionally gives convergence in L1 and Xn​=E[X∞​∣Fn​].
