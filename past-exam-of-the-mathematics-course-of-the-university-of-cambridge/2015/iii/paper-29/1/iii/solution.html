Put Zn​=Xn∧T​. The stopped martingale in discrete time identity
Zn+1​−Zn​=1{T>n}​(Xn+1​−Xn​)
(5)
shows that Z is a martingale: the indicator function is Fn​-measurable and the increment has zero conditional expectation. Integrability follows because Zn​ is selected from the finitely many integrable values X0​,…,Xn​.
For the bounded stopping time τ=n∧T, the optional stopping theorem in its conditional form gives
Xτ​=E[Xn​∣Fτ​].
(6)
Here Fτ​ is the stopping-time sigma-algebra. Indeed, for A∈Fτ​, partition A into A∩{τ=k}∈Fk​ and apply the martingale identity E[Xn​∣Fk​]=Xk​ on each piece. This proves the displayed conditional expectation identity directly.
Let C=supn​E∣Xn​∣<∞ and An​={∣Zn​∣>K}∈Fn∧T​. Conditional absolute-value domination and the Markov inequality give P(An​)≤C/K and, for any R>0,
E[∣Zn​∣1An​​]​≤E[∣Xn​∣1An​​]≤E[∣Xn​∣1{∣Xn​∣>R}​]+RP(An​)≤jsup​E[∣Xj​∣1{∣Xj​∣>R}​]+KRC​.​
(7)
First choose R using the uniform integrability of X, and then choose K. The estimate is uniform in n, proving uniform integrability of a stopped uniformly integrable martingale. No finiteness assumption on T is needed.
