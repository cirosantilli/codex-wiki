Continue with downward coordinate y=H−z and W=Ws​(a)>0. By Stokes drag law, the small-particle isolated settling velocity is W/4. Normalize concentrations by ϕmax​: write qi​=ϕi​/ϕmax​. In suspension the specified independent hindered settling laws give fluxes j1​/ϕmax​=Wq1​(1−q1​) and j2​/ϕmax​=(W/4)q2​(1−q2​). The initial state is (q1​,q2​)=(1/8,1/8).
Two upper sedimentation shocks clear the species separately. The faster front removes large particles, leaving (0,1/8) behind it; the small concentration is unchanged across this front because the species fluxes are independent. The slower front separates that small-only suspension from clear fluid. Their downward velocities are
U1​=87​W,U2​=327​W.​
(37)
There is also a growing mixed deposit at the bottom. Let its normalized solid concentrations be (p1​,p2​), with p1​+p2​=1, and let its upward speed magnitude be s=−U3​>0. Applying the Rankine-Hugoniot condition to each species gives
s(p1​−1/8)=647​W,s(p2​−1/8)=2567​W.
(38)
Adding these relations proves
s=19235​W,U3​=−19235​W,(p1​,p2​)=(4029​,4011​).​
(39)
The deposited flux is zero even though each species occupies only part of the packing: the settling law applies to the suspension, while the deposit is a separate stationary state. These jump relations determine the deposit composition from sedimentation jump conditions.
The large-particle clearing front has height H−7Wt/8 and meets the mixed-deposit front at height 35Wt/192. Thus
t1​=203192​WH​,h1​=20335​H=295​H.​
(40)
At this merger all large particles have entered the deposit. Only the small particles remain suspended, at normalized concentration 1/8. The new material deposited above the mixed layer is pure small-particle solid, at total concentration ϕmax​. Its sedimentation shock therefore has downward speed
U4​=1−1/80−(W/4)(1/8)(7/8)​=−32W​.​
(41)
For t>t1​, the deposit top and the remaining clearing front have heights
zbed​=h1​+32W​(t−t1​),zclear​=H−327​Wt.
(42)
Their meeting, or the total solid-volume balance, gives
t2​=724​WH​,h2​=4H​.​
(43)
This is two-stage bidisperse batch sedimentation: the fast species finishes first, followed by deposition of the remaining slow species.
The final solid composition by volume, and also by mass because the particle densities are identical, is
{72.5% large, 27.5% small,0% large, 100% small,​0<z<5H/29,5H/29<z<H/4.​​
(44)
The upper pure-small layer has thickness 9H/116. As checks, (29/40)h1​=H/8 accounts for all large-particle solid volume, and (11/40)h1​+(h2​−h1​)=H/8 accounts for all small-particle solid volume, in units of ϕmax​. If “percentage of particles” means number rather than volume, a large sphere occupies eight times the volume of a small sphere: the mixed bottom layer contains 29/117≃24.8% large particles by number and 88/117≃75.2% small. The upper layer remains entirely small under either convention.
