Past exam of the mathematics course of the University of Cambridge / 2015 / iii / Paper 74 / 4 / c / Solution

Apply volume conservation, mass conservation and vertical momentum flux balance to a slice of the triangular-profile line plume. Ambient ingestion contributes 2e of volume and 2ρ0​e of mass per unit height and source length. The ambient is quiescent, so it supplies no leading vertical momentum. The integrated driving force is the stored buoyancy B. Using the quantities from part (a), the non-Boussinesq triangular-profile line-plume balances are
∂t​(2b)+∂z​(bW)∂t​[b(ρ+ρ0​)]+∂z​[3bW​(ρ0​+2ρ)]∂t​[3bW​(ρ0​+2ρ)]+∂z​[6bW2​(ρ0​+3ρ)]​=2e,=2ρ0​e,=gb(ρ0​−ρ).​​
(49)
Together with Batchelor entrainment, these are three equations for W,ρ,b. They neglect viscous boundary stresses and streamwise pressure-force corrections within the integral-plume approximation.
Multiplying volume conservation by gρ0​ and subtracting g times mass conservation cancels the ambient sources exactly, giving
∂t​[gb(ρ0​−ρ)]+∂z​[32​gbW(ρ0​−ρ)]=0.​
(50)
This is buoyancy conservation in the homogeneous ambient; it is a consequence of the first two balances, not an additional independent equation.
Now take the Boussinesq approximation in inertia and entrainment, retaining the small density deficit in buoyancy. Then Q=ρ0​bW, M=(2/3)ρ0​bW2 and F=(2/3)gbW(ρ0​−ρ). Consequently
W=23​QM​,2ρ0​b=34​MQ2​,B=MFQ​.
(51)
The mass source is 2αρ0​W=3αρ0​M/Q. Substitution into the mass conservation, momentum and buoyancy balances gives the unsteady Boussinesq triangular-profile line-plume balances
34​∂t​(MQ2​)+∂z​Q∂t​Q+∂z​M∂t​(MFQ​)+∂z​F​=3αρ0​QM​,=MFQ​,=0.​​
(52)
These use density-weighted mass and momentum fluxes. Removing ρ0​ from some flux definitions while retaining it in the source would mix incompatible conventions.
