For a two-dimensional internal gravity wave with fixed horizontal wave number k=0, the vertical velocity amplitude satisfies
wzz​+m(z)2w=0,m(z)2=k2(ω2N(z)2​−1).
(4)
This follows from the Linearized Boussinesq equations just as in part (a), allowing the background buoyancy frequency to vary. The WKB approximation gives locally
w≃∣m(z)∣​A±​​exp(±i∫zm(s)ds).
(5)
It requires slowly varying stratification compared with the local vertical wavelength, in particular ∣mz​∣≪m2, and a small-amplitude wave in the inviscid, nonrotating regime. Propagation requires N>∣ω∣; where N<∣ω∣, the local vertical wave number is imaginary and the solution is evanescent. Turning levels and abrupt changes need a separate connection calculation. Finite viscosity, background flow, rotation or nonlinear effects require modifications of this model.
The ray tracing equations use the local dispersion relation as a Hamiltonian:
x˙=∂k​ω,z˙=∂m​ω,k˙=0,m˙=−∂z​ω.
(6)
The stationary, horizontally uniform background conserves the frequency and horizontal wave number. In two dimensions the group velocity gives dx/dz=−m/k. For the specified buoyancy frequency and ω=1, m2=256k2z2. Hence for z>0 the two geometric internal-wave rays through the specified point are
x=8(z2−1)orx=−8(z2−1).​
(7)
Each curve has both directed propagation senses, giving four local directed rays. Their formal intersections with z=0 are x=−8 and x=8.
At z=0, N=ω and m=0: this is a double-zero internal-wave turning level, since m2 touches zero without changing sign. The ray becomes vertical geometrically, but its group velocity tends to zero. Taking k>0 without loss of generality, the downward branch has
∣cg,z​∣=∣k∣(1+256z2)16z​,Δt=16∣k∣​logzzi​​+8∣k∣(zi2​−z2).
(8)
The formal travel time diverges logarithmically as z→0. Meanwhile ∣mz​∣/m2=1/(16∣k∣z2) grows, so the WKB approximation fails before that limit; its predicted ∣m∣−1/2 amplitude cannot be extrapolated to infinity. The spatial ray reaches a finite limiting position, but finite-wavelength behaviour near the zero must be found beyond WKB. There is no evanescent half-space supplied by this particular N(z), so ordinary simple-turning-point reflection cannot be assumed solely from the local ray construction.
