Let uC,x​:C→R be a colimit cocone for G:Elts(X)→C. Here the preservation hypothesis must include the categorical limit of Gop in Cop; this indexing category can be large. For a morphism f:(C,x)→(D,y) of the category of elements, the defining equation X(f)(y)=x says that the family of distinguished elements (x)(C,x)​ is compatible. Preservation gives a unique r∈X(R) with
X(uC,x​)(r)=xfor every (C,x).
(23)
In particular each uC,x​ is now a morphism (C,x)→(R,r) in the category of elements. Naturality of the colimit cocone gives
uR,r​uC,x​=uC,x​.
(24)
The universal property of the colimit forces uR,r​=1R​, since it and 1R​ agree after every cocone leg. If f:C→R also satisfies X(f)(r)=x, it is a morphism (C,x)→(R,r), and cocone compatibility now yields uC,x​=uR,r​f=f. Thus (R,r) is a universal element. By the preceding representability criterion, the presheaf is represented by the colimit object:
C(−,R)≅X,f⟼X(f)(r).​
(25)
The size qualification matters. If preservation means only small categorical limits, the implication as printed is false for general locally small categories. For an explicit counterexample, let C be the ordered category of all ordinals with an extra greatest object ∞. Put X(α)={∗} for every ordinal α and X(∞)=∅, with the forced restriction maps. Every small colimit in C is the supremum of its object values: it is an ordinal unless the diagram contains ∞. Applying X therefore gives the appropriate small categorical limit of singletons, or the empty set when a value is empty; an empty diagram gives X(0)={∗}. Hence X preserves all small categorical limits.
Its category of elements consists of all ordinals, whose projection has the large colimit ∞ in C. Nevertheless, no ordinal represents X, because its representable vanishes on larger ordinals; ∞ does not represent it either, since C(∞,∞) is nonempty while X(∞) is empty. The valid proof consequently uses preservation of the displayed possibly large limit, or a smallness hypothesis making that diagram small.
