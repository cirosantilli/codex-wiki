The right-adjoint criterion using comma-category colimits is as follows. Under the given colimit-preservation hypothesis, with preservation including the possibly large colimits below, F:X→A has a right adjoint exactly when, for every A, the projection
UA​:(F↓A)→X,(X,f:FX→A)⟼X
(26)
has a colimit in X. The right adjoint is obtained from these comma-category colimits. Thus the objects to construct are
GA=colim(X,f)∈(F↓A)​X.​
(27)
For necessity, if F⊣G with adjunction counit ε, (GA,εA​) is a terminal object of the comma category (F↓A). Its unique incoming morphisms give a colimit cocone for UA​, exactly as for the elements projection in the preceding part.
For sufficiency, choose such a colimit L with legs uX,f​:X→L. The morphisms f:FX→A are a cocone on FUA​. Since F preserves this colimit, there is a unique εA​:FL→A satisfying
εA​F(uX,f​)=f.
(28)
Each uX,f​ is thus a morphism (X,f)→(L,εA​) in the comma category. The original colimit cocone gives uL,εA​​uX,f​=uX,f​ for every object, hence uL,εA​​=1L​ by the universal property. For any other morphism h:(X,f)→(L,εA​), cocone compatibility gives
uX,f​=uL,εA​​h=h.
(29)
So (L,εA​) is a terminal object. Equivalently, L represents the categorical presheaf A(F−,A), via h↦εA​Fh. The functoriality of chosen representations makes these L into G:A→X, yielding a natural bijection
X(X,GA)≅A(FX,A),F⊣G.​
(30)
The preservation of these possibly large colimits is essential to the construction of εA​. Under a small-only interpretation, the preceding ordinal counterexample also disproves the unqualified converse here. Take F=Xop:C→Setop. It preserves all small colimits. For a nonempty set A, the comma category (F↓A) has one object over each ordinal and none over ∞, so its projection has colimit ∞. For A=∅, the projection is the identity of C, again with colimit ∞. Thus all these projection colimits exist, but F has no right adjoint: the categorical presheaf Setop(F−,{∗})=X is not representable. This makes the large-preservation qualification substantive.
