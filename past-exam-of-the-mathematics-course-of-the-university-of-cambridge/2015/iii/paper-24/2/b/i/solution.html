For clarity, each ρβ​(α) is an ordered finite sequence. Its minimal walk along a club sequence is finite because its nodes strictly decrease until reaching α, and an infinite strictly decreasing sequence of ordinals cannot exist. At a step from δ>α, unboundedness of Cδ​ supplies min(Cδ​∖α) in [α,δ).
Compare the two minimal walks along a club sequence to ξ and to α. As long as their current node is the same δ>α, their next nodes agree exactly when Cδ​ has no point in [ξ,α). At the first such point, the walk to ξ moves to
min(Cδ​∖ξ)∈[ξ,α),
(15)
whereas the walk to α stays at or above α. If this does not occur before the second walk terminates, the first walk reaches α with it and then makes its next step below α.
Thus there is an index j, possibly the terminal index of the walk to α, with
βiξ​=βiα​ (i≤j),ξ≤βj+1ξ​<α.​
(16)
It is unique: after this step the first walk stays below α, so it can never again coincide with a node of the walk to α. This is the first-divergence lemma for minimal walks.
