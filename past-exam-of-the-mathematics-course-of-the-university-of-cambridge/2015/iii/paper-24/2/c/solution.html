Take κ=ω1​ and choose a club sequence on ω2​, each Cδ​ of order type at most ω1​. For a successor δ use its predecessor as a singleton; at a limit use a cofinal sequence of minimal length. Form the minimal-walk tree
Tα​={ρβ​↾α:α≤β<ω2​},T=⋃α<ω2​​Tα​,
(22)
ordered by proper extension. Its height is ω2​.
For ξ<δ, the initial segment Cδ​∩ξ has order type strictly below ω1​, and is countable. The strict inequality follows because a point of Cδ​ at or above ξ occurs later in its enumeration. Hence every entry of every trace is countable. Under the Continuum hypothesis, for ∣α∣≤ℵ1​,
∣[α]≤ω∣≤ℵ1ℵ0​​=(2ℵ0​)ℵ0​=ℵ1​.
(23)
There are at most ℵ1​ finite sequences of such sets. The trace coherence lemma for minimal walks says that, for β>α, the value ρβ​(α) determines ρβ​↾α. The case β=α adds at most one node. Thus ∣Tα​∣≤ℵ1​ for every level.
Suppose that T had a cofinal branch, and take the union of its functions, f, with domain ω2​. Every ρβ​ is injective by the proper-initial-segment argument, so f is injective too. On the stationary set
S={α<ω2​:cf(α)=ω1​},
(24)
this set is stationary because the supremum of a strictly increasing ω1​-sequence from any club set has cofinality ω1​ and lies in that club. The union of the finitely many countable entries of f(α) is bounded below α. Assign a strict upper bound below α to obtain a regressive function. By Fodor lemma, there is a stationary S′⊆S and a single η<ω2​ such that every entry of f(α) lies inside η for α∈S′. There are at most ℵ1​ such finite sequences by the same cardinal arithmetic, but ∣S′∣=ℵ2​, contradicting injectivity.
Therefore T is an aleph-two Aronszajn tree:
ht(T)=ω2​,∣Tα​∣<ℵ2​,T has no cofinal branch.​
(25)
