Fix p and a name f˙​ such that p forces a two-coloring of [λ]<ω. For each ground-model finite set a⊆λ, record its full forcing decision pattern
F(a)(q)=⎩⎨⎧​0,1,2,​q≤p and q⊩f˙​(aˇ)=0,q≤p and q⊩f˙​(aˇ)=1,otherwise.​
(72)
There are at most 3∣P∣<λ such patterns, since λ is a strong limit cardinal and ∣P∣<λ. For finite P the number of patterns is finite, also below λ. Encode the patterns by an ordinal β<λ and apply the assumed finite-subset partition property to obtain H⊆λ of size λ such that F is constant on [H]n for every n<ω.
For each n, choose one an​∈[H]n. Conditions below p deciding f˙​(an​) are dense. Whenever one such condition decides the value, equality of patterns means it forces that same value at every a∈[H]n. A generic filter containing p meets this dense set for each n, so f is constant on each [H]n in the extension. The constants may differ with n, exactly as required.
The forcing has size below the regular λ, hence satisfies the λ-chain condition for forcing and preserves its cardinality. The ground-model set H consequently still has size λ. Since the argument works for every name and condition,
⊩P​λ→(λ)2<ω​.​
(73)
This is small forcing preservation of finite-subset partition properties; no closure assumption on the forcing is required.
