Start with the set-theoretic tree of initial characteristic functions
Tα​={χX​↾α:X∈F},α<ω1​,
(32)
ordered by extension. The Kurepa-family hypothesis makes each level countable, because its members are in one-to-one correspondence with the distinct traces X∩α. Every node extends to every higher level using an X that witnesses it. Nodes at limit levels are uniquely determined by their predecessors, and the distinct X give ℵ2​ distinct cofinal branches.
We can also ensure the splitting requirement in the definition of a normal set-theoretic tree. Keep only nodes through which ℵ2​ of these branches pass. There are ℵ1​ nodes in total. For a discarded node, at most ℵ1​ of the selected branches pass through it, so at most ℵ1​ branches meet any discarded node. Remove those branches; ℵ2​ branches remain, and each retained node still has ℵ2​ remaining branches through it. It therefore has two different retained extensions at some later level, and has a retained extension at every higher level.
Choose increasing countable levels ⟨δξ​:ξ<ω1​⟩, starting at 0, continuously at limits, so that all nodes at level δξ​ split before level δξ+1​. This is possible because each selected level is countable. Restrict to these levels and relabel them by ξ. The retained tree now has one root, extensions at every higher level, at least two immediate successors, and unique limits of predecessor chains. Distinct remaining branches stay distinct on this unbounded set of levels. Hence the resulting normal set-theoretic tree is a Kurepa tree, with
height ω1​,countable levels,at least ℵ2​ cofinal branches.​
