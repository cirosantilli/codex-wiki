Countability of the forcing and uncountability of Y are understood internally in M and M[G], respectively. This matters because the model itself is externally countable. Choose a name Y˙∈M and a ground-model set A∈M containing every ground-model element that can occur in Y. Such an A exists: the rank of Y˙ bounds the ranks of its values, so a sufficiently high VθM​ contains Y⊆M.
For p∈P, ground-model axiom schema of separation and the forcing definability lemma give
Xp​={a∈A:p⊩aˇ∈Y˙}∈M.

By the forcing truth lemma,
Y=⋃p∈G​Xp​.
(57)
If every Xp​ for p∈G were countable in M, it would remain countable in M[G], where G⊆P is countable. Then Y would be countable, contrary to the hypothesis. Thus some p∈G has Xp​ uncountable in M; otherwise its ground-model enumeration would still enumerate it in the extension. Let X=Xp​. Every one of its members is forced by p into Y, so
X∈M,M⊨∣X∣>ℵ0​,M[G]⊨X⊆Y.​
(58)
The countable chain condition for forcing also preserves its uncountability. Separativity is not needed for this particular argument. This is the ground-model uncountable subset lemma for countable forcing.
