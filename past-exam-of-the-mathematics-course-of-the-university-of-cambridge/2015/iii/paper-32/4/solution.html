Put Ω=Σ−1, the precision matrix. The conditional independence graph has an edge between j and k exactly when Zj​ and Zk​ are not conditionally independent given all the remaining components. For the multivariate normal distribution,
{j,k} is absent⟺Ωjk​=0.​
(23)
Indeed, the conditional density of the pair given the others has its quadratic term determined by the corresponding two-by-two principal submatrix of Ω; its cross term vanishes exactly when Ωjk​=0, making the two conditional Gaussian variables independent. This is a Gaussian graphical model.
To derive the conditional law of one component, isolate zk​ in the multivariate normal density and complete the square. The terms involving it are
−21​Ωkk​(zk​−μk​)2−(zk​−μk​)Ωk,−k​(z−k​−μ−k​).
(24)
Therefore
Zk​∣Z−k​=z−k​∼N(μk​−Ωkk​Ωk,−k​​(z−k​−μ−k​), Ωkk​1​).​
(25)
Equivalently, its mean is μk​+Σk,−k​Σ−k,−k−1​(z−k​−μ−k​) and its variance is the Schur complement Σkk​−Σk,−k​Σ−k,−k−1​Σ−k,k​. These forms agree because the k,−k block of ΩΣ=I gives −Ωk,−k​/Ωkk​=Σk,−k​Σ−k,−k−1​, and its k,k block then identifies the variance. This proves the relevant conditional multivariate normal distribution formula.
In particular, the population regression of component k on the other components has coefficients
θjk​=−Ωkk​Ωjk​​(j=k),​
(26)
intercept ak​=μk​−μ−kT​θ−k,k​, and a Gaussian residual independent of the predictors with variance 1/Ωkk​. These are the conditional regression coefficients from a Gaussian precision matrix. The coefficient is zero exactly when the corresponding edge is absent. Its counterpart θkj​ has the same zero pattern and sign, but generally a different magnitude.
This motivates nodewise regression: for each k, regress Xk​ on X−k​, estimating an intercept as well. For sparse graph estimation, use the Nodewise Lasso criterion
2n1​∥Xk​−ak​1−X−k​b∥22​+λk​∥b∥1​.
(27)
The Lasso penalty encourages many fitted coefficients to be zero and remains usable when the number of predictors exceeds the sample size. Ordinary unpenalized sample regressions would usually give a dense estimated graph. Separate regressions can disagree about a pair, so an undirected graph can use the OR rule for nodewise graph selection, retaining an edge if either fitted direction is nonzero, or the more conservative AND rule for nodewise graph selection, retaining it only if both are nonzero. These are choices for combining estimated neighbourhoods, rather than identities forced by finite-sample fits.
The printed joint objective uses a Group Lasso penalty on the unordered pairs
gjk​=(Θjk​,Θkj​),∑j<k​∥gjk​∥2​.
(28)
This is paired group Lasso for Gaussian graph estimation. Each group represents a single undirected edge, coupling its two directional regressions. A sum of group norms encourages entire pairs to be zero; a squared group norm would give smooth ridge-type shrinkage instead of edge deletion. The constraint Θkk​=0 removes self-prediction, and the intercepts are not penalized.
The free symbols called μk​ in the objective are regression intercepts, not generally the marginal means of the original Gaussian vector. Profiling them out gives
ak​=Xk​−X−kT​Θ−k,k​,
(29)
and replaces all columns by their centred versions in the squared-error terms. This is profiling intercepts in nodewise regression. Scaling predictors to comparable units can also help interpret a common penalty, although the displayed objective itself is well defined on the given data.
The grouped KKT conditions make the sparsity mechanism precise. With centred residuals rk​=Xk​−X−k​Θ−k,k​, define
cjk​=(nXjT​rk​​, nXkT​rj​​).
(30)
The subdifferential of the Euclidean norm at zero is its closed unit ball, and away from zero is g/∥g∥2​. Hence a minimizer satisfies
gjk​=0⟹∥cjk​∥2​≤λ,gjk​=0⟹cjk​=λ∥gjk​∥2​gjk​​.​
(31)
These are the paired group Lasso optimality conditions. They jointly threshold the two directional scores, matching the population fact that both directions represent the same edge.
Estimate the graph by including {j,k} exactly when ∥g​jk​∥2​>0. This graph is undirected by construction. The penalty does not impose Θjk​=Θkj​, nor does it mathematically require both coordinates of every selected pair to be nonzero. That distinction is appropriate because the population regression coefficients themselves need not have equal magnitudes. The fitted coefficient matrix is not automatically a symmetric positive definite precision matrix; its pair supports, rather than its entries as a precision estimate, supply the requested conditional independence graph.
