There is a genuine conflict in the printed coefficient assumptions: no uniformly positive coefficient in a zero-boundary Sobolev space exists. A function a∈H01​(Ω) cannot also obey a≥a−​>0 almost everywhere. Indeed, the Lipschitz truncation g(s)=min(max(s,0),a−​) has g(0)=0, so Lipschitz truncation preserves zero-boundary Sobolev spaces, giving g(a)∈H01​(Ω). But g(a) would be the nonzero constant a−​. Its zero gradient contradicts the Poincaré inequality in the zero-boundary Sobolev space. Thus the literal coefficient class is empty.
For the meaningful uniformly elliptic problem, take a∈L∞(Ω) with 0<a−​≤a≤a+​, and impose the Dirichlet boundary condition on the unknown and test functions: V=H01​((0,1)2). Additional H1 regularity of a is harmless, but a zero trace for a must be removed. The divergence-form elliptic operator is L=−div(a∇). Integration by parts defines the symmetric bounded bilinear form

Here 2π2 is the first Dirichlet Laplacian eigenvalue on the unit square; the Poincaré inequality follows, for example, by applying the one-dimensional inequality in each coordinate and adding. Thus B is coercive in the gradient norm on V, and the Dirichlet realization of L is a positive definite symmetric operator. On its operator domain, ⟨Lv,v⟩=B(v,v).

Because f∈L2, the right side is a bounded linear functional by the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality and Poincaré inequality. The Lax-Milgram theorem gives a unique weak solution, and part (b) proves that it uniquely minimizes I. These formulas prove the intended conclusion under the repaired coefficient hypothesis; under the literal hypothesis there is no coefficient to which the conclusion can be applied.
