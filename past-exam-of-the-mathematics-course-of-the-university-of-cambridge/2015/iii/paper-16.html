Construct the tensor product of sheaves by first forming the presheaf U↦F(U)⊗OX​(U)​G(U), with restriction maps induced by those of the two sheaves of modules, and then applying sheafification. The local module actions are compatible with restrictions and therefore give the sheaf an OX​-module structure. Its stalks are
(F⊗OX​​G)x​≅Fx​⊗OX,x​​Gx​.
(1)
Indeed, a finite collection of germs of sheaf sections can be represented on a common neighbourhood, and every finite tensor relation holds on a sufficiently small neighbourhood. Equivalently, this construction represents bilinear maps of sheaves of modules that are balanced over the structure sheaf. Sections of the resulting sheaf need not themselves be tensors of global sections: that is why the sheafification step matters.

The opening change-of-rings tensor quotient sends m⊗R​n to m⊗A​n. The target pairing is bilinear and R-balanced because θ(r)m⊗A​n=m⊗A​θ(r)n. On the source, let A act through the first factor; commutativity makes that action compatible with the R-balancing relations, and the map is A-linear. It is surjective: the target is the quotient imposing the additional relations am⊗R​n=m⊗R​an for every a∈A. The construction commutes with module homomorphisms in both variables.
The pullback-direct-image adjunction unit is obtained locally by pulling a section h∈H(V) to ϕ−1V and sending it to 1⊗h in ϕ∗H(ϕ−1V). These maps respect restrictions and the OY​-actions, hence define
ηH​:H⟶ϕ∗​ϕ∗H.
(5)
For H=OY​, multiplication identifies ϕ∗OY​ with OX​, and ηOY​​ is precisely the structure morphism ϕ#:OY​→ϕ∗​OX​.
To construct the direct-image tensor comparison, on V⊆Y send s⊗t, with s∈F(ϕ−1V) and t∈G(ϕ−1V), to its tensor section of F⊗OX​​G. This pairing is OY​(V)-balanced through ϕ# and is compatible with restrictions. The universal property of the tensor product of modules and sheafification therefore give
ϕ∗​F⊗OY​​ϕ∗​G⟶ϕ∗​(F⊗OX​​G).
(6)
Apply this with G=ϕ∗H after tensoring the unit ηH​ with ϕ∗​F. The composite is the map in the projection formula for sheaves. If H∣V​≅OVr​, then ϕ∗H∣ϕ−1V​≅Oϕ−1Vr​, and the composite identifies with
(ϕ∗​F∣V​)⊕r⟶ϕ∗​(F⊕r)∣V​,
(7)
the identity on the r components. Thus it is an isomorphism for a locally free sheaf of finite rank. Neither F nor G was assumed quasi-coherent.

For r=1, the assertion is the assumed vanishing for coherent ideal sheaves. For r>1, project F⊆OXr​ onto the last component. Its image I⊆OX​ is a coherent ideal sheaf, and its kernel F′ is a coherent sheaf contained in OXr−1​. Here images and kernels are coherent because a variety is Noetherian. The short exact sequence
0⟶F′⟶F⟶I⟶0
(8)
gives an exact segment H1(X,F′)→H1(X,F)→H1(X,I) in the long exact sequence in sheaf cohomology. The outer terms vanish by mathematical induction and the hypothesis, so the middle term vanishes. This is ideal-sheaf vanishing for a coherent submodule of a trivial bundle.

Let IY​ be the coherent ideal sheaf of Y, and let mP​ be the ideal sheaf of a closed point. Because P∈/Y, the stalk (IY​)P​ is OX,P​. Evaluation at P therefore gives a surjective morphism of sheaves IY​→kP​ to the skyscraper sheaf at P. Its kernel J=IY​∩mP​ is again a coherent ideal sheaf. From
0→J→IY​→kP​→0
(9)
and H1(X,J)=0, the long exact sequence in sheaf cohomology shows that Γ(X,IY​)→k is onto. Choose f mapping to 1. It vanishes on Y and satisfies f(P)=1. Hence Xf​⊆U, and inside the affine variety U it is the principal open subset defined by f∣U​. A principal open of an affine variety is affine. This gives affine principal neighbourhoods from ideal-sheaf vanishing.

The localization of global sections on a principal open gives Afi​​=Γ(Xfi​​,OX​). Each of these rings is a finitely generated algebra because Xfi​​ is an affine variety. Choose finite generators of every Afi​​ and write them as aij​/fieij​​ with aij​∈A. Let B be the k-subalgebra of A generated by all fi​,gi​,aij​. It is a finitely generated algebra, and Bfi​​=Afi​​ since these localizations contain the chosen generators and fi−1​.
For any integer N≥1, raise ∑i​gi​fi​=1 to the power r(N−1)+1. Every resulting monomial contains some fiN​, so it gives a unit-ideal identity for powers
1=∑i​ci​fiN​,ci​∈B.
(12)
For an arbitrary a∈A, equality Afi​​=Bfi​​ implies fiNi​​a∈B for some Ni​: multiply by an additional power if equality of localized fractions requires it. Choose a common N and the identity above. Then a=∑i​ci​(fiN​a)∈B. Consequently A=B, so A is a finitely generated algebra. It is a reduced ring, since a nilpotent element global regular function has zero germ everywhere on the reduced variety X. This is finite generation from finitely many principal localizations.

Let Y be the affine variety with coordinate ring A. Its principal opens D(fi​) cover Y because ∑i​gi​fi​=1. On Xfi​​, the localization of global sections on a principal open identifies its coordinate ring with Afi​​, giving an isomorphism
ϕi​:Xfi​​∼​D(fi​)⊆Y.
(13)
On an overlap the two maps are induced by the same elements of A, or equivalently by the same identification with Afi​fj​​, so they agree. Glue them to ϕ:X→Y. The inverses agree on the overlaps as well and glue to its inverse. At a closed point P, this is the map corresponding to the evaluation ring homomorphism A→k, a↦a(P). For an affine variety, every coherent ideal sheaf is quasi-coherent; vanishing of quasi-coherent cohomology on an affine scheme therefore gives H1(X,I)=0.
For the projective-space complement, assume n≥1 and choose two distinct closed points P,Q∈X. Take the ideal sheaf of two closed points I=mP​∩mQ​ on X. The codimension-two extension of regular functions on a normal variety gives
Γ(X,OX​)=Γ(Pn,OPn​)=k.
(14)
One can see this directly: on every standard affine chart of Pn, a rational function written in lowest terms cannot have a nonconstant denominator, because an irreducible polynomial factor of the denominator would define a pole along a codimension-one hypersurface, and such a hypersurface is not removed by Z. The extended function is constant because every global regular function on projective space is constant. Now the short exact sequence
0→I→OX​→kP​⊕kQ​→0
(15)
sends k diagonally into k2 on global sections. Its cokernel is k, and the long exact sequence in sheaf cohomology injects that cokernel into H1(X,I). Thus
H1(X,I)=0.​
(16)
The assumption n≥1 is necessary: P0 is already affine and has no such example.

For Ui0​…iq​​=Ui0​​∩⋯∩Uiq​​, the Čech cochain complex has groups
Cq(U,F)=∏i0​<⋯<iq​​F(Ui0​…iq​​),(δc)i0​…iq+1​​=∑j=0q+1​(−1)jci0​…ij​​…iq+1​​∣Ui0​…iq+1​​​.
(17)
Terms in δ2 cancel in pairs, and Čech cohomology is kerδ/imδ. A degree-zero cocycle is exactly a family of compatible local sections. The sheaf gluing axiom gives their unique global section, proving Hˇ0(U,F)≅F(X). If the cover has affine finite intersections, a quasi-coherent sheaf has no higher cohomology on those intersections by vanishing of quasi-coherent cohomology on an affine scheme. The acyclic cover theorem then identifies all Čech groups with sheaf cohomology. In particular, a finite affine open cover of a separated variety has this property.
For the sheaf of units of the structure sheaf, a multiplicative degree-one cocycle is a family gij​∈OX∗​(Ui​∩Uj​) satisfying gij​gjk​=gik​, with gji​=gij−1​. It glues trivial rank-one free modules into an invertible sheaf. Changing the local frames multiplies gij​ by a coboundary, and two sets of transition data give isomorphic line bundles precisely when their cocycles differ this way. Tensoring line bundles multiplies their cocycles. Thus line bundles trivialized by an open cover give the group isomorphism
Pic(X)U​≅Hˇ1(U,OX∗​).
(18)
For the remaining arguments, work over the algebraically closed ground field. On the irreducible variety V, put Q=K∗/OV∗​, a quotient of sheaves of abelian groups. A global section of Q is locally represented by rational functions fi​ whose ratios are regular units. The corresponding unit cocycle fj​/fi​ defines an invertible sheaf. A single global rational function has trivial cocycle. Conversely, every line bundle has a nonzero rational section: choose a nonzero vector in its one-dimensional fibre at the generic point and express it in local frames. This supplies such local fi​. If the associated line bundle is trivial, changing frames makes all fi​ restrictions of one rational function. Therefore
k(V)∗⟶Γ(V,K∗/OV∗​)⟶Pic(V)⟶0
(19)
is exact. This is the Cartier-divisor description of the Picard group. The sheaf of nonzero rational functions on an irreducible variety is flasque, since all restrictions between nonempty open sets are the identity on k(V)∗. Apply the long exact sequence in sheaf cohomology to 1→OV∗​→K∗→Q→1. Since H1(V,K∗)=0, its connecting map has exactly the cokernel just computed, proving
Pic(V)≅H1(V,OV∗​).​
(20)
Finally the Segre description of a smooth quadric surface identifies V with P1×P1. The two rulings of a smooth quadric surface have classes F1​,F2​ generating Pic(V)≅Z2. For completeness, remove one line in each ruling: the remaining chart is the affine plane, with factorial coordinate ring k[s,t] and trivial divisor class group. The localization sequence for the divisor class group makes F1​,F2​ generators, and their degrees on the two ruling lines prove independence. The hyperplane class, and hence the conic C, has bidegree (1,1). By Picard-group localization on a smooth variety, the Picard group of a smooth affine quadric surface is
Pic(U)≅Z2/Z(1,1)≅Z.​
(21)
Explicitly, the restriction of OV​(1,0) is nontrivial: if it were trivial on U, its rational trivialization would have divisor supported on C, forcing (1,0) to be an integer multiple of (1,1), which is impossible.

On an affine chart SpecA⊆V, the module of Kähler differentials ΩA/k​ is generated by symbols da subject to k-linearity and d(ab)=adb+bda. It represents k-derivations. These modules commute with localization, so their associated quasi-coherent sheaves glue to the Kähler differential sheaf ΩV/k1​. It is coherent: if A=k[x1​,…,xN​]/I and I=(f1​,…,fs​), then it has the finite presentation of a module
As(∂fj​/∂xi​)​AN⟶ΩA/k​⟶0.
(22)
For a closed point P, put R=OV,P​ with maximal ideal m and residue field k. The Zariski tangent space is Homk​(m/m2,k), equivalently Derk​(R,k). A derivation kills constants and m2, so it factors through a↦a−a(P); conversely every linear functional on m/m2 defines such a derivation by the product rule. The universal property of Kähler differentials therefore identifies this space with
TV,P​=Homk​(ΩV,P1​/mP​ΩV,P1​,k).​
(23)
In particular, ΩV,P1​/mP​ΩV,P1​≅mP​/mP2​, the algebraic cotangent space.
A point is a smooth point of a variety when its local ring is a regular local ring; over this algebraically closed field this says dimk​TV,P​=n=dimV. Tensor the finite presentation of a module above with k(P). The tangent space is the kernel of the Jacobian matrix J(P), hence has dimension N−rankJ(P). This proves the Jacobian criterion
P smooth⟺rankJ(P)=N−n.​
(24)
For a smooth irreducible variety V, choose at each point an invertible (N−n)-minor and shrink the affine chart so that it remains invertible. Its relations eliminate N−n of the generators of ΩA/k​, yielding a surjection An→ΩA/k​. At the generic point, its target has dimension n: over a perfect ground field, a separating transcendence basis of the function field has differentials forming a basis. Equivalently this follows from the assumed density of the smooth locus. The kernel therefore becomes zero over the fraction field of the integral domain A. As a submodule of An, it is a torsion-free module, so it is already zero. Thus these maps give local isomorphisms with OVn​, proving local freeness of differentials on a smooth variety with rank n.
For an affine chart SpecA⊆V, write W∩SpecA=Spec(A/I) and M=M. The restriction of a module sheaf to a closed subvariety is M⊗A​A/I​=M/IM​. The Conormal exact sequence for Kähler differentials is
I/I2fˉ​↦df⊗1​ΩA/k​⊗A​A/I⟶Ω(A/I)/k​⟶0.
(25)
It follows from the generators and relations: passing to A/I imposes precisely the additional relations df=0 for f∈I. The first map is well defined because d(I2) lies in IΩA/k​. Glue these exact module sequences, using exactness of localization, to obtain
IW​/IW2​⟶ΩV1​∣W​⟶ΩW1​⟶0.
(26)
In the final assertion, interpret locally principal subvariety as a proper local hypersurface. Its ideal on each chart is (f) with f=0. Since V is an irreducible variety and reduced, f is a non-zero-divisor, and A/(f)→(f)/(f2), aˉ↦af, is an isomorphism. These local rank-one descriptions make the conormal sheaf IW​/IW2​ invertible.
Because W is not contained in the singular locus of V, there is a dense open subset of W where both V and W are smooth varieties. At a closed point there, dimW=dimV−1 by the Krull principal ideal theorem. The tangent description TW,P​=ker(df:TV,P​→k) then forces df=0. Hence the conormal map is injective at the generic point of W. Its kernel is a subsheaf of a line bundle on the integral variety W, so it is a torsion-free sheaf; a torsion-free sheaf with zero generic fibre is zero. This proves conormal injectivity for a generically smooth Cartier divisor. If zero equations were allowed in the phrase locally principal, W=V would be a counterexample to invertibility; the proper-hypersurface convention is essential.
