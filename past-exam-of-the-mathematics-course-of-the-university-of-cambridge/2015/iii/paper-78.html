For a stationary, uniformly permeable skeleton, Darcy law gives u=−(Π/μ)∇p. Constant-density conservation of mass gives ∇⋅u=0, hence the pressure is harmonic:
∇2p=0.​
(1)
With uniform gravitational acceleration, the same argument applies to pressure after subtracting its hydrostatic part. The spherical calculation below neglects body-force variation and uses the excess pressure relative to the far field.
Set ϵ=1−ρs​/ρ>0 and β=ϕϵ. When a shell freezes, its pore mass decreases at rate 4πa2ϕ(ρ−ρs​)a˙; the difference must leave as liquid. Since Darcy velocity is volume discharge per total area, not pore-fluid speed, the interfacial and radial balances give
ρu(a)=ϕ(ρ−ρs​)a˙,u(r,t)=βa˙r2a2​(r≥a).​

If the matrix mass density is ρm​, the common specific heat gives C−​=cp​[(1−ϕ)ρm​+ϕρs​] and C+​=cp​[(1−ϕ)ρm​+ϕρ]. These material coefficients are not numerically specified. Write Δ=Tm​−T∞​ and S=L/(cp​Δ), the latent-to-sensible heat ratio. A conductive interfacial balance gives aa˙≲k+​Δ/L. On length scale a, the ratio of thermal diffusion time to growth time is consequently bounded by

Even if mechanical heating is retained, its size relative to latent-heat release is pi​β/L=(Tm​−Ti​)/Tm​≤Δ/Tm​. For fixed water properties this is also small as S→∞, consistently with the linearized Clausius-Clapeyron relation. Thus unsteadiness, thermal advection and mechanical heating are higher-order effects. The local leading temperature fields satisfy Laplace's equation. The approximation describes radii of order a; the distant transient diffusion tail supplies the far-field matching and need not be globally steady.

This is pressure-limited spherical freezing in a porous medium. The interface values are constant during this leading solution, although p(r,t) changes at a fixed exterior radius.
As permeability of a porous medium decreases, Λ increases: the interface pressure rises toward the finite value Δ/γ, while the interface temperature falls toward T∞​. Hydraulic resistance suppresses freezing through pressure-induced freezing-point depression. In the limit Π→0, aa˙∼ΠΔ/(γμβ); a zero-seed radius grows as Πt​ and tends to zero at fixed time. A finite seed instead hardly changes. At exactly zero permeability an incompressible rigid model cannot expel the expansion volume, so sustained growth stops; deformation or compressibility would require a different model.

Choose x=0 at the contact line, with x>0 pointing into the mountains and the plate moving in the positive x direction at speed U>0. Let z=0 be the plate. For viscous mountain spreading over an underthrust plate, the lubrication approximation assumes slow horizontal variation, hydrostatic pressure p=ρg(h−z), and μuzz​=px​=ρghx​≡G. The large slope extremely close to x=0 is an inner region where the lubrication approximation ceases to be quantitatively valid.
Now let sediment occupy 0<z<d and mountains d<z<h, with H=h−d the mountain-material thickness and M=μs​/μ. Equal densities make G=ρghx​ the same in both layers. Let z measure distance inward from the surface. While a≪R, curvature is negligible and this is a planar two-phase Stefan problem. Use a common reference mass density ρ, specific heat capacity cp​, thermal conductivity k and thermal diffusivity κ=k/(ρcp​); retain the mass density differences only in the subsequent buoyancy estimates. Define ΔT=Tm​−Ts​>0, Θ=T−Tm​≥0, and ϑ=Θ/ΔT. The paper's parameter S=L/(cp​ΔT) is the latent-to-sensible heat ratio, reciprocal to the more usual Stefan number.
For superheated two-phase Stefan similarity, solving Tt​=κTzz​ with T(0)=Ts​, T(a)=Tm​ and T(∞)=T gives the Neumann solution of the Stefan problem. With η=z/(2κt​),
a(t)=2λκt​,Ts​(z,t)=Ts​+ΔTerfλerfη​,Tl​(z,t)=T−Θerfcλerfcη​.​

Sλ=π​e−λ2​[erfλ1​−erfcλϑ​],a˙=λtκ​​.​

The right-hand bracket decreases from infinity and crosses zero, while the equivalent left-hand side Sπ​λeλ2 increases, proving uniqueness. The liquid-side flux subtracts from the roof cooling: it is heat brought to the interface by the superheated interior.
For large S, λ≪1. Keeping the superheat term while using erfλ≃2λ/π​ and erfcλ≃1 yields Sλ2+(ϑ/π​)λ≃1/2. Hence the small-interface-parameter growth with liquid superheat approximation is
