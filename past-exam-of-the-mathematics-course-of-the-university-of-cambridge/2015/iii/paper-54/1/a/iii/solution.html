The useful high-density core bound from TOV compactness follows without choosing the unknown high-density equation of state. Let a be the radius at which the decreasing energy density first reaches ρ0​, and put μ=m(a), p0​=p(ρ0​). The intended stellar-mass argument now attaches the known low-density envelope to each admissible pair (a,μ) with pressure p0​. Its Tolman–Oppenheimer–Volkoff equation involves only the known equation of state. For a physical low-density envelope with a uniformly bounded mass on this bounded set of core data, taking the supremum of the envelope-plus-core masses gives an upper mass limit independent of any high-density continuation. Stars with ρc​≤ρ0​ are also determined entirely by the known equation of state. Unknown dense-core physics cannot evade the displayed core bounds.
A qualification is necessary for a claim about the entire star: the printed monotonicity assumptions alone do not guarantee a finite maximum total mass for an arbitrary known low-density equation of state. An explicit low-density polytropic mass divergence occurs for p=Kρ5/4 with fixed K>0. In the low-density Newtonian limit, this is a stellar polytrope of index n=4, whose Lane-Emden equation has a finite first zero. With ρ=ρc​ϑ4 and r=ℓξ, hydrostatic balance fixes
ℓ2=4π5K​ρc−3/4​,M=4πρc​ℓ3μ4​∝ρc−1/8​,R∝ρc−3/8​,

where μ4​>0 is the dimensionless Lane-Emden surface mass constant. Thus M→∞ as ρc​→0, although M/R∝ρc1/4​→0. The first Lane-Emden zero is simple. The continuous dependence of an ODE solution on parameters therefore preserves this finite surface for sufficiently small relativistic parameter. The relativistic correction is controlled by pc​/ρc​=Kρc1/4​→0, so the corresponding regular finite-radius Tolman–Oppenheimer–Volkoff equation solutions have the same limiting scalings. They never sample ρ>ρ0​, and satisfy the local positivity and monotonicity assumptions in their nonvacuum interiors. This shows exactly why a suitable bounded-envelope hypothesis, or an appropriate restriction to physically stable stellar models, must accompany the intended total-mass conclusion. The compactness inequality supplies a bound on the unknown core, not a bound on the mass of every conceivable dilute envelope.
