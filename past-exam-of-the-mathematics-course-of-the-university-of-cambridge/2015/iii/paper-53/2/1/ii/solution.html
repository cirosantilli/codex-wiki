A collisionless relativistic species redshifts with Tν​∝a−1. The coupled plasma instead obeys cosmological entropy conservation, g∗s​T3a3=constant, and is heated relative to a decoupled species as massive particles annihilate. The effective number of relativistic energy degrees of freedom g∗​ and effective number of relativistic entropy degrees of freedom g∗s​ coincide while all species share a common relativistic temperature. Use gd​:=g∗s​(Tdec​)≃100 for the Standard Model bath, excluding the already decoupled extra species.
Just before ordinary neutrino decoupling, the coupled bath contains two photon states, four Electron/Positron states and six ordinary neutrino/antineutrino states. Therefore
g∗s​=2+87​(4+6)=443​.
(15)
The extra-neutrino temperature relative to that bath is then (43/(4gd​))1/3. After the ordinary neutrinos decouple, Electron/Positron annihilation heats only the photon bath: its entropy count changes from 2+(7/8)4=11/2 to 2. Both neutrino populations miss that last heating, so
T0​Tν,0​​=(4gd​43​)1/3(114​)1/3=(11gd​43​)1/3≃0.34.​
(16)
This is the two-stage entropy dilution of an early-decoupled neutrino. Numerically Tν,0​≃6.8×10−14GeV. Relative to ordinary relic neutrinos it is cooler by (43/(4gd​))1/3≃0.48.
Let gν​ count the extra neutrino's populated helicity and antiparticle states. For one conventional neutrino plus antineutrino species gν​=2, the same state count as photons. The Fermi-Dirac distribution supplies the 3/4 number-density factor, so
nγ,0​nν,0​​=43​2gν​​(T0​Tν,0​​)3=83gν​​11gd​43​≃0.0292gν​​.​
(17)
Thus a two-state species has about 2.9% of the photon number density. Stating gν​ explicitly also covers a different choice of populated states.
