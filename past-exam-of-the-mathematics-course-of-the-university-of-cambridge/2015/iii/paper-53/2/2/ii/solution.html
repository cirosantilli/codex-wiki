The preceding bound applies to a species that freezes out while relativistic, retaining an unsuppressed thermal number density apart from entropy dilution. It need not apply to a species that is already massive while reactions remain rapid.
For mν​≫Tdec​, the interaction strength is sufficient to keep the species in thermal equilibrium through its relativistic-to-nonrelativistic transition. At T∼mν​, the relativistic scaling would give Γ/H∼(mν​/Tdec​)3≫1. Subsequent annihilation removes most particle-antiparticle pairs while equilibrium remains possible. At negligible chemical potential, the nonrelativistic equilibrium number density is
neq​≃gν​(2πmν​T​)3/2e−mν​/T.
(21)
This Boltzmann suppression of a nonrelativistic relic can make the eventual abundance much smaller than the relativistically decoupled value. Freeze-out now occurs when neq​⟨σv⟩∼H, with a nonrelativistic cross-section, rather than by extrapolating the massless T5 rate indefinitely. A large mass can therefore have an acceptably small energy density: a hot-relic mass bound is not a universal bound on a heavy thermal relic.
Compatibility depends on the annihilation rate and any conserved particle-antiparticle asymmetry; the supplied relativistic rate alone does not determine the heavy relic's final abundance. Efficient annihilation is the mechanism that permits large masses within the standard expansion and thermal history.
