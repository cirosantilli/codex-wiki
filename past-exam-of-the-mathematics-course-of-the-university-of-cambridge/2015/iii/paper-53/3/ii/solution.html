The slow-roll approximation neglects ϕ¨​ relative to 3Hϕ˙​ and kinetic energy relative to the potential. It is self-consistent on the attractor when the potential slow-roll parameter ϵ≪1 and the second potential slow-roll parameter ∣η∣≪1. Thus
3Hϕ˙​≃−V′,H2≃3Mpl2​V​,ϵ=2Mpl2​​(VV′​)2,η=Mpl2​VV′′​.
(23)
Here, as in the inflaton equations, Mpl​ denotes the reduced Planck mass, not the unreduced mass used in the thermal calculation. The slow-roll curvature power spectrum becomes
ΔR2​=4π2ϕ˙​2H4​≃4π2V′29H6​=12π2Mpl6​V′2V3​,
(24)
so
ΔR2​(k)≃24π2Mpl4​ϵV​​k=aH​.​
(25)
To differentiate with respect to horizon-exit scale, write N=lna, increasing with physical time. Along the slow-roll trajectory,
dNdϕ​=Hϕ˙​​≃−Mpl2​VV′​,dNdlnk​=1−ϵH​≃1−ϵ.
(26)
The difference between differentiating with respect to lnk and N contributes only at second slow-roll order to the tilt. Since lnΔR2​=3lnV−2ln∣V′∣+constant,
ns​−1​≃−Mpl2​VV′​(3VV′​−2V′V′′​)=−3Mpl2​(VV′​)2+2Mpl2​VV′′​.​
(27)
Therefore the scalar spectral index in potential slow-roll parameters is
ns​≃1−6ϵ+2η.​
(28)
All background quantities in these expressions are evaluated when the particular mode exits the Hubble radius.
