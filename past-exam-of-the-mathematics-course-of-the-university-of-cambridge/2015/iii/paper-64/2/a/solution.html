Write Dt​=∂t​+u⋅∇ for the material derivative. For any specific quantity f, conservation of mass gives
∂t​(Σf)+∇⋅(Σfu)=ΣDt​f.
(16)
Dotting the momentum equation with Σu produces the kinetic energy balance
∂t​(21​Σu2)+∇⋅(21​Σu2u)=−u⋅∇P−Σu⋅∇Φt​.
(17)
The Coriolis acceleration does no work because u⋅(ez​×u)=0. Since the shearing-sheet tidal potential is time-independent, its advected potential-energy density obeys
∂t​(ΣΦt​)+∇⋅(ΣΦt​u)=Σu⋅∇Φt​.
(18)
For the isothermal equation of state, define the barotropic energy density U=cs2​Σln(Σ/Σref​), with fixed positive reference surface density Σref​. The continuity equation implies
∂t​U+∇⋅(Uu)=−cs2​Σ∇⋅u=−P∇⋅u.
(19)
Adding these balances combines the two pressure terms into −∇⋅(Pu). This establishes isothermal shearing-sheet energy conservation. Thus the conserved energy density and its flux are
∂t​E+∇⋅F=0,E=21​Σu2+cs2​ΣlnΣref​Σ​+ΣΦt​,F=(E+P)u.​
(20)
Choosing the density unit so that Σref​=1 reproduces the printed logarithm. Changing the reference adds a multiple of the conserved mass to E and its advective energy flux. The barotropic energy density is the mathematical energy of this fixed-isothermal sound speed closure; it is not the microscopic thermal energy of a thermally isolated gas. Maintaining an isothermal process can require heat exchange.
