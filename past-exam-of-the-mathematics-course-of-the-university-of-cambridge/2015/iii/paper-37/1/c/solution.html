For a stationary linear autoregression, causal time series means that the observation uses only current and past driving noise:
Yt​=∑j≥0​ψj​εt−j​,∑j≥0​∣ψj​∣2<∞.
(9)
This is a convergent in the sense of mean-square convergence infinite moving-average representation. The stronger usual stable-filter definition requires absolute summability; the argument below also handles the square-summable definition. Let Φ(z)=1−∑k=1p​ϕk​zk and Ψ(z)=∑j≥0​ψj​zj. Substituting the filter into the recurrence and comparing coefficients of the orthogonal noise gives ψ0​=1 and ψj​=∑k=1min(p,j)​ϕk​ψj−k​. Hence
Φ(z)Ψ(z)=1(∣z∣<1).
(10)
The Cauchy-Schwarz inequality ensures that Ψ is an analytic function in this disk. Thus Φ has no zero strictly inside it. A boundary zero is also impossible: a zero of multiplicity m≥1 at z0​=eiω0​ makes ∣1/Φ(reiω)∣2 at least a constant times [(1−r)2+(ω−ω0​)2]−m near that point. Its integral over ω diverges as r↑1. On the other hand, orthogonality of complex exponentials gives
2π1​∫−ππ​∣Ψ(reiω)∣2dω=∑j≥0​∣ψj​∣2r2j≤∑j≥0​∣ψj​∣2<∞,
(11)
a contradiction. Therefore the causality root criterion for an autoregressive model is
Φ(z)=0 ⟹ ∣z∣>1.​
(12)
Under absolute summability, the shorter boundary argument is continuity of Ψ on the closed disk and the identity ΦΨ=1 there.
