Use a mass-normalized galactic distribution function F, so that ρ=∫Fd3v and ρ⟨vi​vj​⟩=∫vi​vj​Fd3v. Multiplying the stationary Collisionless Boltzmann equation by vj​ and integrating over velocity space gives
∂i​∫vi​vj​Fd3v−Φ,i​∫vj​∂vi​​Fd3v=0.
(1)
Assume the galactic distribution function decays sufficiently rapidly for the velocity boundary term to vanish. Integration by parts then gives ∫vj​∂vi​​Fd3v=−δij​ρ, and therefore the Jeans equations are
∂i​(ρ⟨vi​vj​⟩)=−ρΦ,j​.​
(2)
The second moment includes both ordered motion and velocity dispersion; no assumption of zero mean velocity was needed.
To obtain the tensor virial theorem, multiply the jth Jeans equation by xi​ and integrate over position. For an isolated, finite system with a vanishing spatial surface term,
−∫ρ⟨vi​vj​⟩d3x=−∫ρxi​Φ,j​d3x=Wij​.
(3)
Since the left side is −2Kij​, 2Kij​+Wij​=0. This convention puts all stellar second moments into Kij​; it does not separate ordered and random kinetic energies.
For a self-gravitating system, the trace W is the Newtonian gravitational potential energy. Indeed, symmetrizing the pair integral gives
W=−2G​∬∣x−x′∣ρ(x)ρ(x′)​d3xd3x′=21​∫ρΦd3x.
(4)
Thus the trace of the tensor virial theorem is 2K+W=0, and the total energy obeys
E=K+W=−K=W/2.​
(5)
These identities require self-gravity without an additional external potential or an omitted confining boundary pressure.
Let ⟨vI2​⟩ now denote a mass-weighted average over the entire initial system. Then KI​=MI​⟨vI2​⟩/2. The gravitational radius is defined by RI​=−GMI2​/WI​, so the virial theorem immediately yields
EI​=−21​MI​⟨vI2​⟩=−2RI​GMI2​​.​
(6)
The gravitational radius measures total binding energy, rather than a particular geometric edge or half-mass radius.
For the accreted systems define the mass-weighted internal mean-square speed by MA​⟨vA2​⟩=∑s​Ms​⟨vs2​⟩. If each satellite initially satisfies the virial theorem, its internal energy contributes to EA​=−MA​⟨vA2​⟩/2. In parabolic dry-merger energy accounting, the orbital energy at large separation is zero. Assume a dry galaxy merger, no loss of mass or energy through escaping stars, no external work, and a final relaxed system satisfying the virial theorem. Then conservation of energy gives EF​=EI​+EA​ and MF​=MI​+MA​. Energy transferred by Chandrasekhar dynamical friction remains part of the total energy under these assumptions. Consequently,
EF​=−21​MI​⟨vI2​⟩(1+ϵη),η=MI​MA​​,ϵ=⟨vI2​⟩⟨vA2​⟩​.
(7)
Applying the final virial theorem and dividing by the initial relation gives
⟨vI2​⟩⟨vF2​⟩​=1+η1+ϵη​,RI​RF​​=1+ϵη(1+η)2​.​
(8)
For a mass doubling, η=1. A merger of two identical equilibrated galaxies has ϵ=1, hence RF​/RI​=2. For accretion through many minor galaxy mergers whose satellites have much smaller internal mean-square speeds, ϵ≪1, hence RF​/RI​≃4, tending to 4 in the cold-satellite limit. Equivalently, nearly fixed total energy makes the gravitational radius scale as M2 during cold minor-merger size growth. Small satellite mass alone does not logically imply ϵ≪1: the factor four also requires this weak-binding assumption. Likewise, equal masses require comparable internal binding to give the factor two. These are the physical limits behind the two stated merger comparisons.
