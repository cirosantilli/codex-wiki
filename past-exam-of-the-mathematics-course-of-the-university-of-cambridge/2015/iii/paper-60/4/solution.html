Let eij​=(∂i​uj​+∂j​ui​)/2 be the rate-of-strain tensor of an incompressible flow, and let s(t) be the supremum over the conductor of its largest eigenvalue. State Backus' necessary condition for dynamo action with its magnetic boundary conditions: an isolated bounded conductor of uniform positive magnetic diffusivity η, surrounded by an electrical insulator with a decaying potential exterior field, and no imposed magnetic field or boundary energy input. For definiteness take a no-slip boundary condition on the fluid, which eliminates the stretching surface term. If the conductor lies within a sphere of radius R and S=supt​s(t), a necessary condition for a nondecaying dynamo is
S≥R2ηπ2​,Rmstrain​=ηSR2​≥π2.​
(23)
The constant is the free-decay spectral bound for an insulating exterior, not a universal constant for every magnetic boundary condition. The condition is necessary, not sufficient, and involves maximum stretching rather than an rms velocity.
To see both the condition and the growth-rate bound, include exterior magnetic energy:
EB​=2μ0​1​∫R3​∣B∣2dV,dtdEB​​=μ0​1​∫D​Bi​eij​Bj​dV−μ0​η​∫D​∣∇×B∣2dV.
(24)
This follows from the resistive induction equation and integration by parts, with the stated boundary assumptions. The magnetic free-decay spectral bound is ∫D​∣∇×B∣2dV≥(π2/R2)∫R3​∣B∣2dV. For a sphere its lowest mode is the dipolar poloidal free-decay mode; enclosing a smaller conductor gives the same valid lower bound. Since Bi​eij​Bj​≤s(t)∣B∣2, we obtain
dtdEB​​≤2[s(t)−R2ηπ2​]EB​.
(25)
Integrating this differential inequality gives decay whenever S<ηπ2/R2. More generally the exponential rate of the field norm, rather than of its squared energy, satisfies
gB​=t→∞limsup​2t1​logEB​(0)EB​(t)​≤t→∞limsup​t1​∫0t​s(τ)dτ−R2ηπ2​≤S.​
(26)
Simply discarding the nonnegative resistive dissipation already proves the requested maximum-strain bound. The energy exponent is twice the field-amplitude exponent.
For the alpha-Omega dynamo model, write a=∣α0​∣, w=∣Ω∣, k≥0 and d=ηk2. Direct differentiation gives
dtd​∣A∣2dtd​∣B∣2​=2Re(α0​fBA)−2d∣A∣2≤2a∣A∣∣B∣−2d∣A∣2,=2Re(ikΩAB)−2d∣B∣2≤2kw∣A∣∣B∣−2d∣B∣2.​
(27)
For a,kw>0, use the weighted energy estimate for two coupled modes and form the positive weighted norm N=kw∣A∣2+a∣B∣2. The inequality N≥2akw​∣A∣∣B∣ gives
N˙≤4akw∣A∣∣B∣−2dN≤2(akw​−ηk2)N.
(28)
For each fixed k and model parameters, this norm is equivalent to the amplitude norm; its square-root exponential rate is therefore bounded by h(k)=awk​−ηk2, uniformly over all admissible f(t). Maximizing over k gives
k∗​=(16η2aw​)1/3,gmax​≤28/33​(ηa2w2​)1/3.​
(29)
The exponent is also achievable in order of magnitude. Choose the admissible constant f=1. The growing eigenvalue of the two-component system has real part −ηk2+awk/2​. Its maximum occurs at k=(aw/(32η2))1/3 and equals 32−10/3(a2w2/η)1/3. Thus the bounded-modulation alpha-Omega growth estimate has the scaling
gmax​=Θ(∣α0​∣2/3∣Ω∣2/3η−1/3)for fixed nonzero α0​,η as ∣Ω∣→∞.​
(30)
This means the maximum over allowed modulations and wavenumbers, not that every bounded modulation grows; f=0 supplies no regenerating alpha coupling.
The Omega effect rapidly makes toroidal field from poloidal field, but exponential dynamo action also requires the slower alpha effect to regenerate the poloidal component. The coupled amplification rate is of order aΩk​ rather than Ω; shortening the wavelength to increase it also increases magnetic diffusion as ηk2. Their optimal balance gives k∼Ω1/3 and growth ∼Ω2/3. The Backus' necessary condition for dynamo action estimate controls stretching alone and does not incorporate this regeneration bottleneck. A shear without regeneration can give transient amplification but not this sustained exponential feedback.
