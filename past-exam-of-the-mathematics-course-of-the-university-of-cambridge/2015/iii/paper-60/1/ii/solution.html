A toroidal magnetic field introduces an inward hoop force. In cylindrical coordinates, the radial magnetic tension is −Bϕ2​/(μ0​s), so cylindrical magnetostatic pressure balance in SI units becomes
P′(s)=−μ0​sBϕ​(s)2​,P=p+2μ0​Bz2​+Bϕ2​​.
(3)
At the field-free boundary P(s0​)=pe​. Hence
p(s)+2μ0​Bz​(s)2+Bϕ​(s)2​=pe​+μ0​1​∫ss0​​rBϕ​(r)2​dr.

Regularity on the axis gives Bϕ​=O(s), which makes the integral finite and gives Bϕ​(0)=0. Evaluating at the axis proves the central-field identity:
Bz​(0)2=Bp2​−2μ0​p(0)+2∫0s0​​sBϕ​(s)2​ds.​

The positive hoop-tension contribution allows the central axial field to exceed Bp​ without negative pressure. For an explicit regular example, put x=s2/s02​, choose a constant a>0 and 0<ε<1, and take inside the flux tube
pBz2​​=pe​[1−(1−ε)(1−x)2],Bϕ​=a​(s/s0​)Bz​,=(1−ε)Bp2​(1+ax)2(1−x)2[1+a(1+2x)/3]​.​

Substitution verifies the radial balance, with continuous zero field at s=s0​ and positive gas pressure. For a=3, ε=1/4, the central value is Bz​(0)2=3Bp2​/2.
To obtain the cross-sectional average, multiply the radial balance by s2 and integrate by parts:
s02​pe​−2∫0s0​​sP(s)ds=−μ0​1​∫0s0​​sBϕ​(s)2ds.
(7)
The toroidal contributions cancel. The resulting flux-tube axial field virial identity is
s02​2​∫0s0​​sBz​(s)2ds=Bp2​−s02​4μ0​​∫0s0​​sp(s)ds.​
(8)
This is at most Bp2​, and is strictly smaller whenever the weighted pressure integral is positive. In particular, continuous matching with pe​>0 guarantees strictness. If only p≥0 is assumed without a nondegenerate gas or continuous positive boundary pressure, the justified conclusion is the non-strict bound; the identity specifies exactly when equality occurs.
