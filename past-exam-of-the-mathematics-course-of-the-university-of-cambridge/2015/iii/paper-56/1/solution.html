Rescale the ambient coordinates by qi​=ai​ri​. For each i∈{1,…,n+1} and ε∈{1,−1}, take the relatively open set Uiε​ where εqi​>0. Define a manifold chart by retaining all the qj​ except qi​:
ϕiε​(r)=(q1​,…,qi​​,…,qn+1​)∈Bn.
(1)
Here Bn is the open unit open unit ball. The inverse of this manifold chart is explicit:
rj​=aj​xj​​(j=i),ri​=ai​ε​1−∑j=i​xj2​​.
(2)
The coordinate labels in x retain their original indices. All the ai​ are nonzero, so this inverse exists; the strictly positive radicand makes it smooth on the whole open open unit ball. Projection and this inverse are continuous, so each manifold chart is a homeomorphism onto an open subset of Rn.
These 2(n+1) manifold charts cover the ellipsoid, because at every point at least one qi​ is nonzero. On an overlap with a different chart Ujη​, the coordinates of ϕjη​∘(ϕiε​)−1 are the retained xk​ for k=i,j together with
qi​=ε1−∑k=i​xk2​​.
(3)
Its domain is the open subset ηxj​>0 of Bn. The displayed functions, and the reverse transition functions obtained by exchanging i,j, are smooth. Transitions between identical charts are the identity; opposite-sign charts with the same omitted coordinate do not overlap. The ambient subspace topology is Hausdorff and second countable, as it is inherited from Euclidean space. Thus this coordinate-projection atlas of an ellipsoid is a smooth atlas, proving the ellipsoid is a smooth manifold of dimension n. The rescaling r↦q also gives a diffeomorphism with the unit sphere Sn.
For the three-sphere, use its identification with SU(2) rather than separate local constructions. With the Pauli matrices τi​, put Ti​=−iτi​. These form a real basis of the SU(2) Lie algebra and satisfy
Ti​Tj​=−δij​I+ϵijk​Tk​.
(4)
For q02​+q12​+q22​+q32​=1, the identification SU(2) as the three-sphere is
U(q)=q0​I+qi​Ti​=(q0​−iq3​q2​−iq1​​−q2​−iq1​q0​+iq3​​).
(5)
The matrix is unitary with determinant one. Conversely, every SU(2) matrix has this form, and both the correspondence and its inverse are smooth.
The left translations Lg​(h)=gh are diffeomorphisms. Define three left-invariant vector fields by
Xi​(g)=(dLg​)e​Ti​=gTi​,i=1,2,3.​
(6)
Matrix multiplication makes these vector fields smooth globally. Since (dLg​)e​ is an isomorphism from the SU(2) Lie algebra to Tg​SU(2), the three vectors are linearly independent at every point, and each is nowhere zero. This is the parallelization of a Lie group by left translations.
Transporting the left-invariant vector fields to the three-sphere gives the quaternionic left-invariant frame on the three-sphere:
X1​(q)X2​(q)X3​(q)​=(−q1​,q0​,q3​,−q2​),=(−q2​,−q3​,q0​,q1​),=(−q3​,q2​,−q1​,q0​).​
(7)
Directly, q⋅Xi​=0 and Xi​⋅Xj​=δij​ on the unit three-sphere. Thus the vectors are tangent and form a global orthonormal frame of a vector bundle. The three-sphere is a parallelizable manifold, with tangent bundle TS3≅S3×R3.
