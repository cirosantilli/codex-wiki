Past exam of the mathematics course of the University of Cambridge / 2015 / iii / Paper 3 / 2 / c / Solution

 ... Past exam of the mathematics course of the University of Cambridge 2015 iii Paper 3 2 c
OurBigBook.com Words: 375
First split off the kernel of each source-to-sink arrow. Each kernel contributes copies of the simple representation supported at that source. The remaining arrows are injective, so identify the three source spaces with subspaces U1​,U2​,U3​ of the sink space V. We now give an elementary three-subspace decomposition.
Split off the common intersection U1​∩U2​∩U3​: any projection onto it preserves all three subspaces. Next split off each pairwise intersection. For example, once the triple intersection has gone, W=U1​∩U2​ is disjoint from U3​; a projection onto W that kills U3​ preserves U1​,U2​,U3​. This extracts blocks whose membership is exactly {1,2}. Repeating for the other pairs leaves pairwise disjoint subspaces.
For each i, choose a complement Wi​ to Ui​∩(Uj​+Uℓ​) in Ui​. A projection onto Wi​ killing Uj​+Uℓ​ preserves the triple, so these complements split off as blocks of membership exactly {i}. In the remaining triple, every Ui​ lies in the sum of the other two and all pairwise intersections are zero. Thus the sum is U1​⊕U2​, and both projections of U3​ onto U1​,U2​ are isomorphisms. Hence U3​ is the graph of an isomorphism T:U1​→U2​. Choose a basis ua​ of U1​ and the basis Tua​ of U2​; this graph decomposes into two-dimensional blocks with three distinct lines. A complement to U1​+U2​+U3​ in V contributes sink-only simple blocks.
The complete list of indecomposable blocks is therefore:
  • Three source simples, with zero sink space.
  • Eight blocks with one-dimensional sink, one for each subset S⊆{1,2,3}: the source space is k for i∈S and zero otherwise, and every nonzero arrow is the identity.
  • One exceptional block, with all three source spaces k, sink k2, and arrows 1↦e1​, 1↦e2​, 1↦e1​+e2​.
Each of the first eleven blocks has endomorphism ring k. For the exceptional indecomposable of the three-subspace quiver, an endomorphism of k2 preserving the first two lines is diagonal; preserving the third makes its two diagonal entries equal. Its endomorphism ring is also k, so all twelve are indecomposable. Their dimension vectors of quiver representations distinguish them. The decomposition argument proves completeness, and hence there are exactly 12 isomorphism classes. No general classification theorem is needed.
