Split the sequence as vector spaces at each vertex. In these coordinates, the middle arrow maps have block form
fρY​=(fρX​0​ξρ​fρZ​​).
(30)
For t∈k×, change basis by diag(tIXi​​,IZi​​) at every vertex. It changes the off-diagonal block to tξρ​. These arrow matrices depend polynomially on t and at t=0 give X⊕Z. Thus the split extension as a degeneration shows
OX⊕Z​⊆OY​​.
(31)
We must check that the two orbits are different. If Y≅X⊕Z, apply HomQ​(Z,−) to the original short exact sequence. Exactness makes the kernel of Hom(Z,Y)→End(Z) equal to Hom(Z,X). The assumed isomorphism of the middle representation gives dimHom(Z,Y)=dimHom(Z,X)+dimEnd(Z), so this map is surjective. In particular the identity of Z lifts to Z→Y, splitting the sequence, a contradiction. Hence the orbits are indeed distinct.
The group GL(n) is irreducible, so OY​​ is irreducible. An algebraic-group orbit is locally closed, hence open in its closure; its boundary is a proper closed subset of smaller dimension. The distinct orbit OX⊕Z​ lies in that boundary. Therefore
dimOX⊕Z​<dimOY​.​
(32)
