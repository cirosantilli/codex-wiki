A′=−A⟨f(Acosψ)sin2ψ⟩,θ′=0.

Use the same method of multiple scales and its solvability condition in the method of multiple scales. The position-dependent damping function is odd, so the lemma that odd position-dependent damping has zero first-order amplitude drift applies. In its wave amplitude average, ψ↦ψ+π changes sin(Acosψ) to its negative while preserving sin2ψ, so the average is zero. Hence A′=0; the wave phase average again gives θ′=0.
Equivalently, expand the position-dependent damping in its uniformly convergent power series for bounded A. Each term cos2m+1ψsinψ has only even sine Fourier harmonics, so none resonates with the unit-frequency oscillator. With the initial wave amplitude and wave phase unchanged, the leading uniform approximation is
x(t;ϵ)=2​cos(t−π/4)+O(ϵ)=cost+sint+O(ϵ),0≤t≤T∗​/ϵ.​
(13)
There is no first-order slow drift. Effects at the next order accumulate only an O(ϵ) correction on this time scale.

Let q0​ be the stationary point and let H=D2β(q0​) be its real symmetric Hessian matrix. First suppose the stationary point is interior and nondegenerate, the oscillatory integral amplitude and oscillatory integral phase are sufficiently smooth, and boundary contributions are absent or smaller than the stationary contribution. Diagonalize H by an orthogonal change of coordinates, with eigenvalues h1​,h2​. Locally,
β(q0​+ξ)=β(q0​)+21​ξTHξ+O(∣ξ∣3).
(14)
Scaling each principal coordinate by λ−1/2 and evaluating its Fresnel integral gives the two-dimensional stationary-phase formula:
h(λ)∼λ2π​∣detH∣​α(q0​)​exp(iλβ(q0​)+4iπ​sgnH).​
(15)
Here sgnH is the number of positive eigenvalues minus the number of negative ones: 2, 0 or −2. The formula gives the leading nonzero term when α(q0​)=0. A partition of unity isolates this neighbourhood; integration by parts away from stationary points makes the remaining interior contribution smaller. For a smooth compactly supported oscillatory integral amplitude the local error is O(λ−2).
The printed assumption of a single stationary point alone is insufficient for an unconditional answer. A degenerate oscillatory integral phase β=x4+y2 with a smooth oscillatory integral amplitude supported near the origin has only one stationary point, but scales as λ−3/4. Boundary terms can also contribute at order λ−1: on the unit disk with oscillatory integral amplitude one and β=x2+y2,
h(λ)=iλπ(eiλ−1)​,
(16)
whose stationary contribution is iπ/λ and whose boundary contribution is −iπeiλ/λ. Thus the boxed formula is the standard nondegenerate interior stationary contribution, with the stated hypotheses; the data as printed do not determine all possible leading behaviours.

Set z=x2/ε. The differential operator simplifies because its first-derivative terms cancel:
ε2(yxx​−xyx​​)−4x2y=4x2(yzz​−y).
(17)
Thus the homogeneous solutions are e±z and the transformed forcing is 1/(4εz). For the decaying-sector variation of parameters solution choose the contour directions
θ1​=0,θ2​=π/2.​
(18)
The first integrand decays at positive-real infinity and the second at positive-imaginary infinity. Join each endpoint to x within the first quadrant, avoiding zero. This also fixes the exponentially small homogeneous contributions, which cannot be determined from an algebraic asymptotic expansion alone.
After s=t2/ε, write A(z)=∫+∞z​e−sds/s and B(z)=∫−∞+i0z​esds/s. Their derivatives are A′=e−z/z and B′=ez/z. Direct differentiation of y=(ezA−e−zB)/(8ε) gives yzz​−y=1/(4εz), verifying the original equation. In terms of the exponential integral, with the logarithm continued through the upper half-plane,
y=−8ε1​[ezE1​(z)+e−z(Ei(z)−iπ)].​
(19)
The −iπ is essential: the contour for B starts at argument π, not at real positive infinity. On the positive real axis this choice gives the imaginary part πe−z/(8ε).
Repeated endpoint integration by parts gives ezE1​(z)∼z−1(1−z−1+2!z−2−⋯) and the matching expansion of e−z(Ei(z)−iπ) with positive factorial coefficients in the upper-half-plane sector. Their odd inverse-power corrections cancel. Therefore the leading large-x term in the requested quadrant is
y(x)∼−4x21​.​
(20)
Use the convention that anti-Stokes lines have equal exponential magnitudes, Re(x2/ε)=0. They are argx=π/4+mπ/2. The Stokes lines are argx=mπ/2. Continuing this same solution below the positive-real ray switches on iπe−z/(4ε); continuing above the positive-imaginary ray switches on iπez/(4ε). These exponentials remain decaying up to the neighbouring anti-Stokes lines. Hence the maximal adjacent decay sector on this continuation is
−π/4<argx<3π/4.​
(21)
On its two boundary rays the switched exponential has constant modulus and oscillates, so y does not tend to zero. Within the sector the leading algebraic term above persists. The internal anti-Stokes line at π/4 presents no growing exponential for the chosen solution.
For the formal small-ε series, substitution and comparison of successive powers gives a0​=1 and ar​=2r(2r−1)ar−1​. Thus all coefficients are
ar​=(2r)!,y∼−4ε1​r≥0∑​z2r+1(2r)!​.​
(22)
This is a factorially divergent asymptotic expansion. The ratio of consecutive term magnitudes is (2r+2)(2r+1)/∣z∣2. For optimal truncation, retain r=0,…,n−1, choosing 2n=∣z∣+O(1), so the cutoff is at a least term.
To resolve the remainder near the positive-real Stokes line, use its exact Borel integral with a simple pole:
y=−4ε1​∫C−​​1−t2e−zt​dt,Rn​=−4εe−z​∫C−​​1−t2t2nez(1−t)​dt.
(23)
Here C−​ runs from zero to positive-real infinity below the pole at one. Partial fractions reproduce the exponential-integral solution, including its positive imaginary part. The finite geometric identity for (1−t2)−1 gives the displayed exact remainder after integrating the first n monomials.
Write z=2n+iμn​+ν, with real bounded μ and bounded ν. For a Gaussian saddle coalescing with a simple pole, put t=1+u/n​. The exponent becomes −u2−iμu+O(n−1/2) and dt/(1−t2)=−du/(2u)+O(n−1/2)du. Therefore
Jn​∼−21​∫C−​​ue−u2−iμu​du.
(24)
The integral is its Cauchy principal value plus iπ. If F(μ)=PV∫R​e−u2−iμudu/u, differentiation gives F′(μ)=−iπ​e−μ2/4 and F(0)=0. Consequently F(μ)=−iπerf(μ/2), and Jn​=−iπerfc(μ/2)/2+O(n−1/2).
For x=ρeiθ with fixed ρ>0 and θ=O(ε​), μ/2=2​ρθ/ε​+O(ε). The error-function smoothing of a Stokes multiplier therefore gives the optimally truncated remainder
Rn​=8εiπ​e−x2/εerfc(ε​2​ρθ​)+O(ε∣z∣​∣e−x2/ε∣​).​
(25)
The error estimate is uniform for bounded θ/ε​. On the Stokes line the multiplier is half its switched-on value; above it the exponentially small term switches off, and below it switches on. This term is invisible at every algebraic order. Near the line the remainder can exceed the least retained term by a square-root factor because a pole meets the saddle point.
The printed auxiliary Gaussian hint has inconsistent constants. Direct saddle point expansion of its nonsingular integral gives 21​π/n​e−μ2/4 under its printed scaling, rather than 21​π/(2n)​e−μ2/8. The derivation above uses the correct saddle point normalization and independently fixes both the smoothing width and the contour sign.

Take α>0; a real disturbance with negative wavenumber is recovered by complex conjugation. To obtain the pressure equation for a shear-flow normal mode, apply iα to the streamwise momentum equation and differentiate the normal momentum equation. The mass conservation equation cancels the viscous divergence and the terms proportional to U−c, leaving 2iαU′v=α2p−p′′. Thus the fluid pressure equation and wall value are
p′′−α2p=−2iαU′v,p(0)=iαRu′′(0)​=α2Rv(3)(0)​.​
(26)
The wall value follows from streamwise momentum with no-slip boundary condition. Normal momentum also gives p′(0)=v′′(0)/R.
In the lower viscous boundary layer, mass conservation gives v=O(αδ) if u=O(1). Since U∼y, convection and shear have size αδ when c=O(δ); matching these with transverse viscous diffusion gives R−1δ−2∼αδ. The pressure gradient balance then gives p=O(δ). Thus the lower scalings are
δ=(αR)−1/3,c=δC,v=αδV,p=δP.​
(27)
The neglected streamwise viscous term is smaller by (αδ)2. In these variables the leading equations are
VY​=−iu,i(Y−C)u+V=−iP+uYY​,PY​=0.

Differentiating the second and using the first yields the Airy reduction uYYY​=i(Y−C)uY​​.
Put q=eiπ/6, so q3=i. The bounded matching branch of the Airy function gives uY​=AAi(q(Y−C)); the other independent Airy solution grows along the ray and is excluded. Integrating with the two wall conditions gives
u(Y)=A∫0Y​Ai(q(s−C))ds,V(Y)=−iA∫0Y​(Y−s)Ai(q(s−C))ds,

while the wall momentum equation gives the constant fluid pressure
P0​=−iAqAi′(−qC).

Define the convergent Airy displacement integrals
I0​(C)=∫0∞​Ai(q(s−C))ds,I1​(C)=∫0∞​sAi(q(s−C))ds.

u→B=AI0​,P→P0​=A(CI0​−I1​),V=−iBY+iAI1​+o(1).​

Both differentiated lower momentum and the constant of integration are satisfied; retaining that constant is essential for the final matching.
For the middle region introduce a perturbation stream function Φ with u=Φ′ and v=−iαΦ. The leading inviscid flow streamwise equation is
(U−c)Φ′−U′Φ=−p,(U−cΦ​)′=−(U−c)2p​.
(33)
For a shear flow, this is the integrated form of the Rayleigh equation for inviscid shear flow. The leading matched solution for fixed y=O(1) is Φ∼BU, so u∼BU′ and v∼−iαBU. In the overlap with the lower viscous boundary layer, the first constant correction is
Φ∼B(y−c)+δP0​=By−δAI1​,

which agrees with the integrated lower solution. For a regular smooth profile, the normal momentum equation gives p′∼−α2(U−c)Φ. Thus fluid pressure variation across a fixed-width middle region is O(α2B); in the final distinguished scaling this is smaller than its O(δ) value, so p∼δP0​ throughout that region. Since U→1, the middle velocity tends to v∼−iαB​, which does not decay and requires another region.
The fluid pressure equation becomes p′′−α2p=0 when U∼1. Therefore the upper scale is Δ=1/α, with z=αy. The inviscid decaying upper solution is
p=P∞​e−z,v=−1−ciP∞​​e−z,u=−1−cP∞​​e−z.​
(35)
Matching velocity as z↓0 gives P∞​∼αB; matching fluid pressure to the middle region gives δP0​∼αB. This is three-layer long-wave shear-flow matching, expressed as a matched asymptotic expansion.
Under α=kR−1/4 with fixed positive k, δ=k−1/3R−1/4, and α/δ=k4/3. Eliminating the nonzero amplitude A gives the Airy wall-layer matching determinant and dispersion relation
k4/3I0​(C)+iqAi′(−qC)=0.​
(36)
Equivalently, with T(C)=∫−qC∞eiπ/6​Ai(ζ)dζ=qI0​(C),
k4/3T(C)=e−iπ/6Ai′(−qC).​
(37)
The determinant form does not divide by a possibly vanishing I0​. The wavespeed is c=δC; temporal growth has sign ImC for positive α.
There is a genuine conflict in the printed assumptions: fixed-k α=kR−1/4 cannot satisfy R−1/7≪α as R→∞. For order-one lower matching amplitudes, constant leading middle fluid pressure requires α2≪δ, equivalently α≪R−1/7; this is the reverse inequality and is satisfied by the final distinguished scaling. Thinness also requires αR≫1. The dispersion relation above is therefore derived under the final distinguished limit, for which the complete consistent window is R−1≪α≪R−1/7. Both printed limits cannot be imposed simultaneously.
