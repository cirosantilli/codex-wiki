Choose a projective presentation 0→Kj​Pπ​C→0, with P a projective module. Continuing it to a projective resolution shows
ExtR1​(C,A)≅{ℓj:ℓ∈HomR​(P,A)}HomR​(K,A)​.
(1)
Indeed a degree-one cocycle in the Hom functor applied to the projective resolution descends to K, while the degree-one coboundaries are exactly restrictions of maps from P. This establishes the description of the Ext functor without first assuming the extension classification.
For a module extension with inclusion i:A→B and quotient map r:B→C, projectivity lifts π to s:P→B. Since rsj=0, there is a unique map h:K→A with ih=sj. A different lift changes h by ℓj for some ℓ:P→A. An equivalence of module extensions, which is the identity on both end modules, also preserves this class. We have therefore defined a map from extension classes to ExtR1​(C,A).
Conversely, for h:K→A, form the pushout of a module extension
Bh​=(A⊕P)/{(h(k),−j(k)):k∈K}.
(2)
The map A→Bh​ sends a to [a,0], and Bh​→C sends [a,p] to π(p). The first is injective because j is injective. If π(p)=0, write p=j(k); then [a,p]=[a+h(k),0], proving exactness in the middle. The last map is surjective. Thus Bh​ is a module extension. If h′=h+ℓj, the map [a,p]↦[a−ℓ(p),p] is an equivalence of module extensions from Bh​ to Bh′​. Finally [a,p]↦i(a)+s(p) identifies the pushout of a module extension built from an existing extension with its middle module. These two constructions are inverse, proving the classification:
E(C,A) ≅ ExtR1​(C,A).​
(3)
The zero class corresponds to a split short exact sequence. Fixed end modules matter: equivalence does not permit arbitrary automorphisms of A or C.
In the second calculation the acting group is the infinite cyclic group. Its modules are modules over the group ring S=Z[z,z−1], not merely over the underlying ring Z. The trivial representation has z acting as the identity. Its projective resolution is
0⟶Sz−1​S⟶Z⟶0.
(4)
Multiplication by z−1 is injective, and the augmentation quotient is Z. Applying the Hom functor into the trivial module makes the differential zero, so ExtS1​(Z,Z)≅Z. Taking two copies gives E(Z2,Z)≅Z2​.
An explicit representative of this extension of trivial modules for an infinite cyclic group is the abelian group Bm,n​=Zu⊕Zv⊕Zw, with injection 1↦u, quotient v↦(1,0), w↦(0,1), and action
zu=u,zv=v+mu,zw=w+nu,(m,n)∈Z2.​
(5)
This is an invertible action: the inverse subtracts the same multiples of u. Every underlying abelian-group extension splits because Z2 is free, so any group-module extension has this form after choosing lifts of the quotient basis. Replacing those lifts by multiples of u does not alter m,n. An equivalence fixing the ends has exactly such changes of lifts, so two representatives are equivalent precisely when their ordered pairs agree. Only (0,0) splits as a group-module extension; overlooking the group action would incorrectly give a single class.

For central t1​,…,tn​, define the Koszul complex on central ring elements using formal exterior symbols:
Kk​=⨁1≤i1​<⋯<ik​≤n​Rei1​​∧⋯∧eik​​,K0​=R,
(6)
with zero terms outside 0≤k≤n and differential
d(rei1​​∧⋯∧eik​​)=∑a=1k​(−1)a−1rtia​​ei1​​∧⋯∧eia​​​∧⋯∧eik​​.
(7)
The terms are free bimodules with the formal symbols commuting with coefficients. Centrality makes d a bimodule map, and the terms in d2 cancel in pairs because the ti​ commute. This defines the Koszul complex over a possibly noncommutative ring without using an undefined exterior algebra of arbitrary one-sided modules. In particular K(t)⊗R​M is a well-defined chain complex of left modules.
A regular sequence on a module means that multiplication by ti​ is injective on M/(t1​,…,ti−1​)M for each i, usually with the additional convention that the final quotient is nonzero. The homology vanishing below uses only the injectivity conditions. For one element, the chain complex is Mt1​​M, with zero first homology and zeroth homology M/t1​M.
For the induction let C=K(t1​,…,tn−1​)⊗R​M. Adjoining the last generator identifies the new Koszul complex with the mapping cone of multiplication by tn​ on C. In the convention Cone(tn​)k​=Ck​⊕Ck−1​ its differential is (a,b)↦(da+tn​b,−db); the identification is a+en​∧b. The long exact sequence in homology of this mapping cone gives
⋯⟶Hk​(C)tn​​Hk​(C)⟶Hk​(Cone(tn​))⟶Hk−1​(C)tn​​Hk−1​(C)⟶⋯.
(8)
The induction hypothesis is Hk​(C)=0 for k>0 and H0​(C)=N=M/(t1​,…,tn−1​)M. Regularity makes tn​:N→N injective. Hence the new positive homology is zero and its zeroth homology is N/tn​N. The augmentation to this quotient induces these homology isomorphisms, giving the quasi-isomorphism
K(t)⊗R​M≃M/(t1​,…,tn​)M,​
(9)
where the right side is placed in degree zero. The results used are the long exact sequence in homology of a degreewise short exact sequence of complexes and its mapping cone form, together with the stated induction; no unmentioned acyclicity criterion is needed.
For R=Z[X], (p,X) is a regular sequence: p is a non-zero-divisor in R, and multiplication by X is injective in (Z/pZ)[X], even when p is composite. The Koszul resolution of R/(p,X) is
0⟶R(p−X​)​R2(p  X)​R⟶Z/pZ⟶0.
(10)
With N=Z/qZ and X acting as zero, applying the Hom functor gives the cochain complex
Nn↦(pn,0)​N2(u,v)↦pv​N.
(11)
Thus, more generally, the Ext groups between polynomial-ring residue modules are
ExtR0​=ker(p:N→N),ExtR1​=(N/pN)⊕ker(p:N→N),ExtR2​=N/pN,
(12)
and vanish in all degrees above two. For the equal-modulus case,
ExtZ[X]k​(Z/pZ,Z/pZ)≅⎩⎨⎧​Z/pZ(Z/pZ)20​k=0,2,k=1,k>2.​​
(13)
These are R-modules through X=0 and reduction modulo p. If the multiplicative self-Ext functor is desired, the Koszul self-Ext algebra is the exterior algebra on two degree-one generators over Z/pZ. The two contractions on the Koszul resolution lift these classes, square to zero and anticommute, so this description also holds for composite p and characteristic two. For coprime p,q, multiplication by p is invertible on N, so both its kernel and cokernel vanish:
ExtZ[X]k​(Z/pZ,Z/qZ)=0for every k≥0.​
(14)

For a cohomological spectral sequence, dr​:Erp,q​→Erp+r,q−r+1​ and the next page is its cohomology. In the bounded setting, convergence of a spectral sequence to H∗ means that each Hn has a finite decreasing, exhaustive and separated filtration with
E∞p,q​≅FpHp+q/Fp+1Hp+q.​
(15)
Here E∞p,q​ is the eventual stable value. This determines the associated graded module of Hn, rather than automatically a canonical direct-sum decomposition of Hn. For unbounded filtrations additional completeness/convergence conditions are necessary; boundedness removes those issues here.
The bounded filtered-complex convergence theorem states the following. If FpC is a decreasing filtration by cochain subcomplexes, preserved by the differential, finite in each cochain degree (uniform bounds FaC=C, FbC=0 are sufficient), then there is a spectral sequence
E0p,q​=FpCp+q/Fp+1Cp+q,E1p,q​=Hp+q(FpC/Fp+1C),Erp,q​⇒Hp+q(C).
(16)
The abutment filtration is FpHn(C)=im[Hn(FpC)→Hn(C)]. Its finite length ensures stabilization and the displayed limiting-page identification. The filtered cochain complex need not itself be bounded in cochain degree. In the degreewise finite version, the bounds in degrees n−1,n,n+1 suffice to stabilize the terms of total degree n.
For a double cochain complex Dp,q bounded in both indices, form the total cochain complex TotnD=⨁p+q=n​Dp,q with anticommuting differentials dh​,dv​ and total differential dh​+dv​. If the original differentials commute, inserting the usual sign in one of them gives this convention. Filtering by the first index gives E1p,q​=Hvq​(Dp,∗) and then E2p,q​=Hhp​(Hvq​D). Filtering by the second index gives the spectral sequence taking horizontal cohomology first and vertical cohomology next. Both filtrations are finite, so both converge to H∗(TotD). This is the two spectral sequences of a bounded double complex construction.
The printed left/left tensor expression needs a handedness repair. Over an arbitrary ring, a tensor product of modules over R pairs a right module with a left module. To retain the order of the printed formula, take P to be a bounded cochain complex of projective right R-modules and M a left R-module. Alternatively, keep P left, take M right, and write M⊗R​P and Tor−pR​(M,HqP). For a commutative ring no repair is needed. We prove the first, correctly typed formulation.
Finite projective dimension gives a finite projective resolution Q∙​→M by left modules. Form Dp,q=Pq⊗R​Q−p​ for −d≤p≤0. On x⊗y take dv​=dP​⊗1 and dh​=(−1)q1⊗dQ​. These anticommute, and the double cochain complex is bounded in both directions. Projective modules are flat modules, so taking vertical cohomology first gives
E1p,q​=Hq(P)⊗R​Q−p​,E2p,q​=Tor−pR​(HqP,M).​
(17)
The first identification uses flatness of Q−p​; the second is the Tor functor computed by resolving its left-module argument M. Balancedness of the Tor functor allows either correctly sided projective resolution to compute it, by the double-resolution argument.
Taking horizontal cohomology first instead uses flatness of Pq. The resolution Q∙​ then leaves only Pq⊗R​M in column p=0. Its remaining differential is dP​⊗1, so the augmentation TotD→P⊗R​M is a quasi-isomorphism. Apply the convergence theorem to this underlying abelian-group double complex. The two spectral sequences therefore prove the Künneth spectral sequence:
E2p,q​=Tor−pR​(HqP,M)⇒Hp+q(P⊗R​M).​
(18)
These are abelian groups in general; an extra module structure requires appropriate bimodule hypotheses. The index p is nonpositive, so −p is the nonnegative homological index of the Tor functor.
Finally write the cycle modules as Zq=kerdq and the boundary modules as Bq=imdq−1. If every boundary module Bq is projective, the short exact sequence 0→Zq→Pq→Bq+1→0 splits. Thus Zq is projective. The short exact sequence 0→Bq→Zq→HqP→0 is now a length-one projective resolution, so ToriR​(HqP,M)=0 for i≥2. The E2​ page occupies only columns p=−1,0. Every dr​ for r≥2 goes r columns to the right, hence has zero source or zero target. Therefore
E2​=E∞​.​
(19)
This two-column degeneration of a Künneth spectral sequence gives the edge short exact sequences
0⟶HnP⊗R​M⟶Hn(P⊗R​M)⟶Tor1R​(Hn+1P,M)⟶0.
(20)
Degeneration itself does not supply a canonical splitting. Projective boundaries also do not force HqP to be projective: a complex with differential multiplication by 2 over Z has projective boundary but a Z/2Z cohomology group.

Use homological grading dn​:Cn​→Cn−1​, with zero terms below degree zero. In the projective model structure on nonnegative chain complexes, the classes are:
  • Model weak equivalences are quasi-isomorphisms, inducing isomorphisms on every Hn​.
  • Model fibrations are maps surjective in every strictly positive degree n≥1; surjectivity in degree zero is not part of this definition.
  • Model cofibrations are degreewise injections with projective cokernel in every degree. The underlying module injections then split degreewise, although these splittings need not commute with the differential.
Thus a cofibrant complex has a projective module in each degree, and every object is fibrant. An projective acyclic fibration is equivalently a degreewise surjective quasi-isomorphism, including degree zero. To see the degree-zero assertion, lift a degree-zero homology class using the homology isomorphism, then lift the boundary discrepancy using surjectivity in degree one. Conversely a degreewise surjective quasi-isomorphism has the requisite positive-degree surjections.
Let Sn(R) be the sphere chain complex with R in degree n and zero elsewhere. Let Dn(R), for n≥1, be the disk chain complex with R in degrees n,n−1 and identity differential. The generating cofibrations for nonnegative chain complexes are
I={0→S0(R)} ∪ {Sn−1(R)↪Dn(R):n≥1}.​
(21)
Each is a model cofibration: its degreewise cokernel is a copy of R in one degree. The extra degree-zero generator must not be omitted.
Here is the lifting characterization of an acyclic chain-complex fibration. A right lifting property for Sn−1(R)→Dn(R) says that whenever x∈Zn−1​X and y∈Yn​ satisfy f(x)=dy, there is z∈Xn​ with dz=x and f(z)=y. The generator 0→S0(R) says that f0​ is surjective. For a degreewise surjective quasi-isomorphism, its kernel is acyclic by the long exact sequence in homology. Choose a preliminary lift z0​ of y. Then dz0​−x is a cycle in the kernel; subtract an element of the kernel whose boundary is this cycle. This gives the required z.
Conversely the lifting conditions make the kernel acyclic: take y=0 and any kernel cycle x. They also imply degreewise surjectivity inductively. Having surjectivity below degree n, lift dy to an element of Xn−1​, correct its boundary within the acyclic kernel to make it a cycle, and apply the lifting condition to lift y. For n=1 the chosen degree-zero element is already a cycle. A degreewise surjective map with acyclic kernel is a quasi-isomorphism by the long exact sequence in homology. Hence I-injectives are exactly acyclic fibrations.
An object A is sequentially small if for every sequential diagram Z0​→Z1​→⋯, the natural map
kcolim​Hom(A,Zk​) ⟶ Hom(A,kcolim​Zk​)​
(22)
is bijective. Surjectivity says that a map out of A factors through a finite stage; injectivity says that two such maps agreeing in the colimit agree at a later finite stage. The relative version restricts to the stated class of sequential diagrams. The domains in I have this property: maps from Sn−1(R) are cycles in one degree, and filtered colimits commute with these finite equations. Their sequential smallness is also permitted by the hint.
Apply the small object argument to the map a:X→Y. Put Z0​=X, p0​=a. Given pk​:Zk​→Y, take the set of all commutative squares with a generator i:Ai​→Bi​ on the left and pk​ on the right. Form the pushout of the coproduct of all these i along their maps into Zk​, obtaining Zk+1​. Each bottom map Bi​→Y induces the compatible map pk+1​:Zk+1​→Y. Repeat for all k≥0, and set Z∞​=colimk​Zk​. This gives
Xj​Z∞​p​Y,pj=a.​
(23)
Concretely, attaching Sn−1(R)→Dn(R) adds a free generator in degree n whose boundary is the chosen existing cycle; attaching 0→S0(R) adds a free degree-zero generator. Thus j is an injection, and its cokernel in each degree is free on the newly attached generators. It is therefore a model cofibration; this also illustrates the relative cell complex description of the left factor.
For a lifting square into Z∞​→Y, sequential smallness of its domain Ai​ factors the top map through some Zk​. The commutativity is already an equality in Y, so this is one of the squares attached at stage k+1. Its new cell supplies a lift Bi​→Zk+1​→Z∞​. Hence p has the right lifting property with respect to every element of I, and is an projective acyclic fibration. Every map has the required cofibration–acyclic-fibration factorization. Only the domains need sequential smallness; there is no requirement that X, Y or the coproduct of all cells be small.
