The special orthogonal group in five dimensions is
SO(5)={R∈M5​(R):RTR=I, detR=1}.
(22)
Its Lie algebra consists of real skew-symmetric matrices. There are 5⋅4/2=10 independent entries above the diagonal, so dimSO(5)=10. Equivalently, the orthogonality equations impose fifteen independent constraints on twenty-five matrix entries; the determinant condition chooses a component without changing the dimension.
Fix the fifth coordinate. The matrices diag(S,1), S∈SO(4), form an explicit subgroup. Under this subgroup the defining vector space splits as R4⊕Re5​, so its branching rule is 5↓SO(4)=4⊕1. An element of the so5 Lie algebra can be written uniquely as
X=(B−wT​w0​),B∈so(4),w∈R4.
(23)
Conjugation by diag(S,1) sends B to SBS−1 and w to Sw. The first summand is the six-dimensional Adjoint representation of a Lie algebra of so(4), and the second is its four-dimensional vector representation. Hence the SO5 to SO4 branching gives
5→4⊕1,10→6⊕4.​
(24)
For the left SU(2) subgroup of SO(4), identify R4 with the quaternions. Left multiplication by a unit quaternion is a real orthogonal transformation and gives an embedded SU(2) group. More generally, q↦aqb−1 gives the double cover SU(2)L​×SU(2)R​→SO(4) with kernel {(1,1),(−1,−1)}. After complexifying, the vector representation is (2,2) and the Adjoint representation is (3,1)⊕(1,3). Restricting to the left factor turns the right factor into a multiplicity space. Therefore
4→2⊕2,6→3⊕1⊕1⊕1,1→1.​
(25)
These are decompositions into complex irreducible representations; the real 4 is the underlying real representation of a quaternionic doublet. Combining the branching rules gives
5→22⊕1,10→3⊕22⊕31.
(26)
Write a weight as λ=(x,y). The integrality conditions for the B2 root system give 2x∈Z and y−x∈Z. Hence x=m/2, y=m/2+n, with m,n∈Z. Thus
P=Z2 ∪ (Z+21​)2.​
(27)
This is the B2 weight lattice, with an integer square lattice and a second square lattice shifted by (1/2,1/2). The eight roots of a root system are
±(1,0),±(0,1),±(1,1),±(1,−1).
(28)
The short roots lie on the coordinate axes and the long roots on the diagonals. The positive roots for the given simple-root choice are α, β, α+β, 2α+β.
The integrality conditions determine the weight lattice of the Lie algebra, equivalently of the simply connected Spin group Spin(5). For the global special orthogonal group SO(5), a 2π rotation in either coordinate plane is the identity, so a genuine group representation requires integer x,y. Thus the half-integer coset contains spin representations that do not descend to SO(5). Both representations requested here have integer weights, so their diagrams are unaffected by this distinction.
The root system of the displayed SO(4) subgroup is {±(1,1),±(1,−1)}. The two orthogonal pairs give its two commuting su(2) factors. Choose the left factor to have root δ=(1,1); exchanging the two diagonal pairs exchanges left and right. Its coroot pairs with a weight as
⟨λ,δ∨⟩=δ⋅δ2λ⋅δ​=x+y.
(29)
This demonstrates the diagonal-root SU(2) embedding in SO(5) directly. The short-axis root α would instead give 2x, and therefore a different subgroup: on the vector representation it would produce a triplet and two singlets rather than two doublets and a singlet.
For the vector representation, simultaneously rotate the first and second coordinate planes. Over C, the two planes give opposite pairs of weights, while the fifth coordinate is fixed. Hence its weight diagram is
Wt(5)={(1,0),(−1,0),(0,1),(0,−1),(0,0)},​
(30)
with every weight multiplicity equal to one. Evaluating x+y gives +1 twice, −1 twice and 0 once, exactly two SU(2) representations of dimension two and one singlet.
For the Adjoint representation, the root-space decomposition has one one-dimensional space for each of the eight roots and a two-dimensional zero-weight Cartan subalgebra. Thus
Wt(10)={eight roots of B2​}∪{(0,0) with multiplicity 2}.​
(31)
The coroot values x+y have multiplicities 1,2,4,2,1 at −2,−1,0,1,2. One zero-weight state joins the ±2 states to make a triplet; the ±1 states form two doublets, leaving three zero-weight singlets. This verifies the earlier branching rule and accounts for all ten dimensions.
