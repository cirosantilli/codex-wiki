For a differentiable curve R(t) in the SO(3) group with R(0)=I, differentiating R(t)TR(t)=I gives XT+X=0, where X=R′(0). Conversely, if X is a real skew-symmetric matrix, etX is an orthogonal matrix and has determinant ettrX=1. Hence
so(3)={X∈M3​(R):XT=−X},dimso(3)=3.​
(10)
The displayed generators of the SO(3) Lie algebra are
T1​=​000​001​0−10​​,T2​=​00−1​000​100​​,T3​=​010​−100​000​​.
(11)
They are linearly independent and span the three independent entries of a real skew-symmetric matrix. Equivalently, Ta​v=ea​×v. The vector triple-product identity then gives
[Ta​,Tb​]v=ea​×(eb​×v)−eb​×(ea​×v)=(ea​×eb​)×v.
(12)
Thus the structure constants of a Lie algebra in this basis are
[Ta​,Tb​]=ϵabc​Tc​,fab​c=ϵabc​.​
(13)
Use a real, antisymmetric convention throughout the SO(3) vector Higgs model. With gauge coupling g, the gauge covariant derivative and gauge field strength are
Dμ​Φ=∂μ​Φ+gAμ​Φ,Fμν​=∂μ​Aν​−∂ν​Aμ​+g[Aμ​,Aν​],[Dμ​,Dν​]=gFμν​.​
(14)
There is no factor of i in these formulas because Ta​ are real antisymmetric generators. For a local non-Abelian gauge transformation Φ′=RΦ, with R(x) in the SO(3) group, the compatible transformation of the gauge potential is
Aμ′​=RAμ​R−1−g−1(∂μ​R)R−1.
(15)
It gives Dμ′​Φ′=RDμ​Φ and Fμν′​=RFμν​R−1. These covariance laws fix the relative signs in the gauge covariant derivative and gauge field strength.
An SO(3) invariant quartic scalar potential is
V(Φ)=4λ​(ΦTΦ−v2)2,λ>0,v>0.​
(16)
Since (RΦ)T(RΦ)=ΦTΦ, this scalar potential is gauge-invariant. Its minimum is the sphere ΦTΦ=v2, rather than a preferred direction in the internal vector space.
Writing Aμ​=(Aμ1​,Aμ2​,Aμ3​), the cross product form of the gauge covariant derivative is Dμ​Φ=∂μ​Φ+gAμ​×Φ. In components this is
(Dμ​Φ)1​(Dμ​Φ)2​(Dμ​Φ)3​​=∂μ​Φ1​+g(Aμ2​Φ3​−Aμ3​Φ2​),=∂μ​Φ2​+g(Aμ3​Φ1​−Aμ1​Φ3​),=∂μ​Φ3​+g(Aμ1​Φ2​−Aμ2​Φ1​).​​
(17)
Correspondingly, the gauge field strength has components
Fμνa​=∂μ​Aνa​−∂ν​Aμa​+gϵabc​Aμb​Aνc​.
(18)
A covariantly constant vector Higgs field has constant norm, because
∂μ​(ΦTΦ)=2ΦT∂μ​Φ=−2gΦTAμ​Φ=0.
(19)
The last equality uses antisymmetry of the gauge potential. On a connected region write this norm as ϕ≥0. If ϕ=0, the vector Higgs field is identically zero. If ϕ>0, the SO(3) group acts transitively on unit vectors, so choose a smooth local non-Abelian gauge transformation rotating Φ/ϕ to e3​. In this unitary gauge, Φ=(0,0,ϕ)T with ϕ constant. This is a local construction, and is global on a trivial contractible region; a nontrivial bundle can require more than one gauge patch.
For nonzero ϕ, substituting the constant aligned vector Higgs field into Dμ​Φ=0 gives gϕ(Aμ2​,−Aμ1​,0)T=0. For nonzero gauge coupling, the most general compatible gauge potential and gauge field strength are
Aμ​=aμ​T3​,Fμν​=(∂μ​aν​−∂ν​aμ​)T3​,​
(20)
where aμ​ is an arbitrary real one-form. Thus the surviving gauge group is the SO(2) stabilizer subgroup of e3​. A covariantly constant vector Higgs field does not force the gauge field strength to vanish: it only requires the curvature to annihilate that field.
For the Higgs mechanism, choose a vacuum of the SO(3) invariant quartic scalar potential, so the constant norm is v. The canonically normalized kinetic term then contains
21​(Dμ​Φ)T(DμΦ)⊃21​g2v2(Aμ1​A1μ​+Aμ2​A2μ​).
(21)
The two broken-direction gauge bosons have mass gv (or ∣g∣v if the sign of g is unrestricted), while the T3​ gauge boson remains massless. The two angular Goldstone bosons supply the massive vectors' longitudinal gauge-boson polarizations. The radial fluctuation remains a physical scalar field, with mh2​=2λv2 for this normalization. The condition Dμ​Φ=0 selects backgrounds without broken-direction gauge potentials; fluctuations about such a vacuum exhibit the two massive modes. Constancy of the norm alone does not require it to minimize the scalar potential.
