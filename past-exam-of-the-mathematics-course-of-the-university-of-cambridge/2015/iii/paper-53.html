A collisionless relativistic species redshifts with Tν​∝a−1. The coupled plasma instead obeys cosmological entropy conservation, g∗s​T3a3=constant, and is heated relative to a decoupled species as massive particles annihilate. The effective number of relativistic energy degrees of freedom g∗​ and effective number of relativistic entropy degrees of freedom g∗s​ coincide while all species share a common relativistic temperature. Use gd​:=g∗s​(Tdec​)≃100 for the Standard Model bath, excluding the already decoupled extra species.
Just before ordinary neutrino decoupling, the coupled bath contains two photon states, four Electron/Positron states and six ordinary neutrino/antineutrino states. Therefore
g∗s​=2+87​(4+6)=443​.
(15)
The extra-neutrino temperature relative to that bath is then (43/(4gd​))1/3. After the ordinary neutrinos decouple, Electron/Positron annihilation heats only the photon bath: its entropy count changes from 2+(7/8)4=11/2 to 2. Both neutrino populations miss that last heating, so
T0​Tν,0​​=(4gd​43​)1/3(114​)1/3=(11gd​43​)1/3≃0.34.​
(16)
This is the two-stage entropy dilution of an early-decoupled neutrino. Numerically Tν,0​≃6.8×10−14GeV. Relative to ordinary relic neutrinos it is cooler by (43/(4gd​))1/3≃0.48.
Let gν​ count the extra neutrino's populated helicity and antiparticle states. For one conventional neutrino plus antineutrino species gν​=2, the same state count as photons. The Fermi-Dirac distribution supplies the 3/4 number-density factor, so
nγ,0​nν,0​​=43​2gν​​(T0​Tν,0​​)3=83gν​​11gd​43​≃0.0292gν​​.​
(17)
Thus a two-state species has about 2.9% of the photon number density. Stating gν​ explicitly also covers a different choice of populated states.

The preceding bound applies to a species that freezes out while relativistic, retaining an unsuppressed thermal number density apart from entropy dilution. It need not apply to a species that is already massive while reactions remain rapid.
For mν​≫Tdec​, the interaction strength is sufficient to keep the species in thermal equilibrium through its relativistic-to-nonrelativistic transition. At T∼mν​, the relativistic scaling would give Γ/H∼(mν​/Tdec​)3≫1. Subsequent annihilation removes most particle-antiparticle pairs while equilibrium remains possible. At negligible chemical potential, the nonrelativistic equilibrium number density is
neq​≃gν​(2πmν​T​)3/2e−mν​/T.
(21)
This Boltzmann suppression of a nonrelativistic relic can make the eventual abundance much smaller than the relativistically decoupled value. Freeze-out now occurs when neq​⟨σv⟩∼H, with a nonrelativistic cross-section, rather than by extrapolating the massless T5 rate indefinitely. A large mass can therefore have an acceptably small energy density: a hot-relic mass bound is not a universal bound on a heavy thermal relic.
Compatibility depends on the annihilation rate and any conserved particle-antiparticle asymmetry; the supplied relativistic rate alone does not determine the heavy relic's final abundance. Efficient annihilation is the mechanism that permits large masses within the standard expansion and thermal history.

i OurBigBook.com
A light inflaton behaves approximately as a scalar field in de Sitter space while a wavelength is well inside the Hubble radius. Its Bunch-Davies vacuum has quantum fluctuations. Cosmic inflation stretches each Fourier mode until k=aH, after which its physical wavelength exceeds the Hubble radius. The nearly constant growing field mode has a typical fluctuation per logarithmic wavenumber interval δϕ∼H/(2π).
A field fluctuation changes the local position on the rolling background trajectory. Neighboring regions therefore reach the same field value, and the end of inflation, at slightly different times. A clock displacement of magnitude ∣δϕ/ϕ˙​∣ becomes a difference in local expansion of order H∣δϕ/ϕ˙​∣. Equivalently, in a conventional sign choice the comoving curvature perturbation is related to the field fluctuation on spatially flat slices by
R=ϕ˙​H​δϕflat​,∣R∣∼∣ϕ˙​∣H​2πH​.​
(22)
Changing the sign convention for spatial curvature changes the sign of R, but not its spectrum. This is the inflaton clock-shift origin of curvature perturbations. For a single-field slow-roll attractor there is no independent entropy mode. On a super-Hubble scale, gradient terms are negligible and the superhorizon conservation of single-field comoving curvature preserves the growing adiabatic mode, so fluctuations generated near exit persist as primordial curvature perturbations. This conservation requires the attractor and adiabatic assumptions; a freely chosen non-attractor background would not have the same conclusion.

An adiabatic mode corresponds locally to a common displacement along the background history. Its density changes obey δρi​/ρˉ​˙​i​=δt for every component. The cosmological perfect-fluid continuity equation then implies that Δi​/(1+wi​) is common to all fluids. Thus, in the common comoving gauge in cosmology relevant to the growing superhorizon mode,
Δr​=34​Δc​.​
(35)
Equivalently, the relative entropy perturbation Δc​−(3/4)Δr​ vanishes. A common time-slicing shift preserves this relation because it changes both normalized density perturbations by the same amount.
The flat background Friedmann equation gives 4πGa2ρˉ​=(3/2)H2. During radiation domination, H=1/τ and the matter background is negligible. Substituting the adiabatic relation into the density equation therefore gives
Δc′′​+τ1​Δc′​=τ24​Δc​.
(36)
With Δc​∝τp, the indicial equation is p2=4, so the modes are τ2 and τ−2.
During matter domination, H=2/τ and the radiation background is negligible. The same calculation gives
Δc′′​+τ2​Δc′​=τ26​Δc​,
(37)
whose indicial equation is p2+p−6=(p−2)(p+3)=0. Its modes are τ2 and τ−3. Thus the superhorizon growing density mode of adiabatic matter and radiation satisfies Δc​∝τ2 in both eras. The common conformal-time exponent does not imply the same scale-factor dependence: it is Δc​∝a2 in the radiation era and Δc​∝a in the matter era.

Once a mode is well inside the horizon, radiation pressure opposes gravitational collapse. For an ideal radiation fluid its cosmological sound speed is cs​=1/3​, and its density perturbation undergoes subhorizon radiation acoustic oscillations with frequency approximately k/3​. Unlike the superhorizon growing mode, its amplitude does not acquire sustained gravitational growth. Free-streaming relativistic components can additionally phase-mix the perturbation; the acoustic-fluid description is the appropriate idealization for the stated system.
During deep radiation domination, the radiation source in the dark-matter equation oscillates rapidly compared with a Hubble time. Its accumulated late-time effect averages away. More quantitatively, an oscillatory source of magnitude Δr​/τ2 gives a rapidly varying particular solution of order Δr​/(kτ)2 when kτ≫1. It can therefore be neglected in computing the slowly varying homogeneous dark-matter growth, although its forcing near horizon entry fixes the integration constants.
The dark-matter self-gravity term is also subleading while a/aeq​≪1. Keeping the leading expansion drag and averaging the radiation source yields
Δc′′​+τ1​Δc′​≃0,(τΔc′​)′≃0.
(38)
Consequently
Δc​=C1​+C2​ln(τ/τh​),Δc,grow​∝lnτ.​
(39)
This logarithmic growth of matter perturbations during radiation domination is the Mészáros effect. It is the leading behavior well after horizon entry but before equality; close to equality the neglected self-gravity matters. The radiation perturbation is not small simply because it is radiation: its rapid oscillations, rather than a small radiation background density, justify dropping its source for this slow mode.

In matter domination, the radiation source is negligible and cold matter has negligible pressure. The comoving density equation consequently contains no Jeans-pressure term proportional to k2Δc​. On every linear scale, including superhorizon and subhorizon modes, it reduces to
Δc′′​+τ2​Δc′​−τ26​Δc​=0.
(40)
The matter-era growing and decaying density modes are therefore
Δc​=Cg​τ2+Cd​τ−3,Δc,grow​∝τ2∝a.​
(41)
The absence of a k-dependent pressure term makes the time dependence scale-independent; the coefficients still depend on wavenumber and on the earlier radiation-era evolution. This is linear growth in the stated cold-matter model, before nonlinear collapse or any additional late-time component changes the evolution.

Write Pc​(k,τ)=k3∣Δc​(k,τ)∣2/(2π2) for the dimensionless power spectrum. A radiation-era mode enters the horizon at τh​∼k−1. The early superhorizon law gives Pc​(k,τh​)∼A: the τh4​ and k4 factors cancel.
For kτeq​≪1, the mode remains outside the horizon throughout the radiation era. Its amplitude grows as τ2 both before and after equality, and continues with the same scale-independent growing factor even if it later enters during matter domination. Hence the low-wavenumber shape is preserved:
Pc​(k,τ)∼Aτ4k4,kτeq​≪1.​
(42)
For kτeq​≫1, horizon entry occurs during radiation domination. Its subsequent logarithmic amplitude growth supplies a factor ln(τeq​/τh​)∼ln(kτeq​) by equality. During the matter era the amplitude grows by (τ/τeq​)2. Squaring gives the logarithmic high-wavenumber density-spectrum transfer:
Pc​(k,τ)∼A(τeq​τ​)4[ln(kτeq​)]2,kτeq​≫1.​
(43)
The horizon-entry and equality matching fixes order-one coefficients and additive constants inside the logarithm. The displayed branches capture the requested leading spectrum and share its schematic normalization; their asymptotic forms must not be joined literally at kτeq​=1, where the high-k logarithm alone vanishes.
Thus the present dimensionless power spectrum rises as k4 on large scales and only as ln2k on small scales, with a smooth bend near the matter-radiation equality scale, keq​∼τeq−1​. The ordinary dimensional cosmological density power spectrum instead satisfies
