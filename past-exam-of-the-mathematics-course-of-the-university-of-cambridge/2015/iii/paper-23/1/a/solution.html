Past exam of the mathematics course of the University of Cambridge / 2015 / iii / Paper 23 / 1 / a / Solution

 ... Past exam of the mathematics course of the University of Cambridge 2015 iii Paper 23 1 a
OurBigBook.com Words: 223
Let R=∏i​Mi​/F be the reduced product by a proper filter on a set. Its underlying equivalence relation is f∼g when {i:f(i)=g(i)}∈F. Operations are interpreted coordinatewise, and a relation holds of the classes exactly when its coordinate truth set belongs to F.
Evaluation of a first-order term commutes with passage to the quotient, by mathematical induction on terms. Consequently the desired equivalence holds for every atomic formula, including logical equality. For a formula θ and representatives fˉ​, write Sθ​={i:Mi​⊨θ(fˉ​(i))}.
For logical conjunction, Sθ∧ψ​=Sθ​∩Sψ​. The filter on a set axioms give
Sθ​∩Sψ​∈F⟺Sθ​∈F and Sψ​∈F.
(1)
Thus the induction hypothesis transfers a conjunction in both directions.
For existential quantification, first suppose R⊨∃yθ(y,[fˉ​]). Choose a representative g of a witness. Induction gives Sθ(g,fˉ​)​∈F, and this set is contained in S∃yθ​. Upward closure therefore gives S∃yθ​∈F.
Conversely, suppose A=S∃yθ​∈F. For each i∈A, choose a coordinate witness g(i), and choose an arbitrary element of Mi​ outside A. These simultaneous choices use the axiom of choice, as does the usual product construction. Then A⊆Sθ(g,fˉ​)​, so that truth set belongs to F. Induction gives R⊨θ([g],[fˉ​]), providing the required witness. Therefore
R⊨φ([fˉ​])⟺{i:Mi​⊨φ(fˉ​(i))}∈F​
(2)
for every primitive positive formula. The exam's tame formulas are exactly this fragment, built using logical conjunction and existential quantification. No ultrafilter dichotomy was used.
