The Stone topology on a type space has basic open sets
[φ]={p∈SnM​(A):φ(xˉ)∈p},​

where φ ranges over formulas with parameters in A and n free variables. Here points are complete types consistent with TA​=Th(M,a)a∈A​. Arbitrary open sets are unions of these basic sets.
The identities
[φ]∩[ψ]=[φ∧ψ],SnM​(A)∖[φ]=[¬φ]
(34)
show that the basic sets are clopen sets and form a basis. Distinct complete types contain opposite choices for some formula, so this topology is Hausdorff. The compactness theorem gives compactness: a family of basic closed sets with the finite intersection property determines a consistent set of formulas, which extends to a complete type. Thus logical formulas provide a basis of clopen tests for distinguishing points of the type space.
