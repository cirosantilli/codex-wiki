A holomorphic atlas on a real smooth manifold of dimension 2n consists of compatible smooth charts φa​:Ua​→Va​⊆Cn whose transition maps φb​φa−1​ are biholomorphic on their domains. An almost complex structure is a smooth real vector bundle endomorphism J:TX→TX with J2=−id. In each chart define
Jx​=(dφa​)x−1​i(dφa​)x​.
(1)
The differential of a holomorphic map is a complex-linear map, so the transition differentials commute with multiplication by i. Consequently the local definitions agree on overlaps. They are smooth and square to −id, yielding the natural almost complex structure induced by a complex atlas. This construction uses the atlas, rather than an arbitrary choice of coordinates on the underlying real smooth manifold.

Extend J complex-linearly to the complexification of a real vector bundle TC​X=TX⊗R​C. Since J2=−id, its +i and −i eigenbundles have smooth projections
P1,0=21​(id−iJ),P0,1=21​(id+iJ),TC​X=T1,0X⊕T0,1X.
(2)
Each has complex rank n: complex conjugation interchanges them, and together they have rank 2n. These are the type decomposition of the complexified tangent bundle. For an arbitrary almost complex structure, they are smooth complex vector bundles; a holomorphic vector bundle structure requires integrability.
When J comes from a holomorphic atlas, write a holomorphic coordinate as zj​=xj​+iyj​. Its Wirtinger derivatives are
∂zj​∂​=21​(∂xj​∂​−i∂yj​∂​),∂zˉj​∂​=21​(∂xj​∂​+i∂yj​∂​).
(3)
The induced almost complex structure has J∂xj​​=∂yj​​ and J∂yj​​=−∂xj​​. Hence the displayed vector fields are respectively +i and −i eigenvectors. They are linearly independent, and each collection has n elements. Thus they give local frames for T1,0X and T0,1X respectively, with T1,0X the holomorphic tangent bundle. The repeated T0,1X in the first sentence of the printed item must be read as the two complementary eigenbundles.

With the normalization used here, N=2NJ​, where NJ​ is the standard Nijenhuis tensor. First check that it is a tensor: the Lie bracket identity [fU,V]=f[U,V]−V(f)U gives
NJ​(fU,V)−fNJ​(U,V)​=−(JV)(f)JU+V(f)U+(JV)(f)JU+V(f)J2U=0.​
(4)
Skew symmetry gives the same C∞-linearity in the second input. In coordinates from a holomorphic atlas, the real coordinate vector fields ∂xj​​,∂yj​​ commute, and J sends each to another such field with a constant sign. Every Lie bracket in NJ​ therefore vanishes on these coordinate fields. Tensoriality then gives N=0​ on all smooth vector fields. The factor two in this paper does not change this vanishing conclusion.

Use the complex-bilinear extension of the Nijenhuis tensor expression to complex vector fields. If U,V∈T1,0X, then JU=iU and JV=iV, so
N(U,V)=−4[U,V]−4iJ[U,V]=−8[U,V]0,1.
(5)
For U,V∈T0,1X, the same substitution with −i gives N(U,V)=−8[U,V]1,0. For inputs of opposite types, the two leading Lie brackets cancel, as do the two terms involving J, so N(U,V)=0. Applying the type decomposition of the complexified tangent bundle to both inputs consequently gives
N(α,β)=−8[α′,β′]′′−8[α′′,β′′]′,N(α,β)′′=−8[α′,β′]′′.​
(6)
Thus N=0 precisely when the Lie bracket of any two sections of either eigenbundle remains in that same eigenbundle. In other words, N=0 if and only if both tangent-type distributions are involutive distributions. This proves the equivalence directly and does not assume that an arbitrary almost complex structure already has holomorphic coordinates.

We use complex-valued smooth differential forms. The sheaf of smooth differential forms is Ak(U)=Γ(U,⋀k(T∗X⊗C)), with the usual restriction maps. Dualizing the type decomposition of the complexified tangent bundle and taking exterior powers decomposes this bundle into the summands
⋀p(T1,0X)∗⊗⋀q(T0,1X)∗,p+q=k.
(7)
Their smooth sections form the sheaf of differential forms of type (p, q) Ap,q. Locally a section is a sum of fI,J​dzI​∧dzˉJ​ with ∣I∣=p, ∣J∣=q and smooth coefficients. Holomorphic transition maps preserve types, so the local decompositions agree globally. Hence
Ak(X)=p+q=k⨁​Ap,q(X).​
(8)
The exterior derivative splits as d=∂+∂ˉ, with bidegrees (1,0) and (0,1). Its square being zero gives ∂2=∂ˉ2=0 and ∂∂ˉ+∂ˉ∂=0. The Dolbeault cohomology is therefore
H∂ˉp,q​(X)=∂ˉAp,q−1(X)ker(∂ˉ:Ap,q(X)→Ap,q+1(X))​.​
(9)
Forms in negative or out-of-range bidegrees are understood to be zero.

For a differential form of type (p, q), dα=0 is equivalent to both ∂α=0 and ∂ˉα=0, because the two resulting types are distinct. Moreover ∂∂ˉβ has type (p,q) when β has type (p−1,q−1), and
d(∂∂ˉβ)=0
(10)
by the square-zero and anticommutation identities. Thus the denominator defining Bott-Chern cohomology is a vector subspace of its numerator, making the quotient well defined.
Complex conjugation of differential-form type sends a d-closed (p,q)-form to a d-closed (q,p)-form. For a representative in the denominator,
∂∂ˉβ​=∂ˉ∂βˉ​=−∂∂ˉβˉ​.
(11)
The minus sign does not change the denominator vector subspace. Conjugation consequently induces a conjugate-linear bijection [α]↦[αˉ] between the two Bott-Chern cohomology spaces; applying it twice is the identity. Equivalently, HBCp,q​(X)≅HBCq,p​(X)​​ as complex vector spaces.

We prove the Bott-Chern Poincaré lemma on a polydisc B. Let p,q≥1 and dα=0, with α of type (p,q). The allowed Dolbeault-Poincaré lemma first gives α=∂ˉb0​, where b0​ has type (p,q−1). Since ∂ˉ∂b0​=−∂α=0, repeatedly applying the same lemma constructs
bj​∈Ap+j,q−1−j(B),∂ˉbj​=∂bj−1​(1≤j≤q−1).
(12)
Zero spaces outside the allowed bidegrees cause no difficulty. The form ∂bq−1​ has type (p+q,0), is holomorphic because its Dolbeault operator vanishes, and is ∂-closed. The holomorphic Poincaré lemma on a polydisc gives a holomorphic form h of type (p+q−1,0) with ∂h=∂bq−1​. This holomorphic version follows from the radial homotopy formula: on positive-degree holomorphic forms the ordinary radial Poincaré lemma homotopy preserves holomorphic coefficients. If the displayed form is zero, take h=0.
Now bq−1​−h is ∂-closed. The conjugate Dolbeault-Poincaré lemma, obtained by conjugating the allowed lemma, gives bq−1​−h=∂cq−1​, since its holomorphic degree is positive. Moving backwards, if the last modified form is ∂cj​, then
∂(bj−1​+∂ˉcj​)=0.
(13)
Apply the conjugate Dolbeault-Poincaré lemma again to obtain bj−1​+∂ˉcj​=∂cj−1​. The modification does not change its Dolbeault operator, so this process continues down to b0​. In the case q=1, subtract h at this last step instead. Finally,
α=∂ˉb0​=∂ˉ∂c0​=−∂∂ˉc0​.
(14)
Thus HBCp,q​(B)=0(p,q≥1)​.
This vanishing fails on general complex manifolds. On the complex projective line, let ω be its Fubini-Study form. It is a d-closed (1,1)-form with ∫P1​ω>0. If it were ∂∂ˉf, it would be the exact differential form d(∂ˉf), whose integral is zero by Stokes theorem. Therefore HBC1,1​(P1)=0​.

A d-closed differential form of type (p, q) is ∂ˉ-closed, and a Bott-Chern cohomology coboundary is a Dolbeault cohomology coboundary because
∂∂ˉβ=−∂ˉ(∂β).
(15)
Hence taking the same representative defines a complex-linear map ϕ:HBCp,q​(X)→H∂ˉp,q​(X).
If X is compact and Kähler, the Dolbeault Hodge decomposition supplies a ∂ˉ-harmonic representative α of every Dolbeault cohomology class. The scalar Kähler Laplacian identity is Δd​=2Δ∂ˉ​, so α is also d-harmonic. Consequently
0=⟨Δd​α,α⟩=∥dα∥2+∥d∗α∥2,
(16)
and dα=0. It therefore defines a Bott-Chern cohomology class mapping to the original class. Thus ϕ is surjective on a compact Ka¨hler manifold.​
For clarity, the scalar Kähler Laplacian identity follows from the Kähler identities: they make the mixed anticommutators {∂,∂ˉ∗} and {∂ˉ,∂∗} zero, while the curvature-zero instance of the identity proved in 4(b) gives Δ′=Δ′′. Expanding Δd​ yields Δ′+Δ′′=2Δ′′.

The printed assertion for every cohomological degree is false. The valid conclusion supplied by the stated Hartogs extension theorem is the degree-zero isomorphism. Indeed, around each removed point choose a small coordinate ball on which the holomorphic vector bundle E is trivial. A holomorphic section on the punctured ball has finitely many holomorphic coefficient functions, each of which extends by Hartogs extension theorem when the complex dimension is at least two. Uniqueness of holomorphic extension makes these local extensions agree with the original section on overlaps, giving
H0(X,O(E))≅H0(X0​,O(E∣X0​​)).​
(20)
This also identifies the direct image sheaf j∗​O(E∣X0​​) with O(E) for the inclusion j:X0​↪X. It does not identify higher sheaf cohomology.
Here is an explicit higher-degree obstruction. Take X=P2, remove [0:0:1], and take the trivial line bundle. Cover X0​ by U={x=0} and V={y=0}. On U use coordinates t=y/x, s=z/x; on V use u=x/y=1/t, v=z/y=s/t. Both charts are C2, and the overlap is C∗×C. For every integer m≥2, the holomorphic function
gm​=xm−1yzm​=tsm​
(21)
defines a Čech cocycle. It cannot be a Čech coboundary b(1/t,s/t)−a(t,s) with a,b entire on their charts. Taking the coefficient of sm, the term from a has only nonnegative powers of t, whereas the term from b has the form t−mbm​(1/t) and only powers at most −m. Neither can provide the coefficient t−1. Uniqueness of Laurent series proves the contradiction. The same argument applied to each fibre degree proves that all the classes [gm​], m≥2, are linearly independent.
These classes remain nonzero in Čech cohomology of X0​, rather than merely of this cover. The low-degree Mayer-Vietoris sequence for sheaf cohomology injects the quotient of overlap sections by the two chart-section groups into H1(X0​,O). Concretely, a partition of unity gives smooth fU​,fV​ with fV​−fU​=g; their common Dolbeault operator defines a global (0,1)-form. If that form were ∂ˉ-exact, subtracting its global smooth primitive would make fU​,fV​ holomorphic and split g. The nonsplitting just proved therefore gives the same obstruction in Dolbeault cohomology, and the Dolbeault theorem identifies it with sheaf cohomology. Thus H1(X0​,O) is infinite dimensional. In contrast, H1(P2,O) is finite dimensional by compact Dolbeault Hodge decomposition for the Fubini-Study form. Hence the two degree-one groups cannot be isomorphic, disproving the printed all-degree request already in complex dimension two.
In complex dimension one, even the degree-zero conclusion fails. Take X=P1, remove its point at infinity and use the trivial line bundle. Holomorphic functions on compact connected P1 are constant by the maximum modulus principle, whereas X0​≅C has nonconstant entire functions such as z. Therefore the restriction map is not onto even in degree zero.

The Chern connection of a Hermitian metric on a holomorphic vector bundle is the unique connection on a vector bundle compatible with that metric and whose (0,1) part is the bundle's Dolbeault operator ∂ˉE​. Fix a holomorphic local frame and column coefficients for sections. Write the metric as h(s,t)=sˉTHt, conjugate-linear in the first argument, and write the connection as Ds=ds+As. The condition D0,1=∂ˉE​ forces A to have type (1,0). Metric compatibility requires
dH=A†H+HA,
(25)
where the dagger conjugates the differential-form coefficients as well as transposing the matrix. Taking the (1,0) part gives A=H−1∂H​. Its conjugate-transpose supplies the (0,1) metric equation because H is Hermitian. This proves uniqueness and local existence.
Under a holomorphic change of frame e′=eg, the metric matrix becomes H′=g†Hg. The local formula for the Chern connection on a vector bundle then gives
A′=g−1Ag+g−1∂g.
(26)
This is precisely the transformation rule for a connection on a vector bundle, so the local connections glue and establish global existence. No Kähler hypothesis is needed for this part.
Extend D to vector-bundle-valued differential forms by the graded Leibniz rule. The curvature form of a connection is the tensorial square Fh​=D2, acting by exterior multiplication. In the chosen frame,
Fh​=dA+A∧A=∂ˉ(H−1∂H).
(27)
Indeed ∂A+A∧A=0 by differentiating H−1H=I. It follows that Fh​ has type (1,1); the gauge change is Fh′​=g−1Fh​g. Thus it is a global smooth two-form with values in End(E), namely an element of A2(End(E)).

The Chern connection decomposes on vector-bundle-valued differential forms as D=D′+D′′, where D′=∇h′​ raises holomorphic degree and D′′=∇h′′​=∂ˉE​ raises antiholomorphic degree. In a holomorphic local frame, D′=∂+A∧ and D′′=∂ˉ. The type of the curvature form of a connection gives
(D′)2=(D′′)2=0,D′D′′+D′′D′=Fh​∧.
(28)
The Kähler metric and the Hermitian metric define the L2 inner product and the formal adjoints D′∗,D′′∗. The Dolbeault Laplacians are
Δ′=D′D′∗+D′∗D′,Δ′′=D′′D′′∗+D′′∗D′′.
(29)
Let L=ω∧ be the Lefschetz operator of a Kähler manifold and Λ=L∗ its adjoint Lefschetz operator. With the ordinary commutator convention [A,B]=AB−BA, the printed Kähler identities give
D′∗=i[Λ,D′′],D′′∗=−i[Λ,D′].
(30)
Substitution into the two Dolbeault Laplacians, followed by expansion, yields
Δ′′−Δ′​=−i{D′′,[Λ,D′]}−i{D′,[Λ,D′′]}=i[D′D′′+D′′D′,Λ]=[iFh​∧,Λ].​
(31)
The middle line follows by cancelling the terms with D′ΛD′′ and D′′ΛD′; the remaining terms collect the anticommutator of the two differentials. Hence Δ′′=Δ′+[iFh​,Λ]​, where iFh​ denotes its wedge action. This is the Bochner-Kodaira-Nakano identity with the paper's sign convention.

A positive holomorphic line bundle F admits a Hermitian metric whose Chern connection curvature satisfies that iFF​ is a positive real (1, 1)-form. In a local holomorphic local frame with squared length e−φ, the local formula for the Chern connection on a line bundle gives FF​=∂∂ˉφ, so positivity means i∂∂ˉφ is positive definite. Its closedness makes ω=iFF​ a Kähler form; use this form to define the operators below.
Let n=dimC​X>0, choose a Hermitian metric on E, and equip Gm​=E⊗F−m with the tensor-product metric. The curvature of a tensor product connection gives
iFGm​​=iFE​−mωidE​.
(32)
On Gm​-valued zero-forms, the Lefschetz commutator is [L,Λ]=−nid. Thus the Bochner-Kodaira-Nakano identity gives
ΔGm​′′​=ΔGm​′​+RE​+mnid,RE​=[iFE​,Λ]​A0,0(E)​.
(33)
This last operator is a fixed smooth self-adjoint bundle endomorphism. Compactness supplies a finite C such that ⟨RE​v,v⟩≥−C∥v∥2 at every point. For a holomorphic section s of Gm​, D′′s=0 and D′′∗s=0 by degree, so integrating the identity gives
0=∥D′s∥2+⟨RE​s,s⟩+mn∥s∥2≥(mn−C)∥s∥2.
(34)
Choose an integer m0​ with m0​n>C. Then H0(X,E⊗F−m)=0(m≥m0​)​. The threshold depends on the fixed bundle E, as the curvature bound makes explicit. Positive complex dimension is necessary: on a zero-dimensional manifold positivity is vacuous and a nonzero fibre has nonzero sections for every twist.

For a holomorphic vector bundle E, the sheaf of vector-bundle-valued differential forms is
Ap,q(E)(U)=Γ(U,⋀p(T1,0X)∗⊗⋀q(T0,1X)∗⊗E),

ΔE′′​=∂ˉE​∂ˉE∗​+∂ˉE∗​∂ˉE​.

Choose a positive Hermitian metric on L. Its curvature form ω=iFh​ is a Kähler form on the compact complex manifold of dimension one. Part (c) gives a threshold k0​ with H1(X,O(Lk))=0 for every k≥k0​. We use this to prescribe a finite principal part of a meromorphic section.
Fix a smooth cutoff χ supported inside the given coordinate chart and equal to one near x0​. Let P(z)=∑j=−r−1​aj​zj. On the punctured manifold define tk​=χP(z)ζk inside the chart, extended by zero outside. Its Dolbeault operator
ηk​=∂ˉLk​tk​=(∂ˉχ)P(z)ζk
(43)
is smooth globally: it vanishes near the pole and near the boundary of the chart. It is ∂ˉLk​-closed, either by the square-zero identity or because there are no (0,2)-forms in complex dimension one. The vanishing of H1(X,O(Lk)), together with the Dolbeault theorem, therefore gives a global smooth section uk​ with ∂ˉLk​uk​=ηk​.
The section sk​=tk​−uk​ is holomorphic on X∖{x0​}. Near x0​, ηk​=0, so uk​=fk​(z)ζk with fk​ holomorphic through zero. Its Taylor series then gives
sk​(z)=(j=−r∑−1​aj​zj+j≥0∑​ajk​zj)ζk,z=0,​
(44)
where the second series converges near zero and its coefficients are those of −fk​. The threshold is determined by the fixed bundle L and X, and is independent of the prescribed coefficients. In fact the same threshold works for every finite pole order r, since the Dolbeault cohomology obstruction is the same H1(X,Lk) for every such cutoff construction.
