On the dashed spanning tree, the nonzero flows are
f12​=f13​=f25​=f34​=f46​=2,f58​=1,
(23)
with f78​=0 and all non-tree flows zero. The tree balances give supply four at vertex 1, demands one at 5 and 8, and demand two at 6. Its cost is 25.
Choose π1​=0 and make the tree network reduced costs zero. The resulting network dual potentials are
π=(0,−3,−2,−3,−7,−5,−6,−8).
(24)
For the non-tree edges, in the order (2,4),(3,6),(4,5),(4,7),(5,7),(6,7), the network reduced costs are
(1,0,−1,0,2,1).
(25)
Only (4,5) has negative network reduced cost, so it enters the basis.
Adding this edge to the tree creates the graph cycle 4→5→2→1→3→4. Increasing its flow by θ adds θ on (4,5),(1,3),(3,4) and subtracts θ on (2,5),(1,2). These signed changes preserve every flow balance. The simplex ratio test allows θ≤min(f25​,f12​)=2, and the objective decreases by θ because the cycle's signed cost is −1.
Take θ=2 and remove (2,5) from the basis. The other tied edge (1,2) remains a zero-flow basic edge; this is a legitimate degenerate tree basis and requires no extra pivot. The new flow is
f13​=f34​=4,f45​=f46​=2,f58​=1,all other flows 0.​
(26)
Its cost is 8+4+6+4+1=23. For the new tree, choose
π=(0,−3,−2,−3,−6,−5,−5,−7).
(27)
Every network reduced cost is nonnegative: the new non-tree costs for (2,4),(2,5),(3,6),(4,7),(5,7),(6,7) are (1,1,0,1,2,2). All positive-flow edges have zero network reduced cost, and the dual objective is πTb=6+10+7=23. Thus complementary slackness gives
minimum cost=23,reached in one network simplex pivot.​
(28)
