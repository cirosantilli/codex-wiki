Take the sky frame and disk frame to be right-handed orthonormal bases, with positive angular displacement measured from X toward Y. In particular, positive Z is toward the observer, so material crossing the sky plane with positive Z˙ is at the ascending node. Write cd​=cosΩd​, sd​=sinΩd​, Cd​=cosId​, and Sd​=sinId​. The disk frame's first unit vector lies along its ascending node, and its second has positive Z component. The orbital-frame rotation from inclination and node therefore gives
T=RZ​(Ωd​)RX​(Id​)=​cd​sd​0​−sd​Cd​cd​Cd​Sd​​sd​Sd​−cd​Sd​Cd​​​.​
(1)
Each column is a disk-frame unit vector in sky coordinates, so X=Tx and the inverse is x=TTX. At the disk's ascending node, the Z component of its tangential velocity is positive, confirming the sign of the tilt.
Let Tp​=RZ​(Ωp​)RX​(Ip​) define a provisional planet frame whose first axis is the planet's sky-plane ascending node. Its normal is zp​=(sinΩp​sinIp​,−cosΩp​sinIp​,cosIp​). Expressing that normal in the disk orthonormal basis, with ΔΩ=Ωp​−Ωd​, gives
TTzp​=​sinIp​sinΔΩ−cosId​sinIp​cosΔΩ+sinId​cosIp​cosId​cosIp​+sinId​sinIp​cosΔΩ​​.
(2)
The last component is the dot product of the two orbital normals, hence the cosine of the mutual inclination. The mutual ascending node points along zd​×zp​: motion there has positive disk-normal component. Set
A=cosId​sinIp​cosΔΩ−sinId​cosIp​,B=sinIp​sinΔΩ.
(3)
Then x′=(Ax+By​)/A2+B2​, and the quadrant-correct longitude of ascending node is Ωm​=atan2(B,A). Thus
cosIm​=cosId​cosIp​+sinId​sinIp​cosΔΩ,tanΩm​=cosId​sinIp​cosΔΩ−sinId​cosIp​sinIp​sinΔΩ​.​
(4)
The tangent alone cannot fix the quadrant, and Ωm​ is undefined for exactly coincident orbital planes.
For the two rotation routes, define ψ as the oriented angle, within the planet's orbital plane, from its sky-plane ascending node to its disk-plane ascending node. If xp​,y​p​ are the first two columns of Tp​, take ψ=atan2(x′⋅y​p​,x′⋅xp​). Both routes describe the same planet orthonormal basis:
TRz​(Ωm​)Rx​(Im​)=Tp​Rz​(ψ).
(5)
The left route goes through the disk frame; the right goes through the sky-node planet frame. Comparing their third columns gives the two boxed relations, while comparison of first columns fixes the extra in-plane angle.
Put Id​=π/2+δd​ and Ip​=π/2+δp​. A second-order Taylor expansion gives cosIm​=1−[(δp​−δd​)2+ΔΩ2]/2+O(δ4), and therefore the mutual inclination of nearly edge-on orbits satisfies
Im2​=(Ip​−Id​)2+(Ωp​−Ωd​)2+O(δ4).​
(6)
All expansion angles are in radians. For plotting in degrees this is Im​≃(Ip​−87∘)2+(4∘)2​. The given planet has exact Im​≃4.119∘ and Ωm​≃76.044∘. The exact minimum occurs at Ip​=atan2(sinId​cosΔΩ,cosId​)≃86.993∘, with Im,min​≃3.995∘. At Ip​=85∘ and 95∘, the second-order values are 20​∘≃4.472∘ and 80​∘≃8.944∘.
For the disk evolution, neglect disk self-gravity, collisions that align the planes, and back-reaction on the planet. In the initial disk plane, use the complex inclination w=IeiΩ for a ring and wp​=Im​eiΩm​ for the planet. Linear Laplace-Lagrange secular theory has forced inclination wp​ and solution w(a,t)=wp​[1−e−iν(a)t] for initially flat rings. For an inner circular perturber, the Laplace coefficient formula is ν=n(Mp​/M⋆​)αb3/2(1)​(α)/4, with α=ap​/a. Its quadrupole approximation uses b3/2(1)​≃3α, yielding
ν(a)≃43​nM⋆​Mp​​(aap​​)2∝a−7/2.
(7)
In the (IcosΩ,IsinΩ) plot referenced to the initial disk, every ring runs clockwise on the same circle centered at wp​, starting at the origin. Inner rings advance more quickly. If the axes are instead referenced to the planet's plane, the relative tilt is w−wp​=−wp​e−iνt: these circles are centered at the origin, and the mutual inclination is constant. Both conventions describe the same nodal precession; they should not be mixed.
At an intermediate epoch, rings with νt≳1 have tilted substantially while outer rings with νt≪1 remain near the original disk plane. This radial variation is a planet-induced debris-disk warp. The characteristic affected radius grows like t2/7. Conservative differential nodal precession preserves each ring's tilt relative to the planet, so it does not by itself align every orbit; an unresolved inner region can acquire a mean plane near the planetary plane through phase mixing. The secular interpretation is also described in Wyatt's planetary dynamics lectures.
