Write Rp​ for the physical planet radius and Rc​=ηRH1​ for the debris-cloud radius; the source uses R1​ for both even though their stated size orderings are opposite. A central transit duration of a small planet on a nearly circular Kepler orbit is Dp​≃2R⋆​/(n1​a1​)=P1​R⋆​/(πa1​). Kepler's third law, with M⋆​=4πρ⋆​R⋆3​/3, gives
Dp3​P1​​≃3π2Gρ⋆​​.
(17)
A noncentral planetary exoplanet transit is shorter, so within the small-radius and nearly circular approximations, a duration requiring
D13​P1​​<3π2Gρ⋆​​​
(18)
is too long for the planet alone and favors extended debris. For a central cloud transit with Rc​≫R⋆​, the path length is approximately 2Rc​. Using the Hill radius RH1​=a1​(M1​/(3M⋆​))1/3 gives D1​/P1​≃Rc​/(πa1​), hence the Hill-cloud transit mass estimate
M⋆​M1​​≃3(ηP1​πD1​​)3.​
(19)
This equality assumes a central cloud chord. An orbit described merely as nearly edge-on need not have central cloud crossings. If its projected cloud impact distance is s, the measured half-chord is Rc2​−s2​, so the boxed mass is multiplied by [1−(s/Rc​)2]−3/2. It is a lower estimate when that geometry is unknown.
For the dynamical calculation use μ2​=M2​/M⋆​, A=GM2​/a2​, and R=Af(α)e1​cosϕ1​. The derivatives in the supplied Lagrange planetary equations hold the other osculating orbital elements fixed:
∂λ1​​R=jAfe1​sinϕ1​,∂a1​​R=a2​A​f′e1​cosϕ1​,∂e1​​R=Afcosϕ1​.

λ˙1​=n1​[1+μ2​g(α)e1​cosϕ1​],g(α)=2α​f(α)−2α2f′(α).​

A consistent version writes λ1​=∫0t​n1​(s)ds+ϵint​ and assigns the displayed correction to ϵ˙int​. Equivalently, the canonical mean-longitude equation for a first-order resonant term follows from the disturbing function directly. The result for g is the intended physical equation under that convention.
Away from resonant-argument libration, neglect changes in e1​,α,ϖ1​ on one slow cycle to this order. With n0​=2π/P1​ the reference mean motion, the signed slow frequency is
ω=(j+1)n2​−jn0​=−1+Δjn0​Δ​,Pt​=∣ω∣2π​=j∣Δ∣P1​(1+Δ)​.

n˙1​=−3jμ2​n02​αfe1​sin(ωt).

n1​(t)=ns​+ωC​[cos(ωt)−1],λ1​(t)=(ns​−ωC​)t+(ω2C​+ωn0​μ2​ge1​​)sin(ωt),

For ∣Δ∣≪1, the double-integrated frequency term dominates the direct g term provided g/f stays finite. Relative to the fitted linear ephemeris, λ1​(tk​)=2πk gives
tk​≃P1​k+At​sin(ωtk​),At​=−ω23jμ2​n0​αfe1​​≃−jn0​Δ23μ2​αfe1​​.

Here P1​ denotes the fitted mean period at the retained order. The signed-frequency convention fixes the phase sign. Replacing ω by 2π/Pt​>0 changes the sign of the signed At​ when needed; a nonnegative amplitude has
Pt​∣At​∣​≃2π3​μ2​∣Δ∣α∣f(α)∣e1​​.​

Using a signed Pt​=2π/ω instead gives the source's signed proportionality At​/Pt​≃3μ2​αfe1​/(2πΔ). The leading implicit transit equation can equally use P1​k inside the sine, since the difference is higher order in the perturbation. This is the eccentricity-enhanced near-resonant transit-timing variation; its derivation assumes small longitude oscillations, μ2​e1​≪Δ2 at fixed resonance coefficients, and the effectively fixed eccentricity used in this truncated model. Dominance over forced-eccentricity timing terms typically also requires e1​≫∣Δ∣ at fixed j. A full transit calculation also includes forced orbital eccentricity and the conversion from mean longitude to the actual sky-crossing angle. Those are lower order in this eccentricity-enhanced regime, rather than identically absent; broader near-resonant formulas are derived by Lithwick, Xie and Wu.
Inside a mean-motion resonance, ϕ1​ undergoes resonant-argument libration and its evolution must be solved together with a1​,e1​,ϖ1​. The constant circulation frequency ω and the associated Δ−2 expansion then fail. At approximately fixed eccentricity, the resonant resonant pendulum approximation has libration frequency of order jn0​μ2​α∣f∣e1​​, up to numerical factors. The transit-timing variation follows that libration period and depends on its libration amplitude of a resonant argument. Exactly at a resonant equilibrium there need be no libration signal; near a separatrix the period grows, and eccentricity dynamics can modify the simple estimate. Resonant amplitudes remain controlled by the bounded resonant motion rather than diverging as the circulating detuning tends to zero.
