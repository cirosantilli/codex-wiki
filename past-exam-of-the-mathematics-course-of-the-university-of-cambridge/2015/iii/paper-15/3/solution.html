Integral groups for M4​. Put r=n−1. The space Mn​ is the inversion mapping torus of a torus, fibred over S1 with fiber Tr. The cohomology ring of a torus is the exterior algebra on r degree-one generators. Inversion acts as −1 on each such generator, hence as (−1)q on Hq(Tr;Z)≅Z(qr​).
The Wang sequence therefore gives
0⟶coker(F∗−1:Hq−1(Tr;Z)→Hq−1(Tr;Z))⟶Hq(Mn​;Z)⟶ker(F∗−1:Hq(Tr;Z)→Hq(Tr;Z))⟶0.
(18)
The right-hand term is free, so this short exact sequence splits as a sequence of groups. In even fiber degree, F∗−1=0; in odd fiber degree it is multiplication by −2. For r=3 this computes the integral cohomology of an inversion mapping torus:
Hq(M4​;Z)≅⎩⎨⎧​Z,Z3⊕(Z/2)3,Z3,Z/2,0,​q=0,1,q=2,q=3,q=4,otherwise.​​
(19)
The top torsion group is consistent with M4​ being nonorientable: inversion of its three-dimensional fiber reverses orientation.
Mod-two groups. Over F2​, inversion acts as the identity on the fiber cohomology. The Wang sequence gives
0→Hq−1(Tr;F2​)→Hq(Mn​;F2​)→Hq(Tr;F2​)→0.
(20)
Thus
dimF2​​Hq(Mn​;F2​)=(qr​)+(q−1r​)=(qn​),​
(21)
with out-of-range binomial coefficients interpreted as zero. These are exactly the dimensions of the graded groups H∗(Tn;F2​).
The intersection pairing for M2​. The mapping torus of reflection of the circle is the Klein bottle. Let a be the section loop through a fixed point of the reflection and b a fiber circle. They form a basis of H1​(M2​;F2​). The section has a normal neighborhood homeomorphic to a Möbius band, so it is one-sided and a transverse displacement meets it once modulo two. The fiber is two-sided and can be displaced disjointly. The two loops meet once. The mod-two intersection pairing of the Klein bottle therefore has matrix
Q=(a⋅ab⋅a​a⋅bb⋅b​)=(11​10​)​.
(22)
Take the evaluation-dual basis t,x∈H1(M2​;F2​), with t(a)=1,t(b)=0 and x(a)=0,x(b)=1. By Poincare duality, the cup product matrix in this dual basis is Q−1, not Q:
Q−1=(01​11​).
(23)
Writing w for the nonzero top class gives t2=0, tx=w and x2=w. Hence the cohomology ring is
H∗(M2​;F2​)≅F2​[t,x]/(t2,x2+tx),∣t∣=∣x∣=1.​
(24)
These relations already force all degrees above two to vanish.
The ring for general n. For each of the r fiber coordinates, projection induces Mn​→M2​. Pull back the classes above, calling the common base class t and the fiber-coordinate classes x1​,…,xr​. Naturality of the cup product gives t2=0 and xi2​=txi​. The square-free products xI​=∏i∈I​xi​ restrict to the exterior algebra basis of the fiber cohomology. The Leray-Hirsch theorem then says that xI​ and txI​ form an additive basis. This proves the full mod-two cohomology ring of an inversion mapping torus:
H∗(Mn​;F2​)≅F2​[t,x1​,…,xn−1​]/(t2, xi2​+txi​ (1≤i≤n−1)).​
(25)
For n≥2, xi2​=txi​ is nonzero by this basis description. In the cohomology ring of a torus every degree-one class squares to zero: the generators square to zero and the cross terms occur twice in characteristic two. The degree-one cup-square obstruction to ring isomorphism therefore proves that the rings are not isomorphic for any n≥2, despite their isomorphic graded groups. For n=1, both spaces are circles and the rings are isomorphic. Even if grading is forgotten, the rings differ for n≥2: every element of the torus ring has square either zero or one, whereas xi2​=txi​ is nonzero and is not the unit.
Integral cup products in M4​. To specify H∗(M4​;Z) also as a ring, let a∈H1 be the pullback of the positive generator of H1(S1;Z). Let ρ denote reduction modulo two and set τi​=β​(xi​) using the integral Bockstein homomorphism. The degree-one Bockstein square identity gives ρτi​=xi2​=txi​, so the τi​ are the three independent order-two classes in degree two. Choose free classes b12​,b13​,b23​∈H2 which restrict to the corresponding two-fold fiber products and have reductions ρbij​=xi​xj​. Such choices exist: reduction in degree two is surjective because H3(M4​;Z) is free, and adding the τi​ removes any txi​ terms from an initial lift.
The Wang sequence identifies ab12​,ab13​,ab23​ as a free basis of H3. Let κ generate H4≅Z/2, with ρκ=tx1​x2​x3​. Reduction in degree four is an isomorphism. The integral cup products in the four-dimensional inversion mapping torus are consequently determined by
​a2=0,aτi​=0,τi​τj​=0,bij2​=0,b12​b13​=b12​b23​=b13​b23​=κ​,bij​τk​={κ,0,​{i,j,k}={1,2,3},otherwise,​​2τi​=2κ=0.​
(26)
Together with the unit, graded commutativity and vanishing above degree four, these give every product. For example, ρ(b12​b13​)=x12​x2​x3​=tx1​x2​x3​, whereas ρ(b122​)=t2x1​x2​=0. The products aτi​ vanish because they are torsion in the free group H3.
