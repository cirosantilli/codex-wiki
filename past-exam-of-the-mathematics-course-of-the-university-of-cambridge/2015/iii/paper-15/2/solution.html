The tensor differential. Use homological grading, so each differential lowers degree by one. The tensor product of chain complexes has
(C⊗R​C′)k​=⨁p+q=k​Cp​⊗R​Cq′​,D(x⊗y)=dC​x⊗y+(−1)px⊗dC′​y
(8)
for x∈Cp​. This Koszul sign rule gives
D2(x⊗y)=dC2​x⊗y+((−1)p−1+(−1)p)dC​x⊗dC′​y+x⊗dC′2​y=0.
(9)
Thus the graded tensor product is a chain complex.
The Hom differential. Write Mj​=∏p​HomR​(Cp​,Cp+j′​). For a degree-j element of this graded Hom complex of chain complexes, use the prescribed differential
dM​f=fdC​+(−1)j−1dC′​f.
(10)
The next application uses degree j−1, so
dM2​f=fdC2​+((−1)j−1+(−1)j−2)dC′​fdC​+(−1)2j−3dC′2​f=0.
(11)
In degree zero, dM​f=fdC​−dC′​f, so its kernel consists exactly of chain maps. A degree-one element h has dM​h=hdC​+dC′​h, which is exactly the change between two chain maps related by a chain homotopy. Consequently
H0​(M(C,C′))≅{chain maps C→C′}/chain homotopy.​
(12)
This is natural: precomposition and postcomposition by chain maps preserve both degree-zero cycles and degree-zero boundaries.
The dual complex and the sign adjustment. Define the reversed dual chain complex
Xi​=HomR​(C−i​,R),dX​ϕ=ϕdC​.
(13)
Here dX​ϕ is a functional on C1−i​, so it belongs to Xi−1​, and dX2​=0. The absence of an additional sign in dX​ is intentional.
Interpret finite generation of the free chain complexes as finiteness of their total graded free modules. Then only finitely many degrees occur, and the finite-free evaluation isomorphisms assemble into a graded isomorphism
ψ:X⊗R​C′⟶M(C,C′),ψ(ϕ⊗y)(c)=ϕ(c)y,
(14)
where ϕ∈Xi​, y∈Cj′​, and this component is zero outside C−i​. The dual module construction and evaluation make ψ natural. Finite rank is needed for evaluation to be an isomorphism; finite total support also makes the sums on the tensor side agree with the products on the Hom functor side.
Under ψ, the Hom functor differential has the form
ψ−1dM​ψ(ϕ⊗y)=dX​ϕ⊗y+(−1)i+j−1ϕ⊗dC′​y.
(15)
The usual tensor product of chain complexes instead has second coefficient (−1)i. Set
ρ(j)=2j(j−1)​,A∣Xi​⊗Cj′​​=(−1)ρ(j)id.​
(16)
This is integer-valued for every j∈Z, including negative j, and satisfies ρ(j)−ρ(j−1)=j−1. Conjugating the usual tensor differential by A leaves the first term unchanged and changes its second coefficient to (−1)i+j−1. Hence dM​=ψADA−1ψ−1, giving the sign conjugation for the tensor-Hom identification
Φ=ψA:X⊗R​C′≅​M(C,C′),Φ(ϕ⊗yj​)(c)=(−1)j(j−1)/2ϕ(c)yj​.​
(17)
This is an isomorphism of chain complexes, not merely of their homology. If one instead assumes only degreewise finite rank with unbounded grading, the ordinary tensor need not identify with the product defining M; the finite-total convention is essential to this last conclusion.
