Past exam of the mathematics course of the University of Cambridge / 2015 / ii / Paper 2 / 13I / b / iv / Solution

 ... 2015 ii Paper 2 13I b iv
OurBigBook.com Words: 88
For every countable ordinal β, ωβ is countable: successor stages use a countable product, and countable limit stages a countable union. Hence ωω1​=supβ<ω1​​ωβ≤ω1​. Conversely ωβ≥β by transfinite induction, so these values are cofinal in ω1​. Therefore ωω1​=ω1​.
It is not the least fixed point. Set a0​=1, an+1​=ωan​ and define epsilon zero as ε0​=supn​an​. This is countable, and continuity of ordinal exponentiation at limits gives ωε0​=supn​ωan​=ε0​. Any fixed point of α↦ωα dominates every an​ by induction, hence dominates ε0​. Thus the least fixed point is ε0​<ω1​.
