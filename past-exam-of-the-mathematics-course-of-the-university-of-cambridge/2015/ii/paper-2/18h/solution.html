A covering map is a continuous surjection p:X→X for which each point has an open neighbourhood U with p−1(U) a disjoint union of open sets, each mapped homeomorphically onto U. A connected simply connected covering space is a universal cover.
There is a necessary connectedness qualification in the printed conclusion. Let A0​ be the path component of a0​ in A. Then p(A)=A0​: one inclusion follows by projecting paths, and the other by lifting a path in A0​ from a0​. Thus the asserted surjection onto all of A requires A to be path connected. Without it, take p to be the identity of R and A to be two disjoint intervals; a selected component cannot cover both.
To prove the assertion about a component of a pulled-back universal cover, choose an evenly covered neighbourhood U in X. Local path connectedness of A supplies a path-connected relatively open neighbourhood V⊆U∩A0​. Each inverse sheet over V is path connected, so it lies wholly in A or is disjoint from it. These sheets evenly cover V under p∣A​. Therefore p∣A​:A→A0​ is a covering map.
Under the classification of connected covering spaces, its subgroup consists of based loops in A0​ whose lifts close at a0​. A loop viewed in X closes in the universal cover exactly when its class in π1​(X,a0​) is trivial. Hence the subgroup is
p∗​π1​(A,a0​)=ker{π1​(A,a0​)i∗​​π1​(X,a0​)}.​
(60)
Here π1​(A,a0​)=π1​(A0​,a0​), so the subgroup formula itself needs no change. Injectivity of the covering's induced fundamental-group map is the usual homotopy-lifting property.
