Writing yi​ for the observed counts, independence of the Poisson distributions gives the log-likelihood
ℓ(β)=∑i=1n​{yi​βxi​−eβxi​−log(yi​!)}.
(11)
Its score function and negative second derivative are
U(β)=∑i​xi​(yi​−eβxi​),J(β)=∑i​xi2​eβxi​.
(12)
Unless all xi​ vanish, J(β)>0, so the log-likelihood is strictly concave. Use Newton method, starting from a finite β0​, with
βr+1​=βr​+∑i​xi2​eβr​xi​∑i​xi​(yi​−eβr​xi​)​.​
(13)
Stop when the score function or the change in β is small; a reduced step can ensure that the log-likelihood increases. Any finite zero of the score function is the unique maximum-likelihood estimator.
Existence needs a qualification. If the nonzero xi​ include both signs, U decreases from +∞ to −∞, so a finite root exists. If all nonzero xi​ are positive, a finite root exists exactly when ∑i​xi​yi​>0; otherwise the supremum is approached as β→−∞. The corresponding condition for all negative xi​ is ∑i​xi​yi​<0, with a supremum at +∞ otherwise. When all xi​=0, the parameter is unidentifiable. These cases explain when the iterative algorithm actually has a finite estimate to find.
