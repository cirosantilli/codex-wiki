For h=0, enlarge the nonnegative integral I(h) to all of R, and substitute y=x+∣h∣t. Reflection if necessary gives
I(h)≤∣h∣1−2αCα​,Cα​=∫R​(∣t−1∣−α−∣t∣−α)2dt.
(72)
Near 0 and 1, its integrand is bounded by a constant times 1+∣t∣−2α or 1+∣t−1∣−2α, which is integrable. For large ∣t∣, the mean value theorem bounds the difference by a constant times ∣t∣−α−1, whose square is integrable. Thus Cα​<∞. The preceding estimate yields
∣g(x+h)−g(x)∣≤∥f∥2​Cα​​∣h∣1/2−α.​
(73)
In particular g is continuous on all of R; the proof establishes Hölder continuity of a one-dimensional fractional kernel integral of exponent 1/2−α uniformly in x.
