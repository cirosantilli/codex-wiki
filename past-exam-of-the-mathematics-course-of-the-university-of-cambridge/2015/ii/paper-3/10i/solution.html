Work in the complete Banach space C([0,1]) with the uniform norm. Each Em​ is closed. Indeed if fj​→f uniformly and xj​ witnesses membership in Em​, pass to a subsequence with xj​→x. For each y∈[0,1], uniform convergence and continuity give
∣f(y)−f(x)∣=limj​∣fj​(y)−fj​(xj​)∣≤m∣y−x∣α.
(31)
Thus x witnesses membership for f.
Each Em​ has empty interior. Approximate an arbitrary f uniformly by a piecewise linear function q on a uniform mesh, and let D bound the absolute slopes of q. On a sufficiently fine uniform refinement of mesh δ, add a triangular oscillation of fixed small amplitude b, alternating between b and −b at successive vertices. The resulting function g is still as close to f as desired, and every segment has absolute slope at least 2b/δ−D. For any x, choose the farther endpoint of a segment containing x. Its distance h is between δ/2 and δ, so
∣h∣α∣g(x+h)−g(x)∣​≥(2b/δ−D)δ1−αmin(1,2α−1)>m
(32)
when δ is small. Therefore g∈/Em​.
By the Baire category theorem, the complement of ⋃m​Em​ is dense and nonempty. If the local limsup in the question were finite at some x, its nearby differences would satisfy a finite bound. The remaining differences, with ∣h∣≥δ>0, are bounded by 2∥f∥∞​δ−α. Combining the bounds would put f in some Em​, a contradiction. Hence the required limsup is infinite at every point, including one-sided approaches at the endpoints.
