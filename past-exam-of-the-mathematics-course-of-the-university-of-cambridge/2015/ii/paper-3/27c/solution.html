Use the entire function eikz3. The steepest descent contour starting at 1 is
z(u)=(1+iu)1/3,0≤u<∞,​
(88)
with the cube-root branch starting at 1. Along it ikz3=ik−ku, so the real part decreases and the imaginary part stays constant. It approaches the ray argz=π/6, on which z=eiπ/6u1/3 and ikz3=−ku.
Close a contour using the interval [0,1], these two outgoing paths truncated at parameter R, and a short connecting arc. The two endpoints differ by O(R−2/3), and the integrand on the connector decays exponentially in R. Cauchy integral theorem therefore gives ∫01​eikt3dt=I1​−I2​.
The specified substitutions give
I1​=3eiπ/6​∫0∞​e−kuu−2/3du=3k1/3eiπ/6Γ(1/3)​,
(89)
and
I2​=3ieik​∫0∞​e−ku(1+iu)−2/3du.
(90)
Expand (1+iu)−2/3=1−2iu/3+O(u2) near zero, and apply Watson lemma, or split the integral at a fixed small u and integrate the Taylor remainder. This gives the first two asymptotic terms
∫01​eikt3dt=3k1/3eiπ/6Γ(1/3)​−3kieik​+O(k−2).​
(91)
Keeping the next term gives −2eik/(9k2)+O(k−3). The fractional power comes from the degenerate stationary endpoint at zero; the oscillatory integer-power terms come from the ordinary endpoint at one.
