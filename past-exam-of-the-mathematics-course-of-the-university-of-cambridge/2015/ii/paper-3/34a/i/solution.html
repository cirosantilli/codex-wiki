Vary the four-potential with compactly supported variation. Antisymmetry of the electromagnetic field tensor gives δ(Fμν​Fμν)=4Fμν∂μ​δAν​. Integration by parts therefore gives
δS=μ0​c1​∫[∂μ​Fμν−λ2Aν+μ0​Jν]δAν​d4x.
(121)
Thus the Euler-Lagrange field equation is ∂μ​Fμν−λ2Aν=−μ0​Jν​.
A gauge transformation is Aμ​↦Aμ​+∂μ​χ. Commuting derivatives leave Fμν​ unchanged. At λ=0, the source term changes by c−1∫∂μ​χJμ, a boundary term because the current is conserved. With suitable boundary conditions the action is gauge invariant. For λ>0, the mass term changes by terms involving 2Aμ∂μ​χ+(∂χ)2, so the same arbitrary gauge transformations are not a symmetry.
Take the divergence of the field equation. Antisymmetry gives ∂ν​∂μ​Fμν=0, and conservation gives ∂ν​Jν=0. Thus λ2∂ν​Aν=0. For λ>0 this is the Lorenz constraint in Proca theory, and expanding F gives
(□−λ2)Aν=−μ0​Jν.​
(122)
For λ=0 the same reduced equation holds when the Lorenz gauge ∂μ​Aμ=0 is imposed. More generally it is enough that the divergence be spacetime constant, but the conventional vanishing boundary conditions set that constant to zero.
