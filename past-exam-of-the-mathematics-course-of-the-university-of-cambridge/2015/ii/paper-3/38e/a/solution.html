OurBigBook.com Words: 154
Use u^n(θ)=∑m​umn​e−imθ, initially for finite-support sequences and then by Plancherel theorem for ℓ2. A shift by k multiplies the transform by eikθ. Hence the recurrence gives
A(θ)u^n+1=B(θ)u^n,u^n+1=H(θ)u^n=H(θ)n+1u^0.​
(140)
Here the last equality is understood with time-zero data, and the scheme is assumed well-defined, so the denominator is nonzero or any removable singularity is filled continuously.
If ∣H∣≤1, Plancherel gives ∥un∥2​≤∥u0∥2​ for every n. Conversely, if the continuous amplification factor has ∣H(θ0​)∣>1, it exceeds 1+δ on an interval of positive length. Choose a nonzero initial Fourier transform supported in that interval. Then ∥un∥2​≥(1+δ)n∥u0∥2​, contradicting a uniform stability bound. Therefore uniform power stability holds exactly when
∣H(θ)∣≤1(−π≤θ≤π).​
(141)
Without continuity the exact condition is an essential-supremum bound, since isolated Fourier frequencies have zero measure. The fixed recurrence here uses a bound uniform over all time steps; mesh-dependent finite-time growth bounds are a different stability convention.
