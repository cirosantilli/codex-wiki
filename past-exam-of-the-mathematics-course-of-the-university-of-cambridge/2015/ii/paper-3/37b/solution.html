For the local phase S, define ki​=∂i​S and ω=−∂t​S. The dispersion relation is the Hamilton-Jacobi equation St​+Ω(∇S,x,t)=0. Differentiate it in space and follow a ray with group velocity x˙i​=Ωki​​; symmetry of the phase Hessian cancels the terms involving ∂i​kj​. Thus the ray-tracing equations are
dtdxi​​=Ωki​​,dtdki​​=−Ωxi​​,dtdω​=Ωt​.​
(135)
Here d/dt=∂t​+x˙⋅∇ means differentiation along the moving ray; subscripts on Ω mean partial derivatives holding its other independent arguments fixed.
The depth is stationary and independent of y, so ω=ω∞​ and l are constant. Choose the incident direction with positive l; the other sign reflects the picture. In the far deep water,
κ∞​=ω∞2​/g,l=κ∞​sinθ∞​,k∞​=−κ∞​cosθ∞​.
(136)
Since Ω depends isotropically on the horizontal wavevector, the group-velocity ratio is l/k. Therefore the ray equation is
dxdy​=kl​,k=−κ(x)2−l2​,ω∞2​=gκtanh(καxp).​
(137)

q=1+2p​,A=−ω∞​(1+p/2)lgα​​=−1+p/2ω∞​α/g​sinθ∞​​.​
(138)
For p<2, the near-shore phase is S∼ly−ω∞​t−ω∞​x1−p/2/[gα​(1−p/2)]. A constant-phase wave crest thus has
y−yc​∼lgα​(1−p/2)ω∞​​x1−p/2.
(139)
Its tangent becomes parallel to the shoreline, while rays approach perpendicular to it. The exponent is between zero and one, giving the curved crests sketched below for p=1.
