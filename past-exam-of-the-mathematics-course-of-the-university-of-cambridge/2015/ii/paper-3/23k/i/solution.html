In an interval of length t, condition on the Poisson process count Xt​=k. Independently marking its points gives Yt​∣Xt​=k∼Bin(k,p). The joint probability generating function of kept and discarded counts is
E[sYt​uXt​−Yt​]=E[(ps+(1−p)u)Xt​]=exp{λt[p(s−1)+(1−p)(u−1)]}.
(74)
It factors into the generating functions of independent Poisson variables of means λpt and λ(1−p)t. The same calculation applies to any interval, and counts and marks in disjoint intervals are independent. Hence Y is a Poisson process of intensity λp​, and Yt​ and Xt​−Yt​ are independent. This is the Poisson thinning theorem.
