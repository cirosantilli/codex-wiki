If f is an irreducible polynomial, any two roots α,β have the same minimal polynomial. Thus the map Q(α)→Q(β) carrying α to β is a field embedding. The extension theorem for field embeddings extends it to an embedding of the splitting field L into an algebraic closure. Since L/Q is normal, its image is L, so it is an element of the Galois group.
Conversely, factor f into irreducible factors over Q. An automorphism preserves the vanishing of each factor, so it cannot send a root of one factor to a root of another. Since there are no repeated roots, different factors have disjoint root sets. Transitivity on all roots therefore forces a single irreducible factor. This proves the equivalence.
