The Axiom of foundation states that every nonempty set A has a member a with a∩A=∅. The principle of epsilon induction states that a formula ϕ satisfying ∀x[(∀y∈xϕ(y))⇒ϕ(x)] holds for every set x.
Assume Axiom of foundation and suppose a progressive formula has a counterexample a. In the transitive closure of {a}, use axiom schema of separation to collect the counterexamples into a nonempty set B. Foundation supplies b∈B having no member in B. Since the closure is transitive, every member of b satisfies ϕ, contradicting progressiveness. Conversely, if a nonempty set A had no foundation-minimal member, every x∈A would have a member y∈x∩A. The formula x∈/A is then progressive: if every member of x lies outside A, x cannot belong to A. Epsilon induction would make A empty, a contradiction. This proves equivalence in the presence of the other Zermelo–Fraenkel set theory.
To obtain the cumulative hierarchy, perform epsilon induction on the assertion that x belongs to some Vα​. If this holds for each member y, Axiom schema of replacement collects their least such ordinals into a set. Choose an ordinal β above them all. Then x⊆Vβ​ and x∈Vβ+1​. Thus every set occurs in the hierarchy. Use the rank of a set convention rk(x)=supy∈x​(rk(y)+1), equivalently the least α with x⊆Vα​.
