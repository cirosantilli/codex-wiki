Applying the previous projection argument to the centered design proves the bound with Xj​−Xˉj​1n​ and Xk​−Xˉk​1n​; the normalized inner product is now the usual sample correlation.
Each reported coefficient test is the two-sided Student's t-test of H0​:βj​=0 against H1​:βj​=0, conditional on the other predictors. Under the null, β​j​/(σ(XTX)jj−1​​) has a Student's t-distribution with 100−3=97 degrees of freedom. At five percent the intercept is significant, but neither individual test slope is. The final F-test compares the full model against an intercept-only model: H0​:βMaths​=βStats​=0. It has (2,97) degrees of freedom and rejects at five percent, since its p-value is 0.003166. Thus the predictors jointly explain variation, without strong evidence separating their individual conditional effects.
The two test predictors have sample correlation 0.9371630, although each has only moderate correlation with the final exam response. The variance inflation factor is 1/(1−0.93716302)≃8.22. Their nearly shared direction produces substantial multicollinearity, inflating the individual slope standard errors. The joint F-test can detect this common predictive direction even though either coefficient, given the other, has a large p-value.
