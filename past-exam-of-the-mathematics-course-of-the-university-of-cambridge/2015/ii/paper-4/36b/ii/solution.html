The release produces a rarefaction wave expanding into the dry region. Every incoming C−​ characteristic connects to the undisturbed pool, where J−​=0, so throughout the simple wave u=2(c−c0​). The outgoing C+​ rays emanate from (0,0) and have x/t=u+c=3c−2c0​. Solving gives
c=31​(x/t+2c0​),u=32​(x/t−c0​),−2c0​t≤x≤c0​t.​
(89)
The depth is h=c2/g. At the right edge these formulas match the stationary pool; at the left edge c=0 and the fluid front travels at −2c0​. Beyond it the bed is dry, so a fluid velocity need not be assigned. Values at t=0 are understood as one-sided limits of the fan.
The first wall interaction occurs at t0​=a/c0​. The leftmost reflected signal follows a C−​ ray through (a,t0​). Until a point has encountered this signal it remains in the original fan. In that fan its speed is u−c=x/(3t)−4c0​/3. Thus
x˙−3tx​=−34c0​​,x(t)=Ct1/3−2c0​t.
(90)
Matching at (a,t0​) gives the reflected front
xf​(t)=3a(c0​t/a)1/3−2c0​t.​
(91)
Consequently the same rarefaction formula remains valid for −2c0​t≤x≤xf​(t) when t≥t0​. Between xf​(t) and the wall, information from the wall changes J−​ and the simple-wave relation no longer holds. In particular continuing the old formula to x=a would give a negative velocity for t>t0​, violating the no-penetration condition u(a,t)=0. The reflected region must accommodate both characteristic families; it is not another untouched part of the original fan.
