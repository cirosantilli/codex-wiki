To recover the printed dispersion relation, interpret the surface maintained flat as an imposed flat upper boundary for the perturbation, so its normal velocity is zero. This is the rigid-lid approximation. Work with perturbation velocity potentials ϕ−​=Aekyeikx+σt below the interface and ϕ+​=Bcosh[k(y−h)]eikx+σt in the upper layer. They solve the Laplace equation, decay as y→−∞, and obey ∂y​ϕ+​(h)=0.
The linear kinematic boundary conditions at the interface give (σ+ikU)η0​=kA and ση0​=−kBsinh(kh). Equality of pressures, using the linear Bernoulli equation, gives (σ+ikU)A=σBcosh(kh). The equal fluid densities cancel the interface gravitational restoring term. Eliminating A,B,η0​ yields
(σ+ikU)2+σ2coth(kh)=0.​
(85)
With q=tanh(kh), the roots are
σ=1+qUk​(−iq±q​),Reσ=±1+tanh(kh)Uktanh(kh)​​.​
(86)
For every k>0 one root has positive real part. Thus the vortex sheet has a Kelvin-Helmholtz instability at every wavelength in this model. If kh≫1, q→1 and the growing rate is Uk/2. If kh≪1, q∼kh and it is Ukkh​.
There is a physical qualification to the wording. A dynamically deformable free surface at finite gravity is not exactly a flat lid. Its linear conditions combine to σ2ϕ+​(h)+g∂y​ϕ+​(h)=0. Writing the upper potential as Bcosh(ky)+Csinh(ky) and using the same interface conditions instead gives
(σ+ikU)2[σ2+gktanh(kh)]+σ2[σ2tanh(kh)+gk]=0.
(87)
The stated quadratic follows in the strong-gravity flat-surface limit, or with the flat condition imposed exactly. It is not the exact dispersion relation of a freely moving upper surface at arbitrary finite g. This makes explicit the boundary interpretation needed for the requested formula.
