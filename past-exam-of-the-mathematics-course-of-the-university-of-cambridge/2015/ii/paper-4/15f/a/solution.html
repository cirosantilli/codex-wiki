A one-dimensional continuous group representation is a continuous group homomorphism ρ:S1→C×. Its image is compact. If some ∣ρ(z)∣ differed from one, its positive or negative powers would be unbounded, so the image lies in S1. Lift t↦ρ(eit) to a continuous real argument a(t) with a(0)=0. Then a(s+t)−a(s)−a(t) is a continuous integer multiple of 2π and vanishes at (0,0), hence is always zero. The continuous Cauchy functional equation gives a(t)=ct. Periodicity forces e2πic=1, so c=n∈Z. Consequently ρ(z)=zn​; conversely every such map is a continuous one-dimensional group representation.
