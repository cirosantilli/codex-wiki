Put x=1+y and retain linear terms. The delay differential equation is y˙​(t)=−αy(t−T), with characteristic equation of a delay differential equation λ+αe−λT=0. Write z=λT and q=αT. If a root has Rez≥0, then ∣z∣=qe−Rez≤q. Writing z=a+ib gives a=−qe−acosb. For q<π/2, ∣b∣<π/2 and the right side is negative, contradicting a≥0. All characteristic roots therefore lie in the left half-plane.
An imaginary root z=ib requires cosb=0 and b=qsinb. The first crossing is q=π/2, z=±iπ/2. Implicit differentiation gives dz/dq=−e−z/(1+z), whose real part at the positive crossing is (π/2)/(1+π2/4)>0. The later imaginary crossings occur at q=π/2+2πk and also enter the right half-plane. Thus the equilibrium is locally exponentially asymptotically stable when
αT<π/2,​
(4)
and unstable when αT>π/2. At equality the linearized equation has neutral oscillatory modes; it is the Hopf bifurcation threshold. If stability is meant for the nonlinear equation itself, the borderline can also be resolved. In dimensionless time s=t/T, let q0​=π/2. A small center oscillation has the form y=Rcosθ+R2(cos2θ/5+sin2θ/10)+O(R3). Substitution at quadratic order gives these second-harmonic coefficients. At cubic order the fundamental forcing is q0​R3(cosθ/20+3sinθ/20). Projection onto the center mode divides the complex forcing by the characteristic derivative 1+iq0​, yielding the Hopf normal form amplitude equation
dsdR​=−20(1+q02​)q0​(3q0​−1)​R3+O(R5).
(5)
Its cubic coefficient is negative, so the equilibrium at equality is locally asymptotically stable, with algebraic rather than exponential decay. Thus the usual strictly decaying linear condition is αT<π/2, while nonlinear local asymptotic stability includes αT=π/2. The purely imaginary linear modes alone would not justify that borderline conclusion.
