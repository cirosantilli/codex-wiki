The intermediate value theorem says that a continuous f:[a,b]→R takes every value between f(a) and f(b). For f(a)<y<f(b), the closed sets where f≤y and f≥y cannot separate the connected interval; equivalently, taking the supremum of {x:f(x)≤y} and using continuity gives a point with value y.
Set ϕ(a)=0 and ϕ(x)=sin(1/(x−a)) for x>a. Every interval [a,b] contains a zero c<b; continuity on [c,b] makes ϕ take every value between 0=ϕ(a) and ϕ(b), yet ϕ is discontinuous at a.
A monotone function can be discontinuous only by a jump. If it had a jump at c, any number strictly between the left and right limits would lie between f(a) and f(b) but would not be attained, contrary to the hypothesis. Thus it is continuous.
For the last assertion, pass to subsequences with xn​,yn​ both within 1/n of a and g(xn​) near l, g(yn​) near L. For λ∈(l,L), the intermediate value theorem on the interval joining xn​ and yn​ gives zn​ with g(zn​)=λ; then zn​→a. The endpoint cases use the original sequences.
