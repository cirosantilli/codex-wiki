For prime p, the nonzero residues pair with their distinct inverses except for 1 and −1, so Wilson theorem gives
(p−1)!≡−1(modp).
(25)
If composite n>4 has a factorization n=ab with 1<a<b<n, both factors occur in (n−1)!. If n=m2, the distinct factors m and 2m occur and their product is divisible by n; the excluded case m=2 is exactly n=4. Thus (n−1)!≡0(modn).
The Fermat-Euler theorem states aϕ(n)≡1(modn) when (a,n)=1. For prime p, ϕ(p)=p−1, giving Fermat's little theorem for p∤a; the form ap≡a(modp) also covers p∣a.

If An​ are countable, choose enumerations and map each element of ⋃n​An​ to the first pair (n,k) at which it occurs. Since N2 is countable, the union is countable.
A periodic function of period k is determined by its k values on a complete residue system. Thus all periodic functions form a countable union over k of the countable sets Nk, and are countable.
The set of bijections N→N is uncountable. Indeed, each binary sequence determines a bijection that swaps 2j−1 and 2j exactly when its jth bit is one. This is an injection from the uncountable set of binary sequences.
