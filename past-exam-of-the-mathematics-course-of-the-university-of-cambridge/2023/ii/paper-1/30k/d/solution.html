Condition on the information available before time n. The current wealth is then fixed, while ξn​∼N(b,Σ) is independent of that information. Put
B=bTΣ−1b>0.
(319)
For any portfolio θ, use the inner product induced by the positive-definite matrix Σ to write
θ=λΣ−1b+η,λ=BθTb​,ηTb=0.
(320)
The linear image of a multivariate normal vector shows that the two portfolio returns are jointly normal, and
cov((λΣ−1b)Tξn​,ηTξn​)=ληTΣΣ−1b=ληTb=0.
(321)
Thus independence of uncorrelated jointly normal variables makes the residual return ηTξn​ independent of the aligned return; it also has expected value zero.
The continuation value V(n,⋅) is concave by part (c). Conditional Jensen inequality therefore shows that adding the independent centered residual cannot improve the objective:
E[V(n,m+(λΣ−1b)Tξn​+ηTξn​)]≤E[V(n,m+(λΣ−1b)Tξn​)],
(322)
where m=(1+r)Xn−1​. Since the optimal portfolio is unique, its residual must be zero.
It remains to determine the sign. If λ<0, the portfolios λΣ−1b and −λΣ−1b have the same return variance, namely λ2B, while their means are λB and −λB. The latter return has the distribution of the former plus the positive constant −2λB. Since V(n,⋅) is increasing, replacing λ by −λ cannot reduce the objective, contradicting uniqueness. Hence λ≥0.
Applying this conditional argument at every time gives nonnegative, past-measurable random variables λn​ such that
θn∗​=λn​Σ−1b​,1≤n≤N.
(323)
This is the Gaussian one-fund theorem.
