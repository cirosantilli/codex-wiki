Expand L by a unary function symbol f. Let T∗ contain:
  • every sentence of T;
  • the first-order axiomatization of an automorphism saying that f is bijective and preserves every function and relation of L;
  • the sentence ∃x[f(x)=x];
  • for each n≥1, a sentence θn​ asserting the existence of at least n distinct elements.
Every finite subset S⊆T∗ contains only finitely many size requirements, say no requirement beyond θN​. By hypothesis there is a finite nonrigid model A⊨T with at least N elements. Interpret f as a nonidentity automorphism of A. This expanded structure satisfies S; indeed it satisfies every automorphism axiom, including any finitely many that occur in S.
Thus every finite subset of T∗ is satisfiable. By the compactness theorem, T∗ has a model M∗. The sentences θn​ make its domain infinite, while ∃x[f(x)=x] and the automorphism axioms make fM∗ a nonidentity automorphism. The L-reduct M is therefore an infinite nonrigid model of T. This is the compactness transfer of finite nonrigidity.
