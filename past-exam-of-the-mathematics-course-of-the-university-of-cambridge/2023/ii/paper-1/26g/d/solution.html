The induced metric makes the compact surface S a compact Riemannian manifold. By the Hopf-Rinow theorem it is geodesically complete, so every expp​ is defined on all of Tp​S. In particular, expp​(V(p)) is defined for every p.
The global exponential map
Exp:TS→S,(p,v)↦expp​(v),
(271)
is smooth by smooth dependence of geodesics on initial data. Since p↦(p,V(p)) is a smooth section of TS, their composition
ϕ(p)=Exp(p,V(p))
(272)
is smooth.
For 0≤t≤1, define
ϕt​(p)=expp​(tV(p)).
(273)
Completeness makes this a well-defined smooth homotopy, with ϕ0​=idS​ and ϕ1​=ϕ. By homotopy invariance of degree modulo two,
deg2​(ϕ)=deg2​(idS​)=1.
(274)
This is the exponential displacement map on a compact surface.
