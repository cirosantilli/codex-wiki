Dynkin lemma, also called the pi-lambda theorem, states that if a Dynkin system D contains a pi-system P, then
σ(P)⊆D.
(275)
Let λ(P) be the smallest Dynkin system containing P. It is enough to prove that λ(P) is a sigma-algebra, because then
σ(P)⊆λ(P)⊆D.
(276)
First fix A∈P and define
GA​={B∈λ(P):A∩B∈λ(P)}.
(277)
This is a Dynkin system. It contains Ω because A∈λ(P); closure under relative complements follows from
A∩Bc=A∖(A∩B),
(278)
and closure under disjoint unions follows by distributing A∩− over the union. Since P is closed under intersections, P⊆GA​. Minimality therefore gives
λ(P)⊆GA​.
(279)
Thus A∩B∈λ(P) whenever A∈P and B∈λ(P).
Now fix B∈λ(P) and define
HB​={A∈λ(P):A∩B∈λ(P)}.
(280)
The same argument shows that HB​ is a Dynkin system, and the preceding paragraph shows that it contains P. Hence HB​=λ(P). We have proved that λ(P) is itself a pi-system.
A Dynkin system that is also a pi-system is a sigma-algebra: it is closed under arbitrary finite intersections, hence finite unions by complements, and any countable union can be disjointified before using closure under disjoint unions. Therefore λ(P)=σ(P), proving the lemma.
