A germ of a holomorphic function at z∈D is an equivalence class [f]z​ of pairs consisting of a neighbourhood U of z and a holomorphic function f:U→C. Two pairs represent the same germ if their functions agree on some neighbourhood of z.
The space of germs of holomorphic functions is
G={[f]z​:z∈D}.
(242)
For every holomorphic f on an open U⊆D, the set
Uf​={[f]z​:z∈U}
(243)
is declared open; these sets form a basis. The forgetful map
π:G→D,π([f]z​)=z,
(244)
maps each Uf​ homeomorphically onto U. The inverses of these restrictions give the charts defining the complex structure, so π is a local biholomorphism.
The evaluation map is
E:G→C,E([f]z​)=f(z).
(245)
It is well-defined by the germ equivalence relation. On the chart Uf​, its coordinate expression is
E∘(π∣Uf​​)−1(z)=f(z),
(246)
which is holomorphic. Hence E is analytic, as stated in evaluation map on a space of germs.
Now put
D=C∖{ζ:ζ8=1},R={(z,w)∈D×C:w2=z8−1}.
(247)
This gives the germ surface of the square root of z to the eighth minus one. An explicit gluing description is obtained by pairing the eight roots into four adjacent pairs and cutting the plane along four disjoint arcs joining the members of each pair. On the complement of the cuts choose one branch q(z) of z8−1​. Take two copies, labelled by q and −q, and glue the upper bank of each cut in one copy to the lower bank in the other, and conversely. The cut interiors are restored by the gluing, while the eight endpoints remain absent because they are not in D.
On this surface define
π(z,w)=z,E(z,w)=w.
(248)
For each (z,w)∈R, the holomorphic function z8−1 is nonzero near z, so it has a unique local square root q with q(z)=w. Define
Φ:R⟶G,Φ(z,w)=[q]z​.
(249)
This is well-defined, injective, and analytic in the displayed local sheets; its inverse on its image is [q]z​↦(z,q(z)). Thus Φ is the required analytic embedding, and it intertwines both the forgetful and evaluation maps.
To compactify, first add one point above each of the eight roots of unity. These are simple branch points of the double cover. Since the polynomial has even degree eight, the even-degree hyperelliptic model has two distinct, unbranched points above infinity. Consequently
∣R∖R∣=8+2=10.
(250)
Finally apply the Riemann-Hurwitz formula to the degree-two meromorphic map πˉ:R→C∞​. Its only ramification consists of the eight simple finite branch points, so
2g(R)−2=2(2g(C∞​)−2)+8=2(−2)+8=4.
(251)
Therefore
g(R)=3.​
(252)
This is the compactification of y squared equals x to the eighth minus one.
