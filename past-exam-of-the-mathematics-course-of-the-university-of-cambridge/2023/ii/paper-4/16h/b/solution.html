Under the axiom of choice, identify every infinite cardinal κ with its initial ordinal. We prove κ2=κ by transfinite induction on infinite cardinals. The countable case follows from the usual diagonal enumeration of N×N.
For the induction step, well-order the Cartesian product κ×κ by first comparing
max{α,β}
(150)
and then using lexicographic order among pairs with the same maximum. The predecessors of (α,β) lie in (η+1)×(η+1), where η=max{α,β}<κ. Put μ=∣η+1∣<κ. If μ is infinite, the induction hypothesis makes this predecessor set have cardinal at most μ2=μ<κ; if μ is finite, the same conclusion is immediate.
Let θ be the order type of this well-order. Every proper initial segment of θ therefore has cardinal less than κ. Hence θ<κ+, since otherwise its initial segment of order type κ would have cardinal κ. Thus ∣κ×κ∣=∣θ∣≤κ. The map α↦(α,0) gives the reverse injection, and the Cantor-Schröder-Bernstein theorem yields
κ2=κ.
