If a monic polynomial f has roots α1​,…,αn​ in a splitting field, its polynomial discriminant is
disc(f)=∏1≤i<j≤n​(αi​−αj​)2.
(168)
It is a symmetric polynomial in the roots, so it belongs to the base field; it is nonzero exactly when f is a separable polynomial.
Put
D=∏i<j​(αi​−αj​),
(169)
the Vandermonde determinant. A root permutation σ sends D to sgn(σ)D. Hence the Galois group of a polynomial is contained in the alternating group An​ exactly when every Galois automorphism fixes D, which by the fixed-field property is equivalent to D∈K. This implies that disc(f)=D2 is a square in K. Conversely, if disc(f)=d2 for d∈K, then D2=d2, so D=±d∈K. The Galois group therefore fixes D and consists of even permutations. This proves the discriminant criterion for an alternating Galois group.
Now let
f(T)=T3−2T+2.
(170)
The rational root theorem shows that none of ±1,±2 is a root, so this cubic is irreducible over Q. The discriminant of a depressed cubic is
disc(f)=−4(−2)3−27(2)2=32−108=−76=−4⋅19.
(171)
This is not a square in Q, so the Galois group of an irreducible cubic is S3​ over Q.
Over K=Q(−19​), the discriminant is the square
−76=(2−19​)2.
(172)
The cubic remains irreducible: a root in the degree-two extension K/Q would generate over Q both a degree-three field, by irreducibility, and a subfield of a degree-two field, contradicting the tower law for field extensions. Its Galois group over K is consequently A3​≅C3​.
