The Papperitz symbol

(41)
specifies a second-order Fuchsian differential equation. The first row lists its three distinct regular singular points 0,1,∞; z is the independent variable. The two entries below each singular point are its characteristic exponents. Thus local solutions have leading behaviors
1, z1−c(z→0),
(42)
1, (1−z)c−a−b(z→1),
(43)
and, with the usual convention at infinity,
z−a, z−b(z→∞).
(44)
When an exponent difference is an integer, a logarithmic second solution may replace the naive second Frobenius power. The entries obey the Fuchs relation
0+(1−c)+0+(c−a−b)+a+b=1.
(45)
For a second-order equation with exactly three regular singular points, these exponent data determine the equation up to multiplication by a nonzero function; there is no accessory parameter. The symbol is therefore the one for the Gauss hypergeometric equation. Its distinguished solution F(a,b;c;z) is the exponent-zero solution analytic at zero and normalized to one there. For the ordinary power-series definition one assumes c∈/{0,−1,−2,…}, with exceptional parameter values handled separately or by continuation.
