Two irreducible varieties X and Y are birational if they contain nonempty Zariski-open subsets U⊆X and V⊆Y that are isomorphic. Equivalently, their function fields are isomorphic:
k(X)≅k(Y).
(252)
For an irreducible variety,
dimX=trdegk​k(X).
(253)
An isomorphism of function fields preserves transcendence degree, so dimX=dimY. This is the dimension from the function field.
Now let K/C be a finitely generated field extension. Choose field generators a1​,…,am​ and set
A=C[a1​,…,am​]⊆K.
(254)
Then A is a finitely generated integral C-algebra and Frac(A)=K. The affine irreducible variety
X0​=SpecA
(255)
has function field K. Embed X0​ as a closed affine subvariety of some affine space AN, identify that affine space with the standard open chart of projective space PN, and take the projective closure X of X0​. The closure of an irreducible topological space is irreducible, and X0​ is dense and open in X. Thus X is a projective variety and
k(X)=k(X0​)=K.
(256)
This constructs the projective model of a finitely generated field.
For the curve
X=V(y2−x(x−1)2),
(257)
put
t=x−1y​.
(258)
In its function field, the defining equation gives
t2=x,y=t(x−1)=t(t2−1).
(259)
Hence C(X)=C(t), so X is rational. Equivalently, this is the normalization of y squared equals x times x minus one squared, whose smooth projective normalization is P1 and has geometric genus zero.
A smooth projective curve in the projective plane of degree d has, by the genus of a smooth plane curve formula,
g=2(d−1)(d−2)​.
(260)
Its genus can be zero only for d=1 or d=2. Conversely, a projective line and every smooth conic over C are rational, so each is birational to X. Therefore the required degrees are exactly
d=1 or d=2​.
(261)
Finally, in Ax,y,z3​ take
S=V(F),F(x,y,z)=y2−x(x−1)2.
(262)
The polynomial F is irreducible: as a quadratic in y, it could factor only if x(x−1)2 were a square polynomial, which it is not. Thus S is an irreducible affine hypersurface of dimension two. Its function field is
C(S)=C(t,z),x=t2,y=t(t2−1),
(263)
so S is birational to A2.
The partial derivatives are
Fx​=−(x−1)(3x−1),Fy​=2y,Fz​=0.
(264)
Solving F=Fx​=Fy​=0 gives x=1, y=0, with z arbitrary. By the Jacobian criterion,
Sing(S)={(1,0,z):z∈C}≅A1.
(265)
This is an irreducible subvariety of dimension one, giving the requested singular cylinder over a nodal curve.
