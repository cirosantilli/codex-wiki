For a divisor on an algebraic curve D on a smooth projective curve X of genus g, the Riemann-Roch theorem states
ℓ(D)−ℓ(KX​−D)=degD+1−g,
(282)
where KX​ is a canonical divisor. Taking D=0 gives 1−ℓ(KX​)=1−g, so ℓ(KX​)=g. Taking D=KX​ then gives
g−1=degKX​+1−g,degKX​=2g−2​.
(283)
To obtain a uniform projective embedding, choose a divisor D of degree 2g+1. Since every divisor appearing below has degree greater than 2g−2, Riemann--Roch gives
ℓ(D)=g+2,ℓ(D−P)=g+1,ℓ(D−P−Q)=g,ℓ(D−2P)=g.
(284)
The first two equalities show that the complete linear system of a divisor ∣D∣ has no base point. The strict drops in the last two comparisons show respectively that its sections separate distinct points P,Q and tangent directions at P. Thus D is a very ample divisor, in accordance with the general fact that a high-degree divisor is very ample on a smooth projective curve, and its sections define a closed embedding
X↪Pℓ(D)−1=Pg+1.​
(285)
The ambient dimension therefore depends only on g.
The Riemann-Hurwitz formula for a nonconstant morphism h:C1​→C2​ of degree n is
2g(C1​)−2=n(2g(C2​)−2)+∑P∈C1​​(eP​−1).
(286)
Choose a smooth plane quartic C, so g(C)=3, and form the product of projective varieties S=C×C. This is a smooth projective variety of dimension two. If B⊂S is an irreducible curve, pass to its normalization B. At least one coordinate projection B→C is nonconstant, since otherwise B would be a point. For that projection, Riemann--Hurwitz gives
2g(B)−2≥n(2⋅3−2)≥4,
(287)
so the geometric genus of B is at least three. Hence S is the required surface; this is the product surface without low-genus curves construction.
Finally let X⊂P2 be a smooth plane curve of degree d and let p∈/X. After a projective change of coordinates, take p=[0:0:1]. Projection away from p is
[x:y:z]⟼[x:y].
(288)
Its two homogeneous coordinate functions cannot vanish simultaneously on X, because their common zero in P2 is p. The criterion for a morphism of algebraic varieties therefore shows that the restriction
π:X⟶P1
(289)
is a morphism. A fibre is the intersection with a line through p, and a general such line meets X in d points counted with multiplicity. Thus the projection of a plane curve from an exterior point has degree d.
By the genus of a smooth plane curve, g(X)=(d−1)(d−2)/2. Applying Riemann--Hurwitz to π and its ramification divisor Rπ​ gives
degRπ​=2g(X)−2+2d=(d−1)(d−2)−2+2d=d(d−1).
(290)
Every ramification point contributes at least one to this degree, so
#{P∈X:π ramifies at P}≤d(d−1)​.
(291)
