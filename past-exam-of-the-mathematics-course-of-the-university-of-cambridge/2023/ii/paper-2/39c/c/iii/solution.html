A rigid body obeys the no-slip boundary condition. Its outer potential flow generally has an O(U) tangential slip velocity at the surface, so the boundary layer must make an O(U) velocity correction across thickness δ. Its strain is therefore O(U/δ), and in two dimensions its dissipation per unit axial length is
Dlayer​∼μ(δU​)2aδ=μU2δa​,
(472)
which exceeds the O(μU2) outer dissipation by a/δ∼Re1/2.
The dominant drag consequently depends on the detailed boundary-layer velocity field and possibly on boundary-layer separation. It cannot be obtained by integrating the outer irrotational strain alone.
