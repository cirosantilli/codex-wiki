First let δ=ωα. If β,γ<δ, every exponent appearing in their Cantor normal forms is strictly below α. The rules for ordinal addition can delete lower terms but cannot introduce an exponent at least α. Therefore
β+γ<ωα=δ,
(145)
so δ is additively closed.
Conversely, suppose nonzero δ is additively closed. Part (a) gives
δ=ωαn+ρ,0<n<ω,ρ<ωα.
(146)
If ρ>0, then both ωαn and ρ are smaller than δ, but their sum is δ, contradicting closure. Hence ρ=0. If now n>1, then
ωα(n−1)<δ,ωα<δ,
(147)
while
ωα(n−1)+ωα=ωαn=δ,
(148)
another contradiction. Thus n=1, and
δ=ωα​.
(149)
This proves the classification of additively closed ordinals.
