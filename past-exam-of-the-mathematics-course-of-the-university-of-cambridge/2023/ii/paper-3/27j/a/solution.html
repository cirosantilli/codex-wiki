In Kendall notation, the first two letters M mean Poisson arrivals and exponential service times. An M-M-1 queue has arrival rate λ, one server of rate μ, and queue-length transitions
n→n+1 at rate λ,n→n−1 at rate μ(n≥1).
(271)
It has a stationary distribution of an M-M-1 queue exactly when
ρ:=μλ​<1,
(272)
and then
πn​=(1−ρ)ρn,n≥0.​
(273)
An M-M-infinity queue has infinitely many servers, so every customer begins service immediately. Its occupancy transitions are
n→n+1 at rate λ,n→n−1 at rate nμ.
(274)
The detailed balance for a birth-death process equations give
πn+1​=πn​(n+1)μλ​.
(275)
Thus for every λ,μ>0 the stationary distribution of an M-M-infinity queue exists and is
πn​=e−ρn!ρn​,ρ=μλ​,​
(276)
the Poisson distribution of mean ρ. The Burke theorem for an M-M-infinity queue states that in this stationary regime the departure process is a Poisson process of rate λ; equivalently, time reversal turns departures into arrivals. The past departure process is also independent of the number in the system at the observation time.
