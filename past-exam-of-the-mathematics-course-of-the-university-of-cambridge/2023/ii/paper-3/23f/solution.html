The uniformization theorem states that every simply connected Riemann surface is conformally equivalent to exactly one of the Riemann sphere C∞​, the complex plane C, or the open unit disc D. Equivalently, every connected Riemann surface is a quotient of one of these by a free properly discontinuous group of conformal automorphisms.
For C∞​, every nonidentity Möbius transformation has a fixed point. A free action is therefore trivial, so the only surface uniformized by the sphere is
C∞​.​
(220)
Every conformal automorphism of C has the form z↦az+b. If a=1, it has a fixed point, so a group acting freely consists only of translations. A discrete subgroup of (C,+) has rank zero, one, or two. The corresponding quotients are
C,C/ωZ≅C∗,C/Λ,​
(221)
where Λ is a rank-two lattice and C/Λ is a complex torus. This proves the completeness of the Riemann surfaces uniformized by the complex plane.
Now let D⊆C be a domain whose complement contains two distinct points. Its universal cover cannot be the sphere because no nonconstant holomorphic map from a compact Riemann surface has image in the noncompact proper domain D. If its universal cover were C, the covering map followed by D↪C would be a nonconstant entire function omitting the two chosen complementary points, contradicting the Little Picard theorem. The uniformization theorem therefore leaves only
D≅D,​
(222)
which is the plane domain with two omitted points is hyperbolic result.
Finally let j:C∗↪R be a holomorphic embedding with R compact. At each puncture zero and infinity, an essential singularity would, by the Great Picard theorem in a coordinate chart, force values to be taken repeatedly and contradict injectivity. Thus j extends holomorphically to
J:C∞​→R.
(223)
A generic point of the open image j(C∗) has exactly one preimage, so J has degree one. A degree-one holomorphic map between compact Riemann surfaces is a biholomorphism; equivalently, the Riemann-Hurwitz formula rules out branching and gives the same conclusion. Therefore the compact Riemann surface containing an embedded punctured plane is
R≅C∞​.​
