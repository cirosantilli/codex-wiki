OurBigBook.com Words: 168
The Axiom of foundation says that every nonempty set A contains an x with x∩A=∅. The principle of epsilon induction says that if a formula φ is progressive,
∀x[(∀y∈x, φ(y))⟹φ(x)],
(152)
then φ holds for every set.
Assume foundation and let φ be progressive. If φ(a) failed for some a, use separation to form the nonempty set of failures inside TC({a}). Foundation supplies an ∈-minimal failure x. Every member of x lies in the transitive closure and is not a failure, so progressiveness gives φ(x), a contradiction. Hence epsilon induction holds.
Conversely, assume epsilon induction and suppose a nonempty set A has no ∈-minimal member. Let
φ(x)⟺x∈/A.
(153)
If every member of x satisfies φ but x∈A, then x∩A=∅, making x an ∈-minimal member of A, contrary to the assumption. Thus φ is progressive. Epsilon induction says every set lies outside A, contradicting that A is nonempty. Foundation follows.
