For an algebraically closed field k, the strong Hilbert Nullstellensatz says that every ideal J⊆k[x1​,…,xn​] satisfies
I(V(J))=J​.​
(225)
Its weak form says that V(J)=∅ exactly when J=(1). Now let S=C[x0​,…,xn​] and let I⊆S be a homogeneous ideal. The affine zero set of I is the affine cone over V+​(I) together with the origin. Hence, when the projective zero set is empty, either
I=(1)​
(226)
or the affine zero set is exactly the origin. In the latter case the Hilbert Nullstellensatz gives
I​=(x0​,…,xn​),​
(227)
the irrelevant ideal of projective space. Equivalently, some power (x0​,…,xn​)N is contained in I. These are precisely the alternatives in the Projective Nullstellensatz.
Let V⊆P3 be a smooth quadric surface. After a projective linear transformation, the Segre embedding identifies
V≅P1×P1.
(228)
Choose distinct points p,q∈P1. The two fibres
Cp​=P1×{p},Cq​=P1×{q}
(229)
are the promised disjoint curves on a smooth quadric surface: each is a smooth projective line, and Cp​∩Cq​=∅. By the Bézout theorem, any two nonempty curves in the projective plane intersect. An isomorphism of algebraic varieties preserves intersections and takes projective curves to projective curves, so these disjoint curves prove
V≅P2.​
(230)
Next let W be a smooth projective curve and let f:W⇢Y⊆PN be a rational map to a projective variety. At a point P outside its initial domain, write
f=[f0​:⋯:fN​]
(231)
using elements of the function field C(W). The local ring of a smooth algebraic curve at P is a discrete valuation ring. If m=mini​vP​(fi​), multiplying every coordinate by a function of valuation −m makes all coordinates regular at P and at least one a unit there. They therefore define a morphism near P. Repeating this at the finitely many missing points proves the extension of a rational map from a smooth projective curve.
Smoothness is essential. Let ν:P1→C be the normalization of a nodal curve for a rational nodal cubic C. The inverse is a rational map C⇢P1 on the smooth locus. If it extended to a morphism g:C→P1, then g∘ν would agree with the identity on a dense Zariski-open subset of P1 and hence everywhere. But the two distinct points a,b∈P1 above the node would satisfy
a=(g∘ν)(a)=g(ν(a))=g(ν(b))=(g∘ν)(b)=b,
(232)
a contradiction. This is the failure of rational-map extension on a singular curve.
Finally take the Fermat cubic curve
F=x3+y3+z3
(233)
and define the projective hypersurface
Z={([x:y:z],[s:t])∈P2×P1:sF+txyz=0}.
(234)
Because F and xyz have no common irreducible factor, the bihomogeneous polynomial sF+txyz is irreducible, so Z is an algebraic variety. Let
π:Z⟶P1,([x:y:z],[s:t])⟼[s:t]
(235)
be the second coordinate projection. Every homogeneous cubic in three variables has a projective zero over C, so every fibre is nonempty and π is a surjective morphism. Over p=[1:0] the fibre is the Fermat cubic curve, which is smooth and has genus
2(3−1)(3−2)​=1.
(236)
Over q=[0:1] the fibre is
V(xyz)=V(x)∪V(y)∪V(z),
(237)
the union of exactly three distinct projective lines, hence exactly three irreducible components. This is the cubic pencil with a triangular member required by the question.
