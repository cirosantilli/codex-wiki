At p, the surface S cannot cross the boundary S=∂R, because it lies inside R. Consequently the two surfaces are tangent at p. Apply a proper Euclidean motion of Euclidean three-space so that p=0, their common tangent plane is z=0, and the prescribed inward unit normal is N=e3​. Locally write
S: z=f(x,y),S: z=g(x,y),
(250)
with the region R on the side z≥f(x,y). Then
g≥f,g(0)=f(0),∇g(0)=∇f(0)=0.
(251)
Thus g−f has a local minimum at zero and its Hessian matrix is positive semidefinite. The fundamental forms of a graph surface at a horizontal tangent plane give
H(0)=21​trHessf(0),H(0)=21​trHessg(0).
(252)
Taking the trace of the positive-semidefinite difference proves the mean-curvature comparison at tangential contact:
H(p)≥H(p).​
(253)
There is no analogous conclusion for K. In the same local coordinates take
f(x,y)=−x2−y2,g(x,y)=−21​(x2+y2).
(254)
Then g≥f, so the second graph lies on the inward side of the first, while the Gaussian curvature of a graph surface gives
Hf​(0)=−2,Hg​(0)=−1,Kf​(0)=4,Kg​(0)=1.
(255)
Hence the mean-curvature inequality holds but
K(p)<K(p).​
(256)
Using smooth bump functions, these local graph patches can be completed away from p to a compact region with connected smooth boundary and a closed interior surface without changing their germs at p. This realizes the counterexample under the global hypotheses.
