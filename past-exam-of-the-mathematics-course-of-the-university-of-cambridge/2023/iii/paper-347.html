This mode is most plausible for a nearly stationary supermassive black hole immersed in the hot, pressure-supported, X-ray-emitting atmosphere of a massive elliptical galaxy. Such gas is comparatively smooth, its random and rotational speeds can be smaller than its sound speed, and radiative cooling can be slow on the inflow scale.
The ideal Bondi accretion assumptions can fail in several independent ways. The gas may carry enough specific angular momentum to circularize into an accretion disk; a galaxy's stars and dark matter can dominate the gravitational potential outside the black hole's sphere of influence; the hole can move relative to the gas; cooling, conduction, or external heating can destroy adiabaticity or isothermality; a multiphase or clumpy medium is not a uniform reservoir; magnetic fields, turbulence, and viscosity add stresses absent from the spherical model; and AGN feedback, winds, or jets can expel or recirculate the inflow. Time dependence and self-gravity provide further failures. Thus a Bondi estimate is best interpreted as an idealized supply rate, not automatically as the rate crossing the event horizon.

The standard Shakura--Sunyaev thin disk has three qualitative radial zones. Its hot inner part is dominated by radiation pressure and electron-scattering opacity; farther out, gas pressure overtakes radiation pressure while electron scattering can remain the main opacity; in the cool outer zone, gas pressure remains dominant and free-free opacity becomes important. The transition radii vary with M, M˙, and α.

(21)

The maximal radiative efficiency of black-hole accretion is the fraction of rest-mass energy available if all binding energy released before capture escapes as radiation. In a Newtonian disk ending at Rin​,
ηmax​=2Rin​c2GM​,
(29)
because a circular orbit has specific binding energy GM/(2R). In relativity, ηmax​=1−EISCO​. Black-hole spin moves the innermost stable circular orbit inward for prograde flow and outward for retrograde flow, increasing or decreasing this maximum respectively.
Since L=ηM˙c2, a source of fixed luminosity requires M˙=L/(ηc2), while black-hole mass grows at approximately (1−η)M˙. The actual radiative efficiency of black-hole accretion can lie below the maximum when energy is advected through the horizon or carried away mechanically. A low-density, optically thin advection-dominated accretion flow stores dissipated energy in ions, while a high-rate slim accretion disk traps photons and advects their energy inward; both are radiatively inefficient flows.

The Soltan argument compares the time-integrated luminosity density of the cosmological AGN population with the present comoving mass density in dormant supermassive black holes. If UAGN​ is the emitted energy density corrected for obscuration and bolometric output, accretion with population-averaged efficiency ηˉ​ predicts
ρBH​c2=ηˉ​1−ηˉ​​UAGN​,ηˉ​=UAGN​+ρBH​c2UAGN​​.
(32)
In practice UAGN​ comes from integrating AGN luminosity functions over luminosity and cosmological redshift, with corrections for obscured sources and missed wavebands, while ρBH​ is inferred from local galaxy--black-hole scaling relations.
The inferred efficiency is of order the canonical thin-disk value, about ten per cent, so most cosmic black-hole mass was accumulated in radiatively efficient, optically thick accretion episodes. Radiatively inefficient flows can dominate low-luminosity activity or brief extreme phases, and mergers redistribute existing mass, but neither naturally accounts for the observed integrated AGN radiation while supplying most of the final mass.
