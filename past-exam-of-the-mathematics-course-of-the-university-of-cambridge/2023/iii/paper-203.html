A compact H-hull is a bounded relatively closed set A⊂H such that H∖A is a simply connected domain. Its mapping-out function of a compact H-hull is the unique conformal map gA​:H∖A→H with hydrodynamic normalization at infinity

Write Ar​=[−r,r]×(0,1] and take r≥1. By the Brownian representation of half-plane capacity,
hcap(Ar​)=limy→∞​yEiy​[ImBτ​].
(9)
On hitting Ar​, the exit height is at most one. Moreover, Ar​ lies in the half-disc of radius r2+1​≤2r. The harmonic measure of that semicircle as viewed from iy is O(r/y): mapping its exterior to H by z↦z+(2r)2/z reduces the estimate to the Poisson kernel for the upper half-plane on an interval of length O(r). Consequently
Eiy​[ImBτ​]≤yCr​
(10)
for large y, and hcap(Ar​)≤Cr. This is the half-plane capacity of a low rectangle.

The Conformal Markov property of SLE says that, conditionally on the curve through time t, mapping out the initial hull by gt​−Ut​ turns the future into an independent Schramm–Loewner evolution in (H,0,∞). More precisely,
Ks​=(gt​(Kt+s​∖Kt​)−Ut​)fill

For the Bessel process
dXt​=dBt​+2Xt​d−1​dt,
(15)
the scale function of a one-dimensional diffusion is s(x)=x2−d when d=2. If 0<ϵ<x<R, the boundary hitting probability from a diffusion scale function gives
Px​(TR​<Tϵ​)=s(R)−s(ϵ)s(x)−s(ϵ)​.
(16)
If d<2, then s(0)=0 and s(R)→∞. Letting ϵ↓0 and then R→∞ shows that the process cannot escape to infinity before reaching zero. The exit time from each bounded interval is finite almost surely, so T0​<∞ almost surely.
If d>2, then s(ϵ)→∞ in absolute value as ϵ↓0. Equivalently,
Px​(Tϵ​<TR​)=s(R)−s(ϵ)s(R)−s(x)​⟶0.
(17)
Thus the process does not hit zero. The borderline case d=2 has scale function logx and also does not hit zero. This is the Hitting-zero classification for a Bessel process.

b OurBigBook.com
For a real boundary point x=0, the Boundary-point Bessel flow for SLE says that Vtx​/κ​ is a Bessel process of dimension
d=1+κ4​.
(27)
When κ≤4, one has d≥2, so part 2(b), including its logarithmic d=2 case, shows that no fixed boundary point is swallowed.
If the trace touched the real line away from its starting point, or if a later segment crossed an earlier segment, the resulting hull would disconnect from infinity a nonempty real interval, which contains a rational number. Applying the preceding argument after every rational time and using the Conformal Markov property of SLE rules this out on a countable probability-one event. Continuity of the trace then shows that no two distinct times have the same image. Thus SLEκ​ is simple for 0<κ≤4.

The zero-boundary Gaussian free field h on D is the isonormal Gaussian process over H01​(D): for f,g∈H01​(D), the variables (h,f)∇​ are centered jointly Gaussian and satisfy
E[(h,f)∇​(h,g)∇​]=(f,g)∇​.
(36)
Equivalently, for an orthonormal basis (en​) of H01​(D) and independent standard normal variables (ξn​),
h=∑n≥1​ξn​en​
(37)
as a random generalized function.

For an open subdomain U⊂D, identify H01​(U) with the closed subspace of H01​(D) obtained by zero extension. Its orthogonal complement is
HU​={f∈H01​(D):(f,g)∇​=0 for every g∈H01​(U)}.
(38)
If f∈HU​, then testing against C0∞​(U) and integrating by parts gives Δf=0 in U in the distributional derivative sense. The Weyl lemma therefore gives a representative harmonic on U.
The orthogonal decomposition by a closed subspace gives
H01​(D)=H01​(U)⊕HU​.
(39)
Project the isonormal process defining h onto these two orthogonal subspaces. The projections are jointly Gaussian and uncorrelated, hence independent. The first projection is a zero-boundary Gaussian free field hU​ on U; the second is a random distribution hhar that is harmonic on U. Thus
h=hU​+hhar,
(40)
with independent summands. This proves the Domain Markov property of the Gaussian free field.

Choose an orthonormal basis (en​) of H01​(D) and write the GFF formally as h=∑n​ξn​en​, where the ξn​ are independent standard normal variables. The Green-kernel expansion in the Dirichlet space gives
∑n≥1​(∫D​en​(x)ρ(dx))2=∬D×D​GD​(x,y)ρ(dx)ρ(dy)<∞.
