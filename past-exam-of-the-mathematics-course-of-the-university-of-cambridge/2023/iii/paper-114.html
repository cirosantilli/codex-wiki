dq​=1+(−1)q={2,0,​q even,q odd.​
(1)
Consequently
Hq​(RPn;Z)≅⎩⎨⎧​Z,Z/2,Z,0,​q=0,0<q<n and q odd,q=n and n odd,otherwise.​
(2)
After tensoring the cellular complex with F2​=Z/2, every differential vanishes, so
Hq​(RPn;F2​)≅{F2​,0,​0≤q≤n,q>n.​

The quotient is the mapping torus of
τ:T2→T2,(z2​,z3​)↦(z2​,z3​).
(7)
A mapping torus of a cellular map has a finite CW structure. On integral homology, τ∗​ is 1 on H0​(T2) and H2​(T2) and is −I on H1​(T2)≅Z2. The Wang sequence therefore gives
Hq​(T3/∼;Z)≅⎩⎨⎧​Z,Z⊕(Z/2)2,0,​q=0,2,3,q=1,q>3.​

The long exact sequence in homology of the triple A⊆U⊆X contains
⋯→Hq​(U,A)→Hq​(X,A)→Hq​(X,U)→Hq−1​(U,A)→⋯.
(10)
Because A↪U is a homotopy equivalence, H∗​(U,A)=0. Hence the middle map is an isomorphism in every degree:
H∗​(X,A)≅H∗​(X,U).
(11)
The Excision theorem says that if Z⊆A⊆X and Z⊆intA, then inclusion induces
H∗​(X∖Z,A∖Z)∼​H∗​(X,A).
(12)
A good pair (X,A) has A closed and a neighborhood U that deformation retracts onto A. The collapsing a pair theorem states that the quotient map gives
Hq​(X,A)≅Hq​(X/A).
(13)
To prove it, choose such a neighborhood U. The first result identifies H∗​(X,A) with H∗​(X,U). Excision identifies the latter with H∗​(X/A,U/A), while U/A is contractible because the deformation retraction can be chosen relative to A using the homotopy extension property. The long exact sequence of the pair (X/A,U/A) then identifies this relative group with H∗​(X/A).
For f:(Dn,∂Dn)→(Dn,∂Dn), define degf by
f∗​[Dn,∂Dn]=(degf)[Dn,∂Dn]
(14)
in Hn​(Dn,∂Dn;Z)≅Z. In the long exact sequence of the pair, the connecting homomorphism
Hn​(Dn,∂Dn)∼​Hn−1​(∂Dn)
(15)
is an isomorphism. Naturality shows that f∗​ and (f∣∂Dn​)∗​ multiply the corresponding generators by the same integer, so
degf=deg(f∣∂Dn​).
(16)
Finally,
(Dn×I)/∂(Dn×I)≅Σ(Dn/∂Dn)≅Sn+1.
(17)
Under this identification, the map induced by g(x,t)=(f(x),t) is the suspension of the map induced by f. By degree under suspension,
degg=degf.
(18)
This argument proves the needed product assertion directly from the natural suspension isomorphism in reduced homology.

For a singular (k+l)-simplex σ:[v0​,…,vk+l​]→X, define the cochain-level cup product by
(α⌣β)(σ)=α(σ∣[v0​,…,vk​])β(σ∣[vk​,…,vk+l​]).
(19)
If α∈Ck(X,A;R) and σ lies in A, its front k-face also lies in A, so the first factor vanishes. Thus α⌣β∈Ck+l(X,A;R).
For the projections from X×Y, the exterior product in cohomology is
a×b=pX∗​a⌣pY∗​b.
(20)
For CW pairs over a field R, subject to the usual finite-type condition that makes the graded tensor product commute with the relevant direct products, the Künneth theorem says that
Φ:H∗(X,A;R)⊗R​H∗(Y;R)∼​H∗(X×Y,A×Y;R).
(21)
More generally the conclusion holds over a principal ideal domain when one factor has degreewise finitely generated free cohomology. It fails over Z in general: H∗(RP2;Z) is Z in degree zero and Z/2 in degree two, but the integral Künneth and universal coefficient theorem for cohomology calculations give
H3(RP2×RP2;Z)≅Z/2.
(22)
The tensor product of the two cohomology groups has no degree-three term, so Φ is not surjective.
Use the homeomorphism
T2×T2⟶T2×T2,(x,y)⟼(x,y−x),
(23)
which carries the diagonal Δ to T2×{0}. Hence
T4∖Δ≅T2×(T2∖{0}).
(24)
The punctured torus deformation retracts onto a wedge of two circles. Let a,b be the degree-one generators from the first torus and c,d those from the punctured torus. Since all cohomology groups are free, the Künneth theorem identifies the integral cohomology ring as
H∗(T4∖Δ;Z)≅ΛZ​(a,b)⊗(cd)ΛZ​(c,d)​,∣a∣=∣b∣=∣c∣=∣d∣=1.
(25)
Thus ab, ac, ad, bc, and bd form a basis in degree two; abc and abd form a basis in degree three; and all higher positive degrees vanish.

An R-orientation of a rank-r vector bundle E→B is a coherent choice of generator in
Hr(Eb​,Eb​∖{0};R)
(26)
for every fiber. Equivalently, it is a Thom class uE​∈Hr(D(E),S(E);R) restricting to those generators. The Euler class of a vector bundle is
e(E)=s∗j∗uE​∈Hr(B;R),
(27)
where j:(D(E),∅)→(D(E),S(E)) forgets the subspace and s is the zero section.
The Thom isomorphism theorem is
Hq(B;R)∼​Hq+r(D(E),S(E);R),a⟼π∗a⌣uE​.
(28)
Insert these isomorphisms into the long exact cohomology sequence of (D(E),S(E)) and use the deformation retraction D(E)≃B. This gives the Gysin sequence of a sphere bundle
⋯→Hq−r(B)⌣e(E)​Hq(B)π∗​Hq(S(E))→Hq−r+1(B)⌣e(E)​Hq+1(B)→⋯.
(29)
To verify the labelled maps, represent a under the Thom isomorphism by π∗a⌣uE​. Its image in H∗(D(E)) pulls back along the zero section to
a⌣s∗j∗uE​=a⌣e(E).
(30)
Thus multiplication by the Euler class is exactly the map from relative to absolute cohomology in the pair sequence.
Now let Mm⊂Sn and let V be its tubular neighborhood. The normal bundle has rank r=n−m. With F2​ coefficients it is automatically oriented. Since n>2m+1, one has r>m+1, so its Euler class lies above the dimension of M and vanishes. The Gysin sequence consequently splits into short exact sequences of vector spaces and gives
Hq(∂V;F2​)≅Hq(M;F2​)⊕Hq−r+1(M;F2​).
(31)
On the other hand, Alexander duality gives
Hq(Sn∖M;F2​)≅Hn−q−1​(M;F2​).
(32)
Over the field F2​, the latter is naturally dual to Hn−q−1(M;F2​), which expresses the complement cohomology entirely in terms of that of M.

For unit vectors, the defining inequality for Y is equivalent to v⋅w≥0. Write
w=tv+u,qquadu∈v⊥=Tv​S2n,qquadt≥0.
(33)
Then t=(1−∥u∥2)1/2, so
(v,u)⟼(v,(1−∥u∥2)1/2v+u)
(34)
is a homeomorphism D(TS2n)→Y. Replacing v by −v gives the same description of Y′. Their intersection is
Z={(v,w):v⋅w=0}=STS2n=V2​(R2n+1),
(35)
the unit tangent bundle, equivalently a Stiefel manifold. Since e(TS2n) evaluates to χ(S2n)=2, its Gysin sequence gives the cohomology of the unit tangent bundle of an even-dimensional sphere:
Hq(Z;Z)≅⎩⎨⎧​Z,Z/2,0,​q=0,4n−1,q=2n,otherwise.​
(36)
Every product of positive-degree classes vanishes.
Let G consist of the eight signed permutations of the two factors:
(v,w)⟼(ε1​v,ε2​w)or(v,w)⟼(ε1​w,ε2​v),εi​∈{±1}.
(37)
It is the group of signed permutation matrices in dimension two, hence isomorphic to the dihedral group D8​, and every element preserves the equation v⋅w=0. If a,b generate H2n(X;Z) from the two sphere factors, these maps act by the corresponding signed permutation matrix, because the antipodal map of the even-dimensional sphere has degree −1. The eight actions are distinct, so only the identity can be homotopic to 1X​.
Every element of G acts trivially on H∗(Z;Z). The degree-2n group is Z/2, so its automorphism is forced to be the identity. On the top class, regard Z as the Stiefel manifold of two-frames. The signed permutations are the right action of O(2). The determinant-one component is connected, while a determinant-minus-one element is homotopic within that component to (v,w)↦(v,−w), the antipodal map on the S2n−1 fibers. That map has degree +1, so the top class is also fixed.
The subgroup preserving Y is
H={1,AB,S,ABS},
(38)
where A(v,w)=(−v,w), B(v,w)=(v,−w), and S(v,w)=(w,v). It consists exactly of the elements with ε1​ε2​=1. Under the deformation retraction Y≃S2n onto the diagonal, 1 and S act as the identity, while AB and ABS act as the antipodal map. They therefore act on H2n(Y;Z) by +1,+1,−1,−1, respectively.
The Thom isomorphism theorem identifies H∗(Y,Z) with a degree-2n shift of H∗(S2n). On the Thom class in degree 2n, the same four elements act by +1,+1,−1,−1: the factor swap reverses each normal vector, which preserves the orientation because the normal rank 2n is even, while the simultaneous antipodal map reverses the oriented tangent fiber. On the degree-4n relative class every element acts by +1, since the base and fiber signs for the last two elements cancel.
When n=1, Z=V2​(R3)≅SO(3). Every signed permutation of (v,w) extends, after sending the third frame vector v×w to the required sign, to right multiplication by an element of SO(3). Since SO(3) is path-connected, every right translation is homotopic to the identity. Therefore
g∣Z​≃1Z​for every g∈G.​
(39)
