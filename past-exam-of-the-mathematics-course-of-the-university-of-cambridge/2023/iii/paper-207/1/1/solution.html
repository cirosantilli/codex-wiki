Let Z indicate a recent birthday event, A indicate a social gathering, and Y denote subsequent household COVID-19 infection. The standard instrumental variable conditions are:
  • Instrument relevance: Z changes the probability or intensity of A.
  • Instrumental-variable independence: conditional on chosen baseline covariates, birthday timing is independent of unmeasured causes of infection and of the relevant potential outcomes.
  • The exclusion restriction: Z affects Y only through the social gathering A.
  • For a local average treatment effect, instrumental-variable monotonicity excludes households that would hold a gathering without a birthday event but would suppress it because of one.
The usual consistency in causal inference, well-defined exposure, and absence of relevant interference in causal inference are also needed to interpret the result causally.
