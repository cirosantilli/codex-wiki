The Karhunen–Loève expansion has the following Hilbert-space form. Let X be a square-integrable Random element of a Hilbert space H, with mean μ and covariance operator C. There are nonnegative eigenvalues λ1​≥λ2​≥⋯ and orthonormal eigenvectors ϕk​ spanning the closure of the range of C such that
Cϕk​=λk​ϕk​,X=μ+∑k≥1​ξk​ϕk​
(1)
in L2(Ω;H). The functional principal component scores
ξk​=⟨X−μ,ϕk​⟩
(2)
satisfy
Eξk​=0,E[ξj​ξk​]=λk​1{j=k}​.
(3)
If X is a Gaussian random element, the scores are independent normal random variables.
For the proof, C is positive, self-adjoint, and trace-class, with
trC=E∥X−μ∥2<∞.
(4)
It is therefore compact, so the spectral theorem for compact Hermitian operators supplies the eigenpairs. Their score covariance is
E[ξj​ξk​]=⟨Cϕj​,ϕk​⟩=λj​⟨ϕj​,ϕk​⟩.
(5)
For the residual Rm​=X−μ−∑k=1m​ξk​ϕk​, Parseval identity and the trace formula give
E∥Rm​∥2=trC−∑k=1m​λk​=∑k>m​λk​⟶0.
(6)
The centered variable's projection onto kerC has zero second moment and is therefore zero almost surely, which completes the mean-square expansion. When C has a continuous covariance kernel, Mercer's theorem additionally expands that kernel as c(s,t)=∑k​λk​ϕk​(s)ϕk​(t).
