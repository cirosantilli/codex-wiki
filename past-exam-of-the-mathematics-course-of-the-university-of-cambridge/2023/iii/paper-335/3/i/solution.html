Because A is a real symmetric positive-definite matrix, the finite-dimensional spectral theorem supplies an orthonormal eigenbasis u1​,…,un​ with
0<λ1​≤⋯≤λn​.
(42)
Its eigendecomposition is also its singular value decomposition. If e=y(δ)−y, then
x(δ)−x=A−1e=∑j=1n​λj​⟨e,uj​⟩​uj​,
(43)
so
∥x(δ)−x∥≤λ1​δ​​.
(44)
The operator norms satisfy ∥A∥2​=λn​ and ∥A−1∥2​=1/λ1​. Therefore the worst-case relative perturbation bound is
∥x∥∥x(δ)−x∥​≤κ2​(A)λ1​λn​​​​∥y∥∥y(δ)−y∥​​.
(45)
The ratio κ2​(A) is the spectral condition number of a positive-definite matrix. A large ratio means that data noise aligned with an eigenvector for the smallest eigenvalue is strongly amplified, so the inverse problem is ill conditioned.
