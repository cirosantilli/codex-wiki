Because ϕ is a conserved composition density, an infinitesimal material displacement r↦r+u changes it at fixed position by
δϕ=−∇⋅(ϕu)=−u⋅∇ϕ−ϕ∇⋅u.
(1)
The second term is essential when the deformation is compressible. By the definition of the chemical potential, and taking u to vanish on the boundary,
δF=∫μδϕdr=−∫μ∇⋅(ϕu)dr=∫ϕ∇j​μuj​dr.
(2)
The same free-energy change written in terms of the stress tensor is
δF=∫Σij​∇i​uj​dr=−∫(∇i​Σij​)uj​dr.
(3)
Since u is arbitrary,
∇i​Σij​=−ϕ∇j​μ.​
(4)
This is the Korteweg force density of a diffuse-interface mixture.

A polar liquid crystal distinguishes the two ends of each constituent. Its orientational order is described by a vector p, and p and −p represent different states. A nematic liquid crystal has head-tail symmetry, so its director obeys n∼−n. Its lowest-rank faithful nematic order parameter is the symmetric traceless tensor
Qij​=S(ni​nj​−31​δij​),
(19)
which is unchanged by n↦−n.

Let e1​,e2​ span the rotating plane and let n=e1​×e2​ be its normal. For modulation along x,
p=p0​(e1​cosq0​x+e2​sinq0​x),
(29)

For λ>0, this is minimized by n∥x, so the rotation plane is perpendicular to the modulation direction and the helix is transverse. Thus either sign lifts the full rotational degeneracy, leaving only the rotations consistent with its selected relative orientation. A sufficiently small negative λ does not overcome the stabilizing higher-gradient terms.

The Young–Laplace equation gives a pressure excess 2σ/R inside a spherical droplet. Near coexistence, the common chemical-potential shift is μ≃αδ, where α=f′′(ϕB​), while changing phase changes the composition by approximately 2ϕB​. Balancing the capillary pressure against this thermodynamic shift gives the Gibbs--Thomson relation
2ϕB​αδ≃R2σ​.
(31)
Hence the compositions immediately outside and inside are
ϕ(R+)=−ϕB​+δ(R),ϕ(R−)=+ϕB​+δ(R),
(32)
with
δ(R)=αϕB​Rσ​∝Rσ​.​

The spherically symmetric Laplace equation with ϕ​(R)=δ(R) and ϕ​(∞)=ε has solution
ϕ​(r)=ε+[δ(R)−ε]rR​.

Thermal molecular motion causes the coarse droplet radius to fluctuate as well as drift down F(R), so its effective equation must be an overdamped Langevin dynamics. With ⟨Λ(t)Λ(t′)⟩=δ(t−t′), detailed balance with equilibrium density proportional to e−F/(kB​T) imposes the Fluctuation-dissipation theorem. The Model A fluctuation-dissipation relation gives

A subcritical droplet must make a rare thermal fluctuation from the metastable basin over the free-energy barrier F∗. Its probability carries the Boltzmann factor e−F∗/(kB​T), so the nucleation rate has the Arrhenius form
Γ∼Γ0​e−F∗/(kB​T).
(45)
For a volume containing order one candidate subcritical droplet, the waiting time for a supercritical droplet and subsequent macroscopic growth is therefore
τ∼τ0​eF∗/(kB​T),​
(46)
up to an algebraic kinetic prefactor. This is the Arrhenius nucleation time.
