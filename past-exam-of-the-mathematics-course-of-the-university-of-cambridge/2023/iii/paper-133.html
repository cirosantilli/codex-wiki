Set
f(t)=d(x,γ(t)).
(29)
The triangle inequality makes f Lipschitz continuous and hence a continuous function. Since γ is an isometric embedding,
∣t∣=d(γ(t),γ(0))≤f(t)+f(0),
(30)
so f(t)→∞ as ∣t∣→∞. The function is therefore coercive, and the extreme value theorem on a sufficiently large compact interval gives a minimizing parameter.
Suppose t1​,t2​ both minimize f, put p=γ(t1​) and q=γ(t2​), and let
R=d(x,p)=d(x,q),L=d(p,q)=∣t1​−t2​∣.
(31)
The restriction of γ between the two parameters is a geodesic from p to q. Let m be its midpoint. In the geodesic triangle with vertices x,p,q, the thin geodesic triangle condition gives a point y on one of the other two sides with d(m,y)≤δ. By symmetry suppose y∈[x,p]. Then
d(p,y)≥d(p,m)−d(m,y)≥L/2−δ,
(32)
so
d(x,m)≤d(x,y)+δ=R−d(p,y)+δ≤R−L/2+2δ.
(33)
But m lies on γ(R) and p is a closest point, so R≤d(x,m). Therefore L≤4δ, which is stronger than the required
∣t1​−t2​∣≤6δ.
(34)
This is the closest point on a geodesic line in a hyperbolic metric space estimate.

The defining formulas imply the matching-distance identities
d(z,p)=d(z,r),d(x,p)=d(x,q),d(y,q)=d(y,r).
(35)
For example,
d(z,r)=d(y,z)−d(y,r)=2d(x,z)+d(y,z)−d(x,y)​=d(z,p),
(36)
and the other two follow cyclically. Thus p,q,r are the tripod points of a geodesic triangle.
By the thin geodesic triangle condition, p lies within δ of some point p′ on [x,y] or [y,z]. If p′∈[x,y], then the triangle inequality gives
∣d(x,p′)−d(x,p)∣≤δ.
(37)
Since q is the point of [x,y] at distance d(x,p) from x, the geodesic parametrization gives d(p′,q)≤δ, and hence d(p,q)≤2δ. If instead p′∈[y,z], comparison of distances from z gives d(p,r)≤2δ.
Applying the same argument cyclically, each of p,q,r lies within 2δ of at least one of the other two. The graph on these three points whose edges join pairs at distance at most 2δ therefore has no isolated vertex, so it is connected. Any two vertices are joined by at most two edges, and the triangle inequality yields
d(p,q), d(q,r), d(r,p)≤4δ.​
(38)
