Three distinguished limits are needed.
In the origin layer, put x=εX and y(x;ε)=Y(X). Since sech(εX)=1+O(ε2), the leading equation is
XY′′+(X+2)Y′+Y=0.
(34)
It has the first integral
XY′+(X+1)Y=C.
(35)
Solving this first-order linear differential equation gives
Y=XC+De−X​.
(36)
Regularity at X=0 requires D=−C, and Y(0)=1 then fixes C=1. Thus Region I, x=O(ε), has
Y(X)=X1−e−X​.​
(37)
Its matching limit is Y∼X−1 as X→∞.
In Region II, x=O(1) with x≫ε, setting ε=0 reduces the second-order equation to
xy0′​+(x+1)y0​=0.
(38)
Hence y0​=Ce−x/x. Matching C/x with the inner limit ε/x fixes C=ε, so
y∼xε​e−x.​
(39)
The correction generated by εx3y changes the local decay rate when x=O(ε−1/2). For Region III set
Z=ε​x,y=e−F(Z)/ε​G(Z).
(40)
The terms of order ε−1/2 give the eikonal equation
F′(Z)=1+Z2,
(41)
and the next balance gives ZG′+G=0. Matching to Region II selects
F(Z)=Z+3Z3​,G(Z)=Zε3/2​.
(42)
Therefore
y∼Zε3/2​exp[−ε​Z+Z3/3​]​(x=O(ε−1/2)).
(43)
This solution is already beyond all algebraic orders in ε in the distant region. A leading multiplicative composite expansion that contains all three balances and satisfies the boundary value exactly is
ycomp​(x;ε)=xε​(1−e−x/ε)exp(−x−3εx3​),​
(44)
with its value at x=0 understood by continuity.
Finally, the coefficient of y′′ vanishes at the origin. Taking the regular limit of the original equation there gives
2εy′(0)+y(0)=0,
(45)
so the prescribed value also fixes y′(0)=−1/(2ε). Equivalently, the second inner solution behaves as 1/X and is excluded by regularity. This regular singular point is why one boundary condition determines the unique regular solution.
