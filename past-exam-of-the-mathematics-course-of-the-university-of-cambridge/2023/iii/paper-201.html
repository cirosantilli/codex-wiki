Continuity on the compact interval ensures that a maximum time exists. Fix a rational r∈(0,1) and define
Ur​=max0≤t≤r​Bt​−Br​,Vr​=maxr≤t≤1​(Bt​−Br​).

Start Brownian motions at arbitrary x,y∈Rd. Use the successive meeting times from part b, but after coordinate i meets, drive that coordinate of the second process with the first process's increments forever. Because f is bounded and harmonic, Dynkin formula for Brownian motion shows that f(Btx​) and f(Bty​) are bounded martingales. Therefore
