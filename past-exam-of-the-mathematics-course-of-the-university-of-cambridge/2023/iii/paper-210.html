For a cumulative distribution function F, its quantile function is the generalized inverse
F−1(u)=inf{x∈R:F(x)≥u},0<u≤1,
(1)
with the infimum allowed to be +∞. For observations X1​,…,Xn​, the empirical distribution function is
Fn​(x)=n1​∑i=1n​1{Xi​≤x}​.
(2)
If X(1)​≤⋯≤X(n)​ are the order statistics, then
X(j)​=Fn−1​(j/n).
(3)
The Bennett inequality says that if Y1​,…,YN​ are independent, EYi​=0, Yi​≤b almost surely, and ∑i​EYi2​≤v, then
P(∑i​Yi​≥t)≤exp[−b2v​h(vbt​)],h(u)=(1+u)log(1+u)−u.
(4)
To prove it, convexity of eλy on y≤b, followed by the power-series bound for centered Yi​, gives
EeλYi​≤exp{b2EYi2​​(eλb−1−λb)}.
(5)
Independence and the Chernoff bound therefore yield
P(∑i​Yi​≥t)≤exp{−λt+b2v​(eλb−1−λb)}.
(6)
The minimizing value satisfies eλb=1+bt/v, namely λ=b−1log(1+bt/v). Substitution gives the stated exponent.
For independent Ui​∼Unif[0,1], the uniform order statistic satisfies
U(j)​∼Beta(j,n−j+1),EU(j)​=n+1j​.
(7)
Set p=j/(n+1)−x. The event U(j)​≤p means that at least j sample points lie in [0,p]. If this occurs, some j-element subset consists entirely of such points. The union bound gives
P(U(j)​≤p)≤(jn​)pj≤(jenp​)j.
(8)
This is exactly the required inequality for 0≤x<j/(n+1).

Put r=⌈β⌉−1 and α=β−r∈(0,1]. The density Hölder class F(β,L) consists of nonnegative functions f integrating to one, with derivatives through order r, such that
∣f(r)(x)−f(r)(y)∣≤L∣x−y∣α.
(9)
This is the density version of the Hölder class. A kernel for density estimation is an integrable function K with ∫K=1. It has order ℓ when ∫ujK(u)du=0 for 1≤j<ℓ and ∫∣u∣ℓ∣K(u)∣du<∞.
Choose a bounded kernel of order at least ⌈β⌉ with ∫∣u∣β∣K(u)∣du<∞, and use the kernel density estimator
f​h​(x)=nh1​∑i=1n​K(hx−Xi​​).
(10)
Taylor's theorem at x and the vanishing kernel moments cancel every polynomial term below the remainder. Hence, for a constant depending only on β and the fixed kernel,
∣Ef​f​h​(x)−f(x)∣≤Cβ​Lhβ.
(11)
We also need a uniform density bound. The standard Hölder interpolation argument combines nonnegativity, ∫f=1, and the Hölder constraint to give
∥f∥∞​≤Cβ​L1/(β+1).
(12)
Indeed, near a point where f is close to its maximum M, Taylor's theorem and the derivative bounds implied by the Hölder constraint keep f of order M on an interval of length comparable to (M/L)1/β; integrating over that interval gives M1+1/β≤Cβ​L1/β.

The total variation distance and Kullback-Leibler divergence are
TV(P,Q)=supA​∣P(A)−Q(A)∣,KL(P,Q)=∫log(dQdP​)dP,
(29)
with the divergence +∞ when P is not absolutely continuous with respect to Q.
For P=Laplace(0) and Q=Laplace(μ),
logdQdP​(x)=∣x−μ∣−∣x∣.
(30)
If X∼P, direct integration on the intervals cut by 0 and μ gives
E∣X−μ∣=∣μ∣+e−∣μ∣,E∣X∣=1.
(31)
Consequently
KL(P,Q)=e−∣μ∣−1+∣μ∣.
(32)
One squared-loss form of Assouad's lemma is as follows. Suppose (Pω​,θω​)ω∈{0,1}k​ is an Assouad hypercube such that
d(θω​,θω′​)2≥adH​(ω,ω′)
(33)
and every pair of neighboring vertices satisfies TV(Pω​,Pω′​)≤η. Then
infθ​supω​Eω​d(θ,θω​)2≥8ak​(1−η).
(34)
To prove it, decode from θ a nearest hypercube vertex. The separation condition converts estimation loss into a constant multiple of its Hamming error. Average this error under the uniform prior on ω and sum coordinatewise. For each coordinate, the two equally weighted mixtures with that bit equal to zero or one have total variation at most η by convexity. The minimum average error of any binary test is (1−TV)/2, which gives the displayed bound after the nearest-vertex factor.
We now build such a hypercube inside the monotone cone. Let k=⌊c0​n1/3⌋ and m=⌊n/k⌋. Divide the first km coordinates into k consecutive blocks. For ω∈{0,1}k, set on block j
θω,i​=2δ(j−1)+δωj​,δ=m​c1​​,
(35)
and extend the remaining coordinates at the last level. If c0​,c1​ are sufficiently small universal constants, every signal is nondecreasing and belongs to [0,1]n.
Neighboring vertices differ by δ on one block, so their squared Euclidean separation is mδ2. Their product experiments differ only on that block and have
KL(Pω​,Pω′​)=m(e−δ−1+δ)≤2mδ2​=2c12​​.
(36)
By Pinsker's inequality, choosing c1​ small makes every neighboring total variation distance at most, say, 1/4.
Apply Assouad's lemma with normalized squared loss d2(θ,θ′)=n−1∥θ−θ′∥2. Here a=mδ2/n=c12​/n, and hence
infθ​supθ∈Mn​∩[0,1]n​n1​Eθ​∥θ−θ∥2≥cnk​≥c′n−2/3.
(37)
This proves the lower half of the minimax rate for isotonic sequence estimation.
