For the free presentation G=F/R, conjugation in F gives the relation module
Rab​=R/[R,R],g⋅(r[R,R])=frf−1[R,R],
(6)
where f∈F is any lift of g∈G. A different lift differs by an element of R, whose inner conjugation acts trivially on the abelianization, so this is a well-defined ZG-module action.
Choose free generators x1​,…,xn​ of F. The presentation relation sequence becomes
0⟶Rab​j​(ZG)nd1​​ZGε​Z⟶0,
(7)
where d1​(ei​)=xˉi​−1. In Fox calculus, the first map is
j(r[R,R])=∑i=1n​∂xi​∂r​​ei​.
(8)
The Fox identity r−1=∑i​(∂r/∂xi​)(xi​−1) gives d1​j=0, and the standard lifting argument in the free group proves exactness.
Split the sequence at the augmentation ideal IG​. Applying HomZG​(−,M) to
0⟶Rab​⟶(ZG)n⟶IG​⟶0
(9)
and using projectivity of (ZG)n gives
Mn⟶HomG​(Rab​,M)⟶ExtZG1​(IG​,M)⟶0.
(10)
The other short exact sequence 0→IG​→ZG→Z→0 identifies the last group with H2(G,M). A one-cocycle on F is a derivation and is determined freely by its values on x1​,…,xn​, so H1(F,M) is Mn modulo principal derivations. Principal derivations vanish on R, and restriction sends a derivation d to the G-map r[R,R]↦d(r). The preceding cokernel sequence therefore descends to the Mac Lane exact sequence for a free presentation
H1(F,M)⟶HomG​(Rab​,M)⟶H2(G,M)⟶0.
(11)
The left map need not be injective. Take F=⟨t⟩≅Z, R=⟨tm⟩, G≅Cm​, and the trivial module M=Z/mZ, where m>1. Then H1(F,M)≅M, while restriction sends the derivation determined by d(t)=a to
d(tm)=ma=0.
(12)
Thus the left map is zero although its domain is nonzero, and R is nontrivial.
