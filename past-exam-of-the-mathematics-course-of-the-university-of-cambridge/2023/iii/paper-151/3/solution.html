The Artin–Wedderburn theorem says that every central simple algebra A over k has the form Mr​(D) for a finite-dimensional central division algebra D, uniquely up to the evident data. If A and B are central simple, extend scalars to an algebraic closure kˉ. Both become full matrix algebras, hence
(A⊗k​B)⊗k​kˉ≅Mab​(kˉ)
(13)
for suitable a,b. Any nonzero proper ideal of A⊗k​B would extend to one in this simple matrix algebra, and faithful flatness prevents it from vanishing or becoming the whole algebra. The same scalar-extension argument shows that the center is k. This proves the tensor product of central simple algebras theorem.
The Brauer group Br(k) consists of Morita equivalence classes of central simple k-algebras. Its product is [A][B]=[A⊗k​B], its identity is [k], and [A]−1=[Aop] because A⊗k​Aop≅Endk​(A) is a full matrix algebra.

A(L,GL​,ϕ)=⨁σ∈GL​​Luσ​
(14)
and multiplication
uσ​a=σ(a)uσ​,uσ​uτ​=ϕ(σ,τ)uστ​.
(15)
The cocycle identity is exactly associativity. After scalar extension to L, the algebra acts by the twisted regular representation and becomes M∣GL​∣​(L); Galois descent shows that it is central simple over k. If ϕ is multiplied by the coboundary of a one-cochain bσ​, rescaling uσ​ by bσ​ gives an isomorphic algebra. Hence the cohomological construction of a Brauer class gives a well-defined map
H2(GL​,L×)⟶Br(k).
(16)
It remains to show that every Brauer class is torsion. For a finite group G, restriction and corestriction on normalized bar cochains satisfy
cor∘res=∑g∈G​g∗=∣G∣
(17)
on cohomology: the first equality follows by summing the translated cochain over coset representatives, and each g∗ is the identity because an inner automorphism is cochain-homotopic to the identity. Restriction to the trivial subgroup is zero in positive degree, so this proves that finite-group cohomology is annihilated by the group order. In multiplicative notation, every α∈H2(GL​,L×) therefore satisfies α∣GL​∣=1.
By the permitted assumption, [A] is the image of such an α for some L. Consequently [A]∣GL​∣=1 in Br(k). By the definition of Brauer equivalence, this says that for some n,
