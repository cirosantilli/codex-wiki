A portfolio H∈Rn is an arbitrage when H⋅P0​≤0, H⋅P1​≥0 almost surely, and at least one inequality supplies a strict gain: either H⋅P0​<0 or P(H⋅P1​>0)>0. It is a terminal-consumption arbitrage when H⋅P0​=0, H⋅P1​≥0 almost surely, and the terminal inequality is strict with positive probability.

A numéraire portfolio η satisfies η⋅P0​>0 and η⋅P1​>0 almost surely. If an arbitrage H already has zero initial cost, it is a terminal-consumption arbitrage. Otherwise H⋅P0​<0; set

For a numéraire portfolio η, the normal random variable η⋅P1​ is strictly positive almost surely. A nondegenerate normal variable has support on all of R, so it must be degenerate: ηTVη=0, equivalently Vη=0. Thus η⋅P1​=η⋅μ>0 deterministically. The scaled portfolio
B=η⋅μη​

The bond pays one at maturity. If τ=inf{t<T:PtT​≤0} had positive probability of being finite, buy one bond at τ. A negative price supplies immediate consumption and a positive terminal payoff; a zero price supplies a free positive terminal payoff. Trading only on the stopping event gives an arbitrage. Therefore PtT​>0 almost surely for every t<T.
