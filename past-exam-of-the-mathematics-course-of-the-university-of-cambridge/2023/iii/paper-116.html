Write [f] for the equivalence class of f:κ→Vλ​ in the ultrapower
N=(Vλ​)κ/U,
(1)
and define its membership relation by
[g]E[f]⟺{ξ<κ:g(ξ)∈f(ξ)}∈U.
(2)
The kappa-complete filter property makes E well-founded: an infinite descending E-chain would give countably many members of U whose intersection belongs to U, and every index in that intersection would yield an infinite descending membership chain, contradicting the Axiom of foundation. The relation is extensional by Łoś's theorem.
The Mostowski collapse theorem therefore gives a unique isomorphism π:(N,E)→(M,∈) onto a transitive set M. Recursively, the notation missing from the printed formula may be defined by
π([f])={π([g]):[g]E[f]}.
(3)
The value is independent of the representative because it is defined on the ultrapower class [f]. Moreover M⊆Vλ​: every f:κ→Vλ​ has its range contained in some Vα​ with α<λ, since κ<λ and the strongly inaccessible cardinal λ is regular; induction on the resulting rank bound keeps π([f]) inside Vλ​.
Define the ultrapower embedding
j:Vλ​⟶M,j(x)=π([constx​]).
(4)
The constant-function map into N is elementary by Łoś's theorem, and π is an isomorphism, so their composite j is elementary.

The critical point of an elementary embedding j is the least ordinal α for which j(α)=α.
We first prove by transfinite induction that j(α)=α for every α<κ. Suppose this is known below α<κ. If [f]E[constα​], then
A={ξ<κ:f(ξ)<α}∈U.
(5)
The sets Aβ​={ξ∈A:f(ξ)=β} for β<α partition A into fewer than κ pieces. A kappa-complete filter that is an ultrafilter must contain one cell Aβ​: otherwise all their complements would belong to U, and their intersection would contradict A∈U. Hence [f]=[constβ​]. The predecessors of j(α) are consequently exactly the already-fixed ordinals below α, so j(α)=α.
Now let id(ξ)=ξ. An identical argument shows that the predecessors of [id] in the ultrapower are exactly [constβ​] for β<κ, so
π([id])=κ.
(6)
Since {ξ<κ:ξ<κ}=κ∈U, one has [id]E[constκ​]. After collapsing, κ∈j(κ), and therefore j(κ)>κ. Thus crit(j)=κ.

A property P of κ reflects below κ when
{μ<κ:P(μ)}
(7)
is unbounded in κ. The standard elementary-embedding reflection argument starts with any γ<κ. Since j(γ)=γ and j(κ)>κ, the target model can use κ itself as a witness to
∃μ(γ<μ<j(κ)∧P(μ)).
(8)
Elementarity then gives a witness μ with γ<μ<κ in the domain.
For a concrete example, the property of being a strongly inaccessible cardinal reflects below κ. A measurable cardinal is strongly inaccessible, and the assumed inclusion Vκ+1​⊆M makes this assertion about κ absolute between Vλ​ and M: both models have all subsets of every ordinal below κ. Thus M sees that κ is strongly inaccessible. Given γ<κ, it therefore satisfies
∃μ(γ<μ<j(κ)∧μ is strongly inaccessible).
(9)
Elementarity supplies a strongly inaccessible μ between γ and κ in Vλ​. As γ was arbitrary, the strongly inaccessible cardinals below κ are unbounded.

An strongly inaccessible cardinal κ has the Keisler extension property when there is a proper transitive set X⊋Vκ​ such that
(Vκ​,∈)≺(X,∈).
(10)
Suppose κ is strongly inaccessible and has this property. Because X properly extends the transitive set Vκ​, it contains κ. Strong inaccessibility of κ is downward absolute from the ambient universe to the transitive set X: any internal witness that κ is countable, singular, or not a strong limit would also be an ambient witness. Hence
X⊨“there exists a strongly inaccessible cardinal”,
(11)
with κ as a witness. Since Vκ​≺X, the same sentence holds in Vκ​. Its witness is an ordinal μ<κ. The set Vκ​ contains Vμ+1​, so it computes all subsets of cardinals below μ correctly; strong inaccessibility of μ is therefore absolute between Vκ​ and the universe. Thus there is a strongly inaccessible μ<κ, and κ cannot be the least strongly inaccessible cardinal.

For a first-order theory R extending ZFC, let CR​ be its set of formal consequences and let Cons denote the class of formal consistency statements Con(Q) for recursively axiomatized extensions Q of ZFC. Using Gödel numbering to code proofs and theories, these objects and the following comparison are definable in the base theory ZFC.
The consistency-strength preorder is
T≤Cons​S⟺Cons∩CT​⊆Cons∩CS​.
(12)
Thus every consistency assertion provable in T is also provable in S. Its strict part is
T<Cons​S⟺T≤Cons​S ∧ ¬(S≤Cons​T).​
(13)

b OurBigBook.com
Let IC be the sentence asserting that a strongly inaccessible cardinal exists, and begin with
T0​=ZFC+IC.
(14)
Define the iterated consistency progression
Tn+1​=Tn​+Con(Tn​),T∞​=⋃n<ω​Tn​.
(15)
The construction is effective, so every Tn​ and T∞​ is a recursively axiomatized first-order theory extending ZFC.
Because Tn+1​ extends Tn​, every theorem of Tn​, including every formal consistency statement it proves, is a theorem of Tn+1​; hence Tn​≤Cons​Tn+1​. The theory Tn+1​ proves Con(Tn​) by construction, whereas a consistent Tn​ cannot prove its own consistency by Gödel second incompleteness theorem. Therefore
Tn​<Cons​Tn+1​.
(16)
Likewise T∞​ extends every Tn​ and contains Con(Tn​) as an axiom already at stage n+1, while Tn​ does not prove it. Consequently
T0​<Cons​T1​<Cons​T2​<Cons​⋯<Cons​T∞​,
(17)
assuming the stated consistency hypotheses.

Since the strongly inaccessible cardinal λ is inaccessible, (Vλ​,∈) is a model of ZFC. The Downward Lowenheim-Skolem theorem gives an elementary substructure
X≺Vλ​
(18)
of cardinality κ such that
Vκ​∪{κ,U}⊆X.
(19)
One may obtain X concretely as the Skolem hull of this set; its cardinality remains κ because the language of set theory is countable and ∣Vκ​∣=κ.
Apply the Mostowski collapse theorem to X and write π:X→M for the collapse. Then M is a transitive set, ∣M∣=κ, and π fixes Vκ​ pointwise. It also fixes κ, because it fixes every ordinal below κ. By elementarity, X satisfies ZFC and regards U as a kappa-complete filter that is a nonprincipal ultrafilter on κ. Therefore, with Uˉ=π(U),
(M,∈)⊨ZFC+“κ is a measurable cardinal, witnessed by Uˉ.”
(20)
The internal ultrafilter Uˉ need not equal the original U.

α=(κ+)M,
(21)
the successor cardinal of κ computed by M. Then α∈M, and M regards α as a cardinal number. Because M is transitive, α⊆M, so externally
∣α∣≤∣M∣=κ.
(22)

Yes. Start with the model M0​ constructed in part a. If M0​ has no internally strongly inaccessible cardinal above κ, put M=M0​. Otherwise let δ be the least ordinal above κ that M0​ regards as strongly inaccessible, and put
M=(Vδ​)M0​.
(23)
In the second case M⊨ZFC because M0​ regards δ as inaccessible. The measure witnessing that κ is measurable has rank below κ+3<δ, so it still belongs to M. In both cases M is a transitive set of cardinality κ, contains Vκ​, and has no internally inaccessible ordinal strictly between κ and its height.
We verify absoluteness for every ordinal α∈M. If α<κ, then M and Vλ​ both contain Vα+1​ and therefore compute all subsets and functions relevant to strong inaccessibility in the same way. At α=κ, both models see a measurable cardinal and hence an inaccessible cardinal. Finally, if κ<α∈M, then M says that α is not inaccessible by construction. The larger model Vλ​ cannot say that it is inaccessible, because strong inaccessibility is downward absolute to a transitive model of ZFC: any failure visible in the smaller model remains a failure in the larger one, while ambient inaccessibility would force internal inaccessibility. Hence “α is inaccessible” is absolute between M and Vλ​.
