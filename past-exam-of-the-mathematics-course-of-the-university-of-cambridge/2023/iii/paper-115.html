A submersion is a smooth map between manifolds F:Xn→Ym for which
Dp​F:Tp​X⟶TF(p)​Y
(1)
is surjective at every p∈X.
The local submersion theorem says that around every p∈X there are coordinates (x1,…,xn) centered at p and (y1,…,ym) centered at F(p) in which
F(x1,…,xn)=(x1,…,xm).
(2)
To prove it, surjectivity lets us choose m source coordinates such that dF1,…,dFm are independent. Complete them by source coordinates xm+1,…,xn and define
G=(F1,…,Fm,xm+1,…,xn).
(3)
The derivative of G is invertible at p, so the inverse function theorem makes G a local coordinate system; in these coordinates F is the displayed projection.
For q∈Y, the fiber is locally given by
x1=q1,…,xm=qm.
(4)
These are slice coordinates, so F−1(q) is an embedded submanifold of dimension n−m.

Fix q1​ and choose U as in part c. For q2​∈U, choose the compactly supported w moving q1​ to q2​, and lift it by part b to v. The support of v is contained in
F−1(suppw),
(9)

dtd​F(Φt(p))=DΦt(p)​F(v)=w(F(Φt(p))).
(10)
Uniqueness of integral curves gives
F∘Φt=Ψt∘F.
(11)

If Y is connected, there is only one class. This proves the fiber-diffeomorphism conclusion of the Ehresmann fibration theorem.

We induct on n. The claim is immediate for CP0. In the stated cover, U=U0​≅Cn is contractible. The homotopy
[z0​:z1​:⋯:zn​]⟼[tz0​:z1​:⋯:zn​]

In a local frame of E, a connection on a vector bundle has the form
dA=d+A∧,
(28)
where A is a matrix of one-forms. Under a frame change g, its matrix transforms as
A′=g−1Ag+g−1dg.
(29)
The covariant exterior derivative on an E-valued r-form σ is
dAσ=dσ+A∧σ.
(30)
The curvature form of a connection is
F=dA+A∧A.
(31)

(dA)2σ=d(A∧σ)+A∧dσ+A∧A∧σ=(dA+A∧A)∧σ=F∧σ.
(32)

Regard an E∨-valued r-form λ as a row vector and an End(E)-valued r-form μ as a matrix. The dual connection and endomorphism connection are
dA∨λ=dλ−(−1)rλ∧A,
(33)
dEnd(A)μ=dμ+A∧μ−(−1)rμ∧A.
(34)
For the curvature two-form,
dEnd(A)F=dF+A∧F−F∧A=0,
(35)
because substituting F=dA+A∧A makes all terms cancel. This is the Bianchi identity.
For an endomorphism-valued r-form μ and an E-valued form σ, direct expansion gives the compatible Leibniz rule
dA(μ∧σ)=dEnd(A)μ∧σ+(−1)rμ∧dAσ.
(36)
The cancellation of the two middle A-terms proves the identity.
