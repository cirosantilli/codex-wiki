Landau theory treats the order parameter as spatially uniform and expands the free-energy density in powers allowed by its symmetries. The Landau-Ginzburg theory promotes it to a field ϕ(x) and adds gradient terms such as ∣∇ϕ∣2. It therefore describes spatial fluctuations, interfaces, defects, and correlation lengths, while reducing to Landau theory for uniform fields.

A momentum-shell renormalization group step has three parts:
1. Split the field into slow modes ϕ− with ∣k∣<Λ/ζ and fast modes ϕ+ with Λ/ζ<∣k∣<Λ, then integrate out ϕ+ to obtain a Wilsonian effective action for ϕ−.
2. Rescale momenta by k′=ζk, equivalently coordinates by x′=x/ζ, so the reduced cutoff returns from Λ/ζ to Λ.
3. Rescale the field, at the Gaussian fixed point by ϕ′(x′)=ζ(d−2)/2ϕ−(x), so the coefficient of (∇ϕ)2/2 retains its chosen normalization.
The resulting functional has the original cutoff but changed coefficients. Iterating the operation gives a renormalization-group flow on masses and interaction couplings.

At the Gaussian fixed point, the engineering dimension of the field is
[ϕ]=2d−2​.
(13)
The operator ϕn(∇2ϕ)m contains n+m fields and 2m derivatives. Since its integral must be dimensionless,
[gn,m​]=d−2(n+m)(d−2)​−2m.
(14)
It is marginal when this vanishes, namely
d=n+m−22(n−m)​,
(15)
when n+m=2. Since n,m are positive, the exceptional case is n=m=1: ϕ∇2ϕ has a dimensionless coupling in every d and differs from the kinetic term by integration by parts. If the displayed formula gives no positive d, there is no positive spatial dimension in which that operator is naively marginal.
At an interacting fixed point, field and composite operators acquire anomalous dimensions, and operators with the same symmetries can mix under renormalization. Their full scaling dimensions can therefore differ from these naive engineering dimensions.

Use two external slow-field legs and internal fast-mode propagators. Through the requested orders, the connected mass-correction topologies are:
  • order g0​: one quartic vertex with one fast tadpole;
  • order g02​: two quartic vertices joined either by three fast lines, or by two fast lines with a fast tadpole on the vertex carrying no external legs;
  • order λ0​: one sextic vertex with two fast tadpole loops;
  • order λ0​g0​: a sextic and a quartic vertex joined by two fast lines, with the remaining fast legs closed into tadpoles in the two possible external-leg placements; joined by four fast lines with two external legs on the sextic vertex; or joined by three fast lines with one sextic tadpole and one external leg on each vertex.
A nominal one-line bridge at order λ0​g0​ vanishes in a sharp momentum-shell scheme at small external momentum because that line would have to carry momentum outside the fast shell. These descriptions specify the same diagrams without depending on a particular drawing convention for vertices and external legs.

For a uniform field with r2=ϕ⋅ϕ, the potential is
V(r)=21​μ2r2+gr4.
(23)
For T>Tc​, one has μ2>0, so ϕ0​=0. The Hessian has N positive equal eigenvalues, and all N modes are gapped. The full O(N) symmetry is unbroken.
For T<Tc​, one has μ2<0, and
∣ϕ0​∣2=−4gμ2​.
(24)
The vacuum manifold is SN−1. Choosing one point breaks O(N) spontaneously to O(N−1). The radial fluctuation has squared mass −2μ2, while the N−1 tangent directions are gapless Nambu-Goldstone bosons, one for each broken continuous generator modulo the unbroken subgroup.

For the O(2) model write
ϕ=(v+ρ)(cosθ,sinθ),v2=−4gμ2​.
(25)
At long distances the massive radial field ρ can be neglected, leaving the Goldstone-mode effective free energy
Fθ​=2γv2​∫ddx(∇θ)2.
(26)
Therefore
⟨θ(x)θ(y)⟩=γv21​∫(2π)dddk​k2e−ik⋅(x−y)​.
(27)
The mode is massless, so its correlation length is infinite. For d>2 its large-distance Green function is
⟨θ(x)θ(y)⟩∼4πd/2γv2Γ(d/2−1)​r2−d.
(28)
For d=2 it is −(2πγv2)−1log(r/a) up to an infrared-dependent constant, and in d=1 it is −∣r∣/(2γv2) up to such a constant.
The invariant diagnostic is the phase-difference variance
⟨[θ(r)−θ(0)]2⟩=γv22​∫(2π)dddk​k21−cos(k⋅r)​.
(29)

The lower critical dimension is dlc​=2; the two-dimensional O(2) model can instead show quasi-long-range order below a BKT transition.
