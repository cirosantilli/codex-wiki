Under spatial reflection, a scalar field obeys Pϕ(x)P=ϕ(−x), and hence each spatial derivative changes sign. The interaction contains three such derivatives, so changing the integration variable from x to −x gives
PHint​P=−Hint​.
(1)
Thus this is a parity-odd scalar interaction.
Put O(ka​)=∏a=1n​ϕa​(ka​) and A(ka​)=⟨Hint​O(ka​)⟩. Hermitian conjugation and commutativity of equal-time scalar fields give
⟨O(ka​)Hint​⟩=A(−ka​)∗.
(2)
The parity-invariant vacuum and the odd parity of Hint​ imply A(−ka​)=−A(ka​). Therefore
⟨[Hint​,O]⟩=A−A(−ka​)∗=A+A∗=2ReA​.
(3)
There is no conflict with the usual 2iImA rule for two Hermitian operators: a momentum-space product at fixed ka​ is generally not itself Hermitian, since its adjoint carries momenta −ka​.

Statistical homogeneity forces a scalar two-point function to have momenta (k,−k). Statistical isotropy then makes it a function only of ∣k∣, so it is parity even without using perturbation theory.
For a scalar three-point function, momentum conservation gives k1​+k2​+k3​=0, so all three vectors lie in one plane. A rotation by π around the normal to that plane sends every ka​ to −ka​. Rotational invariance therefore identifies a triangle with its parity reverse, proving nonperturbatively that the scalar primordial bispectrum is parity even.
A rotationally invariant local parity-odd three-scalar vertex must contain a Levi-Civita symbol contracted with three spatial momenta. Its momentum-space factor is proportional to
ϵijk​k1i​k2j​k3k​=k1​⋅(k2​×k3​)=0,
(17)
because momentum conservation makes the momenta linearly dependent. The equivalent position-space expression is a total divergence; its spatial integral vanishes under the stated vanishing boundary condition. Thus a parity-odd interaction of three scalar fields contributes nothing to the action.

The background spatial metric is gij​=a2δij​. For the large spatial diffeomorphism
ϵ0=0,ϵi=ωij​xj,ωij​=ωji​,ωi​i=0,
(18)
homogeneity and isotropy imply that the purely spatial background Christoffel symbols vanish. Hence
∇i​ϵj​=a2ωji​,Δγij​=−2ωij​​.
(19)
The shift is constant, transverse and traceless, and is therefore the zero-momentum adiabatic tensor mode.
A scalar transforms by its Lie derivative:
Δφ(x)=−ωij​xj∂i​φ(x).
(20)
Fourier transformation and integration by parts in momentum space give
Δφ(k)=ωij​ki​∂kj​∂​φ(k)​.
(21)
The term proportional to ωi​i vanishes because the deformation is traceless.

Let the photon phase-space distribution be a Planck distribution whose local temperature is T(η)[1+Θ(η,x,p​)]. In the absence of collisions, Liouville theorem says that the distribution is constant along a photon null geodesic. Linearizing df/dη=0 about the homogeneous distribution and using dx/dη=p​ gives
(∂η​+p​⋅∇)Θ−dηdlogϵ​=0.
(33)
A spatial Fourier transform sends p​⋅∇ to ip​⋅k, so
∂η∂Θ​+i(p​⋅k)Θ−dηdlogϵ​=0​.
(34)
This is the Free-streaming photon Boltzmann equation: the second term transports angular structure, while the last term is the gravitational redshift source.

The line-of-sight solution for free-streaming photons follows the unperturbed ray x(η)=x0​−p​(ηf​−η). If Φ=Ψ is spatially homogeneous, its gradient vanishes and dlogϵ/dη=Φ′. Integration along the ray gives
Θ(ηf​,x0​,p​)=Θ(ηi​,x0​−p​(ηf​−ηi​),p​)+Φ(ηf​)−Φ(ηi​)​.
(40)
The needed initial condition is the temperature pattern on the initial hypersurface; for a superhorizon adiabatic mode during matter domination one may use Θ0​(ηi​)=−2Ψ(ηi​)/3.
This calculation contains free streaming of the initial pattern and the gravitational temperature shift caused by the evolving potential, the homogeneous limit of the Integrated Sachs-Wolfe effect. A perfectly homogeneous potential changes only the unobservable sky monopole and therefore contributes no CMB angular power spectrum for ℓ≥1. Direction-dependent Sachs-Wolfe effect, Doppler, acoustic, polarization, lensing, and rescattering contributions require spatially varying perturbations or collision terms and are outside this special calculation.

Linearizing the cosmological Euler equation gives
v′+Hv=−∇ϕ.
(41)

In standard perturbation theory in cosmology, δ(n) is an n-leg vertex carrying the symmetrized standard perturbation theory density kernel Fn​. For Gaussian random fields, a connected four-point diagram with L loops contains 3+L linear power spectra, hence 6+2L linear fields.
At tree level the required perturbative-order partitions are
3+1+1+1,2+2+1+1.
(49)
The first is a star with one F3​ vertex and three linear external legs. The second has two F2​ vertices joined by one internal power-spectrum line, with one linear external leg attached to each vertex. At one loop all five partitions of eight into four positive parts are required:
5+1+1+1,4+2+1+1,3+3+1+1,3+2+2+1,2+2+2+2.
(50)
These are conventionally denoted T5111​, T4211​, T3311​, T3221​, and T2222​; all inequivalent placements of external legs and all Wick contractions are understood. This list is the complete set of one-loop matter trispectrum topologies.
Let Pa​=PL​(ka​) and kab​=ka​+kb​. With ∑a​ka​=0, the star contribution is
T3111​=6d=1∑4​F3​(−ka​,−kb​,−kc​)Pa​Pb​Pc​​,
(51)
where {a,b,c}={1,2,3,4}∖{d}. For the exchange contribution, sum over the six unordered choices {a,b} of linear external legs and let {c,d} be the complementary pair:
T2211​=4{a,b}∑​Pa​Pb​[PL​(kac​)F2​(−ka​,kac​)F2​(−kb​,−kac​)+PL​(kad​)F2​(−ka​,kad​)F2​(−kb​,−kad​)]​.
(52)
Thus the connected tree trispectrum is Ttree​=T3111​+T2211​.
The pressureless single-stream equations cease to be a complete one-loop prediction because loop momenta probe short nonlinear scales and produce ultraviolet-sensitive terms. A consistent result must include the effective field theory of large-scale structure: effective-stress counterterms, including their insertions into the tree topologies, and stochastic terms at the order allowed by mass conservation and momentum conservation. Their coefficients absorb short-scale dependence and must be fitted or matched. If the observable is a biased tracer rather than matter itself, the corresponding renormalized bias expansion is also required.
