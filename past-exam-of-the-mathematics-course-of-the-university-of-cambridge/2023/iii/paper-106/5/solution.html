A character of an algebra is a nonzero algebra homomorphism φ:A→C. The character space is
ΦA​={φ:A→C:φ is a character}.
(25)
For a unital algebra, φ(1)=1. Moreover φ(a)∈σA​(a), since applying φ shows that a−φ(a)1 cannot be invertible. In a Banach algebra,
∣φ(a)∣≤r(a)≤∥a∥.
(26)
Thus ∥φ∥≤1, while φ(1)=1 gives equality.
If A is commutative and λ∈σA​(a), the proper ideal generated by a−λ1 lies in a maximal ideal. The quotient by that maximal ideal is C, and its quotient map is a character taking a to λ. Therefore
σA​(a)={φ(a):φ∈ΦA​}.
(27)
Since every Banach-algebra element has nonempty spectrum, ΦA​ is nonempty.
For X=Y⊕Z, let P,Q be the complementary coordinate projections. A character must send each idempotent to zero or one, and P+Q=I forces their values to be different. Choose mutually inverse isomorphisms between Y and Z and regard them as off-diagonal operators S,T on X. Then
ST=P,TS=Q.
(28)
Multiplicativity would give φ(P)=φ(S)φ(T)=φ(T)φ(S)=φ(Q), a contradiction. Hence ΦB(X)​ is empty. This is the absence of characters on an operator algebra with isomorphic complementary summands.
If a=a∗ in a unital C*-algebra, then eita is unitary for real t. If λ∈σ(a), the spectral mapping theorem gives eitλ∈σ(eita), whose modulus is one. Varying positive and negative t forces Imλ=0, so σ(a)⊂R.
If B⊆A is a unital C*-subalgebra and x∈B is normal, spectral permanence holds. Indeed, when x−λ is invertible in A, the positive normal element
(x−λ)∗(x−λ)
(29)
has spectrum bounded away from zero. Continuous functional calculus uniformly approximates its reciprocal by polynomials, placing the reciprocal in B. It follows that (x−λ)−1∈B. Thus σB​(x)=σA​(x).
The Commutative Gelfand--Naimark theorem says that the Gelfand transform is an isometric unital star-isomorphism
A≅C(ΦA​)
(30)
for every commutative unital C*-algebra. If a is positive, continuous functional calculus for the function t↦t​ on σ(a)⊂[0,∞) gives a positive b with b2=a. Pointwise uniqueness in C∗(1,a)≅C(σ(a)) gives the unique positive square root. This is the positive square root in a C*-algebra.
