The Kähler potential K(Φ,Φ†) is a real function integrated over all four fermionic coordinates, ∫d4θK. Its complex Hessian gives the scalar Kähler metric and therefore the kinetic terms. The superpotential W(Φ) is holomorphic and is integrated over chiral superspace, ∫d2θW+h.c.; its derivatives determine Yukawa couplings and the F-term scalar potential.
For the canonical Kähler potential K=Φ†Φ, extracting the θ2θˉ2 component of the supplied chiral-superfield component expansion and integrating by parts gives the bosonic action
∫d4xd4θΦ†Φ=∫d4x(−∂μ​ϕ∗∂μϕ+F∗F),
(1)
in the mostly-plus convention. Extracting the θ2 component of a holomorphic function gives
∫d4xd2θW(Φ)=∫d4x(FW′(ϕ)−21​W′′(ϕ)ψψ),
(2)
so its bosonic term is FW′(ϕ), with the Hermitian conjugate understood in a real action. For several fields, eliminating each algebraic auxiliary field by Fi∗​=−Wi​ produces VF​=∑i​∣Wi​∣2.

Suppose ζ>0 and all mi​ are distinct. A nonzero ϕj​ forces x=mj​. Distinctness then makes every other charged field vanish, and ∑i​ϕ​i​ϕi​=0 forces ϕ​j​=0. The D-flatness equation fixes ∣ϕj​∣2=ζ, while its phase is removed by the gauge group. There is therefore one isolated supersymmetric vacuum for each flavor,
x=mj​,∣ϕj​∣2=ζ,ϕ​j​=0,j=1,…,N​,
(14)
for a total of N vacua. Negative ζ gives the analogous vacua with ϕ​j​ nonzero.

For ζ=0 and distinct masses, any nonzero charged field can occur only at one x=mj​. At such a point, D-flatness gives ∣ϕj​∣=∣ϕ​j​∣, whereas F-flatness gives ϕ​j​ϕj​=0; together they force both fields to vanish. Thus only the Coulomb branch remains,

When every mi​ and ζ vanishes, the Coulomb branch again has ϕi​=ϕ​i​=0 and arbitrary x. A Higgs branch also occurs at x=0: it is the zero-level quotient of

N = 1 quotient metric OurBigBook.com
There is a conflict in the printed question: with dynamical X and the displayed superpotential, FX​=ϕ​ϕ=M forces M=0, so the N=1 theory has no vacuum branch parametrized by nonzero M. The natural intended calculation is the Kähler quotient of the N=1 D-flat SQED matter fields before imposing that extra F-term.
On this quotient, ∣ϕ∣=∣ϕ​∣ and the gauge-invariant coordinate is M=ϕ​ϕ. Hence ∣ϕ∣2=∣ϕ​∣2=∣M∣, and the canonical Kähler potential descends to
K(M,M)=2MM​=2∣M∣​.
(17)
The associated Kähler metric is
gMM​=∂M​∂M​K=2∣M∣1​​.
(18)
It is smooth for M=0 and has a conical singularity at M=0, locally C/Z2​. Physically, the charged fields become massless and the U(1) gauge symmetry is restored there, so integrating out the vector multiplet to obtain a sigma-model metric ceases to be valid.

The transformation of the holomorphic strong-coupling scale is fixed by the mixed SU(2)2U(1) anomaly. The baryon-number contributions of Φ and Φ cancel, so Λb0​ is neutral under U(1)B​. Under U(1)A​, all 2Nf​ doublets have charge one and Dynkin index one, giving axial charge 2Nf​. For the R-symmetry, each matter fermion has charge (1−2/Nf​)−1=−2/Nf​, so the matter contribution is −4; the gaugino has R-charge one and contributes I(adj)=4, giving zero total anomaly. Thus
Λ6−Nf​​U(1)B​0​U(1)A​2Nf​​U(1)R​0​​​.

For Nf​=1, antisymmetry makes the would-be baryons vanish, and the only generator of the chiral ring of a supersymmetric gauge theory is the meson operator in supersymmetric quantum chromodynamics M=ΦΦ. It obeys no classical constraint. Holomorphy, mass dimension and all three U(1) charges uniquely permit the Affleck–Dine–Seiberg superpotential
Wdyn​=MΛ5​​
(20)
up to a nonzero constant absorbed into Λ. Since ∂M​W=−Λ5/M2 never vanishes at finite M, there is no finite supersymmetric ground state. Instead the potential approaches zero as ∣M∣→∞: the theory has a supersymmetric runaway vacuum at infinity.

For Nf​=3, the infrared fields at the origin are the nine mesons Mi​j and three baryons plus three antibaryons, for fifteen chiral multiplets. Every elementary quark superfield has R-charge 1/3, so its fermion has charge −2/3. In the ultraviolet there are twelve quark Weyl fermions and three adjoint gauginos. Hence the two anomalies are
TrR=3+12(−32​)=−5,
(24)
TrR3=3+12(−32​)3=−95​.
(25)
Each meson or baryon superfield contains two quarks and has R-charge 2/3, so each composite fermion has charge −1/3. The unconstrained infrared fields therefore give
TrR=15(−31​)=−5,TrR3=15(−31​)3=−95​.
(26)
Both results agree, establishing the requested 't Hooft anomaly matching. This smooth composite description is the SU(2), Nf​=3 example of s-confinement.

Two theories are Seiberg duals when they are distinct ultraviolet gauge theories that flow to the same infrared quantum field theory. Their gauge-invariant operator spectra, global symmetries, 't Hooft anomalies and responses to deformations agree, even though one description may be strongly coupled where the other is weakly coupled.
