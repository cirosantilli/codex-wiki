For a locally small category C, a representation of a functor F:C→Set is an object A and an element a∈F(A) such that
θB​:C(A,B)⟶F(B),f⟼F(f)(a)
(1)
is a bijection for every B, naturally in B. Equivalently, θ:C(A,−)≅F is a natural isomorphism.
Suppose (A,a) and (A′,a′) are two representations. Universality gives unique maps
u:A→A′,v:A′→A
(2)
such that F(u)(a)=a′ and F(v)(a′)=a. Then F(vu)(a)=a, and uniqueness applied to the element a gives vu=1A​. Similarly uv=1A′​. Thus the representing objects are uniquely isomorphic in a way carrying one universal element of a set-valued functor to the other.
For F:C→D and B∈D, the comma category (B↓F) has objects (A,u) with u:B→F(A). A morphism (A,u)→(A′,u′) is a map h:A→A′ satisfying
F(h)u=u′.
(3)
The universal arrow from an object to a functor criterion says that F has a left adjoint exactly when (B↓F) has an initial object for every B. Indeed, an initial (LB,ηB​) represents the functor A↦D(B,FA), and uniqueness makes B↦LB functorial.
When F:C→Set and 1 is a singleton, an arrow 1→F(A) is just an element a∈F(A). Hence (1↓F) is the category of elements, and its initial objects are exactly the representations of F. This proves
F representable⟺(1↓F) has an initial object.
(4)
If F has a left adjoint, the universal-arrow criterion immediately makes it representable. Conversely, suppose C is cocomplete and F≅C(A,−). For a set S, form the copower
L(S)=∐s∈S​A.
(5)
The coproduct in a category universal property gives natural bijections
C(L(S),B)≅∏s∈S​C(A,B)≅Set(S,F(B)),
(6)
so L⊣F.
Cocompleteness cannot be omitted. Let C be the category of ordinals in reverse order: there is one arrow α→β exactly when α≥β in the ordinary ordering. This large poset is locally small and complete. For a set-indexed family (αi​), its product in the reversed order is the ordinary supremum supi​αi​, and equalizers in a poset are automatic. But C has no initial object, since that would be a largest ordinal. Any representable functor C(A,−) is therefore the requested example: if it had a left adjoint L, then
C(L(∅),B)≅Set(∅,C(A,B))
(7)
would be a singleton for every B, making L(∅) initial, a contradiction.
