An exponentiable object X in a category with finite products is one for which
−×X
(34)
has a right adjoint [X,−]. The terminal object is exponentiable because −×1≅1C​. If X and Y are exponentiable, then
−×(X×Y)≅(−×X)×Y
(35)
is a composite of two left adjoints and therefore has the composite right adjoint [X,[Y,−]]. Exponentiable objects are consequently closed under finite products.
In the category of metric spaces and non-expansive maps, the terminal object is the one-point space. The product of X and Y has underlying set X×Y and metric
d((x,y),(x′,y′))=max{dX​(x,x′),dY​(y,y′)}.
(36)
This is the smallest metric making both projections non-expansive, and the product pairing of two non-expansive maps is non-expansive. If X and Y are bounded, so is this product. Hence both Met and the category of bounded metric spaces and non-expansive maps Metb​ have finite products.
For bounded X,Y, define the metric exponential candidate
dˉ(f,g)=sup{dY​(fx,gy):dX​(x,y)<dY​(fx,gy)}.
(37)
The supremum is finite because Y is bounded. For every x,y one has the useful evaluation inequality
dY​(fx,gy)≤max{dˉ(f,g),dX​(x,y)}.
(38)
Indeed, if the second term does not already dominate, the pair (x,y) occurs in the defining supremum.
Assume dˉ is a metric. The evaluation map
ev:[X,Y]×X⟶Y,(f,x)⟼f(x)
(39)
is non-expansive by this inequality. Postcomposition by a non-expansive k:Y→Y′ is non-expansive on function spaces, because every pair contributing to dˉ(kf,kg) also contributes a no-smaller bound to dˉ(f,g). Thus [X,−] is a functor.
If h:Z×X→Y is non-expansive, each hz​(x)=h(z,x) is non-expansive. Whenever
dX​(x,y)<dY​(hz​(x),hz′​(y)),
(40)
non-expansiveness of h forces the latter distance to be at most dZ​(z,z′). Hence z↦hz​ is non-expansive into [X,Y]. Conversely, a non-expansive Z→[X,Y] followed by evaluation gives a non-expansive Z×X→Y. These inverse operations are natural, proving
Metb​(Z×X,Y)≅Metb​(Z,[X,Y]).
(41)
It remains to obtain the triangle inequality from interpolation. Nonnegativity and symmetry of dˉ are immediate. If f=g, taking x=y where f(x)=g(x) proves separation; and dˉ(f,f)=0 follows from non-expansiveness of f.
Let
a=dˉ(f,g),b=dˉ(g,h).
(42)
Fix a pair x,y contributing D=dY​(fx,hy), so t=dX​(x,y)<D. Suppose for contradiction that D>a+b. If t≤a+b, choose r+s=t with r≤a and s≤b. If t>a+b, choose r+s=t with r>a and s>b. Since X is an interpolating metric space, there is z with d(x,z)=r and d(z,y)=s. Applying the evaluation inequality twice gives
D≤dY​(fx,gz)+dY​(gz,hy)≤max{a,r}+max{b,s}.
(43)
In the first case the right side is at most a+b, and in the second it equals r+s=t<D. Both are contradictions. Therefore D≤a+b for every contributing pair, and taking the supremum gives
dˉ(f,h)≤dˉ(f,g)+dˉ(g,h).
(44)
Thus dˉ is a metric whenever X is interpolating, and the preceding adjunction proves every bounded interpolating space is exponentiable in Metb​.
