The predictable sigma-algebra P on Ω×[0,∞) is generated by the sets A×{0} with A∈F0​ and A×(s,t] with 0≤s<t and A∈Fs​. A process is previsible precisely when it is P-measurable.

A simple predictable process has the form
H(ω,t)=H0​(ω)1{0}​(t)+∑k=0m−1​Hk​(ω)1(tk​,tk+1​]​(t),
(11)
where 0=t0​<⋯<tm​<∞, H0​ is bounded and F0​-measurable, and each Hk​ is bounded and Ftk​​-measurable. Finite linear combinations of indicators of predictable rectangles are of this form after refining the finitely many time partitions.
Those rectangles form a semiring of sets generating P. The collection of sets whose indicators can be approximated in L2(ν) by simple predictable processes is a monotone class: for an increasing sequence, truncate the union and use the finiteness of ν; complements and finite disjoint unions are handled by linearity. The Monotone class theorem therefore puts every P-measurable indicator in the closure. Ordinary measurable simple functions are dense in L2(P,ν), so simple predictable processes are dense there as well.
