A Markov kernel K with stationary distribution π is geometrically ergodic if there are ρ<1 and a finite function M(x) such that
∥Km(x,⋅)−π∥TV​≤M(x)ρm
(17)
for every m≥0 and almost every starting point x, where ∥⋅∥TV​ is total variation distance.
A measurable set A is a small set with minorisation constant α>0 if some integer r≥1 and some probability measure ν satisfy
Kr(x,B)≥αν(B)
(18)
for every x∈A and every measurable B.
A standard drift-minorisation condition is that the chain be irreducible and aperiodic, and that there exist a measurable V≥1, a small set A, constants λ<1 and b<∞ such that
KV(x)≤λV(x)+b1A​(x).
(19)
These conditions imply geometric ergodicity.

Let w(θ)=π(θ)/μ(θ) be the importance weight. The two normalized densities give
logw(θ)=−λ2​{Z(θ)−F(θ)}−Z(λ)+F(λ).
(23)
Since ∣F−Z∣<C, there is a finite constant M such that w(θ)≤M everywhere. The Independence Metropolis–Hastings algorithm has an accepted transition density satisfying
μ(y)min{1,w(x)w(y)​}≥M1​μ(y)w(y)=M1​π(y).

Hamiltonian Monte Carlo augments the position x by an independent momentum p∼N(0,M) and uses the Hamiltonian function
H(x,p)=−logπ(x)+21​pTM−1p.
(36)
From the current x, draw a fresh p, apply a fixed number of leapfrog steps to approximate Hamiltonian flow, and obtain (x′,p′). The Metropolis–Hastings acceptance probability is
1∧exp{H(x,p)−H(x′,p′)};
(37)
otherwise retain x. Momentum negation may be included to make the proposal explicitly reversible. The leapfrog map is volume preserving and reversible, while the acceptance step corrects its discretization error, leaving π invariant after the momentum is discarded.

For a sufficiently regular Itô diffusion with D=σσT/2, a density π is stationary if and only if it solves the stationary Fokker-Planck equation
∇⋅(bπ−∇⋅(Dπ))=0,
(38)
with integrable Fokker-Planck probability current and boundary conditions that make its outward flux vanish. Here (∇⋅(Dπ))i​=∑j​∂j​(Dij​π).
For the displayed parametrization, assume
π(x)=Z−1e−H(x),
(39)
where Z<∞, that D(x) is symmetric positive semidefinite, and that Q(x) is antisymmetric. Substituting ∇π=−π∇H and the stated Γ into the probability current cancels all D terms. The remaining divergence is
−∑i,j​∂i​∂j​(Qij​π)=0,
(40)
because the second derivatives are symmetric in i,j whereas Qij​=−Qji​. Thus these conditions, together with the boundary and regularity assumptions, imply stationarity. More generally, the divergence equation above is the exact necessary and sufficient condition; within this construction, π∝e−H and antisymmetric Q are the standard way to satisfy it.
