In an independent Bernoulli product space, an event is increasing if changing any coordinate from 0 to 1 cannot destroy it. Harris' inequality says that two increasing events A,B satisfy
P(A∩B)≥P(A)P(B).
(1)
Equivalently, two decreasing events are positively correlated.
For Janson inequalities, let R be a random subset of a finite ground set whose elements are selected independently, let (Si​)i∈I​ be fixed subsets, and put
Ii​=1{Si​⊆R}​,X=∑i​Ii​,μ=EX.
(2)
Write i∼j when i=j and Si​∩Sj​=∅, and define the ordered dependency sum
Δ=∑i∼j​E(Ii​Ij​).
(3)
The first Janson inequality is
P(X=0)≤exp(−μ+2Δ​).
(4)
The usual extended form is
P(X=0)≤{e−μ/2,e−μ2/(2Δ),​Δ≤μ,Δ≥μ.​
(5)
There is also the complementary lower bound
P(X=0)≥∏i​(1−EIi​),
(6)
and if EIi​≤ε<1 for all i, the product is at least exp(−μ/(1−ε)).
For the proof, order I arbitrarily and expose the avoidance events one at a time. The standard Janson sequential product lemma, obtained by conditioning and using Harris' inequality on the coordinates outside Si​, gives
P(X=0)≤∏i​(1−EIi​+sumj<i:j∼i​E(Ii​Ij​)).
(7)
Using 1−x≤e−x and summing the pair terms yields e−μ+Δ/2. If Δ≤μ, this is at most e−μ/2. If Δ>μ, adjoin an independent Bernoulli coordinate of mean q=μ/Δ to each index and intersect Ai​ with the event that its new coordinate is 1. The event X=0 implies that none of these thinned events occurs, while the thinned family has mean qμ and dependency sum q2Δ. Applying the first inequality in the enlarged product space gives
P(X=0)leqexp(−qμ+2q2Δ​)=e−μ2/(2Δ).
(8)
Finally, the events Ii​=0 are decreasing, so repeated Harris' inequality proves the product lower bound; log(1−x)≥−x/(1−ε) gives its exponential version.
