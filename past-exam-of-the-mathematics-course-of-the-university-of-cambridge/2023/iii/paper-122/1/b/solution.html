Let X count the triangles of the binomial random graph G(n,p). Then
μ=EX=(3n​)p3=Θ(n3p3).
(9)
Two distinct triangle indicators are dependent only when the triangles share an edge. Hence their ordered Janson dependency sum is
Δ=Θ(n4p5).
(10)
If 0<p≤n−1/2, then Δ=O(μ). The first Janson inequality gives
P(X=0)≤e−Ω(μ).
(11)
For the reverse bound, the events that individual triangles are absent are decreasing, so Harris' inequality gives
P(X=0)≥(1−p3)(3n​)=e−O(n3p3),
(12)
where p<1/2 keeps the logarithmic estimate uniform. Thus the probability is e−Θ(p3n3).
If n−1/2≤p<1/2, the extended Janson bound gives
P(X=0)≤exp(−Ω(Δμ2​))=e−Ω(pn2).
(13)
For a lower bound, fix a balanced bipartition of the vertices and require every edge inside either part to be absent. The resulting graph is bipartite, hence triangle-free, and this event has probability
(1−p)2(2⌊n/2⌋​)=e−O(pn2).
(14)
Combining the bounds proves the second regime.
