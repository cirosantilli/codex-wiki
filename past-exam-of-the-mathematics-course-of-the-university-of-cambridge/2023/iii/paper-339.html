f(y)≥f(x)+⟨g,y−x⟩

The first-order subgradient inequality for a differentiable convex function gives
f(v)≥f(w)+⟨∇f(w),v−w⟩
(23)
and, after interchanging v and w,
f(w)≥f(v)+⟨∇f(v),w−v⟩.
(24)
Adding and rearranging yields
⟨∇f(v)−∇f(w),v−w⟩≥0.​
(25)
Thus the gradient F=∇f is a monotone operator.

If T(w)=w, the defining equation becomes M(w)+F(w)=M(w), so F(w)=0​.
A map is firmly nonexpansive in the M-inner product when
∥T(v)−T(w)∥M2​≤⟨T(v)−T(w),v−w⟩M​.
(26)
Put p=T(v) and q=T(w). The two implicit equations give
F(p)=M(v−p),F(q)=M(w−q).
(27)
Because F is a monotone operator,
0≤⟨F(p)−F(q),p−q⟩=⟨(v−w)−(p−q),p−q⟩M​.
(28)
Therefore
∥p−q∥M2​≤⟨p−q,v−w⟩M​,​
(29)
which is precisely firm nonexpansiveness. In particular, the preconditioned proximal point algorithm map T=(M+F)−1M is nonexpansive in the norm induced by the positive-definite matrix M.

Use the sign convention
L(x,z)=f(x)−zT(Ax−b)
(30)
for the Lagrangian function in constrained optimization. The Lagrangian dual problem is
z∈Rmmax​q(z),q(z)=xinf​L(x,z)=bTz−f∗(ATz),​

where f∗ is the convex conjugate. For this convex problem with affine equality constraints, the stationarity and feasibility parts of the Karush-Kuhn-Tucker conditions are

(33)
