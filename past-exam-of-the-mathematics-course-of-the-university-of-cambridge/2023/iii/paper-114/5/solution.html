For unit vectors, the defining inequality for Y is equivalent to v⋅w≥0. Write
w=tv+u,qquadu∈v⊥=Tv​S2n,qquadt≥0.
(33)
Then t=(1−∥u∥2)1/2, so
(v,u)⟼(v,(1−∥u∥2)1/2v+u)
(34)
is a homeomorphism D(TS2n)→Y. Replacing v by −v gives the same description of Y′. Their intersection is
Z={(v,w):v⋅w=0}=STS2n=V2​(R2n+1),
(35)
the unit tangent bundle, equivalently a Stiefel manifold. Since e(TS2n) evaluates to χ(S2n)=2, its Gysin sequence gives the cohomology of the unit tangent bundle of an even-dimensional sphere:
Hq(Z;Z)≅⎩⎨⎧​Z,Z/2,0,​q=0,4n−1,q=2n,otherwise.​
(36)
Every product of positive-degree classes vanishes.
Let G consist of the eight signed permutations of the two factors:
(v,w)⟼(ε1​v,ε2​w)or(v,w)⟼(ε1​w,ε2​v),εi​∈{±1}.
(37)
It is the group of signed permutation matrices in dimension two, hence isomorphic to the dihedral group D8​, and every element preserves the equation v⋅w=0. If a,b generate H2n(X;Z) from the two sphere factors, these maps act by the corresponding signed permutation matrix, because the antipodal map of the even-dimensional sphere has degree −1. The eight actions are distinct, so only the identity can be homotopic to 1X​.
Every element of G acts trivially on H∗(Z;Z). The degree-2n group is Z/2, so its automorphism is forced to be the identity. On the top class, regard Z as the Stiefel manifold of two-frames. The signed permutations are the right action of O(2). The determinant-one component is connected, while a determinant-minus-one element is homotopic within that component to (v,w)↦(v,−w), the antipodal map on the S2n−1 fibers. That map has degree +1, so the top class is also fixed.
The subgroup preserving Y is
H={1,AB,S,ABS},
(38)
where A(v,w)=(−v,w), B(v,w)=(v,−w), and S(v,w)=(w,v). It consists exactly of the elements with ε1​ε2​=1. Under the deformation retraction Y≃S2n onto the diagonal, 1 and S act as the identity, while AB and ABS act as the antipodal map. They therefore act on H2n(Y;Z) by +1,+1,−1,−1, respectively.
The Thom isomorphism theorem identifies H∗(Y,Z) with a degree-2n shift of H∗(S2n). On the Thom class in degree 2n, the same four elements act by +1,+1,−1,−1: the factor swap reverses each normal vector, which preserves the orientation because the normal rank 2n is even, while the simultaneous antipodal map reverses the oriented tangent fiber. On the degree-4n relative class every element acts by +1, since the base and fiber signs for the last two elements cancel.
When n=1, Z=V2​(R3)≅SO(3). Every signed permutation of (v,w) extends, after sending the third frame vector v×w to the required sign, to right multiplication by an element of SO(3). Since SO(3) is path-connected, every right translation is homotopic to the identity. Therefore
g∣Z​≃1Z​for every g∈G.​
(39)
