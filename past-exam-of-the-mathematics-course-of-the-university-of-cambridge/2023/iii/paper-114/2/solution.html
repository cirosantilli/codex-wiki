The long exact sequence in homology of the triple A⊆U⊆X contains
⋯→Hq​(U,A)→Hq​(X,A)→Hq​(X,U)→Hq−1​(U,A)→⋯.
(10)
Because A↪U is a homotopy equivalence, H∗​(U,A)=0. Hence the middle map is an isomorphism in every degree:
H∗​(X,A)≅H∗​(X,U).
(11)
The Excision theorem says that if Z⊆A⊆X and Z⊆intA, then inclusion induces
H∗​(X∖Z,A∖Z)∼​H∗​(X,A).
(12)
A good pair (X,A) has A closed and a neighborhood U that deformation retracts onto A. The collapsing a pair theorem states that the quotient map gives
Hq​(X,A)≅Hq​(X/A).
(13)
To prove it, choose such a neighborhood U. The first result identifies H∗​(X,A) with H∗​(X,U). Excision identifies the latter with H∗​(X/A,U/A), while U/A is contractible because the deformation retraction can be chosen relative to A using the homotopy extension property. The long exact sequence of the pair (X/A,U/A) then identifies this relative group with H∗​(X/A).
For f:(Dn,∂Dn)→(Dn,∂Dn), define degf by
f∗​[Dn,∂Dn]=(degf)[Dn,∂Dn]
(14)
in Hn​(Dn,∂Dn;Z)≅Z. In the long exact sequence of the pair, the connecting homomorphism
Hn​(Dn,∂Dn)∼​Hn−1​(∂Dn)
(15)
is an isomorphism. Naturality shows that f∗​ and (f∣∂Dn​)∗​ multiply the corresponding generators by the same integer, so
degf=deg(f∣∂Dn​).
(16)
Finally,
(Dn×I)/∂(Dn×I)≅Σ(Dn/∂Dn)≅Sn+1.
(17)
Under this identification, the map induced by g(x,t)=(f(x),t) is the suspension of the map induced by f. By degree under suspension,
degg=degf.
(18)
This argument proves the needed product assertion directly from the natural suspension isomorphism in reduced homology.
