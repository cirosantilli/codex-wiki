In the inertial frame, rigid corotation gives u=Ω×r. The ideal magnetohydrodynamics condition is the motional electric field
E=−cu×B0​​.
(1)
Apply Gauss's law in Gaussian units and use the divergence and curl of a cross product:
4πnq​=∇⋅E=−c1​[B0​⋅(∇×u)−u⋅(∇×B0​)].
(2)
The exterior field is produced by currents inside the star, so ∇×B0​=0 there, while rotation with constant angular velocity has ∇×u=2Ω. Hence the required Goldreich-Julian charge density is
nq​(r)=−2πcΩ⋅B0​(r)​​.
(3)
Here nq​ denotes electric charge per unit volume, as in the question. If each carrier has charge q, its signed number density is nq​/q.

Write Y=Bϕ2​, x=R2/L2, and retain the frozen axial field Bz​=(B0​/ρ0​)ρ. The radial magnetostatic equilibrium equation now includes both the gradient of the toroidal magnetic pressure and the inward magnetic tension:

(27)

Bϕ2​∼R24πρ12​L2​(Keff​−πGL2).
