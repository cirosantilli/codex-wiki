If X is sub-Gaussian with variance parameter ν, then
EeλX≤eλ2ν/2
(1)
for every λ∈R. Restricting this inequality to ∣λ∣<1/α proves that X is sub-exponential with parameters (ν,α) for every α>0.
Now let X=Z2−1 for a standard normal distribution variable Z. Its moment-generating function is
EeλX=1−2λ​e−λ​,λ<21​,
(2)
and is infinite for λ≥1/2. A sub-Gaussian moment-generating function must be finite for every real λ, so X cannot be sub-Gaussian with any finite parameter. For ∣λ∣<1/4, the stated inequality gives
EeλX≤e2λ2=eλ2(4)/2.
(3)
Thus X is sub-exponential with parameters (4,4).

Let X1​,…,Xn​ be independent random variables, let f=f(X1​,…,Xn​), and let each fi​ depend on every coordinate except Xi​. The modified logarithmic Sobolev inequality states that, for every real λ for which the expectations exist,
Ent(eλf)≤E[eλf∑i=1n​ϕ(−λ(f−fi​))],ϕ(u)=eu−u−1.
(18)
To prove it, first apply tensorization of entropy:
Ent(eλf)≤∑i=1n​E[EntXi​​(eλf)].
(19)
Condition on all coordinates except Xi​ and use the stated variational formula with the admissible constant u=eλfi​. The ith conditional entropy is at most
EXi​​[eλf(λf−λfi​)−(eλf−eλfi​)]​=EXi​​[eλfϕ(−λ(f−fi​))].​
(20)
Summing and taking the remaining expectations proves the inequality.
