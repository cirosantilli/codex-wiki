f OurBigBook.com
Choose Δt with λΔt≪1 and sΔt small relative to L and the distance to the target. For each of many independent trajectories:
1. Set x=y, v=s, and t=0.
2. Propose xnew​=x+vΔt.
3. If xnew​≤0, record the linearly interpolated target-crossing time and stop. If xnew​≥L, reflect to xnew​=2L−xnew​ and set v=−∣v∣.
4. Otherwise reverse v with probability 1−e−λΔt, set x=xnew​ and t=t+Δt, and repeat.
The sample mean of the recorded times estimates τ+(y). Sampling a Poisson number of reversals and their ordered times inside each step removes the at-most-one-reversal approximation, but the stated Bernoulli scheme converges as Δt→0.

The Fokker-Planck equation corresponds to the Itô diffusion
dX=v(X)dt+2D​dW,​
(24)
absorbed at 0 and reflected at L.
For v(x)=x, an Euler--Maruyama proposal is
Y=Xn​+Xn​Δt+2DΔt​Zn​,Zn​∼N(0,1).
(25)
If Y≤0, kill the path. If Xn​>0 and Y>0, an endpoint-only test can miss a crossing. Conditional on the endpoints, the local Brownian bridge crossing probability is

The stochastic quasi-steady-state simulation evolves only X:
1. At the current integer x≥1, compute q(x)=α3​V2/(α4​x) and the averaged rates λ1​(x)=α1​q(x)2/V and λ2​(x)=α2​x(x−1)/V.
2. Draw a waiting time Δt∼Exp(λ1​+λ2​).
3. Set x←x+1 with probability λ1​/(λ1​+λ2​); otherwise set x←x−1.
4. Advance time by Δt and repeat.
This is a Gillespie algorithm for the averaged slow master equation. It samples the fast conditional equilibrium analytically through its factorial moment and never simulates individual fast Y births or deaths.
