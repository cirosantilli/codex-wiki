The chain rule and
θx​=ΘX​=k,θt​=ΘT​=−ω,Xx​=Tt​=ε
(1)
give
ϕx​=kΦθ​+εΦX​,ϕt​=−ωΦθ​+εΦT​.
(2)
Applying the same rule to L1​ and L2​ in the Euler-Lagrange field equation yields the exact modulated equation
k(L1​)θ​+ε(L1​)X​−ω(L2​)θ​+ε(L2​)T​−L3​=0.​
(3)
Multiply this equation by Φθ​. Since k and ω are independent of the fast phase,
Lθ​=L1​(kΦθθ​+εΦXθ​)+L2​(−ωΦθθ​+εΦTθ​)+L3​Φθ​.
(4)
The product rule then rearranges the field equation into the exact modulated-wave first integral
∂θ​[(kL1​−ωL2​)Φθ​−L]+ε∂X​(Φθ​L1​)+ε∂T​(Φθ​L2​)=0.​
(5)
Average this identity over one 2π period in θ. Periodicity kills the first term, while differentiation of the averaged Lagrangian gives
∂k∂L​=⟨Φθ​L1​⟩,∂ω∂L​=−⟨Φθ​L2​⟩.
(6)
Consequently
∂X​∂k∂L​−∂T​∂ω∂L​=0.​
(7)
The same two equations follow directly from the modulated variational principle. Varying Φ gives
∂θ​(kL1​−ωL2​)+ε∂X​L1​+ε∂T​L2​−L3​=0,
(8)
which is the exact field equation above. For a variation of Θ, use δk=∂X​δΘ and δω=−∂T​δΘ. Integration by parts in X and T gives the averaged equation. Thus variation with respect to the periodic profile reproduces the local wave equation, whereas variation with respect to its slow phase gives the Whitham modulation equation for wave action.

(9)
so the Euler-Lagrange field equation is the variable-coefficient wave equation

(10)

ϕx​∼kΦθ​,ϕt​∼−ωΦθ​,

Since Φθ​=−Asinθ and the average of sin2θ over one period is 1/2, the leading averaged Lagrangian is
L(0)(k,ω,A)=41​A2(ω2−c2k2).​

Three distinguished limits are needed.
In the origin layer, put x=εX and y(x;ε)=Y(X). Since sech(εX)=1+O(ε2), the leading equation is
XY′′+(X+2)Y′+Y=0.
(34)
It has the first integral
XY′+(X+1)Y=C.
(35)
Solving this first-order linear differential equation gives
Y=XC+De−X​.
(36)
Regularity at X=0 requires D=−C, and Y(0)=1 then fixes C=1. Thus Region I, x=O(ε), has
Y(X)=X1−e−X​.​
(37)
Its matching limit is Y∼X−1 as X→∞.
In Region II, x=O(1) with x≫ε, setting ε=0 reduces the second-order equation to
xy0′​+(x+1)y0​=0.
(38)
Hence y0​=Ce−x/x. Matching C/x with the inner limit ε/x fixes C=ε, so
y∼xε​e−x.​
(39)
The correction generated by εx3y changes the local decay rate when x=O(ε−1/2). For Region III set
Z=ε​x,y=e−F(Z)/ε​G(Z).
(40)
The terms of order ε−1/2 give the eikonal equation
F′(Z)=1+Z2,
(41)
and the next balance gives ZG′+G=0. Matching to Region II selects
F(Z)=Z+3Z3​,G(Z)=Zε3/2​.
(42)
Therefore
y∼Zε3/2​exp[−ε​Z+Z3/3​]​(x=O(ε−1/2)).
(43)
This solution is already beyond all algebraic orders in ε in the distant region. A leading multiplicative composite expansion that contains all three balances and satisfies the boundary value exactly is
ycomp​(x;ε)=xε​(1−e−x/ε)exp(−x−3εx3​),​
(44)
with its value at x=0 understood by continuity.
Finally, the coefficient of y′′ vanishes at the origin. Taking the regular limit of the original equation there gives
2εy′(0)+y(0)=0,
(45)
so the prescribed value also fixes y′(0)=−1/(2ε). Equivalently, the second inner solution behaves as 1/X and is excluded by regularity. This regular singular point is why one boundary condition determines the unique regular solution.
