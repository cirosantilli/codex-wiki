Let (z1,…,zn) be a complex manifold chart, with zj=xj+iyj. The almost complex structure induced by a complex atlas is
J∂xj∂​=∂yj∂​,J∂yj∂​=−∂xj∂​.
(1)
Thus J2=−I. On an overlap, the derivative of a holomorphic map is complex linear and hence commutes with multiplication by i. The two coordinate definitions of J therefore agree, so J is globally well-defined.
The complexified cotangent bundle splits into the i- and −i-eigenspaces of J∗, locally spanned by dzj and dzˉj. A differential form of type (p, q) is a sum
α=∑∣I∣=p,∣K∣=q​αIKˉ​dzI∧dzˉK.
(2)
Splitting the exterior derivative according to type defines
d=∂+∂ˉ,∂:Ωp,q→Ωp+1,q,∂ˉ:Ωp,q→Ωp,q+1.
(3)
Complex conjugation sends dzj to dzˉj and conjugates the coefficient derivatives. Term by term this gives the complex conjugation of differential-form type identity
∂ˉαˉ=∂α.
(4)
Let J act on a k-form by
(Jα)(v1​,…,vk​)=α(Jv1​,…,Jvk​).
(5)
It acts on a (p,q)-form by ip−q. Therefore J−1dJ multiplies the ∂ component by −i and the ∂ˉ component by i, proving the d c operator formula
J−1dJ=i(∂ˉ−∂)=dc.
(6)
A holomorphic vector field is a holomorphic section of T1,0X. On the affine chart U0​⊂CPn, put wk​=zk​/z0​. The projection is
φ0​(z)=(1,w1​,…,wn​).
(7)
Direct differentiation gives
(dφ0​)a​(∂zj​∂​)=⎩⎨⎧​a0​1​∂wj​∂​,−a02​1​k=1∑n​ak​∂wk​∂​,​j=0,j=0.​
(8)
If ξ(z) is linear and homogeneous, the pushed-forward coefficients are consequently ξ(1,w) when j=0, and −ξ(1,w)wk​ when j=0. They are holomorphic on U0​.
More intrinsically, ξ(z)∂zj​​ is a linear vector field Az on Cn+1. Its flow etA preserves complex lines and induces the projective transformations
[z]⟼[etAz].
(9)
Differentiating gives a globally defined projectivization of a linear vector field, whose expression on U0​ is the field just computed. This proves extension across the other affine charts.
Finally choose distinct complex numbers λ0​,…,λn​ and the diagonal field
Z=∑j=0n​λj​zj​∂zj​∂​.
(10)
Its projectivization vanishes at [z] precisely when (λ0​z0​,…,λn​zn​) is proportional to z, so [z] is an eigenline. The distinct eigenvalues leave exactly the n+1 coordinate points. Hence CPn has a holomorphic vector field with finitely many zeroes.

An irreducible complex analytic hypersurface in a complex manifold X is a closed irreducible analytic subset of pure complex codimension one. A local defining function of a complex analytic hypersurface Y at x is a holomorphic function f on a neighbourhood U such that
Y∩U={f=0}.
(11)
The necessary local algebra is that the stalk OX,x​ is a regular local ring, hence a unique factorization domain, and that the local branches of a hypersurface germ determine finitely many height-one prime ideals. Each is principal; the product of their generators gives f, and removing repeated factors makes it reduced. This also covers a globally irreducible hypersurface that has several local branches at a singular point.
A divisor on a complex manifold is a locally finite formal sum D=∑Y​nY​Y of irreducible analytic hypersurfaces with integer coefficients. On a sufficiently small Uα​, local defining functions give a meromorphic equation fα​ for D. The quotients fα​/fβ​ are nowhere-zero holomorphic functions. Gluing frames by
eα​=(fβ​/fα​)eβ​
(12)
produces the holomorphic line bundle associated to a divisor [D], and fα​eα​ gives its canonical meromorphic section with divisor D.
The Euler sequence on complex projective space
0⟶O⟶O(1)⊕(n+1)⟶TCPn⟶0
(13)
implies detTCPn≅O(n+1). Taking the dual determinant yields the canonical bundle of complex projective space
KCPn​≅O(−n−1)≅[−(n+1)H],
(14)
where H is a hyperplane divisor.
The hypotheses on the homogeneous polynomial p say that
V={p=0}⊂CPn
(15)
is a smooth projective hypersurface of degree k, so its divisor line bundle is [V]≅O(kH). The Adjunction formula gives
KV​≅(KCPn​⊗[V])∣V​≅OV​(k−n−1)≅[(k−n−1)H∣V​].
(16)
This is the canonical bundle of a smooth projective hypersurface.
Now fix an isomorphism Φ:[P]→[Q] and regard sP​ and Φ−1sQ​ as holomorphic sections of the same holomorphic line bundle. They have no common zero because P=Q. Their homogeneous coordinates therefore define a well-defined holomorphic map
F:S⟶CP1,F(x)=[sP​(x):Φ−1sQ​(x)].
(17)
In a local frame, the quotient
f=Φ−1sQ​sP​​
(18)
is a meromorphic function with divisor (f)=P−Q. Thus F−1(0)=P and F−1(∞)=Q, both with multiplicity one. The degree of a holomorphic map F is therefore one. A nonconstant degree-one holomorphic map between compact connected Riemann surfaces is a biholomorphism, so the displayed map is biholomorphic.

A real (1, 1)-form is a form φ∈Ω1,1(X) satisfying φˉ​=φ. In holomorphic coordinates it has the form
φ=i∑j,k​hjkˉ​dzj∧dzˉk,hjkˉ​=hkjˉ​​​.
(19)
It is a positive real (1, 1)-form when
−iφ(ξ,ξˉ​)>0
(20)
for every nonzero tangent vector ξ of type (1,0), equivalently when the Hermitian matrix (hjkˉ​) is positive definite.
A holomorphic local trivialization of a holomorphic line bundle L→X is equivalently a nowhere-zero holomorphic local frame e. A connection A is unitary when it preserves the fiberwise Hermitian inner product:
dh(s,t)=h(∇As,t)+h(s,∇At).
(21)
The Chern connection is the unique unitary connection whose (0,1) part is the bundle's Dolbeault partial connection ∂ˉL​.
In a holomorphic frame, put h=h(e,e). The local formula for the Chern connection on a line bundle is
∇Ae=(∂logh)e,F(A)=∂ˉ∂logh.
(22)
The curvature therefore has type (1,1). Since a unitary connection has imaginary curvature, F(A)​=−F(A), and hence
iF(A)​=iF(A).
(23)
Thus iF(A) is a real (1,1)-form.
For connections A on L and A on L, the tensor product connection is defined on decomposable local sections by
∇A⊗A(s⊗s)=∇As⊗s+s⊗∇As.
(24)
The curvature of a tensor product connection on line bundles is additive:
F(A⊗A)=F(A)+F(A).
(25)
Consequently
iF(A⊗A)=iF(A)+iF(A),
(26)
which is positive whenever both summands are positive.
For the final assertion, simultaneously diagonalize the positive Hermitian matrices of φ and ψ by congruence at the chosen point. In the resulting coframe,
φ=i∑j​aj​dzj∧dzˉj,ψ=i∑j​bj​dzj∧dzˉj,aj​,bj​>0.
(27)
A direct wedge-product calculation gives
(φ∧ψ)(ξ,η,ξˉ​,ηˉ​)=∑j<k​(aj​bk​+ak​bj​)∣ξj​ηk​−ξk​ηj​∣2.
(28)
If ξ and η are linearly independent, at least one of these 2×2 minors is nonzero. Every coefficient is positive, so the sum is strictly positive. This is wedge positivity for two positive (1, 1)-forms.

The Kähler manifold structure gives a Riemannian metric, its volume form, the complex orientation, and a Hermitian inner product on complex differential forms. The complex Hodge star operator is the complex-linear map characterized by
α∧∗βˉ​=⟨α,β⟩volg​.
(29)
On r-forms in real dimension m=2n, the codifferential is
d∗=(−1)m(r+1)+1∗d∗,
(30)
equivalently the formal adjoint of d for the L2 inner product. Similarly ∂ˉ∗ is the formal adjoint of ∂ˉ. Define the Hodge Laplacian and Dolbeault Laplacian by
Δd​=dd∗+d∗d,Δ∂ˉ​=∂ˉ∂ˉ∗+∂ˉ∗∂ˉ.
(31)
Expanding d=∂+∂ˉ, the Kähler identities make the mixed anticommutators vanish and imply Δ∂​=Δ∂ˉ​. Hence the Kähler Laplacian identity is
Δd​=2Δ∂ˉ​.
(32)
Let Lγ=ω∧γ be the Lefschetz operator of a Kähler manifold. The Kähler identities also imply
[L,Δ∂ˉ​]=0.
(33)
Thus, if Δ∂ˉ​α=0, then
Δ∂ˉ​(α∧ωk)=Δ∂ˉ​Lkα=LkΔ∂ˉ​α=0.
(34)
This is the fact that the Lefschetz operator preserves harmonic forms.
The Dolbeault Hodge decomposition on a compact Hermitian manifold states that
Ωp,q(X)=H∂ˉp,q​⊕∂ˉΩp,q−1(X)⊕∂ˉ∗Ωp,q+1(X),
(35)
an orthogonal direct sum, where H∂ˉp,q​=kerΔ∂ˉ​.
Suppose η=∂ˉγ has type (p,q). Apply this decomposition to γ∈Ωp,q−1. The harmonic and ∂ˉ-exact pieces disappear after applying ∂ˉ, so for some β∈Ωp,q,
η=∂ˉ∂ˉ∗β.
(36)
Put θ=∂ˉ∗β. If also ∂η=0, then
∂ˉ(∂θ)=−∂(∂ˉθ)=−∂η=0.
(37)
The Kähler anticommutation identity ∂ˉ∗∂+∂∂ˉ∗=0 and (∂ˉ∗)2=0 give
∂ˉ∗(∂θ)=−∂(∂ˉ∗θ)=0.
(38)
Therefore ∂θ is Δ∂ˉ​-harmonic. By Δ∂​=Δ∂ˉ​ it is also ∂-harmonic, but it is ∂-exact; orthogonality of harmonic and exact forms forces
∂θ=0.
(39)
This proves both requested claims: ∂∂ˉ∗β=0 is harmonic, and ∂ˉ∗β=θ is ∂-closed.
Finally, θ∈im∂ˉ∗ is orthogonal to ker∂ˉ, and hence to every ∂ˉ-harmonic form. Since the ∂- and ∂ˉ-harmonic spaces agree on a compact Kähler manifold, the ∂-closed form θ has zero harmonic component in its ∂-Hodge decomposition. It follows that θ=∂ϕ for some ϕ∈Ωp−1,q−1(X). Hence
η=∂ˉθ=∂ˉ∂ϕ,
(40)
which is the ddbar lemma in this case.
