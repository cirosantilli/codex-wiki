Assume a small Rossby number, a beta plane, and small fractional changes
f0​∣βy∣​,f0​∣ζ∣​,H0​∣hb​∣​,H0​∣η∣​≪1.
(1)
At leading order, geostrophic balance gives
ug​=(−ψy​,ψx​),ζ=∇2ψ,η=gf0​​ψ.
(2)
Expanding the reciprocal depth in the shallow-water potential vorticity gives
q=H0​−hb​+ηf0​+βy+ζ​≃H0​f0​​(1+f0​βy​+f0​∇2ψ​+H0​hb​​−gH0​f0​ψ​).
(3)
This is the stated shallow-water quasi-geostrophic potential vorticity Pg​. The term ∇2ψ is the parcel's relative vorticity, while −f02​ψ/(gH0​)=−ψ/LD2​ is vortex stretching caused by free-surface displacement, where
LD​=f0​gH0​​​
(4)
is the barotropic deformation radius.
For hb​=αy, omit the irrelevant constant factor f0​/H0​. The active PV anomaly is
Q=∇2ψ−LD2​ψ​+(β+H0​f0​α​)y.
(5)
Linearizing Qt​+J(ψ,Q)=0 about rest and using ψ∝ei(kx+ly−ωt) gives the Topographic Rossby-wave dispersion relation
ω=−k2+l2+LD−2​(β+f0​α/H0​)k​​.
(6)
A northward displacement raises planetary PV when β>0 and raises topographic PV when the bottom rises northward, α>0. PV conservation then requires anticyclonic relative vorticity, producing westward phase propagation. A negative slope opposes β and reverses propagation when f0​α/H0​<−β.
Under a rigid lid, h=H0​−hb​ is fixed but need not be close to H0​. With q=(f+∇2ψ)/h(y), linearization of Dq/Dt=0 gives
∂t​∇2ψ+(β−fhhy​​)ψx​=0​.
(7)
For h=H0​eγy and over a region where f≃f0​, plane waves obey
ω=−k2+l2(β−f0​γ)k​​.
(8)
The planetary-vorticity gradient dominates when ∣β∣≫∣f0​γ∣, while exponential depth variation dominates when ∣f0​γ∣≫∣β∣. If the variation of f=f0​+βy across the region is retained, this is the corresponding local WKB approximation with effective gradient β−fγ.

Let the thermal-wind basic velocity be
U=Λzy​,Λ=fM2​,
(9)
so that fUz​=bx​=M2. Assume constant f,M,N, hydrostatic perturbations, y independence, and normal modes proportional to ei(kx+mz−ωt). The linearized equations are
ut​−fv=−px​,vt​+fu+Λw=0,pz​=b′,
(10)
bt′​+M2u+N2w=0,ux​+wz​=0.
(11)
Incompressibility gives w=−(k/m)u. Eliminating v,p,b′ yields
ω2=f2−2M2mk​+N2m2k2​​.
(12)
This quadratic in k/m is positive for every orientation precisely when
M4<N2f2​.
(13)
Otherwise some disturbances grow monotonically through symmetric instability.
For the stable case, the minimum occurs at
mk​=N2M2​,ωmin2​=f2−N2M4​​.
(14)
Constant-phase lines have slope dz/dx=−k/m=−M2/N2, exactly the slope of the basic isopycnals M2x+N2z=constant. The minimum-frequency displacement follows an isopycnal, allowing buoyancy and Coriolis restoring forces to oppose one another. Its frequency is below the inertial frequency ∣f∣ of an unstratified rotating fluid.
If θ is the counter-clockwise angle of the wavevector from the horizontal, k/m=cotθ, and
cotθ=N2M2±M4+N2(ω2−f2)​​​.
(15)
For ω2>f2, define D=[M4+N2(ω2−f2)]1/2>M2. Taking k>0, the incident wave with group velocity down and right has
cotθi​=N2M2+D​>0.
(16)
A horizontal reflection preserves k and ω but selects the other vertical wavenumber, so
cotθr​=N2M2−D​<0​.
(17)
The incident and reflected phase lines have slopes −(M2+D)/N2 and (D−M2)/N2, respectively. Their group velocities are perpendicular to these phase lines: the incident ray points down-right and the reflected ray up-right. The isopycnal slope −M2/N2 lies between the two phase-line orientations.

The interior equation materially conserves three-dimensional quasi-geostrophic potential vorticity. At each rigid horizontal boundary, ψz​ is proportional to the boundary buoyancy anomaly, so the other two equations express material conservation of that buoyancy.
A background streamfunction ψ​=−Λzy gives ug​=(Λz,0,0). Its interior PV is uniform. Writing ψ=ψ​+ψ′ and retaining first-order terms gives
(∂t​+Λz∂x​)(ψxx′​+ψyy′​+f2N−2ψzz′​)=0​
(18)
in the interior. Since ψ​z​=−Λy, perturbation advection of the boundary buoyancy gives
(∂t​+Λz∂x​)ψz′​−Λψx′​=0​
(19)
at z=0,H. At the lower boundary this is replaced by
(∂t​ψz′​−Λψx′​)z=0​=−αψz′​(0).
(20)
For a Fourier component ψ​(z,t)eikx with zero interior PV,
ψ​zz​−f2N2k2​ψ​=0.
(21)
Solving this boundary-value problem in terms of
q0​=ψ​z​(0,t),qH​=ψ​z​(H,t)
(22)
gives
ψ​(0)=−Aq0​+BqH​,ψ​(H)=−Bq0​+AqH​​,
(23)
where, with μ=NHk/f,
A=Nkf​cothμ,B=Nkf​cschμ​.

For ϵ=0, the quantity under the square root is real:
F(μ)=μ21​−μcothμ​+41​.
(29)
It is negative for small μ and positive for large μ. Therefore the Eady instability occurs for μ<μc​, where
μc2​1​−μc​cothμc​​+41​=0​,
(30)
and modes are neutral for μ>μc​.
For μ≫1, cothμ=1+O(e−2μ) and cschμ is exponentially small. Hence
c+​=1−μ1​+O(e−2μ),c−​=μ1​+O(e−2μ)​.
(31)
These are two decoupled Boundary Rossby waves, one localized near each boundary. Their laboratory phase speeds lie just inside the basic velocities ΛH and 0 because each propagates intrinsically against the local flow.

At μ=μc​, the real undamped part F vanishes. Put
d=21​−μc​cothμc​​.
(32)
The neutral equation implies d=1/4−1/μc2​>0. The radicand is therefore
z=iϵd−4ϵ2​.
(33)
If z​=u+iv is chosen with v>0, then
v2=21​(sqrtϵ2d2+16ϵ4​+4ϵ2​)>4ϵ2​.
(34)
Thus v>ϵ/2, and the plus branch has
Imc+​=−2ϵ​+v>0.
(35)
For kΛ>0 this is exponential growth. Lower-boundary damping therefore destabilizes the formerly neutral cutoff mode, an example of dissipation-induced instability.

When μ≫1, the boundary coupling B/H=cschμ/μ is exponentially small. The two eigenvalues are therefore the diagonal boundary-wave speeds through order 1/μ:
clower​=μ1​−iϵ+O(e−μ),cupper​=1−μ1​+O(e−μ)​.
(36)
The lower wave is damped at the imposed rate, while the upper wave is exponentially isolated from the lower boundary and remains neutral to algebraic order. Damping destroys the phase-locked counterpropagating-wave interaction at short horizontal wavelength rather than damping both waves equally.

Substitute
u=χ(z)U,v=χ(z)V,ϕ=χ(z)Φ.
(37)
The two horizontal momentum equations separate immediately because the same factor χ multiplies every term. Hydrostatic balance gives
σ=χ′(z)Φ​.
(38)
The buoyancy equation then gives
w=−N2χ′​Φt​=N2c2χ′​(Ux​+Vy​)​.
(39)
Differentiating this expression and using three-dimensional incompressibility together with the shallow-water mass equation yields
χ′′+c2N2​χ=0​.
(40)
Thus each vertical eigenfunction supplies an equivalent depth or wave speed c to an equatorial shallow-water system.

For the equatorial Kelvin wave, set V=0. The zonal momentum and mass equations give ω2=k2c2. Meridional trapping selects the eastward branch
ω=kc​,
(41)
for which
U=U0​e−βy2/(2c),Φ=cU0​e−βy2/(2c)​.
(42)
The westward algebraic branch would grow away from the equator and is rejected.
For V=0, let D=ω2−k2c2. The zonal momentum and mass equations give
U=Di(ωβyV−kc2Vy​)​,Φ=Dic2(kβyV−ωVy​)​.
(43)
Substitution in meridional geostrophic balance and use of the stated Hermite differential equation gives the trapped Equatorial Rossby-wave dispersion relation
ω=−2n+1kc​​,n=1,2,….
(44)
For n=1, define Le​=(c/β)1/2, Y=y/Le​, and take
V=2V0​Ye−Y2/2.
(45)
Then
U=4kLe​3iV0​​(3−2Y2)e−Y2/2​,
(46)
Φ=−4kLe​3icV0​​(1+2Y2)e−Y2/2​.
(47)
Without imposing meridional geostrophic balance, the equatorial shallow-water modes satisfy the Matsuno cubic
ω3−[k2c2+(2n+1)βc]ω−βkc2=0​.
(48)
A dispersion diagram therefore contains high-frequency eastward and westward equatorial inertia--gravity waves as well as westward equatorial Rossby waves, plus the separate straight equatorial Kelvin wave branch ω=kc. The Rossby curves approach −kc/(2n+1) only in the long-wave limit and bend toward zero like −β/k at short wavelength. The geostrophic model retains the Kelvin and long-wave Rossby lines but filters the inertia--gravity modes and misses Rossby-wave dispersion at larger ∣k∣.

For a vertical plane wave χ=eimz, the vertical-mode equation gives
c=∣m∣N​​.
(49)
Consequently the Boussinesq equatorial Kelvin wave and n=1 equatorial Rossby wave have
ωK​=∣m∣Nk​​,ωR​=−3∣m∣Nk​​.
(50)
Their meridional structures are those in part b with Le​=[N/(β∣m∣)]1/2.
Let the positive forcing frequency be ω=Nsinθ with θ≪1. Upward radiation into z>0 requires m<0 for both responses. The Kelvin wave has k>0 and
∣m∣k​=sinθ.
(51)
Its group velocity points up and right along a ray making angle approximately θ above the horizontal, so it occupies the region to the right of the localized source. Its phase propagates down and right.
The n=1 Rossby wave has k<0 and
∣m∣∣k∣​=3sinθ.
(52)
Its group velocity points up and left along a ray making angle approximately 3θ above the negative horizontal direction, so it occupies the region to the left. Its phase propagates down and left. Thus energy radiates upward and away from the source on both sides, while the vertical phase propagation is downward in both wave beams.
