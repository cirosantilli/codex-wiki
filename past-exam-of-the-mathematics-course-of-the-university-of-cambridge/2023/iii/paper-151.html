A group extension of G by the ZG-module M is an exact sequence
1⟶Mi​Eπ​G⟶1
(1)
whose conjugation action on i(M) agrees with the prescribed action of G on M. It is a split group extension when π has a group-homomorphic section s:G→E. Two such extensions are equivalent group extensions when an isomorphism of their middle groups is the identity on M and induces the identity on G. Transporting a section through that isomorphism proves that every extension equivalent to a split extension is split.
Choose a set-theoretic section s with s(1)=1. Its failure to preserve multiplication is the normalized two-cocycle
c(g,h)=s(g)s(h)s(gh)−1∈M.
(2)
Associativity gives the two-cocycle identity, and replacing s changes c by a group coboundary. The resulting class [c]∈H2(G,M) is therefore intrinsic to the extension, as expressed by second group cohomology classifies group extensions.
Now write G=⟨x,y⟩≅Z2 and let I be the augmentation ideal of ZG. The Koszul resolution for a rank-two free abelian group gives, after applying HomZG​(−,M), the last coboundary
M2⟶M,(a,b)⟼(1−y)a+(x−1)b.
(3)
Its image is IM. For M=ZG/I2 this yields the second cohomology of a rank-two free abelian group with truncated group-ring coefficients calculation
H2(G,M)=M/IM≅(ZG/I2)/(I/I2)≅ZG/I≅Z.
(4)
The canonical map M→Z induces the identity on these final quotients, so H2(G,M)→H2(G,Z) is surjective; indeed it is an isomorphism.
Let F2​=⟨x,y⟩ and let γi​(F2​) be its lower central series. The class-two quotient F2​/γ3​(F2​) is the Integer Heisenberg group. In the class-three free nilpotent group N=F2​/γ4​(F2​), the module γ2​(F2​)/γ4​(F2​) is cyclic over ZG on [x,y] and is isomorphic to ZG/I2. Quotienting it by I/I2 gives the central kernel ⟨[x,y]⟩≅Z of the Heisenberg group. The kernel of
N⟶F2​/γ3​(F2​)
(5)
is γ3​(F2​)/γ4​(F2​), freely generated by [[x,y],x] and [[x,y],y], and is central. Thus it is Z2. This is the central nonsplit extension of the integer Heisenberg group by Z2. If it split, centrality would give N≅H(Z)×Z2, whose abelianization has rank four; but N has abelianization Z2. Hence the extension is nonsplit.

For the free presentation G=F/R, conjugation in F gives the relation module
Rab​=R/[R,R],g⋅(r[R,R])=frf−1[R,R],
(6)
where f∈F is any lift of g∈G. A different lift differs by an element of R, whose inner conjugation acts trivially on the abelianization, so this is a well-defined ZG-module action.
Choose free generators x1​,…,xn​ of F. The presentation relation sequence becomes
0⟶Rab​j​(ZG)nd1​​ZGε​Z⟶0,
(7)
where d1​(ei​)=xˉi​−1. In Fox calculus, the first map is
j(r[R,R])=∑i=1n​∂xi​∂r​​ei​.
(8)
The Fox identity r−1=∑i​(∂r/∂xi​)(xi​−1) gives d1​j=0, and the standard lifting argument in the free group proves exactness.
Split the sequence at the augmentation ideal IG​. Applying HomZG​(−,M) to
0⟶Rab​⟶(ZG)n⟶IG​⟶0
(9)
and using projectivity of (ZG)n gives
Mn⟶HomG​(Rab​,M)⟶ExtZG1​(IG​,M)⟶0.
(10)
The other short exact sequence 0→IG​→ZG→Z→0 identifies the last group with H2(G,M). A one-cocycle on F is a derivation and is determined freely by its values on x1​,…,xn​, so H1(F,M) is Mn modulo principal derivations. Principal derivations vanish on R, and restriction sends a derivation d to the G-map r[R,R]↦d(r). The preceding cokernel sequence therefore descends to the Mac Lane exact sequence for a free presentation
H1(F,M)⟶HomG​(Rab​,M)⟶H2(G,M)⟶0.
(11)
The left map need not be injective. Take F=⟨t⟩≅Z, R=⟨tm⟩, G≅Cm​, and the trivial module M=Z/mZ, where m>1. Then H1(F,M)≅M, while restriction sends the derivation determined by d(t)=a to
d(tm)=ma=0.
(12)
Thus the left map is zero although its domain is nonzero, and R is nontrivial.

The Artin–Wedderburn theorem says that every central simple algebra A over k has the form Mr​(D) for a finite-dimensional central division algebra D, uniquely up to the evident data. If A and B are central simple, extend scalars to an algebraic closure kˉ. Both become full matrix algebras, hence
(A⊗k​B)⊗k​kˉ≅Mab​(kˉ)
(13)
for suitable a,b. Any nonzero proper ideal of A⊗k​B would extend to one in this simple matrix algebra, and faithful flatness prevents it from vanishing or becoming the whole algebra. The same scalar-extension argument shows that the center is k. This proves the tensor product of central simple algebras theorem.
The Brauer group Br(k) consists of Morita equivalence classes of central simple k-algebras. Its product is [A][B]=[A⊗k​B], its identity is [k], and [A]−1=[Aop] because A⊗k​Aop≅Endk​(A) is a full matrix algebra.
Let L/k be a Finite Galois extension with Galois group GL​, and let ϕ:GL​×GL​→L× be a normalized two-cocycle. The crossed-product algebra of a Galois extension has underlying left L-vector space
A(L,GL​,ϕ)=⨁σ∈GL​​Luσ​
(14)
and multiplication
uσ​a=σ(a)uσ​,uσ​uτ​=ϕ(σ,τ)uστ​.
(15)
The cocycle identity is exactly associativity. After scalar extension to L, the algebra acts by the twisted regular representation and becomes M∣GL​∣​(L); Galois descent shows that it is central simple over k. If ϕ is multiplied by the coboundary of a one-cochain bσ​, rescaling uσ​ by bσ​ gives an isomorphic algebra. Hence the cohomological construction of a Brauer class gives a well-defined map
H2(GL​,L×)⟶Br(k).
(16)
It remains to show that every Brauer class is torsion. For a finite group G, restriction and corestriction on normalized bar cochains satisfy
cor∘res=∑g∈G​g∗=∣G∣
(17)
on cohomology: the first equality follows by summing the translated cochain over coset representatives, and each g∗ is the identity because an inner automorphism is cochain-homotopic to the identity. Restriction to the trivial subgroup is zero in positive degree, so this proves that finite-group cohomology is annihilated by the group order. In multiplicative notation, every α∈H2(GL​,L×) therefore satisfies α∣GL​∣=1.
By the permitted assumption, [A] is the image of such an α for some L. Consequently [A]∣GL​∣=1 in Br(k). By the definition of Brauer equivalence, this says that for some n,
A⊗∣GL​∣≅Mn​(k),
(18)
as required.

For 1→H→G→Q→1, filter a bar resolution of G by the number of quotient variables, or equivalently use the double complex obtained from projective resolutions over ZH and ZQ. Taking cohomology first in the H-direction and then in the Q-direction produces the Lyndon–Hochschild–Serre spectral sequence
E2p,q​=Hp(Q,Hq(H,Z))⟹Hp+q(G,Z).
(19)
The quotient acts on Hq(H,Z) through conjugation. Differentials have bidegree (r,1−r); after determining the E2​-page, one follows these differentials and then resolves the filtration extensions on each total degree.
For the dihedral group of order ten, write G=D10​=C5​⋊C2​, with H=C5​ and Q=C2​. The integral cohomology of a finite cyclic group is
Hq(C5​,Z)=⎩⎨⎧​Z,Z/5,0,​q=0,q>0 even,q odd.​
(20)
If u generates H2(C5​,Z), inversion acts by u↦−u, and hence by (−1)r on ur∈H2r(C5​,Z). Since multiplication by 2 is invertible on Z/5, every positive-degree cohomology group of C2​ with coefficients in this module vanishes. Its invariants are Z/5 when q is divisible by four and zero when q≡2(mod4).
Thus the only nonzero terms are
E20,4r​=Z/5(r≥1),
(21)
together with the bottom row
E20,0​=Z,E22r,0​=Z/2(r≥1).
(22)
Degree considerations leave no possible nonzero differential, so the spectral sequence collapses. In total degrees divisible by four, the Z/2 and Z/5 filtration factors combine uniquely as Z/10 because their orders are coprime. Therefore the integral cohomology of the dihedral group of order ten is
Hn(D10​,Z)≅⎩⎨⎧​Z,0,Z/2,Z/10,​n=0,n odd,n≡2(mod4),n>0 and n≡0(mod4).​
(23)
