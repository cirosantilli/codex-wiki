In the razor-thin disk approximation, the three-dimensional Poisson equation is
∇2Φd​=4πGΣ(x,y)δ(z).
(1)
For a horizontal Fourier mode with wavevector k and k=∣k∣>0, the Fourier transform of this equation is
(dz2d2​−k2)Φd​=4πGΣδ(z).
(2)
The solution that decays away from the disk is
Φd​(k,z)=−k2πG​Σ(k)e−k∣z∣.
(3)
Consequently the required razor-thin disk Poisson kernel in the midplane is
Φd,m​(k)=−k2πG​Σ(k)​.
(4)
The spatially uniform k=0 background is excluded from this local perturbation formula.

The constant term of the real cubic dispersion relation is
c=νk2(3βΩ2−2πGΣ0​k+vs2​k2).
(15)
A negative c guarantees a positive real root because the cubic tends to +∞ as λ→+∞.
If β<0, then c<0 at sufficiently small positive k, producing a viscous instability of an accretion disk. This is the local form of the global thin-disk diffusion equation
∂t∂Σ​=r3​∂r∂​[r1/2∂r∂​(νΣr1/2)].
(16)
Since d(νΣ)/dΣ<0, the effective diffusion reverses sign and amplifies surface-density variations.
If β>0, instability is still possible when the quadratic expression in parentheses is negative. Its minimum occurs at k=πGΣ0​/vs2​, so the condition is
3βΩ2−vs2​(πGΣ0​)2​<0,
(17)
or
Q<3β​1​​.
(18)
This is a secular gravitational instability of an astrophysical disk: viscosity allows self-gravity to overcome rotational support even in part of the range that is stable by the inviscid Q<1 criterion.

For a circular orbit of cylindrical radius r in an axisymmetric gravitational potential,
Ω2=r1​∂r∂Φ​(r,0).

Gas in hydrostatic balance has zero vertical velocity. A dust grain subject to linear drag with aerodynamic stopping time τ therefore obeys
z¨+τ1​z˙+Ωz2​z=0​.
(46)
This is a damped harmonic oscillator. For Ωz​τ>1/2, the motion is underdamped, with angular frequency
Ωz2​−4τ21​​
(47)
and envelope e−t/(2τ). Critical damping occurs at Ωz​τ=1/2. For Ωz​τ<1/2, the motion is overdamped. In the strong-drag limit Ωz​τ≪1, a rapid transient on timescale τ leaves slow dust settling in an astrophysical disk at rate Ωz2​τ; in the weak-drag limit the grain makes many damped vertical oscillations.
