f(t)=d(x,γ(t)).
(29)
The triangle inequality makes f Lipschitz continuous and hence a continuous function. Since γ is an isometric embedding,
∣t∣=d(γ(t),γ(0))≤f(t)+f(0),
(30)
so f(t)→∞ as ∣t∣→∞. The function is therefore coercive, and the extreme value theorem on a sufficiently large compact interval gives a minimizing parameter.
Suppose t1​,t2​ both minimize f, put p=γ(t1​) and q=γ(t2​), and let
R=d(x,p)=d(x,q),L=d(p,q)=∣t1​−t2​∣.
(31)
The restriction of γ between the two parameters is a geodesic from p to q. Let m be its midpoint. In the geodesic triangle with vertices x,p,q, the thin geodesic triangle condition gives a point y on one of the other two sides with d(m,y)≤δ. By symmetry suppose y∈[x,p]. Then
d(p,y)≥d(p,m)−d(m,y)≥L/2−δ,
(32)
so
d(x,m)≤d(x,y)+δ=R−d(p,y)+δ≤R−L/2+2δ.
(33)
But m lies on γ(R) and p is a closest point, so R≤d(x,m). Therefore L≤4δ, which is stronger than the required
∣t1​−t2​∣≤6δ.
(34)
This is the closest point on a geodesic line in a hyperbolic metric space estimate.
