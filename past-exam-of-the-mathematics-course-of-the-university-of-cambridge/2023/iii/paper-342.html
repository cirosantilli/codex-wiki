Let x and y denote the two oriented noncontractible cycles of the torus. Their transport operators intersect once, so the mutual braiding phase gives the Wilson-loop algebra of an Abelian Chern--Simons theory
Tx​(q′)Ty​(q)=e2πiq′TK−1qTy​(q)Tx​(q′).​
(19)
The integer charge vectors are identified modulo local electrons: q and q+Kℓ have identical braiding with every quasiparticle. The distinct topological charges therefore form the finite quotient group
A=Zn/KZn,∣A∣=∣detK∣.
(20)
Choose a ground state that diagonalizes the mutually commuting x-cycle operators. Acting with Ty​(q) multiplies their eigenvalues by the character q′↦e2πiq′TK−1q. This pairing is nondegenerate on A: if the character is trivial for every q′, then K−1q is integral and q is the trivial class. Thus the ∣A∣ states obtained from inequivalent q have distinct loop eigenvalues and are orthogonal. The torus ground-state degeneracy of an Abelian Chern--Simons theory is consequently at least ∣detK∣.
The all-ones matrix has eigenvalue n along t and eigenvalue zero on the (n−1)-dimensional perpendicular subspace. Hence K=In​+pM has eigenvalues 1+np,1,…,1, and
∣detK∣=∣1+np∣.​
(21)

The commuting terms
Sj​=Xj​Xj+1​,1≤j<n,
(22)
are independent stabilizer generators. Since J>0, every ground state has Sj​=+1. The resulting two-dimensional stabilizer subspace is
CnZ​=span{∣+⟩⊗n,∣−⟩⊗n},
(23)
the phase-flip repetition code. A convenient pair of logical Pauli operators is
Z=X1​,X=Z1​Z2​⋯Zn​.
(24)
Indeed, they commute with every stabilizer, anticommute with each other, and are not stabilizers.
For phase-flip errors EA​=∏j∈A​Zj​ and EB​=∏j∈B​Zj​, the operator entering the Knill--Laflamme condition is EA†​EB​=EA△B​ and has weight at most 2t. Every nonempty proper product of Zj​ anticommutes with some Sj​ unless it is the full logical operator X. The Knill--Laflamme conditions therefore hold whenever 2t<n, and fail once two allowed errors can differ by X. Thus
t=⌊2n−1​⌋.​
(25)
Each physical Xj​ commutes with all stabilizers, and Xj​X1​ is a product of stabilizers. Hence every Xj​ acts on the code as the undetectable logical operator Z. The code cannot detect, and therefore cannot correct, even a single bit flip.

A syndrome determines a phase-error set only up to multiplication by X=Z1​⋯Zn​: an error of weight w and its complement of weight n−w have the same error syndrome. Their probability ratio is
pn−w(1−p)wpw(1−p)n−w​=(1−pp​)2w−n.
(26)
For p<1/2, maximum likelihood therefore chooses the representative of smaller weight. This is exactly majority-vote decoding of a repetition code: correction succeeds when fewer than half the qubits are flipped, with a random tie-break at w=n/2 when n is even.
If W∼Bin(n,p), then EW=np and sd(W)=np(1−p)​. For every fixed p<1/2, the distance from the mean to the decision boundary in standard deviations is
np(1−p)​n/2−np​=n​p(1−p)​1/2−p​⟶∞.
(27)
Thus the logical failure probability Pr(W>n/2) tends to zero, in fact exponentially by a Chernoff bound. At p=1/2 the two representatives are equiprobable and decoding cannot improve with n. Hence
pc​=21​.​
(28)

Label the physical qubits by (i,j), with outer block 1≤i≤n and inner position 1≤j≤m. The inner bit-flip repetition code has stabilizers
Si,jZ​=Zi,j​Zi,j+1​,1≤j<m,
(29)
and logical operators Xi​=∏j=1m​Xi,j​ and Zi​=Zi,1​. Replacing each outer Xi​ by Xi​ gives the outer stabilizers
SiX​=∏j=1m​Xi,j​Xi+1,j​,1≤i<n.
(30)
This is the surface code on a chain of spheres. The n+1 pole-touching points are vertices, and the m longitudes on sphere i are its qubit-carrying links. At an interior touching point, the star operator is exactly SiX​. Each face between adjacent longitudes has the two-edge plaquette operator Zi,j​Zi,j+1​; only m−1 of the m face operators on each sphere are independent.
Using the conventions of part a, logical operators are
Z=j=1∏m​Xi,j​,X=i=1∏n​Zi,j​.​
(31)
The first may be placed on any one sphere and is a dual equatorial cut crossing all m longitude links. The second may use any fixed longitude and is a pole-to-pole path through all n spheres. Multiplication by stabilizers deforms either representative without changing its logical action. Their minimum weights are m and n, respectively, so the distance of a stabilizer code is
d=min(m,n).​
(32)

Under independent physical phase flips, an inner block suffers an effective phase flip of its encoded qubit exactly when it contains an odd number of errors. The binomial parity identity gives
Pr(even)+Pr(odd)=1,Pr(even)−Pr(odd)=(1−2p)m,
(33)
and hence the effective outer error probability is
qm​=Pr(odd)=21−(1−2p)m​.​
(34)
For every p<1/2 and finite m, one has qm​<1/2, so outer majority-vote decoding succeeds with probability tending to one as n→∞. At p=1/2, one has qm​=1/2. The physical threshold is therefore still
pc​=21​.​
(35)
For fixed p∈(0,1/2), however, qm​ increases monotonically to 1/2 as m grows. Its distance from threshold is
21​−qm​=2(1−2p)m​.
(36)
Consequently the majority-vote standardized separation is only of order (1−2p)mn​, and the large-deviation exponent is of order n(1−2p)2m. Thus larger m leaves the threshold unchanged but makes the logical phase-error probability decay more slowly with n.

The continuum Hamiltonian is that of a spinless one-dimensional p-wave topological superconductor. Its topological phase has μ>0, whereas the empty vacuum may be modeled as the trivial phase with μ<0. With superconductor at x<0 and vacuum at x>0, this is
sgnμ(x)=−sgnx.​
(37)
Near the transition μ=0, momenta are small and the quadratic term may be neglected. In Pauli-matrix notation, the long-wavelength Bogoliubov--de Gennes Hamiltonian is
Hlw​=−μ(x)τz​−iℏΔτx​∂x​.
(38)
The zero-energy equation becomes
∂x​ψ=ℏΔμ(x)​τy​ψ.
(39)
Choose a constant spinor χ+​ with τy​χ+​=χ+​. Then
ψ(x)=Nexp(∫0x​ℏΔμ(s)​ds)χ+​​
(40)
decays on both sides because μ changes from positive to negative. For asymptotically constant ∣μ∣, the Continuum p-wave Majorana interface mode has width
ξ=∣μ∣ℏΔ​.​
(41)
Its characteristic momentum is ∣p∣∼∣μ∣/Δ. The neglected kinetic energy is small compared with ∣μ∣ when ∣μ∣≪mΔ2 up to a factor of two, equivalently when ξ≫ℏ/(mΔ).
In the Nambu basis, Particle-hole symmetry of a Bogoliubov--de Gennes Hamiltonian lets a zero-energy eigenvector be chosen self-conjugate, v(x)=u(x)∗. The corresponding quasiparticle operator is
γ=∫dx[u(x)c(x)+u(x)∗c†(x)].​
(42)
Taking the adjoint and interchanging the two terms gives γ†=γ. With the usual normalization it obeys {γ,γ}=2, so it is a Majorana fermion operator localized at the interface.

A Majorana zero mode is a localized Hermitian quasiparticle operator γj​=γj†​ that commutes with the Hamiltonian within the low-energy approximation and obeys the Clifford relations
{γi​,γj​}=2δij​.
(43)
An exchange is adiabatic when its duration is long compared with ℏ divided by the bulk excitation gap, so the adiabatic theorem keeps the state in the ground-state manifold. It should also be short compared with the inverse exponentially small zero-mode splitting if that splitting is not neglected.
Locality and conservation of fermion parity imply that exchanging modes a and b maps
γa​⟼∓γb​,γb​⟼±γa​,
(44)
with all other well-separated Majoranas unchanged. Since (γa​γb​)2=−1, exponentiation gives
Rab±​=exp(±4π​γa​γb​)=2​1​(1±γa​γb​).
(45)
Direct conjugation using the Clifford relations produces exactly the displayed quarter-turn of (γa​,γb​). Thus, up to an overall physically irrelevant phase,
Rab±​=e±πγa​γb​/4.​
(46)
The two signs are inverse Majorana braiding operators and correspond to the two braid orientations, clockwise and counterclockwise.

Immediately before measuring Qb0​=iγb​γ0​, the state has definite parity for a bilinear such as Qa0​ or the restored reference QN0​ that anticommutes with Qb0​. If Q∣ψ⟩=s∣ψ⟩ and {Q,Qb0​}=0, then

(53)
If the undesired value of sb​ occurs, perform a reference measurement that projects the ancillary pair back toward its previous parity sector, then measure Qb0​ again. This Forced Majorana parity measurement has a geometric waiting time, succeeds almost surely, and does not measure the encoded parity iγa​γb​ directly. Apply the same repeat-until-success procedure to each required outcome in Π+(N0)​Πsa​(a0)​Πsb​(b0)​; alternatively, keep arbitrary outcomes and track the known inverse-braid or Pauli-frame correction determined by their signs.
