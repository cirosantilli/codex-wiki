The first operator is a Lorentz scalar formed from commuting translations. Since [Pμ​,Pν​]=0,
[P2,Pρ​]=0,
(1)
and the vector transformation law of Pμ gives
[Mρσ​,PμPμ​]=0.
(2)
The Pauli-Lubanski pseudovector commutes with every Pμ​, so [W2,Pμ​]=0. Its Lorentz-vector commutator similarly gives
[Mρσ​,WμWμ​]=​[Mρσ​,Wμ]Wμ​+Wμ[Mρσ​,Wμ​]=0,​
(3)
because the two tensor-rotation terms cancel after relabelling the contracted index. Thus
C1​=P2,C2​=W2
(4)
commute with every generator of the Poincare algebra. They are its two algebraically independent Casimir operators in four spacetime dimensions.

For P2=m2>0, choose the future-pointing rest momentum pμ=(m,0,0,0). Its little group is the rotation group SO(3), or SU(2) on the double cover. Its finite-dimensional unitary irreducible representations are labelled by
j=0,21​,1,23​,…
(5)
and have dimension 2j+1. These are the spin states of a massive particle.
For P2=0, choose pμ=(E,0,0,E). Its little group is the two-dimensional Euclidean group
ISO(2)=SO(2)⋉R2.
(6)
Nontrivial unitary action of the translation subgroup produces infinite-dimensional continuous-spin representations. For the finite-helicity particles observed in ordinary relativistic field theory, that subgroup acts trivially, leaving a one-dimensional representation of rotations about p. Such representations are labelled by helicity.

The helicity of a massless state is the component of angular momentum along its momentum,
λ=∣P∣J⋅P​.
(7)
When the translation part of the massless little group is trivial, the Pauli-Lubanski pseudovector obeys
Wμ=λPμ.
(8)
A rotation through angle θ about P acts by the phase e−iλθ. On the double cover, a 4π rotation is the identity, so
e−4πiλ=1⟹λ∈21​Z.
(9)
Hence λ=0,±21​,±1,±23​,…. A parity-invariant theory pairs the λ and −λ representations, although one chiral massless representation need not contain both.

For a null momentum qμ, impose transversality
qμ​ϵμ(q,λ)=0.
(10)
This removes one of four vector components. The remaining equivalence
ϵμ∼ϵμ+αqμ
(11)
removes a second component, leaving two transverse polarizations. For momentum along the third axis they may be chosen as
ϵ±μ​=2​1​(0,1,±i,0),
(12)
which carry helicity ±1.
The equivalence is a gauge redundancy: vectors that differ by a multiple of qμ describe the same physical state rather than distinct measurable configurations. A Lorentz transformation of a chosen transverse representative can require a compensating gauge transformation. Therefore the amplitude
M=ϵμ​Mμ
(13)
must be invariant under ϵμ​↦ϵμ​+αqμ​. For arbitrary α, this is exactly the Ward identity
qμ​Mμ=0.​
(14)

For emission of a soft photon of momentum q from external charged particles, the Soft photon theorem gives
Mμ(q)=(∑i​ηi​Qi​pi​⋅qpiμ​​)M0​+O(q0),
(15)
where ηi​=+1 for outgoing and −1 for incoming particles. Contracting with qμ​ and applying the Ward identity gives
(∑i​ηi​Qi​)M0​=0.
(16)
For a nonzero underlying amplitude, total outgoing charge equals total incoming charge. Thus photon gauge redundancy enforces conservation of electric charge.
The analogous Soft graviton theorem for helicity two requires universal coupling to energy-momentum and yields conservation of total four-momentum, the seed of the Equivalence principle. For massless helicity greater than two, the corresponding soft consistency conditions demand conserved higher-rank momentum charges that generic interacting scattering cannot satisfy. Under the assumptions of a Lorentz-invariant local S-matrix with long-range interactions, such particles therefore have no nontrivial coupling; this is the soft-theorem obstruction to interacting massless higher-spin particles.

Use singular value decomposition to choose unitary matrices satisfying
UuL†​Mu​UuR​=diag(mu​,mc​,mt​),UdL†​Md​UdR​=diag(md​,ms​,mb​).
(30)
The neutral currents remain flavour diagonal because the same unitary matrix occurs on both sides of each bilinear. The charged current contains the mismatch
VCKM​=UuL†​UdL​,
(31)
and becomes
LW​=2​g​[Wμ+​uiL​γμ(VCKM​)ij​djL​+Wμ−​diL​γμ(VCKM†​)ij​ujL​].
(32)
A general unitary 3 by 3 matrix has nine real parameters. Independent rephasings of the six quark fields remove five phases because one common baryon-number phase changes nothing. The Cabibbo-Kobayashi-Maskawa matrix therefore has four physical parameters: three mixing angles and one CP-violating phase.

2[Φ]+2=4,2[Ψ]+1=4,

Promoting α to a spacetime-dependent parameter makes the scalar kinetic term vary by both terms linear and quadratic in the gauge field. Merely adding eJμAμ​ accounts for the linear term but is not invariant because the scalar current itself changes under a local transformation.
With
Dμ​Φ=(∂μ​−2ieAμ​)Φ,Aμ​↦Aμ​+e1​∂μ​α,
(42)

∣Dμ​Φ∣2=∣∂μ​Φ∣2+eAμ​JΦμ​+4e2Aμ​Aμ∣Φ∣2.

Exact isospin symmetry would make the proton and neutron degenerate. Their observed mass difference is generated by explicit isospin breaking from md​−mu​ together with electromagnetic effects. The approximate symmetry explains why the splitting is small compared with the nucleon mass, while chiral perturbation theory parametrizes it through symmetry-breaking operators and low-energy constants; symmetry alone does not predict its numerical value.

The strange quark is light enough compared with typical hadronic scales to permit an approximate
SU(3)L​×SU(3)R​⟶SU(3)V​
(64)
chiral expansion. It adds kaons and the eta to the pseudo-Goldstone multiplet, although convergence is poorer because ms​ is substantially larger than mu​,md​.
The charm quark is too heavy for the same light-flavour chiral expansion. At low energies it is integrated out; processes containing charm are instead treated using heavy-quark expansions or other effective theories. Thus the analysis extends approximately to s, but not to c as another pseudo-Goldstone-producing light flavour.
