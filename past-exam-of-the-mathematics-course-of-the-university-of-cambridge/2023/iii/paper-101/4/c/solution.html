Suppose first that A is a unique factorization domain, and let p be a minimal nonzero prime ideal. Choose 0=a∈p and factor it into irreducibles. In a UFD each irreducible is a prime element, so one factor π belongs to p. The nonzero prime ideal (π)⊆p must equal p by minimality.
Conversely, the ascending chain condition in the Noetherian domain implies that every nonzero nonunit factors into irreducibles. Let π be irreducible and choose a prime p minimal over (π). The Krull principal ideal theorem gives htp≤1. Since A is a domain and p=(0), it is a minimal nonzero prime and hence is principal, say p=(q). The divisibility q∣π and irreducibility of π force q to be associate to π, so (π)=p is prime. Thus every irreducible is prime, proving that A is a UFD. This is the Minimal-prime criterion for a Noetherian unique factorization domain.
