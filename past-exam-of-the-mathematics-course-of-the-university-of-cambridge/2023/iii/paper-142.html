Let p:P(E)→X be the projectivization of a real vector bundle, let LE​ be its tautological bundle, and put
u=w1​(LE​)∈H1(P(E);F2​).
(1)
The mod-two projective bundle formula says that H∗(P(E);F2​) is a free H∗(X;F2​)-module on 1,u,…,ud−1. The Projective bundle definition of Stiefel–Whitney classes is the unique relation
ud+p∗w1​(E)ud−1+⋯+p∗wd​(E)=0.
(2)
Apply the splitting principle for real vector bundles. After an injective pullback, write
E=L1​⊕⋯⊕Ld​,E′=L1′​⊕⋯⊕Ld′′​.
(3)
If xi​=w1​(Li​), the projective-bundle relation factors as
∏i=1d​(u+xi​)=0,
(4)
so
w(E)=∏i=1d​(1+xi​).
(5)
The line summands of E⊕E′ are the union of the two lists, hence
w(E⊕E′)=w(E)w(E′).
(6)
Comparing the degree-k components proves the Whitney product formula for Stiefel–Whitney classes
wk​(E⊕E′)=∑i+j=k​wi​(E)wj​(E′).
(7)
Injectivity of the splitting pullback returns the identity to X.
For real line bundles, the transition functions take values in O(1)={±1}. Tensor product multiplies these signs, while the identification {±1}≅Z/2 turns multiplication into addition. The corresponding degree-one characteristic classes therefore satisfy the First Stiefel–Whitney class of a tensor product of real line bundles formula
w1​(L⊗L′)=w1​(L)+w1​(L′).
(8)
Equivalently, this follows from the classification of real line bundles by H1(X;F2​).
Now take M=RPn and E=kγR1,n+1​. Since
E=γR1,n+1​⊗Rk,
(9)
a line in Ex​ is the fixed line γx​ tensored with a line in Rk. Thus the projectivization of copies of the real tautological line bundle is
P(E)≅RPn×RPk−1.
(10)
Let x and v be the degree-one generators pulled back from the first and second factors. The mod-two cohomology ring of real projective space and the Künneth theorem give
H∗(P(E);F2​)≅F2​[x,v]/(xn+1,vk).
(11)
The tautological line LE​ is the tensor product of the two tautological lines, so w1​(LE​)=x+v. In the alternative generator u=w1​(LE​), the same ring is
F2​[x,u]/(xn+1,(u+x)k).
(12)
The stable tangent-bundle identity
TRPn⊕1≅(n+1)γR1,n+1​
(13)
gives
w(TRPn)=(1+x)n+1.
(14)
For the vertical part of the tangent bundle of a projectivized real vector bundle,
1⊕Hom(LE​,ωE​)≅LE∗​⊗p∗E.
(15)
Each of the k line summands on the right has first Stiefel–Whitney class
w1​(LE∗​⊗p∗γ)=w1​(LE​)+x=v.
(16)
The Whitney product formula for Stiefel–Whitney classes therefore yields
w(TP(E))=(1+x)n+1(1+v)k,
(17)
the Total Stiefel–Whitney class of the projectivization of copies of the tautological line.

Ki(X) ≅ ​Ki(S2∧X)≅Ki−2(X).

Ki(Sd)≅Ki−d(pt)≅{Z,0,​i−d even,i−d odd.​

∅=Y−1​⊂Y0​⊂⋯⊂Ym​=Y

The exterior product defines
K0(Y)⊗Ki(X)⟶Ki(Y×X).

The splitting principle for complex vector bundles says that for every complex vector bundle E→X there is a map p:F(E)→X such that p∗ is injective on cohomology and
p∗E=L1​⊕⋯⊕Lr​
(29)
splits into complex line bundles. Write xi​=c1​(Li​) for the formal Chern roots.
Define the Chern character after this injective pullback by
ch(E)=∑i=1r​exi​.
(30)
Each homogeneous component is a symmetric polynomial in the xi​ with rational coefficients, hence a polynomial in the elementary symmetric functions cj​(E). It therefore descends uniquely to Hev(X;Q) and depends only on E. Set
ch(E−F)=ch(E)−ch(F)
(31)
on the Grothendieck group K0(X); additivity under direct sums makes this well defined.
If E has roots xi​ and F has roots yj​, then E⊗F has roots xi​+yj​. Consequently
ch(E⊕F)ch(E⊗F)​=i∑​exi​+j∑​eyj​=ch(E)+ch(F),=i,j∑​exi​+yj​=(i∑​exi​)(j∑​eyj​)=ch(E)ch(F).​
(32)
It also sends the trivial line to 1, so it is a unital ring homomorphism.
For S2n, a generator of K0(S2n) is the n-fold exterior product of the degree-two Bott element. The Chern character respects exterior products, and the degree-two Bott element has Chern character equal, up to sign, to the integral generator of H2(S2;Z). Its n-fold product maps to the integral top-dimensional generator. Hence the Chern character on an even-dimensional sphere is integral.
Let the formal Chern roots of E→S2n be x1​,…,xr​ and write pn​=∑i​xin​. Since
H2j(S2n;Z)=0(0<j<n),
(33)
all lower Chern classes c1​(E),…,cn−1​(E) vanish. The Newton identities then reduce to
pn​=(−1)n+1ncn​(E).
(34)
The degree-2n term of the Chern character is therefore
chn​(E)=n!pn​​=(−1)n+1(n−1)!cn​(E)​.
(35)
Its evaluation on the fundamental class is an integer by integrality of the reduced Chern character. Thus
⟨cn​(E),[S2n]⟩
(36)
is divisible by (n−1)!, proving the Divisibility of the top Chern number on an even-dimensional sphere.
