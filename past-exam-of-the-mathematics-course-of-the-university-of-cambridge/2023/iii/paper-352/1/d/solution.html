The local motion is simple shear with flow direction z, gradient direction r, and vorticity direction θ. Writing q=du/dr, a second-order fluid has first and second normal-stress differences
N1​=τzz​−τrr​=Ψ1​q2,N2​=τrr​−τθθ​=Ψ2​q2.
(11)
Up to an isotropic contribution absorbed into pressure, a convenient representation is
τzz​=(Ψ1​+Ψ2​)q2,τrr​=Ψ2​q2,τθθ​=0.
(12)
Thus both the flow-direction and gradient-direction normal stresses can be nonzero, while the radial momentum equation becomes
drdp​=r1​drd(rτrr​)​−rτθθ​​=rΨ2​​drd​(rq2).​
(13)
The first normal-stress coefficient affects τzz​ but cancels from radial balance.
In the yielded layer,
q=η1​[2G​(rb2​−r)−σy​]>0.
(14)
Moreover,
drd​(rq2)=ηq​[−2G​(rb2​+3r)−σy​]<0.
(15)
Consequently dp/dr has the sign opposite to Ψ2​ and is independent of Ψ1​. For the common case Ψ2​<0, pressure increases radially outward toward the free surface.
