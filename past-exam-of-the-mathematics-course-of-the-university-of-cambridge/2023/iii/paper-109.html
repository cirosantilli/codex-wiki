vi​⋅vi​=1,vi​⋅vj​=0(i=j).
(1)

Again let vi​∈F2n​ be the characteristic vectors. They all lie in the even-weight subspace
E={v∈F2n​:v⋅1=0},
(2)
which has dimension n−1. Their Gram matrix is
G=(vi​⋅vj​)i,j​=Jm​+Im​,
(3)
because its diagonal entries are zero and its off-diagonal entries are one. If Gx=0 and s=∑i​xi​, then x=s1. Consequently
rankG={m,m−1,​m even,m odd.​
(4)
Since G=VVT for a matrix whose rows lie in E, its rank is at most n−1. If n is even, an even m cannot reach n, while an odd m is at most n−1; hence m≤n−1. If n is odd, the same calculation gives m≤n.
Both bounds are sharp. For odd n, take Ai​=[n]∖{i} for 1≤i≤n: each set has even size and two distinct sets meet in the odd number n−2. For even n, apply the same construction to [n−1] and regard the resulting n−1 sets as subsets of [n].

Let k=k1​+⋯+ks​ be an integer partition. Partition [2k] into blocks Xj​ of sizes 2kj​. For every x∈Xj​, include
Aj,x​=Xj​∖{x}.
(5)
There are 2k=n sets. Each has odd size; two sets from the same block meet in 2kj​−2 points, and sets from different blocks are disjoint. Thus every pairwise intersection has even size.
The partition can be recovered from the bipartite incidence graph between the sets and ground points. A block with kj​≥2 gives one connected component containing 2kj​ set-vertices and 2kj​ point-vertices, while a block with kj​=1 gives two isolated edges. Therefore isomorphic families yield the same multiset (k1​,…,ks​). Distinct integer partitions give non-isomorphic families, so there are at least p(k) of them.

We prove the Harris-Kleitman inequality by induction on n. For up-sets A,B⊆P([n]), let A0​,A1​ be their sections according as n is absent or present, and write ai​=∣Ai​∣; define bi​ and ci​=∣Ai​∩Bi​∣ similarly. Monotonicity gives a0​≤a1​ and b0​≤b1​. The induction hypothesis on [n−1] gives
2n−1ci​≥ai​bi​(i=0,1).
(6)
It remains only to observe that
2(a0​b0​+a1​b1​)−(a0​+a1​)(b0​+b1​)=(a1​−a0​)(b1​−b0​)≥0.
(7)
Hence
2n∣A∩B∣≥∣A∣∣B∣.
(8)
The case n=0 starts the induction, so this is a proof from first principles.

Use the four families from the hint. Put N=2n, u=∣U1​∣/N, and v=∣V1​∣/N. The families U1​,V1​ are up-sets, while their complements U2​,V2​ are down-sets. Applying the Harris-Kleitman inequality to the up-sets, and equivalently to the down-sets after taking complements of the ground set, gives
∣U1​∩V1​∣≥Nuv,∣U2​∩V2​∣≥N(1−u)(1−v).
(9)
The cross-incomparability assumption gives
A⊆U1​∩V2​,B⊆U2​∩V1​.
(10)
Consequently
∣A∣≤Nu(1−v),∣B∣≤N(1−u)v.
(11)
The Cauchy-Schwarz inequality now yields
∣A∣​+∣B∣​≤N​(u(1−v)​+(1−u)v​)≤N​=2n/2.
(12)
This is the sharp two-family Cross-Sperner inequality.

Discard ground points lying in no set. For every point x that lies in at least two members, form
Lx​={i:x∈Ai​}⊆[n].
(13)
Every pair of indices lies in exactly one Lx​, and no Lx​ equals [n] because the total intersection is empty. Thus the Lx​ form a finite linear space on the n indices. The number of its lines through index i is at most ai​, with equality unless Ai​ contains private points.
The De Bruijn--Erdos pair-covering inequality says that if ri​ is the number of lines through point i in a nontrivial finite linear space on n points, then
∑i=1n​(2ri​​)≥(2n​).
(14)
Applying it here gives
∑i=1n​(2ai​​)≥∑i=1n​(2ri​​)≥(2n​).
(15)
Moreover, a pair of ground points can lie in at most one Ai​, since two different members meet in only one point. Hence
∑i​(2ai​​)≤(2m​).
(16)
Combining the inequalities gives (2m​)≥(2n​), and therefore m≥n.

Choose a Uniformly random maximal chain in a Boolean lattice. A fixed h-element set lies on the chain with probability (hn​)−1. Since an antichain meets any chain at most once, the expected number of its members on the chain is at most one:
∑h=0n​∣Ah​∣(hn​)−1≤1.
(19)
This is the Lubell-Yamamoto-Meshalkin inequality.
For the equality case, every maximal chain must meet A. Suppose S∈A has size h and T is obtained from S by exchanging one element. There is a maximal chain whose rank-h member is T, whose rank-(h−1) member is S∩T, and whose rank-(h+1) member is S∪T. Every lower member is contained in S and every higher member contains S, so antichainness excludes all of them. Equality forces T∈A. The graph of h-subsets joined by one-element exchanges is connected, hence A=[n](h). Conversely, a full level clearly gives equality.
Since (hn​)≤(⌊n/2⌋n​),
(⌊n/2⌋n​)∣A∣​≤∑h​∣Ah​∣(hn​)−1≤1.
(20)
The equality characterization above leaves exactly either middle level. This proves Sperner theorem with its equality cases.

Fix A∈F and consider the traces
T={A∩B:B∈F∖{A}}⊆P(A).
(21)
For distinct B,C∈F∖{A}, the hypothesis applied in both orders says
A∩B⊆C,A∩C⊆B.
(22)
Equivalently, neither trace contains the other. Thus the traces are distinct and form an antichain in the Boolean lattice on the 2r points of A. By Sperner theorem,
∣F∣−1=∣T∣≤(r2r​),
(23)
which is the required bound.
