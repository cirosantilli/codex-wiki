An irreducible complex analytic hypersurface in a complex manifold X is a closed irreducible analytic subset of pure complex codimension one. A local defining function of a complex analytic hypersurface Y at x is a holomorphic function f on a neighbourhood U such that
Y∩U={f=0}.
(11)
The necessary local algebra is that the stalk OX,x​ is a regular local ring, hence a unique factorization domain, and that the local branches of a hypersurface germ determine finitely many height-one prime ideals. Each is principal; the product of their generators gives f, and removing repeated factors makes it reduced. This also covers a globally irreducible hypersurface that has several local branches at a singular point.
A divisor on a complex manifold is a locally finite formal sum D=∑Y​nY​Y of irreducible analytic hypersurfaces with integer coefficients. On a sufficiently small Uα​, local defining functions give a meromorphic equation fα​ for D. The quotients fα​/fβ​ are nowhere-zero holomorphic functions. Gluing frames by
eα​=(fβ​/fα​)eβ​
(12)
produces the holomorphic line bundle associated to a divisor [D], and fα​eα​ gives its canonical meromorphic section with divisor D.
The Euler sequence on complex projective space
0⟶O⟶O(1)⊕(n+1)⟶TCPn⟶0
(13)
implies detTCPn≅O(n+1). Taking the dual determinant yields the canonical bundle of complex projective space
KCPn​≅O(−n−1)≅[−(n+1)H],
(14)
where H is a hyperplane divisor.
The hypotheses on the homogeneous polynomial p say that
V={p=0}⊂CPn
(15)
is a smooth projective hypersurface of degree k, so its divisor line bundle is [V]≅O(kH). The adjunction formula gives
KV​≅(KCPn​⊗[V])∣V​≅OV​(k−n−1)≅[(k−n−1)H∣V​].
(16)
This is the canonical bundle of a smooth projective hypersurface.
Now fix an isomorphism Φ:[P]→[Q] and regard sP​ and Φ−1sQ​ as holomorphic sections of the same holomorphic line bundle. They have no common zero because P=Q. Their homogeneous coordinates therefore define a well-defined holomorphic map
F:S⟶CP1,F(x)=[sP​(x):Φ−1sQ​(x)].
(17)
In a local frame, the quotient
f=Φ−1sQ​sP​​
(18)
is a meromorphic function with divisor (f)=P−Q. Thus F−1(0)=P and F−1(∞)=Q, both with multiplicity one. The degree of a holomorphic map F is therefore one. A nonconstant degree-one holomorphic map between compact connected Riemann surfaces is a biholomorphism, so the displayed map is biholomorphic.
