Let I(L/K) be the inertia group and let φ denote Frobenius on the residue-field extension. The Relative Weil group is
W(L/K)={σ∈Gal(L/K):σˉ=φn for some n∈Z}.
(1)
Its Weil-group topology makes I(L/K) an open profinite group with its usual topology and gives W(L/K)/I(L/K) the discrete topology. Thus every inertia coset is an open copy of I(L/K).
Take L=Knr, the maximal unramified extension of K. Then
Gal(L/K)≅Z,W(L/K)≅Z.
(2)
The subgroup {0}⊂Z is open in the discrete Weil-group topology, but it is not open in the profinite topology inherited from Z.

The main theorem of local class field theory gives a continuous Local Artin map
ArtK​:K×⟶Gal(Kab/K)
(3)
with dense image, normalized by sending a uniformizer to a chosen Frobenius. For every finite abelian extension L/K, it induces the Local Artin reciprocity isomorphism
K×/NL/K​(L×)≅Gal(L/K).
(4)
The existence theorem of local class field theory says that the finite-index open subgroups of K× are exactly the norm subgroups NL/K​(L×) for finite abelian extensions L/K, and that the extension is uniquely determined inside Kab.
Write e=e(L/K) and f=f(L/K). The valuation of a field norm satisfies
vK​(NL/K​x)=fvL​(x),
(5)
so the valuation image of the norm subgroup is fZ. The exact sequence obtained from vK​:K×→Z therefore gives
[K×:NL/K​(L×)]=f[OK×​:NL/K​(OL×​)].
(6)
The left side is [L:K]=ef by Local Artin reciprocity. Cancelling f proves
[OK×​:NL/K​(OL×​)]=e.​

The cyclotomic extension of a p-adic field
L1​=Qp​(ζp​)
(8)
has degree p−1, is Galois with group (Z/pZ)×, and is totally ramified. Indeed, ζp​−1 is a root of the Eisenstein polynomial
X(X+1)p−1​.
(9)
Put α=p−1−p​. Its polynomial Xp−1+p is Eisenstein, so L2​=Qp​(α) is totally ramified of degree p−1. Every (p−1)st root of unity lies in Qp​ by the Teichmuller lifts, so every root ωα of this polynomial lies in L2​. Hence L2​/Qp​ is also Galois.
For either Li​/Qp​, the norm has every possible valuation because the residue-field degree is one. The norm units in a tamely totally ramified extension lie in the principal units 1+pZp​: reduction of a unit norm is the (p−1)st power of its residue, hence is 1. Part (b) says that this unit norm subgroup has index p−1, exactly the index of 1+pZp​ in Zp×​. Consequently
NL1​/Qp​​(L1×​)=pZ(1+pZp​)=NL2​/Qp​​(L2×​).
(10)
The uniqueness clause in the existence theorem of local class field theory now gives
L1​=L2​.​
(11)

One strong form of Hensel lemma is this: if K is complete for a discrete valuation v, f∈OK​[X], and a0​∈OK​ satisfies
v(f(a0​))>2v(f′(a0​)),
(12)
then there is a unique root α satisfying
v(α−a0​)>v(f′(a0​)).
(13)
Define the Newton iteration over a valued field
an+1​=an​−f′(an​)f(an​)​.
(14)
Taylor expansion and the ultrametric inequality show inductively that v(f′(an​))=v(f′(a0​)) and
v(f(an+1​))≥2v(f(an​))−2v(f′(a0​)).
(15)
Thus the valuations of the corrections an+1​−an​ tend to infinity, so (an​) is a Cauchy sequence. Completeness gives a limit α, and continuity gives f(α)=0. If β is another root in the stated ball, Taylor expansion of f(β)−f(α) shows that the linear term has strictly smaller valuation than all higher terms unless β=α, proving uniqueness.

Every x∈Q2×​ has a unique form 2nu with n∈Z and u∈Z2×​. Its square class first records nmod2. An odd 2-adic unit is a square precisely when it is congruent to 1 modulo 8: necessity follows by squaring an odd integer, and sufficiency follows from Hensel lemma applied in its standard 2-adic square-root form.
The odd residues 1,3,5,7 modulo 8 therefore give four unit square classes. Together with valuation parity this yields
Q2×​/(Q2×​)2≅(Z/2Z)3.
(16)
For example, the classes of −1, 2, and 5 form a basis of the square-class group of the 2-adic numbers.

Krasner's lemma says that if K is complete, α is separable over K, and
∣β−α∣<minσ(α)=ασ∈Gal(K/K)​​∣α−σ(α)∣,
(32)
then K(α)⊆K(β).
To prove it, let τ be an automorphism of K fixing K(β). The absolute value has a unique extension to every finite extension of the complete field K, so it is invariant under τ. If τ(α)=α, the ultrametric inequality and the displayed strict inequality give
∣τ(α)−β∣=∣τ(α)−α∣>∣α−β∣.
(33)
But τ(β)=β and invariance gives ∣τ(α)−β∣=∣α−β∣, a contradiction. Every automorphism fixing K(β) therefore fixes α, which proves the field inclusion by Galois correspondence.
Now let L/Qp​ be finite. By the primitive element theorem, write L=Qp​(α) with separable minimal polynomial f∈Qp​[X]. Approximate the coefficients of f closely by those of a polynomial g∈Q[X] of the same degree. Continuity of roots over a non-Archimedean field gives a root β of g arbitrarily close to α. Choose it close enough for Krasner's lemma; then
Qp​(α)⊆Qp​(β).
(34)
The degree bound from degg=degf forces equality. If F=Q(β) and p is the prime selected by the embedding F↪Q​p​, its completion is
Fp​≅Qp​(β)=L.
(35)
Thus every finite extension of Qp​ is the completion of a number field at a prime ideal.

Let G=Gal(L/K) and let P,Q lie over p. These primes define two extensions to L of the p-adic absolute value on K. The conjugacy of extensions of a valuation to a normal extension says that some σ∈G carries the first extension to the second. Equivalently, σP=Q. This proves the transitivity of the Galois action on primes.
The decomposition group is the stabilizer
GP/p​={σ∈G:σP=P}.
(36)
Each such automorphism extends continuously to the completions, and restriction gives the decomposition group of a prime and local Galois group isomorphism
GP/p​≅Gal(LP​/Kp​).
(37)
For the splitting field L=Q(37​,ζ3​) of X3−7, the global Galois group is S3​. Since 7≡1(mod3), the field Q7​ contains ζ3​. The local splitting field is therefore Q7​(37​), an Eisenstein, totally ramified cyclic extension of degree three. Hence there are
[S3​:C3​]=2
(38)
primes of L above 7, and the decomposition group of each is the normal subgroup
A3​≅C3​.​

For the discrete valuation v corresponding to ∣⋅∣, the valuation ring and its maximal ideal are
OK​={x∈K:v(x)≥0},m={x∈K:v(x)>0}.
(40)
The first is a subring of the field K, hence an integral domain. An element of OK​ is a unit exactly when its valuation is zero, so every nonunit lies in m and m is the unique maximal ideal.
Choose a uniformizer π with v(π)=1. If I=0 is an ideal, the set of valuations of its nonzero elements has a least member n. Choose x∈I with v(x)=n. Then x=uπn for a unit u, so (x)=(πn). Every y∈I has v(y)≥n, hence y/x∈OK​, and therefore I=(x). Thus OK​ is a discrete valuation ring, in particular a principal ideal domain.

If K is complete and L/K is finite, the unique extension of an absolute value to a finite extension is
∣x∣L​=∣NL/K​(x)∣1/[L:K].
(41)
It restricts to the given absolute value because NL/K​(a)=a[L:K] for a∈K. The usual construction through multiplication by x on the finite-dimensional K-vector space L, together with completeness, proves that this is the only extending absolute value.
For σ∈Gal(L/K), the map x↦∣σ(x)∣L​ is another absolute value extending ∣⋅∣ on K. Uniqueness therefore gives
∣σ(x)∣L​=∣x∣L​.​

Assume first that K is complete. If x is integral over OK​, its monic equation and the ultrametric inequality imply ∣x∣L​≤1, so x∈OL​. Conversely, if ∣x∣L​≤1, part (i) gives ∣σ(x)∣L​≤1 for every K-embedding σ. The coefficients of the minimal polynomial of x are elementary symmetric polynomials in its conjugates, so they all lie in OK​. Thus x is integral over OK​, proving the integral closure in a finite extension of a complete discretely valued field identity OL​=OK​​L.
Completeness is necessary. Give Q its 5-adic absolute value, take L=Q(i), and choose the extension corresponding to the prime (2+i) above 5. Then
x=2−i2+i​
(43)
has nonnegative valuation at (2+i), so it belongs to the chosen valuation ring OL​. At the conjugate prime (2−i) it has negative valuation, so it does not lie in the integral closure of Z(5)​ in Q(i). Hence the chosen valuation ring can be strictly larger than the integral closure when the base field is not complete.
