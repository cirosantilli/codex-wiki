After integration by parts, the quadratic action is
S0​[x]=−21​∫dtx(t)(∂t2​+ω2)x(t).
(1)
With the pole prescription appropriate to the conventions in the question, its inverse kernel is
D(t)=∫2πdE​ω2−E2−iϵie−iEt​.
(2)
For t>0 close the contour in the lower half-plane and for t<0 close it in the upper half-plane. The enclosed pole in each case gives
D(t−t′)=2ω1​eiω∣t−t′∣,
(3)
which equivalently satisfies (∂t2​+ω2)D(t)=iδ(t).
The source-dependent Gaussian functional integral is evaluated by translating the integration variable by the classical sourced solution. Completing the square gives
Z0​[J]=Z0​[0]exp[−21​∫dtdt′J(t)D(t−t′)J(t′)].
(4)
Changes in the sign of the source term or of the path-integral phase move factors of i between D and the exponent but leave the contraction rules equivalent.

Replace each occurrence of x(t) in the interaction by the functional derivative that inserts it. In standard Minkowski source conventions,
Z[J]=exp[−3!iλ​∫dt(i1​δJ(t)δ​)3]Z0​[J]

The time-domain Feynman rules are:
  • each internal line joining times t,t′ contributes D(t−t′);
  • each cubic vertex contributes −iλ and an integration ∫dt;
  • divide by the graph's Feynman-diagram symmetry factor;
  • attach the external times to the corresponding lines and retain connected graphs.
There is no order-λ connected two-point correction. Through order λ2, the two connected topologies are the two-vertex fish graph and the one-particle-reducible tadpole graph. With the displayed vertex convention,
⟨Tx(t1​)x(t2​)⟩conn​=​D(t1​−t2​)+2(−iλ)2​∫dtdt′D(t1​−t)D(t−t′)2D(t′−t2​)+2(−iλ)2​∫dtdt′D(t1​−t)D(t2​−t)D(t−t′)D(0)+O(λ4),​
(7)
up to the common factors of i associated with the propagator convention. Vacuum normalization removes disconnected vacuum bubbles.
These integrals are ultraviolet finite in one time dimension: a harmonic-oscillator propagator behaves as E−2 at large frequency, and the loop-frequency integrals have negative superficial degree of divergence. They are also infrared finite because ω>0 supplies a gap. The cubic instability affects nonperturbative convergence but does not create a divergence in these fixed-order integrals.

For the convention in the question, a finite Yang-Mills gauge transformation acts covariantly on the field strength:
Fμν​↦Fμν′​=UFμν​U†
(18)
or with U and U† exchanged if the opposite convention is used for Dμ​. Cyclicity of the matrix trace gives
Tr(Fμν′​F′μν)=Tr(UFμν​FμνU†)=Tr(Fμν​Fμν),
(19)
so the Yang-Mills theory Lagrangian is gauge invariant.
The quadratic gauge-field operator has zero directions Aμ​∼Aμ​+Dμ​α along each gauge orbit. It therefore has no inverse on the full field space. Gauge fixing removes this degeneracy and produces a propagator, while the Faddeev-Popov determinant accounts for the corresponding Jacobian.

At lowest derivative order, a general invariant effective Lagrangian through fourth order in the fields has the schematic form
L=​−21​TrFμν​Fμν+Tr(Dμ​ϕDμϕ)−mϕ2​Trϕ2−λabcd​ϕaϕbϕcϕd+ψˉ​(iD−mψ​)ψ−hA​IA​(ψˉ​,ψ,ϕ,ϕ)−GB​JB​(ψˉ​,ψ,ψˉ​,ψ).​
(23)
Here λabcd​ is any invariant symmetric rank-four tensor, the IA​ run over invariant contractions such as (Trϕ2)ψˉ​ψ and ψˉ​ϕ2ψ, and the JB​ run over gauge- and Lorentz-invariant four-fermion contractions. The two sign symmetries forbid scalar cubic terms and Yukawa terms ψˉ​ϕψ. The covariant kinetic terms automatically contain the allowed cubic and quartic interactions involving Aμ​.
The canonical dimensions are
[Aμ​]=[ϕ]=2d−2​,[ψ]=2d−1​,
(24)
and hence
[g]=24−d​,[λ]=4−d,[hA​]=3−d,[GB​]=2−d,[mϕ2​]=2,[mψ​]=1.
(25)
A coupling is relevant, marginal, or irrelevant when its dimension is positive, zero, or negative at the Gaussian fixed point. Thus gauge and scalar-quartic interactions are marginal in d=4, the mixed two-scalar fermion bilinear is marginal in d=3, and four-fermion interactions are marginal in d=2. Quantum corrections replace this engineering classification near an interacting fixed point by the eigenvalues of its RG stability matrix.

The one-loop four-scalar one-particle-irreducible diagrams, together with permutations of the external scalar labels, are:
  • a scalar bubble with two scalar-quartic vertices;
  • a gauge-scalar box with four Aϕϕ vertices;
  • a triangle with two Aϕϕ vertices and one AAϕϕ seagull vertex;
  • a gauge bubble with two AAϕϕ seagull vertices;
  • when the mixed operators IA​ are present, a fermion bubble with two ϕ2ψˉ​ψ vertices.
The first four are forced by the scalar covariant derivative and scalar potential. There is no ordinary Yukawa fermion box because the imposed ϕ↦−ϕ symmetry forbids a one-scalar fermion vertex.
