The Papkovich–Neuber representation writes a homogeneous incompressible Stokes flow in terms of a harmonic vector field Φ and harmonic scalar χ:
2μu=∇(x⋅Φ+χ)−2Φ,p=∇⋅Φ,
(1)
with ∇2Φ=0 and ∇2χ=0. The representation satisfies incompressibility because ∇2(x⋅Φ)=2∇⋅Φ, and substitution then verifies the Stokes equation.
For a sphere translating with constant vector velocity U, rotational covariance and decay at infinity suggest a harmonic vector monopole and scalar dipole:
Φ=−2r3μa​U,χ=2μa3​r3U⋅x​.
(2)
Substitution gives the translating sphere in Stokes flow
u(x)=4r3a​(I+r2xx​)U+4r3a3​(I−3r2xx​)U​,
(3)
p(x)=2r33μa​U⋅x​.
(4)
At r=a the radial tensor terms cancel and u=U, while u→0 as r→∞, so the no-slip boundary condition and far-field condition hold. The resulting traction integrates to the Stokes drag law 6πμaU in magnitude.

The incident strain at sphere 2 is O(Va/R2). A force-free sphere in this strain creates a stresslet of size O(μa3Va/R2)=O(μVa4/R2), whose velocity back at sphere 1 is O(Va4/R4). Hence
U1​−V=O(Va4/R4)​.
(9)
The nearly uniform returned flow merely advects sphere 1 and does not change its fixed Stokeslet strength, because its applied force remains fixed. The next scattered disturbance is therefore generated by the returned velocity gradient, of order Va4/R5. It induces a stresslet of size O(μVa7/R5) at sphere 1 and hence velocity O(Va7/R7) at sphere 2. This method of reflections for Stokes flow explains both the absence of an O(Va5/R5) term and the next order O(Va7/R7).

ux​+wz​=0,w=−zux​​.

Downslope of the barrier, the two side streams turn inward under the transverse hydrostatic pressure gradient. They enclose a thin wake or thickness deficit immediately behind the barrier, while excess fluid initially remains concentrated near its shoulders. Farther downstream, lateral spreading fills the wake and restores the uniform layer.
To estimate the recovery distance, set h=1+η with ∣η∣≪1 in the governing equation. In a far wake that varies slowly in x,
3ηx​≃ηyy​,
(59)
which is a diffusion equation with downslope coordinate acting as time. A transverse disturbance of width R therefore spreads over downslope distance
x=O(R2)​.
(60)
Before this scale the two-dimensional wake retains the barrier's cross-slope structure; after it, transverse leveling has mixed that structure across its full width and the approach to the uniform film changes character.
