The nonzero orthogonal polynomials Q0​,…,Qn​ have distinct degrees and form an orthogonal basis of Pn​. Define
pn∗​=∑k=0n​⟨Qk​,Qk​⟩⟨f,Qk​⟩​Qk​.
(29)
For each j≤n,
⟨f−pn∗​,Qj​⟩=⟨f,Qj​⟩−⟨Qj​,Qj​⟩⟨f,Qj​⟩​⟨Qj​,Qj​⟩=0.
(30)
Thus the residual f−pn∗​ is orthogonal to all of Pn​.
For any p∈Pn​, write
f−p=(f−pn∗​)+(pn∗​−p).
(31)
The two terms are orthogonal, so the Pythagorean theorem in an inner-product space gives
∥f−p∥2=∥f−pn∗​∥2+∥pn∗​−p∥2≥∥f−pn∗​∥2.
(32)
Equality holds only for p=pn∗​. This proves the formula and uniqueness of the least-squares polynomial in an orthogonal-polynomial basis.
