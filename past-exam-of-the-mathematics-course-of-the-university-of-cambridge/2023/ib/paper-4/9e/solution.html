p∤an​,qquadp∣aj​ (0≤j<n),qquadp2∤a0​,

To prove it, suppose that F=GH with G,H∈Z[X] of positive degree. Primitivity and Gauss's lemma let us take such an integral factorisation if a factorisation over Q exists. Reducing modulo p gives
GH=an​​Xn.
(56)
Because p does not divide the leading coefficient of F, neither factor loses degree on reduction. The polynomial ring Fp​[X] is a unique factorization domain, so both reductions are monomials of positive degree. In particular, p divides both constant terms G(0) and H(0). It follows that p2 divides
F(0)=G(0)H(0)=a0​,
(57)
a contradiction. This proves the criterion.
For a prime p, translate the geometric sum by one:
f(X+1)=X(X+1)p−1​=∑k=1p​(kp​)Xk−1.
(58)
Its leading coefficient is one, every other coefficient is divisible by p, and its constant coefficient is p, which is not divisible by p2. It is therefore Eisenstein at p. Translation X↦X+1 is an automorphism of Z[X], so
f(X)=1+X+⋯+Xp−1 is irreducible.​
(59)
This is the geometric-sum irreducibility criterion.
The evaluation homomorphism
evζ​:Z[X]⟶C,G⟼G(ζ),

has image Z[ζ] and contains (f) in its kernel. Since f is monic, division by f in Z[X] writes every G as G=Qf+R with degR<degf. If G(ζ)=0, then R(ζ)=0; the irreducibility of f says that f is the minimal polynomial of ζ, so R=0. Thus ker(evζ​)=(f), and the first isomorphism theorem for rings gives
Z[ζ]≅Z[X]/(f)​.

Now take p=3. Then ζ2+ζ+1=0, and the Eisenstein integers are
Z[ζ]={a+bζ:a,b∈Z}.

N(a+bζ)=(a+bζ)(a+bζ2)=a2−ab+b2=∣a+bζ∣2.

Given z=x+yζ∈C, choose integers m,n with ∣x−m∣,∣y−n∣≤21​. For q=m+nζ,

Hence the norm is a Euclidean function, proving that Z[ζ] is a Euclidean domain. This is the Euclidean norm on the Eisenstein integers.
Finally suppose A∈GLn​(Z) satisfies A2+A+I=0. Make the free abelian group M=Zn into a Z[ζ]-module by defining
ζv=Av.
(66)
This is well-defined precisely because A obeys the same polynomial relation as ζ. The module is finitely generated. It is also torsion-free module: if 0=α∈Z[ζ] and αv=0, multiplication by the conjugate of α gives N(α)v=0, and the additive group Zn has no nonzero integer torsion.
A Euclidean domain is a principal ideal domain, and the structure theorem for finitely generated modules over a principal ideal domain says that a finitely generated torsion-free module over one is free. Thus
M≅Z[ζ]m
(67)
for some m. Since Z[ζ] has free module basis 1,ζ over Z, comparison of abelian ranks gives n=2m. There can consequently be no such matrix when n is odd.
If n=2m, choose a Z[ζ]-basis v1​,…,vm​. Then
v1​,Av1​,…,vm​,Avm​
(68)

Every admissible matrix is therefore conjugate in GLn​(Z) to C⊕m. Hence there is exactly one conjugacy class for even n, and none for odd n. This is the classification of integral matrices satisfying the third cyclotomic polynomial.
