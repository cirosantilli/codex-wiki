Taking the Laplace transform of the differential equation and using
L{y′}(s)=sY(s)−y(0)
(102)
gives
sY−y0​=AY+G.
(103)
Hence
(sI−A)Y=y0​+G.
(104)
Whenever s is not an eigenvalue of A, the matrix sI−A is invertible, and thus
Y(s)=(sI−A)−1(y0​+G(s))​.
(105)
This is the Laplace-transform solution of a constant-coefficient vector ODE.
Set g=0. The given homogeneous solution is y(t)=etAy0​, so the preceding formula says
L{etA}(s)y0​=(sI−A)−1y0​
(106)
for every initial vector y0​. Equality on every vector gives the matrix identity
L{etA}(s)=(sI−A)−1​,
(107)
on the common domain of convergence and in particular away from the eigenvalues of A. This is the Laplace transform of a matrix exponential.
