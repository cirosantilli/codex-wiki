Suppose first that X is connected and f:X→Z is continuous. Its image is connected by the continuous image of a connected space, but Z is discrete, so its only connected nonempty subsets are singletons. Hence f is constant.
Conversely, if X=U∪V is a disconnection into nonempty disjoint open sets, the function equal to zero on U and one on V is continuous and nonconstant. This proves the integer-valued function criterion for connectedness.
Now let f:X→Z be continuous under the hypotheses on the family A. Each restriction f∣A​ is constant because A is connected. If A,B∈A, a point of A∩B shows that their two constants agree. Since the sets cover X, f is constant on X, and the criterion proves that X is connected. This is the pairwise-intersecting connected cover argument.
Finally, fix y0​∈Y. For each x∈X, the set

(3)
