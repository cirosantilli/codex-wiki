The residue theorem states that if a meromorphic function has finitely many poles inside a positively oriented simple closed contour and none on it, then its contour integral is 2πi times the sum of the enclosed residues.
Apply it to
F(z)=(1+z)2eαL(z)​
(55)
on a keyhole contour around the positive real axis. The outer and inner circles vanish as their radii tend to infinity and zero because α<1 and α>−1, respectively. On the upper bank the numerator tends to xα, while on the lower bank it tends to e2πiαxα and the direction is reversed. Therefore the limiting contour integral is
(1−e2πiα)I.
(56)
The only enclosed pole is the double pole at z=−1. Since L(−1)=iπ,
Resz=−1​F=dzd​eαL(z)​z=−1​=−αeiπα.
(57)
The residue theorem now gives
(1−e2πiα)I=−2πiαeiπα.
(58)
For α=0, division and
1−e2πiα=−2ieiπαsin(πα)
(59)

(60)
At α=0, the integral is one, agreeing with the continuous limit. This is the positive-axis keyhole beta integral.
