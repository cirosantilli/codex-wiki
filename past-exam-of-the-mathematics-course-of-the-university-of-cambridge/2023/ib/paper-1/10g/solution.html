A Cauchy sequence (xn​) in a metric space (M,d) satisfies: for every ε>0 there is N such that d(xm​,xn​)<ε whenever m,n≥N. A complete metric space is one in which every Cauchy sequence converges to a point of the space.
Every Cauchy sequence is bounded. Choose N such that d(xn​,xN​)<1 for n≥N, and put
R=1+max1≤j<N​d(xj​,xN​).
(35)
Then every term lies in the ball B(xN​,R).
Now suppose M is complete and (Fn​) is a decreasing sequence of nonempty closed sets with diamFn​→0. Choose xn​∈Fn​. Given ε>0, choose N with diamFN​<ε. For m,n≥N, both points lie in FN​, so d(xm​,xn​)<ε. Completeness gives xn​→x∈M. For each fixed N, the tail lies in the closed set FN​, hence x∈FN​. Therefore
x∈⋂n=1∞​Fn​.
(36)
Conversely, assume the nested-set property and let (xn​) be Cauchy. Define
Fn​={xm​:m≥n}​.
(37)
These sets are nonempty, closed, and decreasing. The Cauchy property implies diamFn​→0; taking a closure does not change the diameter. Choose x∈⋂n​Fn​. Since xn​,x∈Fn​,
d(xn​,x)≤diamFn​⟶0.
(38)
Thus every Cauchy sequence converges and M is complete. This proves the Cantor intersection theorem characterization.
The contraction mapping theorem states that a contraction of a nonempty complete metric space has a unique fixed point.
For each λ∈Λ, the map Tλ​(x)=T(λ,x) is a contraction with the common constant k, so it has a unique fixed point x∗(λ). This defines the required unique function. Fix λ0​. The fixed-point identities and the triangle inequality give
d(x∗(λ),x∗(λ0​))​≤d(T(λ,x∗(λ)),T(λ,x∗(λ0​)))+d(T(λ,x∗(λ0​)),T(λ0​,x∗(λ0​)))≤kd(x∗(λ),x∗(λ0​))+d(T(λ,x∗(λ0​)),T(λ0​,x∗(λ0​))).​
(39)
Consequently
d(x∗(λ),x∗(λ0​))≤1−kd(T(λ,x∗(λ0​)),T(λ0​,x∗(λ0​)))​.
(40)
The numerator tends to zero as λ→λ0​ by the assumed continuity for the fixed point x∗(λ0​). Hence x∗ is continuous, an instance of continuous dependence of the fixed point of a uniform contraction.
