(16)

Let D1​,D2​,D3​,D4​ denote the open quadrants, counterclockwise from the first. Write θ=kx+2k2t. The Lax pair has the Volterra integral equation kernel e−i[k(x−x′)+2k2(t−t′)]σ3​, obtained by integrating its closed matrix differential form from a normalization point.
In column one, the off-diagonal factor is e2i[k(x−x′)+2k2(t−t′)], whose modulus is e−2Im[k(x−x′)+2k2(t−t′)]. Column two has the reciprocal factor. For μ2​, choose the path (0,0)→(0,t)→(x,t), so both differences are nonnegative. Column one requires Imk≥0 and Imk2≥0, giving D1​; column two requires the opposite inequalities, giving D4​.
For μ1​, the time path starts at T and runs backward, while the spatial path runs rightward. Its column-one conditions are Imk≥0, Imk2≤0, giving D2​; column two is bounded in D3​.
Finally, μ3​ integrates spatially from infinity to x, where x−x′≤0. Column one is bounded in the lower half-plane and column two in the upper half-plane. Normalization at (∞,T) is equivalent to normalization at (∞,t) because the decaying potential vanishes on the vertical path at infinity. Thus
μ1​:(D2​,D3​),μ2​:(D1​,D4​),μ3​:(C−​,C+​).​
(34)
The Volterra integral equation Neumann series converges locally uniformly under the usual integrable smooth scattering assumptions, proving holomorphic dependence in the open domains and continuous boundary values. These are the Volterra analyticity sectors for a half-line NLS Lax pair; at x=0, absence of a spatial segment allows the larger time-only domains used for A,B.

At x=0 the time Lax pair coefficient has off-diagonal entries (2k+ic)q and (2k−ic)qˉ​. Let
K(k)=diag(2k+ic,−2k+ic),h(k)=2k+ic2k−ic​.
(47)
Direct multiplication shows V(0,t,k)=K(k)V(0,t,−k)K(k)−1. Because the commutator term depends on k2 and K commutes with σ3​, uniqueness of the time Volterra integral equation gives S(k)=K(k)S(−k)K(k)−1. Therefore
A(−k)=A(k),B(−k)=−h(k)B(k).​
(48)
The exceptional parameter points of K are treated by analytic continuation. These identities express linearizable Robin scattering for defocusing NLS.
For finite horizon T, the exact upper-half-plane ratio is
A(k;T)B(k;T)​=a(k)b(k)​−a(k)A(k;T)e4ik2TcT​(k)​.
(49)
Consequently the often used answer B/A=b/a is not a literal finite-T identity. Its effective time-independent replacement, in the two bounded time sectors, is
R(k)=⎩⎨⎧​b(k)/a(k),−2k−ic2k+ic​a(−k)b(−k)​,​k∈D1​,k∈D3​.​​

The second branch follows from the Robin symmetry; its argument −k lies in the upper half-plane where the initial scattering functions are known. We now justify this replacement for the inverse problem without assuming that boundary values decay as t→∞.
Only Γ=B♯/(ad) is needed for the connection across the upper imaginary ray. The conjugate global relation at −k and the Robin symmetry give
A♯(k)B♯(k)​=−h(k)a♯(−k)b♯(−k)​+h(k)e−4ik2Ta♯(−k)A♯(k)cT♯​(−k)​.

For upper-half-plane k, the notation b♯(−k)=b(−kˉ)​ uses only values of b in its legitimate upper-half-plane domain. Put
Δ(k)=a(k)a♯(−k)+h(k)b(k)b♯(−k),D(k)=(2k+ic)Δ(k).
(52)
The effective boundary coefficient is
Γ(k)=−a(k)Δ(k)h(k)b♯(−k)​=−a(k)D(k)(2k−ic)b♯(−k)​.​
(53)
This Robin spectral determinant for defocusing NLS is explicitly computed from the initial data and c.
For completeness, the finite-horizon defect can be removed algebraically, not by an unproved limit. If r=B♯/A♯, then Γ=r/[a(a−br)]. Subtracting the same expression with r0​=−hb♯(−k)/a♯(−k) gives

Its jump multiplier is
(Γ−Γ)e2iθ=d(k)Δ(k)h(k)cT♯​(−k)​e2ikx−4ik2(T−t).

The exponential decays in D2​ for x>0 and t<T, since Imk>0 and Imk2<0. The coefficient is holomorphic away from the stated determinant zeros and is appropriately decaying at infinity. From the explicit matrices in part (c), C2​=C1​L(Γe2iθ), where L(z)=(1z​01​). Hence replace the sector-two solution by
M2​=M2​L((Γ−Γ)e2iθ).

The reflected upper-triangular change is made in D3​. These analytic changes remove the finite-time defect, leave M1​,M4​ unchanged, and leave the reconstruction from the second column in D2​ unchanged. At apparent poles, the old d-dependent singularities cancel by the connection formula; genuine poles of the new determinant remain as explicit residue data. Equivalently, small circles can first isolate all zeros and the same algebra can be performed on the regularized contours. This is finite-horizon Robin spectral elimination.
Every modified jump is now explicitly known: keep C1​,C4​ from part (c) and replace
C2​⟼C1​L(Γe2iθ),C3​⟼C4​U(Γ♯e−2iθ),U(z)=(10​z1​).

All jumps are again C−−1​C+​, now involving only a,b,c. If D has a simple zero kj​ giving a genuine pole, its coefficient is
γj​=−a(kj​)D′(kj​)(2kj​−ic)b♯(−kj​)​,Resk=kj​​[M]1​=γj​e2iθ(kj​)[M(kj​)]2​,
(58)
with the reflected second-column condition at kˉj​. Common numerator/denominator zeros are checked for removability, and zeros on the cross require contour indentation or local circle jumps. Thus no unknown boundary norming data remain. One must not assume away such zeros for every real c; attractive Robin parameters can support localized modes.
It follows that the equivalent normalized Riemann-Hilbert problem is specified entirely by q0​ and c, including its pole data. When two solutions exist, their quotient has no jump, its prescribed pole singularities cancel, and it tends to I at infinity; Liouville theorem makes the quotient I, proving uniqueness. Smooth corner evolution additionally requires q0′​(0)=cq0​(0) and higher compatibility conditions at higher regularity. Without that compatibility, the same initial data are interpreted in the weak evolution sense, rather than as a smooth solution through the corner.
