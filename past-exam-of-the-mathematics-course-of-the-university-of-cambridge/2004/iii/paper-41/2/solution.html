In particular ES=λEX and VarS=λEX2. These compound Poisson cumulants use raw claim moments. The first two formulas also follow by conditioning: E[S∣N]=NEX and Var(S∣N)=NVarX, so

This is the law of total variance. They therefore remain valid with finite first two moments even when no positive exponential moment exists.
Under per-claim excess of loss reinsurance, put Y=min(X,M) and let W=(X−M)+​ be the positive part of the excess. The insurer and reinsurer totals are sums of these payments over the same Poisson distribution count. Write F(x)=P(X>x). Using the tail integral formula for moments on the tail probabilities of Y and W gives
ESI​ESR​​=λ∫0M​F(x)dx,=λ∫M∞​F(x)dx,​VarSI​VarSR​​=2λ∫0M​xF(x)dx,=2λ∫M∞​(x−M)F(x)dx.​​
(13)
For example, EY2=∫0∞​P(Y2>t)dt=2∫0M​xF(x)dx after t=x2, and shifting by M gives the reinsurer formula. The moment formulas are finite whenever the corresponding claim moments are finite.
For the specified Pareto distribution, with shape three and minimum d>0,
F(x)={1,d3/x3,​0≤x≤d,x≥d,​EX=3d/2,EX2=3d2.

M∗=43d​,V(M∗)=815​λd2.​

The curve starts at 3λd2, reaches this minimum, passes through V(d)=2λd2 with matching slope λd on both branches, and then increases concavely toward the horizontal asymptote 3λd2. The variance-minimizing retention for shape-three Pareto claims is shown below.
The two aggregate payouts are dependent. Conditioning on the common count gives covariance λCov(Y,W)+λEYEW=λE[YW]=λMEW, so V(M)+2Cov(SI​,SR​)=3λd2 for every reinsurance retention, as required by SI​+SR​=S. The covariance of two components of a compound Poisson sum accounts for this distinction between a sum of variances and the variance of a sum.
There is a moment domain qualification for this heavy-tailed example. For every t>0, ∫d∞​etx3d3x−4dx=∞, and moments of order j≥3 also diverge. Thus its positive moment-generating function is not finite and higher finite cumulants cannot be obtained by differentiating around zero. Its mean and variance calculations above are valid by conditioning or by the first two right derivatives at zero of the Laplace transform of a nonnegative random variable.
