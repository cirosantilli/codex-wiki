Let Λ=∑i=1m​λi​. For Λ>0, the resulting compound Poisson distribution has
count parameter Λ,F(x)=i=1∑m​Λλi​​Fi​(x).​
(1)
Thus the merged claim size probability distribution is a rate-weighted mixture distribution, not an equally weighted mixture distribution unless the rates agree.
To prove this without any moment-generating function assumption, let χi​(t) be the characteristic function of a claim of type i. Conditioning on its Poisson distribution count gives
EeitSi​=exp{λi​(χi​(t)−1)}.
(2)
Independence of the aggregate risks therefore gives
EeitS=∏i​exp{λi​(χi​(t)−1)}=exp{Λ(∑i​Λλi​​χi​(t)−1)}.
(3)
The weighted characteristic function inside is precisely that of the displayed mixture distribution. This is the transform of a sum of independent claims with independent count Pois(Λ), proving the claim by uniqueness of characteristic functions. This is Poisson superposition of insurance portfolios. If Λ=0, all risks make no claims almost surely and S=0; the severity law is then irrelevant.
