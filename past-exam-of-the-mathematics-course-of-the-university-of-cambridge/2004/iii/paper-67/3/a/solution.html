In the Maxwell-Boltzmann velocity distribution, each Cartesian component has mean-square velocity kB​Te​/me​. Thus ⟨v2⟩=3kB​Te​/me​. The original PDF calls this quantity an rms velocity, but the rms speed is its square root. Also, its nγ​ must mean the dimensionless mean occupation number n, since its equilibrium value is a Planck photon distribution; it is not the frequency-integrated photon number density.
Take x=hν/(kB​Tγ​) at a fixed local radiation temperature. Because x is proportional to frequency,
ν2∂ν2​n+4ν∂ν​n=x2∂x2​n+4x∂x​n=x−2∂x​(x4∂x​n).
(24)
The hot-electron Kompaneets equation therefore becomes
∂y∂n​=x−2∂x∂​(x4∂x∂n​),dy=me​c2kB​Te​​ne​σT​cdt.​
(25)
For traversal length dℓ=cdt, the Compton y parameter is
y=me​c2σT​​∫ne​kB​Te​dℓ=me​c2σT​​∫Pe​dℓ.​
(26)
It measures electron pressure integrated along the line of sight, or the Thomson scattering optical depth weighted by kB​Te​/(me​c2). In this hot-electron limit the mean fractional energy gain is approximately 4y. The derivation assumes nonrelativistic electrons, Thomson-limit photon energies and Te​≫Tγ​. The original PDF uses hν, as required for ordinary frequency; the converted TeX incorrectly substitutes ℏν.
