The cosmological perfect-fluid continuity equation gives ρ∝a−3(1+w). In a flat universe the Friedmann equation then gives H(z)=H0​(1+z)α, where α=3(1+w)/2>0. Writing u=1+z, the angular diameter distance obtained from the luminosity distance is

It is positive for u>1, starts at zero and tends to zero as u→∞ for every α>0, including α<1 where the radial comoving distance itself is unbounded. Differentiation gives the constant-equation-of-state angular diameter distance maximum:
zm​=α1/(α−1)−1,α=23​(1+w),zm​=e−1 when α=1.​

For α=1 the stationary equation is uα−1=α, which has exactly one solution greater than one. For α=1 the derivative has the sign of 1−logu. Hence this stationary point is always the unique maximum.
To determine the sketch's shape, differentiate log(1+zm​)=logα/(α−1):
dαdlog(1+zm​)​=(α−1)21−1/α−logα​<0,

with limiting value −1/2 at α=1. The inequality follows because logα−1+1/α has its unique minimum zero at one. Thus zm​ decreases from infinity as w↓−1 to 3​−1 as w↑1. Useful points are zm​=e−1 at w=−1/3, 5/4 at w=0, and 1 at w=1/3. The endpoint w=−1 is excluded: there dA​=z/[H0​(1+z)] increases to an asymptote and has no finite turning point.
