Use absorption distance X and its distribution fX​. Summing hydrogen columns per unit X gives ∫NfX​dN. The physical light-path length per X is
dXdℓ​=H0​(1+z)3c​.
(39)
The mean neutral-hydrogen number density is therefore (H0​/c)(1+z)3∫NfX​dN. Divide by (1+z)3 to obtain its comoving density, multiply by hydrogen atomic mass, and divide by today's mass critical density ρcrit,0​=3H02​/(8πG). This derives the neutral gas density from an absorption distribution:
ΩHI​(z)=cρcrit,0​H0​mH​​∫NfX​(N,z)dN.​
(40)
If “neutral gas” includes helium, replace mH​ by μH​mH​, with a stated abundance correction approximately μH​≃1.3. Equivalently, for the distribution per redshift the prefactor inside the integral is mH​H(z)/[cρcrit,0​(1+z)2] multiplying Nfz​. In the PDF's energy-density convention, both the mass density and critical density are multiplied by c2, leaving their ratio unchanged. The usual quoted abundance uses comoving mass density relative to today's critical density; a ratio of physical density to the epoch's critical density instead multiplies this answer by (1+z)3H02​/H(z)2.
To infer total gas, one needs the neutral fraction xHI​=NHI​/NH​ for each absorber. The ionized gas density from an absorption distribution is
Ωg​(z)=cρcrit,0​H0​μH​mH​​∫xHI​(N,z)N​fX​(N,z)dN.​
(41)
This is an ionization-corrected integral, not an inference available from the observed columns alone. For optically thin, almost fully ionized hydrogen, photoionization equilibrium gives ΓnHI​=α(T)ne​nHII​≃α(T)nH2​. If the absorbing depth L is specified, N=α(T)nH2​L/Γ and
NH​=nH​L=(α(T)ΓNL​)1/2.
(42)
Thus photoionization rate, temperature and depth are all needed.
One useful physical closure sets L to a local Jeans length, L∝(fg​T/nH​)1/2, with local gas fraction fg​. Solving N∝[α(T)/Γ](fg​T)1/2nH3/2​ then gives the Jeans-length ionization correction for the Lyman-alpha forest:
NH​∝N1/3Γ1/3(α(T)fg​T​)1/3,Ωg​∝Γ1/3(α(T)fg​T​)1/3∫N1/3fX​dN.
(43)
This illustrates why weak, highly ionized forest lines can dominate the total gas inventory even though damped Lyman-alpha systems dominate the neutral-column-weighted inventory. It is a model for locally confined optically thin gas; optically thick Lyman limit systems and damped Lyman-alpha systems require a separate radiative transfer treatment. The physical basis and observational application of this closure are developed in Schaye's absorption-based matter-density calculation.
