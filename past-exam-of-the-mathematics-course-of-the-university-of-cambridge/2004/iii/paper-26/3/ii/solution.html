State the characteristic convention explicitly: for real α,β, define the theta function with characteristics by
θ[α,β](z,τ)=n∈Z∑​exp(πi(n+α)2τ+2πi(n+α)(z+β)),Imτ>0.​
(28)
Binary subscripts ϑab​ often denote this function with (α,β)=(a/2,b/2), a,b∈{0,1}. On compact subsets of the (z,τ) domain, a summand and any derivative are bounded by a polynomial in ∣n∣ times e−cn2+C∣n∣. Normal convergence therefore gives an entire function of z, holomorphic dependence on τ, and legitimate termwise differentiation. In particular the heat equation for theta functions with characteristics is
4πi∂τ​θ=∂z2​θ.​
(29)
Characteristics are defined modulo integers with a phase: θ[α+1,β]=θ[α,β] and θ[α,β+1]=e2πiαθ[α,β]. Shifting the summation index proves the spatial quasi-periods
θ(z+1)=e2πiαθ(z),θ(z+τ)=e−πiτ−2πi(z+β)θ(z).
(30)
These multipliers describe a degree-one line bundle on the complex torus with periods 1,τ, rather than an ordinary elliptic function. The logarithmic derivative is unchanged under one and decreases by 2πi under tau. Pairing opposite sides of a fundamental cell therefore makes its contour integral 2πi. The argument principle counts exactly one zero in a cell, with multiplicity.
The characteristic function is a translate of the unshifted theta function:
θ[α,β](z,τ)=eπiα2τ+2πiα(z+β)θ[0,0](z+ατ+β,τ).
(31)
At z=(1+τ)/2, the unshifted series cancels in pairs n,−n−1. Hence the zeros of theta functions with characteristics occur exactly and simply at
z=(1/2−α)τ+(1/2−β)(modZ+Zτ).​
(32)
Reindexing also gives θ[α,β](−z)=θ[−α,−β](z). For half-integer characteristics the parity is e4πiαβ, so only (1/2,1/2) is odd and vanishes at zero; the other three theta constants are nonzero.
The modular transformations of theta characteristics are obtained from Gaussian Poisson summation and elementary phase rearrangement. With the holomorphic branch of −iτ​ positive at τ=i,
θ[α,β](z/τ,−1/τ)=−iτ​eπiz2/τ+2πiαβθ[β,−α](z,τ),​
(33)
θ[α,β](z,τ+1)=e−πiα(α−1)θ[α,β+α−1/2](z,τ).​
(34)
For the first formula, apply Poisson summation to F(t)=eπiτ(t+α)2+2πi(t+α)(z+β). Completing the square in its Fourier transform gives
F(m)=(−iτ)−1/2e2πimα−πi(z+β−m)2/τ.
(35)
Rearranging ∑n​F(n)=∑m​F(m) proves the stated inversion law. For translation by one, use eπin2=eπin in the defining series; the remaining characteristic phase is precisely the displayed factor.
Write Q=eπiτ, so the modular-form variable of Question 1 is q=Q2, and put Θ2​=θ[1/2,0](0,τ), Θ3​=θ[0,0](0,τ) and Θ4​=θ[0,1/2](0,τ). Inversion interchanges Θ2​,Θ4​ and fixes Θ3​, each with factor −iτ​. Translation interchanges Θ3​,Θ4​ and multiplies Θ2​ by eπi/4. The Jacobi triple product gives
Θ2​=2Q1/4∏m≥1​(1−Q2m)(1+Q2m)2,
(36)
Θ3​=∏m≥1​(1−Q2m)(1+Q2m−1)2,Θ4​=∏m≥1​(1−Q2m)(1−Q2m−1)2.
(37)
It also gives the conventional odd theta function θ1​=−θ[1/2,1/2] its product 2Q1/4sin(πz)∏m≥1​(1−Q2m)(1−2Q2mcos(2πz)+Q4m). Differentiate at zero and multiply the three even products to obtain the Jacobi derivative formula
θ1′​(0,τ)=πΘ2​Θ3​Θ4​.​
(38)
In the constant-product simplification, ∏m​(1+Q2m)(1−Q4m−2)=1, by separating even and odd positive exponents. The products prove nonvanishing of all three even constants as well.
The Jacobi abstruse identity can be proved directly, without assuming it from the products. Splitting pairs of summation indices according to whether their sum and difference are even or odd gives
Θ3​(τ)2=Θ3​(2τ)2+Θ2​(2τ)2,Θ4​(τ)2=Θ3​(2τ)2−Θ2​(2τ)2,
(39)
Θ2​(τ)2=2Θ2​(2τ)Θ3​(2τ).
(40)
For example, u=(n+m)/2,v=(n−m)/2 are either both integers or both half-integers, and n2+m2=2u2+2v2. The alternating signs give the second identity; shifting both original indices by one half gives the third. Squaring the first two and subtracting then proves Θ34​=Θ24​+Θ44​.
These identities connect theta theory to modular forms and elliptic curves. The fourth powers are weight-two forms on the principal level-two congruence subgroup. The modular lambda function λ=Θ24​/Θ34​ obeys λ(−1/τ)=1−λ(τ) and λ(τ+1)=λ(τ)/(λ(τ)−1); the characteristic permutation kernel is Γ(2). It parametrizes an elliptic curve with its ordered two-torsion in Legendre form y2=x(x−1)(x−λ). The corresponding Klein j-invariant is 256(1−λ+λ2)3/[λ2(1−λ)2]. Finally the same products give
Δ(τ)=2−8(Θ2​Θ3​Θ4​)8.​
(41)
Indeed Θ2​Θ3​Θ4​=2Q1/4∏m​(1−Q2m)3, whose eighth power divided by 256 is q∏m​(1−qm)24. This recovers the normalized modular discriminant of Question 1 and displays the bridge between Gaussian theta sums and the lattice discriminant of Question 2.
