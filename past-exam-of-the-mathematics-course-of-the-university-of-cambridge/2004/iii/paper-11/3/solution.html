Expose the independent coordinates in order and let Mi​=E[f∣X1​,…,Xi​], M0​=Ef. This is the Doob exposure martingale. For fixed earlier coordinates, write hi​(x)=E[f∣Xi​=x,earlier coordinates]. The remaining coordinates have the same distribution for all x, and the coordinate-change hypothesis gives ∣hi​(x)−hi​(y)∣≤ci​. Thus the conditional range of Mi​−Mi−1​ has length at most ci​, and its conditional mean is zero.
Here is the Hoeffding lemma needed for the sharp constant. If a mean-zero random variable Y lies in an interval of length c, set ψ(s)=logEesY. Its second derivative is the variance under the exponentially tilted law. Under any such law, variance is at most c2/4, by comparison with squared distance from the interval midpoint. Since ψ(0)=ψ′(0)=0, integration gives ψ(s)≤s2c2/8 for all real s. Applying this conditionally and iterating the tower property yields
Ees(f−Ef)≤exp(8s2​∑i​ci2​).
(16)
The exponential Markov bound now gives
Pr(f−Ef≥t)≤exp(−st+8s2C​),C=∑i​ci2​.
(17)
For C>0 choose s=4t/C. Apply the same argument to −f and add the two tails, proving the McDiarmid inequality
Pr(∣f−Ef∣≥t)≤2e−2t2/C.​
(18)
For C=0, f is constant almost surely, and every positive-deviation probability is zero.
For the neighbourhood application, the ambient points must be elements of P[n], equivalently binary strings; the printed y∈[n] is a type error. An n-element ambient set could not generally satisfy the stated exponential-size lower bound. Use the uniform product probability space on the Boolean cube and set F(y)=d(y,A). Flipping one coordinate changes this Hamming distance by at most one, so C=n. Put μ=EF. Since Pr(F=0)=∣A∣/2n≥ε, the proved two-sided inequality gives, for μ>0,
ε≤Pr(∣F−μ∣≥μ)≤2e−2μ2/n.
(19)
Consequently μ≤(n/2)log(2/ε)​=t/2; the same bound is immediate if μ=0. Therefore
Pr(F>t)≤Pr(∣F−μ∣≥t−μ)≤2e−2(t−μ)2/n≤2e−t2/(2n)=ε.
(20)
This proves the Boolean cube expansion from bounded differences:
∣At​∣≥(1−ε)2n.​
(21)
The meaningful range is 0<ε≤1; real radii are interpreted by the inequality on the integer-valued Hamming distance. The converted TeX's final 22n is another corruption: the original PDF has 2n.
