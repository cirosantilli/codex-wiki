Write the poloidal magnetic flux function and toroidal amplitude explicitly in spherical components:
Br​=r2sinθχθ​​,Bθ​=−rsinθχr​​,Bϕ​=rsinθψ​.
(8)
Dotting the magnetostatic equilibrium equation with B gives B⋅∇p=0. Since all fields are axisymmetric, the poloidal magnetic field is tangent to the contours of χ in a meridional section. Thus, locally on regular connected flux surfaces, p=p(χ). This is a local flux-surface statement; disconnected surfaces with the same numerical value of χ need not have the same pressure without further global assumptions.
Taking the azimuthal component of the Lorentz force gives
[(∇×B)×B]ϕ​=r3sin2θψr​χθ​−ψθ​χr​​=0.
(9)
Its numerator is the meridional Jacobian determinant of ψ and χ. Vanishing therefore implies locally ψ=ψ(χ) on the same regular surfaces. Define
Δ∗​χ=χrr​+r2sinθ​∂θ​(sinθχθ​​).
(10)
A direct curl calculation now gives
∇×B=ψ′(χ)Bpol​−rsinθΔ∗​χ​eϕ​.
(11)
Since eϕ​×Bpol​=∇χ/(rsinθ), the remaining force is
(∇×B)×B=−r2sin2θΔ∗​χ+ψψ′​∇χ.
(12)
Consequently the spherical magnetostatic pressure balance equation following from the stated physical force balance is
Δ∗​χ+ψdχdψ​=−μ0​r2sin2θdχdp​.​
(13)
The PDF's displayed scalar equation has the wrong sign and omits μ0​ for its stated pressure p. This is not corrected by setting μ0​=1, which leaves the sign discrepancy. For example, χ=r4sin2θ, ψ=0, p=p0​−10χ/μ0​ satisfies the stated vector force balance and the corrected scalar equation, because Δ∗​χ=10r2sin2θ; it fails the printed scalar equation. One could obtain the printed form by replacing its pressure variable by −μ0​pphysical​, but that is not the variable in the given force balance.
Now set ψ=αχ, p=p0​+Pχ, and χ=F(r)sin2θ. The angular operator gives −2Fsin2θ/r2, so
F′′+(α2−r22​)F=−μ0​Pr2.
(14)
For the trigonometric form in the question, direct substitution yields
F=Acosαr+Brsinαr​+cr2⟹F′′+(α2−r22​)F=−r22(A+αB)​cosαr+α2cr2.
(15)
For α=0, this is a solution provided
B=−αA​,P=−μ0​α2c​.​
(16)
The same coefficient relation makes the trigonometric pair regular at the origin: cosαr−sinαr/(αr)=−α2r2/3+O(r4). Thus all components of the magnetic field are finite there. With p0​=0, this explicitly constructs the requested proportional-pressure family; adding p0​ does not change force balance. The coefficient c is the one called C in the final sentence of the PDF.
For a confined linear-pressure spherical magnetic equilibrium, the condition that the entire magnetic field vanish on r=a is precisely F(a)=F′(a)=0. In components these conditions read
Acosx+aB​sinx+ca2=0,−Aαsinx+B(aα​cosx−a21​sinx)+2ca=0,x=αa.
(17)
They eliminate both the radial and meridional components; the toroidal component vanishes too because ψ=αχ. For a nontrivial solution with A=0, substituting B=−A/α gives
c=−a2A​(cosx−xsinx​),(x2−3)sinx+3xcosx=0.​
(18)
The last equation selects the allowed nonzero values of αa. Equivalently, when division is legitimate, tanx=3x/(3−x2); the undivided equation also handles vanishing denominators correctly. Since χ(a,θ)=0, p(a,θ)=p0​​ is independent of latitude. In the strictly proportional convention p=Pχ, this is zero. The zero-field solution is a trivial possibility; the displayed conditions describe the nontrivial confined family.
