The truncated fields must be interpreted as a Galerkin method approximation: project each equation onto its retained spatial modes and discard orthogonal higher harmonics. They are not an exact pointwise solution of the full partial differential equations, because the nonlinear terms generate additional harmonics. Write X=kx, Z=πz, and abbreviate the printed normalizations by
S=k8p​​,T=p8​​,D=kπT​,E=k1​.
(19)
Then ψ=SasinXsinZ, θ=TbcosXsinZ−csin2Z/π, and χ=DdsinXcosZ+Eesin2X. All five amplitudes a,b,c,d,e depend on τ=pt. Use the spatial Jacobian determinant J(F,G)=Fx​Gz​−Fz​Gx​ and the inner product over the stated rectangle. Orthogonality of the trigonometric modes makes projection equivalent to retaining their coefficients. In particular, ∇2ψ=−pψ, so J(ψ,∇2ψ)=0.
For the temperature equation, direct products give
J(ψ,TbcosXsinZ)=2kπST​absin2Z=4πabsin2Z.
(20)
The other temperature contribution is −2kSaccosXsinZcos2Z. Since sinZcos2Z=(sin3Z−sinZ)/2, its retained component is kSaccosXsinZ. Thus the projected temperature equation is
pTb˙=−pTb+kSa−kSac,−πp​c˙+4πab=4πc.

Here the dot denotes d/dτ. Since kS/(pT)=1 and φ=4π2/p, these equations give b˙=−b+a(1−c) and c˙=φ(−c+ab).
Similarly, the two magnetic-advection products are
J(ψ,DdsinXcosZ)=−2kπSD​adsin2X=−k4π2​adsin2X,

while J(ψ,Eesin2X)=−2πSaesinXcosZcos2X has retained component πSaesinXcosZ. The projected induction equation is therefore
pDd˙=πSa−ζpDd−πSae,pEe˙=−4ζk2Ee+k4π2​ad.

Using πS/(pD)=1 and 4k2/p=4−φ gives d˙=−ζd+a(1−e) and e˙=−(4−φ)ζe+φad.
It remains to project the Lorentz force in the vorticity equation. The linear term is J(x,∇2χ)=pπDdsinXsinZ. The nonlinear cross term is
J(χ,∇2χ)=(p−4k2)J(DdsinXcosZ,Eesin2X).
(24)
The Jacobian on the right is 2πkDEdesinXsinZcos2X. Its retained component is −πkDEdesinXsinZ, so the total retained magnetic term is
pπDd[1+(3−φ)e]sinXsinZ.
(25)
Meanwhile the projected acceleration, viscous term and temperature forcing have coefficients −p2Sa˙, p2Sa, and −RkTb. Divide the projected vorticity equation by −p2S to obtain a˙=σ[−a+rb+ζqd{(φ−3)e−1}], using r=k2R/p3 and q=π2Q/p2. This derives the complete five-mode vertical-field magnetoconvection system:
a˙b˙d˙​=σ[−a+rb+ζqd{(φ−3)e−1}],=−b+a(1−c),=−ζd+a(1−e),​c˙e˙​=φ(−c+ab),=−(4−φ)ζe+φad.​
(26)
The Rayleigh number, Chandrasekhar number, Prandtl number and magnetic-to-thermal diffusivity ratio enter through r,q,σ and ζ respectively.
Linearization at the zero-amplitude state leaves c and e with negative rates −φ and −(4−φ)ζ, while a,b,d have coefficient matrix
L=​−σ11​σr−10​−σζq0−ζ​​.
(27)
A zero eigenvector satisfies b=a, d=a/ζ and [−1+r−q]a=0. Thus the stationary threshold is rc​=1+q​. The reversal symmetry (a,b,c,d,e)↦(−a,−b,c,−d,e) makes the nontrivial steady states occur as a pair.
For these nonzero steady states put A=a2. The temperature equations give c=ab and b=a(1−c), so b=a/(1+A) and c=A/(1+A). The induction equations give ζd=a(1−e) and (4−φ)ζe=φad. With μ=(4−φ)ζ2/φ, their solution is
b=1+a2a​,c=1+a2a2​,d=ζ(μ+a2)μa​,e=μ+a2a2​.​
(28)
Here k>0 gives 0<φ<4, and positive diffusivity gives μ>0. Substituting these amplitudes into the stationary a equation and dividing by a yields the stationary branch of five-mode magnetoconvection:
r=1+a2+(μ+a2)2qμ(1+a2)[μ+(4−φ)a2]​.​
(29)
At a=0 this meets r=1+q. Expansion in A=a2 gives
r=1+q+[1+q+μq(2−φ)​]A+O(A2).
(30)
The denominator of its initial slope is positive, and its numerator after multiplication by (4−φ)ζ2 is
N=(4−φ)ζ2(1+q)+φq(2−φ).
(31)
Therefore N>0 gives a supercritical pitchfork bifurcation direction, and N<0 gives a subcritical pitchfork bifurcation direction. If N=0, the leading slope vanishes and higher terms must be retained.
This classification describes which side of the stationary threshold contains the steady branch. It does not alone establish dynamical stability or rule out an earlier oscillatory instability. For example, at rc​ the characteristic polynomial factors as
λ[λ2+(σ+1+ζ)λ+ζ(1+σ)+σq(ζ−1)].
(32)
The zero eigenvalue is simple unless the final coefficient vanishes; the other two eigenvalues need not both be stable for every positive parameter choice. The stationary branch formula and its direction criterion remain the requested algebraic conclusions.
