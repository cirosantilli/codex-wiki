The asymmetric Lovász local lemma says the following. For finitely many bad events with a dependency graph of events, if 0≤xi​<1 satisfy
Pr(Ai​)≤xi​∏j∈Γ(i)​(1−xj​),
(9)
then
Pr(i⋂​Aic​)≥i∏​(1−xi​)>0.​
(10)
Here independence from nonneighbors is independence from their whole generated system, not just pairwise independence.
Prove by induction on ∣S∣ that Pr(Ai​∣⋂j∈S​Ajc​)≤xi​ whenever i∈/S, with all conditioning probabilities positive. At S=∅, the hypothesis gives Pr(Ai​)≤xi​. Split S into its neighbor part S1​ and nonneighbor part S2​. Then
Pr(Ai​∣j∈S⋂​Ajc​)​≤Pr(⋂j∈S1​​Ajc​∣⋂j∈S2​​Ajc​)Pr(Ai​∣⋂j∈S2​​Ajc​)​≤∏j∈S1​​(1−xj​)Pr(Ai​)​≤xi​.​
(11)
The numerator uses joint nonneighbor independence. The denominator is exposed one event at a time; every conditional bad-event probability involves fewer than ∣S∣ conditioning events, so induction supplies the product lower bound. The same product argument gives positivity of the conditioning events before using the fraction. Finally expose all complements in any order and multiply their conditional probabilities to obtain the claimed lower bound.
For maximum dependency degree d≥1 and event probabilities at most p, choose xi​=1/(d+1). Since (1+1/d)d≤e, the symmetric Lovász local lemma follows from
ep(d+1)≤1.​
(12)
For d=0, the events are jointly independent and avoidance has positive probability whenever p<1, so the displayed sufficient criterion also holds.
To obtain the local lemma Ramsey lower bound, colour the edges of KN​ independently with two equally likely colours. For every k-set S, the event that it is monochromatic has probability p=21−(2k​). Events with intersections of size at most one depend on disjoint edge trials. Counting potentially shared pairs gives
d≤(2k​)(k−2N−2​).
(13)
The Lovász local lemma excludes all monochromatic Kk​ if ep(d+1)≤1. For N≤ck2k/2, Stirling's approximation and (k−2N−2​)≤Nk−2/(k−2)! give, uniformly for c bounded above and away from zero,
ep(d+1)=O(k3/2(2​ec​)k)+o(1).

R(k)≥(2​/e+o(1))k2k/2.​

For the cycle, independently choose one of the twelve vertices in each part. Ignore cycle edges whose endpoints lie in the same part, since both endpoints cannot be selected. Every other edge defines a bad event of probability 1/144. It uses just the choices in its two endpoint parts. A part's twelve vertices meet at most twenty-four cycle edges, so a bad event has at most 48−1=47 neighbors in a dependency graph of events. Since e(47+1)/144=e/3<1, the Lovász local lemma gives a selection with no selected adjacent pair. Thus an independent transversal of size n exists, for every stated partition.

Expose the independent coordinates in order and let Mi​=E[f∣X1​,…,Xi​], M0​=Ef. This is the Doob exposure martingale. For fixed earlier coordinates, write hi​(x)=E[f∣Xi​=x,earlier coordinates]. The remaining coordinates have the same distribution for all x, and the coordinate-change hypothesis gives ∣hi​(x)−hi​(y)∣≤ci​. Thus the conditional range of Mi​−Mi−1​ has length at most ci​, and its conditional mean is zero.
Here is the Hoeffding lemma needed for the sharp constant. If a mean-zero random variable Y lies in an interval of length c, set ψ(s)=logEesY. Its second derivative is the variance under the exponentially tilted law. Under any such law, variance is at most c2/4, by comparison with squared distance from the interval midpoint. Since ψ(0)=ψ′(0)=0, integration gives ψ(s)≤s2c2/8 for all real s. Applying this conditionally and iterating the tower property yields
Ees(f−Ef)≤exp(8s2​∑i​ci2​).

The exponential Markov bound now gives
Pr(f−Ef≥t)≤exp(−st+8s2C​),C=∑i​ci2​.
(17)

Pr(∣f−Ef∣≥t)≤2e−2t2/C.​

For C=0, f is constant almost surely, and every positive-deviation probability is zero.

An n-element ambient set could not generally satisfy the stated exponential-size lower bound. Use the uniform product probability space on the Boolean cube and set F(y)=d(y,A). The assertion with the same ε in the degree interval and the final colour bound is false as printed. Fix r=2, ε=1/10, let G=K22m+1​, and take D=20m. Every degree is 22m=(1+ε)D, while every pair hypergraph codegree is one. For any proposed δ>0, choose m large enough that 1≤δD. Nevertheless a matching in a graph contains at most 11m edges, whereas there are (22m+1)11m edges in total. Thus every edge coloring requires at least 22m+1 colours, exceeding (1+ε)D=22m. The obstruction persists with arbitrarily small relative hypergraph codegree.
The meaningful asymptotic target separates the output tolerance from the input regularity error. For fixed rank r and target η>0, choose a sufficiently small δ=δ(r,η) and require degrees in [(1−δ)D,(1+δ)D] as well as pair hypergraph codegrees at most δD. The semi-random method then gives χ′(G)≤(1+η)D. Here is the requested proof essay for that corrected conclusion, including its random rounds, tracking requirements and completion step.
A useful way to run all colour classes together is the auxiliary matching construction for hypergraph edge colouring. Use K=⌈D⌉ initial colours. Create a task vertex for each original edge e, and a resource vertex (v,c) for each original vertex and colour. An assignment (e,c) is an (r+1)-edge consisting of its task and the resources (v,c) for all v∈e. A matching in a hypergraph in this auxiliary system is a proper partial colouring: a task cannot get two colours, and two edges sharing v cannot use the same resource (v,c).
The auxiliary task degrees are K, resource degrees are dG​(v), and its pair hypergraph codegrees are at most max(1,δD). It is therefore nearly regular with small hypergraph codegrees. For r≥2 and a nonempty original hypergraph, the hypergraph codegree condition also forces D≥1/δ; taking δ small absorbs rounding and ensures a large working degree. Rank one is immediate by assigning distinct colours to edges at each vertex.
The extra balance condition is important. Let Sv​ be the set of tasks corresponding to edges through original vertex v. A matching in a hypergraph missing a small fraction of all tasks need not leave few tasks in each Sv​. The Rödl nibble must leave at most βD unmatched tasks in every such star, where one may choose β=η/(4r). This local requirement, rather than just a global almost-perfect matching in a hypergraph, is what permits a cheap final edge colouring.
Run a Rödl nibble on the auxiliary system, whose rank is s=r+1. In a round of working degree Dj​, sample each surviving assignment independently with probability α/Dj​, for a small fixed α. Accept the sampled assignments that meet no other sampled assignment. Delete every auxiliary vertex touched by any sampled assignment, including conflicted ones, before proceeding. The accepted assignments are a matching in a hypergraph, and later assignments cannot reuse a consumed task or resource. Record each discarded but uncoloured task as waste; it must be counted in the final residual graph.
For an auxiliary vertex of degree (1±ξ)Dj​, the probability of remaining untouched is
(1−α/Dj​)dj​(v)=e−α(1+O(ξα+1/Dj​)).
(22)
For an auxiliary edge through a surviving vertex, the other s−1 vertices survive with probability approximately e−(s−1)α. Their incident trial families overlap in at most Os​(δD) trials by the hypergraph codegree bound, so the overlap error in this product calculation is small. This leads to the degree trajectory
Dj+1​≈Dj​e−(s−1)α,
(23)
while the surviving fraction of task vertices is approximately e−α per round. For a sampled assignment, a union bound over at most s(1+O(ξ))Dj​ conflicting assignments gives collision probability at most sα(1+O(ξ)). More explicitly, for a fixed task the expected number of ordered sampled conflict pairs having the first assignment through that task is at most
(1+O(ξ))Dj​⋅s(1+O(ξ))Dj​(Dj​α​)2=Os​(α2).
(24)
This bounds its waste probability. Thus productive deletion is first order in α, and collision loss is second order.
The technical tracking step makes these expected value calculations locally simultaneous and keeps their errors from accumulating. Its requirements are: residual auxiliary degrees stay near their trajectory after controlled pruning; each original star has the predicted number of surviving tasks; and every star's total collision and pruning waste is small. The standard probabilistic tools enter at distinct points.
Within a fixed round, task-survival indicators are independent: trial families for different tasks are disjoint, even when their assignments share resources. Conditional Chernoff bounds therefore control the number of surviving tasks in each star. Collision loss has short certificates consisting of pairs of selected intersecting assignments. The number selected through one resource has a binomial distribution of bounded mean. Chernoff bounds truncate these numbers at a suitable logarithmic cutoff; after truncation one trial has only a logarithmically bounded effect on the certified collision count. A certifiable function concentration bound, or a corresponding exposure argument, then controls loss in each star. These rare local overload events have polynomially bounded local dependency neighborhoods in the working degree, so the Lovász local lemma can select a simultaneous outcome independent of the total number of vertices.
Residual-degree counts require additional care because they are not sums of independent indicators. Two survival tests share at most a hypergraph codegree's worth of trials; expanding their joint probabilities bounds their covariance by the corresponding relative overlap. For a residual-degree count Z with Dj​ summands, overlapping summands and shared survival trials give a variance bound of the form
VarZ≤Cs​(Dj​+ζDj2​),ζ=Dj​maximum current pair codegree​.
(25)
Chebyshev's inequality then bounds Pr(∣Z−EZ∣>ξDj​) by Cs​(Dj−1​+ζ)/ξ2. This gives a small exceptional fraction when the relative hypergraph codegree is sufficiently small; it does not by itself make every degree good. The tracking argument records and prunes these exceptional configurations, bounding their contribution to each star by codegree-weighted exposure and the same local-loss bookkeeping. Markov's inequality controls exceptional mass, and repeated conditional concentration controls the retained counts. One must not apply an independent Chernoff bound directly to a residual degree or infer a uniform star bound just from a global expected loss. Keeping the pruning loss explicitly is the key distinction between a valid Rödl nibble argument and those shortcuts.
Choose the parameters in this order: first r,η and β; next α so small that sαlog(4/β)≪β; next
T≈α−1log(4/β);
(26)
and finally the initial regularity/hypergraph codegree tolerance δ small enough for all T tracking and pruning errors. This is a bounded number of rounds depending on the tolerances, not on the graph order. After T rounds, the surviving task fraction is at most approximately β/4, while accumulated collision loss is Os​(Tα2)=Os​(αlog(4/β)). Reserving the remaining margin for tracking and pruning leaves at most βD uncoloured edges at every original vertex. The working degree is still a fixed positive multiple of D, namely about De−rαT, so the large-degree estimates remain applicable throughout.
Finally use a fresh palette on the residual original hypergraph. Its maximum degree is at most βD; an edge meets at most r(βD−1) others. Greedy colouring therefore needs at most rβD+1 fresh colours. The total is at most
K+rβD+1≤D+2+ηD/4≤(1+η)D
(27)
for sufficiently large D, already ensured by choosing δ small. This completes the passage from the locally balanced Rödl nibble to the hypergraph chromatic index bound. The degree tolerance must be the small input parameter δ; replacing it by the entire output allowance η removes the slack and leads precisely to the complete-graph counterexample above.

Use discrete Shannon entropy with base-two logarithms. The standard facts needed are the chain rule for information entropy, conditioning reduces entropy, nonnegativity of conditional entropy, and the fact that a uniform distribution on M outcomes has Shannon entropy log2​M.
Order the coordinates by their indices and put hj​=H(Xj​∣X1​,…,Xj−1​)≥0. Applying the chain rule within a coordinate subset A and then adding the missing earlier conditioning coordinates gives
H(XA​)​=j∈A∑​H(Xj​∣XA∩[j−1]​)≥j∈A∑​H(Xj​∣X[j−1]​)=j∈A∑​hj​.​
(43)
On summing over the family, coordinate j occurs at least k times. Since each hj​ is nonnegative,
A∈A∑​H(XA​)≥kj=1∑n​hj​=kH(X).​
(44)
This proves Shearer's inequality without any independence assumption on the coordinates. For countable alphabets, apply the finite-valued assertion to refining finite coordinate partitions; the standard monotone approximation of Shannon entropy gives the same result with extended values. Differential entropy is not the Shannon entropy intended here.
For the graph application, let its bipartition be U,V, with degrees a on U and b on V, where a,b≥1. Choose an independent set uniformly from all i(G) such sets, and let X be its vertex-indicator vector. Then H(X)=log2​i(G). For v∈V put Yv​=XN(v)​ and qv​=Pr(Yv​=0).
Each vertex of U belongs to exactly a of these neighbourhoods. Shearer's inequality therefore gives H(XU​)≤a−1∑v∈V​H(Yv​). Conditional on XU​, a vertex of V is forced absent if any neighbor was selected, and otherwise is a free fair binary choice. There are no edges within V, and the conditional independent-set distribution is uniform on all these free choices. Hence
H(XV​∣XU​)=∑v∈V​qv​,
(45)
and
H(X)≤a1​∑v∈V​[H(Yv​)+aqv​].
(46)
There are 2b possible neighborhood patterns. Give the zero pattern weight w0​=2a and every other pattern weight one. For its distribution (py​), concavity of the logarithm, or Jensen's inequality, gives
H(Yv​)+aqv​​=py​>0∑​py​log2​py​wy​​≤log2​py​>0∑​wy​≤log2​(2a+2b−1).​
(47)
This establishes the local weighted-entropy estimate rather than just counting neighborhood patterns.
Edge counting gives a∣U∣=b∣V∣; together with ∣U∣+∣V∣=n, it yields ∣V∣/a=n/(a+b). Exponentiating the Shannon entropy estimate proves the independent-set bound for biregular graphs:
i(G)≤(2a+2b−1)n/(a+b).​
(48)
The complete bipartite graph Kb,a​ has exactly 2a+2b−1 independent sets, since one chooses a subset of one side and subtracts the double-counted empty set. Disjoint unions of such components attain equality. If exactly one degree parameter is zero, edge counting forces the graph to be edgeless and the displayed bound equals 2n, so it remains valid. If a=b=0, the denominator is undefined; the edgeless graph has exactly 2n independent sets.
