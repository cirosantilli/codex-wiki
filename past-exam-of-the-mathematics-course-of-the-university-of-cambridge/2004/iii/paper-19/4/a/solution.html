There is a normalization issue in the printed constant. Let Rictr​ denote the trace convention in the catalog's Ricci curvature, and write Ric=Rictr​/(n−1) for normalized Ricci curvature, with n≥2. The claimed π/k​ bound corresponds to Ric≥kg, or equivalently Rictr​≥(n−1)kg. We prove the estimate in both conventions.
Take a unit-speed minimizing geodesic of length L>0 and perpendicular parallel orthonormal fields E1​,…,En−1​. Set Vi​(t)=sin(πt/L)Ei​(t). The fields vanish at the endpoints. The allowed second variation of geodesic energy makes each Riemannian index form nonnegative. Summing them gives
0≤i∑​I(Vi​,Vi​)​=∫0L​((n−1)L2π2​cos2(πt/L)−Rictr​(γ˙​,γ˙​)sin2(πt/L))dt≤2n−1​(Lπ2​−kL)​
(11)
under the normalized lower bound. Thus L≤π/k​. Completeness and the Hopf-Rinow theorem supply a minimizing geodesic between any two points, so this bounds the diameter. A closed ball of that radius about any point contains the whole manifold and is compact, again by the Hopf-Rinow theorem.
Hence the intended Bonnet-Myers theorem conclusion is
Ric≥kg ⟹ M compact,diamM≤k​π​.​
(12)
If the printed Ric≥k means trace Ricci curvature instead, the same integral has lower curvature term kL/2 and yields
Rictr​≥kg ⟹ diamM≤πkn−1​​.​
(13)
The smaller bound is false under that convention when n>2: the round unit Sn has trace Ricci tensor (n−1)g and diameter π. Taking k=n−1 contradicts the printed π/k​ inequality. The compactness assertion remains true.
