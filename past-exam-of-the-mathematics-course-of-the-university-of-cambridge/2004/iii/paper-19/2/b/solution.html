 ... Past exam of the mathematics course of the University of Cambridge 2004 iii Paper 19 2 b
OurBigBook.com Words: 304
A local isometry preserves the Levi-Civita connection and hence geodesics, as well as the lengths of lifted curves. We first show that a complete source permits finite-length paths to lift all the way to their endpoints. Begin lifting a piecewise smooth path c:[0,1]→N from any chosen point above c(0), using local inverse maps. If its lift is defined only up to a maximal b≤1, then
dM​(c(s),c(t))≤length(c∣[s,t]​)⟶0as s,t↑b.
(7)
Completeness gives a limit point in M. A local inverse at that point continues the lift, including its endpoint. Thus such a finite obstruction is impossible. Connectedness of N and piecewise smooth paths from f(p) to any target point prove that f is onto; no prior completeness of N is used in this step.
Now lift the initial position and velocity of any geodesic in N. The resulting geodesic in M exists for all real time by Hopf-Rinow theorem. Its image extends the original target geodesic by uniqueness of the geodesic initial-value problem. Hence N is geodesically complete, and therefore complete.
The converse is false for a general local isometry, even an onto one. Give the interval (0,4π) its ordinary metric and map it to the unit circle by t↦eit. This is an onto local isometry to a complete circle, but the sequence tj​=1/j is Cauchy in the interval and has no limit there. It is not a covering map: the identity point of the circle has only one preimage, whereas nearby points have two.
The converse holds for a Riemannian covering. If f is also a covering map and N is complete, every target geodesic defined on R lifts globally with any specified initial point. A local isometry makes that lift a geodesic. Its initial velocity can be any prescribed source velocity because df is an isomorphism. Thus all source geodesics extend for all time, and M is complete by the Hopf-Rinow theorem.
