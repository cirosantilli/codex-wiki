Past exam of the mathematics course of the University of Cambridge / 2004 / iii / Paper 19 / 3 / a / Solution

 ... Past exam of the mathematics course of the University of Cambridge 2004 iii Paper 19 3 a
OurBigBook.com Words: 195
A connected complete Riemannian manifold is disconnected at infinity when the complement of some compact set has at least two unbounded connected components. Equivalently it has at least two ends. A line in a Riemannian manifold is a unit-speed geodesic ℓ:R→M satisfying d(ℓ(s),ℓ(t))=∣s−t∣ for every s,t.
Let K be the compact separating set, enlarged inside a fixed compact metric ball if needed for bounds. Choose xj​,yj​ in two different unbounded components of M∖K, with both distances from K tending to infinity. By the Hopf-Rinow theorem, join them by unit-speed minimizing geodesics. Every such segment meets K, since a path avoiding it cannot join its different components. Reparametrize a segment so that one crossing point is at time zero. Its parameter interval is [−aj​,bj​], where aj​,bj​→∞ by the distances from the endpoints to K.
The unit tangent bundle over K is compact, so a subsequence of the initial position/velocity pairs converges. Continuous dependence of the geodesic equation on its initial data, and completeness, give a limiting geodesic defined on all of R. For any fixed s<t, the approximating segments are defined on [s,t] for large j and satisfy d(γj​(s),γj​(t))=t−s. Continuity of the distance passes this equality to the limit. Thus the limiting geodesic is a line, proving a line from disconnection at infinity.
