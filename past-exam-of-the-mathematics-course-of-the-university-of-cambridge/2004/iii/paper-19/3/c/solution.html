Past exam of the mathematics course of the University of Cambridge / 2004 / iii / Paper 19 / 3 / c / Solution

 ... Past exam of the mathematics course of the University of Cambridge 2004 iii Paper 19 3 c
OurBigBook.com Words: 182
Suppose, for a contradiction, that X=M×R has a complete metric with zero Ricci curvature. Since M is closed and connected, the complement of M×[−A,A] has two unbounded components in any complete metric on X: their closures are noncompact, while closed bounded sets are compact by the Hopf-Rinow theorem. Thus X is disconnected at infinity, and part (a) supplies a line. The Cheeger-Gromoll splitting theorem gives an isometry
X≅N3×R.
(10)
The Riemannian product curvature formula makes N complete and Ricci-flat. A three-dimensional Ricci-flat Riemannian manifold is flat: for any orthonormal triple, the three trace equations are Ric11​=K12​+K13​ and their cyclic companions. Solving gives Kij​=Ricii​+Ricjj​−Scal/2=0. Every plane can occur in such a triple, so every sectional curvature vanishes, and hence so does the Riemann curvature tensor.
The complete simply connected universal cover of N is therefore Euclidean R3, and that of X is Euclidean R4. But the topological universal cover of X is M×R. Its deformation retraction onto M×{0} shows that contractibility of this cover would imply contractibility of M, contrary to the hypothesis. Therefore no complete Ricci-flat metric exists on M×R. Notice that no product assumption was made about the hypothetical original metric.
