Let q=∣B∣ and choose h uniformly from the finite family H. All bounds below must hold for every pair of distinct inputs x,y∈A.
A universal hash family, also called universal2, satisfies P[h(x)=h(y)]≤1/q. A strongly universal hash family satisfies
P[h(x)=u, h(y)=v]=q−2for every u,v∈B.
(13)
Thus the two outputs are independent and uniform; summing over u=v gives the collision condition. An almost strongly universal hash family permits a larger conditional guessing bound. In the ε-almost-strongly-universal convention used here,
P[h(x)=u]=q−1,P[h(x)=u, h(y)=v]≤ε/q,
(14)
equivalently P[h(y)=v∣h(x)=u]≤ε. Necessarily ε≥1/q, with ε=1/q giving strong universality. Stating this normalization matters because the name alone does not specify the allowed forgery probability.
Here is an explicit polynomial almost strongly universal hashing construction and a one-use message authentication protocol. Use a publicly specified finite field Fq​, where q=2t. Encode a fixed-length n-bit message as L=⌈n/t⌉ field elements m1​,…,mL​, padding the final block in a fixed way. Both parties know the length, so this encoding is injective. They share independent uniform secret field elements a,b, requiring 2t shared secret bits. Define
ha,b​(m)=b+∑j=1L​mj​aj.
(15)
For every message, the output is uniform because of b. For distinct messages m,m′ and prescribed tags u,v, the condition h(m)=u determines exactly one value of b for each a. The remaining condition is
∑j=1L​(mj′​−mj​)aj=v−u.
(16)
Its left-hand side has a nonzero coefficient of positive degree, so subtracting the specified constant gives a nonzero polynomial of degree at most L. By the root bound for a polynomial, there are at most L possible field elements a. Consequently
P[h(m)=u,h(m′)=v]≤L/q2,
(17)
and this is an ε-almost-strongly-universal family with ε=L/q, provided L<q. The powers start at one deliberately: a message coefficient at power zero could give a known constant tag shift, allowing a deterministic substitution.
Alice sends the message and its tag u=ha,b​(m) over the public channel. Bob accepts only if his independently computed tag agrees. An attacker who has seen no tag guesses one with probability 1/q. After seeing a valid pair (m,u), an attacker substituting a different message m′ and any chosen tag v succeeds with conditional probability at most L/q. This remains true when the choice of m′,v depends on the observed pair: the bound is uniform over all those choices. More explicitly, the observed tag leaves a uniform because each possible a has exactly one compatible secret mask b.
For a desired forgery bound η, choose t=⌈log2​(n/η)⌉. Since L≤n and q≥n/η, the substitution bound is at most η. The secret requirement is
2t=O(logn+log(1/η)) bits,P(substitution accepted)≤L/2t≤η.​
(18)
For a long message this is much shorter than its n bits. This is Wegman–Carter authentication: a keyed hash plus a fresh secret one-time pad on the tag. It authenticates the message rather than concealing its contents. Use the key once for the guarantee just established; multiple authenticated messages require fresh masks and a reuse security analysis. Authenticate lengths and sequence identifiers too if variable lengths or replay protection are required.
