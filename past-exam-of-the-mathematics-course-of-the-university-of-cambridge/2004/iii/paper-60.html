For a vector x, its isotropy group is the stabilizer subgroup
Gx​={g∈G:RG​(g)x=x}.​
(1)
For a subgroup H≤G, its fixed-point subspace of a group action is
Fix(H)={x∈Rn:RG​(h)x=x for every h∈H}.​
(2)
It is a linear subspace, the intersection of the kernels of RG​(h)−I. The subgroup fixes a particular vector pointwise; the fixed-point subspace of a group action consists of all vectors it fixes, and need not be one-dimensional.

Suppose f is sufficiently smooth and equivariant, f(0,μ)=0, and the group representation on the critical space is absolutely irreducible. Its linearization is then Dx​f(0,μ)=a(μ)I. Assume a(0)=0 and a′(0)=0. The equivariant branching lemma states that each isotropy subgroup H with dimFix(H)=1 has a local nontrivial branch of equilibria with that isotropy, apart from the common origin. Conjugate isotropy subgroups give symmetry-related branches, so branch types are counted up to conjugacy.
To see the scalar mechanism, write the fixed-point subspace of a group action as Re and restrict f(se,μ)=eh(s,μ). Equivariance makes the fixed-point subspace of a group action invariant. Since h(0,μ)=0, write h=sk(s,μ); k(0,0)=0 and kμ​(0,0)=a′(0)=0. The implicit function theorem solves k(s,μ(s))=0 near zero. For small nonzero s, the stabilizer subgroup is exactly that of e. A normalizer element reversing e makes h odd and μ(s) even; the generic branch is then a pitchfork bifurcation. The lemma ensures existence, not dynamical stability, and does not exclude further branches of smaller isotropy.

Conjugating the coordinate reflection by the rotations supplies reflections in the other two coordinates. The quarter-turns, together with those reflections, also supply coordinate transpositions. Consequently the represented group consists of all signed permutation matrices, the full symmetry group of a cube of order 23⋅3!=48.
Let C be any real or complex matrix commuting with the group representation. Commutation with the three independent coordinate reflections forces all off-diagonal entries of C to vanish. Commutation with the quarter-turn about the first axis equates its second and third diagonal entries, and the quarter-turn about the second axis equates its first and third entries. Therefore
EndΓ​(C3)=CI.​
(3)
For completeness, if the complexified group representation had a proper invariant subspace, its orthogonal complement would be invariant too, since the generating matrices are unitary. The orthogonal projection onto that subspace would be a nonscalar commuting matrix, contradicting the calculation. Thus the complexification is irreducible, proving absolute irreducibility of the real group representation.

Choose three representative vectors, with nonzero coordinate scale suppressed. Their stabilizer subgroups and fixed-point subspaces of a group action are
vector(1,0,0)(1,1,0)(1,1,1)​stabilizer⟨rx​,κy​,κz​⟩≅D4​⟨κz​,κx​rz​⟩≅C2​×C2​⟨κx​rz​,κy​rx​⟩≅S3​​order846​fixed spaceR(1,0,0)R(1,1,0)R(1,1,1).​​
(4)
Here κx​rz​ exchanges the first two coordinates and κy​rx​ exchanges the last two. On the first line the stabilizer subgroup is every signed permutation of the two zero coordinates; fixing it forces those coordinates to vanish. On the second line it exchanges the equal coordinates and independently reflects the zero coordinate, forcing x=y,z=0. On the third line all coordinate permutations fix the vector, forcing x=y=z.
The three subgroups have different orders and hence cannot be conjugate. Their group orbits contain respectively six face-axis, twelve edge-axis and eight vertex-axis directions. Each fixed-point subspace of a group action is one-dimensional, so the equivariant branching lemma guarantees three distinct symmetry types of bifurcating equilibria. The axial isotropy types of the full cube symmetry group are precisely these: a vector with several distinct nonzero absolute coordinate values has a separate freely variable coordinate block for each such value in the fixed-point subspace of a group action of its stabilizer subgroup, giving dimension greater than one.

Coordinate reflection in xi​ requires the ith component to be odd in xi​, and reflections in the other coordinates require it to be even in those coordinates. Thus no constant or quadratic terms are permitted. The only cubic monomials in component i are xi3​ and xi​xj2​ for j=i. Coordinate permutations equate their coefficients in all components. After using the transverse linear eigenvalue as parameter, the normal form is
x˙y˙​z˙​=μx+ax3+bx(y2+z2)+⋯,=μy+ay3+by(z2+x2)+⋯,=μz+az3+bz(x2+y2)+⋯.​​
(5)
The remainder starts at fifth spatial order, together with parameter-dependent corrections such as μ∣x∣3. This is the cubic equivariants of the full cube symmetry group form. On a branch with m=1,2,3 equal nonzero absolute coordinates s, respectively,
μ+[a+(m−1)b]s2=0.
(6)
Hence the three leading branch amplitudes are s2=−μ/a, −μ/(a+b) and −μ/(a+2b). Their generic nonzero denominators determine on which side the branches occur; existence alone does not make them stable.

Set g(x)=x2+x3/3. Equilibria satisfy μ2=g(x), and their scalar linear eigenvalue is
Fx​=−x(x+2).
(7)
It vanishes only at x=0 or x=−2. Substitution in the equilibrium relation gives the three nonhyperbolic parameter-state points
(μ,x)=(0,0),(2/3​,−2),(−2/3​,−2).​
(8)
The branches with x<−2 and x>0 are stable, while −2<x<0 is unstable. For 0<∣μ∣<2/3​ there are three equilibria; beyond the two outer saddle-node bifurcations there is just the positive stable one. At μ=0 there is also the distinct stable equilibrium x=−3.
Near the crossing, use the smooth coordinate y=x1+x/3​, whose derivative is positive near zero. The equation becomes y˙​=p(y)(μ2−y2) with p>0. A positive time rescaling removes p, and the parameter-dependent state shift u=y−μ gives
dτdu​=−2μu−u2.
(9)
Thus the crossing at μ=0 is a transcritical bifurcation, with parameter −2μ. The two smooth branches y=±μ exchange dynamical stability; labeling the stable branch by ∣μ∣ instead would obscure that exchange. The parameter-dependent coordinate shift in a transcritical bifurcation explains why the original coordinate need not display a branch identically equal to zero.

For a nonzero equilibrium, use λ=v∗2​+2εv∗​ to rewrite the determinant as −2v∗​(v∗​+ε). These formulas determine the local bifurcations of the imperfect soft Duffing-van der Pol oscillator directly.
When ε=0, the origin is a saddle equilibrium for λ<0 and has determinant λ for λ>0; the two equilibria ±λ​ are saddle equilibria. They meet the origin in a symmetry-protected pitchfork bifurcation at λ=0 for κ=0. At κ=0,λ>0 the origin undergoes a supercritical Hopf bifurcation. To determine criticality, take v=rcos(λ​t)+⋯: averaging H˙=(κ−v2)w2 gives r˙=κr/2−r3/8+⋯, so the stable small limit cycle has r2=4κ+⋯.

At zero imperfection the stable limit cycle grows to a heteroclinic cycle connecting the two outer saddle equilibria. Near the double-zero point its leading connection curve can be calculated, rather than inferred just from symmetry. In the Hamiltonian system limit the heteroclinic orbit is v=λ​tanh(λ/2​t), and
∫−∞∞​w2dt=32​4λ3/2​,∫w2dt∫v2w2dt​=5λ​.

The weak-damping persistence condition is therefore κ=λ/5+o(λ), not an exact formula at order-one parameters.
Now take ε>0; negative imperfection reflects the phase portrait. The pitchfork bifurcation unfolds into a transcritical bifurcation crossing at λ=0 and a saddle-node bifurcation at λ=−ε2. The latter occurs at v=−ε. Between those values the origin and v−​ are saddle equilibria, while v+​ lies in (−ε,0) and is a sink or source according to the trace. At λ=0, v+​=λ/(2ε)+⋯ exchanges its saddle equilibrium/non-saddle character with the origin. The double-zero points are now at (λ,κ)=(0,0) and (−ε2,ε2).
The original Hopf bifurcation line κ=0,λ>0 remains, still supercritical. A second Hopf bifurcation curve occurs on the intermediate nonzero equilibrium:
λ=q2+2εq,κ=q2,−ε<q<0.​
(14)
It is important not to assign the same criticality to its entire length. Write s=ε2+λ​, so q=−ε+s. Around that center the restoring quadratic coefficient is g2​=−ε+3s, the damping linear coefficient is f1​=2(ε−s), and ω2=2s(ε−s). The Hopf criticality for an asymmetric Lienard center calculation gives cubic amplitude coefficient
81​(−1+ω2f1​g2​​)=81​(2−ε/s).
(15)
Hence it is supercritical for s<ε/2 and subcritical for s>ε/2, with a generalized Hopf bifurcation point at λ=−3ε2/4,κ=ε2/4. Higher-order terms are needed exactly there. For small imperfection the leading quintic coefficient is positive: writing q1​=v−q and using the coordinate that normalizes the potential energy, y=q1​1−aq1​−bq12​​, with a=2g2​/(3ω2) and b=1/(2ω2), the coefficient of y4 in (f1​q1​−q12​)dq1​/dy at vanishing cubic coefficient is 10b/3>0. Weak-damping averaging therefore supplies the nearby saddle-node bifurcation of stable and unstable limit cycles in the generalized Hopf bifurcation unfolding. None of these small O(ε2) features intersects either requested parameter line.
Globally, the two saddle equilibrium barriers are no longer equal. Their leading energy difference is H(v+​,0)−H(v−​,0)=−4ελ3/2/3+⋯. The two one-way connections consequently separate; matching their energy differences with the connection integral gives
κ=λ/5−2​ε+⋯(v−​→v+​),κ=λ/5+2​ε+⋯(v+​→v−​).
(16)
These weak-damping heteroclinic splitting of a tilted quartic oscillator formulas apply for small λ with ∣ε∣≪λ​. Between the separated connection curves, the central stable limit cycle terminates in a homoclinic orbit to the lower-barrier saddle equilibrium, v+​ for positive imperfection. The simultaneous symmetric heteroclinic-cycle destruction is therefore replaced by two basin-changing one-way connections and a distinct homoclinic orbit loss of the limit cycle. Its period diverges at that loss.
Along κ+λ=1, decrease λ from above one. The sequence is supercritical Hopf bifurcation at λ=1; first heteroclinic orbit; homoclinic orbit loss of the stable limit cycle; second heteroclinic orbit; transcritical bifurcation crossing at zero; saddle-node bifurcation at −ε2. The connection locations are global, not obtained by treating the Melnikov approximation as exact. For illustration, direct stable/unstable manifold matching at ε=0.04 places them at approximately λ=0.882,0.858,0.783, respectively. The diagram below gives the qualitative ordering without prescribing those example values for arbitrary imperfection.
Along κ+λ=−1, the sequence is simply transcritical bifurcation crossing at zero, followed by saddle-node bifurcation at −ε2. Before the latter, κ<0 in the relevant region, so H decreases strictly on nonstationary trajectories and there is no periodic orbit or saddle equilibrium loop. After it only a saddle equilibrium remains, which cannot be enclosed by a periodic orbit by the Poincare-index obstruction to a periodic orbit. The origin does not undergo a Hopf bifurcation at λ=−1, since its determinant there is negative.

ψ=a(t)sinαxsinπz,θ=b(t)cosαxsinπz+c(t)sin2πz+⋯.

The quadratic temperature distortion c is retained for the saturation calculation; it does not enter the linear onset. Substitution of the primary modes gives
dtd​(ab​)=(−σ(s+Qπ2/s)α​σRα/s−s​)(ab​).
(18)
The determinant vanishes when
Rc​(Q,α)=α2s(s2+Qπ2)​.​
(19)
The trace is −[s+σ(s+Qπ2/s)]<0. Thus no conjugate eigenvalues can cross the imaginary axis away from zero, and the initial instability is stationary, not oscillatory. Indeed for R≥0 the discriminant is [σ(s+Qπ2/s)−s]2+4σRα2/s>0. This is quasistatic vertical-field magnetoconvection: there is no independent magnetic evolution mode that could produce a magnetic-thermal Hopf bifurcation instability.
For a fixed box the other allowed horizontal wavenumbers are nπ/L, and the actual first threshold is the minimum of their corresponding Rc​ values. Vertical index m replaces π2 by m2π2 and strictly increases the threshold at fixed horizontal wavenumber, so m=1 is selected. Let a scalar fixed point have magnitude r>0. If z=−r<0, then x and y have the same sign; the two lifts are the fixed points (r,1) and (−r,−1). If z=r>0, their signs are opposite, and the lift is the period-two orbit (r,−1)↔(−r,1). The scalar fixed-point multiplier is
m=Aδrδ−1>0.
(30)
The two-return fixed-point multiplier of the period-two lift is m2, so in either case dynamical stability requires m<1. The negative fixed point represents one of two mirror-related single-lobe periodic orbits in the flow, whereas the positive fixed point represents a double-lobe periodic orbit.
For δ>1, the nearby fixed point is z=μ+o(μ) and its fixed-point multiplier tends to zero. Thus two stable single-lobe limit cycles for μ<0 glue into one stable double-lobe limit cycle for μ>0. Their periods diverge as the separatrix is approached; μ=0,z=0 is not a finite-period limit cycle.
For 0<δ<1, the small branch instead satisfies z∼−sgn(μ)(∣μ∣/A)1/δ and its fixed-point multiplier tends to infinity. Hence one unstable double-lobe limit cycle on μ<0 splits into two unstable single-lobe limit cycles on μ>0. Additional finite-size stable branches depend on the surrounding map; near the resonance δ=1 with A<1 they are resolved by the saddle-node bifurcations in part (c).
