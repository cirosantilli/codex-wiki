Over a finite field Fq​, a linear code of length N and dimension k is a k-dimensional subspace C⊆FqN​. A full-row-rank generator matrix G∈Fqk×N​ gives C={mG:m∈Fqk​}, using row-vector messages. A full-row-rank parity-check matrix H∈Fq(N−k)×N​ gives C={x:HxT=0}. The dual code is C⊥={y:x⋅y=0 for all x∈C}, of dimension N−k. Thus the rows of H span C⊥, and
HGT=0,GC⊥​=H,HC⊥​=G.​
(1)
Conversely the rank conditions and HGT=0 imply that the row space of G equals the kernel of H, by inclusion and equality of dimensions.
For a CSS code, specialize to binary linear codes C2​⊆C1​⊆F2N​, of dimensions k2​,k1​. Define orthonormal encoded states
∣x+C2​⟩=∣C2​∣​1​∑y∈C2​​∣x+y⟩,x∈C1​/C2​.
(2)
Different cosets have disjoint computational-basis support. Their span has dimension 2k1​−k2​, so it encodes K=k1​−k2​ logical qubits. For binary vectors write X(u)=⨂j​Xjuj​​ and Z(v)=⨂j​Zjvj​​. The stabilizer generators are X(u) for a basis of C2​, and Z(v) for a basis of C1⊥​. They fix each encoded state: the former permute its coset terms and the latter contribute phases (−1)v⋅(x+y)=1. Their commutation relation is X(u)Z(v)=(−1)u⋅vZ(v)X(u), so the classical dual inclusion C1⊥​⊆C2⊥​ makes them commute. There are k2​+N−k1​=N−K independent checks, giving precisely this encoded space.
For a Pauli error X(e)Z(f), the Z checks give bit-error syndrome H1​eT, where H1​ checks C1​, and the X checks give phase-error syndrome G2​fT, where G2​ generates C2​ and hence checks C2⊥​. Classical decoders therefore correct up to tX​ bit flips and tZ​ phase flips if d(C1​)>2tX​ and d(C2⊥​)>2tZ​. A Y error has both components; it is not omitted from this correction scheme.
For the precise quantum code distance, harmless stabilizer errors must be excluded. A pure-X error preserves the code exactly when e∈C1​, and is a stabilizer when e∈C2​; a pure-Z error preserves it when f∈C2⊥​, and is a stabilizer when f∈C1⊥​. Consequently, for K>0,
dX​=mine∈C1​∖C2​​wt(e),dZ​=minf∈C2⊥​∖C1⊥​​wt(f),d=min(dX​,dZ​).​
(3)
A mixed Pauli error has weight equal to the size of the union of its X and Z supports. If it is a nontrivial logical operator, at least one component is nontrivial, so its weight is at least min(dX​,dZ​); pure-X or pure-Z operators attain that minimum. Thus t=⌊(d−1)/2⌋ arbitrary-qubit errors are correctable: products of two such Pauli errors have weight below d and act scalarly on the code, or take it to an orthogonal syndrome sector. This is the Knill--Laflamme condition. Expanding arbitrary errors in the Pauli basis extends correction to their coherent linear span.
An explicit example is the Steane code. Take C1​ to be the binary Hamming code with
H=​100​010​110​001​101​011​111​​,C2​=C1⊥​=row(H).
(4)
The distinct nonzero columns show that C1​ has no weight-one or weight-two word, while three dependent columns give a weight-three word. Hence its classical distance is three. Direct multiplication gives HHT=0, proving C2​⊆C1​. Every nonzero word of C2​ has weight four, so the logical X and Z distances are both three. Its six stabilizer checks are the X and Z strings given by the three rows of H. A logical basis is the uniform superposition over C2​ and over 1+C2​, since 1=(1,…,1)∈C1​∖C2​. The resulting [[7,1,3]] CSS code corrects every single-qubit error.
