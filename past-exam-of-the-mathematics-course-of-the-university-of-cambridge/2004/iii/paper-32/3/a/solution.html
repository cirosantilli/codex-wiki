The generalized measurement postulate specifies operators Mi​ with ∑i​Mi†​Mi​=I. On a density matrix ρ, the outcome probability and conditional state are
pi​=Tr(Mi​ρMi†​),ρi​=pi​Mi​ρMi†​​(pi​>0).
(10)
The probabilities are nonnegative and sum to one. More generally an outcome may contain several Kraus operators Miα​, in which case one sums over α in both formulas. A projective measurement with the usual Lüders update is the special case Mi​=Pi​, where Pi​=Pi†​, Pi​Pj​=δij​Pi​ and ∑i​Pi​=I. Projection-valued effects alone fix the projective outcome probabilities, but an additional outcome-dependent unitary can change the conditional state; for the standard projective update the measurement operators themselves are the projectors.
A POVM is a family of positive effects Ei​ summing to I. Its probabilities are pi​=Tr(ρEi​). A generalized measurement gives effects Ei​=Mi†​Mi​, or Ei​=∑α​Miα†​Miα​. Conversely Mi​=Ei​​ realizes any POVM. The POVM does not determine the post-measurement state: different operators with these same effects have the same probabilities and can have different state updates.
A pure POVM has rank-one nonzero effects, Ei​=wi​∣ψi​⟩⟨ψi​∣, with normalized pure states ∣ψi​⟩ and positive wi​. Here pure means rank-one effects, not the different property of extremality in the convex set of POVMs.
The POVM–ensemble duality for the maximally mixed state is a bijection between POVMs in dimension d and ensemble decompositions of I/d. Given effects Ei​, set
pi​=dTrEi​​,σi​=TrEi​Ei​​,
(11)
omitting zero effects. Each σi​ is a density matrix, ∑i​pi​=TrI/d=1, and
∑i​pi​σi​=d1​∑i​Ei​=dI​.
(12)
Conversely, if an ensemble satisfies ∑i​pi​σi​=I/d, then Ei​=dpi​σi​ are positive and sum to I, hence form a POVM. Taking traces recovers the original probabilities, so the transformations are inverse. Pure POVMs correspond exactly to pure-state ensemble decompositions of the maximally mixed state. For such a POVM, pi​=wi​/d and ∑i​wi​=d; it need not be an orthogonal measurement.
