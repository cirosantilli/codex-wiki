On the two-sided range [−π,π] there is also a peak at −π/3; the ratio there is one for every k. This is a counterexample to the claim if read literally on a signed frequency domain. The correct two-sided exclusion is ω=±π/3.
The result illustrates how repeated symmetric filtering can amplify an oscillation. Although H(0)=1 preserves a constant component, H(π/3)=7/6>1 amplifies period-six oscillations. Repetition selects an increasingly narrow frequency band around those peaks; it does not simply smooth all noise away. In fact the unnormalized output variance eventually grows without bound, since a fixed interval around either peak has ∣H∣>1.
