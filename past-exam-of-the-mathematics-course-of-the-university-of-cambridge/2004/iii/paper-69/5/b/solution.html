The stability convention matters. The Cambridge numerical analysis notes define a strict linear stability domain through decay yn​→0. For a multistep recurrence, robust decay requires every amplification root to lie strictly inside the unit disk. With that convention we can prove the stronger result that this method's strict linear stability domain is empty, and therefore bounded. Mere boundedness of oscillatory sequences gives a different answer, described below.
On the Dahlquist test equation, g(y)=λ2y. With z=hλ, the amplification polynomial of a multistep method is
Dξ2−16zξ−A=0,D=z2−7z+15,A=z2+7z+15.
(23)
Exclude D=0, where the intended two-step update cannot be solved. If both polynomial roots have modulus below one, their product requires ∣A∣<∣D∣. But
∣A∣2−∣D∣2=28Rez(∣z∣2+15),
(24)
so necessarily Rez<0. The complex quadratic Schur criterion also requires
∣D(−16z)−(−A)(−16z)∣<∣D∣2−∣A∣2.
(25)
Its left-hand side equals 32∣Rez∣(∣z∣2+15), while for Rez<0 its right-hand side is only 28∣Rez∣(∣z∣2+15). The inequality is impossible. Thus
Ddecay​=∅​.
(26)
For comparison, the bounded-root stability set of the symmetric two-derivative formula is unbounded. On z=iy, rotate the amplification polynomial into a real reciprocal quadratic. Its two roots are distinct and on the unit circle precisely when
(15−y2)2−15y2>0,
(27)
equivalently y2<(45−155​)/2 or y2>(45+155​)/2. In particular, arbitrarily large imaginary parameters give bounded, undamped numerical oscillations. At the two equality thresholds, repeated unit-circle roots produce unbounded solutions. Hence the question's bounded-domain claim holds for the strict decay convention; it does not hold if the phrase is interpreted using the non-growing root condition for a multistep method.
