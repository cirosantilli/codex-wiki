Write the constant limit as c. First, convergence in distribution to a constant implies convergence in probability: the Portmanteau theorem applied to the closed set {y:∣y−c∣≥ε} gives
limsupn→∞​P(∣Yn​−c∣≥ε)≤0.
(51)
Now compare characteristic functions without imposing independence. For fixed t∈R,
​Eeit(Xn​+Yn​)−eitcEeitXn​​​≤E∣eitYn​−eitc∣≤∣t∣ε+2P(∣Yn​−c∣>ε).​
(52)
The second bound uses ∣eiu−eiv∣≤min{2,∣u−v∣}. By convergence in probability, the limiting upper bound is ∣t∣ε; then let ε↓0. Meanwhile Xn​ has weak convergence of random variables to X, so EeitXn​→EeitX. Thus the characteristic function of the sum tends to eitcEeitX, the characteristic function of X+c, which is continuous at zero. The Lévy continuity theorem proves
Xn​+Yn​ ⟹ X+c.​
(53)
This is the additive case of the Slutsky theorem, with its proof supplied. The constant limiting marginal removes the dependence obstruction in part (b).
