Past exam of the mathematics course of the University of Cambridge / 2004 / iii / Paper 28 / 3 / c / Solution

Work from time one; a finite initial prefix does not affect convergence. Fix a positive integer a, and use the stopping time τa​ and budget An​ from part (b). Define
Zn(a)​=Xn∧τa​​+a−Un∧τa​​.
(23)
It is nonnegative. For each finite n, the stopped variable Xn∧τa​​ is integrable: it is bounded by the finite sum X1​+⋯+Xn​, and the budget is bounded by a.
The stopped increment of X is
X(n+1)∧τa​​−Xn∧τa​​=1{τa​>n}​(Xn+1​−Xn​).
(24)
Taking conditional expectations, the assumed drift bound gives
E[X(n+1)∧τa​​∣Fn​]≤Xn∧τa​​+Yn​1{τa​>n}​.
(25)
The last term is bounded by a; no integrability assumption on the unlocalized Yn​ has been used. Its contribution is exactly cancelled by the budget decrement from part (b), giving
E[Zn+1(a)​∣Fn​]≤Zn(a)​.
(26)
Thus Z(a) is an integrable nonnegative supermartingale. By the almost sure supermartingale convergence theorem it has a finite almost-sure limit. The bounded decreasing budget An​ has a finite pathwise limit as well, so Xn∧τa​​=Zn(a)​−An​ converges to a finite limit almost surely.
Take the intersection of these probability-one convergence events over all positive integers a. On the further probability-one event ∑j≥1​Yj​<∞, choose an integer a strictly larger than that total. Then τa​=∞, so the stopped sequence is the original one. Consequently
Xn​ converges almost surely to a finite limit.​
(27)
This proves supermartingale convergence with summable adapted drift under almost-sure summability alone; it does not require E∑j​Yj​<∞.
