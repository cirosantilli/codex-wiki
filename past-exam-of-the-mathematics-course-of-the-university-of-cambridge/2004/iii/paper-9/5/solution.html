An explicit disk map of a slit half disk makes the two boundary approaches visible without assuming an extension theorem for the boundary. We construct the map as a succession of bijections and then determine its limits.
First w=z2 maps the upper half-disc of radius two bijectively onto ∣w∣<4 slit along [0,4). Indeed, its inverse chooses the square root whose argument lies in (0,π). The removed segment on the imaginary axis becomes [−1,0), so the image of D is precisely

The Möbius transformation v=4(w+1)/(w+16) maps the radius-four disc bijectively onto the unit disc, taking −1 to zero and 4 to one. This follows also by writing it as (w/4+1/4)/(1+w/16), an automorphism of the unit disk after the scaling. It takes the removed real interval to [0,1), so the image is the unit disc with this radial slit removed. For any sequence approaching z0​, every infinite right-side subsequence has image tending to α, and every infinite left-side subsequence has image tending to β. At least one side occurs infinitely often. Conversely, any convergent image subsequence has a further subsequence confined to one side, so its limit must be the corresponding one of these two points. The cluster set is therefore exactly {α}, {β} or {α,β}, according to which sides occur infinitely often. Both sides really occur in the domain, for example along i/2±εn​ with positive εn​↓0.
