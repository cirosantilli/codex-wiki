A complex Banach algebra is an associative complex algebra with a complete norm satisfying submultiplicativity, ∥ab∥≤∥a∥∥b∥. A field has a nonzero identity e, and every nonzero element is invertible. We derive the Gelfand-Mazur theorem from these facts.
If ∥z∥<1, the series e+z+z2+⋯ converges in the complete algebra. Multiplying its partial sums by e−z and passing to the limit proves the Neumann series inverse. In particular, for any x∈B and ∣λ∣>∥x∥,
R(λ)=(λe−x)−1=λe​+∑n=1∞​λn+1xn​.
(19)
This resolvent of an element tends to zero in norm at infinity; the estimate follows from the geometric bound on ∥xn∥.
We next prove that the spectrum of an element cannot be empty. Suppose every λe−x were invertible. At each λ0​, write
λe−x=(λ0​e−x)(e+(λ−λ0​)R(λ0​)).
(20)
The Neumann series on a small disk about λ0​ proves that R is analytic there. Consequently, for each continuous linear functional ℓ:B→C, the function λ↦ℓ(R(λ)) is entire. It is bounded outside a large disk by the estimate above and bounded on that disk by continuity. The Liouville theorem makes it constant; its zero limit at infinity makes the constant zero.
Continuous complex linear functionals separate points of B. Here is the needed consequence of the Hahn-Banach theorem: for z=0, define g(tz)=t∥z∥ on the real span of z and extend it as a bounded real linear functional of norm one. Then ℓ(w)=g(w)−ig(iw) is complex linear and continuous, and Reℓ(z)=∥z∥=0. The real extension theorem is proved explicitly in Question 4(i) below. Thus ℓ(R(λ))=0 for every ℓ forces R(λ)=0, contradicting (λe−x)R(λ)=e=0. This proves the nonemptiness of the Banach-algebra spectrum.
Choose λ∈σB​(x). Since B is a field, the noninvertible element x−λe must be zero. Hence every x∈B is a scalar multiple of e. The map
Φ:C⟶B,Φ(λ)=λe​
(21)
is therefore a bijective unital complex-algebra homomorphism. It and its inverse are bounded, since ∥λe∥=∣λ∣∥e∥. Thus it is an isomorphism of Banach algebras. If the usual normalization ∥e∥=1 is included in the definition, it is an isometry; without that normalization, it is still the required continuous algebra isomorphism.
