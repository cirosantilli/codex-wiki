The Tychonoff theorem states that an arbitrary product of compact spaces, with the product topology, is compact; no Hausdorff hypothesis is necessary. We prove it using Zorn's lemma and the axiom of choice.
First, every proper filter on a set extends to an ultrafilter. Order its proper extensions by inclusion. The union of a chain is again a proper filter: any finite collection of its members occurs in one member of the chain, and the empty set occurs in none. Zorn's lemma gives a maximal proper filter U. If A∈/U, adjoining A must destroy properness, so some F∈U has F∩A=∅, and hence X∖A∈U. This proves the decision property of an ultrafilter.
Next prove the ultrafilter characterization of compactness. In a compact space the closed sets F, for F∈U, have the finite intersection property, so their intersection contains a point x. Every open neighbourhood O of x belongs to U: otherwise its closed complement belongs to U and contains x, a contradiction. Thus the ultrafilter converges to x. Conversely, if an open cover has no finite subcover, the closed complements have the finite intersection property and generate a proper filter. Extend it to an ultrafilter. A limit point lies in some member O of the cover, so both O and its complement would belong to the ultrafilter. This is impossible. These arguments also prove the closed-set form of compactness directly by taking complements.
Now let X=∏j∈J​Xj​. If a factor is empty, the product is empty and compact. Otherwise the axiom of choice makes X nonempty. For an ultrafilter U on X, its coordinate image
Uj​={A⊆Xj​:πj−1​(A)∈U}
(9)
is an ultrafilter. Compactness of Xj​ supplies at least one limit xj​; use the axiom of choice to choose one for every j. A basic open neighbourhood of x=(xj​)j​ restricts finitely many coordinates. Each corresponding inverse image belongs to U, so their finite intersection also does. Hence U converges to x in the product topology. The ultrafilter criterion proves the theorem. The explicit choices and the maximal-filter argument are exactly the places where choice enters.
The Banach-Alaoglu theorem states that the closed unit ball of the continuous dual space V∗ of a real or complex normed vector space V is compact in the weak-star topology σ(V∗,V). Completeness of V is not required.
For each v∈V, let Dv​={z∈K:∣z∣≤∥v∥}, a compact scalar disk or interval. By the Tychonoff theorem, P=∏v∈V​Dv​ is compact. Inside P, impose the equations
zu+v​=zu​+zv​,zαv​=αzv​(u,v∈V, α∈K).
(10)
Each equation defines a closed subset, since it concerns continuous coordinate projections into a Hausdorff scalar field. Their intersection K is therefore compact. A point in K defines a linear functional ℓ(v)=zv​, and the coordinate bounds give ∣ℓ(v)∣≤∥v∥. Conversely, every ℓ∈V∗ with ∥ℓ∥≤1 gives such a point. Thus K is exactly the image of the dual unit ball under ℓ↦(ℓ(v))v∈V​. The induced product topology is precisely pointwise convergence on V, which is the weak-star topology. This proves
{ℓ∈V∗:∥ℓ∥≤1} is weak-star compact.​
