If F is empty, the zero linear functional satisfies the conclusion vacuously. Otherwise choose one f0​∈F, put δ=ϵ/2, and form the convex set
E=F+B(0,δ)−f0​.
(35)
It contains B(0,δ) because f0​∈F. The point x=−f0​ is outside E: membership would imply 0=f+u for some f∈F with ∥u∥<δ, contradicting the assumed absence of points of F in B(0,ϵ).
Apply part (i). There is a continuous linear functional T with T(−f0​)≥1 and T(f+u−f0​)≤1 for every f∈F, ∥u∥<δ. Thus T=0 and
Tf+Tu≤1+Tf0​≤0.
(36)
For fixed f, take the supremum of Tu over this open ball; it is δ∥T∥. Consequently Tf≤−δ∥T∥ for every f∈F. The normalization
S=−δ∥T∥T​​
(37)
is a continuous linear functional satisfying Sf≥1 for every f∈F. No compactness, closedness or attainment of the supremum on the open ball is needed.
