Let p be the Minkowski functional of E:
p(v)=inf{t>0:v∈tE}.
(29)
The contained ball makes E absorbing, so p is finite, nonnegative and satisfies p(v)≤∥v∥/ϵ. It is positively homogeneous. To check subadditivity, if v∈sE and w∈tE, the defining property of a convex set gives (v+w)/(s+t)∈E, whence p(v+w)≤s+t; let s,t decrease to the respective infima. Thus p is a sublinear functional. We have p(e)≤1 for every e∈E. Also p(x)≥1: otherwise x∈tE for some 0<t<1, which would put x in E because E is a convex set and 0∈E.
We prove the dominated form of the Hahn-Banach theorem needed here. Suppose g is linear on a subspace M and g≤p there. To extend it to M+Rv with v∈/M, choose the value c at v between
supm∈M​{g(m)−p(m−v)}andinfn∈M​{p(n+v)−g(n)}.
(30)
Every lower candidate is at most every upper candidate, because
g(m)+g(n)=g(m+n)≤p(m+n)≤p(m−v)+p(n+v).
(31)
Taking m=n=0 among the candidates shows the two endpoints are finite: the lower supremum is at least −p(−v) and at most p(v), and the upper infimum lies between them and p(v). Define g​(m+tv)=g(m)+tc. For t>0, the upper inequality applied to m/t gives domination by p(m+tv). For t<0, apply the lower inequality to m/(−t) and multiply by −t. At t=0 domination already holds. Hence this is a dominated one-dimensional extension.
Order all dominated extensions by inclusion of their domains. Chain unions preserve linearity and domination, so Zorn's lemma gives a maximal extension. The one-dimensional argument shows its domain is the whole vector space. This proves the requisite dominated Hahn-Banach theorem, rather than invoking an unproved separation form.
On the line Rx, start with g(tx)=tp(x). For t≥0 this equals p(tx); for t<0 it is nonpositive and therefore at most p(tx). Extend it to T≤p on V. Then
Tx=p(x)≥1,Te≤p(e)≤1(e∈E).
(32)
Applying domination to v and −v also gives
∣Tv∣≤ϵ∥v∥​.
(33)
Thus T is a continuous linear functional, and
Tx≥1≥Tefor every e∈E.​
(34)
This proves separation from an absorbing convex set with no closedness assumption on E. The proof also gives the norm-preserving real extension theorem used earlier: take p(v)=C∥v∥ when the original functional has norm at most C.
