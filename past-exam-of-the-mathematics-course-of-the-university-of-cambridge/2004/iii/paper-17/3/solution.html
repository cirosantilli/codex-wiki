Let F be an oriented genus-g Seifert surface for K, and choose a basis a1​,…,a2g​ of H1​(F;Z). Push a representative curve slightly in the positive normal direction to obtain ai+​. The Seifert matrix in our row convention is
Vij​=lk(ai+​,aj​).
(13)
The difference V−VT is the intersection form of F, up to the convention for its orientation; in a symplectic basis it is a direct sum of unimodular two-by-two skew blocks.
Write X=S3∖intN(K) for the knot exterior. Linking number with K gives the epimorphism π1​(X)→Z sending a positive meridian to one. Let X be the associated infinite cyclic cover, with deck transformation t. The Alexander module of a knot is AK​=H1​(X;Z), regarded as a module over the Laurent polynomial ring Λ=Z[t,t−1]. Its order, the greatest common divisor of the maximal presentation minors, is the Alexander polynomial of a knot, defined up to ±tk. Equivalently these minors generate its zeroth Fitting ideal; we will obtain a square presentation, so its determinant gives the order. No assertion that Λ is a principal ideal domain is needed.
Here is the Seifert-matrix presentation of the Alexander module with its topological justification. Cut X along F, obtaining a connected manifold Y with two boundary copies F+ and F−. Up to collars this is the complement of a thickening of F in S3. Alexander duality gives H1​(Y;Z)≅Z2g and the perfect linking pairing with H1​(F;Z). Choose generators bj​ satisfying lk(bj​,ai​)=δij​. This homology statement does not require Y to be a handlebody. The two inclusions satisfy
i+​(ai​)=∑j​Vij​bj​,i−​(ai​)=∑j​Vji​bj​.
(14)
Indeed the first coefficients are the defining positive linking numbers; for the second, moving the two curves to opposite sides and using symmetry of linking gives lk(ai−​,aj​)=lk(aj+​,ai​).
Stack copies of Y, identifying the positive copy of F in one layer with the negative copy in the next. This is X. The Mayer–Vietoris sequence, with all translates collected into Laurent modules, contains
H1​(F)⊗Λti+​−i−​​H1​(Y)⊗Λ⟶AK​⟶H0​(F)⊗Λt−1​H0​(Y)⊗Λ.
(15)
The last map is injective because Λ is an integral domain. Thus the entire Alexander module is presented by the row relations tV−VT (transpose the matrix if using column vectors). At t=1 its determinant is det(V−VT)=1, so the determinant is nonzero and the module is torsion. Consequently
ΔK​(t)≐det(tV−VT),brΔK​≤2gs​(K),​
(16)
where ≐ denotes equality up to a Laurent unit. The second conclusion follows because a determinant of size 2g with entries linear in t has breadth at most 2g; minimize over Seifert surfaces.
For the displayed knot we give a reproducible diagram calculation. Label its ten crossings A through J as follows: A and B are the two upper crossings from left to right; C,D,E are the next three crossings in descending order; F is the crossing below E on the almost vertical strand; G is the left crossing of the bottom loop; H,I,J are the remaining three bottom crossings from left to right. With one traversal direction the over/under record is
AO​ BU​ JO​ IU​ HO​ FU​ GO​ HU​ IO​ JU​ DO​ CU​ EO​ DU​ BO​ AU​ CO​ EU​ FO​ GU​.
(17)
The crossings have the same sign. Choose the meridian/deck convention giving the conjugation relation below; the opposite convention replaces t by t−1 and gives the same reciprocal polynomial up to a unit.
Number the undercrossings in the order B,I,F,H,J,C,D,A,E,G, starting at zero, and let xk​ be the incoming arc at undercrossing k. The overpassing arc numbers are
(o0​,…,o9​)=(7,4,9,2,1,8,5,0,6,3).
(18)
The Wirtinger presentation has xk+1​=xok​​xk​xok​−1​, with indices modulo ten. Its lifted cellular boundaries, or equivalently Fox derivatives, give the Alexander relation matrix
Mkj​=tδj,k​−δj,k+1​+(1−t)δj,ok​​.
(19)
Delete the redundant final relation and the last column: the resulting matrix presents the Alexander module on the nine relative arc generators. To make the determinant check transparent, set h=2t2−3t+2, k=4t2−7t+5, and ℓ=2t2−2t+1. Eliminating unit pivots reduces this nine-generator presentation to
N=(th+(1−t)2kt(1−t)k​−hℓ​).
(20)
For example, writing the relative generators as z0​,…,z8​, the eliminated relations give z3​=tz2​, z4​=(t2−t+1)z2​, z1​=(t2−2t+2)z2​, z5​=hz2​, z7​=(1−t)hz2​+tz6​, z0​=t−1(t2−2t+2−(1−t)2h)z2​−(1−t)z6​, and z8​=(1−t)kz2​+(2t−1)z6​. The two remaining relations are precisely the rows of N. Hence
ΔK​(t)≐detN=5t4−15t3+21t2−15t+5.​
(21)
As a second determinant check, the unreduced nine-by-nine cofactor of M is t2detN. The polynomial has value one at t=1 and is reciprocal, as required.
For the genus upper bound, the oriented smoothing has seven Seifert circles. This can also be checked from the traversal record: number its twenty entries starting at one; smoothing sends an incoming visit to the entry following its other visit to the same crossing. The cycles are (1,17,13,19,7), (2,16), (3,11,15), (4,10), (5,9), (6,20,8), and (12,18,14). The Seifert algorithm therefore gives seven disks and ten bands, so χ(F)=7−10=−3=1−2g(F) and g(F)=2. The Alexander breadth is four, so the Alexander breadth bound on Seifert genus gives the matching lower bound. Thus gs​(K)=2​.
Finally, modulo two the primitive polynomial becomes t4+t3+t2+t+1. It has neither zero nor one as a root. The only monic irreducible quadratic over F2​ is t2+t+1, whose square is t4+t2+1, so the quartic is irreducible. Reduction modulo two therefore proves irreducibility over the rationals and, by primitivity, over Z and Λ. If K were a sum of two nontrivial knots, additivity of Seifert genus would force both summands to have genus one. Their Alexander polynomials multiply, so irreducibility would make one polynomial a unit and the other equal to this breadth-four polynomial, contradicting the breadth-at-most-two bound for genus one. This uses the full-degree irreducible Alexander polynomial implies a prime knot criterion, rather than irreducibility alone. Therefore K is prime.
