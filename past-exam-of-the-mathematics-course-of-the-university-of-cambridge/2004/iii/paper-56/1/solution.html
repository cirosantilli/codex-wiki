Use units c=1 and take the initially expanding branch with ordinary positive matter or radiation density. In the Friedmann-Lemaître-Robertson-Walker metric, k=+1,0,−1 respectively describe positive, zero and negative spatial curvature. Separately conserved pressureless matter has ρM​∝a−3 and radiation in cosmology has ρR​∝a−4, so either dominates the curvature and vacuum terms sufficiently far back on a hot Big Bang branch.
With negligible cosmological constant, a spatially flat radiation universe has a∝t1/2 and a flat matter universe has a∝t2/3; both expand forever while decelerating. An open universe also expands forever and eventually becomes approximately curvature dominated, with a∝t. A closed universe containing only ordinary matter/radiation reaches a maximum size and recollapses. For example, dust gives a˙2=C/a−k with C>0, so only k=+1 admits the positive turning point a=C.
Curvature alone does not determine the fate when the cosmological constant is retained. Positive Λ can cause late accelerated expansion and prevent even a closed universe from recollapsing; whether a closed expanding branch turns around depends on the relative matter and vacuum terms. Negative Λ can cause recollapse even for flat or open spatial sections. These statements follow by inspecting a˙2=C/a−k+Λa2/3 for dust; the curvature sign is a geometrical classification, not by itself a universal destiny criterion.
Define the critical density ρc​=3H2/(8πG) and cosmological density parameters ΩM​=ρM​/ρc​, ΩR​=ρR​/ρc​, ΩΛ​=Λ/(3H2) and Ωk​=−k/(a2H2). The Friedmann equation gives ΩM​+ΩR​+ΩΛ​+Ωk​=1. Dividing the Friedmann acceleration equation by −H2 gives
q=3H24πG​(ρ+3P)−ΩΛ​=21​ΩM​+ΩR​−ΩΛ​.
(1)
Consequently
q=21​ΩM​−ΩΛ​for dust plus vacuum,q=ΩR​−ΩΛ​for radiation plus vacuum.​
(2)
The deceleration parameter measures the sign and size of the acceleration relative to aH2: q>0 means deceleration, q<0 accelerated expansion and q=0 instantaneously zero acceleration. In particular a falling Hubble parameter alone does not imply deceleration, since q=−1−H˙/H2.
For conformal time dη=dt/a, the conformal Hubble parameter is H=aH. Using matter conservation and differentiating ΩM​∝ρM​/H2,
ΩM​Ω˙M​​=−3H−2HH˙​=H(2q−1),ΩM′​=HΩM​(ΩM​−1−2ΩΛ​).​
(3)
This is the matter density flow with a cosmological constant. Neglecting ΩΛ​ during matter domination reduces it to the requested relation
ΩM′​=HΩM​(ΩM​−1).​
(4)
That last equation is not exact with nonzero Λ. For example, a flat dust-plus-vacuum state with (ΩM​,ΩΛ​)=(0.9,0.1) gives ΩM′​/H=−0.27, whereas the matter-only expression gives −0.09.
For the flatness problem, let Ωtot​=ΩM​+ΩR​+ΩΛ​. Curvature satisfies Ωtot​−1=k/(aH)2, so
dlogadlog∣Ωtot​−1∣​=2q.
(5)
In decelerating near-flat radiation and matter eras, deviations therefore grow as a2 and a respectively. Maintaining small curvature at a late epoch requires very small early deviations unless a prior mechanism suppresses them. Cosmic inflation, with q≃−1, drives the deviation down approximately as a−2. If vacuum energy is present, flatness means Ωtot​=1, not ΩM​=1.
