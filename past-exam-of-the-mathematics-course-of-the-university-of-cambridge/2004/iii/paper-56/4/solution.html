Using the standard present photon abundance and critical-density normalization, this is approximately ΩX​h2≃mX​/(9×102eV), where h=H0​/(100kms−1Mpc−1) is the present Hubble normalization, not a magnetic field. A stable relic must not exceed the permitted matter density; for example, if ΩM​h2=0.15, even assigning all matter to it gives only an order-102eV mass ceiling.
A stronger restriction can follow from neutrino free streaming and the observed formation of large-scale structure of the universe: a light thermal population acts as hot rather than cold dark matter, erasing structure below its streaming scale. If at most a fraction fmax​ of the matter may be in this population, replace the overclosure ceiling by mX​≲9×102fmax​ΩM​h2eV. The allowed fraction is observational input and is not supplied, so the paper does not determine a unique tighter mass limit. A species still relativistic today is radiation rather than matter; its contribution to the effective number of neutrino species is ΔNeff​=(TX​/Tν​)4≃0.051, and must instead satisfy the radiation-density constraints. These conclusions assume stable thermally populated states; decay or nonstandard entropy histories change the bounds.
