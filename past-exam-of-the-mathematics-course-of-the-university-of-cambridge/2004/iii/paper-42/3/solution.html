Let the summands have mean mu, variance σ2>0, and cumulants κj​. For the standardized sum Zn​=(Sn​−nμ)/(σn​), expansion of its log-characteristic function gives a normal leading term and successively smaller cumulant terms. Fourier inversion turns these terms into Probabilists' Hermite polynomials. Under sufficient moments and a smooth nonlattice condition, the Edgeworth expansion begins
pZn​​(z)=ϕ(z)[1+6σ3n​κ3​​(z3−3z)+O(n−1)]
(11)
for bounded z. Higher orders include the fourth cumulant and products of lower cumulants. This is a central expansion in powers of n−1/2; it need not remain positive, normalized, or accurate in far tails after truncation.
For a saddlepoint density approximation, require the summand cumulant-generating function K to be finite on an interval about zero. At a sum value s=ny, find the interior saddle t satisfying K′(t)=y. Exponential tilting makes the tilted mean y, so the tilted sum is approximated locally by its normal probability density at its mean. Undoing the tilt gives
p​Sn​​(s)=2πnK′′(t)​exp{nK(t)−ts}​.
(12)
Equivalently this follows by deforming the transform-inversion contour through its stationary point. Suitable analytic and nonlattice regularity gives relative error O(n−1) at fixed interior y. This approximation incorporates a nonquadratic tail exponent and is positive, while an Edgeworth truncation uses a normal probability density times a polynomial. It uses more information from the distribution and stronger exponential-moment assumptions. Its integral may differ from one by a higher-order amount, so positivity does not imply exact normalization.
For the centered Laplace distribution, K(t)=t2+t4/2+⋯, hence κ2​=2 and κ3​=0. Its skewness vanishes, so the order-n−1/2 Edgeworth correction is zero. With z=s/2n​, the requested expansion, omitting terms of order n−1 in the standardized correction, is
pSn​E​(s)=4πn​1​e−s2/(4n).​
(13)
For bounded z the exact probability density function is this expression times 1+O(n−1). The prefactor is n−1/2 because this is the probability density function of the unstandardized sum; that change of units should not be confused with the orders inside the Edgeworth expansion.
For the saddlepoint, K′(t)=2t/(1−t2). Write y=s/n and d=1+y2​. The root in (−1,1) is
t=1+dy​,1−t2=1+d2​,yt=d−1,K′′(t)=d(1+d).
(14)
These formulas include s=0 without a division by y. Substitution yields
pSn​SP​(s)=2πnd(1+d)​exp{n[log21+d​−d+1]}​,d=1+(s/n)2​.​
(15)
This is the leading saddlepoint approximation, without its order-n−1 relative correction. Expanding d near one recovers the same central normal approximation, while retaining d captures the Laplace-sum large-deviation exponent away from the center.
