Let (Zk​,Fk​)0≤k≤n​ be an integrable real submartingale, and put Zn∗​=max0≤k≤n​Zk​. The refined Doob maximal inequality is
λP(Zn∗​≥λ)≤E[Zn​1{Zn∗​≥λ}​],λ>0.​
(1)
In particular, for a nonnegative submartingale the right-hand side is at most EZn​, giving the usual weak maximal bound.
For the proof, partition A={Zn∗​≥λ} by its first crossing time: Ak​={Zj​<λ (j<k), Zk​≥λ}∈Fk​. The submartingale property gives E[Zn​∣Fk​]≥Zk​, so
E[Zn​1Ak​​]=E[1Ak​​E(Zn​∣Fk​)]≥E[Zk​1Ak​​]≥λP(Ak​).
(2)
Summing over the disjoint crossing events proves the refined inequality, including a possible crossing at time zero. No independence assumption is needed.
The strong form is the Doob Lp maximal inequality. For p>1 and a nonnegative submartingale with Zn​∈Lp,
∥Zn∗​∥p​≤p−1p​∥Zn​∥p​.​
(3)
Indeed, integrate the refined inequality against pλp−2 up to K. The tail integral formula for moments and the Tonelli theorem give
E[(Zn∗​∧K)p]≤p−1p​E[Zn​(Zn∗​∧K)p−1].
(4)
Apply the Holder inequality, divide by ∥Zn∗​∧K∥pp−1​ when it is nonzero, and let K→∞ by the monotone convergence theorem. For a martingale M, the process ∣Mk​∣ is a submartingale by the conditional Jensen inequality, giving the corresponding bound for max∣Mk​∣. On a bounded continuous-time interval the nonnegative, right-continuous version follows by applying these inequalities on refining finite time grids and taking limits.

Use the natural filtration Gn​=σ(ξ1​,…,ξn​). Define the deterministic tail rn​=∑j=n∞​δj​ and put Zn​=ξn​+rn​. This is nonnegative and integrable. Since rn​=δn​+rn+1​,
E[Zn+1​∣Gn​]≤ξn​+δn​+rn+1​=Zn​.
(18)
Thus the deterministic summable drift correction produces a nonnegative supermartingale. The almost sure supermartingale convergence theorem gives a finite limit Z∞​ almost surely. Its finiteness is also reflected in the bound EZn​≤EZ1​ and Fatou's lemma, which gives EZ∞​≤EZ1​<∞. The convergence theorem follows from the upcrossing argument: bounded expected negative parts of −Z rule out infinitely many crossings of rational intervals, and the expectation bound excludes an infinite limit.
Since rn​→0, the required conclusion is ξn​⟶Z∞​ almost surely, with Z∞​<∞​. The original sequence need not itself be a supermartingale; subtracting the vanishing deterministic tail recovers it from one that is.

Work from time one; a finite initial prefix does not affect convergence. Fix a positive integer a, and use the stopping time τa​ and budget An​ from part (b). Define
Zn(a)​=Xn∧τa​​+a−Un∧τa​​.
(23)
It is nonnegative. For each finite n, the stopped variable Xn∧τa​​ is integrable: it is bounded by the finite sum X1​+⋯+Xn​, and the budget is bounded by a.
The stopped increment of X is
X(n+1)∧τa​​−Xn∧τa​​=1{τa​>n}​(Xn+1​−Xn​).
(24)
Taking conditional expectations, the assumed drift bound gives
E[X(n+1)∧τa​​∣Fn​]≤Xn∧τa​​+Yn​1{τa​>n}​.
(25)
The last term is bounded by a; no integrability assumption on the unlocalized Yn​ has been used. Its contribution is exactly cancelled by the budget decrement from part (b), giving
E[Zn+1(a)​∣Fn​]≤Zn(a)​.
(26)
Thus Z(a) is an integrable nonnegative supermartingale. By the almost sure supermartingale convergence theorem it has a finite almost-sure limit. The bounded decreasing budget An​ has a finite pathwise limit as well, so Xn∧τa​​=Zn(a)​−An​ converges to a finite limit almost surely.
Take the intersection of these probability-one convergence events over all positive integers a. On the further probability-one event ∑j≥1​Yj​<∞, choose an integer a strictly larger than that total. Then τa​=∞, so the stopped sequence is the original one. Consequently
Xn​ converges almost surely to a finite limit.​
(27)
This proves supermartingale convergence with summable adapted drift under almost-sure summability alone; it does not require E∑j​Yj​<∞.

Here is the one-parameter Kolmogorov continuity theorem. Suppose a real-valued stochastic process on [0,1] satisfies, for some p>0, q>0 and finite C,
E∣Xt​−Xs​∣p≤C∣t−s∣1+q,s,t∈[0,1].
(28)
Then it has a continuous modification of a stochastic process whose paths are Hölder continuous of every positive exponent γ<q/p. A modification means equality almost surely at each fixed time, not an a priori simultaneous equality at all times. Other compact time intervals follow by rescaling; the vector-valued version uses a norm in place of absolute value.
For the proof fix 0<γ<q/p and use the dyadic grids Dn​={j2−n:0≤j≤2n}. Let Dn∗​ be the largest adjacent increment on this grid. The union bound and Markov inequality give
P(Dn∗​>2−nγ)≤∑j=02n−1​2nγpE∣X(j+1)2−n​−Xj2−n​∣p≤C2−n(q−pγ).
(29)
The right-hand side is summable. The Borel-Cantelli first lemma shows that, outside a null set, an almost-surely finite random constant K can absorb the finitely many early grids so that
Dn∗​≤K2−nγfor every n.
(30)
We now use dyadic increment chaining. For two distinct dyadic points s,t, choose n with 2−(n+1)<∣t−s∣≤2−n. Approximate them from below by sj​,tj​∈Dj​, for j≥n. Each refinement moves by zero or one grid step, while sn​,tn​ are at most two grid steps apart. The sequences eventually equal the original dyadic points. Telescoping gives
∣Xt​−Xs​∣≤2K2−nγ+2K∑j=n+1∞​2−jγ≤Cγ​K∣t−s∣γ,
(31)
where a deterministic finite Cγ​ absorbs the geometric series and 2−n<2∣t−s∣. Thus the sample function on the dense union of dyadic grids has a unique Hölder-continuous extension to [0,1]. Define this extension to be X on the probability-one event, and set it to zero on the exceptional null set.
For a fixed t, take dyadic tj​→t. The moment condition gives Xtj​​→Xt​ in probability, while the constructed extension gives Xtj​​→Xt​ almost surely. Uniqueness of a probability limit yields Xt​=Xt​ almost surely. This proves the required modification property. Performing the argument for a countable increasing sequence of exponents tending to q/p gives one version with all smaller positive Hölder exponents, since its extensions agree on the dyadic points.
For Brownian motion, Gaussian increments give, for any integer r≥2,
E∣Bt​−Bs​∣2r=(2r−1)!!∣t−s∣r.
(32)
Use p=2r and q=r−1. The theorem gives every exponent α<(r−1)/(2r)=1/2−1/(2r). For any 0<α<1/2, choose r large enough. Intersect probability-one events over integer r and compact intervals [0,N] to obtain
Brownian paths are locally Ho¨lder continuous of every exponent 0<α<21​ almost surely.​
(33)
The usual continuous Brownian version has the same property: its continuous paths agree with the constructed version on a countable dense set and hence everywhere, outside one null set. The Hölder constant depends on the compact interval. This is the precise local meaning of Brownian Hölder regularity; it is not a uniform Hölder bound on the entire unbounded time axis.

Take a random variable Z with the Rademacher distribution, and set
Xn​=Z,Yn​={Z,−Z,​n even,n odd.​
(49)
Both marginal distributions equal the law of Z for every n. Hence Xn​ and Yn​ each have weak convergence of random variables to Z; one may take X=Y=Z on this probability space. But their sums alternate:
Xn​+Yn​={2Z,0,​n even,n odd.​
(50)
Indeed, at t=π/2 their characteristic functions are −1 for even n and 1 for odd n, so the sums have no weak limit at all. Separate marginal weak convergence does not imply convergence of the sum. The marginal weak convergence does not control sums counterexample changes the dependence while leaving the marginals fixed. Unlike convergence in probability, separate marginal weak convergence of random variables contains no information about the joint coupling.
