Put K=⋂L​L, where L ranges over all maximal left ideals. For each such L, the action on the simple module A/L has a primitive ideal PL​ as its kernel. An element of that kernel annihilates 1+L, so PL​⊆L. The intersection J of all primitive ideals is consequently contained in every L, hence J⊆K.
Conversely, take x∈K and any nonzero simple module E. For every nonzero v∈E, simplicity makes the evaluation map A→E, a↦av, surjective. Its kernel is a maximal left ideal, so it contains x. Thus xv=0 for every nonzero v, and also for zero. The element x therefore belongs to the annihilator of every simple module, hence to every primitive ideal. This proves
J=L maximal left ideal⋂​L.​
(15)
Since the primitive ideals are two-sided ideals, this argument also makes J a two-sided ideal, despite the one-sided ideals in this alternative description.
