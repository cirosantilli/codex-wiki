Past exam of the mathematics course of the University of Cambridge / 2004 / iii / Paper 47 / 1 / Solution

OurBigBook.com Words: 385
Use a Minkowski external source term +∫Jχ, where χ is the integration variable, and normalize the vacuum functional:
Z[J]=∫DχeiS[χ]∫Dχexp{iS[χ]+i∫d4xJ(x)χ(x)}​,W[J]=logZ[J].
(1)
The usual vacuum boundary prescription is implicit. This is the logarithmic convention Z=eW for the connected generating functional. It makes the given quadratic expression consistent with ΔF​=⟨Tχχ⟩. Another common convention is Z=eiWc​, with Wc​=−iW; mixing the two conventions would change the factors of i below.
Functional derivative generates the full and connected correlation functions:
Gn​(x1​,…,xn​)=i−nδJ(x1​)⋯δJ(xn​)δnZ​​J=0​,Gnc​(x1​,…,xn​)=i−nδJ(x1​)⋯δJ(xn​)δnW​​J=0​.
(2)
These are time-ordered products in the vacuum, and G1​=G1c​=0 by the stated assumption. Differentiating eW four times partitions the four insertions into connected blocks. All partitions with a singleton vanish, leaving
G4​(1,2,3,4)=G4c​(1,2,3,4)+G2c​(1,2)G2c​(3,4)+G2c​(1,3)G2c​(2,4)+G2c​(1,4)G2c​(2,3).​
(3)
The shorthand arguments stand for spacetime points; this is a functional cumulant identity, not an assumption of a free field.
To construct the quantum effective action, define the source-dependent classical field
φ(x)=⟨χ(x)⟩J​=−iδJ(x)δW​.
(4)
Locally invert this relation to obtain J[φ], assuming the response integral kernel is invertible with the chosen regulator and boundary prescription. Take its Legendre transform
iΓ[φ]=W[J[φ]]−i∫d4xJ[φ](x)φ(x).​
(5)
The chain-rule terms proportional to δJ cancel, so δΓ/δφ=−J. A field-independent constant is fixed separately and is immaterial to the vertices. At zero external source the classical field is zero, and there is no linear term.
For the two-point vertex, differentiate the classical field once. If G=G2c​, then
δJ(y)δφ(x)​=iG(x,y),δφ(y)δJ(x)​=−iG−1(x,y),∫d4zG(x,z)G−1(z,y)=δ(4)(x−y).
(6)
The vertex coefficients in this problem are derivatives of iΓ, not of Γ alone. Therefore
Γ2​(x,y)=−G−1(x,y).​
(7)
For the three-point vertex, the derivative of the connected two-point integral kernel at a general external source is
δJ(c)δG(a,b)​=iG3c​(a,b,c).
(8)
Combine this with δJ/δφ=−iG−1 and differentiate the inverse identity, δG−1=−G−1(δG)G−1. This gives the logarithmic-source inverse-kernel vertices relation
Γ3​(x,y,z)=∫d4ad4bd4cG−1(x,a)G−1(y,b)G−1(z,c)G3c​(a,b,c).​
(9)
All kernels here are finally evaluated at zero external source. The connected three-point function is amputated by its three inverse quantum field theory propagators.
For the specified quadratic W, the external source response is φ=iΔF​J, hence J=−iΔF−1​φ. Substitution in the transform gives
Γ[φ]=2i​∫d4xd4yφ(x)ΔF−1​(x−y)φ(y),Γ2​=−ΔF−1​,Γn​=0 (n≥3).​
(10)
For the standard free quantum field theory propagator ΔF​(p)=i/(p2−m2+i0), this is the usual quadratic free action, up to the vacuum prescription. The generating functional is Gaussian, so connected functions beyond second order vanish and there are no interaction vertices or nontrivial scattering amplitudes. Full higher even-point functions can still be nonzero: they are products of two-point functions, as the four-point formula shows.
