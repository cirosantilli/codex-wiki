Gauge reduction and the determinant. A direct integral ∫DAeiS[A] integrates repeatedly over gauge-equivalent configurations. The gauge group volume is infinite, and the ungauge-fixed quadratic operator has longitudinal zero directions, so it has no ordinary inverse quantum field theory propagator. Gauge-invariant observables require dividing out this redundancy rather than treating it as an ordinary physical fluctuation.
The finite-dimensional change-of-variables identity underlying gauge fixing is precise. Suppose a condition χ(xα)=f has one solution in the group-coordinate patch and its derivative matrix at the solution is nonsingular. Then
∫dαδ(r)(χ(xα)−f)​det∂α∂χ(xα)​​=1.

An invariant measure and invariant integrand then allow the group volume to factor out. In the functional version, group coordinates carry Haar measure and the corresponding operator determinant replaces the matrix determinant.

Ghost representation. For a finite matrix M, the Grassmann Gaussian integral over independent anticommuting variables is proportional to its determinant:
∫∏a​dca​dca​exp(ica​Mab​cb​)=constant×detM.
(40)
The constant is independent of M and depends on ordering and factors of i. The functional counterpart introduces Faddeev-Popov ghost fields ca and antighost fields ca, with
Sgh​=∫d4xcaMab[A]cb=−∫d4xca∂μ(Dμ​c)a.
(41)
Integration by parts writes its density as (∂μca)(Dμ​c)a, displaying a kinetic term and a gauge-field/ghost interaction. These are Grassmann-valued fields with scalar Lorentz transformation law. They represent the orbit Jacobian determinant; their closed loops carry the fermionic minus sign, and they are not physical asymptotic particles. In the abelian case Dad=∂, the determinant is gauge-field independent and the ghosts decouple. Nonabelian ghosts interact and are necessary for the consistent cancellation of unphysical gauge contributions.
Physical-state reduction. Let Q be the Hermitian BRST charge. Its key algebraic property is BRST nilpotence, Q2=0, and the gauge-fixed dynamics preserves it. Physical states are its BRST cohomology at ghost number zero:
Hphys​=imQ​gh=0​kerQ​gh=0​​,Q∣ψ⟩=0,∣ψ⟩∼∣ψ⟩+Q∣χ⟩.​
(42)
Here ∣χ⟩ has ghost number −1. BRST nilpotence ensures that exact vectors lie in the kernel, so the quotient is defined. Because Q is Hermitian, an exact vector is orthogonal to every closed vector, ⟨ψ∣Qχ⟩=⟨Qψ∣χ⟩=0, and exact vectors are null, ⟨Qχ∣Qχ⟩=⟨χ∣Q2χ⟩=0. They therefore represent no independent physical state. Commutation of Q with the Hamiltonian preserves these classes under time evolution.
The covariant gauge/ghost space before reduction has an indefinite inner product. This is essential: a Hermitian nilpotent BRST charge requires an indefinite auxiliary space if it is nonzero. On an ordinary positive-definite Hilbert space the same norm identity would force Q=0. Positivity of the resulting physical space uses the gauge-theory structure in addition to the abstract BRST nilpotence identities.
For a nonzero on-shell photon momentum, the one-particle conclusion is
kμεμ​=0,εμ​∼εμ​+αkμ​.​
(43)
