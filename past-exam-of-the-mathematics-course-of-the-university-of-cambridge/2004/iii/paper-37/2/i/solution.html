Treat the counts as independent binomial distributions, Ri​∼Bin(ni​,πi​), conditional on the known numbers of operations. The null hypothesis is that the probabilities πi​ are all equal, with no assumed numerical value. Centre 4 has no operations in this period and hence contributes likelihood one, supplying no information about its mortality probability. The empty groups in a binomial homogeneity test principle leaves 11 contributing centres and 10 test degrees of freedom.
Under the common-probability model the maximum-likelihood estimator is
π=∑i​ni​∑i​ri​​=1353283​=0.209165.
(13)
Use the two-column contingency table of deaths and survivors, deleting its all-zero row. The Pearson chi-squared test of homogeneity has statistic
X2=∑i:ni​>0​{ni​π(ri​−ni​π)2​+ni​(1−π)((ni​−ri​)−ni​(1−π))2​}=∑i:ni​>0​ni​π(1−π)(ri​−ni​π)2​.
(14)
Under the null, the large-sample reference distribution is χ102​. The expected counts are adequate for this approximation here, including at the smallest nonempty centre. Calculating gives
X2=39.3923,p≈2.17×10−5.​
(15)
An alternative is the likelihood-ratio test against separate centre probabilities, with binomial deviance
D=2∑i:ni​>0​{ri​logni​πri​​+(ni​−ri​)logni​(1−π)ni​−ri​​}.
(16)
The unrestricted probabilities are ri​/ni​; subtracting one fitted common probability leaves the same 10 degrees of freedom. Here D=39.5033 and p≈2.07×10−5. Thus there is strong evidence against a common mortality probability. This omnibus test does not identify Centre 1 as the sole source of the heterogeneity. If its comparison with the remaining centres is the primary scientific question, specify a separate planned one-versus-rest comparison; inspection of many pairwise comparisons calls for consideration of multiple testing.
