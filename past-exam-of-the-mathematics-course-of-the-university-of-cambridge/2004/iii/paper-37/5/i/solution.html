Use the time-homogeneous continuous-time multi-state model displayed in part ii. Write Z(t) for the state at time t, Q=(qrs​) for its generator matrix, and
prs​(u)=Pr{Z(t+u)=s∣Z(t)=r},P(u)=euQ.
(38)
For r=s, qrs​=limh↓0​prs​(h)/h is the transition intensity, and qrr​=−∑s=r​qrs​. Condition on the observed initial state, and treat the clinic observation process and administrative end of follow-up as noninformative. A panel-observed interval contributes its transition probability, not the density of a jump at the examination date; the transitions inside that interval need not have been observed.
For the first patient, the two clinic intervals contribute p11​(2)p13​(2). The subsequent death is observed at elapsed time u=0.2 after the last clinic state, so its contribution is the conditional death-time density
g35​(u)=∑s=14​p3s​(u)qs5​=p33​(u)q35​+p34​(u)q45​.
(39)
The equality uses the permitted directions: starting from state 3, only living states 3 and 4 can be occupied before death. To obtain this density directly, survival in a living state s until time u, followed by death in (u,u+du), has probability p3s​(u)qs5​du+o(du); add over the unobserved state s. Hence the mixed panel and exact-death likelihood contribution is
L1​=p11​(2)p13​(2){p33​(0.2)q35​+p34​(0.2)q45​}.​
(40)
It includes the possibility of an unrecorded transition 3→4→5 between the last clinic visit and death. Keeping only p33​(0.2)q35​ would exclude that allowed history, while p35​(0.2) would be the probability of death by that time rather than the density of death at that time. With dates recorded in whole days, an exact interval-likelihood treatment integrates this density over the relevant day; the density formulation is its fine-time approximation.
For the second patient, the three successive intervals with state 1 at both ends, then the interval ending in state 2, and the last interval remaining in state 2 give the panel-observed multi-state likelihood
L2​=p11​(2)3p12​(2)p22​(2).​
(41)
There is no additional death contribution after the final administrative observation. Each likelihood contribution already accounts for being alive in the observed state at that visit. For independent patients their joint conditional likelihood contribution is L1​L2​.
For an explicit form, put λr​=−qrr​. Irreversibility gives
prr​(u)=e−λr​u,p1s​(u)=λs​−λ1​q1s​​(e−λ1​u−e−λs​u)(s=2,3),
(42)
and
p34​(u)=λ4​−λ3​q34​​(e−λ3​u−e−λ4​u).
(43)
These formulas follow by integrating the density of the one possible intermediate jump time; at equal exit rates the limiting expression is qrs​ue−λr​u. Thus
g35​(u)=q35​e−λ3​u+λ4​−λ3​q34​q45​​(e−λ3​u−e−λ4​u).
(44)
Using the displayed fitted rates as numerical values gives g35​(0.2)≈0.289314 per year, L1​≈0.017596 per year and L2​≈0.016048, providing a check of the probability-versus-density distinction.
