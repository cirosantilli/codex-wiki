The Green function of the Laplacian with −∇2Gd​=δ(d) is consequently Gd​(r)=1/[(d−2)Ωd−1​rd−2]. Define the gravitational Poisson coupling κD​ by ∇2Ψ=κD​Mδ(d), and use acceleration g=−∇Ψ. The same flux calculation gives
ΨD​(r)=−κD​MGd​(r)=−(D−3)ΩD−2​rD−3κD​M​.​

This explicitly fixes the normalization of the gravitational coupling: in four dimensions κ4​=4πG4​, so Ψ4​=−G4​M/r. If a higher-dimensional Newton constant is instead defined by the force law ∣g∣=GD​M/rD−2, then κD​=ΩD−2​GD​ in the displayed expression. In all conventions the force scales as r−(D−2) and the potential as r−(D−3). If “D dimensions” denotes spatial dimensions instead, replace d by D and the potential exponent is D−2.
For d=2, integration gives ϕ=−qlog(r/r0​)/(2πε3​) and Ψ=κ3​Mlog(r/r0​)/(2π) for the Poisson-normalized gravitational model; these logarithmic potentials have no zero-at-infinity convention. For d=1, a fundamental solution of the negative Laplacian is G1​(x)=−∣x∣/2, giving linear potentials up to an additive homogeneous solution.
Now compactify the fourth spatial coordinate y on a circle of circumference L=2πR, retaining three noncompact coordinates with radial distance r. In uncompactified five-dimensional spacetime, G4​(r,y)=1/[4π2(r2+y2)]. A periodic point source is therefore represented by its images:
Gcircle​(r,y)=4π21​∑n∈Z​r2+(y+nL)21​.
(24)
For r/L→∞, write the sum as r−2∑n​[1+(nL/r)2]−1 on the source slice y=0. The spacing L/r tends to zero, so its Riemann sum approaches the integrable function's integral:
∑n​r2+n2L21​∼Lr1​∫−∞∞​1+u2du​=Lrπ​.
(25)
Thus the compactified Green function is asymptotic to 1/(4πLr), already proving the four-dimensional inverse-distance law.
An exact expression makes the crossover transparent. The Fourier transform of (r2+y2)−1 is πe−r∣k∣/r, obtained by closing the integration contour around y=ir or −ir. Applying the Poisson summation formula then gives, at y=0,
∑n​r2+n2L21​=Lrπ​(1+2∑n≥1​e−2πnr/L)=Lrπ​coth(Lπr​).
(26)
The last equality sums a geometric series. Hence the point-source potential with one compact spatial dimension is
ϕ(r,0)=4πε5​Lrq​coth(Lπr​),Ψ(r,0)=−4πLrκ5​M​coth(Lπr​).​
(27)
At short distances r≪L, coth(πr/L)∼L/(πr) and these reproduce the five-dimensional 1/r2 potentials. At long distances r≫L, the hyperbolic factor is 1+O(e−2πr/L), giving the four-dimensional 1/r potentials with effective Poisson couplings ε4​=Lε5​ and κ4​=κ5​/L. Equivalently only the massless Kaluza-Klein mode survives at long distance, while the massive modes contribute exponentially suppressed terms.
