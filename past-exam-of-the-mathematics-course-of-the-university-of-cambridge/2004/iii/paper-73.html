Write c=gH​ and Rd​=c/∣f∣, with f=0. For the profiles below take f>0, so α=L/Rd​ agrees with the stated parameter; changing the sign of f reverses the balanced meridional velocity but not the height profile. The linearized shallow water equations are
ut​−fv=−gηx​,vt​+fu=−gηy​,ηt​+H(ux​+vy​)=0.
(1)
Taking the horizontal curl gives (vx​−uy​)t​=−f(ux​+vy​)=fηt​/H. Hence the signed linear shallow-water potential vorticity anomaly is conserved:
∂t​(vx​−uy​−Hf​η)=0.​
(2)
The initial data are independent of y, so the solution remains independent of y and vx​=f(η−η0​)/H. Differentiating continuity in time and using the zonal momentum equation gives
ηtt​=−H(fvx​−gηxx​),ηtt​−c2ηxx​+f2η=f2η0​,η(0)=η0​,ηt​(0)=0.​

For a horizontal Fourier mode with wavenumber k, put Ωk2​=f2+c2k2. The solution splits into a balanced component and inertia-gravity waves:
η​s​=Ωk2​f2​η​0​,η​(t)=η​s​+(η​0​−η​s​)cos(Ωk​t).
(4)
These waves have frequencies ∣ω∣≥∣f∣, phase velocity ω/k, and group velocity c2k/ω, whose magnitude is less than c. They have zero perturbation potential vorticity and carry the unbalanced part away. On the unbounded line, the localized difference between the initial and balanced profiles disperses, leaving a local steady limit. No damping is needed for this local geostrophic adjustment; in a closed periodic domain, undamped waves would persist and a literal pointwise steady limit would generally not exist.
The bounded steady height satisfies the modified Helmholtz equation
Rd2​ηs′′​−ηs​=−η0​.

The steady meridional momentum equation gives us​=0, and the zonal equation gives the geostrophic balance fvs​=gηs′​. Thus

The height is odd and monotone, and the meridional current is even, with maximum vs​(0)=gh(1−e−α)/(fL) for h>0.
For α≪1, the initial ramp is narrow compared with the Rossby deformation radius. To leading order,
ηs​≃hsgn(x)(1−e−∣x∣/Rd​),vs​≃cgh​e−∣x∣/Rd​=fLghα​e−∣x∣/Rd​.
(8)
Near the origin the height rises with slope h/Rd​, rather than h/L; the current has width Rd​=L/α and peak ghα/(fL). For α≫1, the central height remains nearly hx/L and the central velocity nearly gh/(fL). Around each end of the ramp there is a matching layer of width Rd​=L/α, with height correction of order h/α; the exterior current decays over the same width. At x=±L the current is approximately half its central value. In both limits the zonal current is zero. These are the broad rotational adjustment of a narrow disturbance and the weak edge adjustment of a broad disturbance, respectively.
Use the energy normalization specified for the height energy: the linear total energy density is (u2+v2)/2+gη2/(2H). The linear equations imply
∂t​[2u2+v2​+2Hgη2​]+∂x​(gηu)=0.
(9)

ΔV=2Hgh2​∫−∞∞​[2e−∣x∣/Rd​−e−2∣x∣/Rd​]dx≃2H3gh2Rd​​=2Hα3gh2L​​.

ΔT=21​(cgh​)2∫−∞∞​e−2∣x∣/Rd​dx≃2Hgh2Rd​​=2Hαgh2L​​,ΔVΔT​⟶31​​.

Let S(z)=dρs​/dz, N2=−gS/ρ0​>0, and define the quasi-geostrophic streamfunction by ψ=p​/(ρ0​f0​). Use horizontal scale L, vertical scale D, velocity scale U and advective time L/U. The required quasi-geostrophic approximation has small Rossby number ε=U/(∣f0​∣L), βL/∣f0​∣=O(ε), and Burger number N2D2/(f02​L2)=O(1). Hydrostatic, thin-layer scaling has D/L≪1; density variations are small compared with ρ0​, as required by the Boussinesq approximation. The leading horizontal flow is nondivergent, with vertical velocity w=O(εUD/L). Retain forcing of the same slow order as density advection, r=O(Uρ​/L); stronger forcing need not preserve this balanced scaling.
Leading geostrophic balance and the hydrostatic approximation give
ug​=−ψy​,vg​=ψx​,ρ​=−gρ0​f0​​ψz​.
(12)
Write Dg​/Dt=∂t​−ψy​∂x​+ψx​∂y​. The density equation becomes
f0​DtDg​ψz​​+N2w=−ρ0​g​r,w=−N2f0​​DtDg​ψz​​−ρ0​N2g​r.
(13)
Taking the curl of the horizontal momentum equations at the next order and using continuity gives the stretching equation
DtDg​​(∇h2​ψ+βy)=f0​wz​.
(14)
Substitute the expression for w. Vertical differentiation commutes with the geostrophic material derivative acting on ψz​ here: the extra Jacobian is J(ψz​,ψz​)=0. Since N depends only on height, this yields the diabatically forced quasi-geostrophic potential vorticity equation
DtDg​q​=−ρ0​gf0​​∂z​(N2r​),q=∇h2​ψ+∂z​(N2f02​​ψz​)+βy.​
(15)
The minus sign is appropriate to the specified density forcing; a positive upward buoyancy forcing is R=−gr/ρ0​ and gives Dg​q/Dt=f0​∂z​(R/N2).
Now take constant N and linearize about rest. Put s=f02​/N2 and S0​=gf0​μr0​/(ρ0​N2). The forced equation is
∂t​(ψxx​+sψzz​)+βψx​=S0​e−μzcoskxcosω0​t.
(16)
For the harmonic heating response of quasi-geostrophic flow, represent the two traveling Fourier components with frequencies ω+​=ω0​ and ω−​=−ω0​:
ψ=Re∑σ=±​ϕσ​(z)ei(kx−ωσ​t).
(17)
Each component of the forcing has amplitude S0​/2, so
−iωσ​(sϕσ′′​−k2ϕσ​)+iβkϕσ​=2S0​​e−μz.
(18)
Equivalently its vertical structure obeys
ϕσ′′​−λσ2​ϕσ​=Cσ​e−μz,λσ2​=f02​N2​(k2+ωσ​βk​),Cσ​=2ρ0​f0​ωσ​igμr0​​.​
(19)
These are the time-periodic forced responses; unspecified initial data may add free Rossby waves. Selecting no incoming waves describes forcing switched on causally and then its persistent harmonic part.
For β>0, the eastward component always has λ+2​>0. Let λ+​>0. The growing exponential would introduce an unbounded velocity and pressure disturbance at infinity, so discard it. The bottom condition ϕ+​(0)=0 then gives
ϕ+​(z)=μ2−λ+2​C+​​(e−μz−e−λ+​z).​
(20)
If μ=λ+​, this expression has the regular limit −C+​ze−μz/(2μ), rather than a physical singularity.
For ω0​>β/k, the westward component also has positive λ−2​. Choose λ−​>0 and use the same decaying expression with minus subscripts, including the same coincident-exponent limit. Thus both components are vertically evanescent above the heating.
For 0<ω0​<β/k, put
m=∣f0​∣N​ω0​βk​−k2​>0,λ−2​=−m2.
(21)
Both homogeneous solutions e±imz are bounded, so boundedness is insufficient. A free Rossby wave with phase kx+mz−ωt has
ω=−k2+sm2βk​,cgz​=∂m∂ω​=(k2+sm2)22βksm​.
(22)
The radiation condition demands energy propagation away from the heating, upward into z>0. For β,k>0 that selects positive m, hence eimz in the westward component. The resulting solution is
ϕ−​(z)=μ2+m2C−​​(e−μz−eimz).​
(23)
It satisfies ϕ−​(0)=0 and, away from the decaying forcing, ϕ−′​∼imϕ−​. Its far-field amplitude does not decay in this nondissipative model: continuous forcing supplies an outgoing wave train. Thus the low-frequency westward component propagates vertically while the eastward component remains evanescent.
At the threshold ω0​=β/k, the westward vertical wavenumber is zero. Excluding the linearly growing homogeneous solution and retaining the bottom condition gives ϕ−​=C−​(e−μz−1)/μ2. This bounded limiting response tends to a constant and has zero vertical group velocity; demanding both bottom zero and decay to zero at infinity would overdetermine this threshold problem. For β≤0, the branch labels and propagation inequality must be reconsidered; the comparison stated here assumes the usual positive beta-plane gradient.

The hydrostatic buoyancy anomaly is b′=f0​ψz​, and its leading adiabatic evolution is Dg​b′/Dt+N02​w=0. The small vertical velocity advects the background stratification; vertical advection of the perturbation buoyancy is higher order. Consequently
w=−DtDg​​(N02​f0​​ψz​).​
(24)
The impermeability condition at a stationary lower boundary z=αy is w=αv. Using vg​=ψx​ gives the topographic buoyancy boundary condition for quasi-geostrophic flow
DtDg​​(N02​f0​​ψz​)+αψx​=0.
(25)
For application at the reference plane, the actual small-height condition is ∣α∣L/D≪1. Slopes scaled by D/L are of Rossby-number order, so this topographic contribution is retained alongside the small ageostrophic vertical velocity. The dimensional slope should not be compared to the Rossby number without also specifying the aspect ratio.
The basic quasi-geostrophic streamfunction is ψ​=−Λzy. It gives U=Λz, basic buoyancy gradient by​=−f0​Λ, and spatially constant interior quasi-geostrophic potential vorticity. Linearizing its conservation law therefore gives
(∂t​+Λz∂x​)q′=0,q′=ψxx′​+ψyy′​+sψzz′​,s=f02​/N02​.​
(26)
The printed equation omits the shear-advection term. This cannot be omitted for general disturbances: an interior anomaly proportional to cos[k(x−Λzt)] is materially conserved but not independent of time at a fixed location. The intended zero-interior-PV Eady model modes do have q′=0 and satisfy both expressions; the ensuing mode calculation is valid in that subspace.
At either boundary, linearizing the geostrophic material derivative includes v′ψ​zy​=−Λψx′​. Let
A0​=Λ−f0​N02​α​,AD​=Λ−f0​N02​γ​,b0​=ψz′​(0),bD​=ψz′​(D).
(27)
The two boundary equations are
∂t​b0​=A0​ψx′​(0),(∂t​+ΛD∂x​)bD​=AD​ψx′​(D).​
(28)
These are exactly the respective bottom and top slope corrections to the rigid-boundary buoyancy condition for quasi-geostrophic waves.
Assume periodicity horizontally and first consider q′=0. Multiply its defining equation by ψx′​ and integrate over the layer. The two horizontal Laplacian terms vanish by integration by parts; the remaining volume term after one vertical integration is an x-derivative. Therefore
0=∫ψx′​q′dV=s∫dxdy[ψx′​ψz′​]0D​.
(29)
For nonzero A0​,AD​, the boundary equations give
dtd​∫2f0​A0​b02​​dxdy=f0​1​∫b0​ψx′​(0)dxdy,dtd​∫2f0​AD​bD2​​dxdy=f0​1​∫bD​ψx′​(D)dxdy.
(30)
The top advection term integrates to zero. Subtracting proves conservation of the boundary pseudomomentum of a sloping Eady layer:
dtd​∫dxdy[2(Λf0​−N02​γ)ψz′2​(D)​−2(Λf0​−N02​α)ψz′2​(0)​]=0.​
(31)
The original PDF uses squared vertical derivatives and the upper slope γ in the first denominator; these details are corrupted in the converted TeX. For arbitrary nonzero interior PV the same calculation instead gives dP/dt=(f0​s)−1∫ψx′​q′dV, which need not vanish. For example, at an instant take ψ′=coskx+(z/D)2sinkx, independent of y; the zonal average of ψx′​q′ is −ks/D2. Thus the boundary-only conservation statement also needs the zero-interior-PV qualification.
For an exponentially growing smooth normal mode, this qualification is automatic: the interior equation is ik(Λz−c)q​=0, and a phase speed with nonzero imaginary part cannot equal the real basic velocity. Therefore q​=0. If A0​AD​<0, the conserved boundary quadratic form is definite, so a nonzero exponentially growing boundary amplitude is impossible. Instability consequently requires
(1−f0​ΛN02​γ​)(1−f0​ΛN02​α​)>0,​
(32)
for Λ=0. When one A vanishes, the corresponding boundary derivative is simply advected and must vanish for a growing mode. The other boundary then yields a real single-edge-wave speed; if both vanish, the zero-interior-PV Neumann problem has no nonzero growing mode. These degenerate cases are neutral and should not be handled by dividing by zero.
For equal slopes, put ζ=z/D, C=c/(ΛD), M=N0​∣k∣D/∣f0​∣, and a=N02​α/(f0​Λ)−1. The normal-mode interior equation is Φζζ​−M2Φ=0 for the zero-interior-PV modes. Thus
Φ=Pcosh(Mζ)+Qsinh(Mζ),(ζ−C)Φζ​+aΦ=0at ζ=0,1.
(33)
The coefficient equations are
aP−CMQ=0,
(34)
[(1−C)MsinhM+acoshM]P+[(1−C)McoshM+asinhM]Q=0.
(35)
Setting their determinant to zero, without dividing by C or a, gives
C(1−C)MsinhM+acoshM+Ma2​sinhM=0,
(36)
so the Eady model with parallel sloping boundaries has
C=21​±41​+aMcothM​+M2a2​​.​
(37)
For positive k,f0​, M is the printed μ, and a is the printed α. The expression is even in the sign of that vertical scale. For a≥0 the radicand is at least 1/4, so both speeds are real and these modes have no exponential growth. For equal slopes the earlier necessary condition is merely a2>0, which is satisfied in many stable cases and is not sufficient. At a=0 the speeds are 0 and ΛD; the invariant with nonzero denominators cannot be used, but the determinant still correctly gives neutral modes. No claim excluding transient amplification is needed.

Let R=u′v′ be the eddy momentum flux, F=ρ′v′​ the eddy density flux, and S=dρs​/dz<0. The PDF's hydrostatic equation is gρ​=−p​z​; the converted TeX mistakenly replaces the pressure derivative by a density derivative. With N02​=−gS/ρ0​, define
A=F/S,v∗=va​−Az​,w∗=wa​+Ay​.
(38)
The residual mean circulation is nondivergent because vy∗​+wz∗​=vay​+waz​. Substituting in the original mean momentum and density equations gives the transformed Eulerian mean equations
ut​−f0​v∗=−Ry​+f0​Az​=∂y​Fy​+∂z​Fz​,​ρ​t​+Sw∗=0,vy∗​+wz∗​=0,​
(39)
with the unchanged geostrophic and hydrostatic balances. Here the Eliassen–Palm flux components are
Fy​=−R,Fz​=f0​A=N02​f0​v′b′​,b′=−gρ′/ρ0​.
(40)
The eddy buoyancy flux has been absorbed into the residual transport, while both types of wave forcing enter the momentum equation through the Eliassen–Palm flux divergence.
For geostrophic disturbances, the Taylor identity for quasi-geostrophic flux gives
∇⋅F=v′q′​.
(41)
For example, zonal averaging removes ψx′​ψxx′​​ and converts the remaining horizontal and vertical terms in ψx′​q′​ into derivatives of ψx′​ψy′​​ and (f02​/N02​)ψx′​ψz′​​, giving precisely the displayed fluxes.
Locally, for a basic flow with nonzero meridional PV gradient Qy​, the quasi-geostrophic wave pseudomomentum is A=q′2​/(2Qy​). Multiplying the linear perturbation PV equation by q′/Qy​ gives
At​+∇⋅F=Qy​q′D​​,
(42)
where D is a PV source or sink. In a locally uniform conservative Rossby wave packet, F=Acg​. Thus the flux tracks group velocity rather than the generally different phase velocity, with a signed wave activity if Qy​<0.
The non-acceleration theorem for quasi-geostrophic waves states that statistically steady conservative waves, with no wave-activity sources, sinks or independent boundary forcing, produce no balanced mean acceleration. Their flux divergence vanishes. In the present stable, impermeable mean-flow problem the homogeneous residual-circulation equation then has the zero solution, so v∗=w∗=0 and ut​=0. The Eulerian mean flow can still circulate because its eddy-induced correction need not vanish. Dissipation, absorption at a critical layer, time-dependent wave activity or boundary forcing produces a nonzero flux divergence and invalidates the non-acceleration conclusion.

Use the meridional overturning streamfunction convention
va​=−Xz​,wa​=Xy​,v∗=−Xz∗​,w∗=Xy∗​,X∗=X+F/S.​
(43)
Differentiating geostrophic and hydrostatic balance gives the thermal wind relation f0​uz​=(g/ρ0​)ρ​y​. Differentiate it in time and use the mean momentum and density equations. The resulting diagnostic elliptic boundary value problem is
(f02​∂z2​+N02​∂y2​)X=ρ0​g​Fyy​−f0​Ryz​.​
(44)
For the transformed circulation it becomes the Eliassen equation for residual circulation, with signs appropriate to the convention above:
(f02​∂z2​+N02​∂y2​)X∗=f0​∂z​(−Ry​+f0​∂z​(F/S)).​
(45)
At the rigid meridional walls, impermeability requires each streamfunction to be constant along each wall. Set those constants to zero, choosing the solution with no imposed net vertical throughflow. Since the prescribed density flux vanishes at both walls, the same conditions apply to X∗.
There is a genuine data limitation: prescribing F alone does not prescribe R. The term −f0​Ryz​ remains in both circulation equations. To give the usual explicit step-flux circulation, assume vanishing meridional momentum-flux divergence, Ry​=0, with no independently imposed circulation. The formulas below are conditional on that assumption, not a unique consequence of the density flux alone.
Let l=π/L, κ=N0​l/∣f0​∣ and A0​=−1/S>0, using the flux unit in which its lower plateau is −1. The bounded solution has X=sin(ly)χ(z). Its vertical equation is
χ′′−κ2χ=−ρ0​f02​gl2​F(z).
(46)
At vertical infinity require bounded Eulerian flow and vanishing residual flow. Requiring the Eulerian vertical velocity to vanish also at lower infinity would be inconsistent with the continuing lower-region flux divergence. At z=0, continuity of χ and χ′ follows because the forcing has a step but no delta function. The step-flux residual circulation in a stratified channel is therefore
χ(z)={−A0​(1−21​eκz),−21​A0​e−κz,​z<0,z>0,​χ∗(z)={21​A0​eκz,−21​A0​e−κz,​z<0,z>0.​​
(47)
Here X∗=sin(ly)χ∗, because F/S=A0​sin(ly) below zero and vanishes above it. The residual streamfunction decays at both infinities, whereas the Eulerian streamfunction tends to −A0​sin(ly) below and to zero above.
For z=0, the Eulerian velocity is
va​=−2A0​κ​e−κ∣z∣sin(ly),wa​={−A0​l(1−21​eκz)cos(ly),−21​A0​le−κzcos(ly),​z<0,z>0.​
(48)
The flow descends near the y=0 wall and rises near y=L, with a negative meridional return flow concentrated near z=0. Below the dissipation level its vertical flow balances the continuing divergence of eddy density transport. There is no independently added throughflow, but opposing vertical transports at lower infinity are required by this idealized forcing.
Away from the discontinuity the residual meridional velocity equals the Eulerian value, while
w∗={21​A0​leκzcos(ly),−21​A0​le−κzcos(ly),​z<0,z>0.​
(49)
Thus the residual mean circulation forms two oppositely rotating cells, confined within vertical distance O(1/κ) of the termination of the wave flux. Its streamfunction jumps by −A0​sin(ly) at zero. Distributionally this supplies the horizontal return transport
v∗=−2A0​κ​e−κ∣z∣sin(ly)+A0​sin(ly)δ(z).​
(50)
The Dirac delta sheet is a consequence of the discontinuous imposed flux, not an extra boundary condition. Smoothing the flux termination replaces it by a thin finite return current. Streamlines must not be drawn as continuously crossing the jump without that transport.
The physical Eulerian mean flow is continuous. In the transformed momentum equation, the sheet contribution to f0​v∗ cancels the sheet in the flux divergence, leaving the smooth mean acceleration
ut​=−2f0​A0​κ​e−κ∣z∣sin(ly),∫−∞∞​ut​dz=−f0​A0​sin(ly).
(51)
Thus termination of the vertical Eliassen–Palm flux produces a finite column-integrated wave drag, redistributed over depth by the residual mean circulation.
For completeness, the general bounded response can be calculated without setting Ry​ to zero. Expand Ryz​ in wall-compatible sine modes, with coefficients Bn​(z), ln​=nπ/L and κn​=N0​ln​/∣f0​∣. Add to either displayed streamfunction the same correction
XR​(y,z)=∑n≥1​sin(ln​y)2f0​κn​1​∫−∞∞​e−κn​∣z−z′∣Bn​(z′)dz′,
(52)
for decaying momentum-flux forcing. The Green function sign follows from (∂z2​−κn2​)e−κn​∣z−z′∣=−2κn​δ(z−z′).
An explicit counterexample to uniqueness is the additional momentum flux R=ϵ[1−cos(2ly)]e−a∣z∣, with a>0. It vanishes at both walls and leaves the prescribed density flux unchanged. For a=2κ it adds
XR​=f0​(a2−4κ2)2lϵa​sin(2ly)sgn(z)(e−a∣z∣−e−2κ∣z∣)
(53)
to both circulations. This nonzero bounded, wall-impermeable solution verifies that the original mean equations need an additional momentum-flux specification to select a unique sketch.
