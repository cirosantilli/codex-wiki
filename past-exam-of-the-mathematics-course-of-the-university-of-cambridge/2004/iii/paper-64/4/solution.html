For the basic axisymmetric flow, u0​=ΩReϕ​. The steady radial Euler equation for fluid motion gives −Ω2R=−p0,R​/ρ; the other components give p0,z​=p0,ϕ​=0. Thus, in the absence of an additional body force,
p0​(R,z)=pc​+21​ρΩ2R2.​
(36)
The additive constant is undetermined without an imposed reference pressure; the rigid boundary does not prescribe a uniform boundary pressure. Its shape is fixed by the container rather than by a free-surface pressure condition.
Let vR​,vϕ​,vz​ be the small velocity amplitudes and W=p1​/ρ the pressure amplitude. Linearizing the cylindrical Euler equations for an inviscid fluid about uniform rotation, the material derivative is ∂t​+Ω∂ϕ​. The intrinsic frequency for the stated phase convention is therefore
s=σˉ=σ+mΩ.
(37)
The radial and azimuthal curvature terms supply the Coriolis acceleration terms, giving
isvR​−2Ωvϕ​=−WR​,isvϕ​+2ΩvR​=−Rim​W,isvz​=−Wz​.
(38)
For s=0,±2Ω, solving these equations gives
vR​=s2−4Ω2i(sWR​+2mΩW/R)​,vϕ​=−s2−4Ω22ΩWR​+smW/R​,vz​=siWz​​.
(39)
The incompressible flow constraint is
R1​(RvR​)R​+Rim​vϕ​+(vz​)z​=0.
(40)
On substitution, the terms proportional to mΩWR​ cancel. The remaining equation is
s2−4Ω2is​[R1​(RWR​)R​−R2m2W​]+si​Wzz​=0,
(41)
which proves
R1​∂R∂​(RWR​)−R2m2W​+(1−σˉ24Ω2​)Wzz​=0.​
(42)
This is the pressure equation for inertial modes in a rigid rotating sphere. The exceptional frequencies used in the divisions must instead be treated in the original momentum system; they are not needed for the mode below.
A fixed rigid sphere is impermeable, so its normal velocity vanishes:
RvR​+zvz​=0(R2+z2=r02​).
(43)
Using the solved amplitudes and multiplying by s(s2−4Ω2)/i gives
σˉ2RWR​+2mΩσˉW+(σˉ2−4Ω2)zWz​=0on r=r0​.​
(44)
Equivalently divide by σˉ2 for σˉ=0. Smoothness means tangential slip is allowed: there is no no-slip condition on these inviscid normal modes. Nor is there a condition W=0, since the wall supplies the required varying normal pressure.
For an axisymmetric oscillation, m=0 and s=σ. Write β=1−4Ω2/σ2. For W=z(AR2+Bz2+C) the radial and vertical operators are
R1​(RWR​)R​=4Az,Wzz​=6Bz.
(45)
The interior pressure equation therefore requires
2A+3βB=0.
(46)
The boundary equation becomes
z[(2+β)AR2+3βBz2+βC]=0.
(47)
Substituting R2=r02​−z2, this polynomial must vanish for every boundary height. Its constant and z2 coefficients consequently give
(2+β)Ar02​+βC=0,3βB−(2+β)A=0.
(48)
Together with the interior relation the latter condition is (4+β)A=0. A nontrivial nonsingular oscillatory solution needs A=0: if A=0 and β=0, the other equations force B=C=0. Hence β=−4, giving
σ2=54Ω2​,B=6A​,C=−2Ar02​​.​
(49)
The cubic axisymmetric inertial mode of a rigid sphere is explicitly
W=Az(R2+6z2​−2r02​​),σ=±5​2∣Ω∣​.​
(50)
Its cubic pressure is regular at the axis and centre, and its reconstructed velocity is divergence free and tangent to the sphere by the preceding identities. The frequency lies within the inertial wave band ∣σ∣<2∣Ω∣: Coriolis acceleration, rather than compression or a moving surface, supplies the restoring dynamics. The amplitude A is arbitrary in this linear problem.
