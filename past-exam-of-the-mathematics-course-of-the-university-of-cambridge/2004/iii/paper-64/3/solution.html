Let p1​(z) denote the Eulerian perturbation of a fluid variable that is the pressure amplitude, and keep ρ constant. The linearized Euler equations and incompressible flow constraint are
ik(U−c)u+U′w=−ρikp1​​,ik(U−c)w=−ρp1′​​,iku+w′=0.
(25)
The last equation gives u=iw′/k. Substituting it into the first yields
p1​=kiρ​[(c−U)w′+U′w].​
(26)
Differentiating this formula and using the vertical momentum equation produces the Rayleigh equation for inviscid shear flow:
(U−c)(w′′−k2w)−U′′w=0.​
(27)
For a growing normal mode, c is nonreal, so U−c never vanishes on the real z axis. In each open region the base velocity is constant or linear and U′′=0; therefore w′′−k2w=0. Decay away from the layer selects e−kz above and ekz below, while both exponentials are retained inside. Conveniently normalize these as
w(z)=⎩⎨⎧​Ae−k(z−d),Be−k(z−d)+Cek(z+d),Dek(z+d),​z≥d,−d<z<d,z≤−d.​
(28)
These are the spatially localized discrete modes relevant to instability, rather than all possible generalized neutral disturbances at a critical layer in a shear flow.
At each corner U is continuous and U′ jumps. The two sides have the same normal velocity and normal stress: there is no membrane, density jump or singular external force supplying a pressure jump. Thus w and p1​ must be continuous. The pressure formula proves the required continuity of (c−U)w′+U′w. This is pressure matching at a piecewise-linear shear interface; it does not require w′ to be continuous. Equivalently integrating the Rayleigh equation for inviscid shear flow through a corner gives (U−c)[w′]=[U′]w.
Write α=kd, y=c/U0​, and E=e2α. Continuity of normal velocity gives
A=B+EC,D=EB+C.
(29)
At z=d, the outside derivatives are w+′​=−kA, U+′​=0, while the inside derivatives are w−′​=−kB+kEC, U−′​=U0​/d. Hence pressure matching reduces to
B+E(1−2α+2αy)C=0.
(30)
Similarly at z=−d, the outside derivative is kD and the inside derivative is −kEB+kC. Matching gives
E(1−2α−2αy)B+C=0.
(31)
The two equations can be written as
(1−2α+2αye−2α​e−2α1−2α−2αy​)(CB​)=0.
(32)
A nonzero mode therefore requires
(1−2α)2−4α2y2−e−4α=0,U02​c2​=4α2(2α−1)2−e−4α​.​
(33)
This is the dispersion relation for the unbounded piecewise-linear shear layer.
With the phase convention eik(x−ct), positive Imc gives exponential temporal growth. Since the displayed c2 is real, a growing/decaying pair occurs precisely when
∣2α−1∣<e−2α.
(34)
For 0<α≤1/2, e−2α>1−2α by the strict exponential tangent inequality, so every such mode is unstable. For α≥1/2, define f(α)=2α−1−e−2α. Its derivative 2+2e−2α is positive, while f(1/2)<0 and f(1)>0. There is consequently exactly one root in that interval, and
0<kd<αs​ is unstable,2αs​−1=e−2αs​,21​<αs​<1,quadαs​≃0.63923227.​
(35)
At the cutoff the discrete roots merge at c=0; above it the roots are real and have no exponential growth. The physical wavelengths in the unstable band satisfy λx​=2π/k>2πd/αs​≃9.83d.
This is a finite-thickness Kelvin-Helmholtz instability: long disturbances roll up the layer and draw kinetic energy from its shear. The off-diagonal e−2kd terms describe coupling of the two vorticity discontinuities. Short disturbances decay across the layer before strongly coupling its edges and are neutral. Indeed at kd→∞ the two phase speeds approach the velocities ±U0​ of the separated edges. At kd→0, expansion of the numerator gives c2/U02​→−1, recovering the vortex sheet result c=±iU0​; the growth rate then behaves as k∣U0​∣.
