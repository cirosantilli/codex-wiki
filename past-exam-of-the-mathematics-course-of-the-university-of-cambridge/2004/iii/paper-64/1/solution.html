Take u<0 for inward radial velocity, and assume a constant specific-heat ratio γ>1. The isentropic flow has p=Kργ and adiabatic sound speed c2=γKργ−1. Steady spherical mass conservation and the radial Euler equation for fluid motion give
4πr2ρu=−M˙,ρρ′​=−r2​−uu′​,uu′=−c2ρρ′​−r2GM​.
(1)
Eliminating the mass density derivative gives (u−c2/u)u′=2c2/r−GM/r2, or
uu′​=r1​c2−u2GM/r−2c2​.​
(2)
Differentiating c2∝ργ−1 gives the corresponding sound speed equation:
cc′​=−2γ−1​(r2​+uu′​)=−2rγ−1​c2−u2GM/r−2u2​.​
(3)
At a smooth sonic point, us2​=cs2​. A finite derivative requires the numerator of the flow equation to vanish too. Thus
us2​=cs2​=2rs​GM​.​
(4)
This regularity requirement concerns a smooth transonic branch, rather than an arbitrary singular solution reaching unit Mach number.
Integrating the momentum equation using the specific enthalpy c2/(γ−1) yields the Bernoulli equation
2u2​+γ−1c2​−rGM​=γ−1c∞2​​,c∞2​=γKρ∞γ−1​.
(5)
For a finite positive mass accretion rate, its large-radius solution has
u=−4πρ∞​r2M˙​[1+O(r−1)],c2=c∞2​+r(γ−1)GM​+O(r−4),
(6)
ρ=ρ∞​[1+c∞2​rGM​+O(r−2)].
(7)
These expressions satisfy both the flow equations asymptotically and show u→0 and ρ→ρ∞​. In particular u′/u∼−2/r agrees with the first boxed equation. Combining the Bernoulli equation with sonic regularity additionally gives cs2​=2c∞2​/(5−3γ), so a finite positive sonic point occurs in the range identified below.
For the small-radius inward free fall branch, neglecting enthalpy against GM/r in the Bernoulli equation predicts u2∼2GM/r. The continuity equation then fixes ρ∝r−3/2, and consequently c2∝r−3(γ−1)/2. The neglected-to-leading ratio is
u2c2​∝r(5−3γ)/2.
(8)
It tends to zero precisely when
γ<γcrit​=35​.​
(9)
This makes the assumed supersonic flow and leading free fall self-consistent. At equality, the ratio is of constant order rather than vanishing; above it, the proposed asymptotic balance fails. The isothermal limit γ=1 also permits free fall, with logarithmic rather than power-law enthalpy.
For the stellar funnel, write the magnetic scalar potential as ψ=μcosθ/r2, where R=rsinθ and z=rcosθ. Its magnetic dipole field has Br​=−2μcosθ/r3 and Bθ​=−μsinθ/r3, so a magnetic-field-line equation is
rdθdr​=Bθ​Br​​=2cotθ,r=ℓsin2θ.
(10)
The edge of the polar flux tube meets the stellar surface at θ∗​≃a/R∗​. Thus, near the pole, θ2≃θ∗2​r/R∗​, and its transverse radius is R≃rθ. This proves the dipolar flux-tube area law
A(r)≃πr2θ2=πa2(R∗​r​)3.​
(11)
Equivalently magnetic flux conservation gives A∣B∣=constant and ∣B∣∝r−3. The stipulated small cross section keeps the tube close to the polar axis, where its direction is approximately radial; the approximation is not valid all the way to arbitrarily large r.
Outside the star the prescribed field is a gradient, hence ∇×B=0 and j=0. Its Lorentz force density is therefore j×B=0. The magnetic-pressure and magnetic-tension parts cancel in this exact current-free dipole. Field-aligned velocity also has u×B=0, consistent with a steady prescribed field. Now steady mass conservation reads ρuA=constant. With A∝r3, replace the spherical factor 2 by 3:
(u−uc2​)u′=r3c2​−r2GM​.
(12)
The Bernoulli equation is unchanged because the magnetic field supplies no work or force. On a gravity-dominated free fall branch, u2∼2GM/r, but ρ∝r−5/2 and c2∝r−5(γ−1)/2. Hence
u2c2​∝r(7−5γ)/2,γcrit​=57​for the dipolar funnel.​
(13)
The same bound follows from regular transonic accretion in a power-law tube: us2​=cs2​=GM/(3rs​) and the reservoir-matched Bernoulli equation gives cs2​=2c∞2​/(7−5γ). The stellar surface cuts off the mathematical small-r limit; the criterion refers to a funnel that becomes gravity dominated and crosses its sonic point before reaching that surface.
