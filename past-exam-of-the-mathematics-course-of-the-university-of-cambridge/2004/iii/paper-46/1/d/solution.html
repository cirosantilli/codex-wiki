Critical exponents describe leading singular powers near a continuous phase transition. For reduced temperature t=(T−Tc​)/Tc​, common definitions are the spontaneous order parameter M∼(−t)β, magnetic susceptibility χ∼∣t∣−γ, singular heat capacity cs​∼∣t∣−α, correlation length ξ∼∣t∣−ν, and the critical equation of state M∼sgn(h)∣h∣1/δ. At criticality the connected correlation function has decay power D−2+η.
In Landau theory, solve the stationary equation, evaluate its stable minimum, and differentiate with respect to the conjugate variables. For a scalar quartic theory with r=r0​t, r0​>0, the equation of state is h=rM+uM3. Its ordered solution and differentiated response give β=1/2 and γ=1, calculated explicitly in the unheaded solution below. At r=0 the same equation gives δ=3. The minimized zero-field singular potential is zero above the transition and −r2/(4u) below, giving a heat-capacity jump and α=0. Expanding the gradient functional about a stable minimum gives inverse quadratic response κq2+U′′(M), so ξ=κ/U′′(M)​∼∣t∣−1/2 and the Gaussian critical decay has η=0.
These are mean-field critical exponents. Renormalization group methods obtain fluctuation-corrected powers from fixed-point scaling dimensions; they need not equal the Landau values below the upper critical dimension. At a marginal dimension logarithms can supplement the leading powers.
