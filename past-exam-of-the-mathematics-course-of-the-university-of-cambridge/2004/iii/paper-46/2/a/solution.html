Partition the lattice into blocks of side b sites, with b>1, and retain one coarse spin σR′​ per block. A blocking kernel is a nonnegative conditional weight Kb​(σ′,σ) normalized by ∑σ′​Kb​(σ′,σ)=1 for every microscopic configuration. A deterministic majority rule, with fair resolution of ties, is one example.
Define the blocked statistical Hamiltonian and its field-independent constant by
e−βth​[H(u′,σ′)+N′C′]=∑σ​Kb​(σ′,σ)e−βth​[H(u,σ)+NC],N′=N/bD.
(19)
Summing over σ′ proves exact equality of the old and new partition functions. The lattice spacing becomes ba; expressing lengths again in the original units completes the real-space renormalization group step. Blocking generally generates many-body and longer-range interactions, so exactness requires retaining the full operator space and the additive constant. A finite-coupling truncation is a calculational approximation.
