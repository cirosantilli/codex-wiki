Nagao module theorem states that if e∈Z(kG) is a central idempotent, eM=M, and CG​(Q)≤K≤NG​(Q), then BrQ​(e) is central in kK and
M↓K​=BrQ​(e)M⊕(1−BrQ​(e))M.
(9)
Every indecomposable summand of the second term is relatively projective for some subgroup of K not containing Q. In particular, if Q≤K, the error term has no summand with module vertex containing Q. This is the Nagao version of the second main theorem; it identifies the modular block of a module vertex-Q Green correspondent.
Let eB​ be the identity of B, N=NG​(D), and b its Brauer correspondent of a block in kN. Because D◃N, the ideal J(kD)kN is nilpotent: normality allows moving kN past J(kD) and the augmentation ideal of the p-group is nilpotent. Hence b has nonzero image in k(N/D). Choose an indecomposable summand U of bk[N/D]. It is projective over k(N/D) and belongs to modular block b after inflation.
Since k[N/D]=IndDN​k is a permutation module, its summand U has trivial module source and module vertex contained in D. As D acts trivially, all its proper-subgroup traces vanish and its Brauer quotient of a module is U(D)=U=0. The module vertex must also contain D, so it is exactly D. This is inflated quotient projectives have trivial source.
Let M be the Green correspondent of U in G, using the theorem proved in the next solution. It has module vertex D and the same trivial module source. For a normal p-subgroup, each central block idempotent of kN equals its Brauer image: the two central idempotents agree modulo the nilpotent ideal J(kD)kN, because nonfixed conjugation-orbit sums map to zero in k(N/D); central idempotents congruent modulo a nilpotent ideal coincide. The first main theorem therefore gives BrD​(eB​)=BrD​(b)=b.
Let eC​ be the block identity acting on M. Nagao's theorem forces BrD​(eC​)U=U, since U has module vertex D. But bU=U, and for C=B orthogonality would give BrD​(eC​)b=BrD​(eC​eB​)=0. Therefore M belongs to B, has module vertex D and has trivial module source. For D=1, this supplies an indecomposable projective in the defect-zero modular block.
For the representation-type deduction, modules in a block are projective relative to its defect group: the block trace identity acts as a relative trace equal to the module identity, so D. Higman criterion gives split induction from D. If D is cyclic of order d, then kD≅k[t]/(td), whose indecomposable modules are the nilpotent Jordan chains k[t]/(tj), 1≤j≤d. Each indecomposable in B is a summand of the module induction of its module restriction to D, and hence occurs among the summands of these finitely many fixed induced modules. Their finite Krull-Schmidt decompositions give finite representation type.
Conversely, the regular defect-group bimodule inside a block gives
kD∣(kGeB​)↓D×D​.
(10)
Indeed this module restriction is a permutation summand. Its Brauer quotient at ΔD is nonzero, because on the fixed group-basis elements CG​(D) the block projection becomes multiplication by BrD​(eB​)=0. Every transitive D×D orbit in G has stabilizer of order at most ∣D∣. A summand with a nonzero quotient at ΔD must therefore have stabilizer exactly ΔD up to conjugacy, and is the regular kD bimodule. Tensoring this split inclusion on the right with an arbitrary kD-module V yields
V∣ResDG​(eB​IndDG​V).
(11)
If B has only finitely many indecomposable modules, their restrictions contain only finitely many indecomposable summands. The displayed inclusion puts every indecomposable V on that finite list, so kD has finite representation type too.
A noncyclic finite p-group has a quotient Cp​×Cp​, by the Burnside basis theorem for its Frattini quotient. Here is an explicit infinite family over any field, including a finite field. For s≥1, take basis v0​,…,vs​,w1​,…,ws​ and operators x=g−1, y=h−1 defined by
xv0​=0,xvi​=wi​ (1≤i≤s),yvi​=wi+1​ (0≤i<s),yvs​=0,
(12)
and xwi​=ywi​=0. All products of x,y vanish, so 1+x,1+y satisfy the group relations in characteristic p. The radical is W=⟨w1​,…,ws​⟩. An endomorphism preserves W, and commuting with the two shift maps forces its induced maps on V/W and W to be the same scalar. To see this, write the shift matrices as X=(0 Is​) and Y=(Is​ 0): the equations CX=XA, CY=YA force the off-diagonal entries of A,C to vanish and all diagonal entries to agree, by comparing successive rows, including the zero first column of X and zero last column of Y. The remaining linear map sends the top into W and has square zero. Thus the endomorphism ring is a scalar algebra with square-zero radical, hence local; the module is indecomposable. Inflation to D preserves that ring. Its dimensions 2s+1 are unbounded, contradicting finite representation type. Therefore B has finite representation type if and only if D is cyclic, including trivial defect.
