Include temporal dependence eσt and put p=k2+ℓ2. The two Fourier amplitudes obey
(σ+ηp)A=αB,(σ+ηp)B=(ikΩ+αp)A.
(12)
Consequently the dispersion relation and its branches are
(σ+ηp)2=α2p+iαΩk,σ=−ηp±α2p+iαΩk​.​
(13)
For η>0 and ℓ=0, the branch with positive real square root is the potentially unstable one. At marginal stability set σ=iω; comparing real and imaginary parts gives
η2p2−ω2=α2p,2ηpω=αΩk.
(14)
Eliminating ω produces the alpha-squared Omega dynamo threshold
αm2​(k)=4η2(k2+ℓ2)3+Ω2k24η4(k2+ℓ2)4​,ω=2η(k2+ℓ2)αm​Ωk​.​
(15)
Either sign of αm​ is allowed; it changes the wave's propagation sign. Larger ∣α∣ increases the real part of the growing branch through zero.
To minimize the threshold differentiate with respect to x=k2. The sign of this derivative is the sign of
F(x)=4η2(x+ℓ2)3+Ω2(3x−ℓ2).
(16)
Its derivative is strictly positive for x≥0, so there is at most one interior minimum. Its value at zero changes sign at
Ω02​=4η2ℓ4.​
(17)
For Ω2≤Ω02​, F(x)≥0 and the minimum is kc​=0​, with ∣αc​∣=η∣ℓ∣. For stronger shear, the minimizing positive wavenumber is the unique root
4η2(kc2​+ℓ2)3+Ω2(3kc2​−ℓ2)=0,0<kc2​<ℓ2/3.​
(18)
An explicit form follows by setting s=Ω2/(4η2ℓ4)>1 and y=1+kc2​/ℓ2. The cubic becomes y3+3sy−4s=0, with real solution
kc​=∣ℓ∣2s​sinh[31​arsinh(s​2​)]−1​.​
(19)
The negative wavenumber −kc​ has the same threshold. In the strong-shear limit kc​→∣ℓ∣/3​.
The threshold curve is even in k and behaves as η∣k∣ at large ∣k∣. Below the shear threshold it increases monotonically away from its central minimum. Above the threshold, zero is a local maximum flanked by the two nonzero minima. At equality the central minimum has vanishing quadratic curvature but remains a minimum. These two cases are sketched from the derived formula below. If ℓ=0, the zero-wavenumber uniform/gauge mode is degenerate and the positive-wavenumber threshold tends to zero as k→0; the nonzero-ℓ minimum formula must not be used as a nondegenerate onset criterion there.
