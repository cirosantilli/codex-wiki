The unit zero-coupon bond pays one at maturity. Its risk-neutral valuation therefore is
P(t,T)=EQ​[exp(−∫tT​rs​ds)∣rt​=r].​
(53)
No particular rate coefficients were specified, so this expectation is the general answer; evaluating it further requires a chosen one-factor short-rate model.
For the alternative derivation let Dt​=exp(−∫0t​rs​ds) be the bank discount factor. For an integrable discounted payoff, pricing gives
Dt​V(t,rt​)=EQ​[DT​X∣Ft​],
(54)
which is a martingale by the tower property of conditional expectation. For a payoff determined by the Markov rate state, this conditional expectation is represented by the indicated pricing function. A path-dependent claim requires an appropriately enlarged state, as in part (c). Now dDt​=−rt​Dt​dt has finite variation. Applying Itô formula and the product rule under the risk-neutral measure, whose rate drift is b−σθ, yields
d(Dt​V)=Dt​[Vt​+(b−σθ)Vr​+21​σ2Vrr​−rV]dt+Dt​σVr​dWt​.
(55)
Under the smoothness and integrability conditions used above, the martingale has zero finite-variation drift. The bracket therefore vanishes, giving again
Vt​+(b−σθ)Vr​+21​σ2Vrr​−rV=0.​
(56)
For the bond the terminal condition is P(T,T)=1. This argument also shows why stochastic discounting remains inside the conditional expectation.
