Financially the relative risk aversion coefficient is 1−γ. Increasing γ reduces risk aversion, raises the risky fraction, and decreases the bond fraction: its derivative is −(μ−r)/[σ2(1−γ)2]<0. If the risky fraction is below one, some wealth is lent; at one, none is invested in bonds; above one, B∗<0 represents borrowing to leverage the risky position. As γ↑1 the risky fraction becomes unbounded, consistent with the absence of a finite unconstrained optimum for linear utility and positive excess drift. As γ↓0 the limiting fraction is (μ−r)/σ2, the logarithmic-utility allocation obtained by the appropriately rescaled power-utility limit; the literal objective at γ=0 would be constant and would not select a unique strategy.
