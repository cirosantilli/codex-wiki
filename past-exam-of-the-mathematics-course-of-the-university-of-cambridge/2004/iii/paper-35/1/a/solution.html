Let μ be the vector of expected returns and Σ the positive definite covariance matrix of risky returns. For risky investment portfolio weights w satisfying 1Tw=1, the portfolio opportunity set consists of
(σ,m)=(wTΣw​,μTw).
(1)
Short selling permits negative weights. For a fixed expected return, the least possible variance is attained on the left boundary of this set. Its upper branch is the mean-variance efficient frontier: every other attainable point has either a larger standard deviation for the same expected return or a smaller expected return for the same risk. The lower boundary is therefore inefficient.
For example, writing A=1TΣ−11, B=1TΣ−1μ, C=μTΣ−1μ and D=AC−B2, minimization of wTΣw subject to its two linear constraints gives

(2)
To obtain these weights, use Lagrange multipliers: the stationary equation is Σw=λ1+νμ, and the budget and mean constraints determine λ,ν. Positive definiteness makes this the unique constrained minimum. Thus the boundary is a hyperbola in risk/mean coordinates.
Adding the risk-free asset allows borrowing and lending at r. In the usual positive-premium case the market portfolio is the risky tangency portfolio, and combinations of it with the risk-free asset form the upper capital market line

The Sharpe ratio measures excess expected return per unit of standard deviation; its maximum is the market price of risk θ.
