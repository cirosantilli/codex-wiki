Let C be the smooth algebraic curve. A nonzero regular section of a line bundle has an effective zero divisor, so its degree of a line bundle is nonnegative. Degrees add under tensor products and change sign on taking the dual. We use genuine line subbundles, so their quotients in E are locally free. Both bounds can be proved constructively.
For the upper bound, take a rational frame of E∗ over the function field of C. Its two members are rational sections with only finitely many poles. Choose an effective divisor D large enough to bound the pole orders of both rational sections. They become global sections of E∗⊗OC​(D) and define a sheaf map
E⟶OC​(D)⊕OC​(D)
(14)
which is an isomorphism at the generic point. It is therefore injective as a sheaf map: a section in its kernel would vanish generically in a locally free sheaf, which is a torsion-free sheaf. For any line subbundle L⊂E, at least one of its two component maps into OC​(D) is nonzero. Such a map is a nonzero global section of OC​(D)⊗L−1. Its zero divisor on an algebraic curve is effective, so
degL≤degD.​
(15)
This upper degree bound for line subbundles on a smooth curve depends only on a choice made for E, and applies to every one of its line subbundles.
For the lower-bound question we will actually construct subbundles OC​(−mp0​) for every sufficiently large integer m, where p0​ is a fixed point. First prove the globally generated vector bundle property rather than assuming that negative-degree inclusions automatically are subbundles.
Apply the upper-bound argument to the rank-two vector bundle F=E∗⊗KC​, where KC​ is the canonical bundle. Let B be its upper degree bound. Choose m with m−1>B. For any point p∈C, a nonzero global section of F(−mp0​+p) would give a nonzero map
OC​(mp0​−p)⟶F.
(16)
Its image can be saturated to a saturated line subbundle on a smooth curve M⊂F. Locally the local ring of a smooth algebraic curve is a discrete valuation ring: dividing out the common power of a uniformizer makes the image a primitive rank-one summand, and its quotient is free. Thus the saturation is indeed a subbundle. The original map gives a nonzero global section of M⊗OC​(−mp0​+p) and consequently degM≥m−1, contradicting the upper bound. Hence H0(F(−mp0​+p))=0 for every p. By Serre duality,
H1(E(mp0​−p))≅H0(E∗⊗KC​(−mp0​+p))∗=0.
(17)
The exact sequence
0⟶E(mp0​−p)⟶E(mp0​)⟶E(mp0​)∣p​⟶0
(18)
and its long exact sequence in sheaf cohomology show that evaluation H0(E(mp0​))→E(mp0​)∣p​ is onto for every p. This proves the global generation of positive twists on a smooth curve with one bound on m that works at every point.
Write W=H0(E(mp0​)) and N=dimW. The evaluation map W⊗OC​→E(mp0​) is surjective with kernel bundle of rank N−2. Consider the incidence subset
Z={(p,s)∈C×W:s(p)=0}.
(19)
It is the total space of this kernel bundle, so dimZ=1+(N−2)=N−1. Its projection into W is closed because C is proper, and its image has dimension at most N−1<N. Choose s outside this proper closed subset. It vanishes nowhere. In a local trivialization at any point, one component of s is a unit, so it can be completed to a basis. Therefore OC​s​E(mp0​) is a subbundle with a locally free rank-one quotient. Twisting back gives
OC​(−mp0​)⊂E,degOC​(−mp0​)=−m⟶−∞.​
(20)
This proves the unbounded negative degrees of line subbundles. The nowhere-vanishing construction guarantees a subbundle at every point, including the zeros that might otherwise have occurred in a generic meromorphic inclusion. If the curve has several connected components, apply these constructions on each component and add the degrees; the same upper-bound and no-lower-bound conclusions follow.
