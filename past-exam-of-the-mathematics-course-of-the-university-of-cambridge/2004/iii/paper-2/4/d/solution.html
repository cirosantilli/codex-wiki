Past exam of the mathematics course of the University of Cambridge / 2004 / iii / Paper 2 / 4 / d / Solution

OurBigBook.com Words: 528
Replace Fn by a symplectic vector space (V,B) of dimension 2m, and elementary row operations by symplectic transvections
Tv,c​(x)=x+cB(x,v)v.
(24)
The cross terms in B(Tx,Ty) cancel, and B(v,v)=0, so these maps preserve B. Their inverses are Tv,−c​.
They generate Sp(V) by the following symplectic basis elimination. If B(u,v)=0, a single transvection with direction v−u and parameter 1/B(u,v) sends u to v. If B(u,v)=0 and u=v, choose w with both B(u,w) and B(w,v) nonzero: the union of two proper hyperplanes does not exhaust a vector space, even over F2​. Two transvections then suffice. Thus one can make a form-preserving linear map fix the first basis vector e. Next align its partner f, while keeping e fixed. Two partners have difference in e⊥, so the preceding single-transvection construction uses a direction in e⊥ whenever their mutual pairing is nonzero. If it is zero, the intermediate partner f+ae, a=0, has nonzero pairing with both. All these transvections fix e. Once both e,f are fixed, recurse on their nondegenerate symplectic orthogonal complement. This proves generation over every field.
Use the action on the points of projective space, whose kernel is the scalar center Z={λI:λ2=1}. For m=1 this is the SL2​ case. For m≥2 the action is primitive, although it is generally not two-transitive. The stabilizer of a line L has two orbits on other lines: those orthogonal to L and those not orthogonal to L. This follows by extending a hyperbolic pair, or an independent isotropic pair, to a symplectic basis. A block containing L and a second line M contains the entire corresponding orbit of the stabilizer of L. The stabilizer of M then supplies a line of the other type: choose a vector orthogonal to one of L,M and not to the other. Nondegeneracy and independence guarantee such a vector, also in dimension four. Hence the block contains both orbits and is the whole set.
The transvections along L form an abelian normal subgroup of its stabilizer; their conjugates generate the group. Thus the Iwasawa simplicity lemma applies as soon as perfectness is established. For ∣F∣>3, every transvection lies in an embedded SL2​(F) acting on a hyperbolic plane, and 4(c) proves that embedded group perfect.
For the remaining fields, the perfectness of finite symplectic groups has a short direct proof. Over F3​ with m≥2, all parameter-one transvections have the same class t in the abelianization, and 3t=0. In an isotropic plane with basis u,v, the four directions u,v,u+v,u−v give commuting transvections whose product is 1: their rank-one terms add to zero, since
uuT+vvT+(u+v)(u+v)T+(u−v)(u−v)T=0
(25)
over F3​. Thus 4t=0, giving t=0. Parameter-two transvections are inverses, so all generators vanish in the abelianization. Over F2​ with m≥3, use the seven nonzero vectors of an isotropic three-space. Their commuting transvections multiply to 1, since each diagonal coordinate of ∑0=v​vvT occurs four times and each off-diagonal coordinate twice. Their common abelianized class satisfies 2t=7t=0, and again the group is perfect.
Consequently the projective symplectic group is simple, with the necessary exceptions
PSp2m​(F) simple, except (m,F)=(1,F2​),(1,F3​),(2,F2​).​
(26)
The first two are the linear exceptions; the last is Sp4​(2)≅S6​, proved in 6(c). The word “projective” matters: the unquotiented symplectic group may have the nontrivial scalar center {I,−I}.
