Put Φ=Φ(G) and let H be the full preimage of F(G/Φ) in G. The preceding part gives Φ≤F(G), so the normal nilpotent image F(G)/Φ lies in F(G/Φ).
For the reverse inclusion, choose a Sylow subgroup P of H. Its image PΦ/Φ is the unique Sylow subgroup of the nilpotent group H/Φ, hence a characteristic subgroup there and normal in G/Φ. Thus PΦ⊴G. The subgroup P is also a Sylow subgroup of PΦ: the p-part of Φ lies in P, because P∩Φ is a Sylow subgroup of the normal subgroup Φ of H. Apply the Frattini argument to PΦ:
G=(PΦ)NG​(P)=ΦNG​(P).
(8)
The Frattini subgroup property forces NG​(P)=G. Hence every Sylow subgroup of H is normal, so H is a nilpotent group and H≤F(G). Together the inclusions prove
F(G/Φ(G))=F(G)/Φ(G).​
(9)
This is Frattini lifting of nilpotence; simply calling an extension of nilpotent groups nilpotent would not establish it.
