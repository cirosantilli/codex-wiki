We first construct a terminal integrable function without using the martingale convergence theorem. Put G=supn​∣gn​∣ and dη=Gdx on (0,1]d. This is a finite positive measure. If G=0 almost everywhere the conclusion is immediate. Otherwise, for a bounded dyadic step function h measurable with respect to Fn​ in the dyadic filtration, define
Λ(h)=∫hgn​dx.
(14)
The martingale property makes the definition independent of the choice of a sufficiently large n. Moreover ∣Λ(h)∣≤∫∣h∣dη. Dyadic step functions are dense in L1(η): continuous functions on the closed unit cube are dense for this finite Borel measure (extend the measure by zero onto the omitted faces), and uniform continuity approximates each continuous function by step functions on fine dyadic partitions. Thus Λ extends to a bounded linear functional on L1(η) of norm at most one. The duality of Lp spaces representation of this functional gives H∈L∞(η) with ∣H∣≤1 and Λ(h)=∫hHdη. Set g=HG, taking g=0 where G=0. Then g∈L1(dx) and testing indicators of all dyadic atoms proves
gn​=E(g∣Fn​).​
(15)
This argument also works for complex-valued martingales, with a complex linear functional.
To prove convergence, choose a dyadic step function h with ∥g−h∥1​ arbitrarily small. For all n beyond its dyadic level, E(h∣Fn​)=h. The L1 contraction of conditional expectation yields
∥gn​−g∥1​≤∥E(g−h∣Fn​)∥1​+∥g−h∥1​≤2∥g−h∥1​.
(16)
This proves convergence in L1. For convergence almost everywhere, Doob maximal inequality applied to the conditional expectations of g−h gives
​{nlimsup​∣gn​−g∣>2a}​​≤​{nsup​∣E(g−h∣Fn​)∣>a}​+∣{∣g−h∣>a}∣≤a2∥g−h∥1​​.​
(17)
Let the approximation error tend to zero, and then take positive rational a. Hence gn​→g both in L1 and almost everywhere.
For the second assertion, write M=supn​E∣fn​∣<∞ and take the given first-exit stopping time τ. Each gn​=fτ∧n​ is integrable, being a finite sum of restrictions of integrable variables. Its increments satisfy
gn+1​−gn​=1{τ>n}​(fn+1​−fn​).
(18)
The indicator is Fn​-measurable, so taking its conditional expectation proves that (gn​) is a martingale.
The overshoot at τ need not be bounded by N. Instead, for a fixed m, on {τ=j} with j≤m we have ∣fj​∣≤E(∣fm​∣∣Fj​). Integrating over this Fj​-measurable event, summing the disjoint events, and including {τ>m} gives
E∣gm​∣≤E∣fm​∣≤M.
(19)
The variables Zm​=∣fτ​∣1{τ≤m}​ increase to Z, and Zm​≤∣gm​∣. The monotone convergence theorem gives EZ≤M. Before stopping, the process has magnitude less than N; after stopping it remains at fτ​. Consequently
g∗≤Z∨N,E(Z∨N)≤M+N<∞.​
(20)
This is the integrable overshoot of a stopped L1-bounded martingale. The first part now proves that every such stopped process converges almost everywhere.
Finally, Doob maximal inequality gives ∣{supn​∣fn​∣≥N}∣≤M/N, by applying the finite-horizon estimate and then increasing the horizon. Thus supn​∣fn​∣ is finite almost everywhere. Outside the union of the null sets for all positive integer stopping thresholds, choose an integer N larger than this supremum. No stopping occurs at that point, so fn​=gn​ for all n, and fn​ converges to a finite limit almost everywhere. We have not assumed, or concluded, L1 convergence for this general L1-bounded martingale.
