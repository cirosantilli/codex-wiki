First prove the crossing lemma for multigraphs. In an optimal drawing of a loopless multigraph with v vertices, e edges and maximum parallel multiplicity μ, crossings of incident edges can be removed by interchanging initial segments, so each remaining crossing involves four distinct vertices. In each parallel class choose one of its edges with probability 1/μ per edge, selecting none with the remaining probability. Independently keep each vertex with probability p. The resulting simple graph has expected edge count p2e/μ, expected vertex count pv, and expected crossings p4X/μ2, where X is the original crossing count. Now form the circular-arc graph for distinct distances. Around every point draw each positive-distance circle centred there; there are at most nt such circles. A circle with at least three of the original points contributes one edge between each consecutive pair around it. Discard the circles with at most two points. Since the total number of centre-to-point incidences is n(n−1), the remaining loopless multigraph has

Its circular-arc drawing has O(n2t2) crossings: two distinct circles have at most two intersections, and small perturbations remove tangencies and multiple crossings without changing this order.
The apparent difficulty is unbounded parallel multiplicity. The rich-bisector deletion bound controls it. Every centre supporting an arc between fixed endpoints lies on their perpendicular bisector. Distinct parallel arcs come from distinct centres, since their common endpoints determine the radius once the centre is fixed. Thus multiplicity at least k makes that line contain at least k of the points. For a line with s centres, each of its at most st circles has at most two consecutive-point arcs symmetric about that line, one at each end of its diameter along the line.
