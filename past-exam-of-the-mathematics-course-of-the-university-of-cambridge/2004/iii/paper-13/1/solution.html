Let P be the transition matrix of a finite irreducible Markov chain with at least two states, reversible with stationary distribution π. Its Dirichlet form of a Markov chain is
E(f,f)=21​∑x,y​π(x)P(x,y)(f(x)−f(y))2.
(1)
The Discrete Poincare inequality states that Varπ​f≤CE(f,f) for every real f. Its optimal constant is C=(1−λ2​)−1, where λ2​ is the second largest eigenvalue of P. Indeed, detailed balance makes P self-adjoint on L2(π). Expand f−π(f)=∑j≥2​aj​vj​ in an orthonormal eigenbasis. Then Varπ​f=∑aj2​ and E(f,f)=∑(1−λj​)aj2​, proving the inequality and its sharpness.
The useful constructive version is the canonical paths Poincare bound. Choose transition graph paths γxy​ between each ordered pair, write Q(u,v)=π(u)P(u,v), and define
ρ=max(u,v):Q(u,v)>0​Q(u,v)1​∑x,y​π(x)π(y)∣γxy​∣Nuv​(γxy​),
(2)
where Nuv​ counts occurrences of the directed transition. The Cauchy-Schwarz inequality along each path in a graph gives (f(x)−f(y))2≤∣γxy​∣∑γxy​​(f(u)−f(v))2. Multiply by π(x)π(y)/2 and sum. The identity Varπ​f=21​∑x,y​π(x)π(y)(f(x)−f(y))2 proves Varπ​f≤ρE(f,f).
For a lazy Markov chain this also proves the mixing time estimate
∥Pt(x,⋅)−π∥TV​≤2π(x)​1​e−t/ρ.
(3)
To see this, the centred initial density has L2(π) norm at most π(x)−1/2; laziness puts all nonconstant eigenvalues between zero and 1−ρ−1. Their powers contract that norm, and the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality bounds total variation distance by half the density's L2(π) norm.
Here is a precise sufficient density condition for the permanent application: every row and every column has more than n/2 ones. Interpret the permanent of a matrix as the number mn​ of perfect matchings in its bipartite graph, with n vertices per side. Write mk​ for the number of k-edge matchings in a graph.
First prove the short augmenting paths in a dense bipartite graph bound. Given a nonperfect matching in a graph, choose unmatched u,v on opposite sides. If either has an unmatched neighbour, augment with one edge. Otherwise the matched partners of N(u) and the neighbours N(v) are subsets of the same n-element side whose total size exceeds n. Their intersection gives an alternating path in a graph u,b,a,v of length three, with ab a matching edge. Repeating proves mn​>0. Choose augmentations by a fixed deterministic rule. A reverse augmentation is specified by at most four vertices, so a next-size matching in a graph has at most C0​=2n4 preimages. Therefore
mk​≤C0​mk+1​,mn−j​≤C0j​mn​.
(4)
Use the matching generating function Z(λ)=∑k=0n​mk​λk and the weighted matching Markov chain on all matchings in a graph, with stationary weights πλ​(M)=λ∣M∣/Z(λ). It stays put with probability 1/2; otherwise it chooses a graph edge uniformly and proposes its deletion, its addition if both endpoints are unmatched, or its exchange for the unique incident matching edge if precisely one endpoint is matched. Other proposals stay put. Accept with probability min(1,λ∣M′∣−∣M∣). Reverse proposals use the opposite exchanged edge, so detailed balance holds. Deletions connect every state to the empty matching in a graph.
For completeness, the canonical paths for the weighted matching chain give a polynomial bound without assuming a mixing theorem. The symmetric difference I△F consists of alternating graph paths and graph cycles. Order its components deterministically, orient open components from their smaller endpoint, and convert them from I to F by moving an unmatched endpoint with exchanges. If the first edge belongs to I, delete it first. For a graph cycle, delete one I-edge, move the unmatched endpoint around the opened cycle, then insert the last F-edge. Each intermediate state is a matching in a graph, and each entire transition path in a graph has length at most 4n.
Fix a directed transition with initial state M. The complementary edge set K=I△F△M agrees with one of the two matchings in a graph outside the active component and has at most two degree-two vertices on that component. Remove at most two offending edges to obtain a matching in a graph K′. Store those edges and one bit specifying the initial alternating colour of the active component. This encoding is injective for the fixed transition: it restores K and I△F=M△K, identifies the active component from the changed edges, and recovers the two colours using the fixed component order. Completed components have F's colour in M, pending components have I's colour, and the stored bit resolves the active component. Common edges are M∩K.
There are at most 2(m+1)2 auxiliary records per K′, where m is the number of graph edges. If d=∣K∣−∣K′∣≤2, then
πλ​(I)πλ​(F)=λdπλ​(M)πλ​(K′).
(5)
Also Q(M,M′)≥πλ​(M)/(2mmax(λ,λ−1)). Sum over the encodings and use the path length bound to obtain
ρ≤16nm(m+1)2max(1,λ2)max(λ,λ−1).
(6)
This is polynomial whenever λ and λ−1 are polynomially bounded. Starting at the empty matching in a graph, log(1/πλ​(∅))=logZ(λ)≤mlog(1+λ), so the Discrete Poincare inequality gives polynomial-time sampling to any prescribed total variation distance. The addition/deletion/exchange construction is also described in Section 12.4 of Jerrum and Sinclair's survey.
It remains to turn sampling into a relative count; merely sampling perfect matchings would not do that. For 0<ε≤1/2, choose λ0​=ε/(8m) and λ∗​=8C0​/ε. The elementary bounds above give
1≤Z(λ0​)≤(1+λ0​)m≤1+ε/4,1≤mn​λ∗n​Z(λ∗​)​≤∑j=0n​(C0​/λ∗​)j≤1+ε/4.

Choose a schedule λ0​<λ1​<⋯<λJ​=λ∗​ whose successive ratios are at most 1+1/n. It has J=O(nlog(n/ε)) stages. The annealing ratios for a matching generating function satisfy
Z(λj​)Z(λj+1​)​=Eπλj​​​(λj​λj+1​​)∣M∣.
(8)
Each observable lies in [1,e], so the Hoeffding inequality gives relative accuracy η=ε/(10J) with O(η−2log(J/δ)) independent samples per stage. Run independent copies of the chain from the empty matching in a graph; make the total variation distance per sample at most δ/(3JK), where K is that sample count. The coupling characterization of total variation distance and the union bound make the entire sample collection coincide with exact stationary samples except with probability at most δ/3. Choose the sample-count constant so all stationary sample means are accurate except with probability at most δ/3.
Let R be the product of the sample means, and output p​=R/λ∗n​. On the successful event its multiplicative error relative to Z(λ∗​)/Z(λ0​) lies between e−ε/5 and eε/5. Combining the two endpoint bounds proves
P((1−ε)perA≤p​≤(1+ε)perA)≥1−δ.​
(9)
Activities, sample counts, simulation lengths and rational bit lengths are polynomial in n,ε−1,log(δ−1). Thus this is a fully polynomial randomized approximation scheme. Small dimensions can be evaluated directly.
