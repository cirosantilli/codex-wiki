Continue to choose the CDM-comoving synchronous frame. For nonrelativistic baryons, neglect O(cs2​) corrections to gravitational density weights and Hubble friction, but retain cs2​k2, since gradients may compensate its small coefficient. Then
δB′​=−ϑB​−21​h′,ϑB′​+HϑB​−cs2​k2δB​=0.
(33)
Together with the metric tensor equation these give
δC′′​+HδC′​δB′′​+HδB′​+cs2​k2δB​​=23​H2(ΩC​δC​+ΩB​δB​),=23​H2(ΩC​δC​+ΩB​δB​).​​
(34)
The friction terms contain first derivatives, as the original PDF confirms; they are missing in the TeX's transcription of these equations.
When ΩB​≪ΩC​, Cold dark matter sets the gravitational forcing. Its matter-era growing mode is δC​∝a∝τ2. Compare pressure with the gravitational timescale:
cs2​kJ2​=23​H2ΩC​=4πGa2ρˉ​C​.
(35)
Converting the comoving wavenumber to a physical wavelength gives
λJ​=kJ​2πa​≃cs​Gρˉ​C​π​​.​
(36)
For k≪kJ​, pressure is negligible and baryons can follow the growing Cold dark matter mode. In that limit subtracting the two equations gives D′′+2D′/τ=0 for D=δB​−δC​, so D=D0​+D1​/τ. Relative to δC​∝τ2, this offset vanishes: baryons catch up.
The phrase “only grow” needs this gravitational-growth interpretation. For a small baryon fraction the baryon equation is forced, not an autonomous Jeans-instability equation. At k≫kJ​, its slowly varying response is
δB​≃cs2​k24πGa2ρˉ​C​​δC​=k2kJ2​​δC​,
(37)
plus acoustic transients. It is suppressed rather than identically absent. For example, with constant sound speed, H=2/τ and δC​=Aτ2, the equation is δB′′​+2δB′​/τ+cs2​k2δB​=6A. Its particular solution 6A/(cs2​k2) is constant, and its oscillatory homogeneous part decays as 1/τ. This demonstrates Jeans support versus forced baryon response; the scale above is not a theorem forbidding every forced or transient sub-Jeans change.
Before decoupling, tight photon coupling makes the effective sound speed large, about 1/3​. The small-cs​ approximation just derived is not quantitative in that regime; the coupled photon-baryon fluid must be used. Its large pressure supports acoustic oscillations, and baryon clustering below the corresponding large Jeans/sound scale is inhibited while Cold dark matter wells grow. After decoupling the supplied sound speed is tiny and decreases further as the gas cools. The Jeans length drops sharply, so many previously supported modes fall into Cold dark matter wells and approach its growing density contrast. Modes still below the instantaneous pressure-support scale remain suppressed. The subsequent detailed thermal scaling depends on the gas's temperature evolution; the given sound-speed history suffices for this qualitative conclusion.
