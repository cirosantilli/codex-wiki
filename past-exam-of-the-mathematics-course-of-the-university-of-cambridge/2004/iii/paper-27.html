Put X=1+T and let φ be the Frobenius substitution on cyclotomic power series, φ(g)(T)=g(Xp−1). It is injective: modulo p it is the injective map g​(T)↦g​(Tp). If φ(g)=0, this makes every coefficient of g divisible by p; dividing by p and repeating makes every coefficient zero.
We prove that R is a finite free module over S=φ(R) of rank p, with basis 1,X,…,Xp−1. Modulo p, every series has a unique decomposition over Fp​[[Tp]] in the basis 1,T,…,Tp−1. Changing to the powers of X=1+T is an invertible triangular change of basis. Thus, modulo p, every h∈R can be expressed as ∑i=0p−1​Xiφ(hi​). Lift the coefficients to R, subtract this expression, and divide the remainder by p. Repeating and summing the p-adically convergent coefficient series gives
h=∑i=0p−1​Xiφ(hi​),hi​∈R.
(1)
Uniqueness follows by reduction modulo p and successive division by p. This proves finite freeness, not just a formal invariance of the right side in the question.
For f∈R×, multiplication by f is an invertible S-linear map mf​:R→R. Its determinant b=detS​(mf​) belongs to S×. Define
Nf=φ−1(Sdet​(mf​)).​
(2)
This is the norm for a finite free ring extension, pulled back along the injective substitution, and it is multiplicative and unit-valued.
To identify its determinant with the desired product, extend coefficients to O=Zp​[μp​]. The substitutions X↦ξX for ξp=1 are continuous automorphisms of O[[T]] fixing φ(O[[T]]). Evaluation of a series at ξX−1 is legitimate in the (ξ−1,T)-adic topology, because the constant term ξ−1 is topologically nilpotent. On fraction fields this is a degree-p extension with these p distinct automorphisms: finite freeness bounds the degree by p, while the automorphisms force degree at least p. The determinant norm therefore equals the product of its conjugates. Norms commute with this scalar extension, so
φ(Nf)=∏ξp=1​f(ξX−1)
(3)
as required. Finally, any other unit-valued map satisfying this identity has the same image under φ for every f, and injectivity of φ makes it identical to N. The Coleman norm operator exists and is unique.

The shifted cyclotomic polynomial Φpn+1​(1+T) is Eisenstein at p: its constant coefficient is p, and modulo p it is Tpn(p−1). Thus [Kn​:Qp​]=pn(p−1), ζn​−1 is a uniformizer, and [Kn​:Kn−1​]=p for n≥1. The p automorphisms over Kn−1​ send ζn​ to ξζn​ for ξ∈μp​.
Since ζn​−1 has positive valuation, evaluating an integral formal power series there converges. A unit series has unit constant coefficient and evaluates to a unit. Evaluate the Coleman norm operator identity at T=ζn​−1:
NKn​/Kn−1​​(f(ζn​−1))​=ξp=1∏​f(ξζn​−1)=(Nf)(ζnp​−1)=f(ζn−1​−1).​
(4)
The final equality uses norm fixation and the compatible choice of roots. Hence
NKn​/Kn−1​​(un​)=un−1​(n≥1).​
(5)
In particular, norm fixation of a single Coleman power series produces a norm-compatible sequence of local units.

Let u=(un​)∈U∞​. The Coleman interpolation theorem for cyclotomic local units gives a unique unit series fu​∈Zp​[[T]]× with
fu​(ζn​−1)=un​,Nfu​=fu​.
(6)
Since the entries are principal units, fu​(0)≡1(modp). The existence statement can be seen from the norm operator as follows. Choose integral unit polynomials Fj​ interpolating uj​, possible because OKj​​=Zp​[ζj​−1]. The congruence NF≡F(modp) and Coleman norm contraction imply that NjF2j​ differs from N2j−mF2j​ by a factor in 1+pj+1R for each fixed m≤j. The second series evaluates at ζm​−1 to um​ by repeated finite-level norms. Compactness in the (p,T)-adic topology gives a subsequential limit interpolating all the entries. A nonzero difference between two interpolating integral series would have infinitely many zeros in the open unit disk, contradicting the Weierstrass preparation theorem. Applying the norm identity at all levels then proves norm fixation.
Set D=(1+T)d/dT. For k≥1, define the cyclotomic higher logarithmic derivative by
δk​(u)=Dk−1(fu​Dfu​​)​T=0​.​
(7)
The quotient Dfu​/fu​ belongs to Zp​[[T]], and D preserves that ring, so this indeed takes values in Zp​. Equivalently it is Dklogfu​ at zero, where the formal logarithm may be normalized by replacing fu​ by fu​/fu​(0). There is no factorial division in this definition. Uniqueness of the Coleman power series gives fuv​=fu​fv​, so δk​(uv)=δk​(u)+δk​(v).
Let χcyc​:Gal(K∞​/Qp​)→Zp×​ be the cyclotomic character, with σ(ζn​)=ζnχcyc​(σ)​. If b=χcyc​(σ), interpolation and uniqueness give
fσu​(T)=fu​((1+T)b−1).
(8)
The binomial series is integral for b∈Zp​, and for any formal series g the chain rule gives
D(g((1+T)b−1))=b(Dg)((1+T)b−1).
(9)
Iteration, followed by evaluation at zero, therefore proves
δk​(σu)=χcyc​(σ)kδk​(u).​
(10)
Thus these additive Coates–Wiles homomorphisms have cyclotomic weight k.

The Iwasawa main conjecture links two descriptions of cyclotomic arithmetic: Galois modules built from abelian extensions, and p-adic L-functions built by interpolation of special values. A precise statement must distinguish local units, global units and the permitted ramification of the extension.
Use global fields Fn​=Q(ζn​)+, distinct from the local fields Kn​ in the preceding questions. Put F∞​=⋃n​Fn​, G=Gal(F∞​/Q)≅Zp×​/{±1}, and Ω=Zp​[[G]]. Its pro-p subgroup is Γ≅Zp​, and choosing a generator identifies its Iwasawa algebra with Λ=Zp​[[S]]. The prime-to-p finite quotient has order (p−1)/2, so its character idempotents decompose Ω into one-variable power-series rings. Characteristic ideals below are understood componentwise in this decomposition.
Let U be the norm limit of local principal units in the completions of Fn​ at p, E the norm limit of the closures of global principal units there, and C the closed module generated by compatible cyclotomic units. Thus C⊆E⊆U. Let X be the p-ramified Iwasawa module of F∞​, and let Y be its unramified Iwasawa module, equivalently the norm limit of the p-primary ideal class groups. In this real tower the relevant character components are finitely generated torsion modules; the abelian-field Leopoldt theorem for abelian number fields supplies the required rank comparison for X.
For a torsion one-variable Iwasawa module, the Iwasawa module structure theorem gives, up to finite kernel and cokernel, elementary factors Λ/(pai​) and Λ/(qj​(S)bj​) with distinguished irreducible qj​. Its characteristic ideal is generated by p∑ai​∏j​qjbj​​; the generator is determined only up to a unit. This records all height-one elementary divisors, not just the order of a finite class group.
Let I=ker(Ω→Zp​) be the augmentation ideal, and let ζp​ be the even p-adic zeta pseudomeasure, normalized by
∫G​xkdζp​=(1−pk−1)ζ(1−k)(k≥2 even).
(16)
Even powers are well-defined on Zp×​/{±1}. The trivial-character pole means that ζp​ itself need not be integral; Iζp​ is an integral ideal. In this real, p-ramified convention the main conjecture is
charΩ​X=Iζp​.​
(17)
On nontrivial even character components this is an equality with the ideal of a Kubota-Leopoldt p-adic L-function power series. If γ corresponds to u=1+p and S=γ−1, the corresponding interpolation convention is gχ​(u1−s−1)=Lp​(χ,s). Other formulations use odd components of the unramified module of the full complex tower; Kummer reflection in Iwasawa theory introduces the reflected character ωχ−1 and the accompanying Tate twist/involution. These modules and power-series variables cannot be exchanged without those corrections. The main conjecture is a theorem, proved by the Mazur-Wiles theorem and also through the cyclotomic Euler-system method.
The Iwasawa theorem on local cyclotomic units identifies the purely local quotient
U/C≅Ω/(Iζp​).​
(18)
Its mechanism is visible in Q2. The Coleman unit-measure exact sequence, on its even part, has no roots-of-unity kernel or first-moment cokernel, since complex conjugation acts by −1 on those terms. It identifies U with the measure algebra Ω. The measure comes from the Frobenius-corrected logarithm
Lf​(T)=p1​logf((1+T)p−1)f(T)p​,
(19)
whose kth moment is (1−pk−1)δk​(f). This follows by applying Dk and the chain rule to the two logarithms. For the units in Q2 and even k, use ζ(1−k)=−Bk​/k to obtain
(1−pk−1)δk​(c(a))=(p−1)2k(1−ak)(1−pk−1)ζ(1−k).
(20)
Uniqueness of moments therefore identifies their measures with (p−1)[2](1−[a])ζp​. The factors (p−1)[2] are units in Ω, and the differences 1−[a] generate I. Thus the closed cyclotomic-unit module maps exactly to Iζp​. This local interpolation calculation explains Iwasawa's quotient theorem, including the augmentation correction at the trivial character.
What remains globally is made explicit by class field theory. The class-field unit sequence gives
0⟶E/C⟶U/C⟶X⟶Y⟶0.
(21)
Break this into two short exact sequences. Multiplicativity of characteristic ideals yields
char(E/C)char(X)=char(U/C)char(Y).
(22)
Consequently, using the local theorem, the main conjecture is equivalent to
charΩ​Y=charΩ​(E/C).​
(23)
This is the characteristic-ideal bridge from local units to the main conjecture. It shows why the local quotient theorem alone is insufficient: it does not measure how many local cyclotomic-unit classes are supplied by global units. The Euler-system divisibility for real cyclotomic class modules supplies one containment, while the cyclotomic unit-index and norm-defect comparisons upgrade it to equality. Neither vanishing of class groups nor the Vandiver conjecture is assumed.
There is also the Iwasawa class-number growth theorem, often called Iwasawa's theorem in discussions of these towers. For sufficiently large n,
vp​(hFn​​)=μpn+λn+ν.
(24)
The Iwasawa invariants μ,λ come from the characteristic series of Y; Ferrero-Washington theorem gives μ=0 in this abelian cyclotomic setting. This explains eventual linear growth of the p-adic class-number exponent. It is weaker than the main conjecture: the latter determines the complete characteristic ideal, including distinguished polynomial factors, rather than only their total degree and p-power content. The local unit theorem and the class-number growth theorem are thus important inputs and consequences in the framework, but neither should be mistaken for the global ideal equality itself.

A cyclotomic Euler system must relate units at many auxiliary conductors, not just the successive layers of one p-power tower. A useful axiomatization is the root-of-unity Euler system. Fix a finite set of excluded primes S containing 2, and let WS​ be the roots of unity whose orders are prime to every prime in S. A system is a nonzero-valued function Φ:WS​→Q​× with three properties. It is even and Galois equivariant:
Φ(ξ−1)=Φ(ξ),Φ(ξσ)=Φ(ξ)σ.
(25)
It satisfies the distribution relation at every prime ℓ∈/S:
∏ρ∈μℓ​​Φ(ρξ)=Φ(ξℓ).
(26)
Finally, if ℓ∤ord(ξ), it satisfies the local congruence
Φ(ρξ)≡Φ(ξ)(modl)(ρ∈μℓ​, l∣ℓ).
(27)
The values in this congruence are required to be integral at the prime; in the unit example below they are local units. Evenness puts the values in the maximal real cyclotomic subfields. Equivariance identifies conjugates, distribution gives exact norm identities, and the congruence supplies information at newly introduced primes. These are the three distinct inputs used in the descent, rather than a single norm-coherence assumption.
Here is a concrete system extending the units in Q2. Fix a nonzero integer a prime to p and let S contain 2 and the prime divisors of a, but not p. Put
Φa​(ξ)=(ξ−1−ξξ−a−ξa​)p−1(ξ=1),Φa​(1)=ap−1.
(28)
Every value is defined and nonzero: the excluded primes make a invertible modulo the order of ξ, and that order is odd. The quotient is the same Laurent polynomial ba​ used in Q2. Replacing ξ by ξ−1 changes both numerator and denominator by a minus sign, so it is even; its rational coefficients give Galois equivariance.
For the distribution axiom, ℓ∈/S makes ℓ odd and prime to a. Both exponents 2 and 2a permute μℓ​, and ∏ρ∈μℓ​​ρ=1. The calculation in Q2 therefore holds with p replaced by ℓ:
∏ρ∈μℓ​​ba​(ρX)=ba​(Xℓ).
(29)
Raising to p−1 proves the axiom. At removable points it holds by evaluating the regular Laurent polynomials, so it includes ξ=1 and ξℓ=1.
For the congruence axiom, a root ρ of order ℓ reduces to one at every prime above ℓ. The integral Laurent polynomial ba​ thus gives ba​(ρξ)≡ba​(ξ). These values are local units when ξ has order prime to ℓ: its reduction retains that order, and, unless ξ=1, both 1−ξ2 and 1−ξ2a remain nonzero; at ξ=1 the value is the unit a. The stated power preserves the congruence. Thus Φa​ satisfies all three Euler-system axioms, and its values at ζn​ are precisely cn​(a). Products, Galois translates, suitable field norms and integral group-ring powers produce the other cyclotomic-unit systems needed in the theory.
The distribution relation explains the Euler factors. If ℓ∤m, ξ has order m and ρ is a primitive ℓth root, the conjugates over Q(μm​) run through the nontrivial ℓth roots. Removing the single factor with ρ=1 from the distribution product gives
NQ(μmℓ​)/Q(μm​)​Φ(ρξ)=Φ(ξ)Φ(ξℓ)​=Φ(ξ)Frℓ​−1.
(30)
We use arithmetic Frobenius, so Frℓ​(ξ)=ξℓ. This is the cyclotomic Euler norm factor at a new prime. At a higher power of a prime already dividing the conductor, all ℓ translates occur, and the ordinary successive-layer norm relation has no missing-factor quotient. A mere p-power compatible sequence therefore contains only part of the Euler-system information.
To explain the axiomatic method, fix a p-power modulus P=pr and auxiliary primes ℓ with P∣ℓ−1, split in the chosen real base field. For the cyclic auxiliary Galois group of order ℓ−1, choose a generator σℓ​ and define
Dℓ​=∑i=1ℓ−2​iσℓi​,Nℓ​=∑i=0ℓ−2​σℓi​.
(31)
Subtracting consecutive coefficients gives the concrete identity
(σℓ​−1)Dℓ​=(ℓ−1)−Nℓ​≡−Nℓ​(modP).​
(32)
This is the Kolyvagin derivative operator for a cyclic group. Apply products of these operators to Euler-system values at squarefree auxiliary conductors. The norm relations control the apparent failure of invariance modulo P; Kummer descent, using Hilbert 90 and retaining roots-of-unity corrections where present, turns the derivative data into classes over the base field. They have controlled valuations and are unramified away from the selected auxiliary primes and the excluded set. The local congruence relates the valuation component at a newly added prime to the finite residue-symbol component of the preceding class, with the sign and normalization determined by the chosen generator. This finite-to-singular relation is precisely the additional information not obtainable from norm compatibility alone.
The Chebotarev density theorem supplies auxiliary prime ideals in specified ideal classes while satisfying the splitting and congruence conditions. Reading the controlled valuations as principal-ideal relations successively bounds the p-primary elementary divisors of the ideal class group by the divisibility of the initial cyclotomic-unit class. On suitable nontrivial character components this gives the finite bound #Aχ≤#(E/C)χ; passing through the norm tower yields
char(Y)∣char(E/C),​
(33)
where divisibility is between characteristic generators, so the associated ideal containment is reversed. The Euler-system divisibility for real cyclotomic class modules requires the usual local conditions and control of finite errors; it is not a claim that arbitrary compatible units automatically annihilate every class group. The cyclotomic unit index formula, together with the global norm-defect comparison, gives the reverse equality. Q3's exact sequence then proves the main conjecture.
This account separates the theorem inputs from the explicit calculations: the distribution and congruence identities for cyclotomic units are verified above, and the derivative operator is computed directly; the Chebotarev/Kummer descent bounds and the global index comparison are the deeper arithmetic steps. Their usefulness is that the descent uses the axioms rather than a special closed formula for each individual auxiliary unit.
