Write T=t0​, and specify the physical Black-Scholes model as dSt​/St​=μdt+σdWt​, with constant σ>0 and bank rate ρ. The physical drift μ is needed for an expected utility calculation; a pricing model specified only under a risk-neutral measure does not determine the investor's preferences over risky positions. Put
ϑ=σμ−ρ​,Zt​=e−ϑWt​−ϑ2t/2,ξt​=e−ρtZt​.
(31)
Here ϑ is the market price of risk, ZT​ is the density of the risk-neutral measure Q, and ξ is the state-price density. The Girsanov theorem makes WtQ​=Wt​+ϑt a Brownian motion under Q.
For a dollar holding πt​ in the stock and consumption rate ct​, the portfolio wealth equation is
dVt​={ρVt​+(μ−ρ)πt​−ct​}dt+σπt​dWt​,V0​=w0​.
(32)
Since dξt​=−ρξt​dt−ϑξt​dWt​, integration by parts gives the useful deflated wealth equation with consumption
d(ξt​Vt​)+ξt​ct​dt=ξt​(σπt​−ϑVt​)dWt​.
(33)
For strategies with a true deflated gain martingale, terminal wealth X=VT​ and consumption obey the state-price budget constraint
EP​[ξT​X+∫0T​ξt​ct​dt]=w0​.
(34)
For nonnegative wealth and consumption, the admissibility condition can instead give a budget inequality; increasing utilities use the full budget at an optimum. Conversely, suitable integrable payoff and consumption plans with this budget are financeable in the Brownian complete market. To construct the strategy, set
Mt​=EP​[ξT​X+∫0T​ξs​cs​ds​Ft​],Vt​=ξt−1​(Mt​−∫0t​ξs​cs​ds).
(35)
By the Brownian martingale representation theorem, write dMt​=Ht​dWt​. The required dollar stock holding is πt​=(Ht​/ξt​+ϑVt​)/σ; substitution in the deflated gain equation verifies financing. Remaining wealth goes into the bank account. For square-integrable data the representation holds directly, and localization handles the customary integrable admissible data.
First take no consumption. For an increasing strictly concave differentiable utility function whose marginal derivative can be inverted, let Iu​=(u′)−1. Choose λ>0 so that
X∗=Iu​(λξT​),EP​[ξT​X∗]=w0​.​
(36)
This candidate is not merely a first-order guess. The concave supporting-tangent inequality gives, pointwise,
u(X)≤u(X∗)+u′(X∗)(X−X∗)=u(X∗)+λξT​(X−X∗).
(37)
Taking expectations and using the budget proves Eu(X)≤Eu(X∗) for every feasible competitor. Finance the optimizer by its risk-neutral pricing value Vt​=e−ρ(T−t)EQ​[X∗∣Ft​], using the preceding representation to find the holdings.
For a merely concave differentiable utility function, the general formulation is to select X∗(ω) from the maximizers of u(x)−λξT​(ω)x over its wealth domain, and choose the multiplier to satisfy the budget. If increasing utility is not assumed, the equality-budget multiplier may have either sign; the positive-multiplier inverse formula above is for increasing utility. Flat portions allow multiple maximizers; boundaries require one-sided inequalities rather than equality of marginal derivatives. Existence of a multiplier, integrability and an admissible maximizer must be checked. Concavity alone does not guarantee a well-posed problem: with unrestricted dollar positions, linear utility u(x)=x and μ>ρ permits unbounded expected terminal wealth. The explicit utilities below satisfy the needed conditions in their stated domains.
For exponential utility u(x)=(1−e−ax)/a on R, u′(x)=e−ax and Iu​(y)=−logy/a. Therefore
X∗=−a1​log(λξT​)=−alogλ​+aρT​+aϑWT​​+2aϑ2T​.
(38)
The budget is equivalently EQ​X∗=w0​eρT, since ξT​=e−ρTdQ/dP. Under Q, EQ​WT​=−ϑT, giving logλ=ρT−ϑ2T/2−aw0​eρT. Hence
X∗=w0​eρT+aϑ​WTQ​,Vt​=e−ρ(T−t)(w0​eρT+aϑ​WtQ​).
(39)
Matching the Brownian coefficient of dV with σπt​ gives the finite-horizon exponential-utility portfolio
πt∗​=aσ2μ−ρ​e−ρ(T−t).​
(40)
Hold πt∗​/St​ shares and invest Vt​−πt∗​ in the bank account. This is a dollar amount, not a fixed wealth fraction. The Gaussian exponential formula also gives the optimal expected utility
EP​u(X∗)=a1−exp(−aw0​eρT−ϑ2T/2)​.​
(41)
This calculation uses unrestricted wealth and an admissibility class permitting the displayed Gaussian terminal wealth, with integrable deflated gains and the relevant exponential moment. A nonnegative-wealth constraint would change the optimizer; no such constraint is imposed on this exponential-utility example.
For both terminal and running utility function, choose X and a nonnegative adapted consumption rate c to maximize EP​[u(X)+∫0T​v(ct​)dt] subject to the combined budget. Pointwise maximization of u(x)−λξT​x and v(c)−λξt​c gives, for interior strictly concave cases,
X∗=Iu​(λξT​),ct∗​=Iv​(λξt​),EP​[ξT​X∗+∫0T​ξt​ct∗​dt]=w0​.
(42)
Apply the concave supporting-tangent inequality to u and to v at every time, then integrate and take expectations. The difference in objectives is at most λ times the difference in budget expenditures, which is nonpositive. This proves optimality, not just the marginal conditions. The financing value is
Vt​=ξt−1​EP​[ξT​X∗+∫tT​ξs​cs∗​ds​Ft​].
(43)
The question's consumption rate Rt​ is denoted ct​ here to keep it distinct from interest-rate notation.
For logarithmic utility with u(x)=alogx, v(x)=blogx, assume a,b,w0​>0 and positive wealth and consumption. The marginal conditions and budget give
λ=w0​a+bT​,X∗=λξT​a​,ct∗​=λξt​b​.
(44)
Each state-price-weighted payment is deterministic, so conditional valuation immediately gives Vt​=[a+b(T−t)]/(λξt​). Put At​=a+b(T−t). Since the Itô formula gives d(1/ξt​)=(1/ξt​)[(ρ+ϑ2)dt+ϑdWt​], this value satisfies
dVt​=Vt​(ρ+ϑ2−At​b​)dt+ϑVt​dWt​.
(45)
Comparison with the wealth equation verifies the complete finite-horizon logarithmic investment and consumption strategy:
Vt​=λξt​At​​,Rt∗​=ct∗​=At​bVt​​,πt∗​=σ2μ−ρ​Vt​.​
(46)
Thus the optimal share holding is πt∗​/St​, and the remaining value Vt​−πt∗​ is in the bank account; borrowing or shorting is allowed if that value or the stock holding is negative. Terminal wealth is positive and consumption is positive throughout. Finally Elogξt​=−(ρ+ϑ2/2)t, so the optimal objective, if desired explicitly, is
aloga+bTaw0​​+bTloga+bTbw0​​+(ρ+2ϑ2​)(aT+2bT2​).​
(47)
Finite-horizon lognormal moments ensure the budget and expected utilities are finite, completing the feasibility and optimality checks.
