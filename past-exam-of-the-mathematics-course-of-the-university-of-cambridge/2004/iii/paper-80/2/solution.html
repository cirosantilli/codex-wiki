Write the affine variety as X=SpecA. Its coordinate ring is Noetherian, and a coherent sheaf is F=M for a finitely generated A-module M. We first justify the acyclic resolution used to compute its sheaf cohomology.
If I is an injective module, then I is a flasque sheaf. Indeed every open U in this Noetherian scheme has the form X∖V(a) for a finitely generated ideal. Clearing denominators gives
Γ(U,I)=lim​n​HomA​(an,I).
(1)
Here a homomorphism h:an→I gives, on a principal open D(f) with f∈a, the fraction h(fn)/fn; the fractions agree on overlaps. Conversely choose fractions for a section on a finite principal-open cover. A common power clears their denominators and the finitely many compatibility relations. Multiplication by a further power of a kills any elements supported on V(a), producing a well-defined map on some an. Changing that power accounts for the direct limit. This is the sheafification of an injective module is flasque construction. Since I is injective, each map an→I extends to A→I. Thus every section on every open extends to X. Extending globally and restricting again also proves surjectivity for restrictions between arbitrary opens.
Choose an injective module resolution 0→M→I0→I1→⋯. Localization is exact, so sheafifying gives a flasque resolution of F. Its global-section complex is exactly I∙, since Γ(SpecA,I)=I. This complex is exact in positive degrees, and a flasque resolution computes sheaf cohomology. Consequently
Hi(X,F)=0(i≥1).​
(2)
The argument actually proves vanishing of quasi-coherent cohomology on an affine scheme, without finite generation of M.
For the intersection assertion, varieties are taken to be separated, as usual. The diagonal morphism X→X×k​X is a closed immersion. Its inverse image over U×k​V identifies U∩V with a closed subvariety of the affine variety U×k​V. A closed subvariety of an affine variety is affine: its coordinate ring is the ambient coordinate ring modulo its defining ideal. Thus U∩V is affine. Repeated application gives affine finite intersections. Without separatedness this statement need not hold.
Choose a finite affine open cover (Uα​) of the projective variety X. For ordered indices write Uα0​⋯αp​​=Uα0​​∩⋯∩Uαp​​. The answer is the cohomology of the Čech cochain complex
Cp=∏α0​<⋯<αp​​Γ(Uα0​⋯αp​​,F),(dc)α0​⋯αp+1​​=∑j=0p+1​(−1)jcα0​⋯αj​​⋯αp+1​​∣Uα0​⋯αp+1​​​.
(3)
In particular H0=ker(C0→C1) and, in positive degree,
Hi(X,F)≅ker(d:Ci→Ci+1)/im(d:Ci−1→Ci).​
(4)
The chart modules Γ(Uα​,F), together with their restriction and gluing maps, supply the modules on overlaps by localization. The gluing maps are essential; abstract chart modules alone do not specify a sheaf.
For completeness, take a flasque resolution F→I∙ and its double complex of Čech cochain complexes on this cover. In the resolution direction, its cohomology is Hq(Uα0​⋯αp​​,F), which vanishes for q>0 by the affine result. In the cover direction, the augmented Čech cochain complex of a flasque sheaf is exact: sections can be extended from overlaps, successively adjusting a cocycle to eliminate one cover member at a time. Equivalently the augmented sheaf complex is stalkwise contracted by inserting a cover index containing the point. Thus computing the total cohomology in the two directions identifies Čech cohomology with sheaf cohomology, proving the displayed description.
