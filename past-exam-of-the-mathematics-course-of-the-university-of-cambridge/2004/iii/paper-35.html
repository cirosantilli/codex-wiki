Let μ be the vector of expected returns and Σ the positive definite covariance matrix of risky returns. For risky investment portfolio weights w satisfying 1Tw=1, the portfolio opportunity set consists of
(σ,m)=(wTΣw​,μTw).
(1)
Short selling permits negative weights. For a fixed expected return, the least possible variance is attained on the left boundary of this set. Its upper branch is the mean-variance efficient frontier: every other attainable point has either a larger standard deviation for the same expected return or a smaller expected return for the same risk. The lower boundary is therefore inefficient.
For example, writing A=1TΣ−11, B=1TΣ−1μ, C=μTΣ−1μ and D=AC−B2, minimization of wTΣw subject to its two linear constraints gives
w=Σ−1(DC−Bm​1+DAm−B​μ),σmin2​(m)=DAm2−2Bm+C​=A1​+DA​(m−AB​)2.
(2)
To obtain these weights, use Lagrange multipliers: the stationary equation is Σw=λ1+νμ, and the budget and mean constraints determine λ,ν. Positive definiteness makes this the unique constrained minimum. Thus the boundary is a hyperbola in risk/mean coordinates.
Adding the risk-free asset allows borrowing and lending at r. In the usual positive-premium case the market portfolio is the risky tangency portfolio, and combinations of it with the risk-free asset form the upper capital market line
m=r+θσ,θ=σM​mM​−r​.​
(3)
The Sharpe ratio measures excess expected return per unit of standard deviation; its maximum is the market price of risk θ. Points between the risk-free point and the tangency point lend some wealth; points beyond it borrow to leverage the market portfolio. The first panel illustrates the actual data here. The second anticipates the change in part (g), where the normalization of the tangency direction reverses its orientation.

Constant relative risk aversion coefficient means that α=−WU′′(W)/U′(W) does not depend on wealth, for W>0 and U′(W)>0. The defining differential equation integrates as
dWd​logU′(W)=−Wα​,U′(W)=cW−α,c>0.

The power-wealth investment with running utility objective is homogeneous of degree γ in wealth: scaling both wealth and positions by a>0 scales the reward by aγ. Accordingly set J(w,t)=A(t)wγ. Then
Jww​Jw​​=γ−1w​,wB∗​=1−(1−γ)σ2μ−r​,wπ∗​=(1−γ)σ2μ−r​.​
(26)
For completeness the remaining equation is A′+κA+e−ρt=0, A(T)=1, where
κ=γr+2(1−γ)σ2γ(μ−r)2​,A(t)=eκ(T−t)+∫tT​eκ(s−t)−ρsds>0.
(27)
This confirms Jww​<0. The constant optimal risky fraction also makes wealth a positive geometric Brownian motion when started positive.
Financially the relative risk aversion coefficient is 1−γ. Increasing γ reduces risk aversion, raises the risky fraction, and decreases the bond fraction: its derivative is −(μ−r)/[σ2(1−γ)2]<0. If the risky fraction is below one, some wealth is lent; at one, none is invested in bonds; above one, B∗<0 represents borrowing to leverage the risky position. As γ↑1 the risky fraction becomes unbounded, consistent with the absence of a finite unconstrained optimum for linear utility and positive excess drift. As γ↓0 the limiting fraction is (μ−r)/σ2, the logarithmic-utility allocation obtained by the appropriately rescaled power-utility limit; the literal objective at γ=0 would be constant and would not select a unique strategy.

A previsible strategy selects (ϕn​,ψn​) using only Fn−1​, before the price changes from time n−1 to time n occur. Thus these holdings cannot exploit future information.
The self-financing portfolio condition values both the old and newly rebalanced holdings at the same time-n prices:
ϕn​Sn​+ψn​Bn​=ϕn+1​Sn​+ψn+1​Bn​.
(31)
Their equality says that rebalancing uses only the proceeds of selling existing assets: there is no external deposit or withdrawal. In particular, the changes in holdings have zero net cost at the current prices. The holdings for the following period may use the newly available information in Fn​.

At time n−1, the self-financing portfolio can be valued using the holdings about to be carried over the next period:
Vn−1​=ϕn​Sn−1​+ψn​Bn−1​.
(32)
Subtracting this from Vn​=ϕn​Sn​+ψn​Bn​ gives the gains identity
Vn​−Vn−1​=ϕn​(Sn​−Sn−1​)+ψn​(Bn​−Bn−1​).​
(33)

The usual replication argument needs two hypotheses implicit in the pricing conclusion: the strategy must replicate the payoff, VN​=X, and its discounted gains must be integrable. Bounded predictable risky holdings suffice for the latter. Under these conditions, the equivalent martingale measure Q and part (c) give
EQ​[Vn​−Vn−1​∣Fn−1​]=ϕn​EQ​[Sn​−Sn−1​∣Fn−1​]=0.
(36)
Thus discounted wealth is a martingale, and repeated conditioning gives
Vn​=Bn​EQ​[X/BN​∣Fn​].​
(37)
For a normalized initial bank account, B0​=1, deterministic terminal bank value, and trivial initial information, this reduces to the intended risk-neutral pricing formula
V0​=BN−1​EQ​[X].​
(38)
If BN​ is random it must remain inside the expectation. If a claim is not replicable, the existence of an equivalent martingale measure alone does not make its arbitrage-free price unique.
The discounted wealth martingale integrability condition cannot simply be dropped. For a concrete counterexample, set Bn​=1, S0​=3/2, S1​=1+U, and S2​=1+U+ε/2, where U is uniform on (0,1) and ε an independent fair sign. These positive bounded prices form a martingale in the natural filtration. Start with zero holdings; at time one buy 1/U shares financed by borrowing S1​/U. This is previsible and self-financing, but its terminal wealth is ε/(2U), whose absolute expectation is infinite. It is therefore not a martingale. The stated pricing proof applies to the standard integrable replication class, rather than all unconstrained previsible strategies.

Three useful methods are antithetic variates, a control variate, and stratified sampling. Their purpose is to reduce sampling variance at comparable computational cost, rather than merely to take more samples.
For antithetic variates, generate independent pairs (U,1−U) and average both payoffs. Since the call integrand is increasing, the two values have nonpositive covariance. Indeed, for an independent copy U′, twice that covariance is the expectation of [g(U)−g(U′)][g(1−U)−g(1−U′)], which is nonpositive pointwise. With N=2m evaluations, the paired average has variance
NVar(g(U))+Cov(g(U),g(1−U))​≤NVar(g(U))​.
(45)
For a control variate, use the same simulated discounted stock value Y=e−rτST​, whose known mean is S. Replace the discounted call payoff H by H−β(Y−S), preserving its mean. Minimizing its quadratic variance gives
β∗=Var(Y)Cov(H,Y)​,Var(H−β∗(Y−S))=Var(H)(1−Corr(H,Y)2).
(46)
A predetermined coefficient or an independent pilot estimate preserves exact unbiasedness; fitting the coefficient from the same observations requires care.
For stratified sampling, divide [0,1] into q equal intervals, sample independently and uniformly within each, and average their q payoffs. If vj​ is the conditional variance in interval j, this unbiased estimator has variance q−2∑j​vj​. The law of total variance gives Var(H)≥q−1∑j​vj​, so this is at most the variance Var(H)/q of q ordinary independent samples. Stratification removes randomness in how many samples fall in each region, which is particularly valuable when conditional payoff means vary strongly.

The common short-rate market price of risk follows from absence of arbitrage, with the securities driven by the same Brownian motion. Take two traded derivatives V1​,V2​ with drifts m1​,m2​ and diffusion coefficients s1​,s2​. At a given state hold s2​ units of the first and −s1​ of the second, and place −s2​V1​+s1​V2​ in the bank account. The portfolio has zero current value and zero diffusion exposure. Its instantaneous excess drift is
s2​(m1​−rV1​)−s1​(m2​−rV2​).
(49)
If this were nonzero, selecting its positive orientation and maintaining the local risk-cancelling self-financing hedge would generate a gain without risk from zero wealth. Under the usual trading and regularity assumptions this violates absence of arbitrage. Thus the expression vanishes, and at nonzero exposures
s1​m1​−rV1​​=s2​m2​−rV2​​=θ(t,r).​
(50)
At zero exposure the correct relation is m−rV=θs=0, without dividing by zero. This is a condition on traded securities: the physical drift of the nontraded short rate alone does not determine θ.
