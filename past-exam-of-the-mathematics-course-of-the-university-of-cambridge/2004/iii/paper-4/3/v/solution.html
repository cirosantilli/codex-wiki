The printed converse is false without an additional condition. For any odd p, take the Heisenberg group G=UT3​(Fp​) with
x=I+E12​,y=I+E23​,z=I+E13​=[x,y].
(18)
Every element is uniquely xaybzc, with a,b,c∈Fp​, so G is a product of three cyclic subgroups. For A strictly upper triangular, A3=0 and
(I+A)p=I+pA+(2p​)A2=I
(19)
in characteristic p, including p=3. Therefore Gp=1, whereas [G,G]=⟨z⟩=1. This finite p-group is not powerful, disproving the literal request in the authoritative PDF.
A precise nearby correction is the minimal cyclic factorization criterion for powerful p-groups: require a product of d(G) cyclic subgroups, where d(G) is the minimal generator number. To prove it, put d=d(G) and H=G/Gp. The quotient has exponent p and still has minimal generator number d, because Gp⊆Φ(G). Each cyclic factor has image of order at most p, so ∣H∣≤pd. But ∣H/Φ(H)∣=pd by the Burnside basis theorem. Consequently Φ(H)=1, H is an elementary abelian group, and [G,G]⊆Gp. A product of exactly d(G) cyclic subgroups is powerful. A direct product of cyclic groups is also powerful, since it is abelian; neither stronger hypothesis appears in the printed converse.
