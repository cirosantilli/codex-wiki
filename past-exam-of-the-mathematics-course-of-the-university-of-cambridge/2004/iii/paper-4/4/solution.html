Put U0​=G and Uj+1​=Ujp​, where powers mean closed generated subgroups. The finite-quotient arguments of Question 3 pass to a powerful pro-p group: in particular Uj​ is powerfully embedded, the power map is onto Uj+1​, and
[Uj​,G]⊆Uj+1​,(xu)p≡xpup(modUj+2​)(u∈Uj​).
(20)
For the root assertion, apply the finite result to every finite quotient and use the finite intersection property on the closed sets of possible roots in the compact group. Each Uj​ is finitely generated by the appropriate powers of a finite generating tuple, and Uj​/Uj+1​ is finite and elementary abelian. Thus the Uj​ are open. They form an identity-neighbourhood basis: in any finite continuous quotient their images form a terminating lower p-series, so some Uj​ is contained in the kernel. In particular ⋂j​Uj​=1. Collection also gives
[Ua​,Ub​]⊆Ua+b+1​.
(21)
One can see the extra index directly by induction from [Ua​,G]⊆Ua+1​: collect [v,wp] for w∈Ub​. Its leading factor [v,w]p advances one layer, and the remaining iterated commutators, with their binomial coefficients, lie at least as deep. Products and limits preserve the inclusion. This is the usual strongly central property of the lower p-series.
A uniform pro-p group is a finitely generated powerful pro-p group for which all the indices ∣Uj​:Uj+1​∣ equal ∣G:Gp∣. The preliminary congruence gives surjective linear maps
θj​:Uj​/Uj+1​⟶Uj+1​/Uj+2​,uUj+1​⟼upUj+2​.
(22)
If G is uniform, these maps are bijective. If x=y, write y=xu and choose the unique layer j with u∈Uj​∖Uj+1​. Bijectivity of θj​ and the power congruence imply yp(xp)−1∈Uj+1​∖Uj+2​, so xp=yp. Thus the power map is injective. A finite-order element of a pro-p group has p-power order; repeated injectivity of the power map excludes every nonidentity such element. Hence a uniform group is a torsion-free group.
Conversely, suppose some θj​ has a nonzero kernel. Choose x∈Uj​∖Uj+1​ with xp∈Uj+2​. At step r≥j+1, if the current xr​ satisfies xrp​∈Ur+1​, surjectivity of θr​ supplies ur​∈Ur​ with
urp​≡(xrp​)−1(modUr+2​).
(23)
Set xr+1​=xr​ur​. The power congruence gives xr+1p​∈Ur+2​. Since the corrections tend to 1 in the complete profinite group, the xr​ converge to z, with zp=1 and z≡x(modUj+1​). In particular z=1. A torsion-free group cannot have such an element, so every θj​ is injective and all layer indices are equal. This proves the root correction criterion for uniformity and
G is uniform ⟺ G is torsion-free.​
(24)
For a uniform group the power maps are continuous bijections Uj​→Uj+1​, hence homeomorphisms. Their inverses define the unique roots used below. Comparison at the first differing layer also proves the power congruences in a uniform pro-p group:
x≡y(modUb​)⟺xpa≡ypa(modUa+b​).
(25)
The intrinsic Lie algebra of a uniform pro-p group has the same underlying set as G, zero equal to the group identity, and operations
x+L​y=limn→∞​(xpnypn)p−n,[x,y]L​=limn→∞​[xpn,ypn]p−2n,λx=xλ(λ∈Zp​).
(26)
Here xλ is the limit of integral powers approaching λ. The first root is taken in Un​ and the second in U2n​; the commutator actually lies in U2n+1​. The following faithful algebra realization verifies convergence and the Lie algebra axioms, rather than assuming them as part of the definition.
Choose a minimal tuple a1​,…,ad​ of topological generators. The layer isomorphisms θj​ show that the images of aipj​ are bases of Uj​/Uj+1​. The ordered-coordinate argument of Question 3 therefore gives a continuous bijection
Zpd​⟶G,(λi​)⟼a1λ1​​⋯adλd​​.
(27)
Existence follows by successive digits and compactness; uniqueness follows by the first digit at which the coordinates differ, because its images are linearly independent in the corresponding power layer. Thus this map is a homeomorphism.
Here are details of the norm needed on the group algebra. In Zp​[[G]] put bi​=ai​−1. Its elements have unique ordered expansions ∑α​cα​b1α1​​⋯bdαd​​ with cα​∈Zp​. To verify this description, identify the ordered coordinates of G with Zpd​. Elements of the completed group algebra are integral measures on this space; their values on the binomial polynomials ∏i​(αi​λi​​) are precisely the coefficients cα​. The finite-difference identity pairs the ordered monomial bβ with this binomial polynomial as δαβ​. The Mahler expansion of a continuous function therefore proves both existence and uniqueness of the measure with the specified coefficients. This also proves that the ordinary rational group algebra embeds in these expansions.
Choose 1/(p−1)<s<1 and set
v(∑α​cα​bα)=infα​{vp​(cα​)+s∣α∣}.
(28)
This defines the weighted group algebra of a uniform pro-p group. Its multiplication is submultiplicative in the valuation convention, v(AB)≥v(A)+v(B). Indeed, aip​−1=pbi​+(2p​)bi2​+⋯+bip​ has valuation 1+s; collection of ordered coordinates gives the same lower bound for g−1 when g∈U1​. Interchanging two adjacent bi​ in a product introduces a correction coming from [ai​,aj​]−1, of valuation at least 1+s>2s. Iterating the interchange rule never lowers the total weight; the corrections have strictly increasing weight, so infinite collection converges. The same argument with pj-th powers shows
v(g−1)=s+jfor g∈Uj​∖Uj+1​.
(29)
The equality follows from the first nonzero coordinate digit: its leading terms are pjλi′​bi​, at least one with unit λi′​, and cannot cancel in the ordered expansion. Completing the rational group algebra in this norm produces a complete normed algebra A in which G embeds faithfully and with its original topology.
Since v(g−1)≥s>1/(p−1), the series
logg=∑r≥1​r(−1)r+1(g−1)r​,expX=∑r≥0​r!Xr​
(30)
converge on the relevant balls in A and are inverse there. Their convergence follows from vp​(r!)≤(r−1)/(p−1) and vp​(r)≤(r−1)/(p−1); formal one-variable identities prove that they are inverse. Put X=logx and Y=logy. The Baker--Campbell--Hausdorff formula in A now gives
p−nlog(xpnypn)⟶X+Y,
(31)
because every term of degree at least two tends to zero after this rescaling. Similarly expansion of exp(−pnX)exp(−pnY)exp(pnX)exp(pnY) gives
p−2nlog([xpn,ypn])⟶XY−YX.
(32)
The leading term is the indicated associative-algebra commutator and all terms of degree at least three tend to zero. These expressions are the logarithms of the unique roots in the intrinsic formulas. The image logG is compact and hence closed in A, so both limits belong to logG, and the inverse logarithm takes the limits back to G. Also log(xλ)=λlogx. Consequently log identifies the intrinsic operations with addition, scalar multiplication and commutation in A. The Jacobi identity follows by expanding the associative products; thus LG​ is indeed a Lie algebra over Zp​.
The commutator roots in the intrinsic formula belong to U1​, so their limit gives [LG​,LG​]⊆logU1​=pLG​. Iterating the power congruence gives xpnypn≡(xy)pn(modUn+1​). Taking the unique roots shows that intrinsic addition modulo U1​ is the original group product modulo U1​. Consequently LG​/pLG​ is the original elementary abelian quotient G/U1​, and the images of the ai​ form a basis. Successively removing digits expresses every element uniquely as an additive sum ∑i​λi​ai​: existence uses completeness, while a nonzero relation would have a first nonzero digit contradicting the basis in Uj​/Uj+1​. Hence LG​ is a free module of rank d over the p-adic integers and is a powerful Lie lattice.
Conversely a powerful Lie algebra over p-adic integers, for odd p, is a finite free Zp​-module L with a bilinear alternating Lie bracket satisfying the Jacobi identity and [L,L]⊆pL. Define
X∗Y=BCH(X,Y)=X+Y+21​[X,Y]+121​([X,[X,Y]]+[Y,[Y,X]])+⋯.
(33)
For completeness, integrality and convergence can be verified without guessing which individual denominators are units. In Qp​⊗L set M=p−1L. Then [M,M]⊆M, so its integral universal enveloping algebra has an ordered basis by the Poincaré-Birkhoff-Witt theorem. Its coefficientwise p-adic norm is submultiplicative, and L=pM consists of elements of valuation at least one. The homogeneous degree-r terms in log(expXexpY) have coefficients of valuation at least −⌊(r−1)/(p−1)⌋: expand the logarithm, use vp​(k!)≤(k−1)/(p−1) on each nonconstant exponential factor, and vp​(ℓ)≤(ℓ−1)/(p−1) on a product of ℓ such factors. Therefore evaluating degree r on X,Y∈pM gives valuation at least
r−⌊p−1r−1​⌋,
(34)
which tends to infinity and is at least two when r≥2. Each homogeneous term is a rational Lie polynomial by the formal Baker--Campbell--Hausdorff formula. The ordered enveloping-algebra basis shows that its intersection with Qp​M is exactly M, so the estimate places the sum in L, with the non-linear part in pL.
Associativity follows from the identity exp(X∗Y)=expXexpY in the completed universal enveloping algebra. Its identity is 0, its inverse is −X, and X∗m=mX, since the bracket of X with itself is zero. The same coefficient estimate for the group commutator puts it in pL. Conjugation in the BCH group is exp(adX); each term ad(X)r/r! preserves pjL, because each bracket adds a factor of p and vp​(r!)<r. Thus the submodules pjL are normal subgroups for ∗, their intersection is zero, and their finite quotients have p-power order. Thus (L,∗) is a powerful pro-p group, with
(L,∗)pj=pjL,∣pjL:pj+1L∣=prankL.
(35)
A module basis topologically generates this group: it generates the quotient L/pL, and successive power digits lift that generation through all pjL. The BCH construction therefore gives a uniform pro-p group.
