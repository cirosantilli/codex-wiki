The outer solution satisfies y(x)[x+y(x)]=0, so on a connected interior interval the nonoscillatory branches are y=0 and y=−x. Both happen to solve the differential equation exactly away from any endpoint mismatch. Thus the leading admissibility condition for the negative outer branch is α≤a/2 and β≤1/2. Under strict inequalities, each endpoint has two choices when its datum lies between −q and q/2, and one choice below −q. Their independent combinations give one, two or four formal matched profiles. In particular, for α=β=0 there are four negative-bulk profiles in addition to the exact zero solution, so uniqueness certainly cannot be inferred from the outer equation. The endpoint equalities and the constant-layer cases are degenerate; higher-order endpoint matching is needed to decide exact finite-ϵ counts at these cutoffs. These conditions and counts concern leading families under the stated exclusion of interior layers and rapid oscillations.

Additional interior layers cannot be excluded merely by counting the outer roots. Freezing x=q gives a homoclinic orbit to −q with the same hyperbolic-secant pulse. Its location would require a further matching or solvability condition; it is not an arbitrary parameter in the original variable-coefficient problem. Indeed, put z=y+x. The exact equation and an energy identity are

An isolated order-one pulse with exponentially small tails on both sides would decrease this energy by an algebraic amount, whereas its two tail energies would be exponentially small. This obstructs a freely standing isolated pulse well away from the walls. It does not by itself classify oscillatory solutions or pulse interactions with endpoint layers, so a complete global uniqueness claim would exceed this leading construction.
When a=α=0, the two outer roots meet at the left endpoint. Let x=ϵpX, y=ϵqY. Balancing xy with y2 gives p=q, and balancing ϵy′′ with them gives 1−q=2q. The one-third-power scaling at a nonlinear turning endpoint is therefore
x=ϵ1/3X,y=ϵ1/3Y,Y′′+XY+Y2=0​.

Its left condition is Y(0)=0. Matching to the negative branch requires Y(X)+X→0 and Y′(X)+1→0 as X→∞; the right endpoint is then handled by the earlier q=1 layer. Write Y=−X+u. In the intermediate matching region a small correction satisfies u′′−Xu=0, so u=CAi(X)+DBi(X). The growing Airy function excludes D; the decaying Airy function supplies a permissible stable matching direction. In fact Y=−X is an exact inner solution satisfying Y(0)=0, establishing existence of an inner match without solving a nonlinear shooting problem. Since Ai(0)=0, the wall condition excludes an infinitesimal nonzero decaying correction about this solution; it does not prove absence of other finite-amplitude inner solutions. The right wall still requires β≤1/2 at leading order.
Matching instead to zero requires Y→0. Its linearized equation is Y′′+XY=0, with solutions Ai(−X) and Bi(−X). Their large-X forms have envelope X−1/4 and phase 2X3/2/3; although their values decay, they generate rapid spatial oscillations in the overlap region. The imposed nonoscillatory assumption removes these nonzero tails. The exact Y=0 remains, and a globally zero outer branch again requires β=0.
