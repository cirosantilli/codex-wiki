Use the Burgers reduction of weakly nonlinear rightgoing acoustics, with θ=t−x, X=Mx, ∂t​=∂θ​ and ∂x​=−∂θ​+M∂X​. The equation of state expands as
p=ρ+2M​(γ−1)ρ2+O(M2),ρ1​=p1​−2γ−1​p02​.
(46)
At order one, mass conservation and momentum conservation give (ρ0​−u0​)θ​=0 and (u0​−p0​)θ​=0. For the pure rightgoing acoustic wave with no independent leading mean density or velocity shift, ρ0​=u0​=p0​. This is the background choice implicit in the requested traveling-wave expansion; additional mean components would modify its phase convention. The source's duplicated phrase about expanding p should refer to expanding ρ as well as u.
At order M, the mass conservation equation becomes
(ρ1​−u1​)θ​+p0,X​−(p02​)θ​=0,
(47)
whereas the momentum conservation equation becomes
(u1​−p1​)θ​+p0,X​=0.
(48)
In the latter equation, the two nonlinear products ρ0​u0,θ​ and −u0​u0,θ​ cancel. Substitute the equation of state relation into the former equation and add the two equations to eliminate the first corrections. The result is
p0,X​−2γ+1​p0​p0,θ​=0​.
(49)
The same calculation gives u1​−p1​=−(γ+1)p02​/4+C(X), so the first corrections may be chosen without an explicit secular factor of θ. This is why the retarded-time description can remain uniform for ∣θ∣=O(M−1), provided the leading profile and its relevant derivatives remain bounded over that range and the slow propagation interval.
This Inviscid Burgers equation can develop a shock, so the source's uniformity assertion needs a pre-shock qualification. If p0​(θ,0)=F(θ) and a=(γ+1)/2, its method of characteristics gives
p0​=F(s),θ=s−aF(s)X,p0,θ​=1−aXF′(s)F′(s)​.
(50)
The smooth construction ends when this denominator vanishes; if maxF′>0, the first such distance is Xs​=[amaxF′]−1.
For viscosity write β=Mν with bounded positive ν. Since u0,xx​=p0,θθ​+O(M), the order-M momentum conservation equation instead reads (u1​−p1​)θ​+p0,X​=νp0,θθ​. Addition now yields the viscous Burgers equation
p0,X​−2γ+1​p0​p0,θ​=2ν​p0,θθ​=2Mβ​p0,θθ​​.
(51)
Its positive diffusion resolves the inviscid steepening rather than supporting an unqualified smooth inviscid expansion through shock formation.
