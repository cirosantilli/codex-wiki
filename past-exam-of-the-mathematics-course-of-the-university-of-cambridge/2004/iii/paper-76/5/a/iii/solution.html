For positive definite diffusion this is also sufficient: the anisotropic Gaussian heat kernel has exponential factor exp[μt−(x−ct)TΓ−1(x−ct)/(4t)] and a t−1 prefactor, so its fixed-position growth rate is precisely μ−f.
Let the prescribed speed be q and let dmin​≤dmax​ be the positive eigenvalues of Γ. The Rayleigh quotient for Γ−1 gives
min∣c∣=q​f=4dmax​q2​,max∣c∣=q​f=4dmin​q2​.
(61)
Absence of absolute hydrodynamic instability for every direction therefore requires
μ≤4dmax​q2​​.
(62)
This is an upper bound on μ, not a minimum required value. The source's last sentence reverses this monotonicity: reducing the growth parameter always helps, so there is no finite lower threshold guaranteeing absence. If it had asked for instability in every direction, the corresponding condition would be μ>q2/(4dmin​). Without the positive-definite assumption, negative diffusion directions make the forward problem ill posed; a singular diffusion tensor requires a separate transport analysis and does not support the displayed inverse-matrix formula.
