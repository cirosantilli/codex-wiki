Let V=4πb3/3 and B=Vg(ρ0​−ρ)/ρref​. The four balances describe, respectively, fluid entrainment through the spherical surface at normal speed αu, the increase of vertical momentum due to buoyancy, conservation of total buoyancy in a uniform ambient, and motion of the thermal centre. Entrained ambient fluid initially has no vertical momentum, so the momentum equation includes the inertia needed to accelerate it. This model neglects added mass and drag; these effects would alter the numerical similarity coefficients.
With ∣ρ−ρ0​∣≪ρ0​, the Boussinesq approximation replaces inertial density by ρ0​ while retaining the density difference in buoyancy. Take ρref​=ρ0​ to this accuracy. Then
b˙=αu,dtd(Vu)​=B0​,B=B0​,z˙=u.

ρ(t)=ρ0​−4πgb(t)33ρref​B0​​​.

Thus the radius and rise height grow as t1/2, the rise speed decays as t−1/2, and the density deficit decays as t−3/2. In height coordinates, put K=4πα3/3 and C=B0​/(2K)​; then V=Kz3 and u=C/z.
If a reference density different from the inertial ambient density is retained, d(Vu)/dt=(ρref​/ρ0​)B0​: replace B0​ in the kinematic formulas by (ρref​/ρ0​)B0​, while retaining the original B0​ in the density-deficit formula. This keeps reference-density normalization separate from the physical acceleration.
The point-source spherical thermal similarity is singular at t=0 and cannot satisfy the small-density-difference condition arbitrarily close to its formal origin. A finite-radius, zero-impulse regularization has b=bs​+αz and b4=bs4​+3αB0​t2/(2π), with u=B0​t/V. This displays the required initial data rather than attributing the singularity to a physical infinite initial speed.
