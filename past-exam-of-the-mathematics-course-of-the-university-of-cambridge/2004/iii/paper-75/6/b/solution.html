Two source scales obtained from dimensional analysis are
LM​=B01/2​M03/4​​,LQ​=B01/5​Q03/5​​​.
(51)
The jet length LM​ measures how far source momentum remains important relative to buoyancy-generated momentum. The source-volume length of a turbulent plume LQ​ measures how far imposed source volume remains important relative to entrained volume. Numerical constants and entrainment coefficient factors depend on the chosen normalization. From the equations here the useful exact volume-growth scale is ℓQ​=[Q03​/(20α4B0​)]1/5; the pure-similarity height whose flux equals Q0​ is 5ℓQ​/3. A momentum crossover likewise contains a factor of order α−1/2LM​. The source radius Q0​/M0​​ is another useful geometrical combination, but it does not replace the two buoyancy/source crossover comparisons.
Integrate the differential relation from part (a) with finite source values:
M5/2−M05/2​=8α5B0​​(Q2−Q02​).
(52)
Define
c=1−5B0​Q02​8αM05/2​​​.
(53)
This plume flux-balance invariant rearranges to
5B0​Q02​8αM5/2​+c=Q02​Q2​​.
(54)
Use it to express M​=[5B0​Q02​/(8α)]1/5[(Q/Q0​)2−c]1/5, and substitute into Q′=2αM​. The coefficient satisfies (2α/Q0​)5[5B0​Q02​/(8α)]=20α4B0​/Q03​, giving
Q0​1​dzdQ​=(Q03​20α4B0​​)1/5(Q02​Q2​−c)1/5​.
(55)
With positive source fluxes, c<1. For c<0 the source has excess momentum relative to pure plume balance, so it is a momentum-dominated forced plume. For c=0 it is already in pure plume balance, and the exact solution is the pure similarity translated to a finite plume virtual origin. For 0<c<1 the source has deficient momentum relative to its buoyancy and volume fluxes, a lazy plume. Here “forced” in the broad sense merely means finite source fluxes; this sign classification distinguishes its jet-like and lazy branches. At large height, finite cQ02​/Q2 vanishes and every positive branch approaches pure plume balance.
