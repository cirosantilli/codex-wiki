Differentiate b=Q/M​ and use both balances:
dzdb​=M​Q′​−2M3/2QM′​=2α−2M5/2B0​Q2​.
(56)
Substituting Q2=8αM5/2/(5B0​)+cQ02​ gives
dzdb​=56α​−2M5/2cQ02​B0​​​.
(57)
For c<0, the radius grows faster than the pure-plume slope 6α/5 and its growth rate decreases toward that slope as M increases. For c=0, it grows at that slope exactly. For 0<c<1, it grows more slowly, possibly initially contracting, and its slope increases toward 6α/5. Since M′=B0​Q/M>0 and M→∞, all branches satisfy db/dz→6α/5 as z→∞.
Initial necking requires
2α−2M05/2​B0​Q02​​<0⟺B0​Q02​>4αM05/2​⟺c>53​​.
(58)
Thus a sufficiently lazy plume initially narrows as buoyancy accelerates its fluid faster than fluid entrainment supplies additional volume. It still has increasing volume and momentum fluxes; shrinking radius does not imply volume loss. At equality the initial slope is zero, and for c>3/5 the slope later becomes positive once M5/2=5cB0​Q02​/(12α). This gives a finite minimum radius before the eventual pure plume regime.
