Before surface-wave breaking or reflected waves return, the shallow water equations are ht​+(hu)x​=0 and ut​+uux​+ghx​=0. Set c=gh​ and c0​=gH​. Their Riemann invariants are u±2c along dx/dt=u±c. An outgoing simple wave entering resting water has u−2c=−2c0​, so the imposed boundary velocity gives
h(0,t)=g1​[c0​+2U​sin(ωt)]2​.
(4)
This formula requires c0​+u/2>0 and an appropriate inflow/outflow characteristic curve count. In particular U<2c0​/3 ensures that only the outgoing family enters the domain throughout the cycle; stronger forcing can change the boundary problem.
A characteristic curve emitted at time s carries constant u=Usinωs and h=h(0,s) and follows
x=v(s)(t−s),v(s)=c0​+23​Usin(ωs).
(5)
Increasing boundary velocity produces faster following characteristic curves, steepening the advancing side of a crest; decreasing boundary velocity spreads the curves and produces a rarefaction wave. A trough has reduced depth and smaller propagation speed. Once the compressive curves intersect, the single-valued simple wave must be replaced by an admissible hydraulic bore, with mass conservation and momentum conservation across it and loss of mechanical energy. In a very weak or dispersive wavetrain an undular bore can replace the ideal discontinuity.
For a bore of speed sb​ entering undisturbed depth H, the Rankine-Hugoniot condition gives sb​[h]=[hu] and sb​[hu]=[hu2+gh2/2]. If h2​>H is its depth just behind, elimination of the behind-bore velocity yields sb2​=gh2​(h2​+H)/(2H) and u2​=sb​(h2​−H)/h2​. Thus the newly formed front travels faster than c0​, carries a depth jump, and dissipates energy; the behind-bore state must satisfy these balances rather than continuing intersecting smooth curves. The final sketch is schematic and does not claim a solved post-breaking boundary-value problem.
There is a numerical defect in the printed leading-edge claim. The first leading edge is the s=0 curve x=c0​t. The envelope condition ∂s​x=0 gives
t=s+v′(s)v(s)​,xb​(s)=v′(s)v(s)2​.
(6)
At the leading edge,
tlead​=3Uω2c0​​,xlead​=3Uω2gH​​.
(7)
This follows directly from the specified boundary acceleration and the exact Riemann invariant. The printed coefficient 4/9 is incompatible with these data: at that distance the leading-edge characteristic curves have not yet intersected. For the first compression interval, 0<ωs<π/2, one has v′′<0. Consequently d(s+v/v′)/ds=2−vv′′/v′2>0, so the first envelope occurs at s=0. This boundary-driven shallow-water wave steepening calculation also establishes that no later characteristic in the initial compression forms a bore earlier.
