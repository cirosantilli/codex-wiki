First velocity moment. Assume a stationary, spherical, nonstreaming collisionless stellar system with constant mass-to-light ratio Υ. Use a luminosity-weighted galactic distribution function, with luminosity density λ. Multiplying the Collisionless Boltzmann equation by vi​ and integrating over velocities, with its velocity-space boundary terms vanishing, gives
∂j​(λ⟨vi​vj​⟩)=−λ∂i​Φ.
(11)
Spherical symmetry makes the stress tensor
Tij​=qt​δij​+(q−qt​)r2xi​xj​​,q=λσr2​,qt​=λσt2​.
(12)
Here σt2​ is one tangential component, as in the paper; the total tangential variance is 2σt2​. Its radial divergence is q′+2(q−qt​)/r, since the divergence of the radial unit vector is 2/r. With the velocity-anisotropy parameter β=1−σt2​/σr2​, and enclosed mass M(r)=ΥL(r), this gives the Spherical Jeans equation
q′+r2βq​=−λΦ′(r)=−γr2λL(r)​,γ=GΥ.​
(13)
This uses a constant ratio of total gravitating mass to luminosity, rather than an arbitrary independent dark halo.
Projection and elimination of anisotropy. Write R for projected radius and r for spherical radius. The Abel transform of the luminosity density gives
μ(R)=2∫R∞​r2−R2​λ(r)rdr​,
(14)
whose inverse is the displayed density inversion in the paper. At a point on this line of sight, sin2θ=R2/r2. The line-of-sight second stellar velocity moment is σr2​cos2θ+σt2​sin2θ=σr2​(1−βR2/r2). Therefore the measured quantity J(R)=μ(R)σp2​(R) satisfies
J(R)=2∫R∞​(1−β(r)r2R2​)r2−R2​q(r)rdr​.
(15)
The Spherical Jeans equation gives βq=−rq′/2−γλL/(2r). Substitution yields
J(R)=2∫R∞​r2−R2​q(r)rdr​+R2∫R∞​r2−R2​q′(r)dr​+γR2∫R∞​r2r2−R2​λ(r)L(r)dr​.
(16)
The inner γL printed in the PDF's definition of p must therefore be λL: the literal expression has an extra gravitational factor and lacks the density. Both this derivation and dimensional consistency require the correction. Define the projected spherical Jeans residual by
p(R)=J(R)−γR2∫R∞​r2r2−R2​λ(r)L(r)dr​.
(17)
To express it entirely in terms of the radial stress, put
A(R)=∫R∞​r2−R2​q(r)rdr​=∫0∞​q(R2+z2​)dz.
(18)
Differentiating the nonsingular z representation gives A′(R)=R∫R∞​q′(r)/r2−R2​dr. Hence
p(R)=2A(R)+RA′(R)=R1​dRd​[R2A(R)].​
(19)
If R2A vanishes at zero and infinity, then ∫0∞​6πRp(R)dR=6π[R2A]0∞​=0. These are the absence-of-boundary-stress conditions needed for the global result.
The virial relation, term by term. Interchange the order in the residual integral and use
∫0r​r2−R2​R3dR​=r3∫0π/2​sin3θdθ=32​r3.
(20)
It follows that
∫0∞​6πRp(R)dR=6π∫0∞​RJ(R)dR−4πγ∫0∞​rλ(r)L(r)dr.
(21)
The first term is 2K/Υ: spherical averaging assigns one third of the integrated velocity-square to any fixed viewing direction, giving K=3πΥ∫RJdR. The gravitational potential energy is
W=−∫0∞​rGM(r)​dM(r)=−4πGΥ2∫0∞​rλ(r)L(r)dr.
(22)
Thus the integrated residual is exactly (2K+W)/Υ, and its vanishing is the virial theorem. Solving for γ gives the global projected virial estimate of a mass-to-light ratio,
γ=2∫0∞​rλ(r)L(r)dr3∫0∞​Rμ(R)σp2​(R)dR​.​
(23)
It is independent of the radial anisotropy profile when the full aperture and the stated boundary conditions are used. A finite aperture retains a boundary term.
