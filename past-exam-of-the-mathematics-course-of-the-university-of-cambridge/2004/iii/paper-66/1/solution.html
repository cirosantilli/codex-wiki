The separated disk modes. Away from the plane of a razor-thin disk, there is no volume source, so the Newtonian gravitational potential obeys the Laplace equation in cylindrical coordinates. Separation of variables gives
RJ(RJ′)′​=−ZZ′′​=−k2,J′′+R1​J′+k2J=0,Z′′−k2Z=0.
(1)
Here k>0 is a radial wavenumber, with units of inverse length. Regularity on the axis selects the Bessel function of the first kind J0​(kR) rather than the singular second solution. Decay away from the disk, continuity across it and reflection symmetry select Z=e−k∣z∣. These conditions apply to each nonzero-wavenumber mode; a uniform sheet is a separate zero-mode problem.
Integrate the Poisson equation for Newtonian gravity across the disk to obtain the jump condition
Φz​(R,0+)−Φz​(R,0−)=4πGΣ(R).
(2)
For the unit-amplitude separated mode, the jump is −2kJ0​(kR). Thus
Φk​=e−k∣z∣J0​(kR),Σk​=−2πGk​J0​(kR).​
(3)
An individual basis density is signed; positive physical surface densities are built by superposition.
An arbitrary axisymmetric surface density. Define its order-zero Hankel transform by
Σ(k)=∫0∞​Σ(R′)J0​(kR′)R′dR′,Σ(R)=∫0∞​Σ(k)J0​(kR)kdk.
(4)
Matching the coefficients of the mode densities gives the Hankel representation of thin-disk gravity,
Φ(R,z)=−2πG∫0∞​Σ(k)J0​(kR)e−k∣z∣dk.​
(5)
The boundary conditions exclude added external fields. For sufficiently decaying surface density, this is the isolated-disk potential; for infinite scale-free disks, only potential differences or regularized forces need be finite.
The Mestel disk. Put C=Σ0​R0​. The Bessel function integral ∫0∞​J0​(kR′)dR′=1/k, understood with a decaying regulator, gives Σ=C/k. Differentiate the potential before removing the regulator and use J0′​=−J1​:

vc2​=RΦR​=2πGΣ0​R0​=:v02​,M(R)=2π∫0R​Σ(R′)R′dR′=2πΣ0​R0​R.​

Consequently vc2​=GM(R)/R. This equality follows from the specific Mestel disk calculation; the spherical shell theorem cannot be applied to general disks. Its total mass and absolute potential referenced to infinity diverge. A convenient finite midplane reference is Φ(R,0)=v02​log(R/R0​).
The rotating distribution function. Use specific energy E=(vR2​+vϕ2​)/2+Φ and Lz​=Rvϕ​. For the printed positive exponential, take ε=−E and s:=σ>0; s has units of squared velocity. If instead ε means ordinary total energy and σ>0, the velocity integral diverges, so that literal convention cannot define the requested galactic distribution function. Equivalently one can retain ordinary energy and choose the printed σ negative.
