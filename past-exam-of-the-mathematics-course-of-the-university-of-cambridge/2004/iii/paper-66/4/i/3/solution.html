More than three integrals occur when additional symmetry or degeneracy restricts motion beyond a generic three-frequency invariant torus. In a time-independent central gravitational potential, energy and the three components of specific angular momentum are four generically independent isolating integrals. They fix the orbital plane as well as the radial-motion parameters. A nonclosed planar rosette typically has two-dimensional orbit closure, rather than a three-dimensional invariant torus.
The Kepler orbit is a stronger example. In addition to E and L, the conserved Laplace-Runge-Lenz vector
A=v×L−μrr​,μ=GM,
(36)
fixes the apsidal direction. Its constraints A⋅L=0 and A2=μ2+2EL2 leave five independent integrals among E,L,A. It is a maximally superintegrable Hamiltonian system, with closed nondegenerate bound orbits.
More than three integrals need not form a set of Poisson-commuting functions; the three-integral limit applies to an independent commuting set, rather than to every conserved orbit constraint. At a nonstationary point in six-dimensional phase space, integral gradients annihilate the nonzero flow vector, so at most five are independent. This explains the isolating integrals and orbital closure dimension count. A resonance on one isolated orbit does not by itself prove an additional smooth global integral on a neighborhood.
