 ... Mathematics course of the University of Cambridge Past exam of the mathematics course of the University of Cambridge 2004 iii Paper 55 1
OurBigBook.com Words: 459
A linear connection on a manifold is a rule (X,Y)↦∇X​Y for differentiating vector fields which is additive, is C∞-linear in X, is real-linear in Y, and obeys ∇X​(fY)=X(f)Y+f∇X​Y. It extends to covector fields and arbitrary tensor fields by the Leibniz rule and compatibility with contraction. In coordinates,
∇a​Ud=∂a​Ud+Γdac​Uc,∇a​ωc​=∂a​ωc​−Γdac​ωd​,∇a​f=∂a​f.
(1)
This is the covariant derivative; the affine connection terms correct the changes of the coordinate basis. A symmetric affine connection has zero torsion tensor, T(X,Y)=∇X​Y−∇Y​X−[X,Y]=0, or Γcab​=Γcba​ in a coordinate basis. Symmetry does not imply metric compatibility; no metric is required for this question.
Additivity of each covariant derivative immediately gives additivity of Δab​. Expanding the second derivative of a tensor product gives
∇a​∇b​(Q⊗S)=​(∇a​∇b​Q)⊗S+(∇b​Q)⊗∇a​S+(∇a​Q)⊗∇b​S+Q⊗∇a​∇b​S.​
(2)
Subtract the expression with a,b exchanged. The two mixed terms cancel, proving that the curvature commutator is a tensor derivation:
Δab​(Q⊗S)=(Δab​Q)⊗S+Q⊗Δab​S.​
(3)
For a scalar, ∇a​∇b​f=∂a​∂b​f−Γcab​∂c​f. Commuting partial derivatives and using the symmetric affine connection gives Δab​f=0. Consequently Δab​(fU)=fΔab​U: the commutator is linear over smooth functions, not merely over constant scalars. Write U=Ucec​ in a local basis. The tensor-product rule gives Δab​U=UcΔab​ec​, showing that its value at a point depends only on U at that point. Thus it defines the Riemann curvature tensor by
Δab​Ud=Rabc​dUc,Rabc​d=∂a​Γdbc​−∂b​Γdac​+Γdae​Γebc​−Γdbe​Γeac​.​
(4)
The antisymmetrized second covariant derivative is itself tensorial in the derivative indices, so this pointwise map transforms as a tensor. The formula or the defining commutator gives Rabc​d=−Rbac​d, hence Rabc​d=R[ab]c​d with normalized antisymmetrization.
Apply the commutator to the scalar contraction ωc​Uc. Its action vanishes, so
0=(Δab​ωc​)Uc+ωc​Rabd​cUd.
(5)
Since U is arbitrary, Δab​ωc​=−Rabc​dωd​. Repeated use of the tensor-product rule gives one plus curvature action for an upper index and one minus action for each lower index. In particular,
Δab​Scde​=Rabf​cSfde​−Rabd​fScfe​−Rabe​fScdf​.​
(6)
The type notation in the PDF means one contravariant and two covariant indices, not a fractional tensor order.
For the first Bianchi identity, put ωc​=∇c​f and Hbc​=∇b​∇c​f. The symmetric Hessian of a scalar field satisfies Hbc​=Hcb​. Therefore the six third-derivative terms cancel in
Δab​ωc​+Δbc​ωa​+Δca​ωb​=∇a​Hbc​−∇b​Hac​+∇b​Hca​−∇c​Hba​+∇c​Hab​−∇a​Hcb​=0.
(7)
Substituting the covector curvature action gives (Rabc​d+Rbca​d+Rcab​d)∇d​f=0. At a chosen point the gradient of a smooth scalar can be any covector, so
Rabc​d+Rbca​d+Rcab​d=0,R[abc]​d=0.​
(8)
The second form follows from the already proved first-pair antisymmetry.
For the second Bianchi identity, the same third-derivative cancellation can be organized as an operator identity. On vector-valued components set Da​=∂a​+Γa​, with (Γa​)ed​=Γead​. Although a full second covariant derivative includes a term −Γfab​Df​ for its lower derivative index, that term cancels on antisymmetrization because the affine connection is symmetric. Thus [Db​,Dc​]=Rbc​, where (Rbc​)ed​=Rbcd​e acts by multiplication. Expanding the three nested operator commutators makes all six triple products cancel:
[Da​,[Db​,Dc​]]+[Db​,[Dc​,Da​]]+[Dc​,[Da​,Db​]]=0.
(9)
Equivalently, the cyclic sum of ∂a​Rbc​+[Γa​,Rbc​] vanishes. These are the covariant derivative terms on the upper and last lower curvature indices. The two remaining lower indices add −Γfab​Rfcd​e−Γfac​Rbfd​e. In the cyclic sum these additions cancel in pairs, using Γfab​=Γfba​ and Rfcd​e=−Rcfd​e. Hence the Bianchi identities from covariant derivative commutators give
∇a​Rbcd​e+∇b​Rcad​e+∇c​Rabd​e=0,∇[a​Rbc]d​e=0.​
(10)
Both identities here apply to any torsion-free linear affine connection; the second derivation does not require choosing a metric affine connection.
