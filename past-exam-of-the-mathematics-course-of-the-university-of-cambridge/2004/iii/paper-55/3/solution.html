Use units c=1, signature (+−−−), and the weak-field metric gab​=ηab​+hab​. Define the trace-reversed metric perturbation by hˉab​=hab​−ηab​h/2, with h=ηabhab​. In the Lorenz gauge in linearized gravity, ∂ahˉab​=0, the sign convention of Q2 gives
Gab(1)​=21​□hˉab​,□hˉab​=−16πGTab​.
(16)
For completeness, the linearized Ricci tensor in this convention is
Rab(1)​=21​(□hab​+∂a​∂b​h−∂c​∂a​hcb​−∂c​∂b​hca​).
(17)
Substituting the Lorenz condition ∂c​hcb​=∂b​h/2 and trace reversing gives the stated Einstein tensor. For a stationary source, □=−∇2, so ∇2hˉab​=16πGTab​.
Keep the monopole terms of order ϵ=GM/R and the leading term linear in the rotation rate. Since ΩR=O(ϵ1/2), the latter metric term is of order ϵΩR=O(ϵ3/2) on the shell. Thus it would disappear from a strictly order-ϵ truncation; the requested approximation must retain the leading rotation correction as well. Terms Tij​=ρvi​vj​ generate metric corrections of order ϵ(ΩR)2=O(ϵ2) and are neglected. Corrections to four-velocity normalization and the supporting shell stresses affect the omitted higher orders. To retained order,
T00​=ρ,T0i​=−ρvi​,v=Ω×x,Ω=Ωz.
(18)
The minus sign in T0i​ comes from lowering its spatial index; using T0i in its place would reverse the dragging direction.
The scalar Poisson solution regular at the centre and vanishing at spatial infinity is hˉ00​=−4GM/r outside the shell and hˉ00​=−4GM/R=−4ϵ inside. Continuity and the derivative jump hˉ00′​(R+)−hˉ00′​(R−)=4GM/R2 verify the density Mδ(r−R)/(4πR2). Also hˉij​=0 at this order. Undoing trace reversal gives
h00​=21​hˉ00​=−2ϵ,hij​=21​δij​hˉ00​=−2ϵδij​.
(19)
Comparing with the scalar parts of the interior line element yields A=−ϵ and C=ϵ.
For the mass-current contribution write
hˉ0i​=F(r)(z×r)i​.
(20)
Each nonzero angular component is a degree-one spherical harmonic. The supplied Laplacian eigenvalue therefore gives
F′′+r2​F′−r22​F=−R4GMΩ​δ(r−R).
(21)
Away from the shell, the two radial solutions are r and r−2. Regularity at r=0 and decay at infinity select Fin​=ar and Fout​=b/r2. Continuity at R requires b=aR3, and integrating the differential equation across the shell gives
F′(R+)−F′(R−)=−3a=−R4GMΩ​.
(22)
Thus a=4GMΩ/(3R)=ω, with Fin​=ωr. Trace reversal leaves mixed components unchanged, so h0i​=ω(z×x)i​=ω(−y,x,0)i​. The paper writes g0i​=−Bi​, giving the frame dragging inside a slowly rotating spherical shell
−A=C=ϵ,B=ω(y,−x,0),ω=34ϵΩ​.​
(23)
This dipole matching is valid throughout r<R; it does not assume the observation point is very close to the centre. The stationary current is divergence-free, so this solution also satisfies the Lorenz gauge. The exterior result is h0i​=2G(J×x)i​/r3, where the thin shell has J=(2/3)MR2Ω, providing a check on the coefficient.
To identify the freely falling frames inside, let Rz​(θ) be the ordinary spatial rotation by angle θ. Define the inertial coordinates inside a slowly rotating spherical shell by
T=(1−ϵ)t,X=(1+ϵ)Rz​(−ωt)x.
(24)
Differentiating and dropping ϵ2, products beyond the retained rotation order, and ω2 gives
dT2−dX2=(1−2ϵ)dt2−(1+2ϵ)dx2+2(ω×x)⋅dxdt,
(25)
which is precisely the interior metric. Thus the retained interior curvature is zero, despite its nontrivial relation to the nonrotating coordinates anchored at infinity. Timelike geodesics in these local inertial coordinates are straight lines X=X0​+VT, with ∣V∣<1. In the original coordinates this is
x(t)=1+ϵ1​Rz​(ωt){X0​+(1−ϵ)Vt},
(26)
understood to the same perturbative order. Their coordinate equation is d2x/dt2=2ω×dx/dt to first rotation order. There is no interior Newtonian acceleration from the constant potential; the velocity-dependent term is the apparent Coriolis term relative to the locally dragged inertial axes.
A freely transported gyroscope at rest in the interior to the retained order has constant spatial direction in the X frame, hence its direction in the original axes obeys dS/dt=ω×S. Local inertial axes precess in the same sense as the shell, at ω=(4ϵ/3)Ω relative to distant nonrotating axes. This is the interior Lense-Thirring precession. For a weak shell the dragging is only a small fraction of its angular velocity, not perfect corotation. It is compatible with the equivalence principle: rotation relative to distant axes is a comparison between frames, and local flatness at the retained order does not make that comparison vanish. Quadratic rotation terms, which would include centrifugal effects and additional curvature, are outside the requested leading approximation.
