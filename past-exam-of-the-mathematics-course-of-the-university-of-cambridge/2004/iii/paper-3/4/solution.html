Name the four vertices 1,2,2ˉ,1ˉ in their displayed order. This is the crystal of the defining symplectic representation in rank two, of highest weight ω1​ for the C2 root system. Its weights are respectively ε1​,ε2​,−ε2​,−ε1​, with simple roots α1​=ε1​−ε2​ and α2​=2ε2​. Each lowering arrow subtracts the corresponding simple root. Thus ω1​=ε1​ and ω2​=ε1​+ε2​.
Use the crystal tensor-product rule in which f​i​ acts on the first factor if φi​(a)>εi​(b), and on the second factor otherwise. The raising operator ei​ acts on the first factor for φi​(a)≥εi​(b), on the second otherwise. Here εi​(b) and φi​(b) count the raising and lowering steps available in color i. The data for the four vertices are
bε1​(b)φ1​(b)ε2​(b)φ2​(b)​10100​21001​2ˉ0110​1ˉ1000​​.
(31)
The strictness difference between raising and lowering ensures that they are inverse along each colored edge. Testing the sixteen ordered tensor vertices gives exactly three vertices killed by both raising Kashiwara operators:
1⊗1,1⊗2,1⊗1ˉ.
(32)
Their highest weights are 2ε1​=2ω1​, ε1​+ε2​=ω2​ and zero.
For completeness, successive lowering generates the following connected components of the tensor product of crystals:
  • From 1⊗1: 1⊗1, 2⊗1, 2⊗2, 2ˉ⊗1, 2ˉ⊗2, 2ˉ⊗2ˉ, 1ˉ⊗1, 1ˉ⊗2, 1ˉ⊗2ˉ, 1ˉ⊗1ˉ.
  • From 1⊗2: 1⊗2, 1⊗2ˉ, 2⊗2ˉ, 2⊗1ˉ, 2ˉ⊗1ˉ. Their edge colors in order are 2,1,1,2.
  • The vertex 1⊗1ˉ is isolated.
They have sizes ten, five and one, are disjoint, and exhaust all sixteen vertices. This proves the tensor square of the defining C2 crystal decomposition
B⊗B≅B(2ω1​)⊔B(ω2​)⊔B(0),dim=10+5+1.​
(33)
Equivalently, the representation decomposes as L(ω1​)⊗2=L(2ω1​)⊕L(ω2​)⊕L(0). The dimensions agree with the Weyl dimension formula for C2, dimL(aω1​+bω2​)=(a+1)(b+1)(a+b+2)(a+2b+3)/6. The ordered highest vertices would change under the opposite tensor convention, but the irreducible decomposition would be the same.
