For the addition of angular momentum, the uncoupled orthonormal basis is ∣j1​m1​⟩⊗∣j2​m2​⟩, whereas the coupled orthonormal basis diagonalizes J2 and J3​ for J=J1​+J2​. Define the Clebsch-Gordan coefficients by
∣JM⟩=∑m1​,m2​​Cm1​m2​JM​∣j1​m1​⟩∣j2​m2​⟩,Cm1​m2​JM​=⟨j1​m1​,j2​m2​∣JM⟩.
(1)
The Clebsch-Gordan decomposition allows ∣j1​−j2​∣≤J≤j1​+j2​ in unit steps, and a coefficient vanishes unless m1​+m2​=M. Taking the inner product of the expansion with itself proves
∑m1​,m2​​∣Cm1​m2​JM​∣2=⟨JM∣JM⟩=1.
(2)
With the customary real phase convention the modulus signs can be omitted, giving the sum of squares used in the question. In an arbitrary phase convention the modulus-square identity is the invariant statement.
Treat the strong interaction as exactly isospin-invariant for this calculation. Its transition operator T commutes with the three isospin generators and hence with their quadratic Casimir operator. Acting on the initial state ∣IM⟩, it can therefore produce only final states with the same I and M. The tensor product representation of final multiplets I1​,I2​ contains one copy of each permitted total isospin, so T∣IM⟩=aM​∣(I1​I2​)IM⟩. Commuting T with the lowering operator gives
aM​(I+M)(I−M+1)​=aM−1​(I+M)(I−M+1)​.
(3)
Every connecting ladder factor is nonzero, so all aM​ coincide with one reduced coefficient a. Expanding the final coupled state now gives the isospin-invariant two-body decay amplitude
AM1​M2​;M​=a⟨I1​M1​,I2​M2​∣IM⟩.​
(4)
The reduced coefficient may depend on the particle species, momenta and spin channel, but not on the three isospin projections. Summing the squared amplitude over the orthogonal charge channels gives
M1​,M2​∑​∣AM1​M2​;M​∣2=∣a∣2.​
(5)
A physical decay rate also includes phase space, angular integration and any spin sums. In the isospin-degenerate limit these factors are common to the charge channels; absorbing their square root into a gives ΓIM​=∣a∣2 for this specified two-body multiplet channel. If several distinct species or independent spin channels are included in “anything”, their reduced rates must additionally be summed. There is no extra factor 2I+1 when the initial magnetic component is fixed.
The Delta baryon states have I=3/2 and projections 3/2,1/2,−1/2,−3/2 for Δ++,Δ+,Δ0,Δ−. The proton and neutron form I=1/2, with projections +1/2,−1/2, and the pion triplet has I=1 with projections +1,0,−1. The highest coupled state is
∣3/2,3/2⟩=∣pπ+⟩.
(6)
Apply the total lowering operator I−​=I−(N)​+I−(π)​. Its coefficient on the left is 3​, while on the right the nucleon and pion coefficients are 1 and 2​:
3​∣3/2,1/2⟩=∣nπ+⟩+2​∣pπ0⟩.
(7)
The squared Clebsch-Gordan coefficients are consequently 1/3 and 2/3, compared with coefficient one for the Δ++ channel. Thus the Delta baryon pion branching ratios are
Γ(Δ++→pπ+)Γ(Δ+→pπ0)​=32​,Γ(Δ++→pπ+)Γ(Δ+→nπ+)​=31​.​
(8)
At the opposite end of the same isospin multiplet, ∣3/2,−3/2⟩=∣nπ−⟩, so the strong decay is Δ−→nπ−, with the same reduced rate in this symmetry limit. Physical mass splittings and electromagnetic effects perturb these idealized equal-kinematics ratios.

Let V=C3 be the defining flavor SU(3) representation. Under U∈SU(3), qα↦Uαβ​qβ. The antiquark transforms in the dual representation:
qˉ​α​↦(U−1)βα​qˉ​β​.
(9)
Since U is unitary, its lower-index column of components transforms by U∗, the complex conjugate numerical matrix. Thus qαqˉ​α​ is invariant. The defining triplet and its conjugate are inequivalent complex representations; conjugation reverses their weights. Write 3=V and 3=V∗, and distinguish a direct sum ⊕ from a tensor product ⊗.
First split V⊗V into symmetric tensors and totally antisymmetric tensors:
V⊗V=Sym2V⊕Λ2V.
(10)
The symmetric part has dimension 3(3+1)/2=6. Contracting the antisymmetric part with the invariant Levi-Civita symbol gives Aγ​=21​ϵγαβ​Aαβ, which transforms as a dual vector because detU=1. Its inverse is Aαβ=ϵαβγAγ​. Hence
3⊗3=3⊕6.​
(11)
The symmetric part is the irreducible highest-weight representation with Dynkin labels (2,0); its six monomials give the standard symmetric-square representation.
For three triplets, the antisymmetric first-pair sector becomes V∗⊗V. Its tensors Mαβ​ split into their scalar trace and traceless part:
V∗⊗V=1⊕8,M=3trM​1+(M−3trM​1).
(12)
The traceless part is the adjoint representation of SU(3); conjugation acts as M↦UMU−1. It is irreducible because an invariant subspace would be an ideal in the simple complex special linear Lie algebra sl3​.
The symmetric first-pair sector consists of Tabc=Tbac and has dimension 6⋅3=18. Complete symmetrization maps it onto Sym3V, whose dimension is (35​)=10. Its kernel is the mixed-symmetry eight-dimensional sector. More explicitly, the equivariant map Tabc↦Mae​=ϵebc​Tabc has trace zero because the first two indices of T are symmetric; it annihilates the completely symmetric part and is not identically zero. Since the target adjoint module is irreducible, the map is onto its eight-dimensional space. Its kernel is consequently exactly the ten-dimensional completely symmetric part. Complete symmetrization supplies an invariant splitting, so this sector is 10⊕8. Combining the sectors gives the tensor cube of the defining SU(3) representation
3⊗3=1⊕8⊕8⊕10.​
(13)
Here the decuplet has Dynkin labels (3,0), and the singlet is the fully antisymmetric contraction ϵabc​Tabc. The two octets are distinct copies, not two different irreducible types.
For two conjugate SU(3) triplets with a triplet, begin with Tabc​ and split its lower pair. An antisymmetric lower pair is identified by ϵbcd with an upper index, giving V⊗V=6⊕3. The symmetric lower-pair sector has dimension 3⋅6=18, and its contraction is the equivariant map
C(T)b​=∑a​Taba​.
(14)
It is onto V∗, since the invariant embedding
ι(v)abc​=41​(δba​vc​+δca​vb​)
(15)
satisfies Cι(v)=v. Every such tensor therefore splits uniquely as ι(C(T))+(T−ι(C(T))). The latter tensor is symmetric in its lower indices and has zero contraction, with dimension 18−3=15. It contains the highest-weight vector e1​⊗(e3∗​)2 of Dynkin labels (1,2); contraction vanishes because its upper and lower indices differ. The Weyl dimension formula for an SU(3) module with labels (p,q) is dimV(p,q)​=(p+1)(q+1)(p+q+2)/2, which gives 15 for (1,2). These tensor representations are unitary, so invariant subspaces have invariant orthogonal complements; the irreducible highest-weight summand containing this vector exhausts the fifteen-dimensional kernel. Thus
3⊗3⊗3=3⊕3⊕6⊕V(1,2)​.​
(16)
The question's dimension label 15 denotes V(1,2)​, rather than its conjugate V(2,1)​; they have equal dimensions but different tensors. Conjugating the triplet-cube decomposition gives
3⊗3=1⊕8⊕8⊕10.​
(17)
The octet is self-conjugate, whereas the symmetric decuplet changes (3,0) to (0,3).
Now consider the flavor diquark Dα​∝ϵαβγ​qβqγ. This notation describes an antisymmetric two-quark flavor state; color and spin factors are separate. In particular D3​=(ud−du)/2​ has isospin zero, baryon number 2/3, strangeness zero and flavor hypercharge 2/3. The strange antiquark has I=0, B=−1/3, S=+1 and Y=2/3. Thus D3​D3​sˉ has
B=1,S=+1,Y=2,I=0,Q=+1.​
(18)
The charge follows from the Gell-Mann--Nishijima formula. Each of the three factors has lower flavor index 3, so their product is the completely symmetric tensor component A333​. It lies entirely in Sym3V∗=10, the pentaquark antidecuplet. Equivalently, the symmetric diquark pair belongs to 6, and 6⊗3=10⊕8, with this highest-hypercharge state in the antidecuplet.
It cannot be in an octet. The octet has the weights of a traceless qαqˉ​β​ tensor, whose hypercharges are Yα​−Yβ​. For (u,d,s) the quark values are 1/3,1/3,−2/3, so these differences are only 0,+1,−1; there is no Y=2 weight. The octet's top row instead has Y=1 and I=1/2. This is a flavor-representation assignment for a possible pentaquark, not a proof that the configuration binds or that its full color-spin-orbital wavefunction satisfies all Fermi statistics constraints.

In the two-body centre-of-mass frame, spatial reflection reverses the relative coordinate of a quark-antiquark pair. Its orbital state of angular momentum L has orbital parity (−1)L, as follows from YLm​(−r)=(−1)LYLm​(r). Opposite constituent intrinsic parities multiply to minus one. Thus the meson parity and charge conjugation rules begin with
P=ηq​ηqˉ​​(−1)L=(−1)L+1.​
(19)
To obtain charge conjugation, consider a self-conjugate flavor state and write its spin-orbital wavefunction using ordered creation operators bσ†​(p)dτ†​(−p). Charge conjugation interchanges the quark and antiquark operators. Reordering them back to quark-first order produces a fermionic sign minus one. Interchanging the two spin-1/2 labels has eigenvalue −1 in the spin singlet state (S=0) and +1 in the spin triplet (S=1), namely (−1)S+1. Reversing the relative momentum contributes (−1)L. Therefore
C=−(−1)S+1(−1)L=(−1)L+S.​
(20)
Reciprocal phases for the two conjugate creation operators cancel, so the result is independent of that convention. This applies to a state that is mapped to itself by charge conjugation, not to every electrically neutral flavor state. For example a neutral meson with unequal quark and antiquark flavors can be exchanged with a different antimeson. Charged states have no individual C eigenvalue.
Restrict flavor to the u,d isospin doublet. Its product with the conjugate doublet decomposes as 2⊗2=3⊕1. Convenient normalized flavor states, with irrelevant overall phase choices, are
∣1,+1⟩=udˉ,∣1,0⟩=2​uuˉ−ddˉ​,∣1,−1⟩=duˉ,∣0,0⟩=2​uuˉ+ddˉ​.
(21)
Orbital excitation normally costs energy, so the simplest low-lying quark model states have L=0. Coupling the two constituent spins gives S=0 or S=1, and then J=S. The isotriplet with S=0 is the pion triplet, while the isotriplet with S=1 is the rho meson triplet. The isosinglet with S=1 is the nonstrange component of the omega meson. Their quantum numbers are
π0: I=1, JPC=0−+;ρ0: I=1, JPC=1−−;ω: I=0, JPC=1−−.​
(22)
The charged pions have JP=0− and the charged rho mesons have JP=1−; charge conjugation exchanges the positive and negative members. The same tensor product also allows an S=0, I=0 pseudoscalar state, so symmetry alone does not assert that only the listed mesons exist or predict their masses. The physical isosinglet spectrum involves further dynamics and, when strange flavor is included, mixing.
For a vector-meson decay to two spinless pions, conservation of angular momentum forces their relative orbital value to be L=1. Their combined parity is (−1)2(−1)L=−1, and the neutral charge combination has C=(−1)L=−1, matching both ρ0 and ω. Thus C and P alone do not distinguish these two decays. The decisive restriction is isospin together with Bose statistics.
Two isotriplet pions have 3⊗3=1⊕3⊕5, or total I=0,1,2. Under exchange the isospin-I state has sign (−1)2−I: the I=2 highest state and its lowered partners are symmetric, the I=1 highest combination (∣+0⟩−∣0+⟩)/2​ is antisymmetric, and the singlet (∣+−⟩+∣−+⟩−∣00⟩)/3​ is symmetric. The L=1 orbital state is antisymmetric. Since pions are spin-zero bosons, their total state must be symmetric, requiring the antisymmetric isospin sector I=1. This allows ρ0→π+π− but excludes an isospin-conserving ω→π+π− amplitude.
There is no analogous obstruction for three pions. In a Cartesian isospin basis a,b,c, their totally antisymmetric combination ϵabc​∣πaπbπc⟩ is an I=0 state. It can multiply a totally antisymmetric momentum amplitude proportional in the rest frame to
ϵω​⋅(p1​×p2​).
(23)
Momentum conservation p1​+p2​+p3​=0 makes this amplitude change sign under every exchange of two pion labels. The product of the flavor and momentum factors therefore obeys Bose statistics. The cross product supplies J=1 and an even orbital parity; multiplying by the three negative pion intrinsic parities gives total P=−1. In the charged component, charge conjugation exchanges π+ and π− and changes the momentum factor's sign, giving C=−1. Thus ω→π+π−π0 is compatible with I=0 and JPC=1−−.
The same distinction is encoded in G parity: G=C(−1)I for the neutral member gives Gπ​=−1, Gρ​=+1, Gω​=−1. An n-pion state has G=(−1)n, so the G-parity selection rule for pion multiplicities allows two pions for the rho and three for the omega. The printed prohibition of the omega two-pion mode is an exact-isospin strong-interaction selection rule. In the physical theory, isospin-breaking effects allow a small two-pion mode; it is not an absolute prohibition. The scope of C parity is also stated in the Particle Data Group quark-model review, and the physical two-pion mode is recorded in its omega listing.

In the finite-dimensional complex semisimple Lie algebra setting, the rank of a semisimple Lie algebra is the dimension of a Cartan subalgebra h, equivalently the number of independent commuting Cartan generators. For a compact real algebra, use its complexification and the corresponding maximal torus. Simultaneously diagonalizing the commuting adjoint actions of h gives the root-space decomposition
L=h⊕⨁α=0​Lα​,Lα​={X:[H,X]=α(H)X for every H∈h}.
(24)
The nonzero linear functionals α for which this space is nonzero are the roots of a root system. An invariant inner product identifies them with vectors in a real Euclidean root space. These are the usual semisimple assumptions behind the rank and root language here.
The displayed generators H,E+,E− form an sl2 triple. Start with a nonzero lowest-weight element X0​ and define Xn+1​=[E+,Xn​]. The Jacobi identity gives
[H,Xn+1​]=[[H,E+],Xn​]+[E+,[H,Xn​]].
(25)
Induction starting with [H,X0​]=−λX0​ consequently gives the finite sl2 lowest-weight ladder weights
[H,Xn​]=(2n−λ)Xn​.​
(26)
Now commute the lowering equation with E+ and again use the Jacobi identity:
[E+,[E−,Xn​]]=[[E+,E−],Xn​]+[E−,[E+,Xn​]]=(2n−λ)Xn​+qn+1​Xn​.
(27)
The left side is qn​Xn​. Whenever Xn​=0, coefficient comparison gives
qn+1​=qn​+λ−2n.​
(28)
The initial condition is q0​=0, meaning [E−,X0​]=0; directly [E−,X1​]=[−H,X0​]=λX0​, so q1​=λ. Summing the recurrence from 0 to n−1 yields
qn​=nλ−2k=0∑n−1​k=n(λ−n+1).​
(29)
In fact the vector identity [E−,Xn​]=n(λ−n+1)Xn−1​ follows inductively from the same Jacobi identity without needing a separately assumed lowering coefficient.
If Xn0​​ is the last nonzero element and [E+,Xn0​​]=0, apply E− to this zero next element. The lowering identity gives
0=[E−,Xn0​+1​]=(n0​+1)(λ−n0​)Xn0​​,
(30)
so λ=n0​, a nonnegative integer. The nonzero endpoint is necessary. Literally allowing Xn0​​=0 would make the printed conclusion false: take X0​=E− in the adjoint representation of sl2​. Then λ=2, X1​=H, X2​=−2E+, X3​=0. Choosing n0​=3 gives a vanishing next bracket but not λ=3. The intended endpoint is the first terminating bracket after the nonzero ladder.
For the plus-sign nested commutators, use H=H1​, E±=E1±​ and X0​=E2+​. The cross relation gives [E1−​,E2+​]=0, while [H1​,E2+​]=K21​E2+​. Thus the lowest-weight label is λ=−K21​. If the n-fold raised element is nonzero and the (n+1)-fold element vanishes, the preceding endpoint argument gives λ=n and hence
K21​=−n.​
(31)
For the minus-sign ladder, use the sl2 triple H′=−H1​, E′+=E1−​, E′−=E1+​ and start at X0​=E2−​. Its lowest-weight equation is again [H′,X0​]=K21​X0​, and its lowering bracket vanishes by the cross relation. The same argument proves exactly the same result, including the endpoint assumption that is explicit in the nested-commutator question.
For arbitrary roots of a root system α,β, choose the normalized sl2 subalgebra associated with a root β, whose generators obey [Hβ​,Eβ±​]=±2Eβ±​ and [Eβ+​,Eβ−​]=Hβ​. The coroot normalization gives
α(Hβ​)=β⋅β2α⋅β​.
(32)
The normalization has a concrete construction. Use the nondegenerate Killing form B, and define tβ​∈h by B(tβ​,H)=β(H). Invariance makes root spaces orthogonal unless their roots sum to zero, so there exist e∈Lβ​ and f∈L−β​ with B(e,f)=1. Invariance then gives B([e,f],H)=B(e,[f,H])=β(H), hence [e,f]=tβ​. With (β,β)=β(tβ​), take Eβ+​=e, Eβ−​=2f/(β,β) and Hβ​=2tβ​/(β,β). Their brackets have the required normalization and α(Hβ​)=2(α,β)/(β,β). Let v be a nonzero α root vector with Hβ​ weight w=α(Hβ​). Repeatedly apply adEβ−​ until reaching a last nonzero vector v0​=(adEβ−​)pv. Its weight is w−2p. This process terminates because different weights are linearly independent and the Lie algebra is finite-dimensional. Starting at v0​, repeatedly raise; this also terminates for the same reason. The proven lowest-weight endpoint formula says w−2p=−N for a nonnegative integer N. Therefore
β22α⋅β​=w=2p−N∈Z.​
(33)
This proves integrality of Cartan integers directly from the finite ladder, rather than assuming the crystallographic axiom.
Finally use the indices actually printed in the PDF: E1+​=R12​, E2+​=R23​, E1−​=R21​, E2−​=R32​. The converted TeX has corrupted these indices. The prescribed bracket is that of trace-free matrix units. The sum of the diagonal generators is zero, leaving two independent diagonal elements, and their pair brackets give
H1​=R11​−R22​,H2​=R22​−R33​.​
(34)
For example [R12​,R21​]=R11​−R22​, and the analogous second pair gives H2​. The cross raising-lowering brackets vanish. A diagonal H with entries hr​ has [H,Rrs​]=(hr​−hs​)Rrs​. Thus H1​ acts on E1+​,E2+​ with weights 2,−1, and H2​ acts on them with weights −1,2. The negative generators have the opposite weights. In the question's index ordering,
(Kji​)=(2−1​−12​).​
(35)
This is the Cartan matrix of the A2 root system, and the Lie algebra generated by these matrix units has rank two. As an additional bracket check, [E1+​,E2+​]=R13​=0 whereas [E1+​,R13​]=0, so the nested ladder has n=1 and indeed K21​=−1. The negative ladder likewise terminates after one nonzero bracket.
