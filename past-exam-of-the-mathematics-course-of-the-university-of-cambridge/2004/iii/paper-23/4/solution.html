 ... Past exam of the mathematics course of the University of Cambridge 2004 iii Paper 23 Section B 4
OurBigBook.com Words: 644
An adjunction L⊣U induces the monad T=UL with unit η and multiplication UεL. The Eilenberg-Moore comparison functor sends B to (UB,UεB​). A monadic adjunction is one for which that comparison is an equivalence of categories. Iterating comparisons, when their left adjoints exist, gives a tower; its monadic length is the number of steps until equivalence, with length zero for an equivalence and length one for a monadic adjunction that is not already an equivalence.
The precise monadicity theorem says that a right adjoint is monadic exactly when it reflects isomorphisms and creates coequalizers of pairs whose images admit split coequalizers.
Write Ur​:Cr​→Cr−1​ for one-step forgetting. For r=1, the free extension of nested partial unary operations is the familiar set A×N, with α1​(a,j)=(a,j+1) and unit a↦(a,0). A map into a set with endomorphism β1​ extends uniquely by (a,j)↦β1j​f(a).
For r≥2, let
S(A)={a∈A:αr−1​(a) is defined and equals a}.
(7)
Keep A and all its old operations, and adjoin the disjoint set S(A)×N. On the new points define α1​(a,j)=(a,j+1); every old operation αi​ with i≥2 is undefined there. Define the new top operation only at the old eligible points, by αr​(a)=(a,0) for a∈S(A).
These domains satisfy the defining rules of the nested partial unary operation category: the new points have no α1​-fixed points, so none has any higher operation defined. Given f:A→Ur​B, extend it by
f​(a,j)=β1j​βr​(f(a)).
(8)
The expression is defined because f sends eligible points to eligible points and β1​ is total. It preserves every defined operation; preservation of αr​ at old points determines j=0, and preservation of α1​ determines the whole chain. Thus it is the unique extension, proving Lr​⊣Ur​.
For monadicity, Ur​ reflects isomorphisms: an isomorphism of the lower structures takes S(A) bijectively to S(B), so the inverse of a map preserving the new operation preserves it too.
Next prove the split-coequalizer lifting for a fixed-point-domain operation. Let f,g:A⇉B be Cr​-morphisms and suppose their lower images have a split coequalizer q:Ur​B→Q, with s:Q→Ur​B, t:Ur​B→Ur​A satisfying
qf=qg,qs=1,ft=1,gt=sq.
(9)
Equip Q with βQ​(z)=qβB​(sz) on its required eligible domain. The lower-structure maps s,t preserve eligibility. For eligible x∈B,
qβB​(x)=qfβA​(tx)=qgβA​(tx)=qβB​(sqx),
(10)
so q preserves the top operation. If h:B→D coequalizes f,g, its lower factor h:Q→Ur​D satisfies
hβQ​(z)=hβB​(sz)=βD​(hsz)=βD​(hz).
(11)
Thus it is a morphism of the full structures. Uniqueness of the lifted operation follows from the eligible section s and surjectivity of q. This creates the specified coequalizer, so the Beck monadicity theorem proves each one-step adjunction is monadic.
Finally, every free top operation just constructed has no fixed points: for r=1 it shifts the free chain, while for r≥2 it sends old eligible points to new distinct points and is undefined on the new points. Freely adjoining any further operation therefore changes nothing, since its required domain is empty. For n>m, the monad induced on Cm​ by the long free-and-forgetful adjunction is consequently the same monad, including unit and multiplication, as that for Cm+1​→Cm​.
The first comparison category is thus equivalent to Cm+1​. Its algebra action evaluates the new formal value by αm+1​, so the comparison from Cn​ retains precisely that next operation and is the remaining forgetful functor. Iteration recovers one additional operation at each step. These remaining comparisons are not equivalences while an operation is still forgotten: take a two-element set with all retained operations equal to the identity, and choose the next operation to be the identity in one extension and the transposition in another. Higher operations can be identities in the first case and undefined in the second. The identity on the retained structure is not a morphism between these extensions, so the forgetful functor is not full. This includes C1​→Set.
The monadic tower for nested partial unary operations therefore has exactly the claimed length:
length(Cn​⇄Set)=n.​
(12)
