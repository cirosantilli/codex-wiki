Past exam of the mathematics course of the University of Cambridge / 2004 / iii / Paper 23 / 1 / Solution

 ... Past exam of the mathematics course of the University of Cambridge 2004 iii Paper 23 Section A 1
OurBigBook.com Words: 401
Use the Freyd general adjoint functor theorem: a functor U:A→B, with A complete and both categories locally small, has a left adjoint precisely when it preserves small categorical limits and satisfies the solution-set condition. The latter means that every comma category (b↓U) has a small weakly initial set.
Here is the proof. First prove the initial-object lemma for complete categories with a weakly initial set. In a complete locally small category E, take the product in a category W of a small weakly initial family. It is weakly initial: project to whichever member admits a morphism into the required object. Form the simultaneous equalizer i:I→W of every endomorphism of W and 1W​; local smallness makes this a small limit.
For parallel f,g:I→X, let j:Y→I be their equalizer. Weak initiality supplies t:W→Y. Since ijt is an endomorphism of W, the defining equalities give ijti=i. Cancel the monomorphism i to get jti=1I​. Thus j is a split epimorphism as well as a monomorphism, hence invertible, and f=g. Existence of a morphism I→X follows by composing i with a map W→X. Therefore I is an initial object.
Now, if U preserves small categorical limits, each (b↓U) has them: take the limit of the underlying A-diagram and use preservation to assemble its maps out of b. It is locally small and has a weakly initial solution set, so the lemma produces an initial object (Lb,ηb​). Its universal property gives natural bijections
A(Lb,a)≅B(b,Ua).
(1)
The universal arrows define L on morphisms, proving L⊣U. Conversely, a right adjoint preserves categorical limits, and its unit supplies a singleton solution set in each comma category.
Apply this to U=F∗. The functor category [D,Set] is complete and locally small because D is small; pointwise limits in a functor category show F∗ preserves all small limits. For Q:C→Set, verify the bounded subfunctor solution set for precomposition. Choose an infinite cardinal number κ at least as large as the number of D-morphisms and ∑c∈C​∣Q(c)∣. Given η:Q→GF, set
G′(d)={G(u)ηc​(x):c∈C, x∈Q(c), u:Fc→d}.
(2)
Postcomposition makes G′ a subfunctor, each value has cardinality at most κ, and η factors through G′F. After labelling its values by subsets of κ, only a set of possibilities for G′ and Q→G′F exists. Their maps into G give the required weakly initial set. Thus F∗ has a left adjoint.
It is the left Kan extension. The left Kan extension as a comma-category colimit makes it concrete:
(LanF​Q)(d)=colim(c,u:Fc→d)∈(F↓d)​Q(c).​
(3)
Every representative (c,u,x) maps under η:Q→GF to G(u)ηc​(x). The colimit identifications and naturality make this well-defined, giving exactly Nat(LanF​Q,G)≅Nat(Q,GF).
