Every other nonempty clique is a subset of one of these. One junction tree, listing its separators after the edges, is
K0​−K1​K1​−K2​K2​−K3​K1​−K4​K2​−K5​K2​−K6​K3​−K7​K4​−K8​​{A,C}{A,M}{A,F}{C,M}{F,M}{F,M}{B}{D}.​

Each variable appears in a connected subtree, verifying the running intersection property. For example, the cliques containing M are K1​,K2​,K4​,K5​,K6​, and their connecting paths contain M throughout.
The junction tree algorithm assigns every founder factor and every child-with-parents factor to one containing clique, and multiplies any observation or penetrance factors there. The junction-tree sum-product message from clique i to clique j sums out Ci​∖(Ci​∩Cj​) after multiplying the local potential and all incoming messages except j's. An inward pass sums the joint probability or pedigree likelihood; a subsequent outward pass gives each person's posterior genotype probabilities. This is the same variable-elimination principle as the Elston-Stewart algorithm, organized to handle the pedigree loop. Its largest clique has four genotype variables, much smaller than summing naively over fourteen at once.
