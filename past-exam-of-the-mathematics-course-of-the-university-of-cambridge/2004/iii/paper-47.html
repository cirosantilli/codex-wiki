Use a Minkowski external source term +∫Jχ, where χ is the integration variable, and normalize the vacuum functional:
Z[J]=∫DχeiS[χ]∫Dχexp{iS[χ]+i∫d4xJ(x)χ(x)}​,W[J]=logZ[J].
(1)
The usual vacuum boundary prescription is implicit. This is the logarithmic convention Z=eW for the connected generating functional. It makes the given quadratic expression consistent with ΔF​=⟨Tχχ⟩. Another common convention is Z=eiWc​, with Wc​=−iW; mixing the two conventions would change the factors of i below.
Functional derivative generates the full and connected correlation functions:
Gn​(x1​,…,xn​)=i−nδJ(x1​)⋯δJ(xn​)δnZ​​J=0​,Gnc​(x1​,…,xn​)=i−nδJ(x1​)⋯δJ(xn​)δnW​​J=0​.
(2)
These are time-ordered products in the vacuum, and G1​=G1c​=0 by the stated assumption. Differentiating eW four times partitions the four insertions into connected blocks. All partitions with a singleton vanish, leaving
G4​(1,2,3,4)=G4c​(1,2,3,4)+G2c​(1,2)G2c​(3,4)+G2c​(1,3)G2c​(2,4)+G2c​(1,4)G2c​(2,3).​
(3)
The shorthand arguments stand for spacetime points; this is a functional cumulant identity, not an assumption of a free field.
To construct the quantum effective action, define the source-dependent classical field
φ(x)=⟨χ(x)⟩J​=−iδJ(x)δW​.
(4)
Locally invert this relation to obtain J[φ], assuming the response integral kernel is invertible with the chosen regulator and boundary prescription. Take its Legendre transform
iΓ[φ]=W[J[φ]]−i∫d4xJ[φ](x)φ(x).​
(5)
The chain-rule terms proportional to δJ cancel, so δΓ/δφ=−J. A field-independent constant is fixed separately and is immaterial to the vertices. At zero external source the classical field is zero, and there is no linear term.
For the two-point vertex, differentiate the classical field once. If G=G2c​, then
δJ(y)δφ(x)​=iG(x,y),δφ(y)δJ(x)​=−iG−1(x,y),∫d4zG(x,z)G−1(z,y)=δ(4)(x−y).
(6)
The vertex coefficients in this problem are derivatives of iΓ, not of Γ alone. Therefore
Γ2​(x,y)=−G−1(x,y).​
(7)
For the three-point vertex, the derivative of the connected two-point integral kernel at a general external source is
δJ(c)δG(a,b)​=iG3c​(a,b,c).
(8)
Combine this with δJ/δφ=−iG−1 and differentiate the inverse identity, δG−1=−G−1(δG)G−1. This gives the logarithmic-source inverse-kernel vertices relation
Γ3​(x,y,z)=∫d4ad4bd4cG−1(x,a)G−1(y,b)G−1(z,c)G3c​(a,b,c).​
(9)
All kernels here are finally evaluated at zero external source. The connected three-point function is amputated by its three inverse quantum field theory propagators.
For the specified quadratic W, the external source response is φ=iΔF​J, hence J=−iΔF−1​φ. Substitution in the transform gives
Γ[φ]=2i​∫d4xd4yφ(x)ΔF−1​(x−y)φ(y),Γ2​=−ΔF−1​,Γn​=0 (n≥3).​
(10)
For the standard free quantum field theory propagator ΔF​(p)=i/(p2−m2+i0), this is the usual quadratic free action, up to the vacuum prescription. The generating functional is Gaussian, so connected functions beyond second order vanish and there are no interaction vertices or nontrivial scattering amplitudes. Full higher even-point functions can still be nonzero: they are products of two-point functions, as the four-point formula shows.

a OurBigBook.com
Let a connected amputated one-particle-irreducible Feynman diagram have L loops, I internal lines, V quartic vertices and E external legs. Each loop integration contributes four momentum powers, each scalar propagator removes two, and the interaction adds none. Thus its superficial degree of divergence is D=4L−2I. Counting half-edges and independent loops gives
4V=2I+E,L=I−V+1,D=4−E.​
(11)
This is simultaneous large-momentum scaling of the whole Feynman diagram. It need not describe every subregion of loop-momentum space.
For a concrete counterexample, start with a six-point triangle of three quartic vertices, with two external legs at each vertex. Insert a one-loop tadpole diagram self-energy in one internal line. The resulting Feynman diagram has V=4, I=5, L=2, E=6, and D=−2. It is still one-particle irreducible: each edge around the triangle has an alternative route, and cutting the tadpole loop does not disconnect the Feynman diagram. Nevertheless the tadpole subgraph diverges when its loop momentum tends to infinity while the triangle momentum is held fixed. In massive theory the divergent mass insertion is nonzero. This demonstrates that a superficially convergent graph can have a divergent subgraph.
Write renormalized perturbation theory with
L=21​(∂ϕ)2−21​m2ϕ2−4!λ​ϕ4+21​δZ(∂ϕ)2−21​δm2ϕ2−4!δλ​ϕ4+δL0​.
(12)
The last term is a field-independent vacuum counterterm, needed if vacuum energies are retained. A normalized correlation functional removes that sector. The free quantum field theory propagator is i/(p2−m2+i0); interaction and counterterm insertions use
quartic vertex: −iλ,four-point counterterm: −iδλ,two-point counterterm: i(δZp2−δm2).​
(13)
Usual momentum-conservation delta functions accompany the vertices. The coefficients depend on the regulator and renormalization conditions and are expanded order by order in the renormalized coupling.
The reason this list closes is locality of ultraviolet subtraction. After divergent proper subgraphs have been subtracted recursively, the remaining overall divergent part is a polynomial in external momenta of degree at most D. For E=2, D=2, Lorentz symmetry allows only a constant and a p2 term, supplied by the mass and wavefunction counterterms. For E=4, D=0, only the constant quartic counterterm is needed. Odd-point terms vanish by the field's ϕ↦−ϕ symmetry. For E>4, the overall degree is negative and there is no new overall counterterm after subdivergences have been removed. Vacuum graphs are handled separately.
Counterterm insertions subtract each subgraph's local divergence, including overlapping subgraphs, and then the overall divergence. At each perturbative order the regulated sum has a finite ultraviolet limit for renormalized correlation functions at suitable nonexceptional momenta. This is why superficial power counting of every relevant subgraph determines the allowed counterterms, even though it does not alone establish that a bare Feynman diagram diverges or converges. Possible infrared or on-shell singularities are a separate issue; ultraviolet renormalization is not a promise of finiteness in every kinematic limit.

The kinetic normalization fixes [Φ]=(d−2)/2, since a derivative has mass dimension one and the Lagrangian density has dimension d. Every differentiated field is still a field insertion. Hence the interaction has n+r legs, not just n, and its vertex contains a homogeneous polynomial of degree r in the attached momenta. Its coupling dimension is
[λ]=d−(n+r)2d−2​−r.​
(14)
For a connected Feynman diagram of this vertex type,
2I+E=(n+r)V,L=I−V+1.
(15)
The superficial momentum degree is now D=dL−2I+rV. Eliminating I and L gives
D​=d+(d−2)I+(r−d)V=d−2d−2​E+V[2d−2​(n+r)+r−d],​
(16)
so derivative-interaction scalar power counting yields
D=d−2d−2​E−V[λ].​
(17)
The same counting applies to tensor components with quantum field theory propagators scaling as p−2; contractions, identities and cancellations can lower the actual degree. A coupling of negative mass dimension lets the superficial degree increase with the number of vertices, explaining the need for progressively higher-dimensional counterterms in a perturbative effective field theory.

Use Fμνa​=∂μ​Aνa​−∂ν​Aμa​+gfabcAμb​Aνc​ and L=−Fμνa​Faμν/4. Its cubic and quartic Yang-Mills self-interactions are
L3​=−2g​fabc(∂μ​Aνa​−∂ν​Aμa​)AbμAcν,L4​=−4g2​fabcfadeAμb​Aνc​AdμAeν.​
(18)
Antisymmetry of the Lie algebra structure constants lets the cubic expression also be written as −gfabc(∂μ​Aνa​)AbμAcν.
The cubic vertex has three fields and one derivative, corresponding to n=2,r=1 in part (b). The quartic vertex has four fields and no derivatives, corresponding to n=4,r=0. Their dimensions are
[g]=24−d​,[g2]=4−d.
(19)
With both types present, the same Feynman diagram counting gives
D=d−2d−2​E−V3​[g]−V4​[g2].
(20)
In four dimensions both couplings are dimensionless, and the degree is bounded independently of vertex count. Gauge symmetry, implemented consistently after gauge fixing, restricts the ultraviolet counterterms to the allowed renormalizable gauge-invariant and gauge fixing/ghost structures. Thus the counting is consistent with perturbative renormalizability of ordinary four-dimensional Yang-Mills theory.
In six dimensions [g]=−1 and [g2]=−2, giving D=6−2E+V3​+2V4​. Increasing the vertex count permits new divergent momentum structures and higher-dimensional operators without a finite closed list of renormalization parameters. Ordinary Yang-Mills theory is therefore power-counting renormalizable in four dimensions and nonrenormalizable in six dimensions. It can still be used in six dimensions as an effective field theory with a cutoff and higher-operator expansion; this conclusion concerns the usual perturbative theory.

Use the mostly-minus Minkowski metric, quantum field theory propagator i/(p2−m2+i0) and dimensionally continued interaction −μ2ϵλϕ4/4!, with d=4−2ϵ. Take all four external momenta incoming, so ∑j​pj​=0, and set s=(p1​+p2​)2, t=(p1​+p3​)2, u=(p1​+p4​)2.
The three one-loop one-particle-irreducible Feynman diagrams are the bubble graphs with external pairings (12∣34), (13∣24) and (14∣23). Their external quantum field theory propagators are omitted by amputation. Each Feynman diagram has symmetry factor 1/2 from exchanging the two internal lines; the fixed external labels are already accounted for by the three distinct channels.
For a channel carrying momentum q, its amplitude is
B(q2)=2(−iμ2ϵλ)2​∫(2π)dddℓ​ℓ2−m2+i0i​(ℓ+q)2−m2+i0i​=2μ4ϵλ2​∫(2π)dddℓ​AB1​.
(21)
The signs in the last equality follow from the two vertices and two quantum field theory propagators. Apply the Feynman parameter identity
AB1​=∫01​[xA+(1−x)B]2dx​.
(22)
After shifting the loop momentum, the denominator is (k2−Δq​(x)+i0)2, with Δq​(x)=m2−x(1−x)q2. For positive Euclidean Δ, Wick rotation and the Schwinger representation give
∫(2π)dddk​(k2−Δ+i0)21​​=i∫(2π)dddkE​​∫0∞​dααe−α(kE2​+Δ)=(4π)d/2i​∫0∞​dαα1−d/2e−αΔ=(4π)d/2i​Γ(2−d/2)Δd/2−2.​
(23)
Analytic continuation to physical momenta keeps the prescription Δq​−i0. Thus dimensional regularization evaluates the channel as
B(q2)=2(4π)2−ϵiμ4ϵλ2​Γ(ϵ)∫01​dx[Δq​(x)−i0]−ϵ.
(24)
Use Γ(ϵ)=ϵ−1−γE​+O(ϵ) and expand the other factors. The minimal-subtraction scalar four-point bubble channels result through the finite part is
B(q2)=32π2iμ2ϵλ2​[ϵ1​−γE​+log(4π)−∫01​dxlogμ2m2−x(1−x)q2−i0​]+O(ϵ).
(25)
The parameter integral is left unevaluated, as requested. The three channel contributions are B(s)+B(t)+B(u). Their pole is momentum independent and is cancelled by the four-point counterterm −iμ2ϵδλ, with
δλ=32π2ϵ3λ2​+O(λ3).​
(26)
After pure minimal subtraction scheme subtraction, the complete vertex through one loop is the tree vertex −iλ plus
32π2iλ2​∑r∈{s,t,u}​[log(4π)−γE​−∫01​dxlogμ2m2−x(1−x)r−i0​].
(27)
Here the ϵ→0 limit has been taken. The modified minimal subtraction scheme would also absorb log(4π)−γE​; it is not identical to the specified pure MS finite part.
The bare coupling is scale independent and satisfies
λB​=μ2ϵ(λ+ϵaλ2​+O(λ3)),a=32π23​.
(28)
Writing βd​=−2ϵλ+bλ2+O(λ3) and differentiating at fixed λB​ gives
0=2ϵ(λ+ϵaλ2​)+βd​(1+ϵ2aλ​).
(29)
At order λ2, the finite coefficient is b−2a, so the one-loop quartic scalar beta function is
β(λ)=μdμdλ​​λB​​=16π23λ2​+O(λ3).​
(30)
The factor two relative to the pole residue follows from using d=4−2ϵ. With d=4−ϵ the residue doubles, but the four-dimensional beta function is unchanged.
For pure nonabelian gauge theory, the corresponding pure Yang-Mills one-loop beta function has the opposite sign:
β(g)=−3(16π2)11CA​​g3+O(g5),facdfbcd=CA​δab.
(31)
Gauge-boson self-interactions and the gauge-consistency ghost contributions give antiscreening. For a compact nonabelian simple factor CA​>0, the coupling decreases at high energy: asymptotic freedom. In contrast, positive scalar λ increases toward the ultraviolet. Its one-loop extrapolation has a Landau pole, indicating breakdown of weak-coupling perturbation theory, not a proof by itself of nonperturbative triviality. Ultraviolet poles should be extracted at infrared-safe mass or nonexceptional kinematics; a scaleless massless zero-momentum integral cannot be used to read off this ultraviolet counterterm without separating its infrared behavior.

Choose a definite matrix convention: Aμ​=Aμa​Ta​, ω=ωaTa​, and Dμ​=∂μ​+eAμ​. Matter transforms as ψ′=Uψ, so gauge covariance requires Dμ′​=UDμ​U−1. Applying both sides to a test field yields the Yang-Mills gauge transformation
Aμ′​=UAμ​U−1−e1​(∂μ​U)U−1.​
(32)
Define the Yang-Mills field strength by [Dμ​,Dν​]=eFμν​:
Fμν​=∂μ​Aν​−∂ν​Aμ​+e[Aμ​,Aν​],Fμνa​=∂μ​Aνa​−∂ν​Aμa​+efbcaAμb​Aνc​.​
(33)
Conjugating the gauge covariant derivative commutator proves Fμν′​=UFμν​U−1: unlike the connection, the gauge field strength has no inhomogeneous derivative term. The trace normalization supplies an invariant color metric; its Lie algebra structure constants with all indices lowered are antisymmetric.
For U=1+eω+O(ω2) and U−1=1−eω+O(ω2), expand the finite transformations:
δAμ​=e[ω,Aμ​]−∂μ​ω=−Dμad​ω,δFμν​=e[ω,Fμν​],Dμad​ω=∂μ​ω+e[Aμ​,ω].
(34)
In components this is
δAμa​=−∂μ​ωa−efabcAμb​ωc,δFμνa​=−efabcFμνb​ωc.​
(35)
A convention using the opposite matter transformation or gauge covariant derivative sign changes these signs consistently. The formulas above all follow from the explicitly chosen Dμ​.
Gauge reduction and the determinant. A direct integral ∫DAeiS[A] integrates repeatedly over gauge-equivalent configurations. The gauge group volume is infinite, and the ungauge-fixed quadratic operator has longitudinal zero directions, so it has no ordinary inverse quantum field theory propagator. Gauge-invariant observables require dividing out this redundancy rather than treating it as an ordinary physical fluctuation.
The finite-dimensional change-of-variables identity underlying gauge fixing is precise. Suppose a condition χ(xα)=f has one solution in the group-coordinate patch and its derivative matrix at the solution is nonsingular. Then
∫dαδ(r)(χ(xα)−f)​det∂α∂χ(xα)​​=1.
(36)
For several isolated solutions it instead counts those solutions. On the slice, the Jacobian determinant can be factored into the reciprocal of the orbit integral of the delta function. An invariant measure and invariant integrand then allow the group volume to factor out. In the functional version, group coordinates carry Haar measure and the corresponding operator determinant replaces the matrix determinant.
For example, choose the Lorenz gauge χa(A)=∂μAμa​=fa. Its variation is δχa=−∂μ(Dμad​ω)a. Hence on the gauge slice the Faddeev-Popov operator and Faddeev-Popov determinant are
Mab[A]=−∂μDμab​[A],ΔFP​[A]=detM[A].
(37)
Its extension along each local gauge orbit yields the Faddeev-Popov gauge-orbit identity
1=ΔFP​[A]∫DUδ[∂μAμU​−f].
(38)
For an unoriented finite-dimensional real integral the absolute determinant is required. In a perturbative gauge patch, its sign or phase is fixed and the constant convention is absorbed in normalization. Residual zero modes must be removed by boundary conditions or separate treatment. Multiple intersections, the Gribov ambiguity, prevent interpreting this as a globally unique gauge slice without additional work.
Insert the identity, change variables along the orbit using invariance of the action and measure, and divide out the common gauge group volume. Averaging f with Gaussian weight exp[−i∫fafa/(2ξ)] produces the covariant gauge-fixed functional
Zgf​∝∫DAdetM[A]exp{iS[A]−2ξi​∫d4x(∂μAμa​)2}.
(39)
The added term makes the quadratic gauge-field operator invertible after the stated residual-mode treatment.
Ghost representation. For a finite matrix M, the Grassmann Gaussian integral over independent anticommuting variables is proportional to its determinant:
∫∏a​dca​dca​exp(ica​Mab​cb​)=constant×detM.
(40)
The constant is independent of M and depends on ordering and factors of i. The functional counterpart introduces Faddeev-Popov ghost fields ca and antighost fields ca, with
Sgh​=∫d4xcaMab[A]cb=−∫d4xca∂μ(Dμ​c)a.
(41)
Integration by parts writes its density as (∂μca)(Dμ​c)a, displaying a kinetic term and a gauge-field/ghost interaction. These are Grassmann-valued fields with scalar Lorentz transformation law. They represent the orbit Jacobian determinant; their closed loops carry the fermionic minus sign, and they are not physical asymptotic particles. In the abelian case Dad=∂, the determinant is gauge-field independent and the ghosts decouple. Nonabelian ghosts interact and are necessary for the consistent cancellation of unphysical gauge contributions.
Physical-state reduction. Let Q be the Hermitian BRST charge. Its key algebraic property is BRST nilpotence, Q2=0, and the gauge-fixed dynamics preserves it. Physical states are its BRST cohomology at ghost number zero:
Hphys​=imQ​gh=0​kerQ​gh=0​​,Q∣ψ⟩=0,∣ψ⟩∼∣ψ⟩+Q∣χ⟩.​
(42)
Here ∣χ⟩ has ghost number −1. BRST nilpotence ensures that exact vectors lie in the kernel, so the quotient is defined. Because Q is Hermitian, an exact vector is orthogonal to every closed vector, ⟨ψ∣Qχ⟩=⟨Qψ∣χ⟩=0, and exact vectors are null, ⟨Qχ∣Qχ⟩=⟨χ∣Q2χ⟩=0. They therefore represent no independent physical state. Commutation of Q with the Hamiltonian preserves these classes under time evolution.
The covariant gauge/ghost space before reduction has an indefinite inner product. This is essential: a Hermitian nilpotent BRST charge requires an indefinite auxiliary space if it is nonzero. On an ordinary positive-definite Hilbert space the same norm identity would force Q=0. Positivity of the resulting physical space uses the gauge-theory structure in addition to the abstract BRST nilpotence identities.
For a nonzero on-shell photon momentum, the one-particle conclusion is
kμεμ​=0,εμ​∼εμ​+αkμ​.​
(43)
The second relation preserves the first because k2=0. In four dimensions the transverse kernel has dimension three, and quotienting by its null longitudinal direction leaves two physical photon polarizations. This is the positive one-particle BRST cohomology of the photon sector.
