An N-dimensional stratified pseudomanifold has a closed filtration ∅=X−1​⊆X0​⊆⋯⊆XN​=X with each nonempty Xs​∖Xs−1​ an s-dimensional manifold, dense top stratum, and XN−1​=XN−2​. Each point of an s-dimensional stratum has a stratum-preserving conical neighbourhood Rs×cL, where L is a compact (N−s−1)-dimensional stratified pseudomanifold. The definition is inductive, starting with zero-dimensional spaces. There are no singular codimension-one strata; connectedness of links is not required. One may work in the usual triangulable category used for Witt bordism.
A rational Witt space is such a stratified pseudomanifold for which every stratum of odd codimension 2r+1 has link L satisfying IHrmˉ​(L;Q)=0. No extra condition is imposed at even codimension. A Witt space with collared boundary has Witt interior and Witt boundary, and a stratum-preserving collar ∂X×[0,ε) with the product stratification. An orientation means an orientation of the regular stratum, inducing the boundary orientation on its collar.
A three-pronged graph, with its central vertex and open edges as strata, is a stratified space but not a one-dimensional pseudomanifold: the central vertex is a singular codimension-one stratum, and deleting it leaves three local branches instead of the two branches of a one-manifold. For a non-Witt pseudomanifold, take the suspension of T2. Its two vertices have codimension three and link T2, whose middle group is IH1​(T2;Q)=H1​(T2;Q)=Q2. It violates the Witt space condition.
The rational Witt bordism classification in dimensions divisible by four states that, for every k≥1, the middle intersection form induces an isomorphism
Ω4kWitt​ ∼ ​W(Q),[X]⟼[IH2k(X;Q),λX​].​
(22)
Here W(Q) is the Witt group of a field, so a nonsingular symmetric form represents zero precisely when it is metabolic, that is, when it has a half-dimensional totally isotropic subspace. The Complex projective plane gives a nonzero example: its middle group is generated by the degree-two class h, with ⟨h2,[CP2]⟩=1. Its form is ⟨1⟩, of signature one; a metabolic form has signature zero. Thus [CP2]=0 in Ω4Witt​.
For a closed oriented Witt space X of dimension 2k+1, take the closed cone
Y=(X×[0,1])/(X×{0}),∂Y=X×{1}.
(23)
Away from its vertex it is a stratified product with an interval, so all old Witt space conditions persist. At the new vertex the codimension is 2k+2, which is even; hence its odd-dimensional link X creates no new Witt condition. The collar X×(1−ε,1] is explicit, and the product orientation may be chosen to induce the given orientation on X. This is a compact oriented Witt space with collared boundary, proving
Ω2k+1Witt​=0.​
(24)
Now let dimX=2. Its singular strata are zero-dimensional. Their links are compact one-dimensional pseudomanifolds, which have no singular strata and are therefore nonempty finite disjoint unions of circles. A single circle is allowed, since a chosen stratum need not be intrinsically singular. The Witt condition is automatic in dimension two, because singular strata have even codimension.
Construct the normalization of a two-dimensional pseudomanifold concretely. Remove disjoint small conical neighbourhoods of the finitely many zero-stratum points. The remainder is a compact oriented surface with boundary, with one boundary circle for each local branch. Cap every such circle with a separate disk. The result X is a closed compact oriented topological surface. Map each new disk homeomorphically onto its branch of the original cone, sending all the disk centres belonging to one old singular point to that point. Along the disk boundaries this agrees with the identification with the remainder, so it defines a continuous surjection π:X→X. It is a homeomorphism off these finitely many centres, in particular over the prescribed open stratum. Every fibre is a point except over a zero-stratum point, where it is the finite set of its branch centres. Thus every fibre is zero-dimensional, as required.
Each component of X bounds an oriented handlebody: a surface of genus g is the boundary of a three-ball with g one-handles attached. Let H be the disjoint union of these handlebodies, oriented so that ∂H=X. Form
Z=H∪π​(X×[0,1]),
(25)
gluing x∈∂H to (π(x),0). The quotient is compact and Hausdorff, and its only remaining boundary is X×{1}, with an explicit product collar. Near regular points of the gluing locus, two half-ball neighbourhoods glue to a three-ball; the induced orientations match after choosing the collar orientation in the usual boundary-gluing convention.
It remains to check the singular links, rather than merely assert that this quotient is a Witt space. If an old point p has r circle branches, the new one-dimensional stratum {p}×(0,1) has link the disjoint union of those r circles. This has codimension two and imposes no Witt condition. At (p,0), each branch supplies a half-three-ball from H and another from the collar. The halves glue to a full three-ball; the different branches share only the positive collar axis. Intersecting with a small link therefore gives r copies of S2 identified at one common point. This is a two-dimensional pseudomanifold with zero middle intersection homology: an allowable one-cycle avoids the common point, so decomposes into cycles in the r punctured spheres, each homeomorphic to R2; each such cycle bounds there. Consequently IH1​(⋁a=1r​S2;Q)=0. This is exactly the Witt condition at the new codimension-three stratum. The boundary has the required product stratification. Hence Z is a three-dimensional Witt space with collared boundary with ∂Z≅X, proving
Ω2Witt​=0.​
(26)
For the final product assertion, “product” means a nontrivial product with at least two positive-dimensional factors; a single four-dimensional factor would allow the preceding CP2 counterexample. State the standard rational intersection-homology product fact used here: products of Witt spaces are Witt, their intersection cohomology satisfies the Künneth theorem, and the complementary-degree intersection pairing on elementary tensors is the product of the factor pairings, with the sign (−1)degβdegα′ for α⊗β and α′⊗β′. Terms whose degrees in a factor do not add to that factor's dimension pair to zero. Regrouping factors reduces total dimension four to cases 1+3 or 2+2.
In the 1+3 case, write X=A×B. The middle intersection cohomology is
IH2(X)=(IH0(A)⊗IH2(B))⊕(IH1(A)⊗IH1(B)).
(27)
Each summand is totally isotropic, because the degrees in A are respectively 0+0 and 1+1, neither equal to one. They pair perfectly with one another by factor Poincare duality. Thus either summand has half the total dimension and is a Lagrangian for the middle symmetric form.
For dimensions 2+2, the middle group is the direct sum
(IH0(A)⊗IH2(B))⊕(IH1(A)⊗IH1(B))⊕(IH2(A)⊗IH0(B)).
(28)
The outside summands are isotropic and pair perfectly with one another. Each factor's degree-one pairing is a nonsingular skew-symmetric form, so choose a Lagrangian subspace LA​⊂IH1(A). Inside the central summand, LA​⊗IH1(B) is isotropic and has half its dimension. It is orthogonal to both outside summands by their degrees. Therefore
(IH0(A)⊗IH2(B))⊕(LA​⊗IH1(B))
(29)
is an isotropic subspace of half the full middle dimension. In both cases the form is metabolic. The Witt bordism classification now proves the desired [X]=0 in Ω4Witt​. This also proves the assertion for more factors, using the regrouping just described.
