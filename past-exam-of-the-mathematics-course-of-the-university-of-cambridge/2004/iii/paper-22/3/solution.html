All cohomology below has rational coefficients. First describe the incidence space geometrically. The image of g is P(W)≅CP1, inside Gr(1,4)≅CP3. For a line U⊂W, choosing a plane V containing U is equivalent to choosing the line V/U in C4/U. If L is the tautological line bundle on P(W), then
FW​≅P((P(W)×C4)/L).
(14)
This is the projective bundle of a rank-three quotient bundle, with fibre CP2. Local frames of the quotient bundle give local product charts. In particular FW​ is an oriented real six-dimensional manifold.
For V∈SW​∖{W}, the intersection V∩W is exactly a line, so f−1(V) is a single point and the inverse map is V↦(V∩W,V). The intersection line varies continuously, indeed holomorphically, wherever its dimension is constant: in local Grassmannian charts it is the kernel of a matrix of constant rank, whose kernel bases can be chosen locally. Thus f is an isomorphism over the open stratum. At the remaining point,
f−1(W)=P(W)≅CP1.​
(15)
Set K=Rf∗​QFW​​, a derived direct image of sheaves. The proper base change for sheaves theorem identifies its derived stalks with the cohomology complexes of the fibres. Consequently K is the constant rational sheaf in degree zero on SW​∖{W}, and
Ha(iW∗​K)={Q,0,​a=0,2,a=0,2.​
(16)
The cohomology sheaves are therefore finite-rank local systems on the two strata, with no other nonzero degrees. This proves that K is a constructible complex of sheaves and verifies normalization. The open stratum has real dimension six, so the isolated point has codimension six. Its middle perversity is mˉ(6)=2, and the intersection-complex support and cosupport axioms require stalk vanishing in degrees above two. The fibre calculation verifies the support axiom, as well as vanishing in negative degrees.
For an oriented real six-dimensional manifold, Verdier duality gives DFW​​QFW​​≅QFW​​[6]. For a proper map, Verdier duality commutes with derived direct image. Applying these statements here yields
DSW​​K≅Rf∗​DFW​​QFW​​≅Rf∗​QFW​​[6]=K[6],Σ−6DSW​​K≅K.​
(17)
The duality identity iW!​DSW​​K≅D{W}​iW∗​K converts this into
Ha(iW!​K)≅H6−a(iW∗​K)∗.
(18)
Here iW!​K is the costalk, and dualization of rational complexes reverses degrees. Thus its only nonzero groups are Q in degrees four and six. In particular they vanish for a≤3, which is precisely the cosupport axiom at this isolated six-dimensional singularity. The uniqueness characterization by constructibility, normalization, support and cosupport now identifies K with the unshifted intersection complex of SW​. Taking hypercohomology, and using the composition of derived global sections with Rf∗​, gives
IH∗(SW​)≅H∗(SW​,K)≅H∗(FW​).​
(19)
Finally apply the fibration result allowed in the question to g:FW​→P(W). Its base is the simply connected complex projective line, its fibre is the Complex projective plane, and the projective-bundle charts above verify the fibration hypothesis. Hence, as graded rational vector spaces,
IH∗(SW​)≅H∗(CP1)⊗H∗(CP2).​
(20)
The resulting Betti numbers and Poincaré polynomial are
dimIHi(SW​)=⎩⎨⎧​1,2,0,​i=0,6,i=2,4,otherwise,​PIH​(t)=1+2t2+2t4+t6.​
(21)
This uses H∗(CPr)=Q[h]/(hr+1) with degh=2. No assertion that an arbitrary projective fibration has the product cohomology ring is needed.
