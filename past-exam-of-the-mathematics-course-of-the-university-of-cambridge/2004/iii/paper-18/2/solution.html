Use the positive Laplace-Beltrami operator Δ=−div∇. For the global discrete spectral statements, take M to be a closed manifold with a smooth Riemannian metric. The Riemannian heat kernel is the integral kernel H(t,x,y) of the heat semigroup e−tΔ:
(e−tΔf)(x)=∫M​H(t,x,y)f(y)dVy​,(∂t​+Δx​)H=0,H(0,x,y)=δy​(x).
(6)
The initial condition is distributional with respect to the Riemannian volume form. Equivalently, an orthonormal basis of Laplacian eigenfunctions gives the spectral expansion of the Riemannian heat kernel
H(t,x,y)=∑j​e−tλj​ϕj​(x)ϕj​(y)​.
(7)
The usual compact elliptic spectral theorem supplies this basis; polynomial elliptic regularity bounds and exponential decay prove convergence with every space derivative on t≥ε>0. These analytic results may be used as clearly stated external ingredients. We will also construct the kernel locally and prove its short-time expansion.
On a sufficiently small neighbourhood of the diagonal, let r=d(x,y) and write x=expy​ξ in geodesic normal coordinates, with dVx​=J(y,ξ)dξ. The heat kernel expansion has the form
H(t,x,y)∼(4πt)−m/2e−r2/(4t)∑j≥0​tjuj​(x,y),m=dimM.
(8)
The heat invariants are the integrated diagonal coefficients aj​=∫M​uj​(x,x)dVx​, so
Tr(e−tΔ)∼(4πt)−m/2∑j≥0​aj​tj.
(9)
In particular
a0​=vol(M),a1​=61​∫M​RdV,a2​=3601​∫M​(5R2−2∣Ric∣2+2∣Rm∣2)dV.
(10)
Here R is scalar curvature, Ric is the Ricci tensor and Rm is the Riemann curvature tensor. The last expression is the integrated second scalar heat coefficient; a local divergence term integrates to zero on a closed manifold. The exponent of the leading heat-trace term determines dimension, and these coefficients determine volume, total scalar curvature, and the displayed integrated curvature combination. On a surface R=2K, where K is Gaussian curvature, so a1​=31​∫KdA=32π​χ(M) by the Gauss-Bonnet theorem, and a2​=151​∫K2dA. Thus the Euler characteristic, and the genus of a connected orientable closed surface, are determined. The first three coefficients also detect constant Gaussian curvature, since they determine ∫(K−K)2dA. These conclusions do not claim that the pointwise curvature function or the metric is determined.
Here is the local construction. Put gt​=(4πt)−m/2e−r2/(4t). The Gauss lemma and the radial expression for the geometric Laplacian give, for a smooth amplitude u,
(∂t​+Δx​)(gt​tju)=gt​[tj−1(r∂r​u+2r​∂r​logJu+ju)+tjΔx​u].
(11)
The t−2 terms cancel because ∣∇r∣=1. Define D=r∂r​+(r/2)∂r​logJ. The singular term disappears with u0​=J−1/2, normalized by u0​(y,y)=1. For j≥1, cancel the remaining powers of t by the heat-kernel transport equations
(D+j)uj​=−Δx​uj−1​,
(12)
which have the explicit solution
uj​(expy​ξ,y)=−J(y,ξ)−1/2∫01​sj−1J(y,sξ)1/2(Δx​uj−1​)(expy​(sξ),y)ds.
(13)
This formula is obtained by differentiating rjJ1/2uj​ along each radial geodesic. It proves smoothness jointly in x,y, including the diagonal, by induction: the integrand is smooth on a fixed compact integration interval. A homogeneous solution of (D+j)u=0 has rjJ1/2u constant along a ray, and smoothness at r=0 forces this constant to vanish for j>0. Thus the smooth transport coefficients are unique. In particular uj​(y,y)=−(Δx​uj−1​)(y,y)/j. Expanding J=1−Ricab​(y)ξaξb/6+O(∣ξ∣3) gives u1​(y,y)=R(y)/6, checking the sign convention.
Choose a smooth cutoff η(x,y) that is one near the diagonal and supported where these normal coordinates exist, and form the heat parametrix
PN​(t,x,y)=η(x,y)gt​(x,y)∑j=0N​tjuj​(x,y).
(14)
It tends to the identity kernel as t↓0: in normal coordinates the Gaussian is an approximate identity, and u0​J=J1/2→1 at the centre. Its residual RN​=(∂t​+Δx​)PN​ is ηgt​tNΔx​uN​ plus cutoff-derivative terms supported a positive distance from the diagonal. Those latter terms are smaller than every power of t. Hence RN​=O(tN−m/2) uniformly. Every prescribed finite number of derivatives has the same kind of estimate after increasing N; differentiating a Gaussian costs only finitely many powers of t.
For N large enough that the residual is continuous and bounded up to time zero, use the Volterra parametrix correction. For kernels define
(F∗G)(t,x,y)=∫0t​∫M​F(t−s,x,z)G(s,z,y)dVz​ds.
(15)
Set Q=−RN​+RN​∗RN​−RN​∗RN​∗RN​+⋯. A bounded residual with bound C gives
∣RN∗k​(t,x,y)∣≤Ckvol(M)k−1(k−1)!tk−1​,
(16)
so this series converges and Q+RN​+RN​∗Q=0. Because PN​ has identity initial kernel, differentiating the convolution gives (∂t​+Δx​)(PN​∗Q)=Q+RN​∗Q. Thus
H=PN​+PN​∗Q​
(17)
is an exact kernel with the correct initial condition. Taking N sufficiently large gives any required finite regularity; uniqueness of the heat equation makes the kernels obtained from different N equal. For uniqueness, the difference applied to smooth initial data has zero initial value, and the energy identity dtd​∥v∥22​=−2∥∇v∥22​ forces v=0. Parabolic regularity then gives smoothness for positive time.
The Gaussian part of PN​ has uniformly bounded spatial L1 norm, so the correction is O(tN+1−m/2). This proves, uniformly near the diagonal,
H(t,x,y)−gt​(x,y)∑j=0N​tjuj​(x,y)=O(tN+1−m/2),
(18)
with corresponding derivative estimates on increasing the truncation order. In particular on every parabolic neighbourhood r≤Bt​ it is the stated Gaussian expansion with relative remainder O(tN+1), and on the diagonal it yields the complete heat trace expansion. The standard off-diagonal Gaussian version on compact subsets of a sufficiently small convex normal neighbourhood follows from the same convolution estimates with the Gaussian factor retained: the inequality
t−sd(x,z)2​+sd(z,y)2​≥td(x,y)2​
(19)
localizes the convolution to the unique joining geodesic; its transverse Hessian is positive, and Gaussian integration gives the successive transport orders. The additive estimate above already specifies an appropriate uniform near-diagonal remainder without asserting a relative estimate far from the diagonal on the basis of a crude bound.
If M is noncompact, use the minimal Friedrichs extension heat semigroup; if there is a boundary, specify boundary conditions. The same construction is local in the interior. One may extend a relatively compact coordinate neighbourhood to a closed auxiliary manifold; localization errors are supported a positive distance away and are exponentially small near the smaller diagonal neighbourhood. Thus the local coefficients remain the same. Finite integrated heat invariants and a discrete spectrum require the global hypotheses used above.
