The Sunada theorem is the following. Let X→M0​ be a finite normal Riemannian covering of a connected closed Riemannian manifold, with finite deck transformation group T. If U1​,U2​≤T satisfy Gassmann equivalence, meaning
∣U1​∩C∣=∣U2​∩C∣for every conjugacy class C of T,
(20)
then U1​\X and U2​\X have the same scalar Laplacian spectrum, with multiplicities. Equivalently, an epimorphism π1​(M0​)→T yields isospectral covers corresponding to the inverse images of U1​,U2​.
Here is the representation-theoretic proof. Every finite-dimensional Laplacian eigenspace Eλ​ of X is a group representation of T, since isometries commute with the Laplace-Beltrami operator. Eigenfunctions on Ui​\X identify with EλUi​​. Averaging gives the projection Pi​=∣Ui​∣−1∑u∈Ui​​u, so
dimEλUi​​=∣Ui​∣1​∑u∈Ui​​χλ​(u).
(21)
The two subgroup orders agree, and the character of a representation χλ​ is constant on conjugacy classes. The displayed multiplicities are therefore equal for every λ. This proof also applies when T has fixed points on X, provided the two subgroups Ui​ act freely, so their quotients are smooth manifolds. We shall use this extension for an orbifold base in Question 4.
The precise geometric test for an isometry uses the full isometry group of a common universal cover. Equip the simply connected cover X of M0​ with the lifted metric and let Γi​ be the subgroups of its deck transformation group corresponding to the two covers. Then
Γ1​\X≅Γ2​\X isometrically⟺FΓ1​F−1=Γ2​ for some F∈Isom(X).​
(22)
Indeed, such an F descends to an isometry of the quotients. Conversely an isometry of the quotients lifts to their simply connected covers; lift its inverse with compatible basepoints to see that the lift is bijective. Since it preserves the lifted metrics locally, it is a global isometry, and equivariance of the covering maps shows that it conjugates the deck subgroups. This proves the criterion, rather than assuming that an isometry must preserve the map to M0​. In particular, nonconjugacy inside T alone does not establish nonisometry; the full covering space may have additional isometries. Nonisomorphic fundamental groups are a sufficient topological obstruction, and length-identified geometric configurations provide another.
For a concrete nonisomorphic Gassmann equivalent regular subgroups example, fix an odd prime p and take T=Sp3​. Embed E=(Cp​)3 and B=UT3​(Fp​) through their regular permutation actions. Both have order p3. Every nonidentity element of E has order p. For B, write its elements as I+N with N3=0; then
(I+N)p=I+pN+(2p​)N2=I
(23)
over Fp​, including p=3, so its nonidentity elements also have order p. In a regular action, each nonidentity order-p element has cycle type pp2. Thus each subgroup meets the identity class in one element, the class of that cycle type in p3−1 elements, and every other conjugacy class in zero elements. They are Gassmann equivalent. They are not isomorphic: E is abelian, whereas the Heisenberg group B has [I+E12​,I+E23​]=I+E13​=I.
To obtain arbitrarily many examples in dimension five, choose r distinct odd primes p1​,…,pr​ with 2r≥n. For each prime use the preceding pair Ej​,Bj​≤Spj3​​. Set
T=∏j=1r​Spj3​​,Uε​=∏j=1r​Hj,εj​​,Hj,0​=Ej​, Hj,1​=Bj​,ε∈{0,1}r.
(24)
A conjugacy class in a direct product is a product of conjugacy classes, and its intersection count with Uε​ is the product of the individual counts. Thus all 2r subgroups are Gassmann equivalent. They are pairwise nonisomorphic: the unique Sylow pj​-subgroup of Uε​ is abelian precisely when εj​=0, and an abstract group isomorphism preserves this property at each prime.
We use the closed-manifold realization of a finitely presented fundamental group in dimension five. Its construction here is short. From a finite presentation of T with s generators, start with the connected sum of s copies of S1×S4. Its fundamental group is free on those generators. Represent the finitely many relators by disjoint embedded circles, which is possible by general position for curves in a manifold in dimension five. Their oriented normal bundles are trivial. Perform framed circle surgery, removing S1×D4 and inserting D2×S3. The Seifert-van Kampen theorem shows that each surgery kills exactly the normal closure of the corresponding relator: the attaching circle bounds the new disk, and the new S3 factor contributes no fundamental group. The resulting closed connected five-manifold M0​ has π1​(M0​)=T.
Choose any smooth Riemannian metric on M0​ and lift it to its universal cover X. Since T is finite, X is a compact simply connected five-manifold on which T acts freely and isometrically. The Sunada theorem makes all Uε​\X isospectral, while
π1​(Uε​\X)≅Uε​
(25)
shows that they are pairwise nonhomeomorphic, hence nonisometric. Taking any n members proves for every positive integer n, there are n pairwise nonisometric closed five-manifolds with the same Laplacian spectrum.
