In natural units, periodic Euclidean time of period beta is the inverse temperature of a thermal quantum state. The horizon regularity calculation therefore gives the Hawking temperature
TH​=β+​1​=4πV′(r+​)​=2πκ+​​=πr+3​M2−Q2​​.​
(20)
This agrees with the radiation calculation based on the near-horizon redshift. Restoring constants with c=1 multiplies the expression κ+​/(2π) by ℏ/kB​. In the neutral case TH​=1/(2π2M​); the temperature is positive for every nonextremal outer horizon.
The extremal limit is different. At ∣Q∣=M, V∼4(r−rh​)2/M, so the proper Euclidean radial distance to the horizon diverges logarithmically. This extremal Euclidean throat is an infinite end, with no finite polar origin on which to impose the cone-removal condition. Local smoothness therefore does not select a finite period by the preceding argument. The vanishing surface gravity and the nonextremal limit give TH​=0 at extremality​, rather than a universally nonzero temperature for all horizon solutions. A geometry with ∣Q∣>M has no black-hole horizon and no Hawking temperature inferred from this horizon argument.
