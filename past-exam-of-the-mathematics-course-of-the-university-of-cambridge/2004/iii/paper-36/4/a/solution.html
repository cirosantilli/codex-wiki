Normalize the second price to one and write p>0 for the first price and t>0 for the varying endowment. Let Z(p,t) be first-good aggregate excess demand. Under Walras law, the second excess-demand coordinate equals −pZ(p,t), so one market-clearing equation suffices. A regular exchange economy at fixed t has Zp​(p,t)=0 at every equilibrium.
A sufficient joint condition is that zero be a regular value of the smooth map Z:(0,∞)2→R:
Z(p,t)=0⟹(Zp​(p,t),Zt​(p,t))=(0,0).​
(16)
To prove the almost-everywhere conclusion, the regular level set theorem makes M=Z−1(0) a smooth one-dimensional manifold. Project M to the parameter axis by π(p,t)=t. Its tangent space consists of vectors (h,k) with Zp​h+Zt​k=0. If Zp​=0, every k is possible, so the derivative of π is surjective. If Zp​=0, the joint condition gives Zt​=0, forcing k=0; the point is critical for π.
Thus the parameters with a singular equilibrium are exactly the critical values of this projection. Sard theorem makes that set a null set. Therefore almost every parameter gives a regular economy. No claim of existence at every parameter is needed for this sufficient regularity criterion.
