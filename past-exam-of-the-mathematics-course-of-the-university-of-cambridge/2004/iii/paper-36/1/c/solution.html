First pass from the preorder R to a partial order. Define the indifference relation y∼z by yRz and zRy. Reflexivity and transitivity make this an equivalence relation. On the quotient set C=RL/∼, put
[y]⊵[z]⟺yRz.
(4)
Transitivity makes this independent of the representatives, and the quotient relation is antisymmetric, hence a partial order.
We need its order-extension property, including freedom to order incomparable classes. Here is the argument. Order all partial order extensions of ⊵ by inclusion. The union of a chain of extensions is again a partial order: any finite list of comparisons needed to check an axiom occurs together in one member of the chain. Zorn's lemma therefore supplies a maximal extension. If two classes A,B remain incomparable, adjoin A⊵B and take the transitive closure of a relation. The only possible new comparisons have the form X⊵A⊵B⊵Y. A cycle would require a previously existing path B⊵A, contradicting incomparability. The enlarged relation is still a partial order, contradicting maximality. Consequently the maximal extension is a total order. This also proves the Szpilrajn extension theorem used here.
Lift such a total order to the original bundles by comparing their equivalence classes. The lifted preference relation R′ is complete and transitive, contains R, and preserves its strict preference: if yPz, their distinct classes satisfy [y]⊵[z], and antisymmetry of the extended order prevents the reverse comparison. In particular, the class P is nonempty and R⊆⋂R′∈P​R′.
For the reverse inclusion, take a pair with ¬(yRz). If zRy, then zPy, so every member of P strictly ranks z above y and excludes yR′z. If neither comparison holds, their classes are incomparable. First adjoin [z]⊵[y], which creates no cycle by the preceding argument, and then extend to a total order. Its lift belongs to P and again excludes yR′z. Thus every pair outside R is outside at least one member of P, proving
R′∈P⋂​R′=R.​
(5)
The intersection proof is an order-theoretic fact about any preorder. The congruence axiom additionally ensures exact rationalization of the observed demand correspondence: every chosen bundle weakly outranks every affordable bundle, while an affordable unchosen bundle is strictly worse by part (b). Hence each completion's budget-maximizing set is exactly the observed chosen set.
