The measurable structure of an exchange economy includes measurability of consumers' preference relations: in particular, for each rational vector q, the set
Eq​={a:f(a)+q∈R+L​, f(a)+q≻a​f(a)}
(8)
is measurable. Continuity of each individual's preference relation alone would not imply this across-consumer requirement. We use this usual measurability convention explicitly.
There are only countably many vectors in QL. Remove all the null sets Eq​ that have zero measure, obtaining
D=A∖⋃μ(Eq​)=0​Eq​,μ(D)=1.
(9)
Let V={q∈QL:μ(Eq​)>0}. Equivalently, V is the union over a∈D of the rational strict-improvement directions available to consumer a. This choice of D ensures that a direction available to even one consumer in D is available to a positive-measure group.
We claim that 0∈/coV. Otherwise, take finitely many q1​,…,qm​∈V and positive weights λi​ with ∑i​λi​=1 and ∑i​λi​qi​=0. Choose t>0 so small that 2t<mini​μ(Eqi​​). We can choose disjoint measurable sets Fi​⊆Eqi​​ with μ(Fi​)=tλi​. Indeed, after the earlier choices remove at most t mass, the remaining portion of Eqi​​ has mass greater than t. The scalar case of the Lyapunov convexity theorem, applied to its restricted atomless measure, realizes every mass between zero and its total, including tλi​.
Now change f(a) to f(a)+qi​ on Fi​ and leave it unchanged elsewhere. Every changed bundle is a feasible consumption bundle and is strictly preferred by its recipient. This new consumption allocation g remains integrable, and
∫A​(g−f)dμ=∑i​μ(Fi​)qi​=t∑i​λi​qi​=0.
(10)
Thus g is exactly feasible and improves a group of total mass t>0, contradicting Pareto efficiency. Notice that no individual's consumption bundles were averaged: an atomless measure allows different small cohorts to make different improvements. Individual convexity of the preference relations is unnecessary.
If V is nonempty, separate its convex hull from zero using the supporting hyperplane theorem. There exists p=0 with p⋅q≥0 for every q∈V. Only weak separation is needed, so it is harmless if zero belongs to the closure of this convex hull.
Fix any a∈D and any y≻a​f(a). Completeness and continuity make the strictly preferred set relatively open in R+L​. Approximate y−f(a) by rational vectors qn​ for which f(a)+qn​ is feasible and still strictly preferred. At a zero consumption coordinate, choose the rational approximations from the feasible side. Then a∈Eqn​​ implies qn​∈V, and passage to the limit gives p⋅(y−f(a))≥0. The same full-measure set D works for all such bundles y, not merely for each bundle separately.
If V is empty, the same rational approximation argument says that consumers in D have no strictly preferred feasible bundles at all. In that case any nonzero p satisfies the required implication. In either case,
(f,p) is a price quasi-equilibrium with transfers for some p=0.​
(11)
To see why transfers cannot simply be omitted, give all consumers endowment (1,1) and utility function u(x1​,x2​)=x1​+x2​. Allocate (3/2,3/2) to half of them and (1/2,1/2) to the other half. This is Pareto efficient, since any Pareto improvement would increase the integral of utility while aggregate resources fix it. At these interior bundles the support inequalities force p1​=p2​>0: directions arbitrarily close to (1,−1) force equality, and strict increases force positivity. The poorer half then receives less wealth than the original endowment supplies and has a cheaper strict improvement within its original budget. The support theorem concerns the redistributed budget, as defined in part (b).
