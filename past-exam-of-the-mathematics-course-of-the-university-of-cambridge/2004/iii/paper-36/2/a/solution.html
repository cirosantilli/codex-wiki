Use the exact-resource-balance convention for an exchange economy. If ω is the integrable initial-endowment profile, a feasible consumption allocation is a measurable integrable function f:A→R+L​ with
∫A​fdμ=∫A​ωdμ.
(6)
It is Pareto efficient if there is no other feasible consumption allocation g satisfying g(a)⪰a​f(a) for almost every consumer and g(a)≻a​f(a) on a set of positive measure. A Pareto improvement makes nobody worse off, up to a null set, and makes a positive-measure group strictly better off. Positive measure is essential: reallocating to a single consumer of zero mass does not constitute an aggregate Pareto improvement.
