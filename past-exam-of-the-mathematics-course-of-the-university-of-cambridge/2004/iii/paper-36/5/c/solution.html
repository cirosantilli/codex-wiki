No: uniform convergence of utilities on bounded sets does not imply closed convergence of preferences. It does imply the upper condition. Indeed, suppose xj​→x, yj​→y and unj​​(xj​)≥unj​​(yj​). These bundles all lie in one bounded ball. Uniform convergence there, together with continuity of u, gives
u(x)=limj​unj​​(xj​)≥limj​unj​​(yj​)=u(y),
(29)
so x⪰y.
For a strict limiting comparison u(x)>u(y), the fixed pair itself satisfies un​(x)>un​(y) eventually. The obstruction is the lower condition at an indifference relation, where the strict approximating rankings need not disappear.
For a counterexample valid in every dimension L≥1, take
u(x)=0,un​(x)=nx1​​(x∈R+L​).
(30)
On ∥x∥≤k, ∣un​(x)−u(x)∣≤k/n→0, exactly as required. Every un​ induces x⪰n​y if and only if x1​≥y1​, while u makes every pair indifferent. In particular, 0⪰e1​ holds in the limit. Any sequences xn​→0, yn​→e1​ eventually have (xn​)1​<(yn​)1​, so they cannot satisfy xn​⪰n​yn​. The lower graph-limit condition fails.
Equivalently, an open neighborhood of (0,e1​) sufficiently small to keep the first coordinate of its first bundle below that of its second bundle meets the limiting graph and none of the approximating graphs. The corresponding hit-open neighborhood in the Fell topology proves failure of convergence directly. Hence
the upper condition always holds, but the lower condition need not hold.​
(31)
The uniform utility convergence need not preserve closed preference convergence phenomenon reflects the loss of ranking information when the limiting utility has a flat indifference region.
