For the addition of angular momentum, the uncoupled orthonormal basis is ∣j1​m1​⟩⊗∣j2​m2​⟩, whereas the coupled orthonormal basis diagonalizes J2 and J3​ for J=J1​+J2​. Define the Clebsch-Gordan coefficients by
∣JM⟩=∑m1​,m2​​Cm1​m2​JM​∣j1​m1​⟩∣j2​m2​⟩,Cm1​m2​JM​=⟨j1​m1​,j2​m2​∣JM⟩.
(1)
The Clebsch-Gordan decomposition allows ∣j1​−j2​∣≤J≤j1​+j2​ in unit steps, and a coefficient vanishes unless m1​+m2​=M. Taking the inner product of the expansion with itself proves
∑m1​,m2​​∣Cm1​m2​JM​∣2=⟨JM∣JM⟩=1.
(2)
With the customary real phase convention the modulus signs can be omitted, giving the sum of squares used in the question. In an arbitrary phase convention the modulus-square identity is the invariant statement.
Treat the strong interaction as exactly isospin-invariant for this calculation. Its transition operator T commutes with the three isospin generators and hence with their quadratic Casimir operator. Acting on the initial state ∣IM⟩, it can therefore produce only final states with the same I and M. The tensor product representation of final multiplets I1​,I2​ contains one copy of each permitted total isospin, so T∣IM⟩=aM​∣(I1​I2​)IM⟩. Commuting T with the lowering operator gives
aM​(I+M)(I−M+1)​=aM−1​(I+M)(I−M+1)​.
(3)
Every connecting ladder factor is nonzero, so all aM​ coincide with one reduced coefficient a. Expanding the final coupled state now gives the isospin-invariant two-body decay amplitude
AM1​M2​;M​=a⟨I1​M1​,I2​M2​∣IM⟩.​
(4)
The reduced coefficient may depend on the particle species, momenta and spin channel, but not on the three isospin projections. Summing the squared amplitude over the orthogonal charge channels gives
M1​,M2​∑​∣AM1​M2​;M​∣2=∣a∣2.​
(5)
A physical decay rate also includes phase space, angular integration and any spin sums. In the isospin-degenerate limit these factors are common to the charge channels; absorbing their square root into a gives ΓIM​=∣a∣2 for this specified two-body multiplet channel. If several distinct species or independent spin channels are included in “anything”, their reduced rates must additionally be summed. There is no extra factor 2I+1 when the initial magnetic component is fixed.
The Delta baryon states have I=3/2 and projections 3/2,1/2,−1/2,−3/2 for Δ++,Δ+,Δ0,Δ−. The proton and neutron form I=1/2, with projections +1/2,−1/2, and the pion triplet has I=1 with projections +1,0,−1. The highest coupled state is
∣3/2,3/2⟩=∣pπ+⟩.
(6)
Apply the total lowering operator I−​=I−(N)​+I−(π)​. Its coefficient on the left is 3​, while on the right the nucleon and pion coefficients are 1 and 2​:
3​∣3/2,1/2⟩=∣nπ+⟩+2​∣pπ0⟩.
(7)
The squared Clebsch-Gordan coefficients are consequently 1/3 and 2/3, compared with coefficient one for the Δ++ channel. Thus the Delta baryon pion branching ratios are
Γ(Δ++→pπ+)Γ(Δ+→pπ0)​=32​,Γ(Δ++→pπ+)Γ(Δ+→nπ+)​=31​.​
(8)
At the opposite end of the same isospin multiplet, ∣3/2,−3/2⟩=∣nπ−⟩, so the strong decay is Δ−→nπ−, with the same reduced rate in this symmetry limit. Physical mass splittings and electromagnetic effects perturb these idealized equal-kinematics ratios.
