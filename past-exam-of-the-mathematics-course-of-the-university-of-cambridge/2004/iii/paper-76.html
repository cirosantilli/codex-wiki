The outer solution satisfies y(x)[x+y(x)]=0, so on a connected interior interval the nonoscillatory branches are y=0 and y=−x. Both happen to solve the differential equation exactly away from any endpoint mismatch. For a fixed positive endpoint coordinate q (q=a or q=1), introduce the inward distance ξ=(x−a)/ϵ​ on the left or ξ=(1−x)/ϵ​ on the right. Thus the leading admissibility condition for the negative outer branch is α≤a/2 and β≤1/2. Under strict inequalities, each endpoint has two choices when its datum lies between −q and q/2, and one choice below −q. Their independent combinations give one, two or four formal matched profiles. The endpoint equalities and the constant-layer cases are degenerate; higher-order endpoint matching is needed to decide exact finite-ϵ counts at these cutoffs. These conditions and counts concern leading families under the stated exclusion of interior layers and rapid oscillations.

Additional interior layers cannot be excluded merely by counting the outer roots. Freezing x=q gives a homoclinic orbit to −q with the same hyperbolic-secant pulse. Its location would require a further matching or solvability condition; it is not an arbitrary parameter in the original variable-coefficient problem. Indeed, put z=y+x. The exact equation and an energy identity are
ϵz′′−xz+z2=0,dxd​[2ϵ​(z′)2−2x​z2+31​z3]=−21​z2.

When a=α=0, the two outer roots meet at the left endpoint. Let x=ϵpX, y=ϵqY. Balancing xy with y2 gives p=q, and balancing ϵy′′ with them gives 1−q=2q. The one-third-power scaling at a nonlinear turning endpoint is therefore

Assume γ>0, the positive diffusivity requirement for the forward heat kernel. A normal mode eikx−iωt of the linear complex Ginzburg-Landau equation gives
D(k,ω)=−iω+iUk−μ+γk2=0,ω(k)=Uk+i(μ−γk2).
(52)
For real k, the maximum temporal growth rate is μ. The Briggs-Bers criterion starts the inverse time Laplace transform above the temporal growth spectrum and follows the two spatial roots as its frequency contour is lowered. A contributing spatial pinch point occurs when roots continued from opposite sides of the spatial contour coalesce and prevent its deformation. An arbitrary algebraic saddle is not sufficient without this branch selection.
Here D=Dk​=0 gives
k0​=−2γiU​,ω0​=i(μ−4γU2​)​.
(53)
To check the pinch, start at ω=iΩ with Ω large. The roots are k=−iU/(2γ)±iU2+4γ(Ω−μ)​/(2γ) and initially lie in opposite half-planes. They meet at Ω=μ−U2/(4γ), giving the causal saddle. Positive imaginary absolute frequency means fixed-position exponential growth. Therefore
absolute hydrodynamic instability: μ>4γU2​​,convective hydrodynamic instability: 0<μ<4γU2​​.
(54)
For μ≤0 there is no positive temporal growth. At μ=U2/(4γ) the fixed-position exponential rate is zero; its algebraic prefactor, derived below, decays. When U=0 the convective interval is empty.
The source specifies only real coefficients. For γ<0, temporal growth is unbounded at high real wavenumber, so the usual causal forward-diffusion construction is ill posed and the above criterion does not apply. For γ=0 the equation reduces to transport with amplification; its point impulse remains a moving delta, rather than a spreading heat kernel. These cases cannot be included by division by γ.

Select the causal Green function, zero for t<0; otherwise the impulse equation permits arbitrary homogeneous additions. Use the spatial Fourier transform convention e−ikx and the temporal Laplace transform boundary convention eiωt. The transformed impulse equation is
G(k,ω)=−iω+iUk−μ+γk21​.
(55)
If μ>0, a literal real-frequency Fourier transform of the growing response need not exist. Its causal inverse instead starts on Imω=c>max(μ,0) and is continued from there. For t>0 close the frequency contour below; its clockwise orientation and the pole residue 1/(−i)=i give e−iω(k)t. For t<0 the upper closure contains no pole. The unit-mass Gaussian heat kernel tends to δ(x) at t↓0, giving the required impulse normalization.
