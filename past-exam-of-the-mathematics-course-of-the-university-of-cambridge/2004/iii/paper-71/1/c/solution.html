If polynomials with integer monomial coefficients converge uniformly to f, their values at zero and one are integers converging to f(0) and f(1). A convergent integer sequence is eventually constant: once all its terms lie in an interval of length less than one they must agree. Hence both endpoint values are integers.
Conversely assume those values are integers. The rounded Bernstein polynomial is an integer-coefficient polynomial, because its displayed scalar coefficients are integers and each xk(1−x)n−k expands with integer coefficients. Part b gives its vanishing distance from Bn​f. For completeness, the remaining uniform convergence Bn​f→f follows directly from uniform continuity.
