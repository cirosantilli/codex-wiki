Let y=(f(x1​),…,f(xn​))T and write the interpolating spline as Px​f=∑j=1n​cj​Nj​. The interpolation equations are Ax​c=y, so c=Ax−1​y. Since Px​ is defined as a unique interpolant for arbitrary data, its B-spline collocation matrix is invertible. Ordered sites satisfying diagonal positivity ensure this by question 7. Positivity alone without admissible ordering does not: two equal sites in overlapping basis supports could give two identical rows. The norm argument below uses the existence of the operator stipulated here.
Standard partition-normalized B-splines satisfy Nj​≥0 and ∑j=1n​Nj​≤1. The latter is a subpartition of unity for B-splines: extending the knot sequence gives a full partition of unity, and discarding basis functions cannot increase its sum. Hence
∣Px​f(t)∣≤∑j​∣cj​∣Nj​(t)≤∥c∥ℓ∞​≤∥Ax−1​∥ℓ∞​∥y∥ℓ∞​≤∥Ax−1​∥ℓ∞​∥f∥∞​.
(19)
Taking the supremum over t and then over unit-norm f gives the B-spline interpolation operator norm estimate
∥Px​∥L∞​≤∥Ax−1​∥ℓ∞​.​
(20)
Here the matrix norm is the induced maximum-row-sum norm, not the maximum of its individual entries. The proof only needs nonnegative basis functions, their sum at most one, and invertible interpolation.
