Past exam of the mathematics course of the University of Cambridge / 2004 / iii / Paper 58 / 4 / Solution

Use ambient quadratic form Q=−(X0)2−(X4)2+(X1)2+(X2)2. The printed sine coordinate has a duplicated superscript: it must be X2, not another X1. With that correction the embedding lies on Q=−1. Put s=1+r2​. Differentiating the two timelike coordinates gives
(dX0)2+(dX4)2=1+r2r2​dr2+(1+r2)dt2,
(19)
whereas the two spatial coordinates give (dX1)2+(dX2)2=dr2+r2dθ2. Their difference is the induced Lorentzian metric
g=−(1+r2)dt2+1+r2dr2​+r2dθ2.
(20)
The polar coordinate θ collapses at r=0; this is a coordinate degeneracy, not a degeneracy of the metric.
For the matrix model of anti-de Sitter three-space, define
M(X)=(X4+X1X2−X0​X2+X0X4−X1​).
(21)
Then detM=−Q. This linear identification of ambient space with real two-by-two matrices identifies Q=−1 with SL(2,R). Its inverse recovers each X by sums and differences of matrix entries, so the identification is global. The identity corresponds to X4=1 and the other coordinates zero. For a traceless tangent matrix U, the induced squared norm is
gI​(U,U)=−detU=21​tr(U2).
(22)
Polarization gives gI​(U,V)=21​tr(UV).
The Killing form of sl2​(R) is B(U,V)=4tr(UV). This normalization can be checked without assuming it: for H=diag(1,−1), E=(00​10​) and F=(01​00​), the brackets are [H,E]=2E, [H,F]=−2F, [E,F]=H. Taking traces of their three-dimensional adjoint matrices gives B(H,H)=8, B(E,F)=B(F,E)=4, and all other basis pairings zero. These agree with 4tr(UV).
Both left and right multiplication by matrices in SL(2,R) preserve −detM, hence its polarized ambient bilinear form and the induced metric. Equality at the identity therefore propagates throughout the group:
g=81​B.​
(23)
In particular the Killing-form Einstein metric calculation gives Ric=−2g, consistent with unit-radius three-dimensional Anti-de Sitter spacetime.
Every element of SO(2,2) preserves the ambient quadratic form, hence the hyperboloid and its induced metric, and so acts by isometries. Define the left-right double cover of SO0(2,2) by
Φ(A,B)(M)=AMB−1,A,B∈SL(2,R).
(24)
This is a group homomorphism, since successive actions multiply the two factors in the same order. It preserves detM; as a transformation of the four-dimensional matrix space it has determinant (detA)2(detB−1)2=1. Thus it takes values in SO(2,2).
If its action is the identity, applying it to M=I gives A=B. Commuting with every matrix then forces A to be scalar. The condition detA=1 leaves only A=I or A=−I. Hence
kerΦ={(I,I),(−I,−I)}.
(25)
The differential is (a,b):M↦aM−Mb. A zero differential implies a=b and that a is scalar; tracelessness makes a=b=0. It is therefore an isomorphism onto the target Lie algebra, since both dimensions are six. The image is an open subgroup. Since SL(2,R) is connected, the image is connected and is exactly the identity component SO0​(2,2): an open subgroup of a connected group is also closed and must be the whole group. Thus the precise covering statement is
SO0​(2,2)≅(SL(2,R)×SL(2,R))/{±(I,I)}.​
(26)
It is not onto the disconnected full SO(2,2). For example, conjugation M↦DMD−1, D=diag(1,−1), sends (X0,X4,X1,X2) to (−X0,X4,X1,−X2) and belongs to the other component of SO(2,2). It reverses the orientation of the negative two-plane and is not in the image of the connected source.
The Adjoint representation of a Lie group of SL(2,R) preserves its Killing form of metric signature (2,1) and gives a second double cover,
SL(2,R)/{±I}≅SO0​(2,1).​
(27)
Its kernel is the scalar centre; its differential is injective and both dimensions are three, so the same connected-image argument proves surjectivity onto the identity component. The full SO(2,1) again has an additional component. Thus SO0​(2,1) is locally the same Lie group geometry as SL(2,R), with its Killing metric, and globally is its central quotient.
With the printed periodic time, the group model has topology S1×R2 and closed timelike curves, since varying t at fixed spatial position gives a timelike circle. The causally unwrapped version of Anti-de Sitter spacetime instead has t∈R and is the universal covering Lie group. It must not be substituted silently for the periodic spacetime specified here.
