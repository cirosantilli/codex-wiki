Let ηab​=g(ea​,eb​) be the constant diagonal metric matrix in a local pseudo-orthonormal frame, and define θab​=ηac​θcb​. The metric compatibility of the Levi-Civita connection gives
0=dηab​=g(∇ea​,eb​)+g(ea​,∇eb​)=θba​+θab​.
(35)
Thus the lowered connection matrix is antisymmetric, including in indefinite signature. Ordinary matrix antisymmetry of θab​ itself is not the corresponding assertion in indefinite signature.
Let ωa be the dual coframe, and write a vector field as Y=eb​ωb(Y). Then
ωa(∇X​Y)=X(ωa(Y))+θab​(X)ωb(Y).
(36)
Subtract the same expression with X,Y exchanged and subtract ωa([X,Y]). By the definition of the exterior derivative, this gives the components of the torsion tensor:
ωa(∇X​Y−∇Y​X−[X,Y])=dωa(X,Y)+(θab​∧ωb)(X,Y).
(37)
The Levi-Civita connection is torsion-free, so Cartan's first structure equation is
dωa+θab​∧ωb=0.​
(38)
Use R(X,Y)=∇X​∇Y​−∇Y​∇X​−∇[X,Y]​ and define its matrix of curvature two-forms by R(X,Y)eb​=ea​Rab​(X,Y). Applying two covariant derivatives to eb​ yields
Rab​(X,Y)=X(θab​(Y))−Y(θab​(X))−θab​([X,Y])+θac​(X)θcb​(Y)−θac​(Y)θcb​(X).
(39)
The first three terms are dθab​(X,Y) and the last two are (θac​∧θcb​)(X,Y). Therefore Cartan's second structure equation is
Rab​=dθab​+θac​∧θcb​.​
(40)
The associated Riemann curvature tensor components satisfy Rab​=21​Rabcd​ωc∧ωd with this convention.
For a position-dependent change of pseudo-orthonormal frame, write the frame row as E′=EΛ, with ΛTηΛ=η. The dual coframe obeys ω′=Λ−1ω. Applying the Leibniz rule to ∇(EΛ) gives
θ′=Λ−1θΛ+Λ−1dΛ.
(41)
The connection one-form has an inhomogeneous term because differentiating the moving basis produces dΛ. The curvature two-form instead transforms homogeneously. Indeed, curvature is tensorial in the vector to which it is applied, so R(X,Y)(EΛ)=(R(X,Y)E)Λ=E′Λ−1R(X,Y)Λ. Consequently
R′=Λ−1RΛ,R′ab​=(Λ−1)ac​Rcd​Λdb​.​
(42)
Equivalently, substituting the transformed connection into dθ′+θ′∧θ′ and using dΛ−1=−Λ−1(dΛ)Λ−1 cancels all derivative terms and gives the same result. This transformation law holds for a general invertible frame change as well; the orthogonality condition ensures both frames remain pseudo-orthonormal.
