A Poisson manifold is a smooth manifold with an antisymmetric contravariant tensor field π=21​πij∂i​∧∂j​ such that the bracket
{f,g}=πij(∂i​f)(∂j​g)
(12)
satisfies the Jacobi identity. Bilinearity, antisymmetry and the Leibniz rule follow directly from this formula. The Poisson bivector is allowed to be degenerate.
Apply the Jacobi identity to coordinate functions xi,xj,xk. Necessity gives the coordinate Jacobi condition for a Poisson bivector:
Jijk:=πiℓ∂ℓ​πjk+πjℓ∂ℓ​πki+πkℓ∂ℓ​πij=0.​
(13)
For sufficiency, expand {f,{g,h}}+{g,{h,f}}+{h,{f,g}}. The terms differentiating π are Jijkfi​gj​hk​. All terms containing second derivatives cancel. For example, those containing gjℓ​ have coefficient (πiℓπjk+πkℓπij)fi​hk​, which is antisymmetric under j↔ℓ after contraction with fi​hk​, whereas gjℓ​ is symmetric. The same argument handles the second derivatives of f and h. Thus the displayed condition is both necessary and sufficient.
A symplectic manifold has a smooth nondegenerate closed differential form Ω of degree two. Write Ω=21​Ωij​dxi∧dxj and choose the explicit inverse convention πikΩkj​=δji​. This defines a smooth antisymmetric bivector. Differentiating the inverse matrix gives
∂ℓ​πij=−πia(∂ℓ​Ωab​)πbj.
(14)
Substitution into the Jacobi coefficient, followed by relabeling summed indices, gives
Jijk=πiaπjbπkc(∂a​Ωbc​+∂b​Ωca​+∂c​Ωab​)=0,
(15)
since the parentheses are the components of dΩ. Hence every symplectic manifold carries a Poisson structure. The opposite overall convention for the inverse bracket gives the same conclusion. Nondegeneracy and closure together are crucial; an arbitrary invertible antisymmetric tensor need not satisfy the Jacobi identity.
For a finite-dimensional Lie algebra g, its dual space g∗ carries the Lie-Poisson bracket
{f,g}(ℓ)=ℓ([dfℓ​,dgℓ​]),
(16)
where dfℓ​,dgℓ​ are identified with elements of g. If [ei​,ej​]=cij​kek​ and xi​(ℓ)=ℓ(ei​), this reads {xi​,xj​}=cij​kxk​. The Jacobi coefficient is
(ciℓ​mcjk​ℓ+cjℓ​mcki​ℓ+ckℓ​mcij​ℓ)xm​,
(17)
which vanishes by the Lie algebra Jacobi identity.
In particular, the dual of so(3) is R3 with the rotational Lie-Poisson structure on R3,
{x,y}=z,{y,z}=x,{z,x}=y,(πij)=​0−zy​z0−x​−yx0​​.
(18)
The Poisson bivector has rank two away from the origin and rank zero at the origin. The function x2+y2+z2 is a Casimir function, since its bracket with every function vanishes. The centred spheres are its two-dimensional symplectic leaves, and the origin is a point leaf. This is a Poisson manifold but not a symplectic manifold: its bivector is not invertible, and an odd-dimensional manifold cannot have a nondegenerate alternating bilinear form.
