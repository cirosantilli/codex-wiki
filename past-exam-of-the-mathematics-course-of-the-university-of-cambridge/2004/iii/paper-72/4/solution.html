For antiplane shear, the infinitesimal displacement is (0,0,w(x1​,x2​)). The isotropic linear elasticity constitutive law gives σ13​=μw,1​ and σ23​=μw,2​, together with the symmetric entries σ31​,σ32​. All other stress tensor components vanish. Static force balance without body force becomes μ(w,11​+w,22​)=0, so w satisfies the Laplace equation.
Locally choose a harmonic conjugate ϕ by ϕ,1​=w,2​ and ϕ,2​=−w,1​. Compatibility follows from the Laplace equation. These are the Cauchy-Riemann equations for F=ϕ+iw, and hence
F′(z)=ϕ,1​+iw,1​=w,2​+iw,1​,σ23​+iσ13​=μF′(z).​
(29)
Thus the complex stress potential for antiplane shear supplies both stresses and, by integration, the displacement. A global potential must have the displacement periods appropriate to the cracked body; these periods also help fix homogeneous solutions.
For the additional crack-induced field, set G=μF′ and p(t)=σ23A​(t,0). Only p loads the prospective faces, since σ13A​ is tangential to their normal. Denote upper and lower boundary values by G+,G−. The imposed cancellation of face tractions is ReG±=−p. The traction data and geometry are invariant under the displacement reflection w(x1​,x2​)↦−w(x1​,−x2​). For the induced correction with no independently added loading, uniqueness up to a rigid translation allows the odd displacement choice. Then w,2​ is even, w,1​ is odd, and G−=G+ on the real axis. Therefore the Hilbert problem for an antiplane crack is
G++G−=−2p on cracks,G+−G−=0 on intact portions.​
(30)
The second condition expresses continuation through uncracked material. There G is real and the additional σ13​ vanishes. This scalar Riemann-Hilbert problem is supplemented by at most inverse-square-root stress singularities at the crack tips and the usual correction with no additional remote resultant or imposed dislocation. Without a far-field or period normalization, the face conditions alone permit homogeneous terms. The formulas below select this normalized induced field and explicitly identify the excluded terms.
