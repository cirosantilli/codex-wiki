Put r=∣x∣ and s=F⋅x. In the Unscaled Papkovich–Neuber representation, take the harmonic potentials Φ=−F/(8πμr) and χ=0. Differentiating s/r gives the Stokeslet velocity and its pressure:
u=8πμ1​(rF​+r3sx​)​,p=4πr3s​.
(1)
Indeed, writing K=1/(8πμ),
∂j​ui​=K[−r3Fi​xj​​+r3Fj​xi​​+r3sδij​​−r53sxi​xj​​].
(2)
The antisymmetric first two terms cancel in the rate-of-strain tensor, whereas their curl supplies the vorticity. Thus
eij​=8πμr3s​(δij​−r23xi​xj​​),ω=4πμr3F×x​​.
(3)
The rate-of-strain tensor has zero trace, as required by incompressibility. The stress is σij​=−3sxi​xj​/(4πr5); integrating its traction with normal x/r over a sphere gives −F. This fixes the force normalization of the Stokeslet.
For a sphere in a uniform straining Stokes flow, write the ambient velocity as Ex, where E is symmetric and trace-free, and set q=x⋅Ex. Symmetry gives zero translation and rotation of the centred force-free, torque-free sphere. Seek a decaying disturbance using the Unscaled Papkovich–Neuber representation with
Φ=Ca3r3Ex​,χ=Da5r5q​.
(4)
The components of Ex/r3 are derivatives of 1/r, and q/r5 is a trace-free harmonic quadrupole, so both potentials satisfy Laplace's equation outside the sphere. Direct differentiation yields
u′=−3Ca3r5qx​+2Da5r5Ex​−5Da5r7qx​.
(5)
The no-slip boundary condition at r=a requires the coefficient of Ex to be −1 and that of qx to vanish: 2D=−1, −3C−5D=0. Hence D=−1/2, C=5/6, and
u′=−r5a5​Ex−2r55a3​(1−r2a2​)qx​,p′=−r55μa3q​.
(6)
This disturbance cancels the ambient velocity at r=a, decays at infinity, and has a leading stresslet proportional to a3E.
For the two-sphere hydrodynamic interaction, let R=Rn point from the forced sphere to the second sphere. The first sphere's leading far field is the Stokeslet with F=6πμaU0​. Its incident rate-of-strain tensor at the second sphere is
E2​=4R33a​(U0​⋅R)(I−3nn),E2​:RR=−2R3a​(U0​⋅R).
(7)
The force-free sphere translates with the local uniform incident flow, and the torque-free sphere rotates with half the incident vorticity. Those parts cause no leading Stokeslet or rotlet disturbance. The remaining incident strain produces the stresslet just calculated. At displacement −R from the second sphere its velocity is
ΔU=2R55a3​(E2​:RR)R=−4R615a4​(U0​⋅R)R.
(8)
The first sphere translates in this returned incident flow. Finite-size sampling and higher incident multipoles enter only beyond the retained reflection order. Thus the stresslet reflection between two force-free and forced spheres gives
U=U0​−4R615a4​(U0​⋅R)R+O(∣U0​∣(a/R)5)​.
(9)
The factor ∣U0​∣ makes explicit the velocity scale implicit in the printed remainder.
The resulting input power, equal to the viscous dissipation, is
F⋅U=6πμa[∣U0​∣2−4R615a4​(U0​⋅R)2]+higher orders.
(10)
It decreases at fixed force. The Minimum-dissipation theorem for Stokes flow instead compares admissible fields at fixed first-sphere velocity: extending a two-sphere field rigidly into the second sphere gives an admissible one-sphere field, so the extra rigid inclusion can only increase the minimum resistance. Allowing the second sphere to choose its translation and rotation minimizes over its rigid motions, producing zero force and couple there. An increase of this effective hydrodynamic resistance matrix decreases its inverse hydrodynamic mobility matrix, and hence decreases fixed-force power. The negative mobility correction is therefore consistent with minimum dissipation.
If the second sphere is prevented from rotating, its angular velocity relative to the incident fluid becomes
Ωrel​=−21​ω1​(R)=−4R33a​U0​×R.
(11)
Use the rotating sphere in Stokes flow at −R. The additional reflected rotlet gives
ΔUrot​=4R63a4​(U0​×R)×R=−4R43a4​[U0​−(U0​⋅n)n].
(12)
Adding this rotation-clamped reflection between two spheres to the strain reflection,
U=U0​−R4a4​[415​(U0​⋅n)n+43​(U0​−(U0​⋅n)n)]+O(∣U0​∣(a/R)5)​.
(13)
The holding couple supplies no power because the held sphere's angular velocity is zero.
