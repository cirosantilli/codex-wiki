Let (u(1),σ(1)) and (u(2),σ(2)) be two Stokes flows in the same fluid domain, with equal dynamic viscosity and zero body force. The Lorentz reciprocal theorem for Stokes flow is
∫∂V​u(1)⋅σ(2)ndS=∫∂V​u(2)⋅σ(1)ndS.
(25)
To prove it, use the symmetric Newtonian fluid stress tensor σ(k)=−p(k)I+2μe(k) and ∂j​σij(k)​=0. Incompressibility removes the pressure contraction, while symmetry removes the antisymmetric velocity gradient. Therefore
∂j​(ui(1)​σij(2)​−ui(2)​σij(1)​)=2μ(e(1):e(2)−e(2):e(1))=0.
(26)
The divergence theorem proves the boundary identity. It applies to an exterior domain by a limit at infinity; the boundary integral there vanishes for decaying rigid-body Stokes flows.
Use a normal directed outward from the fluid, into the rigid body. Then F=∫σndS and G=∫r×(σn)dS are the force and couple exerted by the body on the fluid. If q=(U,Ω) and (F,G)=Rq, inserting the rigid boundary velocity U+Ω×r into the Lorentz reciprocal theorem for Stokes flow gives
(q(1))TRq(2)=(q(2))TRq(1).
(27)
Arbitrary rigid velocities imply R=RT. A separate integration by parts, using the Stokes equation, gives
qTRq=F⋅U+G⋅Ω=2μ∫V​e:edV≥0.
(28)
Equality requires zero rate-of-strain tensor everywhere. Such a velocity is a rigid motion in the connected exterior fluid; decay at infinity forces it to vanish, and the no-slip boundary condition then forces U=Ω=0. Hence the resistance matrix is symmetric positive definite. Defining force as fluid-on-body would reverse its sign, so the positive convention matters.
For the rods, choose body axes along OA, OB, and their normal. On the first rod, X=sex​ for 0≤s≤2L, and the slender-body force density is
fx​=C(2Ux​​, Uy​+Ωz​s, Uz​−Ωy​s).
(29)
On the second rod, X=sey​, and
fy​=C(Ux​−Ωz​s, 2Uy​​, Uz​+Ωx​s).
(30)
Integrate the force densities and the moments X×f, using ∫ds=2L, ∫sds=2L2, and ∫s2ds=8L3/3. For example, Fx​=C(3LUx​−2L2Ωz​), Gx​=C(2L2Uz​+8L3Ωx​/3), and Gz​=C[−2L2Ux​+2L2Uy​+16L3Ωz​/3]. In the ordering (Ux​,Uy​,Uz​,Ωx​,Ωy​,Ωz​), the full right-angle two-rod resistance matrix is
R=C​3L0000−2L2​03L0002L2​004L2L2−2L20​002L28L3/300​00−2L208L3/30​−2L22L200016L3/3​​​.
(31)
The planar subsystem is
​Fx​Fy​Gz​/L​​=CL​30−2​032​−2216/3​​​Ux​Uy​Ωz​L​​.
(32)
Inverting this symmetric matrix gives the hydrodynamic mobility matrix
CL​Ux​Uy​Ωz​L​​=​1/2−1/61/4​−1/61/2−1/4​1/4−1/43/8​​​Fx​Fy​Gz​/L​​​.
(33)
The upper-right entry is positive, as printed in the PDF; the negative sign in the converted TeX would violate both this inversion and reciprocity.
Let laboratory X point along the initial OA and laboratory Y point upward. At body angle θ, ex​=(cosθ,sinθ) and ey​=(−sinθ,cosθ). The gravitational load, balanced by body-on-fluid resistance, has body components
Fx​=−(1+2λ)mgsinθ,Fy​=−(1+2λ)mgcosθ,Gz​=2λmgL(sinθ−cosθ).
(34)
The last expression follows by taking moments of the two endpoint weights about O. Substitution into the hydrodynamic mobility matrix gives
CLUx​=mg[61−λ​cosθ−21+λ​sinθ],CLUy​=mg[61−λ​sinθ−21+λ​cosθ],
(35)
θ˙=4CL2(1−λ)mg​(cosθ−sinθ)​.
(36)
For λ=1, the initial condition θ=0 persists, and Ux​=0, Uy​=−mg/(CL): vertical descent without rotation. For λ<1, θ˙>0 throughout 0≤θ<π/4, and the zero of cosθ−sinθ at π/4 is stable. A simple zero is approached only as t→∞, so θ increases monotonically to π/4. For λ>1, it decreases monotonically through −π/4 and −π/2 toward the next stable zero: θ→−3π/4, not −π/4.
The laboratory horizontal velocity of O is
X˙=Ux​cosθ−Uy​sinθ=6CL(1−λ)mg​cos2θ.
(37)
For λ=1, divide by the angular equation to eliminate time:
dθdX​=32L​cosθ−sinθcos2θ​=32L​(cosθ+sinθ),X−X(0)=32L​(1+sinθ−cosθ).
(38)
At both limiting orientations the sine and cosine are equal, so the sedimentation drift of a weighted two-rod body is
X(∞)−X(0)=32L​for both λ<1 and λ>1.​
(39)
For λ>1 the initial drift is negative. It reaches X−X(0)=2L(1−2​)/3 at θ=−π/4, reverses, crosses its starting horizontal position at θ=−π/2, and ends 2L/3 to the right. At exactly λ=1 there is no horizontal displacement; the infinite-time displacement is discontinuous there because the rotation time diverges as λ→1.
