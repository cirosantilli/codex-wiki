The saddle-node bifurcations occur at (μ,z)=(−C,r) and (C,−r), and three scalar fixed points coexist for ∣μ∣<C. The two outer branches have fixed-point multiplier below one and are stable, while the middle branch is unstable. Negative points lift to two symmetry-related limit cycles, so these scalar branch counts need not equal the number of flow limit cycles. The cusp records coexistence of distinct return-map branches; even outside it a negative branch represents a symmetry-related pair of flow cycles.

This exponentially narrow region ends in a gluing-map cusp at (μ,δ)=(0,1). At its boundaries the graph of f is tangent to the diagonal at z=+r or −r, as shown below. At exactly δ=1, f=μ+Az and A<1 gives one stable fixed point for nonzero μ.
For δ=1+η, the formal saddle-node bifurcation radius is (Aδ)−1/η→∞, and the corresponding saddle-node bifurcation parameters also diverge. Thus the near-origin gluing-map cusp lies on the δ<1 side; the formal large saddle-node bifurcations of the power-law formula on the other side are outside the local gluing-map approximation. They must not be drawn as a second small gluing-map cusp near the origin.
