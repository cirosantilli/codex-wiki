Define F♯(k)=F(kˉ)​. The defocusing reduction of the Lax pair is μ(k)=σ1​μ(kˉ)​σ1​, so the half-line NLS spectral functions have the form
s=(a♯b♯​ba​),S=(A♯B♯​BA​),aa♯−bb♯=AA♯−BB♯=1.
(37)
Put d=aA♯−bB♯. Select bounded analytic columns and divide by their determinants to define
M=⎩⎨⎧​([μ2​]1​/a,[μ3​]2​),([μ1​]1​/d,[μ3​]2​),([μ3​]1​,[μ1​]2​/d♯),([μ3​]1​,[μ2​]2​/a♯),​k∈D1​,k∈D2​,k∈D3​,k∈D4​.​
(38)
For example, [μ3​]2​=be−2iθ[μ2​]1​+a[μ2​]2​, so the first pair has determinant a before division. The corresponding determinant for the second pair is d. The other two are their reflected counterparts. Hence detM=1 and M→I at spectral infinity.
Here are explicit connection matrices furnishing every jump. Write E=e−2iθ; wherever the boundary values coexist, Mj​=μ2​Cj​, with
C1​=(1/a0​bEa​),C2​=(A♯/dB♯E−1/d​bEa​),C3​=(a♯b♯E−1​BE/d♯A/d♯​),C4​=(a♯b♯E−1​01/a♯​).
(39)
Orient each ray away from zero; the plus side is its left side. Then M+​=M−​J, with J=C−−1​C+​, entirely determined by the spectral functions. For instance, on the positive real axis,
J=C4−1​C1​=(1/(aa♯)−(b♯/a)e2iθ​(b/a♯)e−2iθ1​).
(40)
The relevant Riemann-Hilbert problem contour is therefore
Σ=R∪iR.​
(41)
It is a cross because time analyticity involves the sign of Imk2, not just Imk. Zeros of the denominators require residue conditions or small contour detours; they may not simply be ignored. The half-line NLS Riemann-Hilbert reconstruction recovers q=2ilimk→∞​kM12​, by matching the constant term i[σ3​,M1​]=Q in the spatial Lax pair.
