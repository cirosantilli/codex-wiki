The idele group is the restricted product
JK​=∏v′​Kv∗​,
(18)
with respect to Ov∗​ at the finite places. Thus a=(av​) is an idele if all components are nonzero and av​ is a unit at all but finitely many finite places. Its topology has basic open sets which equal the full unit subgroup outside finitely many places; it is not just the subspace topology of the unrestricted product. The group is locally compact, and the diagonal K∗ consists of the principal ideles. The idèle class group is CK​=JK​/K∗.
The finite valuations give the idele-to-ideal homomorphism
(av​)⟼∏v<∞​pvv(av​)​.
(19)
Its kernel is the product of the finite unit groups and the infinite multiplicative groups; principal ideles map to principal fractional ideals. Thus further quotienting gives the ideal class group. The product of normalized local moduli defines ∣a∣J​; at complex places the normalized modulus is the square of the ordinary absolute value. The product formula says that principal ideles have modulus one. These constructions package ideals, units and archimedean conditions into one group.
Here is the precise reciprocity equivalence. For a finite abelian extension L/K, the global law is a continuous Artin reciprocity map
θL/K​:JK​⟶Gal(L/K)
(20)
which kills K∗ and sends a uniformizer idele at an unramified place to arithmetic Frobenius. Its full class-field-theoretic norm statement is CK​/NL/K​CL​≅Gal(L/K). In the local formulation, the maps
rv​:Kv∗​⟶Dv​⊆Gal(L/K)
(21)
are continuous, kill local units when unramified, send a local uniformizer to the same Frobenius, are compatible with restriction in towers, and obey
v∏​rv​(a)=1for every a∈K∗.​
(22)
Here Dv​ is the decomposition group of any prime above v, independent of that choice because the extension is abelian. The usual local norm theorem identifies Kv∗​/NLw​/Kv​​Lw∗​ with Dv​. The principal-idele condition is the essential global compatibility; existence of unrelated local norm isomorphisms alone would not establish global reciprocity.
Given the compatible local maps, define
θ((av​))=v∏​rv​(av​).​
(23)
Only finitely many factors are nontrivial: there are finitely many ramified places, and an idele is a unit almost everywhere. The product is a homomorphism. Continuity follows by choosing local kernel neighbourhoods at those exceptional places and the full unit groups elsewhere. The boxed compatibility kills K∗, so the map descends to CK​. At every unramified place it has the required Frobenius value. The local norm maps also show that idele norms are killed, since the local component of an idele norm is ∏w∣v​NLw​/Kv​​(bw​). Together with the class-field norm-index statement this gives the global norm-kernel formulation.
Conversely, embed Kv∗​ in JK​ with every other component one, and set rv​=θ∘ιv​. Each restriction is continuous and has the required unramified normalization. An open kernel of the finite-valued global map contains a basic neighbourhood whose unrestricted finite-place factors are full unit groups outside a finite set. Hence those local restrictions kill units. For an arbitrary idele, remove the finitely many exceptional and nonunit components; the remainder lies in that kernel neighbourhood. It follows directly that θ is exactly the product of its local restrictions, not merely a map agreeing on single-component ideles. Its triviality on K∗ is exactly the boxed product relation. With the decomposition and norm assertions of the reciprocity theorem, these restrictions are the local reciprocity maps into Dv​ with kernel NLw​/Kv​​Lw∗​.
One can explicitly connect this idelic formulation with the classical ideal law without assuming an idelic law in advance. A modulus of a number field records finitely many nonnegative exponents at finite places and a selected subset of real places. For a modulus m, let Um​ have factors 1+pvmv​​ at finite places dividing the modulus, full units at other finite places, positive real numbers at specified real places, and full infinite multiplicative groups otherwise. Weak approximation for number fields supplies a global a∈K∗ making a−1j lie in the specified local factors at the finitely many modulus places. Its valuations outside the modulus define an ideal prime to m. A different such a changes that ideal by a ray-principal ideal, and every ideal prime to m is obtained. Hence
JK​/(K∗Um​)≅IK​(m)/PK​(m).
(24)
An ideal Artin map killing PK​(m) therefore extends to JK​ via this isomorphism; restricting it gives the local maps just constructed. Conversely, continuous local maps satisfying the product condition have an open product kernel containing some Um​ and hence make the ideal Artin map kill PK​(m). This proves the idelic local-global reciprocity equivalence at the reciprocity-law level as well as the formal conversion of maps. The arithmetic Frobenius normalization and product relation agree with Milne's local-global reciprocity formulation.
