We first compute the ideal class group, rather than assume the proposed extension has the full Hilbert-class-field degree.
The real quadratic Minkowski bound for ideal classes is 229​/2<8. Thus every ideal class has an integral representative of norm at most seven. The prime two is inert because 229≡5(mod8), while seven is inert because 229≡5(mod7) is a nonsquare. The inert ideal (2) has norm four and is principal; a prime above seven has norm forty-nine. The only possible nonprincipal prime factors of these small representatives are therefore primes above three and five.
Reduction of X2−X−57 gives roots 0,1 modulo three and 2,4 modulo five. Write
P=(3,ω),Pˉ=(3,ω−1),Q=(5,ω−4),Qˉ​=(5,ω−2).
(34)
Their conjugate pairs multiply to (3) and (5). Direct norm calculations give
N(6+ω)=−15,N(15+2ω)=27.
(35)
The element 6+ω vanishes at the residue roots 0 modulo three and 4 modulo five, and not at the conjugate roots. Its absolute norm forces each exponent to be one. Likewise 15+2ω vanishes only at the root zero modulo three, and its norm forces exponent three. Thus
(6+ω)=PQ,(15+2ω)=P3.​
(36)
These also give the factorizations suggested by the hint: (13+229​)=(2)PQ and (16+229​)=P3. The classes of P,Q generate the ideal class group; the first relation makes [Q]=[P]−1 and the second makes [P]3=1. Hence the class number divides three.

This proves the ideal class group of Q of square root 229. No claim that ϵ is a fundamental unit is needed. Its norm minus one also realizes all real sign patterns after multiplication by −1, so the ordinary and narrow class numbers agree.
Now let f(X)=X3−4X−1 and let H be its splitting field. The possible rational roots ±1 are not roots, so f is irreducible. Its discriminant is
−4(−4)3−27(−1)2=256−27=229.
(41)
Thus its Galois group is S3​, since an irreducible cubic has group A3​ precisely when its discriminant is a square. Its quadratic subfield is Q(229​)=K, and H/K is cyclic of degree three. If α is one root, then H=K(α).
For every rational prime ℓ=229, the reduction of f is separable. Its roots lift in an unramified extension containing its residue splitting field, by Hensel's lemma. Hence its splitting field is unramified over Q at all these primes, and consequently over K there.
At 229 there is exactly one double residue root and one simple root:
f(X)≡(X+29)2(X−58)(mod229).
(42)
The simple root lifts to r∈Q229​, and division gives f=(X−r)q with q monic quadratic. The discriminant-product identity is
disc(f)=disc(q)q(r)2.
(43)
Here q(r)=f′(r) is a unit, since r has the simple residue root. Therefore the quadratic discriminant has valuation one, and its root field is the ramified quadratic field Q229​(229​). This is already the completion of K. The local splitting field has no further extension over it: the simple root lies in Q229​ and the other two lie in that quadratic field. Thus H/K is unramified even at 229. Equivalently its rational inertia is a transposition and has trivial intersection with A3​.
The positive cubic discriminant gives three distinct real roots, so H is totally real and there is no infinite ramification over K. We have exhibited an everywhere-unramified cyclic extension of degree hK​=3. The Hilbert-class-field characterization in part (i) now proves
HK​=Q(229​,α),α3−4α−1=0.​

Finally let p=229 be rational prime. A prime ideal of norm p is principal if and only if its Artin symbol in HK​/K is trivial. If it is principal, a generator has norm ±p; multiplying by the norm-minus-one unit if necessary gives norm +p. Conversely an element of norm p generates a prime ideal of norm p. Hence representation by x2+xy−57y2 is equivalent to p splitting in K with trivial Artin symbol in HK​/K, or to complete splitting of p in HK​/Q.
Let τp​∈S3​ be its rational Frobenius, defined up to conjugacy. Its restriction to the discriminant quadratic field is its sign. The splitting criterion for that quadratic field is
p splits in K⟺(p/229)=1.
(45)
For odd p this follows by quadratic reciprocity from the character of discriminant 229; for p=2 both criteria are negative since 229≡5(mod8). The polynomial is separable modulo p, and the degrees of its factors are the cycle lengths of τp​. Thus a root modulo p means that τp​ fixes a root. An even permutation of three letters with a fixed point is the identity: a nonidentity three-cycle has none. Therefore the two stated conditions together are exactly complete splitting in HK​, and
p=x2+xy−57y2 for some x,y∈Z⟺(p/229)=1 and X3−4X−1 has a root modulo p.​
(46)
This is prime representation by the norm form of Q of square root 229, with the norm sign and the prime two explicitly covered.
