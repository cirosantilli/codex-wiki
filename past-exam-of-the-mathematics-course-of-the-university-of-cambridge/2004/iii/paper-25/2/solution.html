Use the following simple-root Hensel lemma. Let R be a complete discrete valuation ring, with maximal ideal m, and let f∈R[X]. If f(a0​)∈m and f′(a0​)∈R∗, there is a unique a∈R with a≡a0​(modm) and f(a)=0.
For the proof, define Newton's method iterates ar+1​=ar​−f(ar​)/f′(ar​). Each denominator stays a unit, because all iterates are congruent to a0​. Taylor's polynomial identity gives
v(f(ar+1​))≥2v(f(ar​)),v(ar+1​−ar​)=v(f(ar​)).
(9)
If an iterate is already a root, the process stops. Otherwise these valuations tend to infinity, so the iterates form a Cauchy sequence in the complete ring. Its limit a has the required residue and satisfies f(a)=0. If a,b were two such roots, then
f(a)−f(b)=(a−b)(f′(b)+(a−b)h(a,b)).
(10)
The parenthesis is a unit, forcing a=b.
Now let K/Qp​ be finite, with residue field k=Fq​. For every m≥1 choose a monic irreducible polynomial fˉ​∈k[X] of degree m and lift it to a monic f∈OK​[X]. The lift is irreducible over K, since a factorization would reduce to a factorization of fˉ​, by the monic form of Gauss's lemma over the valuation ring. If L=K(α) for a root, its residue field contains k(αˉ) of degree m. The inequality e(L/K)f(L/K)≤[L:K]=m forces residue degree m and ramification index one. Thus it is an unramified extension of degree m.
Conversely, in any unramified extension of degree m, choose a generator of its residue field and lift its separable minimal polynomial. Hensel's lemma supplies a root with that residue. Its degree is at least m and hence it generates the extension. The same unique lifting of all residue conjugates shows that the extension is Galois and
Gal(Km​/K)≅Gal(Fqm​/Fq​)≅Cm​.​

Km​=K(μqm−1​),​

Finally prove norm-surjectivity on units using the Herbrand quotient. For a cyclic group G of order m, write
hG​(M)=#H−1(G,M)#H0(G,M)​.

The quotient is multiplicative on short exact sequences, equals one for finite modules, equals m on the trivial module Z, and equals one on induced regular modules. For a finite module, the equality follows directly from ∣MG∣∣(σ−1)M∣=∣M∣=∣kerN∣∣NM∣.
Let UL​=OL∗​. For sufficiently large r, the equivariant p-adic logarithm identifies ULr​=1+pLr​ with pLr​ additively. In the unramified case this is an induced regular lattice. Indeed choose a normal basis of the finite residue extension and lift its generating element. Its m Galois conjugates reduce to a basis and hence form an OK​-basis of OL​; their coordinate determinant is a unit. Since a uniformizer of K is also one of L, multiplication by its rth power gives the same regular-module structure on pLr​. For a regular module, invariants are norm images and a vector of coefficient sum zero is a cyclic difference, so both indicated Tate cohomology groups vanish. Consequently hG​(ULr​)=1. The quotient UL​/ULr​ is finite, so hG​(UL​)=1.
