Put δ=−A2−A−2. The reduced Kauffman bracket is the Laurent polynomial determined by ⟨◯⟩=1, ⟨D⊔◯⟩=δ⟨D⟩, and the crossing expansion ⟨D⟩=A⟨DA​⟩+A−1⟨DB​⟩. Under a positive Reidemeister move of type I, the two resolutions give Aδ+A−1=−A3 times the straight strand; the negative curl gives A−1δ+A=−A−3. Thus the bracket itself is not an unframed link invariant. For type II, use the adjacent cap-cup generator e of the Temperley-Lieb algebra, with e2=δe. VL​(t)=(−A3)−w(D)⟨D⟩,t=A−4,V◯​=1.​

Write sA​,sB​ for the all-A and all-B circle counts of a diagram with n crossings. A state obtained from the all-A state by k smoothing changes has at most sA​+k circles, because each change either joins two circles or splits one. Its largest A exponent is consequently at most n−2k+2(sA​+k−1)=n+2sA​−2. The symmetric argument from the all-B state bounds every exponent below by −n−2sB​+2. brA​⟨D⟩≤2n+2sA​+2sB​−4.

For a connected diagram, construct the Turaev surface by placing A-state circles above the projection sphere, B-state circles below it, inserting one saddle at each crossing, and capping the state circles. Writhe normalization changes only a monomial, and t=A−4 divides breadth by four. Therefore
brt​VL​≤n.​
(6)
For a connected reduced alternating diagram, checkerboard color its complementary regions. Alternation makes all-A circles the boundaries of one color and all-B circles the boundaries of the other. Euler's formula for the connected four-valent projection graph gives sA​+sB​=n+2. Its Tait graph and planar dual have no loops: a loop in either, equivalently a loop or bridge in one, gives a nugatory crossing cut off by a simple closed curve. Reducedness excludes precisely such crossings. Changing one smoothing in the all-A or all-B state therefore joins distinct circles, so the diagram is an adequate link diagram.
This also proves that the extreme coefficients cannot cancel. If k≥1 smoothings are changed from the all-A state, the first change lowers its circle count by one and the remaining k−1 changes increase it by at most k−1. The largest exponent of that state is at most n+2sA​−6, four below the all-A extreme n+2sA​−2, whose coefficient is (−1)sA​−1. The all-B extreme is uniquely attained for the same reason. Consequently
brA​VL​(A−4)=4n,brt​VL​=nfor a connected reduced alternating diagram.​

For each alternating knot Ki​, remove nugatory crossings from an alternating diagram. The equality just proved, together with the general breadth bound for any knot diagram, shows that its reduced crossing count is minimal: c(Ki​)=brVKi​​. Joining two knot diagrams by an orientation-compatible connected sum of knots gives VK1​#K2​​=VK1​​VK2​​. Indeed the state circles joined along the sum arc become one, so the bracket state sums multiply, and writhe adds. Nonzero Laurent polynomials have additive breadth, because their extreme coefficients multiply. Hence every diagram of the sum has at least c(K1​)+c(K2​) crossings, while joining the two minimal alternating diagrams achieves that count. Thus
c(K1​#K2​)=c(K1​)+c(K2​).​
(8)
Finally suppose that the two-component link is split. Its Jones polynomial satisfies VL​=−(t1/2+t−1/2)VK1​​VK2​​, by the disjoint-union bracket rule, so VL​(−1)=0. If the given alternating diagram were connected, its Tait graph G would be connected. Evaluate the bracket at A=eπi/4, where δ=0 and t=−1. Only one-circle states survive. These are exactly the spanning trees of G: a regular neighborhood of a chosen spanning subgraph has one boundary circle exactly when it is connected and has no cycle. All spanning trees use the same number of black-joining smoothings, ∣V(G)∣−1. Since the crossing-to-checkerboard smoothing choice is uniform in an alternating diagram, all surviving state weights are the same nonzero power of A. Therefore ⟨D⟩ is that power times the positive number of spanning trees, and VL​(−1)=0, a contradiction.
The diagram is thus disconnected. Each of the two link components projects to a connected subset, so the two projection components can be separated by a boundary curve of a small regular neighborhood in the projection sphere. Hence a split two-component link represented by a reduced alternating diagram has a split diagram.

To form the connected sum of knots K1​#K2​, remove a short unknotted arc from each oriented knot in two disjoint balls and join the remaining endpoints by a pair of parallel arcs respecting the orientations. Equivalently, a sphere meeting a knot in two points cuts it into two one-string tangles; close each by an arc in the sphere to recover its summands. Sliding the small joining balls along the knots shows that the isotopy type does not depend on these choices. The unknot is the identity for this operation. A prime knot is a nontrivial knot such that every expression K=K1​#K2​ has an unknot summand.
We first prove additivity of Seifert genus, which also supplies a terminating measure for decomposition. Joining minimal Seifert surfaces along their boundary arcs gives gs​(K1​#K2​)≤gs​(K1​)+gs​(K2​). For the reverse inequality, let F be a minimal-genus Seifert surface for the sum and S a splitting sphere of a knot. Arrange transverse intersection so that F∩S consists of one arc joining the two boundary-intersection points and some circles, with the number of circles minimal among genus-minimizing surfaces.
Each circle separates the sphere into two disks; since it is disjoint from the intersection arc, one disk contains neither marked endpoint. Choose an innermost such circle and its disk D⊂S, whose interior misses F. If its boundary were essential in F, compressing F along D would lower the genus of its component with boundary K: for a nonseparating curve genus drops by one, and for a separating essential curve the removed boundaryless side has positive genus. This contradicts minimal genus. Its boundary therefore bounds a disk in F. Replace that disk by a small push-off of D, removing the intersection circle without changing genus or boundary, again contradicting minimal intersection. Thus no circles remain.
Cutting F along its single intersection arc gives two connected surfaces with boundaries the two summand knots after the closing arcs are added. Euler characteristic shows that their genera add to g(F). Each genus is at least the minimum genus of its summand, so
gs​(K1​#K2​)=gs​(K1​)+gs​(K2​).​
(9)
Genus zero means that the knot bounds a disk and is the unknot. Every nontrivial knot therefore has positive genus. Induct on this nonnegative integer: a nontrivial nonprime knot splits into two nontrivial summands, each with strictly smaller genus by the equality above, and induction decomposes both into primes. This proves every knot is a finite connected sum of prime knots, with the unknot the empty sum. Uniqueness is not needed here.
For coprime integers p,q, define the torus knot as the oriented parametrized curve
Tp,q​(s)=2−1/2(eips,eiqs)⊂S3⊂C2,s∈R/2πZ.
(10)
Coprimality makes it an embedded single circle: two parameters with both coordinate phases equal differ by a multiple of 2π. It lies on the standard unknotted torus separating the two solid tori. Negative exponents change the slope/orientation convention; they are allowed. If an exponent is zero, coprimality forces the other to be plus or minus one, giving an unknot.
Assume now ∣p∣,∣q∣≥2. We give a group-theoretic proof that torus knots are prime, making the peripheral ingredient explicit. The Seifert-van Kampen theorem applied to the two solid-torus sides of the exterior, joined along the annulus parallel to the knot, gives
G=⟨a,b∣ap=bq⟩.
(11)
Here a and b are the two core loops. The element z=ap=bq is central. Under the abelianization G→Z, choose a↦q, b↦p; these generate the target because p,q are coprime. Thus z↦pq=0, so z is nontrivial. A meridian has, up to conjugacy, the form m=aubv with qu+pv=1. This follows by tracing the meridional disk across the two torus sides, using a dual primitive slope intersecting the knot slope once. Different Bezout solutions change u by a multiple of p and v by the opposite multiple of q, leaving the same word because z is central.
Quotienting by z gives G/⟨z⟩=C∣p∣​∗C∣q∣​. Neither u nor v is zero modulo the corresponding cyclic order, by qu+pv=1. Hence the image of m is a cyclically reduced word of two syllables. All its nonzero powers remain reduced, so it has infinite order. In particular G is nonabelian and the knot is not an unknot.
We use the Loop theorem in its usual form: a noninjective boundary fundamental-group map of a three-manifold supplies an embedded compression disk. For a nontrivial knot, its boundary torus is incompressible. To see why, every sphere in its exterior bounds a ball on the side not containing the knot, by the Schoenflies theorem. A compression of the boundary torus would produce a sphere and make the exterior a solid torus. The compression disk has zero homology class in the exterior, so its boundary is the preferred longitude; attaching the annulus from that longitude to the knot gives a spanning disk and an unknot. This proves that nontrivial knot exteriors have incompressible boundary. Their peripheral groups inject as Z2, so their groups strictly contain the cyclic meridian subgroup.
If the torus knot were a connected sum of two nontrivial knots, van Kampen on the decomposing annulus would give G=G1​∗⟨m⟩​G2​, with the meridian subgroup proper in each factor. The center of a proper amalgam lies in the amalgamated subgroup. One proof uses its Bass-Serre tree: a central elliptic element fixes a vertex orbit and its convex hull, which is the whole tree; a central translation would force the minimal tree to be its invariant axis, but a nontrivial vertex reflection reverses that translation. Thus a central element fixes every edge and lies in the edge subgroup.
The central element z would therefore equal mk. Its image in G/⟨z⟩ would give mk=1, forcing k=0 because that image has infinite order. This contradicts z=1. Consequently
Tp,q​ is prime whenever gcd(p,q)=1,∣p∣,∣q∣≥2.​
(12)

This uses the full-degree irreducible Alexander polynomial implies a prime knot criterion, rather than irreducibility alone. Therefore K is prime.

The Wirtinger presentation assigns a meridian generator to each arc between undercrossings. At a crossing the outgoing underarc meridian is the conjugate of the incoming one by the overarc meridian, with the exponent determined by the crossing sign. One crossing relation is redundant for a connected knot diagram. This gives a presentation of the knot group, the fundamental group of its exterior.
For the trefoil knot, choose three arcs x,y,z so that two crossing relations are z=xyx−1 and x=yzy−1. The third follows from these. Substituting for z gives xyx=yxy, hence
π1​(S3∖T)=⟨x,y∣xyx=yxy⟩.​
(22)
We use the relator r=xyxy−1x−1y−1.
Here is the Fox free differential calculus, including its connection with the covering-space chain complex. Let F be the free group on x1​,…,xn​, let ZF be its group ring, and let ε:ZF→Z be augmentation. Define additive maps Dj​:ZF→ZF by
Dj​(xi​)=δij​,Dj​(ab)=Dj​(a)ε(b)+aDj​(b),Dj​(1)=0.
(23)
For group words u,v the product rule reads Dj​(uv)=Dj​(u)+uDj​(v). Differentiating xi​xi−1​=1 forces Dj​(xi−1​)=−δij​xi−1​. Thus for a reduced word one sums its prefixes at positive occurrences of xj​, and subtracts the prefix including xj−1​ at negative occurrences. Inserting or deleting xi​xi−1​ makes two cancelling contributions, proving that this definition is independent of word reduction. Linear extension proves the group-ring product rule and uniqueness.
The fundamental identity is

Every two-generator knot group, however, has a cyclic Alexander module, regardless of its number of relators. Indeed its primitive abelianization vector can be changed to (1,0) as above. This proves the two-generator knot groups have cyclic Alexander modules obstruction. Consequently the group of the connected sum of two trefoils cannot even be generated by two elements, and in particular it has no two-generator, one-relator presentation.

For an oriented three-manifold M with prescribed framed boundary endpoints P, the relative Kauffman bracket skein module is the complex vector space generated by properly embedded framed tangles, modulo framed isotopy and the local bracket relations. A crossing resolves with coefficients A and A−1, and adjoining an unknotted circle multiplies by δ=−A2−A−2. We now use the full bracket convention: the empty diagram has value one and a circle has value δ. For a nonempty uncolored link this full bracket is δ times the reduced bracket used earlier. Fixing this normalization is essential for the quantum dimensions. Gluing compatible boundary pieces composes skeins; disjoint union gives tensor products. With no endpoints this is the Kauffman bracket skein module. Framing is retained, so a Reidemeister-I curl is a framing change rather than an allowed isotopy.
For A=0, the Temperley-Lieb algebra TLn​(A) is the skein algebra of a rectangle cylinder with n marked endpoints on each horizontal side, multiplication being vertical stacking. Resolve crossings to get a basis of crossing-free matchings of its 2n endpoints, with every closed circle removed with factor δ. There are n+11​(n2n​) such matchings. Conversely multiplication of these matchings by joining endpoints defines an algebra satisfying the skein relations, which proves their independence. Writing ei​ for an adjacent cap-cup and 1 for the n vertical strands gives
ei2​=δei​,ei​ei±1​ei​=ei​,ei​ej​=ej​ei​(∣i−j∣>1).
(29)
Every crossing-free matching other than 1 contains a turnback; removing an outermost cap successively expresses it in these generators. Thus these diagram rules give the usual generator presentation as well.
Put [m]=(A2m−A−2m)/(A2−A−2), interpreted as the Laurent sum ∑s=0m−1​A2(m−1−2s) if its denominator vanishes. Set Δj​=(−1)j[j+1]. These quantum integers satisfy Δ0​=1, Δ1​=δ, and Δj​=δΔj−1​−Δj−2​. The Jones-Wenzl idempotent fn​ is characterized by identity-matching coefficient one and
fn2​=fn​,ei​fn​=fn​ei​=0(1≤i<n).

(32)
A negative framing twist acts by the inverse scalar.
Choose A=eπi/(2r), with an integer r≥3. Then [r]=0 but [1],…,[r−1] are nonzero. The projector fr−1​ exists and has trace zero. Pass to the reduced Temperley-Lieb skein theory by setting it to zero, including every diagram containing it. The surviving colors are j=0,…,r−2, with dimensions and twists
dj​=Δj​=(−1)jsin(π/r)sin((j+1)π/r)​,θj​=(−1)jAj(j+2).
(33)
Their dimensions are nonzero. The recurrence decomposes fj​⊗1 into the two orthogonal channels fj+1​ and fj−1​: the first is the new projector, while its complementary cap-cup idempotent is the latter, with inclusion/projection normalized by the nonzero partial-closure scalar. At the upper endpoint the r−1 channel vanishes. Iterating gives a complete decomposition of every tensor power into these colors. A matching between projected colors of different sizes has a turnback on the larger side and vanishes; on the same size only the identity matching survives. Thus their endomorphism spaces are one-dimensional and all matrix blocks split. Applying the recurrence to the decompositions proves the fusion of Jones-Wenzl colors rule
a⊗b=⨁c​Nabc​c,Nabc​={1,0,​a+b+c even, ∣a−b∣≤c≤min(a+b,2r−4−a−b),otherwise.​
(34)
One can verify the induction by multiplying by color 1: both sides satisfy the same adjacent-color recurrence and the same zero boundary at r−1. Taking closures proves da​db​=∑c​Nabc​dc​. Cups and caps make every color self-dual, so these fusion coefficients are symmetric in all three labels.
We next prove the Kirby color handle-slide identity, rather than assume it. In the annular skein space let f​b​ be the closure of color b and define the Kirby color Ω=∑b​db​f​b​. For a fixed color a, choose inclusion/projection maps ic​:c→b⊗a, pc​:b⊗a→c for the fusion channels, with pc​ic​=1c​ and ∑c​ic​pc​=1b⊗a​. Bend the a strand with a cup and cap, obtaining
Ucb​=(pc​⊗1a​)(1b​⊗coeva​):b⟶c⊗a,Vcb​=(1b​⊗eva​)(ic​⊗1a​):c⊗a⟶b.
(35)
Closing the resulting diagrams and straightening the bent strand gives tr(Vcb​Ucb​)=tr(pc​ic​)=dc​. Since b is simple, Vcb​Ucb​=(dc​/db​)1b​. The symmetric fusion rule supplies all channels of c⊗a, so their normalized projections are complete. Therefore
∑b​dc​db​​Ucb​Vcb​=1c⊗a​,b∑​db​Ucb​Vcb​=dc​1c⊗a​.​
(36)
To slide an a-colored strand over an Ω-colored component, cut at the slide band and insert the resolution ∑c​ic​pc​. Carrying the band across that loop bends its two maps into U and V. In each outgoing channel c, the boxed identity replaces the sum over the old loop color b by exactly the new loop weight dc​; the rest of the diagrams are isotopic as framed ribbons. This proves invariance for fixed a. Summing a with its own weight proves invariance when both surgery components are colored by Ω. The band framing is carried in this argument, as required for a framed handle slide.
We must also show that the stabilization normalization is nonzero. Let Hij​ be the full bracket of the zero-framed Hopf link with colors i,j. On a fusion channel k, its double braiding has eigenvalue θk​/(θi​θj​): the full twist of the combined ribbon equals double braiding followed by the two separate twists. Taking the trace gives the balancing identity
Hij​=θi​θj​1​∑k​Nijk​dk​θk​.
(37)
For j=1 there are only the i−1,i+1 channels; substitution of their dimensions and twists gives Hi1​/di​=−(A2i+2+A−2i−2), with a vanished endpoint channel simply omitted. A color-1 meridian therefore acts on the simple color i by that scalar. The annular closures obey f​j​=Xf​j−1​−f​j−2​, where X is a color-1 core, by the two-channel fusion decomposition. Applying this recurrence to meridians gives the Hopf pairing of Jones-Wenzl colors
Hij​=(−1)i+j[(i+1)(j+1)].
(38)
Indeed the recurrence is the sine identity sin((j+1)x)=2cosxsin(jx)−sin((j−1)x) with x=(i+1)π/r, and its initial values are Hi0​=di​ and the computed Hi1​. Finite sine orthogonality now gives
H2=D2I,D2=∑j​dj2​=2sin2(π/r)r​,D>0.
(39)
For example, orthogonality follows by writing products of sines as differences of cosines and summing the finite geometric series of rth roots of unity; the result is ∑k=1r−1​sin(ikπ/r)sin(jkπ/r)=2r​δij​. Thus no degeneracy remains in this pairing.
The Gauss sums of Jones-Wenzl colors are p±​=∑j​dj2​θj±1​, the evaluations of the plus- and minus-one-framed Ω-colored unknots. Balancing and dimension fusion prove
∑i​di​θi​Hij​=θj−1​∑i,k​di​Nijk​dk​θk​=p+​θj−1​dj​.
(40)
In the last step ∑i​di​Nijk​=dj​dk​, by symmetry of the fusion coefficients. In vector notation this is HTd=p+​T−1d, with T=diag(θj​). The matrix H and vector d are real, and each twist has modulus one, so conjugation gives HT−1d=p−​Td. Apply H again and use its orthogonality to obtain p+​p−​=D2​. Hence neither factor vanishes.
We may use the surgery existence theorem and Kirby calculus: every closed oriented three-manifold has an integral framed-link surgery description, and two descriptions are related by handle slides and insertion/removal of disjoint plus- or minus-one-framed unknots. For an m-component surgery link L let σ(L) be the signature of its symmetric surgery linking matrix, put κ=p+​/D, and define the Jones polynomial surgery invariant
τr​(ML​)=D−mκ−σ(L)⟨L(Ω,…,Ω)⟩.​
(41)
A handle slide preserves the colored bracket by the proved identity and changes the linking matrix by congruence, preserving its signature. Adding a disjoint plus-one-framed unknot multiplies the bracket by p+​ and changes (m,σ) to (m+1,σ+1), so the net factor is p+​/(Dκ)=1. For a minus-one-framed unknot it is κp−​/D=p+​p−​/D2=1. Kirby calculus therefore proves that the displayed value is a well-defined oriented three-manifold invariant. Our normalization has τr​(S3)=1; reversing orientation conjugates the value. Its construction uses precisely the bracket and colored projectors underlying the Jones polynomial.
For real projective 3-space the surgery diagram is a single unknot of framing two. Thus m=1, σ=1, and its colored evaluation is ∑j​dj2​θj2​. Since Dκ=p+​, the explicit answer is
τr​(RP3)=j=0∑r−2​(−1)j[j+1]2Aj(j+2)j=0∑r−2​[j+1]2A2j(j+2)​,A=eπi/(2r),r≥3.​
(42)
The denominator is nonzero by the proved Gauss-sum identity. For instance at r=3 the twists are 1,−i, the squared dimensions are both one, and the numerator vanishes, so this normalized invariant is zero for RP3 at that level.
