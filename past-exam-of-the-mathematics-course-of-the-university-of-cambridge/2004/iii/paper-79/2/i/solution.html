Work in a meridional R,z plane and denote the interface height by Z(R), to distinguish it from an observation coordinate. Put
s=z0​(α−αf​),d=α2−αf2​​,D(R)=s2+d2R2​.
(10)
The interface is Z=(αs+αf​D)/d2, so Z′=αf​R/D. Its unit normal into the fluid is proportional to (−Z′,1). Consequently the angle between this normal and the vertical satisfies
tanθz​=Z′=Dαf​R​.​
(11)
The outgoing source P wave has ray direction proportional to (R,Z), tilting outwards by arctan(R/Z) from the vertical, while the normal tilts inwards. Thus θP​=arctan(R/Z)+θz​. The tangent addition formula gives
tanθP​=Z−RZ′R+ZZ′​.
(12)
Direct substitution simplifies its denominator to s(αD+αf​s)/(d2D) and its numerator to αR(αD+αf​s)/(d2D). Hence
tanθP​=sαR​=z0​(α−αf​)αR​.​
(13)
The denominator is positive, so these are the physical incidence angles in [0,π/2); the transmitted P wave reaches the fluid rather than propagating back into the solid.
