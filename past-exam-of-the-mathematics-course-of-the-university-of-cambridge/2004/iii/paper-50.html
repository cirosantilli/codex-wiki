TeV-scale softly broken supersymmetry addresses the technical hierarchy problem: equal bosonic and fermionic couplings cancel quadratic ultraviolet contributions to the Higgs mass. Soft mass splittings leave corrections of order δmH2​∼g2Msoft2​log(Λ/Msoft​)/(16π2), so a weak-scale soft spectrum protects the weak scale against a much higher cutoff. Unbroken four-dimensional extended supersymmetry is incompatible with the observed complex chiral gauge spectrum: charged hypermultiplets contain conjugate representations, while vector multiplets have real adjoint matter. It must therefore be broken to at most N=1 before reproducing low-energy Standard Model chirality.

In a supersymmetric Higgs mechanism, an internal gauge symmetry breaks while supersymmetry can remain unbroken. A massless vector multiplet absorbs a chiral multiplet and becomes a massive multiplet with spins (1,21​,21​,0): the gauge boson acquires its longitudinal particle polarization from the Goldstone mode. In the super-Higgs mechanism, local supersymmetry itself breaks: the spin-3/2 gravitino absorbs the spin-1/2 goldstino, gaining two longitudinal polarizations and becoming massive. With only global supersymmetry breaking, the goldstino instead remains a physical massless fermion.

Both Kaluza-Klein theory and the brane-world scenario use a higher-dimensional bulk. Ordinary Kaluza–Klein matter propagates in the compact directions, producing observable massive towers; absence of weak-scale charged modes requires roughly R−1≳TeV, or R≲2×10−19m, and traditional constructions often take Planck-size radii. Brane-world matter is localized while gravity explores the bulk, so large flat dimensions can reach the submillimetre order of magnitude discussed in the 2004 setting. For n flat dimensions, MPl2​∼MDn+2​(2πR)n. These are model-dependent scales, not universal maxima: warped gravity-localizing models can have a noncompact extra dimension. The historical large-flat-dimension mechanism is described in arxiv.org/abs/hep-ph/9803315.

Work with the local Wilsonian effective action, retaining the elementary chiral superfield and using a nonzero infrared cutoff. Its superpotential is holomorphic in both the field and chiral spurions representing the couplings. A convenient pair of spurion symmetries assigns charges
U(1)U(1)R​​Φ13​m−2−4​g−3−7​θ01​W02​​​
(3)
Both couplings and Φ transform nontrivially under each displayed symmetry. Each superpotential monomial has ordinary charge zero and R-charge two, so the integral ∫d2θW is invariant. This R assignment differs from the conventional (1,0,−1) assignment for (Φ,m,g) by twice the ordinary charge, and describes the same two-dimensional charge space. These are formal spurion symmetries; fixing the couplings to numerical values need not leave them as actual symmetries of the fields alone.
For m=0, mΦ2 has the required charges and mass dimension three, while z=gΦ/m has zero charge under both symmetries and is dimensionless. Thus the allowed holomorphic form is
Weff​=mΦ2F(mgΦ​).​
(4)
One can see its generality directly. A holomorphic monomial Φumvgw must obey u−2v−3w=0 and 3u−4v−7w=2. Solving gives u=w+2 and v=1−w, hence precisely mΦ2(gΦ/m)w. A field-independent constant is irrelevant to this global supersymmetric action and can be dropped.
The symmetries alone leave the function arbitrary. To determine its perturbative form, use regularity as well as the two limits. A regular weak-coupling expansion at g=0 has
F(z)=∑r≥0​cr​zr,Weff​=∑r≥0​cr​grm1−rΦr+2.
(5)
The g→0 limit is a free massive theory, so c0​=1/2. Since the elementary field has not been integrated out, the Wilsonian superpotential must remain regular term by term as m→0 with the infrared cutoff fixed. Every term with r≥2 would introduce a pole in m and is excluded. Negative powers of g were already excluded by perturbative regularity at g=0. Consequently F=c0​+c1​z.
At m=0, the only surviving allowed interaction is proportional to gΦ3. Its coefficient is first order in g, and matching the tree three-field vertex gives c1​=1/3. No closed-loop three-field diagram can be made from a single cubic vertex; extra interaction vertices would produce higher coupling order. Equivalently free superspace propagators join chiral to antichiral fields, so a closed loop would require additional vertices or conjugate couplings, which the holomorphic charge constraints exclude for this term. This fixes the coefficient without assuming the desired conclusion. Therefore
F(z)=21​+3z​,Weffpert​=21​mΦ2+31​gΦ3=Wtree​.​
(6)
The regularity condition is essential: it concerns the local Wilsonian superpotential with the field retained, not a nonlocal one-particle-irreducible functional at zero infrared cutoff or a superpotential obtained by eliminating a massive field. Such different operations can generate inverse masses or infrared singularities without contradicting this proof.
The Kähler potential is a real D-term, not a holomorphic F-term, and is not protected by this argument. For example its quadratic part becomes Z(g,gˉ​,m,mˉ;μ)Φ†Φ, with higher real operators also allowed. Terms involving ∣g∣2 are compatible with the symmetries and produce wave-function renormalization. After the canonical rescaling Φc​=Z1/2Φ, the parameters in the superpotential expressed using canonical fields are mc​=m/Z and gc​=g/Z3/2, so physical couplings can run despite the holomorphic non-renormalization statement.
Holomorphy is crucial. Without it, neutral factors such as 1+c∣g∣2 could multiply either superpotential monomial while respecting both spurion symmetries. Excluding conjugate couplings, together with regularity and tree matching, is what turns the charge argument into the non-renormalization theorem.

Start with the Clifford algebra {γμ,γν}=2ημν. For the dimension count, complexify and rescale the timelike matrix so that the generators obey the Euclidean complex relation {γa​,γb​}=2δab​. This changes neither the complex representation dimension nor its decomposition under the complexified even algebra. In D=2k, pair the generators into
aj​=2γ2j−1​+iγ2j​​,bj​=2γ2j−1​−iγ2j​​(j=1,…,k).
(28)
Direct substitution gives {ai​,aj​}={bi​,bj​}=0 and {ai​,bj​}=δij​. The commuting operators nj​=bj​aj​ satisfy nj2​=nj​, so their occupation eigenvalues are zero or one. An irreducible module contains an all-zero occupation state ∣0⟩, obtained by lowering the occupied entries of a simultaneous eigenvector. The states
b1e1​​⋯bkek​​∣0⟩,(e1​,…,ek​)∈{0,1}k,
(29)
are nonzero and independent by their distinct joint occupation eigenvalues. They span an invariant module; irreducibility makes this the full module. Conversely this Fock construction supplies a representation on exactly these 2k states. Products of occupation projectors and raising/lowering operators give its matrix units, so the generated algebra is the full matrix algebra. Therefore the minimal complex Dirac spinor has

This module is irreducible under the full Clifford algebra, but the Spinor representation of the Lorentz group is generated by the even bilinears Σμν=41​[γμ,γν]. The chirality matrix in the Euclideanized basis is
γ∗​=i−kγ1​⋯γ2k​,γ∗2​=1,{γ∗​,γa​}=0,[γ∗​,Σμν]=0.

These are the irreducible Weyl spinors. Globally they are ordinary representations of Spin(D−1,1), the double cover; calling them representations of SO(D−1,1) uses the conventional projective spinor terminology.
For D=2k+1, adjoin γ2k+1​=±γ∗​. It anticommutes with the old matrices and gives a Clifford module of the same dimension 2k. The new even generators [γa​,γ2k+1​] interchange the two old chirality eigenspaces, so the odd-dimensional Lorentz spinor is irreducible of complex dimension 2k, with no Weyl splitting. The product of all odd-dimensional gamma matrices is central and acts as a scalar; it cannot be a new anticommuting chirality operator. Its two signs distinguish the two complex odd-Clifford modules, whose restrictions to Spin group are equivalent. Majorana spinor reality conditions, when the signature permits them, are additional conditions and should not be confused with complex chirality.
