Let S(z)=dρs​/dz, N2=−gS/ρ0​>0, and define the quasi-geostrophic streamfunction by ψ=p​/(ρ0​f0​). Use horizontal scale L, vertical scale D, velocity scale U and advective time L/U. The required quasi-geostrophic approximation has small Rossby number ε=U/(∣f0​∣L), βL/∣f0​∣=O(ε), and Burger number N2D2/(f02​L2)=O(1). Hydrostatic, thin-layer scaling has D/L≪1; density variations are small compared with ρ0​, as required by the Boussinesq approximation. The leading horizontal flow is nondivergent, with vertical velocity w=O(εUD/L). Retain forcing of the same slow order as density advection, r=O(Uρ​/L); stronger forcing need not preserve this balanced scaling.
Leading geostrophic balance and the hydrostatic approximation give
ug​=−ψy​,vg​=ψx​,ρ​=−gρ0​f0​​ψz​.

Write Dg​/Dt=∂t​−ψy​∂x​+ψx​∂y​. The density equation becomes
f0​DtDg​ψz​​+N2w=−ρ0​g​r,w=−N2f0​​DtDg​ψz​​−ρ0​N2g​r.

Taking the curl of the horizontal momentum equations at the next order and using continuity gives the stretching equation
DtDg​​(∇h2​ψ+βy)=f0​wz​.

Substitute the expression for w. Vertical differentiation commutes with the geostrophic material derivative acting on ψz​ here: the extra Jacobian is J(ψz​,ψz​)=0. Since N depends only on height, this yields the diabatically forced quasi-geostrophic potential vorticity equation
DtDg​q​=−ρ0​gf0​​∂z​(N2r​),q=∇h2​ψ+∂z​(N2f02​​ψz​)+βy.​
(15)
The minus sign is appropriate to the specified density forcing; a positive upward buoyancy forcing is R=−gr/ρ0​ and gives Dg​q/Dt=f0​∂z​(R/N2).
Now take constant N and linearize about rest. Put s=f02​/N2 and S0​=gf0​μr0​/(ρ0​N2). The forced equation is
∂t​(ψxx​+sψzz​)+βψx​=S0​e−μzcoskxcosω0​t.

For the harmonic heating response of quasi-geostrophic flow, represent the two traveling Fourier components with frequencies ω+​=ω0​ and ω−​=−ω0​:
ψ=Re∑σ=±​ϕσ​(z)ei(kx−ωσ​t).
(17)
Each component of the forcing has amplitude S0​/2, so
−iωσ​(sϕσ′′​−k2ϕσ​)+iβkϕσ​=2S0​​e−μz.
(18)
Equivalently its vertical structure obeys
ϕσ′′​−λσ2​ϕσ​=Cσ​e−μz,λσ2​=f02​N2​(k2+ωσ​βk​),Cσ​=2ρ0​f0​ωσ​igμr0​​.​
(19)
These are the time-periodic forced responses; unspecified initial data may add free Rossby waves. Selecting no incoming waves describes forcing switched on causally and then its persistent harmonic part.
For β>0, the eastward component always has λ+2​>0. Let λ+​>0. The growing exponential would introduce an unbounded velocity and pressure disturbance at infinity, so discard it. The bottom condition ϕ+​(0)=0 then gives
ϕ+​(z)=μ2−λ+2​C+​​(e−μz−e−λ+​z).​
(20)
If μ=λ+​, this expression has the regular limit −C+​ze−μz/(2μ), rather than a physical singularity.
For ω0​>β/k, the westward component also has positive λ−2​. Choose λ−​>0 and use the same decaying expression with minus subscripts, including the same coincident-exponent limit. Thus both components are vertically evanescent above the heating.
For 0<ω0​<β/k, put
m=∣f0​∣N​ω0​βk​−k2​>0,λ−2​=−m2.
(21)
Both homogeneous solutions e±imz are bounded, so boundedness is insufficient. A free Rossby wave with phase kx+mz−ωt has
ω=−k2+sm2βk​,cgz​=∂m∂ω​=(k2+sm2)22βksm​.
(22)
The radiation condition demands energy propagation away from the heating, upward into z>0. For β,k>0 that selects positive m, hence eimz in the westward component. The resulting solution is
ϕ−​(z)=μ2+m2C−​​(e−μz−eimz).​
(23)
It satisfies ϕ−​(0)=0 and, away from the decaying forcing, ϕ−′​∼imϕ−​. Its far-field amplitude does not decay in this nondissipative model: continuous forcing supplies an outgoing wave train. Thus the low-frequency westward component propagates vertically while the eastward component remains evanescent.
At the threshold ω0​=β/k, the westward vertical wavenumber is zero. Excluding the linearly growing homogeneous solution and retaining the bottom condition gives ϕ−​=C−​(e−μz−1)/μ2. This bounded limiting response tends to a constant and has zero vertical group velocity; demanding both bottom zero and decay to zero at infinity would overdetermine this threshold problem. For β≤0, the branch labels and propagation inequality must be reconsidered; the comparison stated here assumes the usual positive beta-plane gradient.
