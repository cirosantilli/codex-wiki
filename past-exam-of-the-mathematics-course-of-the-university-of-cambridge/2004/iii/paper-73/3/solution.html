Slopes scaled by D/L are of Rossby-number order, so this topographic contribution is retained alongside the small ageostrophic vertical velocity. The dimensional slope should not be compared to the Rossby number without also specifying the aspect ratio.

The intended zero-interior-PV Eady model modes do have q′=0 and satisfy both expressions; the ensuing mode calculation is valid in that subspace.

For arbitrary nonzero interior PV the same calculation instead gives dP/dt=(f0​s)−1∫ψx′​q′dV, which need not vanish. For example, at an instant take ψ′=coskx+(z/D)2sinkx, independent of y; the zonal average of ψx′​q′ is −ks/D2. Thus the boundary-only conservation statement also needs the zero-interior-PV qualification.
For an exponentially growing smooth normal mode, this qualification is automatic: the interior equation is ik(Λz−c)q​=0, and a phase speed with nonzero imaginary part cannot equal the real basic velocity. Therefore q​=0. If A0​AD​<0, the conserved boundary quadratic form is definite, so a nonzero exponentially growing boundary amplitude is impossible. Instability consequently requires
(1−f0​ΛN02​γ​)(1−f0​ΛN02​α​)>0,​
(32)
for Λ=0. When one A vanishes, the corresponding boundary derivative is simply advected and must vanish for a growing mode. The other boundary then yields a real single-edge-wave speed; if both vanish, the zero-interior-PV Neumann problem has no nonzero growing mode. These degenerate cases are neutral and should not be handled by dividing by zero.

C(1−C)MsinhM+acoshM+Ma2​sinhM=0,

C=21​±41​+aMcothM​+M2a2​​.​

For positive k,f0​, M is the printed μ, and a is the printed α. The expression is even in the sign of that vertical scale. For a≥0 the radicand is at least 1/4, so both speeds are real and these modes have no exponential growth. For equal slopes the earlier necessary condition is merely a2>0, which is satisfied in many stable cases and is not sufficient. At a=0 the speeds are 0 and ΛD; the invariant with nonzero denominators cannot be used, but the determinant still correctly gives neutral modes. No claim excluding transient amplification is needed.
