Let R=u′v′ be the eddy momentum flux, F=ρ′v′​ the eddy density flux, and S=dρs​/dz<0. The PDF's hydrostatic equation is gρ​=−p​z​; the converted TeX mistakenly replaces the pressure derivative by a density derivative. With N02​=−gS/ρ0​, define
A=F/S,v∗=va​−Az​,w∗=wa​+Ay​.
(38)
The residual mean circulation is nondivergent because vy∗​+wz∗​=vay​+waz​. Substituting in the original mean momentum and density equations gives the transformed Eulerian mean equations
ut​−f0​v∗=−Ry​+f0​Az​=∂y​Fy​+∂z​Fz​,​ρ​t​+Sw∗=0,vy∗​+wz∗​=0,​
(39)
with the unchanged geostrophic and hydrostatic balances. Here the Eliassen–Palm flux components are
Fy​=−R,Fz​=f0​A=N02​f0​v′b′​,b′=−gρ′/ρ0​.
(40)
The eddy buoyancy flux has been absorbed into the residual transport, while both types of wave forcing enter the momentum equation through the Eliassen–Palm flux divergence.
For geostrophic disturbances, the Taylor identity for quasi-geostrophic flux gives
∇⋅F=v′q′​.
(41)
For example, zonal averaging removes ψx′​ψxx′​​ and converts the remaining horizontal and vertical terms in ψx′​q′​ into derivatives of ψx′​ψy′​​ and (f02​/N02​)ψx′​ψz′​​, giving precisely the displayed fluxes.
Locally, for a basic flow with nonzero meridional PV gradient Qy​, the quasi-geostrophic wave pseudomomentum is A=q′2​/(2Qy​). Multiplying the linear perturbation PV equation by q′/Qy​ gives
At​+∇⋅F=Qy​q′D​​,
(42)
where D is a PV source or sink. In a locally uniform conservative Rossby wave packet, F=Acg​. Thus the flux tracks group velocity rather than the generally different phase velocity, with a signed wave activity if Qy​<0.
The non-acceleration theorem for quasi-geostrophic waves states that statistically steady conservative waves, with no wave-activity sources, sinks or independent boundary forcing, produce no balanced mean acceleration. Their flux divergence vanishes. In the present stable, impermeable mean-flow problem the homogeneous residual-circulation equation then has the zero solution, so v∗=w∗=0 and ut​=0. The Eulerian mean flow can still circulate because its eddy-induced correction need not vanish. Dissipation, absorption at a critical layer, time-dependent wave activity or boundary forcing produces a nonzero flux divergence and invalidates the non-acceleration conclusion.
