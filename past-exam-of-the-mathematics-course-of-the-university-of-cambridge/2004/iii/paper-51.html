The canonical momentum is π=ϕt​, so the Hamiltonian density and total energy are
H=πϕt​−L=21​ϕt2​+21​ϕx2​+U(ϕ),E=∫R​(21​ϕt2​+21​ϕx2​+U)dx.​
(1)
The Euler-Lagrange field equation is ϕtt​−ϕxx​+U′(ϕ)=0. Multiplication by ϕt​ gives the local conservation of energy identity
∂t​H=∂x​(ϕt​ϕx​).
(2)
Thus E is conserved when the energy flux vanishes at spatial infinity. For finite energy with asymptotically constant fields, the endpoints ϕ±​ must be scalar-field vacua, where U=0.
Choose an antiderivative G′(ϕ)=2U(ϕ)​. For either sign s=±1, completing the square gives
E=21​∫ϕt2​dx+21​∫(ϕx​−s2U​)2dx+s[G(ϕ+​)−G(ϕ−​)].
(3)
Choosing s to make the boundary term nonnegative proves the Bogomolny bound
E≥​∫ϕ−​ϕ+​​2U(a)​da​,ϕt​=0,ϕx​=s2U(ϕ)​ at saturation.​
(4)
These Bogomolny equations obtained by square completion for a one-dimensional kink describe minima within a fixed endpoint sector when a saturating configuration exists. Differentiating the spatial equation gives ϕxx​=U′(ϕ) wherever U>0, with smooth extension to its limiting scalar-field vacua. The absolute minimum across all sectors is a constant zero-potential scalar-field vacuum when one exists; the nontrivial minima are sector-specific kinks.
For β=0, write b=∣β∣. The scalar-field vacua are −b,0,b. A static finite-energy solution has the first integral
21​ϕx2​−U(ϕ)=0,
(5)
because differentiating its left side gives ϕx​(ϕxx​−U′)=0, and its value is zero at infinity. In either adjacent interval, 2U​=∣ϕ∣(b2−ϕ2). Set y=ϕ2/b2. The first-order equation becomes yx​=η2b2y(1−y), with η=±1, and separation gives log[y/(1−y)]=2ηb2(x−x0​). Hence
ϕσ,η​(x)=1+e−2ηb2(x−x0​)​σb​,σ,η∈{1,−1}.​
(6)
For η=1 the endpoints are 0→σb; for η=−1 they are σb→0. Thus there are four oriented static kink families, up to translation, namely two increasing kinks and two decreasing antikinks. Each has rest energy
∫0b​a(b2−a2)da=4b4​.
(7)
A static field joining −b directly to b would have to pass through ϕ=0 at finite x. The first integral then gives ϕx​=0, and the Picard-Lindelöf theorem applied to the smooth second-order equation with those initial conditions forces ϕ≡0. Therefore there is no such additional kink; this is the intermediate-vacuum obstruction to a kink. The Bogomolny classification of a rescaled phi-six kink also shows that if β=0, only the scalar-field vacuum 0 remains and there is no nontrivial finite-energy static kink. Indeed the first integral gives a monotone field wherever it is nonzero, precluding a return to the same scalar-field vacuum.
A Lorentz boost of the 0→b profile gives a moving solution with arbitrary ∣v∣<1:
ϕ(x,t)=1+exp[−2b2γ(x−vt−x0​)]​b​,γ=(1−v2)−1/2.​
(8)
If K denotes the static profile and ξ=γ(x−vt−x0​), then ϕtt​−ϕxx​=γ2(v2−1)K′′=−K′′, proving the Euler-Lagrange field equation directly. Its energy is γb4/4: since K′2=2U(K), changing variable to ξ in the energy integral gives the factor γ. Reversing σ or η yields the other moving elementary profiles.

Use the Minkowski metric convention η=diag(1,−1,−1,−1). On the Adjoint representation, the adjoint covariant derivative is Dμ​X=∂μ​X+[Aμ​,X]. Set aμ​=δAμ​ and χ=δΦ. Then
δFμν​=Dμ​aν​−Dν​aμ​,δ(Dμ​Φ)=Dμ​χ+[aμ​,Φ].
(9)
The trace obeys Tr(X[Y,Z])=Tr([Z,X]Y), and covariant integration by parts has the ordinary boundary term because the trace of a commutator is zero. Thus compactly supported variations give
δS=∫d4xTr(=∫d4xTr(​FμνDμ​aν​−DμΦDμ​χ−DμΦ[aμ​,Φ]+m2Φχ)[−Dμ​Fμν+[DνΦ,Φ]]aν​+[Dμ​DμΦ+m2Φ]χ).​
(10)
The trace pairing on su(2) is nondegenerate, so the Euler-Lagrange field equations are
Dμ​Fμν=[DνΦ,Φ],Dμ​DμΦ+m2Φ=0.​
(11)
In particular the sign of the scalar mass term follows from the plus sign in the supplied trace Lagrangian density; it should not be guessed from a different metric convention. This is the adjoint Yang-Mills-Higgs trace variation.
For the static Bogomolny-Prasad-Sommerfield monopole, take the usual purely magnetic extension ∂0​Ai​=∂0​Φ=0 and temporal gauge A0​=0. At m=0, the equations reduce to
Dj​Fji​=[Φ,Di​Φ],Di​Di​Φ=0,
(12)
with the ν=0 equation identically zero. We now verify both spatial equations from Bi​=Di​Φ without assuming them. The gauge-theory Bianchi identity gives Di​Bi​=0, hence immediately Di​Di​Φ=0. Also Fji​=εjik​Bk​, so
Dj​Fji​​=εjik​Dj​Dk​Φ=21​εjik​[Dj​,Dk​]Φ=21​εjik​[Fjk​,Φ]=−[Bi​,Φ]=[Φ,Di​Φ].​
(13)
Here εjik​εjkℓ​=−2δiℓ​. This proves that the Bogomolny equations imply all static zero-mass Yang-Mills equations and the scalar equation. The specification of A0​ matters: the spatial Bogomolny equations by themselves do not constrain an arbitrary extra electric potential, so the implication is for their standard purely magnetic static extension.

Let F+​=(F+∗F)/2 and F−​=(F−∗F)/2, using the oriented Euclidean metric. For a self-dual two-form ω,
ω∧F=⟨ω,∗F⟩vol=⟨ω,F+​⟩vol.
(14)
Apply this componentwise in the Lie algebra. Since the three ωi​ span the self-dual subspace, their vanishing wedges force F+​=0. Thus ∗F=−F. The gauge-theory Bianchi identity is DA​F=0, so DA​∗F=−DA​F=0, the Euclidean Yang-Mills equations. This proves the implication locally; finite action would additionally be needed to call the solution an instanton.
Use the complex orientation for w=x1+ix2 and z=x3+ix4. The real and imaginary parts of dw∧dz, and i(dw∧dwˉ+dz∧dzˉ), span the self-dual two-forms. Accordingly the three gauge curvature equations are
Fwz​=0,Fwˉzˉ​=0,Fwwˉ​+Fzzˉ​=0.
(15)
For a spectral parameter λ∈CP1, consider the Lax pair
(Dw​−λDzˉ​)Ψ=0,(Dz​+λDwˉ​)Ψ=0.​
(16)
Their commutator is
[Dw​−λDzˉ​,Dz​+λDwˉ​]=Fwz​+λ(Fwwˉ​+Fzzˉ​)+λ2Fwˉzˉ​.
(17)
It vanishes for every λ exactly when all three coefficients vanish. The derivative directions ∂w​−λ∂zˉ​ and ∂z​+λ∂wˉ​ are mutually orthogonal null vectors for the complexified metric. Flatness on the corresponding two-planes makes the linear parallel-transport equations locally compatible. Conversely compatibility for an invertible fundamental matrix Ψ forces the commutator to vanish; a single zero solution would not establish it. This is the Euclidean complex-coordinate anti-self-dual Lax pair.
For an Abelian U(1) connection, all commutators of the connection components vanish, and the gauge curvature equations therefore read
∂w​Az​−∂z​Aw​∂wˉ​Azˉ​−∂zˉ​Awˉ​∂z​Azˉ​−∂zˉ​Az​+∂w​Awˉ​−∂wˉ​Aw​​=0,=0,=0.​​
(18)
Locally the first two equations say that A1,0 is ∂-closed and A0,1 is ∂ˉ-closed. The Dolbeault-Poincaré lemma, and its complex conjugate, give scalar functions u,v with
A=∂u+∂ˉv.
(19)
Substituting in the third equation gives
(v−u)zzˉ​+(v−u)wwˉ​=0.
(20)
In a complex gauge transformation, use g=e−u and the derivative-plus-connection convention A↦A+g−1dg. Then
A′=∂ˉf,f=v−u,fzzˉ​+fwwˉ​=0.​
(21)
This is the scalar Laplace equation, since for the stated metric Δ=2(∂z​∂zˉ​+∂w​∂wˉ​). The complex gauge transformation need not remain in U(1).
A reduction using only a unitary gauge transformation is also available. Since A is anti-Hermitian, Awˉ​=−Aw​​ and Azˉ​=−Az​​, so the potential for the barred components can be chosen as v=−uˉ. Write u=p+iq with real p,q. Then A=∂p−∂ˉp+idq, and g=e−iq removes the last term. With f=−2p this gives
A′=21​(∂ˉf−∂f),f real,fzzˉ​+fwwˉ​=0.​
(22)
Its gauge curvature is ∂∂ˉf. Thus the Abelian anti-self-dual connections from harmonic scalar potentials reduction is local and does not require silently enlarging the real gauge group.

Interpret triviality as holomorphic triviality. Choose a global holomorphic bundle frame s1​,…,sk​ for E. Let Hα​ have as its columns the coordinates of these sections in the prescribed local frame over Uα​, with the convention that a fiber coordinate vector transforms as sβ​=Fαβ​sα​. Then
Hβ​=Fαβ​Hα​,Fαβ​=Hβ​Hα−1​.​
(23)
Each Hα​ is holomorphic and invertible because its columns form a basis in every fiber. This proves the requested holomorphic splitting of transition functions on a trivial bundle. Smooth or topological triviality alone would not justify holomorphic splitting.
For the line bundle on projective twistor space, pull its patching function back to the twistor line Lx​ using the incidence convention of this problem, ωA=xAA′πA′​, without an extra factor of i. Triviality on each line gives nonvanishing linewise holomorphic functions H0​,H1​ with F01​=H1​H0−1​. The factors can be chosen as holomorphic functions of x locally: a degree-zero scalar transition function has zero winding on the overlap annulus, hence admits a logarithm there; splitting its positive and negative Laurent series parts gives factors whose coefficients are Cauchy integrals that are holomorphic functions of x. The explicit splitting is worked out below. Their only common linewise ambiguity is multiplication by a nonzero function g(x): the ratio is a global holomorphic function on the compact complex projective line, hence constant on that line.
Take ϵ0′1′​=1, ϵ0′1′=−1 and raise a two-component spinor by left multiplication with ϵA′B′. The derivative along an alpha-plane is ℓA​=πA′∂AA′​. It annihilates the unsplit twistor function, because
ℓA​ωB=δAB​πA′πA′​=0,ℓA​F01​=0.
(24)
Thus
GA​=H0−1​ℓA​H0​=H1−1​ℓA​H1​
(25)
is holomorphic on the whole line and has projective weight one. A section of O(1) is linear in the homogeneous coordinates: in an affine coordinate, holomorphic regularity at infinity bounds its growth to at most first order, so the Cauchy integral formula excludes all higher powers. Therefore
GA​=πA′AAA′​(x).
(26)
The inverse splitting functions obey (ℓA​+GA​)Hα−1​=0. Commuting these two equations proves
πA′πB′FAA′BB′​=0.
(27)
The spinor decomposition of gauge curvature is
FAA′BB′​=ϵAB​φA′B′​+ϵA′B′​φAB​,
(28)
where both φ two-component spinors are symmetric. Vanishing of the contraction for every π forces φA′B′​=0. This is exactly the anti-self-dual Maxwell equations. Since the Abelian gauge curvature is dA, it is closed; its duality condition also makes it co-closed, giving the source-free Maxwell equations. Changing both splitting factors by g(x) changes A by dlogg, the expected Abelian gauge transformation. This derives the rank-one Penrose-Ward correspondence in the needed local form.
For the exponential patching function, choose a representative f of the Čech cohomology class, of homogeneous degree zero. On the overlap of the two line patches write fx​(ζ)=f(xπ,π)=∑n∈Z​fn​(x)ζn. To compute the gauge potential explicitly, use a constant two-component spinor basis in which ιA′​=(1,0) and the finite patch π0′​=ζ,π1′​=1. Then π0′=−1, π1′=ζ and
ιC′​πC′=−1,πD′​dπD′=dζ,ωB=xB0′ζ+xB1′.
(29)
For a positively oriented contour in the overlap annulus, split its Laurent series as
h0​=−∑n≥0​fn​ζn,h1​=∑n<0​fn​ζn,fx​=h1​−h0​.
(30)
The first is holomorphic inside the contour, the second outside including infinity; Hα​=ehα​ split ef. Since (−∂B0′​+ζ∂B1′​)fx​=0, coefficient comparison gives ∂B0′​fn​=∂B1′​fn−1​. Applying that operator separately to h0​,h1​ cancels all nonconstant terms and leaves
GB​=∂B1′​f−1​.
(31)
Thus −AB0′​+ζAB1′​=GB​ yields AB1′​=0 and AB0′​=−∂B1′​f−1​. Moreover
f−1​=2πi1​∮Γ​fx​dζ,∂B1′​fx​=f,B​:=∂ωB∂f​.
(32)
Substitution gives AB0′​=−(2πi)−1∮f,B​dζ, which in homogeneous form is precisely
ABB′​=2πi1​∮Γ​ιC′​πC′ιB′​​f,B​(xπ,π)πD′​dπD′.​
(33)
This is the Abelian Ward splitting by Laurent series. The factors have weights −1,−1,2, respectively, so the integrand is invariant under a change of homogeneous representative. The contour avoids ιC′​πC′=0; the basis chosen above places that point at infinity. One can keep the contour fixed for x in a sufficiently small domain, allowing differentiation under the integral.
Finally differentiate the contour potential for an anti-self-dual Maxwell field. By the twistor incidence relation, ∂AA′​f,B​=πA′​f,AB​. Consequently
FAA′BB′​​=2πi1​∮Γ​ιC′​πC′πA′​ιB′​−πB′​ιA′​​f,AB​πD′​dπD′=ϵA′B′​2πi1​∮Γ​f,AB​πD′​dπD′.​
(34)
The second equality uses the elementary two-spinor identity πA′​ιB′​−πB′​ιA′​=ϵA′B′​(ιC′​πC′) in the declared convention. The remaining two-component spinor is symmetric in A,B, and no primed symmetric gauge curvature part survives. This directly verifies dA is anti-self-dual. In complexified Lorentzian signature duality has Hodge star eigenvalues ±i; here the vanishing primed two-component spinor fixes the stated ASD convention rather than incorrectly imposing the Euclidean equation ∗F=−F on that signature.
