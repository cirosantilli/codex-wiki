Let IX​,IY​ be the original rates and μX​,μY​ their unique zero points. Suppose first that H<G. At speed bn​=n2(1−G), the preceding argument gives X the rate zero at μX​ and infinity elsewhere, while Y retains IY​. Independence allows the product large-deviation principle, and addition is continuous, so the contraction principle for large deviations gives the sum rate
K(z)=infx+y=z​{IX′​(x)+IY​(y)}=IY​(z−μX​).
(10)
This is good, has unique zero μX​+μY​, and inherits a strictly positive finite value by translation from IY​. Thus the sum has Hurstiness G. The case G<H is identical with the roles exchanged.
If G=H, the same product and contraction results apply to the original rates at their common speed, giving their infimal convolution
K(z)=infx+y=z​{IX​(x)+IY​(y)}.
(11)
It is good. Every finite fiber infimum is attained: a minimizing sequence lies in a product of compact sublevel sets and has a subsequence converging in the closed addition fiber. Consequently its only zero is μX​+μY​. Choose x^ with 0<IX​(x^)<∞ and set z^=x^+μY​. Then z^ differs from the zero point and 0<K(z^)≤IX​(x^)<∞. The nondegeneracy requirement is therefore satisfied here too. In all cases,
Hurstiness(Xn​+Yn​)=max(H,G).​
(12)
The general results used are the independent product large-deviation theorem and the continuous contraction principle. Checking the zero set and a positive finite value is necessary in addition to obtaining an LDP.
