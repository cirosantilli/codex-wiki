OurBigBook.com Words: 297
For a finite-dimensional real Lie algebra h, the Lie third theorem asserts the existence of a connected simply connected Lie group whose Lie algebra is h. There are two complementary views of the construction.
Locally, the Baker--Campbell--Hausdorff formula defines multiplication on a sufficiently small neighbourhood of zero in h. Its leading terms are X+Y+21​[X,Y], its identity is zero, and inversion is X↦−X. The convergent BCH series in finite dimensions and the Jacobi identity provide the local associative law. Continuing and globalizing this local group gives its simply connected integration; the globalization is a substantive step, rather than a claim that one exponential chart gives all of the group.
A matrix construction makes that global step more concrete. The Ado theorem gives a faithful finite-dimensional representation
ι:h↪glN​(R).
(28)
Consider the left-invariant distribution DA​=Aι(h) on GLN​(R). It is involutive because ι(h) is closed under commutators. The Frobenius theorem integrates it to its connected leaf through the identity. This leaf carries the intrinsic immersed-submanifold structure of the connected Lie subgroup generated by the matrix exponentials eι(X). Its tangent Lie algebra is exactly ι(h); multiplication and inversion preserve the leaf and give its Lie group operations. Call this immersed group H0​.
Taking its universal covering Lie group gives
Lie(H0​)≅h,H0​ connected and simply connected.​
(29)
Ado's faithfulness matters: the adjoint representation alone loses central elements. Also, the subgroup generated by the exponentials need not be closed in the ambient matrix group. For an irrational a, the map t↦(Rt​,Rat​) gives a dense one-dimensional immersed subgroup of the two-dimensional torus. Its intrinsic group is R, whereas its closure has a two-dimensional Lie algebra. Taking an ambient closure in the construction would therefore be incorrect.
Finally, any other connected Lie group with Lie algebra h is obtained from this simply connected integration by a discrete central quotient, as in part (i). The simply connected integration is unique up to isomorphism, but an arbitrary corresponding group is not.
