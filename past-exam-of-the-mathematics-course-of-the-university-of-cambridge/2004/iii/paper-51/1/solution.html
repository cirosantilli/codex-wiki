The canonical momentum is π=ϕt​, so the Hamiltonian density and total energy are
H=πϕt​−L=21​ϕt2​+21​ϕx2​+U(ϕ),E=∫R​(21​ϕt2​+21​ϕx2​+U)dx.​
(1)
The Euler-Lagrange field equation is ϕtt​−ϕxx​+U′(ϕ)=0. Multiplication by ϕt​ gives the local conservation of energy identity
∂t​H=∂x​(ϕt​ϕx​).
(2)
Thus E is conserved when the energy flux vanishes at spatial infinity. For finite energy with asymptotically constant fields, the endpoints ϕ±​ must be scalar-field vacua, where U=0.
Choose an antiderivative G′(ϕ)=2U(ϕ)​. For either sign s=±1, completing the square gives
E=21​∫ϕt2​dx+21​∫(ϕx​−s2U​)2dx+s[G(ϕ+​)−G(ϕ−​)].
(3)
Choosing s to make the boundary term nonnegative proves the Bogomolny bound
E≥​∫ϕ−​ϕ+​​2U(a)​da​,ϕt​=0,ϕx​=s2U(ϕ)​ at saturation.​
(4)
These Bogomolny equations obtained by square completion for a one-dimensional kink describe minima within a fixed endpoint sector when a saturating configuration exists. Differentiating the spatial equation gives ϕxx​=U′(ϕ) wherever U>0, with smooth extension to its limiting scalar-field vacua. The absolute minimum across all sectors is a constant zero-potential scalar-field vacuum when one exists; the nontrivial minima are sector-specific kinks.
For β=0, write b=∣β∣. The scalar-field vacua are −b,0,b. A static finite-energy solution has the first integral
21​ϕx2​−U(ϕ)=0,
(5)
because differentiating its left side gives ϕx​(ϕxx​−U′)=0, and its value is zero at infinity. In either adjacent interval, 2U​=∣ϕ∣(b2−ϕ2). Set y=ϕ2/b2. The first-order equation becomes yx​=η2b2y(1−y), with η=±1, and separation gives log[y/(1−y)]=2ηb2(x−x0​). Hence
ϕσ,η​(x)=1+e−2ηb2(x−x0​)​σb​,σ,η∈{1,−1}.​
(6)
For η=1 the endpoints are 0→σb; for η=−1 they are σb→0. Thus there are four oriented static kink families, up to translation, namely two increasing kinks and two decreasing antikinks. Each has rest energy
∫0b​a(b2−a2)da=4b4​.
(7)
A static field joining −b directly to b would have to pass through ϕ=0 at finite x. The first integral then gives ϕx​=0, and the Picard-Lindelöf theorem applied to the smooth second-order equation with those initial conditions forces ϕ≡0. Therefore there is no such additional kink; this is the intermediate-vacuum obstruction to a kink. The Bogomolny classification of a rescaled phi-six kink also shows that if β=0, only the scalar-field vacuum 0 remains and there is no nontrivial finite-energy static kink. Indeed the first integral gives a monotone field wherever it is nonzero, precluding a return to the same scalar-field vacuum.
A Lorentz boost of the 0→b profile gives a moving solution with arbitrary ∣v∣<1:
ϕ(x,t)=1+exp[−2b2γ(x−vt−x0​)]​b​,γ=(1−v2)−1/2.​
(8)
If K denotes the static profile and ξ=γ(x−vt−x0​), then ϕtt​−ϕxx​=γ2(v2−1)K′′=−K′′, proving the Euler-Lagrange field equation directly. Its energy is γb4/4: since K′2=2U(K), changing variable to ξ in the energy integral gives the factor γ. Reversing σ or η yields the other moving elementary profiles.
