Let F+​=(F+∗F)/2 and F−​=(F−∗F)/2, using the oriented Euclidean metric. For a self-dual two-form ω,
ω∧F=⟨ω,∗F⟩vol=⟨ω,F+​⟩vol.
(14)
Apply this componentwise in the Lie algebra. Since the three ωi​ span the self-dual subspace, their vanishing wedges force F+​=0. Thus ∗F=−F. The gauge-theory Bianchi identity is DA​F=0, so DA​∗F=−DA​F=0, the Euclidean Yang-Mills equations. This proves the implication locally; finite action would additionally be needed to call the solution an instanton.
Use the complex orientation for w=x1+ix2 and z=x3+ix4. The real and imaginary parts of dw∧dz, and i(dw∧dwˉ+dz∧dzˉ), span the self-dual two-forms. Accordingly the three gauge curvature equations are
Fwz​=0,Fwˉzˉ​=0,Fwwˉ​+Fzzˉ​=0.
(15)
For a spectral parameter λ∈CP1, consider the Lax pair
(Dw​−λDzˉ​)Ψ=0,(Dz​+λDwˉ​)Ψ=0.​
(16)
Their commutator is
[Dw​−λDzˉ​,Dz​+λDwˉ​]=Fwz​+λ(Fwwˉ​+Fzzˉ​)+λ2Fwˉzˉ​.
(17)
It vanishes for every λ exactly when all three coefficients vanish. The derivative directions ∂w​−λ∂zˉ​ and ∂z​+λ∂wˉ​ are mutually orthogonal null vectors for the complexified metric. Flatness on the corresponding two-planes makes the linear parallel-transport equations locally compatible. Conversely compatibility for an invertible fundamental matrix Ψ forces the commutator to vanish; a single zero solution would not establish it. This is the Euclidean complex-coordinate anti-self-dual Lax pair.
For an Abelian U(1) connection, all commutators of the connection components vanish, and the gauge curvature equations therefore read
∂w​Az​−∂z​Aw​∂wˉ​Azˉ​−∂zˉ​Awˉ​∂z​Azˉ​−∂zˉ​Az​+∂w​Awˉ​−∂wˉ​Aw​​=0,=0,=0.​​
(18)
Locally the first two equations say that A1,0 is ∂-closed and A0,1 is ∂ˉ-closed. The Dolbeault-Poincaré lemma, and its complex conjugate, give scalar functions u,v with
A=∂u+∂ˉv.
(19)
Substituting in the third equation gives
(v−u)zzˉ​+(v−u)wwˉ​=0.
(20)
In a complex gauge transformation, use g=e−u and the derivative-plus-connection convention A↦A+g−1dg. Then
A′=∂ˉf,f=v−u,fzzˉ​+fwwˉ​=0.​
(21)
This is the scalar Laplace equation, since for the stated metric Δ=2(∂z​∂zˉ​+∂w​∂wˉ​). The complex gauge transformation need not remain in U(1).
A reduction using only a unitary gauge transformation is also available. Since A is anti-Hermitian, Awˉ​=−Aw​​ and Azˉ​=−Az​​, so the potential for the barred components can be chosen as v=−uˉ. Write u=p+iq with real p,q. Then A=∂p−∂ˉp+idq, and g=e−iq removes the last term. With f=−2p this gives
A′=21​(∂ˉf−∂f),f real,fzzˉ​+fwwˉ​=0.​
(22)
Its gauge curvature is ∂∂ˉf. Thus the Abelian anti-self-dual connections from harmonic scalar potentials reduction is local and does not require silently enlarging the real gauge group.
