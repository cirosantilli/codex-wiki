Interpret triviality as holomorphic triviality. Choose a global holomorphic bundle frame s1​,…,sk​ for E. Let Hα​ have as its columns the coordinates of these sections in the prescribed local frame over Uα​, with the convention that a fiber coordinate vector transforms as sβ​=Fαβ​sα​. Then
Hβ​=Fαβ​Hα​,Fαβ​=Hβ​Hα−1​.​
(23)
Each Hα​ is holomorphic and invertible because its columns form a basis in every fiber. This proves the requested holomorphic splitting of transition functions on a trivial bundle. Smooth or topological triviality alone would not justify holomorphic splitting.
For the line bundle on projective twistor space, pull its patching function back to the twistor line Lx​ using the incidence convention of this problem, ωA=xAA′πA′​, without an extra factor of i. Triviality on each line gives nonvanishing linewise holomorphic functions H0​,H1​ with F01​=H1​H0−1​. The factors can be chosen as holomorphic functions of x locally: a degree-zero scalar transition function has zero winding on the overlap annulus, hence admits a logarithm there; splitting its positive and negative Laurent series parts gives factors whose coefficients are Cauchy integrals that are holomorphic functions of x. The explicit splitting is worked out below. Their only common linewise ambiguity is multiplication by a nonzero function g(x): the ratio is a global holomorphic function on the compact complex projective line, hence constant on that line.
Take ϵ0′1′​=1, ϵ0′1′=−1 and raise a two-component spinor by left multiplication with ϵA′B′. The derivative along an alpha-plane is ℓA​=πA′∂AA′​. It annihilates the unsplit twistor function, because
ℓA​ωB=δAB​πA′πA′​=0,ℓA​F01​=0.
(24)
Thus
GA​=H0−1​ℓA​H0​=H1−1​ℓA​H1​
(25)
is holomorphic on the whole line and has projective weight one. A section of O(1) is linear in the homogeneous coordinates: in an affine coordinate, holomorphic regularity at infinity bounds its growth to at most first order, so the Cauchy integral formula excludes all higher powers. Therefore
GA​=πA′AAA′​(x).
(26)
The inverse splitting functions obey (ℓA​+GA​)Hα−1​=0. Commuting these two equations proves
πA′πB′FAA′BB′​=0.
(27)
The spinor decomposition of gauge curvature is
FAA′BB′​=ϵAB​φA′B′​+ϵA′B′​φAB​,
(28)
where both φ two-component spinors are symmetric. Vanishing of the contraction for every π forces φA′B′​=0. This is exactly the anti-self-dual Maxwell equations. Since the Abelian gauge curvature is dA, it is closed; its duality condition also makes it co-closed, giving the source-free Maxwell equations. Changing both splitting factors by g(x) changes A by dlogg, the expected Abelian gauge transformation. This derives the rank-one Penrose-Ward correspondence in the needed local form.
For the exponential patching function, choose a representative f of the Čech cohomology class, of homogeneous degree zero. On the overlap of the two line patches write fx​(ζ)=f(xπ,π)=∑n∈Z​fn​(x)ζn. To compute the gauge potential explicitly, use a constant two-component spinor basis in which ιA′​=(1,0) and the finite patch π0′​=ζ,π1′​=1. Then π0′=−1, π1′=ζ and
ιC′​πC′=−1,πD′​dπD′=dζ,ωB=xB0′ζ+xB1′.
(29)
For a positively oriented contour in the overlap annulus, split its Laurent series as
h0​=−∑n≥0​fn​ζn,h1​=∑n<0​fn​ζn,fx​=h1​−h0​.
(30)
The first is holomorphic inside the contour, the second outside including infinity; Hα​=ehα​ split ef. Since (−∂B0′​+ζ∂B1′​)fx​=0, coefficient comparison gives ∂B0′​fn​=∂B1′​fn−1​. Applying that operator separately to h0​,h1​ cancels all nonconstant terms and leaves
GB​=∂B1′​f−1​.
(31)
Thus −AB0′​+ζAB1′​=GB​ yields AB1′​=0 and AB0′​=−∂B1′​f−1​. Moreover
f−1​=2πi1​∮Γ​fx​dζ,∂B1′​fx​=f,B​:=∂ωB∂f​.
(32)
Substitution gives AB0′​=−(2πi)−1∮f,B​dζ, which in homogeneous form is precisely
ABB′​=2πi1​∮Γ​ιC′​πC′ιB′​​f,B​(xπ,π)πD′​dπD′.​
(33)
This is the Abelian Ward splitting by Laurent series. The factors have weights −1,−1,2, respectively, so the integrand is invariant under a change of homogeneous representative. The contour avoids ιC′​πC′=0; the basis chosen above places that point at infinity. One can keep the contour fixed for x in a sufficiently small domain, allowing differentiation under the integral.
Finally differentiate the contour potential for an anti-self-dual Maxwell field. By the twistor incidence relation, ∂AA′​f,B​=πA′​f,AB​. Consequently
FAA′BB′​​=2πi1​∮Γ​ιC′​πC′πA′​ιB′​−πB′​ιA′​​f,AB​πD′​dπD′=ϵA′B′​2πi1​∮Γ​f,AB​πD′​dπD′.​
(34)
The second equality uses the elementary two-spinor identity πA′​ιB′​−πB′​ιA′​=ϵA′B′​(ιC′​πC′) in the declared convention. The remaining two-component spinor is symmetric in A,B, and no primed symmetric gauge curvature part survives. This directly verifies dA is anti-self-dual. In complexified Lorentzian signature duality has Hodge star eigenvalues ±i; here the vanishing primed two-component spinor fixes the stated ASD convention rather than incorrectly imposing the Euclidean equation ∗F=−F on that signature.
