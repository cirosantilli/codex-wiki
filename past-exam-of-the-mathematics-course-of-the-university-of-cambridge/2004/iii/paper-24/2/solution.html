The partition relation λ→(μ)νr​ says that every colouring of the r-element subsets of a set of cardinality λ into ν colours has a homogeneous set of cardinality μ. Define the beth numbers over an infinite cardinal κ by ℶ0​(κ)=κ and ℶn+1​(κ)=2ℶn​(κ). The Erdős-Rado theorem for finite arities gives the precise bound
ℶn​(κ)+⟶(κ+)κn+1​(n<ω).​
(6)
Thus any fixed finite arity and any fixed infinite number of colours admit arbitrarily large infinite monochromatic sets once the starting cardinal is sufficiently large. For pairs, the especially useful case is (2κ)+→(κ+)κ2​. The successor in this bound matters: a binary first-difference colouring on a suitably ordered set of binary sequences supplies the familiar obstruction at 2κ. Also, a theorem for every fixed finite arity is not a theorem homogenizing all arities simultaneously.
The mechanism is to build an end-homogeneous routing tree: each new vertex is routed according to its colours to earlier vertices on its branch. A long branch is end-homogeneous, meaning the colour from a fixed earlier vertex is independent of the later vertex. There are at most κα≤2κ routes of length α<κ+, and the whole collection of short routes has size at most 2κ. Inserting (2κ)+ vertices therefore forces a branch of length κ+. One colour occurs at κ+ earlier vertices on that branch, giving the pair theorem. Iterating the higher-arity version of this routing construction explains the successive powers in the Erdős-Rado theorem for finite arities.
A weakly compact cardinal is an uncountable strongly inaccessible cardinal κ with
κ⟶(κ)22​.
(7)
Equivalent formulations include the tree property at the inaccessible cardinal and compactness for theories of size at most κ in the infinitary language Lκ,κ​: if every subtheory of size <κ has a first-order model, so does the theory.
A measurable cardinal is an uncountable κ carrying a nonprincipal ultrafilter closed under intersections of fewer than κ members. Such an ultrafilter contains no set of size <κ, since all singletons have complements in it and it is kappa-complete. Consequently a cofinal sequence of length <κ would partition κ into too few small pieces, so κ is regular cardinal. If λ<κ and κ distinct subsets of λ existed, choose, for each coordinate of λ, the bit selected by the ultrafilter. Kappa-completeness would make all those bits simultaneously constant on a large set of indices; only one distinct subset could have that bit pattern, a contradiction. Hence 2λ<κ: κ is also a strong limit cardinal.
A supercompact cardinal κ has, for every λ≥κ, a fine ultrafilter that is a normal ultrafilter on small subsets and is kappa-complete on
Pκ​(λ)={x⊆λ:∣x∣<κ}.
(8)
Fine means {x:α∈x} is large for every α<λ. Normal means that a function f with f(x)∈x on a large set is constant on some large set. Equivalently, for every such λ there is an elementary embedding j:V→M with critical point κ, j(κ)>λ, and M closed under λ-sequences from the universe.
These notions form the implication chain supercompact ⇒ measurable ⇒ weakly compact. For the first implication, an embedding as above gives the measure U={X⊆κ:κ∈j(X)}. For the second, use a normal ultrafilter on a cardinal obtained by normalizing its measure. Indeed the ultrapower embedding gives the measure U={X⊆κ:κ∈j(X)}; for a regressive f, j(f)(κ)<κ is a fixed ordinal, yielding the required constant large fibre. Given a pair-colouring, select for each α the measure-one colour on the tail above α. One selected colour occurs on a measure-one set of α's. Normality makes the diagonal intersection of their selected tails measure one; any two members of that intersection have the selected colour. This gives the required partition relation, and the preceding regularity and strong-limit argument gives inaccessibility.
