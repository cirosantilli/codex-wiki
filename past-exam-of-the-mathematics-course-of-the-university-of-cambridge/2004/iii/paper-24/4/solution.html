Use the rooted-tree homeomorphic embedding relation: an injective map of vertices preserves lowest common ancestors, so edges may become paths and the source root may map below the target root. We prove the stronger labelled statement for labels in any well-quasi-ordering Q, with labels increasing under the embedding. Forgetting labels gives Kruskal's tree theorem.
First every infinite sequence in a well-quasi-ordering has an infinite nondecreasing subsequence. Colour its index pairs according as the earlier term is or is not below the later term. Ramsey's theorem gives an infinite constant-colour subsequence; the bad colour would contradict well-quasi-ordering. Applying this successively in each coordinate proves finite product closure of well-quasi-orderings.
We need Higman lemma with its proof. Order finite words over Q by subsequence embedding with increased letters. If there were a bad sequence, choose one w0​,w1​,… minimally: at each position choose a shortest word admitting a bad continuation of the fixed prefix. No word is empty. Write wn​=un​an​, separating the last letter, and choose n0​<n1​<⋯ with an0​​≤an1​​≤⋯. Consider
w0​,…,wn0​−1​,un0​​,un1​​,….
(12)
An earlier prefix word cannot embed in a later unj​​, since it would then embed in wnj​​. Nor can uni​​ embed in unj​​: adding the comparable last letters would embed wni​​ in wnj​​. Thus the displayed sequence is bad, contradicting minimality at n0​, since un0​​ is shorter. Finite words over a well-quasi-order are well-quasi-ordered.
Now suppose labelled rooted trees admit a bad sequence T0​,T1​,…, chosen minimally in vertex count at each position. Let A consist of all proper rooted subtrees of these trees, rooted at a descendant and containing all its descendants. We claim A is well-quasi-ordered. Otherwise take a bad sequence S0​,S1​,… from A. Only finitely many subtrees come from any fixed finite prefix of the Tn​'s, and a bad sequence cannot repeat an object. Thin it so that Si​ comes from Tmi​​ with m0​<m1​<⋯. The sequence
T0​,…,Tm0​−1​,S0​,S1​,…
(13)
is bad: Tj​⪯Si​ would imply Tj​⪯Tmi​​, since a rooted subtree embeds in its host; comparisons within the Si​'s are excluded by their choice. But S0​ is strictly smaller than Tm0​​, contradicting minimality. This proves the claim.
Choose an arbitrary ordering of the children of each Tn​. Record its root label and the finite word of rooted child-subtrees:
(ℓn​, (Tn,1​,…,Tn,rn​​))∈Q×A∗.
(14)
By Higman lemma and finite product closure, two records, with indices i<j, are comparable. Map the root of Ti​ to the root of Tj​, and map its selected child-subtrees into the distinct selected branches of Tj​. Within a branch the embeddings preserve lowest common ancestors; between different branches both lowest common ancestors are the roots. Labels increase everywhere. This gives Ti​⪯Q​Tj​, contradicting badness. Finite labelled rooted trees are well-quasi-ordered by label-monotone homeomorphic embedding. In particular, this proves the unlabelled theorem. A root-preserving variant follows by giving the root a special incomparable label unavailable at nonroot vertices; the general labelled theorem then forces root to root. Adjacency-preserving embeddings are a different, more restrictive relation and do not satisfy the same assertion.
For Friedman's finite form of Kruskal's theorem, fix k∈N and consider finite bad sequences satisfying ∣Ti​∣≤k+i, with indices starting at 1. Their prefixes form a finite bad-sequence tree. At each depth there are only finitely many possible unlabelled tree isomorphism types below the size bound, so this tree is finitely branching. If bad sequences existed at every finite length, König infinity lemma would yield an infinite branch, hence an infinite bad sequence of rooted trees, contrary to what was just proved. Therefore
∀k ∃N ∀(T1​,…,TN​) [(∀i≤N) ∣Ti​∣≤k+i ⟹ (∃i<j≤N) Ti​⪯Tj​].​
(15)
The same proof works with any fixed finite label set. The finite size bounds are what make the bad-prefix tree finitely branching; the proof does not claim a small effective numerical bound.
