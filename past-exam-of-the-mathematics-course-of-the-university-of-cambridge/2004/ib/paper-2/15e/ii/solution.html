The weighted binary-sequence metric is finite because 0≤d(ϵ,η)≤∑n≥1​2−n=1. It is symmetric and nonnegative. If d(ϵ,η)=0, every nonnegative summand is zero, so every coordinate agrees; conversely agreement makes the sum zero. For three sequences, apply ∣ϵn​−ζn​∣≤∣ϵn​−ηn​∣+∣ηn​−ζn​∣ in each coordinate and sum. This proves the triangle inequality, so d is a metric.
If two sequences differ in one of their first n coordinates, their distance is at least 2−n. Thus the open ball Bd​(η,2−n) is contained in Cn​(η). For every η∈Cn​(ϵ), the latter cylinder equals Cn​(η), so each cylinder set is open in the metric topology. Every set in the specified topology, being a union of cylinder sets, is therefore metric-open.
Conversely, agreement in the first n coordinates gives
d(ϵ,η)≤∑k>n​2−k=2−n.
(29)
Given a metric-open set containing ϵ, choose a ball Bd​(ϵ,r) inside it, then choose n with 2−n<r. The bound gives Cn​(ϵ)⊆Bd​(ϵ,r), establishing the required prefix-neighbourhood condition. The two topologies coincide. The strict inequality 2−n<r avoids incorrectly treating the closed tail bound as a strict ball bound.
