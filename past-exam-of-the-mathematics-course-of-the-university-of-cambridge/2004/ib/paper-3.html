Nonnegativity and symmetry of the maximum product metric follow from those of the two factor metrics. Its value is zero precisely when both coordinates agree. For three points u=(x,x′), v=(y,y′), w=(z,z′), the two triangle inequalities give
D(u,w)≤max{d(x,y)+d(y,z),d′(x′,y′)+d′(y′,z′)}≤D(u,v)+D(v,w).
(8)
All metric axioms therefore hold.
When the two factors are the same metric space, consider an off-diagonal point (x,y) and set δ=d(x,y)>0. A product-metric ball of radius δ/3 cannot contain any (z,z): such membership would imply d(x,z),d(y,z)<δ/3 and then d(x,y)<2δ/3, a contradiction. The complement of the diagonal is thus open, so
Δ is closed in X×X.​
(9)
This proves the closed diagonal of a metric space. Equality of the factors here means equality as metric spaces, as intended; merely sharing an underlying set while allowing unrelated metrics would be a different assertion.

Let mα​(x) be the monic minimal polynomial. The Cayley-Hamilton theorem says χα​(α)=0. Divide χα​ by mα​; its remainder also annihilates α, and a nonzero remainder of smaller degree would contradict minimality. Thus mα​ divides χα​.
Every characteristic root λi​ is an eigenvalue, with a nonzero eigenvector vi​. Since mα​(α)vi​=mα​(λi​)vi​=0, it must be a root of the minimal polynomial as well. This proves there are no possibilities beyond
mα​(x)=i∏​(x−λi​)ri​,1≤ri​≤ni​.​
(32)
Every such choice really occurs. For each i, take one Jordan block of size ri​ at λi​, together with ni​−ri​ scalar blocks at that eigenvalue, and form their direct sum. The characteristic polynomial has the prescribed multiplicities. The nilpotent part of a block of size ri​ has its ri​th power zero and its (ri​−1)st power nonzero, so this component's minimal exponent is exactly ri​. A polynomial annihilates the direct sum precisely when it annihilates every component, establishing the displayed minimal polynomial without any extra restrictions.

The contraction mapping theorem states: if (X,d) is a nonempty complete metric space and f:X→X satisfies d(fx,fy)≤qd(x,y) for a fixed 0≤q<1, then it has exactly one fixed point and the iteration from every starting point converges to it.
To prove existence, choose x0​ and let xn+1​=f(xn​). Induction gives d(xn+1​,xn​)≤qnd(x1​,x0​). The triangle inequality and geometric sum then imply, for m>n,
d(xm​,xn​)≤1−qqn​d(x1​,x0​).
(43)
The iterates are a Cauchy sequence; completeness supplies their limit x∗​. The contraction is continuous, so f(x∗​)=limf(xn​)=limxn+1​=x∗​. If y∗​ is another fixed point, d(x∗​,y∗​)≤qd(x∗​,y∗​) forces equality of the points. The same estimate gives geometric convergence from every initial point.
For the particular map, the original PDF gives X=[a/2​,∞), which is a closed, complete subset of the real line. For positive x, the arithmetic-geometric mean inequality gives f(x)=(x+a/x)/2≥a​, proving f(X)⊂X. Furthermore
f′(x)=21​(1−x2a​),−21​≤f′(x)<21​(x∈X).
(44)
The mean value theorem therefore gives ∣f(x)−f(y)∣≤∣x−y∣/2, a genuine contraction. The fixed-point equation is x2=a, and only the positive root belongs to X, so
