A norm p is nonnegative with p(x)=0 exactly when x=0, satisfies p(ax)=∣a∣p(x), and obeys p(x+y)≤p(x)+p(y). The Euclidean norm has positivity and absolute homogeneity immediately. For the triangle inequality, positivity of ∥x−ty∥22​ for all real t gives the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality ∣x⋅y∣≤∥x∥2​∥y∥2​ (with y=0 immediate). Hence
∥x+y∥22​≤∥x∥22​+2∥x∥2​∥y∥2​+∥y∥22​,
(27)
which proves the triangle inequality after taking square roots.
For the prescribed set, use the Minkowski functional
pU​(x)=inf{t>0:x∈tU}.​
(28)
Since U is open and contains zero, some Euclidean ball of radius r>0 lies inside it. Since it is bounded, it lies inside a ball of radius R. These inclusions imply ∥x∥2​/R≤pU​(x)≤∥x∥2​/r, so the functional is finite and positive for nonzero x; it is zero at zero. Symmetry gives pU​(−x)=pU​(x), and substitution in the infimum gives pU​(ax)=apU​(x) for a>0, hence absolute homogeneity for all real scalars.
Convexity and 0∈U imply sU⊆U for 0≤s≤1. Consequently, for a>pU​(x) and b>pU​(y), one has x/a,y/b∈U. Convexity then gives
a+bx+y​=a+ba​ax​+a+bb​by​∈U,
(29)
so pU​(x+y)≤a+b. Letting a↓pU​(x) and b↓pU​(y) proves the triangle inequality.
Finally, if pU​(x)<1, there is a t<1 with x∈tU⊆U. Conversely, if x∈U and x=0, openness permits (1+δ)x∈U for some δ>0, so pU​(x)≤1/(1+δ)<1; zero is immediate. Thus U={x:pU​(x)<1}​, completing the norm from a bounded symmetric convex neighbourhood construction.
