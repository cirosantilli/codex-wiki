The angular momentum commutation relations are [Li​,Lj​]=iℏϵijk​Lk​. The commutator product rule gives
[Li​,L2]=iℏ∑j,k​ϵijk​(Lk​Lj​+Lj​Lk​)=0,
(37)
since the bracketed expression is symmetric in j,k and ϵijk​ is antisymmetric. Also
L−​L+​=L12​+L22​+i[L1​,L2​]=L12​+L22​−ℏL3​,
(38)
proving L2=L−​L+​+L32​+ℏL3​​.
For orbital angular momentum, Li​=−iℏϵijk​xj​∂k​. A differentiable radial function has ∂k​f(r)=f′(r)xk​/r, so its contraction with the antisymmetric symbol vanishes: Lf(r)=0. Put s=x1​+ix2​. The coordinate differential operators give L3​s=ℏs, while
L+​=ℏ[x3​(∂1​+i∂2​)−s∂3​]
(39)
annihilates every power of s, because (∂1​+i∂2​)s=0. Since all Li​ annihilate the radial factor, the product rule yields
L3​[snf(r)]=nℏsnf(r),L+​[snf(r)]=0.​
(40)
Substitution into the expression for L2 gives the highest-weight complex-coordinate orbital wavefunction result
L2[snf(r)]=ℏ2n(n+1)snf(r).​
(41)
As [L2,L−​]=0, lowering preserves this eigenvalue whenever the lowered function is nonzero. Explicitly,
L−​[snf(r)]=−2nℏx3​sn−1f(r),unchanged eigenvalue ℏ2n(n+1).​
(42)
For n=0 the lowered function is zero, so it is not an eigenfunction. For n≥0 the original nonzero function has the usual regular angular dependence. The PDF's “any integer” differential identities also hold locally for negative n where s=0, but those negative powers are singular on the axis and are not square-integrable regular angular eigenstates. The converted TeX incorrectly writes L4​ in place of the PDF's L+​.
