The Euler formula for a sphere, applied to the surface of a convex polyhedron, is V−E+F=2. In a regular polyhedron let q≥3 be the common number of edges incident at a vertex. Counting vertex-edge and face-edge incidences, pentagonal faces give qV=2E and 5F=2E. Hence
2=E(q2​−1+52​),
(6)
whose right-hand side can be positive only if q<10/3. Thus q=3. Substitution then yields 2=E/15, proving
E=30,V=20,F=12.​
(7)
These are the counts of the dodecahedron. Here regular polyhedron is understood in the convex, ordinary-face sense used by the stated Euler formula; self-intersecting star constructions do not share these hypotheses.
