ζ=zˉ(q+2)q+1​=q(q+2)z(q+1)​.​
(22)
The other denominators are nonzero automatically because q+1,q+2>0. Writing z=x+iy, the real-part condition becomes
q(q+2)x(q+1)​=qx+1/4​.
(23)
Multiplying by q(q+2) and rearranging gives q+4x+2=0. Thus the first geometric locus in the complex plane is the circle
(x+2)2+y2=2,or equivalently ∣z+2∣=2​.​
(24)
Its center is −2 and radius is 2​; it does not contain the excluded point z=0 on its circumference.
The principal complex logarithm has imaginary part equal to the complex argument, so its equation gives the open ray
z=reiπ/3,r>0,equivalently y=3​x, x>0.
(25)
If one interprets the logarithm as multivalued, requiring a value with imaginary part π/3 gives the same ray. Expanding z2 in the other equation gives 2ixy=2ix, hence x(y−1)=0. Its solution is the union of the entire imaginary axis and the horizontal line y=1.
These latter loci bound a right triangle with vertices
O=0,P=i,Q=3​1​+i.
(26)
The bounded open region is 0<x<1/3​, 3​x<y<1. Its closure is the closed triangle. Although the logarithm is undefined at O, the imaginary-axis locus includes that vertex. The earlier circle is a separate locus and is not a side of this triangle.
The right angle is at P, so the circumcenter is the midpoint of hypotenuse OQ:
zc​=23​1​+2i​,R=2∣Q∣​=3​1​.
(27)
Thus its circumcircle has the complex equation
​z−(23​1​+2i​)​2=31​.​
(28)
To find the incircle, let its radius be r. Tangency to x=0 and y=1 puts its center at zi​=r+i(1−r). The distance from that point to y−3​x=0 is (1−r−3​r)/2. Requiring this distance to be r gives
r=3+3​1​=63−3​​.
(29)
Therefore
∣z−[r+i(1−r)]∣2=r2,r=63−3​​.​
(30)
This tangent circle is indeed the largest contained circle. For any contained disk of radius ρ, its center has distances dj​≥ρ from the three side lines. Splitting the triangle into triangles with that center as vertex gives area Δ=21​∑j​ℓj​dj​≥sρ, where s is its semiperimeter. Here Δ=1/(23​) and s=(1+3​)/2, so ρ≤Δ/s=r. Our circle attains this upper bound, proving maximality rather than just tangency.
