The sketch shows these three cases and omits inaccessible negative values of r˙2 from the physical interpretation.
For completeness, if ℓc<0, W rises monotonically from −∞ to its asymptote; admissible motion then requires v⊥​>∣c∣ and is radially unbound after any inward branch turns. If ℓ=0, W=v⊥2​−c2 is constant; equality gives a circular orbit rather than escape. Above threshold the outgoing branch escapes, whereas an incoming branch reaches the singular axis in finite time and cannot be continued through it without specifying a nonsingular core field. These cases explain why a borderline escape assertion needs an initial-direction qualification.
The transverse escape velocity threshold for radial escape in an inverse-radius axial magnetic field is
v⊥,esc​=m∣qB0​∣​.​
(48)
For a radial launch at r0​, ℓ=cr0​, and equality permits marginal escape; for ℓ=0, larger transverse speed guarantees radial escape in forward time after any initial inward leg turns. The singular incoming ℓ=0 branch is the exception described above. More general launches at equality can instead give the circular exception ℓ=0. A vertical velocity adds to the total speed but cannot pay the transverse escape-energy requirement; if escape merely means unbounded z, any nonzero z˙ already achieves that. If qB0​=0, transverse motion is force-free and there is no positive escape-speed threshold.
