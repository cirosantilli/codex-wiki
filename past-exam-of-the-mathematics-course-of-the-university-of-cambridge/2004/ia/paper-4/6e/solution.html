For a prime number p and an integer x coprime to p, multiplication by x permutes the nonzero residue classes modulo p. Indeed, ax≡bx(modp) implies a≡b(modp), and none of the products is zero. Taking the product over the p−1 classes gives
xp−1(p−1)!≡(p−1)!(modp).
(16)
The factorial is coprime to p and can be cancelled, proving Fermat little theorem: xp−1≡1(modp)​.
Now n=mp and x is coprime to n, so it is coprime to p. Since (n−1)−(m−1)=m(p−1), the proved theorem gives
xn−1=xm−1(xp−1)m≡xm−1(modp).
(17)
Thus the two specified integer congruences modulo p are equivalent. In this particular step the additional hypothesis (m,p)=1 is not needed; it will hold for the squarefree factorization used next.
If n is a squarefree integer, its distinct prime factors are pairwise coprime. Consequently n divides xn−1−1 exactly when every prime factor does. Applying the preceding equivalence with m=n/p proves
xn−1≡1(modn)⟺x(n/p)−1≡1(modp) for every p∣n.​
(18)
Finally retain this squarefree hypothesis. If p−1 divides n−1, write n−1=kp​(p−1). Fermat little theorem gives xn−1=(xp−1)kp​≡1(modp) for every prime factor. Their pairwise coprimality then gives xn−1≡1(modn)​. For composite n this is the sufficient direction of the Korselt criterion for a Carmichael number; primality is not excluded by the requested implication.
