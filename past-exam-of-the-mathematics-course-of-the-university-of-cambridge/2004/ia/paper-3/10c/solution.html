An exact differential is a one-form equal to dΦ for a single-valued scalar function Φ on the domain. In Euclidean coordinates this means its vector coefficients form ∇Φ. Integrating the first component of F with respect to x, then matching the other components, gives
Φ(x,y,z)=z3(ex−ey)+x3(ey−ez)+C.​
(21)
Differentiating checks all three components. Since the domain R3 is connected, two such potentials differ only by a constant, so this is the most general potential.
At both specified endpoints the nonconstant terms vanish. The fundamental theorem for line integrals gives zero for the line integral along every path joining the two points.
For the planar field, choose a continuous lift θ(t) of the polar angle along a curve avoiding the origin. Differentiating the angle gives
G⋅dx=x2+y2−ydx+xdy​=dθ.
(22)
On the closed curve the angle increases by 2π times its winding number. Thus
∮C​G⋅dx=2π.​
(23)
The angle differential is locally exact but not globally exact on the punctured plane: the angle lift need not return to its initial value. This is the nonexact angular one-form, whose nonzero closed-path integral obstructs a global potential.
