For a nonempty set S⊆R, a real number s is its supremum if every x∈S satisfies x≤s and every upper bound u of S satisfies s≤u. Equivalently, s is an upper bound and, for every ε>0, some x∈S has s−ε<x≤s. The least upper bound axiom states that every nonempty set of real numbers bounded above has a real supremum.
Suppose (an​) is nondecreasing and bounded above. Apply the least upper bound axiom to S={an​:n≥1}, obtaining s=supS. For any ε>0, s−ε cannot be an upper bound, so there is N with aN​>s−ε. For every n≥N, monotonicity gives
s−ε<aN​≤an​≤s,
(8)
so ∣an​−s∣<ε. This is precisely the definition of a convergent sequence. If (an​) is nonincreasing and bounded below, apply the proved case to (−an​); its limit is the negative of the infimum of the original terms. Thus the bounded monotone sequence theorem follows directly from the axiom:
an​→nsup​an​ in the nondecreasing case,an​→ninf​an​ in the nonincreasing case.​
