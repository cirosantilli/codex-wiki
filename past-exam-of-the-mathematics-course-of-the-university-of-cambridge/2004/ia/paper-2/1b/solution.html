For a straight-line solution y=mx, substitution into the differential equation gives m=(1−2m)/(2+m). Thus m2+4m−1=0, and the two possible slopes are
m±​=−2±5​.​
(1)
These straight lines solve the differential equation on either side of the origin. At the origin itself the right-hand side is undefined.
An isocline of slope k has equation (1−2k)x−(2+k)y=0. For k=−2 it is the straight line y=(1−2k)x/(2+k); for k=−2 it is x=0, excluding the origin. In particular, y=x/2 is the zero-slope isocline. The line y=−2x is excluded from the domain of the differential equation; slopes tend to infinity on approaching it. Draw the flow vectors proportional to (1,(x−2y)/(2x+y)), with positive horizontal component. This specifies orientation by increasing x, rather than by an auxiliary time variable.
To integrate, put y=xu where x=0. Then
xu′=2+u1−4u−u2​,∫1−4u−u22+u​du=∫xdx​.
(2)
The left-hand integral is −21​log∣1−4u−u2∣, so the nonconstant-u solutions have the first integral
y2+4xy−x2=C.​
(3)
The constant-u solutions omitted by separation are exactly the two straight lines already found, corresponding to C=0. Alternatively, differentiating this first integral gives (2y+4x)y′+4y−2x=0, which recovers the original differential equation wherever 2x+y=0 and also verifies continuation through x=0 when y=0.
The first integral describes hyperbolas with the two straight-line solutions as asymptotes. For C=1, the upper branch crosses the vertical axis at (0,1) and exists for every real x. For C=−1, each branch is confined to ∣x∣≥1/5​ and reaches a vertical tangent on the excluded line y=−2x; the corresponding classical solution intervals exclude those endpoints. These give the two distinct types of curves requested in the sketch.
The initial condition selects C=1 and the upper branch, hence
y(x)=−2x+5x2+1​,−∞<x<∞.​
(4)
Here 2x+y=5x2+1​>0, so this really is a global solution of the initial value problem, and y′(0)=−2 agrees with the right-hand side.
