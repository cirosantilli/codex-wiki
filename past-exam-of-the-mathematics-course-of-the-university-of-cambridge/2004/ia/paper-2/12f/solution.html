Now use N≥1 for the fixed number of independently located ships. Normalize the radius to one and write their positions as unit vectors a1​,…,aN​. A point x on the sphere is reached by ship i precisely when ai​⋅x>0, so each ship reaches an open hemisphere. Coverage fails exactly when there is a unit vector x with ai​⋅x≤0 for every i. Equivalently, all ship positions lie in some closed hemisphere.
With probability one no pair of ship vectors is antipodal and no three are linearly dependent: the exceptional choices lie on sets of zero surface area. For such a configuration, containment in a closed hemisphere implies containment in an open hemisphere. To see this, choose its inward normal u, so u⋅ai​≥0. At most two vectors lie on its boundary. If there are two, their sum has positive dot product with each, since they are not antipodal; if there is one, use that vector itself. Perturb u slightly in this direction. The boundary dot products become positive while all already positive dot products remain positive. Thus the strict and non-strict containment conditions have the same probability here.
Condition on the unoriented axes {ai​,−ai​}. Choose representatives bi​ by any fixed sign convention. By uniform sampling and independence, the actual positions are ai​=εi​bi​ with N independent fair signs εi​∈{−1,1}. Conditional on these axes, each of the 2N sign choices has probability 2−N.
The great circles bi​⋅u=0 partition the sphere into RN​=N(N−1)+2 open regions. In each region the sign vector σ(u)=(sgn(bi​⋅u))i​ is constant. No sign vector labels two different regions: its strict inequalities define a convex cone, and normalized line segments within that cone connect any two points of its spherical section. The regions of a general-position great-circle arrangement therefore yield exactly RN​ realized sign vectors.
The chosen positions lie in an open hemisphere if and only if some u has u⋅ai​>0 for every i, that is, εi​=sgn(bi​⋅u) for every i. Failure of coverage thus corresponds exactly to one of the RN​ realized sign choices. These choices are mutually exclusive events, each of conditional probability 2−N, so the first part applies:
P(failure∣axes)=2NRN​​.
(54)
The result is independent of the axes. Averaging gives the coverage of a sphere by random open hemispheres:
P(every point is reached)=1−2NN(N−1)+2​,N≥1.​
(55)
For N=1,2,3 this is zero; for N=4 it is 1/8. If no ship lands, coverage has probability zero separately. The counting uses equiprobable orientation signs, not an assumption that the spherical regions have equal area.
