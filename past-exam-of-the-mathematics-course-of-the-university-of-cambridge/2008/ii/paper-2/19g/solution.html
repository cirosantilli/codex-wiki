There are seven irreducible characters, one for each conjugacy class. Besides the trivial character and the four supplied rows, let the remaining degrees be d,e. The sum of squared degrees gives d2+e2=360−(1+25+64+64+100)=106, whose positive integer solutions are {d,e}={5,9}. Complex conjugation permutes irreducible characters while preserving degree; the known rows are real and each unknown degree occurs just once among the remaining characters, so the two missing characters are real.

These equations yield two possible rational pairs in some columns. For this integral character reconstruction from column orthogonality, a further constraint is essential: character values are algebraic integers, so rational candidates must be integers. Every nontrivial row has χ(g)=χ(1) for g=1. In a unitary realization, χ(g)=χ(1) exactly when all eigenvalues of the representing matrix are one, so each nontrivial irreducible representation is faithful. If a nontrivial proper normal subgroup existed, a nontrivial irreducible representation of the nontrivial quotient would pull back to a nontrivial representation with that subgroup in its kernel. This contradicts faithfulness. The group is simple.
