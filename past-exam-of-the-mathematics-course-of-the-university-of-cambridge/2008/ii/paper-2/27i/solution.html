Interpret ϕ(x) as the conditional rejection probability. The Type I error is α=E0​ϕ(X), the Type II error is β=E1​[1−ϕ(X)], the size is α, and the power is 1−β. Their weighted sum is
cα+β=1+∫ϕ(x)[cp0​(x)−p1​(x)]dx.
(63)
Pointwise minimization over 0≤ϕ≤1 forces rejection where p1​>cp0​ and acceptance where p1​<cp0​, with arbitrary randomization on equality. Conversely those choices minimize every integrand. The equivalence holds almost everywhere, since changing a test on a common null set cannot change its errors. With both weights strictly positive, any simultaneous improvement of the two errors, strict in at least one, would lower this weighted sum. Every such positive-threshold likelihood ratio test is admissible for the two simple hypotheses.
For the specified densities the likelihood ratio is
Rθ​(x)=⎩⎨⎧​0,x(1−θ)2x−θ​,​0<x<θ,θ≤x≤1.​
(64)
It increases strictly on (θ,1) from zero to 1/(1−θ). For 0<c<1/(1−θ), the likelihood-ratio test rejects when x>θ/[1−c(1−θ)2]. Larger thresholds accept almost surely. Threshold zero rejects everywhere above θ and permits arbitrary rejection below θ, where both weighted choices tie; values at individual endpoints do not affect errors. These describe all nonnegative-threshold likelihood ratio tests, including their limiting accept-all and reject-all cases.
The upper-tail test ϕk​ has size 1−k2. If θ<k, it is a positive-threshold likelihood ratio test and is admissible. If θ=k, it rejects exactly the alternative's support, with power one; no test can retain power one with smaller size, so it remains admissible. If θ>k, it still has power one but wastes rejection probability on (k,θ), where the alternative density vanishes. The test ϕθ​ has the same power and smaller size, so
ϕk​ is admissible against fixed θ⟺θ≤k.​
(65)
Nevertheless ϕk​ is a uniformly most powerful test at size 1−k2 against all 0<θ<1: for θ<k, apply the Neyman-Pearson lemma; for θ≥k, its power is already the maximum one. It is also admissible against the composite alternative. Indeed choose any 0<θ<k. Strict monotonicity of its likelihood ratio makes ϕk​ the unique positive-threshold minimizer up to null sets. A test dominating it for the composite problem would either improve the weighted simple risk at this θ, impossible, or tie that risk and coincide almost everywhere. Since p0​>0 throughout (0,1), such coincidence also precludes improvement at any other θ.
