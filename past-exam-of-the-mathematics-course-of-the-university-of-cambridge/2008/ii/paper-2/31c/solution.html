[L,A]=6uux​−uxxx​,Lt​=[L,A].​

Thus the KdV equation is the compatibility condition of the Lax pair Lψ=λψ, ψt​=−Aψ. The operator A is skew-adjoint on decaying functions. Its evolution transports normalized eigenfunctions unitarily, so the spectrum of L is fixed; alternatively differentiating Lψ=λψ and using the two equations gives λt​ψ=0.
At each time solve the KdV Schrodinger spectral problem. For λ=k2>0, use the left Jost solution f−​, normalized as e−ikx at x→−∞, and write f−​∼a(k,t)e−ikx+b(k,t)eikx at +∞. The right reflection coefficient is R=b/a, and transmission is 1/a. Because −A tends to −4D3 at infinity, maintaining this Jost normalization gives f−,t​=−Af−​+4ik3f−​. Substitution of its two asymptotic waves yields at​=0 and bt​=8ik3b. The negative discrete eigenvalues are −κj2​, fixed in time. and solve, for y≥x,
K(x,y,t)+F(x+y,t)+∫x∞​K(x,z,t)F(z+y,t)dz=0.

The recovered potential is u(x,t)=−2dK(x,x,t)/dx​. Each fixed discrete eigenvalue produces a soliton component, whereas the continuous scattering data encode the dispersive part. The inverse transform, rather than a linear superposition of the potentials, accounts for their nonlinear interaction.
