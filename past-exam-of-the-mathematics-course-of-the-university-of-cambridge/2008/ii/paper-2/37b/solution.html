For isentropic flow of a perfect gas, p=Kργ with γ>1 and c2=dp/dρ. The mass and momentum equations imply
ct​+ucx​+2γ−1​cux​=0,ut​+uux​+γ−12c​cx​=0.
(99)
Adding and subtracting the appropriate multiples therefore proves
[∂t​+(u±c)∂x​][u±γ−12(c−c0​)​]=0.​
(100)
These are the Riemann invariants for one-dimensional isentropic flow.
In the piston-driven rarefaction wave, the incoming minus invariant has its initial value zero. Thus c=c0​+(γ−1)u/2. At the piston up​=−ft, giving cp​=c0​−(γ−1)ft/2. A plus characteristic emitted at time τ has constant u=−fτ and c=c0​−(γ−1)fτ/2, so its straight trajectory is
x(t;τ)=−21​fτ2+[c0​−2γ+1​fτ](t−τ).
(101)
The first characteristic is x=c0​t, ahead of which the gas is undisturbed. Later characteristics have decreasing propagation speed and spread into a rarefaction. More explicitly ∂τ​x=−c0​−(γ+1)ft/2+γfτ<0 for τ≤t<tv​, with equality only at the vacuum endpoint. They never intersect, so no compressive shock forms.
Since p/p0​=(c/c0​)2γ/(γ−1), the vacuum formation at an accelerating withdrawing piston gives
pp​(t)=p0​(1−t/tv​)2γ/(γ−1)(0≤t<tv​),tv​=f(γ−1)2c0​​.​
(102)
At tv​, density, sound speed and pressure at the gas edge vanish, and the piston velocity is X˙(tv​)=−2c0​/(γ−1)​. Its speed is the positive magnitude 2c0​/(γ−1). Afterwards the gas edge continues at this limiting velocity, while the piston keeps accelerating away; a vacuum gap of width f(t−tv​)2/2 opens and the gas pressure on the piston stays zero.
