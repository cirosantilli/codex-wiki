Continue in the bistable regime k>2. At s=0 the reaction function factors as f(g,0)=g(kg−1−g2)/(1+g2). Besides g=0, its positive equilibria are
g1​=2k−k2−4​​<1,g2​=2k+k2−4​​>1.​
(11)
At zero, fg​=−1, so it is stable. At a positive equilibrium, use k=(1+g2)/g to obtain fg​=(1−g2)/(1+g2). Therefore g1​ is unstable and g2​ stable. The sign of f is negative on (0,g1​), positive on (g1​,g2​) and negative beyond g2​; these signs determine the requested reaction-rate sketch.
The stationary points of f(g,0) obey (1+g2)2=2kg. For k>2 its smaller solution gc​<1 is the local minimum, and its larger solution lies above one and is the local maximum. Since increasing s translates the whole graph upward, the low and middle equilibria collide when
sc​=−f(gc​,0)=2gc​(1−gc2​)​,(1+gc2​)2=2kgc​.​
(12)
For s>sc​, both stationary values of f are positive, while f(g,s)→−∞ as g→∞; there is exactly one nonnegative equilibrium, on the descending high branch. At equality a double low equilibrium remains, so the uniqueness assertion requires strict exceedance.
For completeness, if the later positive-input request is considered for arbitrary k>0, the derivative is fg​=2kg/(1+g2)2−1. Its maximum is 9k/(83​)−1, attained at g=1/3​. For k≤83​/9, the reaction graph is decreasing and every s>0 already has a unique steady state, so take sc​=0. For k>83​/9, the smaller positive stationary point gives the same sc​=gc​(1−gc2​)/2; above it the graph crosses zero only on its final descending branch. Persistent robust switching after input is removed uses the previously assumed bistable regime k>2.
Starting near zero, raise s past sc​ and keep it there long enough for g to pass the unstable threshold and approach the high branch. Decrease s back to zero slowly, or after ensuring g>g1​. The trajectory then stays in the basin of the high stable equilibrium and ends at the displayed g2​. The persistent switched state is g2​=(k+k2−4​)/2. This is the saturating autocatalytic switch; the conclusion presupposes k>2 and enough forcing duration, not merely an arbitrarily short pulse of large amplitude.
