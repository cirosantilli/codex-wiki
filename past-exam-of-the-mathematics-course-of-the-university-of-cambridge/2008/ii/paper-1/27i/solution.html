For the fixed observation interval, N3​ has Poisson distribution with mean and variance 3λ. Hence N3​/3 is unbiased and has variance λ/3. Its score function is N3​/λ−3, and its Fisher information is 3/λ. The Cramér-Rao bound for unbiased estimation of λ therefore gives variance at least λ/3, attained by N3​/3.
For the stopping-time observation, T10​ has Gamma distribution with shape 10 and rate λ. Direct integration of its density gives
Eλ​(T10−1​)=9λ​,Eλ​(T10−2​)=9⋅8λ2​.

Thus k=9, and
Λ2​=9/T10​,Var(Λ2​)=λ2/8.​

The score function is 10/λ−T10​, so the Fisher information is 10/λ2. Its Cramér-Rao lower bound is λ2/10, strictly below this estimator's variance.
Nevertheless, 9/T10​ is uniformly minimum-variance unbiased. The observed T10​ is a sufficient statistic, and its family is complete: if Eλ​h(T10​)=0 for all λ>0, then ∫0∞​h(t)t9e−λtdt=0 for all λ>0. Uniqueness of the Laplace transform, applied after an exponential tilt to make an Lebesgue integrable function, implies h=0 almost everywhere. The Lehmann–Scheffé theorem now proves the minimum-variance assertion. Failure to attain the Cramér–Rao bound does not disprove minimum variance within the class of unbiased estimators.
For the actual observations, the two likelihood functions are
L1​(λ)=10!(3λ)10​e−3λ,L2​(λ)=9!λ1039​e−3λ.

They differ by a factor independent of λ. The likelihood principle consequently requires the same inferential conclusions, since the observed data carry proportional likelihoods. But the two minimum-variance unbiased estimates are 10/3 and 3​ respectively. Minimum-variance unbiased estimation therefore violates the likelihood principle: its unbiasedness and variance refer to the sampling plan, including the stopping rule, rather than solely to the observed Likelihood function.
