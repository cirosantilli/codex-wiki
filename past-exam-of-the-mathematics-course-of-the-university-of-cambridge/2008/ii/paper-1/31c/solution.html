A Hamiltonian system with n degrees of freedom is Liouville integrable if it has n functionally independent first integrals, including the Hamiltonian, whose pairwise Poisson brackets vanish. Functional independence is required on a regular open set. The Arnold-Liouville theorem says that a connected compact regular common level of these integrals is an n-torus, with a neighborhood admitting action-angle coordinates; the Hamiltonian flow there has constant angular velocities and fixed actions. Compactness is essential to the torus conclusion.
