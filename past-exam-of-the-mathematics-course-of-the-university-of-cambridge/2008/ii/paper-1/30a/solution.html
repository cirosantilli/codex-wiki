In I(λ) put t=u/λ​. Expanding the cubic exponential near the endpoint t=0 gives
I(λ)∼λ−α−1/2∑n=0∞​n!λn/2(−1)n​∫0∞​u2α+3ne−u2du.
(84)
The substitution v=u2 evaluates the coefficient using the Gamma function. Thus, numbering the first term by n=0,
I(λ)∼n=0∑∞​2n!λα+(n+1)/2(−1)nΓ(α+(3n+1)/2)​.​
(85)
For any fixed truncation the exponential's Taylor remainder is bounded by the next power times e−u2, and replacing the finite upper limit by infinity adds an exponentially small tail. This justifies the asymptotic expansion for α>−1/2.
For large n, the ratio of magnitudes of consecutive terms, by Stirling's formula, is
(n+1)λ​Γ(α+(3n+1)/2)Γ(α+(3n+4)/2)​∼(23​)3/2λn​​.
(86)
The least term consequently has index n∼8λ/27​. An indexing convention starting at one changes this by one, without changing the estimate.
For the second integral, both endpoints minimize t2−t3. The endpoint t=0 gives the same expansion with positive coefficients. Its first two contributions are
J(λ)=2λα+1/2Γ(α+1/2)​+2λα+1Γ(α+2)​+O(λ−1),−1/2<α<0.​
(87)
To check their ordering, near t=1 write t=1−v/λ. Then λ(t2−t3)=v+O(v2/λ) and t2α=1+O(v/λ), producing a contribution 1/λ+O(λ−2). Because α+1<1, this is smaller than the second displayed term. It is the third term, ahead of the next t=0 contribution of order λ−α−3/2. Splitting the integral into small endpoint neighborhoods and an interior interval, where the phase is strictly positive, makes the endpoint estimates rigorous.
