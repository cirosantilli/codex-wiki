For the multiplier −1 problem, write x=X+α/2. To the required weighted order, with X=O(μ1/2), the shifted map is Xn+1​=−Xn​+bXn​+γXn2​+δXn3​, where b=β+αγ. Composing it with itself cancels the quadratic term. The cubic contribution is −2δX3−2γ2X3, while the linear correction is −2bX. Consequently the quadratic-cubic flip criticality calculation gives
μ​=−2(β+αγ),A=2(δ+γ2).​

Thus the second iterate has normal form Xn+2​=Xn​+μ​Xn​−AXn3​, up to terms of weighted order at least four. This is the asymptotic interpretation of the stated remainder under X=O(μ1/2); a uniformly valid local expansion about the exact fixed point also retains mixed parameter remainders.
Assuming μ​ crosses zero transversely and A=0, the small nonzero fixed points of the second iterate satisfy X2∼μ​/A. They form a period-two orbit. For A>0 they exist on the side μ​>0, where the original fixed point has become unstable, and the second-iterate multiplier is 1−2μ​+o(μ​), so the cycle is stable. The period-doubling bifurcation is therefore supercritical precisely when δ+γ2>0. Equality is a degenerate case requiring higher-order terms.
