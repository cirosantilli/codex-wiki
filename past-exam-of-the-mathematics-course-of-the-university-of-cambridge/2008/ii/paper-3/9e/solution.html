Let A=Dy(x) be the Jacobian matrix and K(y)=H(x(y)). The chain rule gives ∇x​H=AT∇y​K. Therefore the Hamilton equations transform to y˙​=AJAT∇y​K, with J=(0I​−I0​). They have the required canonical form for every Hamiltonian exactly when
AJAT=J.​
(16)
Necessity follows by choosing Hamiltonians whose gradients take arbitrary values at a point; sufficiency follows from the displayed transformed equation. This is the symplectic matrix condition for a canonical transformation, and it also forces A to be invertible.
For the given transformation, A=(1−1/4​21/2​) has determinant one. For any 2×2 matrix, AJAT=(detA)J, so the proposed transformation is canonical.
