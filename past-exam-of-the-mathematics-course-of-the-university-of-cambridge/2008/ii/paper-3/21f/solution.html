The real Stone-Weierstrass theorem states that a subalgebra A⊆C(K,R) on a compact Hausdorff space K, containing constants and separating points, is dense in the uniform norm. Let B be its uniform closure. Polynomial approximation to ∣t∣ on a bounded interval shows that f∈B implies ∣f∣∈B. Therefore B is closed under pointwise maximum and minimum, since max(f,g)=(f+g+∣f−g∣)/2 and min(f,g)=(f+g−∣f−g∣)/2.
Fix h∈C(K) and ϵ>0. Point separation and constants supply, for each pair x,y, a function fxy​∈A agreeing with h at both points. For fixed x, finitely many open sets where fxy​>h−ϵ cover K; their corresponding maximum gx​∈B satisfies gx​>h−ϵ everywhere and gx​(x)=h(x). The open sets where gx​<h+ϵ, as x varies, cover K. Choose finitely many and take their minimum g∈B. Then h−ϵ<g<h+ϵ throughout K. Approximating g by members of A proves the theorem.
Now let F be the uniform closure of integer-coefficient polynomials on [a,b]. It is a closed ring. Since q=max[a,b]​∣1−2x∣<1, the integer-coefficient polynomials ∑j=0N​x(1−2x)j converge uniformly to 1/2, with error at most bqN+1/(1−q). Thus 1/2∈F. Ring operations give every dyadic rational constant, and closure gives every real constant. Since x∈F, all real polynomials lie in F. The Stone-Weierstrass theorem now yields F=C([a,b])​, as in integer polynomial approximation away from zero and one.
On [0,b] the answer is no. The constant function 1/2 cannot be uniformly approximated by integer-coefficient polynomials: at zero every such polynomial has an integer value and therefore error at least 1/2.
