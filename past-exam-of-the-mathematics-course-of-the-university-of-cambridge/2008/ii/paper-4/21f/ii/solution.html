Take a homotopy equivalence f:X→Y with inverse g, and universal covering maps pX​,pY​. Since the covering spaces are simply connected, the path lifting theorem constructs lifts f​ of fpX​ and g​ of gpY​, after specifying one value in the appropriate fiber. Explicitly, define the lift at a point by lifting the image of a path from the chosen base point; independence from the path follows from simple connectedness and homotopy lifting.
Lift a homotopy gf≃1X​ starting at g​f​. The homotopy lifting theorem for a covering map makes its endpoint a map dX​:X→X with pX​dX​=pX​. Such a lift of the identity is a deck transformation and is a homeomorphism: lift the identity again with the base point image reversed, and uniqueness of path lifts makes the two compositions identities. Likewise f​g​≃dY​ for a deck transformation of Y.
Thus both compositions are homotopy equivalences, even though the lifted homotopies need not end at the particular identity lift. Part (i) then proves
X≃Y⟹X≃Y.​
(65)
Keeping the possible deck transformations is essential when the original homotopies do not fix base points.
