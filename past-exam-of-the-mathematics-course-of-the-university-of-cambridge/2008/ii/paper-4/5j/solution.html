These are nested Poisson regression models with independent counts and the default log link function. If xi​ is biomass and gi​ is the three-level pH group, model 1 specifies logμi​=α+βxi​: one common intercept and slope. Model 2 specifies logμi​=αgi​​+βxi​: three group-specific intercepts and a common slope. Model 3 specifies logμi​=αgi​​+βgi​​xi​: three intercepts and three slopes, because the multiplication syntax includes both main effects and their interaction. The parameter counts are therefore 2,4,6.
The analysis of deviance compares model 1 against model 2 by testing equality of the three intercepts, conditional on a common biomass slope. The likelihood-ratio test statistic is 407.67−99.24=308.43 with two additional parameters, giving the reported tiny p-value. The next test compares model 2 against model 3 by testing equality of their three slopes. Its statistic is 99.24−83.20=16.04, again with two degrees of freedom and reported p=3.288×10−4. Both restrictions are rejected. Thus model 3 is the supported choice: both baseline richness and its biomass dependence vary with pH. Under the Poisson assumptions, residual deviance 83.20 on 84 residual degrees of freedom is also consistent with a satisfactory fit, although these nested tests themselves establish improvement rather than every possible aspect of model adequacy.
The prediction from model 2 is on its linear-predictor scale. Exponentiating gives its fitted means. The final expression is twice the saturated log likelihood minus the model-2 log likelihood, i.e. the Poisson deviance of model 2. Consequently
