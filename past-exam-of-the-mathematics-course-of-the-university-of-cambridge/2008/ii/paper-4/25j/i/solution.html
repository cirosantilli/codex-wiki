Work in the real L1 space, consistent with the specified real step coefficients. First approximate an integrable function by a finite-valued measurable function with bounded support. Truncate its domain to [−M,M] and its values to [−M,M]; the L1 error tends to zero by integrability. Quantize those remaining values in intervals of mesh δ, producing a simple function u=∑j=1m​cj​1Bj​​ with finite-measure Borel sets Bj​ and error at most 2Mδ on the truncated domain. Thus ∥f−u∥1​ can be made arbitrarily small.
For each Bj​, use the allowed approximation by a finite union of intervals Aj​, choosing the symmetric-difference errors so
∑j​∣cj​∣μ(Aj​△Bj​)<ε.
(84)
These unions can be written as finite disjoint bounded intervals, and changing endpoints to the required half-open convention changes only a null set. The function s=∑j​cj​1Aj​​ is therefore a step function of the specified form, and
∥u−s∥1​≤∑j​∣cj​∣μ(Aj​△Bj​)<ε.
(85)
Combining the two approximations proves the step functions are dense in L1(R). In the complex version of L1, the same argument applies to real and imaginary parts when complex step coefficients are allowed.
