On the unit three-ball B3, the distance in the Poincare ball model is the infimum of the lengths
L(γ)=∫1−∣γ(t)∣22∣γ′(t)∣​dt
(9)
over piecewise smooth paths joining the two points. Equivalently the hyperbolic metric as a metric-space distance is
d(x,y)=arcosh(1+(1−∣x∣2)(1−∣y∣2)2∣x−y∣2​).​
(10)
It has curvature-minus-one normalization; in particular d(0,x)=2artanh∣x∣.
For a nonempty finite set p1​,…,pm​, define R(x)=maxj​d(x,pj​). The triangle inequality gives ∣R(x)−R(y)∣≤d(x,y), so R is continuous. Take R0​=R(p1​). Any improving center satisfies d(x,p1​)≤R(x)≤R0​, and thus lies in the closed hyperbolic ball K=B(p1​,R0​).
This ball is compact: an isometry sending p1​ to the origin identifies it with the Euclidean closed ball of radius tanh(R0​/2)<1, and the Euclidean and hyperbolic topologies agree inside B3. The continuous function R attains a minimum on K. Centers outside K have R>R0​, so this is also the global minimum. Its associated closed ball proves a minimum enclosing ball in hyperbolic space exists. For the empty finite set, any center and radius zero suffice.
