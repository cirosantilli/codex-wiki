Balancing the viscous and bending terms gives ζωh∼Ah/ℓ4, so the elastohydrodynamic penetration length is
ℓ=(A/(ζω))1/4.​
(15)
Inserting h=h0​Re[eiωtF(x/ℓ)] into the linear equation yields F′′′′=−iF, with F(0)=1, F′′(0)=0 and decay at infinity. Write C=cos(π/8), S=sin(π/8). Of the four roots of λ4=−i, precisely two have negative real part:
λL​=−C+iS,λR​=−S−iC.
(16)
The two decaying solutions are eλL​η and eλR​η, and the general decaying response is their linear combination. Both coefficients are nonzero: if one vanished, F′′(0)=0 would force the other to vanish, contradicting F(0)=1.
Their real parts in the time-dependent response are proportional to
e−Cηcos(ωt+Sη+ϕL​),e−Sηcos(ωt−Cη+ϕR​).
(17)
Constant phases move respectively with velocities −ωℓ/S and ωℓ/C, so these are damped travelling waves moving left and right. Since S<C, the right-going wave has the longer attenuation length and dominates at large x. Thus the less strongly damped, outward-moving wave dominates the far-field response. This is the oscillatory bending of a moment-free semi-infinite filament; the two boundary conditions determine its amplitudes without needing them explicitly here.
