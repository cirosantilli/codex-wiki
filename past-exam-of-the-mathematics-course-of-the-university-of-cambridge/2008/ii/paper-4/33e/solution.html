A periodic lattice Hamiltonian commutes with lattice translations. The Bloch theorem therefore classifies its states by characters of the translation group:
ψnk​(x)=eik⋅xunk​(x),
(127)
where u is lattice periodic. For each crystal momentum k, solving on a primitive cell with the corresponding quasiperiodic boundary condition gives discrete eigenvalues indexed by n. Their continuous dependence on k produces the energy bands En​(k) as k ranges through the Brillouin zone. Bragg mixing opens gaps at suitable degeneracies, although bands can overlap in three dimensions and gaps are not guaranteed between every pair.
The quantum numbers are band index, wavevector modulo reciprocal lattice vectors, and spin where spin is conserved. In a finite crystal the allowed wavevectors are discrete; in the thermodynamic limit they become dense and the band description emerges. Spin-orbit coupling can replace a separately conserved spin label by an appropriate internal band label.
Dilute randomly placed impurities break exact translation symmetry, so crystal momentum is no longer an exact quantum number. Most extended states retain approximately the host band structure but acquire impurity scattering and disorder broadening. An impurity potential can also produce impurity levels in a crystal, often in a band gap: levels associated with a donor impurity lie near the conduction-band edge and can supply electrons, while those associated with an acceptor impurity lie near the valence-band edge and can create holes. The impurity's species and potential determine whether such levels exist and how deep they are; not every impurity necessarily makes a gap state. At low density these levels are nearly isolated, while overlap at increasing density can broaden them into an impurity band. Added or removed carriers shift the chemical potential as well.
