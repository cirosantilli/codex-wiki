 ... Past exam of the mathematics course of the University of Cambridge 2008 ii Paper 4 19G a
OurBigBook.com Words: 192
Work over the complex numbers. By Maschke's theorem, restriction to the normal subgroup A is a direct sum of its isotypic components. For an irreducible A-type W, denote its isotypic component in V by VW​. Normality makes G permute these components, by conjugating the A-types. Any union of orbits spans a G-invariant subspace, so irreducibility of V means that all occurring components form a single orbit.
If there is only one component, restriction is isotypic, as claimed. Otherwise take one component VW​ and its stabilizer H={g:gVW​=VW​}, a proper subgroup. This is an irreducible representation of H: if 0=U⊊VW​ were H-invariant, the direct sum of its translates over coset representatives for G/H would be a nonzero proper G-invariant subspace of V. It is direct because the translates lie in distinct isotypic components, and proper because dimU<dimVW​.
The natural map from the induced representation sends g⊗v to gv. It identifies the direct-sum coset decomposition with all isotypic components of V, so is bijective and G-equivariant. Consequently
V≅IndHG​VW​.​
(57)
This proves the dichotomy directly, including the irreducibility of the representation being induced.
