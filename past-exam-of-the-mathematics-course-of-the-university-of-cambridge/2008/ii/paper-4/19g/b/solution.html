Past exam of the mathematics course of the University of Cambridge / 2008 / ii / Paper 4 / 19G / b / Solution

 ... Past exam of the mathematics course of the University of Cambridge 2008 ii Paper 4 19G b
OurBigBook.com Words: 200
Induct on the order of the p-group. Every complex irreducible representation of an abelian group is one-dimensional: commuting group operators preserve a common eigenspace, and irreducibility makes that eigenspace the whole space. This handles the abelian cases.
If V is not faithful, it is an irreducible representation of a smaller quotient G/K, where K is its kernel. Induction expresses it as induced from a one-dimensional representation of a subgroup of G/K. Pulling back the subgroup and inflating the character gives the same induction statement for G, because the coset spaces of the quotient subgroup and its inverse image agree.
Now suppose V is faithful and G is nonabelian. Use the allowed abelian normal subgroup A not contained in the centre. If restriction to A were isotypic, its irreducible type would be one-dimensional, so every a∈A would act as a scalar. It would therefore commute with every representing matrix of G. Faithfulness would imply ga=ag in G for every g, contradicting the choice of A. By part (a), V=IndHG​W for an irreducible representation of a proper subgroup H. Induction on H gives W=IndKH​χ with χ one-dimensional. Transitivity of induction yields
V=IndKG​χ.​
(58)
This proves the monomiality of irreducible representations of finite p-groups.
