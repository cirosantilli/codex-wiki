Let ζ denote free-surface height and h=ζ−b the water depth. The inviscid rotating shallow water equations with constant Coriolis parameter f are
DH​u−fv=−gζx​,DH​v+fu=−gζy​,ht​+(hu)x​+(hv)y​=0,DH​=∂t​+u∂x​+v∂y​.
(24)
It is ζ, not h, that supplies the horizontal pressure gradient. For a fixed bottom, mass conservation equivalently gives DH​h=−h(ux​+vy​).
Write ξ=vx​−uy​ for relative vorticity. Differentiate the second momentum equation in x and the first in y, then subtract. The pressure terms cancel. Expanding the differentiated advection terms gives
DH​ξ+(ξ+f)(ux​+vy​)=0.
(25)
Consequently the absolute vorticity qa​=ξ+f satisfies DH​qa​=−qa​∇H​⋅uH​. Combining this with mass conservation yields exact shallow-water potential vorticity conservation:
DH​(qa​/h)=h2hDH​qa​−qa​DH​h​=0.​
(26)
A sloping bottom changes the depth and hence the stretching, but contributes no curl to the gravitational pressure force.
For the quasi-geostrophic approximation, choose constant reference depth H0​, speed U, and horizontal length L. Require Rossby number Ro=U/(∣f∣L)≪1, slow evolution on the advective time L/U, smooth horizontal scales, and L/LD​=O(1) or smaller, where LD​=gH0​​/∣f∣ is the Rossby deformation radius. Write ζ=ζ0​+η, b=b0​+δb, and h=H0​+η−δb. The leading momentum balance is geostrophic balance:
ug​=−ψy​,vg​=ψx​,ψ=gη/f.
(27)
It gives η/H0​=O(Ro(L/LD​)2), while we require δb/H0​=O(Ro) and slopes on the chosen horizontal scale. Relative vorticity divided by f is also O(Ro). Thus
hqa​​=H0​f​+H0​1​(∇H2​ψ−H0​f​η+H0​f​δb)+O(Ro2H0​∣f∣​).
(28)
The derivative of the constant first term vanishes. In advecting the first-order anomaly, the ageostrophic flow contributes only at the next order. Therefore the shallow-water quasi-geostrophic potential vorticity equation is
q=∇H2​ψ−LD−2​ψ+H0​f​δb,qt​+J(ψ,q)=0,J(ψ,q)=ψx​qy​−ψy​qx​.​
(29)
This retains the balanced vortical branch, including Rossby waves driven here by the topographic potential-vorticity gradient. It filters the freely oscillating inertia–gravity branches of the full shallow water equations, and does not retain independent boundary Kelvin wave dynamics. Small Rossby number alone is insufficient for the reduction: fast waves must be absent at leading order, as in well-prepared balanced initial data.
In the channel take H0​=h00​ and measure the bottom anomaly from mid-channel: δb=ε(y−L/2). Define β=εf/H0​. The resting potential vorticity is β(y−L/2), and the linear Rossby wave equation is
∂t​(∇H2​−LD−2​)ψ+βψx​=0.
(30)
For nonzero zonal wavenumber k, wall conditions ψ=0 select
ψ=Re{Asin(nπy/L)ei(kx−ωt)},ω=−k2+(nπ/L)2+LD−2​βk​,n=1,2,….​
(31)
The k=0 modes are stationary. Write K2=k2+(nπ/L)2+LD−2​ and take real A for the following sketch. The total potential vorticity is q=β(y−L/2)−K2ψ. For a contour whose undisturbed position is y0​, its small meridional displacement is
δy=βK2​ψ(x,y0​,t)+O(A2).
(32)
Thus potential-vorticity conservation says a northward displacement in a positive background gradient carries a negative anomaly, q′=−βδy. Potential-vorticity inversion turns this into positive ψ and anticyclonic motion around the bulge. At a positive cosine crest, v=ψx​ is northward on its western flank and southward on its eastern flank. Advection raises the contour on the west and lowers it on the east, so the crest moves west. Algebraically, δyt​=v and δy=(K2/β)ψ give ω/k=−β/K2, the same phase velocity as above. For β<0 all propagation directions reverse.
For a square basin use a nonzero-frequency normal mode ψ=Re{Ψ(x,y)e−iωt}, with Ψ=0 on all walls. Its equation is
(∇H2​−LD−2​)Ψ+ωiβ​Ψx​=0.
(33)
Set α=β/(2ω) and Ψ=eiαxΨ. Substitution cancels the first derivative and gives
∇H2​Ψ+(α2−LD−2​)Ψ=0,Ψ=0 on the walls.
(34)
The square's Dirichlet Laplacian eigenfunctions are Ψ=Asin(mπx/L)sin(nπy/L), with m,n positive integers. Consequently the square-basin topographic Rossby mode has
ω=±2π2(m2+n2)/L2+LD−2​​β​,Ψ=Ae−iβx/(2ω)sin(mπx/L)sin(nπy/L).​
(35)
For β>0, the positive-frequency branch therefore tends to βL/[2πm2+n2​] as L/LD​→0, as required. The negative-frequency solution is its complex-conjugate representation for a real disturbance; the physical frequency magnitude is ∣β∣/[2π2(m2+n2)/L2+LD−2​​].
