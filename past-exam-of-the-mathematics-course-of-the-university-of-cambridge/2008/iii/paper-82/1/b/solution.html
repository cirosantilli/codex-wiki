For fixed x>1, write a=x2−1​ and A=x+a. The maximum of the integrand occurs at θ=0, and
log(x+acosθ)=logA−2Aa​θ2+O(θ4).
(4)
Here is the required endpoint Laplace method derivation. Outside a fixed neighborhood of zero, the integrand is exponentially smaller than An. Inside that neighborhood, use θ=(A/(na))1/2u. The quadratic term gives e−u2/2 and the higher terms vanish in the scaled limit. The Gaussian integral on the half-line is π/2​, so
Pn​(x)∼2πnx2−1​​(x+x2−1​)n+1/2​,x>1 fixed.​
(5)
This is the Hyperbolic Debye asymptotic for Legendre polynomials. At the endpoint the integrand is identically one, giving Pn​(1)=1 exactly.
The localization width in the Laplace method is O((na)−1/2). It becomes order one when nx−1​=O(1), so the Legendre boundary layer at x equals one has q=2. With x=1+ν/n2 and fixed ν≥0,
nlog(x+x2−1​cosθ)=2ν​cosθ+O(n−1)
(6)
uniformly in θ. The boundary-layer answer is therefore
Pn​(1+ν/n2)∼π1​∫0π​e2ν​cosθdθ=I0​(2ν​).​
(7)
This real integral representation of the modified Bessel function I0 gives one at ν=0, agreeing with the endpoint value. Applying the same endpoint Laplace method to the integral for I0​(z) gives I0​(z)∼ez/2πz​. Thus, as ν→∞, the inner result is e2ν​/2π2ν​​. The outer result has precisely this limit in the overlap 1≪ν≪n. This proves both matches rather than extending the fixed-x formula directly to x=1.
For the derivatives, the power series I0​(2ν​)=1+ν/2+ν2/16+⋯ suggests their leading orders. To justify differentiation, expand the original integral itself near x=1. Its first three even cosine moments give
Pn​(x)=xn+4n(n−1)​xn−2(x2−1)+64n(n−1)(n−2)(n−3)​xn−4(x2−1)2+O((x−1)3).
(8)
Odd cosine moments vanish, and higher even moments start at cubic order. Expanding at x=1 yields
Pn′​(1)=2n(n+1)​∼2n2​,Pn′′​(1)=8(n−1)n(n+1)(n+2)​∼8n4​.​
(9)
