Let the basic temperature be T=T∗​−βz with β>0, the density be ρ0​, gravitational acceleration be −gz, and thermal expansion coefficient be aT​. Denote kinematic viscosity, thermal diffusivity and magnetic diffusivity by ν,κ,η. Write the dimensional magnetic perturbation as bd​, the temperature perturbation as θd​, and use constant permeability μ0​. Linearizing the Boussinesq equations and the resistive induction equation about the static state gives
∂t​ud​∂t​bd​∂t​θd​​=−ρ0−1​∇Pd​+gaT​θd​z+μ0​ρ0​B0​​(c⋅∇)bd​+ν∇2ud​,=B0​(c⋅∇)ud​+η∇2bd​,=βud,z​+κ∇2θd​,∇⋅ud​=∇⋅bd​=0.​
(1)
The Lorentz force identity is (∇×bd​)×B0​c=B0​(c⋅∇)bd​−∇(B0​c⋅bd​). Its gradient term is absorbed into Pd​, the perturbation magnetohydrodynamic total pressure. The temperature source follows from ud​⋅∇T=−βud,z​.
Choose any reference length d, since no vertical boundaries are prescribed. Scale length by d, time by d2/κ, velocity by κ/d, temperature perturbation by βd, magnetic perturbation by B0​, and total pressure by ρ0​νκ/d2. The dimensionless parameters are
σ=κν​,ζ=κη​,R=νκgaT​βd4​,Q=μ0​ρ0​νηB02​d2​.​
(2)
They are the Prandtl number, magnetic-to-thermal diffusivity ratio, Rayleigh number, and Chandrasekhar number, respectively. Division by the chosen scales gives
σ−1u˙b˙θ˙​=−∇p′+Rθz+Qζ(c⋅∇)b+∇2u,=(c⋅∇)u+ζ∇2b,=w+∇2θ,∇⋅u=∇⋅b=0.​
(3)
In particular Qζ=B02​d2/(μ0​ρ0​νκ), so the diffusivity convention agrees with the momentum coefficient.
For a plane wave put l=(k,0,m), q=∣l∣2=k2+m2, and d∥​=c⋅l=ksinα+mcosα. The induction and temperature equations imply b=id∥​u/(s+ζq) and θ=w/(s+q). The projection P=I−llT/q removes pressure. Because (Pz)z​=k2/q, its vertical momentum equation is
(σs​+q+s+ζqQζd∥2​​)w=Rqk2​θ.
(4)
Thus the oblique-field plane-wave magnetoconvection dispersion relation is
q(s+q)[(s/σ+q)(s+ζq)+Qζd∥2​]−Rk2(s+ζq)=0.​
(5)
If out-of-plane velocity is also allowed, its decoupled magnetic branch satisfies (s/σ+q)(s+ζq)+Qζd∥2​=0; it has no buoyant driving. At stationary marginality, for k=0,
Rmathrmstat​(k,m)=k2(k2+m2)3​+Qk2k2+m2​(ksinα+mcosα)2.​
(6)
These are stationary thresholds; an oscillatory marginal mode, if present for particular diffusivity ratios, is a different onset calculation.
For m=1, write Rmathrmstat​=F(k)+QG(k), where F=(1+k2)3/k2 and G=(1+k2)(cosα+ksinα)2/k2. Direct differentiation gives F′(k)=−2k−3+6k+4k3, so its two minima are ±1/2​, with F=27/4 and F′′=36. For Q>0 the negative sign has the smaller magnetic penalty: at every positive k, G(−k)<G(k) because both sine and cosine of the inclination are positive. The wavenumber selection in oblique-field magnetoconvection therefore starts from a=−1/2​. Expanding F′(kc​)+QG′(kc​)=0 gives kc​=a+C1​Q+O(Q2), where C1​=−G′(a)/F′′(a). Taylor expansion of the value then gives
Rc​=427​+3Q(cosα−sinα/2​)2+C2​Q2+O(Q3),C2​=−2F′′(a)G′(a)2​.​
(7)
This proves the small-field forms, including the order of the correction.
For large Q, G is nonnegative and vanishes only at b=−cotα. The candidate k=b has finite threshold F(b), so a global minimizer must satisfy QG(kc​)≤F(b) and hence tend to b. Here G′′(b)=2(1+b2)sin2α/b2>0. Expanding the stationarity condition at b yields
kc​=−cotα+QC3​​+O(Q−2),C3​=−G′′(b)F′(b)​.​
(8)
The value expansion is
Rc​=cot2αcsc6α​+QC4​​+O(Q−2),C4​=−2G′′(b)F′(b)2​.​
(9)
The leading coefficient follows from 1+cot2α=csc2α. The expansion holds at fixed 0<α<π/2.
Finally choose cotα=1/2​. Then the nonmagnetic minimizing wavevector also has d∥​=0, and its magnetic penalty vanishes. Since F(k)≥27/4 and QG(k)≥0, this is the global minimum for every nonnegative field strength:
α=arctan2​,kc​=−1/2​,Rc​=27/4for every Q≥0.​
(10)
This field-independent stationary threshold in oblique magnetoconvection has a simple physical explanation: the disturbance is constant along the imposed field, so it does not bend field lines.
