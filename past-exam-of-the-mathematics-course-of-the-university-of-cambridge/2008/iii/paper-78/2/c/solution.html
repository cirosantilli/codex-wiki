Let θ(z)=Ωlogz+Φ. A fixed point of the Shilnikov return map is a periodic orbit with one marked global excursion, and satisfies
μ=Azδcosθ(z)−z,fS′​(z)=Azδ−1(δcosθ(z)−Ωsinθ(z)).​
(22)
Its local passage takes logarithmically long. If the global travel time tends to Tg​, then its physical period is
T=Tg​+λ+−1​log(z0​/z)+o(1).
(23)
Eliminating z and absorbing section sizes and Tg​ into constants C1​,C2​>0 and a phase ϕ0​ gives the leading period–parameter relation
μ=C1​eλ−​Tcos(ωT+ϕ0​)−C2​e−λ+​T+o(eλ−​T+e−λ+​T).​
(24)
For the idealized leading map with a constant global travel time, the displayed two terms follow exactly; for the original flow, local and global approximation errors qualify their accuracy.
If δ>1, the oscillatory term is o(z) and fS′​(z)→0. In a sufficiently small interval the map is a contraction. For μ<0 there is a unique small attracting fixed point z=−μ+O(∣μ∣δ); it is an attracting periodic orbit also in the transverse direction of the full return. For μ≥0, fS​(z)−z=−μ−z+O(zδ)<0 for sufficiently small positive z, so no periodic return orbit can remain wholly in this neighborhood. As μ↑0 from below, the attracting cycle becomes the homoclinic connection and
μ∼−C2​e−λ+​T,T=λ+−1​log(C2​/∣μ∣)+o(1).​
(25)
Contraction also excludes higher-period cycles wholly inside that small return neighborhood. This does not exclude remote periodic orbits elsewhere in the vector field.
If 1/2<δ<1, the oscillatory term dominates z. At μ=0 the fixed-point equation is
cosθ(z)=A−1z1−δ⟶0.
(26)
There are infinitely many positive solutions approaching zero, with phases approaching successive zeros of cosine. Their leading ratio is zj+1​/zj​→e−π/Ω, so their periods satisfy Tj+1​−Tj​→π/ω. At these roots the sine tends to ±1, and the fixed-point multiplier has magnitude asymptotic to AΩzδ−1→∞. Thus these are longitudinally unstable cycles, with the thin transverse direction contracting: infinitely many saddle periodic orbits accumulate on the homoclinic connection. Expanding oscillatory lobes produce interval-covering walks and horseshoe dynamics in suitable iterates; this is the positive-saddle-value case of the Shilnikov bifurcation.
Away from the connection, the one-passage branches wiggle in the period–parameter plane. To leading order,
μ∼C1​eλ−​Tcos(ωT+ϕ0​),∣μ∣≲C1​eλ−​T.​
(27)
There is no single monotone logarithmic law: the phase matters, and the exponentially smaller −C2​e−λ+​T term matters near cosine zeros. Folds occur when fS′​(z)=1, asymptotically at tanθ=δ/Ω. Their parameter values alternate in sign and accumulate geometrically toward zero. On a fixed-point branch, stability is ∣fS′​(z)∣<1; small stable windows occur near stationary points of the oscillatory map. Crossing fS′​=−1 gives period-doubling bifurcations, while fS′​=1 gives saddle-node bifurcations of periodic orbits. Subsequent doublings and chaotic invariant sets can occur; neither all periodic orbits being stable nor a particular universal sequence for every coefficient choice is implied.
For completeness, an m-cycle of the return map, corresponding to a periodic orbit with m global excursions, obeys
μ=Azjδ​cosθ(zj​)−zj+1​(j=1,…,m),zm+1​=z1​,
(28)
and its total period is
T=mTg​+λ+−1​j=1∑m​log(z0​/zj​)+o(1).​
(29)
Its longitudinal multiplier is ∏j​fS′​(zj​). This is the relevant period relation for multipassage cycles; substituting their total T into the one-passage cosine law would generally be incorrect.
