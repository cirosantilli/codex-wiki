A Riemann surface is a connected one-complex-dimensional complex manifold: it has local complex coordinates with holomorphic transition maps. These coordinates induce an orientation and a conformal structure. Fix a closed oriented topological surface Sg​ of genus g. A point of Teichmüller space is a marked Riemann surface (X,f), where f:Sg​→X is an orientation-preserving marking. Two pairs are equivalent if there is a biholomorphism h:X→X′ with h∘f isotopic to f′. Thus the marking remembers how curves on the reference surface sit in X; forgetting it gives the moduli space of Riemann surfaces, obtained by quotienting by the mapping class group.
For g≥2, the uniformization theorem supplies a unique curvature-−1 hyperbolic metric in each conformal class. A pants decomposition has 3g−3 cuffs, and their positive lengths together with their real twists give Fenchel–Nielsen coordinates. Hence
Tg​≅R6g−6,dimC​Tg​=3g−3(g≥2).​
(30)
For completeness, T0​ is a point, whereas T1​ is the upper half-plane, parametrizing marked complex tori C/(Z+τZ) with Imτ>0.
The Wolpert generic spectral rigidity theorem says that, for fixed g≥2, there is a closed proper real-analytic exceptional subset of Tg​ such that a hyperbolic surface outside that subset is determined up to isometry by its unmarked length spectrum, equivalently its Laplacian spectrum. This is an unoriented isometry statement: reversing orientation cannot be detected by either spectrum.
The Huber spectral equivalence theorem says that two closed curvature-−1 hyperbolic surfaces have the same Laplacian spectrum, with multiplicities, if and only if they have the same unmarked length spectrum, with multiplicities. One may consistently count unoriented closed geodesics including their iterates; alternatively, the primitive multiset determines the multiset with iterates and conversely. This equivalence comes from the Selberg trace formula.
The Buser finite length spectrum theorem is the finite-determination ingredient: for each g≥2 and ε>0 there is a finite number L(g,ε) such that, for any two closed hyperbolic surfaces of genus g with hyperbolic systole at least ε, equality of their length multisets through L(g,ε) implies equality of their entire length spectra. The finite-length statement is used in Buser's chapter on Wolpert's theorem. It determines the full spectrum, not necessarily the isometry class.
Here is a proof of the Buser finite length spectrum theorem through finite spectral matching by polynomial invariants. We use the following subsidiary results, stating the required content. First, Mumford's compactness theorem makes the part of the genus-g moduli space of Riemann surfaces with hyperbolic systole at least ε compact. Second, marked hyperbolic holonomy representations of a closed oriented surface lift from PSL2​(R) to SL2​(R), and normalized representatives can be chosen continuously on sufficiently small parameter charts. Their hyperbolic translation length satisfies
qγ​(ρ):=tr(ρ(γ))2=4cosh2(ℓγ​(ρ)/2).
(31)
Third, the length spectrum of a compact hyperbolic surface is locally finite, and metrics on compact marked parameter charts are uniformly bilipschitz equivalent. Finally, the Hilbert basis theorem implies that a descending sequence of algebraic sets in a finite-dimensional affine space stabilizes. These are compactness, holonomy, elementary geometric finiteness and algebraic finiteness results; none is the finite unmarked-spectrum assertion we are proving.
By compactness, finitely many relatively compact marked charts cover the thick moduli space of Riemann surfaces. Choose normalized lifted hyperbolic holonomy representations on these charts and take their compact closures. Their finite union K is a compact set of determinant-one matrix tuples representing every surface in the thick part, possibly more than once. Each tuple records the images of the 2g generators of the fundamental group. Thus K sits in a fixed finite-dimensional real matrix space. Every group word is a polynomial in these matrix entries: multiplication is polynomial, and for a determinant-one two-by-two matrix its inverse is its adjugate, also polynomial. Consequently each qγ​ is a polynomial on the ambient space. Squared traces remove the sign ambiguity of the lift, and 4cosh2(ℓ/2) is strictly increasing for positive ℓ.
Enumerate the unoriented nontrivial free homotopy classes as γ1​,γ2​,…, including the iterates, and write ℓj​(x) for their geodesic lengths at x∈K. The uniform finiteness of short geodesic classes follows here directly: on each of the finitely many compact marked charts, choose a fixed reference metric h and a uniform bilipschitz constant C. A class with ℓj​(x)≤R somewhere on that chart has reference length at most CR. Only finitely many classes have that reference length, and a finite union of these finite collections is finite. In particular, for every j the candidate set
Fj​={k:minx∈K​ℓk​(x)≤maxx∈K​ℓj​(x)}
(32)
is finite. If two parameters in K have equal lengths for classes j,k, then k∈Fj​. These candidate sets depend on the fixed compact set K and the source label j, not on the number of labels currently being matched.
For every N, define EN​ in the full ambient space of pairs of matrix tuples (x,y) as follows. Require an injective choice σ:{1,…,N}→N with σ(j)∈Fj​ and
qj​(x)=qσ(j)​(y)(1≤j≤N),
(33)
and independently require an injective choice τ:{1,…,N}→N with τ(j)∈Fj​ and
qj​(y)=qτ(j)​(x)(1≤j≤N).
(34)
There are finitely many possible pairs of choices (σ,τ) because every Fj​ is finite. For each choice these are finitely many polynomial equations. Therefore EN​ is a finite union of algebraic sets, hence an algebraic set. Restricting an injective matching to its first N entries proves
E1​⊇E2​⊇E3​⊇⋯.
(35)
The Hilbert basis theorem now supplies N0​ such that EN​=EN0​​ for all N≥N0​. This step takes place in a fixed affine space and uses polynomial equations, so it gives stabilization of the actual sets, not just stabilization of germs at individual parameters.
For (x,y)∈EN0​​∩(K×K), stabilization gives injective partial length matchings in both directions for every N. Fix any length a>0. Only finitely many labels have length a at x or at y, by local finiteness. Take N large enough to contain every such label at x. Its injective matching into the labels at y preserves qj​, and hence preserves length, so the multiplicity of a at x is at most its multiplicity at y. The reverse matching gives the opposite inequality. Thus the two multiplicities are equal for every a, proving equality of the entire unmarked length spectra. No assumed global matching or unjustified exchange of infinitely many neighborhoods is needed.
Finally choose a number
L>max1≤j≤N0​​maxx∈K​ℓj​(x).
(36)
Suppose the two length multisets agree through L. For each of the first N0​ labels at x, select a distinct equal-length label at y, which is possible precisely because the truncated multisets agree with multiplicity. Each selected label belongs to the corresponding Fj​. Do the same in the reverse direction. Then (x,y)∈EN0​​, and the previous paragraph proves equality of the full length spectra. Since K was selected using only the fixed thick genus-g parameter space, the resulting finite L depends only on g,ε. This proves
L(X)∩(0,L]=L(Y)∩(0,L] ⟹ L(X)=L(Y),​
(37)
with each intersection interpreted as a multiset. By the Huber spectral equivalence theorem, the corresponding entire Laplacian spectra also agree. This is the finite matching reduction needed before the analytic and geometric rigidity steps in the Wolpert generic spectral rigidity theorem.
