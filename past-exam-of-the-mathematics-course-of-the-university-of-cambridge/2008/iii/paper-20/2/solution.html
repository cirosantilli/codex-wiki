Set B+​=3I+S, B−​=−3I+S, and define the full Euclidean lattices L±0​=B±​Z4. Their determinants have absolute value 144, since
B+−1​=123I−S​,B−−1​=12−3I−S​.

For arbitrary positive real numbers a1​,…,a4​, put D=diag(a1​​,…,a4​​) and L±​=DL±0​. This is the tetracode length-isospectral lattice construction. We prove equality of the length multisets by an explicit bijection, not just equality of the shortest lengths.
Modulo three, both lattices have image imS=T. The matrix S has rank two over F3​; its four column lines are precisely the four lines in T. Also S2=0 modulo three, so kerS=imS=T. Each nonzero tetracode word has exactly one zero coordinate. Every v∈L±0​ therefore has some coordinate divisible by three. Moreover, all four coordinates of v have the same parity, because every entry of either B±​ is odd.
For v∈L+0​, choose the least index i with 3∣vi​ and set w=v−2vi​ei​. The coordinate reflection bijection for tetracode lattices will show that w∈L−0​. First, w≡v(mod3), so Sw≡0(mod3). Second, positive-lattice membership implies Sv≡3v≡−v(mod4). Hence
(I−S)w≡2(v−vi​ei​+vi​Sei​)(mod4).
(8)
The expression in parentheses has ith coordinate zero and every other coordinate vj​+vi​Sji​ even, by the common parity and Sji​=±1. Thus (I−S)w≡0(mod4). Together the congruences modulo three and four say (−3I−S)w≡0(mod12), which is precisely B−−1​w∈Z4.
Conversely, if v∈L−0​, the same reflection has unchanged reduction modulo three, and now Sv≡v(mod4). Thus
(3I−S)(v−2vi​ei​)≡2(v+vi​ei​+vi​Sei​)(mod4).
(9)
Its ith coordinate is 4vi​ and its other coordinates are even multiples of two, so it vanishes modulo four; it vanishes modulo three as well. The reflected vector is in L+0​. The chosen index is unchanged by reflection, including for vectors reducing to zero, so this map is an involution between the two lattices. It preserves every ∣vj​∣ and therefore preserves ∥Dv∥2=∑j​aj​vj2​. The two lattices have the same length spectrum, including every multiplicity, for every positive choice of weights.
To prove a genuine failure of isometry, choose
(a1​,a2​,a3​,a4​)=(1,2​,3​,5​).
(10)
These four numbers are linearly independent over Q: changing the signs of the independent square roots in the multiquadratic field Q(2​,3​,5​) isolates each coefficient of a rational relation. If an orthogonal transformation O maps L+​ to L−​, then
P=D−1OD=B−​UB+−1​∈GL4​(Q),U∈GL4​(Z),
(11)
and PTdiag(aj​)P=diag(aj​). Comparing rational coefficients of the independent aj​ in the diagonal entries gives Pji2​=δji​. Consequently P is diagonal with entries ±1.
Such a coordinate sign change must preserve T modulo three. For each zero coordinate i, there are exactly two opposite nonzero tetracode words. Preserving their line requires the other three signs to be equal. Taking two different zero coordinates forces all four signs to be equal. Thus P=I or P=−I, either of which would imply L+0​=L−0​. But
v=B+​e1​=(3,−1,−1,−1)T,B−−1​v=(−1/2,1/2,1/2,1/2)T∈/Z4.
(12)
This contradiction proves L+​ and L−​ are not isometric. Allowing affine isometries changes nothing: translating the image of zero back to zero reduces them to the orthogonal case.
For the flat tori, take period lattices equal to the dual lattices:
X±​=R4/L±∨​.
(13)
The standard spectrum of a flat torus lemma states that its characters of a real torus are indexed by the dual of its period lattice. Here they are e2πi⟨v,x⟩ for v∈L±​, with Laplacian eigenvalue 4π2∥v∥2. These characters give a complete orthogonal basis, so our length-preserving bijection proves that X+​ and X−​ are isospectral manifolds. The second standard lemma is that an isometry of flat tori lifts to an affine isometry of their Euclidean universal covers. Its orthogonal part maps the period lattices to one another, and therefore maps their dual lattices to one another. This would give the impossible isometry L+​≅L−​. Hence X+​ and X−​ are isospectral non-isometric flat four-dimensional tori.
