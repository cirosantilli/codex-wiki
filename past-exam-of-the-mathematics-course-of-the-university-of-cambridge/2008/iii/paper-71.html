Assume an axisymmetric, geometrically thin disc in a fixed Keplerian rotation field, neglecting the disk's self-gravity. Write vR​ positive outwards and let
Ω(R)=R3GM​​,h(R)=R2Ω=GMR​.
(1)
The ring supplies the local specific angular momentum. Its source per unit area is
s(R,t)=2πR0​M˙(t)​δ(R−R0​),
(2)
where the Dirac delta function is normalized so that ∫2πRsdR=M˙(t). Define the positive outward viscous torque in an accretion disk by
G=−2πR3νΣdRdΩ​=3πνΣh.
(3)
The height-integrated conservation of mass and conservation of angular momentum equations are
∂t​Σ+R1​∂R​(RΣvR​)=s,
(4)
∂t​(Σh)+R1​∂R​(RΣvR​h)=−2πR1​∂R​G+sh0​.
(5)
Subtract h times the mass equation. Since h is time-independent, this gives
ΣvR​h′=−2πR1​G′+s(h0​−h).
(6)
The last term is zero as a distribution, because the source is supported where h=h0​. Since h′=h/(2R), substituting the torque gives
vR​=−ΣR1/23​∂R​(νΣR1/2).
(7)
Inserting this into the mass equation proves the Keplerian disk evolution with a ring source:
∂t​Σ=R3​∂R​[R1/2∂R​(νΣR1/2)]+2πR0​M˙(t)​δ(R−R0​).​
(8)
The viscosity may be a prescribed function or a function of the local disk state; the conservation derivation is the same.
To track signs in the steady problem, define the inward mass flux
FM​=−2πRΣvR​=6πR1/2∂R​(νΣR1/2).
(9)
Then 0=FM′​/(2πR)+s. Away from the source FM​ is constant, with FM​=M˙in​ on the inner side and FM​=−M˙out​ on the outer side, where both loss rates are positive. Integrating through the source gives FM​(R0+​)−FM​(R0−​)=−M˙. Therefore
M˙=M˙in​+M˙out​.​
(10)
The outward angular momentum flux is FJ​=G−FM​h. The steady angular-momentum equation gives FJ′​=2πRsh0​. At the two torque-free boundaries its values are FJ​(Rin​)=−M˙in​hin​ and FJ​(Rout​)=M˙out​hout​. Integrating across the disk therefore yields
M˙h0​=M˙in​hin​+M˙out​hout​.
(11)
Eliminate M˙out​=M˙−M˙in​ and use h∝R1/2. The two-boundary mass split of a ring-fed disk is
M˙M˙in​​=hout​−hin​hout​−h0​​=Rout​​−Rin​​Rout​​−R0​​​.​
(12)
The complementary outward fraction is (R0​​−Rin​​)/(Rout​​−Rin​​). Both fractions lie between zero and one for a source strictly between the boundaries.
As a local check, integrating the steady mass flux from each zero-torque boundary gives
νΣR​=⎩⎨⎧​3πM˙in​​(R​−Rin​​),3πM˙out​​(Rout​​−R​),​Rin​<R<R0​,R0​<R<Rout​.​
(13)
Continuity of this quantity at R0​ gives the same split. The split depends on boundary and source angular momenta, rather than on the detailed viscosity law.

Use a characteristic midplane temperature T, density ρ and disk scale height H. For an ideal gas of fixed composition, dominant gas pressure gives cs2​∝T, where cs​ is the sound speed. Vertical hydrostatic equilibrium and the alpha disk prescription give
H∼Ωcs​​∝ΩT1/2​,ρ∼HΣ​∝ΣΩT−1/2,ν∼αcs​H∝ΩαT​.
(14)
Numerical factors in the vertical averaging do not affect the exponents. The stipulated opacity becomes
κ∝(ΣΩT−1/2)−3/2T5/4=Σ−3/2Ω−3/2T2.
(15)
For an optically thick, radiatively efficient disk, radiative diffusion gives a cooling rate per unit area proportional to T4/(κΣ). The viscous heating rate is proportional to νΣΩ2. Thus
Q−∝Σ1/2Ω3/2T2,Q+∝αΣΩT.
(16)
Equating heating and cooling gives T∝αΣ1/2Ω−1/2, so
ν∝α2Σ1/2Ω−3/2.
(17)
At fixed α and central mass, Ω∝R−3/2. Therefore the gas-pressure alpha disk with inverse-density opacity satisfies
ν∝Σ1/2R9/4.​
(18)
For thermal stability of an accretion disk, perturb T at fixed R and Σ, since the thermal adjustment is faster than radial mass redistribution. The logarithmic heating and cooling slopes are respectively one and two. At equilibrium, a fractional temperature increase therefore makes the excess heating Q+−Q− decrease, and the disk cools back. Thus the local thermal equilibrium is stable within the assumed gas-pressure, radiative-diffusion closure.
For viscous stability of an accretion disk, use the thermally equilibrated relation between viscosity and surface density. It gives
νΣ∝Σ3/2R9/4,∂Σ∂(νΣ)​​R​>0.
(19)
Locally freezing the background radius in the Keplerian viscous diffusion equation, a short radial Fourier mode has growth rate −3kR2​∂Σ​(νΣ)<0. Density enhancements diffuse rather than grow, so the disk is locally viscously stable as well.

This part uses source-free viscous evolution of an accretion disk. Put R=R0​x2 and Σ=Σ0​x−3S. The prescribed kinematic viscosity then becomes
ν=ν0​(Sx−3)1/2x9/2=ν0​S1/2x3,
(20)
so νΣR1/2=ν0​Σ0​R01/2​xS3/2. Since ∂R​=(2R0​x)−1∂x​, the Keplerian viscous diffusion equation gives
Σ0​x−3∂t​S=4R02​3ν0​Σ0​​x−3∂x2​(xS3/2).
(21)
Canceling the prefactor and setting τ=3ν0​t/(4R02​) proves
∂τ​S=∂x2​(xS3/2).​
(22)
Use the similarity ansatz S=τ−1f(ξ) with ξ=x/τ​. Derivatives at fixed x give
∂τ​S=τ−2(−f−2ξ​f′),∂x2​(xS3/2)=τ−2(ξf3/2)′′.
(23)
On the branch 1−kξ≥0, substitute f=(1−kξ)2. The two expressions reduce to
−f−2ξ​f′=−1+3kξ−2k2ξ2,
(24)
(ξf3/2)′′=(ξ(1−kξ)3)′′=−6k+18k2ξ−12k3ξ2.
(25)
Equality of the constant coefficients requires k=1/6​, and then the other coefficients agree as well.
The physical compact self-similar disk with square-root density viscosity uses this branch up to its outer edge and vacuum beyond it:
S(x,τ)=τ1​(1−6τ​x​)+2​,τ>0.​
(26)
Here (u)+​=max(u,0) is the positive part. The cutoff matters: beyond ξ=6, the untruncated squared expression would have f3/2=∣1−ξ/6∣3, and its right-hand side would no longer be the polynomial used above. At the moving edge, S vanishes quadratically and xS3/2 vanishes cubically. The latter and its first derivative match the vacuum values continuously, so joining to zero creates no distributional mass-flux source. The joined profile satisfies the equation, including in the weak sense at the edge.
Returning to the surface density gives the explicit result
Σ(R,τ)=τΣ0​​(R0​R​)−3/2(1−6τ​R/R0​​​)2,0<R<36R0​τ,​
(27)
with Σ=0 for R≥36R0​τ. Thus the outer edge is Re​=36R0​τ​ and moves linearly in physical time, with speed 27ν0​/R0​. The density decreases monotonically with radius, has an integrable R−3/2 central cusp, and drops quadratically to zero at the outer edge. The formal origin boundary has zero viscous torque in an accretion disk, because νΣR​→0 there, even though the surface density diverges.
To characterize the spreading, let a=6τ​ and integrate in x. The disk mass and angular momentum are
Md​Jd​​=2π∫0Re​​ΣRdR=τ4πΣ0​R02​​∫0a​(1−x/a)2dx=8πΣ0​R02​τ−1/2​,=2π∫0Re​​ΣRGMR​dR=τ4πΣ0​R02​GMR0​​​∫0a​x(1−x/a)2dx=12πΣ0​R02​GMR0​​​.​
(28)
The mass decreases while the angular momentum remains constant: matter accretes at the origin and the remaining disk spreads outward. More explicitly, with u=x/(6τ​), the inward mass flux is
FM​=3πν0​Σ0​τ−3/2(1−u)2(1−4u).
(29)
It is inward for u<1/4 and outward for u>1/4. The dividing radius is 9R0​τ/4, and the origin flux 3πν0​Σ0​τ−3/2 equals −dMd​/dt. The outer edge carries no flux beyond itself. This family is a self-similar accreting disk with finite mass at each positive time, rather than a regular finite-mass initial profile at τ=0.

Each annulus has a torque proportional to Jh​×δJd​, so its scalar product with Jh​ is zero. Summing over annuli preserves this property even when the proportionality coefficient varies with radius. Therefore T⋅Jh​=0​ for the total torque due to Lense-Thirring precession.
The torque specified is the torque on the disk. Assuming a closed disk-plus-hole angular-momentum budget with no external torque, its reaction on the black hole has the opposite sign:
J˙h​=−T=−K1​Jh​×Jd​−K2​Jh​×(Jh​×Jd​),J˙d​=T.​
(30)
Thus J˙t​=0. The K1​ term describes precession, because it is perpendicular to both spins. Since Jh​×Jd​=Jh​×Jt​, the hole precesses about the fixed total vector. The K2​ term changes the spin directions dissipatively. Writing Jd,⊥​ for the disk component perpendicular to the hole, the vector triple product identity gives
Jh​×(Jh​×Jd​)=−Jh2​Jd,⊥​.
(31)
A positive K2​ removes this component from the disk and transfers it into the hole's changing direction. This is black-hole and disk alignment under internal torque.
Since T is perpendicular to Jh​,
dtd​Jh2​=2Jh​⋅J˙h​=−2Jh​⋅T=0.
(32)
Hence Jh​ is constant​, even if the torque coefficients vary in time.
Now differentiate the scalar product with the conserved total vector. The precession term contributes zero, and the vector triple product identity gives
dtd​(Jh​⋅Jt​)​=−T⋅(Jh​+Jd​)=−T⋅Jd​=K2​[Jh2​Jd2​−(Jh​⋅Jd​)2]=K2​∣Jh​×Jd​∣2=A.​​
(33)
Also Jh​⋅Jt​=Jh2​+Jh​⋅Jd​, so constancy of Jh​ gives
dtd​(Jh​⋅Jt​)=dtd​(Jh​⋅Jd​).​
(34)
Finally, Jt2​=Jh2​+Jd2​+2Jh​⋅Jd​ is constant. Differentiating proves
dtd​Jd2​=−2A.​
(35)
Thus the disk's spin magnitude decreases while the hole's magnitude is fixed; their vector sum remains fixed as well.
For the alignment conclusion, assume Jh​,Jt​>0 and let c=cosθ=Jh​⋅Jt​/(Jh​Jt​). Since Jh​×Jd​=Jh​×Jt​,
c˙=K2​Jh​Jt​(1−c2).​
(36)
For a noncollinear initial state, ∣c(0)∣<1, this integrates exactly to
artanhc(t)=artanhc(0)+Jh​Jt​∫0t​K2​(s)ds.
(37)
Consequently a fixed positive K2​, or more generally a positive coefficient of divergent integrated strength, gives c(t)→1. In this persistent dissipative evolution, Jh​→Jh​Jt​​: the hole aligns asymptotically with the total angular momentum. The exactly antiparallel configuration c=−1 is a stationary, nongeneric exception; when Jt​=0 there is no total-vector direction to align with.
If the phrase K2​>0 allows arbitrary time dependence, positivity alone does not logically force complete eventual alignment. The persistent-torque criterion for asymptotic spin alignment is the needed qualification. For example, take Jh​=Jt​=1, K1​=0, K2​(t)=e−t and c(0)=0. The same torque equations give c(t)=tanh(1−e−t), with limit tanh1<1. The final configurations below use the intended persistent-torque regime, including constant positive K2​.
Once the hole has aligned, conservation of angular momentum fixes the disk without any additional torque calculation:
Jh,f​=Jh​Jt​,Jd,f​=(Jt​−Jh​)Jt​.​
(38)
The disk and hole therefore finish parallel only if Jt​>Jh​. If Jt​<Jh​, they finish antiparallel: this is counteralignment of a black hole and accretion disk. In terms of the initial disk-hole angle θ0​, squaring Jt​ shows that counteralignment occurs when
cosθ0​<−2Jh​Jd,0​​.​
(39)
If Jt​=Jh​, the final disk angular momentum vanishes and has no spin direction. Thus alignment of the hole with the total vector does not imply alignment of both component spins.
