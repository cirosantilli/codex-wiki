The Noether gauging procedure starts by allowing a constant symmetry parameter to depend on position. For an internal symmetry written as δϕi=ϵATA​ϕi, integration by parts identifies the Noether current:
δS0​=∫d4xjAμ​∂μ​ϵA
(1)
up to a boundary term. Introduce a gauge field AμA​ and add −gAμA​jAμ​, with the inhomogeneous gauge transformation δAμA​=g−1∂μ​ϵA+⋯. This cancels the displayed variation. The current coupling has its own variation, so one iterates: add the needed higher-order interactions and corrections to the gauge transformations, and require closure of the local symmetry algebra. For an ordinary gauge theory, this reorganizes derivatives into gauge covariant derivatives and fixes the nonlinear gauge field strength. Gauging is therefore more than replacing a constant parameter by a function.
For rigid supersymmetry, the corresponding Noether current is the spinor-valued supercurrent Sμ. A local Majorana spinor parameter gives a variation proportional to (∂μ​ϵˉ)Sμ. A gravitino Ψμ​, with an inhomogeneous transformation δΨμ​∼κ−1∂μ​ϵ, cancels it through a coupling proportional to κΨˉμ​Sμ. The commutator of two rigid supersymmetry transformations is a translation. With local parameters, the translation parameter becomes position dependent, so the completion must also gauge translations: it introduces the vierbein, diffeomorphism invariance of general relativity, and local Lorentz transformations. The gravitino and graviton thus belong to the same supergravity multiplet.
For pure minimal four-dimensional supergravity, start with the free massless Fierz-Pauli action and the free Rarita-Schwinger field kinetic term. The Noether gauging procedure replaces the former by the Einstein-Hilbert action and couples the latter to the vierbein and spin connection. In a canonical normalization the leading transformations can be written
δeμ​a=2κ​ϵˉγaΨμ​,δΨμ​=κ−1Dμ​ϵ.
(2)
An overall sign or phase changes with the curvature and Majorana spinor conventions, but the relative normalization must be used consistently. To see why these terms fit together, first work to quadratic order in fermions, so the spin connection is torsion free. Varying the Rarita-Schwinger field kinetic term under δΨμ​=∇μ​ϵ/κ produces an antisymmetric double spinor covariant derivative. The spinor curvature identity and the Einstein tensor contraction of spin curvature give, in the matching curvature convention,
[∇ρ​,∇σ​]ϵ=41​Rρσab​γabϵ,γμρσ∇ρ​∇σ​ϵ=21​Gμν​γνϵ.
(3)
The resulting Einstein tensor term cancels the variation of the Einstein-Hilbert action under δeμ​a. This is the essential first nontrivial cancellation in the construction.
At higher fermion order, the variations of the vierbein and spin connection produce further terms. Treating the spin connection as independent makes its Euler-Lagrange field equation algebraic. Its solution is a gravitino-induced torsion connection. With Ψa​=ea​μΨμ​ and γab=[γa,γb]/2, a conventional canonical normalization gives
ωμab​=ωμab​(e)+Kμab​,Kμab​=4κ2​(Ψˉμ​γa​Ψb​−Ψˉμ​γb​Ψa​+Ψˉa​γμ​Ψb​).
(4)
Equivalently, with Ta=dea+ωab​∧eb, the torsion tensor obeys
Tμν​a=2κ2​Ψˉμ​γaΨν​.
(5)
The Majorana Grassmann bilinear interchange makes the right-hand side antisymmetric in μ,ν. Eliminating the spin connection generates the four-fermion completion. In the 1.5-order formalism one substitutes its solution but varies the other fields holding the connection fixed: the omitted chain-rule term is (δS/δω)δω=0. The remaining cubic fermion variations cancel by the Clifford algebra and Fierz rearrangements. This produces the displayed supergravity action in its compact connection-dependent form. Calling this formulation on shell means that no independent auxiliary fields have been retained and that closure of the supersymmetry algebra uses the propagating Euler-Lagrange field equations; it does not mean that action invariance is obtained only by setting every variation to zero on a solution.
In the antisymmetrized kinetic term, Ψσ​ is a spinor-valued one-form and the derivative is conventionally written
Dρ​Ψσ​=∂ρ​Ψσ​+41​ωρab​γabΨσ​,γν​=eν​aγa​.
(6)
This is the Lorentz-covariant exterior-derivative convention for that term. If one instead uses the full affine covariant derivative of a vector-spinor, a coordinate-index connection is also present and the antisymmetrization must include its torsion tensor correction. The crucial physical distinction from pure metric general relativity is the spin connection contains gravitino-induced contorsion, rather than being the torsion-free Levi-Civita connection. It reduces to the ordinary spinor covariant derivative when the gravitino vanishes.

Use the Clifford algebra {γμ,γν}=2ημν and unit-weight antisymmetrization. The duality identity between γ5​γν​ and three antisymmetrized gamma matrices turns the equation into
Eμ:=γμνρ∂ν​Ψρ​=0,γμνρ=γ[μγνγρ].
(7)
The overall nonzero factor in this identity depends on the orientation and γ5​ convention and does not change its solutions. This Rarita-Schwinger field equation has the gauge symmetry
Ψμ​⟼Ψμ​+∂μ​ϵ,
(8)
since γμνρ∂ν​∂ρ​ϵ=0. It is the free-field limit of local supersymmetry, so it is suitable for the gravitino.
To check which spin it propagates, put A=γμΨμ​ and B=∂μΨμ​. Expanding the three gamma matrices using the Clifford algebra gives
Eμ=∂Ψμ−∂μA+γμ(∂A−B),γμ​Eμ=2(∂A−B)
(9)
in four dimensions. Thus the Euler-Lagrange field equation requires B=∂A. Under the gauge transformation, A↦A+∂ϵ; locally one may choose gamma-trace gauge fixing, A=0, by solving ∂ϵ=−A. The Euler-Lagrange field equation then yields
γμΨμ​=0,∂μΨμ​=0,∂Ψμ​=0.
(10)
These are the massless Dirac equation, transversality and gamma-trace constraints that remove the lower-spin part of a vector-spinor.
For a more explicit massless Rarita-Schwinger polarization count, take a positive-frequency plane wave with null momentum along the third axis, in signature (−,+,+,+). Transversality relates its temporal and longitudinal components. Residual gauge transformations with pϵ=0 remove both. Write the surviving components as u1​,u2​; their gamma-trace condition reads γ1u1​+γ2u2​=0, hence
u2​=γ1γ2u1​,pu1​=0.
(11)
The massless Dirac equation leaves two complex positive-frequency amplitudes in u1​. If iγ1γ2u1​=su1​, s=±1, then (u1​,u2​) is proportional to (1,−is) in the transverse vector index. With spatial rotation generators J3vec​12​=−i and J3spin​=−iγ1γ2/2, the vector has helicity −s and the Majorana spinor has helicity −s/2, giving total helicity −3s/2. The Majorana spinor condition fixes the negative-frequency coefficients from these amplitudes. Therefore a massless gravitino has spin 3/2, with the two physical helicities ±3/2. A complex vector-spinor would also have independent antiparticle modes.
The requested count is instead the off-shell component count of minimal supergravity, before imposing any Euler-Lagrange field equations. A four-dimensional Majorana spinor has four real components. Its vector index supplies four copies, and local supersymmetry supplies four real gauge functions:
ngravitino,off​=4×4−4=12.​
(12)
One must not additionally subtract transversality and the massless Dirac equation in this count: those are on shell conditions. The gamma-trace gauge fixing already uses the same four gauge functions subtracted above. For the graviton, a symmetric metric tensor has ten real components, with four diffeomorphism gauge functions. Equivalently, a vierbein has sixteen real components, minus six local Lorentz transformation and four diffeomorphism functions:
ngraviton,off​=10−4=16−6−4=6.​
(13)
The gravitino and graviton each have two propagating states on shell, but their off-shell counts disagree by six.
An auxiliary field has an algebraic Euler-Lagrange field equation and no propagating kinetic term. In old-minimal supergravity, a complex scalar M contributes two real bosonic components and a real vector bμ​ contributes four, so the bosonic count becomes 6+2+4=12, matching the gravitino. Their supersymmetry transformations also absorb the terms that would otherwise require the propagating Euler-Lagrange field equations to close the local algebra. Thus auxiliary fields allow off-shell supersymmetry closure and matching component counts without adding physical particles. Eliminating them recovers an on shell formulation.

For a single chiral superfield, the supergravity F-term potential, with κ=mp−1​ restored, is
V=eκ2K(Kzzˉ∣Dz​W∣2−3κ2∣W∣2),Dz​W=Wz​+κ2Kz​W.
(14)
The canonical Kähler potential has Kzzˉ​=Kzzˉ=1, so the Kähler covariant derivative of a superpotential is
Dz​W=μmp​[1+κ2zˉ(z+β)].
(15)
Consequently the Polonyi model has the scalar potential
V=∣μ∣2mp2​eκ2∣z∣2(​1+κ2zˉ(z+β)​2−3κ2∣z+β∣2).​
(16)
The negative term is essential; retaining only the square of the supergravity auxiliary field would give the global F-term scalar potential rather than the supergravity F-term potential.
Set x=κz=a+iy and b=κβ=2−3​. In this notation V=∣μ∣2mp2​e∣x∣2U, where
U=(1+a2+y2+ba)2+b2y2−3[(a+b)2+y2].
(17)
At a0​=3​−1, y=0, one has a0​+b=1 and 1+a0​(a0​+b)=3​, so U=0. To prove that this is a local minimum in both real directions, rather than checking only the real axis, put u=a−a0​. Direct expansion gives the stable zero-energy Polonyi vacuum identity
U=(u2+y2+3​u)2+(23​−3)u2+(4−23​)y2.
(18)
Every coefficient outside the square is strictly positive, and the exponential factor is positive. Thus V≥0 everywhere and equality requires u=y=0, provided μ=0. This proves the stronger conclusion
⟨z⟩=(3​−1)mp​,Vmin​=0,​
(19)
a unique global minimum of this Polonyi model. If μ=0, the scalar potential vanishes identically and this uniqueness and supersymmetry breaking conclusion do not hold.
For an independent local stability check, the Hessian matrix of U at this global minimum is diagonal:
Uaa​=43​,Uyy​=8−43​,Uay​=0.
(20)
Since U and its first derivatives vanish there, differentiating the exponential introduces no additional Hessian matrix terms at the global minimum. For canonically normalized real fluctuations z=z0​+(s+it)/2​, the squared scalar masses are therefore
ms2​=23​∣μ∣2ea02​,mt2​=2(2−3​)∣μ∣2ea02​,
(21)
which are both positive.
Although the vacuum energy is zero, the supergravity auxiliary field is not. In the convention Fz=−eκ2K/2Dz​W​, its vacuum expectation value obeys
∣Fz∣=3​∣μ∣mp​ea02​/2=0.
(22)
The matter fermion transformation contains a term proportional to Fzϵ, so no nonzero constant supersymmetry parameter leaves this vacuum invariant. Hence supersymmetry is spontaneously broken even though the vacuum energy vanishes: the positive auxiliary field contribution cancels the negative gravitational term. The matter fermion supplies the goldstino, which is absorbed by the gravitino in the super-Higgs mechanism.
Using the gravitino mass from a superpotential, and z0​+β=mp​, gives
m3/2​=κ2eκ2K/2∣W∣=∣μ∣e2−3​=3​mp​∣Fz∣​.​
(23)
An auxiliary field Fz has mass dimension two. Defining the supersymmetry breaking scale by ΛSUSY​=∣Fz∣​, one obtains
ΛSUSY​m3/2​​=3​mp​ΛSUSY​​.
(24)
Thus the gravitino mass is small compared with the supersymmetry breaking scale when that scale is sub-Planckian, as in the usual hierarchy ∣μ∣≪mp​. This smallness is gravitational suppression; it is not a conclusion valid for arbitrary choices of the mass parameter.

Use the scalar components of the three chiral superfields, and set a=∣ϕ0​∣2, b=∣ϕ+​∣2, c=∣ϕ−​∣2 and Σ=a+b+c. We assume the physical sign g>0 and a nonzero coupling λ. The coefficient g below is exactly the coefficient in the supplied D-term scalar potential; it should not be silently replaced by a differently normalized squared gauge coupling. The canonical Kähler potential has identity Kähler metric, and the three Kähler covariant derivatives of a superpotential are
D0​WD+​WD−​W​=λϕ+​ϕ−​(1+κ2a),=λϕ0​ϕ−​(1+κ2b),=λϕ0​ϕ+​(1+κ2c).​
(25)
Substitution in the supergravity F-term potential gives
VF​==​∣λ∣2eκ2Σ[bc(1+κ2a)2+ac(1+κ2b)2+ab(1+κ2c)2−3κ2abc]∣λ∣2eκ2Σ[ab+ac+bc+3κ2abc+κ4abcΣ].​
(26)
In particular, the subtraction cancels only half of the six κ2abc terms contributed by the three squares. The full scalar potential is
V=∣λ∣2eκ2Σ[ab+ac+bc+3κ2abc+κ4abcΣ]+g(b−c−ζ)2≥0.​
(27)
Its nonnegativity follows term by term from a,b,c≥0 and g>0. This is a special property of this cubic superpotential, not of a general supergravity F-term potential.
The D-term vacuum branches of a cubic superpotential must be described as branches: there are not two isolated field-space minima. First suppose ζ>0. Vanishing of VF​ requires ab=ac=bc=0, and vanishing of the D-term scalar potential requires b−c=ζ. Together these conditions give the vacuum expectation values
⟨ϕ0​⟩=⟨ϕ−​⟩=0,∣⟨ϕ+​⟩∣=ζ​,V=0.​
(28)
The phase of ϕ+​ lies on a gauge orbit, and one may choose it positive and real. At this global minimum, all Di​W and the real auxiliary field of the vector multiplet vanish. It is a supersymmetric vacuum, even though the internal gauge symmetry is spontaneously broken. For ζ<0, interchange ϕ+​ and ϕ−​: the nonzero vacuum expectation value has magnitude −ζ​. For ζ=0, the supersymmetric vacuum is the entire family ϕ+​=ϕ−​=0, with ϕ0​ arbitrary.
The other branch has ϕ+​=ϕ−​=0 and arbitrary ϕ0​. Here W=Di​W=0 but the real auxiliary field is nonzero for ζ=0, and
V=gζ2.
(29)
Thus this branch has pure D-term supersymmetry breaking and a flat direction of a scalar potential in the neutral scalar. It is not stable at every neutral-field value. At a fixed a=∣ϕ0​∣2, expansion in the charged scalars yields
V=gζ2+(∣λ∣2aeκ2a−2gζ)∣ϕ+​∣2+(∣λ∣2aeκ2a+2gζ)∣ϕ−​∣2+O((b+c)2).
(30)
Hence the two charged squared masses and the stability condition are
m±2​=∣λ∣2aeκ2a∓2gζ,∣λ∣2aeκ2a>2g∣ζ∣.​
(31)
Above this threshold, both charged directions have positive mass squared. Continuity makes them uniformly positive in a sufficiently small neighborhood of the chosen neutral-field value, so the stationary branch is a genuine, non-strict local minimum valley. Its neutral direction stays flat. Below the threshold, a charged mass squared is negative and the branch is a saddle point. At equality it is a boundary of the stable valley, not a local minimum in the full field space: changing a slightly downward and switching on the unstable charged field lowers V. For example, for ζ>0, put a=ac​−ϵ, b=ϵ2, c=0. Since M(a)=∣λ∣2aeκ2a has M′(ac​)>0, one finds V−gζ2=−M′(ac​)ϵ3+O(ϵ4)<0.
There are no further local-minimum branches under these generic assumptions. If b+c>0, the expression for VF​ is strictly increasing in a, so a stationary configuration with a charged condensate must have a=0. At a=0, an interior critical point with b,c>0 is impossible, because
∂b​V+∂c​V=∣λ∣2eκ2(b+c)(b+c+2κ2bc)>0.
(32)
On the boundary c=0, the relevant scalar potential is g(b−ζ)2; on b=0 it is g(c+ζ)2. Their nonnegative-domain minima give exactly the supersymmetric vacuum described above, or the origin of the charged-field branch. Thus, for nonzero ζ, the two types are the zero-energy supersymmetric gauge orbit and the positive-energy D-breaking minimum valley above the threshold. If ζ=0, the two types coalesce into the flat supersymmetric vacuum; the literal two-minimum assertion needs this parameter qualification.
In the global supersymmetry limit κ→0, the F-term scalar potential becomes ∣λ∣2(ab+ac+bc) and the D-term scalar potential retains its form. The same two types of branches occur, with the threshold ∣λ∣2a>2g∣ζ∣. Supergravity instead introduces the exponential and additional positive terms. The goldstino of global supersymmetry breaking is a physical fermion; in local supersymmetry it participates in the super-Higgs mechanism. A positive vacuum energy also gravitates in supergravity. In particular, the positive-energy branch here has W=0 and hence vanishing gravitino mass from a superpotential; it is not the tuned zero-energy broken vacuum of Question 3.
There is a further gauge-consistency qualification. In conventional two-derivative matter-coupled supergravity, a constant Fayet–Iliopoulos term gauges an R-symmetry and requires a corresponding nontrivial transformation of the superpotential. The apparent charges 0,+1,−1 make the supplied cubic superpotential neutral, so a nonzero independent constant ζ does not by itself specify a consistent conventional supergravity action. The computed branches are those of the supplied scalar potential; a full embedding needs compatible charges or additional fields. This restriction is explained in Van Proeyen's derivation of the superpotential gauge-covariance condition.
More generally, the supergravity moment-map constraint on D-term breaking follows directly from gauge covariance. If ki∂i​W=−κ2rW and the moment map is P=i(kiKi​−r), then at a point with W=0,
P=iκ−2WkiDi​W​.
(33)
Consequently all chiral auxiliary fields vanishing implies the D-term also vanishes there. This relation has no analogue forcing that implication in an arbitrary global supersymmetry model. Its W=0 hypothesis matters: the formal D-breaking branch above has W=0, so it does not contradict the identity. These conventional gauge-completion restrictions are additional to the elementary minimization calculation.
