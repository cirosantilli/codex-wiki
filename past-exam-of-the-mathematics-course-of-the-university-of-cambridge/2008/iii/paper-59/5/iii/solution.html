In algebraic quantum field theory, regions carry observable algebras A(O). Microcausality requires
[A,B]=0if A∈A(O1​), B∈A(O2​), O1​ and O2​ are spacelike separated.​
(39)
Field operators are usually distributions, so the corresponding formula uses fields smeared with test functions of spacelike-separated support. Fermionic fields obey graded locality, while their physical even observables commute. This separates the physical observable condition from an unqualified demand that all fields commute.
For a free Klein–Gordon field, the commutator is proportional to the difference of retarded and advanced fundamental solutions. Their causal support makes this commutator vanish outside the light cone. Vacuum two-point correlations can nevertheless be nonzero at spacelike separation: the condition concerns the commutator, not the expectation of a product or statistical independence of outcomes.
To see its operational relevance, take local Kraus operators Mα​∈A(O1​) with ∑α​Mα†​Mα​=I. For a remote B∈A(O2​) the dual operation satisfies
∑α​Mα†​BMα​=B∑α​Mα†​Mα​=B.
(40)
Thus such a local unselected operation changes no remote expectation in any state, proving an algebraic no-signalling result without assuming a tensor-product decomposition of the local algebras. The allowed measurement operations must actually be local; calling an arbitrary ideal projector a measurement is not enough.
Spacelike commutation allows entanglement and Bell-inequality violation while preventing signalling by these local operations. It is a kinematic algebraic condition. An additional dynamical condition is needed to say that initial-region data determine fields in the domain of dependence.
