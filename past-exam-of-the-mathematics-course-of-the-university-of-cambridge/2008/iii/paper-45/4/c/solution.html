An orthonormal multiresolution analysis is a sequence of closed subspaces Vj​⊂L2(R) satisfying Vj​⊂Vj+1​, ⋃j​Vj​​=L2(R), ⋂j​Vj​={0}, and f∈Vj​ iff f(2⋅)∈Vj+1​. A scaling function ϕ has integer translates forming an orthonormal basis of V0​, and its scaled translates ϕj,k​(x)=2j/2ϕ(2jx−k) span Vj​. Thus Vj​ contains information at resolution about 2−j.
The detail space Wj​=Vj+1​∩Vj⊥​ is generated by the corresponding wavelet functions ψj,k​(x)=2j/2ψ(2jx−k). The orthogonal splitting Vj+1​=Vj​⊕Wj​ iterates to
VJ​=VJ0​​⊕j=J0​⨁J−1​Wj​.​
(29)
Accordingly a square-integrable signal has a coarse-and-detail wavelet series,
f=∑k​aJ0​,k​ϕJ0​,k​+∑j≥J0​​∑k​θj,k​ψj,k​,
(30)
and its wavelet projection onto VJ​ keeps the coarse coefficients and details below level J. Increasing J adds finer-scale oscillations and localized features. On the real line there are infinitely many translates at each level; for a bounded observation interval, with an appropriate interval or periodic construction, the relevant approximation dimension satisfies dJ​≍2J. For the Haar wavelet on a unit interval this is exactly 2J, corresponding to a separate mean on each dyadic subinterval.
This hierarchy gives a concrete meaning to wavelet approximation complexity. Coarse resolution uses few coefficients and can miss sharp features; fine resolution uses more coefficients and can fit noise. By orthogonality, projection bias is the energy of the omitted details,
∥f−PJ​f∥22​=∑j≥J​∑k​∣θj,k​∣2.
(31)
For an orthonormal sequence model with independent coefficient noise of variance σ2/n, estimating the dJ​ retained coefficients without shrinkage gives
E∥f​J​−f∥22​=j≥J,k∑​∣θj,k​∣2+nσ2dJ​​.​
(32)
The first term decreases with resolution while the second increases, making the bias-variance tradeoff explicit. Equivalently, a fixed orthogonal regression projection of rank dJ​ has effective degrees of freedom dJ​ and prediction optimism 2σ2dJ​/n under independent equal-variance errors.
Wavelets also permit local rather than only global complexity control. Thresholding small estimated detail coefficients, for example by soft thresholding, retains strong local structure while suppressing fine-scale noise elsewhere. A sparse retained coefficient set can represent localized features much more economically than keeping every coefficient through a high level. Its locations and scales matter as well as its count. If that set is selected using the same noisy responses, however, selection adds adaptivity: its raw coefficient count is not automatically its covariance-based degrees of freedom. Resolution or threshold tuning should therefore account for prediction error, using an appropriate risk estimate or cross-validation, rather than only decreasing training error.
Wavelet complexity is controlled by retained resolutions and coefficients; more detail reduces approximation bias while increasing estimation variance and sensitivity to noise.
