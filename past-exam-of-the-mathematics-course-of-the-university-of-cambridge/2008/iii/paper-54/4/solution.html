The last panel of the preceding process figure shows deep inelastic scattering through a virtual photon. The blob represents the electromagnetic hadronic tensor response, with all final hadronic states included. With charges factored into e and the hadronic current as in the question, a suitable scattering amplitude, up to an irrelevant overall phase, is
MX​=Q2e2​u(p′)γμ​u(p)⟨X∣Jhμ​∣H(P)⟩,Q2=−q2.​
(29)
The electron fermion spin sum, before averaging the two initial spin states, gives the following normalization of the leptonic tensor:
Lμν​=Tr[p′γμ​pγν​]=4(pμ′​pν​+pν′​pμ​−gμν​p⋅p′).​
(30)
The initial electron spin average is a separate factor 1/2. Equivalently one can absorb it into a tensor L/2, but the prefactor in the requested formula uses the unaveraged trace L. The massless Dirac equations imply qμLμν​=qνLμν​=0, the relevant Ward identity.
In the target rest frame the incident flux is 4p⋅P=4ME. This remains valid for a massive target because the electron is massless: the general flux 4(p⋅P)2−p2P2​ reduces to that expression. The outgoing electron contributes d3p′/((2π)32E′), and the inclusive final-state sum is 4πWHμν​. Including the electron spin average therefore gives
d3p′dσ​​=4ME1​(2π)32E′1​(Q2)2e4​21​Lμν​(4πWHμν​)=(2π)28MEE′(Q2)2e4​Lμν​WHμν​.​​
(31)
The implicit hadron spin average in WH​ must not be inserted a second time.
For an unpolarized target, Lorentz invariance permits tensor structures formed from P, q and the metric. Hermiticity relates the two index orders. The electromagnetic response is parity even, excluding the pseudotensor iϵμνρσPρ​qσ​. A possible ordinary antisymmetric Pμqν−qμPν term is excluded by current conservation. The symmetric part initially has four structures, gμν, PμPν, qμqν and Pμqν+qμPν. Imposing both current-conservation conditions leaves the transverse decomposition of an unpolarized hadronic tensor:
WHμν​=−(gμν−q2qμqν​)F1​+(Pμ−q2ν​qμ)(Pν−q2ν​qν)νF2​​,ν=P⋅q.
(32)
For example, contracting q into the first structure gives (1+A)qμ in the printed parametrization, and contracting into either parenthesis gives (1+B)ν or (1+C)ν. Since the two independent deep-inelastic structure functions are not generally linked by symmetry, transversality requires
A=B=C=−1.​
(33)
There are only two invariant variables once P2=M2 is fixed; they may be chosen as Q2 and Bjorken x x=Q2/(2ν).
Now make the leading massless collinear parton approximation. In the hard parton calculation, use a lightlike collinear reference for P, neglecting target-mass effects of relative order M2/Q2 as well as quark masses. Literally combining a massive P with an exactly massless constituent momentum ξP would be inconsistent. The exact rest-frame flux derived above does not require this approximation; it is the following partonic response that does.
Let r=ξP and k=r+q. Strip the quark charge out of the current, since its square is already the factor Qf2​ in the parton convolution. Averaging the initial quark spin and summing the final spin gives the spin-averaged electromagnetic parton tensor
Sμν=21​Tr[rγνkγμ]=2(rμkν+rνkμ−gμνr⋅k).
(34)
The spatial momentum delta fixes k=q+ξP. The remaining one-particle Lorentz-invariant phase space, together with 1/(4πξ) in the definition, supplies
4πξ1​(2π)32Ek​(2π)4​δ(Eq​+ξEP​−Ek​)=4ξEk​δ(Eq​+ξEP​−Ek​)​.
(35)
Since r2=k2=0 and r⋅k=ξP⋅q, the full parton response is
Wμν=Ek​δ(Eq​+ξEP​−Ek​)​[ξPμPν+2Pμqν+qμPν​−2ν​gμν].
(36)
Terms with a free q index vanish when contracted with the conserved leptonic tensor. Thus the part of W contributing to the cross-section is
Wμν ≐ Ek​δ(Eq​+ξEP​−Ek​)​[ξPμPν−2ν​gμν],​
(37)
where ≐ denotes equality in that contraction, rather than an equality of the full tensors. The omitted terms are necessary for the full hadronic tensor to be transverse; the wording about contributing terms in the PDF is important here.
To prove the delta identity with the correct Jacobian, set ω=Eq​+ξEP​. On physical support Ek​>0 and ω>0. Factoring Ek2​−ω2=(Ek​−ω)(Ek​+ω) gives the positive-energy on-shell delta function identity
Ek​δ(ω−Ek​)​=2δ(Ek2​−ω2).
(38)
This is restricted to the positive-energy branch; the other root must not be included. Using the spatial momentum constraint and P2=0 in the leading hard approximation,
Ek2​−ω2=−(q+ξP)2=Q2−2ξν=2ν(x−ξ).
(39)
The Dirac delta function change of variable therefore gives the parton on-shell delta identity
Ek​δ(Eq​+ξEP​−Ek​)​=2δ(Ek2​−(Eq​+ξEP​)2)=νδ(x−ξ)​.​
(40)
Here ν>0. This is the actual printed identity: the converted TeX's final δ(x)/Ek​ is a transcription error. If target mass is formally retained in the delta argument, it becomes Q2−2ξν−ξ2M2, so the stated simple root and Jacobian no longer apply.
Let D(x)=∑f​Qf2​(qf​(x)+q​f​(x)). The parton integral collapses at ξ=x, giving
WHμν​≐D(x)[νx​PμPν−21​gμν].
(41)
The transverse decomposition of an unpolarized hadronic tensor, in the same contraction, is −gμνF1​+PμPνF2​/ν. Comparing the two independent structures yields
F1​(x,Q2)=21​D(x),F2​(x,Q2)=xD(x),F2​=2xF1​.​
(42)
This derives the Callan-Gross relation from the spin-one-half trace and on-shell constraint. Antiquarks give the same symmetric response and therefore the sum qf​+q​f​. The result applies at leading order in the massless parton model; target masses, intrinsic transverse momentum and Quantum chromodynamics radiative effects can generate a nonzero longitudinal deep-inelastic structure function and modify the relation.
