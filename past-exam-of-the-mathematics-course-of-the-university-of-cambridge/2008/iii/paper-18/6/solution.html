A real rank-d vector bundle p:E→M is a continuous family of d-dimensional real vector spaces, locally trivial by homeomorphisms p−1(U)≅U×Rd which are linear on every fiber. Transition matrices are continuous maps into GLd​(R). The Grassmannian Grk​(Rn) is the space of k-dimensional linear subspaces; its topology can be defined by identifying each plane W with its orthogonal projection matrix PW​. Local graph charts identify nearby planes with graphs of maps W0​→W0⊥​.
The tautological bundle is Etaut​={(W,v):v∈W}→Grk​(Rn). In graph charts a basis of W0​ gives a continuously varying basis of each graph, proving local triviality. The universal quotient bundle has fiber QW​=Rn/W. The map [v]↦(I−PW​)v identifies it continuously with the orthogonal-complement bundle W⊥, whose graph charts similarly supply local frames. Thus Qtaut​ is a rank-(n−k) quotient vector bundle.
For the given E→M, at each m choose a basis of Em​ and global sections of a vector bundle realizing its basis vectors, using the evaluation-surjectivity assumption. Those d sections remain independent on a neighbourhood of m, since the corresponding determinant in a local trivialization is nonzero at m and continuous. Compactness gives a finite cover by such neighbourhoods. Collect all the selected sections as s1​,…,sN​; they span every fiber. They define a fiberwise surjective vector bundle morphism
F:M×RN⟶E,Fm​(c1​,…,cN​)=∑j=1N​cj​sj​(m).
(28)
Its kernels have constant dimension N−d and form the kernel bundle of a surjective vector bundle morphism. More concretely, in a local frame of E the map has a continuous full-row-rank matrix A(m) and its kernel projection is
PkerFm​​=IN​−A(m)T(A(m)A(m)T)−1A(m).
(29)
This varies continuously and is unchanged by an invertible change of target frame. The Grassmannian as projection matrices therefore makes
ϕ(m)=kerFm​∈GrN−d​(RN)
(30)
a continuous map. The fiber quotient map induced by Fm​ is an isomorphism RN/kerFm​→Em​. The same local matrix formula gives continuously varying right inverses, so these fiber isomorphisms give a vector bundle isomorphism. This proves the classification of vector bundles by a universal quotient bundle:
E≅ϕ∗Qtaut​.​
(31)
The displayed equality in the classification statement is understood as bundle isomorphism, not literal equality of total spaces.
Finally, the universal quotient bundle need not have a nowhere-zero section. Take Gr1​(R2)=RP1. Its quotient identifies with W⊥, a Möbius line bundle. Write Wθ​=span(cosθ,sinθ), with θ modulo π. A section of the complement line has the form a(θ)(−sinθ,cosθ), and being single-valued requires a(θ+π)=−a(θ). If a is nonzero anywhere, the intermediate value theorem forces a zero between that point and its translate by π; if it is nowhere nonzero the conclusion is immediate. Hence this rank-one quotient bundle has no nowhere-zero section.
