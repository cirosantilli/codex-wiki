For a rank-r real vector bundle with a fiber metric, the Thom isomorphism theorem says that if the bundle is oriented over the coefficient ring R, there is a Thom class u∈Hr(D(E),S(E);R) restricting to the chosen generator on every fiber pair, and
Hq(B;R) π∗(−)⌣u ​Hq+r(D(E),S(E);R)
(32)
is an isomorphism. For R=F2​, every real vector bundle has the required orientation. The disk bundle retracts to B, so apply the pair long exact sequence and the Thom isomorphism to get the Gysin sequence of a sphere bundle
⋯→Hq−r(B;R)⌣e(E)​Hq(B;R)π∗​Hq(S(E);R)π!​​Hq−r+1(B;R)⌣e(E)​Hq+1(B;R)→⋯.​
(33)
The first map is multiplication by the Euler class of a vector bundle, the pullback to the zero section of the absolute image of u. The connecting map followed by inverse Thom isomorphism gives π!​.
Apply this over F2​ to the tautological real line bundle γ on RPn. Its unit sphere bundle is Sn, with projection sending a unit vector to its line. Put a=e2​(γ)=w1​(γ)∈H1(RPn;F2​), the mod-two Euler class of a real line bundle. For n≥1 the degree-zero pullback H0(RPn)→H0(Sn) is an isomorphism. Exactness therefore makes the subsequent transfer zero, and multiplication by a from H0 into H1 injective. In intermediate degrees the sphere cohomology vanishes, making each successive multiplication by a an isomorphism. At degree n, the map from Hn−1 into Hn is injective; the transfer Hn(Sn)→Hn(RPn) is surjective because Hn+1(RPn)=0. Since Hn(Sn)=F2​, that top group is one-dimensional too, and an is its nonzero generator. The same endpoint reasoning covers n=1. This gives the Gysin proof of mod-two projective-space cohomology and the mod-two cohomology ring of real projective space:
H∗(RPn;F2​)=F2​[a]/(an+1),∣a∣=1.​
(34)
For n=0 this says simply H∗(RP0)=F2​.
For a self-map of RP4, the induced map has f∗a=λa with λ∈F2​, so f∗aj=λjaj. The mod-two alternating trace is
L2​(f)=1+λ+λ2+λ3+λ4=1
(35)
for either value of λ. The alternating trace on homology equals that on the finite cellular chain complex; consequently this is the reduction modulo two of the integral Lefschetz number. That integer is nonzero, and the Lefschetz fixed-point theorem forces a fixed point. Thus every self-map of the real projective four-space has a fixed point, a case of the Lefschetz fixed-point property of even-dimensional real projective space.
For the odd-dimensional example, let J(x1​,x2​,x3​,x4​)=(−x2​,x1​,−x4​,x3​), so J2=−I. Its projectivization is
F([x1​:x2​:x3​:x4​])=[−x2​:x1​:−x4​:x3​].​
(36)
It is well-defined and continuous on RP3. A fixed projective point would have Jv=tv for a nonzero real vector and real t, forcing −v=J2v=t2v and therefore t2=−1, impossible. This is a fixed-point-free complex-structure map on odd-dimensional real projective space.
