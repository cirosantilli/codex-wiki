For singular cochains a∈Cp(X;R) and b∈Cq(X;R), define the cup product on a singular (p+q)-simplex by
(a⌣b)(σ)=a(σ∣[v0​,…,vp​])b(σ∣[vp​,…,vp+q​]).
(9)
The identity δ(a⌣b)=δa⌣b+(−1)pa⌣δb makes this descend to cohomology. The induced product is associative, unital and graded-commutative: ab=(−1)pqba in degrees p,q.
For finite CW complexes, the integral cohomological Künneth theorem for finite cell complexes is the split short exact sequence
0⟶⨁p+q=n​Hp(X;Z)⊗Hq(Y;Z)×​Hn(X×Y;Z)⟶⨁p+q=n+1​Tor1Z​(Hp(X;Z),Hq(Y;Z))⟶0.
(10)
The splitting need not be natural. If the factor cohomology is torsion-free, the cohomological cross product is an isomorphism, with multiplication (a×b)(c×d)=(−1)∣b∣∣c∣ac×bd.
A genus-g closed oriented surface has cellular groups H0=Z, H1=Z2g, H2=Z, and no higher cohomology. Choose the positive orientation class ω and a symplectic basis ai​,bi​ of H1. The cohomology ring of a closed oriented surface is specified by
ai​aj​=bi​bj​=0,ai​bj​=δij​ω,bi​aj​=−δij​ω,ωH>0=0,​
(11)
with unit 1∈H0. These products follow by representing their Poincare duality classes by oriented handle curves: the ith meridian meets the ith longitude once positively, curves on distinct handles have zero intersection, and swapping two oriented curves reverses the intersection sign. In general, for closed oriented n-manifolds and transverse oriented submanifolds of codimensions p,q, the cup product of their Poincare-dual classes is the dual class of their oriented intersection. Complementary-dimensional intersections give the signed point count ⟨αβ,[M]⟩.
For Σ2​, the four curves A1​,B1​,A2​,B2​ give a1​b1​=a2​b2​=ω and all other pairings zero apart from their negatives in reversed order. Thus the degree-one intersection pairing on an oriented surface is two symplectic blocks, and the point class is dual to ω.
For Σ2​×Σ2​, let ai​,bi​,ω1​ come from the first factor and cj​,dj​,ω2​ from the second. The Künneth theorem gives their graded tensor-product ring, whose additive ranks in degrees 0,1,2,3,4 are
(1,8,18,8,1).​
(12)
The top orientation class is Ω=ω1​ω2​. The two fiber surfaces {p}×Σ2​ and Σ2​×{q} have dual classes ω1​,ω2​, intersect once positively and have zero self-intersection. The other sixteen degree-two classes are products of degree-one classes and are dual, up to the chosen orientation convention, to products of handle curves. Their intersections are encoded by
ω12​=ω22​=0,(ai​cj​)(bk​dl​)=−δik​δjl​Ω,(ai​dj​)(bk​cl​)=δik​δjl​Ω.
(13)
The minus sign in the first mixed formula comes from commuting cj​ past bk​. These classes together with the fiber classes give the intersection form of a product of two closed oriented surfaces, here nine hyperbolic blocks. Complementary degrees one and three are similarly paired, for example ai​(bk​ω2​)=δik​Ω; geometrically this pairs a three-dimensional product submanifold with a transverse loop. This exhibits the full duality, not just the additive groups.
The Lefschetz fixed-point theorem says that a self-map of a compact polyhedron with nonzero Lefschetz number has a fixed point, where L(f)=∑q​(−1)qtr(f∗​:Hq​(−;Q)→Hq​(−;Q)). A map homotopic to the identity has L(f)=χ, and Euler characteristic of a product gives
0=L(f)=(2−2g1​)(2−2g2​).
(14)
Consequently
g1​=1 or g2​=1.​
(15)
The product g1​g2​ itself is not determined by the printed assumptions. For every h≥0, translation by a nonzero torus point, times the identity on Σh​, is a fixed-point-free map of Σ1​×Σh​ homotopic to the identity, and its genus product is h. Thus the exact conclusion is the criterion for fixed-point-free self-maps homotopic to the identity on products of surfaces, rather than a single value of g1​g2​.
