The integral Poincare duality theorem says that a closed connected oriented n-manifold has a fundamental class [M]∈Hn​(M;Z) for which cap product gives Hq(M;Z)≅Hn−q​(M;Z). The general Poincare duality with the orientation local system uses [M]∈Hn​(M;OM​) and gives
Hq(M;A) ⌢[M] ​Hn−q​(M;OM​⊗A)
(16)
for a local coefficient system A. Over F2​ it holds without orientability. For constant integral coefficients on a nonorientable connected closed manifold, Hn​=H0(M;OM​)=0, while Hn=H0​(M;OM​)=Z/2: the invariants of sign reversal in Z are zero and its coinvariants are Z/(a∼−a).
If the three-manifold is orientable, duality and the universal coefficient theorem for cohomology give
H2​(M;Z)≅H1(M;Z)≅Hom(H1​(M;Z),Z)≅Zb​.
(17)
For a nonorientable three-manifold, H3​=0 and H3=Z/2. The top-degree universal coefficient sequence therefore gives
ExtZ1​(H2​(M;Z),Z)≅Z/2.
(18)
Since the homology groups are finitely generated, their cyclic decomposition shows that this Ext group is isomorphic to the torsion subgroup of H2​. Thus the torsion is exactly Z/2, not the possibly larger torsion subgroup of H1​. Also odd-dimensional closed manifolds have zero Euler characteristic, by pairing complementary mod-two Betti numbers. If r is the free rank of H2​, it follows that 0=χ(M)=1−b+r, giving the second homology of a closed nonorientable three-manifold
H2​(M;Z)≅Zb−1⊕Z/2,b≥1.​
(19)
The inequality is forced by the nonnegative free rank b−1.
For the final graded-group construction, one always has K0=K3=K6=Z, since each of these is copied from H0 of a connected three-manifold. Suppose it were the cohomology of a closed six-manifold N. The group K0 makes N connected, and K6=Z makes it orientable: the nonorientable alternative has top integral cohomology Z/2, as above. Its third rational cohomology would then be one-dimensional. But the middle Poincare duality pairing on H3(N;Q) is nondegenerate and skew-symmetric by graded commutativity, hence must have even dimension. Equivalently, a generator u would satisfy u⌣u=−u⌣u, so its square in the torsion-free top group is zero and the entire rank-one pairing vanishes. This contradicts duality and the even rank of middle cohomology in dimension four k plus two. Therefore
K is never the integral cohomology of a closed six-manifold.​
(20)
Both existence questions have negative answers, independently of the other groups of the original three-manifold.
