Define orthonormal symmetric vectors
∣s0​⟩=∣0⟩,∣sj​⟩=2​∣−j​⟩+∣j​⟩​(1≤j≤N).
(24)
The symmetric-arm reduction of an exchange Hamiltonian here has two arms. To verify it directly, the link between radial distances j and j+1 has strength Jj​ on either side, while the two central links have strength J0​/2​. The calculation of part (a) therefore gives
He​∣s0​⟩He​∣sj​⟩He​∣sN​⟩​=J0​∣s1​⟩,=Jj−1​∣sj−1​⟩+Jj​∣sj+1​⟩(1≤j<N),=JN−1​∣sN−1​⟩.​
(25)
In particular this subspace is invariant and contains the initial vector. Define the linear isometry F∣j⟩=∣sj​⟩. The displayed matrix entries prove He​F=FHT​, or F†He​F=HT​. Induction gives Hem​F=FHTm​ for every nonnegative integer m; hence the convergent exponential series proves e−iHe​tF=Fe−iHT​t. This establishes the mapping for the entire evolution, rather than just its first two powers. The supplied endpoint transfer also forces every Jj​ to be nonzero, since a broken link would disconnect the two endpoints. Successive powers applied to ∣s0​⟩ then generate all ∣sj​⟩, so this is exactly the cyclic subspace governing the initial state.
Using the stated perfect quantum state transfer identity at time π/2,
e−iHe​π/2∣0⟩=F∣N⟩=2​∣−N​⟩+∣N⟩​.​
(26)
The two terms have the excitation at sites 0 and 2N, respectively, and all intermediate qubits are zero in both terms. The full state thus factors into a Bell state at the endpoints and the vacuum on the interior. Its partial trace is
ρ0,2N​=∣Ψ+⟩⟨Ψ+∣,∣Ψ+⟩=2​∣10⟩+∣01⟩​.​
(27)
The coherent cross terms survive the trace because the interior vectors coincide. We have used the transfer phase convention supplied in the question; a common transfer phase would leave this density operator unchanged.
