Write ε=εa​ta​ and Aμ​=Aμa​ta​. The proposed gauge-field transformation law becomes δAμ​=−∂μ​ε+[ε,Aμ​]. Consequently
δ(Dμ​ϕ)=ε∂μ​ϕ+(∂μ​ε)ϕ+δAμ​ϕ+Aμ​εϕ=εDμ​ϕ.
(11)
Both ϕ and its gauge covariant derivative therefore transform as the same multiplet, with no derivative of the local parameter left over. The original algebraic invariance of the Lagrangian density now applies pointwise to L(ϕ,Dϕ), so this constructs the local symmetry.
Also transform J by δJ=εJ. Antisymmetry gives (ta​J)⋅ϕ+J⋅(ta​ϕ)=0, so the source coupling is invariant. Let SAμa​​ denote the functional derivative at a point. Vary the action functional and integrate the derivative of εa​ by parts, taking the local parameter to have compact support:
δS=∫ddxεa​[(ta​ϕ)⋅Sϕ​+(ta​J)⋅SJ​+∂μ​SAμa​​+fabc​Aμb​SAμc​​].
(12)
Since every εa​(x) is arbitrary, its coefficient vanishes. This proves the requested local identity, including the derivative sign and the order of the structure-constant indices.
For the generating functional, the integral of the first term is zero as a change-of-variable identity:
∫Dϕ(ta​ϕ)⋅δϕδ​eiS=0.
(13)
The divergence of this vector field in field space is proportional to trta​=0. Thus an invariant regulated functional measure, with no gauge anomaly, leaves no Jacobian term. Inserting the classical identity into the integral gives
[(ta​J)⋅δJδ​+∂μ​δAμa​δ​+fabc​Aμb​δAμc​δ​]Z[J,A]=0.​
(14)
The same first-order differential operator annihilates the connected generating functional W, because Z=eiW. Its source term is (ta​J)⋅φ.
For the specified Legendre transform, vary Γ=−W+∫Jφ. Since WJ​=φ, the terms involving δJ cancel, leaving
δΓ=∫J⋅δφ−∫WA​δA,Γφ​=J,ΓA​∣φ​=−WA​∣J​.
(15)
Antisymmetry also gives (ta​J)⋅φ=−J⋅(ta​φ). Substitution in the identity for W, followed by multiplication by −1, proves the background gauge invariance of a scalar effective action:
[(ta​φ)⋅δφδ​+∂μ​δAμa​δ​+fabc​Aμb​δAμc​δ​]Γ=0.​
(16)
The derivatives of the quantum effective action are proper, amputated one-particle-irreducible vertices. At each loop order they receive contributions from one-particle-irreducible Feynman diagrams with the indicated external scalar legs, including tree vertices and the required counterterms. Diagrams which disconnect after cutting one internal line are not additional proper-vertex contributions. Translation invariance supplies the overall momentum-conserving delta function factored out in the definition. The signs of these vertices must follow the chosen overall convention for Γ.
Differentiate the effective-action identity twice with respect to φi​(y) and φj​(z) and set φ=A=0. In an invariant zero-field vacuum this gives the Ward identity contact terms
∂μ​Γa,ijμ​(x;y,z)+(ta​)ki​δ(x−y)Γkj(2)​(x,z)+(ta​)kj​δ(x−z)Γki(2)​(x,y)=0.
(17)
The term proportional to A vanishes. With the printed Fourier factors eipr​xr​, integration by parts turns ∂μ​ into −ip1μ​. The first delta function combines p1​ with p2​, and the second combines p1​ with p3​. Using (ta​)ki​=−(ta​)ik​ and symmetry of the scalar two-point kernel then yields
p1μ​τa,ijμ​=i(ta​)ik​τkj​(p1​+p2​,p3​)−τik​(p2​,p3​+p1​)i(ta​)kj​.​
(18)
This derivation fixes the relative signs independently of any vertex rule.
There is an overall-sign inconsistency in the final printed example. At tree level, stationarity of the source integral gives W=S0​[φ,A]+∫Jφ, hence the specified transform gives Γtree​=−S0​. For the free scalar multiplet this implies, after factoring out the delta function,
τij​(p,−p)=(p2+m2)δij​,τa,ijμ​=+i(p2​−p3​)μ(ta​)ij​.​
(19)
The mixed result follows by expanding −S0​ to first order in A: its contribution is ∫Aμa​(∂μφi​)(ta​)ij​φj​. The two scalar derivatives yield ip2μ​(ta​)ij​+ip3μ​(ta​)ji​. Momentum conservation gives
p1​⋅(p2​−p3​)=p32​−p22​,
(20)
so both sides of the Ward identity equal i(p32​−p22​)(ta​)ij​. Because the scalar theory is Gaussian for prescribed A, its fluctuation determinant depends on A but not on φ; it cannot add further two-scalar vertices. Thus this quadratic verification is sufficient, not merely the first term of an omitted scalar-loop correction.
The printed mixed vertex instead has a minus sign. It satisfies the same Ward identity if one uses the opposite effective-action convention Γalt​=W−∫Jφ=S0​ at tree level, because its two-point vertex is then −(p2+m2)δij​ as well. Either overall convention works, but the printed Legendre transform and printed mixed vertex cannot both be used unchanged. For example, with p22​=p32​ and a nonzero generator, they give opposite sides of the claimed identity.
