The exponents are real, as required for this family of real continuous functions. We prove the exponential Chebyshev system property by induction on the number of functions. A single nonzero multiple of an exponential function has no zero, so the base case holds.
Suppose the result holds for n distinct real exponents, and consider a nontrivial combination F(x)=∑j=0n​cj​eλj​x. If it had n+1 distinct zeros, so would
g(x)=e−λ0​xF(x)=c0​+∑j=1n​cj​e(λj​−λ0​)x,
(2)
since the prefactor is everywhere positive. Applying Rolle's theorem between consecutive zeros gives at least n distinct zeros of
g′(x)=∑j=1n​cj​(λj​−λ0​)e(λj​−λ0​)x.
(3)
The n exponents in this derivative are distinct. By induction, a nontrivial combination of them has at most n−1 zeros. Therefore every coefficient cj​(λj​−λ0​) must be zero. Distinctness gives cj​=0 for j≥1. Then g=c0​ has a zero only if c0​=0, contradicting the original nontriviality. Consequently F has at most n zeros, and
(eλ0​x,…,eλn​x) is a Chebyshev system on [a,b].​
(4)
