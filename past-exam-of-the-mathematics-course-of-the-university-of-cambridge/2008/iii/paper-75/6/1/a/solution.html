Past exam of the mathematics course of the University of Cambridge / 2008 / iii / Paper 75 / 6 / 1 / a / Solution

Write r=x−u∗. If u∗ is a best approximation, then for every v∈Un​ and real t, the competitor u∗+tv satisfies
∥r−tv∥2−∥r∥2=−2tRe(r,v)+t2∥v∥2≥0.
(35)
The linear coefficient must be zero, since otherwise choosing sufficiently small t of the appropriate sign makes this expression negative. Hence Re(r,v)=0. In a real inner product space this is the desired orthogonality; in a complex inner product space, apply the same argument to iv to show that the imaginary part also vanishes. Thus (r,v)=0 for all v∈Un​.
Conversely, suppose this orthogonality holds. Every competitor u∈Un​ has u−u∗∈Un​. The Pythagorean identity then gives
∥x−u∥2=∥r−(u−u∗)∥2=∥r∥2+∥u−u∗∥2≥∥r∥2.
(36)
Equality forces u=u∗. We have proved the characterization, as well as uniqueness:
u∗ is best⟺(x−u∗,v)=0 for every v∈Un​.​
(37)
This is the orthogonal projection onto a finite-dimensional subspace. Completeness of the ambient space is not needed. Existence follows, for example, by solving the invertible Gram matrix system in a basis of the finite-dimensional linear subspace.
