Let p be the best uniform approximation to the exponential function from the degree-at-most-n−1 polynomial space. Suppose that En−1​(ex)=En​(ex). Then the same p is best in the larger space. Its error g(x)=ex−p(x) is nonzero, since the exponential is not a polynomial. The Chebyshev alternation theorem supplies n+2 ordered points where g has alternating signs. By the intermediate value theorem, g has at least one zero in each of the n+1 intervening intervals, hence at least n+1 distinct zeros.
Applying Rolle's theorem successively n times forces a zero of g(n). But degp≤n−1, so
g(n)(x)=ex>0
(15)
everywhere. This is a contradiction. Together with the automatic non-increase of best errors, it proves
En−1​(ex)>En​(ex),n≥1.​
(16)
The argument is an instance of strict polynomial error decrease from nonvanishing derivatives.
