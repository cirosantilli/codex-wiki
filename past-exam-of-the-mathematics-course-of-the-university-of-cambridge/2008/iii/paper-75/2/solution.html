The Korovkin theorem states that if positive linear operators on continuous functions Ln​:C[0,1]→C[0,1] satisfy
∥Ln​ej​−ej​∥∞​⟶0,ej​(t)=tj,j=0,1,2,
(7)
then ∥Ln​f−f∥∞​→0 for every real continuous function f.
Let r=k−1≥2 and hn​=∣Δn​∣. For the B-splines in this question, Ni,n​≥0 and their sum is one on the basic interval. Thus the Schoenberg spline operator Vn​ is linear and positive. To verify the three test functions, suppress the index n and write the Marsden identity with ωi​(x)=∏j=1r​(x−ti+j​). Comparing the coefficients of xr,xr−1,xr−2 gives the monomial B-spline coefficients
1=∑i​Ni​(t),t=∑i​ξi​Ni​(t),t2=∑i​qi​Ni​(t),
(8)
where
ξi​=r1​∑j=1r​ti+j​,qi​=(2r​)1​∑1≤j<ℓ≤r​ti+j​ti+ℓ​.
(9)
The first coefficient is the partition of unity; the second is the Greville abscissa. Every internal knot and the sample point τi​ lie in [ti​,ti+k​], of width at most khn​. Consequently ∣τi​−ξi​∣≤khn​. Since all these numbers lie in [0,1], for any two internal knots u,v we also have
∣τi2​−uv∣≤∣τi​∣∣τi​−u∣+∣u∣∣τi​−v∣≤2khn​.
(10)
Averaging gives ∣τi2​−qi​∣≤2khn​. Nonnegative weights of sum one now yield
∥Vn​1−1∥∞​=0,∥Vn​e1​−e1​∥∞​≤khn​,∥Vn​e2​−e2​∥∞​≤2khn​.
(11)
Here the spline order k is fixed, so all three errors tend to zero. The Korovkin theorem therefore proves
∥Vn​f−f∥∞​⟶0for every f∈C[0,1].​
(12)
No special selection such as the Greville abscissae is required for the sample points. In fact the local-support error bound for a Schoenberg spline operator also gives ∥Vn​f−f∥∞​≤ω(f,khn​). Under the usual continuous-spline convention the operators have the codomain required by the stated theorem. If the permissive knot notation is taken to allow full interior multiplicities and hence discontinuous splines, the same conclusion in the uniform norm still follows from this direct bound, although each approximant need not then be continuous.
