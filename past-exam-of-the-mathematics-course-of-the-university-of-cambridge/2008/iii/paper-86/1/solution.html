Work in the laboratory frame, with fluid at rest at infinity and the instantaneous sphere centre at the origin. In the Unscaled Papkovich–Neuber representation, take the harmonic potentials
A=−4r3a​V,ϕ=4r3a3​(V⋅x),r=∣x∣.
(1)
Differentiating the two terms gives the translating sphere in Stokes flow:
u=4r3a​(I+nn)V+4r3a3​(I−3nn)V,p−p∞​=2r23μa​V⋅n.​
(2)
Here n=x/r. The potentials are harmonic outside the sphere, so they satisfy the Stokes equations. At r=a, the two coefficients of nn cancel and the coefficient of I is one, proving the no-slip boundary condition u=V; the field decays at infinity. Its leading part is a Stokeslet of force 6πμaV exerted on the fluid.
Let z point upwards, δ=V1​−V2​, and D=a2​V2​−a1​V1​. Each isolated sphere moves with velocity −Vi​z. To leading order its correction is the other's incident Stokeslet velocity:
x˙1​=−V1​z−4r3a2​V2​​(I+nn)z,x˙2​=−V2​z−4r3a1​V1​​(I+nn)z.
(3)
The forces generating these fields are the fixed sedimenting loads, so their leading strengths are set by the isolated speeds. Subtraction yields the leading interaction of two sedimenting spheres:
r˙=−δz−4r3D​(I+nn)z.​
(4)
Since z=cosθr−sinθθ, this reduces to
r˙=−(δ+2r3D​)cosθ,rθ˙=(δ+4r3D​)sinθ.​
(5)
The stipulated higher-order errors can be appended to both equations; they do not enter this leading calculation.
For the same-sign case, interchange labels if necessary to arrange δ>0,D>0, and put c=3D/4. The vertical separation z=rcosθ satisfies
z˙=−δ−rc​(1+cos2θ)≤−δ.
(6)
If sphere one is initially above sphere two, its vertical order reverses; in every case z→−∞, implying r→∞. For a noncollinear trajectory there is no collision in the point-force equations. Indeed, divide the two polar equations and integrate:
r(δr+2c)δr+c​dr=−cotθdθ,r(δr+2c)sin2θ=C>0.​
(7)
At side-by-side passage, θ=π/2, the closest separation is the positive root of δr2+2cr=C. Before passage r˙<0 and after passage r˙>0, proving overtaking and unbounded subsequent separation. This passing invariant for unequal point-force spheres describes a physical far-field passage only when the closest separation also greatly exceeds the sphere radii. Exactly collinear approach instead reaches the near-contact regime, where this leading approximation cannot describe two finite spheres passing through one another.
For opposite signs, choose δ>0,D<0 and consider the invariant configuration with sphere one vertically above sphere two, θ=0. The radial equation is
r˙=−δ+2r3∣D∣​=δ(rr∗​​−1),r∗​=−2δ3D​.​
(8)
If r>r∗​, separation decreases; if r<r∗​, it increases. Thus it approaches this equilibrium along the vertical line. Linearization gives δr˙=−(δ/r∗​)δr. But a small tilt obeys θ˙=(δ/(2r∗​))θ, so the vertical bound pair in the point-force sedimentation model is stable only against vertical perturbations and is a saddle in the full separation dynamics. The opposite vertical orientation has the reversed stability. Reversing gravity reverses both δ and D, leaves r∗​ unchanged, and reverses every trajectory; radial attraction becomes repulsion. This is fully consistent with kinematic reversibility of Stokes flow.
There is an important restriction in the printed data. Its hierarchy ∣D∣≪(a1​+a2​)∣δ∣ implies r∗​≪a1​+a2​, so the proposed equilibrium would overlap the physical spheres and lie outside the well-separated approximation. With that hierarchy there is no justified physical far-field bound pair. The intended far-field equilibrium instead requires the first inequality reversed, ∣D∣≫(a1​+a2​)∣δ∣. The formal equilibrium and its vertical stability above show the intended mechanism, while also identifying the missing validity and transverse-stability qualifications.
