We construct an infinite residually finite torsion group using the Golod-Shafarevich infinitude criterion for pro-p groups. Its useful form says that a pro-p presentation on d generators with relators ri​ gives an infinite pro-p group whenever
1−dτ+∑i​τD(ri​)<0for some 0<τ<1.
(32)
Here D is the Magnus degree of a free pro-p group element: map each generator to 1+Xj​ in a noncommutative power-series algebra over Fp​, and take the lowest degree of w−1. The criterion permits infinitely many relators with a convergent weight sum. It is a consequence of the Golod-Shafarevich inequality for the completed group algebra; see Ershov's survey, Theorem 3.1.
Take p=2, d=2, and τ=3/4. Let F be the free pro-p group on a,b, and enumerate all ordinary finite words w1​,w2​,… in these generators and their inverses. Choose integers ei​ sufficiently large that
τ2ei​<2−i−2.
(33)
Form the quotient pro-p group
P=F/⟨⟨wi2ei​​:i≥1⟩⟩​,
(34)
where the bar means the closed normal closure. If the Magnus image of wi​ is 1+vi​, characteristic two gives
(1+vi​)2ei​−1=vi2ei​​,D(wi2ei​​)≥2ei​D(wi​)≥2ei​
(35)
for nonidentity wi​; identity relators contribute zero weight. It follows that
1−2τ+∑i​τD(wi2ei​​)≤1−23​+∑i≥1​2−i−2=−41​<0.
(36)
Thus the Golod-Shafarevich infinitude criterion for pro-p groups proves that P is infinite.
Let G=⟨aˉ,bˉ⟩ be the abstract subgroup generated by the two images inside P. This subgroup is dense because the two images topologically generate the quotient, and it is infinite: a finite subgroup of a Hausdorff profinite group is closed, so could not be dense in infinite P. Every element of G is the image of some ordinary word wi​, and the imposed relation makes its order divide 2ei​. Therefore G is a two-generated torsion group. Finally P is an inverse limit of finite 2-groups. Any nonidentity element of G is nonidentity in P and hence has a nonidentity image in one of those finite groups. Restricting that map to G proves that it is a residually finite group. We have obtained
G is finitely generated, infinite, residually finite, and every element has finite order.​
(37)
The abstract subgroup G is essential here: the argument establishes finite order for ordinary words, not for every topological limit in the ambient pro-p group.
There is no surjective homomorphism from this group to an infinite soluble group. In fact finitely generated soluble torsion groups are finite. To prove this, let S be finitely generated, soluble and torsion, and induct on its derived length. In the abelian case, finitely many generators of finite orders generate a finite group. In general the abelianization S/S′ is a finitely generated abelian torsion group, hence finite. Its kernel S′ therefore has finite index and is finitely generated by Schreier's lemma. Explicitly, for a finite coset transversal T containing 1 and a finite symmetric generating set X, the finitely many elements txtx−1 generate the finite-index subgroup, where the bar denotes the chosen representative of the coset. Now S′ is torsion and has smaller derived length, so induction makes it finite. Hence S is finite as well. Every quotient of G is finitely generated and torsion, so any soluble quotient must be finite.
