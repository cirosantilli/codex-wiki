Use geometrized units G=c=1. For a stationary asymptotically flat spacetime, normalize the timelike Killing vector field ξ to have squared norm −1 at infinity. The Komar mass is
MK​=−8π1​limS→∞​∫S​∇aξbdSab​.
(1)
Here the oriented binormal is chosen so that an ordinary positive-mass static solution gives a positive integral. Equivalently, on a static slice with lapse N=−ξ2​ and outward spatial unit normal si,
MK​=4π1​limS→∞​∫S​siDi​NdA.
(2)
This second expression fixes the orientation unambiguously and is convenient for the Majumdar–Papapetrou solution.
The spatial metric is γij​=H2δij​ and N=H−1. On a large coordinate sphere, si=H−1ri and dA=H2r2dΩ. Thus
siDi​NdA=−Hr2​∂r​HdΩ,MK​=−4π1​limr→∞​∫S2​Hr2∂r​H​dΩ.
(3)
The original PDF has the Euclidean norm in each source denominator, which is missing in the TeX aid. With that norm, the harmonic function has asymptotics
H=1+r∑i​Mi​​+O(r−2),∂r​H=−r2∑i​Mi​​+O(r−3).
(4)
Therefore
MK​=i=1∑N​Mi​.​
(5)
If Mi​ are retained as length parameters while G is restored, the physical mass is ∑i​Mi​/G. This is the charge at infinity. A Komar integral is not generally surface-independent here, since Einstein-Maxwell theory has the electromagnetic stress-energy tensor between the surfaces; in particular it should not be confused with a sum of vacuum horizon Komar charges.
