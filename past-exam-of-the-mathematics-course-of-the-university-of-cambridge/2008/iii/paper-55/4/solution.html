For the adjoint covariant derivative Di​=∂i​+[Ai​,⋅], the gauge curvature is
F=dA+A∧A,Fij​=∂i​Aj​−∂j​Ai​+[Ai​,Aj​].​
(30)
The matrix-valued wedge product gives one commutator in each dxi∧dxj coefficient; [Di​,Dj​]Φ=[Fij​,Φ] follows directly. The given trace defines a real positive inner product on the anti-Hermitian algebra su(2).
The additional terms in ⟨Dj​Φ,Ψ⟩+⟨Φ,Dj​Ψ⟩, compared with ordinary differentiation, are
−21​tr([Aj​,Φ]Ψ+Φ[Aj​,Ψ])=−21​tr[Aj​,ΦΨ]=0.
(31)
Thus trace cyclicity proves the compatibility identity
∂j​⟨Φ,Ψ⟩=⟨Dj​Φ,Ψ⟩+⟨Φ,Dj​Ψ⟩.​
(32)
With compactly supported variations, this gives covariant integration by parts without boundary contributions.
Use Fij​=ϵijk​Bk​, so ∣B∣2=21​∑i,j​∣Fij​∣2. Write the independent variations as δAi​=ai​ and δΦ=χ. The curvature and Higgs derivative variations are
δFij​=Di​aj​−Dj​ai​,δ(Di​Φ)=Di​χ+[ai​,Φ].
(33)
The variation of the Yang-Mills theory magnetic energy is
δ∫∣B∣2d3x=2∫∑i,j​⟨Fij​,Di​aj​⟩d3x=−2∫∑i,j​⟨Di​Fij​,aj​⟩d3x.
(34)
For the Higgs field derivative energy, trace cyclicity also gives ⟨X,[a,Φ]⟩=⟨[Φ,X],a⟩. Hence
δ∫∣DΦ∣2d3x=−2∫⟨∑i​Di​Di​Φ,χ⟩d3x+2∫∑i​⟨[Φ,Di​Φ],ai​⟩d3x.
(35)
Arbitrariness of the variations proves the Euler-Lagrange field equations
i∑​Di​Di​Φ=0,i∑​Di​Fij​=[Φ,Dj​Φ](j=1,2,3).​
(36)
The gauge equation can equivalently be written (D×B)j​+[Φ,Dj​Φ]=0, where (D×B)j​=ϵjik​Di​Bk​. This equivalent form also checks the sign of the commutator source.
Now suppose the positive-sign Bogomolny equations Bi​=Di​Φ hold. The gauge-theory Bianchi identity Di​Fjk​+Dj​Fki​+Dk​Fij​=0 implies ∑i​Di​Bi​=0, and therefore ∑i​Di​Di​Φ=0. For the gauge equation,
(D×B)i​=ϵijk​Dj​Dk​Φ=21​ϵijk​[Dj​,Dk​]Φ=21​ϵijk​[Fjk​,Φ]=[Bi​,Φ].
(37)
Consequently (D×B)i​+[Φ,Di​Φ]=[Bi​,Φ]+[Φ,Bi​]=0. Thus the first-order Bogomolny equations imply both second-order field equations. The negative-sign convention works as well, with both occurrences of that sign tracked consistently.
Finally differentiate the norm twice, using the trace compatibility identity at each step:
Δ∣Φ∣2=2∑i​∣Di​Φ∣2+2⟨Φ,∑i​Di​Di​Φ⟩.
(38)
The second term vanishes, and ∣B∣2=∣DΦ∣2 for the Bogomolny equations. Thus the Higgs norm identity for a Bogomolny monopole gives
Δ∣Φ∣2=2∣DΦ∣2=∣B∣2+∣DΦ∣2=v(A,Φ).​
(39)
There is no extra factor 2 multiplying v: the printed energy density already contains both equal terms without an overall 1/2.
