Let T=21​∫∣∇ϕ∣2dnx and W=∫U(ϕ)dnx. For a sufficiently regular finite-energy field configuration with vacuum asymptotics, the scaled field ϕλ​(x)=ϕ(λx) stays in the same boundary sector. Changing variables gives the Derrick scaling formula
V(ϕλ​)=λ2−nT+λ−nW.
(21)
A static solution must be stationary under this variation, so the Derrick virial identity is
(n−2)T+nW=0.​
(22)
For n>2, both terms are nonnegative, so both vanish and the field is constant at a vacuum. Hence there are no nonconstant smooth finite-energy static solitons of this scalar type for n>2. For n=1 the identity is T=W, allowing kinks. For n=2 it requires W=0: a nonzero potential contribution excludes a static soliton, but the scaling identity alone does not exclude a purely derivative energy.
There is a useful refinement specific to the unconstrained flat target Rl. If U is differentiable and nonnegative, W=0 implies U(ϕ(x))=0 and ∇ϕ​U=0 on the image. The static Euler-Lagrange field equations then give Δϕ=0. Each first derivative is an entire harmonic function in L2 space. Its mean-value bound over a disk of radius R is at most its total squared norm divided by πR2; letting R→∞ shows that derivative is zero. Thus even the exceptional two-dimensional case has only constant smooth finite-energy solutions into this flat target. This two-dimensional flat-target Derrick obstruction uses the field equations in addition to the scale identity.
The Yang-Mills theory with a Higgs field escapes the original scaling because the gauge curvature is quadratic in spatial scaling. In three dimensions use Aiλ​(x)=λAi​(λx) and Φλ(x)=Φ(λx). Then Fijλ​=λ2Fij​(λx) and Diλ​Φλ=λDi​Φ(λx). Writing the magnetic, Higgs-gradient and Higgs-potential energies as VB​,VD​,VU​, the Derrick scaling of Yang-Mills-Higgs energy gives
Vλ​=λVB​+λ−1VD​+λ−3VU​,VB​=VD​+3VU​
(23)
at stationarity. These terms can balance. In particular, the zero-potential Bogomolny-Prasad-Sommerfield monopole has VB​=VD​=0.
For a nonlinear sigma model in two dimensions, the harmonic map energy is purely quadratic in first derivatives and is scale invariant: Vλ​=V. Its target is a curved manifold, not the unconstrained Euclidean target of the refinement above. Its equation is the nonlinear harmonic-map equation, not componentwise Δϕ=0. The Derrick theorem therefore gives no obstruction. Nonconstant finite-energy maps into S2, as in the O3 nonlinear sigma model, provide examples with nonzero degree.
For the complex theory, regard ϕ and ϕˉ​ as independent variables in the variation, and write s=∣ϕ∣2, U=U(s). Variation in ϕˉ​ gives
∂ϕˉ​∂L​=−U′(s)ϕ,∂(∂μ​ϕˉ​)∂L​=21​∂μϕ.
(24)
Thus
ϕtt​−Δϕ+2U′(∣ϕ∣2)ϕ=0.​
(25)
The factor 2 follows from the printed 1/2 normalization of the complex kinetic term; equivalently vary its two real components.
A non-topological soliton is a localized finite-energy persistent field solution with no protecting winding or other nontrivial topological sector. In this complex theory a Q-ball has a stationary spatial profile and a rotating phase,
ϕ(t,x)=eiωtf(x),f(x)→0 at infinity,
(26)
with real f. It is not static as a field even though its energy density is time-independent. The global phase symmetry gives the Noether charge
Q=∫Im(ϕˉ​ϕt​)dnx=ωI,I=∫f2dnx.
(27)
The profile equation is −Δf+2U′(f2)f−ω2f=0. Its energy is T+W+ω2I/2. To vary the size at fixed charge one must also vary the frequency, because scaling f at fixed ω would change Q. Eliminating ω=Q/I gives charge-constrained scalar-field scaling:
EQ​[fλ​]=λ2−nT+λ−nW+λn2IQ2​.
(28)
The new positive-power term can balance the other terms for n>1, including n>2, so the static Derrick theorem does not rule out these solutions. Equivalently, at fixed frequency the profile extremizes T+∫[U(f2)−ω2f2/2], whose effective potential need not be nonnegative. This explains both the time dependence and the charge constraint behind the evasion; a complex field by itself is not enough.
For an explicit example take the sextic unique-vacuum Q-ball potential
U(∣ϕ∣2)=∣ϕ∣2−∣ϕ∣4+∣ϕ∣6.​
(29)
It is nonnegative, since U(s)=s[(s−1/2)2+3/4], and its only vacuum is zero. Its small-field mass squared is 2U′(0)=2, while mins>0​2U(s)/s=3/2. It supports localized Q-balls, for example radial ones in three dimensions with 3/2<ω2<2. In that range the profile potential is positive quadratic near zero but negative somewhere away from zero, permitting a localized nontrivial profile. This example uses a conserved phase charge and no vacuum winding.
