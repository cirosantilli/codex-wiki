A credibility estimate combines a risk's own observed experience with a collective or prior mean, in the form Zμ​experience​+(1−Z)μcollective​. The credibility factor Z∈[0,1] is the weight given to that risk's experience. Bayesian credibility starts with a prior distribution for a latent risk parameter, updates it using observations, and estimates the conditional risk mean by its Bayesian posterior expected value under squared-error loss. In conjugate models this posterior mean can have exactly the displayed credibility form.
For the unequal policy exposures, define
M=∑i=1k​mi​,s=∑i=1k​xi​.
(33)
Conditional independence of the binomial distribution observations gives the likelihood, up to a factor not depending on θ,
L(θ)∝∏i=1k​θxi​(1−θ)mi​−xi​=θs(1−θ)M−s.
(34)
Multiplication by the Beta distribution prior therefore gives Beta-binomial conjugacy:
θ∣x1​,…,xk​∼Beta(α+s,β+M−s).​
(35)
Both posterior parameters are positive, since 0≤s≤M and the prior parameters are positive.
The conditional target is μ(θ)=mk+1​θ. To verify the quadratic-loss rule directly, for any proposed estimate d and observed data D,
E[(d−μ(θ))2∣D]=(d−E[μ(θ)∣D])2+Var(μ(θ)∣D).
(36)
The variance term does not depend on d, so the unique Bayes estimator under squared error loss is the posterior mean. Applying the beta mean formula gives
μ​k+1​=mk+1​α+β+Mα+s​.​
(37)
It is also the posterior expected future claim count, by the tower property of conditional expectation.
Let p0​=α/(α+β) be the collective prior claim probability and p​=s/M the observed claims per policy. Then
α+β+Mα+s​=M+α+βM​Ms​+M+α+βα+β​α+βα​.
(38)
Therefore the exact beta-binomial credibility with unequal exposures is
μ​k+1​=Z(mk+1​Ms​)+(1−Z)(mk+1​α+βα​),Z=M+α+βM​.​
(39)
The individual experience must be weighted by exposure: s/M is the policy-weighted frequency, not the unweighted average of xi​/mi​ when the mi​ differ. The factor multiplies next-year expected counts after scaling both component probabilities by mk+1​.
