First recover the induced boundary metric from the boundary distance function. If c(t) is a smooth boundary curve with c(0)=x and c˙(0)=w∈Tx​∂M, the local metric expansion of distance gives
limt→0​t2dgi​​(x,c(t))2​=gi​(w,w).
(23)
One can obtain this expansion in local coordinates from the fact that the metric tends uniformly to its value at x; distances to nearby points have that constant metric's first-order norm. Equality of the two boundary distance functions makes the two quadratic values equal for every boundary-tangent w. Polarization therefore recovers the full bilinear induced metric:
h=g1​∣T∂M​=g2​∣T∂M​.
(24)
This does not yet identify the metrics on normal vectors or mixed tangent-normal pairs.
Let νi​ be the inward unit normal for gi​. The boundary normal coordinates
ci​(x,r)=expxgi​​(rνi​(x)),0≤r<ε,
(25)
are collar neighborhoods for a common sufficiently small ε. The Gauss lemma gives ci∗​gi​=dr2+hi​(r) with hi​(0)=h. Hence the local boundary-fixing map c2​∘c1−1​ already satisfies
g1​=(c2​∘c1−1​)∗g2​on TM∣∂M​,
(26)
because both pulled-back metrics there are dr2+h.
To justify a global diffeomorphism, rather than assume a local collar map extends, interpolate the positive metrics by gs​=(1−s)g1​+sg2​, 0≤s≤1. Their inward unit normals and exponential maps give smoothly varying collars
Cs​(x,r)=expxgs​​(rνs​(x)).
(27)
Compactness of [0,1]×∂M gives a uniform collar size after shrinking ε. On the image of each collar define the time-dependent vector field
Zs​(Cs​(x,r))=∂s​Cs​(x,r).
(28)
Multiply it by a smooth cutoff which is one for small r and zero before the collar's outer edge, and extend it by zero to the rest of M. This gives a smooth global time-dependent vector field. It vanishes on the boundary, because Cs​(x,0)=x for all s. Its flow Ψs​ exists for the full interval and fixes the boundary. For sufficiently small fixed r, the curve s↦Cs​(x,r) solves this flow equation, so uniqueness gives
Ψs​(c1​(x,r))=Cs​(x,r).
(29)
Here C0​=c1​ and C1​=c2​. Thus the final map ψ=Ψ1​ agrees with the local map from the g1​ collar to the g2​ collar near the boundary. In particular Dψ fixes tangential vectors and sends ν1​ to ν2​ there. Since the tangent-normal splittings are orthogonal and the normals have unit length,
ψ∣∂M​=id,g1​=ψ∗g2​ on TM∣∂M​.​
(30)
The construction only needs equality of the induced boundary metrics; the equal-distance hypothesis supplies that equality.
