Take N=NG​(D) and b=BrD​(e). If eM=M, the Nagao module theorem with K=N puts every vertex-D summand of M↓N​ inside bM. In particular its Green correspondence summand M′ satisfies bM′=M′.
Conversely a central idempotent acts as zero or one on the indecomposable module M. If eM=0, apply the same theorem to 1−e. Its Brauer image is 1−b, so (1−b)M′=M′, incompatible with bM′=M′. Thus
eM=M⟺BrD​(e)M′=M′.​
(44)
For the unheaded induction request, write X={D∩gD:g∈/N}. We prove the sharper intersection bound, not just a drop in module vertex size. We use Green correspondence on permutation bimodules and the usual module vertex test for a permutation-module summand: its Brauer quotient of a module at P is nonzero precisely when a module vertex contains a conjugate of P. This test follows by restricting to the p-group P, decomposing the permutation basis into its orbits, and noting that proper-orbit sums are precisely the proper-subgroup transfer terms.
Consider kG as a k[G×D]-module, with (g,d)a=gad−1. It is the transitive permutation module induced from ΔD. Set L=G×D and L0​=N×D; the latter contains NL​(ΔD). Its restriction decomposes as
kG↓L0​​=kN⊕⨁g∈N\G/D, g∈/N​k[NgD].
(45)
Each outside term has stabilizer a conjugate of a subgroup of ΔD. If a diagonal subgroup ΔQ≤ΔD embeds into one of these stabilizers, then Q≤D∩gD for an outside representative. Thus its diagonal module vertices are in the intersection error family.
Every indecomposable L0​-summand of kN has module vertex ΔD. To justify this, its endomorphisms are right multiplications by elements of (kN)D. The coefficient projection BrDN​ on this algebra has nilpotent kernel: every nonsingleton D-orbit sum lies in the kernel of kN→k(N/D), by part 1(b), and that kernel is nilpotent by part 1(a). Hence no nonzero idempotent endomorphism can have zero Brauer image. Its image summand has nonzero Brauer quotient of a module at ΔD, and its module vertex, already contained in a conjugate of ΔD, must be ΔD.
Apply the general Green correspondence from part (a) to L,L0​. If an indecomposable summand of kG has module vertex outside the intersection error family, its correspondent must be a summand of kN, since the outside double-coset modules have only error-family module vertices. It therefore has module vertex ΔD. Consequently every summand of kG not having module vertex ΔD has module vertex conjugate into an outside diagonal intersection. An intersection
ΔD∩(g,d)ΔD
(46)
with (g,d)∈/L0​ projects in its first coordinate into D∩gD with g∈/N; this is the error family required in the question.
Now form the split permutation bimodule
W=(1−e)kGb.
(47)
Its Brauer quotient of a module at ΔD is zero. Indeed kG(ΔD)=kCG​(D), left multiplication by e becomes multiplication by BrD​(e)=b, and right multiplication by b remains multiplication by b. Therefore
W(ΔD)=(1−b)kCG​(D)b=0.
(48)
Here b is central in kCG​(D), so the last product is zero. Every indecomposable bimodule summand of W consequently has module vertex in the outside intersection family, by the preceding paragraph, and has trivial module source since it is a permutation-module summand.
Since V has vertex D, necessarily D≤K. Let S be a module source of V at D. The splitting V∣kK⊗kD​S remains a splitting after applying b, since bV=V. Inducing to G and applying 1−e gives
(1−e)IndKG​V∣W⊗kD​S.
(49)
A trivial-source bimodule summand with module vertex conjugate into ΔQ is a summand of IndΔQG×D​k. Tensoring with S turns this into a module induced to G from Q (with the appropriate conjugate restriction of S). Thus every summand on the right is relatively projective for some Q≤D∩gD, g∈/N. Each Vj​ lies in the left-hand side, and Krull–Schmidt theorem now gives
Dj​≤G​D∩gDfor some g∈G∖NG​(D).​
(50)
This proves Juhász induction refinement. If there are no outside elements, the error family is empty and the complementary module is zero.
