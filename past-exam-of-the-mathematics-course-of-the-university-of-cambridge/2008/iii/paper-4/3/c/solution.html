Let B=kGe have defect group D. Use two vertex-and-source facts, with their relevant content made explicit. Every indecomposable module in B has a module vertex conjugate into D. Also B, as a k[G×G]-module with (g,h)a=gah−1, is an indecomposable permutation-module summand with module vertex ΔD={(d,d):d∈D} and trivial module source. The second fact is the bimodule characterization of a defect group of a block; the first follows by applying its relative-ΔD induction splitting to B⊗kG​M=M.
If D is cyclic, every module source for every module vertex Q≤D occurs among the finitely many indecomposable modules for a cyclic p-group. Each module in the block is a summand of an induction of one of these finitely many module sources. There are finitely many subgroups Q and each induced representation has finitely many indecomposable summands by Krull–Schmidt theorem. Thus cyclic defect implies finite representation type of the block.
For the converse, the restriction B↓D×D​ has the regular kD-bimodule as a direct summand. To see this from module vertices and module sources, its Brauer quotient of a module at ΔD is nonzero. Its summands are permutation modules for the p-group D×D and have module vertices of order at most ∣D∣. A summand detected at ΔD therefore has module vertex ΔD and trivial module source, hence is
IndΔDD×D​k≅kD.
(23)
Here a transitive permutation module for a p-group is indecomposable: it has one-dimensional head of a module over the local group algebra and cannot split into two nonzero modules. This also explains the Brauer-quotient detection used in the argument.
For any kD-module U, tensor that split bimodule inclusion with U. It gives
U∣ResDG​(B⊗kD​U),
(24)
where ∣ denotes being a direct summand. If B has only finitely many indecomposable modules M1​,…,Ms​, each B⊗kD​U is a sum of these. Therefore every indecomposable U must occur among the summands of the finite list Mi​↓D​. Thus kD has finite representation type.
A noncyclic p-group has a quotient Cp​×Cp​: its Frattini quotient has rank at least two, since a one-generator Frattini quotient forces the group itself to be cyclic. Inflation along this quotient embeds all the indecomposable modules of that elementary abelian group, preserving isomorphisms and indecomposability. Part (b) rules out finite type when k is infinite.
The conclusion remains valid if the splitting field k is finite. For completeness, replace the scalar parameter in part (b) by an m×m companion matrix Tf​ of an irreducible polynomial f. On km⊕km use
x=(0I​00​),y=(0Tf​​00​).
(25)
An endomorphism commuting with these has form (AC​0A​) with ATf​=Tf​A. Its endomorphism ring has a square-zero ideal of the C-matrices and quotient k[Tf​]≅k[t]/(f), a field, so it is local and the module is indecomposable. Finite fields have irreducible polynomials of arbitrarily large degrees, giving infinitely many dimensions. This also excludes noncyclic D in that case. Altogether,
B has finite representation type⟺D is cyclic.​
(26)
