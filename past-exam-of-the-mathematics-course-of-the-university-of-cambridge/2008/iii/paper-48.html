Mρσ=41​[γρ,γσ]=21​γργσ−21​ηρσI4​.

Use the PDF's convention in which C multiplies ψ∗, rather than ψˉ​T. Its unitarity converts the given gamma matrix identity into
γμC=−C(γμ)∗.
(12)
Applying it twice gives
MρσC=41​[γρ,γσ]C=C41​[(γρ)∗,(γσ)∗]=C(Mρσ)∗.
(13)
For real infinitesimal Lorentz transformation parameters,
C(I+21​Ωρσ​Mρσ)∗=(I+21​Ωρσ​Mρσ)C.
(14)
Therefore ψc=Cψ∗ transforms in exactly the same spinor representation as ψ. The coordinate pullback is real and transforms identically as well. This is the antilinear charge-conjugation intertwiner relation; exponentiating also gives CS∗=SC for connected transformations.

0=C(−iγμ∗∂μ​−m)ψ∗=(iγμ∂μ​−m)Cψ∗.

(iγμ∂μ​−m)ψc=0.​
(16)
As an explicit check, in the given Weyl representation of the gamma matrices one may take C=iγ2. It is unitary and obeys the required conjugation identity. The free-equation conclusion does not assert that a charge-conjugated field retains the same charge in a fixed electromagnetic background.

Split the free scalar field into its annihilation part ϕ(+) and creation part ϕ(−). Normal ordering moves every creation operator to the left of every annihilation operator, without adding the commutators produced by that rearrangement. For two fields,
:ϕ(x)ϕ(y):=ϕ(−)(x)ϕ(−)(y)+ϕ(−)(x)ϕ(+)(y)+ϕ(−)(y)ϕ(+)(x)+ϕ(+)(x)ϕ(+)(y).
(17)
Its vacuum expectation is zero. Time ordering instead places the field at the later time on the left:
T{ϕ(x)ϕ(y)}=θ(x0−y0)ϕ(x)ϕ(y)+θ(y0−x0)ϕ(y)ϕ(x).
(18)
The equal-time convention is immaterial away from coincident singularities; these expressions are understood as operator-valued distributions.
The only nonzero vacuum contraction uses ap​aq†​. The oscillator commutator therefore gives the Wightman function
W(x−y)=⟨0∣ϕ(x)ϕ(y)∣0⟩=∫(2π)3d3p​2Ep​e−iEp​(x0−y0)+ip⋅(x−y)​.
(19)
Put τ=x0−y0 and r=x−y. Combining the two time orders, and reversing p in the second when necessary, gives
ΔF​(τ,r)=∫(2π)3d3p​2Ep​eip⋅r−iEp​∣τ∣​.
(20)
To recover the four-dimensional Fourier transform, use the Feynman i-epsilon prescription in the energy variable:
∫2πdp0​(p0)2−Ep2​+i0ie−ip0τ​=2Ep​e−iEp​∣τ∣​.
(21)
The positive-energy pole lies just below the real axis, and the negative-energy pole just above it. For τ>0, close below clockwise: the residue at Ep​−i0 is ie−iEp​τ/(2Ep​), and the clockwise factor −i after dividing by 2π gives the required positive coefficient. For τ<0, close above counterclockwise: the residue at −Ep​+i0 is −ieiEp​τ/(2Ep​), and the factor +i gives the other time order. Equivalently the energy contour passes above the positive-energy pole and below the negative-energy pole. Hence
ΔF​(x−y)=ϵ↓0lim​∫(2π)4d4p​p2−μ2+iϵie−ip⋅(x−y)​.​
(22)
The limit is distributional. The scalar Feynman propagator pole prescription is essential; omitting it from the denominator must be accompanied by the specified contour.
Finally, moving the annihilation part of ϕ(x) past the creation part of ϕ(y) gives
ϕ(x)ϕ(y)=:ϕ(x)ϕ(y):+W(x−y)1.
(23)
The normal-ordered two-field product is symmetric in x,y, because the two creation parts commute and the two annihilation parts commute. Applying the two time orders thus proves the two-field scalar Wick identity
T{ϕ(x)ϕ(y)}=:ϕ(x)ϕ(y):+ΔF​(x−y)1.​
(24)

Use the momentum-space Feynman rules with all momenta conserved at each vertex. The free scalar propagator, free Dirac propagator and pseudoscalar Yukawa interaction vertex are respectively
k2−μ2+i0i​,p2−m2+i0i(p+m)​,−iλγ5.​
(25)
The Dirac propagator follows by multiplying the inverse kinetic matrix p−m by p+m, using the Clifford algebra to obtain (p−m)(p+m)=(p2−m2)I4​. The vertex follows by expanding eiSint​ once: there is no factorial because it has one scalar and two distinct spinor field factors. An internal scalar has no spinor indices; an internal fermion line carries the displayed matrix with its momentum directed along fermion flow.
These rules use the coefficient exactly as printed. With the standard Hermitian γ5=iγ0γ1γ2γ3, the Hermiticity of a pseudoscalar Dirac bilinear gives (ψˉ​γ5ψ)†=−ψˉ​γ5ψ. A Hermitian physical interaction therefore requires λ∗=−λ; writing λ=ig with real g changes the vertex to gγ5. This convention issue does not alter the formal rules above, and no unstated real-coupling assumption is needed.

Label the incoming momenta and spin states (p1​,r1​),(p2​,r2​) and the outgoing ones (p3​,r3​),(p4​,r4​), with p1​+p2​=p3​+p4​. Write ui​=uri​(pi​) and vi​=vri​(pi​). The Mandelstam variables are s=(p1​+p2​)2, t=(p1​−p3​)2 and u=(p1​−p4​)2. At lowest order, each connected Feynman diagram has two pseudoscalar Yukawa interaction vertices and one internal scalar propagator.
Define the scattering amplitude by Sficonnected​=i(2π)4δ4(p1​+p2​−p3​−p4​)M. Fix the external Fock state ordering as b1†​b2†​∣0⟩ to b3†​b4†​∣0⟩ for the two-fermion process. The t exchange connects 1 to 3 and 2 to 4; the u exchange connects 1 to 4 and 2 to 3. The two Feynman vertices contribute (−iλ)2, and the internal scalar propagator contributes i divided by its denominator. Exchanging the two identical final fermions supplies a relative fermionic sign. Thus
Mψψ​=−λ2[t−μ2+i0(uˉ3​γ5u1​)(uˉ4​γ5u2​)​−u−μ2+i0(uˉ4​γ5u1​)(uˉ3​γ5u2​)​].​
(26)
This changes sign when the two outgoing labels are exchanged, as identical-fermion antisymmetry requires. There is no s annihilation diagram for two incoming fermions: the interaction preserves Dirac fermion number conservation.
For the fermion-antifermion process take initial state b1†​d2†​∣0⟩ and final state b3†​d4†​∣0⟩. The t exchange uses bilinears (uˉ3​γ5u1​)(vˉ2​γ5v4​), and the s annihilation uses (vˉ2​γ5u1​)(uˉ3​γ5v4​). To fix the sign without a guess, expand the current J=:ψˉ​γ5ψ: in creation and annihilation operators. Its relevant terms have the signs
J⊃b†buˉγ5u−d†dvˉγ5v+dbvˉγ5u+b†d†uˉγ5v.
(27)
The antifermion sign of a normal-ordered bilinear makes the exchange contraction negative, whereas the product of annihilation and creation contractions is positive for the specified external ordering. Multiplying by (−iλ)2i=−iλ2 therefore gives
Mψψˉ​​=λ2[t−μ2+i0(uˉ3​γ5u1​)(vˉ2​γ5v4​)​−s−μ2+i0(vˉ2​γ5u1​)(uˉ3​γ5v4​)​].​
(28)
A different common phase convention for an external state can reverse the overall amplitude, but never the relative sign of its two contributions. Both results are tree scattering with pseudoscalar exchange and are of order λ2.

The Maxwell field has the gauge symmetry Aμ​↦Aμ​+∂μ​α(x) for an arbitrary smooth scalar function α. Commuting the two derivatives shows that Fμν​ is unchanged, so the Maxwell Lagrangian is invariant. Vary the action, use antisymmetry of F and integrate by parts with variations vanishing on the boundary:
δS=−21​∫d4xFμνδFμν​=−∫d4xFμν∂μ​δAν​=∫d4x(∂μ​Fμν)δAν​.
(29)
The Euler-Lagrange field equations are therefore ∂μ​Fμν=0. Expanding F gives
□Aν−∂ν(∂μ​Aμ)=0,□=∂μ​∂μ.
(30)
In Lorenz gauge the second term vanishes, leaving
□Aν​=0.​
(31)
The PDF calls this “Lorentz gauge”; the standard name is Lorenz gauge. A residual gauge symmetry preserves this condition when □α=0.

Put D=∂ρ​Aρ. The gauge-fixing variation is
δ(−21​D2)=−D∂νδAν​.
(32)
After integration by parts its contribution to the Euler-Lagrange field equations is +∂νD. It cancels the −∂νD from the Maxwell Lagrangian, giving □Aν=0 for all four components. This is the Feynman gauge equation; the physical Lorenz gauge condition must still be imposed on states rather than inferred as an additional classical equation from this gauge-fixed density.
Treating the covariant components Aμ​ as coordinates, the derivative of the actual displayed density with respect to ∂0​Aμ​ gives the canonical momenta
πμ=−F0μ−η0μD.​
(33)
In terms of lower-index spatial coordinates,
π0=−A˙0​+∂i​Ai​,πi=A˙i​−∂i​A0​(i=1,2,3).
(34)
The gauge fixing has supplied the nonzero momentum of A0​, absent from the ungauge-fixed Maxwell Lagrangian.
There is a boundary-term convention needed for the next part's printed momentum expansion. Direct expansion gives the Feynman-gauge Maxwell kinetic density after a boundary-term subtraction:
L=L′+∂ρ​Kρ,L′=−21​∂ρ​Aσ​∂ρAσ,Kρ=21​(Aσ​∂σAρ−AρD).

To verify it, the difference of the densities is 21​[(∂ρ​Aσ​)(∂σAρ)−D2]; differentiating Kρ gives these two terms, while the remaining second-derivative terms cancel after renaming indices. Denote the momentum in the printed mode expansion by πν, as it equals −A˙ν for the boundary-subtracted density discussed in part (b). Take a real complete basis of polarization vectors. Its completeness relation with one index raised is
∑λ,λ′​ϵμλ​(p)ϵνλ′(p)ηλλ′=δμ​ν.
(36)
Here raising or lowering the polarization labels uses the same diagonal matrix η, so this is just the completeness relation in the PDF. Write r=x−y. In the mixed field-momentum commutator, the aa† term gives the product of the minus sign in π's creation part and the minus sign in the oscillator commutator. The a†a term gives the same sign. The two mode normalization factors multiply to 1/2, and the momentum delta function removes one integral. Consequently
[Aμ​(x),πν(y)]​=2i​∫(2π)3d3p​λ,λ′∑​ϵμλ​ϵνλ′ηλλ′(eip⋅r+e−ip⋅r)=iδμ​νδ(3)(x−y).​

[Aμ​(x),Aν​(y)]=−ημν​∫(2π)3d3p​2∣p∣eip⋅r−e−ip⋅r​=0,

because the integrand is odd under p↦−p. Similarly,
[πμ(x),πν(y)]=−ημν∫(2π)3d3p​2∣p∣​(eip⋅r−e−ip⋅r)=0.
(39)
These are the requested canonical commutation relations, derived by photon oscillator completeness and canonical brackets.

Since the fields commute at equal times, their mixed brackets with the momenta do not change. The only potentially new momentum bracket is

All other momentum brackets vanish separately. Thus both canonical conventions have [Aμ​,πν]=iδμ​νδ3 and vanishing coordinate-coordinate and momentum-momentum brackets, even though the momentum operators differ by spatial derivatives. This resolves the density/expansion mismatch without changing the printed oscillator algebra.

The oscillator commutator gives
⟨0∣apλ​aqλ′†​∣0⟩=−ηλλ′(2π)3δ(3)(p−q).
(42)
The temporal mode λ=0 has negative norm. More precisely, the nonzero wave packet ∣f,0⟩=∫d3pf(p)ap0†​∣0⟩/(2π)3 has norm −∫d3p∣f(p)∣2/(2π)3<0. The difficulty is an indefinite Hermitian form, not the divergent normalization of an unsmeared momentum eigenstate. The covariant photon Fock space is therefore not a positive physical Hilbert space.
Gupta-Bleuler quantization imposes only the annihilation, or positive-frequency part of a quantum field, of the divergence:
(∂μ​Aμ)(+)∣phys⟩=0.​
(43)
A strong operator identity ∂⋅A=0 is incompatible with the four unconstrained canonical commutation relations; in the literal momentum convention it would set π0=0. The weaker condition instead implies ⟨Φ∣∂⋅A∣Ψ⟩=0 between physical states: the positive-frequency part annihilates the ket, and its adjoint annihilates the bra.
For a nonzero momentum choose upper-index polarization vectors
ϵμ0=(1,0),ϵμ1=(0,e1​),ϵμ2=(0,e2​),ϵμ3=(0,p​),
(44)
where e1​,e2​ are an orthonormal pair perpendicular to p. Labels 1 and 2 have transverse polarization, label 3 has spatial longitudinal polarization, and label 0 has timelike photon polarization. With p0=∣p∣=ω, the divergence of the annihilation part is proportional to −iω(ap0​−ap3​). The physical condition is therefore
(ap0​−ap3​)∣phys⟩=0.
(45)
For a one-mode state ∣χ⟩=∑λ​cλ​aλ†∣0⟩, the condition reads −c0​−c3​=0. Thus
∣χ⟩=c1​a1†∣0⟩+c2​a2†∣0⟩+c0​(a0†−a3†)∣0⟩,⟨χ∣χ⟩=∣c1​∣2+∣c2​∣2.
(46)
The last direction has zero norm and is orthogonal to every constrained state. Its field wavefunction is proportional to ϵμ0+ϵμ3=pμ/ω, hence it is a pure gauge symmetry direction. A purely longitudinal creator a3† alone is not physical, despite having positive norm.
For the multiparticle sketch, put C=a0−a3 and C†=a0†−a3†. The commutator [C,C†]=0 allows polynomials in C† times transverse creator states to obey the constraint. Conversely, on creator polynomials the constraint is −∂a0†​−∂a3†​, so these are all the constrained polynomials. Every term containing C† is orthogonal to every constrained bra, since ⟨Φ∣C†=⟨CΦ∣=0. Quotienting those null vectors and completing the remaining positive space is the Gupta-Bleuler null-state quotient. It gives
two physical transverse photon polarizations, or helicities +1,−1.​
(47)
The one-particle instance is the transverse one-photon physical quotient.

A symmetry of a classical field theory maps solutions to solutions; for the Noether theorem we require a continuous variational symmetry of the action, allowing its density to change by a total divergence. This is stronger than simply observing an accidental symmetry of one solution. Consider first-derivative fields ϕa​ and define
Πaμ​=∂(∂μ​ϕa​)∂L​,Ea​=∂ϕa​∂L​−∂μ​Πaμ​.
(48)
The Euler-Lagrange field equations are Ea​=0. For a fixed-coordinate variation δ0​ϕa​=εQa​, the product rule gives the off-shell identity
εδ0​L​=∑a​Ea​Qa​+∂μ​(∑a​Πaμ​Qa​).
(49)
This identity is the proof's essential integration-by-parts step.
For a general spacetime symmetry write x′μ=xμ+εξμ and ϕa′​(x′)=ϕa​(x)+εΔa​. At a fixed point Qa​=Δa​−ξν∂ν​ϕa​, while the volume-element variation contributes ∂μ​(Lξμ). Suppose action invariance takes the form
εδ0​L​+∂μ​(Lξμ)=∂μ​Kμ.
(50)
Combining with the preceding identity proves
jμ=a∑​Πaμ​Qa​+Lξμ−Kμ,∂μ​jμ=−a∑​Ea​Qa​=0 on shell.​
(51)
This is the Noether current for a spacetime symmetry. It also covers internal symmetries, for which ξ=0. A constant parameter corresponds to one Noether current; several independent parameters give several currents. Integrating the continuity equation gives
dtdQ​=−∫∂Σ​j⋅dS,Q=∫Σ​j0d3x.
(52)
Thus the Noether charge is conserved when the fields have suitable falloff or no flux through the spatial boundary. These are classical identities; quantum symmetries may additionally require absence of an quantum anomaly.
Translations conserve energy and momentum. If the density has no explicit coordinate dependence, take the active fixed-coordinate variation Qa​=∂ν​ϕa​ for translation in direction ν. Then δ0​L/ε=∂ν​L, and the internal-form proof with Kμ=δμν​L gives the canonical energy-momentum tensor
Tμν​=∑a​Πaμ​∂ν​ϕa​−δμν​L,∂μ​Tμν​=0.
(53)
Its four spatial integrals are the conserved energy and momentum. For a real scalar field with density 21​(∂ϕ)2−V(ϕ) this becomes Tμν=∂μϕ∂νϕ−ημνL. Direct differentiation gives ∂μ​Tμν=(□ϕ+V′(ϕ))∂νϕ, which vanishes by the field equation.
Lorentz symmetry conserves angular momentum and boost charges. For that scalar example, Tμν is symmetric, so the Lorentz transformation currents can be written
Jμρσ=xρTμσ−xσTμρ.
(54)
Their divergence is Tρσ−Tσρ=0. Spatial pairs give angular momentum, and time-space pairs give the conserved boost charges. For a spinor field or a vector field, the intrinsic field variation supplies an additional spin current; equivalently the Belinfante-Rosenfeld stress-energy tensor combines spin and orbital pieces into a symmetric stress tensor. The nonunitarity of the finite component Spinor representation of the Lorentz group does not obstruct conservation of these physical spacetime charges.
Global phase symmetry conserves particle charge. For a complex scalar field with density ∂μ​Φ∗∂μΦ−V(Φ∗Φ), the constant transformation Φ↦e−iαΦ leaves the density invariant. Using QΦ​=−iΦ and QΦ∗​=iΦ∗ gives the Noether current
jμ=i(Φ∗∂μΦ−Φ∂μΦ∗).
(55)
For a Dirac field, the corresponding variations Qψ​=−iψ, Qψˉ​​=iψˉ​ yield instead jμ=ψˉ​γμψ, the Dirac current. Its divergence vanishes by the Dirac equation and adjoint Dirac equation. The Yukawa interaction of part 2 also preserves this phase symmetry, because each bilinear has one ψ and one ψˉ​. Thus it preserves fermion number and forbids annihilation of two fermions into a single scalar. These currents express charge or particle-minus-antiparticle-number conservation, rather than conservation of total particle count in arbitrary interacting processes.
A global symmetry in field theory uses a spacetime-independent parameter and ordinarily relates physically distinct configurations. A gauge symmetry instead has arbitrary local parameters and represents redundancy in the description. For example, the Maxwell field is unchanged physically by Aμ​↦Aμ​+∂μ​α(x): its electromagnetic field tensor is unchanged. For charged matter use Dμ​=∂μ​+iqAμ​ together with ψ↦e−iqα(x)ψ. Differentiating shows Dμ′​ψ′=e−iqαDμ​ψ, so the coupled Dirac action is locally gauge invariant. A local phase applied to free matter alone produces extra derivatives of α and is not an invariance; the compensating gauge field is necessary.
Local arbitrariness gives identities among field equations, rather than an independent physical charge for every function α. In the pure Maxwell field, action invariance under a compactly supported α implies
0=∫d4xEν∂ν​α=−∫d4xα∂ν​Eν,Eν=∂μ​Fμν.
(56)
Thus ∂ν​Eν=0 holds off shell, directly because Fμν is antisymmetric. This exemplifies the Noether second theorem. Gauge fixing and the Gupta-Bleuler null-state quotient remove redundant photon components; they do not remove a physical global charge. Gauge transformations that are nontrivial at a boundary can carry boundary charges, so the redundancy statement concerns transformations satisfying the chosen boundary conditions. Finally, discrete symmetries such as the sign reversal of a real scalar or parity symmetry in quantum field theory can be important without furnishing a continuous-parameter Noether current.
