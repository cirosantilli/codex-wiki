We prove the Roth theorem on three-term arithmetic progressions by a density increment on an ordinary integer arithmetic progression. The required increment will be small, but depend only on the current subset density.
First establish the increment step. Suppose A⊆[1,L] has subset density ρ=∣A∣/L>0 and no nonconstant three-term arithmetic progression. Put M=4L+1, regard [1,L] as a subset I of the cyclic group ZM​, and set f=1A​−ρ1I​. Then Ef=0 and ∣f∣≤1. Use the normalized Fourier coefficient on a finite abelian group
g​(r)=Ex∈ZM​​g(x)e−2πirx/M.
(1)
The character orthogonality geometric-series identity Ex​e2πirx/M=1 for r=0 and zero otherwise gives Fourier inversion on a finite group, Parseval identity on a finite group, and
T(g0​,g1​,g2​):=Ex,h​g0​(x)g1​(x+h)g2​(x+2h)=∑r​g​0​(r)g​1​(−2r)g​2​(r).
(2)
Since M is odd, multiplication by two permutes its frequencies. If one argument is f and the other two have absolute value at most one, Cauchy-Schwarz and the Parseval identity on a finite group give ∣T∣≤maxr​∣f​(r)∣.
A modular arithmetic progression whose three entries lie in I satisfies the corresponding integer equation: the absolute value of x+z−2y is less than 2L<M, so congruence to zero forces equality. Thus T(1A​,1A​,1A​)=∣A∣/M2≤1/(16L), since only constant arithmetic progressions remain. Meanwhile, the choices 1≤x≤⌊L/2⌋ and 0≤h≤⌊L/4⌋ give at least L2/16 arithmetic progressions in I for L≥8. As M≤5L, this gives T(1I​,1I​,1I​)≥1/400. Expand 1A​=ρ1I​+f. The seven terms other than the main term are each bounded by max∣f​∣. For L≥50ρ−3,
7maxr​∣f​(r)∣≥ρ3/400−1/(16L)≥ρ3/800.
(3)
Consequently a nonzero frequency r obeys ∣f​(r)∣≥γ, where γ=ρ3/6000. It is nonzero because f​(0)=0.
We turn this coefficient into a local increase. Let Q=⌊L​⌋. Partition the unit circle into Q equal intervals and consider the Q+1 points jr/M, 0≤j≤Q, modulo one. Two occupy the same interval, yielding an integer 1≤q≤Q with ∥qr/M∥R/Z​≤1/Q. Set ℓ=⌊γQ/200⌋. If L exceeds a sufficiently large absolute multiple of ρ−6, then ℓ≥1 and each residue-class arithmetic progression of step q in [1,L] has at least ℓ terms. Partition each into pieces of lengths between ℓ and 2ℓ, distributing the final remainder among the pieces.
On each such arithmetic progression P, the character of a finite abelian group χ(x)=e−2πirx/M differs from its value at the first point by at most 4πℓ/Q<γ/4. Write sP​=∑x∈P​f(x). Therefore
γM≤​∑x∈I​f(x)χ(x)​≤∑P​∣sP​∣+γL/4,
(4)
so ∑P​∣sP​∣≥3γL/4. But ∑P​sP​=∑I​f=0, and hence the sum of the positive sP​ is at least 3γL/8. Some piece must consequently satisfy sP​/∣P∣≥γ/4. On that piece,
∣P∣∣A∩P∣​≥ρ+γ/4.
(5)
Also, once γQ≥400, ∣P∣≥ℓ≥γL​/800. This proves the one-frequency density increment on an integer interval: for absolute constants c,C>0, if L≥Cρ−6, there is a arithmetic progression P⊆[1,L] of length at least cρ3L​ on which the subset density rises by at least cρ3. For example one may decrease c to 10−7 and choose C large enough for the displayed elementary inequalities.
Now fix 0<δ≤1 and suppose the required conclusion fails. On each new arithmetic progression, identify its points in order with [1,L′]. This affine identification preserves all integer arithmetic progressions, so its copy of A∩P is still progression-free. All densities remain at least δ. Put ε=cδ3, H=Cδ−6, and choose an integer t>1/ε. Every increment raises the subset density by at least ε and changes the length by Lj+1​≥εLj​​. Iterating the latter inequality gives
logLj​≥2−jlogN+2(1−2−j)logε≥2−tlogN+2logε(j≤t).
(6)
Choose N>(H/ε2)2t. Then every length up to step t exceeds H, so every increment is available. After t increments the subset density exceeds one, a contradiction. Thus
For every δ>0, all sufficiently large N force a nonconstant three-term progression.​
(7)
For δ>1 there are no sets satisfying the subset density assumption. The argument above proves the substantive case without invoking the theorem being asked for.

We first prove the needed Petridis minimal-growth lemma. For finite nonempty sets A,D, choose a nonempty X⊆A minimizing K0​=∣X+D∣/∣X∣. Thus ∣Y+D∣≥K0​∣Y∣ for every Y⊆X, including the empty set. For any finite T={t1​,…,tr​}, define

The sets Xi​+ti​ partition X+T. If x∈/Xi​, then x+ti​∈X+tj​ for some j<i, and therefore x+D+ti​⊆X+D+tj​. The newly contributed portion of X+D+ti​ consequently has size at most
∣X+D∣−∣(X∖Xi​)+D∣≤K0​∣Xi​∣.

Use D=−A. The hypothesis implies K0​≤∣A−A∣/∣A∣≤C, so ∣X−2A∣≤C2∣X∣. We also prove the Ruzsa triangle inequality: for finite nonempty U,V,W,
∣U−W∣∣V∣≤∣U−V∣∣V−W∣.
(16)
For each s∈U−W, fix us​,ws​ with s=us​−ws​. The map (s,v)↦(us​−v,v−ws​) is injective: summing its coordinates recovers s, and then its first coordinate recovers v. This proves the inequality.

Thus K=C4 suffices​. This is the required case of the Plünnecke-Ruzsa inequality, proved here using the minimal-growth argument and the explicit injection. If A is empty the conclusion holds directly.

This is the finite-group convention implicit in the averaging notation; an arbitrary infinite group has no normalized counting probability measure on all its points. For h∈G, define Δh​f(x)=f(x)f(x+h)​, and let C denote complex conjugation. The Gowers U3 norm is

Homogeneity follows because the cube has four unconjugated and four conjugated factors, so ∥af∥U3​=∣a∣∥f∥U3​. If the norm is zero, every squared term in the finite average vanishes. Taking h1​=h2​=0 gives Ex​∣f(x)∣4=0, so f=0 pointwise. This proves positive definiteness.
