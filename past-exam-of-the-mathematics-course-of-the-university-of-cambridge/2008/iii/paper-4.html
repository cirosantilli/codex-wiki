Write A=RG. An idempotent is an element e with e2=e; it is central idempotent when it commutes with every element of the ring. In a commutative ring centrality is automatic. Two idempotents are orthogonal idempotents if ef=fe=0. A nonzero primitive central idempotent cannot be expressed as the sum of two nonzero orthogonal central idempotents; this is primitivity in Z(A), not necessarily primitivity in A.
A block of a group algebra is an indecomposable two-sided direct-summand ideal of A, equivalently an ideal Ae with e a primitive central idempotent. Here is the equivalence. If A=B⊕C as two-sided ideals, then BC=CB=0. Write 1=e+f with e∈B,f∈C. Multiplication by this equality shows that e is the identity of B, that f is the identity of C, and that e,f are orthogonal central idempotents. Conversely a central idempotent gives A=Ae⊕A(1−e). Splitting the ideal further is therefore equivalent to splitting its central idempotent further.
For uniqueness, suppose 1=∑i​ei​=∑j​fj​ are decompositions into primitive central idempotents. For each i,
ei​=∑j​ei​fj​
(1)
is an orthogonal central-idempotent decomposition. Exactly one summand is nonzero, and that summand equals ei​. Applying the same reasoning to the corresponding fj​ gives ei​=fj​. Thus the blocks, and their identities, are unique up to ordering. Existence in the paper's coefficient rings follows from finite-dimensionality over k, and over O from completeness and idempotent lifting of the decomposition modulo its maximal ideal.
Over k, a principal indecomposable module is an indecomposable summand of the regular left module A=kG, equivalently P=Ae for a primitive idempotent e of A. Put J=J(A), the Jacobson radical. The head of a module P/JP is nonzero and semisimple. It is simple: projectivity gives a surjection
EndA​(P)⟶EndA/J​(P/JP),
(2)
whose kernel consists of maps into JP and is nilpotent, since a product of r such maps sends P into JrP. If the head of a module split, a nontrivial projection on it would lift by idempotent lifting to a nontrivial projection on P, contradicting indecomposability. Every maximal submodule contains JP, since J annihilates simple modules. The simplicity of the head of a module now proves J(P)=JP is the unique maximal submodule, and P is its head of a module's projective cover.
Choose the simple modules S1​,…,Sr​, with corresponding projective covers P1​,…,Pr​. Our convention for the Cartan matrix of a group algebra is
(CG​)ij​=[Pj​:Si​],​
(3)
where brackets denote the multiplicity in a composition series.
For the final two unheaded requests, retain the additional hypothesis G=NCG​(N). Choose a central subgroup Z=⟨z⟩ of N of order p. Every element of N centralizes Z, and so does every element of CG​(N); hence Z≤Z(G). Set t=z−1, so t is central and tp=0, and A/tA≅k(G/Z).
To lift a central idempotent eˉ of this quotient, first lift it as an idempotent e by idempotent refinement theorem. Since eˉ is central, eA(1−e)⊆tA. Multiplying a representation ea(1−e)=tb on the left and right by e,1−e gives
eA(1−e)⊆teA(1−e)⊆⋯⊆tpeA(1−e)=0.
(4)
The other off-diagonal corner vanishes likewise, so e is central. Two central lifts of the same idempotent coincide: their mismatched products e(1−f) and f(1−e) are idempotents in the nilpotent kernel and therefore zero. Primitivity is preserved because orthogonal idempotent decompositions lift. Thus the central p-quotient gives a bijection of block idempotents.
The quotient G/Z still satisfies the required hypothesis for N/Z, because the image of CG​(N) centralizes N/Z. Induction on ∣N∣ therefore proves τ bijects the block idempotents of kG and k(G/N). The centrality argument is essential: a general nilpotent algebra quotient need not give a bijection of central block idempotents.
For the Cartan matrix of a group algebra, every projective A-module is free over kZ: A is free over kZ, a projective A-module is a summand of a finite free A-module, and finite projectives over the local ring kZ≅k[t]/(tp) are free. If P is a principal indecomposable module, Pˉ=P/tP is a principal indecomposable module over A/tA: an idempotent splitting of its endomorphism ring would lift and split P. The p quotients of its filtration
P⊃tP⊃⋯⊃tp−1P⊃0
(5)
are all isomorphic to Pˉ as k(G/Z)-modules, by multiplication by the corresponding power of t. All simple modules are annihilated by the nilpotent ideal tA. Hence [P:S]=p[Pˉ:S], with the natural indexing of simple modules, and CG​=pCG/Z​. Iterating over N gives
CG​=∣N∣CG/N​.​
(6)
This is blocks and Cartan matrices under central p-quotients. The extra hypothesis is still in force: for example N=C3​⊴S3​ in characteristic three does not satisfy it, and its Group algebra Cartan matrix is (21​12​) rather than 3I.

Use the usual Krull–Schmidt theorem setting, in which indecomposable summands have local endomorphism rings; the paper's rings O,k satisfy it for the subgroups involved. The relative projective module condition for H means that M is a direct summand of a module induced from H, equivalently, M is a summand of IndHG​ResHG​M. A vertex of an indecomposable module is an inclusion-minimal such subgroup. A source of an indecomposable module at its module vertex D is an indecomposable RD-summand S of M↓D​ such that M∣S↑G.
Existence of a module source follows by decomposing M↓D​ and using Krull–Schmidt theorem on the induced decomposition: the indecomposable summand M must occur in the induction of one indecomposable summand S. Such S has module vertex D as a D-module, since relative projectivity of S for a proper subgroup would make M relatively projective for that subgroup.
In the paper's modular coefficient rings, an indecomposable module is a trivial source module when its module source is the rank-one trivial module R of its module vertex. It is then a summand of R[G/D], a permutation module. Conversely suppose M is an indecomposable summand of a permutation module. Restriction to a module vertex D is a sum of modules R[D/E], one for each D-orbit of the permutation basis. For a p-group D, each transitive permutation module is indecomposable: over k it has simple head of a module over the local ring kD, and over O a nontrivial splitting would give a nontrivial splitting after reduction. A module source S must be one of these summands by Krull–Schmidt theorem. Its module vertex is D, whereas R[D/E] is relatively E-projective. Thus E=D, and S≅R. Therefore trivial module source is equivalent to being an indecomposable permutation-module summand.
The rank-one formulation uses the paper's coefficient rings. If the earlier general coefficient-ring wording were applied to a disconnected ring, it would need qualification: for R=k×k and G=1, (k,0) is an indecomposable summand of the permutation module R, but is not the rank-one trivial module R. One can instead allow indecomposable summands of the trivial coefficient module as module sources in that generality.
For the homomorphism request, take Mi​=O[G/Hi​]. Frobenius reciprocity and Mackey decomposition give
HomOG​(M1​,M2​)​≅(O[G/H2​])H1​≅g∈H1​\G/H2​⨁​O.​
(11)
The summand for g is generated by the sum of the cosets in the H1​-orbit of gH2​. The identical orbit-sum construction over k gives one copy of k per double coset. These are free bases in both characteristics, since invariance means coefficients are constant on orbits, with no division by orbit size. Hence
HomOG​(M1​,M2​)↠HomkG​(Mˉ1​,Mˉ2​).​
(12)
The same argument applies to direct sums of coset permutation modules.
If M is a trivial module source kG-module, choose a permutation lattice X with M∣Xˉ. Its projection is an idempotent f∈EndkG​(Xˉ). The endomorphism reduction is surjective by the preceding calculation. Its kernel is pEndOG​(X), and the endomorphism algebra is complete over O. By idempotent refinement theorem, f lifts to an idempotent f^​. The lattice f^​X is a permutation-module summand, hence a trivial module source lattice, and reduces to M.
To prove uniqueness within the class of trivial module source lattices, let L,L′ be two such lifts. They are summands of permutation lattices. Lift a modular isomorphism and its inverse between them by the same homomorphism-surjectivity argument, inserting the summand projections on either side. The lifted composites are identity modulo p and therefore invertible over O, by Nakayama lemma or the determinant criterion. The lifted maps are consequently inverse up to an invertible composite and give L≅L′. Thus each trivial module source module has a unique trivial module source lift up to isomorphism. This is permutation homomorphisms lift through modular reduction; uniqueness is not claimed among arbitrary lattice lifts.

Put q=pn and choose a generator h of H. In characteristic p, (h−1)q=hq−1=0, so
kH≅k[t]/(tq),t=h−1.
(18)
A module is a vector space with a nilpotent operator t whose nilpotence index is at most q. The nilpotent Jordan normal form, valid over every field, splits it into Jordan blocks. One block of size i is
Vi​=k[t]/(ti),1≤i≤q.​
(19)
It is indecomposable: its endomorphism ring is k[t]/(ti), a local ring with no nontrivial idempotents. Distinct i have distinct dimensions. Conversely a direct sum of at least two Jordan blocks is decomposable. Thus there are exactly q indecomposable isomorphism classes.
A homomorphism Vi​→Vj​ is determined by the image of 1, which can be any element of Vj​ annihilated by ti. If i≥j this is all of Vj​, of dimension j; if i<j it is the span of tj−i,…,tj−1, of dimension i. Hence
dimk​HomkH​(Vi​,Vj​)=min(i,j).​
(20)
This realizes the indecomposable modules for a cyclic p-group.

Let B=kGe have defect group D. Use two vertex-and-source facts, with their relevant content made explicit. Every indecomposable module in B has a module vertex conjugate into D. Also B, as a k[G×G]-module with (g,h)a=gah−1, is an indecomposable permutation-module summand with module vertex ΔD={(d,d):d∈D} and trivial module source. The second fact is the bimodule characterization of a defect group of a block; the first follows by applying its relative-ΔD induction splitting to B⊗kG​M=M.
If D is cyclic, every module source for every module vertex Q≤D occurs among the finitely many indecomposable modules for a cyclic p-group. Each module in the block is a summand of an induction of one of these finitely many module sources. There are finitely many subgroups Q and each induced representation has finitely many indecomposable summands by Krull–Schmidt theorem. Thus cyclic defect implies finite representation type of the block.
For the converse, the restriction B↓D×D​ has the regular kD-bimodule as a direct summand. To see this from module vertices and module sources, its Brauer quotient of a module at ΔD is nonzero. Its summands are permutation modules for the p-group D×D and have module vertices of order at most ∣D∣. A summand detected at ΔD therefore has module vertex ΔD and trivial module source, hence is
IndΔDD×D​k≅kD.
(23)
Here a transitive permutation module for a p-group is indecomposable: it has one-dimensional head of a module over the local group algebra and cannot split into two nonzero modules. This also explains the Brauer-quotient detection used in the argument.
For any kD-module U, tensor that split bimodule inclusion with U. It gives
U∣ResDG​(B⊗kD​U),
(24)
where ∣ denotes being a direct summand. If B has only finitely many indecomposable modules M1​,…,Ms​, each B⊗kD​U is a sum of these. Therefore every indecomposable U must occur among the summands of the finite list Mi​↓D​. Thus kD has finite representation type.
A noncyclic p-group has a quotient Cp​×Cp​: its Frattini quotient has rank at least two, since a one-generator Frattini quotient forces the group itself to be cyclic. Inflation along this quotient embeds all the indecomposable modules of that elementary abelian group, preserving isomorphisms and indecomposability. Part (b) rules out finite type when k is infinite.
The conclusion remains valid if the splitting field k is finite. For completeness, replace the scalar parameter in part (b) by an m×m companion matrix Tf​ of an irreducible polynomial f. On km⊕km use
x=(0I​00​),y=(0Tf​​00​).
(25)
An endomorphism commuting with these has form (AC​0A​) with ATf​=Tf​A. Its endomorphism ring has a square-zero ideal of the C-matrices and quotient k[Tf​]≅k[t]/(f), a field, so it is local and the module is indecomposable. Finite fields have irreducible polynomials of arbitrarily large degrees, giving infinitely many dimensions. This also excludes noncyclic D in that case. Altogether,
B has finite representation type⟺D is cyclic.​

Write N=NG​(D), C=CG​(D), and use the commutative transfer ideals
ID​=TrDG​((kG)D)⊆Z(kG),JD​=TrDN​(kC)⊆(kC)N.
(33)
The target fixed algebra is commutative, because C≤N makes every N-fixed element of kC central in kC.
The Brauer morphism and relative trace identity is
BrD​(TrDG​a)=TrDN​(BrD​a).
(34)
Indeed let D act on G/D by left multiplication in the trace sum. Its fixed cosets are exactly gD with g∈N; every other orbit has size divisible by p. After Brauer projection the contributions on each such nonfixed orbit are equal, so they cancel. Fixed cosets give precisely the right-hand side. Since the Brauer projection of (kG)D is all of kC, this proves a surjection ID​↠JD​.
We use the trace criterion for defect groups: a block idempotent e belongs to ID​ exactly when its defect group is conjugate into D; among these, BrD​(e)=0 exactly when its defect group is D up to conjugacy. The criterion can be read from the class-sum basis: if E=CD​(x) then
TrDG​(TrED​x)=TrEG​x=[CG​(x):E][xG].
(35)
This coefficient is nonzero exactly when E is a Sylow p-subgroup of CG​(x). Thus ID​ is spanned by classes whose class defect is conjugate into D, and the kernel of its Brauer projection is the sum of the corresponding proper-subgroup transfer ideals. The block version follows by refinement of the central idempotents in these ideals.
For clarity, the refinement fact needed here is primitive idempotents under a surjection of commutative Artinian ideals: a surjection between ideals of commutative Artinian algebras bijects the primitive idempotents in the module source whose images are nonzero with the primitive idempotents in the target. Decompose the algebras into local factors. An ideal in a local Artinian factor either contains the identity and is the whole factor, or lies in its nilpotent maximal ideal and has no nonzero idempotents. A surviving local factor has a local quotient, so its identity remains primitive. This proves the stated refinement fact without imposing nilpotence on the entire kernel.
The primitive idempotents in ID​ are the block idempotents with defect conjugate into D, and the surviving ones have defect D. Applying refinement to the surjection above gives
e⟼BrD​(e):{blocks of G with defect D} ⟷ {primitive idempotents of JD​}.​
(36)
This is the Brauer morphism on a defect transfer ideal; the target is the transfer ideal JD​, not the entire fixed center of an associative algebra.

For the unheaded induction request, write X={D∩gD:g∈/N}. We prove the sharper intersection bound, not just a drop in module vertex size. We use Green correspondence on permutation bimodules and the usual module vertex test for a permutation-module summand: its Brauer quotient of a module at P is nonzero precisely when a module vertex contains a conjugate of P. This test follows by restricting to the p-group P, decomposing the permutation basis into its orbits, and noting that proper-orbit sums are precisely the proper-subgroup transfer terms.
Consider kG as a k[G×D]-module, with (g,d)a=gad−1. It is the transitive permutation module induced from ΔD. Set L=G×D and L0​=N×D; the latter contains NL​(ΔD). Its restriction decomposes as

Every indecomposable L0​-summand of kN has module vertex ΔD. To justify this, its endomorphisms are right multiplications by elements of (kN)D. A finite-dimensional group algebra has finite representation type of a group algebra when there are only finitely many isomorphism classes of finite-dimensional indecomposable modules. The definition concerns all dimensions, not merely finitely many modules in each fixed dimension.
Let eB​ be the block idempotent and choose a maximal Brauer pair (D,bD​): bD​ is a block idempotent of kCG​(D) with bD​BrD​(eB​)=0. The inertial index of a block is
e(B)=∣NG​(D,bD​):DCG​(D)∣,​
(51)
where NG​(D,bD​) is the stabilizer of the chosen pair. Changing the pair by conjugation does not alter this number. For cyclic D, it is a prime-to-p divisor of p−1. Here n≥1, as required for the order-p subgroup Q to exist.
Put H=NG​(Q). Since Q is characteristic in D, NG​(D)≤H, so Green correspondence applies. In fact the nonprojective modules in B have a particularly simple correspondence. If g∈/H, a nontrivial intersection D∩gD would contain the unique order-p subgroup of both cyclic groups, giving Q=gQ and hence g∈H, a contradiction. Thus every such intersection is trivial. The error modules in induction are projective. In restriction, an intersection H∩gD cannot contain a conjugate of a nontrivial subgroup of D by an element of H: such a conjugate contains Q, and that would again force gQ=Q. The general Green correspondence therefore pairs all indecomposable modules with nontrivial module vertex contained in a conjugate of D. In particular it pairs the nonprojective modules of B with the nonprojective modules of its Brauer correspondent b in H; the block-compatible form of the correspondence selects b. Explicitly,
M↓H​=f(M)⊕MY​,f(M)↑G=M⊕P,
(52)
where P is projective and the restriction error family is Y={H∩gD:g∈/H}. One should not replace this restriction error term by projectives without an additional argument about H.
For the final structure calculation we use the general cyclic-defect structure theorem, which is permitted here as a general fact: the basic algebra of a split block with cyclic defect group is a Brauer tree algebra. Its tree has e(B) edges, one simple module per edge, and exceptional multiplicity
m=e(B)∣D∣−1​.
(53)
For a one-edge tree the basic algebra presentation is k[t]/(tm+1). This is the local presentation of the tree algebra, not an assertion that an arbitrary block with one simple module is automatically uniserial.
With e(B)=1 the tree has one edge and m=pn−1. Thus the basic algebra of B is
A0​=k[t]/(tpn).
(54)
It has exactly one simple module, k=A0​/(t). Its ideals are precisely (ti): for any nonzero element, factor out its lowest power of t, after which the remaining factor is a unit. The regular projective therefore has the unique submodule chain
A0​⊃tA0​⊃⋯⊃tpn−1A0​⊃0,
(55)
with each consecutive factor one-dimensional and simple. A Morita equivalence from A0​ to B preserves simple modules, projective covers, submodule lattices and composition length. Hence
B has one simple module S,P(S) is uniserial of length pn.​
(56)
In the split setting one may also write B≅Mdimk​S​(A0​). The projective cover has pn composition factors all isomorphic to S, so its vector-space dimension is pndimk​S, not generally pn.
There is also an elementary reason why the uniserial conclusion follows once the one-simple-module conclusion and finite type are known. If the basic local algebra had dimJ/J2≥2, quotienting its radical square and all but two generators would give k[x,y]/(x,y)2. The two-dimensional parameter family from Question 3(b) would then give infinitely many indecomposables. Thus J/J2 is one-dimensional. Nakayama lemma makes J generated by one element t, so its powers yield a chain and the basic algebra is k[t]/(tr). The cyclic-defect calculation above identifies r=pn. This explains why finite representation type rules out branching in this projective cover.
