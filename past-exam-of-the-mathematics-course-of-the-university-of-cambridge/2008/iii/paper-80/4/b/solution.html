For Fourier stability analysis, take spatial modes eimθ and put
D(θ)=ae−iθ+b+ceiθ,N(θ)=d+eeiθ,G(θ)=N(θ)/D(θ).
(26)
A bounded implicit step requires D to stay away from zero, and von Neumann stability analysis requires ∣G∣≤1 for every frequency. Expanding the squared moduli and using cos2θ=2cos2θ−1 gives
∣D∣2∣N∣2∣D∣2−∣N∣2​​=a2+b2+c2+2b(a+c)cosθ+2accos2θ,=d2+e2+2decosθ,=μ(μ−2)(μ−1)(μ+1)(1−cosθ)2.​​
(27)
Consequently, apart from denominator invertibility, the necessary and sufficient sign condition is
μ(μ−2)(μ−1)(μ+1)≥0.
(28)
Its real solution set is (−∞,−1]∪[0,1]∪[2,∞).
To check the denominator rather than assume it, note
ImD=(1−2μ)sinθ,D(0)=3,D(π)=1+2μ−2μ2.
(29)
If μ=1/2, a zero can occur only at zero or the Nyquist frequency. The zero-frequency value is nonzero, and the Nyquist zeros occur only at μ=(1±3​)/2, both outside the proposed stable intervals. At μ=1/2, D=9/4+(3/4)cosθ≥3/2, also nonzero. Continuity on the frequency circle now gives a positive lower bound on ∣D∣ for each allowed μ.
The implicit convolution operator is therefore boundedly invertible on the square-summable grid sequences. By the Plancherel theorem, ∣G∣≤1 gives
∥un+1∥ℓh2​​≤∥un∥ℓh2​​,μ∈(−∞,−1]∪[0,1]∪[2,∞).​
(30)
Outside these intervals the modulus difference is negative at every nonzero frequency where D is nonzero. There is an interval on which ∣G∣>1, giving arbitrarily large growth of powers of the step operator, so the method is unstable; a zero of D additionally makes the step ill posed.
For the physical choice k,h>0, the stable range is 0<μ≤1 or μ≥2; zero is the limiting zero-step case. The exact-shift values have unit gain and are included.
