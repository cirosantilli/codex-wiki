Let Ω=(0,1)2 and V=H01​(Ω), the closure of smooth compactly supported functions in the Sobolev space H1. Its elements have zero boundary trace. Set
a(v,w)=∫Ω​∇v⋅∇wdx,ℓ(w)=∫Ω​fwdx,f∈L2(Ω).
(37)
The form is symmetric and continuous by Cauchy-Schwarz. For a smooth function with zero boundary values, v(x,y)=∫0x​vx​(s,y)ds gives ∥v∥L2​≤∥vx​∥L2​≤∥∇v∥L2​ after integration. Density extends this Poincaré inequality to H01​. Therefore the gradient norm makes V complete and equivalent to its usual H1 norm, with
a(v,v)=∥∇v∥22​≥21​∥v∥H12​,∣ℓ(w)∣≤∥f∥2​∥w∥2​≤∥f∥2​∥∇w∥2​.
(38)
All the hypotheses of part (a) hold. Green's identity identifies the weak solution of the Dirichlet problem as
∫Ω​∇u⋅∇wdx=∫Ω​fwdxfor every w∈H01​(Ω).​
(39)
For smooth u this is −∇2u=f with zero boundary values; in general it defines the unique weak solution without requiring pointwise second derivatives.
For the Ritz method, choose a finite-dimensional conforming space Vh​⊂H01​(Ω) with basis ϕ1​,…,ϕN​ and put uh​=∑j​Uj​ϕj​. Minimize I(uh​) in the coefficients. Its derivative in each basis direction gives the finite element method equations
j∑​Kij​Uj​=Fi​,Kij​=∫Ω​∇ϕj​⋅∇ϕi​dx,Fi​=∫Ω​fϕi​dx.​
(40)
The stiffness matrix is symmetric positive definite: for a nonzero coefficient vector U, the corresponding basis combination is nonzero and UTKU=∥∇uh​∥22​>0. Hence the Ritz system has a unique solution.
For example, triangulate the square and use continuous piecewise-affine nodal basis functions at the interior vertices, with boundary vertex values fixed to zero. On a triangle T of area AT​, let λi​ be its barycentric basis functions. If its counterclockwise vertices are (xi​,yi​), define βi​=yj​−yk​ and χi​=xk​−xj​ cyclically. Then ∇λi​=(βi​,χi​)/(2AT​), giving the directly assemblable element matrices and loads
KijT​=4AT​βi​βj​+χi​χj​​,FiT​=∫T​fλi​dx.​
(41)
If f is constant on T, FiT​=fT​AT​/3; otherwise integrate or quadrature the load. Assemble by adding element contributions with the same global vertex labels and eliminate prescribed boundary coefficients. Only overlapping basis supports interact, making K sparse. This gives the Ritz-Galerkin equivalence for a symmetric coercive form in a practical finite element form.
