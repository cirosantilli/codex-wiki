Use complex inner products linear in the first argument throughout. The spectral theorem for compact self-adjoint operators states that, for a compact operator T=T∗ on a Hilbert space, its nonzero spectral values are real eigenvalues, each of finite multiplicity, with no possible accumulation point except zero. Moreover,
H=kerT⊕λ=0⨁​ker(T−λI),T=λ=0∑​λPλ​,​
(1)
where Pλ​ are the mutually orthogonal eigenspace projections. The operator series converges in operator norm when the nonzero eigenvalues are listed in decreasing absolute value. The sum is at most countable, even if the kernel is nonseparable. Zero need not be an eigenvalue.
First prove that a nonzero compact self-adjoint operator has an eigenvalue of magnitude its norm, without assuming a spectral decomposition. Put r=∥T∥>0 and choose unit vectors ξn​ with ∥Tξn​∥→r. Since ∥T2ξ∥≤r∥Tξ∥, expansion gives
∥(T2−r2I)ξn​∥2≤r2(r2−∥Tξn​∥2)⟶0.
(2)
Compactness of T2 supplies a subsequence for which T2ξn​ converges; the displayed estimate then forces ξn​ itself to converge to a unit vector ξ satisfying T2ξ=r2ξ. The vectors ξ+Tξ/r and ξ−Tξ/r are eigenvectors for r and −r, respectively, whenever nonzero, and they cannot both vanish. This proves the claim.
Self-adjointness gives reality of every eigenvalue and orthogonality of eigenspaces for distinct eigenvalues. An eigenspace for λ=0 cannot contain an infinite orthonormal sequence: its images under T would have pairwise distance 2​∣λ∣, contradicting compactness. The same argument proves that there are only finitely many mutually orthogonal eigenvectors with eigenvalues of magnitude at least any given ε>0. Hence all nonzero eigenspaces together have a countable orthonormal basis, and their eigenvalues tend to zero if there are infinitely many.
Let K be the orthogonal complement of the closed span of all these eigenspaces. It is invariant under T. If T∣K​ were nonzero, the norm-attaining eigenvalue argument applied to that compact self-adjoint restriction would produce another nonzero eigenvector, a contradiction. Thus K⊆kerT, and the converse inclusion follows from orthogonality. This establishes the asserted decomposition. On its orthogonal summands the tail of the operator series has norm equal to the supremum of the omitted eigenvalue magnitudes, proving norm convergence.
Finally, a nonzero complex number not among these eigenvalues has positive distance from their set and from zero. Inverting T−zI separately on the eigenspaces and kernel therefore gives a bounded inverse. This proves that no other nonzero spectral values occur and completes the theorem.

(7)

∑i​∥Tei​∥2=∑i,j​∣⟨Tei​,fj​⟩∣2=∑j​∥T∗fj​∥2.

⟨T,S⟩2​=i∑​⟨Tei​,Sei​⟩=Tr(S∗T),​
(9)
with the same first-argument linear convention. The sums converge by the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality. The operator-norm bound ∥T∥≤∥T∥2​ follows by applying Cauchy-Schwarz to the coordinates of a unit vector.
To prove completeness, let (Tn​) be Cauchy in this norm. The operator-norm bound gives a bounded-operator limit T. For a finite subset F of the basis,
∑i∈F​∥(Tn​−T)ei​∥2=limm→∞​∑i∈F​∥(Tn​−Tm​)ei​∥2.

For large n, the right side is uniformly small by the Cauchy property. Taking the supremum over F shows Tn​−T is Hilbert-Schmidt and ∥Tn​−T∥2​→0; it also shows T is Hilbert-Schmidt. Thus the Hilbert-Schmidt operators form a Hilbert space. Finite-rank operators are dense there: keeping finitely many basis columns makes the sum of omitted squared column norms tend to zero. The operator-norm bound then shows that every Hilbert-Schmidt operator is compact.
Now let U be a strongly continuous unitary representation of the compact metric group G, as is standard for a representation of a topological group. Fix ξ=0 and average the positive rank-one operator Rξ,ξ​:
K=∫G​Ug​Rξ,ξ​Ug∗​dg.
(11)
The integrand is continuous in the Hilbert-Schmidt norm, so this Bochner integral exists in the complete space just proved. Normalized Haar measure and invariance show that K is positive, compact and commutes with every Ug​. Moreover,
⟨Kξ,ξ⟩=∫G​∣⟨Ug​ξ,ξ⟩∣2dg>0:
(12)
the integrand is positive near the identity and every nonempty open set has positive Haar measure. Thus K=0. A nonzero eigenspace is finite-dimensional and invariant under U, by (a). Within it choose a nonzero invariant subspace of smallest dimension; it is irreducible. Its orthogonal complement is invariant because the representation is unitary, so the finite-dimensional space splits into irreducibles.

H=α⨁​Hα​,dimHα​<∞,U∣Hα​​ irreducible.​
(13)

Write L=d(x,y). If m′ is any other metric midpoint, apply the inequality from (a) to the chosen midpoint m with z=m′. The right side is 21​(L/2)2+21​(L/2)2−L2/4=0, so m=m′. Every pair has a unique midpoint.

For a discrete group, one standard definition of amenability is the fixed-point property: every continuous affine action on a nonempty compact convex set in a locally convex space has a fixed point. The equivalent state formulation, which may be stated without proof here, is that there is a left-invariant invariant mean on ℓ∞(Γ). Equivalently, every unital left-translation-invariant star-subalgebra of ℓ∞(Γ) admits a left-invariant state, with continuity for the supremum norm; one may equally pass to its norm closure. A state means a positive linear functional of norm one with value one on the constant function one. Restriction proves the subalgebra formulation from the full-algebra version, and taking the full algebra proves the converse. Such a mean is finitely additive on indicator functions, not necessarily countably additive.
Write (λ(g)μ)(h)=μ(g−1h) on ℓ1(Γ) and (Lg​f)(h)=f(g−1h) on bounded functions. We prove equivalence of this mean condition, the Reiter condition, and existence of a Følner sequence.
Assume first that an invariant mean m exists. Fix a finite set K⊆Γ. In the real Banach space ⨁g∈K​ℓ1(Γ) with sum norm, consider the convex set
C={(λ(g)μ−μ)g∈K​:μ a finitely supported probability measure}.
(30)
If zero were outside its norm closure, the Hahn-Banach separation theorem would give bounded real functions fg​ and δ>0 such that
∑g∈K​∑h​fg​(h)[(λ(g)μ)(h)−μ(h)]≥δ
(31)
for every such μ. Testing point masses at h shows
∑g∈K​[fg​(gh)−fg​(h)]≥δfor every h.
(32)
Applying the positive invariant mean makes the left side zero and the right side at least δ, a contradiction. Therefore zero is in the closure: for any ε>0 a finitely supported probability measure makes the sum of the translation defects over K less than ε. Enumerate the countable group, take the first n elements for K and ε=1/n, and obtain a sequence satisfying the Reiter condition. This proves (1) implies (2).
Next assume (2). An ℓ1 probability measure can be approximated in norm by finitely supported probability measures, by restricting to a large finite set and renormalizing. Replacing μ by ν changes each defect by at most 2∥μ−ν∥1​. Thus simultaneous small defects for finitely many translations can be achieved with finite support.
For such ν, set Ft​={h:ν(h)>t}. The elementary identity ∣r−s∣=∫0∞​∣1r>t​−1s>t​∣dt for r,s≥0 gives
∫0∞​∣Ft​∣dt=1,∫0∞​∣Ft​△gFt​∣dt=∥λ(g)ν−ν∥1​.
(33)
If the sum of defects over K is less than ε, some t with Ft​=∅ must satisfy ∑g∈K​∣Ft​△gFt​∣<ε∣Ft​∣; otherwise integration would contradict that strict inequality. Applying this layer-cake extraction of Følner sets to successive finite sets of translations gives nonempty finite Fn​ with each relative boundary tending to zero. Hence (2) implies (3).
Conversely, normalized counting measures μn​=1Fn​​/∣Fn​∣ satisfy
∥λ(g)μn​−μn​∥1​=∣Fn​∣∣gFn​△Fn​∣​,
(34)
so (3) implies (2). Finally, from (2) define states mn​(f)=∑h​μn​(h)f(h). The dual unit ball of ℓ∞(Γ) is weak-star compact by the Banach-Alaoglu theorem; take a convergent subnet, since this compactness need not be metrizable. Its limit m is positive with m(1)=1, and
∣mn​(Lg​f)−mn​(f)∣≤∥f∥∞​∥λ(g−1)μn​−μn​∥1​⟶0.
(35)
Thus m is invariant, proving (2) implies (1). Together these implications establish all three equivalences. Countability is used to obtain sequences from finite tests; it is not a justification for replacing the compactness subnet by a subsequence.

The topology needed here is the weak-star topology σ(E∗,E), meaning pointwise convergence on E. With the literal weak topology σ(E∗,E∗∗), the claimed compactness is false. For example, E=c0​ is separable and E∗=ℓ1. Its unit vectors en​ have no weak cluster point: every coordinate functional forces a cluster point to have all coordinates zero, while the bounded functional x↦∑j​xj​ has value one throughout. Thus the dual unit ball is not weakly compact. We prove the intended weak-star assertion in full.
Choose a countable dense set (xk​) in the unit ball of E. On E1∗​ define
d(ϕ,ψ)=k≥1∑​2−k∣ϕ(xk​)−ψ(xk​)∣.​
(39)
Each term is at most 2⋅2−k, so the series converges. The triangle inequality is immediate, and d=0 forces equality on the dense set and hence on all of E. It is therefore a metric. Convergence in this metric is equivalent to convergence on every xk​: one direction follows from any individual term, and the other by bounding the series tail uniformly. Because ∥ϕ∥,∥ψ∥≤1, approximation of any unit vector by xk​ then gives convergence on all of E. The same finite-tail and approximation argument for neighborhoods proves equality with the weak-star topology, not just agreement of its convergent sequences.
Given a sequence (ϕn​) in this ball, diagonal selection gives a subsequence converging at every xk​, since each coordinate lies in a compact disk. Uniform norm bounds then make it pointwise Cauchy at every x∈E. Define ϕ(x) as the limit. Linearity passes to the limit, and ∣ϕ(x)∣≤∥x∥, so ϕ∈E1∗​. The subsequence converges to it in the metric. Thus this metric space is sequentially compact, hence compact, proving
E1∗​ is compact and metrizable in σ(E∗,E).​
(40)

For a unital star-subalgebra A⊆B(H), the Von Neumann double commutant theorem states
ASOT=AWOT=A′′.​
(47)
Here A′ is the commutant of an operator algebra, consisting of operators commuting with every element of A. The double commutant is closed in either topology: multiplication by a fixed bounded operator is continuous in both. Therefore both closures of A lie in A′′.
For the converse, let T∈A′′ and choose finitely many vectors ξ1​,…,ξn​. In Hn put
K={(aξ1​,…,aξn​):a∈A}​.
(48)
It is invariant under the diagonal action of A and its adjoints, so its orthogonal projection Q commutes with that diagonal action. Consequently each matrix entry Qij​ belongs to A′. The diagonal operator with entry T therefore commutes with Q. Since I∈A, the tuple ξ=(ξ1​,…,ξn​) belongs to K, and hence so does (Tξ1​,…,Tξn​). By the definition of K, one element a∈A approximates this entire tuple arbitrarily closely. This is exactly approximation in every strong-operator neighborhood of T. It proves A′′⊆ASOT and thus both asserted equalities. In particular, M=A′′ is a Von Neumann algebra.
The Kaplansky density theorem strengthens the result by preserving norm bounds:
{a∈A:∥a∥≤1}​SOT={x∈M:∥x∥≤1}.​
(49)
It also holds for the self-adjoint and positive unit balls; the full ball can in fact be approximated in the strong-star topology, meaning strong convergence of both operators and adjoints. We prove the bound rather than merely scaling the unbounded approximants furnished by the double commutant theorem.
First put C=A∥⋅∥, a unital C-star algebra. Its self-adjoint part is weak-operator dense in Msa​: symmetrize any weak approximating net. For a convex subset, strong and weak operator closures agree. To see this explicitly, separation from a strong closure supplies a continuous real functional on finitely many image vectors, hence one of the form Re∑j​⟨aξj​,ηj​⟩, which is also weak-operator continuous. The Hahn-Banach separation theorem would then separate the point from the weak closure as well. Thus every self-adjoint x∈M is a strong limit of self-adjoint ai​∈C, initially without a common norm bound.
The needed functional-calculus continuity follows directly from resolvents. The resolvent identity gives
(ai​−iI)−1−(x−iI)−1=(ai​−iI)−1(x−ai​)(x−iI)−1.
(50)
The first factor has norm at most one, so the right side tends strongly to zero. The same holds at −i. Both resolvent nets are uniformly bounded, so their products converge strongly. Their scalar functions generate a self-adjoint algebra separating points of R and vanishing at infinity. The Stone-Weierstrass theorem, applied to the one-point compactification, makes this algebra uniformly dense in C0​(R). Uniform approximation and the functional-calculus norm bound therefore show
f(ai​)⟶f(x) strongly for every f∈C0​(R).
(51)
This proves strong continuity of functional calculus through resolvents without an unjustified uniform bound on the original ai​.
If x=x∗ and ∥x∥≤1, choose a real compactly supported continuous f equal to the identity on [−1,1] and bounded in absolute value by one. Then f(ai​)∈C are self-adjoint contractions and converge strongly to f(x)=x. For a positive contraction x, choose instead 0≤f≤1 equal to the identity on [0,1]; this gives positive contractions. These prove the two restricted versions for C.
For an arbitrary contraction x∈M, apply the self-adjoint result in the matrix algebra M2​(C) to
X=(0x∗​x0​),∥X∥=∥x∥≤1.
(52)

The upper-right entries ci​ of the approximating self-adjoint contraction matrices belong to C, satisfy ∥ci​∥≤1, and converge strongly to x. The lower-left entries are ci∗​ and converge strongly to x∗. This proves the full strong-star assertion for C.

Combining these norm approximations with each finite-vector strong approximation proves all versions for A. The reverse inclusions follow because the relevant norm bounds and the inequalities defining a positive operator are preserved by strong limits.
