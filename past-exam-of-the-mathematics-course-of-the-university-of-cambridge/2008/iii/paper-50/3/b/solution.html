Angle integration gives a dual representation in any lattice dimension. Discrete summation by parts rewrites the phase as
∑x,μ​nx,μ​(θx+μ^​​−θx​)=−∑x​θx​∑μ​(nx,μ​−nx−μ^​,μ​).
(29)
Each normalized angle integral is a Kronecker delta. This proves the integer-current representation of the Villain model
ZV​=(2πβ)−Nb​/2∑divn=0​exp[−2β1​∑e​ne2​].
(30)
The PDF does not specify dimension or boundaries. The point-vortex vortex Coulomb gas interpretation below is two-dimensional. In three dimensions the defects are vortex lines, and the dual constrained-current representation still holds but is not a gas of point charges with logarithmic interactions.
First take a connected simply connected planar lattice, with free outer angles, so no torus winding sectors are silently discarded. Assign an integer height ℓ to each dual face, fixing the exterior face height to zero. A divergence-free integer current is exactly the oriented height difference on the two faces adjoining its bond. To see existence, integrate the rotated current along a dual path: the change around a closed dual path is the total divergence inside, hence zero. Integer currents give integer heights, and fixing the exterior removes their constant-shift ambiguity. Therefore
∑e​ne2​=∑⟨x,y⟩dual​​(ℓx​−ℓy​)2=ℓTLℓ,
(31)
where L is the positive dual Graph Laplacian with the exterior height fixed. This gives the dual integer-height representation of the planar Villain model.
Apply the supplied Poisson summation formula separately to every interior height. For Nf​ interior faces,
ZV​=(2πβ)−Nb​/2∑q∈ZNf​​∫RNf​​dϕexp[−2β1​ϕTLϕ+2πiqTϕ].
(32)
Complete the Gaussian square, or use its characteristic function, to evaluate the integral as
detL​(2πβ)Nf​/2​exp[−2π2βqTL−1q].
(33)
Thus the smooth fluctuations and integer defects separate:
ZV​=Zsw​Zv​,Zv​=q∈ZNf​∑​e−2π2βqTGq,G=L−1.​
(34)
The Gaussian factor is Zsw​=(2πβ)−(Nb​−Nf​)/2/detL​. It really is the spin wave factor of the original lattice: the planar Euler identity gives Nb​−Nf​=Nx​−1, and the matrix-tree theorem identifies detL with the spanning-tree count of the original graph. Its reduced primal Laplacian has the same determinant, so the noncompact Gaussian angle integral with one angle fixed has exactly this prefactor. The compact common-angle mode has been normalized to one. The remaining q variables are integer vortex windings on the faces, giving the vortex Coulomb gas.
For periodic boundaries, L has a zero mode, and there are also two noncontractible current sectors. Neutral charges use the inverse G on the mean-zero subspace. An integer dual height may have twists wx​,wy​∈Z across periods Lx​,Ly​. Subtracting wx​x/Lx​+wy​y/Ly​ makes the remaining height periodic; the gradient energy gains (Ly​wx2​/Lx​+Lx​wy2​/Ly​)/(2β). Its height Poisson summation supplies the phase exp[2πi(wx​Px​/Lx​+wy​Py​/Ly​)], where Px​=∑qx​x and Py​=∑qx​y. A second Poisson summation in wx​,wy​ yields the positive harmonic-sector factor in periodic Villain duality
W(q)=∑Wx​,Wy​∈Z​exp[−2π2β{Ly​Lx​​(Wx​−Lx​Px​​)2+Lx​Ly​​(Wy​−Ly​Py​​)2}].
(35)
Up to charge-independent normalization, the exact periodic result is ZV​=Zsw​∑∑q=0​e−2π2βqTGqW(q). Smooth spin waves still factor out, but the finite-volume global winding sectors couple to vortex dipole moments. The familiar neutral bulk formula suppresses this boundary information; it must not be asserted as an exact finite-torus formula without specifying those sectors.
