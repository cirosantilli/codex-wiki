Besides the Gaussian fixed point, solve the nonzero-coupling fixed-point equations. The quartic equation gives λ∗​=2ϵ(1+ρ∗​)2/(3KD​). Substitute into the mass equation to get 2ρ∗​+ϵ(1+ρ∗​)/3=0, hence ρ∗​=−ϵ/(6+ϵ) within the displayed one-loop truncation. Since K4​=2π2/(2π)4=1/(8π2),
ρ∗​=−ϵ/6+O(ϵ2),λ∗​=16π2ϵ/3+O(ϵ2).​

These are the Wilson-Fisher fixed point coordinates in this cutoff scheme. The higher terms obtained by solving the truncated equations exactly should not be regarded as a full higher-order epsilon calculation.
The attraction claim in the PDF needs a thermal qualification. The stability matrix of a renormalization-group fixed point at this solution has entries
J=(2−ϵ/33KD​λ∗2​/(1+ρ∗​)3​KD​/[2(1+ρ∗​)]−ϵ​).
(43)
The lower-left entry is O(ϵ2), so its eigenvalues are
yt​=2−ϵ/3+O(ϵ2)>0,yg​=−ϵ+O(ϵ2)<0.​
(44)
Increasing ℓ means approaching the infrared. The quartic scaling direction is attractive, but a perturbation in the thermal scaling direction grows as eyt​ℓ. Therefore the Wilson-Fisher fixed point is an infrared attractor on its tuned critical surface, not an attractive fixed point of the entire two-dimensional coupling plane. In the plane it is a saddle; an arbitrary small thermal perturbation is a concrete counterexample to unconditional attraction. This is the thermal relevant direction at the Wilson-Fisher fixed point.
