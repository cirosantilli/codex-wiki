The complex structure on a complex manifold splits its complexified cotangent bundle into the +i and −i eigenspaces, locally spanned by dzj and dzˉj. A differential form of type (p, q) is a smooth section of ⋀p(T1,0X)∗⊗⋀q(T0,1X)∗, so in holomorphic coordinates it has the expression
η=∑∣I∣=p,∣J∣=q​ηI,J​dzI∧dzˉJ.
(1)
Here the increasing multi-indices avoid duplicating alternating factors. The exterior derivative has just two type components: the conjugate Dolbeault operator ∂ and the Dolbeault operator ∂ˉ. On such a local expression they are
∂η=∑I,J,k​∂zk∂ηI,J​​dzk∧dzI∧dzˉJ,∂ˉη=∑I,J,k​∂zˉk∂ηI,J​​dzˉk∧dzI∧dzˉJ.
(2)
Holomorphic changes of coordinates preserve these types, so the definitions are intrinsic. Comparing type components of d2=0 gives ∂2=∂ˉ2=0 and ∂∂ˉ+∂ˉ∂=0.
A real (p, p)-form satisfies ηˉ​=η, where complex conjugation acts on both coefficients and differential factors. Conjugation exchanges the two Dolbeault operators, so
∂η=∂ˉη​.
(3)
Thus ∂ˉη=0 forces ∂η=0 as well. Conversely, if dη=0, its distinct (p+1,p) and (p,p+1) components both vanish. Therefore
dη=0⟺∂ˉη=0for real η of type (p,p).​
(4)
The assertion remains valid in top degree, when the indicated type components are zero automatically.
For a holomorphic function f, ∂ˉf=0 and ∂fˉ​=0. Writing its real part as u=(f+fˉ​)/2 gives
∂ˉ∂u=21​∂ˉ∂f+21​∂ˉ∂fˉ​=−21​∂∂ˉf+0=0.
(5)
Hence the real part of a holomorphic function is a pluriharmonic function.
For the local exactness assertion, work on a smaller polydisc whose closure lies in the original one. The allowed one-variable integral is the Cauchy-Green operator Tj​ in coordinate zj​. Applied coefficientwise, it satisfies ∂zˉj​​Tj​a=a on a smaller disc and commutes with differentiation in all other coordinates; the smooth parameter dependence follows by applying the integral to a smooth compactly supported extension in the integration variable. This extension can be made with a cutoff equal to one on the smaller disc.
Begin with a Dolbeault operator closed form φ and decompose it uniquely as φ=dzˉ1∧α+β, where neither α nor β contains dzˉ1. Set ψ1​=T1​α. The dzˉ1 component of ∂ˉψ1​ is exactly dzˉ1∧α, so
φ1​=φ−∂ˉψ1​
(6)
has no dzˉ1 factor. It is still ∂ˉ-closed. The dzˉ1 component of ∂ˉφ1​=0 now shows that every coefficient of φ1​ is holomorphic in z1​.
Apply the same construction in z2​ to remove its antiholomorphic differential factor. The operator T2​ preserves holomorphicity in z1​, since it commutes with ∂zˉ1​​. Consequently the second subtraction introduces no previously removed factor. Continue through all n coordinates, shrinking the polydisc when necessary. The final remainder has positive antiholomorphic degree but contains none of the dzˉj, and hence is zero. This coordinate elimination for local Dolbeault primitives constructs
ψ=ψ1​+⋯+ψn​,∂ˉψ=φ∣U0​​.​
(7)
If q>n, the original form is already zero, so one can take ψ=0.
Finally let φ be a smooth (0,1)-form on the complex projective line. Choose 0<r<R and the two coordinate neighborhoods U={∣z∣<R} and V={∣z∣>r}∪{∞}, the latter expressed in w=1/z. They cover the sphere. The one-variable construction, integrating over slightly larger closed discs in each coordinate chart, gives smooth functions a on U and b on V with ∂ˉa=φ∣U​ and ∂ˉb=φ∣V​.
Their difference h=a−b is a holomorphic function on the annulus r<∣z∣<R. Its Laurent series splits as
h(z)=h+​(z)+h−​(z),h+​=∑m≥0​cm​zm,h−​=∑m<0​cm​zm.
(8)
The first series extends holomorphically throughout U, while the second extends throughout V, including infinity, where it has value zero. Thus a−h+​ and b+h−​ agree on the overlap and glue to a global smooth function F. Both corrections are holomorphic, so
∂ˉF=φ,H∂ˉ0,1​(P1)=0.​
(9)
This Laurent gluing of Dolbeault primitives on the projective line uses no Hodge theorem.
