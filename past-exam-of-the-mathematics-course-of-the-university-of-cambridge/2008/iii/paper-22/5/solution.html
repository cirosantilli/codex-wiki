For each of the operators D=d,∂,∂ˉ,dc, define its nonnegative Laplacian by ΔD​=DD∗+D∗D using the L2 formal adjoint. In particular, Δ=dd∗+d∗d, Δ∂​=∂∂∗+∂∗∂, Δ∂ˉ​=∂ˉ∂ˉ∗+∂ˉ∗∂ˉ, and Δc=dc(dc)∗+(dc)∗dc. Use the d c operator convention
dc=i(∂ˉ−∂),ddc=2i∂∂ˉ.
(30)
This normalization, without an extra factor of one-half, is the one for which the requested equality with Δc holds.
The Lefschetz operator of a Kähler manifold is Lα=ω∧α, and the adjoint Lefschetz operator is its pointwise metric adjoint Λ=L∗. It lowers type by (1,1) and is a real operator because ω and the metric are real. Write P=∂ and B=∂ˉ, use [A,D]=AD−DA, and use {A,D}=AD+DA for the anticommutators of the odd-degree operators. The allowed relation among the Kähler identities and its complex conjugate give
[Λ,P]=iB∗,[Λ,B]=−iP∗,B∗=−i[Λ,P],P∗=i[Λ,B].
(31)
These identities alone suffice for the calculation. Since P2=B2=0,
{P,B∗}=−i{P,[Λ,P]}=−i(PΛP−P2Λ+ΛP2−PΛP)=0.
(32)
Replacing P by B in the same calculation gives {B,P∗}=0. Thus both mixed terms vanish when expanding the Hodge Laplacian of d=P+B:
Δ=ΔP​+ΔB​.
(33)
The anticommutation PB=−BP now proves equality of the two summands. Indeed,
ΔB​ΔP​​=−i{B,[Λ,P]}=−i(BΛP+PBΛ+ΛPB−PΛB),=i{P,[Λ,B]}=i(PΛB−PBΛ−ΛPB−BΛP),​
(34)
which are the same expression. Finally, (dc)∗=i(P∗−B∗), so its Laplacian expands as
Δc=ΔB​+ΔP​−{B,P∗}−{P,B∗}=ΔB​+ΔP​.
(35)
This proves the Kähler Laplacian identity, with its real-operator version included:
Δ=2Δ∂ˉ​=2Δ∂​=Δc.​
(36)
The action of the almost complex structure on forms is Jα(v1​,…,vk​)=α(Jv1​,…,Jvk​). It multiplies a form of type (p,q) by ip−q. Since Δ=2Δ∂ˉ​ preserves each bidegree, it commutes with this action of J. Thus Δ(Jα)=J(Δα); the invertibility of J gives
Δα=0⟺Δ(Jα)=0.​
(37)
This argument applies to arbitrary sums of types, not only to pure-type forms.
The ddbar lemma for a compact Kähler manifold states that a d-closed form of pure type is ∂∂ˉ-exact if it is d-exact, ∂-exact, or ∂ˉ-exact. Conversely, a ∂∂ˉ-exact form is d-closed and exact for all three operators. In particular a d-exact, d-closed (p,q)-form equals ∂∂ˉβ for a form β of type (p−1,q−1).
The difference γ=ω−ω of the two Kähler forms is a real (1,1)-form, is d-closed, and is d-exact by equality of their de Rham cohomology classes. The ddbar lemma therefore gives a smooth complex-valued function h with γ=∂∂ˉh. Reality and anticommutation imply
γ=γˉ​=∂ˉ∂hˉ=−∂∂ˉhˉ.
(38)
Set f=(h−hˉ)/(2i), which is real. It follows directly that i∂∂ˉf=γ, giving the global potential for cohomologous Kähler forms
ω=ω+i∂∂ˉf,f∈C∞(X,R).​
(39)
If f1​,f2​ are two real potentials, u=f1​−f2​ satisfies ∂∂ˉu=0, so it is a pluriharmonic function. The allowed relation among the Kähler identities gives ∂ˉ∗∂ˉu=−i[Λ,∂]∂ˉu=−iΛ∂∂ˉu=0, because Λ∂ˉu=0 by degree. The Kähler Laplacian identity then gives Δu=2∂ˉ∗∂ˉu=0. Integration by parts on compact X gives
0=⟨Δu,u⟩L2​=∥du∥L22​,
(40)
so u is constant on each connected component. Consequently the potential is unique up to one additive constant when X is connected; on a disconnected X, one constant per component is the exact conclusion. Without the usual connectedness convention the printed uniqueness assertion needs this qualification: distinct constants on the components of a disjoint union leave the same pair of Kähler forms unchanged.
