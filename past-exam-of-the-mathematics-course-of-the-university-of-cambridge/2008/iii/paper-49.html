For a finite-dimensional Lie algebra, write AX​=adX for its Adjoint representation. The Killing form is the symmetric bilinear form
κ(X,Y)=tr(AX​AY​).
(1)
The Jacobi identity gives A[X,Y]​=[AX​,AY​]. Using the cyclic property of the trace,
κ([X,Y],Z)=tr([AX​,AY​]AZ​)=tr(AX​[AY​,AZ​])=κ(X,[Y,Z]).
(2)
Thus it is an invariant bilinear form on a Lie algebra. If [Ta​,Tb​]=fab​cTc​, the adjoint matrix has entries (ATa​​)cb​=fab​c. Its trace product yields the structure constants of a Lie algebra formula
κab​=c,d∑​fac​dfbd​c.​
(3)
A semisimple Lie algebra has zero solvable radical, equivalently no nonzero solvable ideal. In characteristic zero, the Cartan criterion for semisimplicity characterizes it by nondegeneracy of its Killing form. A real compact Lie algebra is the Lie algebra of a compact Lie group, equivalently one admitting a positive-definite invariant inner product. For a real semisimple algebra, the compactness criterion from the Killing form says compactness is equivalent to negative-definiteness of κ. An abelian compact-type algebra has zero Killing form, so this last characterization specifically concerns the semisimple case.

In the ordered basis (K1​,K2​,J), the three Adjoint representation matrices are
AK1​​=​000​001​0−λ0​​,AK2​​=​00−1​000​λ00​​,AJ​=​0λ0​−λ00​000​​.
(4)
Their cross-product traces vanish, while their squared traces are −2λ, −2λ and −2λ2. Consequently
κ=−2diag(λ,λ,λ2).​
(5)
This is the Killing form for cyclic three-generator brackets with constants (1,λ,λ).
For λ>0, the form is nondegenerate and negative-definite. The algebra is semisimple and compact, isomorphic to the SU(2) Lie algebra. For λ<0, it is nondegenerate with signature (2,1), so the algebra is semisimple and noncompact, isomorphic to sl2​(R).
For λ=0, the only nonzero bracket is [K1​,K2​]=J, with J central. This is the Heisenberg Lie algebra, which is nilpotent and neither semisimple nor compact. Compactness is ruled out even though the Killing form is merely degenerate: AK1​​ is nonzero and nilpotent, whereas in a compact Lie algebra every adjoint map is skew-adjoint for a positive inner product. Skew-adjoint nilpotent operators vanish.

Let v=(x,y)T and B(ρ)=(coshρsinhρ​sinhρcoshρ​). Matrix multiplication gives
(ρ,v)(ρ′,v′)=(ρ+ρ′,v+B(ρ)v′).
(6)
Thus G is a semidirect product of the translation plane by the one-parameter Lorentz boost group. Differentiating left multiplication at the identity gives the left-invariant vector fields
X0​=∂ρ​,X1​=coshρ∂x​+sinhρ∂y​,X2​=sinhρ∂x​+coshρ∂y​.​
(7)
They obey [X0​,X1​]=X2​, [X0​,X2​]=X1​ and [X1​,X2​]=0.
Their dual coframe is dρ, coshρdx−sinhρdy, and −sinhρdx+coshρdy. The Haar measure from a left-invariant coframe is their absolute wedge product. Since detB(ρ)=1,
dμ=Cdρdxdy,C>0.​
(8)
One can also check this directly: left multiplication has Jacobian determinant one. Right multiplication has a block triangular Jacobian with determinant one too, so the same measure is both left and right Haar measure.

The commuting Hermitian operators H1​,H2​ admit a weight-space decomposition. The six root vectors shift their simultaneous eigenvalues by
α1​=(1,0),α2​=(−1/2,3​/2),α3​=(1/2,3​/2)
(9)
or their negatives. Put hi​=2αi​⋅H, ei​=2​E+i​ and fi​=2​E−i​. The supplied relations give
[hi​,ei​]=2ei​,[hi​,fi​]=−2fi​,[ei​,fi​]=hi​.
(10)
These are three sl2 triples, coming from the complexifications of the three SU(2) Lie algebra root-pair subalgebras. They act through homomorphisms into End(V). For a nontrivial representation, simplicity of the SU(3) Lie algebra makes its kernel zero, so these images are actual sl2​(C) subalgebras; a trivial representation gives zero actions. The real compact generators can be taken as ihi​, i(E+i​+E−i​) and E+i​−E−i​.
Decomposing under any one of these subalgebras gives finite weight strings in its root direction. The two simple-root highest weights must be nonnegative integers. In the SU(3) highest-weight coordinates used here, this condition is
a=2p∈Z≥0​,b=3​q−p∈Z≥0​.​
(11)
Here (a,b) are the Dynkin labels; (p,q) are the eigenvalues of H1​,H2​. An irreducible representation is determined by these labels and has dimension (a+1)(b+1)(a+b+2)/2. An arbitrary finite-dimensional anti-Hermitian representation is a direct sum of such representations: the orthogonal complement of an invariant subspace remains invariant.
The weight diagram has Weyl group symmetry, namely reflections and rotations by 120 degrees. Its convex boundary is a hexagon with alternating side lengths a,b, degenerating to a triangle when one label vanishes. Generic representations need not have 60-degree symmetry. Boundary weight multiplicities are one. Moving inward through hexagonal layers increases multiplicity by one, up to min(a,b)+1; triangular inner layers retain that maximum. An exact version of this rule is the dominant weight multiplicity formula for sl3: move a weight by the Weyl group to dominant Dynkin coordinates (c,d) and set
r=32a+b−2c−d​,s=3a+2b−c−2d​.
(12)
Its multiplicity is zero unless r,s are integers, and otherwise is max{0,1+min(a,b,r,s)}. These rules construct the diagram while counting coincident weights correctly.
For the Casimir calculation, set t3​=H1​, t8​=H2​, and for each root pair use (E+​+E−​)/2​ and (E+​−E−​)/(i2​). In the defining representation these eight generators satisfy tr(ta​tb​)=δab​/2. Their summed squares are precisely the question's X. The invariant trace form makes their structure constants of a Lie algebra antisymmetric, so
[X,tc​]=∑a​{ta​,[ta​,tc​]}=0:
(13)
the antisymmetric coefficient contracts a symmetric anticommutator. Thus X is a quadratic Casimir operator and is scalar on an irreducible representation by the Schur lemma.
Let v be a highest-weight vector, annihilated by all E+i​. On v, each E−i​E+i​ vanishes and each E+i​E−i​ reduces to the root commutator. Summing those three commutators gives H1​+3​H2​. Hence
Xv=(p2+q2+p+3​q)v,
(14)
and therefore the SU(3) quadratic Casimir eigenvalue is
X=(p+3​q+p2+q2)IV​=3a2+ab+b2+3a+3b​IV​.​
(15)
For a reducible representation the same calculation applies separately to its irreducible blocks.
For light-hadron flavour symmetry, the up quark, down quark and strange quark form 3, and antiquarks form 3. Conventional mesons have 3⊗3=1⊕8. Conventional three-quark baryons have
3⊗3=1⊕8⊕8⊕10,
(16)
obtained from 3⊗3=6⊕3, 6⊗3=10⊕8 and 3⊗3=8⊕1. Their Dynkin labels are (0,0), (1,1) and (3,0), respectively. Thus the conventional meson octet, baryon octet, baryon decuplet and singlet multiplets give X=0, 3, 6​, with the same values for conjugate antibaryon multiplets. More generally, the zero flavour triality of a light-quark hadron imposes a+2b≡0(mod3) on multiplets constructed entirely from light quarks, antiquarks and gluons. Their Casimir values are given by the same formula for these labels. Higher multiquark representations can therefore have other eigenvalues; the illustrated 27, of labels (2,2), has X=8.

Choose the convention ψ′=gψ for a field in the defining representation and Dμ​=∂μ​+Aμ​. Requiring Dμ′​ψ′=gDμ​ψ gives the Yang-Mills gauge transformation
Aμ′​=gAμ​g−1−(∂μ​g)g−1.​
(17)
The gauge field strength is the curvature [Dμ​,Dν​]=Fμν​, so
Fμν′​=gFμν​g−1.​
(18)
For an adjoint field Y, the adjoint covariant derivative is Dμ​Y=∂μ​Y+[Aμ​,Y], and direct substitution gives Dμ′​Y′=g(Dμ​Y)g−1. Consequently Dμ​Fνρ​ transforms by conjugation too.
The Killing-form invariance under a Lie-group adjoint action gives κ(gYg−1,gZg−1)=κ(Y,Z). Its proof is that the adjoint matrices are conjugated by the same invertible map, leaving their trace product unchanged. With s=κ(Fμν​,Fμν), both sp and κ(Dμ​Fνρ​,DμFνρ) are therefore gauge invariant.
For their field equations, take a compactly supported variation aμ​=δAμ​. Curvature varies by
δFμν​=Dμ​aν​−Dν​aμ​.
(19)
The invariant form permits gauge-covariant integration by parts, because ∂μ​κ(Y,Z)=κ(Dμ​Y,Z)+κ(Y,Dμ​Z). Antisymmetry of F gives
δ(sp)=4psp−1κ(Dμ​aν​,Fμν).
(20)
Integrating, the power of the Yang-Mills invariant gives the equation
Dμ​(sp−1Fμν)=0.​
(21)
For p=1 this recovers the Yang-Mills equations. For larger p, keep the displayed product form, including at s=0.
For the derivative-squared action, write Bμνρ​=Dμ​Fνρ​. Varying the derivative as well as the curvature gives
δBμνρ​=Dμ​δFνρ​+[aμ​,Fνρ​].
(22)
Its first variation is
δS2​=2∫κ(DμFνρ,Dμ​δFνρ​)d4x+2∫κ(DμFνρ,[aμ​,Fνρ​])d4x.
(23)
Integrate the first term once, substitute δFνρ​=Dν​aρ​−Dρ​aν​, and integrate again. It becomes 4∫κ(aν​,Dμ​D2Fμν)d4x, where D2=Dλ​Dλ. Invariance converts the second term into 2∫κ(aν​,[Fρσ​,DνFρσ])d4x. Nondegeneracy of the Killing form and arbitrary variations yield the derivative-squared Yang-Mills action equation
2Dμ​Dλ​DλFμν+[Fρσ​,DνFρσ]=0.​
(24)
The derivative order matters because covariant derivatives generally do not commute. In an abelian limit the commutator term vanishes and the equation reduces to □∂μ​Fμν=0, a useful check on the fourth-order equation for the potential.

Consider positive-energy unitary irreducible representations of the proper orthochronous Poincaré group, allowing its double cover for physical half-integer spin. Use metric (+−−−) and define the Pauli-Lubanski pseudovector by
Wμ=−21​ϵμνρσd(Pν​)d(Mρσ​),ϵ0123=1.
(25)
This overall sign gives W=mJ at rest. The antisymmetric tensor and commuting translation generators give Pμ​Wμ=0. The given commutators say W commutes with translations and transforms as a Lorentz vector. Hence both P2 and W2 commute with the Poincare algebra; they are Casimir operators.
The commuting Hermitian translation generators admit generalized joint momentum eigenstates. A Lorentz transformation maps a state at p to a state at Λp, so an irreducible representation has momentum support on a single Lorentz orbit. Choose a standard momentum k. Its little group consists of the transformations fixing k and acts on the internal states there. Transport those internal states over the orbit by chosen transformations L(p)k=p. This gives the construction underlying Wigner's classification.
For timelike future-directed momenta, P2=m2 with m>0 and k=(m,0,0,0). The massive particle little group is SO(3), or SU(2) on the double cover. Write Ji​=ϵijk​d(Mjk)/2. The rotation commutators give [Ji​,Jj​]=iϵijk​Jk​, so the irreducible little-group representation has
J2=s(s+1)I,J3​ eigenvalues −s,−s+1,…,s.
(26)
At rest, W0=0 and W=mJ. Thus the massive Pauli-Lubanski Casimir is
P2=m2I,W2=−m2s(s+1)I.​
(27)
The rest spin space has dimension 2s+1. All s=0,21​,1,… occur for the double cover; only integer spin descends to an ordinary single-valued representation of the proper orthochronous Poincare group.
For the detailed representation, choose a Lorentz boost L(p) from k to every point of the positive-energy mass shell. With invariant normalization, states ∣p,σ⟩=U(L(p))∣k,σ⟩ transform as
U(Λ)∣p,σ⟩=∑τ​Dτσ(s)​(R(Λ,p))∣Λp,τ⟩,R(Λ,p)=L(Λp)−1ΛL(p).
(28)
The matrix R fixes k, so it is the Wigner rotation in the little group. Its cocycle identity
R(Λ2​Λ1​,p)=R(Λ2​,Λ1​p)R(Λ1​,p)
(29)
proves the representation law. Translations give phases; with the convention U(a)∣p,σ⟩=eip⋅a∣p,σ⟩, this extends to the massive induced representation of the Poincare double cover on
L2(Hm+​,2p0d3p​)⊗C2s+1.
(30)
The invariant measure and unitary spin matrices give unitarity. A closed invariant subspace restricts at fixed momentum to a little-group invariant spin subspace; irreducibility of that spin representation and transitivity over the mass shell force the full representation to be irreducible. Conversely the momentum-orbit construction recovers every massive positive-energy irreducible representation. The labels are therefore (m,s).
For a nonzero future null momentum choose k=(E,0,0,E). The massless particle little group is ISO(2). With Ki​=d(M0i), its massless little-group generators are
J3​,N1​=K1​−J2​,N2​=K2​+J1​,
(31)
with [J3​,N1​]=iN2​, [J3​,N2​]=−iN1​ and [N1​,N2​]=0. Evaluating the Pauli-Lubanski vector gives W2=−E2(N12​+N22​).
In a finite-dimensional unitary little-group representation, the joint spectrum of the commuting Hermitian N1​,N2​ is finite and invariant under continuous planar rotations. Its only possible values are zero. Thus finite-dimensional unitary representations of the massless little group have trivial translations. Irreducibility then leaves a one-dimensional rotation character of helicity h, giving
Wμ=hPμ,P2=W2=0.​
(32)
Helicity supplies an additional label that the two zero Casimirs cannot distinguish. It is integer for the ordinary group and may be half-integer on its physical double cover. Nontrivial little-group translations instead lead to an infinite-dimensional continuous-spin representation with W2<0; the Poincare algebra by itself allows these too. This completes the timelike and nonzero null cases of physical interest.
