There is a definitional qualification. If a compactly generated group means a group algebraically generated by a compact subset, the assertion as printed is false. In the usual topology on the additive group Q, the set
K={0}∪{1/n!:n≥1}
(8)
is compact and generates Q: every positive integer denominator divides some factorial. The group is metrizable, but it has no nonzero Haar measure finite on compact sets. Indeed, translation invariance gives a common singleton mass a≥0. If a>0, the infinite compact set K has infinite mass; if a=0, countable additivity gives zero mass to the whole countable group. This proves that compact generation does not imply local compactness or existence of Haar measure.
If the convention includes a compact generating neighbourhood, local compactness is already part of the hypothesis. More explicitly, the standard corrected result is every locally compact, compactly generated metrizable group has Haar measure. Here is a construction, rather than an invocation of that existence theorem.
Fix a nonzero h≥0 in the space of continuous compactly supported functions Cc​(G). For nonzero u≥0 in Cc​(G) and f≥0 in Cc​(G), define the covering number and its normalized functional by
(f:u)=inf{∑j=1r​aj​:f(t)≤∑j=1r​aj​u(xj−1​t) for all t, aj​>0},Iu​(f)=(h:u)(f:u)​.
(9)
These numbers are finite: translates of the region where u>0 cover the compact support of f, and a finite subcover, multiplied by a sufficiently large coefficient, dominates f. They are positive when f=0, since every cover has total coefficient at least ∥f∥∞​/∥u∥∞​. Composition of covers gives (f:u)≤(f:h)(h:u). Thus, with Mf​=(f:h),
0≤Iu​(f)≤Mf​,Iu​(h)=1.
(10)
The functionals are homogeneous, monotone, subadditive and exactly invariant under left translation; the last property follows by relabelling the covering translates.
The key step in Haar measure from normalized covering functionals is asymptotic additivity as suppu shrinks to e. Given f1​,f2​≥0, put f=f1​+f2​ and choose p∈Cc​(G) with p≥1 on both supports. For δ>0, set αi​=fi​/(f+δp), extended by zero where the denominator vanishes. These are continuous compactly supported functions and α1​+α2​≤1. For any η>0, a sufficiently small identity neighbourhood V ensures
∣αi​(xv)−αi​(x)∣<ηfor every x∈G, v∈V.
(11)
Take suppu⊆V and any cover f≤∑j​aj​Txj​​u, where Tx​u(t)=u(x−1t). Since fi​≤αi​f+δp, it follows that
(fi​:u)≤∑j​aj​(αi​(xj​)+η)+δ(p:u).
(12)
Adding and then taking the infimum over the cover of f gives
0≤Iu​(f1​)+Iu​(f2​)−Iu​(f)≤2ηMf​+2δMp​.
(13)
First make δ small, then η small, and finally the support of u small enough for the oscillation bound. The additivity defect therefore tends to zero.
Compact generation gives a countable compact cover by finite products of a compact symmetric generating set. Local compactness upgrades this to a compact exhaustion with interiors covering G. Metrizability supplies a countable identity-neighbourhood base and a countable family of nonnegative Cc​ functions dense in the uniform norm on each fixed compact support; this is the countable family whose existence may be assumed. Choose nonzero un​≥0 with supports shrinking to e. The uniform bounds Iun​​(f)≤Mf​ and a diagonal subsequence give convergence on that countable family. To extend convergence to every nonnegative f supported in a fixed compact L, choose a cutoff pL​≥1 on L. Monotonicity and subadditivity give
∣Iu​(f)−Iu​(g)∣≤Iu​(∣f−g∣)≤∥f−g∥∞​MpL​​
(14)
for f,g supported in L. Uniform approximation therefore makes the chosen subsequence Cauchy on every such f.
Let I(f) be its limit. Asymptotic additivity, homogeneity and invariance give an additive positive invariant functional on the nonnegative cone, with I(h)=1. Extend it by differences to real Cc​(G) and then complex linearly. It is bounded on each fixed compact support by the cutoff bound. The Riesz representation on compactly supported continuous functions consequently produces a positive Radon measure m with I(f)=∫fdm. Invariance of I and uniqueness of that representation imply m(xE)=m(E). The measure is nonzero because I(h)=1, and finite on compact sets by the cutoff estimate. Moreover, for any nonzero f≥0, composition of covers gives Iu​(f)≥1/(h:f)>0, so every nonempty open set has positive measure. Hence
m is a nonzero left Haar measure on the locally compact group.​
(15)
This completes the intended construction under the convention supplying local compactness, while the rational example settles the weaker literal interpretation.
