Include all fixed resource states and all measurement records in an environment, so the entire physical protocol has an linear isometry U from the unknown input to a larger pure output. If every recipient receives a perfect copy, its reduced density matrix is the pure projector ∣ψ⟩⟨ψ∣. A pure marginal forces factorization: write a joint vector in a basis beginning with ∣ψ⟩; its squared components along every orthogonal basis vector vanish because their marginal probabilities are zero. Applying this successively to all recipients gives
U(∣ψ⟩∣R⟩)=∣ψ⟩⊗N∣Eψ​⟩,
(1)
where ∣R⟩ is the fixed initial resource and ∣Eψ​⟩ may depend on the input. This includes Alice's discarded systems and every possible outcome record.
Choose two distinct nonorthogonal pure states, for example ∣0⟩ and ∣+⟩=(∣0⟩+∣1⟩)/2​, and write their overlap as s. Preservation of the inner product by the linear isometry requires
s=sN⟨Eψ​∣Eφ​⟩.
(2)
Normalized environment vectors have overlap of absolute value at most one. Thus 0<∣s∣<1 would imply ∣s∣≤∣s∣N, impossible for N>1. Hence perfect transmission of a copy to every recipient is impossible for N>1. This proves the no-cloning theorem obstruction even though Alice keeps no copy herself.

Write N=2k+1 and abbreviate the Dicke states by ∣Djn​⟩=∣Ψjn​⟩. Alice's shared resource has decomposition

21​[+++​∣00⟩(α∣DkN​⟩+β∣Dk+1N​⟩)∣01⟩(α∣Dk+1N​⟩+β∣DkN​⟩)∣10⟩(α∣DkN​⟩−β∣Dk+1N​⟩)∣11⟩(α∣Dk+1N​⟩−β∣DkN​⟩)].​

(5)
This is the encoding used in Dicke-resource telecloning. The extra Pauli X gate on Alice's resource qubit is essential to the displayed ordering of the amplitudes.

∣DkN​⟩∣Dk+1N​⟩​=Nk+1​​∣0⟩∣DkN−1​⟩+Nk​​∣1⟩∣Dk−1N−1​⟩,=Nk​​∣0⟩∣Dk+1N−1​⟩+Nk+1​​∣1⟩∣DkN−1​⟩.​

ρ=N1​(k+∣α∣2(k+1)α∗β​(k+1)αβ∗k+∣β∣2​),k=2N−1​.​

⟨ψ∣ρ∣ψ⟩​=Np(k+p)+q(k+q)+2(k+1)pq​=Nk+p2+q2+2(k+1)pq​=Nk+1+2kpq​=2NN+1+2∣α∣2∣β∣2(N−1)​​.​

Define E∣0⟩=∣DkN​⟩ and E∣1⟩=∣Dk+1N​⟩. From the branch calculation in part (b), outcome R0​=0,R1​=1 leaves
α∣Dk+1N​⟩+β∣DkN​⟩=E(β∣0⟩+α∣1⟩)=EX∣ψ⟩.
(9)
Thus the effective input teleported was X∣ψ⟩=β∣0⟩+α∣1⟩. A Pauli X gate flips every bit and hence maps a Dicke state with j excitations to one with N−j excitations. Since N−k=k+1, applying X at every receiving party interchanges the two logical basis states and restores E∣ψ⟩. Therefore
Bob and each other recipient should apply X.​
(10)
In particular Bob's uncorrected reduced density matrix is XρX, and his correction returns it to the matrix in part (c). The remaining branches can likewise be corrected: Z⊗N implements logical Z up to the common global phase (−1)k, while X⊗N implements logical X. Thus the outcome dependence can be removed by local Pauli gates even though the resulting copies remain imperfect.

Measuring the state from part (a) in the computational basis succeeds with probability pn​=sin2((2n+1)θ). In the sparse-solution regime 0<M≤2N−1, one has 0<θ≤π/4. The first success interval with pn​≥1/2 is π/4≤(2n+1)θ≤3π/4. Entering it requires n≥π/(8θ)−1/2, so the first half-probability Grover iterate is
nmin​=⌈8arcsinM/2N​π​−21​⌉.​
(15)
For this integer, the angle is at least π/4 and less than π/4+2θ≤3π/4, so the rounding cannot skip the required interval. Every smaller nonnegative integer has angle below π/4 and hence success below 1/2, proving minimality. For M≪2N, the leading size is nmin​∼(π/8)2N/M​. If M≥2N−1, the initial measurement already succeeds with probability at least one half and the smallest count is zero; if M=0 no search can succeed.

For the known bit string y, define the Pauli gate product Ty​=⨂j=0N−1​Zjyj​​. Its diagonal action is Ty​∣x⟩=(−1)x⋅y∣x⟩, where the binary inner product is taken modulo two. ∣Ψg​⟩=M​1​f(x)=1∑​(−1)x⋅y∣x⟩,∣Ψb​⟩=2N−M​1​f(x)=0∑​(−1)x⋅y∣x⟩.​

(17)

The Pauli matrices obey X∣0⟩=∣1⟩, X∣1⟩=∣0⟩, Y∣0⟩=i∣1⟩, and Y∣1⟩=−i∣0⟩. Thus X⊗X and Y⊗Y contribute opposite amplitudes on ∣00⟩,∣11⟩ and equal amplitudes on ∣01⟩,∣10⟩. For the XY exchange interaction E=(X⊗X+Y⊗Y)/2,
E∣00⟩=0,E∣01⟩=∣10⟩,E∣10⟩=∣01⟩,E∣11⟩=0.​
(20)
Equivalently E=∣01⟩⟨10∣+∣10⟩⟨01∣. It transfers an excitation across a link without creating or destroying excitations, so a sum of such links preserves the single-excitation subspace.

In the single-excitation subspace, part (a) turns each coupling into a hopping coefficient between adjacent sites. With ∣n⟩ denoting the excitation at site N+n, the two central links give
He​∣0⟩=2​J0​​(∣−1​⟩+∣1⟩).​
(21)
For N≥2 the links from these neighbors to the next sites have strength J1​. Applying the Hamiltonian operator a second time yields
He2​∣0⟩=J02​∣0⟩+2​J0​J1​​(∣−2​⟩+∣2⟩).​
(22)
The central coefficient is 2(J0​/2​)2=J02​. On the comparison path, direct multiplication gives
HT​∣0⟩=J0​∣1⟩,HT2​∣0⟩=J02​∣0⟩+J0​J1​∣2⟩.
(23)
Thus the two calculations match under ∣0⟩↦∣0⟩ and ∣j⟩↦(∣−j​⟩+∣j​⟩)/2​ for j=1,2. If N=1, the terms involving J1​ and distance two are absent on both sides.

Define orthonormal symmetric vectors
∣s0​⟩=∣0⟩,∣sj​⟩=2​∣−j​⟩+∣j​⟩​(1≤j≤N).
(24)
The symmetric-arm reduction of an exchange Hamiltonian here has two arms. To verify it directly, the link between radial distances j and j+1 has strength Jj​ on either side, while the two central links have strength J0​/2​. The calculation of part (a) therefore gives
He​∣s0​⟩He​∣sj​⟩He​∣sN​⟩​=J0​∣s1​⟩,=Jj−1​∣sj−1​⟩+Jj​∣sj+1​⟩(1≤j<N),=JN−1​∣sN−1​⟩.​
(25)
In particular this subspace is invariant and contains the initial vector. Define the linear isometry F∣j⟩=∣sj​⟩. The displayed matrix entries prove He​F=FHT​, or F†He​F=HT​. Induction gives Hem​F=FHTm​ for every nonnegative integer m; hence the convergent exponential series proves e−iHe​tF=Fe−iHT​t. This establishes the mapping for the entire evolution, rather than just its first two powers. The supplied endpoint transfer also forces every Jj​ to be nonzero, since a broken link would disconnect the two endpoints. Successive powers applied to ∣s0​⟩ then generate all ∣sj​⟩, so this is exactly the cyclic subspace governing the initial state.
Using the stated perfect quantum state transfer identity at time π/2,
e−iHe​π/2∣0⟩=F∣N⟩=2​∣−N​⟩+∣N⟩​.​
(26)
The two terms have the excitation at sites 0 and 2N, respectively, and all intermediate qubits are zero in both terms. The full state thus factors into a Bell state at the endpoints and the vacuum on the interior. Its partial trace is
ρ0,2N​=∣Ψ+⟩⟨Ψ+∣,∣Ψ+⟩=2​∣10⟩+∣01⟩​.​
(27)
The coherent cross terms survive the trace because the interior vectors coincide. We have used the transfer phase convention supplied in the question; a common transfer phase would leave this density operator unchanged.

Replace the two arms by three identical arms of length N attached to one central qubit. Label the hub c and the sites on arm a by (a,j), a=1,2,3, j=1,…,N. Use XY exchange interactions with coupling J0​/3​ on each hub-to-first-site link, and coupling Jj​ on each arm link (a,j) to (a,j+1). Choose an orthonormal eigenbasis of V, with eigenphases ϕ0​,ϕ1​, counting multiplicity. The maximally mixed state is basis independent, so
ρ=21​∣v0​⟩⟨v0​∣+21​∣v1​⟩⟨v1​∣.
(37)
Linearity means that the experiment is the equal mixture of the two eigenstate experiments. After the inverse quantum Fourier transform, the joint state is 21​∑j=01​∣χj​⟩⟨χj​∣⊗∣vj​⟩⟨vj​∣, where ∣χj​⟩=∑m​am​(ϕj​)∣m⟩ and
am​(ϕ)=L1​∑x=0L−1​eix(ϕ−2πm/L).
(38)
Thus the exact output distribution for quantum phase estimation with a maximally mixed target is
Pr(m)=21​∣am​(ϕ0​)∣2+21​∣am​(ϕ1​)∣2,m=0,…,L−1.​
(39)
For a nonzero phase mismatch, the squared probability amplitude is sin2[L(ϕ−2πm/L)/2]/{L2sin2[(ϕ−2πm/L)/2]}, with value one at an exact match. If both eigenphases are exactly representable on the L-point grid, their two labels occur with probability 1/2 each; coincident labels have their probabilities added. For general eigenphases there can be nonzero probabilities at other labels, so it would not be exact to list only the two best approximations with probability 1/2 each. Each eigenphase is sampled with spectral weight 1/2, and its nearest-label event has conditional probability at least 4/π2. In particular a nearest label for one eigenphase has unconditional probability at least 2/π2, with additional contributions possible from the other phase.

With access to the uncontrolled physical gate, use single-qubit process tomography. Prepare the positive eigenstate of each of X,Y,Z, apply V, and on independent copies measure each of X,Y,Z. Arbitrary single-qubit rotation gates let one prepare these inputs and rotate each measurement into the computational basis. The nine measured means are
Rij​=Tr[σi​V2I+σj​​V†]=21​Tr(σi​Vσj​V†),i,j∈{x,y,z}.
(40)
They determine the real rotation matrix R of the Bloch vector, and hence the action of V on every qubit state. To see precisely what this identifies, suppose another unitary U has the same action. Then V†U commutes with every Pauli matrix. Commuting with Z makes it diagonal; commuting with X makes its diagonal entries equal. Therefore U=eiγV.
Consequently tomography determines V, and its eigenvalues, up to a common phase. More explicitly, write a representative as V=eiγ(cosaI−isinan⋅σ). Its eigenvalues are ei(γ−a) and ei(γ+a), and its Bloch vector action is rotation through 2a about n. The measured rotation identifies the unordered relative eigenphase information; for example TrR=1+2cos(2a). Once a representative with a specified overall phase is supplied, diagonalizing that representative gives the corresponding two eigenvalues.
The common phase itself cannot be recovered with the stated uncontrolled access. Indeed, (eiγV)ρ(eiγV)†=VρV† for every input, including inputs entangled with ancillas. Replacing V by eiγV therefore leaves every such preparation-and-measurement experiment unchanged, whereas it multiplies both eigenvalues by eiγ. For example, I and eiγI are indistinguishable physical gates under these operations. This proves that the request for absolute eigenvalue phases needs an additional phase reference or controlled-gate assumption: the global phase of an uncontrolled quantum gate is unobservable. The controlled powers in the earlier parts supply that extra resource, since diag(I,eiγV) changes a relative phase between the two control branches.
The practical advantages are that no unknown eigenstate preparation, controlled powers, entangling gates or inverse quantum Fourier transform are needed: a fixed set of single-qubit preparations and measurements suffices. The disadvantages are statistical estimation and the phase ambiguity just proved. Estimating the bounded Pauli measurement means to additive accuracy ϵ at fixed confidence requires order ϵ−2 independent repetitions, giving the usual sampling cost for this direct procedure. The earlier quantum phase estimation circuit uses N phase qubits and high controlled powers to resolve scale 2−N in one coherent experiment with a constant success bound; constructing those powers from controlled uses of V costs 2N−1 gate calls, so its exponential-looking precision is not obtained with only N uses of V. Repetitions can raise confidence. Finally, full process tomography grows exponentially with the number of system qubits, whereas the eigenstate-based phase procedure can estimate a selected eigenphase without reconstructing the entire operator.
