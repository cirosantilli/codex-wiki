The nucleons are nonrelativistic, so the Maxwell-Boltzmann distribution gives
ni​=gi​(2πmi​T​)3/2e(μi​−mi​)/T,i=n,p.
(38)
Both spin degeneracies are two. Taking the ratio and using the specified negligible chemical potentials yields the neutron-to-proton ratio
rF​=(mp​mn​​)3/2e−(mn​−mp​)/TF​≃e−Q/TF​​.
(39)
The mass prefactor differs from unity only at the per-mille level. The dependence on TF​ is much stronger: dlnrF​/dlnTF​=Q/TF​>0. Thus earlier, hotter weak freeze-out leaves more neutrons relative to protons. For illustration, TF​=1MeV gives rF​≃0.28; TF​=0.8MeV gives rF​≃0.20. Free-neutron decay after freeze-out lowers the ratio further before most Big Bang nucleosynthesis takes place.
