Before electron-positron annihilation in cosmology, photons contribute two helicity states; Electrons and positrons contribute two spin states each. Each of the three light neutrino flavours contributes one active neutrino helicity and one antineutrino helicity. At a common temperature the effective number of relativistic energy degrees of freedom is therefore
g∗​=2+87​(4+6)=443​=10.75.​
(35)
Using GF​=1.17×10−5GeV−2 and MPl​≃2.435×1018GeV in the scaling formula alone gives TF​∼2MeV. Including the explicit expansion coefficient from (ii), but setting the unknown rate coefficient A to one, gives TF​≃1.5MeV. Thus the warranted conclusion is a freeze-out temperature of order 1MeV, above the 0.511MeV Electron mass. The omitted weak-rate coefficient prevents a more precise prediction from this estimate.
Neutrino decoupling occurs before most electron-positron pairs annihilate. Afterwards neutrinos redshift freely, Tν​a=constant, while the photons receive the pair entropy. For the still-coupled photon-pair gas, cosmological entropy conservation gives g∗s​Tγ3​a3=constant. Its entropy degrees of freedom drop from 2+(7/8)4=11/2 to 2. Comparing this with the freely cooling neutrinos gives
Tγ​Tν​​=(114​)1/3,Tν​Tγ​​=(411​)1/3.​
(36)
After annihilation, photon and neutrino temperatures differ. To write the total energy density as (π2/30)g∗​Tγ4​, the neutrino contribution must include that temperature ratio:
g∗​=2+87​6(114​)4/3≃3.36.​
(37)
This is the paper's post-annihilation Neff​. Merely deleting the Electron term while keeping all species at the same temperature would be incorrect. For comparison the entropy count, normalized to Tγ3​, is g∗s​=2+(7/8)6(4/11)=43/11≃3.91. These results assume instantaneous neutrino decoupling and relativistic neutrinos; the two counts represent different thermal moments.
