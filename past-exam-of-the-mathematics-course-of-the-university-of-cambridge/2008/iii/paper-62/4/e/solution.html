First consider kτeq​<1. Such a mode remains outside the horizon through matter-radiation equality, grows as τ2 on both sides, and keeps the same matter-era growth after entry. Squaring this growth preserves the shape of the HPZ spectrum:
P(k,τ)∼Cτ4k,kτeq​<1,
(53)
up to a scale-independent matching coefficient.
For kτeq​≫1, entry occurs in radiation domination at τH​∼k−1, with PH​∼Ck−3. The density amplitude then grows only logarithmically until equality. Thus
δeq​∼δH​ln(kτeq​),Peq​∼Ck−3ln2(kτeq​).
(54)
During matter domination, the matter-era linear growth factor of the density is (τ/τeq​)2, so its square multiplies the matter power spectrum:
P(k,τ)∼C(τeq​τ​)4k−3ln2(kτeq​),kτeq​≫1.​
(55)
The turnover at k∼τeq−1​ records whether a mode entered during radiation domination. Earlier-entering small-scale modes miss the rapid τ2 growth and receive only the logarithmic Mészáros effect. This is the origin of the logarithmic small-scale matter transfer. The formula is a large-k asymptote: the additive constant in the logarithmic solution and smooth equality matching must be retained near kτeq​=1, where the spectrum does not vanish.
Multiplying by k3 gives the small-scale mass-fluctuation variance
⟨(MδM​)k2​⟩∼C(τeq​τ​)4ln2(kτeq​).​
(56)
This gives the requested estimate for modes entering before equality, with the logarithm replaced by a smooth order-one factor in the transition region. On comoving scale L∼k−1, unit variance today requires
C∼ln2(τeq​/L)(τeq​/τ0​)4​.​
(57)
For a numerical illustration faithful to this question's flat, matter-plus-radiation background, take h=0.7 and Ωr​=4.2×10−5h−2, hence ΩC​=1−Ωr​≃1 rather than the 0.3 appropriate to a vacuum-dominated model. Part 1(b), now with Ωm​=ΩC​, gives τeq​≃33Mpc. In this two-component model the same integral is valid all the way to today, giving
τ0​=H0​ΩC​2​(Ωr​+ΩC​​−Ωr​​)≃8.5×103Mpc.
(58)
With L=3Mpc, τ0​/τeq​≃258 and ln(τeq​/L)≃2.39. Therefore C∼[2584(2.39)2]−1≃4×10−11, corresponding to a horizon-entry root-mean-square fluctuation of roughly 6×10−6. A very small primordial amplitude, of order 10−10 in this schematic normalization, can reach unit small-scale variance by today. Factors from the Fourier convention, smoothing window and equality matching preclude a precise normalization here. The calculation marks the onset of nonlinear structure formation, where extrapolating the linear solution ceases to be valid. For a universe with late vacuum domination, one must replace the matter-only growth law by its actual growth factor before making a numerical inference.
