Let G1​=ϕ(r∣r1​) and G2​=ϕ(r∣r2​). Green's second identity gives
∫Ω​[G1​(Δ+k02​)G2​−G2​(Δ+k02​)G1​],dV=∫S​(G1​∂n​G2​−G2​∂n​G1​),dS.
(13)
The common Robin boundary condition makes the surface integrand vanish. Where β=0, substitute ∂n​Gi​=−αGi​/β; a Dirichlet portion also contributes zero. The delta sources therefore give the Helmholtz Green-function reciprocity
ϕ(r1​∣r2​)=ϕ(r2​∣r1​).​
(14)
This is a bilinear identity, with no complex conjugation. Assume the boundary-value problem has a unique Green kernel; in an exterior problem use the same outgoing radiation condition for both kernels.
For the weighted operator, assume smooth positive f and set b=∇logf. Then
L=f1​∇⋅(f∇)+k02​=Δ+b⋅∇+k02​.
(15)
Its unweighted transpose, obtained by integration by parts, is
LTu=Δu−∇⋅(bu)+k02​u=∇⋅[f∇(u/f)]+k02​u.
(16)
The associated boundary identity is
∫Ω​(uLv−vLTu),dV=∫S​[u∂n​v−v∂n​u+(∂n​logf)uv],dS.
(17)
To cancel it when v satisfies the given Robin condition, the required adjoint kernel satisfies
∇⋅[f∇(fψ​​)]+k02​ψ​=δ(r−r2​),​
(18)
αψ​+β(∂n​ψ​−ψ​∂n​logf)=0.​
(19)
Using these two delta sources in the boundary identity proves ψ(r2​∣r1​)=ψ​(r1​∣r2​), the adjoint reciprocity for a weighted Helmholtz operator. As a check, LT(fv)=fLv, so
ψ​(r∣r2​)=f(r2​)f(r)​ψ(r∣r2​).
(20)
This is equivalent to weighted acoustic Green-function reciprocity, f(r2​)ψ(r2​∣r1​)=f(r1​)ψ(r1​∣r2​). Unit delta normalization is essential here. A Hermitian adjoint would instead conjugate coefficients and change the radiation convention; the requested equality uses the transpose.
