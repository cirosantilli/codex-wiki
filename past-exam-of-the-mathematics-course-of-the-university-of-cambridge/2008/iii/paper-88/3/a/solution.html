Write h=logψ and q=2ϵhθ​, with ϵ>0. Direct differentiation gives
qZ​−qqθ​−ϵqθθ​=2ϵ∂θ​[hZ​−ϵ(hθθ​+hθ2​)]=2ϵ∂θ​(ψψZ​−ϵψθθ​​).
(21)
Thus the Cole-Hopf transformation with this negative-flux convention maps the heat equation to the stated viscous Burgers equation. Conversely its residual is independent of θ and can be removed by multiplying ψ by a function of Z.
Convolving each initial exponential with the Gaussian heat kernel gives
ψ(θ,Z)=∑j=1N​eAj​,Aj​=2ϵqj​(θ−θj​)+qj2​Z/2​.
(22)
The resulting finite-exponential Cole-Hopf solution is
q(θ,Z)=∑j​eAj​∑j​qj​eAj​​.​
(23)
It is a softmax function average of the constant states, so q1​≤q≤qN​ and qθ​=Varw​(qj​)/(2ϵ)≥0.
For N=2, put Δ=q2​−q1​>0 and q​=(q1​+q2​)/2. Equality of the two exponents occurs at
θs​(Z)=Δq2​θ2​−q1​θ1​​−q​Z.
(24)
Therefore
q=q​+2Δ​tanh4ϵΔ(θ−θs​)​.​
(25)
This is an increasing viscous shock from q1​ to q2​, moving at speed −q​ and with transition width of order ϵ/Δ. That speed agrees with the Rankine-Hugoniot condition for flux −q2/2; its concavity makes the increasing jump the entropy shock.
For general N, use the shock frame θ=−(q1​+qN​)Z/2+ξ. The leading Z coefficient in Aj​ is qj​(qj​−q1​−qN​)/(4ϵ). The endpoint coefficients coincide, and every interior coefficient is smaller by (qj​−q1​)(qN​−qj​)/(4ϵ)>0. The extremal-state asymptotics of exponential Burgers solutions therefore reduce the large-Z profile to the same tanh shock with states q1​,qN​ and centre
θs​=qN​−q1​qN​θN​−q1​θ1​​−2q1​+qN​​Z.
(26)
At a fixed θ, rather than in the moving frame, the endpoint of largest ∣qj​∣ dominates. If qN​=−q1​, both endpoint rates coincide and the limiting shock is stationary. Intermediate plateaus disappear in the moving-frame limit.
