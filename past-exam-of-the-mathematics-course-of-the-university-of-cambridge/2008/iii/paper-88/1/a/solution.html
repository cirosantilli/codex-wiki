Let R=∣x∣, n=x/R and tR​=t−R/c0​. Convolving the source terms in the Lighthill acoustic analogy with the retarded acoustic Green function, and moving their spatial derivatives outside the integral, gives
ρ′=∂xi​​∂xj​​∫4πc02​∣x−y∣Tij​(y,t−∣x−y∣/c0​)​d3y−∂xi​​∫4πc02​∣x−y∣fi​(y,t−∣x−y∣/c0​)​d3y.
(1)
The integrals have compact spatial support. For an acoustically compact source, its size l satisfies ωl/c0​≪1, so the source-dependent retardation is negligible at leading order. Also take R≫l. Define Sij​=∫Tij​d3y and Fi​=∫fi​d3y. In the radiation terms the spatial derivatives act on tR​, with ∂xi​​tR​=−ni​/c0​. Derivatives of 1/R produce smaller near-field terms. Thus the far-field acoustic force and stress moments give
ρ′(x,t)=4πc04​Rni​nj​S¨ij​(tR​)​+4πc03​Rni​F˙i​(tR​)​.​
(2)
Using ni​=xi​/R gives the equivalent powers of R requested. The force term is an acoustic dipole; the Lighthill stress tensor produces an acoustic quadrupole.
For a small body in low-speed flow, put M=u/c0​≪1. A typical unsteady force has size F=O(ρ0​u2l2) and time scale l/u, whereas S=O(ρ0​u2l3). Therefore
ρ0​ρD′​​=O(Rl​M3),ρ0​ρQ′​​=O(Rl​M4).​
(3)
These are the acoustic dipole Mach-number scaling and compact acoustic quadrupole Mach-number scaling. Since p′=c02​ρ′ and outgoing acoustic intensity is of order p′2/(ρ0​c0​), their sound powers scale as
PD​=O(ρ0​u6l2/c03​),PQ​=O(ρ0​u8l2/c05​).
(4)
Thus an unsteady force on the body can produce the dominant low-Mach number sound. If the integrated force or its time derivative vanishes, that dipole contribution is absent. Force on the body and force on the fluid have opposite signs.
