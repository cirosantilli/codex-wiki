The first matrix contains the leave-one-out samples, and each element of vect is their sample mean. For n≥2,
x(−i)​=n−1nx−xi​​,n1​∑i​x(−i)​=x.
(32)
Thus R4a computes the Jackknife variance estimator of the full sample mean:
nn−1​∑i​(x(−i)​−x)2=n(n−1)1​∑i​(xi​−x)2=sx2​/n​,
(33)
where sx2​ is the unbiased sample variance. R5a computes the estimated bias used in jackknife bias correction. The identity for the average leave-one-out sample mean gives R5a = 0, apart from floating-point rounding.
The second printed loop contains an indexing error: its row index is i, although its loop variable is b. Literally, after the first block, i equals n. When n≤199, every iteration overwrites row n of the new matrix; the other rows remain missing. Hence vect contains one finite mean and 198 missing entries. R's default sorting removes missing entries, leaving a vector of length one, so selecting its fifth and 195th entries gives R7b = c(NA, NA). If n>199, the matrix assignment is out of bounds; if the second block runs independently with i undefined, it fails even earlier. This is a source error, not a property of the bootstrap.
Replacing just the row index by b gives the intended algorithm: draw 199 independent bootstrap samples and sort their sample means as s1​≤⋯≤s199​. The two indices are 200(0.05/2)=5 and 200(1−0.05/2)=195, so
intended R7b=(s5​,s195​).​
(34)
These are the endpoints of an approximate 95% percentile bootstrap confidence interval for the population mean. Their actual numerical values depend on the supplied data and random draws, which are not specified. They are not the reflected endpoints of a basic bootstrap confidence interval, and the choice of ranks does not guarantee exact finite-sample coverage.
