A reversible-jump Markov chain Monte Carlo calculation compares normalized model-specific densities. The fixed-order posterior written only up to proportionality in part (a) omits normalizing factors that depend on k and therefore cannot simply be canceled between dimensions. Moreover, the printed question does not specify the model-order prior distribution or the probabilities of selecting birth and death moves. Denote those by pk​, bk​ and dk​, respectively, retaining them in the answer.
Put s=σ2, c=n−1∑i​xik+1​, vi​=xik+1​−c, and r=y−Xk​β. The centered polynomial birth move in reversible-jump sampling changes the fitted vector by zv, so its new residual is r′=r−zv. Therefore
∥r′∥2−∥r∥2=−2zvTr+z2vTv.
(44)
The map from (β0​,…,βk​,z) to (β0′​,…,βk+1′​) has ones down the diagonal and a single additional entry −c in the intercept row. Its Jacobian determinant is one.
Write Qk​=(β−μk​)TΣk−1​(β−μk​) and Qk+1​=(β′−μk+1​)TΣk+1−1​(β′−μk+1​). The normalized multivariate normal distribution prior ratio is
πk​(β)πk+1​(β′)​=(2π)−1/2∣Σk+1​∣∣Σk​∣​​exp[−21​(Qk+1​−Qk​)].
(45)
Divide also by the forward proposal density q(z)=(2πσβ2​)−1/2e−z2/(2σβ2​). The common inverse-gamma distribution prior for s and the common normal-likelihood normalization cancel, since s stays fixed. The forward acceptance ratio is consequently
R+​=pk​bk​pk+1​dk+1​​σβ​∣Σk+1​∣∣Σk​∣​​exp[szvTr−z2vTv/2​−2Qk+1​−Qk​​+2σβ2​z2​],A+​=min(1,R+​).​
(46)
Here σβ​>0 is the proposal standard deviation. If equal model priors and symmetric birth/death selection are explicitly adopted, their ratios become one; they are not determined by the PDF. The expression assumes the same hyperparameters for the variance prior in both models, as in part (a). A dimension-dependent variance prior would contribute its own ratio.
