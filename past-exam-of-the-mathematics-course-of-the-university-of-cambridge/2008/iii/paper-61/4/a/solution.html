The feedback law printed in the PDF is not sufficient for the asserted monotonicity. We first derive the exact identity, then give a counterexample and the corrected law. Both density operators evolve unitarily, so Trρ2 and Trρd2​ are constant. Thus
V=∥ρ−ρd​∥HS2​=Trρ2+Trρd2​−2Tr(ρd​ρ).
(24)
Cyclicity of the trace gives Tr([−iH0​,ρd​]ρ)=−Tr(ρd​[−iH0​,ρ]). These drift terms cancel in the derivative of the overlap, leaving
dtd​Tr(ρd​ρ)=fC,C=Tr(ρd​[−iH1​,ρ]),V˙=−2fC.​
(25)
Here C is real: −i[H1​,ρ] is Hermitian, and the trace of the product of two Hermitian matrices is real. This is the Hilbert-Schmidt Lyapunov feedback identity.
For a direct counterexample to the source's overlap law, take H0​=0, H1​=σy​/2, ρd​=(I+σz​)/2 and ρ=(I+σx​)/2. All are admissible qubit operators and states. The printed overlap is f=Tr(ρd​ρ)=1/2, but −i[H1​,ρ]=−σz​/2 gives C=−1/2. Hence V˙=1/2>0: the distance initially increases. This proves that feedback based on state overlap need not be stabilizing.
The intended Lyapunov quantum control replaces the overlap by its control-direction derivative:
f=kTr(ρd​[−iH1​,ρ]),k>0,V˙=−2kC2≤0.​
(26)
The conclusion is nonincrease, not strict decrease or automatic convergence. For example, opposite computational-basis pure states, diagonal H0​, and H1​=σx​ give C=0 and zero control even though V=2. Target convergence requires further invariant-set and reachability conditions. A target with a different spectrum is also inaccessible under Quantum Hamiltonian evolution. The density-operator derivatives in the governing equations use the dots visible in the PDF, which the TeX loses.
