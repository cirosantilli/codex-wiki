The local gauge symmetries of the Polyakov action are worldsheet diffeomorphisms and Weyl transformations. Under a reparametrization σα↦σ′α, the embedding coordinates Xμ transform as scalars and the worldsheet metric as a covariant tensor; the contracted integrand is a scalar density. Infinitesimally, with the active convention, δX=−ξα∂α​X and δγ=−Lξ​γ. A Weyl transformation sends γαβ​↦e2ωγαβ​ and leaves X fixed. In two dimensions the factors from −γ​ and γαβ cancel. Target-space Poincaré transformations are additional global symmetries, rather than worldsheet gauge symmetries.
These gauge freedoms put the metric locally in conformal gauge, γαβ​=ηαβ​. Its variation must still be imposed: it gives the worldsheet stress tensor constraints T++​=T−−​=0. The field equation is the wave equation, and periodicity of a closed string gives independent left- and right-moving oscillator sets. In a normalization with α0μ​=α0μ​=α′/2​kμ, their commutators and normal-ordered generators are
[αmμ​,αnν​]=mημνδm+n,0​,Ln​=21​∑r∈Z​:αn−r​⋅αr​:,L0​=4α′k2​+N,
(1)
with the same formulas for the tilded sector and zero mixed commutators.
The closed-string physical-state Virasoro conditions in the critical flat-space theory are
Ln>0​∣Φ⟩=Ln>0​∣Φ⟩=0,(L0​−1)∣Φ⟩=(L0​−1)∣Φ⟩=0.​
(2)
They act on the oscillator Fock space, and physical spurious (null) states are quotiented out. Equivalently physical states are the suitable BRST cohomology classes. Positive modes constrain kets; their adjoints constrain bras, so one does not impose every positive and negative matter mode on the same ket. Subtracting the two zero-mode equations gives closed-string level matching, N=N, and adding their mass-shell content gives M2=4(N−1)/α′. Quantum consistency fixes the intercept to one and, for just these flat coordinate fields, d=26: the matter central charge d cancels the reparametrization ghost central charge −26. Other dimensions require additional worldsheet degrees of freedom or a noncritical completion.
For the residual symmetry, take σ±=τ±σ. The conformal Killing equations leave independent transformations σ+↦f(σ+) and σ−↦f​(σ−), with a compensating Weyl rescaling restoring the flat metric. On the Euclidean plane the corresponding holomorphic vector fields are ℓn​=−zn+1∂z​ and their antiholomorphic partners. They satisfy the Witt algebra [ℓm​,ℓn​]=(m−n)ℓm+n​. The conserved contour generators are the Virasoro modes Ln​=(2πi)−1∮zn+1T(z)dz. Their quantum Virasoro algebra is
[Lm​,Ln​]=(m−n)Lm+n​+12d​(m3−m)δm+n,0​,[Lm​,Ln​]=0,
(3)
with a second independent copy for the right movers. These are the generators of the residual conformal transformations, including their quantum central extension.
The two-dimensional quantum-gravity interpretation of strings follows from what is integrated, not from an analogy: at genus h, the Polyakov path integral integrates over both X and the metric, divided by diffeomorphism and Weyl gauge volume. String perturbation theory sums these surface topologies, weighted by gs2h−2​ before external-state coupling factors. Gauge fixing leaves worldsheet ghost determinants and integrals over worldsheet moduli. Thus it is two-dimensional quantum gravity coupled to the d coordinate matter fields. A flat gauge is local; general higher-genus surfaces retain conformal-structure moduli and need not admit a globally flat representative.
Target-space gravity is a different conclusion. At N=N=1, the state ϵμν​α−1μ​α−1ν​∣k⟩ is massless, transverse in both indices, and has longitudinal gauge identifications. Its symmetric traceless transverse polarization is a graviton; the antisymmetric and scalar polarizations give the Kalb–Ramond field and dilaton. The graviton gauge identification is precisely the linearized metric diffeomorphism. Moreover the leading string-frame massless effective action contains ∫−G​e−2ΦR, and worldsheet Weyl invariance with constant dilaton and zero two-form requires Rμν​=0 at leading order in α′. Thus both the propagating massless spin-two state and its low-energy interactions reproduce gravity. Closed strings contain the graviton and yield Einstein gravity in their low-energy massless sector, together with other fields and higher-derivative corrections; the bosonic tachyon instability remains.

Start by using the metric equation of the Polyakov action to retain the Virasoro constraints after choosing conformal gauge. In the light-cone gauge in string theory, make X+ proportional to worldsheet time on a patch with nonzero k+. The constraints determine X− from the transverse coordinates, so its oscillators are not independent physical excitations. Neumann boundary conditions make each transverse coordinate a cosine standing wave on the open interval. Quantize these waves as independent harmonic oscillators and build their Fock space on the momentum-labelled vacuum. There are d−2=24 transverse directions in the critical bosonic string, and the normal-ordering intercept is one. Thus physical oscillator states have nonnegative occupation numbers rn,i​ and level p=∑n,i​nrn,i​, with M2=(p−1)/α′. Continuous center-of-mass momentum is not part of the discrete oscillator degeneracy.
For each mode n and transverse direction, summing its occupation numbers gives ∑r≥0​wnr=(1−wn)−1. Therefore the open-string oscillator degeneracy generating function is
F(w)=∑p≥0​dp​wp=∏n≥1​(1−wn)−24,dp​=2πi1​∮F(w)w−p−1dw.​
(4)
This is the Cauchy coefficient formula. The original PDF's power w−p is an index error: it extracts dp−1​, and would give zero for the vacuum p=0, which actually has one oscillator state. Any positively oriented circle strictly inside the unit disk works.
Put w=e−t with t>0. Expand the logarithm and sum the geometric series to obtain
logF(e−t)=24∑m≥1​m(emt−1)1​.
(5)
Since t/(emt−1)≤1/m, dominated convergence gives tlogF(e−t)→24∑m≥1​m−2=4π2. Thus the leading singular term is 4π2/t.
The subleading term printed in the PDF also needs correction: the product implies +12logϵ, rather than −6logϵ. To obtain the coefficient needed for the final power law, use τ=it/(2π) and the Dedekind eta function. Its definition gives F(e−t)=e−tη(τ)−24. The modular identity η(−1/τ)=−iτ​η(τ), recorded in NIST's eta transformation formulas, and the product at large imaginary argument give
F(e−t)=(2πt​)12exp(t4π2​−t)[1+O(e−4π2/t)].​
(6)
Since t=−log(1−ϵ)=ϵ+ϵ2/2+O(ϵ3), this yields
logF(1−ϵ)=ϵ4π2​+12logϵ−12log(2π)−2π2+O(ϵ).
(7)
The printed logarithmic correction cannot be used to derive the stated p−27/4 prefactor; the corrected correction does.
For the saddle-point approximation, change the coefficient contour to t=−logw. Its local contribution is
dp​≃2πi1​∫(2πt​)12eS(t)dt,S(t)=(p−1)t+t4π2​.
(8)
Set q=p−1. The positive saddle is t0​=2π/q​, with S(t0​)=4πq​ and S′′(t0​)=q3/2/π. Along the local vertical steepest-descent direction t=t0​+iy, the exponent is S(t0​)−S′′(t0​)y2/2 to quadratic order. The factor (t/2π)12 varies negligibly over its Gaussian width. Hence
dp​∼2πS′′(t0​)​(t0​/2π)12​eS(t0​)=2​1​q−27/4e4πq​∼2​1​p−27/4e4πp​.​
(9)
This proves the large-level degeneracy of an open bosonic string, including the otherwise unspecified leading constant. The factor p−6 comes from the eta prefactor and p−3/4 from the saddle width. As allowed, no bound on the remote part of the contour is needed.
At high mass, p≃α′M2, so the density grows exponentially as eβH​M with
βH​=4πα′​,TH​=4πα′​1​​
(10)
in units kB​=ℏ=c=1. A canonical sum with e−βM cannot converge for β<βH​, regardless of polynomial prefactors. This suggests a Hagedorn threshold and a breakdown or transition of the ordinary dilute-string thermal description. The power prefactor can make the partition sum itself finite at the threshold while sufficiently high energy moments diverge; the asymptotic count alone does not prove an absolute upper temperature. These are properties of the formal high-level spectrum, separate from the tachyon instability of the bosonic vacuum.

First regulate the Gaussian functional integral, separating any constant zero mode. Integration by parts gives S[ϕ]=21​(ϕ,Aϕ) with positive kinetic operator A=−Δ on the nonzero modes. Complete the square:
21​(ϕ,Aϕ)+(J,ϕ)=21​(ϕ+A−1J,A(ϕ+A−1J))−21​(J,A−1J).
(11)
A translation of the regulated integration variables then yields
Z[J]=Nexp[21​(J,A−1J)]=Nexp[−21​(J,GJ)]​,G=−A−1=Δ−1.
(12)
The sign is essential: the kernel in the negative source exponent is the negative of the positive Gaussian covariance. The source-independent prefactor is formally N=∫Dχe−(χ,Aχ)/2∝(det′A)−1/2, with the prime denoting the excluded zero mode and normalization depending on the regulated measure. This is the Euclidean scalar source functional and Laplacian sign convention.
Consequently Δz​G(z,w)=δ(2)(z−w). In the plane,
G(z,w)=2π1​logL∣z−w∣​​
(13)
up to harmonic additions fixed by boundary and infrared conditions. Away from w this logarithm is harmonic. Integrating its outward normal derivative around a small circle centered at w gives 2π, establishing the distributional delta coefficient. The arbitrary infrared length L cancels for a source of zero total integral. On a compact regulated worldsheet the zero-mode-projected equation has the corresponding constant subtraction from the delta function.
For the string action IE​=(4πα′)−1∫(∂X)2, the nonzero-mode covariance is
⟨Xμ(z)Xν(w)⟩0​=−α′ημνlogL∣z−w∣​.
(14)
Compute initially with Euclidean target coordinates and restore target Lorentzian contractions by analytic continuation. Treat the physical tachyon vertices as normal-ordered exponentials; this removes coincident self-contractions. Completing the Gaussian square with the imaginary delta-function sources, or applying Wick contractions, gives
⟨∏r=14​:eikr​⋅X(zr​):⟩nonzero​=∏r<s​∣zr​−zs​∣α′kr​⋅ks​
(15)
after fixing the vertex normalization. Each cross-contraction has the sign i2=−1, canceling the negative logarithmic covariance, which explains the positive exponent.
The constant target-space mode must also be integrated:
∫ddx0​eix0​⋅∑r​kr​=(2π)dδ(d)(∑r​kr​).
(16)
This momentum-conservation factor is momentum dependent and cannot literally be absorbed into the PDF's momentum-independent N. Its intended position integral is the amplitude with this overall delta function stripped off, restricted to conserving momenta. In that convention,
A4​=N∫q=1∏4​d2zq​r<s∏​∣zr​−zs​∣α′kr​⋅ks​.​
(17)
The logarithmic infrared constant only gives a fixed normalization on the common external mass shell, since ∑r<s​kr​⋅ks​=−21​∑r​kr2​. Thus the Gaussian determinant and normalization are independent of the scattering invariants. To make the tree position integral finite one also divides by the residual Möbius gauge volume, equivalently fixes three insertions with their ghost factor. The four-position expression is its formal unfixed representation.
Under z↦(az+b)/(cz+d) with determinant one,
zr′​−zs′​=(czr​+d)(czs​+d)zr​−zs​​,d2zr′​=∣czr​+d∣−4d2zr​.
(18)
At insertion r, the Koba-Nielsen factor therefore contributes ∣czr​+d∣−α′∑s=r​kr​⋅ks​. Momentum conservation makes the exponent α′kr2​. On the tachyon mass shell kr2​=4/α′, it is four and cancels the measure's exponent −4. Hence the full integration density is Möbius invariant. The correlator factor alone is covariant; the density including d2zr​ is invariant.
The supplied Virasoro–Shapiro amplitude has generic simple poles when −1−α′s/4=−n, giving
s=Mn2​=α′4(n−1)​,n=0,1,2,…,​
(19)
and likewise in the other channels. These are exchanges of closed-string states with equal left and right levels n: the tachyon, the massless sector and the massive tower. At special kinematics zeros or crossed-channel coincidences need to be handled by the full constrained amplitude; numerator poles are not three independent sequences of unrelated singularities.
To isolate the three-tachyon coupling, first use momentum conservation and kr2​=−μ2:
s+t+u=6μ2−2k1​⋅(k2​+k3​+k4​)=6μ2+2k12​=4μ2​.
(20)
At the tachyon pole s=μ2=−4/α′, put x=α′t/4 and y=α′u/4. The relation gives x+y=−3. Thus −1−y=2+x and 2+y=−1−x, and the entire t,u gamma-function ratio cancels to one. The remaining numerator is Γ(−α′(s−μ2)/4)∼−4/[α′(s−μ2)], while its denominator tends to Γ(1)=1. Hence
A4​∼μ2−s4C/α′​,s=μ2Res​A4​=−α′4C​.​
(21)
With the exchange convention A4​∼gTTT2​/(μ2−s) and canonically normalized external legs, factorization therefore gives gTTT2​=4C/α′​, or gTTT​=2C/α′​ up to its unobservable overall sign. A convention using the opposite propagator denominator or factors of i changes the corresponding amplitude phase. Because C itself is unspecified, the residue fixes this relation, not an absolute numerical coupling independent of normalization.

Take ηαβ=diag(−1,1) with coordinate zero equal to τ and coordinate one to σ. In the supplied representation set
C=ρ0=(01​−10​),ρ1=(01​10​).
(22)
They satisfy the Clifford algebra {ρα,ρβ}=2ηαβ1. For anticommuting spinor components and an ordinary matrix B, relabelling the two summed indices gives
χTBψ=−ψTBTχ.
(23)
Now CT=−C, whereas Cρ0=−1 and Cρ1=diag(−1,1) are symmetric. Therefore the Majorana Grassmann bilinear interchange yields
ψˉ​1ψ2=ψˉ​2ψ1,ψˉ​1ραψ2=−ψˉ​2ραψ1.​
(24)
These signs require fermionic, Grassmann-odd fields; commuting spinors would have the opposite interchange signs.
In flat conformal gauge, suppress the common factor (4πα′)−1 and write the combined density as L=−∂α​X⋅∂αX+ψˉ​⋅ρα∂α​ψ. The supersymmetry variation including its odd parameter is an even derivation. From (ρα)TC=−Cρα we obtain
δψˉ​=−ϵˉρβ∂β​X.
(25)
Also (Cραρβ)T=−Cρβρα, so interchanging odd spinors gives χˉ​ραρβϵ=ϵˉρβραχ. The two density variations, with target contractions understood, are therefore
δLB​δLF​​=2ϵˉ∂β​X⋅∂βψ,=−ϵˉρβρα∂β​X⋅∂α​ψ+ϵˉψ⋅□X.​
(26)
In the second term the symmetric second derivative annihilates the antisymmetric part of ρβρα. Using 2ηαβ−ρβρα=ραρβ now gives the exact off-shell identity
δL=∂α​(ϵˉραρβψ⋅∂β​X).​
(27)
Thus the action is invariant when this boundary flux vanishes and the transformation preserves the field boundary conditions, for example with suitable falloff or compatible supersymmetric endpoint conditions. On a spatial circle, periodic fields admit the constant parameter; antiperiodic fermions with periodic bosons require the local superconformal treatment, since a nonzero constant parameter would make the boson variation antiperiodic. The constant parameter and ordinary derivatives describe rigid supersymmetry in flat gauge; a locally supersymmetric curved-worldsheet formulation includes the worldsheet gravitino. The result is the rigid worldsheet supersymmetry boundary term without using field equations.
For the commutator on X, keep the order of the odd parameters:
[δ1​,δ2​]X=(ϵˉ1​ραϵ2​−ϵˉ2​ραϵ1​)∂α​X=ℓα∂α​X​,ℓα=2ϵˉ1​ραϵ2​.​
(28)
For the fermion, the order is equally important. Moving ϵ2​ through both odd factors in (ϵˉ1​∂α​ψ)ϵ2​ incurs two minus signs. Consequently
[δ1​,δ2​]ψ=ραM∂α​ψ,M=ϵ1​ϵˉ2​−ϵ2​ϵˉ1​.
(29)
To check the spinor matrix explicitly, set ϵ1​=(a,b)T, ϵ2​=(c,d)T, with all four components anticommuting and monomials ordered a,b,c,d. Then
M=(ad+bc2bd​−2ac−ad−bc​),V0=ϵˉ1​ρ0ϵ2​=−ac−bd,V1=ϵˉ1​ρ1ϵ2​=−ac+bd.
(30)
Direct multiplication gives ραM+Mρα=2Vα1. Hence
[δ1​,δ2​]ψ=2Vα∂α​ψ−M(ρα∂α​ψ).
(31)
Variation of the fermion action gives the Dirac equation ρα∂α​ψ=0. On this equation the second term vanishes, proving
[δ1​,δ2​]ψ=ℓα∂α​ψ,ℓ0=−2(ac+bd),ℓ1=2(bd−ac).​
(32)
This is on-shell closure of rigid worldsheet supersymmetry; the action's invariance was off shell, but its fermionic transformation algebra closes in this field content only on shell.
The associated superconformal current is proportional to ψ⋅∂X, of conformal weight 3/2. Its fermionic modes Gr​ extend the stress-tensor generators to the N=1 super-Virasoro algebra:
[Lm​,Gr​]=(2m​−r)Gm+r​,{Gr​,Gs​}=2Lr+s​+3c​(r2−41​)δr+s,0​.
(33)
The modes are half-integral in the Neveu–Schwarz sector and integral in the Ramond sector. Their anticommutator giving a Virasoro generator is the conformal-mode counterpart of the translation commutator just derived. Ten coordinate bosons and ten Majorana fermions give matter central charge c=10+10/2=15, canceled by the usual reparametrization and superconformal ghosts in the critical RNS string.
