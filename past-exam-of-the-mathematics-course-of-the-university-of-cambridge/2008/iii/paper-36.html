Write D=H∖K. By definition of a compact H-hull, D is a proper simply connected domain, and it agrees with the complex upper half-plane outside a sufficiently large disc. The Riemann mapping theorem supplies a conformal isomorphism f:D→H. Choose its boundary normalization so that the prime end at infinity maps to infinity. This prime end is unambiguous because the domain has an ordinary straight real boundary near infinity.
The Schwarz reflection principle, applied there in the coordinate 1/z, extends f across the real boundary near infinity. It has a simple pole there, with Laurent series
f(z)=az+b+zc​+O(z−2),a>0,b,c∈R.
(1)
The pole is simple because the reflected map is locally conformal at infinity; its leading coefficient is positive because it maps the upper side to the upper side. Postcomposing with the half-plane automorphism w↦(w−b)/a gives
gK​(z)=z+O(1/z),gK​(z)−z⟶0.​
(2)
This is hydrodynamic normalization at infinity.
If g1​,g2​ both satisfy it, g2​∘g1−1​ is a conformal automorphism of the upper half-plane. The asymptotics force it to fix infinity, so it has the form w↦αw+β with α>0 and β∈R. The same asymptotics force α=1, β=0. Hence the normalized mapping-out function exists and is unique. The argument requires reflection only outside a large disc, not regularity of the hull's entire boundary.

For the Joukowski map g(z)=z+z−1,
Img(z)=Imz(1−∣z∣21​).
(3)
It maps the exterior upper half-disc D0​={z∈H:∣z∣>1} into H. It is injective there: g(z)=g(w) gives (z−w)(1−1/(zw))=0, and the second factor cannot vanish when both moduli exceed one. It is onto as well. For v∈H, the two roots of z2−vz+1=0 have product one. Neither lies on the unit circle, since z+z−1 is real there. Exactly one root has modulus greater than one, and the displayed imaginary-part formula makes its imaginary part positive. That root belongs to D0​ and maps to v.
Since gK0​​ takes values in H, its domain must be contained in D0​. The established injectivity and surjectivity of g:D0​→H mean that no proper subdomain of D0​ can map onto all of H using this same function. Therefore
K0​={z∈H:∣z∣≤1},hcap(K0​)=1.​
(4)
The half-plane capacity is the coefficient of 1/z in the normalized expansion. If hulls are regarded as closed subsets of H, take the closure of this half-disc, including its diameter; its relative half-plane hull and capacity are unchanged. This is the half-plane capacity of a half-disc.

At a real point x outside the hull's closure, the Schwarz reflection principle makes the mapping-out function analytic across the boundary and gK′​(x)>0. Use the course's Brownian excursion restriction identity: for an excursion from x to infinity in the complex upper half-plane,
Pxexc​(B∩K=∅)=gK′​(x).
(5)
One way to obtain this identity is to start its Doob h-transform at x+iε: its avoidance probability is ImgK​(x+iε)/ε, which tends to gK′​(x) by reflection. Thus the boundary derivative is an excursion avoidance probability, and in particular it is at most one.
Let D be the filled unit half-disc. Since K⊆D, avoiding D implies avoiding K. The monotonicity of boundary derivatives of mapping-out functions and part (b) give, for ∣x∣>1,
1−x−2=gD′​(x)≤gK′​(x)≤1.​
(6)
The upper bound is attained by the empty hull and the lower by the filled half-disc, so the comparison is sharp on either real tail.

For chordal SLE with parameter κ, the Loewner trace γ starts at zero and is parametrized by half-plane capacity 2t. Let Dt​ be the unbounded component of H∖γ[0,t], and let Kt​=H∖Dt​ be its filled compact H-hull. When the trace is not simple, the hull includes the regions it disconnects from infinity. The associated mapping-out functions of compact H-hulls have expansion
gt​(z)=z+z2t​+O(z−2)
(7)
and satisfy the Chordal Loewner equation
∂t​gt​(z)=gt​(z)−ξt​2​,g0​(z)=z,ξt​=κ​Bt​,​
(8)
where B is standard real Brownian motion. The equation is solved until z is swallowed by the hull. The Loewner transform ξ records the real boundary point at which the mapped hull grows; its relation to the trace is
γt​=limy↓0​gt−1​(ξt​+iy).
(9)
Equivalently the tip maps to ξt​ in the appropriate boundary prime-end sense. Thus the flow encodes the surviving domains, the transform drives that flow, and the trace generates its hulls. For κ=0, the driver is identically zero, gt​(z)=z2+4t​ with the branch asymptotic to z, and γt​=2it​.

For 0<κ≤4, use the allowed probability-one event that the whole SLE trace lies in H at every positive time. At each deterministic rational s>0, part (b) makes the centered mapped future a fresh SLE with the same property. Consequently, almost surely, for every t>0,
γ~​t​∈H,γs+t​=gs−1​(γ~​t​+ξs​)∈Ds​=H∖Ks​.
(13)
This inverse relation holds through the trace's boundary-extension construction and puts positive-time future points in the open surviving domain. It therefore excludes every contact with the old filled hull, not just contacts with a fixed old point.
Intersect these probability-one events over all positive rational s. If γu​=γv​ for 0<u<v, choose rational s with u<s<v. Put A=H∖U. The neighborhood assumptions at zero and infinity mean that A is a bounded hull away from the starting boundary point. For t<T, the conjugacy map ht​ is the hydrodynamically normalized mapping-out function of the remaining forbidden hull after removing Kt​. Mτn​​⟶1{T=∞}​,M0​=Φ′(0)5/8.

Regard a chord as an oriented curve modulo increasing reparameterization, and write [γ] for that class. Scale invariance of μ means that [rγ] has the same law for each r>0. The locality of a chord law under conformal changes of neighborhoods is the following stopped-curve property: if N,N∗ are neighborhoods of the starting boundary point and Φ:N→N∗ is a boundary-preserving conformal isomorphism with Φ(0)=0, then
[Φ(γ stopped on leaving N)]=law[γ stopped on leaving N∗].​
(24)
The two stopped curves are sampled from the same original law μ; equality is for their unparameterized initial segments. A normalization of Φ′(0) can be imposed in an equivalent formulation, since scale invariance removes the positive dilation. In a hull-removal formulation, the Locality property of SLE says that a modification away from the starting point leaves the initial law unchanged until the curve reaches the modified part of the domain.
