Steady axisymmetric mass conservation is ∇⋅(ρup​)=0, permitting the axisymmetric hydrodynamic mass flux function representation
uR​=−ρRψz​​,uz​=ρRψR​​,up​⋅∇ψ=0.
(15)
Thus stream surfaces are regular level surfaces of ψ. Adiabaticity gives u⋅∇s=0, and axisymmetry removes the azimuthal derivative, so the specific entropy is constant along their meridional streamlines. The azimuthal Euler equations for an inviscid fluid has no pressure or gravitational torque:
up​⋅∇uϕ​+uR​uϕ​/R=0⟹up​⋅∇(Ruϕ​)=0.
(16)
The conserved specific angular momentum is ℓ=Ruϕ​. Taking the scalar product of the steady Crocco form of the unsteady Euler equation with u also gives
u⋅∇ε=Tu⋅∇s=0,ε=u2/2+Φ+w.
(17)
Consequently, on each connected regular family of stream surfaces,
s=s(ψ),ℓ=ℓ(ψ),ε=ε(ψ).​
(18)
These functions express entropy, angular momentum and the Bernoulli function carried by a stellar wind. Stagnation regions and disconnected branches with identical flux labels require their own matching information; the argument establishes the functions locally where the poloidal flow is nonzero.
To derive the hydrodynamic transfield equation for an axisymmetric wind, decompose the vorticity. The poloidal part comes from the toroidal velocity:
ωp​=∇(Ruϕ​)×∇ϕ=ℓ′(ψ)∇ψ×∇ϕ=ρℓ′up​.
(19)
The azimuthal part comes from the poloidal velocity:
ωϕ​​=∂z​uR​−∂R​uz​=−∂z​(ρRψz​​)−∂R​(ρRψR​​)=−R∇⋅(ρR2∇ψ​).​
(20)
The final divergence is the full axisymmetric cylindrical divergence, including its radial 1/R factor.
Since up​=∇ψ×eϕ​/(ρR), the meridional part of ω×u is
ωp​×uϕ​eϕ​+ωϕ​eϕ​×up​=(−R2ℓℓ′​+ρRωϕ​​)∇ψ.
(21)
The remaining steady Euler equations for an inviscid fluid is therefore
(−R2ℓℓ′​+ρRωϕ​​)∇ψ=(Ts′−ε′)∇ψ.
(22)
Canceling ∇ψ on a regular stream surface and inserting ωϕ​ gives
ρ1​∇⋅(ρR2∇ψ​)=dψdε​−Tdψds​−R2ℓ​dψdℓ​.​
(23)
The sign of the angular-momentum term follows from up​×eϕ​=−∇ψ/(ρR). To determine the wind, this transverse force equation is supplemented by its Bernoulli function relation
ε(ψ)=2ρ2R2∣∇ψ∣2​+2R2ℓ(ψ)2​+Φ+w(ρ,s(ψ)),
(24)
and the equation of state, with the gravitational potential supplied externally or by the Poisson equation.
