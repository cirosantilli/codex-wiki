For a positive integer N, a Dirichlet character ψ modulo N is a group homomorphism (Z/NZ)×→C×, extended periodically to the integers and set equal to zero on integers not coprime to N. Its nonzero values are roots of unity. For Res>1, its Dirichlet L-function is the absolutely convergent Dirichlet series
LN​(ψ,s)=n=1∑∞​nsψ(n)​=p∤N∏​(1−ψ(p)p−s)−1.​
(12)
Complete multiplicativity of ψ proves the Euler product by expanding each factor as a geometric series and applying unique integer factorization; absolute convergence justifies these rearrangements.
Suppose ψ is a nonprincipal Dirichlet character. Choose a unit u with ψ(u)=1. Multiplication by u permutes a complete residue system, so ∑a=1N​ψ(a)=ψ(u)∑a=1N​ψ(a), and the sum is zero. Consequently A(x)=∑n≤x​ψ(n) is bounded: complete periods contribute zero and the remaining incomplete period has at most N terms. The Abel summation formula gives, initially for Res>1,
LN​(ψ,s)=s∫1∞​A(x)x−s−1dx.
(13)
For Res>0 the integral converges, locally uniformly together with its derivatives in s, because on a compact set with Res≥δ>0 the differentiated integrands are bounded by constants times x−1−δ(1+logx)k. Thus holomorphy of a Dirichlet series from bounded partial sums proves
LN​(ψ,s) is holomorphic on Res>0.​

At a prime dividing N the numerator is 3. At every other prime put ψ(p)k=eiθ; then the numerator is 3+4cosθ+cos2θ=2(1+cosθ)2≥0. This Euler product positivity for L-function nonvanishing proves that the product inside the logarithm is at least one. If LN​(ψ,1)=0, its holomorphy would give LN​(ψ,σ)=O(σ−1). Meanwhile LN​(ψ2,σ) is bounded near one and the Riemann zeta function has a simple pole there. The product would therefore be O(σ−1) and tend to zero, a contradiction.
For a real nonprincipal ψ, suppose instead that LN​(ψ,1)=0. The product H(s)=ζ(s)LN​(ψ,s) would then be holomorphic on the whole half-plane Res>0, because the zero cancels the only zeta pole there. In Res>1 it has coefficients
H(s)=∑n≥1​bn​n−s,bn​=∑d∣n​ψ(d).

These are nonnegative zeta-times-real-L coefficients. They are multiplicative, and their prime-power values are k+1 if ψ(p)=1, one or zero according as k is even or odd if ψ(p)=−1, and one if ψ(p)=0. In particular bn​≥0 and bm2​≥1 for every m.

The argument is the positive-coefficient mechanism of the Landau theorem for a Dirichlet series with nonnegative coefficients, with the needed step proved explicitly.
