Use units with c=1. For the flat FRW metric, the Friedmann equation is H2=8πGρ/3+Λ/3, where H=a˙/a is the Hubble parameter. Work in natural units ℏ=c=kB​=1, at temperatures far below the W-boson mass but of order or above the Electron mass. The Fermi interaction then supplies a local four-fermion amplitude. The Fermi constant has dimension energy−2. In scattering of a thermal lepton from a nonrelativistic nucleon, the nucleon current matrix element has size mN​, while the lepton current has size T. Thus M∼GF​mN​T. The two-body relativistic scattering cross-section contains the kinematic factor s−1∼mN−2​; when the final and initial lepton energies are comparable, their momentum ratio is of order one. Consequently σ∼∣M∣2/mN2​∼GF2​T2. This estimates temperatures above the Electron mass and mass-splitting thresholds; those scales only affect the dimensionless rate function near freeze-out.
The light leptons in the thermal bath have number density n∼T3 and speed of order unity. Their conversion rate per nucleon is therefore
Γn↔p​∼n⟨σv⟩∼GF2​T5.​
(30)
For example, n+νe​↔p+e− and n+e+↔p+νˉe​ change the neutron-to-proton ratio. The bath density here is the lepton density, not the much smaller baryon density. Dimensionless weak couplings, phase-space integrals and the several reaction channels set a numerical coefficient. Electron masses and the neutron-proton splitting also become important for a precision rate near cosmological weak freeze-out.

Before electron-positron annihilation in cosmology, photons contribute two helicity states; Electrons and positrons contribute two spin states each. Each of the three light neutrino flavours contributes one active neutrino helicity and one antineutrino helicity. At a common temperature the effective number of relativistic energy degrees of freedom is therefore
g∗​=2+87​(4+6)=443​=10.75.​
(35)
Using GF​=1.17×10−5GeV−2 and MPl​≃2.435×1018GeV in the scaling formula alone gives TF​∼2MeV. Including the explicit expansion coefficient from (ii), but setting the unknown rate coefficient A to one, gives TF​≃1.5MeV. Thus the warranted conclusion is a freeze-out temperature of order 1MeV, above the 0.511MeV Electron mass. The omitted weak-rate coefficient prevents a more precise prediction from this estimate.
Neutrino decoupling occurs before most electron-positron pairs annihilate. Afterwards neutrinos redshift freely, Tν​a=constant, while the photons receive the pair entropy. For the still-coupled photon-pair gas, cosmological entropy conservation gives g∗s​Tγ3​a3=constant. Its entropy degrees of freedom drop from 2+(7/8)4=11/2 to 2. Comparing this with the freely cooling neutrinos gives
Tγ​Tν​​=(114​)1/3,Tν​Tγ​​=(411​)1/3.​

These results assume instantaneous neutrino decoupling and relativistic neutrinos; the two counts represent different thermal moments.

The small baryon-to-photon ratio suppresses nuclear equilibrium abundances through the factor ηA−1. Cooling eventually makes the binding-energy exponential favourable, but the deuterium bottleneck delays substantial nuclear burning until well after cosmological weak freeze-out. In ordinary Big Bang nucleosynthesis, nearly every neutron surviving that delay ends up in helium-4. This makes the final primordial helium mass fraction chiefly a neutron budget rather than an unrestricted equilibrium abundance.
Let rF​ be the neutron-to-proton ratio at freeze-out, let Δt be the delay until effective nuclear burning, and let τn​ be the neutron mean lifetime. The initial fraction of baryons which are neutrons is xn,F​=rF​/(1+rF​). Because neutron decay converts a neutron into a proton, total baryon number remains constant, while the neutron fraction becomes xn,B​=xn,F​e−Δt/τn​. Each helium nucleus uses two neutrons and four baryons. Hence neutron-limited helium synthesis gives
Y4​≃1+rF​2rF​​e−Δt/τn​,τn​=ln2τ1/2​(n)​.​
(40)
Equivalently, Y4​=2rB​/(1+rB​) with rB​=xn,B​/(1−xn,B​). This accounts for the extra protons produced by neutron decay; multiplying rF​ alone by the survival factor would miss their contribution. The following responses refer to ordinary neutron-limited burning, without exotic particle injection or a change to the nuclear network.

Extra relativistic neutrino species increase the effective number of relativistic energy degrees of freedom. At fixed temperature the Hubble parameter rises as g∗1/2​, while the standard conversion rate is unchanged. Consequently cosmological weak freeze-out moves to higher temperature, TF​∝g∗1/6​, and the neutron-to-proton ratio rF​=e−Q/TF​ increases. The initial neutron fraction rF​/(1+rF​) therefore increases as well.
The quicker expansion also reduces the cosmic time required to cool to the deuterium bottleneck temperature: during radiation domination, t∝g∗−1/2​T−2 while the thermal count is roughly constant. Fewer neutrons decay before burning. Both factors in the neutron budget increase, giving a larger final helium-4 mass fraction. This is the primordial helium response to extra relativistic species, under the assumption that the additional species contribute mainly to expansion rather than independently changing the conversion rates.

A larger baryon-to-photon ratio favours bound nuclei at a fixed temperature through the equilibrium factor ηA−1. In particular it helps deuterium survive photodissociation sooner: there are fewer energetic photons per baryon, and nuclear reaction rates per baryon also rise. A schematic deuterium bottleneck condition is ηeBD​/TB​∼constant, suppressing slowly varying powers of temperature. It gives
TB​BD​​≃constant−lnη,dlnηdTB​​≃BD​TB2​​>0.
(42)
Thus higher η permits burning at a higher temperature. During radiation domination that means earlier cosmic time, tB​∝TB−2​, leaving less time for free-neutron decay after freeze-out. More neutrons enter helium, so the final helium-4 mass fraction increases, ordinarily rather weakly. This is the baryon-density effect on primordial helium.
The equilibrium helium factor η3 indicates the direction of the change but is not a prediction that the final helium abundance grows without bound as η3. Once almost all surviving neutrons are captured, the final primordial helium mass fraction is limited by their supply, as the shared neutron budget shows.

(53)
up to a scale-independent matching coefficient.
For kτeq​≫1, entry occurs in radiation domination at τH​∼k−1, with PH​∼Ck−3. The density amplitude then grows only logarithmically until equality. Thus
δeq​∼δH​ln(kτeq​),Peq​∼Ck−3ln2(kτeq​).
(54)
During matter domination, the matter-era linear growth factor of the density is (τ/τeq​)2, so its square multiplies the matter power spectrum:
P(k,τ)∼C(τeq​τ​)4k−3ln2(kτeq​),kτeq​≫1.​
(55)
The turnover at k∼τeq−1​ records whether a mode entered during radiation domination. Earlier-entering small-scale modes miss the rapid τ2 growth and receive only the logarithmic Mészáros effect. This is the origin of the logarithmic small-scale matter transfer. The formula is a large-k asymptote: the additive constant in the logarithmic solution and smooth equality matching must be retained near kτeq​=1, where the spectrum does not vanish.
Multiplying by k3 gives the small-scale mass-fluctuation variance
⟨(MδM​)k2​⟩∼C(τeq​τ​)4ln2(kτeq​).​
(56)
This gives the requested estimate for modes entering before equality, with the logarithm replaced by a smooth order-one factor in the transition region. On comoving scale L∼k−1, unit variance today requires
C∼ln2(τeq​/L)(τeq​/τ0​)4​.​
(57)
For a numerical illustration faithful to this question's flat, matter-plus-radiation background, take h=0.7 and Ωr​=4.2×10−5h−2, hence ΩC​=1−Ωr​≃1 rather than the 0.3 appropriate to a vacuum-dominated model. Part 1(b), now with Ωm​=ΩC​, gives τeq​≃33Mpc. In this two-component model the same integral is valid all the way to today, giving
τ0​=H0​ΩC​2​(Ωr​+ΩC​​−Ωr​​)≃8.5×103Mpc.
(58)
With L=3Mpc, τ0​/τeq​≃258 and ln(τeq​/L)≃2.39. Therefore C∼[2584(2.39)2]−1≃4×10−11, corresponding to a horizon-entry root-mean-square fluctuation of roughly 6×10−6. A very small primordial amplitude, of order 10−10 in this schematic normalization, can reach unit small-scale variance by today. Factors from the Fourier convention, smoothing window and equality matching preclude a precise normalization here. The calculation marks the onset of nonlinear structure formation, where extrapolating the linear solution ceases to be valid. For a universe with late vacuum domination, one must replace the matter-only growth law by its actual growth factor before making a numerical inference.
