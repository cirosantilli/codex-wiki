Use the spectral definition of a Positive element of a C-star algebra: h is positive if h=h∗ and σA​(h)⊆[0,∞). We use the standard continuous functional calculus for a Hermitian element of a C-star algebra, including its real Banach algebra spectrum and the equality of its norm with its spectral radius. To prove a∗a positive without assuming this as part of the definition, first establish two elementary cone facts.
If p,q are positive and s=∥p∥+∥q∥, the continuous functional calculus gives ∥∥p∥1−p∥≤∥p∥ and the corresponding bound for q, hence
∥s1−p−q∥≤s.
(27)
Since p+q is self-adjoint, every spectral value t is real and satisfies ∣s−t∣≤s, so t≥0. Thus the positive cone of a C-star algebra is closed under addition. It is proper: if both h and −h are positive, their Banach algebra spectra force σ(h)⊆{0}, and the self-adjoint norm equality gives h=0. It is also norm closed: if pn​≥0 converges to p, choose r≥supn​∥pn​∥; then ∥r1−pn​∥≤r passes to the limit and forces every spectral value of p to be nonnegative.
Now put h=a∗a, and form its negative part d=max(−h,0) by continuous functional calculus. Let c=ad1/2. Because d is a function of h, the scalar identity tmax(−t,0)=−max(−t,0)2 gives
c∗c=d1/2hd1/2=−d2.
(28)
This has nonpositive Banach algebra spectrum. The previously proved equality of nonzero product Banach algebra spectra shows that cc∗ also has nonpositive Banach algebra spectrum; it is self-adjoint, so both c∗c and cc∗ are negative elements. Write c=u+iv, where u=(c+c∗)/2 and v=(c−c∗)/(2i) are self-adjoint. Direct multiplication gives
c∗c+cc∗=2(u2+v2).
(29)
By the spectral mapping theorem, the squares u2,v2 are positive, so their sum is positive by the cone fact above. But the same sum is negative, since both its original summands are negative. Properness forces c∗c+cc∗=0. Then c∗c=−cc∗ is both negative and positive, so c∗c=0, whence d2=0. The C-star identity applied to the self-adjoint d gives ∥d∥2=∥d2∥=0. Thus d=0, proving
a∗a≥0.​
(30)
This establishes positivity of adjoint products from spectral positivity. It also proves positivity under congruence: for p≥0 and any z, write p=r2 using its functional-calculus square root; then z∗pz=(rz)∗(rz)≥0.

Every truncated integral has a positive integrand, so is positive; norm convergence and closedness of the positive cone of a C-star algebra make its limit positive. This proves order preservation by the positive square root in the required case:
b2≥a2, a,b≥0⟹b−a≥0.​

It is important that this argument does not factor b2−a2 as (b−a)(b+a), which would be invalid for noncommuting elements.
For p=b−a≥0, apply the continuous function t↦t​ on its nonnegative compact Banach algebra spectrum. The resulting element p1/2 is self-adjoint, has nonnegative Banach algebra spectrum by continuous functional calculus, and satisfies (p1/2)2=p. Thus b−a has a positive square root in a C-star algebra.
Finally, if the positive element a is invertible, its compact Banach algebra spectrum is bounded away from zero. Let ε=minσ(a)>0; continuous functional calculus gives a≥ε1. Since b≥a, we also have b≥ε1, so σ(b)⊆[ε,∞). The reciprocal function is continuous on this Banach algebra spectrum and supplies b−1.
