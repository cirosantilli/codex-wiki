ρ∗​=4πR3M​,P∗​=4πR4GM2​,T∗​=RRμGM​.

The normalized surface has q(1)=l(1)=1. In the idealized zero-pressure, zero-temperature surface used for stellar homology, p(1)=t(1)=0; a physical photosphere supplies small nonzero surface values that are neglected in these bulk homology relations.
The common dimensionless profiles and boundary conditions keep D,E constant. With fixed κ0​,ϵ0​, D constant yields Lμ−7M−5=constant. Substitution into the nuclear coefficient then gives R16∝μ6M10. Consequently the CNO homology with density-dependent inverse-cubic opacity is

The additive constant depends on the fixed physical and structural coefficients; logarithms refer to fixed choices of units.
For physical evolution time s, distinct from the dimensionless temperature t(x), hold stellar mass fixed and put u=3+5X, u0​=3+5X0​. The mean molecular weight approximation and the homology relation give L=L0​(u0​/u)7. Converting hydrogen mass to helium releases energy at rate L, so fuel conservation is MEH​dX/ds=−L. Therefore
dsdu​=−MEH​u75L0​u07​​,dsd​(u8)=−MEH​40L0​u07​​.

(11)
It is valid while hydrogen remains and the homology assumptions hold. Fuel exhaustion occurs when u=3, before the formal denominator vanishes; the apparent infinite luminosity is not a physical endpoint of this stellar model.

Let Ω=−∫0M​Gmdm/r be the gravitational energy. Multiply the stellar hydrostatic equation by 4πr3 and integrate through the star. Integration by parts and zero surface pressure give
∫0R​4πr3drdP​dr=−3∫0R​4πr2Pdr=−3∫0M​ρP​dm,
(12)
while its hydrostatic right-hand side is −∫0R​4πGmrρdr=Ω. Hence the stellar virial theorem is
3∫0M​ρP​dm+Ω=0.​
(13)
There is a genuine reference-sheet error to resolve. A monatomic ideal gas with adiabatic exponent 5/3 has specific internal energy of an ideal gas u=P/[ρ(5/3−1)]=3P/(2ρ), not the printed 3P/ρ on PDF page 2. The latter would give total internal energy −Ω and zero total energy, inconsistent with radiating contraction. The following derivation uses the consistent monatomic value.
A fully convective star is approximately isentropic and has an adiabatic stellar polytrope with index 3/2. The given polytropic gravitational-energy formula yields Ω=−6GM2/(7R). The virial relation gives total internal energy U=−Ω/2, so total energy is U+Ω=−3GM2/(7R). At constant mass with no nuclear heating, Kelvin-Helmholtz contraction obeys d(U+Ω)/ds=−L, whence
dsdR​=−3GM27LR2​.​
(14)
Here s denotes physical time.
In the plane-parallel atmosphere, the inward optical depth satisfies dτ=−κρdr, so hydrostatic equilibrium in optical depth gives dP/dτ=g/κ. Neglecting radiation pressure, ρ=μP/(RT) and the specified opacity becomes κ=(κ0​μ/R)PT16. Therefore
dτd(P2)​=μκ0​T162Rg​=μκ0​Te16​512Rg​(2+3τ)−4.
(15)
The grey atmosphere relation and the boundary P(0)=0 integrate to
P2=9μκ0​Te16​512Rg​[81​−(2+3τ)31​].​
(16)
To apply the Schwarzschild criterion, put w=2+3τ. Logarithmic differentiation gives
dτdlogT​=4w3​,dτdlogP​=2w(w3/8−1)9​,∇rad​=dlogPdlogT​=48w3−8​.
(17)
This increases inward, starting from zero. Marginal convection has ∇rad​=2/5, so wb3​=136/5. Since Tb12​/Te12​=wb3​/64, the grey-atmosphere convection onset with seventeenth-power opacity is
Tb​=(4017​)1/12Te​.​
(18)
The purely radiative atmosphere is used only up to this boundary.
The convective relation P=KT5/2 is spatially uniform in its entropy coefficient K at each instant; K can change as the star radiates and contracts. At the convection boundary wb​ is a fixed number, so Pb​∝g1/2Te−8​ at fixed composition and opacity normalization. Thus
K=Tb5/2​Pb​​∝g1/2Te−21/2​∝M1/2R−1Te−21/2​.

On the other hand, the central scalings for the n=3/2 stellar polytrope give Pc​∝GM2/R4 and Tc​=μPc​/(Rρc​)∝μGM/(RR). Hence, at fixed composition,
K=Tc5/2​Pc​​∝M−1/2R−3/2.

Equating the interior and atmosphere expressions yields Te21/2​∝MR1/2. Applying the Stefan–Boltzmann law then proves the Hayashi contraction with seventeenth-power opacity relation
L∝M8/21R46/21.​

At fixed mass write L=AM8/21Rα, with constant A and α=46/21. The contraction equation is R˙=−CRα+2, where C=7AM8/21/(3GM2). Direct integration gives

L=67Rs9GM2​.​
(24)
This is the luminosity-age relation within the contraction model, rather than an assertion that a physical star begins at literally infinite radius.

For an isotropic degenerate electron gas, pressure is one third of the momentum flux. In the ultrarelativistic limit each occupied mode has speed c, so the supplied momentum-space state count gives
ne​=∫0p0​​h38πp2​dp=3h38πp03​​,Pe​=31​∫0p0​​pch38πp2​dp=3h32πcp04​​.
(25)
A fully ionized helium nucleus provides two electrons for approximately 4mp​ of mass, so ne​=ρ/(2mp​). Eliminating the Fermi momentum p0​=(3h3ρ/(16πmp​))1/3 therefore gives
Pe​=Kρ4/3,K=(2π3​)1/316mp4/3​hc​.​
(26)
The two-spin-state degeneracy is already included in the supplied state count; no further factor 2 is needed.
If electron degeneracy pressure supplies the star's pressure, this is an index-three stellar polytrope. Its central pressure and density obey
c3​R4GM2​=K(4πR33a3​M​)4/3.
(27)
The radius cancels, leaving M2/3=K(3a3​/(4π))4/3/(c3​G). Thus the Chandrasekhar mass from central polytropic constants is
M=(2π3​)1/2(4π3a3​​)264c33/2​1​(Ghc​)3/2mp2​1​.​
(28)
The dependence on microscopic constants reflects the balance of fermionic pressure and gravity. The absence of radius dependence is special to the limiting 4/3 pressure exponent. Ordinary partially relativistic white dwarfs can have a range of masses below this Chandrasekhar limit; the formula does not force every white dwarf to have this mass. As a degenerate stellar core grows toward the limiting mass, compression cannot provide an arbitrarily larger supporting pressure relative to gravity. Above it the ideal cold, nonrotating electron-supported configuration cannot remain in equilibrium; nuclear ignition, collapse or other changes in the support mechanism must intervene. Which outcome occurs depends on the core's composition and evolutionary state.

Outside the thin burning shell, the enclosed mass and luminosity are approximately Mc​ and L. Divide the hydrostatic pressure support equation by radiative diffusion in a star with constant opacity:
dTdP​=3κ0​L16πacGMc​​T3.
(29)
In the constant-opacity radiative-zero giant envelope approximation, the outer pressure and temperature constants are negligible. Integration gives
P=ΛT4,Λ=3κ0​L4πacGMc​​.​
(30)
The radiation pressure is aT4/3, so the gas pressure fraction β is constant and satisfies (1−β)Λ=a/3. Thus also
Λ=3(1−β)a​,β=1−4πcGMc​κ0​L​.​
(31)
The physical branch has positive gas pressure, hence L<4πcGMc​/κ0​, the constant-opacity Eddington luminosity for the core mass.
Using the gas part of the equation of state, define H=μβΛ/R, so ρ=HT3. Substitution into hydrostatic balance gives
4ΛT3drdT​=−r2GMc​​RμβΛ​T3,drdT​=−4Rr2μβGMc​​.
(32)
Consequently, in the same deep-envelope radiative-zero approximation,
T(r)=4RrμβGMc​​.​
(33)
Precisely, a finite outer matching point (Rs​,Ts​) gives T=Ts​+μβGMc​(1/r−1/Rs​)/(4R). The displayed 1/r profile neglects that additive outer-boundary constant; it is appropriate near a compact core when the envelope extends to much smaller temperatures.
To estimate the thin-shell luminosity, write KT​=μβGMc​/(4R), so T=KT​/r. The hydrogen-layer heating law gives
drdLr​​=4πr2ρϵ=4πϵ0​H2r2T22=4πϵ0​H2KT22​r−20.
(34)
This steep radial dependence strongly concentrates the emission near Rc​. Continuing the outer profile across the thin emitting layer and taking a distant upper limit gives
L≃194πϵ0​​H2KT22​Rc−19​.
(35)
The same radiative coefficient implies H=16πacKT​/(3κ0​L). Hence the high-power shell burning in a constant-opacity giant envelope satisfies
L3=194πϵ0​​(3κ0​16πac​)2KT24​Rc−19​.
(36)
Taking the cube root gives the requested form,
L=CRc19/3​(μβMc​)8​,C=(4RG​)8[194πϵ0​​(3κ0​16πac​)2]1/3.​
(37)
Thus C contains only the specified opacity and heating normalizations and physical constants. The explicit coefficient uses the outer radiative profile as a thin-shell estimate; resolving the variation of Lr​ within the emitting layer can change that coefficient. Also β depends on L, so the radiation-pressure-inclusive relation is implicit, not a pure explicit eighth-power core-mass law with independently fixed β.

Put M=M1​+M2​. In a circular orbit, the distances from the centre of mass are r1​=aM2​/M and r2​=aM1​/M. Summing the components' angular momentum gives
J=(M1​r12​+M2​r22​)Ω=MM1​M2​​a2Ω.
(38)
Use Kepler's third law Ω2a3=GM and Ω=2π/Porb​ to obtain the circular-binary orbital angular momentum
J=(2π)1/3M1/3G2/3Porb1/3​M1​M2​​.​
(39)
The numerator contains orbital period, not the erroneous Morb​ token in the converted TeX.
The total mass changes as M˙=(1−f)M˙1​. With the prescribed wind carrying specific angular momentum λJ/M, the angular-momentum loss law is J˙/J=λM˙/M. For constant λ, integration gives J∝Mλ. Cubing the orbital formula then proves the binary period invariant for a constant wind angular-momentum factor:
Porb​∝M1−3​M2−3​Mδ,δ=3λ+1.​
(40)
This integration does not require f to be constant, although its instantaneous value enters the following response coefficients. In the conservative case f=1, both M and J are constant and the usual period-product invariant for conservative mass transfer is recovered.
Kepler's law and the given Roche lobe approximation imply RL​∝M11/3​Porb2/3​, since the total-mass factor cancels. Using the period law therefore gives
logRL​=−35​logM1​−2logM2​+32δ​logM+constant.
(41)
Because M˙2​=−fM˙1​, the instantaneous Roche-lobe radius response exponent is
ζL​=dlogM1​dlogRL​​=−35​+2fq+3(1+q)2δ(1−f)q​,q=M2​M1​​.
(42)
For x=log(R/RL​), subtracting this response from the prescribed stellar radius evolution gives
x˙=tnuc​1​+(n−ζL​)M1​M˙1​​.
(43)
The overflow-rate model requires f>0 and is used for small positive overfill. It gives M˙1​/M1​=−x/(ftdyn​). Hence the nuclear-driven Roche-overfill relaxation equation is
x˙=tnuc​1​−ftdyn​x​[n+35​−2fq−3(1+q)2δ(1−f)q​].​
(44)
Let the bracket be A(q). On the short response time, freeze the slowly changing masses and coefficients. If A>0, the local solution is
x(t)=x∗​+[x(0)−x∗​]e−At/(ftdyn​),x∗​=Atnuc​ftdyn​​.
(45)
The equilibrium donor mass-loss rate is M˙1​/M1​=−1/(Atnuc​). Thus the required stabilizing mass-ratio condition is
n+35​>2fq+3(1+q)2(3λ+1)(1−f)q​.​
(46)
When A is of order one and tdyn​≪tnuc​, the overfill is small and relaxation is fast, while mass transfer proceeds on a nuclear timescale. A positive A arbitrarily close to zero does not by itself ensure this timescale separation or small overfill.
For an explicit threshold put B=n+5/3 and Cw​=2δ(1−f)/3. The inequality is
B+(B−2f−Cw​)q−2fq2>0.
(47)
If B>0 and f>0, its positive root gives
0<q<qcrit​,qcrit​=4fB−2f−Cw​+(B−2f−Cw​)2+8fB​​.​
(48)
For f=1 this reduces to q<(n+5/3)/2, the conservative mass-transfer critical mass ratio. For other parameter signs the unsimplified inequality is the general condition.
If A<0, a larger overfill causes further growth rather than restoration, on the short scale ftdyn​/∣A∣. Transfer runs away towards a dynamical episode, potentially a common envelope or merger; the small-overfill model then fails. At A=0 the overfill grows linearly under nuclear expansion and there is no steady regulated contact. These conclusions use the stipulated donor-radius response; an actual dynamical episode requires its adiabatic response and hydrodynamics.
