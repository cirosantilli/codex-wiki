Use metric gμν​=diag(1,−1,−1,−1) and the hypercharge convention Q=T3+Y. Then the Higgs doublet has Y=1/2 and transforms under electroweak gauge invariance as
ϕ(x)⟼eiβ(x)/2U(x)ϕ(x),U(x)=exp(iαa(x)σa​/2)∈SU(2).
(1)
The covariant derivative sign consistent with the requested expression is
Dμ​ϕ=(∂μ​+ig2σa​​Aμa​+i2g′​Bμ​)ϕ.​
(2)
A convention that instead calls the doublet hypercharge one puts Y/2 in the charge generator and derivative; the physical coupling is the same. Here Bμ​↦Bμ​−∂μ​β/g′ and the matrix-valued SU(2) connection transforms as Aμ​↦UAμ​U−1+(i/g)(∂μ​U)U−1, making Dμ​ϕ transform like ϕ.
In unitary gauge, the upper component of the derivative comes from the off-diagonal Pauli matrices:
ig2Aμ1​−iAμ2​​2​v+H​=2ig​(v+H)Wμ​.
(3)
The lower component is
2​∂μ​H​+22​i(v+H)​(−gAμ3​+g′Bμ​).
(4)
Writing cW​=cosθW​, sW​=sinθW​, inversion of the neutral rotation gives A3=cW​Z+sW​A and B=−sW​Z+cW​A. Since g′=gtanθW​, the coefficient of the photon vanishes, whereas −gcW​−g′sW​=−g/cW​. Thus
Dμ​ϕ=(ig(v+H)Wμ​/2∂μ​H/2​−ig(v+H)Zμ​/(22​cW​)​).​
(5)
The zero photon coupling expresses the neutrality of the surviving radial Higgs boson.
The scalar Lagrangian density is Lϕ​=(Dμ​ϕ)†Dμϕ−V. Adding −Fμνa​Faμν/4−Bμν​Bμν/4 gives the complete bosonic electroweak Lagrangian density. Its scalar and vector mass terms, after electroweak symmetry breaking, are obtained directly as
Lϕ​=​21​(∂H)2+4g2​(v+H)2Wμ†​Wμ+8cW2​g2​(v+H)2Zμ​Zμ−2λv2​H2−2λv​H3−8λ​H4.​
(6)
Indeed, the shifted quartic Higgs potential normalization gives ϕ†ϕ−v2/2=vH+H2/2. Comparing with a canonically normalized real scalar mass term, a complex vector mass term and a real vector mass term yields
MH2​=λv2,MW2​=g2v2/4,MZ2​=g2v2/(4cW2​),MA​=0.​
(7)
The extra factor one half in this paper's Higgs potential is why MH2​ is λv2, rather than 2λv2 in another common convention. The tree-level electroweak gauge-boson masses give cosθW​=MW​/MZ​: measure the two vector masses and take their ratio. Beyond tree level this extraction requires a specified renormalization convention and radiative corrections.
Expanding the vector terms gives the cubic and quartic Higgs couplings to electroweak gauge bosons:
Lint​=(v2MW2​​H+v2MW2​​H2)Wμ†​Wμ+(vMZ2​​H+2v2MZ2​​H2)Zμ​Zμ.
(8)
The four elementary Feynman diagrams and their all-incoming Feynman rules are shown below. Differentiating with respect to two identical Z fields supplies a factor two; differentiating with respect to two identical Higgs fields supplies another factor two in the quartic rules.
There are no elementary Hγγ, HZγ, HHγγ or HHZγ vertices in this tree-level Lagrangian density. Loop-induced photon interactions are a different order. The scalar self-interactions, if included, have rules HHH:−3iMH2​/v and HHHH:−3iMH2​/v2.
The prime LEP2 search used the Higgs boson coupling to Z bosons through Higgsstrahlung, e+e−→Z∗→ZH, shown in the first panel of the process figure in Question 3. This identifies an associated-production interaction, rather than an on-shell decay of a single Z into a heavier Higgs. The channel's historical role is documented in the LEP2 Higgs-search account.
For the free charged W boson, take Wμ​(x)=∫d4pe−ipxWμ​(p)/(2π)4, with the conjugate field carrying eipx. In the action, multiplying the two field strengths gives 2[p2Wμ†​Wμ−(p⋅W†)(p⋅W)]. Therefore
LW​(p)=−Wν†​(p)Oμν(p)Wμ​(p),Oμν=(p2−MW2​)gμν−pμpν.​
(9)
To invert it, separate the transverse and longitudinal projectors PT​=g−pp/p2, PL​=pp/p2. The operator is (p2−MW2​)PT​−MW2​PL​, so
O−1=p2−MW2​PT​​−MW2​PL​​=p2−MW2​g−pp/MW2​​.
(10)
Since the quadratic action kernel is −O, its Proca propagator is
Dμν​(p)=p2−MW2​+i0−i​(gμν​−MW2​pμ​pν​​).​
(11)
For all momentum components small relative to MW​, the low-momentum limit is Dμν​≃igμν​/MW2​, with corrections of relative order p2/MW2​ or pμ​pν​/MW2​. This local limit leads to the Fermi interaction, with GF​/2​=g2/(8MW2​) for the charged-current normalization used here.

The CPT theorem is a structural consequence of relativistic quantum field theory. In flat spacetime, assume a positive physical state space, the positive-energy spectrum condition, Lorentz invariance, microcausality, the usual Spin-statistics theorem, and a Hermitian dynamics. For ordinary field theories these are realized by a local Hermitian Lorentz-scalar Lagrangian density. There is then an antiunitary operator Θ implementing the combined charge conjugation, parity symmetry in quantum field theory and time-reversal symmetry, with a CPT-invariant vacuum. The combined symmetry is required even when the three separate symmetries fail.
Under the combined spacetime transformation, x↦−x. A complex scalar field can be taken to transform into its adjoint at −x; a Hermitian vector field transforms into minus itself at −x. A derivative contributes a minus sign through differentiation of its reflected argument. For a Dirac field the transformation supplied in the paper may be written
Θψ(x)Θ−1=γ5T​ψ†(−x)T.
(12)
Field phases are conventional and do not affect the physical conclusions. Crucially, antiunitarity means ΘiΘ−1=−i and conjugates every numerical coefficient. The reversal of time is compatible with positive energies because this conjugation changes e−iHt to eiHt while leaving the Hamiltonian invariant. Treating time reversal as a unitary change of coordinates would instead give incorrect signs.
One can see explicitly how the CPT transformation of Dirac bilinears works. Let BΓ​(x)=:ψ†γ0Γψ: be a Hermitian fermion bilinear, with Γ†=γ0Γγ0. The colons indicate normal ordering so that the graded reordering below has no vacuum contact term. Using the reflected fields and conjugating γ0Γ, then interchanging the two fermion factors, gives
ΘBΓ​(x)Θ−1​=:ψTγ5∗​(γ0Γ)∗γ5T​ψ†T:−x​=−:ψ†γ5​(γ0Γ)†γ5​ψ:−x​=:ψ​γ5​Γγ5​ψ:−x​.​
(13)
The last step uses the Dirac-Hermiticity condition and {γ0,γ5​}=0. The identities for the gamma matrices then give
bilinearscalarpseudoscalarvectoraxial vectortensor​Γ1iγ5​γμγμγ5​2i​[γμ,γν]​CPT sign++−−+​​
(14)
The pseudoscalar's sign is particularly instructive: its explicit i must also be conjugated. The minus sign from exchanging fermions is equally essential. These signs depend on the number of free vector indices, rather than on a field's separate parity assignment.
This supplies a local Lagrangian argument for CPT invariance. Hermitian local field combinations with r vector indices transform with sign (−1)r, including the appropriate reordered derivative terms. A Lorentz scalar contracts those indices in pairs or with four-index invariant tensors, giving a positive total sign. For charged terms, the reflected conjugate fields and conjugated coefficients exchange a term with its Hermitian-conjugate partner. Thus
ΘL(x)Θ−1=L(−x),ΘSaction​Θ−1=Saction​.​
(15)
The integration variable x↦−x has unit absolute Jacobian. The general field-index argument is discussed in Srednicki's treatment of discrete symmetries, section 40.
For example, the scalar mass term ψ​ψ is even. The Dirac electromagnetic current and vector potential are both odd, making their contracted interaction even. The Hermitian Dirac kinetic term (i/2)ψ​γμ∂μ​ψ is also even: reflection and reordering interchange the two derivative placements, while antiunitarity conjugates its i. A complex Yukawa coupling and its Hermitian conjugate are exchanged rather than requiring the coupling itself to be real. Hence CP violation in the CKM matrix is compatible with exact CPT. Renormalizability is not necessary: local Hermitian higher-dimensional Lorentz-scalar operators satisfy the same argument.
The axiomatic form of the CPT theorem does not start by assuming a polynomial Lagrangian density. The positive-energy spectrum condition provides analytic continuations of Wightman functions, and complexified Lorentz transformations relate configurations to their total spacetime inversions. At suitable spacelike configurations, microcausality permits reversal of the order of field operators with the signs dictated by the Spin-statistics theorem. Uniqueness of analytic continuation extends this relation to the correlators generally, from which the Hilbert-space antiunitary operator is obtained. This explains why locality and positive energy are genuine hypotheses, not incidental features of a particular interaction. The elementary argument above displays the mechanism for the scalar, spinor and vector fields relevant to the Standard Model; it is not a substitute for all the analytic steps of the axiomatic proof.
For one-particle states, CPT reverses additive charges and spin projections. The spatial momentum is unchanged by the combination: parity symmetry in quantum field theory and time reversal each reverse it. Thus, up to phases,
Θ∣p,s,q⟩=∣p,−s,−q⟩.
(16)
Since the energy-momentum spectrum is preserved, the CPT constraints on particle properties give equal particle-antiparticle masses, equal spin magnitudes, and opposite conserved additive charges. Relating the corresponding two-point functions also fixes equal pole positions, so unstable conjugate particles have equal total decay widths, hence equal lifetimes. Magnetic moments at the same chosen spin orientation have opposite signs. These are precise experimental tests of CPT, not tests that every individual discrete symmetry is respected.
For scattering, CPT interchanges incoming and outgoing boundary conditions. The antiunitary operator identity ⟨a∣b⟩=⟨Θb∣Θa⟩ relates ⟨f,out∣i,in⟩ to the amplitude with transformed f incoming and transformed i outgoing. Thus the reversed process with antiparticles and reversed spins has the same transition probability. This is not, in general, equality with the forward CP-conjugate process. In particular, CP violation can make corresponding partial decay widths different while their complete sums remain equal. With CPT preserved, genuine CP violation requires corresponding time-reversal symmetry violation.
Finally, a proposed CPT violation requires revisiting at least one assumption: for example, a nonlocal interaction, a preferred spacetime direction violating Lorentz invariance, or loss of unitary Hermitian dynamics. A generic curved background need not possess the global spacetime inversion used here. Those observations delimit the theorem; they do not weaken its prediction for the ordinary local Standard Model in Minkowski spacetime.

The middle panel shows the Feynman diagram for the leptonic W boson decay. With the charged-current Lagrangian density sign in the question, its vertex Feynman rule is
−22​ig​γμ(1−γ5​).​
(17)
An overall field-phase convention can change the overall amplitude sign but cannot change the decay width. With the standard Dirac field current ordering, the amplitude is
M=−22​g​ϵμ​(p)u(q1​)γμ(1−γ5​)v(q2​).
(18)
The unusual order of barred fields in the printed description should be interpreted as this physical weak charged current matrix element.
For massless final fermions, the fermion spin sums are ∑s​us​us​=q1​ and ∑s​vs​vs​=q2​. The massive-vector spin average divides by three. Thus
∣M∣2​=24g2​Kμν​Tμν,Kμν​=−gμν​+pμ​pν​/p2,
(19)
with the chiral trace
Tμν=Tr[q1​γμ(1−γ5​)q2​γν(1−γ5​)].
(20)
Anticommuting γ5​ through the two following gamma matrices and using (1−γ5​)2=2(1−γ5​) gives its symmetric part
TSμν​=8(q1μ​q2ν​+q1ν​q2μ​−gμνq1​⋅q2​).
(21)
Its remaining part is an antisymmetric epsilon tensor from the gamma matrix trace identities; it vanishes against the symmetric polarization tensor K. Direct contraction gives −gμν​TSμν​=16q1​⋅q2​ and
p2pμ​pν​​TSμν​=8[p22(p⋅q1​)(p⋅q2​)​−q1​⋅q2​].
(22)
Hence
∣M∣2​=3g2​[q1​⋅q2​+p22(p⋅q1​)(p⋅q2​)​].​
(23)
No additional averaging over hypothetical right-handed neutrino states is appropriate: the chiral projector already enforces the physical helicity coupling.
On shell, p=q1​+q2​, p2=MW2​ and qi2​=0, so q1​⋅q2​=MW2​/2 and p⋅qi​=MW2​/2. The squared matrix element reduces to g2MW2​/3. In the parent rest frame, integrate the spatial momentum delta first in two-body Lorentz-invariant phase space. The remaining radial integral is
∫dΦ2​=(2π)21​∫4k2k2dkdΩ​δ(MW​−2k)=8π1​.
(24)
Consequently the massless leptonic W decay width is
Γe​=2MW​1​3g2MW2​​8π1​=48πg2MW​​≡Γℓ​.​
(25)
Lepton universality gives Γ(W−→μ−νμ​)=Γ(W−→τ−ντ​)=Γℓ​ in the same massless approximation. The muon and tau lepton masses produce small corrections if retained.
With an identity CKM matrix, the allowed quark channels are du and sc. The corresponding third-generation channel bt is closed by the top threshold. Each open channel has three orthogonal matching-color final states, so the color multiplicity in a decay width gives Γ(du)=Γ(sc)=3Γℓ​. There is no initial color average because the W boson is colorless. The tree-level W branching fractions are therefore:
Final channelPartial widthBranching fraction, rounded
e−νe​Γℓ​1/9≃11%
μ−νμ​Γℓ​1/9≃11%
τ−ντ​Γℓ​1/9≃11%
du3Γℓ​1/3≃33%
sc3Γℓ​1/3≃33%
The rounded percentages sum to 99% because of rounding. The exact fractions sum to one, with total leptonic fraction 1/3 and hadronic fraction 2/3. The total decay width is
ΓW​=9Γℓ​=16π3g2MW​​=2π2​3GF​MW3​​.​
(26)
As an illustrative numerical estimate, taking g=0.65 and MW​=80GeV gives Γℓ​≃0.224GeV, each open quark width ≃0.672GeV and ΓW​≃2.02GeV. These are tree-level model estimates with the stated illustrative inputs, not a fit to a measured width. Strong radiative corrections increase the inclusive quark widths.
The produced quark and antiquark radiate gluons, generating a parton shower. As their virtualities fall towards the strong-interaction scale, perturbation theory gives way to hadronization: confinement converts the colored partons into color-neutral hadrons, typically seen as two particle jets, with additional radiation sometimes producing resolved extra jets. Unstable hadrons subsequently decay; the primary quarks are not observed as free particles.
Including the CKM matrix, the charged current couples an accessible up-type flavour ui​ to every kinematically accessible down-type flavour dj​. Therefore
Γ(W−→dj​ui​)=3Γℓ​∣Vij​∣2,ui​=u,c,dj​=d,s,b,​
(27)
The sums over j remain 3Γℓ​ by row unitarity, so the massless tree-level total remains 9Γℓ​. Quark mixing redistributes the hadronic branching fractions; it does not create a new total color factor. If a family is above threshold, only its accessible channels enter, and the unitarity sum must not be applied to an incomplete set of final flavours.
For completeness, CKM parameter counting starts with N2 real degrees of freedom of an N×N unitary matrix. This follows by counting 2N2 real entries and N2 independent Hermitian constraints in V†V=I. The real-orthogonal subclass has N(N−1)/2 mixing angles; the remaining N(N+1)/2 parameters can be viewed as phases. Rephasing each up-type and down-type mass eigenfield changes Vij​ by e−iαi​eiβj​. Of the 2N phases, the common phase does nothing, so 2N−1 are removable. Thus
Nphysical​=(N−1)2,Nangles​=2N(N−1)​,NCP phases​=2(N−1)(N−2)​.​
(28)
For three generic nondegenerate families this is three angles and one CP-violating phase. Degenerate masses can allow additional changes of basis and reduce the count.

The last panel of the preceding process figure shows deep inelastic scattering through a virtual photon. The blob represents the electromagnetic hadronic tensor response, with all final hadronic states included. With charges factored into e and the hadronic current as in the question, a suitable scattering amplitude, up to an irrelevant overall phase, is
MX​=Q2e2​u(p′)γμ​u(p)⟨X∣Jhμ​∣H(P)⟩,Q2=−q2.​
(29)
The electron fermion spin sum, before averaging the two initial spin states, gives the following normalization of the leptonic tensor:
Lμν​=Tr[p′γμ​pγν​]=4(pμ′​pν​+pν′​pμ​−gμν​p⋅p′).​
(30)
The initial electron spin average is a separate factor 1/2. Equivalently one can absorb it into a tensor L/2, but the prefactor in the requested formula uses the unaveraged trace L. The massless Dirac equations imply qμLμν​=qνLμν​=0, the relevant Ward identity.
In the target rest frame the incident flux is 4p⋅P=4ME. This remains valid for a massive target because the electron is massless: the general flux 4(p⋅P)2−p2P2​ reduces to that expression. The outgoing electron contributes d3p′/((2π)32E′), and the inclusive final-state sum is 4πWHμν​. Including the electron spin average therefore gives
d3p′dσ​​=4ME1​(2π)32E′1​(Q2)2e4​21​Lμν​(4πWHμν​)=(2π)28MEE′(Q2)2e4​Lμν​WHμν​.​​
(31)
The implicit hadron spin average in WH​ must not be inserted a second time.
For an unpolarized target, Lorentz invariance permits tensor structures formed from P, q and the metric. Hermiticity relates the two index orders. The electromagnetic response is parity even, excluding the pseudotensor iϵμνρσPρ​qσ​. A possible ordinary antisymmetric Pμqν−qμPν term is excluded by current conservation. The symmetric part initially has four structures, gμν, PμPν, qμqν and Pμqν+qμPν. Imposing both current-conservation conditions leaves the transverse decomposition of an unpolarized hadronic tensor:
WHμν​=−(gμν−q2qμqν​)F1​+(Pμ−q2ν​qμ)(Pν−q2ν​qν)νF2​​,ν=P⋅q.
(32)
For example, contracting q into the first structure gives (1+A)qμ in the printed parametrization, and contracting into either parenthesis gives (1+B)ν or (1+C)ν. Since the two independent deep-inelastic structure functions are not generally linked by symmetry, transversality requires
A=B=C=−1.​
(33)
There are only two invariant variables once P2=M2 is fixed; they may be chosen as Q2 and Bjorken x x=Q2/(2ν).
Now make the leading massless collinear parton approximation. In the hard parton calculation, use a lightlike collinear reference for P, neglecting target-mass effects of relative order M2/Q2 as well as quark masses. Literally combining a massive P with an exactly massless constituent momentum ξP would be inconsistent. The exact rest-frame flux derived above does not require this approximation; it is the following partonic response that does.
Let r=ξP and k=r+q. Strip the quark charge out of the current, since its square is already the factor Qf2​ in the parton convolution. Averaging the initial quark spin and summing the final spin gives the spin-averaged electromagnetic parton tensor
Sμν=21​Tr[rγνkγμ]=2(rμkν+rνkμ−gμνr⋅k).
(34)
The spatial momentum delta fixes k=q+ξP. The remaining one-particle Lorentz-invariant phase space, together with 1/(4πξ) in the definition, supplies
4πξ1​(2π)32Ek​(2π)4​δ(Eq​+ξEP​−Ek​)=4ξEk​δ(Eq​+ξEP​−Ek​)​.
(35)
Since r2=k2=0 and r⋅k=ξP⋅q, the full parton response is
Wμν=Ek​δ(Eq​+ξEP​−Ek​)​[ξPμPν+2Pμqν+qμPν​−2ν​gμν].
(36)
Terms with a free q index vanish when contracted with the conserved leptonic tensor. Thus the part of W contributing to the cross-section is
Wμν ≐ Ek​δ(Eq​+ξEP​−Ek​)​[ξPμPν−2ν​gμν],​
(37)
where ≐ denotes equality in that contraction, rather than an equality of the full tensors. The omitted terms are necessary for the full hadronic tensor to be transverse; the wording about contributing terms in the PDF is important here.
To prove the delta identity with the correct Jacobian, set ω=Eq​+ξEP​. On physical support Ek​>0 and ω>0. Factoring Ek2​−ω2=(Ek​−ω)(Ek​+ω) gives the positive-energy on-shell delta function identity
Ek​δ(ω−Ek​)​=2δ(Ek2​−ω2).
(38)
This is restricted to the positive-energy branch; the other root must not be included. Using the spatial momentum constraint and P2=0 in the leading hard approximation,
Ek2​−ω2=−(q+ξP)2=Q2−2ξν=2ν(x−ξ).
(39)
The Dirac delta function change of variable therefore gives the parton on-shell delta identity
Ek​δ(Eq​+ξEP​−Ek​)​=2δ(Ek2​−(Eq​+ξEP​)2)=νδ(x−ξ)​.​
(40)
Here ν>0. This is the actual printed identity: the converted TeX's final δ(x)/Ek​ is a transcription error. If target mass is formally retained in the delta argument, it becomes Q2−2ξν−ξ2M2, so the stated simple root and Jacobian no longer apply.
Let D(x)=∑f​Qf2​(qf​(x)+q​f​(x)). The parton integral collapses at ξ=x, giving
WHμν​≐D(x)[νx​PμPν−21​gμν].
(41)
The transverse decomposition of an unpolarized hadronic tensor, in the same contraction, is −gμνF1​+PμPνF2​/ν. Comparing the two independent structures yields
F1​(x,Q2)=21​D(x),F2​(x,Q2)=xD(x),F2​=2xF1​.​
(42)
This derives the Callan-Gross relation from the spin-one-half trace and on-shell constraint. Antiquarks give the same symmetric response and therefore the sum qf​+q​f​. The result applies at leading order in the massless parton model; target masses, intrinsic transverse momentum and Quantum chromodynamics radiative effects can generate a nonzero longitudinal deep-inelastic structure function and modify the relation.
