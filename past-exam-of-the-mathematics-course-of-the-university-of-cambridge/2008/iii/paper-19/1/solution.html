A smooth vector field on a smooth manifold M is a smooth section X:M→TM of its tangent bundle. It acts on smooth functions by Xh(p)=dhp​(Xp​). In a manifold chart, X=∑i​Xi∂i​. The Lie bracket of vector fields is the commutator of their actions on functions:
[X,Y]h=X(Yh)−Y(Xh).
(1)
Expanding in coordinates makes all second derivatives cancel and gives
[X,Y]=∑i​(∑j​Xj∂j​Yi−Yj∂j​Xi)∂i​.
(2)
Thus the commutator is again a smooth vector field, not a second-order operator. The coordinate expression represents the intrinsic derivation on functions, so agrees on overlaps.
For a diffeomorphism f:M→N, its pushforward of a vector field is
(f∗​X)q​=dff−1(q)​Xf−1(q)​.
(3)
For every smooth function h on N, the chain rule gives ((f∗​X)h)∘f=X(h∘f). Applying this twice yields
([f∗​X,f∗​Y]h)∘f=X(Y(h∘f))−Y(X(h∘f))=([X,Y](h∘f))=((f∗​[X,Y])h)∘f.
(4)
Smooth functions determine a tangent vector, and f is surjective, so
f∗​[X,Y]=[f∗​X,f∗​Y].​
(5)
On a Lie group, a left-invariant vector field satisfies (Lg​)∗​X=X for every left translation Lg​(h)=gh. Given ξ∈Te​G, set
Xξ​(g)=(dLg​)e​ξ.
(6)
This field is smooth by smoothness of multiplication. The equality La​Lg​=Lag​ proves left invariance. Conversely, left invariance forces X(g)=(dLg​)e​X(e), so Xξ​ is the unique left-invariant field with value ξ at e. This is a linear isomorphism between Te​G and the space of left-invariant vector fields.
The pushforward identity applied to Lg​ shows that [Xξ​,Xη​] is again left invariant. Consequently
[ξ,η]=[Xξ​,Xη​](e)

defines a Lie bracket on Te​G. Bilinearity and antisymmetry follow from the operator commutator. Its Jacobi identity follows by expanding the three nested commutators: the six triple compositions cancel in pairs. These properties transfer through the linear isomorphism, making Te​G the Lie algebra of G.
