The initial-value convention is essential. A continuous finite-variation local martingale is constant, so the conclusion is Mt​=M0​ up to indistinguishability of stochastic processes. The stated zero conclusion uses the usual zero-start convention M0​=0; without it, Mt​≡1 is a counterexample.
Put N=M−M0​. Let Vt​ be its total-variation process, and stop when either ∣N∣ or V reaches n. The resulting process Nτn​ is a bounded continuous local martingale, hence a true martingale, and its total variation on every time interval is bounded by n. Fix t and take deterministic partitions 0=t0​<⋯<tk​=t whose mesh tends to zero. Orthogonality of martingale increments gives
E(Ntτn​​)2=∑j=1k​E(Ntj​τn​​−Ntj−1​τn​​)2.
