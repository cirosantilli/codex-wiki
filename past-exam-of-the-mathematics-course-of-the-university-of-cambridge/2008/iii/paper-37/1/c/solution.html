Work first with M0​=0. More generally, the assertion needs M0​∈L2 and then applies to M−M0​. Without this assumption, an integrable but non-square-integrable initial random variable kept constant in time has zero quadratic variation but is not bounded in L2 space.
Choose increasing stopping times
τn​=inf{t:∣Mt​∣≥n or [M]t​≥n}.
(5)
They tend to infinity almost surely, because the continuous path and its quadratic variation are finite on every compact time interval. The stopped process is a bounded martingale, and Mt∧τn​2​−[M]t∧τn​​ is bounded and a local martingale. Taking expectations gives
EMt∧τn​2​=E[M]t∧τn​​.
(6)
Suppose C=E[M]∞​<∞. These squared expectations are at most C. For each fixed t, the stopped values converge almost surely to Mt​ and are uniformly integrable, since they are uniformly bounded in L2 space. They therefore converge in L1. Passing in the stopped conditional-expectation identity, using contraction of conditional expectation in L1, gives E(Mt​∣Fs​)=Ms​ for s≤t. Thus M is a true martingale. The Fatou lemma gives EMt2​≤C, uniformly in t, so it is an L2-bounded continuous martingale.
Conversely, suppose M is a true martingale with supt​EMt2​≤C. For fixed t, bounded-time optional sampling theorem gives Mt∧τn​​=E(Mt​∣Ft∧τn​​). Conditional Jensen inequality then yields
E[M]t∧τn​​=EMt∧τn​2​≤EMt2​≤C.
(7)
The brackets increase to [M]t​. Applying the monotone convergence theorem first in n and then in t↑∞ proves E[M]∞​≤C<∞.
The exact identities and terminal limits can also be justified. On each fixed horizon t, the Doob L2 maximal inequality gives
Esups≤t​∣Ms​∣2≤4E∣Mt​∣2<∞.
(8)
This is an integrable dominating variable for Mt∧τn​2​, so the dominated convergence theorem and increasing convergence of the brackets remove the stopping:
EMt2​=E[M]t​.
(9)
The L2 martingale convergence theorem supplies M∞​ with Mt​→M∞​ almost surely and in L2 space. Consequently the squares converge in L1, while [M]t​↑[M]∞​. We obtain the integrable terminal bracket criterion with the precise terminal identity
M is an L2-bounded martingale⟺E[M]∞​<∞,EM∞2​=E[M]∞​.​
