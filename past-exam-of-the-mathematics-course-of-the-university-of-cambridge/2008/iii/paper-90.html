Assume the shallow-water approximation: the horizontal scale is long compared with the depth, the hydrostatic approximation applies, and the cross-section-averaged velocity is approximately uniform. Neglect viscosity and drag for this part. Volume conservation gives the constant discharge Q=bhu. The steady horizontal momentum equation is
uux​=−g(H+h)x​,
(1)
so its integral is the Bernoulli equation head H=H+h+u2/(2g). The shallow-water specific energy measured above the bed is therefore
E(h;Q,b)=h+2gb2h2Q2​,E+H=H.​
(2)
The Froude number compares the flow velocity with the long-wave speed gh​:
F=gh​u​,E=h(1+2F2​),∂h∂E​​Q,b​=1−F2.​
(3)
At fixed Q,b, the shallow-water specific energy tends to infinity as h tends to zero or infinity. Its unique minimum occurs at the critical depth
hc​=(gb2Q2​)1/3,F=1,Ec​=23​hc​.
(4)
The two depth branches above that minimum are subcritical flow, F<1 with h>hc​, and supercritical flow, F>1 with h<hc​. The long-wave characteristic speeds relative to the bed are u±gh​. Thus a subcritical flow can receive information from downstream, whereas both characteristic curves travel downstream in a positive supercritical flow.
A hydraulic control is a critical section where one characteristic speed vanishes and a smooth flow can pass between the two branches. Regularity there supplies a condition relating Q, the depth and the geometry; together with reservoir or inlet data, it can determine the discharge. Merely finding F=1 is not enough: a finite depth gradient also requires the geometric forcing to vanish in the critical momentum equation.

The shallow-water approximation assumes a horizontal length scale ℓ much greater than the current depth, h/ℓ≪1. Vertical acceleration is then small compared with gravity and pressure is given by the hydrostatic approximation. A depth-averaged model also assumes that the horizontal velocity and excess mass density can be represented by nearly uniform cross-sectional values. Here the ambient fluid is deep or sufficiently undisturbed that its motion and interface-pressure perturbations can be neglected.
The Boussinesq approximation assumes ∣ρ−ρ0​∣/ρ0​≪1: mass density differences are retained in gravitational forces but a common reference mass density is used for inertia. A dense bottom current has the positive reduced gravity
g′=gρ0​ρ−ρ0​​>0.​
(16)
Thus the pressure excess over the ambient hydrostatic value at height z within the current is ρ0​g′(h−z). We neglect bed drag, molecular diffusion and horizontal turbulent stress in the leading balances, but retain the prescribed turbulent entrainment across the interface. Negligible bed drag does not imply that entrainment has no effect on the current's velocity or mass density.

Hindered settling is the reduction of the particle settling velocity by displaced-fluid backflow and interactions with other particles. In the specified model, the downward speed is Vs​ at vanishing concentration and falls to zero at the maximum packing fraction ϕmax​. The characteristic speed of a concentration disturbance will differ from this individual-particle speed.
Take z positive upwards. For a locally uniform horizontal suspension without diffusion or bulk fluid motion, the upward particle flux is
j(ϕ)=−ϕV(ϕ)=−Vs​ϕ+ϕmax​Vs​​ϕ2.
(29)
Particle volume conservation gives the scalar conservation law
ϕt​+∂z​j(ϕ)=0,ϕt​+(−Vs​+ϕmax​2Vs​ϕ​)ϕz​=0.​
(30)
This is the quadratic hindered-settling flux. Along its characteristic curves,
dtdϕ​=0,dtdz​=c(ϕ)=−Vs​+ϕmax​2Vs​ϕ​.​
(31)
For a smooth initial profile ϕ0​(ζ), the map is z=ζ+c(ϕ0​(ζ))t. Its Jacobian is 1+(2Vs​/ϕmax​)ϕ0′​(ζ)t. A shock develops where this first reaches zero: the compressive condition is ϕ0′​<0, and
ts​=−2Vs​minζ​ϕ0′​(ζ)ϕmax​​
(32)
when the minimum is negative and no earlier boundary discontinuity is imposed. A discontinuous compressive initial state can instead contain a shock from t=0.
Let z=s(t) separate states ϕL​ below the shock and ϕR​ above it. Integrating the conservation law across a moving thin interval gives the Rankine-Hugoniot condition
s˙(ϕR​−ϕL​)=j(ϕR​)−j(ϕL​).
(33)
Equivalently, the flux relative to the shock agrees on both sides. For this quadratic flux,
s˙=−Vs​+ϕmax​Vs​​(ϕL​+ϕR​)=2c(ϕL​)+c(ϕR​)​.​
(34)
Since j′′=2Vs​/ϕmax​>0, a physical compression shock has ϕL​>ϕR​ and satisfies c(ϕL​)>s˙>c(ϕR​): characteristic curves enter it from both sides. These conditions select the shock over a nonphysical expansive discontinuity.

Assume the carrier fluid has ambient mass density ρ0​, the particles have mass density ρp​>ρ0​, and volumes add. The well-mixed current then has
ρ=(1−ϕ)ρ0​+ϕρp​,g′=gρ0​ρ−ρ0​​=g0′​ϕ,g0′​=gρ0​ρp​−ρ0​​.​
(41)
Here g0′​ is the particle-fluid reference reduced gravity per unit volume fraction, which is the convention needed in the requested expansion. The initial current reduced gravity is g0′​ϕ0​; it should not be confused with g0′​ itself. The Boussinesq approximation requires ϕ(ρp​−ρ0​)/ρ0​≪1.
For particles to remain well mixed, the turbulent vertical mixing time must be short compared with the settling time. In a turbulent diffusion description with diffusivity Kz​, require h2/Kz​≪h/[Vs​(1−ϕ)], or Kz​≫hVs​(1−ϕ). Equivalently, characteristic turbulent vertical speeds should substantially exceed the particle settling velocity. Turbulence can maintain an approximately uniform concentration in the body while particles still settle relative to the fluid. At the impermeable bed, fluid has no normal transport into the solid boundary, but particles can reach and be captured by it. Assume no resuspension after capture.
Use a gravity-current box model of length L(t), uniform depth h(t) and uniform concentration ϕ(t). Neglect ambient entrainment and the small volume change caused by depositing dilute solids, so Lh=M remains constant. Take a constant front Froude number F>0 and the gravity-current front condition L˙=Fg′h​. With ϕmax​=1, the deposition flux is Vs​ϕ(1−ϕ) per unit bed area. The particle-volume budget is
dtd​(Mϕ)=−LVs​ϕ(1−ϕ).

The model assumes a fixed rear boundary, one advancing front, a deep stationary ambient, negligible drag during the inertial spreading regime, and a perfectly absorbing bed. The supplied hindered-settling law is retained in the deposition closure.
For 0<ϕ<1, eliminating time gives

The entrainment coefficient α is the ratio of the inward ambient-fluid velocity normal to a buoyant plume edge to the characteristic buoyant plume velocity. For an upward buoyant plume, take ue​=αw>0. There are two edges at half-width b, so the full-section volume source is 2ue​. Dividing every integral balance by two gives consistent half-section equations.
For top-hat plume profiles, volume conservation and mass conservation are
bt​+(bw)z​=αw,(ρb)t​+(ρbw)z​=ρ0​(z)αw.​
(48)
The mass entering the buoyant plume is ambient mass with local mass density ρ0​(z). The Boussinesq approximation uses a common reference mass density in inertia while retaining the mass density deficit in buoyancy. The mass equation may be kept to the first order in that deficit to derive its transport.
Multiply the volume equation by the time-independent ambient mass density. The product rule gives
(ρ0​b)t​+(ρ0​bw)z​=ρ0​αw+ρ0′​(z)bw.
(49)
Subtract the mass equation and multiply by g. The entrainment sources cancel exactly, leaving
∂t​[(ρ0​−ρ)gb]+∂z​[(ρ0​−ρ)gbw]=gρ0′​(z)bw=−ρ0​N2bw,​
(50)
where
N2=−ρ0​g​dzdρ0​​.​
(51)
For stable stratification, ρ0′​<0 and the buoyancy frequency N is real. Rising fluid moves into lighter ambient fluid and loses its mass density deficit relative to that local ambient. Thus environmental stratification reduces the buoyant plume's buoyancy flux, even though the ambient fluid entering by entrainment contributes no mass density deficit at its own height.

Keep the half-section convention of part (a). Define the mass density-weighted mass flux, buoyancy flux and momentum flux by
Q=ρbw,F=(ρ0​−ρ)gbw,M=ρbw2.​
(52)
The full-section fluxes are 2Q,2F,2M. These factors must be changed consistently if full fluxes are used; mixing a full-width flux with a half-width entrainment source would introduce a spurious factor two.
For positive Q,M, w=M/Q, ρb=Q2/M, and (ρ0​−ρ)gb=FQ/M. The mass equation in part (a), the momentum equation, and the buoyancy equation therefore become
∂t​(Q2/M)+Qz​Qt​+Mz​∂t​(FQ/M)+Fz​​=ρ0​αM/Q,=FQ/M,=−ρρ0​​N2Q.​​
(53)
These are direct algebraic rewritings with the mass density factors retained. In particular, FQ/M is the buoyancy inventory per unit height as well as the integrated buoyancy force. If desired, ρ can be reconstructed from F/(gQ)=ρ0​/ρ−1.
At leading Boussinesq order use a constant reference mass density R in the inertial fluxes, Q=Rbw, M=Rbw2, and write g′=g(ρ0​−ρ)/R so F=Rg′bw. Set ρ0​/ρ=1 in the stratification source and use N2≃−gρ0′​/R. The unsteady top-hat line-plume balances are then
∂t​(Q2/M)+Qz​=RαM/Q,Qt​+Mz​=FQ/M,∂t​(FQ/M)+Fz​=−N2Q.​
(54)
Equivalently the kinematic fluxes q=Q/R=bw, m=M/R=bw2 and f=F/R=g′bw obey the same equations with R removed from the first source. This explicit distinction between mass density-weighted and kinematic fluxes fixes the normalization for the power-law solution.

In a homogeneous ambient fluid, N=0 and R=ρ0​ is constant. Use kinematic fluxes or, equivalently, the primitive form of the unsteady top-hat line-plume balances:
bt​+(bw)z​=αw,(bw)t​+(bw2)z​=bg′,(bg′)t​+(bg′w)z​=0.
(55)
Here g′=g(ρ0​−ρ)/R>0 is the positive buoyant reduced gravity. Seek a nonsteady separable power-law ansatz with both time differentiation and vertical transport retained. Matching powers in the volume equation forces b∝z with no time dependence. Matching the two momentum terms then gives w∝z/τ, and the buoyancy force has g′∝z/τ2, where τ=t+t∗​>0 is a shifted age. Thus write
b=Bz,w=Aτz​,g′=Cτ2z​.
(56)
Substitution determines the constants rather than just the exponents. The volume equation gives 2BA=αA, so for a nonzero buoyant plume B=α/2. The momentum equation gives C=−A+3A2, while the buoyancy equation gives C(−2+3A)=0. The buoyant nontrivial branch has A=2/3 and C=2/3. Consequently the separable decaying top-hat line plume is
b=2αz​,w=3τ2z​,g′=3τ22z​,ρ=R(1−3gτ22z​).​
(57)
The Boussinesq approximation requires 2z/(3gτ2)≪1. The corresponding mass density-weighted fluxes, to this order, are
Q=3τRαz2​,M=9τ22Rαz3​,F=9τ32Rαz3​.​
(58)
For a direct check, Q2/M=Rαz/2 is time independent and FQ/M=Rαz2/(3τ2). Hence (Q2/M)t​+Qz​=2Rαz/(3τ)=RαM/Q, Qt​+Mz​=Rαz2/(3τ2)=FQ/M, and (FQ/M)t​+Fz​=0. This verifies all three balances and the required width coefficient.
This nonsteady branch is not the steady, continuously supplied, constant-flux line plume. The latter has constant w and half-width b=αz in the same top-hat convention. Also, the unshifted power law has Q,F→0 as z→0 and is singular at τ=0. It is therefore an interior separable solution, not by itself a solution with a nonzero continuously operating point-source flux at the origin. A finite-width source boundary, a virtual origin, or a matching region is needed to connect it to physical source and initial data. For example, replacing z by z+z∗​ supplies a finite base width and time-varying positive base fluxes when z∗​>0. No source or initial flux history is specified in the question to select that matching; the requested power-law width follows from the local balances above.
