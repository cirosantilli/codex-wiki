Identify the ambient real four-dimensional vector space with 2×2 real matrices by
X(u,v,x,y)=(u+xy−v​y+vu−x​),−detX=−u2−v2+x2+y2.
(27)
For A,B∈SL(2,R), the linear action X↦AXB−1 preserves this quadratic form. It gives a group homomorphism to the special orthogonal group of signature (2,2); its determinant on matrix space is (detA)2(detB)−2=1. The group SL(2,R) is connected: an oriented Gram–Schmidt factorization writes every element as a rotation times an upper triangular matrix with positive diagonal and determinant one, and each factor varies in a connected space. Thus the image lies in the identity component SO0​(2,2).
If AXB−1=X for every X, taking X=I gives A=B. A matrix commuting with every real 2×2 matrix is scalar, and its determinant-one condition then gives A=B=±I. The kernel is therefore exactly {(I,I),(−I,−I)}. Infinitesimally the action is X↦aX−Xb, with a,b traceless. If this vanishes for every X, then a=b and it is scalar and traceless, hence zero. The differential is injective; source and target Lie algebras each have dimension six, so it is an isomorphism. The image is therefore an open subgroup of the connected target, which must be the entire target. Consequently
SO0​(2,2)≅{(I,I),(−I,−I)}SL(2,R)×SL(2,R)​.​
(28)
The finite quotient is by the diagonal central subgroup, not by a sign in just one factor. The connected-component subscript is a genuine qualification of the source's displayed formula: the full SO(2,2) is disconnected and cannot be isomorphic to a quotient of the connected product.
Let the anti-de Sitter radius be ℓ>0. Its quadric is −u2−v2+x2+y2=−ℓ2, or detX=ℓ2. Thus the map X↦g=X/ℓ identifies this quadric smoothly and globally with SL(2,R). This is the group model of three-dimensional anti-de Sitter spacetime. The induced Lorentzian metric is bi-invariant, since the two matrix multiplication actions preserve the ambient quadratic form. At the identity, tangent matrices a,b are traceless and
gℓ​(a,b)=2ℓ2​Tr(ab).
(29)
Indeed, for a traceless matrix a, deta=−21​Tr(a2), and polarizing −det(ℓa) gives this metric. For sl2​(R) the Killing form is K(a,b)=4Tr(ab): in the basis H=diag(1,−1), E=(00​10​) and F=(01​00​), the brackets are [H,E]=2E, [H,F]=−2F, [E,F]=H, giving K(H,H)=8, K(E,F)=4 and the remaining independent pairings zero. Therefore gℓ​=ℓ2K/8.
The canonical torsion-free connection on a Lie group is the Levi-Civita connection of this bi-invariant pseudo-Riemannian metric. Its Ricci tensor, already calculated from the double bracket, is
Ric=−41​K=−ℓ22​gℓ​,R=−ℓ26​.​
(30)
Substitution into the vacuum Einstein field equations gives
Ric−21​Rgℓ​+Λgℓ​=(ℓ21​+Λ)gℓ​=0,Λ=−ℓ−2.​
(31)
Thus the group metric solves the three-dimensional vacuum equations with negative cosmological constant.
The antipodal map X↦−X is g↦−g, multiplication by the central element −I. Hence the antipodal quotient is PSL(2,R)=SL(2,R)/{±I}, with the same local curvature and cosmological constant. To see the global time issue, use
u=ℓcoshρcost,v=ℓcoshρsint,x=ℓsinhρcosφ,y=ℓsinhρsinφ.
(32)
The induced metric is
ds2=ℓ2(−cosh2ρdt2+dρ2+sinh2ρdφ2).
(33)
On the quadric t has period 2π; curves with fixed ρ,φ are closed timelike curves. The antipodal identification is (t,φ)∼(t+π,φ+π), and does not eliminate this problem. At ρ=0, even the half-period time shift closes after the antipodal quotient.
If global time is not identified, take the universal cover, with t∈R. The topology of SL(2,R) is S1×R2, as follows from the same rotation–triangular decomposition, so the universal cover is diffeomorphic to R3 and carries the lifted group structure SL(2,R)​. The metric and Einstein equation are unchanged locally. Here t is a global time function with timelike gradient, excluding closed causal curves. Unwrapped anti-de Sitter spacetime is the universal covering group, rather than the original embedded quadric or its antipodal quotient; retaining the antipodal time-shift identification would wrap time again.
