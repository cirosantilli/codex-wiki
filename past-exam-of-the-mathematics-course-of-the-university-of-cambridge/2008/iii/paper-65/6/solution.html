A Poisson manifold is a smooth manifold with a smooth antisymmetric bivector π=21​πij∂i​∧∂j​ whose function bracket {f,g}=πij(∂i​f)(∂j​g) satisfies the Jacobi identity. The bracket is automatically a derivation in each argument. A symplectic manifold has a nondegenerate differential two-form ω satisfying dω=0, that is, a closed differential form; nondegeneracy forces its dimension to be even. It determines a nondegenerate Poisson structure. We use matrix convention π=−ω−1, so that ιXH​​ω=dH corresponds to XH​(f)={f,H}.
Examples of genuinely nonsymplectic Poisson geometries include the zero bracket on R3 and the Lie-Poisson bracket {xi​,xj​}=ϵijk​xk​ on R3. Their three-dimensional underlying manifold cannot carry a nondegenerate two-form. In the latter case the Poisson rank is two away from the origin and zero at the origin, with spheres and the origin as symplectic leaves. Even in even dimension a given Poisson structure need not come from a symplectic form: π=x∂x​∧∂y​ on R2 satisfies Jacobi but vanishes along x=0. The statement concerns this degenerate structure, not a claim that R2 admits no other symplectic structure.
For any candidate bivector, applying the Jacobi identity to coordinate functions gives the necessary condition
Jijk:=πiℓ∂ℓ​πjk+πjℓ∂ℓ​πki+πkℓ∂ℓ​πij=0.​
(34)
It is sufficient as well: expanding {f,{g,h}} and its cyclic companions cancels all terms with second derivatives by antisymmetry of π. What remains is Jijk(∂i​f)(∂j​g)(∂k​h). This is the coordinate Jacobi condition for a Poisson bivector.
For a symplectic form, differentiate ωπ=−I to obtain ∂ℓ​π=π(∂ℓ​ω)π. Substituting this into the cyclic expression gives
Jijk=−πiaπjbπkc(∂a​ωbc​+∂b​ωca​+∂c​ωab​)=0,
(35)
because dω=0. Thus the inverse symplectic structure always satisfies the Jacobi identity. Conversely, for a nondegenerate Poisson structure, multiplying by three inverse matrices proves closure of its associated two-form.
In momentum space, choose Hamiltonian H(p)=E(p) and the constant Poisson bivector
πij=eϵijk​Bk​,π=e(B1​∂p2​​∧∂p3​​+B2​∂p3​​∧∂p1​​+B3​∂p1​​∧∂p2​​).​
(36)
Its Poisson bracket is {f,g}=eB⋅(∇f×∇g). The Hamiltonian vector field therefore gives
p˙​i​={pi​,E}=eϵijk​Bk​∂pj​​E=e(v×B)i​,v=∇p​E,
(37)
exactly the prescribed motion. The sign here uses the signed charge e as printed; an electron's physical charge may be negative. Since the magnetic field is uniform, every derivative of πij vanishes, so every term in Jijk is zero. Jacobi holds independently of the dispersion function E. This is the momentum-space Poisson structure for a uniform magnetic field.
For eB=0 this bivector has rank two and C(p)=B⋅p is a Casimir function of a Poisson manifold: {C,f}=0 for every f. Its symplectic leaves are the planes C=c. For axes with B=B0​e3​, the leaf form is ω=(eB0​)−1dp1​∧dp2​ in our inverse-sign convention. Also E˙={E,E}=0, so trajectories on a Fermi surface lie on its intersections with these planes, typically curves. The energy surface is invariant for this Hamiltonian, but need not be a Poisson submanifold; the bracket has been defined on the whole momentum space. If eB=0, the Poisson structure and motion vanish.
