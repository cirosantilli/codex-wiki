A fiber bundle with base B, fiber F and structure group G acting on F from the left consists of a projection π:E→B and local trivializations ϕi​:π−1(Ui​)→Ui​×F. On overlaps they satisfy
ϕi​ϕj−1​(x,f)=(x,gij​(x)⋅f),gij​gjk​=gik​.
(14)
The smooth transition functions are valued in G. A principal bundle is the case F=G with the left multiplication action on itself; its transition functions multiply the group coordinate from the left.
In each principal chart define (x,h)⋅a=(x,ha). This defines a global right action because gij​(x)(ha)=(gij​(x)h)a: coordinate changes commute with right multiplication. It is free, and acts transitively on each fiber. Thus the claimed right action follows from the left transition convention rather than being an additional choice.
If s:B→P is a global section of a fiber bundle, define Φ:B×G→P by Φ(x,a)=s(x)a. Freeness and transitivity give exactly one a for every point in the fiber, so Φ is bijective and equivariant. In a local trivialization where s(x) has group coordinate hi​(x), the inverse group coordinate is hi​(x)−1h, proving smoothness of the inverse. Hence Φ is a principal-bundle trivialization. Conversely, the identity coordinate in a product trivialization gives s(x)=Φ(x,e). Therefore
P is trivial as a principal bundle⟺P has a global section.​
(15)
This is the principal bundle trivialization by a global section criterion. It is special to principal bundles; a section alone need not trivialize an arbitrary fiber bundle.
For the quotient examples, assume the Lie subgroups are closed when smooth homogeneous-space statements are intended. The map G→G/H1​ is a principal bundle with structure group H1​ and right action g⋅h=gh; G/H1​ is a homogeneous space for left multiplication by G. Similarly, G→H2​\G has free transitive left H2​ action on its fibers. With the usual principal right-action convention one writes g⋅h=h−1g. The quotient H2​\G is a homogeneous space for right multiplication by G. These quotients are groups only when the respective subgroups are normal. Nonclosed immersed Lie subgroups can produce non-Hausdorff quotients, so an arbitrary Lie subgroup does not automatically give a smooth Hausdorff base.
Left H2​ and right H1​ multiplication commute, so taking their orbit equivalence relations in either order gives the same double coset set
H2​\G/H1​=(H2​\G)/H1​=H2​\(G/H1​).
(16)
This equality does not assert that the double quotient is a manifold or that either stage is a principal bundle. The H2​×H1​ action (a,b):g↦agb−1 can have stabilizers: they correspond to H2​∩gH1​g−1. If the induced action is free and proper, its quotient is a smooth principal-bundle quotient; without those hypotheses orbit dimensions can change. For example, with G=SO(3) and both subgroups the rotations fixing the third axis, double cosets are parametrized by e3​⋅ge3​∈[−1,1]. Interior and endpoint stabilizers differ, so this is not a uniform free principal-bundle construction.
For a principal bundle P and a left G action on F, the associated bundle is
P×G​F=(P×F)/∼,(pa,f)∼(p,af),
(17)
with projection [p,f]↦π(p). A local principal section gives coordinates [si​(x),f]↔(x,f), and its changes give precisely the left action of the principal transition functions on F. A linear action on a vector space gives an associated vector bundle.
For a metric of signature (r,s) on an n-manifold, an orthonormal frame at x is an isometry u:Rr,s→Tx​M. The orthonormal frame bundle O(M,g)→M has structure group O(r,s), acting on the right by u⋅a=u∘a. Its associated bundle for the standard representation is the tangent bundle, since
O(M,g)×O(r,s)​Rn⟶TM,[u,v]⟼u(v)
(18)
is well defined: (ua)(v)=u(av). Every tangent vector has exactly one such associated equivalence class, and local frame coordinates show that this is a vector-bundle isomorphism. The full frame bundle works similarly with GL(n,R).
Finally, choose a basis of the Lie algebra of a Lie group G. Its left translates are a global frame, yielding the explicit tangent bundle trivialization
G×g⟶TG,(g,v)⟼(dLg​)e​v.​
(19)
This proves parallelization of a Lie group by left translations and triviality of the full frame bundle. Choosing an inner product at the identity and translating it gives a left-invariant Riemannian metric; an orthonormal basis translates to a global orthonormal frame. The global section criterion therefore trivializes the corresponding orthonormal frame bundle as well. For any other positive-definite metric on G, smoothly applying the Gram-Schmidt process to this global frame gives the same conclusion. Thus the tangent and frame bundles of every Lie group are trivial; the orthonormal statement is with respect to a chosen metric as specified above.
