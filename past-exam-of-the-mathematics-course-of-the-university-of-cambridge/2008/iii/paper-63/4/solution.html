All geodesics of two-dimensional de Sitter spacetime can be written especially simply in the embedding. If P is the initial point and V the initial tangent, with P2=−1, P⋅V=0, V2=ε, the geodesic has normal ambient acceleration X′′=εX. Therefore

An isometry can move the chosen point to P=(0,1,0), namely (t,χ)=(0,0). For future-directed timelike geodesics take V=(coshα,0,sinhα), obtaining

For the complete observer χ=0, let d(χ,0)∈[0,π] be the shortest angular distance on the circle. Since light travels one unit of angular distance per unit conformal time, an event at (T,χ) can signal to the observer at some finite future time precisely when
d(χ,0)<2π​−T.​
(39)
Equality defines its future observer event horizon. Likewise an event can receive a signal emitted by the observer precisely when
d(χ,0)<T+2π​,​
(40)
and equality defines the past horizon. In the lift −π≤χ≤π, their branches are χ=±(π/2−T) and χ=±(T+π/2). The common communication region is the diamond ∣χ∣+∣T∣<π/2, the observer's static patch of de Sitter spacetime. These are the observer horizons in two-dimensional de Sitter spacetime. Every complete timelike observer has analogous horizons after a de Sitter isometry; they depend on the observer, rather than enclosing an intrinsic black-hole region.
Despite these horizons, each constant-T circle is a Cauchy hypersurface of the full spacetime: any inextendible causal curve crosses it exactly once. The global spacetime is therefore globally hyperbolic and has no Cauchy horizons. The conformal boundaries lie at infinite proper time for complete timelike geodesics and infinite affine distance for complete null geodesics, so their finite height in the diagram is not a physical incompleteness.
