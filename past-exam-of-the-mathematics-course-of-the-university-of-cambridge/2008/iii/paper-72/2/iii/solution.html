Use cylindrical coordinates and tracer density ν(R,z). A steady axisymmetric Jeans equation, with no mean radial or vertical flow, is
∂R∂(νvR2​​)​+∂z∂(νvR​vz​​)​+Rν​(vR2​​−vϕ2​​)=−ν∂R∂Φ​.
(18)
Write vR2​​=σR2​, vϕ2​​=vˉϕ2​+σϕ2​, and Vc2​=R∂R​Φ. Rearrangement gives the stellar asymmetric drift equation
Vc2​−vˉϕ2​=σϕ2​−σR2​−νR​∂R∂(νσR2​)​−νR​∂z∂(νvR​vz​​)​.
(19)
For an isotropic velocity ellipsoid, all three dispersions equal σ and the cross stress vanishes. Therefore the required general result is
vˉϕ​=Vc2​−Aσ2​,A=−dlnRdln(νσ2)​.​
(20)
The positive root is for a prograde galactic disk. A declining radial stress gives A>0: random motions supply some support, so mean stellar rotation is slower than cold-gas circular rotation. For Aσ2≪Vc2​, Vc​−vˉϕ​≃Aσ2/(2Vc​). The condition σ<Vc​ alone is insufficient; one also needs Aσ2≤Vc2​.
There is no universal numerical coefficient expressible from Vc​ and σ alone. For constant velocity dispersion and ν∝R−p, A=p. Thus p=2 gives the often-used estimate vˉϕ​=Vc2​−2σ2​, while p=1 gives a different answer with the same Vc​ and σ. For an exponential galactic disk tracer with ν∝e−R/Rd​ and σ2∝e−R/Rσ​, the exponential-disk asymmetric drift has A=R(Rd−1​+Rσ−1​).
Real galactic disks generally have anisotropic velocity dispersions, often σR​>σϕ​>σz​. In the small-random-motion epicyclic motion limit for a flat galaxy rotation curve, σϕ2​/σR2​≃1/2, so even the non-gradient term matters. The velocity ellipsoid can tilt, and bars or spiral structure can violate steady axisymmetry. Age-dependent galactic disk heating makes the lag population dependent. Gas has finite turbulent and thermal stresses too, though a cold gas disk often approximates Vc​ much better than a hot stellar population.
