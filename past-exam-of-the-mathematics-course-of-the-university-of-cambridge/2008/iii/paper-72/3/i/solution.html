Thus the model has a flat galaxy rotation curve, resembling the outer galaxy rotation curves of many spiral galaxies. Central rotation curves usually rise, and not all real curves are flat throughout. A pressure-supported elliptical galaxy does not itself rotate at this test-particle circular speed.
The model has divergent central mass density, although finite enclosed mass at each finite radius, and an infinite total mass because M(<R)∝R. Its potential is logarithmic, requiring an outer cutoff for a finite escape energy. A globally constant isotropic velocity dispersion, spherical symmetry, and one common profile for all gravitating components are further idealizations. Stellar light commonly has a quite different radial profile from the dark matter halo; real disks are flattened, and halos are neither exact isothermal spheres nor infinite. Tidal truncation also changes the distribution near the satellite boundary.
