The Hasse theorem for elliptic curves states
∣#E(Fq​)−(q+1)∣≤2q​.​
(1)
We prove it by the positive degree of an isogeny. Let π be the q-power Frobenius isogeny, of degree q. Its fixed points are precisely E(Fq​), so they form the kernel of 1−π. Since the differential of π is zero, the differential of 1−π is the identity; thus 1−π is a separable isogeny. Its degree is the number of its geometric kernel points. Therefore
N:=#E(Fq​)=deg(1−π).
(2)
We use the standard degree parallelogram law, together with deg[m]=m2: degree, extended by zero at the zero endomorphism, is a positive quadratic form on the endomorphism ring of an elliptic curve. Put t=1+q−N, the Trace of Frobenius. Polarizing the degree form gives, for every pair of integers m,n,
deg([m]−[n]π)=m2−tmn+qn2≥0.
(3)
Indeed, the values on 1 and π are 1 and q, and the cross coefficient is fixed by deg(1−π)=1+q−t. These are standard degree facts about isogenies of elliptic curves, which suffice without an assumption about characteristic or ordinariness.
For n=0, division by n2 shows x2−tx+q≥0 at every rational x=m/n. Density of the rational numbers and continuity of this polynomial make it nonnegative on the real line. Evaluating at its minimum x=t/2 gives q−t2/4≥0, whence ∣t∣≤2q​. Since N=q+1−t, this proves the estimate. This is the degree-form proof of the Hasse bound; it proves both sides simultaneously by positivity of one degree form.
