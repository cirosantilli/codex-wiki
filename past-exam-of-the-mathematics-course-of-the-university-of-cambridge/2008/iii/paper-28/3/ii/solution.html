Reduce P to P∈E(Fp​). Because p∤n, the multiplication-by-n morphism on the reduced elliptic curve is a separable, surjective morphism of degree n2. Choose a point Q​∈E(Fp​) with nQ​=P. Its coordinates lie in some finite field Fpd​.
Let L/Qp​ be the finite unramified extension with residue field Fpd​. The curve retains good reduction over L. Its smooth integral model and the Hensel lemma give surjectivity of good reduction over a local field, so lift Q​ to some Q0​∈E(L). This lift need not satisfy nQ0​=P exactly, but its error
R=P−nQ0​
(24)
has zero reduction and belongs to E1​(L), the kernel of reduction of an elliptic curve.
The parameter t=−x/y at infinity identifies E1​(L) with the maximal ideal mL​ equipped with the formal group of an elliptic curve. The integral power series for [n] and its inverse from part (i) remain integral over OL​. They converge on mL​: terms of degree k tend to zero in valuation because their coefficients are integral and the argument has positive valuation. Hence multiplication by n is bijective on E1​(L). Choose its unique solution T∈E1​(L) to nT=R, and put Q=Q0​+T. Then
Q∈E(L),nQ=nQ0​+R=P,L/Qp​ finite unramified.​
(25)
The residue-field extension supplies a division point modulo p, and the formal-group inverse corrects the error without introducing ramification. This is the local construction underlying the uniform unramified division field over a local field and unramified division torsors at good primes.
