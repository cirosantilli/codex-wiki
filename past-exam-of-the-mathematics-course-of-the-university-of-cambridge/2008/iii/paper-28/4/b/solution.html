Let K be a field, Ks​ a separable closure and GK​ its absolute Galois group. A discrete Galois module A is an abelian group with a continuous GK​-action, so each element has an open stabilizer. Galois cohomology uses continuous cochains. Its zeroth group is H0(K,A)=AGK​, and its first group is
H1(K,A)=Z1(K,A)/B1(K,A),
(41)
where
cστ​=cσ​+σcτ​,B1(K,A)={cσ​=σa−a:a∈A}.
(42)
Thus cocycles measure a failure of descent, and coboundaries account for changing a chosen lift. A short exact sequence of Galois modules gives a long exact sequence in group cohomology. Its connecting map sends an invariant element of a quotient to the cocycle σb−b of a chosen lift b; changing the lift changes this by a coboundary. These constructions provide the bridge between arithmetic divisibility and cohomology.
For Kummer theory, assume the characteristic does not divide n. The multiplicative exact sequence is
1⟶μn​⟶Ks×​,n,​Ks×​⟶1.
(43)
The final arrow is surjective because a root of Tn−a is separable for a=0. Hilbert's theorem 90 says H1(K,Ks×​)=0. Here is its usual finite-extension proof. For a multiplicative cocycle cσ​ on a finite Galois extension L/K, choose a∈L so that
A=∑σ∈Gal(L/K)​cσ​σ(a)=0.
(44)
Such an a exists by the Artin independence theorem. The cocycle identity gives τA=cτ−1​A. With b=A−1 this becomes cτ​=τb/b, a coboundary. Every continuous cocycle with values in Ks×​ is defined over a sufficiently large finite Galois extension, so the same result holds for the absolute group.
The long exact sequence in group cohomology now gives
H1(K,μn​)≅K×/(K×)n.​
(45)
The class of a maps to cσ​=σ(α)/α, where αn=a. This cohomological statement does not require μn​⊂K; it retains the actual Galois action on μn​. If all nth roots of unity do lie in K, the action is trivial, and after choosing a primitive root of unity the cocycles are characters into Z/nZ. Thus adjoining an nth root of a describes a cyclic extension of degree dividing n, and Kummer classes classify the corresponding characters. For n=2, this gives the familiar quadratic extensions and the square-class group of a field.
Now let K be a number field and E/K an elliptic curve. The multiplication-by-n morphism is a surjective isogeny on E(K), with kernel E[n]≅(Z/nZ)2. The Kummer exact sequence of an elliptic curve
0⟶E[n]⟶E(K)[n]​E(K)⟶0
(46)
yields
0⟶E(K)/nE(K)δ​H1(K,E[n])⟶H1(K,E)[n]⟶0.
(47)
Explicitly, choose Q with nQ=P and set δ(P)σ​=σQ−Q. This lies in E[n], satisfies the cocycle identity and is unchanged as a class if Q is replaced by another division point. If it is a coboundary σT−T for T∈E[n], then Q−T is rational over K and P=n(Q−T). Conversely such a rational division point makes the cocycle zero. This proves the injectivity of the Kummer map of an elliptic curve on E(K)/nE(K).
To prove the Weak Mordell-Weil theorem, it remains to prove that this particular cohomological image is finite. The full group H1(K,E[n]) need not be finite, so the injection alone is not enough. Choose a finite set S of finite places containing the bad-reduction places and those dividing n. At any other place, the construction in Question 3 works over the local field: reduce P, find an n-division point over a finite residue extension, lift it by the Hensel lemma, and correct its error by the inverse formal multiplication. Thus P has an n-division point over the maximal unramified extension. Choosing that lift locally makes the cocycle zero on inertia. For an arbitrary lift its restriction is a coboundary, so the cohomology class is unramified. Therefore the image lies in first Galois cohomology unramified outside a finite set:
δ(E(K)/nE(K))⊆HS1​(K,E[n]).
(48)
We prove the required finiteness of this group. Take a finite Galois extension L/K containing all of E[n] and μn​, and enlarge S to include any remaining ramification of L/K. Let SL​ be the places above it. The inflation-restriction exact sequence bounds the kernel of restriction to L by
H1(Gal(L/K),E[n]),
(49)
a finite group, since both its group of arguments and its group of values are finite. Over L the torsion module is constant and can be identified with μn2​, so Kummer theory gives
H1(L,E[n])≅(L×/(L×)n)2.
(50)
Restrictions of our unramified classes remain unramified outside SL​. If a Kummer class [a] is unramified at such a place v, its nth root can be taken in an unramified local extension. The extended valuation still has integer values, and nv(na​)=v(a). Hence v(a) is divisible by n. Each coordinate therefore lies in the S-unramified power class group
L(SL​,n)={[a]∈L×/(L×)n:v(a)≡0(modn) for v∈/SL​}.
(51)
This group is finite. Indeed, the ideal of a away from SL​ is an. Sending [a] to [a] yields the exact sequence
0⟶OL,SL​×​/(OL,SL​×​)n⟶L(SL​,n)⟶Cl(OL,SL​​)[n]⟶0.
(52)
For its kernel, a principal a lets us divide a by an nth power to obtain an SL​-unit. For surjectivity, an ideal class killed by n has an nth power which is principal away from SL​, giving such an a. The S-unit group is finitely generated and the ideal class group is finite; hence both end groups are finite, proving finiteness of S-unramified Kummer classes. Restriction has finite image in L(SL​,n)2 and finite kernel, so HS1​(K,E[n]) is finite. We conclude
#(E(K)/nE(K))<∞(n≥2),​
(53)
which is the Weak Mordell-Weil theorem and its Kummer-theoretic proof of the weak Mordell-Weil theorem.
This framework also defines the Selmer group of an elliptic curve by imposing that a class in H1(K,E[n]) lie in the local Kummer image at every completion. Its classes are unramified outside a fixed finite set, so the same finiteness proof applies. Its relation to rational points is
0⟶E(K)/nE(K)⟶Sel(n)(E/K)⟶Sha(E/K)[n]⟶0.
(54)
The Tate–Shafarevich group here measures the difference between locally soluble torsors and globally soluble ones. Finally, the weak theorem gives finite divisibility quotients; adding the height descent lemma and bounded-height finiteness proves the stronger Mordell-Weil theorem. Those height inputs are additional to the cohomological finiteness just established.
