A family (Yi​)i∈I​ of integrable random variables is uniformly integrable if
K→∞lim​i∈Isup​E[∣Yi​∣1{∣Yi​∣>K}​]=0.​
(45)
This controls the contributions of large values uniformly over the family, rather than merely bounding its L1 norms.
Put Xn​=E[Z∣Fn​] and An​(K)={∣Xn​∣>K}∈Fn​. The conditional Jensen inequality gives ∣Xn​∣≤E[∣Z∣∣Fn​], hence E∣Xn​∣≤E∣Z∣. The Markov inequality therefore gives
P(An​(K))≤KE∣Z∣​.
(46)
Using the measurability of An​(K) in the defining integral identity for conditional expectation, for every R>0 we get
E[∣Xn​∣1An​(K)​]​≤E[∣Z∣1An​(K)​]≤E[∣Z∣1{∣Z∣>R}​]+RP(An​(K))≤E[∣Z∣1{∣Z∣>R}​]+KRE∣Z∣​.​
(47)
The first term tends to zero as R→∞ because Z is integrable, by the dominated convergence theorem. Given ε>0, first choose R to make it less than ε/2, then choose K to make the second term less than ε/2. These choices work for every n. This proves uniform integrability of conditional expectations:
{E[Z∣Fn​]:n≥1} is uniformly integrable.​
(48)
In fact the argument works for an arbitrary family of sigma-algebras; nesting is not needed for this conclusion.
