Every dyadic interval at level n+1 lies in a dyadic interval at level n. Equivalently, bn​(U) is a measurable function of bn+1​(U), so Fn​⊆Fn+1​: these sigma-algebras form a filtration. Since the uniform distribution of U gives E∣f(U)∣=∫01​∣f(u)∣du<∞, the conditional expectations Xn​=E[f(U)∣Fn​] are integrable and adapted. The tower property of conditional expectation gives
E[Xn+1​∣Fn​]=E[E[f(U)∣Fn+1​]∣Fn​]=E[f(U)∣Fn​]=Xn​.
(49)
Thus (Xn​) is a martingale, and part (a) proves that it is uniformly integrable.
For In,k​=[k2−n,(k+1)2−n), the event {U∈In,k​} is an atom of a sigma-algebra Fn​, with probability 2−n. The value of the conditional expectation there is therefore
P(U∈In,k​)E[f(U)1{U∈In,k​}​]​=2n∫In,k​​f(u)du.

Consequently Xn​=fn​(U) almost surely.​ This also shows directly that ∫01​∣fn​∣≤∫01​∣f∣.
By the uniformly integrable martingale convergence theorem, there is an integrable Y such that Xn​→Y almost surely and in the L1 norm. To identify this limit, put F∞​=σ(⋃n​Fn​). Since 0≤U−bn​(U)<2−n away from the harmless endpoint, U=limn​bn​(U), and U is F∞​-measurable. Conversely every bn​(U) is a measurable function of U. Thus F∞​=σ(U), and both Y and f(U) have F∞​-measurable versions.
For A∈Fm​, the defining property of conditional expectation gives E[Xn​1A​]=E[f(U)1A​] whenever n≥m. Passing to the limit in the L1 norm yields
E[Y1A​]=E[f(U)1A​].
(51)
The increasing union ⋃m​Fm​ is an algebra of sets generating F∞​. The class of events for which this integral identity holds is a Dynkin system, so the pi-lambda theorem extends the identity to every A∈F∞​. Since both random variables are measurable there, uniqueness of conditional expectation gives Y=f(U) almost surely. We have proved that the dyadic conditional averages recover integrable functions:
fn​(x)⟶f(x)for Lebesgue-almost every x∈[0,1],​
(52)
because a probability-one assertion for U is a full-Lebesgue measure assertion for x. Likewise, the uniform distribution of U turns convergence in L1 into
∫01​∣fn​(u)−f(u)∣du=E∣Xn​−f(U)∣⟶0.​
