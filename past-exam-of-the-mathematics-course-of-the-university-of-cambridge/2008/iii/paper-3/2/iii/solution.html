A highest-weight vector is a nonzero weight vector v such that gα​v=0 for every positive root α. Its weight of a representation is a highest weight. For an irreducible finite-dimensional Lie algebra representation, this is the unique highest weight of a representation, and v generates the module; a reducible module can have several highest weights of different summands.
Here is an existence argument for every nonzero finite-dimensional module V. For each simple root, its root operators and coroot form an sl2 subalgebra associated with a root. The classification of finite-dimensional sl2 representations shows that this coroot acts diagonalizably with integral eigenvalues. These commuting coroots span the Cartan subalgebra, so simultaneous diagonalization gives a finite weight-space decomposition
V=⨁λ​Vλ​.
(25)
Choose a real linear functional ℓ on the weight lattice span that is positive on every simple root, hence on every positive root, and choose a weight λ maximizing ℓ among the finitely many weights of V. If v∈Vλ​ and Xα​∈gα​, then
H(Xα​v)=Xα​Hv+[H,Xα​]v=(λ(H)+α(H))Xα​v,
(26)
so Xα​v belongs to Vλ+α​ if nonzero. For positive α, that would contradict maximality because ℓ(λ+α)>ℓ(λ). Thus every nonzero v∈Vλ​ is a highest-weight vector. Every nonzero finite-dimensional module therefore has a highest weight. The zero module has no nonzero vector or weight, so it is the vacuous exception to the literal unrestricted wording.
