Let α1​,α2​ be distinct simple roots. Their difference is not a root, since its simple-root coefficients have opposite signs. The root string through α2​ in the direction α1​ therefore starts at α2​ and has the form
α2​, α2​+α1​,…,α2​+qα1​,q=−⟨α2​,α1∨​⟩.
(44)
Here we use the root-string theorem: if the full string runs from β−pα through β+qα, then p−q=⟨β,α∨⟩; in this case p=0. Thus α1​+α2​ is a root exactly when A12​<0, equivalently when the corresponding Dynkin diagram nodes are connected.
The root-space bracket facts are [gα​,gβ​]⊆gα+β​, with a zero space when the sum is not a root, and [gα​,gβ​]=gα+β​=0 when the sum is a root and β=−α. Applying these stated structural facts proves
[gα1​​,gα2​​]=0⟺the two nodes are not connected.​
(45)
If the two names denote the same simple root, its one-dimensional root space has zero bracket with itself, and a Dynkin diagram has no self-edge. Thus the equivalence also holds in that case.
