Past exam of the mathematics course of the University of Cambridge / 2008 / iii / Paper 3 / 1 / b / iii / Solution

Use the positive simple-root raising operators E12​=x1​∂x2​​ and E23​=x2​∂x3​​. On the weight space of 2L1​−L3​, the first maps both s11​⊗s12​ and s12​⊗s11​ to s11​⊗s11​, while the second annihilates both. Consequently
v=s11​⊗s12​−s12​⊗s11​​
(11)
is a nonzero highest-weight vector; the remaining positive-root operator E13​=[E12​,E23​] also annihilates it. Its Dynkin labels are (2,1), and the generated submodule Z is the irreducible highest-weight representation with that highest weight.
Put F1​=E21​=x2​∂x1​​ and F2​=E32​=x3​∂x2​​, acting on both tensor factors. Direct calculation gives
F1​v=s11​⊗s22​−s22​⊗s11​,F2​v=s11​⊗s13​−s13​⊗s11​.
(12)
Define the linearly independent tensors
A=s11​⊗s23​−s23​⊗s11​,B=s12​⊗s13​−s13​⊗s12​.
(13)
They both have weight L1​, and
F2​F1​v=2A,F1​F2​v=A+2B.
(14)
Therefore A,B∈ZL1​​. To prove that they exhaust this weight space, its weight differs from the highest weight by (L1​−L2​)+(L2​−L3​). The Poincaré-Birkhoff-Witt theorem expresses all descendants at that difference using F2​F1​v and F1​F2​v: the third possible operator E31​=[F2​,F1​] is their difference. Hence the space has dimension at most two, and the two independent vectors already found give
ZL1​​=span{A,B}.​
(15)
