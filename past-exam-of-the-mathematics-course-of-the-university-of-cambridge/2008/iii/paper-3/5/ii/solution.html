We prove closure using complete reducibility of semisimple Lie algebra representations, rather than assuming that a Lie subalgebra is closed under polynomials in its elements. Write the matrix Jordan–Chevalley decomposition as X=S+N. On End(V), the commutator operator adS is diagonalizable: if S has eigenspaces Vλ​, then its eigenvalue on Hom(Vλ​,Vμ​) is μ−λ. The operator adN is nilpotent: if Nr=0, then

The two operators commute because [S,N]=0. Uniqueness of the Jordan–Chevalley decomposition therefore says that adS is the semisimple part of adX. Since a matrix's semisimple part is a polynomial in that matrix, and g is invariant under adX, it follows that
[S,g]⊆g.
(50)
Now regard End(V) as a Lie algebra representation of g under commutators. By complete reducibility of semisimple Lie algebra representations, there is an invariant complement M with End(V)=g⊕M. Write S=Y+C, with Y∈g and C∈M. For A∈g, the normalization property gives [C,A]=[S,A]−[Y,A]∈g, while invariance of M gives [C,A]∈M. Thus [C,A]=0: C commutes with the whole Lie algebra.

A semisimple Lie algebra is a perfect Lie algebra, so every element is a sum of Lie brackets and has trace zero on every finite-dimensional representation. Consequently tr(X∣Vj​​)=tr(Y∣Vj​​)=0. Also N∣Vj​​ is nilpotent, so it has trace zero; hence
tr(S∣Vj​​)=0,cj​dimVj​=tr(C∣Vj​​)=0.
(51)
