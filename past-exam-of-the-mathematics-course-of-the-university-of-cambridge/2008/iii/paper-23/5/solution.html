An affine algebraic group is a linearly reductive algebraic group if every finite-dimensional rational representation is completely reducible; equivalently the functor of invariants is exact. It is a geometrically reductive algebraic group if, whenever 0=v∈VG, there is a homogeneous invariant polynomial F∈k[V]G of positive degree with F(v)=0. Linear reductivity permits degree one; geometric reductivity allows higher degree.
Over the algebraically closed field, an algebraic torus is T≅(Gm​)r. Its coordinate ring is the Laurent polynomial ring. A rational action writes the coaction of a vector as a finite sum δ(v)=∑χ​vχ​⊗tχ. The coaction identity and linear independence of Laurent monomials give δ(vχ​)=vχ​⊗tχ and v=∑χ​vχ​. Thus
V=χ∈Zr⨁​Vχ​.​
(15)
Each weight space has scalar action, so any vector-space complement within each weight space is invariant. This proves complete reducibility, and hence tori are linearly reductive in every characteristic. The argument uses characters themselves, not their Lie algebra differentials, which could coincide modulo p.
We now prove geometric reductivity of G=SL2​(k) by determinant separation for positive-characteristic SL2. Fix a rational G-module V and a nonzero fixed vector v. For the diagonal torus, choose a weight-zero functional l∈V∗ with l(v)=1, using the torus decomposition just proved. Define matrix coefficients
Φ(w)(g)=l(g−1w).
(16)
These are regular functions on G, and Φ(hw)(g)=Φ(w)(h−1g) makes Φ equivariant for left translations. Moreover Φ(w)(gt)=Φ(w)(g) because l is torus-invariant. Thus Φ maps into k[G]T for the right torus action, and Φ(v)=1.
Write a matrix's two columns as a=(a0​,a1​) and b=(b0​,b1​), with Δ=a0​b1​−a1​b0​=1. Right torus multiplication scales these columns by t and t−1. Every right-torus-invariant regular function has a weight-zero polynomial lift, hence a sum of polynomials of bidegrees (j,j) in (a,b). To see that a weight-zero lift exists, take any lift and retain its zero-weight component; the ideal (Δ−1) is torus-stable and the quotient map preserves weights. Multiply each piece by Δn−j to put the entire sum in bidegree (n,n) without changing its restriction to G.
Let Pn​ be the space of degree-n polynomials in one column. Restriction gives an injective equivariant map Pn​⊗Pn​→k[G]T. Indeed if a bidegree-(n,n) polynomial vanishes on determinant-one matrices, scale the first column of any invertible matrix to make its determinant one. Homogeneity then makes the polynomial vanish on every invertible matrix, a dense open subset, so it is zero. Denote the image by Rn​. These spaces increase because multiplication by Δ realizes Rn−1​⊂Rn​, and their union is k[G]T. The finite-dimensional image Φ(V) therefore lies in one Rn​, and in any sufficiently larger filtration piece.
Choose q=ps with q−1 at least that bound, and set n=q−1. The identity (1+z)q=1+zq in characteristic p gives
(jq−1​)=(−1)j=0in k,0≤j≤q−1.
(17)
The invariant tensor
D=Δn=∑j=0n​(−1)j(jn​)a0n−j​a1j​b0j​b1n−j​∈Pn​⊗Pn​
(18)
therefore defines an invertible equivariant linear map D:Pn∗​→Pn​: its matrix is anti-diagonal with all entries nonzero. Interpret any tensor B∈Pn​⊗Pn​ as a map Pn∗​→Pn​ and send it to BD−1∈End(Pn​). Equivariance of D makes this identification intertwine the diagonal tensor action with conjugation on endomorphisms. Crucially, D restricts to the constant function 1 on G, and is sent to the identity endomorphism.
Under the resulting equivariant linear map V→End(Pn​), the vector v is sent to I. Since dimPn​=n+1=q, the endomorphism determinant is a homogeneous polynomial of positive degree q, invariant under conjugation. Pull it back to V. The resulting polynomial satisfies
F∈k[V]SL2​(k),degF=q=ps>0,F(v)=detI=1.​
(19)
This constructs the required invariant for every fixed nonzero vector and proves SL2​(k) is geometrically reductive, including characteristic two. No averaging by a group order or division by q is used; those operations would fail in the field's characteristic.
