For nonempty first-order structures (Mi​)i∈I​ and an ultrafilter U on I, define the ultraproduct by quotienting the product domain by
a∼b⟺{i:ai​=bi​}∈U.
(10)
Functions are interpreted coordinatewise; a relation holds when its coordinate truth set belongs to the ultrafilter. Closure under finite intersections makes these interpretations independent of representatives. The Łoś theorem asserts, for every formula φ,
i∏​Mi​/U⊨φ([a1],…,[ar])⟺{i:Mi​⊨φ(ai1​,…,air​)}∈U.​
(11)
Prove it by structural induction. A term evaluates coordinatewise, by induction on its construction, so atomic equality and relations are exactly their defining truth sets. Conjunction uses intersection; negation uses the ultrafilter property that exactly one of a set and its complement belongs to U. These establish all Boolean connectives.
For the existential step put A={i:Mi​⊨∃xψ(x,aˉi​)}. If an ultraproduct witness [b] exists, induction says {i:Mi​⊨ψ(bi​,aˉi​)}∈U, and this set is contained in A, so A∈U. Conversely if A∈U, choose a witness bi​ at every i∈A and any domain element at the other coordinates. Induction makes [b] a witness. This coordinatewise selection uses choice in the metatheory; nonemptiness of the factors is essential. Universal quantification follows from negation and existence, completing the proof.
For the requested compactness theorem, let I=[T]<ω. For each finite F⊆T, choose MF​⊨F. For each sentence σ∈T, its cone Cσ​={F:σ∈F} is nonempty, and intersections of finitely many cones contain the finite union of their requirements. These cones generate a proper filter. The ultrafilter lemma extends it to U. The coordinate models satisfying σ include Cσ​, so the Łoś theorem makes their ultraproduct satisfy σ. Since this holds for each σ∈T, the ultraproduct is a model of T. No completeness theorem is needed in this second compactness proof.
