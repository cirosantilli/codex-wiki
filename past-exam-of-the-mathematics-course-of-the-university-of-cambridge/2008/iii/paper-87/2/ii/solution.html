Since the particle starts at the origin, its Lagrangian trajectory satisfies X(t)=∫0t​v(s)ds. Differentiating the mean-square displacement gives
dtd​⟨∣X(t)∣2⟩=2⟨v(t)⋅∫0t​v(s)ds⟩=2∫0t​⟨v(t)⋅v(t−τ)⟩dτ.
(18)
Here we set τ=t−s and assume finite second moments so that averaging and integration commute. Under Lagrangian stationarity write the autocorrelation as RL​(τ). Uniform fluid-tracer sampling in homogeneous incompressible turbulence gives RL​(0)=⟨∣u∣2⟩. A second integration yields the exact Taylor turbulent dispersion formula
⟨∣X(t)∣2⟩=2∫0t​(t−τ)RL​(τ)dτ.​
(19)
For times short compared with velocity decorrelation, continuity at zero gives RL​(τ)≃RL​(0) throughout the integral. Thus
⟨∣X(t)∣2⟩≃⟨∣u∣2⟩t2.​
(20)
The particle retains nearly its initial velocity: this is ballistic transport.
If RL​ is integrable, then at long times the displacement derivative approaches 2∫0∞​RL​(τ)dτ=2RL​(0)tL​, using the Lagrangian integral time. Therefore
⟨∣X(t)∣2⟩∼2⟨∣u∣2⟩tL​t.​
(21)
This is diffusive transport: successive long-time displacements lose memory of one another. In an isotropic three-dimensional diffusion equation, ⟨X2⟩=6DT​t, so DT​=⟨∣u∣2⟩tL​/3. The shorthand t≪tL​ and t≫tL​ presumes that tL​ is representative of the decorrelation time. With strongly oscillatory correlations, the area-based integral time need not characterize every crossover; the exact integral formula remains the appropriate starting point.
