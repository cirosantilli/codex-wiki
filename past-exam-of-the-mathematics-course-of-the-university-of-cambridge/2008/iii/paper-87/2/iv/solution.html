Use SC​(r)=⟨(C′−C)2⟩ for the scalar structure function, and assume statistical homogeneity as well as isotropy. Expanding the square gives
SC​(r)=2⟨C2⟩−2⟨CC′⟩.
(27)
If the zero-mean scalar decorrelates at large separation, then
SC​(r)⟶2⟨C2⟩.​
(28)
The decorrelation assumption is necessary: a spatially constant, zero-ensemble-mean random scalar is isotropic but has SC​=0 at every separation.
For the small-separation limit assume mean-square differentiability. Taylor expansion gives C′−C=ri​∂i​C+o(r) in mean square. Isotropy implies ⟨∂i​C∂j​C⟩=δij​⟨∣∇C∣2⟩/3. Define the scalar dissipation rate in the half-variance convention, ϵc​=α⟨∣∇C∣2⟩, so that diffusion removes ⟨C2/2⟩ at rate ϵc​. It follows that
SC​(r)=3αϵc​​r2+o(r2).​
(29)
If dissipation is instead defined as the loss rate of the full variance, the coefficient must be adjusted by a factor of two.
In the inertial-convective scalar range, max(η,ηc​)≪r≪ℓ, suppose scalar variance cascades locally at an approximately constant flux ϵc​, while velocity increments have the Kolmogorov 1941 theory turnover time tr​∼ϵ−1/3r2/3. The variance associated with scale r divided by that turnover time is of order its transfer rate, giving the Obukhov-Corrsin theory result
SC​(r)∼ϵc​tr​∼ϵc​ϵ−1/3r2/3.​
(30)
The assumptions include a passive scalar, high Reynolds number, local transfer, adequate scale separation, and statistical equilibrium or sufficiently slow variation of the supply. Isotropy alone does not provide a constant scalar flux.
For weak scalar diffusion, α≪ν, the velocity is smooth below η, with characteristic strain rate s∼(ϵ/ν)1/2. Diffusion rate α/r2 balances this at the Batchelor scalar microscale
ηc​=ηB​∼(α/s)1/2=(α2ν/ϵ)1/4.
(31)
In the viscous-convective scalar range, the derivative of SC​ is assumed to depend only on ϵc​, s and r. Their dimensions force
drdSC​​∼srϵc​​.
(32)
Integration from the diffusion cutoff gives
SC​(r)=SC​(ηc​)+AB​sϵc​​ln(ηc​r​).
(33)
Thus, well within a broad logarithmic range,
SC​(r)∼ϵc​ϵν​​ln(r/ηc​).​
(34)
The additive matching contribution is not exactly zero; the formula is a leading range estimate, not a boundary condition at r=ηc​. Using dSC​/dr isolates the contribution acquired on changing separation. The structure function itself contains scalar variance accumulated from smaller scales and must retain the inner cutoff through its integration constant. If one assumed that SC​ depended only on ϵc​,s,r without that cutoff, dimensional analysis would incorrectly give an r-independent value proportional to ϵc​/s. This explains the use of the derivative with respect to r; the TeX aid changes that derivative to t in its final sentence, but the original PDF consistently uses r.
Finally let α≫ν. Balancing diffusion with the inertial eddy rate gives the Corrsin scalar microscale ηc​=ηC​∼(α3/ϵ)1/4≫η. For η≪r≪ηc​, diffusion is faster than the eddy turnover rate. The scalar is smooth to leading order on those separations, even though the velocity still has inertial-range fluctuations. The inertial-diffusive scalar range therefore has
SC​(r)≃3αϵc​​r2,η≪r≪ηc​.​
(35)
This continues the scalar's leading gradient-controlled quadratic behaviour; it is not a continuation of the inertial-convective two-thirds law below its diffusion cutoff.
