Let C(r)=⟨u(x)⋅u(x+r)⟩, with zero mean velocity, and normalize the turbulent energy spectrum by ∫0∞​E(k)dk=⟨∣u∣2⟩/2. Use the Fourier transform pair
C(r)=∫eik⋅rC(k)d3k,C(k)=(2π)31​∫e−ik⋅rC(r)d3r.
(1)
Isotropic turbulence makes the transform radial, while the energy normalization gives E(k)=2πk2C(k). Thus
E(k)=4π2k2​∫C(r)krsin(kr)​d3r.
(2)
Assume, for example, ∫(1+r4)∣C(r)∣d3r<∞, sufficient to control the remainder. Expanding the radial kernel gives sin(kr)/(kr)=1−k2r2/6+O(k4r4). With the Saffman integral L=∫C(r)d3r and Loitsyansky integral I=−∫r2C(r)d3r, this proves
E(k)=4π2Lk2​+24π2Ik4​+O(k6).​
(3)
The original PDF defines the two distinct integrals; the TeX aid accidentally repeats the definition of I in place of L.
For a growing volume V, let PV​=∫V​udV be its momentum per unit density. Statistical homogeneity gives
⟨∣PV​∣2⟩=∫C(r)∣V∩(V−r)∣d3r.
(4)
For volumes whose boundary-to-volume ratio tends to zero and integrable C, divide by ∣V∣ and use dominated convergence:
L=∣V∣→∞lim​∣V∣⟨∣PV​∣2⟩​≥0.​
(5)
If the volume contains many weakly dependent eddies with uncompensated random impulses, the central limit theorem suggests root-mean-square total momentum proportional to ∣V∣1/2; its variance is extensive and L is nonzero. This is a physical expectation, not a theorem for all turbulent fields: cancellation between cells can eliminate the extensive term.
In particular L=0 for an ensemble built from locally momentum-compensated eddies with zero net impulse, or when the complete incompressible velocity correlation tensor decays rapidly enough to have no longitudinal r−3 tail. Merely imposing zero total momentum in one finite periodic box is insufficient to prove L=0 in an infinite-volume limit: the finite-box constraint fixes one global mode. Rapid decay of the trace C alone is also insufficient; the individual tensor components can retain algebraic tails whose trace cancels.
