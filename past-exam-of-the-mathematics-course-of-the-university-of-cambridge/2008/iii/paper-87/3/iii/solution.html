For isotropic turbulence with incompressible flow, use the spectral velocity correlation tensor
Φij​(k)=4πk2E(k)​(δij​−k2ki​kj​​),Qij​(r)=∫Φij​(k)eik⋅rd3k.
(43)
Choose the polar axis along r and put μ=k⋅r. The longitudinal velocity correlation becomes
QLL​(r)=21​∫0∞​E(k)dk∫−11​(1−μ2)eikrμdμ.
(44)
Starting with ∫−11​eixμdμ=2sinx/x and differentiating twice in x evaluates the weighted angular integral:
∫−11​(1−μ2)eixμdμ=x34(sinx−xcosx)​.
(45)
Therefore QLL​(r)=2∫E(k)(sin(kr)−krcos(kr))/(kr)3dk. At zero separation QLL​(0)=(2/3)∫E(k)dk. Using S2​=2[QLL​(0)−QLL​(r)] proves the exact structure-function spectral filter:
43​S2​(r)=∫0∞​E(k)H(kr)dk,H(x)=1+x23cosx​−x33sinx​.​
(46)
The apparent singularity is removable: expansion gives H(x)=x2/10+O(x4) at zero, while H(x)→1 at infinity. Substitute the given approximate filter and split the integral at kc​=π/r. This gives
43​S2​(r)≃∫π/r∞​E(k)dk+π2r2​∫0π/r​k2E(k)dk.​
(47)
The second term is the contribution of larger eddies: their common velocity cancels, but their velocity gradient creates an increment proportional to r. Their squared gradient contributes the k2 weight. The integral ∫k2E(k)dk is the enstrophy in the convention ⟨∣ω∣2⟩/2, so the increment contains both small-scale energy and cumulative larger-scale enstrophy.
This makes the longitudinal structure function a poor diagnostic of a finite inertial range. With a limited range of k−5/3 spectrum, the two integrals retain outer-scale and dissipation-scale contributions, leading schematically to a mixture a0​+a2​r2+a2/3​r2/3. The local logarithmic slope need not then equal 2/3, even if the spectral inertial interval exhibits a clearer −5/3 slope. This finite-range effect was demonstrated in Davidson and Krogstad's 2008 controlled-field study. It challenges interpreting S2​(r) as energy exclusively at scale r; it does not refute the formal infinite-inertial-range two-thirds law, since inserting an ideal k−5/3 spectrum into the exact transform does produce r2/3 scaling.
