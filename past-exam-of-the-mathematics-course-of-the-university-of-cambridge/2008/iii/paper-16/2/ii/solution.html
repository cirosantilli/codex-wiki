Past exam of the mathematics course of the University of Cambridge / 2008 / iii / Paper 16 / 2 / ii / Solution

We first prove the needed Petridis minimal-growth lemma. For finite nonempty sets A,D, choose a nonempty X⊆A minimizing K0​=∣X+D∣/∣X∣. Thus ∣Y+D∣≥K0​∣Y∣ for every Y⊆X, including the empty set. For any finite T={t1​,…,tr​}, define
Xi​={x∈X:x+ti​∈/⋃j<i​(X+tj​)}.
(14)
The sets Xi​+ti​ partition X+T. If x∈/Xi​, then x+ti​∈X+tj​ for some j<i, and therefore x+D+ti​⊆X+D+tj​. The newly contributed portion of X+D+ti​ consequently has size at most
∣X+D∣−∣(X∖Xi​)+D∣≤K0​∣Xi​∣.
(15)
Sum over i to obtain ∣X+D+T∣≤K0​∣X+T∣. Apply this successively with T=(r−1)D to get ∣X+rD∣≤K0r​∣X∣.
Use D=−A. The hypothesis implies K0​≤∣A−A∣/∣A∣≤C, so ∣X−2A∣≤C2∣X∣. We also prove the Ruzsa triangle inequality: for finite nonempty U,V,W,
∣U−W∣∣V∣≤∣U−V∣∣V−W∣.
(16)
For each s∈U−W, fix us​,ws​ with s=us​−ws​. The map (s,v)↦(us​−v,v−ws​) is injective: summing its coordinates recovers s, and then its first coordinate recovers v. This proves the inequality.
Apply it with U=W=2A and V=X. Both right-hand factors have cardinality ∣X−2A∣, giving
∣2A−2A∣≤∣X∣∣X−2A∣2​≤C4∣X∣≤C4n.
(17)
Thus K=C4 suffices​. This is the required case of the Plünnecke-Ruzsa inequality, proved here using the minimal-growth argument and the explicit injection. If A is empty the conclusion holds directly.
