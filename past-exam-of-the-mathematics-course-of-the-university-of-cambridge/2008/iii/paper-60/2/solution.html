The physical space of n qubits is Hn​=(C2)⊗n. An error operator is a linear operator on this space, for example one Kraus operator of a noise quantum channel. The usual channel description assumes initially uncorrelated system and environment, with the environment subsequently ignored; more general initial correlations need not define a channel on arbitrary system inputs. Finite dimension and the tensor decomposition into identifiable qubits allow an exact Pauli expansion of a quantum error. No independence between errors on different qubits is assumed by this expansion.
The 4n tensor products of I,X,Y,Z form an orthogonal basis of the operator space under the Hilbert-Schmidt inner product. Equivalently, absorbing phases into coefficients, every error has an expansion
E=∑a,b∈F2n​​ca,b​XaZb,Xa=⨂j=1n​Xaj​,Zb=⨂j=1n​Zbj​.
(8)
For a computational basis vector ∣x⟩ labelled by x∈F2n​, this gives
E∣x⟩=a,b∑​ca,b​(−1)b⋅x∣x+a⟩,​
(9)
where addition and the dot product in the exponent are modulo two. Thus X changes a bit, Z changes its phase, and Y performs both up to a scalar phase. A general error is a coherent linear combination of such actions; it need not be a classical random choice of Pauli operators. A restriction to at most t faulty qubits is an additional locality assumption, expressed by the span of Pauli operators of weight at most t.
The weight of a Pauli operator is the number of tensor factors different from I, ignoring global phase. Let P be the orthogonal projector onto a 2k-dimensional code X. Quantum error detection means
PEP=cE​P.
(10)
Indeed, a projective measurement of P flags departure from the code, while its code-space outcome acts as the scalar cE​ on every encoded state and hence does not change logical information. Harmless scalar actions are included in this criterion. The quantum code distance is
d=min{wt(E):E is Pauli and PEP is not scalar on X}.
(11)
Thus every Pauli operator of weight at most d−1 is detectable. The condition is linear in E, so it holds for any error in their span. This proves detection of arbitrary errors affecting at most d−1 qubits, including coherent errors.
For correction, put t=⌊(d−1)/2⌋ and take all phase-free Pauli operators Ea​ of weight at most t. Their pairwise products satisfy
wt(Ea†​Eb​)≤2t≤d−1,PEa†​Eb​P=Cab​P.
(12)
These are the Knill--Laflamme conditions. Here is their recovery construction, rather than merely using the criterion's name. For any normalized code state ∣ψ⟩, the matrix C is a Gram matrix, since Cab​=⟨ψ∣Ea†​Eb​∣ψ⟩, and is therefore positive semidefinite. Choose linear combinations Fj​ of the errors diagonalizing this matrix, so that
PFj†​Fl​P=cj​δjl​P,cj​≥0.
(13)
For cj​>0, the operators Vj​=Fj​P/cj​​ are isometries from the code into mutually orthogonal error-image subspaces: Vj†​Vl​=δjl​P. Measure the projectors Qj​=Vj​Vj†​ and, on outcome j, apply Vj†​. Complete this recovery arbitrarily on the remaining orthogonal subspace to make a trace-preserving quantum channel.
For an error E=∑j​βj​Fj​ and any encoded density operator ρ, the recovered contribution is
∑j​Vj†​EρE†Vj​=(∑j​∣βj​∣2cj​)ρ.
(14)
Terms with cj​=0 annihilate the code. For a physical noise channel whose Kraus operators belong to this span, summing the displayed scalar over those operators gives one by trace preservation. The resulting recovered state is exactly ρ. This proves the constructive recovery from the Knill-Laflamme condition and the requested guarantee
[[n,k,d]] detects d−1 qubit errors and corrects ⌊2d−1​⌋.​
(15)
This argument allows degenerate codes; distinct physical errors may have identical actions on the code.
For the Shor code, define ∣G±​⟩=(∣000⟩±∣111⟩)/2​. Its two normalized basis codewords are
∣0L​⟩=∣G+​⟩⊗3,∣1L​⟩=∣G−​⟩⊗3.​
(16)
Six stabilizer generators are Z1​Z2​,Z2​Z3​,Z4​Z5​,Z5​Z6​,Z7​Z8​,Z8​Z9​; the two phase checks are
g7​=X1​X2​X3​X4​X5​X6​,g8​=X4​X5​X6​X7​X8​X9​.
(17)
All eight have eigenvalue +1 on both basis codewords. A phase error Z4​ commutes with the six Z checks and anticommutes with both g7​ and g8​. Their projective measurements therefore return
(+,+,+,+,+,+,−,−).​
(18)
The two negative phase checks identify the middle three-qubit block. Apply the Pauli Z gate Z4​ to restore the state, since Z42​=I. This procedure acts identically on every logical superposition, and the checks reveal no logical amplitudes. The syndrome cannot distinguish Z4​ from Z5​ or Z6​, but all three have the same action on the code: their pairwise products lie in the stabilizer group. Correcting with any one of them is sufficient. Thus the degeneracy in the syndrome is harmless.
