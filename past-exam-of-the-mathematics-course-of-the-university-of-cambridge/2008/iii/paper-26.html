A monad on C consists of an endofunctor T and natural transformations η:1→T, μ:T2→T satisfying μTη=μηT=1T​ and μTμ=μμT. Its Eilenberg-Moore category has objects (X,a) with a:TX→X, aηX​=1X​ and aμX​=aTa. A morphism f:(X,a)→(Y,b) satisfies fa=bTf. An adjunction F⊣G, with unit η and counit ε, induces T=GF, μ=GεF. It is a monadic adjunction if the Eilenberg-Moore comparison functor K(D)=(GD,GεD​) is an equivalence with the Eilenberg-Moore category.
A fork A⇉p,q​B→rC is a split coequalizer if rp=rq and there are s:C→B, t:B→A with rs=1, pt=1 and qt=sr. It is indeed a coequalizer: if hp=hq, then h=hpt=hqt=hsr, so h factors uniquely through r, which is split epic. Every functor preserves these splitting identities, making such coequalizers absolute.
The precise monadicity theorem states: for an adjunction F:C⇄D:G, the comparison is an equivalence if and only if G reflects isomorphisms and every pair in D whose G-image admits a split coequalizer has a coequalizer which G preserves. Equivalently, the right adjoint creates these split-underlying coequalizers, with creation understood up to the canonical isomorphism of the prescribed underlying coequalizer. Here is a proof.
First consider the forgetful functor from monad algebras. It reflects isomorphisms: the inverse of an invertible algebra morphism also obeys the algebra-morphism identity, by multiplying that identity by the inverses. For an algebra pair with a split underlying coequalizer r:B→C, all powers of T preserve its coequalizer. If b:TB→B is the algebra structure on B, the map rb coequalizes Tp,Tq, so there is a unique c:TC→C with
cTr=rb.
(1)
The unit law follows by composing with the epimorphism r: cηC​r=cTrηB​=rbηB​=r. For associativity, naturality and the algebra law give
cμC​T2r=cTrμB​=rbμB​=rbTb=cT(rb)=cTcT2r.
(2)
Cancel the epimorphism T2r to obtain cμC​=cTc. Thus (C,c) is an algebra and r is an algebra morphism. Any algebra morphism coequalizing the pair factors through r on underlying objects, and its factor is an algebra morphism by cancellation of the epimorphism Tr. This creates the coequalizer. Transporting these facts through an equivalence proves necessity.
For sufficiency, let (X,a) be a T-algebra. In D consider the free pair
FTX Fa⇉εFX​​​ FX.
(3)
Its underlying pair is μX​,Ta:T2X⇉TX, with split coequalizer a:TX→X: the splittings are s=ηX​ and t=ηTX​, using the algebra unit law, the monad unit law and naturality of η. By hypothesis its coequalizer q:FX→D exists and is preserved. Identify GD with X by the unique coequalizer isomorphism, so Gq=a. Naturality of the counit makes Kq an algebra morphism, hence the comparison structure c on GD satisfies cTa=aμX​=aTa. The map Ta is split epic because aηX​=1, so c=a. Every algebra is therefore isomorphic to a comparison algebra.
For an actual object D, its counit εD​:FGD→D is a coequalizer of the analogous free pair. It coequalizes that pair by counit naturality. Compare it with the coequalizer furnished by the hypothesis; applying G gives an isomorphism, since both underlying arrows coequalize the same split pair. Reflection of isomorphisms makes the comparison map an isomorphism. This proves the counit's asserted coequalizer property.
Let f:KD→KE be an algebra morphism. Its transpose is h0​=εE​Ff:FGD→E. The two composites of h0​ with the free pair have adjunct transposes GεE​Tf and fGεD​, equal because f is an algebra morphism. Consequently h0​ factors uniquely through εD​ as h:D→E. Applying G gives (Gh)GεD​=fGεD​; the last factor is split epic, so Gh=f. Conversely any lift of f must satisfy hεD​=εE​Ff by naturality and hence is unique. Thus K is full and faithful, and the earlier construction makes it essentially surjective. The comparison is an equivalence, completing the theorem's proof.
Now take G:CompHaus→Set. Its left adjoint exists by the permitted topological result; it can be realized as the Stone-Čech compactification of the discrete input set. A bijective continuous map from a compact space to a Hausdorff space is a homeomorphism, so G reflects isomorphisms.
For continuous f,g:X⇉Y with a split underlying fork q,s,t, let R be the relation of having the same q-image. We claim
R={(f(x),f(x′)):g(x)=g(x′)}.
(4)
For the forward inclusion choose x=t(y), x′=t(y′): ft=1 and gt=sq supply the equality. For the reverse inclusion use qf=qg. The subset {(x,x′):g(x)=g(x′)}⊂X2 is closed, because Y is Hausdorff, and compact. Its continuous image R⊂Y2 is compact and hence closed. A closed equivalence relation on a compact Hausdorff space has a compact Hausdorff quotient; its quotient map is a coequalizer here, since it identifies exactly the generated relation. Its underlying set coequalizer is the specified one up to the unique isomorphism. Thus G preserves the needed coequalizers, and
CompHaus⇄Set is a monadic adjunction.​
(5)
The closedness argument is the closed relation generated by a set-split pair of compact Hausdorff maps lemma; the splitting maps themselves need not be continuous.

Theorems matter at least as much as definitions: they show when a categorical description captures actual mathematical structure. Definitions in category theory are exceptionally economical, but economy alone does not explain their applications.
An adjunction compresses a universal mapping property into natural hom-set bijections. The free-group adjunction, for example, records that assigning images to generators extends uniquely to a homomorphism. That is a useful definition, but proving that a free group exists and satisfies this property still requires mathematics. The definition organizes that proof rather than replaces it.
The Yoneda lemma offers a sharper example. A representable functor is easy to define; the theorem identifying natural transformations out of it with elements of another functor gives the definition its computational force. The representation of a set-valued functor as a colimit of representables is then a substantive consequence, and the theorem that filtered colimits commute with finite limits turns it into a criterion for finite-limit preservation. None of these conclusions follows merely from choosing attractive terminology.
Likewise a monad packages operations and their compatibility, while the Eilenberg-Moore category defines the corresponding algebras. The Beck monadicity theorem answers a different question: whether a given category is really recovered by that algebraic description. Applying it to compact Hausdorff spaces needs a topological argument about closed quotient relations. The definitions would not by themselves predict or justify the result.
Finally, abelian categories put kernels in a category, cokernels in a category and exactness into a setting where homological arguments can be reused. Some consequences, such as image factorization, become short because strong structural content has deliberately been included in the definition. Yet left derived functors, long exact sequences and the detection of projective objects supply information about obstructions to exactness which the terminology alone cannot provide.
Definitions and theorems therefore play complementary roles. A good definition states what should be invariant across examples; a theorem proves which constructions preserve it, which examples satisfy it, and what can be deduced from it. Category theory makes that partnership unusually visible, rather than making proofs less important.

The comma category (A↓F) has objects (C,u) with C∈C and u:A→FC a set map. A morphism (C,u)→(D,v) is an arrow h:C→D such that Fh∘u=v. Composition and identities are those of C. For A=1, this is the category of elements E of F: objects are (C,x) with x∈FC and arrows satisfy Fh(x)=y.
Associate to (C,x) the covariant representable functor HC​=C(C,−). An arrow h:(C,x)→(D,y) of E gives, by precomposition, HD​→HC​. Thus this is a diagram H:Eop→[C,Set]. There is a compatible natural map HC​→F sending f:C→B to Ff(x).
Colimits in this functor category are pointwise. At an object B, the induced map from the colimit sends a class represented by (C,x,f:C→B) to Ff(x). It is surjective because z∈FB is represented by (B,z,1B​). It is injective because every representative with value z is identified with that same canonical representative: the arrow f:(C,x)→(B,z) in E becomes an arrow of the indexing diagram sending 1B​ to f. These bijections are natural in B, proving
F≅(C,x)∈Eopcolim​C(C,−).​
(6)
Smallness of C and set-valuedness of F ensure that E is small, so the displayed colimit is defined. This proves the introductory density request before the three equivalent conditions.

Assume F preserves finite limits. Fix any set A and a finite diagram of objects (Cj​,uj​) in (A↓F). Let L be the categorical limit of its underlying diagram in C, with projections pj​. Preservation gives FL≅limj​FCj​. The maps uj​:A→FCj​ form a compatible cone, so determine a unique u:A→FL with Fpj​∘u=uj​.
The object (L,u) is the categorical limit in the comma category. Indeed a cone from (B,v) gives a unique underlying h:B→L, and the identities Fpj​Fhv=Fpj​u for all j force Fhv=u by the categorical limit's uniqueness. For the empty diagram, L is terminal and FL is a one-element set, giving the unique map from A. Thus (A↓F) has all finite limits. This proves (i)⇒(ii)​.

Take A=1. By assumption E=(1↓F) has finite limits, so Eop has finite colimits. A filtered category is nonempty, admits a cocone over every pair of objects, and admits a coequalizing arrow for every parallel pair. The initial object of Eop gives nonemptiness, its binary coproducts give the required two-object cocones, and its coequalizers equalize parallel arrows. Consequently (ii)⇒(iii)​. These statements also handle the otherwise possible empty category of elements.

A balanced category is one in which every morphism that is both monic and epic is an isomorphism. If a faithful functor sends f to an isomorphism, then f is monic: equality fg=fh becomes FfFg=FfFh, cancellation gives Fg=Fh, and faithfulness gives g=h. The same argument on the other side makes f epic. Balance therefore makes f an isomorphism. Thus a faithful functor out of a balanced category reflects isomorphisms.
Under the adjunction, the transpose of Ff:FX→FA is ηA​f:X→GFA. Hence if F is faithful, ηA​f=ηA​g forces Ff=Fg and then f=g, making every ηA​ monic. Conversely, if the unit is pointwise monic and Ff=Fg, naturality gives ηB​f=GFfηA​=GFgηA​=ηB​g, whence f=g. Therefore
F faithful⟺η pointwise monic.​
(8)
We also use the standard adjunction fact with its justification: F is full and faithful exactly when η is invertible. Indeed the composite hom-set map C(X,A)→D(FX,FA)≅C(X,GFA) is postcomposition by ηA​. If F is full and faithful these maps are bijective; taking X=GFA produces a right inverse, and taking X=A gives injectivity and therefore a left inverse. Conversely an invertible unit makes these hom-set maps bijective.
For the final example, view the two-element ordered set 0<1 as a category C, and let D have one object and only its identity. The unique F:C→D is left adjoint to the functor G selecting 1, since every C(x,1) is a singleton. Every arrow in an ordered-set category is monic, so the unit arrows x→1 and the identity counit are monic. But F is not full: C(1,0) is empty whereas D(F1,F0) is a singleton. The example does not contradict the upcoming equivalence because C is not balanced; its arrow 0→1 is both monic and epic but not invertible.

Assume both unit and counit are monic. The unit criterion makes F faithful. The triangle identity is εFA​FηA​=1FA​. Since εFA​ is monic, this split epimorphism is an isomorphism: cancelling it in εFA​(FηA​εFA​)=εFA​ gives FηA​εFA​=1. Thus FηA​ is invertible. As C is balanced, the reflection result in the root solution makes ηA​ invertible. Hence F is full and faithful.
Let q:FA→B be a regular epimorphism. Its adjunct factorization is q=εB​Fh for h:A→GB. A regular epimorphism is extremal: if a coequalizer q factors through a monomorphism m, its other factor also coequalizes the defining pair, descends through q, and gives a right inverse for m; monicity then makes m invertible. Apply this to the monic εB​. More explicitly, if q coequalizes r,s, monicity gives Fhr=Fhs, so there is d:B→FGB with dq=Fh. Then εB​dq=q implies εB​d=1, and monicity gives dεB​=1. Therefore B≅FGB. The image is closed under regular quotients, proving (i)⇒(ii)​.

Since the full endofunctor subcategory contains 1 and is closed under composition, 1,T2,T3 all belong to it. Terminality supplies unique natural transformations η:1→T and μ:T2→T. The monad unit laws compare arrows T→T, both necessarily the unique endomorphism, namely the identity. The associativity law compares arrows T3→T, again necessarily equal. Every monad structure uses these two unique arrows, so T has a unique monad structure​.
For a monad S with underlying functor in the subcategory, the unique α:S→T is a monad morphism. Its unit and multiplication compatibility compare, respectively, arrows 1→T and S2→T and therefore follow from terminality. A T-algebra a:TX→X restricts to the S-algebra b=aαX​. The unit law is immediate. For multiplication, naturality and the monad-morphism law give
bSb=aTaTαX​αSX​=aμXT​TαX​αSX​=aαX​μXS​=bμXS​.
(10)
An algebra morphism for T also commutes with the restricted action by naturality of α. Keeping underlying objects and arrows gives the required functor CT→CS.
Now define TA to be the set of ultrafilters on A, and for f:A→B define
Tf(U)={V⊆B:f−1(V)∈U}.
(11)
Inverse images preserve inclusions, intersections and complements, so this is an ultrafilter; composition of inverse images makes T a functor. There are no ultrafilters on the empty set. Every ultrafilter on a disjoint binary union chooses exactly one summand, and restriction to that summand gives an ultrafilter there; extension back is inverse to restriction. Hence T(A⨿B)≅TA⨿TB canonically, and T preserves finite coproducts, including the empty one.
Let H be any other finite-coproduct-preserving endofunctor. For x∈HA, put
αA​(x)={U⊆A:x∈im(H(U↪A))}.
(12)
The decomposition A=U⨿Uc and coproduct preservation show that exactly one of U,Uc belongs. Refining two subsets into the four disjoint parts U∩V,U∖V,V∖U,(U∪V)c shows that an element lying in both indicated summands lies in their intersection summand. The same refinement for U⊆V proves upward closure. Thus αA​(x) is an ultrafilter. For f:A→B, decomposition by f−1(V) and its complement shows
V∈αB​(Hf(x))⟺f−1(V)∈αA​(x),
(13)
proving naturality.
For uniqueness, suppose β:H→T is natural. Since T1 is a singleton, naturality on the two inclusions 1→2 forces β2​ to send every element in each of the two H1 summands of H2 to the corresponding principal ultrafilter. Apply naturality to the characteristic map χU​:A→2. It gives U∈βA​(x) precisely when HχU​(x) lies in the selected summand, which by the decomposition is precisely when x lies in HU⊆HA. Hence βA​=αA​ for every A. Therefore
T is terminal and TA=Ult(A).​
(14)
This proves ultrafilter functor. For clarity, the resulting unit sends a to its principal ultrafilter; multiplication sends an ultrafilter U on Ult(A) to the ultrafilter whose members are U with {V:U∈V}∈U. Naturality and the monad laws for these formulas also follow from the terminal construction.

An abelian category is an additive category with kernels in a category and cokernels in a category in which the canonical comparison from coimage to image of every morphism is an isomorphism. This is the standard image-coimage form of the definition, equivalently expressed by normality of every monomorphism and epimorphism. Additivity means abelian-group hom-sets with bilinear composition, a zero object and finite biproducts. A sequence is exact at an object when the image of the incoming morphism is the kernel in a category of the outgoing one.
For f:X→Y, construct q:X→coimf=coker(kerf) and j:imf=ker(cokerf)→Y. The universal properties give f=jfˉ​q: first f kills its kernel in a category and factors through q; then the resulting map is killed by cokerf because q is epic, so it factors through j. The comparison fˉ​ is invertible by the abelian axiom. Cokernels in a category are epic and kernels in a category monic, directly by uniqueness in their universal properties, so e=fˉ​q is epic and j monic. Thus f=je is an epimorphism-monomorphism factorization​.
In a short exact sequence 0→X→iY→pZ→0, we have i=kerp and p=cokeri, up to the canonical isomorphisms. If r:Y→X retracts i, the map 1Y​−ir kills i and factors as sp for some s:Z→Y. Composing with p gives psp=p, so epicity gives ps=1Z​. Conversely if ps=1Z​, then 1Y​−sp is killed by p and factors as ir. Composing with i gives iri=i, so monicity gives ri=1X​. Consequently
i is split monic⟺p is split epic.​
(15)
In that case Y≅X⊕Z.
For the derived-functor requests, use additive right exact functors, as customary in abelian homological algebra. An abelian category with enough projectives permits an augmented projective resolution ⋯→P2​→P1​→P0​→X→0, obtained by repeatedly taking an epimorphism from a projective onto the previous kernel in a category. Apply F and define
Ln​F(X)=Hn​(FP∙​),L0​F(X)≅F(X).
(16)
Projective lifts construct maps of resolutions; the comparison and chain-homotopy results make the construction independent of choices and functorial. For a short exact sequence, the horseshoe construction produces a degreewise split short exact sequence of resolutions, hence of their images under additive F. Its homology objects give the long exact sequence
⋯→L2​F(X)→L2​F(Y)→L2​F(Z)→L1​F(X)→L1​F(Y)→L1​F(Z)→F(X)→F(Y)→F(Z)→0.
(17)
This supplies the requested sketch; detailed comparison and functoriality proofs are not needed here.
A projective object X has a resolution concentrated in degree zero, so L1​F(X)=0 for every such F. Conversely choose 0→K→iP→X→0 with P projective, and take F=A(−,K) with target Abop. Precomposition makes this covariant to the opposite category. The usual left exactness of contravariant Hom, which follows directly from the cokernel in a category universal property, says exactly that this F is right exact in that opposite target. If L1​F(X)=0, the long exact sequence and L1​F(P)=0 make F(K)→F(P) monic in Abop. Equivalently,
Hom(P,K)⟶Hom(K,K),r⟼ri,
(18)
is epic, hence surjective, in abelian groups. The identity of K has a preimage, giving a retraction of i. The sequence splits, and X is a direct summand of P, hence projective: a lifting problem for X extends along the projection P→X, is solved for P, and restricts along the summand inclusion. Therefore
X projective⟺L1​F(X)=0 for every additive right exact F.​
(19)
This is projectivity detected by all first left derived functors. The following parts prove the three requested equivalent formulations of a hereditary abelian category with enough projectives.
