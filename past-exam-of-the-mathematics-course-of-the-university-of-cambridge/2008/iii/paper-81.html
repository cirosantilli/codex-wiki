In the Boussinesq approximation, replace density by a constant reference density ρ0​ in inertia and incompressible flow, retaining the small density departure only in the gravitational force. Write ρ=ρˉ​(z)+ρ′ and subtract the resting hydrostatic pressure. The upward buoyancy perturbation acceleration and squared buoyancy frequency are
σ=−ρ0​gρ′​,N2(z)=−ρ0​g​dzdρˉ​​.
(1)
Thus stable density stratification has N2>0. Let p henceforth denote the pressure anomaly divided by ρ0​. The ideal, nonrotating Boussinesq equations are
DtDu​=−∇p+σez​,DtDσ​+N2(z)w=0,∇⋅u=0,DtD​=∂t​+u∂x​+v∂y​+w∂z​.
(2)
The buoyancy equation follows by materially conserving the total buoyancy b0​(z)+σ, where b0′​=N2; it does not assume constant N.
For an x-periodic flow of period P, define aˉ=P−1∫0P​adx. An infinite-domain average with vanishing end contributions works as well. The necessary pressure condition is no mean zonal pressure gradient, px​​=0: periodic pressure satisfies it, whereas a pressure containing a term proportional to x does not. Use incompressible flow to write the zonal momentum equation in conservative form,
ut​+(u2)x​+(uv)y​+(uw)z​=−px​.
(3)
Averaging its zonal derivative terms yields the exact Reynolds stress relation
uˉt​=−∂y​uv−∂z​uw.​
(4)
For two-dimensional small disturbances about rest, the Linearized Boussinesq equations are ut​=−px​, wt​=−pz​+σ, σt​=−N2w, and ux​+wz​=0. Differentiate the two momentum equations to eliminate pressure: (uz​−wx​)t​=−σx​. Differentiating in x and using uxz​=−wzz​ gives ∇2wt​=σxx​. One further time derivative gives
∇2wtt​+N2(z)wxx​=0.​
(5)
The equation remains valid for N=N(z). No vertical derivative of N was taken: ∂x2​(N2w)=N2wxx​.
For constant N and k=0, a plane internal gravity wave has
w=Re{aei(kx+mz−ωt)},ω2(k2+m2)=N2k2,m=±∣k∣N2/ω2−1​.
(6)
Both signs give the prescribed lower-boundary velocity. The radiation condition excludes energy arriving from infinity. At positive frequency, the vertical group velocity is
cgz​=∂m∂ω​=−k2+m2ωm​.
(7)
For a source below the fluid, energy must propagate upward, so the outgoing solution has m<0, even though its vertical phase velocity ω/m is downward.
To obtain this selection causally, let T=μt and Z=μz, and replace the constant amplitude by a(Z,T). The method of multiple scales treats (z,t,Z,T) as independent during differentiation: the carrier derivatives hold Z,T fixed, while aT​ holds Z fixed and aZ​ holds T fixed. Only after differentiating do we restrict to T=μt, Z=μz. Acting on the complex wave, the operators become
∂t​⟼−iω+μ∂T​,∂z​⟼im+μ∂Z​.
(8)
With K2=k2+m2, the wave equation has residual
(ω2K2−N2k2)a+2iμ{ωK2aT​−mω2aZ​}+O(μ2).
(9)
The zeroth-order term is the dispersion relation; the first-order term vanishes precisely when
aT​+baZ​=0,b=−mω/K2=cgz​.​
(10)
If a0​(T) is the switched-on boundary amplitude, with a0​(T)=0 for T<0, the outgoing envelope is a(Z,T)=a0​(T−Z/b) for b>0. Its characteristics carry the boundary signal into initially undisturbed fluid. If b<0, characteristics instead arrive from the upper half-space; zero initial disturbances and no incoming signal cannot establish the proposed boundary-forced wavetrain. This is the internal-wave envelope radiation condition.
The leading incompressible flow polarization gives u′=−(m/k)w′. Hence, to second order in amplitude and leading order in the slow modulation,
u′w′=−2km​∣a(Z,T)∣2,uˉt​=2km​∂z​∣a∣2=2kμm​∂Z​∣a∣2.
(11)
There is no meridional transport here. Envelope transport gives ∂t​∣a∣2=−b∂z​∣a∣2, so an initially vanishing mean develops as
uˉ=−2kbm​∣a∣2=2kωK2​∣a∣2.​
(12)
These are the leading expressions for internal-wave momentum deposition; higher slow-modulation corrections to the stress are beyond the displayed approximation. In particular, a spatially uniform fully established wavetrain has zero stress divergence, but the front has already produced its mean flow.
The mean wave energy density per unit mass follows from σ′=−iN2w′/ω:
E=21​u′2+w′2+σ′2/N2​=2k2K2​∣a∣2,p′w′​=−2k2ωm​∣a∣2=bE.
(13)
Thus uˉ=(k/ω)E. In the original frame the mean kinetic energy is uˉ2/2=O(a4). In a frame translating at c=ω/k, the undisturbed velocity is −c, and the change in mean kinetic energy is −cuˉ+O(a4)=O(a2). Indeed E−cuˉ=0 at this order behind the front. Mean-flow energy is therefore essential at the same order as wave energy in the translating frame. This follows also from the frame transformation of energy, E↦E−cP up to the fixed background constant, and is why conservation of momentum cannot be omitted from the moving-frame conservation of energy budget.

A precise stratification–rotation analogy uses constant N, ideal fluid, and independence of one horizontal coordinate. For the stratified system take ∂y​=0, v=0, and allow prescribed forces (Fx​,0,Fz​). The possibly nonlinear Boussinesq equations are
Du=−px​+Fx​,Dw=−pz​+σ+Fz​,Dσ=−N2w,ux​+wz​=0,D=∂t​+u∂x​+w∂z​.
(14)
For the unstratified system choose right-handed coordinates (X,Y,Z)=(z,y,−x), rotation 2Ω=NeZ​, and ∂Y​=0. Its rotating Euler equations, with the static gravitational and centrifugal contributions absorbed into the background pressure, are
DR​U−NV=−PX​+FX​,DR​V+NU=FY​,DR​W=−PZ​+FZ​,UX​+WZ​=0,
(15)
where DR​=∂t​+U∂X​+W∂Z​. The identification is
U=w,V=σ/N,W=−u,P=p,(FX​,FY​,FZ​)=(Fz​,0,−Fx​).​
(16)
Then DR​=D, PX​=pz​, and PZ​=−px​, proving the component-by-component equivalence. In particular, buoyancy perturbation maps to transverse rotating velocity, and stratified horizontal velocity maps to rotation-axis velocity. The mapping permits arbitrary prescribed forces in the two-dimensional plane, but no transverse force: a transverse force in the rotating problem would create a source in the stratified buoyancy equation. A transverse stratified force would also invalidate v=0. Constant N is essential to this constant-rotation equivalence.
A physical free surface introduces extra structure beyond the interior equations. After subtracting hydrostatic pressure, its zero total pressure condition gives p=gη to first order, together with the kinematic condition ηt​=w for the stratified vertical displacement. The mapping takes this normal velocity into U, whereas an actual rotating free surface normal to its physical vertical Z has normal velocity W and a displacement along Z. The image of the stratified horizontal surface is a vertical surface in the rotating coordinates. Consequently the physical free-surface problems are not analogous, although deliberately mapped rigid boundaries or mathematical boundary data can be.
For the first prescribed force, the steady linear stratified equations are satisfied by
u=(u(z),0,0),p=f(x)eimz+b(z),σ=imf(x)eimz+b′(z).
(17)
Real parts are understood. The horizontal pressure gradient balances the force; the vertical pressure gradient is balanced by buoyancy; and w=0 satisfies buoyancy conservation and incompressible flow. Thus neither u(z) nor b(z) is fixed by the steady equations alone.
Initial rest and switching on in the unbounded fluid remove this ambiguity by causal pressure adjustment by internal waves. Work with the eimz coefficient and let F(q) be the horizontal Fourier transform of f′(x). For an abrupt switch at time zero, elimination in the linear equations gives
Ω(q)=q2+m2​Nq​,u(q,t)=(q2+m2)Ω(q)m2F(q)​sin(Ω(q)t),
(18)
p​(q,t)=−qiF(q)​[1−q2+m2m2​cos(Ω(q)t)].
(19)
At q=0 the bracket cancels the apparent singularity at every finite time. The persistent pressure term is the symmetric antiderivative of the force: the inverse transform of −iF/q, interpreted as a Cauchy principal value, is f(x)−ε/2. The oscillatory part propagates away. To see why its zero-frequency part does not leave an arbitrary offset, note that Ω(q)∼cq with c=N/m; its long-wave contribution consists of oppositely moving fronts. At fixed x, the two far-field values of f−ε/2 are −ε/2 and +ε/2, whose average is zero. Nonzero horizontal wavenumbers give dispersive transients that decay locally. Smooth finite-duration switching changes the transient, not this selected steady limit. Therefore
p∞​=[f(x)−ε/2]eimz,b(z)=−2ε​eimz.​
(20)
A spatially constant pressure gauge is immaterial. The long-wave group velocity c=N/m gives the adjustment scale: the elapsed time since switching must satisfy Nt≫1 and (N/m)t≫∣x∣+x1​, and exceed the switching duration. These are asymptotic conditions, not an assertion of exact equilibration after a finite time; the full dispersion relation allows tails. The same long-wave limit gives u∞​=εmeimz/(2N) if desired. Equal-amplitude outward fronts leave opposite pressure offsets and the same velocity on their two sides.
For the force depending instead on y, any steady linear horizontal momentum balance requires px​=f′(x)eiℓy and py​=0. Their mixed derivatives disagree wherever f′=0, since ∂y​px​=iℓf′(x)eiℓy but ∂x​py​=0. Hence no steady linear velocity field can satisfy the equations, irrespective of whether it is x-independent. This statement concerns the linearized problem; nonlinear inertial balances were excluded by the assumptions.
For an unsteady solution put u=(−tϕy​,tϕx​,0) and take σ=0. Its horizontal divergence is zero. The pressure derivatives needed in momentum balance are
px​=f′(x)eiℓy+ϕy​,py​=−ϕx​,pz​=0.
(21)
Their compatibility is exactly
ϕxx​+ϕyy​=−iℓf′(x)eiℓy.​
(22)
Thus the Poisson equation supplies an actual pressure and the proposed velocity solves both momentum equations; the vertical momentum and buoyancy equations hold because w=σ=0. This flow grows linearly in time, so it remains a valid small-disturbance prediction only while its velocities are small.
As x1​→0 with total force ε fixed, f′→εδ(x). Set ϕ=Φ(x)eiℓy. Away from the source Φ′′−ℓ2Φ=0, and decay at both ends requires Φ=Ae−ℓ∣x∣. Integrating across the source gives the jump Φ′(0+)−Φ′(0−)=−iℓε. Since this jump is −2ℓA, A=iε/2, so
ϕ=2iε​eiℓye−ℓ∣x∣.​
(23)
This verifies the distributional source as well as the decay condition.
The corresponding rotating problem is not expected to reproduce this evanescent accelerating flow. Its forcing varies in Y, precisely the coordinate whose independence was required in the stratification–rotation analogy. Moreover, x maps to the rotation-axis coordinate −Z. At small Rossby number, the Taylor–Proudman theorem gives ∂Z​uR​=0 in the unforced exterior regions. Such nonzero columns cannot decay with ∣Z∣, hence cannot reproduce an e−ℓ∣x∣ tail. There is also a direct unsteady obstruction to copying the proposed flow: in a homogeneous fluid rotating about the mapped x axis, a mode with nonzero axial wavenumber has inertial wave frequency of magnitude N∣q∣/q2+ℓ2​, rather than the zero-frequency horizontal vortical response of the stratified problem. The steady or slowly varying exterior rotating response must be columnar or accompanied by outgoing inertial waves; the decaying stratified solution is outside the partial analogy.

Let ζ denote free-surface height and h=ζ−b the water depth. The inviscid rotating shallow water equations with constant Coriolis parameter f are
DH​u−fv=−gζx​,DH​v+fu=−gζy​,ht​+(hu)x​+(hv)y​=0,DH​=∂t​+u∂x​+v∂y​.
(24)
It is ζ, not h, that supplies the horizontal pressure gradient. For a fixed bottom, mass conservation equivalently gives DH​h=−h(ux​+vy​).
Write ξ=vx​−uy​ for relative vorticity. Differentiate the second momentum equation in x and the first in y, then subtract. The pressure terms cancel. Expanding the differentiated advection terms gives
DH​ξ+(ξ+f)(ux​+vy​)=0.
(25)
Consequently the absolute vorticity qa​=ξ+f satisfies DH​qa​=−qa​∇H​⋅uH​. Combining this with mass conservation yields exact shallow-water potential vorticity conservation:
DH​(qa​/h)=h2hDH​qa​−qa​DH​h​=0.​
(26)
A sloping bottom changes the depth and hence the stretching, but contributes no curl to the gravitational pressure force.
For the quasi-geostrophic approximation, choose constant reference depth H0​, speed U, and horizontal length L. Require Rossby number Ro=U/(∣f∣L)≪1, slow evolution on the advective time L/U, smooth horizontal scales, and L/LD​=O(1) or smaller, where LD​=gH0​​/∣f∣ is the Rossby deformation radius. Write ζ=ζ0​+η, b=b0​+δb, and h=H0​+η−δb. The leading momentum balance is geostrophic balance:
ug​=−ψy​,vg​=ψx​,ψ=gη/f.
(27)
It gives η/H0​=O(Ro(L/LD​)2), while we require δb/H0​=O(Ro) and slopes on the chosen horizontal scale. Relative vorticity divided by f is also O(Ro). Thus
hqa​​=H0​f​+H0​1​(∇H2​ψ−H0​f​η+H0​f​δb)+O(Ro2H0​∣f∣​).
(28)
The derivative of the constant first term vanishes. In advecting the first-order anomaly, the ageostrophic flow contributes only at the next order. Therefore the shallow-water quasi-geostrophic potential vorticity equation is
q=∇H2​ψ−LD−2​ψ+H0​f​δb,qt​+J(ψ,q)=0,J(ψ,q)=ψx​qy​−ψy​qx​.​
(29)
This retains the balanced vortical branch, including Rossby waves driven here by the topographic potential-vorticity gradient. It filters the freely oscillating inertia–gravity branches of the full shallow water equations, and does not retain independent boundary Kelvin wave dynamics. Small Rossby number alone is insufficient for the reduction: fast waves must be absent at leading order, as in well-prepared balanced initial data.
In the channel take H0​=h00​ and measure the bottom anomaly from mid-channel: δb=ε(y−L/2). Define β=εf/H0​. The resting potential vorticity is β(y−L/2), and the linear Rossby wave equation is
∂t​(∇H2​−LD−2​)ψ+βψx​=0.
(30)
For nonzero zonal wavenumber k, wall conditions ψ=0 select
ψ=Re{Asin(nπy/L)ei(kx−ωt)},ω=−k2+(nπ/L)2+LD−2​βk​,n=1,2,….​
(31)
The k=0 modes are stationary. Write K2=k2+(nπ/L)2+LD−2​ and take real A for the following sketch. The total potential vorticity is q=β(y−L/2)−K2ψ. For a contour whose undisturbed position is y0​, its small meridional displacement is
δy=βK2​ψ(x,y0​,t)+O(A2).
(32)
Thus potential-vorticity conservation says a northward displacement in a positive background gradient carries a negative anomaly, q′=−βδy. Potential-vorticity inversion turns this into positive ψ and anticyclonic motion around the bulge. At a positive cosine crest, v=ψx​ is northward on its western flank and southward on its eastern flank. Advection raises the contour on the west and lowers it on the east, so the crest moves west. Algebraically, δyt​=v and δy=(K2/β)ψ give ω/k=−β/K2, the same phase velocity as above. For β<0 all propagation directions reverse.
For a square basin use a nonzero-frequency normal mode ψ=Re{Ψ(x,y)e−iωt}, with Ψ=0 on all walls. Its equation is
(∇H2​−LD−2​)Ψ+ωiβ​Ψx​=0.
(33)
Set α=β/(2ω) and Ψ=eiαxΨ. Substitution cancels the first derivative and gives
∇H2​Ψ+(α2−LD−2​)Ψ=0,Ψ=0 on the walls.
(34)
The square's Dirichlet Laplacian eigenfunctions are Ψ=Asin(mπx/L)sin(nπy/L), with m,n positive integers. Consequently the square-basin topographic Rossby mode has
ω=±2π2(m2+n2)/L2+LD−2​​β​,Ψ=Ae−iβx/(2ω)sin(mπx/L)sin(nπy/L).​
(35)
For β>0, the positive-frequency branch therefore tends to βL/[2πm2+n2​] as L/LD​→0, as required. The negative-frequency solution is its complex-conjugate representation for a real disturbance; the physical frequency magnitude is ∣β∣/[2π2(m2+n2)/L2+LD−2​​].

The geostrophic material derivative is
DtDg​​=∂t​+J(ψ,⋅)=∂t​−ψy​∂x​+ψx​∂y​.
(36)
It advects horizontally with the geostrophic flow. The exact material derivative additionally includes horizontal ageostrophic flow and vertical advection w∂z​. The leading balances are geostrophic balance and the hydrostatic approximation:
−f0​v=−px​,f0​u=−py​,0=−pz​+σ.
(37)
The conventional quasi-geostrophic streamfunction is p=f0​ψ, after fixing the horizontally uniform reference pressure consistently with the basic buoyancy profile. Therefore
σ=f0​ψz​.​
(38)
Here and below pressure is divided by reference density, and σ is the anomaly about the prescribed background buoyancy.
For the zonal average assume periodicity or vanishing boundary terms, so that averages of x derivatives vanish and averaging commutes with y,z derivatives. Put S(z)=f02​/N2(z). Since ψx′​​=0, the disturbance quasi-geostrophic potential vorticity is Q′=ψxx′​+ψyy′​+∂z​(Sψz′​). The zonal part of ψx′​Q′​ vanishes as 21​∂x​(ψx′​)2​. For the other parts, integration by parts gives
ψx′​ψyy′​​=∂y​ψx′​ψy′​​−21​∂x​(ψy′​)2​=∂y​ψx′​ψy′​​,
(39)
ψx′​∂z​(Sψz′​)​=∂z​(Sψx′​ψz′​​)−21​S∂x​(ψz′​)2​=∂z​(Sψx′​ψz′​​).
(40)
As u′=−ψy′​, v′=ψx′​, and σ′=f0​ψz′​, the Eliassen–Palm flux is
F=ψx′​ψy′​​=−u′v′,G=Sψx′​ψz′​​=N2f0​​v′σ′.
(41)
We have therefore proved the exact Taylor identity for quasi-geostrophic flux,
Fy​+Gz​=v′Q′​,​
(42)
without imposing small amplitude. This identity is algebraic within the quasi-geostrophic approximation; it does not assert validity of that approximation for arbitrary physical flows.
Now linearize about a steady zonal basic flow uˉ=U(y,z), with Qˉ​y​=0 and no mean meridional velocity. The linear quasi-geostrophic potential-vorticity equation is Qt′​+UQx′​+v′Qˉ​y​=0. Multiply by Q′/Qˉ​y​ and average. The advection term is a zonal derivative and vanishes, giving
A=2Qˉ​y​Q′2​​,At​=−v′Q′​,At​+Fy​+Gz​=0.​
(43)
This is quasi-geostrophic wave-activity conservation law. The basic-state denominator is time-independent at the retained order; its change caused by the waves is higher order. The wave activity is signed if Qˉ​y​<0, and the formula cannot be used where that gradient vanishes. The original PDF includes the zonal average over the squared disturbance in this definition.
For the resonant solution it is helpful to avoid squaring an imaginary coefficient as though it were real. Write
U=β/κ2,S=f02​/N2,B=iB0​,B0​=β2kf0​U2​,C=f0​mN2​,m=π/H,
(44)
where B0​ is real. Assume f0​=0, β=0, and k=0. Define c(z)=cos(mz) and r(z)=(z−H)sin(mz). The complex coefficient of the physical streamfunction disturbance is
ψ′=Re{εeikxsin(ℓy)h(z,t)},h=iB0​tc+Cr.
(45)
The original PDF contains Btcos(mz): the converted TeX's missing t would incorrectly remove the resonance and fails the differential equations.
For the vertical potential-vorticity inversion operator, c′′=−m2c and r′′=−m2r+2mc. Consequently
Q′=Re{εeikxsin(ℓy)q(z,t)},q=−κ2h+2SmCc=−κ2h+2f0​c.
(46)
Substitution in the linear potential-vorticity equation gives
qt​+ikUq+ikβh=(−iκ2B0​+2ikUf0​)c=0,
(47)
since κ2B0​=2kf0​U. Thus this is a genuine resonant topographic quasi-geostrophic wave, with linear amplitude growth rather than an assumed stationary wave.
At the flat upper boundary hz​(H,t)=0, so w′(H)=0. At the lower boundary, linearization of the impermeability condition gives w′(0)=U∂x​hb​, where hb​=Re{εHeikxsin(ℓy)} is the bottom corrugation. Here hz​(0,t)=−CHm is time-independent, so the specified vertical velocity gives
w′(0)=−N2f0​​Re{εeikxsin(ℓy)(∂t​+ikU)(−CHm)}=Re{ikUεHeikxsin(ℓy)},
(48)
using f0​Cm/N2=1. Both boundary conditions are verified. The linear solution is valid while its growing displacement and velocity remain small; it is not a large-time nonlinear solution.
To evaluate all fluxes, use Re(aeikx)Re(beikx)​=21​Re(ab∗). The coefficients of u′,v′,σ′ are respectively −εℓcos(ℓy)h, iεksin(ℓy)h, and εf0​sin(ℓy)hz​. Hence
u′v′=0,F=0.​
(49)
The products of the B0​t terms alone and the C terms alone are imaginary in the vertical flux and contribute nothing. Only the cross terms remain. Since
cr′−rc′=sin(mz)cos(mz)+m(z−H)=:R(z),
(50)
we obtain
v′σ′=−2ε2f0​kB0​Ct​sin2(ℓy)R(z),G=−2ε2SkB0​Ct​sin2(ℓy)R(z).​
(51)
Differentiating R gives R′=m(1+cos(2mz))=2mcos2(mz). As SmC=f0​,
Fy​+Gz​=−ε2kf0​B0​tsin2(ℓy)cos2(mz).​
(52)
For an independent evaluation of the right side of the Taylor identity for quasi-geostrophic flux, write q=(2f0​c−κ2Cr)−iκ2B0​tc. Multiplication of ikh by q∗ gives real part −2kf0​B0​tc2, because the remaining real cross terms cancel. Therefore
v′Q′​=−ε2kf0​B0​tsin2(ℓy)cos2(mz),​
(53)
which verifies the flux identity pointwise in y,z.
The basic potential-vorticity gradient is Qˉ​y​=β, so the wave activity and its derivative are
A=4βε2sin2(ℓy)​[(2f0​c−κ2Cr)2+κ4B02​t2c2],
(54)
At​=2βε2κ4B02​t​sin2(ℓy)c2=ε2kf0​B0​tsin2(ℓy)c2.​
(55)
Here κ4B02​/(2β)=kf0​B0​ follows from U=β/κ2. Thus At​+Fy​+Gz​=0. The growth term involves B02​=∣B∣2=−B2; the distinction matters because the printed complex coefficient B is imaginary. These formulas evaluate both flux components, their divergence, the buoyancy and PV covariances, and wave activity. In particular G(H)=0 and G(0)=ε2kf0​B0​Htsin2(ℓy)/2, so for β>0 the growing activity is supplied by the positive flux from the lower boundary.
Finally put W=π/ℓ and write the height-independent mean velocity change as δU(y). Since δψˉ​y​=−δU and δψˉ​ can be chosen independent of height, the mean potential vorticity becomes
Qˉ​final​=f0​+βy−δUy​.
(56)
To be uniform in y this requires δUy​=βy−K for some constant K. Integrating and applying both endpoint conditions gives δU(0)=0, δU(W)=0, and hence K=βW/2. Therefore
δU(y)=2β​y(y−W),Qˉ​final​=f0​+2βW​.​
(57)
The constant is the initial channel-mean potential vorticity, as also follows from ∫0W​δQdy=−[δU]0W​=0. This is the momentum loss from uniform potential-vorticity mixing. Averaging the velocity change across the channel gives the momentum change per unit mass,
W1​∫0W​δUdy=2Wβ​(3W3​−2W3​)=−12β​(ℓπ​)2.​
(58)
For positive β the mean current is reduced everywhere in the interior. The imposed bottom can exchange momentum with the fluid, so this change is consistent with the complete conservation of momentum budget.
