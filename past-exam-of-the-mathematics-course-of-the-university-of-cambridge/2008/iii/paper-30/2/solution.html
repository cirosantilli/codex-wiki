For a probability measure-preserving system, ergodicity means that every measurable set A satisfying T−1A=A modulo null sets has measure zero or one. Equivalently, the only invariant functions are almost everywhere constant. The pointwise ergodic theorem states that, for f∈L1(μ),

For the Gauss continued-fraction map, the Gauss measure is the probability measure
dν(x)=(log2)(1+x)dx​,0≤x≤1.​

Its density integrates to one and is bounded above and below by positive constants, so it has the same null sets as Lebesgue measure. Apart from countably many endpoints, the inverse branches are ha​(y)=1/(a+y), a=1,2,…. For a bounded measurable test function F, substitution on each branch gives

Thus T is a measure-preserving transformation. Its definition at zero and the rational branch endpoints does not affect these identities.
For ergodicity, we give the full shrinking-cylinder argument. A digit cylinder with first n partial quotients a1​,…,an​ is an interval I=h((0,1)), where h=ha1​​∘⋯∘han​​ and Tn∘h is the identity. The continued fraction convergents give

Here qn​≥qn−1​>0 after the initial step, and the recurrence qn​=an​qn−1​+qn−2​ makes qn​ tend to infinity at least at the Fibonacci rate. Hence the nested cylinders of every irrational point shrink to that point. The bounded distortion of continued-fraction cylinders is uniform: the ratio of the largest to the smallest value of ∣h′∣ is at most four. Since 1/(1+h(y)) ranges between 1/2 and 1, the density

Now suppose T−1A=A modulo null sets and ν(A)>0. Choose an irrational density point x∈A, and let In​ be its successive digit cylinders. The Lebesgue density theorem applies to these intervals even though they need not be centered: an interval of length ℓ containing x lies in the centered interval of radius ℓ, so its relative complement tends to zero. Comparability of the two densities then gives
ν(In​)ν(In​∖A)​⟶0.

ν(In​)ν(In​∖A)​=ν(In​)ν(h(Ac))​≥8log2​ν(Ac).

Letting n→∞ forces ν(Ac)=0. Therefore the Gauss continued-fraction map is ergodic for the Gauss measure.
For an irrational x, the successive continued fraction digits satisfy aj​(x)=⌊1/Tj−1x⌋. The event that a digit equals two is the interval (1/3,1/2], up to null endpoints. Apply the pointwise ergodic theorem to its indicator:

This holds for almost every x with respect to both the Gauss measure and Lebesgue measure, since they have the same null sets.
