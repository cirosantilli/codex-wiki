A nonempty compact dynamical system (X,T) is a minimal dynamical system if it has no proper nonempty closed forward-invariant subset. Equivalently, every forward orbit is dense: the orbit closure of any point is a nonempty closed forward-invariant set, and conversely a closed invariant set contains the orbit closure of each of its points.
Consider the collection of nonempty closed forward-invariant subsets of X, ordered by reverse inclusion. Every chain has an upper bound, its intersection: the chain has the finite-intersection property, compactness makes the intersection nonempty, and continuity is not even needed for its forward invariance. The Zorn lemma supplies a minimal member Y, so (Y,T∣Y​) is a minimal subsystem. Also T(Y) is compact, nonempty and forward invariant, so minimality gives T(Y)=Y. Here the printed word “nontrivial” must mean nonempty; a singleton with its identity map shows that a subsystem with more than one point cannot always be required.
If y∈Y, every tail of its forward orbit is dense in Y, since it is the orbit of TNy∈Y. Therefore every neighborhood of y contains Tny for arbitrarily large n. Choosing these times successively for balls of radii 1/j gives nj​→∞ with Tnj​y→y. This proves the Birkhoff recurrence theorem: every continuous map of a nonempty compact metric space has a recurrent point. In fact every point of the chosen minimal subsystem is recurrent.
Write the continuous circle extension as the skew product
X=X×R/Z,T(x,y)=(Tx,y+ρ(x)).
(1)
A sufficient condition, and in this setting also a necessary one, is the circle skew-product minimality criterion: for every integer m=0 there is no continuous function f:X→S1 such that
f(Tx)=e2πimρ(x)f(x)for every x.​
(2)
The cocycle ρ is assumed continuous; a merely measurable cocycle need not define this topological extension.
To prove sufficiency, choose a minimal subsystem Z of the extension. Its projection is a nonempty closed forward-invariant subset of X, hence all of X. For a∈R/Z, vertical translation sends Z to another minimal subsystem, since it commutes with T. Two such translates either are disjoint or coincide: a nonempty intersection is closed and forward invariant, so minimality forces equality. Define their common stabilizer
H={a:Z+a=Z}.
(3)
This is a closed subgroup of the circle group. Its closedness follows by taking limits of translations of points in the compact set Z, using both a translation and its inverse. Each fiber Zx​ is nonempty. If y,z∈Zx​, the sets Z and Z+(z−y) intersect at (x,z), so z−y∈H. Conversely translating any fiber point by any member of H stays in the fiber. Thus every fiber is exactly one coset of H.
If Z is proper, H is proper and therefore finite, say H={0,1/m,…,(m−1)/m} for some positive integer m. The character y↦e2πimy is constant on each fiber coset, giving a well-defined function f(x) from Zx​. This function is continuous: its graph is the continuous image of Z in X×S1, and projection of that compact graph onto the Hausdorff space X is a continuous bijection and hence a homeomorphism. The relation T(Z)=Z gives f(Tx)=e2πimρ(x)f(x), contrary to the criterion. Hence Z=X and the extension is minimal.
Conversely, a solution of the displayed equation makes F(x,y)=e2πimy/f(x) a nonconstant continuous invariant function on the extension. Its nonempty level sets are proper closed invariant subsets, contradicting minimality. This also proves necessity of the circle skew-product minimality criterion.
Apply it with α=2​, X=R/Z, Tx=x+α, and ρ(x)=2x+α. The base is a minimal dynamical system: the closure of the cyclic subgroup generated by an irrational angle is the whole circle group, and its positive forward orbit has the same closure. To see the subgroup assertion, a proper closed subgroup of the circle is finite; if it contained an irrational angle it would be infinite. The elementary classification follows because a closed subgroup with arbitrarily small nonzero elements is dense, while otherwise its least positive representative generates a finite subgroup.
There can be no forbidden f for any m=0. Indeed, the winding number of a continuous closed path of x↦f(x+α) equals that of x↦f(x), by translation and homotopy invariance of winding number. Consequently their quotient has winding number zero. But the proposed quotient e2πim(2x+α) has winding number 2m=0. Thus the skew product is minimal. Starting at (0,0), induction gives
Tn(0,0)=(nα,n2α)(mod1),
(4)
because n2α+2nα+α=(n+1)2α. Its orbit is dense in the two-dimensional torus, so its second coordinate is dense in the circle. Removing the initial point does not change density, since every orbit tail in a minimal dynamical system is dense. Therefore
{n22​(mod1):n≥1} is dense in R/Z.​
(5)
