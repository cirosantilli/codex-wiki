Assume the invariant set C is nonempty, as required for any fixed-point assertion. For x∈C, put r(x)=supc∈C​∥x−c∥ and r∗​=infx∈C​r(x). Boundedness makes these finite. The parallelogram identity gives, for x,y∈C,

The midpoint lies in C, so a minimizing sequence xn​ satisfies
∥xn​−xm​∥2≤2r(xn​)2+2r(xm​)2−4r∗2​⟶0.

Since C is closed in a Hilbert space, the sequence converges to x∗​∈C. The function r is 1-Lipschitz, hence r(x∗​)=r∗​. The same midpoint inequality proves uniqueness of this minimizing center.
An affine isometry from the group maps C onto itself, since its inverse also preserves C. Therefore it preserves r and sends its unique minimizer to another minimizer. Uniqueness gives
gx∗​=x∗​for every group element g.​
(38)
This proof applies to a closed bounded convex subset, the substantive meaning of the printed “convex subspace”; if it is literally a bounded vector subspace, it is just the zero subspace.
