For a finite-rank operator T, define ∣T∣=(T∗T)1/2 and its trace norm by
∥T∥1​=Tr∣T∣=j=1∑r​sj​,​
(3)
where sj​>0 are its nonzero singular values, counted with multiplicity. The finite-rank spectral theorem gives orthonormal systems (uj​),(vj​) with T=∑j​sj​Ruj​,vj​​, where Rx,y​z=⟨z,y⟩x.
For every bounded operator B,
Tr(BT)=∑j​sj​⟨Buj​,vj​⟩,∣Tr(BT)∣≤∥B∥∑j​sj​.
(4)
Choose the contraction B taking uj​ to vj​ and vanishing on their orthogonal complement. It attains equality, so
∥T∥1​=∥B∥≤1sup​∣Tr(BT)∣.​
(5)
This formula proves the triangle inequality and absolute homogeneity. Also ∥T∥≤∥T∥1​, so vanishing of the trace norm forces T=0. Thus it is a norm on the vector space of finite-rank operators.
The map B↦FB​, where FB​(T)=Tr(BT), is a bounded linear map into the dual of this normed space. Its norm is at most ∥B∥. Rank-one tests give the reverse inequality: ∥Rx,y​∥1​=∥x∥∥y∥ and FB​(Rx,y​)=⟨Bx,y⟩, so taking unit vectors x,y gives ∥FB​∥=∥B∥.
Conversely, let F be a bounded linear functional for the trace norm. The sesquilinear form β(x,y)=F(Rx,y​) satisfies
∣β(x,y)∣≤∥F∥∥x∥∥y∥.
(6)
The Riesz representation theorem gives a unique bounded operator B with β(x,y)=⟨Bx,y⟩, and ∥B∥≤∥F∥. Since every finite-rank operator is a finite sum of rank-one operators, linearity implies F(T)=Tr(BT) for every T. Hence
(F(H),∥⋅∥1​)∗≅B(H) isometrically.​
(7)
The same identification extends to the completion, the trace-class operators. This is trace-class duality; completeness of the original finite-rank space is not required to identify its bounded dual.
