Interpret the involution as the adjoint for the Hilbert-space structure on V. The hypotheses make Ω a cyclic vector for an operator algebra for both A and A′. They also make it a separating vector for each: if aΩ=0, then a vanishes on A′Ω=V, and similarly for A′. Thus the Tomita operator
S(aΩ)=a∗Ω
(53)
is well defined, antilinear, invertible and satisfies S2=I. Define the modular operator and modular conjugation by
Δ=S∗S,J=SΔ−1/2,S=JΔ1/2.​
(54)
We calculate them explicitly to prove the commutant assertion.
The finite-dimensional structure of a unital star-algebra gives an orthogonal decomposition
V=⨁j​(Cnj​⊗Cmj​),A=⨁j​(Mnj​​(C)⊗Imj​​),A′=⨁j​(Inj​​⊗Mmj​​(C)).
(55)
This is the usual matrix-block structure: minimal central projections separate the simple summands, and each simple matrix algebra acts as its defining representation with a multiplicity space. Identify each vector block with an nj​-by-mj​ matrix Dj​ with its Hilbert-Schmidt inner product. The two algebras act by left and right multiplication. If Dj​ has rank rj​, the left orbit has dimension nj​rj​ and the right orbit dimension mj​rj​. Cyclicity on both sides therefore forces rj​=mj​=nj​. Every Dj​ is square and invertible.
Right multiplication by a suitable unitary is a unitary change of this identification commuting with the left algebra. The polar decomposition of each Dj​ therefore lets us assume Dj​>0. In one such block, for an arbitrary matrix X=aD, the Tomita operator is
S(X)=D−1X∗D.
(56)
Its polar factors are
J(X)=X∗,Δ1/2(X)=DXD−1,Δ(X)=D2XD−2.​
(57)
Indeed, J is an antiunitary involution and JΔ1/2(X)=D−1X∗D. The fact that Δ1/2 is a positive operator is transparent in a basis diagonalizing D: it multiplies the matrix unit Ekl​ by the positive ratio dk​/dl​. These are therefore the unique polar factors of S.
Finally, if La​ denotes left multiplication,
(JLa​J)(X)=(aX∗)∗=Xa∗.
(58)
As a ranges over the full matrix block, these are exactly its right multiplications. Applying this on every block and transporting back by the unitary identifications proves
JAJ=A′.​
(59)
This also establishes the required modular definitions intrinsically, independent of the chosen matrix coordinates.
