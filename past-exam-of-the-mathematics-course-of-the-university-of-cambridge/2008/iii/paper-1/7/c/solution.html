The dense commutant orbit makes Ω separating for M, so S0​(aΩ)=a∗Ω is well defined on the dense subspace MΩ. Put τ(a)=⟨aΩ,Ω⟩. The assumed identity says that this positive vector functional is a tracial positive functional. Hence
∥S0​(aΩ)∥2=τ(aa∗)=τ(a∗a)=∥aΩ∥2.
(63)
It extends to an antiunitary involution J on H, with JΩ=Ω. This is the modular conjugation, and the modular operator in this tracial case is Δ=I.
On the dense subspace MΩ,
(JaJ)(bΩ)=J(ab∗Ω)=ba∗Ω.
(64)
Thus it acts by right multiplication and commutes with all left multiplications; JMJ⊆M′. We prove the reverse inclusion rather than assuming the general Tomita commutation theorem.
Let T∈M′, C=∥T∥, and η=TΩ. The set
B={aΩ:a∈M, ∥a∥≤C}
(65)
is weakly compact and convex. Indeed, trace-class duality and the Banach-Alaoglu theorem make the bounded-operator ball compact in a topology stronger than the weak operator topology. The algebra M is weak-operator closed, and a↦aΩ is weakly continuous, so its image is weakly compact.
For b∈M use the polar decomposition of a bounded operator b=uh, h=∣b∣. Both h and u belong to M: continuous functional calculus gives h, and b(h+εI)−1 tends strongly to u. The polar decomposition is proved independently in Question 8. Since T commutes with M,
⟨TΩ,bΩ⟩=⟨Th1/2u∗Ω,h1/2Ω⟩.
(66)
The trace identity gives
∥h1/2u∗Ω∥2=τ(uhu∗)=τ(hu∗u)=τ(h),∥h1/2Ω∥2=τ(h).
(67)
Therefore ∣⟨η,bΩ⟩∣≤Cτ(h). Meanwhile
supa∈M, ∥a∥≤C​Re⟨aΩ,bΩ⟩=Cτ(h),
(68)
with the supremum attained by a=Cu. For the upper bound, traciality gives τ(b∗a)=τ(h1/2u∗ah1/2); evaluating this on Ω bounds its absolute value by ∥a∥τ(h).
These support inequalities imply η∈B. Otherwise the Hahn-Banach separation theorem would separate η from this closed convex set by a real inner-product functional. Since MΩ is dense, its separating vector could be approximated by some bΩ, preserving strict separation, contrary to the displayed inequality. Thus TΩ=aΩ for an a∈M of norm at most C.
The bounded operator Ra​=Ja∗J commutes with M and sends Ω to aΩ. It agrees with T on the cyclic vector and therefore on all of MΩ, hence on H. This proves T∈JMJ, and so
JMJ=M′.​

For the regular representation, take Ω=δe​. It is cyclic for the left action and for the commuting right action. On finite sums of left translations, its vector functional is the identity coefficient, so τ(ab)=τ(ba). This extends to the whole group von Neumann algebra by the bounded strong-star approximations of Question 6, taking limits of the vector functional and products. The conjugation is explicitly
(Jξ)(h)=ξ(h−1)​,Jλ(g)J=ρ(g).
