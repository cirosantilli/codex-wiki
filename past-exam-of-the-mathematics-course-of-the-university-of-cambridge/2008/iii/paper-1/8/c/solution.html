A closed subspace invariant under a star-algebra is reducing, since it is invariant under every adjoint as well. Thus the orthogonal projections P1​,P2​ onto H1​,H2​ commute with M. Consider the bounded intertwiner
T=(I−P2​)∣H1​​:H1​⟶H2⊥​.
(80)
Its kernel is H1​∩H2​, so its initial polar space is
K1​=H1​∩(H1​∩H2​)⊥.
(81)
Its closed range is
K2​=H1​+H2​​∩H2⊥​.
(82)
For the inclusion from left to right, (I−P2​)h1​=h1​−P2​h1​ lies in H1​+H2​ and is orthogonal to H2​. Conversely, approximate any z in the displayed K2​ by h1,n​+h2,n​ and apply I−P2​. The images converge to z and equal Th1,n​, proving the reverse inclusion after closure. The overline on the sum is present in the original PDF and is essential for potentially nonclosed sums.
Take the polar decomposition T=V∣T∣ between these Hilbert spaces. Because T intertwines the two actions of M, its adjoint does also, so T∗T commutes with the action on H1​. Its positive square root therefore commutes as well. The identity V∣T∣ξ=Tξ shows on its dense initial range that V intertwines, and continuity extends this to K1​. It is an isometry onto K2​. Consequently
H1​∩(H1​∩H2​)⊥ ≅M​ H1​+H2​​∩H2⊥​.​
(83)
The unitary here is a unitary between the indicated Hilbert space modules, not an assertion that the unclosed algebraic sum is a Hilbert space.
