The Stone theorem for the circle group says that a strongly continuous unitary representation U of T={z:∣z∣=1} has an orthogonal decomposition
H=n∈Z⨁​Hn​,Uz​∣Hn​​=znI.​
(20)
Equivalently, Uz​=∑n​znPn​ strongly, with mutually orthogonal projections Pn​ summing strongly to the identity. Define these projections using normalized Haar measure:
Pn​ξ=∫T​z−nUz​ξdz.
(21)
Haar invariance gives Uw​Pn​=wnPn​. Changing z to z−1 in the adjoint integral proves Pn∗​=Pn​. Applying the defining integral to Pm​ξ gives Pn​Pm​=δnm​Pm​ by orthogonality of the characters zk. Thus Pn​ are orthogonal projections, and their ranges are exactly the indicated character subspaces.
It remains to prove completeness of these subspaces. The Fejér kernel has the finite expansion
KN​(eit)=∑∣n∣<N​(1−N∣n∣​)e−int=N1​​∑j=0N−1​eijt​2.
(22)
It is nonnegative, has normalized integral one, and its integral outside any fixed neighborhood of the identity tends to zero, since the denominator in the usual geometric-sum formula is bounded away from zero there. Strong continuity therefore gives
∫KN​(z)Uz​ξdz⟶ξ:
(23)
split the integral into that neighborhood, where ∥Uz​ξ−ξ∥ is small, and its complement, where it is bounded by 2∥ξ∥. The integral on the left is ∑∣n∣<N​(1−∣n∣/N)Pn​ξ. Hence the closed span of all Hn​ is H. Orthogonality then gives the unweighted strong sum and the stated representation formula.
Conversely, any orthogonal decomposition indexed by the integers gives this representation; continuity follows first on finite sums and then on all vectors by the uniform unitary norm bound. With z=eit its generator is
Aξ=∑n​nPn​ξ,D(A)={ξ:∑n​n2∥Pn​ξ∥2<∞}.
(24)
This diagonal operator is self-adjoint and Ueit​=eitA. Thus the circle theorem is the integer-spectrum version of the one-parameter theorem.
