Let the summands be independent and identically distributed random variables with mean μ, variance σ2>0, and standardized third cumulant γ1​=E[(Y1​−μ)3]/σ3. Define
Sn∗​=σn​∑i=1n​(Yi​−μ)​.
(30)
An Edgeworth expansion refines the central limit theorem using the higher cumulants of one summand. Some smoothness and moment assumptions are essential: finite variance alone does not supply a density expansion. A convenient sufficient set for the expansions here is a smooth density whose derivatives decay faster than every inverse power, together with Eeδ∣Y1​∣<∞ for some δ>0. These assumptions give all required moments, Fourier smoothing and the nonlattice characteristic-function condition. Less restrictive sufficient conditions are possible, but this set covers the logistic model below.
Write ϕ and Φ for the standard normal density and standard normal distribution function. The first-order density Edgeworth expansion is
pSn∗​​(x)=ϕ(x)[1+6n​γ1​​(x3−3x)]+O(n−1).​
(31)
The remainder is uniform on every fixed compact interval under the stated assumptions. To see the origin of the correction, for Z=(Y1​−μ)/σ the cumulant expansion of its characteristic function gives
nlogEeitZ/n​=−2t2​+6n​γ1​(it)3​+O(n−1).
(32)
Exponentiating and using Fourier inversion yields the displayed correction, since the inverse transform of (it)3e−t2/2 is −ϕ(3)(x)=(x3−3x)ϕ(x). The Probabilists' Hermite polynomial convention is being used: H2​(x)=x2−1 and H3​(x)=x3−3x.
The corresponding distribution function expansion is
FSn∗​​(x)=Φ(x)−6n​γ1​​(x2−1)ϕ(x)+O(n−1),​
(33)
uniformly on fixed compact intervals. The sign follows from dxd​[H2​(x)ϕ(x)]=−H3​(x)ϕ(x). These are expansions of both the density and distribution function; a uniform density error alone would not justify integrating an error bound over the entire real line without the accompanying tail control.
For a fixed α∈(0,1), write z=zα​=Φ−1(α). At leading order the proposed quantile expansion gives Φ(p0​(z))=Φ(z), and strict monotonicity gives p0​(z)=z. Substitute yα​=z+p1​(z)n−1/2+O(n−1) into the distribution function expansion. A Taylor expansion at z gives
FSn∗​​(yα​)=α+n​ϕ(z)​[p1​(z)−6γ1​​(z2−1)]+O(n−1).
(34)
Since ϕ(z)>0, the coefficient of n−1/2 must vanish. Thus the first Cornish-Fisher expansion coefficients are
p0​(z)=z,p1​(z)=6γ1​​(z2−1).​
(35)
For the logistic distribution, set Zi​=Yi​−θ. Its density q(z)=ez/(1+ez)2 satisfies q(−z)=q(z) and has exponentially decreasing tails. Symmetry and integrability give EZi​=0, and the supplied integral gives
EZi2​=2∫0∞​(1+ez)2z2ez​dz=3π2​.
(36)
Therefore EYi​=θ, Var(Yi​)=π2/3, and the central limit theorem gives
Tn​=π/3​n​(Yˉ−θ)​d​N(0,1).
(37)
Writing z−​=zα/2​ and z+​=z1−α/2​, the condition z−​≤Tn​≤z+​ is equivalent to
Yˉ−3n​π​z+​≤θ≤Yˉ−3n​π​z−​.
(38)
Consequently the confidence interval
(Yˉ−3n​π​z1−α/2​,Yˉ−3n​π​zα/2​)​
(39)
has limiting coverage Φ(z+​)−Φ(z−​)=1−α. Whether the finite endpoints are included makes no probability difference because the sample mean has a density.
The centered logistic distribution is symmetric, so γ1​=0. Its smooth density and exponentially decreasing derivatives satisfy the conditions stated above; in particular all moments exist and Fourier smoothing is available. The first correction in the Edgeworth expansion vanishes, leaving FTn​​(z)=Φ(z)+O(n−1) at each of the two fixed quantiles. Subtracting gives the stronger conclusion
Pθ​(θ lies in the interval)=1−α+O(n−1).​
(40)
The standardized law is independent of θ, so this coverage-error conclusion holds with the same bound for every location parameter.
