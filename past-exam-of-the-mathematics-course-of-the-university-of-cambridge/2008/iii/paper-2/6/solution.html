A subgroup H≤G is a benign subgroup when there are an embedding of G into a finitely presented group E and a finitely generated subgroup L≤E with G∩L=H. Finite generation is required of L, not of H itself. We use Britton's lemma, the normal form theorem for an amalgamated free product, and, for the final quotient assertion, the Higman embedding theorem.
Choose witnesses EH​,LH​ and EK​,LK​ for H,K. The amalgamated free product P=EH​∗G​EK​ embeds both witnesses and their common copy of G. It is finitely presented: take their two finite presentations and add the finitely many equalities identifying the images of a finite generating set of G. These equalities identify all elements of G, so they present precisely the amalgam. The original intersections G∩LH​=H and G∩LK​=K are preserved by the embeddings.
Now form the finitely presented group
T=⟨P,t,s∣t−1ℓt=ℓ (ℓ∈LH​),s−1ks=k (k∈LK​)⟩.
(21)
Only finitely many commutation relators, one for each of the chosen subgroup generators, are needed. The subgroup Q=⟨G,t,s⟩ is exactly the multiple HNN extension of G centralizing H and K. To prove this claim, take a word in G,t,s reduced for those associated subgroups. It remains reduced in T: a t-pinch with a coefficient in G occurs precisely when that coefficient lies in LH​∩G=H, and an s-pinch precisely when it lies in K. Britton's lemma therefore prevents any extra relation, and it also embeds G.
Write Gu=u−1Gu. The subgroup L∩​=Gts is finitely generated. If s−1t−1gts∈G, Britton reduction must first pinch t−1gt, requiring g∈H; its value is then g. The remaining s−1gs can represent a base element only if g∈K, and then its value is again g. Conversely every g∈H∩K commutes with both letters. Hence
G∩L∩​=H∩K,​
(22)
so the intersection is benign.
For the join, put J=⟨H,K⟩ and L∨​=⟨Gt,Gs⟩, again finitely generated. The subgroup generated by G,Gt,Gs in Q is
U=(G∗H​Gt)∗K​Gs.
(23)
Indeed, expanding conjugate-copy syllables as t−1gt or s−1gs shows that the only possible Britton reductions are exactly the identifications in the indicated amalgamations; a reduced amalgam word therefore has no additional way to become trivial.
We use this elementary consequence of amalgam normal forms: if C≤D≤A, then inside A∗C​B the subgroup ⟨D,B⟩ is D∗C​B and intersects A exactly in D. A reduced expression with a B∖C syllable cannot lie in A, because multiplying it by an inverse base element still leaves a reduced nontrivial amalgam word. Apply this first to G∗H​Gt with D=J, obtaining D1​=⟨J,Gt⟩ and D1​∩G=J. Since K≤J≤D1​, apply it again in U=(G∗H​Gt)∗K​Gs to obtain ⟨D1​,Gs⟩∩(G∗H​Gt)=D1​.
Each element of H lies in Gt, and each element of K lies in Gs, since the corresponding stable letter centralizes it. Thus J≤L∨​, and L∨​=⟨D1​,Gs⟩. Intersecting the previous identities with G gives
G∩L∨​=J=⟨H,K⟩.​
(24)
This proves the intersection and join of benign subgroups construction with actual finitely generated witnesses inside T.
Finally let N◃G be benign, witnessed by N=G∩L in a finitely presented group E. Choose finite generators X for G and express both X and a finite generating set for L as words in E. Enumerate every word w on X, every word v on those generators of L, and every finite proof in the presentation of E that their substituted words are equal. Dovetail these searches and list w when such a proof is found. Exactly the words representing elements of G∩L=N are listed: membership has a finite expression in the generators of L, and every valid equality has a finite relator proof. Since N is normal, these are exactly the null words of G/N.
Use this enumerable list as relators on the finite alphabet X. Its normal closure is precisely the kernel of F(X)→G/N, so it is a recursive presentation of a group for G/N. The Higman embedding theorem now yields
G/N embeds in a finitely presented group.​
(25)
This is normal benign subgroup quotient embedding; no word algorithm for E or membership algorithm for L was assumed.
