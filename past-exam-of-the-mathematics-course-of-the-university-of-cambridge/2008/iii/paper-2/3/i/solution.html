Past exam of the mathematics course of the University of Cambridge / 2008 / iii / Paper 2 / 3 / i / Solution

 ... Past exam of the mathematics course of the University of Cambridge 2008 iii Paper 2 3 i
OurBigBook.com Words: 212
Suppose G=⟨g1​,…,gn​∣R⟩ has a finite group presentation. Work in the free product B=G∗F(a,b) and put
u0​=a,ui​=b−iabi,v0​=b,vi​=gi​a−ibai(1≤i≤n).
(10)
The ui​ freely generate a subgroup of F(a,b). Indeed, in a reduced word in these conjugates, combine adjacent powers with the same index. Expanding the remaining word leaves nonzero powers of a separated by nonzero powers of b, so the normal form theorem for a free product makes it nonidentity. This proves the free conjugate bases in a rank-two free group fact we need.
The group homomorphism B→F(a,b) killing G sends vi​ to a−ibai, including v0​=b. These are a free conjugate basis by the same argument with a,b interchanged. A nonempty reduced word in the vi​ therefore has nontrivial image and cannot be trivial in B. Thus ui​↦vi​ extends to an isomorphism between the two rank-(n+1) free groups embedded in B.
Adjoin one stable letter implementing this isomorphism:
E=⟨g1​,…,gn​,a,b,t∣R, t−1ui​t=vi​ (0≤i≤n)⟩.
(11)
We assume Britton's lemma, including its assertion that the base embeds in this HNN extension, and the factor-embedding assertion of the normal form theorem for a free product. Hence G↪E. The presentation is finite, and its relations express every generator in a,t:
b=t−1at,gi​=t−1b−iabit(a−ibai)−1.
(12)
Eliminating b,gi​ by Tietze transformations therefore gives a two-generator finite presentation with G↪E=⟨a,t⟩​. This explicitly constructs the two-generator HNN embedding; it does not rely on an arbitrary two-generator embedding preserving finite presentability.
