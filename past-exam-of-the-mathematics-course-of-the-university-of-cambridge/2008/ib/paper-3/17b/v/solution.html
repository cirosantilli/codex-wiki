OurBigBook.com Words: 194
Use real physical fields in the quadratic energy expressions. The vector identity ∇⋅(E×B)=B⋅(∇×E)−E⋅(∇×B) and vacuum Maxwell's equations give
∇⋅(μ0​E×B​)=−μ0​1​B⋅∂t​B−ε0​E⋅∂t​E=−∂t​(2ε0​E2​+2μ0​B2​).
(72)
Integrating over a fixed volume and applying the divergence theorem proves Poynting's theorem:
∮A​μ0​E×B​⋅dA=−dtd​∫V​(2ε0​E2​+2μ0​B2​)dV.​
(73)
The left side is outward energy flux through the closed surface, not merely local intensity.
For a linearly polarized wave with phase χ=k⋅r−ωt, the real fields give Poynting vector S=ε0​cE02​cos2χk. Local intensity oscillates at 2ω. In the closed-surface integral, the time-independent part cancels because ∮A​k⋅dA=0. The remaining flux generally oscillates at 2ω, has zero time average, and may vanish identically for special volume geometries.
For circular polarization, take E=E0​(e1​cosχ+e2​sinχ) with orthonormal transverse vectors. Then E2=E02​ and B=k×E/c, so S=ε0​cE02​k is spatially and temporally constant. Therefore the closed-surface Poynting flux of a plane wave is
∮A​S⋅dA=0at every time for circular polarization.​
(74)
There is still a nonzero steady local energy flow: equal energy enters and leaves every fixed closed volume. Calling the net closed-surface flux a nonzero constant would confuse these two quantities.
