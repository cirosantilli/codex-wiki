Let p=(x0​,y0​), a=fx​(p) and b=fy​(p). For sufficiently small h,k, decompose the increment into an x-segment at height y0​+k and a y-segment at x0​. The one-variable mean value theorem applies on each segment because existence of its partial derivative gives continuity along the segment. Thus, when both increments are nonzero,
f(x0​+h,y0​+k)−f(p)=hfx​(x0​+θh,y0​+k)+kfy​(x0​,y0​+ηk)
(45)
for some 0<θ,η<1. The same formula with the absent segment omitted covers zero increments. Continuity of both partial derivatives at p makes the remainder after subtracting ah+bk at most ε(∣h∣+∣k∣) for small increments, and hence at most 2​εh2+k2​. Therefore the Fréchet derivative is (h,k)↦ah+bk. Continuity throughout the disc is not required; continuity at the point suffices.
For the determinant, expand by multilinearity of the determinant in the columns of I+H. Taking no perturbed column gives one; taking exactly one gives trH; all other terms have degree at least two in the entries of H. Since matrix-entry functionals are bounded in any finite-dimensional norm, their sum is O(∥H∥2). Hence
det(I+H)=1+trH+O(∥H∥2),DIdet​(H)=trH.​
(46)
For invertible A, multiplicativity of the determinant gives det(A+H)=detAdet(I+A−1H). The bounded linear map H↦A−1H transfers the preceding remainder estimate, giving the derivative of the determinant
DAdet​(H)=detAtr(A−1H).​
(47)
To justify an open invertible neighborhood, let ∥K∥<1. Completeness and submultiplicativity make the Neumann series S=∑j=0∞​(−K)j converge in matrix norm. Multiplying finite partial sums by I+K gives I−(−K)N+1, which tends to I. Continuity of multiplication proves (I+K)S=S(I+K)=I. Thus I+K is invertible, and the series gives (I+H)−1=I−H+O(∥H∥2) near zero.
Apply the first-derivative formula at I+H to a test matrix K:
DI+Hdet​(K)​=[1+trH+O(∥H∥2)]tr[(I−H+O(∥H∥2))K]=trK+trHtrK−tr(HK)+O(∥H∥2∥K∥).​
(48)
The last estimate is uniform for ∥K∥≤1, so it proves differentiability of the derivative in the operator norm, rather than merely a directional second derivative. The second derivative of the determinant is the symmetric bilinear map
D2Idet​(H,K)=trHtrK−tr(HK).​
(49)
Its symmetry follows from tr(HK)=tr(KH).
