An R-module M is a free module if it has a basis (ei​)i∈I​: every m∈M has a unique expression as a finite sum ∑i​ri​ei​, with ri​∈R. Equivalently, it is a direct sum of copies of R. Let q:M→M/N be the quotient map. When M/N is free, choose a basis (f​j​) and lifts fj​∈M. The universal property of a free module defines an R-linear map s:M/N→M by s(∑j​rj​f​j​)=∑j​rj​fj​. It satisfies q∘s=id, so every m∈M decomposes uniquely as
m=(m−s(q(m)))+s(q(m)),m−s(q(m))∈N.
(38)
This gives a split short exact sequence and M≅N⊕(M/N). If N is also free, the union of its basis and the lifted quotient basis is a basis of M: spanning follows from the decomposition, and applying q to a putative relation kills all quotient coefficients before independence in N kills the remaining ones. Thus free submodule and free quotient imply a free module.
For the first claim, take R=Z and
M=Z,N=2Z,M′=Z⊕Z/2Z,N′=Z⊕{0}.
(39)
Here N≅N′≅Z are free modules and M/N≅M′/N′≅Z/2Z. But M is a torsion-free module, whereas M′ contains the nonzero torsion element (0,1). Therefore claim (1) is false.
For the second claim, the same lifting construction works without assuming that N is free. Since both isomorphic quotient modules are free, it yields
M≅N⊕(M/N)≅N′⊕(M′/N′)≅M′.
(40)
Thus claim (2) is true. Equivalently, this uses the fact that free modules are projective.
