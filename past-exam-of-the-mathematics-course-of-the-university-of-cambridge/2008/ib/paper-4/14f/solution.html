A basis of a topology is a family B of open sets such that every open set is a union of members of B. Equivalently, whenever x∈U and U is open, there is B∈B with x∈B⊆U. For a metric space, all open balls form a basis. For the product topology on X×Y, the rectangles U×V with U,V open in their respective factors form a basis; rectangles made from factor bases also suffice.
If C⊆X and D⊆Y are countable dense subsets, then C×D is countable. Every nonempty basic rectangle meets it, since U∩C and V∩D are nonempty. Every nonempty open set contains such a rectangle, proving that a product of two separable spaces is separable.
Suppose a metric space has a countable dense set C. The balls B(c,r) with c∈C and positive rational r form a countable basis. Indeed, given x∈U open, choose ϵ>0 with B(x,ϵ)⊆U, then choose c∈C with d(c,x)<ϵ/3 and rational r satisfying
d(c,x)<r<ϵ−d(c,x).
(51)
The triangle inequality gives x∈B(c,r)⊆B(x,ϵ)⊆U. Conversely, from any countable basis select one point of each nonempty member. These points form a countable dense subset, because every nonempty open set contains a basis member. This direction holds for arbitrary topological spaces. Thus a metric space is separable exactly when it is a second-countable space. Intersecting a countable basis with a subspace gives a countable basis for its subspace topology; selecting points as above shows that every subspace of a separable metric space is separable.
For the Sorgenfrey line, Q meets every nonempty interval [a,b), so it is a countable dense subset. In the Sorgenfrey plane, however, for any x∈R and ϵ>0,
([x,x+ϵ)×[−x,−x+ϵ))∩Y={(x,−x)}.
(52)
Indeed, (t,−t) in that rectangle requires both t≥x and t≤x. Every singleton is therefore open in the subspace topology on Y, which is the discrete topology. A dense subset of a discrete space must contain every point. Since Y is in bijection with the uncountable set R, it has no countable dense subset. Hence X is separable and Y is not separable, illustrating why the metric assumption in the inheritance statement matters.
