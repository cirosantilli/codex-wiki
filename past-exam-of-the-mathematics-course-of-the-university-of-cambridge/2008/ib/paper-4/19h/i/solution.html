Assume the explanatory variables are not all equal, so Sxx​=∑i​(xi​−xˉ)2>0. In the Gaussian linear model, the log-likelihood is
ℓ(α,β,σ2)=−2n​log(2πσ2)−2σ21​∑i=1n​(Yi​−α−βxi​)2.
(76)
For fixed σ2, maximum likelihood estimation therefore minimizes the residual sum of squares. Differentiating in α,β gives the least-squares normal equations
∑i​(Yi​−α−β​xi​)=0,∑i​xi​(Yi​−α−β​xi​)=0.
(77)
The first gives α=Yˉ−β​xˉ; substituting it into the second gives β​=Sxy​/Sxx​, with Sxy​=∑i​(xi​−xˉ)(Yi​−Yˉ). Let SSE=∑i​(Yi​−α−β​xi​)2. Differentiating the profiled log-likelihood in σ2 gives
β​=Sxx​Sxy​​,α=Yˉ−β​xˉ,σ2=nSSE​.​
(78)
For the variance estimator this is the interior maximum when SSE>0, which occurs almost surely under the model when n>2.
The coefficient estimators are linear functions of the independent normal errors. In particular,
β​−β=Sxx​∑i​(xi​−xˉ)εi​​,α−α=εˉ−xˉ(β​−β).
(79)
Their multivariate normal distribution has mean (α,β)T and covariance matrix
σ2(1/n+xˉ2/Sxx​−xˉ/Sxx​​−xˉ/Sxx​1/Sxx​​).
(80)
The fitted vector is the orthogonal projection onto the two-dimensional span of (1,…,1)T and (x1​,…,xn​)T. The residual vector is the complementary projection of the spherical normal error vector. Orthogonal normal projections are independent, and in an orthonormal basis of that complementary space its n−2 coordinates are independent N(0,σ2). Consequently
σ2SSE​=σ2nσ2​∼χn−22​,
(81)
independently of (α,β​). This also gives the unbiased estimator s2=SSE/(n−2) rather than the variance MLE.
Under the null hypothesis α=0, for n>2 the Student t test for a simple-regression intercept uses
T=s1/n+xˉ2/Sxx​​α​∼tn−2​,reject if ∣T∣>tn−2,1−η/2​.​
(82)
Here η is the prescribed significance level. The normal numerator divided by its true standard deviation is independent of (n−2)s2/σ2, which proves the stated Student t-distribution. If Sxx​=0, the two coefficients are not separately identifiable; if n≤2, this residual-variance-based test is unavailable.
