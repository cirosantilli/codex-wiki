∫−11​1−x2​Tn​Tm​​dx=2π​δnm​(1+δn0​).​

For Gaussian quadrature, choose the n distinct nodes as the zeros of the degree-n orthogonal polynomial for the weight, here Tn​, and choose the weights as the weighted integrals of their Lagrange interpolation basis functions. If a polynomial p has degree at most 2n−1, divide it as p=qTn​+r, where both q and r have degree at most n−1. Orthogonality makes the integral of qTn​ zero, while its quadrature sum also vanishes; interpolation integrates r exactly. This proves degree 2n−1 exactness. It is optimal because the squared nodal polynomial, of degree 2n, has zero quadrature sum and strictly positive integral.
For n=3, T3​(x)=4x3−3x, so the nodes are −3​/2,0,3​/2. Their interpolation basis functions are
ℓ−​(x)=32​x(x−23​​),ℓ0​(x)=1−34​x2,ℓ+​(x)=32​x(x+23​​).
(27)
The weighted zeroth and second moments are π and π/2, and the first moment is zero. Integrating these three basis functions gives equal weights π/3, hence the Chebyshev–Gauss quadrature rule
∫−11​1−x2​f(x)​dx≈3π​[f(−23​​)+f(0)+f(23​​)],​
(28)
which is exact for every polynomial of degree at most 5.
