Incompressibility makes each perturbation velocity potential a harmonic function. A horizontal mode ei(kx+ℓy)+st has q=k2+ℓ2​>0. Decay away from the interface selects φ1​=A1​eqzei(kx+ℓy)+st below and φ2​=A2​e−qzei(kx+ℓy)+st above. The kinematic boundary condition gives
qA1​=(s+ikU1​)ζ0​,−qA2​=(s+ikU2​)ζ0​.
(50)
Insert these into the dynamic boundary condition for an inviscid interface and cancel a nonzero mode amplitude to obtain
ρ1​(s+ikU1​)2+ρ2​(s+ikU2​)2+qg(ρ1​−ρ2​)=0.
(51)
Writing ρT​=ρ1​+ρ2​ and Uˉ=(ρ1​U1​+ρ2​U2​)/ρT​, completion of the square gives
ρT​(s+ikUˉ)2−ρT​k2ρ1​ρ2​​(U1​−U2​)2+qg(ρ1​−ρ2​)=0.
(52)
Consequently
s=−ikρ1​+ρ2​ρ1​U1​+ρ2​U2​​ ±(ρ1​+ρ2​)2k2ρ1​ρ2​(U1​−U2​)2​−ρ1​+ρ2​qg(ρ1​−ρ2​)​​.​
(53)
The imaginary advection term does not change growth rates. A mode grows exponentially precisely when the radicand is positive, equivalently
k2ρ1​ρ2​(U1​−U2​)2>qg(ρ12​−ρ22​).​
(54)
If the upper fluid is heavier, every nonzero wavevector is unstable even without shear: this is Rayleigh-Taylor instability. If the lower fluid is heavier, gravity stabilizes sufficiently long waves, but any nonzero velocity difference destabilizes sufficiently short waves with ℓ=0: this is Kelvin-Helmholtz instability. For equal densities, shear destabilizes modes with k=0. A negative radicand gives purely imaginary frequencies and oscillatory modes; equality is the marginal threshold for exponential growth.
