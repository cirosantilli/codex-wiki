Write r=1−α−β>0. Differentiating the probability generating function at (1,1) gives
E[X]=rα​​,E[Y]=rβ​.

Expanding its denominator as a geometric series and then applying the binomial theorem gives
1−αs−βtr​=r∑k≥0​(αs+βt)k=r∑x,y≥0​(xx+y​)αxβysxty.

Coefficient identification proves Pr(X=x,Y=y)=r(xx+y​)αxβy​, a one-stop negative multinomial distribution.
For an independent identically distributed sample let SX​=∑Xi​ and SY​=∑Yi​. Ignoring parameter-independent factors, the log-likelihood is ℓ=nlnr+SX​lnα+SY​lnβ. When both sums are positive, its stationary equations are SX​/α=n/r and SY​/β=n/r. Together with r+α+β=1, these give
α=n+SX​+SY​SX​​=1+X+YX​,β​=n+SX​+SY​SY​​.​
(52)
The Hessian is −(n/r2)11T−diag(SX​/α2,SY​/β2), negative definite in this case, so the stationary point is the unique global maximum.
There is an important boundary qualification: if SX​=0, the derivative with respect to α is −n/r<0, so the likelihood increases as α decreases to zero. The displayed formula then gives a boundary estimator, not an attained MLE in the stated open parameter space α,β>0. The analogous issue holds for SY​=0. The formulas are MLEs on the extension allowing zero category probabilities, and interior MLEs when both counts are positive.
For bias, put p=α+β and U=(SX​+SY​)/n. Then E[U]=p/(1−p) and α+β​=g(U) with g(u)=u/(1+u). Its second derivative is −2/(1+u)3<0, and U is nondegenerate. Strict Jensen's inequality gives
E[α+β​]<g(EU)=p.
(53)
In fact both estimates are downward biased. Conditional on Xi​+Yi​, the first count is binomial with success probability α/p; hence conditional on S=SX​+SY​, SX​ is also binomial with that probability. Therefore
E[α]=pα​E[n+SS​]<α,E[β​]<β.
(54)
This proves the finite-sample bias in a negative multinomial probability estimator, using the boundary-extended formulas whenever necessary.
Finally, the strong law of large numbers applies because the nonnegative variables have finite means: almost surely X→α/r and Y→β/r. Continuity of the estimator function then gives
α⟶1+α/r+β/rα/r​=αalmost surely,​

and similarly for β​. Thus the estimates are strongly consistent despite finite-sample bias. Almost surely both sample sums eventually become positive, so the open-parameter MLE issue affects finite samples rather than this asymptotic conclusion.
