Because ∣un​(x)∣≤Mn​ and the numerical series converges, its tails can be made arbitrarily small independently of x:
supx∈E​​∑n=N+1M​un​(x)​≤∑n>N​Mn​⟶0.
(26)
Thus the partial sums satisfy the uniform Cauchy criterion; completeness of the real numbers supplies the pointwise limit, and the same tail bound proves uniform convergence. This is the Weierstrass M-test.
For ER​ with R>0, choose an integer N>2R. If n>N and ∣x∣<R, then ∣x±n∣≥n−R>n/2, so ∣un​(x)∣≤8/n2. The tail is therefore uniformly convergent by the M-test; adding its finitely many earlier terms preserves uniform convergence, even though those earlier terms can be unbounded near excluded integers. For R≤0 the set is empty and the claim is vacuous.
Each summand is continuous on E=R∖Z. Around any x∈E, choose an interval containing x, avoiding integers and lying in some ER​. The uniform-limit continuity theorem then proves f is continuous on E​.
The series is not uniformly convergent on all of E. Uniform convergence would require un​→0 uniformly, but un​(n+1/2)≥4 for every n>0. These evaluation points belong to E, contradicting that necessary condition.
