The equivalence class of x∈X is [x]={y∈X:y∼x}. By the reflexivity of the equivalence relation, x∈[x], so every equivalence class is nonempty and their union is X.
Suppose [x]∩[z]=∅, and choose w in this intersection. We have w∼x and w∼z. Symmetry and transitivity give x∼z. If y∈[x], then y∼x∼z, so y∈[z]; interchanging x,z proves the reverse inclusion. Thus two equivalence classes are either identical or disjoint. The distinct equivalence classes therefore form a set partition of X. This also covers X=∅, whose family of equivalence classes is empty.

For positive integers, m/m=20, establishing reflexivity. If m/n=2k, then n/m=2−k, establishing symmetry. If also n/ℓ=2j, then m/ℓ=2k+j, establishing transitivity. Hence this is an equivalence relation.
Every positive integer can be written uniquely as m=2rd, where r≥0 is an integer and d is an odd positive integer, its odd part of a positive integer. Existence follows by repeatedly dividing by two while the result is even; the process terminates because the positive integers strictly decrease. For uniqueness, 2rd=2se with d,e odd would make one of d,e even if r=s; therefore r=s and d=e.
Multiplying by a power of two preserves the odd part of a positive integer, and two positive integers with the same odd part have ratio a power of two. The equivalence class indexed by odd d is consequently {2rd:r≥0}. The required representative set is A={1,3,5,…}​.

A set is countable if it admits an injection into the natural numbers; this includes finite sets and the empty set. Use N0​={0,1,2,…} for the enumeration. The Cantor pairing function
Π(i,j)=2(i+j)(i+j+1)​+j
(9)
is a bijection N02​→N0​. Indeed, on the diagonal i+j=s, its values are the consecutive integers from s(s+1)/2 through s(s+1)/2+s. These intervals are disjoint, and the next starts one beyond the preceding endpoint. Every nonnegative integer therefore determines exactly one diagonal s, then exactly one j, and finally i=s−j. If one's convention starts the natural numbers at one, translate both coordinates by one. Thus N×N is a countable set.
For countable sets X,Y, choose injections eX​:X→N0​ and eY​:Y→N0​. The map (x,y)↦Π(eX​(x),eY​(y)) is an injection, proving their Cartesian product is countable. Repeating this proves that every finite Cartesian power of a countable set is countable.
For a sequence of countable sets Xn​, choose an injection en​:Xn​→N0​ for each n. For x∈⋃n​Xn​, let n(x) be the least index containing x. The map
x⟼Π(n(x),en(x)​(x))
(10)
is an injection: its value recovers n(x) and then x. This proves the countable union of countable sets theorem. Selecting all en​ uses the usual axiom of countable choice; if enumerations are already supplied, the construction itself needs no additional choices.

For a countable set A, the polynomials of polynomial degree at most d with coefficients in A are described by coefficient tuples in Ad+1. The finite Cartesian power of a countable set result makes this a countable set. Taking the union over d≥0 shows there are only countably many such polynomials altogether; restricting to the nonzero polynomials preserves countability.

Let Ω be the Earth's angular velocity magnitude. Use the local east, north and upward unit vectors eE​,eN​,eU​, with eE​×eN​=eU​. At latitude λ, the Earth's angular velocity and the train's ground-relative velocity are
Ω=ΩcosλeN​+ΩsinλeU​,v′=VeN​.
(52)
Thus the east component of the Coriolis force is
−2mΩ×v′=2mΩVsinλeE​.
(53)
For a north–south track, the train has no eastward acceleration relative to Earth. Its path curvature lies in the local north–vertical plane. The centrifugal acceleration and Newtonian gravity also lie in that plane, and the Earth's constant angular velocity gives no Euler acceleration. Hence the sideways force from the track must cancel the eastward Coriolis force:
Ftrack,side​=−2mΩVsinλeE​.​
(54)
Its magnitude is 2mΩVsinλ, and its direction is west in the Northern hemisphere. The eastward tendency is the apparent Coriolis force; the requested track force has the opposite direction.

A hollow spherical shell of the same mass and radius places its material farther from the centre, giving a larger moment of inertia. Under rolling without slipping, a given centre speed therefore requires more rotational kinetic energy; the same gravitational drop yields a smaller speed. Replacing the solid sphere by a hollow shell increases TR​ and leaves TS​ unchanged. The sliding case remains nonrotating and has the same center of mass path and gravitational drop.
