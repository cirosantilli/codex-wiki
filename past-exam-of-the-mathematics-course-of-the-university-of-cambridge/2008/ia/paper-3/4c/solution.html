For a constant orthogonal Cartesian coordinate change xi′​=Rij​xj​, a Cartesian second-rank tensor has components satisfying
Tij′​(x′)=Rik​Rjl​Tkl​(x).​
(13)
Both indices transform by the coordinate-change matrix; summation over repeated indices is understood by the Einstein summation convention. A vector would have the one-index rule Ui′​=Rik​Uk​.
Define Ui​=∂Tij​/∂xj​. Since xm​=Rjm​xj′​, the chain rule gives ∂/∂xj′​=Rjm​∂/∂xm​. The rotation coefficients are constant, so
Ui′​​=∂xj′​∂Tij′​​=Rjm​Rik​Rjl​∂xm​∂Tkl​​=Rik​δml​∂xm​∂Tkl​​=Rik​∂xl​∂Tkl​​=Rik​Uk​.​
(14)
The identity Rjm​Rjl​=δml​ follows from orthogonality. Hence the contracted derivative transforms as a vector. This proves the divergence of a Cartesian second-rank tensor rule for global Cartesian rotations. In curvilinear coordinates the corresponding construction uses a covariant derivative; ordinary partial derivatives alone would not have the stated tensorial transformation law.
