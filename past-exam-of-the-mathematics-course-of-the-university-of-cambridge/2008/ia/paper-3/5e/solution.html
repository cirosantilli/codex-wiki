For a normal subgroup H⊴G, define multiplication of left cosets by
(gH)(kH)=(gk)H.​
(15)
To check that this is well-defined, replace g,k by gh1​,kh2​ with h1​,h2​∈H. Their product is gh1​kh2​=gk(k−1h1​k)h2​, and normality puts (k−1h1​k)h2​ in H. Thus the product coset is independent of the representatives. Associativity follows from multiplication in G, the identity is H, and the inverse of gH is g−1H. These operations form the quotient group G/H.
For the unheaded finite-index conclusion, let G act on the n left cosets of its proper subgroup H by g⋅(xH)=gxH. This is well-defined even if H is not normal: replacing x by xh leaves gxH unchanged. The maps are bijections with inverses supplied by g−1, and their compositions give a group homomorphism φ:G→Sn​. Its kernel of a group homomorphism N is normal, by the calculation in part (ii).
The action is not trivial, because some g∈/H sends the coset H to gH=H; hence N=G. If N were trivial, φ would be injective, implying ∣G∣≤∣Sn​∣=n!. Under the given strict inequality this is impossible. Thus N is a nontrivial proper normal subgroup, and
∣G∣>n! ⟹ G is not simple.​
(16)
More precisely, N=⋂x∈G​xHx−1 is the subgroup core: g fixes every coset if and only if x−1gx∈H for every x. This identifies the normal subgroup produced by the coset action.
