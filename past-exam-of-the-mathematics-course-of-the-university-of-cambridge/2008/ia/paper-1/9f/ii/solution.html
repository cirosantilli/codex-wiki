For any fixed r>0, logy=o(y1/r) as y→∞. For example, l'Hôpital's rule applied to logy/y1/r gives the limit zero. With y=logn, this implies (loglogn)r<logn eventually. Therefore
n(loglogn)r1​>nlogn1​

for all sufficiently large n. The comparison series diverges by the integral test for convergence, since

The comparison test for series gives
n=3∑∞​n(loglogn)r1​ diverges for every r>0.​
(50)
One may obtain the same growth bound by extending the given logarithmic estimate to real y through ⌈y⌉ and choosing any exponent smaller than 1/r; no special convergence test for iterated logarithms is needed.
