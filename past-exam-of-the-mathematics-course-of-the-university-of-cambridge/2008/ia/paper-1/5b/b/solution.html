The parametric equation of a straight line through a with unit direction t is
r=a+st,s∈R.​
(22)
If the two lines meet, then a1​+st1​=a2​+tt2​ for some real s,t, so a1​−a2​ lies in the linear span of their direction vectors. Its dot product with their cross product is consequently zero:
(a1​−a2​)⋅(t1​×t2​)=0.​
(23)
This is not sufficient without excluding parallel lines. For instance a1​=(0,0,0), a2​=(0,1,0) and t1​=t2​=(1,0,0) satisfy it, but the lines are distinct and never meet. For nonparallel lines the condition is sufficient, because the two directions span the plane perpendicular to their nonzero cross product.
For nonparallel skew lines, put n=(t1​×t2​)/∣t1​×t2​∣. Every joining vector is a1​−a2​+st1​−tt2​, with fixed component (a1​−a2​)⋅n along n. Its length is at least the absolute value of that component. Its perpendicular component can be canceled by a unique choice of s,t, since the two independent directions span n⊥. This lower bound is attained, proving the distance between skew lines:
d=∣t1​×t2​∣∣(a1​−a2​)⋅(t1​×t2​)∣​.​
(24)
