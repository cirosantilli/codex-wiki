During ejection, the remaining mass is m(t)=mo​+mw​−Qt, and the water is exhausted at tc​=mw​/Q. Consider the momentum of the octopus and the small portion of water expelled during dt. Initially it is mu. At the end of this interval, the remaining body has mass m−Qdt and velocity u+du, while the expelled mass Qdt has laboratory velocity u−V, to first order. Hence the total final momentum is
(m−Qdt)(u+du)+Qdt(u−V)=mu+mdu−QVdt+o(dt).
(30)
The external impulse from quadratic drag is −αu2dt. Equating this to the change in total momentum, and dividing by dt, proves the equation of motion
mdtdu​=QV−αu2.​
(31)
The outgoing water's momentum flux is essential: applying d(mu)/dt to the remaining body alone would omit it.
For dimensional analysis, the independent physical inputs are mo​,mw​,Q,V,α, with dimensions
[mo​]=[mw​]=M,[Q]=MT−1,[V]=LT−1,[α]=ML−1.
(32)
The scales mo​, mo​/Q and Vmo​/Q fix units of mass, time and length. In these units, the only remaining independent dimensionless variables are
λ=mo​mw​​,μ=QαV​.​
(33)
For example, with s=Qt/mo​ and w=u/V, the equation of motion becomes (1+λ−s)dw/ds=1−μw2, with w(0)=0 and ejection ending at s=λ. Thus the dimensionless terminal value depends only on λ,μ, proving uc​=Vf(λ,μ).
For its explicit value, put m0​=mo​+mw​ and U=QV/α​. The initially stationary solution stays below U, since the acceleration vanishes at U and is positive below it. Separation of variables gives
∫0u(t)​QV−αv2dv​=∫0t​m0​−Qτdτ​.

The two integrals yield
αQV​1​artanhUu(t)​=Q1​logm0​−Qtm0​​.

This is constant mass-loss propulsion with quadratic drag. At tc​, the remaining mass is mo​, giving
uc​=αQV​​tanh(QαV​​logmo​mo​+mw​​)=μ​V​tanh(μ​log(1+λ)).​

Thus f(λ,μ)=μ−1/2tanh(μ​log(1+λ)), verifying the required dimensional form. The limit as drag tends to zero is Vlog(1+λ), the ordinary rocket equation, while the positive-drag answer is below the terminal velocity U.
