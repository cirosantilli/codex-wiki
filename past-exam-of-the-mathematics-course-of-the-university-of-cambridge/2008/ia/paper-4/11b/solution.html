Let Ω be the Earth's angular velocity magnitude. Use the local east, north and upward unit vectors eE​,eN​,eU​, with eE​×eN​=eU​. At latitude λ, the Earth's angular velocity and the train's ground-relative velocity are
Ω=ΩcosλeN​+ΩsinλeU​,v′=VeN​.
(52)
Thus the east component of the Coriolis force is
−2mΩ×v′=2mΩVsinλeE​.
(53)
For a north–south track, the train has no eastward acceleration relative to Earth. Its path curvature lies in the local north–vertical plane. The centrifugal acceleration and Newtonian gravity also lie in that plane, and the Earth's constant angular velocity gives no Euler acceleration. Hence the sideways force from the track must cancel the eastward Coriolis force:
Ftrack,side​=−2mΩVsinλeE​.​
(54)
Its magnitude is 2mΩVsinλ, and its direction is west in the Northern hemisphere. The eastward tendency is the apparent Coriolis force; the requested track force has the opposite direction.
