A Hausdorff space is a topological space in which any two distinct points have disjoint open neighborhoods. Let K be a compact subset of a Hausdorff space and x∈/K. For each y∈K, choose disjoint open neighborhoods Uy​ of y and Wy​ of x. The Uy​ cover K, so compactness gives a finite subcover Uy1​​,…,Uym​​. Their paired intersection W=⋂r=1m​Wyr​​ is an open neighborhood of x disjoint from K. Thus every point of its complement has an open neighborhood in the complement, proving every compact subset of a Hausdorff space is closed. The empty set case is immediate.
In the cocountable topology on an uncountable set, any two nonempty open sets intersect: their combined complements are countable and cannot exhaust X. Thus the space is not Hausdorff.
Nevertheless every compact subset is closed here. In fact only finite subsets are compact. To prove this, suppose K is infinite and choose distinct points d1​,d2​,…∈K. The open sets

Finite sets are compact in any topological space, and in this cocountable topology they are closed because their complements are open. This proves the claimed behavior of compact subsets of a cocountable space.
