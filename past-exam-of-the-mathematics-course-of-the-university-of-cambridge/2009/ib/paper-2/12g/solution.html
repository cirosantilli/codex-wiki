For stereographic projection, choose the north pole N=(0,0,1) of the unit sphere and identify the plane Z=0 with the complex plane by z=X+iY. Project a point P=(X,Y,Z)=N along the line joining it to N; adjoin z=∞ for the north pole to identify the whole sphere with the Riemann sphere. A point of that line is N+t(P−N). Its last coordinate vanishes when t=1/(1−Z), giving
z=1−ZX+iY​.​
(32)
Using X2+Y2+Z2=1, or solving this line's second intersection with the sphere, gives the inverse
X=1+∣z∣22Rez​,Y=1+∣z∣22Imz​,Z=1+∣z∣2∣z∣2−1​.​
(33)
A great circle lies in a plane h1​X+h2​Y+h3​Z=0 through the origin. Its projected equation is
2h1​Rez+2h2​Imz+h3​(∣z∣2−1)=0.
(34)
It is a straight line only if h3​=0, meaning the plane contains the north pole and the south pole. Thus if all three sides of a nondegenerate spherical triangle projected to straight sides of a Euclidean triangle, all three distinct side planes would contain both poles. Each pair of side planes could then meet on the sphere only at those same two poles, which cannot supply three distinct triangle vertices. A nondegenerate spherical triangle cannot project to a Euclidean triangle. If a side passes through the projection pole, its image is unbounded and also cannot be a finite triangle side.
A nonidentity rotation in three dimensions has an axis through the origin: an orthogonal three-by-three matrix of determinant one has an eigenvalue one, since nonreal eigenvalues pair as conjugates, real eigenvalues are ±1, and their product is one. Its axis meets the unit sphere in an antipodal pair, which is fixed. In the identity case every point is fixed, so an antipodal pair can again be selected. Projecting P and −P gives
p=1−ZX+iY​,q=−1+ZX+iY​,pq​=−1−Z2X2+Y2​=−1
(35)
whenever both points are finite. Thus the associated Möbius transformation fixes an antipodal pair satisfying the claimed relation. The universally valid relation is q=−1/p​ on the Riemann sphere, with 0 and ∞ paired. A rotation about the projection axis fixes exactly that pair, so the PDF's literal finite product needs this extended-coordinate convention. This is the antipodal stereographic coordinate relation.
