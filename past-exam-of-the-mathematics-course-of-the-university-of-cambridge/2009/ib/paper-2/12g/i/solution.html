Take
M(z)=3−z3z−1​.​
(36)
Its defining matrix has nonzero determinant eight, so it is a Möbius transformation. Direct substitution shows that it fixes p=1 and q=−1, which satisfy pq​=−1. However, M′(1)=8/(3−1)2=2. Differentiating the inverse projection formula gives the round spherical line element ds=2∣dz∣/(1+∣z∣2). At a fixed finite point, its local length multiplier under a Möbius transformation is exactly ∣M′(p)∣, since the metric factors in numerator and denominator agree. Here that multiplier is two, so this map is not an isometry and cannot be a rotation in three dimensions of the sphere. It demonstrates that antipodal fixed points do not characterize sphere rotations.
