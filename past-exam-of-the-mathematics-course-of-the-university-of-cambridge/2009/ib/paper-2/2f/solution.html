For a finite group of order pam with p prime and p∤m, the Sylow theorems assert that a subgroup of order pa exists; every subgroup whose order is a power of p is contained in one such Sylow subgroup; all Sylow subgroups for that prime are conjugate; and their number np​ divides m and is congruent to one modulo p. A unique Sylow subgroup is a normal subgroup, since conjugation cannot move it to another one.
Here 56=7⋅8, so n7​ divides eight and is congruent to one modulo seven. Hence n7​=1 or 8. In the first case the Sylow subgroup of order seven is normal. In the second case, any two distinct order-seven subgroups intersect only at the identity, because a nontrivial intersection would generate both prime-order subgroups. Their nonidentity elements therefore account for 8⋅6=48 elements.
Exactly eight elements of the group remain. Every Sylow subgroup of order eight consists entirely of these remaining elements: a nonidentity element in it has order a power of two and cannot be one of the order-seven elements already counted. Each such Sylow subgroup must consequently equal the remaining eight-element set. It is unique and hence normal. Thus a group of order 56 has a normal subgroup of order seven or of order eight.
