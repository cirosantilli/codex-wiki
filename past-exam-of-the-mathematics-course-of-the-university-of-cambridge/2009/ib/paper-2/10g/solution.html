The ascending chain kerT⊆kerT2⊆⋯ must stabilize because V is finite-dimensional. Once kerTl=kerTl+1, it stays stable: if Tl+2v=0, then Tv∈kerTl+1=kerTl, so v∈kerTl+1=kerTl; induction repeats the argument. Choose a positive such l, giving kerT2l=kerTl.
If v∈kerTl∩imTl, write v=Tlw. Then T2lw=0, so w∈kerTl and v=0. By the rank-nullity theorem, the dimensions of those two subspaces sum to dimV. Hence
V=kerTl⊕imTl.​
(27)
This proves the finite-dimensional form of the Fitting lemma.
If detT=0, the kernel contains a nonzero vector u, and imT is a proper vector subspace. Extend a basis of that image of a linear map to a basis of V and choose a nonzero linear functional λ which vanishes on the former basis. Define S(v)=λ(v)u. It is a nonzero endomorphism, since λ takes a nonzero value somewhere, and
TS(v)=λ(v)Tu=0,ST(v)=λ(Tv)u=0.
(28)
Thus a singular endomorphism has a nonzero two-sided annihilator, as required.
For an idempotent linear map T, the decomposition v=(v−Tv)+Tv shows V=kerT⊕imT: the first term is killed by T, the second is fixed, and a vector both fixed and killed is zero. An adapted basis makes its matrix diag(0,Ir​), with r=rankT. Consequently equal ranks give identical adapted matrices and therefore a change of basis relating T1​,T2​. Conversely, similar matrices have the same rank because pre- and postmultiplication by invertible matrices preserve rank. Hence T1​ and T2​ are similar exactly when their ranks agree, proving the similarity classification of idempotent linear maps.
