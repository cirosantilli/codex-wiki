For a one-dimensional wavefunction, the probability density and probability current are
ρ=∣ψ∣2,j=2miℏ​(ψ∗ψx​−ψψx∗​)=mℏ​Im(ψ∗ψx​).
(51)
The Time-dependent Schrödinger equation with a real potential is iℏψt​=−ℏ2ψxx​/(2m)+Vψ. Multiply it and its complex conjugate by ψ∗ and ψ, respectively. Subtracting cancels the real-potential terms and gives
ρt​=2miℏ​(ψ∗ψxx​−ψψxx∗​)=−jx​.
(52)
This derives the continuity equation ρt​+jx​=0.
For k>0, the specified free-region wavefunction combines a right-moving incoming wave of unit amplitude and a left-moving reflected wave with complex reflection amplitude R. Applying the free Schrödinger operator to either plane wave gives E=ℏ2k2/(2m). Its time factor has modulus one, so
ρ=1+∣R∣2+2Re(Re−2ikx),ρt​=0,j=mℏk​(1−∣R∣2).
(53)
The density includes the stationary interference pattern; ∣R∣2, rather than R, is the reflection probability.
For the finite square well, let k=2mE​/ℏ outside and q=2m(E+V0​)​/ℏ inside. Write the stationary parts as eikx+Re−ikx to the left, Aeiqx+Be−iqx inside, and Teikx to the right. At both finite potential steps, the wavefunction and its first spatial derivative are continuous. Integrating the stationary Schrödinger equation across a shrinking step proves derivative continuity; a jump of the wavefunction would create an unmatched distributional derivative and is excluded.
Propagate the boundary values at a back through the well. The values at zero are
χ(0)=Teika(cosqa−iqk​sinqa),χ′(0)=Teika(qsinqa+ikcosqa).
(54)
Matching them to 1+R and ik(1−R), and adding the two equations after division of the second by ik, gives
2=Teika[2cosqa−i(qk​+kq​)sinqa].
(55)
Since the exterior wave numbers agree, the transmission probability is the current ratio ∣T∣2, so
∣T∣2=[1+4k2q2(q2−k2)2​sin2qa]−1.
(56)
When V0​=3E, q=2k=8mE​/ℏ, yielding
∣T∣2=[1+169​sin2ℏa8mE​​]−1.​
(57)
Classically an incoming particle of positive energy crosses the attractive well and emerges with its original energy, so transmission is one. The quantum result agrees exactly when qa is an integer multiple of π:
a=8mE​nπℏ​,n=1,2,….​
(58)
These are widths with resonant transmission through a square well; the degenerate width zero also has unit transmission.
