Use the ideal steady incompressible-flow model, neglecting height change across the smooth nozzle. Flow conservation gives inlet and outlet speeds u0​=Q/A0​ and u1​=Q/A1​. Set atmospheric pressure to zero. The Bernoulli equation between inlet and outlet gives
p0​=2ρ​(u12​−u02​)=2ρQ2​(A12​1​−A02​1​).​
(21)
Choose a control volume containing the fluid in the nozzle, with the positive axis pointing toward the outlet. The integral momentum equation gives
p0​A0​+Fwall on fluid​=ρQ(u1​−u0​).
(22)
Thus the fluid's force on the nozzle is p0​A0​−ρQ(u1​−u0​), in the outlet direction. Substitute the pressure and velocities:
Ffluid on nozzle​​=ρQ2[2A12​A0​​−A1​1​+2A0​1​]=2A0​ρQ2​(A1​A0​​−1)2.​
(23)
The fireman's holding force is equal and opposite. Therefore the required force points upstream and has magnitude
F=2A0​ρQ2​(A1​A0​​−1)2.​
(24)
This is the holding force on a contracting nozzle; retaining the inlet pressure force is essential to its sign and value.
