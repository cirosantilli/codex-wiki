This extends the hitting probability is the minimal nonnegative harmonic extension principle to the superharmonic inequality. Nonnegative sums justify the argument on a countable state space as well as a finite one.
Now suppose the Markov chain is irreducible and recurrent, and let g satisfy condition (a). In such a chain every state is hit almost surely from every other state. To see this directly, recurrence gives infinitely many visits to a starting state i. Irreducibility supplies a finite path from i to any target j with positive probability α, chosen with no return to i before reaching j. At each successive return to i, the Strong Markov property gives another trial with conditional success probability at least α. The chance of avoiding j through N such trials is at most (1−α)N, which tends to zero.
Apply the absorbing modification at the singleton {j}. Part (c) holds for arbitrary g satisfying (a), so
gi​≥(Png)i​≥gj​Pi​(τj​≤n).
(78)
Since gj​≥0 and the probability tends to one, gi​≥gj​. Interchanging i,j yields equality. Thus every nonnegative superharmonic function is constant on an irreducible recurrent Markov chain.
Conversely, if an irreducible Markov chain is not recurrent, choose a state j with return probability rj​=Pj​(τj+​<∞)<1. Its hitting probability h for {j} satisfies condition (a), and hj​=1. The first-step formula for a return after time zero gives (Ph)j​=rj​<1. Therefore h cannot be constant: a constant function with value one at j would have (Ph)j​=1. This supplies a nonconstant nonnegative superharmonic solution. Hence
P is recurrent⟺every solution of (a) is constant.​

The admissible constant solutions are precisely the nonnegative constants. This is recurrence characterized by constant nonnegative superharmonic functions.
