The vacuum Maxwell equations are ∇⋅E=∇⋅B=0, ∇×E=−Bt​ and ∇×B=Et​/c2. Taking the curl of Faraday's equation and using ∇×(∇×E)=∇(∇⋅E)−∇2E gives −∇2E=−Ett​/c2. The same calculation for Ampère's equation gives ∇2B=Btt​/c2. Hence each Cartesian component satisfies the electromagnetic wave equation.
Model the walls as a perfect conductor, with no oscillating fields in its interior. The tangential electric field is zero at the surface, otherwise it would drive unbounded conductor currents. The normal magnetic field is zero, since the divergence-free field has a continuous normal component and the interior oscillating field is zero. Thus n×E=0 and n⋅B=0. Normal E and tangential B may be nonzero because surface charge and surface current support their jumps.
The PDF writes the full magnetic field as B=B0​(x,y)z^ei(kz−ωt). Taken literally, this cannot be a nonzero travelling wave: ∇⋅B=ikBz​, so for k=0 it forces Bz​=0. The subsequent requests require transverse components. The consistent intended reading is Bz​=B0​(x,y)ei(kz−ωt), with transverse fields determined by the Maxwell equations. Use that reading below.
All fields have the same z,t factor. Put Δ=(ω/c)2−k2. The harmonic Maxwell equations are ∇×E=iωB and ∇×B=−iωE/c2. Their x and y components give
Ey​=−ωc2k​Bx​−ωic2​∂x​Bz​,Ex​=ωc2k​By​+ωic2​∂y​Bz​,
(59)
together with ∂y​Ez​−ikEy​=iωBx​ and ikEx​−∂x​Ez​=iωBy​. Substitution and solving, for Δ=0, gives
Bx​=Δi​(k∂x​Bz​−c2ω​∂y​Ez​),By​=Δi​(k∂y​Bz​+c2ω​∂x​Ez​).​
(60)
The corresponding electric components are
Ex​=Δi​(k∂x​Ez​+ω∂y​Bz​),Ey​=Δi​(k∂y​Ez​−ω∂x​Bz​).
(61)
For a transverse electric mode of a rectangular waveguide, Ez​=0. The electromagnetic wave equation becomes
(∂x2​+∂y2​+Δ)B0​=0.
(62)
At x=0,a, the tangential component Ey​=−iω∂x​Bz​/Δ vanishes, so ∂x​B0​=0; at y=0,b, the corresponding condition Ex​=0 gives ∂y​B0​=0. These are Neumann boundary conditions, and the normal-B conditions give the same conclusions through the displayed transverse magnetic components. Separation gives
B0​=Cmn​cosamπx​cosbnπy​,Δ=κmn2​=(amπ​)2+(bnπ​)2,
(63)
where m,n≥0 are integers and (m,n)=(0,0). The constant candidate is not a physical oscillating mode: its transverse derivatives vanish, so it has E=0 and Faraday's equation forces B=0 for ω=0. More generally a hollow single-conductor guide has no transverse-electromagnetic zero-cutoff mode: its transverse electrostatic potential is harmonic with the same constant boundary value, hence constant, leaving zero transverse field.
The dispersion relation is ω2=c2(k2+κmn2​). The waveguide cutoff frequency for this mode is ωmn​=cκmn​; below it k is imaginary and the field is evanescent. Since a≥b, the lowest cutoff occurs at (m,n)=(1,0):
ωc​=aπc​,fc​=2ac​.​
(64)
For a mode propagating in the positive z direction (k>0), above its own cutoff ωmn​, the phase velocity and group velocity are
vph​=kω​=1−(ωmn​/ω)2​c​>c,vg​=dkdω​=c1−(ωmn​/ω)2​<c.​
(65)
These waveguide phase and group velocities have product c2. The superluminal phase speed describes motion of a constant phase, while a narrow wave packet travels at the subluminal group velocity; it does not transmit information faster than light. At cutoff k=0 there is no travelling mode with nonzero longitudinal flux.
