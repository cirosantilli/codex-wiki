The centre of a group is Z(G)={z∈G:zg=gz for every g∈G}. It is a normal subgroup. For ∣G∣=pr with r≥1, let G act on itself by conjugation. The orbit-stabilizer theorem makes the size of the conjugacy class of g equal to [G:CG​(g)], a power of p. A class has size one exactly when g∈Z(G); every other class size is divisible by p. The class equation therefore gives
∣G∣=∣Z(G)∣+∑noncentral classes​[G:CG​(g)],
(29)
so p divides ∣Z(G)∣. Since the identity belongs to the centre of a group, its size is a positive multiple of p. This proves the nontrivial center of a finite p-group.
If G/Z(G) is cyclic, choose a representative a of a generator. Every element is amz for an integer m and z∈Z(G). Any two such elements commute, since their central factors commute with everything and their remaining factors are powers of the same element. Thus G is an abelian group, Z(G)=G, and the cyclic quotient by the centre of a group is trivial.
For a non-abelian group of order p3, the centre of a group has order p, p2 or p3 by the proved result and Lagrange's theorem. The last would make the group abelian. The middle would make G/Z(G) have prime order and hence cyclic, also making G abelian by the preceding argument. Therefore its centre has order p.
Finally write the given upper-unitriangular matrix as M(a,b,c). Direct matrix multiplication gives
