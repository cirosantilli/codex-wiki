Treat the four fixed row totals as four independent samples from binomial distributions. Under independence, each has the same unknown probability p of a third-child boy. The pooled maximum-likelihood estimator is p​=100/200=1/2, so each expected cell count is 25. The Pearson chi-squared test of homogeneity statistic is
X2=∑i=14​[25(Bi​−25)2​+25(Gi​−25)2​]=252​(92+32+02+62)=10.08​.
(17)
There are four independently varying row probabilities under the alternative, and one estimated common probability under the null; the degrees of freedom are therefore 4−1=3. Equivalently this is the (4−1)(2−1) contingency-table count. The fact that the pooled estimate happens to equal one half does not make it a specified null probability and does not change the degrees of freedom to four.
Since 10.08>7.8147, reject independence at the 5% level using the stated chi-squared calibration. All expected counts are sufficiently large for the usual asymptotic test.
