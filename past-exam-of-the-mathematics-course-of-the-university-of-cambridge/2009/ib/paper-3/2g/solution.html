These are the geodesic equation and Christoffel symbols. They follow by applying the Euler-Lagrange equation to the energy 21​gij​u˙iu˙j. Equivalently, in an embedded surface a constant-speed geodesic has acceleration normal to the surface.
Parameterize a circular cylinder by (acosθ,asinθ,z), where a>0. Its induced metric is a2dθ2+dz2, with constant coefficients, so all Christoffel symbols vanish in this chart. Thus
θ(s)=θ0​+cs,z(s)=z0​+ds.​
(3)
These geodesics on a circular cylinder are straight generators when c=0, circles around the cylinder when d=0, and helices when cd=0. Constant curves arise if both vanish. For unit-speed nonconstant curves, a2c2+d2=1. This description is global after allowing the angle to run through arbitrary multiples of 2π.
The unrolled coordinate u=aθ exhibits the intrinsic flatness of a circular cylinder: every displayed family is a straight line in (u,z). A circle is therefore a geodesic despite bending in ambient three-dimensional space; its acceleration is normal to the cylinder.
