On a common domain where all products are defined, expansion of the operator commutator gives
A[B,C]+[A,C]B=ABC−ACB+ACB−CAB=[AB,C]​.
(43)
Use the usual quantum position and momentum operators with pj​=−iℏ∂j​, the canonical commutation relation [xi​,pj​]=iℏδij​, and wavefunctions with the decay/domain conditions needed for adjoints. The cross-coordinate operators commute, so
L†=py​x−px​y=xpy​−ypx​=L.
(44)
For an eigenstate Lψ=λψ, hermiticity gives λ∥ψ∥2=λ∥ψ∥2, hence its eigenvalue is real.
The product identity gives [L,x]=iℏy, [L,y]=−iℏx, [L,px​]=iℏpy​, [L,py​]=−iℏpx​. Thus the commutators with x2,y2 cancel, as do those with px2​,py2​, proving [L,H]=0. Each oscillator energy eigenspace is finite-dimensional and preserved by L; the Hermitian restriction of L can be diagonalized within it. Therefore an energy eigenbasis can be chosen to diagonalize angular momentum as well.
For ϕ0​=e−(x2+y2)/(2ℏ), differentiation gives Δϕ0​=[(x2+y2)/ℏ2−2/ℏ]ϕ0​, so Hϕ0​=ℏϕ0​. Also [H,x]=−iℏpx​ and px​ϕ0​=ixϕ0​, giving H(xϕ0​)=2ℏxϕ0​; the same calculation holds for yϕ0​. Directly L=−iℏ(x∂y​−y∂x​), hence
Lϕ0​=0,Lϕx​=iℏϕy​,Lϕy​=−iℏϕx​.
(45)
The simultaneous normalized states are
ψ1​=π​ℏ(x+iy)ϕ0​​,ψ2​=π​ℏ(x−iy)ϕ0​​,λ1​=ℏ,λ2​=−ℏ,E1​=E2​=2ℏ.​
(46)
Their squared unnormalized norm is ∫r2e−r2/ℏd2x=πℏ2. Their inner product vanishes because the integral of (x−iy)2e−r2/ℏ is zero: the xy term is odd and the x2,y2 terms cancel by rotational symmetry.
Put a=eE. The new Hamiltonian has
[L,H′]=−a[L,x]=−iℏay.​
(47)
For a nonzero applied coupling a, a common normalizable eigenstate would be killed by the commutator, so yψ=0. It would have to be supported on the line y=0, a set of zero planar measure, and would be the zero vector in L2. Thus there are no nonzero common eigenstates in this case. Merely having a nonzero commutator would not prove that conclusion for arbitrary operators; the multiplication operator here has trivial kernel. If a=0, the original simultaneous eigenstates remain.
For the shifted two-dimensional oscillator in a uniform electric field, complete the square:
H′=21​(px′2​+py′2​+x′2+y′2)−2a2​,x′=x−a,y′=y.
(48)
Therefore ψ1​(x′,y′) and ψ2​(x′,y′) have the same shifted energy
E1′​=E2′​=2ℏ−2(eE)2​.​
(49)
The modified angular momentum is
L′=(x−eE)py​−ypx​=L−eEpy​.​
(50)
It is angular momentum about the displaced centre, commutes with H′, and has eigenvalues ±ℏ on the two translated states.
