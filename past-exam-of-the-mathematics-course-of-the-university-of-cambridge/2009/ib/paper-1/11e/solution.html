for every real x. To prove continuity without invoking an unproved uniform-limit theorem, fix x and ε>0. Choose N with 2⋅2−N−1<ε/2, then use continuity of fN​ to make ∣fN​(y)−fN​(x)∣<ε/2 near x. The triangle inequality then gives ∣f(y)−f(x)∣<ε. Thus f is continuous; it is the scaled Takagi function T(2x)/2.
For the general secant claim, differentiability gives g(x+h)=g(x)+g′(x)h+r(h), where ∣r(h)∣≤ε∣h∣ for sufficiently small h, with r(0)=0. Since the two endpoints straddle x,

Hence the secant limit is g′(x), including cases where an endpoint equals x. This proves secants straddling a differentiability point.
Now take the nested dyadic intervals uN​=2−N⌊2Nx⌋, vN​=uN​+2−N. They straddle x and shrink to it. For every n≥N, 2nuN​ and 2nvN​ are integers, so the corresponding summand vanishes at both endpoints. For n<N, all corners of that summand lie on the dyadic grid of mesh 2−N; on the chosen open interval it is linear with slope εn​∈{−1,1}. Therefore its total secant slope is
SN​=vN​−uN​f(vN​)−f(uN​)​=∑n=1N−1​εn​.
(16)
Refining to the nested interval preserves the slopes of all old terms and adds exactly one new term of slope +1 or −1. Thus ∣SN+1​−SN​∣=1 for every N, so SN​ cannot converge. This contradicts the secant consequence of differentiability at any x. Therefore f is nowhere differentiable. At dyadic x the intervals chosen on its right satisfy the same argument. The printed reference to a denominator 2−n is naturally read as mesh 2−n, or denominator 2n, as used here.
