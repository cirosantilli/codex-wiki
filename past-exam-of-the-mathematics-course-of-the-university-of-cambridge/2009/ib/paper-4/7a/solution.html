With consistent flux and loop orientations, Faraday's law is V=−dΦ/dt. The magnetic force on a current-carrying wire is dF=Idℓ×B.
Let z(t) be the top edge height and v=−z˙ the downward speed. While 0<z<l, only the top horizontal edge lies in the field, and the linked magnetic flux is Bwz. Therefore the induced electromotive force has magnitude Bwv, and Ohm's law gives current magnitude I=Bwv/R. Its force opposes the downward motion and has upward magnitude IBw=B2w2v/R.
