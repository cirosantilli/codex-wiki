Interpret a beneficial trade as weakly improving both participants and strictly improving at least one; otherwise the identity trade would always remain available. Put gj​=∇Uj​(yj​). For a globally strictly increasing concave function, every component of its gradient is strictly positive: concavity makes a coordinate derivative nonincreasing along that coordinate, and monotonicity makes it nonnegative. If it vanished at a finite point, it would vanish farther along the coordinate, contradicting strict increase on the whole real line.
To construct a pairwise improvement from nonparallel utility gradients, suppose two positive gradients g,h are not proportional. Strict Cauchy-Schwarz inequality gives
∥h∥2g⋅h​<g⋅h∥g∥2​.
(48)
Choose t between these bounds and put δ=g−th. Then g⋅δ>0 and h⋅δ<0. For sufficiently small ε>0, the feasible transfer yi​↦yi​+εδ, yj​↦yj​−εδ strictly improves both utilities by differentiability. This contradicts the absence of beneficial pairwise trades.
Hence all gradients are proportional. Choose v=g1​; positivity makes each proportionality scalar positive:
∇Uj​(yj​)=λj​v,λj​>0.​
(49)
Now take aj​=1/λj​​. The concave supporting-tangent inequality gives, for any other allocation (zj​) with the same total endowment,
j∑​aj​Uj​(zj​)​≤j∑​aj​Uj​(yj​)+j∑​aj​∇Uj​(yj​)⋅(zj​−yj​)=j∑​aj​Uj​(yj​)+v⋅j∑​(zj​−yj​)=j∑​aj​Uj​(yj​).​
(50)
Thus the final allocations globally maximize the social planner's weighted objective. They need not be unique when utilities are not strictly concave, but the required positive supporting weights exist.
