Put M=XTX, which is positive definite because X has full column rank. The log likelihood differs from −∥Y−Xβ∥2/(2σ2) by a term independent of β. Differentiation gives the normal equations Mβ​=XTY, hence
β​=M−1XTY,β​∼Np​(β,σ2M−1).​
(29)
The distribution follows by writing β​=β+M−1XTε and applying the linear transformation rule for a multivariate normal distribution.
Let s=σ2 and R=∥Y−Xβ​∥2. Orthogonality of the residual to the columns of X gives the exact decomposition
∥Y−Xβ∥2=R+(β−β​)TM(β−β​).
(30)
The prior is density proportional to s−1 with respect to dβds. Thus the joint posterior distribution is
π(β,s∣X,Y)∝s−n/2−1exp[−2sR+(β−β​)TM(β−β​)​].​
(31)
Completing the quadratic identifies
β∣s,X,Y∼Np​(β​,sM−1).​
(32)
The sampling distribution treats the true β as fixed and varies the data, whereas the conditional posterior fixes the observed data and distributes β. Their covariance formulas agree, but their centres and their interpretations differ.
For a new row vector x∗, let h∗=x∗M−1(x∗)T and suppose its new error is independent. With known σ2, the maximum-likelihood plug-in prediction uses mean x∗β​ and noise variance σ2, while a calibrated frequentist prediction must account for the estimator: y∗−x∗β​∼N(0,σ2(1+h∗)). If variance is estimated, the maximum-likelihood estimator is R/n, whereas the exact frequentist prediction pivot uses s=R/(n−p) and has Student t-distribution with n−p degrees of freedom.
In the Bayesian calculation, integrating β first gives y∗∣s,X,Y∼N(x∗β​,s(1+h∗)). Integrating β out of the joint posterior gives s∣X,Y∼InvGamma((n−p)/2,R/2). For ν=n−p, integration of the predictive normal against this inverse-gamma distribution has density proportional to
∫0∞​s−(ν+1)/2−1exp[−2sR+(y∗−x∗β​)2/(1+h∗)​]ds∝[1+R(1+h∗)(y∗−x∗β​)2​]−(ν+1)/2.
(33)
The substitution t=[R+(y∗−x∗β​)2/(1+h∗)]/(2s) reduces the integral to a Gamma function. Normalizing identifies the Student t-distribution, giving
y∗∣X,Y∼tn−p​(x∗β​, n−pR​(1+h∗)​),​
(34)
where the second parameter is a scale, not its variance. These Bayesian intervals coincide with the usual exact Gaussian-regression prediction intervals for this prior, although their probability interpretations differ. Posterior propriety requires R>0 and n>p; the former holds almost surely under the nondegenerate Gaussian model. Prediction of the noiseless conditional mean omits the added one in 1+h∗.
