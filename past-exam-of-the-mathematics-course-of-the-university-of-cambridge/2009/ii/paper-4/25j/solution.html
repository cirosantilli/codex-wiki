Let M be the linear subspace of L2(P) consisting of classes admitting G-measurable representatives. It is closed. Indeed, if Yn​∈M converges in L2 to Y, choose a subsequence converging almost surely: take ∥Ynj​​−Y∥22​≤2−3j and apply Markov inequality and the Borel-Cantelli lemma to errors exceeding 2−j. The pointwise limit of its G-measurable representatives, set to zero wherever the finite limit does not exist, is G-measurable and equals Y almost surely. This also works when G is not complete.
Here is a proof of the orthogonal projection construction using only completeness. Put d=infW∈M​∥X−W∥2​ and take Wn​ approaching this infimum. The parallelogram law gives
∥Wn​−Wm​∥22​=2∥X−Wn​∥22​+2∥X−Wm​∥22​−4​X−2Wn​+Wm​​​22​⟶0.
(75)
Completeness and closedness give a minimizer Y∈M. For every Z∈M, expand ∥X−Y−tZ∥22​ for real t. Its minimum at zero implies
E((X−Y)Z)=0for every Z∈M.​
(76)
This Y is unique almost surely, because the difference of two possible choices belongs to M and is orthogonal to itself. It is the conditional expectation E(X∣G), as also follows by testing indicators of events in G.
For the jointly Gaussian pair, if u>0, set
Y=ν+uv​(G−μ).​
(77)
The residual R=X−Y has mean zero and covariance Cov(R,G)=v−(v/u)u=0. Since (R,G) is jointly Gaussian, its characteristic function factors when this covariance is zero, so R and G are independent. Hence E(RZ)=E(R)E(Z)=0 for every square-integrable function Z of G; independence first proves this for bounded functions, and truncation plus Cauchy-Schwarz inequality gives the general case. This proves the Gaussian conditional expectation formula, including a zero residual variance. If u=0, G=μ almost surely and the covariance inequality gives v=0; Y=ν is the answer.
