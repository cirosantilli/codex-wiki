On (−π,0), crossing w=1 has w′=−2sinθ>0, so there is one crossing θb​∈(−π,0). There cannot be another crossing before π: on (0,π) crossings have negative derivative, while the local quadratic form at π is positive on its left. Thus rc​>0 for θb​<θ<π, and its closure is a homoclinic orbit to the origin. At θb​, rc′​=−2sinθb​>0, so departure from the origin likewise takes infinite time.
Scalar-solution comparison now gives the basin picture. Initial points inside that loop have w<wc​ at the same angle and approach r=0 before reaching angle π. Points on its boundary return to the origin along the homoclinic trajectory. Points outside the loop reach angle π with w>1 or lie outside the unit circle; they approach r=1. To check the inner exterior region quantitatively, the return map at θ=π is w↦e2π(w−I), where I=∫02π​e−u−2+2cosudu<1−e−2π. It sends every w>1 increasingly to infinity, equivalently r→1.
There are no other nonconstant periodic orbits: an orbit reaching the section with w>1 has strictly increasing successive w values, and the remaining inner trajectories tend to the origin. The origin, defined by the continuous Cartesian extension, attracts the interior of the loop but is not Lyapunov stable, since arbitrarily small lower-sector initial points outside the loop approach the unit circle. This is the homoclinic basin boundary in a sinusoidal radial flow.
