An isometry preserves the Levi-Civita connection. To justify this, transport the connection on X by dψ. It is torsion-free and preserves the metric on Y, since ψ preserves the metric and Lie brackets. These two properties characterize the Levi-Civita connection uniquely: if two such connections differ by A, the tensor a(U,V,W)=⟨A(U,V),W⟩ is symmetric in its first two arguments and antisymmetric in its last two. Cycling these symmetries yields a=−a, hence A=0. Therefore
dψ(∇UX​V)=∇dψUY​(dψV).
(73)
Applying this along γ shows that the covariant acceleration of ψ∘γ is zero, so an isometry carries affinely parametrized geodesics to geodesics.
The same identity and the definition R(U,V)W=∇U​∇V​W−∇V​∇U​W−∇[U,V]​W show that dψ intertwines the Riemann curvature tensor. If e1​,e2​ is an orthonormal tangent basis, its image is orthonormal and
KY​(ψ(p))=⟨RY​(dψe1​,dψe2​)dψe2​,dψe1​⟩=⟨RX​(e1​,e2​)e2​,e1​⟩=KX​(p).
(74)
Both proposed converses are false. Take X=Y=R2 with the Euclidean metric and ψ(x)=2x. A geodesic p+tv maps to the geodesic 2p+2tv, and both Gaussian curvatures vanish identically. Nevertheless ψ multiplies lengths by two, so is not an isometry. This one counterexample answers both unheaded continuation requests.
