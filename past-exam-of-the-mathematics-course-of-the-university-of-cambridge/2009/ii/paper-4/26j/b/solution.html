The second continuous-time Markov chain runs in the reverse direction at rate ρ, as in the right diagram. Its nonzero eigenvalues have real part −3ρ/2, and its transition probabilities tend to 1/3 for each vertex. Independence therefore gives
p(t)=∑v=A,B,C​P(Xt​=v)P(Yt​=v)⟶3(31​)2.
(81)
Thus the limit exists for every pair of starting vertices and equals 1/3​.
One can also see the limit directly without spectral multiplication. For fixed starting vertices, Xt​−Yt​ modulo three advances with each jump of either flea. Its jump count is the sum of independent Poisson processes with rates 1 and ρ, hence is Poisson with rate 1+ρ (multiply their generating functions). The formula from part (a), with t replaced by (1+ρ)t and the appropriate initial residue, gives p(t) and the same limit.
