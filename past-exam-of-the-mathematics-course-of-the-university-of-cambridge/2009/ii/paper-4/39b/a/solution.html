Interpolate through the s+1 values at tn​,…,tn+s​, and differentiate the interpolation polynomial at its newest node. In the backward coordinate τ=(t−tn+s​)/h, the Newton form is
P(τ)=∑j=0s​j!τ(τ+1)⋯(τ+j−1)​∇jyn+s​,
(140)
with the j=0 factor interpreted as one and ∇ym​=ym​−ym−1​. For j≥1, the derivative of the factor at zero is (j−1)!/j!=1/j. Thus the backward differentiation formula is
∑j=1s​j1​∇jyn+s​=hfn+s​.
(141)
If w denotes the forward-shift variable, ∇jyn+s​ corresponds to ws−j(w−1)j. Consequently the requested polynomial is
ρ(w)=j=1∑s​j1​ws−j(w−1)j=wsj=1∑s​j(1−w−1)j​.​
(142)
The first form is a polynomial valid also at w=0. The second form is convenient for relating it to the truncated series of logw. Differentiation of the degree-s interpolant has local derivative error O(hs), giving order s for smooth solutions.
