First, T is bounded precisely when supn​∣λn​∣<∞, with operator norm equal to that supremum: the upper bound follows by summing ∣λn​xn​∣2, and the coordinate unit vectors en​ give the lower bound. If the supremum is infinite, choose distinct indices with ∣λnj​​∣≥j and set xnj​​=1/j, other coordinates zero. Then x∈ℓ2 but Tx∈/ℓ2, so the displayed prescription would not even define an everywhere-defined map into ℓ2.
If T is compact but λn​→0, infinitely many indices satisfy ∣λn​∣≥ϵ>0. Along those indices,
∥Ten​−Tem​∥2=∣λn​∣2+∣λm​∣2≥2ϵ2(n=m),
(66)
contradicting compactness. Conversely, if λn​→0, truncate to TN​x=(λ1​x1​,…,λN​xN​,0,…). This is a finite-rank compact operator, and ∥T−TN​∥=supn>N​∣λn​∣→0. To see compactness directly, approximate the image of the unit ball by TN​ to within ϵ/2, and cover its bounded finite-dimensional image by finitely many ϵ/2-balls. Hence T's unit-ball image is totally bounded; its closure is compact because ℓ2 is complete.
