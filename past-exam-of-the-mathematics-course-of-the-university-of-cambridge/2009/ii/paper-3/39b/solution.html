Set θk​=kπ/(M+1) and extend the proposed vector by v0​=vM+1​=0. For vm​=imsin(mθk​), the angle-addition identity gives
vm+1​−vm−1​=im+1[sin((m+1)θk​)+sin((m−1)θk​)]=2icosθk​vm​.
(119)
Hence the Toeplitz antisymmetric tridiagonal matrix has eigenvalues
λk​=a+2ibcosM+1kπ​,1≤k≤M.​
(120)
The real sine vectors are eigenvectors of the real symmetric tridiagonal matrix with both off-diagonals one, with distinct eigenvalues 2cosθk​; thus they are orthogonal and form a basis. Multiplication by the unitary diagonal matrix diag(i,i2,…,iM) preserves orthogonality. Their squared norms are (M+1)/2, so multiplying by 2/(M+1)​ gives a common orthonormal basis for all a,b, even when some eigenvalues coincide.
Let K have superdiagonal one, subdiagonal minus one and diagonal zero. The prescribed endpoint values remove the exterior unknowns, and the scheme is
B=I−4μ​K,C=I+4μ​K,Bun+1=Cun.​
(121)
In the common orthonormal eigenbasis, the two eigenvalues are 1∓i(μ/2)cosθk​. Every eigenvalue of B is nonzero for real μ, and the amplification matrix Q=B−1C has eigenvalues
qk​=1−i(μ/2)cosθk​1+i(μ/2)cosθk​​,∣qk​∣=1.
(122)
Thus Q is a unitary matrix and ∥un+1∥2​=∥un∥2​ exactly. The same holds for the mesh-weighted norm. This matrix argument proves stability for every real μ, hence every physical μ≥0​, without a Fourier stability calculation.
The printed mesh ratio has a consistency error: advection requires μ=Δt/Δx, while the PDF prints Δt/(Δx)2. Indeed the centered spatial difference is 2Δxux​+O((Δx)3), so division of the scheme by Δt gives leading right-hand coefficient μΔx/Δt. The printed choice makes this 1/Δx rather than one. The stability conclusion is valid for the algebraic scheme with either definition, but does not fix this inconsistency. The continuous first-order advection problem also ordinarily specifies only the inflow endpoint, here x=1; zero data at both endpoints restrict admissible initial data. For example the initially boundary-zero profile sinπx develops nonzero outflow u(0,t)=sinπt before inflow changes reach that boundary. The matrix norm calculation concerns the stated two-endpoint discretization and does not establish well-posedness for arbitrary continuous initial data.
