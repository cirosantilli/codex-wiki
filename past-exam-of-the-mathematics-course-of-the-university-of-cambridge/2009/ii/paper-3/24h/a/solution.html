The Theorema Egregium states that the Gaussian curvature of a surface in R3 is determined entirely by its first fundamental form; in particular a local isometry preserves it.
Let r(u1,u2) be a regular parametrization, gij​=ri​⋅rj​, n a unit normal, and bij​=rij​⋅n the second fundamental form. Differentiating the metric and decomposing rij​ into tangent and normal parts gives the Gauss formula
rij​=Γijk​rk​+bij​n,Γijk​=21​gkℓ(∂i​gjℓ​+∂j​giℓ​−∂ℓ​gij​).
(52)
Differentiating n⋅rj​=0 gives ni​=−bi​krk​, the Weingarten formula. Compute two successive ambient derivatives of a tangent vector and subtract them. Ambient derivatives commute, so their tangential parts yield the Gauss equation
R(X,Y)Z=b(Y,Z)SX−b(X,Z)SY,
(53)
where S is the shape operator and R(X,Y)Z=∇X​∇Y​Z−∇Y​∇X​Z−∇[X,Y]​Z is the intrinsic curvature of the connection with the displayed Christoffel symbols. Taking X=∂1​,Y=∂2​,Z=∂2​ and the inner product with ∂1​ gives
⟨R(∂1​,∂2​)∂2​,∂1​⟩=b11​b22​−b122​.
(54)
But K=detS=det(b)/det(g), so
K=g11​g22​−g122​⟨R(∂1​,∂2​)∂2​,∂1​⟩​.​
(55)
Both the connection and its curvature on the right are computed from g and its derivatives alone. This proves the theorem, rather than merely using its name as a substitute for the argument.
