The equilibrium-delay theorem for a renewal process with positive holding time S, finite mean μ, and distribution function F says that the first future renewal must have the equilibrium residual-life distribution,
Fe​(t)=μ1​∫0t​(1−F(s))ds(t≥0),
(64)
independently of subsequent ordinary holding times. This is the residual lifetime seen at a stationary observation time, rather than an ordinary inter-renewal lifetime.
Here μ=3/λ and 1−F(t)=e−λt(1+λt+λ2t2/2). Thus the required initial-delay density is
fS1D​​(t)=3λ​e−λt(1+λt+2λ2t2​)1t>0​.​
(65)
Equivalently, choose with equal probability a sum of one, two, or three independent rate-λ exponential random variables. The three Erlang distributions have densities that average to the displayed density. A concrete stationary construction chooses the phase modulo three of a stationary Poisson clock uniformly; the number of exponential stages until its next third-stage renewal is then uniform on {1,2,3}.
