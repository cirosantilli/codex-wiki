Past exam of the mathematics course of the University of Cambridge / 2009 / ii / Paper 3 / 21H / b / Solution

Assume first that F is compact in the supremum norm. A finite norm-net shows that the norms of its members are bounded, so pointwise boundedness follows. Given x and ε>0, choose a finite ε/3-net g1​,…,gN​ in the closure. Their continuity gives a common neighborhood U of x where ∣gj​(y)−gj​(x)∣<ε/3 for every j. For any f∈F, choose gj​ with ∥f−gj​∥∞​<ε/3; the triangle inequality then gives ∣f(y)−f(x)∣<ε. Thus the family is equicontinuous.
Conversely, suppose pointwise boundedness and equicontinuity. For a prescribed ε>0, choose neighborhoods Ux​ where every f varies from f(x) by less than ε/3. Compactness supplies a finite subcover Ux1​​,…,UxN​​. Pointwise boundedness makes the set of vectors (f(x1​),…,f(xN​)) bounded in RN. Partition a bounding box into finitely many boxes of coordinate diameter less than ε/3, and choose one function from each occupied box. If f,g belong to the same box and y∈Uxj​​, then
∣f(y)−g(y)∣≤∣f(y)−f(xj​)∣+∣f(xj​)−g(xj​)∣+∣g(xj​)−g(y)∣<ε.
(48)
The chosen functions are therefore a finite ε-net in the supremum norm. Thus F is totally bounded. Its closure is complete and totally bounded, hence compact. To recall the last metric-space fact, successively selecting subsequences in balls of radii 2−j produces a Cauchy diagonal subsequence from every sequence; completeness supplies its limit. This proves both directions of the Arzelà-Ascoli theorem.
