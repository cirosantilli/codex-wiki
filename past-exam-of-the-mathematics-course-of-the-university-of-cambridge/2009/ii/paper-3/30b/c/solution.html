u(x)=(2π)n1​∫Rn​eix⋅ξ1+∣ξ∣2ϕ​(ξ)​dξ.​

A smooth compactly supported function is a Schwartz function, as is its Fourier transform. The multiplier (1+∣ξ∣2)−1 and all its derivatives have polynomial bounds, so their product with ϕ​ is again a Schwartz function. The inverse integral can therefore be differentiated to verify −Δu+u=ϕ classically. By part (b), its Fourier-space integrand factor ϕ​/(1+∣ξ∣2) is radial. Changing variables by any orthogonal transformation in the inverse integral gives u(Qx)=u(x), proving radiality.
This is also the unique tempered-distribution solution: the nonvanishing multiplier 1+∣ξ∣2 can be inverted on that space. Unrestricted classical solutions need not be unique, since one may add, for example, ex1​; the Fourier construction selects the rapidly decaying solution.
