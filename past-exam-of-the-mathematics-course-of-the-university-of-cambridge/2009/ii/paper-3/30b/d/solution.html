The real Lax-Milgram theorem states that if V is a Hilbert space, B:V×V→R is a continuous bilinear form with B(v,v)≥α∥v∥V2​ for some α>0, and ℓ∈V∗ is continuous, then there is a unique u∈V such that B(u,v)=ℓ(v) for all v∈V. Moreover ∥u∥V​≤∥ℓ∥/α.
Take the Sobolev space V=H01​(Ω) with norm ∥v∥V​=∥∇v∥2​. Since Ω is bounded, the Poincaré inequality gives ∥v∥2​≤CP​∥∇v∥2​, making this norm equivalent to the full Sobolev norm. For the given bounded measurable coefficient define
B(u,v)=∫Ω​(∇u⋅∇v+a(x)uv)dx,ℓ(v)=∫Ω​fvdx.

Then ∣B(u,v)∣≤(1+aˉCP2​)∥u∥V​∥v∥V​, B(v,v)≥∥v∥V2​ since a≥0, and ∣ℓ(v)∣≤CP​∥f∥2​∥v∥V​. The Lax-Milgram theorem therefore gives a unique u∈H01​(Ω) satisfying this identity, which is exactly the required weak solution. Strict positivity of the lower coefficient bound is unnecessary: the zero boundary condition and boundedness supply coercivity through the gradient term.
