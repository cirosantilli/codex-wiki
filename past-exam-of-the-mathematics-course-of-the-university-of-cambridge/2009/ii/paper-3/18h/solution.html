The Artin fixed-field theorem says that for a finite group G of distinct automorphisms of a field K, the extension K/KG is Galois extension and has degree ∣G∣. Here y=xp−x is invariant under translations because (x+a)p−(x+a)=xp−x for a∈Fp​. Hence Fp​(y)⊂KG. Also x is a root of Tp−T−y, so [K:Fp​(y)]≤p. The fixed-field theorem gives [K:KG]=p, forcing KG=Fp​(y)​. The extension is separable; explicitly the derivative of Tp−T−y is −1.
For the affine substitutions, y transforms to dy, since dp=d and ap=a. Therefore z=yp−1 is fixed by all p(p−1) substitutions. The polynomial (Tp−T)p−1−z has x as a root and degree p(p−1). Apply the same degree comparison and the Artin fixed-field theorem to obtain
KH=Fp​(z),z=(xp−x)p−1.​
(43)
Moreover [Fp​(y):Fp​(z)]=p−1, so the minimal polynomial of y is Tp−1−z​. It splits as ∏d∈Fp×​​(T−dy), and its derivative is nonzero at every root. Thus KG/KH is a Galois extension with group Fp×​, acting by y↦dy.
There is a harmless convention issue in labeling the substitutions: f(x)↦f(dx+a) composes in the reverse order to the displayed affine matrices. It is naturally a right action on functions, or a left action after inserting inverses. The set of automorphisms and all the fixed fields just computed are unchanged by that convention.
