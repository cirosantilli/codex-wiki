The induced ordering on x is a well-order. By transfinite recursion define h(t)={h(s):s∈x, s<t} for t∈x. Induction shows that each h(t) is an ordinal, that these values strictly increase, and that their range is the ordinal μ(x)=⋃t∈x​(h(t)+1). The map h is an order isomorphism from x to this ordinal. Any order isomorphism between ordinals must fix their elements successively, so this ordinal is unique.
An increasing map j:κ→λ between ordinals satisfies j(ξ)≥ξ by induction: its predecessors include j(η) for all η<ξ. Hence κ≤λ. Apply this to the inclusion x⊂y, transported through their order isomorphisms, and then to y⊂α. It follows that μ(x)≤μ(y)≤α​.
If each xn​ is an initial segment of later xm​, uniqueness makes their ordinal enumerations compatible: the enumeration of xm​ restricts to that of xn​. Their union is consequently an order isomorphism from ⋃n​xn​ to ⋃n​μ(xn​). This proves the asserted equality. Without the initial segment condition it fails: in α=ω+1, take xn​={0,…,n−1}∪{ω}. Then μ(xn​)=n+1, whose union is ω, but μ(⋃n​xn​)=ω+1.
For decreasing sets, μ(xn​) is a nonincreasing sequence of ordinals. Its set of values has a least member, attained at some n0​; all later values must equal it. Hence the sequence is eventually constant. The intersection formula nevertheless fails: take xn​={n,n+1,…}⊂ω. Every xn​ has order type ω, whereas their intersection is empty and has order type zero. Thus μ(⋂n​xn​)=0=⋂n​μ(xn​)=ω.
