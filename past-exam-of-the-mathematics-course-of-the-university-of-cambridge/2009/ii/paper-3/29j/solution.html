A standard Brownian motion Wt​ starts at zero, has continuous sample paths, and has independent increments Wt​−Ws​∼N(0,t−s) for 0≤s<t. The Black-Scholes model assumes a frictionless market permitting continuous, self-financing trading and short selling, a bank account Bt​=ert at constant interest rate, and a non-dividend-paying asset
dSt​=μSt​dt+σSt​dWt​,S0​>0,σ>0,
(74)
with constant coefficients and no arbitrage.
For an option price C(S,t), Itô's formula gives diffusion coefficient σSCS​. Holding CS​ units of the asset hedges this diffusion. The hedged portfolio has value C−SCS​ and instantaneous gain (Ct​+21​σ2S2CSS​)dt, so no arbitrage requires
Ct​+21​σ2S2CSS​+rSCS​−rC=0,C(S,T)=(S−K)+.
(75)
Equivalently, the risk-neutral measure makes e−rtSt​ a martingale and gives the solution C(S0​,0)=e−rTEQ​(ST​−K)+. Under that measure,
ST​=S0​exp((r−σ2/2)T+σT​Z),Z∼N(0,1).
(76)
Put d2​=[log(S0​/K)+(r−σ2/2)T]/(σT​) and d1​=d2​+σT​. Then PQ​(ST​>K)=Φ(d2​), and completing the square in the normal integral gives EQ​[ST​1ST​>K​]=S0​erTΦ(d1​). Therefore the Black-Scholes formula is
C0​=S0​Φ(d1​)−Ke−rTΦ(d2​).​
(77)
Here K>0; a zero strike has value S0​ directly.
For the forward-start call option, condition at the strike-fixing time t. Given Ft​, the remaining asset-price ratio has the same lognormal law over τ=T−t, independently of past increments. Thus its time-t value is
Ct​=St​[Φ(σ(r+σ2/2)τ​​)−e−rτΦ(σ(r−σ2/2)τ​​)].
(78)
Since EQ​(e−rtSt​)=S0​, conditioning once more yields
C0​=S0​[Φ(σ(r+σ2/2)T−t​​)−e−r(T−t)Φ(σ(r−σ2/2)T−t​​)].​
(79)
The strike is random before t; inserting an unknown St​ as a fixed strike at time zero would miss the conditioning step.
