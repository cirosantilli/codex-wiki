For Laplace's method, assume the phase and amplitude are smooth near the nondegenerate maximum, as required for a full expansion. Localize to a small neighbourhood of c: outside it the unique maximum and continuity make the exponential smaller by a factor e−λε. Set
ζ=sgn(t−c)2(ϕ(c)−ϕ(t))​.

Near c this is a smooth increasing coordinate, with ζ′(c)=∣ϕ′′(c)∣​. It need not be monotonic on the entire integration interval. In the localized integral put A(ζ)=F(t(ζ))dt/dζ. Extending its Gaussian integration to both infinite endpoints incurs exponentially small error. Taylor expansion of A and integration of even Gaussian moments gives
J(λ)∼eλϕ(c)2π​∑j≥0​2jj!λj+1/2A(2j)(0)​.
