Use signature (−,+,+,+), for which □=−∂t2​+Δ. With the Lorenz constraint in Proca theory, the Proca equation is (□−m2)Aa=−μ0​Ja. A static solution therefore satisfies (Δ−m2)Aa=−μ0​Ja.
For R>0 direct radial differentiation gives (Δ−m2)(e−mR/R)=0. At the origin its 1/R singularity contributes −4πδ0​: integrating the Laplacian over a small sphere gives limiting outward flux −4π, while the mass term's integral tends to zero. Thus Gm​(R)=e−mR/(4πR) obeys (Δ−m2)Gm​=−δ0​. Convolution proves the printed Yukawa potential solution
Aa(x)=4πμ0​​∫∣x−x′∣e−m∣x−x′∣​Ja(x′)d3x′.​
(90)
The integral requires suitable source decay, for example compact support. For conserved static sources, differentiating the convolution and integrating by parts gives ∂a​Aa=0 as well; its spatial divergence is the convolution with ∂i​Ji=0.
For the usual nonnegative mass parameter this is the decaying static solution, and m=0 gives the Coulomb kernel with Lorenz gauge imposed. If the arbitrary real parameter is negative, the printed kernel still gives a particular solution for compact sources, but grows at infinity; the decaying boundary condition selects e−∣m∣R/(4πR) instead. Static sources also allow homogeneous time-dependent solutions, so this construction establishes a static solution, not that every solution is static.
