Parameterize the second contour by ζ=−is, s≥0. Then
F(z)=−i∫0∞​1+is3e2izs​ds.
(33)
The exponential decays when Imz>0, uniformly on compact subsets of that half-plane. Differentiation under the integral there proves holomorphy. Thus the maximal open sector of convergence and holomorphy in this representation is α=0,β=π​. The boundary integrals themselves can converge, but that does not enlarge this open analytic sector.
For 0<argz<π/2, rotate the original contour clockwise through the fourth quadrant. On a large quarter-circle the integrand is O(R−3) times a decaying exponential, so the arc integral tends to zero. The only enclosed pole is ζ0​=e−iπ/3, and its residue is e−2zζ0​/(3ζ02​). The clockwise orientation gives, by the residue theorem,
F(z)−F(z)=−2πi3ζ02​e−2zζ0​​=−32πi​e2πi/3exp(−2ze−iπ/3).
(34)
Consequently the desired analytic continuation for π/2≤argz<π is
Fcont​(z)=F(z)−32πi​e2πi/3exp(−2ze−iπ/3).​
(35)
Both terms are holomorphic in the upper half-plane and the expression agrees with the original function in the overlap, so it is an analytic continuation. The residue term is essential: the pole lies between the two rays.
