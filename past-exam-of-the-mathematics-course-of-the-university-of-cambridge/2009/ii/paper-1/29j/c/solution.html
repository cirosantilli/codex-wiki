At the prescribed mean, w1​−b has a normal distribution with mean d=m−b and standard deviation σ. Integrating its negative part gives the expected loss
L(d,σ)=σφ(d/σ)−dΦ(−d/σ)(σ>0),
(68)
where φ,Φ are the standard normal density and distribution function. Differentiation at fixed d yields ∂L/∂σ=φ(d/σ)>0; the expression extends continuously to σ=0. Therefore minimizing expected shortfall at a fixed mean chooses exactly the minimum-variance portfolio θ∗ from part (a).
For m≥b it lies on the mean-variance efficient frontier. If “efficient frontier” has its usual undominated meaning, the printed claim needs this qualification: for m<b, the same optimizer lies on the lower minimum-variance branch and is dominated by the all-bank point (b,0). It still solves the fixed-mean problem, but is not efficient in that unconstrained comparison. If the term instead means the full fixed-mean minimum-variance boundary, the claim holds for every feasible m.
