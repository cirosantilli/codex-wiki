Let k0​=⌈(n+1)/2⌉, θ=π/(n+1), and define the high-frequency endpoint parameters
a=1−cos(k0​θ),b=1+cosθ.
(112)
They obey 1≤a≤b<2 (with equality of endpoints for a single high mode). Since ∣1−ωt∣ is convex in t and the endpoint modes are included, the exact high-frequency attenuation is
μω​=max{∣1−ωa∣,∣1−ωb∣}.
(113)
The minimax choice balances the endpoint values with opposite signs. If they were not balanced, moving ω toward their balancing point would lower the larger endpoint value. Thus
ω∗(n)​=a+b2​,μ∗(n)​=a+bb−a​.​
(114)
For odd n, a=1, giving ω∗​=2/(2+cosθ) and μ∗​=cosθ/(2+cosθ). For even n, a=1+sin(θ/2), giving
ω∗(n)​=2+cosθ+sin(θ/2)2​,μ∗(n)​=2+cosθ+sin(θ/2)cosθ−sin(θ/2)​.
(115)
These give the exact finite-grid high-frequency Jacobi smoothing optimum, including complete annihilation when there is only one high-frequency mode. Their grid-independent limits are ω∗​=2/3, μ∗​=1/3​. Choosing 2/3 damps every high mode by at most 1/3 on every grid, while its low-mode factor remains 1−O(n−2). Thus high frequencies decay geometrically much faster. The familiar 2/3,1/3 pair is the optimal uniform-in-grid smoother; it need not be the exact optimum on a particular finite sine-mode grid.
