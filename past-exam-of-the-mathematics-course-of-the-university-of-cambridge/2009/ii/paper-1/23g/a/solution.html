A rational function is a quotient r=P/Q of polynomials with Q=0. Cancel common factors. It determines a map on the Riemann sphere by assigning infinity at poles and assigning at infinity the limit in the sphere. Equivalently homogenize P,Q to the same degree d=max(degP,degQ) and set f([Z:W])=[Ph​(Z,W):Qh​(Z,W)]. They have no common projective zero: finite ones would contradict coprimality, and at infinity at least one has a nonzero leading coefficient. This also proves the map is holomorphic.
The degree of the nonconstant rational function is d. The degree of a holomorphic map of compact Riemann surfaces is the number of preimages of any target point, counted with local mapping multiplicity. For target [a:b], the homogeneous equation bPh​−aQh​=0 is a nonzero homogeneous polynomial of degree d. Over C it factors into d linear factors, with multiplicity, giving exactly d projective zeros. Locally its zero order is precisely the map's multiplicity, since the other target-coordinate denominator is nonzero. The two degrees are equal.
There is a Möbius transformation sending any ordered triple of distinct points to 0,1,∞. When all are finite one can use TA​(z)=(z−a1​)(a2​−a3​)/[(z−a3​)(a2​−a1​)]; the formulas for infinity are its limits. Define TB​ in the same way. Then TB−1​∘TA​ sends A to B and restricts to a biholomorphism X∖A→X∖B.
