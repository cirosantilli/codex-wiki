A frieze group is a discrete subgroup of Euclidean isometries whose translation subgroup is generated by one nonzero vector and has finite index. A plane crystallographic group is a discrete subgroup of Euclidean isometries with a rank-two translation lattice of finite index; equivalently its action has a compact fundamental region.
Here are three frieze groups, with explicit generators. The translation T(x,y)=(x+1,y) alone gives Z. Adding the vertical reflection in mathematics S(x,y)=(−x,y) gives Z⋊C2​, the infinite dihedral group, since STS=T−1. Instead adding the horizontal reflection in mathematics H(x,y)=(x,−y) gives Z×C2​, since HT=TH. Their translation parts are exactly ⟨T⟩ and their point parts have respectively one, two and two elements.
For plane crystallographic groups, let T1​(x,y)=(x+1,y) and T2​(x,y)=(x,y+1). These generate Z2. Adding the half-turn R2​(x,y)=(−x,−y) gives Z2⋊C2​, with inversion on both translation generators. Adding the quarter-turn R4​(x,y)=(−y,x) gives Z2⋊C4​, with R4​T1​R4−1​=T2​ and R4​T2​R4−1​=T1−1​. These are the translation, half-turn and square-rotation wallpaper groups. Integer translation vectors and a finite point part make each group a discrete subgroup; the unit square meets every orbit, establishing a cocompact group action.
For the final claim, suppose a Euclidean isometry R has order five. An orientation-reversing planar isometry has finite order only when it is a reflection in mathematics, so R is a rotation through θ=2πj/5, j=1,2,3,4, about some centre. Write A for its linear rotation part. If tv​∈G is a nonzero translation, conjugation gives Rtv​R−1=tAv​. Thus G contains translations by v and by (A+A−1)v=cv, where c=2cosθ.
For a primitive fifth root ζ, dividing 1+ζ+ζ2+ζ3+ζ4=0 by ζ2 gives c2+c−1=0. Hence c is irrational. The pigeonhole principle, applied to N+1 fractional parts of 0,c,…,Nc in N equal intervals, gives integers m,n with 0<∣nc−m∣≤1/N. Consequently G contains nonidentity translations by (nc−m)v tending to the identity. This contradicts the discrete subgroup hypothesis. There is no element of order five. This argument needs only the given nonzero translation, not a rank-two lattice.
