An elliptic function for a lattice Λ is a meromorphic function on C satisfying f(z+λ)=f(z) for all λ∈Λ. Its order is the number of poles, counted with multiplicity, in a fundamental parallelogram, equivalently the degree of its map from the torus to the Riemann sphere. Differentiation preserves the periods. If the distinct poles have orders r1​,…,rs​, the derivative has exactly the same pole locations with orders rj​+1. Therefore
n=m+s,m+1≤n≤2m.​
(66)
The bounds use 1≤s≤m, valid for a nonconstant elliptic function.
For the Weierstrass elliptic function ℘, f=℘℘′ has a single pole of order five, so its derivative has order six. For the second example choose c not a critical value of ℘ and choose a away from zero and the two points where ℘=c. Then
f(z)=℘(z−a)+℘(z)−c℘′(z)​
(67)
has a double pole at a and three simple poles: the quotient has residue −2 at zero and residue 1 at each of its two other poles. No locations overlap. Hence its order is five and its derivative has order 3+2+2+2=9, as required.
