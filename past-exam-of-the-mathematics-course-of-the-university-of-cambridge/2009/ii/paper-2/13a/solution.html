For a positive-speed travelling wave, the nutrient equation gives a′=kb/c. Integrating the bacterial equation once, with zero flux in the uncolonized limit, gives
Db′−χbaa′​=−cb.
(34)
Hence
b′=cDb​(akχb​−c2),a′=ckb​.​
(35)
In the region where b>0, divide the two equations. With γ=χ/D,
dadb​−aγ​b=−kDc2​.
(36)
The condition b(1)=0 fixes the integration constant, giving
b(a)=k(χ−D)c2​(a−aχ/D)(χ=D).​
(37)
At χ=D the limiting expression is b=−(c2/kD)aloga. These formulas are positive between zero and one. For χ<D the nutrient reaches zero at a finite rear coordinate, so the strictly positive smooth whole-line ansatz with logarithmic chemotactic sensitivity needs a separate zero-nutrient interpretation there; for χ≥D it reaches zero only as z→−∞.
For χ=2D, the nutrient equation reduces to a′=(c/D)a(1−a) and integration gives
a(z)=1+Ke−cz/D1​,b(z)=kDc2K​(1+Ke−cz/D)2e−cz/D​.​
(38)
The factor K in the bacterial numerator is required by a′=kb/c; it is absent in the printed expression unless K=1. A translation of z sets K=1, which is the version plotted below. The nutrient rises monotonically from zero behind the band to one ahead, while bacteria form a localized pulse with maximum c2/(4kD) at the nutrient half-height. Its total population is ∫bdz=c/k. The band advances into fresh nutrient, consumes it and leaves a depleted region behind; chemotaxis draws bacteria up the nutrient gradient while diffusion spreads them.
