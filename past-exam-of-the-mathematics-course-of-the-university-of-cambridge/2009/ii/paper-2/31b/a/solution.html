Characteristics beginning at ξ have x=ξ+tuI​(ξ) and carry the constant value uI​(ξ). In the sloping interval, x=t+(1−t)ξ. For 0≤t<1 the resulting single-valued solution is
u(x,t)=⎩⎨⎧​1,(1−x)/(1−t),0,​x≤t,t<x<1,x≥1.​​
(87)
It is continuous and piecewise smooth, locally Lipschitz for t<1, but not a classical C1 solution across the two derivative corners. Its weak derivatives satisfy the equation, since the function itself has no jumps there. The sloping gradient is −1/(1−t) and blows up at t=1, when those characteristics coalesce.
Thus the continuous characteristic description has maximal interval 0≤t<1. If “weak solution” is taken literally, it does not cease to exist then: the entropy continuation is a shock with left/right states 1 and 0 and Rankine-Hugoniot speed 1/2, situated at x=(t+1)/2 for t≥1. It is a global entropy weak solution, with the step at t=1 as its limiting trace. This distinguishes breakdown of the characteristic parametrization from nonexistence of a weak continuation.
