Use signed velocities in Skaro's inertial frame; the returning velocity w must be negative. For the first two rockets, the Lorentz transformation of infinitesimal separations is dx=γu​(dx′+udt′), dt=γu​(dt′+udx′/c2). Dividing with dx′/dt′=v′ gives the relativistic velocity-addition formula
v=1+uv′/c2u+v′​​.
(38)
For the third rocket, its signed velocity in the second frame is −w′′, so another application gives
w=1−vw′′/c2v−w′′​,w=1+uv′/c2−w′′(u+v′)/c2u+v′−w′′(1+uv′/c2)​​.
(39)
Since 0<v<c and 0<w′′<c, the denominator 1−vw′′/c2 is positive. The third rocket returns from positive x to Skaro if and only if w<0, hence
w′′>v=1+uv′/c2u+v′​​.
(40)
Equality leaves it stationary in Skaro's frame, so it never returns from x=2L; a smaller w′′ leaves it travelling outward.
Let O denote departure, A boarding the second rocket, B boarding the third, and C reunion with Skaro. In coordinates (t,x), their positions on the worldline are
O=(0,0),A=(L/u,L),B=(L/u+L/v,2L),C=(L/u+L/v−2L/w,0).​
(41)
Indeed, the first two coordinate durations are L/u and L/v, and the returning leg has duration −2L/w>0. Equivalently, their (ct,x) coordinates are obtained by multiplying each displayed time by c; these are the coordinates used for the spacetime diagram.
The diagram uses representative velocities satisfying the return condition; its event labels apply to the general formulas above. Skaro's elapsed time is
T=uL​+vL​−w2L​​.
(42)
On a constant-velocity leg the traveller's proper time is Δt/γV​=Δt1−V2/c2​, by time dilation. Neglecting the specified brief accelerations, Davros therefore ages by
Δτ=uL​1−c2u2​​+vL​1−c2v2​​−w2L​1−c2w2​​.​
(43)
Every contribution is positive because w<0. Each square-root factor is smaller than one, so Δτ<T: Davros returns younger than a companion who remained on Skaro. The elapsed times compare the same departure and reunion events; they cannot be obtained by applying one time-dilation factor to the whole journey, whose worldline has three different velocities.
