 ... Past exam of the mathematics course of the University of Cambridge 2009 ia Paper 4 2E a
OurBigBook.com Words: 69
Eliminate x by twice the first modular congruence minus three times the second. This gives
3y≡2(4)−3(14)=−34≡13(mod47).
(3)
The modular inverse of 3 is 16, because 3⋅16=48≡1. Hence y≡16⋅13≡20. Substitution into the second modular congruence gives 6x≡14−7(20)≡15, and 6−1≡8, so x≡8⋅15≡26. Thus
x=26+47r,y=20+47s,r,s∈Z.​
(4)
In particular, (x,y)=(26,20) works: 9(26)+12(20)=474=10(47)+4 and 6(26)+7(20)=296=6(47)+14. The elimination is reversible because 3 and 6 have modular inverses, so the displayed classes contain all solutions.
