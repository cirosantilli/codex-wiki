If A is nonempty, take a∈A. The stated implication repeatedly gives a+jt∈A for j=0,1,…,p−1. These residues are all distinct: equality of two would give (j−ℓ)t=0 in the prime field Zp​, and the nonzero t can be cancelled. They therefore exhaust the field. Thus
A+t⊆A,t=0⟹A=∅ or A=Zp​.​
(26)
Equivalently, a nonzero element generates the additive cyclic group of prime order.
Now assume 0<k<p. If A+s=A+t with s=t, translating by −s gives A=A+(t−s), contradicting the preceding result. Hence all p translates are distinct. Translation defines equivalence classes on the collection of all k-element subsets: two subsets are equivalent if one is a translate of the other, and each class contains exactly p subsets. Since there are (kp​) such subsets, their partition into classes gives
p∣(kp​)(1≤k≤p−1).​
(27)
This is the free translation action on nontrivial subsets of a prime cyclic group.
The binomial theorem now gives, in the prime field,
(a+1)p=ap+1+∑j=1p−1​(jp​)aj=ap+1.
(28)
Starting with 0p=0 and applying this identity successively to the representatives 0,1,…,p−1 proves ap=a for every residue a. For nonzero a, cancellation gives
ap=a in Zp​;ap−1=1 if a=0,​
(29)
which is Fermat's little theorem, or ap≡a(modp) for every integer a and ap−1≡1 when p∤a.
The same vanishing of intermediate binomial coefficients proves (R+S)p=Rp+Sp for any commuting elements in a ring whose ring characteristic is p, in particular for polynomials over Zp​. Applying this repeatedly to the terms of Q, and applying Fermat's little theorem to each coefficient, gives the Frobenius endomorphism identity
Q(x)p=∑j=0n​(aj​xj)p=∑j=0n​ajp​xpj,Q(x)p=j=0∑n​aj​xpj=Q(xp).​
(30)
This is an equality of formal polynomials. It does not assert xp=x as a polynomial; that latter equality holds only after evaluating at elements of Zp​.
