For any linear system Ax=c, a solution exists exactly when c belongs to the image of a linear map represented by A. If x0​ is one solution, all others are exactly
x=x0​+v,v∈kerA,
(27)
since A(x−x0​)=0, and conversely adding a kernel vector preserves the equation. Thus there are no solutions if c∈/imA, one if c∈imA and kerA={0}, and infinitely many if c∈imA and the kernel is nontrivial: every real multiple of a nonzero kernel vector gives a distinct solution. Any two solutions differ by a kernel vector. Equivalently, consistency means the augmented matrix and A have equal rank of a matrix; a consistent square linear system is unique exactly when its determinant is nonzero.
