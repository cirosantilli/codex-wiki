A subgroup K is a normal subgroup of G when gKg−1=K for every g∈G.
For a group homomorphism ϕ:G→H, its kernel contains the identity and, for x,y in the kernel, ϕ(xy−1)=ϕ(x)ϕ(y)−1=e. Hence the kernel of a group homomorphism is a subgroup. Also ϕ(gkg−1)=ϕ(g)eϕ(g)−1=e for k in the kernel; applying this to g and g−1 gives equality under conjugation. Thus the kernel is always normal.
The image contains the identity and is closed under products and inverses, so the image of a group homomorphism is always a subgroup of the target. It need not be normal: include C2​={e,(12)} into S3​. Conjugation by (123) takes (12) to (23), outside that image. Thus the image is not always normal.

Its entries are still in the same ring and its determinant is one. This proves that both sets are special linear groups under multiplication. Over F2​, minus and plus agree, and the same formula applies.
Reduction of integers modulo two preserves addition and multiplication. Therefore reducing entries preserves the determinant condition and satisfies ϕ(AB)=ϕ(A)ϕ(B), proving the stated map is a group homomorphism.
Its target acts on the three nonzero vectors of F22​. The action is faithful: a matrix fixing all three vectors fixes the two standard basis vectors and so is the identity. Thus its image, and in particular the image of ϕ, is isomorphic to a permutation subgroup of S3​. In fact SL2 over F2 as a permutation group identifies the entire image. There are 3 choices for the first nonzero column and 2 for a second independent column, hence six invertible matrices. Every invertible matrix has determinant one over F2​, so the faithful action is onto S3​.
