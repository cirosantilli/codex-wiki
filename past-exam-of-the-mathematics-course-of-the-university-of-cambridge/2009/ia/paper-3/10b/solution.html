For a twice differentiable vector potential B, commuting partial derivatives in J=∇×B gives the necessary condition
∇⋅J=0.​
(36)
The potential is not unique: for any sufficiently smooth scalar ψ, B+∇ψ has the same curl, since the curl of a gradient is zero. This is the usual gradient freedom of a vector potential. A divergence-free condition alone need not suffice globally on every domain; here only its necessity is requested.
Stokes theorem states that for an oriented surface with compatible boundary orientation,
∫S​(∇×B)⋅ndS=∮∂S​B⋅dr.
(37)
For the integrand in this problem, a convenient globally smooth vector potential is
B=31​(z3,x3,y3),∇×B=(y2,z2,x2).
(38)
The boundary of the upper ellipsoid is the ellipse z=0. The outward normal on the upper surface induces counterclockwise orientation viewed from above, so parameterize it as r(θ)=(acosθ,bsinθ,0), 0≤θ≤2π. Along this curve the first component of B is zero and dz=0, leaving
∮B⋅dr=31​∫02π​(acosθ)3bcosθdθ=3a3b​∫02π​cos4θdθ.
(39)
Since the last angular integral is 3π/4,
∫S​(y2,z2,x2)⋅dA=4πa3b​.​
(40)
The result is independent of the vertical semiaxis c. As a sign check, the field is divergence-free, so closing the cap with the downward-oriented base disc makes its flux the positive integral of x2 over that disc, which has the same value.
