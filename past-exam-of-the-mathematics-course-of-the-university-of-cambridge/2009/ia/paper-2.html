On an interval where y>0, the Bernoulli differential equation is transformed by u=y1−p:
u′=(1−p)y−py′=(1−p)(f1​u+f2​).
(8)
For f1​=1, f2​=x, put s=1−p=0. An integrating factor e−sx solves u′−su=sx, giving u=Cesx−x−s−1. Thus the positive branches are
y(x)=(Ce(1−p)x−x−1−p1​)1/(1−p),​
(9)
on intervals where the expression in parentheses is positive. The transformation omits the separate solution y≡0. For 0<p<1, the right-hand side need not be locally Lipschitz continuous at zero, so nonnegative solutions can also join zero and positive branches where the original equation permits; the formula describes every strictly positive segment.
For the autonomous case, y′=y−α2yp. Its two nonnegative equilibrium points are
y=0,y∗​=∣α∣2/(1−p).​
(10)
At the positive equilibrium point, f′(y∗​)=1−p. If p>1, f(y)>0 below y∗​ and f(y)<0 above it; hence 0 is unstable and y∗​ is asymptotically stable. Every strictly positive solution tends to y∗​ forward in time.
If 0<p<1, those signs reverse: 0 is stable from the nonnegative side and y∗​ is unstable. The stability at zero follows from the phase-line signs, not a finite derivative there. For initial value 0<y0​<y∗​, the transformed equation is u′=s(u−α2) with s>0. The solution reaches zero at
T=1−p1​logα2−y01−p​α2​,
(11)
and remains zero thereafter as a nonnegative forward solution. This is finite-time extinction in a sublinear Bernoulli equation. Initial values above y∗​ increase without bound; the sublinear negative term prevents neither that growth nor the instability of y∗​.

Use the usual assumption that the two die scores are independent random variables. A fair score has expected value 7/2 and variance 35/12. Linearity of expectation and the variance of a sum therefore give
EX=7,EY=0,VarX=VarY=635​.​
(26)
Count the ordered pairs of scores to obtain the probability mass functions
P(X=x)=366−∣x−7∣​,x=2,…,12;P(Y=y)=366−∣y∣​,y=−5,…,5.
(27)
On these attainable probability supports, the maxima occur at x=7 and y=0, each with probability 1/6; the minima occur at x=2,12 and y=−5,5, each with probability 1/36. Outside the respective probability support, the mass is zero.
They are not independent: Y=0 implies equal die scores and hence X is an even number, so P(X=7,Y=0)=0, whereas P(X=7)P(Y=0)=1/36>0. In fact Cov(X,Y)=Var(S1​)−Var(S2​)=0: this is an example of uncorrelated random variables that are dependent.

View the accessible rooms as the open cluster of bond percolation on a rooted ternary tree. Each accessible room has three forward corridors, independently open with probability 2/3, so its number of accessible children has the binomial distribution Bin(3,2/3). The counts at successive levels form a binomial branching process with offspring probability generating function
ϕ(t)=(31​+32​t)3.​
(30)
Let qN​ be the probability that the root has no open path to level N. Then q0​=0, and independence of the three descendant subtrees gives
qN+1​=(31​+32​qN​)3=ϕ(qN​).
(31)
The sequence increases to the smallest fixed point q=ϕ(q) in [0,1], the branching extinction probability. Thus qN​ is close to that solution for large N.
Connectivity is the relevant escape event: if no window is reachable, escape is impossible; if one is reachable, ordinary random wandering reaches a window eventually with probability one. Before reaching level N the guest moves within a finite connected graph, so an accessible exit cannot be avoided forever. This interpretation does not require the guest to move monotonically outward.
