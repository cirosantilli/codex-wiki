 ... Past exam of the mathematics course of the University of Cambridge 2009 ia Paper 2 10F a
OurBigBook.com Words: 159
View the accessible rooms as the open cluster of bond percolation on a rooted ternary tree. Each accessible room has three forward corridors, independently open with probability 2/3, so its number of accessible children has the binomial distribution Bin(3,2/3). The counts at successive levels form a binomial branching process with offspring probability generating function
ϕ(t)=(31​+32​t)3.​
(30)
Let qN​ be the probability that the root has no open path to level N. Then q0​=0, and independence of the three descendant subtrees gives
qN+1​=(31​+32​qN​)3=ϕ(qN​).
(31)
The sequence increases to the smallest fixed point q=ϕ(q) in [0,1], the branching extinction probability. Thus qN​ is close to that solution for large N.
Connectivity is the relevant escape event: if no window is reachable, escape is impossible; if one is reachable, ordinary random wandering reaches a window eventually with probability one. Before reaching level N the guest moves within a finite connected graph, so an accessible exit cannot be avoided forever. This interpretation does not require the guest to move monotonically outward.
