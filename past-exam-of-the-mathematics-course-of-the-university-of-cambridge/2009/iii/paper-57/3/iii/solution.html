For photon-dominated inertia, R≫1 and cs2​≃1/3. The baryon expansion and pressure terms are suppressed by 1/R. Ignoring metric driving as stipulated, the common-velocity Euler equation is θ′=−δγ​/4+σγ​. Differentiating the photon continuity equation gives
δγ′′​+31​k2δγ​−34​k2σγ​=0.​
(38)
This limit is compatible with a matter-dominated background: cold dark matter can dominate the expansion while photons dominate the inertia of the photon-baryon subsystem.
In the first nonzero quadrupole approximation, neglect σγ′​ relative to its collision damping. The shear equation gives
σγ​≃−154​τc​k2θ=−51​τc​δγ′​,τc​=κ−1.
(39)
Substitution yields the shear-only photon diffusion damping equation
δγ′′​+154​τc​k2δγ′​+31​k2δγ​=0.​
(40)
The sign of the first-derivative term is positive, so finite photon mean free time damps acoustic energy.
Put ω=k/3​ and γ(τ)=4k2τc​(τ)/15. For constant τc​, the characteristic roots are −γ/2±iω2−γ2/4​. The frequency shift begins at second order in the mean free time. To the requested order the general solution is
δγ​=e−2k2τc​(τ−τi​)/15[Acos(ω(τ−τi​))+Bsin(ω(τ−τi​))]+O(τc2​),
(41)
where the error describes the frequency expansion at fixed k and time interval. Keeping the exponential retains the accumulated damping rather than expanding away a possibly important effect.
For a general time-dependent coefficient, a first-order solution with prescribed initial conditions can also be written as
δγ​(τ)=δ0​(τ)−∫τi​τ​ωsin[ω(τ−s)]​γ(s)δ0′​(s)ds+O(τc2​),
(42)
where δ0​=Acos[ω(τ−τi​)]+Bsin[ω(τ−τi​)]. Substitution verifies that the integral solves the oscillator forced by −γδ0′​, and its value and first derivative vanish initially. This gives the general first-order result without assuming arbitrary time dependence may be absorbed exactly into an exponential.
To obtain the slowly varying subhorizon form, set δγ​=e−Gy with G′=γ/2. The exact transformed equation is
y′′+[ω2−21​γ′−41​γ2]y=0.
(43)
Dropping the quadratic term alone leaves ω2−γ′/2, so slow variation is also needed. When k≫H, kτc​≪1, and the mean free time changes on an expansion timescale, ∣γ′∣/ω2≪1. The WKB method solution has local frequency Ω=(ω2−γ′/2)1/2 and prefactor Ω−1/2. At leading acoustic order both reduce to constants; the small derivative corrections to phase and prefactor can be neglected. This slowly varying shear-damped photon oscillator therefore gives
δγ​(k,τ)≃[A(k)cos(kτ/3​)+B(k)sin(kτ/3​)]e−k2/kD2​(τ),kD−2​(τ)=152​∫τi​τ​τc​(s)ds.​
(44)
A change in the phase origin is absorbed into A,B. At zero mean free time the damping vanishes; as time passes the diffusion length grows and kD​ decreases. The coefficient is that of the question's isotropic-scattering, shear-only truncation, not the polarization-inclusive full Silk damping formula.
If ionization remains approximately constant during matter domination, ne​∝a−3, so τc​∝a2∝τ4. Away from the lower limit this gives
kD−2​≃752​ττc​(τ),kD​∝τ−5/2.

Near recombination the rapidly changing ionization fraction and eventual failure of tight coupling require the full hierarchy; the integral formula specifies the damping accumulated during the regime used here.
