A blackbody photon density perturbation gives an intrinsic temperature fluctuation Θ0​=δγ​/4. The baryon/electron bulk velocity produces a Doppler effect at last scattering. In the common-potential convention of part (ii),
θb​≃θγ​=4k23​δγ′​.
(46)
Ignoring the slowly varying damping derivative at leading acoustic order, differentiating the density cosine produces a velocity sine. Thus density and velocity acoustic sources are approximately a quarter cycle out of phase. For regular adiabatic initial conditions the density oscillation has approximately a common cosine phase across modes; this phase coherence is what makes a sequence of peaks instead of a smooth incoherent superposition.
Let D∗​=τ0​−τ∗​ be the comoving distance to last scattering and rs​(τ∗​)=∫τ∗​cs​dτ its sound horizon. In the present limit rs​≃τ∗​/3​. Density extrema have krs​≃mπ, whereas velocity extrema have krs​≃(m+1/2)π. The projection from spatial wavenumber to high angular multipoles is roughly ℓ≃kD∗​, giving an acoustic spacing
Δℓ≃πrs​D∗​​.​
(47)
The Doppler effect fills some density troughs and modifies the detailed peak shapes; its different projection prevents treating it as an identical second series of narrow peaks.
For clarity, choose the sightline to point outward from the observer and let its angle to k have cosine μ. The intrinsic-plus-emitter-Doppler source is then [δγ​/4−ikμθb​]eikμD∗​. Expanding its angular dependence gives the angular projection of density and Doppler acoustic sources
Θℓ​(k)≃4δγ​(k,τ∗​)​jℓ​(kD∗​)−kθb​(k,τ∗​)jℓ′​(kD∗​).
(48)
The second sign follows from the chosen outward sightline and vbi​=ikiθb​; it changes with the sightline convention together with the velocity projection. The jℓ​ are Spherical Bessel functions. Both sources are proportional to the primordial curvature. If Tℓ​=Θℓ​/ζ∗​, their angular spectrum in the usual continuum Fourier normalization is
Cℓ​=π2​∫0∞​k2dkPζ​(k)∣Tℓ​(k)∣2.
(49)
This formula includes the density-velocity cross term, not just a sum of independent source powers.
Photon diffusion multiplies both leading source amplitudes by e−k2/kD2​, so their power carries e−2k2/kD2​. The approximate angular damping scale is
ℓD​≃kD​(τ∗​)D∗​;high-multipole power is exponentially suppressed.​
(50)
This produces the damping tail in the Cosmic microwave background power spectrum. Finite last-scattering thickness adds further small-scale averaging. Gravity, baryon loading and later propagation alter detailed peak locations and heights, but the oscillatory peak sequence, phase-shifted velocity contribution and diffusion damping follow directly from the simplified solution obtained here.
