Put Dt​=∂t​+u⋅∇. The continuity equation and the expanded ideal magnetohydrodynamic induction equation are
Dt​ρ=−ρ∇⋅u,Dt​B=(B⋅∇)u−B∇⋅u,
(4)
so Dt​(Bi​/ρ)=(Bj​/ρ)∂j​ui​. Because B=∇×A, the induction equation permits the magnetic vector potential equation ∂t​A=u×B−∇φ, with an arbitrary scalar electric potential φ carrying the gauge choice. Using (u×∇×A)i​=uj​∂i​Aj​−uj​∂j​Ai​ yields
Dt​Ai​=∂i​(u⋅A−φ)−Aj​∂i​uj​.
(5)
Contracting with Bi​/ρ and differentiating that factor gives
Dt​(ρAi​Bi​​)​=ρBi​​∂i​(u⋅A−φ)−ρBi​Aj​​∂i​uj​+ρAi​Bj​​∂j​ui​=ρB​⋅∇(u⋅A−φ).​
(6)
The last two terms cancel by exchanging their dummy indices. Thus the requested material derivative is
Dt​(A⋅B/ρ)=ρB​⋅∇(u⋅A−φ).​
(7)
This is material transport of magnetic helicity density.
For a material volume, mass conservation gives d∫V(t)​ρhdV/dt=∫V(t)​ρDt​hdV. Apply this with h=A⋅B/ρ, and use ∇⋅B=0 and the divergence theorem:
dtdHm​​=∫V(t)​B⋅∇(u⋅A−φ)dV=∫∂V(t)​(u⋅A−φ)B⋅ndS.
(8)
If the magnetic field lines do not penetrate the boundary, B⋅n=0, so dHm​/dt=0​.
For uniqueness under a regular, single-valued gauge transformation A↦A+∇Λ,
ΔHm​=∫V​∇Λ⋅BdV=∫∂V​ΛB⋅ndS=0.
(9)
Consequently this magnetic helicity is both conserved and gauge independent. As usual, vector potentials are understood to be globally regular potentials for the same field, rather than unrelated harmonic-circulation choices on a multiply connected restricted domain.
