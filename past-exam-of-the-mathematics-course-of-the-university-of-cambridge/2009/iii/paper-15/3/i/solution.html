Independence or another ergodicity hypothesis is necessary. Read literally for an arbitrary translation-invariant site percolation measure, the assertion is false: the mixture assigning probability 1/2 to the all-open configuration and 1/2 to the all-closed configuration is translation-invariant, but the invariant event of an infinite percolation cluster has probability 1/2. We prove the zero–one law for the invariant independent law used in part (ii); the proof also works for the stated finite-range independent alternative.
Let Γ be the group of translations, or the specified group of graph automorphisms, and let A be invariant under it, up to null sets. An event determined by the states in a finite vertex set is a cylinder set. For every ε>0, approximation in the product sigma-algebra provides a cylinder set B, supported on a finite set F, with P(A△B)<ε. Choose g∈Γ so that F and gF are disjoint. Then B and gB are independent events, have equal probabilities, and P(A△gB)<ε. Hence
​P(A)−P(B∩gB)​<2ε,P(B∩gB)=P(B)2,
(17)
and
∣P(A)−P(A)2∣≤2ε+2∣P(B)−P(A)∣<4ε.
(18)
Letting ε↓0 proves the zero–one law P(A)∈{0,1}​. This is translation ergodicity of Bernoulli percolation.
For a quasi-transitive graph, the required displacement exists: put F in a ball about a vertex v; its orbit is infinite, so an automorphism can move v, and therefore that whole ball, beyond any prescribed finite neighborhood. A k-independent site percolation law means that the sigma-algebras generated by vertex sets at graph distance greater than k are independent. Move F far enough that dist(F,gF)>k and the same proof applies. Thus finite-range independence does give the zero–one law, although it will not give uniqueness.
