We use three precise inputs. The Harris-Kesten theorem says that independent bond percolation on the square lattice has percolation critical probability pc​=1/2. Expand the left-hand side as a sum over ordered marked vertices x1​,…,xk​ of the probability that 0,x1​,…,xk​ belong to one open cluster. Whenever that event occurs, a finite open tree connects the marked vertices. Delete unmarked leaves and suppress unmarked degree-two vertices. Treat a marked internal vertex as a leaf attached by a zero-length connection, and split any higher-degree branch into degree-three branch points, again with zero-length connections. This gives an abstract binary tree with k+1 labelled leaves, k−1 branch points and 2k−1 connections. Its nonempty connecting paths use disjoint sets of open bonds. Coincident marked vertices are allowed: their zero-length connections have empty witnesses.
There are (2k−3)!! such binary tree topologies. For example, the topology with two leaves is unique; inserting the next labelled leaf subdivides one of the 2k−1 edges of a topology with k+1 leaves, giving the usual double-factorial recursion. For a fixed topology and fixed positions of all its vertices, the BK inequality bounds the disjoint connection event by the product of the corresponding τ factors. Sum over all xi​ and all branch-point positions. Keep the root fixed, and successively sum a leaf away. Translation invariance gives ∑y​τ(z,y)=χ at every step. The resulting sum is χ2k−1. Summing the topologies proves the bound.
