Being constant on the two-element orbits is equivalent to f(−x,−y)=f(x,y). In characteristic zero, comparing monomial coefficients shows that precisely the terms of even total degree survive. Thus a vector space basis is
{x2ay2b, x2a+1y2b+1:a,b≥0}.​
(21)
Set u=x2, v=xy, w=y2. The two basis types are uawb and vuawb, so they prove R=k[u,v,w]. Hence R is a finitely generated algebra. It is an integral domain as a subring of the integral domain k[x,y].
The generators satisfy uw−v2=0. To prove this is the whole relation ideal, reduce any polynomial in U,V,W modulo the monic relation V2−UW to
A(U,W)+VB(U,W).
(22)
Its image in k[x,y] is A(x2,y2)+xyB(x2,y2). The first summand has both exponents even and the second both odd; their distinct monomials are linearly independent. Thus the image vanishes only when both A,B are zero. Consequently
R≅k[U,V,W]/(UW−V2),Y=V(UW−V2)⊆A3.​
(23)
This gives the requested closed embedding and the quadratic cone invariant ring presentation.
The associated morphism of algebraic varieties is explicitly
f:A2⟶Y,(x,y)⟼(x2,xy,y2).
(24)
It is surjective. For (u,v,w)∈Y, if u=0, choose x with x2=u using algebraic closedness and put y=v/x. Then y2=v2/u=w. If u=0, the relation forces v=0; take x=0 and choose y2=w. This covers every point of Y. Except over the origin, the two preimages differ by the simultaneous sign change, consistent with the orbit description.
Finally the gradient of UW−V2 is (W,−2V,U), so the Jacobian criterion makes every nonzero point smooth. At the origin, the maximal ideal modulo its square has the three independent classes of U,V,W, since the defining relation is quadratic. The tangent space there has dimension three, while Y has dimension two. Therefore
SingY={(0,0,0)}.​
(25)
