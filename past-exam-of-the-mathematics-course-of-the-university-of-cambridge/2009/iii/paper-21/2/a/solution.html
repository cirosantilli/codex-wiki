Here the degree-zero Picard group of a curve uses tensor product as its group operation. The map is the Abel map of a pointed smooth projective curve. If α(p)=α(q), then O(p−q) is trivial. By the divisor description of line bundles, there is a nonzero rational function f with principal divisor (f)=p−q.
If p=q, this function has exactly one pole, of order one. It defines a nonconstant morphism of algebraic varieties X→P1: locally at a pole one uses 1/f as the target coordinate. The degree of a morphism of curves is the total pole degree, so it is one. The map is finite, since a nonconstant map of projective integral curves has finite fibers and is proper, and a degree-one finite map to the normal projective line is an isomorphism. Algebraically, on a target affine chart the finite coordinate algebra lies inside the common function field and is integral over the integrally closed coordinate ring, so it equals that ring. Thus X≅P1 and its genus is zero, contradicting g≥1. Consequently
g≥1⟹α is injective.​

This spells out why a principal divisor with one simple zero and one simple pole cannot occur on a curve of positive genus.
If g=0, the chosen point p0​ is enough to identify X with P1, even over a nonclosed field. By the Riemann-Roch theorem, h0(O(p0​))=2, since the complementary canonical twist has degree −3 and no nonzero section. Besides constants there is a rational function with its sole pole a simple pole at p0​, giving the same degree-one isomorphism. On P1, every degree-zero divisor is principal: over an algebraically closed field write a product of powers of linear forms for its finite point coefficients, with the degree-zero condition giving the coefficient at infinity. Over a general field the same construction uses irreducible polynomial factors for closed points, weighted by their degrees. Therefore
Pic0(X)=0(g=0).​
