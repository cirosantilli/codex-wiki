We take α,β>0, as is necessary for the assertion. The nonvacuous cases have 0<α≤1/2 and 0<β≤1. Put n=∣A∣ and
K=maxb∈B​n∣A+bA∣​.
(27)
We prove a lower bound for K by transferring small sumset growth through algebraic operations on the scalars. This gives a self-contained polynomial skew-sumset expansion over a prime field proof using the permitted Ruzsa triangle inequality, Ruzsa covering lemma and Plünnecke-Ruzsa inequality.
Since ∣B∣>1, choose b0​∈B∖{0}. The bound ∣b0​A+A∣≤K∣b0​A∣ and the mixed-set Plünnecke-Ruzsa inequality give
∣rA−sA∣≤Kr+sn(r,s nonnegative integers).
(28)
In particular ∣A+A∣≤K2n and ∣3A−2A∣≤K5n. Here repeated-set sums such as 3A allow independent choices from each copy.
For a scalar t, write D(t)=∣A+tA∣/n, and put Q=A−A for the difference set. The Ruzsa covering lemma gives tA⊆Tt​+Q with ∣Tt​∣≤D(t). Indeed choose a maximal family of disjoint translates u+A, with u∈tA; counting their union bounds the family size, and maximality forces every remaining translate to meet one of them.
For any r,s, the containments A+(r+s)A⊆A+rA+sA and A+rsA⊆rTs​+A+rQ give, after covering each copy of rA,sA,
D(r+s)≤K5D(r)D(s),D(rs)≤K5D(r)2D(s).
(29)
For the second bound, specifically rQ=rA−rA⊆Tr​−Tr​+2Q, and A+2Q=3A−2A. The Ruzsa triangle inequality also gives ∣A−rA∣≤∣A+A∣∣A+rA∣/n, so D(−r)≤K2D(r). These are the closure bounds for small skew-sumset scalars.
We next prove a growth lemma for the scalar sets. For S⊆Fp​ with m=∣S∣≥2, let
R={c−da−b​:a,b,c,d∈S, c=d}.
(30)
If R=Fp​, then R+1 is not contained in R: otherwise equal cardinality would make R invariant under addition of one, which generates the entire prime field. Choose r=1+(a−b)/(c−d)∈/R. The map (x,y)↦x+ry on S2 is injective. Indeed a collision with different second coordinates would express r as a ratio of two differences from S, while equal second coordinates force equal first coordinates. Clearing the nonzero denominator puts its image inside
(c−d)S+(c−d+a−b)S⊆3SS−3SS,
(31)
so the latter set has at least m2 elements. Here SS is the product set, and 3SS−3SS denotes three independent product terms minus three others, not multiplication by the scalar three.
If R=Fp​, let Et​ count the collisions x1​+ty1​=x2​+ty2​ with all four variables in S. Summing over t gives
∑t∈Fp​​Et​=pm2+m3(m−1)≤pm2+m4.
(32)
The first term comes from equal second coordinates, which force equal first coordinates. For unequal second coordinates, each quadruple determines exactly one scalar. Some t consequently satisfies Et​≤m2+m4/p. The Cauchy-Schwarz inequality on the representation counts gives
∣S+tS∣≥Et​m4​≥1+m2/pm2​≥21​min(p,m2).
(33)
Representing t as a ratio from R and clearing its denominator embeds a dilate of this sumset in 2SS−2SS⊆3SS−3SS, padding with the same product in the positive and negative sums. We have proved the quadratic growth of a sixfold product difference set estimate
∣3SS−3SS∣≥21​min(p,∣S∣2).​
(34)
Starting from S0​=B, define Sj+1​=3Sj​Sj​−3Sj​Sj​. Choose
k=1+⌈log2​(1/β)⌉.
(35)
For p≥max(4,22/β), the growth lemma implies ∣Sk​∣≥p/2. To see the constants explicitly, put vj​=∣Sj​∣/2. Then v0​≥pβ/2 and vj+1​≥min(p/4,vj2​). Induction gives vj​≥min(p/4,pβ2j−1), and the chosen k makes the exponent at least one.
Every t∈S0​ has D(t)≤K. If D(t)≤KLj​ on Sj​, the closure bounds show that each product has exponent at most 3Lj​+5, its negative at most 3Lj​+7, and a sum of six such terms has exponent at most 18Lj​+67. Thus, with
L0​=1,Lj+1​=18Lj​+67,
(36)
we have ∣A+tA∣≤KLk​n for every t∈Sk​. The integer Lk​ depends only on β.
Now apply the collision count to A instead of S. Summing over all scalars gives at most pn2+n4. Since ∣Sk​∣≥p/2, some t∈Sk​ has collision count at most 2n2+2n4/p. The Cauchy-Schwarz inequality therefore gives
KLk​n≥∣A+tA∣≥2+2n2/pn2​≥41​min(n2,p).
(37)
It follows that
KLk​≥41​min(n,p/n)≥41​pα.
(38)
For p≥42/α this gives K≥pα/(2Lk​)≥nα/(2Lk​), proving the desired positive exponent for all sufficiently large primes.
For completeness, the same conclusion with a possibly smaller exponent holds for the remaining primes. Choose an integer
P0​≥max(4,22/β,42/α),cα,β​=min(2Lk​α​,logP0​log(1+1/P0​)​)>0.
(39)
If p<P0​, choose any nonzero b∈B. We have 1<n<p. If ∣A+bA∣=n, all its translates A+u for u∈bA would coincide. Two distinct such u would make A invariant under a nonzero translation, forcing A=Fp​, a contradiction. Thus ∣A+bA∣≥n+1≥n1+cα,β​ by the definition of the second exponent. Together with the large-prime case this proves
∃b∈B: ∣A+bA∣≥∣A∣1+cα,β​.​
(40)
Positivity of the parameters cannot be dropped: at α=0, taking A=Fp​ prevents any expansion; at β=0, the allowed set B={0} prevents expansion of any proper A with more than one element. No external sum-product theorem was invoked: the scalar growth and transfer arguments were proved above.
