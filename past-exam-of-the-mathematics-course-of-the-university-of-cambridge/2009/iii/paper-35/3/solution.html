For NP-completeness, use the decision version with an objective threshold. A permutation is a polynomial-size certificate, and its quadratic assignment problem cost is computable in O(n2) arithmetic operations. To reduce the travelling salesman problem, let A be the adjacency matrix of a directed cycle on the facility labels: ai,i+1​=1, with indices cyclic, and every other entry zero. Let B be the matrix of city-to-city distances. Then
f(π)=∑i=1n​bπ(i),π(i+1)​,π(n+1)=π(1),
(9)
which is exactly the tour length. Every tour arises from such a permutation. Hence the decision QAP is NP-complete, and finding its optimal value is NP-hard. No factor of two appears in this directed-cycle construction, even when the distance matrix is symmetric.
Fix any complete assignment π. Its restriction to the facilities other than i is an admissible bijection in the definition of ℓi,π(i)​, so
∑j=i​aij​bπ(i),π(j)​≥ℓi,π(i)​.
(10)
Summing this inequality over i gives f(π)≥∑i​ℓi,π(i)​. Restricting to Πk​ and minimizing proves
π∈Πk​min​f(π)≥g(Πk​).​
(11)
This is the Gilmore-Lawler bound. Its row minima may come from mutually incompatible assignments, so equality is not automatic. To find g(Πk​), fix facility one at location k, delete that row and column from L, and solve the residual assignment problem using the Hungarian algorithm, adding ℓ1k​ to its value.
For the supplied instance, the two residual assignment sums at each initial branch are
g(Π1​)g(Π2​)g(Π3​)​=31+min(26+37,20+48)=94,=38+min(22+37,20+41)=97,=29+min(22+48,26+41)=96.​
(12)
Use branch and bound to explore Π1​, the branch with smallest bound. Its minimizing linear assignment is π=(1,2,3). Evaluating the true ordered-pair objective gives
f(1,2,3)=2(2⋅5+7⋅3+4⋅4)=94.
(13)
This supplies an incumbent of value 94 and meets the lower bound of Π1​. Alternatively, after branching on facility two, the other leaf (1,3,2) has row-sum bound 31+20+48=99 and is pruned. The initial bounds 97 and 96 prune Π2​ and Π3​ as well, since neither can improve the incumbent. Thus
OPT=94,π=(1,2,3) is optimal.​
(14)
The factor two in this numerical evaluation is necessary because both matrices are symmetric and the objective counts both directions of each unordered pair.
