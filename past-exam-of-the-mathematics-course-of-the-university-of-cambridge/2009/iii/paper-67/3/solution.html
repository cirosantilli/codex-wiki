Let the layer depth be d, its imposed temperature drop ΔT, and its dimensional vertical field B0​z. Use length d, thermal time d2/κ, velocity κ/d, temperature ΔT and field B0​ as units. Write the total dimensionless field as z+b, and temperature as the conductive profile plus θ. The Boussinesq equations coupled to the resistive induction equation are
∂t​u+u⋅∇u∂t​θ+u⋅∇θ∂t​b∇⋅u​=−∇p+σ∇2u+σRθz+σQ(∇×b)×(z+b),=w+∇2θ,=∇×[u×(z+b)]+ζ∇2b,=∇⋅b=0.​
(27)
The dimensionless parameters in this normalization are
R=νκgαT​ΔTd3​,σ=κν​,ζ=κη​,Q=μ0​ρνκB02​d2​.​
(28)
Here αT​ is the thermal expansion coefficient, ν is kinematic viscosity, κ is thermal diffusivity, and η is magnetic diffusivity. The first parameter is the Rayleigh number and σ is the Prandtl number.
The displayed dispersion relation fixes the magnetic normalization: its Q must be the thermal-diffusion magnetic-field parameter above. The conventional Chandrasekhar number is instead QCh​=B02​d2/(μ0​ρνη), so Q=ζQCh​. If Q is reserved for that conventional definition, the magnetic term in the printed polynomial requires an extra factor ζ. We keep Q for the printed polynomial throughout the following calculation.
Linearize about the conductive state. Absorbing linear magnetic pressure into p, the magnetic force becomes σQ∂z​b. The pressure-eliminated vertical momentum equation is
(∂t​−σ∇2)∇2w=σR∇h2​θ+σQ∂z​∇2bz​.
(29)
The normal mode implicit in the given relation has w,θ∝sinπz and bz​∝cosπz, as for stress-free, fixed-temperature boundaries with the corresponding vertical-field magnetic conditions. Put x=k2, p0​=π2 and m=x+p0​=β2. Taking growth proportional to est+ik⋅xh​ gives the amplitude equations
(s+m)Θ=W,(s+ζm)C=πW,m(s+σm)W=σRxΘ−σQmπC.
(30)
Their determinant is
m(s+m)(s+σm)(s+ζm)+σQmp0​(s+m)−Rσx(s+ζm)=0.​
(31)
This is the stated vertical-field magnetoconvection dispersion relation after accounting for its field-strength convention. The determinant derivation remains valid even at values where an intermediate division by s+m or s+ζm would not be allowed.
For steady marginality set s=0. One obtains
Rs​(x)=xm3+(Qp0​/ζ)m​.​
(32)
For oscillatory marginality write s=iω with ω=0. Dividing the polynomial by m gives a real cubic s3+As2+Bs+C0​, where
A=m(1+σ+ζ),B=m2(σ+ζ+σζ)+σQp0​−mRσx​,
(33)
C0​=σζm3+σQp0​m−Rσζx.
(34)
Its real and imaginary parts imply ω2=B and C0​=AB. Solving for the oscillatory convection threshold gives
Ro​(x)=σ(σ+ζ)(1+ζ)​xm3​+1+σσ+ζ​xQp0​m​,​
(35)
and
ω2=1+σσ(1−ζ)​Qp0​−ζ2m2.​
(36)
A physical oscillatory branch therefore requires 0<ζ<1 and positive frequency squared. In particular an algebraic continuation of Ro​ into ω2<0 is not an oscillatory onset.
The steady-Hopf merger in vertical-field magnetoconvection occurs at zero frequency:
Qp0​=σ(1−ζ)ζ2(1+σ)​m2.​
(37)
Substitution verifies Rs​=Ro​. Equivalently the merged horizontal wavenumber satisfies
km2​=[ζ2(1+σ)σ(1−ζ)Qp0​​]1/2−p0​.
(38)
A finite-wavenumber intersection exists only when this expression is positive. At the intersection the growth polynomial has a double zero root: the nonzero-frequency neutral branch ends there.
For the requested merger at a magnetoconvection neutral-curve minimum, first note that a curve of the form R=[A∗​m3+C∗​m]/x has derivative zero exactly when
A∗​m2(2x−p0​)=C∗​p0​.
(39)
Its unique positive minimum has x>p0​/2, because the left side increases there from zero to infinity. At this minimum Rc​=2A∗​m3/p0​.
Merger at the steady minimum. For Rs​, the stationarity condition is ms2​(2xs​−p0​)=Qp02​/ζ. Combining it with the merger equation gives
xs​=2σ(1−ζ)p0​(σ+ζ)​,Qs​=4σ3(1−ζ)3p0​ζ2(1+σ)(3σ−2σζ+ζ)2​.​
(40)
The corresponding critical Rayleigh number is Rs,c​=2(xs​+p0​)3/p0​. For fixed positive σ and large Q, set ζ=1−δs​ and retain the leading powers. Then
δs​∼σ1+σ​(4Qπ2​)1/3,ks​∼(2Qπ4​)1/6,Rs,c​∼π2Q.​
(41)
More generally, before taking ζ→1, the steady minimum obeys ks2​∼[Qπ4/(2ζ)]1/3.
Merger at the oscillatory minimum. Define Ao​=(σ+ζ)(1+ζ)/σ. The stationarity condition for Ro​ is
mo2​(2xo​−p0​)=(1+σ)(1+ζ)σQp02​​.
(42)
Combining it with the zero-frequency relation instead gives
xo​=2(1−ζ2)p0​​,Qo​=4σ(1−ζ)3(1+ζ)2p0​ζ2(1+σ)(3−2ζ2)2​.​
(43)
Here Ro,c​=2Ao​(xo​+p0​)3/p0​. For fixed σ>0 and Q≫1, setting ζ=1−δo​ gives
δo​∼[16σQ(1+σ)π2​]1/3,ko​∼[4(1+σ)σQπ4​]1/6,Ro,c​∼π2Q.​
(44)
These two merger conditions are distinct parameter surfaces, not simultaneous assertions about the same minimum. Both have the strong-field wavenumber selection in magnetoconvection scaling k=O(Q1/6).
If the first subleading Rayleigh corrections are desired, let Xs​=[Qp02​/(2ζ)]1/3 and Xo​=[σQp02​/(2(1+σ)(1+ζ))]1/3. With parameters bounded in the above regime,
Rs,c​=ζQp0​​+3Xs2​+O(Q1/3),Ro,c​=1+σσ+ζ​Qp0​+3Ao​Xo2​+O(Q1/3).
(45)
These retain the O(Q2/3) corrections, including the effect of the O(Q−1/3) diffusivity deficit at a merger.
