Write m0​=μ0​ρ and treat the imposed mean magnetic field as uniform on the forcing scale. In first-order smoothing, retain terms linear in the fluctuating fields in their equations, while keeping their quadratic correlation when calculating the mean-field electromotive force. The inertial fluctuation, nonlinear induction term and quadratic fluctuating Lorentz force density are neglected in the steady response. The linear magnetic force is
m0​1​(∇×b)×B=m0​1​(B⋅∇)b−∇m0​B⋅b​.
(15)
Absorb the gradient into the total pressure. The resulting equations are
0=−ρ−1∇P+m0−1​(B⋅∇)b+ν∇2u+f,0=(B⋅∇)u+η∇2b+g,
(16)
with ∇⋅u=∇⋅b=0. Taking the divergence of the first equation gives ∇2P=0 for solenoidal forcing. In a periodic or homogeneous Fourier calculation its nonconstant part therefore vanishes.
For each Fourier mode with wavevector q, ∣q∣=k, define ℓ=B⋅q and
D=νηk4+m0​ℓ2​>0.
(17)
Solving the two coupled linear equations gives the monochromatic coupled magnetic and velocity response
u=Dηk2f+iℓg​/m0​​,b=Dνk2g​+iℓf​.​
(18)
The magnetic coupling suppresses modes with large projection of their wavevector along the imposed field.
To calculate the mean-field electromotive force, group each pair of opposite wavevectors into a real mode and average its spatial phase. Put n=q/k, and define its forcing correlations
Cq​=⟨fq​×gq​⟩,hf​(q)=⟨fq​⋅∇×fq​⟩,hg​(q)=⟨gq​⋅∇×gq​⟩.
(19)
For any solenoidal real mode v=acos(q⋅x)+csin(q⋅x), its two amplitude vectors are perpendicular to q. Direct multiplication gives
⟨v×(B⋅∇)v⟩=−n(n⋅B)⟨v⋅∇×v⟩.
(20)
Also, integration by parts gives ⟨(Lg)×(Lf)⟩=−ℓ2⟨f×g⟩, where L=B⋅∇. Expanding the response products therefore yields
Eq​=D2νηk4−ℓ2/m0​​Cq​+D2k2​n(n⋅B)(m0​ν​hg​(q)−ηhf​(q)).​
(21)
Summing over independent real modes, or integrating over their angular spectrum, is the required general answer for ⟨u×b⟩.
At zero field, E(0)=∑q​Cq​/(νηk4). Thus the stated zero-field condition removes the unweighted sum of the cross correlations. For anisotropic correlated forcing it does not necessarily remove their differently weighted sum at nonzero field. The displayed general formula retains that allowed contribution. It disappears if cross forcing is absent mode by mode, or under the jointly isotropic statistics assumed for the final part.
The helicity content can also be expressed directly using the actual fluctuating fields. Define their kinetic helicity Hu​(q)=⟨uq​⋅∇×uq​⟩ and current helicity Hb​(q)=⟨bq​⋅∇×bq​⟩. Substitute f=νk2u−Lb/m0​ and g=ηk2b−Lu into their cross correlation. This gives the exact identity
(νηk4−m0​ℓ2​)Eq​−Cq​=k2n(n⋅B)(m0​η​Hb​(q)−νHu​(q)).
(22)
It shows the competing kinetic and magnetic contributions without introducing a spurious singularity when the factor on the left vanishes. For a monochromatic field, ab​=∇×b/k2 is a magnetic vector potential, so Hb​=k2⟨ab​⋅b⟩: current helicity is k2 times magnetic helicity. In the isotropic weak-field limit, with zero-field fluctuations u0​,b0​, the scalar alpha effect is
α(0)=−3ηk2⟨u0​⋅∇×u0​⟩​+3νm0​k2⟨b0​⋅∇×b0​⟩​.
(23)
This identifies both signs and their different viscous and magnetic relaxation times.
For jointly isotropic forcing let hf​,hg​ now denote the total forcing helicities, and set Sh​=νhg​/m0​−ηhf​. The cross-forcing contribution is even in B, whereas isotropy requires any vector depending only on B to be parallel to it and odd under reversal. Hence that contribution vanishes. A natural symmetric alpha tensor is
αijnat​=k2Sh​⟨[νηk4+k2∣B∣2μ2/m0​]2ni​nj​​⟩n​,μ=n⋅B.
(24)
Only its longitudinal coefficient contributes to E=α∥​B. Introduce h=∣B∣/[km0​νη​] and A0​=νηk4. Angular integration gives
α∥​=A02​k2Sh​​∫01​(1+h2μ2)2μ2dμ​=2A02​h3k2Sh​​(arctanh−1+h2h​).
(25)
Therefore the strong-field quenching of an isotropic electromotive force is
α∥​∼4A02​h3πk2Sh​​∝∣B∣−3,∣E∣∝∣B∣−2,​
(26)
provided Sh​=0. If the two helicity contributions cancel exactly, this electromotive force vanishes.
There is a tensor qualification to the printed scaling claim. The natural transverse coefficient instead contains 21​∫01​(1−μ2)(1+h2μ2)−2dμ∼π/(8h) and scales as ∣B∣−1. The relation Ei​=αij​Bj​ by itself leaves transverse components undetermined. One may choose the effective representative αij​=α∥​δij​, for which the requested ∣B∣−3 scaling holds. It is the observable longitudinal response that is fixed, rather than every component of a unique field-dependent tensor.
