Use left Grassmann derivatives and define the supersymmetric covariant derivatives D±​=∂θ±​−iθ±∂±​. Multiplication in the indicated order gives exactly the printed differential operator:
iD−​D+​=i∂θ−​∂θ+​+θ−∂θ+​∂−​−θ+∂θ−​∂+​−iθ−θ+∂−​∂+​.
(15)
Thus the equation is iD−​D+​Φ=W(Φ) in two-dimensional N=(1,1) superspace.
Under a Lorentz boost, take x±↦e±ωx± and θ±↦e±ω/2θ±. The derivatives ∂±​ have boost weights ∓1, and D±​ have weights ∓1/2. Consequently D−​D+​ has weight zero, and is a Lorentz scalar. Both Φ and the ordinary function W(Φ) are scalar superfields, so the equation is Lorentz invariant.
For supersymmetry, {∂θ​,θ}=1 and anticommutation of distinct odd coordinates give
{Ds​,Qt​}=0(s,t∈{+,−}),Q±2​=i∂±​,{Q+​,Q−​}=0.

δ(iD−​D+​Φ−W(Φ))=∑t=±​ϵtQt​(iD−​D+​Φ−W(Φ)).

Therefore a solution is mapped to a solution, proving supersymmetry invariance.
To compute the supersymmetric Liouville equation, order the odd coordinates as θ−θ+ and anticommute the fermionic component fields with both coordinates. In particular, ∂θ+​(θ−θ+)=−θ−. Direct application of the operator gives
iD−​D+​Φ=F+iθ−∂−​ψ−​−iθ+∂+​ψ+​−iθ−θ+∂−​∂+​ϕ.

Let N=Φ−ϕ. Only the two products of its degree-one terms survive in N2; for example, (iθ−ψ+​)(iθ+ψ−​)=θ−θ+ψ+​ψ−​. Hence N2=2θ−θ+ψ+​ψ−​ and N3=0. The exponential function expands exactly as
eΦ=eϕ[1+iθ−ψ+​+iθ+ψ−​+θ−θ+(iF+ψ+​ψ−​)].
(19)

∂−​ψ−​=eϕψ+​,∂+​ψ+​=−eϕψ−​,∂−​∂+​ϕ=−e2ϕ+ieϕψ+​ψ−​.​
