At a fixed time, the complete spatial derivative is
dxdS​=(∂x∂S​)h​+∂h∂S​hx​.​
(15)
The first term describes geometric variation along the channel at a fixed surface elevation. The second describes the extra wetted area produced by a varying water level; Sh​ is the surface width.
Assume a fixed channel, negligible lateral inflow, uniform cross-sectional velocity, incompressible water and the hydrostatic long-wave approximation. Integrating volume conservation over a short channel segment gives
St​+(Su)x​=0,Sh​(ht​+uhx​)+Sux​+uSx​=0.​
(16)
Inviscid horizontal momentum, with h measured from the fixed datum, is ut​+uux​+ghx​=0. The coefficient matrix acting on (hx​,ux​) has eigenvalues
dtdx​=u±c,c2=Sh​gS​.​
(17)
These are the variable-section channel characteristics. Put D±​=∂t​+(u±c)∂x​. Adding the corresponding linear combinations of the two equations gives
cg​D±​h±D±​u=−Suc​Sx​.​
(18)
The opposite signs in the two combinations cancel both derivative terms in the source calculation.
If c=c(h), then D±​c=ch​D±​h and dc2/dh=2cch​. Taking ξ=t along a characteristic gives the requested form
dc2/dh2g​dξdc​±dξdu​=−Suc​(∂x∂S​)h​.​
(19)
A sufficient geometry assumption is S(x,h)=b(x)A(h), for which S/Sh​=A/A′ is independent of x at fixed height.
For a completely general S(x,h), the printed form omits an explicit wave-speed geometry term. The correct conversion is instead
∂h​(c2)2g​[D±​c−(u±c)(∂x​c)h​]±D±​u=−Suc​Sx​.
(20)
For example, a lake at rest with constant h but position-dependent c has D±​c=±ccx​. The printed left side is then nonzero while its right side is zero. The h-based compatibility equation remains valid, including when ∂h​(c2)=0 makes the speed-based expression singular.
