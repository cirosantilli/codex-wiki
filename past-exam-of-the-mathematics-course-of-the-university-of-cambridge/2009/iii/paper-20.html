Hj​(W;Z)=⎩⎨⎧​Z,Z2,0,​j=0,2,j=1,j≥3.​​

There are no one-handles, so attaching the three two-handles to a four-ball cannot create a fundamental group generator: π1​(W)=1. The handle chain complex has C2​=Z3, C1​=0 and C0​=Z, giving
H0​(W)=Z,H2​(W)=Z3,Hj​(W)=0 (j=0,2).​
(5)
To calculate the boundary, orient the two long components down their central strands, and orient the small component clockwise. The six mutual crossings of the long components have negative sign, giving linking number −3. Each long component has two positive crossings with the small component, giving linking number 1. The surgery linking matrix, in the order of framings 4,3,2, is consequently
Q=​4−31​−331​112​​,detQ=−7.
(6)
Changing a component orientation changes the corresponding row and column signs, and does not change the resulting homology. The long exact sequence of a pair and Poincare-Lefschetz duality identify H2​(∂W)=kerQ and H1​(∂W)=cokerQ. The determinant is nonzero, so the kernel vanishes. The gcd of matrix entries is one; the two-by-two minors include −6 and 7, so their gcd is also one. The Smith normal form therefore has diagonal 1,1,7, and
Hj​(∂W;Z)=⎩⎨⎧​Z,Z/7,0,​j=0,3,j=1,j=2 or j≥4.​​
(7)
In particular the sign of the braid crossings matters: replacing −3 by +3 while leaving both meridian linkings positive would represent a different link and would give the wrong determinant.

Start with a disk in the zero-handle and attach two untwisted bands passing through the two one-handles. The four passages around its boundary occur in the order x,y,x−1,y−1, giving an embedded once-punctured torus whose boundary is the attaching circle of the two-handle. Push its interior into the existing four-dimensional handlebody, keeping its boundary on the attaching locus, and cap it with the core disk of a handle. The matching product framing makes the union smooth after rounding its corner. This gives an embedded closed torus T⊂W.
The torus uses the core of the two-handle exactly once. Equivalently, it intersects that handle's relative co-core disk transversely in one point. Its cellular two-chain is therefore the generator of C2​=Z, which is a cycle and is not a boundary because there are no three-cells. Thus
[T] generates H2​(W)≅Z,T⋅T=0.​
(8)
This is the zero section in the commutator handlebody of the torus T2×D2.

For the shaded bigon, a holomorphic strip is a conformal parametrization of its interior. Send its two corners to the two ends of R×[0,1]. The Riemann map is unique up to translation, so quotienting the moduli space by translations leaves a single point. The embedded strip is regular, and a coherent orientation assigns it sign +1 or −1. Thus the signed count is ±1. The other three small bigons have the same argument.
For the shaded rectangle, a disk in Sym2(Σ) is represented by a two-sheeted surface over the strip, with its projection to Σ covering the rectangle once. There is one simple branch point. The four alternating boundary arcs and the conformal modulus of the rectangular region determine the branched cover up to strip translation: cutting along an arc from the branch point produces two simply connected strips, whose conformal maps are fixed by their marked corners, and gluing fixes the relative translation. The index calculation in part (ii) gives a zero-dimensional quotient; regularity of this embedded empty rectangle makes its one solution an isolated regular point. Hence its signed count is also ±1. The same construction applies to the lower rectangle. This is the empty bigon and rectangle counts in Heegaard Floer homology calculation, including the geometric reason for the counts.
The listed bigons and rectangles give all positive index-one domains in these diagrams. One can check this by following the alpha and beta boundary arcs: the four two-corner regions in Figure 2a and the two empty four-corner regions in Figure 2b are the possibilities with convex corners; a domain reaching around a handle has an additional corner or a negative region coefficient. There is no nonzero basepoint-normalized periodic domain, because the displayed homology presentations have nonzero determinant. Adding the whole surface increases the Maslov index by two, so does not give another index-one representative. Thus no extra chain differential term is hidden in the calculations below.

Only finitely many zero-surgery structures contribute, by the genus bound on a capped Seifert surface, so the middle direct sum causes no difficulty. The infinite sums of trace maps do require completion. The printed minus notation should be understood in this completed sense; with the polynomial version, a sum over every extension need not even have values in Z[U]. The two ordinary surgery traces give Ak​ and Ck​. For X0​:S3→K0​, H2(X0​)=Z, and its Spin-c structures qi​ have determinant evaluations 2i on the capped Seifert surface. c1​(s)=c1​(s)+∑j=1r​PD(Ej​).
(38)
Since Ej2​=−1, its exceptional evaluations are −1, so the Blowup formula for the mixed Heegaard Floer invariant has no extra power of U. Thus ΦM,s​=0, and
⟨c1​(s),[Σ]⟩=⟨c1​(s),[Σ]⟩+r.
(39)
This calculation is why the self-intersection appears with a plus sign.
It remains to bound the Chern pairing for a square-zero surface S. Its oriented normal bundle has Euler number zero and is trivial, so ∂νS=S×S1. Choose an embedded positive-square surface T disjoint from S, with positive direction left in its complement, and cut along N=∂νT. To see why the choice is possible, impose orthogonality to [S] in the intersection form. A nonzero isotropic class uses at most one positive direction; b2+​≥3 therefore leaves at least two. Choose an integral positive-square class in that complement, represent it by a surface, and remove algebraically cancelling intersections with S by tubing along S. This alters genus but not the class. The neighborhood of T supplies one positive direction and the complement supplies another. Since its normal Euler number is nonzero, H1(νT)→H1(N) is onto, proving that the cut is admissible.
Put the outgoing puncture inside νS, so that the plus Heegaard Floer cobordism map on the side containing S passes through the Floer group of ∂νS. On the chain level this means splitting the cobordism along that boundary and composing its handle maps; with the fixed determinant class, every intermediate generator has restriction t=s∣S×S1​. If the Chern pairing exceeded 2g(S)−2, the given completed minus group would be zero. As in Question 3, this is the completed convention: for a non-torsion structure, ordinary infinity may retain (1−UN)-torsion, whereas 1−UN is invertible after completion. Such a pairing is positive for g≥1, so t is non-torsion and the completed infinity group HF∞(S×S1,t) is zero as well. The completed fundamental long exact sequence, whose plus group is the usual plus theory, then makes that plus group zero. The Heegaard Floer cobordism map, and consequently the Mixed Heegaard Floer invariant, would vanish, contradicting nonvanishing. Therefore
⟨c1​(s),[Σ]⟩≤2g(Σ)−2.

⟨c1​(s),[Σ]⟩+Σ⋅Σ≤2g(Σ)−2.​
(41)
Reversing the orientation of the surface gives the usual absolute-value form of the Adjunction inequality for the mixed Heegaard Floer invariant.
The printed assumptions omit the positive-genus qualification. With the actual Floer groups, the asserted three-dimensional vanishing is false for S=S2: the torsion structure on S2×S1 has two nonzero minus towers, although its pairing is 0>−2. Correspondingly, a small sphere embedded in a ball of a K3 surface has [S]=0, S2=0 and Chern pairing zero, while the canonical Mixed Heegaard Floer invariant is nonzero; the desired inequality would read 0≤−2. Thus the displayed proof establishes the intended positive-genus statement. If the supplied vanishing is instead taken literally as a formal additional axiom for every genus, the same factorization would force the inequality even for a sphere, but that axiom contradicts the actual S2×S1 computation.

For a rational homology three-sphere and any Spin-c structure, the infinity version of Heegaard Floer homology is
HF∞(Y,s)≅Z[U,U−1],degU=−2.​
(42)
It is one Laurent tower, with one integral generator in each degree of a fixed parity and none in the opposite parity. Its absolute rational grading has an overall offset determined by (Y,s).
To compute the Euler characteristic of the hat group, tensor with Q, which preserves integral ranks. Put V=HF−(Y,s;Q). It is a finitely generated graded Q[U] module. Localizing at U gives HF∞, so the structure theorem for finitely generated modules over a principal ideal domain gives one free summand and finitely many U-primary torsion summands. The torsion is U-primary because it vanishes after localization, or directly because the reduced graded group is supported in finitely many degrees.
The exact sequence of Heegaard Floer chain complexes given by multiplication by U and passage to the hat quotient identifies the hat group, as a parity-graded vector space, with an extension of V/UV by ker(U:V→V) with the latter parity reversed. The extra absolute shift by two between CF−/UCF− and CF is even, so it does not affect this argument. Thus
χ(HF)=χ(V/UV)−χ(kerU).
(43)
The free summand contributes one vector to V/UV and none to kerU. A torsion summand Q[U]/Ur, whose top generator has parity ϵ, contributes one vector of parity ϵ to the quotient and one of the same parity to the kernel, since Ur−1 changes degree by an even number. Their contributions cancel. Only the single free summand remains, proving
χ(HF(Y,s))=(−1)ϵ∈{1,−1}.​
(44)
Integer torsion does not enter an Euler characteristic defined by ranks.

Remove two disjoint four-balls from M, giving W:S3→S3. An Admissible cut for a Heegaard Floer mixed map is a separating three-manifold N with
W=W1​∪N​W2​,b2+​(W1​),b2+​(W2​)>0,δH1(N;Z)=0⊂H2(W;Z).
(48)
The last condition is the Mayer-Vietoris connecting-map condition. It makes a Spin-c structure on W uniquely determined by its restrictions to the two pieces, avoiding an uncontrolled sum of extensions in the composition law. Such cuts exist when b2+​(M)>1: represent an integral positive-square class by an embedded surface whose tubular neighborhood carries one positive direction and whose complement retains another. One can construct an embedded representative by resolving intersections of a representative cycle; the genus may increase without changing its class or square. The nonzero normal Euler number makes the restriction H1(νT)→H1(∂νT) surjective, so the connecting-map condition holds. Puncture on either side of the cut.
Write t=s∣N​ and si​=s∣Wi​​. Both infinity Heegaard Floer cobordism maps vanish by positivity. Naturality with respect to the exact sequence
⋯→HF−(N,t)ι​HF∞(N,t)π​HF+(N,t)δ​HF−(N,t)→⋯

gives imFW1​,s1​−​⊂kerι and FW2​,s2​+​π=0. Exactness identifies δ with a degree-−1 isomorphism from HF+(N)/imπ onto kerι, namely the two versions of Reduced Heegaard Floer homology. Hence the inverse on these reduced groups is defined, and one forms

This is the construction of the Mixed Heegaard Floer invariant, rather than the zero ordinary composite.
Let Θ− be the top minus generator, of degree −2, and Θ+ the bottom plus generator, of degree zero. For ζ∈Λ∗(H1​(M)/torsion), insert its degree-one homology actions in the Heegaard Floer cobordism maps and define
ΦM,s​(Ur⊗ζ)=coefficient of Θ+ in FW,smix​(UrΘ−⊗ζ).​
(51)
This is an integer up to the overall orientation sign. cobordism composition and associativity show independence of the admissible cut and handle choices. Equivalently, fixing the relevant orientation data removes the overall sign ambiguity.
The grading condition can be checked explicitly. Since χ(W)=χ(M)−2 and σ(W)=σ(M), the sum of the ordinary cobordism shifts is d(s)+1, where d(s)=(c1​(s)2−2χ(M)−3σ(M))/4. Finally, two positive pieces are needed, so that both infinity maps vanish and the reduced-group factorization is available. For an admissible cut the connecting-map condition and signature additivity imply that the two positive subspaces inject as orthogonal subspaces of the closed intersection form; hence b2+​(M)≥2. With only one positive direction no such admissible cut exists, and a chamber-dependent construction requires extra choices. With no positive direction, the ordinary infinity maps need not vanish at all. This explains the stated bound for the invariant defined here.
