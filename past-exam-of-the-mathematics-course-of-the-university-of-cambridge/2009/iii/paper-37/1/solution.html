Write mj​=EYj and M=MY​. The derivatives of the cumulant-generating function are
κY′​=MM′​,κY′′​=MM′′​−M2(M′)2​,κY′′′​=MM′′′​−M23M′M′′​+M32(M′)3​.
(1)
Since M(0)=1 and M(j)(0)=mj​, the first three cumulants are
κY,1​=m1​=EY,κY,2​=m2​−m12​=Var(Y),κY,3​=m3​−3m1​m2​+2m13​=E(Y−EY)3.​
(2)
The last equality follows by expanding the cube and taking expectations. We use the paper's unstandardized third central moment throughout. The usual skewness coefficient divides this quantity by Var(Y)3/2; its sign is the same whenever the variance is positive.
There is a minor domain qualification: finite ordinary moments do not guarantee a finite moment-generating function for positive arguments. The differentiations are valid in a neighborhood of zero when a positive exponential moment exists. For positive variables with the stated finite moments, all formulas also follow from left derivatives at zero, because YjetY≤Yj for t≤0 and dominated convergence applies. Thus no extra exponential-moment assumption is needed for the moment identities themselves.
For the random sum of independent claims, condition on N=k. Independence of the claims and independence from the count give
E[etS∣N=k]=MX1​​(t)k.
(3)
Taking expectations and then logarithms yields the random-sum transform identity and the cumulant composition for a random sum:
MS​(t)=GN​(MX1​​(t)),κS​(t)=κN​(logMX1​​(t))=κN​(κX1​​(t)).​
(4)
Here GN​ is the probability generating function. These identities hold wherever the transforms are finite, in particular for t≤0 with positive claims.
