Use the elementary, or weak, topos axioms: finite limits and a power object PA for every object A, naturally representing Sub(A×X). No arbitrary products or coproducts are assumed. In particular Ω=P1 is the subobject classifier. Inverse image of the membership relation defines Pf:PB→PA for f:A→B.
Interchanging the factors in a relation gives natural bijections
E(A,PB)≅Sub(A×B)≅E(B,PA).
(1)
Thus the contravariant power-object functor P:Eop→E has left adjoint Pop. Its adjunction unit ηA​:A→PPA sends a generalized element a to the predicate on PA given by membership of a.
This unit is monic. If x,y:T→A have the same image under ηA​, membership of x and y agrees in every T-parameterized subobject of A. Apply this to the graph of x in A×T: membership of x is true, so membership of y is true, which means y=x. This argument uses parameterized relations, not an assumption that global elements detect morphisms.
The functor P reflects isomorphisms. Suppose Pf is invertible. Then PPf is invertible, and naturality PPfηA​=ηB​f together with monicity of ηA​ shows that f is monic. Regard f as a subobject of B. Its inverse image along itself is the whole of A, just as for the whole subobject of B. Those two subobjects correspond to maps 1→PB; since Pf is monic, they coincide. Therefore the subobject f is all of B, and f is an isomorphism.
We now prove the preservation condition for the crude monadicity theorem. A reflexive pair in Eop is a coreflexive pair f,g:B⇉A in E, with rf=rg=1B​ for some r:A→B. Let e:E↪B be its equalizer. For any monomorphism m, define ∃m​ on power objects by composing a parameterized subobject with m; no general image construction is needed because m is monic. Inverse image followed by this extension obeys Pm∃m​=1.
Here both f,g are monic. For a parameterized subobject S of B, an element b belongs to g−1(f(S)) exactly when gb=fb′ for some b′∈S. Applying r gives b=b′, so this condition is precisely b∈S and fb=gb. Consequently, as morphisms of power objects,
Pf∃f​=1PB​,Pg∃f​=∃e​Pe,Pe∃e​=1PE​.
(2)
If h:PB→Z equalizes Pf,Pg, these identities give
h=hPf∃f​=hPg∃f​=h∃e​Pe.
(3)
It therefore factors uniquely through Pe, which is a split epimorphism with section ∃e​. Since fe=ge, Pe also equalizes Pf,Pg. Hence Pe is their coequalizer. This proves explicitly that power objects turn coreflexive equalizers into coequalizers.
Finite equalizers in E give all coequalizers of reflexive pairs in Eop, and we have shown that P preserves them and reflects isomorphisms. The crude monadicity theorem now yields
P is monadic,Eop≃EPP.​
(4)
Fix a set I of cardinality κ. If E has I-indexed products in a category, its Eilenberg-Moore category for the double-power monad has them, since the forgetful functor creates existing limits. The displayed equivalence then gives I-products in Eop, hence I-coproducts in a category in E.
For the converse, suppose those coproducts exist. The diagonal Δ:E→EI is a logical functor, because all the elementary topos structure in EI is pointwise, and it has left adjoint ∐I​. Apply power-object monadicity to both toposes. Under their monadic identifications, Δop is the lift of Δ to the double-power Eilenberg-Moore categories. The base functor Δ is a right adjoint to ∐I​, so the adjoint lifting theorem for monad algebra functors gives a left adjoint to its algebra lift. Its required reflexive coequalizers exist in Eop, since they are opposites of finite equalizers in E. Taking opposites gives a right adjoint to Δ, which is exactly the I-product functor. Thus the products and coproducts of fixed cardinality in a topos satisfy
E has κ-products⟺E has κ-coproducts.​
(5)
