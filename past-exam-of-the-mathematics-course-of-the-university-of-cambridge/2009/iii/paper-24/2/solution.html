Let (G,ε,δ) be a Cartesian comonad, so its endofunctor preserves finite limits. A coalgebra for a comonad is (A,a) with a:A→GA, εA​a=1A​ and δA​a=Gaa. Its morphisms satisfy bf=Gfa. Write U:EG→E for the forgetful functor and R(X)=(GX,δX​) for the cofree coalgebra. Transposition is
EG((A,a),R(X))≅E(A,X),f⟼εX​f,h⟼Gha.
(6)
The forgetful functor creates finite limits, since G preserves them. We construct both exponential objects and the subobject classifier.
For coalgebras (A,a),(B,b), set D=BA and W=(GB)A in the ambient topos. Define p,q:GD→W by transposing
p♭=bev(εD​×1A​),q♭=Gev(1GD​×a),
(7)
where GD×GA≅G(D×A). Their cofree transposes are coalgebra morphisms
p​=GpδD​,q​=GqδD​:R(D)⇉R(W).
(8)
Let H be their coalgebra equalizer. For a coalgebra (C,c), an arrow C→R(D) corresponds to a map h:C×A→B. Its evaluation respects coalgebra structure exactly when
bh=Gh(c×a).
(9)
Under the displayed transpositions this is precisely the condition that it equalize p​,q​. Thus EG(C,H)≅EG(C×A,B) naturally, and H is the required exponential in a coalgebra topos.
Let κ:GΩ→Ω classify G⊤:G1≅1↪GΩ. In R(Ω) take the equalizer
ΩG​↪R(Ω)1​GκδΩ​​​R(Ω).
(10)
The truth map into R(Ω) is G⊤ and factors through this equalizer. To verify its universal property, take an underlying subobject m:S↪A of a coalgebra. It carries a compatible coalgebra structure exactly when a maps S into GS. Such a structure, if it exists, is unique because Gm is monic; its laws follow by cancellation of m,Gm,G2m. Moreover a−1(GS)⊆S always: applying εA​ to a point of GS lands in S. Thus stability is equivalent to S=a−1(GS).
If χ:A→Ω classifies S, this last equality is exactly
χ=κGχa.
(11)
The cofree transpose χ​=Gχa:A→R(Ω) satisfies this equation precisely when it factors through ΩG​. Indeed transposing the equality χ​=GκδΩ​χ​ gives the equation above. Pulling back its truth map recovers S, because κ classifies G⊤. Hence ΩG​ is the subobject classifier of a coalgebra topos. We have obtained finite limits, exponentials and a classifier, proving EG is a topos.
Now let f:H→E be a geometric morphism, and put L=f∗, V=f∗​, with unit η. Both functors preserve finite limits, so G=LV is a Cartesian comonad on H. Put D=HG. Its comparison functor
K:E⟶D,K(X)=(LX,LηX​)
(12)
is left exact and has right adjoint
J(A,a)=Eq(Va,ηVA​:VA⇉VLVA).
(13)
To check the adjunction, transpose an underlying map LX→A to X→VA. The coalgebra-morphism equation becomes exactly the equality of the two composites into VLVA, so the transposed arrow factors uniquely through the displayed equalizer.
The counit KJ(A,a)→(A,a) is invertible. Applying L to the defining equalizer gives the equalizer of Ga,δA​:GA⇉G2A. The structure map a:A→GA is exactly that equalizer: it equalizes by coassociativity; if t:T→GA equalizes, apply εGA​ to obtain t=aεA​t, giving its unique factorization through a. Thus the underlying comparison counit is an isomorphism, and the forgetful functor reflects isomorphisms. Therefore J is full and faithful.
The adjunctions U⊣R and K⊣J define
Hp​Di​E,f=i∘p.​
(14)
Here p∗=U is faithful, so p is a surjective geometric morphism; i∗​=J is full and faithful, so i is a geometric embedding. Their inverse-image composite is UK=L, establishing the surjection-embedding factorization of a geometric morphism.
For uniqueness, let S be the class of arrows in E inverted by f∗. In any surjection-embedding factorization f=i′p′, conservativity of p′∗ shows that S is also the class inverted by i′∗. The essential image of the fully faithful i∗′​ consists exactly of the S-local objects Y, namely those for which E(B,Y)→E(A,Y) is bijective for every A→B in S. One direction follows from the adjunction. For the converse, the reflector unit Y→i∗′​i′∗Y belongs to S; locality gives a retraction, and locality of the reflected object makes that retraction also a right inverse. Thus the unit is invertible. The image subcategory is consequently determined by f, independently of the factorization. The two intermediate toposes are equivalent over E, and their inverse-image composites with the embeddings determine the surjection legs. This proves uniqueness up to equivalence.
