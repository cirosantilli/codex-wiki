Use one ring sort with 0,1,+,−,⋅ and the usual unital commutative ring equations. Write Inv(x) for the coherent formula ∃y(xy=1). Add the coherent sequents
0=1⊢⊥,⊤⊢x​Inv(x)∨Inv(1−x).
(40)
This presents the coherent theory of local rings. For an ordinary nonzero ring satisfying these axioms, the nonunits form an ideal: a multiple of a nonunit cannot be a unit; and if nonunits a,b had unit sum, multiplying by (a+b)−1 would make two nonunits sum to 1, contradicting the second axiom. They are also closed under additive inverses. This proper ideal contains every proper ideal, since a proper ideal contains no unit, and its quotient is a field; it is the unique maximal ideal. Conversely in a local ring, x and 1−x cannot both belong to the unique maximal ideal, so one is a unit. The first sequent excludes the zero ring. In a topos, the disjunction asserts that these unit alternatives hold locally.
Let C be a small skeleton of the opposite of the category of finitely presented unital commutative rings; write CA​ for the object corresponding to A. The Zariski coverage on finitely presented rings is generated by
{CA[fi−1​]​⟶CA​}i=1n​,(f1​,…,fn​)=A,
(41)
and the empty cover of C0​. Localization is finitely presented, and its compatibility with base change proves the covering axioms.
Here is the classifying argument. For an internal commutative ring M and a presentation A=Z[x1​,…,xn​]/(p1​,…,pr​), define FM​(CA​) as the subobject of Mn satisfying the polynomial equations pi​=0. Ring maps induce maps of these solution objects in the site direction. This is independent of presentation, and it preserves finite limits: finite colimits of finitely presented rings correspond to joining finite generators and equations, whose solution objects are finite limits. Conversely a left-exact functor F:C→E reconstructs a commutative ring F(CZ[t]​) using the polynomial maps for addition, multiplication, constants and additive inverse. Presentations by generators and equations then show F≅FM​. Thus flat functors on C correspond to internal commutative rings.
Under FM​, the localization arrow above identifies its domain with the locus where fi​ evaluates to a unit; the inverse witness is unique. The family is jointly epimorphic exactly when these unit loci cover the solution object for A. The locality axiom implies this for every unit-ideal family: from a finite sum ∑fi​gi​=1, repeatedly apply the two-summand unit alternative, rescaling a unit partial sum when necessary; some fi​gi​, hence some fi​, is a unit locally. Conversely the cover of Z[t] by its localizations at t and 1−t gives precisely the locality axiom, while the empty cover of the zero ring gives 0=1⊢⊥. Therefore continuous flat functors are exactly models of the displayed coherent theory. The Diaconescu equivalence for geometric morphisms now identifies
Geom(E,Sh(C,Z))≃Modlocal rings​(E),
(42)
naturally in E. This proves the asserted classifying topos identification.
Since the site is subcanonical, the generic local ring is the representable sheaf
R=yCZ[t]​,R(CA​)=HomRing​(Z[t],A)=A.
(43)
Its ring operations are induced by polynomial substitution. Its infinitesimal subobject is likewise representable:
D=yCZ[ε]/(ε2)​,D(CA​)={a∈A:a2=0}.
(44)
Products of representables correspond to tensor products of their rings. For every finitely presented A, exponential adjunction and the Yoneda lemma give
(RD)(CA​)​≅Hom(yCA​×D,R)≅Hom(yCA[ε]/(ε2)​,yCZ[t]​)≅A[ε]/(ε2).​
(45)
Every element of this ring has a unique expression a+bε, because 1,ε form an A-basis. This holds even when A has nilpotents or zero divisors. The expression is natural under every ring homomorphism A→B, which sends its two coefficients to their images. The canonical morphism R×R→RD, transposed from (a,b,d)↦a+bd, is consequently a bijection at every stage CA​, with a natural inverse extracting the coefficients. Therefore
R×R∼​RD,(a,b)⟼(d↦a+bd).​
(46)
This proves the Kock-Lawvere axiom for the generic local ring. The uniqueness is an internal sheaf assertion tested at all finitely presented rings, including the ring with its generic square-zero element; restricting the test to fields would lose that element and would not prove uniqueness.
