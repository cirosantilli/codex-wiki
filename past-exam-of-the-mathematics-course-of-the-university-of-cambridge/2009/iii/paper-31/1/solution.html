For a<b, let UN​[a,b] be the number of completed upcrossings by the observations X1​,…,XN​: buy when the value is at most a, sell at the first subsequent value at least b, and repeat. With z−=max{−z,0}, one form of the Doob upcrossing inequality for a martingale is
(b−a)EUN​[a,b]≤E(XN​−a)−.
(1)
For completeness, hold either zero or one unit according to this rule. The holding at each step is measurable before the next martingale increment, so the total gain GN​ has expected value zero. Completed trades earn at least b−a each; an unfinished trade loses at most (XN​−a)−. Thus GN​≥(b−a)UN​[a,b]−(XN​−a)−, proving the inequality.
Suppose C=supn​E∣Xn​∣<∞. For each rational pair a<b the inequality and monotone convergence give
EU∞​[a,b]≤b−aC+∣a∣​<∞.
(2)
Every rational interval therefore has only finitely many upcrossings, simultaneously outside a countable union of null sets. If liminfXn​<limsupXn​, there is a rational interval strictly between them and infinitely many upcrossings, a contradiction. Thus the limit X∞​ exists in the extended real line almost surely. Fatou's lemma now gives
E∣X∞​∣≤liminfn​E∣Xn​∣≤C.
(3)
The limit is finite almost surely and integrable. This proves the martingale convergence theorem rather than using it as the deduction.
The exact extra condition for convergence in L1 is uniform integrability:
K→∞lim​nsup​E[∣Xn​∣1{∣Xn​∣>K}​]=0.​
(4)
To see sufficiency, first discard the tails of Xn​ uniformly and the tail of the integrable X∞​. On the set where both absolute values are at most K, their difference is bounded by 2K and tends to zero almost surely; dominated convergence finishes the proof. Conversely, if Xn​→X∞​ in L1, then
E[∣Xn​∣1{∣Xn​∣>K}​]≤2E∣Xn​−X∞​∣+E[∣X∞​∣1{∣X∞​∣>K/2}​].
(5)
Make the first term uniformly small for all sufficiently large n, then control the finitely many remaining integrable variables by increasing K. Hence uniform integrability is necessary too. In this case the conditional expectation identity Xn​=E[X∞​∣Fn​] follows by taking the L1 limit in Xn​=E[Xm​∣Fn​] for m≥n.
The coin-doubling martingale shows why an L1 bound is not enough. Let ξ1​,ξ2​,… be independent fair coin indicators and set
Xn​=2n1{ξ1​=⋯=ξn​=1}​.
(6)
Conditional on the first n coins, the next value is either 2Xn​ or zero with equal probabilities, so this is a nonnegative martingale. Its expected value is always 1, while a first failure occurs almost surely, giving Xn​→0 almost surely. Yet E∣Xn​−0∣=1 for every n. Indeed, whenever 2n>K, its tail expected value above K is still 1. Thus convergence in L1 fails because uniform integrability fails.
