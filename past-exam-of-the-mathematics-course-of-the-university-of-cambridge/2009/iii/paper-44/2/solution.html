The supplied matrices are the chiral gamma-matrix representation. The Pauli matrix multiplication law implies {σi,σj}=2δijI2​. Block multiplication gives (γ0)2=I4​, {γ0,γi}=0, and {γi,γj}=−2δijI4​. Hence their Clifford algebra relation is
{γμ,γν}=2gμνI4​.​
(12)
Since partial derivatives commute, multiplying the Dirac equation by iγν∂ν​+m gives
0=(iγν∂ν​+m)(iγμ∂μ​−m)ψ=−(gμν∂μ​∂ν​+m2)ψ.
(13)
Thus every component of the Dirac spinor satisfies (□+m2)ψ=0​, the Klein-Gordon equation with the same mass.
For a Lorentz transformation x′=Λx, set ψ′(x′)=S(Λ)ψ(x). The derivative transforms as ∂μ′​=(Λ−1)νμ​∂ν​. With the given spinor-matrix identity,
S−1(iγμ∂μ′​−m)ψ′=[iΛμρ​γρ(Λ−1)νμ​∂ν​−m]ψ=(iγν∂ν​−m)ψ=0.
(14)
This proves the Lorentz covariance of the Dirac operator explicitly.
For parity, let xP​=(x0,−x) and ψP​(x)=γ0ψ(xP​). Spatial differentiation brings a minus sign, which is canceled by γiγ0=−γ0γi. Therefore
(iγμ∂μ​−m)ψP​(x)=γ0[(iγμ∂μ​−m)ψ](xP​)=0.
(15)
Since γ0 is Hermitian and squares to one, the Dirac adjoint transforms as
ψˉ​P​(x)=ψˉ​(xP​)γ0.​
(16)
The chirality matrix γ5=iγ0γ1γ2γ3 anticommutes with γ0. The parity transformation of a Dirac bilinear consequently gives
ψˉ​P​ψP​(x)=ψˉ​ψ(xP​),ψˉ​P​γ5ψP​(x)=−ψˉ​γ5ψ(xP​).​
(17)
The first bilinear is a Lorentz scalar even under parity, and the second is a pseudoscalar.
In this representation, γ5=diag(−I2​,I2​). Its square is one, so the chiral projectors P±​=(1±γ5)/2 are idempotent, mutually orthogonal and sum to the identity. They select ψL​=P−​ψ and ψR​=P+​ψ, the two Weyl spinors. Because γμP±​=P∓​γμ, a solution of the massless Dirac equation has each chiral component solving that equation separately. In two-component form,
i(∂t​−σ⋅∇)ψL​=0,i(∂t​+σ⋅∇)ψR​=0.
(18)
The two chirality sectors are thus independent under massless propagation and proper Lorentz transformations. A nonzero Dirac mass term couples them, while parity exchanges them. This explains why the projectors are particularly useful in the massless theory.
