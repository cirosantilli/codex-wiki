Use natural units ℏ=c=1 and the Minkowski metric g=diag(1,−1,−1,−1). The canonical momentum is π=∂L/∂ϕ˙​=ϕ˙​, and the Legendre transform in mechanics gives
H=21​∫d3x[π2+(∇ϕ)2+m2ϕ2].
(1)
For canonical quantization of a real scalar field, impose the equal-time canonical commutation relations
[ϕ(t,x),π(t,y)]=iδ3(x−y),[ϕ(t,x),ϕ(t,y)]=[π(t,x),π(t,y)]=0.
(2)
The Heisenberg picture equation O˙=i[H,O] then gives ϕ˙​=π. For the other equation, the momentum term commutes with π; commuting the gradient term past π and integrating the derivative of the Dirac delta function by parts gives π˙=∇2ϕ−m2ϕ. Consequently
(□+m2)ϕ=0,□=∂t2​−∇2.​
(3)
The Fourier transform reduces this Klein-Gordon equation to oscillators of frequency Ep​=p2+m2​. Hermiticity of the real scalar field pairs their positive- and negative-frequency coefficients, so its general mode expansion can be normalized as
ϕ(x)=∫(2π)32Ep​​d3p​[a(p)e−ip⋅x+a†(p)eip⋅x],p0=Ep​.
(4)
To verify its normalization rather than guess the oscillator algebra, use scalar field oscillator inversion at t=0:
a(p)=∫d3xe−ip⋅x[Ep​/2​ϕ(0,x)+2Ep​​i​π(0,x)].
(5)
Its adjoint has the conjugate phase and the opposite sign in front of iπ. The two mixed field-momentum commutators give
[a(p),a†(q)]=2Ep​Eq​​Ep​+Eq​​∫d3xei(q−p)⋅x=(2π)3δ3(p−q)​.
(6)
The Fourier representation of the Dirac delta function sets the energies equal in the last step. Similarly, [a(p),a(q)] has a factor Eq​−Ep​ multiplying δ3(p+q) and vanishes; the two-creator commutator vanishes as well.
Substituting the expansion in H and integrating the spatial phases, the two-annihilator and two-creator coefficients cancel by Ep2​=p2+m2. The free real scalar Hamiltonian in oscillator variables is
H=21​∫(2π)3d3p​Ep​[a†(p)a(p)+a(p)a†(p)].
(7)
It differs from :H:=∫d3pEp​a†a/(2π)3 by its constant vacuum energy; normal ordering removes that constant. Using the oscillator commutators, for either Hamiltonian,
[H,a(p)]=−Ep​a(p),[H,a†(p)]=Ep​a†(p).​
(8)
Choose the Fock vacuum annihilated by every a(p). Each creation operator adds a particle of energy Ep​ and momentum p; its annihilation operator removes one. Their mutual commutativity gives bosonic Fock space and symmetric many-particle states. Thus the scalar mass-shell frequency has a particle interpretation, rather than merely describing a classical oscillation.
For t=x0−y0 and r=x−y, vacuum contraction and time ordering give the Feynman propagator
DF​(t,r)=∫(2π)3d3p​2Ep​eip⋅r​[θ(t)e−iEp​t+θ(−t)eiEp​t].
(9)
In the scalar Feynman propagator pole prescription, the integral of ie−ip0t/((p0)2−Ep2​+i0) has poles at Ep​−i0 and −Ep​+i0. Close clockwise below for t>0 and counterclockwise above for t<0. The residue theorem gives exactly the corresponding term above, proving
DF​(x−y)=∫(2π)4d4p​p2−m2+i0ie−ip⋅(x−y)​.​
(10)
As a direct check of the distributional source, gE​(t)=e−iE∣t∣/(2E) is continuous, but gE′​(0+)−gE′​(0−)=−i. The derivative jump of a free scalar time-ordered two-point function therefore gives (∂t2​+E2)gE​=−iδ(t). Spatial Fourier inversion yields
(□+m2)DF​(x−y)=−iδ4(x−y).​
