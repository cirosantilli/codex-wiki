First fix a source convention needed for physical coordinates. The PDF's displayed definition of σz​ omits a factor of i: half the commutator of the two Hermitian transverse matrices is anti-Hermitian and cannot supply a real Bloch coordinate. The Hermitian choice consistent with the supplied dynamical matrix is
σz​=2i[σx​,σy​]​=(−10​01​).
(39)
With this convention the three Pauli expectation values use the unit-radius normalization, unlike the orthonormal coordinates of Question 2. For example, writing K=(0b​a0​) with a=1+λ, b=−λ, the dissipator gives rates −(a−b)2/2 for x, −(a+b)2/2 for y, and population equation z˙=b2−a2−(a2+b2)z. The Quantum Hamiltonian adds 2αz to x˙ and −2αx to z˙. This independently reproduces the supplied real Bloch dynamics.
Put q=2λ+1. Since y decays independently, every steady state has y∗​=0. The reduced steady-state equations are
q2x∗​−4αz∗​=0,4αx∗​+(q2+1)z∗​=−2q.
(40)
Their coefficient determinant is
D=q2(q2+1)+16α2.
(41)
It is strictly positive unless q=0 and α=0. Inverting this two-by-two system gives
x∗​=−D8αq​,z∗​=−D2q3​.​
(42)
Thus the reduced system has a unique steady state except at α=0, λ=−1/2. At that exceptional point the equations reduce to x˙=0, z˙=−z/2, so every (x,0) is stationary; physical coordinates restrict −1≤x≤1.
As a consistency check on positivity in the nonsingular case,
1−x∗2​−z∗2​=D2[16α2+q2(q2−1)]2​≥0.
(43)
The calculated state is therefore in the unit Bloch disk. The original PDF correctly labels the second steady-state expression as z∗​; the TeX conversion's repeated x∗​ is another transcription defect, not a second formula for x∗​.
