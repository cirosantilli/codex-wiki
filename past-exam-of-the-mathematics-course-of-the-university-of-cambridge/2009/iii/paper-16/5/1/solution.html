False. Take M=CP2#CP2 and N=S2×S2, with their standard product and connected-sum orientations. They are closed simply connected four-manifolds. For the connected sum, removing a four-ball leaves the fundamental group unchanged, and the Seifert-van Kampen theorem across the separating three-sphere preserves simple connectivity. The Mayer–Vietoris sequence computes integral cohomology Z,Z2,Z in degrees zero, two and four, and zero in other degrees. The Künneth theorem gives the same groups for S2×S2. Thus their Betti numbers agree and all their integral cohomology is torsion-free.
Their cohomology rings nevertheless differ. In M, let u,v come from the two projective summands and let z generate degree four. The cohomology ring of the connected sum of two complex projective planes has
u2=v2=z,uv=0.
(22)
These relations follow by using cycles in the separate summands and the degree-one pinch projections onto them. In N, the sphere-factor generators x,y satisfy
x2=y2=0,xy=z.
(23)
Consequently every degree-two class of N has square
(ax+by)2=2abz,
(24)
an even multiple of the top generator, whereas u2 in M is a primitive generator. An integral graded-ring isomorphism cannot change this property, since it sends a generator of H4 to its positive or negative. A homotopy equivalence induces a graded-ring isomorphism, so none exists. Equivalently, their intersection forms are diag(1,1) and (01​10​), an odd form and an even form respectively.
