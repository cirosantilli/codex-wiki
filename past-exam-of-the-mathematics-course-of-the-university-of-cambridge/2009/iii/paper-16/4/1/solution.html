OurBigBook.com Words: 166
Take CP3. It is a closed smooth manifold and is simply connected: its CW complex structure has no one-cells, and attaching cells of dimension at least two to a point cannot create a fundamental group. Its cohomology ring is Z[x]/(x4) with ∣x∣=2, so x3=0.
The theorem used is the Lusternik–Schnirelmann critical point theorem: every smooth real-valued function on a closed connected smooth manifold has at least its unnormalized Lusternik–Schnirelmann category many distinct critical points, without a nondegeneracy assumption. Here category is the minimum number of open subsets covering the manifold whose inclusions are null-homotopic. The cup length bound gives category at least four. To justify that bound, a cover by three categorical sets would make each positive-degree class vanish on its assigned set. The long exact sequence of each pair lifts three copies of x to relative classes, and their relative cup product would lie in H6(M,M;Z)=0, contradicting x3=0. Thus every smooth function has at least four critical points, including functions with degenerate critical points. The critical-point theorem in this formulation is described in Conley pairs in geometry—Lusternik–Schnirelmann theory and more.
