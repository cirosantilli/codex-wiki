Multiply by a test function vanishing at the endpoints and apply integration by parts. The positive operator is −u′′+xu, so the forcing changes sign. The weak formulation of this forced Airy boundary-value problem is: find u∈V=H01​(0,1) such that
a(u,v)=ℓ(v)(v∈V),a(u,v)=∫01​(u′v′+xuv)dx,ℓ(v)=−∫01​vdx.​
(1)
Here V is the zero-boundary Sobolev space and can be equipped with ∥v∥V​=∥v′∥L2​. The interval Poincaré inequality states ∥v∥L2​≤π−1∥v′∥L2​, so this norm makes V a Hilbert space and is equivalent to its usual H1 Sobolev norm. The form is symmetric and satisfies
∣a(u,v)∣≤(1+π−2)∥u∥V​∥v∥V​,a(v,v)≥∥v∥V2​,∣ℓ(v)∣≤π−1∥v∥V​.
(2)
The Lax-Milgram theorem says that a bounded coercive bilinear form on a Hilbert space and a bounded linear functional determine exactly one weak solution. All its hypotheses have just been verified. Moreover, u′′=xu+1∈L2 gives u∈H2 on the interval; the right-hand side is then continuous, so this weak solution is the classical solution with the prescribed endpoint values.
Since the form is symmetric, the equivalent Ritz method minimizes
J(v)=21​∫01​[(v′)2+xv2]dx+∫01​vdxover H01​(0,1).​
(3)
Indeed, stationarity gives the weak equation and
J(v)−J(u)=21​a(v−u,v−u)≥0,
(4)
with equality only at u. This proves the minimization equivalence, including the sign of the linear term.
Restricting to a conforming finite element space Vh​⊂V gives a unique discrete solution and Galerkin orthogonality, a(u−uh​,vh​)=0 for every vh​∈Vh​. The Céa lemma states that if the continuity and coercivity constants are M and α, then ∥u−uh​∥V​≤(M/α)infvh​∈Vh​​∥u−vh​∥V​. For this symmetric problem the exact best-approximation constant in the energy norm is one. The piecewise-linear finite element interpolation estimate ∥u−Ih​u∥H1​≤Ch∥u∥H2​ on regular finite element meshes therefore gives convergence of a numerical method of the Ritz method, with first-order error in the energy norm.
