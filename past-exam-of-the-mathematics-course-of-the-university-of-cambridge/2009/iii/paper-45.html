The Adjoint double cover from SU(2) to SO(3) is obtained by writing a traceless Hermitian matrix as X=xi​σi​, where the σi​ are the Pauli matrices, and defining AXA†=(R(A)x)i​σi​. Explicitly,
Rij​(A)=21​tr(σi​Aσj​A†).
(1)
Conjugation preserves 21​trX2=∣x∣2, so R(A) is orthogonal; connectedness of SU(2) gives detR(A)=1. Composition of conjugations makes R a group homomorphism. A matrix in its kernel commutes with every Pauli matrix, so is scalar, and unit determinant then gives A=±I. Every spatial rotation occurs: A=exp(−iθn⋅σ/2) induces the rotation through angle θ about n. Thus
SO(3)≅SU(2)/{±I}.​
(2)
Since SU(2) is the simply connected three-sphere, this is the universal double cover; the two Lie algebras are isomorphic, although the groups are not.
Define the conjugate fundamental spinor of SU(2) by ηˉ​α​=(ηα)∗. Regard ηˉ​ as a row, so unitarity gives
ηˉ​α′​=ηˉ​β​(A†)βα​=ηˉ​β​(A−1)βα​.​
(3)
Consequently ηˉ​α​ξα is an invariant Hermitian pairing. The basic invariant tensors are the Kronecker delta δαβ​ and the alternating tensors ϵαβ​ and ϵαβ. Choose ϵ12​=1, ϵ12=−1, so that ϵαγϵγβ​=δαβ​. The unit-determinant identity ATϵA=ϵ makes [η,ξ]=ϵαβ​ηαξβ invariant; its inverse gives the corresponding upper-index invariant. These contractions generate the tensor invariants of the defining representation. In particular η​α=ϵαβηˉ​β​ transforms by A, showing that the conjugate representation is equivalent to the defining one. The antilinear map η↦η​ squares to −I, so this is a pseudoreal representation, rather than a real structure.
For Representation theory of SU2, take Vn​=SymnC2, with A acting on every index of a symmetric tensor. Its independent components are determined by the number r of indices equal to 2, so there are n+1 of them. Equivalently, the homogeneous polynomial representation of SU2 has basis z1n−r​z2r​, 0≤r≤n, with
H=z1​∂z1​​−z2​∂z2​​,E+​=z1​∂z2​​,E−​=z2​∂z1​​.
(4)
The basis weights are n,n−2,…,−n, and the raising operator and lowering operator connect consecutive basis vectors with nonzero coefficients until their endpoints. Any nonzero invariant subspace contains a weight vector, by applying polynomial spectral projections in H. Raising it reaches z1n​, and lowering then obtains the entire basis. Thus Vn​ is an irreducible representation, of spin j=n/2. The classification of finite-dimensional representations of SU2 gives every finite-dimensional complex irreducible representation in this way. The central element −I acts on a rank-n tensor as (−1)n, so descent of an SU(2) representation to SO(3) gives
dimVn​=n+1,Vn​ descends to SO(3)⟺n is even.​
(5)
For the tensor contractions in the SU2 Clebsch-Gordan decomposition, contract r indices of S with r indices of T using ϵ, and symmetrize all the remaining free indices:
Urα1​…αm+n−2r​​=Symα1​,…,αm+n−2r​​(ϵβ1​γ1​​⋯ϵβr​γr​​Sα1​…αm−r​β1​…βr​Tαm−r+1​…αm+n−2r​γ1​…γr​),0≤r≤min(m,n).
(6)
Each Ur​ is an irreducible symmetric tensor in Vm+n−2r​. To prove these are precisely the constituents, use two sets of formal variables z,w. The highest-weight vectors
Fr​=(z1​w2​−z2​w1​)rz1m−r​w1n−r​
(7)
are killed by the total raising operator z1​∂z2​​+w1​∂w2​​ and have weights m+n−2r. The determinant factor is invariant; lowering the remaining factor gives an irreducible string of dimension m+n−2r+1. Distinct highest weights give inequivalent summands. The unitary representation on this tensor product makes invariant complements available, and the dimension check
∑r=0min(m,n)​(m+n−2r+1)=(m+1)(n+1)
(8)
exhausts the space. Hence the contractions above are its irreducible projections, up to normalization, and the Clebsch-Gordan decomposition for SU2 is
Vm​⊗Vn​≅r=0⨁min(m,n)​Vm+n−2r​.​
(9)
For the decomposition of three SU(2) spinors, write Qαβγ=η1α​η2β​η3γ​ and Sαβγ=Q(αβγ). The contractions
d1α​d2α​d3α​​=ϵβγ​Qαβγ=η1α​[η2​,η3​],=ϵβγ​Qβαγ=η2α​[η1​,η3​],=ϵβγ​Qβγα=η3α​[η1​,η2​]​
(10)
transform as defining spinors. Alternation in three indices in dimension two vanishes, giving d1​−d2​+d3​=0; there are exactly two independent contractions. An explicit inverse, with the epsilon convention above, is
Qαβγ=Sαβγ+ϵαβ32d1γ​−d2γ​​−ϵαγ3d1β​+d2β​​.
(11)
Symmetrizing this expression gives S, while its first two contractions give d1​,d2​, which verifies the decomposition. Thus
V1⊗3​=V3​⊕V1​⊕V1​,21​⊗21​⊗21​=23​⊕21​⊕21​.​
(12)
In the quark model, three spin-1/2 quarks therefore have total quark spin S=3/2 or S=1/2. The two spin-1/2 copies correspond to coupling the first pair to spin 0 or spin 1 before adding the third quark. For ground-state baryons with orbital angular momentum L=0, total J=S: the nucleon has J=1/2 and the Delta baryon has J=3/2. These two coupling copies alone do not assert two unrestricted physical baryon families. The antisymmetric three-quark colour singlet, the spatial wavefunction and flavor symmetry must also satisfy the Pauli constraint on three-quark flavour multiplets; orbital excitations can give other total angular momenta.

Let Ra​=ad(Ta​), where ad(X)Y=[X,Y]. In the given basis, the Adjoint representation of a Lie algebra is
(Ra​)cb​=fcab​.
(13)
The Jacobi identity gives [adX,adY]=ad[X,Y], so [Ra​,Rb​]=fcab​Rc​, as required for a Lie algebra representation. The Killing form is the symmetric Trace form of a Lie algebra representation
κab​=tr(Ra​Rb​)=fcad​fdbc​.​
(14)
Cyclicity of the trace proves its invariance directly:
κ([Ta​,Tb​],Tc​)+κ(Tb​,[Ta​,Tc​])​=tr([Ra​,Rb​]Rc​+Rb​[Ra​,Rc​])=tr(Ra​Rb​Rc​−Rb​Rc​Ra​)=0.​
(15)
In components this is precisely κdc​fdab​+κbd​fdac​=0​, so the Killing form is an invariant bilinear form on a Lie algebra.
Choose the infinitesimal gauge transformation convention δϕ=ωϕ, with ω(x)=ωa(x)ta​, and write Aμ​=Aμa​ta​. A gauge covariant derivative must satisfy Dμ′​=UDμ​U−1 for U=1+ω+O(ω2); hence
Aμ′​=UAμ​U−1−(∂μ​U)U−1,δAμ​=−∂μ​ω+[ω,Aμ​].​
(16)
Equivalently δAμa​=−∂μ​ωa+fabc​ωbAμc​. Expanding δ(Dμ​ϕ) makes the cancellation transparent:
δ(Dμ​ϕ)​=(∂μ​ω)ϕ+ω∂μ​ϕ+(−∂μ​ω+[ω,Aμ​])ϕ+Aμ​ωϕ=ω(∂μ​ϕ+Aμ​ϕ).​
(17)
Thus δ(Dμ​ϕ)=ωata​Dμ​ϕ​: it transforms in the same representation as ϕ. In adjoint covariant derivative notation, δAμ​=−Dμad​ω.
The commutator of two gauge covariant derivatives defines the gauge field strength:
[Dμ​,Dν​]ϕ=Fμνa​ta​ϕ,Fμν​=∂μ​Aν​−∂ν​Aμ​+[Aμ​,Aν​].
(18)
Thus
Fμνa​=∂μ​Aνa​−∂ν​Aμa​+fabc​Aμb​Aνc​.​
(19)
Conjugating the derivative commutator gives Fμν′​=UFμν​U−1, so the gauge field strength transforms homogeneously in the Adjoint representation:
δFμν​=[ω,Fμν​],δFμνa​=fabc​ωbFμνc​.​
(20)
Even if the matrices ta​ in a particular matter representation are not faithful, the displayed component formula defines the Lie-algebra-valued curvature independently of that representation.
Only g(ab)​ contributes to the Yang-Mills theory Lagrangian, since Fμνa​Fbμν is symmetric in a,b. Thus take gab​ to be a constant real symmetric bilinear form. Its infinitesimal variation is
δL=−41​ωa(gdc​fdab​+gbd​fdac​)Fμνb​Fcμν.
(21)
Arbitrary local field strengths and gauge parameters give the necessary and sufficient invariant gauge kinetic form condition
gdc​fdab​+gbd​fdac​=0,gab​=gba​.​
(22)
An antisymmetric part is unconstrained but contributes nothing. A nondegenerate kinetic term additionally needs a nondegenerate bilinear form; positive energy additionally needs a positive internal metric in the signature (+−−−) convention. Neither extra condition follows solely from gauge invariance. For a compact simple real Lie algebra, −κ is positive definite and is the standard choice up to a positive factor.
For the intended complex-simple setting, the Killing form is nondegenerate. Consequently write any invariant bilinear form on a Lie algebra uniquely as g(X,Y)=κ(SX,Y). Invariance of both forms implies
κ((SadZ−adZS)X,Y)=0
(23)
for every X,Y,Z, so S commutes with the Adjoint representation. A subspace invariant under all adjoint maps is an ideal, hence simplicity makes the adjoint representation irreducible. By Schur lemma, S=cI, giving the uniqueness of an invariant bilinear form on a simple Lie algebra:
gab​=cκab​.​
(24)
The same conclusion holds for compact simple real algebras by complexification, with c real; nondegeneracy excludes c=0, and positive energy selects c<0 in the stated convention.
There is a genuine real-simple exception to uniqueness of the Killing form if “simple” is read as an arbitrary real simple algebra. Regard sl2​(C) as real. It is real simple: its complexification consists of two complex simple factors exchanged by conjugation, so a conjugation-stable ideal is zero or the whole algebra. Both ReκC​ and ImκC​ are real symmetric invariant forms, while κR​=2ReκC​. With H=diag(1,−1), the Killing form of the special linear Lie algebra gives κC​(H,H)=8 and κC​(H,iH)=8i. Therefore the imaginary part cannot be a real multiple of κR​. The uniqueness conclusion requires the customary complex-simple or compact-real interpretation.

Use units c=ℏ=1 and the Minkowski metric g=diag(1,−1,−1,−1). Composing two affine Poincaré group transformations, with the rightmost applied first, gives
(Λ,a)(Σ,b)=(ΛΣ,a+Λb),(Λ,a)−1=(Λ−1,−Λ−1a).​
(25)
The translations form a normal subgroup, so this is the semidirect product of the Lorentz group with R1,3. The transformations (diag(1,R),0), R∈SO(3), fix time and the spatial origin. Their products and inverses correspond to RR′ and R−1, hence they form a subgroup isomorphic to SO(3).
A rotationless Lorentz boost changes the velocity of a frame without adding a spatial rotation. For ∣v∣<1 a convenient active convention is
B(v)=(γγv​γvTI+(γ−1)vv,T​),γ=(1−∣v∣2)−1/2,
(26)
with B(0)=I. It satisfies BTgB=g, detB=1, and B(m,0)=(mγ,mγv). Collinear boosts form a one-parameter subgroup with additive rapidity, but general boosts do not. For nonzero boosts along x and y, (Bx​By​)02​=γx​γy​vy​ while (Bx​By​)20​=γy​vy​: their product is not symmetric, whereas every rotationless boost above is symmetric. The product contains a Wigner rotation. Infinitesimally, the Poincare algebra gives [Ki​,Kj​]=−iϵijk​Jk​ for Hermitian generators, so the boost generators do not close amongst themselves.
There is a group-versus-cover qualification in the spin range: an honest unitary representation of the proper orthochronous Poincaré group permits integer spin only. Half-integer spin requires its double cover SL(2,C)⋉R1,3, or a projective unitary representation of the ordinary group. The construction below gives the requested range on that double cover and descends for integer spin.
Fix m>0 and the rest momentum k=(m,0). Its little group consists of rotations: if a proper orthochronous Lorentz transformation fixes k, its time column is (1,0), the metric condition forces the corresponding time row, and the remaining block belongs to SO(3). On the Lorentz spinor double cover, this little group is SU(2). Choose its irreducible spin-s representation, with rest-frame states ∣k,s,σ⟩, σ=−s,−s+1,…,s, and matrices D(s), the Wigner D-matrices.
For each p on the forward mass shell, p2=m2, p0>0, choose the standard rotationless Lorentz boost L(p)=B(p/p0) and define
∣p,s,σ⟩=U[L(p),0]∣k,s,σ⟩.
(27)
The labels are canonical spin projections, defined by this choice of boosts. For any proper orthochronous Λ, factor
ΛL(p)=L(Λp)W(Λ,p),W(Λ,p)=L(Λp)−1ΛL(p).
(28)
The last factor fixes k and hence is a Wigner rotation. On the double cover, use its lift to SU(2). Taking the translation convention U[I,a]∣p,s,σ⟩=eip⋅a∣p,s,σ⟩ yields the massive induced representation of the Poincare double cover:
U[Λ,a]∣p,s,σ⟩=ei(Λp)⋅aτ=−s∑s​Dτσ(s)​(W(Λ,p))∣Λp,s,τ⟩.​
(29)
The argument of the translation phase is Λp, since U[Λ,a]=U[I,a]U[Λ,0]. The Wigner rotation identity
W(Λ2​Λ1​,p)=W(Λ2​,Λ1​p)W(Λ1​,p)
(30)
proves the Lorentz part of the representation law; the phases multiply according to the affine product already derived.
Normalize the generalized momentum states by ⟨p,s,σ∣q,s,τ⟩=2p0(2π)3δ3(p−q)δστ​. The invariant mass shell measure d3p/(2p0) and unitarity of D(s) make the induced representation unitary on L2(d3p/(2p0))⊗C2s+1. This covariant normalization accounts for the absence of an energy square-root factor in the displayed transformation law. To see irreducibility, translations force an operator in the commutant to act within momentum fibres; the little-group action and Schur lemma make its action scalar on each irreducible spin fibre. Lorentz transitivity on the forward mass shell makes this scalar constant. The commutant is therefore scalar, so the unitary representation is irreducible. Conversely, the simultaneous translation spectrum and its little-group representation give the massive positive-energy sector of Wigner's classification.
Thus every m>0 and s∈{0,21​,1,…} gives such an irreducible representation on the double cover. Its central element over a 2π spatial rotation acts as (−1)2s. Since this rotation is the identity in the ordinary Poincaré group,
all integer and half-integer s occur on the double cover;descent to the ordinary group requires s∈Z≥0​.​
(31)
For the parity operator, put Π=diag(1,−1,−1,−1) and pˉ​=Πp=(p0,−p). The extension obeys PU[Λ,a]P−1=U[ΠΛΠ,Πa]. At rest, P commutes with the rotations; Schur lemma therefore gives P∣k,s,σ⟩=ηP​∣k,s,σ⟩ with a common phase ∣ηP​∣=1. Since ΠL(p)Π=L(pˉ​), the parity action on canonical massive spin states is
P∣p,s,σ⟩=ηP​∣p0,−p,s,σ⟩.​
(32)
For the usual reflection convention P2=I, ηP​=±1 is the intrinsic parity. Canonical spin is an axial quantity and its label σ is unchanged; a helicity label instead changes sign because momentum is reversed. Either intrinsic parity extends a given massive spin representation.

Start with a nonzero highest-weight vector v0​=∣n⟩ and set vr​=E−r​v0​. The sl2 highest-weight lowering formula follows inductively from the commutator relations:
Hvr​=(n−2r)vr​,E+​vr​=r(n−r+1)vr−1​,E−​vr​=vr+1​.
(33)
Indeed [H,E−​]=−2E−​ gives the weight formula, and E+​E−​=E−​E+​+H turns the coefficient r(n−r+1) at step r into (r+1)(n−r) at step r+1. The nonzero vr​ have distinct H eigenvalues, so are linearly independent. Finite dimensionality therefore forces a last nonzero vector vN​. Applying E+​ to vN+1​=0 gives (N+1)(n−N)vN​=0, hence N=n. This also proves that no earlier vector can vanish. Thus the cyclic highest-weight representation generated by ∣n⟩ is
Vn​=span{v0​,v1​,…,vn​},dimVn​=n+1.​
(34)
It is irreducible: spectral projections in H select a weight vector from any nonzero invariant subspace, and its nonzero raising and lowering coefficients then generate the whole string. This gives the classification of finite-dimensional sl2 representations used for each root direction below.
For the SU(3) Lie algebra, the repeated-index relation is the trace constraint ∑i​Tii​=0. The displayed generators are a basis of its complexification, not nine independent generators or three individually vanishing diagonal elements. Realize them by matrix units as Tij​=Eij​−δij​I/3; the compact real algebra is obtained from anti-Hermitian combinations. Then
H1​=diag(1,−1,0),H2​=diag(0,1,−1),
(35)
and E1+​=E12​, E1−​=E21​, E2+​=E23​, E2−​=E32​. Substituting into the given commutators gives
[E1+​,E1−​][E2+​,E2−​]​=T11​−T22​=H1​,=T22​−T33​=H2​,​[H1​,E1±​][H2​,E2±​]​=±2E1±​,=±2E2±​.​
(36)
Thus both root pairs obey the required sl2 Lie algebra relations. More generally, their Cartan matrix is
[Hi​,Ej±​]=±Cij​Ej±​,C=(2−1​−12​).
(37)
A weight vector with eigenvalues (h1​,h2​) moves by (−2,1) under E1−​ and by (1,−2) under E2−​. The third negative-root operator is [E2−​,E1−​]=E31​ and moves it by (−1,−1). In the required weight diagram coordinates
x=h1​,y=3​h1​+2h2​​,
(38)
these moves are (−2,0), (1,−3​) and (−1,−3​) respectively. Successive lowerings from a highest-weight vector, with linear dependencies removed, construct the following bases.
For Dynkin labels (1,0), use the defining action of SU(3) on C3. Its highest vector is e1​; lowering gives e2​=E21​e1​ and e3​=E32​e2​. There are no further independent vectors. The weight diagram of the defining SU(3) representation, in the present rescaled coordinates, is
basise1​e2​e3​​(h1​,h2​)(1,0)(−1,1)(0,−1)​(x,y)(1,1/3​)(−1,1/3​)(0,−2/3​)​​
(39)
and dimR(1,0)=3​. Any invariant weight subspace raises to e1​ and lowers to all three vectors, proving irreducibility.
For (1,1), use the adjoint representation of SU(3) on traceless matrices, with X acting by Y↦[X,Y]. The highest vector is E13​: both [E12​,E13​] and [E23​,E13​] vanish, while [Hi​,E13​]=E13​. Lowering gives [E21​,E13​]=E23​ and [E32​,E13​]=−E12​. Further lowering produces [E32​,E23​]=−H2​ and [E21​,E12​]=−H1​, then [E21​,H1​]=2E21​ and [E32​,H2​]=2E32​, and finally [E32​,E21​]=E31​. Consequently the basis is the six off-diagonal matrix units together with H1​,H2​:
basisE12​E21​E23​E32​E13​E31​H1​,H2​​(h1​,h2​)(2,−1)(−2,1)(−1,2)(1,−2)(1,1)(−1,−1)(0,0)​(x,y)(2,0)(−2,0)(−1,3​)(1,−3​)(1,3​)(−1,−3​)(0,0)​​
(40)
The six nonzero weights have weight multiplicity one, and zero has multiplicity two. Thus dimR(1,1)=6+2=8​. Its irreducibility follows also because an adjoint-invariant subspace is an ideal and sl3​(C) is a simple Lie algebra.
For (3,0), take the symmetric cubic representation of SU(3), Sym3C3, realized by homogeneous degree-three polynomials. Here
Tij​=zi​∂zj​​−3δij​​N,N=∑k​zk​∂zk​​,
(41)
so z13​ is a highest-weight vector with eigenvalues (3,0). For a+b+c=3,
E2−c​E1−b+c​z13​=a!3!​b!(b+c)!​z1a​z2b​z3c​,
(42)
which is nonzero and constructs every monomial. These monomials form a basis and have distinct weights (a−b,b−c). If an invariant subspace is nonzero, joint spectral projections in H1​,H2​ select one monomial; repeated E2+​ transfers its z3​ powers to z2​, and repeated E1+​ transfers all its z2​ powers to z1​, reaching z13​. Lowering then generates the whole basis, proving irreducibility without merely assuming the dimension formula. The plotted weights are
c0123​y=(a+b−2c)/3​3​0−3​−23​​allowed x=a−b−3,−1,1,3−2,0,2−1,10​​
(43)
with multiplicity one at every point. Hence dimR(3,0)=(25​)=10​.
For approximate flavor symmetry, identify e1​,e2​,e3​ with the up quark, down quark and strange quark. The defining representation is their flavour triplet. The baryon octet realizes R(1,1) and contains the spin-1/2 nucleons and the Λ,Σ,Ξ multiplets; its central double weight distinguishes Λ and Σ0. The baryon decuplet realizes the symmetric flavour representation R(3,0), containing the spin-3/2 Δ,Σ∗,Ξ∗,Ω multiplets. In standard isospin and flavor hypercharge normalization, I3​=h1​/2 and Y=(h1​+2h2​)/3, so these plots use x=2I3​ and y=3​Y. Three-quark spin and flavour combine subject to the Pauli constraint on three-quark flavour multiplets and the antisymmetric three-quark colour singlet. The adjoint flavour representation also describes a meson octet. A distinct, exact colour SU(3) gauge symmetry places quarks in its defining representation and gluons in its adjoint octet; colour and flavour here are different physical group actions.
