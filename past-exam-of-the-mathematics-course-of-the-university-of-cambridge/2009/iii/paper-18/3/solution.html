We regard N as positive and invertible in k; N=1 gives the trivial group and the same arguments apply. Put A=Pic0(C), an abelian variety of dimension g.
First compute the prime-to-characteristic torsion of an abelian variety. The differential of [N]:A→A at the origin is Nid, and translation gives the same assertion everywhere. Consequently [N] is an étale morphism, its fibres have dimension zero, and properness makes it finite. Its image is closed of dimension g, hence all of A. Choose an ample line bundle L0​ on A and put L=L0​⊗[−1]∗L0​, which is symmetric and ample. The Theorem of the Cube gives the recurrence
[n+1]∗L⊗[n−1]∗L≅([n]∗L)⊗2⊗L⊗2.
(10)
After fixing a trivialization at the origin, induction from n=0,1 gives [n]∗L≅L⊗n2. Comparing top intersection products under the finite map [N] gives
deg[N](c1​(L)g)=(c1​([N]∗L)g)=N2g(c1​(L)g).
(11)
The intersection number is positive because L is ample, so deg[N]=N2g. Since [N] is étale and k is algebraically closed field, its kernel has precisely N2g points.
For a prime power ℓr∣N, write the finite abelian group A[ℓr] as a product of cyclic groups of orders ℓaj​, with 1≤aj​≤r. Its subgroup killed by ℓ is A[ℓ], so the number of factors is 2g. Its total order is ℓ2gr, so ∑aj​=2gr forces every aj​=r. Combining the prime-power factors by the Chinese remainder theorem proves
A[N]≅(Z/NZ)2g.​
(12)
The same reasoning works for the dual abelian variety A, also of dimension g.
To define the Weil pairing for an abelian variety on two copies of A[N], we first justify the required theta identification with A. Identify X=Picg−1(C) with an A-torsor and use ordinary translation Tq​(x)=x+q. The Theorem of the square, a consequence of the Theorem of the Cube, makes
λ(q)=Tq∗​OX​(Θ)⊗OX​(Θ)−1
(13)
a group homomorphism A→A. Fix the negative Abel map of an algebraic curve j:C→A, P↦[P0​−P], and let ρ:A→Pic0(C)=A be pullback by j. Pullback by a translate of j gives the same map: algebraically trivial line bundles on an abelian variety are translation-invariant as Picard classes, by the Theorem of the square.
For any q∈A, choose a degree-g class D such that both D and D+q have one-dimensional section spaces. This is possible because the nonspecial degree-g classes form a nonempty open subset of the irreducible Picard scheme of a curve, and its translate has a nonempty intersection with it. Since Tq​ϕD​=ϕD+q​, the restriction of the theta divisor to an Abel curve proved above gives
ϕD∗​λ(q)≅OC​(D+q)⊗OC​(D)−1≅q.
(14)
Each ϕD​ differs from j by a translation. Thus ρλ=idA​. This equality on closed points is equality of morphisms, since A is reduced and the target is separated. In particular λ is a closed immersion, being a section of the separated morphism ρ. Its image has dimension g=dimA, so it is all of the reduced irreducible abelian variety A. Therefore λ is an isomorphism of schemes. This proves the needed theta autoduality of a Jacobian, including possible infinitesimal issues in positive characteristic.
Here is a definition of the Weil pairing for an abelian variety that also proves nondegeneracy. For M∈A[N], the Theorem of the Cube gives [N]∗M≅M⊗N≅OA​. Choose a trivialization of [N]∗M. The finite étale cover [N]:A→A has deck group G=A[N]. Its natural descent action on [N]∗M becomes multiplication by scalars under this trivialization: these scalars are constant because every invertible regular function on the proper connected abelian variety A is constant. They form a character
χM​:G⟶k×,
(15)
whose values lie in μN​, since NP=0 for every P∈G. Rescaling the trivialization changes none of these scalars. Translation gives multiplicativity in P, and tensor product gives χM⊗M′​=χM​χM′​. Define
eN​(P,Q)=χλ(Q)​(P)∈μN​.​
(16)
This fixes a convention for the Weil pairing for an abelian variety; reversing the convention for the descent action inverts all values and changes no nondegeneracy assertion.
If χM​=1, the trivialization is equivariant for the deck group. Descent then makes M the trivial line bundle on the target. Thus
A[N]⟶Hom(A[N],μN​),M⟼χM​
(17)
is injective. The source and target both have N2g elements, since k contains all N distinct roots of unity and A[N]≅(Z/N)2g. Hence it is an isomorphism. Characters of this group separate its points, so the Weil pairing for an abelian variety is nondegenerate in both variables. Composing with the isomorphism λ proves the pairing on A[N] is perfect.
