Fix a point [L]∈Picd(C) and put V=H0(C,L). The set of effective Cartier divisors in its divisor class is the complete linear system of a divisor, but identifying points alone would not prove the requested scheme assertion. We identify its functor on arbitrary parameter schemes, including nonreduced ones.
Let T be a k-scheme and let D⊂C×T be a relative effective degree-d Cartier divisor whose Abel map of an algebraic curve is the constant morphism [L]. The definition of the Picard functor of a curve says
O(D)≅prC∗​L⊗π∗M
(28)
for a line bundle M on T. Its canonical section is nonzero on every geometric fibre. Since the constant family has direct image V⊗k​OT​, this section determines a line subbundle
M−1↪V⊗k​OT​.
(29)
It is a subbundle, rather than merely an injective sheaf map: locally some coordinate is nonzero on each residue field, hence a unit on a neighbourhood, so the quotient is locally free. Such a subbundle is exactly a T-point of the projective space Plines​(V).
Conversely, its tautological line subbundle evaluates to a section of prC∗​L⊗π∗M, nonzero on every geometric fibre. On the smooth integral fibres its zero locus is an effective Cartier divisor. The fibrewise regularity criterion gives a relative Cartier divisor flat over T, of degree d, and its Picard class is constant [L]. These constructions are inverse and commute with arbitrary base change. Thus they identify functors, and therefore the scheme fibre itself:
ad−1​([L])≅Plines​(H0(C,L))≅Pkh0(C,L)−1​.​
(30)
If h0(C,L)=0, the fibre is empty, which is smooth as well. The argument remains valid over a point's residue field, so every scheme fibre, not just each closed geometric fibre, is smooth. Fibre dimensions can jump when h0(C,L) jumps; this does not assert that the entire Abel map of an algebraic curve is a smooth morphism.
