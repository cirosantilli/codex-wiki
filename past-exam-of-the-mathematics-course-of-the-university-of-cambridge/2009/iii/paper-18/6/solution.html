Let V=H0(C,ΩC1​), of complex dimension g, and choose a symplectic basis a1​,…,ag​,b1​,…,bg​ of H1​(C,Z), with ai​⋅bj​=δij​. Integration gives a homomorphism
I:H1​(C,Z)⟶V∨,I(γ)(ω)=∫γ​ω.
(31)
We first prove both injectivity and discreteness, rather than treating periods as a lattice by definition.
Cut the compact Riemann surface along this basis to a polygon. For closed one-forms ω,η, choose a primitive F of ω on the cut polygon and apply Stokes theorem to d(Fη)=ω∧η. Pairing its boundary edges records the jumps of F across the cuts and gives the Riemann bilinear relations for a compact surface:
∫C​ω∧η=∑i​(Ai​(ω)Bi​(η)−Bi​(ω)Ai​(η)),Ai​(ω)=∫ai​​ω, Bi​(ω)=∫bi​​ω.
(32)
If a holomorphic differential form ω has all a-periods zero, take η=ω. The right side is zero, while in a holomorphic coordinate, for ω=f(z)dz,
2i​ω∧ω=∣f(z)∣2dx∧dy.
(33)
Its integral is strictly positive unless ω=0. Thus the a-period map V→Cg is injective, hence an isomorphism. Choose a basis ω1​,…,ωg​ with ∫aj​​ωi​=δij​, and put τij​=∫bj​​ωi​. Applying the same boundary identity to ωi​,ωj​, whose wedge product is zero, gives τij​=τji​. Applying it to ω=∑ci​ωi​ and its conjugate gives
2i​∫C​ω∧ω=cT(Imτ)c>0(c=0).
(34)
Hence Imτ is positive definite.
In the dual coordinates, I(∑mi​ai​+ni​bi​)=m+τn. The real-linear map (x,y)↦x+τy from R2g to Cg is an isomorphism: taking imaginary parts of a zero gives (Imτ)y=0, then y=0 and x=0. Its image of Z2g is therefore discrete and cocompact, and no nonzero integral cycle has zero image. Thus
I is injective, with full lattice Λ=Zg+τZg⊂V∨.​
(35)
The Abel-Jacobi map of a compact Riemann surface sends an effective complex analytic divisor D=∑Qi​ to ∑i​∫P0​Qi​​(ω1​,…,ωg​) modulo Λ. Changing a path adds an integral period, so the map is well-defined. For two degree-d complex analytic divisors, let E=D−D′=∑P​nP​P, with ∑nP​=0, and put
vi​=∑P​nP​∫P0​P​ωi​.
(36)
We prove that E is principal exactly when v∈Λ, including the construction of a meromorphic function.
First construct a differential of the third kind η with residues nP​ at the support of E and no other poles. For the reduced support S, the residue exact sequence is
0⟶KC​⟶KC​(S)⟶⨁P∈S​CP​⟶0.
(37)
On sheaf cohomology, the obstruction to prescribing residues belongs to H1(C,KC​)≅C by Serre duality; its pairing with the constant section of OC​ is the sum of residues. This follows directly by integrating the local simple-pole terms around the boundary circles. Thus the obstruction is zero because ∑nP​=0, and the required η exists. Subtract a linear combination of the normalized holomorphic differential forms so that all its a-periods are zero.
Take its poles and the integration paths inside the same cut polygon. Apply the boundary calculation above to a primitive Fi​(P)=∫P0​P​ωi​, removing small circles around the poles of η. Since d(Fi​η)=0 away from the poles, the paired outer edges contribute Bi​(η), while the small circles contribute 2πi∑P​nP​Fi​(P) by the residue theorem. Therefore
∫bi​​η=2πivi​.
(38)
The same argument without normalizing η gives the useful reciprocity identity
Bi​(η)−∑j​τij​Aj​(η)=2πivi​.
(39)
These identities use compatible cuts and paths; the final conclusions are unchanged by integral periods.
Suppose v=m+τn with m,n∈Zg. Define
η′=η−2πi∑j​nj​ωj​.
(40)
Its a-periods are −2πini​, and its b-periods are 2πimi​. Around a pole its integral is 2πinP​. These cycles generate the first homology group of the punctured surface, so every period of η′ belongs to 2πiZ. Choosing an auxiliary point Q away from the poles, set
f(P)=exp(∫QP​η′).
(41)
It is single-valued away from the poles. Locally η′=nP​dt/t+a holomorphic one-form, so f=tnP​ times a nonvanishing holomorphic function. It extends meromorphically with div(f)=E. This proves sufficiency and explicitly produces the required principal complex analytic divisor.
Conversely, if E=div(f), the meromorphic differential df/f has residues nP​ and every period in 2πiZ, since each integral counts the winding number of f about zero. Write its a-period vector as 2πia and its b-period vector as 2πib, with a,b∈Zg. The reciprocity identity gives v=b−τa∈Λ. Since E has degree zero, changing the base point cancels in its Abel sum; a base point at the support of E causes no problem, because the Abel integrals use holomorphic forms. We have proved
AJ(D)=AJ(D′)⟺D−D′ is principal⟺D∼D′.​
(42)
This establishes the Abel theorem for divisors directly from the period and residue calculations.
