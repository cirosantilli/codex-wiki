For the uniform prior, write s=x+y for the pooled success count in m+n trials. Conditional on s, exchangeability makes all placements of the successes equally likely. The successes in the second batch therefore have a hypergeometric distribution, and the factor in question is
B=(nm+n​)(ys​)(n−ym+n−s​)​=Pr(Y=y∣X+Y=s)at s=x+y.
(10)
This is hypergeometric allocation of exchangeable Bernoulli counts: sample n positions without replacement from a population of m+n positions containing s successes. The uniform-count predictive property gives Pr(X+Y=s)=1/(m+n+1) and Pr(X=x)=1/(m+1), so
Pr(Y=y∣X=x)=m+n+1m+1​B.​
(11)
As y varies with x fixed, s=x+y also varies. Therefore B alone is not a normalized hypergeometric distribution over y; its normalization factor is essential.
