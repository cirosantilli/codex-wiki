ℓ(β,σ2)=−2n​logσ2−2σ21​∥Y−Xβ∥2.

β​∼Np​(β,σ2(XTX)−1).​

Put Qn​(θ)=n−1∑i=1n​logf(Yi​;θ) and Q(θ)=Eθ0​​logf(Y1​;θ). The integrable absolute-log envelope makes these expectations finite. Compactness and continuity of the likelihood ensure that a maximum exists, and maximizing it is equivalent almost surely to maximizing Qn​.
First identify the unique maximizer of Q. Let r(y)=f(y;θ)/f(y;θ0​) on the support of the true density. Since logr≤r−1,
Q(θ)−Q(θ0​)=Eθ0​​logr(Y1​)≤Eθ0​​r(Y1​)−1=∫{f(y;θ0​)>0}​f(y;θ)dy−1≤0.
(14)
Equality requires r=1 almost surely under the true distribution and no mass outside its support, so the two probability distributions coincide. By identifiability, this happens only for θ=θ0​. Equivalently Q(θ0​)−Q(θ) is the Kullback-Leibler divergence from the true distribution to the candidate.
Fix ε>0 and let Kε​={θ∈Θ:∥θ−θ0​∥≥ε}. If this set is empty there is nothing to prove. Otherwise it is compact, so continuity and the strict maximum imply
δε​=Q(θ0​)−maxθ∈Kε​​Q(θ)>0.
(15)
On the event supΘ​∣Qn​−Q∣<δε​/3, every point of Kε​ has Qn​(θ)<Q(θ0​)−2δε​/3, whereas Qn​(θ0​)>Q(θ0​)−δε​/3. Hence no maximizer can belong to Kε​. The supplied uniform law of large numbers therefore proves compact-parameter consistency of maximum likelihood:
Pθ0​​(∥θn​−θ0​∥≥ε)≤Pθ0​​(supΘ​∣Qn​−Q∣≥3δε​​)⟶0,θn​p​θ0​.​
(16)
Under the usual regularity conditions, with nonsingular one-observation Fisher information matrix I(θ0​), the asymptotic normality of a maximum likelihood estimator is
n​(θn​−θ0​)d​Nd​(0,I(θ0​)−1),​
(17)
where I(θ0​)=Eθ0​​[Sθ0​​(Y1​)Sθ0​​(Y1​)T] and Sθ​=∇θ​logf(⋅;θ) is the score function.
For the uniform model, let Mn​=maxi​Yi​. For a sample in its support, the likelihood is θ−n1{θ≥Mn​}​, decreasing on its nonzero region. Thus the uniform endpoint maximum-likelihood estimator is
θn​=Mn​.​
(18)
The all-zero sample is a probability-zero exception and may be assigned an arbitrary positive estimate. Independence gives Pθ​(Mn​≤y)=(y/θ)n on [0,θ]. For 0<ε<θ,
Pθ​(∣Mn​−θ∣>ε)=(1−θε​)n⟶0;
(19)
for ε≥θ this probability is zero. This proves consistency directly.
Let Tn​=n(θ−Mn​)/θ. Its exact cumulative distribution function is
Pθ​(Tn​≤t)=⎩⎨⎧​01−(1−t/n)n1​t<0,0≤t≤n,t>n.​
(20)
For fixed t≥0, this converges to 1−e−t, so Tn​d​Exp(1). In particular the errors are of order 1/n. Explicitly, for every fixed η>0 and sufficiently large n,
Pθ​(n​∣Mn​−θ∣>η)=(1−θn​η​)n≤e−ηn​/θ⟶0.
(21)
Consequently
θn(θ−θn​)​d​Exp(1),θn​=θ+op​(n−1/2).​
(22)
One violated regularity condition is parameter-independent support: the density is supported on [0,θ]. Differentiation under the density integral is not valid in the usual regular-model form. Indeed the interior score equals −1/θ, whose expectation is not zero; the changing endpoint contributes the omitted boundary term. Thus the uniform model is handled by its exact maximum distribution, rather than by the regular asymptotic normality theorem.

Put P=I−p−11p​1pT​, Z=PX=X−Xˉ1p​, Q=∥Z∥2 and a=p−3. Componentwise,
θj​=Xj​−Qa​(Xj​−Xˉ),p1​∑j​θj​=Xˉ.
(23)
Thus the James–Stein shrinkage toward the sample mean leaves the average unchanged and multiplies every contrast about it by the same data-dependent factor 1−a/Q. If Q>a the contrasts shrink without changing sign; if Q<a their signs reverse, and if Q<a/2 their magnitudes increase. The event Q=0 has probability zero, and the estimator can be assigned Xˉ1p​ there.
The sample mean is a linear normal statistic with mean θˉ and variance 1/p, so
Xˉ∼N(θˉ,1/p).​
(24)
The error splits into orthogonal mean and contrast components:
θ−θ=(Xˉ−θˉ)1p​+(1−Qa​)Z−Pθ.
(25)
Since the second term is perpendicular to 1p​, its cross product with the first is zero for every sample. Taking squared norms and expectations, pE(Xˉ−θˉ)2=1, which yields the risk function
R(θ,θ)=1+Eθ​​(1−Qa​)Z−Pθ​2.​
(26)
To compute the contrast risk, choose an orthonormal basis matrix U for the range of P, with UTU=Im​, UUT=P and m=p−1≥3. Then W=UTX∼Nm​(η,I) with η=UTθ, Q=∥W∥2, and the contrast norm is the norm of (1−a/Q)W−η. Expand it:
Eθ​​(1−Qa​)W−η​2=m−2a∑j=1m​EQ(Wj​−ηj​)Wj​​+a2EQ1​.
(27)
For gj​(w)=wj​/∥w∥2, the Gaussian Stein identity gives
E[(Wj​−ηj​)gj​(W)]=E∂j​gj​(W),∑j=1m​∂j​gj​(w)=∥w∥2m−2​.
(28)
This application at a singularity is valid: near zero the reciprocal squared radius is integrable in dimension m>2, since its radial integral is ∫01​rm−3dr. Apply Gaussian integration by parts outside a ball of radius ε; the inner boundary contribution is bounded by a constant times εm−2 and vanishes. The Gaussian tail eliminates the boundary at infinity. Therefore all required expectations are finite and the displayed identity holds.
Since a=m−2=p−3, the Stein risk identity now gives contrast risk m−a2E(1/Q). Adding the unit mean risk proves
R(θ,θ)=p−(p−3)2Eθ​∥X−Xˉ1p​∥21​.​
(29)
Finally Q has the noncentral chi-squared distribution χm2​(λ) with λ=∥Pθ∥2. To establish where its reciprocal expectation is largest, complete the square in the normal representation Q=∥W∥2 to obtain its Laplace transform:
Ee−tQ=(1+2t)−m/2exp(−1+2tλt​),t≥0.
(30)
Since 1/q=∫0∞​e−tqdt, the Tonelli theorem gives the reciprocal moment of a noncentral chi-squared variable
EQ1​=∫0∞​(1+2t)−m/2exp(−1+2tλt​)dt.
(31)
Its integrand is positive and strictly decreasing in λ for every t>0. The expectation is therefore strictly largest at λ=0, where
EQ1​=∫0∞​(1+2t)−m/2dt=m−21​=p−31​.
(32)
Because this expectation is subtracted in the risk, the minimizing set is exactly Pθ=0, that is, all coordinates of θ equal. On this line the risk is p−(p−3)=3:
θ∈Rpargmin​R(θ,θ)={c1p​:c∈R},θmin​R(θ,θ)=3.​

Write y∈Rn for the observed response and xj​ for column j of the working design matrix X. The usual LARS convention gives every covariate unit Euclidean norm. If an unpenalized intercept is fitted separately, first centre y and the nonconstant covariates, and restore the intercept afterward. For an intercept-free model no centring is required. The algebra below also applies to an unscaled full-rank design; only the literal equal-angle interpretation uses unit-length columns. The penalty is defined on the regression coefficients of whichever working design is used, since rescaling covariates changes the units of an L1 penalty.
Initialize β​0=0, μ​0=Xβ​0=0 and residual r0=y. At iteration k, let
rk−1=y−μ​k−1,cjk​=xjT​rk−1,Ck=maxj​∣cjk​∣.
(34)
These cjk​ are residual inner products, often called residual correlations. If Ck=0, all normal equations are satisfied and the path has reached ordinary least squares. Otherwise the active set Ak consists of the selected columns tied at the maximal absolute correlation. Initially select the maximizing column, or all such columns in a tie. Ordinary least angle regression retains previously selected columns and adds each new one when it ties the active correlations. Put sjk​=sign(cjk​) for active j.
To define the direction, form the signed active design XAs​=(sjk​xj​)j∈Ak​ and its Gram matrix GA​=(XAs​)TXAs​. Full column rank makes this matrix positive-definite. Let 1A​ denote the vector of ones of length ∣Ak∣, and set
αk=(1AT​GA−1​1A​)−1/2,wk=αkGA−1​1A​,uk=XAs​wk.
(35)
Then ∥uk∥2=1 and (XAs​)Tuk=αk1A​. This is the equiangular direction in least angle regression. Define ajk​=xjT​uk for every covariate, and define the regression coefficient direction by djk​=sjk​wjk​ on Ak, zero otherwise. It satisfies Xdk=uk.
Along the line μ(γ)=μ​k−1+γuk, residual correlations are cjk​−γajk​. For an active index these equal sjk​(Ck−γαk), so all active absolute correlations decrease together until level zero at γ0k​=Ck/αk. An inactive covariate first joins when its correlation equals either sign of the common level. Solving these two equalities gives
γ​entryk​=minj∈/Ak+​{αk−ajk​Ck−cjk​​, αk+ajk​Ck+cjk​​}.
(36)
Here min+ means the smallest finite strictly positive candidate; zero denominators do not produce a future entry, and an empty candidate set has minimum +∞. Candidates beyond the zero-correlation endpoint are capped by that endpoint. Take
γLARk​=min{γ​entryk​,γ0k​},β​k=β​k−1+γLARk​dk,μ​k=μ​k−1+γLARk​uk.​
(37)
At an entry add the newly tied covariate, or all simultaneous ties, and recompute the direction. Once all covariates are active there is no inactive candidate: the final move is to correlation level zero and the full least-squares fit. These definitions specify every term in the stated step formula and the entry knot in least angle regression.
For definiteness use the following penalty normalization for the Lasso estimator:
β​λLasso​=β∈Rpargmin​{21​∥y−Xβ∥2+λj=1∑p​∣βj​∣},λ>0.​
(38)
Full column rank makes the criterion strictly convex, so the minimizer is unique. With the alternative squared-error normalization 1/(2n), the penalty parameter is divided by n. The Karush-Kuhn-Tucker conditions for the Lasso in the chosen normalization are
xjT​(y−Xβ)=λsign(βj​)(βj​=0),∣xjT​(y−Xβ)∣≤λ(βj​=0).
(39)
Hence the zero vector is the solution for every λ≥λmax​:=maxj​∣xjT​y∣. Along a sign-compatible least-angle segment the corresponding penalty is λ(γ)=Ck−γαk. Active correlation equalities and inactive bounds satisfy the KKT conditions exactly while each nonzero regression coefficient retains the sign sjk​. Ordinary LARS can let a regression coefficient cross zero while its correlation sign stays unchanged; continuing past that crossing would violate the KKT conditions.
The required modification is therefore to monitor regression coefficient zero crossings. Along β(γ)=β​k−1+γdk, define the Lasso dropout knot
γ​k=minj∈Ak:−β​jk−1​/djk​>0​(−djk​β​jk−1​​),
(40)
with djk​=0 ignored and the empty minimum again +∞. Stop at
γLassok​=min{γ​entryk​,γ​k,γ0k​}.​
(41)
If a nonzero active regression coefficient reaches zero before the next entry, remove it from the active direction and recompute; the fitted value is continuous at this event. If entry comes first, add that covariate as before. A removed covariate is allowed to re-enter later, so the active set need not increase monotonically. The interior of a segment represents all penalty values through γ=(Ck−λ)/αk, rather than just the estimates at its knots.
For complete treatment of simultaneous events, full column rank alone does not imply that entries and dropouts occur singly. At a knot with positive level C, let E={j:∣xjT​(y−Xβ)∣=C}, sj​ be those correlation signs, and N={j:βj​=0}⊆E. The KKT-consistent resolution of tied Lasso knots chooses the next tangent v by
minv​{21​vTXTXv−∑j∈E​sj​vj​},vEc​=0,sj​vj​≥0(j∈E∖N).
(42)
This strictly convex quadratic program has a unique minimizer. For nonzero or newly entering coordinates its stationarity gives xjT​Xv=sj​; for tied zero coordinates kept out, sj​xjT​Xv≥1. Thus for a small decrease h of the penalty, β+hv has the correct regression coefficient signs, active residual scores sj​(C−h), and inactive scores within [−(C−h),C−h]. Strictly inactive coordinates remain feasible by continuity. Choose the resulting support for the next segment and normalize u=Xv/∥Xv∥; this reduces to the preceding equiangular formulas. It handles ties, including a newly tied zero regression coefficient whose unconstrained direction would point against its correlation sign, without perturbing the data.
At each successive event update the support in this manner and continue down to λ=0. Between knots the regression coefficients are linear in λ and satisfy the sufficient KKT conditions. For a fixed support A and sign vector sA​, the equalities give βA​(λ)=(XAT​XA​)−1(XAT​y−λsA​); all sign and inactive-score constraints are linear inequalities in λ. Each support/sign pattern therefore has an interval of validity, and there are only finitely many such patterns, so the complete path has finitely many linear segments. Together with the initial zero solution for λ≥λmax​, these interpolated segments give
the entire unique Lasso regularization path for every λ>0.​
(43)
