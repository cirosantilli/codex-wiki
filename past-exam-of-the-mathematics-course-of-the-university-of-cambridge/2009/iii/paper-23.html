Let D:I→C be a finite diagram. Form the finite categorical products
P=∏i∈ObI​D(i),Q=∏u:i→j∈MorI​D(j).
(1)
There are two morphisms s,t:P⇉Q: their components at u:i→j are D(u)πi​ and πj​, respectively. Let e:L→P be their equaliser. Then ℓi​=πi​e satisfy D(u)ℓi​=ℓj​, so they constitute a cone over a diagram.
Any other cone xi​:X→D(i) uniquely determines x:X→P. Its cone equations say precisely that sx=tx. The equaliser therefore gives a unique h:X→L with eh=x, and thus ℓi​h=xi​. Conversely these component equations force eh=x by the categorical product universal property, so there is exactly one such mediator. This proves that (L,ℓi​) is a categorical limit of D.
The empty categorical products are included: if I is empty, P and Q are terminal and the construction gives the empty categorical limit. Thus finite categorical products and equalisers give every finite categorical limit, as a special case of the construction of small limits from products and equalizers.

Let 1 be a terminal object. The pullback of the unique arrows A→1←B is a binary categorical product: maps X→A and X→B automatically have equal composites into 1, so the pullback universal property is exactly the categorical product universal property.
For parallel arrows f,g:A⇉B, form B×B and take the pullback of ⟨f,g⟩:A→B×B along the diagonal δ:B→B×B. Write the resulting projections as e:E→A and b:E→B. The pullback equation gives
fe=b=ge.
(2)
If u:X→A satisfies fu=gu, the pair (u,fu) is a pullback cone, producing a unique v:X→E with ev=u and bv=fu. The second condition is forced by the first, since b=fe. Thus e is the equaliser of f,g.
The terminal object and iterated binary categorical products give all finite categorical products, including the nullary one. Part (a) therefore proves pullbacks and a terminal object give all finite categorical limits.

For a diagram shape I, a functor F:C→D preserves categorical limits of shape I if every limiting cone (L,ℓi​) over every diagram D:I→C is carried to a limiting cone (FL,Fℓi​) over FD. It reflects categorical limits of that shape if a cone in C is limiting whenever its image cone is limiting. These properties concern cones which are given; reflection is not an assertion that every cone in the target lifts.
Suppose F is a full and faithful functor and the image of (L,ℓi​) is limiting. A cone (X,xi​) in C becomes a cone in D, so there is a unique h:FX→FL with (Fℓi​)h=Fxi​. Fullness writes h=Fk for some k:X→L. Faithfulness gives ℓi​k=xi​, and also ensures uniqueness: two such k would have the same image, by uniqueness of h. Hence the original cone is limiting. This proves every full and faithful functor reflects categorical limits, the limits reflected by full and faithful functors criterion, with no essential-surjectivity requirement.

Write an object of the slice category D/X as a:A→X. Its morphisms are arrows in D commuting with these structure maps, and the forgetful functor UX​ removes the structure map.
Suppose a square in the slice becomes a pullback square in D, with vertex p:P→X. Given a compatible pair of slice arrows u:Z→A and v:Z→B, the underlying pullback gives a unique h:Z→P with the required projection equations. If r:P→A is one projection, then
ph=arh=au=z,
(3)
where z:Z→X is the structure map. Thus h is automatically a slice morphism, and underlying uniqueness gives uniqueness in the slice. Hence UX​ reflects pullbacks.
Now let e:(E,c)→(A,a) be an equaliser of slice arrows f,g:(A,a)⇉(B,b). Consider any underlying arrow u:Z→A with fu=gu. Give Z the structure map au:Z→X. Then u is a slice arrow into (A,a), and the slice equaliser gives a unique h:Z→E with eh=u. Conversely any underlying h with that equation automatically obeys ch=aeh=au, so there are no additional factorization possibilities outside the slice. Therefore UX​e has the ordinary equaliser universal property in D. This proves UX​ preserves equalisers.

A Cartesian closed category has finite categorical products and, for every P,Q, an exponential object QP with an evaluation morphism ev:QP×P→Q such that composition with evaluation gives natural bijections
E(R,QP)≅E(R×P,Q).
(10)
Finite categorical products in the presheaf category are pointwise; in particular its terminal object is the constant singleton categorical presheaf. For categorical presheaves P,Q define
(QP)(c)=Nat(Hc​×P,Q).​
(11)
For u:d→c, restriction sends α to α∘(Hu​×1P​). Composition and identity laws follow from those of the Yoneda embedding, so this is a categorical presheaf.
Define evaluation at c by evc​(α,x)=αc​(1c​,x). For u:d→c, naturality of α gives Q(u)αc​(1c​,x)=αd​(u,P(u)x), which is the evaluation of the restricted pair. Hence evaluation is a natural transformation.
Given t:R×P→Q, its currying sends z∈R(c) to the transformation whose component at a is
(tc​(z))a​(v,x)=ta​(R(v)z,x)(v:a→c, x∈P(a)).
(12)
Naturality in a follows from that of t, and naturality in c follows by composing v with the relevant arrow. Evaluating t at (1c​,x) recovers tc​(z,x). Conversely, if s:R→QP, naturality of s along v:a→c says that evaluating sa​(R(v)z) at (1a​,x) gives (sc​(z))a​(v,x). Thus currying the evaluation of s recovers s. These constructions are inverse and natural, proving the universal property. This is the exponential in a presheaf category, and proves the presheaf category is Cartesian closed.

A poset becomes a category whose objects are its elements and whose hom-set X(x,y) is a singleton if x≤y and empty otherwise. Reflexivity supplies identity arrows and transitivity supplies composition; all possible composites are unique, so the category axioms hold. Antisymmetry says that isomorphic objects are equal. A functor between such categories is exactly an order-preserving function: an inequality must be sent to an inequality, and there is no further choice of arrow map.

Since the hom-sets in these categories are either empty or singleton, an adjunction f⊣g is precisely the condition
f(x)≤y⟺x≤g(y)(x∈X, y∈Y).​
(23)
The hom-set bijections are then the unique possible ones, and naturality is automatic. This is a Galois connection between the two posets.
Equivalently, the monotone functions satisfy the unit and counit inequalities x≤gf(x) and fg(y)≤y. The equivalence implies these by taking y=f(x) and x=g(y). Conversely, if the inequalities hold, f(x)≤y implies x≤gf(x)≤g(y), while x≤g(y) implies f(x)≤fg(y)≤y.

The Freyd general adjoint functor theorem is as follows. Let G:D→C, with D a complete category which is locally small. Then G has a left adjoint if and only if it preserves all small categorical limits and satisfies the solution-set condition: for each c∈C, there is a set-indexed family
ui​:c→Gdi​
(33)
such that every u:c→Gd can be written u=G(h)ui​ for some i and some h:di​→d. The target category need not be complete. We prove all the existence steps.
First establish the initial-object lemma for complete categories with a weakly initial set. Let E be locally small and complete, with a weakly initial set {wi​}, meaning every object receives a map from at least one wi​. Its categorical product w=∏i​wi​ is weakly initial: compose a categorical product projection with the map from the appropriate wi​. Local smallness makes E(w,w) a set. Let e:v→w be the simultaneous equaliser of every endomorphism of w and the identity. Explicitly it is the equaliser of the two arrows
w⇉∏k∈E(w,w)​w
(34)
with components k and 1w​. Thus ke=e for every endomorphism k, and e is monic.
Every object x receives a morphism from v, by composing e with a map w→x. To prove uniqueness, take parallel maps a,b:v→x and their equaliser m:y→v. Weak initiality of w gives t:w→y. The composite emt is an endomorphism of w, so (emt)e=e. Cancelling the monomorphism e gives mte=1v​. Hence m is both monic and a split epimorphism, and is therefore invertible. Since am=bm, this forces a=b. We have proved v is initial, without using a class-sized categorical product or equaliser.
For sufficiency in the theorem, fix c and consider the comma category (c↓G). Its objects are pairs (d,u:c→Gd); a morphism h:(d,u)→(d′,u′) satisfies G(h)u=u′. This category is locally small because each hom-set is a subset of D(d,d′). It is complete: for a small diagram of such pairs, take the underlying categorical limit d in D. The arrows from c form a cone to its image under G, and preservation of the categorical limit gives a unique u:c→Gd inducing them. The same universal property gives all mediators in the comma category. The solution-set family is a weakly initial set in this comma category, so the lemma supplies an initial pair
(Fc,ηc​:c→GFc).
(35)
For f:c→c′, initiality defines a unique Ff:Fc→Fc′ satisfying G(Ff)ηc​=ηc′​f. Uniqueness proves the identity and composition laws, making F a functor. Initiality also gives bijections
D(Fc,d)≅C(c,Gd),h⟼G(h)ηc​.​
(36)
The definition of Ff proves naturality in c, and postcomposition proves naturality in d. These bijections are the required adjunction F⊣G.
For necessity, if F⊣G, the single arrow ηc​:c→GFc is a solution set: the transpose of any u:c→Gd gives its factorization through ηc​. If d is a limiting cone vertex of a small diagram Dj​ in D, then, naturally in c,
C(c,Gd)≅D(Fc,d)≅limj​D(Fc,Dj​)≅limj​C(c,GDj​).
(37)
These are the canonical cone-factorization bijections, so Gd with its image projections is a categorical limit of GDj​. Thus G preserves all small categorical limits, completing both directions of the theorem.

A monad on C consists of an endofunctor T:C→C and natural transformations η:1C​⇒T and μ:T2⇒T satisfying, at every object A,
μA​TηA​=1TA​=μA​ηTA​,μA​TμA​=μA​μTA​.​
(38)
These are the unit and associativity laws for the unit and multiplication of a monad.
An algebra for a monad is a pair (A,a) with a:TA→A satisfying
aηA​=1A​,aμA​=aTa.​
(39)
A monad algebra morphism f:(A,a)→(B,b) is a morphism f:A→B with fa=bTf. Identities satisfy this condition, and composing two such maps preserves it by functoriality of T. The resulting Eilenberg-Moore category is denoted CT. Its forgetful functor U:CT→C sends (A,a) to A and leaves the underlying arrows unchanged.

The right side says exactly that x:(A,a)→Kd is a monad algebra morphism.
For sufficiency, choose a coequalizer qa​:FA→L(A,a) for every monad algebra. Its universal property and the equivalence just proved give natural bijections
D(L(A,a),d)≅{h:FA→d:hFa=hεFA​}≅CT((A,a),Kd).
(55)
To see explicitly that the chosen objects form a functor, an monad algebra morphism u:(A,a)→(B,b) makes qb​Fu coequalize the pair for a: use ua=bTu and naturality of the counit. Hence there is a unique Lu with Luqa​=qb​Fu. Uniqueness proves its identity and composition laws and the naturality of the displayed bijections. Thus L⊣K.
Conversely, suppose L⊣K with unit λ(A,a)​:(A,a)→KL(A,a). Transpose its underlying arrow A→GL(A,a) through F⊣G to obtain qa​:FA→L(A,a). Since the unit is an monad algebra morphism, the equivalence above shows that qa​ coequalizes the specified pair. Any coequalizing h:FA→d transposes to an monad algebra map (A,a)→Kd, which factors uniquely through the unit by L⊣K. Transposing back gives a unique factorization of h through qa​. Thus qa​ is the required coequalizer. This proves both directions of the Left adjoint to the Eilenberg-Moore comparison functor criterion.

Assume the coequalizers from part (b) exist and write them qa​:FA→L(A,a). The underlying arrow of the comparison-adjunction unit is
λa​=Gqa​ηA​:A→GL(A,a).
(56)
We first relate it to the monad algebra action. Naturality of η, the coequalizer equation and a monad unit law give
λa​a​=Gqa​ηA​a=Gqa​,TaηTA​=G(qa​Fa)ηTA​=G(qa​εFA​)ηTA​=Gqa​μA​ηTA​=Gqa​.​
(57)
In C, a:TA→A is itself a split coequalizer of Ta,μA​:T2A⇉TA. Indeed,
aTa=aμA​,aηA​=1A​,μA​ηTA​=1TA​,TaηTA​=ηA​a.
(58)
For an explicit universal-property proof, if r:TA→X satisfies rTa=rμA​, then s=rηA​ obeys
sa=rηA​a=rTaηTA​=rμA​ηTA​=r.
(59)
Its uniqueness follows by composing any equation sa=r with ηA​.
Now Gqa​ equalizes Ta and μA​. If it is a coequalizer, then a and Gqa​ are coequalizers of the same pair. Their unique comparison map is λa​, since λa​a=Gqa​, and is therefore invertible. Conversely, if λa​ is invertible, Gqa​=λa​a is an isomorphic copy of the coequalizer a, so it is a coequalizer too.
The forgetful functor CT→C reflects isomorphisms: if an monad algebra morphism u has an inverse as an underlying arrow, the equation ua=bTu rearranges to u−1b=aT(u−1), making that inverse an monad algebra morphism. Thus underlying invertibility here is exactly invertibility of the unit component. For all monad algebras together, the comparison-adjunction unit criterion is
λ is invertible⟺G preserves every specified coequalizer qa​.​
(60)
Equivalently, the necessary-and-sufficient conditions for a left adjoint with invertible unit are existence of the coequalizers in part (b) and their preservation by G. No preservation of arbitrary coequalizers is required.
