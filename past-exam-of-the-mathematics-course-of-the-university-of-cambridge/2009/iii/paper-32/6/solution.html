A unit-face-value zero-coupon bond pays one unit of currency at its maturity T and makes no earlier payments. Write its time-t price as P(t,T), with P(T,T)=1. The instantaneous forward rate is related to the bond price by
ft​(T)=−∂T​logP(t,T),P(t,T)=exp(−∫tT​ft​(u)du).​
(43)
The short rate is rt​=ft​(t), and the money-market account is Bt​=exp(∫0t​rs​ds).
Apply Itô formula to ft​(T)=F(T−t,rt​), holding maturity fixed:
dft​(T)=(−Fθ​+a(rt​)Fr​+21​b(rt​)2Frr​)dt+b(rt​)Fr​dWt​.
(44)
The supplied equation identifies its volatility and drift as
σt​(T)=b(rt​)Fr​(T−t,rt​),αt​(T)=σt​(T)∫tT​σt​(u)du.
(45)
This is exactly the one-factor Heath-Jarrow-Morton model drift restriction with zero market price of risk. The initial condition also verifies ft​(t)=F(0,rt​)=rt​.
To verify absence of arbitrage directly, without merely naming the drift restriction, put
G(θ,r)=∫0θ​F(s,r)ds,Σt​(T)=b(rt​)Gr​(T−t,rt​).
(46)
Integrate the equation in its first variable. Since F(0,r)=r and
∫0θ​Fr​(s,r)(∫0s​Fr​(u,r)du)ds=21​Gr​(θ,r)2,
(47)
we obtain
F(θ,r)−r=a(r)Gr​+21​b(r)2Grr​−21​b(r)2Gr2​.
(48)
The bond price from the forward curve is P(t,T)=e−G(T−t,rt​). Its logarithm therefore satisfies
dlogP(t,T)=(F−aGr​−21​b2Grr​)dt−bGr​dWt​=(rt​−21​Σt​(T)2)dt−Σt​(T)dWt​.
(49)
A second application of Itô formula gives
P(t,T)dP(t,T)​=rt​dt−Σt​(T)dWt​,P(t,T)/Bt​d(P(t,T)/Bt​)​=−Σt​(T)dWt​.​
(50)
Thus every discounted bond price is a positive local martingale under the original measure. The sufficient no-arbitrage theorem is: if a common equivalent probability measure makes all discounted traded prices local martingales, then there is no arbitrage using self-financing strategies whose discounted wealth has a deterministic lower bound. Its proof is the supermartingale bound on such wealth. Apply it to any finite collection of bond maturities, using the original measure itself. This establishes no admissible arbitrage in the bond market, on horizons where the stated short rate model and smooth forward curve are defined. It is the Forward-rate equation for a Markov short-rate diffusion construction.
For the constant-coefficient case, substitute F(θ,r)=A(θ)r+B(θ). The initial condition requires A(0)=1 and B(0)=0. Since Fr​=A and Frr​=0, the equation becomes
A′(θ)r+B′(θ)=a0​A(θ)−b02​A(θ)∫0θ​A(s)ds.
(51)
The right-hand side does not depend on r, so A′=0 and A=1. It follows that B′=a0​−b02​θ, giving
A(θ)=1,B(θ)=a0​θ−21​b02​θ2,ft​(T)=rt​+a0​(T−t)−21​b02​(T−t)2.​
(52)
The corresponding zero-coupon bond price is
P(t,T)=exp(−(T−t)rt​−2a0​​(T−t)2+6b02​​(T−t)3).​
(53)
It has discounted volatility −b0​(T−t), which is deterministic and square-integrable on finite horizons. Its discounted price is consequently a true martingale, so the no-arbitrage conclusion follows again. This is the Gaussian short-rate model with constant coefficients.
As a separate check of the signs, conditional on Ft​,
∫tT​rs​ds=(T−t)rt​+2a0​​(T−t)2+b0​∫tT​(Ws​−Wt​)ds.
(54)
