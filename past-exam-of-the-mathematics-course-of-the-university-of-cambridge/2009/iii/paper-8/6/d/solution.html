Amplify the chiral phase by the identity on the auxiliary C2, and let P act by multiplication on each chirality. The displayed projection has image line Ez​=C(1,z). On the chart w=1/z near infinity it has frame (w,1). On the equator the normalized second frame is z times the first, so its clutching function has winding +1. Equivalently it is the conjugate of the tautological degree-−1 line. Thus E has degree +1 with the complex orientation fixed in part (c).
It is equivariant for the conjugate defining action on the auxiliary C2; this representation is equivalent to the defining representation of SU(2). At z=0 its isotropy character under diag(eit,e−it) is e−it. Tensoring with the positive spinor weight +1 gives weight zero on S+⊗E, whereas tensoring with the negative weight −1 gives weight −2 on S−⊗E.
The compressed map is
PVP:P(H+​⊗C2)⟶P(H−​⊗C2).
(37)
Its parametrix is PV∗P: the two products equal the respective identity P modulo compacts, since every matrix entry of P is a continuous function and the phase commutators in part (c) are compact. Hence this is an equivariant Fredholm operator.
Use the precisely stated homogeneous-bundle multiplicity rule from part (c). The domain, of weight zero, decomposes as E0​⊕E2​⊕E4​⊕⋯. The target, of weight −2, decomposes as E2​⊕E4​⊕⋯. Part (b) subtracts these multiplicities, leaving only the trivial representation. Therefore
indG​(PVP)=[E0​],ind(PVP)=1.​
(38)
This computes the index from the actual projection and representation weights without invoking an index theorem that merely supplies the requested number. Reversing the chiral direction gives −1. If V instead denotes the full self-adjoint phase on H+​⊕H−​, its compression is self-adjoint Fredholm and has index zero; the nonzero answer uses the standard chiral interpretation made explicit above.
