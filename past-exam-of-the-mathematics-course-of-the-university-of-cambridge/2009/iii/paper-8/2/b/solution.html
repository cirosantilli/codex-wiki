Apply part (a) to the compact positive operator T∗T. Its positive square root B=∣T∣ is defined by multiplying each eigenvector of eigenvalue λ by λ​ and vanishing on the kernel. The eigenvalues tend to zero, so B is compact. For every x, ∥Tx∥2=⟨T∗Tx,x⟩=∥Bx∥2.
Consequently U(Bx)=Tx is a well-defined isometry on ranB. Extend it to the closure of that range and set it to zero on its orthogonal complement. This gives a partial isometry with initial space (kerT)⊥, final space ranT, and
T=U∣T∣,kerU=kerT.​
(12)
For uniqueness, the positive factor in any such polar decomposition of a bounded operator satisfies B2=T∗T. The positive-square-root uniqueness proved in 4(b) fixes B. The relation U(Bx)=Tx then fixes U on the dense subset ranB of its initial space and the kernel convention fixes it elsewhere. This proves uniqueness with the canonical initial-space condition. Without that condition the word “unique” would be false: T=0 can be written U⋅0 with many different unitary U.
