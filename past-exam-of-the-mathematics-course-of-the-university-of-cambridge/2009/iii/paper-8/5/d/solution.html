Past exam of the mathematics course of the University of Cambridge / 2009 / iii / Paper 8 / 5 / d / Solution

 ... Past exam of the mathematics course of the University of Cambridge 2009 iii Paper 8 5 d
OurBigBook.com Words: 305
Work with bounded self-adjoint operators on separable infinite-dimensional Hilbert spaces, the setting in which the classification holds. If UTU∗−S is compact for a unitary U, their images in the Calkin algebra have identical spectra, by compact perturbation invariance and unitary conjugation. Thus their essential spectra coincide.
Conversely apply the Weyl-von Neumann theorem to write T=Da​+Ka​ and S=Db​+Kb​ in orthonormal bases, with bounded real diagonal sequences and compact errors. The essential spectrum of a diagonal operator is exactly its sequence cluster set. Indeed outside that cluster set all but finitely many entries stay a positive distance from λ, so the diagonal has an inverse on a finite-codimensional subspace and is Fredholm. At a cluster point, infinitely many distinct basis vectors have (D−λ)ej​→0; these are weakly null unit vectors, contradicting any Fredholm parametrix. This also handles a value with infinite multiplicity.
The two diagonal sequences therefore have the same cluster set. Part (c) provides permutations with amk​​−bnk​​→0. Send the mk​th basis vector for T to the nk​th basis vector for S. Under this unitary, the diagonal difference has entries tending to zero and is compact, by finite-rank truncation. Adding the two conjugated compact errors gives UTU∗−S compact​.
Without separability, equality of essential spectra need not suffice, even on the same Hilbert space. Let H have uncountable dimension and let T,S be projections with infinite-dimensional kernels, but with range dimensions respectively countable and uncountable. Both essential spectra are {0,1}. After any unitary conjugation the range of T is still separable. If its difference from S were compact, the range of S would lie in the sum of that separable range and the separable closure of the compact error's range, which is impossible. Finite-dimensional spaces also need a separate dimension convention, since essential spectra are then empty. These are genuine qualifications to a statement made without the standard separable infinite-dimensional setting.
