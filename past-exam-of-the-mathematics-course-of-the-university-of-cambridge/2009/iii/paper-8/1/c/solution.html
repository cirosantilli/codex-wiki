OurBigBook.com Words: 207
Fix a Fredholm operator T. Split its domain as X⊕N=(kerT)⊥⊕kerT and its target as Y⊕M=ranT⊕kerT∗. For a sufficiently small norm perturbation R, write
T+R=(AC​BD​),
(3)
where A:X→Y remains invertible by the Neumann series. Invertible triangular row and column operations reduce this matrix to
(A0​0D−CA−1B​).
(4)
The second block maps the finite-dimensional space N into M. Hence the perturbed operator has closed range, finite kernel and cokernel, and
ind(T+R)=dimN−dimM=indT,
(5)
since the rank of that finite block cancels from the difference. Thus the Fredholm set is open and its index is locally constant, hence norm continuous.
If S,T are Fredholm, their product is Fredholm by multiplying their invertible images in the Calkin algebra. For index additivity use the following finite-dimensional exact sequence:
0→kerT→kerSTT​kerSq​cokerTS​cokerST→cokerS→0.
(6)
Here q is reduction modulo ranT, the induced S-map sends [x] to [Sx], and the last map is the quotient by the larger range ranS. These maps are well-defined. For example, kerq=kerS∩ranT is the image of the preceding map. If Sx∈ranST, subtracting a suitable Ty makes x−Ty∈kerS, proving exactness at cokerT. The other positions follow directly from the definitions. Alternating dimensions in this exact sequence gives
ind(ST)=indS+indT.​
(7)
