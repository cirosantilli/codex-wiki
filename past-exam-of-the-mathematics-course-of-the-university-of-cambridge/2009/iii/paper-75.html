The homogeneous fixed point must satisfy v=u2. The singular point u=v=0 is outside the domain of the reaction term, so substitution in the other equation gives the unique regular spatially homogeneous equilibrium
u∗​=1+r,v∗​=(1+r)2.​
(1)
This is the basally forced quadratic activator-inhibitor model. The linearization of its reaction terms gives the Jacobian matrix
J=​r+1r−1​2q(1+r)​−(1+r)2r​−q​​.
(2)
Put a=(r−1)/(r+1), so −1<a<1. Its trace and determinant are a−q and q, and its eigenvalues are
λ±​=2a−q±(a−q)2−4q​​.
(3)
Since q>0, both eigenvalues have negative real part exactly when q>a. Hence
q>r+1r−1​: linearly asymptotically stable;0<q<r+1r−1​: unstable.​
(4)
For r≤1 the equilibrium is always strictly linearly stable. For r>1, equality q=a is an oscillatory threshold with λ±​=±ia​; the nonlinear qualification at this threshold is proved below.
Now insert a perturbation proportional to eλt+ikx. The diffusion terms subtract k2diag(1,p) from J. With s=k2 the mode trace and determinant are
τ(s)=a−q−(1+p)s,Δ(s)=ps2+(q−pa)s+q.
(5)
For a strictly stable homogeneous state, q>a makes τ(s)<0 for all s≥0. A nonzero Fourier mode can therefore grow only if Δ(s)<0, in which case its two real eigenvalues have opposite signs. The determinant parabola has its minimum at s∗​=(pa−q)/(2p), and that minimum is q−(pa−q)2/(4p). The two-species Turing criterion is consequently derived here as
q>a,pa>q,(pa−q)2>4pq.​
(6)
These force a>0, hence r>1. Equivalently, solving pa−q>2pq​ for p​ gives
r>1,q>a,p>pc​=(1+a​−1)2q​.​
(7)
Thus the inhibitor in a reaction-diffusion system must diffuse sufficiently fast. Inside this region, the growing wavenumbers obey
s−​<k2<s+​,s±​=2ppa−q±(pa−q)2−4pq​​.
(8)
At onset, the minimum touches zero and the two determinant roots coalesce. Therefore
kc2​=2ppa−q​=pq​​,λc​=kc​2π​=2π(qp​)1/4.​
(9)
Here p is evaluated on the onset surface. These are the Turing threshold of a basally forced quadratic activator-inhibitor model and its continuum critical wavelength. On a finite or domain with periodic boundary conditions an admissible nonzero spatial mode must additionally fall in the stated band. Equality in the diffusion criterion is marginal onset, while q=a is the distinct homogeneous oscillatory threshold.
For completeness, the weak focus at the Hopf threshold of a basal activator-inhibitor model resolves the nonlinear homogeneous stability when q=a>0. Let h=(1+a)/2, β=(1−a)/2, ω=a​, and set u=(1+r)(1+x), v=(1+r)2(1+y), X=x, Y=(hy−ax)/ω. Expanding the rational reaction term to cubic order gives
X˙=−ωY+F2​+F3​+O(4),Y˙=ωX+G2​+G3​+O(4),
(10)
where
F2​=h(βX−ωY)2​,F3​=−h2(aX+ωY)(βX−ωY)2​,G2​=ω(hX2−F2​),G3​=−ωF3​.
(11)
Write X=ϱcosθ, Y=ϱsinθ and evaluate these homogeneous polynomials on the unit circle. If A=cosθF2​+sinθG2​, B=cosθF3​+sinθG3​, and C=cosθG2​−sinθF2​, division of the radial and angular equations gives
dθdϱ​=ωA​ϱ2+(ωB​−ω2AC​)ϱ3+O(ϱ4).
(12)
The angular integral of A is zero because it changes sign under θ↦θ+π. Iterating its quadratic term once over a full cycle contributes the square of this zero integral, so the first nonzero return map term is cubic. Using ⟨cos4θ⟩=3/8, ⟨cos2θsin2θ⟩=1/8 and the corresponding sixth moments gives ⟨B⟩=a/4 and ⟨AC⟩/ω=(a+1)/8. The Poincaré map is
ϱ⟼ϱ+ω2π​8a−1​ϱ3+O(ϱ4).
(13)
Because 0<a<1, sufficiently small positive radii decrease on every return. The equilibrium at q=a is therefore nonlinearly asymptotically stable, with weak rather than exponential attraction, despite its neutral linear eigenvalues. The strict inequalities above describe the ordinary diffusion-driven instability from a strictly linearly stable homogeneous state.

Write the reaction term as f(u;r)=u(1−u)(u−r) and assume D>0. At r=1/2, multiply the stationary travelling-wave reduction of a reaction-diffusion system DU′′+f(U;1/2)=0 by U′ and integrate once. Since U′=0 at both asymptotic phases and ∫0U​f(s;1/2)ds=−U2(1−U)2/4, the integration constant is zero:
2D​(U′)2−41​U2(1−U)2=0.
(14)
The boundary orientation selects U′=−U(1−U)/2D​. Separating variables gives the logistic front of a bistable cubic equation, with arbitrary center q0​:
U(x)=1+exp[(x−q0​)/2D​]1​=21​[1−tanh22D​x−q0​​].​
(15)
The arbitrary translation is expected from translation invariance.

where U0​(z)=(1+ez/2​)−1. The explicit T derivative of εU1​ is second order. Since ∂r​f=−u(1−u), the first-order equation is

Multiplying the first-order equation by U0′​ and integrating by parts makes its left side zero. Therefore the translation-mode solvability for a bistable front requires
0=−c1​∫−∞∞​(U0′​)2dz+a∫−∞∞​U0′​U0​(1−U0​)dz.

Independence of the segment vectors makes the end-to-end distribution an N-fold convolution. Use the characteristic function convention ψ​(k)=∫eik⋅rψ(r)d3r. For a three-dimensional isotropic freely jointed chain, the radial delta function fixes ∣r∣=b, and angular integration gives
ψ​(k)=4π1​∫eikbcosθdΩ=21​∫−11​eikbμdμ=kbsin(kb)​.
(23)
The characteristic function of a sum of independent variables therefore gives Φ(k,N)=[sin(kb)/(kb)]N. In the central large-N scaling, k=O((bN​)−1), so
Nlnkbsin(kb)​=−6Nb2k2​+O(Nb4k4)=−6Nb2k2​+O(N−1).
(24)
Inverting this Gaussian Fourier transform yields
Φ(R,N)∼(2πNb23​)3/2exp[−2Nb23R2​].​
(25)
This Gaussian limit of the end-to-end distribution of a freely jointed chain is normalized with respect to d3R, has ⟨R⟩=0 and ⟨R2⟩=Nb2, and is valid on the coil scale R=O(bN​). If a density of the scalar distance R is wanted instead, it is 4πR2Φ. The Gaussian approximation does not describe the nearly fully stretched tail or the exact finite-chain cutoff at R=Nb.
For the scattering calculation, radial single-segment distributions together with the given independent-segment assumption imply rotational invariance of the whole chain. Therefore each pair separation S=Rn​−Rm​ has a uniform direction conditional on its magnitude S. The same angular average gives
⟨eik⋅S∣S⟩=21​∫−11​eikSμdμ=kSsin(kS)​.
(26)
Averaging its magnitude and then summing the pairs proves the required expression for the polymer scattering function:
g(k)=N1​m,n=1∑N​⟨k∣Rn​−Rm​∣sin(k∣Rn​−Rm​∣)​⟩.​
(27)
The ratio has value one at zero separation, including every diagonal term m=n.
The printed gyration formula leaves m unsummed. The intended radius of gyration must use a double pair sum, equivalently the mean square distance from the chain's center of mass. To establish that equivalence, put Rcm​=N−1∑n​Rn​ and expand both sides of
∑m,n​∣Rm​−Rn​∣2=2N∑n​∣Rn​−Rcm​∣2.
(28)
Thus the consistent definition is Rg2​=(2N2)−1∑m,n​⟨∣Rm​−Rn​∣2⟩. Expanding sinz/z=1−z2/6+O(z4) gives
g(k)​=N−6Nk2​m,n∑​⟨∣Rm​−Rn​∣2⟩+O(k4)=N[1−3k2Rg2​​+O(k4)].​
(29)
Therefore the general small-k result is
Ng(k)​=1−3k2Rg2​​+O(k4),kRg​≪1.​
(30)
This Guinier expansion of a polymer scattering function requires isotropy and finite pair moments, but not Gaussian distributions.
For a Gaussian chain, each pair difference across ℓ=∣n−m∣ links has variance ⟨∣Rn​−Rm​∣2⟩=b2ℓ and characteristic function e−k2b2ℓ/6. Using exactly the N scattering sites in the printed double sum, the finite-chain expression is
gN​(k)=1+N2​ℓ=1∑N−1​(N−ℓ)zℓ=1−z1+z​−N(1−z)22z(1−zN)​,z=e−k2b2/6.​
(31)
The closed expression is understood by continuity at z=1, where it equals N. The factor N−ℓ counts pairs with n=m+ℓ, with the prefactor two supplying their reversed order. In this finite-site convention,
Rg2​=N2b2​∑ℓ=1N−1​(N−ℓ)ℓ=6Nb2(N2−1)​.
(32)
This also holds for a freely jointed chain because its link covariances give the same mean square pair distances.
To obtain the complete long-chain scaling function, set x=Nk2b2/6≃k2Rg2​ and replace contour sums by integrals, keeping x fixed as N→∞. With a=k2b2/6,
g(k)​∼N1​∫0N​∫0N​e−a∣n−m∣dndm=N2​∫0N​(N−s)e−asds=x22N(e−x−1+x)​.​
(33)
The requested Debye scattering function for a Gaussian chain is consequently
g(k)=ND(k2Rg2​),D(x)=x22(e−x−1+x)​,D(0)=1,​
(34)
exactly for the continuous Gaussian contour convention Rg2​=Nb2/6, and asymptotically for the long discrete chain. Its small-x expansion is D(x)=1−x/3+x2/12+⋯, agreeing with the general gyration result, and D(x)∼2/x at large x. In the continuum coil range 1≪x≪N this gives g∼12/(k2b2). At still larger kb, the discrete expression instead tends to the self-scattering value one.
There is a harmless finite-size convention in the source: N links have N+1 endpoints, whereas its scattering sum counts N sites. The formulas above retain its scattering normalization; if all endpoints are counted, replace the site count by N+1 in the finite formulas. The large-N Gaussian distribution and Debye scaling function are unchanged to leading order.
