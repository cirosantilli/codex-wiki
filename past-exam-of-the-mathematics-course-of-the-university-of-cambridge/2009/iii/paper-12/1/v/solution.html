With nonempty boundary, the classical Euler-Lagrange equation also illustrates the missing condition: in dimension n≥3, on Ω={∣x∣>1}, both 0 and 1−∣x∣2−n are harmonic functions with zero boundary values. The latter has finite Dirichlet energy, because its gradient-energy integral is proportional to ∫1∞​r1−ndr, and is stationary under compactly supported variations. It approaches 1 at infinity while the zero solution approaches 0.
This is nonuniqueness of Dirichlet data without control at infinity. For a global variational formulation one must specify the admissible variations: the exterior example is not critical against every finite-energy zero-boundary variation, since varying it in its own direction changes its energy to first order. Prescribing an appropriate limit at infinity, or a variational space in which the difference is an admissible zero-boundary test function and constants are excluded, can restore uniqueness.
