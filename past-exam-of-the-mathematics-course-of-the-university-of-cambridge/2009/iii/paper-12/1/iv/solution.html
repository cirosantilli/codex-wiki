Let u,v be two critical points with the same classical boundary values, and put w=u−v. Since F is independent of z, the Euler-Lagrange equation for each is Di​Fpi​​(x,Du)=0. By the fundamental theorem of calculus along the segment between the gradients,
Fpi​​(x,Du)−Fpi​​(x,Dv)=Aij​(x)Dj​w,Aij​(x)=∫01​Fpi​pj​​(x,Dv+tDw)dt.
(8)
Subtracting the equations gives Di​(Aij​Dj​w)=0. The uniformly convex variational integrand makes A symmetric and uniformly elliptic with the same positive lower bound. Since F is smooth and u,v∈C2(Ω), A is continuously differentiable locally in Ω, so
Aij​Dij​w+(Di​Aij​)Dj​w=0
(9)
is a classical nondivergence-form elliptic operator with no zeroth-order term.
To avoid assuming that derivatives of F stay bounded all the way to the boundary, apply the proved weak maximum principle on inner open sets Ωδ​={x∈Ω:dist(x,∂Ω)>δ}. Their closures lie compactly in Ω, so all linearized coefficients there are bounded. The maximum principle applies on each component and yields
supΩδ​​∣w∣≤sup∂Ωδ​​∣w∣.
(10)
Every point of the inner boundary is at distance δ from ∂Ω. Since w is continuous on the compact closure and equals zero on that boundary, the right-hand side tends uniformly to zero as δ↓0. Every fixed interior point eventually belongs to Ωδ​, proving
u=v in Ω.​
(11)
This establishes uniqueness for uniformly convex gradient Dirichlet problems using precisely the boundary continuity and ellipticity needed for the comparison.
