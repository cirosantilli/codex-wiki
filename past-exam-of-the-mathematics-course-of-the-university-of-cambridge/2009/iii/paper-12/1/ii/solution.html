The weak maximum principle for elliptic operators gives the clipped boundary bounds
Ωsup​u≤max{0,∂Ωsup​u},Ωinf​u≥min{0,∂Ωinf​u}.​
(4)
If c=0, constants solve the equation, and applying these bounds after subtracting the boundary maximum or minimum gives the sharper conclusion that both extrema occur on the boundary. For c<0, the clipping by zero is necessary in the general statement.
To prove the upper bound, write B=∥b1​∥∞​ and C0​=∥c∥∞​, and choose α>0 large enough that λα2−Bα−C0​>0. For ϕ(x)=eαx1​, uniform ellipticity gives
Lϕ=ϕ(a11​α2+b1​α+c)≥ϕ(λα2−Bα−C0​)>0.
(5)
Put uε​=u+εϕ. Then Luε​>0. If uε​ had a positive maximum at an interior point, its gradient would vanish there and its Hessian matrix would be negative semidefinite. Only the symmetric part of aij​ contracts with this Hessian, and its positive definiteness implies aij​Dij​uε​≤0. Also cuε​≤0. These facts would give Luε​≤0, a contradiction.
Since Ω is compact and uε​ is continuous on it, its maximum exists. It is therefore either nonpositive or attained on the boundary, proving supΩ​uε​≤max{0,sup∂Ω​uε​}. The exponential is bounded on the bounded domain; letting ε↓0 proves the upper estimate. Apply the same argument to −u for the lower estimate. This is the exponential perturbation proof of the weak maximum principle with drift and requires no continuity of the coefficient functions.
