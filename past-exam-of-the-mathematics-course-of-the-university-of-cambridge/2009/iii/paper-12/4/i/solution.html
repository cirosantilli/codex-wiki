We prove the Interior second-derivative estimate for the Poisson equation using difference quotients, rather than differentiating the L2 forcing. Fix a coordinate ek​ and write δh​v(x)=(v(x+hek​)−v(x))/h. Choose a smooth cutoff function η equal to one on Ω′ with support compactly contained in Ω, leaving room for all translations with sufficiently small ∣h∣. We can arrange ∣Dη∣≤C(n)/d, where d=dist(Ω′,∂Ω).
The standard difference quotient lemmas give discrete integration by parts, ∫aδ−h​b=−∫(δh​a)b, and ∥δh​v∥2​≤∥Dk​v∥2​ on the relevant enlarged set. In the weak formulation ∫Du⋅Dφ=−∫fφ, use the admissible test function φ=−δ−h​(η2δh​u). Moving a difference quotient onto Du gives
∫η2∣Dδh​u∣2+2∫ηδh​uDδh​u⋅Dη=∫fδ−h​(η2δh​u).
(32)
Set X=∥ηDδh​u∥2​ and Y=∥Dηδh​u∥2​. The cross term has absolute value at most 2XY. Since 0≤η≤1, the difference quotient lemma on the right gives
​∫fδ−h​(η2δh​u)​≤∥f∥2​∥Dk​(η2δh​u)∥2​≤∥f∥2​(X+2Y).
(33)
The Young inequality now implies X2≤C(Y2+∥f∥22​). Moreover Y≤C(n)d−1∥Du∥L2(Ω)​, independently of h. Hence, for every coordinate k,
∥Dδh​u∥L2(Ω′)​≤C(n,d)(∥Du∥L2(Ω)​+∥f∥L2(Ω)​).
(34)
By the Sobolev characterization by bounded difference quotients, applied to each first weak derivative on smaller interior sets, these uniform bounds give all second weak derivatives. Their bounds pass to the weak limit as h→0. Thus u∈Wloc2,2​(Ω) and
∥u∥W2,2(Ω′)​≤C(n,d)(∥u∥W1,2(Ω)​+∥f∥L2(Ω)​).​
(35)
No derivative of f is used, and no boundary condition is needed for this interior elliptic regularity argument.
