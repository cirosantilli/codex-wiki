The Cauchy-Schwarz inequality, bounded coefficients and the Poincaré inequality show that ℓ is a bounded linear functional on H. The Lax-Milgram theorem states that a bounded coercive bilinear form on a real Hilbert space represents each bounded linear functional in this way with a unique w∈H. It therefore yields u=ψ+w with the required boundary condition and weak equation. For completeness, if u1​,u2​ are solutions, z=u1​−u2​∈H01​ satisfies B(z,z)=0; coercivity and the Poincaré inequality imply z=0.
With the missing bounded-form hypothesis, the weak solution exists and is unique. For nonsymmetric matrices the printed quadratic inequalities omit the hypothesis needed for this standard formulation and proof. No maximum principle is used. In part (iii), symmetry makes the quadratic upper bound a bound on the whole matrix, so no such qualification is needed there.
