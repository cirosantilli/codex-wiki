In the ordered computational basis 00,01,10,11, the first two qubits of ∣ψ0000​⟩ have amplitudes (1,1,1,1)/2, while those of ∣ψ1111​⟩ have amplitudes (−1,1,1,1)/2. Both have the same normalized answer qubit ∣−⟩. Their inner product is therefore
⟨ψ0000​∣ψ1111​⟩=4−1+1+1+1​=21​.​
(8)
A common unitary operator U⊗I preserves this nonzero inner product. To distinguish the alternatives with certainty using the prescribed quantum measurement in the computational basis, their first-register outcome supports would have to be disjoint: every outcome possible under one alternative must be impossible under the other. Vectors with disjoint computational basis supports have zero inner product, contradicting its preservation. Thus no such U exists. This is also an instance of perfect discrimination of pure states requires orthogonality; allowing a more general measurement in quantum mechanics would not rescue this pair of states. The obstruction concerns these prepared oracle-output states, rather than every conceivable oracle-query procedure.

A classical query with known answer bit z reveals exactly the one bit f(x,y), since it returns z⊕f(x,y). For the four promised Boolean functions, the value table is
(x,y)00011011​11111​y0101​x0011​x⊕y0110​​
(9)
Every possible first query splits the four alternatives into one class of size one and another of size three. On the branch with three remaining alternatives, a second binary answer can separate at most two classes. At least two alternatives therefore still agree on that branch, regardless of how the second query was chosen adaptively. This decision tree argument proves that a procedure guaranteed to identify every alternative needs at least three queries in the worst case.
For sufficiency, query 00,01,10. The three-bit answer strings for 1,y,x,x⊕y are respectively 111,010,001,011, which are all distinct. Hence the exact classical worst-case query requirement is three. Some individual branches can terminate sooner; the lower bound does not assert that every oracle requires three queries.

List the known distinct strings in A as a0​,…,aN−1​, where N=2m, and use an m-qubit index quantum ancilla. Apply Hadamard gates to that register to prepare
∣0n⟩⊗N​1​∑j=0N−1​∣j⟩.
(19)
First, for each j with aj​=0n, apply the available unitary operator that transposes ∣0n,j⟩ and ∣aj​,j⟩. The pairs are disjoint because they have distinct index labels. Skip a transposition when the two vectors coincide. This produces
N​1​∑j​∣aj​,j⟩.
(20)
Second, for each j=0, transpose ∣aj​,j⟩ and ∣aj​,0m⟩. These pairs are disjoint because the data strings aj​ are distinct. No pair contains the term with j=0, and no later transposition moves an earlier output. The final state is exactly
N​1​j∑​∣aj​⟩⊗∣0m⟩=∣ψA​⟩⊗∣0m⟩.​
(21)
This clean subset superposition preparation uses at most N+(N−1)=2N−1 of the given transpositions and m Hadamard gates. Since m=O(log2​n), N is polynomial in n; multiplying this count by the assumed polynomial size of each transposition gives a polynomial-size quantum circuit. The index quantum ancilla returns to zero, so the data register is a pure uniform superposition state, rather than a mixture obtained by discarding its index. The construction uses an available enumeration of the known subset; a membership oracle alone would not justify that enumeration.

Using the printed measurement-vector convention, the unitary operator is
U(θ)=2​1​(11​e−iθ−e−iθ​)=Hdiag(1,e−iθ)=J(−θ).

(25)

Label the chain cluster state from top to bottom by 1,2,3,4, and let k,l,m be the phase-measurement outcomes on the first three vertices, with raw computational basis outcome s on vertex four. Repeated one-bit teleportation leaves the last qubit, up to global phase, in
∣χ⟩=XmU(γ)XlU(β)XkU(α)∣+⟩.
(30)
This sequential description applies even though the whole graph state is prepared first: a later Controlled-Z gate acts only on unmeasured qubits and commutes with earlier single-qubit measurement operators. It can therefore be moved past those measurements for the purpose of calculating each conditional branch.
Take α=θ and β=0, so U(0)=H. After the second measurement the logical state is
XlHXkU(θ)∣+⟩=XlZkHU(θ)∣+⟩.
(31)
For binary p,q, the identities proved above imply the Pauli-frame propagation along a measurement wire rule
U(η)XpZq≃XqZpU((−1)pη),
(32)
where ≃ means equality up to global phase. Choose the third angle adaptively as γ=(−1)l⊕1ϕ. Then (−1)lγ=−ϕ, and
∣χ⟩≃Xm⊕kZlU(−ϕ)HU(θ)∣+⟩.
(33)
Reading the target circuit in temporal order gives its premeasurement state
∣T⟩=U(ϕ)XHU(θ)H∣0⟩=e−iϕZU(−ϕ)HU(θ)∣+⟩.
(34)
Consequently
∣χ⟩≃Xm⊕kZl⊕1∣T⟩.
(35)

α=θ,β=0,γ=(−1)l⊕1ϕ,δ=k⊕m,r=s⊕k⊕m.​

(37)
Hence averaging over the earlier random outcomes leaves exactly the target probability distribution. This implements the desired measurement-based quantum computation without physically removing the final Pauli frame.

For nonnegative integers n, assume the storage channel over successive intervals of length τ composes as Dϵn​. This is the memoryless interpretation of the specified storage law. In the computational basis, write the density operator as
ρ=(uz​z1−u​).
(41)
Conjugation by the Pauli Z gate reverses the off-diagonal entries and leaves the diagonal entries unchanged, so
Dϵ​(ρ)=(u(1−2ϵ)z​(1−2ϵ)z1−u​).
(42)
After n applications of the phase-flip channel, the multiplier is (1−2ϵ)n. Comparing with the multiplier 1−2ϵn​ of a single phase-flip channel gives
Dϵn​=Dϵn​​,ϵn​=21−(1−2ϵ)n​.​
(43)
For n=0 this gives the identity channel. Equivalently, composing two consecutive phase errors cancels them because Z2=I, so their effective probability obeys ϵn+1​=ϵ+(1−2ϵ)ϵn​. The same expression solves this recurrence. The iterated phase-flip channel formula presumes this composition law; specifying a channel at one time alone would not constrain a memory-bearing environment at later times.

Number the three data qubits from top to bottom. It suffices initially to follow a pure input ∣ψ⟩=a∣0⟩+b∣1⟩; linearity will extend the result to every density operator. The first two controlled-NOT gates create a∣000⟩+b∣111⟩. The first layer of Hadamard gates then encodes this as
a∣+++⟩+b∣−−−⟩,
(46)
the three-qubit phase-flip repetition code. Let ei​ indicate whether a Pauli Z gate occurred on data qubit i during storage. The next layer of Hadamard gates uses HZH=X and gives
a∣e1​e2​e3​⟩+b∣1⊕e1​,1⊕e2​,1⊕e3​⟩.
(47)
The two initially zero syndrome quantum ancillas receive the adjacent parities. In the diagram's top-to-bottom order, their error syndrome is
s1​=e1​⊕e2​,s2​=e2​⊕e3​.
(48)
Both terms of the logical superposition have these same parities, so the syndrome is independent of a,b. Measuring it reveals no logical-state information.
At the marked position of R in the printed circuit, there has already been another layer of Hadamard gates on the data. There is also a layer immediately after R. Thus a desired correction Xi​ in the parity-extraction frame must be implemented as R=Zi​ at the actual recovery location, since H⊗3Zi​H⊗3=Xi​. Choose the recovery according to
(s1​,s2​)00101101​weight-zero or one pattern000100010001​complementary pattern111011101110​RIZ1​Z2​Z3​​​​

For a weight-zero or one error, the recovery removes the entire error in the parity-extraction frame. The remaining state is a∣000⟩+b∣111⟩, and the two final controlled-NOT gates give ∣ψ⟩∣00⟩.
For a weight-two or three error, minimum-weight recovery instead leaves all three bits flipped, giving a∣111⟩+b∣000⟩. The final controlled-NOT gates send this to (a∣1⟩+b∣0⟩)∣00⟩=X∣ψ⟩∣00⟩. Consequently, for every input density operator,

Independent applications of the phase-flip channel are a classical mixture of the eight physical Pauli Z gate error patterns. A specified weight-k pattern has probability ϵk(1−ϵ)3−k. The recovery from the preceding part succeeds on weight zero and one, and yields the logical Pauli X gate on weight two and three. There are three weight-two patterns and one weight-three pattern, so the logical failure probability is
pL​=3ϵ2(1−ϵ)+ϵ3=3ϵ2−2ϵ3.
(51)
Averaging the corrected branches gives the complete decoded quantum channel
ρ′=(1−3ϵ2+2ϵ3)ρ+(3ϵ2−2ϵ3)XρX.​
(52)
In particular, the leading logical error is 3ϵ2, rather than an error of first order in ϵ. For the stated range,
ϵ−pL​=ϵ(1−ϵ)(1−2ϵ)>0.
(53)
Thus the failure probability is smaller than the physical error probability. The decoded channel is a bit-flip channel; it is not DpL​​, whose error operator would be Z. This distinction follows from the logical codeword exchange in the phase-flip repetition code.
