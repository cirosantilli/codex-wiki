Conditional on Z=k, the summands are independent with the common Laplace transform of a nonnegative random variable ϕ(q). The empty sum for k=0 is zero. Therefore the compound Poisson distribution has
E[e−qY∣Z=k]=ϕ(q)k,
(51)
and applying the probability generating function from part (a) gives
LY​(q)=Ee−qY=exp{λ[ϕ(q)−1]}(q≥0).​
(52)
If m=EX1​<∞, differentiating at zero from the right gives ϕ′(0+)=−m and LY′​(0+)=−λm. Hence
EY=λEX1​.​
(53)
The differentiation is justified by (1−e−qX1​)/q≤X1​ and the dominated convergence theorem. No finite moment was assumed in the question, so the formula must also be interpreted when m=∞. For any nonnegative random variable V, the monotone convergence theorem gives
EV=limq↓0​q1−Ee−qV​∈[0,∞].
(54)
Apply this to X1​ and Y, and use 1−e−λh∼λh as h↓0, with h=1−ϕ(q); it yields the same formula with value +∞ when m=∞. If m=0, nonnegativity makes all marks zero almost surely and the result is immediate.
For the second method, condition on the count rather than differentiating a transform. For finite m, the tower property of conditional expectation gives E[Y∣Z]=Zm, and EZ=λ gives EY=λm. More generally, Tonelli's theorem and independence give
EY=∑j=1∞​E[Xj​1{Z≥j}​]=m∑j=1∞​P(Z≥j)=mEZ.
(55)
For m=∞, already the term j=1 is infinite since P(Z≥1)>0. Thus the two methods agree, including in the extended-valued case.
