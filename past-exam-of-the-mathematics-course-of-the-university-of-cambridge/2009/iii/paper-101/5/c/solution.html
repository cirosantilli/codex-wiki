As in the intended independent-mark construction, take the entire sequence (Xj​) independent of the Poisson process, not merely independent of one fixed count. Then Y is a Compound Poisson process. For deterministic s<t, its increment Yt​−Ys​ is independent of Fs​=σ(Yu​:0≤u≤s). To justify this carefully, first condition on Ns​=k. The past of Y uses only the counting history up to s and marks among X1​,…,Xk​. The future count has independent rate-λ Poisson process increments, and future marks Xk+1​,Xk+2​,… are fresh with the common law. Their joint conditional law is the same for every k and every past history, so the increment is independent even of this larger marked history, hence of Fs​. Marks equal to zero do not cause a problem: Fs​ may then reveal less than the full counting history.
By part (b), with Poisson parameter λ(t−s),
Ee−q(Yt​−Ys​)=exp{λ(t−s)[ϕ(q)−1]}.
(56)
The proposed process is adapted to Ft​, nonnegative, and
E∣Zt​∣=eλt[1−ϕ(q)]Ee−qYt​=1<∞.
(57)
In particular, no finite expectation of a mark is required. Also Zs​>0, and
Zs​Zt​​=exp{−q(Yt​−Ys​)+λ(t−s)[1−ϕ(q)]}
(58)
is independent of Fs​ and has mean one. Therefore the exponential Laplace martingale of a compound Poisson process satisfies
E[Zt​∣Fs​]=Zs​E[Zt​/Zs​]=Zs​(0≤s<t).​
(59)
This proves every condition for a continuous-time martingale in the requested filtration. When q=0, ϕ(0)=1 and the same conclusion reduces to the constant martingale Zt​=1.
