One constructive definition of a rate-λ Poisson process uses independent and identically distributed exponential random variables Ej​ of rate λ. Set T0​=0, Tk​=∑j=1k​Ej​ and
Nt​=max{k≥0:Tk​≤t}.

The strong law of large numbers gives Tk​/k→1/λ almost surely, so the maximum is finite at every finite t. The process starts at zero, has right-continuous nondecreasing integer paths, and jumps by one at the arrival times.
For k≥1, exactly k arrivals by t means Tk​≤t<Tk+1​. Integrating the first k exponential gaps and the survival probability of the next gap gives

At a fixed time s, conditional on the counting history, the residual time until the next arrival has rate-λ exponential distribution, independently of that history, by the memoryless property. After that arrival all subsequent gaps are fresh independent exponentials. Thus the process of future counts Ns+u​−Ns​ has the same law as Nu​ and is independent of the past. This proves stationary independent increments, the equivalent usual defining properties of the Poisson process, and gives
