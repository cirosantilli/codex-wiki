The global drift hypothesis as printed is impossible on a finite state space. Indeed, let x∗​ minimize f. Every possible value of f(Xn+1​) is at least f(x∗​), so
E[f(Xn+1​)∣Xn​=x∗​]≥f(x∗​),
(23)
contradicting a decrease by δ>0. Equivalently, its formal iteration would give Ex​f(Xn​)=f(x)−nδ, whereas f is bounded on the finite state space. This is the constant nonzero drift is impossible on a finite Markov chain obstruction. Thus there is no genuine chain satisfying all the literal premises.
The meaningful intended version imposes the drift only on S∖D, and stops at D. In the natural filtration Fn​=σ(X0​,…,Xn​), the Markov property then gives, on {T>n},
E[f(Xn+1​)−f(Xn​)∣Fn​]=−δ.
(24)
The correct stopped martingale is
Yn​=f(Xn∧T​)+δ(n∧T).​
(25)
It is adapted and integrable, since f is bounded and n∧T≤n. Its increment equals
Yn+1​−Yn​=1{T>n}​(f(Xn+1​)−f(Xn​)+δ),
(26)
whose conditional expectation is zero. Taking expectations yields
Ex​f(Xn∧T​)+δEx​(n∧T)=f(x).
(27)
Since T<∞ almost surely and f(XT​)=0, the bounded convergence theorem gives Ex​f(Xn∧T​)→0. The monotone convergence theorem gives Ex​(n∧T)↑Ex​T, without presupposing its finiteness. Consequently the constant-drift hitting-time identity is
Ex​T=δf(x)​<∞.​
(28)
For x∈D, both sides are zero. Under the repaired hypothesis the unstopped f(Xn​)+nδ need not be a martingale after hitting D; it is the displayed stopped martingale that proves the result.
