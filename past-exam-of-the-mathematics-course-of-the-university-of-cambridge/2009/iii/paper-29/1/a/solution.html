The previsible sigma-algebra P on Ω×[0,∞) is generated by E×{0} with E∈F0​ and by E×(s,t] with E∈Fs​, 0≤s<t. Equivalently it is the smallest sigma-algebra making every left-continuous adapted process measurable. A previsible process is a process measurable for P.
A bounded simple predictable process has finitely many time intervals and bounded coefficients known at their left endpoints. One may use deterministic endpoints, or the customary more general elementary predictable process with stopping-time intervals:

When a stopping time is infinite, use the terminal value M∞​, which exists in L2 and almost surely for an L2-bounded continuous martingale. Refining intervals leaves this definition unchanged.
For a continuous local martingale M, its quadratic variation [M] is the unique continuous adapted nondecreasing process starting at zero such that (Mt​−M0​)2−[M]t​ is a local martingale. It is predictable because it is continuous and adapted. To recover it from the path, use refining deterministic grids, for example tk(n)​=k2−n, and form
