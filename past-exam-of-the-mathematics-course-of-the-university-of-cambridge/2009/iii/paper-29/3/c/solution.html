The clock At​=∫0t​e2βs​ds is continuous, strictly increasing and unbounded. Define τr​=inf{t:At​≥r}. Part (a), with the initial vector retained, proves that
Br​=Zτr​​ is planar Brownian motion started at (1,0) in Fτr​​.​
(32)
Every finite r has finite τr​, and ∣Br​∣=eβτr​​>0. On the common event where the clock is a continuous bijection of [0,∞), this holds simultaneously for all times. Thus the origin is a polar point for planar Brownian motion started at (1,0):
P(Br​=0 for all r≥0)=1.​
(33)
To prove unbounded-time visits near zero, let tn​=inf{t≥n:βt​=−n}. Brownian recurrence makes each tn​ finite almost surely, and tn​≥n→∞. Set rn​=Atn​​. Since At​→∞, rn​→∞, while
∣Brn​​∣=∣Ztn​​∣=e−n⟶0.
(34)
Consequently
r→∞liminf​∣Br​∣=0almost surely.​
(35)
For every fixed neighbourhood of zero there are visits at arbitrarily large times. Since B has the law of planar Brownian motion with this initial value, both conclusions hold for that Brownian law. This complex exponential construction of planar Brownian motion proves the two properties together without confusing recurrence near a point with hitting the point.
