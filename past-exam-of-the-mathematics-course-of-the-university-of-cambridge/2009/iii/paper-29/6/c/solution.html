The same compatibility holds for other threshold choices, so the lifetime is the limit as ε↓0, not a property of the chosen sequence.
To prove T=∞, put ν=2a−1>0 and s(y)=y−ν. Its generator vanishes. On each finite horizon, Itô's formula makes s(Xt∧Tεε​ε​) a true martingale, because its stochastic integrand is bounded by νε−ν−1. Positivity therefore implies
ε−νP(Tεε​≤t)≤Es(Xt∧Tεε​ε​)=x−ν,P(Tεε​≤t)≤(xε​)ν.

Since {T≤t} is contained in each {Tεε​≤t}, letting ε↓0 gives P(T≤t)=0. Taking a countable union over integer t proves T=∞ almost surely. This is the truncation construction of a positive Bessel strong solution.
For two positive solutions driven by the same Brownian motion, localize at their first hits of ε. The cutoff equations and pathwise uniqueness make them equal up to those times. On every finite interval both positive continuous paths have positive minima, so decreasing ε removes the localization and proves indistinguishability. Thus
a>21​:a global positive strong solution exists for every driving Brownian motion, and pathwise uniqueness holds.​
(74)
The resulting global solution also supplies the stopped solution for any threshold ε≥x, completing the literal all-threshold existence request. The monotonicity used to construct the zero-boundary lifetime concerns the thresholds ε<x tending to zero; upper-threshold hitting times have the opposite ordering. Strong adaptation is preserved because every cutoff solution is strong and the patch uses their stopping times in the given filtration.
