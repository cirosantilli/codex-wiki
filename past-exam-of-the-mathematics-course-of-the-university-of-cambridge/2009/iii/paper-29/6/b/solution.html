s′(x)=γxγ−1,s′′(x)=γ(γ−1)xγ−2,21​s′′+xa​s′=γ(2γ−1​+a)xγ−2=0.

Thus s is a scale function of a one-dimensional diffusion on every [ε,∞) with ε>0. It is strictly decreasing if a>1/2; multiplying by −1 gives the conventionally increasing scale, without changing any hitting ratio.
Fix 0<ε<x<R. Exit from (ε,R) is almost surely finite. Indeed the coefficients are locally Lipschitz there, so there is no lifetime obstruction in this compact interval. If the process stayed inside forever, the equation and a/X>0 would give Bt​≤R−x for every t, contradicting the almost sure unboundedness above of Brownian motion. Therefore part (a)'s bounded optional-stopping argument applies and gives
Px​(Tε​<TR​)=εγ−Rγxγ−Rγ​,Px​(TR​<Tε​)=Rγ−εγxγ−εγ​.

If a>1/2, then γ<0 and εγ→∞, so Px​(Tε​<TR​)→0. Hitting zero before R would entail hitting every ε before R; it therefore has probability zero. Every continuous path reaching zero in finite time is bounded up to that time, so it belongs to this event for some integer R>x. A countable union proves
a>21​⟹T0​=∞ almost surely.​

If 0<a<1/2, then γ∈(0,1). Taking ε↓0 in the probabilities alone does not yet exclude approaching zero only at infinite time. To establish finite-time access to zero for Bessel dimensions below two, define
vR​(y)=1+2aR2−γyγ−y2​(0≤y≤R).

It is continuous, nonnegative, zero at 0,R, and satisfies LvR​=−1 on (0,R), since L(yγ)=0 and L(y2)=1+2a. Itô's formula stopped at τε,R​=Tε​∧TR​, followed first by expectation at t∧τε,R​, gives
E(t∧τε,R​)=vR​(x)−EvR​(Xt∧τε,R​​)≤vR​(x).

Let t→∞ and then ε↓0. The stopping times increase and have uniformly bounded expectation, so their limit τ0,R​ is finite almost surely. On this finite interval X≤R, and the increasing drift integral is bounded by R−x−infu≤τ0,R​​Bu​. Hence it has a finite limit, and the stochastic equation makes X extend continuously to τ0,R​. If the upper endpoint was not reached, the lower stopped values tend to zero, so the terminal value is zero. Thus τ0,R​=T0​∧TR​ genuinely denotes a finite hitting time.
The lower-exit events decrease to {T0​<TR​} as ε↓0, giving Px​(T0​<TR​)=1−(x/R)γ. Let R→∞ to obtain

There is no finite explosion to +∞: on a finite horizon, any excursion above level 1 has drift at most a, so its height is bounded by max{x,1}+2supu≤t​∣Bu​∣+at.
