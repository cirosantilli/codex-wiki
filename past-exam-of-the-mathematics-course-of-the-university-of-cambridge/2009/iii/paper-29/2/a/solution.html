The intended setting is X=M+A with A continuous and adapted. These conditions are needed for the requested continuous-semimartingale conclusion; finite variation alone does not supply them. For example, M=0, At​=1{t≥1}​ satisfies the printed deterministic bound and starts at zero, but fn​(X) has a jump for sufficiently large n. We use the continuous adapted interpretation throughout this question.

The initial value is zero. The first term is a continuous martingale, since M is bounded and the integrand is bounded. The second is continuous adapted finite variation, hence predictable. This decomposition is unique because a continuous finite-variation local martingale is constant. The phrase Doob-Meyer here must mean this semimartingale decomposition: V(n) need not be nondecreasing, and the hypotheses do not make fn​(X) a submartingale as required by the Doob-Meyer decomposition theorem. Indeed fn′​(0)=−1, so the bounded deterministic drift At​=t∧δ makes fn​(At​) initially decrease, excluding the submartingale property.
The sign process is predictable because it is a Borel function of the continuous adapted, and therefore predictable, process X. Its paths need not themselves be left-continuous. Pointwise, including at x=0,
