For 0≤s≤t and E∈Fs​, the definition of the Radon-Nikodym derivative gives
EP​[1E​Zt​]=Q(E)=EP​[1E​Zs​].
(40)
The density is nonnegative and integrable; with the probability normalization used here its expectation is one. Hence
EP​[Zt​∣Fs​]=Zs​,​
(41)
so Z is a martingale. When Zt​=h(Xt​,t), the Itô formula under Wiener measure gives
dZt​=hx​(Xt​,t)dXt​+g(Xt​,t)dt,g=ht​+21​hxx​.
(42)
The first term is a continuous local martingale, by localization on compact space-time sets. Since Z is a martingale, uniqueness of the continuous semimartingale decomposition makes ∫0t​g(Xs​,s)ds=0 identically. The integrand is continuous in time along every continuous path, so g(Xt​,t)=0 for every t, outside one common null event.
For each fixed t>0, Xt​ has a Gaussian distribution with strictly positive density everywhere on R. Since g(⋅,t) is continuous, g(Xt​,t)=0 almost surely implies g(x,t)=0 for every real x. Therefore
ht​(x,t)+21​hxx​(x,t)=0(x∈R, t>0).​
(43)
If the stated smoothness includes time zero, continuity extends the equation there. This is the backward heat equation. The full-support argument is needed to pass from an identity along Brownian paths to an equation at every spatial point.
