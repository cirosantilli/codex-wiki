Define k(x,t)=hx​(x,t)/h(x,t)=∂x​logh(x,t). Positivity and smoothness of h make k continuous and bounded on compact space-time sets. Brownian paths remain in compact spatial sets on each finite horizon, so ∫0T​k(Xs​,s)2ds<∞ almost surely for every T. Thus
Mt​=∫0t​k(Xs​,s)dXs​
(44)
is a well-defined continuous local martingale with M0​=0. The backward heat equation from part (b) removes the drift in Itô's formula and yields
dZt​=hx​(Xt​,t)dXt​=Zt​dMt​.
(45)
By the allowed fact Z0​=1 and the uniqueness in part (a),
Zt​=E(M)t​=exp(∫0t​k(Xs​,s)dXs​−21​∫0t​k(Xs​,s)2ds).​
(46)
The finite-horizon form of the Girsanov theorem used here says: if M0​=0 is a continuous local martingale and its stochastic exponential Z is a strictly positive true martingale of mean one on [0,T], then under dQ∣FT​​=ZT​dP∣FT​​ every continuous P-local martingale N becomes the Q-local martingale N−[N,M]. Quadratic variation is unchanged by this locally equivalent change of measure.
All the assumptions hold. The integrand defining M is locally square integrable as checked above; Z is strictly positive; and it is already a true martingale because it is the density process. On each finite horizon Zt​=E[ZT​∣Ft​], so it is uniformly integrable on that horizon. There is no need to establish a separate Novikov condition, nor to assume equivalence on the infinite-time sigma-algebra.
Take N=X. Its covariation with M is [X,M]t​=∫0t​k(Xs​,s)ds. Hence Bt​=Xt​−∫0t​k(Xs​,s)ds is a continuous Q-local martingale starting at zero with [B]t​=t. The Lévy characterization of Brownian motion makes it Brownian under Q. Since this argument holds on every finite horizon for the same process B,
Xt​=Bt​+∫0t​h(Xs​,s)hx​(Xs​,s)​ds,B is Brownian motion under Q.​
(47)
This is the Brownian change of measure by a positive space-time harmonic function, a Doob h-transform with drift ∂x​logh.
