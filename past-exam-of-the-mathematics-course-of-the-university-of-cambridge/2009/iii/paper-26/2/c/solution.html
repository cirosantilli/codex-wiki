We first determine the torsion. The rational 2-torsion points are O,(0,0),(5,0),(−5,0). At the good primes three and seven, part (b) gives group orders four and eight. Prime-to-residue-characteristic torsion injects under reduction: an element in the kernel has a formal parameter t of positive valuation, and multiplication by an integer prime to the residue characteristic has series mt+O(t2) with unit leading coefficient, so cannot kill a nonzero such parameter. At seven this excludes rational 3-primary torsion; at three it excludes 7-primary torsion and every odd-primary component other than the already excluded 3-primary component. Thus all torsion is 2-primary and injects at three, bounding its order by four. Consequently
E5​(Q)tors​={O,(0,0),(5,0),(−5,0)}≅(Z/2Z)2.
(14)
For the rank, use two-isogeny descent with
E:y2=x3−25x,E′:Y2=X3+100X.
(15)
The two-isogeny formula gives
ϕ(x,y)=(x−x25​, y(1+x225​)),ψ(X,Y)=(4X2Y2​, 8Y​(1−X2100​)).
(16)
They extend at the exceptional points, have kernels {O,(0,0)} on their respective curves, and satisfy ψϕ=[2]. These assertions can be checked by substitution; for example the composed x-coordinate is (x2+25)2/[4x(x2−25)], the duplication x-coordinate. The maps respect addition because they are morphisms fixing O: pushforward of divisor classes is additive, takes principal divisors to principal divisors by the norm of a rational function, and under the identification in part 1(a) sends [(P)−(O)] to [(ϕP)−(O)], and likewise for ψ.
Define the two-torsion square-class homomorphism α:E(Q)→Q×/Q×2 by α(x,y)=[x], α(O)=1, and α((0,0))=[−25]=[−1]. Define α′ on E′ similarly, with α′((0,0))=[100]=1. To see the homomorphism property, a nonvertical line y=mx+c cuts either curve in three points whose x-coordinates have product c2. The chord-and-tangent law therefore makes their square classes multiply to one. When the line passes through (0,0), the two remaining x-coordinates have product equal to the coefficient of x, exactly explaining the assigned value there. Vertical lines give the inverse-point relation. The same argument includes repeated tangent intersections.
The kernels are kerα=ψE′(Q) and kerα′=ϕE(Q). Here is an explicit check, not just an invocation of the descent correspondence. The x-coordinate of every nonexceptional ψ-image is a square. Conversely, if (x,y)∈E has x=s2=0, put X=2x+2y/s and Y=2sX. Then X2−4xX+100=0, so (X,Y)∈E′ and the displayed ψ maps it to (x,y). For E′, every nonexceptional ϕ-image has X=(y/x)2; if X=s2=0, put x=(X+Y/s)/2, y=sx. Substitution gives a point of E mapping to (X,Y). The point (0,0)∈E′ has square class one and equals ϕ(5,0); the point (0,0)∈E has nonsquare class −1. Together with the origins these checks handle every exception.
The prime-support bound in two-isogeny descent can be read directly from y2=x(x2+B). At a prime not dividing the integral coefficient B, positive valuation of x gives 2v(y)=v(x), while negative valuation gives 2v(y)=3v(x); either way the valuation of x is even. Thus the first image has only the candidate classes 1,−1,5,−5. All four occur, already at the four torsion points. Hence #α(E)=4.
On E′, nonzero real x-coordinates are positive since X(X2+100) has the sign of X. Prime support leaves only 1,2,5,10. The point (5,25) supplies class five. Class two would give the quartic covering in a two-isogeny descent
W2=2U4+50V4,gcd(U,V)=1.
(17)
Indeed write X=2(U/V)2 and clear the square factor in the curve equation. If 5∤U, the right side is two modulo five, impossible for a square. If 5∣U, then 5∤V and 5∣W; division by twenty-five gives (W/5)2≡2V4≡2(mod5), again impossible. This is the five-adic obstruction to square class two on the curve with coefficient one hundred. Because the image is a subgroup and contains five, class ten is excluded as well. Therefore α′(E′)={1,5} has size two.
Now [E:ψE′]=4. The quotient ψE′/2E is isomorphic to E′/ϕE: the kernel of the induced surjection is kerψ/(kerψ∩ϕE), which is trivial because (0,0)∈E′ equals ϕ(5,0). Thus [E:2E]=4⋅2=8. Finite generation follows from the Mordell-Weil theorem, whose height-descent proof is supplied in the essay below. A finitely generated group with the torsion already found has [E:2E]=2r+2, so r=1. Hence
E5​(Q)≅(Z/2Z)2×Z.​
(18)
The point (−4,6) has infinite order, since it is not one of the torsion points. No assertion that this particular point generates the free factor is needed.
