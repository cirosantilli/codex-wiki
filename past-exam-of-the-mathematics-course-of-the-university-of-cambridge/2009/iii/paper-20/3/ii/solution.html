The lens-space absolute grading can be calculated from the standard torus surgery triple. For 0≤k<n, the reverse trace structure with evaluation 2k−n has a small embedded holomorphic triangle with basepoint multiplicity zero and signed count ±1, taking yk​ to Θ−. Its degree shift is Δk​=(1−(2k−n)2/n)/4. Thus (dk​−2)+Δk​=−2, which gives the boxed formula for dk​. Additional triangles wind around the surgery region and give the higher powers of U calculated below.
Their infinity groups are obtained by inverting U: one Laurent tower for S3 and each lens-space structure, two for the torsion structure on S1×S2, and Z[U,U−1]/(1−U∣i∣) in its non-torsion structures. Their plus groups are respectively T0+​, T1/2+​⊕T−1/2+​, and Tdk​+​. The hat groups are Z in degree zero, Z in each degree ±1/2, and Z in degree dk​. These follow directly by straightening the standard genus-one Heegaard diagrams: the lens space has one generator in each structure and no chain differential, while the two bigons in the torsion S1×S2 diagram cancel with coherent signs. The sphere adjunction condition eliminates its non-torsion plus and hat groups, but not its ordinary minus group. The Non-torsion Heegaard Floer homology of the sphere-circle product calculation comes from a two-generator chain complex with chain differential 1−U∣i∣; its cokernel is the displayed quotient. For the triangle, complete the free minus towers by replacing R with R. The non-torsion summands disappear after completion, because 1−U∣i∣ has an inverse 1+U∣i∣+U2∣i∣+⋯ in R.

For n=1 the auxiliary lens space is S3, and this is simply the other degree-−1/2 surgery trace map.

For 0<k<n the term ℓ=0 is the unique constant term, and every other exponent is positive. Thus Pk​=1+U(⋯) is a unit in R. The zero-surgery summand for such a k vanishes, and the exact triangle is

It is isomorphic, as an ungraded completed-module triangle, to one with the final map the identity. The sum generally has no single absolute Heegaard Floer grading shift; each individual trace component has shift Δj​. This distinction and the power series explain why replacing the completed triangle by a polynomial-module triangle is not innocuous.
