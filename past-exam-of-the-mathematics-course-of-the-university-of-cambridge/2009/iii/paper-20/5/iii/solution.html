Remove two disjoint four-balls from M, giving W:S3→S3. An Admissible cut for a Heegaard Floer mixed map is a separating three-manifold N with
W=W1​∪N​W2​,b2+​(W1​),b2+​(W2​)>0,δH1(N;Z)=0⊂H2(W;Z).
(48)
The last condition is the Mayer-Vietoris connecting-map condition. It makes a Spin-c structure on W uniquely determined by its restrictions to the two pieces, avoiding an uncontrolled sum of extensions in the composition law. Such cuts exist when b2+​(M)>1: represent an integral positive-square class by an embedded surface whose tubular neighborhood carries one positive direction and whose complement retains another. One can construct an embedded representative by resolving intersections of a representative cycle; the genus may increase without changing its class or square. The nonzero normal Euler number makes the restriction H1(νT)→H1(∂νT) surjective, so the connecting-map condition holds. Puncture on either side of the cut.
Write t=s∣N​ and si​=s∣Wi​​. Both infinity Heegaard Floer cobordism maps vanish by positivity. Naturality with respect to the exact sequence
⋯→HF−(N,t)ι​HF∞(N,t)π​HF+(N,t)δ​HF−(N,t)→⋯
(49)
gives imFW1​,s1​−​⊂kerι and FW2​,s2​+​π=0. Exactness identifies δ with a degree-−1 isomorphism from HF+(N)/imπ onto kerι, namely the two versions of Reduced Heegaard Floer homology. Hence the inverse on these reduced groups is defined, and one forms
FW,smix​=FW2​,s2​+​∘δ−1∘FW1​,s1​−​:HF−(S3)→HF+(S3).​
(50)
This is the construction of the Mixed Heegaard Floer invariant, rather than the zero ordinary composite.
Let Θ− be the top minus generator, of degree −2, and Θ+ the bottom plus generator, of degree zero. For ζ∈Λ∗(H1​(M)/torsion), insert its degree-one homology actions in the Heegaard Floer cobordism maps and define
ΦM,s​(Ur⊗ζ)=coefficient of Θ+ in FW,smix​(UrΘ−⊗ζ).​
(51)
This is an integer up to the overall orientation sign. cobordism composition and associativity show independence of the admissible cut and handle choices. Equivalently, fixing the relevant orientation data removes the overall sign ambiguity.
The grading condition can be checked explicitly. Since χ(W)=χ(M)−2 and σ(W)=σ(M), the sum of the ordinary cobordism shifts is d(s)+1, where d(s)=(c1​(s)2−2χ(M)−3σ(M))/4. The inverse connecting map contributes another +1. Therefore the output degree on UrΘ−⊗ζ is d(s)−2r−degζ, and the displayed invariant vanishes unless 2r+degζ=d(s).
Finally, two positive pieces are needed, so that both infinity maps vanish and the reduced-group factorization is available. For an admissible cut the connecting-map condition and signature additivity imply that the two positive subspaces inject as orthogonal subspaces of the closed intersection form; hence b2+​(M)≥2. With only one positive direction no such admissible cut exists, and a chamber-dependent construction requires extra choices. With no positive direction, the ordinary infinity maps need not vanish at all. This explains the stated bound for the invariant defined here.
