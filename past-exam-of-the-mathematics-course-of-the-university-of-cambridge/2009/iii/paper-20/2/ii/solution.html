Past exam of the mathematics course of the University of Cambridge / 2009 / iii / Paper 20 / 2 / ii / Solution

A generator selects one point from each alpha circle and each beta circle. Here the points 1,3 lie on α1​∩β2​, 2 on α1​∩β1​, a,c on α2​∩β1​, and b on α2​∩β2​. Hence, with R=Z[U], the Heegaard Floer chain complex has free basis
(1,a), (1,c), (3,a), (3,c), (2,b).​
(15)
A pair such as (2,a) is invalid because it uses beta-one twice.
Orient the attaching circles so that their algebraic-intersection matrix is (1−2​−21​). The presentation of H1​(Y) is then m1​−2m2​=0, m2​−2m1​=0, giving H1​(Y)=Z/3 with m2​=−m1​. The upper rectangular domain connects (2,b) to (3,c); the lower one connects (2,b) to (1,a). Thus these three generators have zero joining-cycle obstruction. Changing just the point on the second alpha circle from a to c, or just the point on the first from 1 to 3, gives the two opposite nonzero meridian classes. This distinguishes the other two generators. The equivalence classes of Whitney disk classes are therefore
{(1,a),(2,b),(3,c)},{(1,c)},{(3,a)}.​
(16)
The dot in the central unshaded region represents the basepoint. Each displayed rectangle has nz​=0, Euler measure zero and four corner averages 1/4, hence Maslov index one. The relative grading in Heegaard Floer homology satisfies
gr(2,b)−gr(1,a)=gr(2,b)−gr(3,c)=1.
(17)
Thus a convenient normalization is degree zero for (1,a),(3,c) and degree one for (2,b); either singleton can independently be put in degree zero. As before, multiplication by Um subtracts 2m from every degree.
