Let T=(10​11​) and S=(01​−10​). For γ=(ac​bd​) in the modular group, left multiplication by powers of T and by S performs subtraction and exchange on its first-column entries. The Euclidean algorithm reduces (a,c) to (±1,0), since the determinant condition makes them coprime. The resulting matrix is ±Tm, and −I=S2 is already generated. Hence S,T generate SL2​(Z).
The standard fundamental domain of the modular group is D={z∈H:∣Rez∣≤1/2, ∣z∣≥1}, with the vertical sides identified by T and the circular sides by S. To prove every orbit meets it, choose a coprime integer pair (c,d) minimizing ∣cz+d∣. A minimum exists because only finitely many pairs have this quantity below any fixed bound: ∣c∣Imz≤∣cz+d∣ bounds c, then d. Complete the pair to a determinant-one matrix. Its image has maximal imaginary part in the orbit, since Im(γz)=Imz/∣cz+d∣2. Translate its real part into [−1/2,1/2]. If its modulus were below one, inversion would increase its imaginary part, a contradiction. This is the reduction to the standard modular region.
