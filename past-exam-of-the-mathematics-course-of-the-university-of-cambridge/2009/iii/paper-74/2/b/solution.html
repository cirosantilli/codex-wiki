Take the upward direction as ez​, with polar angle θ measured from it. The liquid is quiescent at infinity. On a translating clean inviscid bubble, the kinematic boundary condition is (u−Uez​)⋅n=0, and the stress-free boundary condition is zero tangential traction. The normal traction must balance uniform gas pressure and any constant-curvature surface-tension jump.
For a vertical Stokeslet of strength F, the radial velocity is ur​=Fcosθ/(4πμr). Its traction on a concentric sphere is purely normal, so zero tangential traction is automatic. The kinematic condition at r=a gives
F=4πμaU.​
(16)
The singularity is only a device for generating the exterior solution; there is no liquid point force inside the actual bubble. The integrated dynamic traction on the bubble is −Fez​, giving the clean-bubble Stokes drag. The upward buoyancy is 4πρga3/3, hence force balance gives
U=3μρga2​.​
(17)
The normal stress also shows that surface tension is not required for this particular spherical solution. Let the ambient hydrostatic pressure at the bubble centre be pc​, so on its surface ph​=pc​−ρgacosθ. Adding the Stokeslet's dynamic normal traction gives
σnntotal​=−pc​+(ρga−4πa23F​)cosθ=−pc​,
(18)
where the terminal buoyancy value of F cancels the dipole. This is the spherical clean bubble without surface tension balance: an initially spherical bubble can remain spherical in this ideal steady Stokes solution even when surface tension is zero. With uniform surface tension γ, choose gas pressure pb​=pc​+2γ/a; with γ=0, pb​=pc​. This proves compatibility of the spherical shape, not stability of arbitrary deformations or validity when inertia matters.
