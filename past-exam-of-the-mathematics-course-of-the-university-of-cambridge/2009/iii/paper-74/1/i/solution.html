For a fixed fluid domain, consider an incompressible Newtonian Stokes flow with prescribed velocities on its boundaries and prescribed far-field behaviour. Conservative body forces, such as gravity, may be absorbed into the pressure. Among all sufficiently regular divergence-free trial velocity fields with exactly those boundary velocities and far-field behaviour, the Stokes solution minimizes the viscous dissipation
D[u]=2μ∫D​eij​(u)eij​(u)dV,eij​=21​(∂i​uj​+∂j​ui​).
(1)
To see this Minimum-dissipation theorem for Stokes flow, write a trial field as v=u+w, with ∇⋅w=0 and homogeneous velocity boundary data. The Newtonian fluid stress tensor satisfies ∇⋅σ=0, so integration by parts gives
2μ∫e(u):e(w)dV=∫σ:∇wdV=0.
(2)
Therefore
D[v]−D[u]=2μ∫e(w):e(w)dV≥0.​
(3)
Equality requires the difference to be a rigid motion, eliminated by the fixed wall and far-field data. The theorem varies the velocity field in one fixed geometry; it does not minimize over particle positions. If a nonconservative body force is prescribed, the appropriate variational functional also includes its work, rather than being the dissipation alone.
