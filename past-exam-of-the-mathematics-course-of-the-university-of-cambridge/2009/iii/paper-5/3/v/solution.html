Set P0​=G and Pi+1​=Pip​. The permitted power-embedding result, applied repeatedly, gives [Pi​,G]≤Pi+1​, and each Pi​ is a powerful p-group. These subgroups eventually reach 1: if Pi​=1, its Frattini subgroup Pip​ is proper. In fact these are the lower p-series layers, with their indices shifted by one.
For x∈G and y∈Pi​, the commutator [y,x] lies in Pi+1​, is central modulo Pi+2​, and has exponent dividing p there. The class-two collection calculation from part (iv) therefore gives
(xy)p≡xpyp(modPi+2​).
(14)
Applying part (iv) inside Pi​, whose third lower-series term is contained in Pi+2​, shows that the map y↦yp is onto Pi+1​/Pi+2​.
Given a∈P1​, first choose x with xp≡a(modP2​). Suppose xp≡a(modPi+1​). Choose y∈Pi​ with yp≡x−pa(modPi+2​). The displayed collection congruence then gives (xy)p≡a(modPi+2​). Replacing x by xy improves the approximation by one layer. Since the filtration on a group terminates, a finite sequence of corrections gives xp=a exactly. The reverse inclusion is the definition of Gp. Hence
Gp=G{p}={xp:x∈G}.​
