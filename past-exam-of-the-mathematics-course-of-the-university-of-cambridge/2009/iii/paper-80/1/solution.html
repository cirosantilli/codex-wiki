Use the flat-surface Boussinesq approximation, with small thermal expansion, constant properties, negligible surface deformation, and constant surface tension. The top is impermeable and shear-free, while the solid walls are no-slip. Temperature-dependent surface tension would introduce a separate Marangoni stress, which is not included in this buoyancy-driven model. The stated time scale requires κ to mean thermal diffusivity; if the material property given is a dimensional thermal conductivity k, use κ=k/(ρ0​cp​).
Take z positive downwards and define the nondimensional streamfunction by u=ψz​, w=−ψx​. Velocity is scaled by κ/L, streamfunction by κ, and temperature is measured from the cold-end surface value. Define the Prandtl number and horizontal-length Rayleigh number by
Pr=κν​,Ra=νκgαT​ΔTL3​,
(1)
where αT​ is the thermal expansion coefficient. Put J(ψ,f)=ψz​fx​−ψx​fz​, and let ∇2=∂x2​+∂z2​. Taking the curl of the Boussinesq equations eliminates pressure and gives the full nonlinear vorticity-temperature system
Pr−1[∂t​∇2ψ+J(ψ,∇2ψ)]=∇4ψ+RaTx​,Tt​+J(ψ,T)=∇2T.​
(2)
The buoyancy sign follows from the downward coordinate: warmer fluid has body acceleration −gαT​ΔTTez​.

In steady infinite-Prandtl-number convection, momentum inertia disappears but thermal advection remains:

For a shallow lake, with fixed Ra as ϵ→0, the leading interior temperature is T0​=x. This is an outer solution: it does not satisfy the insulated sidewall derivative right at x=0,1. The leading global conductive solution, which resolves those end regions, is

It is harmonic, has Tc​(x,0)=x, and satisfies the insulated sides and bottom. Away from sidewall layers of horizontal width O(ϵ) it reduces to x with exponentially small end corrections. These layers also turn the flow and enforce the sidewall no-slip boundary condition.
In the shallow interior the dominant momentum derivatives are vertical, so ψ0,zzzz​=−Ra. Integrating with the surface and bottom conditions gives the shallow horizontal convection with an insulated bottom solution, with y=z/ϵ:
T0​=x,ψ0​=−48Raϵ4​y(1−y)2(2y+1),u0​=−48Raϵ3​(8y3−9y2+1),w0​=0.​

Surface flow is toward the colder end, with a deeper return flow. The depth-integrated volume flux is zero because ψ0​(ϵ)=ψ0​(0)=0. The bulk expressions apply away from the turning regions, not unchanged on the vertical walls. A useful small-advection condition in the end layers is Raϵ4≪1, automatic for fixed Rayleigh number in the stated thin limit.
The heat flux in these units is q=(uT−Tx​,wT−Tz​). For the leading bulk temperature, the horizontal transport through a vertical section per unit out-of-plane width is
Hx​=∫0ϵ​(u0​T0​−T0,x​)dz=x[ψ0​(ϵ)−ψ0​(0)]−ϵ,Hx​=−ϵ.​

Thus leading advective horizontal transport vanishes: both layers have the same temperature at a given x, and their opposing volume fluxes cancel. The leading conductive transport is from the hot end toward the cold end. In dimensional form it is −ρ0​cp​κΔTϵ=−kΔTϵ per unit width. Heat enters through the warmer portion of the maintained upper surface and leaves through its colder portion, with the conductive end adjustments particularly important near the two insulated sidewalls. It cannot enter through the insulated sides or bottom.

Vertical transport must distinguish local flux from net transport. In the leading interior w0​=T0,z​=0, whereas the temperature correction gives a local downward conductive flux qz​=−Θz​=−ψ0​>0.
