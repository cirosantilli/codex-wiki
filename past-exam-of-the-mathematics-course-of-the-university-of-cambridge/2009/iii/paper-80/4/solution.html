Let a(t) be the particle radius and ΔT=Ts​−T∞​>0 the supercooling. Use spherical symmetry, constant thermal properties, equal phase densities ρ, and negligible fluid advection. Let L be latent heat per unit mass, cp​ the liquid specific heat, kℓ​ its thermal conductivity and κ=kℓ​/(ρcp​) its thermal diffusivity. The exterior temperature satisfies the radial heat equation
Tt​=κr21​∂r∂​(r2Tr​),r>a(t),​
(28)
with T(r,0)=T∞​ for r>a0​, a(0)=a0​ and T(r,t)→T∞​ as r→∞.
The Gibbs-Thomson effect gives curvature-induced melting-temperature depression. For a spherical interface write
T(a(t),t)=Ts​−a(t)Γ​,Γ=ρL2γTs​​>0,​
(29)
where γ is solid-liquid interfacial energy per area and the factor two is the sphere's curvature 2/a. Here Ts​ in the thermodynamic formula is an absolute temperature. The notation Γ also permits a given measured capillary coefficient without requiring its microscopic derivation.
In the one-phase Stefan problem, the solid has no internal heat flux and the Stefan condition is
ρLa˙=−kℓ​Tr​(a+,t).​
(30)
The sign follows from heat balance: positive outward liquid heat flux releases latent heat as the solid grows. If the solid's thermal storage is retained, its temperature T(s) instead satisfies its own radial heat equation, ∂r​T(s)(0,t)=0, and T(s)(a−,t)=T(a+,t). The two-phase condition is ρLa˙=ks​Tr(s)​(a−,t)−kℓ​Tr​(a+,t). It reduces to the one-phase condition in the quasistatic limit because a regular harmonic temperature inside a sphere is spatially constant. An initial solid temperature profile is additional data for a fully time-dependent two-phase problem.
Two reciprocal Stefan number conventions are common. The large parameter appropriate to the requested slow-interface limit is the latent-to-sensible heat ratio
S=cp​ΔTL​.​
(31)
It measures the energy required for phase change relative to cooling or heating by the supercooling. The often-used sensible-to-latent definition is Ste=cp​ΔT/L=1/S. Thus “large Stefan number” here means large S, not large Ste in that other convention.
The thermal diffusion time for scale a0​ is a02​/κ, whereas the interface time is Sa02​/κ. More explicitly, with R=r/a0​, θ=(T−T∞​)/ΔT and slow time τ=κt/(Sa02​), the liquid equation is
S−1θτ​=R21​(R2θR​)R​.
(32)
For S≫1, temperature therefore equilibrates before the radius changes appreciably. The quasistatic spherical Stefan problem with capillarity has a harmonic exterior temperature, fixed by its interface and far-field values:
T(r,t)=T∞​+rΔTa(t)−Γ​.​
(33)
The small initial diffusion adjustment is omitted at this order. Inserting Tr​(a)=−ΔT/a+Γ/a2 into the Stefan condition gives
a˙=Sκ​a2a−ac​​,ac​=ΔTΓ​=ρL(Ts​−T∞​)2γTs​​.​
(34)
This is the critical radius for a supercooled spherical seed. For a0​>ac​, the interface is warmer than the liquid and loses heat, driving growth. For 0<a0​<ac​, its curvature-depressed interface temperature is colder than the liquid, so heat enters and the solid melts. At a=ac​, the uniform temperature T∞​ supplies an equilibrium radius. It is unstable, since the radius-velocity derivative there is κ/(Sac2​)>0. For a growing particle with a≫ac​, a2 increases asymptotically as 2κt/S.
For a subcritical particle the radius decreases monotonically and remains below a0​. Hence
dtd​a3=−S3κ​(ac​−a)≤−S3κ​(ac​−a0​)<0.
(35)
It cannot remain positive beyond Sa03​/[3κ(ac​−a0​)], proving finite-time extinction of a subcritical spherical seed. The exact extinction time in the reduced model is obtained by separating variables:
te​=κS​∫0a0​​ac​−aa2​da.
(36)
With η0​=a0​/ac​∈(0,1), this is
te​=κSac2​​[−log(1−η0​)−η0​−21​η02​]<∞.​
(37)
Near disappearance, the capillary term dominates and a˙∼−κac​/(Sa2), so a3∼3κac​(te​−t)/S​. For a0​≪ac​, the lifetime is te​∼Sa03​/(3κac​); it diverges logarithmically as a0​→ac−​, consistently with the unstable equilibrium. The threshold and lifetime above are conclusions of the specified large-S quasistatic continuum model, rather than an assertion that its approximation is uniform through every finite-S shrinking-interface transient.
