Use the steady axisymmetric top-hat plume model, with plume radius b(z), uniform mean upward speed w(z)>0 and reduced gravity g′(z)=g[ρa​(z)−ρp​(z)]/ρ0​. The Boussinesq approximation uses constant reference density ρ0​ in inertia and retains the small density difference only in buoyancy. Assume a quiescent hydrostatic ambient, a slender fully turbulent plume, negligible molecular transport and an entrainment velocity αw, with constant positive entrainment coefficient α. Define the ambient buoyancy frequency by N2=−(g/ρ0​)dρa​/dz; positive N2 is stable stratification.
The integrated volume, momentum and buoyancy balances of a Boussinesq top-hat plume in a stratified ambient are
dzd​(b2w)=2αbw,dzd​(b2w2)=b2g′,dzd​(b2wg′)=−N2b2w.​
(11)
The first follows from side entrainment, the second from the integrated buoyancy force, and the third accounts for the changing ambient density. Common factors of π are cancelled. Equivalently, the requested radius, speed and reduced-gravity equations are
b′=2α−2w2bg′​,w′=wg′​−b2αw​,(g′)′=−N2−b2αg′​.​
(12)
These equations stop describing an upward light plume once w vanishes, and their light-plume interpretation requires g′>0.
For nonzero C, seek plume similarity in power-law stratification in the form b=βz, w=Azn and g′=Gzm. The first balance gives β=2α/(n+2). Momentum gives m=2n−1 and G=2(n+1)A2. The buoyancy balance then requires 3n=2+n+p, so
n=2p+2​,m=p+1,β=p+64α​,G=−3p+82C​,A2=−(p+4)(3p+8)2C​.
(13)
Choosing A>0 gives the exact power-law family, including its normalization:
b=p+64α​z,w=[(p+4)(3p+8)−2C​]1/2z(p+2)/2,g′=−3p+82C​zp+1.​
(14)
These are the amplitudes and physical branches of a power-law stratified plume. No source flux was specified, so an arbitrary finite-source solution is not determined by C,p alone; the displayed family is an exact similarity solution, rather than the general solution for arbitrary initial fluxes.
With the specific flux convention Q=b2w, M=b2w2 and B=b2wg′, their values are
Q=β2Az3+p/2,M=β2A2z4+p,B=β2AGz4+3p/2.​
(15)
The physical volume flux is πQ, mass flux is ρ0​πQ, momentum flux is ρ0​πM, and kinematic buoyancy flux is πB. Thus the “specific mass flux” is the mass flux divided by reference density, with or without the explicitly stated factor π.
The parameter restrictions follow directly from positivity. Positive radius needs p>−6. Positive g′ and real positive w further require p>−4 and −C/(3p+8)>0, giving
C>0: −4<p<−8/3;C<0: p>−8/3.​
(16)
For stable C>0, buoyancy flux decreases with height, while volume and momentum fluxes increase. However, B∝z4+3p/2 diverges at z=0 throughout this interval. Also the ambient density gradient and plume speed are singular there. Thus this stable similarity branch can describe a region away from a virtual source, but cannot represent a finite-buoyancy-flux point source extending all the way to z=0. For ordinary regular stable stratifications, including positive p, a finite-source rising plume instead loses buoyancy and eventually reaches neutral buoyancy and a finite rise limit; the pure-power ansatz does not supply such a solution.
For unstable C<0 and p>−8/3, the buoyancy flux grows with height and tends to zero at the origin. The plume draws its increasing buoyancy from an unstable ambient rather than a finite positive source buoyancy flux. Such an ambient must be maintained or treated locally, since it is itself convectively unstable. If p≤−1, its density profile is singular at the source, while for p>−1 its increasing density contrast eventually violates the Boussinesq approximation at sufficiently large height. These are formal local similarity solutions with corresponding physical limits. For C<0 and −6<p<−4, the algebra also gives a real positive speed but negative g′: this is a heavy upward flow, not the specified light plume.
At nonzero C, the values p=−6,−4,−8/3 do not give finite coefficients. Respectively they would require zero entrained volume-flux growth, zero momentum-flux growth despite nonzero buoyancy, or constant buoyancy flux despite nonzero ambient stratification. They are not omitted regular endpoints.
When C=0, the ambient is unstratified regardless of the nominal value of p. The nontrivial positive-buoyancy point-source solution is the Boussinesq point-source plume:
b=56α​z,w=A0​z−1/3,g′=34​A02​z−5/3,A03​=48α225B0​​.​
(17)
Here B0​>0 is the constant specific buoyancy flux with π removed. Its Q∝z5/3 and M∝z4/3 vanish at the point source, while B=B0​. Its near-source singularity is the usual ideal point-source limit; a finite vent replaces that limit by finite source data or a virtual origin.
