Use an inertial gravity-current box model in a horizontal prismatic V-shaped valley. Let its section have area A(h)=λh2 and width 2λh, where the dimensionless constant λ>0 is half-width divided by depth. Let X(t) be front position and h(t) the representative uniform current depth. Assume negligible entrainment and volume loss, hydrostatic cross-sections, large Reynolds number, and negligible bed friction and axial bed slope. These assumptions describe the bulk spreading after the short dam-break adjustment; they do not give a universal law for a steep or friction-dominated valley.
For water of fixed volume W, conservation gives W=λh2X. Close the front speed by the gravity-current front condition X˙=Frgh​, where Fr is a constant order-one Froude number for the chosen inertial front model. Therefore
X˙=Kw​X−1/4,Kw​=Frg​(W/λ)1/4.
(18)
Integration gives X5/4=X05/4​+5Kw​t/4. Once the initial reservoir length X0​ is negligible, the inertial dam-break current in a triangular valley satisfies
X(t)=[45​Frg​(W/λ)1/4t]4/5∝W1/5t4/5.​
(19)
The time exponent differs from a flat-bottomed channel because a V-shaped section stores volume proportional to h2. A viscous V-shaped channel gravity current obeys a different spreading law, so retaining the inertial assumption is essential.
For the short-lived eruption, interpret W as the bulk mixture volume released during the event. Let ϕ(t) be the well-mixed particle volume fraction, initially ϕ0​, let ρp​ and ρa​ be particle and ambient carrier densities, and assume a dilute Boussinesq approximation for the mixture. The particles provide reduced gravity
g′(t)=Gϕ(t),G=gρa​ρp​−ρa​​,g0′​=Gϕ0​.
(20)
Assume thermal buoyancy, compressibility, erosion and continued injection are negligible, and that the carrier volume remains W. Turbulent mixing keeps the particles suspended uniformly while they settle vertically at a constant terminal settling velocity ws​. For small spherical particles of radius ap​ in carrier viscosity μa​, the Stokes settling velocity is ws​=2(ρp​−ρa​)gap2​/(9μa​) when the particle Reynolds number is small. If an eruption volume is quoted as solid particle volume rather than mixture volume, replace the W used here by that volume divided by ϕ0​.
The particle deposition flux per horizontal projected bed area is ws​ϕ. The current's projected settling area is 2λhX, so
dtd​(ϕW)=−2λhXws​ϕ,ϕ˙​=−h2ws​​ϕ.​
(21)
The factor two reflects the triangular section's mean depth h/2. Put s=ϕ/ϕ0​​ and K=Frg0′​​(W/λ)1/4. The front and concentration balances become
X˙=KsX−1/4,s˙=−ws​λ/W​sX1/2,dXds​=−Kws​​λ/W​X3/4.

s=1−(X/R)7/4,ϕ(X)=ϕ0​[1−(X/R)7/4]2,R=[4ws​7Frg0′​​​(W/λ)3/4]4/7.​

The whole time dependence is determined analytically by the quadrature
t=KR5/4​∫0X/R​1−y7/4y1/4​dy.​

At late times the concentration loss slows the flow, and R−X decays exponentially with rate 7K/(4R5/4)=ws​/hR​, where hR​=W/(λR)​. The final length R is a limiting runout reached as t→∞, not a finite-time stopping event in this ideal model.
To calculate the deposit profile of a finite triangular-valley particle current, let md​(x) be final deposited particle mass per unit downstream length, and M0​=ρp​ϕ0​W the initial particle mass. Deposition at a fixed section starts at arrival time ta​(x), when the front first reaches x. With the instantaneous front coordinate denoted ξ, use dt=ξ1/4dξ/[K(1−(ξ/R)7/4)] to obtain
md​(x)=∫ta​(x)∞​2λh(t)ws​ρp​ϕ(t)dt=K2ws​ρp​ϕ0​λW​​∫xR​ξ−1/4[1−(ξ/R)7/4]dξ.
(25)
Writing η=x/R, this evaluates to
md​(x)=RM0​​[314​(1−η3/4)−57​(1−η5/2)],0≤x≤R,​
(26)
and md​=0 beyond R. The profile decreases downstream, vanishes quadratically at the runout, and obeys ∫0R​md​(x)dx=M0​. This integral check ensures that all initially suspended particle mass is accounted for.
For an areal rather than a linear deposit density, the changing valley width must also be specified. At lateral coordinate y⊥​, the current covers the bed only while ∣y⊥​∣≤λh. Set Z=min(R,λW/y⊥2​), with Z=R on the valley axis. The mass per horizontal projected bed area is zero if Z<x, and otherwise is
Σd​(x,y⊥​)=Kρp​ws​ϕ0​​[54​(Z5/4−x5/4)−3R7/4Z3−x3​].​
(27)
On the axis this reduces to ρp​ϕ0​hR​[57​(1−η5/4)−127​(1−η3)]. Divide projected areal density by 1+λ−2​ to obtain mass per actual sloping bed area. A specified packed deposit density then converts this areal mass to local deposit thickness; the spreading model itself predicts deposited mass, not a packing fraction.
