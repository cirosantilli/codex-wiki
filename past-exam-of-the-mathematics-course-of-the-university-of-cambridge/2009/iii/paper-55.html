For pressureless matter, the cosmological perfect-fluid continuity equation gives ρ∝a−3. The flat Friedmann equation then gives a∝t2/3, and conformal time dη=dt/a gives a∝η2. Choose the initial conformal time to be zero. A galaxy seen at emission time ηe​ has comoving radius χ=η0​−ηe​ and cosmological redshift 1+z=(η0​/ηe​)2.
Two opposite comoving galaxies are separated by 2χ. Direct one-way communication between their worldlines before emission requires the particle horizon ηe​ to cover this separation: 2(η0​−ηe​)≤ηe​. Thus the threshold is ηe​=2η0​/3, and
zc​=45​for direct communication.​
(1)
The distinction in direct cosmological communication and a shared causal past matters here. If causal contact means that their past light cones overlap, the condition instead is 2χ≤2ηe​, giving ηe​=η0​/2 and zc​=3 for a shared causal past​. These are different operational meanings of the horizon criterion; the boundary corresponds to the limiting initial-time contact.

Count one neutrino species as the usual two thermally populated particle-antiparticle helicity states, with negligible chemical potential. Its entropy weight is g∗s,X​=(7/8)2=7/4. In the ideal high-temperature Standard Model, the bosonic count is 16+8+4=28 from gluons, electroweak gauge bosons and the Higgs field. The fermionic count is 72+12+6=90 from quarks, charged leptons and the three ordinary neutrino species. Hence the relativistic entropy content of the Standard Model is g∗s,D​=28+(7/8)90=106.75 for the remaining bath. Before the new species leaves, the total count is 108.5; its separately cooling states are excluded from the subsequent bath count.
Near ordinary neutrino decoupling, the still-coupled bath has
g∗s​=2+87​(4+6)=10.75
(14)
from photons, electron-positron pairs and the three ordinary neutrinos. After those neutrinos decouple, the electromagnetic bath falls from 2+(7/8)4=11/2 to two when the pairs annihilate. Cosmological entropy conservation in that bath gives Tν​/Tγ​=(4/11)1/3.
The new species has cooled as TX​∝a−1 since its earlier decoupling, whereas annihilation of Standard Model species heated the remaining bath. The temperature ratio of an early-decoupled neutrino is thus
Tν​TX​​=(106.7510.75​)1/3,Tγ​TX​​=(11×106.7543​)1/3≃0.332.​
(15)
These are ideal-gas estimates with the high-temperature count implicit in the question; precise electroweak thresholds change them slightly.
Entropy degrees of freedom weight temperature ratios with their cube, while energy degrees of freedom use their fourth power. The remaining Standard Model entropy today, in photon-temperature units, is g∗s,0SM​=2+(7/8)6(4/11)=43/11. The new contribution is (7/4)(TX​/Tγ​)3. Therefore the fractional entropy of a relativistically decoupled species is
stotal​sX​​=43/11+(7/4)(TX​/Tγ​)3(7/4)(TX​/Tγ​)3​=106.75+7/47/4​≃0.0161.​
(16)
About 1.6% of the total entropy is in the new species. Separate comoving entropy conservation also derives this fraction directly at decoupling. A model populating four helicity states instead would require the corresponding doubled species count.

Assume the new neutrino is stable and retains the abundance established by relativistic thermal decoupling in cosmology. While it remains relativistic, its contribution to the effective number of neutrino species is
ΔNeff​=(TX​/Tν​)4=(106.7510.75​)4/3≃0.047.
(17)
Thus its effect on the radiation density and expansion during Big Bang nucleosynthesis is reduced relative to a fully heated extra neutrino species.
After it becomes nonrelativistic, its energy density is approximately mX​nX​. A two-state relativistic fermion relic has nX​/nγ​=(3/4)(TX​/Tγ​)3≃0.0275. The density bound on a relativistically decoupled massive relic then gives
Ωγ​ΩX​​=⟨Eγ​⟩mX​​nγ​nX​​,mX​≤Ωγ​ΩX,max​​30ζ(3)π4Tγ,0​​0.02751​.​
(18)
Here ΩX,max​ is the allowed present matter-density fraction and ⟨Eγ​⟩=π4Tγ,0​/(30ζ(3)). Choosing the total available matter fraction yields the basic overclosure bound. Its mass cannot be raised freely at a fixed relic number density.
There is also a perturbation constraint. Neutrino free streaming suppresses clustering below the distance traveled before the species becomes nonrelativistic. The ratio TX​/mX​ controls this distance, and the relic matter fraction controls the strength of the suppression. A significant hot or warm relic component must therefore satisfy the observed structure-formation constraints in addition to the abundance bound. Its allowed dark-matter contribution is limited jointly by these two effects. Numerical observational bounds depend on the chosen matter budget and perturbation data; the temperature and abundance dilution derived here are the factors needed to apply them.

A scalar cosmological perturbation has two scalar gauge freedoms. In Newtonian gauge in cosmology, these remove the scalar shift and spatial shear, leaving the lapse potential Φ and spatial-curvature potential Ψ:
ds2=a2(η)[(1+2Φ)dη2−(1−2Ψ)δij​dxidxj].
(19)
A perfect fluid has no scalar anisotropic stress. The traceless spatial Einstein field equations therefore set Φ=Ψ=ϕ for nonzero spatial Fourier modes, producing the line element in the question. Homogeneous perturbations include background and gauge changes and require separate treatment. The single potential describes the physical scalar perturbation in this gauge.

Choose the conformal-time origin so that ηB​=0 and work on the appropriate expansion branch. Write β=2/(1+3w), so H=β/η and 2H′+(1+3w)H2=0.
Combining the supplied energy and momentum constraints gives the comoving-gauge density contrast Poisson relation
∇2ϕ=4πGa2ρˉ​[δ−3H(1+w)v]=4πGa2ρˉ​Δ.

For this barotropic fluid δP=wρˉ​δ. Substitute the energy constraint into the pressure equation to obtain
ϕ′′+3(1+w)Hϕ′+[2H′+(1+3w)H2]ϕ−w∇2ϕ=0,

so the bracket vanishes and the coefficient of ϕ′/η is A=6(1+w)/(1+3w).
Because a2ρˉ​=Cη−2, the Poisson relation gives Δ=(η2/(4πGC))∇2ϕ. Apply the same potential equation to F=η2∇2ϕ: differentiating η−2F gives

For radiation in cosmology, w=1/3, and the Fourier mode of the comoving-gauge density contrast satisfies
Δk′′​+(3k2​−η22​)Δk​=0.
(25)
Set x=kη/3​, the ratio to the radiation sound horizon scale. The radiation-era comoving density mode has the exact basis
Δk​=A(xsinx​−cosx)+B(xcosx​+sinx).​
(26)
Direct differentiation verifies the equation. For x≪1, the first function is x2/3+O(x4) and the second is x−1+O(x), so the outside-horizon solutions grow as η2 or decay as η−1. Regularity at early time removes the second one: B=0. For x≫1, the regular solution is −Acosx+O(x−1), a constant-amplitude acoustic oscillation. A regular mode thus grows while outside the sound horizon and oscillates after entering it.
The amplitude is arbitrary; the plotted mode sets A=1. The radiation-era comoving density mode approaches the early-time quadratic growth and the late-time cosine shown alongside it.

The inflaton has quantum fluctuations while their wavelengths are shorter than the Hubble radius. Choosing the Bunch-Davies vacuum fixes the short-wavelength positive-frequency modes. During near-de Sitter slow-roll inflation, expansion carries each mode through k=aH, leaving a field fluctuation of amplitude approximately H/(2π) on spatially flat slices.
The inflaton clock-shift origin of curvature perturbations converts this field displacement into a local time shift of magnitude ∣δΦ/Φ˙∣, and hence into a comoving curvature perturbation of magnitude H∣δΦ/Φ˙∣. For the single-field adiabatic growing mode on an attractor, the superhorizon conservation of single-field comoving curvature makes this perturbation approximately constant at k≪aH. These vacuum-generated perturbations seed the later density fluctuations.
