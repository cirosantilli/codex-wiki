Bn​(f,x)=j=0∑n​f(j/n)pn,j​(x).​

Differentiation and the identities j(jn​)=n(j−1n−1​) and (n−j)(jn​)=n(jn−1​) give pn,j′​=n(pn−1,j−1​−pn−1,j​). Shifting the first sum's index therefore yields
Bn′​(f,x)=nj=0∑n−1​[f((j+1)/n)−f(j/n)]pn−1,j​(x).​
(7)
Repeating this calculation takes another forward finite difference of the coefficients and lowers the degree by one. Induction proves the derivatives of Bernstein polynomials formula
Bn(r)​(f,x)=(n)r​j=0∑n−r​Δ1/nr​f(j/n)pn−r,j​(x),(n)r​=(n−r)!n!​.​
(8)
For n>r, define a continuous function on [0,1] by
gn,r​(t)=(n)r​Δ1/nr​f(nn−r​t).​
(9)
All sampled arguments lie in [0,1], since the largest is (n−r)t/n+r/n≤1. At the degree-(n−r) sampling point t=j/(n−r), this gives exactly the coefficient (n)r​Δ1/nr​f(j/n), and hence Bn(r)​f=Bn−r​gn,r​​. The rescaling is essential: the sampling meshes are 1/n and 1/(n−r), and the function gn,r​ depends on n. For n=r, the derivative is the constant n!Δ1/nn​f(0), interpreted as a degree-zero Bernstein polynomial.
Now fix r and assume f∈Cr[0,1]. The supplied uniform limit of normalized finite differences, together with (n)r​/nr→1 and uniform continuity of f(r), shows that
∥gn,r​−f(r)∥∞​⟶0:nn−r​t⟶tuniformly in t.
(10)
Positivity and ∑j​pm,j​=1 imply ∥Bm​h∥∞​≤∥h∥∞​. For completeness, if X∼Bin(m,x) then Bm​(h,x)=E[h(X/m)], and Var(X/m)=x(1−x)/m≤1/(4m). Splitting according to whether ∣X/m−x∣≤δ and using Chebyshev's inequality gives
∥Bm​h−h∥∞​≤ω(h,δ)+2mδ2∥h∥∞​​.
(11)
First let m→∞ and then δ→0; this proves uniform convergence for each continuous h. Finally,
∥Bn(r)​f−f(r)∥∞​≤∥Bn−r​(gn,r​−f(r))∥∞​+∥Bn−r​f(r)−f(r)∥∞​⟶0.
(12)
Thus Bernstein polynomial derivatives converge uniformly to the corresponding derivatives of f, for each fixed r with f∈Cr[0,1]. Continuity of f alone is sufficient for the definition of Bn​, but not for this derivative conclusion.
