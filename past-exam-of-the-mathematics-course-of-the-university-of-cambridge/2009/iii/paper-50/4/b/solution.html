Yes, the conversion can be deterministic using local operations and classical communication. Put s=sinθ and c=cosθ. The initial state's squared Schmidt coefficients are (1/2,1/2), while the target's decreasingly ordered squared Schmidt coefficients are (c2,s2). Since 0<θ<π/4, one has 1/2<c2 and both pairs sum to one. Thus
(1/2,1/2)≺(c2,s2).
(43)
The direction of this majorization is the allowed one in Nielsen's pure-state conversion theorem: maximal entanglement can be reduced by deterministic LOCC.
Here is an explicit protocol, so no conversion theorem need be assumed. The sender first applies a Pauli Z gate to remove the initial Bell-state minus sign. Starting from ∣Φ+⟩, she performs a two-outcome local measurement with Kraus operators
M0​=(s0​0c​),M1​=(c0​0s​).
(44)
They define a valid measurement because M0†​M0​+M1†​M1​=I. The two unnormalized states after measurement are
(M0​⊗I)∣Φ+⟩=2​s∣00⟩+c∣11⟩​,(M1​⊗I)∣Φ+⟩=2​c∣00⟩+s∣11⟩​.
(45)
Each outcome has probability 1/2. For outcome zero, the normalized state is already s∣00⟩+c∣11⟩. For outcome one, both parties apply Pauli X gates, obtaining the same state. The sender communicates only which outcome occurred. Finally the receiver applies a Pauli X gate, giving s∣01⟩+c∣10⟩ in either branch. This is deterministic two-qubit entanglement dilution, with total success probability one; it does not rely on postselection.
