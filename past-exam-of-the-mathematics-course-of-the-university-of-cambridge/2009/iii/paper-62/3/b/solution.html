To distinguish the moment quantity from the gravitational constant, write it as G=∑i​pi​⋅ri​. Newton's equations give p˙​i​=Fi​ and r˙i​=pi​/mi​. Differentiating proves
dtdG​=i∑​mi​pi2​​+i∑​Fi​⋅ri​=2T+i∑​Fi​⋅ri​.​
(33)
Average from 0 to τ, divide by τ, and apply the supplied long-time boundary condition. The result is
0=2⟨T⟩+⟨∑i​Fi​⋅ri​⟩,2⟨T⟩=−⟨i∑​Fi​⋅ri​⟩.​
(34)
The PDF's intermediate formula omits this minus sign. It cannot follow from the preceding derivative identity: an attractive inverse-square circular orbit already has F⋅r<0 and T>0, contradicting the printed positive-sign version. The later homogeneous-potential relation is consistent with the corrected sign.
Interpret W as the total pair potential energy, with each pair counted once, for example W=∑i<j​C∣ri​−rj​∣p+1. The printed pair sum leaves one particle index unspecified; an ordered-pair convention with the corresponding factor absorbed into C has the same degree of homogeneity. Under a common dilation of all positions,
W(λr1​,…,λrN​)=λp+1W(r1​,…,rN​).
(35)
Differentiating with respect to λ at one is the Euler theorem for homogeneous functions. Since Fi​=−∇i​W, it gives
∑i​Fi​⋅ri​=−(p+1)W.
(36)
Substitution into the correctly signed averaged identity proves the virial theorem for a homogeneous pair potential:
2⟨T⟩−(p+1)⟨W⟩=0.​
(37)
For p=−1, the conserved total energy can be evaluated using these averages:
Etot​=⟨T⟩+⟨W⟩=p+1p+3​⟨T⟩.
(38)
Use the kinetic temperature convention ⟨T⟩=κΘ, where κ=3NkB​/2>0 for three-dimensional thermalized particles, or the same positive coefficient as a kinetic-temperature definition. At fixed particle number and interaction parameters,
dΘdEtot​​=κp+1p+3​.
(39)
Thus the equilibrium heat capacity is negative precisely when
−3<p<−1,​
(40)
subject to existence of the bounded equilibrium sequence assumed by the virial theorem. At p=−3 the virial energy is zero and this derivative vanishes. At p=−1 the written power-law potential is constant, its force vanishes, and the bounded-motion virial condition excludes nonzero kinetic energy; division by p+1 is not permissible. The Newtonian case p=−2 recovers Etot​=−⟨T⟩.
