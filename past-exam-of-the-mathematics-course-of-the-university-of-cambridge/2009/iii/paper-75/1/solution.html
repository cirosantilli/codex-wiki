The homogeneous fixed point must satisfy v=u2. The singular point u=v=0 is outside the domain of the reaction term, so substitution in the other equation gives the unique regular spatially homogeneous equilibrium
u∗​=1+r,v∗​=(1+r)2.​
(1)
This is the basally forced quadratic activator-inhibitor model. The linearization of its reaction terms gives the Jacobian matrix
J=​r+1r−1​2q(1+r)​−(1+r)2r​−q​​.
(2)
Put a=(r−1)/(r+1), so −1<a<1. Its trace and determinant are a−q and q, and its eigenvalues are
λ±​=2a−q±(a−q)2−4q​​.
(3)
Since q>0, both eigenvalues have negative real part exactly when q>a. Hence
q>r+1r−1​: linearly asymptotically stable;0<q<r+1r−1​: unstable.​
(4)
For r≤1 the equilibrium is always strictly linearly stable. For r>1, equality q=a is an oscillatory threshold with λ±​=±ia​; the nonlinear qualification at this threshold is proved below.
Now insert a perturbation proportional to eλt+ikx. The diffusion terms subtract k2diag(1,p) from J. With s=k2 the mode trace and determinant are
τ(s)=a−q−(1+p)s,Δ(s)=ps2+(q−pa)s+q.
(5)
For a strictly stable homogeneous state, q>a makes τ(s)<0 for all s≥0. A nonzero Fourier mode can therefore grow only if Δ(s)<0, in which case its two real eigenvalues have opposite signs. The determinant parabola has its minimum at s∗​=(pa−q)/(2p), and that minimum is q−(pa−q)2/(4p). The two-species Turing criterion is consequently derived here as
q>a,pa>q,(pa−q)2>4pq.​
(6)
These force a>0, hence r>1. Equivalently, solving pa−q>2pq​ for p​ gives
r>1,q>a,p>pc​=(1+a​−1)2q​.​
(7)
Thus the inhibitor in a reaction-diffusion system must diffuse sufficiently fast. Inside this region, the growing wavenumbers obey
s−​<k2<s+​,s±​=2ppa−q±(pa−q)2−4pq​​.
(8)
At onset, the minimum touches zero and the two determinant roots coalesce. Therefore
kc2​=2ppa−q​=pq​​,λc​=kc​2π​=2π(qp​)1/4.​
(9)
Here p is evaluated on the onset surface. These are the Turing threshold of a basally forced quadratic activator-inhibitor model and its continuum critical wavelength. On a finite or domain with periodic boundary conditions an admissible nonzero spatial mode must additionally fall in the stated band. Equality in the diffusion criterion is marginal onset, while q=a is the distinct homogeneous oscillatory threshold.
For completeness, the weak focus at the Hopf threshold of a basal activator-inhibitor model resolves the nonlinear homogeneous stability when q=a>0. Let h=(1+a)/2, β=(1−a)/2, ω=a​, and set u=(1+r)(1+x), v=(1+r)2(1+y), X=x, Y=(hy−ax)/ω. Expanding the rational reaction term to cubic order gives
X˙=−ωY+F2​+F3​+O(4),Y˙=ωX+G2​+G3​+O(4),
(10)
where
F2​=h(βX−ωY)2​,F3​=−h2(aX+ωY)(βX−ωY)2​,G2​=ω(hX2−F2​),G3​=−ωF3​.
(11)
Write X=ϱcosθ, Y=ϱsinθ and evaluate these homogeneous polynomials on the unit circle. If A=cosθF2​+sinθG2​, B=cosθF3​+sinθG3​, and C=cosθG2​−sinθF2​, division of the radial and angular equations gives
dθdϱ​=ωA​ϱ2+(ωB​−ω2AC​)ϱ3+O(ϱ4).
(12)
The angular integral of A is zero because it changes sign under θ↦θ+π. Iterating its quadratic term once over a full cycle contributes the square of this zero integral, so the first nonzero return map term is cubic. Using ⟨cos4θ⟩=3/8, ⟨cos2θsin2θ⟩=1/8 and the corresponding sixth moments gives ⟨B⟩=a/4 and ⟨AC⟩/ω=(a+1)/8. The Poincaré map is
ϱ⟼ϱ+ω2π​8a−1​ϱ3+O(ϱ4).
(13)
Because 0<a<1, sufficiently small positive radii decrease on every return. The equilibrium at q=a is therefore nonlinearly asymptotically stable, with weak rather than exponential attraction, despite its neutral linear eigenvalues. The strict inequalities above describe the ordinary diffusion-driven instability from a strictly linearly stable homogeneous state.
