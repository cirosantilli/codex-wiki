Independence of the segment vectors makes the end-to-end distribution an N-fold convolution. Use the characteristic function convention ψ​(k)=∫eik⋅rψ(r)d3r. For a three-dimensional isotropic freely jointed chain, the radial delta function fixes ∣r∣=b, and angular integration gives
ψ​(k)=4π1​∫eikbcosθdΩ=21​∫−11​eikbμdμ=kbsin(kb)​.
(23)
The characteristic function of a sum of independent variables therefore gives Φ(k,N)=[sin(kb)/(kb)]N. In the central large-N scaling, k=O((bN​)−1), so
Nlnkbsin(kb)​=−6Nb2k2​+O(Nb4k4)=−6Nb2k2​+O(N−1).
(24)
Inverting this Gaussian Fourier transform yields
Φ(R,N)∼(2πNb23​)3/2exp[−2Nb23R2​].​
(25)
This Gaussian limit of the end-to-end distribution of a freely jointed chain is normalized with respect to d3R, has ⟨R⟩=0 and ⟨R2⟩=Nb2, and is valid on the coil scale R=O(bN​). If a density of the scalar distance R is wanted instead, it is 4πR2Φ. The Gaussian approximation does not describe the nearly fully stretched tail or the exact finite-chain cutoff at R=Nb.
For the scattering calculation, radial single-segment distributions together with the given independent-segment assumption imply rotational invariance of the whole chain. Therefore each pair separation S=Rn​−Rm​ has a uniform direction conditional on its magnitude S. The same angular average gives
⟨eik⋅S∣S⟩=21​∫−11​eikSμdμ=kSsin(kS)​.
(26)
Averaging its magnitude and then summing the pairs proves the required expression for the polymer scattering function:
g(k)=N1​m,n=1∑N​⟨k∣Rn​−Rm​∣sin(k∣Rn​−Rm​∣)​⟩.​
(27)
The ratio has value one at zero separation, including every diagonal term m=n.
The printed gyration formula leaves m unsummed. The intended radius of gyration must use a double pair sum, equivalently the mean square distance from the chain's center of mass. To establish that equivalence, put Rcm​=N−1∑n​Rn​ and expand both sides of
∑m,n​∣Rm​−Rn​∣2=2N∑n​∣Rn​−Rcm​∣2.
(28)
Thus the consistent definition is Rg2​=(2N2)−1∑m,n​⟨∣Rm​−Rn​∣2⟩. Expanding sinz/z=1−z2/6+O(z4) gives
g(k)​=N−6Nk2​m,n∑​⟨∣Rm​−Rn​∣2⟩+O(k4)=N[1−3k2Rg2​​+O(k4)].​
(29)
Therefore the general small-k result is
Ng(k)​=1−3k2Rg2​​+O(k4),kRg​≪1.​
(30)
This Guinier expansion of a polymer scattering function requires isotropy and finite pair moments, but not Gaussian distributions.
For a Gaussian chain, each pair difference across ℓ=∣n−m∣ links has variance ⟨∣Rn​−Rm​∣2⟩=b2ℓ and characteristic function e−k2b2ℓ/6. Using exactly the N scattering sites in the printed double sum, the finite-chain expression is
gN​(k)=1+N2​ℓ=1∑N−1​(N−ℓ)zℓ=1−z1+z​−N(1−z)22z(1−zN)​,z=e−k2b2/6.​
(31)
The closed expression is understood by continuity at z=1, where it equals N. The factor N−ℓ counts pairs with n=m+ℓ, with the prefactor two supplying their reversed order. In this finite-site convention,
Rg2​=N2b2​∑ℓ=1N−1​(N−ℓ)ℓ=6Nb2(N2−1)​.
(32)
This also holds for a freely jointed chain because its link covariances give the same mean square pair distances.
To obtain the complete long-chain scaling function, set x=Nk2b2/6≃k2Rg2​ and replace contour sums by integrals, keeping x fixed as N→∞. With a=k2b2/6,
g(k)​∼N1​∫0N​∫0N​e−a∣n−m∣dndm=N2​∫0N​(N−s)e−asds=x22N(e−x−1+x)​.​
(33)
The requested Debye scattering function for a Gaussian chain is consequently
g(k)=ND(k2Rg2​),D(x)=x22(e−x−1+x)​,D(0)=1,​
(34)
exactly for the continuous Gaussian contour convention Rg2​=Nb2/6, and asymptotically for the long discrete chain. Its small-x expansion is D(x)=1−x/3+x2/12+⋯, agreeing with the general gyration result, and D(x)∼2/x at large x. In the continuum coil range 1≪x≪N this gives g∼12/(k2b2). At still larger kb, the discrete expression instead tends to the self-scattering value one.
There is a harmless finite-size convention in the source: N links have N+1 endpoints, whereas its scattering sum counts N sites. The formulas above retain its scattering normalization; if all endpoints are counted, replace the site count by N+1 in the finite formulas. The large-N Gaussian distribution and Debye scaling function are unchanged to leading order.
