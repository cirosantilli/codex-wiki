At a regular neutral Rayleigh equation for inviscid shear flow mode, multiply the equation by U−c0​ and evaluate it at the critical layer in a shear flow. The term multiplying ϕ0′′​−k02​ϕ0​ vanishes, leaving U′′(0)p=0. Therefore U′′(0)=0. If this condition failed, the coefficient U′′/(U−c0​) would have a simple pole and a nonzero p would force a logarithmically singular slope instead of a regular eigenfunction.
There is a sign error in the printed first-order equation. Direct expansion of the original Rayleigh equation for inviscid shear flow gives
L0​ϕ1​=Hϕ0​,L0​=dy2d2​−k02​−U−c0​U′′​,H=2k0​k1​+(U−c0​)2c1​U′′​.

The coefficient on the left must have a minus sign. Indeed, 1/(U−c)=1/(U−c0​)+εc1​/(U−c0​)2+⋯, and moving both first-order parameter corrections to the right gives exactly this equation. The plus sign printed there is incompatible with the original equation and its neutral eigenfunction. The later matching and integral formulas follow from the corrected equation. The PDF also has y3h/6 in the Taylor expansion of U, and U′′′(0) in the requested logarithmic coefficient; these are mistranscribed in the converted TeX.
Near zero, U−c0​=dy+hy3/6+⋯ and U′′=hy+O(y2). Thus U′′/(U−c0​)→h/d, whereas U′′/(U−c0​)2=h/(d2y)+O(1). The singular part of the first-order equation is

The outer expansion of 1/(U−c) fails when ∣dy∣ is comparable with ε∣c1​∣. Therefore the distinguished limit is
y=εη,ℓ=1.​
(31)

Φηη​=ε2(k2+U(εη)−cU′′(εη)​)Φ.

Φ2′′​=pk02​+dη−c1​phη​.

Integrating, or using the supplied hint, gives
Φ2​=2p​(k02​+dh​)η2+d3phc1​​[(dη−c1​)log(dη−c1​)−(dη−c1​)]+Bη+C.​

The constants B,C are homogeneous matching freedoms; they do not affect the slope jump. The quadratic term agrees with ϕ0′′​(0)=p(k02​+h/d), another check on the sign of L0​.
For Imc1​>0, dη−c1​ runs below the real axis. Choose the continuous branch of the complex logarithm there. At positive infinity its argument tends to zero; at negative infinity it tends to −π. Thus the linear parts of the two large-∣η∣ expansions of Φ2​ are
η→+∞:η→−∞:​βηlog∣η∣+[B+β(logd−1)]η,βηlog∣η∣+[B+β(logd−1)−iπβ]η.​

The sign comes from passing below the pole; taking Imc1​<0 would reverse it.
Finally use the Wronskian W=ϕ1​ϕ0′​−ϕ0​ϕ1′​. Since L0​ϕ0​=0 and L0​ϕ1​=Hϕ0​, subtraction gives W′=−Hϕ02​. The wall boundary conditions give W(a)=W(b)=0, so

W(δ)−W(−δ)⟶−p(b+​−b−​)=−d2iπc1​hp2​.
