Unlike the small-argument Taylor series, these large-argument asymptotic expansions must be truncated; adding indefinitely many terms at a fixed argument does not improve the approximation.
A calculator needs elementary arithmetic and its built-in sine and cosine, together with stored approximation coefficients. A convenient global construction uses compactified approximation from endpoint asymptotics. Define the exact real tail functions by
Q+iP=e−iλ2(A(1+i)−C−iS),u=1+λλ​.
(11)
Then P(0)=Q(0)=A, while P∼1/(2λ) and Q∼1/(4λ3) at infinity. Consequently
p(u)=(1+λ)P(λ),q(u)=(1+λ)3Q(λ)
(12)
extend continuously to the compact interval 0≤u≤1, with p(1)=1/2 and q(1)=1/4. Choose stored polynomial approximations pN​,qN​ on that interval, constrained by the small-argument Taylor series and large-argument asymptotic expansions, and calibrated across the intervening interval. The resulting elementary formula is
Ccalc​(λ)=A+1+λpN​(u)​sinλ2−(1+λ)3qN​(u)​cosλ2.​
(13)
Uniform errors δp​,δq​ in the stored polynomial approximations give ∣Ccalc​−C∣≤δp​+δq​ for every positive argument. Existence of arbitrarily accurate such approximations follows from the Weierstrass approximation theorem. A Padé approximant with a denominator having no positive zero is an alternative implementation. Matching the two end expansions alone is not an error guarantee: the middle interval must also be controlled. For very small arguments, switching to the short Taylor series avoids cancellation. Ordinary finite-precision limitations in evaluating λ2 and reducing its trigonometric phase remain those of the calculator itself.
