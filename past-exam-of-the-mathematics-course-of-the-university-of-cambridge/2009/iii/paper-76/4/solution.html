For real physical disturbances, take the real part of these complex Fourier modes. The vorticity generally keeps oscillating rather than approaching a pointwise limit, even while the velocity decays. This is linear inviscid damping in unbounded Couette flow caused by phase mixing.
For arbitrary initial vorticity, the characteristics satisfy x˙=y, y˙​=0, and ω˙=0. Therefore the exact solution is
ω(x,y,t)=ω0​(x−yt,y).​
(51)
Putting ω0​=−F(y)eiαx recovers the preceding nondecaying mode. In general the shear is area preserving, so its transport preserves every finite Lp norm of vorticity. Local pointwise behavior for arbitrary ω0​ depends on its spatial profile: an initially localized profile can move away from a fixed point. There is no universal pointwise nonzero limit or t−2 decay of vorticity for arbitrary initial data.
The stated smoothness and decay of ϕ alone do not guarantee the claimed algebraic rates. The intended integration by parts calculation requires control of derivatives and tails; convergence of the displayed integral alone is insufficient.
