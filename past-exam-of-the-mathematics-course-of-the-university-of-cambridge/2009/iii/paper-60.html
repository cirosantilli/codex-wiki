Use signature (−,+,+,+), {γμ,γν}=2ημν, and unit-weight antisymmetrized gamma products. The massless Rarita-Schwinger field has the gauge freedom
ψμ​⟼ψμ​+∂μ​ϵ,
(1)
since the antisymmetric γμνρ annihilates ∂ν​∂ρ​ϵ. This gauge freedom is essential to remove the lower-spin components of the vector-spinor.
Put χ=γμψμ​. Contracting the field equation with γμ​ and using γμ​γμνρ=2γνρ gives
∂χ−∂μψμ​=0.
(2)
Expanding the original three-gamma product then reduces its equation to
∂ψμ−∂μχ=0.
(3)
A gauge transformation changes χ by ∂ϵ. Locally solve ∂ϵ=−χ to impose gamma-trace gauge. The two equations become
γ⋅ψ=0,∂⋅ψ=0,∂ψμ​=0.​
(4)
Applying ∂ again gives □ψμ​=0. Nonzero propagating Fourier modes therefore have p2=0: the physical modes are massless.
For the massless Rarita-Schwinger polarization count, choose a nonzero future null momentum pμ=(ω,0,0,ω). The remaining gauge parameters satisfy pϵ=0. Transversality gives ψ0​+ψ3​=0, and this residual gauge removes ψ0​, hence also ψ3​, because ψ0​ itself satisfies pψ0​=0. Only ψ1​,ψ2​ remain, with
pψ1​=pψ2​=0,γ1ψ1​+γ2ψ2​=0,ψ2​=γ1γ2ψ1​.
(5)
The kernel of p on four-component spinors has complex dimension two, so there are exactly two independent polarization amplitudes.
To identify their helicities, the spinor rotation generator about the third axis in this convention is S3​=−iγ1γ2/2. Its two eigenvalues on that kernel are hs​=±1/2. If ψ1​ is such an eigenvector, then ψ2​=2ihs​ψ1​. Its transverse vector polarization is consequently (1,+i) for hs​=+1/2 or (1,−i) for hs​=−1/2. These carry vector helicity +1 or −1, respectively. Adding the vector and spinor angular momenta leaves
h=+23​,h=−23​,two physical helicity states.​
(6)
The unwanted opposite-sign vector-spinor combinations, which would have helicity ±1/2, violate the gamma-trace constraint. For the gravitino of simple supergravity, its Majorana spinor reality condition identifies the negative-frequency antiparticle modes, giving two propagating degrees of freedom. If one instead chooses a complex Dirac vector-spinor, its independent antiparticle sector doubles the particle-plus-antiparticle count; the equation alone does not impose a Majorana condition.

The chirality matrix is covariantly constant under the metric-preserving spin connection and anticommutes with every γμ. Hence
∇(γ5​ψ)=−γ5​∇ψ=mγ5​ψ,(∇−m)γ5​ψ=0.​
(7)
Thus multiplication by γ5​ reverses the sign of the mass parameter. At m=0 it maps solutions into solutions of the same Dirac equation.
Use the spinor curvature identity
[∇μ​,∇ν​]ψ=41​Rμνab​γabψ.
(8)
Here the rough Laplacian includes the affine connection on the index of the first derivative. Clifford expansion gives
∇2ψ=∇2ψ+21​γμν[∇μ​,∇ν​]ψ=∇2ψ+81​γμνRμνab​γabψ.
(9)
In the last contraction, the four-gamma part vanishes by the first Bianchi identity, the two-gamma parts vanish by symmetry of the Ricci tensor, and the scalar contractions give −2R. Therefore the Lichnerowicz spinor-square formula is
∇2=∇2−4R​.
(10)
Multiplying the first-order equation by −∇+m now proves
(−∇2+4R​+m2)ψ=0.​
(11)
The explicit curvature coefficient is fixed by the minimal spinor coupling; it is not an arbitrary scalar-field curvature coupling.
For the general metric-preserving connection, define Tρμν​=Γμνρ​−Γνμρ​ and let RT be its curvature. Distinguish the spin connection acting on the spinor from the full second derivative acting also on its derivative index. Their antisymmetric relation is
∇μT​∇νT​ψ−∇νT​∇μT​ψ=41​RμνabT​γabψ−Tρμν​∇ρT​ψ.
(12)
Thus the torsion correction to the Dirac square gives the exact squared field equation
[−(∇T)2+21​γμνTρμν​∇ρT​−81​γμνRμνabT​γab+m2]ψ=0.​
(13)
General torsion removes the curvature symmetries used in the preceding simplification. The curvature contraction may now contain nonscalar Clifford terms, in addition to the displayed first-order torsion term; simply replacing R by a torsionful scalar in the torsion-free answer is not valid.
Equivalently, write the connection as Levi-Civita plus contorsion tensor Cμab​. Then ∇T​=∇0​+Q, where Q=γμCμab​γab/4, and the square contains {∇0​,Q}+Q2. This exhibits the extra couplings through derivatives of contorsion, first-order spinor derivatives and quadratic contorsion. Metric compatibility still preserves γ5​, so the mass-sign result remains valid. Spin therefore couples directly to curvature through its connection and the universal R/4 term, and to torsion through additional spin-dependent terms. The scalar reduction in the torsion-free square does not mean the full spinor transport is insensitive to the remaining Riemann curvature.

Work through terms quadratic in the fermions, with Grassmann-odd Majorana spinors ψμ​ and ϵ. At this order the spin connection can be taken torsion-free: eliminating its gravitino-induced torsion changes the action only at four-fermion order. The Christoffel term needed to turn the printed Dν​ψσ​ into a full vector-spinor covariant derivative drops out after contraction with γμνσ, since the connection is symmetric in ν,σ.
First establish the Einstein tensor contraction of spin curvature. Expanding the Clifford product and using the first Bianchi identity, its five-gamma and antisymmetric three-gamma curvature contributions vanish, leaving
γμνρRνρab​γab=4Rμλ​γλ−2Rγμ.
(14)
Since [∇ν​,∇ρ​]=Rνρab​γab/4, it follows that
γμνρ∇ν​∇ρ​ϵ=21​Gμλ​γλϵ.​
(15)
The factor 1/2 arises from antisymmetrizing the two derivatives, not from a choice of Einstein-Hilbert normalization.
For the Einstein-Hilbert action, the metric variation is
δgμν​=2κ​(ψˉ​μ​γν​ϵ+ψˉ​ν​γμ​ϵ).
(16)
Variation with respect to the covariant metric, including the determinant variation and discarding the usual boundary divergence, gives
δSEH​=−2κ21​∫∣e∣Gμνδgμν​d4x=−2κ1​∫∣e∣ψˉ​μ​Gμλ​γλϵd4x.
(17)
No gravitational field equation has been assumed here.
For the Rarita-Schwinger field action, the determinant, gamma-matrix and connection variations each multiply a term already quadratic in ψ. Since δe is bilinear in ψ,ϵ, these contributions have four fermionic factors and are outside the requested order. Retain only the variations of the two explicit gravitino factors. The four-dimensional Majorana bilinear interchange gives χˉ​γμνρη=ηˉ​γμνρχ for odd spinors. After integration by parts, the first gravitino variation is
21​∫∣e∣δψμ​​γμνρ∇ν​ψρ​=−21​∫∣e∣ψˉ​ρ​γμνρ∇ν​δψμ​=21​∫∣e∣ψˉ​μ​γμνρ∇ν​δψρ​,
(18)
where the last step swaps μ,ρ and reverses the three-gamma sign. It is exactly equal to the variation of the second gravitino factor. Therefore
δSRS​=∫∣e∣ψˉ​μ​γμνρ∇ν​δψρ​=κ1​∫∣e∣ψˉ​μ​γμνρ∇ν​∇ρ​ϵ=2κ1​∫∣e∣ψˉ​μ​Gμλ​γλϵ.
(19)
The curvature contribution cancels δSEH​ pointwise. Thus
δ(SEH​+SRS​)=0​
(20)
up to boundary terms and the omitted four-fermion-order variations. This directly proves the stated local supersymmetry invariance with its printed coefficients. At higher order, the torsion and quartic-fermion completion of minimal four-dimensional supergravity is needed; it cannot be inferred by dropping the terms indefinitely.

On a bosonic background, a Killing spinor is a nonzero spinor parameter that makes all fermionic supersymmetry variations vanish. It identifies an unbroken supersymmetry of the background; the number of independent solutions counts preserved supercharges. In simple ungauged minimal four-dimensional supergravity, with the gravitino set to zero, its equation is ∇μ​ϵ=0 and the bosonic field equations are Rμν​=0. In theories with additional fields or a cosmological deformation, the Killing-spinor equation instead uses the corresponding modified connection.
Use commuting spinors to form ordinary geometric bilinears, and orient the Dirac current to the future with ϵˉ=−ϵ†γ0^ in mostly-plus signature. Since the spinor and gamma matrices are parallel,
∇μ​Kν​=(∇μ​ϵˉ)γν​ϵ+ϵˉγν​∇μ​ϵ=0.
(21)
Thus K is actually parallel, and in particular is a Killing vector field. In a local orthonormal frame,
K0^=ϵ†ϵ>0,Ki^=ϵ†αiϵ,αi=−γ0^γi^.
(22)
For every spatial unit vector u, ui​αi is Hermitian and squares to one. Its eigenvalues are ±1, so ∣ui​Ki∣≤K0. Taking u along the spatial current gives
Kμ​Kμ=−(K0)2+∣K∣2≤0.​
(23)
A nonzero parallel spinor cannot vanish at an isolated point, so this causal Dirac current is nonzero everywhere on a connected solution. Its squared norm is constant: it cannot alternate between timelike and null regions.
If K is timelike, normalize its constant norm. Its parallel dual one-form is closed, and its orthogonal distribution is integrable. Adapted local coordinates put the metric in the product form
ds2=−dt2+hij​(x)dxidxj.
(24)
The time independence follows because K is Killing, and the unit lapse and zero cross terms follow from parallelism. The vacuum equations give Rij​(h)=0. In three dimensions the Riemann tensor is completely determined by the Ricci tensor and its scalar contraction, so h is flat. Therefore
Every timelike-current solution is locally Minkowski spacetime.​
(25)
Constant spinors in its flat frame provide all local Killing spinors. Global identifications are possible only when they preserve a compatible spin structure and the required spinors.
If K is null, its parallelism first gives a Brinkmann coordinate system with K=∂v​. The parallel-spinor condition further restricts the local spin holonomy to the stabilizer of that spinor. In four dimensions, for a nonzero Weyl component represented by (1,0)T, its stabilizer in SL(2,C) is the upper-triangular group (10​b1​), b∈C: the two real null rotations, with no transverse rotation. Consequently the transverse frame is flat and the allowed curvature is of the wave type Ruiuj​. Using the coordinate freedom to remove transverse cross terms gives the local plane-fronted gravitational wave form
ds2=−2dudv+dx2+dy2+H(u,x,y)du2,Hxx​+Hyy​=0.​
(26)
Here Ruiuj​=−Hij​/2 and the only potentially nonzero Ricci component is Ruu​=−(Hxx​+Hyy​)/2, so the last equation is exactly the vacuum equation. Conversely, in the coframe e+=du, e−=dv−Hdu/2, ei=dxi, the nonzero spin-connection matrices are proportional to Hi​γ+idu. Constant spinors with γ+ϵ=0 are therefore parallel. Every harmonic transverse profile, with arbitrary u dependence, gives such a supersymmetric vacuum wave; the flat profile is included. This verifies both the form and sufficiency in the parallel-spinor classification of vacuum four-geometries.
There is a reality-condition subtlety in interpreting the branches. A single commuting four-dimensional Majorana spinor has a null current of a commuting Majorana spinor: its two-component representation gives ∣ξ†σξ∣2=(ξ†ξ)2. A timelike current can instead come from a Dirac Killing spinor or a sum of independent real-spinor currents. The conditional timelike classification above remains valid, but does not assert a generic timelike current for a single real Majorana parameter.
For the final question use constant sectional curvature −1/a2, so Rμν​=−3gμν​/a2. The spinor curvature identity gives
[∇μ​,∇ν​]ϵ=−2a21​γμν​ϵ.
(27)
Define the Anti-de Sitter Killing-spinor connection Dμ​=∇μ​+γμ​/(2a). Since ∇μ​γν​=0 and the connection is torsion-free,
[Dμ​,Dν​]=−2a21​γμν​+4a21​[γμ​,γν​]=0.
(28)
Thus the modified connection is flat. On a simply connected region, transport any initial spinor with this connection; flatness makes the result path independent and supplies actual nonzero solutions, not merely a necessary integrability condition. Hence
∇μ​ϵ+2a1​γμ​ϵ=0​
(29)
has a full local space of solutions on Anti-de Sitter spacetime, and globally on its universal cover. Global quotients require compatible spinor monodromy. These are Killing spinors of the cosmological deformation, not parallel-spinor vacua of the undeformed zero-cosmological-constant theory.

Take a complete smooth asymptotically flat spacelike initial-data hypersurface Σ, carrying a spin structure, without inner boundary. Let n be its future unit normal, h its induced metric, and use the extrinsic curvature convention kij​=−g(∇i​n,ej​). Assume the Einstein constraints, the dominant energy condition, and the standard decay sufficient for finite ADM charges. In one asymptotically Cartesian end it is enough here to work with hij​=δij​+qij​, q=O(r−1), ∂q=O(r−2), k=O(r−2), and the corresponding differentiated decay and integrability assumptions. The desired conclusion is positivity of the whole ADM four-momentum, not merely of the energy in one selected frame.
Introduce a commuting auxiliary spinor ϵ; this is not an anticommuting gravitino field. With ϵˉ=−ϵ†γ0^ its causal Dirac spinor current is Kμ=ϵˉγμϵ, future causal. The Nester two-form is
Bμν=ϵˉγμνρ∇ρ​ϵ−∇ρ​ϵ​γμνρϵ.
(30)
It is real in these conventions and antisymmetric in μ,ν. Differentiating, the two terms quadratic in first derivatives become equal after exchanging ν,ρ. The remaining terms are curvature commutators. The Einstein tensor contraction of spin curvature gives γμνρ∇ν​∇ρ​=Gμλ​γλ/2; the adjoint term supplies the other half. Thus
∇ν​Bμν=2∇ν​ϵ​γμνρ∇ρ​ϵ+Gμλ​Kλ.
(31)
This is the central spinor-curvature identity, derived by differentiation rather than posited as a positivity formula.
Project onto Σ and put bi=nμ​Bμi. In an adapted orthonormal frame nμ​=(−1,0,0,0), Clifford algebra gives
bi=−2Re(ϵ†γij∇j​ϵ),nμ​2∇ν​ϵ​γμνρ∇ρ​ϵ=2(∑i​∣∇i​ϵ∣2−∣γi∇i​ϵ∣2).
(32)
Indeed the expansion of ∣γi∇i​ϵ∣2 is ∑i​∣∇i​ϵ∣2+(∇i​ϵ)†γij∇j​ϵ. With a Gaussian normal extension of the slice, the symmetric second fundamental form has zero contraction with the antisymmetric B, so the projected divergence is the intrinsic spatial divergence. This proves the Witten-Nester identity
Di​bi=2(i∑​∣∇i​ϵ∣2−∣Dϵ∣2)+Gμν​nμKν,D=γi∇i​.​
(33)
Here Di​ on b denotes the spatial metric derivative. On spinors, ∇i​ is the Sen spinor connection, the restriction of the spacetime connection, not just the intrinsic three-dimensional one:
∇i​ϵ=Di(3)​ϵ−21​kij​γjγ0^ϵ.
(34)
The extrinsic-curvature term is essential when the data have momentum.
Choose ϵ to solve the Witten spinor equation, Dϵ=0, approaching a prescribed constant ϵ∞​ in the chosen end and zero constants in any other ends. The Einstein constraints identify Gμν​nμKν=8πGTμν​nμKν. The dominant energy condition makes this nonnegative because both n and K are future causal. Consequently the right-hand side of the identity becomes a sum of nonnegative terms.
Existence of that spinor is an analytic part of the proof, not something established by the positivity identity alone. Extend ϵ∞​ smoothly using a cutoff and solve Du=−Dϵ0​ for a decaying correction. The spatial principal symbol γiξi​ squares to ∣ξ∣h2​, so the equation is elliptic. For a compactly supported spinor, the integrated identity gives
∥Dϵ∥L22​=∥∇ϵ∥L22​+4πG∫Σ​T(n,K)dV≥∥∇ϵ∥L22​.
(35)
Together with the weighted asymptotic estimates this gives the coercivity needed for elliptic existence of a Witten spinor. A decaying homogeneous solution would be parallel by the same identity and hence zero, excluding the kernel; the appropriate weighted elliptic solvability gives the correction. Under the stated smooth decay assumptions it has ϵ−ϵ∞​=O(r−1) and first derivative O(r−2). The rigorous analytic step is supplied by the spinor existence proof. This also explains why completeness, asymptotic data and the absence of uncontrolled inner boundary terms are hypotheses of the argument.
It remains to identify the boundary integral with the physical ADM charge. In the asymptotic symmetric coframe ei​a=δi​a+qia​/2+O(r−2), the leading spatial spin connection is
ωjkl(3)​=21​(∂l​qjk​−∂k​qjl​)+O(r−3).
(36)
Insert this and the Sen correction into bi. The real scalar part of γijγkl is δilδjk−δikδjl; its two-gamma part has imaginary expectation and drops out. It follows that the intrinsic connection contributes 21​(∂j​qij​−∂i​qjj​)K∞0^​. For the other term, symmetry of k removes the three-spatial-gamma product, leaving −(kij​−khij​)K∞j^​​. Thus the ADM boundary term of the Nester two-form has the expansion
bi=21​(∂j​qij​−∂i​qjj​)K∞0^​−(kij​−khij​)K∞j^​​−2Re(ϵ∞†​γij∂j​(ϵ−ϵ∞​))+O(r−3).
(37)
The constant-spinor derivative contribution integrates to zero on a large coordinate sphere. Its coefficient is antisymmetric in i,j; the corresponding tangential vector on the sphere is divergence free, so integration by parts removes it. Equivalently, the flux of γij∂j​u has identically zero divergence, with its asymptotic leading term having zero flux. The quadratic correction terms are O(r−3) and their surface integrals vanish in the limit.
Define the ADM energy and ADM momentum with the same conventions:
E=16πG1​lim∫Sr​​(∂j​qij​−∂i​qjj​)sidS,Pi​=8πG1​lim∫Sr​​(kij​−khij​)sjdS.
(38)
The boundary calculation then gives, including every normalization factor,
8πG1​lim∫Sr​​bisi​dS=EK∞0^​−P⋅K∞​.​
(39)
This is the energy associated with the asymptotic spinor's causal translation. In the zero-momentum rest frame it is the ADM mass times K∞0^​; more generally it proves the full energy-momentum bound.
Finally apply the divergence theorem to the Witten identity. There is no inner boundary, and the spinors assigned zero at other ends give no charge there. Therefore
EK∞0^​−P⋅K∞​=4πG1​∫Σ​i∑​∣∇i​ϵ∣2dV+∫Σ​T(n,K)dV≥0.​
(40)
Asymptotic constant spinors can produce every future null direction. Normalize K∞0^​=1 and choose its spatial direction along P; the inequality gives
E≥∣P∣≥0.​
(41)
This establishes the positive energy theorem and nonnegativity of the invariant mass when it is defined. For zero ADM energy, repeat the identity for a basis of asymptotic spinors: all become parallel and all matter contractions vanish. Their curvature integrability forces the spacetime curvature to vanish on the initial surface; the standard rigidity conclusion is that the data embed in Minkowski spacetime. Merely having a parallel null spinor in a local wave geometry would not by itself prove this global rigidity: completeness and asymptotic flatness remain essential.
