Suppress the common time factor eiωt and invert the stated Fourier transform with (2π)−1∫Φ(k,y)e−ikxdk. Use limiting absorption principle k0​↦k0​−i0, and choose γ=k2−k02​​ with positive real part for the evanescent spectrum. For ∣k∣<k0​ its outgoing continuation is γ=+ik02​−k2​. The pole −k0​ lies above the inversion contour and +k0​ below it. These prescriptions fix both radiation and pole signs.
The even interior scattered transform solves Φyy​−γ2Φ=0, so it is A(k)cosh(γy). The exterior transform is B(k)e−γ(∣y∣−b). Let D−(k) be the transform of their common upward normal derivative on y=b. On x>0 the rigid plates set both derivatives to zero; on x<0 continuity requires them to agree. Hence this derivative is supported on the negative half-line and is analytic below the transform contour. It follows that
A=γsinh(γb)D−​,B=−γD−​.
(16)
The difference between exterior and interior scattered traces on y=b is −D−/L, where L=γsinh(γb)e−γb. At the open negative half-line the total traces must match, so this difference equals the incident wave eik0​x. Its negative-half transform is −i/(k+k0​). The positive-half trace difference has an unknown upper-half-plane transform C+, giving the Wiener-Hopf equation
−LD−​=C+−k+k0​i​.
(17)
Factor L=L+L−, divide by L−, and subtract the forcing pole using C0​=L+(−k0​):
L−D−​−k+k0​iC0​​=−L+C++ik+k0​L+(k)−C0​​.
(18)
The left side is analytic below and the right side above; the pole in the last quotient is removable. Analytic continuation makes their common value entire. The finite-energy edge condition and algebraic factor growth make this entire remainder vanish: the allowed normal-velocity edge singularity is at most inverse-square-root, so its half-line transform is O(∣k∣−1/2), while L±=O(∣k∣1/2). The same edge condition excludes a growing polynomial remainder. This pole subtraction in a Wiener-Hopf equation yields
D−=k+k0​iC0​L−(k)​,Φscin​=γsinh(γb)(k+k0​)iC0​L−(k)cosh(γy)​.​
(19)
The corresponding exterior result for the Wiener-Hopf solution for an open parallel-plate waveguide is
Φscout​=−γ(k+k0​)iC0​L−(k)​e−γ(∣y∣−b)=−L+(k)(k+k0​)iC0​sinh(γb)​e−γ∣y∣.​
(20)
Both forms are useful: the first makes the common normal derivative transparent, and the second removes the apparent factor singularity at a grazing saddle.
The final unheaded request has a sign error in its printed exponent. To see this from the derived solution rather than an outgoing-decay assertion alone, simplify the interior transform to
Φscin​=L+(k)(k+k0​)iC0​e−γbcosh(γy)​.
(21)
Near k=−k0​, C0​/L+(k)=1+O(k+k0​) and e−γbcosh(γy)=1−bγ+O(γ2) for fixed ∣y∣<b. Thus
Φscin​=k+k0​i​−k+k0​ibγ​+O(1).
(22)
For x<0, inversion of the first term gives −eik0​x, cancelling the continued incident potential. The remaining branch-point term uses γ∼i2k0​​(k+k0​)1/2 on the specified contour, so it is b2k0​​(k+k0​)−1/2. Its inverse transform at x=−r is proportional to e−ik0​r+iπ/4/πr​. Therefore the upstream decay of an open parallel-plate waveguide is
ϕtot​(x,y)∼bπ(−x)2k0​​​eik0​x+iπ/4,x→−∞,∣y∣<b.​
(23)
The amplitude decays as (−x)−1/2, not (−x)1/2. The positive exponent is present in the original PDF, so it cannot be proved under the stated outgoing problem. The incident pole cancellation and the explicit branch expansion give the corrected conclusion.
