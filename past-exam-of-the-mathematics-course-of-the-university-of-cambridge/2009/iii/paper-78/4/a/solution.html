Fix time dependence e−iωt and use pressure amplitude for ψ. Write the incident acoustic plane wave as ψi​=Aeiαx−iβz, where α=kcosθ, β=ksinθ>0. “Perfectly reflecting” specifies zero absorbed energy but does not uniquely select a boundary condition: a rigid wall has a Neumann boundary condition, whereas a pressure-release surface has a Dirichlet boundary condition. We first give the rigid-wall result, and then the alternative pressure-release result.
For the rigid surface the normal-velocity condition, without an immaterial normalizing factor, is
(∂z​−h′(x)∂x​)ψ(x,h(x))=0.
(43)
Expand ψ=ψ[0]+ψ[1]+ψ[2]+⋯ in the height amplitude, scaling a fixed differentiable profile. The flat total field is ψ[0]=Aeiαx(e−iβz+eiβz). Taylor expansion gives
∂z​ψ[1](x,0)=−h∂z2​ψ[0](x,0)+h′∂x​ψ[0](x,0)=2Aeiαx(β2h+iαh′).
(44)
Let h(q)=∫h(x)e−iqxdx and β(q)=k2−q2​ on the outgoing sheet: it is positive real for propagating modes and positive imaginary for evanescent modes. Fourier transforming this boundary derivative gives 2A(k2−αq)h(q−α). The outgoing angular spectrum therefore yields the small-height Neumann scattering field
ψs​(x,z)=Aeiαx+iβz+2πi2A​∫R​β(q)k2−αq​h(q−α)eiqx+iβ(q)zdq+O(h2).​
(45)
For an infinite stationary surface the Fourier quantities may be interpreted as random distributions or through a finite illumination window and its limiting mean. The correction is linear in h and h′, so zero mean height and stationarity give
⟨ψs​⟩=Aeiαx+iβz+O(h2)for a rigid reflector.​
(46)
Only the rough correction has zero mean at first order; the flat reflected wave remains.
The first nontrivial coherent correction depends on correlation, not just the r.m.s. height. Define Ch​(s)=⟨h(x+s)h(x)⟩, its power spectrum of surface height Sh​(q)=∫Ch​(s)e−iqsds, and σ2=(2π)−1∫Sh​(q)dq. At second order the flat-field third normal derivative and mixed derivative vanish at zero, leaving
∂z​ψ[2](x,0)=−h∂z2​ψ[1](x,0)+h′∂x​ψ[1](x,0).
(47)
For each first-order outgoing Fourier component, its derivative data are g1​(q)=2A(k2−αq)h(q−α), its second normal derivative is iβ(q)g1​(q), and its tangential derivative is qg1​(q)/β(q). Averaging their products with h,h′ gives the mean derivative
⟨∂z​ψ[2](x,0)⟩=−2iAeiαx∫β(q)(k2−αq)2​Sh​(q−α)2πdq​.
(48)
Dividing by the specular outgoing derivative iβ therefore gives oblique coherent reflection from a stationary rigid rough surface:
⟨ψs​⟩=ArN​eiαx+iβz,rN​=1−πβ1​∫β(q)(k2−αq)2​Sh​(q−α)dq+O(h3).​
(49)
This second-order expression requires the relevant spectral moments to exist and the boundary expansion to remain controlled. Small ∣kh∣ alone does not control arbitrarily large slopes or high-frequency roughness. The given stationarity and σ determine the first-order mean above, but do not determine this spectrum-weighted correction numerically.
For a pressure-release surface, instead impose ψ(x,h)=0. The flat reflected coefficient is −1, and Taylor expansion gives ψ[1](x,0)=2iβAheiαx. Thus small-height Dirichlet scattering gives
ψs​=−Aeiαx+iβz+2π2iβA​∫h(q−α)eiqx+iβ(q)zdq+O(h2).​
(50)
The zero-mean first correction again vanishes on averaging. At second order ψ[2](x,0)=−h∂z​ψ[1](x,0), giving
⟨ψs​⟩=ArD​eiαx+iβz,rD​=−1+πβ​∫β(q)Sh​(q−α)dq+O(h3).​
(51)
If the roughness spectrum is concentrated near q=α after the incident tangential shift, the additional long-correlation approximation yields rN​≃1−2β2σ2 and rD​≃−1+2β2σ2. A local Gaussian random-height phase model would then give rN​=e−2β2σ2 and rD​=−e−2β2σ2, but Gaussian statistics and that local approximation were not supplied. They must not be inferred from stationarity and r.m.s. height alone.
