Put μ=μ1​+μ2​. In this heterogeneous two-server queue, the total population alone is not a Markov process: when only one customer is present, its departure rate depends on which server is occupied. Use states 0,a,b,2,3,…, where a and b distinguish the two single-customer possibilities, and n≥2 records the total population. The memoryless property of the exponential distributions gives these nonzero transition rates:
q0a​=q0b​=ν/2,qa0​=μ1​,qb0​=μ2​,qa2​=qb2​=ν,q2a​=μ2​,q2b​=μ1​,
(1)
and qn,n+1​=ν for n≥2, qn,n−1​=μ for n≥3. In particular, a departure from state 2 leaves the other server occupied.
The detailed balance equations suggest
πa​=2μ1​ν​π0​,πb​=2μ2​ν​π0​,πn​=2μ1​μ2​ν2​(μν​)n−2π0​(n≥2).
(2)
For example, both πa​ν=π2​μ2​ and πb​ν=π2​μ1​ hold; these are the two transitions that would be lost by using only population as the state. All other adjacent-state detailed balance equations hold as well. Since ν/μ<1, the geometric series converges, and normalization gives
π0−1​=1+2μ1​μ2​νμ​+2μ1​μ2​(μ−ν)ν2μ​=1+2μ1​μ2​(μ−ν)νμ2​.
(3)
Thus the stationary distribution exists, and the requested probability is
Pr{both busy}=n≥2∑​πn​=2μ1​μ2​(μ−ν)+νμ2ν2μ​.​
(4)
With equal server rates μ1​=μ2​=m, this reduces to ν2/[m(2m+ν)], as for an M-M-s queue with two servers.
To prove the output claim, put the continuous-time Markov chain in its stationary distribution. The preceding detailed balance equations make it a reversible Markov chain: time reversal of a continuous-time Markov chain has the same transition rates. A forward departure becomes a reversed arrival. In every reversed state, the total rate of such arrivals is ν: from 0 the rates ν/2 add, from a or b the upward rate is ν, and from every n≥2 it is also ν. The reversed process can therefore be constructed with an independent rate-ν Poisson process of arrival epochs, choosing the idle server by a fair coin when appropriate. Consequently reversed arrival counts on disjoint time intervals are independent Poisson random variables with the corresponding means. Reversing those intervals proves that the stationary departure stream is a rate-ν Poisson process, rather than merely a stream with mean rate ν.
Stationarity matters here. If the queue starts empty, a departure by time h requires both an arrival and a completed service time, so its probability is O(h2); a rate-ν Poisson process would have probability νh+O(h2). Thus the output assertion is the equilibrium assertion in the context of the preceding stationary calculation.

An open migration process has finitely many colonies and state n∈NJ. External arrivals to colony j form independent Poisson processes with rates νj​. When nj​=k>0, individuals leave that colony at total rate gj​(k)>0, with gj​(0)=0. A departing individual moves to k with probability pjk​ or exits with probability pj0​=1−∑k​pjk​. Each user maintains a transmission rate xr​. Resource j measures its aggregate load yj​=∑r:j∈r​xr​ and returns the resource congestion price μj​=pj​(yj​). This is a distributed congestion control algorithm: resources need only local loads, and users need only prices along their own routes.
There is a needed distinction in the word increasing. If it permits nondecreasing price functions, the all-zero prices are allowed. Then x˙r​=κr​wr​>0, so there is no equilibrium. The intended convergence statement holds when each nonempty route meets at least one resource whose price is positive at some load. This is automatic if every price is strictly increasing and nonnegative. We prove it under the more general nondecreasing assumption with this explicit condition, for finite sets of nonempty routes and nonnegative initial rates.

With positive arrival rates this is the unique stationary distribution of the irreducible chain. Zero arrival rates are handled by restricting to the accessible class with their counts zero, interpreting 00=1.
Summing this distribution over n0​ gives local-count independence in a linear flow network:
Pr{n1​=k1​,…,nI​=kI​}=∏i=1I​(1−1−ρ0​ρi​​)(1−ρ0​ρi​​)ki​.
(51)
These are independent geometric distributions. Differentiating a geometric series, or summing its mean directly, gives
Eni​=1−ρi​/(1−ρ0​)ρi​/(1−ρ0​)​=1−ρ0​−ρi​ρi​​(1≤i≤I).​
(52)
This independence concerns the local counts at one stationary time; their evolving processes share the through route and are not independent processes.
