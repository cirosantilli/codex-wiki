There is an admissibility qualification in passing from a bounded polygon to this unbounded quadrant. Sufficient conditions are H(z)→0 at infinity uniformly away from the axes, with the corresponding large-arc integral tending to zero, and ∣z∣H(z)→0 uniformly on small corner arcs. The latter allows logarithmic gradient singularities but excludes a pole with nonzero corner contribution. Smooth decaying data alone, as printed, do not impose these conditions on q.
For a concrete homogeneous corner ambiguity in a quadrant Laplace problem, with β1​=β2​=0 and zero data, q=xy satisfies both prescribed tangential derivative conditions but has qz​=−iz/2, while the displayed particular representation is zero. With β1​=β2​=π/2, the zero-data solution q=log∣z∣ has qz​=1/(2z): it decays at infinity but has an excluded corner pole. Thus no unique representation of every unrestricted solution follows from the printed data alone. Without the arc conditions, one must add a homogeneous holomorphic term P(z) to the displayed qz​, with reflected parity P(−z)=−ϵP(z); permitted corner singularities are included if the admissible class allows them. With the stated decay and corner conditions that term vanishes, giving the boxed formulas. This identifies the missing solution-class conditions without assuming real-valued data.
