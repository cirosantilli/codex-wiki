First distinguish concentration conventions. If C is cell number density, the excess mixture density is vΔρC. If φ=vC is cell volume fraction, it is Δρφ. The printed definition of R is dimensionless for number density, whereas the prose calls n^0​ a volume fraction. Use C0​ for mean number density and φ0​=vC0​ for mean volume fraction. Then the consistent Rayleigh number is
R=ρνDgC0​vΔρH3​=ρνDgφ0​ΔρH3​.​
(50)
The dimensionless concentration n=C/C0​=φ/φ0​ is the same in both conventions. The extra factor v must be removed if the source symbol is literally a volume fraction.
Remove the hydrostatic basic pressure and use a dilute Boussinesq approximation. With Ce​=C−C0​nˉ and perturbation fluid velocity u, the linear incompressible Newtonian fluid equations are
∇⋅u=0,∂t^​u=−ρ1​∇Pe​+νΔu−ρgvΔρ​Ce​e3​.
(51)
The cell conservation equation linearizes to
∂t^​Ce​+wC0​dz^dnˉ​+Vs​∂z^​Ce​+Vs​C0​∇⊥​⋅(nˉp⊥​)=DΔCe​.
(52)
At this order the vertical orientation perturbation is zero, because ∣p∣=1. The instantaneous-orientation approximation from the introductory calculation gives
p⊥​=2B​(ω×e3​)⊥​.
(53)
Incompressibility implies ∇⊥​⋅(ω×e3​)⊥​=−Δw. This identity fixes the sign of the gyrotactic term.
Apply the length, viscous-time, velocity, concentration and pressure scales in the paper. Writing ne​=n−nˉ, the dimensionless equations are
∇⋅u∂t​uS∂t​ne​​=0,=−∇Pe​+Δu−Rne​e3​,=Δne​−h∂z​ne​−nˉ′w+GnˉΔw,​
(54)
where S=ν/D and G=Vs​B/(2H). The factor S appears in the cell equation because its diffusive time differs from the viscous time used for t.
For a normal mode [ne​,w]=[N,W]eσt+iκx, set L=d2/dz2−κ2. Taking the divergence of the momentum equation gives LPe​=−RN′. Applying L to its vertical component eliminates pressure and yields
(L−σ)LW=−Rκ2N.​
(55)
The concentration equation becomes
(dz2d2​−hdzd​−κ2−Sσ)N=nˉ′W−Gnˉ(dz2d2​−κ2)W.​
(56)
These reproduce the requested modal equations, with the physically consistent sign of the orientation balance and concentration convention made explicit.
At both plates, the no-slip boundary condition gives w=0 and horizontal perturbation velocity zero. For a nonzero horizontal wavenumber, incompressibility then gives W′=0. The normal cell flux is nˉw+hN−N′; the orientation correction has no vertical component at this order. Impermeability therefore gives
W=W′=0,N′−hN=0at z=−1,0.​
(57)
If orientation lag is retained rather than the given instantaneous balance, p⊥​ acquires the relaxation response (1+B∂t^​)−1 and the modal gyrotactic term changes accordingly. The displayed equations are the quasistatic-orientation model requested here.
