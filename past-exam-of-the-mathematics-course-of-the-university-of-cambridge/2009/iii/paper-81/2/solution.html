The actual small-slope condition is k∣H∣ekL≪1 for fixed kL; kH≪1 alone is not uniform when kL grows.

Anisotropic resistance is essential: γ=1 removes propulsion at this order. The mean motion is opposite to the direction of wave propagation when the bracket is positive, since the head's axial velocity is −U.
For the basal bending moment of a planar flagellum, take positive moment to be the positive out-of-plane torque exerted on the distal filament at its base. The leading hydrodynamic torque on that filament is −μKN​∫0L​s(Yt​−V)ds, so balance requires
M0​(t)=μKN​[∫0L​sYt​(s,t)ds−2L2​V(t)].​
(22)
An explicit unsimplified time-dependent answer is
M0​(t)=μKN​Re[−iωH{q2E(qL−1)+1​−2q(1+δ)L(E−1)​}e−iωt].​
(23)
The first term follows from ∫0L​seqsds=[E(qL−1)+1]/q2. This is the total internal bending moment required by the hydrodynamic load. It equals the contractile moment when passive elasticity is neglected. If a specified passive moment Mpass​ also acts, the required active moment is M0​−Mpass​; no elastic modulus is supplied here.
The prescribed base coordinate needs a geometrical qualification. Literally, a coordinate measured from the attachment point must have Y(0,t)=0, whereas the printed waveform has Y(0,t)=Hcosωt. The preceding calculation reproduces the printed speed by treating that waveform as the prescribed coordinate, including its moving transverse offset. If the intention is instead a fixed attachment at the origin, replace it by Y=H[ekscosk(s−ct)−cosωt]. Then
V=V+1+δωHsinωt​,U=U−2L(δ+γ)(1+δ)(1−γ)δωH2ekLsinkL​.
(24)
These follow by replacing Yt​ with Yt​−Yt​(0,t) in the same force balance. The basal moment likewise uses Yt​ and V. Thus the printed kinematics and its claim that the origin is the actual attachment cannot both be imposed without this correction.
