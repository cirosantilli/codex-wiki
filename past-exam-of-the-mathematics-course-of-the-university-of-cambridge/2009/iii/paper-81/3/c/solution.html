∇⋅[n(u+Vs​⟨p⟩)−D∇n]=0.
(35)
Using the rapid-rotation mean gyrotactic orientation, put as​=2Vs​/(3BΩ), so the swimming drift is −as​e1​. In the transverse plane, x1​=Rrcosθ′ and x3​=Rrsinθ′. Thus u⋅∇=Ω∂θ′​ and
∂x1​​=R1​(cosθ′∂r​−rsinθ′​∂θ′​).

nrr​+r1​nr​+r21​nθ′θ′​−β2nθ′​+ε(cosθ′nr​−rsinθ′​nθ′​)=0,​

with β2=ΩR2/D and ε=as​R/D=2Vs​R/(3BΩD). The base fluid velocity is tangent to the wall. Zero normal cell flux there is −as​ncosθ′−Dnr​/R=0, or

Normalization requires the cross-sectional mean of n to be one.
For ε≪1, set n=1+εRe[a(r)eiθ′]+O(ε2). The drift term acting on the leading constant vanishes, and the first angular harmonic satisfies
a′′+r1​a′−r21​a−iβ2a=0,a′(1)=−1.
(39)
Writing q=eiπ/4β turns this into the Modified Bessel differential equation of order one. Its regular solution is
a(r)=−qI1′​(q)I1​(qr)​,​
(40)
where I1​ is the Modified Bessel function of the first kind. The singular second solution is excluded at the axis, and the displayed coefficient enforces the wall boundary condition. The angular harmonic has zero area average, so the normalization is satisfied at this order.
When β≫1, the gyrotactic concentration layer at a rotating-cylinder wall has stretched coordinate s=β(1−r). The leading radial equation is ass​−ia=0. The solution decaying into the interior is proportional to e−eiπ/4s; the wall condition fixes its coefficient to −1/q. Hence
n≃1−βε​Re[ei(θ′−π/4)exp{−eiπ/4β(1−r)}].​
(41)
This also follows from the large-argument asymptotic expansion of I1​, which gives a∼−r−1/2e−q(1−r)/q near the wall; r−1/2=1+O(β−1) within the layer. Its thickness is R/β=D/Ω​. The quoted exponential is a wall-layer approximation, not an exact solution all the way to r=0; the Modified Bessel function of the first kind formula supplies the regular continuation through the axis.
