The reaction sign and the meaning of recoil translation matter here. In Lighthill elongated-body theory, put D=∂t​+U∂x​. The transverse momentum imparted to the fluid per unit length is mDh, so the lateral force on the fish is −D(mDh). Thus body mass and positive added mass enter effective inertia with the same sign. The periodic trailing-edge thrust, to quadratic order in slope, is
T=2m​⟨ht2​−U2hx2​⟩x=L​.​
(1)
The reaction sign and this small-amplitude thrust formula can also be checked in the primary derivation of the elongated-body force, equations (2.6), (3.2) and the discussion after (3.5). For the long-body limit below, the recoiling leading edge contributes only at relative order α−2.
The requested recoil and thrust formulas cannot all hold for the literal fish described. In particular, the printed thrust would remain −mU2/4 when h1​=0, although a straight, unperturbed fish has ht​=hx​=0 and zero reactive thrust. Its negative term is missing an amplitude-squared slope factor. The stated effective translational coefficient M′−1 also has the wrong sign for positive added mass, and the literal centre of mass translation cannot be equated to the translation of an arbitrarily chosen undeformed body reference line. The PDF defines I′=I/[m(αL)3]; the converted TeX incorrectly uses the square.
Here are the force balance and angular momentum calculations for a consistent interpretation of the stated uniform fish. They give a corrected recoil solution, as well as a direct test of the printed equations. Define
ℓ=(α+1)L,xc​=2(1−α)L​,ξ=x−xc​,μb​=ℓM​,
(2)
and let R be actual centre of mass displacement. The prescribed deformation has mean a(t)=h1​f(t)/[2(α+1)]. Its mass-weighted mean must be removed in the recoil correction in elongated-body theory:
h(x,t)=R(t)+ξθ(t)+h0​(x,t)−a(t).
(3)
Indeed ∫hdx=ℓR, since ∫ξdx=0 and ∫h0​dx=ℓa. Introduce the two geometric integrals
J=∫−αLL​ξ2dx=12ℓ3​,C=f1​∫−αLL​ξh0​dx=12h1​L2(3α+1)​.
(4)
For uniform mass per unit length, I=μb​J at leading small-amplitude order. Retaining the symbol I makes the rotational momentum balance transparent.
The needed integrals of transverse acceleration and convective derivatives are
∫htt​dx∫ξhtt​dx​=ℓR¨,=Jθ¨+Cf¨​,​∫hxt​dx∫ξhxt​dx​=ℓθ˙+h1​f˙​,=2αLh1​​f˙​,​∫hxx​dx∫ξhxx​dx​=Lh1​​f,=2(α−1)h1​​f.​
(5)
The second spatial derivative includes the hinge's slope jump, as a distributional derivative; discarding it would incorrectly remove the U2 terms. Newton's lateral equation is MR¨=−m∫D2hdx. The angular momentum of the deforming fish is Iθ˙+μb​Cf˙​, so its time derivative equals −m∫ξD2hdx. Consequently the consistent uniform-added-mass recoil equations are
(M+mℓ)R¨=−2mU(ℓθ˙+h1​f˙​)−LmU2h1​​f,​
(6)
(I+mJ)θ¨=−(μb​+m)Cf¨​−mUαLh1​f˙​−2mU2(α−1)h1​​f.​
(7)
These follow directly from the local Lighthill elongated-body theory model with the physical reaction sign. An alternative reference-line translation replaces R by R−a, and changes the shape forcing, but cannot change M+mℓ to a difference of two positive inertias. Leading-edge details of a uniform-depth idealization can require an end model; they do not alter this reaction-sign test or the zero-stroke counterexample.
For f=cosωt, write R=Re(reiωt) and θ=Re(τeiωt), choosing the periodic response and omitting free rigid-motion constants. The two balances give the explicit recoil amplitudes
τ=I+mJ−(μb​+m)C+imUαLh1​/ω+mU2(α−1)h1​/(2ω2)​,​
(8)
r=M+mℓ2imU(ℓτ+h1​)/ω+mU2h1​/(Lω2)​.​
(9)
At the tail the displacement and slope amplitudes are HL​=h1​+r+ℓτ/2−h1​/[2(α+1)] and SL​=h1​/L+τ. Thus a corrected, directly calculable mean thrust is
T=4m​(ω2∣HL​∣2−U2∣SL​∣2),​
(10)
within the trailing-edge approximation, with any retained leading-edge contribution treated consistently. This expression has the correct dimensions and vanishes quadratically with stroke amplitude.
A particularly simple independent check is the limit U=0. For the uniform fish, I=μb​J, the balances give R¨=0 and θ=−Cf/J for the periodic response. The tail displacement amplitude is then exactly
HL​=h1​[1−2(α+1)1​−2(α+1)23α+1​]=h1​(α+1)2α2​.
(11)
Hence, to the requested relative order,
T=4mh12​ω2​[1−α4​+O(α−2)](U=0).​
(12)
The leading-edge contribution is only O(α−2) here. This recoil reduction contradicts the printed positive correction, for example for M′=2, I′=1/6. It also shows explicitly why treating the prescribed shape's moving mass as stationary would give a different model.
For completeness, the positive correction quoted in the question has a simple conditional origin if its two displayed recoil equations are accepted as formal equations rather than derived physical balances. Put J′=I′+1/6 and keep U/(ωL) fixed as α→∞, away from M′=1. Adding those equations and then using the first gives their leading periodic response
θ∼4α2LJ′h1​f​,R∼2α(M′−1)h1​​(f+L4U​∫fdt).
(13)
Under the additional reference-line interpretation h=h0​+R+ξθ, the in-phase tail amplitude is h1​[1+1/(2α(M′−1))+1/(8αJ′)]. Squaring it in the trailing-edge thrust gives the printed frequency correction. Even this formal route, however, gives the amplitude-consistent result
Tformal​=4m​[h12​ω2−L2U2h12​​+αh12​ω2​{M′−11​+4J′1​}]+O(α−2),
(14)
not a term −mU2/4 independent of h1​. The quadrature part of R first changes the squared displacement at relative order α−2, while recoil changes the tail slope at that order as well. This conditional calculation explains the intended algebra without presenting the flawed starting equations as a physical theorem.
Reactive thrust is positive when the tail's lateral-motion term exceeds its streamwise slope penalty; for an unrecoiling hinged tail this requires ωL>U. A real steady swimming speed additionally balances the thrust against resistive drag force, absent from this inertial idealization. Recoil can alter either term and is not universally thrust-enhancing. A pole at M′=1 is not a physical negative-added-mass resonance: the genuine combined inertias remain positive.
