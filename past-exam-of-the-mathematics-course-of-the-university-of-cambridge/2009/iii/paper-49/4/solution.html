Take a smooth gauge transformation g:R3→SU(2). In the anti-Hermitian convention of the adjoint covariant derivative, set
Φg=gΦg−1,Ajg​=gAj​g−1−(∂j​g)g−1.​
(34)
Expanding ∂j​(gΦg−1) shows that its two differentiated-g terms cancel with the extra terms in [Ajg​,Φg]. Thus Djg​Φg=g(Dj​Φ)g−1. The gauge field strength is Fjk​=∂j​Ak​−∂k​Aj​+[Aj​,Ak​], and the same cancellation, or the commutator of covariant derivatives, gives Fjkg​=gFjk​g−1.
The matrix trace is unchanged by conjugation, so
⟨gXg−1,gYg−1⟩=−tr(gXYg−1)=−tr(XY)=⟨X,Y⟩.
(35)
Each field-strength norm, covariant-gradient norm and Higgs norm is therefore invariant pointwise. It follows that Vλ​(Ag,Φg)=Vλ​(A,Φ). The displayed tensor made from these fields is also gauge invariant wherever Φ=0.
There is a normalization problem in the requested final identity. With the ordinary defining 2×2 trace and the printed inner product, an explicit orthonormal basis is
ea​=−2​iσa​​,−tr(ea​eb​)=δab​,[ea​,eb​]=κεabc​ec​,κ=2​.
(36)
The last identity follows from the Pauli matrix multiplication law. In particular it is not permissible to combine this orthonormality with unit structure constants of a Lie algebra.
Write Aj​=aja​ea​ and Φ=φe3​ with φ=0, and put s=sgnφ. Define Cjk​=aj1​ak2​−aj2​ak1​. Then
⟨Fjk​,e3​⟩=∂j​ak3​−∂k​aj3​+κCjk​,Dj​Φ=(∂j​φ)e3​+κφ(aj2​e1​−aj1​e2​).
(37)
Only the bracket of the transverse terms contributes in the e3​ direction, giving
⟨Φ,[Dj​Φ,Dk​Φ]⟩=κ3φ3Cjk​.
(38)
Hence the literal tensor printed in the question is
fjk​=s[∂j​ak3​−∂k​aj3​+(κ−κ3)Cjk​]=s[∂j​ak3​−∂k​aj3​−2​Cjk​].​
(39)
The transverse term does not cancel with this trace normalization of su(2).
A concrete counterexample to the printed closure assertion is Φ=e3​, A1​=e1​, A2​=x3​e2​, A3​=0 on a ball. Directly, F12​=2​x3​e3​, F23​=−e2​ and F31​=0, while D1​Φ=−2​e2​, D2​Φ=2​x3​e1​ and D3​Φ=0. It follows that
f12​=−2​x3​,f23​=f31​=0,∂3​f12​+∂1​f23​+∂2​f31​=−2​=0.​
(40)
The Higgs field never vanishes. If global finite energy is desired, multiply both nonzero gauge-potential components by a smooth compactly supported cutoff equal to one on this ball and keep Φ=e3​ everywhere. The energy is finite and the same local counterexample remains. Thus the claimed closure is false with the ordinary trace normalization actually printed; it is not a consequence of gauge invariance alone.
The consistent 't Hooft electromagnetic tensor for the printed inner product is instead
Fjk​=⟨Fjk​,∣Φ∣Φ​⟩−2∣Φ∣31​⟨Φ,[Dj​Φ,Dk​Φ]⟩.​
(41)
In the directional gauge, its commutator coefficient is 1/κ2, so the terms cancel exactly and
Fjk​=s(∂j​ak3​−∂k​aj3​).
(42)
On a connected region where φ=0, s is constant. Commutation of ordinary partial derivatives proves
∂i​Fjk​+∂j​Fki​+∂k​Fij​=0.​
(43)
At any nonzero-Higgs point, use the permitted local gauge transformation to align the field with a fixed internal direction. Since F is gauge invariant, the identity derived in that gauge holds in the original gauge on the same ball. These balls cover every nonzero-Higgs region, proving that the corrected tensor is a closed differential form there.
Alternatively, keeping the tensor's printed coefficient one but replacing the inner product by −2tr(XY) gives the orthonormal basis ea​=−iσa​/2 with κ=1. The same calculation then gives fjk​=s(∂j​ak3​−∂k​aj3​) and the intended identity. Either repair makes the bracket and inner-product normalizations agree; they must not be silently mixed.
