Use the simple-root form of Hensel lemma. Let R be a complete discrete valuation ring, let π be a uniformizer, and let F∈R[T]. If aˉ∈R/(π) is a root of Fˉ with Fˉ′(aˉ)=0, then there is exactly one root a∈R reducing to aˉ.
Choose a lift a1​ of aˉ. Suppose F(ar​)∈πrR. To construct a lift satisfying the next congruence, write ar+1​=ar​+πrtr​. The polynomial expansion gives
F(ar​+πrtr​)≡F(ar​)+πrtr​F′(ar​)(modπr+1).
(1)
Since F′(ar​) is a unit, its residue is invertible in the residue field. There is a unique residue class of tr​ making this zero modulo πr+1. Thus ar+1​≡ar​(modπr) and F(ar+1​)≡0(modπr+1). This produces a Cauchy sequence in the complete discrete valuation ring. Its limit a satisfies F(a)=0 by continuity of polynomial evaluation, and a≡a1​(modπ).
For uniqueness, suppose a,b are two such roots. Polynomial division of their difference gives
F(b)−F(a)=(b−a)(F′(a)+(b−a)H(a,b)),H∈R[S,T].
(2)
Because b−a∈πR, the second factor is a unit. Hence b−a=0. The simple-root hypothesis is used both for constructing successive lifts and for this uniqueness argument.

First reduce the polynomial modulo seven. At residues 0,1,…,6, its values are respectively 4,2,6,1,0,2,6. Its only residue root is therefore 4, and f′(4)=45≡3(mod7) is a unit. By Hensel lemma, this residue root lifts uniquely to the 7-adic integers; any other root would have to reduce to the same residue root. All the local fields here carry normalized discrete valuations. Write OF​ for the valuation ring, mF​ for its maximal ideal, and kF​ for its residue field. We use the basic degree identity [B:A]=e(B/A)f(B/A) for finite extensions of non-Archimedean local fields, with e the ramification index and f the residue degree.
First construct the maximal unramified subextension of a local field extension inside an arbitrary finite extension L/K. Choose a primitive element of a field extension zˉ for the finite residue field extension kL​/kK​. Let hˉ be its monic irreducible minimal polynomial of an algebraic element, of degree f, and lift its coefficients to obtain a monic h∈OK​[T] of the same degree. Finite finite fields are perfect fields, so hˉ′(zˉ)=0. By Hensel lemma applied in L, there is z∈OL​ reducing to zˉ with h(z)=0. For U=K(z) we have
f=[kL​:kK​]≤[kU​:kK​]≤[U:K]≤degh=f.
(7)
Every inequality is therefore an equality. In particular U/K is unramified, kU​=kL​, and h is the minimal polynomial of an algebraic element z.
If L/K is unramified, then [L:K]=f, so this construction gives U=L. Taking x=z supplies the required generator, and h′(x) is a unit. Conversely suppose L=K(x) with x integral and its monic minimal polynomial of an algebraic element g satisfying g′(x)∈/mL​. Its coefficients lie in OK​, since all conjugates of an integral element are integral and K is complete. In the constructed unramified extension U the element xˉ∈kU​=kL​ is a simple root of gˉ​. Hensel lemma in U produces a root x′∈U with residue xˉ. In L both x and x′ lift this same simple residue root, so Hensel uniqueness gives x=x′. Thus L⊆U⊆L, proving
L/K is unramified⟺L=K(x), x∈OL​, g′(x)∈OL×​.​
(8)
For lifting residue-field embeddings of unramified extensions, use the generator z and polynomial h just constructed when L/K is unramified. Given a kK​-field homomorphism ι:kL​→kM​, its value ι(zˉ) is a simple root of hˉ. Hensel lemma in M produces the unique root z′∈OM​ with that residue. The assignment z↦z′ defines a K-field homomorphism K[T]/(h)→M; it induces ι because zˉ generates kL​. Conversely any such field homomorphism preserves the unique extended valuation, hence induces a residue field homomorphism, and its image of z is uniquely forced by Hensel uniqueness. This proves the asserted bijection. The same proof only requires that M be a non-Archimedean local field containing K with its compatible valuation; its finiteness over K is not essential.
Now set M=L. The finite residue field extension has exactly f=[L:K] automorphisms, cyclically generated by a↦a∣kK​∣. Their unique lifts give [L:K] automorphisms of L. Consequently L/K is a Galois extension, and reduction identifies its Galois group with that cyclic residue Galois group. Its generator is the arithmetic Frobenius:
Gal(L/K)≃Gal(kL​/kK​)≃Z/fZ.​
(9)
For the compositum, choose a uniformizer πM​ of the totally ramified extension M/K. The Eisenstein generator of a totally ramified extension characterization, proved in the next solution, says that M=K(πM​) and its minimal polynomial of an algebraic element is Eisenstein over OK​. A uniformizer of K remains a uniformizer in the unramified extension L. All its Eisenstein coefficient conditions therefore remain true over OL​. Thus the same Eisenstein polynomial is irreducible over L and generates LM=L(πM​) as a totally ramified extension. This proves LM/L is totally ramified, of degree [M:K].
Here is an explicit example showing that the converse decomposition of an arbitrary extension can fail. Take K=Q3​ and L=K(i) with i2=−1. Since T2+1 is irreducible and separable modulo three, the degree and residue-degree argument above shows that L/K is unramified quadratic. Put
α2=3(1+i),N=L(α).

α4−6α2+18=0.

We verify its Galois group, since that is what rules out a totally ramified complement. By Hensel lemma there is r∈K with r2=−2: the residue root 1 of T2+2 is simple modulo three. Set β=(1+i)/r. Then
β2=−i,ββˉ​=−1.

The assignments σ(i)=−i and σ(α)=βα respect the defining relation, because (βα)2=−3i(1+i)=3(1−i). They therefore define a K-automorphism of N. Its square satisfies σ2(i)=i and σ2(α)=βˉ​βα=−α, so σ has order four. There are thus four automorphisms of the degree-four extension, and

Let d=[L:K], with normalized discrete valuations vK​,vL​. Suppose first that L/K is totally ramified, so e(L/K)=d. Choose a uniformizer x of L. The elements 1,x,…,xd−1 are linearly independent over K: the nonzero terms cj​xj in any proposed relation have valuations dvK​(cj​)+j, distinct modulo d, and a sum with a unique least valuation cannot vanish by the ultrametric inequality. Hence [K(x):K]≥d, proving L=K(x).
Extend vK​ to an algebraic closure with values in Q. Every conjugate of x has valuation 1/d, by uniqueness of the extended valuation on finite extensions of the complete field K. In its monic minimal polynomial of an algebraic element g(T)=Td+ad−1​Td−1+⋯+a0​, the coefficient ad−j​ is a sum of products of j conjugates, counted with multiplicity. Thus vK​(ad−j​)≥j/d>0 for j≥1, so every nonleading coefficient is in mK​. The constant coefficient is the product of all d conjugates, so has valuation exactly one. This proves that g is an Eisenstein polynomial. This argument also works for inseparable finite extensions, since the conjugates can be counted with their multiplicities.
Conversely let g be an Eisenstein polynomial of degree d, with root x and L=K(x). The Eisenstein criterion proves irreducibility. For completeness, monic factors would have integral coefficients; their reductions would be positive-degree powers of T, so both constant coefficients would be divisible by the uniformizer of K, contradicting vK​(g(0))=1. To determine the root valuation, put t=vK​(x) in the extended valuation. If t<0, the leading term is the unique term of least valuation; if t=0, the leading term again has uniquely least valuation zero. Both are impossible. If t>0, every nonconstant lower term has valuation at least 1+jt>1. Cancellation in g(x)=0 therefore requires the leading term and constant term to have equal valuation:
dt=1.
(14)
Since t=vL​(x)/e(L/K) and vL​(x) is a positive integer, e(L/K)=dvL​(x)≥d. The opposite inequality follows from [L:K]=e(L/K)f(L/K)=d. Hence e=d, f=1, and vL​(x)=1. We have proved both the equivalence and the uniformizer assertion:
L/K is totally ramified⟺L=K(x) with gx​ Eisenstein;vL​(x)=1.​

Now take q=pa with a≥1 and ζ=ζpa​. The translated cyclotomic polynomial is

In characteristic p, putting U=Tpa−1 reduces this to ∑j=0p−1​(1+U)j=Up−1=Tpa−1(p−1). Its constant coefficient over the integers is p. It is therefore Eisenstein at p, of degree d=pa−1(p−1). Consequently Qp​(ζpa​)/Qp​ is totally ramified of degree d, with uniformizer ζpa​−1. If q=1, the field is simply Qp​, a trivial totally ramified extension.
For a general positive integer n=pam with p∤m, the field generated by the prime-to-p root of unity ζm​ is unramified. Indeed Tm−1 has distinct roots modulo p. In a finite unramified field whose residue field contains those roots, Hensel lemma lifts all of them uniquely. Reduction preserves their orders: a prime-to-p root reducing to one must itself be one, by Hensel uniqueness at the root one of Tm−1. The arithmetic Frobenius therefore acts as ζm​↦ζmp​, and the extension degree is the multiplicative order of p modulo m, with degree one when m=1.
The identity

follows by taking powers of a primitive nth root of unity and using the coprimality of pa,m in the opposite direction. The prime-to-p field is unramified and the p-power field is totally ramified; unramified base change preserves total ramification proves that their compositum has the same residue degree as the prime-to-p field. Qp​(ζn​)/Qp​ is totally ramified⟺n=pam,a≥0,m∣p−1.​

This includes trivial extensions when a=0. For p=2 only m=1 is possible.

The multiplicative Hilbert theorem 90 states that, for a finite Galois extension L/K with Galois group G, every multiplicative cocycle c:G→L× satisfying cστ​=cσ​σ(cτ​) has the form cσ​=σ(y)/y for some y∈L×. In the cyclic case G=⟨σ⟩, it says
NL/K​(x)=1⟺x=σ(y)/y for some y∈L×.​
(27)
Here is the full existence argument. Distinct field homomorphisms σ:L→L are linearly independent as functions over L. To see this Artin independence theorem directly, assume a nonzero identically vanishing relation with the fewest nonzero coefficients. There are at least two terms. Choose two participating embeddings σ1​,σj​ and an element a with σ1​(a)=σj​(a). Evaluate the relation at at and subtract σj​(a) times its value at t. The jth term disappears while the first remains nonzero, giving a shorter vanishing relation, a contradiction.
Apply this independence to
A(t)=∑σ∈G​cσ−1​σ(t).
(28)

Fix a non-Archimedean local field K, write Q=∣kK​∣, and use the arithmetic Frobenius normalization throughout: a uniformizer maps to the automorphism inducing a↦aQ on the maximal unramified extension. The Local Artin map, or local reciprocity map, is the canonical continuous group homomorphism
ArtK​:K×⟶Gal(Kab/K),
(31)
where Kab is the maximal abelian extension inside a fixed algebraic closure with its separable part understood. Its finite projection to every finite abelian extension L/K is onto and has kernel exactly the norm subgroup of a local field extension:
K×/NL/K​(L×)∼​Gal(L/K).​
(32)
In particular, the quotient has order [L:K]. This is how local class field theory turns abelian extension problems into computations inside a multiplicative group.
One precise definition of the finite reciprocity map uses the cup-product construction of local reciprocity. For a finite Galois extension with group G, the local fundamental class uL/K​∈H2(G,L×) is specified by local Brauer invariant 1/[L:K]. Cup product gives an isomorphism
H−2(G,Z)=Gab ⌣uL/K​ ​H0(G,L×)=K×/NL/K​(L×).
(33)
The finite Local Artin map is its inverse, composed with the quotient map from K×. For abelian G the target is G itself. Taking the compatible inverse limit over finite abelian extensions defines ArtK​.
The essential inputs to this cohomological construction have concrete arithmetic content. The local Brauer invariant identifies Br(K) with Q/Z; restriction to L multiplies the invariant by [L:K], so its kernel is cyclic of that order and has the distinguished generator uL/K​. For every subgroup H⊆G, Hilbert theorem 90 gives H1(H,L×)=0, while the same invariant calculation over the fixed field gives H2(H,L×) cyclic of order ∣H∣. These are the hypotheses of the algebraic cup-product isomorphism in Tate cohomology of a finite group. It is obtained by dimension shifting in group cohomology with a projective resolution of the trivial module and the extension representing the fundamental class. Thus the norm-kernel statement comes from the degree-zero definition of Tate cohomology of a finite group, rather than from an arbitrary assignment of an automorphism to each element. In an unramified extension of degree d, the fundamental class is the cyclic algebra with arithmetic Frobenius generator and parameter a uniformizer; the isomorphism sends that Frobenius to the class of the uniformizer. This explains the chosen sign of reciprocity.
Several properties follow immediately from this construction. If L⊇E⊇K are finite abelian extensions, restricting ArtL/K​(a) to E gives ArtE/K​(a). The Local Artin map is therefore compatible with the finite projections defining its infinite target. For any finite separable extension E/K and a∈E×, its norm compatibility is
ArtK​(NE/K​a)=ArtE​(a)∣Kab​.
(34)
Here KabE/E is abelian, so the displayed restriction makes sense. An outline of the compatibility proof is to use the corestriction map in group cohomology: on multiplicative coefficients its degree-zero map is the norm. The cup-product projection formula, together with restriction and corestriction of the local fundamental class, gives exactly the commuting reciprocity diagram. The compatibility is an arithmetic instance of the functoriality of the cohomological definition.
The Local Artin map has dense image, because every finite abelian quotient receives a surjective map. Its kernel is trivial, and it extends to an isomorphism
K×≃Gal(Kab/K),​
(35)
where this profinite completion uses the open finite-index subgroups of the topological multiplicative group. The original map is generally not onto: on the unramified quotient it sends the integer valuation into Z, rather than producing every profinite integer. Its units map onto the abelian inertia group. For a finite abelian extension, the corresponding unit image is its inertia group, while the valuation accounts for its residue extension. Indeed vK​(Nz)=f(L/K)vL​(z), so the valuation quotient has order f; the full norm quotient has order ef, leaving unit image of order e. Since unit images act trivially on the residue field, they give precisely the inertia group. More finely, the image of the higher principal-unit group 1+mKr​ in a finite abelian extension is its rth upper ramification group, for each integer r≥1. The use of upper ramification numbering is essential for compatibility with quotients.
The existence theorem of local class field theory states that every open finite-index subgroup H⊆K× is NL/K​(L×) for a unique finite abelian extension L/K. Inclusions are reversed: larger extensions have smaller norm subgroups. In the established reciprocity isomorphism, the proof is simply to take the fixed field of the open subgroup corresponding to H. Conversely a finite extension gives an open kernel of a finite continuous projection. Uniqueness follows because that kernel specifies its fixed field. The substantive existence problem is therefore to prove that the reciprocity isomorphism realizes all open finite-index subgroups; it is not just this final fixed-field argument.
The Lubin–Tate description of local reciprocity provides an explicit route to that existence problem. Choose a uniformizer π and a Lubin–Tate series f(T)=πT+TQ, with its associated Lubin–Tate formal group F. Solving f(F(X,Y))=F(f(X),f(Y)) successively by total degree constructs the law with linear part X+Y; the analogous equations construct the endomorphisms [a] with linear coefficient a. The congruence f(T)≡TQ(modπ) supplies the divisibility needed at each step. These endomorphisms satisfy [a][b]=[ab] and make the torsion an OK​-module.
If fr​ denotes the rfold iterate, the primitive level-r Lubin–Tate torsion points are roots of
fr−1​(T)fr​(T)​=π+fr−1​(T)Q−1.
(36)
Reduction modulo π is TQr−1(Q−1), and the constant term is π, so this is an Eisenstein polynomial. Its roots therefore generate a totally ramified extension Kπ,r​ of degree (Q−1)Qr−1. A primitive point generates the entire torsion module, and all points are its images under the integral convergent endomorphisms [a]. The torsion field is therefore a Galois extension of K. The action map into (OK​/πr)× is injective, and the computed degree equals the order of this unit group, proving it is an isomorphism. Passing to the inverse limit gives Gal(Kπ​/K)≃OK×​ for the Lubin–Tate tower Kπ​.
The unramified tower and Kπ​ are linearly disjoint field extensions: a finite common subextension would be both unramified and totally ramified, hence trivial. The Lubin–Tate description of local reciprocity on their compositum is
ArtK​(πmu)∣Knr​=FrobKm​,ArtK​(πmu)(λ)=[u−1](λ)(λ∈F[πr]).
(37)
The inverse on the unit action goes with the arithmetic Frobenius convention; changing to geometric Frobenius inverts the whole reciprocity map. The reciprocity theorem identifies this explicit action with the finite cup-product maps, independently of the chosen uniformizer. Its proof compares the fundamental class with the norm relations between consecutive torsion fields. The construction makes the possible finite kernels concrete: on the compositum of the degree-d unramified field with Kπ,r​ the kernel is
πdZ(1+πrOK​).
(38)
These subgroups are cofinal among open finite-index subgroups. Indeed any such H contains 1+πrOK​ for sufficiently large r, and contains πd for some positive d, since the class of π in K×/H has finite order. Taking the appropriate fixed subgroup in this explicit finite compositum realizes H. This proves the open-subgroup part of existence once the norm compatibility of the explicit and cohomological constructions is established. Together with the finite reciprocity maps it also shows that Kab=KnrKπ​.
As a direct check, on a finite unramified extension the construction reduces to
ArtL/K​(a)=ϕL/KvK​(a)​.
(39)
The earlier elementary unit-norm proof shows its kernel is exactly πdZOK×​. At K=Qp​, the Lubin–Tate cyclotomic example takes [p](T)=(1+T)p−1 and F(X,Y)=X+Y+XY. Its torsion is ζ−1, so a unit u acts by ζ↦ζu−1. These examples exhibit both the valuation and unit parts of local reciprocity with consistent signs.
