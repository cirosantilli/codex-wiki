The Frankl-Wilson theorem in its uniform form says: let p be a prime number, let L⊆Fp​ have size s≤min(k,n−k), and let F⊆(k[n]​) have kmodp∈/L, while ∣A∩B∣modp∈L for every distinct pair of members. Then
∣F∣≤(sn​).​
(18)
The modular-intersection, nonuniform form of the Frankl-Wilson theorem is as follows. Let p be a prime number, let L⊆Fp​ have s elements, and let F⊆P([n]) satisfy ∣A∣modp∈/L for each A∈F, whereas ∣A∩B∣modp∈L for distinct A,B∈F. Then
∣F∣≤j=0∑min(s,n)​(jn​).​
(19)
This nonuniform Frankl-Wilson theorem allows different sizes and different diagonal residues; in particular it applies when all sizes have one common residue outside L.
To prove it by the polynomial method in combinatorics, work over the finite field Fp​ and associate to each member the intersection polynomial
PA​(x1​,…,xn​)=∏ℓ∈L​(∑i∈A​xi​−ℓ).
(20)
Replace each positive power of xi​ in its expansion by xi​ to obtain its multilinear reduction on the Boolean cube PA​. This preserves evaluation on every characteristic vector, whose coordinates are zero or one, and leaves degree at most s. At the characteristic vector 1B​,
PA​(1B​)=∏ℓ∈L​(∣A∩B∣−ℓ).
(21)
It is zero for distinct A,B, and nonzero for B=A by the diagonal hypothesis. If ∑A∈F​cA​PA​=0, evaluating at each 1B​ forces cB​=0. Thus the multilinear polynomials are linearly independent. They belong to the vector space with basis the square-free monomials ∏i∈S​xi​ for ∣S∣≤s, whose dimension is the displayed sum. This proves the nonuniform form, including s=0, where distinct members are impossible and the empty product is one.
For the sharp uniform form, we need the low-degree evaluation rank on a uniform layer. Form the integer matrix M whose rows are indexed by S⊆[n] with ∣S∣≤s, columns by k-subsets T, and entries MS,T​=1S⊆T​. These are evaluations of the square-free monomials. Over the rational numbers, the row of a j-subset S satisfies
∑R⊇S∣R∣=s​​MR,T​=(s−jk−j​)MS,T​for every T.
(22)
Indeed, when S⊆T the left side counts the s-subsets between S and T, and otherwise both sides vanish. Since s≤k, the coefficient is a positive integer. Thus the degree-s rows span all the rows over the rational numbers, giving rankQ​M≤(sn​). Every square submatrix of larger size has integer determinant zero, so its determinant remains zero modulo p. Hence
rankFp​​M≤rankQ​M≤(sn​).
(23)
This reduction of matrix rank is the reason one must not try to divide by (s−jk−j​) directly in Fp​.
The evaluations of the PA​ on the kth uniform layer of the Boolean cube lie in the row linear span of M over Fp​. They are linearly independent as functions there, because restriction to the columns corresponding to F still gives the nonzero diagonal evaluation matrix. Their number is consequently at most (sn​). This completes the proof of the uniform Frankl-Wilson theorem as well.
For the odd-cardinality problem the sharper Oddtown theorem follows directly from linear algebra. Let vA​=1A​∈F2n​ be the characteristic vector. The parity conditions say
vA​⋅vB​=0(A=B),vA​⋅vA​=1.
(24)
For any linear combination ∑A​cA​vA​=0, take its dot product with vB​. All off-diagonal terms vanish, leaving cB​=0. Hence these vectors are linearly independent in an n-dimensional vector space, giving
∣A∣≤n.​
(25)
The singleton sets show that the bound can be attained. Notice why the constant term in the general nonuniform Frankl-Wilson theorem is unnecessary here: these dot products already give a diagonal evaluation matrix using only the n coordinate functions.
For even n, partition [n] into the pairs {1,2},{3,4},…,{n−1,n} and take every union of a selection of these pairs. This gives exactly 2n/2 distinct sets, including the empty set. Each has even size; every pairwise intersection is again a union of entire pairs and therefore has even size. This attains the proposed bound.
For any set family satisfying the even-cardinality conditions, let W be the linear span of its characteristic vectors over F2​. Now the self-dot products vanish as well as the off-diagonal ones. By bilinearity, u⋅v=0 for all u,v∈W, so W⊆W⊥: it is a self-orthogonal binary subspace.
For completeness the standard bilinear form on F2n​ is nondegenerate, because a vector orthogonal to every coordinate vector has every coordinate zero. Thus the map v↦(w↦v⋅w) from F2n​ onto the dual space W∗ is surjective: every linear functional on W extends to the whole vector space by extending a basis, and every such functional on F2n​ is a dot product with a vector. Its kernel is W⊥. The rank-nullity theorem consequently gives dimW⊥=n−dimW. The inclusion W⊆W⊥ implies dimW≤n/2. Finally, distinct members have distinct characteristic vectors, all in W, and a k-dimensional vector space over F2​ has exactly 2k elements. Therefore
∣A∣≤∣W∣=2dimW≤2n/2.​
(26)
This proves the Eventown theorem and also explains the suggested dimension contradiction if the set family had more than 2n/2 members.
