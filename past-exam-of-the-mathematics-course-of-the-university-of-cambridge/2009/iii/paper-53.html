Take the vectors in the quantum state ensemble to be normalized, with pi​≥0 and ∑i​pi​=1. The corresponding density matrix is
ρ=i∑​pi​∣ψi​⟩⟨ψi​∣.​
(1)
Each rank-one projector is a self-adjoint operator, so ρ†=ρ. For every ∣v⟩ in the Hilbert space,
⟨v∣ρ∣v⟩=∑i​pi​∣⟨ψi​∣v⟩∣2≥0,
(2)
proving that ρ is positive semidefinite. The trace is Trρ=∑i​pi​⟨ψi​∣ψi​⟩=1.
Conversely, diagonalize a self-adjoint operator ρ in an orthonormal eigenbasis, writing ρ=∑j​λj​∣ej​⟩⟨ej​∣. Its positive semidefiniteness implies λj​=⟨ej​∣ρ∣ej​⟩≥0, and its unit trace gives ∑j​λj​=1. Thus {λj​,∣ej​⟩} is a quantum state ensemble with precisely this density matrix. Terms with zero eigenvalue may be omitted. This argument includes rank-one pure states; an ensemble representation need not describe a genuinely mixed state.

The stated condition is the definition of an extreme point in the convex set of density matrices. Suppose first that ρ=∣ψ⟩⟨ψ∣ with ∥ψ∥=1, and consider any convex combination ρ=∑i​ai​ρi​ with all ai​>0. For every ∣w⟩ orthogonal to ∣ψ⟩,
0=⟨w∣ρ∣w⟩=∑i​ai​⟨w∣ρi​∣w⟩.
(3)
Every term is nonnegative, so ⟨w∣ρi​∣w⟩=0 for each i. To see that this implies ρi​w=0, expand ρi​=∑j​μj​∣uj​⟩⟨uj​∣, μj​≥0. The equality ∑j​μj​∣⟨uj​∣w⟩∣2=0 implies μj​⟨uj​∣w⟩=0 for every j, which kills ρi​w. Thus each density matrix ρi​ vanishes on ψ⊥. By self-adjointness its range lies in the line Cψ, so ρi​=ci​∣ψ⟩⟨ψ∣. Its unit trace forces ci​=1, establishing the required purity.
Conversely, suppose ρ is not rank one. In its spectral decomposition, at least two eigenvalues are positive. Select a normalized eigenvector ∣e1​⟩ with positive eigenvalue λ1​; then 0<λ1​<1. Define
σ=1−λ1​ρ−λ1​∣e1​⟩⟨e1​∣​.
(4)
The remaining eigenvalues show that σ is a density matrix. The decomposition ρ=λ1​∣e1​⟩⟨e1​∣+(1−λ1​)σ is a convex combination of two different density matrices, neither equal to ρ. This violates the given purity condition. Hence the extreme points of the density-operator state space obey
ρ is pure⟺ρ=∣ψ⟩⟨ψ∣ for a normalized ∣ψ⟩.​
(5)

Let R=∣ψ⟩⟨ψ∣ and choose an orthonormal basis {∣eμ​⟩} of H2​. The reduced density matrix on S1​ is the partial trace
ρ1​=Tr2​R=μ∑​(I1​⊗⟨eμ​∣)R(I1​⊗∣eμ​⟩).​
(6)
Here I1​ is the identity on H1​, and the bras and kets in the sum contract only the second tensor factor. Equivalently, if ∣ψ⟩=∑a,μ​caμ​∣fa​⟩⊗∣eμ​⟩, then (ρ1​)ab​=∑μ​caμ​cbμ∗​. The definition is independent of the chosen basis: it is characterized by Tr(Mρ1​)=Tr[(M⊗I2​)R] for every operator M on H1​.
The generalized measurement postulate takes Ai​ to be operators on H2​ satisfying ∑i​Ai†​Ai​=I2​. The Born rule and the conditional state update give
pi​=⟨ψ∣I1​⊗Ai†​Ai​∣ψ⟩,∣ψi​⟩=pi​​(I1​⊗Ai​)∣ψ⟩​(pi​>0).​
(7)
If the outcome is not supplied to S1​, the appropriate post-measurement density matrix is R′=∑i​(I1​⊗Ai​)R(I1​⊗Ai†​). For any local operator M, cyclicity of the full trace gives
Tr[(M⊗I2​)R′]​=i∑​Tr[(M⊗Ai†​Ai​)R]=Tr[(M⊗I2​)R].​
(8)
Since this holds for every M, Tr2​R′=ρ1​​. This is the no-communication theorem.
The invariance concerns the nonselective measurement in quantum mechanics. A conditional reduced density matrix ρ1∣i​=Tr2​∣ψi​⟩⟨ψi​∣ can change, although ∑i​pi​ρ1∣i​=ρ1​. For example, measuring one half of a Bell pair in the computational basis changes the other half conditionally to ∣0⟩ or ∣1⟩; before learning the outcome, its density matrix remains I/2. Thus distant measurement in quantum measurements cannot transmit a controllable signal without classical communication. This quantum no-signalling is compatible with special relativity, even though some entangled states have correlations incompatible with a local hidden-variable theory.

The EPR criterion of reality says that if a physical quantity can be predicted with certainty without disturbing the system, then it represents an element of physical reality. For separated particles, the EPR locality assumption regards choices of measurements on the other particles as nondisturbing. The perfect correlations below therefore imply predetermined local values for both X=σx​ and Y=σy​, independent of which distant measurements are chosen.
Put ∣0⟩=∣↑⟩ and ∣1⟩=∣↓⟩. The Pauli matrices satisfy
X∣0⟩=∣1⟩,X∣1⟩=∣0⟩,Y∣0⟩=i∣1⟩,Y∣1⟩=−i∣0⟩.
(9)
For three particles, each operator with one X and two Y takes ∣000⟩ to −∣111⟩ and ∣111⟩ to −∣000⟩, whereas X⊗X⊗X interchanges the two strings without a minus sign. Thus the GHZ state in the question has the certain outcomes
XYY=YXY=YYX=+1,XXX=−1.​
(10)
Each local X or Y value can be predicted from measurements at the other two sites in one of these contexts. If the EPR criterion of reality and locality assign values xj​,yj​∈{±1}, the first three equalities require
x1​y2​y3​=1,y1​x2​y3​=1,y1​y2​x3​=1.
(11)
Multiplying these ordinary numbers and using yj2​=1 yields x1​x2​x3​=1, contradicting the certain quantum outcome −1. This derives the GHZ theorem explicitly; the multiplication is of hypothetical local values, not of mutually incompatible local observables.
For general N, let Oj​=Xj​⨂k=j​Yk​, with the tensor factors ordered by their site labels, and let XN​=X⊗N. Directly,
Oj​∣0⟩⊗N=iN−1∣1⟩⊗N,Oj​∣1⟩⊗N=(−i)N−1∣0⟩⊗N.
(12)
For the difference of the two strings to be an eigenstate, these phases must agree, which happens exactly when N−1 is even. For odd N the common phase is (−1)(N−1)/2, and interchanging the strings negates their difference. Consequently
N odd:Oj​∣ψ⟩N​=(−1)(N+1)/2∣ψ⟩N​ for every j,XN​∣ψ⟩N​=−∣ψ⟩N​.​
(13)
For even N, XN​ still has eigenvalue −1, but every Oj​ takes the difference state to a phase times the sum state, which is orthogonal to it. Thus it is not an eigenstate of any Oj​; those operators each have outcomes ±1 with equal probabilities. Among the stated N≥4, simultaneous eigenstates therefore occur precisely at N=5,7,9,….
For odd N, certainty of each Oj​ again permits remote prediction of both local X and Y. Writing λN​=(−1)(N+1)/2, the local hidden-variable theory would require xj​∏k=j​yk​=λN​ for all j. Their product is
(∏j​xj​)∏k​ykN−1​=λNN​,
(14)
hence ∏j​xj​=λN​, since N−1 is even and N is odd. Quantum theory requires the same product to be −1. The contradiction occurs when λN​=+1, giving the infinite family
N≡3(mod4);N≥4 gives N=7,11,15,….​
(15)
For N≡1(mod4), these particular certain product constraints are consistent; for example xj​=−1, yj​=+1 satisfies them. This distinguishes the GHZ contradiction for N congruent to 3 modulo 4 from the larger set of odd N for which simultaneous eigenstates exist.

Write a=∣α∣2, b=∣β∣2=1−a, so 1/2<a<1 and 0<b<1/2. Regrouping the four copies by party gives
∣Ψ⟩=∑x∈{0,1}4​αz(x)β4−z(x)∣x⟩A​∣x⟩B​,
(16)
where z(x) counts zeroes. Alice must measure only this count, preserving coherence inside each degenerate eigenspace, rather than measuring her four bits separately. Her projective measurement has projectors Πk​=∑z(x)=k​∣x⟩⟨x∣, and the Born rule gives
pk​=(k4​)akb4−k,k=0,1,2,3,4.​
(17)
Alice sends k to Bob. Up to the common phase of αkβ4−k, their conditional pure state is
∣Tk​⟩=dk​​1​∑z(x)=k​∣x⟩A​∣x⟩B​,dk​=(k4​).
(18)
This is Schmidt projection entanglement concentration: its equal Schmidt coefficients give a maximally entangled state of Schmidt rank dk​.
For k=0 or 4, dk​=1, and the branch is a product state. No Bell pair can be extracted using local operations and classical communication. The respective probabilities are b4 and a4.
For k=1 or 3, dk​=4. List the four surviving strings in lexicographic order as x0​,x1​,x2​,x3​. For k=1 this list is 0111,1011,1101,1110; for k=3 it is 0001,0010,0100,1000. Each party applies a local unitary operation extending the basis relabelling
∣xj​⟩⟼∣j1​j0​⟩⊗∣00⟩,j=2j1​+j0​.
(19)
Such a unitary exists because these are four distinct orthonormal basis vectors mapped to four distinct basis vectors; extend the mapping to a permutation of all sixteen. Discarding the two fixed local ancillary bits leaves
21​∑j1​,j0​=01​∣j1​j0​⟩A​∣j1​j0​⟩B​=∣Φ+⟩A1​B1​​⊗∣Φ+⟩A0​B0​​.
(20)
Thus these branches give exactly two Bell pairs, with probabilities 4ab3 and 4a3b, and no further measurement in quantum mechanics is needed.
For k=2, the six strings in lexicographic order are x0​=0011, x1​=0101, x2​=0110, x3​=1001, x4​=1010, x5​=1100. A suitable local POVM gives two Bell pairs with certainty, rather than losing some of these branches to a simple subspace projection. Partition these labels into the three pairs T0​={0,1}, T1​={2,3}, T2​={4,5}. Alice uses the Kraus operators
Kr​=2​1​∑j∈/Tr​​∣xj​⟩⟨xj​∣,r=0,1,2.
(21)
On the six-dimensional support, each label is retained by two operators, so ∑r=02​Kr†​Kr​=Π2​. Add K⊥​=I−Π2​ to define a complete measurement in quantum mechanics on Alice's four qubits; that extra outcome has probability zero on this branch. Each nonzero outcome has conditional probability (1/2)(4/6)=1/3, and gives the normalized state
21​∑j∈/Tr​​∣xj​⟩A​∣xj​⟩B​.
(22)
Alice sends r to Bob. Both parties relabel the four surviving strings in increasing order to ∣00⟩,∣01⟩,∣10⟩,∣11⟩ on two output qubits, with two fixed ancillary bits, just as above. Every r therefore gives two Bell pairs. The joint probability of (k,r)=(2,r) is p2​/3=2a2b2 for each r.
For an explicit local implementation of this POVM, Alice can append an ancilla in a fixed state and apply an isometry on the six-dimensional support,
∣xj​⟩⟼∣xj​⟩⊗2​1​∑r:j∈/Tr​​∣r⟩.
(23)
It preserves inner products, extends to a unitary on system and ancilla, and measuring the ancilla realizes precisely the operators Kr​. No nonlocal operation has been used. This is a deterministic reduction of maximally entangled Schmidt rank from six to four.
The complete output distribution for this strategy is consequently
Pr(2 Bell pairs)=1−a4−b4,Pr(0 Bell pairs)=a4+b4.​
(24)
The mean yield is 2(1−a4−b4) pairs per four-copy block. These are the maximum possible pair counts after the stipulated count measurement: any nonzero branch has Schmidt rank at most six, whereas three independent Bell pairs require Schmidt rank eight. In any refined LOCC branch the coefficient matrix is multiplied by local matrices on the left and right, which cannot increase its rank.

The relevant security model gives Eve the purifying system and access to the public transcript. A useful preliminary fact is that purity decouples a subsystem from its purification. If the Alice–Bob marginal is ∣u⟩⟨u∣ and ∣Ω⟩ABE​ is a purification of a density operator, then for every v⊥u,
∥(⟨v∣AB​⊗IE​)∣Ω⟩∥2=⟨v∣ρAB​∣v⟩=0.
(25)
Hence ∣Ω⟩ABE​=∣u⟩AB​∣e⟩E​: Eve is uncorrelated with their joint pure state. A Bell pair then supplies a uniform shared secret bit when both parties measure in the computational basis. Pure product states have no shared random correlations, while a mixed state must be assessed with its purifying information included. The classifications below use the usual quantum key distribution setting with an authenticated public classical channel; no full security proof is required.

A secure key can be produced, at one ideal secret bit per copy. Denote Alice's additional flag qubit by F. Alice can measure F in the computational basis and, when its outcome is 1, apply the Pauli Z gate to her half of the shared pair. The two flag outcomes each have probability 1/2, and ZA​∣Φ−⟩=∣Φ+⟩; either branch therefore supplies the same pure Bell pair.
Equivalently, Alice applies the local controlled unitary
U=IAB​⊗∣0⟩⟨0∣F​+(ZA​⊗IB​)⊗∣1⟩⟨1∣F​.
(30)
This local flag correction of a Bell-state phase mixture changes the full density matrix into
∣Φ+⟩⟨Φ+∣AB​⊗IF​/2.​
(31)
The corrected pair is pure, so every purification factors between AB and FE. Eve may know the flag without knowing the key bit obtained from AB. Alice need not disclose the flag, but even disclosing it does not compromise the corrected pure Bell pair. Tracing out the flag before correction would give the nonprivate mixture in the preceding case; retaining Alice's local side information is essential.

Protocol 1 OurBigBook.com
Insecure as a proposed test-and-purify protocol: the basis string and check positions are announced before receipt. Eve can intercept and store the travelling qubits until the announcement. For each data position with basis bit bj​, she measures the stored qubit in the basis {Hbj​∣0⟩,Hbj​∣1⟩}. If the outcome is zj​, the Alice–Bob pair becomes
∣zj​⟩A​⊗Hbj​∣zj​⟩B​,
(32)
and Eve knows zj​. She forwards that qubit, and forwards every publicly identified check qubit untouched. After Bob's Hadamard gate decoding, every data pair is the known product state ∣zj​zj​⟩, while the unmodified check pairs remain perfect. Thus the check bit error rate is zero even though Eve knows all raw data bits. This is premature basis announcement in quantum key distribution.
The retained data are a separable quantum state with a classical label held by Eve; LOCC cannot turn them into private Bell pairs. Consequently the listed check does not justify the claimed success of the subsequent entanglement purification. A sound purification procedure that independently detects the missing entanglement must fail or abort on this attack. If Step 9 were instead read as an independently guaranteed production of private, nearly perfect Bell pairs, their subsequent key would of course be private; the flaw is that this protocol's test cannot establish that guarantee. Naming an EPP does not repair the information leak or the invalid sampling test.

Impossible to implement as written: Bob is never given the basis string. Step 7 requires him to know which incoming qubits were acted on by a Hadamard gate, but the announcement contains only the check positions. Since H−1=H, knowing that a transformation is its own inverse does not identify which qubits need it.
Bob cannot recover the missing information from the qubits alone. For each encoded Bell pair, his reduced density matrix is
TrA​[(I⊗Hb)∣Φ+⟩⟨Φ+∣(I⊗Hb)]=I/2
(33)
for either b=0 or b=1. His entire received register has the same density matrix I/22n for every string b, so every local measurement in quantum mechanics has statistics independent of b. Nor can one fixed unitary undo both possibilities: preserving ∣Φ+⟩ requires that unitary to be proportional to I, whereas decoding (I⊗H)∣Φ+⟩ requires it to be proportional to H. Supplying the missing basis announcement after receipt would repair the protocol and make it the third one, but that communication is absent from the printed sequence.

Protocol 3 OurBigBook.com
Secure in the intended ideal setting, with a sound entanglement-purification procedure. Bob confirms receipt before Alice reveals either the random basis string or the randomly chosen check positions. Eve must therefore release the travelling qubits before knowing which basis to use and which positions to protect from disturbance. Bob can then decode because H2=I.
The random check sample tests the same ensemble of encoded transmissions as the retained data. Random computational basis and Hadamard basis encodings make the test sensitive to complementary disturbances; Eve cannot use the targeted zero-error attack available in Protocol 1. For example, measuring every intercepted qubit in one fixed one of these two bases uses the wrong basis half the time, and then disagrees with Alice half the time, producing a bit error rate of 1/4. A sound entanglement purification protocol uses the tested error bounds, corrects both relevant types of error, and aborts if the noise is too high; secrecy is not claimed for every possible attack without an abort option.
When the protocol succeeds in producing nearly perfect Bell pairs, purity decouples a subsystem from its purification explains why they are nearly independent of Eve, and matching computational basis measurements produce an approximately uniform shared secret key. The disclosed check outcomes are discarded rather than included in that key. This is the standard entanglement-based quantum key distribution ordering: receipt first, basis and sample disclosure second, testing and purification next, and private measurements last.
