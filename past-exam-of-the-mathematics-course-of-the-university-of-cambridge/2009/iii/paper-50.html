Let P be the orthogonal projection onto the code, and let {Ea​} be the specified error operators, including their linear span. The necessary and sufficient Knill--Laflamme condition is
PEa†​Eb​P=Cab​Pfor every a,b,​
(1)
where the scalar matrix C is independent of the encoded state. In an orthonormal basis {∣jL​⟩} of the code this means
⟨iL​∣Ea†​Eb​∣jL​⟩=Cab​δij​.
(2)
There must be one recovery quantum channel which corrects every allowed error channel, rather than a different recovery chosen using the unknown logical input.
The scalar condition means that any information carried away by the errors depends on their labels but not on the encoded amplitudes. To see how recovery works, diagonalize the positive semidefinite matrix C and take corresponding linear combinations Fℓ​ of the errors. Then PFℓ†​Fm​P=λℓ​δℓm​P. For each positive λℓ​, the map Fℓ​P/λℓ​​ is an linear isometry into an error-image subspace, and these subspaces are mutually orthogonal. Measuring their label and reversing the associated linear isometry recovers the logical state without measuring its amplitudes. Zero eigenvalues correspond to errors that annihilate the code. This also explains why the condition corrects arbitrary coherent combinations of the listed errors.

Let A be the known set of affected qubits, with ∣A∣≤d−1. Expand arbitrary operators on A in tensor products of I,X,Y,Z. These Pauli operators span the complete error space on A.
By the distance of a quantum error-correcting code, every Pauli operator F of weight below d has scalar compression PFP=cF​P. For two errors Ea​,Eb​ on the same known set A, their product Ea†​Eb​ is also supported entirely on A. Its weight is therefore at most ∣A∣, rather than twice ∣A∣. Expansion in the Pauli basis consequently gives
PEa†​Eb​P=Cab​P.
(3)
The Knill--Laflamme condition applies to the full operator space on A, so it gives a recovery for any error channel acting on that subsystem. Every set of at most d−1 known error locations is correctable. This is quantum erasure correction; the ability to use the locations is what improves the guarantee over unknown-location errors.

Use base-two logarithms, so Von Neumann entropy is measured in bits. Take an orthonormal basis of the code and purify its maximally mixed logical state as
∣Ω⟩RQ​=2−k/2∑j=12k​∣j⟩R​∣jL​⟩Q​.
(4)
Only a 2k-dimensional reference support is needed; it can be embedded in the larger reference Hilbert space in the hint. Its reduced density matrix is maximally mixed on that support, so S(R)=k.
For any correctable erased subsystem A, the Knill--Laflamme condition gives P(OA​⊗I)P=c(OA​)P for every operator OA​. Taking its matrix elements in the logical basis shows that TrQ∖A​∣iL​⟩⟨jL​∣=0 for i=j, and that the diagonal reductions are all the same state ρA​. Thus
ρRA​=ρR​⊗ρA​.
(5)
In particular, the reference is decoupled from every set of at most m=d−1 erased qubits.
For an information-carrying code k>0, first justify that two disjoint sets of size m fit. If 2m≥n, partition all the physical qubits into sets A,B each of size at most m. Both are correctable. Additivity of Von Neumann entropy for a product state, and equality of complementary entropies for the pure state on RAB, give
S(R)+S(A)=S(B),S(R)+S(B)=S(A).
(6)
Adding yields 2S(R)=0, contradicting S(R)=k>0. Hence 2m<n.
Now choose disjoint A,B of size m, and let C contain the remaining n−2m qubits. The state on RABC is pure. Product-state entropy additivity, equality of complementary entropies, and Subadditivity of Von Neumann entropy give
S(R)+S(A)=S(RA)=S(BC)≤S(B)+S(C),
(7)
and
S(R)+S(B)=S(RB)=S(AC)≤S(A)+S(C).
(8)
Adding and cancelling proves S(R)≤S(C). Finally use the maximum entropy of a quantum state bound S(C)≤log2​dimHC​=n−2m. Therefore the quantum Singleton bound is
k≤n−2(d−1),n−k≥2(d−1).​
(9)
This proof uses quantum erasure correction, not nondegeneracy, so it applies to degenerate codes as well. A code encoding no logical information requires a separate distance convention; the argument above concerns the usual k>0 coding problem.

Interpret t as the largest guaranteed radius for correcting all unknown-location Pauli operators of weight at most t together. Products of two such errors have weight at most 2t, so the Knill--Laflamme condition holds when 2t<d. Conversely, let F be a minimum-weight Pauli operator with nonscalar compression PFP. If d≤2t, split its support into two sets of size at most t and write F=Ea†​Eb​, up to an irrelevant phase. These two admissible errors violate the Knill--Laflamme condition. Thus the correctable Pauli error radius is
t=⌊2d−1​⌋,d∈{2t+1,2t+2}.​
(10)
In particular, d=2t+1 requires the additional assumption that d is odd. Distance is not uniquely determined by t alone. Nor does the weight of a particular correctable error characterize this radius: particular higher-weight errors can also be correctable.
For a concrete even-distance example, the stabilizer code generated by X⊗4 and Z⊗4 has parameters [[4,2,2]]. Every single-qubit nonidentity Pauli operator anticommutes with a generator, so it has zero compression. But Z1​Z2​ commutes with both generators and acts nonscalarly on the code: it is unitary there and its code trace is zero. Hence the distance is two and its guaranteed radius is zero. This shows why an unconditional odd-distance formula would be incorrect.
Ignoring overall phases, an error of weight j has (jn​) choices of support and three choices, X,Y,Z, at each affected qubit. The identity is the weight-zero term, so
N(t)=j=0∑t​3j(jn​).​
(11)
For a nondegenerate quantum code, distinct correctable Pauli errors have orthogonal error-image subspaces. Each has dimension 2k, since a Pauli operator is unitary. All N(t) subspaces lie in the physical Hilbert space of dimension 2n. Counting dimensions proves the quantum Hamming bound
N(t)2k≤2n,N(t)≤2n−k.​
(12)
Degeneracy would allow distinct errors to share an error-image subspace, so this particular counting argument would then fail.

Let D=dimHA​ and introduce an isomorphic reference system R. Choose a basis {∣j⟩}j=1D​ and the maximally entangled state
∣Ω⟩RA​=D​1​∑j=1D​∣j⟩R​∣j⟩A​.
(13)
Complete positivity makes
ωRA​=(idR​⊗Φ)(∣Ω⟩⟨Ω∣)
(14)
a positive density operator. This is the normalized Choi matrix, with the reference placed first. Its spectral decomposition can be written
ωRA​=∑ℓ=1r​λℓ​∣vℓ​⟩⟨vℓ​∣,λℓ​>0.
(15)
The number r is at most D2.
Here an index state is a chosen basis vector ∣j⟩R​ in the reference. The corresponding relative state of ∣vℓ​⟩ is the generally unnormalized vector
∣ηℓj​⟩A​=(⟨j∣R​⊗IA​)∣vℓ​⟩RA​.
(16)
Thus ∣vℓ​⟩=∑j​∣j⟩R​∣ηℓj​⟩A​. Define a linear map Aℓ​ by its columns:
Aℓ​∣j⟩=Dλℓ​​∣ηℓj​⟩.
(17)
Then (IR​⊗Aℓ​)∣Ω⟩=λℓ​​∣vℓ​⟩, so
ωRA​=∑ℓ​(IR​⊗Aℓ​)∣Ω⟩⟨Ω∣(IR​⊗Aℓ†​).
(18)
Expand both sides in reference matrix units. The block with reference indices i,j on the left is D−1Φ(∣i⟩⟨j∣); on the right it is D−1∑ℓ​Aℓ​∣i⟩⟨j∣Aℓ†​. Since matrix units span all operators and Φ is linear,
Φ(ρ)=ℓ∑​Aℓ​ρAℓ†​.​
(19)
This constructs the Kraus representation explicitly from the relative states, rather than assuming it.
Finally, trace preservation implies for every density operator ρ that
Trρ=TrΦ(ρ)=Tr[ρ∑ℓ​Aℓ†​Aℓ​].
(20)
Taking every rank-one orthogonal projection as ρ forces the Hermitian operator in brackets to have the same quadratic form as the identity. Therefore
ℓ∑​Aℓ†​Aℓ​=I.​
(21)
The argument is finite-dimensional, as appropriate to the quantum-information setting; a channel with a different output dimension has the same construction with rectangular Kraus operators.

Fix a basis for the transposition map T. If M≥0, write M=BB†. Then
T(M)=MT=BBT=B(B)†≥0.
(22)
Thus transposition is a positive linear map and also preserves the trace.
For dimension D≥2, apply it to one subsystem of the maximally entangled state from part (a):
(id⊗T)(∣Ω⟩⟨Ω∣)=D1​∑i,j​∣i⟩⟨j∣⊗∣j⟩⟨i∣=DF​,
(23)
where F is the swap operator, F(∣u⟩⊗∣v⟩)=∣v⟩⊗∣u⟩. It satisfies F†=F and F2=I, so its eigenvalues are +1 on symmetric vectors and −1 on antisymmetric vectors. In particular,
∣η⟩=2​∣1⟩∣2⟩−∣2⟩∣1⟩​,⟨η∣DF​∣η⟩=−D1​<0.
(24)
A positive input has therefore acquired a negative expectation value after adjoining an identity channel. Transposition is positive but not completely positive in dimension at least two. The one-dimensional case is the identity map and is completely positive; the dimension qualification is necessary.

Write ρ​=∑i​pi​ρi​. The Holevo quantity is
χ(E)=S(ρ​)−i∑​pi​S(ρi​),​
(25)
where S is Von Neumann entropy, with a consistent logarithm base. Attach an orthogonal classical label register X to form the classical-quantum state
ωXB​=∑i​pi​∣i⟩⟨i∣X​⊗ρi​.
(26)
The entropy of an orthogonal quantum mixture gives
S(X)=H(p),S(XB)=H(p)+∑i​pi​S(ρi​),S(B)=S(ρ​).
(27)
Here H(p) is the Shannon entropy of the classical label. Hence I(X:B)=χ(E), where I denotes quantum mutual information.
Let Φ:B→C be the proposed quantum channel. Using its Kraus representation, define an linear isometry V=∑ℓ​Aℓ​⊗∣ℓ⟩E​, since V†V=I. This is a Stinespring representation. Apply it to B, obtaining a state ηXCE​; tracing out E applies Φ to every ensemble member. An linear isometry preserves nonzero eigenvalues and therefore Von Neumann entropy, so
I(X:CE)η​=I(X:B)ω​=χ(E),I(X:C)η​=χ({pi​,Φ(ρi​)}).
(28)
Their difference is
I(X:CE)−I(X:C)=S(XC)+S(CE)−S(C)−S(XCE).
(29)
The Strong subadditivity of Von Neumann entropy states that this expression is nonnegative for every tripartite state. Consequently
χ({pi​,Φ(ρi​)})≤χ(E).​
(30)
Thus the Holevo quantity under a quantum channel decreases because discarding the dilation environment cannot improve correlations with the classical label. The probabilities remain unchanged throughout.

Using base-two logarithms, define the quantum conditional entropy by
S(A∣B)=S(ρAB​)−S(ρB​).​
(31)
The Subadditivity of Von Neumann entropy, S(AB)≤S(A)+S(B), immediately gives S(A∣B)≤S(A).
For the lower bound, purify ρAB​ by an auxiliary system C. Complementary subsystems of a pure state have the same nonzero reduced-state eigenvalues, by the Schmidt decomposition. Hence S(BC)=S(A) and S(C)=S(AB). Applying Subadditivity of Von Neumann entropy to BC gives
S(A)=S(BC)≤S(B)+S(C)=S(B)+S(AB).
(32)
Rearranging yields S(A∣B)≥−S(A). This is the relevant side of the Araki–Lieb inequality, here derived using purification.
Finally the maximum entropy of a quantum state gives S(A)≤log2​dimHA​. For example, this follows directly from
D(ρA​​dimHA​IA​​)=log2​dimHA​−S(A)≥0
(33)
by nonnegativity of quantum relative entropy. Combining the two bounds proves
∣S(A∣B)∣≤log2​dimHA​.​
(34)
Both signs can be attained: a maximally mixed A independent of B has the positive extreme, while a maximally entangled state with a sufficiently large B has the negative extreme.

Let P be the pinching map determined by the projectors, and put σ=P(ρ). This is a nonselective projective measurement; its projectors are its Kraus operators. We will prove the exact relative entropy of a pinched state identity.
First the support of ρ is contained in that of σ. Indeed, if v lies in the kernel of σ, positivity gives
0=⟨v∣σ∣v⟩=∑i​⟨Pi​v∣ρ∣Pi​v⟩=∑i​∥ρ1/2Pi​v∥2.
(35)
Every summand is zero. Since ∑i​Pi​=I, also ρ1/2v=0, and thus v is in the kernel of ρ. Therefore the logarithm of σ may be used on its support without an infinite relative-entropy term.
The operator σ is block diagonal, so each Pi​ commutes with logσ on that support. Cyclicity of the trace gives
Tr(σlogσ)=∑i​Tr(Pi​ρPi​logσ)=Tr(ρ∑i​Pi​logσPi​)=Tr(ρlogσ).
(36)
Expand the quantum relative entropy:
D(ρ∥σ)=Tr(ρlogρ)−Tr(ρlogσ)=S(σ)−S(ρ).
(37)
Its nonnegativity now proves the entropy increase under nonselective projective measurement:
S(σ)≥S(ρ).​
(38)
The equality case in nonnegativity of quantum relative entropy says equality holds exactly when ρ=σ. Equivalently,
Pi​ρPj​=0 for i=j,or, equivalently, [ρ,Pi​]=0 for every i.​
(39)
Thus equality means the state already has no coherence between distinct measurement blocks. It need not be diagonal inside a block of rank greater than one.

Yes: use superdense coding, with a shared Bell state. Let the sender and receiver initially hold the first and second qubits of ∣Φ+⟩=(∣00⟩+∣11⟩)/2​. To encode bits (a,b), the sender applies ZaXb to her qubit. These Pauli operators produce four orthogonal states:
(a,b)(0,0)(1,0)(0,1)(1,1)​(ZaXb⊗I)∣Φ+⟩(∣00⟩+∣11⟩)/2​(∣00⟩−∣11⟩)/2​(∣01⟩+∣10⟩)/2​(∣01⟩−∣10⟩)/2​​
(42)
She sends this qubit through the noiseless quantum channel. The receiver then possesses both qubits. He applies a controlled-NOT gate from the received qubit to his original qubit, followed by a Hadamard gate on the received qubit. This maps the four states in the table to ∣ab⟩. A measurement in the computational basis therefore recovers the two bits with certainty. No classical message is sent.
The resource assumption deserves care: one-qubit superdense coding needs a Bell pair already shared before encoding. If none is initially available, the sender can prepare a Bell pair locally and send one half through the same noiseless quantum channel in advance. The protocol then uses two transmitted qubits in total, including distribution of the entanglement, still with no classical communication. Alternatively, sending two computational basis qubits directly also communicates two bits. With a pre-shared Bell pair, one transmitted qubit suffices; without it, the entanglement-distribution cost must be counted. An unassisted single transmitted qubit cannot perfectly encode four classical messages: its Holevo bound is at most log2​2=1 bit.

Yes, the conversion can be deterministic using local operations and classical communication. Put s=sinθ and c=cosθ. The initial state's squared Schmidt coefficients are (1/2,1/2), while the target's decreasingly ordered squared Schmidt coefficients are (c2,s2). Since 0<θ<π/4, one has 1/2<c2 and both pairs sum to one. Thus
(1/2,1/2)≺(c2,s2).
(43)
The direction of this majorization is the allowed one in Nielsen's pure-state conversion theorem: maximal entanglement can be reduced by deterministic LOCC.
Here is an explicit protocol, so no conversion theorem need be assumed. The sender first applies a Pauli Z gate to remove the initial Bell-state minus sign. Starting from ∣Φ+⟩, she performs a two-outcome local measurement with Kraus operators
M0​=(s0​0c​),M1​=(c0​0s​).
(44)
They define a valid measurement because M0†​M0​+M1†​M1​=I. The two unnormalized states after measurement are
(M0​⊗I)∣Φ+⟩=2​s∣00⟩+c∣11⟩​,(M1​⊗I)∣Φ+⟩=2​c∣00⟩+s∣11⟩​.
(45)
Each outcome has probability 1/2. For outcome zero, the normalized state is already s∣00⟩+c∣11⟩. For outcome one, both parties apply Pauli X gates, obtaining the same state. The sender communicates only which outcome occurred. Finally the receiver applies a Pauli X gate, giving s∣01⟩+c∣10⟩ in either branch. This is deterministic two-qubit entanglement dilution, with total success probability one; it does not rely on postselection.

Label the atomic ground state ∣0⟩ and excited state ∣1⟩, with 0≤p≤1. The process is the amplitude damping channel, with Kraus operators
A0​=∣0⟩⟨0∣+1−p​∣1⟩⟨1∣=(10​01−p​​),A1​=p​∣0⟩⟨1∣=(00​p​0​).​
(46)
They satisfy A0†​A0​+A1†​A1​=I, so the resulting Kraus representation is trace preserving.
The operator A1​ represents emission: it maps the excited state to p​ times the ground state and annihilates the ground state. Its probability for an initially excited atom is p. The operator A0​ represents no emission: it leaves the ground state unchanged and retains the excited amplitude with factor 1−p​. This branch is not generally the identity operation; the absence of a photon itself changes the conditional relative amplitudes. For an arbitrary atomic density operator, the emission probability is Tr(A1​ρA1†​)=pρ11​ and the no-emission probability is 1−pρ11​.

A unital quantum channel preserves the identity operator: Φ(I)=I. For equal input and output dimensions, this is equivalent to preserving the maximally mixed density operator.
For the amplitude damping channel,
Φ(I)=A0​A0†​+A1​A1†​=(1+p0​01−p​).
(47)
Thus the channel is not unital for p>0; it is unital only at p=0, when it is the identity channel. In particular, it biases the maximally mixed state toward the atomic ground state. The trace-preservation condition involves ∑j​Aj†​Aj​, whereas unitality involves the opposite products ∑j​Aj​Aj†​.

Take an environment initially in its vacuum state ∣0⟩E​, with ∣1⟩E​ recording an emitted photon. In atom-environment order, put q=1−p​ and u=p​. A unitary dilation of amplitude damping is defined by
U∣00⟩U∣01⟩​=∣00⟩,=q∣01⟩−u∣10⟩,​U∣10⟩U∣11⟩​=q∣10⟩+u∣01⟩,=∣11⟩.​
(48)
The middle two images are orthonormal, and they are orthogonal to the two fixed images. Thus this completion really is a unitary operator. For the occupied initial environmental subspace, it leaves a ground-state atom unchanged and splits an excited-state atom into no-emission and emission alternatives with probabilities 1−p and p.
Tracing out the environment after this unitary evolution gives
Φ(ρ)=TrE​[U(ρ⊗∣0⟩⟨0∣E​)U†].
(49)
The environment matrix elements are ⟨0∣E​U∣0⟩E​=A0​ and ⟨1∣E​U∣0⟩E​=A1​, recovering the Kraus operators in part (a). More explicitly, the basis operators transform as
∣0⟩⟨0∣∣1⟩⟨1∣∣0⟩⟨1∣∣1⟩⟨0∣​⟼∣0⟩⟨0∣,⟼(1−p)∣1⟩⟨1∣+p∣0⟩⟨0∣,⟼1−p​∣0⟩⟨1∣,⟼1−p​∣1⟩⟨0∣.​
(50)
Applying linearity to the atomic density operator therefore yields
Φ(ρ)=(ρ00​+pρ11​1−p​ρ10​​1−p​ρ01​(1−p)ρ11​​).​
(51)
The lost excited population goes to the ground state, the trace stays one, and coherences decrease by 1−p​. Discarding the distinguishable environmental photon record is precisely what turns the joint unitary evolution into a noisy atomic quantum channel.

Let the quantum channel send B to B′. From its Kraus representation, choose a Stinespring representation V:B→B′E and form
σAB′E​=(IA​⊗V)ρAB​(IA​⊗V†).
(52)
The channel output is σAB′​=TrE​σAB′E​. Isometric invariance of Von Neumann entropy gives
S(B′E)σ​=S(B)ρ​,S(AB′E)σ​=S(AB)ρ​,S(A)σ​=S(A)ρ​.
(53)
Therefore the quantum mutual information satisfies I(A:B)ρ​=I(A:B′E)σ​. Expanding its loss after discarding the environment gives
I(A:B)ρ​−I(A:B′)σ​​=I(A:B′E)σ​−I(A:B′)σ​=S(AB′)σ​+S(B′E)σ​−S(B′)σ​−S(AB′E)σ​.​
(54)
The final expression is the quantum conditional mutual information I(A:E∣B′). The Strong subadditivity of Von Neumann entropy states exactly that it is nonnegative. Hence
I(A:B′)≤I(A:B).​
(55)
This proves data processing for quantum mutual information for any mixed input and any local quantum channel, not just the atomic channel in earlier parts. The correlations lost are exactly the conditional correlations with the discarded environment.
