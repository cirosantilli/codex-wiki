A Noetherian module satisfies the ascending chain condition on its submodules; equivalently every submodule is a finitely generated module. If the latter condition holds, the union of an ascending chain has a finite generating set lying in one member, so the chain stabilizes. Conversely, a submodule with no finite generating set allows successive choices outside the submodule generated so far, producing a strictly ascending chain. A Noetherian ring is a ring that is Noetherian as a module over itself, equivalently one whose ideals are all finitely generated.
The formal power series ring consists of sequences written ∑i≥0​ai​xi, with coefficientwise addition and the Cauchy product:
(∑i≥0​ai​xi)(∑j≥0​bj​xj)=∑n≥0​(∑i=0n​ai​bn−i​)xn.
(7)
Every coefficient uses a finite sum; no analytic convergence is involved. Evaluation at zero gives A[[x]]/(x)≅A. A quotient ring of a Noetherian ring is Noetherian, because its ascending chains of ideals lift to such chains upstairs. Thus one direction of Noetherianity of a formal power series ring is immediate.
For the other direction suppose A is Noetherian, and let I⊆A[[x]] be an ideal. Define the coefficient ideals of a formal power series ideal
Ir​={[xr]g:g∈I∩xrA[[x]]}⊆A.
(8)
Each is an ideal, and multiplication by x gives Ir​⊆Ir+1​. Choose N with IN​=IN+1​=⋯. For 0≤r≤N, choose generators ar1​,…,armr​​ for Ir​, and choose series grj​∈I∩xrA[[x]] whose xr coefficients are those generators. Empty generating sets are allowed for zero ideals.
These finitely many series generate I. To prove this without assuming that I is closed, start with g∈I and cancel its coefficients successively. If the residual hr​ lies in I∩xrA[[x]], its xr coefficient lies in Ir​. Put s=min(r,N); since Ir​=Is​ when r≥N, write that coefficient as ∑j​crj​asj​ and subtract ∑j​crj​xr−sgsj​. The new residual belongs to I∩xr+1A[[x]].
For s<N, the generators gsj​ are used only at the single step r=s. For s=N, their accumulated multipliers are the genuine formal power series ∑r≥N​crj​xr−N. Thus the construction yields the exact coefficientwise identity
g=∑s<N​∑j​csj​gsj​+∑j​(∑r≥N​crj​xr−N)gNj​.
(9)
Every coefficient of the residual vanishes after finitely many steps. This is an ordinary finite generating expression in A[[x]], not merely an approximation by elements of a smaller ideal. Therefore every ideal is finitely generated, proving
A is Noetherian⟺A[[x]] is Noetherian.​
(10)
Finally, for the surjective module endomorphism f, the kernels kerf⊆kerf2⊆⋯ stabilize because M is a Noetherian module. Take N with kerfN=kerfN+1. If f(m)=0, surjectivity of fN gives u with fN(u)=m. Then fN+1(u)=0, so u∈kerfN and m=0. Hence f is injective as well as surjective; its inverse is automatically A-linear. This proves f is an isomorphism, the statement that Noetherian modules are Hopfian.
