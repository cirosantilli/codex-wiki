Choose a surjection P0​=Ar0​↠M. Since A is a Noetherian ring, a finite free module is a Noetherian module, and its kernel K1​ is a finitely generated module. Choose a finite free surjection P1​↠K1​ and repeat. This constructs a resolution by finitely generated free modules
⋯⟶P2​⟶P1​⟶P0​⟶M⟶0,
(22)
whose terms are, in particular, finitely generated projective modules. It may be infinite; the request is finite generation of every term, not finite length.
Now suppose the nonzero finitely generated module M has finite projective dimension n. For n≥1, its nth syzygy Kn​ in this free resolution is projective. Indeed, dimension shifting along the successive kernel sequences gives
ExtA1​(Kn​,N)≅ExtAn+1​(M,N)=0
(23)
for every module N. The vanishing follows by computing the latter Ext functor from any length-n projective resolution. Vanishing of Ext1(Kn​,−) makes every short exact sequence ending in Kn​ split: apply the long exact sequence of Ext groups to a free surjection onto Kn​ to lift its identity. Thus Kn​ is a projective module. It is finitely generated by the preceding construction. Truncating gives
0⟶Pn​dn​​Pn−1​⟶⋯⟶P0​⟶M⟶0
(24)
with every term finite projective.
Apply the Hom functor HomA​(−,A) and write Pi∗​=HomA​(Pi​,A). At the top degree,
ExtAn​(M,A)=coker(dn∗​:Pn−1∗​→Pn∗​).
(25)
If this group were zero, dn∗​ would be surjective. The dual module of a finite projective module is finite projective, so this surjection has a section s:Pn∗​→Pn−1∗​. Finite projective modules identify with their double dual modules: the evaluation map is an isomorphism for a finite free module and passes to its direct summands. Dualizing dn∗​s=id therefore gives s∗dn​=idPn​​. Hence dn​ splits, and Pn−1​/dn​(Pn​) is projective. Replacing the last two terms by this quotient shortens the projective resolution to length n−1, contradicting minimality. This proves top Ext detects finite projective dimension:
ExtAn​(M,A)=0.​
(26)
For n=0, M is a nonzero finite projective module and embeds as a summand of Ar. Some coordinate projection restricts nontrivially to M, proving HomA​(M,A)=0 as required.
For infinite projective dimension, take the dual-number algebra A=k[ε]/(ε2) and M=k=A/(ε). Multiplication by ε has image and kernel both equal to (ε), giving the periodic resolution over dual numbers
⋯ε​Aε​Aε​A⟶k⟶0.
(27)
Applying HomA​(−,k) makes every differential zero, so ExtAi​(k,k)≅k for every i≥0. A finite projective resolution would force all sufficiently high Ext functors to vanish. Thus pdk[ε]/(ε2)​k=∞.
The finite-generation hypothesis is essential if the middle sentence's “a module” is interpreted as an unrestricted new module. A concrete vanishing top Ext without finite generation occurs for A=k[[t]] and K=A[1/t]=k((t)). This Noetherian ring has the exact free presentation
0⟶⨁i≥0​Aei​d​⨁i≥0​Aei​⟶K⟶0,d(ei​)=ei​−tei+1​,ei​⟼t−i.
(28)
Injectivity of d follows by examining the largest index of a finitely supported input, since t is a non-zero-divisor. For exactness in the middle, use the relations to move a finite sum to its largest index N; its remaining coefficient is ∑i=0N​ai​tN−i, which is zero precisely when its image in K is zero. The map onto K is surjective because every Laurent series has a lower bound on its exponents.
Applying HomA​(−,A) gives on products the map (bi​)↦(bi​−tbi+1​). It is surjective: for any (ci​), set bi​=∑j≥0​tjci+j​. These are well-defined formal power series, because each coefficient receives only finitely many contributions, and bi​−tbi+1​=ci​. Consequently ExtA1​(K,A)=0. But HomA​(K,A)=0: the image of every t−i must lie in ⋂r≥0​trA=0, since it is divisible by every power of t. A nonzero projective module is a summand of a free module and therefore has a nonzero coordinate map to A. Thus K is not projective, while its displayed resolution has length one. In particular,
pdk[[t]]​k((t))=1,Extk[[t]]1​(k((t)),k[[t]])=0.
(29)
The nonvanishing conclusion proved above retains the finitely generated M introduced at the start of the question.
