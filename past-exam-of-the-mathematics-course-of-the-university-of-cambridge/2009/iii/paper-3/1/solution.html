Throughout, rings are commutative with identity and ring homomorphisms preserve identity. The spectrum of a commutative ring is SpecA={p⊂A:p is a prime ideal}. For an ideal I, put V(I)={p:I⊆p}; these are the closed sets of the Zariski topology. Its axioms follow from
V(0)=SpecA,V(A)=∅,⋂λ​V(Iλ​)=V(∑λ​Iλ​),V(I)∪V(J)=V(IJ).

For the last equality, a prime ideal containing IJ must contain I or J: if it contained neither, choose a∈I∖p and b∈J∖p, contradicting ab∈p. The other equalities follow directly from containment. Thus arbitrary intersections and finite unions of closed sets are closed, proving that this is a topology.
The Zorn lemma says that a nonempty partially ordered set in which every chain has an upper bound has a maximal element. For A=0, apply it to proper ideals ordered by inclusion. The zero ideal is proper. The union of a nonempty chain is an ideal and is proper, because containing one would put one in a member of the chain; the empty chain has the zero ideal as an upper bound. There is therefore a maximal ideal m. If a∈/m, then m+(a)=A, so the class of a in A/m is invertible. This quotient ring is a field, making m a prime ideal. Hence A=0⟹SpecA=∅.

For the specified inclusion, the fundamental theorem of algebra gives the points 0 and (x−z) of SpecC[x]. Over R, the nonzero prime ideals are generated either by x−r with r∈R, or by (x−a)2+b2 with a∈R, b>0; all irreducible real polynomials have degree one or two. Contraction gives the spectrum map for the complexification of a real polynomial ring:
0↦0,(x−z)↦{(x−z),((x−Rez)2+(Imz)2),​z∈R,z∈/R.​​

Indeed, a real polynomial vanishing at a nonreal z also vanishes at zˉ, and is divisible by their quadratic factor; the converse is immediate. This map is surjective. Its fibers contain one point over a real linear factor, two conjugate points over an irreducible quadratic factor, and only the generic point over zero. It identifies complex-conjugate pairs. Since each proper Zariski-closed set in either one-variable polynomial ring is a finite set of closed points, images of closed sets are closed; hence the Zariski topology on the target is the quotient topology for this identification.
