An algebraic line bundle over A is an invertible module: a finitely generated projective module L such that Lp​≅Ap​ for every prime ideal p. Define the Picard group of a ring as the set of its isomorphism classes, with
[L][K]=[L⊗A​K].
(11)
The tensor product of modules of two finite projective modules is finite projective: write each as a direct summand of a finite free module and tensor the two decompositions. Localization gives (L⊗A​K)p​≅Ap​, so this multiplication stays within the set of invertible modules. It is well defined on isomorphism classes. The associativity and symmetry isomorphisms of the tensor product give associativity and commutativity, and [A] is the identity.
Let L∗=HomA​(L,A) be the dual module. A finite direct-summand presentation shows that L∗ is finite projective and that localization commutes with this dual. Since Lp​ is free of rank one, so is (L∗)p​. The evaluation homomorphism
L⊗A​L∗⟶A,ℓ⊗λ⟼λ(ℓ)
(12)
is an isomorphism after every prime localization. Its kernel and cokernel are therefore zero, by exactness of localization and localization detects zero elements for modules. Thus [L]−1=[L∗], proving that Pic(A) is an abelian group.
For a ring homomorphism f:A→B, tensoring a split presentation L⊕L′≅Ar gives (B⊗A​L)⊕(B⊗A​L′)≅Br, so B⊗A​L is finite projective. For q∈SpecB put p=f−1(q). Every element of A∖p becomes a unit in Bq​, and
(B⊗A​L)q​≅Bq​⊗Ap​​Lp​≅Bq​.
(13)
This proves base change of invertible modules. Moreover, the canonical map
(B⊗A​L)⊗B​(B⊗A​K)⟶B⊗A​(L⊗A​K),(b⊗ℓ)⊗(b′⊗k)⟼bb′⊗(ℓ⊗k)
(14)
is an isomorphism, with inverse b⊗(ℓ⊗k)↦(b⊗ℓ)⊗(1⊗k). It shows that [L]↦[B⊗A​L] is a group homomorphism. Identity maps induce the identity, and for A→B→C, the associativity isomorphism C⊗B​(B⊗A​L)≅C⊗A​L proves composition compatibility. Therefore Pic is a covariant functor to abelian groups.
The polynomial ring C[x] is a principal ideal domain by division with remainder. A submodule N of a finite free module over a principal ideal domain is free: induct on the ambient rank, project onto the first coordinate, and note that the image is a principal ideal, hence either zero or a free rank-one module. This gives a split exact sequence with that image as quotient; the kernel lies in a free module of one smaller rank, completing the induction. A finite projective module is such a submodule, and so is free. An invertible module has rank one after passing to the fraction field, so its free rank is exactly one. Hence every algebraic line bundle over C[x] is trivial and
Pic(C[x])=0.​
(15)
