We construct the Jordan decomposition of a bounded functional on C(K) directly, without assuming a measure representation. For f≥0, define
P(f)=sup{ϕ(g):g∈C(K), 0≤g≤f}.
(19)
This is finite, nonnegative, and positively homogeneous, since 0 is allowed and ∣ϕ(g)∣≤∥ϕ∥∥f∥∞​. If f,h≥0, separately chosen approximate maximizers add to an admissible function for P(f+h), giving P(f+h)≥P(f)+P(h). For the converse, any 0≤g≤f+h splits into the continuous functions
g1​=min(g,f),g2​=g−g1​=max(g−f,0),
(20)
with 0≤g1​≤f and 0≤g2​≤h. Hence ϕ(g)≤P(f)+P(h) and P is additive on the positive cone.
Extend it to a linear functional by ϕ+(u−v)=P(u)−P(v) for u,v≥0. This is well defined: two decompositions satisfy u+v′=u′+v, and additivity gives the same difference. Every continuous real function has such a decomposition, for example into its pointwise positive and negative parts. The extension is positive. Define ϕ−=ϕ+−ϕ; for f≥0, the choice g=f shows P(f)≥ϕ(f), so ϕ− is also positive. Part (a) makes both positive linear functionals continuous.
Their operator norms satisfy
∥ϕ+∥+∥ϕ−∥=2P(1)−ϕ(1)=sup0≤g≤1​ϕ(2g−1)=sup∥h∥∞​≤1​ϕ(h)=∥ϕ∥.
(21)
The last equality uses symmetry of the real unit ball. Therefore
ϕ=ϕ+−ϕ−,∥ϕ∥=∥ϕ+∥+∥ϕ−∥.​
(22)
