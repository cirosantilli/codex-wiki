Number the simple roots so that α1​ is short and α2​ is long. In a Euclidean plane take
α1​=(1,0),α2​=(−3/2,3​/2).
(35)
Their squared lengths are 1,3, and their angle is 150∘. The positive roots of a root system are
α1​,α2​,α1​+α2​,2α1​+α2​,3α1​+α2​,3α1​+2α2​;
(36)
their negatives give the remaining six roots of the G2 root system. The three short positive roots of a root system are α1​, α1​+α2​, and 2α1​+α2​.
Solving ⟨ωj​,αi∨​⟩=δij​ gives the fundamental weights
ω1​=2α1​+α2​=(1/2,3​/2),ω2​=3α1​+2α2​=(0,3​).​
(37)
In this case both fundamental weights are themselves roots; the diagram marks their locations on the two hexagons.
Let a=n1​ and b=n2​. Relative to the simple coroots, the six positive coroots, in the root order above, have coordinates
(1,0),(0,1),(1,3),(2,3),(1,1),(1,2).
(38)
For example, α1​+α2​ has squared length one, so its coroot is 2α1​+2α2​=α1∨​+3α2∨​; the analogous length computation gives each other entry. Since ρ=ω1​+ω2​, the pairings of λ+ρ with these coroots are a+1, b+1, a+3b+4, 2a+3b+5, a+b+2, and a+2b+3. Their values at a=b=0 multiply to 1⋅1⋅4⋅5⋅2⋅3=120. The Weyl dimension formula therefore yields the G2 dimension polynomial
dimL(aω1​+bω2​)=120(a+1)(b+1)(a+b+2)(a+2b+3)(a+3b+4)(2a+3b+5)​.​
(39)
This holds for all nonnegative integers a,b. In particular it gives dimensions 7 and 14 for L(ω1​) and L(ω2​), and 27 for L(2ω1​). Reversing the numbering of the simple roots would interchange a,b in the polynomial, so the short-first convention matters.
