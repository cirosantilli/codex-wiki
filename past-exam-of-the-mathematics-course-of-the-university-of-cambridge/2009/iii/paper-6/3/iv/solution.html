A=(1,2,…,n,nˉ,n−1​,…,1ˉ),

with weights wt(i)=εi​ and wt(iˉ)=−εi​. The crystal of the defining symplectic representation is the chain
11​22​⋯n−1​nn​nˉn−1​⋯2​2ˉ1​1ˉ.
(29)
The label of an arrow is its simple root color; a Kashiwara operator f​i​ follows an arrow of color i and lowers the weight by αi​.
Here is a grid description of the entire tensor product of crystals, valid for every n. Put one vertex a⊗b at row a and column b, in the alphabet order above. Let Pi​ be the set of chain positions with outgoing color i, and Qi​ the positions with incoming color i:
Pi​={i,2n−i},Qi​={i+1,2n−i+1}(i<n),Pn​={n},Qn​={n+1}.
(30)
Their indicators are φi​ and εi​. Use the crystal tensor-product rule
f​i​(a⊗b)={f​i​(a)⊗b,a⊗f​i​(b),​φi​(a)>εi​(b),φi​(a)≤εi​(b).​
(31)
An arrow is omitted if the selected factor has no outgoing arrow of that color. Thus the complete grid has a downward color-i arrow when a∈Pi​ and b∈/Qi​, and a rightward color-i arrow when b∈Pi​ and a∈/Pi​. There are no other arrows. These two coordinate rules specify every vertex and every edge of the requested general-rank drawing. The following figure displays the full grid at n=3, including all components, together with the standard chain; increasing n uses exactly the same grid construction.
For completeness, highest vertices can be read from the raising rule, which selects the first factor when φi​(a)≥εi​(b). If a has an incoming edge of color i, then φi​(a)=0; either the raising operator acts nontrivially on a, or εi​(b)=1 and it acts nontrivially on b. Thus a highest vertex must have a=1. At a=1, all φi​(a) vanish except φ1​(a)=1. To be highest, the incoming color of b, if any, must consequently be 1. For n≥2, the possibilities are exactly
1⊗1,1⊗2,1⊗1ˉ.
(32)
Their weights are 2ε1​=2ω1​, ε1​+ε2​=ω2​, and zero. Each occurs once. Connected highest-weight components of the crystal basis describe the irreducible representations in the tensor product of Lie algebra representations, so
V⊗V≅L(2ω1​)⊕L(ω2​)⊕L(0)(n≥2).​
(33)
This is the tensor-square decomposition of the defining symplectic representation. The symmetric square is L(2ω1​); the exterior square is the direct sum of its invariant inverse-form line and the primitive exterior square L(ω2​). Their dimensions are respectively n(2n+1), n(2n−1)−1, and one, summing to 4n2=dim(V⊗V). At n=1, the chain has two vertices and the highest tensor vertices are only 1⊗1 and 1⊗1ˉ: the decomposition is L(2ω1​)⊕L(0), of dimensions three and one.
