Past exam of the mathematics course of the University of Cambridge / 2009 / iii / Paper 6 / 3 / ii / Solution

Let U be a four-dimensional symplectic vector space, with a symplectic basis e1​,f1​,e2​,f2​. Its invariant inverse-form bivector may be chosen as η=e1​∧f1​+e2​∧f2​. Put vol=e1​∧f1​∧e2​∧f2​, so η∧η=2vol. Define a symmetric bilinear form on the six-dimensional exterior square by
ξ∧ζ=B(ξ,ζ)vol.
(21)
It is symmetric because both factors have degree two. It is nondegenerate: in the six-element wedge basis, every ea​∧eb​ pairs nontrivially with the wedge of the complementary pair and with no other basis vector. Since B(η,η)=2, the orthogonal complement
W=η⊥⊂Λ2U
(22)
has dimension five and inherits a nondegenerate bilinear form. It is the primitive exterior square, equivalently the kernel of the symplectic contraction of an exterior square.
The symplectic Lie algebra preserves η. It also preserves vol, since its elements have trace zero and the induced action on Λ4U is multiplication by that trace. The exterior-power Lie algebra representation therefore preserves B and W, giving a Lie algebra homomorphism
Φ:sp4​⟶so(W,B).
(23)
To show injectivity, suppose A acts as zero on W. It already acts as zero on the invariant line Cη, so it kills all of Λ2U=W⊕Cη. For any basis u1​,…,u4​, this means
Aui​∧uj​+ui​∧Auj​=0(i=j).
(24)
If k=i, choose j distinct from i,k; the coefficient of uk​∧uj​ forces Aki​=0. Thus A is diagonal. The same identity gives Aii​+Ajj​=0 for every pair. Applying this to three distinct indices forces each diagonal entry to vanish. Hence A=0 and Φ is injective.
Finally dimsp4​=2(2⋅2+1)=10 and dimso(W,B)=5⋅4/2=10. Injectivity between these equal-dimensional vector spaces gives surjectivity. Every nondegenerate complex symmetric bilinear form on a five-dimensional space is equivalent to the standard one. We have therefore constructed the exceptional isomorphism between sp4 and so5:
sp4​≅so5​.​
(25)
