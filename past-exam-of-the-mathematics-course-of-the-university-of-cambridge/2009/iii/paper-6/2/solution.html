Let B be the given invariant bilinear form on a Lie algebra. Choose a basis x1​,…,xd​ and its B-dual basis x1,…,xd, so B(xi​,xj)=δij​. The Casimir element is
ΩB​=i∑​xi​xi∈U(g).​
(6)
The tensor ∑i​xi​⊗xi is the inverse tensor of B, so it is independent of the chosen basis. Multiplication in the universal enveloping algebra therefore makes ΩB​ independent of that choice as well.
For z∈g, write [z,xi​]=∑j​aji​xj​. Invariance means B([z,x],y)+B(x,[z,y])=0, whence [z,xi]=−∑j​aij​xj. Therefore
[z,ΩB​]=∑i,j​aji​xj​xi−∑i,j​aij​xi​xj=0,
(7)
by interchanging i,j in the second sum. Since g generates its universal enveloping algebra, ΩB​ is central. This argument does not require adding a symmetry assumption to the given bilinear form.
For the sl2 Lie algebra, take
e=(00​10​),f=(01​00​),h=(10​0−1​),B(X,Y)=tr(XY).
(8)
The cyclic identity for the trace gives B([Z,X],Y)+B(X,[Z,Y])=0. The form is nondegenerate, since B(e,f)=1, B(h,h)=2, and all the other pairings except B(f,e) are zero. The dual of (e,f,h) is (f,e,h/2), so
ΩB​=ef+fe+21​h2.
(9)
By the classification of finite-dimensional sl2 representations, an irreducible of dimension n has a highest-weight vector v with ev=0 and hv=mv, where m=n−1. Since ef=fe+h, one obtains
ΩB​v=(m+21​m2)v.
(10)
Centrality and the Schur lemma then give the answer on the entire representation:
ΩB​=2n2−1​I.​
(11)
This Casimir eigenvalue for sl2 depends on normalization. Choosing the Killing form instead, K=4B, scales the dual basis and the Casimir element by 1/4, giving (n2−1)I/8.
