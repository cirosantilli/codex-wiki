The null hypothesis is (PAD)222​=0, against a nonzero value. Removing it increases the residual sum of squares by 21025 and removes one parameter. The stepAIC command performs stepwise selection by the Akaike information criterion. Its upper scope is the full factorial model and its lower scope is the intercept-only model. Starting from the full model, it compares admissible additions and deletions, preserving the hierarchy of interaction terms, and chooses changes that reduce the Akaike information criterion. With the supplied scope its default search permits both directions. For these Gaussian fits the displayed criterion, up to constants common to all models, is 16log(RSS/16)+2k, where k counts mean coefficients. The first deletion changes it from 226.72 to 224.76: the small increase in residual sum of squares is outweighed by removing a parameter. This is a greedy model selection procedure, not an exhaustive search or a sequence of fixed-level hypothesis tests. The command's arguments are described in the MASS documentation.

A preference for one angle is not supported by this selected model. The strong paper-design interaction reverses the design comparison: the simple design is better on heavy paper, whereas the high-performance design is better on light paper. Consequently a single design effect averaged over paper types would conceal the main result. There is little evidence here for an angle effect, and considerable flight-to-flight variability remains.
Before fitting, inspect raw-distance dot plots for all eight factor combinations, and interaction plots of cell means against design, with separate lines for paper and panels for angle. With only two replicates per cell, showing individual observations is more informative than elaborate box plots. These reveal reversals, spread and possible unusual flights. Afterwards inspect a residual-versus-fitted plot, a Q-Q plot of residuals, a scale-location plot, residuals against each factor and, if recorded, flight order, and Cook's distance for influential observations. Residual patterns by angle would question its deletion, while unequal spread or departures from the normal distribution would question the error assumptions rather than merely demand another mean term.
