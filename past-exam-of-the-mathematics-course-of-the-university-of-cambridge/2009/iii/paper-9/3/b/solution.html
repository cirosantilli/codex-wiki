The appropriate Poincare-Wirtinger inequality, with no zero-boundary assumption, is
∥u−uΩ​∥Lp(Ω)​≤CΩ,p​∥∇u∥Lp(Ω)​,uΩ​=∣Ω∣1​∫Ω​u,1≤p<n.​
(19)
Assume Ω is nonempty, bounded, connected and has the stated cone property. If this failed, after subtracting the mean and normalizing there would be uj​∈W1,p(Ω) with
(uj​)Ω​=0,∥uj​∥p​=1,∥∇uj​∥p​⟶0.
(20)
The sequence is bounded in W1,p. By the permitted Rellich-Kondrachov compactness theorem, a subsequence converges strongly in Lp to u. For every compactly supported test function, integration by parts and these two convergences show that each distributional derivative of u is zero. A function with zero weak gradient is constant on a connected open set: mollification makes it locally constant, and connectedness identifies the local constants.
Convergence in Lp on a finite-measure set also preserves the integral, so uΩ​=0 and the constant is zero. But strong convergence preserves ∥u∥p​=1, a contradiction. This proves the inequality; connectedness prevents different constants on separate components.
