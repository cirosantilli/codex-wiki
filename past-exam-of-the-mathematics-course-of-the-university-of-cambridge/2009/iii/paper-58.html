Assume the level set is regular and non-null. The latter hypothesis is necessary: a null hypersurface has a degenerate induced metric and hence no ordinary intrinsic Levi-Civita connection determined by that metric. Normalize the normal vector and define the non-null hypersurface projection by
na​=∣gbc∂b​f∂c​f∣​∂a​f​,ε=na​na=±1,Pab​=δab​−εnanb​,hab​=gab​−εna​nb​.
(1)
Here h denotes the induced metric restricted to tangent vectors, while its ambient extension annihilates n. The intrinsic covariant derivative is DX​Y=P∇X​Y for tangent vector fields. Projection of the ambient torsion-free connection and metric compatibility shows that D is the Levi-Civita connection of h. Define the extrinsic curvature by K(X,Y)=g(∇X​n,Y), or Kab​=Pca​Pdb​∇c​nd​. It is symmetric: differentiating the defining function twice and projecting removes the derivative of its normalization, leaving a multiple of its symmetric Hessian. This sign convention makes the scalar second fundamental form g(n,∇X​Y)=−K(X,Y).
Let S be the tangent linear map characterized by h(SX,Y)=K(X,Y). Differentiating g(n,n)=ε and g(n,Y)=0 gives the Gauss formula
∇X​n=SX,∇X​Y=DX​Y−εK(X,Y)n.
(2)
Fix the Riemann curvature tensor convention
R(X,Y)Z=∇X​∇Y​Z−∇Y​∇X​Z−∇[X,Y]​Z,Rabcd​=g(ea​,R(ec​,ed​)eb​).
(3)
Applying the Gauss formula twice gives
P∇X​∇Y​Z=DX​DY​Z−εK(Y,Z)SX.
(4)
Subtracting the expression with X,Y interchanged, and then subtracting the bracket derivative, proves
R(X,Y)Z=PR(X,Y)Z+ε(K(Y,Z)SX−K(X,Z)SY).
(5)
Thus the Gauss–Codazzi equations for a non-null hypersurface give the intrinsic Riemann curvature tensor as
Rabcd​=Pea​Pfb​Pgc​Phd​Refgh​+ε(Kac​Kbd​−Kad​Kbc​).​
(6)
All free indices here are tangent to the hypersurface. The normal component of the same calculation is
g(n,∇X​∇Y​Z)=−K(X,DY​Z)−X(K(Y,Z)).
(7)
After antisymmetrizing and using DX​Y−DY​X=[X,Y], this yields the curved-ambient Codazzi equation
g(n,R(X,Y)Z)=−(DX​K)(Y,Z)+(DY​K)(X,Z).​
(8)
The extrinsic curvature measures how tangent planes turn in the ambient manifold; the Gauss equation says that this turning contributes to intrinsic curvature even when the ambient Riemann curvature tensor is zero.
For the quadric in Minkowski spacetime, write its position vector as Xa. On the hypersurface, g(X,X)=1 and differentiating this identity in a tangent direction gives g(X,Y)=0. Hence n=X is a unit normal and a spacelike vector, ε=1, and ∇Y​n=Y in flat Cartesian coordinates. Therefore K=h. The ambient Riemann curvature tensor vanishes, so
Rabcd​=hac​hbd​−had​hbc​,Rab​=3hab​,R=12>0.​
(9)
This is unit-radius de Sitter spacetime. Reversing the normal changes K to −K but does not change this curvature. An opposite overall convention for the Riemann curvature tensor would reverse both curvature signs below.
To retain a four-dimensional Lorentzian metric while reversing the curvature, take a flat ambient metric with two negative directions and the quadric
−dt2−dw2+dx2+dy2+dz2,−t2−w2+x2+y2+z2=−1.
(10)
Now the position vector is a unit normal with negative squared norm, so ε=−1 while K=h remains true. The Gauss equation gives
Rabcd​=−(hac​hbd​−had​hbc​),R=−12.​
(11)
This is unit-radius Anti-de Sitter spacetime. If a positive-definite induced metric is acceptable, an even simpler alternative is to keep the original one-time ambient Minkowski metric and change the quadric level to g(X,X)=−1. Each sheet then gives four-dimensional hyperbolic space with the same negative curvature, but its induced signature differs from that of de Sitter spacetime.

Choose a local orthonormal coframe eA with g=ηAB​eAeB. The Levi-Civita connection is represented by the connection 1-forms satisfying
deA+ωAB​∧eB=0,ωAB​=ηAC​ωCB​=−ωBA​.
(12)
The gamma matrices satisfy the Clifford algebra relations {γA,γB}=2ηABI. The matrices 41​[γA,γB] represent the infinitesimal orthogonal transformations on spinor fields; lifting the frame connection to this representation defines the spinor covariant derivative
∇μ​ϵ=∂μ​ϵ+41​ωμAB​γAγBϵ=∂μ​ϵ+81​ωμAB​[γA,γB]ϵ.​
(13)
Both frame indices are summed, so each antisymmetric pair occurs twice. Under a local Lorentz transformation with spin lift S, the spin connection transforms as Ωμ​↦SΩμ​S−1−(∂μ​S)S−1 and ϵ↦Sϵ; consequently ∇μ​ϵ↦S∇μ​ϵ. This establishes covariance. Global construction requires a compatible spin structure (and the appropriate orientation and time orientation); the formulas themselves work on a local orthonormal frame patch.
The supplied gamma matrices have squares −I,I,I and anticommute pairwise, so η=diag(−1,1,1). Work on a connected regular patch with r>0 and V=0. Since the metric depends only on V2, we may use its positive smooth square root V>0. Take
e0=Vdt,e1=dr/V,e2=rdθ.
(14)
Their exterior derivatives are de0=−V′e0∧e1, de1=0, and de2=(V/r)e1∧e2. Solving Cartan's first structure equation and imposing Lorentzian connection-form antisymmetry gives
ω01​=ω10​=VV′dt,ω21​=Vdθ,ω12​=−Vdθ.
(15)
In particular ω01​=−VV′dt and ω12​=−Vdθ. The parallel spinor equations are therefore
∂t​ϵ−2VV′​γ0γ1ϵ=0,∂r​ϵ=0,∂θ​ϵ−2V​γ1γ2ϵ=0.
(16)
Differentiate the angular equation with respect to r and use ∂r​ϵ=0. This gives V′γ1γ2ϵ=0. Since (γ1γ2)2=−I, this matrix is invertible. A parallel spinor that vanishes at any point vanishes everywhere on the connected domain, by uniqueness of parallel transport. Thus a nonzero parallel spinor implies V′=0 throughout the patch. There are no nonzero parallel spinors unless V is constant. The zero spinor field is always a solution, so “nonzero” is essential in interpreting the printed assertion. A smooth original V of either sign is constant on such a patch exactly when ∣V∣ is constant; V=0 is excluded because the given metric is not defined there.
Write the constant as c=∣V∣>0, and introduce T=ct, ρ=r/c, and ϕ=cθ. The metric becomes
ds2=−dT2+dρ2+ρ2dϕ2.
(17)
A circle at small ρ has circumference cPρ if θ has period P, whereas its proper radius is ρ. Smoothness requires circumference divided by radius to tend to 2π, not a multiple of 2π. Conversely, when cP=2π, the coordinates X=ρcosϕ, Y=ρsinϕ exhibit a smooth flat spatial disk. Hence
θ must have period 2π/c.​
(18)
For a prescribed period 2π, this requires c=1; otherwise there is a conical singularity at the apex.
For constant V=c, the time and radial equations make the components independent of t,r. The angular equation has all solutions
ϵ(t,r,θ)=exp(2cθ​γ1γ2)ϵ0​=[cos2cθ​I−sin2cθ​γ0]ϵ0​,​
(19)
where ϵ0​ is an arbitrary constant two-component spinor field and γ1γ2=−γ0. There are two independent real solutions for real spinor fields, or two independent complex solutions if complex spinors are used. At the smooth angular period, these polar-frame components change sign. This is exactly the polar-frame parallel spinor transformation under the spin lift of a full rotation: the rotating polar orthonormal frame does not extend through the origin. The spin structure extending over the disk uses this antiperiodic polar trivialization; in a Cartesian orthonormal frame the same parallel spinors have constant components and extend smoothly. Requiring periodic components in the rotating frame would select the other spin structure on the punctured disk, which does not extend over its center.

Use matrix multiplication together with the wedge product of differential forms, suppressing wedge symbols in this calculation. Set F=dA+A2 and a=δA. The gauge field strength varies as δF=da+Aa+aA, the covariant exterior derivative of a. The matrix trace has graded cyclicity, Tr(αβ)=(−1)pqTr(βα) for forms of degrees p,q. Thus the Chern-Simons 5-form can be expanded as
Q5​=Tr(A(dA)2+23​A3dA+53​A5).
(20)
For example, the two cross terms in F2A both become Tr(A3dA); combining the remaining two terms gives the displayed coefficients. This expansion keeps track of the noncommuting products rather than treating A as a scalar one-form.
Put B=dA. Vary the first term and use the graded exterior derivative to move derivatives off a:
δTr(AB2)​=Tr(aB2+AdaB+ABda)=3Tr(aB2)−dTr(AaB+ABa)=3Tr(aB2)+dTr[a(BA+AB)].​
(21)
Here dB=0. For the next term use d(A3)=BA2−ABA+A2B. Graded cyclicity then gives
δTr(A3B)​=Tr(aA2B+AaAB+A2aB+A3da)=2Tr[a(A2B+BA2)]+dTr(aA3).​
(22)
Finally all five contributions to δTr(A5) are equal under the matrix trace, giving 5Tr(aA4). Combining these results proves the variation of the Chern-Simons 5-form:
δQ5​=3Tr(aF2)+dΘ,Θ=Tr[a(FA+AF−21​A3)].​
(23)
Consequently, on an oriented five-manifold N,
δICS​=3∫N​Tr(δA∧F∧F)+∫∂N​Θ.​
(24)
The boundary term vanishes for a closed manifold, for variations supported in its interior, or when the tangential boundary connection is held fixed. A different boundary variational problem would require boundary conditions or a boundary action as well.
Write A=AαTα​ in a basis of the Lie algebra. Independent compactly supported variations of each component yield the bulk Euler-Lagrange equations
Tr(Tα​F∧F)=0for every α.​
(25)
Equivalently, with dαβγ​=21​Tr[Tα​{Tβ​,Tγ​}], these are dαβγ​Fβ∧Fγ=0. The symmetry of the two curvature two-forms accounts for the anticommutator. Writing simply F∧F=0 as a full matrix equation requires an additional assumption: variations must span a matrix algebra paired nondegenerately by the matrix trace. For a smaller Lie algebra, only its trace-paired projection is constrained. If the trace is literally taken in the adjoint representation of a semisimple Lie algebra, its symmetric cubic invariant vanishes, and these bulk equations are identically zero. Indeed, the adjoint generators are skew with respect to the nondegenerate invariant Killing form; transposition in this pairing reverses the sign of the symmetrized trace of three generators. The usual nontrivial five-dimensional theory therefore needs an invariant cubic trace that does not vanish; saying that the gauge field transforms in the adjoint alone does not specify such a trace.
Under a Yang-Mills gauge transformation, use Ag=g−1Ag+g−1dg, so Fg=g−1Fg. For any Lie-algebra element T,
Tr(TFg∧Fg)=Tr(gTg−1F∧F).
(26)
Since gTg−1 is still in the Lie algebra, the right-hand side vanishes whenever the original field satisfies every equation. Every gauge transform of a bulk solution remains a bulk solution. This conclusion follows from covariance of the field equations; it does not require the action itself to be exactly invariant under all large gauge transformations or under transformations changing prescribed boundary data.

Work on a regular patch with a smooth nonzero conformal factor. Its sign is constant there, so replace it by ∣Ω∣ if necessary and assume Ω>0. Put u=logΩ, and let all partial derivatives, index raising on ui​,Ωi​, and the operators Δ,∣∇⋅∣2 in the following formulas use the flat Cartesian metric δ. The orthonormal coframe Ei=Ωdxi has
dEi=Ω−2Ωk​Ek∧Ei.
(27)
The proposed connection 1-forms reduce to
ωij​=Ω−2(Ωj​Ei−Ωi​Ej)=uj​dxi−ui​dxj.​
(28)
Lowering the first frame index with δ makes these antisymmetric, which establishes metric compatibility. Moreover,
ωij​∧Ej=Ω−2Ωj​Ei∧Ej=−dEi.
(29)
Thus Cartan's first structure equation has zero torsion. Uniqueness of the metric-compatible torsion-free connection identifies these forms as the Levi-Civita connection; this also explains why merely guessing a skew connection would not have sufficed.
To calculate the Riemann curvature tensor, use Cartan's second structure equation. Direct differentiation and multiplication give
dωij​ωik​∧ωkj​​=ujk​dxk∧dxi−uik​dxk∧dxj,=uj​uk​dxi∧dxk−∣∇u∣2dxi∧dxj+ui​uk​dxk∧dxj.​
(30)
Introduce the Schouten tensor in Cartesian coordinate components,
Sij​=−uij​+ui​uj​−21​∣∇u∣2δij​=−ΩΩij​​+Ω22Ωi​Ωj​​−2Ω2∣∇Ω∣2​δij​.
(31)
The preceding calculation groups into the curvature 2-forms
Rij​=Sjl​dxi∧dxl+Sik​dxk∧dxj.
(32)
Keep the convention Rijkl​=g(∂i​,R(∂k​,∂l​)∂j​), with R(X,Y)=[∇X​,∇Y​]−∇[X,Y]​, as in the first solution. Converting frame indices to coordinate indices gives the curvature of a conformally Euclidean metric:
Rijkl​=Ω2(δik​Sjl​+δjl​Sik​−δil​Sjk​−δjk​Sil​).​
(33)
These are coordinate components. Components in the dual orthonormal frame are Ω−4Rijkl​, hence the same expression with the overall factor Ω−2. This distinction is important when contracting the Riemann curvature tensor.
Contract with gik=Ω−2δik. In four dimensions the Ricci tensor becomes Rjl​=2Sjl​+δjl​∑k​Skk​, and therefore
Rij​=−2uij​+2ui​uj​−δij​(Δu+2∣∇u∣2)=−Ω2Ωij​​+Ω24Ωi​Ωj​​−δij​(ΩΔΩ​+Ω2∣∇Ω∣2​).​
(34)
Taking the remaining metric trace yields the Ricci scalar
R=−6Ω−2(Δu+∣∇u∣2)=−6Ω−3ΔΩ.​
(35)
The cancellation of the gradient-squared terms in the last expression is special to dimension four.
For the Tolman wormhole, compute on r>0:
Ωi​=−r42λxi​​,Ωij​=−r42λδij​​+r68λxi​xj​​,ΔΩ=0,∣∇Ω∣2=r64λ2​.
(36)
Substitution into the Ricci tensor and Ricci scalar gives
R=0,Rij​=(r2+λ)24λ​(δij​−r24xi​xj​​).​
(37)
For example, the coordinate radial eigenvalue is −12λ/(r2+λ)2, while each of the three tangential eigenvalues is 4λ/(r2+λ)2. Their sum vanishes, but none vanishes when λ=0. The Vacuum Einstein equations with zero cosmological constant require Rij​−21​Rgij​=0 and hence Rij​=0. The metric is scalar-flat but not a vacuum solution for any nonzero λ; only λ=0 gives flat vacuum space. A nonzero cosmological constant does not rescue it: its trace would force R=4Λ, so R=0 would first force Λ=0.
The smooth wormhole interpretation uses λ>0; r=0 is another asymptotically flat end, not an included origin, as inversion ρ=λ/r shows. For λ<0, the conformal factor vanishes at r=−λ​, where the metric is degenerate. The local non-vacuum calculation remains valid on each regular region away from that sphere, but such a metric is not a smooth wormhole across it.

Let a dot denote differentiation with respect to an affine geodesic parameter s. The five-dimensional geodesic Lagrangian is
L=21​γij​x˙ix˙j+21​(z˙+Ai​x˙i)2.
(38)
Because z is cyclic, its Euler-Lagrange equation gives the conserved quantity
k=∂z˙∂L​=z˙+Ai​x˙i,k˙=0.​
(39)
This is the conserved momentum per unit five-dimensional mass associated with the Killing vector field ∂z​. For the other coordinates, differentiate ∂L/∂x˙i=γij​x˙j+kAi​. Using k˙=0 gives
dsd​(γij​x˙j)−21​∂i​γjl​x˙jx˙l+k(∂j​Ai​−∂i​Aj​)x˙j=0.
(40)
The first two terms combine into γij​(x¨j+Γjlm​x˙lx˙m), with the Christoffel symbols of γ. Define the four-dimensional electromagnetic field tensor Fij​=∂i​Aj​−∂j​Ai​ and raise its index with γ. The complete reduced geodesic equations are
dsDx˙i​:=x¨i+Γijl​x˙jx˙l=kFij​x˙j,z˙=k−Ai​x˙i.​
(41)
This is the Kaluza-Klein geodesic Lorentz force. The Christoffel symbols describe the gravitational motion in general relativity in the four-dimensional metric tensor, while the right-hand side is antisymmetric in its lowered indices and gives the relativistic Lorentz force. In particular,
dsd​(γij​x˙ix˙j)=2kFij​x˙ix˙j=0.
(42)
Thus the force preserves the four-dimensional velocity norm, as a Lorentz force must. A particle with k=0 is neutral and follows an ordinary four-dimensional geodesic. Nonzero compact momentum gives electric charge, with its sign determined by the direction of motion around the circle.
To identify the actual charge-to-mass ratio, suppose the five-dimensional particle has mass M>0 and s is its five-dimensional proper time, so g5​(X˙,X˙)=−1. Then γ(x˙,x˙)=−(1+k2). Four-dimensional proper time obeys dτ/ds=1+k2​; since k is constant, ui=dxi/dτ satisfies
dτDui​=1+k2​k​Fij​uj=mq​Fij​uj,q=pz​=Mk,m=M2+pz2​​.​
(43)
Here q is in the geometric normalization of the supplied potential A. The relation m2=M2+pz2​ also follows directly from the five-dimensional mass shell, and continues to identify the effective mass when the higher-dimensional particle is massless. The distinction between the two proper times prevents mistaking k itself for the physical four-dimensional charge-to-mass ratio. A rescaling of A to a canonically normalized electromagnetic potential correspondingly rescales q.
For the wave calculation, take a scalar field of five-dimensional mass M in units ℏ=c=1, obeying (□5​−M2)Ψ=0. In the coordinate order (xi,z), direct inversion of the metric tensor gives
g5ij​=γij,g5iz​=−Ai,g5zz​=1+Ai​Ai,detg5​=detγ.
(44)
The determinant follows by the triangular change of coframe from (dxi,dz) to (dxi,dz+Ai​dxi), whose determinant is one. Write J=∣detγ∣​. Since all coefficients are z-independent, the scalar covariant wave operator is
□5​Ψ=J−1(∂i​−Ai​∂z​)[Jγij(∂j​−Aj​∂z​)Ψ]+∂z2​Ψ.
(45)
Expanding the right-hand side verifies both cross terms −2Ai∂i​∂z​Ψ, the term −J−1∂i​(JAi)∂z​Ψ, and the coefficient 1+Ai​Ai of ∂z2​Ψ.
Single-valuedness under the compact coordinate period requires the Fourier series
Ψ(x,z)=∑n∈Z​ψn​(x)einz/R.
(46)
For the nth Kaluza-Klein mode, put qn​=n/R and Di​=∂i​−iqn​Ai​. The five-dimensional Klein-Gordon equation reduces to
[J−1Di​(JγijDj​)−mn2​]ψn​=0,mn2​=M2+R2n2​.​
(47)
This is the charged four-dimensional Klein-Gordon equation: the divergence expression includes both the spacetime connection and the gauge connection. Its interpretation as electric charge can also be checked directly. Under the fibre coordinate change z′=z−χ(x), the metric retains its form with A′=A+dχ, and the scalar Fourier coefficient changes by ψn′​=eiqn​χψn​. Hence Di′​ψn′​=eiqn​χDi​ψn​, exactly the transformation law for a field of charge qn​.
The Kaluza-Klein charge quantization is therefore
qn​=Rn​,qunit​=R1​(ℏ=1).​
(48)
Positive and negative integers give opposite charges, and the zero mode is neutral. Restoring ℏ gives compact momenta pz​=nℏ/R, so their fundamental spacing is ℏ/R. These are charges in the metric's geometric normalization. If Ai​=κAi​ for a physically normalized electromagnetic potential Ai​, the physical charge unit is κ/R in natural units, or κℏ/R with ℏ restored. Determining κ requires an electromagnetic action normalization not specified by the metric ansatz alone.
