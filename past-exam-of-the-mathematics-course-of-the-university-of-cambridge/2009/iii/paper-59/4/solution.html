The starting point is the Grassmannian of complex two-planes W⊂T≅C4. Choose a basis u,v and form the simple bivector P=u∧v∈Λ2T. A change of basis multiplies P by a nonzero determinant, so its projective class is independent of that choice. The Plücker embedding is therefore
Gr(2,4)⟶P(Λ2C4)≅CP5,W⟼[u∧v].
(30)
It is injective because W={z:z∧P=0}. In a basis of T, its six homogeneous coordinates pij​=ui​vj​−uj​vi​ satisfy
p12​p34​−p13​p24​+p14​p23​=0.​
(31)
This is the condition P∧P=0. Conversely, a nonzero antisymmetric 4×4 matrix satisfying it has Pfaffian zero, hence rank at most two; its nonzero rank is even, so it has rank two and is a simple bivector. Therefore the equation characterizes the image, the Klein quadric Q⊂CP5. Its gradient cannot vanish at a projective point, since its six partial derivatives are the six coordinates up to permutation and sign. Thus Q is smooth and has complex dimension four, agreeing with Gr(2,4).
The Klein quadric is the complexified compactified Minkowski space of the twistor correspondence. A volume form on T turns P∧P′ into a scalar symmetric bilinear form B(P,P′). It is zero exactly when the two underlying two-planes have nontrivial intersection: the four spanning vectors are then linearly dependent. In projective twistor space this says that the two twistor lines meet. In the affine graph chart supplied by the twistor incidence relation, it is det(x−y)=0, so it is precisely null separation.
This also determines a conformal geometry on Q. At [P], the tangent space is P⊥/⟨P⟩ for B. The restriction of B descends to a nondegenerate quadratic form on this four-dimensional quotient. Rescaling the representative P changes the identification by a scalar, so its invariant content is a conformal class. In a graph chart its null tangent vectors are the rank-one matrices in Hom(W,T/W), equivalently dxAA′=aAbA′.
The compact complex geometry does not yet specify a real spacetime. The Lorentzian slice comes from the twistor Hermitian form h of signature (2,2): real points are maximal totally isotropic two-planes. In the incidence chart, isotropy of Wx​={(ixπ,π)} is equivalent to x=x†, the usual real Minkowski condition. Its twistor lines lie wholly in projective null twistor space. Diagonalizing h as positive and negative two-dimensional blocks also gives a useful global description: an isotropic two-plane projects isomorphically to the positive block, so it is a graph (z,Uz), and isotropy is U†U=1. The real compact space is therefore U(2), equivalently (S1×S3)/Z2​.
The same result is especially transparent in coordinates of the Plücker embedding after a complex linear change of coordinates on Λ2T. The real Lorentzian cone has equation
T2+V2−W2−X2−Y2−Z2=0.
(32)
Its nonzero real generators represent points. Normalize the lengths of its positive and negative parts to one; real projectivization identifies their simultaneous antipodes. Consequently
ML​≅(S1×S3)/{(u,v)∼(−u,−v)}.​
(33)
This is the Projective Minkowski compactification. Its universal cover is R×S3, with deck generator (s,v)↦(s+π,−v), so its fundamental group is Z. The projective version includes periodic identifications; an unwrapped Einstein-cylinder or Penrose diagram description uses the appropriate cover and affine domain instead.
For example, use homogeneous cone coordinates [U,V,t,x] with quadratic equation 2UV+t2−∣x∣2=0. The affine patch U=0 sets U=1 and then V=−(t2−∣x∣2)/2. The complement U=0 is the null cone t2−∣x∣2=0 at infinity. It is much larger than a single point: different null directions retain different limits there.
For the Euclidean slice, choose an antilinear quaternionic structure on a complex vector space J on twistor space, for example
J(z1​,z2​,z3​,z4​)=(−z2​,z1​,−z4​,z3​),J2=−1.
(34)
The Euclidean reality structure on twistor space selects J-invariant complex two-planes. Such a plane is a quaternionic line in H2, giving
ME​≅HP1≅S4.​
(35)
There are no fixed projective twistors for J: if JZ=cZ, applying J again would give −Z=∣c∣2Z. Every projective twistor instead lies on the unique invariant line Pspan(Z,JZ). Thus the Euclidean correspondence becomes a fibration CP3→S4 with fiber CP1, in contrast to the Lorentzian correspondence where only null twistors represent real null geodesics. Distinct Euclidean real points have disjoint twistor lines, since the line through any Z and JZ is unique. This reflects the absence of nonzero real null vectors for a positive-definite metric.
On the Klein quadric the Euclidean real cone has signature (1,5); normalize its timelike coordinate to one and obtain the unit sphere S4. Its affine Euclidean chart omits one point. Inverse stereographic projection explicitly gives the round conformal metric
dsS42​=(1+∣y∣2)24∑j=14​dyj2​​,​
(36)
which extends smoothly across that point. This proves the one-point conformal compactification of Euclidean space. The distinction between the Lorentzian Hermitian and Euclidean quaternionic reality structures is also described in Adamo's lectures on twistor theory; the global statements here follow from the explicit models just derived.
Finally, Wick rotation t↦it compares affine real slices through their complexification. Locally it changes dt2−∣dx∣2 to −dτ2−∣dx∣2, the negative of the Euclidean metric; the overall sign does not change its null cone in the complexified setting. It does not identify the two real conformal compactifications globally. The Lorentzian projective space has fundamental group Z, while S4 is simply connected. Its causal universal cover R×S3 is noncompact, so passing to that cover does not repair the mismatch with the compact Euclidean sphere.
There is also a direct algebraic obstruction. In the homogeneous coordinates above the affine Wick substitution extends only as the rational map
[U,V,t,x]⟼[U2,UV+t2,iUt,Ux].
(37)
Substitution verifies that the image satisfies the same complex quadric equation. For U=0 and t=0 it collapses the point to [0,1,0,0], while at some infinity points every output coordinate vanishes. In particular it is indeterminate at [0,1,0,0]. This is a genuine failure of holomorphic extension: the affine paths (t,x)=(0,(s,0,0)) and (s,(is,0,0)), as s→∞, both tend to that input point. Their Wick images tend respectively to [0,1,0,0] and [0,0,1,(1,0,0)], which are distinct points of the complex quadric.
Thus the answer to the global compatibility question is no: ordinary affine Wick rotation is a local analytic continuation, not a globally regular invertible map of the conformally compactified real spacetimes. The global obstruction to Wick rotation of conformal compactifications does not prevent both real slices from being studied inside the same smooth complex Klein quadric.
