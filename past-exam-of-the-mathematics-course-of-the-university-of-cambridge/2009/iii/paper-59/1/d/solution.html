First use a genuine simple bivector of the specified chirality, with spinor part ϕAB=αAαB. It is a exterior product: choose independent primed spinors ρA′,σA′ and take uAA′=αAρA′, vBB′=αBσB′. Their antisymmetric product is proportional to wAA′BB′=αAαBϵA′B′.
For any antisymmetric wab, the Riemann curvature tensor acts on both tensor indices. In the printed curvature convention its complete contraction is
[∇a​,∇b​]wab=Rabc​awcb+Rabc​bwac=−Rbc​wcb+Rac​wac=0,
(8)
because the Ricci tensor is symmetric. Substituting the one-chirality bivector and the chiral spinor curvature operator decomposition, the primed-operator term vanishes on contraction with the symmetric αAαB. The other term is 2ΔAB​(αAαB). Hence ΔAB​(αAαB)=0.
Repeat with α+β and subtract the identities for α and β. Linearity leaves twice the mixed symmetric product, giving the contracted chiral curvature identity
ΔAB​(α(AβB))=0.​
(9)
The polarization works for spinor fields as well as pointwise spinors: the chiral spinor curvature operator is algebraic by the preceding part.
The displayed source expression αA​βB​ϵA′B′​ needs this clarification. For independent α,β it is not antisymmetric in the spacetime indices. Its antisymmetric part is α(A​βB)​ϵA′B′​, which is generally not simple. The proof above uses a genuinely simple bivector first and then polarization, so it establishes the requested conclusion without relying on the incorrect simplicity assertion.
