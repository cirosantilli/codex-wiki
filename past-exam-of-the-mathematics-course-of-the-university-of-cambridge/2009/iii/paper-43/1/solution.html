A phase diagram partitions a space of control parameters into regions with distinct equilibrium phases. Its boundaries mark nonanalyticities of the thermodynamic-limit free energy; a first-order phase transition has a jump in a first derivative, whereas a continuous phase transition has a continuously vanishing order parameter and singular fluctuations.
An explicit three-parameter example is the scalar Landau free energy
V(M;r,u,h)=2r​M2+4u​M4+6v​M6−hM,v>0,
(1)
with parameter axes (r,u,h). These can be realized locally by the temperature, single-ion anisotropy and magnetic field of the Blume–Capel model. The three dimensions here refer to control-parameter space, not necessarily to three spatial dimensions. At h=0, minimizing this potential gives a continuous phase transition at r=0 for u>0. For u<0, coexistence occurs at r=3u2/(16v), where
V(M)=6v​M2(M2+4v3u​)2.
(2)
There are three global minima, M=0 and M=±−3u/(4v)​. Their order parameter jump decreases to zero as u↑0. Thus (r,u,h)=(0,0,0) is a tricritical point. The two transition curves in the h=0 plane meet there.
In the three-dimensional phase diagram, crossing h=0 inside the ordered region switches between opposite signs of M, so that region is itself a first-order phase transition sheet. For u<0, two tricritical wings at opposite nonzero fields separate weakly and strongly magnetized phases. Their wing critical edges can be obtained rather than merely sketched: require V′=V′′=V′′′=0. The wings meet the zero-field coexistence sheet along the tricritical three-phase line and terminate at these edges. For the unheaded Ising model calculation, assume ferromagnetic exchange J>0 and let M denote the mean Ising spin. On the hypercubic lattice the coordination number of a lattice is z=2D, even though the original positive-direction bond sum counts each bond only once.
