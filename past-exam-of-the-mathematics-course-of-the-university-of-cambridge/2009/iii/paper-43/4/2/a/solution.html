Write S=H/T and take n≥2. The unit-length constraint prevents an arbitrary multiplicative field rescaling, so the running coupling is found by renormalizing the coefficient of ∣∇σ∣2. We compute that coefficient in a slowly rotating planar background, which is enough because O(n) symmetry makes the two-derivative stiffness isotropic on the sphere.
Let n0​=(cosθ,sinθ,0,…), e1​=(−sinθ,cosθ,0,…) and ea​ for 2≤a≤n−1 the remaining constant orthonormal tangent vectors. Parameterize the fast tangent fluctuations by
σ=n0​q+∑a=1n−1​ea​πa​,q=1−π2​,π2=∑a​πa2​.
(46)
This satisfies σ2=1 exactly. Put Ωμ​=∂μ​θ, and choose it locally constant and small compared with the shell momenta. Since ∂μ​n0​=e1​Ωμ​ and ∂μ​e1​=−n0​Ωμ​, direct differentiation gives
∣∂μ​σ∣2=∣∂μ​π∣2+(∂μ​q)2+Ωμ2​(1−∑a=2n−1​πa2​)+2Ωμ​(q∂μ​π1​−π1​∂μ​q).
(47)
At quadratic order in the fast fields, the cross term is 2Ωμ​∂μ​π1​, whose integral is zero for constant Ωμ​. The term (∂q)2 is quartic in the fast fields. Remaining cross terms are cubic and have zero Gaussian expectation; their effects on the two-derivative stiffness start at higher loop order. Field-measure corrections independent of the background contribute normalization terms and do not alter the leading gradient coefficient in this calculation.
The quadratic fast action is (2g)−1∫∣∇π∣2. In the shell Λ/b<∣p∣<Λ its connected correlation function is ⟨πa​(p)πb​(−p)⟩=gδab​/p2 with the momentum delta function understood. Thus ⟨πa​(x)2⟩=gID​, where
ID​(b)=∫Λ/b<∣p∣<Λ​(2π)DdDp​p21​=(2π)DSD​​Λϵϵ1−b−ϵ​,D=2+ϵ,
(48)
with the limit I2​(b)=(2π)−1logb. Here SD​=2πD/2/Γ(D/2) is the angular measure of the unit (D−1)-sphere. Averaging the background-gradient coefficient gives the one-loop stiffness of a spherical nonlinear sigma model:
geff​1​=g1​[1−(n−2)gID​]=g1​−(n−2)ID​.
(49)
The coefficient is n−2, not n−1: the fluctuation tangent to the rotation plane does not reduce the gradient cost, because its contribution cancels between q2 and π12​. All other n−2 transverse components do reduce it.
After integrating out the shell, put x=bx′ to restore the cutoff. The constrained field keeps unit norm, so g′=b−ϵgeff​, or at this order
g′=b−ϵ[g+(n−2)g2ID​(b)+O(g3)].
(50)
For b=edℓ, ID​=(SD​/(2π)D)Λϵdℓ+O(dℓ2). Therefore the renormalization-group beta function is
dlogbdg​=−ϵg+(2π)DSD​​Λϵ(n−2)g2+O(g3).​
(51)
The displayed terms are the requested one-loop flow. They are a weak-coupling expansion; the discrete n=1 target has no tangent modes and must instead be treated by domain walls, as in the first part.
