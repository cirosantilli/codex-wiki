Assume short-range ferromagnetic exchange J>0 and zero external field. In the one-dimensional Ising model, a domain wall costs only 2J, independent of the lengths of the domains it separates. There are order-N positions for one wall, so its positional entropy offsets any finite energy cost at nonzero temperature as the system grows. Domain walls consequently have a positive density at every T>0, destroying a uniform spontaneous spin magnetization. Periodic boundaries require an even number of walls, but that constraint does not remove their nonzero thermodynamic-limit density.
The One-dimensional Ising correlation function makes this argument explicit. A bond is broken with probability pw​=e−2βth​J/(1+e−2βth​J), and the infinite-chain correlation is
G(r)=(1−2pw​)r/a=(tanhβth​J)r/a,ξ=−logtanhβth​Ja​<∞(T>0).
(44)
The dominant eigenvalue of the transfer matrix for the one-dimensional Ising model is 2coshβth​J, so the free energy per spin −kB​Tlog[2coshβth​J] is analytic for T>0. There is no finite-temperature transition in one dimension; only the zero-temperature limit orders.
In two dimensions a reversed cluster is enclosed by a Peierls contour. A contour of length ℓ costs 2Jℓ, while the number of such contours surrounding a fixed site grows at most as a polynomial in ℓ times 3ℓ: after the initial edge, a nonbacktracking square-lattice walk has at most three choices per step. With plus boundary conditions, flipping the interior of a specified contour yields the Boltzmann suppression e−2βth​Jℓ. Therefore
Pr(σ0​=−1)≤∑ℓ≥4​Cℓ2(3e−2βth​J)ℓ.
(45)
For sufficiently low temperature this bound is below 1/2, giving a positive spontaneous magnetization. This is the energy-entropy mechanism of the Peierls argument.
At sufficiently high temperature, the graphical expansion bounds correlations by sums of connecting paths weighted by (tanhβth​J)ℓ. The same nonbacktracking count converges geometrically when 3tanhβth​J<1, giving exponential decay and a disordered phase. Since an ordered low-temperature phase and a disordered high-temperature phase both occur, the two-dimensional model has a finite-temperature phase transition. The argument establishes its existence and the dimensional distinction, not the exact value of its critical temperature.
