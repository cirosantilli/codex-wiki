Let ϕ be the scaling function and ψ an orthonormal wavelet, with ϕj,k​(x)=2j/2ϕ(2jx−k) and ψj,k​(x)=2j/2ψ(2jx−k). For f∈L2(R), the wavelet series at a coarse level j0​ is
f=∑k∈Z​⟨f,ϕj0​,k​⟩ϕj0​,k​+∑j≥j0​​∑k∈Z​⟨f,ψj,k​⟩ψj,k​.
(10)
The series converges in the L2 norm. Its coarse part captures broad variation and successive wavelet levels add finer detail. Truncating at levels j<J produces the orthogonal projection onto VJ​; Parseval identity makes its squared approximation error the sum of the omitted squared coefficients. Merely square-integrable functions need not have pointwise convergence.
For the Haar wavelet, use the cell convention Ij,k​=(k2−j,(k+1)2−j] and let Ij​(x) be the unique such interval containing x. The Haar scaling functions are 2j/21Ij,k​​, so the Haar projection is
Kj​f(x)=2j∫Ij​(x)​f(t)dt.
(11)
For a merely locally integrable function this cell-average formula remains defined, even if a global L2 orthogonal projection is unavailable. If ∥f′∥∞​=L, the mean value theorem gives Lipschitz continuity and, since ∣t−x∣≤2−j on the cell,
∣Kj​f(x)−f(x)∣≤2j∫Ij​(x)​L∣t−x∣dt≤L2−j.
(12)
Thus the requested bound holds with c=∥f′∥∞​​, by the Haar projection error for a Lipschitz function.
To estimate a density, replace each scaling or wavelet coefficient ∫fϕj,k​ or ∫fψj,k​ by the sample average of that basis function. The finite-resolution Haar density estimator is therefore
f​jW​(x)=∑k​(n1​∑i=1n​ϕj,k​(Xi​))ϕj,k​(x)=n2j​∑i=1n​1Ij​(x)​(Xi​).
(13)
It is the histogram on the dyadic cells, is nonnegative, and integrates to one. Its expectation is Kj​f(x). A density with bounded derivative is bounded: the Lipschitz density height bound gives f(x)2≤L by integrating the triangular lower envelope (f(x)−L∣t−x∣)+​. The case L=0 cannot be a probability density on the whole real line. Hence B=∥f∥∞​<∞.
The cell probability is pj​(x)=∫Ij​(x)​f≤B2−j. The sample count has a binomial distribution, so
Var(f​jW​(x))=n22j​pj​(x)(1−pj​(x))≤nB2j​.
(14)
The Cauchy-Schwarz inequality bounds the mean centered absolute deviation by the square root of this variance. Adding the projection bias gives
E∣f​jW​(x)−f(x)∣≤L2−j+nB2j​​.
(15)
Choose jn​=⌊(log2​n)/3⌋, so 2jn​≍n1/3. The two terms have the same order, and
E∣f​jn​W​(x)−f(x)∣=O(n−1/3).​
(16)
This is a bias-variance tradeoff: refining the cells decreases the averaging bias but increases the sampling variance.
The Haar projection averages this normal distribution density on each cell; the finer cells follow its variation more closely. A Haar density estimator replaces these exact cell probabilities by empirical frequencies and therefore also introduces sampling noise.
