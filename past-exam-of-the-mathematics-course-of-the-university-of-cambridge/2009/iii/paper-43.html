A phase diagram partitions a space of control parameters into regions with distinct equilibrium phases. Its boundaries mark nonanalyticities of the thermodynamic-limit free energy; a first-order phase transition has a jump in a first derivative, whereas a continuous phase transition has a continuously vanishing order parameter and singular fluctuations.
An explicit three-parameter example is the scalar Landau free energy
V(M;r,u,h)=2r​M2+4u​M4+6v​M6−hM,v>0,
(1)
with parameter axes (r,u,h). These can be realized locally by the temperature, single-ion anisotropy and magnetic field of the Blume–Capel model. The three dimensions here refer to control-parameter space, not necessarily to three spatial dimensions. At h=0, minimizing this potential gives a continuous phase transition at r=0 for u>0. For u<0, coexistence occurs at r=3u2/(16v), where
V(M)=6v​M2(M2+4v3u​)2.
(2)
There are three global minima, M=0 and M=±−3u/(4v)​. Their order parameter jump decreases to zero as u↑0. Thus (r,u,h)=(0,0,0) is a tricritical point. The two transition curves in the h=0 plane meet there.
In the three-dimensional phase diagram, crossing h=0 inside the ordered region switches between opposite signs of M, so that region is itself a first-order phase transition sheet. For u<0, two tricritical wings at opposite nonzero fields separate weakly and strongly magnetized phases. Their wing critical edges can be obtained rather than merely sketched: require V′=V′′=V′′′=0. Since V′′′=6uM+20vM3, the nonzero solutions satisfy
Mc2​=−10v3u​,rc​=20v9u2​,hc​=25v6u2Mc​​.
(3)
The fourth derivative there is −12u>0, giving ordinary continuous critical endpoints. The wings meet the zero-field coexistence sheet along the tricritical three-phase line and terminate at these edges. Their exact tricritical wing coexistence factorization supplies the surfaces in the figure: for two nonnegative minima a≤b, set s=a+b, p=ab and
u=32v​(−2s2+3p),r=3v​(s4−s2p+3p2),h=3v​s3p.
(4)
Direct expansion gives V(M)−V(a)=(v/6)(M−a)2(M−b)2[(M+s)2+p]≥0, proving that these surfaces describe coexistence of global, not merely local, minima.
For the unheaded Ising model calculation, assume ferromagnetic exchange J>0 and let M denote the mean Ising spin. On the hypercubic lattice the coordination number of a lattice is z=2D, even though the original positive-direction bond sum counts each bond only once. The mean-field approximation neglects products of deviations from M:
σi​σj​≃Mσi​+Mσj​−M2.
(5)
There are ND bonds, so the approximate energy is HMF​=−2DJM∑i​σi​+NDJM2. Each independent Ising spin therefore sees the effective field 2DJM. With βth​=1/(kB​T), averaging its two Boltzmann weights gives the self-consistency equation
M=e2βth​DJM+e−2βth​DJMe2βth​DJM−e−2βth​DJM​=tanh(kB​T2DJM​).​
(6)
The slope of its right side at zero is Tc​/T, where
Tc​=kB​2DJ​.​
(7)
Above Tc​, tanhx<x for x>0 excludes a nonzero solution. Below Tc​ the initial slope exceeds one, and concavity of the hyperbolic tangent on the positive axis gives one positive solution and its negative partner. The Ising auxiliary mean-field free energy DJM2−kB​Tlog[2cosh(2βth​DJM)] has positive quartic coefficient near Tc​, while its quadratic coefficient changes sign there. Its stable minima therefore move continuously away from zero; this is a continuous phase transition in the mean-field theory of the Ising model.
Put τ=(Tc​−T)/Tc​>0 and c=Tc​/T=(1−τ)−1. The Taylor expansion tanhx=x−x3/3+O(x5) gives, on the nonzero branch,
0=c−1−3c3​M2+O(M4),M2=c33(c−1)​+O((c−1)2)=3τ+O(τ2).
(8)
Consequently M∼3​τ1/2 and the mean-field order-parameter exponent is β=1/2. The inverse-temperature symbol βth​ used above is distinct from this order-parameter critical exponent.

For a ferromagnet the scalar order parameter is the spontaneous spin magnetization. It distinguishes a symmetry-invariant phase with zero mean from one of two phases with a nonzero mean of opposite signs. At zero field, a finite system with spin inversion symmetry has exactly zero mean. The spontaneous value is defined by taking the thermodynamic limit first and then sending the symmetry-breaking conjugate field to zero from one side.
A Landau-Ginzburg theory promotes the coarse magnetization to a scalar field M(x) and uses
F[M]=∫dDx[2κ​∣∇M∣2+2r​M2+4u​M4+6v​M6−hM],κ>0, v>0.
(9)
At h=0, spin inversion symmetry permits only even local powers. The gradient term penalizes spatial variations. The equilibrium statistical weight is e−F/(kB​T); the Landau approximation chooses a stable minimum of F, whereas the full functional integral retains fluctuations around it. In a homogeneous phase this gives the equation of state h=rM+uM3+vM5. The field h is conjugate to the order parameter, with M=−∂feq​/∂h.

Use the connected correlation function G(x)=⟨M(x)M(0)⟩−⟨M⟩2, since the disconnected constant in an ordered phase does not measure fluctuation decay. Away from criticality its large-distance exponential defines the correlation length:
ξ−1=−limr→∞​r1​log∣G(r)∣,
(10)
when that limit exists. Algebraic prefactors do not affect this definition.
Expand the Landau-Ginzburg theory about a stable homogeneous minimum M0​. With A=V′′(M0​)>0, the quadratic fluctuation energy is 21​∫dDx[κ∣∇δM∣2+A(δM)2]. The Gaussian functional integral gives
G(k)=κk2+AkB​T​,ξ=κ/A​.​
(11)
This Ornstein--Zernike correlation function shows why the curvature of the free-energy minimum controls correlations. At a continuous phase transition that curvature vanishes, allowing long-range fluctuations. In the ordinary quartic theory with u>0, A=r above the transition and A=−2r on either ordered branch below, so ξ∝∣r∣−1/2 on both sides. At an equilibrium first-order transition the competing minima generally retain nonzero curvature, and the correlation length remains finite. The limit of local stability at a spinodal point is a distinct mean-field phenomenon.

c OurBigBook.com
Microscopically different systems can have the same critical exponents and appropriately normalized scaling functions. This universality means that they belong to one universality class when coarse-graining brings them to the same renormalization-group fixed point. Spatial dimension, order parameter symmetry and the range of the interactions affect that classification; changing a lattice spacing or a microscopic coupling generally changes nonuniversal amplitudes and the critical temperature instead.
In the scalar short-range Landau-Ginzburg theory, a positive quartic term and one thermal tuning produce ordinary Ising-type criticality. A generic sextic term does not change that fixed point, but tuning the quartic coefficient to zero exposes a different tricritical point with different exponents. The mean-field approximation itself neglects fluctuations. Its numerical exponents are reliable only when the Ginzburg criterion makes those fluctuations subleading; the ordinary scalar upper critical dimension is four, while the tricritical one is three. At an upper critical dimension logarithmic corrections can remain.

Set h=0 in the even sextic Landau free energy. For u>0, near r=0 the nonzero stationary branch satisfies M2=−r/u+O(r2) for r<0. The minimum value is Vmin​=−r2/(4u)+O(r3) below the transition and zero above. The order parameter is continuous, and if r crosses zero linearly with temperature, the first temperature derivative of the minimized free-energy density is continuous too. There is no latent heat; a second derivative has a finite jump at this level.
For u<0, solve the two conditions V′(M)=0 and V(M)=V(0) with M=0. Setting q=M2 gives r+uq+vq2=0 and r/2+uq/4+vq2/6=0. Eliminating r gives
M2=−4v3u​,r=16v3u2​.​
(12)
Both the disordered and ordered minima have positive curvature there, but their values coincide. Crossing this phase coexistence boundary changes the global minimum discontinuously. The order parameter jumps; for r=r(T) at fixed u,v, ∂T​Vmin​=r′(T)M2/2 jumps as well, generically yielding a nonzero latent heat. Local extrema can persist past coexistence as metastable states, which explains hysteresis in a dynamically delayed transition. The spinodal points, where local curvature vanishes, do not locate the equilibrium transition.

A critical exponent is the leading power of a singular observable as a control variable approaches a critical value. Use r∝t=(T−Tc​)/Tc​ and distinguish the exponents α,β,γ,δ,ν,η from coefficient names in a Hamiltonian. Their standard definitions are
M∼(−t)β,χ∼∣t∣−γ,Cs​∼∣t∣−α,M(t=0)∼sgn(h)∣h∣1/δ,ξ∼∣t∣−ν,G(r;t=0)∼r−(D−2+η).
(14)
The Landau approximation predicts these powers by minimizing the Landau free energy and differentiating the equation of state. At an ordinary quartic point, M2=−r/u, χ−1=r above and 2∣r∣ below, and h=uM3 on the critical isotherm. The equilibrium singular free energy is proportional to −r2 below, giving a heat-capacity jump. The quadratic fluctuation denominator is κk2+V′′, so ν=1/2 and η=0. Thus the ordinary mean-field critical exponents are
(α,β,γ,δ,ν,η)=(0,21​,1,3,21​,0).​
(15)
At a tuned tricritical point, the preceding solution gives M∝(−r)1/4, Vmin​∝−∣r∣3/2 and χ−1=4∣r∣ below. At r=0 the equation of state is h=vM5. The curvature still vanishes linearly, and the Gaussian gradient term still gives zero anomalous dimension. Therefore
(α,β,γ,δ,ν,η)=(21​,41​,1,5,21​,0).​
(16)
These mean-field tricritical exponent calculations require approach along the tuned quartic-zero direction. Fluctuations modify the powers below the appropriate upper critical dimension, and marginal effects can add logarithms at it.

Take the connected correlation function
G(r)=⟨σ0​σr​⟩−⟨σ0​⟩⟨σr​⟩.

Choose a nonnegative blocking kernel Kb​(σ′,σ) normalized by ∑σ′​Kb​(σ′,σ)=1. It may enforce a block average, a majority rule or a chosen rescaled coarse spin. Define the transformed weight by

A coordinate rescaling subsequently restores the reference cutoff if desired. The transformation is exact only if all generated operators are retained; a finite-coupling truncation is an approximation.

The parameter Cp​ is an accumulated additive energy per coarse site. It multiplies the identity operator and leaves normalized spin probabilities unchanged, but it must be kept when calculating the absolute free energy. It records the eliminated-mode entropy and the normalization of their statistical measure.

The affine equation naturally applies to the unsubtracted free energy. To extract its singular part, write f=freg​+Fs​ and choose a regular background satisfying freg​(u)=b−Dfreg​(Rb​u)+g(u). Subtraction then gives Fs​(u)=b−DFs​(Rb​u). This analytic subtraction of an inhomogeneous renormalization recursion is justified when the eliminated finite-shell contribution is analytic near the fixed point and admits a regular subtraction. It can fail in a resonant direction, producing additive logarithms. The simple homogeneous formulas also assume the remaining irrelevant operators are not dangerously irrelevant couplings, and that marginal operators do not produce unaccounted logarithmic corrections. Calling the quantity in the affine equation “singular” does not make g vanish automatically.
Near a renormalization-group fixed point u∗​, linearize the coupling map. In scaling coordinates vi​, write vi′​=bλi​vi​ to leading order. Here bλi​ is a discrete multiplier, whereas λi​ is its logarithmic scaling exponent. A relevant operator has λi​>0 and grows under coarse-graining; an irrelevant operator has λi​<0 and decays. At m2=0 and h=0 the model is at a Gaussian fixed point. Its connected correlation function has the power-law critical form r−(D−2) rather than a finite exponential decay length. Approaching from m2>0, the uniform susceptibility G0​(0)=1/m2 and the correlation length both diverge. If the thermal scaling field is linear in m2 with α finite and positive, then
ξ=(αm2)−1/2∝∣t∣−1/2,ν=21​.​
(42)
The same result follows from the mass multiplier b2: its logarithmic thermal eigenvalue is two, so ν=1/2.
The determinant also shows a thermodynamic singularity. Two derivatives of the free-energy density with respect to m2 give, up to sign and a nonzero normalization,
∫Λ(2π)DdDp​(α−1p2+m2)21​∝(m2)D/2−2
(43)
as m2↓0. Rescaling p=αm2​q establishes the power, and the dimensionless integral is ultraviolet-convergent for D<4. Integrating back gives a nonanalytic free-energy term proportional to (m2)D/2 after regular backgrounds are removed. This is critical behavior of the quadratic model.
There is a stability qualification: a pure Gaussian potential with m2<0 is unbounded below in the uniform mode, so it does not by itself provide a stable ordered phase on the other side. The ordered-phase obstruction in a Gaussian scalar model is removed by adding a positive quartic interaction. In 2<D<4 that interaction is relevant, so the resulting generic scalar critical point need not retain the Gaussian exponents. Thus the calculation proves the Gaussian critical singularity and ν=1/2, without claiming that the unmodified quadratic integral defines both phases of a physical ordered transition.

Assume short-range ferromagnetic exchange J>0 and zero external field. In the one-dimensional Ising model, a domain wall costs only 2J, independent of the lengths of the domains it separates. There are order-N positions for one wall, so its positional entropy offsets any finite energy cost at nonzero temperature as the system grows. Domain walls consequently have a positive density at every T>0, destroying a uniform spontaneous spin magnetization. Periodic boundaries require an even number of walls, but that constraint does not remove their nonzero thermodynamic-limit density.
The One-dimensional Ising correlation function makes this argument explicit. A bond is broken with probability pw​=e−2βth​J/(1+e−2βth​J), and the infinite-chain correlation is
G(r)=(1−2pw​)r/a=(tanhβth​J)r/a,ξ=−logtanhβth​Ja​<∞(T>0).
(44)
The dominant eigenvalue of the transfer matrix for the one-dimensional Ising model is 2coshβth​J, so the free energy per spin −kB​Tlog[2coshβth​J] is analytic for T>0. There is no finite-temperature transition in one dimension; only the zero-temperature limit orders.
In two dimensions a reversed cluster is enclosed by a Peierls contour. A contour of length ℓ costs 2Jℓ, while the number of such contours surrounding a fixed site grows at most as a polynomial in ℓ times 3ℓ: after the initial edge, a nonbacktracking square-lattice walk has at most three choices per step. With plus boundary conditions, flipping the interior of a specified contour yields the Boltzmann suppression e−2βth​Jℓ. Therefore
Pr(σ0​=−1)≤∑ℓ≥4​Cℓ2(3e−2βth​J)ℓ.
(45)
For sufficiently low temperature this bound is below 1/2, giving a positive spontaneous magnetization. This is the energy-entropy mechanism of the Peierls argument.
At sufficiently high temperature, the graphical expansion bounds correlations by sums of connecting paths weighted by (tanhβth​J)ℓ. The same nonbacktracking count converges geometrically when 3tanhβth​J<1, giving exponential decay and a disordered phase. Since an ordered low-temperature phase and a disordered high-temperature phase both occur, the two-dimensional model has a finite-temperature phase transition. The argument establishes its existence and the dimensional distinction, not the exact value of its critical temperature.

The zero point attracts small positive couplings, whereas the nonzero point is the separatrix between the low-temperature weak-coupling flow and the strong-coupling regime. This is a perturbative description for small ϵ; extrapolation of its nonzero root to large ϵ is not controlled.
At ϵ=0 and n>2, the only finite one-loop fixed point is g=0, a double root. Positive g flows to strong coupling.
