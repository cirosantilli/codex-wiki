We use complex representations. By Maschke's theorem, restriction to any finite subgroup is a semisimple module, so it has a canonical isotypic decomposition. In particular, the θ-isotypic component Wθ​ is the sum of all copies of the irreducible N-module with character θ. The central character idempotent
eθ​=∣N∣θ(1)​∑n∈N​θ(n−1)n
(15)
projects onto this component. These projections preserve every N-submodule, which consequently decomposes as the direct sum of its intersections with the isotypic components.
Let W afford ξ∈Irr(T∣θ). The component Wθ​ is nonzero. Since T=IG​(θ) fixes θ, it preserves Wθ​. Irreducibility of W as a T-module therefore gives W=Wθ​: its restriction to N consists entirely of copies of θ.
Form the induced representation
V=C[G]⊗C[T]​W=⨁g∈R​g⊗W,
(16)
where R represents the left cosets G/T. Because N◃G, for n∈N we have
n(g⊗w)=g⊗(g−1ng)w.
(17)
Thus g⊗W is N-isotypic of type θg, where θg(n)=θ(g−1ng). These types are distinct for distinct cosets gT, precisely by the definition of the inertia group of a character T.
Let Y be a nonzero G-submodule of V. Its isotypic decomposition as an N-module shows that it meets some g⊗W nontrivially. Acting by g−1 gives Y∩(1⊗W)=0. This intersection is a T-submodule of the irreducible W, hence equals 1⊗W. The G-translates of that component span V, so Y=V. The induced representation is therefore irreducible, and its restriction contains θ. Its induced character satisfies
ξG∈Irr(G∣θ).​
(18)
This proves the irreducibility assertion directly from isotypic components; no form of the correspondence being proved has been assumed.
