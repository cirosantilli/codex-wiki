We prove closure of N through character kernels, rather than assuming that a conjugacy-invariant set is a subgroup. Let η∈Irr(H)∖{1H​}, write d=η(1), and define the generalised character
Ψη​=(η−d1H​)G+d1G​.
(4)
Here 1H​,1G​ are the trivial characters. Since (η−d1H​)(1)=0, part (i) gives (Ψη​)H​=η and Ψη​(1)=d. Put ϑ=η−d1H​. By Frobenius reciprocity and character orthogonality,
⟨ϑG,ϑG⟩G​=⟨ϑ,(ϑG)H​⟩H​=⟨ϑ,ϑ⟩H​=1+d2,⟨ϑG,1G​⟩G​=−d.
(5)
Consequently
⟨Ψη​,Ψη​⟩G​=(1+d2)−2d2+d2=1.
(6)
To justify the resulting irreducibility, write any generalised character as an integer combination ∑χ​aχ​χ of irreducible characters. Its squared norm is ∑aχ2​, so norm one makes it χ or −χ for one irreducible χ. Positive degree rules out the negative sign. Thus Ψη​ is an irreducible character, an instance of the fact that positive-degree norm-one virtual characters are irreducible.
If x∈N∖{1}, no conjugate of x lies in H, so the induction formula gives ϑG(x)=0. At 1 it is also zero. Hence Ψη​(x)=d for all x∈N. For a finite-group representation we may choose an invariant positive Hermitian form, making every representing matrix unitary. Its trace equals its dimension only when all its unit-modulus eigenvalues are 1. Thus the kernel of a character is {g:Ψη​(g)=Ψη​(1)}, and N⊆kerΨη​.
Conversely, if g∈/N, then g is conjugate to some h∈H∖{1}. The character of the regular representation of H is
ρH​=∑η∈Irr(H)​η(1)η,ρH​(1)=∣H∣,ρH​(h)=0.
(7)
If every nontrivial η satisfied η(h)=η(1), the sum at h would instead equal ∑η(1)2=∣H∣, a contradiction. Choose an η for which these values differ. Since Ψη​ restricts to η, it has Ψη​(g)=η(h)=d, so g∈/kerΨη​. Therefore
N=η∈Irr(H)∖{1H​}⋂​kerΨη​◃G.​
(8)
This proves the Frobenius kernel theorem's subgroup assertion. The definition also gives N∩H={1}. Using the permitted cardinality ∣N∣=[G:H], normality ensures NH is a subgroup and
∣NH∣=∣N∩H∣∣N∣∣H∣​=∣G∣,NH=GandG=N⋊H.​
(9)
Thus N is the Frobenius kernel, and H supplies the complementary factor.
