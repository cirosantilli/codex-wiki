The preceding parts give a simple Specht module for each partition and show that different partitions give nonisomorphic simples. The conjugacy classes of Sn​ are indexed by partitions of n, namely cycle types. We use the standard finite-group character theorem: the number of irreducible complex characters equals the number of conjugacy classes, and they form an orthonormal basis of complex class functions. Consequently the χλ are the complete list of irreducible characters of Sn​.
By Maschke's theorem, the Young permutation module Mμ decomposes as a direct sum of these simple modules. For a semisimple complex module, Schur lemma makes the multiplicity of a simple module equal to the dimension of the homomorphism space from that simple; character orthogonality identifies the same number with the character inner product. Hence
Mμ≅⨁λ⊢n​(Sλ)⊕aλμ​,aλμ​=dimHomCSn​​(Sλ,Mμ)=⟨ξμ,χλ⟩∈Z≥0​.
(36)
Part (ii) makes aλμ​=0 unless λ⊵μ, and gives aμμ​=1. Taking characters proves
ξμ=λ⊵μ∑​⟨ξμ,χλ⟩χλ,⟨ξμ,χμ⟩=1.​
(37)
Thus the permutation-character decomposition is triangular in the dominance order on partitions, with diagonal multiplicity one. These are the dominance and diagonal-multiplicity consequences of Young's rule, obtained here without assuming its full multiplicity formula.
