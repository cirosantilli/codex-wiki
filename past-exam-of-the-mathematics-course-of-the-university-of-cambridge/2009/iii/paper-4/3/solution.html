For integer partitions λ,μ of n, padded by zero parts, the dominance order on partitions is
λ⊵μ⟺i=1∑r​λi​≥i=1∑r​μi​for every r≥1.​
(24)
A λ-tableau fills the Young diagram of λ with 1,…,n, each once. A tabloid [t] remembers the sets of entries in each row, ignoring their order within that row. The Young permutation module Mλ=CXλ​ has these tabloids as basis, with Sn​ acting by permuting the entries.
The row and column stabilizers Rt​,Ct​ independently permute entries within the rows and columns of t. The column antisymmetrizer is κt​=∑c∈Ct​​sgn(c)c, and et​=κt​[t] is the associated polytabloid. Since Cgt​=gCt​g−1 and conjugation preserves permutation sign,
κgt​=gκt​g−1,egt​=κgt​[gt]=gκt​[t],get​=egt​.​
(25)
Thus the Specht module is the submodule
Sλ=spanC​{et​:t is a λ-tableau}⊆Mλ.​
(26)
Every tableau is gt for a fixed t, so any single et​ generates it under Sn​. Also Ct​∩Rt​={1}, since a permutation preserving both rows and columns must fix every cell. The tabloids [ct] for c∈Ct​ are consequently distinct, and et​ has coefficient 1 at [t]; in particular et​=0.
We use the following precise column antisymmetrizer facts. If some row of a μ-tableau s contains two entries from one column of t, the transposition interchanging them pairs equal tabloids with opposite signs in κt​[s], so κt​[s]=0. Otherwise each row of s meets each column of t at most once. The first r rows of s therefore contain at most min(r,λj′​) entries from column j of t, whence
∑i=1r​μi​≤∑j​min(r,λj′​)=∑i=1r​λi​.
(27)
This states and explains dominance from a nonzero column antisymmetrizer: κt​[s]=0 implies λ⊵μ.
When μ=λ, the nonzero column antisymmetrizer criterion says more: either κt​[s]=0, or a column permutation takes [s] to [t], and κt​[s]=±et​. Thus κt​Mλ=Cet​. This follows by matching the row positions within each column when all intersections have size at most one; the equal row and column sizes force the Ferrers incidence pattern.
For the inner product, use the positive Hermitian extension of the orthonormal tabloid basis, linear in its first argument. It has the supplied self-adjointness of κt​. Its orthogonal complement of Sλ agrees with that obtained from the complex tabloid bilinear form, since the spanning polytabloids have real coefficients. Because the coefficient of [t] in et​ is 1, self-adjointness and the one-dimensional image give the useful identity
κt​u=⟨u,et​⟩et​(u∈Mλ).​
(28)
Indeed, the scalar multiplying et​ is the [t]-coefficient of κt​u, which is ⟨κt​u,[t]⟩=⟨u,κt​[t]⟩=⟨u,et​⟩.
