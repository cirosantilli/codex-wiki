A Non-Abelian gauge theory describes fields with a local redundancy, not an independent physical degree of freedom for each component of its gauge field. Take a compact gauge group with Hermitian generators Ta and Lie algebra structure constants defined by [Ta,Tb]=ifabcTc. In a mostly-plus metric signature, the pure Yang-Mills theory has
LYM​=−41​Fμνa​Faμν,Fμνa​=∂μ​Aνa​−∂ν​Aμa​+gfabcAμb​Aνc​.
(36)
With the matter gauge covariant derivative Dμ​=∂μ​−igAμ​, a finite gauge transformation acts as Aμ​↦UAμ​U−1−(i/g)(∂μ​U)U−1. Expanding U=eigωaTa gives
δAμa​=(Dμ​ω)a=∂μ​ωa+gfabcAμb​ωc.
(37)
The Yang-Mills action is constant along each gauge orbit. Consequently a naive functional integral ∫DAeiS[A] repeatedly integrates the same physical configuration and contains the infinite formal volume of the local gauge group. At quadratic order the same problem appears as noninvertibility: the free kinetic operator annihilates Aμ​=∂μ​ω, so no unique gauge-boson propagator exists. In canonical language the Gauss law constraint in gauge theory and the absence of an independent momentum for the time component show that the field components cannot all be independent oscillators. In covariant quantization, time-like and longitudinal polarizations must not be counted as additional physical states.
The Faddeev-Popov determinant implements a local quotient by the gauge orbit. Choose Ga[A]=∂μAμa​ and first impose Ga[A]=fa(x) for a specified function f. This is the Lorenz gauge condition when f=0. Its infinitesimal change is
δGa=∂μ(Dμ​ω)a,(Dμ​)ab=δab∂μ​+gfacbAμc​.
(38)
The direct Faddeev-Popov operator is therefore M0ab​=∂μ(Dμ​)ab. Locally, if the condition cuts each orbit once and residual zero modes have been removed by boundary conditions, changing variables from ω to G[Aω]−f proves the Faddeev-Popov gauge-orbit identity
1=ΔFP​[A]∫Dωδ(G[Aω]−f),ΔFP​[A]=detM0​[A].
(39)
The determinant is the functional Jacobian of this change of variables. A finite-dimensional version is ∫dωδ(G(ω)−f)=1/∣det(∂G/∂ω)∣ near a unique root; the perturbative determinant has a fixed sign or phase, absorbed into normalization. This is the same change-of-variables mechanism used in Bastianelli's derivation of the Faddeev–Popov construction.
Insert the identity into the normalized functional integral for a gauge-invariant observable O[A]:
Z⟨O⟩=VolG1​∫DAO[A]eiS[A]ΔFP​[A]∫Dωδ(G[Aω]−f).
(40)
For each fixed ω, change the integration variable from A to Aω. The action, observable and measure are invariant, and the determinant is the orbit Jacobian at the resulting gauge slice. The integrand is now independent of the separately integrated orbit parameter. Its integral is VolG, canceling the denominator. Thus, up to normalization,
Z⟨O⟩=∫DAΔFP​[A]δ(G[A]−f)O[A]eiS[A].
(41)
This assumes an anomaly-free measure and a local perturbative gauge slice; it is not an assertion of a globally unique representative on every gauge orbit.
Average over f with weight exp[−i∫d4xfafa/(2ξ)]. The delta functional sets f=G[A], so the average produces the covariant gauge term
Lgf​=−2ξ1​(∂μAμa​)2.
(42)
Next represent the Faddeev-Popov determinant by a Grassmann Gaussian integral. Choose M=−M0​=−∂μDμ​; changing M0​ to −M0​ changes the regulated determinant by a field-independent factor that is absorbed in normalization. For a Faddeev-Popov ghost field c and independent antighost field cˉ,
detM ∝ ∫DcˉDcexp(i∫d4xcˉaMabcb).
(43)
To see why it is a determinant rather than its reciprocal, diagonalize a finite-dimensional quadratic form formally: for each Grassmann pair only the term proportional to cˉj​cj​ survives integration, supplying one eigenvalue. Multiplying the surviving eigenvalues gives detM, with an overall factor of i per pair. The functional version is defined with the same regulator as the rest of the theory.
Combining these steps gives the gauge-fixed Yang–Mills Lagrangian
Ltot​=−41​Fμνa​Faμν−2ξ1​(∂μAμa​)2−cˉa∂μ(Dμ​c)a.​
(44)
This is the gauge-fixed Yang-Mills Lagrangian in a covariant gauge. Integrating its ghost term by parts gives
Lgh​=(∂μcˉa)(∂μ​ca)+gfabc(∂μcˉa)Aμb​cc.
(45)
The second term is the ghost-gluon vertex in the antighost convention chosen here. The Faddeev-Popov ghosts are anticommuting Lorentz scalars introduced to represent a determinant; they are not extra asymptotic particles. A closed ghost loop has a minus sign from their Grassmann parity. In a Non-Abelian gauge theory the determinant depends on A, so these loops are essential. In an Abelian gauge theory Dμ​ in the adjoint sector reduces to ∂μ​, the determinant is field independent, and its ghost sector decouples.
The quadratic gauge field kernel at non-null momentum is
Kabμν​(k)=δab​[−k2ημν+(1−ξ−1)kμkν].
(46)
Define the mixed-index longitudinal projector of a vector field PL​μν​=kμkν​/k2 and transverse projector of a vector field PT​=1−PL​. The kernel is −k2(PT​+ξ−1PL​) and hence has inverse −k−2(PT​+ξPL​). Multiplying by i gives the gauge-boson propagator, off its poles,
Dμνab​(k)=−k2iδab​[ημν​−(1−ξ)k2kμ​kν​​].​
(47)
The vacuum Feynman i-epsilon prescription completes this off-pole formula; with the mostly-plus convention its physical transverse denominator is k2−i0. The choice ξ=1 is Feynman gauge; ξ→0 is covariant Landau gauge. The invertible quadratic kernel supplies perturbation theory, while the ghost determinant ensures the gauge-orbit measure has been accounted for.
The consistency of the unphysical sector is organized by BRST symmetry. Introduce a bosonic Nakanishi-Lautrup field Ba and a left-acting BRST differential
sAμa​=(Dμ​c)a,sca=−2g​fabccbcc,scˉa=Ba,sBa=0.
(48)
The graded Leibniz rule and the Jacobi identity give s2=0: for example s2A=D(sc)+g[Dc,c]=0 since D[c,c]=2[Dc,c], and s2c vanishes by the graded Jacobi identity. Nilpotence on cˉ and B is immediate. Using the odd antighost field, the same graded Leibniz rule gives
s[cˉa(Ga+2ξ​Ba)]=BaGa+2ξ​BaBa−cˉa∂μ(Dμ​c)a.
(49)
Eliminating Ba by its algebraic field equation Ba=−Ga/ξ recovers the displayed gauge-fixing and ghost terms. Thus they are BRST-exact together, and the total action preserves BRST symmetry. At the level of states, the BRST charge Q implements physical states as the ghost-number-zero BRST cohomology kerQ/imQ. This prescription removes longitudinal, time-like and ghost excitations from physical amplitudes while allowing them on internal lines. For a BRST-closed observable, changing ξ inserts s(cˉaBa/2); its expectation vanishes by the BRST change-of-variables identity when the measure preserves the symmetry. This explains the independence of physical quantities from the arbitrary gauge parameter.
Finally, a local condition such as Lorenz gauge can have several solutions on the same gauge orbit, the Gribov ambiguity. The preceding Faddeev-Popov gauge-orbit identity and perturbative gauge fixing construction apply in a neighborhood where the linearized operator is invertible after residual modes are excluded. They resolve perturbative gauge overcounting and supply consistent covariant Feynman rules; they do not by themselves prove a globally unique nonperturbative gauge fixing.
