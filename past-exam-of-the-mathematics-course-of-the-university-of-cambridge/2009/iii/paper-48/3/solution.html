Let ϕB​=Zϕ1/2​ϕ define the renormalized field in Phi-fourth theory. For an n-point correlation function, with no composite operator insertions, this implies
Gn,B​=Zϕn/2​Gn​.
(21)
The bare correlation function is independent of the arbitrary renormalization scale μ when the bare parameters and external momenta are fixed. Define
β​λ​=μdμdλ​​B​,γm2​=μdμdlogm2​​B​,γϕ​=21​μdμdlogZϕ​​​B​.
(22)
The definitions of the running mass and wave-function renormalization functions specify their signs and the factor of two. Differentiating Zϕn/2​Gn​ using the chain rule yields the Callan–Symanzik equation
[μ∂μ​+β​λ​∂λ​+m2γm2​∂m2​+nγϕ​]Gn​=0.​
(23)
Here β​λ​ denotes the renormalization-group beta function itself, not the function multiplied by an additional λ. The Callan-Symanzik equation concerns dependence on the arbitrary reference scale, with the displayed external momenta kept fixed.
For the minimal subtraction scheme in dimensional regularization, write
F(λ,ϵ)=λ+∑k≥1​ϵkfk​(λ)​,H(λ,ϵ)=1+∑k≥1​ϵkbk​(λ)​,
(24)
so λB​=μϵF and mB2​=m2H. The μϵ factor gives the engineering contribution −ϵλ to the renormalization-group beta function. Put β​λ​=−ϵλ+βλ​. Scale independence of the bare coupling then gives
0=ϵF+β​λ​∂λ​F=ϵ(F−λF′)+βλ​F′.
(25)
The coefficient of ϵ0 is f1​−λf1′​+βλ​. Its vanishing proves the first of the simple-pole formulas for minimal-subtraction renormalization group functions:
βλ​=β​λ​+ϵλ=(λ∂λ​−1)f1​(λ).​
(26)
The coefficients of ϵ−k, k≥1, also vanish and impose (λ∂λ​−1)fk+1​=βλ​fk′​. These relations explain how the higher poles are consistent with a finite renormalization-group beta function.
For the mass, differentiate the second bare-parameter relation:
0=γm2​H+β​λ​H′.
(27)
Its finite coefficient is γm2​−λb1′​. Therefore
γm2​=λ∂λ​b1​(λ).​
(28)
The higher poles impose λbk+1′​=γm2​bk​+βλ​bk′​. The formulas for the running mass and renormalization-group beta function thus follow directly from bare-parameter independence rather than from an identification with diagram coefficients by name.
At the specified loop order, f1​=3λ2/(16π2) and b1​=λ/(16π2). There is no wave-function renormalization at this order. Hence
βλ​=16π23λ2​+O(λ3),γm2​=16π2λ​+O(λ2),γϕ​=0+O(λ2).​
(29)
The regulated renormalization-group beta function is β​λ​=−ϵλ+3λ2/(16π2)+O(λ3); after removing the regulator, β​λ​=βλ​.
Now take m2=0 and the four-dimensional limit. This massless surface is preserved since μdm2/dμ=m2γm2​. Set b=3/(16π2), choose a reference scale μ0​, and define the running coupling by
dlogμdλ(μ)​=bλ(μ)2,λ(μ0​)=λ0​.
(30)
Differentiating 1/λ gives d(1/λ)/dlogμ=−b, so
λ(μ)=1−bλ0​log(μ/μ0​)λ0​​.​
(31)
The zero-coupling solution is obtained by continuity. At one-loop accuracy γϕ​=0, and the chain rule turns the massless Callan-Symanzik equation into
dlogμd​Gn​(p1​,…,pn​;0,λ(μ),μ)=0.
(32)
Thus the one-loop solution along a running-coupling trajectory is
Gn​(p1​,…,pn​;0,λ(μ),μ)=Gn​(p1​,…,pn​;0,λ0​,μ0​).​
(33)
For a prescribed endpoint coupling λ, substitute λ0​=λ/[1+bλlog(μ/μ0​)] on the right. The reference correlation function supplies arbitrary boundary data; the Callan-Symanzik equation determines their scale evolution, not their full momentum dependence. The running coupling formula is used on a branch on which its denominator is nonzero and the retained perturbative approximation is appropriate.
If one keeps ϵ=0, the same characteristic calculation uses dλ/dlogμ=−ϵλ+bλ2. Writing r=μ/μ0​ gives λ(μ)=λ0​r−ϵ/[1−(bλ0​/ϵ)(1−r−ϵ)], which tends to the displayed four-dimensional result as ϵ→0.
With a nonzero anomalous dimension, the characteristic equation instead reads dGn​/dlogμ=−nγϕ​(λ(μ))Gn​. Integration proves the characteristic solution of the massless Callan-Symanzik equation:
Gn​(p;0,λ(μ),μ)=exp[−n∫μ0​μ​γϕ​(λ(ν))νdν​]Gn​(p;0,λ0​,μ0​).
(34)
For the stipulated γϕ​=cλ2 and the same one-loop renormalization-group beta function, change variables using dlogν=dλ/(bλ2). The exponent's integral becomes (c/b)(λ(μ)−λ0​). Therefore the modified explicit solution is
Gn​(p;0,λ(μ),μ)=exp[−bnc​(λ(μ)−λ0​)]Gn​(p;0,λ0​,μ0​).​
(35)
Equivalently its multiplicative factor is exp[−ncλ02​logr/(1−bλ0​logr)]. This solves the equation with the stated truncated renormalization-group beta function and anomalous dimension; it does not introduce additional higher-order coefficients that were not specified.
