The superficial degree of divergence is the power of a common large-momentum scale obtained by scaling all independent internal momenta together, before cancellations or subtraction of divergent subdiagrams. In a scalar field theory with a standard quadratic kinetic term and non-derivative interactions, each loop momentum integral supplies d powers of momentum and each internal propagator supplies minus two. Thus
D=dL−2I.
(9)
For a connected Feynman diagram, counting vertex half-edges and applying the stated identity for the loop order gives
2I+E=∑n​nVn​,I=L+V−1,V=∑n​Vn​.
(10)
It follows that D=(d−2)L−2V+2. Meanwhile, ∑n​(n−4)Vn​=2I+E−4V=2L−2V+E−2. Substitution proves
D=(d−4)L+n∑​(n−4)Vn​−E+4.​
(11)
This power counting in quantum field theory concerns the overall scaling: even a Feynman diagram with D<0 can contain divergent subdiagrams, and cancellations may improve convergence when D≥0.
The canonical mass dimensions are [ϕ]=(d−2)/2 and [gn​]=d−n(d−2)/2. A finite polynomial scalar field theory has power-counting renormalizability when its interactions have nonnegative mass dimensions and the allowed local counterterms form a finite set closed under subtraction of divergent subdiagrams. For d>2, non-derivative interactions therefore satisfy n≤2d/(d−2). Equivalently,
D=d−2d−2​E−∑n​[gn​]Vn​,
(12)
so adding vertices of such interactions cannot force an indefinitely increasing number of external legs or derivatives in divergent counterterms. In dimensions d≤2 a fixed finite polynomial still has this favorable power counting in quantum field theory; the displayed upper bound on n is intended only for d>2.
For six-dimensional cubic scalar field theory, 3V=2I+E and L=I−V+1 yield
D=6−2E.​
(13)
The coupling g has zero mass dimension. The possible overall divergences have E≤3: vacuum energy, a linear tadpole diagram term, a mass term, a kinetic term, and a cubic interaction. These are finitely many local counterterms. Divergent subdiagrams of higher-point Feynman diagrams are removed by the same counterterms, establishing perturbative renormalizability of cubic scalar theory in six dimensions. A real cubic potential is unbounded below, so this claim is one of perturbative quantum field theory, not a claim that the polynomial defines a stable nonperturbative vacuum.
