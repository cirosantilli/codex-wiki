Let Ra​=ad(Ta​), where ad(X)Y=[X,Y]. In the given basis, the Adjoint representation of a Lie algebra is
(Ra​)cb​=fcab​.
(13)
The Jacobi identity gives [adX,adY]=ad[X,Y], so [Ra​,Rb​]=fcab​Rc​, as required for a Lie algebra representation. The Killing form is the symmetric Trace form of a Lie algebra representation
κab​=tr(Ra​Rb​)=fcad​fdbc​.​
(14)
Cyclicity of the trace proves its invariance directly:
κ([Ta​,Tb​],Tc​)+κ(Tb​,[Ta​,Tc​])​=tr([Ra​,Rb​]Rc​+Rb​[Ra​,Rc​])=tr(Ra​Rb​Rc​−Rb​Rc​Ra​)=0.​
(15)
In components this is precisely κdc​fdab​+κbd​fdac​=0​, so the Killing form is an invariant bilinear form on a Lie algebra.
Choose the infinitesimal gauge transformation convention δϕ=ωϕ, with ω(x)=ωa(x)ta​, and write Aμ​=Aμa​ta​. A gauge covariant derivative must satisfy Dμ′​=UDμ​U−1 for U=1+ω+O(ω2); hence
Aμ′​=UAμ​U−1−(∂μ​U)U−1,δAμ​=−∂μ​ω+[ω,Aμ​].​
(16)
Equivalently δAμa​=−∂μ​ωa+fabc​ωbAμc​. Expanding δ(Dμ​ϕ) makes the cancellation transparent:
δ(Dμ​ϕ)​=(∂μ​ω)ϕ+ω∂μ​ϕ+(−∂μ​ω+[ω,Aμ​])ϕ+Aμ​ωϕ=ω(∂μ​ϕ+Aμ​ϕ).​
(17)
Thus δ(Dμ​ϕ)=ωata​Dμ​ϕ​: it transforms in the same representation as ϕ. In adjoint covariant derivative notation, δAμ​=−Dμad​ω.
The commutator of two gauge covariant derivatives defines the gauge field strength:
[Dμ​,Dν​]ϕ=Fμνa​ta​ϕ,Fμν​=∂μ​Aν​−∂ν​Aμ​+[Aμ​,Aν​].
(18)
Thus
Fμνa​=∂μ​Aνa​−∂ν​Aμa​+fabc​Aμb​Aνc​.​
(19)
Conjugating the derivative commutator gives Fμν′​=UFμν​U−1, so the gauge field strength transforms homogeneously in the Adjoint representation:
δFμν​=[ω,Fμν​],δFμνa​=fabc​ωbFμνc​.​
(20)
Even if the matrices ta​ in a particular matter representation are not faithful, the displayed component formula defines the Lie-algebra-valued curvature independently of that representation.
Only g(ab)​ contributes to the Yang-Mills theory Lagrangian, since Fμνa​Fbμν is symmetric in a,b. Thus take gab​ to be a constant real symmetric bilinear form. Its infinitesimal variation is
δL=−41​ωa(gdc​fdab​+gbd​fdac​)Fμνb​Fcμν.
(21)
Arbitrary local field strengths and gauge parameters give the necessary and sufficient invariant gauge kinetic form condition
gdc​fdab​+gbd​fdac​=0,gab​=gba​.​
(22)
An antisymmetric part is unconstrained but contributes nothing. A nondegenerate kinetic term additionally needs a nondegenerate bilinear form; positive energy additionally needs a positive internal metric in the signature (+−−−) convention. Neither extra condition follows solely from gauge invariance. For a compact simple real Lie algebra, −κ is positive definite and is the standard choice up to a positive factor.
For the intended complex-simple setting, the Killing form is nondegenerate. Consequently write any invariant bilinear form on a Lie algebra uniquely as g(X,Y)=κ(SX,Y). Invariance of both forms implies
κ((SadZ−adZS)X,Y)=0
(23)
for every X,Y,Z, so S commutes with the Adjoint representation. A subspace invariant under all adjoint maps is an ideal, hence simplicity makes the adjoint representation irreducible. By Schur lemma, S=cI, giving the uniqueness of an invariant bilinear form on a simple Lie algebra:
gab​=cκab​.​
(24)
The same conclusion holds for compact simple real algebras by complexification, with c real; nondegeneracy excludes c=0, and positive energy selects c<0 in the stated convention.
There is a genuine real-simple exception to uniqueness of the Killing form if “simple” is read as an arbitrary real simple algebra. Regard sl2​(C) as real. It is real simple: its complexification consists of two complex simple factors exchanged by conjugation, so a conjugation-stable ideal is zero or the whole algebra. Both ReκC​ and ImκC​ are real symmetric invariant forms, while κR​=2ReκC​. With H=diag(1,−1), the Killing form of the special linear Lie algebra gives κC​(H,H)=8 and κC​(H,iH)=8i. Therefore the imaginary part cannot be a real multiple of κR​. The uniqueness conclusion requires the customary complex-simple or compact-real interpretation.
