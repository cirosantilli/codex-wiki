Use target signature (−,+,…,+) and worldsheet signature (−,+). The Polyakov action has local worldsheet diffeomorphism and Weyl invariance, together with global target-space Poincaré symmetry. Under a worldsheet coordinate change σ↦σ′, the embedding coordinates are scalars and the worldsheet metric is a tensor:
X′μ(σ′)=Xμ(σ),gab′​(σ′)=∂σ′a∂σc​∂σ′b∂σd​gcd​(σ).
(1)
A Weyl transformation acts as gab​↦e2ρ(σ)gab​ and leaves X unchanged. In two dimensions −g​ scales by e2ρ and gab by e−2ρ, so the action is invariant. Target translations act by Xμ↦Xμ+aμ, and Lorentz transformations by Xμ↦Λμν​Xν with ΛTηΛ=η; they leave g unchanged. Infinitesimally the latter have δXμ=ωμν​Xν, where ωμν​=−ωνμ​.
Locally, the two diffeomorphism functions put the metric into the form gab​=e2ρηab​, and Weyl invariance removes the conformal factor. In this conformal gauge, the embedding coordinates are free scalar fields and satisfy
(∂τ2​−∂σ2​)Xμ=0.​
(2)
The metric equation must still be imposed after gauge fixing. Varying the ungauged metric gives the worldsheet stress-energy tensor constraint
∂a​X⋅∂b​X−21​gab​gcd∂c​X⋅∂d​X=0.
(3)
With a dot and prime denoting the two coordinate derivatives, the independent Virasoro constraints are
X˙2+X′2=0,X˙⋅X′=0,​
(4)
equivalently (X˙±X′)2=0. Local conformal gauge leaves residual conformal transformations; on general worldsheet topologies it also leaves global moduli, rather than removing all metric information.
For target translations, differentiation of the Lagrangian with respect to ∂a​Xμ​ gives the Noether current
jaμ=−2πα′1​−g​gab∂b​Xμ.
(5)
The embedding equation is ∂a​jaμ=0. In conformal gauge, jτμ=X˙μ/(2πα′), so integration over the closed string gives
Pμ=2πα′1​∫02π​X˙μdσ.​
(6)
Periodic boundary conditions make its time derivative vanish. For an infinitesimal Lorentz transformation, the antisymmetric coefficient of ωμν​ gives the current ja,μν=Xμjaν−Xνjaμ. Its divergence vanishes because the remaining product of embedding derivatives is symmetric in μ,ν. Thus the Noether charge is
Jμν=2πα′1​∫02π​(XμX˙ν−XνX˙μ)dσ.​
(7)
These are the target-space Noether charges of a string.
For the circular solution take R>0, and let er​=(cosσ,sinσ) and eσ​=(−sinσ,cosσ). Its derivatives are
X˙=(R,−Rsinτer​),X′=(0,Rcosτeσ​).
(8)
Each spatial coordinate has equal second derivatives in τ and σ, while the time coordinate has both second derivatives zero. Furthermore
X˙2=−R2cos2τ,X′2=R2cos2τ,X˙⋅X′=0,
(9)
so both the field equations and the Virasoro constraints hold, including the collapse instants.
At target time t=X0=Rτ, the string is a circle in the X1X2 plane of radius R∣cos(t/R)∣. It contracts from radius R to zero and re-expands; the negative cosine reverses material labels by a half-turn rather than creating a negative radius. The spatial configuration repeats after target time πR. This is a pulsating circular string. Its induced metric degenerates at collapse, but the conformal-gauge embedding and constraints give its continuation.
The integrals of sinσ and cosσ vanish, so the momentum is
Pμ=(R/α′,0,0,…),M=R/α′.​
(10)
The spatial velocity is radial, giving zero angular momentum density in the plane; all boost-charge terms integrate to zero as well. Hence Jμν=0 for every pair of indices.
At maximum expansion, all string elements are instantaneously at rest, and the length is L0​=2πR. Thus the rest mass per unit length, equal to the string tension, is
L0​M​=2πα′1​.​
(11)
At other times the instantaneous circumference is L=2πR∣cosτ∣, and the elements have speed ∣sinτ∣. Their Lorentz factor is 1/∣cosτ∣, so the energy remains TL/1−v2​=2πTR. The rest-energy density is T; the total rest-frame energy divided by the changing circumference is not constant. This distinguishes the requested intrinsic mass density from the larger energy density of moving string elements.
