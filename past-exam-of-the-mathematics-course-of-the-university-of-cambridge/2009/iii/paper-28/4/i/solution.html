Let π∈K be a uniformizer. Because L/K is unramified, the same π is a uniformizer of L, and vL​∣K​=vK​. The conjugates of z∈L× all have the same valuation, so
vK​(NL/K​z)=nvL​(z).
(23)
This proves that every field norm has valuation divisible by n. Conversely NL/K​(πr)=πnr for every integer r, so it remains to prove surjectivity of unit norms in unramified extensions.
Write ∣kK​∣=Q, so ∣kL​∣=Qn. Reduction of the field norm on units is the finite-field norm
kL×​⟶kK×​,a⟼a1+Q+⋯+Qn−1.
(24)
The multiplicative group of a finite field is cyclic. The displayed power map on a group of order Qn−1 has image of order Q−1, and is therefore onto kK×​. Given a unit u∈OK×​, first choose b1​∈OL×​ with N(b1​)≡u(modπ).
We can correct the norm at each subsequent power of π. For r≥1 and a∈OL​, expanding the product over the cyclic Galois group gives
N(1+πra)≡1+πrTrkL​/kK​​(aˉ)(modπr+1).
(25)
All products involving at least two correction terms are divisible by π2r, hence by πr+1. The residue field trace is onto even if the residue characteristic divides n. Indeed its polynomial T(X)=X+XQ+⋯+XQn−1 is nonzero of degree Qn−1<Qn; it cannot vanish at every element of kL​. Its values lie in kK​, since raising T(a) to Q cyclically permutes the summands. Thus it is a nonzero kK​-linear map to the one-dimensional space kK​, and is surjective.
Suppose N(br​)≡u(modπr). Choose aˉr​ whose trace is the coefficient of πr in u/N(br​)−1, and put br+1​=br​(1+πrar​). Then N(br+1​)≡u(modπr+1), and br+1​≡br​(modπr). By completeness the units br​ converge to a unit b. The field norm is continuous, being a polynomial in coordinates of multiplication, so N(b)=u. Multiplying such a unit by a power of π proves
NL/K​(L×)={x∈K×:vK​(x)≡0(modn)}.​
(26)
The proof uses no local class field theory, and works in either characteristic.
