Fix a non-Archimedean local field K, write Q=∣kK​∣, and use the arithmetic Frobenius normalization throughout: a uniformizer maps to the automorphism inducing a↦aQ on the maximal unramified extension. The Local Artin map, or local reciprocity map, is the canonical continuous group homomorphism
ArtK​:K×⟶Gal(Kab/K),
(31)
where Kab is the maximal abelian extension inside a fixed algebraic closure with its separable part understood. Its finite projection to every finite abelian extension L/K is onto and has kernel exactly the norm subgroup of a local field extension:
K×/NL/K​(L×)∼​Gal(L/K).​
(32)
In particular, the quotient has order [L:K]. This is how local class field theory turns abelian extension problems into computations inside a multiplicative group.
One precise definition of the finite reciprocity map uses the cup-product construction of local reciprocity. For a finite Galois extension with group G, the local fundamental class uL/K​∈H2(G,L×) is specified by local Brauer invariant 1/[L:K]. Cup product gives an isomorphism
H−2(G,Z)=Gab ⌣uL/K​ ​H0(G,L×)=K×/NL/K​(L×).
(33)
The finite Local Artin map is its inverse, composed with the quotient map from K×. For abelian G the target is G itself. Taking the compatible inverse limit over finite abelian extensions defines ArtK​.
The essential inputs to this cohomological construction have concrete arithmetic content. The local Brauer invariant identifies Br(K) with Q/Z; restriction to L multiplies the invariant by [L:K], so its kernel is cyclic of that order and has the distinguished generator uL/K​. For every subgroup H⊆G, Hilbert theorem 90 gives H1(H,L×)=0, while the same invariant calculation over the fixed field gives H2(H,L×) cyclic of order ∣H∣. These are the hypotheses of the algebraic cup-product isomorphism in Tate cohomology of a finite group. It is obtained by dimension shifting in group cohomology with a projective resolution of the trivial module and the extension representing the fundamental class. Thus the norm-kernel statement comes from the degree-zero definition of Tate cohomology of a finite group, rather than from an arbitrary assignment of an automorphism to each element. In an unramified extension of degree d, the fundamental class is the cyclic algebra with arithmetic Frobenius generator and parameter a uniformizer; the isomorphism sends that Frobenius to the class of the uniformizer. This explains the chosen sign of reciprocity.
Several properties follow immediately from this construction. If L⊇E⊇K are finite abelian extensions, restricting ArtL/K​(a) to E gives ArtE/K​(a). The Local Artin map is therefore compatible with the finite projections defining its infinite target. For any finite separable extension E/K and a∈E×, its norm compatibility is
ArtK​(NE/K​a)=ArtE​(a)∣Kab​.
(34)
Here KabE/E is abelian, so the displayed restriction makes sense. An outline of the compatibility proof is to use the corestriction map in group cohomology: on multiplicative coefficients its degree-zero map is the norm. The cup-product projection formula, together with restriction and corestriction of the local fundamental class, gives exactly the commuting reciprocity diagram. The compatibility is an arithmetic instance of the functoriality of the cohomological definition.
The Local Artin map has dense image, because every finite abelian quotient receives a surjective map. Its kernel is trivial, and it extends to an isomorphism
K×≃Gal(Kab/K),​
(35)
where this profinite completion uses the open finite-index subgroups of the topological multiplicative group. The original map is generally not onto: on the unramified quotient it sends the integer valuation into Z, rather than producing every profinite integer. Its units map onto the abelian inertia group. For a finite abelian extension, the corresponding unit image is its inertia group, while the valuation accounts for its residue extension. Indeed vK​(Nz)=f(L/K)vL​(z), so the valuation quotient has order f; the full norm quotient has order ef, leaving unit image of order e. Since unit images act trivially on the residue field, they give precisely the inertia group. More finely, the image of the higher principal-unit group 1+mKr​ in a finite abelian extension is its rth upper ramification group, for each integer r≥1. The use of upper ramification numbering is essential for compatibility with quotients.
The existence theorem of local class field theory states that every open finite-index subgroup H⊆K× is NL/K​(L×) for a unique finite abelian extension L/K. Inclusions are reversed: larger extensions have smaller norm subgroups. In the established reciprocity isomorphism, the proof is simply to take the fixed field of the open subgroup corresponding to H. Conversely a finite extension gives an open kernel of a finite continuous projection. Uniqueness follows because that kernel specifies its fixed field. The substantive existence problem is therefore to prove that the reciprocity isomorphism realizes all open finite-index subgroups; it is not just this final fixed-field argument.
The Lubin–Tate description of local reciprocity provides an explicit route to that existence problem. Choose a uniformizer π and a Lubin–Tate series f(T)=πT+TQ, with its associated Lubin–Tate formal group F. Solving f(F(X,Y))=F(f(X),f(Y)) successively by total degree constructs the law with linear part X+Y; the analogous equations construct the endomorphisms [a] with linear coefficient a. The congruence f(T)≡TQ(modπ) supplies the divisibility needed at each step. These endomorphisms satisfy [a][b]=[ab] and make the torsion an OK​-module.
If fr​ denotes the rfold iterate, the primitive level-r Lubin–Tate torsion points are roots of
fr−1​(T)fr​(T)​=π+fr−1​(T)Q−1.
(36)
Reduction modulo π is TQr−1(Q−1), and the constant term is π, so this is an Eisenstein polynomial. Its roots therefore generate a totally ramified extension Kπ,r​ of degree (Q−1)Qr−1. A primitive point generates the entire torsion module, and all points are its images under the integral convergent endomorphisms [a]. The torsion field is therefore a Galois extension of K. The action map into (OK​/πr)× is injective, and the computed degree equals the order of this unit group, proving it is an isomorphism. Passing to the inverse limit gives Gal(Kπ​/K)≃OK×​ for the Lubin–Tate tower Kπ​.
The unramified tower and Kπ​ are linearly disjoint field extensions: a finite common subextension would be both unramified and totally ramified, hence trivial. The Lubin–Tate description of local reciprocity on their compositum is
ArtK​(πmu)∣Knr​=FrobKm​,ArtK​(πmu)(λ)=[u−1](λ)(λ∈F[πr]).
(37)
The inverse on the unit action goes with the arithmetic Frobenius convention; changing to geometric Frobenius inverts the whole reciprocity map. The reciprocity theorem identifies this explicit action with the finite cup-product maps, independently of the chosen uniformizer. Its proof compares the fundamental class with the norm relations between consecutive torsion fields. The construction makes the possible finite kernels concrete: on the compositum of the degree-d unramified field with Kπ,r​ the kernel is
πdZ(1+πrOK​).
(38)
These subgroups are cofinal among open finite-index subgroups. Indeed any such H contains 1+πrOK​ for sufficiently large r, and contains πd for some positive d, since the class of π in K×/H has finite order. Taking the appropriate fixed subgroup in this explicit finite compositum realizes H. This proves the open-subgroup part of existence once the norm compatibility of the explicit and cohomological constructions is established. Together with the finite reciprocity maps it also shows that Kab=KnrKπ​.
As a direct check, on a finite unramified extension the construction reduces to
ArtL/K​(a)=ϕL/KvK​(a)​.
(39)
The earlier elementary unit-norm proof shows its kernel is exactly πdZOK×​. At K=Qp​, the Lubin–Tate cyclotomic example takes [p](T)=(1+T)p−1 and F(X,Y)=X+Y+XY. Its torsion is ζ−1, so a unit u acts by ζ↦ζu−1. These examples exhibit both the valuation and unit parts of local reciprocity with consistent signs.
