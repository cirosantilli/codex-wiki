All the local fields here carry normalized discrete valuations. Write OF​ for the valuation ring, mF​ for its maximal ideal, and kF​ for its residue field. We use the basic degree identity [B:A]=e(B/A)f(B/A) for finite extensions of non-Archimedean local fields, with e the ramification index and f the residue degree.
First construct the maximal unramified subextension of a local field extension inside an arbitrary finite extension L/K. Choose a primitive element of a field extension zˉ for the finite residue field extension kL​/kK​. Let hˉ be its monic irreducible minimal polynomial of an algebraic element, of degree f, and lift its coefficients to obtain a monic h∈OK​[T] of the same degree. Finite finite fields are perfect fields, so hˉ′(zˉ)=0. By Hensel lemma applied in L, there is z∈OL​ reducing to zˉ with h(z)=0. For U=K(z) we have
f=[kL​:kK​]≤[kU​:kK​]≤[U:K]≤degh=f.
(7)
Every inequality is therefore an equality. In particular U/K is unramified, kU​=kL​, and h is the minimal polynomial of an algebraic element z.
If L/K is unramified, then [L:K]=f, so this construction gives U=L. Taking x=z supplies the required generator, and h′(x) is a unit. Conversely suppose L=K(x) with x integral and its monic minimal polynomial of an algebraic element g satisfying g′(x)∈/mL​. Its coefficients lie in OK​, since all conjugates of an integral element are integral and K is complete. In the constructed unramified extension U the element xˉ∈kU​=kL​ is a simple root of gˉ​. Hensel lemma in U produces a root x′∈U with residue xˉ. In L both x and x′ lift this same simple residue root, so Hensel uniqueness gives x=x′. Thus L⊆U⊆L, proving
L/K is unramified⟺L=K(x), x∈OL​, g′(x)∈OL×​.​
(8)
For lifting residue-field embeddings of unramified extensions, use the generator z and polynomial h just constructed when L/K is unramified. Given a kK​-field homomorphism ι:kL​→kM​, its value ι(zˉ) is a simple root of hˉ. Hensel lemma in M produces the unique root z′∈OM​ with that residue. The assignment z↦z′ defines a K-field homomorphism K[T]/(h)→M; it induces ι because zˉ generates kL​. Conversely any such field homomorphism preserves the unique extended valuation, hence induces a residue field homomorphism, and its image of z is uniquely forced by Hensel uniqueness. This proves the asserted bijection. The same proof only requires that M be a non-Archimedean local field containing K with its compatible valuation; its finiteness over K is not essential.
Now set M=L. The finite residue field extension has exactly f=[L:K] automorphisms, cyclically generated by a↦a∣kK​∣. Their unique lifts give [L:K] automorphisms of L. Consequently L/K is a Galois extension, and reduction identifies its Galois group with that cyclic residue Galois group. Its generator is the arithmetic Frobenius:
Gal(L/K)≃Gal(kL​/kK​)≃Z/fZ.​
(9)
For the compositum, choose a uniformizer πM​ of the totally ramified extension M/K. The Eisenstein generator of a totally ramified extension characterization, proved in the next solution, says that M=K(πM​) and its minimal polynomial of an algebraic element is Eisenstein over OK​. A uniformizer of K remains a uniformizer in the unramified extension L. All its Eisenstein coefficient conditions therefore remain true over OL​. Thus the same Eisenstein polynomial is irreducible over L and generates LM=L(πM​) as a totally ramified extension. This proves LM/L is totally ramified, of degree [M:K].
Here is an explicit example showing that the converse decomposition of an arbitrary extension can fail. Take K=Q3​ and L=K(i) with i2=−1. Since T2+1 is irreducible and separable modulo three, the degree and residue-degree argument above shows that L/K is unramified quadratic. Put
α2=3(1+i),N=L(α).
(10)
The element 1+i is a unit in OL​, so T2−3(1+i) is Eisenstein over L. Hence [N:L]=2, e(N/K)=2, and f(N/K)=2. Moreover i=α2/3−1 gives N=K(α), with
α4−6α2+18=0.
(11)
We verify its Galois group, since that is what rules out a totally ramified complement. By Hensel lemma there is r∈K with r2=−2: the residue root 1 of T2+2 is simple modulo three. Set β=(1+i)/r. Then
β2=−i,ββˉ​=−1.
(12)
The assignments σ(i)=−i and σ(α)=βα respect the defining relation, because (βα)2=−3i(1+i)=3(1−i). They therefore define a K-automorphism of N. Its square satisfies σ2(i)=i and σ2(α)=βˉ​βα=−α, so σ has order four. There are thus four automorphisms of the degree-four extension, and
Gal(N/K)=⟨σ⟩≃C4​.
(13)
This is the cyclic quartic local extension without a totally ramified complement. Its unique quadratic subfield is L, fixed by σ2, and is unramified. If N=L′M′ with L′/K unramified and M′/K totally ramified, then [L′:K]≤f(N/K)=2 and [M′:K]≤e(N/K)=2. To obtain the full degree four both would have degree two, forcing M′ to be a totally ramified quadratic subfield. There is no such subfield. Thus N/Q3​ is the required counterexample.
