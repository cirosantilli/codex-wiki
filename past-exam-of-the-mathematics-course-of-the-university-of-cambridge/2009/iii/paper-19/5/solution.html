Let g be the real Riemannian metric underlying the Hermitian metric, and let J be the complex structure. The fundamental form of a Hermitian manifold is
ω(u,v)=g(Ju,v).
(27)
The identity g(Ju,Jv)=g(u,v) implies g(Ju,v)=−g(u,Jv), so this is an alternating real two-form. Moreover ω(Ju,Jv)=ω(u,v). A two-form with this invariance has type (1,1): on two vectors of type (1,0), applying J to both multiplies its value by i2=−1, forcing that value to vanish; the same argument applies to type (0,1). Hence ω is a real (1,1)-form.
Use the complex-linear convention for the complex Hodge star operator, extending the real star. The complex orientation and metric define dV=ωn/n!, and
α∧∗βˉ​=⟨α,β⟩g​dV,∗:Ap,q⟶An−q,n−p,∗2=(−1)r(2n−r) on total degree r.
(28)
Here the Hermitian inner product is linear in the first argument. With this convention, on the even-dimensional underlying real manifold the formal adjoints are d∗=−∗d∗, ∂ˉ∗=−∗∂∗ and ∂∗=−∗∂ˉ∗. A conjugate-linear star is another convention, but then the adjoint formulas must change accordingly.
The Hodge Laplacian and Dolbeault Laplacian are
Δd​=dd∗+d∗d,Δ∂ˉ​=∂ˉ∂ˉ∗+∂ˉ∗∂ˉ.
(29)
Also write Δ∂​=∂∂∗+∂∗∂. They are nonnegative, since for example
(Δ∂ˉ​α,α)L2​=∥∂ˉα∥L22​+∥∂ˉ∗α∥L22​.
(30)
The requested Dolbeault Hodge decomposition on a compact Hermitian manifold is a compactness statement: on a compact Hermitian manifold without boundary,
Ap,q=H∂ˉp,q​⊕⊥∂ˉAp,q−1⊕⊥∂ˉ∗Ap,q+1,H∂ˉp,q​=kerΔ∂ˉ​.
(31)
The harmonic space is finite-dimensional, and taking the harmonic representative induces an isomorphism H∂ˉp,q​≅H∂ˉp,q​(X). Compactness is essential to this formulation; it is not asserted on an arbitrary noncompact Hermitian manifold.
Now suppose X is compact and Kähler, so dω=0. The permitted Kähler Laplacian identity is Δd​=2Δ∂ˉ​=2Δ∂​, making the three harmonic spaces identical in each bidegree. A d-closed pure-type form has ∂α=∂ˉα=0, since these two terms have different types. We will construct a Green operator potential for ddbar exactness, rather than invoking the ddbar lemma as the conclusion to be proved.
Let G be the inverse of Δ∂ˉ​ on the orthogonal complement of its harmonic forms, zero on the harmonic space. It is twice the Green operator of the Hodge Laplacian. The Hodge decomposition theorem and elliptic inversion give Δ∂ˉ​G=I−H, with H harmonic projection. It preserves bidegrees. The operators ∂ and ∂ˉ commute with Δ∂ˉ​: the first follows from Δ∂ˉ​=Δ∂​ and ∂2=0, and the second directly from ∂ˉ2=0. They and their formal adjoints annihilate harmonic forms, so they preserve the orthogonal complement of the harmonic space. Consequently they commute with G, by uniqueness of the inverse on the harmonic complement. Thus u=Gα and v=G2α are killed by both differential operators.
The assumed orthogonality makes Hα=Hu=0. Applying the two Laplacians gives
α=Δ∂ˉ​u=∂ˉ∂ˉ∗u,u=Δ∂​v=∂∂∗v.
(32)
We also need the mixed anticommutator, which follows from the permitted Laplacian identity rather than an extra unproved lemma. Expand Δd​ using d=∂+∂ˉ and d∗=∂∗+∂ˉ∗. Since Δd​=Δ∂​+Δ∂ˉ​, its mixed terms sum to zero. Their two bidegrees (1,−1) and (−1,1) are distinct, so each vanishes separately, in particular ∂ˉ∗∂+∂∂ˉ∗=0. Hence
α=∂ˉ∂ˉ∗∂∂∗v=−∂ˉ∂∂ˉ∗∂∗v=∂∂ˉ(∂ˉ∗∂∗v).
(33)
We have obtained the explicit answer
α=∂∂ˉβ,β=∂ˉ∗∂∗G2α∈Ap−1,q−1.​
(34)
The positive assumptions on p,q ensure these degrees are available. Conversely such a form is orthogonal to harmonic forms by integration by parts, so the same proof also explains the harmonic orthogonality criterion for ddbar exactness.
For injectivity of the Lefschetz operator on a Hermitian manifold on real one-forms, work at one point in a metric-orthonormal coframe (e1,f1,…,en,fn) with ω=∑j​ej∧fj. If a=0, a unitary change of coframe makes a=ce1, c=0. Then
a∧ω=c∑j=2n​e1∧ej∧fj=0(n>1),
(35)
since the displayed basis three-forms are linearly independent. Therefore L(a)=a∧ω has zero kernel. Injectivity on complex one-forms follows by taking real and imaginary parts, since ω is real. This pointwise proof of injectivity of powers of the Lefschetz operator for degree one does not require the hard Lefschetz theorem.
Closedness of ω gives ∂ˉL=L∂ˉ. Using the permitted Kähler identities, specifically [∂ˉ∗,L]=i∂, gives
[Δ∂ˉ​,L]=∂ˉ[∂ˉ∗,L]+[∂ˉ∗,L]∂ˉ=i(∂ˉ∂+∂∂ˉ)=0.
(36)
Multiplying by two proves [Δd​,L]=0. In particular the Lefschetz operator preserves harmonic forms and the injective map on one-forms restricts to an injection Hd1​↪Hd3​. The de Rham Hodge theorem identifies these real harmonic spaces with the corresponding real cohomology groups. Taking dimensions yields
b3(X)≥b1(X).​
(37)
