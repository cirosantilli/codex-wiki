In the classical risk model, let N(t) be the Poisson process of claim arrivals, independent of the independent claim sizes. The reserve starts at u and earns premiums continuously at rate c, so

Here ψ is the ultimate ruin probability and ϕ=1−ψ is the survival probability. The relative safety loading is θ=(c−λμ)/(λμ)>0.
To derive the survival integro-differential equation, condition on the first claim time T, which has the exponential distribution of rate λ. Immediately before that claim the available capital is u+cT. A claim x≤u+cT leaves survival probability ϕ(u+cT−x), since the future claim process restarts with the same independent law; a larger claim causes ruin immediately. The first-claim decomposition for survival probability is therefore

Let the Bühlmann–Straub model structural parameters be
m0​=E[μ(Θ)],a=Var(μ(Θ)),v=E[σ2(Θ)],W=∑j=1n​mj​.

Here a is the variance of hypothetical means and v is the expected process variance. Assume the structural second moments are finite and the exposures mj​ are positive, as required by this least-squares problem. Write A=∑j​aj​. Conditional independence separates the squared prediction error into squared conditional bias and conditional variance. The law of total expectation gives
E[(μ(Θ)−a0​−∑j​aj​Xj​)2]=a(1−A)2+((1−A)m0​−a0​)2+v∑j​mj​aj2​​.
(39)
Indeed, conditional on Θ, the prediction has mean a0​+Aμ(Θ) and variance σ2(Θ)∑j​aj2​/mj​; there are no cross-covariances because the annual observations are conditionally independent.
For fixed slope coefficients the optimal intercept is a0​=(1−A)m0​. For fixed sum A, the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality gives
A2=(∑j​mj​​aj​​mj​​)2≤W∑j​mj​aj2​​,
(40)
with equality when aj​=Amj​/W. Thus the remaining minimization is the scalar quadratic

(41)
Differentiation gives A=Z=aW/(aW+v). In the nondegenerate case a,v>0, the quadratic is strictly convex and the equality condition determines the unique slope coefficients. The Bühlmann–Straub credibility factor and premium are therefore
Z=aW+vaW​,aj​=aW+vamj​​,a0​=(1−Z)m0​,​

(43)
This derives the Bühlmann–Straub credibility estimate from its minimization criterion. Increasing exposure increases the weight on experience; increasing process noise decreases it. No exposure for year n+1 appears because the estimate concerns the expected claim per car, not the variance of next year's observed average.
The degenerate cases have a direct interpretation. If a=0 and v>0, the unknown conditional mean is the known constant m0​, so take Z=0. If v=0 and a>0, each observation already equals μ(Θ) almost surely, so Z=1 gives exact prediction. If both vanish, all observations and the target equal m0​ almost surely; use the constant predictor rather than the undefined ratio 0/0.
In the approximate normal model, μ(Θ)=Θ, m0​=μ, Var(Θ)=a, and σ2(Θ)=v. Substitution into the derived credibility estimate gives
μ​cred​=aW+va∑j​mj​Xj​+vμ​.​

Θ∣X1​,…,Xn​∼N(m∗​,V∗​),V∗​=(1/a+W/v)−1=v+aWav​,

For any decision d, the posterior quadratic loss is
E[(Θ−d)2∣X]=V∗​+(m∗​−d)2.
(48)
Its unique minimizer is the posterior mean d=m∗​. Hence the Bayesian and credibility estimates coincide exactly in this normal model:
μ​Bayes​=m∗​=μ​cred​.​

The reason is that the posterior mean is already affine in the observations, so restricting prediction to affine functions loses nothing. In the general Bühlmann–Straub model, the posterior mean need not be affine; the credibility estimate is then only the best affine squared-error predictor. The zero-variance limits of the normal formulas agree with the degenerate cases above.
