Take n to be the unit normal, so P(v)=v−⟨v,n⟩n is orthogonal projection onto TM. With D the Euclidean Levi-Civita connection, the proposed derivative is
∇X​Y=P(DX​Y)∣M​.
(38)
It is a smooth tangent field, and independence of the extensions is given. Linearity follows from linearity of D and P. Moreover, for f∈C∞(M), take local extensions of f,X,Y to obtain
∇fX​Y=f∇X​Y,∇X​(fY)=X(f)Y+f∇X​Y,
(39)
since PY=Y. Thus it is an affine connection on M.
For tangent fields Y,Z, the normal component of DY pairs to zero with Z. Differentiating the ambient inner product along X gives
Xg(Y,Z)=⟨DX​Y,Z⟩+⟨Y,DX​Z⟩=g(∇X​Y,Z)+g(Y,∇X​Z).
(40)
Hence it is a metric connection for the induced Riemannian metric. The ambient torsion vanishes, so
∇X​Y−∇Y​X=P(DX​Y−DY​X)=P[X,Y]=[X,Y].
(41)
Here the restriction of the ambient bracket is the intrinsic bracket and is tangent to M: derivations of functions restricted to M give the same bracket, or this follows in submanifold coordinates. Thus the projected derivative is also a torsion-free connection. Uniqueness now proves
∇ is the Levi-Civita connection of the induced metric.​
(42)
This is the projected ambient connection. The projection formula requires a unit normal. If a nonunit normal is used, its normal term must instead be ⟨DY,n⟩n/⟨n,n⟩. For example, on the unit circle, taking n=2p and differentiating its unit tangent along itself gives DY=−p; the unnormalized printed expression would be 3p, which is not tangent. Normalizing the normal removes that ambiguity.
