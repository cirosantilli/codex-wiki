A smooth differential form of degree p is a smooth section of ΛpT∗M, so
Ωp(M)=Γ(ΛpT∗M).
(9)
It is equivalently a smooth alternating covariant p-tensor field. In a coordinate chart x1,…,xn it has a unique expression
ω=∑i1​<⋯<ip​​ai1​⋯ip​​dxi1​∧⋯∧dxip​
(10)
with smooth coefficients. At p=0 these are the smooth functions, and for p>n the space is zero.
Define the exterior derivative in a chart by
dω=∑I,j​∂xj∂aI​​dxj∧dxI.
(11)
To check that it is well-defined, let y1,…,yn be other smooth coordinates. The expression dya=∑i​(∂i​ya)dxi satisfies
dx​(dya)=∑i,j​(∂j​∂i​ya)dxj∧dxi=0
(12)
because the coefficients are symmetric and the wedge products antisymmetric. The coordinate formula also satisfies the graded Leibniz rule, and for a function b the chain rule gives dx​b=∑a​(∂b/∂ya)dya=dy​b. Applying these two facts to ω=∑I​bI​dyI yields dx​ω=∑I​dy​bI​∧dyI=dy​ω. Thus the local operators agree on overlaps and define a global linear map d:Ωp(M)→Ωp+1(M).
A second application in any chart gives
d2ω=∑I,j,k​(∂k​∂j​aI​)dxk∧dxj∧dxI=0,
(13)
again by symmetry of mixed derivatives and antisymmetry of the wedge product of differential forms. Consequently every exact differential form is closed. Put Ω−1(M)=0 and define the de Rham cohomology by
HdRp​(M)=im(d:Ωp−1→Ωp)ker(d:Ωp→Ωp+1)​.​
(14)
The identity d2=0 is exactly what puts the denominator inside the numerator, making this quotient meaningful. Since Ωp(M)=0 for p>dimM, HdRp​(M)=0 in those degrees.
