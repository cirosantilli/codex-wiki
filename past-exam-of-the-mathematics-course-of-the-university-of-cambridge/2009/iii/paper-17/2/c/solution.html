The direct-sum assertion fails in both degrees when the pieces overlap. Take genuine open covers, so the failure does not depend on any subtle choice of smooth structures on subsets.
For degree zero, let M=R, U=(−∞,1) and V=(−1,∞). By degree-zero de Rham cohomology, HdR0​ consists of locally constant functions. Each of M,U,V is connected and nonempty, so
HdR0​(M)=R,HdR0​(U)⊕HdR0​(V)=R2.
(17)
They are not isomorphic as real vector spaces. The restriction map is c↦(c,c); the overlap forces equality instead of allowing two independent constants.
For degree one, let M=S1, and remove distinct points a,b to form U=S1∖{a} and V=S1∖{b}. Each is diffeomorphic to an open interval. Every one-form f(t)dt on an interval has the smooth primitive ∫t0​t​f(s)ds, so both pieces have zero first de Rham cohomology. On the circle, the form η=xdy−ydx is closed because all two-forms on a one-dimensional manifold vanish. The parameterization (x,y)=(cost,sint) gives
∫S1​η=∫02π​dt=2π.
(18)
An exact form has zero integral around this loop: the integral of dF is the difference of the endpoint values of the periodic function F. Thus [η]=0, while the proposed right side is zero, disproving the isomorphism.
In fact HdR1​(S1)≅R. For a periodic one-form f(t)dt, subtract cdt where c=(2π)−1∫02π​f(t)dt. The remainder has the periodic primitive ∫0t​(f(s)−c)ds, and hence is exact. This also explicitly identifies the nonzero class used above.
