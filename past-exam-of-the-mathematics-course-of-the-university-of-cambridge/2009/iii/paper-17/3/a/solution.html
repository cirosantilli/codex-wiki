A left-invariant vector field X on a Lie group satisfies
(dLg​)h​Xh​=Xgh​(g,h∈G),
(19)
or equivalently (Lg​)∗​X=X for every g. For ξ∈Te​G, define
Xgξ​=(dLg​)e​ξ.
(20)
This is smooth because multiplication is smooth and its differential depends smoothly on the base points. The identity Lg​∘Lh​=Lgh​ gives (dLg​)h​Xhξ​=Xghξ​, so Xξ is a left-invariant vector field. Conversely, left invariance forces any such field to have this form with ξ=Xe​. Evaluation at e and the construction above are inverse linear maps. Hence
Te​G≅{left-invariant vector fields on G}.​
(21)
The Lie bracket of vector fields is their commutator as derivations on smooth functions: [X,Y]f=X(Yf)−Y(Xf). It is another vector field because the cross terms cancel in the Leibniz rule for its action on a product. The bracket is preserved by a diffeomorphism: conjugating the derivation operators by pullback of a smooth function preserves their commutator. Therefore
(Lg​)∗​[X,Y]=[(Lg​)∗​X,(Lg​)∗​Y]=[X,Y]
(22)
for left-invariant X,Y. These fields are consequently closed under the bracket.
Transport the bracket to the tangent space by
[ξ,η]g​=[Xξ,Xη]e​.​
(23)
Bilinearity and antisymmetry come from the commutator. The Jacobi identity follows by expanding the three nested commutators of derivation operators: their six terms cancel in pairs. Thus Te​G is a Lie algebra, and the displayed correspondence is a Lie algebra isomorphism onto the algebra of left-invariant vector fields.
