For an asymptotically flat spacetime with future null infinity I+, its black-hole region is
B=M∖J−(I+),​
(6)
where the causal past is understood in the conformal completion. Choose the future orientation in the advanced chart so that −∂r​ is future null. For any future causal tangent X, the inequality g(X,−∂r​)=−X(v)≤0 gives X(v)≥0. The causality inequality is
−fX(v)2+2X(v)X(r)+R2γAB​XAXB≤0.
(7)

This argument concerns the black-hole interior covered by ingoing coordinates; the additional white-hole region of the maximal extension has a different future causal orientation.

There is only one zero of f, at r=2m, and the Kruskal–Szekeres coordinates give four quadrants. Both exterior quadrants have r>2m, while the future and past interior quadrants have 0<r<2m. The tortoise coordinate is finite at r=0: for q>0, dr∗​/dr∼−q​r1/2/(2m), and for q=0 it is −r/(2m). Throughout each interior grr=f<0, so constant-r hypersurfaces are spacelike. The assumed curvature singularity at r=0 therefore forms a spacelike future boundary in the black-hole region and a spacelike past boundary in the white-hole region. At infinity, f→1 and R/r→1, giving two standard asymptotically flat ends.
The resulting Penrose diagram is the same four-region causal diagram as the maximal Schwarzschild spacetime:
Each exterior has its own past null infinity, future null infinity, spatial infinity and timelike infinities. The future and past event horizons intersect on the bifurcation surface. The diagram describes the eternal analytic solution; a spacetime formed by collapse need not contain its white-hole region or second exterior.
Unlike a nonextreme Reissner-Nordstrom black hole, this electrically charged solution has one horizon and spacelike singularities, with no inner Cauchy horizon. A nonextreme Reissner-Nordstrom solution has two distinct horizons, including an inner Cauchy horizon, and a timelike singularity; its ideal maximal extension contains an infinite sequence of exterior and interior blocks. Charge by itself does not determine this causal structure: the metric and field equations of the gravitational theory matter.

An asymptotically flat spacetime is a stationary spacetime if it admits a complete stationary Killing vector field K, with LK​g=0, timelike near infinity and normalized there to a unit time translation. It need not remain timelike in an ergoregion of a stationary spacetime. It is axisymmetric if it admits an axial Killing vector field Φ, whose flow is a rotational U(1) action: its spacelike orbits away from the axis are closed with period 2π, and it vanishes on the regular rotation axis. For a stationary axisymmetric spacetime, the generators can be chosen to commute, [K,Φ]=0. These are geometric symmetries, not merely time-independent or angle-independent coordinate labels.

b OurBigBook.com
During gravitational collapse, nonspherical time-dependent multipoles can be lost in gravitational radiation or absorbed by the forming black hole. Matter remaining outside must ultimately escape or fall in if the final object is to be an isolated vacuum black hole. Assume that the classical exterior settles to a regular stationary asymptotically flat vacuum geometry, with a connected horizon and the regularity conditions of the black-hole uniqueness theorem. This settling assumption is physical input, rather than a consequence of charge conservation alone.
For a rotating remnant, the black-hole rigidity theorem supplies axial symmetry; the uniqueness theorem then identifies the exterior as Kerr spacetime. The nonrotating equilibrium case is Schwarzschild spacetime. Gravitational radiation carries energy and angular momentum but no net electric charge. In the intended neutral collapse, with no net charge carried away by expelled matter, charge conservation therefore leaves zero black-hole charge. Initial total neutrality alone would not prove this if oppositely charged matter were expelled; the two-parameter conclusion assumes the uncharged vacuum remnant just described. This leaves
M and J,a=J/M,​
(13)
as its two intrinsic continuous parameters. The final M,J are what remain after escaping matter and radiation have carried off their shares; they need not equal the star's initial values. A spatial rotation may align the spin axis, and a translation or boost chooses the position and rest frame, so these do not add intrinsic parameters. All higher equilibrium multipoles are fixed by M,J; for example Kerr's mass quadrupole is −J2/M. A regular Kerr black hole obeys ∣J∣≤M2 in geometrized units. This is the content of the black-hole no-hair theorem in vacuum general relativity, with its stated hypotheses, rather than a statement about arbitrary matter theories or an unproved guarantee of dynamical settling. Hawking evaporation is ignored in this classical late-time equilibrium description.

For the superextreme Kerr metric,
Δ=(r−M)2+a2−M2>0
(16)
for all real r, so there are no coordinate singularities at horizon roots. The only stipulated curvature singularity is Σ=0, which forces r=0, θ=π/2. In the supplied Cartesian coordinates this is the Kerr ring singularity x2+y2=a2, z=0.
Write ρ2=x2+y2. The coordinate transformation implies
ρ2=(r2+a2)sin2θ,z=rcosθ,
(17)
and hence
r4−(ρ2+z2−a2)r2−a2z2=0.
(18)
The positive-radius Cartesian sheet uses
r2=21​[ρ2+z2−a2+(ρ2+z2−a2)2+4a2z2​],r≥0.
(19)
Its zero set is the disk z=0, ρ≤a. At an interior disk point, ρ<a, one has cosθ=0, so Σ=a2cos2θ>0. The disk interior is therefore not a singularity: in signed (r,θ,ϕ) coordinates, the metric is analytic through r=0. In particular, grr​=Σ/Δ is finite and the metric determinant −Σ2sin2θ is nonzero away from the ordinary polar coordinate axis. The polar axes are regular using Cartesian angular charts and 2π periodicity.
The required two-sheeted extension of superextremal Kerr spacetime is constructed by taking a second Cartesian sheet with the negative root r≤0, cutting both sheets along the disk, and gluing the upper bank of one to the lower bank of the other, and conversely. Signed r then passes continuously through zero as the disk is crossed. The ring is omitted. A useful check near an interior disk point is
z=rcosθ,cos2θ​r=0​=1−ρ2/a2>0,
(20)
so signed r is a smooth transverse coordinate there. The apparent factors z/r in the Cartesian metric represent cosθ and have regular signed-coordinate limits; they do not justify deleting the whole disk. Identifying the two signs of r on a single Cartesian copy would also identify two physically different metric values, since the Kerr-Schild coefficient changes sign.
Continue the two sheets out to r→±∞. Both ends are asymptotically flat; at the negative-radius end the mass term has the opposite sign, as gtt​=−1+2M/r+O(r−2). There are no further finite-radius horizon boundaries requiring additional blocks. Removing only the curvature-singular ring, and removing coordinate artifacts with the charts described above, gives the standard maximal analytic extension. The ring cannot be filled by an analytic nondegenerate metric because its curvature diverges. This extension is not globally hyperbolic; analyticity of an extension should not be confused with unique evolution from Cauchy data.

For 0<M<a, the ring is a naked singularity and there is no event horizon shielding the extension's closed timelike curves. The weak cosmic censorship conjecture predicts that such superextreme Kerr geometries are not formed by generic collapse of regular isolated initial data satisfying the appropriate energy conditions. This is a conjectural physical restriction, not a proof from the exact metric alone.
For M>a>0, the closed timelike curves lie deep inside the ideal analytic extension, beyond the inner Cauchy horizon, so weak cosmic censorship does not itself exclude them. However, that inner horizon is unstable to perturbations, with mass inflation expected in realistic collapse. The strong cosmic censorship conjecture is the relevant obstruction to a regular continuation into the ideal chronology-violating region. Thus the exact Kerr extension exhibits such curves mathematically, but neither regular collapse nor the uniqueness theorem establishes that they occur in Nature.

Transport two connecting vectors between neighbouring rays so that they commute with U, and project them onto the screen. Their infinitesimal transverse lengths and angles evolve according to the symmetric part of the optical tensor. The trace part gives uniform fractional dilation, while the trace-free null shear stretches one transverse direction and contracts the other, changing a circular beam to an ellipse without changing its area to first order. With zero null twist, there is no antisymmetric rotation of the beam.

The second law of black-hole mechanics states that the total area of a classical future event horizon cannot decrease toward the future, under the appropriate causal and energy assumptions. Use the null convergence condition Rab​UaUb≥0; in general relativity the Einstein field equations imply it from the null energy condition. Assume, as allowed here, that the horizon's null generators remain on the horizon and are complete to the future. The horizon is an achronal boundary, and its smooth generators are hypersurface-orthogonal, so their null twist is zero. The Null Raychaudhuri equation therefore gives
dλdθ​=−21​θ2−σ2−Rab​UaUb≤−21​θ2.
(33)
If some smooth horizon point had θ0​<0, then while the optical description remains regular, θ stays negative and
dλd​θ1​=−θ2θ′​≥21​.
(34)
Thus
θ(λ)≤1+21​θ0​(λ−λ0​)θ0​​,
(35)
forcing focusing no later than λ0​+2/∣θ0​∣.
The assumed geometrical result is that this focusing produces a conjugate point to a spacelike surface, namely a focal point relative to a small spacelike horizon cross-section through the original point. A null geodesic beyond such a focal point ceases to generate an achronal boundary: a timelike variation connects the cross-section to points just beyond it. Since the cross-section and the generator lie on the achronal horizon, this is impossible. Future completeness ensures that a singular endpoint cannot terminate the generator before the finite focusing parameter. This future-complete horizon focusing proof of the area theorem rules out θ0​<0.
Therefore θ≥0 on smooth horizon pieces, and the transverse-area identity gives
dλdA​=θA≥0.​
(36)

At crease sets, new generators may enter the horizon from the past, adding area; horizon generators do not leave it toward the future under the stated assumptions. Hence the statement also applies to total horizon area through black-hole mergers. Hawking's area theorem is classical: quantum stress-energy can violate the null energy condition, so it does not forbid a shrinking evaporating black hole.

(41)
In a foliation with induced metric determinant h, the canonical momentum is π=h​na∇a​ϕ. The normalized complete mode expansion implements the equal-time canonical commutation relations [ϕ​(x),π(y)]=iδ3(x−y) and vanishing field-field and momentum-momentum commutators on Σ. Propagation by the field equation gives microcausality: field observables commute at spacelike separation. Thus there is a local quantum field theory before one has selected a particle interpretation.
The chosen positive-mode space completes to a one-particle Hilbert space H1​. Its bosonic Fock space is ⨁n=0∞​SymnH1​. The Fock vacuum obeys ai​∣0⟩=0; acting with ai†​ produces multiparticle states, and Ni​=ai†​ai​ counts particles in mode i. The vacuum two-point function is W(x,y)=∑i​ui​(x)ui​(y)∗. The Hadamard condition restricts physically admissible states by prescribing their local short-distance singularity; it permits renormalization of the stress-energy tensor but does not select one unique vacuum.
The particle ambiguity is that there is generally no distinguished choice of positive-mode space. A second complete normalized basis can be
vi​=∑j​(αij​uj​+βij​uj∗​).
(42)
The Bogoliubov transformation preserves the mode normalization precisely when
αα†−ββ†=I,αβT−βαT=0.
(43)
Its operators obey bi​=(vi​,ϕ​)KG​=∑j​(αij∗​aj​−βij∗​aj†​). Consequently a state with no a-particles generally has b-particles if β=0. A unitary change of basis within the same positive-mode space has β=0 and merely relabels modes; a change mixing the positive and negative subspaces changes the vacuum and particle notion. Arbitrary coordinates called “time” do not resolve this choice. This is the vacuum ambiguity in a nonstationary spacetime, while the field equation and local commutator remain unchanged.
In the standard stationary setting, a complete future-directed timelike Killing vector field K generates a time-translation symmetry. With a stable positive energy operator and suitable boundary conditions, choose positive-frequency solutions by
iLK​uω​=ωuω​,ω>0.
(44)
The positive stationary Hamiltonian selects a particle splitting: positive spectral modes define annihilation operators, and the normal-ordered Hamiltonian is HK​=∑i​ωi​ai†​ai​. Its ground state is the vacuum state in a stationary spacetime. The Killing symmetry transports this splitting unchanged, so no positive-negative mixing is induced by stationary evolution. Degeneracies allow unitary basis changes without changing the vacuum. Stationarity fixes the ground-state particle interpretation relative to this time flow; it does not forbid excited or thermal states.
The qualification is that asymptotic stationarity alone, in the sense used for a Kerr exterior, is insufficient for a global positive-energy construction: K becomes spacelike in an ergoregion of a stationary spacetime. The usual unambiguous construction applies to a strictly stationary spacetime with the stated stability and spectral assumptions, or to appropriate stationary asymptotic regions for scattering. Zero modes or instability need separate treatment. Likewise different observer time flows on different regions need not define the same particles.
For particle creation by a nonstationary spacetime, choose normalized positive-frequency modes ujin​ in the stationary early region and uiout​ in the stationary late region. Propagate both sets by the Klein-Gordon equation through the intermediate time-dependent geometry. Compute their overlaps on any common Cauchy hypersurface:
uiout​=∑j​(αij​ujin​+βij​ujin∗​),αij​=(ujin​,uiout​)KG​,βij​=−(ujin∗​,uiout​)KG​.
(45)
The initial in-vacuum has ajin​∣0in​⟩=0. Since aiout​=∑j​(αij∗​ajin​−βij∗​ajin†​), contracting the in operators yields the particle number from Bogoliubov coefficients:
⟨0in​∣Niout​∣0in​⟩=j∑​∣βij​∣2,⟨Nout​⟩=i,j∑​∣βij​∣2.​
(46)
Thus the procedure is to fix asymptotic mode choices, solve the classical wave equation, project using the conserved inner product, and take squared negative-frequency overlaps. Particle energy comes from the time-dependent background, so particle creation does not contradict the linear deterministic field equation. For an initial state diagonal in in-mode occupation numbers nj​, with no anomalous correlations, the more general expectation is ∑j​[∣αij​∣2nj​+∣βij​∣2(nj​+1)].
For continuous spectra, sums become integrals and normalized wave packets avoid meaningless squares of delta functions. A finite total count requires the appropriate convergence of the double sum or integral. The bosonic mode mixing implementability condition is that β be Hilbert-Schmidt; otherwise the two Fock representations need not be unitarily equivalent, even though the local field algebra and finite-mode particle calculations still make sense. This distinction between deterministic field evolution and a chosen representation is essential to quantum field theory in curved spacetime.

Classically, the laws of thermodynamics and black-hole thermodynamics have parallel structures, but an ordinary black hole absorbs and does not emit: its temperature would appear to be zero, despite nonzero surface gravity. Hawking radiation changes this by giving an actual temperature measurable from a thermal particle spectrum. In units G=ℏ=c=kB​=1,
TH​=2πκ​.​
(47)
Scattering through the exterior modifies the observed flux by greybody factors; for rotation and charge the occupation involves ω−mϕ​ΩH​−qΦH​. These transmission and chemical-potential factors do not change the underlying Hawking temperature.
The Zeroth law of black-hole mechanics makes κ constant on a stationary horizon, so it gives a uniform equilibrium temperature, matching the zeroth law of thermodynamics. The full First law of black-hole mechanics is
dM=8πκ​dA+ΩH​dJ+ΦH​dQ.
(48)
Comparing it with dE=TdS+ΩdJ+ΦdQ and using the actual Hawking temperature fixes
SBH​=4A​,dM=TH​dSBH​+ΩH​dJ+ΦH​dQ.​
(49)
With constants restored, SBH​=kB​c3A/(4Gℏ). If κ is defined geometrically with dimensions of inverse length, TH​=ℏcκ/(2πkB​); if surface gravity is expressed as an acceleration, the equivalent formula is ℏκ/(2πckB​). The first-law comparison fixes entropy up to an additive constant. This is the Bekenstein-Hawking entropy, so the mechanical analogy becomes a quantitative thermodynamic identification, including its normalization.
The classical second law of black-hole mechanics then corresponds to increasing black-hole entropy. Quantum evaporation can reduce A, because the classical energy-condition hypotheses fail, while outgoing radiation carries entropy. The appropriate thermodynamic statement is the generalized second law,
Sgen​=SBH​+Soutside​ does not decrease.​
(50)
Finally, the third law of black-hole mechanics, that zero surface gravity cannot be achieved by a finite physical process under its regularity assumptions, becomes the unattainability of zero temperature. It does not assert the Nernst entropy formulation: an extremal horizon can have nonzero area. Hawking radiation supplies the physical temperature, the first law fixes entropy, and the generalized second law includes both the black hole and its exterior matter.
