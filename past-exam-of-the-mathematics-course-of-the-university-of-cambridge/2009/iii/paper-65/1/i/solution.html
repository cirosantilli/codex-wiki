Let G be the remaining gas mass, S the formed stellar mass, and G0​ the initial gas mass; initially S=0 and the gas-phase metallicity is zero. Take the stellar yield y and the mass-loading factor ν to be constant, with homogeneous mixing and no galactic gas inflow. With the specified zero bulk return, mass conservation gives
dG=−(1+ν)dS,G=G0​−(1+ν)S.
(1)
New metals add ydS, while star formation and the galactic outflow remove metals at the existing gas-phase metallicity Z. Thus
d(GZ)=ydS−Z(1+ν)dS,GdZ=ydS=−1+νy​dG.
(2)
The leaky-box model of galactic chemical evolution therefore gives
Z=1+νy​lnGG0​​.
(3)
For the usual gas fraction of a galaxy, μ=G/(G+S), the denominator is the baryonic mass still in the system, which decreases under the galactic outflow. Since G0​=G+(1+ν)S, we have G0​/G=1+(1+ν)(1−μ)/μ. In the remaining-mass convention,
Z(μ)=1+νy​ln[μ1+ν(1−μ)​].​
(4)
If instead μ denotes G/G0​ relative to the fixed initial reservoir, the same calculation gives Z=yln(1/μ)/(1+ν)​. Stating the denominator matters: these two gas fraction of a galaxy conventions coincide for the closed-box model of galactic chemical evolution, recovered at ν=0, but differ when gas escapes. Neglecting bulk return while retaining a nonzero stellar yield is the intended trace-metal approximation.
