The escape-speed scaling of wind mass loading makes the proposed trend dynamically plausible: the shallower Newtonian gravitational potential of a dwarf galaxy lets stellar feedback expel more gas per unit stellar mass. If coupled feedback supplies specific energy e∗​, escape requires νvesc2​/2≲e∗​; fixed coupling suggests ν∝vesc−2​ for an energy-driven outflow. Fixed specific momentum p∗​ instead gives νvesc​≲p∗​, or ν∝vesc−1​ for a momentum-driven outflow.
Since escape velocity has vesc2​∼GM/R, galaxies whose characteristic radius grows more slowly than their gravitating mass have increasing binding energy per unit mass with increasing mass. For R∝Ma with a<1, the energy-driven outflow estimate gives ν∝Ma−1. Equivalently, greater velocity dispersion in massive spheroids implies greater escape energy and more effective retention of metals.
For scale, one core-collapse supernova of 1051erg per 100M⊙​ formed supplies about 5×1015cm2s−2 before coupling losses. At ten percent coupling the energetic upper estimate 2e∗​/vesc2​ is about 110 for vesc​=30kms−1, but about 0.4 for 500kms−1. Efficient loss from shallow potentials and retention in deep potentials can span the required range. These are illustrative energetics rather than a unique prediction: cooling, entrainment, fallback and the actual initial mass function determine the escaping mass-loading factor.
