The Freyd general adjoint functor theorem is as follows. Let G:D→C, with D a complete category which is locally small. Then G has a left adjoint if and only if it preserves all small categorical limits and satisfies the solution-set condition: for each c∈C, there is a set-indexed family
ui​:c→Gdi​
(33)
such that every u:c→Gd can be written u=G(h)ui​ for some i and some h:di​→d. The target category need not be complete. We prove all the existence steps.
First establish the initial-object lemma for complete categories with a weakly initial set. Let E be locally small and complete, with a weakly initial set {wi​}, meaning every object receives a map from at least one wi​. Its categorical product w=∏i​wi​ is weakly initial: compose a categorical product projection with the map from the appropriate wi​. Local smallness makes E(w,w) a set. Let e:v→w be the simultaneous equaliser of every endomorphism of w and the identity. Explicitly it is the equaliser of the two arrows
w⇉∏k∈E(w,w)​w
(34)
with components k and 1w​. Thus ke=e for every endomorphism k, and e is monic.
Every object x receives a morphism from v, by composing e with a map w→x. To prove uniqueness, take parallel maps a,b:v→x and their equaliser m:y→v. Weak initiality of w gives t:w→y. The composite emt is an endomorphism of w, so (emt)e=e. Cancelling the monomorphism e gives mte=1v​. Hence m is both monic and a split epimorphism, and is therefore invertible. Since am=bm, this forces a=b. We have proved v is initial, without using a class-sized categorical product or equaliser.
For sufficiency in the theorem, fix c and consider the comma category (c↓G). Its objects are pairs (d,u:c→Gd); a morphism h:(d,u)→(d′,u′) satisfies G(h)u=u′. This category is locally small because each hom-set is a subset of D(d,d′). It is complete: for a small diagram of such pairs, take the underlying categorical limit d in D. The arrows from c form a cone to its image under G, and preservation of the categorical limit gives a unique u:c→Gd inducing them. The same universal property gives all mediators in the comma category. The solution-set family is a weakly initial set in this comma category, so the lemma supplies an initial pair
(Fc,ηc​:c→GFc).
(35)
For f:c→c′, initiality defines a unique Ff:Fc→Fc′ satisfying G(Ff)ηc​=ηc′​f. Uniqueness proves the identity and composition laws, making F a functor. Initiality also gives bijections
D(Fc,d)≅C(c,Gd),h⟼G(h)ηc​.​
(36)
The definition of Ff proves naturality in c, and postcomposition proves naturality in d. These bijections are the required adjunction F⊣G.
For necessity, if F⊣G, the single arrow ηc​:c→GFc is a solution set: the transpose of any u:c→Gd gives its factorization through ηc​. If d is a limiting cone vertex of a small diagram Dj​ in D, then, naturally in c,
C(c,Gd)≅D(Fc,d)≅limj​D(Fc,Dj​)≅limj​C(c,GDj​).
(37)
These are the canonical cone-factorization bijections, so Gd with its image projections is a categorical limit of GDj​. Thus G preserves all small categorical limits, completing both directions of the theorem.
