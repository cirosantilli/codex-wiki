For each algebra for a monad (A,a), consider the specific pair in D
FTAFa​εFA​​​FA.
(50)
Then the exact condition is
K has a left adjoint⟺each of these pairs has a coequalizer in D.​
(51)
Each pair is reflexive, with common section FηA​: the two composite identities follow from aηA​=1A​ and εFA​FηA​=1FA​. Thus existence of all reflexive coequalizers is sufficient, but the displayed specified pairs give the necessary-and-sufficient condition.
To prove it, take h:FA→d and its adjoint transpose x=GhηA​:A→Gd. The transpose of hFa is xa, by naturality of η. The transpose of hεFA​ is
GhGεFA​ηTA​=GhμA​ηTA​=Gh.
(52)
Moreover counit naturality and a triangle identity give
Gεd​Tx=Gεd​,GF(GhηA​)=Gh,GεFA​,GFηA​=Gh.
(53)
Because transposition is bijective, the coequalizing equation is therefore equivalent to
hFa=hεFA​⟺xa=Gεd​Tx.
(54)
The right side says exactly that x:(A,a)→Kd is a monad algebra morphism.
For sufficiency, choose a coequalizer qa​:FA→L(A,a) for every monad algebra. Its universal property and the equivalence just proved give natural bijections
D(L(A,a),d)≅{h:FA→d:hFa=hεFA​}≅CT((A,a),Kd).
(55)
To see explicitly that the chosen objects form a functor, an monad algebra morphism u:(A,a)→(B,b) makes qb​Fu coequalize the pair for a: use ua=bTu and naturality of the counit. Hence there is a unique Lu with Luqa​=qb​Fu. Uniqueness proves its identity and composition laws and the naturality of the displayed bijections. Thus L⊣K.
Conversely, suppose L⊣K with unit λ(A,a)​:(A,a)→KL(A,a). Transpose its underlying arrow A→GL(A,a) through F⊣G to obtain qa​:FA→L(A,a). Since the unit is an monad algebra morphism, the equivalence above shows that qa​ coequalizes the specified pair. Any coequalizing h:FA→d transposes to an monad algebra map (A,a)→Kd, which factors uniquely through the unit by L⊣K. Transposing back gives a unique factorization of h through qa​. Thus qa​ is the required coequalizer. This proves both directions of the Left adjoint to the Eilenberg-Moore comparison functor criterion.
