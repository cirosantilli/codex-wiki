 ... Past exam of the mathematics course of the University of Cambridge 2009 iii Paper 23 1 b
OurBigBook.com Words: 136
Let 1 be a terminal object. The pullback of the unique arrows A→1←B is a binary categorical product: maps X→A and X→B automatically have equal composites into 1, so the pullback universal property is exactly the categorical product universal property.
For parallel arrows f,g:A⇉B, form B×B and take the pullback of ⟨f,g⟩:A→B×B along the diagonal δ:B→B×B. Write the resulting projections as e:E→A and b:E→B. The pullback equation gives
fe=b=ge.
(2)
If u:X→A satisfies fu=gu, the pair (u,fu) is a pullback cone, producing a unique v:X→E with ev=u and bv=fu. The second condition is forced by the first, since b=fe. Thus e is the equaliser of f,g.
The terminal object and iterated binary categorical products give all finite categorical products, including the nullary one. Part (a) therefore proves pullbacks and a terminal object give all finite categorical limits.
