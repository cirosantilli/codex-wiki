Use the elementary, or weak, topos axioms: finite limits and a power object PA for every object A, naturally representing Sub(A×X). No arbitrary products or coproducts are assumed. In particular Ω=P1 is the subobject classifier. Inverse image of the membership relation defines Pf:PB→PA for f:A→B.
Interchanging the factors in a relation gives natural bijections
E(A,PB)≅Sub(A×B)≅E(B,PA).
(1)
Thus the contravariant power-object functor P:Eop→E has left adjoint Pop. Its adjunction unit ηA​:A→PPA sends a generalized element a to the predicate on PA given by membership of a.
This unit is monic. If x,y:T→A have the same image under ηA​, membership of x and y agrees in every T-parameterized subobject of A. Apply this to the graph of x in A×T: membership of x is true, so membership of y is true, which means y=x. This argument uses parameterized relations, not an assumption that global elements detect morphisms.
The functor P reflects isomorphisms. Suppose Pf is invertible. Then PPf is invertible, and naturality PPfηA​=ηB​f together with monicity of ηA​ shows that f is monic. Regard f as a subobject of B. Its inverse image along itself is the whole of A, just as for the whole subobject of B. Those two subobjects correspond to maps 1→PB; since Pf is monic, they coincide. Therefore the subobject f is all of B, and f is an isomorphism.
We now prove the preservation condition for the crude monadicity theorem. A reflexive pair in Eop is a coreflexive pair f,g:B⇉A in E, with rf=rg=1B​ for some r:A→B. Let e:E↪B be its equalizer. For any monomorphism m, define ∃m​ on power objects by composing a parameterized subobject with m; no general image construction is needed because m is monic. Inverse image followed by this extension obeys Pm∃m​=1.
Here both f,g are monic. For a parameterized subobject S of B, an element b belongs to g−1(f(S)) exactly when gb=fb′ for some b′∈S. Applying r gives b=b′, so this condition is precisely b∈S and fb=gb. Consequently, as morphisms of power objects,
Pf∃f​=1PB​,Pg∃f​=∃e​Pe,Pe∃e​=1PE​.
(2)
If h:PB→Z equalizes Pf,Pg, these identities give
h=hPf∃f​=hPg∃f​=h∃e​Pe.
(3)
It therefore factors uniquely through Pe, which is a split epimorphism with section ∃e​. Since fe=ge, Pe also equalizes Pf,Pg. Hence Pe is their coequalizer. This proves explicitly that power objects turn coreflexive equalizers into coequalizers.
Finite equalizers in E give all coequalizers of reflexive pairs in Eop, and we have shown that P preserves them and reflects isomorphisms. The crude monadicity theorem now yields
P is monadic,Eop≃EPP.​
(4)
Fix a set I of cardinality κ. If E has I-indexed products in a category, its Eilenberg-Moore category for the double-power monad has them, since the forgetful functor creates existing limits. The displayed equivalence then gives I-products in Eop, hence I-coproducts in a category in E.
For the converse, suppose those coproducts exist. The diagonal Δ:E→EI is a logical functor, because all the elementary topos structure in EI is pointwise, and it has left adjoint ∐I​. Apply power-object monadicity to both toposes. Under their monadic identifications, Δop is the lift of Δ to the double-power Eilenberg-Moore categories. The base functor Δ is a right adjoint to ∐I​, so the adjoint lifting theorem for monad algebra functors gives a left adjoint to its algebra lift. Its required reflexive coequalizers exist in Eop, since they are opposites of finite equalizers in E. Taking opposites gives a right adjoint to Δ, which is exactly the I-product functor. Thus the products and coproducts of fixed cardinality in a topos satisfy
E has κ-products⟺E has κ-coproducts.​

Let (G,ε,δ) be a Cartesian comonad, so its endofunctor preserves finite limits. A coalgebra for a comonad is (A,a) with a:A→GA, εA​a=1A​ and δA​a=Gaa. Its morphisms satisfy bf=Gfa. Write U:EG→E for the forgetful functor and R(X)=(GX,δX​) for the cofree coalgebra. Transposition is
EG((A,a),R(X))≅E(A,X),f⟼εX​f,h⟼Gha.
(6)
The forgetful functor creates finite limits, since G preserves them. We construct both exponential objects and the subobject classifier.
For coalgebras (A,a),(B,b), set D=BA and W=(GB)A in the ambient topos. Define p,q:GD→W by transposing
p♭=bev(εD​×1A​),q♭=Gev(1GD​×a),
(7)
where GD×GA≅G(D×A). Their cofree transposes are coalgebra morphisms
p​=GpδD​,q​=GqδD​:R(D)⇉R(W).
(8)
Let H be their coalgebra equalizer. For a coalgebra (C,c), an arrow C→R(D) corresponds to a map h:C×A→B. Its evaluation respects coalgebra structure exactly when
bh=Gh(c×a).
(9)
Under the displayed transpositions this is precisely the condition that it equalize p​,q​. Thus EG(C,H)≅EG(C×A,B) naturally, and H is the required exponential in a coalgebra topos.
Let κ:GΩ→Ω classify G⊤:G1≅1↪GΩ. In R(Ω) take the equalizer
ΩG​↪R(Ω)1​GκδΩ​​​R(Ω).

The truth map into R(Ω) is G⊤ and factors through this equalizer. To verify its universal property, take an underlying subobject m:S↪A of a coalgebra. It carries a compatible coalgebra structure exactly when a maps S into GS. Such a structure, if it exists, is unique because Gm is monic; its laws follow by cancellation of m,Gm,G2m. Moreover a−1(GS)⊆S always: applying εA​ to a point of GS lands in S. Thus stability is equivalent to S=a−1(GS).
If χ:A→Ω classifies S, this last equality is exactly
χ=κGχa.

The cofree transpose χ​=Gχa:A→R(Ω) satisfies this equation precisely when it factors through ΩG​. Indeed transposing the equality χ​=GκδΩ​χ​ gives the equation above. Pulling back its truth map recovers S, because κ classifies G⊤. Hence ΩG​ is the subobject classifier of a coalgebra topos. We have obtained finite limits, exponentials and a classifier, proving EG is a topos.
Now let f:H→E be a geometric morphism, and put L=f∗, V=f∗​, with unit η. Both functors preserve finite limits, so G=LV is a Cartesian comonad on H. Put D=HG. Its comparison functor
K:E⟶D,K(X)=(LX,LηX​)
(12)
is left exact and has right adjoint
J(A,a)=Eq(Va,ηVA​:VA⇉VLVA).
(13)
To check the adjunction, transpose an underlying map LX→A to X→VA. The coalgebra-morphism equation becomes exactly the equality of the two composites into VLVA, so the transposed arrow factors uniquely through the displayed equalizer.
The counit KJ(A,a)→(A,a) is invertible. Applying L to the defining equalizer gives the equalizer of Ga,δA​:GA⇉G2A. The structure map a:A→GA is exactly that equalizer: it equalizes by coassociativity; if t:T→GA equalizes, apply εGA​ to obtain t=aεA​t, giving its unique factorization through a. Thus the underlying comparison counit is an isomorphism, and the forgetful functor reflects isomorphisms. Therefore J is full and faithful.
The adjunctions U⊣R and K⊣J define
Hp​Di​E,f=i∘p.​
(14)
Here p∗=U is faithful, so p is a surjective geometric morphism; i∗​=J is full and faithful, so i is a geometric embedding. Their inverse-image composite is UK=L, establishing the surjection-embedding factorization of a geometric morphism.
For uniqueness, let S be the class of arrows in E inverted by f∗. In any surjection-embedding factorization f=i′p′, conservativity of p′∗ shows that S is also the class inverted by i′∗. The essential image of the fully faithful i∗′​ consists exactly of the S-local objects Y, namely those for which E(B,Y)→E(A,Y) is bijective for every A→B in S. One direction follows from the adjunction. For the converse, the reflector unit Y→i∗′​i′∗Y belongs to S; locality gives a retraction, and locality of the reflected object makes that retraction also a right inverse. Thus the unit is invertible. The image subcategory is consequently determined by f, independently of the factorization. The two intermediate toposes are equivalent over E, and their inverse-image composites with the embeddings determine the surjection legs. This proves uniqueness up to equivalence.

A local operator is an inflationary, idempotent, finite-meet-preserving map j:Ω→Ω with j(⊤)=⊤, expressed in the internal logic of a topos. For a subterminal object U with truth value u, the closed local operator and quasi-closed local operator are respectively
c(u)(p)=u∨p,q(u)(p)=((p⇒u)⇒u).
(15)
The first is a local operator by distributivity. For the second put n(p)=p⇒u. Then p≤n2p, n is order-reversing, and n3p=np: the first inequality gives n3p≤np, while its application to np gives the reverse inequality. Thus q=n2 is inflationary and idempotent, and q(⊤)=⊤.
For meet preservation, monotonicity gives q(p∧r)≤qp∧qr. Conversely put t=(p∧r)⇒u. From t∧p≤r⇒u and qr∧(r⇒u)≤u, we obtain t∧qr≤p⇒u. Intersecting with qp gives qp∧qr∧t≤u, hence qp∧qr≤q(p∧r). This proves all local-operator axioms. Also q(u)(0)=u and q(u)(u)=u. Equivalently, it is the largest local operator leaving u fixed: if k(u)=u, then
kp∧k(p⇒u)≤u⟹kp≤k(p⇒u)⇒u≤(p⇒u)⇒u.
(16)
This explains the quasi-closed terminology as relative double negation above the closed bottom u.
The subobject classifier of shj​(E) is the closed-subobject classifier Ωj​={p:jp=p}. Its bottom is j0, its meet is inherited, its join is j(p∨r), and implication between closed truth values is the ambient implication. To verify the last claim, if jv=v, then
j(p⇒v)∧jp=j((p⇒v)∧p)≤v.
(17)
It follows that j(p⇒v)≤jp⇒v≤p⇒v; inflationarity gives equality and also jp⇒v=p⇒v.
Suppose j=q(u). Every closed p lies above u, and its complement in Ωj​ is np=p⇒u, also closed because n3=n. We have p∧np=u. Moreover n(p∨np)=np∧n2p=np∧p=u, so q(p∨np)=⊤. Thus the fixed truth values form a Boolean algebra, and the sheaf topos is a Boolean topos.
Conversely suppose the sheaf topos is Boolean and put u=j0. Complementation in its classifier is p↦p⇒u. Applying the double-complement identity to jp, and using the implication calculation above, gives
jp=((jp⇒u)⇒u)=((p⇒u)⇒u).
(18)
Therefore
shj​(E) is Boolean⟺j=q(j0).​
(19)
For the cover, work in the slice category E/Ω. Its generic subterminal object is T=(⊤:1↪Ω). Form its quasi-closed Boolean subtopos B=shq(T)​(E/Ω), and compose its embedding with the slice projection to obtain b:B→E. The slice projection has inverse image X↦X×Ω, and sheafification is left exact, so this is a geometric morphism.
To prove surjectivity, take a monomorphism S↪X with characteristic predicate p(x), and suppose b∗ makes it invertible. Pullback to the slice followed by sheafification makes this exactly the assertion that it is q(T)-dense. Internally on X×Ω, therefore,
((p(x)⇒u)⇒u)=⊤
(20)
for the generic truth value u. Substitute u=p(x) by pulling back along the graph X→X×Ω. The left side becomes (⊤⇒p(x))=p(x), so p(x)=⊤ and the original mono is invertible. Thus b∗ reflects invertible monos. If it identifies two parallel arrows, their equalizer becomes invertible; reflection then shows the arrows were already equal. Hence b∗ is faithful, and
shq(T)​(E/Ω)⟶E is a surjection from a Boolean topos.​
(21)
The generic truth value is essential: substituting only a fixed global value such as 0 would give a Boolean subtopos, but need not give a surjection onto the original topos.

A signature Σ specifies sorts, finite-arity function symbols and finite-arity relation symbols. A coherent formula is built from atomic relations and equality using ⊤,⊥, finite conjunction, finite disjunction and existential quantification. A coherent theory consists of coherent sequents ϕ⊢x​ψ in finite typed contexts. The context expresses universal quantification of the free variables; unrestricted universal quantification or negation is not a formula constructor here.
Construct the coherent syntactic category CT​ as follows. Objects are formulas in context {x.ϕ}, modulo renaming and provable equivalence. An arrow to {y​.ψ} is a formula ρ(x,y​), modulo provable equivalence, for which the theory proves
ρ⊢ϕ∧ψ,ϕ⊢x​∃y​ρ,ρ(x,y​)∧ρ(x,y​′)⊢y​=y​′.
(22)
These say that the relation is supported on the specified objects, total and single-valued. The identity is equality on the context, and composition is
(σ∘ρ)(x,z)=∃y​(ρ(x,y​)∧σ(y​,z)).
(23)
The logical rules give associativity and the identity laws. The empty-context true formula is terminal; conjunction in disjoint contexts gives products; conjunction with equality of the two functional relations gives equalizers. Existential quantification gives images, and finite disjunction gives unions of subobjects. Substitution preserves these constructions, so images and unions are stable under pullback. This makes CT​ a coherent category.
Let M be a T-model in a topos E. Interpret each context as a finite product of its sort objects and each formula as its corresponding subobject. Define
FM​({x.ϕ})=[[x.ϕ]]M​.
(24)
The interpretation of an arrow relation ρ is a total single-valued relation between these objects. Its projection to the domain is both an epimorphism, by totality, and a monomorphism, by single-valuedness, hence invertible in a topos. Its other projection therefore determines a unique morphism. Composition agrees with relational composition, so FM​ is a functor. Finite limits interpret equality and conjunction; images interpret existential quantification; finite unions interpret disjunction, including the empty union for ⊥. Consequently FM​ is a coherent functor.
Conversely there is a canonical syntactic model U in CT​. Interpret a sort s by {x:s.⊤}, a function symbol by its term graph, and a relation symbol by its atomic formula-in-context subobject. Induction on formulas identifies the interpretation of every ϕ in U with its formula-in-context object. Every axiom is then an inclusion of the associated subobjects, because it is provable in T.
For a coherent functor F:CT​→E, set MF​=F(U). Preservation of finite limits, images and unions proves inductively that F preserves all coherent interpretations, so every axiom holds in MF​. The same induction supplies natural isomorphisms
F({x.ϕ})≅[[x.ϕ]]MF​​,
(25)
compatible with the functional relations defining arrows. Thus F≅FMF​​; directly on the sorts, operations and relations, MFM​​≅M.
Finally these constructions agree on morphisms. A model homomorphism h:M→N induces maps on finite contexts. Atomic relations are preserved by definition; finite conjunction and disjunction and existential witnesses then show that each formula object maps to the corresponding formula object. Compatibility with functional relations makes these maps a natural transformation FM​→FN​. Conversely a natural transformation between coherent functors restricts to its components on sort objects; naturality for term graphs preserves operations, and naturality for relation inclusions preserves relations. It is therefore a model homomorphism. The two correspondences are inverse. We have proved the categorical, not merely objectwise, equivalence
ModT​(E)≃Coh(CT​,E).​
(26)

A coverage on a category specifies covering families stable under refinement along arrows: if (fi​:Ci​→C) covers and h:B→C is any arrow, there is a covering family (gj​:Bj​→B) such that every hgj​ factors through some fi​. Equivalently one works with the associated saturated covering sieves on a category, satisfying maximality, pullback stability and local transitivity. These determine the same sheaves on a site. We use this sieve on a category formulation of J.
An object is a J-irreducible object of a site if its only covering sieve on a category is the maximal sieve on a category. The coverage on a category is a rigid coverage if every object is covered by the sieve on a category generated by arrows whose domains are J-irreducible.
Assume C has finitely many arrows and every idempotent morphism splits. There are finitely many sieves on a category on each object C, so the intersection of all covering sieves on a category is a covering sieve on a category RC​: finite intersections of covers are covers by the topology axioms. It is the least cover. Let I be the collection of all arrows f:B→C lying in RC​. This is an idempotent ideal in a finite category.
Here are the details. It is closed under precomposition because RC​ is a sieve on a category. For h:C→D, the sieve on a category h∗RD​ covers C; minimality gives RC​⊆h∗RD​, so hf∈RD​. Thus it is also closed under postcomposition. Local transitivity says that the sieve on a category
SC​=⋃f∈RC​​fRdomf​
(27)
covers C. It is contained in RC​, while minimality gives the opposite inclusion. Hence SC​=RC​, or I2=I.
We prove that such an ideal is generated by the identities it contains. Take f∈I. Since I2=I, repeatedly factor its remaining right factor to obtain
f=a1​b1​=a1​a2​b2​=⋯=a1​⋯an​bn​,
(28)
with every factor in I. Write pn​=a1​⋯an​. Finiteness gives pi​=pj​ for some i<j. Their domains coincide, and t=ai+1​⋯aj​ is an endomorphism in I satisfying pi​t=pi​. Some positive power e=tm is idempotent: the powers are eventually periodic, so choose m beyond the eventual threshold and divisible by its period. Then pi​e=pi​. Split e=ir with i:B→dom(pi​), r:dom(pi​)→B and ri=1B​. Since I is a two-sided ideal,
1B​=rei∈I,f=pi​ebi​=(pi​i)(rbi​).
(29)
Thus f factors through an object B whose identity lies in I. The latter means 1B​∈RB​, hence RB​=yB; every covering sieve on a category on B must therefore be maximal. So B is J-irreducible.
Every arrow of RC​ consequently factors through a J-irreducible object. The sieve on a category generated by arrows from all such objects contains RC​ and is a cover. This proves every coverage on the finite idempotent-complete category is rigid. If RC​ is empty, the same conclusion holds because the empty sieve on a category already covers C.
Let D be the full subcategory of J-irreducible objects. For a covering sieve on a category S on C and any f:D→C with D∈D, the pulled-back sieve on a category f∗S covers D and is maximal, so f∈S. Therefore S∣D​=yC∣D​. For a categorical presheaf F on D, define its extension by
HF​(C)=Nat(yC∣D​,F).
(30)
This is the right Kan extension along the inclusion. Its adjunction gives
Nat(S,HF​)≅Nat(S∣D​,F)=HF​(C),
(31)
which is precisely unique amalgamation on S. Hence HF​ is a sheaf. The Yoneda lemma gives HF​(D)≅F(D) for D∈D.
Conversely, if G is a sheaf, its sections on all arrows from D into C determine a section on their covering sieve on a category. A compatible family extends to an arrow factoring through such a D by restriction from its given D-section. If an arrow has two factorizations, restrict to the covering sieve on a category generated by arrows from D into its domain; compatibility makes the two restrictions equal, and the sheaf's uniqueness of amalgamation makes the sections equal. The resulting matching family glues uniquely over C. Thus G(C)≅Nat(yC∣D​,G∣D​). Restriction and F↦HF​ are inverse up to natural isomorphism, proving
Sh(C,J)≃[Dop,Set].​
(32)

Use one ring sort with 0,1,+,−,⋅ and the usual unital commutative ring equations. Write Inv(x) for the coherent formula ∃y(xy=1). Add the coherent sequents
0=1⊢⊥,⊤⊢x​Inv(x)∨Inv(1−x).
(40)
This presents the coherent theory of local rings. For an ordinary nonzero ring satisfying these axioms, the nonunits form an ideal: a multiple of a nonunit cannot be a unit; and if nonunits a,b had unit sum, multiplying by (a+b)−1 would make two nonunits sum to 1, contradicting the second axiom. They are also closed under additive inverses. This proper ideal contains every proper ideal, since a proper ideal contains no unit, and its quotient is a field; it is the unique maximal ideal. Conversely in a local ring, x and 1−x cannot both belong to the unique maximal ideal, so one is a unit. The first sequent excludes the zero ring. In a topos, the disjunction asserts that these unit alternatives hold locally.
Let C be a small skeleton of the opposite of the category of finitely presented unital commutative rings; write CA​ for the object corresponding to A. The Zariski coverage on finitely presented rings is generated by
{CA[fi−1​]​⟶CA​}i=1n​,(f1​,…,fn​)=A,
(41)
and the empty cover of C0​. Localization is finitely presented, and its compatibility with base change proves the covering axioms.
Here is the classifying argument. For an internal commutative ring M and a presentation A=Z[x1​,…,xn​]/(p1​,…,pr​), define FM​(CA​) as the subobject of Mn satisfying the polynomial equations pi​=0. Ring maps induce maps of these solution objects in the site direction. This is independent of presentation, and it preserves finite limits: finite colimits of finitely presented rings correspond to joining finite generators and equations, whose solution objects are finite limits. Conversely a left-exact functor F:C→E reconstructs a commutative ring F(CZ[t]​) using the polynomial maps for addition, multiplication, constants and additive inverse. Presentations by generators and equations then show F≅FM​. Thus flat functors on C correspond to internal commutative rings.
Under FM​, the localization arrow above identifies its domain with the locus where fi​ evaluates to a unit; the inverse witness is unique. The family is jointly epimorphic exactly when these unit loci cover the solution object for A. The locality axiom implies this for every unit-ideal family: from a finite sum ∑fi​gi​=1, repeatedly apply the two-summand unit alternative, rescaling a unit partial sum when necessary; some fi​gi​, hence some fi​, is a unit locally. Conversely the cover of Z[t] by its localizations at t and 1−t gives precisely the locality axiom, while the empty cover of the zero ring gives 0=1⊢⊥. Therefore continuous flat functors are exactly models of the displayed coherent theory. The Diaconescu equivalence for geometric morphisms now identifies
Geom(E,Sh(C,Z))≃Modlocal rings​(E),
(42)
naturally in E. This proves the asserted classifying topos identification.
Since the site is subcanonical, the generic local ring is the representable sheaf
R=yCZ[t]​,R(CA​)=HomRing​(Z[t],A)=A.
(43)
Its ring operations are induced by polynomial substitution. Its infinitesimal subobject is likewise representable:
D=yCZ[ε]/(ε2)​,D(CA​)={a∈A:a2=0}.

(RD)(CA​)​≅Hom(yCA​×D,R)≅Hom(yCA[ε]/(ε2)​,yCZ[t]​)≅A[ε]/(ε2).​
(45)
Every element of this ring has a unique expression a+bε, because 1,ε form an A-basis. This holds even when A has nilpotents or zero divisors. The expression is natural under every ring homomorphism A→B, which sends its two coefficients to their images. The canonical morphism R×R→RD, transposed from (a,b,d)↦a+bd, is consequently a bijection at every stage CA​, with a natural inverse extracting the coefficients. Therefore
R×R∼​RD,(a,b)⟼(d↦a+bd).​
(46)
This proves the Kock-Lawvere axiom for the generic local ring. The uniqueness is an internal sheaf assertion tested at all finitely presented rings, including the ring with its generic square-zero element; restricting the test to fields would lose that element and would not prove uniqueness.
