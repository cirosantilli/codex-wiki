The fluctuation speed q and the integral scale of turbulence Lu​ give a turnover time Lu​/q. Dividing the velocity-variance scale q2 by that time gives a turbulent kinetic energy dissipation rate proportional to q3/Lu​. Likewise mass density variance ρ′2​ removed over scalar mixing time Lρ​/q gives the density-variance dissipation rate proportional to ρ′2​q/Lρ​. Absorbing dimensionless proportionality coefficients into the specified length scales gives
ϵ=Lu​q3​,χ=Lρ​ρ′2​q​.​
(36)
These are integral-scale closure estimates, not exact consequences of dimensional analysis alone. In particular q2=2k for the turbulent-energy convention in Question 3. The normalization of χ is the one fixed by the printed scalar balance; if the budget is written for the full variance rather than one half of it, both production and dissipation terms acquire a factor two.
