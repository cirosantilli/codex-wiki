For a target probability density function π and a proposal distribution with density q(x′∣x), the Metropolis–Hastings algorithm starts in the target support and repeats: propose x′∼q(⋅∣x), draw an independent uniform U, and move to x′ if
U≤α(x,x′),α(x,x′)=min(1,π(x)q(x′∣x)π(x′)q(x∣x′)​).​
(36)
Otherwise retain x. A zero reverse-proposal density makes the acceptance zero. Unknown multiplicative constants of π cancel. For distinct states,
π(x)q(x′∣x)α(x,x′)=min{π(x)q(x′∣x),π(x′)q(x∣x′)},
(37)
which is symmetric in the states. Together with the probability of holding at the current state, this proves detailed balance and invariance of π. Successive values form a dependent Markov chain; convergence from arbitrary initial states additionally needs the appropriate irreducibility and aperiodicity, not just this invariant-density identity.
The Gibbs sampler updates a coordinate using its full conditional distribution while keeping the others fixed. For coordinate i, write r=x−i​ and π(x)=π−i​(r)πi​(xi​∣r). Its proposal replaces xi​ by xi′​∼πi​(⋅∣r). The MH ratio, interpreted on this one-coordinate proposal space, is
π−i​(r)πi​(xi​∣r)πi​(xi′​∣r)π−i​(r)πi​(xi′​∣r)πi​(xi​∣r)​=1.
(38)
Thus every Gibbs coordinate update is an MH update with acceptance one. A random choice of coordinate gives a mixture of these invariant kernels; a deterministic sweep gives their composition. Both preserve π. The latter composition need not be reversible, so the special-case statement is about the coordinate kernels, not a claim that every entire systematic sweep is one reversible MH kernel.
