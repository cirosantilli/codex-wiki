Put θ0​=1 and define the autoregressive polynomial and moving-average polynomial by
Φ(z)=1−∑k=1p​ϕk​zk,Θ(z)=∑k=0q​θk​zk.
(1)
For a minimal, coprime ARMA representation, the usual simultaneous causality and invertibility root criteria for an ARMA model are
Φ(z)=0 and Θ(z)=0for all ∣z∣≤1.​
(2)
The autoregressive condition makes Θ/Φ a stable one-sided filter and gives the causal second-order stationary process. The moving-average condition makes its inverse a stable one-sided filter, so the white noise can be reconstructed from present and past observations. If the two polynomials have a common factor, first use common factor cancellation in an ARMA model; necessary-and-sufficient root statements apply to the reduced noise-driven representation. Stationarity here is second-order stationarity; weak white noise alone does not assert strict stationarity of all finite-dimensional laws.
For p=1, write ϕ=ϕ1​, so ∣ϕ∣<1. Expanding (1−ϕz)−1=∑r≥0​ϕrzr gives the Wold representation
Xt​=∑j≥0​cj​ϵt−j​,cj​=k=0∑min(j,q)​θk​ϕj−k.​
(3)
In particular c0​=1, cj​=ϕcj−1​+θj​ for 1≤j≤q, and cj​=ϕj−qcq​ for j≥q. The coefficients are square-summable, so this series converges in mean square. Causality and invertibility identify the driver with the linear innovation process, justifying the Wold terminology. For ϕ=0, powers with exponent zero equal one and the formula reduces to the finite moving average.
To obtain the covariance equations, subtract ϕXt−1​ and take covariance with Xt−k​. Orthogonality of the white noise and the causal expansion imply
Cov(ϵt−i​,Xt−k​)={σ2ci−k​,0,​i≥k,i<k.​
(4)
Thus the innovation coefficients of an ARMA(1,q) process give the entire triangular system in one formula:
γk​−ϕγk−1​=σ2i=k∑q​θi​ci−k​(0≤k≤q),γk​−ϕγk−1​=0(k>q).​
(5)
At k=0, use γ−1​=γ1​, since the autocovariance of a real stationary process is symmetric. The terms are consequently σ2(c0​+θ1​c1​+⋯+θq​cq​) at zero, σ2θq​c0​ at q, and the intervening partial sums as required.
For q=1, put θ=θ1​ and c1​=ϕ+θ. The equations become
γ0​−ϕγ1​=σ2(1+θϕ+θ2),γ1​−ϕγ0​=σ2θ,γk​=ϕγk−1​ (k≥2).
(6)
Substitution of the second into the first, followed by the geometric recursion, yields the autocovariance of a causal ARMA(1,1) process:
γ0​=1−ϕ2σ2(1+θ2+2ϕθ)​,γk​=1−ϕ2σ2(ϕ+θ)(1+ϕθ)​ϕ∣k∣−1(k=0).​
(7)
This includes ϕ=0: only lags ±1 survive, as for an MA(1) process.
