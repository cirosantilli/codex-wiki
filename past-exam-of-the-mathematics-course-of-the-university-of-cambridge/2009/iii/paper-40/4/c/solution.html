There is a genuine normalization error in the printed function. Write Yi​∼Unif(0,2) independently, g(y)=1/(π(1+y2)) and H(y)=y2−4/3. The internal sum produces a scalar; taking its mean does not divide by n. The literal returned value is therefore
Tβ,printed​=21​−2∑i=1n​{g(Yi​)−βH(Yi​)}.
(28)
Since EH=0 and Eg=arctan(2)/(2π)=1/4−θ/2, its expectation is
ETβ,printed​=nθ+21−n​.
(29)
It does not estimate θ unbiasedly unless n=1, and no fixed control variate coefficient removes this bias. In particular the actual call with β=0 returns
θˇprinted​=21​−2i=1∑n​π(1+Yi2​)1​.​
(30)
This is an instance of summing before taking a mean changes Monte Carlo normalization.
The intended corrected method replaces the sum by a sample mean:
Tβ​=21​−n2​∑i=1n​{g(Yi​)−βH(Yi​)}.
(31)
It uses symmetry to write the tail as θ=1/2−∫02​g(y)dy, then Monte Carlo integration on a bounded interval, and finally a mean-zero control variate. For β=0 the intended estimator is 1/2−(2/n)∑i​g(Yi​), whose summands are smooth and bounded. Keeping the average normalization, ETβ​=θ and
Var(Tβ​)=n4​{Var(g)−2βCov(g,H)+β2Var(H)}.
(32)
Differentiating this quadratic gives the Cauchy tail control variate on a bounded interval coefficient
β∗​=Var(H)Cov(g,H)​.
(33)
Here EY2=4/3, EY4=16/5, so Var(H)=64/45. Also
Cov(g,H)=2π1​∫02​1+y2y2−4/3​dy=π1​−6π7arctan2​=π1​−127​+67θ​.
(34)
Consequently
β∗​=6445​(π1​−127​+67θ​)≈−0.06528.​
(35)
The negative sign is consistent with g decreasing and H increasing. One may estimate the covariance and variance using a separate pilot sample and fix the resulting coefficient for a fresh sample. This coefficient minimizes the literal function's variance as well, but its missing factor of 1/n still leaves the bias above.
