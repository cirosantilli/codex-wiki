A branching process models reproduction through successive generations. In the Galton-Watson process used here, every individual independently produces a nonnegative integer number of offspring with a common offspring distribution; these reproduction counts are independent across individuals and generations. If Xn​ is the generation size, then
Xn+1​=∑j=1Xn​​ξn,j​,
(47)
where the ξn,j​ have that distribution and the empty sum is zero. Thus zero is absorbing: once a generation is empty, every subsequent generation is empty.
