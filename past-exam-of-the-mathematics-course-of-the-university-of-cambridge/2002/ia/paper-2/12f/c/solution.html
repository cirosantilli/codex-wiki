The binomial series gives
1−α(1−s)β=(1−α)+∑k≥1​α(−1)k+1(kβ​)sk.
(49)
Thus p0​=1−α>0 and, for k≥1,
pk​=k!αβ(1−β)(2−β)⋯(k−1−β)​>0,​
(50)
with the factors after β omitted for k=1. The signs follow from 0<β<1. As s↑1, the series has nonnegative terms and its sum increases to G(1)=1, so monotone convergence gives ∑k≥0​pk​=1. These are therefore genuine probabilities on the nonnegative integers, proving that G is a probability generating function. Its expected value is infinite, since G′(s)=αβ(1−s)β−1→∞; finiteness of the mean is not required for this branching process.
Write Gn​(s)=1−An​(1−s)βn. The composition formula gives An+1​=An​αβn, with A0​=1. Consequently An​=α1+β+⋯+βn−1, and summing the geometric series yields the explicit generation generating function
Gn​(s)=1−α(1−βn)/(1−β)(1−s)βn,n≥0.​
(51)
This is the fractional-power offspring generating function and its iterates.
