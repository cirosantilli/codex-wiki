Using Einstein summation convention and the contraction of two Levi-Civita symbols,
[a×(b×c)]i​​=ϵijk​ϵkℓm​aj​bℓ​cm​=(δiℓ​δjm​−δim​δjℓ​)aj​bℓ​cm​=bi​(a⋅c)−ci​(a⋅b).​
(15)
This proves the vector triple product identity directly from the Levi-Civita symbol and Kronecker delta.
Define the scalar triple product by [a,b,c]=a⋅(b×c). Its expression ϵijk​ai​bj​ck​ is unchanged by cyclically relabeling the three indices: a three-cycle has positive sign. Thus
[a,b,c]=[b,c,a]=[c,a,b].
(16)
In particular, let Δ=[e1​,e2​,e3​]. Since the three vectors form a basis, their determinant Δ is nonzero, so their reciprocal basis vectors are well defined. Cyclic invariance and vanishing of a scalar triple product with repeated vectors give
ei​⋅ej​=δij​.
(17)
To calculate the new scalar triple product explicitly, apply the already proved vector triple product identity with A=e3​×e1​:
e2​×e3​​=Δ2(e3​×e1​)×(e1​×e2​)​=Δ2[(e3​×e1​)⋅e2​]e1​−[(e3​×e1​)⋅e1​]e2​​=Δe1​​.​
(18)
Consequently
[e1​,e2​,e3​]=Δ1​=0,​
(19)
so the reciprocal vectors also form a basis. Taking the reciprocal basis of that basis now gives
e1​=[e1​,e2​,e3​]e2​×e3​​=1/Δe1​/Δ​=e1​.
(20)
The same cyclic calculation gives e2​=e2​ and e3​=e3​, proving reciprocal basis involution.
Finally, write V=V1​e1​+V2​e2​+V3​e3​. Taking a dot product with ej​ isolates Vj​. Applying the same argument to the reciprocal basis, whose reciprocal is the original basis, gives the two sets of coordinates:
Vj​=V⋅ej​,Vj​=V⋅ej​.​
(21)
No orthonormality assumption is needed; the reciprocal basis is what makes these coordinate-extraction formulas work.
