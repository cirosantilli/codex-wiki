In Einstein summation convention, the curl of a cross product has components
[∇×(a×b)]i​​=ϵijk​∂j​(ϵkℓm​aℓ​bm​)=∂j​(ai​bj​−aj​bi​)=ai​∂j​bj​−bi​∂j​aj​+bj​∂j​ai​−aj​∂j​bi​.​
(49)
The second line uses the contraction of two Levi-Civita symbols; the last uses the product rule. These components prove the curl of a cross product identity, including both divergence and directional-derivative terms.
Now apply that identity to m×v. On the orientable surface, m=n, m⋅v=0, and ∣m∣=1. Differentiating m⋅m=1 gives mi​∂j​mi​=0. Taking a dot product with m in the curl of a cross product formula therefore gives, on S,
n⋅∇×(m×v)​=∇⋅v−(m⋅v)∇⋅m+mi​vj​∂j​mi​−mi​mj​∂j​vi​=(δij​−ni​nj​)∂j​vi​.​
(50)
Only tangency on the surface is needed; no claim is made that the extension v is tangent away from it. The right side is the surface divergence of the tangent vector field.
With the boundary oriented according to Stokes theorem, cyclic invariance of the scalar triple product gives
(m×v)⋅dr=v⋅(dr×n)=v⋅uds,
(51)
where u is the outward conormal of a surface boundary. Thus Stokes theorem proves
∫S​(δij​−ni​nj​)∂j​vi​dS=∮C​u⋅vds.​
(52)
This is the surface divergence theorem for a tangent field.
For the disc in the z=0 plane, take n=(0,0,1) and v=r=(x,y,z). The field is tangent on the disc, and ∂j​vi​=δij​. The contraction is 3−∣n∣2=2, so the surface integral is 2πR2. On the positively oriented circular boundary, u=(cosθ,sinθ,0) and v=Ru, so the boundary flux is R(2πR)=2πR2. Both sides agree.
