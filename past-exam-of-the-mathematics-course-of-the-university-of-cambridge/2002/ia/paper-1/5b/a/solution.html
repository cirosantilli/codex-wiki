Take the scalene triangle vertex set T={(0,0),(1,0),(0,2)}. Its edge lengths are 1,2,5​. Each vertex has a different unordered pair of incident edge lengths. A Euclidean isometry preserving the set must preserve these pairs, and therefore fixes every vertex.
To justify rigidity of a scalene triangle fully, Euclidean distances from an arbitrary plane point to these three fixed points determine that point uniquely: subtracting its squared Euclidean distance to the origin from the other two squared Euclidean distances determines its two coordinates. Thus an isometry fixing the three vertices fixes every plane point. Only the identity preserves T.
