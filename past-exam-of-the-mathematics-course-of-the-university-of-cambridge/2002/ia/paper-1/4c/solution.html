Here is a nested-interval proof of the Bolzano-Weierstrass theorem. Put every term of the bounded real sequence in a closed interval I0​=[−M,M], with M>0. Bisect this interval and choose a closed half containing infinitely many terms. Repeating gives nested closed intervals Ij​=[lj​,rj​], each containing infinitely many sequence terms, with length 2M/2j.
By the least-upper-bound property, x∗​=supj​lj​ exists. For each j, every lower endpoint lies at most rj​, while lj​≤x∗​; hence x∗​∈Ij​. Choose increasing indices nj​ with anj​​∈Ij​, possible because each interval contains infinitely many terms. Then
∣anj​​−x∗​∣≤2M/2j⟶0.
(6)
Thus every bounded real sequence has a convergent subsequence.
For a sequence with no convergent subsequence, take an​=n: any subsequence has nj​≥j, so its terms tend to positive infinity rather than to a finite limit of a sequence. For an unbounded sequence with a convergent subsequence, take a2n​=0 and a2n−1​=n. Its odd terms are unbounded, while its even subsequence converges to zero.
