Use the least-upper-bound property as the least upper bound axiom: every nonempty set of real numbers bounded above has a least upper bound in R. It implies convergence of a bounded increasing sequence: if L is the supremum of its terms, then for every ε>0 some term exceeds L−ε, and all subsequent terms lie between that term and L. A bounded decreasing sequence converges by applying this argument to its negative.
The alternating series test states that if bn​≥0, bn+1​≤bn​, and bn​→0, then ∑n≥1​(−1)n−1bn​ converges. To prove it, let SN​ denote its partial sums. The even partial sums are increasing because S2m+2​−S2m​=b2m+1​−b2m+2​≥0. The odd partial sums are decreasing because S2m+3​−S2m+1​=−b2m+2​+b2m+3​≤0. Moreover,
0≤S2m​≤S2m+1​≤b1​.
(17)
Completeness of the real numbers therefore gives a limit of a sequence for each subsequence, denoted Le​,Lo​ respectively for the even and odd subsequences. Their difference is S2m+1​−S2m​=b2m+1​→0, so Le​=Lo​. Every sufficiently late partial sum belongs to one of these two subsequences, proving convergence of the full sequence of partial sums. Multiplying the terms by −1 leaves convergence unchanged.
