 ... Past exam of the mathematics course of the University of Cambridge 2002 ia Paper 1 Section II 10C
OurBigBook.com Words: 109
Let f be continuous on a closed bounded interval [a,b]. First prove that f is a bounded function. If it failed, we could choose xn​∈[a,b] with ∣f(xn​)∣>n. The Bolzano-Weierstrass theorem gives a subsequence xnj​​→x∗​. Because the interval is closed, x∗​∈[a,b]. Continuity gives f(xnj​​)→f(x∗​), contradicting ∣f(xnj​​)∣>nj​→∞.
Now let M=sup{f(x):x∈[a,b]}, which exists by completeness of the real numbers and the bound just proved. Choose yn​∈[a,b] with f(yn​)>M−1/n. A convergent subsequence has limit of a sequence y∗​∈[a,b], and continuity gives f(y∗​)=M. Applying the same argument to −f gives attainment of the infimum. This proves the extreme value theorem: the maximum and minimum both exist and are attained. The argument also covers a one-point interval, where the conclusions are immediate.
