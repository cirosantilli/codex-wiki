There is no density; scaling by two gives a law singular to Wiener measure. On [0,1] form the pathwise dyadic sums
Qn​(w)=∑k=12n​(w(k2−n)−w((k−1)2−n))2.
(37)
Under Wiener measure, independent normal increments give EQn​=1 and var(Qn​)=21−n. Chebyshev's inequality and the Borel-Cantelli lemma show Qn​→1 almost surely, because the variances are summable. For 2B, every sum is four times its Brownian counterpart and hence tends to four almost surely. The event that the limit equals one separates the two laws. Unlike the linear-drift case, this obstruction already occurs on a finite interval: it is the Pathwise quadratic variation distinguishes Brownian speeds argument.
