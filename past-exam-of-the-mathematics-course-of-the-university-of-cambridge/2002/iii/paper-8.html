For 0<q<∞, weak type with a normed domain means that there is a constant C independent of f∈E and a>0 such that
λ{x:∣Tf(x)∣>a}≤(aC∥f∥E​​)q​.
(1)
Here L0 consists of measurable functions modulo equality almost everywhere. A sublinear operator satisfies absolute homogeneity and the pointwise domination ∣T(f+g)∣≤∣Tf∣+∣Tg∣. In particular the definition controls the output's superlevel measures, not necessarily its strong Lq norm. At q=∞, weak type is interpreted as the bound ∥Tf∥∞​≤C∥f∥E​.
A useful theorem is dense approximation under a weak-type maximal bound. Let At​:E→L0 be linear operators indexed by parameters tending to zero, and let A0​:E→L0 be another linear operator. Suppose there is a nonnegative sublinear operator S of weak type (E,q) such that ∣At​f∣,∣A0​f∣≤Sf, simultaneously in t outside a null set for each input. Assume the suprema and limiting suprema used below are measurable. If At​g→A0​g almost everywhere for every g in a dense subspace D, then At​f→A0​f almost everywhere for every f∈E. For a sequence of operators all suprema are automatically countable; continuously parametrized averages below also have measurable suprema.
To prove the theorem, fix f and g∈D. Outside the exceptional sets for these inputs, linearity and domination give
limsupt→0​∣At​f−A0​f∣≤2S(f−g).
(2)
Thus for every a>0 the set where this limiting error exceeds a has measure at most (2C∥f−g∥E​/a)q. Choose a sequence gk​∈D converging to f in norm and take the union of their null exceptional sets. Letting k→∞ makes the bound zero. Taking countably many positive rational a proves convergence almost everywhere. This proves the requested sufficient condition rather than merely invoking a convergence theorem.
For the measure estimate, give finite signed measures the total variation norm of a measure ∥ν∥=∣ν∣(Rd). The uncentered maximal function of a finite measure is sublinear because ∣ν+η∣≤∣ν∣+∣η∣. Its set Ea​={mu​ν>a} is open: it is precisely the union of open balls whose variation mass exceeds a times their volume.
Fix a compact subset K⊂Ea​ and select a finite cover of K by these high-density balls. Order that finite collection by decreasing radius. Keep a ball if it is disjoint from all previously retained balls. Every discarded ball intersects a retained ball of at least its radius and is contained in that retained ball's triple dilation. The retained balls Bj​ are disjoint and their triples cover K. Therefore
λ(K)≤∑j​λ(3Bj​)=3d∑j​λ(Bj​)<a3d​∑j​∣ν∣(Bj​)≤a3d∥ν∥​.
(3)
By inner regularity of Lebesgue measure, take the supremum over compact K⊂Ea​ to obtain
λ{mu​ν>a}≤a3d​∥ν∥​.
(4)
This is the uncentered maximal weak-type inequality, and proves weak type (M(Rd),1) even when the measure has atoms.
Finally work in dimension one. For h=0 define the linear operator
Ah​f(x)=hF(x+h)−F(x)​=h1​∫xx+h​f(t)dt.
(5)
Its absolute value is bounded by mu​(fdt)(x): first enlarge the interval slightly to put x inside an open interval and then let the enlargement tend to zero. The endpoint masses vanish because fdt is absolutely continuous. Taking A0​f=f, both operators are dominated by Sf=mu​(fdt)+∣f∣. The maximal bound and the Markov inequality give λ{Sf>a}≤2(3+1)∥f∥1​/a.
Continuous compactly supported functions are dense in the L1 space, and their averages tend to their values as h→0 from either side. For a fixed x, the interval integral varies continuously for h=0, so the required suprema can be taken over rational h and are measurable. Apply the proved weak-type theorem to conclude Ah​f(x)→f(x) almost everywhere. Hence
F′(x)=f(x)for almost every x​.

For example identification follows by selecting an almost-everywhere convergent subsequence from the L2 convergence. Hence

The convolutions converge uniformly under this approximation by the same Cauchy-Schwarz bound, and the multiplier outputs converge in L2 by their uniform operator bound, so these two definitions agree. If “bump function” means merely smooth with compact support, the stated limit needs the unit-integral qualification. For arbitrary such ϕ, the limit is (∫ϕ)Tf instead. This is also the almost-everywhere limit: at each Lebesgue point of Tf, subtract (∫ϕ)Tf(x) inside the convolution and bound the remaining local average by the boundedness and compact support of ϕ. As a concrete counterexample without normalization, take K(x)=1/(πx) in dimension one and ϕ=2ρ for a unit-integral mollifier ρ. The uncentered maximal weak-type inequality gives the weak L1 bound with Cd​=3d, and plainly Mf≤∥f∥∞​ for bounded inputs. For f∈Lp, split it into its parts on {∣f∣>a/2} and {∣f∣≤a/2}.
