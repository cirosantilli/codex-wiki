Use Minkowski spacetime signature (+,−) and put κ=∣λ∣>0. The Euler-Lagrange field equation for the real scalar field is
ϕtt​−ϕxx​+2κ2ϕ(ϕ2−1)=0.​
(1)
Indeed, differentiating the potential U(ϕ)=κ2(ϕ2−1)2/2 gives U′=2κ2ϕ(ϕ2−1), while the kinetic term contributes ∂μ​∂μϕ.
Translation invariance gives the symmetric stress-energy tensor
Tμν=∂μϕ∂νϕ−ημνL.
(2)
Its divergence is (∂μ​∂μϕ+U′)∂νϕ, which vanishes by the Euler-Lagrange field equation. Consequently the conserved energy and physical spatial momentum are
E=∫R​[21​ϕt2​+21​ϕx2​+2κ2​(ϕ2−1)2]dx,P=−∫R​ϕt​ϕx​dx.​
(3)
The minus sign in P=∫T01dx follows from ∂1=−∂x​. Conservation assumes the associated stress-energy fluxes vanish at spatial infinity, as they do for the localized fields below.
A static finite-energy field configuration approaches vacua ϕ=±1. Multiplying ϕ′′=U′(ϕ) by ϕ′ gives
21​ϕ′2−U(ϕ)=constant=0,
(4)
where the vacuum limits fix the constant. For a kink increasing from −1 to 1, ϕ′=κ(1−ϕ2). Integration gives artanhϕ=κ(x−X) and hence
ϕK​(x)=tanh[κ(x−X)].​
(5)
Its negative is the antikink. The arbitrary center X is a translational collective coordinate. The static kink mass is its rest energy; using ϕ′2=2U and y=κ(x−X) gives
M=∫R​ϕ′2dx=κ∫−∞∞​sech4ydy=κ∫−11​(1−z2)dz=34κ​​.
(6)
Equivalently, completing the static energy into a square gives E=21​∫[ϕ′−κ(1−ϕ2)]2dx+κ[ϕ−ϕ3/3]−∞∞​, so this kink saturates its Bogomolny bound.
A Lorentz boost with velocity v gives
ϕ(t,x)=tanh[κγ(x−X−vt)],γ=(1−v2)−1/2.​
(7)
Writing z=γ(x−X−vt), the derivatives are ϕt​=−γvϕK′​(z) and ϕx​=γϕK′​(z). The momentum integral therefore becomes
P=γ2v∫ϕK′​(z)2γdz​=γMv​.
(8)
The boosted energy is
E=21​[γ(1+v2)+γ−1]∫ϕK′​(z)2dz=γM​,
(9)
because γ−1=γ(1−v2). Thus E2−P2=M2 and P/E=v, exactly the energy–momentum relation of a relativistic particle of rest mass 4κ/3. This is the relativistic energy and momentum of a phi-four kink. If λ=0, the double-well potential disappears and there is no localized static kink connecting these vacua.

The Bogomolny equations are first-order equations obtained by expressing a static energy as nonnegative squares plus a term fixed by the topological charge. Vanishing of the squares minimizes the energy in that topological sector. In particular, the solutions satisfy the full second-order field equations, since their first variation vanishes for compactly supported variations preserving the boundary data. Both monopole and vortex realizations illustrate this mechanism.
For a Yang-Mills theory with gauge group SU(2) and an adjoint Higgs field Φa, take the static, purely magnetic energy
E=∫R3​[21​Bia​Bia​+21​(Di​Φ)a(Di​Φ)a+4λH​​(ΦaΦa−vH2​)2]d3x.
(10)
Here Bia​=21​ϵijk​Fjka​, and e is the gauge coupling. In the Bogomolny limit λH​=0, retain the boundary condition ∣Φ∣→vH​>0; it breaks SU(2) to U(1) at infinity even though the scalar potential has been removed. The normalized Higgs field on the sphere at infinity defines a map S∞2​→S2 of topological degree n. In a consistent magnetic orientation the asymptotic flux is gm​=4πn/e, and
∫S∞2​​ΦaBia​dSi​=vH​gm​.
(11)
For example, this equality follows by projecting the asymptotic gauge curvature on the Higgs direction: its flux is the integral of the target-sphere area form, divided by e.
The gauge-theory Bianchi identity Di​Bi​=0 makes the cross term a divergence:
Bia​(Di​Φ)a=∂i​(ΦaBia​).
(12)
For either sign s=±1, complete the square:
E=21​∫R3​∣B−sDΦ∣2d3x+s∫S∞2​​Φ⋅BdS.
(13)
Choosing s=sgnn gives
E≥e4πvH​​∣n∣,Bi​=sDi​Φ at equality.​
(14)
These are the Bogomolny-Prasad-Sommerfield monopole equations. A nonzero scalar potential would add a positive term; a nontrivial smooth monopole with a core cannot generally saturate this same bound at λH​>0. The Bogomolny monopole equations imply Yang-Mills-Higgs equations: the Bianchi identity already gives Di​Di​Φ=0, and the square-completion argument gives stationarity with respect to the gauge field as well.
An explicit charge-one solution is furnished by the hedgehog ansatz for a monopole. To fix its signs, use component conventions Di​Φ=∂i​Φ−eAi​×Φ and Fij​=∂i​Aj​−∂j​Ai​−eAi​×Aj​. With ρ=evH​r and x^=x/r, put
Φa=vH​H(ρ)x^a,Aia​=er1−K(ρ)​ϵiaj​x^j.
(15)
The radial and transverse parts of Di​Φ are evH2​H′x^i​x^a and vH​HK(δia​−x^i​x^a)/r. The corresponding parts of Bia​ are (1−K2)x^i​x^a/(er2) and −vH​K′(δia​−x^i​x^a)/r. Therefore B=DΦ reduces to
dρdK​=−KH,dρdH​=ρ21−K2​.
(16)
The Prasad-Sommerfield radial monopole solution is
K(ρ)=sinhρρ​,H(ρ)=cothρ−ρ1​.​
(17)
Direct differentiation verifies both equations. Near ρ=0, K=1−ρ2/6+O(ρ4) and H=ρ/3+O(ρ3), so the apparent angular singularities disappear and the fields are smooth. At infinity K decays exponentially while H=1−1/ρ+O(e−2ρ), leaving an Abelian 1/(er2) magnetic field and mass 4πvH​/e.
Translations of this magnetic monopole give three collective coordinates; a residual U(1) phase provides another in the framed description, where gauge transformations are fixed at infinity. Multimonopole solutions of a common sign also saturate the linear bound. Their widely separated constituents have position and phase parameters, with no static force: the magnetic repulsion and massless-Higgs attraction cancel in this limit. Relative motion is described at low speeds by the moduli-space approximation. An opposite-sign monopole pair does not solve one common sign of the Bogomolny equations and is not protected by that no-force argument.
For the Abelian Higgs model, a second realization occurs in the plane. Let ψ be a complex Higgs field, Di​=∂i​−ieAi​, B=∂1​A2​−∂2​A1​, and choose the critically coupled static energy
E=∫R2​[21​B2+∣Di​ψ∣2+2e2​(∣ψ∣2−vH2​)2]d2x.
(18)
Different conventions for the scalar kinetic coefficient change the numerical coefficients of both the bound and the equations. This normalization gives scalar and gauge masses 2​evH​, explaining the critical balance of their long-distance forces. In three spatial dimensions the planar solutions describe straight strings with this energy per unit length.
Finite energy requires ∣ψ∣→vH​ and Di​ψ→0. On a large circle the Higgs phase winds by 2πn, so the magnetic flux quantization of an Abelian Higgs vortex gives
ΦB​=∫Bd2x=∮Ai​dxi=e2πn​.
(19)
Writing Ji​=Im(ψˉ​Di​ψ), expansion and integration by parts give
∣D1​ψ∣2+∣D2​ψ∣2=∣(D1​+iD2​)ψ∣2+eB∣ψ∣2+∂1​J2​−∂2​J1​.
(20)
The divergence integrates to zero for the usual localized vortex boundary conditions. Thus the Bogomolny square completion for an Abelian Higgs vortex is
E=∫R2​[∣(D1​+iD2​)ψ∣2+21​{B−e(vH2​−∣ψ∣2)}2]d2x+evH2​ΦB​.
(21)
For n≥0 the Bogomolny vortex equations and bound are
(D1​+iD2​)ψ=0,B=e(vH2​−∣ψ∣2),E=2πvH2​n.​
(22)
For n<0, reverse the sign in both first-order equations and obtain E=2πvH2​∣n∣.
For a rotationally symmetric positive vortex number, use ψ=vH​f(r)einθ and the physical angular component Aθ​=na(r)/(er). The Bogomolny vortex equations become
f′=rn(1−a)​f,a′=ne2vH2​r​(1−f2),
(23)
with f(0)=a(0)=0 and f(∞)=a(∞)=1. The regular core behaves as f∼crn and a∼e2vH2​r2/(2n). Linearization at infinity gives exponentially decaying massive tails. Unlike the radial monopole, these planar radial profiles have no elementary closed form in general.
The general multi-vortex solution can be encoded by the Taubes equation. Away from zeros, write ψ=vH​eh/2+iχ. The first equation gives
A1​=e1​(∂1​χ+21​∂2​h),A2​=e1​(∂2​χ−21​∂1​h).
(24)
At zeros za​ of multiplicity ma​, the phase contributes circulation 2πma​. The second equation becomes, including these Dirac delta terms,
Δh+2e2vH2​(1−eh)=4π∑a​ma​δ(2)(x−za​),h→0 at infinity.
(25)
For prescribed zeros the planar existence result gives a solution; the prescribed-zero construction of planar Abelian Higgs vortices reconstructs smooth fields, with h=2ma​log∣x−za​∣+O(1) near each zero. Uniqueness is visible directly: for two solutions with the same zeros, their nonsingular difference w satisfies Δw=2e2vH2​(eh1​−eh2​). Multiplying by w and integrating gives
−∫∣∇w∣2d2x=2e2vH2​∫w(eh1​−eh2​)d2x≥0,
(26)
hence w=0. The Abelian Higgs vortex moduli space has arbitrary unordered positions of the n zeros, giving 2n real position parameters. The saturated energy is independent of their positions, so critically coupled vortices have no static interaction energy. Away from critical coupling this cancellation is lost.

The topological degree assigns an integer to a continuous map f:M→N between closed, connected, oriented manifolds of the same dimension d. Their top homology groups are infinite cyclic, generated by their fundamental classes. The induced map defines
f∗​[M]=(degf)[N],degf∈Z.​
(27)
For a smooth map and a regular value y, its equivalent local description is
degf=∑x∈f−1(y)​sgndet(dfx​),
(28)
where the sign records preservation or reversal of orientation. The preimage is a finite set because the manifolds are compact and y is regular. This is a signed count; opposite-orientation preimages cancel. If ω is a normalized target volume form with ∫N​ω=1, the pullback of a differential form gives the equivalent integral degf=∫M​f∗ω.
The homotopy invariance of mapping degree follows because homotopic maps induce the same homology map. In the smooth description, apply Stokes theorem to a homotopy F:M×[0,1]→N:
∫M​f1∗​ω−∫M​f0∗​ω=∫M×[0,1]​d(F∗ω)=0,
(29)
since a top-dimensional form on N is closed. Thus degree cannot change during a smooth deformation preserving the relevant boundary conditions. Degrees multiply under composition, and a nonzero-degree map is surjective: a missed target point would give a regular value with an empty preimage and hence degree zero.
For maps Sd→Sd, the homotopy group πd​(Sd)=Z is completely detected by degree. This supplies integer topological sectors for many classical field-theory solitons. It is important to distinguish two ways spheres arise. In a nonlinear sigma model with the field approaching one fixed target value at infinity, compactify all of Rd to Sd and classify the resulting map into the target. For a defect whose vacuum field varies with direction, retain the sphere S∞d−1​ surrounding the core and map it into the vacuum manifold. This is the vacuum-boundary degree as a defect charge. An asymptotic limit is a boundary hypothesis; finite energy alone in every possible model need not imply one.
For an Abelian Higgs vortex, the phase at infinity defines S1→U(1)≅S1. Its degree is the winding number
n=2π1​∮dχ=2πe​∫Bd2x.
(30)
Nonzero n forces a zero of the Higgs field inside: a nowhere-vanishing field would extend its normalized phase through the disk and make the boundary loop contractible. This is also the flux classification of the Bogomolny vortex equations.
For a 't Hooft-Polyakov monopole, the normalized adjoint Higgs field Φ^ gives S∞2​→SU(2)/U(1)≅S2. With outward orientation its degree is
n=8π1​∫S∞2​​ϵabc​Φ^adΦ^b∧dΦ^c.
(31)
The integrand is the normalized target area form, so the hedgehog ansatz for a monopole has degree one. Nonzero degree obstructs extending the normalized Higgs direction through the ball; the full Higgs field must leave the vacuum manifold, normally vanishing in its core. The asymptotic unbroken Abelian magnetic flux measures the same integer with a convention-dependent sign.
For the Skyrme model, a field U:R3→SU(2) with U→1 at infinity gives S3→S3, because SU(2) as the three-sphere identifies the target. A choice of orientations gives its topological baryon number in the Skyrme model as the winding integral ±(24π2)−1∫tr(U−1dU)3; specifying the orientation fixes the sign. Similarly, the transition map at infinity of a four-dimensional Yang-Mills instanton has degree in π3​(SU(2)), measured by its Chern charge.
A scalar-field kink illustrates the discrete-vacuum case: its endpoint data lie in π0​({±1}), rather than being a map between connected positive-dimensional spheres. For the double-well field its topological charge is Q=[ϕ(+∞)−ϕ(−∞)]/2. A kink has Q=1, an antikink Q=−1; they cannot individually deform to vacuum while their endpoints are fixed.
Degree classifies topology, rather than every solution. Smooth motion or local perturbations with fixed boundary data preserve it, and a nonzero degree obstructs decay to the vacuum within that class. It does not guarantee existence of a smooth energy minimizer, a fixed size, uniqueness, or dynamical stability. The degree and energetic stability of a field configuration are distinct: a field can shrink towards a singular limit at fixed degree, as Derrick's theorem diagnoses in many two-derivative theories. Moreover degree need not classify all homotopy classes for an arbitrary target; other homotopy groups or invariants may be needed. Within one degree there can be continuous moduli spaces and several stationary solutions.

Use an SU(2) connection one-form A=Aμ​dxμ with Skew-Hermitian generators Ta​=−iσa​/2, so tr(Ta​Tb​)=−δab​/2. Absorb the gauge coupling into A, and write its gauge curvature as F=dA+A∧A. The Yang-Mills gauge transformation convention is Ah=h−1Ah+h−1dh, giving Fh=h−1Fh.
In four dimensions the Second Chern form and Second Chern number of the associated fundamental rank-two bundle are
c2​(A)=8π21​tr(F∧F),kC​=∫M​c2​(A)∈Z.
(32)
Here M is a closed oriented four-manifold. This sign follows by expanding the total Chern class det(1+iF/(2π)) and using trF=0. The first Chern class vanishes for SU(2); the second is the degree-four characteristic class relevant here. With the same curvature convention the commonly positive self-dual instanton number is
Q=−8π21​∫M​tr(F∧F)=−kC​.
(33)
Both sign conventions occur; fixing them at the outset avoids identifying opposite-oriented integers.
The form is gauge-invariant by conjugation and cyclicity of the matrix trace. The gauge-theory Bianchi identity DA​F=0 makes it closed. To see that its integral is independent of the connection, vary A:
δF=DA​δA,δtr(F∧F)=2dtr(δA∧F).
(34)
On a closed M the integral of this exact form vanishes. This is Chern-Weil connection transgression, showing that kC​ depends on the bundle's topology rather than on a particular gauge field.
Locally the same four-form is an exterior derivative. The Chern-Simons 3-form
ω3​(A)=tr(A∧dA+32​A∧A∧A)
(35)
satisfies
dω3​=tr(F∧F).
(36)
For verification, differentiating gives tr(dA∧dA+2dA∧A∧A); expansion of F∧F gives these terms and trA4. Graded cyclicity moves the first one-form through the other three, changing the sign, so trA4=0. This local exactness does not make the Second Chern number zero on every closed manifold: on a nontrivial bundle the gauge potentials and ω3​ are only patchwise defined.
For the usual finite-action sector on R4, impose pure-gauge behavior A→h−1dh at infinity. Compactification gives a bundle over S4, with transition map h:S3→SU(2). The Maurer-Cartan equation gives d(h−1dh)=−(h−1dh)2, hence
ω3​(h−1dh)=−31​tr(h−1dh)3.
(37)
With the outward boundary orientation, Stokes theorem therefore gives
kC​=−24π21​∫S∞3​​tr(h−1dh)3,Q=24π21​∫S∞3​​tr(h−1dh)3.
(38)
These are opposite choices of the winding number, and either becomes the topological degree of h after specifying the orientation of the target SU(2) sphere. Thus integrality also follows from π3​(SU(2))=Z. Smooth deformations preserving the asymptotic sector cannot change it.
The positive Euclidean Yang-Mills action is
S=−g21​∫M​tr(F∧∗F)=4g21​∫M​Fμνa​Fμνa​d4x.
(39)
Define ∥X∥2=−∫tr(X∧∗X). Since the Hodge star has square one on two-forms in Euclidean dimension four,
S=2g21​∥F−s∗F∥2+g28π2s​Q,s=±1.
(40)
Choosing s=sgnQ gives the Yang-Mills instanton Bogomolny bound
S≥g28π2​∣Q∣,F=s∗F at equality.​
(41)
The self-dual Yang-Mills equations and their anti-self-dual version imply the Yang-Mills equations DA​∗F=0 by the Bianchi identity. Their finite-action solutions are Yang-Mills instantons.
For a concrete self-dual example, the BPST instanton with center a and size ρ>0 has
Aμa​=∣x−a∣2+ρ22ημνa​(x−a)ν​,Fμνa​=−(∣x−a∣2+ρ2)24ρ2ημνa​​,
(42)
where ημνa​ are self-dual 't Hooft symbols. Their contraction is ∑a,μ,ν​(ημνa​)2=12. Thus Fμνa​Fμνa​=192ρ4/(∣x−a∣2+ρ2)4, and
∫R4​(∣x∣2+ρ2)4ρ4d4x​=2π2∫0∞​(r2+ρ2)4ρ4r3dr​=6π2​.
(43)
This gives S=8π2/g2 and Q=1. Reversing duality gives Q=−1. The free center, size and framed gauge orientation illustrate the instanton moduli space.
On a three-dimensional slice Σ, there is no four-form Chern integral intrinsic to the slice. Its transgression is the Chern-Simons number of a gauge field, in the instanton-charge convention
NCS​[A]=−8π21​∫Σ​ω3​(A).
(44)
It is a real number for a general connection, not necessarily an integer, and is not strictly gauge invariant. Set θ=h−1dh and θˉ=dhh−1. Substituting Ah=h−1Ah+θ, using dθ=−θ2 and graded trace cyclicity, gives the gauge change of the Chern-Simons three-form
ω3​(Ah)=ω3​(A)−dtr(θˉ∧A)−31​trθ3.
(45)
The mixed terms collect into the displayed exact form; the last term is also fixed by the pure-gauge case A=0. Therefore on a closed Σ, or with boundary conditions making the exact-form integral vanish,
NCS​[Ah]=NCS​[A]+w(h),w(h)=24π21​∫Σ​tr(h−1dh)3∈Z.​
(46)
Thus NCS​modZ, or exp(2πiNCS​), is gauge invariant. Gauge maps homotopic to the identity have w=0; maps of nonzero winding are large transformations even when they equal the identity at infinity.
For a Yang-Mills vacuum on R3, zero magnetic energy requires Fij​=0. On simply connected space this is a pure gauge A=h−1dh, and NCS​=w(h) is an integer after fixing the asymptotic trivialization. Quotienting only by transformations homotopic to the identity leaves integer-labelled vacuum representatives. Allowing all winding transformations identifies them classically; quantum states can instead transform by a phase, producing the theta vacuum. A three-dimensional pure-gauge representative carries a winding number but is not a localized positive-energy monopole. In fact no static finite-energy lump in three-dimensional pure Yang-Mills theory exists with the usual decay conditions: Ai(s)​(x)=sAi​(sx) gives E(s)=sE(1), and stationarity forces E=0.
Finally, on an oriented spacetime slab [t−​,t+​]×Σ, with no side-boundary contribution, the transgression identity gives
Q=NCS​(t+​)−NCS​(t−​).​
(47)
A Yang-Mills instanton therefore interpolates between vacuum representatives whose Chern-Simons numbers differ by its integer charge. In a four-dimensional quantum theory a Yang-Mills theta term weights such sectors by eiθQ, making θ periodic modulo 2π. In an intrinsically three-dimensional theory an action 2πℓNCS​ has a gauge-invariant phase eiS only for integer level ℓ under all winding gauge maps. These statements distinguish the gauge-invariant integer four-dimensional Chern charge from the three-dimensional connection-dependent quantity defined modulo integers.
