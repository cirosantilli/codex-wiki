We first extend the scalar Hadamard three-circle theorem to a holomorphic Banach-space-valued function F. Apply its inequality to ℓ(F(z)) for every bounded linear functional ℓ of norm at most one, and use the Hahn-Banach theorem to recover the norm. For the radii 1/R,1,R this gives

Now take F(z)=p(z)2n. Even for noncommuting coefficients this is an algebra-valued holomorphic polynomial. Taking the 2−nth power gives
∥p(1)2n∥2/2n≤max∣z∣=R​∥p(z)2n∥1/2nmax∣z∣=1/R​∥p(z)2n∥1/2n.
(26)
On each boundary circle the continuous functions fn​(z)=∥p(z)2n∥1/2n decrease, by submultiplicativity, to rA​(p(z)) by the spectral radius formula. Their maxima decrease to the supremum of this pointwise limit. To justify that passage without assuming continuity of the spectral radius, take maximizing points along a convergent subsequence on the compact circle. For any fixed k, all later fn​ are bounded by fk​; continuity of fk​ therefore bounds the limiting maxima by fk​ at the limiting point. Taking the infimum over k gives the desired upper bound, while the reverse bound is immediate.
Passing to the limit proves the spectral-radius three-circle inequality at the geometric mean:

(27)
For a nonunital algebra, use its unitization of an algebra; the spectral radius of an original algebra element is unchanged. Zero boundary suprema cause no difficulty in the decreasing-limit argument.
