Here is a direct verification of the algebra properties and uniqueness using the allowed Runge theorem. The version required is that every holomorphic function on an open plane set is a locally uniform limit of rational functions with poles outside that set, allowing a polynomial part. A rational function r=p/q of this kind has the unambiguous value r(x)=p(x)q(x)−1, since the roots of q avoid the Banach algebra spectrum. These rational values form a unital algebra homomorphism.

For the exponential conclusion, let K=σA​(x). There is a simple polygonal arc from zero through its unbounded complementary component to outside a disk containing K, continued by a ray to infinity and avoiding K. Loops in an initial polygonal path can be removed to make the arc simple. The complement of this slit is a simply connected open neighborhood of K avoiding zero, and hence admits a holomorphic logarithm ℓ. Set y=Θx​(ℓ). The composition rule gives

The calculus of the entire exponential agrees with the usual algebra exponential because its power-series partial sums converge locally uniformly. Finally an invertible matrix over the complex numbers has a finite Banach algebra spectrum avoiding zero. Removing finitely many plane points leaves a connected set, so the hypothesis always holds: every invertible matrix over the complex numbers is an exponential of a matrix over the complex numbers.
