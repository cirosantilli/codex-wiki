Put K=σ(T) and use the continuous functional calculus π:C(K)→B(H). It is a unital star-homomorphism taking the coordinate function to T. This follows from the commutative C-star theorem applied to C∗(I,T,T∗), together with spectral permanence for C-star subalgebras, which is among the permitted C-star results.
Take the Hilbert space inner product linear in its first variable. For a vector ξ, let Hξ​={π(f)ξ:f∈C(K)}​. This cyclic subspace is invariant under every π(f) and its adjoint, so it is reducing. The functional f↦⟨π(f)ξ,ξ⟩ is positive because f≥0 implies π(f)=π(f​)∗π(f​). It has, by the Riesz-Markov-Kakutani representation theorem, a finite regular positive Borel measure μξ​ on K. The identity
∥π(f)ξ∥2=∫K​∣f∣2dμξ​
(17)
makes f↦π(f)ξ an isometry on its quotient by the functions of zero L2 norm. Density of continuous functions in L2(μξ​) extends it to a unitary Uξ​:L2(μξ​)→Hξ​. Multiplication by a continuous function intertwines with π on this subspace.
Choose a maximal family of mutually orthogonal nonzero cyclic reducing subspaces. Their orthogonal sum is all of H: otherwise its reducing orthogonal complement contains a nonzero vector generating another such subspace. This argument does not require separability. For a bounded Borel measurable function g on K, multiplication Mg​ on every L2(μξ​) is bounded with norm at most ∥g∥∞​. Define on that orthogonal sum
βT​(g)=ξ⨁​Uξ​Mg​Uξ−1​.​
(18)
Multiplication operators satisfy Mfg​=Mf​Mg​, Mg​​=Mg∗​ and M1​=I. The same identities hold for their direct sums, and the norm bound is uniform in ξ. Thus βT​ is a norm-decreasing unital star-homomorphism on the algebra of actual bounded Borel measurable functions, and βT​(Z)=T because it extends π. No quotient by a single unspecified measure is being substituted for that Borel algebra.
Choose the bounded Borel measurable square root s(0)=0 and s(z)=∣z∣1/2eiArg(z)/2 for z=0, taking Arg∈[0,2π). Let S=βT​(s). Then
S2=T,S∗S=βT​(∣s∣2)=SS∗.​
(19)
Thus every bounded normal operator has a normal square root. The Borel branch is essential in general: on a circle winding about zero a continuous square-root branch need not exist.
