Write the diagonal maximal torus of U(n) as t=diag(z1​,…,zn​), ∣zi​∣=1, and let δ=(n−1,n−2,…,0). Irreducible complex representations are indexed by dominant integer tuples λ1​≥⋯≥λn​, their highest weights. The Weyl character formula in integer-exponent form is
χλ​(t)=det(zin−j​)i,j=1n​det(ziλj​+n−j​)i,j=1n​​.​
(12)
The ratio initially uses distinct zi​, and extends to repeated eigenvalues by continuity. Every unitary matrix is conjugate to such a diagonal matrix, so this determines the character on the entire group. The shift δ differs from the usual Weyl vector ρ=((n−1)/2,(n−3)/2,…,(1−n)/2) by a multiple of (1,…,1); the corresponding determinant factors cancel between numerator and denominator.
We first justify the highest-weight information needed in the proof. Restriction to the torus splits a representation into integer weight spaces. In an irreducible representation choose a lexicographically largest weight λ and a nonzero vector v of that weight. A matrix unit Eij​ with i<j raises the weight by ei​−ej​, so it annihilates v. The character is invariant under permutations of diagonal coordinates, because permutation matrices conjugate the torus. Hence permutations of λ are also weights, and lexical maximality forces λ1​≥⋯≥λn​.
Irreducibility implies that v generates the representation under the complexified Lie algebra. Indeed, a Lie-algebra invariant subspace is preserved by exponentials of skew-Hermitian matrices, which generate U(n). Reorder any product of matrix units into lower-triangular units, diagonal units and upper-triangular units, using
[Eij​,Ekl​]=δjk​Eil​−δli​Ekj​.
(13)
Induction on length and on the number of out-of-order pairs gives the required spanning version of the Poincaré-Birkhoff-Witt theorem. Upper-triangular units kill v, and diagonal units act by scalars. Every nonempty product of lower-triangular units lowers its weight by a nonzero sum of positive roots. Therefore the top weight space is exactly Cv, and its multiplicity is one. This supplies the leading coefficient without assuming a character formula.
For completeness, every dominant integer tuple occurs. Put dj​=λj​−λj+1​≥0 and form the representation
(det)λn​⊗⨂j=1n−1​(ΛjCn)⊗dj​.
(14)
Its lexicographically highest weight is λ, with one-dimensional highest space, represented by the tensor products of e1​∧⋯∧ej​. A negative determinant exponent is a legitimate one-dimensional unitary-group character. Averaging an inner product over normalized Haar measure gives invariant orthogonal complements, so the tensor representation splits into irreducible summands. One of them has highest weight λ. Uniqueness will also follow from the formula and Schur orthogonality relations.
Define the alternant Aκ​(z)=det(ziκj​​) for a strictly decreasing integer tuple κ. The denominator
Aδ​(z)=∏i<j​(zi​−zj​)
(15)
is the Vandermonde determinant, and its modulus is the denominator modulus in the Weyl integration formula. The Laurent polynomial χλ​Aδ​ is alternating. Any alternating Laurent polynomial decomposes as a finite sum
χλ​Aδ​=∑κ​cκ​Aκ​:
(16)
monomials with repeated exponents have zero coefficient, and the remaining permutation orbits are indexed uniquely by strictly decreasing tuples.
The lexicographically highest monomial in the product is zλ+δ with coefficient one. Indeed, the highest monomial of the character is zλ with coefficient one, and the unique lexical maximum among permutations of δ is δ itself. It follows that cλ+δ​=1.
Fourier orthogonality on the torus, whose probability measure is ∏i​dθi​/(2π), gives alternant orthogonality on a unitary torus:
∫T​Aκ​Aη​​dt=n!δκη​.
(17)
Expand both determinants: an integral survives exactly when the two exponent tuples are identical up to permutation. Strict decreasing order then forces κ=η, and its n! matching terms each contribute one.
Finally, Schur orthogonality relations give ∫U(n)​∣χλ​∣2=1. Applying the allowed Weyl integration formula to this class function gives
1=n!1​∫T​∣χλ​Aδ​∣2dt=∑κ​∣cκ​∣2.
(18)
Since the coefficient cλ+δ​ already equals one, every other coefficient vanishes. Thus χλ​Aδ​=Aλ+δ​, proving the formula. If two irreducible representations had the same highest weight, their characters would now coincide; the Schur orthogonality relations imply that they are isomorphic. Apparent singularities at repeated diagonal eigenvalues are removable because the quotient equals the actual finite Laurent-polynomial character. No dimension formula has been assumed.
