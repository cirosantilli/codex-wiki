For a real inner product space V=R5, define
Φ(u∧v)(w)=⟨v,w⟩u−⟨u,w⟩v.
(21)
This map takes values in the Special orthogonal Lie algebra: its matrices are skew-symmetric. It sends ei​∧ej​ to Eij​−Eji​, so it is an isomorphism between the two ten-dimensional spaces. For g∈SO(5), invariance of the inner product gives
Φ(gu∧gv)=gΦ(u∧v)g−1.
(22)
This proves the exterior square realization of the orthogonal adjoint representation, including equivariance, rather than merely matching dimensions.
After complexification, part (a) is therefore the weight decomposition of the Adjoint representation. Its zero weight space is the complex centralizer of t. The two zero-weight wedges are nonzero multiples of e1​∧e2​ and e3​∧e4​, which map under Φ to the two infinitesimal rotation generators of T. Hence
Zso(5)​(t)=t.
(23)
If a larger torus contained T, its Lie algebra would commute with t and therefore lie in t. Equality of Lie algebras and connectedness of both tori force equality of the groups. Thus T is a maximal torus.
Let εj​ denote the real coordinate functional xj​. In the real weight convention, with the common factor i removed from the differentiated torus weights, the root system is
Φ={±ε1​,±ε2​,±(ε1​+ε2​),±(ε1​−ε2​)}.​
(24)
These are the roots of a root system of type B2 root system. In the imaginary-valued compact Lie-algebra convention each displayed functional is multiplied by i. Every root space is one-dimensional, as part (a) explicitly shows.
