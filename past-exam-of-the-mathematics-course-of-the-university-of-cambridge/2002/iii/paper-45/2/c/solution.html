For the growing density mode, write δ(x,t)=b(t)δ∗​(x). The new Poisson equation gives ∇x2​ψ=δ∗​ with no time dependence. With periodic zero-mean or fixed decaying boundary conditions, this determines a time-independent ψ up to a spatially constant, dynamically irrelevant function. Thus
∂t​ψ=0for the growing density mode, with fixed boundary conditions.​
(17)
In the linear continuity equation, ∂b​δ=δ∗​, so ∇x​⋅u=−δ∗​=−∇x2​ψ. For the pure longitudinal growing mode, this gives u=−∇x​ψ and hence a time-independent scaled velocity. Equivalently, neglect the quadratic advection term in the motion equation and put h=u+∇x​ψ. It obeys
dbdh​=−2bf23Ω​h,h(x,t)=a2b˙C(x)​.

The second expression follows directly from b¨+2Hb˙=Cb/a3: its logarithmic derivative with respect to b is the negative coefficient in the first equation. Excluding the homogeneous decaying-velocity component, C=0, yields the pure growing-mode scaled velocity
∂b​u=0,∂t​u=0.​
(19)
The same holds along a trajectory to first order, since u⋅∇u is second order.
The pure growing-velocity assumption matters. A growing density alone does not exclude a divergence-free decaying velocity. For example, in an Einstein-de Sitter universe with b=a, one may add uh​=a−3/2sin(kx)ey​. Its divergence is zero, it solves the homogeneous linear motion equation, and it changes neither δ=bδ∗​ nor ψ, yet it is not constant. Thus the printed constant-velocity statement is correct for the intended pure scalar growing mode; without that condition the displayed field is a counterexample. This extra mode is removed by the usual irrotational, no-decaying-mode initial conditions.
