Use c=1 and signature +−−−. The positively curved FLRW metric in the usual radial chart is
ds2=dt2−a2(t)[1−r2dr2​+r2(dΘ2+sin2Θdϕ2)].
(1)
Set r=sinχ and introduce conformal time by dt=adη. Then dr2/(1−r2)=dχ2 in the chart, and the spherical polar coordinate χ∈[0,π] extends the spatial description to the full three-sphere. Thus
ds2=a2(η)[dη2−dχ2−sin2χ(dΘ2+sin2Θdϕ2)].​
(2)
The r coordinate itself is not one-to-one across the equator, which is why the new polar coordinate is useful.
For Λ=0 and k=1, the Friedmann equation is a˙2=(8πG/3)ρa2−1. Since a′=aa˙, it becomes
(a′)2=38πGρ​a4−a2.​
(3)
For pressureless matter, the background continuity equation gives ρa3=ρ0​a03​. Define the conserved normalization M=(4π/3)ρ0​a03​. It makes the equation (a′)2=2GMa−a2. The closed matter-dominated Friedmann solution is the cycloid
a(η)=GM(1−cosη),t(η)=GM(η−sinη).​
(4)
Indeed a′=GMsinη and 2GMa−a2=(GM)2sin2η. Integrating dt/dη=a gives the second expression, with the Big Bang at t=η=0. It expands to a=2GM at η=π and recollapses at η=2π. Here M is the constant appearing in the Friedmann equation; it is not the total mass of the entire closed spatial slice, whose volume is 2π2a3.
