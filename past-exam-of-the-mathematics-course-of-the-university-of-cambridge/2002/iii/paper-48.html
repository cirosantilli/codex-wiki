Put L=logλ and integrate by parts using x−2=−d(x−1)/dx. The exponential cutoff has derivative λe−xe−λe−x, so the endpoint at infinity vanishes and the lower endpoint gives
I(λ)=e−λ/e+∫1∞​xλe−xe−λe−x​dx.
(1)
With u=λe−x this becomes
I(λ)=e−λ/e+∫0λ/e​L−logue−u​du.
(2)
The mass of the transformed integral lies at u=O(1), where
L−logu1​=L1​+L2logu​+O(L3(logu)2​).
(3)
To control this asymptotic expansion, split at u=e−L/2 and u=eL/2. On the central interval the geometric-series remainder is bounded by 2(logu)2/L3, whose integral against e−u is finite. The lower tail has length e−L/2 and denominator at least L; the upper tail is exponentially small in eL/2, with the original denominator at least one. The omitted tails of the logarithmic moments are likewise negligible. Thus extending the moments to (0,∞) is justified.
The Gamma function gives ∫0∞​e−udu=1, and the first logarithmic moment is −γ, with γ Euler's constant. Therefore
I(λ)=logλ1​−(logλ)2γ​+O((logλ)−3)​.
(4)
This is the moving Gumbel cutoff in an inverse-power tail: replacing the original cutoff by a sharp step at x=logλ gives the first term, but its smooth transition supplies the Euler-constant correction.

The relevant boundary values are a=π and σ=0, but the original complex integral defining g(0) requires σ>0. Substituting zero into it is invalid: its real part has a nonintegrable dx/(xlogx) singularity near zero. Its imaginary part is different, since for positive σ
F(t,a,σ)=∫0∞​a2+log2xxσ−1e−tx​dx.
(20)
This has a finite limit as σ↓0. On (0,1), xσ−1≤x−1, whose displayed logarithmic denominator makes the integral finite; at infinity, a fixed upper bound on σ and exponential damping provide an integrable dominator. Thus dominated convergence gives N(t)=limσ↓0​F(t,π,σ).
For the asymptotics, take the imaginary-part parameter identity before passing to that limit, with a fixed 0<s<1/2. Its endpoint factor converges to
h0​(r)=πsin(πr)​Γ(r)=Γ(1−r)1​

by the Gamma reflection formula. Near zero, sin(πr)Γ(σ+r) is bounded uniformly for small σ≥0, because the sine zero compensates the gamma pole. At s>0, the remainder integral itself remains absolutely convergent at σ=0 and is O(t−s). Consequently
N(t)=∫0s​e−rlogtΓ(1−r)dr​+O(t−s).

Putting ε=0 gives the local regular perturbation result y0​=(x2+1)/2. It cannot describe the whole half-line: its derivative grows like x, making εy′ order one at x=O(ε−1).
The useful exact change of variables is
r=logx,z(r)=xεy(x)​,w(r)=εy′(x).
(25)
Direct substitution turns the equation into two autonomous differential equations,
zr​=w−z,wr​=(1−ε−w)w+2εz​.
(26)
The initial values here are z(0)=w(0)=ε. Near the origin, linearizing this system gives eigenvalues 1 and −1−ε. Equivalently, neglecting only the derivative-square nonlinearity in the original equation gives the two modes x2 and x−ε. Their matching coefficients are
A=2+ε1+ε​,B=2+ε1​,C=2εA=2+ε2ε(1+ε)​.
(27)
Thus the growing derivative has w∼Cx. Keeping these unexpanded linear-mode coefficients is convenient for matching; it does not assert that the linearized solution is the exact nonlinear one.
At the distinguished limit X=Cx=O(1), set R=logX. To leading order the derivative equation becomes wR​=w(1−w), the logistic differential equation. Matching w∼eR as R→−∞ selects
w=1+XX​.
(28)
Then zR​+z=w, and the growing-mode matching z∼X/2 gives
z=X1​∫0X​1+ss​ds=XH(X)​,H(X)=X−log(1+X).
(29)
This proves the logistic crossover in a weakly nonlinear Euler equation. In particular the nonlinear-region leading solution is y∼H(Cx)/(εC), or ε−2H(εx) with the constants taken at their lowest order.
A composite asymptotic expansion retaining the small-distance stable mode and satisfying the initial value exactly is
ycomp​(x)=A+Bx−ε+εCH(Cx)−H(C)​,A=2+ε1+ε​, B=2+ε1​, C=2εA​.
(30)
Since H(X)=X2/2+O(X3), this matches Ax2+Bx−ε when Cx≪1 and hence (x2+1)/2 at fixed x as ε→0. Its derivative at one differs from the prescribed value only by O(ε), within leading-order accuracy. For Cx≫1 it gives y∼x/ε. Thus the composite covers the initial region, the nonlinear transition and the arbitrarily large-x regime; uniform leading accuracy is understood relative to the growing solution, not as an absolute error independent of unbounded x.
There is no later hidden growth regime. The exact positive system keeps z,w in [0,1+ε]2, because its vector field points inward at every boundary. Its positive equilibrium point is z=w=1+ε, with Jacobian matrix having trace −2−3ε and determinant 1+ε. The origin has no attracting direction inside the positive quadrant, while the divergence −ε−2w<0 excludes cycles by the Bendixson-Dulac theorem. The Poincaré-Bendixson theorem therefore gives convergence to the positive stable equilibrium. At fixed small positive ε, the exact far-field slope is y/x→(1+ε)/ε, whose difference from 1/ε is only relative O(ε), consistent with the global leading composite.

Write T=εt and q(T)=2e−T. The forcing acts on the fast initial-time scale, so first remove a localized particular solution. Expanding the coefficient there gives
up​=e−2t+εp1​(t)+⋯,p1′′​+4p1​=8te−2t.
(35)
A particular choice is p1​=(t+1/2)e−2t, hence
up​=e−2t[1+ε(t+1/2)]+O(ε2).
(36)
Its initial value is 1+ε/2 and its initial derivative is −2+O(ε2). The localized forcing determines WKB phase constants; omitting it before imposing the initial conditions would miss the permanent free oscillation.
For the homogeneous part, the slow-time equation is ε2uTT​+q2u=0. Write its WKB approximation as A(T)[CcosΦ+BsinΦ], normalizing A(0)=1. The leading amplitude transport equation gives A=eT/2 and leading phase ε−1∫0T​q(s)ds=2(1−e−T)/ε.
To include every O(ε) contribution on bounded slow-time intervals, the first phase correction is needed. If the local phase wavenumber is q+ε2q2​, the real part of the amplitude-phase equation gives q2​=ATT​/(2qA)=eT/16. The WKB phase correction for a slowly varying oscillator, anchored to zero at the initial time, is therefore
Φ(t)=ε2​(1−e−εt)+16ε​(eεt−1)​.
(37)
There is no first-order bulk amplitude correction in this normalization. At t=0, the homogeneous initial value must be −ε/2+O(ε2) and its initial derivative 1+O(ε2). Since At′​(0)=ε/2 and Φt′​(0)=2+O(ε2), these give C=−ε/2+O(ε2) and B=1/2+O(ε2). The requested forced approximation is
u(t)=e−2t[1+ε(t+1/2)]+eεt/2[21​sinΦ(t)−2ε​cosΦ(t)]+O(ε2)​
(38)
for 0≤εt≤T0​ with fixed T0​. The approximate initial value is exactly one through this order and its derivative is −1+O(ε2). The particular-solution residual is uniformly localized and O(ε2); the corrected homogeneous residual is O(ε3) on the long interval, producing O(ε2) solution error after integration over times of order ε−1.
The WKB approximation requires the relative frequency-change parameter
q2∣q˙​∣​=2ε​eεt≪1.

It therefore fails when εeεt=O(1), even though the coefficient has no finite-time zero. This is the Bessel transition for an exponentially decaying oscillator. Introduce the shifted slow time and amplitude
τ=εt−log(ε−1),u(t)=ε−1/2U(τ)​.

The exact rescaled equation is
Uττ​+4e−2τU=8ε−3/2exp[−ε2​(log(ε−1)+τ)].

For fixed τ its right side is smaller than every algebraic power of ε. The leading transition problem is consequently
U0,ττ​+4e−2τU0​=0​.
(42)
With Z=2e−τ this becomes Z2U0,ZZ​+ZU0,Z​+Z2U0​=0, the order-zero Bessel differential equation. This identifies the governing equation without solving it.
The amplitude-phase series also supplies overlap data while εeT≪1, although its bounded-T absolute error estimate is not uniform up to the transition. For matching, combine the earlier sine and cosine terms into 21​sin(Φ−ε) through first order. In the overlap ε≪eτ≪1, their phase is
Φ−ε=αε​−2e−τ+16eτ​,αε​=ε2​−1617ε​
(43)
to the retained order. Thus amplitude and phase matching as τ→−∞ require
U0​∼21​eτ/2sin(αε​−2e−τ),U0,τ​∼e−τ/2cos(αε​−2e−τ)​.
(44)
These are oscillatory amplitude-phase conditions, rather than pointwise ratio statements at zeros. The smaller eτ/16 phase term records the first overlap correction if required. The rapidly varying matching constant αε​ must be retained as data; there is no single epsilon-independent limiting phase. The two matching conditions determine the leading transition solution, which is deliberately left unsolved.
