Past exam of the mathematics course of the University of Cambridge / 2002 / iii / Paper 27 / 6 / c / Solution

OurBigBook.com Words: 217
For each fixed m≥0 and T≥m, remove the first m weight factors but retain the same walk law:
ZT(m)​=⟨∏j=m+1T​(1+εh(j,ξj​))⟩.
(34)
This quantity is measurable with respect to Gm+1​=σ(h(j,x):j≥m+1,x∈Zd), since the averaging over all walk positions is deterministic and involves no earlier environment variables. The bounds on each deleted factor give, pointwise for every environment,
(1−ε)mZT(m)​≤ZT​≤(1+ε)mZT(m)​.
(35)
Both constants are finite and strictly positive. Taking lower limits gives the exact identity of events
{liminfT​ZT​=0}={liminfT​ZT(m)​=0}∈Gm+1​.
(36)
There is no exceptional-set issue in this comparison. Choose the version of ζ to equal liminfT​ZT​ when this is finite and to equal 1 otherwise; it agrees with the almost-sure martingale limit, and its zero event is precisely the left-hand event above. Since the identity holds for every m, {ζ=0} belongs to the printed tail sigma-algebra ⋂n≥1​Gn​. This proves the tail event for a vanishing positive path-weight limit without pretending that the whole value of ζ is tail-measurable.
The independent time layers of the environment are independent random vectors, so the Kolmogorov zero-one law applies to their tail sigma-algebra. It yields P(ζ=0)∈{0,1}. Under the small-noise condition from part (b), Eζ=1 and ζ≥0, so that probability cannot be 1. Hence
P(ζ=0)=0,ζ>0 almost surely.​
(37)
The tail-event argument itself works for every 0<ε<1; the deduction of positivity here uses the mean-one conclusion supplied by part (b).
