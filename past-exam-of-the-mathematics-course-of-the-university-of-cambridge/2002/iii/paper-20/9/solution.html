For the general alternative, let G:D→C be a functor between locally small categories, with D a complete category. The Freyd general adjoint functor theorem says that G has a left adjoint if and only if it preserves small categorical limits and satisfies the solution-set condition. Explicitly, for every c∈C, there must be a set of morphisms xj​:c→Gdj​ such that every x:c→Gd has the form G(h)xj​ for some j and h:dj​→d. Equivalently, each comma category (c↓G) has a weakly initial set.
We prove the initial-object lemma for complete categories with a weakly initial set. Let E be a locally small category which is complete and has a weakly initial set (wj​)j∈J​. Its product in a category p=∏j​wj​ is weakly initial: for each z, choose a morphism wj​→z and compose with the projection p→wj​. The hom-set E(p,p) is a set. Form e:i→p equalizing every endomorphism of p with 1p​, by taking the equalizer of the two morphisms
p⇉∏v∈E(p,p)​p,
(34)
whose v-coordinates are v and 1p​. Then ve=e for every endomorphism v, and e is monic. There is a morphism i→z for every z, by composing e with a weakly initial morphism out of p.
To prove uniqueness, let u,v:i⇉z and take their equalizer q:w→i. Weak initiality of p gives a:p→w. The composite eqa:p→p is an endomorphism, so eqae=e. Cancel the monomorphism e to obtain qae=1i​. Thus q is both monic and split epic, hence an isomorphism: if qk=1, cancellation from qkq=q gives kq=1. Since uq=vq, it follows that u=v. Therefore i is an initial object, proving the lemma fully.
Now assume categorical limit preservation and the solution-set condition. The comma category (c↓G) is locally small. It is complete: for a small diagram in a category (dj​,xj​), take its underlying categorical limit l in D. Since G preserves this categorical limit, the compatible xj​:c→Gdj​ give a unique x:c→Gl. The same universal property shows that (l,x) is the categorical limit in the comma category. Its weakly initial set and the proved lemma therefore supply an initial object (Fc,ηc​). For t:c→c′, its universal property defines a unique Ft:Fc→Fc′ with G(Ft)ηc​=ηc′​t. Uniqueness proves the identity morphism and composition in a category laws and the naturality of η. The same universal property gives the natural bijection D(Fc,d)≅C(c,Gd), so F⊣G.
For necessity, an adjunction F⊣G supplies the singleton weakly initial set (Fc,ηc​) at c. It also proves that G preserves categorical limits: maps c→Gd correspond to maps Fc→d, and compatible families of the latter factor uniquely through any categorical limit in D. Hence the image categorical cone under G has the required universal property. This proves both directions of the general alternative.
For the special alternative, the limit form of the special adjoint functor theorem says: if D is complete, locally small and well-powered, with a small cogenerating family (Qi​)i∈I​, and C is locally small, then G:D→C has a left adjoint exactly when it preserves small categorical limits. A cogenerating set means that unequal parallel morphisms u,v:X⇉Y are distinguished by some morphism Y→Qi​. We prove the needed solution-set condition, so that the general theorem applies.
Fix x:c→Gd. A supporting subobject of d is a monomorphism m:d′→d through whose image under G the morphism x factors. There is at least one, namely 1d​. Because D is well-powered, choose a set of representatives of all these supporting subobjects. A limit-preserving functor preserves monomorphisms: a map is monic exactly when its diagonal into its self-pullback in a category is an isomorphism, and both the pullback in a category and this isomorphism are preserved. Consequently each factorization of x through Gm is unique. Form the intersection of subobjects of the supporting representatives, as a wide pullback in a category over d. The unique lifts of x are compatible and, since G preserves that categorical limit, they give a factorization
x=G(m0​)x0​,m0​:d0​↪d,x0​:c→Gd0​.
(35)
This is a minimal supported subobject. More explicitly, if q:e↪d0​ also supports x0​, then m0​q is one of the supporting subobjects of d. The intersection therefore factors through m0​q, giving r:d0​→e with m0​qr=m0​. Monicity of m0​ gives qr=1d0​​; monicity of q then makes q invertible.
For any u,v:d0​→Qi​, if G(u)x0​=G(v)x0​, the equalizer of u,v supports x0​, because G preserves that equalizer. Minimality makes it invertible, so u=v. We have therefore obtained injective functions
D(d0​,Qi​)⟶C(c,GQi​),u⟼G(u)x0​.
(36)
Let Si​ be the realized image subset of the fixed set C(c,GQi​). Use the unique morphism corresponding to each s∈Si​ to form the evaluation embedding into cogenerator products
d0​⟶PS​=∏i∈I​∏s∈Si​​Qi​.
(37)
This morphism is monic: if two morphisms into d0​ have identical product composites, every morphism from d0​ to every Qi​ gives identical composites, and the cogenerating property makes the two morphisms equal. There is only a set of possible families of subsets (Si​), and hence a set of possible products PS​. Each has only a set of subobjects by well-poweredness, and each chosen subobject a has only a set of morphisms c→Ga by local smallness. Take all these pairs (a,z:c→Ga) as a weakly initial set. Every original (d,x) receives a morphism from a pair representing its (d0​,x0​), through m0​. Thus the set is weakly initial in (c↓G).
The general theorem now supplies a left adjoint. Necessity again follows from preservation of categorical limits by a right adjoint. This proves the special alternative, including its size argument. Using only the realized subsets Si​ avoids assuming that an arbitrary object admits a morphism into every cogenerator. Both adjoint functor theorem alternatives are proved.
