For a functor D:J→C with finite indexing category, form the finite products in a category
P=∏j∈ObJ​D(j),Q=∏u:i→j in J​D(j).
(6)
Define s,t:P⇉Q by requiring the coordinate indexed by u:i→j to be D(u)πi​ for s and πj​ for t. Let e:L→P be their equalizer, and put λj​=πj​e. The equality se=te says exactly that D(u)λi​=λj​ for every morphism u, so these morphisms form a categorical cone.
For any other categorical cone (xj​:X→D(j)), the product in a category gives a unique x:X→P with πj​x=xj​. The categorical cone equations give sx=tx, so the equalizer gives a unique xˉ:X→L with exˉ=x. This is exactly the universal property of a categorical limit. Empty indexing products are terminal objects, so the construction includes the empty diagram in a category. Thus finite products and equalizers give all finite limits.
