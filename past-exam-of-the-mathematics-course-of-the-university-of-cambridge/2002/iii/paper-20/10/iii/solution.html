For (A,a), consider the reflexive free-algebra presentation of a monad algebra
FT(TA) μA​​Ta​​ FT(A)a​(A,a).
(43)
Both parallel morphisms are monad algebra morphisms: μA​ satisfies the monad associativity law, while Ta is the image under FT of a. The map a is a monad algebra morphism because aμA​=aTa, and the same equation says it equalizes the pair.
Suppose h:FT(A)→(B,b) is a monad algebra morphism with hμA​=hTa. Put k=hηA​:A→B. Naturality of η and the unit law give
ka=hηA​a=hTaηTA​=hμA​ηTA​=h.
(44)
Moreover, since h is a monad algebra morphism,
bTk=bThTηA​=hμA​TηA​=h=ka.
(45)
Thus k is a monad algebra morphism and factors h through a. If k′a=h, then k′=k′aηA​=hηA​=k, proving uniqueness. Therefore every monad algebra is the stated coequalizer of free algebras.
The underlying fork is a split coequalizer: take q=a, s=ηA​ and t=ηTA​. Then qs=1A​, μA​t=1TA​, and (Ta)t=sa. Its splitting maps need not be monad algebra morphisms; the preceding argument separately proves the coequalizer in CT.
