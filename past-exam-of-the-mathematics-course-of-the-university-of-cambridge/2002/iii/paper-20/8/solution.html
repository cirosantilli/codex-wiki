α(f)=Gfηc​:D(Fc,d)→C(c,Gd),β(g)=εd​Fg:C(c,Gd)→D(Fc,d).

They are natural in c,d, using naturality of η,ε. The one supplied triangle identity for an adjunction gives
αβ(g)=Gεd​GFgηc​=Gεd​ηGd​g=g.
(30)
Naturality of ε also gives
βα(f)=εd​FGfFηc​=fεFc​Fηc​=fec​,ec​=εFc​Fηc​.

Since αβ=1, the composite βα is idempotent. Evaluating its equation (βα)2=βα on f=1Fc​ gives ec2​=ec​. As e is a composite of natural transformations, this proves that e is an idempotent in the functor category, including the required naturality.
Suppose this one-triangle adjunction idempotent splits as natural transformations Fr​Hi​F with ri=1H​ and ir=e. The maps h↦hr and f↦fi give inverse bijections between D(Hc,d) and the subset of D(Fc,d) consisting of maps with fec​=f. Indeed (hr)ec​=hrir=hr, (hr)i=h, and (fi)r=fec​=f. The maps α,β give inverse bijections between that subset and C(c,Gd), since βα(f)=fec​ and αβ=1. Combining them gives the natural bijection
D(Hc,d)≅C(c,Gd),h⟼G(hr)ηc​,
(32)
with inverse g↦εd​F(g)ic​. Hence H⊣G. Its adjunction unit is Grη and its adjunction counit is εiG​.
Conversely, suppose H⊣G, with natural bijection θ:D(Hc,d)≅C(c,Gd). Set τ=θ−1α and σ=βθ. Then τσ=1 and στ(f)=fec​. By the covariant Yoneda lemma, the transformation τ:D(Fc,−)→D(Hc,−) is precomposition with a unique morphism ic​:Hc→Fc, and σ is precomposition with a unique morphism rc​:Fc→Hc. The equation τσ=1 evaluated at 1Hc​ gives rc​ic​=1Hc​; the equation στ(f)=fec​ evaluated at 1Fc​ gives ic​rc​=ec​. Naturality in c of τ,σ implies, again by the same Yoneda lemma, that i,r are natural transformations. Thus they split e in the whole functor category, rather than merely at individual objects.
