Let p be kinematic pressure, with the resting hydrostatic part subtracted, and let σ be the buoyancy perturbation about background buoyancy N2z. The nonrotating inviscid Boussinesq equations, with no buoyancy diffusion, are
DtDu​=−px​,DtDw​=−pz​+σ,ux​+wz​=0,DtDσ​+N2w=0,DtD​=∂t​+u∂x​+w∂z​.
(1)
Here the third equation is mass conservation for incompressible flow, and the last expresses buoyancy conservation following a parcel. Linearization about rest removes the products of disturbances. For a plane internal gravity wave proportional to ei(kx+mz−ωt), the resulting Linearized Boussinesq equations become
−iωu=−ikp​,−iωw=−imp​+σ,ku+mw=0,−iωσ+N2w=0.
(2)
With K=(k2+m2)1/2 and k=0, the first and third equations give p​=−mωw/k2. Substitution in vertical momentum and buoyancy gives the dispersion relation
ω2=k2+m2N2k2​.​
(3)
Since the velocity is the time derivative of the displacement field, u=−iωξ​. Thus the complete buoyancy polarization of a plane internal gravity wave is
w=N2iω​σ,u=−kN2imω​σ,ζ​=−N2σ​,ξ​=kN2mσ​,p​=−K2im​σ.​
(4)
Both velocity and displacement are perpendicular to the wavevector; pressure and velocity are in quadrature with buoyancy and displacement.
For upward/rightward phase velocity, choose k,m>0 and ω=Nk/K. Differentiating this dispersion relation gives the group velocity, while the vector phase velocity points along the wavevector:
cg​=(K3Nm2​,−K3Nkm​),cp​=K3Nk​(k,m).​
(5)
Their dot product is zero and cp​+cg​=(N/K,0). Draw cg​ from the tip of cp​: the right-triangle geometry of internal-wave velocities has horizontal hypotenuse N/K. This hypotenuse is distinct from the horizontal phase velocity ω/k.
At an instant with real complex amplitude σ=A, write ϕ=kx+mz−ωt. The spatial fields are
σ=Acosϕ,(ξ,ζ)=N2A​(m/k,−1)cosϕ,(u,w)=N2ωA​(m/k,−1)sinϕ,p=K2mA​sinϕ.
(6)
The constant-phase lines of an internal gravity wave slope down to the right, with dz/dx=−k/m. Buoyancy maxima lie a quarter wavelength from pressure maxima; the oscillating displacement and velocity run along the phase lines. The group velocity points down and right along them, despite upward/rightward motion of the wave phase.
Let P=ξx​u+ζx​w​, using horizontal averaging over a wavelength. This is the displacement pseudomomentum of an internal gravity wave, whose sign convention is opposite to kE/ω. For complex coefficients, ab=21​Re(ab∗). The above wave polarization consequently gives
P=−2kN4ωK2∣σ∣2​,ζx​p​=2N2K2km∣σ∣2​.
(7)
Since cgz​=−ωm/K2, these obey ζx​p​=cgz​P. With weak kinematic viscosity, each leading monochromatic velocity component satisfies ∇2u=−K2u, ∇2w=−K2w. The dissipative term is therefore −νK2P, so the supplied averaged balance reduces to
Pt​+∂z​(cgz​P)=−νK2P.
(8)
For constant k,m,N and a steady wave envelope, cgz​Pz​=−νK2P. As P is proportional to squared wave amplitude, viscous attenuation of an internal gravity wave gives
∣σ(z)∣=∣σ(zs​)∣exp[−2cgz​νK2(z−zs​)​].​
(9)
Here cgz​<0, so amplitude decreases below a source at zs​: the source is above the receiving level. The same conclusion follows directly because downward group velocity carries energy from the source downward. Upward phase velocity does not locate the source.
For a slowly varying mean shear flow U(z), the local intrinsic frequency is ω=ω−kU(z). A local WKB approximation uses ω2=N2k2/(k2+m(z)2), with varying vertical wavenumber and group velocity. The appropriate transported quantity is wave action, or horizontal wave pseudomomentum kE/ω; its vertical flux has viscous loss, and the changing polarization factors must be retained. In particular, P/∣σ∣2 is no longer constant.
Near a critical level of an internal gravity wave, ω→0, so ∣m∣∼N∣k∣/∣ω∣ and ∣cgz​∣∼ω2/(N∣k∣). The damping per vertical distance, of order νK2/∣cgz​∣, grows rapidly. The waves are strongly attenuated over a narrow altitude range and transfer momentum to the mean flow there or before reaching the critical level. The inviscid WKB approximation eventually fails; viscosity and possibly internal-wave breaking regulate the small scales.

Let h be layer thickness and g gravitational acceleration. The unforced shallow water equations on the equatorial beta plane are
ut​+uux​+vuy​−βyv=−ghx​,vt​+uvx​+vvy​+βyu=−ghy​,ht​+(hu)x​+(hv)y​=0.
(10)
Linearize about u=v=0, h=h0​. Put c0​=gh0​​ and L=c0​/β​, and scale (x,y)=L(X,Y), t=(L/c0​)T, (u,v)=c0​(U,V) and h=h0​(1+η). Dropping capitals, the linearized shallow water equations become
ut​−yv=−ηx​,vt​+yu=−ηy​,ηt​+ux​+vy​=0.
(11)
The choice βL2/c0​=1 makes both the wave speed and the coefficient of the dimensionless Coriolis parameter equal to one.
Take Fourier modes {u(y),v(y),η(y)}ei(kx−ωt) that decay as y→±∞. Their equations are
ωu−kη=iyv,ωη−ku=−iv′,−iωv+yu=−η′.
(12)
For v=0 and nonzero k, the first two require ω2=k2. The root ω=k gives u=η, and meridional balance becomes u′=−yu. Hence the equatorial Kelvin wave is
ω=k,v=0,u=η=Ce−y2/2.​
(13)
It travels eastward at speed c0​ in dimensional units. The other sign gives η=−u and u′=yu, so u∝ey2/2 is not trapped.
For v=0, ω=0 and ω2=k2, inversion of the first two equations gives the equatorial wave velocity polarization
u=ω2−k2i(ωyv−kv′)​,η=ω2−k2i(kyv−ωv′)​.
(14)
Substitute in the remaining momentum equation. Multiplication by (ω2−k2)/i leaves
−ω(ω2−k2)v+ωy2v+kv−ωv′′=0,
(15)
so
v′′+(λ−y2)v=0,λ=ω2−k2−ωk​.
(16)
Writing v=e−y2/2H(y) yields H′′−2yH′+(λ−1)H=0, the Hermite differential equation. The trapped solutions have the Hermite oscillator quantization λ=2n+1 and H=Hn​.
For completeness, quantization follows without assuming that every acceptable solution is a polynomial. On decaying smooth functions, define a=d/dy+y, a†=−d/dy+y and H=−d2/dy2+y2=a†a+1. Integration by parts gives λ≥1 and ∥av∥2=(λ−1)∥v∥2. If av=0, it is an eigenfunction of H with eigenvalue λ−2. Repeated lowering must terminate: otherwise it eventually produces an eigenvalue below one. At termination av=0, so the terminal eigenfunction is e−y2/2 and has eigenvalue one. Repeated raising gives Hn​(y)e−y2/2 and λ=2n+1; uniqueness of a decaying solution at one end of this second-order equation fixes each mode up to scale.
Thus, away from the exceptional quotients, all fields are determined by
v=CHn​(y)e−y2/2,ω2−k2−ωk​=2n+1,n=0,1,2,….​
(17)
Insert this v and its derivative in the polarization formulae to obtain u and η. For n≥1, the cubic ω3−(k2+2n+1)ω−k=0 supplies the low-frequency equatorial Rossby wave and two equatorial inertia--gravity wave branches. Their meridional structures are Gaussian-weighted Hermite polynomials.
For n=0, multiplication by ω factorizes the equatorial shallow-water dispersion relation as
(ω+k)(ω2−kω−1)=0.
(18)
The physically admissible quadratic gives the mixed Rossby-gravity wave, equivalently the Yanai wave:
ω−k−ω1​=0,v=Ce−y2/2,u=η=iωyv.​
(19)
These fields satisfy all three original equations directly and decay at both infinities, including where the quotient formulae are singular.
To test the other factor, set ω=−k=0. The original equations give u+η=−iyv/k and v′=−yv. Put δ=u−η; meridional momentum then gives
δ′−yδ=i(2k−k−1)v,(e−y2/2δ)′=i(2k−k−1)Ce−y2.
(20)
Decay of u,η at both infinities requires the integral of the right side to vanish. Hence C=0 unless k2=1/2. Ordinarily the extra factor supplies no trapped mode. At the exceptional root of the mixed Rossby-gravity mode, k2=1/2, it coincides with the quadratic root and gives the same physical fields, not an additional branch. This distinction avoids discarding a regular physical mode at the crossing.
These arguments concern nonzero wave angular frequency. At k=0, the oscillatory nonzero-v modes have ω=±2n+1​, while zero-frequency v=0 geostrophic states must be considered in the original equations rather than in expressions divided by ω.

The basic quasi-geostrophic streamfunction ψˉ​=−Λyz gives uˉ=Λz, vˉ=0 and constant quasi-geostrophic potential vorticity Qˉ​=f. Define
q′=∇h2​ψ′+N2f2​ψzz′​.
(21)
Because the background has no potential-vorticity gradient, the linear potential-vorticity equation is simply
(∂t​+Λz∂x​)q′=0.
(22)
Its characteristics have constant y,z,x−Λzt, so the general solution is q′=F(y,z,x−Λzt). In particular, q′=F(y,z)G(x−Λzt) is the requested arbitrary separated family; one product is not the most general initial field.
Linearizing the vertical velocity requires retaining advection of ψˉ​z​=−Λy by v′=ψx′​. Therefore
w′=−N2f​[(∂t​+Λz∂x​)ψz′​−Λψx′​].
(23)
The impermeable boundary at z=0 imposes
∂t​ψz′​−Λψx′​=0(z=0).​
(24)
This is conservation of boundary buoyancy, not merely a condition that ψz′​ vanish.
For zero interior potential vorticity, write ψ′=Re{ϕ(z,t)ei(kx+ly)}. With Kh​=k2+l2​>0, stratified quasi-geostrophic inversion gives
ϕzz​−f2N2Kh2​​ϕ=0.
(25)
The decaying solution is
ϕ=A(t)e−μz,μ=fNKh​​.​
(26)
No time dependence has been assumed to obtain this vertical structure. Applying the boundary condition gives −μA˙−iΛkA=0, hence
A=A0​e−iωt,ω=μΛk​=Nk2+l2​fΛk​.​
(27)
Thus the Eady edge wave travels eastward with horizontal phase velocity ω/k=Λ/μ for k=0.
The propagation mechanism can be made explicit using boundary parcels. Thermal wind gives the background buoyancy gradient bˉy​=fψˉ​zy​=−fΛ. A small meridional parcel displacement d produces boundary buoyancy perturbation b′=−dbˉy​=fΛd. Meanwhile the decaying potential-vorticity inversion gives b′=fψz′​=−fμψ′ at the boundary. Consequently ψ′=−Λd/μ there, and the parcel equation dt​=v′=ψx′​ becomes
dt​+μΛ​dx​=0.
(28)
A displaced buoyancy contour therefore induces a velocity field that advances that displacement eastward. No interior potential-vorticity gradient is needed because the boundary buoyancy supplies the invertibility data.
Vortex stretching is what sets the induced flow's vertical reach. In the interior, the zero-potential vorticity condition requires
relative vorticity∇h2​ψ′​​=−Kh2​ψ′,stretchingN2f2​ψzz′​​​=+Kh2​ψ′.
(29)
Boundary isopycnal displacement decreases with height as e−μz, so fluid columns are stretched or compressed over depth μ−1=f/(NKh​). Conservation of potential vorticity makes this stretching balance the relative vorticity. The resulting inversion factor b′=−fμψ′ gives the propagation speed: stronger density stratification reduces penetration and speed, while larger f increases them. Zero interior potential vorticity therefore does not imply zero interior flow.
For imposed boundary vertical velocity, retain the same decaying structure and write
w′(0)=Re{ϵei(kx+ly−ω0​t)}.
(30)
The boundary relation becomes the forced Eady edge wave equation
A˙+iωA=Ce−iω0​t,C=fμN2ϵ​.
(31)
The integrating factor eiωt gives, for ω0​=ω,
A(t)=Be−iωt+i(ω−ω0​)C​e−iω0​t,ψ′=Re{A(t)e−μz+i(kx+ly)}.​
(32)
The arbitrary homogeneous coefficient B sets the initial disturbance. With A(0)=0, this is A=C(e−iω0​t−e−iωt)/[i(ω−ω0​)].
At resonance, ω0​=ω, integration instead yields
A(t)=(B+Ct)e−iωt.​
(33)
An initially undisturbed fluid has ψ′=Cte−μzcos(kx+ly−ωt). The imposed pumping reinforces the same boundary normal mode every cycle, with no dissipative loss, so the linear amplitude grows secularly. The divergence describes resonance of the undamped linear model; sufficiently large wave amplitude invalidates the assumed small-disturbance approximation.

Potential-vorticity inversion is the diagnostic recovery of the flow from its advected vortical scalar, using the balance relation and specified boundary conditions. Its usefulness is a separation of tasks: transport the scalar by the current velocity, invert to find the new velocity, and repeat. The inverse is spatially nonlocal even though the transport law is local. The four models differ chiefly in the operator being inverted and the information needed at boundaries.
In two-dimensional vortex dynamics, planar incompressibility permits u=(−ψy​,ψx​), and vertical vorticity is ζ=∇2ψ. The curl of inviscid momentum gives
ζt​+J(ψ,ζ)=0,J(ψ,ζ)=ψx​ζy​−ψy​ζx​.
(34)
There is no vortex stretching in strictly planar motion, so vorticity is materially conserved. The two-dimensional potential-vorticity inversion is the Poisson equation ∇2ψ=ζ. On the plane, with suitable integrability and far-field behavior, its Green's function is (2π)−1log∣x−x′∣, giving
ψ(x)=2π1​∫ζ(x′)log∣x−x′∣d2x′+ψh​(x).
(35)
Differentiation gives the planar Biot-Savart kernel. Here ψh​ is a harmonic function fixed by boundary, circulation and far-field data. A rigid impermeable wall is a streamline, so ψ is constant on it; constants or circulations around distinct holes require specification. The vorticity alone cannot distinguish two flows differing by a permitted harmonic contribution, such as a uniform current on an unbounded plane. A constant added to ψ has no physical effect.
In nonrotating layerwise-two-dimensional vortex dynamics, strong stable density stratification constrains motion to almost horizontal layers. In the ideal independent-layer limit, vertical velocity vanishes at leading order, (u,v)=(−ψy​,ψx​) and
ζt​+J(ψ,ζ)=0,∇h2​ψ=ζ
(36)
hold separately at each fixed z. Height is a parameter: the horizontal inverse Laplacian contains no vertical derivative. With background buoyancy N2z, the leading Ertel potential vorticity is Q=ω⋅∇b≃N2ζ, so the materially advected normalized scalar is Q/N2≃ζ. The same horizontal Poisson equation is inverted layer by layer, using each layer's boundary data. This is an asymptotic constrained model, not arbitrary three-dimensional Euler motion with vertical velocity set to zero: its validity requires small Froude number and controlled vertical scales. At finite buoyancy frequency, the weak vertical motion and tilted isopycnals restore coupling. The inversion may be independent by layer while the reconstructed fields vary with height. The horizontal Euler equations for an inviscid fluid determine the pressure gradient, while hydrostatic balance relates its vertical derivative to buoyancy.
In shallow-water quasi-geostrophic inversion, begin with the exact shallow-water potential vorticity (f+ζ)/h. Linearizing its anomaly about depth H gives, after multiplying by H, ζ−f0​η/H. For small Rossby number, geostrophic balance gives ψ=gη/f0​, while ζ=∇h2​ψ. Keeping the beta plane background gradient gives
q=∇h2​ψ−LD2​ψ​+βy,LD​=∣f0​∣gH​​,qt​+J(ψ,q)=0.
(37)
Thus q−βy is inverted with the modified Helmholtz equation (∇h2​−LD−2​)ψ=q−βy, under the specified boundary and far-field conditions. The velocity follows from ψ and surface elevation from η=f0​ψ/g. A horizontal Fourier mode of the anomaly satisfies
ψ​=−∣kh​∣2+LD−2​q−βy​​.
(38)
The Rossby deformation radius therefore limits the reach of the balanced response. Layer stretching, represented by −ψ/LD2​, is absent from ordinary planar vorticity inversion. Unlike the pure Poisson equation, the finite-deformation operator also responds to the constant part of ψ, which specifies mean elevation rather than an arbitrary streamfunction offset. Specified bottom topography adds a known source to q; it is subtracted along with the background before inversion.
In stratified quasi-geostrophic inversion, continuously varying vertical displacement replaces the shallow layer's single stretching term. For positive N2(z) and constant nonzero f0​, the three-dimensional quasi-geostrophic potential vorticity is
q=∇h2​ψ+∂z​(N2f02​​ψz​)+βy,qt​+J(ψ,q)=0.
(39)
Geostrophic balance and hydrostatic balance give velocity (−ψy​,ψx​) and buoyancy perturbation b=f0​ψz​. Invert the anisotropic elliptic boundary value problem using interior q, prescribed top and bottom buoyancy as Neumann boundary conditions, and the lateral, far-field and global-flow data. Boundary buoyancy evolves by its own material conservation when the boundary is impermeable. It cannot be discarded: the Eady edge wave in the preceding solution has zero interior potential vorticity anomaly but nonzero boundary buoyancy and a nonzero induced flow.
For constant N,f0​, the stretched coordinates for quasi-geostrophic inversion Z=Nz/∣f0​∣ turn the inversion operator into the three-dimensional Laplacian. Its infinite-space Green's function is −1/(4π∣X−X′∣) in (x,y,Z) coordinates. Hence a horizontal disturbance of scale L communicates vertically over scale ∣f0​∣L/N, explicitly coupling layers. In a bounded domain with homogeneous difference data, multiplying the difference of two inversions by its streamfunction and applying integration by parts gives
∫(∣∇h​δψ∣2+N2f02​​∣δψz​∣2)dV=0.
(40)
This proves uniqueness up to a constant when the relevant boundary terms vanish; a mean or pressure reference fixes the remaining constant. Compatible boundary fluxes are needed for a purely Neumann boundary-value problem.
Across all four models, inversion provides the induced flow, while scalar transport supplies time evolution. The independent horizontal inverse in the first two models becomes a finite-deformation inverse in shallow water and a vertically coupled elliptic inverse in continuous stratification. The principle applies within the stated balance or constrained model. In the unrestricted Boussinesq equations or shallow water equations, gravity waves and divergent motion can share the same potential vorticity, so the vortical scalar alone does not reconstruct every possible flow.
