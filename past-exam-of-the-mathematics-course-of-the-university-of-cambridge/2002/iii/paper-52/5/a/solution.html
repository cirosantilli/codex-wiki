Past exam of the mathematics course of the University of Cambridge / 2002 / iii / Paper 52 / 5 / a / Solution

 ... 2002 iii Paper 52 Section C 5 a
OurBigBook.com Words: 238
Use G=gβ(T−T0​(z)) for the plume reduced gravity, and take z upwards. For a symmetric line plume define fluxes per unit source length,
q=∫wdx,m=∫w2dx,B=∫wGdx.
(41)
The integrals span the plume; its edges can move horizontally with z. The fluid entrainment rate at one moving edge is the inward normal transport relative to that edge. For an edge x=b(z) it is E=wb′−u; symmetry supplies equal transport through the other edge. The Batchelor entrainment hypothesis closes its mean value as E=αwc​, where wc​ is a representative vertical velocity and α is the entrainment coefficient. The integrated volume conservation equation is therefore q′=2αwc​.
Using volume conservation, the vertical acceleration equation has conservative form ∂x​(uw)+∂z​(w2)=G. Incoming ambient has zero vertical momentum; the integrated momentum conservation equation is consequently m′=∫Gdx. For the temperature anomaly, T0​ depends on height and
∂x​(uG)+∂z​(wG)=−N2w,N2=gβdzdT0​​.
(42)
Ambient fluid enters with G=0, giving the buoyancy flux balance. The integral model is
q′=2αwc​,m′=∫Gdx,B′=−N2q.​
(43)
Here N is the buoyancy frequency when N2>0; the same algebra permits unstable temperature gradients with N2<0.
These are mean integral balances. The advective equations printed in the question omit explicit turbulent stresses and heat fluxes; a turbulent plume interpretation requires the usual entrainment closure for that unresolved transverse transport. Assuming vanishing edge stresses and heat fluxes, and negligible additional axial turbulent-flux contributions, gives the displayed bulk model. It would be inconsistent to treat a discontinuous top-hat profile as a smooth exact pointwise solution of the inviscid temperature-advection equation.
