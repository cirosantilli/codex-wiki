Let W≥0 be the downward fluid suction speed and V≥0 the downward particle settling velocity relative to that fluid. Set Q=uh and g′=g0′​ϕ. The carrier volume conservation and particle-volume balances per unit channel width are
Q′=−W,(Qϕ)′=−(W+V)ϕ.​
(28)
The particle sink includes suction as well as relative settling: the particles follow the well-mixed withdrawn fluid, in addition to settling through it. Subtracting ϕQ′ yields Qϕ′=−Vϕ. Thus for W>0, as long as Q>0,
Q=Q0​−Wx,ϕ=(1−Q0​Wx​)V/W.​
(29)
For W=0 the continuous limit is Q=Q0​ and ϕ=e−Vx/Q0​. In particular suction alone does not change the well-mixed particle fraction: V=0 implies ϕ=1.
Withdrawn fluid carries its local horizontal momentum, so the momentum flux and hydrostatic pressure force obey
(hu2+21​g0′​ϕh2)′=−Wu.​
(30)
Expand this and use Q′=−W. The suction momentum terms cancel in the primitive acceleration equation, leaving
uu′+g0′​ϕh′+21​g0′​hϕ′=0.
(31)
These are the governing equations for a particle-laden current with suction. Substituting u=Q/h and the concentration equation gives the convenient depth equation
(1−Fr2)h′=g0′​ϕWu​+2QVh​,Fr2=g0′​ϕhu2​.​
(32)
The steady carrier flux is exhausted at the formal length x∗​=Q0​/W​ when W>0. Positive Q cannot continue beyond it. Near that limit a chosen depth branch, mixing or shallow-layer assumptions may fail, so this is the mass-budget extent, not a guarantee that every arbitrary inlet state has a regular shallow continuation to it. When W=0, settling makes the driving reduced gravity exponentially small but does not remove carrier fluid; this ideal steady model has no finite mass-budget runout.
