If the branch continued for arbitrarily large q, j would remain bounded, contradicting j≥4q2. Hence it reaches j=4q2 at a finite qo​>0. Its height is finite because dζ/dq=(1−s)/(2m​) is finite away from the source and behaves like a constant times q−2/5 at the source, an integrable singularity.

A convention using αw instead of α(w−v) would produce different numerical amplitudes; the relative-speed convention was stated in part (a).
Physically zo​ is a turnover or breakdown height for a slender steady plume surrounded by uniform counterflow. The opposing streams then occupy equal areas and have equal speeds; the single-valued small-area branch cannot continue smoothly. Above it one expects lateral spreading, stronger recirculation, mixing and unsteady buoyant structures rather than a continuation of the same slender top-hat column. A detailed flow or upper-layer evolution requires restoring pressure and stress dynamics and, eventually, the modified ambient stratification. The model does not predict an infinite physical velocity, nor does it require that all buoyant fluid forever remain below zo​.
