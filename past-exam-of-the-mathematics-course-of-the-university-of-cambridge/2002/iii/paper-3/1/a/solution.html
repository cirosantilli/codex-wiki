Let f∈R[X] have degree n. A useful reduction prime must preserve its degree and make its reduction separable. Thus exclude the leading coefficient and discriminant from P; after localizing at those elements one may make f monic. The residue base field is k=Frac(R/P), not necessarily the ring R/P itself. Then the Galois group of the reduced polynomial embeds in the generic group, with the same action on the corresponding roots.
Here is the unramified reduction of a polynomial Galois group argument. A UFD is integrally closed. Let E be the splitting field over F, and use the root algebra B obtained by adjoining all roots after inverting the discriminant. It is finite étale over the resulting normal base: successive simple-root adjunctions are unramified, and the distinct generic components separate over a normal ring. Choose a prime Q above P. The residue field kQ​ is generated over k by the reduced roots, so it is their splitting field. The decomposition group
DQ​={σ∈Gal(E/F):σQ=Q}
(1)
acts on kQ​. Its residue action is onto Gal(kQ​/k); this is the local decomposition-group description of the unramified fiber. Equivalently, the geometric points of that fiber form a free transitive Galois-group set, and the commuting residue-field action identifies the residue group with a stabilizer subgroup of the generic group.
The kernel is the inertia group. An element in that kernel fixes every reduced root. Since the discriminant is a unit, distinct roots have distinct reductions; consequently it fixes every original root and is the identity. Therefore
DQ​≃Gal(f​/k)≤Gal(f/F).​
(2)
Choosing another prime changes this subgroup by conjugacy, which does not change the cycle types available for computing the generic group.
When k=Fq​, its splitting-field group is generated by Frobenius. A reduced irreducible factor of degree di​ contributes one di​-cycle. Hence a squarefree factorization with degrees d1​,…,dr​ proves that the generic group contains that cycle type. Irreducible degree-preserving reduction also proves generic irreducibility by Gauss's lemma: a nontrivial monic factorization would reduce to one. Several good primes, a discriminant-square test and permutation-group arguments can then identify the group. A ramified or degree-dropping prime does not justify the same cycle test.
