For a finite point p, use the valuation vp​ with local parameter u=t−p; at infinity use u=1/t. Its extensions w to E are permuted transitively by G. The completed residue field is F, because F is algebraically closed, so the local decomposition group equals its inertia group.
Write e=e(w/vp​). Characteristic zero makes all local ramification tame. One can choose a uniformizer π in the completion for which
u=πe,Ew​=F((π)),F(t)vp​​=F((u)).
(9)
Indeed, initially u is πe times a unit; that unit has an eth root in F[[π]], by its algebraically closed residue field and the invertibility of e. Absorb it into π. The local automorphisms are then π↦ζπ, ζ∈μe​(F), and form a cyclic group of order e. Define ep​=e.
To specify an element rather than a subgroup, fix a compatible choice of primitive roots of unity ζe​∈F. Define τw​ by its uniformizer character
τw​(π)/π​=ζe​,
(10)
and let Cp​ be its conjugacy class in G. Over C, take ζe​=e2πi/e, corresponding to positive local continuation around p. At an unramified point, ep​=1 and Cp​={1}.
Changing the uniformizer does not affect this character. If π′=aπ with a a unit, inertia fixes its residue, so
τ(π′)/π′​=τ(a)/a​τ(π)/π​=τ(π)/π​.
(11)
The character is injective: a nonidentity finite-order automorphism with derivative one would have τ(π)=π+bπr+⋯ for r≥2, and its Nth iterate would have coefficient Nb at that first nonzero displacement. Characteristic zero contradicts finite order. Thus the chosen primitive character identifies a unique generator.
For another place w′=σw, conjugation gives Dw′​=σDw​σ−1, preserves its order, and carries τw​ to τw′​, since σ fixes the constants and hence ζe​. This proves that ep​ and the branch-cycle conjugacy class are independent of the place and uniformizer. Without the stated roots-of-unity convention, only the cyclic inertia-subgroup conjugacy class is intrinsic: replacing ζe​ by another primitive root replaces the generator by a coprime power, which need not lie in the same conjugacy class.
