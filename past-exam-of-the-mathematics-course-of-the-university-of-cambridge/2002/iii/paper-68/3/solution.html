Use Planck units and restrict to the physical Kähler metric domain
x=S+Sˉ>0,y=T+Tˉ−∣C∣2>0.
(15)
Put A=ae−αS and w=A+b, so that W=C3+w. There are no gauged interactions specified, hence no additional D-term scalar potential. The supergravity F-term potential is V=eK(Kijˉ​Di​WDj​W​−3∣W∣2), with eK=1/(xy3). Its Kähler covariant derivatives of a superpotential are
DS​W=−αA−xW​,DT​W=−y3W​,DC​W=3C2+y3CˉW​.
(16)
The nonzero metric entries are
KSSˉ​=x−2,(KTTˉ​KCTˉ​​KTCˉ​KCCˉ​​)=y23​(1−Cˉ​−CT+Tˉ​).
(17)
The determinant of this T,C block is 9/y3>0, so it is positive definite. With the inverse-index convention Kijˉ​​Kkjˉ​=δik​, the raised components are
KSSˉ=x2,(KTTˉKCTˉ​KTCˉKCCˉ​)=3y​(T+TˉC​Cˉ1​).
(18)
Keeping these complex-conjugate off-diagonal entries in their correct index positions is essential.
To exhibit the no-scale supergravity cancellation, write the T,C norm as
Kijˉ​Di​WDj​W​​T,C​=3y​[y∣DT​W∣2+∣DC​W+CˉDT​W∣2]=3∣W∣2+3y∣C∣4.
(19)
Here DC​W+CˉDT​W=3C2. The 3∣W∣2 term cancels the negative term in the supergravity F-term potential. The result is the nonnegative scalar potential
V=xy3∣C3+b+(1+αx)A∣2​+xy23∣C∣4​.​
(20)
This is the matter-logarithm no-scale potential, specialized to the exponential superpotential.
Choose the supergravity auxiliary field convention Fi=−eK/2Kijˉ​Dj​W​. Substitution into the full inverse Kähler metric gives
FSFTFC​=eK/2xC3+b+(1+αx)A​,=eK/2yA+b​,=−eK/2yCˉ2.​​
(21)
An overall auxiliary-field phase is conventional. The C dependence of FT cancels between the two metric terms. If all three auxiliary fields vanished at a finite physical point, FC=0 would force C=0, FT=0 would force A+b=0, and FS=0 would then force αxA=0. Thus a=b=0 is necessary. For any nontrivial choice of a,b, supersymmetry is broken at every finite physical point. The allowed exception a=b=0, C=0 is supersymmetric; the statement cannot hold for all arbitrary parameters without this qualification.
The no-scale volume runaway criterion also determines whether the minimum is attained. For fixed S,C, differentiation with respect to y gives
∂y∂V​=−xy43∣C3+b+(1+αx)A∣2​−xy36∣C∣4​.
(22)
It is negative unless both numerators vanish. Therefore a finite stationary vacuum must satisfy
C=0,b+(1+αx)ae−αS=0.​
(23)
Every such point is a global minimum with Vmin​=0. For a,b=0, setting r=∣b/a∣ and z=αx>0 reduces existence to
r=(1+z)e−z/2,arga−αImS=arg(−b)(mod2π).
(24)
The derivative of the magnitude function is (1−z)e−z/2/2. It rises from 1 at the excluded boundary z=0 to 2e−1/2 at z=1, then falls to zero. Consequently a finite zero-energy minimum exists exactly when
a,b=0,0<∣b/a∣≤2e−1/2,​
(25)
or in the separate trivial family a=b=0. There is one positive root for 0<r≤1, two for 1<r<2e−1/2, and one double root at equality. If only one of a,b vanishes, or r exceeds this maximum, there is no finite minimum: the infimum is zero as ReT→∞. Calling this runaway a finite zero-energy vacuum would be incorrect.
At a nontrivial zero-energy minimum, W=A+b=−αxA=0, and the auxiliary expectation values reduce to
FS=FC=0,FT=eK/2yWˉ=0.
(26)
This explicitly verifies supersymmetry breaking with zero vacuum energy in no-scale supergravity. Both real components of T are exact flat directions of a scalar potential on the minimum locus. Away from that locus, ImT is still absent from the potential, but ReT is not flat. At a minimum, C is lifted at quartic order: at fixed S,T, V=3∣C∣4/[x(T+Tˉ)2]+O(∣C∣6). A zero quadratic mass does not make C an exact flat direction. The quartic stabilization at a double no-scale root likewise gives a vanishing quadratic mass, but no exact flat direction, for ReS at z=1. For a=b=0, the whole C=0 family has both S and T flat and supersymmetry unbroken.
Finally, the gravitino mass from a superpotential is
m3/2​=x​y3/2∣C3+A+b∣​.​
(27)
At a nontrivial zero-energy minimum this becomes
m3/2​=y3/2αx​∣A∣​=(1+αx)y3/2αx​∣b∣​>0.​
(28)
It depends on the unfixed volume modulus, so the model does not predict one numerical mass. For a=b=0, C=0, the gravitino is massless.
