A large molecular Schmidt number Sc=ν/κ separates the velocity's smallest smooth length scale from the scalar's smaller diffusive length scale. Follow a Lagrangian trajectory X(t) and measure displacement x from it. Within a sufficiently small scalar packet, Taylor expansion of the smooth velocity field gives
u(X(t)+x,t)−u(X(t),t)=C(t)x+O(∥∇2u∥∣x∣2),Cij​(t)=∂j​ui​(X(t),t),trC=0.
(27)
The moving frame removes uniform sweeping, leaving a linear random velocity gradient tensor. Thus the local scalar equation is the advection-diffusion equation with velocity Cx, and averaging over realizations of C gives the corresponding ensemble average. The approximation requires the packet to lie within the velocity's locally smooth region. Its thin, diffusive direction can satisfy this condition even when its long direction eventually becomes too large for one global linearization; applications to a spatially varying flow then use local packet descriptions. The statistical conclusions below refer to the linear-flow model and the usual mixing assumptions, not to every conceivable trace-free random matrix process.
Write Q=∫χdA and assume sufficiently rapid decay to discard boundary terms. Integration by parts gives
Q˙​=−∫(Cx)⋅∇χdA+κ∫ΔχdA=(trC)Q=0.
(28)
For Jij​=∫xi​xj​χdA, integrate the advective term once and the diffusive term twice. Since ∂k​(xi​xj​)=δki​xj​+δkj​xi​ and Δ(xi​xj​)=2δij​, one obtains
Q˙​=0,J˙=CJ+JCT+2κQI.​
(29)
These are mass conservation and the evolution of the unnormalized second moment tensor. The first moment obeys M˙=CM, so a centered packet stays centered.
For a positive Gaussian scalar packet in a linear incompressible flow, B must be a positive-definite matrix. Diagonalizing its symmetric matrix and applying the Gaussian integral gives
Q=detB​πf​,J=2Q​B−1,B=2Q​J−1,f=2πdetJ​Q2​.​
(30)
The factor Q2 in the last identity matters because J has not been divided by the scalar mass. Substitution into the advection-diffusion equation also gives
B˙=−CTB−BC−4κB2,ff˙​​=−2κtrB.
(31)
These identities agree with the second moment equation and prove that the Gaussian family is preserved. For an initially negative packet the same analysis applies after replacing χ by −χ and taking its positive mass.
To see the roles of stretching and diffusion explicitly, let the deformation gradient F solve F˙=CF, F(0)=I. Incompressibility gives detF=1. Differentiating F−1JF−T integrates the moment equation:
J(t)=F(t)[J(0)+2κQ∫0t​F(s)−1F(s)−Tds]F(t)T.
(32)
Without diffusion, J=FJ(0)FT and detJ is constant. In a statistically stationary, mixing flow with positive Lyapunov exponent λ, the two stretching exponents are λ and −λ. Writing the ordered eigenvalues of J as e2ρ1​,e2ρ2​, their typical initially nondiffusive evolution is
ρ1​≃λt+O(t​),ρ2​≃−λt+O(t​),ρ1​+ρ2​=constant.
(33)
The fluctuation scale presumes the usual central limit theorem for accumulated strain; it is not implied by incompressibility alone. During this stage the packet becomes long and thin but keeps its area and peak concentration.
In the instantaneous principal-axis basis, write C for the rotated velocity gradient tensor. Differentiation of an eigenvalue gives
ρ˙​i​=Cii​+κQe−2ρi​,dtd​(ρ1​+ρ2​)=κQtr(J−1)≥0.​
(34)
The formulas hold through a repeated eigenvalue by continuity of their sum. The compressed physical variance is e2ρ2​/Q. Its contraction is arrested when it is of order κ/λ, the square of the Batchelor scalar microscale ℓB​∼κ/λ​. If its initial length is L≫ℓB​, the crossover occurs at
tB​∼λ−1log(L/ℓB​).​
(35)
Thereafter ρ2​ has stationary fluctuations associated with the strain and the diffusive floor, while ρ1​ continues to grow typically at rate λ. Under sufficiently fast mixing, the central part of the distribution of ρ1​ is approximately Gaussian with mean λt+O(1) and variance Dρ​t+o(t) for a strain-dependent coefficient Dρ​. This central approximation does not determine far tails, which control high concentration moments; those require a full large deviation principle. The two logarithmic widths need not be independent. The area now increases and f decreases typically as e−λt. This is diffusion arrest of Gaussian packet compression.
For example, a constant principal strain C=diag(λ,−λ) gives the exact physical variances
Qj1​(t)​=(L12​+λκ​)e2λt−λκ​,Qj2​(t)​=(L22​−λκ​)e−2λt+λκ​.
(36)
The thin variance approaches its diffusive floor, the long variance grows exponentially, and the mass-to-area relation gives the quoted typical amplitude decay. A random strain additionally makes rare histories important for concentration moments.
For a precise statistical formulation define the logarithmic area increase
Ht​=ρ1​(t)+ρ2​(t)−ρ1​(0)−ρ2​(0)=21​logdetJ(0)detJ(t)​≥0.
(37)
The Gaussian identities give the exact relation f(t)=f(0)e−Ht​; hence
⟨∣χ(0,t)∣μ⟩=∣f(0)∣μEe−μHt​.
(38)
Assume the long-time strain statistics give a large deviation principle for Ht​/t on s≥0, with convex good rate function I(s), I(λ)=0. At exponential accuracy the probability of Ht​/t near s is e−tI(s). Combining that cost with the amplitude factor and applying the Laplace principle for probability measures gives the large-deviation decay rates of a Gaussian scalar packet
γμ​=s≥0inf​{I(s)+μs},⟨∣χ(0,t)∣μ⟩=exp[−γμ​t+o(t)].​
(39)
The initial amplitude only changes a time-independent prefactor. One can see the minimization directly by dividing the possible area-growth rates into small intervals: each contributes its probability cost times e−μts, and the interval with least total exponential cost dominates. An interval around a minimizing s gives the matching lower bound. The nonnegative area increase bounds the exponential weight by one, so unusually large positive growth cannot produce a divergent weighted tail. Using the area rather than only ρ1​ also avoids discarding possibly important fluctuations of the compressed direction.
Suppose I is strictly convex and differentiable, with its minimum at λ>0 and finite negative right derivative at zero. Define
μ∗​=−I′(0+).​
(40)
For 0<μ<μ∗​ the minimizing rate sμ​ is positive and uniquely solves
I′(sμ​)=−μ,γμ​=I(sμ​)+μsμ​.
(41)
For μ≥μ∗​, convexity puts the minimum at the endpoint s=0, giving
γμ​=I(0)(μ≥μ∗​).​
(42)
High moments are dominated by rare histories with little net area growth, in which a packet keeps a substantial concentration. Their probability cost is I(0), independent of the moment order. A finite threshold is conditional: if I′(0+)=−∞, the minimizer can approach zero without reaching it at any finite μ.
Below the threshold, differentiation of the minimizing expression cancels the terms involving sμ′​ because I′(sμ​)+μ=0. Thus γμ′​=sμ​, and
dμd​(μγμ​​)=−μ2I(sμ​)​<0(0<μ<μ∗​).​
(43)
Strict negativity uses a nondegenerate rate function with a unique zero at λ: for positive μ, sμ​<λ and I(sμ​)>0. For deterministic strain the ratio can instead be constant. Near the typical rate, if I(s)≃(s−λ)2/(2D) with D>0, then γμ​≃λμ−Dμ2/2 for small μ. If this quadratic expression holds all the way to zero, it gives the illustrative full result
γμ​={λμ−Dμ2/2,λ2/(2D),​0<μ<λ/D,μ≥λ/D.​
(44)
The quadratic form is an example, not a universal law of turbulent strain.
A thin scalar packet is stretched until molecular diffusion broadens its compressed direction; conservation of its scalar mass then converts area growth into dilution. Ensemble averages sample different stretching histories, producing different decay rates for different moments and saturation when weakly stretched histories dominate. The present moment calculation is specifically for the centered Gaussian packet and averaging over the flow; signed random initial fields with overlapping packets can also have cancellations and require their initial-field statistics.
Finally, positive exponential decay is a statistical conclusion requiring the strain assumptions above, not a consequence of the local differential equation by itself. For C≡0 and initially isotropic physical variance L2, the exact solution has J=Q(L2+2κt)I and f=Q/[2π(L2+2κt)]. Its moments decay algebraically as t−μ. Thus the general equations for the exponential rates are the rate-function minimization above; numerical rates and a finite saturation threshold require the long-time statistics of the flow.
