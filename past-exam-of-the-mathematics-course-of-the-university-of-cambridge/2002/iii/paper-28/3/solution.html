The joint law is the Edwards-Sokal coupling. For a fixed spin configuration, the sum over each edge state contributes (1−p)+pδe​(σ). Since δe​ is either zero or one, setting β=−log(1−p) gives
(1−p)+pδe​(σ)=(1−p)eβδe​(σ).
(20)
Consequently the spin marginal distribution is the Potts measure
μ1​(σ)=Z′1​exp(βe∈E∑​δe​(σ)),e−β=1−p,Z′=(1−p)∣E∣Z​​.
(21)
This follows from a finite sum that factorizes over edges, without any independence assumption on the spin marginal.
For a fixed edge configuration, the joint weight vanishes unless the spins agree across every open edge, equivalently unless they are constant on each open percolation cluster. Each of the k(ω) clusters, including isolated vertices, may independently be assigned any of q colors. Thus exactly qk(ω) spin configurations have nonzero weight, and their common weight is the edge-product factor divided by Z. The edge marginal distribution is therefore the random-cluster measure
μ2​(ω)=Z1​[e∈E∏​pω(e)(1−p)1−ω(e)]qk(ω)​.
(22)
In this convention Z′′=Z. Dividing the joint mass by this positive marginal gives the conditional distribution
μ(σ∣ω)=q−k(ω)1{σ is constant on every open cluster}​.
(23)
Equivalently the clusters receive independent uniformly chosen colors. Conversely, conditionally on the spins, edges are independent: an unequal-spin edge must be closed, while an equal-spin edge is open with probability p. These two conditional descriptions explain the Edwards-Sokal coupling directly.
If x and y lie in the same open cluster, their conditional spin-equality probability is one. If they lie in different clusters, their two independent uniform colors agree with probability 1/q. Averaging over the random-cluster measure yields
μ1​(σ(x)=σ(y))=μ2​(x↔y)+q1​[1−μ2​(x↔y)],
(24)
and hence the spin-connectivity identity for the Potts model
μ1​(σ(x)=σ(y))−q1​=(1−q1​)μ2​(x↔y)​.
(25)
The identity also includes x=y, when connectivity and spin equality are both certain.
