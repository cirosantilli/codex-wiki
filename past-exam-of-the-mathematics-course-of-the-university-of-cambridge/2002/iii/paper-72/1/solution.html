Use sequential semicolon indices: Xa;kl​=∇l​(∇k​Xa), where the second covariant derivative also differentiates the lower index of ∇k​Xa. For a torsion-free connection, direct expansion gives
Xa;kl​=Xa,kl​+Γabk,l​Xb+Γabk​Xb,l​+Γabl​Xb,k​+Γapl​Γpbk​Xb−Γpkl​Xa,p​−Γpkl​Γabp​Xb.
(1)
Subtract the expression with k,l exchanged. Commuting partial derivatives cancel the first term; the first-derivative terms cancel in pairs; symmetry of the lower Christoffel symbol indices cancels both terms containing Γpkl​. What remains is
Xa;kl​−Xa;lk​=(Γabk,l​−Γabl,k​+Γapl​Γpbk​−Γapk​Γpbl​)Xb=Rabkl​Xb.
(2)
This is the Ricci identity with the curvature convention printed on the PDF's first page. In terms of derivative operators it is [∇l​,∇k​]Xa=Rabkl​Xb; writing the operator order explicitly avoids reversing the sign.
The lower-index rule follows by applying the commutator to the scalar contraction Ya​Xa. Its second derivatives commute, so the product rule implies Ya;kl​−Ya;lk​=−Rpakl​Yp​. The derivative commutator is a derivation on tensor products, since the cross terms cancel. It therefore acts once on every index, with a positive sign on each upper index and a negative sign on each lower index. For an arbitrary tensor of valence (r,s),
Ta1​⋯ar​b1​⋯bs​;kl​−Ta1​⋯ar​b1​⋯bs​;lk​=α=1∑r​Raα​pkl​Ta1​⋯p⋯ar​b1​⋯bs​​−β=1∑s​Rpbβ​kl​Ta1​⋯ar​b1​⋯p⋯bs​​.​
(3)
Local sums of decomposable tensors establish the formula for every tensor field, rather than only for a single tensor product.
