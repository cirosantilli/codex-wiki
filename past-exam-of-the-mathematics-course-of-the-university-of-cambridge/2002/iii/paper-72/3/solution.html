The last is particle number conservation in a relativistic fluid. Off shell n,ϵ are independent variational fields; on shell the thermodynamic relation ties them to the specified function p(ϵ).
For the Hilbert stress-energy tensor, keep these matter fields fixed while varying the inverse metric. The volume variation is δ−g​=−21​−g​gab​δgab, and δQ=χ,a​χ,b​δgab. Therefore

The velocity equation follows directly from the potential, rather than requiring an independent assumption about the fluid force. Since ϵua​=∇a​χ and the connection has zero torsion, the canonical vorticity of a relativistic fluid vanishes:
∇a​(ϵub​)−∇b​(ϵua​)=0.

Contract with ua and use uaua​=1 and ua∇b​ua​=0. This gives ϵab​=∇b​ϵ−ub​ϵ˙. Multiplication by n and use of ∇b​p=n∇b​ϵ produces the relativistic Euler equation. The evolution system is therefore

It is equivalent to stress-energy conservation together with number conservation, subject to the potential-flow restriction. The action describes an isentropic barotropic sector of a perfect fluid in general relativity; it does not provide general vortical or entropy-carrying fluid motions. The constraint on canonical vorticity is stronger than the Euler equation alone.
For the weak static metric, use c=1. To the retained order, −g​=1+ϕ, u0=1−ϕ+O(ϕ2,∣v∣2) and ui=vi. The correction to u0 is required by normalization, even though its zeroth-order value is one. The relevant Christoffel symbols are
Γ00i​=Γ0i0​=∂i​ϕ+O(ϕ∇ϕ),Γi00​=∂i​ϕ,

with no spatial connection at this order. The cancellation −g​u0=1+O(ϕ2,∣v∣2) makes the leading number equation particularly simple:
∂t​n+∇⋅(nv)=0.

The corresponding leading energy transport equation is ∂t​ρ+v⋅∇ρ+(ρ+p)∇⋅v=0. The spatial Euler equation contains the gravitational acceleration −∇ϕ and pressure force −∇p/(ρ+p). In a strict first-order expansion, convective velocity acceleration and products of the small quantities are discarded.
To exhibit a fully linear system, perturb a homogeneous rest state: n=n0​+δn, ρ=ρ0​+δρ, p=p0​+δp, and ϵ=ϵ0​+δϵ, with the perturbations, v and ϕ all first order. Let w0​=ρ0​+p0​=n0​ϵ0​. The weak-field slow-motion equations for a relativistic fluid are

δp=cs2​δρ,cs2​=dρdp​​0​=ϵ0​p′′(ϵ0​)n0​​.

Without this invertibility, the primitive conservation equations remain valid and this sound-speed parametrization must not be used.
The potential furnishes a further check on the pressure and gravity signs. Write χ=ϵ0​t+δχ. Its time and spatial components imply
∂t​δχ=δϵ+ϵ0​ϕ,v=−ϵ0​∇δχ​.

Their compatibility reproduces the linear Euler equation. Pressure gradients oppose gravitational acceleration, and hydrostatic balance is ∇δp=−w0​∇ϕ. Combining the energy and Euler equations gives the forced acoustic equation
∂t2​δρ−cs2​∇2δρ=w0​∇2ϕ.​
(28)
For positive cs2​, disturbances propagate as sound waves; the prescribed potential supplies a static forcing. A nonrelativistic equation of state gives p≪ρ≃mn, ϵ≃m, and reduces the inertia ρ+p to mass density, recovering the ordinary linearized continuity and Euler equations. Slow velocity alone does not justify dropping p from the inertia of a relativistically hot fluid. The given metric supplies an external gravitational potential; the matter action by itself supplies no Poisson equation for it.
