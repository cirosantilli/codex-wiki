For b=kμ=0, the integral is (τ0​−τ∗​W)/(ib)+(1−W)/b2. Substitution and collection of endpoint terms gives

At μ=0 the original source is zero and the expression has the continuous limit Δ0​=Δ∗​. The integral form is numerically preferable near that limit, where the separated endpoint terms cancel.
The last two terms are respectively a local observer monopole and dipole. For measured higher angular multipoles, remove them. The remaining emission bracket contains initial brightness, an emission velocity-type term, and a gravitational Sachs-Wolfe effect term. Under ordinary adiabatic initial conditions for the growing synchronous matter mode, Δ∗​∼Ak1/2τ∗2​, while the velocity endpoint scales as Ak−1/2τ∗​. Relative to Ak−3/2 these are suppressed by (kτ∗​)2 and kτ∗​ respectively. Thus for modes outside the horizon at last scattering,
Δℓ≥2​ is dominated by the emission term −Ak−3/2e−ikμD.​
(18)
Its k−3/2 amplitude is the scale-invariant large-angle contribution. In Newtonian language it is the ordinary endpoint Sachs-Wolfe effect, not a late integrated effect in a constant-potential matter universe. With completely arbitrary prescribed Δ∗​, no unconditional dominance theorem is possible: an independently large initial anisotropy could dominate. The exact solution above remains valid without that adiabatic assumption.
