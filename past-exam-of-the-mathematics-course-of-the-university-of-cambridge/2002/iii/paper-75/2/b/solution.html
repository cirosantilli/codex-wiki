To match the positive source printed in the question, choose synchronous gauge metric
ds2=a2[−dτ2+(δij​−hij​)dxidxj].
(10)
The commonly used convention with δij​+hij​ gives the opposite source sign; switching conventions means replacing h and hS​ by their negatives together. This distinction is necessary when comparing with the usual synchronous photon brightness equation.
Let pα=dxα/dλ be the photon tangent for an affine parameter. The covariant geodesic equation is
dλdp​ν​​=21​gαβ,ν​pαpβ.
(11)
For ν=0, the homogeneous scale-factor contributions cancel by the null geodesic condition, leaving dp​0​/dλ=−a2hij′​pipj/2. Since p​0​=−a2p0=−q and dτ/dλ=p0, use the background pi=p0ni in this first-order term to obtain
q′=21​qhij′​ninj.​
(12)
Using the affine form also avoids confusing a covariant p0​ with the contravariant factor p0 required when changing the geodesic parameter to coordinate time.
In conformal time, the equation from part(a) becomes f1′​+ni∂i​f1​+q′f0′​=0. A Fourier mode proportional to eik⋅x has streaming term ikμf1​. Multiply by −4/(qf0′​); its prefactor is background-time independent. With Δ=−4f1​/(qf0′​),
Δ′+ikμΔ=4q′/q=2hij′​ninj.
(13)
Contracting the scalar decomposition explicitly gives
hij′​ninj=3h′​+(μ2−31​)hS′​,
(14)
so
Δ′+ikμΔ=32​(h′−hS′​)+2μ2hS′​.​
(15)
This is the photon brightness equation for a negative spatial perturbation. For a perturbation of blackbody-temperature form, f1​=−qf0′​Θ, so Δ=4Θ is the photon brightness perturbation. A more general initial spectral distortion can retain q dependence; the gravitational forcing is still independent of q in this linear massless equation.
