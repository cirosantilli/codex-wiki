Set z=ey. The metric becomes
ds2=z−2[dz2−dt2+dx2],z>0,
(23)
so these are Poincare coordinates on anti-de Sitter spacetime with unit curvature radius. They cover one patch, not the whole global extension.
On the two-dimensional section with the three transverse xi​ suppressed, choose embedding coordinates X−1​=secρcosT, X0​=secρsinT, Xd​=tanρ. The Poincaré transformation is
z=cosT+sinρcosρ​,t=cosT+sinρsinT​.​
(24)
Choose the connected component containing T=ρ=0. Its denominator is positive, giving
−π/2<ρ<π/2,∣T∣<π/2+ρ.​
(25)
Its two sloping edges are Poincaré horizons, null coordinate boundaries at z→∞. The right vertical edge is the z→0 timelike conformal boundary. Substituting the transformation into z−2(dz2−dt2) gives sec2ρ(dρ2−dT2), verifying the chart and its null edges.
The full coordinates in this part have y∈R, hence the whole shaded z>0 patch. If one additionally retains only the Randall–Sundrum bulk y≥0 as in Question4, keep z≥1. The brane y=0, or z=1, has cosT=cosρ−sinρ and is drawn in orange; the retained side contains T=0, ρ<0. A patch horizon is not a curvature singularity or the timelike conformal boundary. A geodesic can cross it in finite proper time while the patch coordinate t diverges.
