In the ambient space of signature (−,−,+,…,+), AdS is X⋅X=−L2. Let an arbitrary event have embedding vector XP​, and let VP​ be any future unit timelike tangent, so
XP​⋅VP​=0,VP​⋅VP​=−1.
(20)
The complete family through that event is
X(s)=XP​cos(s/L)+LVP​sin(s/L).​
(21)
Direct substitution gives X2=−L2 and X˙2=−1. Its ambient acceleration is X¨=−X/L2, normal to the hyperboloid, so its tangential covariant acceleration vanishes. This proves that s is proper time and that each curve is a timelike geodesic, rather than just guessing sinusoidal tracks from the diagram.
An AdS isometry can place any chosen P at T=ρ=0. Rotate the spatial direction into the displayed signed diameter. Write the initial local velocity as v=tanhχ, with ∣v∣<1. Then X−1​=Lcos(s/L), X0​=Lcoshχsin(s/L) and X1​=Lsinhχsin(s/L). Eliminating s using the global coordinates gives
sinρ=vsinT,∣v∣<1.​
(22)
Varying the speed gives the drawn family; suppressed spatial directions can be restored by varying the tangent direction. It represents all timelike initial directions after the isometry and spatial rotations.
Every finite-speed curve turns before the conformal boundary. At proper time πL, all reach −XP​; at 2πL their ambient coordinates return to XP​. This is Anti-de Sitter timelike geodesic refocusing. On the universal cover the global time has advanced by π and 2π, respectively, so these are later events. They are not closed timelike curves. The limits ∣v∣→1 approach the null directions and are not timelike members of the family.
