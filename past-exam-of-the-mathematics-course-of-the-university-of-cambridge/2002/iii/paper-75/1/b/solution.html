For adiabatic initial conditions, the coupled photon-baryon fluid begins with a coherent displacement and approximately zero initial acoustic velocity. Neglecting slowly varying gravitational driving, its effective temperature satisfies an oscillator equation with phase krs​, where
cs2​=3(1+R)1​,R=4ργ​3ρb​​,rs​(τ∗​)=∫0τ∗​​cs​dτ.
(4)
Thus the Sachs-Wolfe combination has a dominant cosine phase, while the velocity has a sine phase. At last scattering, the first three temperature Cosmic microwave background acoustic peaks correspond approximately to krs​=π,2π,3π: first compression, first rarefaction, and second compression of the initially displaced plasma in a potential well. Baryon inertia shifts the oscillator's equilibrium and enhances compressions relative to rarefactions, producing the familiar odd/even peak contrast. Potential decay and radiation driving alter the amplitudes, especially of the earliest peaks.
Adjacent displacement extrema differ by π, so
Δk=rs​π​,Δℓ≃rs​(τ∗​)πDM​(τ∗​)​=θs​π​,θs​=DM​rs​​.​

Here DM​ is the transverse comoving distance to last scattering; in a flat model it is τ0​−τ∗​. If cs​ is treated as constant, this reduces to Δℓ≃π(τ0​−τ∗​)/(cs​τ∗​). No numerical spacing is determined without a cosmological distance and sound horizon. Gravitational phase shifts, baryon loading and projection displace the exact peak locations from integer multiples of this spacing. These are primarily coherent density-plus-potential acoustic peaks; the label “Doppler peaks” does not mean the velocity term alone creates their sequence.
