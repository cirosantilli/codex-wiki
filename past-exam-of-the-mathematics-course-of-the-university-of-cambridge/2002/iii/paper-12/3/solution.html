Past exam of the mathematics course of the University of Cambridge / 2002 / iii / Paper 12 / 3 / Solution

A singular two-dimensional affine toric variety in the normal fan construction must come from a full-dimensional pointed cone; cones of smaller dimension give smooth charts with a torus factor. Let its primitive boundary generators be v0​,vt+1​. Choose an oriented lattice basis with v0​=(1,0), and write the other generator as (a,r), r>0. Primitivity gives gcd(a,r)=1. Changing the second basis vector by a multiple of the first lets us write the cone as
σ=R≥0​(1,0)+R≥0​(−q,r),0<q<r,gcd(q,r)=1.
(14)
Here r>1 because determinant one would give a smooth chart. The sublattice N′=Z(1,0)+Z(−q,r) has index r, and its affine chart is C2. The quotient N/N′ is cyclic, generated by (0,1), whose coordinates in the two boundary generators are (q/r,1/r). Thus the kernel of the corresponding finite map of tori acts on the two affine coordinates as (x,y)↦(ζqx,ζy). More explicitly, invariant monomials are exactly those xiyj with qi+j≡0(modr), which are the elements of the original dual lattice in the first quadrant. Their ring is C[σ∨∩M]. Hence the singularity is the cyclic quotient
Uσ​≅C2/μr​=Xr,q​.​
(15)
The choice of the two boundary axes is unordered; exchanging them replaces q by its inverse modulo r.
The Hirzebruch–Jung resolution is computed by the negative Euclidean algorithm. Put r0​=r, r1​=q and recursively set
bi​=⌈ri​ri−1​​⌉,ri+1​=bi​ri​−ri−1​.
(16)
The nonzero remainders strictly decrease, 0≤ri+1​<ri​, and their greatest common divisor stays one; the process terminates with rt​=1, rt+1​=0. All bi​≥2, and unwinding the recursion gives the negative continued fraction
qr​=[b1​,…,bt​]−:=b1​−b2​−⋯−1/bt​1​1​.
(17)
Start with v0​=(1,0) and v1​=(0,1) and define vi+1​=bi​vi​−vi−1​. The determinant of every consecutive pair is one, by induction. Moreover
det(vi​,(−q,r))=ri​,
(18)
again by the same recursion and the initial values r,q. Each intermediate ray is therefore inside σ. To check the ordering explicitly, the first coordinates after v1​ are negative, the second coordinates positive, and successive determinants are positive. At the last step the determinant with the endpoint is zero. The vector vt+1​ is primitive, points in the same direction as (−q,r), and hence equals it. Subdivide by all the intermediate rays v1​,…,vt​. Every new cone is regular, and the subdivision has the same support, so it gives a proper smooth resolution.
Each internal ray has a compact invariant curve Ei​≅P1, because the quotient fan at it has the two opposite primitive rays. The relation vi−1​+vi+1​=bi​vi​ gives its self-intersection Ei2​=−bi​. This can also be checked directly: on the chart for (vi−1​,vi​) use coordinates (x,y) with Ei​={y=0}; on the next chart the coordinates are (u,v)=(xbi​y,x−1), with Ei​={u=0}. The normal-coordinate transition is that of OP1​(−bi​), proving the self-intersection. Neighboring exceptional curves meet transversely once and nonneighbors do not meet.
Since bi​≥2, there is no exceptional minus-one curve. Combinatorially, deleting vi​ gives a cone of determinant bi​>1. Deleting a longer consecutive block cannot give a regular cone either: determinants of successively more distant rays satisfy the same recursion with coefficients at least two and grow from 0,1 to integers at least two. Thus no proper smooth coarsening of this subdivision exists. This proves the minimality of the minimal resolution of a cyclic quotient surface singularity; equivalently its exceptional chain has no curve that can be blown down to a smooth point. The number of exceptional curves is the length t of the negative continued fraction.
If t=1, then r/q=b1​ is an integer, so coprimality gives q=1. Conversely q=1 gives the single coefficient [r]− and one curve. If t=2, then
qr​=b1​−b2​1​=b2​b1​b2​−1​.
(19)
The numerator and denominator on the right are coprime, so r=b1​b2​−1 and q=b2​≥2. Therefore q>1 and q∣r+1. Conversely, if these conditions hold, then b1​=(r+1)/q≥2, b2​=q≥2 give exactly this two-term expansion. Hence the requested equivalences are
t=1⟺q=1,t=2⟺q>1 and q∣r+1.​
(20)
Now consider a complete fan in dimension two with three rays and at most one singularity. Its three two-dimensional cones are the consecutive ray pairs. At least two cones are regular, and they share a ray. After an oriented lattice change, take their consecutive generators to be v1​=(1,0), v2​=(0,1) and v3​=(−1,b): the first cone is a basis, and det(v2​,v3​)=1 forces the first coordinate −1. Completeness forces b<0, because the third pair must span the missing sector with positive determinant det(v3​,v1​)=−b. Write b=−r, r≥1. The unique positive primitive relation is v1​+rv2​+v3​=0, so the fan is that of the weighted projective plane P(1,r,1), or P(1,1,r) after permuting weights. For r=1 this is P2; for r>1 its remaining cone has type Xr,1​. This proves the full three-ray classification
XΣ​≅P(1,1,r),r≥1.​
(21)
These are the complete three-ray toric surfaces with at most one singular point, including the smooth case.
For four rays and exactly the specified singularity, place the three regular cones consecutively. Normalize the middle one to have generators (1,0),(0,1). Its neighboring regular cones then force the cyclic ray list
v1​=(a,−1),v2​=(1,0),v3​=(0,1),v4​=(−1,b).
(22)
The remaining cone has determinant det(v4​,v1​)=1−ab=r, so ab=1−r. Thus a,b have opposite signs, and s=∣a∣ is a positive divisor of r−1. In the oriented lattice basis v4​,(0,−1),
v1​=−av4​+r(0,−1),
(23)
so the singular cone has quotient parameter amodr. It follows that its parameter is congruent to ±s. If identification with the specified singularity exchanges the two boundary rays, the parameter is inverted; this does not change the criterion, since for st=r−1 we have s−1≡−t(modr), and t is the complementary positive factor. The same identity with a minus sign handles (−s)−1.
Conversely suppose s∣r−1 and s≡εq(modr), ε∈{1,−1}. Choose a=εs and b=−ε(r−1)/s in the displayed four rays. They are primitive and cyclically ordered, with all four gaps less than a half-plane; the opposite signs of a,b ensure completeness. Three consecutive determinants are one, and the fourth is r. Its quotient parameter is a≡q(modr), so the only singular point has the required type. We have proved the four-ray completion criterion for a cyclic quotient surface singularity:
∃ such complete four-ray surface⟺∃s>0: s∣r−1, s≡±q(modr).​
(24)
