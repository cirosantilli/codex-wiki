Critical exponents describe leading singular powers near a continuous phase transition. Using the reduced temperature t=(T−Tc​)/Tc​, the standard definitions include
M(t,0)∼(−t)β(t<0),χ(t,0)∼∣t∣−γ,Csing​(t)∼∣t∣−α,ξ(t)∼∣t∣−ν.
(6)
These are the order-parameter critical exponent, magnetic-susceptibility critical exponent, heat-capacity critical exponent and correlation-length critical exponent. The critical-isotherm exponent is defined by M(0,H)∼sgn(H)∣H∣1/δ. The anomalous dimension η gives the critical long-distance connected correlation function, Gc​(x)∼∣x∣−(d−2+η), when an ordinary scale-invariant critical point exists.
In the Landau approximation, minimize the Landau free energy to obtain the equation of state, differentiate it with respect to the field conjugate to an order parameter to obtain magnetic susceptibility, and differentiate the minimized free energy with respect to temperature to obtain heat capacity. These operations determine β,δ,γ,α, respectively. Expanding the Landau-Ginzburg theory about its uniform minimum gives the quadratic fluctuation kernel Kq2+V′′(M), whose Fourier transform determines the correlation length and the anomalous dimension. At the ordinary quartic critical point this gives ξ2=K/V′′(M)∝∣t∣−1 and η=0.
For u>0, r=0 gives H=uM3 and hence δ=3; the equilibrium free energy −r2/(4u) gives a finite heat capacity discontinuity, conventionally α=0. Together with the explicit calculation below, the ordinary mean-field critical exponents are
α=0,β=21​,γ=1,δ=3,ν=21​,η=0.​
(7)
These describe neglect of fluctuation corrections, rather than the exact exponents of every low-dimensional Landau-Ginzburg theory.
