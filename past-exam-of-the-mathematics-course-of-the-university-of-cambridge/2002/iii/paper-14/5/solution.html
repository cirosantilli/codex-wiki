A Riemannian metric is a smooth positive-definite symmetric inner product gx​ on every tangent space. A Levi-Civita connection is a connection on the tangent bundle which is torsion-free, ∇X​Y−∇Y​X=[X,Y], and metric-compatible,
Xg(Y,Z)=g(∇X​Y,Z)+g(Y,∇X​Z).
(21)
Apply this identity with the cyclic triples (X,Y,Z), (Y,Z,X) and (Z,X,Y), add the first two and subtract the third, and replace differences of covariant derivatives by Lie brackets. The result is the Koszul formula
2g(∇X​Y,Z)=​Xg(Y,Z)+Yg(Z,X)−Zg(X,Y)−g(X,[Y,Z])+g(Y,[Z,X])+g(Z,[X,Y]).​​
(22)
Its right side depends only on g and the vector fields; nondegeneracy of g determines ∇X​Y uniquely. This proves uniqueness.
For existence, in a coordinate chart define
Γijk​=21​gkℓ(∂i​gjℓ​+∂j​giℓ​−∂ℓ​gij​),(∇X​Y)k=Xi∂i​Yk+Γijk​XiYj.
(23)
This is linear over smooth functions in X and obeys the Leibniz rule in Y. Symmetry Γijk​=Γjik​ proves zero torsion, and direct substitution gives
∂i​gjk​=gℓk​Γijℓ​+gjℓ​Γikℓ​,
(24)
which proves metric compatibility. On chart overlaps the two resulting connections satisfy the same intrinsic Koszul identity and therefore agree by uniqueness. They glue globally. Thus the existence and uniqueness of the Levi-Civita connection holds on every Riemannian manifold.
Fix the curvature convention
R(X,Y)Z=∇X​∇Y​Z−∇Y​∇X​Z−∇[X,Y]​Z.
(25)
For coordinate vectors ei​, define the Riemann curvature tensor components by Rij,kl​=g(R(ei​,ej​)el​,ek​). In these indices the Ricci and scalar contractions are
Ricjl​=gikRij,kl​,s=gjlRicjl​=gikgjlRij,kl​​.
(26)
Explicitly, Rij,kl​=gka​Ralij​, where
Ralij​=∂i​Γjla​−∂j​Γila​+Γipa​Γjlp​−Γjpa​Γilp​.
(27)
These expressions define Ricci curvature and scalar curvature without ambiguity about which indices are contracted. With this convention a round unit m-sphere has Ric=(m−1)g and s=m(m−1).
If Ric=λg with a constant real λ, tracing gives s=mλ​, so every Einstein metric has constant scalar curvature. The converse is not valid in arbitrary dimension. On S2×S1 with the product Riemannian metric of the unit round sphere and a circle, the connection splits between factors. The mixed curvatures and circle curvature vanish, while the sphere has curvature one. Hence
Ric=gS2​⊕0,s=2 everywhere​.
(28)
A scalar multiple of the whole metric would require λ=1 on the sphere directions and λ=0 on the circle direction, which is impossible. This proves that constant scalar curvature does not imply an Einstein metric, and supplies a counterexample to the unrestricted requested equivalence.
In dimension two, the curvature tensor is determined by the Gaussian curvature K:
Rij,kl​=K(gik​gjl​−gil​gjk​),Ric=Kg,s=2K.
(29)
There the valid equivalence is
s constant⟺Ric=λg with constant λ=s/2​.
(30)
In dimension one both curvature and scalar curvature vanish identically. Without a two-dimensional restriction or another added geometric hypothesis, the general converse cannot be proved.
