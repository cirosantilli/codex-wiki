Use metric signature (+,−,…,−) and initially set ℏ=1. For an internal scalar line with momentum p, the Feynman propagator is i/(p2−m2+i0). An r-leg Feynman vertex, for r=3,4,6, contributes −iλr​, with all incident momenta incoming. Its 1/r! in the Lagrangian density is canceled by the permutations of its identical fields. Each vertex conserves momentum and supplies (2π)dδ(d)(∑p). Integrate each independent loop momentum with ddk/(2π)d, multiply the line and vertex factors, and divide by the Feynman-diagram symmetry factor for labeled external legs. Sum the distinct graphs and external-momentum assignments without counting the same assignment twice.
An unamputated momentum-space correlation function also contains the external propagators. Dividing the generating functional by its zero-source value removes disconnected vacuum bubbles; taking its logarithm selects connected correlation functions. Amputation of external propagators removes the external line factors when computing vertex amplitudes. Translational invariance leaves one overall momentum-conservation delta function for each connected graph.
The loop order is the number of independent graph cycles, equivalently the number of independent momentum integrations left after vertex conservation. A spanning tree through V interaction vertices contains V−1 internal edges; hence, with I internal lines,
L=I−V+1.​
(1)
Restoring ℏ in exp(iS/ℏ) gives ℏ for each internal propagator and ℏ−1 for each interaction vertex. Thus an amputated connected amplitude has
ℏI−V=ℏL−1.​
(2)
The same counting holds for a diagram in logZ[J] when the external source is coupled as Jϕ/ℏ: every external propagator brings ℏ, canceled by the source insertion's ℏ−1. This is Planck-constant loop counting with source normalization. An ordinary connected expectation of n fields, with no such source factors, instead scales as ℏL+n−1; multiplying logZ by −iℏ to form W shifts its source-kernel counting to ℏL. The normalization must be specified when applying the printed loop rule.

A one-particle-irreducible Feynman diagram is connected and remains connected after any single internal line is cut. The left graph below contains two quartic vertices and two parallel internal lines: cutting either line leaves the vertices connected by the other. The right graph contains two cubic vertices joined by one internal line, whose removal disconnects it, so it is a one-particle-reducible Feynman diagram. Both have four external legs.
The quantum effective action generates the one-particle-irreducible vertices. Their ultraviolet divergences and those of their proper divergent subgraphs are removed by local counterterms. Reducible connected graphs are reconstructed by joining these vertices with full propagators; they do not introduce independent local interaction counterterms merely because they contain bridges. This makes the one-particle-irreducible Feynman diagram decomposition useful for organizing renormalization. Overall power counting still has to be supplemented by subtraction of divergent subgraphs.
