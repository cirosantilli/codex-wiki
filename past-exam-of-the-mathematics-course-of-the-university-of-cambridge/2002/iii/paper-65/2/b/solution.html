A linear operator on finite-dimensional vectors acts by (Au)i​=∑j​Aij​uj​. For a field the discrete index becomes a continuous coordinate, and a suitable operator is represented by an integral kernel, possibly a distribution:

The identity operator has kernel δ(d)(x−y). If an inverse exists on the chosen domain and with the chosen boundary prescription, its kernel satisfies
∫ddzA(x,z)A−1(z,y)=δ(d)(x−y),​

with the analogous reversed identity. A differential operator is a distributional kernel: for example, A(x,y)=(−□x​−m2)δ(d)(x−y). Its inverse is a Green function. Boundary conditions, a causal prescription and the treatment of zero modes are part of the inverse's definition, not consequences of formal matrix notation. An operator with an untreated zero mode does not have an inverse on the full field space.
