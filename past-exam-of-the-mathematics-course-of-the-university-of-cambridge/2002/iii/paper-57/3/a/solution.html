Use fractions of all farms, so Sn​+In​+Rn​=Pn​ and ∑n​Pn​=1. Assume farm size is fixed, contacts mix randomly, infectious periods have size-independent exponential mean 1/γ, and the farm-size distribution has finite moments μ=∑n​nPn​ and μ2​=∑n​n2Pn​. Write I=∑n​In​. The farm-size stratified SIR model with susceptibility proportional to size is
S˙n​=−βnSn​I,I˙n​=βnSn​I−γIn​,R˙n​=γIn​.
(14)
Here β includes the common contact-rate constant; using frequency-dependent mixing avoids introducing an extra total-farm factor. An infectious farm of any size generates βnPn​/γ infections in initially susceptible size-n farms. The next-generation matrix Knm​=βnPn​/γ has rank one, giving the basic reproduction number
R0​=βμ/γ.​
(15)
The comparison SIR model replaces every size by μ, so it has the same basic reproduction number and the same initial exponential growth rate βμ−γ.
As the epidemic develops, put Λ(t)=β∫0t​I(u)du. For negligible initial infection, Sn​=Pn​e−nΛ. Large farms leave the susceptible class faster. If μS​=∑n​nSn​/S with S=∑n​Sn​, differentiation gives
μ˙​S​=−βIVarS​(n)≤0.
(16)
The mean size of newly infected farms is μinc​=∑n​n2Sn​/∑n​nSn​, larger than the susceptible mean unless all sizes agree. If μI​=∑n​nIn​/I, then
μ˙​I​=IβI∑n​nSn​​(μinc​−μI​).
(17)
Recovery cancels from this equation because its rate is size-independent. Starting with representative infectives, μI​ initially rises toward the size-biased mean μ2​/μ; later depletion of large susceptible farms lowers μinc​ and usually lowers μI​. It is incorrect to assert that the mean size of infectives must decrease from every possible initial condition. In the growing linearized mode, In​∝nPn​, so its mean is already μ2​/μ.
The aggregate growth equation is I˙=I(βSμS​−γ). The falling susceptible mean adds a depletion effect absent from the homogeneous SIR model. The final size relation for an epidemic, with vanishing initial seed and attack fraction A, is
A=1−∑n​Pn​e−(β/γ)nA.
(18)
By Jensen's inequality, the right side is at most 1−e−R0​A, so A is no larger than the homogeneous attack fraction. For a common small representative seed the illustrated epidemic is flatter and infects fewer farms; exact pointwise ordering of infected fractions for every time and arbitrary initial seed is not a consequence of equality of R0​.
The original sketch uses equally many farms of sizes 1 and 3, recovery rate γ=1, and a representative infected seed of 0.001. Each homogeneous comparison has R0​=2. The left, middle and right columns correspond to parts (a), (b) and (c). The top row shows infected farm fractions, and the bottom row distinguishes the mean sizes of susceptible and infected farms. These are illustrative trajectories under the stated model, not universal empirical outbreak curves.
