Take “cousins” to mean first cousins with unrelated, outbred founders and unrelated spouses in the intervening generation. Interpret a homozygous chromosome as two copies identical by descent, and first use an idealization of independent, nonrecombining chromosome blocks. Ordinary sequence homozygosity is a different quantity: unrelated copies can have the same allele, so its probability also requires allele frequencies. Similarly, identity of an entire recombining chromosome requires a genetic map, not just a pedigree.
At a single autosomal locus, the probability of autozygosity in their child is the parents' kinship coefficient. Each of the two common grandparents contributes a path of length two from each parent, with probability (1/2)2+2+1. Thus the first-cousin inbreeding coefficient is
F=2(1/2)5=1/16.
(43)
If there are L independent autosomal blocks, the probability that none is autozygous is (1−F)L. Hence the usual 23-block idealization gives
Pr(at least one autozygous block)=1−(15/16)23.​
(44)
The exponent describes the stated independent-block approximation; it is not a claim that all 23 human chromosome pairs inherit like autosomes.
Humans have 22 autosomal pairs. A son has one X and one Y and cannot have two homologous X copies. A daughter can have an autozygous X, with probability FX​ determined by the sex-linked cousin kinship calculation in part (b). Thus a sex-aware version is
Pr(at least one autozygous paired block)=1−(15/16)22(1−pD​FX​),​
(45)
where pD​ is the probability of a daughter, or is one or zero for a specified daughter's or son's sex. The independent assortment approximation applies between the autosomes and the X. Opposite-sex parentage alone does not determine FX​: the parents may be cousins through their mothers, their fathers or one of each. Without these pedigree and chromosome-block assumptions, the wording does not specify a unique probability of an entire homozygous human chromosome.
