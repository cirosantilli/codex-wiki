The subspace spanned by e4​,e5​,e6​ is the center of a Lie algebra. Each of their tensor squares is therefore invariant. On tensor products the infinitesimal Adjoint representation of a Lie algebra acts by a derivation, Dx​(u⊗v)=[x,u]⊗v+u⊗[x,v]. Write u⊙v=u⊗v+v⊗u and let S=e1​⊙e5​+e3​⊙e6​−e2​⊙e4​. The only potentially nonzero checks are
De1​​S=e4​⊙e6​−e6​⊙e4​=0,De2​​S=−e6​⊙e5​+e5​⊙e6​=0,De3​​S=−e4​⊙e5​+e5​⊙e4​=0.
(1)
The remaining generators are central, so they act trivially. This proves every claimed tensor invariant.
These tensors are contravariant. To obtain an ordinary metric tensor, take an invertible linear combination and invert it. For arbitrary real a4​,a5​,a6​ and t=0, set
Q=tS+a4​e4​⊗e4​+a5​e5​⊗e5​+a6​e6​⊗e6​.
(2)
In the ordered basis (e1​,e2​,e3​;e4​,e5​,e6​) its matrix is
Q=(0tKT​tKA​),K=​0−10​100​001​​,A=diag(a4​,a5​,a6​).
(3)
Since KTK=I, detQ=−t6=0, and
Q−1=(−t−2KAKTt−1KT​t−1K0​).
(4)
Let λa be the dual left-invariant coframe. The resulting neutral invariant metrics on the free two-step Lie algebra on three generators are
g=t−1(λ1⊙λ5+λ3⊙λ6−λ2⊙λ4)−t−2[a5​(λ1)2+a4​(λ2)2+a6​(λ3)2].​
(5)
Here (λa)2=λa⊗λa. Infinitesimal invariance of Q implies that its inverse is an adjoint-invariant bilinear form. Its left-translated metric is right-invariant on a connected G, giving the desired four-parameter family of bi-invariant metrics. A change of variables removes the central A block from the quadratic form, leaving three off-diagonal two-dimensional blocks. Each has one positive and one negative eigenvalue. Thus the metric signature is (3,3); these are bi-invariant pseudo-Riemannian metrics, not positive-definite ones.
Connectedness, conventionally understood here, matters for the passage from infinitesimal to full group invariance. If G is disconnected, its other components must preserve Q separately. For example the Lie algebra automorphism reversing e1​,e4​,e6​ and fixing e2​,e3​,e5​ preserves all brackets but sends S to −S. In the semidirect product of the simply connected group with this order-two automorphism, none of these nondegenerate t=0 tensors is invariant under that component. Thus the Lie-algebra data alone give the stated family on the identity component, rather than an unconditional extension to every disconnected realization.

The exterior derivative is a linear map d:Ωk(M)→Ωk+1(M), agrees with the ordinary differential on functions, satisfies d2=0, and obeys the graded Leibniz rule
d(α∧β)=dα∧β+(−1)kα∧dβ(α∈Ωk).
(6)
In local coordinates it differentiates the coefficient functions:
d(k!1​αi1​⋯ik​​dxi1​∧⋯∧dxik​)=k!1​∂j​αi1​⋯ik​​dxj∧dxi1​∧⋯∧dxik​.
(7)
Antisymmetry cancels the second partial derivatives, proving d2=0 locally. These properties determine the coordinate-independent derivative.
For any smooth ϕ:N→M, the pullback of a differential form is defined by
(ϕ∗α)x​(v1​,…,vk​)=αϕ(x)​(dϕx​v1​,…,dϕx​vk​).
(8)
There is no requirement that ϕ be invertible or an immersion. Pullback preserves wedge products and sends df to d(f∘ϕ) by the chain rule. Locally every form is a sum of fdxi1​∧⋯∧dxik​; applying the preceding identities and d2ϕi=0 proves
d(ϕ∗α)=ϕ∗(dα).​
(9)
Describe the brane by an embedding X:Σ→M, with nondegenerate induced worldvolume metric h=X∗g. Locally the Poincaré lemma gives F=dA because F is a closed differential form. For a timelike worldvolume, the geometric area and Wess-Zumino brane coupling give the action
S[X]=−T∫Σ​dp+1σ∣deth∣​+q∫Σ​X∗A,hαβ​=gμν​(X)∂α​Xμ∂β​Xν.​
(10)
T is the brane tension and q the charge in this normalization. The geometric term is the higher-dimensional version of the Nambu–Goto action. Both integrals are invariant under changes of worldvolume coordinates.
The additive gauge transformation is A↦A+dΛ. Its change of action is
δΛ​S=q∫Σ​X∗dΛ=q∫Σ​d(X∗Λ)=q∫∂Σ​X∗Λ.
(11)
The last step is the Generalized Stokes theorem. It vanishes for a closed worldvolume, or with suitable boundary restrictions. An open worldvolume can instead have a compensating boundary coupling, as in gauge invariance of an open-brane coupling.
More directly, for a variation vector V of the embedding, Cartan's magic formula gives
δ∫Σ​X∗A=∫Σ​X∗(ιV​F)+∫∂Σ​X∗(ιV​A).
(12)
Thus the interior force depends on F, not the gauge potential. Define the trace of the second fundamental form by
Kμ=∣h∣​1​∂α​(∣h∣​hαβ∂β​Xμ)+Γνρμ​hαβ∂α​Xν∂β​Xρ.
(13)
The area variation is −∫∣h∣​g(V,K) plus a boundary term. For compactly supported interior variations the resulting equations are
T∣h∣​Kμ​+(p+1)!q​εα0​⋯αp​Fμν0​⋯νp​​∂α0​​Xν0​⋯∂αp​​Xνp​=0.​
(14)
They exhibit the field-strength force on a charged brane and are gauge invariant.
Two global qualifications are necessary. First, replacing A by dΛ alone, as printed, gives dA′=0 and would erase a nonzero F; the intended transformation must be the additive shift above. Second, closedness of F only guarantees a potential locally. If a global potential does not exist and the worldvolume bounds a filling B, its charged term can be defined as q∫B​F. Different fillings differ by a period of F; independence of the quantum phase requires q∫C​F∈2πℏZ for the corresponding closed cycles. Without the required global potential or patching/filling data, the given closed form still defines the local variational equations, but not automatically a single globally defined real-valued action.

Use the index convention [ea​,ec​]=Ca​bc​eb​. For the left- and right-invariant coframes, the Maurer-Cartan equations are
dλb=−21​Ca​bc​λa∧λc,dρb=+21​Ca​bc​ρa∧ρc.​
(15)
Their dual invariant vector fields satisfy
[La​,Lc​]=Ca​bc​Lb​,[Ra​,Rc​]=−Ca​bc​Rb​,[La​,Rc​]=0.​
(16)
For example, evaluating dλb on two dual fields gives −λb([La​,Lc​]), since its invariant coefficient functions are constant. The opposite right-invariant bracket gives the other sign. The mixed bracket vanishes because left and right translations commute.
For a semisimple Lie algebra, the Killing form B(X,Y)=Tr(adXadY) is nondegenerate. It is adjoint invariant: using ad[Z,X]=[adZ,adX] and cyclicity of trace gives
B([Z,X],Y)+B(X,[Z,Y])=0.
(17)
Group conjugation preserves the trace as well, so this is invariant under the full group adjoint action. Translation supplies a bi-invariant pseudo-Riemannian metric.
Lower the indicated structure index by Cabc​=Bbe​Ca​ec​. Then
Cabc​=B(eb​,[ea​,ec​])=−B(ea​,[eb​,ec​]).
(18)
This changes sign under exchange of a,c by the bracket and under exchange of a,b by invariance. Hence Cabc​=C[abc]​.
Let η(X,Y,Z)=B(X,[Y,Z]) at the identity and extend it as a left-invariant differential form. Its value is adjoint invariant, so the extended form is also right-invariant. The three-form in the question is −η with the index-lowering convention just specified, an irrelevant overall sign for these properties. To prove closedness rather than only invariance, apply the exterior derivative on four left-invariant fields. Its six bracket terms combine by invariance into
dη(W,X,Y,Z)​=−2[B([W,X],[Y,Z])−B([W,Y],[X,Z])+B([W,Z],[X,Y])]=−2B(W,[X,[Y,Z]]+[Y,[Z,X]]+[Z,[X,Y]])=0.​
(19)
The final equality is the Jacobi identity. Therefore the Cartan three-form
3!1​Cabc​λa∧λb∧λc​
(20)
is both bi-invariant and closed.

Define the raw Killing form by
K(X,Y)=Tr(adXadY).​
(21)
For a finite-dimensional real or complex Lie algebra in characteristic zero, it is nondegenerate exactly when the algebra is a semisimple Lie algebra. For a real compact semisimple algebra it is negative definite. Consequently the positive compact Killing metric is g=−K: for example on su(2) with [ei​,ej​]=εijk​ek​, Kij​=−2δij​ and −K gives a Riemannian metric on SU(2). An overall minus sign in the term “Killing metric” is common here; raw trace K itself is negative definite in this example.
For a Lorentzian metric use sl(2,R) with [h,e]=2e, [h,f]=−2f, [e,f]=h. Direct traces give
K(h,e,f)​=​800​004​040​​,
(22)
whose metric signature is (2,1). For a non-Abelian degenerate example, the three-dimensional Heisenberg Lie algebra has [x,y]=z central. Every product of two adjoint maps is zero, so K=0.
For any adjoint-invariant nondegenerate metric on a Lie group, the Koszul formula on left-invariant fields simplifies to
2g(∇X​Y,Z)=g([X,Y],Z),∇X​Y=21​[X,Y].​
(23)
Take R(X,Y)Z=∇X​∇Y​Z−∇Y​∇X​Z−∇[X,Y]​Z. The Jacobi identity gives
R(X,Y)Z=41​([X,[Y,Z]]−[Y,[X,Z]])−21​[[X,Y],Z]=−41​[[X,Y],Z].
(24)
Taking the trace of Z↦R(Z,X)Y=−adYadX(Z)/4 yields
Ric(X,Y)=−41​K(X,Y).​
(25)
Thus the metric g=K is a Killing-form Einstein metric with Einstein constant −1/4. With g=−K the same connection has constant +1/4. This proves the Einstein manifold assertion for every semisimple group, including products when the unscaled Killing form is used on all factors.
There is also a different, flat metric-preserving connection. Declare the global left-invariant frame parallel:
DX​(Yaea​)=X(Ya)ea​.
(26)
Its curvature is zero because applying two derivatives to coefficient functions and subtracting their commutator gives zero. Its metric derivative vanishes since gab​ is constant in this frame. Its torsion tensor on invariant fields is
T(ea​,ec​)=−[ea​,ec​],Ta​bc​=−Ca​bc​.​
(27)
For each fixed c, antisymmetry gives Ta​bc​=Cc​ba​, so this is the matrix of adec​. The torsion contractions of a left-parallel Lie-group connection therefore are
Ta​ac​=Tr(adec​)=0,Ta​bc​Tb​ad​=Kcd​.​
(28)
The zero trace follows because a semisimple algebra is perfect: every element is a sum of brackets, and the adjoint of a bracket is a matrix commutator with zero trace. If the metric convention is g=−K, the second contraction is −gcd​. These flat translation connections on a Lie group are not the torsion-free Levi-Civita connection; nonzero torsion permits flatness alongside the curved Killing metric.

A Poisson manifold has a skew bilinear bracket on smooth functions which is a derivation in each argument and satisfies the Jacobi identity. Locally a Poisson bivector Π represents it by
{f,g}=Πij∂i​f∂j​g,Πij=−Πji.
(29)
The derivation property is automatic for this expression. On applying the Jacobi identity to coordinate functions, and then using the derivation property for arbitrary functions, the necessary and sufficient condition is
Πiℓ∂ℓ​Πjk+Πjℓ∂ℓ​Πki+Πkℓ∂ℓ​Πij=0.​
(30)
Equivalently its Schouten-Nijenhuis bracket [Π,Π] vanishes.
A symplectic manifold has a closed nondegenerate two-form ω. Write its coefficient matrix as Ωij​ and set Πij=(Ω−1)ij. It is skew and defines the bracket above. Differentiating ΠΩ=I gives ∂ℓ​Π=−Π(∂ℓ​Ω)Π. Substitution into the Jacobi condition gives
Πiℓ∂ℓ​Πjk+cyclic=ΠiaΠjbΠkc(∂a​Ωbc​+∂b​Ωca​+∂c​Ωab​)=0,
(31)
where the last equality is dω=0. Thus every symplectic manifold is Poisson. In this convention the Hamiltonian vector field satisfies ιXf​​ω=−df, and {f,g}=Xg​f; this will also be the convention below.
The converse fails because a Poisson bivector need not be invertible. On R2, take Π=0. Its bracket is identically zero and satisfies every Poisson axiom, but it cannot be the inverse of any nondegenerate two-form. This is an even-dimensional counterexample, so odd dimension is not the only obstruction.

Use ιXf​​ω=−df and {f,g}=Xg​f, so in the stated real coordinates {qi,pj​}=δji​ and f˙​={f,H}. For ξ∈g, let ξP​ be the actual fundamental vector field of the left action, obtained by differentiating exp(tξ)⋅x at zero. Since the action preserves ω, Cartan's magic formula gives d(ιξP​​ω)=0.
A global component Hamiltonian μξ​ requires this closed one-form to be exact, with
dμξ​=−ιξP​​ω,⟨μ(x),ξ⟩=μξ​(x).​
(32)
Choose these functions linearly in ξ. This defines the moment map, with equivariance when the constants have the required normalization. A symplectic action alone need not admit it: translation in a periodic coordinate of a symplectic two-torus contracts ω to a closed one-form with nonzero period. A sufficient existence condition is HdR1​(P)=0; more generally one assumes the action is weakly Hamiltonian and resolves its moment-map equivariance obstruction.
Because the group also preserves H,
{H,μξ​}=Xμξ​​H=ξP​H=0,μ˙​ξ​={μξ​,H}=0.​
(33)
The component vector fields commute with the Hamiltonian flow: in this convention [Xf​,Xg​]=−X{f,g}​, hence [Xμξ​​,XH​]=0. These are both conserved observables and infinitesimal symmetries of the dynamics.
For a left action, fundamental vector fields obey [ξP​,ηP​]=−[ξ,η]P​. Together with the Hamiltonian-field identity, this makes
c(ξ,η)={μξ​,μη​}−μ[ξ,η]​
(34)
have zero Hamiltonian vector field. It is therefore constant on each connected component of P. One sufficient condition for c=0 is an equivariant moment map, μ(gx)=Adg∗​μ(x), where ⟨Adg∗​ℓ,ξ⟩=⟨ℓ,Adg−1​ξ⟩. Differentiating this condition along ηP​ gives directly
{μξ​,μη​}=μ[ξ,η]​.​
(35)
A useful concrete sufficient condition is a common fixed point x0​ and the normalization μξ​(x0​)=0. The constant c is zero there because all fundamental fields vanish, and hence zero everywhere on connected P. This is fixed-point normalization removes the moment-map cocycle. If the action has an ineffective Lie-algebra kernel, the map to observables can have that kernel; equality of brackets alone is not a claim of a faithful representation.
For the oscillator, the printed complex coordinates give idzi​∧dzˉi​=dpi​∧dqi and {zi​,zˉj​}=iδij​. For an anti-Hermitian matrix ξ∈u(3), the fundamental field is δz=ξz. Direct contraction yields
ιξP​​ω=id(z†ξz).
(36)
Consequently the unitary moment map of an isotropic oscillator in our declared convention is
μξ​(z)=−iz†ξz.​
(37)
It is real because ξ is anti-Hermitian. Identifying u(3)∗ with Hermitian matrices by ⟨M,ξ⟩=−iTr(Mξ), this is simply μ(z)=zz†. Unitary invariance of H=z†z proves conservation of all nine real matrix components. Under z↦Uz, the matrix transforms as zz†↦Uzz†U−1, exactly the coadjoint transformation in this pairing. The origin is the required fixed point.
The bracket can also be checked without an equivariance theorem. For f=−izˉξz and g=−izˉηz, use
{f,g}=i∑j​(∂zj​∂f​∂zˉj​∂g​−∂zˉj​∂f​∂zj​∂g​).
(38)
Differentiation gives {μξ​,μη​}=−iz†[ξ,η]z=μ[ξ,η]​. The central generator ξ=iI has μξ​=H, while a real antisymmetric generator ξv=a×v has
μξ​=−izˉ⋅(a×z)=a⋅(q×p).
(39)
Thus the special orthogonal group subgroup has the usual angular momentum components L=q×p, with {Li​,Lj​}=εijk​Lk​. The remaining unitary generators supply the other conserved quadratic observables, with their signs fixed by the stated choice of complex coordinates and Hamiltonian convention.

A principal bundle with structural Lie group G is locally a product U×G, with changes of trivialization of the form (x,g)↦(x,t(x)g). Define a right action locally by (x,g)⋅h=(x,gh). It is independent of trivialization because t(x)(gh)=(t(x)g)h. It is smooth and obeys the group-action law. Right multiplication in G is free and transitive, so the global action is free and each fiber is a single orbit. Equivalently these properties and equivariant local triviality can be included in the definition of a principal bundle.
For a representation ρ:G→GL(V), form the associated vector bundle
E=P×ρ​V=(P×V)/[(p,v)∼(ph,ρ(h)−1v)].​
(40)
Projection sends [p,v] to the base point of p. A local section s(x) gives coordinates [s(x),v] and hence a local product U×V; on overlaps the principal transition functions act through ρ. Because these maps are linear in v, the fibers have well-defined vector-space structures.
For an n-dimensional manifold, its frame bundle has points u:Rn→Tx​M that are linear isomorphisms. Right action is composition u⋅A=u∘A for A∈GL(n,R). Coordinate frames provide local trivializations. Its associated bundle for the defining representation is the tangent bundle, by the well-defined map [u,v]↦u(v). A metric restricts the frames to linear isometries and reduces the group to O(r,s); choosing orientation gives the corresponding special-orthogonal reduction.
In Minkowski spacetime choose a reference oriented pseudo-orthonormal frame at the origin. Every such frame is specified by its base point a∈R3,1 and a Lorentz transformation L∈SO(3,1). The Poincaré group has multiplication
(a,L)(b,M)=(a+Lb,LM),
(41)
and its right Lorentz action is (a,L)⋅h=(a,Lh). Hence
E(3,1)=R3,1⋊SO(3,1) ⟶ E(3,1)/SO(3,1)≅R3,1,π(a,L)=a​
(42)
is the oriented orthonormal frame bundle of Minkowski spacetime. The same argument with O(3,1) gives the full unoriented bundle. If time orientation is fixed, use the orthochronous components throughout; the use of SO in the printed quotient is an orientation reduction.
For four-dimensional de Sitter spacetime, use the spacelike hyperboloid x⋅x=ℓ2 in R4,1. An element of SO(4,1) maps a fixed unit spacelike vector to x/ℓ, and its other columns give an oriented pseudo-orthonormal tangent frame. The stabilizer of that unit vector is SO(3,1). This identifies the oriented frame bundle of de Sitter spacetime and its projection:
SO(4,1)⟶dS4​=SO(4,1)/SO(3,1).​
(43)
The right stabilizer action changes the tangent frame without changing x.
For Anti-de Sitter spacetime, take x⋅x=−ℓ2 in R3,2. A unit timelike normal has orthogonal complement of signature (3,1), so its stabilizer is again SO(3,1). Appending this normal to a tangent frame realizes the oriented orthonormal frame bundle of anti-de Sitter spacetime as
SO(3,2)⟶AdS4​=SO(3,2)/SO(3,1).​
(44)
Connected components or orientation/time-orientation restrictions must be chosen consistently. This quotient is the anti-de Sitter quadric, which has a periodic timelike coordinate. If “anti-de Sitter spacetime” denotes its universal cover, pull the frame bundle back to that cover, rather than identify the covered spacetime with the unmodified quotient.
