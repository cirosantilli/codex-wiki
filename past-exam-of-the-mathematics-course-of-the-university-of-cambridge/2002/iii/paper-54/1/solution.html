Because s is arc length, ∣dx/ds∣=1. Thus y=n/α, where n is the unit tangent to the elastic ray. In an isotropic medium this is also the wavefront normal. Consequently y is the vector slowness, of magnitude 1/α; locally y=∇T=k/ω, with T the travel time and k the wavevector.
For depth-dependent wave speed α(z), the ray equation gives dyx​/ds=dyy​/ds=0. The horizontal component of dx/ds therefore has a fixed direction, and the elastic ray lies in the vertical plane determined by that direction and its initial point. A vertical elastic ray is the limiting case. If i is the angle to the vertical, its conserved horizontal slowness gives
α(z)sini​=pflat​.​
(1)
At a horizontal interface, matching the tangential wavevector at fixed frequency gives the same invariant on both sides: this is Snell's law.
For radial wave speed α(r), take the cross product of position with slowness. The ray equations give
dsd​(x×y)=αy×y+x×∇(1/α)=0,
(2)
since the second vector is radial. A nonzero constant L=x×y is perpendicular to every position on the elastic ray, so the ray lies in a fixed plane through the centre. Radial rays obey the same conclusion with a nonunique plane. The spherical elastic ray invariant is therefore
p=∣L∣=α(r)rsini​,​
(3)
where i is measured from the radial direction. Across a spherical interface the radius is common and tangential slowness is continuous, so this form of Snell's law survives an interface discontinuity.
In a homogeneous sphere a P wave follows the straight chord between the two surface points. If their principal angular separation is 0≤Δ≤π, its length is 2Rsin(Δ/2). For constant P wave speed cP​,
TD​(Δ)=cP​2R​sin2Δ​.​
(4)
Now put a=R′ and denote the mantle and core P wave speeds by cm​>cc​. Equal incidence and reflection angles put the core-boundary reflection point on the angular bisector. Each mantle leg has length L given by L2=R2+a2−2Racos(Δ/2). Hence the core-reflected travel time is
TR​(Δ)=cm​2​R2+a2−2Racos(Δ/2)​,0≤Δ≤Δg​=2arccos(a/R).​
(5)
The stated domain is essential. At the reflection point the incoming line approaches from the mantle only if Rcos(Δ/2)≥a. Otherwise it has already crossed the core boundary. The mantle-only direct chord also exists only for Δ≤Δg​, with TD​=2Rsin(Δ/2)/cm​. In this interval,
L2−R2sin2(Δ/2)=[Rcos(Δ/2)−a]2≥0,
(6)
so the reflected arrival is later. Its intercept is 2(R−a)/cm​, whereas the direct intercept is zero. At grazing incidence the branches meet at T=2R2−a2​/cm​, and both have slope dT/dΔ=a/cm​. A continuation of the homogeneous-sphere direct formula beyond this endpoint is not a mantle-only arrival in the modified model.
The core-transmitted elastic rays consist of three straight legs. Their impact distances in mantle and core are bm​=pcm​ and bc​=pcc​, respectively. At core entry,
cm​sinim​​=cc​sinic​​=ap​,siniR​=Rpcm​​,0≤p≤a/cm​.
(7)
Since cc​<cm​, the elastic ray bends towards the normal on entry and away from it on exit. The mantle leg subtends im​−iR​ at the centre, and the core chord subtends π−2ic​. Thus the total angular sweep along the transmitted path is 2(im​−iR​)+π−2ic​. This sweep need not equal the principal separation if it exceeds π; the diagram uses a path with sweep below π.
