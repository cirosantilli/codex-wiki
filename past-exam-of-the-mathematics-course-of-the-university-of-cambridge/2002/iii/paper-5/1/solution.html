Equality in the first inequality for distinct points, or in the derivative inequality at one point, occurs exactly when h is an automorphism of the unit disk.
Here is a proof, including the underlying Schwarz lemma. If u:D→D is holomorphic and u(0)=0, then u(z)/z has a removable singularity at zero. On ∣z∣=r<1 its modulus is at most 1/r. The maximum modulus principle on this circle, followed by r↑1, gives ∣u(z)∣≤∣z∣ and ∣u′(0)∣≤1. Equality at a nonzero point, or ∣u′(0)∣=1, forces u(z)/z to be a constant of modulus one by the same maximum modulus principle.
Set ϕa​(z)=(z−a)/(1−az), an automorphism of the unit disk. The composite ϕh(a)​∘h∘ϕa−1​ fixes zero. The Schwarz lemma gives the first inequality, while its derivative at zero gives the second. The equality conclusion follows because a composite attaining equality is a rotation. Conversely, every automorphism of the unit disk attains equality. This also proves contraction of the hyperbolic metric.
The boundary lower bound first guarantees a zero inside the unit circle. If f(0)=0, there is nothing to prove about existence. Otherwise, if f had no zeros on the closed unit disc, 1/f would be holomorphic on a neighborhood of it and satisfy ∣1/f∣≤1/A on the boundary. The maximum modulus principle would give ∣f(0)∣≥A, contradicting the hypothesis. The boundary lower bound excludes boundary zeros, so there is a zero a with ∣a∣<1.
The upper boundary bound and the maximum modulus principle give ∣f∣≤B on the unit circle and its interior. The function is nonconstant under the stated hypotheses, so f/B takes its interior values in the open unit disc. Apply the Schwarz-Pick lemma to the points a and 0:
∣a∣≥B∣f(0)∣​.​

This is the zero location bound from Schwarz-Pick, and it applies to every zero inside the unit circle.
