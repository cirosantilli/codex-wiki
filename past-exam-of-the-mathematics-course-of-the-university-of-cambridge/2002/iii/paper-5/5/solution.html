Take the Jacobi theta functions at zero, with q=eπiτ:
θ2​(τ)=∑n∈Z​q(n+1/2)2,θ3​(τ)=∑n∈Z​qn2,θ4​(τ)=∑n∈Z​(−1)nqn2.
(22)
Define the modular lambda function by
λ(τ)=θ3​(τ)4θ2​(τ)4​.​
(23)
This is an explicit holomorphic expression on the complex upper half-plane. The product forms
θ2​θ3​θ4​​=2q1/4m≥1∏​(1−q2m)(1+q2m)2,=m≥1∏​(1−q2m)(1+q2m−1)2,=m≥1∏​(1−q2m)(1−q2m−1)2​
(24)
show that all three are nonzero there. The theta identity θ34​=θ24​+θ44​ gives 1−λ=θ44​/θ34​, so λ omits 0,1 and infinity on the half-plane.
The theta-constant inversion and translation laws, obtained from Poisson summation and changing indices in the defining sums, give
λ(−1/τ)=1−λ(τ),λ(τ+1)=λ(τ)−1λ(τ)​.
(25)
These formulas imply invariance under the projective group
Γ(2)​={(ac​bd​)∈SL2​(Z):a,d odd, b,c even}/{±I},
(26)
generated by τ↦τ+2 and τ↦τ/(2τ+1). This is the effective level-two principal congruence subgroup.
Its action is properly discontinuous because it is a discrete subgroup of PSL2​(R). It is also free. An elliptic integral matrix has trace 0 or ±1. The odd traces are excluded because a+d is even. Trace zero would give d=−a and bc=−a2−1≡2(mod4), impossible when b,c are even. Thus the only elements acting trivially are the already quotiented central matrices ±I.
A fundamental domain is the ideal quadrilateral
−1<Reτ<1,∣τ−1/2∣>1/2,∣τ+1/2∣>1/2,
(27)
with vertices −1,0,1,∞. The vertical sides are paired by translation by two, and the semicircular sides by τ↦τ/(2τ+1). Reduction using these pairings gives the domain; equivalently it is a union of six translates of the standard modular fundamental domain, consistent with the six elements of PSL2​(F2​). On adjoining the cusp points, there are three vertex classes, two paired edges and one face. The resulting orientable compact surface has Euler characteristic 3−2+1=2, hence is a sphere. This is the compactification of the level-two modular curve.
All three cusp widths are two, so their local parameters are obtained from q=eπiτ after a modular change of coordinate. The defining products give
λ(τ)=16q−128q2+O(q3)
(28)
at infinity: there is a simple zero. At the cusp zero, inversion gives 1−λ=16q0​+O(q02​), so the value is 1. At the cusp one, combine inversion and translation; the value is infinity and the pole is simple. The three cusp classes are precisely the parity classes of primitive rational pairs (p,q): odd-even, even-odd and odd-odd. A Bézout identity, with parity adjusted by adding multiples of (p,q), places any such cusp in the orbit of ∞, 0 or 1, respectively.
The descended meromorphic function on the compactified quotient thus has exactly one simple pole. Its degree is one, since the degree equals its total pole multiplicity; it is therefore a biholomorphic coordinate on that sphere. Removing the three cusps identifies the quotient with C∖{0,1}. Consequently
λ:H⟶C∖{0,1,∞}​
(29)
is a covering map with deck group Γ(2)​. The source half-plane is simply connected, so it is the required universal covering map. This proves the universal covering by the modular lambda function, including the absence of interior branching. Three arbitrary distinct punctures are handled by a target Möbius transformation.
