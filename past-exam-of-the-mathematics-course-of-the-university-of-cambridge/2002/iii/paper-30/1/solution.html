Let a1​,…,an​ be distinct elements of a finite field Fq​, with 1≤k≤n. The Reed-Solomon code consists of the evaluation vectors
RSk​(a)={(f(a1​),…,f(an​)):f∈Fq​[X], degf<k}.
(1)
Evaluation is an injective linear map: a nonzero polynomial of degree at most k−1 cannot vanish at all n distinct points. Thus the dimension is k. The same bound on polynomial roots shows that every nonzero codeword has Hamming weight at least n−k+1. Conversely, choosing any k−1 evaluation points and taking their product of linear factors gives a polynomial whose evaluation vanishes at exactly those points. Its Hamming weight is n−k+1. Hence
d=n−k+1.​
(2)
For completeness, deleting d−1 coordinates from a linear code of minimum Hamming distance d is injective on codewords, since two words with the same retained coordinates would differ in fewer than d positions. Therefore qk≤qn−d+1, giving the Singleton bound d≤n−k+1. The Reed-Solomon code attains this bound and is a maximum distance separable code. Multiplying individual coordinates by nonzero field elements changes neither the dimension nor the Hamming weight, so the same proof applies to a generalized Reed-Solomon code.
To specify the generator polynomials of a cyclic code unambiguously, use the primitive cyclic Reed-Solomon code convention. Put n=q−1, choose a primitive element of a finite field ω, and set
Ck,b​=⟨gb​(X)⟩⊆Fq​[X]/(Xn−1),gb​(X)=∏t=bb+n−k−1​(X−ωt).
(3)
Exponents are taken modulo n. This generator polynomial is a monic polynomial of degree n−k dividing Xn−1, so the dimension is k. It agrees with the evaluation form
Ck,b​={(aj1−b​f(aj​))j=0n−1​:degf<k},aj​=ωj.
(4)
Indeed, at a defining exponent t=b,…,b+n−k−1, a monomial f(X)=Xs contributes the finite geometric series ∑j​ajt+1−b+s​. Its exponent lies between 1 and n−1, so the sum vanishes: ωt+1−b+s=1 but its nth power is one. Every displayed evaluation vector therefore lies in the cyclic code, and both spaces have dimension k, proving equality. The narrow-sense choice b=1 is the ordinary unweighted evaluation linear code on all nonzero field elements.
The duality of primitive cyclic Reed-Solomon codes now follows directly. A vector in Ck,b​ has coordinates aj1−b​f(aj​), while a vector in Cn−k,1−b​ has coordinates ajb​h(aj​), with degh<n−k. Their dot product is ∑j​aj​f(aj​)h(aj​). Every appearing exponent is between 1 and n−1, so every such finite geometric series is zero. The two spaces are orthogonal and their dimensions sum to n, giving
Ck,b⊥​=Cn−k,1−b​.​
(5)
Thus the full cyclic family is closed under dual codes. The offset matters: the dual code of the narrow-sense linear code generally has offset zero, rather than one.
For arbitrary evaluation points, the precise statement is closure of generalized Reed-Solomon codes under duality. If C=GRSk​(a,v), put P(X)=∏i​(X−ai​) and ui​=(vi​P′(ai​))−1. Lagrange interpolation shows ∑i​p(ai​)/P′(ai​)=0 whenever degp≤n−2: this sum is the coefficient of Xn−1 in the interpolation formula for p. Taking p=fh with degf<k and degh<n−k proves orthogonality of C and GRSn−k​(a,u). Comparing dimensions gives equality with C⊥. An unweighted evaluation linear code at arbitrary points need not have an unweighted evaluation dual at the same points; the multiplier form and the cyclic offset formula resolve that convention issue.
For the length-15, dimension-11 linear code, choose b=1 and ω∈F16​ as in the field table. Then d=15−11+1=5 and
g1​(X)=∏t=14​(X−ωt).
(6)
The field characteristic is two, and the table gives ω4=ω+1. In the basis 1,ω,ω2,ω3, the four roots are represented by 0010,0100,1000,0011. Their elementary symmetric functions are
s1​s2​s3​s4​​=ω+ω2+ω3+ω4=ω13,=ω3+ω4+ω5+ω5+ω6+ω7=ω6,=ω6+ω7+ω8+ω9=ω3,=ω1+2+3+4=ω10.​
(7)
The repeated terms in s2​ cancel because the field characteristic is two. There are no alternating minus signs in this characteristic, so
g1​(X)=ω10+ω3X+ω6X2+ω13X3+ω0X4,d=5.​
(8)
For example, the coefficient vectors in ascending order are 0111,1000,1100,1101,0001, giving the required single powers of ω.
For the length-10, dimension-6 linear code over F11​, take the table's primitive element of a finite field ω=2 and again b=1. The four roots are 2,4,8,5. Expanding in F11​[X] gives
g2​(X)​=(X−2)(X−4)(X−8)(X−5)=(X2+5X+8)(X2+9X+7)=X4+3X3+5X2+8X+1.​
(9)
Thus
g2​(X)=1+8X+5X2+3X3+X4,d=10−6+1=5.​
(10)
The coefficients are reduced modulo 11, including the constant term 2⋅4⋅8⋅5=1(mod11).
A suitable two-error-correcting Reed-Solomon code over F16​ is precisely the first linear code:
[n,k,d]16​=[15,11,5]16​,g(X)=g1​(X).​
(11)
Two distinct codewords cannot both be within Hamming distance two of a received word, because the triangle inequality would put them at distance at most four. Thus this linear code corrects any two symbol errors. Its generator polynomial has four consecutive defining zeros of a cyclic code, and its dimension and exact minimum Hamming distance have already been proved.
