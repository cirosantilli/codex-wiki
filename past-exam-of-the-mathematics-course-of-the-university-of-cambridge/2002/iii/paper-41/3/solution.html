Fix the sign convention first: depth z increases downward, while the vertical displacement amplitude ξ is positive upward. Write Π=δp for the Lagrangian pressure perturbation, and p1​,ρ1​ for Eulerian fluid perturbations. Hydrostatic equilibrium gives dp/dz=ρg, and the adiabatic relation gives δρ=Π/c2. Since the vertical displacement vector has component −ξ along increasing z,
p1​=Π+ρgξ,ρ1​=Π/c2+(dρ/dz)ξ.
(18)
In the Cowling approximation, horizontal momentum gives ξh​=ikp1​/(ρω2). The mass conservation equation δρ=−ρ(ik⋅ξh​−ξ′) and vertical momentum ω2ρξ=−p1′​+gρ1​ then eliminate the horizontal displacement. The plane-parallel Cowling displacement-pressure equations are
ξ′Π′​=−ω2gk2​ξ−(ω2k2​−c21​)ρΠ​,=−ρ(ω2−ω2g2k2​)ξ+ω2gk2​Π.​​
(19)
For ω2=gk, the second equation admits Π=0 and the first reduces to ξ′=−kξ. Thus the plane-parallel stellar f-mode has
ω2=gk,ξ=Ce−k(z−z0​),Π=0,ξh​=ikξ/k.​
(20)
Its Eulerian eigenfunctions are p1​=ρgξ and ρ1​=ρ′ξ, while δρ=0. Hence it has no Lagrangian compression even in a stratified star. It decays inward but grows upward, explaining the failure of a pure small-amplitude continuation to z→−∞.
For the lower part of the matched chromospheric interfacial mode, put ω2=gk(1−ε), ξ=e−k(z−z0​+εϕ) and Π=εfξ. The pressure amplitude is of order ε because the limiting f-mode has identically zero Lagrangian pressure amplitude; the frequency correction perturbs that zero solution. Expand gk2/ω2=k(1+ε)+O(ε2) and ω2−g2k2/ω2=−2gkε+O(ε2). The leading equations are
ϕ′=1+ρf​(g1​−kc21​),f′−2kf=2gkρ.​
(21)
Exclude the lower homogeneous component f∝e2kz, which would make Π grow into the star. Integrating the remaining equation with its integrating factor gives
f(z)=−2gke2k(z−z0​)∫z∞​e−2k(z′−z0​)ρ(z′)dz′.​
(22)
The accompanying correction is ϕ(z)=∫z0​z​[1+(f/ρ)(1/g−1/(kc2))]ds, with the integrand evaluated at s. This fixes ϕ(z0​)=0 and determines the lower eigenfunctions to the stated order.
For the upper part, distinguish the rescaled sound speed by writing ρ=ερ​ and c2=ε−1c2. Set Π=εψ, ψ=Aek(z−z0​+εϕ) and ξ=μψ. To leading order the first displacement equation becomes ξ′=−kξ−kψ/(gρ​), while the pressure equation gives ψ′=kψ+ε[kψ+2gkρ​ξ]+O(ε2). Consequently
μ′+2kμ=−gρ​k​,ϕ′=1+2gρ​μ.​
(23)
The component μ∝e−2kz would give ξ∝e−kz, which diverges upward. Remove it by the upper boundary condition. The leading upper solution is
μ(z)=−gk​e−2k(z−z0​)∫−∞z​ρ​(z′)e2k(z′−z0​)​dz′,ψ(z)≃Aek(z−z0​).​
(24)
Integrating ϕ′ with ϕ(z0​)=0 gives the first correction to the upper exponential. The displayed ξ=μψ satisfies the required decay only for compatible upper density profiles; integral convergence alone is not the full boundary condition.
To match, define the positive weighted integrals
Ib​=∫z0​∞​e−2k(z−z0​)ρ(z)dz,Ia​=∫−∞z0​​ρ(z)e2k(z−z0​)​dz.
(25)
The lower normalization is ξ(z0​)=1 and f(z0​)=−2gkIb​. Continuity of Lagrangian pressure perturbation makes the upper A=f(z0​); continuity of fluid displacement then requires μ(z0​)A=1. Since 1/ρ​=ε/ρ, the upper integral gives μ(z0​)=−(k/g)εIa​. The two matching conditions therefore give
ε−1≃2k2Ia​Ib​.​
(26)
As a sign and normalization check, two incompressible constant-density layers give Ia​=(2kρa​)−1 and Ib​=ρb​/(2k), so ε≃2ρa​/ρb​. This agrees with expansion of the exact interfacial gravity-wave dispersion relation ω2=gk(ρb​−ρa​)/(ρb​+ρa​).
For validity, require ε≪1, convergent weighted integrals, a matching level separating the low-density high-sound-speed atmosphere from the denser envelope, and small accumulated corrections εk∣ϕ∣ over the regions carrying the matching integrals. The upper expansion also needs g/(kc2)≪1. The resulting upper displacement must tend to zero: for example if ρ​∝ea(z−z0​) at great height, its leading amplitude is proportional to e(k−a)(z−z0​), requiring a<k; the weaker condition a<2k merely makes the upper integral converge. Plane geometry, constant g and the Cowling approximation must be appropriate on the wavelength scale. The physical mode amplitude must remain small compared with both its wavelength and local background scale heights, so the adiabatic fluid perturbation remains linear. If these requirements fail, neither the matching formula nor continuation of the leading expansions is controlled.
