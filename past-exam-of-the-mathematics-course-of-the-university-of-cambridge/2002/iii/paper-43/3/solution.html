Let g>0 be the magnitude of the downward gravity and write αT​ for the thermal expansion coefficient, to distinguish it from the dynamo coefficient of the previous question. The conductive state has u=0, B=B0​z^ and T0​(z)=Tr​−ΔTz/d. Its gravitational term is absorbed in a background hydrostatic pressure.
For the dimensional two-dimensional fields, choose the stream function and Cartesian magnetic flux function conventions
u=(−ψz​,0,ψx​),B=(−Az​,0,Ax​),A=B0​x+χdim​.
(16)
These automatically give zero divergence. Define the Jacobian determinant bracket J(f,h)=fx​hz​−fz​hx​, so u⋅∇h=J(ψ,h). The full magnetic flux function, including the imposed field, must be retained in the magnetic force; using the perturbation alone would lose its linear restoring term.
The scalar vorticity is (∇×u)y​=−∇2ψ. Also (∇×B)y​=−∇2A, and hence
(∇×B)×B=−∇2A∇A,[∇×((∇×B)×B)]y​=−J(A,∇2A).
(17)
The buoyancy perturbation is gαT​(T−T0​)z^, whose curl in the y direction is −gαT​(T−T0​)x​. Taking the curl of the momentum equation and multiplying by minus one eliminates pressure and therefore gives
∂t​∇2ψ+J(ψ,∇2ψ)=ν∇4ψ+gαT​(T−T0​)x​+μ0​ρr​1​J(A,∇2A).
(18)
We have restored μ0​ in this dimensional expression. The printed magnetic force uses units in which μ0​=1, or equivalently a correspondingly rescaled field.
Use length scale d, time scale d2/κ, velocity scale κ/d, stream-function scale κ, magnetic flux-function scale B0​d, and temperature perturbation scale ΔT. Thus the dimensionless total flux function is
A=x+χ,T=Tr​+ΔT(Θ−z).​
(19)
Here x,z now denote coordinates divided by d, and the same T notation in the second identity denotes the physical temperature. The dimensionless parameters for thermal-diffusion scaling of planar magnetoconvection are
R=νκgαT​ΔTd3​,Q=μ0​ρr​νηB02​d2​,σ=κν​,ζ=κη​.​
(20)
These are the Rayleigh number, Chandrasekhar number, Prandtl number and magnetic-to-thermal diffusivity ratio. The nondimensional magnetic-force coefficient is B02​d2/(μ0​ρr​κ2)=σζQ, so omitting the factor ζ would use a different magnetic parameter.
The resistive induction equation becomes At​+J(ψ,A)=ζ∇2A. Since J(ψ,x)=−ψz​, its perturbation equation has source +ψz​. Similarly, advection of the conductive temperature gradient gives J(ψ,−z)=−ψx​, producing the source +ψx​ in the perturbation heat equation. The resulting nonlinear equations are therefore
(∇2ψ)t​+J(ψ,∇2ψ)Θt​+J(ψ,Θ)χt​+J(ψ,χ)​=σ[∇4ψ+RΘx​+ζQJ(A,∇2A)],=ψx​+∇2Θ,=ψz​+ζ∇2χ.​
(21)
This derives both forcing signs from the background fields, rather than selecting them by a stream-function convention after the fact.
For the specified roll mode put K=π/λ and pL​=K2+π2, the positive eigenvalue of minus the Laplacian on each spatial factor. Write
fs​=sinKxsinπz,fT​=cosKxsinπz,fA​=sinKxcosπz,
(22)
and take ψ=(pL​/K)afs​, Θ=bfT​, χ=λcfA​, with amplitudes depending on τ=pL​t. These factors satisfy the printed boundary conditions: sine factors make the normal velocity and Bx​=−χz​ vanish at the required faces; the second derivatives of the velocity factors give the stress-free conditions; Θ vanishes at the top and bottom and Θx​ vanishes at the sides.
Discard quadratic perturbation terms. Since ∇2A=∇2χ, the magnetic force linearizes to J(x,∇2χ)=(∇2χ)z​. The needed derivatives are
ψx​=pL​afT​,ψz​=pL​λafA​,Θx​=−Kbfs​,(∇2χ)z​=pL​πλcfs​.
(23)
The heat and induction equations give b′=a−b and c′=a−ζc. In the vorticity equation, its left side is −(pL3​/K)a′fs​ and its three right-side contributions are σ[(pL3​/K)a−RKb+ζQpL​πλc]fs​. Consequently, with
r=pL3​K2R​=λ2pL3​π2R​,q=pL2​π2Q​,
(24)
the three-amplitude vertical-field magnetoconvection system is
a′=σ(−a+rb−ζqc),b′=a−b,c′=a−ζc.​
(25)
Primes now mean d/dτ. They are genuine time derivatives, as the original PDF shows.
For its linear stability, seek amplitudes proportional to esτ. Eliminating b,c or taking the determinant gives
P(s)=(s+σ)(s+1)(s+ζ)−σr(s+ζ)+σζq(s+1)=s3+A1​s2+A2​s+A3​,
(26)
where
A1​=σ+1+ζ,A2​=σ+σζ+ζ−σr+σζq,A3​=σζ(1+q−r).
(27)
Assume the physical range σ>0, ζ>0, q≥0. The Routh-Hurwitz stability criterion gives strict decay exactly when A2​>0, A3​>0 and A1​A2​>A3​. At r=0 these hold. As r increases, the first loss of stability can occur through a zero eigenvalue or a conjugate imaginary pair.
The stationary threshold is
rs​=1+q.​
(28)
At this value, P(s)=s(s2+A1​s+C) with
C=ζ(σ+1)−σ(1−ζ)q.
(29)
If C>0, the other two roots have negative real parts and the zero root is simple. Its derivative with respect to r is ds/dr=σζ/C>0. This is stationary onset; the reflection symmetry of the roll permits a generic pitchfork bifurcation. The linear calculation does not determine whether the nonlinear branch is supercritical or subcritical.
For oscillatory onset set s=iω, ω=0. Separating real and imaginary parts gives ω2=A2​ and A1​A2​=A3​. Solving the latter for r yields
rH​=σ(σ+ζ)(1+ζ)​+σ+1ζ(σ+ζ)​q,ωH2​=ζ[σ+1σ(1−ζ)​q−ζ].​
(30)
A genuine Hopf bifurcation onset requires ωH2​>0, so
0<ζ<1,q>q∗​:=σ(1−ζ)ζ(σ+1)​.​
(31)
At this threshold P(s)=(s+A1​)(s2+ωH2​), giving the third root −A1​<0. The pair crosses transversely: differentiating P(s,r)=0 at iωH​ gives
ℜdrds​=2(A12​+ωH2​)σ(A1​−ζ)​=2(A12​+ωH2​)σ(σ+1)​>0.
(32)
The first instability of the conductive state is therefore stationary at rs​ if ζ≥1, or if 0<ζ<1 and q<q∗​. It is oscillatory at rH​<rs​ if 0<ζ<1 and q>q∗​. At q=q∗​, the two thresholds meet and P(s)=s2(s+A1​): the frequency is zero and neither a simple pitchfork nor a nonzero-frequency Hopf test applies. The oscillatory marginality in vertical-field magnetoconvection formula must not be used where its frequency squared is negative.
For conversion back to physical parameters, Rs,H​=λ2pL3​rs,H​/π2, while the dimensional Hopf frequency is (κpL​/d2)ωH​. These are onset conditions for the specified spatial mode. Identifying the globally first mode would additionally require comparison over the allowed spatial modes. Nonlinear saturation and the stability of the bifurcating finite-amplitude states are not determined by this linear three-amplitude system alone.
