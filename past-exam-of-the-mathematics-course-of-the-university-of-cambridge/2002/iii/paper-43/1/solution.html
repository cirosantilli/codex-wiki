A density deficit in a flux tube is not by itself an instability calculation. For example, a straight magnetized tube in otherwise field-free gas at the same temperature has lateral balance pi​+Bi2​/(2μ0​)=pe​. The perfect gas law then gives ρi​<ρe​, so the tube rises unless some other force balances its weight deficit. This configuration simply lacks magnetostatic equilibrium. A magnetic buoyancy instability, in contrast, starts from a force-balanced atmosphere and asks whether a small allowed displacement grows. A balanced horizontal magnetic layer can be stable or unstable; the presence of magnetic pressure alone does not decide this.
Assume a smooth equilibrium, with downward gravitational acceleration g>0, and write R for the gas constant per unit mass. The ambient total pressure and force balance are
P(z)=p(z)+2μ0​B0​(z)2​,P′(z)=−ρ(z)g,p=RρT.
(1)
We use ideal magnetic flux freezing on the displacement time scale and rapid lateral pressure equilibration. A bodily displacement perpendicular to a straight horizontal tube does not change its length or bend its field. If its area is A, conservation of magnetic flux and mass per unit length gives BA=constant and ρA=constant. Thus the tube preserves
f=ρB​.
(2)
Efficient heat exchange fixes its temperature to the ambient temperature at its new height, not to its original temperature. This is the thermal assumption that distinguishes the requested criterion from the adiabatic one.
Take a tube initially at z0​ and let f0​=B0​(z0​)/ρ(z0​). After a small upward displacement ξ, let ρi​(z) be the density it would have in lateral balance at z=z0​+ξ. Its preserved f0​ and imposed local temperature give
RT(z)ρi​(z)+2μ0​f02​ρi​(z)2​=P(z).
(3)
At the initial position ρi​=ρ. Differentiate this relation at z0​:
(cT2​+vA2​)ρi′​+RρT′=−ρg,cT2​=ρp​=RT,vA2​=μ0​ρB02​​.
(4)
For the ambient atmosphere the same differentiation must also differentiate f(z)=B0​(z)/ρ(z). It gives
(cT2​+vA2​)ρ′+RρT′+μ0​ρB0​​f′=−ρg.
(5)
Subtracting isolates the actual displaced density contrast:
ρi​(z0​+ξ)−ρ(z0​+ξ)=μ0​(cT2​+vA2​)ρB0​​f′ξ+O(ξ2).
(6)
The pressure-gradient force on the tube balances the weight of the displaced ambient gas, so its leading vertical buoyancy acceleration is
ξ¨​=−μ0​(cT2​+vA2​)gB0​​dzd​(ρB0​​)ξ.
(7)
The coefficient multiplying ξ is positive precisely when the tube becomes lighter on upward displacement. Thus the isothermal interchange criterion for magnetic buoyancy is
B0​dzd​(ρB0​​)<0,equivalentlydzd​(ρ2B02​​)<0.​
(8)
Where B0​>0, this is simply d(B0​/ρ)/dz<0, or dlogB0​/dz<dlogρ/dz. The squared form is independent of the field's choice of direction. Equality is neutral at this order, and the reversed inequality is restoring for these displacements. A layer with an interval satisfying the strict inequality admits local growing interchange disturbances under the assumed pressure/thermal equilibration. The calculation does not address field-bending undular modes, finite heat-transfer times or magnetic diffusion; those can change the criterion. In particular, the localized interchange criterion for a magnetized atmosphere with an adiabatic gas response is not interchangeable with this fast-thermal-exchange result.
