If θ has one sign, A=∣μ∣ and the ideal proposal gives zero variance. Its normalization or exact sampling may nevertheless be impractical. If A=0, the integrand vanishes almost everywhere and the estimator is identically zero.
The minimizing probability density function may vanish where θ=0 even though f>0 there. This is valid for the ordinary estimator, which needs probability support only for its integrand. If one insists on exactly the same positive probability support as f, the bound is instead an infimum whenever this vanishing occurs, approached by gδ​=(1−δ)g0​+δf as δ↓0: because gδ​≥(1−δ)g0​, the second moment lies between A2 and A2/(1−δ), proving convergence to the bound.
