Discarding zero observations and resolving ties is unnecessary with probability one under the stated independent continuous model. Rank the absolute observations from 1 to n, let Ri​ be the rank of ∣Xi​∣, and form the positive Wilcoxon signed-rank statistic
W+=∑i​Ri​1Xi​>0​.
(30)
Reject the null hypothesis for large W+. Under symmetry about zero, each sign is a fair independent binary variable, independent of all the absolute values. To see this independence, for every measurable positive set B, symmetry gives Pr(X∈B)=Pr(X∈−B)=21​Pr(∣X∣∈B). Independence of observations then gives independence of the whole sign vector from the absolute-value vector. Conditional on those absolute values, relabeling by rank therefore gives
W+=d∑r=1n​rBr​,Br​ independent Bernoulli(1/2).
(31)
The null distribution is consequently independent of the unknown symmetric shape. Its probability generating function is 2−n∏r=1n​(1+zr), so the coefficient of zw gives the exact probability of W+=w. Choose an upper critical value with null tail probability at most the desired level, or randomize at the boundary to obtain that level exactly. The observed upper-tail probability is an exact one-sided p-value. A positive translation raises each Walsh average, as established below, so this upper-tail rule has the correct direction against positive centers for every fixed symmetric shape.
The independent-sign representation proves
E0​W+=21​r=1∑n​r=4n(n+1)​,Var0​W+=41​r=1∑n​r2=24n(n+1)(2n+1)​.​
(32)
The asymptotic null distribution, stated without proof, is
n(n+1)(2n+1)/24​W+−n(n+1)/4​ d​ N(0,1).​
(33)
This supplies the usual upper normal distribution critical value when exact calculation is inconvenient; for the discrete upper tail Pr(W+≥c), use c−1/2 in the continuity correction.
For exact equivalence with the proposed statistic, reorder the observations so ∣X(1)​∣<⋯<∣X(n)​∣. Partition the unordered pairs, including diagonals, by the member with larger absolute value. The block belonging to X(r)​ consists of the r−1 pairs with an earlier member and its diagonal pair. For an earlier member X(j)​, the strict inequality ∣X(j)​∣<∣X(r)​∣ means the sign of X(j)​+X(r)​ is the sign of X(r)​; the diagonal sum has that same sign. Thus the entire block supplies exactly r positive averages when X(r)​>0, and none otherwise. Summing the blocks proves
#{(i,j):i≤j, (Xi​+Xj​)/2>0}=r=1∑n​r1X(r)​>0​=W+.​
(34)
The two tests therefore have exactly the same statistic, critical values and p-values, not merely the same limiting distribution. The Walsh averages are dependent, so their positivity indicators must not instead be treated as an independent binomial sample. For a translation Xi​=Zi​+θ of any null-symmetric sample, every Walsh average increases by θ; their positive count is nondecreasing, which also verifies the direction of the one-sided test.
