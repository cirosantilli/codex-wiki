The P-star approximation describes the sampling density of a regular maximum-likelihood estimator in sample coordinates consisting of the fitted parameter θ and a suitable ancillary statistic a. For a d-dimensional parameter, write ℓ(θ;θ,a) for the log-likelihood expressed in these coordinates, and j(θ;θ,a) for the fitted observed information. The approximation is
p∗(θ∣a;θ)=c(θ,a)∣j(θ;θ,a)∣1/2exp{ℓ(θ;θ,a)−ℓ(θ;θ,a)}.
(1)
The determinant factor supplies the local volume scale, while the exponential retains the full likelihood ratio, rather than replacing it by a quadratic. The normalizing constant is chosen by integrating over the fitted-parameter coordinates; its leading regular large-sample value is (2π)−d/2. These ingredients arise from a statistical saddlepoint approximation. Their use requires an interior, locally unique fit, nonsingular information and suitable smoothness; an ancillary statistic specifies the conditioning surface when the fit alone does not exhaust the relevant sample coordinates. In a regular model the unnormalized leading expression has relative error of order n−1 locally; normalization and conditioning matter for refined inference. It is not a universal exact density. Under a smooth one-to-one parameter transformation, the Hessian at the fit transforms without a score term, so its determinant supplies precisely the density Jacobian determinant. This proves the invariance of the construction under one-to-one changes of statistical parameters.
