Write Γ=SL2​(Z) and use the determinant-normalized slash operator
(f∣k​α)(τ)=(detα)k/2(cτ+d)−kf(ατ),α=(ac​bd​),detα>0.

The automorphy factor identity proves (f∣k​α)∣k​β=f∣k​(αβ), and positive scalar matrices act trivially. Let Mn​ be all integral matrices of determinant n>0. It is stable under left and right multiplication by Γ. Its left orbits have unique representatives
Πn​={(a0​bd​):ad=n, a,d>0, 0≤b<d}.
(28)
Indeed Bézout's identity reduces the first column to (a,0), the determinant fixes d=n/a, and a row shear reduces b modulo d. These are the determinant-n matrix representatives for Hecke operators. For composite n, Mn​ can contain more than one double coset; keeping every integral matrix, including nonprimitive ones, is essential.
Define the Hecke operator by

Changing a representative on the left leaves its summand unchanged. Right multiplication by Γ permutes left orbits of Mn​, so the result satisfies the weight-k transformation law. Each summand is holomorphic. Its expansion at infinity is bounded, and vanishes there when f is a cusp form; the group has just one cusp class. Therefore Tn​ preserves both Mk​ and Sk​, and T1​ is the identity.

For cusp forms the Petersson inner product is
⟨f,g⟩=∫Γ\H​f(τ)g(τ)​yky2dxdy​.

To see the change-of-variables mechanism, fix a double coset ΓαΓ⊂Mn​ and put H=Γ∩α−1Γα. Representatives γ of H\Γ give left representatives αγ. Unfolding the sum in ⟨∑γ​f∣k​αγ,g⟩ changes the integral over Γ\H to the integral over H\H of (f∣k​α)g​. The substitution w=ατ preserves dxdy/y2 and has ℑw=(detα)y/∣cτ+d∣2; these factors move the slash from f to g∣k​α−1. Refold over αHα−1=Γ∩αΓα−1 to get the reverse double coset. Since scalar matrices act trivially, α−1 has the same slash action as its adjugate α∗=nα−1, an integral matrix of determinant n. The adjugate involution permutes all the double cosets in Mn​. The common real factor nk/2−1 is unchanged, giving ⟨Tn​f,g⟩=⟨f,Tn​g⟩.
Since Sk​ is finite dimensional, commuting self-adjoint linear operators have a common orthonormal basis of Hecke eigenforms: diagonalize one operator, restrict the others to its eigenspaces, and repeat until these spaces cannot split further. If f is a nonzero common Hecke eigenform with Tn​f=λn​f, the coefficient at q gives an​=λn​a1​. Were a1​=0, every positive coefficient would vanish, contrary to f=0 being cuspidal. L(f,s)=p∏​(1−ap​p−s+pk−1−2s)−1.​

Finally, for positive even k≥4, Mk​=CEk​⊕Sk​, by subtracting the constant coefficient times the normalized Eisenstein series. The divisor identity
σk−1​(m)σk−1​(n)=∑h∣gcd(m,n)​hk−1σk−1​(mn/h2)

Thus Mk​ has an Eisenstein series eigenline and a basis of cuspidal Hecke eigenforms. Odd and negative weights have no forms, M2​=0, and on M0​=C the operator is the scalar σ−1​(n). These statements fix the normalization and describe the exceptional small weights as well.
