The product defining the modular discriminant converges locally uniformly for ∣q∣<1 and has integral Fourier coefficients. Its logarithmic derivative is
Dlog(q∏r≥1​(1−qr)24)=1−24∑r≥1​1−qrrqr​=1−24∑n≥1​σ1​(n)qn=E2​.
(13)
Therefore DΔ=E2​Δ. Comparing the coefficient of qn gives nτ(n)=τ(n)−24∑j=1n−1​σ1​(j)τ(n−j), or
(1−n)τ(n)=24j=1∑n−1​σ1​(j)τ(n−j).​
(14)
This also fixes the initial value τ(1)=1.
For the Ramanujan tau congruence modulo five, first justify the link between this product and E4​,E6​. Question 1 constructs Δ0​=(E43​−E62​)/1728∈S12​ with first coefficient one. Its Serre derivative is in S14​, which vanishes by the valence formula for the modular group: a nonzero such cusp form would contribute at least 1+1/2>14/12, since it vanishes at infinity and at i. Hence DΔ0​=E2​Δ0​. The product and Δ0​ have the same differential equation and leading coefficient. Equivalently their quotient has derivative zero near q=0 and limit one; the identity theorem gives
1728Δ=E43​−E62​.
(15)
Also D6​E6​∈M8​=CE42​. Its constant coefficient is −1/2, so the Serre derivative gives 2DE6​=E2​E6​−E42​.
Work now in the formal power series ring F5​[[q]]. The integral expansions give E4​≡1 and E2​≡E6​(mod5), because d5≡d(mod5), −24≡−504≡1, and 240≡0. The two identities reduce to
3Δ≡1−E62​,2DE6​≡E62​−1(mod5).
(16)
Thus 3Δ≡−2DE6​≡3DE6​, and division by the nonzero element 3 gives Δ≡DE6​. Since the coefficient of qn in DE6​ is −504nσ5​(n), we conclude
τ(n)≡nσ5​(n)(mod5)(n≥1).​
(17)
