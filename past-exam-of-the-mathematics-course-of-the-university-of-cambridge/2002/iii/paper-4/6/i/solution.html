No: there are at most three, including zero and the whole group. A two-generator ordered Abelian group is torsion-free and has rank of an abelian group at most two. A convex subgroup of an ordered group is pure: if ng∈C with n>0, then g lies between zero and ng or their negatives, so g∈C. Therefore a proper inclusion C<D of convex subgroups increases rational rank: an element of D∖C cannot have a nonzero integer multiple in C. Convex subgroups form a chain, so a rank-two group permits at most two strict inclusions. This is the bound for convex subgroups of a finite-rank ordered Abelian group. It is sharp: lexicographically ordered group Z2 has exactly 0, {0}×Z, and Z2.
