The additive Abelian group G is divisible because it is a real vector space. Each convex subgroup Cn​ is divisible too: for c≥0 in Cn​ and a positive integer m, its unique additive quotient c/m satisfies 0≤c/m≤c, hence belongs to Cn​. Negative elements follow by taking inverses. Thus all Cn​ are rational vector subspaces, independently of whether the given real scalar multiplication respects the order.
Choose an ordered-group isomorphism θn​:Cn+1​/Cn​→R. It is rational-linear, since additive homomorphisms between divisible torsion-free groups preserve division by integers. Extend a rational basis of Cn​ to one of Cn+1​; equivalently choose a rational-linear section sn​:R→Cn+1​ of θn​ composed with the quotient map. Then
Cn+1​=Cn​⊕sn​(R).
(22)
Since C1​=0 and the union is G, each g∈G has a unique expression g=∑j=1N​sj​(rj​) with only finitely many nonzero coefficients. Define Φ(g)=(r1​,r2​,…). This is an injective additive group homomorphism. If rN​=0 is the largest active coefficient, the image of g in the ordered quotient CN+1​/CN​ has sign equal to that of rN​. Convexity ensures that this is also the sign of g.
The maximum-support convention for the Hahn group V(Z>0​,R) uses precisely finite supports and the sign of the largest active coefficient. Hence Φ preserves and reflects the total order, and therefore preserves join and meet. This proves the finite-support Hahn embedding along a countable convex chain. In fact every finite coefficient sequence is obtained, so Φ is an ordered-group isomorphism in this convention; the requested embedding follows.
