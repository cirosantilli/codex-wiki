A map p:E→B is a Serre fibration if every homotopy H:Dk×I→B, k≥0, with a lift of its initial slice has a lift on the whole cylinder extending that initial lift. By attaching cells, this gives the relative homotopy lifting property for CW pairs: a specified lift on the initial slice and on the subcomplex cylinder can be extended.

The two expressions coincide on their common boundary, the initial path at s=0 is exactly γx​, and the endpoint is H(x,s). The adjoint map on Dk×I×I is continuous by pasting; compactness of the path parameter gives continuity into the path space. Thus endpoint evaluation PY→Y is a Serre fibration. The variable subdivision avoids changing the initial lift by an unwanted reparametrization.

The boundary sends a relative disk map to its restriction on the boundary sphere. An absolute class has constant boundary, so ∂j∗​=0. Conversely, if a relative map has null-homotopic boundary in F, choose a based null-homotopy there. Attach this null-homotopy as a collar to the disk, leaving the original map on a smaller inner disk. The resulting boundary is constant at e0​, so it defines an absolute class in πn​(E); inserting the collar is a relative homotopy, so its image is the original relative class. This proves imj∗​=ker∂. At n=1, append a path in F from the terminal point to e0​, giving the corresponding based loop and pointed-set exactness.
Now suppose F=p−1(b0​). Use a cube model in which the bottom and side faces are based and the top face maps into F. A based n-cube in B lifts from its constant bottom face, with sides held at e0​ by relative lifting. Its top face lies in F, proving surjectivity of the relative homotopy of a Serre fibration comparison p∗​:πn​(E,F)→πn​(B,b0​).
For injectivity, lift a based homotopy of two projected cubes from the first relative lift; this ends at some lift of the second base cube. Any two lifts of that same base cube are relatively homotopic: insert an extra parameter between them, prescribe the two liftings on its two ends and the constant lift on the bottom, and apply relative lifting in the remaining cube direction. The top face stays in F because its projection is b0​. Combining these homotopies proves injectivity. Therefore
p∗​:πn​(E,F)≅​πn​(B,b0​)(n≥2).​
(8)
At n=1 the same construction gives a bijection of pointed sets, and one can transport the base fundamental-group law across it. An arbitrary pair's relative π1​ has no general group law. The printed target index a is a typographical error: the map preserves degree n.
The last-column map U(n)→S2n−1 is surjective because a unit vector can be completed to a unitary basis. Its stabilizer is the block subgroup diag(U(n−1),1), so it induces a continuous bijection U(n)/U(n−1)→S2n−1. Unitary matrices form a compact set in their matrix-space topology; the quotient is compact and the sphere Hausdorff. Hence
U(n)/U(n−1)≅S2n−1.​
(9)
This quotient is a fiber bundle. Near the last standard unit vector, project the other standard vectors by I−vv∗ onto v⊥ and apply Gram-Schmidt orthogonalization; their independence persists in a small neighborhood. The resulting orthonormal columns together with v give a continuous local section. Translating these sections covers the sphere and trivializes the projection, so it is a Serre fibration.
Apply this to U(n)→U(n+1)→S2n+1. Its homotopy sequence contains
πi+1​(S2n+1)→πi​(U(n))→πi​(U(n+1))→πi​(S2n+1).
(10)
For i≤2n−1, both outer groups vanish by cellular approximation theorem. Thus πi​(U(n))≅πi​(U(n+1)) in the stated range. The component assertion also holds: diagonalizing a unitary matrix and varying its eigenvalue phases continuously connects it to the identity. This establishes the unitary sphere quotient and stability range.
