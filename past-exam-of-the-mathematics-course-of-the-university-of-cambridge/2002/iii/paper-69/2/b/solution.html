Let b,c>1, rescale q∥′​=bq∥​, q⊥′​=cq⊥​, and set B=bcd−1. Use the Fourier-amplitude convention
m′(q′)=m(q)/z.
(15)
The momentum measure contributes B−1, and substitution into the quadratic statistical Hamiltonian gives
K′=Bb2z2​K,L′=Bc4z2​L,t′=Bz2​t,h′=zh.​
(16)
In the uniform source the momentum delta function cancels the Jacobian determinant of the measure, explaining why h′ does not have an additional B−1. In a full momentum-shell renormalization group transformation integrate out the fast modes first. Their uncoupled Gaussian integrals contribute an additive free-energy density; the displayed transformations describe the slow-mode statistical Hamiltonian after coordinate and field rescaling.
The field convention must be stated. In real space, x∥′​=x∥​/b and x⊥′​=x⊥​/c, and Fourier inversion gives m′(x′)=Bm(x)/z. Thus if instead the symbol m′=m/zx​ refers to real-space amplitudes, z=Bzx​. Then the same transformation reads K′=Bzx2​K/b2, L′=Bzx2​L/c4, t′=Bzx2​t, h′=Bzx​h. These are equivalent normalizations, not different critical exponents.
