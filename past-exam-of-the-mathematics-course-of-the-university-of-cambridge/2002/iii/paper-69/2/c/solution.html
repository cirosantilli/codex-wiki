Keeping both gradient coefficients fixed requires z2=Bb2=Bc4. Hence
c=b1/2,B=b(d+1)/2,z=b(d+5)/4.
(17)
Equivalently the real-space field normalization is zx​=z/B=b(3−d)/4. The transformed thermal and source couplings are
t′=b2t,h′=b(d+5)/4h,yt​=2,yh​=(d+5)/4.​
(18)
This is the Gaussian fixed point at t=h=0, with anisotropic effective volume exponent Deff​=(d+1)/2. The stiffnesses remain positive parameters labeling the normalization of coordinates and fields. A strictly positive t is a relevant perturbation away from the renormalization-group fixed point, not itself a critical renormalization-group fixed point.
The two correlation lengths also follow directly by balancing the kernel: Kq∥2​∼t gives ξ∥​∼(K/t)1/2, while Lq⊥4​∼t gives ξ⊥​∼(L/t)1/4. Their different exponents are consistent with the required anisotropic scaling. An omitted scalar quartic interaction has scaling eigenvalue 4−Deff​=(7−d)/2, so the quadratic renormalization-group fixed point alone does not establish the interacting smectic's critical behavior below physical dimension seven.
