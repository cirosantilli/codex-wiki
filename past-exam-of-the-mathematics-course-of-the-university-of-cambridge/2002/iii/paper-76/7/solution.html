Use the pointed complete partial order convention: a partially ordered set has a least element and every nonempty directed set has a least upper bound. The argument also works for the convention requiring only suprema of increasing countable chains. The function space of complete partial orders [D→E] consists of Scott continuous maps, namely monotone maps preserving directed suprema, ordered pointwise. Its least element is the constant map with value ⊥E​.
For a directed family {fi​}, define f(x)=supi​fi​(x). This is its pointwise directed supremum and is monotone. If A⊆D is directed, then
f(supA)=supi​fi​(supA)=supi​supa∈A​fi​(a)=supa∈A​supi​fi​(a)=supa∈A​f(a).
(29)
The interchange is justified because {fi​(a):i,a} is directed: choose a common upper map and a common upper input. Thus f is Scott continuous, proving that [D→E] is a cpo. Evaluation is also jointly Scott continuous: for a directed family (fi​,xi​), the pairs of independently chosen indices are cofinal in a common larger index, so (supi​fi​)(supi​xi​)=supi​fi​(xi​). The same pointwise calculation proves continuity of currying.
Suppose the continuous maps e:[D→D]→D, p:D→[D→D] satisfy p∘e=id. Define
a⋅b=p(a)(b).​
(30)
For an environment ρ, interpret a variable by ρ(x), application by ⋅, and an abstraction by
[[λx.M]]ρ​=e(d↦[[M]]ρ[x:=d]​).
(31)
Induction on syntax, using evaluation, currying and continuity of e,p, shows that every environment-dependent interpretation is Scott continuous, so this abstraction is defined. A further syntax induction proves the substitution lemma
[[M[x:=N]]]ρ​=[[M]]ρ[x:=[[N]]ρ​]​.
(32)
For abstraction the bound variable is first renamed fresh. Consequently p(e(f))=f proves the beta equation for every environment. Consistent renaming proves alpha invariance, and the compositional interpretation respects all contexts. This is a lambda model. A retraction alone does not assert e∘p=id, so eta conversion need not be valid.
For the graph model of lambda calculus, take Pω=P(N) with inclusion, bottom ∅ and directed supremum equal to union. Let ek​ be the finite set of positions of one-bits in k, and choose the pairing function
⟨k,n⟩=2(k+n)(k+n+1)​+n.
(33)
Define
p(a)(b)=a⋅b={n:∃k(ek​⊆b and ⟨k,n⟩∈a)},e(f)={⟨k,n⟩:n∈f(ek​)}.
(34)
A finite witness ek​ lies in one member of any directed union containing it, proving continuity in b; a graph entry lies in one member of any directed union of graphs, proving continuity in a. The displayed definition of e preserves pointwise directed unions as well. Finally
p(e(f))(b)=⋃ek​⊆b​f(ek​)=f(b),
(35)
since the finite subsets of b are directed with union b. This proves the retraction and all claimed model properties. The composite e∘p generally enlarges a graph by adding entries with larger finite-input witnesses; it is not generally the identity.
