The Böhm tree BT(M) is ⊥ if M is an unsolvable lambda term. Otherwise choose a head normal form
M→β∗​λx1​⋯xn​.yM1​⋯Mm​.
(25)
Its root is labeled by the binder prefix and head variable, and its ordered children are BT(M1​),…,BT(Mm​). Repeat recursively, possibly forever. Bound names are identified by alpha equivalence; the Church-Rosser theorem makes this tree independent of the selected head reduct. An address α is a finite sequence of child numbers; Mα​ denotes the corresponding argument term encountered after these head reductions.
A Böhm transformation is a finite composition of capture-avoiding substitutions and applications to chosen auxiliary terms. Here is a constructive Böhm-out lemma. Along the finite path to α, choose q at least the number of children of each preceding head node, and define
Uq​=λz1​⋯zq​w.wz1​⋯zq​,Piq​=λz1​⋯zq​.zi​.
(26)
Initially replace all relevant free variables by Uq​. At a head node with n initial binders and m children, supply its n binders with Uq​. Its head is now Uq​ and its arguments are the substituted children N1​,…,Nm​. Apply q−m extra copies of Uq​ as padding and then Piq​. Direct beta reduction gives
Uq​N1​⋯Nm​q−mUq​⋯Uq​​​Piq​→β∗​Ni​.
(27)
Repeat with the next child number. The term reached at each stage is the selected original child with the accumulated substitutions for its ancestors' binders. Thus the finite composite π satisfies Mπ≡β​Mα​σ for a suitable substitution σ. The empty path uses the identity transformation. Tupling, rather than immediately replacing a head variable by a fixed projection, is important: the same variable can occur later where a different child must be selected.
For the Böhm separation theorem, two distinct closed normal forms for both beta reduction and eta conversion have finite Böhm trees with a genuine disagreement in a head label or argument structure. A Böhm transformation first extracts their common-prefix node. Projections at a differing head variable select different supplied Church Booleans; differing arities are handled by padding, with the exclusion of mere eta expansions ensuring a genuine disagreement. This yields a context separating the two normal forms. Distinct beta-normal forms that differ only by eta conversion are not covered by this separation statement.
The PDF prints a free α in both the definitions of K and T. Taken literally, this makes the last request impossible. To see why, suppose MKα​≡β​Kα​, where Kα​=λxy.α, and choose a fresh f. By solvability and its stability under substitution, Mf has a head normal form λx1​⋯xn​.hP1​⋯Pr​. If h=f, substituting either Kα​ or S leaves the same variable-headed spine; it cannot be Kα​ in one case and F in the other. If h=f, obtaining Kα​ requires r≤2 and n+2−r=2, hence n=r. Substituting S=λxyz.xz(yz) then leaves at least n+3−r=3 leading abstractions, whereas F has two. A variable-headed normal form cannot lose initial abstractions under beta reduction. The Church-Rosser theorem rules out the required equality.
For the intended closed first projection K=T=λxy.x and F=λxy.y=KI, an explicit answer is
M=λf.f(KK)(KI)I.​
(28)
Indeed MK→β∗​(KK)I→β​K=T, whereas MS→β∗​(KK)I((KI)I)→β​KI=F. These computations solve the corrected closed-combinator version while identifying the obstruction in the literal printed one.
