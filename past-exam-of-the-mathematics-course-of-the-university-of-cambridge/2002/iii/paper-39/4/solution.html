A randomized complete block design groups similar experimental units into experimental blocks and places every treatment once in each experimental block, independently randomizing treatment allocation within experimental blocks. Blocking removes background between-block variation from within-block treatment comparisons. A balanced incomplete block design has v treatments and b experimental blocks of size k<v, with each treatment occurring in r experimental blocks and every distinct treatment pair together in λ experimental blocks. Counting treatment occurrences and co-occurrences gives vr=bk and r(k−1)=λ(v−1).
A Latin square of order q places each of q treatment symbols once in every row and every column of a q×q array. This controls two nuisance directions. A Graeco-Latin square superposes two mutually orthogonal Latin squares: each square is Latin, and each ordered pair of symbols from the two squares occurs exactly once. The two symbol factors can then be fitted together with row and column effects. Random permutations of row labels, column labels and treatment symbols provide a suitable randomized allocation without destroying these balances.

To prove the required orthogonality, let ui​ be any centered row coefficients and wo​ any centered operator coefficients. Their observation-space inner product is ∑i,j​ui​wo(i,j)​=∑i​ui​∑o​wo​=0, since every operator occurs once in each row.
