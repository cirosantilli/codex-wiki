Code each factor level by xj​∈{−1,1}. A nonconstant factorial contrast indexed by a nonempty factor set A has sign column gA​(x)=∏j∈A​xj​. Flipping any coordinate in A reverses that sign and pairs the treatment combinations, so exactly 2m−1 have each sign. Allocating the two signs to two experimental blocks therefore divides the full factorial design into equal experimental blocks. The contrast is confounded with experimental blocks because its column is constant within each experimental block: changing its coefficient and compensating by the two experimental block intercepts leaves every fitted value unchanged. It has no information separate from experimental block differences. Randomize the allocation of the two groups to days and the run order within days.
Write A,B,C for temperature, pressure and catalyst, respectively, with low levels and catalyst one coded minus. In the supplied allocation, ABC=−1 on day one and ABC=1 on day two. Thus the three-factor interaction ABC is confounded with days. Every other nonconstant factorial column is orthogonal to the day contrast, because its product with ABC is a nonconstant sign column on the full cube and consequently sums to zero.
If all interactions are negligible, the fitted model contains an intercept, one day contrast and the three main effects. The corrected total statistical degrees of freedom partition as
7=1 (days)+1 (temperature)+1 (pressure)+1 (catalyst)+3 (residual).​
(22)
The three residual statistical degrees of freedom are the unused two-factor interaction columns AB,AC,BC, pooled as error under the stated negligible-interaction assumption. There is no independent pure-error replication of treatment combinations.
For four days with two runs each, all three main effects can be estimated. One regular allocation pairs each treatment combination with its complete opposite:
block1234​first (A,B,C)(−,−,−)(−,−,+)(−,+,−)(−,+,+)​second (A,B,C)(+,+,+)(+,+,−)(+,−,+)(+,−,−)​​
(23)
The block-generating columns can be AB and AC; their product BC is also constant in experimental blocks. Thus AB,AC,BC span the three experimental block contrasts, while A,B,C and ABC vary within experimental blocks. The main effects remain mutually orthogonal and separate from the experimental block effects; with an additive main-effect model there is one residual degree of freedom, represented by ABC.
For the further request about AC, the distinction between regular confounding and unrestricted pairing is important. A regular four-block allocation cannot retain all three main effects and AC. Its three confounded columns form a rank-two defining subgroup. To preserve main effects, these columns must lie in {AB,AC,BC,ABC}. If the subgroup includes ABC and a two-factor column, their product is a main-effect column, which is forbidden. Hence the only permissible nonidentity subgroup is {AB,AC,BC}, and it necessarily confounds AC. This proves impossibility in the usual regular factorial-blocking framework, rather than relying on a count of available statistical degrees of freedom.
The literal request does not explicitly restrict arrangements to regular experimental blocks. If arbitrary pairings are permitted, a nonregular construction can estimate A,B,C,AC together, assuming the other interactions are omitted from the model. An explicit allocation is
block1234​first (A,B,C)(−,−,−)(−,+,−)(−,+,+)(+,−,+)​second (A,B,C)(−,−,+)(+,−,−)(+,+,+)(+,+,−)​​
(24)
Every treatment combination occurs exactly once. Fit one unrestricted intercept per day and the four coefficients βA​,βB​,βC​,βAC​. By estimability from within-block differences, subtracting the second run from the first in each experimental block removes all day effects and yields the coefficient matrix

Thus all four coefficients are uniquely determined from the four day differences. In this broader design class the answer is yes, under the reduced four-effect model. The resulting fit is saturated: four experimental block intercepts plus four treatment coefficients use all eight observations, leaving no residual estimate of error variance. Replication or external error information would be needed for inference. This does not contradict the impossibility for regular orthogonal confounding, and it does not claim simultaneous unbiased estimation when arbitrary additional interactions are retained.
