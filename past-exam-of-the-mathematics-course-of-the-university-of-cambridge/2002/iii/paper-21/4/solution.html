For the effective counterexample, let K be the diagonal halting set and let Ks​ be the finite approximation obtained by running the first s programs for s steps. For x<y<z, define the recursive triple colouring with no computable infinite homogeneous set by
c({x,y,z})={0,1,​Ky​∩{0,…,x}=Kz​∩{0,…,x},otherwise.​
(11)
This is a total recursive two-coloring: only bounded computations are involved. An infinite homogeneous set H cannot have color one. Fix x∈H and take y,z∈H sufficiently large that every halting computation with index at most x that ever halts has already halted by stage y; then the color is zero. If H has color zero, it computes K: given e, find x<y in H with x≥e and inspect Ky​(e). Agreement with every later stage z∈H, and the unboundedness of H, give Ky​∩{0,…,x}=K∩{0,…,x}. Hence this is the correct answer. The set K is not recursive: a purported halting decider would produce a program that halts exactly when the decider says it does not halt on its own index. Thus there is no recursive infinite homogeneous set. Here infinite is essential: every finite subset is recursive, and sets with fewer than three elements are vacuously homogeneous.
A precise finite-arity Erdős-Rado theorem for finite arities is
ℶr​(λ)+⟶(λ+)λr+1​(r<ω, λ an infinite cardinal),​
(12)
where ℶ0​(λ)=λ and ℶr+1​(λ)=2ℶr​(λ). We prove the closed elementary-submodel construction of an end-homogeneous sequence and then induct. We work with AC, as in the usual cardinal partition calculus.
Put θ=(2μ)+, where μ is infinite, and let c:[θ]m+1→λ with λ≤μ and m≥1. Choose a regular cardinal χ large enough that this coloring and its domain belong to Hχ​, the sets of hereditary size below χ. There is M≺Hχ​ of size 2μ containing c,θ,μ, every ordinal below μ+, and closed under externally given sequences of length at most μ. Here is the closure construction: start with a Skolem hull of the named parameters and the ordinals below μ+; iterate hulls after adjoining all at-most-μ-sequences of the preceding hull for μ+ stages. Every stage and the union have size 2μ, since (2μ)μ=2μ and μ+≤2μ. The elementary chain theorem gives elementarity. Any sequence of at most μ members of the final union lies in a bounded stage, because μ+ is regular, and is added at the next stage. This proves the required closure.
Let β=sup(M∩θ). Regularity of θ gives β<θ, and β∈/M: otherwise β+1 would also belong to M∩θ. Recursively choose increasing xα​∈M∩θ for α<μ+ so that, for every m-subset u of the earlier points,
c(u∪{xα​})=c(u∪{β}).
(13)
To justify the recursion, the earlier sequence has length at most μ, hence belongs to M. There are at most μ constraints. All their points and color values are in M, so their coded table belongs to M by closure, even though β itself is external to M. The supremum of the earlier points, plus one, also belongs to M∩θ and is below β. Thus β witnesses in Hχ​ that a point above that bound satisfying the table exists. Elementarity supplies such a point in M. This completes the recursion.
The sequence is end-homogeneous: on any increasing (m+1)-tuple from it, the color depends only on its first m entries. Define the induced m-tuple coloring by g(u)=c(u∪{β}). For the induction, the arity-one assertion λ+→(λ+)λ1​ is the infinite pigeonhole principle: a union of λ sets each of size at most λ has size at most λ. At step r≥1, take μ=ℶr−1​(λ), so θ=ℶr​(λ)+. The constructed sequence has size μ+=ℶr−1​(λ)+. The induction hypothesis makes g constant on a subset of size λ+; end-homogeneity makes c constant on its (r+1)-subsets. This proves the theorem. In particular λ=ℵ0​ gives an uncountable homogeneous set for every finite-arity coloring with countably many colors, on a sufficiently large cardinal.
There is no unrestricted infinite-exponent analogue under AC. For any infinite cardinal κ, choose one representative R from each equivalence class of [κ]ω modulo finite symmetric difference. Color X by the parity of ∣X△R∣. Removing one element of X leaves it in the same class and reverses the parity. Every infinite proposed homogeneous set contains both a countably infinite X and X∖{x}, so cannot be homogeneous. Thus the finite-symmetric-difference colouring of infinite subsets proves
κ⟶(ω)2ω​for every infinite κ, under AC.​
(14)
The contrast is between arbitrary colorings at countably infinite arity and the finite arities of the theorem; restrictions on the definability of an infinite-arity coloring can change the problem.
