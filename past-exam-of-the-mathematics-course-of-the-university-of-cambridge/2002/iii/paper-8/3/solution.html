For f∈L2, the convolution Kε​∗f is absolutely convergent for each x by Cauchy-Schwarz inequality. Its Fourier transform is mε​f​: first prove this for Schwartz functions and then approximate any L2 input. The convolutions converge uniformly under this approximation by the same Cauchy-Schwarz bound, and the multiplier outputs converge in L2 by their uniform operator bound, so these two definitions agree. T(ϕε​)∗f=ϕε​∗(Tf).
(29)
The allowed standard approximate identity results, applied to Tf∈L2, prove convergence to Tf in norm and almost everywhere. This is the mass normalization in mollified singular integrals; it does not require proving almost-everywhere convergence of the sharp truncations Tε​f.
If “bump function” means merely smooth with compact support, the stated limit needs the unit-integral qualification. For arbitrary such ϕ, the limit is (∫ϕ)Tf instead. This is also the almost-everywhere limit: at each Lebesgue point of Tf, subtract (∫ϕ)Tf(x) inside the convolution and bound the remaining local average by the boundedness and compact support of ϕ. As a concrete counterexample without normalization, take K(x)=1/(πx) in dimension one and ϕ=2ρ for a unit-integral mollifier ρ. This kernel has the required size, odd annular cancellation, and translation-difference integral log(3)/π on {∣x∣>2∣y∣} for every y=0. Its limit operator is the nonzero Hilbert transform; by the Hilbert-transform Fourier multiplier, it is an L2 isometry. For any nonzero f∈L2 the mollified limit is 2Tf, not Tf.
