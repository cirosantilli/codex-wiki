The change of time is dτ/dt=u, which is positive in the half-plane under consideration. Dividing the two approximate equations by u gives
dτdu​=μ+u−v,dτdv​=λu.
(19)
Set w=v−μ. The inhomogeneous system becomes (u,w)′=(u−w,λu), with eigenvalues (1±1−4λ​)/2. For λ>1/4, write ω=4λ−1​/2. Integrating with the prescribed initial data gives
u(τ)=eτ/2[hcos(ωτ)+ωμ+h/2−v0​​sin(ωτ)],​
(20)
and
v(τ)=μ+eτ/2[(v0​−μ)cos(ωτ)+ωλh−(v0​−μ)/2​sin(ωτ)].​
(21)
These expressions also verify both initial values and their initial derivatives. For fixed μ>0 and small h,v0​ on the outgoing branch, the sine coefficient in u is positive and dominates the initial cosine term. The first return to small u is near the first positive zero, ωτ≃π. Hence
τexcursion​≃ωπ​=4λ−1​2π​.​
(22)
The factor is a half-turn of the growing spiral, not its full rotation period. After this excursion the approximate trajectory approaches the small-u, large-v region. In the full system the term −λv, omitted during the large excursion, allows the motion to descend along that region and return.
As λ↓1/4, the rescaled excursion time diverges and the factor eτ/2 produces arbitrarily large excursions. At the critical value the matrix is a nontrivial Jordan block, and the limiting formula is
u(τ)=eτ/2[h+(μ+h/2−v0​)τ].
(23)
For the small outgoing initial data this is positive and unbounded: the unstable manifold no longer turns back. Below 1/4 the two eigenvalues are real and positive, so there is again no oscillatory return. This quadratic escape desingularization explains loss of the periodic orbit at infinity, together with the unstable-manifold escape, as λ decreases through 1/4. It is not a finite homoclinic bifurcation to the origin. The divergent τ travel time must not be identified with the physical period without integrating dt=dτ/u; the time change becomes singular near u=0.
