A constant number star formation rate over 10Gyr produces a uniform present-age distribution. The birth-age distribution under constant star formation therefore gives
Y(t)=10Gyrt​=0.1Gyrt​(0≤t≤10Gyr).​
(22)
Outside this age interval the cumulative distribution function is zero or one. The census here retains stellar remnants as objects, as required by the population model.
Write m=M/M⊙​. The initial mass function is proportional to m−3 on m≥0.2. Normalizing the tail integral gives the power-law initial-mass-function tail coordinate:
X(m)=∫0.2∞​s−3ds∫m∞​s−3ds​=m20.04​(m≥0.2).​
(23)
For lower thresholds X=1. Because the birth mass and formation time have independence, the probability integral transform makes (X,Y) uniform on the unit square. X is an upper-tail coordinate, so larger masses have smaller X.
A red giant of current age t lies in the stellar lifetime regions in a mass-age diagram
m28​≤Gyrt​<m210​,0≤Gyrt​≤10.
(24)
There are no red giants below m=0.8​ in this model. For 0.8​<m<1 the upper boundary is the population's age limit, while for m≥1 both lifetime boundaries are present. A white dwarf lies above t/Gyr=10/m2.
In uniform probability coordinates the main-sequence boundary is Y=20X and the end of the giant phase is Y=25X. The giant region is therefore 20X≤Y<25X, clipped to the unit square: it is the triangle with vertices (0,0),(1/25,1),(1/20,1). Its area, and hence the individual giant fraction, is
Pr(G)=21​(201​−251​)=2001​=0.5%.​
(25)
The white dwarf region Y≥25X is the triangle with vertices (0,0),(0,1),(1/25,1), giving
Pr(W)=21​251​=501​=2%.​
(26)
The remaining 97.5% are on the main sequence. Boundaries have zero probability and do not affect these counts. Treating every post-giant object as a white dwarf is a stipulated toy-model assumption; real high-mass stars have other stellar remnants.
For the binary calculations, use a coeval binary population: components share their birth time even though their birth masses are independently drawn and their subsequent evolution is independent. Hence (X1​,X2​,Y) is uniform in the unit cube. At a fixed age coordinate Y=y, a component is a giant when y/25<X<y/20, and a white dwarf when 0<X<y/25. Define the conditional state fractions
g(y)=100y​,w(y)=25y​,e(y)=g(y)+w(y)=20y​.
(27)
Components have conditional independence given y; averaging over the common age must be done after forming the conditional pair probabilities. The continuous-birth binary state fractions below are consequently volumes in this cube, not products of the marginal single-star fractions.
