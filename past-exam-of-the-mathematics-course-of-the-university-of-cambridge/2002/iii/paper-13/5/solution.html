Identify Picg−1(X) with the Jacobian variety using the base point and the translation of Solution 4. A line bundle L of degree g−1 corresponds to the point z=u(D)−κ for any divisor representation L≃OX​(D). The Riemann singularity theorem is
multz​Θ=h0(X,L).​
(26)
In particular, the theta divisor is singular exactly at the degree-g−1 line bundles with at least two independent sections. We prove the equality, including the upper bound on multiplicity.
Choose a fixed effective divisor A of degree N≥g, and a local holomorphic family Lη​ representing nearby points of Picg−1(X). Such a family can be constructed locally from the holomorphic exponential sequence, by varying line-bundle transition functions through exponentials of Čech cohomology representatives. By Serre duality, H1(Lη​(A)) is dual to H0(KX​⊗Lη−1​(−A)), whose degree is g−1−N<0, so it vanishes. The Riemann-Roch theorem gives h0(Lη​(A))=N. By cohomology and base change for line bundles on a curve, these spaces form a holomorphic vector bundle E of rank N. The spaces H0(Lη​(A)∣A​) form another holomorphic vector bundle F of rank N. Evaluation gives a holomorphic square matrix T(η) locally, with exact sequence
0⟶H0(Lη​)⟶Eη​T(η)​Fη​⟶H1(Lη​)⟶0.
(27)
Consequently detT(η)=0 exactly when Lη​ has a nonzero section, namely on the support of the theta divisor.
At the point L, put r=h0(L)=h1(L), the equality being Riemann-Roch theorem. An invertible minor of size N−r persists near this point. Holomorphic row and column operations, or the Schur complement, give
detT(η)=a(η)detM(η),a(0)=0,M(0)=0,
(28)
where M is an r×r holomorphic matrix. This is the Schur complement presentation of a theta singularity. Its linear term in a tangent direction ξ∈H1(OX​) is the cup product map
dM0​(ξ):H0(L)⟶H1(L),s⟼ξ⌣s.
(29)
To see this concretely, vary transition functions gij​ of L to gij​(1+ϵξij​). A section si​ lifts to si​+ϵsi′​ precisely when its first-order compatibility equation can be solved; the obstruction is the cocycle ξij​sj​. This is ξ⌣s. Passing to the kernel and cokernel of T(0) gives exactly the derivative of the reduced matrix M, proving the assertion without assuming a multiplicity formula.
Before extracting the order of detM, we verify that detT is a reduced equation of the theta divisor. Generic points of Wg−1​ have h0(L)=1 by Solution 3. At such a point, both H0(L) and H0(KX​⊗L−1) are one-dimensional. Their nonzero sections s,t have nonzero product st∈H0(KX​). Choose ξ∈H1(OX​) with ⟨ξ,st⟩=0 under Serre duality. The scalar dM0​(ξ) is nonzero, so detT has a simple zero generically on the irreducible theta divisor. Since its support is exactly that irreducible hypersurface, it defines the reduced divisor. Solution 4 independently proved the same reduced structure for the Riemann theta function. On the smooth ambient Jacobian variety, local equations of the same reduced divisor differ by a nonvanishing holomorphic unit. Hence the orders of the local theta equation and detM agree everywhere.
Every entry of M vanishes at the point, so each term in its determinant has order at least r. This proves multz​Θ≥r. To obtain equality, we exhibit an invertible cup-product direction for a line bundle on a curve. Set V=H0(L) and W=H0(KX​⊗L−1), both of dimension r. There are r distinct points p1​,…,pr​ at which evaluation is an isomorphism for V: choose them inductively, since a surviving nonzero section has only finitely many zeros. The same holds for W. The nonvanishing of each evaluation determinant defines a nonempty open subset of the irreducible variety Xr. Their intersection, also avoiding the diagonals, is therefore nonempty. Choose points in it and local coordinates and compatible trivializations of L and KX​⊗L−1 at each point.
Let ξ be the connecting homomorphism class of the meromorphic principal parts λi​/ti​ at these points, with all λi​=0. The residue form of Serre duality gives
⟨ξ⌣s,t⟩=⟨ξ,st⟩=∑i=1r​λi​s(pi​)t(pi​).
(30)
If S and U are the two invertible evaluation matrices, the matrix of this pairing is Stdiag(λ1​,…,λr​)U, and is invertible. Thus the cup product map ξ⌣−:H0(L)→H1(L) is an isomorphism. Along the corresponding analytic parameter line,
M(ϵξ)=ϵdM0​(ξ)+O(ϵ2),detM(ϵξ)=ϵrdet(dM0​(ξ))+O(ϵr+1),
(31)
with nonzero leading coefficient. The order is at most r, completing the proof of the Riemann singularity theorem.
This argument also identifies the tangent cone to a theta divisor: its degree-r equation is det(ξ⌣−), which is not the zero polynomial. For r=1, the tangent hyperplane is ⟨ξ,st⟩=0, in agreement with the annihilator description of the derivative of the Abelian sum map. For r≥2 all first derivatives vanish, proving singularity. In genus 1 the theta divisor is a single smooth point of multiplicity 1. In genus 0 the effective degree-−1 locus is empty, so there is no singularity statement at a point of it.
