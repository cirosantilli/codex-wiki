Fix p0​∈X and use the normalized holomorphic one-forms and period lattice of Solution 1. The Abel-Jacobi map of a compact Riemann surface is
u(p)=(∫p0​p​ω1​,…,∫p0​p​ωg​)(modΛ).
(7)
Changing paths changes the vector by a period, so this is well defined and holomorphic. Adding these vectors gives a symmetric map on Xd and therefore the Abelian sum map ud​:X(d)→Jac(X). Locally, even where points coincide, symmetric holomorphic functions descend to the elementary symmetric coordinates of the symmetric product of a curve. For any divisor D=∑p​np​p of degree zero, define u(D)=∑p​np​u(p). Abel's theorem says that D is a principal divisor if and only if u(D)=0. Equivalently, two effective divisors of the same degree have the same Abel sum exactly when they are linearly equivalent.
We first establish the residue version of the bilinear identity. Let η be a meromorphic differential on a Riemann surface with only simple poles and total residue zero, and let F=∫p0​p​ω on the cut polygon, for a holomorphic one-form ω. Excise small disks about the poles. Since d(Fη)=ω∧η=0 off the poles, the outer boundary integral is the sum of the positive small-circle integrals. The latter are 2πiresp​(η)F(p). Pairing the outer edges as in Solution 1 therefore gives
∑i​(Ai​(ω)Bi​(η)−Bi​(ω)Ai​(η))=2πi∑p​resp​(η)∫p0​p​ω.
(8)
The paths and the cut polygon are fixed together in this equality. Changing them changes the Abel vector by its period lattice, which is precisely the ambiguity we need.
For the sufficiency direction, we need a differential of the third kind with prescribed residues np​. Let S be the reduced support of D, consisting of s points. When s>0, the Riemann-Roch theorem gives h0(KX​(S))=g−1+s. The residue map from this space has kernel H0(KX​), of dimension g: a differential with at most a simple pole and residue zero has no pole. Its image therefore has dimension s−1. The residue theorem puts the image inside the s−1 dimensional space of tuples with sum zero, so it is all that space. In particular there is a meromorphic differential on a Riemann surface η with residues np​. Subtracting a combination of the normalized holomorphic one-forms makes all its a-periods zero. The bilinear identity then says
(B1​(η),…,Bg​(η))=2πiu(D),
(9)
where on the right we temporarily choose the representative determined by the paths. If D=0, we simply take η=0.
Suppose now that u(D)=0 in the Jacobian variety, so the chosen vector is m+Bn, with m,n∈Zg. Replace η by
η′=η−2πi∑j​nj​ωj​.
(10)
Its a-periods are −2πinj​ and its b-periods are 2πimi​. The periods around the punctures are 2πinp​. These loops together generate the homology of the punctured Riemann surface, so every period of η′ lies in 2πiZ. Consequently f(p)=exp(∫pη′) is a single-valued nonzero holomorphic function off the support of D. At a support point, η′=np​dt/t+ a holomorphic differential, so f=tnp​ times a nonvanishing holomorphic function. It extends meromorphically and has divisor exactly D. This proves sufficiency, including negative coefficients.
Conversely, if D=div(f), take η=df/f. Its residues are the coefficients of D, while its a- and b-periods are integer multiples of 2πi, by the winding numbers of f along these loops. Writing them as 2πia and 2πib, the residue bilinear identity gives u(D)=b−Ba∈Λ. Thus u(D)=0 in the Jacobian variety, proving necessity and hence
div(f)=D for some meromorphic f=0 ⟺ degD=0 and u(D)=0.​
(11)
Here the displayed necessity of degree zero is also the residue theorem applied to df/f.
There is a useful geometric version of the conclusion. For an effective divisor D, the fiber of ud​ through D consists precisely of the effective divisors linearly equivalent to D. A nonzero section of OX​(D) is a meromorphic function f with div(f)+D≥0, and its zero divisor as a section is div(f)+D. Two such sections give the same effective divisor exactly when their ratio is a nonzero constant. Thus the fiber is the complete linear system of a divisor ∣D∣=PH0(X,OX​(D)).
