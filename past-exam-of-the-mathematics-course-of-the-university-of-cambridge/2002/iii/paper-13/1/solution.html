Let X be a connected compact Riemann surface of genus g. Choose an oriented symplectic basis a1​,…,ag​,b1​,…,bg​ of H1​(X,Z), with ai​⋅bj​=δij​. For a closed complex differential form α, write Ai​(α)=∫ai​​α and Bi​(α)=∫bi​​α. The Riemann bilinear relations begin with the identity
∫X​α∧β=∑i=1g​(Ai​(α)Bi​(β)−Bi​(α)Ai​(β)).
(1)
To prove it, cut X along the chosen cycles to obtain a fundamental polygon P. On this simply connected polygon, a closed differential form has a primitive F, with dF=α. Stokes theorem gives ∫P​α∧β=∫∂P​Fβ. The two copies of each edge have opposite orientations, and their values of F differ by the period acquired in passing between them. Pairing the copies of the ai​ and bi​ edges gives, respectively, −Bi​(α)Ai​(β) and Ai​(α)Bi​(β). Summing proves the identity; the convention ai​⋅bi​=1 fixes its sign.
For holomorphic one-forms ω,η, their wedge product is zero because locally both are multiples of dz. Thus the first holomorphic Riemann bilinear relations is
∑i​(Ai​(ω)Bi​(η)−Bi​(ω)Ai​(η))=0.
(2)
For a nonzero holomorphic one-form ω=f(z)dz, the second relation follows by applying the bilinear identity to ω,ω:
i∑i​(Ai​(ω)Bi​(ω)​−Bi​(ω)Ai​(ω)​)=i∫X​ω∧ω=2∫X​∣f∣2dxdy>0.
(3)
In particular, a holomorphic one-form with all a-periods zero must vanish. Since dimH0(X,KX​)=g, the map taking a holomorphic one-form to its a-periods is an isomorphism. We can therefore choose normalized holomorphic one-forms ω1​,…,ωg​ with ∫ai​​ωj​=δij​. Put Bij​=∫bi​​ωj​. The first relation applied to ωj​,ωk​ gives Bjk​=Bkj​, and the second applied to ∑j​cj​ωj​ gives 2c,t(ImB)c>0 for c=0. Consequently
B=Bt,Y=ImB>0.​
(4)
These conclusions make the construction of the Jacobian variety work. Integration identifies the image of H1​(X,Z) in H0(X,KX​)∗ with the period lattice Λ=Zg+BZg. The real-linear map (m,n)↦m+Bn is invertible: its imaginary part is Yn, and Y is invertible. Hence Λ is a discrete lattice of full real rank 2g, and
Jac(X)=H0(X,KX​)∗/H1​(X,Z)≃Cg/(Zg+BZg)​
(5)
is a compact complex torus, rather than a non-Hausdorff quotient. Changing the symplectic basis changes the displayed coordinates but not this intrinsic quotient.
The Riemann bilinear relations also supply its polarization of a complex torus. Use the Hermitian form H(z,w)=ztY−1w, linear in the first argument, and set E=−ImH. Symmetry of B gives
E(m+Bn,m′+Bn′)=mtn′−ntm′,E(v,iv)=H(v,v)>0.
(6)
Thus E is an integral positive Riemann form on a complex torus. Its matrix on the displayed lattice basis is the unimodular symplectic matrix. The corresponding positive holomorphic line bundle, equivalently the one constructed from theta automorphy in Solution 4, gives a principal polarization of a complex torus. By the Kodaira embedding theorem this makes the complex torus projective, hence an abelian variety. The normalization E=−ImH here is important because H was chosen linear in its first argument. If g=0, the spaces of holomorphic one-forms and periods are zero and the Jacobian variety is a point.
