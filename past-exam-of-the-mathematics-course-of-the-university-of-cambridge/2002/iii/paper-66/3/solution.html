Thus Dμ​q↦VDμ​q and the kinetic term is gauge-invariant.
Since the gauge coupling is independent of flavor and chirality, the classical internal global transformations are qL​↦UL​qL​, qR​↦UR​qR​, with constant flavor matrices UL​,UR​∈U(2). Equivalently these consist of SU(2)L​×SU(2)R​, a common vector phase, and a singlet axial phase q↦eiηγ5​q, modulo shared discrete factors. The vector phase measures quark number, or baryon number after dividing its generator by three. The nonsinglet axial rotations may be written q↦e−iθa​γ5​τa​/2q. At the quantum level the singlet axial phase is anomalous; the exact continuous internal symmetry is SU(2)L​×SU(2)R​×U(1)V​ up to those discrete identifications. The usual spacetime Poincaré transformations also leave the theory invariant; the massless classical theory has dilation symmetry, which is broken by quantum running. These distinctions are massless two-flavor QCD symmetries.
At equal time the canonical relations, including all indices, are
{qαif​(t,x),qβjg†​(t,y)}=δαβ​δij​δfg​δ(3)(x−y),{q,q}={q†,q†}=0.​
(19)
No constraint removes any of the four spinor components of the Dirac field in these equal-time relations.
To evaluate the axial charges, first prove the elementary bilinear identity. For any constant matrix K on the spinor/flavor space,
[∫qI†​KIJ​qJ​d3y,qK​(x)]=−KKJ​qJ​(x),[∫q†Kqd3y,qK†​(x)]=qI†​(x)KIK​.

For example [qI†​qJ​,qK​]=qI†​{qJ​,qK​}−{qI†​,qK​}qJ​; the spatial delta function collapses the integral. This proves the charge action directly from the canonical anticommutation relations.
For Ka​=γ5​τa​/2, anticommutation of γ0 and γ5​ then gives
[Q5,a​,q]=−Ka​q,[Q5,a​,qˉ​]=q†Ka​γ0=−qˉ​Ka​.

The charge is an even operator, so its commutator obeys the ordinary product rule. Consequently
[Q5,a​,S]=−qˉ​(Ka​+Ka​)q=−qˉ​γ5​τa​q=iPa​​.
(22)
Similarly, using γ52​=1 and the Pauli matrices identity {τa​,τb​}=2δab​I,
[Q5,a​,Pb​]=−2i​qˉ​(τa​τb​+τb​τa​)q=−iδab​S​.
(23)
Composite fields can be defined with a common symmetry-preserving regulator; these nonsinglet identities have no color axial anomaly. This is the nonsinglet axial charge algebra of quark densities.
If every conserved axial charge left the vacuum invariant, the vacuum expectation of each such commutator would vanish. A nonzero quark chiral condensate instead makes ⟨[Q5,a​,Pb​]⟩=−iδab​⟨S⟩=0 for a=b. It therefore diagnoses spontaneous breaking of the nonsinglet chiral symmetry. In the massless two-flavor limit, the observed hadronic pattern is described by SU(2)L​×SU(2)R​→SU(2)V​, with three Goldstone boson modes identified with the pions. The vacuum condensate is a dynamical property of QCD, not something derivable from the canonical algebra alone. The light pion multiplet and absence of parity-degenerate light hadrons support this pattern; the singlet axial anomaly prevents interpreting an additional singlet mode as a fourth Goldstone boson.
For equal nonzero masses, add Lm​=−mqˉ​q. It is invariant under local color gauge transformations and common vector flavor rotations, but not under independent left/right rotations. The exact continuous flavor symmetry becomes SU(2)V​×U(1)V​. The kinetic term and canonical anticommutators are unchanged, and the two proved equal-time commutators still hold. The charges, however, are no longer conserved:
∂μ​Aaμ​=mPa​,Aaμ​=21​qˉ​γμγ5​τa​q,Q˙​5,a​=m∫Pa​d3x.​
(24)
The first equation follows from the massive Dirac equations, or the last from Hm​=m∫S and Q˙​=i[H,Q]. This is the flavor-nonsinglet axial Ward identity with equal quark masses. The pions become pseudo-Goldstone bosons with mπ2​=O(m), rather than exact massless modes. To leading order, in the convention S=uˉu+dˉd, fπ2​mπ2​=−m⟨S⟩+O(m2). The condensate persists for small masses, but the symmetry is now explicitly broken, so the exact-symmetry Goldstone argument is replaced by an approximate one. The singlet anomaly and gauge symmetry are unaffected.
