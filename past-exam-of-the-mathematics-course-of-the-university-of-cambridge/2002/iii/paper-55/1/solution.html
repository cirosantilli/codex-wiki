The similarity model describes a layer starting with zero thickness, or the long-time limit when an initial finite thickness no longer sets the dominant scale.
For the small-diffusivity-ratio limit, put A=ϵλa​ and Δ=Tm​−T∞​, keeping S=H/Δ and the imposed concentrations fixed. Seek A=O(1) and λb​=O(1). Then F(ϵλb​)∼π​ϵλb​ and λb​−λa​∼−λa​. The upper heat balance gives

The resulting Cb​=(Tm​−Tb​)/m lies strictly between zero and C0​. The salt relation therefore determines a finite negative λb​, confirming its O(1) scaling. The top interface travels on the thermal scale κt​, whereas the lower melting interface travels on the shorter solutal scale Dt​. This is the brine-ice sandwich with disparate diffusion scales; S is the latent-to-sensible heat ratio, the reciprocal of the usual sensible-to-latent Stefan number convention.
The sketches show constant fresh-water temperature, a declining linear ice temperature, and a much broader thermal than solutal boundary layer in the brine. Concentration is zero in freshwater and pure ice, jumps to Cb​ across the lower interface, and rises to C0​. Accordingly the local liquidus is Tm​ on the salt-free side, jumps to Tb​ and falls rapidly to TL​(C0​). In the phase diagram, the brine starts on the liquidus at (Cb​,Tb​) and ends above it at (C0​,T∞​); the pure ice has concentration zero, and the interface tie-line joins it to the diluted brine.
Physically, the cold saline liquid permits freshwater to freeze above, but salt lowers the liquidus and allows the existing ice to melt into brine below even though that brine is colder than Tm​. Dilution raises the lower interfacial freezing temperature. Heat released by upper freezing supplies the lower melting and the conductive loss to the colder deep brine. With ∣λa​∣≫∣λb​∣, upper freezing exceeds lower melting and the ice layer thickens as b−a∝t​.
