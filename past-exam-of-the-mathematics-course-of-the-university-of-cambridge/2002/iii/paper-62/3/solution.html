Use the Minkowski metric gμν=diag(1,−1,−1,−1) and Feynman slash notation p=γμpμ​. The Pauli matrices satisfy σj​σk​+σk​σj​=2δjk​I2​. In the Dirac representation of the gamma matrices, direct block multiplication gives
(γ0)2=I4​,γ0γj=(0σj​​σj​0​)=−γjγ0,
(21)
and
γjγk=(−σj​σk​0​0−σj​σk​​).
(22)
Thus the spatial anticommutators are −2δjk​I4​, proving the Clifford relations
{γμ,γν}=2gμνI4​.​
(23)
Since partial derivatives commute, their symmetric product selects the symmetric part of the gamma matrices. Multiplying the Dirac equation by iγμ∂μ​+m gives
(i∂+m)(i∂−m)ψ=(−γμγν∂μ​∂ν​−m2)ψ=−(□+m2)ψ=0.
(24)
Hence every spinor component satisfies the Klein-Gordon equation with the same mass.
For parity, let xP​=(t,−x) and ψP​(x)=γ0ψ(xP​). The chain rule gives ∂0​ψ(xP​)=(∂0​ψ)(xP​) and ∂j​ψ(xP​)=−(∂j​ψ)(xP​). Combining these signs with γjγ0=−γ0γj proves
(i∂−m)ψP​(x)=γ0[(i∂−m)ψ](xP​)=0.
(25)
This establishes parity symmetry in quantum field theory for the free Dirac equation, rather than merely asserting its covariance.
A positive-energy plane wave ψ=u(p)e−ip⋅x solves (p−m)u=0. Write u=(χ,η)T. The two block equations are
(Ep​−m)χ−(σ⋅p)η=0,(σ⋅p)χ−(Ep​+m)η=0.
(26)
The second gives η=(σ⋅p)χ/(Ep​+m). In the first, (σ⋅p)2=p2I2​ and Ep2​−p2=m2 make the remaining coefficient zero. There are two independent two-component choices χs​, so
us​(p)=(χs​(σ⋅p)χs​/(Ep​+m)​),ψp,s​(x)=us​(p)e−ip⋅x.​
(27)
For a massive Dirac spinor, choose χs​ as the two rest-frame spin states along a fixed axis, for example σ3​χs​=±χs​. The label s distinguishes these canonical spin states after boosting; it is not automatically a helicity label.
Now p⋅xP​=pP​⋅x and γ0us​(p)=us​(pP​) for the same fixed χs​. Therefore
ψp,s​(x)⟼ψpP​,s​(x).​
(28)
The parity action on canonical massive spin states leaves the canonical spin label unchanged while reversing the momentum. Spin is an axial vector under parity; its orientation is unchanged. Consequently helicity, the projection of spin along momentum, reverses. If s had instead been chosen to denote helicity, the transformed label would have the opposite sign.
The Dirac adjoint is ψˉ​=ψ†γ0, with γ0†=γ0. Thus
ψˉ​P​(x)=[γ0ψ(xP​)]†γ0=ψ†(xP​)=ψˉ​(xP​)γ0.
(29)
The chirality matrix has γ5=iγ0γ1γ2γ3=(0I2​​I2​0​) in this representation, so (γ5)2=I4​ and it anticommutes with every γμ. In particular γ0γ5γ0=−γ5. The parity transformation of a Dirac bilinear therefore yields
ψˉ​P​(x)ψP​(x)=ψˉ​(xP​)ψ(xP​),ψˉ​P​(x)γ5ψP​(x)=−ψˉ​(xP​)γ5ψ(xP​).​
(30)
The first bilinear is a scalar under parity, whereas the second is a pseudoscalar.
For the requested gamma matrix trace identities, use the cyclic property of the trace. Conjugation of a product of n gamma matrices by γ5 multiplies it by (−1)n, but leaves its trace unchanged. All odd products therefore have zero trace. Taking the trace of the Clifford algebra relation and using TrI4​=4 gives Tr(γαγβ)=4gαβ. For four factors, move the first gamma matrix successively past the next three using the anticommutators. If A=Tr(γαγβγργδ), this gives
A=2gαβTr(γργδ)−2gαρTr(γβγδ)+2gαδTr(γβγρ)−A.
(31)
Thus A=4(gαβgρδ−gαρgβδ+gαδgβρ). Contracting with the relevant four-vectors proves all four answers:
Tr(p)Tr(pq)Tr(pqk)Tr(pγμqγν)​=0,=4p⋅q,=0,=4[pμqν+pνqμ−(p⋅q)gμν].​​
(32)
