a OurBigBook.com
Three independent motivations are radiative stability, gauge unification and dark matter. First, supersymmetric cancellation of quadratic divergences protects the Higgs scalar mass from the hierarchy problem; residual corrections are controlled by soft supersymmetry breaking, so avoiding large cancellations favours a breaking scale near the weak scale. Second, supersymmetric gauge coupling unification can reconcile the running of the three Standard Model couplings when superpartner thresholds lie near that scale. Third, with conserved R-parity, a stable lightest supersymmetric particle can provide dark matter, and weak-scale annihilation can produce an appropriate thermal abundance. These are conditional motivations, not proofs of a TeV threshold. Even without low-scale discovery, supersymmetry remains important as a structure in supergravity and superstring theory, potentially broken at a higher scale, and as an exact calculational tool through superpotential non-renormalization, BPS states and control of strongly coupled quantum field theories.

For physical finite-spin one-particle states, a positive-energy Poincaré group representation is labelled by mass and spin (m,j) when m>0, or mass and helicity (0,h) when massless. The individual states also carry their four-momentum and a spin projection. For four-dimensional N=1 supersymmetry, the irreducible labels become (m,jmsuper​), with jmsuper​ the superspin, or (0,hmsuper​), with a chosen superhelicity convention. The constituent representation labels are
massive: (m,j),(m,j),(m,j+21​),(m,j−21​),​
(1)
with the last entry omitted for j=0, and
massless: (0,h),(0,h+21​).​
(2)
A required CPT completion of a supermultiplet adds the conjugate labels (0,−h),(0,−h−21​) and conjugates any internal charges.

c OurBigBook.com
Unbroken extended supersymmetry obstructs the observed chiral matter structure. In four dimensions, the matter hypermultiplets of N≥2 theories contain conjugate gauge group representations, so their fermions are vectorlike rather than the chiral Weyl fermions of the Standard Model. Extended supersymmetry also relates interactions more strongly, restricting independent Yukawa couplings and superpotentials. It can occur at higher energies and break to N=1 or to no supersymmetry; the objection is to its unbroken realization in an ordinary low-energy chiral particle theory.

d OurBigBook.com
In the supersymmetric Higgs mechanism, an internal gauge symmetry is broken by a charged scalar field expectation value while supersymmetry can remain unbroken. A massless vector multiplet absorbs a chiral multiplet to form a massive N=1 vector multiplet: the vector absorbs a scalar Goldstone boson, and the gaugino and matter fermion acquire the matching masses. In the super-Higgs mechanism, local supersymmetry itself is spontaneously broken: the gravitino absorbs the spin-1/2 Goldstino and becomes a massive spin-3/2 particle. In a flat vacuum this increases its physical states from two to four.

The supersymmetry algebra closes on spacetime translations. Its supercharges are Weyl spinors, and
{Qα​,Qˉ​β˙​​}=2σαβ˙​μ​Pμ​.
(3)
Thus the composition of two odd supersymmetry transformations moves the spacetime argument. The generators also change spin and connect bosons with fermions. They extend the Poincaré group to the Super-Poincaré group, rather than forming an internal symmetry that commutes with spacetime transformations.

Dependence on Grassmann variables alone does not specify a superfield transformation law. A superfield is a function on superspace, with components transforming under the induced supertranslation and Lorentz transformation action. Any suitable superfunction can be an unconstrained superfield once equipped with that action; a fixed element of a Grassmann algebra with independently assigned components need not be covariant. A chiral superfield, for example,
Φ(y,θ)=A(y)+2​θψ(y)+θ2F(y),yμ=xμ+iθσμθˉ,
(4)
also obeys Dˉα˙​Φ=0. Keeping only a nonconstant A(x) and forbidding the other components is not a closed supermultiplet: applying a supercharge generates Grassmann-dependent derivative terms. A real unconstrained superfield can instead describe a vector superfield.

At two derivatives, specify the gauge group and matter representations, a real Kähler potential K(Φ,Φˉ), a holomorphic superpotential W(Φ) and a symmetric holomorphic gauge kinetic function fab​(Φ). Gauge couplings and topological angles are contained in f; global theories can also have allowed Fayet–Iliopoulos terms for Abelian factors. Local theories require the gravitational coupling and consistent gauging: a constant Fayet–Iliopoulos term is not an independent addition to arbitrary supergravity data. A constant in W affects supergravity although it is irrelevant in the global theory. K is not holomorphic or protected; W has no perturbative Wilsonian renormalization; holomorphic f is perturbatively one-loop exact. Nonperturbative corrections can modify W and f, and wave-function renormalization makes physical couplings run despite superpotential non-renormalization. Higher-derivative actions require further data.

Choose the extended supersymmetry normalization
{QαA​,Qˉ​β˙​B​}=2σαβ˙​μ​Pμ​δAB​,{QαA​,QβB​}=ϵαβ​ZAB,ϵ12​=1.
(5)
The central charge in supersymmetry is antisymmetric in A,B, because the full anticommutator is symmetric and ϵαβ​ is antisymmetric. In a massive one-particle state rest frame, Pμ​=(M,0,0,0), and the first anticommutator becomes 2Mδαβ​δAB​.
A unitary skew-diagonalization of an antisymmetric matrix puts Z into blocks (0−zr​​zr​0​), with a remaining zero direction if N is odd. For one block write z=∣z∣eiφ, and label its two supersymmetries 1,2. The combinations
A±​=2​Q11​±eiφ(Q22​)†​,B±​=2​Q21​∓eiφ(Q12​)†​
(6)
have
{A±​,A±†​}={B±​,B±†​}=2M±∣z∣.
(7)
For example the mixed contribution to the first expression is ±(e−iφz+eiφzˉ)/2=±∣z∣. This is the phase-aligned version of taking a supercharge minus a unitary combination of its adjoints, as suggested by the hint.
For every operator X and normalized state ∣v⟩,
⟨v∣{X,X†}∣v⟩=∥Xv∥2+∥X†v∥2≥0.
(8)
Applying this to A−​,B−​ in every block proves the BPS bound
M≥21​rmax​∣zr​∣.​
(9)
If the algebra is instead written with 2Z on its right side, the same result is M≥maxr​∣Zr​∣; the factor is purely a central-charge convention.
A BPS state saturates at least one of these inequalities. The corresponding zero-norm combinations of supercharges and their adjoints annihilate the representation, so part of supersymmetry is preserved. Its massive supermultiplet is shortened: each saturated block removes two complex fermionic oscillators, giving (2j+1)22N−2k states from a spin-j Clifford vacuum when k blocks saturate, before any necessary CPT completion of a supermultiplet. It preserves 4k of the 4N real supercharges. Its mass is tied to the central charges in supersymmetry and remains protected while it stays on the same short branch. Stability is not unconditional: a wall of marginal stability can permit a charged BPS state to decay into other BPS states with the same total charge.

At fixed nonzero four-momentum, take the finite physical supermultiplet and write PF​=(−1)F for fermion parity. Each supercharge is odd, so PF​Qα​=−Qα​PF​ and the same holds for its adjoint. By the cyclic property of the trace,
Tr(PF​Qα​Qα†​)=Tr(Qα†​PF​Qα​)=−Tr(PF​Qα†​Qα​).
(10)
Consequently Tr[PF​{Qα​,Qα†​}]=0. Sum the two spinor components of the N=1 Super-Poincaré algebra. Since Tr2×2​σi=0, their sum is 4H. On a fixed-energy representation, H=EI, yielding
0=4ETrPF​=4E(nB​−nF​).
(11)
For a nonzero physical one-particle momentum, E>0, including the massless case. Hence the bosonic and fermionic physical state counts agree:
nB​=nF​.​
(12)
Equivalently a nonzero normalized supercharge and its adjoint define an odd involution pairing the two subspaces. This is supertrace pairing at positive energy; it counts spin polarizations, not merely distinct names of particles.
The phrase “every supermultiplet” requires this positive-energy qualification. A zero-energy supersymmetric vacuum with all supercharges acting as zero is a one-dimensional bosonic representation with no fermionic partner. It is a counterexample to the unrestricted statement, because division by E is then impossible. It does not contradict the requested equality for particle supermultiplets.

Sum the diagonal spinor entries of the N=1 Super-Poincaré algebra, obtaining
H=41​α=1∑2​{Qα​,Qα†​}.​
(13)
The expectation in any state is therefore
⟨H⟩=41​∑α​(∥Qα​∣v⟩∥2+∥Qα†​∣v⟩∥2)≥0.
(14)
This proves energy positivity in global supersymmetry, with the energy origin fixed by the algebra.
If a ground state is invariant under supersymmetry, all its supercharge variations vanish and its energy is zero. Conversely, zero energy makes every nonnegative squared norm above zero, so every supercharge annihilates that vacuum. Thus strictly positive vacuum energy implies supersymmetry breaking: some supercharge fails to annihilate the vacuum. A finite-volume regulator can be used before passing to vacuum-energy density. This is a statement about the global supersymmetry algebra; it is not a positivity theorem for the general supergravity F-term potential.

Use Planck units and restrict to the physical Kähler metric domain
x=S+Sˉ>0,y=T+Tˉ−∣C∣2>0.
(15)
Put A=ae−αS and w=A+b, so that W=C3+w. There are no gauged interactions specified, hence no additional D-term scalar potential. The supergravity F-term potential is V=eK(Kijˉ​Di​WDj​W​−3∣W∣2), with eK=1/(xy3). Its Kähler covariant derivatives of a superpotential are
DS​W=−αA−xW​,DT​W=−y3W​,DC​W=3C2+y3CˉW​.
(16)
The nonzero metric entries are
KSSˉ​=x−2,(KTTˉ​KCTˉ​​KTCˉ​KCCˉ​​)=y23​(1−Cˉ​−CT+Tˉ​).
(17)
The determinant of this T,C block is 9/y3>0, so it is positive definite. With the inverse-index convention Kijˉ​​Kkjˉ​=δik​, the raised components are
KSSˉ=x2,(KTTˉKCTˉ​KTCˉKCCˉ​)=3y​(T+TˉC​Cˉ1​).
(18)
Keeping these complex-conjugate off-diagonal entries in their correct index positions is essential.
To exhibit the no-scale supergravity cancellation, write the T,C norm as
Kijˉ​Di​WDj​W​​T,C​=3y​[y∣DT​W∣2+∣DC​W+CˉDT​W∣2]=3∣W∣2+3y∣C∣4.
(19)
Here DC​W+CˉDT​W=3C2. The 3∣W∣2 term cancels the negative term in the supergravity F-term potential. The result is the nonnegative scalar potential
V=xy3∣C3+b+(1+αx)A∣2​+xy23∣C∣4​.​
(20)
This is the matter-logarithm no-scale potential, specialized to the exponential superpotential.
Choose the supergravity auxiliary field convention Fi=−eK/2Kijˉ​Dj​W​. Substitution into the full inverse Kähler metric gives
FSFTFC​=eK/2xC3+b+(1+αx)A​,=eK/2yA+b​,=−eK/2yCˉ2.​​
(21)
An overall auxiliary-field phase is conventional. The C dependence of FT cancels between the two metric terms. If all three auxiliary fields vanished at a finite physical point, FC=0 would force C=0, FT=0 would force A+b=0, and FS=0 would then force αxA=0. Thus a=b=0 is necessary. For any nontrivial choice of a,b, supersymmetry is broken at every finite physical point. The allowed exception a=b=0, C=0 is supersymmetric; the statement cannot hold for all arbitrary parameters without this qualification.
The no-scale volume runaway criterion also determines whether the minimum is attained. For fixed S,C, differentiation with respect to y gives
∂y∂V​=−xy43∣C3+b+(1+αx)A∣2​−xy36∣C∣4​.
(22)
It is negative unless both numerators vanish. Therefore a finite stationary vacuum must satisfy
C=0,b+(1+αx)ae−αS=0.​
(23)
Every such point is a global minimum with Vmin​=0. For a,b=0, setting r=∣b/a∣ and z=αx>0 reduces existence to
r=(1+z)e−z/2,arga−αImS=arg(−b)(mod2π).
(24)
The derivative of the magnitude function is (1−z)e−z/2/2. It rises from 1 at the excluded boundary z=0 to 2e−1/2 at z=1, then falls to zero. Consequently a finite zero-energy minimum exists exactly when
a,b=0,0<∣b/a∣≤2e−1/2,​
(25)
or in the separate trivial family a=b=0. There is one positive root for 0<r≤1, two for 1<r<2e−1/2, and one double root at equality. If only one of a,b vanishes, or r exceeds this maximum, there is no finite minimum: the infimum is zero as ReT→∞. Calling this runaway a finite zero-energy vacuum would be incorrect.
At a nontrivial zero-energy minimum, W=A+b=−αxA=0, and the auxiliary expectation values reduce to
FS=FC=0,FT=eK/2yWˉ=0.
(26)
This explicitly verifies supersymmetry breaking with zero vacuum energy in no-scale supergravity. Both real components of T are exact flat directions of a scalar potential on the minimum locus. Away from that locus, ImT is still absent from the potential, but ReT is not flat. At a minimum, C is lifted at quartic order: at fixed S,T, V=3∣C∣4/[x(T+Tˉ)2]+O(∣C∣6). A zero quadratic mass does not make C an exact flat direction. The quartic stabilization at a double no-scale root likewise gives a vanishing quadratic mass, but no exact flat direction, for ReS at z=1. For a=b=0, the whole C=0 family has both S and T flat and supersymmetry unbroken.
Finally, the gravitino mass from a superpotential is
m3/2​=x​y3/2∣C3+A+b∣​.​
(27)
At a nontrivial zero-energy minimum this becomes
m3/2​=y3/2αx​∣A∣​=(1+αx)y3/2αx​∣b∣​>0.​
(28)
It depends on the unfixed volume modulus, so the model does not predict one numerical mass. For a=b=0, C=0, the gravitino is massless.

Use the printed hypercharge convention; reversing every hypercharge relative to another common convention does not change the gauge invariants. Write U,D,E,N for the singlet chiral superfields labelled with a superscript R in the paper, and define the weak-doublet contraction A⋅B=ϵab​AaBb. The most general homogeneous cubic superpotential with right-handed-neutrino superfields separates as W3​=WB,L​+Wviol​, where
WB,L​=(yu​)ij​(Qi​⋅H2​)Uj​+(yd​)ij​(Qi​⋅H1​)Dj​+(ye​)ij​(Li​⋅H1​)Ej​+(yν​)ij​(Li​⋅H2​)Nj​,​
(29)
and
Wviol​=​21​λijk​(Li​⋅Lj​)Ek​+λijk′​(Li​⋅Qj​)Dk​+21​λijk′′​ϵabcUia​Djb​Dkc​+ηi​Ni​(H1​⋅H2​)+61​κijk​Ni​Nj​Nk​.​​
(30)
Repeated generation and color indices are summed. Because the chiral superfields commute while the gauge contractions are antisymmetric,
λijk​=−λjik​,λijk′′​=−λikj′′​,κijk​=κ(ijk)​.
(31)
The factors 1/2 and 1/6 remove the corresponding overcounting.
Here is a completeness check, not merely a list of possible terms. A color-singlet cubic can have no colored fields, a fundamental–antifundamental pair, or three fundamentals or three antifundamentals. A colored pair must be QU or QD: the remaining weak doublet and hypercharge select respectively H2​ or H1​,L. Three antifundamentals allow only UDD at zero hypercharge; three Q doublets have no weak singlet. With no colored fields, weak invariance requires zero or two doublets. Two positive-hypercharge doublets require E, giving LH1​E and LLE, while H1​⋅H1​=0. Opposite-hypercharge doublets require N, giving LH2​N and H1​H2​N. With no doublets, only NNN has zero hypercharge. These are exactly the nine structures above.
For baryon number and lepton number, assign
B(Q)=31​,B(U)=B(D)=−31​,L(L)=1,L(E)=L(N)=−1,
(32)
and zero charges to the Higgs fields. This is the conjugate-singlet convention, so the neutrino Yukawa coupling conserves lepton number. The first four terms conserve both charges. The LLE and LQD terms carry L=1, the UDD term carries B=−1, the NH1​H2​ term carries L=−1, and NNN carries L=−3. The gauge-singlet neutrino interactions must therefore be included in the violating sector.
If “cubic” instead means a polynomial of degree at most three, there are also the lower-degree gauge invariants
W≤2​=W0​+ti​Ni​+21​Mij​Ni​Nj​+μH1​⋅H2​+μi​Li​⋅H2​,Mij​=Mji​.
(33)
The Higgs mass term conserves both charges; ti​Ni​, Mij​Ni​Nj​ and μi​Li​⋅H2​ violate lepton number. These additional terms distinguish the full renormalizable superpotential from its homogeneous cubic part.
To obtain squark-mediated proton decay, combine a lepton number-violating LQD vertex with a baryon number-violating UDD vertex. A nonzero example is λ112′​ with λ112′′​, exchanging the singlet strange quark squark of mass Ms​. The antisymmetry forbids λ111′′​, so using three first-generation singlets would not be a valid example. The relevant component vertices contain eL​uL​sc and ucdcsc, with the required color contraction. Connecting one vertex to the complex conjugate of the other through the massive scalar propagator produces schematically
Leff​⊃Ms2​λ112′​λ112′′∗​​ϵabc​(uRa​dRb​)(eL​uLc​)+h.c.
(34)
Parentheses denote Lorentz scalar two-spinor contractions, dotted for the right-handed pair and undotted for the left-handed pair. This dimension-six proton-decay operator contains the three quarks uud and an electron field that can create a Positron. Its hadronic matrix element ⟨π0∣ϵabc​(uRa​dRb​)uLc​∣p⟩ gives the channel p→e+π0. The internal strange flavor need not appear in the final state. The two interactions together violate both continuous charges, as required for this channel.
The coefficient of the dimension-six effective operator has mass dimension −2. Thus dimensional analysis gives
Γ(p→e+π0)∼ch​∣λ′λ′′∣2Ms4​mp5​​,τp​∼ch​∣λ′λ′′∣2mp5​Ms4​​,​
(35)
where ch​ is a dimensionless hadronic and phase-space factor. Its detailed value cannot be fixed by dimensional reasoning. Applying the historical lower bound supplied in the paper gives the proton-lifetime bound on a product of R-parity-violating couplings
∣λ′λ′′∣≲ch​(2.4×1064GeV−1)mp5​​Ms2​​≃ch​​6.5×10−27​(1TeVMs​​)2(mp​1GeV​)5/2.​
(36)
The numerical coefficient is only the accuracy of the requested dimensional estimate; the squark mass was not fixed by the question.
Finally, R-parity is Rp​=(−1)3(B−L)+2s. At the superfield level use matter parity PM​=(−1)3(B−L): Q,U,D,L,E,N are odd and H1​,H2​ are even. Each conserving Yukawa coupling contains two matter chiral superfields and one Higgs field, so it is even. Every violating homogeneous cubic above is odd: LLE,LQD,UDD,NNN contain three matter fields, while NH1​H2​ contains one. R-parity forbids all the listed cubic violations and preserves the Yukawa terms that generate fermion masses. The Higgs bilinear is also even, whereas the lepton bilinear LH2​ and the singlet linear term are odd.
The final assertion must not be read as forbidding every possible violation of continuous baryon number or lepton number. In particular, matter parity allows Majorana neutrino masses: Ni​Nj​ is even but carries L=−2, a concrete counterexample once quadratic terms are included. Higher-dimensional operators such as QQQL and (LH2​)2 are likewise even and violate the continuous charges. The verified statement here concerns the homogeneous cubic sector, including the neutrino singlet terms.
