A tensor-product surface basis is formed from two independent univariate basis families ϕi​(u) and ψj​(v) on separate parameter intervals. Given a rectangular control net pij​, its parametric surface is
S(u,v)=i∑​j∑​pij​ϕi​(u)ψj​(v).​
(18)
The parameters and indices vary independently over a product domain. The representation can be evaluated first in either parameter, giving a convenient computational advantage. Assume a finite basis, or locally finite spline bases, so differentiation and summation below are justified locally.
For positivity, if each factor basis is nonnegative, then ϕi​(u)ψj​(v)≥0. If both factors are strictly positive at a point, their product is strictly positive there. Nonnegative weights are the usual geometric meaning of positivity; zero boundary weights are allowed. With partition of unity, this gives the local convex hull enclosure of the active control points.
For continuity, suppose the first family is Cr and the second Cs, including matching derivatives across their respective spline knots. On every polynomial piece, and by matching at the knot lines,
∂up​∂vq​S(u,v)=∑i,j​pij​ϕi(p)​(u)ψj(q)​(v),0≤p≤r,0≤q≤s.
(19)
Every factor on the right is continuous in its own variable, so its product is jointly continuous. Thus all these mixed derivatives are continuous, in particular the surface is jointly Cmin(r,s). The separate coordinate smoothness can be better: r orders in u and s in v. For piecewise polynomials of degrees du​,dv​, an interior knot of multiplicity k in the first factor normally gives Cdu​−k matching in u, inherited along that knot line; similarly for v. The statement is an inherited lower bound. Special control points can cancel derivative jumps, so a particular surface may be smoother than a generic member of its basis.
For summation to unity, independent summation factors exactly:
i,j∑​ϕi​(u)ψj​(v)=(i∑​ϕi​(u))(j∑​ψj​(v))=1.​
(20)
This also explains why translations of all control points translate the surface by the same vector, as in affine equivariance of a geometric basis. These three arguments establish tensor-product inheritance of geometric basis properties directly from the factor identities.
A triangular Bézier patch is a non-tensor-product example. For barycentric parameters u,v,w≥0 with u+v+w=1, a quadratic patch is
S=u2p200​+v2p020​+w2p002​+2uvp110​+2uwp101​+2vwp011​.
(21)
The six basis functions have total degree two and sum to (u+v+w)2=1. Their index constraint and triangular parameter domain do not arise from two independently indexed univariate factors on a rectangle. An individual triangular polynomial could be embedded in another larger representation, but this triangular basis and control net are not a tensor-product surface definition.
