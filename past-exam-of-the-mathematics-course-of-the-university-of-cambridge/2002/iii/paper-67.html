Use Minkowski spacetime with signature (−,+,…,+), and write the string tension as T=1/(2πα′). If Xμ(τ,σ) is the string embedding map, its induced worldsheet metric is γab​=∂a​X⋅∂b​X. The Nambu–Goto action is
S=−T∫dτdσ−detγ​.
(1)
The first variation uses δγab​=2∂(a​Xμ​∂b)​δXμ and therefore gives
δS=−T∫−γ​γab∂a​Xμ​∂b​δXμdτdσ.
(2)
An integration by parts yields the bulk Nambu–Goto equations of motion and the endpoint term:
∂a​(−γ​γab∂b​Xμ)=0​,Πμa​=−T−γ​γab∂b​Xμ​,Πμσ​δXμ​∂Σ​=0.
(3)
For a closed string, periodicity cancels the endpoint term. For a freely moving open string, arbitrary endpoint variations require zero momentum flux, which is the free-end string boundary condition.
Choose orthogonal conformal coordinates on the nondegenerate part of the worldsheet, so that γτσ​=0 and γττ​+γσσ​=0. The Nambu–Goto equations of motion then become a wave equation, with the accompanying Virasoro constraints:
X¨−X′′=0,X˙⋅X′=0,X˙2+X′2=0.
(4)
Here a dot and a prime mean ∂τ​ and ∂σ​. The last two equations are essential: a solution of the wave equation alone need not be a relativistic fundamental string motion.
For a free-ended open string, take 0≤σ≤π and a>0, and set
X0=aτ,X1=acosσcosτ,X2=acosσsinτ,
(5)
with the other coordinates constant. Every component satisfies the wave equation, and
X˙2=−a2sin2σ,X′2=a2sin2σ,X˙⋅X′=0.
(6)
Moreover X′=0 at both endpoints, giving the Neumann boundary condition. At fixed target time t=aτ, the spatial image is the straight segment
X=r(cos(t/a),sin(t/a)),−a≤r≤a.
(7)
Thus its angular velocity is 1/a. The speed at coordinate r is ∣r∣/a, so the endpoints have the null motion of a free string endpoint. The induced worldsheet metric degenerates there; the conserved densities below have finite limits, so the endpoint degeneracy causes no divergent charge.
In these coordinates Πτμ=TX˙μ. The target-space Noether charges of a string give the energy, momentum and angular momentum:
E=∫0π​TX˙0dσ=πTa,P=T∫0π​X˙dσ=0,
(8)
J12​=T∫0π​(X1X˙2−X2X˙1)dσ=Ta2∫0π​cos2σdσ=2πTa2​.
(9)
Since this is the centre-of-momentum frame, the rest mass is M=E. Eliminating a proves the free-ended straight-string Regge relation:
J=2πTM2​=α′M2(free-ended open string)​.
(10)
A spacetime Lorentz transformation of the solution changes its frame, not this rest-frame relation.
There is also a folded closed string version: use the same formula over 0≤σ≤2π. The spatial segment is then covered twice, with folds at σ=0,π, and the closed-string mode expansion is periodic. Each charge is twice its open string value, so
M=2πTa,J=πTa2,J=4πTM2​=2α′M2​(folded closed string)​.
(11)
The folds also have degenerate induced metric and are understood through the finite canonical densities or a limiting smooth motion. The two classical Regge trajectories have different slopes because the closed string has two coincident branches. No quantum string intercept enters this classical calculation.

The Nambu–Goto action is invariant under worldsheet diffeomorphisms. Its canonical Nambu–Goto phase-space constraints are
Π⋅X′=0,Π2+T2X′2=0.
(12)
In conformal gauge, Π=TX˙, so these become (X˙±X′)2=0. Their smeared generators implement the residual conformal transformations rather than independent propagating degrees of freedom. Classically their Fourier modes form the classical Virasoro constraint algebra. Quantization promotes them to the Virasoro algebra, and its constraints select the physical string states from the indefinite covariant Fock space.
For clarity, start with a Neumann boundary condition at both ends of an open string, 0≤σ≤π. Its open-string mode expansion is
Xμ=xμ+2α′pμτ+i2α′​∑n=0​nαnμ​​e−inτcosnσ,α0μ​=2α′​pμ.
(13)
Canonical quantization gives the string oscillator relations
[αmμ​,αnν​]=mδm+n,0​ημν,(αnμ​)†=α−nμ​.
(14)
With ∂±​=(∂τ​±∂σ​)/2, the worldsheet stress-energy tensor obeys
α′1​∂±​X⋅∂±​X=∑m∈Z​Lm​e−im(τ±σ).
(15)
Substituting the open-string mode expansion into this quadratic expression derives the Virasoro generators:
Lm​=21​r∈Z∑​:αm−r​⋅αr​:​,L0​=α′p2+N,N=∑r>0​α−r​⋅αr​.
(16)
The colons mean normal ordering, placing the negative-mode creation operators to the left. The string level operator N increases by r when an oscillator α−r​ is added.
Using the commutator once gives
[Lm​,αnμ​]=−nαm+nμ​.
(17)
Applying this to the quadratic expression for Ln​ produces (m−n)Lm+n​, together with the double-contraction Virasoro central extension. To determine its coefficient, take m>0 and evaluate [Lm​,L−m​] on a zero-momentum oscillator vacuum. Only the creation pairs with 1≤r≤m−1 contribute, giving
2D​∑r=1m−1​r(m−r)=12D​(m3−m).
(18)
Here D is the target spacetime dimension; the timelike free boson also contributes one to the central charge, since a double contraction contains two metric factors. Thus
[Lm​,Ln​]=(m−n)Lm+n​+12D​(m3−m)δm+n,0​​,Lm†​=L−m​.
(19)
This derives both the Virasoro generators and their quantum anomaly.
In old covariant string quantization, the physical-state Virasoro conditions for an open string are
Ln>0​∣ψ⟩=0,(L0​−a)∣ψ⟩=0,
(20)
where a is the normal-ordering constant of a string. Requiring all positive and negative modes to annihilate a state would contradict the nonzero Virasoro central extension; the positive-mode prescription is the appropriate covariant subsidiary condition. The zero-mode equation gives the open bosonic string mass spectrum,
α′M2=N−a.
(21)
For a closed string, there are two commuting Virasoro algebras. With uncompactified zero modes, L0​=α′p2/4+NR​ and L0​=α′p2/4+NL​. Imposing both positive-mode conditions and both zero-mode conditions gives the closed-string level matching NL​=NR​ and M2=4(NL​−a)/α′.
Covariance initially retains the timelike string oscillators. For example, α−10​∣0;p⟩ has negative norm because η00=−1. Its existence in the unconstrained space is not an inconsistency if it is absent from the physical quotient. The no-ghost theorem for the critical bosonic string states that, at D=26 and a=1, the inner product on physical string states at ordinary nonzero momentum is positive semidefinite. After quotienting physical null string states, this physical space is represented by the 24 positive-norm transverse string oscillators.
A spurious string state has the form ∑n>0​L−n​∣χn​⟩. Its inner product with every physical string state vanishes, by Ln​∣ψ⟩=0 and Ln†​=L−n​. Whenever such a state is itself physical, it is a null string state and represents a gauge redundancy, not an extra polarization. Consequently the physical quotient has no negative-norm propagating states, and agrees with light-cone gauge in string theory. This is the significance of the theorem; a proof is not needed here. It does not eliminate the bosonic tachyon, whose negative mass squared is distinct from negative norm, nor does it remove the auxiliary worldsheet ghost fields used in gauge fixing.

The Polyakov action introduces an independent worldsheet metric hab​:
SP​=−4πα′1​∫d2σ−h​hab∂a​X⋅∂b​X.
(22)
Its redundancies are worldsheet diffeomorphisms and Weyl transformations. A Polyakov path integral over all metrics without dividing by this gauge volume would count physically equivalent configurations repeatedly. Fixing conformal gauge therefore needs the Faddeev-Popov determinant, not merely the substitution of a flat metric into the action.
After using the Weyl transformation to remove the trace of a metric variation, the infinitesimal worldsheet diffeomorphism ghost operator is
(P1​c)ab​=∇a​cb​+∇b​ca​−hab​∇d​cd.
(23)
The Faddeev-Popov determinant of this operator is represented by anticommuting fields: a vector Faddeev-Popov ghost ca and a symmetric traceless Faddeev-Popov antighost field bab. In Euclidean coordinates their worldsheet ghost action is, up to a consistent overall normalization,
Sgh​=2π1​∫h​bab(P1​c)ab​d2σ=2π1​∫d2z(b∂ˉc+bˉ∂cˉ).
(24)
The two chiral copies of this bc system have conformal weights (2,−1) and anticommutators {bm​,cn​}=δm+n,0​. They are auxiliary fields for the gauge determinant, not additional spacetime particles. Their use is different from the unwanted timelike negative-norm string states of covariant matter quantization.
The chiral holomorphic stress-energy tensor of the bc system is
Tgh​=−2:b∂c:−:(∂b)c:.
(25)
Double contractions in its operator product expansion give the central charge of reparameterization ghosts cgh​=−26. Equivalently, a fermionic bc system with antighost weight λ has c=1−3(2λ−1)2, and λ=2 gives −26. The free coordinate matter contributes cm​=D. Thus the total worldsheet Weyl anomaly vanishes for the flat critical bosonic string theory precisely when
ctot​=D−26=0​.
(26)
This statement assumes no additional matter or compensating Liouville field theory.
BRST symmetry expresses the gauge symmetry after gauge fixing by replacing its infinitesimal parameter with the Grassmann variable c. In one chiral conformal patch, its graded action has the form
sXμ=c∂Xμ,sc=c∂c,sb=Ttot​;
(27)
there is an analogous barred copy for a closed string. The Grassmann parity makes s2X=0: the term from sc cancels the term from applying s to ∂X, while the term with c2 vanishes. The gauge-fixed chiral transformations are on shell when their conservation equations are used. At the quantum level, BRST nilpotence also tests the anomaly.
For example, an open string BRST charge can be written using the matter Virasoro generators as
QB​=∑n​c−n​Lnm​−21​∑m,n​(m−n):c−m​c−n​bm+n​:−ac0​.
(28)
Use the ghost oscillator vacuum bn≥0​∣0⟩mathrmgh​=cn>0​∣0⟩mathrmgh​=0 and the corresponding oscillator normal ordering. In this convention the last term is −c0​ in the critical theory. With a differently shifted ghost L0​, the displayed intercept must be shifted consistently; one must not count the same shift twice. Squaring QB​ shows why both critical conditions are needed. Its anomalous terms, in this convention, have coefficients
QB2​=21​∑n∈Z​[12D−26​(n3−n)+2(a−1)n]c−n​cn​.
(29)
The independent cubic and linear terms vanish at D=26, a=1. The equivalent chiral BRST current is jB​=c(Tmathrmm​+Tmathrmgh​/2)+(3/2)∂2c, with its contour integral defining the charge.
The graded identity {QB​,bn​}=Lntot​ recovers the gauge constraints. In particular, on ∣ψ⟩⊗∣0⟩mathrmgh​, BRST-closed imposes Ln>0m​∣ψ⟩=0 and (L0m​−1)∣ψ⟩=0. Physical states are classes in BRST cohomology,
Hphys​=kerQB​/imQB​​,∣ψ⟩∼∣ψ⟩+QB​∣χ⟩,
(30)
at the appropriate ghost number: one for the usual open-string vertex and two for the closed-string vertex, with the closed-string zero-mode and closed-string level matching conditions also imposed. Since QB2​=0, an exact shift preserves closure. BRST Ward identities make exact insertions decouple from physical amplitudes and establish gauge independence. Together with the no-ghost theorem for the critical bosonic string, this explains how the auxiliary ghosts enforce gauge invariance while the physical spectrum retains positive norm.

Let Y be the compact coordinate, identified modulo 2πR. The closed string may wrap the circle:
Y(τ,σ+2π)=Y(τ,σ)+2πwR,w∈Z.
(31)
Single-valued momentum eigenfunctions eipY require p=n/R, n∈Z. These are the momentum and winding modes. Write the chiral zero-mode momenta as
pL​=Rn​+α′wR​,pR​=Rn​−α′wR​.
(32)
The left-moving string oscillators will be tilded: NL​=N, NR​=N. This fixes the sign in compact-circle closed-string level matching.
Define the lower-dimensional mass by M2=−pnoncompact2​. The two closed-string physical-state Virasoro conditions, with bosonic string intercept one, are
4α′​(−M2+pL2​)+NL​−1=0,4α′​(−M2+pR2​)+NR​−1=0.
(33)
Their average and difference derive the complete free bosonic string mass spectrum on the circle:
M2=R2n2​+α′2w2R2​+α′2​(NL​+NR​−2)​,NL​−NR​+nw=0​.
(34)
The allowed states have nonnegative integer oscillator levels and obey the Virasoro constraints within each chiral sector. Compactification has not removed oscillator polarizations; it has added quantized compact momentum and allowed winding while changing the lower-dimensional interpretation of those polarizations.
At large R, the momentum and winding modes with w=0 have closely spaced momentum energies ∣n∣/R, whereas nonzero winding costs ∣w∣R/α′. At small R their roles reverse. The precise equality is T-duality:
R′=Rα′​,n′=w,w′=n​.
(35)
It leaves pL​ unchanged and reverses pR​, and leaves the mass formula and closed-string level matching unchanged. On the string embedding map it reverses the right-moving part of Y. Therefore the small-radius spectrum is the large-radius spectrum of the dual theory, rather than a spectrum in which all charged states simply become heavy.
At a generic radius, the neutral level (NL​,NR​)=(1,1) contains the lower-dimensional graviton, Kalb–Ramond field and dilaton, two gauge bosons from the mixed components of the metric and two-form, and the radius modulus of a string compactification. The two vector charges are the Cartan charges of U(1)L​×U(1)R​.
Consider instead the following charged states:
(NL​,NR​)=(0,1),(n,w)=(1,1),(−1,−1),
(36)
(NL​,NR​)=(1,0),(n,w)=(1,−1),(−1,1).
(37)
Every state satisfies compact-circle closed-string level matching. The single noncompact string oscillator supplies a vector polarization. At the massless point, the level-one Virasoro constraint imposes transverse polarization and its physical null string state identifies polarizations differing by the noncompact momentum. Thus these are genuine lower-dimensional vector states. Their squared mass is
M2=R21​+α′2R2​−α′2​=(R1​−α′R​)2.
(38)
Thus four additional charged massless spin-one particles appear at
R=α′​​.
(39)
For (NL​,NR​)=(0,1) the charges have pR​=0, pL​=±2/α′​; for (1,0) they have pL​=0, pR​=±2/α′​. This identifies the two pairs of charged roots.
The enhanced gauge group can be derived rather than just inferred from counting. At the self-dual circle, the chiral compact boson satisfies YL​(z)YL​(u)∼−(α′/2)log(z−u). The self-dual circle current algebra has currents
JL3​=α′​i​∂YL​,JL±​=:exp(±α′​2iYL​​):.
(40)
The exponentials have conformal weight α′pL2​/4=1. Free-boson contractions give the operator product expansions
J3(z)J3(u)∼2(z−u)21​,J3(z)J±(u)∼z−u±J±(u)​,
(41)
J+(z)J−(u)∼(z−u)21​+z−u2J3(u)​.
(42)
They form the level-one SU(2) Lie algebra, and there is an independent right-moving copy. The string vertex operators JLa​∂ˉXμ and ∂XμJRa​ give the six massless vectors. Consequently
U(1)L​×U(1)R​⟶SU(2)L​×SU(2)R​​.
(43)
Changing R gives the four charged vectors the mass found above, consistent with the radius deformation as a Higgs mechanism.
For completeness, the enhanced spectrum also has scalar fields. Using a compact rather than noncompact oscillator in the four singly excited charged families gives four scalars. At (NL​,NR​)=(0,0), closed-string level matching requires nw=0; the mass equation then gives the four charges (±2,0),(0,±2). These exceptional massless ground states of a bosonic circle give another four scalars. Together with the radius scalar they form nine scalars in the (3,3) representation, in addition to the neutral dilaton. The ever-present neutral bosonic tachyon still has M2=−4/α′. The enhancement establishes the additional massless vectors, not stability of the entire bosonic vacuum.

The RNS string adds worldsheet Majorana fermions ψμ to the coordinate fields Xμ. The new fields are spinors on the worldsheet but vectors under the target Lorentz group; this distinction matters when interpreting their oscillator states. In a flat target, a convenient Lorentzian convention for the free gauge-fixed action is
S=−4πα′1​∫d2σ(∂a​Xμ∂aXμ​−iψˉ​μρa∂a​ψμ​),
(44)
where ρa are two-dimensional gamma matrices. The bosonic and fermionic kinetic terms are related by worldsheet supersymmetry. Before fixing local worldsheet supersymmetry, one introduces a worldsheet gravitino along with the worldsheet metric. After superconformal gauge fixing, the residual matter constraints are the Virasoro constraints and the constraints generated by the superconformal current G∝ψ⋅∂X.
Each real worldsheet Majorana fermion contributes 1/2 to the chiral central charge, while each coordinate contributes one. The reparameterization bc system contributes −26, and fixing local worldsheet supersymmetry also produces commuting superconformal ghosts of weights (3/2,−1/2), with central charge 11. Thus
ctot​=23​D−26+11=0⟹D=10​.
(45)
In light-cone gauge in string theory there are eight physical transverse coordinate fields and eight transverse worldsheet Majorana fermions.
On the doubled spatial interval of an open string, the two allowed fermionic periodicities are
ψ(σ+2π)=+ψ(σ)(Ramond),ψ(σ+2π)=−ψ(σ)(Neveu–Schwarz).
(46)
They arise from the relative signs of the left-right fermion gluing conditions at the two ends. Thus the Ramond sector has integer-mode oscillators dn​, including d0​, and the Neveu–Schwarz sector has half-integer oscillators br​, r∈Z+1/2, without zero modes. In either sector,
{ψrμ​,ψsν​}=ημνδr+s,0​.
(47)
A closed string has independent left- and right-moving periodicities, giving four sector choices rather than two.
The Ramond zero-mode Clifford algebra is
{d0μ​,d0ν​}=ημν,Γμ=2​d0μ​,{Γμ,Γν}=2ημν.
(48)
Hence its ground states carry a Spinor representation of the Lorentz group. The zero-mode supercurrent constraint reduces on a ground state to the Dirac equation pμ​Γμu=0. In the Neveu–Schwarz sector the oscillator vacuum instead carries a scalar representation. Consequently the open string NS sector describes spacetime bosons, while the R sector describes spacetime fermions. Exciting a target-vector oscillator does not change this statistics assignment.
The string intercepts follow from the transverse zero-point energy. Per boson the integer-mode contribution is 21​ζ(−1)=−1/24. Per fermion it is the negative of the corresponding half-sum. Since ζ(−1,1/2)=1/24, the eight transverse pairs give
E0NS​=8(−241​−481​)=−21​,E0R​=8(−241​+241​)=0.
(49)
Thus aNS​=1/2, aR​=0, and the open string mass formula is α′M2=N−a. This distinguishes the lowest states explicitly:
  • In the NS sector, the level-zero scalar is a tachyon with M2=−1/(2α′). At level 1/2, ϵμ​b−1/2μ​∣0;p⟩ is a massless vector. The supercurrent and Virasoro constraints give p⋅ϵ=0 and the equivalence ϵ∼ϵ+λp. There are eight physical vector polarizations.
  • In the R sector, the level-zero states are massless spacetime spinors satisfying the Dirac equation. Before the GSO projection, the two chiralities together give 16 on-shell polarizations; they split into the two eight-dimensional transverse spinor representations.
The supersymmetric GSO projection keeps odd oscillator fermion number in the NS sector. Defining Fosc​ so that the NS vacuum has Fosc​=0, its projector is
PNS​=21​(1−(−1)Fosc​).
(50)
It removes the scalar tachyon and keeps the massless vector. In the R sector, fermion parity contains the zero-mode chirality operator, so one may write
PR​=21​(1+ηΓ11​(−1)Fosc​),η=±1.
(51)
Here Fosc​ counts nonzero-mode fermionic excitations. On the ground states, this keeps one Majorana-Weyl spinor chirality: 16 real covariant components, or eight on-shell polarizations after the Dirac equation. The projected massless open-string states are an eight-polarization gauge vector and an eight-polarization gaugino. The compatible projected tower exhibits spacetime supersymmetry; unprojected worldsheet supersymmetry by itself did not eliminate the tachyon or equalize these massless counts.
For a closed string, both chiral zero-mode constraints must hold:
M2=α′4​(NL​−aL​)=α′4​(NR​−aR​)​,NL​−aL​=NR​−aR​​.
(52)
This shifted closed-string level matching is particularly important in the mixed sectors. The lowest states and their spacetime nature are as follows.
  • The NS-NS sector is bosonic. Before projection, (NL​,NR​)=(0,0) is a scalar tachyon with M2=−2/α′. The GSO projection on each side removes it. The surviving lowest level is (1/2,1/2), with transverse states b−1/2i​b−1/2j​∣0;p⟩. Decomposing their tensor product into symmetric traceless, antisymmetric and trace parts gives the graviton, Kalb–Ramond field and dilaton, with 35+28+1=64 physical polarizations.
  • The RR sector is bosonic, since it combines two spacetime spinors. Its lowest states have (NL​,NR​)=(0,0) and are massless. The two Dirac equations on the bispinor polarizations identify them with on-shell differential-form field strengths. After projection they supply 64 bosonic polarizations. The actual form degrees depend on the two chosen Ramond sector chiralities.
  • The NS-R sector and R-NS sector are fermionic. Their respective lowest level-matched pairs are (1/2,0) and (0,1/2), not two oscillator vacua. A vector tensored with a chiral spinor decomposes into its gamma-traceless vector-spinor and gamma trace. These give a massless gravitino and dilatino, with 56+8=64 polarizations in each mixed sector. The vacuum pair would fail the shifted closed-string level matching, so it cannot be counted as a mixed-sector tachyon.
For type IIA superstring theory, the two R sector ground-state chiralities are opposite. The Ramond–Ramond potentials can be represented by C1​,C3​, with transverse counts 8+56=64. The two mixed-sector gravitinos have opposite chiralities, and the ten-dimensional theory is nonchiral. For type IIB superstring theory, the two R sector chiralities agree; the Ramond–Ramond potentials are C0​,C2​,C4​, with self-dual five-form field strength, giving 1+28+35=64. Its two gravitinos have the same chirality. Thus both projected type II theories have 128 massless bosonic and 128 massless fermionic polarizations and no tachyon, while their chiralities and form spectra differ.
If unoriented open strings are included in the complete theory, the supersymmetric example is type I string theory. Worldsheet orientation reversal projects the closed type IIB superstring theory spectrum and identifies the two mixed sectors; the surviving closed fields include the graviton, dilaton, a Ramond–Ramond potential C2​, and one gravitino and dilatino. The open states form vector and gaugino multiplets with Chan-Paton factors; tadpole and anomaly cancellation select SO(32). This is an additional orientation projection, not part of the GSO projection itself. The heterotic string instead pairs one supersymmetric chiral side with a bosonic chiral side carrying an internal gauge sector; it does not have a Ramond choice on both sides.
