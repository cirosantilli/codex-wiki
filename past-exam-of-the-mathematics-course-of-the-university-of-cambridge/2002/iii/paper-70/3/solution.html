The topological degree assigns an integer to a continuous map f:M→N between closed, connected, oriented manifolds of the same dimension d. Their top homology groups are infinite cyclic, generated by their fundamental classes. The induced map defines

This is a signed count; opposite-orientation preimages cancel. If ω is a normalized target volume form with ∫N​ω=1, the pullback of a differential form gives the equivalent integral degf=∫M​f∗ω.

Degrees multiply under composition, and a nonzero-degree map is surjective: a missed target point would give a regular value with an empty preimage and hence degree zero.
For maps Sd→Sd, the homotopy group πd​(Sd)=Z is completely detected by degree. This supplies integer topological sectors for many classical field-theory solitons. It is important to distinguish two ways spheres arise. In a nonlinear sigma model with the field approaching one fixed target value at infinity, compactify all of Rd to Sd and classify the resulting map into the target. For a defect whose vacuum field varies with direction, retain the sphere S∞d−1​ surrounding the core and map it into the vacuum manifold. This is the vacuum-boundary degree as a defect charge. An asymptotic limit is a boundary hypothesis; finite energy alone in every possible model need not imply one.

Nonzero degree obstructs extending the normalized Higgs direction through the ball; the full Higgs field must leave the vacuum manifold, normally vanishing in its core. The asymptotic unbroken Abelian magnetic flux measures the same integer with a convention-dependent sign.
For the Skyrme model, a field U:R3→SU(2) with U→1 at infinity gives S3→S3, because SU(2) as the three-sphere identifies the target. A choice of orientations gives its topological baryon number in the Skyrme model as the winding integral ±(24π2)−1∫tr(U−1dU)3; specifying the orientation fixes the sign. Similarly, the transition map at infinity of a four-dimensional Yang-Mills instanton has degree in π3​(SU(2)), measured by its Chern charge.
A scalar-field kink illustrates the discrete-vacuum case: its endpoint data lie in π0​({±1}), rather than being a map between connected positive-dimensional spheres. For the double-well field its topological charge is Q=[ϕ(+∞)−ϕ(−∞)]/2. A kink has Q=1, an antikink Q=−1; they cannot individually deform to vacuum while their endpoints are fixed.
Degree classifies topology, rather than every solution. Smooth motion or local perturbations with fixed boundary data preserve it, and a nonzero degree obstructs decay to the vacuum within that class. It does not guarantee existence of a smooth energy minimizer, a fixed size, uniqueness, or dynamical stability. The degree and energetic stability of a field configuration are distinct: a field can shrink towards a singular limit at fixed degree, as Derrick's theorem diagnoses in many two-derivative theories. Moreover degree need not classify all homotopy classes for an arbitrary target; other homotopy groups or invariants may be needed. Within one degree there can be continuous moduli spaces and several stationary solutions.
