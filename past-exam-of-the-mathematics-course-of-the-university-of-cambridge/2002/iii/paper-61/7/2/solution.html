Use the standard L∞​-oriented partition normalization of B-splines, in contrast to the unit-integral normalization. Thus Ni​≥0 and ∑i​Ni​(t)≤1 on [0,1], with equality for a complete basis on its basic knot interval. This terminology does not mean that each basis function's maximum is exactly one; for example an order-three cardinal spline has maximum 3/4. Assume the basis has positive support on the integration interval and is continuous, as is needed for a projection taking values in C[0,1].
Let
Hij​=∫01​Ni​(t)Nj​(t)dt,di​=∫01​Ni​(t)dt>0,D=diag(di​).
(42)
The ordinary Gram matrix H is positive definite by linear independence. The appropriate row-normalized, or mixed-normalization spline Gram matrix, is
G=D−1H,Gij​=∫01​Mi​(t)Nj​(t)dt,Mi​=Ni​/di​.​
(43)
Here Mi​ is a unit-integral nonnegative spline. Unlike H, G need not be symmetric. If the full support lies inside the interval, di​=(ti+k​−ti​)/k; otherwise use the actual interval integral rather than that full-support formula.
The normal equations for the L2​ orthogonal projection PS​f=∑i​ai​Ni​ say Ha=c, with ci​=∫fNi​. Divide each row by di​ to obtain
Ga=b,bi​=∫01​f(t)Mi​(t)dt.
(44)
Nonnegativity and unit integral give ∣bi​∣≤∥f∥∞​. The subpartition of unity for B-splines then gives
∣PS​f(t)∣≤∑i​∣ai​∣Ni​(t)≤∥a∥ℓ∞​≤∥G−1∥ℓ∞​∥f∥∞​.
(45)
Taking the supremum over t and then over nonzero f proves the uniform norm bound for B-spline orthogonal projection:
∥PS​∥C[0,1]→C[0,1]​≤∥G−1∥ℓ∞​.​
(46)
The row normalization is essential; replacing G with the ordinary Gram matrix would not be the same asserted estimate. The operator is defined on C[0,1] and maps into its finite-dimensional spline subspace, onto S, not onto all of C[0,1] as one phrase in the PDF says. Its range follows both from the formula and from reproducing every s∈S.
If “L∞​-normalized” were instead read literally as individually peak-one splines, their sum can exceed one. The basis-dependent factor K=∥∑i​Ni​∥∞​ must then be retained: ∥PS​∥∞​≤K∥(D−1H)−1∥ℓ∞​. Equivalently the displayed bound without an outside factor uses G=K−1D−1H. The standard partition-normalized convention has K≤1 and yields the sharper bound proved above.
