The reproduction hypothesis gives U1=1, U(⋅)(t)=t and U((⋅)2)(t)=t2. If “quadratic” were read as degree exactly two, the same identities follow by subtracting pairs of such reproduced polynomials. Hence
U((⋅−t)2)(t)=0.
(5)
Apply the same positive-barrier argument as in part (a), now with no moment errors. It gives ∣Uf(t)−f(t)∣≤ε for every t and every ε>0. Therefore
Uf=f for every f∈C[0,1],U=I.​
(6)
The identity is itself a positive linear operator on continuous functions reproducing all quadratics, so existence as well as uniqueness is established. In particular, reproducing three small test functions is much stronger for a positive operator than merely fixing one finite-dimensional polynomial space without positivity.
