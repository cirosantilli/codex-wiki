The original PDF prints O(nα), with a positive exponent. That premise is insufficient; the intended decay rate is O(n−α). Indeed every bounded continuous periodic function has En​(f)≤∥f∥∞​=O(nα). For an explicit counterexample, on [−π,π] set
h(0)=0,h(x)=log(e/∣x∣)1​ (0<∣x∣≤1),h(x)=1 (1≤∣x∣≤π),
(13)
and extend periodically. This is continuous, but ω(h,δ)≥1/log(e/δ) for 0<δ<1, which is not O(δα) for any α>0. Thus the sign correction is required in the actual printed question, not just in its converted TeX.
For real continuous 2π-periodic f, write En​ for best uniform approximation by degree-at-most-n trigonometric polynomials and ω(f,δ)=sup∣h∣≤δ​∥f(⋅+h)−f∥∞​. A quantitative inverse theorem for trigonometric approximation is
ω(f,1/n)≤nC​ν=0∑n​Eν​(f),n≥1,​
(14)
where C is universal. Here is a direct reason for the sum. Choose best approximants pj​ and 2r≤n<2r+1. Write
p2r​=p0​+(p1​−p0​)+∑j=1r​(p2j​−p2j−1​).
(15)
The Bernstein inequality for trigonometric polynomials gives
∥p2r′​∥∞​≤2E0​+∑j=1r​2j+1E2j−1​.
(16)
For j≥2, monotonicity of best errors bounds 2j+1E2j−1​ by 8∑ν=2j−22j−1−1​Eν​. The first terms are bounded by a fixed multiple of E0​. Also 2E2r​≤4n−1∑ν=0n​Eν​. Now
ω(f,1/n)≤2E2r​+n1​∥p2r′​∥∞​
(17)
proves the theorem, for example with a sufficiently large absolute constant C=20.
Suppose, as intended, Eν​(f)≤Aν−α for ν≥1, after enlarging A to include any finite exceptional indices, where 0<α<1. Since ∑ν=1n​ν−α≤n1−α/(1−α), the inverse theorem gives
ω(f,1/n)≤C(E0​/n+1−αA​n−α)≤Cf​n−α.
(18)
For 0<δ≤1 take n=⌊1/δ⌋. Then 1/(n+1)<δ≤1/n, so monotonicity of the modulus of continuity and n−α≤2αδα give ω(f,δ)≤2αCf​δα. For δ>1, the separate bound ω(f,δ)≤2∥f∥∞​≤2∥f∥∞​δα covers the remaining scales. Therefore the corrected conclusion is
En​(f)=O(n−α)⟹ω(f,δ)=O(δα),0<α<1.​
(19)
Now put β=log2/log3. The given best approximant to the Weierstrass function leaves the uniformly convergent tail ∑k=m+1∞​2−kcos(3kx). Its supremum norm is exactly 2−m, attained at x=0; hence
En​(g)=2−m(3m≤n<3m+1),En​(g)≍n−β.
(20)
The corrected inverse theorem gives ω(g,δ)=O(δβ). To show that this order is sharp, set hm​=π/3m+1. Every term of g(0)−g(hm​) is nonnegative, and for k≥m+1 the cosine equals −1 because 3k−m−1 is odd. Thus
g(0)−g(hm​)≥2∑k=m+1∞​2−k=21−m=4π−βhmβ​.
(21)
Given small δ>0, select m with hm​≤δ<3hm​. Then ω(g,δ)≥4(3π)−βδβ. Combining the estimates gives the precise small-scale order
ω(g,δ)≍δlog2/log3(δ↓0).​
(22)
The exact approximation error can also be verified independently of the supplied fact: at xj​=jπ/3m+1 the tail has alternating values (−1)j2−m. There are enough such points in one period for the trigonometric Chebyshev alternation theorem, since n<3m+1. This is the positive lacunary trigonometric series mechanism behind the best partial sums.
