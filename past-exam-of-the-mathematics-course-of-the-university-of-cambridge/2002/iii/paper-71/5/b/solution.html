For small baryon fraction the CDM growing mode is δC​∝a, and its gravity forces
δB′′​+HδB′​+cs2​k2δB​≃4πGa2ρˉ​C​δC​.
(38)
For an adiabatic cosmological perturbation to follow the unsuppressed growing mode, pressure must be below the gravitational forcing frequency: cs2​k2≲4πGa2ρˉ​C​. With physical wavelength λ=2πa/k, this becomes
λ≳λJ​=cs​Gρˉ​C​π​​​.
(39)
This is Jeans support versus forced baryon response. The wording “only grow” must refer to unsuppressed gravitational structure growth; it is not a theorem that all shorter-wavelength baryon perturbations are absent or constant. In the pressure-dominated regime a forced response δB​≃(kJ​/k)2δC​ remains. In particular, a falling sound speed can make this small response grow even before it catches up to CDM.
Before photon decoupling, the photon-baryon fluid has sound speed of order c/3​. Its Jeans scale is roughly a horizon scale, so smaller perturbations oscillate acoustically instead of freely following the growing CDM structures. After decoupling the baryon sound speed drops sharply. Above the new Jeans scale, pressure is negligible and subtraction of the CDM equation gives (δB​−δC​)′′+H(δB​−δC​)′=0. For a∝τ2, the difference is a constant plus a decaying 1/τ term; its ratio to the growing CDM contrast tends to zero. Baryons therefore catch up. Under the stipulated post-decoupling cs​∝T∝a−1, the physical Jeans length scales as a1/2, the comoving scale as a−1/2 and the corresponding Jeans mass as a−3/2.
For the requested instantaneous mass estimates, define the baryonic Jeans mass as the baryon content of a sphere of radius λJ​/2:
MJB​=6π​ρˉ​B​λJ3​,ρˉ​B​≃0.1ρˉ​C​.
(40)
