Use the convention consistent with the supplied fluid transformations: δgij​=a2hij​, so the synchronous spatial metric is gij​=−a2δij​+a2hij​. For a passive coordinate displacement ξ0=Teik⋅x, ξi=ik^iLeik⋅x, the metric perturbation changes by δg​μν​=δgμν​−(Lξ​gˉ​)μν​. Computing its components gives
δg​00​=−2a2(HT+T′),δg​0i​=ia2ki​(L′/k−T),
(50)
a2δg​ij​​=[3h−hS​​+2HT]δij​+(hS​−2kL)k^i​k^j​.
(51)
The Newtonian gauge has no mixed component and no traceless spatial part. Thus
L=2khS​​,T=kL′​=2k2hS′​​​.
(52)
Matching δg​00​=2a2Φ and δg​ij​=2a2Ψδij​ gives
Φ=−HT−T′=−2k2HhS′​+hS′′​​,Ψ=6h−hS​​+2k2HhS′​​​.
(53)
This is the synchronous-to-Newtonian transformation with positive spatial perturbations. The original PDF uses ik^L in the spatial displacement; the converted TeX loses the hat. Using ikL would require L=hS​/(2k2) instead. The nonzero modes k>0 are understood.
