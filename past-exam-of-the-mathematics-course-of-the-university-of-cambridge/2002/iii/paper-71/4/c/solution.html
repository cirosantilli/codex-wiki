There is no minimum about which this scalar can undergo the damped oscillations normally used for reheating. Its slow-roll parameters are constant, so an inflating exponential solution has no automatic exit. An independent stopping and energy-transfer mechanism is needed.
At the assumed exit, VR​=mpl4​e−30. Equating this to the thermal radiation density gives
TR​≃(π2g∗​30​)1/4mpl​e−30/4≃1.5×1015 GeV​
(31)
for g∗​=103. Corrections from the small kinetic fraction do not change this rough estimate.
Curvature obeys ∣Ω−1∣∝(aH)−2. For constant ϵH​=A2/(16π) and N=log(aR​/ai​), its suppression during cosmic inflation is e−2(1−ϵH​)N. Starting from order-one curvature and requiring present curvature no larger than order one gives
N≳1−ϵH​log(aR​HR​/a0​H0​)​.
(32)
After instantaneous reheating, entropy conservation implies aR​/a0​=(T0​/TR​)(g∗s,0​/g∗s,R​)1/3, with HR​≃1.66g∗​​TR2​/mpl​. Present scales make the numerator roughly 60; using the literal approximate units from question2 gives about 63. A tighter present curvature tolerance adds its corresponding half-logarithm. In slow-roll approximation this is the conventional requirement of roughly sixty e-folds.
Because the field increases toward ϕR​, enough expansion requires
ϕi​≲A30mpl​​−8πAmpl​​1−A2/(16π)60​​.
(33)
This is the flatness bound on the initial field in exponential inflation. It is an upper bound on the initial field, not a nonzero minimum: starting earlier at a smaller field produces more expansion. If one additionally restricts the initial potential below mpl4​, then ϕi​≥0 supplies a lower bound, but that is a separate restriction. Without initial-curvature and final-tolerance choices there is no unique numerical initial-field value.
