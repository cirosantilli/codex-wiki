A photon loses energy by 1+z=a0​/ae​ between emission and reception. The interval between successive photon arrivals is also longer by 1+z, so the received energy per unit time is reduced by (1+z)2. An isotropic wavefront at reception has area 4π(a0​r)2: the astronomical radial coordinate is an areal coordinate, even in curved space. Hence
F=4π(a0​r)2(1+z)2L​,dL​≡4πFL​​=a0​r(1+z).​
(1)
This defines the luminosity distance. Photon absorption or conversion would require a further transmission factor; the relation assumes freely propagating conserved photons and bolometric luminosity. The radial cosmological proper distance is generally different from the areal distance a0​r.
