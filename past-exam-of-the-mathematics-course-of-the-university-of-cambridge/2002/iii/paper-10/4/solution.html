Past exam of the mathematics course of the University of Cambridge / 2002 / iii / Paper 10 / 4 / Solution

Write [A]ω for the infinite subsets of an infinite A⊆N. A Ramsey set of infinite subsets is a family E⊆[N]ω such that each infinite ground set A has an infinite subset B with
[B]ω⊆Eor[B]ω∩E=∅.
(23)
The convention asking only for one such homogeneous cone is weaker; the proof below establishes the displayed property and, in fact, the stronger completely Ramsey set property.
For a finite stem s and an infinite reservoir A lying above every element of s, put
[s,A]={s∪X:X∈[A]ω}.
(24)
These sets form the basic open neighbourhoods of the Ellentuck topology, or star topology. Here s is an initial segment of every member of [s,A]. A star-Borel set means a member of the sigma-algebra generated by these open sets. A family is completely Ramsey if every [s,A] has a homogeneous refinement [s,B] with B⊆A infinite and the same stem s. We prove this for every star-Borel set, beginning with open sets.
Fix a star-open family O and a basic neighbourhood [s,A]. An infinite reservoir T above a finite stem t accepts t if [t,T]⊆O. It rejects t if no infinite subset of T accepts t. A reservoir decides t if it accepts or rejects it. Any reservoir has a refinement deciding a prescribed stem: take an accepting refinement if there is one, and otherwise the current reservoir rejects. Both kinds of decision persist under infinite thinning.
First refine A to decide s. If the decision is acceptance, the required homogeneous neighbourhood has already been found. Suppose it is rejection. We use deciding all finite stems by fusion to select
b1​<b2​<⋯
(25)
from nested infinite reservoirs. After selecting bn​, successively thin the remaining reservoir to decide every stem s∪a with a⊆{b1​,…,bn​}. There are only finitely many such a, so every stage can be completed. Choose bn+1​ from that thinned reservoir and repeat. Let B={b1​,b2​,…}. For every finite a⊆B, the tail
B/a={b∈B:b>maxa}
(26)
decides s∪a; for a=∅, use all of B. Indeed, the stem was decided at the stage when its largest point was selected, and its final tail is a subset of the reservoir retained at that stage. The empty stem remains rejected by the initial decision.
The crucial finite-obstruction fact is that a rejected stem has only finitely many accepting one-point extensions. More precisely, suppose B/a rejects s∪a. If infinitely many b∈B/a had B/b accepting s∪a∪{b}, collect those b into an infinite C. Any X∈[C]ω has a least point b, and its remaining tail lies in B/b. Acceptance at b would then imply s∪a∪X∈O. Thus C would accept s∪a, contradicting rejection. This proves the finitely many accepting extensions of a rejected stem assertion with the reservoirs specified.
Thin B a second time. Having selected a finite prefix of C, maintain that every stem s∪a with a a subset of that prefix is rejected relative to its tail in B. Each such stem has finitely many forbidden next points leading to acceptance. Choose the next point of C above the prefix and outside the union of these finitely many finite forbidden sets. The deciding property of B then makes every newly created stem rejected. Starting with the rejected empty extension gives an infinite C⊆B all of whose finite extensions of s are rejected.
We claim [s,C]∩O=∅. Otherwise take Y∈[s,C]∩O. Star-openness supplies a basic neighbourhood inside O containing Y. Extend its finite stem along Y until it contains s; the new stem has the form s∪a for a finite initial segment a of Y∖s. The remaining infinite tail of Y is a subset of B/a and accepts that stem, contradicting rejection. This proves the fusion proof for open Ellentuck sets: every star-open set is completely Ramsey.
Next call N completely Ramsey-null if every [s,A] has a same-stem refinement disjoint from N. Every star-nowhere dense set is completely Ramsey-null. Indeed, the complement D of its star-closure is star-open and dense. Apply the just-proved open-set result inside [s,A]. The homogeneous alternative disjoint from D would be a nonempty open set contained in the closure of a nowhere-dense set, which is impossible. Hence some [s,B] lies entirely in D and avoids N.
Countable unions of completely Ramsey-null sets are again completely Ramsey-null. Here is the full Ellentuck meagre-set fusion lemma. Given N1​,N2​,… and [s,A], construct selected prefixes Pj​={b1​,…,bj​} and nested unused infinite reservoirs. At stage j, refine the reservoir, still lying above maxPj​, to arrange
[s∪a,Tj​]∩Ni​=∅for every a⊆Pj​ and every 1≤i≤j+1.
(27)
Each constraint can be imposed by complete Ramsey-nullness, with all earlier constraints preserved by thinning; there are finitely many constraints. Choose bj+1​∈Tj​ and retain the tail above it for the next stage. Start with P0​=∅ and the reservoir A.
Let B={b1​,b2​,…}. If Y∈[s,B] and i is fixed, take any j≥i−1 and put a=(Y∖s)∩Pj​. Every remaining point of Y∖s lies in Tj​, so Y∈[s∪a,Tj​] and Y∈/Ni​. Therefore
[s,B]∩⋃i=1∞​Ni​=∅.
(28)
This construction handles all subsets of a selected prefix, not merely the full prefix: members of [s,B] may skip arbitrarily many selected points. Since a meagre set is a countable union of nowhere-dense sets, every star-meagre set is completely Ramsey-null.
It remains to pass from star-open sets to star-Borel sets. Let C consist of the families E expressible as
E△O⊆M,
(29)
where O is star-open and M is star-meagre. We verify directly that C is a sigma-algebra. It contains every open set. Countable unions remain in C, because
(⋃i​Ei​)△(⋃i​Oi​)⊆⋃i​(Ei​△Oi​),
(30)
and a countable union of meagre sets is meagre. For complements, replace Oc by the open set int(Oc). The omitted part is O∖O, the boundary of the open set O, which is closed and nowhere dense: a nonempty open subset of O must meet O, so it cannot lie in this boundary. Thus
Ec△int(Oc)⊆M∪(O∖O),
(31)
a meagre set. Subsets of meagre sets are meagre as well, since taking a subset preserves nowhere denseness. These observations prove the claimed sigma-algebra property. Hence every star-Borel set belongs to C.
Finally, for E△O⊆M and any [s,A], first use complete Ramsey-nullness to obtain [s,B] avoiding M. On that neighbourhood E and O coincide. Apply the open-set result to O within [s,B] to get a homogeneous [s,C]. Therefore
Every star-Borel family is completely Ramsey, and hence Ramsey.​
(32)
Taking s=∅ gives the requested homogeneous infinite-subset cone inside any given infinite ground set. Every closure, interior, open set and meagreness used in this proof refers to the Ellentuck topology; substituting the coarser ordinary topology at an intermediate step would change the assertion.
