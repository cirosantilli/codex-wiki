The integral is entire and invariant under u↦1−u. Hence Ξ(u)=Ξ(1−u), with only simple poles at u=0,1, of residues −1,1. This is the pole-bearing completion, not the entire xi function.
For fixed y>0, the nonconstant part of the Eisenstein expansion is entire in s and locally normally convergent: the divisor sums grow at most polynomially on a compact set of s, whereas Ks−1/2​(2π∣r∣y) decays exponentially in ∣r∣. The same argument gives local uniformity for z in compact subsets of the complex upper half-plane. Thus only the constant terms can have poles. Their apparent poles at s=1/2 cancel: Ξ(2s) has principal part 1/(2(s−1/2)), while Ξ(2s−1) has its negative, and both multiply y1/2 there. The remaining poles are simple, at s=0,1, with residues −1/2,1/2.
Under s↦1−s, the two constant terms interchange by the zeta functional equation. The nonconstant terms are unchanged because Kν​=K−ν​ and
∣r∣s−1/2σ1−2s​(∣r∣)=∣r∣1/2−sσ2s−1​(∣r∣),
(49)
which follows by replacing a divisor d by ∣r∣/d. Therefore
E(z,s) is meromorphic on C,E(z,s)=E(z,1−s),Ress=0​E=−21​,Ress=1​E=21​.​
(50)
For the Kronecker limit formula, evaluate the finite Laurent coefficient at s=1. Write γE​ for the Euler--Mascheroni constant. From ζ(u)=1/(u−1)+γE​+O(u−1) and Γ′(1/2)/Γ(1/2)=−γE​−2log2 we obtain
Ξ(2s−1)=2(s−1)1​+2γE​−log(4π)​+O(s−1).
(51)
The gamma derivative follows by differentiating the Gamma duplication formula and using Γ′(1)=−γE​. Multiplication by y1−s adds −21​logy to the constant term; the other constant term contributes Ξ(2)y=πy/6.
The half-order Modified Bessel function of the second kind is K1/2​(Y)=π/(2Y)​e−Y. For example, in its even integral representation set v/2 as variable and then u=sinh(v/2): coshv=1+2u2 and the cosh(v/2) factor converts the integral to a Gaussian. At s=1 the nonconstant Eisenstein coefficients are consequently σ−1​(∣r∣)e−2π∣r∣y. On the other hand, the absolutely convergent logarithmic series gives
log∏n≥1​(1−qn)=−∑r≥1​σ−1​(r)qr.
(52)
Adding this identity to its conjugate identifies the nonconstant sum as −2log∣∏n≥1​(1−qn)∣. With the analytic Dedekind eta function product η(z)=q1/24∏n≥1​(1−qn), the resulting answer is
E(z,s)=2(s−1)1​+2γE​−log(4π)​−log(y​∣η(z)∣2)+O(s−1).​
(53)
This derivation of the Kronecker limit formula uses the analytic eta product only; it does not assume its modular transformation. The lattice series is invariant for ℜs>1 by an integral change of the pair (m,n) and ℑ(γz)=y/∣cz+d∣2. Analytic continuation preserves this invariance, so its finite Laurent coefficient is invariant too. This supplies the independent weight-two transformation argument used in Question 2.
