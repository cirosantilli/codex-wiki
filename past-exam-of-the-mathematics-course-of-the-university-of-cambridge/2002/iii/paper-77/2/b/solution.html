The contraction principle for large deviations states that if random elements XL in a space E satisfy a large deviation principle with speed aL​→∞ and good rate function I, and f:E→F is continuous, then f(XL) satisfies a large deviation principle at the same speed with
J(y)=inf{I(x):f(x)=y},inf∅=+∞.​
(12)
For example, take E,F to be Polish spaces. The rate function J is good: its finite sublevel sets are the continuous images f({I≤r}) of compact sublevel sets. Compactness and lower semicontinuity ensure that a finite fibre infimum relevant to such a sublevel set is attained. The probability bounds follow by taking preimages of open and closed sets.
