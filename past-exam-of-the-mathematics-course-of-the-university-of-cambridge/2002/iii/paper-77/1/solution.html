Let N=4700 and let the daily distances be independent and identically distributed random variables with probabilities p1​=p3​=0.4995, p100​=0.001. The unconditioned mean daily distance is 2.098, whereas the observed trip requires a daily average near 10. Conditioning on that rare average changes the typical frequencies substantially. It does not determine an exact boost count.
Use the method of types: for a fixed finite alphabet, the probability of an empirical distribution q is
P(type q)=∏i​(Nqi​)!N!​∏i​piNqi​​=exp[−ND(q∥p)+O(logN)],
(1)
where D(q∥p)=∑i​qi​log(qi​/pi​) is relative entropy, using natural logarithms. This follows directly from Stirling's approximation. There are only polynomially many types, so the empirical distribution conditional on a mean constraint concentrates at its unique relative entropy minimizer, when admissible lattice types approximate that minimizer. Any fixed neighborhood of it has a strictly smaller minimum cost than its complement.
Minimize D(q∥p) subject to ∑i​qi​=1 and q1​+3q3​+100q100​=10. The entropy minimization under a prescribed sample mean gives the exponential tilting
qi​=Z(θ)pi​eθi​,Z(θ)=0.4995eθ+0.4995e3θ+0.001e100θ.
(2)
To verify minimization without relying only on Lagrange multipliers, any feasible distribution r satisfies
D(r∥p)=D(r∥q)+10θ−logZ(θ).
(3)
Relative entropy nonnegativity makes q the unique minimizer. Its mean determines θ through Z′(θ)/Z(θ)=10, or
0.09e100θ=0.4995(9eθ+7e3θ).
(4)
The derivative of the tilted mean is the tilted variance, which is strictly positive. The mean rises from 2.098 at θ=0 to 100 as θ→∞, so this equation has one positive solution. Numerically,
θ=0.0457316645,(q1​,q3​,q100​)=(0.4384035050, 0.4803922990, 0.0812041960).
(5)
The predicted boost count is Nq100​=381.6597211. Thus the conditional large-deviation estimate is about 382 boosts.
For exact arrival at total distance 47000, let k,m,l count days of distances 100,3,1. The constraints give
k+m+l=4700,99k+2m=42300.
(6)
Consequently k can be any even integer from 340 to 426. In particular, a unique count cannot be inferred from the return time. The exact conditional weights are proportional to
k!m!l!4700!​(0.001)k(0.4995)4700−k,m=242300−99k​,l=4700−k−m.
(7)
Their most probable count is k=382, consistent with the exponential tilting calculation. This finite-sample inference is a conditional probability statement, not an assertion that all journeys with the stated duration have that count.
If returning home means first crossing the distance threshold on day 4700, the final total instead lies between 47000 and 47099. The last increment must exceed the overshoot; for fixed counts this selects the corresponding fraction of possible last days. Near the tilted optimum that factor is bounded away from zero, so it does not alter the leading exponential type cost. The same approximate answer follows. Exact conditional enumeration of this first-arrival interpretation also has mode 382.
