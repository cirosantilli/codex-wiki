The family-history table contains 462 men, of whom 160 have the response present, giving an overall observed fraction 160/462=0.3463. The unadjusted fractions are 96/192=0.5 with a positive family history and 64/270=0.2370 without one. Their crude odds ratio is (96/96)/(64/206)=3.219; this is not the covariate-adjusted effect from the fitted logistic regression.
The full model has an intercept and nine slopes, hence 462−10=452 residual degrees of freedom. The binomial distribution dispersion is fixed at one. Although the software labels coefficient-to-standard-error ratios as t values, here they are asymptotic normal Wald statistics, not statistics with an estimated Gaussian residual variance and an exact Student reference. The full conditional coefficients provide evidence for tobacco, cholesterol, family history, behaviour score and age: their absolute Wald ratios are approximately 2.99,2.92,4.06,3.22,3.73. The other four ratios are smaller in magnitude, especially alcohol at about 0.027. These are conditional associations; a weak adjusted coefficient does not by itself establish absence of an underlying association, and multicollinearity can affect the estimates.
The null deviance is 596.1084 on 461 degrees of freedom and the full residual deviance is 472.14 on 452. Their difference 123.9684 has a nominal nine-degree-of-freedom likelihood-ratio test reference for the nine slopes jointly. The absolute residual deviance should not automatically be compared with χ4522​ for the reasons in part (i).

At the final step, every remaining single-term deletion increases AIC. This is a local stopping condition of the search, not a proof that it found the best subset among all possible models. Obesity is retained for several steps because its successive deletion costs differ after the other terms are removed; the procedure does not simply sort the initial Wald ratios.

The selected model's residual degrees of freedom are 456, consistent with its six coefficients. All five retained slopes have nominal normal-Wald p values below 0.004 in that model, but these tests and confidence intervals do not incorporate the prior model selection. Its improvement over the intercept-only fit is 596.1084−475.6856=120.4228 on five nominal degrees of freedom; it loses only 3.5456 deviance units relative to the nine-slope fit while using four fewer parameters. Selection optimism remains relevant to predictive performance. Check possible nonlinear continuous-predictor effects, influential observations and clinically motivated interactions; assess out-of-sample discrimination and agreement of predicted probabilities with observed frequencies by cross-validation or external data. The printed output alone does not supply those diagnostics, and the selected high-risk male population limits extrapolation to other populations. Nothing here establishes causal effects of these observational predictors.
