Use the index convention [ea​,ec​]=Ca​bc​eb​. For the left- and right-invariant coframes, the Maurer-Cartan equations are
dλb=−21​Ca​bc​λa∧λc,dρb=+21​Ca​bc​ρa∧ρc.​
(15)
Their dual invariant vector fields satisfy
[La​,Lc​]=Ca​bc​Lb​,[Ra​,Rc​]=−Ca​bc​Rb​,[La​,Rc​]=0.​
(16)
For example, evaluating dλb on two dual fields gives −λb([La​,Lc​]), since its invariant coefficient functions are constant. The opposite right-invariant bracket gives the other sign. The mixed bracket vanishes because left and right translations commute.
For a semisimple Lie algebra, the Killing form B(X,Y)=Tr(adXadY) is nondegenerate. It is adjoint invariant: using ad[Z,X]=[adZ,adX] and cyclicity of trace gives
B([Z,X],Y)+B(X,[Z,Y])=0.
(17)
Group conjugation preserves the trace as well, so this is invariant under the full group adjoint action. Translation supplies a bi-invariant pseudo-Riemannian metric.
Lower the indicated structure index by Cabc​=Bbe​Ca​ec​. Then
Cabc​=B(eb​,[ea​,ec​])=−B(ea​,[eb​,ec​]).
(18)
This changes sign under exchange of a,c by the bracket and under exchange of a,b by invariance. Hence Cabc​=C[abc]​.
Let η(X,Y,Z)=B(X,[Y,Z]) at the identity and extend it as a left-invariant differential form. Its value is adjoint invariant, so the extended form is also right-invariant. The three-form in the question is −η with the index-lowering convention just specified, an irrelevant overall sign for these properties. To prove closedness rather than only invariance, apply the exterior derivative on four left-invariant fields. Its six bracket terms combine by invariance into
dη(W,X,Y,Z)​=−2[B([W,X],[Y,Z])−B([W,Y],[X,Z])+B([W,Z],[X,Y])]=−2B(W,[X,[Y,Z]]+[Y,[Z,X]]+[Z,[X,Y]])=0.​
(19)
The final equality is the Jacobi identity. Therefore the Cartan three-form
3!1​Cabc​λa∧λb∧λc​
(20)
is both bi-invariant and closed.
