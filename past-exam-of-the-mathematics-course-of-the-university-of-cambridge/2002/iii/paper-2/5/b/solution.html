Frobenius reciprocity is the adjointness of character induction and restriction: for class functions f on H and u on G,
⟨fG,u⟩G​=⟨f,uH​⟩H​​.
(26)
For characters this says that the multiplicity of an irreducible G-character in an induced character equals the corresponding multiplicity after restriction. To prove the identity directly, insert the induced class function formula and interchange the two finite sums. For each x, substitute g=xhx−1 with h∈H. Since u is constant on conjugacy classes,
⟨fG,u⟩G​​=∣G∣∣H∣1​x∈G∑​h∈H∑​f(h)u(xhx−1)​=∣H∣1​h∈H∑​f(h)u(h)​=⟨f,uH​⟩H​.​
(27)
This proves the theorem for all class functions, not just irreducible characters.
For the group-theoretic conclusion, suppose that p divides ∣G′∩Z(G)∣. A central element of order p lies in every Sylow p-subgroup: adjoining it to a Sylow subgroup gives a p-subgroup, so maximality forces it into that subgroup. Hence there is a subgroup Q≤P∩G′∩Z(G) of order p. Choose a nontrivial linear character λ of Q.
Since P is abelian, λ extends to a linear character μ of P. One direct justification is to extend from an abelian subgroup A to ⟨A,x⟩ one generator at a time: if d is the least positive exponent with xd∈A, choose a complex dth root of λ(xd) as the value at x. The formula on axj is then well defined and multiplicative. Iteration yields the claimed extension to P.
The induced character μG has degree [G:P], which is prime to p. In its decomposition into irreducible characters, at least one constituent χ must have degree prime to p; otherwise the total degree would be divisible by p. Frobenius reciprocity also gives ⟨χP​,μ⟩P​>0 for this constituent. Restriction to the central subgroup Q forces
χQ​=χ(1)λ.
(28)
Indeed, the induced representation itself restricts to Q as copies of λ, because conjugation by every coset representative is trivial on Q; alternatively this follows from Schur's lemma and the positive multiplicity after restriction.
Each q∈Q consequently acts in the representation affording χ as the scalar λ(q). Taking its determinant character gives
(detχ)Q​=λχ(1).
(29)
The left side is trivial since Q≤G′ and every linear character kills G′. The right side is nontrivial since λ has order p and p∤χ(1). This contradiction proves
p∤∣G′∩Z(G)∣​.
(30)
The argument is the central commutator has no torsion from an abelian Sylow subgroup determinant obstruction.
