Suppose first that N is the Frobenius kernel. An element g∈/N fixes a point in the Frobenius action. If n∈CN​(g), then n maps that point to another point fixed by g. Since g has only one fixed point and N acts regularly, n=1. Thus
CN​(g)=1(g∈/N).
(6)
No nonidentity conjugacy class of N can be fixed by such a g. If the class of n=1 were fixed, gng−1=vnv−1 for some v∈N, so v−1g∈/N would centralize n, a contradiction. Conjugation by g consequently fixes just the identity class. By Brauer's permutation lemma it fixes just one irreducible character, necessarily 1N​.
For every nonprincipal φ∈Irr(N), its inertia group of a character is therefore IG​(φ)=N. Since N is normal, the restriction of the induced character is the sum of its conjugates, counted over G/N:
(φG)N​=∑gN∈G/N​φg.
(7)
Frobenius reciprocity and character orthogonality give
⟨φG,φG⟩G​=⟨φ,(φG)N​⟩N​=∣IG​(φ):N∣=1.
(8)
An actual character of norm one is irreducible. Every nonprincipal irreducible character of N therefore induces irreducibly to G.
