Use Q=T3​+Y and Ta​=τa​/2. For each generation r=e,μ,τ, the leptons are
Lr​=(ℓrL​νrL​​): (2,−21​),Rr​=ℓrR​: (1,−1).
(1)
Both are color singlets. The conjugate antilepton fields transform in the conjugate representations and have opposite hypercharge; they are already included by the conjugate fields in the Lagrangian. The two components of Lr​ have electric charges 0,−1, and Rr​ has charge −1. There is no right-handed neutrino representation in this field content. These assignments use the electroweak representation and hypercharge table in the same hypercharge normalization as the scalar doublet.
For a doublet of hypercharge Y, define
Dμ​=∂μ​−ig2τa​​Aaμ​−ig′YBμ​.
(2)
The SU(2) term is absent for a singlet. Under the local transformation ψ↦eiYωUψ, the gauge fields transform so that Dμ​ψ↦eiYωUDμ​ψ. Consequently the required gauge-covariant kinetic terms are
Lkin​=∑r​(iLˉr​γμDμ​Lr​+iRˉr​γμDμ​Rr​)+(Dμ​ϕ)†Dμϕ.
(3)
The fields of definite chirality are defined with PL,R​=(1∓γ5​)/2. Since Lˉ has hypercharge +1/2, the Yukawa interaction
LY​=−∑r,s​Lˉr​ϕ(Yℓ​)rs​Rs​+h.c.
(4)
is invariant: its hypercharges add to zero, and Lˉϕ contracts the two doublet indices. A bare charged-lepton mass violates these assignments. No renormalizable neutrino Yukawa term is available without a right-handed neutrino. A gauge-invariant scalar potential depending on ϕ†ϕ can select the nonzero vacuum, without altering these kinetic and Yukawa constructions.
There is an explicit local unitary gauge transformation whenever rϕ​=(∣ϕ1​∣2+∣ϕ2​∣2)1/2=0:
Uϕ​=rϕ​1​(ϕ2​ϕ1∗​​−ϕ1​ϕ2∗​​),Uϕ†​Uϕ​=I,detUϕ​=1,Uϕ​ϕ=(rϕ​0​).
(5)
Thus, with the real field ρ=2​rϕ​−v,
ϕ=2​v+ρ​(10​).​
(6)
This proves the polar coordinates for the electroweak Higgs doublet constructively. The gauge choice is local about the nonzero vacuum; it need not be nonsingular at zeros of the doublet.
The three identical-generation kinetic terms permit unitary flavor changes. A singular value decomposition gives UL†​Yℓ​UR​=diag(ye​,yμ​,yτ​) with yr​≥0. Rotate both components of each left doublet together, and the right singlets independently. In this basis,
LY​=−r∑​mr​(1+vρ​)ℓˉr​ℓr​,mr​=2​yr​v​.​
(7)
The left/right cross terms combine into this Dirac mass. Since the neutrinos have no mass matrix, their simultaneous left rotation preserves a diagonal charged current. This is charged-lepton Yukawa matrix diagonalization; the neutrinos remain massless in the stated renormalizable model.
The scalar kinetic term gives
(Dμ​ϕ)†Dμϕ=21​(∂μ​ρ)2+8(v+ρ)2​[g2(A1μ​A1μ​+A2μ​A2μ​)+(gA3μ​−g′Bμ​)2].
(8)
Introduce sW​=g′/g2+g′2​ and cW​=g/g2+g′2​. The combinations W+=(A1​−iA2​)/2​, W−=(W+)∗, Z=cW​A3​−sW​B and Aγ​=sW​A3​+cW​B therefore diagonalize the quadratic terms:
Lmass​=MW2​Wμ+​W−μ+21​MZ2​Zμ​Zμ,MW​=2gv​,MZ​=2vg2+g′2​​=cW​MW​​,Mγ​=0.​
(9)
The factor 1/2 applies to the real neutral field, not to the complex charged field. The massless photon corresponds to the unbroken generator T3​+Y, which annihilates the vacuum. This gives the electroweak doublet gauge-boson mass matrix and its diagonal spectrum.

Take metric gμν​=diag(1,−1,−1,−1) and write M=MW​. The outgoing electron and antineutrino momenta are k and k′. Up to an irrelevant overall phase, the amplitude is
M=22​g​ϵμ​uˉ(k)γμ(1−γ5​)v(k′).
(10)
A fermion spin sum gives
∑final​∣M∣2=8g2​ϵμ​ϵν∗​Tμν,Tμν=Tr[kγμ(1−γ5​)k′γν(1−γ5​)].
(11)
Anticommuting γ5​ through the massless momentum and gamma matrix, and using (1−γ5​)2=2(1−γ5​), reduces the trace to twice the four-gamma trace minus twice its γ5​ counterpart. In particular its symmetric part is
TSμν​=8(kμk′ν+kνk′μ−gμνk⋅k′).
(12)
The epsilon-tensor part is antisymmetric and contributes neither to the unpolarized polarization sum nor to the real linear polarization used below. There is no extra factor for selecting the neutrino helicity: the chiral vertex has already done so.
The external massless equations imply pμ​Jμ=0 for p=k+k′, so pμ​Tμν=0. With k⋅k′=M2/2, averaging over the three initial spin states gives
∣M∣2​=24g2​(−gμν​+M2pμ​pν​​)Tμν=3g2M2​​.
(13)
Indeed −gμν​TSμν​=16k⋅k′=8M2. This is a massive-vector spin average, not an average over the final spins.
In the rest frame, eliminating the momentum delta function and integrating the remaining radial delta function δ(M−2∣k∣) reduces the two-body Lorentz-invariant phase space to dΦ2​=dΩ/(32π2). Therefore
dΩdΓ​=64π2M∣M∣2​,Γunpol​=48πg2M​​.
(14)
This derives the massless leptonic W decay width without invoking a precomputed decay formula.
For the specified polarization there is no initial spin average. Put k=(M/2)n, k′=−(M/2)n and nz​=cosθ. The contraction selects
TS33​=8(−2M2​cos2θ+2M2​)=4M2sin2θ.
(15)
It follows that
final∑​∣Mz​∣2=2g2M2​sin2θ,dΩdΓz​​=128π2g2M​sin2θ,Γz​=48πg2M​.​
(16)
Here ∫sin2θdΩ=8π/3. The total rate agrees with the spin-averaged result, but its normalized polar distribution is (1/Γz​)dΓz​/dcosθ=3(1−cos2θ)/4 instead of the isotropic unpolarized distribution. This is polarized massless leptonic W decay.

Let tA be Hermitian color generators with Tr(tAtB)=δAB/2, and take Dμ​=∂μ​−igs​GμA​tA. In explicit spinor, color and flavor indices the massless kinetic term is
Lq​=i∑α,β,i,j,f​qˉ​αif​(γμ)αβ​[δij​∂μ​−igs​GμA​(tA)ij​]qβjf​.
(17)
The full Quantum chromodynamics Lagrangian also has −GμνA​GAμν/4. A local color gauge transformation acts as
qαif​(x)↦Vij​(x)qαjf​(x),qˉ​αif​↦qˉ​αjf​(V†)ji​,Gμ​↦VGμ​V†−gs​i​(∂μ​V)V†.
(18)
Thus Dμ​q↦VDμ​q and the kinetic term is gauge-invariant.
Since the gauge coupling is independent of flavor and chirality, the classical internal global transformations are qL​↦UL​qL​, qR​↦UR​qR​, with constant flavor matrices UL​,UR​∈U(2). Equivalently these consist of SU(2)L​×SU(2)R​, a common vector phase, and a singlet axial phase q↦eiηγ5​q, modulo shared discrete factors. The vector phase measures quark number, or baryon number after dividing its generator by three. The nonsinglet axial rotations may be written q↦e−iθa​γ5​τa​/2q. At the quantum level the singlet axial phase is anomalous; the exact continuous internal symmetry is SU(2)L​×SU(2)R​×U(1)V​ up to those discrete identifications. The usual spacetime Poincaré transformations also leave the theory invariant; the massless classical theory has dilation symmetry, which is broken by quantum running. These distinctions are massless two-flavor QCD symmetries.
At equal time the canonical relations, including all indices, are
{qαif​(t,x),qβjg†​(t,y)}=δαβ​δij​δfg​δ(3)(x−y),{q,q}={q†,q†}=0.​
(19)
No constraint removes any of the four spinor components of the Dirac field in these equal-time relations.
To evaluate the axial charges, first prove the elementary bilinear identity. For any constant matrix K on the spinor/flavor space,
[∫qI†​KIJ​qJ​d3y,qK​(x)]=−KKJ​qJ​(x),[∫q†Kqd3y,qK†​(x)]=qI†​(x)KIK​.
(20)
For example [qI†​qJ​,qK​]=qI†​{qJ​,qK​}−{qI†​,qK​}qJ​; the spatial delta function collapses the integral. This proves the charge action directly from the canonical anticommutation relations.
For Ka​=γ5​τa​/2, anticommutation of γ0 and γ5​ then gives
[Q5,a​,q]=−Ka​q,[Q5,a​,qˉ​]=q†Ka​γ0=−qˉ​Ka​.
(21)
The charge is an even operator, so its commutator obeys the ordinary product rule. Consequently
[Q5,a​,S]=−qˉ​(Ka​+Ka​)q=−qˉ​γ5​τa​q=iPa​​.
(22)
Similarly, using γ52​=1 and the Pauli matrices identity {τa​,τb​}=2δab​I,
[Q5,a​,Pb​]=−2i​qˉ​(τa​τb​+τb​τa​)q=−iδab​S​.
(23)
Composite fields can be defined with a common symmetry-preserving regulator; these nonsinglet identities have no color axial anomaly. This is the nonsinglet axial charge algebra of quark densities.
If every conserved axial charge left the vacuum invariant, the vacuum expectation of each such commutator would vanish. A nonzero quark chiral condensate instead makes ⟨[Q5,a​,Pb​]⟩=−iδab​⟨S⟩=0 for a=b. It therefore diagnoses spontaneous breaking of the nonsinglet chiral symmetry. In the massless two-flavor limit, the observed hadronic pattern is described by SU(2)L​×SU(2)R​→SU(2)V​, with three Goldstone boson modes identified with the pions. The vacuum condensate is a dynamical property of QCD, not something derivable from the canonical algebra alone. The light pion multiplet and absence of parity-degenerate light hadrons support this pattern; the singlet axial anomaly prevents interpreting an additional singlet mode as a fourth Goldstone boson.
For equal nonzero masses, add Lm​=−mqˉ​q. It is invariant under local color gauge transformations and common vector flavor rotations, but not under independent left/right rotations. The exact continuous flavor symmetry becomes SU(2)V​×U(1)V​. The kinetic term and canonical anticommutators are unchanged, and the two proved equal-time commutators still hold. The charges, however, are no longer conserved:
∂μ​Aaμ​=mPa​,Aaμ​=21​qˉ​γμγ5​τa​q,Q˙​5,a​=m∫Pa​d3x.​
(24)
The first equation follows from the massive Dirac equations, or the last from Hm​=m∫S and Q˙​=i[H,Q]. This is the flavor-nonsinglet axial Ward identity with equal quark masses. The pions become pseudo-Goldstone bosons with mπ2​=O(m), rather than exact massless modes. To leading order, in the convention S=uˉu+dˉd, fπ2​mπ2​=−m⟨S⟩+O(m2). The condensate persists for small masses, but the symmetry is now explicitly broken, so the exact-symmetry Goldstone argument is replaced by an approximate one. The singlet anomaly and gauge symmetry are unaffected.

a OurBigBook.com
A loop integral in a theory with dimensionless classical couplings can contain a logarithmic ultraviolet divergence. After subtraction, an expression such as log(−p2/μ2) remains; one must specify the momentum scale at which the measured or renormalized coupling is fixed. A logarithm of a dimensionful momentum alone is not meaningful. In dimensional regularization, the same necessity appears through λbare​=μdλ​Zλ​λ(μ) in the continued dimension, with dλ​ tending to zero in the physical dimension.
Thus the renormalization prescription introduces an arbitrary renormalization scale, even when no mass or length occurs in the classical action. Physical quantities cannot depend on its arbitrary choice: explicit logarithms cancel the implicit change of the running coupling, governed by μdλ/dμ=β(λ). A nonzero renormalization-group beta function is a quantum scale anomaly. An integration constant may encode the same data as a dimensionful scale, which is dimensional transmutation. The scale labels a renormalization condition, not a new arbitrary measurable parameter. At a true fixed point the coupling need not run; the use of a subtraction scale is then a convention rather than an intrinsic generated physical scale.

Set
y=αλ​4π​=λ2(4π)2​,t=logμ2.
(25)
The chain rule gives
dtdy​=−(4π)2λ−3β(λ)=β0​+yβ1​​+O(y−2).
(26)
For the ultraviolet expansion take β0​>0 and L=log(μ2/Λ2)≫1. The leading solution is y∼β0​L. Substituting it in the next term gives dy/dL=β0​+β1​/(β0​L)+O(logL/L2), whose integration yields
αλ​4π​=β0​L+β0​β1​​logL+O(LlogL​).​
(27)
Choosing Λ removes the additive constant. This proves the two displayed leading terms of the two-loop asymptotic running coupling.
The printed remainder is too small in general. This can be checked even for an exactly two-loop renormalization-group beta function, so it does not depend on unknown higher-loop effects. Put c=β1​/β0​. Separating variables and fixing the integration constant gives
y−clog(β0​y+c​)=β0​L.
(28)
Expanding this identity one order further gives
y=β0​L+clogL+β0​c2​LlogL+1​+O(L2(logL)2​).
(29)
The logL/L term cannot be absorbed into a fixed change of Λ. For example, with β0​=β1​=1, the exact implicit relation is y−log(y+1)=L, and the next term is (logL+1)/L, which is not O(1/L). The unspecified O(y−2) contribution to the differential equation changes the constant coefficient of 1/L, while the coefficient β12​/β03​ of logL/L is already fixed. Only special cases such as β1​=0 avoid this particular obstruction.
The asymptotic formula also presupposes an asymptotically free ultraviolet regime. If β0​=0, division by it is invalid and the leading scaling changes. If β0​<0, the weak-coupling asymptotic direction is generally toward the infrared and the logarithmic variable must be chosen accordingly. These qualifications distinguish the valid perturbative solution from an unconditional formula.

Use the canonically normalized Abelian coupling g1​=−5/3​g′, with g2​=g and g3​=gs​, and write αi​=gi2​/(4π). The sign in g1​ is a generator convention; its square is what enters running and matching. Let α5​=g52​/(4π) and LX​=log(MX2​/μ2). To leading order,
dlogμ2d​αi​1​=4πbi​​,αi​(μ)1​=α5​1​−4πbi​​LX​.
(30)
Use exactly the common-flavor coefficients stipulated for this model:
b3​=11−32nf​​,b2​=322​−32nf​​,b1​=−32nf​​.
(31)
The same nf​ cancels from their differences. In particular b3​−b2​=11/3 and b2​−b1​=22/3, so eliminating the unified coupling gives
α2​1​−αs​1​=12π11​LX​,α1​1​−α2​1​=12π22​LX​=2(α2​1​−αs​1​).
(32)
At the low reference scale, e=gsinθW​=g′cosθW​. With w=sin2θW​ this means
α2−1​=αw​,α1−1​=5α3(1−w)​.
(33)
The last difference equation becomes 3(1−w)/(5α)−w/α=2(w/α−1/αs​). Solving gives
sin2θW​=61​+9αs​5α​.​
(34)
Substitute this into the first difference equation to obtain
logμMX​​=11π​(α1​−3αs​8​),MX​=μexp[11π​(α1​−3αs​8​)].​
(35)
All low-scale couplings on the right must be evaluated at the same specified μ. A dimensionful MX​ cannot be fixed by dimensionless coupling values without such a reference scale. The result is independent of the common nf​ in these differences, although α5​ is not. An inferred MX​>μ requires α−1>8/(3αs​), and perturbative matching requires a positive small α5​.
This is the one-loop SU(5) prediction with common flavor coefficients. It is the prediction of the supplied simplified coefficient model, not the full matter-and-Higgs calculation for a realistic SU(5) grand unified theory. Actual representations, intermediate thresholds and higher-loop terms change the running and require the corresponding matching calculation.
