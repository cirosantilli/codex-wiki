Write t:1↪Ω for truth. Let g:U↪Ω be the subobject classified by the monomorphism f. Its classifier pullback gives fg=t!U​. The left side is monic, so !U​:U→1 is monic as well: U is subterminal.
Let i:V↪U be classified by g. The pullback square for i says that V→1 is also the pullback of g along t. Since g is classified by f, the composite ft:1→Ω therefore classifies V↪1. Pulling this subobject back along !U​ gives i:V↪U: for subterminal objects with V⊆U, U×V≅V. Uniqueness of characteristic maps now gives ft!U​=g. Combining with fg=t!U​ proves
f2g=ft!U​=g.​

To deduce the full identity, let u:1→Ω classify U↪1. The pullback of g along f is t!U​:U↪Ω. Indeed ft!U​=g, and if fa=gb, monicity of f forces a=t!U​b, giving the universal property. The classifier of that pullback is f2. But the subobject t!U​ imposes both truth of the input and the subterminal condition u, so its characteristic map is p↦u∧p. The meet operation here is defined using only finite limits and the classifier. Thus f2=u∧(−). In particular f2t=u=f2u, by the unit and idempotence laws for meet. Since f2 is monic, t=u, and hence
f2=1Ω​.​
(15)
This proves the monic endomorphism of a subobject classifier property under exactly the stated finite-limit assumptions; no Boolean logic was used.
For the epic counterexample, use the covariant functor topos [N,Set], with one arrow n→m when n≤m. A classifier value at stage n is an upward-closed set of future stages, so it is a threshold k≥n, or ∞ for the empty set. The transition from n to n+1 sends k to max(k,n+1) and fixes ∞. Define
Fn​(k)=max(n,k−1),Fn​(∞)=∞.
(16)
For the transition to n+1, both composites send finite k to max(n+1,k−1), so the maps are natural. Each component is surjective: threshold k is the image of k+1, and ∞ is its own image. Epimorphisms in a functor topos are objectwise surjective, so F is epic. But Fn​(n)=Fn​(n+1)=n, making it noninjective and not invertible. Thus the classifier has an epic endomorphism which is not an isomorphism, the shift epimorphism of the natural-number presheaf classifier.
