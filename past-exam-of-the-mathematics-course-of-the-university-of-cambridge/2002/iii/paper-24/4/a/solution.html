Work near the identity O of a generalized Weierstrass equation of an elliptic curve. On the projective chart where Y=0, use the local parameter t=−x/y and the auxiliary coordinate w=−1/y. Substitution gives
w=t3+a1​tw+a2​t2w+a3​w2+a4​tw2+a6​w3.
(22)
The coefficient of w in the linearized equation at (0,0) is one. Consequently, successive coefficient comparison determines a unique formal power series w(t), without dividing by any coefficient of the equation. Its initial terms are
w(t)=t3+a1​t4+(a12​+a2​)t5+(a13​+2a1​a2​+a3​)t6+O(t7).
(23)
Recover the affine coordinates as Laurent series x=t/w(t) and y=−1/w(t); in particular x=t−2+O(t−1) and y=−t−3+O(t−2).
The elliptic curve group law is a regular morphism near (O,O). Applying the local parameter to the sum and passing to completed local rings produces
F(T1​,T2​)=t(P+Q)∈k[[T1​,T2​]].
(24)
One can construct the series without any nonunit division. Put
λ=T2​−T1​w(T2​)−w(T1​)​,ν=w(T1​)−λT1​.
(25)
The divided difference is an integral formal power series, including its tangent line specialization at T1​=T2​. The line w=λt+ν meets the cubic in the two chosen points and a third point R. Substitution in the equation shows that its cubic and quadratic coefficients in t are −A,−B, where
A=1+a2​λ+a4​λ2+a6​λ3,B=a1​λ+a2​ν+a3​λ2+2a4​λν+3a6​λ2ν.
(26)
Since λ has total degree at least two and ν at least three, A is a unit. The sum of the roots therefore gives
tR​=−T1​−T2​−B/A,wR​=λtR​+ν,F(T1​,T2​)=−1−a1​tR​−a3​wR​tR​​.
(27)
The last expression reflects the third intersection according to the chord-and-tangent group law. Both denominators have constant term one. This proves integrality of the universal construction and also provides an explicit coefficient algorithm. Expanding it, the resulting formal group of an elliptic curve begins
F(T1​,T2​)=T1​+T2​−a1​T1​T2​−a2​(T12​T2​+T1​T22​)+O((T1​,T2​)4).​
(28)
The integral coefficient recursion and the unit-denominator addition calculation work universally over Z[a1​,a2​,a3​,a4​,a6​]; thus these coefficients specialize in every characteristic of a field. This is the initial coefficients of the elliptic formal group at infinity construction, rather than a definition requiring division by the elliptic-curve discriminant.
The identity, commutativity and associativity of the elliptic curve group law give the defining identities of a one-dimensional commutative formal group law:
F(T,0)=T,F(X,Y)=F(Y,X),F(F(X,Y),Z)=F(X,F(Y,Z)).
(29)
Reflection in the generalized Weierstrass equation of an elliptic curve gives its formal inverse explicitly:
ι(T)=−1−a1​T−a3​w(T)T​.
(30)
Successive addition gives the multiplication series [n]F​(T)=nT+O(T2).
In characteristic zero, an especially useful description comes from the invariant differential on an elliptic curve:
ω=2y+a1​x+a3​dx​=(1+a1​t+(a12​+a2​)t2+O(t3))dt.
(31)
Integrating with zero constant term constructs the formal logarithm
L(t)=t+2a1​​t2+3a12​+a2​​t3+O(t4).
(32)
Translation invariance of the invariant differential on an elliptic curve implies L(F(X,Y))=L(X)+L(Y). Thus the formal logarithm converts the formal group law into the formal additive group in characteristic zero. Expanding this identity to degree three also recovers the displayed coefficients of F; in positive characteristic the integral addition construction remains valid although the logarithmic denominators may not exist.
