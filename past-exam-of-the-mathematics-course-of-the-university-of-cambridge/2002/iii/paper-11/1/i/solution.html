Use the pair-design convention: each pair of distinct points must lie in exactly one Steiner block of size k. With this convention the printed endpoint k=1 is false for every v≥2, since a singleton contains no pair. Thus the intended assertion requires k≥2. For k=2, take all pairs as Steiner blocks for any v≥2. We now give a finite-field construction for every fixed k≥3, without assuming the desired design-existence theorem.
Put m=(2k​) and take an odd prime power q≡1(mod2m). By cyclicity of Fq×​, it has a subgroup H of index m; its order (q−1)/m is even, so −1∈H. We will find a k-set A={a1​,…,ak​} such that its m unordered pair differences occupy the m distinct cyclotomic classes of a finite field modulo H. The following argument supplies that set for every sufficiently large compatible q.
First establish the required two-class intersection estimate by elementary character sums. Let χ be a multiplicative character of a finite field of order m, with χ(γ)=ζm​ for a generator γ. Extend every power χj, including the trivial character, by zero at zero. For Ci​=γiH,
1Ci​​(x)=m1​∑j=0m−1​ζm−ij​χj(x).
(1)
For a nontrivial additive character ψ of Fq​ and a nontrivial multiplicative character η, its Gauss sum of a finite-field character satisfies
∣G(η)∣2=∑t=0​η(t)∑y=0​ψ((t−1)y)=(q−1)−∑t=0,1​η(t)=q.
(2)
Here the inner sum equals q−1 for t=1 and −1 otherwise, by additive-character orthogonality; multiplicative-character orthogonality gives the last equality. Such a nontrivial additive character exists by identifying the additive group with a vector space over its prime field and choosing a nonzero linear functional into that prime field.
If η,ξ,ηξ are nontrivial, substituting x=uz, y=(1−u)z in the product of their Gauss sums proves
G(η)G(ξ)=J(η,ξ)G(ηξ),J(η,ξ)=∑u​η(u)ξ(1−u).
(3)
The terms with x+y=0 sum to zero because ηξ is nontrivial. Hence ∣J∣=q​. If exactly one character is trivial, subtracting the excluded zero and one terms gives absolute value one. If both are nontrivial but their product is trivial, the substitution u/(1−u) gives J(η,η−1)=−η(−1), again of absolute value one. If both are trivial, the sum is q−2. Expanding the two cyclotomic indicators and scaling a nonzero translate to one therefore gives, uniformly in i,j and t=0,
∣Ci​∩(t+Cj​)∣=m2q​+Om​(q​).
(4)
All constants depend only on the fixed m.
To extend a prescribed difference pattern on r coordinates, fix desired classes D1​,…,Dr​ for the new differences x−ai​ and set
M(a)=#{x:x−ai​∈Di​ for all i},a∈Fqr​.
(5)
Average over all qr tuples, allowing repeated coordinates just for this average. With f=(q−1)/m, counting choices for each ai​ independently gives
EM=q(f/q)r=q/mr+Om,r​(1).
(6)
For the second moment, sum over the two potential extensions x,y. The x=y terms contribute qfr/qr. For x=y, the choices of ai​ number q/m2+Om​(q​) each, by the preceding intersection estimate. Thus
EM2=q2/m2r+Om,r​(q3/2),VarM=Om,r​(q3/2).
(7)
The Chebyshev inequality shows that the proportion of tuples with M(a)<q/(2mr) is Om,r​(q−1/2). Inductively, any fixed compatible difference pattern on r distinct coordinates is realized by at least cr​qr tuples, with cr​>0 independent of sufficiently large q. The assertion starts with r=1. Almost all tuples are outside the exceptional set, so at least half the realizing tuples have at least q/(2mr) extensions. Each extension is distinct from the old coordinates because its differences belong to nonzero classes. This proves the assertion for r+1. This cyclotomic pattern extension by a second-moment bound avoids the false assumption that every partial choice can be extended.
Prescribe a different one of the m cosets to each pair aj​−ai​, i<j. Since −1∈H, changing an orientation does not change its coset, so the prescriptions are compatible. The preceding induction supplies A.
Choose S⊂H containing one element from each pair {h,−h}. Form the Steiner blocks
B={sA+t:s∈S, t∈Fq​}.
(8)
For two distinct points x,y, their difference identifies exactly one unordered pair {ai​,aj​} of A having the same H-coset. Exactly one of (x−y)/(ai​−aj​) and its negative lies in S. Choose the orientation a,b of that pair making s=(x−y)/(a−b)∈S, and then set t=x−sa. This yields x=sa+t, y=sb+t. Both the oriented base pair and (s,t) are unique, so precisely one Steiner block contains the pair. In particular different Steiner block parameters cannot produce repeated Steiner blocks. Equivalently, the Steiner blocks sA form a difference family for a Steiner 2-design, and translation gives the cyclotomic construction of a Steiner 2-design.
There are infinitely many suitable field orders: choose one odd prime ℓ not dividing 2m, and take q=ℓφ(2m)u for integers u≥1. Euler's congruence gives q≡1(mod2m), and these orders become arbitrarily large. Consequently
For every k≥2, there are 2-(v,k,1) designs for infinitely many v.​
(9)
The qualification at k=1 is necessary for the standard pair-design definition.
