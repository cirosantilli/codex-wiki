For fixed μ, differentiation in σ2 gives the maximizer [SXX​+n(Xˉ−μ)2]/n. Thus the unrestricted fit has σ2=SXX​/n, whereas under the null hypothesis μ=μ0​ the fitted variance is [SXX​+n(Xˉ−μ0​)2]/n. Both maximize rather than minimize the likelihood: it tends to zero at either variance endpoint when the residual sum of squares is positive.

The generalized likelihood-ratio test rejects for small Λ, equivalently large ∣T∣. Under the null, Z=n​(Xˉ−μ0​)/σ is standard normal, U=SXX​/σ2 has a chi-squared distribution with n−1 degrees of freedom, and they are independent. To see the independence, apply an orthogonal matrix to the standardized normal sample, taking its first row to be (1,…,1)/n​: the transformed coordinates remain independent standard normal variables, and U is the sum of squares of the other n−1 coordinates. Hence T=Z/U/(n−1)​ has Student's t-distribution with n−1 degrees of freedom.
