The strict distance inequality implies d(fx,fy)≤d(x,y) for all points, so f is Lipschitz continuous. Hence
g(x)=d(fx,x)
(24)
is a continuous function, since ∣g(x)−g(y)∣≤d(fx,fy)+d(x,y)≤2d(x,y). On the nonempty compact metric space, g attains its minimum at some p.
If fp=p, the strict distance inequality applied to fp,p gives
g(fp)=d(f2p,fp)<d(fp,p)=g(p),
(25)
a contradiction. Thus fp=p. If p,q were different fixed points, then d(p,q)=d(fp,fq)<d(p,q), again impossible. There is exactly one fixed point. This proves strict distance decrease on a compact metric space without assuming a uniform contraction constant.
