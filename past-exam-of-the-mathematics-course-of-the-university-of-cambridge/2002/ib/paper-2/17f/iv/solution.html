Proceed by induction on dimV. The zero-dimensional case has the empty orthonormal basis. In positive dimension, the fundamental theorem of algebra gives a root of the characteristic polynomial, hence a unit eigenvector e of α, say with eigenvalue λ. Part (ii) makes it an eigenvector of α∗ as well. Thus the line spanned by e and its orthogonal complement are invariant under both operators by the introductory adjoint argument.
On {e}⊥, the restriction of α∗ is the adjoint of the restriction of α, and the restrictions still commute. The restricted linear operator is therefore normal. By induction it has an orthonormal basis of eigenvectors. Adjoining e proves an orthonormal eigenbasis exists for V, establishing the finite-dimensional spectral theorem by construction.

The real nonnegative diagonal entries make β a Hermitian operator, and the modulus-one diagonal entries make γ a unitary operator. Both are diagonal in the same orthonormal basis, so they commute and βγej​=λj​ej​ even when λj​=0. This proves the required factorization without assuming invertibility.
Conversely, if α=βγ with β∗=β, γ∗γ=γγ∗=I, and βγ=γβ, then α∗=γ∗β and
αα∗=β2,α∗α=γ∗β2γ=β2.

Thus α is normal. Normality is equivalent to a commuting Hermitian–unitary factorization, with the Hermitian factor even selectable nonnegative. This is the polar decomposition with a unitary extension on the kernel.
