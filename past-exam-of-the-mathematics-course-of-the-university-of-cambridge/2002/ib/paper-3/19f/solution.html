OurBigBook.com Words: 455
Use the standard reduced-form convention ∣b∣≤a≤c, with b≥0 when ∣b∣=a or a=c. To reduce a positive definite integral binary quadratic form, choose a primitive integer vector at which the positive integer value is least. Complete that vector to a unimodular basis; it makes the leading coefficient a that least value. A shear of the second basis vector by an integer multiple of the first replaces b by b+2ka, allowing ∣b∣≤a. The new c is the value at another primitive vector, hence c≥a. If b=−a, a shear makes b=a without changing a,c; if a=c and b<0, the determinant-one swap (x,y)↦(−y,x) reverses its sign. Thus every form is properly equivalent to a reduced positive definite binary quadratic form. This argument also gives the reduction algorithm for a positive definite binary quadratic form by successively decreasing its positive leading coefficient.
For a negative integral discriminant d≡0 or 1(mod4), a prime p is represented by some integral form of discriminant d exactly when
b2≡d(mod4p) is soluble​.
(74)
Indeed a representing vector is primitive, since a common divisor of its coordinates would have its square divide p. A unimodular change takes it to the first basis vector, giving a form [p,b,c] and d=b2−4pc. Conversely a solution b gives the positive definite form [p,b,(b2−d)/(4p)], which represents p at (1,0). For odd p this is the discriminant criterion for prime representation by a binary quadratic form: d must be a square modulo p, with parity chosen to satisfy the modulo-four condition.
For d=−32 and odd p, the Legendre symbol is (−32/p)=(−2/p)=(−1/p)(2/p). The supplementary laws make it positive exactly for p≡1,3(mod8). The prime 2 also works directly, since [2,0,4] has discriminant −32 and represents 2. Therefore
p is represented by some form of discriminant −32⟺p≡1,2,3(mod8)​.
(75)
The class 2(mod8) here consists only of the prime 2.
For a reduced form, 32=4ac−b2≥3a2, so a≤3. The integer checks yield the reduced forms of discriminant minus thirty-two:
[1,0,8],[2,0,4],[3,2,3]​.
(76)
If one omits the boundary sign convention, [3,−2,3] also appears, but is properly equivalent to [3,2,3]. The middle form is imprimitive and represents no odd integer. An odd integer represented by x2+8y2 has x odd and is 1(mod8). For 3x2+2xy+3y2 to be odd, one of x,y must be odd and the other even; substitution modulo eight gives 3(mod8) in either case. If both are odd or both even, the value is even. Thus a prime represented by the last form is necessarily 3(mod8).
Conversely, a prime p≡3(mod8) is represented by some discriminant-−32 form, which reduces to one of the three displayed forms without changing its represented integers. The first and second cannot represent it, so the third must. Consequently
p=3x2+2xy+3y2 for integers x,y⟺p≡3(mod8)​.
(77)
The prime 2 is not an exception for this particular form: its minimum positive value is 3, as also follows from 3x2+2xy+3y2=2(x2+y2)+(x+y)2.
