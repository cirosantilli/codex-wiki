The function ϕ(t)=t/(1+t) increases for t≥0, and
ϕ(b)+ϕ(c)=1+bb​+1+cc​≥1+b+cb​+1+b+cc​=ϕ(b+c).
(31)
Thus a≤b+c implies ϕ(a)≤ϕ(b)+ϕ(c)​. Applying this to the triangle inequality for a metric proves the triangle inequality for the bounded metric transform db​=ϕ∘d. Symmetry and nonnegativity are immediate, and db​(x,y)=0 exactly when d(x,y)=0, so db​ is a metric​.
Each Kn​ is compact and lies inside D, so the supremum norm ∥f−g∥n​ is finite for continuous f,g. Every summand of σ lies between zero and 2−n, giving absolute convergence and 0≤σ≤1/2. Symmetry is inherited from these norms. If σ(f,g)=0, all the nonnegative summands vanish, hence f=g on every Kn​; their union is D, so f=g everywhere. Finally
∥f−h∥n​≤∥f−g∥n​+∥g−h∥n​
(32)
and the subadditivity already proved give the triangle inequality term by term and then after summation. Therefore σ is a metric on F​. This is a weighted metric for local uniform convergence, representing the compact-open topology.
The geometric-series sum is s(z)=1/(1−z) and its remainder is s−sk​=zk+1/(1−z). Writing rn​=1−1/n, the triangle inequality gives
​1−zzk+1​​≤1−rn​rnk+1​​=nrnk+1​(∣z∣≤rn​).
(33)
Equality occurs at the positive real point z=rn​. Thus
∥sk​−s∥n​=n(1−1/n)k+1​.
(34)
For every integer N≥2, split the defining sum into a finite head and its tail. Since ϕ(t)≤t and ϕ(t)≤1,
σ(sk​,s)≤n=2∑N​∥sk​−s∥n​+n>N∑​2−n=n=2∑N​∥sk​−s∥n​+2−N​.
(35)
Given ε>0, choose N with 2−N<ε/2. For that fixed N, each of the finitely many displayed norms tends to zero, so their sum is below ε/2 for sufficiently large k. Hence σ(sk​,s)→0​, even though convergence is not uniform on the whole open disk.
