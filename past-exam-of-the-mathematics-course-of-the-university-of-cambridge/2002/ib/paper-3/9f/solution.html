A nonzero residue class a(modp) is a quadratic residue if a≡x2(modp) for some nonzero x. If it is a square, Fermat's little theorem gives a(p−1)/2=xp−1≡1. Conversely, the map x↦x2 on the nonzero classes identifies exactly the pairs {x,−x}, giving (p−1)/2 distinct squares. They are all roots of X(p−1)/2−1 over the field Fp​; a polynomial of this degree has at most that many roots. Its roots are therefore precisely the squares. This proves Euler's criterion
a is a nonzero quadratic residue ⟺a(p−1)/2≡1(modp)​.
(25)
Applying it to −1 gives (−1/p)=1⟺p≡1(mod4)​. The exclusion of a=0 is the usual nonzero-residue convention needed for this equivalence.
For distinct odd primes p,q, quadratic reciprocity states
(qp​)(pq​)=(−1)(p−1)(q−1)/4​.
(26)
Using the multiplicativity of the Legendre symbol, and the supplementary law (2/p)=(−1)(p2−1)/8,
(12773​)=(73127​)=(7354​)=(732​)(733​).
(27)
