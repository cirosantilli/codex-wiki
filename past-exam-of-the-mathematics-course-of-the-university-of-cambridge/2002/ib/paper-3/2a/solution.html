Let the mass per unit length be m and the constant tension be T. Small transverse displacement obeys the wave equation
mytt​=Tyxx​,ytt​=c2yxx​,c=T/m​​.
(4)
With characteristic coordinates ξ=x+ct and η=x−ct, the equation becomes yξη​=0. Integrating once in each variable gives D'Alembert's formula
y(x,t)=f(x+ct)+g(x−ct)​.
(5)
For a smooth solution with vanishing endpoint energy flux, multiplication by yt​ and integration by parts give
dtdE​=∫0∞​yt​(ytt​−c2yxx​)dx+[c2yt​yx​]0∞​=0.
(6)
The fixed endpoint has yt​(0,t)=0. Decay of displacement alone does not guarantee either a pointwise derivative-flux limit or finiteness of the energy integral; the conservation statement is understood in the usual finite-energy class. The profile formula below proves it directly there without requiring such a pointwise limit at infinity.
The endpoint condition gives g(−ct)=−f(ct), allowing the reflected wave to be expressed through one profile:
y(x,t)=f(x+ct)−f(ct−x).
(7)
For forward time one may define the negative-argument part of this profile using the original g; no restriction on the independent initial waves is lost. Differentiation gives yt​=c[f′(x+ct)−f′(ct−x)] and yx​=f′(x+ct)+f′(ct−x). Hence
E=c2∫0∞​[f′(x+ct)2+f′(ct−x)2]dx=c2∫−∞∞​f′(s)2ds​.
(8)
The two substitutions partition the real line at ct, making conservation of energy explicit.
