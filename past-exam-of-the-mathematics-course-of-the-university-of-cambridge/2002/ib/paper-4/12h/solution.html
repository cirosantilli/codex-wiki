A prior distribution assigns uncertainty to a parameter before observing data. Multiplying its density by the likelihood and normalizing gives the posterior distribution. A Bayes estimator minimizes the posterior expected loss function for each observed sample, and therefore minimizes integrated Bayes risk when the expectations exist.
For quadratic loss (d−θ)2, the conditional risk is Var(θ∣x)+(d−E(θ∣x))2, so its minimizer is the posterior mean. For absolute loss ∣d−θ∣, the one-sided derivatives or subgradients show that a minimizer is any posterior median, characterized by P(θ<d∣x)≤1/2 and P(θ>d∣x)≤1/2.
