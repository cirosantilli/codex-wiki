By equivalent norms, choose c,C>0 with c∥h∥1​≤∥h∥2​≤C∥h∥1​. Differentiability in the first norm means that there is a bounded linear map L:V→R with f(a+h)−f(a)−Lh=r(h) and ∣r(h)∣/∥h∥1​→0. The same L is bounded in the second norm, since ∣Lh∣≤K∥h∥1​≤Kc−1∥h∥2​. Moreover,
∥h∥2​∣r(h)∣​≤c1​∥h∥1​∣r(h)∣​⟶0.
(14)
The two norms define the same approach h→0, so this proves differentiability with the same Fréchet derivative in the second norm. Reversing the norms proves the converse.
For completeness, the derivative is unique: if L1​,L2​ both work, fix v and take h=tv. The two remainder estimates imply ∣(L1​−L2​)v∣=o(1) as t→0, so (L1​−L2​)v=0 for every v. Thus differentiability and its derivative are independent of the chosen norm. This is differentiability under equivalent norms, not merely invariance of continuity.
