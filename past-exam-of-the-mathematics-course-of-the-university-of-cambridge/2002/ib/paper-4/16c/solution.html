Use the standard constant-density incompressible flow model, with conservative body force. Write u=(u,v,0) independent of z, and ω=vx​−uy​. Differentiate the y-momentum Euler equations with respect to x and subtract the y-derivative of the x-momentum equation. Mixed pressure derivatives and the curl of a conservative body force cancel, giving
ωt​+uωx​+vωy​+(ux​+vy​)ω=0.
(32)
The last term vanishes by incompressibility, so the material derivative of vorticity is zero. Thus Dω/Dt=0. Incompressibility is important: inviscidness alone does not suffice; in a compressible barotropic planar flow the equation is Dω/Dt=−ω∇⋅u, conserving ω/ρ instead.
Take vortex strength κ to denote circulation, positive counterclockwise. A point vortex at x0​ generates
u(x)=2πκ​∣x−x0​∣2J(x−x0​)​,J(x,y)=(−y,x).
(33)
It has zero vorticity away from its core, with circulation κ around the core. Each vortex moves with the other's regular induced velocity, not its own singular field. With d=x1​−x2​,
x˙1​=2π∣d∣2κ2​​Jd,x˙2​=−2π∣d∣2κ1​​Jd.
(34)
Multiplication by the two strengths proves q˙​=κ1​x˙1​+κ2​x˙2​=0. Also d˙=(κ1​+κ2​)Jd/(2π∣d∣2), so d∣d∣2/dt=2d⋅d˙=0.
For equal strengths κ and the specified initial positions, q=0 and ∣d∣=2a. The motion of two point vortices is rotation with angular velocity Ωv​=κ/(4πa2):
x1​(t)=a(cosΩv​t,sinΩv​t),x2​(t)=−x1​(t).​
(35)
Negative circulation reverses the rotation. If vortex strength is defined instead as the coefficient of 1/r velocity, absorb the factor 2π consistently into its definition.
