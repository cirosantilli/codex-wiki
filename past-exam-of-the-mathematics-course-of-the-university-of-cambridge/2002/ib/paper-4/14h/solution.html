First take si​>0, the hypothesis required for the printed ratios and their optimization formula to be well-defined. Since ∑pi​=1,
∑i​si​+xi​pi​xi​​=1−∑i​si​+xi​pi​si​​.
(25)
The maximizing and minimizing problems therefore have exactly the same solutions.
Let τ>0 and minimize the separable optimization Lagrangian
L(x,τ)=∑i​si​+xi​pi​si​​+τ(∑i​xi​−b),xi​≥0.
(26)
Each summand is convex, and its derivative is −pi​si​/(si​+xi​)2+τ. Its minimizer is xi​(τ)=(pi​si​/τ​−si​)+. The sum of these coordinates is continuous in τ, decreases from infinity to zero, and is strictly decreasing wherever positive. Thus there is a unique τ for which the sum is b. For every feasible y, coordinatewise minimization gives L(y,τ)≥L(x(τ),τ); both constraint terms vanish. The Lagrangian sufficiency theorem proves global optimality, not just a necessary stationary condition.
Put ri​=pi​/si​ in decreasing order. A positive bet occurs exactly when ri​>τ. Hence the positive bets form an initial block. To interpret the maximal index in the question, one may include any subsequent indices tied at ri​=τ: their bets are zero but their marginal-return equality still holds. Take k=max{i:ri​≥τ}. Then
(s1​+x1​)2p1​s1​​=⋯=(sk​+xk​)2pk​sk​​=τ,xi​=0 (i>k).​
(27)
For this block, τ​=(∑i≤k​pi​si​​)/(b+∑i≤k​si​), with the threshold inequalities determining k. Zero-probability horses receive zero bets.
Let H be the set attaining ρ=maxri​, and write SH​=∑i∈H​si​, PH​=∑i∈H​pi​=ρSH​. For sufficiently small b, the solution uses only H, with xi​=bsi​/SH​ there and τ=ρ[SH​/(SH​+b)]2. If the next ratio is r∗​<ρ, this remains valid when b≤SH​(ρ/r∗​​−1); if all outside ratios are zero there is no finite threshold. Put S=∑i​si​. The fixed pool is S+b, so the expected gross payout and net gain are
E(payout)=(S+b)SH​+bPH​b​,E(net gain)=(S+b)SH​+bPH​b​−b.​
(28)
Tied best horses receive bets proportional to their existing stakes; betting all on one tied horse is not generally optimal for finite b. This is pari-mutuel expected-return allocation.
The literal allowance si​=0 needs qualification. At si​=xi​=0 the printed objective contains 0/0 and the ordered ratios are undefined. Even defining an unbacked horse's payout to be zero does not always yield an attained optimum. For example, take b=1, p1​=p2​=1/2, s1​=0, s2​=1. For x1​>0, the normalized expected payout is 1/2+(1/2)(1−x1​)/(2−x1​), tending to 3/4 as x1​↓0 but never attaining it; at x1​=0 that zero-payout convention gives 1/4. Thus the proved allocation formula requires positive existing stakes, or an explicitly modified market model. The zero-stake case cannot be justified by silently dividing by zero.
