Past exam of the mathematics course of the University of Cambridge / 2002 / ib / Paper 1 / 4E / Solution

OurBigBook.com Words: 271
Let G be the finite group and choose a point p. Its orbit E={gp:g∈G} is a nonempty finite set. Every group element permutes E and maps a closed metric disc onto a disc of the same radius. By the permitted uniqueness of the smallest enclosing disc, it fixes that disc and therefore its centre o. Thus G lies in the orientation-preserving stabilizer of one point.
In the Euclidean plane, translate o to zero. Every isometry fixing zero is an orthogonal linear map: distance preservation gives preservation of inner products by the polarization identity, and its values on an orthonormal basis then determine the linear map. Orientation preservation gives determinant one, so it is a rotation. Consequently G embeds in the circle group of rotation angles.
For the hyperbolic plane, use this precise standard fact about its isometries: after placing a point at the origin of the Poincare disc model, every orientation-preserving isometry fixing it has the form z↦eiθz. Equivalently, its point stabilizer is the rotation group, and its action on directions at the point is faithful. This is a general hyperbolic-plane fact allowed by the question. It again identifies G with a finite subgroup of the circle group.
Finally, a nontrivial finite rotation group has a least positive angle θ in [0,2π). Divide any other angle ϕ by θ: the remainder ϕ−kθ is another group angle in [0,θ), so it must be zero. Similarly the remainder of 2π is zero, and θ=2π/m for an integer m. The rotation through θ generates the group. The trivial group is cyclic as well. Therefore both finite orientation-preserving plane isometry groups are cyclic. This proves the finite orientation-preserving plane isometry groups are cyclic result using the same enclosing-disc argument in both geometries.
