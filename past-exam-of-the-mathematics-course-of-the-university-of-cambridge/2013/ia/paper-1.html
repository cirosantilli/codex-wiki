The mean value theorem states that a function continuous on [a,b] and differentiable on (a,b), where a<b, has a point ξ∈(a,b) such that
f′(ξ)=b−af(b)−f(a)​.​
(50)
To prove it using Rolle's theorem, subtract the secant line:
h(t)=f(t)−f(a)−b−af(b)−f(a)​(t−a).
(51)
This function has the required continuity and differentiability, with h(a)=h(b)=0. Rolle's theorem gives h′(ξ)=0, which is exactly the claimed equation. Thus a tangent slope somewhere in the interval equals the overall secant slope.

Boundedness makes these finite. The lower Darboux sum and upper Darboux sum are
s(f,D)=k=1∑n​mk​Δxk​,S(f,D)=k=1∑n​Mk​Δxk​.​
(59)
Since mk​≤Mk​ and the widths are positive, s(f,D)≤S(f,D).
Splitting a partition interval into smaller intervals can only increase each infimum and decrease each supremum. The smaller widths sum to the original width. Its new lower contribution is therefore at least its old lower contribution, and its new upper contribution at most its old upper contribution. Repeating this for every inserted point proves Darboux sum refinement monotonicity:
D⊆D′ ⟹ s(f,D)≤s(f,D′),S(f,D′)≤S(f,D).​
(60)
For arbitrary partitions, take their common partition refinement D∗​=D1​∪D2​. Then
s(f,D1​)≤s(f,D∗​)≤S(f,D∗​)≤S(f,D2​).​

No compatibility of the original partitions is required.
