Use the arch geometry of two hinged rods sliding on a floor, with A=O=(0,0) and C on the other side of B. As long as C remains in contact with the floor,
B=(2lcosθ,2lsinθ),C=(4lcosθ,0).
(60)
The centres of mass of the two rods are (lcosθ,lsinθ) and (3lcosθ,lsinθ). Their squared speeds are respectively l2θ˙2 and l2(9sin2θ+cos2θ)θ˙2. Each uniform rod has moment of inertia Ml2/3 about its midpoint, and their angular velocities are θ˙ and −θ˙. Applying the kinetic energy decomposition about the center of mass to each rod gives

(61)
The gravitational potential energy is U=2Mglsinθ. The fixed pivot does no work; neither does the frictionless floor reaction while C slides horizontally. Conservation of energy from rest therefore gives
θ˙2=2l3g​1+3sin2θsinα−sinθ​.​

On the falling branch θ˙ is the negative square root. Just before the hinge reaches the floor, θ=0, and its speed is ∣B˙∣=2l∣θ˙∣. Thus, under the maintained-contact interpretation,
vB​=6glsinα​.​
(63)
There is a contact hypothesis behind this calculation. A floor can push upward but cannot pull downward. For s=sinθ and a=sinα, the required normal reaction at C, found from the second rod's linear and angular equations, is
MgNC​​=(1+3s2)241​+433​s2+9s4−29​as​.
(64)
For the usual acute arch, this stays nonnegative throughout 0≤θ≤α exactly when sinα≤35/54. Indeed, for s>0, its nonnegativity is a≤1/(18s)+11s/6+2s3, whose minimum is 35/54 at s=1/6. For larger initial angles, the endpoint lifts before the hinge hits the floor, and the one-coordinate formula no longer governs the whole motion. The displayed impact speed is the intended floor-constrained answer; for unrestricted initial angles, maintained contact is an additional assumption. Subsequent free motion and possible reimpact would require a different model and contact/impact conditions.
