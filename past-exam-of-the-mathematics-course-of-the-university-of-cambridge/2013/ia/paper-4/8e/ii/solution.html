The prime-power contraction under pth powers is the key step. If a=b+pkh with k≥1, the binomial theorem gives
ap−bp=pk+1hbp−1+∑j=2p​(jp​)pkjhjbp−j.
(37)
The first term is divisible by pk+1. Each later term is also divisible by that power because kj≥k+1 for j≥2. This includes p=2 and k=1. Therefore
a≡b(modpk)⟹ap≡bp(modpk+1).
(38)
Start with x≡y(modpn) and apply this implication r times, increasing both the exponent and modulus at each step:
xpr≡ypr(modpn+r)(r≥0).​
(39)
The case r=0 is precisely the original congruence.
By Fermat's little theorem, xp≡x(modp). Apply the same result with initial modulus p and r=n−1:
xpn≡xpn−1(modpn).​
(40)
