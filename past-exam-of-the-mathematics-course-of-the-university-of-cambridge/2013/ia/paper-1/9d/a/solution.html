For a nonzero x, the absolute successive-term ratio in the exponential series is ∣x∣/(n+1)→0, so the first series converges for every x. For the factorial series the ratio is (n+1)∣x∣→∞, so its terms fail to tend to zero for every x=0.
The third series is sparse: its exponent is n2, as in the PDF. If tn​=(n!)2∣x∣n2, then
tn​tn+1​​=(n+1)2∣x∣2n+1⟶0(0<∣x∣<1).

For ∣x∣>1, the terms grow without bound; at ∣x∣=1 their moduli are (n!)2, so they again fail the term test. Equivalently, the Cauchy-Hadamard theorem for the sparse coefficients uses (n!)2/n2→1, not (n!)2/n→∞. Thus the three radii of convergence are
R1​=∞,R2​=0,R3​=1.​
(40)
Zeros at non-square coefficient indices do not change the relevant limsup in the last root test. This illustrates the radius of convergence of a sparse power series.
