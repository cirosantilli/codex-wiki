Past exam of the mathematics course of the University of Cambridge / 2013 / ia / Paper 2 / 6A / Solution

Expand the function into separated terms: f=g(x)+h(y), where g=x2−x4 and h=y8−y4. Then
fx​=2x(1−2x2),fy​=4y3(2y4−1).
(17)
Put a=2−1/2 and b=2−1/4. The nine critical points are all pairs from x∈{0,±a} and y∈{0,±b}. The diagonal Hessian matrix has entries 2−12x2 and 56y6−12y2.
At (0,±b) both entries are positive, giving strict local minima of value −1/4. At (±a,±b) they have opposite signs, giving four saddle points of value zero. At the three points on y=0 the Hessian is degenerate, so its sign alone does not classify them. Use the higher-order test for separated extrema: h(y)=−y4+O(y8) is negative for small nonzero y. At (0,0) the positive x2 term and negative y4 term give a saddle. At (±a,0), g−1/4=−(x2−1/2)2, so every sufficiently small nonzero displacement decreases f and these are strict local maxima.
There are two maxima at (±a,0), two minima at (0,±b), and five saddle points: (0,0) and (±a,±b).
For the level curves, the exact zero set consists of the two parabolas x=±y2 and the oval x2+y4=1. They cross at the four nondegenerate saddle points. Small positive levels enclose each maximum; small negative levels enclose each minimum. The degenerate central saddle has quartic narrowing, locally x2−y4=c. Far along the x axis the function is negative, while far along the y axis it is positive. These signs and zero curves determine the connectivity shown in the sketch.
