Positivity means u>0 almost everywhere, since the real logarithm must belong to L1. A positivity-preserving operator gives s=Tu≥0. For fixed x, the scalar shifted Poisson data fidelity is fx​(s)=s−g(x)log(1+s), with
fx′​(s)=1−1+sg(x)​,fx′′​(s)=(1+s)2g(x)​>0.
(6)
Composition with the linear operator T proves convexity in u, and strict convexity holds along pairs whose forward images differ on a set of positive measure. The admissible class itself is convex: the concavity of the logarithm gives log(θu+(1−θ)v)≥θlogu+(1−θ)logv, controlling its negative part, while log+w≤w controls its positive part.
Because ∣Ω∣=1 and log(1+s)≥0, the Jensen inequality gives the requested bound:
∫Ω​[s−glog(1+s)]dx​≥∥s∥1​−∥g∥∞​∫Ω​log(1+s)dx≥∥s∥1​−∥g∥∞​log(1+∥s∥1​)=∥Tu∥1​−∥g∥∞​log∥Tu+1∥1​.​
(7)
The final equality uses nonnegativity and the unit area. Since t−Glog(1+t)→∞, this controls the forward-image norm on energy sublevels.
The printed strictly positive problem has no minimizer. This is nonattainment under strict positivity for shifted Poisson fidelity, rather than a failure of the coercivity calculation. Indeed, with 0<g<1 and s>0, log(1+s)<s implies fx​(s)>0. Also Tu cannot vanish identically for a strictly positive u. To see this, let En​={u≥1/n}. If Tu=0, positivity and 0≤χEn​​≤nu give TχEn​​=0. But χEn​​→χΩ​ in L1 and continuity of T would imply TχΩ​=0, contradicting the hypothesis. Thus every admissible u has positive fidelity and hence positive total energy.
Conversely, the constants uε​=εχΩ​ are admissible, have zero total variation, and satisfy
0<E(uε​)≤ε∥TχΩ​∥1​⟶0.
(8)
Their logarithms are integrable for each ε>0, but the limit is excluded. Therefore
infE=0,argminE=∅in the printed domain.​
(9)
A bounded minimizing sequence and bounded-variation compactness do not repair a nonclosed positivity/logarithm constraint. In particular, a literal existence or uniqueness proof for that domain is impossible.
The natural correction is to minimize over BV(Ω) with u≥0, omitting the unnecessary logu∈L1 condition: the fidelity only contains log(1+Tu), which is already integrable. Here is the full existence for nonnegative shifted Poisson regularization argument, also valid for any bounded nonnegative data g. Let G=∥g∥∞​, mG​=inft≥0​{t−Glog(1+t)}>−∞, and take a minimizing sequence of energy at most C. The displayed bound gives ∥Tun​∥1​≤C1​ and αTV(un​)≤C−mG​. Write cn​=∫Ω​un​≥0. The Poincaré inequality for total variation and mean control for positive imaging operators yield
cn​∥TχΩ​∥1​≤∥Tun​∥1​+∥T∥∥un​−cn​∥1​≤C1​+CP​∥T∥TV(un​).
(10)
The denominator is nonzero, so the full BV norm is bounded. Bounded-variation compactness gives un​→u in L1 along a subsequence, with u≥0. Continuity gives Tun​→Tu in L1. On s≥0, ∣fx′​(s)∣≤1+G, so the fidelity converges in the integral; lower semicontinuity of variation completes the direct method in the calculus of variations.
For the actual printed data 0<g<1, the corrected problem has the unique minimizer u=0, even if T is not injective. Zero attains energy zero. Any other zero-energy candidate would have both Tu=0 and TV(u)=0; on the connected square, zero variation makes u a nonnegative constant, and TχΩ​=0 forces that constant to vanish. For general positive bounded data, an injective T is a sufficient uniqueness condition, because its fidelity is strictly convex; injectivity is not a necessary condition in every instance.
In the finite-dimensional interpretation, let λij​=1+(Tu)ij​. Independent Poisson observations with these intensities have negative log-likelihood ∑ij​[λij​−gij​logλij​]+C(g). Removing the constant ∑1 gives precisely the stated fidelity. Thus the model is Poisson counting noise with a unit background intensity, or an approximate version of it for rescaled/continuous grey values. Literal Poisson counts are integers; the constraint 0<g<1 is a grey-value normalization, not a literal unscaled count sample.
