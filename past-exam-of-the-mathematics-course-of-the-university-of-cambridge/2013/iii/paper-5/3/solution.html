For a Young tableau t, let Rt​ permute the entries within its rows and Ct​ within its columns. We use the Young symmetrizer convention
rt​=∑σ∈Rt​​σ,ct​=∑τ∈Ct​​sgn(τ)τ,ht​=ct​rt​.
(9)
Reversing the order gives another usual realization of the same irreducible polynomial module. On the tensor power T=V⊗n, the actions are
σ(v1​⊗⋯⊗vn​)=vσ−1(1)​⊗⋯⊗vσ−1(n)​,g(v1​⊗⋯⊗vn​)=gv1​⊗⋯⊗gvn​.
(10)
They commute because applying g to every factor commutes with permuting the factors.
We state the permitted combinatorial input explicitly. A Young symmetrizer satisfies ht2​=Hλ​ht​, where Hλ​ is the nonzero hook product of a partition. Thus et​=Hλ−1​ht​ is a primitive idempotent. The standard-tableau decomposition of the right regular module is CSn​=⨁λ,t standard​et​CSn​. Tensor this right-module direct sum with the left module T. The map et​CSn​⊗CSn​​T→et​T sending et​a⊗v to et​av is an isomorphism, with inverse w↦et​⊗w. Therefore the Young-symmetrizer tensor decomposition is
T=⨁λ,t standard​et​T=⨁λ,t standard​ht​T.
(11)
This is a direct sum of GL(V)-modules; individual summands need not be Sn​-invariant.
We also state the allowed Schur algebra result, namely Schur–Weyl duality: the two actions are mutual commutants, and
T≅⨁λ⊢n, ℓ(λ)≤m​Sλ⊗Dλ​(V),
(12)
where the Dλ​(V) are pairwise nonisomorphic irreducible homogeneous polynomial representations of degree n. We also use the standard Schur algebra equivalence between its modules and homogeneous degree-n polynomial representations, so these exhaust the irreducibles in that category. A primitive idempotent et​ has one-dimensional image on Sλ and zero image on the other simple factors, so ht​T≅Dλ​(V). This identifies the requested irreducibles. They classify the polynomial degree-n representations in this tensor power, not all rational representations of every degree.
The length bound for a Schur module is Dλ​(V)=0 if and only if ℓ(λ)≤m. A column of length greater than m antisymmetrizes more than m vectors and gives zero. Conversely, for ℓ(λ)≤m, fill every tensor position in row i with the ith basis vector of V. Row symmetrization multiplies it by ∏i​λi​!, and column antisymmetrization is nonzero because the vectors within each column are distinct basis vectors.
A rational representation of an algebraic group is a regular morphism into the general linear group of its representation space. For GLm​, its matrix entries belong to C[gij​,det(g)−1]; rational here permits determinant denominators but not arbitrary poles on GLm​. A one-dimensional rational character of C× is a Laurent polynomial p(z) with p(zw)=p(z)p(w) and p(1)=1. Comparing Laurent coefficients shows that just one monomial occurs and its coefficient is 1, hence p(z)=zr for r∈Z.
Restrict a one-dimensional rational character of GLm​ to its diagonal torus. The same argument in several variables gives ∏i​xiri​​. Conjugation by permutation matrices makes all ri​ equal. On every diagonalizable invertible matrix it consequently agrees with (detg)r. The allowed Zariski-density statement, and equality of regular functions on a dense subset, give
χ(g)=(detg)r,r∈Z.​
(13)
These are the one-dimensional rational characters of the general linear group.
For complete reducibility of rational GL and SL representations, use the compact-group averaging argument. Average any positive definite Hermitian inner product over SU(m) using normalized Haar measure. The orthogonal complement of an invariant subspace is then SU(m)-invariant. Differentiating makes it invariant under su(m) and therefore under its complex span slm​(C). The elementary unipotent matrices exp(tEij​) generate SLm​(C), so the complement is SLm​-invariant. This proves complete reducibility. Averaging over U(m) similarly gives complete reducibility for rational GLm​ representations.
Here is an explicit rational extension from SL to GL. Decompose the representation space by the finite scalar center of SLm​ into subspaces Wk​ on which ζI acts as ζk, with 0≤k<m and ζm=1. These subspaces are invariant. For g∈GLm​, choose a with am=detg, set s=a−1g∈SLm​, and define
ρ′(g)∣Wk​​=akρ(s)∣Wk​​.
(14)
Changing a to ζa changes s to ζ−1s, so the two factors cancel. It is a homomorphism, since scalar roots multiply up to the same harmless factor, and it restricts to ρ on SLm​.
It is rational as well. Every matrix coefficient of ρ∣Wk​​ has a polynomial representative on SLm​. Averaging that representative over the finite scalar center selects its homogeneous parts Pd​ with d≡k(modm). Substitution in the extension gives ∑d​(detg)(k−d)/mPd​(g), a regular function on GLm​. Each SLm​-invariant subspace decomposes into its Wk​ parts, and the extension acts on each part by a scalar times an SLm​ action. Thus these subspaces are also invariant under the chosen extension. Consequently ρ is irreducible if and only if this ρ′ is irreducible. For m=1, SL1​ is trivial and the trivial extension supplies the same conclusion.
