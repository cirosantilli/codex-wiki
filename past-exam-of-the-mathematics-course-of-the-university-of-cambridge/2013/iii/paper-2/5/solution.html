Let R be a commutative ring and f=(f1​,…,fr​). The Koszul complex packages these elements and their relations in a finite chain complex of free modules. With E=Rr and basis e1​,…,er​, set Ki​(f;R)=⋀iE for 0≤i≤r, and define
∂(ej1​​∧⋯∧eji​​)=∑a=1i​(−1)a−1fja​​ej1​​∧⋯∧eja​​​∧⋯∧eji​​.
(26)
Deleting two distinct basis elements in the two possible orders gives opposite signs and the same coefficient, so ∂2=0. Equivalently K(f;R) is the tensor product of chain complexes of r two-term complexes [Rfi​​R] in degrees one and zero. The tensor differential is ∂(u⊗v)=∂u⊗v+(−1)deguu⊗∂v. This fixes the sign convention. On the exterior algebra, the differential is a graded derivation determined by ∂ei​=fi​.
For an R-module M, define the Koszul complex with module coefficients by K(f;M)=K(f;R)⊗R​M. Its degree-zero Koszul homology is
H0​(f;M)=M/(f1​,…,fr​)M.
(27)
Its higher Koszul homology measures the failure of these equations to form a regular sequence on a module. Each fj​ annihilates every homology group. Indeed the Koszul homotopy for multiplication by a generator is hj​(u)=ej​∧u, and the graded product rule gives
∂hj​+hj​∂=fj​id.
(28)
For coefficients in M the same identity holds after tensoring. Thus multiplication by fj​ is zero on homology, and the homology groups are naturally modules over R/(f). If (f)=R, choose ∑j​aj​fj​=1; then ∑j​aj​hj​ is a contracting homotopy, so the entire complex is contractible. These identities are also useful after localization of a ring: wherever one generator is a unit, the complex has zero homology.
The construction is functorial under a ring homomorphism, and base change gives K(f;R)⊗R​S=K(fˉ​;S), since its terms are free with the displayed basis and differential. An invertible change of generators fi​=∑j​uij​gj​ gives a chain isomorphism by sending ei​ to ∑j​uij​ej​ and extending to exterior powers. In particular, permuting the generators changes only the exterior signs, not the isomorphism class of the complex.
The essential exactness theorem is that a regular sequence on a module gives zero positive Koszul homology. Recall that fi​ must act injectively on M/(f1​,…,fi−1​)M, with final quotient nonzero. Put f′=(f1​,…,fr−1​). Adding the final two-term complex identifies K(f;M) with the mapping cone of multiplication by fr​ on K(f′;M), with the chosen tensor signs. The long exact sequence in homology consequently has segments
⋯⟶Hi​(f′;M)fr​​Hi​(f′;M)⟶Hi​(f;M)⟶Hi−1​(f′;M)fr​​Hi−1​(f′;M)⟶⋯.
(29)
For r=1, positive homology is exactly ker(f1​:M→M). By induction the preceding homology vanishes above zero. The final injectivity assumption kills H1​(f;M), while the exact sequence kills every Hi​ for i>1. Therefore
Hi​(f;M)=0 (i>0),H0​(f;M)=M/(f)M​
(30)
for a regular sequence on a module. In particular K(f;R) is a finite free resolution of R/(f) when f is a regular sequence, with ranks (ir​) and length r.
There is a precise converse under local finiteness assumptions. Suppose (R,m) is a Noetherian local ring, 0=M is finitely generated, and all fi​∈m. If positive Koszul homology vanishes, the same exact sequence makes multiplication by fr​ surjective on every Hi​(f′;M) for i>0. These modules are finitely generated because the ring is Noetherian and the terms of the complex are finite modules. The Nakayama lemma gives Hi​(f′;M)=0. Induction makes f′ regular on M, and the degree-one part of the exact sequence makes fr​ injective on H0​(f′;M). The final quotient is nonzero, again by the Nakayama lemma. This proves the Koszul acyclicity criterion in a Noetherian local ring:
f is M-regular⟺Hi​(f;M)=0 for every i>0.​
(31)
The hypotheses matter: a unit ideal makes the complex contractible but cannot be a proper regular sequence.
Examples make both sides visible. For R=k[x,y] and f=(x,y), the Koszul resolution is
0⟶R(x−y​)​R2(x  y)​R⟶k⟶0.
(32)
The signs agree with ∂(e1​∧e2​)=xe2​−ye1​. For R=k[x] and f=(x,x), the degree-one cycles are R(1,−1) and the boundaries are xR(1,−1); hence H1​≅R/(x), while H2​=0. The repeated element has exposed a relation that the first element already kills.
The Koszul complex also computes derived functors. If f is a regular sequence on R, its free resolution gives
ToriR​(R/(f),M)=Hi​(f;M).
(33)
Here no regularity of f on M is assumed. For S=k[x1​,…,xr​], tensor the Koszul resolution on the variables with k. Every differential becomes zero, so
ToriS​(k,k)≅⋀ikr.
(34)
The top group is nonzero, showing that a free resolution of k cannot be shorter than r.
Finally, wedging complementary exterior degrees gives a perfect pairing Ki​⊗R​Kr−i​→⋀rRr≅R. It identifies the dual cochain complex with the degree-reversed Koszul complex, after the appropriate signs. This self-duality of the Koszul complex shows, for a regular sequence, that
ExtRi​(R/(f),R)=0(i=r),ExtRr​(R/(f),R)≅R/(f).
(35)
Thus the same explicit complex simultaneously records quotient equations, regularity, relations, Tor functor computations and Ext functor computations. Its finiteness and exterior structure are what make it especially effective in commutative algebra.
