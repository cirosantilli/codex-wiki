Here sym∇b is the symmetric part of a matrix formed by the coefficients of the quadratic polynomial. For constants A,α,β>0, choose a=logA−α∣x∣2, b=2αtx and c=−β−αt2. All four coefficient equations hold. The resulting expanding Gaussian solution of the Boltzmann equation is

Direct verification is particularly simple: x−tv and v are constant along free characteristic curves, so the transport derivative is zero. At fixed (t,x) its logarithm is the displayed Maxwellian quadratic, so the collision term is also zero. Its spatial dependence is nonconstant, it is positive and rapidly decaying, and its Gaussian velocity integral is finite because β+αt2>0. It is not a compactly supported example, nor does this final part require one.
