Here v is the expected process variance and a is the variance of hypothetical means. The target is the latent conditional mean m(λ), rather than the realized next count. The Bühlmann credibility estimate is the best affine predictor of that target under mean squared error.

The ratio notation assumes a>0; the credibility factor formula na/(na+v) also handles a=0,v>0. If a=0, the target is the constant m0​ almost surely. If v=0,a>0, one observation already equals m(λ) almost surely, and the average gives it exactly. If both vanish, the target and observations are constant.
The result optimizes over affine functions of the observations. It need not equal the unrestricted posterior mean; exact Bayesian inference generally depends on the whole prior and likelihood, whereas the Bühlmann credibility estimate uses these second-moment structural parameters.
