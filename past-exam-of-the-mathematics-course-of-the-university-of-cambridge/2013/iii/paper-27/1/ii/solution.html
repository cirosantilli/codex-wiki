The capacity in this question is harmonic capacity from infinity in the upper half-plane, which has units of length; it is distinct from half-plane capacity, which has units of length squared.
Here is a proof of existence that also works with irregular real attachments. The function
u(z)=Pz​(BT(H)​∈K)
(5)
is bounded and harmonic on H, by the Strong Markov property and the mean-value characterization of harmonic functions. Therefore u∘gK−1​ is a bounded harmonic function on the complex upper half-plane, with a Poisson kernel representation
u(gK−1​(w))=∫R​π∣t−w∣2Imw​f(t)dt,0≤f≤1.
(6)
The boundary function f vanishes outside a bounded interval. Indeed, far enough along either real ray the original domain contains a half-disc neighbourhood, and gK​ extends there; the probability of hitting the bounded hull before the real boundary tends to zero as the starting point approaches that ray. Applying the same dominated-limit calculation as in part (i) yields
cap(K)=∫R​f(t)dt<∞.​
(7)
When the intrinsic boundary pieces landing on the hull are identified, f is their indicator almost everywhere and this is the length of their image under gK​. For ordinary finite slit hulls this is exactly the image of δH∖H0​, since real attachment endpoints have zero harmonic measure. The Poisson representation avoids requiring that boundary identification in the general existence argument.
If K⊂K′, couple the two exit events using the same planar Brownian motion, stopped at its first hit of the real axis. Any path that hits K before the real axis also hits K′ before the real axis. Consequently
Piy​(BT(H∖K)​∈K)≤Piy​(BT(H∖K′)​∈K′),
(8)
and taking the limits proves monotonicity of this capacity.
For a half-disc of radius r centred at b∈R, the mapping-out function is
g(z)=b+(z−b)+z−br2​.
(9)
Its semicircular boundary maps onto [b−2r,b+2r], of length 4r. Hence its harmonic capacity from infinity in the upper half-plane is 4r. With
rad(K)=inf{r>0:K⊂{z:∣z−b∣≤r} for some b∈R},
(10)
enclose K in such a half-disc and use monotonicity. Letting the enclosing radius decrease to the infimum proves
cap(K)≤4rad(K).​
(11)
The same conclusion holds if radius is instead measured about a specified real centre.
