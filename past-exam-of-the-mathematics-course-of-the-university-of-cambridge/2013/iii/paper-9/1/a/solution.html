For a unit direction e, let Tδ​(a,e) be a length-one Kakeya tube with transverse radius δ, centered at a, and define the Kakeya maximal function by
Kδ​f(e)=supa​∣Tδ​(a,e)∣1​∫Tδ​(a,e)​∣f(x)∣dx.
(1)
Using normalized surface measure on the direction sphere, the Kakeya maximal conjecture is the following family of estimates, in the formulation relevant to this paper:
∥Kδ​f∥Ln(Sn−1)​≤Cn,ε​δ−ε∥f∥Ln(Rn)​(0<δ<1, ε>0).​
(2)
The constant is independent of δ and f. Replacing round Kakeya tubes by comparable rectangular tubes changes only dimensional constants.
A bounded Kakeya set contains a unit line segment in every direction. The Kakeya Minkowski dimension conjecture says that every such set has full Minkowski dimension n. The maximal estimate in fact gives full lower as well as upper Minkowski dimension.
To prove that implication, let Eδ​={x:dist(x,E)<δ}. Each unit segment in E has a thinner tube contained in Eδ​, so Kcδ​1Eδ​​(e)≥1 for every e, with a fixed dimensional c>0. Apply the maximal estimate to this indicator function:
1≲Cn,ε​δ−ε∣Eδ​∣1/n,∣Eδ​∣≳n,ε​δnε.
(3)
If Nδ​(E) is the smallest number of radius-δ balls covering E, that cover, enlarged by a fixed factor, covers Eδ​. Thus ∣Eδ​∣≲n​δnNδ​(E) and
Nδ​(E)≳n,ε​δ−n+nε.
(4)
Taking the lower limit of logNδ​(E)/log(1/δ) and then letting ε↓0 gives lower Minkowski dimension at least n. Bounded subsets of Rn have upper Minkowski dimension at most n. Consequently both dimensions equal n, which proves the requested implication.
