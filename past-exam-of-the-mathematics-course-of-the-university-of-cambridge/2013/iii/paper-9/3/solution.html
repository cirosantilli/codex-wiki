Set m=⌈p/100⌉ and d=m−1, so that d<p/100<p. The dimension of a bounded-total-degree polynomial space in n variables over Fp​ is (nn+d​). If #N were smaller than this dimension, evaluation at the points of N would impose fewer homogeneous linear conditions than unknown coefficients. The rank-nullity theorem would give a nonzero multivariate polynomial P of total degree at most d, vanishing on N.
For every x, select one of the promised rich affine lines in a vector space through x, and write it as ℓ={a+tv:t∈Fp​} with v=0. The polynomial restriction to a line Q(t)=P(a+tv) has degree at most d, and at least m=d+1 distinct roots of a polynomial from N∩ℓ. By the root bound for a polynomial, Q is identically zero, so P(x)=0. Since this works for every x, P vanishes at all pn points of Fpn​.
The Schwartz-Zippel lemma says that a nonzero multivariate polynomial of total degree d has at most dpn−1 zeros on this grid. Here d<p, so that count is strictly less than pn, a contradiction. The distinction between a formal polynomial and its function on a finite field is crucial: our degree bound is what rules out a nonzero polynomial vanishing everywhere.
We have proved the stronger quantitative rich line covering bound over a finite field
#N≥(nn+⌈p/100⌉−1​)=n!(d+1)⋯(d+n)​≥100nn!pn​.​
(22)
Thus #N≳n​pn, as required. The implied constant may depend on the fixed dimension n. The very large numerical lower bound on p is more than this proof needs.
