In this setting a formal group law means a one-dimensional commutative series F(X,Y)∈R[[X,Y]] with identity zero and
F(X,0)=X,F(0,Y)=Y,F(X,Y)=F(Y,X),F(F(X,Y),Z)=F(X,F(Y,Z)).
(10)
It starts with X+Y; there is a unique inverse series ι(T)=−T+⋯ satisfying F(T,ι(T))=0. For coefficients in Z, the series and its inverse converge on pZp​, making this ideal a topological abelian group under F.
Set a(T)=∂Y​F(T,0)∈1+TZ[[T]], and write a(T)−1=∑j≥0​bj​Tj. The formal logarithm is
L(T)=∫0T​a(U)dU​=T+∑j≥1​j+1bj​​Tj+1∈Q[[T]].
(11)
Differentiate associativity in the third variable at zero. It gives a(F(X,Y))=∂Y​F(X,Y)a(Y), so ∂Y​L(F(X,Y))=L′(Y). Subtracting L(Y) leaves a series independent of Y, whose value at zero is L(X). Thus L(F(X,Y))=L(X)+L(Y).
For odd p, the logarithm converges on I=pZp​: the valuation of its degree-n term is at least n−vp​(n), which tends to infinity. Put u(T)=L(T)−T. For x,y∈I, factor xn−yn to obtain
vp​(nbn−1​​(xn−yn))≥vp​(x−y)+n−1−vp​(n)≥vp​(x−y)+1
(12)
for every n≥2 and odd p. Hence u is a contraction with constant at most 1/p and maps I into pI. The equation L(x)=z is equivalent to x=z−u(x); the contraction mapping theorem gives a unique solution in I for every z∈I. The same estimate shows ∣L(x)−L(y)∣p​=∣x−y∣p​, so the logarithm is a topological group isomorphism. Dividing its values by p gives
F(pZp​)≅(pZp​,+)≅(Zp​,+)(p odd).​
(13)
This is the deep logarithm subgroup of a formal group argument, here with depth one.
For p=2, the degree-two estimate at depth one need not be strict. At depth two, however,
2(n−1)−v2​(n)≥1(n≥2),
(14)
so the same proof gives F(4Z2​)≅(4Z2​,+)≅Z2​. This subgroup has index two in F(2Z2​), because F(x,y)≡x+y(mod4) when x,y∈2Z2​.
Indeed the topological structure of a formal group on twice the 2-adic integers has just two possibilities. Let G=F(2Z2​), let its index-two subgroup H have topological generator h, and choose v∈/H. Write 2v=ch, with c∈Z2​, using group notation. If c is odd, 2v generates H and v generates G, giving G≅Z2​. If c is even, v−(c/2)h has order two and lies outside H, giving G≅H×Z/2Z. The Z2​ multiples are defined by continuity in this compact pro-two group.
For explicit contrasting examples, the formal additive group Fa​(X,Y)=X+Y gives Fa​(2Z2​)≅Z2​, which is a torsion-free group. The formal multiplicative group Fm​(X,Y)=X+Y+XY is identified by x↦1+x with 1+2Z2​. Its element x=−2 corresponds to −1 and has order two. Moreover
1+2Z2​={±1}×(1+4Z2​),
(15)
and the logarithm identifies the second factor with 4Z2​. The two resulting groups are Z2​ and Z/2Z×Z2​, so they are not isomorphic.
