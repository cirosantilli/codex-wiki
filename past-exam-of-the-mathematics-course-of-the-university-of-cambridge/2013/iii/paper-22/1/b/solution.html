Put N(x)=x3+x+2, H(x)=x3+2x+1 and R(x)=N(x)/(x−1)2, all in characteristic three. Since N(1)=1, the numerator and denominator have no common factor, and R has degree three. The map x:E→P1 has degree two. Comparing the degrees in x∘ϕ=R∘x gives 2degϕ=3⋅2, so degϕ=3. This is the degree of an isogeny from its x-coordinate map.
In characteristic three, direct differentiation gives
R′(x)=(x−1)3H(x)​.
(7)
The supplied y-coordinate is H(x)y/(x−1)3, so the invariant differential on an elliptic curve satisfies
ϕ∗ω=2H(x)y/(x−1)3R′(x)dx​=ω.
(8)
In particular ϕ is separable. Its dual isogeny satisfies ϕ​∘ϕ=[3]. Since [3]∗ω=0 and ϕ∗ω=ω, the scalar by which ϕ​ pulls back the differential is zero.
Pullback on the elliptic invariant differential is additive for sums of homomorphisms, by the addition identity proved in (a). Consequently
(mϕ+nϕ​)∗ω=mω.
(9)
The differential criterion for separability of an isogeny therefore gives separability exactly when 3∤m, with n arbitrary. Such a map is automatically nonzero. When 3∣m, every nonzero resulting map is inseparable; the zero map is not a separable isogeny. In fact the zero map occurs only at (m,n)=(0,0): equality of the degrees of [m]ϕ and [n]ϕ​ in a nontrivial vanishing relation would force m=±n, and then cancellation would force ϕ=±ϕ​, contradicting their different differential scalars.
