At P1​ the tangent slope is zero. The elliptic-curve addition formula gives
2P1​=(0,−15)=−P1​.​
(17)
The chord from P1​ to P2​ has slope (−3−15)/(−6)=3, so its sum has x-coordinate 32−0−(−6)=15 and y-coordinate 3(0−15)−15=−60. Thus P1​+P2​=(15,−60).
Reduction at the good prime seven gives E:y2=x3+1. For x=0,1,2,3,4,5,6, the right sides are respectively 1,2,2,0,2,0,0. There are respectively 2,2,2,1,2,1,1 choices of y. Including the point at infinity gives #E(F7​)=12. Its nonzero torsion points of an elliptic curve of order two are (3,0),(5,0),(6,0), so it cannot be cyclic. Its two-primary component is (Z/2Z)2 and its three-primary component is cyclic of order three; hence
E(F7​)≅Z/2Z×Z/6Z.​
(18)
In particular its exponent is six.
The reduction of an elliptic curve is a group homomorphism defined on every Q7​-point by projectivity. Thus 6P reduces to the identity for every rational P. A finite point with integral coordinates reduces to an affine point, whose projective last coordinate is one, so it cannot reduce to the identity at infinity. Therefore every nonzero finite point 6P has nonintegral coordinates. If 6P=O, it has no affine coordinates at all. This is the reduction exponent obstruction to integral multiples.
