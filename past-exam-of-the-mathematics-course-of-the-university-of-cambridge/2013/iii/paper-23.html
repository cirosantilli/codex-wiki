A primitive Dirichlet character modulo q is a Dirichlet character which is not induced from a character of a proper divisor of q. To determine the inducing primitive Dirichlet character, use the Chinese remainder theorem to decompose
(Z/qZ)×≅∏pk∥q​(Z/pkZ)×.
(1)
On each factor choose the least exponent cp​∈{0,…,k} through whose reduction the restricted character factors. Exponent zero means the trivial unit group modulo one. Put q0​=∏p​pcp​ and define χ0​ on the units modulo q0​ by these descended factors, extending by zero off the units. Every reduction of unit groups is surjective, so the descended character is unique. Its local exponents cannot be decreased, hence it is primitive. The original character is χ(n)=χ0​(n) when (n,q)=1, and zero otherwise.
Any other inducing modulus must have exponent at least cp​ at every prime, by restriction to the corresponding local factor. Therefore q0​ is the unique minimal modulus, the conductor of a Dirichlet character, and χ0​ is the unique primitive Dirichlet character inducing χ. The argument also explains why removing extra prime factors can change values at integers that were nonunits for q.

The completed Dirichlet L-function is
Λ(s,χ)=(πq​)(s+a)/2Γ(2s+a​)L(s,χ).
(12)
For nonprincipal primitive Dirichlet characters, termwise Mellin transformation initially in ℜs>1 gives

(13)

Λ(s,χ)=εχ​Λ(1−s,χ​).​
(14)
The same calculation with the extra x−1 power gives the odd functional equation with its corresponding root number.
The gamma function has no zeros and has simple poles at nonpositive integers. Thus the nontrivial zeros of L and Λ coincide with multiplicities. The trivial zeros of a Dirichlet L-function are 0,−2,−4,… for a nonprincipal even character, and −1,−3,−5,… for an odd character. Assume Riemann hypothesis. First fix 0<δ<1/4 and work on σ0​=1/2+δ. We prove the subpower zeta bound to the right of the critical line, then move back to the line by the functional equation and Phragmén–Lindelöf principle.
Put x=logt for large positive t and integrate the supplied smoothed logarithmic derivative identity horizontally from σ0​ to two. There are no zeros on this path under Riemann hypothesis, so the Euler-product logarithm at 2+it continues along it. Each prime-power term contributes at most Λ(n)n−σ0​/logn≤n−σ0​, and the smoothing weights are at most one. Hence the integrated prime terms are bounded by
Cδ​∑n≤x2​n−σ0​≪δ​x1−2δ=o(logt).
(47)
All zeros have ρ=1/2+iγ. The local zero-count estimate from Question 3, with its reflected version for negative ordinates, gives uniformly for σ0​≤σ≤2
∑ρ​∣ρ−σ−it∣21​≤Cδ​logt.
(48)
To see the uniformity, sum the O(log(2+∣γ∣)) zeros in successive unit ordinate intervals against (δ2+∣t−γ∣2)−1; the distant dyadic tails are summable.
