An effective bandwidth is an exponential-moment measure of demand at a chosen tail parameter s: independence makes these quantities additive. The spare capacity γ/s pays for the desired exponential tail bound. For a cumulative-demand process over time t, the corresponding bandwidth would be logEesX(t)/(st); here the time horizon is one. It is generally larger than mean demand because it charges for fluctuations, and mathematical optimization over s selects the useful tradeoff.

This is necessary and sufficient when v>0, even when ϕ is negative. The Chernoff coefficient 2γ​ is more conservative.
There is an important boundary qualification. If v=0, then X=m deterministically, and for γ>0 the exact requirement is C>m, not C≥m. For example, X≡0, C=0, γ=1 satisfies the printed square-root condition but has P(X≥C)=1>e−1. Thus both Gaussian non-strict displayed forms require positive total variance, which follows if at least one flow is present and its variance is positive. With no positive variance, the deterministic boundary in an upper-tail chance constraint must be treated separately. If γ≤0, the target upper bound is at least one and imposes no restriction; the displayed square-root discussion naturally assumes γ>0.
