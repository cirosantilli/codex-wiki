For a real weakly stationary process, extend its autocovariance by γ−k​=γk​. The exact existence of a time-series spectral density condition is that its spectral measure of a stationary time series be absolutely continuous with respect to Lebesgue measure. Absolute summability γ0​+2∑k≥1​∣γk​∣<∞ is a useful sufficient condition, not a necessary one.
We use the conventional angular-frequency density on [−π,π], restricted to [0,π] by symmetry. Under the absolute-summability condition, the Fourier series and its inverse relation are
f(ω)=2π1​(γ0​+2k≥1∑​γk​cos(kω)),γk​=2∫0π​f(ω)cos(kω)dω.​
(10)
Thus 2∫0π​f=γ0​. If a one-sided density is instead normalized to integrate to the full variance, use g=2f and omit the factor two in the inverse formula. This is the positive-frequency spectral normalization convention difference. With merely an integrable spectral density, the inverse relation remains valid; one must not assume pointwise convergence of the unweighted Fourier series. Its Fejér sums recover the density in L1:
fN​(ω)=2π1​[γ0​+2∑k=1N​(1−N+1k​)γk​cos(kω)]⟶f.
(11)
If X and Y are independent stationary processes, the cross covariances vanish, so
γZ​(k)=Cov(Xt+k​+Yt+k​,Xt​+Yt​)=γX​(k)+γY​(k).
(12)
Linearity of the inverse Fourier series relation, or addition of the spectral measures of a stationary time series, gives fZ​=fX​+fY​. Independence can in fact be weakened to zero cross-covariances at every lag.
For the ARMA(1,1) representation, away from an uncancelled unit root the transfer function gives
fX​(ω)=2πv​1+ϕ2−2ϕcosω1+θ2+2θcosω​.​
(13)
The usual causal case has ∣ϕ∣<1; the same expression holds for the two-sided stationary solution when ∣ϕ∣>1. We work in the nondegenerate case v,w>0 and ϕ=±1; cancellations and zero-noise cases are obtained by the appropriate reduced representation or limits. Since independent white noise of variance w contributes w/(2π), put
A=v(1+θ2)+w(1+ϕ2),C=vθ−wϕ.
(14)
Then
fZ​(ω)=2π1​1+ϕ2−2ϕcosωA+2Ccosω​.​
(15)
The white-noise addition to an ARMA(1,1) process problem is therefore the factorization
A+2Ccosω=λ(1+α2+2αcosω).
(16)
Define
P=A+2C=v(1+θ)2+w(1−ϕ)2,Q=A−2C=v(1−θ)2+w(1+ϕ)2.
(17)
Both are positive under the stated nondegeneracy conditions. An invertible choice is
α=P​+Q​P​−Q​​,λ=4(P​+Q​)2​.​
(18)
These satisfy ∣α∣<1, λ(1+α)2=P, and λ(1−α)2=Q. Hence
(1−α1+α​)2=v(1−θ)2+w(1+ϕ)2v(1+θ)2+w(1−ϕ)2​.​
(19)
In particular, it is the autoregressive coefficient ϕ that enters the terms from the added observation noise.
To obtain a representation of the actual Z, define
ξt​=(1+αB)−1(1−ϕB)Zt​.
(20)
The stable inverse exists because ∣α∣<1, and spectral filtering gives fξ​=λ/(2π). Thus ξ is weak white noise, and
Zt​=ϕZt−1​+αξt−1​+ξt​.​
(21)
Its variance can equivalently be written
λ=1+α2v(1+θ2)+w(1+ϕ2)​=2A+A2−4C2​​.​
(22)
When α=0, also λ=(vθ−wϕ)/α; when α=0, necessarily C=0 and λ=A, so the latter quotient should not be used. The model may reduce in order through cancellation. Boundary limits with P=0 or Q=0 give ∣α∣=1 and need not be stably invertible; the displayed stable reconstruction applies to the nondegenerate case above.
