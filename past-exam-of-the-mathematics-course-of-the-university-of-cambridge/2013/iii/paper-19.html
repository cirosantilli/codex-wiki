An inner model of ZFC is a transitive class containing every ordinal and satisfying all its axioms. Write this class as A with its inherited membership relation. Satisfaction is interpreted by restricting all quantifiers to A. In a first-order formulation this is a schema for a definable class, possibly with fixed parameters. The class may equal the whole universe. Transitivity means x∈y∈A⇒x∈A; containing all ordinals rules out treating an arbitrary transitive set model as an inner model.

A strongly inaccessible cardinal is an uncountable cardinal λ that is both regular and a strong limit:
cf(λ)=λ,2μ<λ for every cardinal μ<λ.​
(1)
Regularity excludes expressing λ as the supremum of a shorter increasing sequence, while the strong-limit requirement concerns all smaller power sets. Merely being an uncountable regular limit cardinal is the weaker notion of a weakly inaccessible cardinal.

A first-order formula φ(x1​,…,xn​) is an absolute formula for membership structures A and B if, for every tuple a∈(A∩B)n,
(A,∈)⊨φ(a)⟺(B,∈)⊨φ(a).​
(2)
The same elements interpret the free variables in both structures; bound variables range over their respective domains. Thus absoluteness requires both directions, rather than only preservation of truth from the smaller structure to the larger one.

Use a definable cumulative hierarchy of set-sized stages: Aα​⊆Aβ​ for α<β, Aη​=⋃α<η​Aα​ at nonzero limits, and A=⋃α∈Ord​Aα​. Class parameters and the hierarchy are fixed definable data. The reflection theorem for definable hierarchies says that for every finite collection Φ of formulas there is a closed unbounded class of ordinals C such that
α∈C ⟹ [Aα​⊨φ(a) ⟺ A⊨φ(a)](φ∈Φ, a∈Aα​).​
(3)
This is a schema of ZFC for each finite collection and class definition, not a purported truth predicate for all formulas over the universe at once.
Close Φ under subformulas. For each existential subformula ∃yψ(y,x) and each tuple in Aα​, if a witness exists in A, take the least stage index containing a witness. There are only set-many parameter tuples and finitely many formulas. The Axiom schema of replacement therefore bounds all these least indices by an ordinal h(α), chosen larger than α. No definable selection of the witnesses themselves is needed.
Above any prescribed bound choose α0​<α1​<⋯ with αm+1​>h(αm​), and put η=supm​αm​. Every tuple in Aη​ lies in some Aαm​​, and each true existential instance for that tuple has a witness in Aαm+1​​⊆Aη​. Induction over the subformulas now proves agreement between Aη​ and A: atomic formulas use the same membership relation, Boolean operations preserve agreement, and the existential step uses this witness property. Thus reflecting stages are unbounded.
For closedness, suppose reflecting stages have limit η. Every tuple in Aη​ lies in a reflecting stage below η, and every true existential instance has a witness there. The same subformula induction proves reflection at η. Hence the reflecting stages for the subformula-closed collection form a closed unbounded class, proving the Lévy reflection theorem in this relative form.

The printed inclusion-and-elementarity assertion is false if transitivity is required of the same submodel. Take the theorem of ZFC that combines the axiom of infinity and the Axiom of power set. Whenever Vδ​ satisfies this sentence, it contains ω, every subset of ω, and their actual power set P(ω). A submodel M≺Vδ​ contains ω and P(ω), since these are uniquely definable in Vδ​. If M were transitive, it would contain every element of P(ω), contradicting countability by Cantor theorem.
The corrected conclusion uses an elementary embedding rather than elementary inclusion. By Lévy reflection theorem, choose δ>ω+2 with Vδ​⊨φ and with Extensionality true there. The Downward Lowenheim-Skolem theorem says that an infinite structure in a countable language has a countable elementary substructure. Apply it to obtain a countable N≺(Vδ​,∈). The membership relation on N is externally well-founded, and elementarity makes it extensional. The Mostowski collapse theorem gives an isomorphism π:N→M onto a countable transitive set. Hence
M⊨φ,j=π−1:M⟶Vδ​ is elementary.​
(4)
Indeed M⊆Vδ​: rank induction gives rank(π(x))≤rank(x) for every x∈N. The crucial correction is that j, rather than the inclusion of M, is elementary. For a formula with free variables, apply this argument to its universal closure.

Let θ=ξ∗, and work with the set structure Vθ​⊨ZFC. Its ordinal height is an infinite limit: a transitive model of ZFC has no largest ordinal, since it can take the successor of each ordinal it contains. Thus cf(θ)≥ω.
Suppose instead that cf(θ)>ω. Enumerate all first-order formulas, and close each finite initial collection under subformulas. The structure Hκ​ of hereditarily small sets is transitive. If κ≤ω, all its sets are hereditarily finite, so it cannot satisfy Infinity. Therefore the assumed model has κ>ω.
Let μ<κ be a cardinal. The ordinal μ belongs to Hκ​, and every actual subset of μ also belongs to Hκ​: its transitive closure has size at most max(μ,ω)<κ. The Power set axiom inside Hκ​ therefore produces the actual P(μ), since all subsets relevant to the internal definition are present. This power set itself belongs to Hκ​, so
2μ=∣P(μ)∣<κ.
(6)
Thus κ is a strong limit. Its regularity was assumed, and we have proved uncountability. Hence κ is strongly inaccessible.

A set-theoretic tree is a partial order for which the strict predecessors of every node are well-ordered by the tree order. The height of a node is that predecessor order type, and Tα​ is the set of nodes of height α. Under the ordinary height-and-width definition, a kappa-tree satisfies
ht(T)=κ,0<∣Tα​∣<κ(α<κ).​
(16)
For an arbitrary cardinal in this definition one must distinguish it from additional conventions such as being well-pruned, normal or splitting. Splitting means that every node has two incompatible extensions; it is not implied by the height-and-width clauses. This distinction is material in Question 5(ii)(a).

With this paper's order convention, larger conditions are stronger. A generic filter G⊆P over M is nonempty, closed toward weaker conditions, and directed toward stronger conditions: if p,q∈G, some r∈G satisfies r≥p,q. It also meets every D∈M which M regards as dense in P.
For a countable transitive M, such a filter containing any prescribed condition can be built by enumerating its dense sets and successively choosing stronger conditions in them. The definition does not require G∈/M: for an atomic forcing a generic filter may already belong to M.

D={δ<κ:δ is limit and (∀α<δ) h(α)<δ}
(28)
is a club set by the usual countable closure iteration and regularity. For every δ∈D, p forces C˙∩δ unbounded in δ, and closedness forces δ∈C˙. Hence p⊩Dˇ⊆C˙. In M, choose δ∈S∩D; that same ordinal belongs to S∩C in the extension. Stationarity at the fixed ground κ is preserved.
There is an important qualification to the printed ω2​ notation. If it is recomputed internally as (ω2​)M[G], the assertion is false without preservation of smaller cardinals. Finite partial maps from ω to (ω1​)M form a forcing of size (ℵ1​)M, hence have the (ℵ2​)M-chain condition, but collapse (ω1​)M to countable. Then (ω2​)M=(ω1​)M[G], while (ω2​)M[G] is larger. The old S is bounded in this new ω2​, so cannot be stationary there. The proved statement uses the fixed ground ordinal, or alternatively requires the lower-cardinal preservation needed to retain its aleph index.

Under the ordinary height-and-width definition of an ℵ1​-tree, the printed claim is false. The chain (ω1​,≤) has one node at each level, every antichain has size at most one, and it is itself an uncountable chain.
Here is the intended argument under the additional splitting convention. Any uncountable chain in a tree with countable levels is unbounded in height, because a bounded set of levels below ω1​ has only countably many nodes. Its predecessor closure therefore gives a cofinal branch B. At each node on B, splitting supplies an extension off B, incompatible with a later node on B. Recursively for ξ<ω1​, choose such an off-branch node sξ​, then move sufficiently far along B that all subsequent choices are above a branch node incompatible with sξ​. At a limit stage the previous countably many heights are bounded below ω1​, so the recursion continues. The sξ​ are pairwise incompatible, an uncountable antichain.
Thus a splitting ℵ1​-tree with only countable antichains has no uncountable chains. A splitting convention must be stated; the raw tree hypothesis alone does not suffice.

Use the equivalent tree formulation of the Suslin hypothesis: there is no normal, well-pruned Suslin tree of height ω1​, with countable levels and no uncountable chains or antichains. This is equivalent to the linear-order formulation that every complete dense order without endpoints satisfying the countable chain condition for a linear order is separable.
If such a tree T existed, use its nodes as forcing conditions, with a higher extension stronger. It is CCC because its antichains are countable. For every α<ω1​, the set Dα​={t:ht(t)≥α} is dense because the tree is well-pruned. The assertion MAℵ1​​ is precisely that a CCC forcing and a family of at most ℵ1​ dense sets admit a filter meeting all of them.
Apply it to these dense sets. A directed filter in a tree is a chain: two compatible nodes are comparable, since both lie among the well-ordered predecessors of a common extension. Meeting every Dα​ makes this chain cofinal, contrary to the defining absence of uncountable chains in a Suslin tree. Consequently

Start with a ground model containing a normal splitting Suslin tree T, and force with Add(ω,ω2​), the finite partial functions from ω2​×ω to 2. Such a starting model is relatively consistent with ZFC: the constructible universe has ◊, and ◊ implies the existence of a Suslin tree.
This Cohen forcing is Knaster. In an uncountable family of finite conditions, the delta-system lemma yields an uncountable family whose domains have one common root; thinning to common values on that finite root makes its members pairwise compatible. If P is Knaster and T is CCC, the product P×T is CCC: thin any uncountable family to pairwise compatible P coordinates, then use CCC to find two compatible T coordinates. Thus P forces that the old T is still CCC A name for an uncountable antichain would otherwise produce an uncountable antichain in this product by deciding its nodes.
Countable levels and the tree's splitting property persist, and the splitting argument from the previous part rules out a new cofinal branch in a CCC tree. Therefore the old T is still Suslin. Meanwhile the forcing adds at least ℵ2​ distinct reals, with all cardinals preserved, so 2ℵ0​≥ℵ2​.
We obtain a model with a Suslin tree and ¬CH. Hence existence of a Suslin tree does not imply the Continuum hypothesis. This uses Knaster forcing preserves Suslin trees, not a claim that arbitrary CCC forcing preserves them.

The forcing theorem has two central clauses. Definability: for each first-order formula φ, the relation p⊩φ(τ) on conditions and ground-model names is first-order definable in M. Truth lemma: for every M-generic G and every tuple of names in M,

Work internally in the ground model and write P=Levy(ℵ0​,λ). Conditions are finite partial functions with the coordinate-wise value bounds in the PDF, ordered by inclusion, so compatible conditions agree on their common domain and their union is a common strengthening.
Suppose there were λ many pairwise incompatible conditions. Each fixed finite domain carries fewer than λ possible functions: it uses finitely many ordinals below λ, and each coordinate has fewer than λ choices. Regularity therefore lets us select λ distinct domains. The finite-set form of the generalized delta-system lemma gives a subfamily of size λ with common intersection r. There are fewer than λ possible value assignments to the finite root r. Regularity gives a further subfamily of size λ agreeing on the root. Any two of its conditions have a union in P, contradicting incompatibility. Thus P has the λ-chain condition. Strong inaccessibility is more than is needed for this finite-support argument; regular uncountability suffices.
For each 0<α<λ and n<ω, requiring (n,α) to be in the domain is dense. For each ξ<α, requiring ξ to occur as a value at some (n,α) is also dense: choose a fresh n and extend the condition. Hence the generic union defines, for each such α, a surjection gα​:ω→α. Every ground ordinal below λ is therefore countable in M[G].

ℵ1M[G]​=λ.​
