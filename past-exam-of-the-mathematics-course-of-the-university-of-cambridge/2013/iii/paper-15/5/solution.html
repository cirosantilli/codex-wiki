The Bonnet-Myers theorem says that a connected geodesically complete n-dimensional Riemannian manifold, with n≥2 and Ric≥(n−1)κg for a constant κ>0, has
diamM≤κ​π​,M compact,∣π1​(M)∣<∞​.
(17)
The dimension and positive lower bound are essential: in dimension one the Ricci condition is vacuous, and a zero lower bound does not imply bounded diameter.
By Hopf-Rinow, any two distinct points have a unit-speed minimizing geodesic γ:[0,L]→M. Choose parallel orthonormal fields E1​,…,En−1​ perpendicular to its tangent T. For the endpoint-vanishing fields Vi​(t)=sin(πt/L)Ei​(t), the second variation of geodesic energy gives nonnegative index forms
I(Vi​,Vi​)=∫0L​(∣Dt​Vi​∣2−g(R(Vi​,T)T,Vi​))dt≥0.
(18)
Summing and using the Ricci lower bound produces the sine index-form bound for positive Ricci curvature:
0≤∑i​I(Vi​,Vi​)≤(n−1)∫0L​[L2π2​cos2(πt/L)−κsin2(πt/L)]dt=2n−1​(Lπ2​−κL).
(19)
If L>π/κ​, the last expression is negative, a contradiction. This proves the diameter bound. Hopf-Rinow makes closed bounded sets compact, so the entire manifold is compact.
Give the universal cover the pullback metric. Local isometry preserves its Ricci bound, and lifting complete base geodesics proves completeness of the cover. The same diameter and compactness argument applies there. A fiber of the covering is closed and discrete, hence finite in this compact cover; its cardinality is that of the fundamental group. This proves the final assertion.
For a counterexample that also breaks the diameter conclusion, use the incomplete positively curved strip with infinite diameter
M=(−π/4,π/4)×R,g=du2+cos2udv2.
(20)
The map Φ(u,v)=(cosucosv,cosusinv,sinu) is a local isometry to the round unit sphere: its coordinate derivatives are orthogonal, with squared lengths one and cos2u. Thus K=1 and Ric=g, satisfying the required lower bound with n=2, κ=1.
The meridian (u,v)=(t,0) is a unit-speed geodesic and reaches the excluded boundary at t=π/4, so this metric is incomplete. Every curve joining (0,0) to (0,L) has length at least ∫cosu∣v′∣dt≥∣L∣/2​, because cosu≥1/2​ on the strip. Therefore diamM=∞​ despite the positive Ricci bound. Completeness cannot be omitted.
