Combining metric compatibility with the torsion-free condition forces the Koszul formula:
2g(∇X​Y,Z)=Xg(Y,Z)+Yg(Z,X)−Zg(X,Y)−g(X,[Y,Z])+g(Y,[Z,X])+g(Z,[X,Y]).
(7)
Nondegeneracy of g proves uniqueness of the Levi-Civita connection. For existence, use the right side to define 2g(∇X​Y,Z). Expanding brackets shows that this expression is C∞-linear in Z, so it defines a smooth one-form; the musical isomorphism gives the required vector field. The same expansion shows ∇aX​Y=a∇X​Y, additivity, and ∇X​(aY)=X(a)Y+a∇X​Y, establishing the connection rules. Subtracting the formulas with X,Y exchanged gives ∇X​Y−∇Y​X=[X,Y]. Adding the formulas pairing ∇X​Y with Z and ∇X​Z with Y gives metric compatibility. Thus this construction has both required properties.
In coordinate vector fields the brackets vanish. Consequently the Christoffel symbols and coordinate derivative are
Γijk​=21​gkℓ(∂i​gjℓ​+∂j​giℓ​−∂ℓ​gij​),∇X​Y=(Xi∂i​Yk+Γijk​XiYj)∂k​​.
(8)
Repeated indices are summed. The displayed connection type in the source is best understood in its standard form on two vector fields; if the first input is an individual tangent vector at a point, the output lies in the tangent fiber at that point rather than in the space of global sections.
For the parallel metrics with a common Levi-Civita connection, let γ be a piecewise smooth path from the point x of equality to any y. Connected smooth manifolds admit such paths because coordinate balls are path connected. Parallel transport Pγ​ for the common connection is invertible and preserves both metrics. Therefore
g​y​(Pγ​u,Pγ​v)=g​x​(u,v)=gx​(u,v)=gy​(Pγ​u,Pγ​v).
(9)
Every pair of tangent vectors at y arises this way, so g​=g​ everywhere.
Dropping the agreement at one point removes the conclusion. For any constant c>0, g​=cg has the same Christoffel symbols. Nor must the two metrics be proportional: on Rn, n≥2, the constant metrics ∑i​(dxi)2 and 2(dx1)2+∑i≥2​(dxi)2 both have zero connection coefficients. In general write g​(u,v)=g(Au,v). Since both metrics are parallel, 0=(∇X​g​)(Y,Z)=g((∇X​A)Y,Z), so ∇A=0. Conversely, a positive g-self-adjoint parallel A makes the same torsion-free connection compatible with g​, proving equality of their Levi-Civita connections. Thus one-point agreement specifies A=I and forces it everywhere; without it, nontrivial parallel choices can remain.
