We prove Kantorovich duality theorem here by a positive-functional extension argument; it produces the minimizing transport plan at the same time as equality of the values. Let Y=X×X and c(x,y)=d(x,y). This is a bounded continuous function because X is a compact metric space. Write C(Y) for the real space of continuous functions on a compact space, and let
S={s∈C(Y):s(x,y)=f(x)+g(y), f,g∈C(X)}.
(23)
On this vector subspace define ℓ(s)=∫fdP+∫gdQ. It is well-defined: two representations differ by a constant in the first variable and the opposite constant in the second, and both measures have mass one. It is positive, since s≥0 implies minf+ming≥0 and hence ℓ(s)≥0. Also ℓ(1)=1.
Define the majorant envelope, which is a sublinear functional:
p(h)=inf{ℓ(s):s∈S, s≥h}(h∈C(Y)).
(24)
Constants majorize every h, and positivity gives minh≤p(h)≤maxh, so this quantity is finite. Taking approximate minimizing majorants proves subadditivity; rescaling majorants proves positive homogeneity. Moreover p(s)=ℓ(s) for s∈S, and subtracting s from a majorant proves
p(h+s)=p(h)+ℓ(s).
(25)
The dual value in the question is exactly
md​=sup{ℓ(s):s∈S, s≤c}=−p(−c).
(26)
Subadditivity at c+(−c)=0 gives md​≤p(c).
On S+Rc, assign the value ℓ(s)+tmd​ to s+tc. If c∈/S the representation is unique. If c∈S, then md​=ℓ(c), so the assignment is still well-defined. It is dominated by p: for t≥0, use tmd​≤tp(c)=p(tc); for t<0, positive homogeneity gives p(tc)=(−t)p(−c)=tmd​. Together with the translation identity, these verify domination in both cases.
The real Hahn-Banach theorem extends this functional to T:C(Y)→R with T≤p. If h≥0, then p(−h)≤ℓ(0)=0, so T(h)≥0. Thus T is a positive linear functional, with T(1)=1 and T(c)=md​. Positivity also gives ∣T(h)∣≤∥h∥∞​, so T is continuous. The Riesz-Markov-Kakutani representation theorem supplies a Borel probability measure π0​ on the compact space Y, satisfying T(h)=∫Y​hdπ0​.
For every f∈C(X), its pullback f(x) belongs to S, so ∫f(x)dπ0​=∫fdP. Likewise ∫g(y)dπ0​=∫gdQ. Uniqueness in the Riesz-Markov-Kakutani representation theorem shows that these are exactly the two marginal distributions. Finally, every feasible pair f+g≤c gives a lower bound for the cost of every transport plan, by integration. Our constructed plan achieves that bound:
∫X×X​d(x,y)dπ0​(x,y)=md​=π with marginals P,Qmin​∫ddπ.​
(27)
This Kantorovich duality by positive extension establishes the requested existence and equality without assuming an optimal plan in advance.
The metric structure additionally gives the Kantorovich–Rubinstein theorem formulation. For a feasible pair define h(x)=infy​{d(x,y)−g(y)}. The triangle inequality for d makes h one-Lipschitz continuous, h≥f, and h(y)≤−g(y). Hence the dual objective is at most ∫hdP−∫hdQ. Conversely (h,−h) is feasible for every one-Lipschitz h. Therefore
md​=supLip(h)≤1​(∫hdP−∫hdQ).
(28)
Normalizing h(x0​)=0 for a fixed x0​ gives a uniformly bounded equicontinuous family; it is closed and compact in the uniform topology by the Arzelà-Ascoli theorem. The objective is uniformly continuous on this family, so the supremum is attained as well. In particular, md​ is the first Wasserstein distance between the two measures.
