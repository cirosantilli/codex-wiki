The two identities. Set Fβγ​=∇β​Kγ​. The Killing equation says Fβγ​=−Fγβ​. The Levi-Civita connection has symmetric lower connection indices, so they cancel in the antisymmetric difference:
2∇β​Kγ​=∇β​Kγ​−∇γ​Kβ​=∂β​Kγ​−∂γ​Kβ​.​
(13)
Use normalized antisymmetrization, so
3K[α​Fβγ]​=Kα​Fβγ​+Kβ​Fγα​+Kγ​Fαβ​.
(14)
Put aσ=Kτ∇τ​Kσ. Contracting with Fβγ, the second term is aγFγα​ and the third is the same after using antisymmetry. Thus
3FβγK[α​Fβγ]​=Kα​FβγFβγ​+2Fσα​aσ.
(15)
Rearranging proves the Killing derivative contraction identity
Kα​(∇βKγ)(∇β​Kγ​)=3(∇βKγ)K[α​∇β​Kγ]​−2(∇σ​Kα​)(Kτ∇τ​Kσ).​
(16)
Restriction to a Killing horizon. On a Killing horizon, the generator K is normal to the horizon as well as tangent to its null generators. Consequently K[α​∇β​Kγ]​=0 there. One can see this without assuming hypersurface orthogonality away from the horizon: if the horizon is locally Φ=0, write its dual one-form as K=fdΦ+Φq near it; then K∧dK=0 on Φ=0. By the definition of surface gravity, aσ=κKσ on the horizon, and lowering that relation gives Kσ∇σ​Kα​=κKα​. The contraction identity becomes
Kα​FβγFβγ​=−2κ2Kα​.
(17)
Away from points where K vanishes, cancel a nonzero component of Kα​; at a regular bifurcation surface extend by continuity. Therefore
κ2=−21​(∇βKγ)(∇β​Kγ​).​
(18)
This is surface gravity from the Killing derivative; it concerns the horizon, not every point in the exterior.
Static spherical metric. Choose the Killing vector K=∂t​. Its dual one-form is −Adt, and the first identity immediately gives
Frt​=−2A′​,Ftr​=2A′​,
(19)
with all other components zero. Hence
FβγFβγ​=2grrgttFrt2​=−2ABA′2​.
(20)
Using the horizon identity and the simultaneous simple zeros,
κ2=r→r+​lim​4ABA′2​=4A′(r+​)B′(r+​)​,κ=21​A′(r+​)B′(r+​)​.​
(21)
Here the positive value is chosen for a regular outer Killing horizon with A,B>0 on the exterior side. This is surface gravity of a static spherical horizon. A different normalization K↦cK rescales surface gravity by c; when an asymptotically flat normalization is wanted, the time coordinate is chosen so A→1 at infinity.
Euclidean regularity and temperature. Let δ=r−r+​, a=A′(r+​) and b=B′(r+​). On the exterior side, define the proper radial coordinate R=2δ/b​. After Wick rotation, the near-horizon metric is
dsE2​=dR2+κ2R2dτ2+r+2​dΩ2+terms smooth in R2.
(22)
The radial-time plane is a polar plane with angular coordinate ϑ=κτ. Its circumference-to-radius ratio is κΔτ; the conical singularity disappears precisely when
Δτ=κ2π​.​
(23)
This is the Euclidean black-hole regularity condition. If a signed surface gravity convention is used, the period uses ∣κ∣.
In a thermal quantum field theory, imaginary-time periodicity is the inverse-temperature condition expressed by the KMS condition. Therefore smoothness identifies the Hawking temperature
TH​=2πκ​​
(24)
in units ℏ=kB​=1, or TH​=ℏκ/(2πkB​) when κ is measured as an inverse time. It provides the thermal interpretation of surface gravity in black-hole thermodynamics. The regular Euclidean construction describes an equilibrium thermal state; the collapse calculation of Hawking radiation in the last solution supplies the outgoing spectrum. The simple-zero hypothesis excludes an extremal black hole, for which this polar-plane argument changes.
