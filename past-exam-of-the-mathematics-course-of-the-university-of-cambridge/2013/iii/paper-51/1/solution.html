Use geometrized units G=c=1 and metric signature (−+++); τ is proper time. The Killing vectors ∂t​ and ∂ϕ​ of the Schwarzschild metric give the conserved specific Killing energy and specific angular momentum
e=mE​=(1−r2M​)t˙=1,h=r2ϕ˙​.
(1)
Put f=1−2M/r. In the equatorial plane θ˙=0, and normalization of the four-velocity gives
−1=−ft˙2+f−1r˙2+r2ϕ˙​2,r˙2=1−f(1+r2h2​)=r32Mr2−h2(r−2M)​.
(2)
Choosing the inward branch therefore yields
uμ=(r−2Mr​, −r3/22Mr2−h2(r−2M)​​, 0, r2h​).​
(3)
The divergence of the time component at the Schwarzschild event horizon is a coordinate effect; the inward radial component tends to −1.
Capture from infinity. To reach the event horizon from infinity, the radial square must remain nonnegative throughout r>2M. Equivalently,
h2≤r−2M2Mr2​for all r>2M.
(4)
Differentiating the right side gives 2Mr(r−4M)/(r−2M)2, so its minimum occurs at r=4M and equals 16M2. Thus
∣h∣≤4M​
(5)
is the necessary capture bound. At equality the radial numerator is 2M(r−4M)2. An inward particle arriving from larger radii approaches the unstable orbit r=4M only after infinite proper time, because r˙ is proportional to −(r−4M) near that orbit. Actual plunges from infinity require ∣h∣<4M. This is Schwarzschild marginally bound capture.
The origin-at-infinity hypothesis is important and is not explicit in the PDF. A particle already inside the angular-momentum barrier can plunge with larger ∣h∣. For example, h=10M and initial radius r0​=2.01M give positive radial numerator 7.0802M3, remaining positive as r decreases to 2M. This trajectory has e=1 and reaches the event horizon. Thus an unrestricted claim about every inward particle would be false; the bound is the intended capture-from-infinity statement.
Invariant collision energy. At a collision, the total four-momentum is Pμ=m(u1μ​+u2μ​). The invariant center-of-mass energy uses the covariant metric:
Ecom2​=−gμν​PμPν.
(6)
The PDF instead prints a raised metric multiplying raised velocities. That contraction is not a tensor scalar; the corrected expression above, or a raised metric with lowered momenta, is required. Since each four-velocity has norm −1,
Ecom2​=2m2(1−gμν​u1μ​u2ν​).​
(7)
Let Di​=2Mr2−hi2​(r−2M). For two inward trajectories the radial product is positive, and direct substitution into the Schwarzschild metric gives
gμν​u1μ​u2ν​=−r−2Mr​+r2(r−2M)D1​D2​​​+r2h1​h2​​.
(8)
Putting these terms over one denominator proves
Ecom2​=r2(r−2M)2m2​[2r2(r−M)−h1​h2​(r−2M)−D1​D2​​].​
(9)
Horizon limit and the upper bound. A cancellation-free way to take the limit is to write ai​=1+hi2​/r2 and r˙i​=−1−fai​​. As f→0+,
(1−fa1​)(1−fa2​)​=1−2f​(a1​+a2​)+O(f2).
(10)
Hence
1−g(u1​,u2​)=1+f1−(1−fa1​)(1−fa2​)​​−r2h1​h2​​=2+2r2(h1​−h2​)2​+O(f),
(11)
and therefore
r↓2Mlim​Ecom2​=m2[4+4M2(h1​−h2​)2​].​
(12)
For particles captured from infinity, ∣h1​−h2​∣≤8M, giving Ecom​≤m20​ in the horizon limit. For actual captured trajectories the inequality is strict, but the supremum is approached by h1​→4M− and h2​→−4M+. Their azimuthal starting positions can be chosen so that the trajectories meet. If particles may instead be prepared near the event horizon, the counterexample h1​=10M, h2​=−10M has limiting energy m104​; no universal m20​ bound then follows.
