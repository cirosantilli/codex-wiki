Use spin one-half states ∣↑⟩,∣↓⟩ with Sz=±1/2, so σi=2Si and H=−J∑n​(σnx​σn+1x​+gσnz​). At g=0, every ferromagnetic bond has its lowest energy −J when adjacent x spins agree. On the connected periodic chain the normalized ground states are
∣+x⟩⊗N,∣−x⟩⊗N,E0​=−NJ,​
(1)
where ∣±x⟩=(∣↑⟩±∣↓⟩)/2​. Their span is the two-dimensional ground-state subspace; any normalized superposition is another ground state. They are exchanged by the global spin-flip discrete symmetry generated by ∏n​σnz​.
As g→+∞, the field dominates and selects
∣↑⟩⊗N,E0​∼−NJg.​
(2)
The limiting polarized state is unique. At large finite g, exchange admixes virtual spin flips, so the product state is the limiting wavefunction rather than an exact finite-field eigenstate. The bulk expansion below gives E0​/N=−J[g+1/(4g)+O(g−3)]. The exact twofold degeneracy stated at g=0 should be distinguished from thermodynamic spontaneous symmetry breaking in the ordered phase: a finite chain at nonzero field can have a split pair of symmetry eigenstates.

Put nn​=cn†​cn​ and Pn​=∏m<n​(1−2nm​). The Jordan–Wigner transformation is Sn+​=Pn​cn​, Sn−​=Pn​cn†​ and Snz​=1/2−nn​. Since σnx​=Pn​(cn​+cn†​), Pn+1​=Pn​(1−2nn​) and (cn​+cn†​)(1−2nn​)=cn†​−cn​, adjacent bonds become

For distinct paired modes k=−k, choose cosθk​=ak​/rk​, sinθk​=−sk​/rk​ and θ−k​=−θk​. The rotation Uk​=e−iθk​σy​/2 satisfies Uk†​Mk​Uk​=rk​σz​, and its first transformed component is

At zero field, rk​=1 and ϵk​=2J for every k. The Ising-chain quasiparticle dispersion is flat: the bulk quadratic excitation costs no momentum-dependent energy. In spin language these are domain-wall excitations. A periodic finite spin configuration has an even number of domain walls, so the single bulk quasiparticle energy 2J does not imply an allowed isolated one-wall spin state; the first such closed-chain spin excitation costs 4J.

On a connected bipartite graph, every exchange bond can attain its lower classical energy −JS2. Set Si​=Sn on one sublattice and Sj​=−Sn on the other, for any unit vector n. This Néel state minimizes all bonds simultaneously and has
Ecl​=−JS2×number of bonds.​
(13)
The direction n∈S2 parametrizes the continuous orientation degeneracy; disconnected components can choose their orientations independently.
A triangular lattice illustrates geometric frustration. For three fixed-length spins on a triangle,
S1​⋅S2​+S2​⋅S3​+S3​⋅S1​=21​∣S1​+S2​+S3​∣2−23​S2.
(14)
The minimum requires their sum to vanish, giving coplanar 120-degree spins rather than antiparallel alignment on every bond. A three-sublattice pattern realizes this condition on the nearest-neighbour triangular lattice. Its energy per bond is −JS2/2, and global rotations produce equivalent classical states.

The Holstein–Primakoff transformation acts on the physical Fock states ∣n⟩, 0≤n≤2S, with the specified operator ordering. Its matrix elements are
Sz∣n⟩=(S−n)∣n⟩,S−∣n⟩=(2S−n)(n+1)​∣n+1⟩,S+∣n⟩=n(2S−n+1)​∣n−1⟩.
(15)
The unavailable endpoint states have zero coefficient. These formulas directly give [Sz,S±]∣n⟩=±S±∣n⟩. Moreover
[S+,S−]∣n⟩​=[(2S−n)(n+1)−n(2S−n+1)]∣n⟩=2(S−n)∣n⟩=2Sz∣n⟩.​
(16)
Thus the spin commutation relations hold in units ℏ=1. The Holstein–Primakoff occupation constraint is essential: the spin Hilbert space has dimension 2S+1, so unrestricted boson Fock space is not itself this finite-spin representation. The square root and oscillator operators must retain their order for the displayed matrix elements and endpoint conditions to agree.

Si​⋅Sj​=−Siz​Sjz​+21​(Si+​Sj+​+Si−​Sj−​).

H=−NJS2+JS∑⟨ij⟩​(ni​+nj​+ai​aj​+ai†​aj†​)+O(S0).

The vacuum of the diagonal quasiparticles contains original spin bosons: ⟨ak†​ak​⟩=vk2​. Thus the quantum depletion of Néel order gives
Ms​=S−2N1​k∑​(1−γk2​​1​−1).​
(24)
In one dimension 1−γk2​​=∣sink∣. Removing the global zero modes with an infrared cutoff, the continuum integral contains ∫dk/∣k∣ near both gapless points and diverges logarithmically as the cutoff is removed. This is a breakdown of the assumed Néel order reference, not a physical infinitely negative magnetization. It indicates that the one-dimensional ordered spin-wave expansion cannot maintain a finite staggered moment; it does not determine the exact spectral gap or all properties of the spin chain for arbitrary S.
At zero temperature in d dimensions the singular contribution scales as ∫0​kd−2dk. It is infrared finite for d≥2, permitting a finite quantum reduction and a self-consistent ordered spin-wave description in an appropriate regime. This differs from the positive-temperature contribution, where nB​(ω)∼T/ω yields ∫0​kd−3dk. Its divergence for d≤2 agrees with the Mermin-Wagner theorem for short-range continuous-symmetry models. Finite-temperature nonordering and the zero-temperature one-dimensional depletion argument are separate statements.

⟨qf​∣e−Hτ/ℏ∣qi​⟩=∫q(0)=qi​q(τ)=qf​​Dqexp[−ℏ1​∫0τ​(2m​q˙​2+V(q))dt].​

For a trajectory connecting the degenerate minima in infinite Euclidean time, the first integral gives mq˙​2/2=V(q). The quartic double-well instanton and its reverse are
qI​(t)=atanh2ω(t−t0​)​,qIˉ​(t)=−qI​(t).
(26)
Their action is
S0​=∫−aa​2mV(q)​dq=2amω​∫−aa​(a2−q2)dq=32​mωa2.​
(27)
The instanton fluctuation prefactor K has dimensions of inverse time. It incorporates nonzero Gaussian fluctuation eigenvalues and the translation zero-mode Jacobian; a conventional determinant expression is
K=2πℏS0​​​[det′(−∂t2​+V′′(qI​)/m)det(−∂t2​+ω2)​]1/2,
(28)
with common endpoint regularization and the prime removing the translation mode. Only its positive quantum tunnelling rate κ=Ke−S0​/ℏ is needed here.
In the dilute instanton gas, well-separated crossings alternate in direction. Integrating n ordered centers gives τn/n!. A path returning to the same well has n an even number, and one connecting opposite wells has n an odd number. The common leading harmonic endpoint factor follows from the supplied harmonic oscillator transition kernel:
A0​=πℏmω​​,⟨a∣e−Hτ/ℏ∣a⟩⟨a∣e−Hτ/ℏ∣−a⟩​≃A0​e−ωτ/2cosh(κτ),≃A0​e−ωτ/2sinh(κτ).​
(29)
Expanding these functions gives the sums over even numbers and odd numbers, with the position-kernel normalization A0​ that its abbreviated formula suppresses. Validity requires S0​/ℏ≫1, ωτ≫1 and κ≪ω, so typical crossing separation greatly exceeds an instanton width. Local anharmonic corrections replace the harmonic well energy by its perturbatively corrected value; the leading formula does not claim uniformly negligible relative error at arbitrarily large τ.
The two exponents identify the double-well tunneling splitting:
Eeven​≃ℏω/2−ℏκ,Eodd​≃ℏω/2+ℏκ,ΔE=2ℏκ.​
(30)
The superposition with an even spatial wavefunction is the lower state. Coherent quantum tunnelling removes the classical degeneracy, and the exponentially small splitting sets the long quantum tunnelling timescale.

For the new periodic potential, let κ be the single-neighbour hopping rate and Δϵ=ℏκ. It is determined by the barrier between adjacent minima; an unspecified periodic potential does not determine it from the preceding quartic potential's numerical action. The minimum spacing is now a.

⟨na∣e−Hτ/ℏ∣ma⟩≃A0​e−ωτ/2∑r,s≥0​r!s!(κτ)r+s​δr−s,n−m​.

A homogeneous static saddle minimizes f(ρ)=−μρ+gρ2/2 for ρ=∣ψ∣2≥0. With g>0 and μ>0,
ρ0​=μ/g,ψ0​=ρ0​​eiϕ0​,ϕ0​∈R/(2πZ).​
(36)
The mean-field Bose-Einstein condensate has a macroscopic coherent occupation of the uniform mode. Its continuous particle-number phase symmetry is ψ↦eiχψ, the circle group U(1) generated by particle number. Choosing one phase breaks it, and the saddle manifold is a circle. For μ≤0, the constrained minimum is the vacuum ρ0​=0, so the condensed saddle requires the stated positive-density regime.
Spontaneous symmetry breaking is understood through a phase-selected thermodynamic description: an exact finite-volume number eigenstate has vanishing field expectation. The resulting Goldstone boson is a gapless phase/sound mode, while phase gradients carry the superfluid velocity v=∇ϕ/m. This is the saddle-point conclusion requested, not a proof of true condensate order in every dimension or temperature. Long-wavelength phase fluctuations can invalidate the assumed order, as quantified below.

The density-phase action of a Bose gas follows directly from ψ=ρ​eiϕ:
ψˉ​∂τ​ψ=21​∂τ​ρ+iρ∂τ​ϕ,∣∇ψ∣2=4ρ∣∇ρ∣2​+ρ∣∇ϕ∣2.
(37)

S=∫[iρ∂τ​ϕ+8mρ∣∇ρ∣2​+2mρ​∣∇ϕ∣2+2g​(ρ−ρ0​)2−2gμ2​].
(38)
A uniform density fluctuation has nonzero quadratic cost g(δρ)2/2, whereas a uniform phase change has no energy cost. Thus the radial fluctuation is massive in the static quadratic-action sense, while the phase is massless. The number-phase conjugacy term iδρ∂τ​ϕ couples their dynamics: this does not imply an additional independent gapped quasiparticle branch in the nonrelativistic Bose gas.
The compact phase winding term iρ0​∫∂τ​ϕ vanishes for the smooth zero-winding phonon sector. Globally the phase can wind by 2π while the complex field remains periodic, so that term should not be discarded across all topological sectors without a further argument.

Put ρ=ρ0​+δρ and retain quadratic fluctuations in the smooth zero-winding sector. The quadratic density-phase action is
S2​=∫[2g​(δρ)2+iδρ∂τ​ϕ+8mρ0​∣∇δρ∣2​+2mρ0​​∣∇ϕ∣2].

2g​(δρ)2+iδρ∂τ​ϕ=2g​(δρ+gi∂τ​ϕ​)2+2g(∂τ​ϕ)2​.
(40)
The real Gaussian density integral, or its equivalent contour shift, contributes only a field-independent determinant. Absorb that normalization and the uniform saddle action into S0​. To this quadratic long-wave accuracy,
Z≃e−S0​∫Dϕe−Seff​,Seff​=21​∫0β​dτ∫ddr[g1​(∂τ​ϕ)2+mρ0​​∣∇ϕ∣2].​

The Gaussian extension of δρ to the full real line is a fluctuation approximation around positive ρ0​; it is not an exact replacement of the global density constraint. The compact field's vortex/winding sectors also lie beyond this smooth-phonon integral.
The Bose-gas phase-only action is a continuum harmonic chain with Euclidean inverse propagator ωn2​/g+ρ0​k2/m. Continuing to real frequency gives
Ek​∼cs​∣k∣,cs​=gρ0​/m​=μ/m​.​
(42)
Restoring ℏ gives Ek​∼ℏcs​∣k∣ for a wavenumber k. This is the phonon branch of the Bogoliubov spectrum. Keeping the omitted density-gradient term replaces g by g+k2/(4mρ0​) and yields Ek2​=cs2​k2+k4/(4m2) in the adopted units, so the full quadratic spectrum is ϵk​(ϵk​+2gρ0​)​ with ϵk​=k2/(2m).
The phase-only action also tests the condensate assumption. At zero temperature its equal-time phase variance has an infrared contribution ∫ddk/∣k∣, logarithmically divergent in one dimension; at positive temperature the zero Matsubara mode gives ∫ddk/k2, divergent in dimensions at or below two. Thus this same low-energy theory exposes the regimes where the mean-field condensate cannot describe true thermodynamic long-range order. It permits a finite infrared fluctuation at zero temperature for d≥2 and at positive temperature for d>2, within the remaining weak-coupling assumptions.
