Write the finite abelian group additively. A character of a finite abelian group is a group homomorphism χ:G→{z:∣z∣=1}. We first prove that there are exactly ∣G∣ such characters of a finite abelian group, without assuming a structure theorem.
Use extension of a character across a cyclic quotient. Given a subgroup H and a∈/H, let k be the least positive integer with ka∈H. The subgroup K=H+⟨a⟩ has k cosets of H. If ϕ is a character of a finite abelian group on H, choose any of the k roots λk=ϕ(ka) and define
ϕ​(h+ja)=ϕ(h)λj.
(46)
This is well-defined: two representations differ by an integer multiple of ka, and the root equation exactly cancels that difference. It is a character of a finite abelian group, and every extension arises from one of the k choices of λ. Build a chain from the trivial subgroup to G by adjoining elements. The character of a finite abelian group count multiplies by the same factor as the subgroup order at every step, so ∣G∣=∣G∣.
