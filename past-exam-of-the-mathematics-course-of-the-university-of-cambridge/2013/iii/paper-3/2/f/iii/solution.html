Use the projective-line realization from part (ii). The subgroup K of translations is abelian and normal in the stabilizer subgroup G of infinity. We verify both remaining conditions of Iwasawa's simplicity lemma, instead of concluding simplicity from transitivity alone.
Let R be generated by all conjugates of K in G+. It contains the matrices
Tt​=(10​t1​),Ut​=xTt​x=(1t​01​).
(21)
Here matrices act by fractional linear transformations. For t=0,
w(t)=Tt​Ut−1​Tt​=(0t−1​t0​).
(22)
In particular w(1)=x∈R, and w(t)w(1)=diag(t,t−1) acts as multiplication by t2. Squaring is a bijection of F∗, so every Ms​ belongs to R. Hence R contains G and x, and R=G+​.
Choose s=1. The commutator, with convention [u,v]=uvu−1v−1, is
[Ms​,Tt​]=T(s−1)t​.
(23)
As t varies, this gives all translations. Thus (G+)′ contains K, and normality makes it contain every conjugate of K. Since those generate G+, the group is a perfect group.
A sharply three-transitive action on nine points is a primitive group action, and this permutation action is a faithful group action. All Iwasawa hypotheses now hold, so
G+ is simple, of order 504.​
(24)
It may be identified with PSL2​(8)=PGL2​(8): the generators are fractional linear transformations and ∣PGL2​(8)∣=8(82−1)=504, while all nonzero field elements are squares. The simplicity proof above does not rely on assuming simplicity of that named family.
