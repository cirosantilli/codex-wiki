The divisor determines the bundle through its local equations. Choose an open cover on which V has a reduced holomorphic defining equation fi​, using fi​=1 on sets disjoint from V. Because V is a complex submanifold of codimension of a submanifold one, its ideal is locally generated by a coordinate; consequently fi​/fj​ is a nowhere-zero holomorphic function on each overlap. Inside the sheaf of meromorphic functions on a complex manifold, consider the locally free sheaf of rank one
OM​(V)∣Ui​​=fi−1​OUi​​.
(1)
These local descriptions agree on overlaps. With ei​=1/fi​, its transition functions of a vector bundle are specified by
ei​=fi​fj​​ej​.
(2)
The ratios satisfy the Čech cocycle condition, and the associated holomorphic line bundle associated to a divisor is [V]. The section 1=fi​ei​ is holomorphic and vanishes to order one along V. Replacing fi​ by ui​fi​, with ui​ a holomorphic unit, replaces the frame by ui−1​ei​ without changing the subsheaf of meromorphic functions. Refining the cover changes no local sections either. This proves choice independence up to isomorphism rather than merely producing a bundle for one cover.
For a complex manifold of dimension n, the canonical bundle is
KM​=⋀n(T1,0M)∗.​
(3)
It is the holomorphic line bundle of holomorphic top-degree differential forms. To compute it on Complex projective space, let S=O(−1) be the complex tautological line bundle, so that S∗=[H]=O(1) is the hyperplane line bundle. At a line ℓ⊂Cn+1, the tangent space is Hom(ℓ,Cn+1/ℓ). Tensoring the tautological quotient sequence by S∗ gives the Euler sequence on complex projective space
0⟶O⟶O(1)⊕(n+1)⟶T1,0Pn⟶0.
(4)
Taking the top exterior power gives detT1,0Pn=O(n+1); taking its dual bundle yields
KPn​≅[H]⊗(−n−1).​
(5)
Here a negative tensor power means the corresponding positive tensor power of the dual bundle.
The proposed sections over U form a sheaf: compatible maps into L glue uniquely, and their projections remain ϕ. Addition and multiplication by holomorphic functions are defined in the fibres of L, making this a sheaf of OX​-modules. If ei​ is a holomorphic local frame of L on Wi​⊂Y, every section over U⊂ϕ−1(Wi​) has the unique expression
θ(x)=a(x)ei​(ϕ(x)),a∈OX​(U).
(6)
Thus the sheaf is a locally free sheaf of rank one. Its transition functions of a vector bundle are those of L composed with ϕ, so the corresponding pullback vector bundle is precisely ϕ∗L. This proves invertibility locally and compatibility globally.
For the local blowup of a complex manifold at a point, work on the projective chart Yi​=0 and write
u=xi​,tj​=Yj​/Yi​(j=i).
(7)
All the incidence equations reduce to xj​=utj​. Therefore (u,(tj​)j=i​) gives a holomorphic coordinate chart isomorphic to Cn. On an overlap with Yℓ​=0,
u′=utℓ​,ti′​=1/tℓ​,tj′​=tj​/tℓ​(j=i,ℓ),
(8)
which is a biholomorphism where tℓ​=0. The incidence subset is closed in the product of two Hausdorff spaces and is covered by these charts; hence it is a complex manifold of dimension n. In each chart the exceptional fibre is u=0, so it is a smooth complex submanifold, with its induced projective coordinates giving
E≅Pn−1.​
(9)
Away from the origin the inverse of the projection is x↦(x,[x]), which is holomorphic; the projection is therefore a biholomorphism outside E.
To find the canonical bundle, pull back the nowhere-zero top form on Cn. In the chart above, its Jacobian determinant gives
π1∗​(dx1​∧⋯∧dxn​)=±un−1du∧⋀j=i​dtj​.
(10)
The sign depends only on the ordering of the coordinates. This is a section of KX​ with divisor on a complex manifold exactly (n−1)E: it has that vanishing order in every exceptional chart and no zeros elsewhere. A nonzero meromorphic section of a holomorphic line bundle with divisor D identifies its holomorphic line bundle with [D], by sending the local generator 1/fi​ of O(D) to the nowhere-zero frame obtained by dividing the section by fi​. Consequently
KX​≅[E]⊗(n−1).​
(11)
This also includes n=1, when the local map is an isomorphism and the exponent is zero.
For a general complex manifold, choose a coordinate neighbourhood U of P, with P sent to 0, and replace U by the inverse image of this coordinate neighbourhood in the local blowup of a complex manifold at a point. Glue this space to M∖{P} along U∖{P} using the preceding biholomorphism. The resulting charts give a complex manifold M and a holomorphic map ϕ; the exceptional fibre is Pn−1 and the map is a biholomorphism off that fibre. For completeness, the local projection is proper because its inverse image over a compact subset is closed in that subset times compact Complex projective space. This ensures the gluing is Hausdorff: separate points with distinct images downstairs, and points over P inside the local blowup. The atlas is second countable as well.
The top exterior power of the differential defines a global holomorphic bundle map ϕ∗KM​→KM​. Equivalently, it is a section of KM​⊗(ϕ∗KM​)−1. The same local Jacobian determinant has vanishing order n−1 on E, and the differential is invertible elsewhere. Applying the holomorphic line bundle associated to a divisor construction gives the canonical bundle formula for a point blowup
KM​≅ϕ∗KM​⊗[E]⊗(n−1).​
(12)

A connection on a vector bundle is a complex-linear map from smooth sections to vector-bundle-valued one-forms satisfying
D(fs)=df⊗s+fDs.
(13)
Choose a locally finite trivializing cover, its componentwise flat connections on a vector bundle Di​, and a subordinate smooth partition of unity ρi​. The formula D=∑i​ρi​Di​ is globally meaningful: each weighted term extends by zero outside its chart. Its Leibniz rule follows from ∑i​ρi​=1, proving every smooth complex vector bundle admits a connection.
Extend the connection on a vector bundle to bundle-valued differential forms by
D(β⊗s)=dβ⊗s+(−1)degββ∧Ds.
(14)
The curvature form of a connection is ΘD​=D2. Applying this twice to fs shows that the df terms cancel, so D2 is tensorial and defines an End(E)-valued two-form. With column coordinates for sections and connection one-form A, the local expression is D=d+A∧. Direct expansion on an arbitrary bundle-valued form gives
(d+A∧)2=dA∧+A∧A∧.
(15)
The terms containing a derivative of the argument cancel by the graded Leibniz rule. Thus Cartan curvature matrix equation is
Θ=dA+A∧A.​
(16)
Here multiplication includes matrix multiplication and the exterior product of form entries. For a frame change e′=eg, the connection one-form and curvature form of a connection transform as
A′=g−1Ag+g−1dg,Θ′=g−1Θg.
(17)
The trace is consequently frame independent. Also
Tr(A∧A)=∑i,j​Aij​∧Aji​=0:
(18)
the diagonal terms vanish and the off-diagonal terms cancel in pairs. Locally TrΘ=dTrA, and hence dTrΘ=0. The local primitives need not agree, but the two-form does. We obtain a global closed two-form TrΘD​.
The determinant connection on detE=⋀rE is defined intrinsically by
D(r)(s1​∧⋯∧sr​)=∑j=1r​s1​∧⋯∧Dsj​∧⋯∧sr​,
(19)
where the differential-form coefficient of Dsj​ is placed first. In a local frame, only the diagonal components contribute to the derivative of e1​∧⋯∧er​, so its connection one-form is TrA. A line-bundle connection has curvature dTrA, because a scalar one-form wedges with itself to zero. Therefore
ΘD(r)​=TrΘD​.​
(20)
To see independence of the de Rham cohomology class, write D1​−D0​=a, a globally defined endomorphism-valued one-form. The curvature difference formula and the same trace cancellations give the trace curvature transgression
TrΘD1​​−TrΘD0​​=dTra.
(21)
Their difference is globally an exact differential form, so their de Rham cohomology classes coincide.
For the Hermitian metric on a holomorphic vector bundle, use the displayed ordering of the local matrices and put B=(∂h)h−1. The identity ∂ˉh−1=−h−1(∂ˉh)h−1 gives
∂ˉB=(∂ˉ∂h)h−1+(∂h)h−1∧(∂ˉh)h−1.
(22)
The plus sign comes from applying the graded Leibniz rule to the one-form ∂h. By the derivative formula for a determinant,
TrB=∂logdeth.
(23)
The Hermitian positive-definite matrix h has positive real determinant, so this logarithm is an ordinary smooth real function. The trace of Chern curvature in these conventions is therefore
α=∂ˉ∂logdeth=d(∂logdeth)=−∂∂ˉlogdeth.​
(24)
It is locally an exact differential form and in particular closed.
A holomorphic local frame change multiplies deth by ∣detg∣2. A nowhere-zero holomorphic function has a local holomorphic logarithm, so ∂ˉ∂log∣detg∣2=0. Thus the preceding expression for α is independent of the frame and glues globally. For two Hermitian metrics on a holomorphic vector bundle, the function
f=logdeth0​deth1​​
(25)
is globally defined because the frame-change factors cancel. Their two-forms differ by d(∂f). Hence the class of α is independent of the metric. The two-form is generally imaginary-valued; the metric independence statement is in complex de Rham cohomology, or equivalently for the real form iα.

Use the normalization in which a Hodge metric is a Kähler metric whose Kähler form ω represents the image of an integral cohomology class in real de Rham cohomology. Equivalently, all its periods on integral two-cycles are integers. A convention using ω/(2π) only rescales the metric and does not change existence. An invariant metric on a complex torus is one preserved by every translation; its pullback to Cn has constant coefficients.
Let T=Cn/Λ carry a Hodge metric. Average its Kähler form over translations using normalized Haar measure:
ω0​=∫T​ta∗​ωda.
(26)
This averaging Kähler forms over a complex torus preserves reality, type (1,1), closedness and positivity. Indeed, for any nonzero tangent vector, the quantity being averaged in ω(v,Jv) is strictly positive. Every translation is homotopic to the identity, since a path from 0 to a supplies such a homotopy. Thus [ω0​]=[ω], as is seen either on de Rham cohomology or by integrating over two-cycles. The average is translation invariant by invariance of Haar measure and determines a Kähler metric g0​(v,w)=ω0​(v,Jw). Its class is still integral. The reverse implication is immediate. Therefore a Hodge metric exists exactly when an invariant one does.
Here is the Riemann bilinear criterion for a period matrix, with the signs kept explicit. For the period matrix of a complex torus, use real coordinates t1​,…,t2n​ along the lattice basis, so z=Ωt. An invariant real two-form is
ω=21​∑i,j​Qij​dti​∧dtj​,Qt=−Q.
(27)
Its period on the coordinate two-torus (i,j) is Qij​; these tori generate integral second homology. Thus integrality of the class is exactly Q∈M2n​(Z). Positivity of a Kähler form makes Q nonsingular. Because the lattice basis is a real basis of Cn, the complex matrix
T0​=(ΩΩˉ​)
(28)
is invertible: its rows recover the real and imaginary parts of the coordinates. In the coordinates (z,zˉ), the matrix of the two-form is T0−t​QT0−1​, whose inverse is T0​Q−1T0t​.
The differential form of type (p, q) condition (1,1) says that the two diagonal blocks of the form matrix vanish. Since the form is nonsingular, this is equivalent to the diagonal blocks of its inverse vanishing. Because Q is real, these inverse blocks vanish exactly when
ΩQ−1Ωt=0.
(29)
Set H=−iΩQ−1Ωˉt. Skew-symmetry and reality of Q give H∗=H, and the full inverse matrix is
T0​Q−1T0t​=(0−iHt​iH0​).
(30)
Inverting these blocks identifies the two-form explicitly:
ω=i∑a,b​(H−1)ba​dza​∧dzˉb​.
(31)
For a vector with complex coordinate column v, evaluation yields ω(v,Jv)=2v∗H−1v. Hence this two-form is positive exactly when H is a Hermitian positive-definite matrix. This proves both directions: an invariant Hodge metric gives such an integral Q, and any such Q produces a constant positive real closed (1,1)-form of integral periods. In particular,
T admits a Hodge metric⟺∃Q∈M2n​(Z), Qt=−Q, detQ=0, ΩQ−1Ωt=0, −iΩQ−1Ωˉt>0.​
(32)
As a sign check, for Ω=(I,iI) and Q=(0−I​I0​) the last matrix is 2I, and the associated form is (i/2)∑dza​∧dzˉa​.
For the specified two-dimensional complex torus, put
J=(01​−10​),s=2​,A=I+sJ.
(33)
The real and imaginary period vectors are independent because detA=3, so they do form a full lattice. Suppose a polarization matrix Q existed. Its inverse R=Q−1 is a rational skew-symmetric matrix, and hence has the block expression
R=(aJ−Bt​BcJ​),a,c∈Q,B∈M2​(Q).
(34)
The first condition of the Riemann bilinear criterion for a period matrix becomes
0=ΩRΩt=(a−3c)J+i(BAt−ABt).
(35)
Separating real and imaginary parts gives a=3c and
B−Bt=s(BJ+JBt).
(36)
The matrices on both sides have rational entries except for the factor 2​. Irrationality of 2​ therefore implies B=Bt and BJ+JB=0. For a symmetric two-by-two matrix the latter expression is (TrB)J, so TrB=0. The candidate Hermitian matrix is now
H=−iΩRΩˉt=AB+BAt−6icJ.
(37)
Since A=I+sJ, B is symmetric and traceless, and J is skew-symmetric, Tr(AB+BAt)=2TrB=0 and TrJ=0. Thus TrH=0. A Hermitian positive-definite matrix has strictly positive eigenvalues, hence strictly positive trace. This contradiction proves
C2/Λ admits no Hodge metric.​
(38)

Let Ap,q denote the sheaf of differential forms of type (p, q) printed in the PDF. Take the pointwise Hermitian inner product to be linear in its first argument, and use vol=ωn/n!. The bidegrees in the question specify the conjugate-linear Hodge star: it is uniquely characterized by
α∧∗β=⟨α,β⟩vol,α,β∈Ap,q.
(39)
Thus ∗(cβ)=cˉ∗β, and ∗ maps bidegree (p,q) to (n−p,n−q). If S is the complex-linear extension of the real Hodge star operator, then ∗=S∘complex conjugation. The operator S is real and S2=(−1)k(2n−k) on degree k=p+q, so
∗∗ψ=(−1)p+qψ.​
(40)
Using the complex-linear star instead would give a different bidegree, (n−q,n−p); keeping the convention explicit prevents that ambiguity.
The Hermitian Lefschetz operator is exterior multiplication by ω. Define Λ pointwise by
⟨Lα,β⟩=⟨α,Λβ⟩.
(41)
It lowers bidegree by (1,1). In a unitary real coframe with ω=∑j​ej∧fj, it is Λ=∑j​ιfj​​ιej​​, the corresponding sum of interior product of a differential form operators. This gives its existence and identifies it as a smooth operator. On a compact manifold, integrate the pointwise equality against vol to obtain
(Lα,β)L2​=(α,Λβ)L2​.
(42)
No integration by parts is needed for this order-zero operator. Thus Λ is both the pointwise and the global formal adjoint.
Write [A,B]=AB−BA, and let k denote the degree of the input form. The given Lefschetz commutator is [L,Λ]=(k−n)I. For r=1 the required formula is exactly this identity. If it holds for r, the commutator derivation identity gives
[Lr+1,Λ]=L[Lr,Λ]+[L,Λ]Lr.
(43)
The second commutator acts on degree k+2r, so the coefficient on the right is
r(k−n)+r(r−1)+(k+2r−n)=(r+1)(k−n)+(r+1)r.
(44)
This proves the commutator formula for powers of the Lefschetz operator
[Lr,Λ]=r(k−n+r−1)Lr−1.​
(45)
For injectivity of powers of the Lefschetz operator, r=0 is immediate. Suppose r≥1 and r+k≤n. If k=0 or 1, then Λψ=0, and Lrψ=0 implies
r(k−n+r−1)Lr−1ψ=0.
(46)
The scalar is nonzero, since k+r≤n makes k−n+r−1≤−1. Repeating with the smaller power shows ψ=0. For k≥2, induct on r+k, treating all r=0 cases as already established. The same commutator calculation yields, with c=r(k−n+r−1)=0,
Lr−1(LΛψ−cψ)=0.
(47)
The induction hypothesis applies to Lr−1 on degree k, since (r−1)+k<r+k and (r−1)+k≤n. Hence ψ=Lα with α=c−1Λψ of degree k−2. Moreover Lr+1α=Lrψ=0. Since (r+1)+(k−2)=r+k−1, induction also makes Lr+1 injective on that degree, so α=0 and ψ=0. All operators preserve restriction to open sets; the argument applies on every open set. Therefore Lr is an injective morphism of sheaves whenever r+k≤n.
On a Kähler manifold, the Kähler identities, with the positive form convention used above, are
[Λ,∂ˉ]=−i∂∗,[Λ,∂]=i∂ˉ∗.
(48)
Taking formal adjoints conjugates the scalar and reverses the order in the commutator. Since Λ∗=L, this gives
[L,∂ˉ∗]=−i∂,[L,∂∗]=i∂ˉ.​
(49)
To derive the Kähler Laplacian identity, put P=∂ and B=∂ˉ, so P2=B2=0 and PB+BP=0. Write {A,B}=AB+BA for an anticommutator. The Kähler identities say B∗=−i[Λ,P] and P∗=i[Λ,B]. Therefore
{P,B∗}=−i{P,[Λ,P]}=0,{B,P∗}=i{B,[Λ,B]}=0,
(50)
because expansion leaves only terms containing P2 or B2. Furthermore,
Δ∂​Δ∂ˉ​​=i{P,[Λ,B]},=−i{B,[Λ,P]}.​
(51)
Expanding these expressions and using PB=−BP makes them equal. Since d=P+B and d∗=P∗+B∗, the mixed anticommutators already vanish, and consequently
Δd​=2Δ∂​=2Δ∂ˉ​.​
(52)
Closedness of the Kähler form implies [L,P]=[L,B]=0. Using the adjoint identity above,
[L,Δ∂ˉ​]=B[L,B∗]+[L,B∗]B=−i(BP+PB)=0.
(53)
The equality of the three Laplacians therefore proves each Laplacian commutes with L.
For a compact Kähler manifold, the Dolbeault Hodge decomposition is the orthogonal decomposition
Ap,q(M)=Hp,q⊕im∂ˉ⊕im∂ˉ∗.
(54)
Here harmonicity for the Dolbeault Laplacian and for the Hodge Laplacian agrees by the preceding identity. If ∂ˉα=0 and α=h+∂ˉu+∂ˉ∗v, then ∂ˉ∂ˉ∗v=0. Taking its inner product with v gives ∥∂ˉ∗v∥2=0. Thus α is cohomologous to h. Conversely, a harmonic form is ∂ˉ-closed, and a harmonic exact form h=∂ˉu satisfies ∥h∥2=(∂ˉ∗h,u)=0. This proves existence and uniqueness of the harmonic representative and hence
H∂ˉp,q​(M)≅Hp,q(M).​
(55)
These spaces are finite dimensional by the ellipticity of the Dolbeault Laplacian on the compact manifold.
Complex conjugation commutes with the real operator Δd​ and interchanges the bidegrees (p,q) and (q,p). It is therefore a conjugate-linear bijection of the harmonic spaces, proving Hodge symmetry. The real Hodge star operator commutes with Δd​, as does complex conjugation; hence their composition, our conjugate-linear Hodge star, sends harmonic (p,q)-forms bijectively to harmonic (n−p,n−q)-forms. Its square is the nonzero scalar established above. This proves Hodge duality, and gives
hp,q=hq,p,hp,q=hn−p,n−q.​
(56)
For the final hard Lefschetz isomorphism on Dolbeault cohomology, take k=p+q≤n and r=n−k. This restriction is necessary to make the displayed power nonnegative. The Hermitian Lefschetz operator raises bidegree by (1,1), and the Lefschetz operator preserves harmonic forms; therefore
Ln−k:Hp,q⟶Hn−q,n−p
(57)
is well defined. It is injective by injectivity of powers of the Lefschetz operator, since r+k=n. Hodge symmetry and Hodge duality give hn−q,n−p=hq,p=hp,q, so it is a bijection between finite-dimensional spaces of equal dimension. Because [L,∂ˉ]=0, the map on harmonic representatives agrees with exterior multiplication by [ω]n−k on Dolbeault cohomology. We conclude
Ln−p−q:H∂ˉp,q​(M) ≅ ​H∂ˉn−q,n−p​(M),p+q≤n.​
(58)
For p+q>n, the corresponding valid statement is the inverse of the positive-power isomorphism from bidegree (n−q,n−p) to (p,q); a negative exterior-multiplication power is not defined.
