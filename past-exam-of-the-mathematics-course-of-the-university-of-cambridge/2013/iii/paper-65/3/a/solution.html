Let h be the water-layer thickness and z=0 the horizontal bedrock. In the hydrostatic approximation, with constant ice-interface pressure,
p(x,z,t)=p0​+ρw​g[h(x,t)−z],px​=ρw​ghx​.
(28)
The prescribed two-wall stress balance gives
δv​2μu​=−ρw​ghhx​,u=−Dhhx​,q=hu=−Dh2hx​,D=2μρw​gδv​​>0.​
(29)
In this subglacial current with constant viscous wall layers, the uniform-pressure ice roof is treated as a movable confining boundary; the closure neglects inertia in comparison with pressure and wall stress. The bulk may be turbulent, while the specified wall viscous boundary layer supplies the linear stress relation. A different turbulent drag law would give a different transport coefficient and similarity exponent.
Without melting, the depth-integrated continuity equation gives ht​+qx​=0, hence
ht​=D∂x​(h2hx​).​
(30)
To write this in the PDF's displayed plus-sign form, its constant must be
γ=−D=−2μρw​gδv​​.​
(31)
The signed value is essential. If the printed γ were interpreted as a positive diffusivity, its equation would drive water uphill and be backward parabolic. For example, linearizing around a uniform depth H would give a Fourier perturbation growth rate +γH2k2, whereas the derived physical equation damps it at −DH2k2. The plus-sign form is consistent only with the negative γ above.
