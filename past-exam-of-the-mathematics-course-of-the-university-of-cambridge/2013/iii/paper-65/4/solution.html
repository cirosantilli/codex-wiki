Use the planar geometry indicated by the supplied x,z equation and streamfunction; the source heat input is then measured per unit out-of-plane span. Let Θ=T−T0​ and define
β=μρ0​gαΠ​>0,w=βΘ.
(39)
This is the buoyancy part of Darcy law. Let Ch​ denote the constant heat capacity per volume consistent with the given transport equation; for the fluid heat-flux convention Ch​=ρ0​cp​. Porosity or matrix heat-storage factors, if retained, must be used consistently in this coefficient and the effective transport parameters.
For plume width b≪z, continuity gives u∼wb/z. Both uΘx​ and wΘz​ are consequently of order wΘ/z: lateral advection must not be discarded. Lateral thermal diffusion is of order κΘ/b2, while vertical diffusion is smaller by (b/z)2. The slender steady porous thermal plume model is therefore
uΘx​+wΘz​=κΘxx​,ux​+wz​=0,w=βΘ.​
(40)
Writing it in conservative form,
∂z​(wΘ)+∂x​(uΘ−κΘx​)=0,
(41)
and integrating across the plume, where Θ and lateral diffusive/advective heat transport vanish at large ∣x∣, proves
Fh​=Ch​∫−∞∞​wΘdx=constant.​
(42)
In steady state with no lateral heat loss and negligible vertical conductive flux, this equals the heat supplied at the source. Define Q=βFh​/Ch​=∫w2dx>0.
For characteristic velocity W and width b, flux conservation gives W2b∼Q and the advection-diffusion balance gives Wb2∼κz. Consequently
b∝z2/3,W∝z−1/3,Θ∝z−1/3,u∝z−2/3.​
(43)
The dimensional scales are b∼(κ2z2/Q)1/3 and W∼(Q2/(κz))1/3. The plume becomes relatively more slender, b/z∝z−1/3, away from the source.
To find the profile, put η=x/b(z) and ψ=−W(z)b(z)f(η), so w=Wf′ and
u=−3zWb​(f−2ηf′).
(44)
Inserting into uwx​+wwz​=κwxx​ gives
f′′′+3κzWb2​(ff′′+f′2)=0.
(45)
Its coefficient is constant under the derived scaling. Choose the width normalization Wb2=6κz. Integrating once with decaying velocity derivatives gives f′′+2ff′=0. A second integration gives f′+f2=1 after normalizing the centreline velocity to W; symmetry has f(0)=0. The decaying, positive solution is f=tanhη. Hence the hyperbolic-secant porous plume profile is
w=Wsech2(x/b),T−T0​=βW​sech2(x/b),ψ=−Wbtanh(x/b).​
(46)
Since ∫−∞∞​sech4ηdη=4/3, the precise amplitudes are
b(z)=(Q48κ2z2​)1/3,W(z)=(32κz3Q2​)1/3.​
(47)
They satisfy both Wb2=6κz and (4/3)W2b=Q. The derived u supplies lateral entrainment; setting u=0 would not reproduce this profile or satisfy continuity.
A heat-weighted head speed of a porous plume estimate follows by filling the column below a head height Zh​(t) with this steady profile. Its excess heat per unit height is
H(z)=Ch​∫Θdx=β2Ch​Wb​.
(48)
Conservation of the total injected heat gives Fh​t≃∫0Zh​​H(z)dz. Differentiating,
Z˙h​≃H(Zh​)Fh​​=∫wdx∫w2dx​=32​W(Zh​).​
(49)
Thus a heat-weighted head advances at a speed of order the local centreline velocity and decelerates as Zh−1/3​. With K=(3Q2/(32κ))1/3,
Zh​(t)≃(98​Kt)3/4.​
(50)
The factor 2/3 is the energy-conserving estimate for a truncated steady column; the foremost centreline parcels have characteristic speed W(Zh​) instead. The steady calculation does not resolve the transient nose or define an exact sharp temperature front. A truly axisymmetric point-source plume would require different geometry and cannot use this planar profile unchanged.
