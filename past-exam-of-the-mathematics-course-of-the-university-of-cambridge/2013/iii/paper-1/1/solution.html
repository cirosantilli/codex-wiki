The endomorphisms of the finite-dimensional vector space V over the complex numbers form the general linear Lie algebra L=EndC​(V) with the usual addition and scalar multiplication and Lie bracket [x,y]=xy−yx. This commutator is bilinear and antisymmetric, and expanding the six products verifies the Jacobi identity.
For a Lie subalgebra g=L1​, being an abelian Lie algebra means [g,g]=0. A nilpotent Lie algebra has γ1​=g, γj+1​=[g,γj​] eventually zero; a solvable Lie algebra has g(0)=g, g(j+1)=[g(j),g(j)] eventually zero. These are the lower central series of a Lie algebra and derived series of a Lie algebra, respectively. Nilpotence is a condition on the Lie bracket, and does not require every member to be a nilpotent endomorphism: a nonzero scalar multiple of the identity spans an abelian Lie algebra.
A flag of a vector space is an increasing chain of vector subspaces. The flag we construct is a complete flag, 0=V0​⊂V1​⊂⋯⊂Vd​=V, where dimVi​=i, and each Vi​ is an invariant subspace for g. We first prove the common-eigenvector assertion in the Lie theorem, by induction on dimg; the zero algebra is immediate. For nonzero solvable g, its derived algebra is proper, so there is a codimension-one ideal of a Lie algebra k containing [g,g]. Write g=k⊕Cx. By induction there are v=0 and a linear functional λ on k such that kv=λ(k)v for every k∈k.
Let W be the cyclic subspace spanned by v,xv,x2v,…. The commutator derivation identity and [k,x]⊆k show inductively that
kxjv≡λ(k)xjv(modspan{v,xv,…,xj−1v}).
(1)
Thus W and all its initial cyclic spans are k-invariant. If m=dimW, the first m cyclic vectors form a basis, W is also x-invariant, and trW​k=mλ(k). For k∈k, the trace of a matrix commutator gives
0=trW​[k,x]=mλ([k,x]).
(2)
Hence λ([k,x])=0, since the field has characteristic zero. The nonzero common weight space
Vλ​={w∈V:kw=λ(k)w for all k∈k}
(3)
is x-invariant: k(xw)=x(kw)+[k,x]w=λ(k)xw. The restriction of x to Vλ​ has an eigenvector, because C is an algebraically closed field. This is a common eigenvector for g. Its line is invariant, and repeating the argument on the quotient vector space gives the complete invariant flag. Equivalently, this proves simultaneous triangularization of a Lie algebra representation.
In a basis adapted to this complete flag, every member of g is upper triangular, so every member of its derived algebra is strictly upper triangular. Products of d strictly upper triangular matrices vanish, and each iterated Lie bracket of d such matrices is a sum of these products. Consequently the derived algebra is a nilpotent Lie algebra. We may therefore take L2​=[L1​,L1​]: it is an ideal, and the quotient Lie algebra L1​/L2​ is abelian. This also covers dimV=0.
