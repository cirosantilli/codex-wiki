Write Ph=Eh(X) and Pn​h=n−1∑i=1n​h(Xi​). A function bracket [ℓ,u] contains the measurable functions h satisfying ℓ(z)≤h(z)≤u(z) for every z∈T. Require its endpoints to be integrable and call P(u−ℓ) its L1(P) width. A sufficient condition is that, for every ε>0, finitely many brackets of width at most ε cover the whole class H. Under this condition the uniform strong law from finite L1 bracketing states
h∈Hsup​∣Pn​h−Ph∣⟶0almost surely.​
(1)
The conclusion holds outside a common measurable null set. If the supremum is not initially known to be measurable, this formulation means pathwise convergence on a measurable probability-one event; a pointwise separable function class, including the application below, has a measurable supremum. Pointwise brackets also ensure the sample inequalities hold simultaneously over the class.
To prove the result, choose a finite ε-cover [ℓr​,ur​], 1≤r≤N. If h belongs to bracket r, monotonicity of the empirical measure and of expectation gives
Pn​h−Ph≤Pn​ur​−Pur​+P(ur​−h)≤∣Pn​ur​−Pur​∣+ε,
(2)
and
Ph−Pn​h≤P(h−ℓr​)+Pℓr​−Pn​ℓr​≤ε+∣Pn​ℓr​−Pℓr​∣.
(3)
Consequently
suph∈H​∣Pn​h−Ph∣≤ε+maxr≤N​max{∣Pn​ur​−Pur​∣,∣Pn​ℓr​−Pℓr​∣}.
(4)
Apply the strong law of large numbers to these finitely many integrable endpoints. On a probability-one event, the maximum tends to zero. Repeat with ε=1/q, q∈N, and intersect the countably many probability-one events. The limiting supremum is bounded by 1/q for every q, hence is zero. This proves the uniform law of large numbers without a boundedness assumption on the class itself.
For the moment-generating function, use the empirical measure estimator
mn​(s)=n1​i=1∑n​esXi​,0≤s≤t.​
(5)
If t=0, this estimator and m are both identically one. Otherwise, X≥0 makes esX increasing in s, and esX≤etX provides an integrable envelope. The dominated convergence theorem shows that m is continuous on [0,t], hence uniformly continuous.
For any ε>0, choose a partition 0=s0​<s1​<⋯<sN​=t so that m(sr​)−m(sr−1​)≤ε for every r. If s∈[sr−1​,sr​], then esr−1​z≤esz≤esr​z for every z≥0. These endpoint functions form finitely many integrable function brackets with the required L1(P) widths. The just-proved uniform law of large numbers therefore gives
s∈[0,t]sup​∣mn​(s)−m(s)∣⟶0almost surely.​
(6)
Both functions of s are continuous; their supremum equals the supremum over a countable dense subset, so it is measurable. This proves uniform consistency of an empirical moment-generating function using only the observed sample.

For the piecewise constant probability density function, integrability forces the constants on both unbounded outer intervals to be zero. Use half-open intervals and set
φ(x)=1[0,1)​(x),ψ(x)=1[0,1/2)​(x)−1[1/2,1)​(x).
(14)
The Haar scaling functions and Haar wavelets are
φj,k​(x)=2j/2φ(2jx−k),ψl,k​(x)=2l/2ψ(2lx−k).​
(15)
For fixed j, each scaling function has squared integral 2j2−j=1, and different k have disjoint supports, proving that this family is orthonormal. Each wavelet also has norm one and integral zero. At a fixed level, distinct wavelets have disjoint supports. At different levels, their dyadic supports are either disjoint or the finer support lies in a half on which the coarser wavelet is constant. The finer wavelet has integral zero, so their inner product vanishes. Finally, a wavelet with l≥0 is supported inside a unit interval; it is orthogonal to its containing φ0,k​ because its integral is zero, and to all other unit scaling functions because their supports are disjoint. Thus both the scaling family at each fixed level and the complete stated Haar family are orthonormal sets.
The Haar refinement identity is
φj,k​=2​φj+1,2k​+φj+1,2k+1​​,ψj,k​=2​φj+1,2k​−φj+1,2k+1​​.
(16)
It shows that the closed scaling spaces Vj​ satisfy Vj+1​=Vj​⊕Wj​, where Wj​ is the closed span of the level-j wavelets. The union of the Vj​ is dense in L2(R): continuous compactly supported functions can be approximated in L2 by their dyadic cell averages, and such continuous functions are dense in L2. Hence the orthonormal Haar family is also a complete orthonormal basis.
For a locally integrable function, define the compact-support coefficient integrals
aj,k​=∫R​f(x)φj,k​(x)dx,bl,k​=∫R​f(x)ψl,k​(x)dx.
(17)
These exist without requiring f∈L2. The two formulas for the Haar approximation are
Hj​(f)=k∈Z∑​aj,k​φj,k​=k∈Z∑​a0,k​φ0,k​+l=0∑j−1​k∈Z∑​bl,k​ψl,k​.​
(18)
For j=0 the wavelet sum is empty. For every fixed j, these sums are locally finite, so they make sense pointwise and locally in L1. The refinement identities and their orthogonal two-by-two coefficient transformation establish equality by induction, even for merely locally integrable f. If f∈L2, this is also the orthogonal projection onto Vj​.
In particular, for δ=2−j and x∈Ik​=[kδ,(k+1)δ),
Hj​(f)(x)=δ1​∫Ik​​f(u)du.
(19)
Now impose the symmetry and monotonicity conditions. The limit zero and decrease on [0,∞) imply 0≤f(x)≤f(0) for x≥0. For k≥0, the cell average and every value inside Ik​ lie between f((k+1)δ) and f(kδ). Therefore
∫Ik​​∣Hj​(f)−f∣≤δ(f(kδ)−f((k+1)δ)).
(20)
Summing over the positive half-line telescopes and gives a bound δf(0). Reflection maps each dyadic cell to another dyadic cell up to endpoints. Since f is even, its Haar approximation is even almost everywhere as well, so the negative half-line has the same error. Consequently
∥Hj​(f)−f∥1​≤2δf(0)=f(0)21−j.​
(21)
The argument proves integrability of the difference, even if f itself is not integrable on the whole line. It is the symmetric monotone case of the Haar approximation error for a function of bounded variation.

For the unit-width box kernel, the order-zero local polynomial estimator minimizes, over constants a,
Qx​(a)=∑i=1n​K(hxi​−x​)(Yi​−a)2.
(22)
A common factor such as h−1 in these weights does not change the minimizer. Put Ix​={i:∣i/n−x∣≤h/2} and Nx​=∣Ix​∣. If Nx​>0, differentiating the quadratic gives its unique minimizer:
mn,hP​(x)=Nx​1​i∈Ix​∑​Yi​=∑i​K((xi​−x)/h)∑i​K((xi​−x)/h)Yi​​=mn,hK​(x).​
(23)
The last expression is the Nadaraya–Watson estimator. Every x∈[0,1] is within 1/n of some design point i/n. Since nh≥2 gives h/2≥1/n, that point is in the closed kernel window. Thus Nx​≥1 and the equality holds throughout the domain, including x=0 and x=1.
For the error bound, first take the usual bandwidth range 0<h≤1. The clipped window [x−h/2,x+h/2]∩[0,1] has length at least h/2. Any closed interval of length L in [0,1] contains at least ⌊nL⌋ points of the design {1/n,…,1}. Consequently
Nx​≥⌊nh/2⌋≥nh/4,
(24)
where the last inequality uses nh/2≥1. This is a window occupancy for an equally spaced regression design bound that remains valid at the boundary.
Let Lm​=∥m′∥∞​, the Lipschitz constant supplied by the bounded-derivative assumption. The bias is bounded by
∣Emn,hP​(x)−m(x)∣≤Nx​1​∑i∈Ix​​∣m(xi​)−m(x)∣≤Lm​h/2.
(25)
Independence and the variance bound give
Var(mn,hP​(x))=Nx2​1​∑i∈Ix​​V(xi​)≤Nx​σ2​.
(26)
By the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality and the triangle inequality,
E∣mn,hP​(x)−m(x)∣≤nh​2σ​+21​∥m′∥∞​h≤2(nh​σ​+∥m′∥∞​h).​
(27)
Thus κ=2 works uniformly in x and the design size in this bandwidth range. The same clipped-length proof in fact works for 0<h≤2.
There is a genuine omission in the unrestricted formulation: an upper restriction on bandwidth is necessary for the displayed 1/nh​ variance scale. Take n=1, m≡0, Y1​∼N(0,σ2) and h≥2. The window contains the sole observation for every x∈[0,1], and nh≥2 holds. Its mean absolute error is σ2/π​, whereas the proposed variance term is κσ/h​. No fixed κ can satisfy this for unbounded h.
A valid statement for every h>0 satisfying nh≥2 uses q=min(h,1). The clipped window has length at least q/2, so Nx​≥nq/2−1, while the preceding coverage argument gives Nx​≥1. If nq≥4, the first bound gives Nx​≥nq/4; if nq<4, the second does. Therefore the same bias and variance calculation proves the unrestricted correction
E∣mn,hP​(x)−m(x)∣≤nmin(h,1)​2σ​+21​∥m′∥∞​h.​
(28)
This mean absolute error of local constant regression bound reduces to the requested rate for ordinary small bandwidths and saturates its stochastic term when the window covers the whole design.
