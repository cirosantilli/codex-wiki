The word dynamically needs a qualification. Thermal equilibrium specifies ζeq​=1, but actual dynamical stability of binary mass transfer depends on the adiabatic stellar radius response exponent ζad​, which need not equal one. Stability after a rapid small mass loss requires ζad​>ζL​, because dlogM2​<0. The equilibrium relation alone does not supply this adiabatic response. As a formal counterexample to deriving a universal dynamical threshold from it, a radius model with a dimensionless entropy label S, R(M2​,S)=CM22​eS, can have equilibrium Seq​=S0​−log(M2​/M∗​), giving Req​∝M2​ but ζad​=2 at fixed S. At q=3/2, ζL​=4/3<2, so that model is dynamically stable despite the extrapolated q>4/3 condition. This shows the missing response hypothesis, not a claim that this toy radius law describes the given low-mass star.
A more relevant illustration is an ideal fully convective adiabatic stellar polytrope of index 3/2. At fixed entropy its polytropic mass-radius relation is R∝M2−1/3​, giving ζad​=−1/3 and a dynamical critical ratio q=2/3 under the same Roche lobe approximation. For example, q=0.9<4/3 can be unstable dynamically even though the equilibrium comparison has negative feedback. The requested 4/3 threshold is the intended result with instantaneous response R2​∝M2​; it is not a universal dynamical criterion. The subsequent cycle calculation uses the supplied equilibrium sequence and presumes a physically stable, sufficiently slow transfer regime.
Two important angular-momentum-loss mechanisms are gravitational-wave emission from a binary system and magnetic braking of a binary star. Gravitational waves directly remove orbital energy and angular momentum. A magnetized stellar wind removes donor spin angular momentum with a large magnetic lever arm; tidal synchronization replenishes that spin from the orbit, so the net effect is orbital angular-momentum loss. Treating the wind mass loss as small compared with the Roche-transfer flow is consistent with the conservative approximation used here; otherwise the mass and angular-momentum equations must both be modified. Neglecting the stored spin does not eliminate the torque transmitted through it.
Such losses shrink the Roche lobe and offset the donor's tendency to move inside it. Maintaining contact on the equilibrium sequence requires R˙L​/RL​=M˙2​/M2​, hence the binary mass-transfer contact equation becomes

The stipulated circular orbit is an important idealization. Isotropic loss that is slow compared with the orbital period can preserve circularity. If it is truly impulsive compared with that period, an initially circular point-mass orbit instead acquires eccentricity. With unchanged velocity and position, its new specific orbital energy is ε′=GM/(2a)−G(M−δm)/a=−G(M−2δm)/(2a) and its squared specific angular momentum stays h2=GMa. The Kepler orbit energy and eccentricity relations then give a′=a(M−δm)/(M−2δm) and e′=δm/(M−δm). The latter still has the same first-order separation increase. The question assumes circularity; its first-order circular budget is consistent with adiabatic orbital mass loss or with subsequent circularization that changes the separation only at second order. Work symbolically with δm, since the printed numerical layer mass has no explicit unit.
