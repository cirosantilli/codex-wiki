Sample Brownian motion at integer times and put Zn​=Bn​/n​. Every Zn​ has normal distribution N(0,1), although the Zn​ are correlated. Let
L=limsupn→∞​Zn​.
(42)
For every fixed integer m,
L=limsupn→∞​n​Bn​−Bm​​,
(43)
because Bm​/n​→0 almost surely. The right-hand expression depends only on the future independent increments Bj​−Bj−1​ with j>m. Thus {L≥a} is, up to a null set, a tail event of those independent increments, and its probability is zero or one by the Kolmogorov zero-one law.
For any finite real a, every Zn​ exceeds a with the same positive probability pa​=P(N(0,1)≥a). For each N,
P(⋃n≥N​{Zn​≥a})≥pa​.
(44)
Taking the decreasing intersection over N shows that Zn​≥a infinitely often with probability at least pa​. This event implies L≥a, so P(L≥a)>0. The zero-one law makes it one. Intersecting over positive integers a gives L=+∞ almost surely. The continuous-time limit superior is at least the one along integers, hence
t→∞limsup​t​Bt​​=+∞almost surely.​
(45)
This proves that Brownian fluctuations exceed the square-root scale without needing the lower bound in the law of the iterated logarithm.
