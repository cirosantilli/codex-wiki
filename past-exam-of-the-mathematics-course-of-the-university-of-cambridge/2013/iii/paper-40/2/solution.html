To see why this solves the investment-consumption problem, optimize directly over Brownian portfolio exposures, writing y=σ(x)θ. The portfolio wealth equation becomes dw=ydW+(rw+κy−c)dt, which no longer contains X. With nonzero volatility the same admissible exposure processes are available for every factor state, so the attainable wealth-consumption pairs, and therefore the value, are exactly those of the Merton consumption-investment problem. This also excludes extraneous solutions of the linear equation without imposing artificial factor boundary data.
The printed boundedness assumptions do not ensure nonzero volatility or a finite value. The unsimplified HJB equation remains the correct control equation at a zero of σ. There the hedge term vanishes; if μ=r, the riskless excess return gives arbitrage with unrestricted holdings. Under μ−r=σκ, a zero-volatility state offers only the bank exposure at that instant. For example σ≡0, μ≡r satisfies this relation for any chosen κ, but its value uses γ0​=[ρ−pr]/R, not a fictitious nonzero risk premium. The constant-value formula using κ presupposes access to the Brownian exposure, with sufficient integrability for the corresponding holdings. Additive utility constants again only shift V by a constant divided by ρ.
