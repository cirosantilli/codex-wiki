Pricing kernel and replication. In the nondegenerate Black-Scholes model, put κ=(μ−r)/σ and normalize the state-price density by ζ0​=1. The process is
ζt​=exp{−rt−κWt​−21​κ2t},dζt​=−ζt​(rdt+κdWt​).​
(45)
The density ertζt​ changes probability to the risk-neutral measure. Under that measure Wt​+κt is a Brownian motion and the stock drift is r. Thus an integrable contingent claim H has time-t price
Pt​=ζt​E[ζT​H∣Ft​]​=e−r(T−t)EQ[H∣Ft​].​
(46)
In the usual augmented natural Brownian filtration, the Brownian martingale representation theorem supplies a replicating strategy; this is the complete market assumption. For a nonnegative admissible trading strategy without intermediate consumption, the state-price budget constraint is E[ζT​wT​]≤w0​, with equality for a fully invested replicated claim. In particular
EζT​=e−rT,E[ζT​ST​]=S0​.
(47)
Feasibility and the largest slope. First take the intended regime r>0, T>0, w0​,S0​>0, and the usual nonnegative portfolio wealth constraint. The terminal wealth floor is
ξ=w0​+α(ST​−S0​).
(48)
For any feasible claim the state-price budget constraint implies
w0​≥E[ζT​wT​]≥E[ζT​ξ]=w0​e−rT+αS0​(1−e−rT).
(49)
Therefore α≤w0​/S0​. Conversely, when 0≤α≤w0​/S0​, hold α shares and put the remaining w0​−αS0​ in the continuous-time bank account. Its terminal portfolio wealth is
αST​+(w0​−αS0​)erT≥αST​+(w0​−αS0​)=ξ.
(50)
Hence
αˉ=S0​w0​​.​
(51)
At equality, ξ=αˉST​ has cost exactly w0​. Positivity of the state-price density forces wT​=αˉST​ almost surely: any strict improvement would cost more. Invest all initial portfolio wealth in w0​/S0​ shares and hold them until T.
Optimal payoff below the feasibility limit. For α<αˉ the floor is strictly positive, and its price is strictly less than w0​. Assume the utility function is increasing, differentiable and strictly concave, satisfies the Inada conditions, and has the integrability needed for the finite-budget optimization. These are the usual hypotheses implicit in using inverse marginal utility. For each positive multiplier λ, maximize
U(y)−λζT​yover y≥ξ
(52)
separately in every state. Its derivative decreases through zero at I(λζT​), so the floored marginal utility optimizer is
wT∗​=max{w0​+α(ST​−S0​),I(λζT​)}.​
(53)
The multiplier is characterized by
E[ζT​max{ξ,I(λζT​)}]=w0​,λ>0.​
(54)
Under the stated integrability hypotheses the left side is continuous and decreasing, tends to the floor cost as λ↑∞, and tends to infinity as λ↓0. It is strictly decreasing wherever it exceeds the floor cost: on the event where the inverse-marginal-utility payoff exceeds the floor, a larger multiplier strictly reduces that payoff. Therefore the budget determines a unique finite multiplier. For CRRA utility the inverse marginal utility is I(y)=y−1/R; lognormal moments provide the needed integrability.
For completeness, pointwise maximality gives, for any feasible competing terminal portfolio wealth Y,
U(Y)−λζT​Y≤U(wT∗​)−λζT​wT∗​.
(55)
Taking expectations and using E[ζT​Y]≤w0​=E[ζT​wT∗​] proves optimality. Strict concavity gives uniqueness of the terminal claim. Its price process
wt∗​=ζt​E[ζT​wT∗​∣Ft​]​
(56)
is nonnegative and starts from w0​; claim replication therefore turns the payoff optimizer into an admissible portfolio.
What the missing interest-rate hypothesis changes. The PDF does not explicitly assume r>0 or give the utility and admissibility hypotheses above. These omissions matter. At r=0, under nonnegative admissibility, the largest feasible slope remains w0​/S0​: for larger slopes the positive-part floor costs strictly more than w0​, since ST​ has support (0,∞). But every α≤w0​/S0​ already gives floor cost exactly w0​, so the only feasible terminal claim is ξ. There is no spare budget for an inverse-marginal-utility improvement. For example α=0, κ=0 and CRRA utility make E[ζT​max{w0​,I(λζT​)}]>w0​ for every finite λ. The prescribed positive finite multiplier then does not exist.
For negative r, nonnegative admissibility requires the effective floor ξ+​=max{ξ,0}. Define its cost
G(α)=E[ζT​(w0​+α(ST​−S0​))+​].
(57)
This is continuous and convex. For α≤w0​/S0​ it equals w0​e−rT+αS0​(1−e−rT), exceeding w0​ below the endpoint. At α0​=w0​/S0​, G(α0​)=w0​ and G′(α0​)=S0​(1−e−rT)<0. Above that endpoint the lognormal stock gives strict convexity, and G(α)→∞. Thus there is a unique second root α1​>α0​ of G(α1​)=w0​, the feasible slopes are [α0​,α1​], and the largest is α1​. Equivalently, for α>α0​ the floor price is α times the European call option price with strike S0​−w0​/α. At the upper endpoint replicate ξ+​; in the interval with strict budget slack the same floored marginal utility optimizer applies with ξ+​.
If instead portfolio wealth may be negative and utility is defined on all real portfolio wealth, the floor itself has its affine replication cost: at r=0 every slope is feasible, and at negative r every α≥w0​/S0​ is feasible. There is then no largest finite slope. The intended stock-only endpoint and strict-slack optimizer use positive interest and standard nonnegative admissibility.
