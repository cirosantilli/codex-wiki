A self-avoiding walk is a finite sequence of graph vertices v0​,…,vn​ in which successive vertices are adjacent and no graph vertex is repeated; its length is n, the number of edges. Include the zero-length walk.
Split a length-m+n walk from v into its first m edges and remaining n edges. After fixing the first segment, the number of possible suffixes is at most σn​, since forgetting the prohibition on revisiting its earlier vertices can only increase that number. Thus σm+n​(v)≤σm​(v)σn​, and taking the supremum gives
σm+n​≤σm​σn​​.

Every finite-radius ball is finite because degrees are bounded. An infinite connected graph has vertices arbitrarily far from each root, so shortest paths give σn​(v)≥1. Also σn​≤Δ(Δ−1)n−1 for n≥1: after the first edge, an immediate reversal is forbidden. In particular Δ≥2 and these suprema are finite.
