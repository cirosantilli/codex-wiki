For each fixed z, choose the convex perturbation function
fz​(x,u)=k(x)+h(z+u−x).​
(14)
It is jointly convex and has marginal
pz​(u)=infx​[k(x)+h(z+u−x)]=F(z+u).
(15)
Because h and k are nonnegative and finite everywhere,
0≤F(w)≤k(0)+h(w)<∞
(16)
for every w. The inf-convolution is convex: for approximate decompositions of w1​,w2​, take their convex combination and use convexity of both costs; then let their approximation errors tend to zero. Thus F is a finite convex function on all of Rn, hence continuous everywhere. In particular pz​ is proper and finite near zero, so the qualification in the previous solution applies at every z:
xinf​[k(x)+h(z−x)]=ymax​[−fz∗​(0,y)].​
(17)
This is strong duality for a finite-valued infimal convolution.
No primal attainment was used. For example k(x)=ex and h(x)=0 meet all the stated assumptions, but F(z)=0 is approached only as x→−∞. This illustrates why a proof based on an assumed minimizing decomposition would be incomplete. The printed functions are real-valued everywhere, not merely extended-real; that distinction supplies continuity and the strong-duality qualification.
