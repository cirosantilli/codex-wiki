Compute the convex conjugate of fz​ at a general pair (v,y). With w=z+u−x, the independent variables become (x,w) and u=w+x−z, so
fz∗​(v,y)​=x,wsup​{⟨v+y,x⟩+⟨y,w⟩−⟨y,z⟩−k(x)−h(w)}=k∗(v+y)+h∗(y)−⟨y,z⟩.​
(18)
Therefore the Lagrange dual function is
ψz​(y)=⟨y,z⟩−k∗(y)−h∗(y).​
(19)
Using strong duality from the preceding solution gives
F(z)=ysup​[⟨y,z⟩−k∗(y)−h∗(y)],F=(k∗+h∗)∗.​
(20)
Equivalently the conjugate of an infimal convolution is F∗=k∗+h∗, and the continuous convex F equals its biconjugate.
For practical subgradient computation, minimize the known convex dual objective
k∗(y)+h∗(y)−⟨z,y⟩.
(21)
Every optimizer, and only an optimizer, belongs to ∂F(z) by Fenchel–Young inequality. The full characterization is
∂F(z)=argymax​ψz​(y)={y:z∈∂(k∗+h∗)(y)}.​
(22)
This is infimal-convolution dual subgradients; the set is nonempty because F is finite convex everywhere. If both conjugates are differentiable at the optimizer, solve ∇k∗(y)+∇h∗(y)=z. For nonsmooth conjugates, use the displayed aggregate subdifferential or a convex minimization algorithm. Replacing it by ∂k∗(y)+∂h∗(y) requires the usual subdifferential sum rule qualification; it is not automatically justified solely by knowing the two conjugates.
