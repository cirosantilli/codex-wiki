Use the nonnegative-pairing dual cone K∗={y:⟨y,s⟩≥0 for all s∈K}. The Lagrangian is L(x,y)=cTx−yT(Ax−b) for y∈K∗. Its infimum over unrestricted x is finite exactly when ATy=c. Therefore the conic dual problem is

The final equality uses the self-dual cone assumption.
For the canonical self-concordant barrier, use its logarithmically homogeneous barrier normalization
F(ts)=F(s)−νlogt,⟨∇F(s),s⟩=−ν.
(41)
At parameter μ>0, the primal barrier problem is
minAx−b∈intK​{cTx+μF(Ax−b)}.
(42)
Write F†​(y)=F∗(−y), the Legendre dual cone barrier. The matching dual barrier problem is
minATy=cy∈intK​​{−bTy+μF†​(y)}.
(43)
Defining the dual barrier this way is valid generally; self-duality of the cone alone does not assert that an arbitrarily selected barrier equals its Legendre dual.
The joint central path characterization is
Ax−b=s∈intK,ATy=c,y∈intK,y=−μ∇F(s).​
(44)
The primal stationarity equation is c+μAT∇F(s)=0, exactly the dual feasibility equation after defining y. For the dual relation, logarithmic homogeneity gives ∇F(s/μ)=−y, so ∇F†​(y)=−s/μ. Thus dual stationarity is −b+μ∇F†​(y)+Ax=0, giving the same primal equation. At these points,
cTx−bTy=sTy=νμ.​
(45)
Newton step. At a chosen target parameter μ​>0, define
rp​=Ax−b−s,rd​=ATy−c,rc​=y+μ​∇F(s),H=∇2F(s).
(46)
Linearizing the three equality conditions gives the central-path Newton system
AΔx−ΔsATΔyΔy+μ​HΔs​=−rp​,=−rd​,=−rc​.​​
(47)
Eliminating the slack and dual directions yields
μ​ATHAΔxΔsΔy​=rd​−ATrc​−μ​ATHrp​,=AΔx+rp​,=−rc​−μ​HΔs.​​
(48)
The barrier Hessian is positive definite. Full column rank of A therefore makes ATHA positive definite and the reduced solve unique. Use a damped step with s+αΔs and y+αΔy remaining interior; a full Newton step need not do so.
At an exact point with parameter μ, choosing μ​<μ gives rp​=rd​=0 and rc​=(μ​−μ)∇F(s), the usual predictor towards the next path point. Infinitesimally,
dμdx​=−μ1​(ATHA)−1AT∇F(s),dμds​=Adμdx​,dμdy​=−∇F(s)−μHdμds​.
(49)
The path definition presupposes interior feasibility and attainment of the barrier problems; strict primal-dual feasibility is a standard sufficient setting. The printed full-rank condition by itself is insufficient. For example K=R+2​, A=(1,−1)T, b=0 and c=0 give the sole primal feasible point x=0, with no interior slack at all, despite full column rank. Rank guarantees the Newton solve at an interior point, not existence of the central path.
