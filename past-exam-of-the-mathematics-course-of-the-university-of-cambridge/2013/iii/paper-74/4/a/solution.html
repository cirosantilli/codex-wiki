Past exam of the mathematics course of the University of Cambridge / 2013 / iii / Paper 74 / 4 / a / Solution

 ... Past exam of the mathematics course of the University of Cambridge 2013 iii Paper 74 4 a
OurBigBook.com Words: 162
A decidable B has injective action maps. First prove that evaluation at 1 distinguishes equivariant functions. Suppose f(1,a)=g(1,a) for every a. Fix m∈M and choose p,q with pmq=p. For each a, equivariance gives
p⋅f(m,a)=f(pm,p⋅a)=f(pm,pm⋅(q⋅a))=pm⋅f(1,q⋅a).
(19)
The same equation holds for g, so these values agree. Cancel the injective action of p on B to obtain f(m,a)=g(m,a). Hence f=g. This proves the hinted contrapositive and, more precisely, injectivity of the trace map f↦(a↦f(1,a)).
Now suppose m⋅f=m⋅g in the exponential. Evaluating this equality at (1,m⋅a) gives
f(m,m⋅a)=g(m,m⋅a),m⋅f(1,a)=m⋅g(1,a).
(20)
Cancel the action of m on B. The trace maps agree, so the preceding argument gives f=g. Every action map on BA is therefore injective. By the introductory criterion, BA is decidable whenever B is under the specified monoid condition. Neither cancellation in M nor injectivity of its action on A is assumed.
