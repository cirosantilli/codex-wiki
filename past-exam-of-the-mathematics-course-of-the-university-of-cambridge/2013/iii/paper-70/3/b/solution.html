Set q=logψ and use the sign-appropriate Cole-Hopf transformation f=2εqθ​. Direct differentiation gives
fz​−ffθ​−εfθθ​=2ε∂θ​(qz​−εqθθ​−εqθ2​)=2ε∂θ​(ψψz​−εψθθ​​).
(42)
Thus the heat equation ψz​=εψθθ​ implies the viscous Burgers equation with the required negative nonlinear sign. Conversely, vanishing of the Burgers residual makes the final quotient depend only on z, and a multiplicative time-dependent factor in ψ removes it. The algebraic substitution works for any nonzero ε where ψ=0.
For a forward dissipative initial-value solution, take ε>0 and z>0. This is essential for the supplied heat kernel: at ε<0, z>0, its real Gaussian integral diverges even for ψ(0,ϕ)=1. More generally that kernel requires εz>0. The physical viscosity convention selects the positive case; the mere condition ε=0 does not justify a forward Gaussian function formula.
Integrating ∂θ​logψ(0,θ)=f0​(θ)/(2ε) gives a positive continuous initial factor, normalized as
ψ(0,ϕ)={1,eUϕ/(2ε),​ϕ≤0,ϕ≥0.​
(43)
Its heat kernel convolution converges for both signs of U, since a Gaussian function dominates the one-sided exponential. Define
J(b)=∫b∞​e−y2/(4εz)dy,S=4εU(2θ+Uz)​.
(44)
The left half-line contributes J(θ); completing the square on the right half-line gives eSJ(−θ−Uz). Thus
ψ(z,θ)=4πεz​J(θ)+eSJ(−θ−Uz)​.
(45)
When differentiating, the two moving-endpoint Gaussian function terms cancel, because
eSe−(θ+Uz)2/(4εz)=e−θ2/(4εz).
(46)
Only ∂θ​eS=(U/(2ε))eS remains in the logarithmic derivative. The viscous Burgers step solution with negative flux is therefore
f(z,θ)=1+αe−SU​,α=J(−θ−Uz)J(θ)​.​
(47)
The denominator is strictly positive, so f lies between 0 and U, irrespective of the sign of U. Its initial limits away from θ=0 are the required step.
For the spatial limits, the Gaussian function tails must be compared with eS; inspecting that exponential alone gives the wrong inference when U<0. As b→+∞, the complementary error function asymptotic gives
J(b)∼b2εz​e−b2/(4εz),J(b)→4πεz​(b→−∞).
(48)
At θ→+∞, J(θ)e−S tends to zero even for U<0, because its quadratic decay dominates any exponential linear in θ. Hence αe−S→0. At θ→−∞, use the identity above to obtain
eSJ(−θ−Uz)∼−θ−Uz2εz​e−θ2/(4εz)→0,
(49)
while J(θ) approaches its full Gaussian integral. Thus
f(z,θ)→U (θ→+∞),f(z,θ)→0 (θ→−∞),​
(50)
for both signs of U.
Since J is strictly decreasing, α=1 holds exactly when θ=−θ−Uz. At this point S=0, so the midpoint symmetry of a viscous Burgers step gives
α=1⟺θ=−Uz/2,f=U/2.​
(51)
More generally the exact symmetry is f(z,−Uz−θ)=U−f(z,θ). For U>0, the midpoint follows the inviscid shock trajectory. For U<0, it is instead the center of an expanding fan, not a shock.
The vanishing viscosity approximation makes the comparison precise. For fixed z>0 and U>0, near θ=−Uz/2 both tail integrals tend to the full Gaussian integral, so α→1 and
f∼1+exp{−U(θ+Uz/2)/(2ε)}U​.
(52)
The layer has width O(ε/U) and tends to the entropy shock, with value U/2 at its center. Outside it the limits are 0 and U.
For U<0, inside 0<θ<−Uz both Gaussian function tails have large positive lower limits. Their asymptotics give
αe−S∼θ−θ−Uz​,f⟶−zθ​.
(53)
Outside this interval the limits are 0 on the left and U on the right. Thus positive viscosity selects the rarefaction wave, including the same midpoint value as the inviscid fan. The two sign cases agree with the preceding entropy solution construction, while a negative diffusivity would not furnish this dissipative selection.
