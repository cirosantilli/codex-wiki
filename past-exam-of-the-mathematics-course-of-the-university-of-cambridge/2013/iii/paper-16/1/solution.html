Use the positive Laplace-Beltrami operator Δ=−divgrad. A Riemannian submersion is a surjective smooth submersion π:(M,g)→(N,h) for which, at every p, the restriction of dπp​ to Hp​=(kerdπp​)⊥ is a linear isometry onto Tπ(p)​N. The spaces Vp​=kerdπp​ and Hp​ are its vertical and horizontal spaces. Its fibres are totally geodesic submanifolds precisely when ∇U​V is vertical for vertical vector fields U,V: their second fundamental form vanishes. Equivalently, a geodesic initially tangent to a fibre remains in that fibre while defined.
For a smooth f:N→R, its basic function F=f∘π is constant along each fibre. The Riemannian gradient of F is the horizontal lift of a vector field through a submersion of gradN​f, since
g(gradM​F,X)=dF(X)=df(dπX)=h(gradN​f,dπX)
(1)
for horizontal X, and dF(U)=0 for vertical U. In particular no derivative of f in a vertical direction occurs.
Here is the needed connection fact, which also follows directly from the Koszul formula: for horizontal lifts X,Y of vector fields Xˉ,Yˉ on N, the horizontal component of ∇X​Y projects to ∇XˉN​Yˉ. To see this, pair the Koszul formula with a third horizontal lift Z. The horizontal inner products are pulled back from N, and the horizontal components of their Lie brackets of vector fields project to the brackets on N. Thus all six terms are the pullbacks of the corresponding terms on N.
Choose an adapted Riemannian orthonormal frame X1​,…,Xn​,U1​,…,Ur​, with the Xa​ horizontal lifts. Using HessF(A,A)=A(AF)−(∇A​A)F, the connection fact gives
HessM​F(Xa​,Xa​)=(HessN​f)(Xˉa​,Xˉa​)∘π.
(2)
For a vertical Ub​, both Ub​F=0 and (∇Ub​​Ub​)F=0, the latter because the fibres are totally geodesic submanifolds. Taking the negative metric trace of the Riemannian Hessian therefore proves the basic-function Laplacian identity
ΔM​(f∘π)=(ΔN​f)∘π.​
(3)
This identity is local and does not require compactness. Vanishing mean curvature of the fibres would already suffice; total geodesicity makes each vertical summand vanish separately.
For the discrete eigenspace assertion, assume the two Riemannian manifolds are closed manifolds. Without a discrete spectral realization, an unrestricted noncompact version need not have an eigenbasis. The projections of the Riemannian product M×N are Riemannian submersions with totally geodesic submanifolds as fibres. Its Levi-Civita connection splits into the two factor connections. Consequently its positive Laplace-Beltrami operator is
ΔM×N​=ΔM​⊗I+I⊗ΔN​,ΔM×N​(u(x)v(y))=(ΔM​u)(x)v(y)+u(x)(ΔN​v)(y).
(4)
The cross term in the product rule for the positive Laplace-Beltrami operator is zero because the two factor Riemannian gradients are orthogonal.
We use the standard compact elliptic compact elliptic spectral theorem: the positive Laplace-Beltrami operator on a closed manifold is self-adjoint, has compact resolvent, and has a complete orthonormal eigenbasis of smooth eigenfunctions, with finite-dimensional eigenspaces and eigenvalues tending to infinity. Let ΔM​ui​=μi​ui​ and ΔN​vj​=νj​vj​. Fubini's theorem and completeness on each factor show that ui​(x)vj​(y) form a complete orthonormal basis of L2(M×N). For example, a function orthogonal to all these products has, for each i, zero ui​-coefficient as an L2(N) function, hence is zero.
The displayed operator identity makes ui​vj​ an eigenfunction with eigenvalue μi​+νj​. Conversely, if Δw=λw, self-adjointness of the positive Laplace-Beltrami operator gives
(μi​+νj​−λ)⟨w,ui​vj​⟩=0.
(5)
All other coefficients vanish. Only finitely many pairs can have μi​+νj​=λ, since both spectra are nonnegative and have finitely many eigenvalues below any fixed bound. Thus the product Laplacian eigenspace decomposition is
Eλ​(M×N)=μ+ν=λ⨁​Eμ​(M)⊗Eν​(N).​
(6)
The tensor product summands are mutually orthogonal; their elements are actual smooth eigenfunctions, so this is an equality of eigenspaces, not just a formal expansion.
