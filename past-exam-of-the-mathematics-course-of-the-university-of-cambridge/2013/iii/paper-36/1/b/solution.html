which is impossible. Hence the null space contains no nonzero sparse vector of order 2s.
The feasible vector x has ∥x∥0​≤s, where the L0 sparsity count counts its nonzero coordinates. Any different feasible z with ∥z∥0​≤∥x∥0​ would give a nonzero null space vector z−x with at most 2s nonzero coordinates, contrary to sparse injectivity. Consequently every different feasible z has strictly larger L0 sparsity count. The unique sparsest feasible vector is x:

This proof does not treat the L0 sparsity count as a genuine norm; no triangle inequality for it is needed.
