Past exam of the mathematics course of the University of Cambridge / 2013 / iii / Paper 19 / 3 / iii / Solution

 ... Past exam of the mathematics course of the University of Cambridge 2013 iii Paper 19 3 iii
OurBigBook.com Words: 185
Write ◊S′​ for the countable-family assertion in the PDF; its prime is not a superscript c. A single stationary diamond at a regular cardinal sequence gives such families by taking singletons, so ◊S​⇒◊S′​.
Conversely enumerate each countable family as Eα​(n), n<ω, padding finite families and allowing the empty set as a default. Fix a bijection π:λ×ω→λ. The previous part applied to B(ξ)={π(ξ,n):n<ω} gives a club set D on which π[α×ω]⊆α. Define, for each n, a single candidate sequence
Dαn​={ξ<α:π(ξ,n)∈Eα​(n)}(α∈S).
(19)
Suppose no candidate sequence witnesses ◊S​. For each n choose Xn​⊆λ and a club set Cn​ such that Dαn​=Xn​∩α for every α∈S∩Cn​. Code all the counterexamples into
X={π(ξ,n):n<ω, ξ∈Xn​}.
(20)
The countable-family hypothesis guesses X on a stationary set. Choose a guessing α in the club set D∩⋂n​Cn​, and choose n with Eα​(n)=X∩α. For every ξ<α, closure under π gives
ξ∈Dαn​ ⟺ π(ξ,n)∈X ⟺ ξ∈Xn​.
(21)
Thus Dαn​=Xn​∩α, contradicting α∈Cn​. At least one candidate is a diamond sequence, proving
◊S′​ ⟺ ◊S​.​
(22)
This is the countable-family diamond equivalence, for every regular uncountable λ and stationary S⊆λ.
