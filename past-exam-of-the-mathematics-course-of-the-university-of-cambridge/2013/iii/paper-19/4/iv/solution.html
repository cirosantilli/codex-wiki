Past exam of the mathematics course of the University of Cambridge / 2013 / iii / Paper 19 / 4 / iv / Solution

 ... Past exam of the mathematics course of the University of Cambridge 2013 iii Paper 19 4 iv
OurBigBook.com Words: 291
Let κ=(ω2​)M be the fixed ground ordinal. We prove that S remains stationary in this ordinal. The chain condition for forcing ensures that κ remains regular: possible values of each coordinate of an ordinal-valued name form a ground set of size less than κ, by a maximal deciding forcing antichain. A hypothetical cofinal map with domain below κ would have its range covered by fewer than κ such small sets, hence bounded by regularity in M.
Let C˙ name a club set in κ, and take p∈G forcing this. For each α<κ, choose in M a maximal forcing antichain above p deciding the least point of C˙ strictly above α. Its set of possible values has size less than κ, so choose a ground bound h(α)<κ larger than all of them. The ground set
D={δ<κ:δ is limit and (∀α<δ) h(α)<δ}
(28)
is a club set by the usual countable closure iteration and regularity. For every δ∈D, p forces C˙∩δ unbounded in δ, and closedness forces δ∈C˙. Hence p⊩Dˇ⊆C˙. In M, choose δ∈S∩D; that same ordinal belongs to S∩C in the extension. Stationarity at the fixed ground κ is preserved.
There is an important qualification to the printed ω2​ notation. If it is recomputed internally as (ω2​)M[G], the assertion is false without preservation of smaller cardinals. Finite partial maps from ω to (ω1​)M form a forcing of size (ℵ1​)M, hence have the (ℵ2​)M-chain condition, but collapse (ω1​)M to countable. Then (ω2​)M=(ω1​)M[G], while (ω2​)M[G] is larger. The old S is bounded in this new ω2​, so cannot be stationary there. The proved statement uses the fixed ground ordinal, or alternatively requires the lower-cardinal preservation needed to retain its aleph index.
