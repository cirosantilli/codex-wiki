We use only the definability and truth clauses of the forcing theorem. For every formula, its forcing relation on names is definable inside M; forcing is preserved by strengthening; and M[G]⊨φ(τG) exactly when some condition in G forces φ(τ). These clauses do not presuppose the Separation axiom we are proving.
Let a=τG and let σG be the parameters in the desired instance. In M, form the name
ρ={(ν,q):∃p[(ν,p)∈τ ∧ q≥p ∧ q⊩φ(ν,σ)]}.
(26)
This is a set in M by its Separation axiom and forcing definability, using the subnames of τ and P as a set-sized bound.
If x∈ρG, some q∈G activates (ν,q) and q≥p activates (ν,p)∈τ. Thus x=νG∈a, and the forcing theorem gives φ(x,σG). Conversely, if x∈a satisfies that formula, choose an active pair (ν,p)∈τ with x=νG and a condition r∈G forcing the formula. Directedness gives q∈G stronger than both p and r. Monotonicity puts (ν,q)∈ρ, so x∈ρG.
Therefore
ρG={x∈a:M[G]⊨φ(x,σG)}.​
(27)
Every requested instance of separation in a generic extension follows.
