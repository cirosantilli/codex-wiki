For real k, σ is the temporal growth rate. Since β=0, its supremum is finite exactly when

For β<0, arbitrarily short wavelengths grow arbitrarily fast. For β>0, the finite maximum temporal growth rate is

The Briggs-Bers criterion starts the inverse temporal Laplace transform above all temporal singularities and then deforms its contour downward while following the spatial roots. A finite growth bound supplies such an initial contour and a causal, high-frequency-controlled Green function. Unbounded temporal growth prevents that standard construction.
For absolute wave-packet instability, a candidate spatial pinch point must satisfy D=0, Dk​=0, and Imω>0. Here
Dk​=−2αk+4βk3=0
(35)
gives
k0​=0,ω0​=−iγ;k±​=±2βα​​,ω±​=i(4βα2​−γ).
(36)
Thus candidate growing saddles require γ<0 at k0​, or γ<α2/(4β) at k±​. Together with β>0, existence of at least one such candidate requires γ<α2/(4β).
A growing double root is not sufficient: the roots must pinch the spatial inversion contour from opposite sides. Collisions of branches originating in the same spatial half-plane do not obstruct the relevant deformation. This distinction is part of the Briggs-Bers criterion; it is stated, for example, in the primary study doi.org/10.1017/jfm.2016.195.
An explicit false spatial saddle in quartic dispersion is α=−2, β=1, γ=1/2. Its candidates k=±i have ω=i/2, but all real modes have σ=−k4−2k2−1/2<0. Each imaginary collision joins two branches in the same half-plane; neither is a relevant pinch. For ω=iΩ, the spatial roots obey k2=−1±1/2−Ω​, making those same-half-plane collisions transparent as Ω↓1/2.
