In a steady flow the ideal magnetohydrodynamic induction equation gives ∇×(u×B)=0. Since both vectors have only poloidal magnetic field components, write u×B=Fϕ​eϕ​. Axisymmetry implies
∂z​Fϕ​=0,∂R​(RFϕ​)=0,Fϕ​=RC​.
(26)
For a field regular on the axis, C=0; equivalently one may impose zero toroidal electromotive force. Under this physical condition, u is parallel to B. Write ρu=kB. Continuity and ∇⋅B=0 then give B⋅∇k=0. The poloidal magnetic flux function has B⋅∇ψ=0, so locally on each connected magnetic surface,
u=ρk(ψ)​B.​
(27)
This regular-axis alignment of steady poloidal ideal flow needs its zero-circulation condition: axisymmetry alone does not imply this alignment on a domain excluding the axis. An explicit counterexample is available in a cylindrical annulus. Take nonzero constants U,b, positive ρc​,cs​, and
u=Uez​,B=Rb​eR​,ρ=(1+R2/a2)2ρc​​,p=cs2​ρ,Φ=2cs2​ln(1+R2/a2),a2=πGρc​2cs2​​.
(28)
Here ψ=−bz, ∇⋅B=∇×B=0 and u×B=Ubeϕ​/R has zero curl. The flow satisfies continuity, p′=−ρΦ′, and ∇2Φ=4πGρ. Its acceleration and Lorentz force density vanish, so it solves the steady equations while u and B are perpendicular. The nonzero toroidal circulation and the axial singularity explain why this counterexample is excluded from a regular polar accretion column. The remaining derivation uses the regular, aligned branch.
For a narrow stream tube, integrating continuity gives constant Aρ∣u∣. On a nonzero-flow tube the magnetohydrodynamic mass loading k is constant, so Aρ∣u∣=A∣k∣∣B∣. Therefore A∣B∣ is constant and A∝∣B∣−1. This also follows directly from conserved magnetic flux through a flux tube.
For constant isothermal sound speed, ∇p/ρ=cs2​∇ln(ρ/ρ0​), with arbitrary reference density ρ0​. Project momentum along B. The Lorentz force density has no component in that direction, and alignment implies B⋅(u⋅∇)u=B⋅∇(∣u∣2/2). Thus the isothermal magnetic Bernoulli integral is
21​∣u∣2+Φ+cs2​ln(ρ/ρ0​)=ϵ(ψ).​
(29)
Changing ρ0​ only shifts the Bernoulli function by a constant. Since k is constant along the magnetic field,
B⋅∇(2∣u∣2​)=∣u∣2[B⋅∇ln∣B∣−ρB⋅∇ρ​].
(30)
Differentiating the isothermal magnetic Bernoulli integral therefore gives
(cs2​−∣u∣2)B⋅∇ρ=−ρ[B⋅∇Φ+∣u∣2B⋅∇ln∣B∣].​
(31)
In the slender polar accretion column, the dipolar flux-tube area is A(z)=A∗​(z/R∗​)3. The decreasing axial field is a leading approximation, not an exactly solenoidal field (0,0,Bz​(z)) throughout a cylinder. Indeed ∇⋅B=∂z​Bz​=0 for that literal field. Near the axis a small radial component BR​≃−(R/2)∂z​Bz​=3RBz​/(2z) supplies the required radial divergence. Its magnitude is smaller by R/z, even though its divergence is leading order. Keeping this expanding flux tube while neglecting transverse forces yields the intended one-dimensional model.
Use the positive inward speed v=−uz​; the signed sonic velocity is uz​=−cs​. Conservation of mass and the Bernoulli equation give
Aρv=M˙,ρρ′​=−vv′​−z3​,vv′+z2GM∗​​+cs2​ρρ′​=0.
(32)
Eliminating ρ′ yields the isothermal dipolar accretion equation,
(v−vcs2​​)v′=z3cs2​​−z2GM∗​​.
(33)
A smooth transonic branch requires both sides to vanish at its sonic point, so
vs​=cs​,zs​=3cs2​GM∗​​.​
(34)
This is the critical point of the flow equation; a generic subsonic solution need not cross it. A crossing in the exterior column requires zs​≥R∗​, and a sonic point strictly outside the star requires zs​>R∗​. Differentiating the equation at the crossing gives 2(vs′​)2=3cs2​/zs2​. For inward accretion that accelerates toward the star, vs′​=−3/2​cs​/zs​.
The reservoir boundary condition fixes ϵ=cs2​ln(ρ∞​/ρ0​). At the sonic point, Φs​=−3cs2​ and vs2​/2=cs2​/2, giving
lnρ∞​ρs​​=3−21​=25​,ρs​=e5/2ρ∞​.​
(35)
Finally use the sonic mass flux through the dipolar flux-tube area:
M˙=ρ∞​cs​A∗​e5/2(3cs2​R∗​GM∗​​)3.​
(36)
This rate belongs to the smooth transonic branch of the idealized column, with A∗​ the supplied total loaded area. The reservoir condition alone does not force every steady solution onto this branch. A real narrow dipolar column must match an outer flow where the slender approximation ceases to apply.
