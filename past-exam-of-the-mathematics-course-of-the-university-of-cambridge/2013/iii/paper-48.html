Assume positive fluid density, ρ0​>0, and write m=1+3w>0. A perfect fluid with constant equation of state obeys the cosmological perfect-fluid continuity equation, giving
ρ˙​+3H(ρ+P)=0,ρ=ρ0​a−3(1+w).
(1)
In the stated units the Friedmann equation is H2=ρ/3−k/a2+Λ/3. Multiplication by a2/2 yields the Friedmann effective potential for a constant-equation-of-state fluid,
21​a˙2+V(a)=0,V(a)=−6ρ0​​a−m+2k​−6Λ​a2​.
(2)
The allowed region has V≤0. The Friedmann acceleration equation is equivalently a¨=−V′(a), including turning points by continuity. Thus a zero-energy mechanical trajectory reproduces the cosmological evolution, with a>0.
For k=0, Λ<0, the potential increases strictly from negative infinity to positive infinity. Its unique zero gives
amax​=(∣Λ∣ρ0​​)1/[3(1+w)]​.
(3)
Expansion from the Big Bang stops there, with negative acceleration, then reverses into a Big Crunch. Both the turning point and the final singularity occur in finite proper time: dt=da/−2V​ is integrable near a simple turning point and behaves as a constant times am/2da near zero.
For Λ=0, k=+1, V increases from negative infinity to 1/2 and crosses zero at
amax​=(3ρ0​​)1/(1+3w)​.
(4)
This is again expansion followed by finite-time recollapse. For k=−1, the same increasing curve has asymptote −1/2 and never meets zero. Expansion continues without a finite maximum; asymptotically a˙→1 and a∼t, as spatial curvature dominates the diluted fluid.
For k=0, Λ>0, V tends to negative infinity at both ends. It has a maximum at
a∗m+2​=2Λmρ0​​,V(a∗​)=−12(m+2)ρ0​​a∗−m​<0.
(5)
There is no turning point. Expansion initially decelerates, then accelerates once a>a∗​, and approaches de Sitter spacetime expansion a∝eΛ/3​t. Thus positive flat dark-energy expansion is unbounded; negative flat dark energy and positive curvature without dark energy recollapse.

Use conformal time, dt=adτ, and let primes mean d/dτ. Then a′=aa˙ and a′′=aa˙2+a2a¨. For k=1, Λ=0, the Friedmann equation and acceleration equation give
a˙2=3ρ0​​a−(1+3w)−1,a¨=−6ρ0​​(1+3w)a−(2+3w).
(6)
Substitution yields
a′′+a=6ρ0​​(1−3w)a−3w​.
(7)
Use the supplied solution without deriving it. Its first maximum has sine equal to one, so its amplitude must equal the physical turnaround scale in part (a):
A=(3ρ0​​)1/(1+3w)​.
(8)
Choose the expanding branch with B=0 and the first zero at τ=0.
For pressureless matter, w=0, the closed matter-dominated Friedmann solution is
a(τ)=Asin2(τ/2)=2A​(1−cosτ),t(τ)=2A​(τ−sinτ),A=ρ0​/3​.
(9)
The Big Crunch is at τc​=2π, or proper time tc​=πA=πρ0​/3, measured from the Big Bang.
For radiation, w=1/3, the closed radiation-dominated Friedmann solution is
a(τ)=Asinτ,t(τ)=A(1−cosτ),A=ρ0​/3​​.
(10)
The Big Crunch is at τc​=π, or tc​=2A.
A radial null geodesic in FLRW spacetime obeys dχ/dτ=±1. The spatial slice is a unit three-sphere times a, so a great circle has comoving circumference 2π. The dust lifetime supplies conformal distance 2π: one circumference, with the return occurring only in the crunch limit. Strictly before the singular endpoint no full return is completed. The radiation lifetime supplies conformal distance π: the photon reaches the antipode, half a great circle, in the crunch limit. These are limiting null rays from near the initial singularity, not photons at a regular event on the singular surface.

For the reaction p+e↔H+γ, chemical equilibrium requires μp​+μe​=μH​ because thermal photons have zero chemical potential. Insert the nonrelativistic Maxwell-Boltzmann distribution number densities and use mH​=mp​+me​−B:
nH​ne​np​​=gH​ge​gp​​(mH​me​mp​​2πT​)3/2e−B/T.
(11)
For ground-state hydrogen, including its spin states, ge​=gp​=2, gH​=4; hence the degeneracy factor is one. Since mp​/mH​≃1, the right side is (me​T/2π)3/2e−B/T.
Charge neutrality gives np​=ne​, while baryon conservation gives nb​=np​+nH​. Consequently ne​=np​=Xe​nb​ and nH​=(1−Xe​)nb​. Inverting the preceding ratio and inserting the photon number density with nb​=ηnγ​ gives the hydrogen-only Saha equation
Xe2​1−Xe​​=π22ζ(3)​η(me​2πT​)3/2eB/T​.
(12)
Writing y=B/T, its coefficient is (2ζ(3)/π2)η(2πB/me​)3/2≃3.2×10−16, consistent with the rounded 3×10−16. The negligible mass-ratio correction and ground-state approximation are the assumptions behind this form.

At Xe​=1/2, the left side of the Saha equation is two. With its rounded coefficient, the recombination temperature therefore satisfies
2=3×10−16y3/2ey​,y−23​lny=ln(32​×1016)≃36.

(14)
This is the requested rough scale. Retaining the prefactor and the unrounded logarithm gives y≃42.0 and Trec​≃0.32 eV; the displayed 0.4 eV is not a high-accuracy numerical root of the Saha equation.
The small baryon-to-photon ratio means that radiation and the entropy of the free charged particles strongly favor ionization. A low mean photon energy does not eliminate the energetic tail, and ionized particles have a large phase-space advantage. The exponential binding factor must become very large before neutral atoms dominate this dilute gas. Accordingly B/T is of order forty rather than order one, so recombination occurs well below the hydrogen binding energy.

Photon decoupling is a dynamical loss of frequent scattering, distinct from the chemical conversion of charged particles into neutral atoms. Before recombination, Thomson scattering on free Electrons keeps photons coupled to the baryon plasma. The physical scattering rate is
Γγ​=ne​σT​=Xe​nb​σT​,
(15)
in units with c=1. Define the approximate decoupling temperature by
Γγ​(Tdec​)≃H(Tdec​)​.
(16)
Equivalently the photon mean scattering time becomes comparable to an expansion time. A refined last-scattering definition uses the optical depth and the maximum of the visibility function; it need not coincide exactly with the local rate criterion. As Xe​ falls, scattering becomes inefficient, ordinarily after the half-ionized recombination stage, so Tdec​<Trec​.
Residual electron freeze-out concerns the remaining ionized fraction. The Saha equation presumes sufficiently rapid forward and reverse reactions to maintain chemical equilibrium. Real recombination is slowed by radiative bottlenecks and expansion; the free-electron fraction can therefore exceed its equilibrium value. Eventually the effective recombination rate per free Electron, roughly αeff​(T)ne​, falls below H. The remaining Electrons cannot all find and recombine with protons within an expansion time. A small nonzero residual fraction survives while the Saha prediction decreases exponentially toward zero.
The sketch shows this lag and residual floor. Its kinetic curve and the position of the decoupling marker are schematic: the data supplied determine the equilibrium curve, but not a quantitative recombination history or an exact Tdec​. Determining those needs the expansion history and atomic transition rates. The horizontal axis decreases toward the right to follow cosmological cooling.

δm​=C1​+C2​ln(a/a∗​),u=[C1​+C2​ln(a/a∗​)]/a​.

Thus matter has at most logarithmic growth during this leading radiation approximation, together with a constant independent mode. The constant is non-growing, not a mode that literally falls as a−1; that power belongs to u rather than δm​.
For an increasing/decreasing basis of the displayed equation with matter self-gravity retained, put x=6a/aeq​​. Its equation becomes x2δxx​+xδx​−x2δ=0. Hence
δ+​=I0​(x),δ−​=K0​(x)​.
(23)
The Modified Bessel function of the first kind gives I0​(x)=1+3a/(2aeq​)+O((a/aeq​)2), which increases slowly. The Modified Bessel function of the second kind gives K0​(x)=−21​ln(a/aeq​)+21​ln(2/3)−γE​+O((a/aeq​)∣ln(a/aeq​)∣), where γE​ is Euler's constant; this mode decreases as a increases. Promote the canonical field and momentum π=f′ to operators satisfying the canonical commutation relation, [f^​(τ,x),π^(τ,y)]=iδ(3)(x−y). A real field has Fourier reality condition f^​k†​=f^​−k​. Its oscillator expansion is
f^​k​=uk​a^k​+uk∗​a^−k†​​.
(32)
The creation and annihilation operators add or remove excitations in the selected mode basis, with
[a^k​,a^k′†​]=δ(3)(k−k′),[a^k​,a^k′​]=[a^k†​,a^k′†​]=0,a^k​∣0⟩=0.
(33)
Canonical normalization requires the Wronskian uk​uk∗′​−uk∗​uk′​=i.
For the Bunch-Davies vacuum, choose the positive-frequency short-wavelength behavior uk​∼e−ikτ/2k​ as k∣τ∣→∞. For the proposed solution, direct differentiation gives
uk′​=2k​e−ikτ​[−ik−τ1​+kτ2i​],uk′′​=2k​e−ikτ​[−k2+τik​+τ22​−kτ32i​].
(34)
Substitution cancels every term in uk′′​+(k2−2/τ2)uk​. Its Wronskian is i, and its large-∣kτ∣ limit is the required flat-spacetime positive-frequency mode. Thus
uk​=2k​e−ikτ​(1−kτi​)​.

The canonical mode grows as 1/∣τ∣ outside the Hubble radius, while the physical field perturbation f/a freezes. A slowly varying H produces an almost scale-invariant spectrum rather than exact scale invariance.
For a slowly rolling single field, fluctuations in the inflaton clock become the comoving curvature perturbation, R≃−Hδϕ/ϕˉ​˙​ up to sign convention. Restoring the reduced Planck mass and using ϵ=ϕˉ​˙​2/(2Mpl2​H2) gives
ΔR2​≃8π2ϵMpl2​H2​​.
(38)
This conversion presumes nonzero background roll; a strictly constant test scalar in exact de Sitter spacetime does not by itself define a finite comoving curvature perturbation through this formula. The curvature perturbations seed the later density fluctuations.
When metric fluctuations and their Einstein-gravity normalization are restored, the two graviton polarizations have the same massless mode equation. The usual total primordial tensor power spectrum is Δt2​=2H2/(π2Mpl2​), so the single-field leading ratio is r=16ϵ. Tensor amplitudes therefore probe the inflationary energy scale, whereas scalar amplitudes also depend on the roll rate. These tensor statements explain the relevance of the mode result; they are not derived from a scalar action with metric perturbations omitted.
