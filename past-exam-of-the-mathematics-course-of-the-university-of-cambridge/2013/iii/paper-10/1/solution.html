Write [n](r) for the rank-r uniform layer of the Boolean cube. For a uniform set family F⊆[n](r), with 1≤r≤n, let ∂F be its lower shadow. The Local LYM inequality is
(r−1n​)∣∂F∣​≥(rn​)∣F∣​​.
(1)
For its proof, count incidences (B,A) with A∈F, B⊂A, and ∣B∣=r−1. Each member of F contributes r incidences, while each member of the lower shadow contributes at most n−r+1. Thus r∣F∣≤(n−r+1)∣∂F∣, which is the displayed bound by the ratio of consecutive binomial coefficients. Complementing every set gives the corresponding upper shadow bound, ∣∇F∣/(r+1n​)≥∣F∣/(rn​) for r<n.
For an antichain A in the Boolean lattice, the LYM inequality is
r=0∑n​(rn​)∣A∩[n](r)∣​≤1​.
(2)
Here is the proof using the Local LYM inequality. Denote the rank-r part by Ar​, and let Dr​ consist of the r-sets containing some member of A of size at most r. These families satisfy
Dr​=Ar​∪˙∇Dr−1​(1≤r≤n),D0​=A0​.
(3)
The union is disjoint because a member of the antichain cannot contain a strictly smaller member. Apply the upper shadow form of the Local LYM inequality to Dr−1​:
(rn​)∣Dr​∣​≥(rn​)∣Ar​∣​+(r−1n​)∣Dr−1​∣​.
(4)
Iterating gives a bound for the entire sum by ∣Dn​∣/(nn​)≤1. This also covers the empty antichain and an antichain containing the empty set.
For the second proof, each permutation of [n] specifies a maximal chain in a Boolean lattice by taking its successive prefixes. There are n! such maximal chains in a Boolean lattice, and a fixed r-set belongs to r!(n−r)! of them. A maximal chain in a Boolean lattice meets an antichain at most once. Double counting these incidences therefore gives ∑r​∣Ar​∣r!(n−r)!≤n!, exactly the LYM inequality.
The Sperner theorem says that an antichain in P([n]) has at most (⌊n/2⌋n​) members; a full middle rank attains this bound. Indeed, every binomial coefficient (rn​) is at most the central one, so the LYM inequality gives
(⌊n/2⌋n​)∣A∣​≤∑r​(rn​)∣Ar​∣​≤1.
(5)
Now let F be an intersection-free uniform set family. If it is empty, there is nothing to prove. Fix X∈F and consider the traces
T={X∩A:A∈F∖{X}}⊆P(X).
(6)
If A,B=X are distinct and X∩A⊆X∩B, then X∩A⊆B. This contradicts the defining restriction on the three distinct members X,A,B. The intersection has size less than r, so it is a proper subset of B even if the printed subset symbol is interpreted strictly. In particular, equal traces are impossible, and the traces form an antichain. Thus ∣T∣=∣F∣−1. Applying the Sperner theorem to the r-element ground set X proves the antichain trace bound for intersection-free families
∣F∣≤1+(⌊r/2⌋r​)​.
(7)
The argument also handles r=0: a uniform set family then has at most one member.
