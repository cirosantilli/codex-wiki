Past exam of the mathematics course of the University of Cambridge / 2013 / iii / Paper 38 / 3 / a / Solution

Label the vertices S (left), A (upper middle), B (lower middle), C (upper right), T (lower right). Use the flow balance convention outgoing minus incoming equals supply. The printed initial flows, in the order (SA,SB,AB,AC,BC,BT,CT), are
x(0)=(25,0,15,10,10,5,20).
(16)
They obey all capacity constraints and the flow balances (25,0,0,0,−25). The four strictly interior edges SA,AB,BC,BT form a spanning tree. The remaining edges are at a bound: SB at zero, AC and CT at their upper bounds. Thus this is already a feasible network simplex tree basis; no artificial feasibility phase is needed. Its total cost is 340.
For a tree, choose network dual potentials with πS​=0 and zero network reduced cost rij​=cij​−πi​+πj​ on each tree edge. Initially
(πS​,πA​,πB​,πC​,πT​)=(0,−7,−12,−14,−14).
(17)
The nonbasic lower-bound edge SB has network reduced cost −6. Increase its flow and adjust around the graph cycle S→B→A→S, reversing AB and SA. The available step is min(20,15,25)=15, so AB leaves at zero. This gives
x(1)=(10,15,0,10,10,5,20),cost=250.
(18)
The tree is now SA,SB,BC,BT, and the potentials are (0,−7,−6,−8,−8).
Now AC is at its upper bound but has network reduced cost 3>0, so decreasing it improves cost. Its reverse residual network edge enters along the cycle A→S→B→C→A. The forward edges SB,BC increase; SA,AC decrease. The step is
min(xSA​,mSB​−xSB​,mBC​−xBC​,xAC​)=min(10,5,10,10)=5.
(19)
Thus SB leaves at its upper bound, and
x(2)=(5,20,0,5,15,5,20),cost=235.
(20)
The new tree SA,AC,BC,BT has potentials (0,−7,−9,−11,−11).
The upper-bound edge CT now has network reduced cost 1>0. Enter its reverse direction along T→C→B→T, decreasing CT and BC and increasing BT. The step is min(20,15,25−5)=15, so BC leaves at zero. The resulting flow is
x∗=(5,20,0,5,0,20,5),minimum cost=220.​
(21)
The tree is SA,AC,BT,CT, with potentials (0,−7,−10,−11,−12). The nonbasic edges have network reduced costs rSB​=−4 at its upper bound, rAB​=2 at its lower bound, and rBC​=1 at its lower bound. All have the correct signs, so there is no improving network simplex pivot. The next part proves these signs certify optimality.
