Past exam of the mathematics course of the University of Cambridge / 2013 / iii / Paper 38 / 4 / b / Solution

Let the bipartite graph have parts L,R. Add a source s, a sink t, capacity-one edges s→u for u∈L and v→t for v∈R, and capacity M=∣V∣+1 on each original edge directed L→R. Use the max-flow min-cut theorem: maximum flow value equals minimum cut capacity. Also use Integrality of the Ford-Fulkerson algorithm: integral capacities admit an integral maximum flow, found by integral augmenting paths.
An integral flow selects a matching in a graph, because each left and right vertex carries at most one unit. Conversely every matching gives a unit flow along its selected s→u→v→t paths. Hence maximum flow value equals maximum matching cardinality.
A minimum cut has capacity at most ∣L∣, whereas crossing even one original edge would cost M>∣L∣. For its source side Z, no edge of the original graph runs from L∩Z to R∖Z. Therefore
U=(L∖Z)∪(R∩Z)
(26)
is a vertex cover, and its cardinality is exactly the cut capacity. Conversely, given any cover U, use Z={s}∪(L∖U)∪(R∩U). No original edge crosses this cut, because both its endpoints would otherwise be outside the cover; its capacity is ∣U∣. Thus minimum cut capacity equals minimum cover size, proving Kőnig's theorem for bipartite matching:
maximum matching size=minimum vertex-cover size.​
(27)
For the algorithm, repeatedly find augmenting paths in the residual network, then take Z to be the vertices reachable from s. Each integral augmentation increases flow by at least one; the total value is at most min(∣L∣,∣R∣). Each reachability search takes O(∣V∣+∣E∣) time, so this procedure takes O(∣V∣(∣V∣+∣E∣)) time. The network uses only polynomially many edges and integer capacities, yielding the requested polynomial-time algorithm and an explicit cover.
