Let H replicate ξ1​, so ξ1​=HTP1​. The positive-definite Gram matrix Q=E[P1​P1T​] is invertible. Multiplication by P1​ and taking expected values give
E[P1​ξ1​]=QH,H=Q−1E[P1​ξ1​].
(23)
All quantities are integrable: the finite-atom representation from completeness gives finite terminal values, and the matrix Q has finite entries. Positive definiteness also guarantees uniqueness of holdings, since hTP1​=0 would imply hTQh=0 and hence h=0.
Thus the replication cost is
HTP0​=P0T​Q−1E[P1​ξ1​]=E[(P0T​Q−1P1​)ξ1​].
(24)
Consequently the one-period Gram-matrix replication formula is
ξ0​=E[Zξ1​],Z=P0T​Q−1P1​,W=Q−1P1​,H=E[Wξ1​].​
(25)
The symmetry of Q−1 makes the displayed orientations consistent. Positivity of this Z would require an additional no-arbitrage condition; positive definiteness of the payoff Gram matrix alone proves the representation and uniqueness, not positivity of prices across states.
