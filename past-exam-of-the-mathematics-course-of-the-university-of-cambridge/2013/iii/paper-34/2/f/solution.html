The claim needs a complete number of logarithmic decades. For the normalized log-uniform distribution on the specified range, U=log10​X is uniform on (a,b). Digit i corresponds to {U}∈[log10​i,log10​(i+1)). If b−a=m is a positive integer, the integral of a period-one indicator over (a,a+m) is m times its integral over one period. Thus Benford law from logarithmic uniformity gives
P(D=i)=log10​(1+1/i)when b−a∈N.​
(23)
This proves the intended case of integers a<b, and even permits noninteger a when the span is an integer.
For arbitrary real a<b, the exact formula instead is
P(D=i)=b−a1​k∈Z∑​[min{b,k+log10​(i+1)}−max{a,k+log10​i}]+​.​
(24)
Only finitely many terms are nonzero. For a counterexample take a=0, b=log10​2: then 1<X<2, so the leading digit is always one. That is not Benford law. The unrestricted range in the PDF needs this qualification.
