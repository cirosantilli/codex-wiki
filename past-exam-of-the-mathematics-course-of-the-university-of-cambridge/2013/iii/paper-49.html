Let ϕs​ be the local flow of the smooth vector field X, with ϕ0​=id and dsd​ϕs​(p)=Xϕs​(p)​. The flow definition of the Lie derivative of a tensor field is
(LX​T)p​=dsd​​s=0​(ϕs∗​T)p​.​
(1)
For a tensor field of type (r,s), the mixed tensor pullback transports the tensor at ϕs​(p) back to p: it applies (dϕs​)p−1​ to every contravariant factor and (dϕs​)p∗​ to every covariant factor. Thus every difference quotient belongs to the same tensor space at p. This definition gives another tensor field of the same type and uses no choice of affine connection. The tensor Lie derivative is defined wherever the local flow exists, including points where X vanishes.

For a diffeomorphism, the differential and its inverse preserve the natural pairing of a vector with a covector. Consequently the mixed tensor pullback commutes with every tensor contraction C:
ϕs∗​(CT)=C(ϕs∗​T).
(2)
Differentiating at zero gives LX​(CT)=C(LX​T). The mixed tensor pullback also preserves tensor products, so
ϕs∗​(S⊗T)=ϕs∗​S⊗ϕs∗​T.
(3)
The ordinary product rule for differentiation therefore yields
LX​(S⊗T)=(LX​S)⊗T+S⊗(LX​T).​
(4)
This proves the contraction and Leibniz rule properties for all smooth generators, without needing straightening coordinates.
The suggested flow-box theorem applies locally only where X=0. For example X=x∂x​ vanishes at x=0, so it cannot equal a coordinate basis vector there. Its local flow is nevertheless ϕs​(x)=esx and ϕs∗​dx=esdx, giving LX​dx=dx even at that zero. This Lie derivative at a zero of its generator illustrates why the general flow proof is needed to cover every point.

For a smooth function, pullback of a smooth function is composition. The chain rule gives
LX​f=dsd​​0​f(ϕs​(p))=X(f).​
(5)
For a vector field Y, use any local coordinates. To first order,
ϕsμ​(x)=xμ+sXμ(x)+O(s2),(dϕs​)−1μν​=δμν​−s∂ν​Xμ+O(s2).
(6)
Multiplying this inverse differential by Y(ϕs​(x)) gives
(ϕs∗​Y)μ=Yμ+s(Xν∂ν​Yμ−Yν∂ν​Xμ)+O(s2).
(7)
Thus
LX​Y=[X,Y],[X,Y]μ=Xν∂ν​Yμ−Yν∂ν​Xμ.​
(8)
This is the Lie bracket of vector fields, whose action on a function is X(Yf)−Y(Xf).

Write the covector field as ω=ωμ​dxμ. From the contraction and product rule properties,
(LX​ω)(Y)=X(ω(Y))−ω([X,Y]).
(9)
Take Y=∂μ​. The Lie bracket of vector fields is [X,∂μ​]=−(∂μ​Xν)∂ν​, so

Equivalently, the covariant-factor contribution comes from LX​dxμ=dXμ=(∂ν​Xμ)dxν. The formula holds in any coordinate basis and is the one-form case of the coordinate tensor Lie derivative.

Expand T=Tμν​∂μ​⊗dxν. Using the tensor Lie derivative of functions, vectors and covectors and its Leibniz rule gives
(LX​T)μν​=Xρ∂ρ​Tμν​−Tρν​∂ρ​Xμ+Tμρ​∂ν​Xρ.​
(11)
The contravariant slot has a minus sign and the covariant slot a plus sign.
For the commutator identity for Lie derivatives, put D=[LX​,LY​]−L[X,Y]​. The commutator of two tensor derivations is itself a tensor derivation, and D commutes with tensor contractions. On a function, Df=XYf−YXf−[X,Y]f=0. On a vector field Z,
DZ=[X,[Y,Z]]−[Y,[X,Z]]−[[X,Y],Z]=0
(12)
by the Jacobi identity, which follows here by expanding the commutators of the operators X,Y,Z acting on functions. For a type (1,1) tensor, T(Z) is a vector field, and contraction compatibility gives
0=D(T(Z))=(DT)(Z)+T(DZ)=(DT)(Z).
(13)
As this holds for every Z, DT=0. Therefore
LX​LY​T−LY​LX​T=L[X,Y]​T.​
(14)
The derivation argument also establishes the identity for arbitrary tensor types by applying it to covector–vector pairings and then to tensor products.

a OurBigBook.com
Use G=c=1, select the retarded source field with no added homogeneous radiation, and work to leading order in the weak-field approximation and long-wavelength source approximation. The retarded fundamental solution of the Linearized Einstein equations in Lorenz gauge in linearized gravity is
hˉij​(t,x)=4∫∣x−x′∣Tij​(t−∣x−x′∣,x′)​d3x′.
(15)
For r≫d, the denominator is r to leading order. The delay is t−r+n⋅x′+O(d2/r), where n=x/r. In the usual slowly evolving source regime, its characteristic time τ obeys d/τ≪1, so the source-size part of the delay is negligible:
hˉij​(t,x)≃r4​∫Tij​(t−r,x′)d3x′.
(16)
The stress-energy conservation equation ∂μ​Tμν=0 is required at the approximation order used; it follows from the divergence of the gauge-fixed field equation. Compact support removes the integration by parts surface terms. Since T00=T00​ and Tij=Tij​ in this signature,
I˙ij​=∫(T0ixj+T0jxi)d3x,I¨ij​=∫(Tji+Tij)d3x=2∫Tij​d3x.
(17)
Combining these identities gives the retarded quadrupole field
hˉij​(t,x)≃r2​I¨ij​(t−r).​
(18)
For an ordinary nonrelativistic bound source, τ∼d/v makes d/τ∼v≪1. More generally the size relative to the variation timescale must also be small; speed alone does not exclude a rapidly varying small-amplitude motion.
The radiation boundary condition for linearized gravity is essential for a statement about the full field. Without it, the displayed implication is false: take Tμν​=0 and add hˉ11​=Acos(k(t−z)), hˉ22​=−hˉ11​, all other components zero. This weak plane gravitational wave satisfies both the homogeneous wave equation and Lorenz gauge in linearized gravity, while every Iij​ is zero. The proved formula is for the retarded source contribution, with the standard slow-source qualification. Its transverse–traceless projection gives the physical radiative strain.

b OurBigBook.com
The separation of the stars is 2R. Newton's law of universal gravitation and circular acceleration give
MΩ2R=(2R)2M2​,Ω2=4R3M​.​
(19)
To leading nonrelativistic order, the mass density is the sum of the two translated point-mass Dirac delta distributions. Thus
Ixx​=MR2(1+cos2Ωt),Iyy​=MR2(1−cos2Ωt),Ixy​=Iyx​=MR2sin2Ωt,
(20)
with all components involving z zero. The trace is 2MR2, a constant. Define the trace-free mass quadrupole moment Qij​=Iij​−δij​Ikk​/3; its third derivatives equal those of Iij​. If K=8MR2Ω3, then
Q...​​xx​=Ksin2Ωt,Q...​​yy​=−Ksin2Ωt,Q...​​xy​=Q...​​yx​=−Kcos2Ωt.
(21)
Both off-diagonal entries count in the contraction. Therefore Q...​​ij​Q...​​ij​=2K2=128M2R4Ω6, independent of phase. The quadrupole formula gives the equal-mass circular-binary quadrupole luminosity
⟨P⟩=5128​M2R4Ω6=5R52M5​.​
(22)
Restoring units, Ω2=GM/(4R3) and
⟨P⟩=5c5R52G4M5​.​
(23)
Here R is each star's radius about the centre of mass, not the separation. The rest-frame prescription supplies the leading mass density; a moving star does not still have zero momentum density and spatial stress. The calculation uses the assumed quadrupole formula and the Newtonian orbit rather than imposing those rest-frame zeros on the moving binary.

At fixed masses, the equal-mass circular-binary quadrupole luminosity grows as R−5. The binary's Newtonian binding energy is −GM2/(4R), so reducing the separation increases both the binding and the radiated power. Equivalently, its luminosity scaling is
P=52​Gc5​(Rc2GM​)5,
(24)
which makes the importance of orbital compactness explicit.
Ordinary extended stars cannot remain separate at very small orbital radii: contact, mass transfer and tidal disruption intervene. A neutron star or black hole can remain a compact orbiting object down to separations of order a few gravitational radii, allowing high orbital speeds, rapidly changing mass quadrupole moments and strong gravitational waves. Thus compact, tightly bound binaries are especially efficient emitters. The Newtonian quadrupole formula explains the scaling; precision predictions near merger require relativistic dynamics, where that approximation itself ceases to be reliable.

Write hab​=δgab​ and Cabc​=δΓabc​. The difference between two affine connections is a tensor, so its infinitesimal change C is a type (1,2) tensor field. Both affine connections are torsion-free, giving Cabc​=Cacb​. Varying metric compatibility gives
∇c​hab​=gdb​Cdca​+gad​Cdcb​.
(25)
Lower the first index of C. Add the versions with derivatives b,c and subtract the one with derivative d; the lower-slot symmetry cancels the unwanted terms, leaving
∇b​hdc​+∇c​hdb​−∇d​hbc​=2gda​Cabc​.
(26)
Therefore
δΓabc​=21​gad(∇c​hdb​+∇b​hdc​−∇d​hbc​).​
(27)
All covariant derivatives use the original Levi-Civita connection. The PDF has ∇b​hdc​ as its second term; the converted TeX's ∇d​hdc​ is a transcription error.

Adopt the printed Riemann curvature tensor convention and contract Rab​=Rcacb​. At an arbitrary point choose normal coordinates for the original metric tensor. There the original connection coefficients vanish. Varying the coordinate curvature formula leaves
δRabcd​=∂c​Cabd​−∂d​Cabc​.
(28)
At that point these partial derivatives equal the covariant derivatives of the tensor C. Both sides are tensors, so the result is valid in every coordinate system:
δRabcd​=∇c​Cabd​−∇d​Cabc​.
(29)
Contracting its first and third indices proves the Palatini identity
δRab​=∇c​δΓcab​−∇b​δΓcac​.​
(30)
The normal-coordinate argument is applied independently at every point; it does not assume a flat background or set derivatives of the original connection to zero.

Variation of the inverse metric relation gacgcb​=δab​ gives δgab=−hab. Hence the metric variation of scalar curvature is
δR=−Rabhab​+gabδRab​.

gabCcab​=∇a​hca−21​∇ch,Ccac​=21​∇a​h.

gabδRab​=∇c​∇a​hca−∇c​∇ch.

Take compactly supported metric variations, or impose boundary conditions that remove the integration by parts terms. Let F=f′(R) and □=∇a∇a​. Since δ−g​=21​−g​gabhab​, the gravitational action varies as
δSg​=∫−g​[(21​fgab−FRab)hab​+F(∇a∇bhab​−□h)]d4x.
(35)
Applying integration by parts twice to the derivative terms gives
δSg​=−∫−g​Eabhab​d4x,Eab​=FRab​−21​fgab​−∇a​∇b​F+gab​□F.​
(36)
For the usual stress-energy tensor definition Tab​=−2(−g)−1/2δSm​/δgab, varying the covariant metric tensor gives δSm​=21​∫−g​Tabhab​d4x. Thus the action's stated normalization implies
Eab​=21​Tab​.​
(37)
There is no implicit 1/(16π) in this gravitational action. This is the normalization of the metric f(R) field equation, rather than the commonly normalized version with 8πTab​ on the right.
Finally, the chain rule gives ∇a​∇b​F=f′′∇a​∇b​R+f(3)∇a​R∇b​R and □F=f′′□R+f(3)(∇R)2. Substitution yields
Eab​=f′Rab​−f′′∇a​∇b​R−f(3)∇a​R∇b​R+(−21​f+f′′□R+f(3)∇c​R∇cR)gab​.​
(38)
This is a metric f(R) gravity variation: the connection is always the Levi-Civita connection of the varied metric tensor, not an independent variable.

The pure gravitational action is invariant under diffeomorphisms. An infinitesimal diffeomorphism generated by a compactly supported vector field ξ changes the metric by its tensor Lie derivative,
δgab​=Lξ​gab​=∇a​ξb​+∇b​ξa​.
(39)
Using symmetry of Eab and the variational expression already derived,
0=δξ​Sg​=−2∫−g​Eab∇a​ξb​d4x=2∫−g​(∇a​Eab)ξb​d4x.
(40)
As ξb​ is arbitrary, the off-shell metric divergence identity is
∇aEab​=0.​
(41)
This Noether identity follows for every metric tensor without imposing either the gravitational field equation or the matter equations. It is a consequence of the metric action's diffeomorphism invariance, not a conclusion requiring a long component calculation.

In four dimensions, Lovelock's theorem restricts a local natural symmetric divergence-free metric tensor with at most second derivatives of the metric to a constant linear combination of the Einstein tensor and the metric. A generic nonlinear f(R) gravity equation contains ∇a​∇b​f′(R): the Ricci scalar already has second metric derivatives, so this term generally introduces fourth metric derivatives. It therefore violates the second-order hypothesis, although it remains covariant, symmetric and divergence-free.
The exception must be stated. For f(R)=aR+b, the same tensor reduces to
Eab​=aGab​−2b​gab​,
(42)
which is precisely of Lovelock form. In particular, f(R)=R is a counterexample to a blanket claim that the theorem never applies. The intended exclusion concerns generic nonlinear f, with f′′ not identically zero. Restricting attention to a special constant-curvature solution of a nonlinear theory does not turn its off-shell equations into a universally second-order metric tensor.

A four-dimensional vacuum Einstein solution with the fixed cosmological constant Λ has Rab​=Λgab​ and R=4Λ, constant. Thus every derivative of f′(R) vanishes and
Eab​=(Λf′(4Λ)−21​f(4Λ))gab​.
(43)
The metric tensor is nondegenerate, so this is zero precisely when
f(4Λ)=2Λf′(4Λ).​
(44)
This is the necessary and sufficient Einstein metric condition in f(R) gravity for the specified Λ, and works for every such Einstein metric, even when its Weyl tensor is nonzero. There is no need to divide by f′(4Λ); the degenerate case where both f and f′ vanish at that curvature is included. At Λ=0, the condition is simply f(0)=0.
If the intention is to demand the inclusion for every real Λ simultaneously, the stronger functional condition is Rf′(R)=2f(R) for all R. On each nonzero half-line it integrates to f(R)=CR2; smoothness across zero makes the constants equal. Thus the all-Λ version gives f(R)=CR2, including C=0. This stronger reading is distinct from fixing one cosmological constant.

Treat the printed eμ as an orthonormal coframe, with frame metric η=diag(−1,1,1,1). Work on a patch z=0 and F=1−αz3>0, so the given real coframe exists. Define f=F​ and A=zf′−f. The exterior derivatives are
de0=Ae3∧e0,de1=−fe3∧e1,de2=−fe3∧e2,de3=0.
(45)
The connection 1-forms satisfying Cartan's first structure equation and metric compatibility are
ω03​=ω30​=Ae0,ω13​=−fe1,ω31​=fe1,ω23​=−fe2,ω32​=fe2.​
(46)
All other forms vanish. With a Lorentzian frame, it is the lowered forms ωab​=ηac​ωcb​ that are antisymmetric; the two mixed time–space forms are equal. These displayed forms solve the torsion-free structure equation, and uniqueness of the Levi-Civita connection identifies them as the required connection.
Apply Cartan's second structure equation. For example,
Θ03​=d(Ae0)=−(zfA′+A2)e0∧e3,Θ01​=ω03​∧ω31​=Afe0∧e1.
(47)
The remaining derivatives give Θ13​=Afe1∧e3 and Θ12​=−f2e1∧e2, with analogous forms for index 2. Put q=αz3 and B=1+q/2. From f2=1−q,
Af=zff′−f2=−B,zfA′+A2=F.
(48)
Therefore all six independent curvature 2-forms are
Θ01​Θ03​Θ13​​=−Be0∧e1,=−Fe0∧e3,=−Be1∧e3,​Θ02​Θ12​Θ23​​=−Be0∧e2,=−Fe1∧e2,=−Be2∧e3.​​
(49)
The other six are fixed by Θba​=−ηaa​ηbb​Θab​ for a=b, with no sum: equal for time–space pairs and opposite for spatial pairs. All diagonal forms are zero. The Lorentzian connection-form antisymmetry is essential to these signs.

Use the convention Θab​=21​Rabcd​ec∧ed and Rab​=Rcacb​. Each independent two-form has only one coordinate-plane wedge, so the Ricci tensor is diagonal. Contracting the six coefficients gives
R00​=2B+F,R11​=R22​=R33​=−(2B+F).
(50)
But 2B+F=2(1+q/2)+(1−q)=3, so
Rab​=−3ηab​,R=−12,Gab​=3ηab​.​
(51)
In coordinate-independent form, Rμν​=−3gμν​. Thus the vacuum Einstein field equations Gμν​+Λgμν​=0 hold with
Λ=−3.​
(52)
The curvature radius is one in the metric's normalization. The parameter α changes the individual curvature 2-forms but cancels from their Ricci contraction. For α=0 this is Anti-de Sitter spacetime; for nonzero α it is the planar Einstein metric with cubic radial function, with a nontrivial Weyl tensor rather than universally constant sectional curvature. The result is local on regular coordinate patches; a zero of F invalidates this particular static coframe, not the tensor equation in a suitable regular extension.
