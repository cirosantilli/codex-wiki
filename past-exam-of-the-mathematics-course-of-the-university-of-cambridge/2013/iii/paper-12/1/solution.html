We now prove the needed dense clique family blow-up lemma, with the following stronger induction invariant. Given any family M of at least ηns distinct s-cliques, there is a complete s-partite subgraph with each class of size ⌊as​(η)logn⌋, containing that many pairwise vertex-disjoint transversal members of M, each with one vertex in every class. Extra edges within its classes can be ignored. We may suppose 0<η<1/2. For s=1, any ηn singletons suffice, and we may take a1​(η)=1 once n is large.
For s≥2, put θ=η/2. Repeatedly delete every member of the current family containing an (s−1)-clique whose number of extensions is at most θn. Each such face is processed at most once, and there are at most ns−1 possible faces. At most θns members are deleted. The remaining family L has size at least (η/2)ns, and every face that remains has more than θn extensions. The logarithmic order cannot be increased in a uniform forcing result. Put ρ=1−1/r+ϵ<1, choose p strictly between ρ and one, and take a binomial random graph. Its edge density exceeds ρ with probability tending to one, by the variance estimate for a sum of independent edge indicators. Both events hold simultaneously for some graphs of every sufficiently large order. There are graphs meeting the density hypothesis whose largest balanced blow-up has part size O(logn). This upper bound concerns what density can force; it is not an upper bound for every dense graph, since a complete graph has much larger blow-ups.
Finally, Hk​ is the square of a cycle, for k≥3. Any three consecutive vertices form a triangle in a graph. In a proper three-colouring, once the first three colours are fixed, each subsequent vertex must repeat the colour three positions earlier. Closing the cycle is possible precisely when 3∣k. Conversely, repeating the three colours gives such a colouring whenever 3∣k. In that case Hk​ embeds in K3​(k/3), and the density 0.51(2n​) exceeds the bipartite Turan theorem threshold by a fixed amount, so the theorem forces Hk​ eventually. If 3∤k, the balanced Turán graphs T3​(n) have density at least 2/3 and contain no graph of chromatic number greater than three. They give a counterexample sequence. Exactly the cycle lengths k≥3 divisible by three have the required property.
