Use the intrinsic boundary of a simply connected domain, so different approaches to the two sides of a slit remain distinct. The mapping-out function gK​ extends as a homeomorphism from this intrinsic compactification to that of the complex upper half-plane. Set T=T(H). The imaginary coordinate of planar Brownian motion hits zero in finite time almost surely, and T is no larger than that time. Thus T<∞ and continuity gives a finite Euclidean exit point.
By conformal invariance of planar Brownian motion, gK​(Bt​) is a planar Brownian motion in the complex upper half-plane, run with clock
A(t)=∫0t​∣gK′​(Bs​)∣2ds.
(1)
The terminal clock cannot be infinite: that would make the transformed Brownian motion stay in the upper half-plane forever. It cannot stop while the transformed path is in the interior either, since continuity of gK−1​ would then put the original exit point inside H. Hence the terminal clock is precisely the transformed Brownian exit time. The transformed path converges to a real boundary point, and applying the extended inverse proves almost sure convergence to a point of the intrinsic boundary.
Write gK​(x+iy)=u+iv, and let E=gK​(S)∩R. The point at infinity has zero harmonic measure. Conformal invariance of planar Brownian motion and the Poisson kernel for the upper half-plane give
Px+iy​(BT​∈S)=∫E​π((t−u)2+v2)v​dt.
(2)
The hydrodynamic normalization at infinity gives gK​(z)=z+O(1/z), so along the specified approach v/y→1 and u/y→0. For each fixed real t,
(t−u)2+v2yv​⟶1,0≤(t−u)2+v2yv​≤vy​.
(3)
If Leb(E)<∞, dominated convergence applies because y/v is eventually bounded. If Leb(E)=∞, Fatou's lemma makes the limit infinite. This proves the harmonic-measure asymptotic at infinity
y→∞, x/y→0lim​πyPx+iy​(BT​∈S)=Leb(gK​(S)),​
(4)
with the equality understood in the extended nonnegative reals.

The capacity in this question is harmonic capacity from infinity in the upper half-plane, which has units of length; it is distinct from half-plane capacity, which has units of length squared.
Here is a proof of existence that also works with irregular real attachments. The function
u(z)=Pz​(BT(H)​∈K)
(5)
is bounded and harmonic on H, by the Strong Markov property and the mean-value characterization of harmonic functions. Therefore u∘gK−1​ is a bounded harmonic function on the complex upper half-plane, with a Poisson kernel representation
u(gK−1​(w))=∫R​π∣t−w∣2Imw​f(t)dt,0≤f≤1.
(6)
The boundary function f vanishes outside a bounded interval. Indeed, far enough along either real ray the original domain contains a half-disc neighbourhood, and gK​ extends there; the probability of hitting the bounded hull before the real boundary tends to zero as the starting point approaches that ray. Applying the same dominated-limit calculation as in part (i) yields
cap(K)=∫R​f(t)dt<∞.​
(7)
When the intrinsic boundary pieces landing on the hull are identified, f is their indicator almost everywhere and this is the length of their image under gK​. For ordinary finite slit hulls this is exactly the image of δH∖H0​, since real attachment endpoints have zero harmonic measure. The Poisson representation avoids requiring that boundary identification in the general existence argument.
If K⊂K′, couple the two exit events using the same planar Brownian motion, stopped at its first hit of the real axis. Any path that hits K before the real axis also hits K′ before the real axis. Consequently
Piy​(BT(H∖K)​∈K)≤Piy​(BT(H∖K′)​∈K′),
(8)
and taking the limits proves monotonicity of this capacity.
For a half-disc of radius r centred at b∈R, the mapping-out function is
g(z)=b+(z−b)+z−br2​.
(9)
Its semicircular boundary maps onto [b−2r,b+2r], of length 4r. Hence its harmonic capacity from infinity in the upper half-plane is 4r. With
rad(K)=inf{r>0:K⊂{z:∣z−b∣≤r} for some b∈R},
(10)
enclose K in such a half-disc and use monotonicity. Letting the enclosing radius decrease to the infimum proves
cap(K)≤4rad(K).​
(11)
The same conclusion holds if radius is instead measured about a specified real centre.

As printed, the lower bound needs a connectedness hypothesis. This illustrates that radius gives no positive lower bound for disconnected harmonic hull capacity. A compact H-hull need not have connected closure. To see the obstruction, take 0<ε<1/12, set c=1−ε2​, and use three disjoint vertical slits,
Kε​=(0,iε]∪(c,c+iε]∪(−c,−c+iε].
(12)
Their complement in the complex upper half-plane is a simply connected domain: the three slits attach to the real boundary, and create no interior holes. Symmetry shows that the smallest enclosing half-disc is centred at zero and has radius one. More explicitly, any real centre b has maximum distance to the two outer tips at least c2+ε2​=1, while the unit half-disc contains every slit. Also 0∈Kε​ and c+iε has modulus one.
For a vertical slit of height ε, the mapping-out function of a vertical slit maps its two faces onto an interval of length 2ε, so its harmonic capacity from infinity in the upper half-plane is 2ε. Subadditivity of harmonic hull capacity, from the union bound for the Brownian hitting events, gives
cap(Kε​)≤6ε<21​.​
(13)
Thus the requested conclusion does not follow from the printed assumptions.
The intended reflection lower bound for harmonic hull capacity works when the closure is a connected continuum joining the two specified points, as for a slit hull. Reflect in the vertical line through x, using ρ(z)=2x−zˉ. The reflected continuum joins 2x to x+iy. Together the original and reflected continua form a barrier between infinity and the segment I together with the real interval between 0 and 2x. One can first verify this separation for polygonal simple arcs, then use decreasing connected neighbourhoods of the continuum. Hence, for Brownian motion started at x+iY, Y large, reaching I or the real interval J between 0 and x requires a hit of the original or reflected barrier. Reflection symmetry and the union bound give
Px+iY​(BT(H∖I)​∈I∪J)≤2Px+iY​(BT(H)​∈K).
(14)
For the connected slit setting, the real attachment endpoints have zero harmonic measure, so the last event may be written with K.
For y>0, use the branch of the square root fixed by hydrodynamic normalization at infinity:
gI​(z)=x+(z−x)2+y2​.
(15)
The two slit faces together have image length 2y. The interval J has image length x2+y2​−y=1−y, by the square-root formula on the appropriate real side. Thus the image length of I∪J is 1+y. Multiply the probability inequality by πY and use part (i); the allowed starting approach includes x+iY. We obtain
cap(K)≥21+y​≥21​​
(16)
under the stated connected-barrier interpretation. If y=0, the model slit is empty and J has length ∣x∣=1, giving the same lower bound by the real-interval argument. The connectedness repair is essential, as the explicit three-slit counterexample demonstrates.

Use the Chordal Loewner equation with Loewner driving function ξt​=κ​Wt​, and set Zt​=gt​(z)−ξt​=Xt​+iYt​. Before the Loewner swallowing time,
dZt​=Zt​2​dt−κ​dWt​.
(17)
The upper-half-plane branch of the logarithm has imaginary part ht​. The Itô formula gives
dlogZt​=2Zt2​4−κ​dt−Zt​κ​​dWt​,
(18)
and therefore the SLE angle process satisfies
dht​=(κ−4)∣Zt​∣4Xt​Yt​​dt+κ​∣Zt​∣2Yt​​dWt​.​
(19)
At κ=4 the drift vanishes. Since 0<ht​<π, the stopped local martingale is a true bounded martingale, and the SLE4 angle martingale is global for each fixed point almost surely, using fixed-interior-point avoidance of SLE4 for the simple parameter-four Loewner trace.
Conversely, take κ>0. If this semimartingale were a local martingale, uniqueness of its finite-variation decomposition would force (κ−4)Xt​Yt​=0 throughout every compact interval before swallowing. Because Yt​>0, for κ=4 this would force Xt​ to be identically zero on such an interval. Its Brownian component has quadratic variation κt, which makes that impossible. Thus for positive κ, the martingale parameter is exactly four.
If the degenerate value κ=0 is admitted, there is one exception to a fixed-point reading of the assertion: for z on the positive imaginary axis the deterministic flow stays on that axis until swallowing, and ht​=π/2 is constant. For all starting points simultaneously, the unique parameter giving the martingale property is still four.

For a simple Loewner trace, the domain mapped by gt​ is Dt​=H∖γ[0,t]. For a non-simple Loewner trace, use instead the unbounded component Dt​=H∖Kt​; the map gt​ is not defined on swallowed bounded components. This is the necessary domain interpretation when κ>4.
The function Imlog(gt​(z)−ξt​) is harmonic in Dt​ because the logarithm is holomorphic on the complex upper half-plane. Let St−​ and St+​ be the intrinsic boundary sets mapped to (−∞,ξt​) and (ξt​,∞) respectively. The Dirichlet problem has boundary data
ht​=π on St−​,ht​=0 on St+​.​
(20)
For a simple Loewner trace these are the left bank of the Loewner trace together with the negative real boundary, and the right bank together with the positive real boundary. Left and right refer to the orientation from the starting point towards the tip. The tip and infinity correspond to discontinuities of the data; no unique limit is imposed there. They have zero harmonic measure.
More precisely, the bounded solution is
ht​(z)=πωDt​​(z,St−​),
(21)
by the Poisson kernel for the upper half-plane and conformal invariance of planar Brownian motion. This establishes the boundary values in the intrinsic sense and uniqueness among bounded solutions of the Dirichlet problem, without treating the two banks as one Euclidean boundary point.

For parameter four, the SLE4 angle martingale is bounded, so the Continuous-time martingale convergence theorem gives an almost sure limit. The imaginary part of the Chordal Loewner equation gives
dtd​Yt​=−Xt2​+Yt2​2Yt​​,dtd​Yt2​=−4sin2ht​.
(22)
For a point off the full simple Loewner trace the flow exists at every finite time. If the limiting angle lay strictly between 0 and π, the last derivative would eventually be bounded above by a strictly negative constant. That would make Yt2​ negative, a contradiction. Hence the terminal angle is either zero or π.
The simple transient chordal Loewner trace from 0 to infinity splits the complex upper half-plane into a left component D−, adjacent to the negative real boundary, and a right component D+, adjacent to the positive real boundary. The Dirichlet boundary values of the SLE angle process identify which terminal value occurs. Indeed, run an independent planar Brownian motion from z until it first hits the full Loewner trace or the real axis. This time is finite. The Brownian path up to that time is bounded, and Transience of chordal SLE ensures that any Loewner trace point it meets belongs to a finite initial segment. Thus its exit side for the truncated domains eventually agrees with its exit side for the full component. In D− every such exit is through the left bank or negative real boundary; in D+ every exit is through the right bank or positive real boundary. Dominated convergence in the harmonic-measure representation therefore gives
F(z)={π,0,​z∈D−,z∈D+.​​
(23)
This is a random side indicator, not the deterministic value argz. Uniform integrability also gives E[F(z)]=argz, so the SLE4 left-passage probability is
P(z∈D−)=πargz​.​
(24)

The complex form of the Chordal Loewner equation, driven by a continuous real Loewner driving function, is
∂t​gt​(z)=gt​(z)−ξt​2​,g0​(z)=z.​
(25)
For z∈C∖{ξ0​} this is an ordinary differential equation up to its maximal lifetime, when the solution reaches the driving singularity. The coefficients respect complex conjugation, so the lower-half-plane flow is the conjugate of the upper-half-plane flow. On surviving real points it is the real boundary flow. The hydrodynamic normalization at infinity and factor two correspond to half-plane-capacity parameterization hcap(Kt​)=2t.

Assume κ>0 and x=0, since the initial driving point does not admit the displayed ordinary boundary flow. The sign of the driver in this part is negative, so
d(gt​(xκ​)−ξt​)=gt​(xκ​)−ξt​2​dt+κ​dWt​.

After division by κ​, the Boundary-point Bessel flow for SLE is
dXt​=dWt​+Xt​a​dt,X0​=x,a=κ2​.​
(27)
For x>0 this is a Bessel process of dimension δ=1+2a. For x<0, −Xt​ obeys the same equation driven by −Wt​ until hitting zero. It suffices to treat a positive initial value.
The infinitesimal generator is Lf=21​f′′+(a/u)f′. An increasing scale function of a one-dimensional diffusion is
s(u)={u1−2a/(1−2a),logu,​a=1/2,a=1/2.​
(28)
It satisfies Ls=0. For 0<ε<x<b, optional stopping theorem gives the boundary hitting probability from a diffusion scale function
Px​(τε​<τb​)=s(b)−s(ε)s(b)−s(x)​.
(29)
When a<1/2, put β=1−2a>0. The inner boundary is reached in finite time: the nonnegative function
v(u)=1+2ab2−βuβ−u2​
(30)
vanishes at 0,b and satisfies Lv=−1. Applying the Itô formula before exiting (ε,b) gives E(τε​∧τb​)≤v(x). As ε↓0, these times increase to a finite limiting exit time almost surely. Thus the scale limit is an actual hitting event, not just asymptotic approach to zero, and
Px​(τ0​<τb​)=1−(x/b)β.

The Phase classification of the SLE trace has its simple range
0≤κ≤4,​
(33)
with κ=0 the deterministic vertical slit. For positive κ, the dividing parameter is precisely δ=1+4/κ=2 in the Boundary-point Bessel flow for SLE.
Here is the reason this diffusion threshold controls simplicity. For κ≤4, no nonzero real boundary point is swallowed. It suffices to check rational boundary points: a first meeting with either nonzero real half-axis would close a boundary crosscut and swallow a nonempty real interval, including a rational point. Thus the Loewner trace stays in the complex upper half-plane apart from its starting point. By the domain Markov property of a chordal Loewner chain, after any fixed rational time s the future mapped and centred Loewner trace has the same boundary-avoidance property.
Suppose two Loewner trace times r<r′ had the same image. Positive half-plane capacity growth rules out constancy on a nonempty time interval, so continuity supplies a rational s∈(r,r′) with γs​=γr​. In the mapped future, the point at r′ is either in the open upper half-plane or at the starting boundary point 0; it cannot lie at another real point. The first case puts γr′​ inside the surviving domain at s, whereas γr​ lies on the past Loewner trace. The second gives γr′​=γs​, also contradicting the choice of s. Boundary continuity of the inverse mapping-out function makes these identifications valid. This proves that the Loewner trace has no repeated points.
For κ>4, part (ii) makes a fixed positive real point have finite swallowing time, so the SLE boundary swallowing criterion ensures that the Loewner trace hits the positive real axis. In fact positive boundary-interval hitting probability for SLE above parameter four holds: any interval J⊂(0,∞) has positive hitting probability. To prove this, cover the positive axis by countably many dilates of J. If J had zero hitting probability, Scaling invariance of SLE would give zero probability for every dilate, contradicting the almost sure hit of the positive axis. Reflection gives the same conclusion for negative intervals.
At a fixed positive time, if the past Loewner trace already repeats a point there is nothing to prove. Otherwise, boundary continuity of the inverse mapping-out function supplies a nonempty real interval, away from the current driving point, mapped back into the earlier Loewner trace in the open upper half-plane. Such an interval exists because positive capacity growth creates a genuine Loewner trace boundary in the interior; choose an accessible boundary point away from the tip and then a small interval around its preimage. Conditional on the past, the domain Markov property of a chordal Loewner chain gives a future centred SLE. With positive conditional probability its Loewner trace hits this interval, by the preceding boundary-interval argument. Mapping back then gives a visit to the earlier Loewner trace. Thus a repeated point occurs by some finite time with positive probability.
Finally let Et​ be the event of a repeated point by time t. Scaling invariance of SLE makes P(Et​) the same for every t>0. The positive finite-time probability just proved makes this common value positive. Hence ⋂n​E1/n​ also has positive probability. This event belongs to the Brownian germ sigma-field; the Blumenthal zero-one law forces its probability to be one. Consequently the Loewner trace is not simple almost surely for every κ>4. At κ=4, the logarithmic scale function gives non-hitting of zero, so equality belongs to the simple range.

A compact H-hull is a bounded relatively closed subset K of the complex upper half-plane such that H∖K is a simply connected domain. This does not require its closure to be connected. Its mapping-out function is the unique conformal map gK​:H∖K→H with hydrodynamic normalization at infinity,
gK​(z)=z+zaK​​+O(∣z∣−2).
(34)
The half-plane capacity is
hcap(K)=aK​=z→∞lim​z(gK​(z)−z)≥0.​
(35)
The coefficient is zero exactly for the empty hull.
In terms of the least radius of a real-centred enclosing half-disc, the sharp displacement bound for a compact H-hull, also called the continuity estimate, is
∣gK​(z)−z∣≤3rad(K),z∈H∖K.​
(36)
The differentiability estimate for a mapping-out function is a uniform small-hull expansion: there is an absolute constant C such that if K⊂{z:∣z−ξ∣≤r} with ξ∈R, then
​gK​(z)−z−z−ξhcap(K)​​≤∣z−ξ∣2Crhcap(K)​,∣z−ξ∣>2r.​
(37)
These are statements of the two requested estimates. The second is not merely a bound for gK′​: it controls the first-order change of a mapping-out function when a small hull is removed.

Take increasing to mean strict growth on every nonempty time interval; otherwise a constant family has no uniquely determined driving point. The half-plane-capacity parameterization imposed below guarantees strict growth. For s<t, define the increment hull in the mapped domain by
Ks,t​=H∖gs​(H∖Kt​).
(38)
This definition includes filling and boundary conventions automatically. Its mapping-out function is gs,t​=gt​∘gs−1​. The Loewner local growth property means
0≤t≤Tsup​rad(Kt,t+h​)⟶0(h↓0)​
(39)
for every finite T within the parameter range. Equivalently, the mapped increments have uniformly vanishing diameter on compact time intervals.
For fixed t, the nonempty compact Euclidean closures Kt,t+h​ are nested as h decreases. Their diameters tend to zero, so their intersection is a singleton. Its point lies on the real axis: the imaginary part of any point in a hull is at most its enclosing radius. Define the Loewner transform by
{ξt​}=h>0⋂​Kt,t+h​.​
(40)
Since ξt​ lies in each closure, every point of Kt,t+h​ is at distance at most 2rad(Kt,t+h​) from it.
To prove continuity, choose z∈Kt+2h​∖Kt+h​ and write w=gt​(z), w′=gt+h​(z). Then w∈Kt,t+2h​, w′∈Kt+h,t+2h​ and w′=gt,t+h​(w). The continuity estimate from part (i) gives
∣ξt+h​−ξt​∣​≤∣ξt​−w∣+∣w−w′∣+∣w′−ξt+h​∣≤2rad(Kt,t+2h​)+3rad(Kt,t+h​)+2rad(Kt+h,t+2h​).​
(41)
The right-hand side tends to zero uniformly on compact time intervals. Applying the same inequality with the earlier time as the base proves left continuity as well. Thus the Loewner transform is continuous.
Now impose hcap(Kt​)=2t. The half-plane-capacity composition rule follows by composing Laurent expansions at infinity and gives
hcap(Ks,t​)=2(t−s).
(42)
For a point not yet swallowed, put zs​=gs​(z) and rs,t​=2rad(Ks,t​). The increment is contained in the half-disc of radius rs,t​ centred at ξs​. The continuity estimate first proves continuity of s↦gs​(z): its increment is bounded by 3rad(Ks,t​). The differentiability estimate for a mapping-out function then gives, when t−s is small enough,
gt​(z)−gs​(z)=gs​(z)−ξs​2(t−s)​+O(∣gs​(z)−ξs​∣2rs,t​(t−s)​).
(43)
On a compact interval before swallowing the denominator stays away from zero. Divide by t−s and let t↓s. The local-growth property makes the error tend to zero. The analogous backward quotient has the same limit, using continuity of gs​(z) and ξs​. Thus the Chordal Loewner equation follows:
∂t​gt​(z)=gt​(z)−ξt​2​,g0​(z)=z.​
(44)
The initial value follows from hcap(K0​)=0, hence K0​=∅. Without the capacity parameterization, the same argument gives dgt​(z)=dhcap(Kt​)/(gt​(z)−ξt​), interpreted with the capacity clock.
