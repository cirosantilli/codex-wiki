There is a small domain issue in the printed notation. If R3/R2 means a quotient vector space by the horizontal plane, the displayed formula does not descend to that quotient vector space: (0,0,0) and (1,0,0) represent the same class but give different values. The intended construction is stereographic projection, restricted to the unit sphere with the north pole removed. Interpreting the slash as removal of the plane z=1 also supplies a suitable ambient domain. The holomorphic stereographic atlas of the sphere is obtained as follows.
Write N=(0,0,1) and S=(0,0,−1). On UN​=S2∖{N} use ζ=(x+iy)/(1−z). Its inverse, with ζ=u+iv, is
x=1+u2+v22u​,y=1+u2+v22v​,z=1+u2+v2u2+v2−1​.
(1)
These formulas give a smooth manifold chart from UN​ onto C. On US​=S2∖{S} choose the second manifold chart
η=1+zx−iy​.
(2)
Its inverse is x=2Reη/(1+∣η∣2), y=−2Imη/(1+∣η∣2) and z=(1−∣η∣2)/(1+∣η∣2), so this is also a smooth manifold chart onto C. The conjugation in this second stereographic projection is essential. On the overlap, x2+y2=1−z2, so
ζη=1−z2x2+y2​=1,η=ζ−1​.
(3)
Both directions of this transition are holomorphic maps on C×, with nonzero derivative. The two manifold charts cover the sphere, hence define a holomorphic atlas, giving precisely the Riemann sphere. If one instead used x+iy in both manifold charts, the transition would be 1/ζˉ​ and would not be holomorphic.
Orient the sphere by this holomorphic atlas. The given volume form is smooth at infinity: replacing ζ by 1/η in its exterior product gives the same expression
Ω=(1+∣η∣2)2idη∧dηˉ​​.
(4)
Moreover, idζ∧dζˉ​=2du∧dv, so the normalization of this volume form is
∫S2​Ω=2∫02π​∫0∞​(1+r2)2r​drdθ=2π.
(5)
Thus the printed volume form has half the area of the standard round unit sphere; replacing its integral by 4π would introduce an erroneous factor of two.
For k≥1, the holomorphic map f(ζ)=ζk extends over infinity, since the target reciprocal coordinate is ηk when the source reciprocal coordinate is η. Its pullback of a differential form is
f∗Ω=(1+∣ζ∣2k)2ik2∣ζ∣2k−2dζ∧dζˉ​​.
(6)
Using s=r2k gives
∫S2​f∗Ω=4πk2∫0∞​(1+r2k)2r2k−1​dr=2πk∫0∞​(1+s)2ds​=2πk.
(7)
The degree of a map between oriented manifolds is therefore
degf=∫S2​Ω∫S2​f∗Ω​=k​.
(8)
For k=0, the formula on the finite manifold chart is the constant 1. Its unique continuous extension is also 1 at infinity, rather than an undefined expression ∞0. Its pullback of a differential form is zero and its degree of a map between oriented manifolds is zero.
For the preimage calculation when k≥1, choose a regular value w∈C×. There are exactly k distinct roots of ζk=w. At each root the real Jacobian determinant is ∣kζk−1∣2>0, so every local contribution to the degree of a map between oriented manifolds is +1. Their sum is k, agreeing with the integral. The exceptional values 0 and infinity are avoided because they are branch values when k>1. For the constant map, any w=1 is a regular value with no preimages, giving the same answer zero. This establishes the degree of a power map of the Riemann sphere for every allowed k.
