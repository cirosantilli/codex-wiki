For an incompressible flow, write the Newtonian fluid stress tensor as σ=−pI+2μe, where e=(∇u+∇uT)/2 is the rate-of-strain tensor. The Stokes equation gives ∂j​σij​=0. Symmetry of the Cauchy stress tensor therefore gives
∂j​(ui​σij​)=σij​∂j​ui​=σ:e=2μe:e.
(1)
Integrating and applying the divergence theorem expresses the viscous dissipation as boundary power:
D=∫∂V​u⋅σndS.​
(2)
At a moving rigid body, the surface velocity is U+Ω×(x−xc​). Its boundary-power contribution is U⋅F+Ω⋅G, with the force and torque evaluated using the fluid's outward normal vector. Both resultants vanish for a force-free, torque-free inclusion. Thus the new viscous dissipation comes entirely from the unchanged outer boundary velocity. Subtracting the particle-free boundary power gives
D′=∫∂V0​​U0​⋅σ′ndS.
(3)
Apply the Lorentz reciprocal theorem to (u0​,σ0​) and (u′,σ′) in the fluid outside the inclusion. On the outer boundary u′=0, so
0=D′+∫A​(u0​⋅σ′−u′⋅σ0​)ndS.
(4)
Writing np​=−n for the particle's outward normal vector gives the required extra dissipation due to a rigid inclusion:
D′=∫A​(u0​⋅σ′−u′⋅σ0​)np​dS.​
(5)
This subtraction already accounts for the fluid volume displaced by the inclusion; it is not just the integral of the disturbance's local viscous dissipation.
For the sphere, the ambient rate-of-strain tensor E is symmetric and trace free. The sphere in a uniform straining Stokes flow has zero translational and rotational velocity: inversion symmetry eliminates its force, and symmetry of E eliminates its torque. To derive the disturbance, put r=∣x∣, Q=x⋅Ex and seek
u′=f(r)Ex+g(r)Qx,p′=μj(r)Q.
(6)
The incompressible flow condition and Stokes equation reduce to
rf′​+rg′+5g=0,f′′+r4​f′+4g=2j,g′′+r8​g′=rj′​.
(7)
A decaying family satisfying these equations is f=Ar−5, g=Br−5−5Ar−7/2, j=2Br−5. The no-slip boundary condition requires f(a)=−1 and g(a)=0, so A=−a5 and B=−5a3/2. Consequently
u′=−r5a5​Ex−2r55a3​(1−r2a2​)(x⋅Ex)x,p′=−r55μa3​(x⋅Ex).​

The total velocity is zero at r=a, and the disturbance decays as r−2. The pressure constant has been set to zero. These boundary and far-field conditions, together with Uniqueness of Stokes flow, establish the solution. There is no rotational background in a pure strain flow.
