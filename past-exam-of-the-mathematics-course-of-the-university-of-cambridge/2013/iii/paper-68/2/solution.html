For two Stokes flows (u(1),σ(1)) and (u(2),σ(2)) of the same dynamic viscosity in the same region, with no volume force, the Lorentz reciprocal theorem states
∫∂V​u(1)⋅σ(2)ndS=∫∂V​u(2)⋅σ(1)ndS.​
(12)
Indeed, the divergence of their cross-work difference is
∂j​(ui(1)​σij(2)​−ui(2)​σij(1)​)=2μ(e(1):e(2)−e(2):e(1))=0.
(13)
The pressure terms vanish by incompressible flow, and the derivatives of the Cauchy stress tensors vanish by the Stokes equation. The divergence theorem proves the result.
Use the convention that F,G are the force and torque exerted by the body on the fluid. By Linearity of Stokes flow, (F,G)=R(U,Ω). Applying the Lorentz reciprocal theorem to two independent rigid motions gives q1T​Rq2​=q2T​Rq1​, so the hydrodynamic resistance matrix is a symmetric matrix. The boundary-power identity gives
qTRq=F⋅U+G⋅Ω=2μ∫e:edV.
(14)
This is strictly positive for nonzero rigid motion: equality would imply e=0, hence a rigid motion throughout the connected fluid, which must vanish since the fluid is at rest at infinity. The no-slip boundary condition would then force q=0. Therefore R is a positive-definite matrix. Reversing to the fluid-on-body force changes the sign of the force law, not the positive resistance coefficients.
For the two rods, take the torque about O and use body axes. On the x-rod, X=(s,0,0), 0≤s≤2L, and its slender-body force density is
fx​=C(2Ux​​, Uy​+sΩz​, Uz​−sΩy​).
(15)
On the y-rod it is
fy​=C(Ux​−sΩz​, 2Uy​​, Uz​+sΩx​).
(16)
Integrate f and X×f along both rods, using ∫ds=2L, ∫sds=2L2 and ∫s2ds=8L3/3. A convenient dimensionally uniform statement of the complete right-angle two-rod resistance matrix is
​Fx​Fy​Fz​Gx​/LGy​/LGz​/L​​=CL​30000−2​030002​0042−20​0028/300​00−208/30​−2200016/3​​​Ux​Uy​Uz​LΩx​LΩy​LΩz​​​.​
(17)
In physical coordinates this means translation–translation entries scale as CL, the two translation–rotation blocks as CL2, and rotation–rotation entries as CL3. The planar block has inverse
​30−2​032​−2216/3​​−1=​1/2−1/61/4​−1/61/2−1/4​1/4−1/43/8​​.
(18)
This verifies the printed hydrodynamic mobility matrix with its third velocity component LΩz​; the TeX aid's Ωx​ is an OCR error.
Take laboratory vertical velocity positive upwards. The body axes are e1​=(cosθ,sinθ) and e2​=(−sinθ,cosθ). In quasistatic sedimentation the force and torque exerted on the fluid equal the gravitational resultants on the body:
Fx​=−(1+2λ)mgsinθ,Fy​=−(1+2λ)mgcosθ,Gz​=2λmgL(sinθ−cosθ).
(19)
The out-of-plane block is unforced, so its positive hydrodynamic resistance matrix gives Uz​=Ωx​=Ωy​=0. Substitution in the planar hydrodynamic mobility matrix gives
mgCL​Ux​=61−λ​cosθ−21+λ​sinθ,mgCL​Uy​=61−λ​sinθ−21+λ​cosθ,
(20)
and
CL2θ˙=4(1−λ)mg​(cosθ−sinθ).​
(21)
For 0≤λ<1, this ordinary differential equation has positive right side on 0≤θ<π/4, with a stable zero at π/4. Uniqueness prevents crossing that equilibrium in finite time. For λ>1 the initial angular velocity is negative, and the stable equilibrium reached from zero is −3π/4. The heavier-end body turns clockwise through 3π/4; it does not settle at π/4. More explicitly, with K=(1−λ)mg/(4CL2),
tan2θ−π/4​=−tan(π/8)e−2​Kt,
(22)
where the continuous branch has θ−π/4∈(−π,0). At λ=1, θ remains zero.
Transforming the translational velocity back to laboratory axes gives
Uh​=6CL(1−λ)mg​cos2θ​,Uv​=CLmg​[−21+λ​+61−λ​sin2θ]<0.
(23)
For λ=1, eliminate time between Uh​ and the angular velocity:
dθdxO​​=32L​(cosθ+sinθ),xO​(θ)−xO​(0)=32L​(1+sinθ−cosθ).
(24)
At either limiting orientation sinθ=cosθ. Thus both cases have the same net horizontal displacement,
xO​(∞)−xO​(0)=2L/3.​
(25)
For λ<1 the drift is monotonically rightwards. For λ>1 it first moves left, reaching xO​−xO​(0)=(2L/3)(1−2​) at θ=−π/4, then reverses. The total horizontal path length in this second case is (2L/3)(22​−1), whereas its net displacement is 2L/3 rightwards. At λ=1 it falls vertically without rotation or drift; taking the infinite-time limit before λ→1 is consequently singular.
For the requested sedimentation drift of a weighted two-rod body sketch, a full parametric trajectory follows by also integrating Uv​/θ˙. Put ψ=θ−π/4 and set the initial height to zero:
zO​(θ)=3(1−λ)22​L(1+2λ)​lntan(π/8)∣tan(ψ/2)∣​−322​L​(cosψ−2​1​).
(26)
It tends to −∞ in either case while xO​ tends to 2L/3. At λ<1 both rods end pointing upwards from O symmetrically; at λ>1 they end pointing downwards symmetrically. The asymptotic downward speed is (1+2λ)mg/(3CL).
