Particle gauge fixing and BRST. Put C=(p2+μ2)/2. The classical constraint generates δε​xm=εpm, δε​pm​=0, δε​e=ε˙. Consequently the variation of e−eˉ along a gauge orbit is the operator ∂t​ on ε. A path integral restricted to that gauge must include its Faddeev-Popov determinant; anticommuting FP ghosts represent the determinant, rather than its inverse. Constant gauge zero modes and any proper-time modulus must be treated separately, so the relevant determinant is det′∂t​.
A convenient point-particle Faddeev–Popov ghost action at eˉ=1 is
S=∫dt[x˙⋅p−C+ibc˙].​
(26)
The FP ghost normalization has been chosen to give the graded Poisson bracket {b,c}=−i, with the bracket symmetric on two odd variables. The particle BRST charge and Klein–Gordon constraint are
Q=cC,δF={F,ΛQ},
(27)
where the odd constant parameter Λ is placed on the left. The product ΛQ is even. Accounting for the odd parameter in this convention gives
δxm=Λcpm,δpm​=0,δc=0,δb=iΛC.​
(28)
These are canonical BRST transformations. Direct variation checks the sign: the bosonic kinetic term changes by Λ(c˙p2+cp⋅p˙​), while the FP ghost term changes by −ΛCc˙. Hence
δL=dtd​[Λc(p2−C)],
(29)
and the action is invariant for the corresponding boundary conditions. The BRST charge is conserved because C commutes with the gauge-fixed Hamiltonian and c is constant on the FP ghost equation of motion.
Quantization gives {b,c}+​=1 and c2=0. Since C commutes with the FP ghosts,
Q​2=c2C2=0.​
(30)
It generates the same variations by δF=−i[F,ΛQ​], with an ordinary commutator against the even generator. Represent c by multiplication and b by differentiation. On a ghost-number-zero wavefunction Ψ(x), Q​Ψ=0 is exactly CΨ=0, namely
(−□+μ2)Ψ=0,​
(31)
the Klein-Gordon equation for the mostly-plus metric. The specified FP ghost sector matters: for an unrestricted wavefunction Ψ0​+cΨ1​, BRST closure only constrains Ψ0​, not every component independently. The complete physical prescription uses BRST cohomology, not an assertion that every vector in the entire ghost-extended kernel is a new Klein–Gordon particle.
String oscillator algebra and FP ghost Virasoro generators. Use the same bosonic brackets as above and the odd brackets
{αkm​,αln​}PB​=−ikηmnδk+l,0​,{bk​,cl​}PB​=−iδk+l,0​.
(32)
Quantization turns them into
[αkm​,αln​]=kηmnδk+l,0​,{bk​,cl​}+​=δk+l,0​,{bk​,bl​}+​={ck​,cl​}+​=0.​
(33)
The b,c here are worldsheet ghost fields, not the matter fermion oscillators used in the NS question.
Contract bm​ with the two c factors in the cubic FP ghost term of the given BRST operator. The first contraction contributes −21​∑q​(m−q)c−q​bm+q​ and the second contributes +21​∑p​(p−m)c−p​bp+m​. Their sum is ∑q​(q−m)c−q​bm+q​. Reordering and normal ordering therefore give the ghost oscillator Virasoro generators
Lm​=Lm(α)​+Lm(gh)​,Lm(α)​=21​k∑​:αk​⋅αm−k​:,Lm(gh)​=k∑​(m−k):bm+k​c−k​:.​
(34)
For m=0 these formulas have no additive ambiguity. At m=0, moving annihilators past creators produces infinite zero-point sums. Their regularized finite constant shifts L0​ and corresponds to a term proportional to c0​ in Q. The convention fixed below has a ghost oscillator vacuum weight −1, or equivalently the usual intercept-one shift. Whether that shift is written outside the normally ordered sum or incorporated in its definition is a convention; it must not be omitted twice or counted twice. In the chosen oscillator convention this is explicitly
L0​=21​α02​+∑k>0​α−k​⋅αk​+∑k>0​k(b−k​ck​+c−k​bk​)−1.
(35)
Equivalently, the normally ordered BRST charge contains the intercept term −c0​; the formal un-ordered operator in the question includes that choice only after its ordering prescription is fixed.
The oscillator brackets directly give
[Lm​,bn​]=(m−n)bm+n​,[Lm​,cn​]=−(2m+n)cm+n​.
(36)
If Q2=0, then [Ln​,Q]=[{bn​,Q},Q]=[bn​,Q2]=0. The graded Jacobi identity now yields Virasoro closure from BRST nilpotence:
[Lm​,Ln​]​=[{bm​,Q},Ln​]={[bm​,Ln​],Q}+{bm​,[Q,Ln​]}=(m−n){bm+n​,Q}=(m−n)Lm+n​.​
(37)
In particular a central extension cannot remain in the total generators if the quantum BRST charge is nilpotent.
Low-level descendant calculation. Let ∣Ω;p⟩ be the oscillator ground state, annihilated by αn>0​, bn≥0​ and cn>0​. All Ln>0​ annihilate it. The nonzero-mode FP ghost generators act as
L−1(gh)​∣Ω⟩=−b−1​c0​∣Ω⟩,L−2(gh)​∣Ω⟩=−2b−2​c0​∣Ω⟩−3b−1​c−1​∣Ω⟩.
(38)
For example L1​(−b−1​c0​∣Ω⟩)=−2b0​c0​∣Ω⟩=−2∣Ω⟩. Similarly L2​ on the two level-two terms gives −8∣Ω⟩ and −9∣Ω⟩, hence −17∣Ω⟩. These signs are essential: FP ghosts have an indefinite pairing.
The matter generators create
L−1(α)​∣Ω⟩=α0​⋅α−1​∣Ω⟩,L−2(α)​∣Ω⟩=(α0​⋅α−2​+21​α−1​⋅α−1​)∣Ω⟩.
(39)
Contracting the level-one term with L1​ gives α02​. At level two the first term gives 2α02​, and the double contraction of the second gives D/2; cross terms vanish because oscillator levels differ. Thus the normalized oscillator/Virasoro descendant coefficients, conventionally denoted by the requested norms, are
∥L−1​∣Ω⟩∥osc2​=α02​−2,∥L−2​∣Ω⟩∥osc2​=2α02​+2D​−17.​
(40)
Comparing the first with [L1​,L−1​]=2L0​ gives the oscillator highest weight
h0​=21​α02​−1.​
(41)
In this normalization the question's ⟨0∣L0​∣0⟩ is h0​. Comparing level two with [L2​,L−2​]=4L0​ gives
2α02​+2D​−17=4(21​α02​−1),D=26.​
(42)
This is the bosonic-string critical dimension from ghost descendants. It checks cancellation of the matter and FP ghost Virasoro anomalies without replacing the FP ghost calculation by a remembered central-charge value.
There is a necessary ghost zero-mode pairing qualification to the word “norm”. If b0​,c0​ are Hermitian in the full FP ghost space and b0​∣Ω⟩=0, then
⟨Ω∣Ω⟩=⟨Ω∣{b0​,c0​}∣Ω⟩=0.
(43)
A bare full-ghost inner product cannot at the same time normalize this ket to one. FP ghost zero modes need saturation in actual amplitudes. The above equations are the coefficients of L1​L−1​∣Ω⟩ and L2​L−2​∣Ω⟩, or the corresponding normalized contravariant Virasoro form. They are not positive-definite Hilbert norms. The algebraic coefficient calculation is sufficient for the requested critical-dimension argument and remains valid without that false normalization.
Finally, impose the stated ground-state conditions Q∣Ω;p⟩=b0​∣Ω;p⟩=0. They imply L0​∣Ω;p⟩={b0​,Q}∣Ω;p⟩=0, so h0​=0 and
α02​=2,p2=α′1​,Mground2​=−α′1​.​
(44)
This is the bosonic tachyon. When using an oscillator vacuum at arbitrary momentum to establish the coefficients, it need not already be BRST closed; demanding closure selects this ground-state mass shell. The two uses should not be confused.
