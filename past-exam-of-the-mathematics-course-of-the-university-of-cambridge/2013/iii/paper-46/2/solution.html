First-class reduction. A regular set of independent constraints is first class when their Poisson brackets vanish on the constraint surface, equivalently locally {φi​,φj​}=Cij​kφk​. The coefficients can be functions on phase space. With the generator G=∑i​ξiφi​, an infinitesimal canonical gauge transformation is
δF={F,G}.​
(13)
Each independent first-class condition removes one phase-space dimension, and quotienting its independent gauge orbit removes another. Thus the regular physical phase space has
dimΓphys​=2N−2n.​
(14)
This regular first-class phase-space reduction requires independent constraints and gauge directions. The count can fail at singular strata or for reducible constraints; first-class closure alone does not guarantee independence. For a concrete counterexample to counting redundant equations, take two canonical pairs and φ1​=p1​, φ2​=2p1​. Both equations are first class, but there is only one independent condition and one gauge direction. The surviving pair (q2,p2​) has dimension two, whereas blindly substituting N=n=2 gives zero.
Oscillator symplectic form. The center-of-mass pair (xm,pm​) describes translations and total momentum. The nonzero Fourier coefficients αkm​ describe the standing-wave modes compatible with free-end Neumann boundary conditions; their reality condition is α−k​=αk∗​, and α0m​=2α′​pm in the standard normalization. For k>0, regarding αkm​ as a complex coordinate makes its conjugate momentum iα−k,m​/k. Equivalently write αk​=(qk​+isk​)/2​. Up to a total derivative, its kinetic term is −qk​⋅s˙k​/k, a real canonical form. Inverting this oscillator symplectic form of an open string gives
{xm,pn​}=δmn​,{αjm​,αkn​}=−ijηmnδj+k,0​.​
(15)
The zero oscillator commutes with nonzero oscillators but is not independent of p: {xm,α0n​}=2α′​ηmn. Brackets between the center pair and independent nonzero oscillators vanish.
The quadratic constraints obey
{αkm​,Ln​}=−ikαk+nm​.
(16)
For example, the two terms in the bracket with Ln​=21​∑j​αj​⋅αn−j​ give equal contributions after relabelling j. Applying this identity to both factors of Lm​ gives
{Lm​,Ln​}=−i(m−n)Lm+n​.​
(17)
This classical Virasoro constraint algebra has no central term and closes on the constraints, hence is first class. For G=∑n​ξ−n​Ln​, the oscillator gauge transformation is
δαkm​=−ikn∑​ξ−n​αk+nm​.​
(18)
Reality is respected when ξ−n​=ξn∗​.
Light-cone reduction and mass. Choose light-cone coordinates X±=(X0±XD−1)/2​; a vector square is −2α+α−+α2, with D−2 transverse components. On the proposed gauge slice αk=0+​=0, the variation becomes
δαk+​=−ikξk​α0+​.
(19)
For α0+​=0, every nonzero-mode gauge parameter has an invertible coefficient, so the conditions locally fix the corresponding gauge freedom. Globally this is the usual patch in which X+ is an admissible worldsheet clock; it is not a claim about strings for which that coordinate has turning points. The zero-mode reparameterization is left over.
On that slice the nonzero Virasoro constraints are linear in the longitudinal oscillators:
αk−​=2α0+​1​j∑​αj​⋅αk−j​,k=0.​
(20)
No nonzero longitudinal oscillator remains independent. The residual action is
Sred​=∫dt[x˙mpm​+∑k>0​ki​α−k​⋅α˙k​−λ0​(α′p2+∑k>0​α−k​⋅αk​)].
(21)
This residual mass-shell action in light-cone string gauge displays the remaining zero-mode constraint. At the quantum level normal ordering replaces its oscillator term by N−a. One can further set x+ equal to time and solve for the light-cone Hamiltonian p−=[p2+(N−a)/α′]/(2p+).
Canonical quantization gives the transverse relations
[αjI​,αkJ​]=jδIJδj+k,0​.​
(22)
Here ℏ=1, αk>0​ annihilate the oscillator vacuum, and α−k​ create excitations. Define akI​=αkI​/k​ for k>0. The string level operator is
N=∑k>0,I​α−kI​αkI​=∑k>0,I​kakI†​akI​.
(23)
Thus it counts oscillator number weighted by mode number, and has nonnegative integer eigenvalues. The residual mass-shell condition gives
M2=−p2=α′N−a​,α′=2πT1​.​
(24)
The constant a is the normal-ordering constant of a string, or intercept, not an extra classical tension. In the usual Lorentz-invariant critical bosonic string, level one carries the D−2 transverse polarizations of a massless vector. A massive vector would need D−1 polarizations; the longitudinal one is not present. Lorentz consistency therefore requires that this vector level be massless, giving a=1. Equivalently the regularized transverse zero-point value a=(D−2)/24 gives D=26. This critical-vector assumption in the string intercept argument concerns the usual critical quantum theory. In D=3 the lone transverse level-one polarization transforms trivially under the transverse rotation group; a massive scalar interpretation is not excluded by counting. Thus the stated masslessness conclusion is not a consequence of polarization counting for arbitrary D. An arbitrary intercept in a transverse oscillator model also need not define the usual covariant critical theory.
The full self-dual massless spectrum. In the closed-string sector, N and N are the independent nonnegative integer oscillator levels of the two chiral sectors. The integer n quantizes center momentum around the circle, pcircle​=n/R, and w counts how many times the string winds it. At the self-dual circle R=α′​, zero mass requires
2(N+N−2)+n2+w2=0,N−N=nw.
(25)
Both levels are nonnegative, so their sum is at most two. Exhausting these possibilities gives the massless spectrum at the bosonic self-dual circle:
  • N=N=1, (n,w)=(0,0): all states α−1I​α−1J​∣0;n=0,w=0⟩, with arbitrary transverse polarizations.
  • N=1,N=0, (n,w)=(1,1) or (−1,−1): one left-sector level-one oscillator with any transverse polarization.
  • N=0,N=1, (n,w)=(1,−1) or (−1,1): one right-sector level-one oscillator with any transverse polarization.
  • N=N=0, (n,w)=(2,0),(−2,0),(0,2),(0,−2): four oscillator ground states made massless by their momentum or winding energy.
The last family is easy to miss because the uncompactified ground state is a tachyon; its positive compact energy cancels that negative contribution at these charges. There are no other possibilities: at level sum one, n2+w2=2 forces both charges to be ±1; at sum zero, n2+w2=4 gives exactly the four listed pairs. With d=D−2 transverse oscillators the number of independent massless polarizations is d2+4d+4=D2, hence 676 in the critical D=26 bosonic theory. The circle oscillator is included among the d components; it should not be discarded when interpreting the lower-dimensional scalar states.
