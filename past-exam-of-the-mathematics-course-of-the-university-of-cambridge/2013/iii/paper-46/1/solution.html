Actions and equations. Use a mostly-plus Minkowski metric and define the induced worldsheet metric hab​=∂a​X⋅∂b​X. For a nondegenerate timelike worldsheet, the Nambu–Goto action is
SNG​=−T∫d2σ−deth​.
(1)
Varying the independent metric in the Polyakov action sets its worldsheet stress tensor to zero:
hab​−21​γab​γcdhcd​=0.
(2)
In two dimensions this says hab​=Ω2γab​ for a positive local factor. The factor drops out of −γ​γab, and substitution gives the Nambu–Goto action. Conversely, any nondegenerate induced metric solves the auxiliary-metric equation up to a Weyl transformation. This classical equivalence of Polyakov and Nambu–Goto actions is a statement about classical embeddings; quantum equivalence additionally requires treatment of the metric measure and anomaly.
Variation of Xm in the metric action, followed by elimination of γ, gives the Nambu–Goto equations of motion
∂a​(−h​hab∂b​Xm)=0.​
(3)
A closed string has periodic X and no spatial endpoint variation.
For a background metric G, Kalb–Ramond field b, and dilaton Φ, one consistent Lorentzian convention is
SL​=−4πα′1​∫d2σ−γ​γabGmn​(X)∂a​Xm∂b​Xn+4πα′1​∫d2σϵabbmn​(X)∂a​Xm∂b​Xn−4π1​∫d2σ−γ​Φ(X)R(2),T=2πα′1​.
(4)
The orientation fixes the two-form sign; here the Lorentzian curvature convention is chosen so Wick rotation gives the positive Euclidean dilaton term SE,Φ​=∫γ​ΦR(2)/(4π). This avoids hiding the convention in the topology argument. For constant vacuum value Φ0​, the Gauss-Bonnet theorem gives SE,Φ​=Φ0​χ. A connected closed oriented surface of genus g has Euler characteristic χ=2−2g, so
e−SE,Φ​=e−Φ0​(2−2g)=gs2g−2​,gs​=eΦ0​.​

The connected vacuum amplitude has a string genus expansion ∑g≥0​gs2g−2​Ag​; disconnected vacuum diagrams exponentiate the connected sum. Each additional handle supplies a factor gs2​. This is dilaton Euler-characteristic weighting.
The rotating circle. For the specified embedding, the squared spatial speed and tangent length are both 1/2, and their spatial inner product is zero. Including X0=t therefore gives
hab​=(−1/20​01/2​),−h​=1/2.​
(6)
The metric is constant. Its equation reduces to X¨−X′′=0, satisfied by the left- and right-moving trigonometric components and the linear time component. On a constant-time slice with 0≤σ<2π, the proper length of a string is
L=∫02π​hσσ​​dσ=2​π.​
(7)
It is time-independent.
Let R(θ) be an ordinary two-dimensional rotation matrix. Rotate the first coordinate pair by R(t) and the second by R(−t). In these time-dependent Cartesian coordinates both pairs become (cosσ,sinσ)/2. A further fixed orthogonal change of basis gives
Y1​=2​Xrot1​+Xrot3​​=2​cosσ​,Y2​=2​Xrot2​+Xrot4​​=2​sinσ​,Y3​=Y4​=0.
(8)
Thus every spatial slice is a planar circle of radius 1/2​; its plane rotates in the ambient four-space. This is a rigid circular string in four spatial dimensions, not a pulsating circle. The rotating axes establish its spatial shape, not a transformation to an inertial spacetime frame.
The momentum density obtained from the Nambu–Goto action is Pm​=−T−h​hta∂a​Xm​. Here P0​=−T, hence the conserved target-space energy is
E=−∫02π​P0​dσ=2πT=2​TL.​
(9)
The material velocity is transverse to the tangent and has magnitude 1/2​. Its Lorentz factor is 2​, so the excess over TL is kinetic energy. The spatial circle being stationary in rotating axes does not eliminate this energy.
Constraints and endpoints. Varying the multipliers in the Nambu-Goto phase-space action imposes
C=21​(P2+T2X′2)=0,D=P⋅X′=0.
(10)
These are first-class constraints generating normal and tangential worldsheet diffeomorphisms. They remove the two longitudinal embedding degrees of freedom; they are not extra physical force laws. Hamilton's equations are
X˙m=ePm+uX′m,P˙m​=(eT2Xm′​+uPm​)′.​
(11)
For an open string, integration by parts leaves the spatial boundary variation
δSend​=−∫dt[(eT2Xm′​+uPm​)δXm]σ=0σ=π​.
(12)
Allowed variational open-string boundary conditions must make this vanish for every permitted endpoint displacement, and be preserved by the evolution. The bracketed expression is the open-string endpoint momentum flux. For free displacements, set that flux to zero. In a boundary-preserving gauge with u=0 and finite nonzero e, this is the Neumann boundary condition X′m=0 at each end. This shows that free-end string boundary conditions are consistent.
At such an end, C=0 gives P2=0, and X˙=eP gives X˙2=0. For a nontrivial endpoint trajectory with nonzero time velocity, its spatial speed is therefore one. This null motion of a free string endpoint follows from the boundary condition together with the constraints, not merely from the bulk wave equation. The induced metric may degenerate right at a free end; the result is understood as the endpoint limit or in the Polyakov description.
For a fixed spatial p-plane, split coordinates into XA along its (p+1)-dimensional worldvolume, including time, and Xi transverse to it. Set X′A=0 and Xi=yi at the ends. Tangential variations are free and their momentum flux vanishes; normal variations vanish, so their flux need not vanish. Evolution preserves the fixed normal values with Pi=0 at the boundary in the same gauge. These mixed Neumann boundary conditions and Dirichlet boundary conditions consistently restrict the endpoint worldlines to the plane.
The massless open-string excitations then separate into a gauge vector along the worldvolume and transverse scalar fields. A scalar displacement changes the corresponding yi; its interpretation is a fluctuation of the plane's embedding. These worldvolume fields from open-string massless states support the interpretation as a dynamical planar D-brane. In the bosonic theory the still lower ground state is a tachyon, indicating an unstable brane; the interpretation does not require pretending that this tachyon is a stable massless field. Appropriate superstring sectors can remove that instability.
