The printed upper summation limit introduces Zn​ although only qubits 0,…,n−1 were defined. Literally the final term is undefined. Use the natural open-chain repair
Hopen​=∑i=1n−1​hi​,hi​=Xi−1​Zi​.
(23)
This preserves the stated n-qubit system and agrees with the supplied sum-of-squares hint. If a cyclic convention Zn​=Z0​ was intended instead, it must be stated; the same argument works for its n terms when n≥2. For n=1, the repaired open chain is empty and the target is the identity.
Here is a product-formula Hamiltonian simulation using exactly the two supplied lemmas. Let L=n−1 and choose an integer r≥L. Every hi​ is a norm-one Hermitian matrix. One time slice is the product of two-qubit gates
Pr​=eih1​/r⋯eihL​/r,U=Prr​.
(24)
To compare a partial product with ei(h1​+⋯+hj​)/r, first propagate the previous error through the next unitary gate, which preserves the spectral norm, and then use Lemma A with A=−(h1​+⋯+hj−1​)/r, B=−hj​/r. Both norms are at most j/r≤1. Its new error is at most c(j/r)2 for a universal constant c. For an explicit choice, c=4 follows from the unitary Taylor bounds ∥eiA−I−iA∥≤∥A∥2/2 and ∥eiA−I∥≤∥A∥. Induction and the triangle inequality therefore give
∥Pr​−eiHopen​/r∥≤r2c​∑j=2L​j2=O(r2L3​).
(25)
Lemma B, the unitary product telescoping bound, now compares the r repeated slices with (eiHopen​/r)r=eiHopen​:
∥U−eiHopen​∥≤rc∑j=2L​j2​.
(26)
Take, for example, r=max(1,L,⌈2c∑j=2L​j2/ϵ⌉). If the sum is zero the product is already exact; otherwise its error is at most ϵ/2<ϵ. There are Lr two-qubit gates. This explicit lemma-based construction has fourth-degree dependence on n for fixed precision:
gate count=O(n2+n4/ϵ),∥U−U∥<ϵ.​
(27)
This is a sufficient polynomial, not an optimality claim. The polynomial-degree statement treats ϵ as fixed; the inverse-precision dependence is displayed separately. No first-order term error is accumulated without the required repeated-slice factor.
