A genuinely nonlinear example is the bracket-linear frequency function

It has ≫N3 exact additive quadruples, yet agrees with any affine function ah+b(mod1) at only o(N) shifts, uniformly in the affine function. The question permits giving this example without proof, but the mechanism is useful. For a pair with sum s, the value ⌊2​h1​⌋+⌊2​h2​⌋ has only two possibilities, ⌊2​s⌋ or ⌊2​s⌋−1. There are O(N) pair classes, so Cauchy-Schwarz produces ≫N3 collisions with both additive relations.
To see why no long affine agreement occurs, three agreement points force the corresponding lattice points (h,⌊2​h⌋) to be collinear: eliminating the affine slope gives 3​ times an integer determinant equal to an integer, and irrationality makes that determinant zero. A rational line of slope p/q contains agreement shifts in one residue class modulo q, and closeness of ⌊2​h⌋ to 2​h bounds their span by 1/∣2​−p/q∣. Lines with q large have arbitrarily small possible density; for bounded q, irrationality of 2​ bounds the number uniformly. Thus the maximum agreement is o(N).
