The adjugate matrix is the transpose of the cofactor matrix: adj(A)ij​=(−1)i+jdetAj​,i​. Cofactor expansion gives the adjugate identity
Aadj(A)=adj(A)A=(detA)I.
(1)
For an invertible matrix this yields A−1=adj(A)/detA. Integer entries of A imply integer cofactors, so detA=±1 makes its inverse integral. Conversely, if A and A−1 both have integer entries, their determinants are integers and (detA)(detA−1)=1. The only integer units are ±1. Hence an invertible integer matrix has an integer inverse exactly when it is a unimodular matrix, with determinant ±1. Invertibility is implicit whenever A−1 is used.

3F OurBigBook.com
Use the upper-half-plane model of the hyperbolic plane, whose geodesics are vertical lines and semicircles perpendicular to the real boundary. Hyperbolic and Euclidean angles agree. An isometry takes one of the ultraparallel hyperbolic lines to the imaginary axis. The other then has two finite endpoints on the same side; reflecting if necessary, write them as 0<a<b. Its Euclidean centre and radius are c=(a+b)/2 and r=(b−a)/2.
A geodesic perpendicular to the imaginary axis must be a semicircle centred at zero, of some radius q. Two intersecting Euclidean circles meet orthogonally exactly when their squared centre distance equals the sum of their squared radii. Orthogonality to the second line therefore requires
c2=q2+r2,q=ab​.​
(7)
Since a<ab​<b, the two semicircles intersect at one point in the upper half-plane. This constructs the common perpendicular of ultraparallel hyperbolic lines and proves uniqueness, because its radius is forced.
For the three-line continuation, the three common perpendiculars can be pairwise disjoint, but need not be. Explicit configurations of three ultraparallel lines make both claims transparent. For the positive example, take the three semicircles of radius 1/2 centred at −5/2,0,5/2. Their endpoint intervals are disjoint. Their common perpendiculars are the semicircles with centres −5/4,5/4,0 and radii 21​/4,21​/4,6​, respectively. The two smaller semicircles are disjoint because 2(21​/4)<5/2; each is nested inside the largest because 5/4+21​/4<6​. Hence their full geodesics are disjoint. For the negative example, take three concentric semicircles of radii 1,2,3. They are pairwise ultraparallel, but every common perpendicular is the imaginary axis, so the perpendiculars coincide.

The external direct sum of vector spaces consists of pairs (v1​,v2​) with componentwise operations. For subspaces of one ambient space, the sum of vector subspaces is V1​+V2​={v1​+v2​:vi​∈Vi​}. The addition map from the external direct sum onto this subspace is surjective, with kernel {(w,−w):w∈V1​∩V2​}. This kernel is isomorphic to the intersection. For the intended linearly elastic balloon discharge model, take an initially stationary, inviscid plug of water in the constant-area tube. Put B=πa2 and pin​−patm​=KV with K>0. (54)

(55)
Acceleration stays positive until emptying, so the maximum speed is reached at that instant. The time is independent of initial volume, while the maximum speed is not.

First realize the chain as a random walk on a multigraph: put three parallel edges between a,b, two between a,c, and one between b,c. There are six edges and vertex degrees 5,4,3. Choosing an incident edge uniformly gives exactly the stated transition probabilities. The stationary distribution of a graph random walk is the degree divided by twelve, π=(5,4,3)/12, and detailed balance follows because each edge multiplicity contributes the same flux in both directions.
For the first positive return time, Kac's lemma gives
k(a,a)=1/πa​=12/5.​

k(a,b)=21/11,k(c,b)=25/11.​

The chain is a reversible Markov chain, so detailed balance makes the stationary path probability
πi0​​Pi0​i1​​⋯Pim−1​im​​=πim​​Pim​im−1​​⋯Pi1​i0​​.

A path contains an ordered occurrence of a followed later by b exactly when its reversal contains b followed later by a. These occurrences need not be adjacent. Therefore Pr(τ(a,b)≤m)=Pr(τ(b,a)≤m) for every m, and in particular the two finite expectations are equal. This is ordered-state visit times in a reversible chain.
To decompose the expectations, distinguish the entrance time h(i,j), which allows time zero and has h(j,j)=0, from the positive return convention k(j,j) in part (i). The Strong Markov property at the first visit to a or b gives
Eτ(a,b)=∑i​πi​h(i,a)+k(a,b),Eτ(b,a)=∑i​πi​h(i,b)+k(b,a).
