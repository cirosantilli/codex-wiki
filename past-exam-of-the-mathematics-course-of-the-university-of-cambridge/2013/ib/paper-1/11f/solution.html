For uniform convergence to k on A, for every η>0 there is N such that ∣kn​(x)−k(x)∣<η for all n≥N and all x∈A. The bound ∣sin(nx)/n​∣≤1/n​ proves uniform convergence to zero, whereas its derivative is n​cos(nx). On an interval containing zero these derivatives diverge at zero, so they cannot converge uniformly. This supplies a counterexample to differentiating a uniformly convergent sequence without additional hypotheses.
For uniform derivative convergence with an anchored value, put g=limkn′​ and a=limkn​(x0​). A uniform limit of continuous functions is continuous. The fundamental theorem of calculus gives
kn​(x)=kn​(x0​)+∫x0​x​kn′​(t)dt.
(28)
Thus kn​(x)→k(x)=a+∫x0​x​g(t)dt, with error bounded by
∣kn​(x0​)−a∣+∣x−x0​∣supA​∣kn′​−g∣.
(29)
This is uniform on bounded subintervals, and on all of A if it is bounded. The fundamental theorem of calculus now gives k′=g, so k is continuously differentiable. For an unbounded A, no global uniform convergence of kn​ follows merely from the displayed hypotheses, but local uniform convergence and the claimed differentiability do.
For the series, use partial sums and differentiate their individual terms:
dxd​n3+1xnsin(nx)​=n3+1nxn−1sin(nx)+nxncos(nx)​.
(30)
For ∣x∣≤1, their absolute values are at most 2n/(n3+1), a summable bound. The Weierstrass M-test makes the derivative series uniformly convergent, while every partial sum is zero at x=0. The anchored-value result therefore proves continuous differentiability on (−1,1), with derivative equal to the displayed termwise derivative series.
