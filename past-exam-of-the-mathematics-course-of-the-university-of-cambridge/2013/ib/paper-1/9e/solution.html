The external direct sum of vector spaces consists of pairs (v1​,v2​) with componentwise operations. For subspaces of one ambient space, the sum of vector subspaces is V1​+V2​={v1​+v2​:vi​∈Vi​}. The addition map from the external direct sum onto this subspace is surjective, with kernel {(w,−w):w∈V1​∩V2​}. This kernel is isomorphic to the intersection. The rank-nullity theorem, together with dim(V1​⊕V2​)=dimV1​+dimV2​, proves the dimension formula for a sum of subspaces.
For the supplied bases, solving for annihilating linear forms gives
V1​={x:x1​+x2​−x3​+x4​=0},V2​={x:2x1​−3x3​+x4​=0}.
(23)
Each basis has rank three, so these hyperplanes are the whole subspaces. The two independent equations make the intersection two-dimensional. Imposing the requested zero components yields
v1​=(0,−2,1,3),v2​=(−2,0,−1,1).​
(24)
Both satisfy the two equations and are independent, since their first components differ while v1​=0. Hence they form the required basis.
