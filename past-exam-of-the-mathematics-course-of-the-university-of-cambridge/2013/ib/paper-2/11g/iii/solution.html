Factor X3+X=X(X−i)(X+i). These factors generate pairwise comaximal ideals, so the Chinese remainder theorem gives
Φ([p])=(p(0),p(i),p(−i)).
(16)
The three copies of C have different module structures: X acts by 0,i,−i, respectively. Thus q(X) acts on (a,b,c) as (q(0)a,q(i)b,q(−i)c), making Φ a module homomorphism.
The inverse, obtained by Lagrange interpolation, is
Φ−1(a,b,c)=[a(1+X2)−2b​(X2+iX)−2c​(X2−iX)].​
(17)
Evaluating at 0,i,−i recovers (a,b,c). Conversely, a polynomial whose three evaluations vanish is divisible by X(X−i)(X+i), so these maps are mutual inverses on the quotient module. Three copies with identical trivial X-action would not give the required isomorphism.
