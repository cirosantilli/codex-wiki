Write U∗=UT for the conjugate transpose. Taking determinants in U∗AU=A gives ∣detU∣2detA=detA. Since the Hermitian matrix A is invertible, every such U is invertible and ∣detU∣=1. The identity belongs to UA​; if U,V∈UA​, then (UV)∗A(UV)=V∗AV=A; and multiplying U∗AU=A by (U∗)−1 and U−1 gives (U−1)∗AU−1=A. Associativity comes from matrix multiplication, so this is a group.
The Hermitian form hA​(v,w)=v∗Aw obeys hA​(Uv,Uw)=hA​(v,w) precisely when U∈UA​. Thus UA​ is the group of linear isometries of this nondegenerate Hermitian form; positivity of A is unnecessary.
For A=I, U is a unitary matrix. Here is a direct diagonalization of a matrix proof. Over C, its characteristic polynomial has a root, giving a unit eigenvector v with Uv=λv. Preservation of the inner product gives ∣λ∣=1. If w⊥v, then ⟨Uw,Uv⟩=⟨w,v⟩=0, so v⊥ is invariant. The restriction is again a unitary matrix. Induction on the dimension supplies an orthonormal basis of eigenvectors. In that basis, U is diagonal, with diagonal entries on the unit circle.
