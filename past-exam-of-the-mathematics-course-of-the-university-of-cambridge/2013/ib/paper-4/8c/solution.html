For distinct nodes, define the divided difference recursively by
f[x0​]=f(x0​),f[x0​,…,xk​]=xk​−x0​f[x1​,…,xk​]−f[x0​,…,xk−1​]​.
(15)
Only the function values at the nodes are needed. Induction in this recursion gives
f[x0​,…,xk​]=∑j=0k​∏0≤i≤k, i=j​(xj​−xi​)f(xj​)​.
(16)
For an interior index j, the two terms in the recursive numerator combine using (xj​−x0​)−(xj​−xk​)=xk​−x0​; the endpoint terms give the same displayed denominators. This proves the formula, including its symmetry in the nodes.
The Lagrange interpolation polynomial on the first k+1 nodes is
pk​(x)=∑j=0k​f(xj​)∏i=j​(xj​−xi​)∏i=j​(x−xi​)​.
(17)
Its leading coefficient is therefore f[x0​,…,xk​]. Meanwhile pk​−pk−1​ vanishes at x0​,…,xk−1​ and has degree at most k. The factor theorem and the leading coefficient imply
pk​−pk−1​=f[x0​,…,xk​]∏i=0k−1​(x−xi​).
(18)
Starting from p0​=f(x0​) and summing these identities proves the Newton interpolation polynomial formula
pn​(x)=f(x0​)+k=1∑n​f[x0​,…,xk​]i=0∏k−1​(x−xi​).​
(19)
The same factor theorem proves uniqueness: the difference of two interpolants of degree at most n has n+1 distinct zeros and must vanish.
