Put u=ct−x and v=ct+x. The chain rule turns the wave equation into 4yuv​=0. Integrating first in v and then in u gives the D'Alembert formula
y(x,t)=f(ct−x)+g(ct+x).
(8)
At the left endpoint, g(u)=−f(u) for u>0. At the right endpoint, f(ct−L)+g(ct+L)=0, hence
f(v+2L)=f(v)(v>−L).
(9)
Thus the travelling waves can be represented by g=−f, with f and g both 2L-periodic. More precisely, the positive-time boundary conditions constrain only f on (−L,∞) and g on (0,∞); their unused arguments may be filled in by this periodic extension. They do not constrain arbitrary pre-existing choices outside those ranges. This is periodic reflection for a fixed-end string.
Using g=−f, differentiation gives yt​=c(f′(ct−x)−f′(ct+x)) and yx​=−f′(ct−x)−f′(ct+x). The cross terms in the wave energy cancel, so
E(t)=∫0L​(f′(ct−x)2+f′(ct+x)2)dx=∫ct−Lct+L​f′(s)2ds.
(10)
This integrates a 2L-periodic function over exactly one period. E(t) is constant. Equivalently, the wave equation gives E′(t)=[yt​yx​]0L​=0, because the fixed endpoint values imply yt​=0 there.
