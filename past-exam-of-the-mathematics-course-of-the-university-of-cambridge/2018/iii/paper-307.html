Work in four-dimensional Minkowski spacetime, with Qˉ​α˙A​=(QαA​)† in a unitary representation. The usual Super-Poincaré algebra has the Poincare algebra and possible internal Lorentz scalars as its even generators, and only the stated Weyl spinors as its odd generators. This closure assumption is essential: Lorentz covariance alone would also permit additional tensor-valued generators. It is the setting selected by the Haag–Łopuszański–Sohnius theorem for ordinary interacting relativistic theories.
The Spinor representation of the Lorentz group gives
[QαA​,Mμν]=(σμν)α​βQβA​.
(1)
Here σμν denotes the Lorentz algebra matrices in the convention of the paper. Moving Mμν to the left changes the sign; definitions using Hermitian Lorentz algebra generators may also put an explicit i into these matrices. The conjugate relation acts on the dotted Weyl spinor indices.
The tensor product of group representations
(21​,0)⊗(0,21​)=(21​,21​)
(2)
is a Lorentz four-vector. The only such even generator is Pμ​, so the mixed anticommutator must be
{QαA​,Qˉ​β˙​B​}=HAB​σαβ˙​μ​Pμ​.
(3)
Applying Hermitian conjugation makes H a Hermitian matrix. Positivity of {S,S†} for every linear combination S of supercharges makes H a positive semidefinite matrix on a positive-energy physical superalgebra representation. Remove any null supercharges, which act trivially, and use a unitary diagonalization of a normal matrix followed by rescaling to obtain HAB​=2δAB​. Thus N counts the independent nontrivial supercharges.
For equal chirality, the tensor product of group representations is
(21​,0)⊗(21​,0)=(0,0)⊕(1,0).
(4)
Consequently the tentative equal-chirality anticommutator can contain ϵαβ​ZAB and a symmetric spinor tensor Tαβμν​Mμν​YAB, where T is the intertwining operator for Lorentz covariance onto (1,0). Symmetry of the anticommutator under (A,α)↔(B,β) forces ZAB=−ZBA and YAB=YBA. At this stage ZAB is an internal Lorentz scalar, hence commutes with Pμ​; its full centrality will follow below.
To determine the commutator with translations, Lorentz covariance and the absence of additional odd generators leave only
[Pμ​,QαA​]=CAB(σμ​)αβ˙​​Qˉ​Bβ˙​​
(5)
and the relation obtained by Hermitian conjugation. The graded Jacobi identity for (Pμ​,Pν​,Q), using [Pμ​,Pν​]=0, gives
CC(σμ​σˉν​−σν​σˉμ​)=0,CC=0,
(6)
where C means entrywise complex conjugation. This equation alone would not imply C=0. Contract the graded Jacobi identity
[Pμ​,{QαA​,QβB​}]={[Pμ​,QαA​],QβB​}+{QαA​,[Pμ​,QβB​]}
(7)
with ϵαβ. The M term drops out because its spinor tensor is symmetric, and [Pμ​,ZAB]=0 makes the remaining left side vanish. Substitution of the mixed anticommutator makes the right side a nonzero numerical multiple of (CAB−CBA)Pμ​. Thus C=CT. It follows that C=C† and CC†=0, whence C=0. Applying Hermitian conjugation proves the same assertion for Qˉ​:
[Pμ​,QαA​]=[Pμ​,Qˉ​α˙A​]=0.​
(8)
Returning to the uncontracted graded Jacobi identity now gives [Pμ​,{QαA​,QβB​}]=0. Since the Lorentz algebra generators do not commute with translations, the tentative M term must have YAB=0. Hence
{QαA​,Qˉ​β˙​B​}=2σαβ˙​μ​Pμ​δAB​,{QαA​,QβB​}=ϵαβ​ZAB,ZAB=−ZBA.​
(9)
In particular Z11=0 for N=1. The dotted-dotted anticommutator follows by Hermitian conjugation applied to the second relation.
The graded Jacobi identity for (QA,QB,Qˉ​C​), together with [P,Q]=0, gives [ZAB,Qˉ​C​]=0. As a Lorentz scalar, ZAB commutes with Mμν​, and it already commutes with Pμ​. Closure and Lorentz covariance make [ZAB,QαC​]=DAB,CD​QαD​ for some coefficients. Apply the graded Jacobi identity once more:
0=[ZAB,{QαC​,Qˉ​β˙​E​}]={[ZAB,QαC​],Qˉ​β˙​E​}=2DAB,CE​σαβ˙​μ​Pμ​.
(10)
Thus D=0. Since every Z and Z† is itself an anticommutator of supercharges, the Z's also commute with each other and their conjugates. Therefore ZAB are central charges in supersymmetry of the displayed algebra. Additional R-symmetry automorphisms are not among the generators specified here.
For boson-fermion degeneracy in a supermultiplet, fix a physical four-momentum p with p0​=E>0, and take the finite internal space of states at that four-momentum. Let Γ=(−1)F be fermion parity. It anticommutes with each supercharge and its adjoint. For any fixed A, summing the diagonal spinor indices gives
∑α=12​{QαA​,(QαA​)†}=4E1,
(11)
because σ0=1 and the Pauli matrices are traceless. Cyclicity of the operator trace and ΓQ=−QΓ imply
Tr(ΓQQ†)=−Tr(ΓQ†Q).
(12)
The supertrace of the preceding summed anticommutator therefore vanishes:
0=4ETrΓ=4E(nB​−nF​),nB​=nF​.​
(13)
This counts physical boson and fermion degrees of freedom, including polarizations. The phrase “any representation” needs a qualification: a one-dimensional even supersymmetric vacuum with P=Q=Z=0 has one boson and no fermion. Thus the equality applies to positive-energy physical supermultiplets with finite state counts at fixed four-momentum, not to arbitrary abstract superalgebra representations or unregulated infinite-dimensional operator traces.

Use a positive-energy unitary representation with finite helicity states, excluding continuous-spin representations. In the specified frame the Pauli matrices give
2σμpμ​=2E(1+σ3)=4E(10​00​).
(14)
For every A and every state ∣χ⟩,
0=⟨χ∣{Q2A​,(Q2A​)†}∣χ⟩=∥Q2A​∣χ⟩∥2+∥(Q2A​)†∣χ⟩∥2.
(15)
Positivity therefore gives Q2A​=Qˉ​2˙A​=0 on this massless supermultiplet. The equal-chirality anticommutator {Q1A​,Q2B​}=ϵ12​ZAB then shows that every central charge in supersymmetry vanishes on this superalgebra representation.
Normalize the remaining supercharges as fermionic raising and lowering operators:
aA​=4E​Q1A​​,aA†​=4E​Qˉ​1˙A​​.
(16)
They obey the canonical anticommutation relations
{aA​,aB†​}=δAB​,{aA​,aB​}={aA†​,aB†​}=0.
(17)
Choose a Clifford vacuum ∣λ⟩ of helicity λ∈21​Z annihilated by every aA​. Such a vector exists because the commuting fermion number operators aA†​aA​ have eigenvalues 0 and 1, and lowering every occupied mode produces a nonzero empty state. In an irreducible representation this empty space has dimension one; multiple empty states produce a direct sum of copies. The states are
∣A1​⋯Ak​;λ⟩=aA1​†​⋯aAk​†​∣λ⟩,1≤A1​<⋯<Ak​≤N,0≤k≤N.
(18)
The canonical anticommutation relations make these states orthonormal for a normalized Clifford vacuum. In the helicity convention [J3​,Q1A​]=21​Q1A​, its adjoint has [J3​,aA†​]=−21​aA†​. Thus the Clifford vacuum is the highest-helicity state and
hk​=λ−2k​,d(hk​)=(kN​).​
(19)
Reversing the rotation convention reverses the displayed ordering and leaves all counts unchanged. The level-k states form the exterior power ΛkCN, which explains the binomial coefficient. Summing the binomial coefficients gives the helicity spectrum of a massless supermultiplet:
dimHp​=k=0∑N​(kN​)=2N,hmax​−hmin​=2N​.​
(20)
Each application of a fermionic raising and lowering operator reverses fermion parity. The even and odd levels each contain 2N−1 states, so a physical massless supermultiplet has 2N−1 boson and 2N−1 fermion states.
These formulas describe one irreducible representation of the massless Super-Poincaré algebra at fixed four-momentum. A reducible superalgebra representation can contain several such supermultiplets. Also, the CPT theorem sends helicity h to −h and conjugates internal charges. CPT completion of a supermultiplet may therefore require a second 2N-state supermultiplet, with highest helicity N/2−λ. A necessary condition for the original helicity spectrum of a massless supermultiplet to be self-conjugate is λ=N/4; internal charges must also admit the conjugation in a realization compatible with the Spin-statistics theorem. Thus this helicity condition alone is not sufficient for CPT completion of a supermultiplet without doubling. In particular, the original irreducible count is not automatically the count after CPT completion of a supermultiplet.
For the conventional bounds on extended supersymmetry, suppose all massless states satisfy ∣h∣≤smax​. The helicity spectrum of a massless supermultiplet occupies an interval of width N/2, whereas the available interval has width 2smax​. Hence
N≤4smax​.​
(21)
An ordinary four-dimensional renormalizable quantum field theory without gravity uses interacting massless fields of spin angular momentum at most one. Massless spin angular momentum 3/2 requires supergravity, whose gravitational coupling has negative mass dimension and is a nonrenormalizable interaction by power counting in quantum field theory. Setting smax​=1 gives N≤4 for conventional renormalizable theories. The bound is attained by four-dimensional N=4 super Yang-Mills theory: choosing λ=1 gives
hd(h)​11​21​4​06​−21​4​−11​​
(22)
for its supersymmetric vector multiplet.
Allowing gravity permits smax​=2, so N≤8 for conventional theories including gravity. The four-dimensional N=8 supergravity supergravity multiplet attains the bound with λ=2:
hd(h)​21​23​8​128​21​56​070​−21​56​−128​−23​8​−21​​
(23)
It has 256 states, 128 boson and 128 fermion states. The conventional claim about a maximum “in general” assumes local interacting four-dimensional theories in Minkowski spacetime with a finite spectrum of fields and no massless spin angular momentum above two. It is not a theorem excluding arbitrary free higher-spin angular momentum constructions or all theories in other spacetime settings.
Finally, a chiral gauge spectrum means that the independent left-handed Weyl spinors occur in complex gauge group representations without obligatorily paired conjugate left-handed Weyl spinors. In four-dimensional N=1 supersymmetry, an irreducible matter supermultiplet with helicities (0,−21​) can carry a complex gauge group representation R. Its CPT completion of a supermultiplet supplies the right-handed antiparticle in Rˉ, as every left-handed Weyl spinor requires; it does not supply a second independent left-handed Weyl spinor in Rˉ. Consequently a chiral gauge spectrum is possible.
For N=2, the matter hypermultiplet has helicities (21​,0,0,−21​) before internal charge conjugation. If it carries a complex gauge group representation R, its CPT completion of a supermultiplet adds the conjugate hypermultiplet. Equivalently, the resulting full hypermultiplet contains two N=1 chiral superfields, one in R and one in Rˉ. These give a vectorlike gauge spectrum. Half-hypermultiplets can occur in pseudoreal representations, which also do not produce genuinely complex chiral gauge spectra. A supersymmetric vector multiplet has Adjoint representation, hence real gauge quantum numbers. Since every N≥2 algebra contains an N=2 subalgebra, the same obstruction applies to greater extended supersymmetry.
Therefore a conventional chiral gauge spectrum is possible only for N=0 or N=1. Here N=0 means no supersymmetry; among theories with actual supersymmetry, only N=1 qualifies. These are necessary structural conditions, not a guarantee that a chosen chiral gauge spectrum satisfies anomaly cancellation.

Use the canonical Kähler potential and normalize superspace integration by ∫d2θθ2=1. The superspace Lagrangian density is
L=∫d4θΦ†Φ+[∫d2θ(2m​Φ2+3g​Φ3)+h.c.].​
(24)
The full superspace integration produces a D-term, while the chiral superfield integral produces an F-term. Work in chiral coordinates for the chiral-superfield component expansion, so the omitted spacetime-derivative terms do not enter the extraction of F.
Set δΦ=2​θψ+θ2F. A Taylor expansion of the superpotential gives
W(φ+δΦ)=W(φ)+W′(φ)δΦ+21​W′′(φ)(δΦ)2.
(25)
Higher terms vanish because there are only two Grassmann variables θα. With θψ=θαψα​ and ψψ=ψαψα​, their anticommutation gives (θψ)2=−21​θ2ψψ. Thus
W(Φ)∣θ2​=FW′(φ)−21​W′′(φ)ψψ,W′=mφ+gφ2,W′′=m+2gφ.
(26)
The canonical Kähler potential contributes F∗F. Consequently the part of the Lagrangian density containing the auxiliary field is
LF​=F∗F+F(mφ+gφ2)+F∗(m∗φ∗+g∗φ∗2).​
(27)
The fermion terms coming from the superpotential are
Lmass+Yukawa​=−21​(m+2gφ)ψψ−21​(m∗+2g∗φ∗)ψˉ​ψˉ​.
(28)
The Weyl spinor kinetic term and the complex scalar field kinetic term are canonical because K=Φ†Φ.
Treat F and F∗ as independent variables in their algebraic Euler-Lagrange equations. They give
F∗=−W′(φ),F=−W′(φ)​.
(29)
Equivalently,
LF​=​F+W′(φ)​​2−∣W′(φ)∣2.
(30)
After eliminating the auxiliary field, the Lagrangian density contains −V, so the F-term scalar potential is
V(φ)=∣mφ+gφ2∣2=∣m∣2∣φ∣2+m∗gφ∗φ2+mg∗φφ∗2+∣g∣2∣φ∣4≥0.​

If m=0 and g=0 these coincide; if g=0 and m=0 only the origin remains; if m=g=0 every constant φ is a supersymmetric vacuum.
To compare masses, expand around a supersymmetric vacuum v satisfying W′(v)=0, writing φ=v+η. With M=W′′(v)=m+2gv,

mA2​=mB2​=∣W′′(v)∣2,mψ​=∣W′′(v)∣.​
(34)
In particular, at v=0 the boson and fermion masses are both ∣m∣. At the second supersymmetric vacuum v=−m/g the sign of M reverses, but the physical mass is again ∣m∣. Equality here concerns fluctuations about a supersymmetric vacuum, not arbitrary backgrounds with W′(v)=0.
In the convention V⊃λ∣φ∣4 and LYukawa​=−yφψψ+h.c., the same superpotential coefficient gives λ=∣g∣2 and y=g. The supersymmetric relation between quartic and Yukawa couplings is therefore

The perturbative non-renormalization theorem applies to the local holomorphic superpotential in the Wilsonian effective action, using regularization in quantum field theory that preserves supersymmetry. There are two complementary ways to see why it applies here.
First, in supergraph calculations in perturbative quantum field theory, the algebra of supersymmetric covariant derivatives puts any candidate local correction to the superpotential into a full d4θ superspace integration. It therefore corrects a D-term, such as the Kähler potential, rather than a local chiral d2θ F-term. Turning such a contribution into an apparent chiral integral would require nonlocal inverse spacetime derivatives, schematically 1/□. These are excluded from the local Wilsonian effective action with a fixed nonzero momentum cutoff. This gives the diagrammatic non-renormalization theorem.
Second, the holomorphy argument for superpotential non-renormalization treats m and g as background chiral spurions, so the Wilsonian effective action superpotential is a holomorphic function of Φ,m,g, with no dependence on m∗ or g∗. Assign an ordinary U(1) spurion charge and an R-charge as follows:
U(1)R​Φ11​m−20​g−3−1​θ01​​
(36)
The superpotential has ordinary charge zero and R-charge two, since d2θ has R-charge minus two. A prospective holomorphic monomial magbΦc must therefore satisfy
c−2a−3b=0,c−b=2,hencea=3−c,b=c−2.
(37)
For perturbative corrections, regularity at m=0 and g=0 requires nonnegative powers of the couplings. Regularity in m is justified by keeping a nonzero Wilsonian momentum cutoff and retaining Φ as a light field. Hence only c=2 and c=3 survive: the existing mΦ2 and gΦ3 structures. The quadratic coefficient is fixed by the free theory at g=0, where there are no interaction loops. The cubic coefficient is fixed at first order in g, where a connected three-field interaction has only its tree-level Feynman diagram; loop corrections would require further powers of the interaction couplings, which the holomorphy argument for superpotential non-renormalization charge constraints forbid. Thus
WWilsonian​(Φ)=2m​Φ2+3g​Φ3to all perturbative orders in holomorphic variables.​
(38)
A field-independent constant is physically irrelevant in this rigid model; the spurion charges would in any case require m3/g2, which is not regular at g=0.
The Kähler potential can still receive wave-function renormalization. For example, K=ZΦ†Φ+⋯ gives the canonical field normalization Φc​=Z1/2Φ, and consequently
mc​=Zm​,gc​=Z3/2g​.
(39)
These canonically normalized parameters can run with the renormalization scale; this does not contradict the holomorphic non-renormalization theorem. When massless modes are included in the full quantum effective action, nonlocal terms associated with infrared divergences can also imitate an F-term. Distinguishing that action from the local Wilsonian effective action is part of the theorem's convention.
