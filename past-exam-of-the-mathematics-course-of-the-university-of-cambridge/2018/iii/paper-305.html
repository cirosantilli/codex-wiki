Use the Minkowski metric gμν=diag(1,−1,−1,−1) throughout. Taking the Hermitian conjugation of the massive Dirac equation and using the Dirac adjoint gives
i(∂μ​ψˉ​)γμ+mψˉ​=0.
(1)
Taking the matrix transpose and multiplying by the charge conjugation matrix C yields
iCγμTC−1∂μ​(Cψˉ​T)+mCψˉ​T=0,−iγμ∂μ​ψc+mψc=0.
(2)
Thus the charge conjugation of a Dirac field preserves the free massive Dirac equation:
(iγμ∂μ​−m)ψc=0.​
(3)
The unitarity of C and γ0C−1γ0=−C−1γ0T also give
ψc​=(Cψˉ​T)†γ0=ψTγ0TC−1γ0=−ψTC−1.
(4)
Consequently
C^ψˉ​(x)C^−1=−ψT(x)C−1.​
(5)
Here C^ is the unitary operator acting on the relativistic quantum field, whereas C acts on Dirac spinor indices.

The bosonic annihilation operator aλ(p) removes a vector particle of four-momentum p and polarization vector εμ,λ(p). The bosonic creation operator cλ†(p) creates its antiparticle with the same four-momentum and polarization label. The polarization vector describes the corresponding spin-one mode expansion of a free field.
Because charge conjugation acts through a unitary operator, it leaves the numerical polarization vector coefficients unchanged. Applying the specified charge conjugation to the operators gives
C^Vμ(x)C^−1=ηC​∑p,λ​[εμ,λ(p)cλ(p)e−ip⋅x+εμ,λ∗(p)aλ†(p)eip⋅x].
(6)
Comparison with the Hermitian conjugation of the original expansion therefore gives
C^Vμ(x)C^−1=ηC​Vμ†(x).​
(7)
The factor ηC​ is the intrinsic charge-conjugation phase.

Write JVμ​=ψˉ​γμψ for the Dirac current and JAμ​=ψˉ​γμγ5ψ for the axial current. Under parity symmetry in quantum field theory, the Dirac spinor phases cancel and
P^JVμ​(x)P^−1=Pμν​JVν​(xP​),P^JAμ​(x)P^−1=−Pμν​JAν​(xP​).
(13)
The extra sign for the axial current follows from γ0γ5γ0=−γ5. Combining these transformations with part (c), both currents transform under CP symmetry as JV,Aμ​(x)↦−Pμν​JV,Aν​(xP​). The vector relativistic quantum field transforms as Vμ(x)↦ηC​Pμν​Vν†(xP​). The two parity matrices cancel in the tensor contraction, giving
(CP)[ψˉ​γμ(gv​−ga​γ5)ψVμ​](x)(CP)−1=−ηC​[ψˉ​γμ(gv​−ga​γ5)ψ](xP​)Vμ†​(xP​).​
(14)
For a real vector relativistic quantum field with ηC​=−1, the interaction is invariant under CP symmetry. A complex vector relativistic quantum field instead maps to the conjugate-field interaction, which must be compared with its Hermitian conjugation partner.

The flavour-diagonal weak neutral current coupled to the real Z boson has real vector and axial current couplings. With ηC​(Z)=−1, part (e) shows that this tree-level interaction is invariant under CP symmetry, although the simultaneous presence of the Dirac current and axial current generally violates charge conjugation and parity symmetry in quantum field theory separately. This conclusion concerns the tree-level weak neutral current, rather than every interaction or radiative effect in the Standard Model.
The weak charged current couples distinct quark flavours and involves the Cabibbo-Kobayashi-Maskawa matrix:
Lcc​=−2​g​uˉLi​γμVij​dLj​Wμ+​+h.c.
(15)
Here uLi​ and dLj​ are left-handed quark fields. A CP symmetry transformation exchanges the two conjugate-field operators; because it acts through a unitary operator, it does not complex-conjugate their numerical coefficients. Equality with the original interaction therefore requires a Cabibbo-Kobayashi-Maskawa matrix that can be made real by quantum field rephasings. With three generations, a physical CP-violating phase remains in general, measured by the Jarlskog invariant. Therefore the W boson interactions can violate CP symmetry. Merely seeing a complex coefficient is insufficient: the phase must survive quantum field rephasing, unlike the real two-generation Cabibbo angle rotation.

a OurBigBook.com
The scalar potential can be written as
V=4λ​(ϕa​ϕa​−v2)2−4λv4​,v2=−λm2​.
(16)
Its vacuum manifold is the sphere ϕa​ϕa​=v2. The Adjoint double cover from SU(2) to SO(3) rotates any nonzero vacuum expectation value to (0,0,v), with v>0. A local unitary gauge removes the two angular fluctuations, leaving ϕ=(0,0,v+η). This choice holds in a neighbourhood of the nonzero vacuum; a single global rotation cannot align arbitrary position-dependent fields, and topologically nontrivial configurations can obstruct a global unitary gauge.
The generator t3 fixes the vacuum expectation value, while t1,t2 do not. Thus the Higgs mechanism in this SU(2) gauge theory with an adjoint Higgs field gives
SU(2)⟶U(1).​
(17)
The two angular Goldstone bosons become the longitudinal polarizations of two massive gauge bosons. Define physical fields
Aμ​=Bμ3​,Wμ±​=2​Bμ1​∓iBμ2​​.
(18)
These labels describe the fields of this model; they do not identify it with the Standard Model. With the stated adjoint covariant derivative,
(Dμ​ϕ)1​=−g(v+η)Bμ2​,(Dμ​ϕ)2​=g(v+η)Bμ1​,(Dμ​ϕ)3​=∂μ​η.
(19)
To express all Yang-Mills theory terms in physical fields, introduce the gauge field strengths
fμν​=∂μ​Aν​−∂ν​Aμ​,Qμν​=Wμ+​Wν−​−Wν+​Wμ−​,Wμν±​=(∂μ​±igAμ​)Wν±​−(∂ν​±igAν​)Wμ±​.
(20)
Then Fμν3​=fμν​+igQμν​ and (Fμν1​∓iFμν2​)/2​=Wμν±​. Dropping the constant vacuum energy, the Lagrangian density is
L=​−41​(fμν​+igQμν​)(fμν+igQμν)−21​Wμν+​W−μν+21​∂μ​η∂μη−λv2η2−λvη3−4λ​η4+g2(v+η)2Wμ+​W−μ.​
(21)
Taking the positive gauge coupling convention g>0, the canonically normalized mass terms give
mA​=0,mW+​=mW−​=gv,mη2​=2λv2=−2m2.​
(22)
The residual U(1) gauge symmetry makes W± oppositely charged. The Yang-Mills theory terms supply interactions of A with W± and four-vector interactions. The Higgs mode has cubic and quartic self-interactions, together with 2g2vηW+⋅W− and g2η2W+⋅W− couplings; it is neutral under the surviving U(1) gauge symmetry.
Coupling fermions permits a massless electromagnetic gauge boson and massive charged mediators of the weak interaction; fermion couplings with chirality can produce parity-violating weak charged currents. However, this model has no massive neutral Z boson, and its only charge generator is t3. A fundamental doublet has opposite charges, so it cannot reproduce the observed doublet charge assignments through an independent hypercharge. The Standard Model instead has SU(2)L​×U(1)Y​→U(1)Q​, a complex Higgs doublet, three absorbed Goldstone bosons, a massive Z boson and a weak mixing angle.

Let ∣1⟩=∣D0⟩, ∣2⟩=∣Dˉ0⟩. In the rest frame, the CPT theorem interchanges these states up to irrelevant phases. For the weak Hamiltonian operator H′, with H′=H′†, and its antiunitary CPT symmetry operator Θ,
⟨Θ1∣H′∣Θ1⟩=⟨1∣H′∣1⟩∗.
(27)
Its diagonal matrix elements are real by Hermitian conjugation, so
R11​=R22​.​
(28)
In the specified phase convention, the CP symmetry operator on this two-state subspace is −σx​. If the weak interaction preserves CP symmetry, σx​Rσx​=R, giving
R12​=R21​.​
(29)
Independently, Hermitian conjugation gives R21​=R12∗​ for this H′. Hence CP symmetry makes the off-diagonal element real in this convention; that conjugation relation alone does not imply CP symmetry.
For neutral meson mixing described by the decay-effective Hamiltonian operator R=M−iΓ/2, the effective matrix is generally not a Hermitian matrix. The CPT constraints on neutral-meson mixing still give equality of the complex diagonal entries, expressing equal masses and total decay widths, and CP symmetry gives equality of the off-diagonal entries in the specified convention. One must not additionally impose R21​=R12∗​ on this decay-effective matrix.

The tree-level Feynman diagrams contain an unchanged spectator quark, of up quark flavour and the two possible weak charged current transitions of the charm antiquark:
For Dˉ0→K+π−, cˉ→sˉW− and W−→duˉ. The spectator quark combines with sˉ into the kaon K+=usˉ, while duˉ forms the pion π−. The Cabibbo-Kobayashi-Maskawa matrix factor is Vcs​Vud∗​.
For Dˉ0→K−π+, cˉ→dˉW− and W−→suˉ. The spectator quark combines with dˉ into the pion π+=udˉ, while suˉ forms the kaon K−. The Cabibbo-Kobayashi-Maskawa matrix factor is Vcd​Vus∗​.

Γ(Dˉ0→K+π−)Γ(Dˉ0→K−π+)​≈​Vcs​Vud∗​Vcd​Vus∗​​​2≈tan4θC​.​
(30)
Here θC​ is the Cabibbo angle. The second process is doubly Cabibbo suppressed: it contains two small Cabibbo suppression factors in its amplitude. The Cabibbo angle estimate assumes similar Quantum chromodynamics matrix elements; it is not an exact rate equality.

Both the D meson and kaon are spin-zero pseudoscalars. The strong interaction states obey parity symmetry in quantum field theory. Consequently an axial current matrix element between them would have to be a pseudovector formed from only p and k, which is impossible: an totally antisymmetric tensor would require more linearly independent vectors. Thus
⟨K+(k)∣cˉγμγ5s∣Dˉ0(p)⟩=0.
(31)
Lorentz covariance then leaves two linearly independent vectors for the vector current matrix element, giving the pseudoscalar-to-pseudoscalar form factors
⟨K+(k)∣cˉγμs∣Dˉ0(p)⟩=(p+k)μf+​(q2)+(p−k)μf−​(q2),q=p−k.
(32)
The vanishing axial current here follows from strong interaction parity symmetry in quantum field theory, not parity symmetry in quantum field theory of the weak interaction.
To obtain the requested decay formula, use naive factorization of a nonleptonic meson decay: approximate the four-quark matrix element by the product of the Dˉ0→K+ current matrix element and the vacuum-to-pion matrix element. This is an additional hadronic approximation; tree-level weak vertices alone do not establish it, and nonfactorizable Quantum chromodynamics effects can change the result. The vacuum-to-pion vector current matrix element vanishes by parity symmetry in quantum field theory, while the specified pion decay constant normalization gives
⟨π−(q)∣dˉγμ​(1−γ5)u∣0⟩=+i2​Fπ​qμ​.
(33)
The 2​ cancels the 1/2​ in the four-fermion interaction. Up to an irrelevant overall phase, the scattering amplitude is therefore
M=GF​Vcs​Vud∗​Fπ​[(mD2​−mK2​)f+​(q2)+q2f−​(q2)].
(34)
For mπ​=0, q2=0, so only f+​(0) survives. Integrating the two-body decay phase space in the D meson rest frame gives
∣k∣=2mD​mD2​−mK2​​,Γ=8πmD2​∣k∣​∣M∣2.
(35)
Hence
A=16πmD3​(mD2​−mK2​)3​,Γ=16πmD3​GF2​(mD2​−mK2​)3​∣Vud∗​Vcs​Fπ​f+​(0)∣2.​
(36)
This coefficient uses exactly the stated pion decay constant convention and the stated massless-pion approximation.

For an unpolarized electromagnetic target, Lorentz covariance and parity symmetry in quantum field theory permit a symmetric hadronic tensor built from gμν, Pμ and qμ, where P=PH​. Its surviving general form is
Wμν=agμν+bPμPν+cqμqν+d(Pμqν+qμPν).
(40)
The totally antisymmetric tensor structure ϵμνρσPρ​qσ​ is excluded by electromagnetic parity symmetry in quantum field theory for this unpolarized target. The other possible antisymmetric structure, Pμqν−qμPν, cannot be transverse for generic scattering kinematics and hence is excluded by current conservation. Current conservation gives the Ward identities qμ​Wμν=qν​Wμν=0, imposing
bν+dq2=0,a+cq2+dν=0,ν=P⋅q.
(41)
Two scalar functions remain. Taking Pμ=Pμ−νqμ/q2 gives the transverse basis
WHμν​=(−gμν+q2qμqν​)F1​(x,q2)+PμPννF2​(x,q2)​,x=−2νq2​.​
(42)
These are the deep-inelastic structure functions; x is Bjorken x. At fixed target mass, the two independent Lorentz scalar invariants can equivalently be chosen as x,q2. In this paper ν=P⋅q has dimensions of mass squared, rather than the alternative convention ν=P⋅q/M used for energy transfer.

Use the massless collinear parton approximation in a high-energy frame: P2=0, k=ξP, and k′=ξP+q with k′2=0. This neglects target-mass corrections to the parton model; it does not literally set a stationary massive target to a massless particle in the earlier flux formula.

21​∑spins​JμJν∗=2Qf2​(kμk′ν+kνk′μ−gμνk⋅k′).
