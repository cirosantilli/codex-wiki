Use the simple-root form of the Hensel lemma: for F∈OK​[T] and aˉ∈kK​ satisfying F(aˉ)=0 and F′(aˉ)=0, there is a unique a∈OK​ with aˉ as its reduction and F(a)=0.
Let d=[ℓ:kK​]. The residue field kK​ is finite, so ℓ/kK​ is separable, and the primitive element theorem gives ℓ=kK​(αˉ). Let Fˉ be the monic irreducible polynomial of αˉ, of degree d, and choose a monic coefficient lift F∈OK​[T]. Irreducible reduction implies that F is irreducible over K: any monic factorization over K has integral coefficients, since all its roots are integral over the valuation ring, and would reduce to a factorization of Fˉ.
Adjoin a root α and put L=K(α). Then [L:K]=d and α∈OL​. Its reduction satisfies Fˉ, so kL​ contains a copy of ℓ over kK​. For finite extensions of non-Archimedean local fields, the fundamental degree relation is
[L:K]=e(L/K)[kL​:kK​].
(1)
The residue degree is at least d, while the left side equals d, hence
e(L/K)=1,kL​≅ℓ,[L:K]=[ℓ:kK​].​
(2)
This constructs the required unramified extension.
For uniqueness, let L′/K be another unramified extension with a chosen identification kL′​≅ℓ. The element corresponding to αˉ is a simple residue root of F, because finite fields are perfect. The Hensel lemma in L′ gives a root α′∈OL′​ with this reduction. Since F is irreducible of degree d, [K(α′):K]=d=[L′:K], so L′=K(α′). Sending α to α′ yields a K-isomorphism L→L′, compatible with the prescribed residue identification. Thus
the unramified lift of ℓ/kK​ is unique up to K-isomorphism.​
(3)
This proves the existence and uniqueness of unramified local extensions using the stated version of Hensel's lemma, without assuming the classification in advance.
