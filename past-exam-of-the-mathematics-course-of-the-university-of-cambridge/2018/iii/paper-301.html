At this order there is one annihilation tree-level Feynman diagram. The squared-amplitude diagram is its product with its complex conjugate, with the same external momenta and spin labels in both factors:
 e-(p,s) --->\          /---> mu-(p',s')
             o ~~~~~~ o
 e+(q,r) <---/          \<--- mu+(q',r')

                     times

 e-(p,s) --->\          /---> mu-(p',s')
             o ~~~~~~ o                       *
 e+(q,r) <---/          \<--- mu+(q',r')
A wavy line is the virtual photon; the arrows show fermion-number flow. The star means complex conjugation of the entire second graph, including its external spinors. This represents ∣M∣2=MM∗ for fixed spins, without adding a new physical loop to the amplitude.
For the spin sum in part (iv), identify each external spin label between the two copies and sum it. The fermion spin sum sews each spinor chain into a gamma-matrix trace. The two incoming unpolarized spin states are then handled by a spin average, giving the factor 1/4. In particular, the diagrammatic contraction becomes
∣M∣2​=4s2e4​tr(qγμpγν)tr(p′γμ​q′γν​).​

First derive the needed gamma-matrix traces directly from the Clifford algebra. The cyclic property of the trace gives
2tr(γμγν)=tr{γμ,γν}=2ημνtrI4​,
(6)
so tr(γμγν)=4ημν. For T=tr(γμγνγργσ), move γμ successively through the other three matrices and cycle the final term back to the front:
2T=2ημνtr(γργσ)−2ημρtr(γνγσ)+2ημσtr(γνγρ).
(7)
Therefore
tr(γμγνγργσ)=4(ημνηρσ−ημρηνσ+ημσηνρ).
(8)
These are all the trace identities required.
For massless external Dirac spinors, the fermion spin sums are ∑s​us​(p)uˉs​(p)=p and ∑r​vr​(q)vˉr​(q)=q. With the initial spin average, part (iii) becomes a contraction of
Lμν=4(qμpν+qνpμ−ημνp⋅q),Hμν​=4(pμ′​qν′​+pν′​qμ′​−ημν​p′⋅q′).
(9)
Their contraction is
LμνHμν​=32[(q⋅p′)(p⋅q′)+(q⋅q′)(p⋅p′)].
(10)
The massless Mandelstam variables give p⋅p′=q⋅q′=−t/2 and p⋅q′=q⋅p′=−u/2, hence
∣M∣2​=s22e4​(t2+u2).
(11)
In the center of mass frame each particle has energy s​/2, and
t=−2s​(1−cosθ),u=−2s​(1+cosθ).
(12)
Substitution gives the concise angular answer:
∣M∣2​=e4(1+cos2θ).​
(13)

For the two free real scalar fields, let L=L1​+L2​. The infinitesimal internal rotation symmetry of two real scalar fields has δϕ1​=θϕ2​ and δϕ2​=−θϕ1​, with constant θ. Its kinetic terms are invariant because this is an orthogonal rotation of the two-component field.
Under the assumed invariance of the full Lagrangian density, Noether's theorem gives the current per unit transformation parameter:
jμ=∑a=12​∂(∂μ​ϕa​)∂L​θδϕa​​=ϕ2​∂μϕ1​−ϕ1​∂μϕ2​.
(27)
The Noether current and Noether charge are therefore
jμ=ϕ2​∂μϕ1​−ϕ1​∂μϕ2​,Q=∫d3x(ϕ2​π1​−ϕ1​π2​).​
(28)
Independent species commute at equal time, so these products require no extra ordering sign. With vanishing boundary flux, Q is conserved. Part (vii) checks precisely what the symmetry assumption requires of the masses.

Differentiate the Noether charge and use ϕ˙​a​=πa​ and the Klein-Gordon equation for each species. The momentum products cancel because the two species commute:
Q˙​​=∫d3x[π2​π1​+ϕ2​(∇2−m12​)ϕ1​−π1​π2​−ϕ1​(∇2−m22​)ϕ2​]=∫d3x[ϕ2​∇2ϕ1​−ϕ1​∇2ϕ2​]+(m22​−m12​)∫d3xϕ1​ϕ2​.​
(29)
The first integral is the boundary flux of ϕ2​∇ϕ1​−ϕ1​∇ϕ2​, so it vanishes under the same boundary condition used for charge conservation. Hence
dtdQ​=(m22​−m12​)∫d3xϕ1​ϕ2​.​
(30)
Conservation must hold for arbitrary configurations, rather than only a state in which this particular integral happens to vanish. Thus the internal rotation symmetry of two real scalar fields requires
m12​=m22​,and therefore m1​=m2​ for nonnegative masses.​
(31)
Equivalently, the local divergence is ∂μ​jμ=(m22​−m12​)ϕ1​ϕ2​, exhibiting exactly how unequal masses break the symmetry.

In phi cubed theory, each vertex has three legs. A connected four-point tree-level Feynman diagram therefore needs two cubic vertices and one internal propagator. There are exactly three ways to partition the labeled external legs between these vertices:
       s channel               t channel              u channel

 p \          / p'         p ---o--- p'            p ---o--- q'
    o--------o                  |                       |
 q /          \ q'         q ---o--- q'            q ---o--- p'

 internal: p+q             internal: p-p'          internal: p-q'
Solid lines denote the real scalar field propagator. The labels specify which external pair meets at each vertex, so the last two graphs are different even though their unlabeled shapes agree. This is the four-point tree amplitude in phi cubed theory:
three exchange diagrams, in the s,t,u channels; each is of order λ2.​
(32)
There is no four-point contact vertex in the given cubic interaction.

For the interaction sign in this paper, the cubic Feynman rule is +iλ, while the scalar Feynman propagator is i/(k2−m2+iϵ). Each exchange graph therefore contributes −iλ2/(k2−m2+iϵ) to iM. The three graphs give
M=−λ2(s−m2+iϵ1​+t−m2+iϵ1​+u−m2+iϵ1​).
(33)
The equal-mass Mandelstam variables satisfy s+t+u=4m2, so u−m2=3m2−s−t. In the physical elastic region with m>0, none of these denominators vanishes, and the tree amplitude is real up to its irrelevant overall convention. Thus
∣M∣2=λ4[s−m21​+t−m21​+3m2−s−t1​]2.​
(34)
The square includes interference between all three channels; summing their individual squared contributions would omit physical cross terms. If a denominator approaches a pole, retain the Feynman i-epsilon prescription in the modulus before taking a limit.

In four spacetime dimensions with ℏ=c=1, a relativistic scattering cross-section has mass dimension −2, since it is an area. The action is dimensionless, so the Lagrangian density has dimension four. The scalar kinetic term then gives [ϕ]=1, and the cubic term gives [λ]=1.
Each term of the four-point tree amplitude in phi cubed theory has dimension [λ2]−[s−m2]=2−2=0. Hence [∣M∣2]=0. In the final integral,
[dt]=2,[s]=[s−4m2]=2,

[∫dts(s−4m2)∣M∣2​]=2+0−4=−2=[σ].​

Under a gauge transformation, the electromagnetic field tensor changes by
δFμν​=∂μ​∂ν​ξ−∂ν​∂μ​ξ=0.
(42)
The partial derivatives commute for a smooth gauge parameter, or in the distributional formulation. Thus the Maxwell Lagrangian depends only on a gauge-invariant tensor:
Fμν′​=Fμν​,L′=−41​Fμν′​F′μν=L.​
(43)
Strictly, the pointwise calculation requires the second derivatives to exist; smooth gauge parameters are the usual convention. Mere first differentiability in the wording can instead be understood through commuting distributional derivatives.

A Lorentz transformation preserves the quadratic form of the Minkowski metric for every four-vector. Substituting x′σ=Λσμ​xμ gives
ηστ​Λσμ​Λτν​xμxν=ημν​xμxν.
(44)
The coefficient difference is a symmetric matrix, and a symmetric quadratic form that vanishes on every vector is zero: evaluating it on basis vectors and their sums recovers each coefficient. Consequently
ΛTηΛ=η,ημν​=ηστ​Λσμ​Λτν​.​
(45)
This is the defining metric-preservation relation of the Lorentz group.

For the active Lorentz transformation of a vector field, coordinates are held fixed and A′μ(x)=Λμν​Aν(Λ−1x). Its field strength transforms as a two-index Lorentz tensor. The index contractions in the Maxwell Lagrangian are invariant by ΛTηΛ=η, so the density transforms as a Lorentz scalar:

(48)

δL(x)=−αωμν​xν∂μ​L(x).​

δL=−∂μ​(αωμν​xνL).​
(50)

For a translation with the sign used in the paper, the field variation at fixed coordinates is δAρ​=βϵν∂ν​Aρ​, and δL=∂μ​(βϵμL). Differentiating the Maxwell Lagrangian with respect to the field gradient gives
∂(∂μ​Aρ​)∂L​=−Fμρ.
(51)
Thus Noether's theorem yields jμ=ϵν​Tμν, where the canonical stress-energy tensor of the Maxwell field is
Tμν=−Fμρ∂νAρ​−ημνL=−Fμρ∂νAρ​+41​ημνFρσ​Fρσ.​
(52)
It is conserved on the source-free Maxwell equations, but it is not generally symmetric. Indeed,
Tμν−Tνμ=−Fμρ∂νAρ​+Fνρ∂μAρ​
(53)
need not vanish. For example, take A0​=ax1, A2​=bx0, and the other components zero, with nonzero constants a,b. This produces constant field strength and satisfies the source-free equations, but T12=0 and T21=ab.
Nor is it gauge-invariant: while F and L are unchanged, a gauge transformation gives
δξ​Tμν=−Fμρ∂ν∂ρ​ξ,​
(54)
which is generally nonzero. These shortcomings motivate the improvement in part (vi); they do not contradict conservation of the canonical stress-energy tensor.

Antisymmetry gives −Fρμ∂ρ​Aν=Fμρ∂ρ​Aν. Combining this with the canonical stress-energy tensor removes the potential in favour of the electromagnetic field tensor:
Θμν​=−Fμρ(∂νAρ​−∂ρ​Aν)+41​ημνFρσ​Fρσ=−FμρFνρ​+41​ημνFρσ​Fρσ.​​
(55)
This is the electromagnetic stress-energy tensor in the (+,−,−,−) convention. The product FμρFνρ​ is symmetric in μ,ν, since the contracted metric is symmetric. Its expression only in F makes it gauge-invariant. These two properties hold without using the equations of motion.
For conservation, the source-free Maxwell equations are ∂ρ​Fρμ=0. Hence the given correction equals
−Fρμ∂ρ​Aν=−∂ρ​(FρμAν)
(56)
on shell. This is an antisymmetric superpotential improvement: Bρμν=−FρμAν is antisymmetric in ρ,μ, so ∂μ​∂ρ​Bρμν=0. Conservation of the canonical stress-energy tensor therefore implies ∂μ​Θμν=0. Equivalently, direct differentiation gives ∂μ​Θμν=−Fνρ​∂μ​Fμρ after the homogeneous Maxwell equations cancel the field-strength derivative terms.
There are four conserved currents, one for each fixed ν, with charges Pν=∫d3xΘ0ν. The Belinfante-Rosenfeld stress-energy tensor differs from the canonical tensor only by boundary contributions to these charges when the fields decay suitably.
Finally, in four spacetime dimensions,
Θμμ​=−FμρFμρ​+44​Fρσ​Fρσ=0.
(57)
Thus all requested properties are
∂μ​Θμν=0 on shell,Θμν=Θνμ,δξ​Θμν=0,Θμμ​=0.​
