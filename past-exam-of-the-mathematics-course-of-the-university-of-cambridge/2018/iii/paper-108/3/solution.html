Let ξ0n−1​=⋁j=0n−1​T−jξ denote the join of measurable partitions recording the first n observations, and set Hn​=Hμ​(ξ0n−1​), with H0​=0. The entropy of a countable measurable partition is Hμ​(ξ)=−∑A∈ξ​μ(A)logμ(A), using natural logarithms and 0log0=0. Null atoms can be discarded. Throughout, μ is a probability measure.
The stronger property needed for monotonicity of normalized block entropy is that its increments decrease. Define
ck​=Hμ​(ξ​⋁j=1k​T−jξ),c0​=Hμ​(ξ).
(24)
Since conditioning reduces entropy, ck+1​≤ck​. The conditional entropy chain rule and measure preservation give
Hk+1​=Hμ​(⋁j=1k​T−jξ)+ck​=Hk​+ck​,Hn​=∑k=0n−1​ck​.
(25)
It follows that cn​≤Hn​/n, and consequently
n+1Hn+1​​=n+1Hn​+cn​​≤nHn​​.​
(26)
This uses stationarity as well as the entropy chain rule; subadditivity alone would not establish monotonicity of every successive ratio.

The Shannon-McMillan-Breiman theorem states that for a countable measurable partition with Hμ​(ξ)<∞, the normalized information
n1​Iμ​(ξ0n−1​)(x)=−n1​logμ(ξ0n−1​(x))
(28)
converges almost everywhere and in L1 to a T invariant function hξ​(x) with integral hμ​(T,ξ). Here ξ0n−1​(x) is the atom containing x. If T is an ergodic transformation, then hξ​(x)=hμ​(T,ξ) almost everywhere. We prove the general form, including an explicit formula for its limit.
Let Fk​ be the sigma-algebra generated by ⋁j=1k​T−jξ, let F0​ be trivial, and put F∞​=⋁j=1∞​T−jσ(ξ). For every atom A, the probabilities
pkA​=Eμ​[1A​∣Fk​]
(29)
form a bounded conditional-expectation martingale. The martingale convergence theorem gives pkA​→p∞A​=Eμ​[1A​∣F∞​] almost everywhere and in L1. Each of these probabilities is positive almost everywhere on A: for example, integrating 1A​ over the F∞​ measurable set {p∞A​=0} gives zero. Countability of ξ lets us choose a common full-measure set for every atom.
The conditional information functions
fk​=Iμ​(ξ∣Fk​)=−∑A∈ξ​1A​logpkA​
(30)
therefore converge almost everywhere to f∞​=Iμ​(ξ∣F∞​). We need an integrable bound on this sequence; convergence of the probabilities alone would not supply one after taking logarithms. The allowed maximal inequality for conditional information functions gives, for F=supk≥0​fk​,
μ(A∩{F>t})≤e−t(A∈ξ, t≥0).
(31)
Combining this with the bound by μ(A) and the tail integral formula for moments yields
∫Fdμ≤∑A∈ξ​∫0∞​min{μ(A),e−t}dt=∑A∈ξ​μ(A)(1−logμ(A))=1+Hμ​(ξ)<∞.
(32)
Thus 0≤f∞​≤F, and the dominated convergence theorem gives fk​→f∞​ in L1. In particular,
∫f∞​dμ=limk​ck​=hμ​(T,ξ),
(33)
because Hn​/n is the average of the decreasing sequence (ck​).
The information chain rule, applied from the final observation backwards, gives the exact identity
Iμ​(ξ0n−1​)(x)=∑j=0n−1​fn−1−j​(Tjx).
(34)
Indeed, the symbol at time j is conditioned on times j+1,…,n−1; pulling that conditional probability back by Tj gives fn−1−j​∘Tj. Measure preservation is sufficient for this pullback identity.
We now prove the required triangular ergodic averaging lemma in this instance. Put gM​=supk≥M​∣fk​−f∞​∣. Then gM​↓0 almost everywhere, gM​≤2F, and ∥gM​∥1​→0. Split the difference between the displayed triangular sum and ∑j=0n−1​f∞​(Tjx). Terms whose index is at least M are bounded by ∑j=0n−1​gM​(Tjx). The remaining terms are at most
∑k=0M−1​∣fk​−f∞​∣(Tn−1−kx).
(35)
After division by n, each of these finitely many end terms tends to zero almost everywhere by the linear growth bound for integrable observables proved in Question 1. Applying the Birkhoff ergodic theorem to gM​, for every fixed M, therefore gives
limsupn→∞​​n1​∑j=0n−1​(fn−1−j​−f∞​)(Tjx)​≤Eμ​[gM​∣I](x).
(36)
These conditional expectations decrease to zero almost everywhere: they decrease, and their integrals ∫gM​dμ tend to zero. Letting M→∞, then applying the Birkhoff ergodic theorem to f∞​, proves
−n1​logμ(ξ0n−1​(x))⟶hξ​(x)=Eμ​[f∞​∣I](x)almost everywhere.​
(37)
For L1 convergence, measure preservation gives the direct estimate
​n1​∑j=0n−1​(fn−1−j​−f∞​)∘Tj​1​≤n1​∑k=0n−1​∥fk​−f∞​∥1​⟶0.
(38)

−n1​logμ(ξ0n−1​(x))⟶hμ​(T,ξ)almost everywhere and in L1.​
