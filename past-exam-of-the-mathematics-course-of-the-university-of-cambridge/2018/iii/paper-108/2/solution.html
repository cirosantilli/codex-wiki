For an increasing sequence (nk​) of nonnegative integers, full-density sequence means that its range S has natural density one:
N∣S∩{0,…,N−1}∣​⟶1.
(13)
For a sequence (zn​) in a metric space, convergence in density of a sequence to z means that, for every ε>0,
N1​​{0≤n<N:d(zn​,z)≥ε}​⟶0.
(14)
In particular, a bounded nonnegative scalar sequence converging to zero in density has averages tending to zero: the average is at most ε plus its bound times the proportion of indices above ε.
Here is a Hilbert space form of the Van der Corput lemma, including the density version. Suppose ∥un​∥≤M, and put
ch​=limsupN→∞​​N1​∑n=0N−h−1​⟨un+h​,un​⟩​(h≥1).
(15)
Then
H1​h=1∑H​ch​⟶0⟹​N1​n=0∑N−1​un​​⟶0.​
(16)
Since 0≤ch​≤M2, it suffices for (ch​) to converge to zero in density. A frequently used special case is that the correlation average tends to zero for every fixed h≥1.
To prove the lemma, extend the finite list u0​,…,uN−1​ by zero outside this range, and write SN​=∑n=0N−1​un​. For 1≤H≤N,
HSN​=∑n=−H+1N−1​∑r=0H−1​un+r​.
(17)
The Cauchy-Schwarz inequality for this sum, followed by expansion of the squared Hilbert space norm, gives
H2∥SN​∥2≤(N+H−1)[H∑n=0N−1​∥un​∥2+2∑h=1H−1​(H−h)Re∑n=0N−h−1​⟨un+h​,un​⟩].

The coefficient H−h counts the pairs of shifts separated by h. Dividing by H2N2, bounding real parts by absolute values, and first taking the limit superior in N for fixed H yields
limsupN→∞​​NSN​​​2≤HM2​+H2​∑h=1H−1​(1−Hh​)ch​.

The right side tends to zero as H→∞ under the stated hypothesis. This proves both the lemma and its density consequence.
For the polynomial application, fix a nonzero integer m and let un​=e2πimP(n)∈C. For each fixed h≥1, the correlation phase is linear:
P(n+h)−P(n)=2ahn+ah2+bh.
(20)
Thus, with qh​=e2πi(2mah),
N1​∑n=0N−h−1​un+h​un​​=e2πim(ah2+bh)N(1−qh​)1−qhN−h​​.
(21)
The coefficient 2mah is irrational, so qh​=1. This geometric series is bounded in modulus by 2/(N∣1−qh​∣) and tends to zero. All ch​ vanish, so the Van der Corput lemma gives
N1​n=0∑N−1​e2πim(an2+bn+c)⟶0(m∈Z∖{0}).​
(22)
The Weyl criterion now shows that the fractional parts form an equidistributed sequence. For completeness, its sufficiency here follows directly: the displayed limits give the correct average for every trigonometric polynomial, including the constant term. The Stone-Weierstrass theorem extends this to every continuous function by uniform approximation. Approximating an interval's indicator function above and below by continuous functions gives its length as the limiting frequency. Hence
(an2+bn+c)mod1 is equidistributed in R/Z.​
(23)
This proves the equidistribution of a quadratic polynomial with irrational leading coefficient using the required correlation argument.
