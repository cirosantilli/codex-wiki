The action contains neither N˙ nor N˙i, so their conjugate momentum vanishes. They impose constraints rather than independent evolution equations: after the Legendre transform in mechanics, the lapse function and shift vector are Lagrange multipliers in the Hamiltonian formulation of general relativity.
Use precisely the paper's sign convention for extrinsic curvature, Kij​=(h˙ij​−Di​Nj​−Dj​Ni​)/(2N). At fixed hij​,N,Ni,
δKij​=2Nδh˙ij​​,δ[h​N(Kab​Kab−K2)]=h​(Kij−Khij)δh˙ij​.
(1)
Consequently the canonical momentum of the spatial metric is
πij=h​(Kij−Khij).​
(2)
This is a weight-one tensor density, with indices raised and lowered using hij​. Its trace and inverse relation are
π=hij​πij=−2h​K,Kij=h​1​(πij−21​πhij).
(3)
The sign of Kij​ used here is opposite to the convention in extrinsic curvature of a spatial hypersurface; mixing the two conventions would reverse the momentum formula.

Choose the asymptotic time translation: N=1+O(r−1), Ni=O(r−1), with their derivatives O(r−2), in asymptotically Cartesian coordinates. Use the usual differentiable falloff hij​=δij​+O(r−1), ∂k​hij​=O(r−2), πij=O(r−2), and analogous falloff for allowed variations; impose the standard additional conditions needed for finite bulk integrals. These variations need not fix the Arnowitt-Deser-Misner energy.
Only the spatial curvature term produces the leading nonvanishing surface variation. Applying the variation of the Ricci scalar and applying integration by parts to its two derivative terms yields
δHbulk​=∫d3x(Fijδhij​+Gij​δπij)−limr→∞​∫Sr​​dAni(∂j​δhij​−∂i​δhjj​).
(8)
The terms involving derivatives of the asymptotic lapse function, and the shift boundary terms, vanish under these falloff conditions. The displayed surface integral need not vanish: its integrand is O(r−2) and the area is O(r2).
Hamilton's equations require a Hamiltonian with a variation expressed by the bulk functional derivatives alone for the allowed variations. The unwanted term is exactly −δEADM​, so differentiability of the gravitational Hamiltonian at spatial infinity requires
Htotal​=Hbulk​+EADM​.​
(9)
Its variation cancels the surface term. On the constraint surface, Htotal​=EADM​. Restoring units inserts 1/(16πG) in the surface integral; the paper has set this factor to one. The original PDF has the standard equation ∂t​hij​=δH/δπij; the extra t in the extracted TeX is a transcription error.

A closed spatial Cauchy hypersurface is compact and has no boundary. Thus there is no asymptotic surface charge, and the complete gravitational Hamiltonian is the integral of the Hamiltonian constraint and momentum constraint derived above. Both constraints vanish on solutions, giving
Htotal​=0.​
(10)
This is the vanishing canonical Hamiltonian of a closed universe: canonical evolution is generated by constraints and there is no asymptotic time translation whose boundary charge could define an Arnowitt-Deser-Misner energy. It does not require local matter energy densities or local curvature to vanish. With matter included, the corresponding total constraints still vanish.

Let k generate the Killing horizon and let ℓ=fk be an affinely parametrized generator wherever k=0. Choose an auxiliary null vector n with n⋅ℓ=−1 and the screen-space projector qab​=gab​+ℓa​nb​+na​ℓb​. The optical tensor is the screen projection Bab​=qa​cqb​d∇d​ℓc​.
Since the projector annihilates k, the symmetric part is
B(ab)​=fqa​cqb​d∇(d​kc)​=0
(11)
by the Killing equation. Its trace is the null expansion and its trace-free symmetric part is the null shear, so both vanish. Also ℓ is normal to the null hypersurface. Frobenius theorem gives ℓ[a​∇b​ℓc]​=0, which implies qa​cqb​d∇[d​ℓc]​=0: the null twist vanishes. These arguments use derivatives tangent to the horizon and hence apply to any smooth null geodesic congruence containing its generators, independently of its extension off the horizon. At a regular bifurcation surface, use the smooth affine generator and continuity. Thus
θ=0,σ^ab​=0,ω^ab​=0on the horizon.​
(12)
This is the vanishing of the optical scalars on a Killing horizon.

b OurBigBook.com
The Physical-process first law for a rotating black hole applies to a small perturbation of a stationary nonextremal vacuum black hole by infalling neutral matter, followed by relaxation to stationarity. Its first-order form is
δM−ΩH​δJ=8πGκ​δA.​
(13)
Here the background horizon generator is ξ=t+ΩH​φ, normalized by the unit time translation at infinity, and its surface gravity is constant, κ>0, as in the Zeroth law of black-hole mechanics. The formula neglects terms quadratic in the perturbation. Choose the process sufficiently weak that the horizon generators used below have no large caustics, and assume appropriate early and late stationary limits and convergent fluxes.
On the background Killing horizon, choose an affine generator ℓ=d/dλ such that ξ=κλℓ; the bifurcation or early stationary limit corresponds to λ=0. The background null expansion and null shear vanish. The null twist is zero because the perturbed event horizon is a null hypersurface. Linearizing the Null Raychaudhuri equation and using the Einstein field equations gives
dλd(δθ)​=−δRab​ℓaℓb=−8πGδTab​ℓaℓb.
(14)
The expansion squared and shear squared are second order, and the metric-trace part of the Einstein equations vanishes in this null contraction. Future stationarity sets δθ→0. To first order δθ is the derivative of the fractional area change, so, using the background area element dA0​,
δA=∫dA0​∫0∞​δθdλ=8πG∫dA0​∫0∞​λδTab​ℓaℓbdλ.
(15)
The integration by parts uses [λδθ]0∞​=0, guaranteed by the stated endpoint conditions.
The conserved stress-energy current from a Killing vector gives the absorbed mass and angular momentum, with signs
δM=∫N​δTab​taℓbdλdA0​,δJ=−∫N​δTab​φaℓbdλdA0​.
(16)
Consequently
δM−ΩH​δJ=∫N​δTab​ξaℓbdλdA0​=κ∫N​λδTab​ℓaℓbdλdA0​,
(17)
which proves the law. Any other escaping flux must be accounted for separately when identifying these absorbed charges with the changes in the black hole. Electric-charge variations would require the corresponding potential-charge term; they are excluded in this version. The convention 16πG=1 in Question 1 is not needed here.

For a Kerr black hole, the future horizon generator is ξ=t+ΩH​φ. A future-directed causal four-momentum p and a future-directed null vector ξ satisfy g(p,ξ)≤0 in the (−+++) convention. The conserved quantities from the Killing vector field are E=−p⋅t and L=p⋅φ. Therefore
E−ΩH​L=−p⋅ξ≥0.​
(18)
For a massive particle the inequality is strict. Equality for a null particle requires momentum parallel to the horizon generator and describes the limiting tangent case. In a Penrose process the absorbed particle may have E<0; for ΩH​>0 this requires sufficiently negative angular momentum.
In the nonextremal test-particle regime, the Physical-process first law for a rotating black hole identifies δM=E and δJ=L, so
E−ΩH​L=8πGκ​δA≥0
(19)
by the second law of black-hole mechanics. The usual hypotheses of that area law, including the null energy condition and the required global regularity conditions, are understood. This thermodynamic derivation is first order in the capture process; the causal horizon flux inequality for causal matter also applies to the extremal horizon without dividing by κ.

A radial null geodesic has L=0 and ϵ=0, so r˙=±E and t˙=E/V. In the exterior, E>0 for a future-directed ray. Define the analogue of the Schwarzschild tortoise coordinate by

Given an asymptotically flat spacetime with a conformal completion and future null infinity I+, its black hole region is
B=M∖J−(I+).​
(28)
The event horizon is its boundary. For the magnetically charged dilaton black hole, first assume 0≤a=r−​<b=r+​. The physical asymptotic component has r>a, and the ingoing chart extends regularly across r=b. Its future ingoing null direction is −∂r​. For a<r<b,
∇r=∂v​+V∂r​,g(∇r,∇r)=V<0,g(∇r,−∂r​)=−1.
(29)
Hence ∇r is future timelike. Every nonzero future causal tangent X satisfies X(r)=g(∇r,X)<0. A future causal curve from this interior can neither cross back to r=b nor reach the asymptotic end r→∞, so it cannot reach I+. It lies in B.
This proof uses the future black-hole extension. The radial interval alone does not distinguish it from the past white hole interior of the maximal extension; that interior has the same interval and can send signals to infinity. Thus the statement needs this future-component qualification if the maximal spacetime is intended.
The Kretschmann scalar confirms that r=a is a genuine curvature singularity: for 0<a<b, its numerator at r=a is 3a4(a−b)2, and
Rabcd​Rabcd∼4a2(r−a)43(a−b)2​.
(30)
For a=0 it instead reduces to 12b2/r6. Since V<0 on approaching the singularity from the interior, the singularity is spacelike. At r=b>a the curvature is finite.
For the maximal extension, take U=−e−u/(2b), W=ev/(2b) in the right exterior and continue analytically across the horizons. Then
UW=(1−br​)er/b,ds2=−r4b3​e−r/bdUdW+R2dΩ22​.
(31)
The singularity is UW=(1−a/b)ea/b>0 and the horizons are U=0 and W=0. Rescaling the null coordinates by the square root of this positive constant before arctangent compactification produces the usual qualitative Penrose diagram of Schwarzschild spacetime: two exteriors, a future black-hole region, a past white-hole region, and future and past spacelike singularities. The dashed lines below are the horizon branches; only the future interior is the black-hole region.
If a>b, the physical domain remains r>a, but now V>0 throughout it. The apparent zero r=b is beyond the curvature singularity and is not an accessible horizon. The singularity is timelike, and outgoing radial null rays with r˙=E>0 escape to infinity from arbitrarily near it. Thus
r−​>r+​: a timelike naked singularity, with no black-hole horizon.​
(32)
The excluded equality r−​=r+​ is a singular limiting case, not a regular horizon. These facts describe the causal structure of the magnetic dilaton black hole.

For complex solutions of the Klein-Gordon equation, define the current
ja=i(α∇aβ−β∇aα).
(34)
Its divergence is
∇a​ja=i(α□β−β□α)=0,
(35)
because □β=m2β and □α=m2α for real m2. The Klein-Gordon inner product is the flux ∫Σ​na​jadΣ. Applying Stokes theorem to a slab between two Cauchy hypersurfaces gives equality of the fluxes, with the future normal on each surface and opposite boundary orientations.
For noncompact surfaces this requires the integrals to converge and no net flux through spatial infinity, for example spacelike compact support or suitable decay. Global hyperbolicity alone does not ensure convergence for arbitrary solutions. Under these conditions,
(α,β)Σ1​​=(α,β)Σ2​​.​
(36)
Complex conjugation also gives
(β,α)​=−i∫Σ​dΣna(β∇a​α−α∇a​β)=(α,β).
(37)
Thus it is a Hermitian form, antilinear in its first argument and linear in its second. It is indefinite on the full complex solution space; positivity will arise only on a chosen positive-frequency solution subspace.

The Klein-Gordon inner product satisfies (α,β​)=−(β,α), so (ψ​i​,ψ​j​)=−δij​. Orthogonality of positive and negative modes in the field expansion therefore gives (ψi​,Φ)=ai​ and (ψ​i​,Φ)=−ai†​.
Extracting the primed coefficient in the same way, and using antilinearity in the first entry,
ai′​=(ψi′​,Φ)=∑j​[Aij​(ψj​,Φ)+Bij​(ψ​j​,Φ)].
(40)
Consequently the Bogoliubov transformation of the creation and annihilation operators is
ai′​=j∑​(Aij​aj​−Bij​aj†​).​
(41)
The minus sign comes from the negative norm of the conjugate modes, and the complex conjugates come from the first argument of the Hermitian pairing.

Use the canonical commutation relation for the creation and annihilation operators and the transformation derived above. Directly,
[ai′​,ak′​†]=∑j​(Aij​Akj​−Bij​Bkj​),[ai′​,ak′​]=∑j​(−Aij​Bkj​+Bij​Akj​).
(42)
Requiring these to equal δik​ and zero, respectively, and conjugating the equations yields the canonical identities for a bosonic Bogoliubov transformation:
AA†−BB†=I,ABT=BAT.​
(43)
The commutator of the primed creation operators then vanishes by taking adjoints. These are exactly the corresponding positive-mode norm and positive-negative orthogonality conditions for the Bogoliubov transformation. They are sufficient for these algebraic commutators whenever the sums are well defined. Infinitely many modes introduce further questions of convergence and whether the two choices admit a common bosonic Fock space representation; the matrix identities alone do not settle unitary implementability.

Choose the unprimed modes as the inertial positive-frequency modes in the far past, and the primed modes as those in the far future. In the in-vacuum and out-vacuum construction, ∣0in​⟩ is annihilated by every aj​. The number of late-time quanta in mode i is measured by Ni′​=ai′​†ai′​. Expanding using the Bogoliubov transformation, the only surviving vacuum contraction is ⟨0in​∣aj​ak†​∣0in​⟩=δjk​. Thus the particle number from Bogoliubov coefficients is
⟨0in​∣Ni′​∣0in​⟩=j∑​∣Bij​∣2.​
(44)
If B=0, at least one row has positive norm and late-time observers detect particles. If B=0, each ai′​ is a linear combination of past annihilation operators and annihilates the same state, so all these occupation numbers vanish. The intervening nonstationary geometry can mix positive and negative frequencies, realizing vacuum ambiguity in a nonstationary spacetime even though the asymptotic inertial choices are unambiguous.
For infinitely many modes, the expressions are understood with suitable convergence or wave-packet regularization. The total expected number is ∑ij​∣Bij​∣2, finite precisely when B is a Hilbert-Schmidt operator in the specified orthonormal mode spaces. A divergent sum is not a finite occupation number in a common vacuum representation; it signals the extra infinite-dimensional issue rather than changing the finite-mode calculation.
