If M has complex dimension n, its complexified cotangent bundle splits into the (1,0) and (0,1) cotangent bundles. The space of global smooth differential forms of type (p, q) is
Ap,q(M)=ΓC∞​(M,Λp(T1,0M)∗⊗Λq(T0,1M)∗).​
(1)
In holomorphic coordinates, its elements have the form α=∑I,J​aI,J​dzI∧dzˉJ, where the coefficients are complex-valued smooth functions, ∣I∣=p, and ∣J∣=q.
The exterior derivative decomposes as d=∂+∂ˉ. The conjugate Dolbeault operator ∂ and Dolbeault operator ∂ˉ are its type components:
∂α=I,J,k∑​∂zk​∂aI,J​​dzk​∧dzI∧dzˉJ,∂ˉα=I,J,k∑​∂zˉk​∂aI,J​​dzˉk​∧dzI∧dzˉJ.​
(2)
They have bidegrees (1,0) and (0,1) respectively. Keeping the displayed wedge order avoids hiding a sign; moving dzˉk​ past dzI introduces (−1)p. Decomposing d2=0 by type gives ∂2=∂ˉ2=0 and ∂∂ˉ+∂ˉ∂=0.

A pure-type d-closed form is both ∂-closed and ∂ˉ-closed, because these two derivatives have different types. Let k=p+q. The smooth Poincaré lemma on the polydisc gives η∈ACk−1​ with dη=α. We will modify η by exact terms without changing dη.
Write η=∑r​ηr,k−1−r. Starting with the smallest holomorphic degree, eliminate every component with r<p−1. Once the lower components are zero, the (r,k−r) component of dη says ∂ˉηr,k−1−r=0, since r<p. Its antiholomorphic degree is positive, so the Dolbeault-Poincaré lemma supplies νr,k−2−r with ∂ˉν=ηr,k−1−r. Replace η by η−dν: this removes the component at r and changes only the next holomorphic degree.
Next, work downwards from the largest holomorphic degree and eliminate the components with r>p. Once the higher components are zero, the corresponding component of dη gives ∂ηr,k−1−r=0. By part (b), write this as ∂νr−1,k−1−r and again subtract dν. This affects only the next lower holomorphic degree. The two finite procedures leave
η=u+v,u∈Ap−1,q,v∈Ap,q−1.
(4)
The components of dη=α outside type (p,q) now give ∂ˉu=0 and ∂v=0, while its (p,q) component gives α=∂u+∂ˉv.
Since q≥1, the Dolbeault-Poincaré lemma gives u=∂ˉa with a of type (p−1,q−1). Since p≥1, part (b) gives v=∂b of the same type. The anticommutation identity therefore yields
α=∂∂ˉa+∂ˉ∂b=∂∂ˉ(a−b).
(5)
This proves the Bott-Chern Poincaré lemma using precisely the permitted primitives:
HBCp,q​(Pn)=0(p,q≥1).​

Let CC∞​ be the additive sheaf of complex-valued smooth functions, and CC∞,∗​ its multiplicative sheaf of nowhere-zero functions. The smooth exponential sequence is
0⟶Z​⟶CC∞​f↦e2πif​CC∞,∗​⟶1.
(12)
Its kernel is the integer-valued locally constant functions, namely the integer constant sheaf. It is surjective on stalks because a nowhere-zero smooth function has a smooth logarithm on a sufficiently small neighbourhood.
The additive smooth-function sheaf is a fine sheaf, using a partition of unity, and the smooth manifold is a paracompact space. Its positive-degree sheaf cohomology vanishes. The long exact sequence in sheaf cohomology consequently gives an isomorphism
δ:H1(X,CC∞,∗​)∼​H2(X,Z).
(13)
To connect this with bundles, trivialize a smooth complex line bundle over an open cover. Its transition functions gij​ form a multiplicative Čech cocycle, satisfying gij​gjk​=gik​. Changing trivializations changes it by a Čech coboundary. Conversely, any such cocycle glues trivial line bundles, and cohomologous cocycles give isomorphic bundles. Thus the isomorphism classes are H1(X,CC∞,∗​).
The connecting isomorphism is the First Chern class. Locally choose logarithms gij​=e2πifij​; on triple intersections
nijk​=fij​+fjk​−fik​∈Z
(14)

The restriction maps of this point sheaf satisfy the presheaf composition identities: every possible restriction is either the identity on C or the unique map to the zero group. We check the sheaf identity and gluing axioms for any open cover U=⋃i​Ui​.
If p∈/U, every section group in the cover is zero, so a compatible family has the unique zero gluing. If p∈U, at least one Ui​ contains p. For any two members containing p, their intersection also contains p, and compatibility forces their complex values to be equal, since the restriction maps there are identities. Call the common value c. Members not containing p have only the zero section. The section c∈Cp​(U) restricts to every member of the family, proving existence of a gluing; its restriction to any member containing p determines c, proving uniqueness.
This also proves local identity for sections, and Cp​(∅)=0 handles the empty open set. Hence
Cp​ is a sheaf of abelian groups.​
(16)
It is the point-pushforward sheaf, usually called a skyscraper sheaf. Its restrictions are all surjective, so it is also a flasque sheaf. No separation assumption on X is needed for the preceding sheaf-axiom argument.

Let ω be the Kähler form associated with the metric. The Lefschetz operator of a Kähler manifold and adjoint Lefschetz operator are
Lα=ω∧α,Λ=L∗.​
(34)
Here the adjoint is taken with respect to the pointwise Hermitian inner product on complexified forms, or equivalently the global L2 inner product. It lowers degree by two and bidegree by (1,1). In terms of the Hodge star operator, Λ=∗−1L∗.
Since dω=0, dL=Ld, so L takes closed differential forms to closed differential forms and exact differential forms to exact differential forms. This already gives its action on de Rham cohomology. For both operators together, use the Kähler identities: L commutes with the Hodge Laplacian, and taking adjoints shows that Λ does too. Thus both preserve harmonic differential forms.
When M is compact, the Hodge decomposition theorem identifies Hk(M,C) with its space of harmonic k-forms. If hα​ is the unique harmonic differential form representing [α], define
L[α]=[Lhα​],Λ[α]=[Λhα​].​
(35)
The resulting forms are harmonic and therefore closed, and uniqueness of hα​ makes these definitions independent of the initial representative. The cohomological definition of Λ uses harmonic differential forms as representatives: Λ need not take an arbitrary closed form to a closed form.

Let ZM1​ be the sheaf of closed holomorphic one-forms, namely ker(∂:ΩM1​→ΩM2​). The local exactness proved in part (b) gives the short exact sequence of sheaves
0⟶C​⟶OM​∂​ZM1​⟶0.
(52)
Its long exact sequence in sheaf cohomology begins
H0(M,C)⟶H0(M,OM​)⟶H0(M,ZM1​)δ​H1(M,C)⟶H1(M,OM​).
(53)
The first arrow is an isomorphism: on a compact complex manifold, a global holomorphic function is constant on each connected component, just as a global section of the constant sheaf is. Thus δ is injective. By part (c), every global holomorphic differential form of degree one is closed, so H0(M,ZM1​)=H0(M,ΩM1​).
Concretely, for a holomorphic differential form of degree one choose local holomorphic primitives fi​. The connecting class δ(α) is represented by the locally constant Čech cocycle fj​−fi​ on overlaps. The following arrow is induced by the inclusion of the constant sheaf into the sheaf of holomorphic functions. Exactness is inherited from the long exact sequence, giving
0⟶H0(M,ΩM1​)δ​H1(M,C)⟶H1(M,OM​).​
(54)
