Use the wiki's Cartan matrix convention
Aij​=⟨αi​,αj∨​⟩=(αj​,αj​)2(αi​,αj​)​.
(17)
Label the long simple root first when the lengths differ. The matrix of an irreducible rank-two root system has the form A=(2−b​−a2​), where a,b are positive integers. Integrality and the signs follow from the Cartan integers for distinct simple roots, and irreducibility rules out a zero off-diagonal pair. Its positive-definite symmetrization gives ab<4, equivalently ab=4cos2θ<4. Moreover a/b=∥α1​∥2/∥α2​∥2≥1. Thus (a,b)=(1,1),(2,1),(3,1), giving the entire classification of rank-two root systems relevant to a simple algebra:
typeA2​B2​=C2​G2​​A(2−1​−12​)(2−1​−22​)(2−1​−32​)​∥α1​∥/∥α2​∥12​3​​θ2π/33π/45π/6​​​
(18)
These correspond to sl3​(C), so5​(C)≅sp4​(C), and the exceptional algebra g2​. Here B2​=C2​ denotes the Isomorphism between so5 and sp4, not an additional case. The disconnected A1​×A1​ system corresponds to a semisimple but nonsimple algebra and is excluded.
The Dynkin diagrams, with nodes in the same order as the matrices, are
A2​B2​G2​​:::​∘α1​−∘α2​,∘α1​⇒∘α2​,∘α1​≡>∘α2​.​
(19)
The second and third diagrams have two and three bonds respectively, with the arrowhead pointing to the short root α2​. In the last diagram ≡ draws the three bonds and > their arrowhead.
To enumerate roots, use the standard root string theorem: for β=±α, the roots β+nα are consecutive from β−pα through β+qα, with p−q=⟨β,α∨⟩. Also use the standard results that the root system is reduced, every root is conjugate under the Weyl group to a simple root, and each root space has dimension one.
Write m=a∈{1,2,3}. Since a difference of simple roots is not a root, the α2​-string starting at α1​ has p=0 and q=m. It produces α1​,α1​+α2​,…,α1​+mα2​. The α1​-string starting at α2​ has q=1. In type G2​, the further string through β=α1​+3α2​ has
⟨β,α1∨​⟩=2−3=−1,
(20)
and β−α1​=3α2​ is not a root, so it also produces 2α1​+3α2​. The resulting positive roots are
Φ+(A2​)Φ+(B2​)Φ+(G2​)​={α1​,α2​,α1​+α2​},={α1​,α2​,α1​+α2​,α1​+2α2​},={α1​,α2​,α1​+α2​,α1​+2α2​,α1​+3α2​,2α1​+3α2​}.​​
(21)
All roots are these and their negatives. To check completeness, the simple root reflections act on coordinates by
s1​(rα1​+sα2​)=(−r+s)α1​+sα2​,s2​(rα1​+sα2​)=rα1​+(mr−s)α2​.
(22)
Each listed set together with its negatives is stable under both reflections. It contains the simple roots and consists of roots already forced by strings. Since every root lies in a Weyl orbit of a simple root, no other roots can occur. This proves the lists for the A2 root system, B2 root system, and G2 root system. The root-space decomposition then gives
dimsl3​=2+6=8,dimso5​=2+8=10,dimg2​=2+12=14.​
(23)
For the restriction to a sl2 subalgebra associated with a root, normalize its Cartan element to be hα​=α∨. On a root vector of root β, its eigenvalue is ⟨β,α∨⟩. The classification of finite-dimensional sl2 representations says that R(Λ) has weights Λ,Λ−2,…,−Λ and dimension Λ+1. A root string of Λ+1 nonparallel roots therefore supplies one such irreducible module: the raising and lowering brackets connect its consecutive root spaces.
For clarity, the positive-side strings for each simple-root direction are listed below. Brackets denote the whole consecutive string; a single listed root is a string of length one. In every row also include each negative string in reverse order, and separately the triple formed by the root αi​, its negative, and hαi​​.

The root's own triple is always R(2). The one-dimensional subspace of h annihilated by αi​ commutes with this subalgebra and gives R(0). Each singleton root in the table, and its negative, contributes another R(0). Reading the other string lengths now gives the adjoint branching to root sl2 subalgebras in rank two:
