A closed subgroup of R is zero, τZ for some τ>0, or all of R. Two qualifications matter. If X=0, the image is the trivial group, a third possibility omitted by the wording “two”. Also, a general one-parameter subgroup need not be closed or embedded: an irrational winding t↦diag(R(t),R(2​t)) in a two-torus is an example, where R(t) is a planar rotation. The classification uses the intrinsic immersed Lie subgroup structure, rather than assuming that the matrix subspace topology is the topology of R.
