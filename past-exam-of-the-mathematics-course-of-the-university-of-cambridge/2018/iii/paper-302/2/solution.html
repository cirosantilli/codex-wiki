Work over R or C, in finite dimension and characteristic zero. The Killing form of a Lie algebra is the trace form of its Adjoint representation:
κ(x,y)=tr(adx​ady​),adx​(z)=[x,z].​
(9)
It is bilinear and symmetric by the cyclic property of the trace. It is an invariant bilinear form on a Lie algebra, since the Jacobi identity gives ad[x,y]​=[adx​,ady​] and hence
κ([x,y],z)​=tr([adx​,ady​]adz​)=tr(adx​[ady​,adz​])=κ(x,[y,z]).​
(10)
Equivalently, κ([z,x],y)+κ(x,[z,y])=0. A Lie algebra automorphism preserves the form, because it conjugates the adjoint matrices. Its radical of a bilinear form rκ​={x:κ(x,g)=0} is an ideal of a Lie algebra by invariance. The center of a Lie algebra lies in this radical, so the form vanishes for an abelian algebra. On a direct sum of ideals the summands are orthogonal and the form restricts to their own Killing forms.
A short argument proves the requested implication without assuming the Cartan criterion for semisimplicity. Let a be an abelian ideal of a Lie algebra, x∈a, and y∈g. The map adx​ takes g into a and vanishes on a, while ady​ preserves a. Therefore adx​ady​ has image in a and is zero on a. In a basis extending a basis of a, both diagonal blocks are zero, so its trace vanishes. This proves that abelian ideals lie in the radical of the Killing form.
If the solvable radical r were nonzero, its derived series of a Lie algebra would have a last nonzero term a. The Jacobi identity makes every derived term an ideal of g, and the last one is abelian. Thus 0=a⊆rκ​, contradicting nondegeneracy. We obtain
κ nondegenerate ⟹ r=0 ⟹ g is a semisimple Lie algebra.​
(11)
The converse holds as well in characteristic zero; together these implications are the Cartan criterion for semisimplicity.
For a complex simple Lie algebra, that criterion gives nondegeneracy on g. Let h be a Cartan subalgebra. Use the standard root-space decomposition
g=h⊕⨁α∈Φ​gα​,[h,eα​]=α(h)eα​.
(12)
For h,h′∈h and a root vector eα​, choose t∈h with α(t)=0. Invariance gives
α(t)κ(h,eα​)=κ(h,[t,eα​])=κ([h,t],eα​)=0.
(13)
Thus h is orthogonal to every nonzero root space. If h∈h is also orthogonal to h, it is orthogonal to all of g and hence is zero. This establishes nondegeneracy of the Killing form on a Cartan subalgebra:
κ∣h×h​ is nondegenerate.​
(14)
The same invariance calculation shows that κ(gα​,gβ​)=0 unless α+β=0. Opposite root spaces are therefore paired nondegenerately. Tracing the adjoint action on the root-space decomposition yields
κ(h,h′)=∑α∈Φ​α(h)α(h′),
(15)
since the root spaces of a complex semisimple algebra are one-dimensional and its adjoint action on h is zero.
The relevant Euclidean subspace of a Cartan subalgebra is
hR​=spanR​{hα1​​,…,hαℓ​​}={h∈h:α(h)∈R for all α∈Φ},
(16)
where the hαi​​ are the simple coroots, normalized by αi​(hαi​​)=2. The standard Euclidean property is that κ is real and positive definite on this space. The displayed trace formula explains it: κ(h,h) is a sum of real squares, and the roots span h∗, so all squares vanish only for h=0. Via the nondegenerate form, the roots can consequently be regarded as vectors in a real Euclidean normed vector space. The induced dual inner product makes root reflections orthogonal and allows root lengths and angles to be encoded by Cartan integers and the Dynkin diagram.
This positivity is on a specified real subspace; the complex Killing form is a bilinear form, not a positive Hermitian inner product. On a compact real form the Killing form is instead negative definite. For example the Killing form of the special linear Lie algebra gives κ(X,Y)=4tr(XY) for sl2​, so κ(L0​,L0​)=8 but κ(iL0​,iL0​)=−8.
