Use N={0,1,…}, as required by the formula at zero, and composition of functions as multiplication in M. The displayed T preserves identities, and for n>0,
T(gf)(n)=g(f(n−1))+1=Tg(Tf(n));
(33)
both sides send zero to zero. Thus T is a functor on the one-object category.
For the shift monad on order-preserving maps of natural numbers, set
η(n)=n+1,μ(n)=max(n−1,0).
(34)
These are order-preserving functions. The equations Tfη=ηf and μT2f=Tfμ hold pointwise, giving the required natural transformations. For the second equation, both sides are zero at n=0,1, and are f(n−2)+1 for n≥2. The two unit laws are μη=μTη=1. Associativity is checked by
(μTμ)(n)=(μμ)(n)=max(n−2,0).
(35)
Consequently these maps define a monad. It is not an idempotent monad, since μ(0)=μ(1), so μ cannot be invertible.
An algebra for a monad is a map a∈M with aη=1 and aTa=aμ. The first equation forces a(n+1)=n; monotonicity then forces a(0)=0. Thus a=μ, which satisfies the second equation by the monad associativity law. The Eilenberg-Moore category therefore has exactly one object. Its endomorphisms h satisfy hμ=μTh, and this equation holds precisely when h(0)=0: evaluate at zero for necessity, and at n>0 both sides equal h(n−1).
The Kleisli comparison functor takes its only object to this only algebra and sends f∈M to
K(f)=μTf,K(f)(0)=0,K(f)(n+1)=f(n).
(36)
It is a bijection from the Kleisli arrows to the algebra endomorphisms, with inverse h↦(n↦h(n+1)). It preserves identities and composition by the comparison construction; directly, K(η)=1 and K(g⋆f)=K(g)K(f). Bijectivity on objects and arrows makes it an isomorphism of categories:
MT​≅MT,μ is not invertible.​
(37)
