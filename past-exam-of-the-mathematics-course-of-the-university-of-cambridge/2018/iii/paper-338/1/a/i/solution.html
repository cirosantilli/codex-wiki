Past exam of the mathematics course of the University of Cambridge / 2018 / iii / Paper 338 / 1 / a / i / Solution

 ... 2018 iii Paper 338 1 a i
OurBigBook.com Words: 234
Use the gnomonic projection associated with an undistorted focal plane. Write Δ=α−A and rotate the celestial Cartesian coordinate system so that the pointing meridian has longitude zero. The stellar direction and an orthonormal basis adapted to the optical axis are
s=(cosδcosΔ,cosδsinΔ,sinδ),b=(cosD,0,sinD),e=(0,1,0),n=(−sinD,0,cosD).
(1)
Here e points east and n points north. Intersect the ray ts with the plane x⋅b=f. It gives t=f/(s⋅b), so the detector coordinates are
ξ=fcosδcosΔcosD+sinδsinDcosδsinΔ​,η=fcosδcosΔcosD+sinδsinDsinδcosD−cosδcosΔsinD​.
(2)
These equations also avoid spurious singularities caused by writing individual tangents or cotangents.
To put this gnomonic projection in the desired form, let ρ=(cos2δcos2Δ+sin2δ)1/2 and choose q locally by
ρcosq=cosδcosΔ,ρsinq=sinδ.
(3)
Thus cotq=cotδcosΔ. The denominator becomes ρcos(q−D), the numerator for η becomes ρsin(q−D), and cosδsinΔ=ρcosqtanΔ. Consequently
ξ=fcos(q−D)cosqtan(α−A)​,η=ftan(q−D).​
(4)
Use the local meridian chart cosΔ>0, with q∈[−π/2,π/2] chosen continuously near D and q=D at the image centre. A visible gnomonic projection requires s⋅b>0, but this front-hemisphere condition alone does not select that meridian chart. A narrow field near a celestial pole can cross the opposite meridian; for such fields use the Cartesian expressions above and distinguish the oriented great circle parameter from ordinary declination. In particular, the small-field limit is ξ≃fcosD(α−A) and η≃f(δ−D), with angles in radians. The factor cosD is the shrinking angular distance per unit right ascension near the pole.
