The Physical-process first law for a rotating black hole applies to a small perturbation of a stationary nonextremal vacuum black hole by infalling neutral matter, followed by relaxation to stationarity. Its first-order form is
δM−ΩH​δJ=8πGκ​δA.​
(13)
Here the background horizon generator is ξ=t+ΩH​φ, normalized by the unit time translation at infinity, and its surface gravity is constant, κ>0, as in the Zeroth law of black-hole mechanics. The formula neglects terms quadratic in the perturbation. Choose the process sufficiently weak that the horizon generators used below have no large caustics, and assume appropriate early and late stationary limits and convergent fluxes.
On the background Killing horizon, choose an affine generator ℓ=d/dλ such that ξ=κλℓ; the bifurcation or early stationary limit corresponds to λ=0. The background null expansion and null shear vanish. The null twist is zero because the perturbed event horizon is a null hypersurface. Linearizing the Null Raychaudhuri equation and using the Einstein field equations gives
dλd(δθ)​=−δRab​ℓaℓb=−8πGδTab​ℓaℓb.
(14)
The expansion squared and shear squared are second order, and the metric-trace part of the Einstein equations vanishes in this null contraction. Future stationarity sets δθ→0. To first order δθ is the derivative of the fractional area change, so, using the background area element dA0​,
δA=∫dA0​∫0∞​δθdλ=8πG∫dA0​∫0∞​λδTab​ℓaℓbdλ.
(15)
The integration by parts uses [λδθ]0∞​=0, guaranteed by the stated endpoint conditions.
The conserved stress-energy current from a Killing vector gives the absorbed mass and angular momentum, with signs
δM=∫N​δTab​taℓbdλdA0​,δJ=−∫N​δTab​φaℓbdλdA0​.
(16)
Consequently
δM−ΩH​δJ=∫N​δTab​ξaℓbdλdA0​=κ∫N​λδTab​ℓaℓbdλdA0​,
(17)
which proves the law. Any other escaping flux must be accounted for separately when identifying these absorbed charges with the changes in the black hole. Electric-charge variations would require the corresponding potential-charge term; they are excluded in this version. The convention 16πG=1 in Question 1 is not needed here.
