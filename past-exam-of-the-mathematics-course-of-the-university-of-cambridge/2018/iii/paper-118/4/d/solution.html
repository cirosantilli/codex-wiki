Use the usual connected-manifold convention for this assertion. Suppose, for contradiction, that H2(M,OM​)=0. The Dolbeault theorem gives H0,2=0, and the Hodge decomposition theorem for compact Kähler manifolds and its conjugation symmetry give H2,0=0. Hence every real class in H2(M,R) has a real harmonic differential form of type (1,1) as its representative.
The intersection form is Q([u],[v])=∫M​u∧v. Its value on the Kähler class is positive: Q([ω],[ω])=∫M​ω2>0. Every real harmonic (1,1) form splits uniquely as cω+u0​, with u0​ primitive and c constant. Indeed, Λu is a harmonic function, hence constant by connectedness, and Λω=2; subtracting cω kills Λu, and part (b) makes u0​ primitive.
Part (c) gives ∗u0​=−u0​, while ω∧u0​=0. Thus
Q([ω],[u0​])=0,Q([u0​],[u0​])=−∥u0​∥L22​<0(u0​=0).
(40)
This proves the Hodge index theorem for compact Kähler surfaces in this case: Q has signature (1,b2​−1).
On the alleged two-dimensional totally isotropic subspace V, the linear functional v↦Q(v,[ω]) has a nonzero vector in its kernel. Its harmonic differential form representative is primitive, so the preceding negative-definiteness statement gives Q(v,v)<0, contradicting the vanishing of Q on V. Therefore
dimR​V=2,Q∣V⊗V​=0 ⟹ H2(M,OM​)=0.​
(41)
Connectedness is essential if one's definition of a manifold allows disconnected spaces. For M=Y⊔Y, with Y=CP1×CP1, one has H2(M,OM​)=0. Take on each component the pullback of the degree-two class from its first factor. Each class has square zero, and their mutual pairing is zero because their supports lie on different components. Their span is then a two-dimensional totally isotropic subspace, disproving the unqualified disconnected version.
