Let q denote the quotient map and write [X] for a point of Real projective space. For i=1,…,n+1, take the open set
Ui​={[X]:Xi​=0}.
(1)
It is open in the quotient topology because its inverse image under q is the open set {X:Xi​=0}. These sets cover, since a nonzero vector has at least one nonzero coordinate. Define the manifold chart
φi​([X])=(X1​/Xi​,…,Xi​/Xi​​,…,Xn+1​/Xi​)∈Rn.
(2)
The hat means that the ith coordinate is omitted. Ratios are unchanged by multiplying X by any nonzero real number, so the chart is well defined. Its inverse inserts 1 into position i and takes the resulting equivalence class. The ratios and this inverse are continuous, giving a homeomorphism Ui​→Rn.
For a compact description of the smooth transition maps, write ai​=1 and let ak​=Xk​/Xi​ for k=i. On Ui​∩Uj​ one has aj​=0, and
(φj​∘φi−1​)(a)k​=aj​ak​​(k=j).​
(3)
Each coordinate is a rational smooth function on the open domain aj​=0; reversing i,j gives a smooth inverse. Thus these n+1 charts form the standard affine atlas of real projective space.
For completeness, the underlying space is Hausdorff: normalizing X identifies it with the antipodal quotient of the unit sphere, and the continuous injective map [X]↦XXT/(XTX) into the space of symmetric matrices separates any two distinct classes by disjoint open neighborhoods. It is also a second-countable space: pull back the countable bases of rational balls in these finitely many charts, and take their union. Therefore
RPn is a smooth manifold of dimension n, with an atlas of n+1 charts.​
(4)

Use the curvature convention [∇b​,∇c​]Vd=Rdabc​Va, consistent with the coordinate formula later in the paper. Choose normal coordinates at an arbitrary point p. The Levi-Civita connection has Γdab​(p)=0 and is torsion free, so Γdab​=Γdba​. At p,
Rdabc​=∂b​Γdac​−∂c​Γdab​.
(11)
Hence the cyclic sum is
Rdabc​+Rdbca​+Rdcab​=​∂b​Γdac​−∂c​Γdab​+∂c​Γdba​−∂a​Γdbc​+∂a​Γdcb​−∂b​Γdca​=0.​
(12)
Each derivative cancels using symmetry of the two lower connection indices. Because the Riemann curvature tensor is antisymmetric in its last two indices, full antisymmetrization over a,b,c equals one third of this cyclic sum. Thus
Rd[abc]​=0.​
(13)
The statement is tensorial and p was arbitrary, so it holds in all coordinates everywhere. This is the first Bianchi identity.

Let Dabc​=∇a​∇b​Kc​. Differentiating the Killing equation gives Dabc​=−Dacb​. For a covector, the Ricci identity is
Dabc​−Dbac​=−Rdcab​Kd​=Rcdab​Kd.
(14)
Call this difference Cabc​. The differentiated Killing equation then gives
Cabc​=Dabc​+Dbca​,Cbca​=Dbca​+Dcab​,Ccab​=Dcab​+Dabc​.
(15)
Taking the first minus the second plus the third yields
2Dabc​=(Rcdab​−Radbc​+Rbdca​)Kd.
(16)
The pair symmetries of the Riemann curvature tensor and the first Bianchi identity reduce the coefficient to 2Rcbad​. Therefore the second covariant derivative of a Killing vector is
∇a​∇b​Kc​=Rcbad​Kd,∇a​∇b​Kc=Rcbad​Kd.​
(17)
For an affine parameter λ on a geodesic, its tangent obeys Vb∇b​Va=0. Thus
dλd​(Ka​Va)=VbVa∇b​Ka​+Ka​Vb∇b​Va=0.
(18)
The second term vanishes by the geodesic equation; the first contracts a symmetric product VaVb with the antisymmetric derivative from the Killing equation. This establishes the geodesic conserved quantity from a Killing vector:
Ka​Va is constant along every affinely parametrized geodesic.​

In Cartesian coordinates on Euclidean space, the Levi-Civita connection and the Riemann curvature tensor vanish. The second covariant derivative of a Killing vector therefore gives
∂μ​∂ρ​Kν=0.
(20)
Every first partial derivative is constant on the connected space Rn. Integrating once more gives Kν=Aν+Mμ​νxμ, with constant coefficients. The Killing equation now becomes
0=∂μ​Kν​+∂ν​Kμ​=Mμν​+Mνμ​,Mμν​=Mμ​κδκν​.
(21)
Thus the coefficient is an antisymmetric matrix. Conversely, any such constant coefficients satisfy the Killing equation. The complete Euclidean Killing vector field is therefore
K=Aν∂xν∂​+Mμ​νxμ∂xν∂​,MT=−M.​
(22)
The n translation parameters and n(n−1)/2 rotation parameters give n(n+1)/2 independent Killing vector fields.

The index order in Mμ​νxμ fixes the rotation sign. With the entries specified in the paper, the integral curves of a vector field satisfy
dλdx1​=A1+x2,dλdx2​=A2−x1,dλdxj​=Aj(j≥3).
(23)
Shift to u=x1−A2, v=x2+A1. Then u˙=v, v˙=−u, so u2+v2 is constant. If x(0)=x0​, put u0​=x01​−A2 and v0​=x02​+A1. Solving this linear ordinary differential equation gives the full local flow:
x1(λ)x2(λ)xj(λ)​=A2+u0​cosλ+v0​sinλ,=−A1+v0​cosλ−u0​sinλ,=x0j​+Ajλ(j≥3).​​
(24)
The planar motion is a circle centred at (A2,−A1), traversed clockwise as λ increases. A nonzero vector (A3,…,An) produces a helical orbit of a Euclidean Killing vector; zero drift gives a circle. At zero radius the curves are straight lines in the remaining directions, or stationary points if that drift also vanishes. These formulas are defined for every real λ.

Use geometrized units G=c=1 and signature (−,+,+,+). The original PDF starts with the d'Alembert operator □hˉμν​=−16πTμν​; the local TeX incorrectly transcribes its derivative indices. The harmonic condition is the Lorenz gauge in linearized gravity.
Choose the retarded solution, excluding an incoming homogeneous wave. For a spatially localized source, the Linearized Einstein equations give
hˉμν​(t,x)=4∫∣x−y∣Tμν​(t−∣x−y∣,y)​d3y.
(25)
Let the source size be D and the characteristic angular frequency be ω. Assume the weak-field approximation, nonrelativistic source velocities, and D≪ω−1≪r for a radiative far-zone measurement. Then ∣x−y∣=r−n⋅y+O(D2/r). At leading order in D/r and ωD, one may replace the denominator by r and the retarded time throughout the source by t−r, obtaining
hˉij​(t,x)≃r4​∫Tij​(t−r,y)d3y.
(26)
To identify this integral, use stress-energy conservation ∂μ​Tμν=0 for a symmetric source tensor. Compact support or sufficient decay permits integration by parts with no boundary flux. Because T00=T00​ in this signature, the second mass moment tensor satisfies
I˙ij​I¨ij​​=−∫∂k​Tk0yi​yj​d3y=∫(Ti0yj​+Tj0yi​)d3y,=−∫(∂k​Tkiyj​+∂k​Tkjyi​)d3y=2∫Tijd3y.​
(27)
Spatial indices are raised with δij​, so Tij=Tij​. Substitution gives the retarded quadrupole field:
hˉij​(t,x)≃r2​I¨ij​(t−r).​
(28)
The assumptions are an isolated conserved source, retarded boundary conditions, weak gravity, a source small compared with the wavelength, and observation far from it. The physical radiative field follows by the transverse-traceless projector applied to this trace-reversed metric perturbation.

Put hab​=δgab​, h=gabhab​, and hab=gacgbdhcd​. Throughout this part use the Riemann curvature tensor convention printed in the paper. Varying gacgcb​=δab​ gives the inverse-metric variation. The determinant identity δlog∣detg∣=gabhab​ gives the volume form variation. Together these are
δgab=−hab,δ(dvolg​)=21​hdvolg​.​

For the connection, vary metric compatibility ∇a​gbc​=0:
∇a​hbc​=gdc​δΓdab​+gbd​δΓdac​.

Add the equations with derivatives a,b, subtract that with derivative c, and use symmetry of the lower indices of the Levi-Civita connection. This gives

δS=21​∫d4x−g​Tabδgab​.

Tabkin​=∇a​Φ∇b​Φ−21​gab​∇c​Φ∇cΦ.

δSξ​=−ξ∫−g​[(21​Rfgab−fRab)hab​−f□h+f∇a∇bhab​]d4x.

The Euler-Lagrange field equation obtained by varying the nonminimally coupled scalar field is
□Φ−2ξRΦ=0.​
(49)
One can check stress-energy conservation directly. The divergence of the kinetic contribution is (□Φ)∇b​Φ, because second derivatives of a scalar commute. With f=Φ2, the contracted Bianchi identity gives ∇aGab​=0, while the Ricci identity gives
□∇b​f=∇b​□f+Rb​c∇c​f.
(50)
Consequently
∇a[fGab​+gab​□f−∇a​∇b​f]​=Gab​∇af+∇b​□f−□∇b​f=(Gab​−Rab​)∇af=−21​R∇b​f.​

The general reason is the diffeomorphism Noether identity for a scalar field. Under the Lie derivative along a compactly supported vector field X, δgab​=2∇(a​Xb)​ and δΦ=Xa∇a​Φ. Invariance of the action under diffeomorphisms gives, after integration by parts,
