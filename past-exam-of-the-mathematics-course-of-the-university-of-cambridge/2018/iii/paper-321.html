a OurBigBook.com
The specific orbital energy released in a black-hole accretion flow reaching an inner radius rin​ is of order GM/rin​. For a circular Keplerian orbit, the binding energy is more precisely GM/(2rin​), with the other half of the potential-energy decrease retained as orbital kinetic energy. Multiplying by the mass accretion rate gives the accretion luminosity scale
Lacc​∼rin​GMM˙​.
(1)
For an inner radius of order the Schwarzschild radius, rin​∼2GM/c2, this becomes
Lacc​∼M˙c2.​
(2)
This is an energy-release scaling, not unit radiative efficiency of black-hole accretion. The actual inner orbit, stresses and advected energy determine the coefficient and the emitted luminosity; the innermost stable circular orbit of a Schwarzschild black hole lies outside its horizon.

The magnetic torque transfers angular momentum from the faster rotator to the slower one. When Ωm​>ΩK​(rm​), the neutron star supplies angular momentum to the inner accretion disk and possible propeller flow, so it tends to spin down. When the inner accretion disk rotates faster than the star, the magnetic connection and accreted angular momentum tend to spin it up.
For slowly varying mass accretion rate and magnetic dipole moment, these tendencies favour a magnetic spin equilibrium with the corotation radius near the magnetospheric truncation radius:
Ωeq​∼(rm3​GM​)1/2,Peq​∼(GM)5/7M˙3/72πϵ3/7μ6/7​.​
(10)
The precise balance depends on accreted and ejected angular momentum and on the extent of magnetic coupling across the accretion disk; corotation is a scaling estimate, not an exact zero-torque theorem.

Use M˙>0 for inward mass accretion rate, and Tm​>0 for magnetic torque adding angular momentum to the disk. Combining the continuity equation with conservation of angular momentum identifies the inward mass flux as (dh/dr)−1dG/dr. In a steady Keplerian accretion disk, this is the supplied constant M˙, so
drdG​=M˙drdh​,G(r)=Tm​+M˙(h−hin​),h=GMr​.
(11)
The boundary condition is G(rin​)=Tm​. Since dΩ/dr=−3Ω/(2r), the viscous torque in an accretion disk is G=3πνˉΣh. Hence the steady Keplerian accretion disk with an inner torque has
Σ(r)=3πνˉM˙​[1+(λ−1)(rrin​​)1/2],λ=M˙hin​Tm​​.​
(12)
In particular, Σ(rin​)=λM˙/(3πνˉ). For a physical steady solution with this boundary condition, λ≥0.
For 0≤λ≪1, the surface density of a disk rises from a small inner value toward M˙/(3πνˉ): this is the usual Keplerian accretion disk with an approximately zero-torque inner boundary condition. For λ≫1, the inner surface density of a disk is large and decreases approximately as r−1/2, giving a torque-dominated accretion disk. This approximation applies where r/rin​≪(λ−1)2; sufficiently far out, any fixed finite λ again approaches the same constant asymptote.
The constant net inward angular-momentum flux is
M˙h−G=M˙hin​(1−λ).
(13)
For λ>1, net angular momentum moves outward. The strong-torque profile is therefore decretion disk-like in its stress-dominated structure, but the specified mass flux remains inward: a genuine net decretion disk requires an outward mass flux, not merely large λ.
At rin​=rm​, the Maxwell stress tensor estimate gives
Tm​∼4πrm3​ϵμ2​,λ∼4πM˙GM​rm7/2​ϵμ2​.
(14)
Using the magnetospheric truncation radius scaling in part (b), all dimensional parameters cancel:
λ∼1up to geometric numerical factors.​
(15)
If the displayed 4π is retained and the scaling radius is assigned unit coefficient, the estimate is 1/(4π); retaining Σ≃2Hρ and exact free-fall speed throughout gives 2​/(4π). Those prefactors are not controlled by the scaling argument. In particular, it does not generate a parametrically large λ≫1.

The vertical acceleration in the thin disk is of order Ω2H. Balancing it against the characteristic pressure force gives
ρHP​∼Ω2H,cs​∼ΩH,
(23)
up to factors depending on the adiabatic exponent and the vertical profile. A displacement of size δz has a restoring acceleration of order Ω2δz, so δz¨∼−Ω2δz. Equivalently the sound-crossing time is H/cs​. Both estimates give the vertical dynamical timescale of a disk:
tdyn​∼cs​H​∼Ω−1.​
(24)
This is a mechanical response time, not an unconditional damping time. An inviscid stable layer can exhibit a vertical breathing mode of an astrophysical disk without settling; its adiabatic frequency satisfies ωH2​=(γ+1)Ω2. Moreover the profile in part (b) need not be convectively stable: its specific entropy satisfies
sgas​=cv​log(P/ργ)+constant=cv​[1−β(γ−1)]logT+constant.
(25)
For z>0, T decreases upward, so convective stability requires β>1/(γ−1), with equality neutrally stratified. Thus interpreting the timescale as re-establishment of a stable equilibrium presupposes suitable stability and damping; the stated equations alone do not guarantee either.

For slow cooling of an astrophysical disk, let tcool​≫Ω−1 and assume a vertically symmetric laminar column with no significant radial mass exchange. Its vertical velocity is of order H/tcool​, so the vertical material derivative of that velocity is of order H/tcool2​. Relative to gravity or pressure acceleration ∼Ω2H, the inertial correction is O[(Ωtcool​)−2].
The hydrostatic approximation therefore holds to leading order while the column slowly cools and contracts. Non-turbulent evolution removes the prescribed turbulent heating, but not the compressional work in the ideal gas energy equation:
∂z​P=−Ω2zρ,γ−11​(Dt​P−ργP​Dt​ρ)=−AT1+β,P=RρT.​
(26)
The continuity equation must still determine the slow vertical flow; Dt​ is not generally ∂t​ at fixed height. This is a quasi-static approximation for a well-prepared, mechanically stable column, with fast free oscillations or growing convection excluded from the assumed slow solution.

Let ζ=∇×u and ω=ζ+2Ωez​ be the relative vorticity and absolute vorticity. The nonlinear acceleration identity rewrites the momentum equation as
∂t​u−u×ω=−∇(ρ0​P​+21​∣u∣2−23​Ω2x2)−N2θex​.
(39)
Taking the curl removes the pressure, kinetic energy gradient and shearing-sheet tidal potential. Thus
∂t​ω−∇×(u×ω)=−N2∇θ×ex​.
(40)
Both ∇⋅u and ∇⋅ω vanish. The vorticity equation is consequently
∂t​ωj​+ui​∂i​ωj​−ωi​∂i​uj​=−N2(∇θ×ex​)j​.
(41)
For the absolute-vorticity flux tensor Tij​=ui​ωj​−ωi​uj​, use the flux convention (∇⋅T)j​=∂i​Tij​, with the first index denoting transport direction. This gives
∂t​ω+∇⋅T=−N2∇θ×ex​.​
(42)
The scalar θ is a buoyancy displacement variable, so dimensional consistency gives it units of length; the printed potential-temperature terminology presupposes this normalization. Its curl source directly forces only the y,z components of absolute vorticity. Hence radial vorticity conservation in a shearing sheet takes the local flux form
∂t​ωx​+∂i​(ui​ωx​−ωi​ux​)=0.​
(43)
Its volume integral is constant when the net boundary flux vanishes. However Dt​ωx​=ω⋅∇ux​ still permits vortex stretching; the radial component is not generally a materially conserved scalar in three dimensions. Using divergence on the second index of the stated tensor would reverse the transport sign, so the tensor convention matters.

For an axisymmetric vertical mode of a shearing sheet in this Boussinesq approximation, the perturbations have no horizontal gradients, so background-shear advection vanishes. The linear Coriolis acceleration and perturbation advection of the background shear give
sux′​−2Ωuy′​=−N2θ′,suy′​+2Ω​ux′​=0,suz′​=−ρ0​ik​P′,

(47)
Here β is the thermal diffusion rate of a Fourier mode, not the cooling exponent in Question 2. Since k=0, incompressibility forces uz′​=0, and the vertical momentum equation then forces P′=0 for this nonzero-wavenumber amplitude.
The three remaining amplitudes satisfy
​sΩ/2−1​−2Ωs0​N20s+β​​​ux′​uy′​θ′​​=0.
(48)
A nontrivial solution requires the determinant to vanish, giving the convective overstability dispersion relation
(s+β)(s2+Ω2)+N2s=0,ors3+βs2+(N2+Ω2)s+βΩ2=0.​

No division by s or s+β was needed, so the thermal energy mode of a shearing sheet is retained. The notation N2 denotes a signed buoyancy frequency squared and can be negative for an adverse stratification; it must not be restricted to the square of a real positive frequency.

For either epicyclic motion root, the implicit function theorem applied to the dispersion relation gives
