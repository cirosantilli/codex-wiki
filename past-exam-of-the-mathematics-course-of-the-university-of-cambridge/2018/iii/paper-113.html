The tensor product of sheaves is the sheafification of the presheaf
U⟼F(U)⊗OX​(U)​G(U),
(1)
with restriction maps induced by those of F, G, and the structure sheaf of a scheme. Sending the germ of f⊗g to fP​⊗gP​ gives a canonical map
(F⊗OX​​G)P​⟶FP​⊗OX,P​​GP​.
(2)
It is surjective because a finite sum of tensors of germs has representatives on one common neighborhood. It is injective because an equality in a tensor product of modules is witnessed by finitely many additive and balancing relations; all their germs, including the scalars in OX,P​, can be represented and their equalities made valid on a common smaller neighborhood. Equivalently, a filtered colimit of modules commutes with these tensor products. Since sheafification preserves the stalk of a sheaf, this proves the asserted isomorphism.
For the pullback of a sheaf of modules, first form the inverse image sheaf ϕ−1F: sheafify U↦colimV⊇ϕ(U)​F(V). The morphism of sheaves of rings ϕ−1OY​→OX​ then defines
ϕ∗F=OX​⊗ϕ−1OY​​ϕ−1F,(ϕ∗F)P​≅OX,P​⊗OY,ϕ(P)​​Fϕ(P)​.
(3)
Exactness of a sequence of sheaf of modules morphisms is checked on every stalk of a sheaf. Applying the right exactness of the tensor product of modules to the stalk sequence over OY,ϕ(P)​ gives the exact stalk sequence of the pullbacks. Thus ϕ∗ preserves sequences ending in zero, as required.
A quasi-coherent sheaf is a sheaf of modules locally of the form N for a module N over the coordinate ring of an affine neighborhood. On an affine variety V with ring B, this associated sheaf is characterized on principal opens by N(D(b))=Nb​, using localization of a module. Suppose F∣V​≅N and U⊆ϕ−1V is affine, with ring C. The induced algebra map B→C gives a canonical map
C⊗B​N​⟶(ϕ∗F)∣U​.
(4)
At a point P∈U, both sides identify with OX,P​⊗B​N: on the right use Fϕ(P)​≅OY,ϕ(P)​⊗B​N and associativity of the tensor product of modules. The map is therefore an isomorphism on every stalk of a sheaf, hence an isomorphism of sheaves. Such U cover X, proving that the pullback is a quasi-coherent sheaf.
Finally, on an affine open V⊆Y with ring B, the presheaf defining MY​ has, on D(b)⊆V, the value
M⊗R​Bb​≅(M⊗R​B)b​.
(5)
Consequently MY​∣V​≅M⊗R​B​, so MY​ is a quasi-coherent sheaf. The algebra map for MX​ is the composite R→Γ(Y,OY​)→Γ(X,OX​). Restriction of functions and the tensor-product construction give a canonical sheaf morphism ϕ∗MY​→MX​. At P, it is the associativity isomorphism
OX,P​⊗OY,ϕ(P)​​(M⊗R​OY,ϕ(P)​)≅M⊗R​OX,P​.
(6)
It is therefore a sheaf of modules isomorphism. In particular,
(F⊗G)P​≅FP​⊗OX,P​​GP​,MX​≅ϕ∗MY​.​

For a product in a category of prevarieties, the projections pX​,pY​ must induce a bijection
Homk​(T,X×Y)⟶Homk​(T,X)×Homk​(T,Y),h⟼(pX​h,pY​h)
(8)
for every prevariety T. If Z and Z′ both have this property, their projection pairs determine morphisms Z→Z′ and Z′→Z. Each composite has the same projections as the identity, so uniqueness in the universal property makes both composites identities. This gives the unique isomorphism respecting the projections.
Here the classical convention is that a prevariety is locally an affine variety, with a finite affine cover, and is irreducible; an algebraic variety is a separated prevariety, meaning that its diagonal morphism has closed image. The same separation argument works in the convention that also permits reducible varieties. For separated X,Y, under the rearrangement (X×Y)2≅X2×Y2, the diagonal is
ΔX×Y​=ΔX​×ΔY​=πX2−1​(ΔX​)∩πY2−1​(ΔY​).
(9)
It is closed, proving separation. For completeness, irreducibility in the classical convention follows from the affine calculation below: the products of affine charts are irreducible, and their pairwise intersections are nonempty since nonempty open subsets of each irreducible factor meet. An open cover by irreducible open sets with pairwise nonempty intersections has irreducible union. Thus the product is an algebraic variety.
To establish the coordinate ring of a product of affine varieties, write A=k[X]=k[x1​,…,xr​]/J and B=k[Y]=k[y1​,…,ys​]/L. The algebra
A⊗k​B≅k[x1​,…,xr​,y1​,…,ys​]/(J,L)
(10)
has zero set X×Y. To see directly that (J,L) is prime when X,Y are irreducible, take nonzero F,G∈A⊗k​B. Express F=∑i​ai​⊗bi​ with the bi​ linearly independent over k. The set of x∈X for which Fx​=∑i​ai​(x)bi​ is nonzero is a nonempty open set: it is the union of the nonvanishing loci of the ai​, and at least one ai​ is nonzero. The Hilbert Nullstellensatz guarantees that such a function cannot vanish at every point. The analogous set for G is also nonempty; their intersection contains a point x by irreducibility. Since B is an integral domain, Fx​Gx​=0, whence FG=0. Thus A⊗k​B is an integral domain, and the Hilbert Nullstellensatz identifies (J,L) with the defining ideal of its zero set. The resulting affine variety has the universal property: algebra maps from A⊗k​B to Γ(T,OT​) correspond to pairs of algebra maps from A,B, and these correspond to morphisms into X,Y. This dictionary holds for arbitrary T, since coordinate functions give the morphisms on affine charts and agree on overlaps. Uniqueness of the product now proves
k[X×Y]≅k[X]⊗k​k[Y].​
(11)
For the final part put T=A⊗k​A and μ(x⊗y)=xy. Restriction to the diagonal morphism is μ, so I=kerμ. Write δ(a)=1⊗a−a⊗1. The elements δ(a) generate I: quotienting by them identifies the two copies of A and gives precisely the multiplication quotient T/I≅A. The map D(a)=[δ(a)] is k-linear, vanishes on k, and takes values in I/I2. Since
δ(ab)=(1⊗a)δ(b)+(b⊗1)δ(a),1⊗a≡a⊗1(modI),
(12)
it obeys D(ab)=aD(b)+bD(a) for the stated first-factor action. Hence it is a derivation of an algebra. The universal property of the module of Kähler differentials supplies an A-linear map θ:ΩA/k1​→I/I2 with θ(da)=D(a).
Define the k-linear map q:T→ΩA/k1​ by q(x⊗y)=xdy. It is A-linear for the first-factor action and, by the Leibniz rule, satisfies
q(rs)=μ(r)q(s)+μ(s)q(r)(r,s∈T).
(13)
Therefore q(I2)=0 and restriction induces ψ:I/I2→ΩA/k1​. We have ψθ(da)=da, so ψθ is the identity because the da generate the module of Kähler differentials. Conversely, if z=∑i​xi​⊗yi​∈I, then ∑i​xi​yi​=0 and
[z]=∑i​xi​[1⊗yi​−yi​⊗1]=θ(∑i​xi​dyi​)=θψ([z]).
(14)
Thus the conormal module of the diagonal gives
ΩA/k1​ ∼ ​I/I2,da⟼[1⊗a−a⊗1].​
(15)

First consider the preliminary cohomological facts. The exam's term flabby means flasque sheaf: every restriction map is surjective. For open sets V⊆U, lift a section of F3​(V) to F2​(V) using the given surjectivity when F1​ is flasque. Extend that lift to U using the flasque sheaf F2​, then project to F3​(U). This proves that F3​ is a flasque sheaf.
One construction of a flasque resolution starts with the sheaf C0(F)(U)=∏P∈U​FP​, with pointwise restriction. It is a sheaf of abelian groups with surjective restrictions, and sending a section to all its germs embeds F in it. Repeating on the cokernels constructs an exact sequence
0⟶F⟶I0⟶I1⟶⋯
(16)
with each Ij flasque. Define sheaf cohomology by the cohomology of the cochain complex of global sections of such a resolution; the groups are independent of the chosen flasque resolution. If F is itself flasque, the quotient result just proved makes every successive cokernel flasque. Applying the assumed surjectivity of sections to each successive short exact sequence of sheaves proves exactness of the global-section complex in positive degrees. Thus Hi(X,F)=0 for i>0.
Now let K=k(X) be the function field of the smooth algebraic curve. Its divisor class group is
Cl(X)=Div(X)/Prin(X),Div(X)=⨁P closed​ZP,div(f)=∑P​νP​(f)P(f∈K∗).
(17)
Here νP​ is the discrete valuation of the discrete valuation ring OX,P​, and Prin(X) consists of the principal divisor elements div(f). These sums have finite support: on a finite affine cover, represent f as a fraction of regular functions; each nonzero numerator or denominator has only finitely many zeros on a curve, since a proper closed subset of a Noetherian one-dimensional irreducible space is finite.
For a divisor on an algebraic curve D=∑P​nP​P, define the line bundle associated to a divisor by
OX​(D)(U)={f∈K:νP​(f)+nP​≥0 for all closed P∈U}∪{0}.
(18)
In this expression the valuation condition applies to nonzero f. If tP​ is a uniformizer, the stalk is tP−nP​​OX,P​. Shrinking around P removes all other zeros and poles of tP​ and all other points in the support of D, so this also gives an actual local generator. Thus OX​(D) is a locally free sheaf of rank one. Multiplication gives an isomorphism
OX​(D)⊗OX​​OX​(E)≅OX​(D+E)
(19)
on every stalk. If D=div(g), then OX​(D)=g−1OX​, so the construction factors through a homomorphism Cl(X)→Pic(X).
It is injective: if OX​(D) is trivial, an isomorphism from OX​ supplies a nonzero global rational generator h. At each closed point, h generates tP−nP​​OX,P​, giving νP​(h)=−nP​, hence D=−div(h).
It is surjective: take a line bundle L and a nonzero rational section s, obtained by choosing a frame on any nonempty trivializing open set. If ei​ is a local frame, write s=fi​ei​ with fi​∈K∗. Since changes of frame are units, νP​(fi​) is independent of the frame near P. A finite trivializing cover shows that these numbers have finite support. Set D=∑P​νP​(fi​)P. The map f↦fs gives OX​(D)≅L, because at P the condition νP​(f)+νP​(fi​)≥0 is exactly regularity of fs. Replacing s by gs changes D by div(g), so this construction is well defined on classes. This proves the divisor class group and Picard group of a smooth curve identification
Cl(X)≅Pic(X),[D]⟼[OX​(D)].​
(20)

Put Q=KX∗​/OX∗​, the quotient in sheaves of abelian groups, where KX∗​ is the sheaf of nonzero rational functions on an irreducible variety and OX∗​ is the sheaf of units of the structure sheaf. At a closed point, the discrete valuation induces an isomorphism
QP​=K∗/OX,P∗​ ∼ ​Z,[f]⟼νP​(f).
(21)
The kernel consists precisely of the units of the discrete valuation ring, and surjectivity follows by taking powers of a uniformizer.
A global section of Q is locally represented by nonzero rational functions. Since X has a finite affine cover and is Noetherian, it is quasi-compact, so finitely many such representatives suffice. Each has only finitely many zeros and poles; consequently the valuations of the section define a finite divisor on an algebraic curve. A section with zero valuation at every closed point is zero in every stalk and therefore zero. This gives an injective map
Γ(X,Q)⟶Div(X).
(22)
It is surjective as well. For D=∑P​nP​P, take a rational uniformizer tP​ at each point of its finite support. On a sufficiently small neighborhood of P, the function tPnP​​ has the prescribed divisor there: remove the other points in its zero and pole set and the other support points of D. On the complement of the support use the rational function 1. On overlaps these representatives have the same valuations at every point, so their ratios are units everywhere on the overlap, and their classes in Q agree. The sheaf gluing axiom now supplies a global section. Thus
Γ(X,Q)≅Div(X).
(23)
Since X is irreducible, Γ(X,KX∗​)=K∗. Under this identification the map to Γ(X,Q) is f↦div(f). Its image is exactly the group of principal divisor elements, and hence
coker(H0(X,KX∗​)→H0(X,Q))≅Cl(X).​
(24)

The sheaf of nonzero rational functions on an irreducible variety KX∗​ is a flasque sheaf. Indeed, its sections on any nonempty open subset are K∗: irreducibility makes nonempty open subsets connected and makes any two of them meet, so locally constant rational-function values must agree. Restrictions between such opens are identities; restriction to the empty set is also surjective. The preliminary argument therefore gives
Hi(X,KX∗​)=0(i>0).
(25)
Apply the long exact sequence in sheaf cohomology to the short exact sequence of sheaves of multiplicative abelian groups
1⟶OX∗​⟶KX∗​⟶Q⟶1.
(26)
Its relevant part is
K∗⟶Γ(X,Q)⟶H1(X,OX∗​)⟶H1(X,KX∗​)=0.
(27)
Part (b) identifies the first map with the principal divisor map K∗→Div(X). Exactness therefore identifies its cokernel with H1(X,OX∗​). Combining this with part (a) gives
Cl(X)≅Pic(X)≅H1(X,OX∗​).​
(28)
The connecting map sends a divisor to the class of the unit ratios of its local rational representatives; these are precisely the transition data of the associated line bundle, up to the usual choice of inverse transition convention.

The resolution principle for sheaf cohomology states that an exact sequence
0⟶F⟶A0⟶A1⟶⋯
(29)
with Hq(X,Aj)=0 for every q>0 computes Hi(X,F) as the cohomology of Γ(X,A∙). In particular one may use a flasque resolution.
An affine morphism ϕ:X→Y is one for which the inverse image of every affine open subset of Y is affine. We use vanishing of quasi-coherent cohomology on an affine scheme: a quasi-coherent sheaf has zero higher sheaf cohomology on an affine variety. Take a flasque resolution F→I∙. Each direct image sheaf ϕ∗​Ij is flasque, because restriction on V⊆U⊆Y is restriction on ϕ−1V⊆ϕ−1U. On an affine open U⊆Y, the augmented complex of sections is
0⟶Γ(ϕ−1U,F)⟶Γ(ϕ−1U,I0)⟶Γ(ϕ−1U,I1)⟶⋯.
(30)
Its positive-degree cohomology vanishes by the affine theorem, and its degree-zero kernel is Γ(ϕ−1U,F). Checking exactness on this affine basis shows that ϕ∗​I∙ is a flasque resolution of ϕ∗​F. The global-section complexes are identical, proving cohomology under an affine morphism:
Hi(Y,ϕ∗​F)≅Hi(X,F)(i≥0).​
(31)
For projective space, put S=k[x0​,…,xn​] with its usual grading and Ui​={xi​=0}. Construct the twisting sheaf on projective space by setting its sections on Ui​ to be the degree-m part (Sxi​​)m​, viewed as a module over (Sxi​​)0​. It is free of rank one with generator xim​, even when m is negative. On overlaps the generators differ by the unit (xi​/xj​)m, so these modules glue to a line bundle O(m).
Assume first n≥1. These local modules all embed in the graded fraction field, and agreement on overlaps identifies global sections with ⋂i​(Sxi​​)m​. Since S is a unique factorization domain and at least two distinct variables occur, a reduced fraction belonging to every Sxi​​ has no nonconstant denominator. Thus the intersection is Sm​. Counting monomials by stars and bars gives
H0(Pn,O(m))=Sm​,hn0​(m)={(nm+n​),0,​m≥0,m<0.​
(32)
Here hni​(m)=dimk​Hi(Pn,O(m)). The printed formula needs the convention that a counting binomial coefficient is zero when its upper integer is smaller than its nonnegative lower integer; the generalized polynomial convention for negative upper integers would give incorrect dimensions. For n=0, every twist on the single point P0 is trivial, so h00​(m)=1 for every m and its higher cohomology is zero. This case supplies the base of dimension induction; the displayed top-cohomology formula below is for n≥1.
The standard n+1 affine opens have affine finite intersections. The acyclic cover theorem and affine vanishing identify sheaf cohomology with a Čech cochain complex ending in degree n. In particular Hi(Pn,O(m))=0 for i>n.
Let H={xn​=0}≅Pn−1, with inclusion i. Multiplication by xn​ gives the hyperplane exact sequence for twisting sheaves
0⟶OPn​(m−1)xn​​OPn​(m)⟶i∗​OH​(m)⟶0.
(33)
Exactness is checked in the local trivializations, where a hyperplane equation is a non-zero-divisor and the quotient is its restriction to H. Since a closed immersion is an affine morphism, the first part identifies the cohomology of i∗​OH​(m) with that on H.
For n=1, the long exact sequence in sheaf cohomology and H≅P0 give
h11​(m−1)−h11​(m)=1−(h10​(m)−h10​(m−1))={0,1,​m≥0,m<0.​
(34)
Starting from the given vanishing for sufficiently large m and descending gives h11​(m)=0 for m≥−1 and h11​(m)=−m−1 for m≤−2.
Now let n≥2, and assume the formulas in dimension n−1. Restriction
H0(Pn,O(m))⟶H0(H,OH​(m))
(35)
is surjective for every m: for m≥0 it is polynomial restriction, and for m<0 both groups are zero since dimH≥1. The long exact sequence in sheaf cohomology therefore makes H1(Pn,O(m−1))→H1(Pn,O(m)) injective. For 2≤j<n, dimension induction gives Hj−1(H,OH​(m))=0, so the same sequence makes
Hj(Pn,O(m−1))⟶Hj(Pn,O(m))
(36)
injective. Composing these injections up to a sufficiently large twist, where the target is zero by the given hypothesis, proves Hj(Pn,O(m))=0 for every m and 0<j<n.
The remaining part of the long exact sequence in sheaf cohomology is consequently
0⟶Hn−1(H,OH​(m))⟶Hn(Pn,O(m−1))⟶Hn(Pn,O(m))⟶0,
(37)
where the last zero uses the affine-cover dimension bound on H. Hence hnn​(m−1)=hnn​(m)+hn−1n−1​(m). Using dimension induction and eventual vanishing, downward summation yields
hnn​(m)=∑j=m+1−n​(n−1−j−1​)=(n−m−1​)(m≤−n−1),
(38)
by telescoping Pascal's identity; for m≥−n it is zero. Thus, without invoking Serre duality in the induction,
Hj(Pn,O(m))=0 (j>0, j=n),hnn​(m)={(n−m−1​),0,​m≤−n−1,m≥−n.​​
(39)
Finally, the canonical bundle of projective space is ωPn​≅O(−n−1), obtained by taking determinants in the dual Euler sequence. Serre duality therefore predicts
Hj(Pn,O(m))≅Hn−j(Pn,O(−m−n−1))∗.
(40)
It pairs the two computed extreme-degree dimensions and pairs zero intermediate groups with zero intermediate groups, exactly as required.
