The weak-star topology σ(X∗,X) is generated by the seminorms px​(g)=∣g(x)∣, x∈X. Thus every evaluation is continuous, so fn​w∗​f implies fn​(x)→f(x) for each x. Conversely, a basic neighborhood of f imposes finitely many conditions ∣(g−f)(xj​)∣<ε. Pointwise convergence eventually satisfies all of them simultaneously. Hence
fn​w∗​f⟺fn​(x)⟶f(x) for every x∈X.​
(1)
The Uniform boundedness principle, applied on the Banach space X, gives M=supn​∥fn​∥<∞. If xn​→x in the norm topology, then
∣fn​(xn​)−f(x)∣≤M∥xn​−x∥+∣fn​(x)−f(x)∣⟶0.
(2)
Therefore fn​(xn​)→f(x)​. Completeness of X is used in this uniform boundedness step.
If C⊆X∗ is norm compact, the identity map from (C,∥⋅∥) to (C,w∗) is a continuous bijection. The target is Hausdorff, since distinct functionals differ at some x∈X. The compact-to-Hausdorff continuous bijection theorem gives
the norm and weak-star topologies coincide on C.​
(3)
The Banach-Alaoglu theorem states that the closed dual unit ball BX∗​ is compact in the weak-star topology; in fact X need only be a normed vector space. To prove it, work over F=R or C and form
Q=∏x∈X​Dx​,Dx​={z∈F:∣z∣≤∥x∥}.
(4)
Each Dx​ is compact, so Q is compact by the Tychonoff theorem. Embed BX∗​ by g↦(g(x))x∈X​. Its image consists exactly of those families (zx​) satisfying
zx+y​=zx​+zy​,zαx​=αzx​(x,y∈X, α∈F).
(5)
These are closed conditions in the product topology. Conversely, any family satisfying them defines a linear functional bounded by ∣zx​∣≤∥x∥, hence an element of BX∗​. The image is therefore closed and compact. The induced product topology is precisely the weak-star topology, proving the theorem. This is a compactness proof for arbitrary nets, with no separability assumption.
For the dual map of bounded linear operator T∗:Y∗→X∗, each x∈X satisfies
(T∗g)(x)=g(Tx).
(6)
Evaluation at the fixed vector Tx is weak-star continuous, so T∗ is weak-star-to-weak-star continuous​.
Suppose T is a compact operator. By the allowed Schauder theorem for compact operators, T∗ is compact. If S⊆Y∗ is bounded, the norm closure C=T∗(S)​ is norm compact. We already proved that its norm and weak-star topologies coincide. The weak-star continuous map T∗∣S​ into C is consequently norm continuous. Conversely, if T∗ is weak-star-to-norm continuous on bounded subsets, its restriction to BY∗​ maps a weak-star compact set, by Banach-Alaoglu theorem, to a norm compact set. Thus T∗ is a compact operator, and the Schauder theorem for compact operators gives
T compact⟺T∗∣S​:(S,w∗)→(X∗,∥⋅∥) is continuous for every bounded S⊆Y∗.​
