The Beurling–Gelfand spectral radius formula states that for every element a of a complex Banach algebra,
r(a)=λ∈σ(a)max​∣λ∣=n→∞lim​∥an∥1/n=n≥1inf​∥an∥1/n.​
(50)
For a Hermitian element of a C-star algebra a=a∗, the C-star identity gives ∥a2∥=∥a∥2. Iterating,
∥a2m∥=∥a∥2m.
(51)
The subsequence n=2m in the spectral-radius limit therefore proves r(a)=∥a∥​.
Let θ:A→B be a unital C-star homomorphism. If a−λ1 is invertible in A, its inverse is sent to an inverse of θ(a)−λ1 in B. Thus σB​(θ(a))⊆σA​(a) without assuming continuity of θ. For Hermitian a, θ(a) is also Hermitian, so
∥θ(a)∥=rB​(θ(a))≤rA​(a)=∥a∥.
(52)
For arbitrary x∈A, apply this inequality to x∗x and the C-star identity in both algebras:
∥θ(x)∥2=∥θ(x)∗θ(x)∥=∥θ(x∗x)∥≤∥x∗x∥=∥x∥2.
(53)
Hence ∥θ(x)∥≤∥x∥​ for every x.
Now assume θ is injective. For a fixed x, it is enough to prove norm preservation on C=C∗(1,x∗x), a commutative unital C-star algebra: the preceding square-norm identity will then recover the norm of x. By the Commutative Gelfand--Naimark theorem, identify C with C(K). Let D=θ(C)​⊆B. It is also a commutative unital C-star algebra, and its character space ΦD​ is compact. Each χ∈ΦD​ restricts along θ to a character of C(K), hence to evaluation at a point q(χ)∈K. The map q:ΦD​→K is continuous, so L=q(ΦD​) is closed.
If L=K, the Urysohn lemma provides a nonzero f∈C(K) vanishing on L. Then χ(θ(f))=f(q(χ))=0 for every χ∈ΦD​. Faithfulness of the Gelfand transform in D gives θ(f)=0, contradicting injectivity. Thus q is onto. The isometric Gelfand transform of D now gives
∥θ(f)∥=supχ∈ΦD​​∣χ(θ(f))∣=supt∈K​∣f(t)∣=∥f∥(f∈C).
(54)
Finally,
∥θ(x)∥2=∥θ(x∗x)∥=∥x∗x∥=∥x∥2,∥θ(x)∥=∥x∥.​
(55)
This proves that an injective C-star homomorphism is isometric without assuming its image is closed before norm preservation has been established.
