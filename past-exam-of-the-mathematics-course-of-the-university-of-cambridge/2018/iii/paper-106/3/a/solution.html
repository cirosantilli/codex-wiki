The Hahn-Banach separation theorem for two convex sets states that disjoint nonempty convex sets A,B in a real locally convex space, with A open, admit a nonzero continuous linear functional ℓ and α∈R such that
ℓ(a)<α≤ℓ(b)(a∈A, b∈B).​
(20)
There need not be a positive uniform gap between the two sets.
For a proof, set C=A−B. It is open and convex, and 0∈/C. Choose c0​∈C, let V=C−c0​, and let p be the Minkowski functional of V. Because V is open, convex and contains zero, p is a finite sublinear functional, and V={x:p(x)<1}. Put z=−c0​. Since z∈/V, p(z)≥1. On Rz define ℓ0​(tz)=tp(z). For t≥0 this equals p(tz); for t<0 it is nonpositive and hence at most p(tz). The Hahn-Banach theorem extends it to a linear ℓ with ℓ≤p. Both p(x) and p(−x) are bounded near zero, so this domination makes ℓ continuous; it is nonzero because ℓ(z)=p(z)≥1. For c∈C,
ℓ(c)=ℓ(c−c0​)−p(z)≤p(c−c0​)−p(z)<1−p(z)≤0.
(21)
Thus ℓ(a)<ℓ(b) for every a∈A,b∈B. Define α=supa∈A​ℓ(a), which is finite because any fixed b∈B bounds it above. Openness of A and nonzeroness of ℓ ensure ℓ(a)<α for every a∈A, and α≤ℓ(b) for every b∈B. This proves the stated separation.
