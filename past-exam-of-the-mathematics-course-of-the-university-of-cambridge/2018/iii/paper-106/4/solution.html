The Mazur theorem states that the weak closure and norm closure of a convex set C in a normed vector space coincide:
Cw=C∥⋅∥.​
(33)
Norm closure is contained in weak closure because the weak topology is coarser. Conversely, if x∈/C∥⋅∥, the Hahn-Banach separation theorem provides a continuous real linear functional strictly separating x from that closed convex set. In a complex space this is the real part of a continuous complex linear functional. A weak neighborhood of x then misses C, so x∈/Cw. This proves the equality. It also gives the usual Mazur lemma: if xn​⇀x, then x lies in the norm closure of the convex hull of each tail, so one can choose tail convex combinations yn​ with ∥yn​−x∥<1/n.
Now let K be a weakly compact set in a normed space X. Each φ∈X∗ is bounded on K because it is weakly continuous. The family {Jx:x∈K} in B(X∗,F), where J is the canonical embedding into the bidual, is therefore pointwise bounded. The space X∗ is Banach even if X is not. Apply the Uniform boundedness principle and use ∥Jx∥=∥x∥ to obtain
x∈Ksup​∥x∥<∞.​
(34)
This proves that a weakly compact set is norm bounded without assuming completeness of the original space.
For the real-valued dual and integral formulas that follow, take X to be real, as in the PDF. In a complex space the norming formula uses real parts, and the integral identities use complex-valued functionals instead.
If the separable Banach space X is nonzero, choose a norm-dense sequence (un​) in its unit sphere. By the Hahn-Banach theorem, choose φn​∈X∗ with ∥φn​∥=1 and φn​(un​)=1. For any unit vector u, arbitrarily close un​ satisfy
1−∥u−un​∥≤φn​(u)≤1.
(35)
Scaling gives the countable norming family identity
∥x∥=nsup​φn​(x)(x∈X).​
(36)
For X={0} use the constant sequence of zero functionals. If f:Ω→X is norm-Borel measurable, every φn​∘f is measurable, so its countable supremum ∥f(⋅)∥ is measurable. Equivalently, this also follows directly from continuity of the norm.
For any φ∈X∗, continuity makes φ∘f measurable, and
∣φ(f(ω))∣≤∥φ∥∥f(ω)∥.
(37)
Thus the assumed integrability of the norm implies scalar integrability, and
Tf​(φ)=∫Ω​φ(f(ω))dμ(ω),∣Tf​(φ)∣≤∥φ∥∫Ω​∥f∥dμ
(38)
defines a bounded linear functional on X∗. Use the granted weak-star continuity of Tf​. By the continuous dual of a weak-star topology, Tf​ is evaluation at a vector of X. Indeed, continuity gives finitely many x1​,…,xm​ and ε>0 such that ∣Tf​(φ)∣<1 whenever ∣φ(xj​)∣<ε for all j. Scaling shows that Tf​ vanishes on the common kernel of these evaluations. It therefore factors through their finite-dimensional coordinate map, so Tf​(φ)=∑j​aj​φ(xj​)=φ(∑j​aj​xj​). The Hahn-Banach theorem makes this representing vector unique. Hence
v=∫Ω​fdμ∈X,φ(v)=∫Ω​φ∘fdμ(φ∈X∗),∥v∥≤∫Ω​∥f∥dμ.​
(39)
In this separable setting the vector is the Bochner integral.
Return to a weakly compact set K⊆X and its inclusion f:K→X. For each fixed a∈X, the identity ∥x−a∥=supn​φn​(x−a) makes x↦∥x−a∥ weakly Borel measurable. Norm balls are consequently weakly Borel measurable. Separability gives a countable base of such balls, so every norm-open set is weakly Borel measurable. This proves measurability of f, and establishes the equality of the weak and norm Borel sigma-algebras in a separable Banach space.
Put M=supx∈K​∥x∥<∞. For every finite signed Borel measure μ on K,
∫K​∥f(x)∥d∣μ∣(x)≤M∣μ∣(K)<∞.​
(40)
For positive measures this is the integral in the question. For signed measures, the correct integrability condition uses the variation measure; define the integral by taking the difference of the positive and negative integrals. The printed Ω in this clause should be K, the domain of the inclusion.
The Riesz-Markov-Kakutani representation theorem now defines the bounded linear map
T:C(K)∗→X,T(μ)=∫K​xdμ(x),∥T(μ)∥≤M∥μ∥TV​.
(41)
For each φ∈X∗ the restriction φ∣K​ is in C(K), and
φ(T(μ))=μ(φ∣K​).
(42)
The right side is weak-star continuous in μ. The defining property of the weak topology therefore proves that T is weak-star-to-weak continuous, for arbitrary nets. For a Dirac measure, T(δx​)=x​.
If K=∅, let P(K) be its regular probability measures. This is a weak-star closed subset of BC(K)∗​: its conditions are μ(1)=1 and μ(g)≥0 for every nonnegative g∈C(K). It is compact by Banach-Alaoglu theorem. Thus T(P(K)) is weakly compact and convex, and contains K because it contains all T(δx​). It is weakly closed, hence norm closed, so it contains C=convK∥⋅∥. By Mazur theorem, C is weakly closed. Therefore it is a closed subset of the weakly compact set T(P(K)), proving
convK∥⋅∥ is weakly compact.​
(43)
The empty case is immediate. In fact T(P(K))=C: a barycenter of a measure on a Banach space outside C would be strictly separated by a functional φ, contradicting φ(T(μ))=∫K​φdμ≤supK​φ.
