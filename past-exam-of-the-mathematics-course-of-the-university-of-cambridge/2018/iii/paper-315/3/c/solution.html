Use the standard local chemical quench level approximation: carbon monoxide-rich gas from the hot deep region is transported upward, and conversion becomes slower as the gas enters the cooler layers. Assume that neither a faster loss process nor strong compositional fractionation removes carbon monoxide above the quench level. For an effective mixing length L, the eddy mixing time is τmix​≃L2/Kzz​, so outrunning conversion near 1bar requires
Kzz​≳105sL2​​.
(22)
The usual order-of-magnitude choice is L≃hb​, the local atmospheric scale height at Tb​=1400K. Equal planetary mass and radius give the same gravity as Jupiter; with the same mean molecular weight, the atmospheric scale height scales linearly with temperature. To use the supplied 30km reference, additionally adopt a representative Jovian reference temperature TJ​=150K. Then
hb​≃30km1501400​≃280km,Kzz​≳8×105m2s−1≃8×109cm2s−1​.
(23)
Using kB​T/(μmH​g) directly with μ≃2.3 and Jovian g≃25ms−2 gives hb​≃200km and Kzz​≳4×105m2s−1, the same order of magnitude. The supplied Jovian height is approximate and does not specify its reference temperature. Inserting 30km unchanged for the hot gas would instead give 9×103m2s−1 and neglect this temperature scaling.
The pressure separation is relevant to a stronger, whole-column transport estimate. With constant gravity and mean molecular weight, the profile gives
Δz​=∫10−3bar1bar​h(P)dlnP=150K30km​[21400+500​ln100+500ln10]≃1.1×103km.​
(24)
If one additionally requires diffusion through the entire column within 105s, a sufficient conservative condition is Kzz​≳Δz2/τchem​∼107m2s−1. It is not a necessary local quench condition: the chemical relaxation time is expected to become much longer in the cooler gas, so a longer total transit time can still preserve carbon monoxide.
The usual quench estimate is Kzz​ of order 106m2s−1 under the stated scale-height assumptions. A unique bound for survival to 10−3bar cannot be inferred from one reaction time without assumptions about its variation, the mixing length and upper-atmospheric losses.
