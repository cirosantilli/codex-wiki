No choice of the noise constant repairs this example. Also s=1 must be excluded: A=(1,1) has δ1​=0, but its two unit-coordinate vectors have identical measurements and both minimize basis pursuit.
The corrected conclusion, for s≥2, is
∥x^−x0​∥2​≤C1​η+C2​s​σs​(x0​)​,​
(45)
with both constants depending on the restricted isometry constant. The order-s threshold itself is valid for s≥2; it need not be replaced by an order-2s threshold. A precise sharp robust recovery theorem gives, for δ=δs​<1/3, the admissible constants
C1​=1−3δ22(1+δ)​​,C2​=1−3δ22​(2δ+(1−3δ)δ​)+2(1−3δ)​.

This is the standard stable recovery result, invoked here explicitly; its order restriction and constants are given in arxiv.org/pdf/1302.1236, Theorem 3.3.
For completeness, the mechanism behind a robust null space property proof is as follows. Put h=x^−x0​, let S index the largest s entries of x0​, and write σ=∥x0,Sc​∥1​. Feasibility and minimality give the tube and cone inequalities
∥Ah∥2​≤2η,∥hSc​∥1​≤∥hS​∥1​+2σ.
(47)
If A has the robust null space property with 0≤ρ<1 and τ>0, namely ∥hT​∥2​≤ρ∥hTc​∥1​/s​+τ∥Ah∥2​ for every ∣T∣≤s, then
∥h∥1​≤1−ρ2(1+ρ)​σ+1−ρ2τ​s​∥Ah∥2​.
(48)
Apply that property again to the largest s entries T of h. Sorted coefficients satisfy ∥hTc​∥2​≤∥h∥1​/s​, because each tail entry is at most ∥h∥1​/s. Therefore
∥h∥2​≤1−ρ2(1+ρ)2​s​σ​+1−ρτ(3+ρ)​∥Ah∥2​.
