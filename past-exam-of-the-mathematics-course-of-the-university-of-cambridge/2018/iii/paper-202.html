Let P be the predictable sigma-algebra on Ω×[0,∞), generated by A×{0} with A∈F0​ and A×(s,u] with A∈Fs​. A stochastic process H is previsible precisely when (ω,t)↦Ht​(ω) is P-measurable.
Every left-continuous adapted process is previsible. Indeed, on intervals (k2−n,(k+1)2−n], put Ht(n)​=Hk2−n​, and put H0(n)​=H0​. Each approximation is a predictable process, since its coefficient is Fk2−n​-measurable; left continuity gives Ht(n)​→Ht​ for every t, so measurability passes to the limit.
An adapted càdlàg process need not be previsible. For a concrete example, let ξ take values 0,1 with equal probabilities, let Ft​ be trivial for t<1 and Ft​=σ(ξ) for t≥1, and set Ht​=ξ1[1,∞)​(t). This is an adapted càdlàg process, but H1​=ξ is not F1−​-measurable, where F1−​=σ(⋃s<1​Fs​). Sections at time 1 of the generators of P all belong to F1−​, so every predictable process has an F1−​-measurable value there; this proves the counterexample directly.

Use the standard convention that the finite-variation process X has càdlàg paths and that the stochastic integral starts from zero. Write V(X) for its total-variation process. The necessary admissibility condition is ∫(0,t]​∣Hs​∣dVs​(X)<∞ almost surely for each finite t; it holds, for example, for locally bounded H. Define the pathwise Lebesgue-Stieltjes integral
(H⋅X)t​=∫(0,t]​Hs​dXs​,(H⋅X)0​=0.​
(1)
An atom at s>0 contributes Hs​ΔXs​, so this convention also fixes the treatment of jumps.
Fix t. For a generator Hs​=1A​1(a,b]​(s) of the predictable sigma-algebra, with A∈Fa​, the Lebesgue-Stieltjes integral is
1A​(Xb∧t​−Xa∧t​).
(2)
It vanishes when t≤a and is Ft​-measurable otherwise. The total-variation process is adapted: for a càdlàg path its value at t is the supremum over a countable collection of partitions of an interval using rational interior points and endpoint t. Thus the positive and negative Lebesgue-Stieltjes measures dX±=(dV(X)±dX)/2 have Ft​-measurable masses on the generator intervals.
The Monotone class theorem, applied separately to these positive random measures on [0,t], extends this measurability from the generators of the predictable sigma-algebra to all nonnegative predictable processes. Truncation and positive/negative decomposition then cover every admissible H. Consequently H⋅X is adapted.
The integrability condition is essential. Taken literally, an arbitrary previsible process cannot always be integrated against a finite-variation process: with Xt​=t and deterministic Hs​=s−1 for s>0, H0​=0, the proposed value at every t>0 diverges. The usual càdlàg convention is also what identifies dX by dX((a,b])=Xb​−Xa​.
