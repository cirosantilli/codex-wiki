Associate nonnegative Lagrange multipliers α with −Pz−1≤0 and β with Pz−1≤0. The Lagrangian dual problem is obtained from
L(z,α,β)=r0T​x−1T(α+β)+[x−PT(α−β)]Tz.
(3)
Its infimum over unrestricted z is finite exactly when PT(α−β)=x. Thus the dual is
α,β≥0sup​ r0T​x−1T(α+β)subject to PT(α−β)=x.​
(4)
The precise finiteness condition is x∈rangePT. If x=PTq, then xTz=qTPz≥−∥q∥1​ on the feasible set. The primal is feasible and bounded below, and splitting q into positive and negative parts makes the dual feasible. Linear programming duality therefore gives attained equal finite optima. Equivalently, the strictly feasible point z=0 satisfies the Slater condition, with the required finiteness hypothesis. This is strong duality in the usual finite sense.
If x∈/rangePT=(kerP)⊥, there is d∈kerP with xTd<0. Every z=td, t≥0, is feasible and the objective tends to −∞. The dual is then infeasible. Equality of extended values still holds if its supremum over the empty feasible set is defined as −∞, but there are no finite optimizers. The paper does not assume full column rank of P, so this qualification is necessary. The same calculation gives the support function of an inverse image of an infinity-norm ball, infr​rTx=r0T​x−minPTq=x​∥q∥1​ for finite decisions.

For every decision with finite worst-case value, the previous dual represents that value as a maximum over α,β. Maximizing it jointly with x yields the robust linear optimization over the probability simplex formulation:
x,α,βmax​subject to​r0T​x−1T(α+β),x≥0,1Tx=1,α,β≥0,PT(α−β)=x.​​
(5)
Indeed every feasible triple gives a dual lower bound on that decision's worst-case revenue, while dual attainment supplies a triple reaching it. This argument only combines two maximizations after dualization; it does not interchange the original max and min.
If the probability simplex intersects rangePT, this linear program is feasible and bounded above by maxi​(r0​)i​, and hence attains an optimum. Its maximizing x is a robust optimizer. If the intersection is empty, every decision has worst-case value −∞ and the linear program is infeasible, consistently with the extended-value convention. Full column rank of P ensures every simplex decision has a finite inner optimum, but is not needed for the qualified equivalence.

First address the positivity request in part (a), which has no separate Solution heading in the stub. For x∈[0,1], each xk(1−x)n−k and each coefficient ck​ is nonnegative. Their sum is therefore nonnegative, including at the endpoints:
f(x)≥0(0≤x≤1).​
(15)
This is the elementary direction of the positive Bernstein coefficients for a strictly positive polynomial criterion; the converse developed later needs strict positivity.
For completeness, the convergence fact listed as (i) also has a short proof. If K∼Bin(n,x), the Bernstein polynomial is Bn​(f)(x)=E[f(K/n)]. Its mean argument is x and Var(K/n)=x(1−x)/n≤1/(4n). Given ϵ>0, uniform continuity of f on the compact interval supplies δ>0 such that ∣f(s)−f(t)∣<ϵ whenever ∣s−t∣≤δ. By Chebyshev's inequality, uniformly in x,
∣Bn​(f)(x)−f(x)∣≤ϵ+2∥f∥∞​Pr(∣K/n−x∣>δ)≤ϵ+2nδ2∥f∥∞​​.
(16)
Letting n→∞ and then ϵ↓0 proves ∥Bn​(f)−f∥∞​→0​. The two numbered items in the source are assumptions supplied for later parts, not additional exam questions; their stub sections contain these supporting derivations.

Consider Bn​ on the finite-dimensional vector space E=R[x]≤d​ and use the monomial basis. For j≥1, a binomial distribution and the falling factorial moment formula give
Bn​(xj)=nj1​∑ℓ=0j​S(j,ℓ)(n)ℓ​xℓ,
(17)
where S(j,ℓ) is the Stirling number of the second kind and (n)ℓ​=n(n−1)⋯(n−ℓ+1). This follows by expanding Kj=∑ℓ​S(j,ℓ)(K)ℓ​ and using E(K)ℓ​=(n)ℓ​xℓ. Also Bn​(1)=1.
Each monomial maps to a polynomial of no larger degree. The resulting matrix is triangular with diagonal entries 1 and (n)j​/nj, 1≤j≤d. When n≥d and n≥1, all entries are positive. Therefore
Bn​(E)⊆E,Bn​:E→E is invertible for n≥max(1,d).​
(18)
For constant polynomials the conclusion is immediate. Taking positive indices avoids the undefined sample expression k/n at n=0.

vn​=λ∈Rmax​{λ:λ≤βk,n​,0≤k≤n}=kmin​βk,n​.​

βk,n+1​=n+1k​βk−1,n​+(1−n+1k​)βk,n​,
