A log-flat improper prior p(v)∝1/v is not appropriate when all known rs​=σm,s2​ are positive: the integrated likelihood in (iii) has a positive finite limit as v↓0, so its integral against dv/v diverges. One defensible noninformative choice is the scalar Jeffreys prior for an additive variance component, calculated holding the mean parameters fixed:
p(v)∝[21​s∑​(v+rs​)−2]1/2,v≥0​.
(14)
Indeed the normal variance score has information Ivv​=21​∑s​(v+rs​)−2. This is a scalar conditional-information prior, not a claim that its product with the flat mean prior is the joint Jeffreys prior. It is bounded near zero and is O(v−1) at infinity. The integrated likelihood is uniformly bounded near zero and is bounded by a constant times v−(N−1)/2 at infinity, independently of the mean-shape parameters, since e−Q/2≤1. Thus N>1 and a proper external prior on the physically admissible (w,ΩM​) ensure posterior propriety. Proper weak scale priors are another option. If known errors vanish, the boundary argument and appropriate prior need separate reconsideration.
For an efficient parameterization, use θ=(β,u,w,ΩM​) with u=logv and β=M0​+h(H0​). A chain on this joint reduced parameter space targets
π(θ)∝L(β,eu,Href​,w,ΩM​)p(eu)eup(w,ΩM​).
(15)
The factor eu is the Jacobian determinant. At each iteration propose θ′=θ+ε, with ε∼N(0,Σq​) for a fixed nonsingular proposal covariance, and accept with probability min{1,π(θ′)/π(θ)}. Proposals outside the physical prior domain have target zero. Tune Σq​ during warmup and freeze it for the retained Random-walk Metropolis algorithm. To obtain samples of the full original parameter vector, independently draw H0​ from its retained positive prior and reconstruct M0​=β−h(H0​); the resulting vector is (M0​,v,H0​,w,ΩM​). Analytically marginalizing β using (iii) would also be valid.
Use several dispersed chains, trace plots, rank-normalized split R, and the effective sample size of a Markov chain for each parameter and for w,w2. These Markov chain Monte Carlo convergence diagnostics reveal poor mixing and disagreement but do not prove convergence. Estimate the integrated autocorrelation time τint​; with B retained draws, Beff​≈B/τint​. Default to no thinning of a Markov chain, so the thinning factor is 1. If storage requires thinning, choose a spacing after inspecting the autocorrelation and verify the retained-chain effective sample size; no finite spacing guarantees independent draws.
Using the retained wb​ samples, compute
E[w]=B1​b∑​wb​,SD(w)=[B−11​b∑​(wb​−wˉ)2]1/2​.
(16)
These are posterior summaries, provided the corresponding moments exist, not uncertainties of the numerical estimates. A proper prior alone does not ensure finite second moments; choose or check the external prior accordingly. The Monte Carlo standard error of the posterior mean is approximately SD(w)/Beff​​.
