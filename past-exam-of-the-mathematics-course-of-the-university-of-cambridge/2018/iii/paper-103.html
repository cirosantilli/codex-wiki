Fix a Young tableau t of shape λ, let C(t) be its column stabilizer, and put
bt​=∑g∈C(t)​sgn(g)g,et​=bt​{t}.
(1)
These are the Column antisymmetrizer of a Young tableau and its polytabloid. The Specht module is generated by et​ as a cyclic module, so the nonzero R-module homomorphism ϕ has ϕ(et​)=0. Because its extension Φ commutes with the group algebra action,
0=ϕ(et​)=Φ(bt​{t})=bt​Φ({t}).
(2)
Expand Φ({t}) in the tabloid basis of the Young permutation module Mμ. At least one tabloid {u} therefore satisfies bt​{u}=0.
If a row of u contains two entries from the same column of t, their transposition τ∈C(t) fixes {u}. Pairing the terms indexed by g and gτ in bt​{u} makes them cancel. This remains valid in characteristic two, since the paired coefficients sum to zero there as well. Thus each row of u meets each column of t in at most one entry.
The first r rows of u consequently contain at most min(r,λj′​) entries from column j of t, where λ′ is the conjugate partition. Hence
∑i=1r​μi​≤∑j​min(r,λj′​)=∑i=1r​λi​(r≥1).
(3)
By the dominance order on partitions, this proves
λ⊵μ.​
(4)
This is dominance from a nonzero column antisymmetrizer. The extension is what allows us to apply bt​ to an element of Mμ without dividing by the column stabilizer's order, which might vanish in the given field.

The Young permutation module M(n−k,k) is the symmetric-group subset permutation representation on the k-element subsets of {1,…,n}: the second row of a tabloid specifies the subset. For k≥1, restriction of a character to Sn−1​ fixing n splits these subsets into two orbits of a group action, according as they contain n.
The corresponding permutation representations are on the k-subsets and the (k−1)-subsets of {1,…,n−1}. Their Young permutation modules have two row sizes n−1−k,k and n−k,k−1, respectively. Sort each pair into decreasing order and omit any zero part; swapping the two row labels gives an isomorphic permutation representation. This sorting matters at n=2k.
We use Young's rule: over the complex numbers, the multiplicity of Sρ in Mη is the Kostka number Kρη​, and it can be nonzero only if ρ dominates η in the dominance order on partitions. Since each η above has at most two parts and size n−1, dominance gives
ρ1​+ρ2​≥η1​+η2​=n−1.
(5)
Thus ρ has at most two nonzero parts. The character inner product measures this constituent multiplicity, so
⟨ResSn−1​Sn​​π(n−k,k),χρ⟩=0when ℓ(ρ)≥3.​
(6)
If k=0, the original and restricted modules are the trivial representations, giving the same conclusion. For n=1, there is no partition of n−1=0 with three parts, so the assertion is vacuous.

Apply Young's rule, stated as
M(n−k,k)≅⨁ρ⊢n​(Sρ)⊕Kρ,(n−k,k)​.
(7)
Here the Kostka number counts Semistandard Young tableaux of shape ρ with n−k entries equal to 1 and k entries equal to 2. Their rows weakly increase and their columns strictly increase. A column can therefore have at most two cells, so only a partition of an integer ρ=(n−j,j) can occur.
In such a semistandard Young tableau, all j entries in the second row must be 2 and all j entries above them must be 1. The whole first row is then fixed by the content: its first n−k entries are 1 and its remaining k−j entries are 2. This is possible exactly when j≤k and j≤n−k. Because k≤n/2, the second inequality follows from the first. There is exactly one filling for every 0≤j≤k, and none for any other shape.
Consequently each displayed Specht module has multiplicity one, proving the two-row Young permutation module decomposition
M(n−k,k)≅j=0⨁k​S(n−j,j).​
(8)
For j=0, the zero second part is omitted. As a dimension check, the Hook-length formula gives dimS(n−j,j)=(jn​)−(j−1n​), with (−1n​)=0; the sum telescopes to (kn​), the dimension of the original permutation representation.

Use the hook criterion in a beta set: for a finite beta set of a partition X, each hook length is a difference b−c with b∈X, 0≤c<b and c∈/X, and every such difference is a hook length.
A hook length pr therefore gives a bead b and a gap c=b−pr. Along the progression
b, b−p, b−2p,…, b−rp=c,
(9)
the first position is a bead and the last is a gap. Some adjacent positions are consequently a bead followed by a gap. Their difference is p, so p is a hook length. Similarly the progression from b to c in steps of r gives a hook length r. Every position used is nonnegative because it lies between c and b.
Thus the divisor closure of hook lengths yields
pr∈H(λ) ⟹ p,r∈H(λ).​
(10)
Neither p nor r needs to be a prime number.

∣η∣=∑r≥0​cr​(η)pr,νp​(χη(1))=p−1∑r≥0​cr​(η)−dp​(∣η∣)​.

Indeed carrying p units from one position to the next decreases the sum of coefficients by p−1; the valuation vanishes precisely when there are no carries.
For k≥1, we also use power-core truncation of a prime-core tower: for γ=Cpk​(λ), its core tower agrees with that of λ below level k and is empty at every level at least k. Moreover qk​(λ)=wpk​(λ), the weight of a partition for modulus pk. To see the truncation, removal of a rim hook of length pk preserves the first p-core and, under the abacus divisible-hook correspondence, becomes removal of a pk−1-hook in one quotient component. Iterating preserves all the lower cores; after all such removals, quotient level k is empty.
If p∤χλ(1), the criterion gives ck​(λ)=a, cr​(λ)=0 for r>k, and the lower cr​(λ) are the base-p digits of m. It follows that ∣γ∣=m, p∤χγ(1) and
wpk​(λ)=pkn−∣γ∣​=a.
(17)
Conversely, suppose wpk​(λ)=a and p∤χγ(1). Then ∣γ∣=m and its lower core-tower sizes are the digits of m. Quotient level k of λ has total size a<p. Each component there has size less than p, hence is already a p-core and has empty p-quotient. Thus ck​(λ)=a and every higher level is empty. Combined with truncation, this gives exactly the digits of n, so p∤χλ(1).

p∤χλ(1) ⟺ wpk​(λ)=a and p∤χCpk​(λ)(1).​
(18)

We use the abacus divisible-hook correspondence in its multiset form: the hook lengths of λ that are divisible by p, divided by p, form exactly the disjoint union of the multisets of hook lengths of the components of its quotient of a partition for modulus p.
Iterating this correspondence gives, for every r≥1,
{h/pr:h∈H(λ), pr∣h}=⨆η∈TQ(λ)r​​H(η)
(23)
as multisets. Each Young diagram has one hook for every cell, so the cardinality of the right-hand side is ∣TQ(λ)r​∣, the sum of the component sizes.
For any positive integer h, its P-adic valuation is the number of positive r for which pr∣h. Summing this identity over hooks and interchanging the finite sums proves
νp​​h∈H(λ)∏​h​​=h∈H(λ)∑​r≥1∑​1pr∣h​=r≥1∑​∣TQ(λ)r​∣.​
(24)
Thus
νp​​h∈H(λ)∏​h​=r≥1∑​∣TQ(λ)r​∣.​
(25)
The sums are finite because hook lengths are bounded by ∣λ∣, and the total size at each level of the quotient tower of a partition decreases by at least a factor p until it reaches zero.

We use the core-quotient bijection for partitions: an ordered p-tuple (ν0​,…,νp−1​) and a fixed p-core determine a unique partition of an integer, with
∣λ∣=∣Cp​(λ)∣+p∑i​∣νi​∣.
(26)
The irreducible characters in both B and C therefore correspond to the same ordered tuples of total size w. Replacing the empty core of a partition by γ while keeping the quotient of a partition fixed gives a bijection χλ↦χμ from B to C. In particular ∣B∣=∣C∣.
For such a pair, all levels of the core tower of a partition above level zero agree. At level zero, their sizes are 0 and m, respectively. Put D=∑r≥1​∣TC(λ)r​∣=∑r≥1​∣TC(μ)r​∣. When 0≤m<p, the base-p digit sum satisfies
dp​(wp)=dp​(w),dp​(wp+m)=dp​(w)+m.
(27)
The P-adic valuation of a symmetric-group character degree from the core tower then gives
νp​(χλ(1))=p−1D−dp​(w)​=p−1m+D−[dp​(w)+m]​=νp​(χμ(1)).
(28)
Thus the same bijection preserves degree coprimality to p and restricts to a bijection between the indicated sets of irreducible characters of degree coprime to p. Consequently
∣B∣=∣C∣,∣B∩Irrp′​(Swp​)∣=∣C∩Irrp′​(Sn​)∣.​
(29)
For m=0, take γ to be the empty partition; the construction becomes the identity.

For every χμ∈C, level zero of the core tower of a partition is its core of a partition γ, of size m. The character degree coprime to p from the core tower criterion says that if p∤χμ(1), this level must have size equal to the units digit α0​ of n in base p.
But 0≤α0​<p, whereas m≥p. Hence every character in C has degree divisible by p, and
∣C∩Irrp′​(Sn​)∣=0.​
(30)
Equivalently, the core-tower size coefficient m at position zero needs at least one carry in normalization to the base-p digits. Each carry contributes one to the P-adic valuation of a symmetric-group character degree from the core tower.
