Assume M satisfies the countable chain condition for forcing. We prove cardinal preservation by chain-condition forcing using the possible-values lemma for chain-condition forcing, and give the argument explicitly for ccc.
Let κ be an uncountable cardinal of M. If it ceased to be a cardinal, some ordinal μ<κ would admit a surjection onto κ in an extension. By the forcing theorem, there would be a condition p and a forcing name f˙​ such that
p⊩f˙​:μˇ​↠κˇ.
(40)
Inside M, for each ξ<μ choose a maximal antichain in a forcing order below p deciding the ordinal value of f˙​(ξ). Conditions deciding that value are dense below p. The chain condition makes the chosen antichain countable in M, so the corresponding set Bξ​⊆κ of possible decided values is countable in M.
A generic filter containing p meets the downward closure of every such maximal antichain, so its value f(ξ) belongs to Bξ​. Therefore its whole range lies in the ground-model set
B=⋃ξ<μ​Bξ​,∣B∣M≤∣μ∣M⋅ℵ0​<κ.
(41)
The last inequality is infinite cardinal arithmetic and uses only that κ is uncountable and μ<κ; no regularity of κ is needed. Some ordinal in κ∖B exists already in M and cannot occur in the range, contradicting the forced surjectivity.
Finite cardinals cannot collapse, and ω cannot become finite: a finite domain has finite image, and transitive models agree on the natural numbers. Old non-cardinals cannot become cardinals because their old bijections persist. Thus
P is ccc in M⟹CardM[G]=CardM.​
(42)
