For the reverse implication, suppose p∈G and M⊨p⊩∗∃xφ(x). By the existential clause of syntactic forcing, the set
D={q≤p:M⊨∃σ(q⊩∗φ(σ))}
(34)
is dense below p; the existential quantifier ranges over forcing names of M. Definability of the syntactic forcing relation and axiom schema of separation put D in M.
To apply genericity, augment it to the globally dense set
D′=D∪{q∈P:q⊥p}.
(35)
A condition compatible with p has an extension below p, then one in D, and an incompatible condition already lies in D′. Thus D′ is a dense subset of a forcing order in M. The generic filter meets it. Since p∈G, directedness prevents G from containing a condition incompatible with p, so some q∈G∩D exists.
There is consequently a name σ∈M with M⊨q⊩∗φ(σ). The assumed truth lemma gives M[G]⊨φ(val(σ,G)), and hence
∃p∈GM⊨p⊩∗∃xφ(x)⟹M[G]⊨∃xφ(x).​
(36)
Together with part (a), this completes the existential step of the forcing theorem without assuming the desired existential truth lemma in advance.
