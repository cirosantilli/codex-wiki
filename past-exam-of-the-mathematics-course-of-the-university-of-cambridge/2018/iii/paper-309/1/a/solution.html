Let q denote the quotient map and write [X] for a point of Real projective space. For i=1,…,n+1, take the open set
Ui​={[X]:Xi​=0}.
(1)
It is open in the quotient topology because its inverse image under q is the open set {X:Xi​=0}. These sets cover, since a nonzero vector has at least one nonzero coordinate. Define the manifold chart
φi​([X])=(X1​/Xi​,…,Xi​/Xi​​,…,Xn+1​/Xi​)∈Rn.
(2)
The hat means that the ith coordinate is omitted. Ratios are unchanged by multiplying X by any nonzero real number, so the chart is well defined. Its inverse inserts 1 into position i and takes the resulting equivalence class. The ratios and this inverse are continuous, giving a homeomorphism Ui​→Rn.
For a compact description of the smooth transition maps, write ai​=1 and let ak​=Xk​/Xi​ for k=i. On Ui​∩Uj​ one has aj​=0, and
(φj​∘φi−1​)(a)k​=aj​ak​​(k=j).​
(3)
Each coordinate is a rational smooth function on the open domain aj​=0; reversing i,j gives a smooth inverse. Thus these n+1 charts form the standard affine atlas of real projective space.
For completeness, the underlying space is Hausdorff: normalizing X identifies it with the antipodal quotient of the unit sphere, and the continuous injective map [X]↦XXT/(XTX) into the space of symmetric matrices separates any two distinct classes by disjoint open neighborhoods. It is also a second-countable space: pull back the countable bases of rational balls in these finitely many charts, and take their union. Therefore
RPn is a smooth manifold of dimension n, with an atlas of n+1 charts.​
(4)
