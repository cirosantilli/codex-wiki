If 1<p<2, the reverse comparison is Qn​≤δn2−p​Zn​. The assumed finite limsupZn​ makes Zn​ eventually bounded almost surely, so Qn​→0 almost surely. By uniqueness of a limit in probability, ⟨M⟩1​=0 almost surely. Its monotonicity gives ⟨M⟩t​=0 for t≤1.
To finish without assuming any integrability of M, apply the bound from part (i) to both M and −M, with t=1, and then let b↓0. For each a>0 the probability of a positive or negative excursion beyond a is zero. Taking countably many a↓0 proves M is indistinguishable from zero on [0,1]. The same argument in fact works for every 0<p<2 under the stated pathwise boundedness assumption.
