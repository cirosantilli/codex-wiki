Use the standard definition of a local martingale, including an integrable initial value, and let Tn​↑∞ be a localizing sequence. If X≥0, the Fatou lemma gives EXt​≤liminfn​EXt∧Tn​​=EX0​<∞. For s≤t, the Conditional Fatou lemma gives
E(Xt​∣Fs​)≤liminfn​E(Xt∧Tn​​∣Fs​)=liminfn​Xs∧Tn​​=Xs​.
(21)
For clarity, the Conditional Fatou lemma itself follows by setting Um​=infn≥m​Un​ for nonnegative Un​, applying the conditional version of the monotone convergence theorem to Um​↑liminfUn​, and observing E(Um​∣Fs​)≤infn≥m​E(Un​∣Fs​). Hence a nonnegative local martingale is a supermartingale.
If ∣Xt​∣≤C simultaneously for all t, with a deterministic C<∞, the dominated convergence theorem applied to the stopped martingale identity gives, for each A∈Fs​,
E(1A​Xt​)=limn​E(1A​Xt∧Tn​​)=limn​E(1A​Xs∧Tn​​)=E(1A​Xs​).
(22)
This is exactly the conditional expectation identity, so a bounded local martingale is a true martingale. The same proof works for deterministic bounds on every finite time interval; merely having a finite random pathwise bound supplies no dominating integrable random variable and is a different convention.
