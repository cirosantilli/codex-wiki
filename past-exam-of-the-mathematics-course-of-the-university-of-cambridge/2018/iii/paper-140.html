Moser theorem says that if M is a compact smooth manifold without boundary and ωt​, 0≤t≤1, is a smooth family of symplectic forms with constant de Rham cohomology class, then there is a smooth isotopy ψt​, with ψ0​=id, such that
ψt∗​ωt​=ω0​.​
(1)
Thus the endpoints are symplectomorphic. The hypothesis is a whole path of symplectic forms, not merely equality of the endpoint de Rham cohomology classes.

A symplectomorphism is a diffeomorphism, so it preserves the genus of a surface. It also preserves the orientation determined by the symplectic form and, by integration of a pullback of a differential form, preserves the symplectic area.
Conversely, equal genus of a surface gives a diffeomorphism f:Σ0​→Σ1​. We can choose f to preserve the orientations determined by ω0​,ω1​: if necessary, compose with an orientation-reversing diffeomorphism of the standard closed orientable surface of that genus of a surface, obtained by reflection of a symmetric handle model. On Σ0​ put η=f∗ω1​. Both η and ω0​ are positive volume forms for the same orientation, and their integrals agree. By top-degree de Rham cohomology, [η]=[ω0​].
The family ωt​=(1−t)ω0​+tη consists of positive volume forms, hence symplectic forms in dimension two, with constant de Rham cohomology class. Moser theorem supplies ψ with ψ∗η=ω0​. Consequently
(f∘ψ)∗ω1​=ω0​.​
(2)
Therefore the genus of a surface and symplectic area classify compact connected symplectic surfaces.

There is an orientation convention in this statement: the two volume forms must be positive for one fixed orientation, as is usual in Moser theorem for volume forms. Without that convention, the stated necessity is false. For example, on an oriented two-sphere, take a positive volume form β0​ and an orientation-reversing diffeomorphism r, and set β1​=r∗β0​. Then (r−1)∗β1​=β0​ but ∫β1​=−∫β0​. We prove the intended result with a common orientation.
If ϕ∗β1​=β0​, positivity forces ϕ to preserve the orientation, and integration of the pullback of a differential form gives equality of the integrals.
For sufficiency, top-degree de Rham cohomology gives a differential form α of degree n−1 with β1​−β0​=dα. Put βt​=(1−t)β0​+tβ1​. Positivity makes every βt​ a volume form. The interior product of a differential form gives an linear isomorphism
Tp​M⟶Λn−1Tp∗​M,v⟼ιv​βt​.
(3)
Thus there is a unique smooth time-dependent vector field Xt​ satisfying ιXt​​βt​=−α. Since M is compact, its local flow exists throughout 0≤t≤1 and defines a smooth isotopy ϕt​ starting at the identity. By Cartan's magic formula, and since a top-degree differential form has zero exterior derivative,
dtd​ϕt∗​βt​=ϕt∗​(dα+LXt​​βt​)=ϕt∗​(dα+dιXt​​βt​)=0.
(4)
Hence
ϕ1∗​β1​=β0​.​
(5)
The zero-dimensional case, a single point, is immediate from equality of the integrals.

Let dimV=2n and let V+​=ker(S−I) and V−​=ker(S+I) be the two eigenspaces. The involution identity gives a direct sum, since
v=2v+Sv​+2v−Sv​,V=V+​⊕V−​.
(6)
For u,v in either one of these eigenspaces, the anti-symplectic involution identity implies
Ω(u,v)=Ω(Su,Sv)=−Ω(u,v),
(7)
so both are isotropic subspaces of a symplectic vector space. Such a subspace has dimension at most n: W⊂WΩ and the nondegenerate bilinear form Ω gives dimWΩ=2n−dimW, where WΩ is the symplectic orthogonal complement. As their dimensions add to 2n, both have dimension n. Therefore
Fix(S)=V+​ is a Lagrangian subspace.​
(8)

Use the decomposition into Lagrangian subspaces V=V+​⊕V−​ from part (a). The bilinear form Ω pairs V+​ and V−​ nondegenerately: a vector in either summand annihilating the other already annihilates its own summand and hence all of V, so is zero.

For p∈Fix(σ), differentiation gives (dσp​)2=I and (dσp​)∗ωp​=−ωp​. Thus dσp​ is an anti-symplectic involution of the symplectic vector space Tp​M. By part (a), its +1 eigenspace is a Lagrangian subspace.
In the local manifold chart supplied in the question, σ is linear, so its fixed-point set is the intersection of the chart with this +1 eigenspace. Therefore the fixed-point set is an embedded submanifold and
Tp​Fix(σ)=ker(dσp​−I).
(11)
Every such tangent space has half the ambient dimension and the symplectic form vanishes on it. Hence every nonempty fixed-point set is a Lagrangian submanifold. The fixed-point set itself need not be connected.

As usual for Complex projective space and Real projective space, homogeneous coordinates are represented by nonzero vectors. Componentwise complex conjugation descends to the diffeomorphism
τ:CPn⟶CPn,[z]⟼[z].
(15)
It is well defined because λz=λz, and τ2=I. Every standard manifold chart is invariant under τ, with φi​∘τ=σ∘φi​. Part (d) and the gluing of the Fubini-Study form therefore imply τ∗ωFS​=−ωFS​.
A point [z] in the fixed-point set satisfies z=λz. Applying complex conjugation again gives ∣λ∣=1. Choose a=0 with a/a=λ; then az=az, so [z] has a real representative. Conversely every real representative determines a point in the fixed-point set. Hence Fix(τ)=RPn, and part (c) gives
RPn⊂(CPn,ωFS​) is a Lagrangian submanifold.​
(16)

Take the usual convention that the smooth isotopy starts at ρ0​=id. The exterior derivative commutes with a pullback of a differential form, so
d(ρt∗​λ−λ)=ρt∗​dλ−dλ=ω−ρt∗​ω.
(17)
Consequently
ρt∗​λ−λ is closed for every t⟺ρt∗​ω=ω for every t.​
(18)
The right-hand condition says precisely that ρt​ is a symplectic isotopy.
Here an exact symplectic manifold is necessarily noncompact in positive dimension: if dimM=2n and ω=−dλ, then ωn=−d(λ∧ωn−1), contradicting the positive symplectic volume and the Generalized Stokes theorem on a compact smooth manifold without boundary. Thus the paper's introductory word “closed” must here be read as “without boundary”, as its parenthesis suggests, rather than as including compactness.

Differentiating the assumed primitive identity and using Cartan's magic formula gives
dF˙t​=ρt∗​LXt​​λ=ρt∗​(d(ιXt​​λ)−ιXt​​ω).
(22)
Apply the pullback of a differential form by ρt−1​ and rearrange:
ιXt​​ω=dHt​,Ht​=ιXt​​λ−F˙t​∘ρt−1​.​
(23)
This smooth family of Hamiltonian functions proves that ρt​ is a Hamiltonian isotopy in the convention of part (b).
To connect this with the preceding assertion that every ρt∗​λ−λ is an exact differential form, the smooth choice of primitives causes no additional obstruction. Fix p0​∈M and normalize Ft​(p0​)=0. Define Ft​(p) by the line integral of ρt∗​λ−λ from p0​ to p. The fundamental theorem for line integrals makes this independent of the path. Near any p, use one fixed path to a nearby chart center followed by coordinate line segments; this expression is smooth jointly in t,p. Thus these normalized primitives form the required smooth family.

Let π:T∗X→X be the cotangent bundle projection and λ its canonical one-form on a cotangent bundle, defined by λ(x,ξ)​(v)=ξ(dπ(v)). Use the symplectic form ω=−dλ, so in local coordinates ω=∑j​dxj​∧dξj​.
The graph map sβ​(x)=(x,βx​) is a smooth embedding, with sβ∗​λ=β, and hence
sβ∗​ω=−dβ.
(24)
Its image has dimension dimX, half the dimension of T∗X. By the definition of a Lagrangian submanifold,
graph(β) is Lagrangian⟺dβ=0.​
(25)
This is the graph of a closed one-form is Lagrangian criterion. The opposite conventional sign for the symplectic form gives the same criterion.

The cotangent fiber translation f is a diffeomorphism, with inverse (x,ξ)↦(x,ξ−βx​). Because π∘f=π, the definition of the canonical one-form on a cotangent bundle gives
f∗λ=λ+π∗β.

Write the exact differential form as β=dh and take the cotangent fiber translation family ρt​(x,ξ)=(x,ξ+tdhx​). Its generating vector field is vertical and in local coordinates is
X=∑j​∂xj​∂h​∂ξj​∂​.

A compatible almost complex structure is a smooth bundle map J:TM→TM such that, for all tangent vectors u,v,
J2=−I,ω(Ju,Jv)=ω(u,v),ω(u,Ju)>0(u=0).​
(30)
It determines the Riemannian metric
g(u,v)=ω(u,Jv).​
(31)
Indeed, the invariance of the symplectic form under J and its antisymmetry imply ω(v,Ju)=ω(u,Jv), so g is symmetric; the positivity condition makes it positive definite. A compatible triple consists of this symplectic form, this compatible almost complex structure, and this Riemannian metric. Useful equivalent identities are ω(u,v)=g(Ju,v) and g(Ju,Jv)=g(u,v).

At a point p, write W=Tp​L and take the orthogonal complement with respect to the Riemannian metric g. The compatible triple identity gives
g(Ju,v)=ω(u,v).
(32)
If L is a Lagrangian submanifold, then ω(u,v)=0 for u,v∈W, so JW⊂W⊥. Both have dimension 21​dimM, hence equality.
Conversely, if JW=W⊥, equality of dimensions gives dimW=21​dimM, and g(Ju,v)=0 for u,v∈W gives ω∣W​=0. Therefore
L is Lagrangian⟺J(Tp​L)=(Tp​L)⊥ for every p.​
(33)
A positive-definite inner product has W∩W⊥={0}. Hence W∩JW={0}, proving that every Lagrangian submanifold is a totally real submanifold for a compatible almost complex structure.

Use C2 with the standard symplectic form ω0​=dx1​∧dy1​+dx2​∧dy2​ and the compatible almost complex structure given by multiplication by i. Consider the embedded submanifold
L={(s+it,t):s,t∈R}.​

Use the paper's Fubini-Study form normalization ωFS​=2i​∂∂log(1+∣z∣2), the Hamiltonian vector field convention ιXH​​ω=dH, and the Lie algebra generator whose circle action is eiθ. Write D=∣z0​∣2+∣z1​∣2+∣z2​∣2. The normalized moment map for the torus action is
ϕ([z0​,z1​,z2​])=−2D1​(∣z1​∣2,∣z2​∣2).​
(40)
The moment map image is the filled triangle
ϕ(CP2)={(u,v):u≤0, v≤0, u+v≥−21​}.​
(41)
Indeed, the three quantities ∣zj​∣2/D are nonnegative and add to one, and any such triple is realized by choosing their square roots as homogeneous coordinates.
For the diagonal circle action, a point in the fixed-point set satisfies [z0​,tz1​,tz2​]=[z0​,z1​,z2​] for every t∈S1. If z0​=0, the projective scaling must be one, forcing z1​=z2​=0. If z0​=0, all coordinates are scaled together. Therefore
Fix(S1)={[1,0,0]} ⊔ {[0,z1​,z2​]}≅{point}⊔CP1.​
(42)
By part (b), the diagonal moment map is
μ([z])=−2D∣z1​∣2+∣z2​∣2​,μ(CP2)=[−21​,0].​
(43)

On N=μ−1(−1/4), the diagonal fundamental vector field X is nonzero, since the fixed-point set lies at moment map values 0 and −1/2. The nondegenerate bilinear form ωp​ and dμ=ιX​ω imply dμ=0, so −1/4 is a regular value. By the regular level set theorem, N is a three-dimensional embedded submanifold of the four-dimensional Complex projective plane.
At p∈N, set W=Tp​N=kerdμp​. Then ω(X,v)=0 for every v∈W, so X∈Wω, the symplectic orthogonal complement. This complement is one-dimensional and dμ(X)=ω(X,X)=0, giving
(Tp​N)ω=RXp​⊂Tp​N.​
(44)
Thus the level set is a coisotropic submanifold, neither an isotropic submanifold nor a Lagrangian submanifold. Indeed an isotropic subspace of a symplectic vector space in dimension four has dimension at most two, whereas dimN=3. Its characteristic line field is generated by the diagonal circle action.

The level equation is ∣z1​∣2+∣z2​∣2=∣z0​∣2. In particular z0​=0, so the global affine coordinates on this level are wj​=zj​/z0​. They identify it with
N={(w1​,w2​)∈C2:∣w1​∣2+∣w2​∣2=1}=S3.
(45)
The diagonal circle action becomes (w1​,w2​)↦(tw1​,tw2​) and is free: at least one coordinate is nonzero, so fixing a point forces t=1. Since the circle is compact, the Free proper Lie-group action theorem makes the quotient a smooth two-dimensional smooth manifold.
The map (w1​,w2​)↦[w1​,w2​] is the Hopf fibration. It is onto the complex projective line, and two unit representatives determine the same complex line precisely when they differ by a scalar of modulus one, hence by a circle orbit. Local sections, for example [1,u]↦(1,u)/1+∣u∣2​, show that the induced identification is a diffeomorphism. Therefore
Mred​=μ−1(−1/4)/S1≅CP1≅S2.​
(46)
This identifies the symplectic reduction as a smooth manifold; no computation of its reduced symplectic form is needed.
