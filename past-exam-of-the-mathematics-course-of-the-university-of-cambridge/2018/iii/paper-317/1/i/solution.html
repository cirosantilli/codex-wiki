Work in an inertial frame centred on the centre of mass, and follow a fixed material mass, with no stellar wind or accretion through its boundary. Assume nonrelativistic motion, Newtonian self-gravity, no external gravitational field, and sufficiently regular integrable fields. Define
I=∫r2dm,T=21​∫∣v∣2dm,Ω=−2G​∬∣r−r′∣dmdm′​=21​∫ρΦdV.
(1)
Here I is the scalar second mass moment, rather than a moment of inertia about one axis. The kinetic energy T measures resolved internal motions relative to the centre of mass; random microscopic motion contributes to pressure and internal energy, and must not be counted again in T.
The paper's symmetric compressive tensor P is minus the tensile stress tensor, so its force per unit volume is −∂j​Pij​. Differentiating the material integral twice and substituting the equation of motion gives
21​I¨=2T−∫ri​∂j​Pij​dV−∫r⋅∇Φdm.
(2)
Integration by parts and the divergence theorem turn the stress contribution into
−∫ri​∂j​Pij​dV=∫trPdV−∮ri​Pij​nj​dS.
(3)
For the requested scalar stellar virial theorem, assume isotropic pressure, Pij​=Pδij​, with uniform surface pressure PS​. Then trP=3P and the surface integral is PS​∮r⋅ndS=3PS​V. No spherical shape is needed for this step.
Finally, symmetrizing the pair integral for the Newtonian gravitational potential gives
−∫r⋅∇Φdm=−2G​∬∣r−r′∣3(r−r′)⋅(r−r′)​dmdm′=Ω.
(4)
Therefore
21​I¨=2T+3∫PdV−3PS​V+Ω.​
(5)
Anisotropic magnetic or viscous stresses retain their full trace and boundary terms; a changing integration mass adds transport terms.
