A Brownian excursion in the upper half-plane from 0 to infinity is killed planar Brownian motion conditioned to stay in H, with its entrance law at 0. Precisely, it is the Doob h-transform for h(z)=Imz, obtained by starting at iε and letting ε↓0. Equivalently,
Bt​=Wt​+iRt​,​
(50)
where W is standard Brownian motion and the independent process R is a dimension-three Bessel process started at zero. The transformed generator is 21​Δ+y−1∂y​, which explains this representation.
First compute avoidance from an interior point z. Let τ be the exit time of ordinary planar Brownian motion from H∖A. The harmonic correction
v(z)=Im(z−gA​(z))
(51)
has boundary values Imz on A and zero on the real axis. The hydrodynamic normalization at infinity gives gA​(z)=z+hcap(A)/z+O(∣z∣−2). Together with the maximum principle for harmonic functions this gives the useful bound 0≤v≤supa∈A​Ima. Since ordinary Brownian motion hits the real axis almost surely, the optional stopping theorem applied to this bounded harmonic function gives
v(z)=Ez​[ImBτ​]=Ez​[ImBτ​1{Bτ​∈A}​].
(52)
The Doob h-transform weights a stopped path by its final height divided by its initial height. Applying this at the first hit of A, with bounded-time localization and then a limit, therefore yields
Pzh​(τA​<∞)=Imzv(z)​,Pzh​(τA​=∞)=ImzImgA​(z)​.
(53)
For the entrance law the same result follows by conditioning at a small positive time and letting that time decrease to zero: the avoidance function has the boundary limit computed next, and the excursion is initially in a neighbourhood disjoint from A.
Because the closure of the hull avoids 0, the Schwarz reflection principle makes gA​ analytic in a neighbourhood of zero, with real gA​(0) and positive real gA′​(0). Thus
limε↓0​εImgA​(iε)​=gA′​(0).
(54)
In fact, the reflected Taylor expansion gives ImgA​(z)/Imz=gA′​(0)+O(∣z∣) uniformly as z→0 inside the half-plane, so the same limit applies to the entrance process. The map in the question is ψA​=gA​−gA​(0), so its derivative agrees with gA′​. We obtain the restriction probability of a Brownian half-plane excursion:
P[B([0,∞))∩A=∅]=ψA′​(0).​
(55)
