OurBigBook.com Words: 229
For a fixed-step linear multistep method with a nonzero leading coefficient, applied to an ordinary differential equation with a locally Lipschitz continuous vector field, the Dahlquist equivalence theorem states that
convergence ⟺ consistency and zero-stability.​
(1)
Here convergence is uniform on each fixed finite time interval as the step size tends to zero, for every set of starting values tending to the corresponding exact values. Consistency of a numerical method means that the normalized local defect tends to zero; for the characteristic polynomials of a linear multistep method it gives ρ(1)=0 and ρ′(1)=σ(1). Zero-stability is equivalent to the root condition for a multistep method: every root of a polynomial ξ of ρ satisfies ∣ξ∣≤1, and roots with ∣ξ∣=1 are simple. Order p≥1, together with zero-stability and starting errors O(hp), gives global error O(hp) under the usual smoothness assumptions.
To prove the necessity of the root condition for multistep convergence, use the test problem y′=0, y(0)=0. Its discrete error satisfies ρ(E)en​=0, where Een​=en+1​. Set h=T/N. If ∣ξ∣>1, the exact recurrence solution
en​=∣ξ∣−Nξn
(2)
has starting errors tending to zero at every fixed starting index, but ∣eN​∣=1. Thus convergence fails.
If a unit-modulus root has multiplicity m≥2, the recurrence instead admits
en​=N−(m−1)nm−1ξn.
(3)
Again every fixed starting error tends to zero while ∣eN​∣=1. These polynomial-times-exponential solutions follow from the repeated factor (E−ξ)m. Complex modes can be interpreted as a real two-component system, or their real and imaginary parts can be used. Therefore convergence requires precisely the root condition for a multistep method. Repeated roots strictly inside the unit disk are allowed, since their polynomial factors are dominated by exponential decay.
