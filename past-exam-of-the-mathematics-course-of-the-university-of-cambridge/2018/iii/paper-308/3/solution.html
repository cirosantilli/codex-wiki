There is an exact rational-map description of sigma-model lumps and a restricted variational rational-map description of Skyrmions. The first follows from a Bogomolny equation; the second separates angular and radial dependence in a field that generally does not saturate a Bogomolny bound.
For the exact example, take the two-dimensional O3 nonlinear sigma model with a unit field n, energy E=21​∫∣∇n∣2d2x, and a fixed limit at infinity. Regular fields then have a one-point compactification to maps S2→S2. Choose the orientation so that the stereographic field
u=1+n3​n1​+in2​​,z=x+iy,
(19)
has positive charge when it is holomorphic. Equivalently, this is the CP1 nonlinear sigma model since the target sphere is the complex projective line. Its energy and topological charge are
E=4∫(1+∣u∣2)2∣uz​∣2+∣uzˉ​∣2​d2x,N=π1​∫(1+∣u∣2)2∣uz​∣2−∣uzˉ​∣2​d2x.
(20)
Subtracting 4πN gives 8∫∣uzˉ​∣2/(1+∣u∣2)2, so for N>0,
E≥4πN,uzˉ​=0 at equality.​
(21)
The Cauchy-Riemann equations therefore give the minimal-energy fields. A holomorphic map from the compactified domain Riemann sphere to the target Riemann sphere is a rational map u=p(z)/q(z), with common polynomial factors cancelled. Its degree of a rational map of the Riemann sphere is N=max(degp,degq). Poles of u are coordinate singularities, not singularities of n. For instance, u=(z−a)/λ with λ=0 is an exact unit lump with energy 4π, arbitrary centre a, and scale ∣λ∣. Its density is 4∣λ∣2/(∣z−a∣2+∣λ∣2)2, whose plane integral is 4π. For negative charge use antiholomorphic maps. Scale invariance allows arbitrarily small lumps and does not by itself prevent a singular concentration limit in the time-dependent theory.
The local angular geometry of any degree-N rational map is measured by its angular Jacobian of a rational map,
JR​(z)=[1+∣R∣21+∣z∣2​∣R′∣]2,R∗dΩ=JR​dΩ,∫S2​JR​dΩ=4πN.
(22)
For R=p/q, the Wronskian of a rational map is
W(z)=p′(z)q(z)−p(z)q′(z),R′=q2W​.​
(23)
At ordinary finite points its zeros mark ramification points of a holomorphic map, where the angular density vanishes. At a pole use q/p as the target coordinate, and at infinity use 1/z as the domain coordinate. A pole of order m is ramified by m−1. The Riemann-Hurwitz formula counts total ramification 2N−2, including infinity, even if the affine Wronskian has smaller degree. For R=zN, W=NzN−1 accounts for N−1 zeros at zero; the reciprocal coordinate shows the other N−1 at infinity.
For the approximate example, the rational map approximation for Skyrmions uses spherical radius r, angular stereographic projection coordinate z, and
U(r,z)=cosf(r)+isinf(r)nR​(z)⋅σ,nR​=1+∣R∣2(2ReR,2ImR,1−∣R∣2)​,f(0)=π,quadf(∞)=0.
(24)
The Pauli matrices make this an SU(2) group-valued field. The boundary conditions give a continuous centre and the vacuum value at infinity; the profile must also make the energy finite. Angular degree and radial winding factorize to give
B=−π2N​∫0∞​f′sin2fdr=N.​
(25)
At radii with nonzero −f′sin2f, the angular baryon number density is proportional to JR​. Thus the Wronskian of a rational map locates zero-density directions and reveals the holes or face directions in shell-like Skyrmion configurations.
In dimensionless Skyrme model units, insert this ansatz into the quadratic and quartic derivative energies. The angular terms integrate to 4πN or to the angular integral in the rational map approximation,
I[R]=4π1​∫S2​JR2​dΩ,E=4π∫0∞​[r2f′2+2N(f′2+1)sin2f+Ir2sin4f​]dr.
(26)
First minimize I over degree-N maps, then solve the radial variational equation
(r2+2Nsin2f)f′′+2rf′+Nsin(2f)(f′2−1)−r2I​sin2fsin(2f)=0
(27)
with the stated boundary conditions. The Cauchy-Schwarz inequality gives I≥N2 because JR​ has sphere average N. For N=1 an isometric map such as R=z has JR​=1, I=1, and reduces to the Skyrmion hedgehog ansatz; its profile still requires solving the radial equation. For general N, angular and radial separation restrict the allowed fields. The minimum within this class is an upper bound on the full sector minimum, not a claim that every exact Skyrmion has this separated form. The original construction is Houghton, Manton and Sutcliffe's arxiv.org/abs/hep-th/9705151.
A rotational symmetry of a rational map must satisfy R(gz)=hR(z), where g,h are the domain and target rotations written as Möbius transformations from SU(2) matrices. Since a target rotation is an isometry, this identity implies JR​(gz)=JR​(z). For R=zN, R(eiαz)=eiNαR(z), giving axial spatial symmetry accompanied by an internal rotation. This explains the axial symmetry of the degree-two toroidal ansatz.
A useful degree-four example is
R(z)=z4−23​iz2+1z4+23​iz2+1​,W(z)=83​iz(1−z4).
(28)
Its finite ramification directions are 0,±1,±i, with the sixth at infinity. They are the six coordinate-axis directions under stereographic projection, so they are the face normals of a cube. The full map has octahedral rotational equivariance: for example R(iz)=1/R(z), and the cyclic-axis generator g(z)=(iz+1)/(1−iz) obeys R(gz)=e2πi/3R(z). These generate the cube's rotational group and give a cubic angular density. The associated profile then produces the familiar cubic charge-four approximation. Symmetry of the Wronskian is a useful necessary diagnostic but is not sufficient: replacing this map by cR, c>0, leaves the same ramification directions, while the identity under z↦iz becomes cR(iz)=c2/(cR(z)), whose target transformation is a sphere rotation only when c=1. Equivariance of the entire rational map, rather than symmetry of its critical-point set alone, determines the physical rotational symmetry.
