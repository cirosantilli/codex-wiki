A Bogomolny bound expresses a static field energy as nonnegative squares plus a term fixed by the topological charge or boundary data. Setting the squares to zero gives the first-order Bogomolny equations. Their solutions minimize the energy in that sector and satisfy the second-order Euler-Lagrange field equations, although a general stationary solution need not attain the bound.
For one real scalar in one spatial dimension, take the Lagrangian density
L=21​ϕ˙​2−21​ϕx2​−21​[W′(ϕ)]2.
(1)
A finite-energy field configuration in this static sector must approach scalar-field vacua ϕ±​ with W′(ϕ±​)=0 at the two ends. Completing the square gives the square completion for a one-dimensional kink:
E=21​∫R​(ϕx​∓W′)2dx±∫R​ϕx​W′(ϕ)dx=21​∫R​(ϕx​∓W′)2dx±[W(ϕ+​)−W(ϕ−​)].
(2)
Choose the sign for which the boundary term is nonnegative. Thus
E≥∣W(ϕ+​)−W(ϕ−​)∣,ϕx​=±W′(ϕ) at equality.​
(3)
Taking the derivative of the equality equation gives ϕxx​=W′W′′=d([W′]2/2)/dϕ, the static Euler-Lagrange field equation. The boundary term is invariant under deformations keeping the asymptotic scalar-field vacua fixed; it is not a contribution from the local shape of the kink.
For a phi-four kink, let c>0, W(ϕ)=c2ϕ−ϕ3/3, and select the sector ϕ−​=−c, ϕ+​=c. Then W′=c2−ϕ2, and the increasing Bogomolny equation is ϕx​=c2−ϕ2. Separating variables yields
2c1​logc−ϕc+ϕ​=x−X,ϕK​(x)=ctanh(c(x−X)),M=34c3​.​
(4)
The integration constant X is the translational collective coordinate. Directly, ϕK′​=c2sech2(c(x−X)) and its energy density is c4sech4(c(x−X)), whose integral is 4c3/3. The decreasing antikink has the reversed boundary values, profile −ϕK​, and the same energy. A Lorentz boost produces the exact uniformly moving kink ctanh[cγ(x−X0​−vt)], with Lorentz factor γ=(1−v2)−1/2 and energy γM.
In two spatial dimensions, choose the Abelian Higgs model at critical coupling, with Di​=∂i​−iAi​, B=∂1​A2​−∂2​A1​, and energy
E=∫R2​[21​B2+21​(∣D1​ϕ∣2+∣D2​ϕ∣2)+81​(1−∣ϕ∣2)2]d2x.
(5)
The gauge covariant derivative is used throughout this normalization. Integration by parts, using [D1​,D2​]=−iB, gives ∫∣Di​ϕ∣2=∫∣(D1​+iD2​)ϕ∣2+∫B∣ϕ∣2, with a vanishing boundary divergence for the decaying vortex fields. Combining this identity with the magnetic and potential terms gives the Bogomolny square completion for an Abelian Higgs vortex:
E=∫[21​∣(D1​+iD2​)ϕ∣2+21​(B−21−∣ϕ∣2​)2]d2x+21​∫Bd2x.
(6)
Finite-energy field configurations have ∣ϕ∣→1 at infinity, and Di​ϕ→0 makes the magnetic flux equal to the phase winding 2πN. For N>0,
E≥πN,(D1​+iD2​)ϕ=0,B=21−∣ϕ∣2​.​
(7)
These are the Bogomolny vortex equations for an Abelian Higgs vortex. Opposite signs give antivortices and the bound E≥π∣N∣. The coefficient π follows from the explicit energy normalization above; other conventions can give 2π. Static solutions of fixed positive vortex number have equal energy independent of their positions, giving the Abelian Higgs vortex moduli space used for slow dynamics.
