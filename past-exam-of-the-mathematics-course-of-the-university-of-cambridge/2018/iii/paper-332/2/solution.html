Let w be downward Darcy flux, measured per unit total horizontal area. Neglect the resistance of the displaced phase, capillary pressure, and horizontal subsurface flow. Taking atmospheric pressure as zero, the upper end of the saturated column has pressure ρgh and its advancing lower end has pressure zero. Across a column of depth l, Darcy law therefore gives
w=μk​(lρgh​+ρg)=νgk​(1+lh​).
(10)
The pressure head from the overlying layer supplies h/l, and gravity supplies one. Because the new liquid fills a pore volume ϕdl per unit area, the vertical imbibition under a draining liquid layer obeys ϕl˙=w. Volume conservation gives h+ϕl=h0​, so, until the surface layer is exhausted,
l˙=ϕνgk​(lh0​​+1−ϕ),h=h0​−ϕl,0<l<h0​/ϕ​.

The divergent initial Darcy flux is an idealization of sudden contact with a dry continuum; it cannot describe penetration below the pore scale.
For the spreading surface layer, measure z upwards from the substrate. A thin, slow gravity current has hydrostatic pressure p=ρg(h−z) and a leading horizontal viscous balance νuzz​=ghx​. A no-slip boundary condition at z=0 and a stress-free boundary condition at z=h give
u=νg​hx​(2z2​−hz),q=∫0h​udz=−3νg​h3hx​.

This lubrication gravity-current flux drives horizontal spreading down the surface-pressure gradient. Surface volume conservation is ht​+qx​=−w, while conservation in the pores is ϕlt​=w. Combining these balances gives the deep-substrate drainage of a gravity current model
ht​−3νg​(h3hx​)x​=−νgk​(1+lh​)=−ϕlt​​.
(15)
Drainage transfers liquid from the surface layer into the substrate. Indeed (h+ϕl)t​+qx​=0.
For definiteness take a one-sided current on 0<x<xN​(t), supplied at x=0. The imposed volume is per unit span, so V0​ has dimensions of area. The boundary conditions and volume conservation constraint are

(16)
The source starts from zero injected volume. Initially dry points acquire l>0 only when the advancing current reaches them; the formula h/l is not applied outside that region. At the advancing nose these are limiting conditions for a singular drainage equation. For a source feeding two identical sides, each side has half the volume and inlet flux; only the corresponding numerical prefactors change. The integral and inlet-flux conditions are equivalent once the local balance and nose conditions hold.
The physical approximations are lubrication theory, negligible inertia, slowly varying surface height, small pore-scale Reynolds number, and negligible capillary entry pressure. Treating the substrate as vertically draining columns also requires its lateral flow to be negligible and its depth to exceed the penetration depth. The model is valid while surface liquid remains available above each draining column.
For a similarity solution, let h and l scale as ta, and let the current length scale as tb. Since h/l then has no time dependence, drainage requires a−1=0. Balancing ht​ with the horizontal spreading term gives a−1=4a−2b, so a=1 and b=2. These exponents also give injected volume ta+b=t3, explaining the particular supply law.
