At the Gaussian fixed point, [ϕi​]=(d−2)/2, so all three quartic couplings have engineering dimension d−4[ϕi​]=4−d. Thus
α1​=α2​=α3​=ϵ​.
(23)
Take A>0 and ϵ>0, as in the perturbative epsilon expansion. Define x=Ag1​/ϵ, y=Ag2​/ϵ, z=Aλ/ϵ. The renormalization-group fixed point equations become
x−36x2−z2=0,y−36y2−z2=0,z[1−8z−12(x+y)]=0.
(24)
If z=0, each of x,y is independently 0 or 1/36, giving four renormalization-group fixed points. If z=0, subtracting the first two equations gives
(x−y)[1−36(x+y)]=0.
(25)
The branch x+y=1/36 forces z=1/12; inserting this in the first equation yields a repeated root x=y=1/72. Hence every nonzero-z solution has x=y. Then z=1/8−3x, and
45x2−47​x+641​=0
(26)
has roots x=1/40 and x=1/72. The complete list of six coupled Ising fixed points near four dimensions, in coordinates (g1​,g2​,λ), is
Aϵ​⎩⎨⎧​(0,0,0),(361​,0,0),(0,361​,0),(361​,361​,0),(401​,401​,201​),(721​,721​,121​)​⎭⎬⎫​​.
(27)
The internal symmetries of these renormalization-group fixed points are:
  • The Gaussian fixed point has an O(2) orthogonal group acting on the two identical free massless fields.
  • (ϵ/(36A),0,0) is one Ising model Wilson-Fisher fixed point and one free field. Its linear internal symmetry is Z2​×Z2​; the two sectors cannot be exchanged. The point (0,ϵ/(36A),0) has the same symmetry with the roles reversed.
  • (ϵ/(36A),ϵ/(36A),0) describes two identical decoupled Ising models. It additionally permits field exchange, giving (Z2​×Z2​)⋊S2​, the eight-element dihedral group D4​.
  • (ϵ/(40A),ϵ/(40A),ϵ/(20A)) has λ=2g1​=2g2​. Its interaction is g(ϕ12​+ϕ22​)2, so it is the O(N) model Wilson-Fisher fixed point with N=2 and O(2) symmetry.
  • (ϵ/(72A),ϵ/(72A),ϵ/(12A)) has λ=6g1​=6g2​. Its symmetry is again the eight-element dihedral group D4​, not O(2).
The last point displays the field-rotation equivalence of decoupled Ising theories. With the orthogonal transformation ψ±​=(ϕ1​±ϕ2​)/2​,
g(ϕ14​+ϕ24​+6ϕ12​ϕ22​)=2g(ψ+4​+ψ−4​),2g=36Aϵ​.
(28)
It is the same pair of decoupled Ising models written in fields rotated by 45∘, but remains a distinct coordinate solution of the stated renormalization-group beta functions. Here D4​ denotes the dihedral group of order eight; an alternative convention calls it D8​. We have described linear internal transformations preserving the gradient energy; free massless sectors also have constant scalar-field shift symmetries. At ϵ=0, the six coordinates coalesce at the Gaussian fixed point.
