Use the meridian of a knot μ and Seifert longitude λ of the unknot on its boundary torus. The unknot's knot exterior is a solid torus whose disk-bounding curve is λ. A genus-one Heegaard diagram is therefore
Σ=R2/Z2,α=λ=(0,1),β=5μ+3λ=(5,3).​
(1)
Here the horizontal coordinate is μ and the vertical coordinate is λ. The drawing uses a small translate of α to avoid intersections on the edge of the square; opposite edges are identified. The algebraic intersection number of curves on an oriented surface has absolute value five.
Starting with Σ×[0,1], attach a three-dimensional two-handle along α×{0}, using its surface framing, and cap the resulting sphere with a three-handle. This gives the solid torus on that side. Attach a two-handle along β×{1}, again using the surface framing, and cap its sphere with a three-handle. The second solid torus has its disk-bounding curve identified with 5μ+3λ, so the resulting oriented lens space is precisely the specified 5/3 Dehn filling.

The elliptization theorem says that a closed oriented three-manifold with finite fundamental group has spherical geometry. When the fundamental group is a cyclic group, it is a lens space. Consequently the candidates are L(5,q) with q∈{1,2,3,4}, using the same surgery orientation convention throughout.
The oriented classification of lens spaces says that L(p,q) and L(p,q′) are related by an orientation-preserving homeomorphism exactly when q′≡q±1(modp). Since 1−1=1, 4−1=4, and 2−1=3 modulo five, the complete list is
L(5,1),L(5,2)≅L(5,3),L(5,4).​
(3)
These are three distinct oriented homeomorphism classes. Reversing orientation interchanges L(5,1) and L(5,4); the class containing L(5,2) and L(5,3) admits an orientation-reversing homeomorphism. The elliptization theorem and the stated oriented classification of lens spaces are the general classification results used here.

Reflection through the projection plane reverses the orientation of S3 and exchanges the overpassing and underpassing strands at every crossing. Thus switching all crossings gives the mirror of a link, with the component orientations transported by the reflection.
Define W(L)(q)=V(L)(q−1). Reflection exchanges D−​ with D+​ and preserves the oriented smoothing. Apply the printed skein relation to the reflected triple at parameter q−1:
q−1W(D+​)−qW(D−​)=(q−1/2−q1/2)W(D0​).

Let H+​ be the positively oriented Hopf link obtained by closing a two-strand braid with two positive crossings. Switching one crossing gives the two-component unlink; the oriented smoothing gives the unknot. The printed skein relation gives
qV(U2​)−q−1V(H+​)=q1/2−q−1/2,V(H+​)=q5/2+q1/2.
(12)
Represent the right-handed trefoil knot by the closure of the two-strand braid with three positive crossings. Switching one crossing gives the unknot, while its oriented smoothing gives H+​. Consequently
q−q−1V(T+​)=(q1/2−q−1/2)(q5/2+q1/2),
(13)
so the two Jones polynomials are
V(T+​)=q+q3−q4,V(T−​)=q−1+q−3−q−4.​
(14)
The second expression follows from the mirror of a link identity. They are unequal Laurent polynomials, so invariance under link isotopy proves that the right-handed and left-handed trefoils are not isotopic.

Label the four crossings A,B,C,D in top, left-middle, right-middle, bottom order. Label the bounded regions d (left), e (right), a (upper middle), b (central bigon), c (lower middle), and the unbounded region o. The drawing preserves the crossings and the orientation of the supplied figure-eight knot diagram. Its overpassing strands are the outer-left/upper-right strand at A, the upper-left/lower-central strand at B, the upper-central/lower-right strand at C, and the lower-left/outer-right strand at D.
Here is the Dehn presentation Heegaard diagram specified by this drawing. Replace the underlying four-valent planar graph G by a small three-dimensional regular neighborhood N(G) and put Σ=∂N(G). Since G has four vertices and eight edges, Σ has genus 8−4+1=5. On Σ, take the five α curves bordering the bounded faces, pushed slightly onto the boundary of N(G). They bound a complete disk system in the complementary handlebody S3∖intN(G); their labels are αd​,αe​,αa​,αb​,αc​.
The four β curves bound the disks dual to the four short crossing tunnels in N(G)∖intνK8​. Lift the crossing circles shown in red onto the upper and lower sheets of Σ, routing them along the corresponding face boundaries; the crossing's overpassing strand determines the lift. Orient the α disk normals and the β curves so that their signed successive intersections with the α curves are
βA​βB​βC​βD​​a e−1 d−1a d−1 c b−1b a−1 e c−1c d−1 e−1.​
(15)
At A,D the missing fourth corner is the unbounded region o, whose generator is omitted. This sheet-lifting prescription, together with the projected graph, specifies the curves on the Heegaard surface; the red circles alone are only their local projections.
To recover the knot exterior from Σ×I, attach five two-handles along the α curves on one side and cap the resulting sphere with a three-handle. On the other side attach four two-handles along the β curves. Compressing that side leaves the boundary torus of the knot exterior, which is retained. In particular, the counts are five α compressions and four β compressions, rather than two complete genus-five disk systems.

Use the region labels and curve orientations from part (a). A Dehn presentation of a knot group generator goes from the basepoint above the projection plane through its region and returns through the unbounded region. Equivalently it is dual to the correspondingly labeled α disk of the Heegaard diagram. A crossing relator records the signed intersections of its β curve with these disks. Setting o=1 gives
π1​(X8​)=⟨d,e,a,b,c∣ae−1d−1, ad−1cb−1, ba−1ec−1, cd−1e−1⟩.​
(16)
For example, the overpassing strand at A equates the meridian of a knot words d and ae−1; at B it equates ad−1 and bc−1. The other two crossings equate ba−1 with ce−1 and cd−1 with e.
Choose the region-generator direction so that e is the shown meridian of a knot μ. The Alexander numbering of the regions, starting from zero outside and increasing by one on crossing an oriented strand from right to left, is
φ(d)=φ(e)=φ(μ),φ(a)=φ(c)=2φ(μ),φ(b)=3φ(μ).​
(17)
One can also obtain this directly by abelianization of the relators: a=d+e, c=d+e, b=a−d+c, and b−a+e−c=0. The last two imply d=e, and the resulting first homology group is freely generated by e. Thus H1​(X8​;Z)≅Z, with no extra torsion.

Write V=MS2​(p∗/q,∗). Filling one end of S1 times an annulus gives a solid torus, so V is a solid torus. The prescribed algebraic intersection number of curves on an oriented surface convention gives f~​2​⋅h~2​=1. Hence
μ2​⋅λ2​=(−q∗)q−(−p)p∗=pp∗−qq∗=1.
(24)
In H1​(V;Z), the first Dehn filling imposes p∗f−qh1​=0, while h1​+h2​=0. Therefore
ι2∗​(λ2​)=p∗f+qh2​=0.​
(25)
The class λ2​ is primitive because gcd(p∗,q)=1, a consequence of pp∗−qq∗=1. It is therefore the disk-bounding meridian of a solid torus of V. Since μ2​ meets it once, Dehn filling V along μ2​ glues two solid torus pieces with disk boundaries meeting once. This is the standard genus-one Heegaard splitting of S3, so
MS2​(p∗/q,−q∗/p)≅S3.​
(26)
To identify the regular fiber geometrically, put it on their common boundary torus. Invert the displayed basis change:
f~​2​=qμ2​+pλ2​.​
(27)
Here μ2​ bounds a disk in the newly attached solid torus and λ2​ bounds a disk in V. Thus the curve has winding numbers p and q in the two complementary solid torus pieces. Both coefficients are positive and gcd(p,q)=1, so it is the positive (p,q) torus knot. This identifies the torus knot directly from the genus-one splitting, without invoking a theorem specifically about torus knots.

All classes in the nontrivial homological equalities are taken in H1​(X(p,q)​;Z), where X(p,q)​=MS2​(p∗/q,−q∗/p,∗). The two internal gluing torus components still have inclusion maps into this knot exterior. Interpreting the maps instead as maps into the closed S3 would make every displayed class zero.
Before the two Dehn fillings, H1​(S1×pair of pants;Z) has generators f,h1​,h2​,h3​ and relation h1​+h2​+h3​=0. The Dehn fillings add p∗f−qh1​=0 and −q∗f−ph2​=0. Put m=−h3​. Eliminating the relations using pp∗−qq∗=1 gives
H1​(X(p,q)​;Z)=Z⟨m⟩,f=pqm,h1​=pp∗m,h2​=−qq∗m.​
(28)
For completeness, the three relation rows in generators (f,h1​,h2​) are (p∗,−q,0), (−q∗,0,−p), (0,1,1) when computing the quotient by m. Their determinant is pp∗−qq∗=1, so m really generates the entire first homology group, and no finite torsion or index is hidden in the elimination.
Orient each exceptional-fiber longitude by μi​⋅λi​=1. In the boundary basis (mi​,f~​i​)=(−h~i​,f~​i​), write μi​=αi​mi​+βi​f~​i​ and λi​=ui​mi​+vi​f~​i​, where αi​vi​−βi​ui​=1. Then
f~​i​=−ui​μi​+αi​λi​.
(29)
The filled meridian of a solid torus maps to zero, and (α1​,α2​)=(q,p), yielding
f=pqι3∗​(m)=qι1∗​(λ1​)=pι2∗​(λ2​).​
(30)
Adding multiples of μi​ to λi​ has no effect. The word “any” therefore means any longitude with this compatible orientation; negating a longitude would negate the corresponding equality.
To compute Turaev torsion, use the general product formula τ(S1×F)≐(1−[f])−χ(F) and multiplicativity under gluing along torus components. For a pair of pants, χ(F)=−1, so the unfilled piece contributes 1−[f]. The two filling solid torus pieces contribute (1−[λ1​])−1 and (1−[λ2​])−1. With t=[m], the equalities above give [f]=tpq, [λ1​]=tp, and [λ2​]=tq. Therefore
τ(X(p,q)​)≐(1−tp)(1−tq)1−tpq​.​
(31)
Here ≐ allows the unit ±tn: a fully refined Turaev torsion requires an Euler structure and a homology orientation, neither of which the statement specifies. The displayed rational function is the representative whose expansion at t=0 starts with 1, equivalently the standard nonnegative-exponent normalization.

The fiber monodromy is periodic when isotopy is allowed to move the boundary, in the sense of a periodic surface homeomorphism. Here is a justification from the Seifert fibered space structure, rather than a special formula about torus knots. Its base is a disk with cone-point orders p,q. The fiber Seifert surface is an incompressible surface because its inclusion in the mapping torus is injective on fundamental groups. It is also a boundary-incompressible surface: lift a proposed boundary-compressing disk to the infinite cyclic cover Σ×R and project to Σ. This would homotope an essential arc of Σ into ∂Σ, contradicting its essentiality. The classification of incompressible surfaces in Seifert fibered spaces therefore applies. A vertical surface in a Seifert fibered space has zero intersection with a regular Seifert fiber, whereas the fibration class evaluates on f=pqm as pq. Thus the Seifert surface is isotopic to a horizontal surface in a Seifert fibered space, with pq intersections per regular orbit.
Following the oriented Seifert fibers from one intersection to the next gives a first-return homeomorphism of the horizontal topological surface. It is a representative of the fiber monodromy, and its pq-th power is the identity. It has order pq: a regular orbit meets the connected horizontal surface in a Seifert fibered space in pq cyclically ordered points. This realizes the periodic case of the Nielsen–Thurston classification theorem. It is neither an Anosov homeomorphism nor a pseudo-Anosov homeomorphism.
There is a boundary convention here. A representative fixed pointwise on the boundary is not periodic relative to the boundary: its pq-th power is a boundary Dehn twist (with sign determined by the return-map convention). Capping the boundary, or allowing it to rotate during isotopy, gives the finite-order representative intended by “periodic”. This distinguishes periodic surface type from literal finite order in the mapping class group relative to the boundary.
For any fibered knot, its Alexander module is H1​(Σ;Z) with the deck transformation given by homological monodromy, so its Alexander polynomial of a knot is det(tI−ϕ∗​) up to a unit. The general Turaev torsion relation Δ(t)≐(1−t)τ(X) and part (b) therefore give
Δ(t)≐P(t):=(1−tp)(1−tq)(1−t)(1−tpq)​.
(32)
This rational expression is a polynomial: in its factorization into cyclotomic polynomials, gcd(p,q)=1 cancels all denominator factors. It is monic, has constant coefficient one, and has polynomial degree pq+1−p−q=(p−1)(q−1). The characteristic polynomial has polynomial degree 2g, is monic, and has constant coefficient detϕ∗​=1, because homological monodromy preserves the intersection form. These facts remove the unit ambiguity and give
det(ϕ∗​−tI)=(1−tp)(1−tq)(1−t)(1−tpq)​,g=2(p−1)(q−1)​.​
(33)
The sign change between det(ϕ∗​−tI) and det(tI−ϕ∗​) is trivial because rankH1​(Σ)=2g is even. As a separate geometric check, the orbifold Euler characteristic of the base is 1/p+1/q−1, and its pq-sheeted horizontal surface in a Seifert fibered space has Euler characteristic χ(Σ)=p+q−pq=1−2g.

Choose a fiber monodromy representative fixing the boundary of the fiber Seifert surface pointwise. The boundary of each fiber is the Seifert longitude, precisely the slope used by zero Dehn filling. Attach the filling solid torus as D2×S1, matching ∂D2×{θ} with the boundary of the fiber at θ. The fibers cap off to closed topological surfaces Σ of the same genus g; extend the fiber monodromy across the disk by the identity. Thus
M=X(p,q)​(0)≅Mϕ​​,F={Σ×{θ}}θ∈S1​.​
(34)
This is a foliation by fibers of a bundle over S1, and every leaf is compact with genus (p−1)(q−1)/2.
Choose a point in the interior of the capping disk, which ϕ​ fixes. Its suspension is an embedded closed curve {x}×S1 in the mapping torus. It is transverse to the foliation and meets every fiber once. Hence every leaf meets a closed transversal to a foliation, which is the defining criterion for a taut foliation. Therefore the fiber foliation is taut.
