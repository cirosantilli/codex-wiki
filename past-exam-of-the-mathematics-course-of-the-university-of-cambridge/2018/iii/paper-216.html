Let P be the Markov kernel and μ its invariant probability measure. Using the convention
∥ν−μ∥TV​=supA​∣ν(A)−μ(A)∣,
(1)
geometric ergodicity means that there are r∈(0,1) and a finite measurable state-dependent prefactor M(x) such that
∥Pk(x,⋅)−μ∥TV​≤M(x)rk(k≥0).​
(2)
Here total variation distance is used, and the rate r does not depend on x. The prefactor need not be uniformly bounded; that stronger property is uniform geometric ergodicity. For a deterministic starting state, this estimate must hold at that state. If the definition is made only μ-almost everywhere, the subsequent assertions concern those starting states where the prefactor is finite. The reversible Markov chain assumption means μ(dx)P(x,dy)=μ(dy)P(y,dx).

For the planar uniform distribution with a Lebesgue measure density, assume that the compact convex set C has nonempty interior, equivalently positive area. This assumption is missing from the wording: a line segment or singleton would not have such a planar uniform density.
The hit-and-run sampler at a current point x chooses u=(cosΘ,sinΘ) with Θ uniform on [0,2π), finds the interval
I(x,u)={t:x+tu∈C}=[t−​(x,u),t+​(x,u)],
(11)
and samples T uniformly on that interval, setting x′=x+Tu. Convexity makes the intersection an interval and compactness makes it bounded.
If x is in the interior, every direction has positive chord length, and the next point is in the interior almost surely. For a definition valid at every boundary point as well, redraw the direction whenever the chord has zero length. We use this convention in the following parts. Positive-length directions have positive angular probability at every x, since an interior ball is visible from x, so the redraw terminates almost surely.
The Markov kernel has the uniform distribution on C as its invariant law, as follows from the density symmetry proved next. Repeating the step therefore produces approximate uniform samples after convergence. The boundary convention matters: if singleton chords were instead treated as holding moves, a square's corner would have a positive atom at its starting point, contradicting part (b)'s claim of an everywhere absolutely continuous kernel.

Write ℓ(x,u)=t+​(x,u)−t−​(x,u) and let
a(x)=2π1​∫02π​1{ℓ(x,uθ​)>0}​dθ>0.
(12)

The signed polar representation has Jacobian determinant ∣t∣. Each point y=x is represented twice, once with positive t and once with negative t and the opposite direction. Since the chord is the same in either representation, the hit-and-run kernel density is
p(x,y)=πa(x)ℓ(x,(y−x)/∣y−x∣)∣y−x∣1​(y=x)​

for almost every y∈C, and it is zero outside the body. Thus the transition is absolutely continuous with respect to planar Lebesgue measure. Redrawing removes the only potential zero-chord atoms.
Let D=diam(C)>0. For distinct x,y∈C, the chord contains their segment, so it has positive length, and
a(x)≤1,ℓ(x,u)≤D,∣y−x∣≤D.
(15)
Hence p(x,y)≥1/(πD2). Values on the diagonal, and on other null sets where a density is only specified almost everywhere, can be assigned this same lower bound. This gives a version satisfying
x,y∈Cinf​p(x,y)≥πD21​>0.​
(16)
For interior x,y, a(x)=a(y)=1 and the chord length is unchanged on interchanging its endpoints, so p(x,y)=p(y,x). A convex body's boundary has area zero; for example contractions about an interior point fill the interior and their areas tend to the body's area. Thus this symmetry holds almost everywhere for two uniform points, proving detailed balance and stationarity of the uniform distribution. Without positive area or the boundary convention in part (a), the literal assertion need not hold.

The geometric drift condition requires a measurable V:X→[1,∞), a small set C, constants λ∈(0,1) and b<∞, and
PV(x)≤λV(x)+b1C​(x)(x∈X).​
(17)
Smallness means that for some m≥1, ε>0, and probability measure ν, Pm(x,⋅)≥εν(⋅) for every x∈C. With irreducibility and aperiodicity, this drift-minorisation condition implies geometric ergodicity. The function V controls the chain away from the small set; inside that set the additive constant allows unrestricted bounded drift.

Let A=Leb(C)>0 and let μ be the uniform distribution on the body. The hit-and-run kernel density lower bound implies
P(x,B)≥πD2Leb(B∩C)​≥εμ(B),ε=2πD2A​∈(0,1).
(18)
For the last range, C lies in a radius-D disc about any of its points, so A≤πD2 and ε≤1/2. This global Doeblin condition proves irreducibility with respect to μ and aperiodicity. The entire state space is a small set; V=1, λ=1/2, and b=1/2 satisfy the geometric drift condition.
In fact the conclusion is stronger. Write P=εΠ+(1−ε)R, where Π(x,⋅)=μ and R is the residual Markov kernel. Since μP=μ, also μR=μ. Each transition has a probability ε of resetting to stationarity, and after such a reset every subsequent distribution remains stationary. Equivalently,
x∈Csup​∥Pn(x,⋅)−μ∥TV​≤(1−ε)n.​
(19)
Thus the algorithm has uniform geometric ergodicity, using the full-dimensional and boundary conventions stated in part (a).

Write λ=σ2, v=σ02​, and a=ρ​. We keep the model's printed coefficient σ2Zi​ literally. Its latent log odds can be written
θi​=xiT​β+λZi​+ηi​,ηi​∼iidN(0,v).
(20)
The independence of the θi​ is conditional on Z: they are not marginally independent when the random effects Zi​ are correlated.
Holding the other predictors and the random-effect realization fixed, increasing predictor j by one shifts the latent log odds by βj​, so it multiplies the conditional odds of a late departure by eβj​. For a binary snow indicator, this compares the snow and no-snow days with other predictors held fixed. This is a conditional odds ratio, not generally the same as a marginal odds ratio after integrating the random effects.
Randomizing θi​ allows unmeasured daily conditions to change the success probability beyond the systematic linear predictor. In this logistic-normal regression with autoregressive random effects, let Pi​=logit−1(θi​) and pˉ​i​=E[Pi​]. The law of total variance gives
Var(Yi​)=ni​pˉ​i​(1−pˉ​i​)+ni​(ni​−1)Var(Pi​),
(21)
so the mixture allows overdispersion beyond a binomial distribution with fixed probability. The correlated part also allows related outcomes on nearby days.
The stationary autoregressive process of order one has Var(Zi​)=1 and Cov(Zi​,Zj​)=a∣i−j∣. Hence
Var(θi​)=σ4+σ02​,Cov(θi​,θj​)=σ4ρ∣i−j∣/2(i=j).​
(22)
The σ02​ term is unstructured daily variability, whereas σ4 is the temporally correlated component under the printed parameterization. Larger ρ gives greater persistence: the lag-h correlation of the latent log odds is σ4ρh/2/(σ4+σ02​).
The PDF itself prints both the coefficient σ2Zi​ and the requested combination σ2+σ02​. They are inconsistent as a marginal variance. If the intended coefficient were σZi​, the variance and off-diagonal covariance would instead be σ2+σ02​ and σ2ρ∣i−j∣/2. The prior's argument list also repeats σ2; its displayed density indicates the intended second variance parameter is σ02​.
There is a further substantive issue with that improper prior. After integrating the latent variables, the observed-data likelihood function is positive and continuous at λ=0 for fixed finite β, positive v, and ρ∈(0,1). On a compact positive-volume set of those parameters it therefore has a positive lower bound for small λ. The prior density is 1/(λv), so integrating over λ gives ∫0ε​dλ/λ=∞. Thus the stated joint posterior distribution is improper, an instance of improper posterior from a log-uniform random-effect scale prior. The requested fixed-parameter conditional laws below are nevertheless proper; their existence does not remedy the joint impropriety.

Given the parameters and Z, the coordinates are conditionally independent. Write mi​=xiT​β+σ2Zi​, v=σ02​, and let yi​ denote the observed count. Their full conditional distributions have scalar densities
qi​(t)∝exp[yi​t−ni​log(1+et)−2v(t−mi​)2​],t∈R.​
(26)
Each is proper: the binomial distribution likelihood factor is bounded, and the remaining factor is a proper normal distribution density.
One completely specified exact algorithm is rejection sampling from N(mi​,v). Define
Hi​(t)=(1+et)ni​eyi​t​,Mi​=supt​Hi​(t).
(27)
For 0<yi​<ni​,
Mi​=(ni​yi​​)yi​(1−ni​yi​​)ni​−yi​;
(28)
for yi​=0 or yi​=ni​, take Mi​=1, and for ni​=0 take Hi​=Mi​=1. Propose t∼N(mi​,v) and accept with probability Hi​(t)/Mi​, repeating until acceptance. The accepted density is proportional to the proposal times Hi​, exactly qi​. Its acceptance probability is positive, so it terminates almost surely, although it can be inefficient.
An efficient exact alternative is adaptive rejection sampling. The log density has
ℓi′​(t)=yi​−ni​1+etet​−vt−mi​​,ℓi′′​(t)=−ni​(1+et)2et​−v1​<0.
(29)
Thus it is a log-concave probability density. Choose one tangent point with positive derivative in the left tail and one with negative derivative in the right tail; these exist because the Gaussian term dominates there. Their tangent upper hull gives an integrable piecewise exponential rejection envelope. Sample that envelope, accept using the target-to-envelope ratio, and add evaluated points to improve it. Concavity guarantees the upper bound, so accepted samples remain exact. Sample each coordinate independently by either method; a finite number of Metropolis steps would not provide the requested exact draws.

Q(θ∣θ0​)=∫q0​(z)logpθ​(Y,z)dz.

Let ϕd​(z)=(2π)−d/2exp(−∥z∥2/2) be the standard multivariate normal density. Independence gives the proposal distribution density
q(x,y)=∫ϕd​(y−Ax)ν(dA).
(39)
For an orthogonal matrix A,
∥y−Ax∥=∥ATy−x∥=∥x−ATy∥.
(40)
The isotropic Gaussian density is unchanged by this transformation. Since AT has the same distribution as A,
q(x,y)=∫ϕd​(x−ATy)ν(dA)=∫ϕd​(x−Ay)ν(dA)=q(y,x).
(41)
Thus this is an orthogonal-mixture Metropolis proposal with a symmetric density; inversion invariance, rather than any assumption of a uniform distribution on orthogonal matrices, is what is needed.
With α(x,y)=min{1,π(y)/π(x)}, the accepted off-diagonal transitions satisfy
π(x)q(x,y)α(x,y)=q(x,y)min{π(x),π(y)}=π(y)q(y,x)α(y,x).
(42)
The rejection part is supported on the diagonal and satisfies detailed balance automatically. Values of the acceptance rule at starting points where π(x)=0 can be specified separately; they do not affect stationarity under π. Therefore the Metropolis–Hastings algorithm kernel is reversible with invariant density π, proving
π is a stationary distribution.​
(43)
