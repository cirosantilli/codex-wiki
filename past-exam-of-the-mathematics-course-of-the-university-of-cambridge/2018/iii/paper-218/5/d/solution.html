Apply EM for a missing observation in a Gaussian AR1 process, starting from θold=(μold,ϕold,(σ2)old) in the stationary parameter space. The Gaussian AR1 bridge gives the E-step quantities
m=μold+1+(ϕold)2ϕold(x1​+x3​−2μold)​,v=1+(ϕold)2(σ2)old​.
(38)
Thus E[X2​∣xobs​]=m, E[X22​∣xobs​]=m2+v, and E[X1​X2​∣xobs​]=x1​m, E[X2​X3​∣xobs​]=mx3​.
Let x2∗​=m and xt∗​=xt​ otherwise. Keeping m,v fixed throughout the M-step, define
R(μ,ϕ)=(1−ϕ2)(x1​−μ)2+∑t=231​{(xt∗​−μ)−ϕ(xt−1∗​−μ)}2+(1+ϕ2)v.
(39)
The last term comes from the missing observation's conditional variance in the two adjacent innovation squares. The expected complete-data log-likelihood is
Q(μ,ϕ,σ2∣θold)=C−231​logσ2+21​log(1−ϕ2)−2σ2R(μ,ϕ)​.
(40)
For fixed ϕ, differentiating the quadratic in μ gives
μ∗​(ϕ)=(1−ϕ2)+30(1−ϕ)2(1−ϕ2)x1​+(1−ϕ)∑t=231​(xt∗​−ϕxt−1∗​)​.
(41)
Also the maximizing variance is σ2=R(μ,ϕ)/31. The M-step is therefore a one-dimensional profile likelihood maximization:
ϕnew∈arg∣ϕ∣<1max​{21​log(1−ϕ2)−231​logR(μ∗​(ϕ),ϕ)},μnew=μ∗​(ϕnew),(σ2)new=31R(μnew,ϕnew)​.​
(42)
Repeat the E- and M-steps until the observed-data likelihood and parameters stabilize. Exact M-steps make that likelihood nondecreasing; different initializations help detect different local optima, and convergence alone is not a guarantee of a global maximum. At an interior local maximum, impute the missing temperature by its conditional mean m evaluated at the fitted parameters, retaining v as its conditional uncertainty. Replacing X2​ by m and discarding v would not implement the expectation-maximization algorithm.
