For the First Chern class identity, use finite-dimensional classifying maps. A complex line bundle on a compact Hausdorff space embeds in a finite-dimensional trivial vector bundle, so it is a pullback vector bundle of the tautological bundle on some Complex projective space. For the two complex line bundles, the universal tensor product of vector bundles is classified by the map
m:CPN×CPM⟶CP(N+1)(M+1)−1,([v],[w])⟼[v⊗w].
(1)
Its restriction to either factor pulls the tautological bundle back to the tautological bundle on that factor. The degree-two cohomology of the product is the direct sum of the degree-two cohomology of its factors, so these two restrictions determine its First Chern class. Pulling back to X proves
c1​(L1​⊗L2​)=c1​(L1​)+c1​(L2​).​
(2)
This proves the integral identity, including possible torsion elements in the cohomology of X; an argument using only curvature forms would not detect those torsion elements.
The splitting principle for complex vector bundles says that for a vector bundle E→X there is an iterated projective bundle p:F(E)→X for which p∗ is injective on integral cohomology, and p∗E is a direct sum of complex line bundles. The same assertion holds with rational coefficients, and a common such space can split any finite collection of vector bundles. The injectivity follows at each stage from the projective bundle formula for complex vector bundles; a Hermitian metric splits the resulting filtration into a direct sum.
Write xi​=c1​(Li​) for the Chern roots of the pulled-back vector bundle. Define the degree-2j component of its Chern character by
ch0​(E)=rankE,chj​(E)=j!1​∑i​xij​,ch(E)=∑j≥0​chj​(E).
(3)
The Fundamental theorem of symmetric polynomials expresses each power-sum symmetric polynomial ∑i​xij​ uniquely as an integral polynomial in the elementary symmetric polynomials, which are the Chern classes of E. Thus the definition makes sense on X itself, without choosing actual Chern roots there. On a compact Hausdorff space, each vector bundle is classified by a finite-dimensional Grassmannian; its high-degree Chern character components therefore vanish. Hence this defines a class in the direct sum H2∗(X;Q) even if X has no finite dimension. On more general spaces an infinite Chern character is instead interpreted in the product of the even-degree cohomology groups.
On a common splitting space, the Chern roots of a direct sum are the combined root lists, whereas the First Chern class identity makes the Chern roots of a tensor product of vector bundles the pairwise sums xi​+yj​. Consequently
ch(E⊕F)ch(E⊗F)​=i∑​exi​+j∑​eyj​,=i,j∑​exi​+yj​=(i∑​exi​)(j∑​eyj​).​
(4)
The injectivity in the splitting principle for complex vector bundles descends both identities to X. Additivity extends the Chern character to the Grothendieck group by ch([E]−[F])=ch(E)−ch(F). Multiplicativity and ch(1)=1 then show that the Chern character is a unital ring homomorphism.
For Complex K-theory of complex projective space, let γ be the tautological bundle, put t=1−[γ], and set h=−c1​(γ). The cohomology ring of complex projective space is Z[h]/(hn+1). The CW filtration gives a cofibration
CPn−1⟶CPn⟶S2n.
(5)
The K-theory six-term exact sequence and Complex K-theory of a sphere give, inductively,
K−1(CPn)=0,0⟶Z{bn​}⟶K0(CPn)⟶K0(CPn−1)⟶0.
(6)
Here bn​ is the image of a Bott element from S2n, normalized so that ch(bn​)=hn. This normalization follows from the multiplicative form of Bott periodicity: the Chern character of the degree-two Bott element is an integral generator, and multiplication yields the top-degree generator on S2n. In particular, the abelian group K0(CPn) is a free abelian group of rank n+1.
Assume inductively that 1,t,…,tn−1 form an integral basis on CPn−1. Their lifts, together with bn​, form an integral basis on CPn. Since
ch(t)=1−e−h=h−2!h2​+3!h3​−⋯,
(7)
the Chern characters of this basis are linearly independent over Q: their first nonzero degrees are 0,2,…,2n. Thus the Chern character is injective on K0(CPn). By induction tn restricts to zero on CPn−1, so tn=abn​ for an integer a. Comparing Chern characters gives a=1. Also ch(tn+1)=0, so tn+1=0. This proves the integral answer, rather than merely its rationalization:
K0(CPn)≅Z[t]/(tn+1),t=1−[γ].​
(8)
The induction begins with CP0 a point and t=0.

Let E have rank r≥1, let p:P(E)→X be its projectivization of a real vector bundle, and let λ⊂p∗E be its tautological bundle. Put u=w1​(λ)∈H1(P(E);F2​). The Projective bundle definition of Stiefel–Whitney classes starts from the isomorphism
⨁j=0r−1​H∗−j(X;F2​) ≅ ​H∗(P(E);F2​),(a0​,…,ar−1​)⟼∑j=0r−1​p∗aj​⌣uj.
(9)
In particular, p∗ is injective. Expand ur uniquely in this basis. The coefficients define the Stiefel–Whitney classes through the relation
ur+p∗w1​(E)ur−1+⋯+p∗wr​(E)=0.​
(10)
Set w0​(E)=1 and wi​(E)=0 for i>r; for a rank-zero vector bundle set w(E)=1. Uniqueness also proves naturality of the Stiefel–Whitney classes under a pullback vector bundle.
To prove the Whitney product formula for Stiefel–Whitney classes, apply the splitting principle for real vector bundles simultaneously to E and E′. Its injective pullback on mod-two cohomology follows by iterating the Projective bundle definition of Stiefel–Whitney classes. It suffices to compute for a split vector bundle E=⨁i=1r​Li​. Put xi​=w1​(Li​).
On P(E), the coordinate projections of the tautological inclusion λ↪p∗E give sections of the real line bundles λ∗⊗p∗Li​ with no common zero. The mod-two Euler class of a real line bundle is its first Stiefel–Whitney class, and the First Stiefel–Whitney class of a tensor product of real line bundles gives u+p∗xi​. Multiplication of Euler classes under a direct sum, and vanishing of the Euler class of a vector bundle with a nowhere-zero section, therefore give
∏i=1r​(u+p∗xi​)=0.
(11)
Comparing with the unique monic relation defining the Stiefel–Whitney classes shows that wj​(E) is the jth elementary symmetric polynomial in the xi​. Thus w(E)=∏i​(1+xi​). A direct sum joins the two lists, so injectivity of the splitting pullback gives
w(E⊕E′)=w(E)w(E′),wk​(E⊕E′)=i+j=k∑​wi​(E)⌣wj​(E′).​
(12)
For the immersion obstruction, use the notation g=(f,f): the printed f×f here means the map from one copy of Real projective space into the product. Write x=w1​(γR​) and let z be the mod-two reduction of c1​(γC∗​). The mod-two cohomology ring of real projective space and the cohomology ring of complex projective space give
H∗(RP2k+1;F2​)=F2​[x]/(x2k+2),H∗(CPk;F2​)=F2​[z]/(zk+1),∣x∣=1,∣z∣=2.
(13)
Nontriviality in degree two forces f∗z=x2. Necessarily k≥1, since the degree-two cohomology of CP0 vanishes.
The standard tangent bundle identities for Real projective space and Complex projective space are
TRP2k+1⊕R≅(2k+2)γR​,TCPk⊕C≅(k+1)γC∗​.
(14)
For the underlying real vector bundle of a complex line bundle, its total Stiefel–Whitney class is 1+c1​mod2. Hence
w(TRP2k+1)=(1+x)2k+2,w(TCPk)=(1+z)k+1.
(15)
If g is homotopic to an immersion j, its normal bundle ν has rank 4k−(2k+1)=2k−1, and
TRP2k+1⊕ν≅j∗T(CPk×CPk).
(16)
The Whitney product formula for Stiefel–Whitney classes and homotopy invariance of cohomology yield
w(ν)=(1+x)2k+2(1+x2)2k+2​=(1+x)2k+2.
(17)
The division is legitimate in the truncated cohomology ring, since 1+x is a unit. The degree-2k component is
w2k​(ν)=(2k2k+2​)x2k=(k+1)(2k+1)x2k=(k+1)x2k.
(18)
It must vanish because 2k>rankν, while x2k=0. Therefore k must be odd.

All Topological K-theory groups below are complex and are indexed modulo two using Bott periodicity. For n≥1, Complex K-theory of a sphere gives
n evenn odd​K0(Sn)Z⊕ZZ​K−1(Sn)0Z​​​
(19)
For an even-dimensional sphere, choose a generator u of Reduced topological K-theory. Its square vanishes: the Chern character sends u to a top-degree cohomology class, whose square is zero, and the Chern character is injective on this torsion-free abelian group. Thus the ring answers are
K0(S2m)≅Z[u]/(u2)(m≥1),K0(S2m+1)≅Z.​
(20)
The exceptional zero-dimensional sphere consists of two points: K0(S0)=Z×Z with coordinatewise multiplication and K−1(S0)=0. In particular its reduced generator is idempotent rather than square-zero.
For the Euler characteristic identity, induct over the cells of a finite CW complex. The starting CW complex consisting of v vertices has K0≅Zv, K−1=0, and χ=v. If X is obtained from A by attaching one d-dimensional cell, the quotient is Sd, and the K-theory six-term exact sequence is
K0(Sd)⟶K0(X)⟶K0(A)⟶K−1(Sd)⟶K−1(X)⟶K−1(A)⟶K0(Sd).
(21)
These abelian groups are finitely generated by induction. Tensoring the exact sequence with Q preserves exactness. The alternating sum of dimensions in a cyclic six-term exact sequence is zero, so
rankK0(X)−rankK−1(X)​=rankK0(A)−rankK−1(A)+rankK0(Sd)−rankK−1(Sd)=χ(A)+(−1)d=χ(X).​
(22)
Therefore
χ(X)=rankK0(X)−rankK−1(X).​
(23)
There is a genuine convention issue in the nilpotence assertion. With the usual definition of Reduced topological K-theory as the kernel of restriction to one basepoint, the assertion needs X to be connected. For X=S0 based at its first point, (0,1) lies in K0(X) and satisfies (0,1)m=(0,1) for every m≥1. Thus it is not a nilpotent element. For an arbitrary finite CW complex, the correct assertion uses the rank map in topological K-theory on every connected component:
I(X)=ker(K0(X)⟶H0(X;Z)).
(24)
For a connected space, I(X)=K0(X).
Here is an induction proving the corrected assertion. On the vertices, I(X)=0. For a positive-dimensional cell attachment A⊂X, the ideal J=ker(K0(X)→K0(A)) is square-zero. Indeed, lift two elements of J to relative topological K-theory K0(X,A). Their product is induced by the reduced diagonal
X/A=Sd⟶Sd∧Sd=S2d.
(25)
This map is null-homotopic, since d<2d and S2d is (2d−1)-connected. The relative product in topological K-theory is therefore zero, giving J2=0. If a∈I(X), its restriction belongs to I(A), so induction gives (a∣A​)m=0 for some m. Then am∈J and a2m=0. We have proved every Topological K-theory class of rank zero on every connected component is a nilpotent element, and hence the requested result for connected X.

For the K-theory transfer of a finite covering, let V→Y be a vector bundle and define
(p!​V)x​=⨁y∈p−1(x)​Vy​.
(26)
On an evenly covered neighborhood the n sheets identify this with a direct sum of n local vector bundles. On overlaps the identifications permute the sheets and apply the transition maps of V, so these local descriptions glue to a vector bundle on X. This operation respects direct sums, hence extends to a homomorphism of Grothendieck groups
p!​:K0(Y)⟶K0(X),p!​([V]−[W])=[p!​V]−[p!​W].​
(27)
It obeys the projection formula for the K-theory transfer:
p!​(p∗a⋅b)=a⋅p!​b.
(28)
For actual vector bundles, this follows fiberwise by distributing the tensor product of vector bundles over the direct sum; it then extends to their Grothendieck groups.
Let P=p!​(1Y​) be the permutation vector bundle of the covering. It has rank n on every connected component, but need not be a trivial vector bundle; in particular one cannot replace p!​p∗ by multiplication by n integrally. Instead write
[P]=n+η,η∈I(X).
(29)
The componentwise nilpotence result from the preceding solution gives ηN=0 for some N. In the localization of a ring K0(X)⊗Z[1/n], the finite geometric series
(n+η)−1=n1​∑j=0N−1​(−nη​)j
(30)
is an inverse. The projection formula for the K-theory transfer gives
p!​p∗(a)=[P]a=(n+η)a.
(31)
Thus p∗(a)=0 implies a=0 after inverting n. More explicitly, (n+η)−1p!​ is a left inverse, proving
p∗:K0(X)⊗Z[1/n]↪K0(Y)⊗Z[1/n].​
(32)

Use the standard complex K-theory Thom class convention. For a rank-r complex vector bundle, its K-theory Euler class is the pullback of the K-theory Thom class along the zero section, namely
eK​(E)=λ−1​(E∗)=j=0∑r​(−1)j[ΛjE∗]∈K0(X).​
(33)
Here Λj denotes an exterior power, and a Hermitian metric identifies the dual bundle with the conjugate vector bundle. Choosing λ−1​(E) instead changes the K-theory Euler class by the unit (−1)r[detE], so its kernel and cokernel computations are unchanged.
For the disk bundle D(E) and sphere bundle S(E), the K-theory Thom class identifies Ki(D(E),S(E)) with Ki(X). The exact sequence of this pair becomes the K-theory Gysin sequence of a sphere bundle:
⋯⟶Ki(X) eK​(E)⋅ ​Ki(X)π∗​Ki(S(E))∂​Ki+1(X) eK​(E)⋅ ​Ki+1(X)⟶⋯.​
(34)
The groups are two-periodic by Bott periodicity.
View Real projective space as the circle bundle of γ⊗2 over Complex projective space, where γ→CPn is the tautological bundle. Indeed, S2n+1/{±1}=RP2n+1 maps to S(γ⊗2) by [v]↦v⊗v; in each fiber squaring identifies exactly v and −v.
Use Complex K-theory of complex projective space in the form
R=K0(CPn)=Z[t]/(tn+1),t=1−[γ],K−1(CPn)=0.
(35)
The K-theory Euler class of γ⊗2 is 1−[γ]−2. Multiplying it by the unit −[γ]2 gives 1−[γ]2=2t−t2. The K-theory Gysin sequence of a sphere bundle therefore gives
K0(RP2n+1)≅R/(2t−t2),K−1(RP2n+1)≅ker((2t−t2)⋅:R→R).
(36)
Under the projection q:RP2n+1→CPn, the pullback vector bundle q∗γ is the complexification of a real vector bundle L of the tautological bundle of Real projective space: the fiber map sends [v,z] to zv. Thus t pulls back to 1−[L]. Put μ=[L]−1. The relation L⊗2≅1 gives μ2=−2μ, and tn+1=0 gives 2nμ=0. Hence
K0(RP2n+1)≅Z[μ]/(μ2+2μ,2nμ),μ=[L]−1.​
(37)
For completeness, these relations give precisely the abelian group Z⊕Z/2n. In the basis t,…,tn, the image of multiplication by 2t−t2 is generated by 2t−t2,2t2−t3,…,2tn; its relation matrix is triangular with determinant 2n, and the relations express every tj as 2j−1t. Thus there is exactly one cyclic torsion subgroup of order 2n.
If n≥1 and a=a0​+a1​t+⋯+an​tn is in the kernel, successive coefficients of (2t−t2)a give 2a0​=0, 2a1​−a0​=0, and so on, up to 2an−1​−an−2​=0. Hence a0​=⋯=an−1​=0, with an​ arbitrary. For n=0 the multiplier is zero on R=Z. In all cases,
K−1(RP2n+1)≅Z(n≥0).​
(38)
Finally let E=L⊕k with k≥1, over B=RP2n+1. Since L∗≅L, multiplicativity of the K-theory Euler class gives
eK​(E)=(1−[L])k=(−μ)k=−2k−1μ.
(39)
On K0(B)=Z⊕(Z/2n)μ, the image of multiplication by this K-theory Euler class is the subgroup generated by 2k−1μ: multiplication by an integer produces every such multiple, while multiplication by μ only adds multiples of 2kμ. Therefore
coker(eK​(E):K0(B)→K0(B))≅Z⊕Z/2min(n,k−1).
(40)
Multiplication by μ is zero on K−1(B)≅Z, since 2nμ=0 and this abelian group has no nonzero torsion elements. Thus the K-theory Gysin sequence of a sphere bundle gives a short exact sequence
0⟶Z⊕Z/2min(n,k−1)⟶K0(S(E))⟶Z⟶0.
(41)
It splits as an exact sequence of abelian groups because its quotient is a free abelian group. Consequently
K0(S(L⊕k))≅Z2⊕Z/2min(n,k−1)(n≥0, k≥1).​
(42)
The convention Z/1=0 covers n=0 and k=1. For example, when k=1 the sphere bundle is (S2n+1×S1)/{(v,z)∼(−v,−z)}, a mapping torus of the antipodal map; its Topological K-theory has no nonzero torsion elements, agreeing with the formula. If k=0 is allowed, the rank-zero vector bundle has an empty unit sphere bundle, so K0(S(E))=0 instead.
