Evaluate all derivatives at zero long perturbation, where ν=δc​/σG,s​, and write nˉ(ν) for the unperturbed abundance. The peak-background split changes the halo peak height to
νloc​=σG,s​(1+2fNL​φG,L​)δc​−δL​​=ν−δc​ν​δL​−2fNL​νφG,L​+⋯.
(3)
The chain rule in the specified bias expansion therefore gives
b10​=−δc​ν​dνdlognˉ​,b01​=−2fNL​νdνdlognˉ​=2fNL​δc​b10​​.
(4)
No explicit abundance law is specified, so these derivatives are the general answer. For example, if nˉ has the Press-Schechter halo mass function dependence νe−ν2/2, then b10​=(ν2−1)/δc​ and b01​=2fNL​(ν2−1).
For the linear long mode, the cosmological Poisson equation gives φG,L​(k)=δG,L​(k)/α(k). Consequently the deterministic galaxy bias and power spectrum are
δg​(k)=[b10​+α(k)b01​​]δG,L​(k),Pg​(k)=b102​[1+α(k)2fNL​δc​​]2Pm​(k)​.
(5)
Here Pm​ is the linear cosmological density power spectrum; stochastic tracer noise has been omitted. This scale-dependent halo bias from local non-Gaussianity is proportional to k−2 in the specified α(k)=2k2/(3H2Ωm​) convention.
For b10​=0, plot the ratio Pg​/(b102​Pm​) to separate the effect from the shape of Pm​. Positive fNL​ increases the ratio as k decreases. Negative fNL​ first suppresses it, gives a zero when α(k)=2∣fNL​∣δc​, and then makes it rise again after the effective galaxy bias changes sign. The auto-power spectrum never becomes negative. A truncation Pg​≃b102​[1+4fNL​δc​/α(k)]Pm​ is valid only when ∣2fNL​δc​/α(k)∣≪1 and must not be extrapolated through the zero. Within the deterministic expression, both signs give Pg​∝k−4Pm​ at sufficiently small k. For a nearly scale-invariant primordial spectrum, Pm​∝kns​ in the large-scale transfer limit, so this formal asymptote is kns​−4.
There is a convention issue in calling the specified abundance response an observed galaxy overdensity. The threshold response is naturally a linear Lagrangian halo bias. If b10​ instead denotes the physical linear Eulerian halo bias, the Lagrangian-to-Eulerian linear bias relation adds one to the density coefficient: b01​=2fNL​δc​(b10​−1), and Pg​=[b10​+b01​/α]2Pm​. The boxed formulas follow the abundance expansion explicitly supplied in the paper. The Newtonian treatment also cannot be extrapolated beyond its physical large-scale domain without relativistic projection effects.
