A photon scatters in an interval dη with rate factor Γdη and then reaches us without another scattering with probability E, so gdη is its probability of last scattering in that interval. For an optically thick initial epoch,
∫ηi​η0​​g(η)dη=1−e−τ(ηi​)≃1.

If the initial optical depth is finite, the missing weight represents photons that have already stopped scattering before ηi​.
Before cosmological recombination, the optical depth to the present is enormous: E≃0, and g is also small because almost every scattering is followed by another. Through photon decoupling, E rises rapidly and g has its dominant positive peak. After decoupling, without reionization, E is close to one and reaches exactly one at η0​, while g is small because the remaining electron density and scattering rate are small. Residual ionization can give a weak tail; there is no second reionization peak. The peak marks the Cosmic microwave background last-scattering surface.
