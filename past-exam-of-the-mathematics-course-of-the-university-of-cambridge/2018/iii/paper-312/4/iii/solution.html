Each small-scale primary temperature amplitude is multiplied by e−τre​, so its Cosmic microwave background power spectrum is multiplied by e−2τre​. Holding transfer parameters fixed,
CℓTT,primary​∝As​e−2τre​on the damped angular scales​.

Many measured high-ℓ modes determine this combination accurately. Their derivatives ∂logCℓ​/∂logAs​=1 and ∂logCℓ​/∂τre​=−2 are degenerate: a change dlogAs​=2dτre​ leaves the leading temperature spectrum unchanged. The undamped large-scale modes can distinguish the parameters, but there are few of them and cosmic variance gives fractional full-sky uncertainty 2/(2ℓ+1)​ per multipole. This explains the primordial-amplitude–optical-depth degeneracy. Polarization from reionization, lensing, and more complete late-time effects partly break it.
