Consider Bn​ on the finite-dimensional vector space E=R[x]≤d​ and use the monomial basis. For j≥1, a binomial distribution and the falling factorial moment formula give
Bn​(xj)=nj1​∑ℓ=0j​S(j,ℓ)(n)ℓ​xℓ,
(17)
where S(j,ℓ) is the Stirling number of the second kind and (n)ℓ​=n(n−1)⋯(n−ℓ+1). This follows by expanding Kj=∑ℓ​S(j,ℓ)(K)ℓ​ and using E(K)ℓ​=(n)ℓ​xℓ. Also Bn​(1)=1.
Each monomial maps to a polynomial of no larger degree. The resulting matrix is triangular with diagonal entries 1 and (n)j​/nj, 1≤j≤d. When n≥d and n≥1, all entries are positive. Therefore
Bn​(E)⊆E,Bn​:E→E is invertible for n≥max(1,d).​
(18)
For constant polynomials the conclusion is immediate. Taking positive indices avoids the undefined sample expression k/n at n=0.
