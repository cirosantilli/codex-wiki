The exact mixture equation of state is ρ=(1−ϕ)ρf​+ϕρp​. Keeping first-order terms in the small particle volume fraction and thermal expansion gives
ρ=ρ0​(1+γϕ−θ)​.
(16)
The omitted term is ρ0​ϕθ. The downward solid-volume particle deposition flux is Jv​=Ws​ϕ; the corresponding particle mass flux is Jm​=ρp​Ws​ϕ. Uniform vertical mixing and constant layer depth imply
Hϕ˙​=−Ws​ϕ,θ˙=βϕ.
(17)
For Ws​>0, set r=Ws​/H. Then
ϕ(t)=ϕ0​e−rt,θ(t)=rβϕ0​​(1−e−rt)​,
(18)
and hence
ρ0​ρ(t)−ρ0​​=ϕ0​[(γ+rβ​)e−rt−rβ​].
(19)
The heated particle-laden layer loses its stable density stratification when this contrast vanishes. For β>0, solving for the neutral-buoyancy time gives
Ts​=Ws​H​log(1+βHγWs​​)​.
(20)
The layer is neutral at Ts​ and statically unstable for t>Ts​. The subsequent uniformly mixed lower-layer solution cannot represent the resulting overturning.
If Ws​=0, then ϕ=ϕ0​, θ=βϕ0​t, and the continuous limit is Ts​=γ/β. If β=0, the layer stays denser than its surroundings at every finite time and becomes neutral only asymptotically when Ws​>0; thus Ts​=∞. These limiting cases must replace the printed formula when its denominator vanishes.
