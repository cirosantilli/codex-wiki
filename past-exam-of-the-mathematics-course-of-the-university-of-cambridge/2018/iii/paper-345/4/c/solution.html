The Exner equation is solid-volume mass conservation: a downstream increase in sediment transport flux removes material and lowers the bed. The factor ϕb​ converts bed-height change into solid-volume change. The saturation length equation models downstream adjustment of actual sediment transport to the equilibrium sediment flux. Grain acceleration and entrainment/deposition need a finite distance, so q lags qsat​. The last relation is an empirical transport law above the sediment entrainment threshold, with χ>0 and normally γ>0; below threshold it must be interpreted with a positive part rather than raising negative excess stress to an arbitrary power. It is a constitutive closure, not a consequence of mass conservation.
Take the physical parameters Lsat​>0, ϕb​>0, χ>0 and γ>0. Linearize about a horizontal, uniformly transporting bed with Δ=τ0​−τth,0​>0 and q0​=ϕb​χΔγ. Set λ=σ−ikc and use the real parts of
η=η^​eλt+ikx,q=q0​+q^​eλt+ikx,qsat​=q0​+q^​sat​eλt+ikx,τ=τ0​+τ^eλt+ikx.
(57)
The locally planar approximation uses the small instantaneous bed slope to compute the inclined-bed sediment threshold. Since α=ηx​+O(ηx3​),
τ^th​=μs​ikτth,0​​η^​​.
(58)
A sinusoidal disturbance has both uphill and downhill slopes: the printed 0<α≪1 describes the uphill derivation, and its first-order continuation applies to both signs. The zero-slope reference is the one implicit in the printed decomposition with constant term τth,0​; a finite mean inclined bed would require a shifted base threshold.
Define C=χγΔγ−1. The linearized transport equations are
ϕb​λη^​=−ikq^​,(1+ikLsat​)q^​=q^​sat​,q^​sat​=ϕb​C(τ^−μs​ikτth,0​​η^​)​.
(59)
The linearization requires ∣ηx​∣≪1 and ∣τ^−τ^th​∣≪Δ.
A fluid-dynamical shear-response closure is missing from the printed question. The three sediment equations and local slope correction do not determine τ^ from η^​. Write the general bed shear response as τ^=T(k)η^​. A frequently used scale-invariant closure for k>0 is
T(k)=τ0​k(A+iB)​.
(60)
Here A is the component in phase with bed height and B represents an upstream phase lead in bed shear stress. They require an independent flow model and can depend on k. Introducing them explicitly makes the linear stability analysis complete conditional on a specified flow response; it does not turn them into data supplied by the question. An example of this hydrodynamic closure is given in Fourrière, Claudin and Andreotti's bedform-instability analysis.
