If A>0, B>0 are treated as constants, the upstream shear stress phase lead promotes growth, while the inclined-bed sediment threshold reduces it and the finite saturation length delays sediment transport. There is an unstable band precisely when b>0: 0<k<b/(aLsat​). Within this band c>0, so the growing bedforms migrate downstream. The fastest normal mode satisfies

Just above the sediment entrainment threshold,
b≃τth,0​(B−1/μs​).
(65)
Thus the common regime B<1/μs​ is stable near onset: the slope penalty overwhelms the hydrodynamic phase lead. This conclusion is conditional on that inequality, not universal. For constant B>0, instability begins only when τ0​>τth,0​/(μs​B) as well as τ0​>τth,0​. The transport sensitivity C=χγΔγ−1 vanishes at onset for γ>1, is finite for γ=1, and is singular for 0<γ<1. Exactly at threshold, the positive-part transport law needs separate treatment; the above linearization with perturbations small compared with Δ is unavailable.
Far above the sediment entrainment threshold, b≃τ0​B. If A,B>0, the unstable cutoff tends to B/(ALsat​), and the dominant wavelength is set by the saturation length. Both growth rate and migration velocity scale with Cτ0​∼χγτ0γ​, apart from their length factors. Their numerical values and any detailed dependence on wavenumber still require a flow closure.
