Use locally bed-tangent drag force, consistent with interpreting τ as bed shear stress. Let the bed rise downstream at angle α and retain the same effective drag coefficient. The normal reaction is R=Gcosα, while the opposing downslope component of submerged weight is Gsinα. At impending upslope motion,
FD​=Gsinα+μs​Gcosα.
(53)
Thus the inclined-bed sediment threshold is
Θth,0​(α)=3CD​4​(μs​cosα+sinα)=Θth,0​(cosα+μs​sinα​)​.
(54)
For ∣α∣≪1,
τth​(α)=τth,0​(1+μs​α​)+O(α2)​.
(55)
An uphill slope increases the sediment entrainment threshold; a downhill slope lowers it, until spontaneous gravity-driven motion invalidates the resting-bed model.
There is a geometric convention to specify: if the drag force remains horizontal while the bed tilts, then R=Gcosα+FD​sinα. The corresponding force balance gives instead
Θthhorizontal drag​=3CD​4​cosα−μs​sinαμs​cosα+sinα​=Θth,0​[1+(μs​+μs−1​)α+O(α2)].
(56)
The subsequent bed shear stress analysis uses the first, locally tangent convention. The two interpretations should not be mixed.
