A valid covariance matrix must be real symmetric and a positive semidefinite matrix, with Cii​=σi2​≥0. Conversely, every such matrix is the covariance of a possibly degenerate multivariate normal distribution. The diagonal correlations are ρii​=1; the printed strict inequality can only concern distinct indices. Pairwise bounds do not suffice: the 3 by 3 correlation matrix with diagonal 1 and every off-diagonal entry −0.9 has eigenvalue −0.8 along 1 and is invalid. Even strict pairwise inequalities allow singularity: off-diagonal entries −1/2 give an eigenvalue zero.
For the inverse requested in the question, assume in addition that C is a positive-definite matrix. Write x=(μ​i​), Λ=C−1 and S=1TΛ1>0. Up to a constant, the log-likelihood is −21​(x−μ1)TΛ(x−μ1). Its score function and second derivative are
ℓ′(μ)=1TΛ(x−μ1),ℓ′′(μ)=−S<0.
(6)
The correlated Gaussian common-mean estimator is therefore
μ​MLE​=1TΛ11TΛx​=∑i,j​Λij​∑i,j​Λij​μ​j​​​.
(7)
Its expectation is μ, and direct covariance propagation gives
Bias(μ​MLE​)=0,Var(μ​MLE​)=S21TΛCΛ1​=S−1​.
(8)
The Fisher information is −Eℓ′′(μ)=S, so this variance attains the Cramér-Rao bound and the estimator is an efficient estimator.
If C is singular, an ordinary Lebesgue density and C−1 are unavailable. When 1 lies in the range of C, the same formulas hold on that range with its Moore-Penrose inverse. Otherwise there is u∈kerC with uT1=0, and uTx=μuT1 determines μ exactly with zero variance. The usual nonsingular Cramér–Rao calculation then does not apply.

Choose a fixed Href​>0, and write h(H0​)=−5log10​(H0​/Href​) and ds​(w,ΩM​)=μ(zs​;Href​,w,ΩM​). The predicted magnitude is M0​+h(H0​)+ds​. Thus the likelihood depends on M0​,H0​ only through β=M0​+h(H0​), the absolute magnitude–Hubble constant degeneracy. Since dM0​=dβ at fixed H0​, integrating with a flat density over the entire real line removes H0​.
Explicitly, let as​=(v+rs​)−1, S=∑s​as​, xs​=ms​−ds​, xˉ=S−1∑s​as​xs​ and Q=∑s​as​(xs​−xˉ)2. Completing the square gives ∑s​as​(xs​−β)2=Q+S(β−xˉ)2, so flat-prior elimination of a Gaussian common mean yields
I∝(2π)−(N−1)/2S−1/2s∏​(v+rs​)−1/2e−Q/2​,
(12)
independently of H0​. The proportionality includes the arbitrary constant of the flat prior, which cancels within this posterior analysis.
If the remaining posterior integral is finite, prior independence gives p(H0​∣D)∝p(H0​) and hence p(H0​∣D)=p(H0​)​: these data supply no marginal update of the Hubble constant. The quoted Gaussian is consequently retained with its stated a,b. Strictly, the model uses H0​>0 inside a logarithm, so the Gaussian prior must be restricted and normalized on that domain:
p(H0​∣D)=bΦ(a/b)ϕ((H0​−a)/b)​1{H0​>0}​,
(13)
where ϕ,Φ are the standard normal density and distribution function. Ignoring its negligible negative tail gives the stated untruncated Gaussian approximation.

A log-flat improper prior p(v)∝1/v is not appropriate when all known rs​=σm,s2​ are positive: the integrated likelihood in (iii) has a positive finite limit as v↓0, so its integral against dv/v diverges. One defensible noninformative choice is the scalar Jeffreys prior for an additive variance component, calculated holding the mean parameters fixed:
p(v)∝[21​s∑​(v+rs​)−2]1/2,v≥0​.
(14)
Indeed the normal variance score has information Ivv​=21​∑s​(v+rs​)−2. This is a scalar conditional-information prior, not a claim that its product with the flat mean prior is the joint Jeffreys prior. It is bounded near zero and is O(v−1) at infinity. The integrated likelihood is uniformly bounded near zero and is bounded by a constant times v−(N−1)/2 at infinity, independently of the mean-shape parameters, since e−Q/2≤1. Thus N>1 and a proper external prior on the physically admissible (w,ΩM​) ensure posterior propriety. Proper weak scale priors are another option. If known errors vanish, the boundary argument and appropriate prior need separate reconsideration.
For an efficient parameterization, use θ=(β,u,w,ΩM​) with u=logv and β=M0​+h(H0​). A chain on this joint reduced parameter space targets
π(θ)∝L(β,eu,Href​,w,ΩM​)p(eu)eup(w,ΩM​).
(15)
The factor eu is the Jacobian determinant. At each iteration propose θ′=θ+ε, with ε∼N(0,Σq​) for a fixed nonsingular proposal covariance, and accept with probability min{1,π(θ′)/π(θ)}. Proposals outside the physical prior domain have target zero. Tune Σq​ during warmup and freeze it for the retained Random-walk Metropolis algorithm. To obtain samples of the full original parameter vector, independently draw H0​ from its retained positive prior and reconstruct M0​=β−h(H0​); the resulting vector is (M0​,v,H0​,w,ΩM​). Analytically marginalizing β using (iii) would also be valid.
Use several dispersed chains, trace plots, rank-normalized split R, and the effective sample size of a Markov chain for each parameter and for w,w2. These Markov chain Monte Carlo convergence diagnostics reveal poor mixing and disagreement but do not prove convergence. Estimate the integrated autocorrelation time τint​; with B retained draws, Beff​≈B/τint​. Default to no thinning of a Markov chain, so the thinning factor is 1. If storage requires thinning, choose a spacing after inspecting the autocorrelation and verify the retained-chain effective sample size; no finite spacing guarantees independent draws.
Using the retained wb​ samples, compute
E[w]=B1​b∑​wb​,SD(w)=[B−11​b∑​(wb​−wˉ)2]1/2​.
(16)
These are posterior summaries, provided the corresponding moments exist, not uncertainties of the numerical estimates. A proper prior alone does not ensure finite second moments; choose or check the external prior accordingly. The Monte Carlo standard error of the posterior mean is approximately SD(w)/Beff​​.

Let π be the normalized density π in the transformed coordinates of (iv). Its fixed Gaussian increment gives a symmetric proposal distribution: q(θ′∣θ)=q(θ∣θ′). For distinct states, the accepted transition density is K(θ,θ′)=q(θ′∣θ)α(θ,θ′), where α=min{1,π(θ′)/π(θ)}. Then
π(θ)K(θ,θ′)=q(θ′∣θ)min{π(θ),π(θ′)}=π(θ′)K(θ′,θ).
(17)
The rejection probability gives a mass on the diagonal, which trivially satisfies the same balance identity. Thus π(dθ)K(θ,dθ′)=π(dθ′)K(θ′,dθ)​, the detailed balance condition, and integrating it proves invariance of the target.
The transformed density includes its Jacobian, so mapping back preserves the desired posterior law. Warmup adaptation is excluded from this fixed-kernel proof. Independent augmentation with H0​ is itself a reversible conditional update; alternatively the original joint augmented space can be sampled directly. This is the Metropolis–Hastings algorithm balance argument.

Use the periodic covariance function
kP​(t,t′)=A2exp[−ℓ22​sin2(Pπ(t−t′)​)],H=(A,ℓ,P),A,ℓ,P>0.
(19)
Its positive semidefiniteness follows by pulling back a Gaussian kernel to the circle (cos(2πt/P),sin(2πt/P)). A zero Gaussian process mean is an ensemble statement, whereas an exactly zero long-term average for every periodic light curve is stronger. To impose the latter literally, use a periodic Gaussian process with zero period average, replacing the kernel by
k0​(t,t′)=kP​(t,t′)−c0​,c0​=A2e−ℓ−2I0​(ℓ−2).
(20)
Here I0​ is a modified Bessel function, and c0​ is the period average of the kernel. Subtracting each process's random period average produces this covariance, so it remains positive semidefinite. We use k0​ to enforce the stated mean subtraction; using kP​ gives the usual ensemble-zero-mean formulation and the same likelihood derivation.
Let Kij​=k0​(ti​,tj​) and R=diag(σ12​,…,σN2​). The prior latent vector is f∼N(0,K) and the independent Gaussian noise is ε∼N(0,R), so y=f+ε∼N(0,V) with V=K+R. The Gaussian-process marginal likelihood is
p(y∣t,H)=(2π)−N/2∣V∣−1/2exp(−21​yTV−1y)​.
(21)
Positive measurement variances make V positive definite even if K is singular. Maximize ℓ(H)=−21​yTV−1y−21​log∣V∣−2N​log(2π) over all three hyperparameters, using a broad search in P to find competing aliases, followed by joint optimization. Cholesky decomposition evaluates the likelihood without explicitly forming an inverse.
For a well-resolved interior maximum, let J=−∇2ℓ(H) be the Observed Fisher information in the coordinates (A,ℓ,P). Then P=(H)P​,σP​≈(J−1)PP​​​. Equivalently, the profile-likelihood curvature in P gives the same local nuisance-adjusted variance; keeping the other hyperparameters fixed would generally underestimate it. A profile interval with ℓprof​(P)−ℓprof​(P)=1/2 is a local 1σ approximation. If the period likelihood is multimodal or poorly constrained, fit the complete posterior with proper hyperparameter priors and report its modes and marginal credible intervals rather than a single Hessian error bar. The sampling pattern can create period aliases that a single local search misses.

At the fitted hyperparameters, define K=k0​(t,t), K∗∗​=k0​(t∗,t∗), and K∗t​=k0​(t∗,t), with the matrices formed by pairwise kernel evaluations. Conditional independence of observation noise and future latent values gives
(yf∗​​)∼N(0,(VK∗t​​Kt∗​K∗∗​​)),V=K+R.
(22)
The residual f∗​−K∗t​V−1y has zero covariance with y, so the joint normal law makes it independent of y. This derives the conditional multivariate normal distribution and the Gaussian process regression posterior:
f∗​∣y,t,t∗,H∼N(m∗​,Σ∗​),m∗​=K∗t​V−1y,Σ∗​=K∗∗​−K∗t​V−1Kt∗​​.
(23)
The covariance is a Schur complement and is positive semidefinite. When nonsingular, its joint density is (2π)−M/2∣Σ∗​∣−1/2exp[−21​(f∗​−m∗​)TΣ∗−1​(f∗​−m∗​)]. Repeated phases can make it singular, in which case the displayed normal law is interpreted on its support.
This predicts the true latent light curve, so no future measurement variance is added. For noisy future observations, add their independent noise covariance to Σ∗​. Fixing the hyperparameters omits their estimation uncertainty, as requested; integrating over their posterior would add it. The periodic model extrapolates by phase, even though all prediction times follow the observed times.

Keeping the powers and exponentials of v and τ in the joint kernel gives the two inverse-gamma distributions, using the printed shape–scale convention:
v∣rest∼IG(2N​,21​s∑​(Cs​−μC​)2),τ∣rest∼IG(N,s∑​Es​)​.
(30)
Indeed their kernels are v−(N/2+1)exp[−∑s​(Cs​−μC​)2/(2v)] and τ−(N+1)exp[−∑s​Es​/τ]. With positive measurement variances and generic continuously drawn latent values, the two scale parameters are positive almost surely. Together with (a),(b), these are all 2N+3 formal full conditional updates.
There is, however, no joint posterior probability distribution to sample under the stated priors. Integrate out the intrinsic Cs​ first. For fixed μC​,v>0, the observational marginal density is
L(μC​,v,τ)=∏s​∫0∞​τ−1e−E/τϕv+rs​​(os​−μC​−E)dE,
(31)
where ϕV​ is the centred normal density of variance V. Substitute E=τu. Dominated convergence gives
limτ↓0​L(μC​,v,τ)=∏s​ϕv+rs​​(os​−μC​)>0.
(32)
The convergence is uniform on any compact μC​ interval and any compact positive v interval, since the normal densities are uniformly bounded and continuous there. Hence some such region has L≥c>0 for all sufficiently small τ, and
∫0ε​L(μC​,v,τ)τdτ​=∞.
(33)
Integrating over those positive-measure intervals proves that the full normalizing constant is infinite. If all rs​>0, the v↓0 boundary also gives a positive marginal likelihood and a divergent dv/v integral at fixed τ>0. Thus the stated joint posterior is improper​, even though its generic full conditionals are proper. Apparent stability of a simulated chain cannot establish a nonexistent stationary probability law.
A valid repair that preserves direct sampling is to replace the two log-flat priors by independent proper v∼IG(av​,bv​) and τ∼IG(aτ​,bτ​), with all four constants positive. The normal and truncated-normal updates remain as above, while
v∣rest∼IG(av​+2N​,bv​+21​∑s​(Cs​−μC​)2),τ∣rest∼IG(aτ​+N,bτ​+∑s​Es​).
(34)
With positive rs​, the flat μC​ prior is then harmless: after integrating it out, the Gaussian measurement likelihood is bounded uniformly over the latent shifts and the scales, and the remaining proper priors integrate to one. A proper prior on μC​ is also possible.
For this repaired model, initialize v,τ>0, Es​≥0 and finite Cs​,μC​. Repeatedly choose one of the 2N+3 coordinates with fixed positive probabilities and redraw from its full conditional using the latest values. This Random-scan Gibbs sampler preserves the proper target and satisfies detailed balance: for each coordinate, both forward and reverse flows equal the marginal density of the remaining coordinates times the product of the two conditional densities. A systematic full sweep is another invariant scheme, though it need not itself be reversible.
Run several dispersed chains; inspect traces of both scales, μC​, representative latent values and the log posterior. Assess rank-normalized split R, autocorrelation, and the effective sample size of a Markov chain, and extend runs until relevant Monte Carlo errors are sufficiently small. Scale–latent dependence can cause slow mixing, so diagnostics must include boundary exploration and alternative initializations. Keep post-warmup draws without automatic thinning. These are convergence checks for the repaired posterior; the printed model admits only the formal update algorithm, not a valid posterior-sampling interpretation.
