Let pK​=C(T,K+1)−2C(T,K)+C(T,K−1)>0 and DK​=C(T+1,K)−C(T,K). Given ST​=K, the next value is one of K−1,K,K+1. The martingale property makes its upward and downward conditional probabilities equal; their sum is the conditional variance of the increment, σ2(T,K)=2DK​/pK​. Thus
P(ST+1​=H∣ST​=K)=⎩⎨⎧​DK​/pK​,1−2DK​/pK​,0,​H=K−1 or K+1,H=K,otherwise.​​
(31)
The discrete Dupire equation therefore recovers all positive-probability one-step conditional transitions from the European call option price surface. No Markov property is required: these are conditional probabilities given the current value, not necessarily given the whole past.
Recovery is impossible at a zero-probability state. For example, St​≡S0​ satisfies every stated assumption, but at T≥1 a different integer K can satisfy ∣K−S0​∣≤T while pK​=0. Its conditional transitions can be specified arbitrarily without altering C. Thus the literal all-states claim needs the positive-probability qualification.
