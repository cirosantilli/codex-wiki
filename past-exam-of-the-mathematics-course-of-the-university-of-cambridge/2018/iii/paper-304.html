Assume m2>0 and take λ→0+. The free Gaussian integral gives Z0​=2π​/∣m∣, and the normalized free Gaussian measure has covariance C=m−2. Expanding only the interaction exponential gives the asymptotic expansion
Z0​Z​=∑V≥0​V!(6!)V(−λ)V​⟨ϕ6V⟩0​.
(1)
In this zero-dimensional scalar field theory, the path integral has become an ordinary integral, but its combinatorics are precisely those of sextic scalar field theory.
The free generating functional is ⟨eJϕ⟩0​=eCJ2/2. Differentiating it proves Wick theorem: odd moments vanish, while each even moment is the sum over pairings of its factors. Each Wick contraction contributes C, so
⟨ϕ2r⟩0​=(2r−1)!!Cr.
(2)
Thus the Feynman rules are a quantum field theory propagator C for each internal line and a factor −λ for each six-valent interaction vertex. The factorials V! and (6!)V remove vertex and half-edge labels; the remaining weight is (−λ)VCI/S, where S is the Feynman-diagram symmetry factor, the order of the automorphism group of the resulting diagram, including its half-edge symmetries. A tadpole diagram has an additional interchange symmetry of the two ends of its loop.
There is one empty Vacuum Feynman diagram, contributing 1. At one interaction vertex, the six half-edges form three loops. Its Feynman-diagram symmetry factor is 233!=48, so its contribution is −λC3/48.
At two interaction vertices let r count the lines joining them. Each interaction vertex has (6−r)/2 self-loops, so r can only be 0,2,4,6. These four possibilities exhaust all Vacuum Feynman diagrams at this order. Their Feynman-diagram symmetry factors are
Sr​=2r![2(6−r)/2(26−r​)!]2,rSr​​04608​2256​4192​61440​​.
(3)
Here the leading 2 exchanges the two interaction vertices, r! permutes the joining lines, and the remaining factors permute and reverse the self-loops. The r=0 graph is disconnected and must be included in Z/Z0​; only a connected generating functional would discard it.
Consequently,
Z(m,0)Z(m,λ)​=1−48m6λ​+7680m1277λ2​+O(λ3).​
(4)
Indeed ∑r​Sr−1​=77/7680, independently agreeing with 11!!/[2(6!)2] from Wick theorem.
This is an asymptotic expansion, rather than a convergent perturbation series: the coefficient of λV contains (6V−1)!!/[V!(6!)V] and grows too rapidly for a nonzero radius of convergence. For any fixed truncation order, the Taylor expansion remainder bound for e−x on x≥0, followed by the finite Gaussian moment integral, proves the stated remainder estimate. For negative real λ the original integral diverges.

Take integer n≥3 and positive integer d, so the interaction is genuinely non-Gaussian. For a connected two-point Feynman diagram with V interaction vertices and I internal lines, counting half-edges and using the loop order gives
nV=2I+2,L=I−V+1.
(5)
At one loop I=V, hence (n−2)V=2. There are only two possibilities: a two-interaction vertex bubble diagram for n=3, or a one-interaction vertex tadpole diagram for n=4. The latter is independent of external momentum and changes the mass, not the kinetic term.
For cubic scalar field theory, introduce a positive auxiliary mass μ if needed to separate infrared divergences from ultraviolet divergences. The one-loop self-energy in the quantum effective action is −λ2Bd​(p)/2, with
Bd​(p)=∫01​du∫(2π)dddq​[q2+μ2+u(1−u)p2]21​.
(6)
This Feynman parameter representation follows by shifting the loop momentum; it is directly valid for a translation-invariant regulator, or for the convergent subtracted integral. Differentiating at p2=0 gives
dp2dBd​​​0​=−31​∫(2π)dddq​(q2+μ2)31​.
(7)
Thus the coefficient of the kinetic term changes by λ2∫ddq/[(2π)d(q2+μ2)3]/6.
Only the cubic interaction has a momentum-dependent one-loop two-point correction.
n=3for every d≥1.​
(8)
There is a terminology qualification: if “wave-function renormalization” means a required ultraviolet counterterm, rather than a finite correction to the field normalization, the large-q radial integral behaves as ∫dqqd−7, and the answer is
n=3, d≥6for an ultraviolet-divergent kinetic correction.​
(9)
It is logarithmically divergent at d=6 and power divergent above 6 with a momentum cutoff. Among interactions with a relevant coupling or marginal coupling, the divergent case is uniquely (n,d)=(3,6). The massless low-dimensional amplitude needs an infrared divergence prescription; one cannot take a local expansion at zero momentum without one. The n=1,2 cases, if admitted, are a source and a Gaussian mass term and have no interaction-induced wave-function renormalization.

The kinetic term fixes the mass dimension of the scalar field, and the interaction then fixes that of its coupling:
[ϕ]=2d−2​,[λ]=d−2n(d−2)​.
(10)
A relevant coupling has positive mass dimension; a marginal coupling has zero mass dimension. Therefore
d≤2: n≥3 arbitrary;d>2: 3≤n≤d−22d​.​
(11)
For integer dimensions the complete interacting list is
d1,23456d≥7​relevant nevery n≥33,4,533nonenone​marginal nnone64none3none​​.
(12)
If the noninteracting n=1,2 terms are included, their couplings have mass dimensions (d+2)/2 and 2, respectively, and are relevant for every positive d. This is a classification by power counting in quantum field theory; it does not assert the stability of odd monomial potentials.

Literal absence of new interaction vertices in the full quantum effective action holds only for Gaussian field theories.
For every interacting n≥3, the one-loop scalar effective action already contains counterexamples. On a constant background, with a positive auxiliary mass μ to control infrared divergences, its field-dependent part is
Γ1​[ϕ]=21​Trlog[1+(n−2)!(−∂2+μ2)λϕn−2​].
(13)
The term of order λV has E=(n−2)V external scalar fields and is a one-loop polygon one-particle-irreducible Feynman diagram. Choose V>n/(n−2) and 2V>d. Then E>n, its loop momentum integral is ultraviolet convergent, and its coefficient is nonzero. Removing the ultraviolet cutoff does not remove this finite higher-point scalar vertex. For d>2, generic nonexceptional external momenta give the same conclusion without retaining the auxiliary mass. In d≤2, the massless theory still needs an infrared prescription; removing the ultraviolet cutoff does not remove that need or the induced vertices. Hence there are no interacting pairs (n,d) under the literal wording. For n=2 the functional determinant is field independent; n=1 merely shifts a Gaussian integral when an infrared prescription exists.
There is a different conventional interpretation: absence of new independent divergent counterterms. The superficial degree of divergence of a connected diagram is
D=dL−2I=d−2d−2​E−V[λ].
(14)
The standard perturbative counterterm closure criterion is [λ]≥0, with the same pairs as in part ii, provided one includes all symmetry-allowed lower-degree potential terms, the kinetic term, and the vacuum energy. A pure monomial family need not itself have counterterm closure: a sextic interaction in three dimensions generates a quartic counterterm, and a quartic interaction generates a mass counterterm. This interpretation concerns the local divergent part or the continuum defining action, not the complete quantum effective action with its finite interaction vertices and derivative expansion.
For the massive four-dimensional quartic scalar field theory, write λB​ for the bare quartic coupling, λR​ for its renormalized value, and m>0 for the mass held fixed by a renormalization condition. Define the scalar bubble integral
BΛ​(P)=∫∣q∣<Λ​(2π)4d4q​(q2+m2)((q+P)2+m2)1​.
(15)
The quartic one-particle-irreducible correlation function, defined as a derivative of the Euclidean quantum effective action, is
ΓB(4)​(p1​,p2​,p3​,p4​)=λB​−2λB2​​[BΛ0​​(p1​+p2​)+BΛ0​​(p1​+p3​)+BΛ0​​(p1​+p4​)]+O(λB3​),∑i​pi​=0.
(16)
There are three bubble diagrams, one for each pairing of external momenta, and each has Feynman-diagram symmetry factor 2. The minus sign follows equivalently from the quadratic term in the functional determinant 21​Trlog[1+(λB​/2)(−∂2+m2)−1ϕ2].
Radial integration at zero external momentum gives
BΛ​(0)=16π21​[log(1+m2Λ2​)+Λ2+m2m2​−1].
(17)
The bare quartic vertex is not finite at fixed bare coupling. Its logarithmic ultraviolet divergence must be subtracted. Impose the momentum-subtraction scheme condition ΓR(4)​(0,0,0,0)=λR​. To one loop this requires
λB​=λR​+23λR2​​BΛ0​​(0)+O(λR3​).
(18)
For the finite difference, a Feynman parameter and a shift of loop momentum give
limΛ→∞​[BΛ​(P)−BΛ​(0)]=−16π21​∫01​dulog[1+m2u(1−u)P2​].
(19)
The boundary error due to shifting a sharp cutoff vanishes in this limit. Substitution yields the finite renormalized quartic scalar vertex
ΓR(4)​=λR​+32π2λR2​​P=p1​+p2​,p1​+p3​,p1​+p4​∑​∫01​dulog[1+m2u(1−u)P2​]+O(λR3​).​
(20)
Thus finiteness requires holding the renormalized coupling fixed and allowing the bare coupling to depend on Λ0​. The unsubtracted assertion would be false.
The same one-loop scalar effective action explains the other effective interactions. For a constant background its expansion is
VolΓ1​​=∑r≥1​2r(−1)r+1​(2λR​​)rϕ2rIr​(Λ0​),Ir​(Λ)=∫∣q∣<Λ​(2π)4d4q​(q2+m2)r1​.
(21)
The r=1 tadpole diagram gives a mass correction δm2=λR​I1​/2, with
I1​(Λ)=16π21​[Λ2−m2log(1+m2Λ2​)].
(22)
It requires a mass counterterm. The field-independent vacuum energy also requires subtraction. The r=2 term is the quartic logarithm already treated. There is no one-loop wave-function renormalization from the quartic tadpole diagram.
For r≥3 the polygon diagrams generate finite higher-point scalar vertices, with
Ir​(∞)=16π2Γ(r)Γ(r−2)​m4−2r(r≥3).​
(23)
For example, the induced sextic term in the effective potential is +λR3​ϕ6/(768π2m2), or a six-point interaction vertex 15λR3​/(16π2m2) when normalized by 6!. These finite terms survive the removal of the ultraviolet cutoff at fixed m,λR​.
The external-momentum dependence of the bubble diagrams also generates a derivative expansion of quartic interactions. At small P, the subtracted scalar bubble integral is −P2/(96π2m2)+O(P4/m4), giving finite derivative couplings. At order λR2​ these are the new field-dependent interactions beyond the mass and quartic terms; the sextic and higher interaction vertices require higher powers of λR​, though they still occur at one loop. Such coefficients are suppressed by powers of the physical mass or external momentum, not by powers of Λ0​. The calculation establishes an order-by-order perturbative limit; it does not establish a nonperturbative interacting continuum limit of a quantum field theory in four dimensions.

Let the charge parameter depend on position temporarily. Using the gauge-covariant derivative of a charged scalar field, the variation of the matter action is δS=∫d4xjμ∂μ​α. The resulting Noether current is
jμ=i[ϕ∇μϕˉ​−ϕˉ​∇μϕ]=i(ϕ∂μϕˉ​−ϕˉ​∂μϕ)−2Aμ∣ϕ∣2.​
(24)
Here ∇μ​ϕˉ​=∂μ​ϕˉ​+iAμ​ϕˉ​. The potential does not contribute, and jμ=−δSmatter​/δAμ​. Because A has not been rescaled to a canonical kinetic term, there is no factor e in this Noether current or in the matter interaction vertices.
A local change of variables in the path integral gives δ⟨ϕ(y)ϕˉ​(z)⟩=⟨δ(ϕ(y)ϕˉ​(z))−(δS)ϕ(y)ϕˉ​(z)⟩=0. Integration by parts in the term containing ∂α produces the two opposite contact terms of the Ward identity.
For clarity about phases, transform the current correlator with ei(p−p′)⋅x−ip⋅y+ip′⋅z and suppress its overall momentum conservation Dirac delta function. Denote the result by Cμ(p,p′). The coordinate Ward identity becomes
−i(p−p′)μ​Cμ=−iΔ(p′)+iΔ(p),(p−p′)μ​Cμ=Δ(p′)−Δ(p).
(25)
Amputation of the two external quantum field theory propagators then fixes the longitudinal part of the current interaction vertex. Since the action couples Aμ​ to −jμ, the gauge interaction vertex has the opposite sign. The phase convention for the paper's gauge interaction vertices is Γμ=iGμ, where Gμ is the corresponding derivative of the Euclidean quantum effective action.
One can derive the exact one-particle-irreducible correlation function directly, without assuming the entire connected current correlator is one-particle irreducible. Local gauge invariance of the quantum effective action implies
∂μ​δAμ​(x)δΓeff​​=iϕ(x)δϕ(x)δΓeff​​−iϕˉ​(x)δϕˉ​(x)δΓeff​​.
(26)
A linear covariant gauge fixing adds a breaking term independent of the scalars, which disappears after the scalar differentiations below. The identity requires a regularization in quantum field theory and counterterms preserving the Abelian gauge theory Ward identity; scalar quantum electrodynamics has no gauge anomaly.
Differentiate with respect to ϕ(y) and ϕˉ​(z), then set all background fields to zero. The two-point derivative is the inverse exact quantum field theory propagator. Use the Fourier transform convention ϕ(x)=∫p​eip⋅xϕ(p), ϕˉ​(x)=∫p′​e−ip′⋅xϕˉ​(p′), with the photon momentum p′−p incoming. This gives
(p−p′)μ​Gμ(p,p′)=Δ(p′)−1−Δ(p)−1,(p−p′)μ​Γμ(p,p′)=i[Δ(p′)−1−Δ(p)−1].​
(27)
At tree level Gμ=−(p+p′)μ, so Γμ=−i(p+p′)μ, and the identity reduces to −i(p2−p′2)=i(p′2−p2).
To obtain the two-photon scalar Ward identity, differentiate the same functional Ward identity also with respect to Aν​(w). In terms of Euclidean action derivatives it reads
∂xμ​Gμν​(x,w;y,z)=iδ4(x−y)Gν​(w;x,z)−iδ4(x−z)Gν​(w;y,x).
(28)
This construction automatically includes the seagull vertex: the Noether current depends on A, with δjμ(x)/δAν​(w)=−2δμνδ4(x−w)∣ϕ(x)∣2. It cannot be discarded when differentiating a current insertion.
Take the first photon momentum to be incoming k, and the second incoming p′−p−k. On the first scalar leg the contact term shifts p to p+k; on the other it shifts p′ to p′−k. Consequently
kμ​Gμν(k;p,p′)=Gν(p,p′−k)−Gν(p+k,p′).
(29)
With Γμν=i2Gμν=−Gμν, the result is
kμ​Γμν(k;p,p′)=iΓν(p,p′−k)−iΓν(p+k,p′).​
(30)
There is a momentum-routing sign error in the printed second identity. With the scalar momentum convention of the first identity, its first shifted argument must be p′−k, not k−p′. This is already forced at tree level: the seagull vertex has Gμν=2δμν and hence Γμν=−2δμν. The corrected right-hand side is −2kν, matching the left-hand side, whereas the printed right-hand side is −2p′ν. Changing to an all-incoming scalar convention would also change the first identity, so it does not fix both printed formulas simultaneously.

The Grassmann field c is odd and the adjoint scalar field ϕ is even. The two printed signs are consistent with a right-acting BRST differential, whose graded Leibniz rule is
Q(XY)=X(QY)+(−1)∣Y∣(QX)Y.
(31)
The bracket between two odd fields is graded, so [c,c]=2c2. Applying this rule to the ordinary Lie bracket [c,ϕ]=cϕ−ϕc gives
Q2ϕ=[Qc,ϕ]+[c,Qϕ].
(32)
The second bracket here is graded because both its entries are odd. The graded Jacobi identity gives [c,[c,ϕ]]=21​[[c,c],ϕ]. Therefore
Q2ϕ=−21​[[c,c],ϕ]+21​[[c,c],ϕ]=0.​
(33)
The side of the odd derivation is essential. With the usual left graded Leibniz rule and the same two printed signs, the result would be −[[c,c],ϕ], which is generally nonzero. A left-acting convention must reverse one of those signs. All subsequent BRST symmetry formulas here use the right-acting convention.
Choose an anti-Hermitian basis Ta with [Tb,Tc]=fabcTa, an invariant positive invariant bilinear form on a Lie algebra, and the adjoint covariant derivative Dμ​X=∂μ​X+[Aμ​,X]. Couplings are absorbed into this convention; restoring Dμ​=∂μ​+g[Aμ​,⋅] multiplies each ghost interaction vertex below by g. The associated BRST charge acts as QAμ​=−Dμ​c, Qcˉ=ih, and Qh=0.
Write Fa=∂⋅Aa+(n⋅∂ϕa)/2​. For the gauge-fixing fermion Ψ=∫cˉa(Fa−iha/2), the right graded Leibniz rule gives
QΨ=∫d4x[21​haha+ihaFa+cˉa(QF)a],QF=−∂μDμ​c−2​1​n⋅∂[ϕ,c].
(34)
The Gaussian functional integral over the Nakanishi-Lautrup field produces the positive gauge fixing term FaFa/2. The ghost operator is −∂⋅D−(n⋅∂)adϕ​/2​.
Now use the canonical free kinetic terms Fμνa​Fμνa​/4+(∂ϕa)2/2. At nonzero momentum in Euclidean space p, put η=n⋅p. The quadratic kernel for (Aμa​,ϕa), per color, is
K(p)=(p2δμν​ηpν​/2​​ηpμ​/2​p2+η2/2​).
(35)
The transverse gauge-field kernel plus the gauge-fixing longitudinal term has become p2δμν​. The scalar quantum field theory propagator is the inverse Schur complement, not merely the inverse of the scalar diagonal entry:
Kϕϕ​−KϕA​KAA−1​KAϕ​=p2+2η2​−2p2η2p2​=p2.
(36)
Hence the free adjoint-scalar propagator in scalar-dependent gauge fixing is
⟨ϕa(p)ϕb(−p)⟩0​=p2δab​.​
(37)
For completeness the mixed quantum field theory propagator is ⟨Aμa​ϕb⟩0​=−δabηpμ​/(2​p4); ignoring this mixing would give an incorrect scalar answer.
The scalar propagator is independent of the gauge vector n, not of the momentum component parallel to n. For a unit n, p2=p⊥2​+p∥2​, and the free scalar quantum field theory propagator still depends on p∥​. Thus the literal momentum-independence clause in the PDF is false for the standard minimally coupled massless scalar action; the cancellation above establishes the natural gauge-vector-independence statement. The usual massless zero mode in field theory at p=0 needs a separate infrared prescription.
Finally, integration by parts gives the ghost action in an unambiguous convention:
Sgh​=∫d4x[(∂μ​cˉa)(∂μ​ca)+fabc(∂μ​cˉa)Aμb​cc+2​fabc​(n⋅∂cˉa)ϕbcc].
(38)
Use eip⋅x for the Fourier transform of every field, with all momenta incoming. Let the antighost carry color a and momentum q, the boson color b and momentum k, and the ghost color c and momentum p, so q+k+p=0. Expansion of e−Sgh​ gives the ghost vertices in scalar-dependent gauge fixing
cˉa(q)Aμb​(k)cc(p): −ifabcqμ​,cˉa(q)ϕb(k)cc(p): −2​i​fabc(n⋅q).​
(39)
The free Faddeev-Popov ghost field propagator is δab/p2 and every closed ghost loop contributes a minus sign. There are no further ghost interaction vertices in this gauge. Factors of i and an overall ghost-vertex sign depend on the Fourier transform and ghost-ordering conventions; the displayed ghost action fixes both here.
