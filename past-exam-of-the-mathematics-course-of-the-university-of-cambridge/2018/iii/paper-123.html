Use the simple-root form of the Hensel lemma: for F∈OK​[T] and aˉ∈kK​ satisfying F(aˉ)=0 and F′(aˉ)=0, there is a unique a∈OK​ with aˉ as its reduction and F(a)=0.
Let d=[ℓ:kK​]. The residue field kK​ is finite, so ℓ/kK​ is separable, and the primitive element theorem gives ℓ=kK​(αˉ). Let Fˉ be the monic irreducible polynomial of αˉ, of degree d, and choose a monic coefficient lift F∈OK​[T]. Irreducible reduction implies that F is irreducible over K: any monic factorization over K has integral coefficients, since all its roots are integral over the valuation ring, and would reduce to a factorization of Fˉ.
Adjoin a root α and put L=K(α). Then [L:K]=d and α∈OL​. Its reduction satisfies Fˉ, so kL​ contains a copy of ℓ over kK​. For finite extensions of non-Archimedean local fields, the fundamental degree relation is
[L:K]=e(L/K)[kL​:kK​].
(1)
The residue degree is at least d, while the left side equals d, hence
e(L/K)=1,kL​≅ℓ,[L:K]=[ℓ:kK​].​
(2)
This constructs the required unramified extension.
For uniqueness, let L′/K be another unramified extension with a chosen identification kL′​≅ℓ. The element corresponding to αˉ is a simple residue root of F, because finite fields are perfect. The Hensel lemma in L′ gives a root α′∈OL′​ with this reduction. Since F is irreducible of degree d, [K(α′):K]=d=[L′:K], so L′=K(α′). Sending α to α′ yields a K-isomorphism L→L′, compatible with the prescribed residue identification. Thus
the unramified lift of ℓ/kK​ is unique up to K-isomorphism.​
(3)
This proves the existence and uniqueness of unramified local extensions using the stated version of Hensel's lemma, without assuming the classification in advance.

Write q=#kK​=pf. For every a∈kK​, the polynomial F(T)=Tq−T has a as a residue root and F′=−1. By the Hensel lemma there is a unique element [a]∈OK​ such that
[a]​=a,[a]q=[a].​
(4)
This defines the Teichmuller lift, including [0]=0. The two displayed properties determine each value uniquely by Hensel's uniqueness statement.
They also imply the standard Teichmuller lift properties. The elements 0,1 already satisfy their defining equations, so [0]=0 and [1]=1. The product [a][b] reduces to ab and satisfies ([a][b])q=[a][b], so uniqueness gives [ab]=[a][b]. Likewise [a]p reduces to ap and is fixed by its qth power, giving
[ab]=[a][b],[ap]=[a]p.​
(5)
For a=0, division of [a]q=[a] by [a] gives [a]q−1=1. Thus the nonzero Teichmuller representatives are precisely the prime-to-p roots of unity lifting the nonzero residue classes. This is a multiplicative section of reduction; additivity is not asserted in mixed characteristic.
For the convergence, use the normalized discrete valuation vK​ with vK​(π)=1, and put e=vK​(p)≥1. A useful binomial estimate is
vK​(up−vp)≥min{e+r,pr}≥r+1if u,v∈OK​, vK​(u−v)≥r≥1.
(6)
Indeed, writing u=v+δ, every intermediate coefficient (jp​) is divisible by p, so all terms of (v+δ)p−vp have valuation at least min{e+r,pr}. The ultrametric inequality gives the bound for their sum.
Finite fields are perfect, so the successive pth roots xn​ exist uniquely and satisfy xnpn​=x. Let an​=[xn​]. Since yn​−an​ lies in the maximal ideal, iterating the estimate n times gives
vK​(ynpn​−anpn​)≥n+1.
(7)
Multiplicativity, or the Frobenius identity above, gives anpn​=[xnpn​]=[x]. Therefore
vK​(ynpn​−[x])≥n+1,ynpn​⟶[x].​
(8)
This proves the approximation of Teichmuller lifts by iterated pth powers for completely arbitrary choices of the lifts yn​, including x=0. The original PDF confirms x∈kK​, correcting the capital KK​ in the TeX transcription.

Give Kp​ the unique extension of the absolute value of Kp​. We use two standard facts: a complete non-Archimedean absolute value extends uniquely to every finite algebraic extension, and any two embeddings of a finite separable extension into an algebraic closure are conjugate by an automorphism of that algebraic closure.
A K-embedding τ:L→Kp​ pulls back this absolute value to a finite place of a number field on L extending the given place of K. By the permitted equivalence of places and primes, it determines a prime ideal qτ​ above p. Every element of Gal(Kp​/Kp​) preserves the absolute value by uniqueness, so qτ​ is constant on each orbit.
Every prime q above p occurs: its completion of a number field at a prime ideal Lq​ is a finite extension of Kp​ and admits a Kp​-embedding into Kp​. Composing with L↪Lq​ gives an embedding inducing q.
Conversely, suppose two embeddings induce the same prime. Their pulled-back absolute values have the same normalization on K, so they extend continuously to embeddings of the same field Lq​. To see that their images are still inside the algebraic closure rather than only its completion, the closure of τ(L) is exactly the finite complete field Kp​τ(L). The two resulting embeddings of Lq​ are conjugate by the stated separable-extension fact. Restricting the conjugacy to L proves that the original embeddings are in the same orbit. Thus the prime ideals and local embedding orbits are naturally identified:
{q⊂OL​:q∣p}≅Gal(Kp​/Kp​)\HomK​(L,Kp​).​
(9)
If L=K(α), an embedding is determined by the image of α, which may be any root of its minimal polynomial f. The orbits on these roots are exactly the root sets of the monic irreducible factors of f over Kp​. Hence
{q∣p}≅{monic irreducible factors of f in Kp​[T]}.​
(10)
If fj​ corresponds to qj​, then Lqj​​≅Kp​[T]/(fj​) and [Lqj​​:Kp​]=degfj​. This is the local factorization and extended absolute values correspondence and does not require an integral power basis.

The polynomial is Eisenstein at 2: every nonleading coefficient is even, and its constant term 126 is not divisible by 4. Therefore it is irreducible over Q and
[M:Q]=5.​
(11)
By part (a), the number of primes above a rational prime is the number of irreducible factors over the corresponding p-adic field.
We use the Newton polygon theorem in this form: a segment of slope −r and horizontal length d accounts for d roots of valuation r, counted with multiplicity, in an algebraic closure with vp​(p)=1. We also use the denominator criterion for Newton polygon irreducibility: if all roots have valuation a/n with gcd(a,n)=1, any monic factor of degree d has constant-term valuation da/n∈Z, hence n∣d.
At 3, the nonzero coefficient points are
(0,2), (2,2), (3,1), (5,0).
(12)
The lower hull is the single segment from (0,2) to (5,0), with slope −2/5. All five roots therefore have valuation 2/5. A proper factor of degree d would require 5∣d, which is impossible for 1≤d<5. Thus g is irreducible over Q3​, giving
#{q⊂OM​:q∣3}=1.​
(13)
The completion has ramification index 5 and residue degree 1: the value 2/5 forces its ramification index to be divisible by 5, and its degree is 5.
At 7, the nonzero coefficient points are
(0,1), (2,1), (3,0), (5,0).
(14)
The lower hull consists of a length-three segment of slope −1/3 and a length-two horizontal segment. Thus three roots have valuation 1/3, and two have valuation zero. Reduction gives
gˉ​(T)=T3(T2−1)=T3(T−1)(T+1)in F7​[T].
(15)
Both 1 and −1 are simple roots, so the Hensel lemma gives two distinct linear factors over Q7​. The remaining cubic accounts for all the valuation-1/3 roots and is irreducible by the same denominator argument. Hence the local factor degrees are 1,1,3, and
#{q⊂OM​:q∣7}=3.​
(16)
The two linear factors give (e,f)=(1,1), and the cubic gives (e,f)=(3,1). These data sum to degree five, as required. This computes the local prime decomposition of T5 plus 6T3 plus 252T2 plus 126. Counting distinct factors merely modulo 3 would not justify the result, since that reduction is T5.

The only possible rational roots of the monic cubic are 1,−1, and neither is a root. Thus f is irreducible. The discriminant of a depressed cubic gives
Δ=−4⋅23−27⋅12=−59.
(17)
It is not a rational square. By the permitted cubic discriminant criterion, the splitting field has Galois group S3​, degree six, and contains its quadratic subfield generated by Δ​. Consequently
K=Q(−59​)⊆L,[L:K]=3,Gal(L/K)=A3​≅C3​.​
(18)
For a rational prime r=59, the reduction of f is separable because its discriminant is a unit at r. Choose a finite extension of Fr​ in which that reduction splits. Part 1(a) lifts it to an unramified extension of Qr​, and the Hensel lemma lifts all the simple roots into that extension. The local splitting field is therefore unramified over Qr​. In particular all primes of L/K above such r are unramified.
At 59, the supplied simple residue root −28 lifts to a root in Q59​. The remaining factor is quadratic, so the local splitting field has degree at most two over Q59​. It contains −59​, which is not a square in Q59​ because its valuation is odd. It is therefore exactly Q59​(−59​). This is also the completion of K at its unique prime above 59: the polynomial T2+59 is Eisenstein there. Thus
LP​=Kp​=Q59​(−59​)
(19)
for a prime P above p. All the other primes above it give the same local conclusion by Galois conjugacy. The relative extension is therefore unramified at 59 as well; indeed that prime splits completely in L/K.
The field K has only complex places, so there is no infinite-place ramification. Hence L/K is unramified everywhere​.
We use the Hilbert class field theorem: for a number field E, its maximal everywhere unramified abelian extension HE​ is finite, contains every such extension, and satisfies Gal(HE​/E)≅ClE​, so [HE​:E]=hE​. The cyclic degree-three extension just constructed is contained in HK​. Therefore
3∣hK​,hK​>1.​
(20)
This is the unramified cubic extension of Q of square root minus fifty-nine; only divisibility of the class number, rather than its exact value, is needed here.

Use the Hilbert class field theorem stated in part (a), and let HL​ be the Hilbert class field of L. Put F=M∩HL​ inside a common algebraic closure.
Any intermediate extension of M/L is totally ramified at the given prime. To verify this even when M/L is not Galois, there is a unique prime of F above p, its residue field embeds into the residue field at the unique prime of M and therefore equals kp​, and the fundamental identity for prime decomposition gives ramification index [F:L]. On the other hand F/L is a subextension of HL​/L, so it is unramified there. Its ramification index is consequently both [F:L] and 1, forcing
M∩HL​=L.​
(21)
Because HL​/L is Galois, this intersection identity gives
[MHL​:M]=[HL​:L]=hL​,Gal(MHL​/M)≅Gal(HL​/L).
(22)
Thus MHL​/M is abelian. We use the standard local fact that unramified extensions remain unramified under finite base extension: after base change each factor is obtained by lifting an extension of the residue field. Since HL​/L is unramified at every finite prime, MHL​/M is also unramified at every finite prime. There are no real places of M, since every embedding of M restricts to an embedding of the totally imaginary field L. Hence there is no infinite-place obstruction.
The maximality of HM​ now implies MHL​⊆HM​. Taking degrees over M proves the class number divisibility under total ramification:
hL​=[MHL​:M]∣[HM​:M]=hM​.​
(23)
The argument uses the degree of an unramified class-field extension, and does not assume that extension of ideals induces an injection of ideal class groups.

We use the following standard facts about cyclotomic fields. For an odd prime ℓ, the unique quadratic subfield of Q(ζℓ​) is
Q((−1)(ℓ−1)/2ℓ​).
(24)
Also Q(ζℓr​) has degree φ(ℓr)=ℓr−1(ℓ−1), and ℓ is totally ramified, with a unique prime and residue field Fℓ​. These are the quadratic subfield of a prime cyclotomic field and total ramification in a prime-power cyclotomic field results.
Take
Er​=Q(ζ59r​)(r≥1).
(25)
Since 59≡3(mod4), every Er​ contains K=Q(−59​), through its subfield E1​. The unique prime above 59 is totally ramified in Er​/K: the absolute ramification index is 58⋅59r−1, the index in K/Q is 2, and the relative index is therefore
e(Er​/K)=29⋅59r−1=[Er​:K].
(26)
The field K has no real places. By part (b), hK​∣hEr​​, and part (a) showed 3∣hK​. Thus
3∣hEr​​for every r≥1.​

Normalize the discrete valuation by vL​(πL​)=1, and put vL​(0)=+∞. For σ=1, define its ramification number
iG​(σ)=mina∈OL​​vL​(σ(a)−a),
(29)
and set iG​(1)=+∞. The minimum exists: all finite values are nonnegative integers, and a nonidentity automorphism moves some integral element. The lower ramification numbering is
Gs​={σ∈G:iG​(σ)≥s+1}(s∈R≥0​).​
(30)
Thus Gs​=G⌈s⌉​, G0​ is the inertia group, and G1​ is the wild inertia group. This states the convention at integer endpoints as well as between them. Automorphisms preserve vL​, so these are normal subgroups of G.
Define the Herbrand function and its inverse by
φL/K​(s)=∫0s​[G0​:Gt​]dt​,ψL/K​=φL/K−1​.
(31)
The integrand is positive and piecewise constant. Since Gt​=1 for sufficiently large t, the function is continuous, strictly increasing and unbounded, and its inverse is defined on all nonnegative reals. The upper ramification numbering is
Gu=GψL/K​(u)​(u≥0).​
(32)
In particular, the denominator in the Herbrand integral involves G0​, even when L/K is not totally ramified. These conventions are also given in Milne's Class Field Theory, Chapter I, §4.
Now use the monogenic hypothesis OL​=OK​[α]. Since L/K is Galois, its minimal polynomial has the distinct conjugates σ(α) as roots, so
f′(α)=∏σ=1​(α−σ(α)).
(33)
Taking valuations proves the first equality.
For every polynomial P∈OK​[T], the difference P(σ(α))−P(α) is divisible in OL​ by σ(α)−α. Every element of OL​ is such a polynomial value, and taking the element α attains the resulting lower bound. Hence
iG​(σ)=vL​(σ(α)−α)(σ=1).
(34)
For an integer iG​(σ)=m≥0, the element σ lies in exactly the groups G0​,…,Gm−1​; if m=0, it lies in none. Summing this count over the nonidentity elements and interchanging the two finite sums gives
vL​(f′(α))=σ=1∑​vL​(σ(α)−α)=s∈Z≥0​∑​(∣Gs​∣−1).​
(35)
This proves the ramification-group sum for a monogenic integer ring. Equivalently it is the different exponent from ramification groups, since the different ideal is generated by f′(α) under the same monogenic hypothesis.

The polynomial is Eisenstein at 3, so a root α generates a totally ramified cubic field F=Q3​(α) and is a uniformizer of F. Its cubic discriminant is
Δ=−4(−3)3−27⋅32=−135=−5⋅33.
(36)
Its 3-adic valuation is 3, so it is not a square. The cubic discriminant criterion therefore gives
Gal(M/Q3​)≅S3​,[M:Q3​]=6.​
(37)
The unique quadratic subfield is generated by Δ​. Since the permitted square root −5​ is in Q3​, this subfield is
E=Q3​(3​).
(38)
It is totally ramified of degree two, by the Eisenstein polynomial T2−3. The ramification index of M/Q3​ is divisible by both the index 3 of F and the index 2 of E. Since the whole degree is 6, it follows that
e(M/Q3​)=6,kM​=F3​.​
(39)
In particular G0​=G.
We use the following standard facts about totally ramified extensions: every uniformizer generates the valuation ring over the base valuation ring, and for a totally ramified Galois extension
Gi​={σ:vM​(σ(π)−π)≥i+1}(i≥0).
(40)
Thus we can use the uniformizer criterion for lower ramification groups. Put s=3​. Since vM​(α)=2 and vM​(s)=3,
π=sα2​,vM​(π)=4−3=1
(41)
is a uniformizer of M.
Let α1​,α2​,α3​ be the three roots. The Galois group acts transitively on their unordered pairs and preserves the valuation, so all differences between distinct roots have the same valuation d. The discriminant identity gives
18=vM​(Δ)=2∑i<j​vM​(αi​−αj​)=6d,
(42)
hence d=3. Thus, for any σ, either σ(α)=α or σ(α)−α has valuation 3.
A nontrivial three-cycle fixes s, since it fixes the quadratic subfield, and sends α to a different root. Also vM​(σ(α)+α)=2: write the sum as 2α+(σ(α)−α), whose two terms have unequal valuations 2,3. Therefore
vM​(σ(π)−π)=3+2−3=2for either nontrivial three-cycle.

σ(π)−π=−sσ(α)2+α2​.
(44)
The numerator has valuation 4: it is 2α2 plus terms of strictly higher valuation if the root changes, and exactly 2α2 if the root is fixed. Thus every transposition has vM​(σ(π)−π)=1. We conclude that the ramification groups of the splitting field of T3 minus 3T plus 3 over Q3 are
G0​=S3​,G1​=A3​≅C3​,Gi​={1}(i≥2).​
(45)

The decomposition group of the chosen prime is its stabilizer under the Galois group:
D=DP∣p​={σ∈G:σ(P)=P}.​
(46)
The transitivity of the Galois action on primes identifies the primes of M above p with G/D through gD↦gP.
Put H=Gal(M/L). Contraction of prime ideals to OL​ is constant on every H-orbit, because every element of H fixes L pointwise. It is surjective onto the primes of L above p, since prime ideals extend in the integral extension OM​/OL​. Moreover, two primes of M contracting to the same prime of L are conjugate by H: the extension M/L is Galois and the same transitivity theorem applies to it.
Thus the fibers of contraction are exactly the H-orbits on G/D. These orbits are the double cosets H\G/D, proving the primes in an intermediate field as double cosets correspondence
H\G/D∼​{q⊂OL​:q∣p},HgD⟼gP∩OL​.​
(47)
Explicitly, replacing g by hgd does not change the contracted prime. Conversely, if gP and g′P contract to the same prime, some h∈H sends the former to the latter, so g′−1hg∈D and Hg′D=HgD. This also verifies the stated order of the two sides of the double coset.

A prime is completely split in an extension of degree n exactly when there are n primes above it. Indeed, the fundamental identity for prime decomposition is ∑q∣p​eq​fq​=n, and every summand is a positive integer. Thus n primes means that every ramification index and residue degree equals one, and conversely.
By part (a), the number of primes of L above p is the number of right D-orbits on H\G. This set has [G:H]=[L:K] elements. It has that many orbits exactly when every orbit is a singleton, equivalently when
HgD=Hgfor all g∈G.
(48)
This condition says gDg−1⊆H for every g, or
D⊆⋂g∈G​g−1Hg=CoreG​(H),
(49)
the subgroup core.
Because M is the Galois closure of L/K, it is generated by all the fields g(L). The fixed field of gHg−1 is g(L), so the fixed field of the core is their compositum, namely M. The Galois correspondence therefore gives CoreG​(H)=1. Hence
p completely split in L⟺D=1.
(50)
In the Galois extension M/K, the number of primes above p is [G:D], so D=1 is equivalent to complete splitting in M. This proves that complete splitting is preserved by Galois closure:
p completely split in L⟺p completely split in M.​
(51)
No prior unramifiedness assumption is needed: complete splitting already forces all ramification indices to be one, and the decomposition-group argument includes the ramified case.
