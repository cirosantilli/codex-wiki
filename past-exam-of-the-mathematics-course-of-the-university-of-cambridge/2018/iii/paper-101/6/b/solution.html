For a discrete valuation ring A,
dimA=1.​
(62)
Let π be a uniformizer, so its unique nonzero maximal ideal is m=(π). We use the defining description of a discrete valuation ring: it is a non-field local ring that is a principal ideal domain, and every nonzero element is uπn for a unit u and an integer n≥0.
Every nonzero proper ideal is therefore (πn) for some n≥1. Such an ideal is a prime ideal only when n=1: for n≥2, ππn−1∈(πn) while neither factor belongs to (πn). The ideal (π) is prime because it is maximal. Also (0) is prime because A is an integral domain. Thus
SpecA={(0),m},(0)⊊m,
(63)
and the longest strict chain has length one.
The original PDF asks about a discrete valuation ring; the local TeX transcription's phrase “discrete variable X” is an extraction error.
