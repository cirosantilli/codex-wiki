Let ej​ be the sequence with 1 in coordinate j and zero elsewhere. In the direct product of rings R=kN, the ideals
(e1​)⊊(e1​,e2​)⊊(e1​,e2​,e3​)⊊⋯
(21)
form a strict ascending chain: every element of (e1​,…,en​) vanishes after coordinate n, whereas en+1​ does not. Hence R is not a Noetherian ring.
Nevertheless, each a=(aj​)∈R has a coordinatewise generalized inverse b=(bj​), defined by bj​=aj−1​ when aj​=0 and bj​=0 otherwise. It satisfies
a=a2b.
(22)
This is the defining property of a Von Neumann regular ring. Fix any prime ideal P, and take a∈P. Since ab∈P, 1−ab∈/P; but
(1−ab)a=0.
(23)
Consequently a/1=0 in the localization at a prime ideal RP​. All elements of PRP​ therefore vanish. By the local ring description in part (a), PRP​ is the unique maximal ideal, so RP​ is a nonzero field. A field has only the ideals zero and itself, and thus is Noetherian.
We have proved the stronger conclusion
RP​ is a field for every prime ideal P⊂R.​
(24)
The argument applies to every prime ideal, without assuming that it comes from a coordinate projection.
