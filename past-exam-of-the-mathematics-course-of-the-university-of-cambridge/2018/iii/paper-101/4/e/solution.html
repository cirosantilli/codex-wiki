No. Take R=k[t] over a field k, and inside R[x]=k[t,x] take
S=k[t]+txk[t,x].
(46)
This is a subring containing R: its elements have arbitrary constant coefficient in k[t] as polynomials in x, and every positive-degree coefficient is divisible by t; that condition is preserved by sums and products. The ring R is Noetherian. Indeed, a nonzero ideal of k[t] has an element g of least degree, and division by g shows that every element of the ideal is a multiple of g. Thus k[t] is a principal ideal domain, and its ideals are finitely generated.
In S, let Jn​=(tx,tx2,…,txn)S​. These form an ascending chain. We show that txn+1∈/Jn​. If it belonged, we could write
txn+1=∑j=1n​sj​txj,sj​∈S.
(47)
Cancel t in the integral domain k[t,x], then apply the evaluation homomorphism t↦0. Every sj​∈S becomes a scalar in k, so this would give
xn+1=∑j=1n​sj​(0,x)xj,sj​(0,x)∈k,
(48)
which is impossible by comparison of degrees. Hence Jn​⊊Jn+1​ for every n, and
k[t]⊆k[t]+txk[t,x]⊆k[t,x],k[t] Noetherian but k[t]+txk[t,x] not Noetherian.​
(49)
This is a constant-plus-ideal non-Noetherian subring construction: the restricted coefficients prevent a finite list of positive powers of x from generating all the required powers.
