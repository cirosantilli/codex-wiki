Here the coefficient fields are R and C. A polynomial ring in one variable over a field is a Euclidean domain, hence a principal ideal domain; its nonzero prime ideals are generated by irreducible polynomials. The fundamental theorem of algebra says that all irreducible polynomials over C are linear. Combining it with complex conjugation shows that the monic irreducible polynomials over R are exactly the linear polynomials and quadratics with no real root. Thus
SpecR[x]={(0)}∪{(x−a):a∈R}∪{((x−a)2+b2):a∈R, b>0}.​
(10)
Every nonzero prime ideal in this list is a maximal ideal; (0) is a prime ideal because R[x] is an integral domain. Likewise,
SpecC[x]={(0)}∪{(x−z):z∈C}.
(11)
The map is contraction of an ideal along the inclusion. It sends (0) to (0). For z=a+ib, the contraction of (x−z) consists of the real polynomials vanishing at z. If b=0, the factor theorem gives the ideal (x−a). If b=0, a real polynomial vanishing at z also vanishes at its complex conjugate z, so it is divisible by (x−z)(x−z)=(x−a)2+b2. Division by this real quadratic gives the converse and hence
f((x−z))={(x−a),((x−a)2+b2),​z=a∈R,z=a+ib, b=0.​​
(12)
Thus each real linear prime ideal has one preimage, each real quadratic prime ideal has the two conjugate preimages (x−a−ib) and (x−a+ib), and (0) has only (0) above it. In particular, f is surjective.
