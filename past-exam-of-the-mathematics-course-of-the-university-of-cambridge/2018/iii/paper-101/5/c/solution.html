Let D be a Dedekind domain. The zero ideal is generated by 0, and D is generated by 1, so consider a nonzero proper ideal I. Choose 0=a∈I. We use these consequences of the unique factorization of ideals in a Dedekind domain: every nonzero ideal has finitely many nonzero prime-ideal valuations in a Dedekind domain; containment reverses the valuations as in part (b); and
vP​(J+K)=min{vP​(J),vP​(K)}.
(52)
The last formula follows from the containment rule: J+K is the smallest ideal containing both J and K, and the product with the indicated minimum exponents is exactly that ideal.
Let T be the finite set of prime ideals dividing (a). For each P∈T, set rP​=vP​(I). Since (a)⊆I, we have rP​≤vP​((a)). The containment rule also gives I⊆PrP​+1, so choose
bP​∈I∖PrP​+1.
(53)
Distinct nonzero prime ideals of D are distinct maximal ideals and hence comaximal ideals; their positive powers are also comaximal. Indeed, if u+v=1 with u∈P, v∈Q, for positive integers m,n expanding (u+v)m+n−1 shows 1∈Pm+Qn, since each term contains either at least m factors of u or at least n factors of v. We use the Chinese remainder theorem in its ideal form: for pairwise comaximal ideals J1​,…,Js​, the map D→∏j​D/Jj​ is surjective. Apply it to the powers PrP​+1 to choose eP​∈D with
eP​≡1(modPrP​+1),eP​≡0(modQrQ​+1)(Q∈T, Q=P).
(54)
Then b=∑P∈T​eP​bP​ belongs to I and satisfies b≡bP​(modPrP​+1) for every P∈T. Thus b∈/PrP​+1, whereas b∈I⊆PrP​, proving vP​((b))=rP​.
For P∈T, therefore,
vP​((a,b))=min{vP​((a)),vP​((b))}=rP​.
(55)
For P∈/T, vP​((a))=0, and (a)⊆I forces vP​(I)=0 as well. Hence (a,b) and I have the same valuation at every nonzero prime ideal. Uniqueness of their factorizations gives
I=(a,b).​
(56)
This proves two generators for an ideal in a Dedekind domain; in fact the first nonzero generator a∈I can be chosen in advance.
