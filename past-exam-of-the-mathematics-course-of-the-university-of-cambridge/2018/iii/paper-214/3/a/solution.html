The Thomson principle states that the effective resistance is the minimum energy of a flow among all unit flows from a to b:
Reff​(a,b)=mindivϑ=1a​−1b​​∑e∈E​re​ϑ(e)2.
(17)
The flow is antisymmetric on oriented edges, and the sum counts each unoriented edge once. The Rayleigh monotonicity principle states that increasing edge resistances, including deleting edges by setting their resistance to infinity, cannot decrease the effective resistance. Decreasing resistances or identifying vertices cannot increase it.
Take a shortest path in a graph from a to b, of length d=dG​(a,b), and send one unit of flow along it, with zero flow on all other edges. Its energy of a flow is d because each edge resistance is one. The Thomson principle gives
Reff​(a,b)≤dG​(a,b).​
(18)
Alternatively, delete all edges outside that path and use the Rayleigh monotonicity principle; the remaining edges are in series, with total resistance d. When a=b, both quantities are zero.
