Let G=E□F. To prove convexity, take u1​,u2​ with finite G values, 0<t<1, and ε>0. By the definition of infimum, choose vi​ with
E(vi​)+F(ui​−vi​)≤G(ui​)+ε.
(64)
Put ut​=tu1​+(1−t)u2​ and vt​=tv1​+(1−t)v2​. Convexity of E and F gives
G(ut​)​≤E(vt​)+F(ut​−vt​)≤t[E(v1​)+F(u1​−v1​)]+(1−t)[E(v2​)+F(u2​−v2​)]≤tG(u1​)+(1−t)G(u2​)+ε.​
(65)
Let ε↓0. If either endpoint value is infinite, the convexity inequality is automatic, and t=0,1 gives equality. Thus the infimal convolution of two convex functions is convex under the stated properness assumption. The proof uses approximate minimizing splits, so no attainment of the inner infimum is assumed.
