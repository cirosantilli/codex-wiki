The Hahn-Banach separation theorem for two convex sets states that disjoint nonempty convex sets A,B in a real locally convex space, with A open, admit a nonzero continuous linear functional ℓ and α∈R such that
ℓ(a)<α≤ℓ(b)(a∈A, b∈B).​
(20)
There need not be a positive uniform gap between the two sets.
For a proof, set C=A−B. It is open and convex, and 0∈/C. Choose c0​∈C, let V=C−c0​, and let p be the Minkowski functional of V. Because V is open, convex and contains zero, p is a finite sublinear functional, and V={x:p(x)<1}. Put z=−c0​. Since z∈/V, p(z)≥1. On Rz define ℓ0​(tz)=tp(z). For t≥0 this equals p(tz); for t<0 it is nonpositive and hence at most p(tz). The Hahn-Banach theorem extends it to a linear ℓ with ℓ≤p. Both p(x) and p(−x) are bounded near zero, so this domination makes ℓ continuous; it is nonzero because ℓ(z)=p(z)≥1. For c∈C,
ℓ(c)=ℓ(c−c0​)−p(z)≤p(c−c0​)−p(z)<1−p(z)≤0.
(21)
Thus ℓ(a)<ℓ(b) for every a∈A,b∈B. Define α=supa∈A​ℓ(a), which is finite because any fixed b∈B bounds it above. Openness of A and nonzeroness of ℓ ensure ℓ(a)<α for every a∈A, and α≤ℓ(b) for every b∈B. This proves the stated separation.

The Commutative Gelfand--Naimark theorem states that a complex commutative unital C-star algebra A is isometrically star-isomorphic to C(ΦA​) through its Gelfand transform, with ΦA​ a compact Hausdorff space. The l-infinity sequence space is such an algebra under coordinatewise multiplication and conjugation, with the supremum norm. Therefore
K=Φℓ∞​,Γ:ℓ∞⟶C(K),Γ(a)(χ)=χ(a)​
(28)
gives the required isometric isomorphism.
Uniqueness as a Banach space representation uses the Banach–Stone theorem, rather than just uniqueness as an algebra representation. To justify the relevant theorem, the Riesz-Markov-Kakutani representation theorem gives
extBC(K)∗​={αδt​:t∈K, ∣α∣=1},
(29)
the extreme points of the dual unit ball of C(K). A norm-one measure whose variation measure is not concentrated at one point splits into two normalized restrictions to disjoint sets of positive variation, and so is not extreme. Conversely, equality in the variation bound shows that any decomposition of αδt​ into the average of two dual-unit-ball elements forces both to be αδt​: after removing the phase, the two measures must be positive and their average is concentrated at t.
If U:C(K)→C(L) is a surjective linear isometry, its dual map preserves these extreme points and their scalar orbits. Hence U∗δs​=u(s)δh(s)​ for a bijection h:L→K and ∣u(s)∣=1. Evaluating at 1 gives the continuous function u=U1, while

The Mazur theorem states that the weak closure and norm closure of a convex set C in a normed vector space coincide:
Cw=C∥⋅∥.​
(33)
Norm closure is contained in weak closure because the weak topology is coarser. Conversely, if x∈/C∥⋅∥, the Hahn-Banach separation theorem provides a continuous real linear functional strictly separating x from that closed convex set. In a complex space this is the real part of a continuous complex linear functional. A weak neighborhood of x then misses C, so x∈/Cw. This proves the equality. It also gives the usual Mazur lemma: if xn​⇀x, then x lies in the norm closure of the convex hull of each tail, so one can choose tail convex combinations yn​ with ∥yn​−x∥<1/n.
Now let K be a weakly compact set in a normed space X. Each φ∈X∗ is bounded on K because it is weakly continuous. The family {Jx:x∈K} in B(X∗,F), where J is the canonical embedding into the bidual, is therefore pointwise bounded. The space X∗ is Banach even if X is not. ∥θ(a)∥=rB​(θ(a))≤rA​(a)=∥a∥.
(52)
For arbitrary x∈A, apply this inequality to x∗x and the C-star identity in both algebras:
∥θ(x)∥2=∥θ(x)∗θ(x)∥=∥θ(x∗x)∥≤∥x∗x∥=∥x∥2.
(53)
Hence ∥θ(x)∥≤∥x∥​ for every x.
Now assume θ is injective. For a fixed x, it is enough to prove norm preservation on C=C∗(1,x∗x), a commutative unital C-star algebra: the preceding square-norm identity will then recover the norm of x. By the Commutative Gelfand--Naimark theorem, identify C with C(K). Let D=θ(C)​⊆B. It is also a commutative unital C-star algebra, and its character space ΦD​ is compact. Each χ∈ΦD​ restricts along θ to a character of C(K), hence to evaluation at a point q(χ)∈K. The map q:ΦD​→K is continuous, so L=q(ΦD​) is closed.
If L=K, the Urysohn lemma provides a nonzero f∈C(K) vanishing on L. Then χ(θ(f))=f(q(χ))=0 for every χ∈ΦD​. Faithfulness of the Gelfand transform in D gives θ(f)=0, contradicting injectivity. Thus q is onto. The isometric Gelfand transform of D now gives
∥θ(f)∥=supχ∈ΦD​​∣χ(θ(f))∣=supt∈K​∣f(t)∣=∥f∥(f∈C).
(54)
Finally,
∥θ(x)∥2=∥θ(x∗x)∥=∥x∗x∥=∥x∥2,∥θ(x)∥=∥x∥.​
(55)
This proves that an injective C-star homomorphism is isometric without assuming its image is closed before norm preservation has been established.
