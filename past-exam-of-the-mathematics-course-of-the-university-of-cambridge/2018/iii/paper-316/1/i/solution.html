Put μ⋆​=GM⋆​. Conservation of specific orbital energy and specific angular momentum gives
a=2Q+q​,h2=Q+q2μ⋆​qQ​,vc2​=μ⋆​(ap​2​−Q+q2​),vp2​=ap​μ⋆​​.
(1)
The last comet expression is the vis-viva equation at the planet's orbit. The planet has only tangential velocity, while the comet's tangential component is vt​=h/ap​. Consequently its prograde relative velocity is exactly, before planetary deflection,
vp2​vrel2​​=3−Q+q2ap​​−2ap​(Q+q)2qQ​​.
(2)
In the near-parabolic encounter limit Q≫ap​, this reduces to
vrel​≃vp​[3−22q/ap​​]1/2.​
(3)
The displayed approximation also requires Q≫ap​. The assumptions Q≫q and q≪ap​ alone do not guarantee it: an orbit with Q=ap​ encounters the planet near apoapsis and has vrel​≃vp​, not 3​vp​.
If all coplanar near-parabolic planet-crossing comets are admitted, so that 0≤q≤ap​, the tangential component ranges from −2​vp​ to +2​vp​ when retrograde orbits are included. Thus
(2​−1)vp​≤vrel​≤(2​+1)vp​.​
(4)
For prograde orbits alone the upper limit is 3​vp​; for the stated deep-crossing limit q≪ap​, both prograde and retrograde encounters approach 3​vp​. These are the speeds outside the planet's gravitational well. The surface impact speed is vimp​=vrel2​+vesc,p2​​, where vesc,p​ is the planetary escape velocity; under negligible gravitational focusing the two speeds agree.
