Assume negligible fragment release speed relative to a circular parent planetesimal. Its initial speed is vb​=GM⋆​/rb​​, but radiation pressure reduces the grain's gravitational parameter to (1−β)GM⋆​. Its specific orbital energy and specific angular momentum are
ε0​=rb​GM⋆​​(β−21​),h02​=GM⋆​rb​.
(31)
For 0≤β<1/2, the resulting dust orbit released from a circular parent ring has
a0​=1−2β(1−β)rb​​,e0​=1−ββ​,q0​=rb​,Q0​=1−2βrb​​.​
(32)
Release is at periapsis: the inherited tangential speed exceeds the new circular speed. Low-β grains remain near the dust birth ring; higher bound β gives increasingly eccentric orbits extending well outside it. Uniform release longitude produces an approximately axisymmetric dust halo, with long residence times near apoapsis. At β=1/2 the conservative release orbit is parabolic; above this radiation-pressure blowout threshold it is initially unbound. For β≥1, zero or repulsive effective gravity also prevents a bound ellipse.
The left panel shows one release longitude; rotating it around the ring fills the halo. At longer times Poynting–Robertson drag also carries bound grains inward, so an initially outward-spanning release distribution does not imply an empty inner region in a continuously supplied, collision-free population.
