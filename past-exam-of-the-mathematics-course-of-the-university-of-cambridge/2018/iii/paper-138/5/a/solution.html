For the preliminary definitions, a block is Re for a primitive central idempotent e, and a module belongs to it when eM=M. For a semisimple algebra, the Artin–Wedderburn theorem identifies its blocks with the factors Mni​​(Di​) in its product decomposition. Each factor is a block of a finite-dimensional algebra.
We prove Ext separation of finite-length modules. The extension hypothesis is Ext1(S,T)=Ext1(T,S)=0 for simples in opposite sets. First, for a simple S∈C1​ and a module N of finite composition length with factors in C2​, we have Ext1(S,N)=0. Induct on the length of N: for 0→N′→N→T→0 with T simple, the long exact sequence of the Ext functor contains
Ext1(S,N′)⟶Ext1(S,N)⟶Ext1(S,T),
(26)
whose outer groups are zero. The same argument works with the two sets interchanged.
Now induct on the length of M, with M=0 immediate. Take a simple quotient in 0→N→M→S→0. By induction, N=N1​⊕N2​ with factors in the respective sets. Suppose S∈C1​; the other case is symmetric. Quotienting by N1​ gives
0⟶N2​⟶M/N1​⟶S⟶0.
(27)
This splits by the preceding Ext vanishing. Let U1​ be the inverse image in M of the chosen complementary copy of S. Then U1​∩N2​=0, U1​+N2​=M, and 0→N1​→U1​→S→0. Thus U1​ has only factors in C1​, while U2​=N2​ has only factors in C2​.
If two finite-length modules have factors in disjoint sets, any homomorphism between them is zero: a nonzero image would, by the Jordan–Hölder theorem, have a simple factor belonging to both sets. For any submodule V of M with factors in C1​, projection onto U2​ is consequently zero, so V⊂U1​. The analogous argument applies to U2​. Hence
M=U1​⊕U2​,Ui​ is the unique largest submodule with factors in Ci​​.
(28)
In particular both summands are preserved by every endomorphism of M.
