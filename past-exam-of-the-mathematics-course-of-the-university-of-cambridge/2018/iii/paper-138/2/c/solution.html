Past exam of the mathematics course of the University of Cambridge / 2018 / iii / Paper 138 / 2 / c / Solution

 ... Past exam of the mathematics course of the University of Cambridge 2018 iii Paper 138 2 c
OurBigBook.com Words: 185
A nonabelian simple group is a perfect group, so every one-dimensional group representation, a homomorphism to the abelian group k×, is trivial. Thus a nontrivial irreducible Brauer character cannot have degree 1.
Suppose instead that its representation has dimension 2, working over a splitting extension if necessary. Its determinant is a one-dimensional representation and is therefore trivial. Its kernel is a normal subgroup, and the representation is nontrivial, so simplicity makes it faithful. Consequently G embeds in SL2​(k).
We use the Feit–Thompson theorem: every finite group of odd order is solvable. Therefore this nonabelian simple group has even order, and Cauchy's theorem for finite groups supplies an involution t. In odd characteristic, its representing matrix A satisfies A2=I and is diagonalizable with eigenvalues in {1,−1}. Since detA=1, it is I or −I. Faithfulness excludes I, so t acts as the scalar matrix −I. It commutes with the whole image; faithfulness then makes t central in G, contradicting nonabelian simplicity. Hence χ(1)>2​.
The odd order theorem is the deep standard group-theoretic input in this proof; its use is explicit rather than hidden in an unsupported assertion that G has an involution.
