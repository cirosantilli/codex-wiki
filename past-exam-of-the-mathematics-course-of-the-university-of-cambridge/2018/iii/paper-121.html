i OurBigBook.com
The Tarski-Vaught test says that (M,∈) is an elementary substructure of (N,∈) if and only if every existential statement true in N with parameters from M has a witness in M that satisfies its matrix in N. Explicitly, for every first-order formula ψ(x,yˉ​) and every parameter tuple aˉ∈M∣yˉ​∣, require
N⊨∃xψ(x,aˉ)⟹∃b∈MN⊨ψ(b,aˉ).​
(1)
The truth of the matrix in this test is evaluated in the larger structure N. Under this criterion, (M,∈)≺(N,∈) means that all first-order formulas with parameters from M have identical truth values in the two structures.

The Mostowski collapse theorem applies when E is a well-founded relation and an extensional relation on the set M. Extensionality here means that different points have different sets of E-predecessors. There is then a unique transitive set T and a unique bijection π:M→T such that
xEy⟺π(x)∈π(y).​
(2)
The collapsing map is characterized by well-founded relation recursion:
π(x)={π(y):y∈M, yEx}.
(3)
Thus (M,E) is isomorphic to (T,∈). Both well-foundedness and extensionality are hypotheses of the theorem; well-foundedness alone does not make the recursive map injective.

A cardinal number is identified with an initial ordinal, namely an ordinal not in bijection with any smaller ordinal. An inaccessible cardinal, in the strong sense used here, is an uncountable regular cardinal that is also a strong limit cardinal. Thus
κ>ℵ0​,cf(κ)=κ,2λ<κ for every cardinal λ<κ.​
(4)
A worldly cardinal is a cardinal number whose level of the Von Neumann hierarchy satisfies all of ZFC:
(Vκ​,∈)⊨ZFC.​
(5)
Worldliness is this model-theoretic condition; regularity is not part of its definition. The original PDF asks for these two notions; the extra comma in the local TeX makes “cardinal” look like a separate requested item.

a OurBigBook.com
We construct the two transitive models together, taking care that the later collapse really retains the earlier model. First, a worldly cardinal κ is greater than ω1​: ZFC in Vκ​ contains the full power set P(ω), and the axiom of choice there gives a bijection between it and an ordinal γ<κ. That power set is externally uncountable by Cantor theorem, so γ≥ω1​. Infinity rules out the finite cardinals and ω before this argument is applied.
By the Downward Lowenheim-Skolem theorem, choose a countable elementary substructure X≺(Vκ​,∈). Its inherited membership is a well-founded relation, and elementarity transfers extensionality from Vκ​. Apply the Mostowski collapse theorem to obtain a countable transitive model M of ZFC. Because M is a countable transitive set, it is a hereditarily countable set and its rank of a set is below ω1​. In particular, M∈Vκ​.
Now take the Skolem hull in Vκ​ of the seed
S=ω1​∪M∪{ω1​,M}.
(9)
Call it H. The seed has external cardinality ℵ1​ and lies inside Vκ​. The hull is an elementary substructure of Vκ​ with ∣H∣=ℵ1​: closure under countably many finitary Skolem functions gives the upper bound, and ω1​⊆H gives the lower bound. Let π:H→N be its Mostowski collapse theorem map. Then N is a transitive model of ZFC.
The transitive collapse fixes transitive subsets lemma gives π(m)=m for every m∈M, since M⊆H is transitive. Since also M∈H, the collapse sends that element to M itself. Thus M∈N, and transitivity of N yields the required inclusion:
M∈Nand henceM⊆N.​
(10)
This is the construction of nested transitive models from a worldly cardinal; the next two parts verify its size and ordinal requirements.

The collapse π:H→N is a bijection, so ∣N∣=∣H∣=ℵ1​. Every α<ω1​ and every element of α belong to H. The transitive collapse fixes transitive subsets lemma therefore gives π(α)=α, so every ambient countable ordinal belongs to N.
Moreover, Vκ​ correctly regards each such α as countable. An injection α→ω has rank below max(α,ω)+ω<κ, so a witness belongs to Vκ​. Elementarity gives H⊨ “α is a countable ordinal”. Transporting this statement across the collapse, which fixes α, gives the same statement in N.
The collapse also fixes the element ω1​∈H. No injection ω1​→ω exists externally, hence none belongs to Vκ​; together with the countability witnesses below it, this shows that Vκ​ correctly identifies ω1​ as its first uncountable ordinal. Elementarity and the collapse preserve that identification. Thus the construction gives
∣N∣=ℵ1​,α∈N and N⊨“α is countable” (α<ω1​),(ω1​)N=ω1​.​

Take the formula saying that a set is transitive:
φ1​(x):∀u∈x∀v∈u(v∈x).​
(13)
This is a bounded formula in set theory. If x∈M, transitivity of M ensures that every u∈x and every v∈u is already in M, and the same holds in N. Both structures therefore run through exactly the same bounded witnesses and use the same actual membership relation. They agree on φ1​(x) for every x∈M, proving set-theoretic absoluteness.

Let C(x) be the formula expressing cardinal number status:
φ2​(x)=C(x):x is an ordinal ∧ ∀α∈x¬∃f(f is a bijection α→x).​
(14)
Ordinalhood and the assertion that a given set is a bijection between fixed sets are each expressible by a bounded formula in set theory. If M regarded x as a non-cardinal ordinal, a witnessing smaller ordinal and bijection would remain present and valid in N. Ordinalhood itself is absolute between the transitive models. Thus N⊨C(x) implies M⊨C(x): this is downward absoluteness of cardinalhood.
For failure in the other direction, put β=(ω1​)M. This ordinal belongs to M, and M⊨C(β) with β>ω. Externally β is countable, since β⊆M and M is countable. By the construction of N, it contains a countability witness for β, and hence a bijection ω→β. Therefore N⊨¬C(β). This formula is downward absolute and fails to be upward absolute.

The constructible hierarchy is defined by transfinite recursion using the definable power set. With parameters permitted in the internal definitions, write Def(A,1) for the definable subsets of A. Then
L0​=∅,Lα+1​=Def(Lα​,1),Lλ​=α<λ⋃​Lα​(λ a nonzero limit ordinal).​

We prove simultaneously that each Lα​ is a transitive set and that Lα​⊆Lα+1​. The key observation is that if A is transitive and x∈A, then x⊆A and
x={y∈A:(A,∈)⊨y∈x}.
(17)
This is an internal definition with parameter x, so x∈Def(A,1). Hence A⊆Def(A,1). Every z∈Def(A,1) is a subset of A; if y∈z, then y∈A⊆Def(A,1). Thus the definable power set is transitive too.
Start with the empty set. At successor stages the observation proves transitivity and inclusion. At a limit stage the union of the earlier transitive sets is transitive, and the same observation then proves its inclusion in its own definable power set. This completes the transfinite induction and gives
∀αLα​ is transitive.​
(18)

Induct on α using the transitivity and inclusion already established. The zero stage is immediate. Suppose Lα​∩Ord=α. Being an ordinal is expressible by a bounded formula in set theory, for example transitivity, transitivity of all members, and membership comparability. It is therefore absolute for the transitive set Lα​. The subset of its elements that are ordinals is internally definable, so
α={x∈Lα​:x is an ordinal}∈Def(Lα​,1)=Lα+1​.

All members of γ are ordinals, so γ⊆Lα​∩Ord=α and γ≤α. Hence the ordinal part of the successor level is exactly α+1.

Lλ​∩Ord=⋃α<λ​(Lα​∩Ord)=⋃α<λ​α=λ.

Let θ=M∩Ord be the ordinal height of a model of set theory. First θ≥ω1​. Indeed, if θ were countable, the internal axiom of choice would give, for every x∈M, a bijection in M between x and some ordinal below θ. Such a bijection is also valid externally, making x externally countable. In particular every internal rank VξM​, ξ<θ, would be countable. Every element of M lies in one of these internal ranks, so
M=⋃ξ<θ​VξM​
(23)
would be a countable union of countable sets, a contradiction. This is why an uncountable transitive set model has uncountable ordinal height.
For ξ<θ, absoluteness of constructible levels gives LξM​=Lξ​, and that level is an element of M. The reason is that satisfaction in a fixed set structure uses the same domain and finite formulas in both universes, so successor definitions agree; transfinite recursion then also makes limit stages agree. Transitivity consequently gives
Lω1​​⊆Lθ​⊆M.
(24)
It remains to locate a countability witness. Given an ambient countable ordinal α, choose an injection f:α→ω. Since V=L, it belongs to the constructible universe. Its transitive closure, together with f itself, is countable. Choose a countable elementary substructure X≺Lη​ of a sufficiently large limit level containing this closure pointwise. The condensation lemma for the constructible universe identifies the collapse with Lβ​ for some countable β. The collapse fixes f, since all its hereditary members were included. Thus f∈Lβ​⊆Lω1​​⊆M.
The ordinal α itself is in M, since α<ω1​≤θ. Being an injection between these fixed sets is a bounded formula in set theory, so M recognizes f as a countability witness. This proves countable-ordinal correctness under constructibility:
α∈MandM⊨“α is countable”(α<ω1​).​
(25)
The use of condensation supplies a witness below the height of M; merely knowing that f belongs somewhere to L would not be sufficient.

We give a relative-consistency argument using a finite-function collapse to countable size. The assumed consistency implies that ZFC is consistent. Passing to the constructible universe gives consistency of ZFC+V=L, which also satisfies the Generalized continuum hypothesis. Work in this ground theory, let δ=ω1L​, and force with finite partial functions ω⇀δ, ordered by reverse inclusion.
The union of a generic filter H is a total surjection h:ω→δ: prescribing a new domain coordinate is dense, and putting any specified γ<δ into the range is dense. Hence δ becomes countable. Set forcing preserves ordinals, and absoluteness of constructible levels implies that the extension has the same constructible universe as the ground model. Its ω1L​ is therefore still the old δ, so it satisfies the stated countability of constructible omega-one condition.
We must also verify GCH after the collapse. The order has ground-model size δ, hence the δ+-chain condition. By cardinal preservation by chain-condition forcing, all old cardinals at least δ+ survive. Every old ordinal below δ+ has size at most δ and becomes countable, so the old δ+ is exactly the new ω1​.
A name for a subset of a fixed ground-model ordinal λ can be chosen as a set of pairs (ξˇ​,p) with ξ<λ and p a condition: use an antichain deciding membership at each coordinate. Thus the number of such names in the ground model is at most 2∣λ×P∣. For subsets of ω this is 2δ=δ+ by ground-model GCH. In the extension it gives 2ℵ0​≤δ+=ℵ1​, and Cantor theorem gives the reverse lower bound. For every old cardinal λ≥δ+ the bound is
2λ⋅δ=2λ=λ+
(26)
in the ground model. Both λ and λ+ remain cardinals, so again the upper bound and Cantor theorem give the extension's equality 2λ=λ+. These are all its uncountable cardinals. This proves GCH preservation by a finite-function collapse in the required case.
The forcing relative-consistency theorem now yields
Con(ZFC+NC) ⟹ Con(ZFC+NC+GCH).​
(27)
The construction in fact only needs consistency of ZFC. This argument uses the formal inner-model and forcing consistency theorems; it does not infer the existence of a countable transitive model merely from consistency.

Use standard notation for forcing, so q≤p means that q is stronger. Let pˇ​ be the canonical forcing name for the ground-model condition p. Define
τ={(pˇ​,q):p,q∈P, q⊥p}.​
(28)
Here q⊥p means that they are incompatible forcing conditions. The checks and their collection are formed by recursion and Axiom schema of replacement in M, and the displayed set is selected by axiom schema of separation, so this is a forcing name in M.
By evaluation of a forcing name,
val(τ,G)={p∈P:∃q∈G (q⊥p)}.
(29)
If p∈G, directedness of the generic filter gives a common stronger condition for p and each q∈G, so p is absent from this value.
Conversely, for fixed p∈P the set
Dp​={q∈P:q≤p or q⊥p}
(30)
is a dense subset of a forcing order belonging to M. A condition incompatible with p is already in it, and one compatible with p has a common extension below p. Genericity supplies q∈G∩Dp​. If p∈/G, upward closure of G rules out q≤p, hence q⊥p. Therefore
val(τ,G)=P∖G.​
(31)
This forcing name for the complement of a generic filter works without a separativity assumption on the order.

A Delta-system is a family A of sets with a fixed root r such that
A∩B=rwhenever A,B∈A are distinct.​

The Delta-system lemma says that every uncountable family of finite sets has an uncountable subfamily forming a Delta-system. The root may be empty. Finiteness is a hypothesis on the family to which this lemma is applied, rather than a requirement in the general definition of a Delta-system.

Assume M satisfies the countable chain condition for forcing. We prove cardinal preservation by chain-condition forcing using the possible-values lemma for chain-condition forcing, and give the argument explicitly for ccc.
Let κ be an uncountable cardinal of M. If it ceased to be a cardinal, some ordinal μ<κ would admit a surjection onto κ in an extension. By the forcing theorem, there would be a condition p and a forcing name f˙​ such that
p⊩f˙​:μˇ​↠κˇ.
(40)
Inside M, for each ξ<μ choose a maximal antichain in a forcing order below p deciding the ordinal value of f˙​(ξ). Conditions deciding that value are dense below p. The chain condition makes the chosen antichain countable in M, so the corresponding set Bξ​⊆κ of possible decided values is countable in M.
A generic filter containing p meets the downward closure of every such maximal antichain, so its value f(ξ) belongs to Bξ​. Therefore its whole range lies in the ground-model set
B=⋃ξ<μ​Bξ​,∣B∣M≤∣μ∣M⋅ℵ0​<κ.
(41)
The last inequality is infinite cardinal arithmetic and uses only that κ is uncountable and μ<κ; no regularity of κ is needed. Some ordinal in κ∖B exists already in M and cannot occur in the range, contradicting the forced surjectivity.
Finite cardinals cannot collapse, and ω cannot become finite: a finite domain has finite image, and transitive models agree on the natural numbers. Old non-cardinals cannot become cardinals because their old bijections persist. Thus
P is ccc in M⟹CardM[G]=CardM.​
(42)

Let sp​ be the finite stem of a condition p=(np​,sp​,Ap​), and define
f=⋃p∈G​sp​.
(43)
The stems of two conditions in the generic filter agree on their common domain, because they have a common stronger extension. Hence this union is a function. For every ℓ∈ω, the set of conditions with np​≥ℓ is dense: append values from the nonempty infinite reservoir until the desired length is reached. Genericity therefore makes f total on ω. The generic stems, and thus their union, are available in M[G].
Fix g∈M∩ωω and K∈ω. In M form the set
Dg,K​={(n,s,A):∃k (K≤k<n ∧ g(k)<s(k))}.
(44)
We show that it is a dense subset of a forcing order. Given p=(n,s,A), put k=max(n,K). Fill the new positions n,…,k−1 with any fixed element of A, and choose a∈A with a>g(k) for the new position k. This is possible because an infinite subset of ω is unbounded. Let t be the resulting stem of length k+1, and keep the reservoir A unchanged. Then
q=(k+1,t,A)≤p,q∈Dg,K​.
(45)
Every new stem value came from the old reservoir, exactly as required by the extension relation. The construction is performed in M, so Dg,K​∈M and is internally dense.
Genericity gives a condition in G∩Dg,K​. Its witnessing coordinate remains fixed in every later stem and hence in f, so some k≥K satisfies g(k)<f(k). Since this holds for every K, there are infinitely many such k. As g was arbitrary,
f∈ωω∩M[G],∀g∈M∩ωω∃∞k(g(k)<f(k)).​
(46)
Thus this infinite-reservoir stem forcing produces an unbounded real over a model, which is precisely the stipulated meaning of bounding M.
