Use the Minkowski metric ηmn​=diag(−1,1,…,1) and units with speed of light one. The contractions are P2=ηmnPm​Pn​ and (TX′)2=T2ηmn​(Xm)′(Xn)′; (Xm)′Pm​ and X˙mPm​ pair a vector with a covector without another metric. The Lagrange multipliers impose the two first-class constraints
H=21​(P2+T2X′2)=0,D=X′⋅P=0.
(1)
They generate worldsheet diffeomorphisms, so the phase space contains both constrained directions and gauge redundancy. Two first-class constraints remove two canonical pairs, leaving D−2 physical degrees of freedom per point.
In Monge gauge, X0=t and X1=σ. Write the transverse canonical variables as X=(X2,…,XD−1) and P=(P2​,…,PD−1​). Solving the first-class constraints gives
P1​=−X′⋅P,P0​=−E,E=T2(1+∣X′∣2)+∣P∣2+(X′⋅P)2​.
(2)
The negative root selects positive energy. Substitution into the phase-space action gives the Hamiltonian reduction
Ired​=∫dt∫0π​dσ(X˙⋅P−E).​
(3)
For a static segment, P=0, its proper length element is dℓ=1+∣X′∣2​dσ, and Edσ=Tdℓ. Thus the string tension is the rest energy per unit proper length. In particular a straight resting segment has E=T. Monge gauge is a local choice on a string embedding map for which X1 is a valid coordinate; it need not cover folded strings or all endpoint configurations.

The canonical momentum equation is X˙=eP+uX′. Eliminating P from the phase-space action yields the Lagrangian density
L=2e(X˙−uX′)2​−2eT2​X′2.
(4)
This is the Polyakov action after identifying its inverse metric tensor density as
−γ​γμν=Te1​(−1u​uT2e2−u2​),γ=detγμν​.
(5)
The determinant of this matrix is −1, as required in two dimensions. The Weyl transformation leaves the matrix unchanged, so e,u encode the metric modulo its conformal factor.
Define the induced worldsheet metric by gμν​=ηmn​∂μ​Xm∂ν​Xn. Varying the independent inverse metric tensor in the Polyakov action gives
gμν​−21​γμν​γρσgρσ​=0.
(6)
Consequently gμν​=fγμν​, where f=21​γρσgρσ​. For a nondegenerate Lorentzian string worldsheet with the usual compatible time orientation, take f>0, or γμν​=e2ωgμν​. The undetermined function is precisely Weyl invariance. Since −γ​γμνgμν​=2−g​, elimination of the independent metric tensor gives
ING​=−T∫dtdσ−detgμν​​.​
(7)
Thus the Nambu–Goto action measures minus string tension times Lorentzian worldsheet area. The metric elimination holds in the nondegenerate interior; null endpoints are understood as boundary limits.

In conformal gauge, γμν​=e2ωdiag(−1,1). The Weyl invariance of the Polyakov action removes ω, leaving
Iconf​=2T​∫ti​tf​​dt∫0ℓ​dσ(X˙2−X′2).
(8)
Its complete variation is
δIconf​=T∫dtdσ(X′′−X¨)⋅δX+T[∫0ℓ​dσX˙⋅δX]ti​tf​​−T∫dt[X′⋅δX]0ℓ​.
(9)
Fix the string embedding map on the initial and final time slices. The interior Euler-Lagrange field equation is the wave equation
X¨m−Xm′′=0.​
(10)
At each spatial endpoint the remaining variation vanishes under either Dirichlet boundary conditions, δXm=0, or Neumann boundary conditions, Xm′=0, independently for each target-space component. Thus a fixed endpoint or a free endpoint makes the boundary contribution vanish. Fixing a spatial point throughout time imposes Dirichlet boundary conditions on its spatial coordinates, while the time coordinate may retain Neumann boundary conditions.
The independent metric tensor equation must also be retained after gauge fixing. Its Virasoro constraints are
X˙⋅X′=0,X˙2+X′2=0.
(11)
Together with the wave equation, these ensure equivalence to the Nambu–Goto action; varying only the already fixed metric would lose these Virasoro constraints.

Let q=σ/L, with L>0, and let Z=X1+iX2. Direct differentiation gives Z¨=Z′′=−Z/L2, while the time coordinate also satisfies the wave equation. Using the Minkowski metric,
X˙⋅X′=0,X˙2=−cos2q,X′2=cos2q.
(12)
Thus both Virasoro constraints hold, and the induced worldsheet metric is gμν​=cos2qdiag(−1,1). The configuration solves the Nambu–Goto action equations in its interior.
Place the fixed end at σ=0. The first free endpoint has Z′=eit/Lcosq=0 at q=π/2. For the usual single unfolded rotating Nambu–Goto string,
0≤σ≤2πL​,ℓproper​=∫0πL/2​∣Z′∣dσ=L.​
(13)
This parameter interval need not equal the earlier interval [0,π], since that interval was a coordinate convention. The proper length is measured on a constant-X0 slice; the local motion is perpendicular to the string, so there is no longitudinal Lorentz contraction.
All points have the same phase t/L, hence the segment rotates rigidly with angular velocity Ω=1/L. At radius r=Lsinq, its speed is v=r/L. Therefore the free tip moves at the speed of light. This is consistent with its Neumann boundary condition: X′=0 and the Virasoro constraints imply X˙2=0 at the free tip. The null endpoint is a boundary limit, not a nondegenerate interior point.
In this conformal gauge, Pm​=TX˙m​, so the energy density per σ is −P0​=T. Equivalently, the energy density per proper length is T/1−r2/L2​. Thus
E=T∫0πL/2​dσ=2πTL​,Erest​=TL,
(14)
and the rotational kinetic energy is
Erot​=(2π​−1)TL=(1−π2​)E.​
(15)
The angular momentum about the fixed point is
J=∫dσ(X1P2​−X2P1​)=TL∫0πL/2​sin2(σ/L)dσ=4πTL2​.
(16)
Therefore the Regge trajectory is
E=2πTL​,J=πTE2​,β=πT1​.​
(17)
The endpoint conditions alone also admit folded extensions with parameter length (n+21​)πL, n≥0. Their proper length, energy and angular momentum are (2n+1) times the values above, and β=1/[(2n+1)πT]. The displayed answer uses the implicit unfolded-segment convention.

The real canonical variables xm,pm​ describe the center-of-mass position and total momentum of the open string. The nonzero string oscillators are complex Fourier modes with α−km​=(αkm​)∗; the Lagrange multipliers obey λ−j​=λj∗​. The Virasoro constraints satisfy L−j​=Lj∗​, so the multiplier term is real.
For each k>0, the oscillator kinetic term differs from the manifestly real expression
2ki​(α˙k​⋅α−k​−αk​⋅α˙−k​)
(18)
by 2ki​dtd​(αk​⋅α−k​). Thus the written action is real up to a boundary term, which does not change its symplectic form or bulk dynamics. Adding the corresponding endpoint term makes reality exact.
The independent nonzero Poisson brackets are
{xm,pn​}=δnm​,{αjm​,αkn​}=−ijηmnδj+k,0​,j,k=0.​
(19)
All brackets between these independent center-of-mass and oscillator variables vanish. The dependent zero mode is α0m​=pm/πT​, so, if it is used, {xm,α0n​}=ηmn/πT​.
The Fourier coefficients of the Virasoro constraints are
Lj​=21​∑k∈Z​αj−k​⋅αk​,L0​=2πTp2​+∑k=1∞​α−k​⋅αk​.
(20)
Their Poisson brackets are
{Lj​,Lk​}=−i(j−k)Lj+k​.​
(21)
They form the classical Witt algebra, with no Virasoro central extension. In particular they are first-class constraints, closing on the constraint surface, and generate the remaining worldsheet diffeomorphisms rather than independent physical excitations.

In the displayed version of light-cone gauge in string theory, the oscillators have D−2 transverse components and
N=∑k=1∞​α−k​⋅αk​.
(22)
The center-of-mass Poisson brackets remain {xm,pn​}=δnm​, and the transverse oscillator Poisson brackets are {αja​,αkb​}=−ijδabδj+k,0​. Other independent brackets vanish. With ℏ=1, canonical quantization gives the canonical commutation relations
[xm,pn​]=iδnm​,[αja​,αkb​]=jδabδj+k,0​,(αka​)†=α−ka​.
(23)
The oscillator Fock vacuum at momentum p is defined by αka​∣0;p⟩=0 for k>0. It is the ground state of one string, rather than the empty spacetime vacuum. Set aka​=αka​/k​ for k>0. The normal ordering prescription gives the string level operator
N=∑k>0,a​α−ka​αka​=∑k>0,a​k(aka​)†aka​,N∣0;p⟩=0.
(24)
A Fock state basis is obtained by applying ∏k,a​((aka​)†)nka​/nka​!​ to ∣0;p⟩, with string level operator eigenvalue N=∑k,a​knka​. In particular, its level-one states are
α−1a​∣0;p⟩,a=1,…,D−2.​
(25)
They transform as the transverse vector of the little group rotation subgroup SO(D−2), precisely the vector-particle polarizations of a massless vector. A massive vector would instead require D−1 vector-particle polarizations. This conclusion uses a quantization compatible with the Lorentz group of the bosonic string theory.
The classical mass constraint alone has no quantum zero-point energy shift. Its quantum version includes the normal-ordering constant of a string a:
(p2+2πT(N−a))∣phys⟩=0,M2=2πT(N−a).
(26)
Masslessness at level one fixes a=1, so
M12​=0,M02​=−2πT.​
(27)
Thus the ground state is a tachyon. Without the quantum ordering shift, the displayed classical constraint would give M12​=2πT and would not support the stated massless interpretation. In the usual transverse vacuum regularization, a=(D−2)/24; consistency with a=1 also gives the critical dimension of string theory D=26.

For covariant quantization of the bosonic string,
[xm,pn​]=iδnm​,[αjm​,αkn​]=jηmnδj+k,0​,(αkm​)†=α−km​.
(28)
All other commutators between independent canonical variables vanish. The covariant Fock vacuum obeys αkm​∣0;p⟩=0 for every k>0 and every m. Normal ordering moves these annihilating string oscillators to the right and gives
Lj​=21​∑k∈Z​:αj−k​⋅αk​:,L0​=2πTp2​+Ncov​,Ncov​=∑k>0​α−k​⋅αk​.
(29)
Then Ncov​∣0;p⟩=0. Its commutator [Ncov​,α−km​]=kα−km​ grades a Fock state basis by N=∑knkm​, although its inner product is indefinite because of the timelike oscillator.
The quantum Virasoro algebra has central charge D:
[Lj​,Lk​]=(j−k)Lj+k​+12D​j(j2−1)δj+k,0​.​
(30)
The Virasoro central extension comes from commutators needed to order infinite oscillator sums; it is a quantum effect absent from the classical Poisson brackets. Replacing a classical Poisson bracket by a commutator cannot recover that term without a regularized ordering calculation.
If a state were annihilated by every nonzero Lj​, the j=1,k=−1 commutator would imply L0​∣ψ⟩=0. The j=2,k=−2 commutator would then imply (D/2)∣ψ⟩=0. For D>0, only the zero vector is annihilated by all nonzero Virasoro constraints. This explains why only positive modes annihilate a physical string state.
The vacuum is a physical string state when p2=2πTa. At level one all states have the form ∣A;p⟩=Am​α−1m​∣0;p⟩. Since [Lj​,αkm​]=−kαj+km​, the positive-mode Virasoro constraints give
L1​∣A;p⟩=πT​A⋅p​∣0;p⟩,Lj​∣A;p⟩=0(j≥2).
(31)
Thus the complete level-one conditions and norm are
p2=2πT(a−1),A⋅p=0,⟨A;p∣A;p⟩=Am∗​ηmnAn​,​
(32)
with the common vacuum normalization suppressed.
For a>1, choose spacelike momentum pm=(0,k,0,…), k2=2πT(a−1). A purely timelike polarization has A⋅p=0 and norm −1. Hence a negative-norm physical string state exists when a>1.
For a<1, the mass-shell condition gives M2=2πT(1−a)>0. In a rest frame, A⋅p=0 forces A0​=0, leaving D−1 positive-norm vector-particle polarizations, those of a massive vector.
For a=1 and nonzero null momentum, A⋅p=0 leaves a null direction Am​∝pm​. The corresponding null string state is p⋅α−1​∣0;p⟩=πT​L−1​∣0;p⟩. It is orthogonal to every physical string state because L1​ annihilates them. Quotienting by this gauge redundancy, Am​∼Am​+ζpm​, leaves D−2 positive vector-particle polarizations. Thus the level-one spectrum agrees with light-cone gauge in string theory at a=1. This level-one argument alone does not establish consistency or absence of negative norms at all higher levels.

Use δF=ϵ{F,φ} with the canonical Poisson bracket. The first-class constraint generates
δxm=ϵpm,δpm​=0,δe=ϵ˙.​
(33)
Indeed the variation of the relativistic particle phase-space action is
δI=∫dt{21​dtd​[ϵ(p2−M2)]+(ϵ˙−δe)φ}.
(34)
The transformation is therefore a gauge invariance when its parameter vanishes at the time endpoints, or suitable boundary conditions remove the total derivative.
On a fixed interval of parameter length Δt, the proper-time modulus
s=Δt1​∫ti​tf​​e(t)dt
(35)
is unchanged by these gauge transformations. Choosing ϵ(t)=∫ti​t​(s−e(u))du sets e+δe=s, with ϵ(ti​)=ϵ(tf​)=0. Thus the nonconstant part of the worldline einbein can be fixed, but its constant modulus must still be integrated over. Fixing e=1 as well would remove inequivalent values of the proper-time modulus; it is legitimate only if the parameter interval is allowed to vary instead. The name proper-time modulus refers to the Schwinger proper-time parameter; after eliminating p, the geometric proper length for a massive on-shell trajectory is M∫edt in this normalization.
For the gauge fixing functional F=e−s, its variation is δF=ϵ˙. The Faddeev-Popov determinant is consequently
ΔFP​=det[∂t​δ(t−t′)].
(36)
The Grassmann Gaussian integral represents this determinant using anticommuting Faddeev-Popov ghost fields b,c:
IFP​=i∫dtbc˙,∫DbDceiIFP​ ∝ det(∂t​).​
(37)
The phase and normalization of the determinant depend on the measure convention. Its domain carries the chosen boundary conditions: on an interval the constant modulus is excluded from the gauge-fixed directions, and on a periodic worldline the constant ghost zero mode in field theory is removed with the residual gauge volume treated separately. A bare determinant with such zero modes left in would vanish.

The Jacobi identity for the Poisson bracket implies
(fij​lfkl​m+fjk​lfil​m+fki​lfjl​m)φm​=0.
(38)
For first-class constraints with linearly independent differentials this gives f[ij​lfk]l​m=0, the Jacobi identity for the structure constants of the constraint algebra. Independence is an implicit assumption: for constraints obeying identities or vanishing identically, only the contracted identity follows, and arbitrary coefficients multiplying such constraints need not satisfy a Lie algebra identity.
With G=ϵiφi​ and δF={F,G}, the canonical variables transform as
δqI=ϵi∂pI​∂φi​​,δpI​=−ϵi∂qI∂φi​​.​

δI=[pI​δqI−ϵiφi​]ti​tf​​+∫dt(ϵ˙i+ϵjλkfjk​i−δλi)φi​.
(40)

Mij​=δji​∂t​+λˉkfjk​i.
(41)

IFP​=i∫dtbi​(c˙i+cjλˉkfjk​i).​

The original PDF has a sign error in the stated multiplier transformation; the TeX transcription has the consistent plus sign. Keeping the PDF's displayed minus sign mechanically would instead give i∫dtbi​(c˙i−cjλˉkfjk​i). As for the particle, constant multiplier moduli and any residual zero mode in field theory must be handled separately; the constant gauge fixing is understood locally on the gauge orbit.

Use the classically equivalent Polyakov action, fix worldsheet diffeomorphisms and Weyl invariance, and perform a Wick rotation of the string worldsheet. In complex coordinates, the worldsheet ghost action for the closed string is
Sbc​=2π1​∫d2z(bzz​∂ˉcz+bˉzˉzˉ​∂cˉzˉ).​
(43)
The worldsheet ghost fields are anticommuting. Their conformal weights are b:(2,0), c:(−1,0) and bˉ:(0,2), cˉ:(0,−1). The two chiral sectors are counted separately.
In either chiral sector, each embedding coordinate is a free boson conformal field theory with central charge one. For the bc system, putting J=2 in cbc​=−2(6J2−6J+1) gives cbc​=−26. Cancellation of the worldsheet Weyl anomaly requires
ctot​=D−26=0,D=26.​
(44)
The antiholomorphic sector gives the same equation, rather than doubling the required critical dimension of string theory. This dimension count assumes flat target spacetime with just the embedding fields; more general internal conformal field theories change the matter accounting.
For the spinning string, the worldsheet Majorana fermions have conformal weight 1/2. At J=1/2, the complex anticommuting bc system has c=1; a real Majorana spinor contributes half of this. Hence D real worldsheet Majorana fermions contribute D/2 per chiral sector. Ramond sector versus Neveu–Schwarz sector changes their mode numbers and vacuum energies, not this local central charge.
The additional local worldsheet supersymmetry has a fermionic gauge parameter, so its superconformal ghosts are commuting fields forming a beta-gamma system, with conformal weights β:3/2 and γ:−1/2 in the holomorphic sector. Commuting statistics reverse the sign of the bc system formula, giving
cβγ​=2(6(23​)2−6(23​)+1)=11.
(45)
Together with the reparameterization worldsheet ghost fields, their total central charge is −26+11=−15. Thus
ctot​=D+2D​−26+11=0,D=10.​
(46)
These cancellations make the string worldsheet gauge symmetries compatible with quantization; in the BRST symmetry formulation they enter the nilpotence condition for the BRST operator.

The closed string already contains the particle required for quantum gravity. For bosonic string theory in its critical dimension of string theory, the mass-shell condition and closed-string level matching are
M2=α′4​(N−1),N=N,α′=2πT1​.
(47)
At N=N=1, the states ϵmn​α−1m​α−1n​∣0;k⟩ are massless. After imposing the Virasoro constraints and quotienting null string states, the transverse polarization tensor decomposes into symmetric trace-free, antisymmetric and scalar pieces under SO(D−2). They describe a graviton, the Kalb–Ramond field and the dilaton. In particular, the symmetric trace-free sector is a massless spin-two graviton. Its number of degrees of freedom is D(D−3)/2.
The state–operator correspondence associates each physical string state with a string vertex operator. For example, the matter part of a massless closed-string vertex operator is
Vϵ,k​(z,zˉ)=ϵmn​:∂Xm∂ˉXneik⋅X:,k2=0,kmϵmn​=knϵmn​=0.
(48)
Its conformal weights are (1,1), so its integrated form ∫d2zVϵ,k​ is invariant under changes of string worldsheet coordinates. At fixed insertion positions it is accompanied by ccˉ from the worldsheet ghost fields. Physical vertices represent BRST cohomology classes; longitudinal changes of the polarization tensor are BRST-exact operators and decouple from physical scattering amplitudes. For the graviton, this string-state gauge redundancy becomes the linearized target-space diffeomorphism hmn​↦hmn​+∂m​ξn​+∂n​ξm​.
The Polyakov path integral computes a closed-string scattering amplitude by inserting the external string vertex operators, integrating their unfixed positions and the inequivalent worldsheet moduli, and including the worldsheet ghost fields. On a Riemann sphere three positions are fixed by the residual conformal group. For example, contractions of tachyon vertex operators produce the Koba-Nielsen factor, whose position integral gives the Virasoro–Shapiro amplitude.
The string dual resonance property means that one crossing-symmetric scattering amplitude has equivalent expansions in the different channels: its poles exhibit intermediate string states in each channel, rather than separate channel contributions being added again. The scattering-amplitude factorization at a pole identifies the intermediate particle and its couplings. Set 8πT=1, so α′=4 and the closed-string tachyon has M2=−1. For four such external states the Mandelstam variables obey s+t+u=−4. The Virasoro–Shapiro amplitude has generic s-channel poles at s=−1,0,1,…, exactly the bosonic string mass spectrum in these units.
The massless pole gives an especially direct check. With overall normalization C and u=−4−s−t, the Gamma function recurrence gives
lims→0​sA(s,t)=CΓ(−2−t)Γ(2+t)Γ(−1−t)Γ(3+t)​=−C(t+2)2.
(49)
Thus
A(s,t)=−sC(t+2)2​+O(1)(s→0).​
(50)
The quadratic residue in t contains a spin-two exchange. A scalar exchange alone could not give this angular dependence. Combined with the known massless spectrum and scattering-amplitude factorization, it identifies exchange of the graviton, with possible scalar contributions from the dilaton; the antisymmetric Kalb–Ramond field does not couple to two identical scalar tachyons here. Consequently the graviton participates in interactions, rather than being an isolated free state.
Decoupling longitudinal graviton polarizations forces a universal coupling to the conserved stress-energy tensor. Consistency of this massless spin-two gauge invariance extends the linearized coupling to the nonlinear dynamics of general relativity. At distances large compared with α′​, the gravitational sector of the effective action begins with the Einstein-Hilbert action, alongside dilaton and Kalb–Ramond field terms and higher-derivative string corrections. The infinite tower of string states supplies the short-distance completion of this quantum gravity expansion.
Interactions are organized by string worldsheet topology. A constant dilaton gives the string coupling gs​=eΦ0​, and a connected oriented surface of genus g has Euler characteristic χ=2−2g. Its weight is gs−χ​; with n normalized external vertices,
An​=g=0∑∞​gs2g−2+n​Ag,n​.​
(51)
The sphere is tree order, the torus is one loop, and each additional handle adds a factor gs2​. Each coefficient is an integral over worldsheet moduli, so this is string perturbation theory in gs​, with an independent low-energy expansion in α′.
For the one-loop vacuum contribution, torus modular invariance identifies τ with (aτ+b)/(cτ+d), ad−bc=1. Integration is over the standard fundamental domain of the modular group,
F={τ=τ1​+iτ2​: ∣τ1​∣≤21​, ∣τ∣≥1, τ2​>0},τ2​≥23​​.
(52)
The potential short-proper-time region τ2​→0, responsible for a point-particle ultraviolet divergence, is absent. It would count metrics already represented elsewhere in F. Torus modular invariance is therefore the geometric reason for one-loop ultraviolet finiteness. More explicitly, in D=26 the vacuum integrand is proportional to d2ττ2−14​∣η(τ)∣−48, with η the Dedekind eta function; the complete expression is invariant under the modular group.
Ultraviolet finiteness does not make the bosonic one-loop vacuum energy finite. The remaining long-tube region τ2​→∞ is an infrared divergence from the tachyon: ∣η(τ)∣−48∼e4πτ2​. It signals instability of the bosonic vacuum. Tachyon-free consistent backgrounds remove this particular obstruction, though other infrared effects must still be treated. The ultraviolet improvement comes from the extended string and the complete spectrum together with the worldsheet gauge identifications; the genus expansion remains a perturbative description of quantum gravity.
