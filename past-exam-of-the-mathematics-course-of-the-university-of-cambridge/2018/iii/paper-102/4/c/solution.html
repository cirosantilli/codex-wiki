 ... Past exam of the mathematics course of the University of Cambridge 2018 iii Paper 102 4 c

In the defining representation of the symplectic Lie algebra sp4​, the weights are ε1​,ε2​,−ε1​,−ε2​. Their highest weight, for the root basis of part (b), is therefore
highest weight(V)=ε1​=ω1​.​
(30)
The flip on the tensor square commutes with the action of sp4​, giving V⊗V=Sym2V⊕Λ2V with dimensions 10 and 6.
If v1​ is a highest-weight vector of weight ε1​, then v1​⊗v1​ is a highest-weight vector of weight 2ω1​ in the symmetric square. By Weyl complete reducibility theorem, this ensures an irreducible summand V(2ω1​) occurs. Its dimension is 10 by part (b), exhausting the symmetric square.
Let ω denote the preserved symplectic form. The symplectic contraction of an exterior square is the nonzero equivariant map
c:Λ2V⟶C,c(v∧w)=ω(v,w),
(31)
where the target is a trivial Lie algebra representation. Thus its kernel has dimension 5. Choose a weight vector v2​ of weight ε2​ in a symplectic basis, with ω(v1​,v2​)=0. The vector v1​∧v2​ belongs to this kernel and has weight ε1​+ε2​=ω2​. It is a highest-weight vector: none of ω2​+α, for α∈Φ+, is a weight of Λ2V, whose weights are ±ε1​±ε2​ and zero. Hence the kernel contains V(ω2​); its dimension 5 exhausts the kernel. Weyl complete reducibility theorem supplies a complementary invariant line V(0).
The resulting tensor-square decomposition of the defining sp4 representation is
V⊗V≅V(2ω1​)⊕V(ω2​)⊕V(0),16=10+5+1.​
(32)
