We first prove the useful lemma that dominant root-lattice highest weights have zero weight. Write Q=ZΦ for the root lattice, and let a dominant integral weight λ∈Q be the highest weight of a finite-dimensional irreducible representation.
First, λ is a nonnegative integral combination of simple roots. Indeed, write λ=λ+​−λ−​, separating its positive and negative coefficients in the root basis. The two parts have disjoint supports, and distinct simple roots have nonpositive inner product, so (λ+​,λ−​)≤0. If λ−​=0, then
(λ,λ−​)=(λ+​,λ−​)−(λ−​,λ−​)<0.
(36)
On the other hand, dominance gives (λ,αi​)≥0 for each simple root, hence (λ,λ−​)≥0. This contradiction proves λ−​=0.
Now suppose a nonzero weight μ=∑i​mi​αi​ has all mi​∈Z≥0​. The identity
0<(μ,μ)=∑i​mi​(μ,αi​)
(37)
shows that some i satisfies mi​>0 and ⟨μ,αi∨​⟩>0. We use the standard sl2 Lie algebra fact that its lowering operator is injective on any positive h eigenspace in a finite-dimensional representation. This follows from the classification of finite-dimensional sl2 representations: in each irreducible V(n), the only weight killed by the lowering operator is the lowest weight −n≤0.
Consequently a nonzero vector of weight μ lowers to a nonzero vector of weight μ−αi​. Its simple-root coefficients remain nonnegative and their sum decreases by one. Starting at λ, repeated lowering must therefore reach the zero weight. Notice that intermediate weights need not remain dominant; positivity of the chosen coroot pairing is enough at each step.
For a minuscule representation, every weight belongs to Wλ, so the zero weight just obtained lies in this orbit. Every Weyl group element is invertible, and wλ=0 forces λ=0. This proves that minuscule weights in the root lattice are zero. Under the assumption X=Q, every possible highest weight lies in Q, hence
X=ZΦ ⟹ the only minuscule irreducible representation is V(0), the trivial one.​
(38)
Here V(0) is the one-dimensional trivial Lie algebra representation, by the classification of finite-dimensional irreducible highest-weight representations.
