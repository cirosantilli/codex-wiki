Let Rs​ be the finite, nonempty set of available routes for source-sink pair s, and write xr​ for a route flow. The link-route incidence matrix has Ajr​=1 when route r uses link j, and zero otherwise. The source-sink route incidence matrix has Hsr​=1 when r∈Rs​. Thus y=Ax gives link throughputs and f=Hx gives aggregate source-sink flows. A route's delay is
dr​(x)=∑j​Ajr​Dj​(yj​).
(15)
A Wardrop equilibrium uses only routes of minimum delay for their source-sink pair:
dr​(x)≥λs​:=q∈Rs​min​dq​(x),xr​>0 ⟹ dr​(x)=λs​(r∈Rs​).​
(16)
An infinitesimal user cannot improve its delay by changing route.
For fixed fs​≥0, the feasible route flows form a nonempty compact convex set: for each s, xr​≥0 and ∑r∈Rs​​xr​=fs​. Define the Beckmann potential
V(y)=∑j​∫0yj​​Dj​(u)du.
(17)
It is continuous, so the extreme value theorem gives a minimizer. Since every Dj​ is strictly increasing, V is a strictly convex function of y. Its route-flow gradient is ∂V(Ax)/∂xr​=dr​(x).
If an occupied route r∈Rs​ has greater delay than another route q∈Rs​, moving a small amount of flow from r to q gives a negative directional derivative, contradicting minimality. Conversely, at a Wardrop equilibrium, for any feasible x,
∇x​V(Ax)⋅(x−x)​=s∑​r∈Rs​∑​dr​(x)(xr​−xr​)≥s∑​λs​r∈Rs​∑​(xr​−xr​)=0.​
(18)
The first-order condition for convex optimization therefore proves optimality. Consequently
Wardrop equilibria are exactly the minimizers of V(Ax) subject to x≥0, Hx=f.​
(19)
If two minimizers had different y, their midpoint would give a strictly smaller Beckmann potential. Thus the equilibrium link throughputs are unique. The route flows need not be unique, since V(Ax) need not be a strictly convex function of x.
For a concrete example, take two successive stages of parallel links: a,b in the first stage and c,d in the second, with one source-sink pair and four routes ac,ad,bc,bd. Put Dj​(y)=y on every link and total flow f=2. All allocations
(xac​,xad​,xbc​,xbd​)=(t,1−t,1−t,t),0≤t≤1​
(20)
have the same link throughputs (1,1,1,1) and every route has delay two. These distinct allocations are all Wardrop equilibria.
For elastic demand, use the standard physical convention that Dj​≥0 and Bs​:[0,∞)→[0,∞) is finite, continuous, and strictly decreasing. Set Ms​=Bs​(0) and ℓs​=limλ→∞​Bs​(λ), and define the inverse demand function Ps​=Bs−1​ on (ℓs​,Ms​]. It is continuous and strictly decreasing, with Ps​(Ms​)=0 and Ps​(u)→∞ as u↓ℓs​. Choose any reference value fs0​∈(ℓs​,Ms​) and set
G(f)=s∑​∫fs0​fs​​Ps​(u)du,Ψ(x)=V(Ax)−G(Hx).​
(21)
Each summand of −G is a strictly convex function. The reference values only add a constant to the objective. In the usual case ℓs​=0, one may use a lower limit zero whenever the improper integral is finite; that integrability is not guaranteed merely by continuity of Bs​.
Minimize Ψ over the compact feasible set x≥0, ℓs​≤(Hx)s​≤Ms​, interpreting the objective at fs​=ℓs​ by its one-sided limit, which can be +∞. This extension is sequentially lower semicontinuous, and an interior-demand feasible point has finite value, so a minimizer exists. It cannot have fs​=ℓs​: adding a small amount on any route for s has bounded marginal link cost, whereas its marginal demand benefit Ps​(fs​) tends to infinity. Thus fs​>ℓs​.
For ℓs​<fs​<Ms​, the route-flow partial derivative is dr​−Ps​(fs​). The first-order conditions for convex optimization give
dr​≥Ps​(fs​),xr​>0 ⟹ dr​=Ps​(fs​).
(22)
If fs​=Ms​, reducing any occupied route cannot improve the objective only if that route has zero delay; nonnegative link delays then give λs​=0=Ps​(Ms​). Therefore the conditions in every case are
λs​=Ps​(fs​),fs​=Bs​(λs​),xr​>0 ⟹ dr​=λs​.​
(23)
Conversely these conditions give the nonnegative directional derivative for every feasible competitor and hence minimize the convex function Ψ. Allowing f explicitly yields the stated form with Hx=f and Ax=y, with inverse-demand endpoint conventions understood. In particular, G is defined on its demand range, rather than evaluating Bs−1​ outside that range.
Strict convexity in the pair (y,f) proves that the equilibrium aggregate source-sink flows and link throughputs are unique. Individual route flows may still be nonunique. Nonnegative, finite demand, nonnegative delays, finite route sets, and a usable route for each source-sink pair are the implicit modeling assumptions needed for the existence assertions.
