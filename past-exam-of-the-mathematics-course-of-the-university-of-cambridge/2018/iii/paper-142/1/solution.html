For the First Chern class identity, use finite-dimensional classifying maps. A complex line bundle on a compact Hausdorff space embeds in a finite-dimensional trivial vector bundle, so it is a pullback vector bundle of the tautological bundle on some Complex projective space. For the two complex line bundles, the universal tensor product of vector bundles is classified by the map
m:CPN×CPM⟶CP(N+1)(M+1)−1,([v],[w])⟼[v⊗w].
(1)
Its restriction to either factor pulls the tautological bundle back to the tautological bundle on that factor. The degree-two cohomology of the product is the direct sum of the degree-two cohomology of its factors, so these two restrictions determine its First Chern class. Pulling back to X proves
c1​(L1​⊗L2​)=c1​(L1​)+c1​(L2​).​
(2)
This proves the integral identity, including possible torsion elements in the cohomology of X; an argument using only curvature forms would not detect those torsion elements.
The splitting principle for complex vector bundles says that for a vector bundle E→X there is an iterated projective bundle p:F(E)→X for which p∗ is injective on integral cohomology, and p∗E is a direct sum of complex line bundles. The same assertion holds with rational coefficients, and a common such space can split any finite collection of vector bundles. The injectivity follows at each stage from the projective bundle formula for complex vector bundles; a Hermitian metric splits the resulting filtration into a direct sum.
Write xi​=c1​(Li​) for the Chern roots of the pulled-back vector bundle. Define the degree-2j component of its Chern character by
ch0​(E)=rankE,chj​(E)=j!1​∑i​xij​,ch(E)=∑j≥0​chj​(E).
(3)
The Fundamental theorem of symmetric polynomials expresses each power-sum symmetric polynomial ∑i​xij​ uniquely as an integral polynomial in the elementary symmetric polynomials, which are the Chern classes of E. Thus the definition makes sense on X itself, without choosing actual Chern roots there. On a compact Hausdorff space, each vector bundle is classified by a finite-dimensional Grassmannian; its high-degree Chern character components therefore vanish. Hence this defines a class in the direct sum H2∗(X;Q) even if X has no finite dimension. On more general spaces an infinite Chern character is instead interpreted in the product of the even-degree cohomology groups.
On a common splitting space, the Chern roots of a direct sum are the combined root lists, whereas the First Chern class identity makes the Chern roots of a tensor product of vector bundles the pairwise sums xi​+yj​. Consequently
ch(E⊕F)ch(E⊗F)​=i∑​exi​+j∑​eyj​,=i,j∑​exi​+yj​=(i∑​exi​)(j∑​eyj​).​
(4)
The injectivity in the splitting principle for complex vector bundles descends both identities to X. Additivity extends the Chern character to the Grothendieck group by ch([E]−[F])=ch(E)−ch(F). Multiplicativity and ch(1)=1 then show that the Chern character is a unital ring homomorphism.
For Complex K-theory of complex projective space, let γ be the tautological bundle, put t=1−[γ], and set h=−c1​(γ). The cohomology ring of complex projective space is Z[h]/(hn+1). The CW filtration gives a cofibration
CPn−1⟶CPn⟶S2n.
(5)
The K-theory six-term exact sequence and Complex K-theory of a sphere give, inductively,
K−1(CPn)=0,0⟶Z{bn​}⟶K0(CPn)⟶K0(CPn−1)⟶0.
(6)
Here bn​ is the image of a Bott element from S2n, normalized so that ch(bn​)=hn. This normalization follows from the multiplicative form of Bott periodicity: the Chern character of the degree-two Bott element is an integral generator, and multiplication yields the top-degree generator on S2n. In particular, the abelian group K0(CPn) is a free abelian group of rank n+1.
Assume inductively that 1,t,…,tn−1 form an integral basis on CPn−1. Their lifts, together with bn​, form an integral basis on CPn. Since
ch(t)=1−e−h=h−2!h2​+3!h3​−⋯,
(7)
the Chern characters of this basis are linearly independent over Q: their first nonzero degrees are 0,2,…,2n. Thus the Chern character is injective on K0(CPn). By induction tn restricts to zero on CPn−1, so tn=abn​ for an integer a. Comparing Chern characters gives a=1. Also ch(tn+1)=0, so tn+1=0. This proves the integral answer, rather than merely its rationalization:
K0(CPn)≅Z[t]/(tn+1),t=1−[γ].​
(8)
The induction begins with CP0 a point and t=0.
