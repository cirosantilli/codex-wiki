All Topological K-theory groups below are complex and are indexed modulo two using Bott periodicity. For n≥1, Complex K-theory of a sphere gives
n evenn odd​K0(Sn)Z⊕ZZ​K−1(Sn)0Z​​​
(19)
For an even-dimensional sphere, choose a generator u of Reduced topological K-theory. Its square vanishes: the Chern character sends u to a top-degree cohomology class, whose square is zero, and the Chern character is injective on this torsion-free abelian group. Thus the ring answers are
K0(S2m)≅Z[u]/(u2)(m≥1),K0(S2m+1)≅Z.​
(20)
The exceptional zero-dimensional sphere consists of two points: K0(S0)=Z×Z with coordinatewise multiplication and K−1(S0)=0. In particular its reduced generator is idempotent rather than square-zero.
For the Euler characteristic identity, induct over the cells of a finite CW complex. The starting CW complex consisting of v vertices has K0≅Zv, K−1=0, and χ=v. If X is obtained from A by attaching one d-dimensional cell, the quotient is Sd, and the K-theory six-term exact sequence is
K0(Sd)⟶K0(X)⟶K0(A)⟶K−1(Sd)⟶K−1(X)⟶K−1(A)⟶K0(Sd).
(21)
These abelian groups are finitely generated by induction. Tensoring the exact sequence with Q preserves exactness. The alternating sum of dimensions in a cyclic six-term exact sequence is zero, so
rankK0(X)−rankK−1(X)​=rankK0(A)−rankK−1(A)+rankK0(Sd)−rankK−1(Sd)=χ(A)+(−1)d=χ(X).​
(22)
Therefore
χ(X)=rankK0(X)−rankK−1(X).​
(23)
There is a genuine convention issue in the nilpotence assertion. With the usual definition of Reduced topological K-theory as the kernel of restriction to one basepoint, the assertion needs X to be connected. For X=S0 based at its first point, (0,1) lies in K0(X) and satisfies (0,1)m=(0,1) for every m≥1. Thus it is not a nilpotent element. For an arbitrary finite CW complex, the correct assertion uses the rank map in topological K-theory on every connected component:
I(X)=ker(K0(X)⟶H0(X;Z)).
(24)
For a connected space, I(X)=K0(X).
Here is an induction proving the corrected assertion. On the vertices, I(X)=0. For a positive-dimensional cell attachment A⊂X, the ideal J=ker(K0(X)→K0(A)) is square-zero. Indeed, lift two elements of J to relative topological K-theory K0(X,A). Their product is induced by the reduced diagonal
X/A=Sd⟶Sd∧Sd=S2d.
(25)
This map is null-homotopic, since d<2d and S2d is (2d−1)-connected. The relative product in topological K-theory is therefore zero, giving J2=0. If a∈I(X), its restriction belongs to I(A), so induction gives (a∣A​)m=0 for some m. Then am∈J and a2m=0. We have proved every Topological K-theory class of rank zero on every connected component is a nilpotent element, and hence the requested result for connected X.
