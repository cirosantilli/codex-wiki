Use the standard complex K-theory Thom class convention. For a rank-r complex vector bundle, its K-theory Euler class is the pullback of the K-theory Thom class along the zero section, namely
eK​(E)=λ−1​(E∗)=j=0∑r​(−1)j[ΛjE∗]∈K0(X).​
(33)
Here Λj denotes an exterior power, and a Hermitian metric identifies the dual bundle with the conjugate vector bundle. Choosing λ−1​(E) instead changes the K-theory Euler class by the unit (−1)r[detE], so its kernel and cokernel computations are unchanged.
For the disk bundle D(E) and sphere bundle S(E), the K-theory Thom class identifies Ki(D(E),S(E)) with Ki(X). The exact sequence of this pair becomes the K-theory Gysin sequence of a sphere bundle:
⋯⟶Ki(X) eK​(E)⋅ ​Ki(X)π∗​Ki(S(E))∂​Ki+1(X) eK​(E)⋅ ​Ki+1(X)⟶⋯.​
(34)
The groups are two-periodic by Bott periodicity.
View Real projective space as the circle bundle of γ⊗2 over Complex projective space, where γ→CPn is the tautological bundle. Indeed, S2n+1/{±1}=RP2n+1 maps to S(γ⊗2) by [v]↦v⊗v; in each fiber squaring identifies exactly v and −v.
Use Complex K-theory of complex projective space in the form
R=K0(CPn)=Z[t]/(tn+1),t=1−[γ],K−1(CPn)=0.
(35)
The K-theory Euler class of γ⊗2 is 1−[γ]−2. Multiplying it by the unit −[γ]2 gives 1−[γ]2=2t−t2. The K-theory Gysin sequence of a sphere bundle therefore gives
K0(RP2n+1)≅R/(2t−t2),K−1(RP2n+1)≅ker((2t−t2)⋅:R→R).
(36)
Under the projection q:RP2n+1→CPn, the pullback vector bundle q∗γ is the complexification of a real vector bundle L of the tautological bundle of Real projective space: the fiber map sends [v,z] to zv. Thus t pulls back to 1−[L]. Put μ=[L]−1. The relation L⊗2≅1 gives μ2=−2μ, and tn+1=0 gives 2nμ=0. Hence
K0(RP2n+1)≅Z[μ]/(μ2+2μ,2nμ),μ=[L]−1.​
(37)
For completeness, these relations give precisely the abelian group Z⊕Z/2n. In the basis t,…,tn, the image of multiplication by 2t−t2 is generated by 2t−t2,2t2−t3,…,2tn; its relation matrix is triangular with determinant 2n, and the relations express every tj as 2j−1t. Thus there is exactly one cyclic torsion subgroup of order 2n.
If n≥1 and a=a0​+a1​t+⋯+an​tn is in the kernel, successive coefficients of (2t−t2)a give 2a0​=0, 2a1​−a0​=0, and so on, up to 2an−1​−an−2​=0. Hence a0​=⋯=an−1​=0, with an​ arbitrary. For n=0 the multiplier is zero on R=Z. In all cases,
K−1(RP2n+1)≅Z(n≥0).​
(38)
Finally let E=L⊕k with k≥1, over B=RP2n+1. Since L∗≅L, multiplicativity of the K-theory Euler class gives
eK​(E)=(1−[L])k=(−μ)k=−2k−1μ.
(39)
On K0(B)=Z⊕(Z/2n)μ, the image of multiplication by this K-theory Euler class is the subgroup generated by 2k−1μ: multiplication by an integer produces every such multiple, while multiplication by μ only adds multiples of 2kμ. Therefore
coker(eK​(E):K0(B)→K0(B))≅Z⊕Z/2min(n,k−1).
(40)
Multiplication by μ is zero on K−1(B)≅Z, since 2nμ=0 and this abelian group has no nonzero torsion elements. Thus the K-theory Gysin sequence of a sphere bundle gives a short exact sequence
0⟶Z⊕Z/2min(n,k−1)⟶K0(S(E))⟶Z⟶0.
(41)
It splits as an exact sequence of abelian groups because its quotient is a free abelian group. Consequently
K0(S(L⊕k))≅Z2⊕Z/2min(n,k−1)(n≥0, k≥1).​
(42)
The convention Z/1=0 covers n=0 and k=1. For example, when k=1 the sphere bundle is (S2n+1×S1)/{(v,z)∼(−v,−z)}, a mapping torus of the antipodal map; its Topological K-theory has no nonzero torsion elements, agreeing with the formula. If k=0 is allowed, the rank-zero vector bundle has an empty unit sphere bundle, so K0(S(E))=0 instead.
