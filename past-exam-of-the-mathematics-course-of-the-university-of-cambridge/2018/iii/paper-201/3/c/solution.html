The sigma-algebras Gn​=σ(Sn​,Sn+1​,…) decrease with n, and (b) gives Sn​/n=E[X1​∣Gn​]. The permitted reverse martingale convergence theorem therefore gives an integrable random variable L such that Sn​/n→L almost surely and with convergence in L1. In particular,
EL=limn​E[Sn​/n]=m.
(22)
It remains to prove that L is constant. We must not assume that ⋂n​Gn​ is the tail sigma-algebra of the Xj​; instead use tail measurability of limits of sample averages directly. Define the random variable valued in the extended real numbers L∗=limsupn​Sn​/n. For every fixed N,
L∗=limsupn→∞​nXN+1​+⋯+Xn​​,
(23)
because SN​/n→0 pointwise. Thus L∗ is measurable with respect to σ(XN+1​,XN+2​,…) for every N, and hence to the tail sigma-algebra. Also L∗=L almost surely.
By the Kolmogorov zero-one law, each tail event {L∗≤q}, for a rational number q, has probability zero or one. Since L∗ is finite almost surely, its distribution function can only be that of a constant c: taking c=inf{q∈Q:P(L∗≤q)=1} and using the density of the rational numbers gives L∗=c almost surely. Its expected value identifies c=m. This proves the strong law of large numbers:
nSn​​⟶malmost surely.​
(24)
