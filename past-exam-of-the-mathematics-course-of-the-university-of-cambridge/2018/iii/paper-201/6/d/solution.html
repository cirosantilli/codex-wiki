Let Fn​ be the distribution function of Xn​. The limiting constant zero has distribution function F(x)=0 for x<0 and F(x)=1 for x≥0. For every ε>0, both −ε and ε are continuity points of F. Thus convergence in distribution implies Fn​(−ε)→0 and Fn​(ε)→1. Therefore
0≤P(∣Xn​∣>ε)≤Fn​(−ε)+1−Fn​(ε)⟶0.
(50)
This proves
Xn​d​0 ⟹ Xn​P​0.​
(51)
The same proof after subtracting a fixed real c proves convergence in distribution to a constant implies convergence in probability. A deterministic limit fixes the coupling automatically; the counterexample in (c) exploits a nonconstant limit whose probability distribution alone does not fix that coupling.
