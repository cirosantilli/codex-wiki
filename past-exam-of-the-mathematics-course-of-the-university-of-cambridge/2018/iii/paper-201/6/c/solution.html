Suppose Xn​→X with convergence in probability. For real t and δ>0, the elementary inequality ∣eiu−eiv∣≤min(2,∣u−v∣) gives
​EeitXn​−EeitX​≤∣t∣δ+2P(∣Xn​−X∣>δ).
(48)
First let n→∞, then let δ↓0. The characteristic functions converge pointwise to that of X, which is continuous at zero. The Lévy continuity theorem gives convergence in probability implies convergence in distribution.
For the converse, let Z have the fair Bernoulli distribution, put X=Z, and set Xn​=1−Z for every n. All Xn​ have the same probability distribution as X, so Xn​d​X. But ∣Xn​−X∣=1 always, hence
P(∣Xn​−X∣>1/2)=1for every n.​
(49)
There is no convergence in probability to X. In this example Xn​ does have convergence in probability to 1−Z: the counterexample concerns the converse with the specified limit X, as usual. If one interprets the question as denying convergence in probability to any limit, take Xn​=Z for even n and Xn​=1−Z for odd n. Its probability distribution is still constant, while the two subsequences have distinct limits from almost sure convergence, ruling out any common limit in probability by uniqueness of a limit in probability.
