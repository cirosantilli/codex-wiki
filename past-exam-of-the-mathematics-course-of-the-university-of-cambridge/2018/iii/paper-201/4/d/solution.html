OurBigBook.com Words: 114
Fix an integer k≥1 and consider the independent increments
Δj,k​=B(j+1)/k​−Bj/k​,j=0,1,2,….
(33)
For fixed k these are independent and identically distributed random variables with the normal distribution N(0,1/k), so pk​=P(∣Δj,k​∣>1)>0. The second Borel-Cantelli lemma gives ∣Δj,k​∣>1 for infinitely many j, almost surely. Intersecting these probability-one events over the countably many k preserves probability one.
On any path in this intersection, uniform continuity on [0,∞) would give some δ>0 such that ∣Bt​−Bs​∣<1 whenever ∣t−s∣<δ. Choosing k with 1/k<δ contradicts the existence of an increment ∣Δj,k​∣>1. Therefore Brownian paths are not uniformly continuous on the half-line:
P(B is uniformly continuous on [0,∞))=0.​
(34)
The event is measurable: for continuous paths, uniform continuity can be tested by countably many pairs of nonnegative rational numbers and tolerances 1/m. Brownian motion has uniform continuity on every compact time interval, but almost surely fails it on the whole half-line.
