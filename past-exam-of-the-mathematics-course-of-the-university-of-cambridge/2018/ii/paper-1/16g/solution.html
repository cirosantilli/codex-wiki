Ordinal exponentiation is defined by transfinite recursion:
α0=1,αβ+1=αβα,αλ=supξ<λ​αξ
(104)
when λ is a nonzero limit ordinal. If α≥1, each successor step and each supremum show that β↦αβ is nondecreasing. If α≥2, transfinite induction strengthens this to strict increase: αβ<αβ+1, and the value at a limit exceeds every earlier value.
Strict increase can disappear when the base changes. For example, with ω<ω+1<ω+2,
(ω+1)ω=(ω+2)ω=ωω,
(105)
because for every positive finite n, both finite powers have leading term ωn, and taking the supremum gives ωω.
We next prove
 αβ+γ=αβαγ ​.
(106)
Apply transfinite induction to γ. The zero and successor cases follow directly from the recursive definitions and the associativity of ordinal multiplication. At a limit λ, continuity of ordinal multiplication in its right argument gives
αβ+λ=supξ<λ​αβ+ξ=supξ<λ​αβαξ=αβαλ.
(107)
The superficially similar identity (αβ)γ=αγβγ is false: for α=ω, β=2, and γ=2,
(ω2)2=ω22=ω24=ω222.
(108)
Finally,
 αω1​≥ω1​⟺α≥2 ​, αω1​≥ω2​⟺α≥ω2​ ​.
(109)
For the first equivalence, bases zero and one fail, while strict increase embeds every countable ordinal into the powers of any base α≥2. For the second, α≥ω2​ immediately gives αω1​≥α. Conversely, if α<ω2​, then ∣α∣≤ℵ1​. Every exponent below the first uncountable ordinal ω1​ is countable, so each αβ has cardinality at most ℵ1​; the supremum of ℵ1​ such ordinals still has cardinality at most ℵ1​ and is therefore below the second uncountable ordinal ω2​.
