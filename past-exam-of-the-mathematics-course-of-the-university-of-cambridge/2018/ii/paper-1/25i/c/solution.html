Suppose first that f(X)​=Y. If g∈kerf∗, then g∘f=0, so the zero set of the regular function g contains f(X) and hence its closure Y. Thus g=0 in the coordinate ring k[Y], and f∗ is injective.
Conversely, if f(X)​ were a proper closed subset of Y, its vanishing ideal in k[Y] would contain a nonzero regular function g. Then g vanishes on f(X), so f∗(g)=0, contradicting injectivity. Therefore
 f(X)​=Y⟺f∗:k[Y]→k[X] is injective. ​
(191)
This is the coordinate-ring criterion for a dominant morphism of affine varieties.
For an irreducible affine variety, define dimension by
dimX=trdegk​k(X),k(X)=Frac(k[X]).
(192)
Irreducibility makes both coordinate rings integral domains. The injective map f∗:k[Y]↪k[X] extends to an embedding of function fields
k(Y)↪k(X).
(193)
An algebraically independent family in k(Y) remains algebraically independent in k(X), so the dimension of an irreducible affine variety by transcendence degree gives
 dimX≥dimY. ​
