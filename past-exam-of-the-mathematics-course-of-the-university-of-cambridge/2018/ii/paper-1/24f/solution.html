A function element is a pair (f,D) consisting of a domain D⊆G and a holomorphic function f:D→C belonging to the given complete analytic function. Its germ of a holomorphic function at z∈D is the equivalence class [f]z​, where two elements are equivalent when they agree on a neighbourhood of z.
For every function element (f,D) and sufficiently small open U⊆D, set
Uf​={[f]z​:z∈U}.
(174)
These sets form a basis for the space of germs of holomorphic functions. The projection
π:G→G,π([f]z​)=z,
(175)
restricts to a homeomorphism Uf​→U. The inverse projections π∣Uf​​ are compatible complex charts, making G a Riemann surface and π a covering map and local biholomorphism. In such a chart, the evaluation map on a space of germs has coordinate expression
E∘(π∣Uf​​)−1(z)=f(z).
(176)
It is therefore holomorphic on every chart, hence analytic on each component of G.
Now let f:R→S be a nonconstant analytic map of compact connected Riemann surfaces, and let B be its finite branch-value set. Each point of B is a branch value of a holomorphic map. At every point of R∖f−1(B) the local degree is one, so the holomorphic inverse-function theorem supplies a neighbourhood on which f is biholomorphic. For y∈S∖B, the fibre is finite. Choose disjoint inverse neighbourhoods around all its points and shrink their common image; compactness, or equivalently properness of f, ensures that no additional preimages enter. Thus
f:R∖f−1(B)⟶S∖B
(177)
is an ordinary unramified covering map.
Fix P∈S∖B. Given a loop γ based at P, lift it from every Q∈f−1(P) and send Q to the endpoint of its lift. The path lifting theorem makes this a permutation; concatenation of loops composes the permutations and reversal gives inverses. Its image is the monodromy group of a covering. The punctured surface R∖f−1(B) is path connected. Given two points of the fibre, join them there by a path; its projection is a loop whose lift joins those points. Hence the monodromy action is transitive.
Finally consider
F(z)=z2+z−2=z2z4+1​.
(178)
It has degree four. Its finite critical points satisfy
F′(z)=2z−2z−3=0,z4=1,
(179)
with branch values 2 and −2; the double poles at zero and infinity give the branch value infinity. For a regular value, the fibre is
{z,−z,z−1,−z−1}.
(180)
The two commuting involutions z↦−z and z↦z−1 preserve F and act transitively on this fibre. They generate all four deck transformations, so the covering is normal and its monodromy group is
 H≅V4​=C2​×C2​. ​
(181)
With the displayed ordering of the fibre, its elements are
1,(12)(34),(13)(24),(14)(23).
(182)
This is the monodromy group of z squared plus z to the minus two.
