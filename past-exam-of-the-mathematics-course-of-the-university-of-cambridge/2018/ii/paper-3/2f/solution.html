One form of the Baire category theorem says that a nonempty complete metric space cannot be a countable union of closed nowhere dense sets.
For each nonnegative integer n, put
Zn​={z∈C:f(n)(z)=0}.
(6)
Because f is an entire function, every derivative f(n) is continuous and Zn​ is closed. No f(n) is identically zero: if f(n)≡0 for some n, repeated integration shows that f is a polynomial. By the identity theorem, the zero set of the nonzero holomorphic function f(n) has empty interior. Thus each Zn​ is nowhere dense.
The complex plane is complete, so the Baire category theorem gives
C=⋃n=0∞​Zn​.
(7)
Choose z0​ outside this union. Then f(n)(z0​)=0 for every n, and hence every coefficient f(n)(z0​)/n! in the Taylor series about z0​ is nonzero. Such a point z0​ therefore exists.
