For a nonconstant holomorphic map f:X→Y between compact connected Riemann surfaces, its degree is
degf=∑x∈f−1(y)​ex​,
(177)
where ex​ is the ramification index of a holomorphic map at x. The valency theorem makes this sum independent of y∈Y. The Riemann-Hurwitz formula is
2gX​−2=(degf)(2gY​−2)+x∈X∑​(ex​−1).​
(178)
Write E=C/Λ. The map ψ(z+Λ)=−z+Λ is well-defined and biholomorphic, with ψ2=id. Its fixed points satisfy
z+Λ=−z+Λ⟺2z∈Λ.
(179)
They are therefore indexed by 21​Λ/Λ≅(Z/2Z)2, so there are four, as in the negation map on a one-dimensional complex torus.
For each x∈E′ choose a small open disc Ux​ for which Ux​∩ψ(Ux​)=∅. The projection restricts to a homeomorphism
p∣Ux​​:Ux​⟶p(Ux​).
(180)
Use (p∣Ux​​)−1 followed by a holomorphic coordinate on Ux​ as a chart on p(Ux​). Choosing ψ(Ux​) instead changes this coordinate by the holomorphic map induced by ψ, so the transition functions are holomorphic. These charts make S′ a Riemann surface and p:E′→S′ a holomorphic double covering map.
At each omitted fixed point, a centred local coordinate changes by z↦−z, so the invariant quotient coordinate is w=z2. Hence the extension has degree two and exactly four ramification points, each with ex​=2. Since the complex torus E has genus one, Riemann--Hurwitz gives
0=2(2gS​−2)+4.
(181)
Thus gS​=0​, in agreement with the general quotient of a one-dimensional complex torus by negation.
