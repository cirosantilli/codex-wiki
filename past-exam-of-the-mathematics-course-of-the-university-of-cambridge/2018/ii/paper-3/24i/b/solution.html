Choose a point Q∈E. Since g(E)=1, a nonzero regular differential has an effective divisor of degree 2g−2=0, so KE​∼0. The Riemann-Roch theorem therefore gives
ℓ(nQ)=n(n≥1).

Choose
x∈L(2Q)∖L(Q),y∈L(3Q)∖L(2Q).
(184)
The rational functions x and y have their only poles at Q, of orders two and three respectively.
The seven functions
1,x,y,x2,xy,x3,y2
(185)
belong to the six-dimensional Riemann-Roch space L(6Q), so they satisfy a nontrivial linear relation. Comparing pole orders shows that the coefficients of both x3 and y2 are nonzero. After rescaling, the relation has the Weierstrass form
y2+a1​xy+a3​y=x3+a2​x2+a4​x+a6​.
(186)
The divisor 3Q is very ample, so its complete linear system embeds E in P2 and the displayed relation cuts out its image; this is the Weierstrass construction from Riemann-Roch in genus one.
Because chark=2, completing the square removes the terms linear in y, and because chark=3, translating x removes the quadratic term. Thus the equation becomes
y2=x3+Ax+B.

x3+Ax+B=(x−λ1​)(x−λ2​)(x−λ3​).
(188)
