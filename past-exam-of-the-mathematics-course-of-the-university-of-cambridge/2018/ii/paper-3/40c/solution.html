Let a slowly varying wave have phase S(x,t), wavevector k=∇S, and angular frequency ω=−∂t​S. The local dispersion relation is the Hamilton-Jacobi equation ω=Ω(k;x,t). Equality of mixed derivatives, followed along a curve with velocity x˙=∇k​Ω, gives the Hamiltonian ray-tracing equations
dtdxi​​=∂ki​∂Ω​,dtdki​​=−∂xi​∂Ω​,dtdω​=∂t∂Ω​.​
(343)
Here d/dt=∂t​+x˙⋅∇ is the total derivative evaluated along a ray, and x˙ is the group velocity.
For Ω=kc(z), ω, kx​, and ky​ are conserved. If ψ is the angle between the ray and the z-axis, then ksinψ=(kx2​+ky2​)1/2 and k=ω/c. Hence Snell's law is
csinψ​=ω(kx2​+ky2​)1/2​=constant.​
(344)
For the ray launched with k=k(cosϕ,0,sinϕ) at z=0, this constant is ∣cosϕ∣/c0​. Therefore
sinψ=∣cosϕ∣(1+β2z2)≤1,
(345)
which proves
∣z∣≤zm​=β1​(∣cosϕ∣1​−1)1/2.​
(346)
For 0<ϕ<π/2, write a=cosϕ. Since the ray and wavevector are parallel,
(dxdz​)2=[a(1+β2z2)1​]2−1.
(347)
Near the upper turning point, put z=zm​−ζ. Expansion gives
(dxdz​)2≃4aβ2zm​ζ.
(348)
If x=xm​ at the turn, integration yields the local ray path
z≃zm​−aβ2zm​(x−xm​)2.​
(349)
The ray is tangent to the line z=zm​, turns smoothly, crosses z=0, and repeats symmetrically between −zm​ and zm​. Increasing ϕ decreases zm​, while ϕ↓0 sends the turning level outward.
