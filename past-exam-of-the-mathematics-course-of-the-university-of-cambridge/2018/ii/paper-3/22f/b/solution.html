Let q be the Holder conjugate exponent of p. On Rn with Lebesgue measure, the duality of Lp spaces is
(Lp)∗≅Lq(1≤p<∞),​
(166)
under the pairing Λg​(f)=∫fg. Thus (L1)∗=L∞, while for 1<p<∞ the dual exponent satisfies 1/p+1/q=1.
A Banach space is reflexive when its canonical map into its bidual is surjective. The spaces Lp(Rn) are reflexive exactly for
1<p<∞.​
(167)
For these exponents, applying Lp duality twice identifies the bidual with Lp. The endpoint spaces L1 and L∞ are not reflexive; for example, disjoint unit cubes give closed copies of ℓ1 and ℓ∞, respectively.
Integration gives an isometric embedding L1↪(L∞)∗, but it is not onto. On the subspace
V=C1⊕C0​(Rn)⊂L∞,

define Λ(c+h)=c, the limit at infinity. Since ∣Λ(v)∣≤∥v∥∞​, the Hahn-Banach theorem extends Λ to all of L∞. If this extension were integration against some g∈L1, its vanishing on every compactly supported bounded function would force g=0 almost everywhere, but Λ(1)=1. Therefore
(L∞)∗=L1.​
(169)
