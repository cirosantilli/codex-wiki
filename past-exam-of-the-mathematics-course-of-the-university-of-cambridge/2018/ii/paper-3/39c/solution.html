For each incompressible Stokes flow without body force, ∂j​σij​=0 and
σij​=−pδij​+2μeij​,eij​=21​(∂i​uj​+∂j​ui​).
(335)
The divergence theorem gives
∫S​ui(1)​σij(2)​nj​dS=∫V​∂j​ui(1)​σij(2)​dV.
(336)
Incompressibility removes the pressure term, antisymmetric velocity-gradient parts do not contract with the symmetric stress, and the remaining integrand is 2μeij(1)​eij(2)​, symmetric under 1↔2. This proves the Lorentz reciprocal theorem for Stokes flow:
∫S​ui(1)​σij(2)​nj​dS=∫S​ui(2)​σij(1)​nj​dS.​
(337)
By Linearity of Stokes flow, the drag on a translating body is linear in its velocity, so Fi​=Aij​Uj​. Apply the reciprocal theorem to two translation solutions with velocities U(1) and U(2). Their surface velocities are constant and the far-field contribution vanishes, yielding
Ui(1)​Aij​Uj(2)​=Ui(2)​Aij​Uj(1)​.
(338)
Since the two vectors are arbitrary,
Aij​=Aji​.​
(339)
Thus the hydrodynamic resistance matrix is symmetric and, for fixed fluid viscosity, depends only on body geometry.
A straight rod has fore-aft symmetry about its centre. Gravity acts through that centre, so the translational-rotational coupling and applied torque vanish. Kinematic reversibility of Stokes flow and Uniqueness of Stokes flow then rule out spontaneous rotation: the inclination θ remains constant.
Let the speed for perpendicular translation be U0​. The given factor of two makes the parallel speed 2U0​ for the same force component. Resolving gravity parallel and perpendicular to the rod gives
U∥​=2U0​cosθ,U⊥​=U0​sinθ.
(340)
Resolving these velocities horizontally and vertically gives
UH​=U0​sinθcosθ,UV​=U0​(1+cos2θ).
(341)
Therefore
tanϕ=UV​UH​​=1+cos2θsinθcosθ​.​
(342)
