The uniformization theorem states that every simply connected Riemann surface is biholomorphic to exactly one of the Riemann sphere C, the complex plane C, or the unit disc D. A surface is uniformized by one of these when that surface is its universal cover. The only surface uniformized by C is the sphere itself, because every nonidentity Möbius transformation has a fixed point and therefore cannot act as a deck transformation. The Riemann surfaces uniformized by the complex plane are
C,C∗,C/Λ for a lattice Λ.​
(149)
Let U⊆C have a complement containing at least two points. Its universal cover cannot be the sphere because U is noncompact. If it were the plane, the covering map C→U↪C would be a nonconstant entire function omitting two values, contrary to the Little Picard theorem. The plane domain with two omitted points is hyperbolic, so
U is uniformized by D.​
(150)
Now put S=R∖{P1​,…,Pn​}. The spherical possibility occurs only for (g,n)=(0,0). By the preceding list, the plane-uniformized possibilities are
(g,n)=(0,1),(0,2),(1,0),
(151)
which give respectively 2g−2+n=−1,0,0. Every remaining pair has 2g−2+n>0 and must have disc universal cover. Thus the uniformization of a punctured compact Riemann surface gives
S is uniformized by DS is uniformized by C​⟺2g−2+n>0,⟺2g−2+n∈{−1,0}.​​
(152)
For the complex torus X=C/Λ, any analytic map f:C→X lifts through the universal covering map to an entire function F:C→C. If F is constant, so is f. Otherwise Little Picard says that F omits at most one complex number. For every x∈X, its lifts form an infinite coset z+Λ, so at least one lift is attained by F. Hence the holomorphic map from the complex plane to a one-dimensional complex torus satisfies
f is constant or surjective.​
(153)
Finally, C and D are homeomorphic by
z⟼1+∣z∣z​,w⟼1−∣w∣w​.
(154)
They are not conformally equivalent: a biholomorphism C→D would be a nonconstant bounded entire function, contradicting the Liouville theorem. This proves the claimed example of complex plane and unit disc are homeomorphic but not conformally equivalent.
