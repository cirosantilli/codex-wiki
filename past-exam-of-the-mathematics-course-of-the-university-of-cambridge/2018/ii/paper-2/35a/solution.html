Label the two atoms in primitive cell j by u2j​ and u2j+1​, with j understood modulo N because of the periodic boundary condition. Choose the spring between them to have constant λ. Newton's second law gives
mu¨2j​mu¨2j+1​​=λ(u2j+1​−u2j​)+αλ(u2j−1​−u2j​),=λ(u2j​−u2j+1​)+αλ(u2j+2​−u2j+1​).​
(255)
For a longitudinal normal mode, put
u2j​=Aei(2jka−ωt),u2j+1​=Bei((2j+1)ka−ωt).
(256)
Periodicity requires e2iNka=1, so k=πr/(Na). Since the primitive-cell length is 2a, wavevectors differing by π/a describe the same mode, and the first Brillouin zone may be chosen as
−2aπ​≤k<2aπ​.​
(257)
The amplitudes obey
(λ(1+α)−mω2−λ(e−ika+αeika)​−λ(eika+αe−ika)λ(1+α)−mω2​)(AB​)=0.
(258)
Setting its determinant to zero gives the alternating-spring chain dispersion relation
ω±2​(k)=mλ​[1+α±(1−α)2+4αcos2(ka)​].​
(259)
The lower sign is the acoustic phonon branch and the upper sign is the optical phonon branch.
At the edge of the Brillouin zone,
ω±2​=mλ​(1+α±∣1−α∣),
(260)
so the frequency band gap is
Δω=m2λ​​∣1−α​∣.​
(261)
It vanishes at α=1, when the apparent two-atom primitive cell can be reduced to one atom.
Near the centre of the Brillouin zone, a Taylor expansion gives
ω−​(k)=am(1+α)2αλ​​∣k∣+O(∣k∣3),​
(262)
and
ω+​(k)=m2λ(1+α)​​[1−2(1+α)2αa2k2​+O(k4)].​
(263)
The group velocity of the acoustic branch at long wavelength is the speed of sound,
cs​=am(1+α)2αλ​​.​
(264)
