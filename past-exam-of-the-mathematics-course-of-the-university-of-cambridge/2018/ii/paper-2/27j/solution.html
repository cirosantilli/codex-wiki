For the finite state space S, the generator or Q-matrix G=(gij​) is defined by
gij​=limh↓0​hPi​(Xh​=j)−1{i=j}​​.
(182)
Its off-diagonal entries are nonnegative jump rates and gii​=−∑j=i​gij​. The transition semigroup of a continuous-time Markov chain is P(t)=etG.
An invariant distribution of a continuous-time Markov chain is a probability row vector π satisfying πP(t)=π for every t≥0. Differentiation at zero gives πG=0; conversely, πG=0 implies πetG=π. Thus
π is invariant⟺πG=0.​
(183)
Invariant distributions need not be unique: each closed communicating class supports one, and their convex combinations are invariant. A finite irreducible chain has a unique invariant distribution.
Let 0=T0​<T1​<⋯ be the arrival times of the independent rate-λ Poisson process. The increments Tn+1​−Tn​ are independent exponential variables of rate λ. Conditional on Yn​=XTn​​=i, the Markov property, time homogeneity, and independence of the next increment give
P(Yn+1​=j∣Y0​,…,Yn​)​=∫0∞​λe−λtPij​(t)dt=(Pλ​)ij​.​
(184)
Hence (Yn​) is a discrete-time Markov chain. Its transition matrix is the Poisson-sampled continuous-time Markov chain resolvent
Pλ​=∫0∞​λe−λtetGdt=λ(λI−G)−1.​
(185)
If πG=0, then πetG=π and integration gives πPλ​=π. Conversely, if a probability row vector ν satisfies νPλ​=ν, multiplying
νλ(λI−G)−1=ν
(186)
on the right by λI−G yields νG=0. Therefore the sampled chain and the original chain have exactly the same invariant distributions.
