The intrinsic parity η of a particle is the parity eigenvalue of its one-particle state at rest:
P∣0,s,m⟩=η∣0,s,m⟩.
(251)
For the particles in this question, η=±1. A two-particle state has multiparticle parity η1​η2​(−1)ℓ, where ℓ is its relative orbital angular momentum.
The pionic-deuterium ground state has atomic ℓ=0. Since the pion has spin zero, its total angular momentum is therefore the deuteron spin, J=1, and its parity is ηd​ηπ​=ηπ​. The two neutrons are identical fermions. Their antisymmetric spin-one-half singlet state would require even orbital ℓ, but S=0 and J=1 would instead require ℓ=1. Hence they must occupy the symmetric spin-one-half triplet state, so the orbital state is antisymmetric and ℓ is odd. The addition of angular momentum with S=J=1 permits ℓ=0,1,2, leaving ℓ=1. Thus parity conservation gives
ηπ​=ηn2​(−1)1=−1,
(252)
so the pion has intrinsic parity ηπ​=−1​.
In Δ−→π−+n, the final total spin is S=1/2. Coupling it to ℓ to obtain J=3/2 gives
​ℓ−21​​≤23​≤ℓ+21​,
(253)
so the allowed orbital angular momenta are ℓ=1,2​. The observed angular probability is cos2θ=∣P1​(cosθ)∣2, where P1​ is a Legendre polynomial; it therefore selects the ℓ=1 partial wave rather than ℓ=2. The parity selection rule for a two-body decay now gives
ηΔ−​=ηπ​ηn​(−1)ℓ=(−1)(+1)(−1)=+1.
(254)
Therefore the delta particle has intrinsic parity ηΔ−​=+1​.
