The Simplicial approximation theorem states that if K is a finite simplicial complex, L is a simplicial complex, and f:∣K∣→∣L∣ is continuous, then for some r≥0 there is a simplicial approximation g:K(r)→L to f.
The target open stars form an open cover of ∣L∣, so their inverse images under f form an open cover of the compact metric space ∣K∣. The Lebesgue number lemma supplies δ>0 such that every subset of diameter below δ lies in one inverse image. Choose r so large that every vertex star in K(r) has diameter below δ; this is possible because its diameter is at most twice μ(K(r)) and the mesh tends to zero.
For each vertex v choose a vertex w(v) of L such that
f(stK(r)​(v))⊆stL​(w(v)).
(140)
If v0​,…,vq​ span a simplex, a point in its relative interior belongs to all their open stars. Its image belongs to all the open stars of w(v0​),…,w(vq​), so these target vertices span a simplex. Thus v↦w(v) extends to a simplicial map g, and its defining inclusion says exactly that g approximates f. This proves the theorem.
