Let Fn​=σ(X1​,…,Xn​) be the natural filtration. An integer-valued random variable M is a stopping time with respect to (Xi​) when
{M≤n}∈Fn​for every n​.
(240)
Thus whether one has stopped by time n can be decided from the first n observations, without seeing future variables. Equivalently, {M≥i}∈Fi−1​.
Write the stopped sum as
∑i=1M​Xi​=∑i=1∞​Xi​1{M≥i}​.
(241)
The event {M≥i} depends only on X1​,…,Xi−1​, so its indicator and Xi​ are independent random variables. Since E∣X1​∣<∞ and EM<∞, Tonelli theorem gives
E∑i=1∞​∣Xi​∣1{M≥i}​=E∣X1​∣∑i=1∞​P(M≥i)=E∣X1​∣EM<∞.
(242)
We may consequently exchange expectation and summation. Using the tail-sum formula for M proves Wald's equation:
E[i=1∑M​Xi​]=i≥1∑​μP(M≥i)=μEM​.
(243)
For the renewal process, put Sn​=X1​+⋯+Xn​ and Nt​=max{n:Sn​≤t}. The random variable Nt​+1 is a stopping time. Since SNt​+1​>t, Wald's equation gives
μ(m(t)+1)=ESNt​+1​>t,
(244)
and hence
liminft→∞​tm(t)​≥μ1​.
(245)
For c>0, set Yi​=Xi​∧c and μc​=EYi​. Wald's equation still applies to the stopping time Nt​+1 and the independent variables Yi​. Because SNt​​≤t,
∑i=1Nt​+1​Yi​≤SNt​​+c≤t+c.
(246)
Therefore
μc​(m(t)+1)≤t+c,limsupt→∞​tm(t)​≤μc​1​.
(247)
By the monotone convergence theorem, μc​↑μ as c→∞. Combining the bounds proves the elementary renewal theorem
tm(t)​⟶μ1​​.
(248)
For a fixed word over q equally likely symbols, the waiting time for a word in independent uniform symbols has mean ∑qk, where k runs through the lengths of the borders of a word, including the full word. The word lava' has no proper nonempty border, so $$ \boxed{\mathbb E\tau_{\mathtt{lava}}=100^4=100\,000\,000}. $$ The word aa' has borders of lengths 1 and 2, so
Eτaa​=100+1002=10100​.
(249)
For a direct check, let E0​ be the expected remaining time with no trailing a' and $E_1$ with one trailing a'. Then
E0​=1+1001​E1​+10099​E0​,E1​=1+10099​E0​,
(250)
which gives E0​=10100.
