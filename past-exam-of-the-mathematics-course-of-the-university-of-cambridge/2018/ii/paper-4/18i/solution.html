Because K has characteristic p, the prime field Fp​ lies in K, and
f(α+c)=(α+c)p−(α+c)+a=f(α)+cp−c=0
(130)
for every c∈Fp​. These are all p roots. Moreover f′(t)=−1, so f is a separable polynomial.
Let m(t) be the minimal polynomial of an algebraic element of α over K. Translation by c∈Fp​ sends an irreducible factor g(t) of f(t) to the irreducible factor g(t−c). The additive group of Fp​ therefore acts on the irreducible factors, and every orbit has size 1 or p. An orbit of size p would already contribute pdegm to the degree-p polynomial f, forcing degm=1. Then α∈K, all roots α+c lie in K, and L=K, contrary to the hypothesis.
Thus m(t−c)=m(t) for every c∈Fp​. If 1≤d=degm<p, comparison of the coefficient of td−1 in m(t−c)−m(t) for c=0 gives −dc times the nonzero leading coefficient, a contradiction. Hence degm=p, and since m is a monic divisor of the monic degree-p polynomial f, we have m=f. Thus f is irreducible over K.
All roots α+c already belong to K(α), so the splitting field is
L=K(α)​.
(131)
As the splitting field of a separable polynomial, L/K is a Galois extension. For each c∈Fp​, irreducibility gives a distinct K-automorphism
σc​(α)=α+c,σc​σd​=σc+d​.
(132)
These exhaust the p=[L:K] automorphisms. This is an Artin–Schreier extension, and
Gal(L/K)≅(Fp​,+)≅Cp​​.
(133)
