The Fermat-Euler theorem states that
gcd(a,n)=1⟹aφ(n)≡1(modn).
(70)
Indeed, if r1​,…,rφ(n)​ are the reduced residue classes modulo n, multiplication by a permutes them. Therefore
∏i​ari​≡∏i​ri​(modn).
(71)
The product ∏i​ri​ is invertible modulo n, so cancellation gives the theorem.
Now let p be prime. If k≡1(modp−1), then for p∤b, Fermat little theorem gives bk−1≡1(modp), while p∣b makes bk≡b≡0. Thus bk≡b for every integer b.
Conversely, assume this congruence holds for every b. Choose a primitive root g modulo p. Then gk−1≡1(modp). Since g has order p−1, one has p−1∣k−1. Hence
bk≡b(modp) for every b⟺k≡1(modp−1).​
(72)
