OurBigBook.com Words: 253
An odd composite N coprime to b is a Fermat pseudoprime to base b when bN−1≡1(modN). It is a Carmichael number when this congruence holds for every b coprime to N.
Suppose a Carmichael number has pa∥N with a≥2. The group (Z/paZ)∗ is cyclic, so choose a residue of order
φ(pa)=pa−1(p−1).
(73)
By the Chinese remainder theorem, extend it to an integer b that is congruent to 1 modulo every other prime-power factor of N. Then gcd(b,N)=1, and the Carmichael congruence forces the order of b modulo pa to divide N−1. In particular p∣N−1, contradicting p∣N. Therefore every Carmichael number is square-free.
Now assume N is square-free. If p−1∣N−1 for every p∣N, then for every b coprime to N, Fermat little theorem gives
bN−1≡1(modp)
(74)
for each p∣N. The Chinese remainder theorem then gives the congruence modulo N, so N is Carmichael.
Conversely, if N is Carmichael, fix p∣N. Choose a primitive root g modulo p and use the Chinese remainder theorem to choose b≡g(modp) and b≡1(modN/p). Then bN−1≡1(modp), so the order p−1 of g divides N−1. This proves the Korselt criterion in the required form:
N is Carmichael⟺N is square-free and p−1∣N−1 for every p∣N.​
(75)
A Carmichael number cannot have one prime factor because it is composite. If N=pq with distinct primes p<q, the condition q−1∣pq−1 implies q−1∣p−1, which is impossible. Hence every Carmichael number has at least three prime factors.
