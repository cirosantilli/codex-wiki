A three-dimensional Bravais lattice is the set of all integer combinations of three linearly independent primitive vectors. Its reciprocal lattice is
Λ∗={G:G⋅R∈2πZ for every R∈Λ},
(300)
and the first Brillouin zone is the Wigner-Seitz cell of the origin in reciprocal space.
For the stated FCC lattice, the primitive-cell volume is
Ω=a1​⋅(a2​×a3​)=4a3​.
(301)
The reciprocal-basis formula gives
b1​=a2π​(−e1​+e2​+e3​),b2​=a2π​(e1​−e2​+e3​),b3​=a2π​(e1​+e2​−e3​)​.
(302)
A reciprocal primitive cell, and hence the Brillouin zone, has volume
Ω(2π)3​=a332π3​​.
(303)
In the first Born approximation, with momentum transfer q=k−k′, the scattering amplitude is
fV​(k;k′)=−2πℏ2m​∫R3​eiq⋅rV(r)d3r.
(304)
For the spherical shell, align the polar axis with q and use its radial Dirac delta function:
∫eiq⋅rV0​δ(r−d)d3r=4πV0​d2qdsin(qd)​.
(305)
Therefore
f​V​(k;k′)=−ℏ22mV0​d2​qdsin(qd)​​.
(306)
Translation of each atomic potential multiplies its transform by a phase, so the crystal lattice structure factor gives
fVΛ​​(k;k′)=f​V​(k;k′)∑R∈Λ​eiq⋅R.
(307)
For an infinite lattice, the lattice Poisson summation formula turns the sum into
∑R∈Λ​eiq⋅R=Ω(2π)3​∑G∈Λ∗​δ(3)(q−G).
(308)
Thus the amplitude vanishes away from reciprocal-lattice momentum transfers.
In elastic scattering, ∣k∣=∣k′∣ and hence
∣q∣=2ksin2θ​.
(309)
The two shortest relevant reciprocal-vector lengths of this FCC lattice are
∣G2​∣=a2π3​​,∣G1​∣=a4π​.
(310)
Both are kinematically available when k>2π/a. Assigning their scattering angles θ2​ and θ1​, respectively, gives
sin(θ2​/2)sin(θ1​/2)​=∣G2​∣∣G1​∣​=3​2​​.
(311)
