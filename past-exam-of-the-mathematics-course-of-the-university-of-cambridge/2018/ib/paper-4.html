A quadratic form on a finite-dimensional real vector space V is a function q(v)=B(v,v) for some symmetric bilinear form B. A positive-definite quadratic form satisfies q(v)>0 for every v=0.
Completing squares gives
x2+2xy+2y2+2yz+3z2=(x+y)2+(y+z)2+2z2.
(1)
Set u=x+y, v=y+z, w=z. The corresponding ordered basis in the original coordinates is
(1,0,0), (−1,1,0), (1,−1,1),​
(2)
and the diagonal form is u2+v2+2w2. All diagonal coefficients are positive, so the form is positive definite.

Use the presentation D6​=⟨r,s:r3=s2=1,srs=r−1⟩. Its subgroup ⟨r⟩ is the unique subgroup of order 3, hence is a characteristic subgroup. An automorphism must send r to r or r−1 and s to one of the three reflections rjs. Thus there are at most six automorphisms.

The Bolzano-Weierstrass theorem in R says that every bounded real sequence has a convergent subsequence. For a bounded sequence in Rn, successively extract subsequences on which the first, second, and so on through the nth coordinate converge. The final diagonal subsequence converges in Euclidean space, proving the theorem in Rn.
Put K=D∖⋃z∈S​Bε​(z). Suppose the asserted δ did not exist. Then there would be xk​∈D and yk​∈K with ∥xk​−yk​∥<1/k but ∣f(xk​)−f(yk​)∣≥ε. Since K is closed and bounded, Bolzano-Weierstrass gives a subsequence yk​→y∈K, and then xk​→y. The point y is not in S, because otherwise y∈Bε​(y), so f has continuity at y. Both f(xk​) and f(yk​) tend to f(y), a contradiction. Hence the required uniform δ>0 exists.

A self-adjoint operator T satisfies ⟨Tx,y⟩=⟨x,Ty⟩. If Tx=λx, then λ⟨x,x⟩=⟨Tx,x⟩=⟨x,Tx⟩=λ⟨x,x⟩, so λ∈R. An eigenvector exists over C; its orthogonal complement is T-invariant. Induction on dimension produces an orthonormal basis of eigenvectors, proving the finite-dimensional spectral theorem.
A self-adjoint T is positive definite exactly when every eigenvalue is positive, since ⟨Tx,x⟩=∑i​λi​∣xi​∣2 in an eigenbasis. The quadratic forms of two positive-definite operators add, so their sum is positive definite.
The same eigenbasis shows that specT⊂[a,b] exactly when T−λI is positive definite for every λ<a and negative definite for every λ>b. Finally, if v is a unit eigenvector of α+β with eigenvalue γ, the Rayleigh quotient bounds give
γ=⟨αv,v⟩+⟨βv,v⟩∈[a+c,b+d].
(15)
Thus every eigenvalue of α+β lies in [a+c,b+d].

A complete metric space is one in which every Cauchy sequence converges. On I=(0,1] define
d(x,y)=∣x−1−y−1∣.
(18)

20H (Optimisation)

Words: 181 Articles: 1
Paper 4 OurBigBook.com

Solution

Words: 181
20H OurBigBook.com
A cut is a partition (S,T) of the vertices with the source in S and sink in T; its capacity is the sum of capacities of directed edges from S to T. The max-flow min-cut theorem says that maximum flow value equals minimum cut capacity. With integral capacities, there is an integral max-flow theorem attaining the maximum.
Construct a bipartite graph with one vertex for each row and column and an edge ri​→cj​ whenever Aij​=1. Add source-to-row and column-to-sink edges of capacity 1, and give row-to-column edges infinite capacity. An integral flow is precisely a set of independent 1s, so its maximum value is the largest such set.
A finite-capacity cut places some rows on the sink side and some columns on the source side; these lines cover every 1, since an uncovered row-to-column edge would cross the cut with infinite capacity. Its capacity is the number of selected lines. Conversely every line cover defines such a cut. Max-flow min-cut therefore proves the maximum number of independent 1s equals the minimum number of covering lines, the matrix form of König's theorem for bipartite matching.
