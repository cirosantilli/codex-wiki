Assume the external field varies negligibly across the current distribution. Expanding it to first order, the uniform term gives no net force because ∫JdV=0. Using the supplied integral identity and ∇⋅B=0, the first-order term in ∫J×BdV reduces to
F=∇(m⋅B).
(44)
Since F=−∇U, the magnetic dipole potential energy is
U=−m⋅B.​
(45)
Here
∣B∣=B02​+B12​(x2+y2)​
(46)
increases away from the z-axis. A dipole parallel to B has energy −m∣B∣ and is drawn toward stronger field, hence outward from the axis. An antiparallel dipole has energy +m∣B∣ and is driven toward the weak-field axis. The field therefore spatially separates the two orientations.
