A metric space metric d:X×X→[0,∞) satisfies positivity, d(x,y)=0 exactly when x=y, symmetry, and the triangle inequality. A subset U⊆X is an open set when every x∈U has some ε>0 with the open ball Bd​(x,ε)⊆U.
Both ∅ and X are open. An arbitrary union of open sets is open because a point lies in one member supplying a ball. A finite intersection is open because the minimum of the finitely many available radii supplies a ball. Thus the metric-open sets satisfy the topology axioms.
For C[0,1], the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality gives d1​(f,g)≤d2​(f,g), so the d2​ topology is at least as fine as the d1​ topology. To see strictness, define the continuous triangular spikes

(11)

First, every nonzero nonunit in a principal ideal domain factors into irreducible elements. Otherwise repeatedly choose a proper nonunit factor aj+1​ of aj​; then the principal ideals
(a1​)⊊(a2​)⊊⋯
