A topological space is connected if it is not the union of two disjoint nonempty open sets, or equivalently has no nontrivial subset that is both an open set and a closed set.
Let Kx​ be the union of all connected subspaces containing x. A union of connected subsets with a common point is connected: if a separation of a topological space existed, every member containing x would lie wholly in the same side. Hence Kx​ is connected and contains every connected subspace through x, so it is the connected component of x.
If Kx​∩Ky​=∅, their union is connected and maximality gives Kx​=Ky​. Otherwise they are disjoint. Since every x belongs to Kx​, the connected components partition X.
