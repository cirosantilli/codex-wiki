In atomic units the Time-independent Schrodinger equation is
(−21​∇2−r1​)ψ=Eψ.
(74)
The displayed equation is the Radial Schrodinger equation for the hydrogen atom. Its radial derivative terms come from the Laplacian in spherical coordinates, the Coulomb term is the potential −1/r, and separation into spherical harmonics uses the orbital angular momentum eigenvalue l(l+1), producing the centrifugal term −l(l+1)R/r2.
For E=−1/(2n2), substitute R=rαe−r/n. Dividing the equation by R and comparing powers of r gives
α(α+1)=l(l+1),nα+1​=1.
(75)
The solution regular at the origin has α=l, and when l=n−1 both equations agree. This is the circular Coulomb bound state. Thus
α=n−1.​
(76)
The radial probability measure is ∣R(r)∣2r2dr. With a=2/n and the Gamma integral, the mean radius of a circular Coulomb bound state is
⟨r⟩=∫0∞​r2ne−ardr∫0∞​r2n+1e−ardr​=a2n+1​=2n(2n+1)​​.
(77)
At fixed principal quantum number n, the allowed orbital angular momentum quantum numbers are l=0,…,n−1, and each has magnetic quantum number m=−l,…,l. Hence the orbital quantum degeneracy is
l=0∑n−1​(2l+1)=n2.​
(78)
Including electron spin would double this to 2n2, but spin is absent from the stated wavefunctions.
