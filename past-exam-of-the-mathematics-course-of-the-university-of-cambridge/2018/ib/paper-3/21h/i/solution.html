The Lagrange sufficiency theorem says that if a feasible point x∗ and nonnegative multipliers satisfy complementary slackness and x∗ globally maximizes the Lagrangian function in constrained optimization, then x∗ globally solves the constrained maximization problem. Indeed, for every feasible x, the signs of the constraints give f(x)≤L(x)≤L(x∗)=f(x∗). Concavity and the Karush-Kuhn-Tucker conditions are a common way to verify the global Lagrangian maximum.
