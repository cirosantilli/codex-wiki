Rotational diffusion of a sphere (Dr​=kB​T/(8πηa3))

 ... Physics Branches of physics Statistical physics Thermodynamics Diffusion Rotational diffusion
OurBigBook.com Words: 257
For a sphere of radius a in an otherwise stationary, unbounded Newtonian fluid with dynamic viscosity η, impose the no-slip boundary condition and the slow-flow assumptions of Stokes flow. The resisting torque on a rotating sphere in Stokes flow is −ζr​Ω, where Ω is its angular velocity and ζr​=8πηa3. Thermal rotational diffusion at absolute temperature T then gives
Dr​=ζr​kB​T​=8πηa3kB​T​,Dt​=6πηakB​T​,Dt​Dr​​=4a23​.
(58)
Here kB​ is the Boltzmann constant; the translational expression is the Stokes–Einstein relation. The ratio has units of inverse length squared, so comparing the numerical values of Dr​ and Dt​ without their units is meaningless. At fixed temperature and dynamic viscosity, decreasing radius tenfold increases Dr​ a thousandfold but Dt​ only tenfold. This strong size dependence explains why small particles can lose directional memory extremely quickly.

These are bulk three-dimensional sphere calculations, explicitly not a fit to the confined ellipsoid experiment below. Walls, nonspherical shape and departure from the no-slip boundary condition can change rotational resistance. A visible surface mark is still necessary to read the sphere's turning in an image, even when its rotational diffusion coefficient is well defined physically.
