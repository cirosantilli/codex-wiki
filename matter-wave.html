Matter wave

 Home Physics Branches of physics Quantum mechanics Wave function
OurBigBook.com Words: 2k Articles: 12
A matter wave is the quantum wave associated with a material particle. Its phase can interfere after coherent paths recombine.
In the position representation, a matter wave is described by a wavefunction: a complex number probability amplitude, with detection probabilities given by the Born rule. A localized wave packet combines different momenta; its envelope can move while its individual quantum phase crests move at another speed. Neither curve is a literal mechanical oscillation of material along a particle's path. The de Broglie phase harmony explains the historical route to λ=h/p; the relativistic matter-wave velocities distinguish the two speeds. The Davisson–Germer experiment and the Thomson electron transmission diffraction experiment connect that spatial quantum phase to measured electron diffraction.
