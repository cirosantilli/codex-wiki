Alternating dyadic colouring excludes symmetric weighted pair sums (c(n)=⌊log2​n⌋(mod2))

 ... Analysis Real analysis Riemann integration Partition of an interval Dyadic partition Dyadic interval
OurBigBook.com Words: 109
Define a finite colouring of the positive integers by c(n)=⌊log2​n⌋(mod2). No increasing infinite sequence has all the numbers xi​+2xj​, i=j, in one colour class.
Write δ(y)=2⌊log2​y⌋+1−y. If δ is unbounded on the sequence, fix a term x and choose a later term y>2x with δ(y)>2x. With L=2⌊log2​y⌋, the numbers y+2x and 2y+x lie respectively in the adjacent dyadic intervals [L,2L) and [2L,4L), so their colours differ. If δ is bounded by D on the sequence, choose x>2D and then y>2x. Now y+2x=2L−δ(y)+2x lies in [2L,4L), whereas 2y+x=4L−2δ(y)+x lies in [4L,8L). Their colours again differ. The finite colouring therefore excludes the symmetric weighted-pair configuration.
