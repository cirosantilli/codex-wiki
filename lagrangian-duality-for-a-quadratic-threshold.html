Lagrangian duality for a quadratic threshold (minx≥4​((x−1)2+2)=11)

 Home Mathematics Area of mathematics Mathematical optimization Lagrangian duality
OurBigBook.com Words: 285
Choose the one-variable quadratic program
minx∈R​f(x)=(x−1)2+2subject to g(x)=4−x≤0.
(35)
Without the optimization constraint, the global minimum is at 1. On the feasible set [4,∞), write x=4+s with s≥0: then f(x)=11+6s+s2≥11. Hence x∗​=4 is the unique primal optimizer and p∗​=11.
For any λ≥0, completing the square in the optimization Lagrangian gives
L(x,λ)=(x−1)2+2+λ(4−x)=(x−1−2λ​)2+2+3λ−4λ2​.
(36)
The inner minimization problem is over all real numbers, so its unique optimizer is xλ​=1+λ/2. Therefore
q(λ)=2+3λ−4λ2​=11−4(λ−6)2​,λ≥0.
(37)
The best lower bound is d∗​=11, attained at λ∗​=6. At λ=2, the inner optimizer is 2, which is infeasible for the primal problem; nevertheless q(2)=7 is a valid lower bound. At λ=9, the inner optimizer is 5.5, which is feasible, but q(9)=8.75 is weaker than 11. Feasibility of an inner optimizer alone does not make the bound exact.
At (x∗​,λ∗​)=(4,6), the Karush-Kuhn-Tucker conditions reduce to
4−x∗​=0,λ∗​≥0,2(x∗​−1)−λ∗​=0,λ∗​(4−x∗​)=0.
(38)
The last equality is complementary slackness. The strongest certificate is visible without solving another optimization problem: L(x,6)=(x−4)2+11, so every feasible point satisfies
f(x)≥L(x,6)≥11=f(4).
(39)
The same Lagrange multiplier simultaneously makes the inner optimizer feasible and removes the slack between L and f. This is a convex KKT optimality certificate; x=5 also verifies Slater's condition.
The domain in the inner infimum is essential. If one mistakenly keeps x≥4 when computing q(2), the restricted minimum becomes L(4,2)=11, whereas the actual Lagrange dual function has value 7. Constraints deliberately retained in X are allowed in a Lagrangian relaxation, but that is a different choice of which restrictions to dualize.
