Inverse beta decay threshold (Eν,thr​=2mp​(mn​+me​)2−mp2​​c2)

The neutron and Positron together have more rest mass than the initial proton, so a finite incident energy is necessary even before recoil is considered. In the proton's rest frame, use four-momentum conservation and a negligible neutrino mass. The initial squared center-of-mass energy is mp2​c4+2mp​c2Eν​; the least final squared center-of-mass energy is (mn​+me​)2c4. Equating them gives the threshold above. At threshold the final particles share one center-of-mass velocity; they are not both at rest in the laboratory.
Writing Δ=mn​−mp​, the result separates into
Eν,thr​=(Δ+me​)c2+2mp​(Δ+me​)2​c2.
(12)
The positive second term is the laboratory recoil correction. With rounded rest energies mp​c2=938.272MeV, mn​c2=939.565MeV and me​c2=0.511MeV, the calculated threshold is about 1.806MeV. These rounded inputs follow the recommended particle masses.[ref]
As a further ideal energy balance, suppose the Positron stops and annihilates with an electron, and all prompt annihilation gamma rays are contained. Its prompt visible energy is Te+​+2me​c2. Neglecting neutron recoil gives Evis​≃Eν​−(mn​−mp​)c2+me​c2≃Eν​−0.782MeV. Escaping gamma rays, detector response and recoil change that measured sum. The delayed capture signal is a separate energy deposit, not an extra contribution to the incident electron antineutrino's prompt energy reconstruction.
