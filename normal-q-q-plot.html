Normal Q-Q plot

 Home Mathematics Area of mathematics Probability and statistics Q-Q plot
OurBigBook.com Words: 459
A normal Q-Q plot compares order statistics with standard normal quantiles zi​=Φ−1(pi​). For a normal distribution N(μ,σ2) with σ>0, the population quantile function is μ+σΦ−1(p), so the diagnostic sample relation is
x(i)​≈μ+σzi​.
(9)
The appropriate reference is a line with an intercept and positive slope, not necessarily the identity line. A line can be fitted through two central sample quantiles; R's qqline uses the quantile levels p=0.25 and p=0.75 by default. Neither that line nor its fitting rule is part of the definition of a normal Q-Q plot. [ref]
To isolate curvature, retain the same 19 plotting positions and construct two more ordered sequences:
bi​=2pi​−1,ci​=sinh(0.7zi​).
(10)
The bi​ are continuous uniform distribution quantiles on [−1,1], while the ci​ apply the hyperbolic sine to the reference quantiles. Both examples are symmetric about zero. At ranks 1,10,19, bi​ is approximately −0.947368,0,0.947368 and ci​ is approximately −1.812618,0,1.812618. Again, these are constructed values rather than random draws or real data.
Read these shapes relative to their central slope. The uniform distribution example has b(z)=2Φ(z)−1, with derivative b′(0)=2/π​≈0.797885; at the upper endpoint the tangent predicts 1.546246, while b19​=0.947368. Its lower end lies above the tangent and its upper end below it: the outer quantiles are compressed. The hyperbolic sine example has c′(0)=0.7; its tangent predicts 1.356552 at the upper endpoint, while c19​=1.812618. Its lower end lies below the tangent and its upper end above it: the outer quantiles are stretched. Unequal departures at the two ends can suggest skewness. The direction of these descriptions assumes that reference quantiles are horizontal and observed values vertical. The graphs illustrate finite-range shape differences; a sample pattern cannot establish an asymptotic tail probability law or membership in a heavy-tailed distribution class.
A nearly straight normal Q-Q plot is a diagnostic, not a proof of a normal distribution or a formal hypothesis test. Random statistical samples fluctuate, particularly at extreme order statistics, and apparent curvature depends on sample size, plotting positions and the reference line. A calibrated hypothesis test or uncertainty band requires its own assumptions and must account for parameters estimated from the same observations. Sorting also discards the original observation order: rearranging a time series leaves its Q-Q plot unchanged, so the plot cannot establish independence.
For a normal linear model, R's diagnostic normal Q-Q plot uses standardized regression residuals, also called internally studentized residuals. [ref] Even with independent errors, the regression residuals have covariance matrix σ2(I−H), where H is the hat matrix; fitting parameters generally creates dependencies. Dividing by a shared estimated standard deviation creates further dependence, and the distribution of an internally studentized Gaussian residual is not exactly the standard normal distribution. The normal Q-Q plot therefore helps assess error shape alongside other diagnostics rather than certifying independent normal random variables.
