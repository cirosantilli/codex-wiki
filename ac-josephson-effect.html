AC Josephson effect (fJ​=2e∣V∣/h)

 ... Physics Branches of physics Statistical physics Superconductivity Josephson junction Josephson effect
OurBigBook.com Words: 120
For constant nonzero voltage V, direct integration gives
ϕ(t)=ϕ0​+ℏ2eV​t,Is​(t)=Ic​sin(ϕ0​+ℏ2eV​t),fJ​=h2e∣V∣​.
(23)
A constant electric potential difference therefore produces an oscillating pair electric current. The average of this ideal sinusoid over a complete cycle is zero; a measured nonzero steady electric current can include single-electron superconducting tunneling. Josephson's lecture explicitly separates the ordinary quasiparticle term from the phase-dependent term. [ref]
For an original numerical example, use the exact SI values e=1.602176634×10−19C and h=6.62607015×10−34Js. Then
h2e​=483.5978484…MHz/μV,V=10μV ⟹ fJ​=4.835978484…GHz.
(24)
The corresponding period is about 0.2067834ns. These values describe an assumed exact applied voltage, not a historical measurement. The factor 2e identifies coherent pair transfer; replacing it by e would halve the predicted frequency.
