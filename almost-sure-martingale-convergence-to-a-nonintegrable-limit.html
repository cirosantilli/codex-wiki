Almost-sure martingale convergence to a nonintegrable limit

 ... Area of mathematics Probability and statistics Probability theory Martingale Doob upcrossing inequality Martingale convergence theorem
OurBigBook.com Words: 90
Let independent increments ξk​ equal 4k or −4k with probabilities 2−k−1 each, and zero otherwise. Every partial sum Mn​=∑k≤n​ξk​ is integrable and is a martingale. The Borel-Cantelli lemma makes only finitely many increments nonzero almost surely, so Mn​ has a finite almost-sure limit Y. Yet E∣Y∣=∞: the disjoint events that only the kth increment is nonzero have probabilities at least c2−k for c=∏j​(1−2−j)>0, and contribute at least c2k each. This does not contradict the martingale convergence theorem, whose integrability conclusion requires a uniform first-moment bound.
