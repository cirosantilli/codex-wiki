First establish a stronger form of the coset-counting argument. If K is a finitely generated infinite-index subgroup of a finitely generated group L of growth at most q, choose a finite generating set T for K, and let M bound its lengths in a generating set S of L. The Schreier graph supplies r+1 distinct left-coset representatives of K of S-length at most r. The corresponding translates of BT​(r) are disjoint and lie in BS​((M+1)r). Hence
(r+1)∣BT​(r)∣≤C(1+(M+1)r)q.
(15)
Thus an infinite-index finitely generated subgroup has growth at most q−1 whenever the ambient upper bound is q.
Every finitely generated K≤H has infinite index in G. Applying this result with L=G gives growth at most n−1. Minimality of n among the supposed counterexamples therefore makes each such K virtually nilpotent. Here n≥1, since a group with a uniform bound on its finite word balls is finite, and finite groups are virtually nilpotent.
For each finitely generated K≤H, let d(K) be the least nonnegative integer giving a polynomial growth upper bound. It exists and belongs to {0,…,n−1}. Choose K for which this integer is maximal. If K≤L≤H and L is finitely generated, then d(L)≤d(K). Were [L:K] infinite, the strengthened bound would make K have growth at most d(L)−1≤d(K)−1, contradicting the definition of d(K). If d(K)=0, the same inequality would force the nonempty balls of K to have cardinality tending to zero, also impossible. Hence every such overgroup has [L:K]<∞.
Since K is virtually nilpotent, choose a nilpotent finite-index subgroup N≤K. It is finitely generated by Schreier's lemma. For any h∈H, the subgroup L=⟨K,h⟩ is finitely generated and [L:K]<∞. Pigeonholing the cosets hjK gives a positive a with ha∈K. Pigeonholing the cosets of N in K then gives a positive b with (ha)b∈N. Consequently
For every h∈H there is r=ab>0 with hr∈N.​
(16)
No normality of K in L or of N in K is required. Using least integer upper bounds also avoids needing any further structural theorem about the exact growth degree of a virtually nilpotent group.
