Past exam of the building infinite groups course of the University of Oxford / 2011 / 3 / b / Solution

Put A=Am​ and let T be the rooted tree of finite words over {1,…,m}. View a∈A as a rooted automorphism: it permutes the first letter by a and fixes the suffix. Denote this rooted copy by A again.
For a∈A, define a directed tree automorphism b(a) by putting local permutation a at every vertex
2,12,112,1112,…
(21)
and identity local permutation at every other vertex. These marked vertices are pairwise incomparable, so the prescribed actions are on disjoint rooted subtrees and define a unique automorphism. Equivalently its root permutation is trivial and its sections are
b(a)=(b(a),a,1,…,1).
(22)
The map a↦b(a) is an injective homomorphism: it is the same copy of a acting on each of those disjoint subtrees. Let B=b(A) and define
H=⟨A,B⟩≤Aut(T).
(23)
Both A and B are finite, so H is finitely generated. For m≥5, Am​ is a nonabelian simple group and hence perfect. Thus A,B≤H′, and because they generate H, H is perfect.
Every section of either defining generator belongs to H, so sections of every element of H belong to H. Its root permutation belongs to A. For a tree automorphism g, write g(iw)=σ(i)gi​(w), where gi​ is its section at input letter i. In the semidirect-product convention of part (a), define
θ(g)=(gσ−1(1)​,…,gσ−1(m)​;σ).
(24)
Composition of tree automorphisms gives exactly the wreath-product multiplication: this is why θ is a homomorphism. It is injective because an automorphism is determined by its first-level permutation and all its first-level sections. On the generators,
θ(a)=(1,…,1;a),θ(b(a))=(b(a),a,1,…,1;1).
(25)
It remains to prove surjectivity; merely writing these recursions would not establish it. Choose σ=(234)∈A, which fixes 1 and sends 2 to 3. For a,c∈A, the two base tuples θ(b(a)) and θ(σb(c)σ−1) overlap only in their first coordinate. Their commutator is therefore
([b(a),b(c)],1,…,1;1)=(b([a,c]),1,…,1;1).
(26)
Since A is perfect, products of these commutators give every element of the copy of B localized in coordinate 1. Canceling this first coordinate from θ(b(a)) then gives (1,a,1,…,1;1), the rooted copy of A localized in coordinate 2. Conjugating by the transitive rooted group Am​ supplies both localized A and localized B in every coordinate. Since H=⟨A,B⟩, the image contains the full base Hm. It also contains the top Am​. Therefore it is the entire wreath product and
θ:H⟶∼​HwrAm​.​
(27)
Finally H is infinite: it contains the nontrivial rooted Am​, and a finite order h=∣H∣ would satisfy h=hm∣Am​∣, which is impossible for m≥5.
