Bounding volume

 Home Mathematics Area of mathematics Analysis Numerical analysis Computer aided geometric design
OurBigBook.com Words: 1k Articles: 7
A bounding volume is a simple enclosing set B for an object S: every point of S belongs to B. Its purpose is to make a cheap rejection possible before examining the object itself. For example, an axis-aligned bounding box can enclose a triangle using just its smallest and largest coordinates.
The guarantee is one-sided. If S⊆BS​ and T⊆BT​, then
S∩T=∅ ⟹ BS​∩BT​=∅.
(32)
Thus an empty set intersection of the bounding volumes certifies an empty set intersection of the objects. An overlap of the bounding volumes is necessary for actual intersection, but does not prove it. This is a conservative enclosure test: rejection is conclusive, acceptance means further examination is needed.
Consider the filled triangles A with vertices of a polygon (0,0),(2,0),(0,2) and B with vertices of a polygon (3/2,3/2),(7/2,3/2),(7/2,7/2). Their axis-aligned bounding boxes overlap in [3/2,2]×[3/2,2]. Nevertheless, every point of A satisfies x+y≤2, whereas every point of B satisfies x+y≥3. The two triangles are disjoint. The shaded square in the bounding volume hierarchy illustration makes this false candidate visible.
For collision detection, a broad phase produces possible object pairs from bounding volume tests; a narrow phase examines the actual shapes, for example with a triangle-triangle intersection algorithm. Scott Le Grand's Chapter 32. Broad-Phase Collision Detection with CUDA explains this separation in its opening paragraphs. Whether touching counts as a collision must be specified by the application.
An enclosure must cover the entire object being queried. A box fitted to samples of a parametric curve only guarantees enclosure of the sampled points, unless a separate bound covers the whole parametric curve. For a Bézier curve, the convex hull of its control points provides such a bound on its defining parameter interval. In floating-point arithmetic, outward rounding can preserve certified coordinate bounds; an inward roundoff error can invalidate a rejection. Bounds must also be updated when objects move.
