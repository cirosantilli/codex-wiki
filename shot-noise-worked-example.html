For an original model, set q=1.602176634×10−19C, I=1μA, RF​=100kΩ, G=10, and the exponential low-pass filter's τ=100μs. Assume an ideal detector without charge multiplication and omit all additive noise for this calculation. Then
fc​≈1591.55Hz,Bn​=2500Hz,SI(1)​=3.204353268×10−25A2/Hz,
(52)
SI(1)​​≈0.56607pA/Hz​,σv,shot2​=8.01088317×10−10V2,σv,shot​≈28.3035μV.
(53)
Doubling I doubles the voltage variance and multiplies its standard deviation by 2​. Halving τ also doubles the variance, provided the detector remains approximately white throughout the enlarged passband. Using fc​ in place of Bn​ underestimates the variance by the factor 2/π. These are calculated predictions, not measured values or settings claimed for the laboratory below.
To read the dimensionless figure, set a=λτ and x=τifiltered​/q, with frequency ν=fτ in cycles per unit t/τ. A gate of duration τ has count mean and variance a. The continuously filtered x instead has
E[x]=a,Var(x)=a/2,Sx(1)​(ν)=1+(2πν)22a​,∫0∞​Sx(1)​(ν)dν=a/2.
(54)
The count and the filtered electric current are different observables. The factor 1/2 comes from the exponential weighting, not from non-Poisson arrivals. The dimensionless count illustration is a separate choice of rate; it does not assert that the 1μA example has only four electrons per 100μs.
