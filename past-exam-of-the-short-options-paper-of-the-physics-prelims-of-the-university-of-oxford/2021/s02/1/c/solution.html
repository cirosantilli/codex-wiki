For spherical hydrostatic equilibrium, the stellar structure equations include
drdP​=−r2Gm(r)ρ(r)​,drdm​=4πr2ρ(r).
(29)
Eliminate ρ to obtain dP/dm=−Gm/(4πr4). Since r(m)<R⊙​ through the interior, integration gives
Pc​−Ps​=∫0M⊙​​4πr(m)4Gm​dm>4πR⊙4​G​∫0M⊙​​mdm.

With negligible surface pressure, this proves
Pc,⊙​>8πR⊙4​GM⊙2​​≃4.48×1013Pa.​

This is a conservative lower bound; concentrating mass near the centre increases the required pressure.
For the temperature inference assume predominantly ideal gas support, a nearly fully ionized hydrogen–helium mixture with mean molecular weight μ≃0.61, and the hottest region at the centre. A rigorous scale estimate follows from the stellar virial theorem, rather than substituting the mean mass density for the unknown central mass density. With negligible surface pressure,
3∫PdV=−W,−W=∫0M⊙​​r(m)Gm​dm>2R⊙​GM⊙2​​.
(32)
The ideal gas law gives ∫PdV=kB​∫Tdm/(μmp​). The mass-weighted mean temperature consequently obeys
Tc​≥⟨T⟩M​>6kB​R⊙​μmp​GM⊙​​≃2.35×106K.​
(33)
This establishes several million kelvin under the stated physical assumptions. The central pressure inequality alone does not give a central temperature bound without an equation of state and information about mass density or an integrated argument.
