Evaluate the source's printed logarithm using the numerical magnitude value 21.0, with base-ten logarithms:
log10​kpcRe​​=1.3log10​(250)−0.8log10​(21)−1.36=0.69955,Re​≃5.01kpc.​
(43)
This is the literal printed-fit convention identified above. Convert the mean visual surface brightness to a linear luminosity per projected area. At 10 parsecs, one square arcsecond subtends 100/(206264.806)2pc2. The definition of absolute magnitude consequently gives
LV,⊙​pc−2Ie​​=10−0.4(μe​−MV,⊙​−21.572)=10−0.4(21.0−4.8−21.572)≃140.9.
(44)
The constant 21.572=5log10​(206264.806)−5 is the conversion between the angular and physical projected area. Since the effective radius encloses half the total luminosity,
LV​=2πRe2​Ie​≃2.22×1010LV,⊙​.​
(45)
For the stipulated stellar population of Solar-type stars, approximate the stellar mass-to-light ratio by M⊙​/LV,⊙​. Summing 100 such galaxies then gives
M∗,cluster​≃100LV,⊙​LV​​M⊙​=2.22×1012M⊙​.​
(46)
This assumes negligible interstellar extinction and that the supplied surface brightness is an intrinsic or already corrected value; an observed cosmological surface brightness requires additional dimming and passband corrections before this conversion.
For comparison only, if the empirical coefficients and intercept were instead meant for log10​(Ie​/[LV,⊙​pc−2]), substituting 140.9 in that fit would give Re​=1.09kpc, LV​=1.06×109LV,⊙​, and M∗,cluster​=1.06×1011M⊙​. Those are conditional alternative results, not an unannounced correction of the printed units.
