Work in the initial rest frame of the free electron. If it absorbed a photon of positive energy Eγ​, conservation of momentum would give the final electron momentum magnitude pf​=Eγ​/c, while conservation of energy would give Ef​=me​c2+Eγ​. But the final electron must obey the relativistic energy–momentum relation,
Ef2​=me2​c4+pf2​c2=me2​c4+Eγ2​.
(63)
Combining these statements gives
(me​c2+Eγ​)2=me2​c4+Eγ2​⟹2me​c2Eγ​=0,
(64)
which contradicts Eγ​>0. Thus
An isolated free electron cannot absorb a single real photon.​
(65)
The initial rest frame always exists for an electron, so this excludes the process in every inertial frame. Compton scattering is allowed because an outgoing photon carries additional energy and momentum. Absorption by an electron bound in matter is also possible because the rest of the system can share recoil.
