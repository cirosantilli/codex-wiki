The estimate assumes the entire 2m2 surface remains exposed at 35∘C continuously. Clothing reduces direct exposure and presents a cooler outer emitting surface; the skin temperature also varies across the body and decreases in cooler surroundings through changes in blood flow. Body posture and nearby surfaces reduce the effective exchange area, while blankets and warm surrounding surfaces reduce net radiative heat transfer. These effects make the full-area, constant-temperature daily radiative estimate too large for ordinary clothed conditions.
The unit emissivity approximation is already fairly good for skin in the thermal infrared, so reducing emissivity alone is not the main correction. Also, convection, thermal conduction, and evaporation are additional channels of heat transfer; their existence does not subtract from the calculated radiative term. The computed number is a simplified radiative loss, not a complete metabolic energy budget.
