Assume a circular orbit and an orbital inclination i≃90∘, as appropriate for a simple eclipsing binary estimate. At quadrature the two radial velocities are opposite and equal to the orbital speeds. The more massive component has the smaller amplitude. With the centre of mass as origin,
a1​=2πK1​P​,a2​=2πK2​P​,a=a1​+a2​,M2​M1​​=K1​K2​​=2.
(15)
Thus a1​=0.1103AU, a2​=0.2206AU and Kepler's third law gives
a=0.3309AU,M1​=8.06M⊙​,M2​=4.03M⊙​.​
(16)
The orbital plane contains the observer's line of sight; at quadrature the two velocities point towards and away from that observer, as labelled in the original schematic below. For an elliptic orbit, the measured amplitudes instead give asini=P(K1​+K2​)1−e2​/(2π); the numerical answer requires the circular assumption.
