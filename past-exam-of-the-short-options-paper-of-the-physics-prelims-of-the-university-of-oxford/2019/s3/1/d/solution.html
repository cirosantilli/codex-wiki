First convert from a spectral energy density per unit frequency to one per unit wavelength, including the Jacobian matrix and determinant:
Uλ​=Uν​​dλdν​​=λ58πhc​ehc/(λkB​T)−11​.
(33)
Set dUλ​/dλ=0 and define x=hc/(λkB​T). The nonzero maximum satisfies 5(1−e−x)=x, with numerical root x=4.96511. Consequently
λmax​T=4.96511kB​hc​=2.89777×10−3mK.​
(34)
This is Wien's displacement law. One must maximize the wavelength density, rather than find the frequency maximum and merely put λ=c/νmax​: the frequency and wavelength peaks of Planck's law are different because a spectral density changes under a change of coordinate.
