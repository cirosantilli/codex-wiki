Using the stipulated 570nm as a wavelength-density peak, Wien's displacement law gives
Tinferred​=570×10−90.002897​≃5.08×103K.​
(35)
This is a rough estimate from the supplied characteristic wavelength, not an accurate measurement of the Sun's effective temperature. The Sun emits a broad, approximately thermal visible spectrum and appears white outside the atmosphere; its familiar yellow appearance does not identify a monochromatic thermal peak. A photospheric effective temperature near 5770K instead corresponds to a wavelength-density maximum near 502nm.[ref][ref]
The core must be much hotter to provide the pressure required for hydrostatic equilibrium and to sustain nuclear fusion. Outward energy transport through an opaque interior proceeds down a temperature gradient, by radiative diffusion and then convection through much of the outer envelope. The observable photosphere radiates to space at the temperature needed to carry the solar luminosity, L⊙​=4πR⊙2​σTeff4​; it is not an unobstructed view of the hot core. The printed core estimate above 20 million degrees is also too high for the present Sun: the standard central temperature is about 1.56×107K. This correction leaves the large core-to-surface contrast and its explanation intact.[ref]
