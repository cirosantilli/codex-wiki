Let r be radial position, F=L/(4πr2) the outward radiative energy flux, and Pr​=aT4/3 the radiation pressure. A thin layer absorbs a fraction κρdr of an incident directed beam. Since a photon carries momentum E/c, the outward force per unit volume deposited by the radiation is κρF/c. Balance it against the radiation pressure gradient:
drdPr​​=−cκρ​F=−4πr2cκρL​.
(20)
Substituting dPr​/dr=(4a/3)T3dT/dr yields the radiative diffusion equation
L=−4πr23κρ4ac​T3drdT​.​
(21)
The sign expresses outward energy flux down the temperature gradient. The approximation assumes optically thick material and near local thermodynamic equilibrium, with an appropriate mean opacity.
The printed attenuation law describes the loss from an incident beam; it is not generally the radial derivative of the net luminosity, since absorption is accompanied by emission. For a concrete counterexample, in a steady envelope without local energy generation the net luminosity is constant even though κρ>0 and dT/dr<0. Applying dL/dr=−κρL to that net luminosity would contradict energy conservation. The local momentum deposition argument supplies the physically correct derivation.
