Conformality and analyticity. Use the orientation-preserving convention for a conformal map, with a continuously differentiable, nonzero differential. Its Jacobian matrix must be a positive scale times a rotation,
D(u,v)=λ(cosϕsinϕ​−sinϕcosϕ​),λ>0.
(6)
Thus ux​=vy​, uy​=−vx​, the Cauchy-Riemann equations; continuous partial derivatives then imply that u+iv is a holomorphic function. If angle preservation is allowed to reverse orientation, the statement needs this qualification: z↦zˉ preserves unsigned angles but is an anticonformal map and is not a holomorphic function.
The mapping. Put a=π/L. The displayed components combine to give
w=cosh(az),w′=asinh(az).​
(7)
Its derivative does not vanish in the open half-strip x>0, 0<y<L, so it is a conformal map there. Also Imw=sinh(ax)sin(ay)>0. The lower edge maps to [1,∞), the upper edge to (−∞,−1], and the vertical edge to [−1,1]. The open half-strip maps bijectively onto the upper half-plane. Indeed, coshζ=coshη implies ζ=±η+2πim; the half-strip allows only equality. Surjectivity follows by solving t+t−1=2w and choosing the root t=eaz with ∣t∣>1 and 0<argt<π. The two corners are boundary critical points, not interior failures of conformality.
Transformed boundary values. A conformal map preserves Laplace's equation up to a positive scale. Thus the transformed Dirichlet problem is to find a real harmonic function U(u,v) on v>0 with
U(u,0)={−V0​,V0​,​−1<u<1,∣u∣>1.​
(8)
The conflicting values at the original corners are interpreted as one-sided edge limits.
Logarithmic solution. Choose the complex logarithms of w±1 with arguments between zero and π in the upper half-plane. A linear combination F(w)=αlog(w+1)+βlog(w−1)+γ is a complex-valued harmonic function, since it is a holomorphic function; the real physical potential must be a real or imaginary part. In particular, set
F(w)=V0​+π2iV0​​[log(w−1)−log(w+1)].
(9)
Then
U=ReF=V0​−π2V0​​[arg(w−1)−arg(w+1)].
(10)
On the three real boundary intervals, the argument difference is respectively 0,π,0, which gives the required values. This is the bounded harmonic solution, with U→V0​ at infinity; uniqueness holds in this bounded class by the maximum principle. Without a boundedness or far-field condition, the edge data alone do not give uniqueness: one may add Csinh(πx/L)sin(πy/L).
Back substitution. Since
w+1w−1​=tanh2(az/2),tanh(az/2)=cosh(ax)+cos(ay)sinh(ax)+isin(ay)​,
(11)
the required continuous argument difference is 2arctan[sin(ay)/sinh(ax)] in the open half-strip. Consequently
V(x,y)=V0​−π4V0​​arctan(sinh(πx/L)sin(πy/L)​).​
(12)
As y→0,L with x>0, the arctangent tends to zero; as x→0+ with 0<y<L, it tends to π/2. These limits verify every open edge of the Dirichlet problem.
