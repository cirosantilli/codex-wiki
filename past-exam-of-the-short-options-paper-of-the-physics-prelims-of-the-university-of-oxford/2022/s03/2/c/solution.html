For a normalized quantum state, ∣α∣2+∣β∣2=1, and the Born rule gives p(z+)=∣α∣2, p(z−)=∣β∣2​. If the displayed coefficients are not normalized, divide both by ∣α∣2+∣β∣2. A quantum superposition is a coherent linear combination: relative phase affects other spin measurements, unlike an incoherent statistical mixture with the same z-measurement probabilities.
Every pure qubit quantum state is an eigenstate of spin along some axis. Its Bloch vector is
n=(2Re(α∗β), 2Im(α∗β), ∣α∣2−∣β∣2).
(48)
For real normalized α,β, choose θ modulo 2π with α=cos(θ/2) and β=sin(θ/2), allowing an irrelevant global sign. Then
n=(2αβ,0,α2−β2),Sn​∣ψ⟩=2ℏ​∣ψ⟩.​
