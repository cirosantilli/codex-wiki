Alice's outcome is random. Conditional on knowing it, Bob may assign a different quantum state, but without that classical record he sees the same density matrix. For the displayed pure two-qubit state, his reduced density matrix before Alice's measurement is
ρB​=(∣α∣2+∣γ∣2α∗β+γ∗δ​αβ∗+γδ∗∣β∣2+∣δ∣2​).
(62)
After Alice's computational-basis measurement with the outcome unannounced, the weighted mixture p0​ρB∣0​+p1​ρB∣1​ is exactly this same matrix. More generally, for any local trace-preserving operation with measurement operators Ka​,
ρB′​=TrA​∑a​(Ka​⊗I)ρAB​(Ka†​⊗I)=TrA​ρAB​=ρB​,∑a​Ka†​Ka​=I.
(63)
The equality follows from the cyclic property of the partial trace for operators acting on Alice's subsystem. Bob's every local outcome probability therefore remains unchanged. This is the no-communication theorem: entanglement alone cannot send information faster than light​. Conditional state changes become usable correlations only when the classical outcomes are compared.
