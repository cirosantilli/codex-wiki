When p0​,p1​>0, the observed relative frequencies converge, for large sample size, to the conditional probabilities
nn0​​⟶∣α∣2+∣β∣2∣α∣2​,mm0​​⟶∣γ∣2+∣δ∣2∣γ∣2​.

Equality says that the second binary outcome has the same distribution whichever first outcome occurred; this is independence of these two particular measurement results. Cross-multiplication gives
∣α∣2(∣γ∣2+∣δ∣2)=∣γ∣2(∣α∣2+∣β∣2),∣αδ∣=∣βγ∣.​
(57)
For finite samples the two empirical fractions fluctuate; they are estimates, not exact deterministic equalities. If p0​=0 or p1​=0, the missing conditional fraction is undefined, but one outcome is constant, so the joint measurement results are independent and the displayed modulus identity still holds.
This criterion only addresses the computational-basis statistics. For example
∣χ⟩=21​(∣00⟩+∣01⟩+∣10⟩−∣11⟩)
(58)
has all four joint probabilities equal to 1/4, yet is maximally entangled: its first-qubit density matrix is I/2 and αδ=−1/4=1/4=βγ. Moreover classical correlations of mixed separable states also exist, for example 21​∣00⟩⟨00∣+21​∣11⟩⟨11∣. Correlations in a single basis alone neither certify nor exclude quantum entanglement.
