Perpetual American call option (S∗​=pK/(p−1))

 ... Mathematics Area of mathematics Mathematical optimization Mathematical finance American option American call option
OurBigBook.com Words: 287 Articles: 1
An American call option with no expiry. For geometric Brownian motion with drift r−y under a risk-neutral measure, positive dividend yield y, r>0, and volatility σ>0, let p>1 solve σ2p(p−1)/2+(r−y)p−r=0. Its value is (S∗​−K)(S/S∗​)p below S∗​=pK/(p−1) and S−K above. The power solves the stationary Black-Scholes equation with continuous stock dividends; value matching and smooth fit determine the threshold.
The candidate dominates the positive part (S−K)+ because its continuation branch is convex and tangent to S−K at S∗​. In the exercise region its discounted-generator residual is rK−yS≤0: the root equation gives yS∗​=K[r+σ2p/2]>rK. Applying Itô's formula to the discounted value, localized if necessary, gives an upper bound on every exercise policy. Stopping at the first hit of S∗​ attains it; on paths not yet hitting, the bounded discounted continuation value tends to zero. This supplies the optimal stopping verification. As y↓0, p↓1, the threshold diverges and the value tends to S.
