Write D=f(x)−m(s) for the predicted decrease, assuming a nonstationary point and a step at least as good as the Cauchy point. When Δ≤∥g∥2​, part (ii) gives D≥21​∥g∥2​Δ. Part (iv), using its weaker requested bound, then gives
∣1−ρ∣=D∣f(x+s)−m(s)∣​≤∥g∥2​2Δ​.
(24)
Consequently Δ≤21​(1−η)∥g∥2​ implies ρ≥η​.
For the radius conclusion, use the standard update rules from part (i). Suppose no subsequence of the generated gradient norms tends to zero. Then some ε>0 bounds those norms below eventually. If Δk​→0, eventually Δk​≤21​(1−η2​)ε, so every subsequent step is very successful by the preceding estimate. The radius is then maintained or increased, contradicting convergence to zero. Hence a radius tending to zero forces a subsequence of gradient norms to tend to zero. The conclusion uses the trust-region update rules as well as the model estimates.
