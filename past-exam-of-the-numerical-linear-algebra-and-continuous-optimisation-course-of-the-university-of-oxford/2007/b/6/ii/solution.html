The sequential quadratic programming step minimizes a quadratic local objective model while enforcing the first-order constraint expansion c(xk​+s)≈ck​+Ak​s=0. When the iterate and multipliers are close to a regular solution and Bk​ approximates the Lagrangian Hessian, the resulting correction approximates a Newton step for the nonlinear KKT conditions.
Full row rank of Ak​ ensures that Ak​s=−ck​ is solvable for every ck​ and that the multiplier of the quadratic subproblem is unique. A positive-definite Bk​ makes the objective strictly convex and coercive on the affine feasible set, giving a unique global subproblem minimizer. In fact positivity on kerAk​, rather than on every vector, is enough for uniqueness and boundedness on that set; full positive definiteness is a convenient stronger assumption.
