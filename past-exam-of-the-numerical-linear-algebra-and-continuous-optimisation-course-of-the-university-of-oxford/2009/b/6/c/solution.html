On the strictly feasible set Ωc∘​={x:cj​(x)>0 for all j}, use the logarithmic barrier
Φμ​(x)=f(x)−μj=1∑M​logcj​(x),μ>0.​
(32)
Choose a strictly feasible starting point and parameters μn​↓0. For each parameter minimize the barrier functional by an unconstrained method confined to Ωc∘​, warm-starting from the preceding point. In the exact version take a stationary point xn​ of the barrier subproblem. The stationarity equation is
∇f(xn​)−∑j​λn,j​∇cj​(xn​)=0,λn,j​=μn​/cj​(xn​)>0,λn,j​cj​(xn​)=μn​.​
(33)
These are the natural Lagrange multiplier estimates; the basic method need not perform a separate primal-dual correction.
The barrier method KKT convergence theorem is a global stationarity result: if the exact barrier stationary points form a bounded sequence, then every cluster point satisfying the linear independence constraint qualification is a KKT point. To prove it, take any subsequence xn​→x∗​. Continuity gives feasibility of x∗​. For an inactive index, cj​(x∗​)>0 and therefore λn,j​=μn​/cj​(xn​)→0.
The active gradients at x∗​ are independent. The supplied dual-basis result gives bounded vectors φn,i​, converging as n→∞, with ∇cj​(xn​)Tφn,i​=δji​ for active i,j. Pair stationarity with these vectors:
λn,i​=φn,iT​(∇f(xn​)−∑j∈/A(x∗​)​λn,j​∇cj​(xn​)).
(34)
Thus all active multipliers are bounded as well. Extract a convergent multiplier subsequence. Its limit is nonnegative, satisfies stationarity by continuity, and satisfies complementarity because λn,j​cj​(xn​)=μn​→0. This proves the claimed KKT conclusion. The same proof permits approximate barrier stationary points whose stationarity residual tends to zero. Bounded iterates and the constraint qualification are substantive hypotheses, not automatic consequences of writing a barrier functional.
For a global-minimum conclusion one needs global minimization of the barrier subproblems and appropriate access to every feasible point. Specifically, suppose xn​ globally minimizes Φμn​​, has a cluster point x∗​, and Ωc∘​​=Ω. For any fixed strictly feasible z,
f(xn​)≤f(z)+μn​∑j​(logcj​(xn​)−logcj​(z)).
(35)
Along the convergent subsequence the cj​(xn​) are bounded above, so taking the upper limit gives f(x∗​)≤f(z). Density of strictly feasible points and continuity of f extend the inequality to every feasible z. Hence x∗​ is a global minimizer under these stronger hypotheses.
