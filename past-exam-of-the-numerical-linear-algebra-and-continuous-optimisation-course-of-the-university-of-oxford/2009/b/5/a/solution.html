A local minimizer of a differentiable function on RN necessarily satisfies ∇f(x∗​)=0​. A sufficient condition for a strict local minimum is ∇f(x∗​)=0,∇2f(x∗​) positive definite​. Indeed, the second-order Taylor expansion has a strictly positive quadratic term that dominates its o(∥x−x∗​∥2) remainder near x∗​.
For Newton's method, solve Hn​sn​=−gn​ with Hn​=∇2f(xn​), gn​=∇f(xn​), and set xn+1​=xn​+sn​. A positive-definite Hessian makes this a descent direction; general Hessians may need modification or a separate line search for a globally useful method.
The sequence is q-quadratically convergent if there is C<∞ such that ∥xn+1​−x∗​∥≤C∥xn​−x∗​∥2 eventually. Sufficient local hypotheses are ∇f(x∗​)=0, a nonsingular H∗​=∇2f(x∗​), a Lipschitz continuous Hessian near x∗​, and x0​ sufficiently close. For convergence to a local minimum take H∗​ positive definite. Near x∗​, inverse Hessians are uniformly bounded, and integration along the error segment gives
