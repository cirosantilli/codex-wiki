Initial and boundary data for evolution equations

 Home Mathematics Area of mathematics Analysis Partial differential equation
OurBigBook.com Words: 217 Articles: 1
On 0<x<L, t>0, an initial condition u(x,0)=g(x) gives the starting profile. For the heat equation, Dirichlet boundary conditions u(0,t)=a(t), u(L,t)=b(t) can model ends held at prescribed temperatures. Alternatively, zero Neumann boundary conditions describe insulated ends in the constant-conductivity model. These choices can produce different evolutions from the same initial condition.
For a continuous classical solution reaching the corners, the Dirichlet boundary data must satisfy g(0)=a(0) and g(L)=b(0); higher smoothness can require further compatibility. In dimensionless variables, a complete worked heat equation problem is
ut​=uxx​,0<x<1,t>0,u(x,0)=x+sin(πx),u(0,t)=0,u(1,t)=1.
(1)
Its exact classical solution is u(x,t)=x+e−π2tsin(πx). The straight profile x is the equilibrium; the extra bump decays. Substituting verifies the equation and every datum, and the energy estimate proves uniqueness among smooth solutions with these data.
For the transport equation with c>0 on the same interval, prescribe an inflow transport boundary condition only at x=0. Backtracking a characteristic curve gives g(x−ct) if x≥ct, and a(t−x/c) otherwise. Values at x=L are determined by this formula; independently prescribing them can make the problem inconsistent.[ref]
