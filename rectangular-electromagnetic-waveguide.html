Rectangular electromagnetic waveguide

 Home Physics Branches of physics Electromagnetism Electromagnetic wave
OurBigBook.com Words: 2k Articles: 7
Take a straight hollow rectangular electromagnetic waveguide with vacuum interior 0<x<a, 0<y<b and axis z, where a>b>0. Its four walls are ideal perfect conductors. The time-dependent Maxwell equations require zero tangential electric field and zero normal oscillating magnetic field at these walls. Real metal has finite electrical conductivity, so real guides lose electromagnetic energy by Joule heating; the perfect-wall model isolates confinement and wave dispersion from that loss.
The simplest propagating transverse electric mode of a rectangular waveguide is TE10​. “Transverse electric” means Ez​=0, not that the magnetic field is also wholly transverse. With real peak scale E0​, its complex amplitudes, including the common axial factor, can be written
Ey​Bx​Bz​​=E0​sin(πx/a)eiβz,=−ωβ​E0​sin(πx/a)eiβz,=−aωiπ​E0​cos(πx/a)eiβz.​Ex​By​​=Ez​=0,=0,​
(3)
The physical fields are the real parts after multiplication by e−iωt. In particular, Ey​ is zero at x=0,a, maximal at x=a/2, and independent of y. At y=0,b it is normal to the metal, so it need not vanish there. The TE10​ electric field crosses the narrow gap, while its strength varies across the broad dimension.
For an original example choose a=30mm, b=12mm and f=1.25fc​, where fc​=c/(2a). With c=299792458m/s, this gives
fc​λ0​​=4.99654097GHz,=48mm,​fβ​=6.24567621GHz,=25πm−1,​λg​​=80mm.​
(4)
Here λ0​=c/f is vacuum wavelength and λg​=2π/β is guided wavelength. The next cutoff is TE20​ at 2fc​; TE01​ starts at (a/b)fc​=2.5fc​. Thus only TE10​ can propagate at the chosen frequency, although a source or discontinuity can additionally excite local evanescent waves. These are calculated values, not specifications or measurements of a commercial guide.
