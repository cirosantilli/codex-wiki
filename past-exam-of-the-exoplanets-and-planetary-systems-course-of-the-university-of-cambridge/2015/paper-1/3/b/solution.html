The Hill radius compares planetary gravity with the differential stellar tide. Expanding the stellar tide near a circular orbit gives GMp​/rH2​≃3Ω2rH​, so
rH​=a(3M⋆​Mp​​)1/3.​
(12)
It sets the size of the planet's tidal neighbourhood, controls encounter and feeding zone scales, and limits the region within which material can remain bound against stellar shear. It is not itself a solid surface or a guarantee of capture.
The Bondi radius compares planetary potential energy per unit mass with the gas thermal scale,
rB​=cs2​GMp​​.​
(13)
This convention uses the ambient sound speed; definitions based on escape speed can differ by a factor of two. Inside rB​, gravity can substantially perturb thermal gas. Gas still needs a way to dissipate energy and angular momentum to accrete; a geometric boundary alone does not supply a gas-capture rate.
For an embedded hydrostatic envelope a useful outer scale is rout​∼min(rB​,rH​), subject to disk thickness and gas recycling. Let h=H/a=cs​/(aΩ) and q=Mp​/M⋆​. The Bondi-Hill crossover in a protoplanetary disk obeys
arB​​=h2q​,arH​​=(3q​)1/3,rB​=rH​ ⟹ q=3​h3​.​
(14)
Small cores are usually thermally limited, whereas larger ones become tidally limited. These radii also organize pebble accretion: gas drag can turn an encounter into capture, with drift-dominated and shear-dominated regimes depending on particle stopping and encounter times. There is no universal accretion efficiency fixed by rH​ or rB​ alone.[ref]
