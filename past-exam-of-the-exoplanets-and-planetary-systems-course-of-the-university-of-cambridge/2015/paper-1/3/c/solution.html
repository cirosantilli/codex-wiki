Type I migration is orbital angular-momentum exchange between an embedded low-mass planet and its disk without a deep gap. The planet excites spiral density waves near Lindblad resonances; the disk's reaction torques act back on the planet. The outer wake usually supplies a negative torque and the inner wake a positive one, with their small imbalance often driving inward migration. Gas on horseshoe orbits in a planetary disk near corotation adds a corotation torque that depends on entropy and vortensity gradients.

Thus a more massive embedded core generally migrates faster within this regime, unlike a picture in which migration starts only after giant-planet formation.
In a simple locally isothermal smooth disk the net torque is commonly negative, potentially removing cores before they acquire gas. In a non-isothermal disk an unsaturated entropy-related corotation torque can balance or exceed the negative wave torque, giving a planet trap or outward migration. Viscosity and thermal diffusion help maintain the background gradients across the horseshoe region; without adequate replenishment corotation-torque saturation can suppress that contribution. The linear low-mass scaling requires a small disturbance, roughly q≪h3; larger planets, gap formation and strong disk feedback require a different treatment. Type I migration is therefore neither universally inward nor literally viscous advection of the planet.[ref]
