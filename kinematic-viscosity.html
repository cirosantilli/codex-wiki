Kinematic viscosity (ν=μ/ρ)

 Home Physics Branches of physics Fluid mechanics Dynamic viscosity
OurBigBook.com Words: 292 Articles: 3
The kinematic viscosity is dynamic viscosity divided by mass density:
ν=ρμ​,[ν]=m2s−1.
(3)
It measures the rate at which viscous transport smooths differences in velocity. For constant μ and ρ in incompressible flow, dividing the Navier-Stokes equation by mass density makes the viscous acceleration ν∇2u, where ∇2 is the Laplacian. [ref]
For example, stipulate two Newtonian fluids with the same dynamic viscosity 0.10Pas but different mass densities. In the same steady Couette flow as above, both have shear stress 2.0Pa, but
ρ1​ρ2​​=1000kgm−3,=500kgm−3,​ν1​ν2​​=1.0×10−4m2s−1,=2.0×10−4m2s−1.​
(4)
The same viscous stress transports momentum through less mass in the second fluid, so its velocity responds faster to a given nonuniform viscous force.
To see the diffusion equation directly, consider an unsteady parallel velocity field u=(u(y,t),0,0) with constant material coefficients and no streamwise pressure gradient or body force. Its convective acceleration vanishes, and the Navier-Stokes equation reduces to
∂t​u=ν∂y2​u.
(5)
Balancing u/t against νu/h2 gives the viscous diffusion time tν​∼h2/ν. For the 1.0mm gap, the two fluids have scales 0.010s and 0.0050s. This is a characteristic scale, not an exact settling time. For example, a perturbation of the Couette flow profile satisfying zero perturbation velocity at both plates is
δu(y,t)=Vsin(hπy​)exp(−h2π2νt​).
(6)
Substituting verifies the diffusion equation; its amplitude falls by a factor e in h2/(π2ν). Kinematic viscosity is therefore useful for predicting how quickly a moving wall influences the interior, while dynamic viscosity gives the shear stress at a prescribed shear rate.
