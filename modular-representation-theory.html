p-local module

Words: 54
Modular representation theory OurBigBook.com
A p-local module here is a finite direct sum of induced representations from normalizers NG​(Q) of nontrivial p-subgroups Q. This usage includes induction from G itself when G normalizes such a subgroup. Green correspondence and induction on vertex order express every finite module, or lattice over a complete valuation ring, as a difference of p-local modules after adjoining projective modules.

Splitting p-modular system ((K,O,k))

Modular representation theory OurBigBook.com
Here O is a complete discrete valuation ring with fraction field K of characteristic zero and residue field k of characteristic p. The system is splitting for the finite group and its subgroups when their simple representations over K and k are absolutely irreducible. It permits ordinary and modular representation theory to be compared by reduction of lattices modulo the maximal ideal.

Indecomposable modules for a cyclic p-group (Vi​=k[t]/(ti))

Words: 48
Modular representation theory OurBigBook.com
For P cyclic of order q=pn in characteristic p, its group algebra is k[t]/(tq). The nilpotent Jordan normal form classifies its indecomposable finite-dimensional modules as Vi​, 1≤i≤q. A module homomorphism Vi​→Vj​ is determined by an element annihilated by ti, so its dimension is min(i,j).

Modular representation ring (Rk​(G))

Words: 82
Modular representation theory OurBigBook.com
The modular representation ring is the Grothendieck group of finite-dimensional group representations, with relations [V]=[U]+[W] for every short exact sequence 0→U→V→W→0, and multiplication [V][W]=[V⊗k​W] using the tensor product of group representations. The Jordan–Hölder theorem makes the classes of simple modules a free integral basis. Tensoring over a field is exact, so this multiplication is well defined. When k is a splitting field for finite group representations, Brauer characters identify C⊗Z​Rk​(G) with the algebra of complex class functions on p-regular elements. The splitting hypothesis is essential: F2​C3​≅F2​×F4​ has two simple modules, although C3​ has three 2-regular conjugacy classes.

If G is a finite p-group and k has characteristic p, then the augmentation ideal is the Jacobson radical of kG and kG/J(kG)≅k. Thus kG is a local ring, has only the trivial simple module, and its regular module is indecomposable.

Integral form of a group representation (W⊂V)

Words: 165 Articles: 1

For a p-modular system (K,O,k), an integral form of a finite-dimensional KG-module V is a G-stable finite free O-submodule W⊂V with K⊗O​W≅V. Starting with a basis lattice L, the sum ∑g∈G​gL supplies such a form: it is finitely generated and torsion-free, hence free over the discrete valuation ring O. Brauer–Nesbitt theorem

Words: 167 Articles: 1
Brauer character OurBigBook.com
The Brauer character of a finite-dimensional modular representation determines its semisimplification. More strongly, the irreducible Brauer characters are linearly independent as complex-valued functions on the p-regular conjugacy classes.

Brauer character basis theorem

Words: 139
Brauer–Nesbitt theorem OurBigBook.com
Over a splitting field for finite group representations of characteristic p, the irreducible Brauer characters form a complex basis of the class functions on the p-regular elements. Independence is the character form of the Brauer–Nesbitt theorem. To obtain spanning, extend such a class function by zero on the p-singular classes. Ordinary irreducible characters form a basis of all class functions by character orthogonality. Restricting them to p-regular elements yields Brauer characters of reductions of an integral form of a group representation in a compatible splitting p-modular system; if needed, first extend scalars, which does not change the simple-module list under the splitting hypothesis. Each restriction is a nonnegative integral sum of simple Brauer characters by exact-sequence additivity and the Jordan–Hölder theorem. Thus these restrictions span, proving the assertion. Consequently the number of simple modules equals the number of p-regular conjugacy classes, and evaluation identifies the complexified modular representation ring with the product of one copy of C for each such class.

Head-socle identity for a symmetric algebra (SocP≅P/RadP)

Words: 71
Symmetric algebra (Frobenius algebra) OurBigBook.com
For a finite-dimensional symmetric algebra over any field, an indecomposable projective module P=Ae has isomorphic simple head of a module and socle. A symmetrizing functional pairs eA with Ae nondegenerately, identifying Ae with the left dual module D(eA). Taking its socle gives D(eA/eAJ(A)). This dual of the right simple top and the left simple top Ae/J(A)e belong to the same matrix block of the semisimple algebra A/J(A), so they are isomorphic. The result does not require an algebraically closed ground field.

Full-vertex modules for a noncyclic p-group

Words: 132
Vertex of an indecomposable module OurBigBook.com
Every noncyclic finite finite p-group P has a quotient Cp​×Cp​. Over any field of characteristic p, define a square-zero representation with basis v0​,…,vr​,w1​,…,wr​ by xvi​=wi​ for 1≤i≤r, xv0​=0, yvi​=wi+1​ for 0≤i<r, yvr​=0, and xW=yW=0. Here x=g−1, y=h−1, and W is the span of the wi​. An endomorphism induces the same scalar on the top and on W; the remaining maps from the top to W form a square-zero ideal. Thus the endomorphism ring is local and the module is indecomposable. Inflating to P and choosing p∤2r+1 gives infinitely many distinct dimensions with vertex P: the trace of a relative-trace identity from any proper subgroup would force dimension zero in the field. This construction works over finite fields too.

Green correspondence

Words: 263 Articles: 3

Let NG​(D)≤H≤G. Set X={D∩gD:g∈/H} and Y={H∩gD:g∈/H}. Green correspondence pairs indecomposable modules with vertex D for G and H: restriction has a unique vertex-D summand, with the other summands relatively projective for Y; induction has a unique vertex-D summand, with the other summands relatively projective for X. The two assignments are inverse and preserve sources. Its more general form applies to vertices contained in D but not conjugate into any member of X.
Green correspondence from Mackey multiplicity
Words: 93
Green correspondence OurBigBook.com
Let NG​(D)≤H≤G and let U be indecomposable with vertex D over H. The Mackey restriction formula writes ResHG​IndHG​U=U⊕T, where the vertices in T lie in H∩gD for g∈/H. None of these subgroups contains an H-conjugate of D: equality would put g in NG​(D)H=H. Every summand of the induction with a G-conjugate of vertex D contributes a vertex-D summand on restriction. Krull-Schmidt decomposition therefore forces exactly one such summand, with multiplicity one. Choosing sources and using transitivity of induction proves the inverse correspondence and preservation of sources.

Inflated quotient projectives have trivial source
Words: 63
Trivial source module OurBigBook.com
If D is a normal p-subgroup of N, any indecomposable projective module of k(N/D), inflated to N, is a summand of k[N/D]=IndDN​k. It has trivial source and vertex D. Its Brauer quotient of a module at D is the entire module, since D acts trivially and all proper-subgroup relative traces multiply by an index divisible by p. Permutation homomorphisms lift through modular reduction
Words: 62
Trivial source module OurBigBook.com
For coset permutation modules, Mackey restriction formula and Frobenius reciprocity identify the homomorphism space with a free module having one orbit-sum basis vector per double coset. The same basis works over the residue field, so reduction of homomorphisms is surjective. Applied to endomorphisms of a permutation module, idempotent refinement theorem lifts each modular direct summand to a trivial source lattice. Lifting inverse isomorphisms between reductions shows uniqueness among trivial source lattices.

Parameter family for an elementary abelian p-group

Higman criterion for finite representation type of a group algebra

Nakayama block theorem

Words: 45

At fixed degree n in positive characteristic p, two Specht modules belong to the same block of a group algebra exactly when their labels have the same core of a partition at modulus p. The simple labels in that block are precisely the regular partitions with that core. The theorem follows by combining the residue-content criterion for symmetric-group blocks with the Jucys–Murphy description of the center of a symmetric-group algebra.

Modular reduction of block idempotents

Words: 62

For a complete discrete valuation ring O with residue field k, class sums give bases of Z(OG) and Z(kG), so reduction of the center of an associative algebra is surjective. Idempotent lifting in this complete commutative algebra gives a bijection of primitive central idempotents, hence of blocks of a group algebra. Corresponding blocks of a group algebra have the same defect groups of a block: relative traces reduce, and a lifted trace whose reduction is the block identity is a unit in its complete block center.

Trace criterion for defect groups

Words: 194
Defect group of a block OurBigBook.com
For a p-subgroup D of a finite group G, set ID​=TrDG​((kG)D), a transfer ideal of conjugation-fixed elements in Z(kG). A block of a group algebra idempotent b lies in ID​ if and only if a defect group of a block of b is conjugate to a subgroup of D. In particular, defect groups can equivalently be defined as minimal subgroups D with b∈ID​.
Here is a proof using the Brauer morphism. A subgroup of smallest order with b∈ID​ exists, since a Sylow subgroup P gives b=TrPG​(b/[G:P]). Write b=TrDG​(a) and replace a by ba. If BrD​(b)=0, then ba belongs to the kernel of the Brauer morphism, which is the sum of proper-subgroup relative traces. Hence b∈∑Q<D​IQ​. The algebra bZ(kG) is a commutative Artinian ring and a local ring, so multiplying this equality by b forces some ideal bIQ​ to contain its identity b. This contradicts minimality. On the other hand, BrE​(ID​)=0 unless E is conjugate into D: the E-action on G/D has no fixed coset otherwise, and nonfixed orbit sizes vanish in characteristic p. Thus these minimal subgroups are precisely the maximal subgroups with nonzero Brauer image. They are all conjugate. Trace transitivity proves the stated criterion for larger D.
