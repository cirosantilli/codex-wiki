Increasing and decreasing approximations to a common limit (1−1/n↑1,1+1/n↓1)

 ... Analysis Real analysis Sequence and series Sequence Monotone sequence Bounded monotone sequence theorem
OurBigBook.com Words: 112
For the two sequences in bounded monotone sequence theorem,
an+1​−an​=n(n+1)1​>0,bn+1​−bn​=−n(n+1)1​<0.
(11)
Their directions and bounds are explicit. One is a succession of lower approximations and the other a succession of upper approximations to the same limit of a sequence. To obtain absolute error strictly below ε>0, it suffices to choose an integer N>1/ε, and then every n≥N works. For ε=0.01, the first such index is 101; at 100 the absolute error equals the tolerance.
A geometric sequence offers a faster example. The recurrence relation c1​=1/2, cn+1​=(cn​+1)/2 gives cn​=1−2−n. Thus (cn​) is an increasing bounded sequence with limit of a sequence one and absolute error 2−n. For ε=0.01, n=7 already works, since 2−7=1/128<0.01, whereas 2−6=1/64>0.01. The bounded monotone sequence theorem guarantees both examples converge; the formulas explain their different speeds.
