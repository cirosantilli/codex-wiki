Pushforward of a finite probability distribution (q(y)=∑x:f(x)=y​p(x))

 ... Mathematics Area of mathematics Probability and statistics Probability theory Probability distribution Finite probability distribution
OurBigBook.com Words: 112
For a finite probability distribution p with support S and a total function f:S→Y, assign each image value the combined mass of its preimages. Its support is exactly the finite image f(S), listed without repetitions. Every listed mass is positive because it contains at least one positive source mass.
The fiber of a function construction gives a set partition of S, so finite summation gives ∑y​q(y)=∑x∈S​p(x)=1. This proves nonnegativity and normalization without assuming that f is injective. In code, mapping support values and then overwriting repeated image keys is incorrect; repeated keys must have their masses added. For example, mapping a uniform distribution on four consecutive integers to parity produces two outcomes with mass one half each.
