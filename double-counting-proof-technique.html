Double counting (proof technique)

 Home Mathematics Area of mathematics Combinatorics
OurBigBook.com Words: 132
Double counting establishes an identity by computing the cardinality of the same finite set in two ways. The useful step is to identify exactly what the members of that collection are; an algebraic equality then records two legitimate ways to organize them.
Take the ordered pairs (S,x) where S is a k-element subset of an n-element set and x∈S is marked. Choosing S first gives k(kn​) pairs. Choosing x first leaves k−1 members to choose from the other n−1, giving n(k−1n−1​). Hence
k(kn​)=n(k−1n−1​)(1≤k≤n).
(22)
For five people and two-person teams, both counts give 20: ten teams with two choices of a marked member, or five choices of that member with four choices of a teammate. The identity is explained by the bijection between these descriptions, rather than merely verified through factorials.
