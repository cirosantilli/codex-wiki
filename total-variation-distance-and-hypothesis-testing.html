Total variation distance and hypothesis testing (optimal success probability=21​(1+dTV​(P,Q)))

 Home Mathematics Area of mathematics Probability and statistics Total variation distance
OurBigBook.com Words: 144
Suppose one observation comes from either P or Q, each with prior probability 1/2. A decision rule that chooses P on an event E and Q otherwise has success probability
21​P(E)+21​Q(Ec)=21​(1+P(E)−Q(E)).
(41)
On a finite set, choosing E={x:P(x)>Q(x)} therefore gives 21​(1+dTV​(P,Q)). This is the equal-prior version of minimum sum of errors in a simple hypothesis test; allowing a statistical test to use an auxiliary random variable cannot improve it, because for each outcome the better of the two decisions already maximizes its contribution.
For the four-outcome example, choose P after A and Q after B or C, with either decision allowed after D. Choosing Q after D yields 21​(0.4+0.9)=0.65. The best success probability is 65%, with a 35% error probability. A smaller total variation distance means the two sources are harder to distinguish from one observation. Unequal prior probabilities, unequal costs, or multiple observations change this particular decision problem; the 65% figure is not a universal bound for every experiment.
