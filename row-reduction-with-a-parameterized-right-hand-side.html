Row reduction with a parameterized right-hand side (τ=5)

 ... Area of mathematics Analysis Numerical analysis Numerical linear algebra Gaussian elimination Row echelon form
OurBigBook.com Words: 278
Work over the rational numbers, allowing unknowns in the real numbers when interpreting the geometry. Consider four equations in three unknowns:
A=​1231​2573​−1102​​,b(τ)=​14τ3​​,Ax=b(τ).
(64)
The third row of A is the sum of its first two rows, and the fourth is their difference, second minus first. The same relations must hold on the right: consistency requires τ=1+4=5 and 3=4−1. The latter condition already holds. Gaussian elimination makes this compatibility test explicit while exposing the remaining freedom.
Apply the elementary row operations in this order, using each row's current value:
R2​←R2​−2R1​,R3​←R3​−3R1​,R4​←R4​−R1​,
(65)
then R3​←R3​−R2​ and R4​←R4​−R2​. The resulting augmented matrix is in row echelon form for every τ:
​1000​2100​−1300​12τ−50​​.
(66)
When τ=5, the third row has its pivot element in the augmented column. It says 0=τ−5, so the linear system has no solution. When τ=5, that entire row vanishes and only two pivot elements remain. More equations than unknowns therefore do not force a unique solution: some equations may repeat information.
The final operation R1​←R1​−2R2​ reduces the coefficient block to
R=​1000​0100​−7300​​,Eb(τ)=​−32τ−50​​.
(67)
The product of all six elementary matrices is
E=​5−2−11​−21−1−1​0010​0001​​,EA=R.
(68)
Direct matrix multiplication verifies both displayed transformations. Its determinant is one, so this transformation is reversible. In particular, the third row of E extracts the discrepancy between the third equation and the sum of the first two.
For τ=5, [R∣Eb] is the reduced row echelon form of the full augmented matrix. For τ=5, only its coefficient block is reduced: scale the third row by 1/(τ−5) and clear the entries above its augmented pivot element. The full reduced row echelon form is then
​1000​0100​−7300​0010​​.
(69)
The last column is part of the matrix when testing the echelon conditions. Calling [R∣Eb] fully reduced before clearing that column would miss this distinction.
