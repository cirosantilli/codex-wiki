Ising model

 Home Physics Branches of physics Statistical physics Spin model
OurBigBook.com Words: 4k Articles: 48
The Ising model is a spin model with an Ising spin σv​∈{−1,1} at every graph vertex v. For a finite graph with vertex set V and edge set E, a uniform coupling J and field parameter h give the Hamiltonian
H(σ)=−J∑{v,w}∈E​σv​σw​−h∑v∈V​σv​,β=kB​T1​.
(4)
Each edge is counted once. Here T>0 is temperature, kB​ is the Boltzmann constant, and β is inverse temperature; J and h have units of energy. For magnetic applications, h includes the magnetic moment multiplying the applied magnetic field. The canonical ensemble assigns the Boltzmann distribution
P(σ)=Ze−βH(σ)​,Z=∑τ∈{−1,1}V​e−βH(τ).
(5)
The partition function Z makes the probabilities sum to one. Positive J rewards equal neighboring Ising spins, modeling ferromagnetism; negative J rewards opposite neighbors, giving an antiferromagnetic Ising model. Positive h rewards +1, while negative h rewards −1.[ref]
A single local rule can organize a whole graph: rewarding agreement on each edge makes all-equal Ising spin patterns favorable even though no edge directly couples distant vertices. The four-spin Ising ring makes this visible with only sixteen microstates. It also separates three different questions: whether neighbors agree, whether the mean spin magnetization is nonzero, and how large its thermal fluctuations are.
A motivating experiment is the University of Colorado Boulder's Curie-temperature demonstration. An iron rod suspended from a twisting wire on a stand is initially held near a permanent magnet by ferromagnetism. A gas burner supplied from a propane cylinder heats the rod; the readout is its motion away from the magnet, followed by return as it cools. Repeated cooling supplies a reversible comparison on the same sample. The source explicitly calls for heating the rod while keeping the magnet cool, since heating the magnet can reduce its strength. The inference is that warming the sample weakens its magnetic attraction across the Curie temperature. This is a qualitative force readout, with no calibrated temperature or magnetization trace supplied: it motivates competition between alignment and thermal fluctuations, but does not identify J, measure individual Ising spins, or realize a four-spin Ising ring.[ref]
Ernst Ising's 1925 paper examined a linear chain of two-orientation magnetic elements with neighboring interactions. In its calculation for a large chain, the zero-field mean magnetization vanishes and the model has no spontaneous magnetization. The inspected English translation states this immediately after equation (8) in §2. This historical result concerns the chain; it does not establish the behavior of a two-dimensional Ising model.[ref]
