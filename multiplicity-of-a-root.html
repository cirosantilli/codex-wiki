Multiplicity of a root

 ... Area of mathematics Algebra Polynomial Zero of a function Root of a polynomial Multiplicity (mathematics)
OurBigBook.com Words: 152 Articles: 2
For a nonzero polynomial over a field, the multiplicity of a root a is the largest integer m≥1 such that (X−a)m divides it. Write p(X)=(X−a)mq(X) with q(a)=0. For real polynomials, continuity keeps the sign of q fixed sufficiently near a, so an odd integer m makes the graph cross the axis and an even integer m makes it touch without crossing.
The right-hand panel above replaces (X−2)2 by (X−2)2−ε. For ε=0.04, its two roots of a polynomial are 1.8 and 2.2; for ε=−0.04, they are 2±0.2i. The unperturbed double root lies between these two cases. Small coefficient changes can therefore split a repeated root into distinct real roots or move it off the real axis, illustrating why repeated-root problems can be numerically sensitive.
