Covector (φ∈V∗)

 Home Mathematics Area of mathematics Algebra Linear algebra Dual space
OurBigBook.com Words: 217 Articles: 1
A covector is an element of the dual space, hence a linear functional on a vector space. In finite-dimensional coordinates, it acts as a row multiplied by a coordinate vector column. The row (2​−1​) acts on (31​) to give five. The terminology emphasizes its role as a scalar-valued measurement and its behavior under a change of basis.
For example, the total derivative of f(x,y)=x2+y2 at (1,2) sends a small displacement h to the linear change df(h)=2hx​+4hy​. This covector describes the first-order response. The gradient ∇f=(2,4) represents the same rule through the chosen Euclidean inner product. In differential geometry, the cotangent space collects these scalar-valued measurements of tangent vectors.
