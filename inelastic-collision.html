Inelastic collision

 Home Physics Branches of physics Classical mechanics Particle dynamics Collision mechanics
OurBigBook.com Words: 158 Articles: 1
An inelastic collision changes total kinetic energy. In the dissipative case it decreases, transferring energy to internal energy, including deformation and microscopic motion. Isolated colliding bodies still obey momentum conservation; conservation of energy requires accounting for the transferred energy. Bodies need not stick in every inelastic collision. The decrease in translational kinetic energy drives dissipative spreading of a planetary ring.
For the incoming masses and velocities in the elastic collision example, stipulate that the bodies stick. This perfectly inelastic collision has a shared outgoing velocity

The outgoing momenta are 4 and 2kgm/s, totaling 6kgm/s. The outgoing kinetic energies are 4 and 2J, totaling 6J. Thus 9−6=3J of kinetic energy becomes internal energy within the isolated pair. Nothing in momentum conservation requires this 3J to remain as kinetic energy. [ref]
