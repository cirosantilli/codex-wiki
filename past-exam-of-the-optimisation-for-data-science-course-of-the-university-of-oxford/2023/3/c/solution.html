For arbitrary x,y, write the Bregman divergence
Df​(x,y)=f(x)−f(y)−∇f(y)T(x−y)≥0,δi​=∂i​f(x)−∂i​f(y).
(47)
Part (b), with its supporting affine function constructed at this y, implies the coordinate derivative bound by Bregman divergence
δi2​≤2Li​Df​(x,y).
(48)
For a nonempty block B, set SB​=∑i∈B​Li​ and δB​=∇B​f(x)−∇B​f(y). Sum the coordinate inequalities:
∥δB​∥2≤2SB​Df​(x,y).
(49)
Since y in part (b) was arbitrary, interchange x and y to obtain the same left side bounded by 2SB​Df​(y,x). Adding the two inequalities and dividing by two gives
∥δB​∥2≤SB​(Df​(x,y)+Df​(y,x))=SB​(∇f(x)−∇f(y))T(x−y).
(50)
Now require that x−y have zero coordinates outside B. The last dot product is then δBT​(x−y). By the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality,
∥δB​∥2≤SB​∥δB​∥∥x−y∥.
(51)
If δB​=0, the desired estimate is immediate; otherwise divide by its Euclidean norm. We have proved the stronger block Lipschitz constant from coordinate smoothness estimate
∥∇B​f(x)−∇B​f(y)∥≤(i∈B∑​Li​)∥x−y∥when x−y is supported in B.​
(52)
In particular, take y=x−α∇B​f(x) for any real α. This proves (6), with a valid choice
LB​=i∈B∑​Li​≤∣B∣i∈Bmax​Li​.​
(53)
The least admissible Lipschitz constant is therefore no larger than this choice. For the empty block, the block coordinate gradient vanishes identically and its least nonnegative Lipschitz constant is zero. The proof uses only C1 differentiability, convexity and coordinate Lipschitz continuity, with no assumed Hessian matrix.
