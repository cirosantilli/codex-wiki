Photoelectron peak overlap and resolution

Choose two hypothetical environments A and B of one chemical element, with line centers μA​=284eV and μB​=286eV. Model each line by a normalized Gaussian function with common standard deviation σ, and stipulate integrated count weights wA​=3/4, wB​=1/4. On the binding energy axis E, the normalized count density is
G(E;μ,σ)=2π​σ1​exp[−2σ2(E−μ)2​],I(E)=43​G(E;284,σ)+41​G(E;286,σ).
(26)
All energies in this expression use electronvolts. Each component's integrated area equals its count weight, and ∫I(E)dE=1. There is no background, statistical counting fluctuation, or additional emission feature in this stipulated model. These choices deliberately isolate photoelectron peak overlap and resolution.
Direct integration, or the law of total expectation applied to the component label, gives the centroid
Eˉ=wA​μA​+wB​μB​=284.5eV.
(27)
The centroid lies between the chemical environments and is not either component's binding energy. Expanding (E−Eˉ)2 within each component, the odd cross term integrates to zero. The resulting variance is
Var(E)=σ2+wA​wB​(μB​−μA​)2=σ2+0.75eV2.
(28)
For σ=0.25eV the variance is 0.8125eV2 and the two maxima are separated. For σ=1.00eV it is 1.7500eV2 and there is one maximum. Each Gaussian's full width at half maximum is 22ln2​σ, approximately 0.589 and 2.355eV respectively. The chemical separation remains 2eV in both cases. The number of visible maxima therefore does not equal the number of chemical components.
One interpretation of this change is additional independent Gaussian instrument blur. The convolution of probability densities adds the variances, so broadening σ=0.25 to 1 requires response standard deviation 1−0.252​=15​/4≃0.968246eV. A normalized response with zero mean preserves the centroid and integrated counts. Real line shapes and backgrounds can depart from this model, and separating hidden components is an inverse problem; a plausible fit does not alone establish uniqueness.
Finally, count weights describe detection. In a simple response model, let ni​ denote the number of emitting sites sampled in environment i, si​ its effective photoionization cross section, and Ti​ the probability that its emitted electron contributes to the selected measured line. Then
Ai​=Cni​si​Ti​,wi​=∑j​nj​sj​Tj​ni​si​Ti​​.
(29)
The common factor C includes exposure. Here Ti​ includes escape from the sample, analyzer transmission and detection. Count weights equal site fractions only if the relevant response factors are equal or corrected. They are not automatically molecular mole fractions; different molecules can supply different numbers of the selected emitting sites. In an original example, equal site numbers with sA​TA​=3sB​TB​ give the plotted 3:1 count ratio despite a 1:1 site ratio.
