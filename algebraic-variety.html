Rational map of algebraic varieties

Words: 44
Algebraic variety OurBigBook.com
A rational map is a morphism of algebraic varieties defined on a dense open subset, with representatives identified when they agree on a smaller dense open subset. For an integral source and a separated variety as target, agreement on a dense open determines a morphism uniquely. This definition also permits nonprojective sources.

Ring of rational functions on a reduced variety (Rat(X))

Words: 151 Articles: 1
Algebraic variety OurBigBook.com
For a possibly reducible algebraic variety X, take pairs (U,f) with U a dense open subset and f a regular function on U. Identify pairs agreeing on a common dense open subset. Addition and multiplication on intersections make these classes a ring. For an affine variety with reduced coordinate ring A, this ring is its total quotient ring. For an irreducible variety, it is the function field. An isomorphism of dense open subsets induces an isomorphism of these rings.

Local ring embeds in rational functions on incident components (OX,P​↪Rat(Y))

Words: 83
Ring of rational functions on a reduced variety OurBigBook.com
Let Y be the reduced union of the irreducible components of an affine variety X containing P. Removing all other components leaves an open neighbourhood of P on which X and Y agree, so their local rings at P agree. In the coordinate ring B=k[Y], every denominator not vanishing at P avoids all minimal primes: each component of Y contains P. Such a denominator is a non-zero-divisor. Consequently BmP​​ injects into the total quotient ring Q(B)=Rat(Y). One cannot generally replace Y by a single component through an intersection point.

Rational function on an algebraic variety (k(X))

Words: 52
Algebraic variety OurBigBook.com
On an integral algebraic variety, a rational function is an equivalence class of regular functions on nonempty open subsets, with equality on a common dense open subset. These form the function field k(X). On an affine open SpecA, this field is the fraction field of A.

Quasi-projective algebraic set

Words: 52 Articles: 1
Algebraic variety OurBigBook.com
A locally closed affine algebraic set in a projective space, equivalently an open subset of a closed subset of projective space cut out by homogeneous polynomials. Reducibility is allowed for an algebraic set; an irreducible variety with such an embedding is a quasi-projective variety.

Quasi-projective variety

Words: 19
Quasi-projective algebraic set OurBigBook.com
An irreducible variety which admits a locally closed embedding into projective space. Open subsets of affine varieties and blowups of algebraic varieties along closed centres give important examples.

Irreducible variety

Words: 34
Algebraic variety OurBigBook.com
An algebraic variety whose underlying topological space with its Zariski topology is an irreducible topological space: it is nonempty and cannot be a union of two proper closed subsets. For a reduced affine variety, this is equivalent to its coordinate ring being an integral domain.

Constructible subset of a variety

Words: 62 Articles: 1
Algebraic variety OurBigBook.com
A constructible subset is a finite union of locally closed subsets in the Zariski topology. A dense constructible subset contains a dense open subset. Constructibility alone is weaker than closedness, but a constructible subgroup is closed.

Chevalley constructibility theorem

Words: 30
Constructible subset of a variety OurBigBook.com
The image of a morphism of algebraic varieties is constructible. This replaces the generally false assertion that all morphisms have closed image. Group structure adds the fact that a constructible subgroup is closed.

Rational point (X(K))

Words: 83
Algebraic variety OurBigBook.com
For an algebraic variety X defined over a field K, a K-rational point is a morphism SpecK→X over K. In affine coordinates it is a solution of the defining equations with every coordinate in K; in projective coordinates it is a nonzero coordinate vector over K, considered up to common nonzero scaling. The set is denoted X(K). Over a number field, having points over every completion need not imply having a rational point; for genus-one coverings this failure is recorded by the Tate–Shafarevich group.

Smooth algebraic variety

Words: 61 Articles: 1
Algebraic variety OurBigBook.com
An algebraic variety is smooth over its ground field when its structural morphism is smooth. Over an algebraically closed field its local rings are regular; on an irreducible variety its tangent spaces all have the variety's dimension of a scheme.

Smoothness of an algebraic variety

Words: 24
Smooth algebraic variety OurBigBook.com
Over a perfect field, a point of an algebraic variety is smooth exactly when its local ring is a regular local ring. The smooth locus of a variety is open; its closed complement is the singular locus.

Normalization of an algebraic variety

Words: 41
Algebraic variety OurBigBook.com
The normalization of an integral algebraic variety is obtained by taking the integral closure of its affine coordinate rings in its function field. Its natural morphism is finite and birational, and its source is normal. For Cartier divisors, bigness under finite normalization compares section growth on the two varieties.

Closed subvariety

Words: 24
Algebraic variety OurBigBook.com
A closed subvariety is an integral closed subscheme of an algebraic variety. It supplies the lower-dimensional loci on which positivity is tested by the Nakai–Moishezon criterion.

Algebraic curve

Words: 844 Articles: 14
Algebraic variety OurBigBook.com
An algebraic curve is a one-dimensional algebraic variety. On a smooth algebraic curve, the local ring at every closed point is a discrete valuation ring, which allows orders of zeros and poles to be recorded as a divisor on an algebraic curve.

Cissoid of Diocles (y2(2a−x)=x3)

Words: 82
Algebraic curve OurBigBook.com
For a>0, this algebraic curve can be constructed using a circle of radius a centred at (a,0) and its tangent line x=2a. On a ray from the origin, subtract the distance to its nonzero circle intersection from the distance to the line. The resulting radial distance is r=2asin2θ/cosθ, for −π/2<θ<π/2. Thus a parametric curve for it is
x=1+t22at2​,y=1+t22at3​,t∈R.
(1)
Eliminating t gives the displayed cubic equation. It has a cusp at the origin, horizontal tangent direction there, and vertical asymptote x=2a. Its name honours Diocles.

Integral projective curve

Words: 19
Algebraic curve OurBigBook.com
An algebraic curve which is projective, reduced and irreducible. Over an algebraically closed field, its only global regular functions are constants and its arithmetic genus is h1(OC​)≥0.

Nonsingular point of an algebraic curve (Fx​(P)=0 or Fy​(P)=0)

Words: 58
Algebraic curve OurBigBook.com
For an affine plane algebraic curve F(x,y)=0, a point over an algebraic closure is nonsingular when the displayed condition holds. Its Zariski tangent space has dimension one, and a local coordinate can be chosen in the direction of a nonzero partial derivative. A smooth algebraic curve has all points nonsingular. Over a complete local field, the Hensel lemma lifts a nonsingular residue point by solving for a coordinate with unit partial derivative.

Affine algebraic curve

Words: 130 Articles: 1
Algebraic curve OurBigBook.com
An affine algebraic curve is an affine variety of Krull dimension one. An irreducible affine curve has a finitely generated one-dimensional domain as its coordinate ring. Quotienting this ring by a nonzero element gives a zero-dimensional Noetherian ring, hence an Artinian ring. Consequently a nonzero regular function has finitely many zeros. No nonsingularity assumption is part of the definition.

For nonzero real a, the folium of Descartes is the algebraic curve x3+y3=3axy, with rational parametrization of an algebraic curve

(2)

Cuspidal cubic

Words: 165 Articles: 2
Algebraic curve OurBigBook.com
The projective algebraic curve D={y2z=x3}⊂P2 is an irreducible cubic with a cusp at [0:0:1]. In the chart z=1, its equation is y2=x3, with parametrization (x,y)=(t2,t3). Its affine version y2=z3, used below, differs only by renaming a coordinate. At the point [0:1:0], the chart y=1 gives z=x3, which is smooth and exhibits its unique smooth flex of a cuspidal cubic.

Unique smooth flex of a cuspidal cubic (p=[0:1:0],L={z=0})

Words: 63
Cuspidal cubic OurBigBook.com
For D={y2z=x3}, the determinant of the homogeneous Hessian matrix is 24xy2. Its intersections with D are the cusp [0:0:1] and the smooth point [0:1:0]. The Hessian criterion for a flex therefore gives a unique smooth flex, with tangent z=0 and contact order three. Consequently every pair consisting of a triple line and a cuspidal cubic meeting only at a smooth point is projectively equivalent to (3{z=0},D).

xy=0,y2−z3+xz=0,x(x+y+2z+1)=0
(4)

Symmetric product of a curve (C(d))

Words: 56
Algebraic curve OurBigBook.com
The scheme C(d) parametrizes effective degree-d Cartier divisors on a smooth algebraic curve. Near a Cartier divisor ∑mi​Pi​, its completed local ring is a power-series ring in the elementary symmetric coordinates of mi​ local parameters at each Pi​. It is therefore smooth of dimension d, even when the characteristic of a field divides d!. For projective C it is projective.

Monomial curve (Speck[ta1​,…,tan​])

Words: 142 Articles: 1
Algebraic curve OurBigBook.com
Over a field k, an affine curve parametrized by positive integer powers of one parameter. Its coordinate ring is the subalgebra generated by those powers, and its defining prime ideal is the kernel of k[x1​,…,xn​]→k[t] sending xi​ to tai​. Equal exponent sums yield binomial relations. Studying those relations connects an additive semigroup of exponents to the geometry of the curve.

Monomial curve with exponents three, four and five (k[t3,t4,t5])

Words: 83
Monomial curve OurBigBook.com
The kernel of x↦t3, y↦t4, z↦t5 is generated by y2−xz, yz−x3, z2−x2y. Reducing these three relations leaves a unique normal form A0​(x)+A1​(x)y+A2​(x)z: its images have distinct exponents modulo three. The curve ring is therefore finite free of rank three over k[x] and has dimension one. In a three-variable polynomial ring its ideal has height two, but its three independent quadratic initial forms force at least three generators. This is a concrete failure of generation by codimension many equations.

Rational parametrization of an algebraic curve

Words: 53
Algebraic curve OurBigBook.com
A rational parametrization expresses the coordinates of an algebraic curve as rational functions of a parameter, with a rational inverse on a dense open subset. It identifies the function field with K(t) and the smooth projective normalization of an algebraic curve with the projective line. A nonconstant parametrizing map without an inverse is a weaker notion; even this cannot exist for an elliptic curve in characteristic zero.

Arithmetic genus (pa​(C)=1−χ(OC​))

Words: 106 Articles: 1
Algebraic curve OurBigBook.com
The arithmetic genus of a proper curve C is pa​(C)=1−χ(C,OC​). For an integral projective curve over an algebraically closed field, H0(C,OC​)=k, so pa​=h1≥0. Its finite normalization has pa​(C)=g(Cν)+length(ν∗​OCν​/OC​). Hence arithmetic genus zero forces the curve to be smooth and rational.

Genus of a complete-intersection space curve (pa​=1+2ab(a+b−4)​)

Words: 62
Arithmetic genus OurBigBook.com
For a projective complete intersection curve cut out by a homogeneous regular sequence of positive degrees a,b in Pk3​, the Koszul resolution gives Hilbert polynomial abm+ab(4−a−b)/2. Hence its arithmetic genus is 1+ab(a+b−4)/2. The same resolution shows H0(C,OC​)=k, and the dimension of a scheme is one, so this genus equals dimk​H1(C,OC​). Smoothness and irreducibility of the curve are not required. For degrees five and seven, the value is 141.

Prevariety

Words: 59
Algebraic variety OurBigBook.com
In the classical convention over an algebraically closed field k, a prevariety is an irreducible topological space with a sheaf of rings which has a finite open cover by affine varieties, with compatible local identifications. A prevariety need not be separated. A classical algebraic variety is a separated prevariety: its diagonal morphism has closed image. Some authors allow reducible prevarieties and varieties; the separation criterion and product construction remain the same under that convention.

Algebraic group action (G×V→V)

Words: 51
Algebraic group OurBigBook.com
This is a group action whose action map G×V→V is a morphism of varieties. Orbits are locally closed: constructibility gives a relatively open piece, and its translates cover the orbit. An orbit of an irreducible group is irreducible and open in its closure. These facts control degenerations of quiver representations.

Dimension formula for an algebraic group homomorphism (dimG=dimkerφ+dimimφ)

The kernel is a closed identity fiber. The image is closed because a constructible subgroup is closed. Every nonempty fiber is a kernel translate, so the fiber dimension theorem gives dimG=dimkerφ+dimimφ, even for inseparable morphisms.

Constructible subgroup is closed

Algebraic group OurBigBook.com
Let S be constructible in an algebraic group, and C=S. The closure is a subgroup. Choose a dense open U⊆C contained in S. For every c∈C, the dense opens U,cU intersect, giving u=cv for u,v∈S. Hence c=uv−1∈S and S=C. The Chevalley constructibility theorem therefore proves closedness of homomorphism images.

Product dimension bound for sections on a curve (dim(VW)≥dimV+dimW−1)

Words: 80
Smooth algebraic curve OurBigBook.com
For nonzero finite-dimensional spaces V,W of sections of two line bundles on a smooth integral curve over an algebraically closed field, their product span in sections of the tensor product satisfies this bound. At one point choose bases with strictly increasing orders of vanishing. Multiplying every first-space basis vector by the first second-space vector, and then the last first-space vector by the remaining second-space vectors, produces distinct increasing orders and hence independent sections. This proves the dimension inequality needed for the Clifford inequality for curves.

Affineness from a unit-ideal principal affine cover (∑i​gi​fi​=1,Xfi​​ affine ⟹ X affine)

Words: 68
Affine algebraic set OurBigBook.com
A classical variety is affine if finitely many global regular functions generate the unit ideal and their nonvanishing opens are affine. Localization of global sections on a principal open identifies their coordinate rings with Afi​​, where A=Γ(X,OX​). Choose a finite-type subalgebra of A containing the functions, their unit-ideal coefficients and numerators of generators of every Afi​​. Its affine variety has the same principal affine charts, whose isomorphisms glue globally. This avoids assuming finite generation of A before affineness is known.

Coordinate ring of a product of affine varieties (k[X×Y]≅k[X]⊗k​k[Y])

Words: 114
Affine algebraic set OurBigBook.com
For affine varieties X,Y over an algebraically closed field k, their product in a category is affine, with coordinate ring A⊗k​B, where A=k[X] and B=k[Y]. Presenting A=k[x]/J and B=k[y]/L identifies this ring with k[x,y]/(J,L). If both varieties are irreducible, this is an integral domain: express a nonzero tensor F using linearly independent second factors. The points x where its specialization Fx​ is nonzero form a nonempty open subset of X. For two nonzero tensors F,G, choose x in the intersection of these open subsets; then Fx​Gx​=0 in the domain B. The Hilbert Nullstellensatz identifies (J,L) with the defining ideal of X×Y. The universal property follows from the universal property of the tensor product of modules as a tensor product of commutative algebras.

Conic bundle (f:X→B)

A flat proper morphism of algebraic varieties whose geometric fibres are plane conics, usually presented as a quadratic equation in a projective bundle of relative dimension two. Conic discriminant (Δ=detM)

Words: 154 Articles: 1

In characteristic different from two, a local equation for a conic bundle is zTMz=0 with M symmetric. A fibre is singular exactly when the determinant vanishes. Changing the fibre coordinates multiplies that determinant by a nonzero square, so its zero locus is intrinsically defined even when the quadratic coefficients belong to twisting line bundles.
Reduced singular fibres of a smooth conic bundle (rankM=2,Δ′(t)=0)
Words: 103
Conic discriminant OurBigBook.com
Let the base be a smooth algebraic curve, the total surface be smooth, and the field be algebraically closed of characteristic different from two. At a rank-two singular fibre with kernel vector v, the fibre derivatives vanish at [v]. Smoothness requires vTM′v=0. The adjugate matrix is a nonzero multiple of vvT, so the determinant has a simple zero. At rank at most one, the projective kernel contains a line and the quadratic vTM′v has a zero on it; that point would make the total surface singular. Thus every singular fibre is a pair of distinct projective lines and its discriminant zero is simple.

Generically finite morphism

Words: 45
Morphism of algebraic varieties OurBigBook.com
A dominant morphism of varieties is generically finite when its extension of function fields is finite. Its degree is the degree of this field extension. A dominant morphism between integral varieties of equal dimension is generically finite; over a suitable nonempty open subset of the target its fibers are finite.

Isomorphic local rings determine isomorphic neighborhoods

Words: 85
Morphism of algebraic varieties OurBigBook.com
A k-algebra isomorphism between the local rings at closed points extends to a morphism on some neighborhoods: represent the images of finitely many affine coordinate generators by fractions, and shrink where their denominators are nonzero and their finitely many relations hold. Extend its inverse the same way. The two compositions agree with the identity as germs; finitely many coordinate equalities make them identities after shrinking again. Taking inverse images of these neighborhoods then gives mutually inverse actual neighborhood isomorphisms, not just a formal-completion isomorphism.

Degree of a morphism of curves (degf=[k(C):k(D)])

Words: 113
Morphism of algebraic varieties OurBigBook.com
A nonconstant morphism of algebraic varieties f:C→D between smooth projective integral curves is finite, and its degree is the degree of the induced extension of function fields. It is also the degree of the pullback of a rational point, with ramification multiplicities and residue degrees included: locally the finite coordinate algebra is a torsion-free module over the target discrete valuation ring, hence free of its generic rank. For D=P1 and a rational function defining f, the pullback of infinity is its pole divisor, so the total pole degree equals degf. A degree-one finite map to a normal curve is an isomorphism, since a finite integral algebra inside its unchanged function field equals the integrally closed target ring.

Algebraic quotient by a finite group (X/G=Spec(AG))

Words: 62
Morphism of algebraic varieties OurBigBook.com
For an affine variety with coordinate ring A acted on by a finite group G, the affine quotient is SpecAG. Elements of A satisfy monic orbit polynomials over AG, so the quotient map is a finite morphism. Over an algebraically closed field, invariant functions separate finite orbits; in characteristic not dividing ∣G∣, interpolate and average a function separating two orbits. The quotient fibres are consequently the orbits.

For a classical algebraic variety X and affine variety Y, a homomorphism on the indicated rings gives a morphism by evaluating the images of affine coordinate generators at each point of X. The relations defining Y remain zero. Local fractions pull back to local regular fractions, proving regularity; the coordinate ring generators also prove uniqueness.

Changing t multiplies it by a local unit in a ring and does not change that order. This is a valuation definition and remains meaningful for inseparable morphisms.

Fiber dimension theorem

Words: 302 Articles: 4

For a dominant morphism of irreducible varieties, generic fiber dimension is source dimension minus target dimension, and this dimension occurs over a nonempty open subset. In particular, constant fiber dimension d gives source dimension equal to target dimension plus d. For algebraic groups the equal-dimensional components and coset fibers allow the same dimension formula componentwise.

For an irreducible source, regard the morphism of algebraic varieties as dominant onto the closure of its image, with generic point η. The fiber dimension theorem gives the displayed formula. A proper morphism has closed image, so the closure can then be omitted.
Isolated fibre point forces dominance in equal dimensions
Words: 58

Let f:X→Y be a proper morphism of irreducible algebraic varieties with equal dimension. If some fibre has an isolated point, its corresponding irreducible component has dimension zero. Apply the fiber dimension theorem to X→f(X): 0≥dimX−dimf(X). Thus the closed image has dimension dimY and equals Y. The incidence proof that a cubic surface contains a line is an application; the entire witness fibre need not be finite.
Generic equidimensionality of fibres (dimXy​=dimX−dimY)
Words: 57
Fiber dimension theorem OurBigBook.com
For a dominant morphism of algebraic varieties between irreducible varieties, there is a nonempty Zariski-open subset of the target over which every fibre is nonempty and every irreducible component has the displayed dimension. Apply Noether normalization to affine source charts over the target function field and clear denominators on the target to get a generic upper bound. Combine it with the closed-fibre lower bound in the fiber dimension theorem.
Closed-fibre lower bound by finite normalization (dimXy​≥dimX−dimY)
Words: 96
Fiber dimension theorem OurBigBook.com
For a surjective morphism of irreducible varieties, choose an affine neighborhood of the target point and a finite Noether normalization to affine space of target dimension r. On an affine source neighborhood the composite fibre is cut out by r functions, so repeated principal hypersurface dimension lemma gives dimension at least source dimension minus r on every component. The normalization fibre is finite. The original fibre is therefore an open-and-closed union of components of that composite fibre, proving the bound without assuming target smoothness. For a reducible source the bound must instead be applied using the dimension of each source component.

Finite morphisms are proper

Words: 84
Finite morphism OurBigBook.com
A finite morphism is of finite type and separated: on affine charts the multiplication map B⊗A​B→B is surjective and represents its closed diagonal. Each element of a finite algebra satisfies a monic equation by the determinant trick. In a valuative lifting problem, its image in the fraction field is therefore integral over the valuation ring and belongs to that ring, since valuation rings are integrally closed. This gives a lift, unique because the valuation ring embeds in its fraction field. The valuative criterion for properness proves properness.
