Work done by a constant force (W=Fdcosθ)

 Home Physics Branches of physics Classical mechanics Energy Work
OurBigBook.com Words: 170
For a fixed force F and endpoint displacement Δr, the line integral reduces to a dot product:
W=F⋅Δr=Fdcosθ,F=∣F∣,d=∣Δr∣.
(104)
Here θ is the angle between the force and the displacement. For straight motion, d is also the distance traveled. A 3N force acting along a 4m displacement does 12J of work. Turning the same force perpendicular to the displacement gives cos(π/2)=0 and zero work; reversing it gives cosπ=−1 and −12J. At θ=π/3, the work is 6J. These signs describe energy transferred to or from the moving body. [ref]
