On the circle put ζ=aeiθ. The Joukowski map gives z=2acosθ, so its image is the slit S=[−2a,2a]. Away from that circle, the inverse equation is ζ2−zζ+a2=0. For z∈/S, neither root has modulus a, and their product is a2, so exactly one root has modulus greater than a. Selecting that root defines the exterior inverse.
More explicitly, define s(z)=z2−4a2​ to be the branch analytic on C∖S normalized by s(z)/z→1 at infinity. It is positive for real z>2a and negative for real z<−2a. Then
g(z)=2z+s(z)​.​
(67)
Existence and analyticity of this branch also follow from the exterior inverse: J′(ζ)=1−a2/ζ2 is nonzero for ∣ζ∣>a, so the selected simple root varies analytically; s=2g−z has the stated normalization. The exterior map is injective because J(ζ1​)=J(ζ2​) with distinct roots would imply ζ1​ζ2​=a2, impossible for two exterior points. Thus g is a conformal bijection throughout C∖S.
The proposed potential is a uniform stream plus its cylinder doublet and the circulation term. On ζ=aeiθ, the first two terms sum to the real quantity 2Uacos(θ−α), while the imaginary part of the logarithmic term is the constant −Γloga/(2π). Thus the cylinder is a streamline. At infinity the complex velocity tends to Ue−iα and the circulation is Γ, verifying both far-field conditions. Logarithms may be chosen locally; their derivative gives a single-valued velocity.
Composition therefore gives the plate potential
w(z)=U(g(z)e−iα+g(z)a2eiα​)−2πiΓ​logg(z).​
(68)
At the edge z=2a, corresponding to ζ=a, the physical complex velocity is W′(ζ)/J′(ζ). Since the denominator has a simple zero, finiteness requires
W′(a)=−2iUsinα−2πaiΓ​=0,
(69)
which is exactly
Γ+4πUasinα=0.​
(70)
With this cancellation, the limiting ratio is W′′(a)/J′′(a). The Kutta value of the circulation gives W′′(a)=2Ucosα/a and J′′(a)=2/a. Hence the finite edge velocity is
u−iv=Ucosα,or (u,v)=(Ucosα,0).​
(71)
