Choose a nonzero quadratic nonresidue c modulo q. By the Chinese remainder theorem, choose a positive integer k satisfying
k≡1(mod4),k≡1(modb),k≡c(modq).
(21)
The moduli are pairwise coprime, and k is coprime to qb. Jacobi reciprocity, with k≡1(mod4), gives
(kb​)=(bk​)=1,(kq​)=(qk​)=−1,(k−1​)=1.
(22)
Since t is odd, multiplying these symbols yields
(k±qtb​)=(−1)t=−1.​
(23)
When b=1 its congruence imposes no restriction and its Jacobi factor equals one.
