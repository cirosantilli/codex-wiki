Given Xn​=k, the next child takes a fresh uniform choice from 1,…,k, making Xn+1​ uniform on 0,…,k−1. At zero the count stays zero. This conditional law depends only on the current count, so Xn​ is a Markov chain.
The amounts taken do not generally retain that state information. For N≥3, compare two positive-probability histories with the same current value Y2​=1: if Y1​=N−2, exactly one sticker remains and Y3​=1; if Y1​=N−1, none remains and Y3​=0. Thus the next amount depends on earlier history even after conditioning on the current amount, violating the Markov property.
The non-Markov assertion needs this nondegenerate range of N. With N=1 the amount process is trivial; for N=2 it is also Markov if time-inhomogeneous transitions are permitted. The history counterexample establishes the intended failure for every N≥3.
