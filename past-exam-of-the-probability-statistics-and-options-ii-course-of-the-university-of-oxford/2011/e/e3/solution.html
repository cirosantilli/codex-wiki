A connected space has no separation into two disjoint nonempty open subsets whose union is the space. A continuous surjection to the discrete space {0,1} produces such a separation by the two point preimages. Conversely, from a separation define a function equal to zero on one open piece and one on the other; it is continuous and surjective. Thus these formulations of connectedness are equivalent.
An interval in R is connected: if a continuous map from it to {0,1} took both values, the intermediate value theorem would force value 1/2, impossible. Conversely, a subset of R that is not an interval has points a<b in it and a point c∈(a,b) outside it. Intersecting it with (−∞,c) and (c,∞) separates it. The empty set is connected, and singleton intervals are included.
For the union assertion, any separation of X must put each nonempty connected Ai​ entirely on one side, since intersecting the separation with Ai​ would otherwise disconnect it. Pairwise nonempty intersections force all the Ai​ onto the same side. Their union is X, contradicting a nonempty second side. The empty-union case is already connected.
The plane is the union of all lines through the origin. Each line is homeomorphic to R and connected, and every pair meets at the origin, so R2 is connected. For the punctured plane, let R+​ be the positive horizontal ray, and for each r>0 let Cr​ be the circle of radius r. Each circle is a continuous image of [0,2π] and hence connected; Cr​∪R+​ is connected because its two connected pieces meet at (r,0). These unions all contain R+​ and cover the punctured plane, proving it connected as well.
Finally suppose a continuous injection g:R2→R existed. Its image is a connected interval, since a continuous image of a connected space is connected. It contains at least three distinct values; choose p whose value lies strictly between two others. The punctured plane R2∖{p} is connected by translation of the preceding result. By injectivity its image is precisely g(R2)∖{g(p)}, which is disconnected by the missing interior point. This contradicts connectedness of a continuous image. Therefore
There is no continuous injection R2→R.​
