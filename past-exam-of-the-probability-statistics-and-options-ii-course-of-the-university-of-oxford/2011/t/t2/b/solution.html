Thus an extra unit of R is worth 3/4 while the current basis stays feasible. Increasing its supply by δ changes the basic values to
B−1(6+δ11​)=(3+δ/21−3δ/4​).

For increases, feasibility is retained exactly up to δ=4/3. Therefore the firm can buy up to 4/3 extra units at a marginal value of 3/4 per unit. The reduced profits do not change during this right-hand-side variation.
For the proposed fourth good, the reduced profit is 8−(4u+4v)=8−9=−1. Its profit is below the resource opportunity cost, so it is not worth producing at the current optimum. The old dual solution also satisfies the new good's inequality, certifying that introducing it cannot improve the optimum.
