Given a prime factorization n=p1​⋯pr​, repeatedly apply part (c) to the cyclic group of order n: it has a chain of subgroups of successive orders
n,n/p1​,n/(p1​p2​),…,1.
(8)
Read in increasing order, this is a composition series with factors isomorphic to the cyclic groups of orders p1​,…,pr​. Any second prime factorization of the same integer produces another composition series of the same group Cn​.
The Jordan–Hölder theorem equates the two multisets of simple factors. Since Cp​≅Cq​ for primes p,q implies p=q by equality of group orders, the two prime multisets agree. Hence
The prime factorization is unique up to permutation.​
(9)
For n=1 both factor lists are empty, corresponding to the trivial group.
