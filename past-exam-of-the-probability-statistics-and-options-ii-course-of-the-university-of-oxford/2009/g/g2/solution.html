At a regular constrained stationary curve, choose a constant multiplier λ and make the augmented functional I−λJ stationary under endpoint-fixed variations. Its integrand is H=F−λG and the equation is dHy′​/dx−Hy​=0. This normal multiplier formulation requires the first variation of the constraint not to vanish identically; the usual abnormal case would require two multipliers. When Hx​=0,
dxd​(y′Hy′​−H)=y′(dxd​Hy′​−Hy​)−Hx​=0,
(14)
so y′Hy′​−H=c0​​ is constant, proving the requested first integral.
For the chain, ds=1+(y′)2​dx, so the fixed physical length is exactly J[y]. Its gravitational potential, apart from the constant factor ρg, is I[y]=∫−aa​y1+(y′)2​dx. Thus H=(y−λ)1+(y′)2​, and the Euler–Lagrange equation is
dxd​(1+(y′)2​(y−λ)y′​)−1+(y′)2​=0.​
(15)
The first integral becomes (y−λ)/1+(y′)2​=c, after renaming the constant with the opposite sign. The differential equation also gives (y−λ)y′′=1+(y′)2, so the nondegenerate solution integrates to y=λ+ccosh((x−x0​)/c). Equal endpoint heights force x0​=0: the two cosh values agree only when their arguments are equal or opposite, and a>0 excludes the former. Hence
y(x)=λ+ccosh(x/c).​
(16)
For the hanging energy minimum take c>0. Since 1+(y′)2​=cosh(x/c),
L=2csinh(a/c),λ=b−ccosh(a/c).​
(17)
For L>2a the length equation has exactly one positive solution: its right side decreases from infinity to 2a as c increases from zero to infinity. For L=2a the only feasible shape is the straight segment, obtained as a limiting case; for L<2a no chain can span the endpoints. A negative c gives the inverted stationary catenary, rather than the physically hanging minimum.
