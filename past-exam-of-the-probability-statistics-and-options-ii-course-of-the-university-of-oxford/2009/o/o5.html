The multinomial likelihood is a parameter-independent factor times ∏i​πini​​. Maximize its logarithm subject to ∑πi​=1: the multiplier equations give ni​/πi​=c, and summation makes c=n. Thus π^i​=ni​/n. Positivity of all counts makes this the unique interior maximum by strict concavity.
The likelihood ratio compares the fitted null likelihood with this saturated likelihood. Taking minus twice its logarithm gives Λ(θ^)=2∑i​ni​log[ni​/(nπi​(θ^))]. Under regularity and large expected counts, compare it to χk−22​ for one fitted parameter: the saturated probabilities have k−1 free coordinates and the null has one. If the parameter is fixed at θ0​, use Λ(θ0​) and χk−12​. Reject for a value above the chosen upper quantile.
For the ungrouped logarithmic-series observations write n=1188 and S=∑r​Xr​=6968. The log likelihood is
ℓ(θ)=−nlog[−log(1−θ)]+Slogθ−∑r​logXr​,0<θ<1.
(42)
Setting its derivative to zero gives
(1−θ^full​)log(1−θ^full​)−θ^full​​=11886968​.​

Bisection of this monotone mean equation, or Newton's method with a valid bracket, obtains θ^full​≈0.9442280.
The supplied table instead uses ten grouped cells, with the last probability π≥10​(θ)=1−∑i=19​πi​(θ). Its likelihood is determined by the cell counts and is not the ungrouped likelihood just differentiated. From its listed values, the minimum deviance occurs near θ^grouped​≈0.93​; direct minimization of this grouped deviance gives about 0.9333714. The supplied two forms of the estimate therefore concern different likelihoods.
