For a finite upper barrier L>i, let hi(L)​ be the probability of hitting zero before L. It satisfies h0(L)​=1, hL(L)​=0, and hi(L)​=phi+1(L)​+qhi−1(L)​. For 0<q<p, solving the second-order recurrence with roots 1 and r=q/p gives
hi(L)​=1−rLri−rL​.
(38)
As L→∞, the events increase to eventual hitting of zero: any finite path reaching zero has a finite maximal height. Since r<1, this yields hi​=(q/p)i​, with h0​=1. In the endpoint case q=0, a positive initial state only moves upward and the hitting probability is zero, agreeing with the positive-state formula. The finite-barrier argument selects the correct solution rather than the also-harmonic constant one.
