For a closed Eulerian trail, every visit to a vertex pairs one entering edge with one departing edge, including the initial/final vertex. Thus every degree is even.
Conversely, in a finite connected graph with all degrees even, start at a vertex and follow unused edges until no extension is possible. The walk cannot stop away from its starting point: at a different endpoint an odd number of its incident edges would have been used, leaving at least one unused edge because its total degree is even. Thus it closes. If any edge remains unused, connectedness gives a vertex of the current closed trail incident with an unused edge. Repeat the construction from that vertex in the unused-edge subgraph and splice the resulting closed trail into the first one. Finitely many edges make the procedure terminate in a closed trail using every edge exactly once.
Hence a connected finite graph has an Eulerian circuit exactly when all degrees are even. If “trail” is allowed to be open, the printed equivalence needs correction: a connected graph has an Eulerian trail when it has zero or two odd-degree vertices. A single edge is an immediate counterexample to the unqualified printed statement.
