The moment generating function is MX​(t)=E(etX) wherever that expectation is finite. A useful continuity theorem says that if the moment generating functions Mn​ are finite on a common open interval containing zero and converge pointwise there to the moment generating function of a probability distribution, then the corresponding variables converge in distribution to that law. Equivalently one may assume a finite limiting function on such a neighborhood, with the usual continuity at zero, and obtain the uniquely determined limiting law.
For the gamma density, integrating the exponential against the density and changing variable to (λ−t)x gives
MX​(t)=(λ−tλ​)α,t<λ.​

For the waiting time to one head, independence of successive tosses gives Pr(Y=j)=p(1−p)j−1 for j≥1. Summing the geometric series gives
MY​(t)=1−(1−p)etpet​,(1−p)et<1.​
(51)
The waiting times between consecutive heads are independent copies of Y, so their sum N has moment generating function MY​(t)k. Consequently
M2pN​(t)=[1−(1−p)e2ptpe2pt​]k⟶(1−2t)−k.
(52)
For fixed t<1/2, numerator and denominator have leading terms p and p(1−2t) as p↓0. A fixed neighborhood of zero is inside the convergence domain for all sufficiently small p, so the continuity theorem applies. The limiting distribution is
Gamma(shape k, rate 1/2),​
(53)
equivalently a chi-squared distribution with 2k degrees of freedom.
