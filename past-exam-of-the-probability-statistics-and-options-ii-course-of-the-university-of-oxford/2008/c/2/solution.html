For existence of a primitive root one may apply the finite-field cyclicity criterion proved in the preceding section. There is also a direct counting proof: for each divisor d of p−1, either no elements have order d, or the preceding argument shows there are exactly ϕ(d). Lagrange's theorem partitions all p−1 nonzero residue classes by such orders, while ∑d∣p−1​ϕ(d)=p−1. Every count is at most its corresponding ϕ(d), so equality of the totals forces equality at every divisor. In particular there are ϕ(p−1)​ primitive roots.
