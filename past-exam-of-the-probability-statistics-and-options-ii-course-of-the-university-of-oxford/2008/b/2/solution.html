The characteristic is the smallest positive integer p with p1F​=0. Such an integer exists because the additive group is finite. If it factored as p=ab with 1<a,b<p, then (a1F​)(b1F​)=0 with both factors nonzero, contradicting the absence of zero divisors. Hence it is prime. The elements generated by 1F​ form a subfield isomorphic to Fp​. The finite set F is a finite-dimensional vector space of some dimension a≥1 over that subfield, so ∣F∣=pa​.
A nonzero polynomial of polynomial degree n over a field has at most n distinct roots. This follows by induction: a root r gives a factor X−r, and every different root must be a root of the remaining factor by cancellation. Thus Xn−1 has at most n roots. In the finite multiplicative group, the elements whose order divides n are exactly those satisfying xn=1. The supplied cyclicity criterion therefore proves that the multiplicative group is cyclic.
For the prime of the specified form write m=2rs with s odd. If s>1, then 22r+1 is a proper factor of (22r)s+1, impossible. Thus m is a power of two. Since 2m≡−1(modp), the order of two divides 2m but does not divide m. All divisors of the power of two 2m other than 2m itself divide m. Consequently
ordp​(2)=2m.​
