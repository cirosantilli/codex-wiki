As explained by admissible polynomials for a singular inverse-linear weight, there is a domain issue: the constant polynomial does not belong to the weighted space, because ∫01​x−1dx=∞. Hence the full space Πn−1​ is not a subspace of V, and the preceding inner-product projection theorem cannot be applied to it as stated.
Nevertheless a meaningful finite-error best approximation does exist after restricting to admissible polynomials. The target xn lies in V for every n≥1, since its squared integrand near zero is x2n−1. A polynomial p∈Πn−1​ has finite error exactly when p(0)=0: otherwise the residual tends to a nonzero constant and its weighted squared integral diverges; if p(0)=0, it has at least a simple zero and is integrable in the required norm. Thus the admissible approximation space is
Πn−1​∩V=span{x,x2,…,xn−1}.​
(69)
For n=1 it is {0}, giving best approximation zero. For n≥2 it is a finite-dimensional inner-product subspace with positive definite Gram matrix, so it has a unique orthogonal projection of xn. Therefore the full polynomial-space formulation is not defined inside V, but the finite-error interpretation has a unique best approximation. Declaring that no best approximation can exist under any interpretation would miss this admissible subspace.
