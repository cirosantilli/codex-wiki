In vacuum the source-free equations are
∇⋅E=∇⋅B=0,∇×E=−Bt​,∇×B=c−2Et​.
(37)
Adding the divergence equations with weights 1,ic gives the complex divergence equation. Adding the curl equations in the same way gives ∇×W=(i/c)Wt​. Conversely real and imaginary parts of these two complex equations recover all four real equations.
For the cosine ansatz, differentiation yields divergence (Ω⋅K)sin(ωt−K⋅x) and curl −(Ω×K)sin(ωt−K⋅x). The time derivative is −ωΩsin(ωt−K⋅x). Since that sine is not identically zero, the required conditions are precisely
Ω⋅K=0,Ω×K=(iω/c)Ω.​
(38)
Dotting the second equation with Ω gives Ω⋅Ω=0 because ω=0. This dot product is complex bilinear, not Hermitian. Taking the cross product again with K and using transversality gives −∣K∣2Ω=−ω2Ω/c2. Nonzero amplitude therefore requires ∣K∣2=ω2/c2​.
Taking real and imaginary parts yields the full conditions
e⋅K=b⋅K=0,K×e=ωb,b×K=(ω/c2)e.​
(39)
They imply e⋅b=0 and ∣e∣=c∣b∣, together with the dispersion relation. Conversely a nonzero transverse e, this dispersion relation and b=K×e/ω satisfy all the conditions.
The physical fields are E=ecos(ωt−K⋅x) and B=bcos(ωt−K⋅x). Each oscillates along one fixed real direction, so both fields are linearly polarized. Complex encoding of the two fields here does not mean either real field is circularly polarized.
