The conformal map ζ=z3 sends the wedge to the upper half-plane. A fluid vortex at c3 there has an opposite image at cˉ3, so the pulled-back potential is
w(z)=2πiΓ​(−log(z3−c3)+log(z3−cˉ3)).
(36)
The two boundary rays map to the real axis, where the imaginary part is constant, giving the required impermeability. Factoring z3−c3=(z−c)(z2+cz+c2) gives the printed form up to branch constants.
The fluid vortex moves with the regular part of the complex velocity, excluding its own singular term. Since w′=u−iv, its motion is the conjugated derivative. Differentiating the remaining terms and setting z=c gives
dtdcˉ​=2πiΓ​(−c1​+c3−cˉ33c2​).​
(37)
The overbar on the time derivative is essential.
Write c=reiθ. Using c3−cˉ3=2ir3sin3θ and multiplying the velocity equation by eiθ gives
r˙−irθ˙=4πrΓ​(3cot3θ+i).
(38)
Thus r˙=3Γcot3θ/(4πr) and θ˙=−Γ/(4πr2). Direct differentiation now yields
dtd​(rsin3θ)=r˙sin3θ+3rθ˙cos3θ=0.
(39)
Hence rsin(3θ)=constant​ along the trajectory while it stays inside the wedge.
