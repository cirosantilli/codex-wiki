Assume the intended nondegenerate interval a<b and real-valued continuous functions. The integral is bilinear and symmetric. Since w>0, it gives ⟨f,f⟩≥0. If a continuous f is nonzero at some point, continuity makes f2w strictly positive on a subinterval of positive length, so its integral is positive. Hence ⟨f,f⟩=0 forces f=0, proving positive definiteness. Orthogonality means ∫ab​f(x)g(x)w(x)dx=0​. If a=b, the displayed integral is identically zero and is not an inner product on the stated function space; that degenerate case must be excluded.
