For a rejection function φ(x)∈[0,1], its power is β(θ)=Eθ​φ(X). A test has level at most α if supθ∈H0​​β(θ)≤α; its size is that supremum. In a simple null the significance probability is just the rejection probability under that null.
For two simple hypotheses with joint sample densities f0​,f1​, the Neyman–Pearson lemma states that a test rejecting where f1​>kf0​, accepting where f1​<kf0​, and randomizing if necessary on equality to have size α, is most powerful among level-α tests. Let φ∗​ be that test and φ any competitor. Pointwise,
(φ∗​−φ)(f1​−kf0​)≥0.
(58)
Integration gives
∫(φ∗​−φ)f1​≥k∫(φ∗​−φ)f0​≥0,
(59)
since k≥0, the first test has size α, and the other has size at most α. This proves the claimed power bound, allowing null-density-zero regions as well. Equality implies agreement away from the likelihood-ratio boundary, up to null sets, when the inequalities are strict.
