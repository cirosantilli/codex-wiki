L(λ0​)L(λ)​=(λ0​λ​)nexp((λ0​−λ)∑i​xi​),

strictly increasing in S=∑i​xi​. The Neyman–Pearson critical region is therefore an upper tail in S, and its null threshold depends only on the desired size, not on which λ<λ0​ was chosen. Thus the same test is uniformly most powerful for the entire one-sided alternative.
Under the null S∼Gamma(n,λ0​) with the second parameter interpreted as rate. Its continuous distribution permits the exact threshold
cα​=q(1−α;n,λ0​).​
(61)
Under any fixed alternative its rejection probability is Q(λ)=1−F(cα​;n,λ)​. Applying the lemma separately to each such λ shows that every level-α test has power at most this value there. The constructed test attains the bound for all λ<λ0​ simultaneously.
