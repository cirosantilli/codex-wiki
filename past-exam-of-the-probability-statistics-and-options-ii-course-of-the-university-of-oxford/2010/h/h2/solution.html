By reflection symmetry, G is the midpoint of the cylinder axis. With position r measured from G, define JijG​=∫(∣r∣2δij​−ri​rj​)dm. Symmetry makes the off-diagonal entries zero. Uniform density gives ⟨x2⟩=⟨y2⟩=a2/4 and ⟨z2⟩=b2/3, by integrating over a disk and the interval [−b,b]. Hence
JG=Mdiag(a2/4+b2/3, a2/4+b2/3, a2/2).​
(26)
For parallel axes based at P, the tensor form of the parallel-axis theorem is JP=JG+M(∣d∣2I−ddT), where d=PG. This follows by substituting r+d in the defining integral and using ∫rdm=0. Here d is an axial vector of length b, so
A=M(a2/4+4b2/3),C=Ma2/2,JP=diag(A,A,C).​
(27)
The Euler angles ψ,ϕ are cyclic coordinates. Their conserved conjugate momenta are pψ​=C(ψ˙​+ϕ˙​cosθ)=Cn and pϕ​=Aϕ˙​sin2θ+Cncosθ=h. The Lagrangian has no explicit time dependence, so the conserved energy is kinetic plus gravitational potential, namely the expression given with constant E.
Eliminate the angular velocities using ϕ˙​=(h−Cncosθ)/(Asin2θ) and ψ˙​+ϕ˙​cosθ=n. The energy equation becomes
Aθ˙2=2E−Cn2+2Mgbcosθ−Asin2θ(h−Cncosθ)2​.
(28)
Thus
F(θ)=Asin2θ(2E−Cn2+2Mgbcosθ)−(h−Cncosθ)2.​
(29)
The elimination is valid away from the coordinate poles; the multiplied equation extends by continuity to any pole reached by a regular motion.
