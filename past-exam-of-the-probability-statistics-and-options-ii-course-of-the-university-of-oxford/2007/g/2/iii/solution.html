The functional is unbounded above. Let ℓ be the positive affine interpolant of the endpoint values and choose a nonnegative, nonzero, continuously differentiable h vanishing at both endpoints. The admissible functions yM​=ℓ+Mh satisfy
J[yM​]≥2π∫x1​x2​​yM​dx⟶∞.

This excludes a global maximum, but does not prove that a stationary curve is a minimum. The concluding assertion is false without additional conditions.
An explicit counterexample uses x1​=−2, x2​=2, y1​=y2​=cosh2 and the stationary function y=coshx. Its second variation, for a perturbation h vanishing at the endpoints, is
2πδ2J[h]​=∫−22​(2tanhxhh′+sech2xh′2)dx=∫−22​sech2x(h′2−h2)dx.
(28)
Choose h=1−x2/4. Since sech2x≤4e−2∣x∣, the positive contribution is at most ∫R​x2e−2∣x∣dx=1/2. On [−1,1], h2≥9/16, so the magnitude of the negative contribution is at least (9/8)tanh1>9/16>1/2. Thus δ2J[h]<0. Small perturbations in this direction preserve positivity and the endpoint data while decreasing surface area. Consequently this catenoid is not a local minimum. The valid conclusion from part (ii) is a necessary stationary form; minimization requires a separate stability and existence analysis.
