In vacuum, the source-free Maxwell equations are
∇⋅E=∇⋅B=0,∇×E=−∂t​B,∇×B=c−2∂t​E.
(34)
Put ψ=ω(t−z/c) and denote the constant electric amplitude by E0​. A corresponding traveling-wave magnetic field is
B=cE0​​(−sinψ,cosψ,0).​
(35)
Both divergences vanish because both the electric field and the magnetic field depend only on z,t and have zero z component. Direct differentiation gives ∇×E=(ωE0​/c)(cosψ,sinψ,0)=−∂t​B and ∇×B=(ωE0​/c2)(−sinψ,cosψ,0)=c−2∂t​E. A time-independent background magnetic field satisfying the homogeneous equations could be added; the formula is the wave component.
Introduce the magnetic vector potential and electric potential by B=∇×A and E=−∇ϕ−∂t​A. These identities automatically give the divergence equation for B and Faraday's law. For this wave choose ϕ=0 and A=(c/ω)B; the derivatives just calculated verify both potential equations.
The electromagnetic force is the Lorentz force, Q(E+v×B). On the smooth constraint plane z=z0​, the constraint force is normal to the plane. Both v and B are tangential, so v×B is normal and is balanced by that constraint force; it does not affect the tangential motion. Thus
r¨∥​=mQE0​​(cosψ,sinψ).
(36)
A particular motion is r∥​=r0​−[QE0​/(mω2)](cosψ,sinψ), a circle of radius
mω2∣QE0​∣​.​
(37)
The general planar motion also includes a constant drift velocity; the particle moves on a circle when that drift is zero. The constraint force can do no work because it is perpendicular to the velocity.
