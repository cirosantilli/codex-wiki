The state-zero stationary distribution equation is π0​=qπ0​+qπ1​, so π1​=(p/q)π0​. The state-one equation is π1​=pπ0​+qπ2​, giving π2​=(p/q)π1​. More generally, the stationary probability flow across the cut {0,…,i} is balanced, so pπi​=qπi+1​. This also follows inductively from πi​=pπi−1​+qπi+1​.
Hence πi​=(p/q)iπ0​. Since p<q, the geometric series is summable, and normalization gives
πi​=(1−qp​)(qp​)i,i≥0.​
(51)
These probabilities satisfy detailed balance and therefore all the stationarity equations. The Markov chain is an irreducible Markov chain, so this normalized stationary distribution is unique.
