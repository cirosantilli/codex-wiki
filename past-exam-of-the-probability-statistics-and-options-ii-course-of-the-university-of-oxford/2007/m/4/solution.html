The independent identically distributed central limit theorem states that for independent identically distributed random variables Uk​ with finite mean μ and positive finite variance σ2,
σn​∑k=1n​Uk​−nμ​ d​ N(0,1).

For 0<u<1, continuity, strict monotonicity and the endpoint limits of the cumulative distribution function give a unique x=F−1(u). Then P(F(X)≤u)=P(X≤x)=F(x)=u. Thus Y=F(X) has the uniform distribution on (0,1), by the probability integral transform.
For W=−log(1−Y), P(W≤w)=1−e−w when w≥0, and it is zero otherwise. Therefore its probability density function is e−wχ(0,∞)​(w): the exponential distribution of rate one. By integration by parts, E[W]=1 and E[W2]=2, so Var(W)=1.
The variables Wk​=−log(1−Fk​(Xk​)) are independent because they are separate measurable functions of independent random variables. The probability integral transform makes all of them identically distributed as Exp(1), even though the Xk​ need not be identically distributed. The displayed Zn​ is exactly n−1/2∑k=1n​(Wk​−1). The central limit theorem therefore gives
Zn​d​N(0,1).​
