The claimed endpoint bounds with radius ϵ are false for positive corner perturbations. For example, take n=3, ϵ=1/1000 and δ=9/10<1−ϵ. The characteristic polynomial is
P(λ)=(λ−1)(λ−2)(λ−3)−40001​(2λ−4)−1609​.
(68)
Throughout ∣λ−1∣≤1/1000, the absolute value of the sum of its first two terms is below 0.003, whereas 9/160=0.05625. Hence this interval contains no root, contradicting the first requested bound. The actual eigenvalues are approximately 1.029144,1.943599,3.027257, which also violate the last requested bound.
There is a valid real-eigenvalue conclusion with wider endpoint bounds. For 0<ϵ<1/4 and δ<(n−1)/2−ϵ, the Gershgorin discs from rows have one enlarged component on the left; the Gershgorin discs from columns have one enlarged component on the right. Each has an enlarged radius r=δ+ϵ<(n−1)/2. A small disc that meets the enlarged left disc has index j satisfying j−1≤r+q<n/2. A small disc meeting the enlarged right disc satisfies n−j<n/2. Thus, when n=2m, every disc with index at most m is isolated in the column family and every disc with index at least m+1 is isolated in the row family. Their counts give all n eigenvalues, one in each disjoint small disc, so all are real.
When n=2m+1, the same argument isolates the first m discs in the column family and the last m in the row family. It accounts for 2m real eigenvalues. The one remaining eigenvalue is real by conjugation, and belongs to the central small disc: the enlarged left and right discs are disjoint, and their two components can intersect only within the disc centered at m+1. Therefore the valid uniform bounds are
∣λ1​−1∣≤41​,∣λi​−i∣≤41​+ϵ (2≤i<n),∣λn​−n∣≤41​.​
(69)
The sharper endpoint radii ϵ hold in part (d) when δ=0, and cannot simply be carried over to the perturbed matrix.
