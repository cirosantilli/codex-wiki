Up to a parameter-independent multinomial factor, the likelihood function is L=∏i,j​θijnij​​. A Lagrange multiplier for ∑θij​=1 gives nij​/θij​=λ, whence λ=n and
θij​=nij​/n.​
(52)
Since all counts are positive, the log-likelihood function is strictly concave on the positive simplex and tends to minus infinity at its boundary, proving this is the unique maximum.
Under independence of random variables, θij​=ϕi​ρj​, so the log-likelihood function separates as ∑i​ni⋅​logϕi​+∑j​n⋅j​logρj​. Applying the same constrained maximization twice gives ϕ​i​=ni⋅​/n, ρ​j​=n⋅j​/n, and
θij0​=ni⋅​n⋅j​/n2.​
(53)
Twice the difference between the unrestricted and null maximized log-likelihood functions is
2logsupH0​​LsupL​=2∑i,j​nij​logni⋅​n⋅j​nij​n​,
(54)
the required likelihood-ratio test statistic. Under the null hypothesis, its asymptotic chi-squared distribution has (r−1)(c−1) statistical degrees of freedom, the parameter-count difference (rc−1)−(r+c−2). Here that is four. Since 25.192>q4​=18.467, reject independence at the 0.1 percent significance level. For four statistical degrees of freedom, the asymptotic upper-tail probability is e−25.192/2(1+25.192/2)≈4.60×10−5. This establishes an association in these data; it is not a causal conclusion.
