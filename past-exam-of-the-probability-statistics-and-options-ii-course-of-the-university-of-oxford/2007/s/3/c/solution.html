Initialize an n×n array d with dii​=0 and dij​=aij​ for i=j. For k=1,…,n, replace every entry in place by
dij​←min{dij​,dik​+dkj​}.
(76)
This is the Floyd-Warshall algorithm. At the start of stage k, the induction hypothesis is dij​=uij(k−1)​. During that stage, row k and column k do not change: dkk​=0 and no negative cycle can lower it. Hence the operands dik​,dkj​ remain the stage-start values even with in-place updates. Part (b) proves that after the stage every entry is uij(k)​. Induction gives all shortest path lengths after stage n. The algorithm uses n2 stored lengths and O(n3) arithmetic operations, with only a fixed number of loop counters beyond the array.
