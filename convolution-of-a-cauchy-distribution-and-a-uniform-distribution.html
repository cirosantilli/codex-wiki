Convolution of a Cauchy distribution and a uniform distribution

 ... Area of mathematics Probability and statistics Probability theory Probability distribution Joint probability distribution Convolution of independent random variables
OurBigBook.com Words: 106
Let X have the Standard Cauchy distribution and let Y, independently, have the uniform distribution on [−h,h], where h>0. The convolution of probability densities gives the probability density of Z=X+Y as
ph​(z)=2hπ1​∫−hh​1+(z−y)2dy​=2hπarctan(z+h)−arctan(z−h)​.
(10)
This is an even function. Its derivative is
ph′​(z)=π[1+(z+h)2][1+(z−h)2]−2z​,
(11)
so its unique mode is zero. Its tails satisfy ph​(z)∼1/(πz2) as ∣z∣→∞: multiplying the integral by z2 makes the integrand converge uniformly to 1 on the bounded interval [−h,h]. Consequently both positive and negative parts of the first moment diverge, so its expected value does not exist. The symmetric Cauchy principal value of that moment is zero, which is a different assertion. Adding a bounded independent displacement smooths the probability density without removing the divergent first moment of the Cauchy distribution.
