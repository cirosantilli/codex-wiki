Point interaction in a second-order differential equation (Lα​y=y′′+αy(c)δc​)

 ... Area of mathematics Analysis Differential equation Ordinary differential equation Linear ordinary differential equation Second-order linear differential equation
OurBigBook.com Words: 317 Articles: 2
A point interaction concentrates a coefficient of a linear differential equation at a single interior point. On an interval 0<x<ℓ, with 0<c<ℓ, consider Lα​y=y′′+αy(c)δc​. The domain consists of continuous functions that are twice differentiable away from c, with the required one-sided derivatives. Multiplication of the Dirac delta by y means the unambiguous point value y(c)δc​. It does not mean an undefined product of a delta with an arbitrary discontinuous function.
The equation is interpreted using distributional derivatives. Smooth forcing gives an ordinary equation on each side, with transmission conditions for a delta coefficient at the interaction. A delta potential in the one-dimensional Schrödinger equation is a physical example after choosing the corresponding sign and constants.
