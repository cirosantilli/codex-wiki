Start with a fundamental solution of the second derivative operator:
g​(x,ξ)=(x−ξ)H(x−ξ),∂x​g​=H(x−ξ),∂x2​g​=δ(x−ξ).
(3)
Here H is the Heaviside step function and the last equality holds in the sense of distributions. The factor x−ξ removes a jump in the function itself, while its first derivative has a unit jump. Adding any homogeneous solution preserves the Dirac delta source.
Choose y1​(x)=x−2 and y2​(x)=x−3. They are independent homogeneous solutions, with B1​y1​=0, B2​y2​=0, B1​y2​=−1, and B2​y1​=1. Since 1<ξ<2, the fundamental solution has B1​g​=1 and B2​g​=2−ξ. Imposing the two boundary conditions on the proposed combination therefore gives −A2​+1=0 and A1​+2−ξ=0. Consequently
A1​(ξ)=ξ−2,A2​(ξ)=1,g(x,ξ)=(ξ−2)(x−2)+(x−3)+(x−ξ)H(x−ξ).​
(4)
Equivalently,
g(x,ξ)={(ξ−1)x+1−2ξ,ξx+1−3ξ,​1≤x≤ξ,ξ≤x≤2.​
(5)
At x=ξ both expressions equal ξ2−3ξ+1 and the right slope minus the left slope is ξ−(ξ−1)=1. At the endpoints, g(1,ξ)=−ξ and gx​(2,ξ)=ξ, while gx​(1,ξ)=ξ−1 and g(2,ξ)=1−ξ. These explicitly verify both coupled boundary conditions. The homogeneous equation has only affine solutions; its two boundary conditions give 2a+b=3a+b=0, hence a=b=0, proving uniqueness of the Green function.
The reciprocity defect is g(x,ξ)−g(ξ,x)=2(x−ξ). It records how the coupled boundary conditions affect self-adjointness, even though the differential expression D2 is formally self-adjoint. The construction is an instance of the boundary-matrix construction of an ordinary differential Green function.
