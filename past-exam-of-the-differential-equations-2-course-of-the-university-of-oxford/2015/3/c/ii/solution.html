Choose the real leading eigenfunction y1​=Csinx, keeping its amplitude C=0 fixed. At first order in the regular perturbation expansion, the equation becomes
y11′′​+y11​=−λ11​y1​−y12​,y11​(0)=y11​(π)=0.
(17)
The operator L=D2+1 with these homogeneous Dirichlet boundary conditions is self-adjoint, as allowed in the question, and its null space is spanned by y1​. The Fredholm solvability condition requires the forcing to be orthogonal to that null space:
0=∫0π​y1​(−λ11​y1​−y12​)dx.
(18)
The same condition follows directly by integrating y1​Ly11​ by parts: the endpoint term [y1​y11′​−y1′​y11​]0π​ vanishes, and Ly1​=0. Since a nonzero real y1​ has a positive squared integral,
λ11​=−∫0π​y1​(x)2dx∫0π​y1​(x)3dx​.​
(19)
This is the requested expression and is an instance of the leading nonlinear eigenvalue shift in a Sturm-Liouville problem. In the amplitude convention above, ∫0π​sin2xdx=π/2 and ∫0π​sin3xdx=4/3, so λ11​=−8C/(3π). For the particular choice C=1, it is −8/(3π).
The amplitude dependence of a nonlinear eigenvalue correction follows here from the quadratic term: rescaling the eigenfunction changes the eigenvalue shift. There is no single numerical correction independent of the chosen leading amplitude. Once the solvability condition is met, y11​ is determined up to addition of a multiple of y1​; a normalization such as ∫0π​y1​y11​dx=0 can fix that remaining freedom.
