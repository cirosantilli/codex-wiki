Write the operator in divergence form:
Ly=((1−x2)y′)′.
(22)
The Legendre differential equation therefore gives
LPn​=−n(n+1)Pn​,n=0,1,2,…​.
(23)
These are the eigenvalues of L. With the common positive-sign convention for a Sturm-Liouville problem, use −L instead:
−(pPn′​)′=λn​wPn​,p=1−x2,q=0,w=1,λn​=n(n+1)​.
(24)
This is a singular Sturm-Liouville problem, because p vanishes at the endpoints. Bounded eigenfunctions select the nonlogarithmic branches and satisfy pPn′​→0 there. The Lagrange identity for a Sturm-Liouville operator gives
[m(m+1)−n(n+1)]∫−11​Pm​Pn​dx=[(1−x2)(Pm​Pn′​−Pn​Pm′​)]−11​=0.
(25)
The eigenvalues are distinct, proving the orthogonality condition
∫−11​Pm​(x)Pn​(x)dx=0(m=n)​.
(26)
The weight is one. No choice of normalization of the Legendre polynomials is needed for this conclusion.
