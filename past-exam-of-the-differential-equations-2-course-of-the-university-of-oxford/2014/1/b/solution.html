The Bendixson-Dulac theorem says that a continuously differentiable planar vector field has no nonconstant periodic orbit in a simply connected region if a continuously differentiable Dulac function makes its rescaled divergence have one sign and not vanish identically on any open subset of that region. Isolated zeros of the divergence are allowed in this form.
Take the Dulac function to be one. For the exact vector field (P,Q),
∂x∂P​+∂y∂Q​=3x2+7y2+12x2y2.
(7)
This is positive except at the origin. The plane is simply connected, so there are no nonconstant periodic solutions. Equivalently, a hypothetical closed trajectory would have zero outward flux of its tangent vector field, while Green's theorem would make that flux the strictly positive area integral of the divergence.
The origin is the only equilibrium point: Q=0 forces y=0, and then P=x3 forces x=0. Its Jacobian matrix is zero, so the linearized equations are x˙=y˙​=0. They give no stability classification.
Keeping cubic terms gives the homogeneous approximation
x˙=x(x2+y2),y˙​=2y3.
(8)
In a sector where xy=0, use y as the independent variable and put w=x−2. Dividing the two equations gives
dydx​=2y3x(x2+y2)​,dydw​+yw​=−y31​.
(9)
The integrating factor y gives (yw)′=−y−2, hence yw=1/y+C0​. Rearranging and renaming the arbitrary constant yields the implicit trajectories
y1​−x2y​=C,equivalentlyx2−y2=Cx2y.​
(10)
This is the first integral of the cubic approximation, not an exact first integral of the higher-degree original system. The invariant axes, excluded by the divisions, must be added: on y=0, x˙=x3; on x=0, y˙​=2y3. The diagonal trajectories y=±x correspond to C=0. For fixed finite C, non-axis trajectories approaching the origin satisfy y2/x2=1−Cy→1, so their limiting tangent lines are the diagonals. This is the cubic planar flow with diagonal approach directions.
Every nonzero trajectory moves outward locally. For V=x2+y2, the exact system gives
V˙=2x4+2x2y2+4y4+2x4y2+6x2y4≥V2>0.
(11)
This implies departure from any fixed small circle for every nonzero starting point inside it: while a trajectory stayed inside forever, dtd​(1/V)≤−1 would eventually force 1/V negative. Thus the origin is unstable and repelling for both the cubic approximation and the full system. The argument establishes nonlinear instability despite the zero linearization.
