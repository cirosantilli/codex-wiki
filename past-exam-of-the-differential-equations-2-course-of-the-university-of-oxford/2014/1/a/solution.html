At an equilibrium point, x(1−x−y)=0 and 2−2x−y=0. The first equation gives x=0 or y=1−x. Substituting in the second gives, respectively,
(x,y)=(0,2)or(1,0).​
(1)
These are all the equilibrium points. The Jacobian matrix of the vector field is
J(x,y)=(1−2x−y−2​−x−1​).
(2)
At (0,2),
J(0,2)=(−1−2​0−1​).
(3)
The eigenvalue −1 has algebraic multiplicity two and eigenspace span{(0,1)T}. For the linearized coordinates u=x, v=y−2,
u(t)=Ae−t,v(t)=(B−2At)e−t.
(4)
Both components tend to zero. The repeated eigenvalue with one independent eigenvector makes this a stable improper degenerate node, a sink equilibrium. The term te−t is the signature of the nontrivial Jordan block. Since it is a hyperbolic equilibrium point, the linearization stability theorem gives local asymptotic stability of the nonlinear system. Approaching trajectories are tangent to the vertical eigendirection.
At (1,0),
J(1,0)=(−1−2​−1−1​),λ+​=−1+2​,v+​=(1−2​​),λ−​=−1−2​,v−​=(12​​).
(5)
One eigenvalue is positive and one negative, so this point is a saddle equilibrium. Its stable manifold is tangent to y=2​(x−1), and its unstable manifold is tangent to y=−2​(x−1).
For the phase portrait, the nullclines are
x˙=0:x=0 or y=1−x,y˙​=0:y=2−2x.
(6)
Above the second line the vertical flow is downward, and below it upward. For x>0, horizontal flow is to the right below y=1−x and to the left above it; for x<0 the directions reverse. The line x=0 is invariant, with y˙​=2−y, so it flows toward (0,2). Together with the saddle's two invariant directions, these signs determine the sketch.
