Laughlin wavefunction (Ψm​=N∏i<j​(zi​−zj​)me−∑i​∣zi​∣2/(4ℓB2​))

Robert Laughlin's 1983 variational method uses pairwise zeros to suppress close electron encounters within the lowest Landau level. For aligned spin, odd m gives fermionic exchange symmetry.[ref]
Here N enforces wavefunction normalization, ℓB​ is the magnetic length, and complex coordinates zj​=xj​−iyj​ describe electrons in a magnetic field Bz, B>0, with the symmetric gauge. Reversing the orientation conjugates the polynomial without changing probability densities. The Gaussian function confines the wavefunction.
The exchange check is especially simple for two electrons. Writing w=z1​−z2​, exchange sends w↦−w, so wm↦(−1)mwm. An aligned two-electron spin state is symmetric, requiring the spatial wavefunction to change sign. Hence m=1,3,5,… works, whereas m=2 would give the wrong sign. At a close encounter, the probability density vanishes as ∣w∣2m: m=3 imposes a sixth-order zero, stronger than the quadratic zero at m=1. This is the local geometric mechanism for avoiding a large repulsive Coulomb potential energy.
A useful large-N counting outline follows directly from the polynomial. Each complex coordinate has maximum degree m(N−1). A lowest Landau level orbital proportional to zke−∣z∣2/(4ℓB2​) has its areal probability density maximum at r2=2kℓB2​, by differentiating its logarithm. This suggests droplet area A≃2πmNℓB2​, neglecting the boundary correction. Dividing N/A by the degeneracy of a Landau level per area, 1/(2πℓB2​), gives filling factor ν≃1/m. This counting is a motivation for the fraction, not an independent proof that the wavefunction minimizes the interacting Hamiltonian.
