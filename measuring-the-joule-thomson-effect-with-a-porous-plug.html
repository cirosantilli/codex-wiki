Measuring the Joule-Thomson effect with a porous plug

 ... Physics Branches of physics Statistical physics Thermodynamics Enthalpy Joule-Thomson effect
OurBigBook.com Words: 201
The 2024 operating instructions for PHYWE's 04361-00 apparatus describe a frit between two gas chambers. A copper-coil heat exchanger first restores inlet temperature after cylinder-regulator cooling. An upstream manometer reads gauge pressure; the outlet stays at atmospheric pressure. Two NiCr-Ni thermocouples sit 2–5 mm from the frit. The digital meter's difference mode reports 0.01K precision.[ref]
The manual specifies at most 1bar gauge pressure, one hour of apparatus acclimatization, half an hour of meter warm-up, about 1.5 minutes of flow settling, then 10–15 seconds of averaging. It identifies a stagnant-gas zeroing error that shifts the fitted intercept and notes a maximum nitrogen signal of about 0.3K in this setup.[ref]
Define x=p1​−p2​>0 and y=T1​−T2​, so a small-drop fit is y≃μJT​x+c. A constant sensor offset c leaves the slope unchanged; pressure-dependent drift or heat leakage can alter it. The observable is the differential temperature slope across the plug, not cylinder-outlet cooling. Display precision alone does not establish accuracy or prove an isenthalpic process. This low-pressure, room-temperature apparatus does not map a complete Joule-Thomson inversion curve.
