Semiconductor mass-action law (np=ni2​)

 Home Physics Semiconductor Intrinsic semiconductor Intrinsic carrier concentration
OurBigBook.com Words: 134 Articles: 2
In thermal equilibrium, nondegenerate conduction electrons and valence electron holes share one chemical potential. Multiplying n=Nc​e−(Ec​−μ)/(kB​T) by p=Nv​e−(μ−Ev​)/(kB​T) cancels that chemical potential:
np=Nc​Nv​e−Eg​/(kB​T)=ni2​.
(6)
This relation remains valid with semiconductor doping under the stated assumptions. Equality of the two individual densities, n=p=ni​, additionally requires the intrinsic neutral case. Separate electron and hole quasi-Fermi levels in nonequilibrium need not obey this equilibrium product.
With one parabolic valley and two spin states at each band edge, the prefactor multiplying (kB​T)3 in the product is 4(mc​mh​)3/2/(2πℏ2)3. Both populations must be nondegenerate; the Pauli reduction of the pinned-edge mass-action product quantifies a failure of the classical approximation.
