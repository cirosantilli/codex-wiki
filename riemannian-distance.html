Riemannian distance (dg​)

 ... Mathematics Area of mathematics Geometry and topology Differential geometry Riemannian geometry Arc length
OurBigBook.com Words: 893 Articles: 9
The distance on a connected Riemannian manifold is the infimum of the arc lengths of piecewise smooth curves joining the endpoints:
dg​(p,q)=infγ:p⇝q​Lg​(γ).
(8)
It answers “how far must I travel while staying in this geometry?” The Riemannian metric measures an instantaneous tangent vector, whereas Riemannian distance is the global result of comparing complete routes.
In Euclidean space the straight segment achieves this infimum, giving d(p,q)=∥q−p∥. On a round sphere it gives great-circle distance, Rarccos(u⋅v) for positions Ru,Rv with unit vectors u,v. For R=1 and a central angle π/3, the surface distance is π/3, while the chord length is 1. On a circular cylinder, routes with different winding lead to the minimum in Riemannian distance on a circular cylinder.
The word infimum matters: a shortest route need not exist. In the Euclidean plane with the origin removed, the points (−1,0) and (1,0) still have Riemannian distance 2, approached by paths detouring around an arbitrarily small circle. Every allowed route has length strictly greater than 2, since the only Euclidean straight segment passes through the missing point. The Hopf-Rinow theorem guarantees a minimizing geodesic between any two points when a connected Riemannian manifold is complete.
