Gaussian sum truncation bound (RN​(b)=2∑n=N+1∞​e−πbn2)

 ... Mathematics Area of mathematics Analysis Fourier analysis Poisson summation formula Gaussian Poisson summation identity
OurBigBook.com Words: 181
For b>0 and a nonnegative integer N, define the symmetric partial sum and its positive remainder by
SN​(b)=1+2∑n=1N​e−πbn2,RN​(b)=S(b)−SN​(b).
(17)
Successive omitted terms have ratio e−πb(2n+1)≤e−πb(2N+3) for n≥N+1. Bounding them by a geometric series gives
0<RN​(b)≤BN​(b):=1−e−πb(2N+3)2e−πb(N+1)2​.
(18)
Consequently the physical approximation to S(a) has error at most BN​(a), while the dual approximation a−1/2SN​(1/a) has error at most a−1/2BN​(1/a). The prefactor must be included when setting an absolute error tolerance.
For a truncation error below 10−12, at a=0.04 the physical side first passes this bound with N=15: 31 lattice points give B15​(0.04)<2.172×10−14, whereas N=14 gives B14​(0.04)>10−12. The dual side needs only k=0. At a=25 the physical side needs only n=0, whereas the dual side first passes the bound with N=14: 29 lattice points give 25−1/2B14​(0.04)<2.146×10−13. These counts measure symmetric lattice terms; using the even function symmetry reduces the distinct exponential function evaluations to N+1. Truncation bounds do not include rounding error in a numerical implementation.
