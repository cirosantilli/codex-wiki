Observed radiative flux (F=L/(4πd2))

 ... Physics Branches of physics Astrophysics Radiative transfer Radiation-field moment Radiative flux
OurBigBook.com Words: 254 Articles: 2
For a steady point-like astronomical source with isotropy of emission, in transparent space with negligible relativistic or cosmological effects, conservation of energy spreads its luminosity over a sphere of surface area 4πd2. Its bolometric observed radiative flux is
F=4πd2L​,L=4πd2F.
(44)
This inverse-square law reflects geometric dilution: the same emitted power crosses increasingly large spheres. It does not require light to lose energy on the journey. For a fixed luminosity, doubling Euclidean distance quarters the observed radiative flux. [ref].
With L0​=1026W and d0​=20 parsecs, the result is F0​≃2.09×10−11Wm−2. At 40 parsecs, the same source gives F0​/4≃5.22×10−12Wm−2. An ideal collecting area of 1m2 at 20 parsecs intercepts approximately 2.09×10−11W, before detector losses.
