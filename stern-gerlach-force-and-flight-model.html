Stern-Gerlach force and flight model (Fz​≃μz​∂z​Bz​,zs​−z0​=μz​Gℓ(D+ℓ/2)/(Mv2))

 ... Physics Branches of physics Quantum mechanics Angular momentum operator Spin Stern-Gerlach experiment
OurBigBook.com Words: 364
Let the atomic beam travel along x, with velocity v>0, and let z measure displacement along the dominant magnetic field gradient. For a fixed effective magnetic moment component μz​, the magnetic dipole potential energy is U≃−μz​Bz​. Taking its negative partial derivative gives
Fz​=−∂z∂U​≃μz​∂z∂Bz​​=μz​G.
(14)
A spatially uniform magnetic field can produce torque and Larmor precession, but cannot produce this translational separation. In a quantum state following a local internal energy eigenvalue En​(B), the more general expression is Fz​=−∂z​En​, with effective magnetic moment −∂En​/∂B. Treating that moment as constant is an approximation with a physical regime, not an extra measured fact. Assume the local magnetic field direction stays nearly fixed across the sampled beam, and that internal states follow it without transitions, as in the adiabatic theorem. The scalar gradient model describes the beam's neighborhood; it omits the full magnetic field's transverse components and fringe regions.
Assume a constant G over magnet length ℓ, followed by a field-free drift D. Neglect transverse motion at entry and changes in longitudinal velocity. Newton's second law gives transverse acceleration az​=μz​G/M for whole-atom mass M. The magnet transit time is tm​=ℓ/v, and the drift time is td​=D/v. The magnet supplies both a displacement and an exit transverse velocity:
zm​−z0​=2az​tm2​​,vz,m​=az​tm​,zs​−z0​=Mv2μz​G​(2ℓ2​+ℓD).
(15)
The ℓD contribution is motion with the acquired transverse velocity, not continued acceleration outside the magnet. For the plate-at-exit geometry, set D=0. For a nonzero entrance transverse velocity w, add w(ℓ+D)/v. These terms show why initial collimation and flight geometry matter when inferring a magnetic moment from a deposit.
Choose hypothetical parameters M=1.79×10−25kg, G=300Tm−1, ℓ=0.035m, D=0.100m, and v=500ms−1. Approximate the two effective magnetic moments by μz​=±μB​, where the Bohr magneton is μB​≃9.274×10−24JT−1. Then ∣az​∣≃1.55×104ms−2, tm​=70μs and td​=200μs. The positive displacement is about 0.0381mm inside the magnet plus 0.2176mm during drift: d=0.2557mm, with full separation 2d=0.5114mm. These chosen values illustrate the model and are not reconstructed 1922 measurements.
The exit transverse velocity is only about 1.09ms−1, small compared with the chosen forward velocity; this checks the small-deflection assumption. Halving G halves d, doubling v divides d by four, and setting D=0 removes most of this example's separation. A real pole has fringe magnetic fields and a position-dependent gradient; these require a more complete trajectory calculation.
