Lebesgue inner measure (m∗​(E)=sup{m(K):K⊆E, K compact})

The Lebesgue inner measure approximates a subset of the real line from within by compact sets, while Lebesgue outer measure approximates it from outside by countable interval covers. For a finite closed interval I containing E,
m∗​(E)=m(I)−m∗(I∖E).
(51)
Indeed, every compact K⊆E gives m∗(I∖E)≤m(I)−m(K). Conversely, an open cover of I∖E with total length arbitrarily close to its outer measure has compact complement in I lying inside E, giving the reverse inequality in the limit.
For m∗(E)<∞, equality of inner and outer measures holds precisely when E is Lebesgue measurable. For an unbounded set, measurability is equivalently checked on all bounded intersections E∩[−n,n]. Equality m∗​(E)=m∗(E)=∞ alone is insufficient: adjoining a bounded nonmeasurable subset to a disjoint half-line makes both quantities infinite without making the union measurable.
