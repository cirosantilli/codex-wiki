Albedo (α)

 Home Physics Branches of physics Geophysics
OurBigBook.com Words: 818 Articles: 5
Albedo is the fraction of incident electromagnetic radiation power reflected, summed over the outgoing directions included in the measurement. For incident power Pin​>0 and reflected power Pref​,
α=Pin​Pref​​,0≤α≤1.
(5)
Thus α=0.30 means 30 percent reflected. For an opaque surface the remaining 70 percent is absorbed; for a transmitting layer, transmission must also be accounted for. Specify the incident wavelengths and illumination geometry: a visible-band surface albedo and a sunlight-weighted broadband surface albedo need not agree. NASA's definition explicitly allows a specified spectral band. [ref]
For a whole planet, broadband Bond albedo sums reflected power over all outgoing directions and all incident wavelengths, weighted by the illuminating spectrum. It is the fraction needed for a whole-planet absorbed-sunlight budget. A local surface albedo describes one surface under specified illumination; it cannot simply replace the Bond albedo of Earth, whose clouds and terrestrial atmosphere also reflect and absorb sunlight. Nor is Bond albedo the same as geometric albedo, which compares brightness in a particular viewing geometry. NASA's account of Earth's budget separates reflection by clouds, particles and the surface from absorption within the terrestrial atmosphere and at the surface. [ref]
The usefulness of albedo is that it turns optical behavior into an energy budget. Bright snow and sea ice usually absorb less sunlight than exposed water under comparable illumination, but their actual surface albedos depend on the spectrum and surface conditions. The ice-ocean albedo feedback makes that optical property part of the dynamics, while the following zero-dimensional planetary energy balance isolates the geometry and competing thermal radiation.
