Valley masses of the Haldane model (mτ​=M−τ33​t2​sinϕ)

The only zeros of f, modulo the reciprocal lattice, are the two Dirac valleys in graphene,
Kτ​=(τ33​a4π​,0),τ=±1.
(13)
At these momenta ∑j​sin(Kτ​⋅bj​)=τ33​/2. Thus the two Dirac mass coefficients, measured in energy units, are mτ​=M−τμ, with μ=33​t2​sinϕ. For k=Kτ​+q,
Hτ​−d0​(Kτ​)I=ℏv(τqx​σx​+qy​σy​)+mτ​σz​+O(∣q∣2),v=2ℏ3t1​a​.
(14)
The two Dirac cones have opposite orientation, encoded by τ. A scalar sublattice offset M gives equal-sign Dirac masses; imaginary second-neighbour hopping at M=0 gives opposite-sign Dirac masses. Both mechanisms open valley gaps
Δτ​=E+​(Kτ​)−E−​(Kτ​)=2∣mτ​∣.
(15)
An observed band gap alone therefore cannot establish a Chern insulator. In particular, the gapless nearest-neighbour model of graphene and its sublattice-mass deformation should not be confused with the topologically nontrivial Haldane model.
A band gap closes when all three components of d vanish. Since dx​=dy​=0 only at the two valleys, the phase boundaries are exactly M=±33​t2​sinϕ. Usually one valley becomes massless; at M=0, sinϕ=0, both do. The occupied-band Chern number is undefined at every such touching, even if a numerical momentum grid misses its location.
