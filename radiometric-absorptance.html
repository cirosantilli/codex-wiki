Radiometric absorptance (a=Pabs​/Pin​)

 Home Physics Branches of physics Optics Radiometry
OurBigBook.com Words: 117
Radiometric absorptance is the fraction of incident radiant flux converted to heat in the specified receiver: Pabs​=aPin​, with 0≤a≤1 for a passive receiver. Specify the entrance boundary, wavelength, incidence angle and receiver temperature. Radiation reflected or transmitted out of that receiver is not absorbed power. A cavity can improve radiometric absorptance by giving light repeated opportunities for wave absorption; a dark coating alone does not establish a=1.
For broadband light, spectral radiant flux matters: Pabs​=∫a(λ)Φe,λ​(λ)dλ. A single effective radiometric absorptance therefore depends on the incident optical spectrum as well as on the receiver. If the stated incident boundary lies before a window of known power transmission τw​, the receiver absorbs aτw​Pbefore​ when other optical losses are absent. Window loss and receiver radiometric absorptance are separate corrections.
