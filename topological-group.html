Locally profinite group

Words: 77 Articles: 1
Topological group OurBigBook.com
A locally profinite group is a Hausdorff, locally compact, totally disconnected topological group. It has a neighbourhood basis at the identity consisting of compact-open subgroups. In smooth representation theory this is also called an ℓ-group; that terminology is unrelated to a lattice-ordered group.

Compact-open subgroup

Words: 35
Locally profinite group OurBigBook.com
A compact-open subgroup is both a compact subset and an open subgroup. Its coset space is discrete, and compact subsets meet only finitely many cosets. Finite intersections of compact-open subgroups are again compact-open, making them useful common stabilizers for finitely many vectors.

Continuous unitary character (χ:G⟶T)

Words: 36
Topological group OurBigBook.com
A continuous unitary character is a continuous group homomorphism from a topological group to the unit circle with multiplication. For Abelian groups, multiplication of characters is pointwise. Such characters must be distinguished from the traces of higher-dimensional group representations and from characters of an algebra.

Right-invariant group metric (d(xa,ya)=d(x,y))

Words: 54
Topological group OurBigBook.com
Right translations are isometries for a right-invariant group metric. Given a compatible left-invariant group metric d, the formula dR​(x,y)=d(x−1,y−1) defines a compatible right-invariant group metric, since inversion is a homeomorphism and (xa)−1=a−1x−1. Compatible invariant metrics need not be Lipschitz equivalent; on the additive group R, both ∣x−y∣ and ∣x−y∣​ are compatible invariant distances.

Left-invariant group metric (d(ax,ay)=d(x,y))

Words: 282 Articles: 4
Topological group OurBigBook.com
This is a distance function satisfying the displayed identity, rather than a Riemannian metric tensor. When it induces the group's given topology, it is a compatible left-invariant group metric. Left translations are isometries.

Bi-invariant group metric (d(axb,ayb)=d(x,y))

Words: 88 Articles: 1
Left-invariant group metric OurBigBook.com
A group distance is bi-invariant when both left and right translations are isometries. Conjugation then fixes the identity element and is an isometry, so each metric ball there is invariant under every inner automorphism. A compatible bi-invariant group metric therefore requires arbitrarily small conjugation-invariant neighbourhoods. This concerns a distance function, not merely a bi-invariant Riemannian metric tensor.

Affine conjugation obstruction to a bi-invariant group metric (Da​Tb​Da−1​=Tab​)

Words: 41
Bi-invariant group metric OurBigBook.com
In the orientation-preserving affine group of the real line, translations T1/n​ converge to the identity element, but conjugation by dilation Dn​ sends each one to T1​. A compatible bi-invariant group metric would give d(e,T1/n​)=d(e,T1​)>0 for every n, contradicting convergence. The group is nonetheless metrizable in its ordinary two real coordinates.

Birkhoff-Kakutani theorem (G metrizable⟺G first countable)

Words: 163 Articles: 1
Left-invariant group metric OurBigBook.com
A Hausdorff topological group admits a compatible left-invariant group metric exactly when it has a countable neighbourhood basis at the identity element. Choose symmetric neighbourhoods of the identity element Un​ with Un+13​⊂Un​ and trivial intersection. Assign a step in Un​ cost 2−n, and take the infimum of total costs of chains joining two points. The dyadic product estimate for group neighbourhoods makes this a genuine compatible metric. The Hausdorff assumption separates distinct points; without it the same construction can give only a pseudometric.

Dyadic product estimate for group neighbourhoods (∑j​2−nj​≤2−n⟹∏j​gj​∈Un−1​)

Words: 95
Birkhoff-Kakutani theorem OurBigBook.com
Let U0​=G, and let the Un​ be decreasing symmetric sets containing the identity element with Un3​⊂Un−1​. If gj​∈Unj​​ and their dyadic costs sum to at most 2−n for n≥1, split the ordered word at an edge crossing its half-cost point. The words on either side each have cost at most 2−n−1. Induction on word length puts both side words in Un​, while the middle edge also belongs to Un​. Their threefold product lies in Un−1​. This controls the short-chain collapse that would otherwise make a path-distance construction degenerate.

Closed subgroup

Words: 24
Topological group OurBigBook.com
A closed subgroup is a subgroup which is closed in the ambient topology. It is a topological group in its subspace topology, but need not be an open subgroup.

Open subgroup

Words: 82 Articles: 1
Topological group OurBigBook.com
An open subgroup is also closed, since its complement is the union of its other open cosets. Conversely a closed subgroup need not be open, as Z⊂R shows.

Open normal subgroup

Words: 53
Open subgroup OurBigBook.com
An open normal subgroup N of a topological group is simultaneously open in its topology and invariant under conjugation. For a profinite group, the quotient G/N is a finite discrete group: its cosets form a disjoint open cover, so compactness gives finitely many. Conversely the group homomorphism kernel of a continuous homomorphism to a finite discrete group is open and normal. Such subgroups form an identity-neighbourhood basis of every profinite group.

Compactly generated group

Words: 141 Articles: 1
Topological group OurBigBook.com
A topological group is compactly generated in the algebraic sense when a compact subset generates its underlying group. Adjoining its inverse and the identity gives a compact symmetric generating set K, and G=⋃n≥1​Kn is a sigma-compact space. Some conventions restrict the term to locally compact groups or require a compact generating neighbourhood; those stronger conventions supply local compactness. Compact generation by an arbitrary compact subset alone does not.

Compact generation does not imply local compactness

Words: 73
Compactly generated group OurBigBook.com
The compact set K={0}∪{1/n!:n≥1} generates the additive group Q, since every positive integer denominator divides some factorial. With its usual topology, Q is metrizable and is not locally compact. Nor can it have a nonzero Haar measure finite on compact sets: invariance gives equal masses to all singletons; a positive singleton mass makes K have infinite mass, whereas zero singleton masses make the countable group have zero total mass.

Locally compact group

Words: 83 Articles: 1
Topological group OurBigBook.com
A locally compact group is a topological group whose underlying space is a locally compact space. In Haar measure and Fourier transform theory one normally also assumes it is a Hausdorff space. Local compactness supplies compact neighbourhoods, not just compact sets which algebraically generate the group.

Sigma-compact open subgroup of a locally compact group

Words: 46
Locally compact group OurBigBook.com
A compact identity neighbourhood K produces a symmetric compact neighbourhood S=K∪K−1∪{e}. The subgroup ⋃n≥1​Sn is open because it contains an identity neighbourhood, and is sigma-compact because each finite product Sn is compact. This construction does not require the original group itself to be sigma-compact.

Neighbourhood criterion for a topological group

Words: 84
Topological group OurBigBook.com
A topology on a group makes it a topological group precisely when its neighbourhood bases satisfy two local tests: every neighbourhood U of xy contains VW for some neighbourhoods V of x and W of y, and every neighbourhood of x−1 contains V−1 for some neighbourhood V of x. These are exactly continuity of multiplication in the product topology and continuity of inversion. Equivalently, bases can be chosen as left translates of a basis at the identity, with arbitrarily small squares, inverses and conjugates inside each prescribed identity neighbourhood.

Discrete subgroup (Γ≤G)

Words: 949 Articles: 15
Topological group OurBigBook.com
A subgroup G of a topological group is discrete when the identity has a neighbourhood containing no other element of G. Translation then isolates every group element. In a Hausdorff topological group, such a subgroup is closed: any accumulation at a point would make differences of distinct nearby group elements approach the identity. Consequently its intersection with a compact set is finite. For isometries of hyperbolic space, this converts compactness of the set of isometries moving one point a bounded distance into a properly discontinuous group action.

Discrete additive subgroup of a real vector space (Λ=Zv1​+⋯+Zvr​)

Words: 84
Discrete subgroup OurBigBook.com
A discrete additive subgroup of Rn is generated by real-linearly independent vectors. Indeed, isolation of zero gives uniform separation, so compact sets meet the subgroup finitely. Choose a real basis from the subgroup for its real span. Its integer span has finite index, since reduction into a compact fundamental parallelepiped gives finitely many coset representatives. The structure theorem for finitely generated abelian groups then gives a free integer basis, whose size is the dimension of the real span. Thus the subgroup is a Euclidean lattice in its span, although it need not have full rank in the ambient space.

Planar discrete-subgroup basis

Words: 97
Discrete subgroup OurBigBook.com
A nonzero discrete additive subgroup of R2 has a basis of one or two real-linearly independent vectors. Choose a shortest nonzero vector w1​. If the subgroup is not contained in its line, reduce parallel components modulo w1​ and choose a vector w2​ of least positive perpendicular height. Such a height exists: heights tending to zero would give infinitely many reduced points in a bounded region, contrary to discreteness. Reducing any group vector modulo w2​ leaves height smaller than this minimum, hence height zero, and reducing along w1​ then gives an integer multiple of w1​.

Kleinian group (Γ≤PSL2​(C))

Words: 689 Articles: 12
Discrete subgroup OurBigBook.com
A discrete subgroup of PSL2​(C), acting by Möbius transformations on the Riemann sphere or orientation-preserving isometries of hyperbolic space H3. A group may be Kleinian even when some elements fix points; freeness and absence of torsion are additional conditions.

Gaussian Bianchi group (PSL2​(Z[i]))

Words: 47
Kleinian group OurBigBook.com
The Gaussian Bianchi group is the Kleinian group of determinant-one two-by-two matrices over the Gaussian integers, modulo {±I}. Its matrix entries lie in a discrete subring of C, so it is a discrete subgroup of PSL2​(C). The Borromean link group is a torsion-free subgroup of index 24.

Jørgensen's inequality (∣tr2A−4∣+∣tr[A,B]−2∣≥1)

Words: 47
Kleinian group OurBigBook.com
For a non-elementary discrete subgroup generated by A,B∈SL(2,C), the displayed inequality is a necessary trace constraint. Failure of it proves nondiscreteness when non-elementarity has independently been checked. It is not a sufficient condition for discreteness and must not be applied to an elementary pair without its hypothesis.

Schottky group

Words: 162 Articles: 1
Kleinian group OurBigBook.com
A Schottky group is a free, discrete group of Möbius transformations constructed by pairing disjoint Jordan curves on the Riemann sphere, taking the exterior of one bounded disk to the interior of its partner. For round circles it is classical. Strictly separated defining disks give a Cantor set as the limit set of a Kleinian group. The tangent-disk extension permits parabolic elements and is often called a noded or T-Schottky group; its Kleinian limit set need not be a Cantor set.
Tangent-disk Schottky group
Words: 97
Schottky group OurBigBook.com
A Tangent-disk Schottky group uses side-pairing disks with disjoint interiors but permits boundaries to meet. This convention must be distinguished from the strictly separated, purely loxodromic Schottky group convention. For example, the maps A(z)=z+2 and B(z)=z/(iz+1) pair the half-plane disks ℜz<−1,ℜz>1 and the disks ∣z−i∣<1,∣z+i∣<1. The ping-pong lemma gives a free discrete group. With C=ABA−1B−1, the subgroup generated by B,C is conjugate by M(z)=iz/(2−z) to the principal level-two modular group, generated by (12​01​) and (10​21​). Thus its Kleinian limit set contains the circle ∣z−1∣=1 and its translate ∣z−3∣=1. Its Hausdorff dimension is at least one and it cannot be homeomorphic to a single circle.

Rank-one convergence of divergent Möbius transformations

Words: 77
Kleinian group OurBigBook.com
A divergent sequence of Möbius transformations has a subsequence converging uniformly on compact subsets of the Riemann sphere outside one exceptional point to a constant. Represent the transformations by determinant-one 2×2 matrices and divide each by its matrix norm. A convergent subsequence has a nonzero rank-one limit: its determinant tends to zero because the original norms tend to infinity. The kernel line is the exceptional point, and the image line is the constant limit. This converts the limit set of a Kleinian group into a tractable boundary dynamics problem.

Elliptic stabilizer obstruction to a hyperbolic manifold quotient

Words: 46
Kleinian group OurBigBook.com
A nontrivial finite-order elliptic Möbius transformation fixes an axis in hyperbolic space. The quotient metric has cone angle 2π/m along that axis for a stabilizer of order m, so it is locally an orbifold metric rather than a smooth hyperbolic-manifold metric. The underlying topological quotient may nevertheless be a manifold.

Non-elementary Kleinian group

Words: 25
Kleinian group OurBigBook.com
A Kleinian group whose Kleinian limit set contains at least three points. A discrete group fixing a common boundary point has at most two limit points and is therefore elementary.

Limit set of a Kleinian group (Λ(Γ)=Γo∩S∞2​)

Words: 147 Articles: 2
Kleinian group OurBigBook.com
For a discrete isometry group of hyperbolic space, its limit set is the boundary accumulation set of one interior orbit. All interior base points give the same set: corresponding orbit points stay a fixed hyperbolic distance apart, and bounded-distance sequences approaching the boundary have the same endpoint. This definition does not require finite generation.
Minimality of the Kleinian limit set
Words: 94 Articles: 1
Limit set of a Kleinian group OurBigBook.com
Every nonempty closed boundary subset invariant under a non-elementary Kleinian group contains the Kleinian limit set. Such a subset cannot be a singleton. Its hyperbolic convex hull contains an interior point, whose orbit lies in the hull; in the Klein ball model the boundary of this hull is contained in the original closed boundary subset.
Limit set of an infinite normal Kleinian subgroup (N◃Γ,∣N∣=∞⟹Λ(N)=Λ(Γ))
Words: 44
Minimality of the Kleinian limit set OurBigBook.com
The Kleinian limit set of an infinite subgroup is nonempty. If it is normal, its limit set is invariant under the ambient group by base-point independence and conjugation. Minimality of the Kleinian limit set gives one inclusion, and subgroup containment gives the other. Neither properness nor finite generation of the subgroup is needed.

Gaussian-integer parabolic construction ((1+rnr2​−n21−rn​))

Words: 47
Kleinian group OurBigBook.com
For n∈Z[i] and a nonzero integer r, the displayed determinant-one matrix has trace two and represents a nonidentity parabolic Möbius transformation fixing n/r. Its fixed-point equation is (rz−n)2=0. These parabolic fixed points are dense in the Riemann sphere, proving that the Gaussian-integer determinant-one Kleinian group has full limit set.

A free Kleinian group generated by two parabolic transformations (⟨z↦z+2, z↦z/(2z+1)⟩≅F2​)

Words: 54
Kleinian group OurBigBook.com
The integer matrices A=(10​21​) and B=(12​01​) generate a discrete subgroup. For every nonzero integer m, Am sends ∣x∣<1 into ∣x∣>1, while Bm sends ∣x∣>1, including infinity, into ∣x∣<1. The ping-pong lemma identifies their subgroup as the free product of two infinite cyclic groups. This separates the proof of discreteness from the proof of freeness.

Compact group

Words: 187 Articles: 2
Topological group OurBigBook.com
A compact group is a topological group whose underlying topological space is compact and Hausdorff.

Compact groups with a descending chain condition are Lie groups

Words: 67
Compact group OurBigBook.com
The Peter-Weyl theorem makes finite-dimensional unitary representations separate elements of a compact group. Intersecting their closed kernels gives the identity subgroup. If the descending chain condition holds for closed subgroups, choose finitely many representations whose kernel intersection is already trivial: otherwise repeatedly exclude a surviving nonidentity element to obtain an infinite strict chain. Their direct sum is a faithful representation into a finite-dimensional unitary group. Compactness makes its image closed, and the closed-subgroup theorem gives the original group a compatible Lie group structure.

Closed subsemigroup of a compact group

Words: 111
Compact group OurBigBook.com
A nonempty closed subsemigroup S of a compact group is a subgroup. For x∈S and a neighbourhood U of the identity, choose V with V−1V⊆U and cover the compact group by finitely many translates of V. Two of sufficiently many powers 1,x,x2,… lie in the same translate, so some positive power of x belongs to U. Hence the identity is a limit of positive powers and lies in S. The preceding powers converge to x−1, which also lies in S. Applied to the closure of a positive-word monoid, this proves that it equals the closed subgroup generated by its generators, without assuming they already include their inverses.

Profinite group

Words: 3k Articles: 46
Topological group OurBigBook.com
A profinite group is an inverse limit of finite discrete groups. Equivalently, it is a compact Hausdorff totally disconnected topological group.

Just-infinite profinite group

Words: 94 Articles: 1
Profinite group OurBigBook.com
An infinite profinite group is just infinite if every nontrivial closed normal subgroup has finite index, equivalently is open. Requiring closedness is part of the topological convention. This property says that every proper Hausdorff quotient is finite.

Hereditarily just-infinite profinite group

Words: 62
Just-infinite profinite group OurBigBook.com
An infinite profinite group is hereditarily just infinite if every open subgroup is a just-infinite profinite group. To prove this, it suffices to show that every nonidentity element of every open subgroup has open closed normal closure: any nontrivial closed normal subgroup then contains that normal closure. The Nottingham group is an example. In odd characteristic its depth-commutator formula constructs nonzero elements of each sufficiently high layer inside any such normal subgroup, and successive coefficient correction fills the entire tail.

Rank of a profinite group (rk(G)=supK≤c​G​d(K))

Words: 105
Profinite group OurBigBook.com
The rank of a profinite group is rk(G)=supK≤c​G​d(K), where K runs through closed subgroups and d(K) is the least number of topological generators. If an open subgroup H has rank r, its normal core C is open and has rank at most r. Every closed K≤G has a normal subgroup K∩C generated by at most r elements, while K/(K∩C) has order at most [G:C]. A finite group of order at most M is generated by at most ⌊log2​M⌋ elements, since each necessary new generator at least doubles the generated subgroup. Lifting generators yields d(K)≤r+⌊log2​[G:C]⌋. Thus finite rank is preserved in both directions under passage to an open subgroup.

Procyclic group

Words: 125
Profinite group OurBigBook.com
A profinite group G is procyclic if every quotient by an open normal subgroup is cyclic. Equivalently G=⟨g⟩​ for one element g. For the forward implication, the sets of lifts of generators of G/N are nonempty clopen sets. Any finite intersection contains a lift of a generator of the cyclic quotient by the intersection of those open normal subgroups. Compactness supplies one element generating every finite quotient, and hence generating densely. The reverse implication follows by projecting that generator. For a pro-p group, two distinct maximal open subgroups would give a quotient Cp2​, so a nontrivial procyclic group has a unique maximal open subgroup M. Its profinite Frattini subgroup is M, and any g∈/M generates topologically: otherwise the proper closed subgroup it generates lies in a maximal open subgroup. The additive group of p-adic integers is an infinite example, generated topologically by 1.

Open normal subgroup basis of a profinite group

Words: 72
Profinite group OurBigBook.com
Every identity neighbourhood of a compact Hausdorff totally disconnected topological group contains an open normal subgroup. Choose a clopen set U containing the identity inside that neighbourhood. Compactness and continuity give a symmetric identity neighbourhood V with VU⊆U, hence VU=U. The setwise stabilizer H={g:gU=U} is an open subgroup contained in U. Its index is finite, so its normal core is an open normal subgroup contained in U. These subgroups recover the profinite group as the inverse limit of its finite quotients.

Profinite Frattini subgroup (Φ(G))

Words: 67
Profinite group OurBigBook.com
The intersection of the maximal proper open subgroups of a profinite group. A subset topologically generates the profinite group exactly when its image topologically generates its quotient by this intersection: a proper closed generated subgroup is contained in a maximal open subgroup, obtained from a suitable finite quotient. For a pro-p group, Φ(G)=Gp[G,G]​, where the bar denotes closure of the algebraically generated power and commutator subgroups. Closedness cannot simply be discarded in infinite rank.

Profinite abelian group

Words: 30
Profinite group OurBigBook.com
A profinite abelian group is an inverse limit of finite abelian groups. Its topology is compact and totally disconnected. An abelian pro-p group acquires scalar multiplication by p-adic integers by taking limits of integer multiples.

Pro-p group (P=lim​Pi​)

Words: 2k Articles: 25
Profinite group OurBigBook.com
A pro-p group is an inverse limit of finite p-groups. Every continuous action on a finite set or finite module factors through a finite p-group. In particular its action on μp​ is trivial when the field characteristic differs from p, because Aut(μp​) has order p−1.

Pro-p subgroup

Words: 19
Pro-p group OurBigBook.com
A closed subgroup of a profinite group that is a pro-p group with the induced topology. Its images in finite continuous quotients are p-groups.

R-analytic pro-p group

Words: 77
Pro-p group OurBigBook.com
For a pro-p ring R, an R-analytic pro-p group is a pro-p group equipped with an analytic manifold structure locally modelled on open subsets of a finite free R-module, such that multiplication and inversion are given locally by convergent power series. The number of local coordinates is its analytic dimension. The Nottingham group admits no such structure over any pro-p ring: its hereditary just infiniteness, unbounded finite cyclic torsion and sub-base-three derived-series growth exclude both alternatives in the analytic just-infinite structure theorem.

Nottingham group (N(Fp​))

Words: 289 Articles: 3
Pro-p group OurBigBook.com
Over k=Fp​, the Nottingham group is N(k)={f(t)=t+∑i≥2​ai​ti:ai​∈k} with composition and the t-adic topology. Equivalently it is the group of continuous k-automorphisms of the Laurent series field k((t)) that act trivially on (t)/(t2). With ordinary series composition, the isomorphism sends f to the automorphism h(t)↦h(f−1(t)); this inverse avoids reversing the group product. It is a pro-p group, recovered from its finite truncations, and contains elements of arbitrarily large finite p-power order.
Finite p-group embedding in the Nottingham group
Words: 72
Nottingham group OurBigBook.com
Realize a finite p-group P as the Galois group of a totally ramified extension E/Fp​((t)), using Artin-Schreier embedding problems over a characteristic-p field. A uniformizer u of E gives E≅Fp​((u)): every element has a unique convergent Laurent expansion with coefficients in the residue field. An automorphism has u↦a1​u+a2​u2+⋯, and a1​ defines a homomorphism P→Fp×​. Its image is trivial because ∣P∣ is a p-power and p∤∣Fp×​∣. Thus the faithful Galois action lies in the Nottingham group, embedding P.
