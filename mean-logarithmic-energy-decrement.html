Mean logarithmic energy decrement (ξ=E[log(E/E′)])

Consider a nonrelativistic particle of mass m undergoing an elastic collision with a stationary free target of mass M=Am, where A≥1. Assume the outgoing direction is uniformly distributed over solid angle in the center of mass frame; this assumption concerns the scattering angle distribution and need not hold for every target or energy. Let μ be the cosine of that frame's scattering angle. Transforming the outgoing velocity back to the laboratory gives

For equal masses, take the limit α→0: ξ=1. The expected value of the fractional energy after a collision is instead E[x]=(1+α)/2. These are different averages: averaging a logarithm does not give the logarithm of an average.
For independent collisions with the same distribution, the logarithms of successive energy ratios add. Thus
E[log(En​/E0​)]=−nξ,Egeom​=E0​e−nξ,nlog​=ξlog(E0​/E∗​)​.

Here Egeom​ is the geometric-mean energy, and nlog​ is a characteristic collision count for reaching an energy scale E∗​. It is not an exact first-passage expected value. Individual histories fluctuate, and neither the time between collisions nor absorption is specified by this model.
