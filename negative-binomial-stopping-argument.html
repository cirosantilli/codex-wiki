Negative binomial stopping argument

 ... Area of mathematics Probability and statistics Probability theory Probability distribution Discrete probability distribution Negative binomial distribution
OurBigBook.com Words: 577 Articles: 3
For the negative binomial distribution with positive integer target r and success probability 0<p<1, the rth success occurs after exactly x failures only if the final trial is a success, while the preceding x+r−1 trials contain x failures and r−1 successes. Choosing the failure locations gives the binomial coefficient (xx+r−1​). Each such stopped sequence has probability (1−p)xpr by independence, and the sequences describe mutually exclusive events, so their probabilities add. This proves the probability mass function. Counting arrangements among all x+r positions would wrongly include sequences that already reached the target earlier. The same argument is used for three heads in MIT 18.05, spring 2022, problem set 9 solutions, problem 2(b).
A short route to normalization and moments is to split the failures count into successive waiting blocks:
X=F1​+⋯+Fr​,
(37)
where Fi​ counts failures after success i−1 and before success i, with F1​ starting at the first trial. Fresh independent Bernoulli trials make the blocks independent, each with the failures-count geometric distribution. Each block has total mass p∑j=0∞​(1−p)j=1 by the geometric series, so their sum also defines a normalized probability distribution. The linearity of expectation and variance additivity for independent random variables give EX=r(1−p)/p and Var(X)=r(1−p)/p2. This makes the moments transparent without a long calculation of sums. For p=1, every block is zero.
