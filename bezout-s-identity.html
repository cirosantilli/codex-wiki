Bézout's identity (gcd(a,b)=ax+by)

 Home Mathematics Area of mathematics Algebra
OurBigBook.com Words: 115 Articles: 1
For integers a,b not both zero, there are integers x,y such that ax+by=gcd(a,b). The extended Euclidean algorithm constructs them. For example, 4⋅252−5⋅198=18 and 7⋅5−2⋅17=1; the second identity also gives the modular inverse of 5 modulo 17.
The identity converts a greatest common divisor into a solvability certificate. The linear Diophantine equation ax+by=c is solvable in integers precisely when gcd(a,b) divides c. Every common divisor divides any integer linear combination; conversely, multiplying a Bézout identity by c/gcd(a,b) constructs a solution when that quotient is integral. The coefficients generally are not unique, as the description of all solutions of a linear Diophantine equation makes explicit.
