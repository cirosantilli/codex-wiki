Sesquilinear form

 Home Mathematics Area of mathematics Algebra Linear algebra
OurBigBook.com Words: 2k Articles: 13
A sesquilinear form on a complex vector space V is a map B:V×V→C which, with the convention used throughout this article, is a linear map in its first argument and a conjugate-linear map in its second. For vectors u,v,w and complex numbers α,β, the defining identities are
B(αu+βv,w)=αB(u,w)+βB(v,w),B(u,αv+βw)=αB(u,v)+β​B(u,w).
(7)
The bars denote complex conjugation. No symmetry or positivity is required. Some authors choose the opposite convention; every formula here keeps the first argument linear.[ref]
For a basis e1​,…,en​ of a finite-dimensional vector space, write u=∑i​xi​ei​, v=∑j​yj​ej​, and Aij​=B(ei​,ej​). Expanding by the two identities gives
B(u,v)=∑i,j​xi​Aij​yj​​=xTAy​.
(8)
Every complex matrix A gives a sesquilinear form this way. The superscript T means transpose, not conjugate transpose. Equivalently, the scalar is y∗ATx, where y∗=y​T. This is the convention used for the matrix of a Hermitian form when symmetry is imposed. The coordinate formula follows directly by expanding the basis coefficients.[ref]
For a concrete example on C2, let B(z,w)=z1​w2​​. Its matrix is (00​10​): B(e1​,e2​)=1 but B(e2​,e1​)=0, so it is not a Hermitian form. Also B((1,i),(1,i))=−i, so even real diagonal values cannot be assumed for an arbitrary sesquilinear form. By comparison, the bilinear form F(z,w)=z1​w1​+z2​w2​ satisfies F(iz,w)=F(z,iw)=iF(z,w) and has F((i,0),(i,0))=−1; it cannot supply positive squared lengths on this complex vector space.
The effect of changing the complex argument of a multiplier is particularly revealing. For real numbers θ,ϕ, sesquilinear forms satisfy
B(eiθu,eiϕv)=ei(θ−ϕ)B(u,v),B(αu,αu)=∣α∣2B(u,u).
(9)
Thus a common change of complex argument cancels from the diagonal, whereas the two arguments rotate the output in opposite directions. Conjugating one argument is what replaces the α2 scaling of a bilinear form by the nonnegative ∣α∣2 scaling needed for a Hermitian inner product. This mechanism also works in function spaces: for complex continuous functions on [0,1], B(f,g)=∫01​f(t)g(t)​dt obeys the same identities, letting integration measure overlap rather than merely multiply coefficients.
