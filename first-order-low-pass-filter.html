First-order low-pass filter (G(s)=K/(τs+1))

 Home Mathematics Area of mathematics Control theory Transfer function
OurBigBook.com Words: 281
A first-order low-pass filter with positive constant gain K and time constant τ>0 obeys the ordinary differential equation
τy′(t)+y(t)=Ku(t).
(51)
The derivative term limits how quickly the plant output follows a changing plant input. A larger time constant means slower adjustment. This standard form, with K=1, is developed in Derek Rowell, MIT 2.004, lecture 20, section 1.1: first-order systems; the following calculation keeps the gain K explicit.
Write y0​=y(0). The Laplace transform of the derivative is sY(s)−y0​, so
τ(sY(s)−y0​)+Y(s)=KU(s),Y(s)=τs+1K​U(s)+τs+1τy0​​.
(52)
Setting the initial condition y0​=0 gives the transfer function G(s)=K/(τs+1). Its single simple pole is s=−1/τ, which has negative real part. The unforced response decays, and the input/output map has BIBO stability. The eventual gain for a constant plant input is G(0)=K.
For a unit step, represented by the Heaviside step function u(t)=H(t), the Laplace transform is U(s)=1/s. From zero initial condition,
Y(s)=s(τs+1)K​=sK​−s+1/τK​,y(t)=K(1−e−t/τ)(t≥0).
(53)
Take K=2 and τ=3s, with dimensionless plant input and plant output. Then y(t)=2(1−e−t/(3s)) rises toward 2. After one time constant, at t=3s, it reaches 2(1−e−1)≈1.2642, or 63.2% of its final value. At t=9s=3τ, it reaches 2(1−e−3)≈1.9004, or 95.0%. These percentages describe the rise from zero, rather than an instantaneous jump to the target.
With a nonzero initial condition, the same step instead gives
y(t)=K(1−e−t/τ)+y0​e−t/τ.
(54)
For K=2 and τ=3s, compare y0​=0 with y0​=1. Their plant outputs differ by e−t/(3s) despite having the same transfer function and the same plant input. The extra exponential decay term must be supplied from the initial condition; G and u alone cannot determine it.
