Waveguide cutoff frequency (ωmn​=cπm2/a2+n2/b2​)

 Home Physics Branches of physics Electromagnetism Electromagnetic wave Rectangular electromagnetic waveguide
OurBigBook.com Words: 561 Articles: 1
The waveguide cutoff frequency separates oscillatory axial propagation from exponential decay for a particular normal mode. For the vacuum-filled rectangular electromagnetic waveguide, the dispersion relation is
κmn2​=(amπ​)2+(bnπ​)2,ωmn​=cκmn​,β2=c2ω2−ωmn2​​.
(5)
For a transverse electric mode of a rectangular waveguide, m,n are nonnegative integers but not both zero. For a mode with Bz​=0 and nonzero Ez​, both indices must be positive. The lowest cutoff for a>b is consequently ω10​=πc/a, or ordinary frequency fc​=c/(2a).
A square cross-section instead gives equal TE10​ and TE01​ cutoffs. The absence of a transverse electromagnetic mode is specific to this hollow pipe, rather than all electromagnetic waveguides.[ref]
For ω>ωmn​, choose real β>0 for propagation toward +z. For 0<ω<ωmn​, a field decaying into a semi-infinite guide has
β=iα,α=cωmn2​−ω2​​>0,eiβz=e−αz.
(6)
A finite section can contain both growing and decaying axial solutions set by its two ends; the single decaying solution is not a complete finite-section transmission calculation. At exact cutoff β=0, the two axial solutions become a constant and a linear function of z: there is no finite axial wavelength for a traveling sinusoid.
In the chosen a=30mm guide at f=0.8fc​=3.99723277GHz, the TE10​ branch has α=20πm−1. Its amplitude decay length is 1/α≃15.9155mm; after 50mm, its normalized wave amplitude is e−π≃0.0432139. This is geometric evanescence even with ideal walls, not Joule heating. A single decaying branch has zero cycle-averaged axial Poynting vector: substituting β=iα makes Bx​ a quarter-cycle out of phase with Ey​. Coupling across a finite section requires the complete boundary-value solution.
