A degenerate optical parametric amplifier sends a strong pump at 2ω through a phase-matched second-order nonlinear medium, creating pairs in a selected mode near ω. With the pump treated classically and undepleted, the effective interaction is quadratic in the mode's creation and annihilation operators. Vacuum input evolves into an optical squeezed vacuum; the pump phase fixes the quiet optical quadrature. A cavity enhances the interaction below oscillation threshold. balanced homodyne detection compares this optical quadrature's noise with the blocked-signal vacuum reference; phase locking, detector balance, electronic-noise measurement and loss calibration distinguish squeezing from an instrumental noise reduction. A primary cavity experiment observed below-vacuum noise by degenerate down conversion and balanced homodyne readout.[ref]
