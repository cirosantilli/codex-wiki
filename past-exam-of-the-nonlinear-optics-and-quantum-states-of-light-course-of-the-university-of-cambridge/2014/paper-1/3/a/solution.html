An optical soliton maintains a localized profile because nonlinear refraction compensates linear spreading. A temporal optical soliton balances group velocity dispersion against self-phase modulation; a spatial optical soliton balances diffraction against self-focusing. The relevant coordinate and sign of nonlinearity must be specified.
For anomalous group velocity dispersion and positive γ, a fundamental temporal bright soliton is

The identity (sechu)′′=sechu−2sech3u verifies both the width relation and propagation phase. Its dispersion length LD​=T02​/∣β2​∣ equals its nonlinear length LNL​=1/(γPs​). Bright pulses decay to zero background. dark solitons are intensity depressions on a nonzero background with a phase change across the dip; black dips reach zero intensity, while grey dips do not. For positive γ the corresponding temporal defocusing regime has normal group velocity dispersion.
A higher-order temporal soliton has soliton order Ns​=γPs​T02​/∣β2​∣​ equal to an integer greater than one for the special initial Ns​sech profile in normalized units. It breathes through compression and splitting/recombination, rather than keeping one fixed shape. Separated fundamental solitons can interact and acquire collision shifts. An arbitrary pulse with noninteger Ns​ is not automatically a pure higher-order soliton; dispersive radiation can carry part of its energy.
In ideal one-dimensional cubic propagation, dispersion and nonlinearity support stable fundamental bright pulses. Loss, higher-order dispersion, delayed nonlinear response and finite bandwidth change real-fibre propagation. A one-dimensional spatial bright solution also does not guarantee stability against two-dimensional collapse. Profile preservation, finite-energy background conditions and robustness should be checked rather than inferred from any solitary-looking peak.
