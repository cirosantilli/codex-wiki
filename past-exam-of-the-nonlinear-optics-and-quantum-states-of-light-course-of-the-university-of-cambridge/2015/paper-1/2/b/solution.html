Its susceptibility is a rank-(n+1) tensor. Initially it has 3n+1 components in three dimensions, before symmetries and frequency constraints are applied. nonlinear-susceptibility tensor symmetry involves several logically different restrictions.
Neumann's principle requires invariance under the material point group. For an operation R,
χij1​…jn​​=Ria​Rj1​b1​​⋯Rjn​bn​​χab1​…bn​​.
(13)
For inversion, R=−I, so χ(n)=(−1)n+1χ(n). Thus all even orders vanish in a centrosymmetric material's local bulk electric-dipole response. In particular χ(2)=0 there, while χ(3) is permitted. Surface symmetry breaking, an applied bias or nonlocal magnetic/quadrupole response lies outside that statement. A noncentrosymmetric crystal permits some second-order components, but other point-group operations can still eliminate a chosen component.
Intrinsic permutation symmetry follows from interchanging commuting input field factors, provided the associated frequency labels are interchanged too. At second order,
χijk(2)​(ωs​;ω1​,ω2​)=χikj(2)​(ωs​;ω2​,ω1​).
(14)
For equal input frequencies this makes the last two spatial indices symmetric, reducing 27 components to 18 before material symmetries. At unequal frequencies it does not identify the two index arrangements with the arguments held fixed. Reality of the time-domain polarization separately gives
χ(n)(−ωs​;−ω1​,…,−ωn​)=χ(n)(ωs​;ω1​,…,ωn​)∗.
(15)
causality imposes dispersion relations; neither arbitrary real coefficients nor full index symmetry should be inferred merely from the word lossless.
Kleinman symmetry is a stronger approximation far from relevant resonances when frequency dispersion can be neglected. A frequency-independent effective interaction energy makes its derivatives interchangeable, allowing full permutation of output and input spatial indices. At second order a fully symmetric rank-three tensor has (35​)=10 components. Close to resonance or across a dispersive band, that reduction can fail even when absorption at the measured frequencies is small. The original analysis distinguishes high-resonance electronic response from strongly dispersive lattice mechanisms and identifies additional symmetry constraints on the former.[ref]
For an isotropic fourth-rank susceptibility,
χijkl(3)​=aδij​δkl​+bδik​δjl​+cδil​δjk​.
(16)
If the relevant input slots are fully interchangeable, the coefficients coincide and χxxxx(3)​=3χxxyy(3)​. With distinct or mixed-sign frequency slots, frequency arguments must be followed and isotropy alone leaves the three displayed structures. These distinctions explain why tensor contractions, pump polarizations and operating frequencies must be specified before comparing effective self- and cross-phase coefficients.
