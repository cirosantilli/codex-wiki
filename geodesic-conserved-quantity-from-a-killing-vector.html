Geodesic conserved quantity from a Killing vector (CK​=g(K,U))

 Home Physics Branches of physics General relativity Killing vector field
OurBigBook.com Words: 592 Articles: 4
Each Killing vector field supplies a conserved quantity for freely moving test particles: it turns a geometric symmetry into a first integral of the geodesic equation. Let γ(λ) be a geodesic with affine parameter λ, tangent vector Uμ=dxμ/dλ, and ∇U​U=0. Wherever a Killing vector field K is defined along the geodesic,
CK​=g(K,U)=Kμ​Uμ,dλdCK​​=0.
(12)
The statement applies to Riemannian manifolds and to timelike geodesics, null geodesics and spacelike geodesics in a Lorentzian manifold. It requires an affine parameter; for a massive test particle, proper time is one choice. See Tong, section 4.3.2, equation (4.32).
For a worked example in the Euclidean plane, take γ(t)=(1+t,2−t) with affine parameter t and velocity U=(1,−1). The Euclidean Killing vector fields for translation and rotation give
Ctr​=vx​=1,Crot​=xvy​−yvx​=−(1+t)−(2−t)=−3.
(13)
The rotational field along the geodesic is Krot​(γ(t))=(t−2,1+t): it changes from (−2,1) at t=0 to (−1,2) at t=1, yet its pairing with U stays −3. With toy mass m=1 and t interpreted as time, the first constant is the x component of linear momentum, and the second is the signed z component of angular momentum about the origin. Their numerical values use toy units. The geodesic is straight; the rotation flow orbits are circles.
This conservation law follows from metric compatibility and the Killing equation:
dλd​(Kμ​Uμ)=UμUν∇μ​Kν​+Kν​(∇U​U)ν=UμUν∇(μ​Kν)​=0.
(14)
The geodesic equation kills the acceleration term, and the symmetric product UμUν selects the vanishing symmetric derivative. This tensor contraction is the whole proof outline; see Carroll, equation (5.43).
In a stationary spacetime, a chosen time-translation Killing vector field K gives Killing energy. With metric signature (−+++), c=1, and a massive test particle's four-momentum pμ=mUμ, the physical normalization is
E=−pμ​Kμ,Lz​=pμ​Φμ.
(15)
Here Φ generates the axial rotational symmetry when one exists. In coordinates with K=∂t​ and Φ=∂ϕ​, these are E=−pt​ and Lz​=pϕ​. Both are conserved quantities along the geodesic, even if the metric has a time-angle cross term. A future timelike K makes E positive for a future massive particle; the scale of E depends on the normalization of K. Not every spacetime has a global timelike Killing vector field, and a stationary field can become spacelike in an ergoregion of a stationary spacetime. Conservation on the field's domain does not imply a universal global positive energy; see Tong, sections 4.3.1-4.3.2 on the timelike-domain qualification and conserved charges.
