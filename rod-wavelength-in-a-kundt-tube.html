Rod wavelength in a Kundt tube (crod​/cgas​=λrod​/λgas​)

 Home Physics Branches of physics Fluid mechanics Linear acoustics Kundt's tube
OurBigBook.com Words: 160
A freely moving rod end has zero axial stress, not zero axial displacement. For a uniform slender rod of length ℓ, its low-frequency extensional modes have shapes ξr​(x)∝cos(nπx/ℓ) and wavelengths λr​=2ℓ/n. A centre clamp is compatible with the n=1 displacement node and λr​=2ℓ; clamps at ℓ/4 and 3ℓ/4 are compatible with n=2 and λr​=ℓ. The support positions therefore matter before a gas wavelength can determine a rod speed of sound.
For a chosen model with E=200GPa, ρr​=8000kg/m3 and ℓ=1.60m, the slender-rod extensional relation cr​=E/ρr​​ gives 5000m/s. The two-quarter-support mode then has f=3125Hz. If the gas has stipulated cg​=343m/s, its half-wavelength is dg​=54.88mm, and
cg​cr​​=λg​λr​​=2(0.05488m)1.60m​≃14.5773.
(6)
The centre-supported fundamental would instead have f=1562.5Hz and twice that gas spacing. The same rod material has not changed its wave speed. In this slender-rod model, E is Young's modulus; a bulk compressional wave involves a different elastic-modulus combination, so the two wave speeds are not interchangeable.
