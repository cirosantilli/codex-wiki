Energy-norm proof of convergence of SOR (0<ω<2⟹∥Cω​∥A​<1)

 ... Area of mathematics Analysis Numerical analysis Numerical linear algebra Stationary iterative method for a linear system Successive over-relaxation
OurBigBook.com Words: 102
Let A=D+L+LT be a real symmetric positive-definite matrix, with D diagonal and L strictly lower triangular. Put B=D/ω+L and Cω​=I−B−1A for ω=0. The matrix norm induced by an energy norm reduces the question to T=A1/2Cω​A−1/2. Direct multiplication and B+BT−A=(2/ω−1)D give
TTT=I−(ω2​−1)A1/2B−TDB−1A1/2.
(109)
The left side is positive semidefinite. The matrix following the scalar coefficient on the right is positive-definite, since D has positive diagonal and B−1A1/2 is an invertible matrix. For 0<ω<2, every eigenvalue of TTT therefore lies in [0,1). Hence its spectral radius is below one and ∥Cω​∥A​=∥T∥2​<1. Iteration errors consequently decay geometrically in the energy norm. The argument proves convergence from every initial vector, without requiring the original iteration matrix to be symmetric.
