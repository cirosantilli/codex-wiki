Finite-gain operational-amplifier integrator (H(s)=1+sC{ro​+R[1+A(s)]}sCro​−A(s)​)

Consider an op amp integrator with infinite input impedance, an internal controlled voltage u=−A(s)v where v is the inverting-input voltage, and a series output impedance equal to a resistance ro​. Assume no external output load. Writing y for the output, Kirchhoff's current law gives
Rvin​−v​=sC(v−y),ro​u−y​=sC(y−v).
(18)
Eliminating u and v yields
H(s)=vin​y​=1+sC{ro​+R[1+A(s)]}sCro​−A(s)​.​
(19)
The expression at ro​=0 is obtained by its limit. In particular, constant gain A0​ and ro​=0 give H(s)=−A0​/[1+sRC(1+A0​)]: a finite direct-current gain followed by an integrating regime. For the dominant-pole model A(s)=A0​/(1+s/ωa​) and ro​=0,
H(s)=1+s[ωa−1​+(1+A0​)RC]+s2RC/ωa​−A0​​.
(20)
If A0​≫1 and A0​ωa​RC≫1, there are separated turnover frequencies near 1/(A0​RC) and A0​ωa​. The gain magnitude is respectively constant, proportional to ω−1 and proportional to ω−2. With the convention eiωt a continuous phase choice passes from 180∘ through 90∘ to 0∘. If ro​>0 and A(iω)→0, capacitive feedthrough instead gives the ultimate limit ro​/(R+ro​). Specifying finite gain alone therefore does not specify a unique high-frequency response.
