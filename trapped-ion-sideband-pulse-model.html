Trapped-ion sideband pulse model (Pr​(n,t)=sin2(ηΩtn​/2))

Consider an original ideal calculation with no heating, decoherence, detuning or spectator modes. In the ordered basis (∣g,n⟩,∣e,n−1⟩) for n≥1, the red motional sideband has Hr​=ℏηΩn​σx​/2. Here σx​ is the Pauli matrix exchanging the two basis states. Since σx2​=I, its unitary time evolution is
Un​(t)=cos2θn​​I−isin2θn​​σx​,θ=ηΩt.
(15)
Thus Un†​Un​=I, and excitation from ∣g,n⟩ has probability Pr​(n,t)=sin2(θn​/2). For n=0 it vanishes. The blue motional sideband similarly gives Pb​(n,t)=sin2(θn+1​/2). A red Rabi pi pulse calibrated for n=1 has θ=π; it is not a Rabi pi pulse for every occupied n.
Choose η=0.08, Ω/(2π)=100 kHz and ν/(2π)=2 MHz. The n=1 red Rabi pi pulse lasts tπ​=π/(ηΩ)=62.5 μs. Let motion have the Boltzmann distribution of a quantum harmonic oscillator, with chosen mean occupation nˉ=0.25:
pn​=(1−r)rn,r=1+nˉnˉ​=0.2,Pr​(t)=∑n=0∞​pn​sin22θn​​,Pb​(t)=∑n=0∞​pn​sin22θn+1​​.
(16)
Here ∑pn​=1, ∑npn​=nˉ and p0​=0.8. Reindex the red sum using n=m+1 and pm+1​=rpm​: Pr​(t)=rPb​(t) for equal resonant pulse parameters, even outside the short-pulse limit. At times with Pb​>0, the ratio R=Pr​/Pb​ therefore gives nˉ=R/(1−R). A nonthermal distribution, unequal drive strengths, detuning or state-detection bias can invalidate that thermometer. Ground-state population cannot generally be deduced from the mean alone without specifying the distribution.
The chosen pulse gives Pr​≃0.181393 and Pb​≃0.906967. These are internal excitation probabilities, not quantum gate fidelities or cooling-cycle efficiencies. An independent matrix exponentiation of each two-state block agrees with the sine formula and preserves normalization. Summing n=0,…,79 omits probability r80<1.21×10−56; because every sine squared lies between zero and one, this also bounds each omitted response. The thermal relation supplies a separate dimensional check:
nˉ=eℏν/(kB​T)−11​,T=kB​log(1+1/nˉ)ℏν​≃59.6 μK.
(17)
Here kB​ is the Boltzmann constant. Substitution recovers nˉ=0.25; the thermal root-mean-square expansion parameter is η2nˉ+1​≃0.098. This checks the populated part of the Lamb-Dicke regime, while exponentially rare large n still exceed any fixed small-parameter criterion. The pulse is a leading-order model; it does not bound corrections from the full coupling or experimental noise.
