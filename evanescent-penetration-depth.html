Evanescent penetration depth (δ=1/κ)

Define the amplitude evanescent penetration depth as the distance at which the electric field wave amplitude falls to 1/e of its surface value:
δ=κ1​=2πn12​sin2θ−n22​​λ0​​,∣E(0)∣∣E(z)∣​=e−z/δ.
(35)
The squared-field envelope decays twice as fast:
∣E(0)∣2∣E(z)∣2​=e−2z/δ,d1/e​=2δ​.
(36)
The same normalized exponential decay describes the tangential average Poynting vector for the TE polarization above. An intensity penetration depth convention often means d1/e​; it must not be confused with amplitude depth δ or interpreted as outgoing normal light intensity.
For the stipulated n1​=1.5, n2​=1, λ0​=633nm and θ=50∘,
k0​δ​≈0.00992604314nm−1,≈177.99534nm,​κd1/e​​≈0.00561812463nm−1,≈88.99767nm.​
(37)
At z=200nm the normalized wave amplitude is approximately 0.325099, and its squared envelope is approximately 0.105689. At z=δ, these become e−1 and e−2, respectively. The quantity inside the square root is dimensionless, so κ has inverse-length units and both depths have length units.
The sharp angular dependence has a simple explanation. For θ=θc​+ε with ε>0 small and measured in radians, a first-order Taylor expansion gives
δ∼2π2n12​sinθc​cosθc​​λ0​​ε−1/2.
(38)
Thus δ→∞ as θ↓θc​: exponential confinement disappears continuously. With fixed refractive indices, δ decreases as the angle of incidence increases and approaches λ0​/(2πn12​−n22​​)≈90.1091nm as θ→90∘. This is a limit of the normalized depth; exactly grazing incidence supplies zero incident normal power. Real beam widths, angular spreads and optical dispersion matter when applying these ideal plane electromagnetic wave predictions near the threshold.
