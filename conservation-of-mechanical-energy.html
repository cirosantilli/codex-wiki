Conservation of mechanical energy (T+V=constant)

 Home Physics Branches of physics Classical mechanics Energy Mechanical energy
OurBigBook.com Words: 94 Articles: 1
For constant masses, a time-independent potential energy V and forces Fi​=−∇i​V+Ri​ with zero total extra power ∑i​Ri​⋅vi​=0, Newton's second law gives dT/dt=−dV/dt. Thus mechanical energy is conserved. Smooth stationary constraint reactions and a purely magnetic Lorentz force supply zero power. This is more restrictive than conservation of energy in general, which may include thermal or other internal forms.
