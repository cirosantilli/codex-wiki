Relative entropy to a product law (D(P∥⨂i​Qi​)=TC(P)+∑i​D(Pi​∥Qi​))

 Home Mathematics Area of mathematics Probability and statistics Information theory Total correlation
OurBigBook.com Words: 131
Let P be a joint probability distribution on a finite product alphabet, with marginals Pi​. Compare it with a product model Q=⨂i​Qi​, assuming Qi​(xi​)>0 wherever Pi​(xi​)>0. Adding and subtracting the logarithm of ∏i​Pi​(xi​) gives
D(P∥Q)=TC(P)+i∑​D(Pi​∥Qi​).​
(75)
The first term is the unavoidable error from discarding dependence; the other terms are errors in the individual marginal probability distributions. Averaging a term that depends only on xi​ under P is the same as averaging it under Pi​, which proves the identity directly.
Among product laws, the choice Qi​=Pi​ minimizes this relative entropy, leaving exactly total correlation. The related cross-entropy equals entropy plus relative entropy identity interprets this as extra average logarithmic loss from using the independent model. It is a statement about a specified joint law and loss function, not a claim that fitting a finite sample automatically recovers that law.
