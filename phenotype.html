Phenotype

 Home Biology Genetics
OurBigBook.com Words: 265 Articles: 5
A phenotype is an observable characteristic of an organism, arising from its genotype, environment and other influences. Seed shape is a phenotype; Aa is a genotype. The distinction prevents appearance from being mistaken for a complete inherited description. See NHGRI: phenotype.
For a specified binary phenotype T, let its penetrance in genotype g be rg​=P(T∣g). The law of total probability gives P(T)=∑g​rg​P(g). Thus the ideal Aa×Aa example has P(T)=rAA​/4+rAa​/2+raa​/4: the familiar 3/4 follows only when these penetrances are 1,1,0. A pedigree likelihood keeps inheritance and the conditional phenotype model as separate factors.
