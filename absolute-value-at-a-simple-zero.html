Suppose a real function g is differentiable at a, with g(a)=0 and g′(a)=0. The derivative definition gives g(a+h)=g′(a)h+o(∣h∣). The reverse triangle inequality then implies
∣g(a+h)∣=∣g′(a)∣∣h∣+o(∣h∣).
(47)
Thus ∣g∣ has unequal one-sided derivatives, ∣g′(a)∣ on the right and −∣g′(a)∣ on the left. More generally, multiplication by a function w continuous at a with w(a)=0 gives right and left slopes w(a)∣g′(a)∣ and −w(a)∣g′(a)∣. This elementary expansion explains how a smooth oscillation can acquire infinitely many corners under the absolute value function, even after multiplication by a factor that makes it differentiable at an accumulation point.
