Centralizer and normalizer

 ... Mathematics Area of mathematics Algebra Group theory Group action Conjugation action
OurBigBook.com Words: 1k Articles: 6
Both constructions are stabilizer subgroups for the conjugation action. The centralizer CG​(x) fixes an individual element x; the normalizer NG​(H) preserves a subgroup H as a whole, while possibly moving its elements. The centralizer of a subset CG​(H) fixes every member of H individually, so
CG​(H)⊆NG​(H).
(45)
For a cyclic subgroup H=⟨r⟩, commuting with the group generator r is equivalent to commuting with all its powers, giving CG​(H)=CG​(r).
In the symmetric group S3​, let r=(123) and H=⟨r⟩={e,(123),(132)}. Then
CS3​​(r)=CS3​​(H)=H,NS3​​(H)=S3​.
(46)
Every transposition sends r to r−1 under conjugation. In particular, for t=(12),
trt−1=r−1=r,tHt−1={e,r−1,r}=H.
(47)
Thus t belongs to the normalizer of H but not to the centralizer of r. Preserving the collection of powers allows the two possible directions of its group generator to be exchanged.
This distinction lets one ask separately which operations leave a chosen operation unchanged and which operations preserve the subgroup it generates. The normalizer also supplies a group homomorphism to the automorphism group of H, by g↦(h↦ghg−1); its group kernel is CG​(H). In this example the group homomorphism has two possible outputs, fixing r or replacing it by r−1. The quotient group NS3​​(H)/CS3​​(H) is therefore a cyclic group of group order two. Milne develops the normalizer as a stabilizer subgroup in Group Theory (v4.01) by James S. Milne.
