Failure of Minkowski inequality below exponent one (0<p<1:Qp​((1,1))=21/p>2)

For 0<p<1, write Qp​(f)=(∫∣f∣pdμ)1/p to emphasize that this is a quasi-norm, as in the Lq quasi-norm branch. On two coordinates with counting measure, u=(1,0) and v=(0,1) give Qp​(u)=Qp​(v)=1, but Qp​(u+v)=21/p>2. At p=1/2, this is the explicit failure 4>1+1 in the right panel of the diagram. The midpoint (1/2,1/2) has Qp​=21/p−1>1, so the corresponding unit sublevel set is not a convex set.
A useful replacement survives: for nonnegative real numbers a,b, (a+b)p≤ap+bp. Together with the scalar triangle inequality, this yields
Qp​(f+g)p≤Qp​(f)p+Qp​(g)p.
(68)
Consequently dp​(f,g)=Qp​(f−g)p is a metric on the equivalence classes of measurable functions with finite Qp​, even though Qp​(f−g) itself need not be a metric. Do not replace the failed Minkowski inequality by a universal reversed bound for signed or complex functions: g=−f=0 would give a zero sum but two positive separate sizes.
