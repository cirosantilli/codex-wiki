Euler characteristic with different coefficient fields (χ(RP2)=1)

OurBigBook.com Words: 197
For a concrete example of cellular homology of real projective space, the real projective plane RP2 has one topological cell in each of dimensions zero, one and two. Its one-dimensional topological cell is a loop, and the face attaches around that loop twice. Choose orientations so that the cellular chain complex over the integers is
0⟶Z2​Z0​Z⟶0,
(25)
with nonzero chain groups in degrees two, one and zero. The twice-around attachment is why the first boundary operator shown is multiplication by two.[ref]
Over the rational numbers, multiplication by two is invertible, so H2​ and H1​ vanish, while H0​≅Q. Over the finite field F2​, two equals zero. Both boundary operators then vanish, giving one-dimensional homology groups in all three degrees. Their respective Betti numbers are
(b0​,b1​,b2​)Q​=(1,0,0),(b0​,b1​,b2​)F2​​=(1,1,1).
(26)
Nevertheless,
χ(RP2)=1−0+0=1−1+1=1.​
(27)
The direct cell count is also 1−1+1=1.
This calculation makes the strength and loss of information visible. The Euler characteristic survives a change of the field used for coefficients, while individual homology groups can change. Over the integers, the same chain complex has H1​≅Z/2Z and H2​=0: a torsion subgroup that rational Betti numbers do not record. The rank cancellation in the Euler-Poincare formula explains why the alternating total still agrees with the cell count.
