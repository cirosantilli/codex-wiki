Necessary conditions for a Hamilton cycle

 ... Foundations of mathematics Graph theory Path in a graph Cycle in a graph Cycle graph Hamilton cycle
OurBigBook.com Words: 142
A Hamiltonian graph must be a connected graph and have minimum degree δ(G)≥2: each vertex has two distinct neighbours on its Hamilton cycle. It also has no cut vertex. Indeed, deleting any vertex from the Hamilton cycle leaves a graph path through every remaining vertex, so the remaining graph is connected. These are quick ways to rule out a Hamilton cycle.[ref]
Even these conditions together are not sufficient: the complete bipartite graph K2,3​ is connected, has minimum degree two and no cut vertex, but has no Hamilton cycle. Every graph cycle in a bipartite graph alternates between its two parts, using equally many vertices from each; it cannot use all two and all three.
In the other direction, Dirac's theorem gives a sufficient condition: a finite simple graph with n≥3 and δ(G)≥n/2 has a Hamilton cycle. Failure of this inequality proves nothing by itself: the cycle graph C5​ has a Hamilton cycle despite δ(C5​)=2<5/2.[ref]
