Let x∈{00,01,10,11} and let f(x) be one only at the marked input. A three-qubit implementation of Grover's algorithm starts with ∣00⟩∣1⟩, applies a Hadamard gate to each qubit, queries the reversible quantum oracle Uf​:∣x,y⟩↦∣x,y⊕f(x)⟩, applies the Grover diffusion operator D=2∣s⟩⟨s∣−I to the first two qubits, and measures those two qubits in the computational basis. Here ∣s⟩=21​∑x​∣x⟩. The full three-wire quantum circuit is the composition
∣00⟩∣1⟩H⊗3​∣s⟩∣−⟩Uf​​(Of​∣s⟩)∣−⟩D⊗I​∣x∗​⟩∣−⟩measure first two​x∗​.​
(39)
Here ∣−⟩=(∣0⟩−∣1⟩)/2​. Since X∣−⟩=−∣−⟩, phase kickback gives Uf​(∣x⟩∣−⟩)=(−1)f(x)∣x⟩∣−⟩. The third qubit remains separable and unchanged. A direct marked-state phase oracle Of​∣x⟩=(−1)f(x)∣x⟩ therefore replaces the three-qubit query using only two qubits:
∣00⟩H⊗H​∣s⟩Of​​Of​∣s⟩D​∣x∗​⟩measure​x∗​.​
(40)
For the explicit choice f(x)=1 only at x=11, Of​ is a Controlled-Z gate. Immediately after it,
Of​∣s⟩=21​(∣00⟩+∣01⟩+∣10⟩−∣11⟩).
(41)
The mean of these four probability amplitudes is a=1/4. The Grover diffusion operator maps each probability amplitude ax​ to 2a−ax​. The three unmarked probability amplitudes become zero and the marked one becomes one. A single query consequently finds 11 with probability one.
A gate-level implementation of D is H⊗2(2∣00⟩⟨00∣−I)H⊗2. Up to an irrelevant global phase, it is H⊗2X⊗2CZX⊗2H⊗2. For any other chosen marked word, conjugate the Controlled-Z gate by Pauli X gates on precisely the positions where the marked word has a zero.
