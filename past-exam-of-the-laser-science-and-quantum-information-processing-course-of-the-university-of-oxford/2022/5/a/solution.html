This is a nonselective quantum measurement, which removes the initial computational basis coherence. It is not generally the original pure quantum state.
After the Hadamard gate, Alice assigns H∣a⟩=∣+⟩ for a=0, or ∣−⟩ for a=1. Bob assigns
HρB,1​H=∣α∣2∣+⟩⟨+∣+∣β∣2∣−⟩⟨−∣.
(45)
Both ∣+⟩ and ∣−⟩ give either final computational basis outcome with probability 1/2. Thus, after Alice records the second outcome b, she assigns ∣b⟩, whereas Bob assigns
ρB,2​=21​∣0⟩⟨0∣+21​∣1⟩⟨1∣=I/2.​
(46)
Alice's conditional quantum states are pure; Bob's are averages over her unobserved records. They agree on all probabilities when supplied the same information. Their first descriptions can coincide if the initial quantum state is a computational basis state; after the second quantum measurement, Bob's maximally mixed state differs from each conditional pure quantum state.
