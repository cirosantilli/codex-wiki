Starting at port 1, the first beam splitter encounter sends amplitude c straight out through port 0 and stores amplitude −c towards M2. A return from M2 enters port 1′; it sends the stored amplitude times −c out through port 1 and times c towards M1. A return from M1 enters port 0′; it sends amplitude times c out through port 0 and times c towards M2. Both internal continuations carry +c, so two additional encounters multiply the surviving amplitude by +c2.

Distinct exit events are orthogonal because they differ in output port or arrival-time bin. This requires the incident wavepacket to resolve the delay. Summing the squared moduli as geometric series gives
P(0)=j=0∑∞​2−(2j+1)=32​,P(1)=j=1∑∞​2−2j=31​.​

Their sum is one. After n encounters the probability of still being inside is 2−n, which tends to zero, so no trapped component is omitted.
The source drawing places the delay line in the M2 arm. A visit to M2 crosses that line in both directions. If τ is its one-way delay, then an exit after n encounters has crossed it 2⌊n/2⌋ times:
tn​=2τ⌊n/2⌋={(n−1)τ,nτ,​n odd,n even.​​
(61)
If the phrase “delay time τ” instead denotes the complete M2-arm round-trip delay, replace 2τ by τ in the floor formula. The wording does not explicitly distinguish these conventions. Consecutive encounters can produce the same exit time in different ports: for example n=2,3 both follow one M2 visit. If the incident wavepacket were much longer than the arm delay, different histories in the same port would overlap and their probability amplitudes would have to be added before squaring; the asserted time-bin expansion would then not be an orthonormal event basis.
