In second-harmonic generation, the quadratic nonlinear polarization contains products of pairs of comb components. Their angular frequencies are
ωp​+ωq​=2ωce​+(p+q)Δω,
(10)
including 2ωp​ when p=q. Original comb components have angular frequencies ωr​=ωce​+rΔω, hence
(ωp​+ωq​)−ωr​=ωce​+(p+q−r)Δω.
(11)
When the doubled low-frequency region overlaps the original high-frequency region, choosing r=p+q gives the beat at ∣ωce​∣. More generally the nearest positive beat has angular frequency
ωbeat,min​=k∈Zmin​∣ωce​+kΔω∣,​
(12)
which is ∣ωce​∣ for the stipulated small offset. For 0≤ωce​<Δω it is min(ωce​,Δω−ωce​), provided the corresponding teeth are in the overlap.
In comb self-referencing, overlap the doubled red part and the original blue part on a photodiode. The beat measures the carrier-envelope offset frequency. Feedback on an intracavity dispersion control or pump setting can set ωce​=0 modulo Δω, giving ϕslip​=0(mod2π)​. A phase-sensitive lock or calibrated approach to zero resolves the sign ambiguity of an unsigned beat measurement.[ref]
The coherent optical spectrum must span an octave, ωmax​≳2ωmin​, with sufficient overlapping spectral strength after second-harmonic generation. This calls for very short broadband optical pulses, or coherent external spectral broadening. The two branches must preserve optical coherence, overlap in time and polarization, and have adequate phase matching; bandwidth alone does not guarantee a detectable beat.
