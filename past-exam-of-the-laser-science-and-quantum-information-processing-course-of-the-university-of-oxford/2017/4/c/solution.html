Use basis order 000,001,010,011,100,101,110,111, with the ancilla first, and put x=eiϕ1​, y=eiϕ2​. Initially the ancilla is at the north pole of the Bloch sphere. The other two qubits lie on its equator at azimuths ϕ1​,ϕ2​, with Bloch vectors (cosϕj​,sinϕj​,0).
The four dotted cuts, in left-to-right order, have the explicit normalized vectors
v0​=21​(1,y,x,xy,0,0,0,0)T,​
(31)
v1​=22​1​(1,y,x,xy,1,y,x,xy)T,​
(32)
v2​=22​1​(1,y,x,xy,1,x,y,xy)T,​
(33)
v3​=41​(2,x+y,x+y,2xy,0,y−x,x−y,0)T.​
(34)
The first Hadamard gate copies the lower-register amplitudes into two control branches; the Fredkin gate exchanges them only in the second branch; the final Hadamard gate adds and subtracts these branches.
Summing the squared magnitudes of the first four entries of v3​ gives
P0​=168+2∣x+y∣2​=43​+41​cos(ϕ1​−ϕ2​).
(35)
Meanwhile ⟨ϕ2​∣ϕ1​⟩=(1+ei(ϕ1​−ϕ2​))/2, so
P0​=21+∣⟨ϕ2​∣ϕ1​⟩∣2​.​
(36)
Identical input qubits give P0​=1; orthogonal equatorial inputs give P0​=1/2. The factor one half is an ancilla-interference effect, not a direct projective measurement of the lower qubits individually.
