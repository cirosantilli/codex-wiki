Free propagation is convolution with a chirp, KL​(x)=CL​eikLeikx2/(2L). The convolution theorem therefore turns it into multiplication by its Fourier transform. Complete the square:
2Lkx2​−qx=2Lk​(x−kLq​)2−2kLq2​.
(18)
The Gaussian integral then gives
E(q,L)=CL​k2πiL​​eikL−iLq2/(2k)E(q,0).
(19)
Thus the wavevector-dependent phase is
φ(k⊥​)=kL−2kLk⊥2​​,​
(20)
up to an overall phase convention; after removing the carrier it is −Lk⊥2​/(2k). This also follows from kz​=k2−k⊥2​​≃k−k⊥2​/(2k).
Normalization qualification: the source keeps a two-dimensional-style prefactor while integrating over only one transverse coordinate. With its literal CL​=ik/(2πL), there is an additional q-independent factor, rather than solely a unit-modulus multiplier. A properly normalized one-dimensional Fresnel propagator has CL​=k/(2πiL)​, which cancels the square-root factor above and makes propagation a pure Fourier-domain phase. For example, the literal source kernel multiplies a plane-wave Fourier mode by a factor of magnitude k/(2πL)​, not one. The spatially dependent phase and the proportional Fourier-transform results remain unchanged. [ref]
