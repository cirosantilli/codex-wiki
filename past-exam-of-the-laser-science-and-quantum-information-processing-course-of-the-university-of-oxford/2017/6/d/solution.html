The requested bare labels require n≥1. Their four energies are
Ee,n−1​=ℏω(n−1)+2ℏω0​​,Eg,n−1​=ℏω(n−1)−2ℏω0​​,
(52)
Ee,n​=ℏωn+2ℏω0​​,Eg,n​=ℏωn−2ℏω0​​.
(53)
Among these four, the pair that is fully present in one interacting sector is ∣e,n−1⟩,∣g,n⟩. Its center is C=ℏω(n−1/2) and its dressed branches are
E±,n−1​=C±2ℏ​(ω−ω0​)2+4g2n​.​
(54)
Their endpoint values at ω0​=0 and 2ω are the same, C±ℏω2+4g2n​/2. At ω0​=ω they are C±ℏ∣g∣n​. The avoided crossing therefore has resonant gap
E+,n−1​−E−,n−1​=2ℏ∣g∣n​.​
(55)
At the three horizontal-axis landmarks, the bare energies divided by ℏω are respectively
∣e,n−1⟩∣g,n−1⟩∣e,n⟩∣g,n⟩​ω0​/ω=0n−1n−1nn​1n−1/2n−3/2n+1/2n−1/2​2nn−2n+1n−1​​
(56)
which specifies the vertical-axis labeling without assigning an unstated numerical g. The original plot below uses illustrative n=3 and g/ω=0.08 and shows this complete central doublet together with all four bare lines.
Sector qualification: the outer displayed state ∣e,n⟩ couples to ∣g,n+1⟩, outside the requested bare set. Likewise ∣g,n−1⟩ couples to ∣e,n−2⟩ if n≥2; for n=1 it is the isolated ∣g,0⟩. Thus the four-state set cannot be diagonalized as a closed four-dimensional block. If branches associated with these outer states are also desired, complete those neighboring sectors and use the formula in part c with indices n and n−2.
Finally, “adjacent dressed states” within the shown resonant pair have the gap just boxed. Adjacent rungs of the dressed ladder are not equally spaced: at resonance,
E±,n​−E±,n−1​=ℏω±ℏ∣g∣(n+1​−n​).​
(57)
When neighboring doublets remain ordered, the gap between their nearest branches is ℏω−ℏ∣g∣(n+1​+n​). This distinguishes the within-doublet Rabi splitting from differences between adjacent excitation sectors.
