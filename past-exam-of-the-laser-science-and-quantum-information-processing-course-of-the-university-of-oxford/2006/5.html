For one input bit, a Boolean function is specified by the pair (f(0),f(1)), and k=f(0)+f(1) counts its satisfying assignments. In the ordered computational basis (∣0⟩,∣1⟩), the supplied phase oracle convention gives
Uf​∣x⟩=(−1)f(x)⊕1∣x⟩,Uf​=diag(2f(0)−1,2f(1)−1).
(1)
Here ⊕ is exclusive or: the oracle changes the sign of the inputs with f(x)=0. With I the identity matrix and Z the Pauli Z gate, all four cases are
f(x)0x1−x1​(f(0),f(1))(0,0)(0,1)(1,0)(1,1)​k0112​Uf​(−10​0−1​)=−I(−10​01​)=−Z(10​0−1​)=Z(10​01​)=I​​
(2)
The two middle cases are balanced Boolean functions; the others are constant functions. The specified zero-state sign change and the Hadamard gate are
U0​=(−10​01​)=−Z,H=2​1​(11​1−1​).​
(3)
Use the top qubit as the first tensor product factor. Write the controlled unitary gate as C(V)=∣0⟩⟨0∣⊗I+∣1⟩⟨1∣⊗V. Reading the bracketed quantum circuit from left to right, its four operations are C(Uf​), C(H), C(U0​), C(H). Thus the target operation in its control-one branch is
G=HU0​HUf​,C(G)=C(H)C(U0​)C(H)C(Uf​),
(4)
where the rightmost matrix acts first. The control-zero branch is the identity operator. The Hadamard conjugation of a controlled gate identity gives
C(H)C(U0​)C(H)=(I⊗H)C(U0​)(I⊗H).​
(5)
Indeed, on control zero the replacement applies HIH=I to the target, while on control one both sides apply HU0​H. By linearity, the identity holds on every quantum superposition and therefore in every repetition of the bracket. This replaces the two internal controlled Hadamard gates as a pair.
The global phase of a controlled quantum gate must be preserved: C(−I)=Z⊗I, whereas C(I)=I⊗I. Consequently the minus signs in U0​=−Z and the special phase oracles Uf​=−I,−Z cannot be discarded as an unobservable global phase of the complete quantum circuit.

Define the Hadamard basis states ∣±⟩=(∣0⟩±∣1⟩)/2​. The initial two Hadamard gates prepare ∣+⟩∣+⟩. For one bracket, the two control branches give
∣+⟩∣+⟩ ⟼2​∣0⟩∣+⟩+∣1⟩G∣+⟩​.
(6)
Using the Pauli X gate X=(01​10​), unitary conjugation by the Hadamard gate gives HU0​H=−X, so G=−XUf​. Keeping every sign, the target states along the control-one branch, in the exact gate order, are
f0x1−x1​Uf​∣+⟩−∣+⟩−∣−⟩∣−⟩∣+⟩​HUf​∣+⟩−∣0⟩−∣1⟩∣1⟩∣0⟩​U0​HUf​∣+⟩∣0⟩−∣1⟩∣1⟩−∣0⟩​G∣+⟩∣+⟩−∣−⟩∣−⟩−∣+⟩​HG∣+⟩∣0⟩−∣1⟩∣1⟩−∣0⟩​​
(7)
The last column also includes the final target Hadamard gate, which lies outside the bracket. Writing ∣χf​⟩=HG∣+⟩, applying the final top Hadamard gate gives the exact normalized two-qubit quantum state
∣Ψf​⟩=21​[∣0⟩(∣0⟩+∣χf​⟩)+∣1⟩(∣0⟩−∣χf​⟩)].
(8)
Thus the complete outputs, in top-then-bottom computational basis order, are
f0x1−x1​∣Ψf​⟩∣00⟩21​(∣00⟩−∣01⟩+∣10⟩+∣11⟩)21​(∣00⟩+∣01⟩+∣10⟩−∣11⟩)∣10⟩​⟨Z⊗I⟩+100−1​​​
(9)
For the first qubit, the Pauli matrix σz​=Z has expectation value P(0)−P(1) by the Born rule. The two k=1 cases each have P(0)=P(1)=1/2, while the k=0 and k=2 cases are deterministic. Equivalently, this Hadamard test measures
⟨Z⊗I⟩=Re⟨+∣G∣+⟩=1−k(r=1, n=1).
(10)
This explains the approximate quantum counting interpretation: repeated preparations estimate the expectation value, and hence k. A single top-qubit computational-basis measurement need not identify k.

For r brackets the control-zero branch still does nothing, and the control-one branch applies Gr to the target. Immediately before the final two Hadamard gates, the quantum state is
2​∣0⟩∣+⟩+∣1⟩Gr∣+⟩​.
(11)
For the constant function with k=0, the phase oracle is Uf​=−I, so G=(−X)(−I)=X. Since ∣+⟩ is an eigenstate of the Pauli X gate with eigenvalue +1, Gr∣+⟩=∣+⟩ for every integer r. The final two Hadamard gates therefore give
∣Ψk=0​(r)⟩=∣00⟩,⟨Z⊗I⟩=+1.​
(12)
In particular, the first qubit always ends in ∣0⟩.
For the constant function with k=2, Uf​=I and G=−X, so Gr∣+⟩=(−1)r∣+⟩. Thus the target remains in ∣+⟩, and the top qubit before its final Hadamard gate is (∣0⟩+(−1)r∣1⟩)/2​. The sign is a relative quantum phase between the control branches. The Hadamard gate converts it into the integer parity of r:
∣Ψk=2​(r)⟩=∣rmod2⟩⊗∣0⟩,⟨Z⊗I⟩=(−1)r.​
(13)
The first qubit is therefore ∣0⟩ for even r and ∣1⟩ for odd r, including all requested r>1.
