Define the Hadamard basis states ∣±⟩=(∣0⟩±∣1⟩)/2​. The initial two Hadamard gates prepare ∣+⟩∣+⟩. For one bracket, the two control branches give
∣+⟩∣+⟩ ⟼2​∣0⟩∣+⟩+∣1⟩G∣+⟩​.
(6)
Using the Pauli X gate X=(01​10​), unitary conjugation by the Hadamard gate gives HU0​H=−X, so G=−XUf​. Keeping every sign, the target states along the control-one branch, in the exact gate order, are
f0x1−x1​Uf​∣+⟩−∣+⟩−∣−⟩∣−⟩∣+⟩​HUf​∣+⟩−∣0⟩−∣1⟩∣1⟩∣0⟩​U0​HUf​∣+⟩∣0⟩−∣1⟩∣1⟩−∣0⟩​G∣+⟩∣+⟩−∣−⟩∣−⟩−∣+⟩​HG∣+⟩∣0⟩−∣1⟩∣1⟩−∣0⟩​​
(7)
The last column also includes the final target Hadamard gate, which lies outside the bracket. Writing ∣χf​⟩=HG∣+⟩, applying the final top Hadamard gate gives the exact normalized two-qubit quantum state
∣Ψf​⟩=21​[∣0⟩(∣0⟩+∣χf​⟩)+∣1⟩(∣0⟩−∣χf​⟩)].
(8)
Thus the complete outputs, in top-then-bottom computational basis order, are
f0x1−x1​∣Ψf​⟩∣00⟩21​(∣00⟩−∣01⟩+∣10⟩+∣11⟩)21​(∣00⟩+∣01⟩+∣10⟩−∣11⟩)∣10⟩​⟨Z⊗I⟩+100−1​​​
(9)
For the first qubit, the Pauli matrix σz​=Z has expectation value P(0)−P(1) by the Born rule. The two k=1 cases each have P(0)=P(1)=1/2, while the k=0 and k=2 cases are deterministic. Equivalently, this Hadamard test measures
⟨Z⊗I⟩=Re⟨+∣G∣+⟩=1−k(r=1, n=1).
(10)
This explains the approximate quantum counting interpretation: repeated preparations estimate the expectation value, and hence k. A single top-qubit computational-basis measurement need not identify k.
