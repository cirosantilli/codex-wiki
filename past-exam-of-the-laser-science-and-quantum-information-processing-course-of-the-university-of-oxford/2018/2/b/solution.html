Use the forward-slope convention established in part a, with x′=Ax+Bθ and θ′=Cx+Dθ, where AD−BC=1. Assume B=0. Solve for the endpoint slopes:
θ=Bx′−Ax​,θ′=BDx′−x​.
(14)
Fermat's principle makes the optical path stationary against variations at internal interfaces. Consequently only endpoint terms survive when its endpoints change. For optical path l(x,x′), these are
∂x∂l​=−θ=BAx−x′​,∂x′∂l​=θ′=BDx′−x​.
(15)
Integrate the first equation with respect to x, then match the second and impose l(0,0)=L0​. The resulting ABCD optical path generating function is
l(x,x′)=L0​+2BAx2+Dx′2−2xx′​.​
(16)
Thus the geometrical kernel defined in the source would have the form
K(x,x′)=L0​+(Ax2+Dx′2−2xx′)/(2B)exp{ik[L0​+(Ax2+Dx′2−2xx′)/(2B)]}​.​
(17)
The printed minus sign is inconsistent with forward propagation under these conventions. A concrete counterexample is free space: A=D=1, B=L>0, C=0. Its true geometrical path is
L2+(x′−x)2​=L+2L(x′−x)2​+O((x′−x)4/L3),
(18)
whereas the printed expression predicts a path shorter than L for nonzero transverse separation. For example, L=1m, x=0, x′=1mm gives 1m+0.5μm, rather than the printed 1m−0.5μm. Reversing the slope convention would also reverse the free-space B entry and must be done consistently; it cannot justify the minus sign with P(L) from part a.
The printed form can be recovered by explicitly using the opposite slope coordinate θ=−dx/dz. Then B=−B, free space has B=−L, and the same physical path is l=L0​−(Ax2+Dx′2−2xx′)/(2B). Thus the sign can be a convention choice, but the printed minus sign must not be combined with the positive-B free-space ray transfer matrix used here.
For physical field propagation, the paraxial diffraction kernel retains this quadratic optical-path phase while using the leading spreading amplitude. In one transverse dimension the normalized linear canonical transform is
E(x′)=iλB​eikL0​​∫exp[2Bik​(Ax2+Dx′2−2xx′)]E(x)dx,
(19)
with a consistent square-root branch and λ=2π/k. The source's prefactor and 1/l kernel are a schematic one-dimensional version of the Fresnel-Kirchhoff diffraction integral, not this normalized general-system propagator. The quadratic denominator is normally replaced by its leading value when making the Fresnel approximation. When B=0, propagation is an imaging limit rather than an ordinary integral with division by B.
