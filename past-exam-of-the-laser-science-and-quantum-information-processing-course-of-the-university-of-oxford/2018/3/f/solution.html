Four complementary classical features of a large-amplitude coherent state are:
  • It is an eigenstate of the annihilation operator, a∣z⟩=z∣z⟩. Normally ordered field amplitudes therefore behave like a classical complex amplitude; normalized photon subtraction leaves the same state.
  • Free harmonic evolution preserves its form, ∣z⟩→∣ze−iωt⟩. The mean electric field follows a classical sinusoidal wave with an amplitude and phase set by z.
  • Its photon number has Poisson distribution, with Δn/nˉ=1/nˉ​ and g(2)(0)=1. Relative intensity fluctuations become small at large amplitude.
  • Its optical quadratures X=(a+a†)/2 and P=(a−a†)/(2i) have minimum, balanced uncertainties, ΔX=ΔP=1/2 and ΔXΔP=1/4. The fixed vacuum-scale fluctuation becomes small relative to the large phase-space displacement.
These properties identify a particular classical oscillation, rather than a state of definite photon number. Absolute quantum fluctuations remain even when their relative size is small.
