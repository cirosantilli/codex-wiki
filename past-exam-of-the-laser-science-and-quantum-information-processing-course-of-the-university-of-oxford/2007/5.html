Represent the single-photon state by its two path probability amplitudes. The first Hadamard gate sends ∣0⟩ to (∣0⟩+∣1⟩)/2​. With P(ϕ)=diag(1,eiϕ) for the lower-arm phase shifter, the complete Mach-Zehnder interferometer gives
HP(ϕ)H∣0⟩=21+eiϕ​∣0⟩+21−eiϕ​∣1⟩=eiϕ/2(cos2ϕ​∣0⟩−isin2ϕ​∣1⟩).
(1)
The global phase has no effect on detection. By the Born rule,
P0​(ϕ)=cos22ϕ​,P1​(ϕ)=sin22ϕ​.​
(2)
They sum to one. At ϕ=0, port 1 is a dark port; at ϕ=π, the roles reverse. The result uses coherent addition of the path amplitudes, rather than addition of separate path probabilities.

Write Rz​(θ)=e−iθZ/2 and Rx​(θ)=e−iθX/2, using the Pauli operators. The Hadamard gate obeys HZH=X, so unitary conjugation of the matrix exponential gives HRz​(β)H=Rx​(β). Therefore the symmetric z-x-z beam splitter factorization is
Rz​(α)HRz​(β)HRz​(α)=Rz​(α)Rx​(β)Rz​(α)=(e−iαcos(β/2)−isin(β/2)​−isin(β/2)eiαcos(β/2)​).
(3)
The rightmost operator acts first. Each factor is unitary, which also proves the given matrix is unitary. Choosing
α=2π​,β=2π​​
(4)
gives UBS​=−iH, so it is exactly a Hadamard gate up to global phase.
For the specified identical splitters with α=0, put c=cos(β/2) and s=sin(β/2). Here UBS​=Rx​(β), and direct multiplication gives
UBS​P(ϕ)UBS​∣0⟩=(c2−eiϕs2)∣0⟩−ics(1+eiϕ)∣1⟩.
(5)
Thus the upper-port probability is
P0​=c4+s4−2c2s2cosϕ=1−sin2βcos22ϕ​,​
(6)
with P1​=sin2βcos2(ϕ/2). These identical beam splitters in a two-path interferometer give a nonconstant interference fringe whenever sinβ=0. With this matrix and port convention, two identical balanced splitters swap the bright and dark ports relative to the two-Hadamard model: at ϕ=0, Rx​(π/2)2=−iX. The reflection phases must remain in the calculation.
The upper-port maximum and minimum are 1 and cos2β. Hence the peak-to-peak modulation is sin2β and its normalized interferometric visibility is
V=Pmax​+Pmin​Pmax​−Pmin​​=1+cos2βsin2β​.
(7)
Both measures of contrast are maximal precisely for
β=2π​+kπ,k∈Z.​
(8)
These are balanced beam splitters, with equal single-input transmission and reflection probabilities. When β∈πZ, only one route contributes to the output and the fringes disappear.

Calibrate the two-Hadamard Mach-Zehnder interferometer so that the passive box leaves no relative wave phase or path disturbance. Then H2∣0⟩=∣0⟩, and port 1 is dark. A photon at port 1 is therefore impossible for the passive box under these assumptions.
For an active ideal detector in arm 1, the first Hadamard gate prepares (∣0⟩+∣1⟩)/2​. Absorption, which triggers the detector, has probability 1/2. On a no-absorption outcome, the surviving unnormalized state is ∣0⟩/2​. Applying the second Hadamard gives
H2​∣0⟩​=2∣0⟩+∣1⟩​.
(9)
Its squared norm is 1/2, the survival probability; normalizing it earlier and forgetting that probability would overcount successful trials. The unconditional outcomes for an active detector are
P(absorption)=21​,P(port 0)=41​,P(port 1)=41​.​
(10)
Thus a port-1 click identifies an active detector without triggering it, with single-pass success probability 1/4, conditional on testing an active device. A port-0 click is inconclusive. Among trials in which the photon survives, half are successful, but that conditional fraction is not the single-pass success probability.
This is the balanced Hadamard interaction-free measurement version of the Elitzur-Vaidman bomb tester. Conditioning on no absorption changes the quantum coherence available for recombination; a calibrated dark-port click reveals that change. An uncontrolled phase shift in the passive arm could also brighten the port, so the inference depends on the stated optical calibration. It does not assign an observed classical trajectory to the photon before detection. If the same active device is retested after inconclusive clicks, the repeated weak-rotation bomb-test efficiency gives eventual success (1/4)/(1−1/4)=1/3; the requested single pass remains 1/4.
