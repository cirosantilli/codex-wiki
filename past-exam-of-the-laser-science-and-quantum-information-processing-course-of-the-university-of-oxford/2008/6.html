For fixed nuclei and electronic environments, the individual NMR angular frequencies scale with the static magnetic field B0​: schematically ωi​=κi​B0​, where κi​ includes the gyromagnetic ratio and magnetic shielding. The NMR scalar coupling J in angular frequency units is approximately independent of B0​. Therefore a nonzero separation ∣Δ∣=∣κ1​−κ2​∣B0​ increases with magnetic field, improving the approximation. In particular, a fixed difference of chemical shifts gives a larger separation in hertz at higher magnetic field. If the two resonances remain exactly equal, increasing their common frequency does not suppress their exchange.

The three paired Pauli operators X⊗X, Y⊗Y, Z⊗Z commute pairwise: for example both products (X⊗X)(Y⊗Y) and (Y⊗Y)(X⊗X) equal −Z⊗Z. Thus PHr​P commutes with Hr​, and

The spin echo cancels the XX+YY exchange terms and retains the Ising coupling. Equivalently, over the total free-evolution time 2t it produces the effective Hamiltonian operator JZ⊗Z/2 exactly for ideal instantaneous gates. It is generally not the identity. A physical π rotation about z is e−iπZ/2=−iZ; using two such pulses instead of the ideal Z gates changes W only by a physically irrelevant global phase of minus one.
The implementation requires frequency-selective spin control. Under the usual common-radio frequency NMR assumptions, equal resonance frequencies give no way to apply a pulse to the first spin while leaving the second untouched. For equally coupled spins in a spatially uniform control field, the transverse control Hamiltonian operator has the form
Hc​(t)=2ux​(t)​(X⊗I+I⊗X)+2uy​(t)​(Y⊗I+I⊗Y).
(16)
Both this control and the drift Hamiltonian operator commute with the SWAP gate S. Every reachable time-evolution operator consequently commutes with S. But SPS=I⊗Z=P: the individual pulse required by the diagram fails this symmetry test. This is the exchange symmetry obstruction to individual spin control. Applying simultaneous Z gates gives Q=Z⊗Z, which commutes with Hr​ and instead yields QUQU=U2; it does not refocus the exchange. A common change of the RF phase reference also supplies only a collective rotation.
This obstruction assumes the usual common-RF control with no extra handle distinguishing the spins. Equal resonance frequencies do not universally forbid individual addressing: a spatially selective field, unequal control couplings, or another symmetry-breaking interaction could alter controllability. Such resources are absent from the specified equivalent-spin NMR model.

Quantum error correction encodes a logical qubit redundantly, obtains an error syndrome without measuring its unknown logical amplitudes, and applies a recovery that reverses the specified errors. A decoherence-free subspace instead uses a symmetry of a specified noise model: each noise operator acts as a scalar on the entire encoded space, so no syndrome-dependent recovery is required. This is passive protection and can also be viewed as a special case of quantum error correction.
For the two-spin decoherence-free encoding, choose
∣0L​⟩=∣01⟩,∣1L​⟩=∣10⟩,∣ψL​⟩=α∣01⟩+β∣10⟩,∣α∣2+∣β∣2=1.
(17)
Simultaneous Pauli Z gates give
(Z⊗Z)∣01⟩=−∣01⟩,(Z⊗Z)∣10⟩=−∣10⟩,
(18)
so
(Z⊗Z)∣ψL​⟩=−∣ψL​⟩.​
(19)
Both logical components acquire the same global phase; their relative phase and amplitudes survive. The density matrix is exactly unchanged, so even an unknown random choice between applying I and Z⊗Z causes no logical dephasing.
More strongly, the encoding protects against arbitrary collective dephasing:
[Rz​(ϕ)⊗Rz​(ϕ)]∣ψL​⟩=e−iϕ(Z⊗I+I⊗Z)/2∣ψL​⟩=∣ψL​⟩,
(20)
because both basis states have total Z eigenvalue zero. At ϕ=π, Rz​(π)⊗Rz​(π)=−Z⊗Z, consistent with the previous overall phase. The protection relies on correlated noise: independent Z rotations generally change the logical relative phase, and this two-spin code does not protect a whole logical qubit against arbitrary collective rotations about all three axes or against arbitrary single-spin errors.
