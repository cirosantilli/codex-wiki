A good atomic qubit needs two reproducible, orthogonal internal states whose populations and relative quantum phase survive for many gate times. Both should be long lived, with weak spontaneous emission and little decoherence from fluctuating fields or atomic motion. Their transition must nevertheless permit fast, selective, coherent control with little leakage to other states. Reliable initialization and state-selective quantum measurement are also required; optical pumping and fluorescence readout can supply these functions.
For rubidium-87, use the two ground state hyperfine levels in the 52S1/2​ electronic configuration. The electronic angular momentum is J=1/2, and addition of angular momentum to the given nuclear spin I=3/2 gives F=∣I−J∣,…,I+J=1,2. A suitable Rubidium-87 clock qubit is
∣0⟩=∣52S1/2​;F=1,mF​=0⟩,∣1⟩=∣52S1/2​;F=2,mF​=0⟩.​
(1)
These are coupled electron-nucleus angular momentum eigenstates, rather than two bare nuclear-spin projections. Their hyperfine structure supplies a resolvable splitting ν01​≃6.835GHz. Both belong to the electronic ground state, avoiding a short optical excited-state lifetime. Since the weak-field Zeeman shift is proportional to gF​mF​B, both have zero linear shift at B=0: their rubidium-87 clock transition suppresses first-order magnetic-field dephasing near zero field. A quadratic Zeeman shift of a clock transition and differential optical shifts remain. A small stable bias field can define the quantization axis and help resolve unwanted transitions, at the cost of some residual magnetic sensitivity.[ref]
Drive the magnetic dipole transition with a resonant microwave field of suitable polarization, here ΔmF​=0, or use two phase-coherent optical fields for a far-detuned stimulated Raman transition with their frequency difference resonant with ν01​. The optical excited state is then only virtually populated, reducing spontaneous emission. In the rotating frame and rotating-wave approximation, define the drive phase ϕ by the effective Hamiltonian
Hrot​=2ℏΩ​(cosϕX+sinϕY),Rϕ​(θ)=e−iθ(cosϕX+sinϕY)/2,θ=Ωt.
(2)
Here X,Y,Z are the Pauli matrices on the chosen qubit, and Ω is the Rabi angular frequency. Pulse duration controls the rotation gate angle, while phase selects the axis in the equatorial plane of the Bloch sphere. A π pulse exchanges the populations; a π/2 pulse makes an equal quantum superposition from either basis state. Controlled Rabi detuning, a differential energy shift, or compositions of these pulses give z rotations. For example, Rz​(α)Ry​(β)Rz​(γ) realizes any one-qubit unitary operator up to a global phase. Thus the atomic encoding admits arbitrary single-qubit quantum gates.
