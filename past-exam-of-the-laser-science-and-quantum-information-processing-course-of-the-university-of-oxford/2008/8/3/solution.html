Use the ordered computational basis (∣00⟩,∣01⟩,∣10⟩,∣11⟩), with qubit 1 written first. The half-time gate requires a branch convention. Even stationary Hamiltonian evolution with W(2τ)=SWAP does not uniquely specify W(τ): different accumulated eigenstate phases can agree at 2τ and differ at τ. In particular, both S and S† below square to SWAP gate and leave ∣00⟩,∣11⟩ unchanged. Adopt the conventional continuous isotropic exchange interaction branch, with a relative singlet phase increasing from 0 to π during the full pulse. This is the exchange square-root branch convention.

To obtain the standard CNOT gate controlled by qubit 1, append an X gate to qubit 2 and P to qubit 1. They commute, and
CNOT1→2​=P1​X2​U=P1​X2​H2​SZ1​SP2​H2​=​1000​0100​0001​0010​​.​

For control zero, X2​ undoes the original flip. For control one, it supplies the desired flip and P1​ cancels the phase because i(−i)=1. This phase-corrected exchange network for a CNOT gate realizes the standard gate exactly with the declared conventions.
Finally, S∣01⟩=(eiπ/4∣01⟩+e−iπ/4∣10⟩)/2​ has equal Schmidt coefficients and is maximally entangled: a half exchange creates quantum entanglement although a full SWAP gate does not. More constructively, the network above produces a CNOT gate from two half exchanges and local gates. Since CNOT gates and arbitrary single-qubit unitary operators form a universal quantum gate set, the square root of the SWAP gate and arbitrary single-qubit gates are universal. The alternative inverse exchange branch remains entangling and universal, but its phases and local correction must be recomputed consistently.
