The Bloch vectors of ∣a⟩ and ∣b⟩ are
ra​=(1/2​,0,1/2​),rb​=(1/2​,0,−1/2​).
(6)
They share the same x component and differ along z. The equal-prior discrimination of qubit states therefore selects the Z axis: it maximizes the separation of their projected outcome probabilities. This also explains why the X measurement in part 3 is uninformative.
For ∣0⟩ and ∣+⟩, the Bloch vectors are (0,0,1) and (1,0,0). Their difference points along n=(−1,0,1)/2​. Hence Carl W. Helstrom's Helstrom measurement for the zero and plus states measures
Mopt​=2​Z−X​,P±​=2I±Mopt​​.​
(7)
Guess ∣0⟩ after P+​ and ∣+⟩ after P−​. An explicit orthonormal basis for these projectors is
∣h+​⟩=cos(π/8)∣0⟩−sin(π/8)∣1⟩,∣h−​⟩=sin(π/8)∣0⟩+cos(π/8)∣1⟩.
(8)
The positive measurement axis is tilted by π/4 from +z towards −x on the Bloch sphere; the corresponding state-vector rotation angle is −π/8.
For completeness, if a projective measurement guesses 0 along a unit Bloch axis n, its average success is 1/2+(nz​−nx​)/4. The Cauchy-Schwarz inequality bounds nz​−nx​≤2​, with equality at the axis above. The Holevo–Helstrom theorem shows this optimum also holds among general POVMs. Thus
Psuccessopt​=21​(1+2​1​),Perroropt​=21​(1−2​1​).​
