A forward-biased p-n junction injects electrons from its n-type semiconductor and electron holes from its p-type semiconductor into an active region. A direct band gap allows radiative transitions without a compensating phonon, so stimulated emission can compete effectively with nonradiative processes. At thermal equilibrium there is one Fermi level; forward injection instead establishes separate electron and valence-electron quasi-Fermi levels Fc​ and Fv​. Sufficient injection makes conduction states more occupied than the corresponding valence states, producing optical gain.
Two parallel cleaved facets supply an optical resonator. At normal incidence their approximate intensity reflectance follows the Fresnel equations:
R=(n+1n−1​)2=0.3195.
(1)
A resonant normal mode grows when its round-trip amplification exceeds its losses. For length L, material optical gain g, overlap factor Γ, internal intensity loss αi​ and facet reflectances R1​,R2​,
R1​R2​e2(Γg−αi​)L≥1,Γg≥αi​+2L1​lnR1​R2​1​.​
(2)
The pump supplies the emitted energy; the facets provide feedback and let some output escape. Positive optical gain alone does not establish laser threshold.

For a vertical interband transition, the crystal momentum is effectively unchanged because the photon momentum is small. A stimulated downward transition needs an occupied upper state and an empty lower state, with factor fc​(E2​)[1−fv​(E1​)]; upward photon absorption has factor fv​(E1​)[1−fc​(E2​)]. The two matrix elements agree by microscopic reversibility. Their difference is therefore
fc​(1−fv​)−fv​(1−fc​)=fc​−fv​,α(ω)∝gj​(ω)[fc​(E2​)−fv​(E1​)].​
(3)
A common slowly varying transition-strength factor is implicit. Broadening replaces the sharp transition by a convolution; multiple valence bands contribute separate terms.
For one isotropic pair of parabolic energy bands, write
E2​=Ec​+2me∗​ℏ2k2​,E1​=Ev​−2mh∗​ℏ2k2​,mr​1​=me∗​1​+mh∗​1​.

fc​(E2​)>fv​(E1​)⟺E2​−Fc​<E1​−Fv​⟺Eg​<ℏω<Fc​−Fv​.​

A double heterostructure laser places a lower-band gap active layer between wider-band gap layers with suitable type-I band alignment. The band offsets confine injected electrons and electron holes in the same small volume, increasing their overlap and reducing escape. Usually the active layer also has larger refractive index, so the same sandwich forms an optical waveguide. Optical confinement increases the gain-overlap factor Γ, while carrier confinement lowers the injection needed to reach the required quasi-Fermi-level splitting. These two distinct mechanisms reduce threshold current and heating. A band gap difference alone does not establish the sign of both confinement barriers; suitable band offsets are essential.

For bulk electric-dipole second-harmonic generation, the crystal must lack inversion symmetry and have a nonzero relevant component of its second-order nonlinear optical susceptibility. It should transmit both pump and harmonic, tolerate the required intensity, and allow phase matching by suitable birefringence or another phase-compensation mechanism. For short times, survival under unitary time evolution is 1−(ΔH)2t2/ℏ2+O(t3). Dividing a fixed time into N intervals and projecting back after each gives survival approaching one as N→∞. This suppression of departure by repeated projective measurements is the quantum Zeno effect.
For measurement-guided Zeno transfer, rotate the measured basis gradually to transport a known state. Let
∣bj​⟩=cosθj​∣0⟩+sinθj​∣1⟩,∣bj⊥​⟩=−sinθj​∣0⟩+cosθj​∣1⟩,θj​=2Njπ​.
(36)
Measure in these orthonormal bases successively for j=1,…,N. Starting in ∣0⟩=∣b0​⟩, the probability to follow the bj​ outcome at every step is
Pfollow​=cos2N(2Nπ​)⟶1.​
(37)
Its final state is ∣bN​⟩=∣1⟩. If every outcome is retained without postselection, the final probability for ∣1⟩ is [1+cosN(π/N)]/2, also approaching one; histories with two switches can return to that branch. These two finite-N probabilities should not be confused.
For a general unknown input, the same slowly turning measurements first resolve its amplitudes into the two branches, then drag each branch separately. In the large-N limit, after discarding the records,
ρout​⟶∣α∣2∣1⟩⟨1∣+∣β∣2∣0⟩⟨0∣.​
(38)
The first basis approaches the computational one as N grows. Inserting an initial computational measurement makes this branch decomposition exact at the outset, without changing the limiting result. Relative phase is lost, so this procedure transports a known basis state with high probability rather than implementing a coherent Pauli X gate on an arbitrary qubit.

Quantum error correction detects an error syndrome and actively restores a logical state within a correctable error set. A decoherence-free subspace instead encodes into a subspace on which every allowed noise operator acts as the same scalar; no relative logical phase or mixing is produced by that noise. It relies on the specified correlation structure, and does not protect against arbitrary independent errors.
For collective rotations Rz​(ϕ)⊗Rz​(ϕ),
∣01⟩↦e−iϕ/2eiϕ/2∣01⟩=∣01⟩,∣10⟩↦∣10⟩.
(39)
Thus every encoded superposition α∣01⟩+β∣10⟩ is invariant. Equivalently it lies in the zero-eigenvalue subspace of Z⊗I+I⊗Z.
By contrast,
α∣00⟩+β∣11⟩↦αe−iϕ∣00⟩+βeiϕ∣11⟩.
(40)
The relative phase changes by 2ϕ, although Z⊗Z acts as +I on both basis states. Invariance under one discrete operation does not establish invariance under its continuous rotation family. A concrete counterexample is ϕ=π/2: the equally weighted encoded plus state becomes the encoded minus state, up to a global phase, and is orthogonal to its original state.

Use a classical target bit t and initialized auxiliary bits. The NOT gate maps t↦1⊕t, the CNOT gate maps (a,t)↦(a,t⊕a), and the Toffoli gate maps (a,b,t)↦(a,b,t⊕ab). They are bijections and are their own inverses. Explicit circuits with their retained input wires are:
  • AND: a Toffoli gate on (a,b,0) produces (a,b,ab).
  • OR: apply NOT to both controls, a Toffoli gate with target initialized to 1, then restore both controls. The target becomes 1⊕(1−a)(1−b)=a∨b.
  • CLONE: a CNOT gate on (a,0) gives (a,a).
  • SWAP: apply CNOTa→b​, CNOTb→a​ and CNOTa→b​. The successive pairs are (a,b⊕a), (b,b⊕a), then (b,a).
These maps also give the truth tables for all input combinations. AND and OR are not themselves reversible maps if the inputs are discarded; the retained inputs are what make their implementations reversible. Classical CLONE copies a known orthogonal computational basis alphabet; the no-cloning theorem forbids interpreting it as a copier of arbitrary quantum superpositions.
A Boolean circuit can express any finite Boolean function using AND, OR and NOT. Replace its operations by the reversible versions, keeping inputs and intermediate bits. Copy the final output into a fresh answer register with CNOT gates, then run the entire computing network backward. Intermediate auxiliary bits return to zero, yielding
(x,0,y)⟼(x,0,y⊕f(x)).​
(43)
This compute-copy-uncompute construction proves universality in the usual model with initialized auxiliary bits. If f is a reversible permutation, compute f(x), use a reversible implementation of f−1 to clear x, and swap registers, leaving f(x) with clean auxiliaries. The ancillary-resource qualification matters; a blanket claim about all permutations on an arbitrary fixed number of unassisted wires has parity restrictions.

In the oracle machine model, a function is accessed through a black box whose internal gate cost is excluded; the resource counted is the number of calls. A reversible classical oracle maps basis inputs as (x,y)↦(x,y⊕f(x)). Its quantum oracle extension applies the same permutation coherently to arbitrary superpositions, without measuring its input. A classical random mixture of inputs is not that coherent extension.
Write the two truth-table entries as f(0)=b, f(1)=a⊕b. Then f(x)=ax⊕b, with the four cases
(a,b)(0,0)(0,1)(1,0)(1,1)​f(0)0101​f(1)0110​f(x)01x1⊕x​​.
(44)
Two classical query circuits, (0,0)Uf​​(0,f(0)) and (1,0)Uf​​(1,f(1)), recover the whole function with certainty. One call fixes only one entry, leaving two compatible functions; hence two are necessary classically. Randomization cannot remove this obstruction to certainty.
The minimum for full exact function identification remains two even with coherent quantum queries. To see this, expand the oracle target into its X eigenstates: it is identity on ∣+⟩, and gives phases (−1)f(x) on ∣x⟩∣−⟩. Any one-query input, including an auxiliary system, has orthogonal sector weights w+​, w0​ and w1​ summing to one. The output overlap between functions differing only at 0 is 1−2w0​, so perfect distinction requires w0​=1/2; differing only at 1 likewise requires w1​=1/2. Then w+​=0, but functions differing at both entries have overlap 1−2(w0​+w1​)=−1. They are the same ray rather than distinguishable states. Thus one query cannot identify all four functions. The following one-query task recovers only whether the entries agree.
