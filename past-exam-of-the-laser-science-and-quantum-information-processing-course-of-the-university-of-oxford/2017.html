a OurBigBook.com
A laser relaxation oscillation is the damped exchange between stored upper-level excitation and intracavity photon number after a small perturbation of a lasing steady state. Extra photons increase stimulated depletion of the upper level; the resulting smaller gain then reduces the photon population. Pumping subsequently replenishes the inversion, so the two populations overshoot their equilibrium values before damping settles them.
Laser spiking is the corresponding large-signal sequence of intense output pulses, often during switch-on. Excitation first accumulates while the optical field is small. Once gain exceeds loss, rapid stimulated emission releases this stored energy in a spike, depleting the inversion below threshold. Recovery can produce further spikes. This is distinct from an externally imposed Q-switching cycle, although both draw on stored excitation.
Many solid-state gain media have upper-state lifetimes far longer than the cavity photon lifetime. The optical polarization relaxes rapidly, leaving the two slow rate-equation variables of a class-B laser. A long-lived population reservoir and a much faster photon response make underdamped recovery and pronounced startup spiking common. The criterion and frequency follow from the linearization in part c; they are not universal consequences of the word “solid-state”.

Place the input plane one focal length before a single positive lens and the output plane one focal length after it. P(f)L(f)P(f)=(10​f1​)(1−1/f​01​)(10​f1​)=(0−1/f​f0​).​

Alice knows her actual result, so her conditional description is a pure state. Her fidelity of quantum states with the initial state is p after outcome 0 or 1−p after outcome 1. Its outcome average is p2+(1−p)2.
Bob knows the preparation but not the outcome, so his density matrix is the nonselective postmeasurement state
ρB​=p∣0⟩⟨0∣+(1−p)∣1⟩⟨1∣,FB​=⟨ψ∣ρB​∣ψ⟩=p2+(1−p)2.​
(22)

ρC​=I/2,FC​=1/2.​
(23)

The two D states are metastable, so a quantum superposition can retain its relative phase much longer than one involving a rapidly decaying P state. In the stipulated transition diagram they have no allowed downward electric-dipole transition; real weaker decay channels limit, rather than eliminate, this lifetime advantage. Their distinct energies permit resolved state-selective optical control.
Both D levels couple to P3/2​. Apply coherent beams near 850 nm and 854 nm, detuned from that common intermediate level by a large Rabi detuning ΔR​. Absorption from one beam and stimulated emission into the other transfer population between the two D states without appreciably populating the short-lived intermediate level. Tune the frequency difference to their splitting, including differential AC Stark shifts. In the far-detuned limit,
Ωeff​≃2ΔR​Ω850​Ω854∗​​,PD5/2​​(t)=sin2(2∣Ωeff​∣t​)​
(25)
for an initially pure D3/2​ state on two-photon resonance. The controllable relative optical phase sets the rotation axis; the large Rabi detuning suppresses spontaneous-scattering errors. Suitable polarizations and a chosen pair of magnetic sublevels are required in an actual experiment.

Use state-dependent fluorescence. With 397 nm light on S1/2​↔P1/2​ and an 866 nm repumper on D3/2​↔P1/2​, an ion initially in D3/2​ is brought into a bright cycling manifold. The repumper prevents population trapping in D3/2​. An ion in D5/2​ remains dark while the 854 nm light is off, since it has no allowed link to this cycle. A detected bright or dark record therefore measures the qubit in its logical basis by electron shelving.
To reset, apply 854 nm light to empty the shelved state, and use 397 nm plus repumping for Doppler cooling and internal-state preparation. Doppler cooling alone generally does not reach the ground state. In the resolved-sideband and Lamb-Dicke regime, drive the red motional Raman sideband
∣D3/2​,n⟩⟶∣D5/2​,n−1⟩
(26)
and then optically repump to D3/2​ with little change in motional occupation. Each cycle removes approximately one vibrational quantum. The n=0 state is dark to this red sideband because its matrix element contains n​. Repeat with appropriate wavevector projections and frequency differences for all modes to be cooled, then optically pump into the selected D3/2​ sublevel. Small recoil during repumping is necessary for a high ground-state probability. [ref]

Wave plates and polarization phase shifters implement deterministic single-qubit unitaries: a suitable quarter-wave/half-wave/quarter-wave sequence realizes an arbitrary polarization rotation. A deterministic entangling gate on two independently encoded photons needs an effective interaction or additional resources that make one photon's logical action conditional on the other. For example, a sufficiently strong conditional phase followed by Hadamards on the target implements CNOT gate. Ordinary passive linear optics alone does not provide this deterministic interaction on independent polarization qubits. The practical difficulty is that optical nonlinearities at the single-photon level are weak and commonly accompanied by loss or noise; auxiliary photons, measurements and feed-forward trade determinism for resource overhead.
The polarizing beam splitter eigenpolarizations at angle α are the orthonormal states
∣Tα​⟩=cosα∣H⟩+sinα∣V⟩,∣Rα​⟩=−sinα∣H⟩+cosα∣V⟩.​
(38)
The first is perfectly transmitted and the second perfectly reflected, from either input port. Port-dependent reflection phases can be fixed by a consistent optical phase convention or compensated locally; they do not alter the transmitted/reflected basis.

A herald tells the experimenter when an entangled resource has actually been prepared while leaving the two resource photons available for subsequent operations. This permits synchronization, conditional storage or switching, and feed-forward. Direct down-conversion emission is probabilistic; inferring that a pair existed by destructively detecting both photons does not leave that same pair available as an input resource.
The second network uses passive optical elements, auxiliary entanglement and detection rather than a strong deterministic photon–photon CNOT gate interaction. Its smaller success rate can be acceptable if trials are inexpensive and successful events are reliably announced. Heralding is still probabilistic, not deterministic on demand; losses, dark counts and multiple-pair emission can give false or degraded heralds. The ideal 1/64 result assumes the specified input pairs and valid detection model, so it should not be confused with a complete laboratory production efficiency.
