Quantize a single electromagnetic normal mode of angular frequency ωc​ and represent one isolated atomic transition as a two-level system with raising operator σ+​=∣e⟩⟨g∣. The bare Hamiltonian operator is
H0​=ℏωc​a†a+2ℏωa​​σz​.
(62)
The first main approximation is the electric-dipole approximation: the field varies negligibly across an atom, kaatom​≪1, and the leading coupling is Hd​=−d⋅E(r0​). For states of definite opposite parity, the relevant dipole part is off-diagonal. After choosing operator phases, this gives Hd​=ℏg(a+a†)(σ+​+σ−​), with g fixed by the transition dipole and the one-photon field wave amplitude.
In the interaction picture, aσ+​ and a†σ−​ oscillate at the difference ωa​−ωc​, whereas aσ−​ and a†σ+​ oscillate at the sum ωa​+ωc​. The second main approximation is the rotating-wave approximation: near resonance and for gn+1​≪ωa​+ωc​, the latter terms average away on the interaction time scale. The resulting Jaynes-Cummings model has
HI​=ℏg(aσ+​+a†σ−​).​
(63)
The two-level system and single-normal mode truncations are additional assumptions: other atomic transitions and optical normal modes must be sufficiently far off resonance or weakly coupled. Dissipation is neglected over the interaction time. The retained terms exchange one atomic excitation for one photon, conserving a†a+σ+​σ−​.
