Put s=z−z0​. In the source's complex beam parameter convention,
q1​=s2+zR2​s+izR​​=R(z)1​+ikw(z)22​.
(1)
Substitution separates the transverse wave amplitude and phase:
U=s−izR​U0​ei(kz−ωt)​exp[−w(z)2ρ2​]exp[i2R(z)kρ2​].
(2)
Thus the Gaussian beam is circularly symmetric, has a Gaussian function transverse light intensity, and narrows to a beam waist at z0​ before spreading by diffraction. Its wavefront is planar at the beam waist, converging before it and diverging after it. The quadratic transverse phase identifies the signed radius of curvature:
R(z)=s+szR2​​,R(z0​)=∞.​
(3)
Define spot size as the wave amplitude 1/e radius, equivalently the light intensity 1/e2 radius. Then
w(z)2=kzR​2(s2+zR2​)​=w02​(1+zR2​s2​),w02​=k2zR​​=πλzR​​.​
(4)
The Rayleigh range is where w=2​w0​; the far-field beam divergence half-angle is λ/(πw0​). The factor 1/q also produces a longitudinal phase shift relative to a plane wave, changing by π across the beam waist; its sign here follows the given ei(kz−ωt) convention. The on-axis light intensity decreases as 1/(s2+zR2​) while transverse integration leaves the power constant. These are scalar paraxial approximation results.
