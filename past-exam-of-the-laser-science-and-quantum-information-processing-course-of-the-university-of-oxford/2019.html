a OurBigBook.com
Q-switching stores excitation in a pumped laser gain medium while preventing oscillation, then rapidly changes the optical cavity from high loss, or low quality factor, to low loss, or high quality factor. The initially suppressed stimulated field can then grow rapidly from a seed and deplete a large population inversion in a short pulse. The resulting peak power is much higher than for continuous operation at the same average pumping rate. This is energy storage and release, rather than the phase locking of many longitudinal modes used in mode locking.
An active implementation uses a Pockels cell and a polarizer. An applied voltage makes the cell change the intracavity polarization, causing the polarizer to introduce sufficient loss to prevent lasing while pumping builds the inversion. Rapidly changing the voltage removes that loss and opens the high-Q state. The switching time should be short relative to the pulse build-up and depletion times. A saturable absorber provides an alternative passive Q switch: its absorption decreases as the intracavity intensity rises.

a OurBigBook.com
For a paraxial Gaussian beam, choose the complex beam parameter with
q1​=Rb​1​−iπw2λ​,q=z+izR​,zR​>0.
(16)
Here Rb​ is the wavefront radius and w the intensity 1/e2 radius. The ABCD law is
qout​=Cqin​+DAqin​+B​.​
(17)
For a real, lossless round-trip ray transfer matrix M in free space, AD−BC=1. A reproducing cavity mode requires q=(Aq+B)/(Cq+D), hence
Cq2+(D−A)q−B=0,(D−A)2+4BC=(A+D)2−4.
(18)
Except for a scalar identity matrix, a finite physical solution requires a complex-conjugate root pair, one with positive imaginary part. The generic strict stability condition is therefore
−2<A+D<2.​
(19)
Equivalently, the ray eigenvalues are distinct unit-modulus numbers e±iμ, so repeated round trips do not cause unbounded ray excursions. Adequate mirror apertures and longitudinal resonance are also needed for a low-loss resonant mode.
The strict inequality has a degenerate exception relevant to this paper. If M=−I, then q′=q for every physical q even though A+D=−2. A symmetric confocal resonator has exactly this matrix and supports Gaussian modes; it is not excluded as a physical cavity. Thus the printed strict condition should be understood as the generic, nondegenerate stability criterion, not a universal necessary condition covering the confocal case in c.

Let ∣g⟩,∣e⟩ be the atomic levels, σ+​=∣e⟩⟨g∣, and a the annihilation operator of the single field mode. Choose the joint ground energy to be zero. The resonant Jaynes-Cummings model is
H=ℏωa†a+ℏω∣e⟩⟨e∣+ℏg(aσ+​+a†σ−​).​
(28)
The first term counts field photons, the second the atomic excitation, and the interaction exchanges one photon for one atomic excitation. A phase redefinition makes g real and positive. Resonance means ω=ωeg​; the total excitation N=a†a+∣e⟩⟨e∣ is conserved. Adding an overall constant gives the equivalent convention using σz​/2 and a zero-point field term.
The model follows from the electric-dipole interaction and the rotating-wave approximation, retaining the energy-conserving terms while dropping aσ−​ and a†σ+​. We assume a single quantized monochromatic mode and negligible cavity leakage and atomic relaxation over the time considered. With this convention, the resonant population Rabi angular frequency in the one-excitation subspace is 2g.

From b, the excited-state probability is
Pe​(t)=cos2(gt).​
(31)
For a single short measurement interval Δt beginning in ∣e,0⟩, requiring Pe​>0.9 before the first departure from unity gives
Δt<garccos0.9​​=g0.32175…​.​
(32)
A zero-photon outcome resets the conditional state to ∣e,0⟩. After n=T/Δt such tests, the probability of the all-zero-photon record is
Psurv​(T)=[cos2(gΔt)]n≃e−g2TΔt.
(33)
Thus a 90 percent survival requirement over a specified total duration T is stronger than the single-interval condition: approximately Δt<−ln(0.9)/(g2T) in the short-interval limit. The limiting preservation as Δt→0 is the quantum Zeno effect.
The measurement instrument matters. If photons are absorbed and their first detection removes the excitation, this survival probability also equals the unconditional excited population. For ideal nondestructive photon-number measurements, excitation can transfer back after a one-photon result; the unconditional result after n equal intervals is instead Pe(n)​=[1+cosn(2gΔt)]/2. It also tends to one for fixed T as the intervals shrink. Neither interpretation guarantees indefinite 90 percent survival merely by imposing a 90 percent bound on each separate interval.

Allow clean workspace bits, as in standard reversible computation. Boolean algebra expresses any Boolean function as XORs of ANDs and constants. A Toffoli gate maps (a,b,c) to (a,b,c⊕ab), so an initially zero target computes an AND without erasing the inputs. A CNOT gate accumulates XORs, and a NOT gate supplies complemented bits or constants. Intermediate products can be computed into workspace, XORed into an output, and then uncomputed by reversing the workspace computation. This realizes the reversible embedding (x,y)↦(x,y⊕f(x)) for any Boolean function and, with reversible clean-up, universal reversible classical computation.
The Fredkin transformation has control a and targets b,c. A network of three gates, shown in i, is
CNOTb→c​ ;Toffolia,c→b​ ;CNOTb→c​,​
(50)
where the sequence is executed left to right. After the first gate c1​=c⊕b; after the second b1​=b⊕a(c⊕b); and the last gives c2​=c⊕b⊕b1​. If a=0, (b1​,c2​)=(b,c); if a=1, (b1​,c2​)=(c,b). Thus the circuit is exactly a Fredkin gate.
The workspace qualification matters for the unrestricted universality claim. On four or more wires, every embedded NOT, CNOT and Toffoli gate is an even permutation of all computational states. Without ancillary wires they cannot implement, for example, a permutation swapping just 0000 and 0001 while fixing the other four-bit strings. Clean ancillas remove this obstruction on the designated input subspace.

In a small trapped-ion quantum computer, use two long-lived internal states of each ion, for example a pair of hyperfine ground states or a narrow optical transition, as ∣0⟩,∣1⟩. Electric trapping fields confine a short ion string, laser cooling reduces its motion, and optical pumping initializes the internal states. State-dependent fluorescence supplies readout: one qubit level participates in a bright cycling transition and the other is dark.
A resonant microwave, Raman or optical drive performs a single-qubit Rabi rotation. The drive phase fixes a rotation axis in the Bloch equatorial plane and its pulse area fixes the angle; detuning or phase-frame control supplies Z rotations. Individual spatial or frequency addressing selects the intended ion.
For a two-qubit gate, couple internal states to shared quantized vibrational modes. Resolved motional sidebands let one ion's spin influence the collective oscillator and then the second ion's spin. For example, bichromatic red- and blue-sideband drives produce a spin-dependent force; a closed motional phase-space trajectory returns the oscillator to its initial state while accumulating an entangling interaction proportional to Xi​Xj​. Together with single-qubit rotations this implements a CNOT. The short chain has well-separated normal-mode frequencies, and sideband cooling and the Lamb-Dicke regime suppress unwanted motion and make coherent bus control practical.

As a single trapped-ion chain grows, it has more collective vibrational modes with increasingly crowded frequencies. A drive intended to address one motional sideband can excite nearby modes, leaving unwanted spin-motion entanglement and crosstalk. Resolving the modes requires longer or more carefully shaped gates, while heating and frequency drift become more consequential. Thus the shared motional bus that is simple for a few ions becomes difficult to isolate and return cleanly to its initial state for large registers. This is a control and scaling limitation, rather than a failure of qubits or gates to exist.
