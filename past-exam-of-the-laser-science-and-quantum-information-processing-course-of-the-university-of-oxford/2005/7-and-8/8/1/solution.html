A universal quantum gate set can implement any unitary operator on any finite number of qubits, exactly when arbitrary continuous gate parameters are allowed, or to arbitrary accuracy with a suitable finite gate alphabet. More precisely, for every target V and ϵ>0 there is a finite quantum circuit W with minγ​∥W−eiγV∥<ϵ in operator norm. This ignores only an overall global phase of the complete operation. Universality lets a fixed collection of controllable elementary operations run arbitrary quantum algorithms rather than requiring a different physical interaction for each algorithm.
All single-qubit unitary gates, together with the controlled-NOT gate between selectable pairs, form an exact universal quantum gate set. One way to see sufficiency is to eliminate entries of a finite unitary matrix by two-level rotations between computational basis vectors; these rotations decompose into controlled single-qubit rotations, which in turn decompose into single-qubit gates and controlled-NOT gates. Single-qubit gates alone preserve product states and cannot supply general quantum entanglement. A finite approximate universal example is the Hadamard gate, the T gate T=diag(1,eiπ/4), and the controlled-NOT gate; finite exact words over a finite alphabet cannot represent every continuously parameterized unitary operator exactly.
Distinguish the Hamiltonian operator Hint​ from the Hadamard gate HHad​. For the printed collisional phase gate,
Hint​=Ω∣01⟩⟨01∣.
(14)
As written, Ω has units of energy. The Schrödinger equation gives
initial computational basis state∣00⟩∣01⟩∣10⟩∣11⟩​state after time t∣00⟩e−iΩt/ℏ∣01⟩∣10⟩∣11⟩​​
(15)
because only ∣01⟩ has a nonzero energy eigenvalue. In the ordered computational basis (00,01,10,11), define
Uϕ​=e−iHint​τ/ℏ=I+(e−iϕ−1)∣01⟩⟨01∣=diag(1,e−iϕ,1,1),ϕ=ℏΩτ​.​
(16)
If an interaction strength is instead quoted as an angular frequency Ωf​, the Hamiltonian operator is ℏΩf​∣01⟩⟨01∣ and ϕ=Ωf​τ. An alternative definition with e+iϕ would have ϕ=−Ωτ/ℏ. The minus sign above follows the usual time-evolution operator convention. At ϕ=π these conventions give the same Uπ​.
For the quantum circuit, use X on the first qubit to move the selective minus sign from 01 to 11:
(X⊗I)Uπ​(X⊗I)=diag(1,1,1,−1)=CZ.
(17)
Conjugating the target of this Controlled-Z gate by the Hadamard gate gives the required controlled-NOT gate:
CNOT1→2​=(I⊗HHad​)(X⊗I)Uπ​(X⊗I)(I⊗HHad​)=(X⊗HHad​)Uπ​(X⊗HHad​).​
(18)
The rightmost matrix acts first. Thus the network applies X1​,H2​, then Uπ​, then X1​,H2​, and maps ∣a,b⟩ to ∣a,b⊕a⟩, where ⊕ is exclusive or.
