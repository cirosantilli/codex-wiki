The reduced density matrix of either member of the initial pair has eigenvalues α,1−α. Since the joint state is pure, its Von Neumann entropy is zero. With logarithms to base two and h2​ the binary entropy function,
S(AB)=0,S(A)=S(B)=h2​(α),I(A:B)=2h2​(α).​
(43)
The two nonzero Schmidt coefficients show that this pure pair is entangled exactly when 0<α<1. Its mutual information rises from zero to two bits at α=1/2 and returns to zero.
For this known pure family, every positive mutual information signals entanglement. For an otherwise unspecified two-qubit state, positive mutual information can also be classical. The mutual information bound for separable states gives the stronger general witness I(A:B)>1 bit. On this curve it holds for approximately 0.110028<α<0.889972; the rest of the open interval is still entangled, but that generic one-bit witness alone would not certify it.
