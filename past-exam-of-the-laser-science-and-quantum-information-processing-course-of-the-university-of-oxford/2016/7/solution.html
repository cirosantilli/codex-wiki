Encode ∣0⟩=∣H⟩ and ∣1⟩=∣V⟩ in two orthogonal linear polarizations. Then ∣±⟩=(∣H⟩±∣V⟩)/2​ are linear polarizations at opposite forty-five-degree angles, while (∣H⟩±i∣V⟩)/2​ are the two circular polarizations. Their right/left names depend on the viewing convention. A half-wave plate with its axis at 22.5∘ to H implements H up to a common phase, since its Jones matrix has entries cos2θ,sin2θ and −cos2θ.
In principle, a nonlinear interaction producing eiπn1V​n2V​ supplies a deterministic Controlled-Z gate: only the joint VV component acquires a minus sign. A strongly coupled matter mediator can also provide the required conditional interaction. At optical single-photon energies the direct nonlinear interaction is weak; loss, unwanted noise and maintaining indistinguishable spatial and temporal modes make a high-fidelity deterministic gate difficult. Passive linear optics by itself does not supply this deterministic entangling interaction; measurement-based optical gates introduce additional resources and success conditions.

Define ∣sX​⟩=(∣0⟩+s∣1⟩)/2​ and ∣sY​⟩=(∣0⟩+is∣1⟩)/2​ for s=±1. In any of the XYY, YXY and YYX bases, direct overlaps give

∣ψ⟩=21​∑stu=−1​∣sX​tY​uY​⟩=21​∑stu=−1​∣sY​tX​uY​⟩=21​∑stu=−1​∣sY​tY​uX​⟩.

In each sum the four sign triples are (+,+,−),(+,−,+),(−,+,+),(−,−,−). They have equal probabilities 1/4. Thus two Y outcomes determine the X outcome with certainty: their product times the X outcome is −1.
In the XXX basis,
⟨sX​tX​uX​∣ψ⟩=41+stu​,∣ψ⟩=21​stu=+1∑​∣sX​tX​uX​⟩.​
(39)
The four allowed triples are (+,+,+),(+,−,−),(−,+,−),(−,−,+), so the XXX outcome product is +1.
The GHZ theorem follows without a statistical inequality. If local predetermined values Xi​,Yi​∈{±1} reproduced the three mixed-basis correlations, multiplying them would give
(X1​Y2​Y3​)(Y1​X2​Y3​)(Y1​Y2​X3​)=−1⟹X1​X2​X3​=−1,
(40)
because every Y value is squared. Quantum mechanics instead predicts the XXX product +1. This is an all-versus-nothing contradiction with local realism using ideal perfect correlations. Standard two-qubit Bell-state tests compare statistical correlations through a Bell inequality; the GHZ construction makes the inconsistency visible already at the level of these deterministic parity constraints.
