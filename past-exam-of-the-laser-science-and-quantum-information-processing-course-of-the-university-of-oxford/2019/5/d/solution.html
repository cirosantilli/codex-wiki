In a small trapped-ion quantum computer, use two long-lived internal states of each ion, for example a pair of hyperfine ground states or a narrow optical transition, as ∣0⟩,∣1⟩. Electric trapping fields confine a short ion string, laser cooling reduces its motion, and optical pumping initializes the internal states. State-dependent fluorescence supplies readout: one qubit level participates in a bright cycling transition and the other is dark.
A resonant microwave, Raman or optical drive performs a single-qubit Rabi rotation. The drive phase fixes a rotation axis in the Bloch equatorial plane and its pulse area fixes the angle; detuning or phase-frame control supplies Z rotations. Individual spatial or frequency addressing selects the intended ion.
For a two-qubit gate, couple internal states to shared quantized vibrational modes. Resolved motional sidebands let one ion's spin influence the collective oscillator and then the second ion's spin. For example, bichromatic red- and blue-sideband drives produce a spin-dependent force; a closed motional phase-space trajectory returns the oscillator to its initial state while accumulating an entangling interaction proportional to Xi​Xj​. Together with single-qubit rotations this implements a CNOT. The short chain has well-separated normal-mode frequencies, and sideband cooling and the Lamb-Dicke regime suppress unwanted motion and make coherent bus control practical.
