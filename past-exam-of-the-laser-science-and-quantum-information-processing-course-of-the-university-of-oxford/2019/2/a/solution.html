For a paraxial Gaussian beam, choose the complex beam parameter with
q1​=Rb​1​−iπw2λ​,q=z+izR​,zR​>0.
(16)
Here Rb​ is the wavefront radius and w the intensity 1/e2 radius. The ABCD law is
qout​=Cqin​+DAqin​+B​.​
(17)
For a real, lossless round-trip ray transfer matrix M in free space, AD−BC=1. A reproducing cavity mode requires q=(Aq+B)/(Cq+D), hence
Cq2+(D−A)q−B=0,(D−A)2+4BC=(A+D)2−4.
(18)
Except for a scalar identity matrix, a finite physical solution requires a complex-conjugate root pair, one with positive imaginary part. The generic strict stability condition is therefore
−2<A+D<2.​
(19)
Equivalently, the ray eigenvalues are distinct unit-modulus numbers e±iμ, so repeated round trips do not cause unbounded ray excursions. Adequate mirror apertures and longitudinal resonance are also needed for a low-loss resonant mode.
The strict inequality has a degenerate exception relevant to this paper. If M=−I, then q′=q for every physical q even though A+D=−2. A symmetric confocal resonator has exactly this matrix and supports Gaussian modes; it is not excluded as a physical cavity. Thus the printed strict condition should be understood as the generic, nondegenerate stability criterion, not a universal necessary condition covering the confocal case in c.
