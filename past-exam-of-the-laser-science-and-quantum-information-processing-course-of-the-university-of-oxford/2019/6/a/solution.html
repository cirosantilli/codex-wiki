The inverse relations are ∣H⟩=(∣H′⟩+∣V′⟩)/2​=(∣R⟩+∣L⟩)/2​ and ∣V⟩=(∣H′⟩−∣V′⟩)/2​=−i(∣R⟩−∣L⟩)/2​. The X states are linearly polarized at +45∘ and −45∘ to horizontal, while the Y states have equal orthogonal amplitudes in quadrature and hence opposite circular polarizations. We define R as the +Y state; descriptions as clockwise or anticlockwise must specify the observation direction and time convention.
A polarizing beam splitter and two single-photon detectors directly measure Z, routing H and V to separate detectors. For X, place a half-wave plate with its axis at 22.5∘ before that splitter; it implements a Hadamard transformation up to a global phase. For Y, first apply a quarter-wave retardance Q=diag(1,−i) in the H/V axes and then the same half-wave plate. The total analyzer transformation is HQ, with HQ∣R⟩=∣H⟩ and HQ∣L⟩=∣V⟩. The physical fast/slow-axis assignment is selected to realize this stated retardance.
