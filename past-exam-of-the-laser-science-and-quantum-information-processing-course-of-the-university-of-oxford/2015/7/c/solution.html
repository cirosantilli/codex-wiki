Use the same protocol: distribute one resource photon through C, and send Alice's two classical bits error-free in the D,A alphabet from part a. At p=1/2 the distributed resource is
ρ23​=21​∣Φ+⟩⟨Φ+∣+21​∣Ψ+⟩⟨Ψ+∣,∣Ψ+⟩=(I⊗X)∣Φ+⟩.
(52)
For the second component Bob has an extra X before his conditional correction. Conjugation by that correction changes X at most by a sign, which cancels in the density operator. This bit-flipped Bell resource teleportation therefore induces the bit-flip map,
ρtel​=21​∣ψ⟩⟨ψ∣+21​X∣ψ⟩⟨ψ∣X.
(53)
Using the squared pure-target quantum fidelity convention,
Fψ​=⟨ψ∣ρtel​∣ψ⟩=21​(1+∣⟨ψ∣X∣ψ⟩∣2)=21​(1+4[Re(α∗β)]2).​
(54)
The maximum is 1 precisely for the Pauli X eigenstates D,A. The minimum is 1/2 whenever Re(α∗β)=0, the great circle with zero x component of the Bloch vector, including H,V and both circular polarizations. Uniform averaging over pure qubits gives F=2/3.
The resource-distribution and classical-encoding assumptions are essential. If a perfect shared pair had already been supplied independently and this channel were used only for the robustly encoded classical bits, teleportation would remain perfect even at p=1/2. Conversely, unprotected H,V transmission of Alice's correction bits would add classical errors. The stated expression applies to the resource sent through the given channel and the error-free classical encoding explicitly available in part a.
