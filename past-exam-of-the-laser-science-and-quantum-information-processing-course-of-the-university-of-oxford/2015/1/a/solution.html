A forward-biased p-n junction injects electrons from its n-type semiconductor and electron holes from its p-type semiconductor into an active region. A direct band gap allows radiative transitions without a compensating phonon, so stimulated emission can compete effectively with nonradiative processes. At thermal equilibrium there is one Fermi level; forward injection instead establishes separate electron and valence-electron quasi-Fermi levels Fc​ and Fv​. Sufficient injection makes conduction states more occupied than the corresponding valence states, producing optical gain.
