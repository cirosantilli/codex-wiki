The second calculation uses the supplied 257nm indices as rounded harmonic data: the exact half-wavelength of 515nm is 257.5nm. The index dispersion between those two ultraviolet values was not supplied.
For an extraordinary wave, its Poynting vector makes an angle θS​ with the optic axis satisfying tanθS​=(no2​/ne2​)tanθ. Thus the spatial walk-off angle relative to the wave vector is
ρ=arctan(ne,2ω2​no,2ω2​​tanθ)−θ,ρ347​=1.601∘,ρ257.5​≃0.731∘.​
(30)
Both angles refer to the harmonic. The ordinary pump does not have this angular walk-off. For beam radius wb​, the approximate overlap length is wb​/tanρ, so the shorter-wavelength geometry has about 2.19 times the walk-off-limited interaction length.[ref]
To compare coupling, take propagation azimuth φ and ordinary unit polarization (−sinφ,cosφ,0). Its quadratic source is Pz​=−ϵ0​d36​E02​sin2φ. The optimal azimuth is 45∘. Here η=P2ω​/Pω​ in the small-conversion, equal-area model, with harmonic index phase-matched to the pump. Its smaller spatial walk-off further favors the 515nm pump in a walk-off-limited geometry. Equal input power alone does not determine an absolute efficiency ranking: ultraviolet absorption, wavelength-dependent d36​, beam size, crystal length and coatings are unspecified. For example, a sufficiently strongly absorbing 257.5nm output can reverse the ideal ranking. The stated preference is conditional on comparable transparent crystals and comparable focusing, not a prediction independent of those parameters.
