Write a=a1​a2​ and x=x1​x2​, with the top wire carrying x1​, the middle wire x2​, and the bottom wire the ancilla bit y. The binary inner product is a⋅x=a1​x1​⊕a2​x2​, where ⊕ is exclusive or. Let Cj→y​ denote a CNOT gate controlled by wire j and targeting y. The four linear Boolean functions and their Boolean quantum oracles are:
afa​(x)Reversible circuit for Ua​000I: three unchanged wires01x2​C2→y​10x1​C1→y​11x1​⊕x2​C2→y​C1→y​
The CNOT gates in the last row commute: their common target acquires the exclusive or of the two controls. Each input wire is preserved, and applying any of these reversible circuits twice restores y.
For classical reversible computation, call the unknown function oracle on ∣10⟩∣0⟩ and ∣01⟩∣0⟩. The resulting ancilla bits are respectively a1​ and a2​:
Ua​∣10⟩∣0⟩=∣10⟩∣a1​⟩,Ua​∣01⟩∣0⟩=∣01⟩∣a2​⟩.
(1)
Use a fresh zero ancilla bit for each call, or preserve the first result in workspace before restoring the target to zero. Thus two classical calls determine the two-bit string.
For n input bits, query the standard basis strings ej​, since a⋅ej​=aj​. This gives an upper bound of n calls. It is also optimal for exact identification: every classical query reveals at most one new bit, since x is unchanged and y⊕fa​(x) reveals just fa​(x) when y is known. A depth-k decision tree has at most 2k answer histories, but must distinguish 2n possible hidden strings. Hence k≥n, including adaptive strategies. The classical query complexity of linear Boolean function identification is therefore
D(n)=n(n≥1).​
(2)

Use the Hadamard basis states ∣±⟩=(∣0⟩±∣1⟩)/2​. The Hadamard gate satisfies H∣0⟩=∣+⟩, H∣1⟩=∣−⟩, and H2=I. After the first three Hadamard gates, the full quantum state is
∣ψ1​⟩=∣+⟩∣+⟩∣−⟩=21​(∣00⟩+∣01⟩+∣10⟩+∣11⟩)∣−⟩.
(3)
The Pauli X gate obeys X∣−⟩=−∣−⟩. Consequently the Boolean quantum oracle produces phase kickback:
Ua​∣x⟩∣−⟩=(−1)a⋅x∣x⟩∣−⟩.
(4)
Factoring out the ancilla qubit, the four states immediately after the function oracle are
∣ψ2,00​⟩∣ψ2,01​⟩∣ψ2,10​⟩∣ψ2,11​⟩​=21​(∣00⟩+∣01⟩+∣10⟩+∣11⟩)∣−⟩=∣++⟩∣−⟩,=21​(∣00⟩−∣01⟩+∣10⟩−∣11⟩)∣−⟩=∣+−⟩∣−⟩,=21​(∣00⟩+∣01⟩−∣10⟩−∣11⟩)∣−⟩=∣−+⟩∣−⟩,=21​(∣00⟩−∣01⟩−∣10⟩+∣11⟩)∣−⟩=∣−−⟩∣−⟩.​
(5)
The final Hadamard gates send ∣+⟩ to ∣0⟩ and ∣−⟩ to ∣1⟩. In particular, the printed quantum circuit includes a final Hadamard gate on the ancilla qubit too. Its full outputs, ordered as x1​,x2​,y, are
a00011011​∣ψout​⟩∣001⟩∣011⟩∣101⟩∣111⟩​​​
(6)
Thus one oracle call identifies a with certainty: a computational-basis measurement of the first two qubits returns a1​a2​. The ancilla qubit returns to ∣1⟩ and carries no additional information about a. The Bernstein-Vazirani algorithm turns the pattern of relative quantum phases into a definite computational-basis state.

The required identity is CNOT control reversal in the Hadamard basis. To derive it using standard quantum circuit identities, let CZ be the Controlled-Z gate, which multiplies ∣11⟩ by −1 and fixes the other computational-basis states. Since HXH=Z,
C1→2​=(I⊗H)CZ(I⊗H).
(7)
The Controlled-Z gate is symmetric in its two wires. Combining this symmetry with H2=I gives
(H⊗H)C1→2​(H⊗H)​=(H⊗I)CZ(H⊗I)=C2→1​​.​
(8)
This is an exact operator identity, including its global phase, and applies to arbitrary inputs. One can also check it directly from the orthogonal projectors Pb​=∣b⟩⟨b∣: conjugating C1→2​=P0​⊗I+P1​⊗X by H⊗H gives
2I+X​⊗I+2I−X​⊗Z=I⊗P0​+X⊗P1​=C2→1​.
(9)
For the three-wire quantum circuit, put W=H⊗3. Conjugation distributes over products because W2=I: WABW=(WAW)(WBW). Each CNOT gate in Ua​ therefore reverses direction, while the Hadamard gates on untouched wires cancel. The Hadamard-conjugated linear oracle is
WUa​W=Cy→2a2​​Cy→1a1​​.​
(10)
These are the simplified quantum circuits in the figure: for a=00 there are no gates; for a=01 there is Cy→2​; for a=10 there is Cy→1​; and for a=11 there are both. The ancilla qubit becomes their common control. On every computational-basis state, the entire simplified circuit acts as
∣x1​x2​⟩∣b⟩⟼∣x1​⊕ba1​, x2​⊕ba2​⟩∣b⟩.
(11)
Starting the data qubits at ∣00⟩, the prescribed computational basis preparation of the ancilla qubit must therefore be ∣1⟩: it activates the required flips and gives ∣a⟩∣1⟩. Starting it at ∣0⟩ instead gives ∣00⟩∣0⟩ for every a, so no information is obtained.
More generally, an initial ancilla qubit state α∣0⟩+β∣1⟩ in this same fixed quantum circuit gives
α∣00⟩∣0⟩+β∣a⟩∣1⟩.
(12)
For nonzero a, the desired data output has probability ∣β∣2, and exact identification for every a requires α=0. This condition refers to the displayed ancilla preparation for the Bernstein-Vazirani algorithm: the state entering the function oracle must be ∣−⟩, up to global phase. Other quantum state preparation procedures that deliver this same state are equivalent; ∣1⟩ is the prescribed input before the first Hadamard gate.
For arbitrary n, linearity of the linear Boolean function supplies
Ua​=∏j=1n​Cj→yaj​​,H⊗(n+1)Ua​H⊗(n+1)=∏j=1n​Cy→jaj​​.
(13)
This decomposition is for analysis: the Bernstein-Vazirani algorithm calls the unknown function oracle as one supplied operation. The common control starts at 1, so the simplified quantum circuit takes ∣0⟩⊗n∣1⟩ to ∣a⟩∣1⟩. Equivalently, phase kickback gives a product state
2n​1​∑x∈{0,1}n​(−1)a⋅x∣x⟩=⨂j=1n​2​∣0⟩+(−1)aj​∣1⟩​,
(14)
which the final Hadamard transform decodes to ∣a⟩. Thus one quantum oracle call suffices for every n≥1, compared with n classical calls. This counts function oracle uses; the surrounding Hadamard gates and final computational-basis measurement still act on n+1 and n qubits, respectively.
