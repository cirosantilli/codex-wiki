Power iteration (xk+1​=Axk​/∥Axk​∥2​)

 Home Mathematics Area of mathematics Analysis Numerical analysis Numerical linear algebra
OurBigBook.com Words: 1k Articles: 6
Power iteration finds a dominant eigenvalue and its eigenvector by repeatedly applying a matrix and rescaling the resulting vector. The essential idea is selective amplification: components along different eigenvectors grow at different rates, so one direction can eventually dominate. It is the computational form of the power method and is useful when applying a large sparse matrix to a vector is cheap but computing its whole spectral decomposition is expensive.
For a square matrix over the real numbers or complex numbers A, choose a unit vector x0​ and repeat
yk+1​=Axk​,xk+1​=∥yk+1​∥2​yk+1​​.
(22)
Here ∥⋅∥2​ is the Euclidean norm. If yk+1​=0, stop before division: the current vector is an eigenvector for the zero eigenvalue, and this iteration cannot continue. Otherwise, in exact arithmetic,
xk​=∥Akx0​∥2​Akx0​​.
(23)
Repeated normalization keeps the stored vectors at manageable size without changing their directions; it does not guarantee that an individual multiplication cannot exceed the range of floating-point arithmetic. The dominant eigenvalue means largest modulus, which need not mean largest signed real value.[ref]
