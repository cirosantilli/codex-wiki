Alternating sample and reference readings can suppress instrument drift. In this original four-reading model, measure objects A,B,B,A at equally spaced times 0,Δt,2Δt,3Δt. Assume the objects are stable during that short sequence and each reading is its object's length plus the same affine function of time, a+bt. Then
B−A​=2y1​+y2​−y0​−y3​​=LB​−LA​,
(4)
because both pairs have mean time 3Δt/2. The constant offset and linear drift cancel. With a further drift term ct2, direct substitution instead leaves bias −2cΔt2. A shorter sequence reduces that term, but neither this ordering nor averaging corrects unequal temperatures, sample changes or reference measurement uncertainty. This is a modern illustrative protocol; the historical account below establishes alternation, without attributing this particular four-reading sequence to Charles-Édouard Guillaume.
