A foehn wind is relatively warm, dry downslope air on a mountain's lee side. In the classical condensation pathway, ascending windward air expands and cools at the dry adiabatic lapse rate until the lifting condensation level. Condensation then releases latent heat, reducing the cooling rate to the moist adiabatic lapse rate. Atmospheric precipitation removes condensate, and the descending parcel warms by compression at approximately the dry rate. Its relative humidity falls.
For equal starting and finishing heights, complete condensate removal and a constant moist lapse rate above condensation height zc​, the precipitation-driven foehn warming is
Tlee​−Twindward​≃(Γd​−Γm​)(H−zc​)>0.​

Here H>zc​ is the parcel's maximum height and Γd​>Γm​. The warming is adiabatic compression during descent, not heating by friction. Without condensation or a difference in source air, a reversible dry parcel returning to its original height would regain its original temperature.
Precipitation is not essential to every foehn event. Isentropic foehn drawdown can bring air of higher potential temperature from aloft down the lee slope; turbulent mixing and extra solar heating beneath clear skies can also contribute.
