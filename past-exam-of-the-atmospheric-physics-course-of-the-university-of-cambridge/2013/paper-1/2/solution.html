Use spectral radiant exitance per unit wavelength. The Planck law is
Mλ​(T)=λ52πhc2​ehc/(λkB​T)−11​.
(4)
A hotter blackbody radiates more at every wavelength, its wavelength peak moves to shorter wavelengths according to Wien's displacement law, and its integrated flux rises as σT4 by the Stefan–Boltzmann law. The wavelength convention matters: a spectrum plotted per unit frequency has a different peak transformation.
For planetary equilibrium temperature, assume an isotropic blackbody star, a circular orbit, negligible internal heating, unit surface thermal emissivity and efficient global heat redistribution so the surface has one temperature. The received stellar irradiance is F∗​=σT∗4​(R∗​/a)2. A planet of radius Rp​ intercepts πRp2​F∗​ but radiates over 4πRp2​, giving the projected-area factor in planetary energy balance
T0​=T∗​2aR∗​​​=303.4K.​
(5)
Without the uniform-temperature assumption, this calculation gives ⟨T4⟩1/4, not a unique arithmetic mean of surface temperature.
With Bond albedo A=0.05, the absorbed fraction is 1−A. Hence
TA​=T0​(1−A)1/4=299.5K,​
(6)
a decrease of approximately 3.87K. This albedo weights incident stellar light and does not imply thermal emissivity 0.95.
Define the planetary effective emissivity by outgoing top-of-atmosphere flux Fout​=ϵeff​σTs4​. Maintaining the same absorbed stellar power with Ts​=312K gives
ϵeff​=(Ts​TA​​)4=0.849.​
(7)
The greenhouse effect allows thermal radiation to escape from colder layers, so the outgoing flux is less than a black surface at Ts​ would emit. Downward atmospheric emission contributes to the surface energy balance; this does not violate the top-of-atmosphere energy balance.[ref]
A single-layer greenhouse model makes the distinction quantitative. Take a shortwave-transparent, isothermal layer with infrared absorptivity and emissivity ϵa​, above a surface black in the thermal infrared. Writing Q=(1−A)F∗​/4, its surface and atmospheric balances are
Q+ϵa​σTa4​=σTs4​,ϵa​σTs4​=2ϵa​σTa4​.
(8)
For ϵa​>0, Ta4​=Ts4​/2. Radiation escaping to space is (1−ϵa​)σTs4​+ϵa​σTa4​, so
ϵeff​=1−2ϵa​​,ϵa​=0.301,Ta​=262.4K.​
(9)
Thus 0.849 is the planet's effective emissivity; 0.301 is the layer's infrared emissivity within this particular model. They are not interchangeable. Real atmospheres require wavelength-dependent absorption, a vertical temperature profile and nonradiative heat transport; the measured temperatures alone do not uniquely retrieve an atmospheric optical profile.
