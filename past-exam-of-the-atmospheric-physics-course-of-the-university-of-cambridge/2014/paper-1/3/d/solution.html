Use a uniform westerly Barotropic Rossby wave model on a local beta plane. Its dispersion is ω=Uk−βk/(k2+l2). A zonal wavenumber-two pattern at latitude ϕ has k2​=2/(acosϕ), so stationarity requires
U=k22​+l2β​,β=a2Ωcosϕ​,ϕ=45∘.​
(6)
For the zonally varying, meridionally uniform idealization l=0, this is U=Ωacos3ϕ/2. Meridional structure, deformation radius, mean-flow shear and full spherical geometry change the condition; the latitude and zonal wavenumber alone do not fix a unique observed jet speed.
For a shorter zonal perturbation n>2 with the same l=0 assumptions,
cn​=U(1−n24​)>0,cg,n​=U(1+n24​).​
(7)
Its phase travels eastward relative to the stationary long wave, but westward relative to the air at speed 4U/n2; its packet propagates downstream faster than the mean flow in this idealization. For example n=6 gives cn​=8U/9 and cg​=10U/9.
Within the meandering flow, poleward parcel motion lowers relative vorticity and equatorward motion raises it through Dζ/Dt≃−βv. Synoptic anomalies can reinforce or oppose the background ridges/troughs according to phase, exchange momentum with it, and transfer energy downstream. Linear superposition alone does not cause mutual growth; stronger interactions require shear, baroclinicity or nonlinear PV advection. No unique amplification rate follows from the supplied information.
