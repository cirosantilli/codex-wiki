Use a locally uniform eastward barotropic current on a beta plane. For horizontal wavevector (k,l), the Barotropic Rossby wave dispersion relation is
ω=Uk−k2+l2βk​,β=a2Ωcosϕ​,kn​=acosϕn​.
(12)
The stationary zonal-wavenumber-two Rossby-wave condition is therefore U=β/(k22​+l2)>0. For the usual assumption l=0,
U=k22​β​=2Ωacos3ϕ​≃82.5ms−1,eastward.​
(13)
Here a=6.4×106m is supplied; Ω=7.292115×10−5s−1 is an explicitly identified external nominal rotation constant, not an invented historical handbook value. [ref] The number assumes no meridional structure; a nonzero l or finite deformation radius changes the required speed. Zonal wavenumber alone does not fix those additional parameters.
For a shorter zonal disturbance with integer n>2 and l=0, the shorter Rossby-wave propagation inside a stationary long wave has
cphase​=U(1−n24​)>0,cgroup,x​=U(1+n24​)>U.​
(14)
Its intrinsic phase motion is westward relative to the air, but both the weather pattern and its packet move eastward relative to the ground. Packet energy propagates downstream faster than the phase, giving Rossby-wave downstream development: downstream systems can amplify as upstream systems decay. Disturbances also superpose and interfere. In the strictly linear uniform-flow model they do not directly scatter from one another; finite-amplitude eddies can exchange momentum and heat and modify the background. Their detailed nonlinear interaction requires amplitudes and geometry, not just a wavenumber. [ref]
