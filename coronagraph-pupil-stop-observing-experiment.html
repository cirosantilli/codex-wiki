Coronagraph pupil-stop observing experiment

 Home Physics Branches of physics Optics Coronagraph Lyot coronagraph
OurBigBook.com Words: 180
Schmid et al. tested the Lyot stop on sky using the close binary α Hydri with SPHERE/ZIMPOL at the Very Large Telescope. They kept the CLC-S-WF coronagraphic focal plane mask, the NB730 filter and pupil-stabilized imaging mode, and compared observations without a pupil stop and with stopB1_2. Each configuration used ten 8-second exposures. One camera imaged the optical pupil while the other simultaneously imaged the focal plane, connecting the blocked pupil features to the image improvement. [ref].
Bright rings occurred at the inner and outer pupil boundaries; the stop covered them and other unwanted features. To compare point spread functions, the authors multiplied the no-stop image by 0.68, accounting for lost off-axis transmission. The companion's reported detection significance rose from 5.2 to 9.7. This demonstrates a useful gain despite lost companion light. It measures the complete stop, including obstruction, support-vane and faulty-actuator blockers; it does not isolate outer-rim clipping alone or establish a universal suppression factor. The figures here are independent toys, not reproductions of those observations. [ref].
