Carothers equation (Xn​=1/(1−p))

 Home Chemistry Polymer Polymerization Step-growth polymerization
OurBigBook.com Words: 453 Articles: 1
The balanced Carothers equation follows immediately from linear step-growth molecule count and functional-group conversion in step growth:
Xn​=1−p1​.
(75)
The same Carothers equation extends to unequal group counts once the limiting-group convention is specified.[ref]
For an original balanced feed of 2000 monomer molecules, 1900, 1960, and 1990 intermolecular joins give p=0.95, 0.98, and 0.995. The remaining counts are 100, 40, and 10, hence number-average degree of polymerization values 20, 50, and 200. The last thirty joins quadruple the average even though they consume only another 1.5% of the initial groups of either type. These are exact synthetic counts, not experimental measurements.
The derivative dXn​/dp=(1−p)−2 explains the sensitivity: near p=0.99, an absolute conversion uncertainty of 0.001 changes the predicted degree of polymerization substantially. The exact balanced values at p=0.989 and 0.991 are approximately 90.9 and 111.1, instead of 100. An instrument reporting rounded “complete conversion” cannot distinguish those chain lengths. In a finite no-ring system, even one chain retains two ends; the maximum p is 1−1/N0​ and Xn​=N0​. The formal divergence at p=1 is a large-system limit, not an infinite chain in a finite flask.
