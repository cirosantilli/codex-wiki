Hazard function (h(t))

 Home Mathematics Area of mathematics Probability and statistics Survival analysis Survival function
OurBigBook.com Words: 928 Articles: 11
For a survival time with an absolutely continuous probability distribution, probability density function f, and survival function S, the hazard function is
h(t)=S(t)f(t)​=−S(t)S′(t)​
(4)
where S(t)>0, with the derivative identity holding almost everywhere. At points where the local limit exists, equivalently
h(t)=limΔ↓0​ΔP(t<T≤t+Δ∣T>t)​.
(5)
The hazard function measures an instantaneous conditional rate. If time is in years, its units are inverse years, and its value can exceed one. A probability for a short interval is approximately h(t)Δ, not h(t); for an absolutely continuous probability distribution, the probability of an occurrence at the single exact time t is zero. The discrete hazard, by contrast, is a conditional probability at a possible event time.
The factorization f(t)=h(t)S(t) distinguishes a probability density function for the original population from a hazard function for those still event-free. With an exponential distribution of rate 0.2 per year, h(5)=0.2 per year but f(5)=0.2e−1≈0.073576 per year: the latter also accounts for the probability of reaching year five. The exponential survival risk over an interval example computes finite-horizon probabilities from this rate.
