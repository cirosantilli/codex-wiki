Repeated-bath dialysis model (cn​/c0​=(Vs​/(Vs​+Vb​))n)

 Home Chemistry Biochemistry Dialysis (biochemistry)
OurBigBook.com Words: 470
Consider an original ideal model with sample volume Vs​, bath volume Vb​, and initial sample solute concentration c0​. Assume each volume is fixed, a freely permeant, nonbinding solute, no chemical reactions, complete mixing on each side, and equal concentrations at equilibrium. Each fresh bath initially contains none of this solute. The protein is perfectly retained in this model.
The first equilibration redistributes the initial amount of substance c0​Vs​ through both compartments. Conservation gives
c0​Vs​=c1​(Vs​+Vb​),c1​=c0​Vs​+Vb​Vs​​.
(1)
This denominator includes the sample itself. For the chosen Vs​=1mL and Vb​=9mL, c1​/c0​=1/10, rather than 1/9. Nine-tenths of the initial solute is in the bath and one-tenth remains in the sample; nothing has vanished during equilibration.
Discarding the equilibrated bath removes its solute. A second fresh bath starts with only c1​Vs​ of solute in the entire system, so the same argument applies again. Induction gives the geometric progression
q=Vs​+Vb​Vs​​,cn​=qcn−1​=c0​qn,c0​cn​​=10−n(Vs​=1, Vb​=9).
(2)
Here n counts completed equilibrations with fresh baths: n=1 is the first bath, n=2 follows one replacement, and n=3 follows two replacements. The normalized sample concentrations are respectively 0.1, 0.01 and 0.001. Leaving the first bath in place for longer cannot lower the ideal equilibrium value below 0.1.
The same conservation argument handles a fresh bath already containing a permeant solute at fixed concentration b:
cn​=Vs​+Vb​Vs​cn−1​+Vb​b​,cn​−b=q(cn−1​−b),cn​=b+(c0​−b)qn.
(3)
Thus the sample approaches b, not necessarily zero. For example, if a hypothetical new buffer component starts at zero in the sample and at b in every bath, its sample concentration reaches 0.9b, 0.99b and 0.999b in the three equilibrations. Outward removal and inward replacement follow the same mathematics.
Equilibrium fractions do not specify elapsed time. An illustrative well-mixed linear ordinary differential equation model within one bath is
Vs​dtdcs​​=−K(cs​−cb​),Vb​dtdcb​​=K(cs​−cb​),K>0.
(4)
Here K is an effective transfer coefficient with units of volume per time. Adding the two equations conserves Vs​cs​+Vb​cb​; subtracting their normalized forms gives
dtd(cs​−cb​)​=−K(Vs​1​+Vb​1​)(cs​−cb​).
(5)
The difference undergoes exponential decay. A bath replacement resets cb​, renewing the driving difference without making cs​ jump instantly. The plotted points are equilibrium endpoints, not a measured time course or a timetable for an arbitrary protein.
Real sample volume can change through osmosis; proteins can be lost through leakage or adsorption to the semipermeable membrane. Consequently, a measured decrease in protein concentration alone cannot distinguish dilution from loss of protein. Measuring recovered volume as well as protein concentration permits an estimate of recovered protein mass.[ref]
