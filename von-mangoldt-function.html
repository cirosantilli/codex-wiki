Von Mangoldt function (Λ(n))

 Home Mathematics Area of mathematics Number theory Arithmetic function
OurBigBook.com Words: 2k Articles: 17
The Von Mangoldt function assigns a natural logarithm weight to each positive power of a prime number. For a positive integer n,
Λ(n)={logp,0,​n=pk,p prime, k≥1,otherwise.​
(40)
Here and throughout the Chebyshev functions, log means the natural logarithm. In particular, Λ(1)=0: the exponent must be positive, even though the convention in prime power allows exponent zero. The support of a function of Λ therefore consists precisely of the prime powers greater than one. [ref]
For example, Λ(2)=Λ(4)=Λ(8)=log2 and Λ(3)=Λ(9)=log3, whereas Λ(6)=Λ(12)=0. A composite number can have a nonzero weight: 8 has just one distinct prime factor. But 12=22⋅3 has two distinct prime factors, so its weight is zero. At pk the weight is logp, not log(pk)=klogp. This choice lets successive powers record successive copies of the same prime factor in the Von Mangoldt divisor identity.
The Von Mangoldt function is useful because it connects two views of the prime numbers: the prime factorization of an individual integer, through a divisor sum, and the accumulated weights below a cutoff, through the Second Chebyshev function. The latter turns questions about the distribution of prime numbers into questions about a partial sum of an arithmetic function.
