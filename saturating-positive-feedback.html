Saturating positive feedback

Saturating positive feedback limits the reinforcing rate as activity increases. Here is an explicitly synthetic ordinary differential equation for normalized nonnegative input u, response x, and return strength β:
dtdx​=f(x;u,β)=u+1+x2βx2​−x,u≥0,β≥0.
(11)
The return is an increasing, nonproportional function of x, while removal is proportional to x. Time and activities are dimensionless variables. At x=0 the vector field points into the nonnegative domain, and dx/dt≤u+β−x prevents unbounded growth there. This makes the toy useful for asking about a bounded switch; it supplies no molecular mechanism for the chosen rate law.
At an equilibrium point, f=0. For a simple equilibrium point, the sign of the derivative
fx​=(1+x2)22βx​−1
(12)
determines local asymptotic stability. If β=1, the maximum reinforcing slope is 33​/8<1, so f decreases strictly: every u≥0 has one asymptotically stable equilibrium point despite positive feedback.
Three equilibrium points become possible only if the reinforcing slope exceeds removal's slope somewhere. Its maximum is 33​β/8, at x=1/3​. Consequently this toy requires
β>33​8​≃1.53960
(13)
for two distinct folds. This is a necessary threshold for a bistability interval in this toy, not a universal biochemical number. At a fold, both f=0 and fx​=0, giving
(1+x2)2=2βx,u=x−1+x2βx2​.
(14)
For β=1.8, the folds are at (x,u)≃(0.350003,0.153563) and (0.887240,0.094405). Between these input values there are two asymptotically stable equilibrium points separated by an unstable one. At u=0.12, the three activities are approximately 0.171331, 0.621230 and 1.127439; the low and high ones are stable.
A sufficiently slow increasing sweep follows the low branch to uon​≃0.153563 and then jumps upward. A decreasing sweep follows the high branch to uoff​≃0.094405 and then jumps downward: hysteresis. These are limiting quasistatic thresholds, with relaxation between input changes and negligible perturbations that could cause earlier switching. Finite sweep rates and fluctuations can alter observed thresholds. A switchlike response with only one stable equilibrium point does not have this equilibrium hysteresis mechanism.
