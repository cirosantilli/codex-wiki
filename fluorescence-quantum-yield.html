Fluorescence quantum yield (Φ)

 Home Physics Branches of physics Optics Physical optics Fluorescence
OurBigBook.com Words: 130
Fluorescence quantum yield counts emitted fluorescent photons per absorbed excitation photon. It differs from quantum efficiency, which describes the photodetector.[ref]
For an original constant-rate model, let kr​ be the radiative decay rate and knr​ the sum of competing rates without fluorescent photon emission. An initial population N0​ in the emitting excited state obeys the rate equation
dtdN​=−(kr​+knr​)N,N(t)=N0​e−t/τ,τ=kr​+knr​1​.
(2)
Integrating kr​N(t) over all time gives
Φ=N0​1​∫0∞​kr​N(t)dt=kr​+knr​kr​​=kr​τ.
(3)
Here every absorbed excitation is assumed to reach this emitting population; additional losses before that stage require another factor. Under steady weak excitation, absorbed photon rate Rabs​ and total detection fraction η give C=ηΦRabs​. The count rate therefore mixes excitation, yield, and detection, whereas τ specifies a decay timescale.
