Newtonian gravitational field (g)

 Home Physics Branches of physics Classical mechanics Newton's law of universal gravitation
OurBigBook.com Words: 7k Articles: 64
The Newtonian gravitational field is the gravitational force per unit mass of a test particle. For a point mass M at the origin,
g(r)=mF​=−r3GM​r,g(r)=∣g∣=r2GM​.
(10)
The test particle's mass cancels: different small masses have the same gravitational acceleration at the same position. In SI, the Newtonian gravitational field has units Nkg−1=ms−2. The same exterior expression holds for a spherically symmetric source, by the spherical shell theorem.
Choose a reference radius r0​>0, set g0​=GM/r02​, and use nondimensionalization with z=r/r0​. Then g/g0​=z−2: at z=1,2,4, the magnitudes are 1,1/4,1/16. This makes the spatial variation visible without specifying a planet. [ref]
