Velocity potential (u=∇ϕ)

 Home Physics Branches of physics Fluid mechanics
OurBigBook.com Words: 591 Articles: 9
Use a velocity potential to reconstruct an irrotational flow from one scalar field. A velocity potential ϕ(x,t) satisfies
u=∇ϕ.
(60)
It has units m2s−1 and is unchanged as a description of the velocity field when any function of time alone is added. For twice continuously differentiable ϕ, the identity curl of a gradient proves zero vorticity. In incompressible flow, taking the divergence also gives ∇2ϕ=0, the Laplace equation. This turns a three-component velocity field into a single harmonic function with appropriate boundary conditions. [ref]
For uniform flow u=(U,0,0), take ϕ=Ux. With U=2 ms−1, the change from x=0 to x=3 m is Δϕ=6 m2s−1. By the fundamental theorem for line integrals, the line integral of velocity between these points equals that change along any path in the domain; a closed curve gives zero circulation.
For the potential line vortex, choose a local angle branch in polar coordinates and set ϕ=Kθ. Then ur​=∂r​ϕ=0 and uθ​=r−1∂θ​ϕ=K/r. One counterclockwise turn changes the continued velocity potential by 2πK, although the velocity field returns to its original value. With K=1 m2s−1, this change is 2π m2s−1, exactly the circulation. A full annulus therefore admits local angle branches and a multivalued velocity potential, but no global single-valued velocity potential when K=0. Cutting the annulus along a radial line allows a single-valued angle branch on the remaining domain. The obstruction is path independence, not a failure of the local gradient formula.
