Right censoring

 Home Mathematics Area of mathematics Probability and statistics Survival analysis Censoring (statistics)
OurBigBook.com Words: 154
An unobserved survival time is right-censored when it is known to exceed the last event-free follow-up time. For a potential survival time T and potential observation-ending time C, record
Y=min(T,C),δ=1{T≤C}​.
(13)
When δ=1, the occurrence was observed at Y; when δ=0, only T>Y=C is known. An intact machine at the study's closing date and an event-free subject who stops attending follow-up can both yield right censoring, but the reasons for stopping matter for independent censoring. A subject right-censored at year two has contributed two years to person-time at risk and belongs to earlier risk sets. Neither recording a failure at year two nor discarding that subject preserves this information.
Under independent censoring and separate parameters for the observation mechanism, the part of the survival likelihood involving an absolutely continuous probability distribution for T is f(Y)δS(Y)1−δ. Thus a right-censored observation contributes the survival function S(Y), rather than the event probability density function f(Y). See Lu Tian's Stanford notes, Section 2.1, equations (2.1)–(2.2). The Kaplan–Meier six-subject cohort shows exactly how this distinction changes the calculation.
