Offset-adjusted cumulative hazard estimator (H1​=∫YdN−∑i​Yi​h0i​dt​)

 Home Mathematics Area of mathematics Probability and statistics Survival analysis Nelson–Aalen estimator
OurBigBook.com Words: 101
Suppose individual event hazards have the additive form hi​=h0i​+h1​, where h0i​ is known and h1​ is common. The aggregate counting-process intensity in survival analysis is λ=∑i​Yi​h0i​+Yh1​, with Y=∑i​Yi​ the current risk set size. Writing dN=λdt+dM and dividing by Y gives
H1​(t)=∫0t​Y1{Y>0}​​dN−∫0t​1{Y>0}​Y∑i​Yi​h0i​​du=∫0t​1{Y>0}​h1​du+∫0t​Y1{Y>0}​​dM.
(15)
The last term is a counting-process martingale integral. This establishes the cumulative common-hazard target on the observed at-risk time range. The known offset must be averaged over the current risk set, not the initial sample. Between event jumps, its subtraction can make the unconstrained estimate decrease. If all known hazards equal h0​ and Y>0 up to t, the estimate reduces to the Nelson–Aalen estimator minus H0​(t).
