Independently of regularity, positivity necessarily implies the ordinary Legendre necessary condition P≥0. If P<0 on an interval, localized rapidly oscillating test functions have a negative derivative contribution of order frequency squared, dominating the bounded zero-order term. This contradicts positivity.
The assumptions stated here do not themselves impose P>0. That strengthened condition is a regularity hypothesis for the familiar Jacobi criterion, and is not necessary for strict positivity: the integrand f=u2 gives q(φ)=2∫φ2>0 with P=0. For a criterion covering the full possibly degenerate class, use Regularized Jacobi criterion for a degenerate second variation. The following conditions are necessary and sufficient:
  • P≥0;
  • for every η>0, the regular equation −((P+η)w′)′+Qw=0 has no conjugate point to a in (a,b];
  • the original weak Jacobi boundary problem has no nonzero Lipschitz solution with zero endpoint values.
These conditions are necessary because q>0 gives qη​(φ)=q(φ)+η∫φ′2>0, so the regular criterion just proved applies; a weak Jacobi solution tested against itself would give q=0. They are sufficient as well: the regular criterion gives qη​>0 for every η>0, hence q≥0 by letting η↓0. If q(φ)=0, nonnegativity of q(φ+tψ) for every real t forces its mixed bilinear term to vanish for every test ψ. This is precisely the weak Jacobi equation. The third condition then forces φ=0. Thus the criterion treats degeneracy without silently assuming the strengthened condition.
