A convenient form of the Tonelli existence theorem is: on a finite interval with prescribed endpoint values, if a continuous integrand F(x,z,v) is convex in v and satisfies F(x,z,v)≥c∣v∣p−C for some p>1, then, provided a finite-energy admissible competitor exists, its integral attains a minimum in the corresponding W1,p class. The lower bound may more generally subtract an integrable function of x. Compact one-dimensional Sobolev embedding controls z, while convexity gives weak lower semicontinuity in the derivative.
Here p=4/3 and Fε​=(z4−x)2+ε∣v∣4/3 is continuous, convex in v, and bounded below by ε∣v∣4/3. The competitor u(x)=x has finite energy. Thus for every ε>0 the Tonelli existence theorem gives an admissible minimizer in W1,4/3(0,1).
Any finite-energy member of the larger stated W1,1 class necessarily has derivative in L4/3 and bounded function values by absolute continuity; it therefore belongs to W1,4/3. All other competitors have infinite energy. Hence the same function uε​ is a global minimizer in the full stated class.
