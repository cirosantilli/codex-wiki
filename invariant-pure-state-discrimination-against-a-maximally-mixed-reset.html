Invariant-pure-state discrimination against a maximally mixed reset (Psucc​=1−1/(2d))

 Home Physics Branches of physics Quantum theory Quantum information theory Quantum state discrimination
OurBigBook.com Words: 134
Suppose one equally likely action returns a known normalized pure state ∣s⟩ unchanged, while another returns the maximally mixed state I/d. A basic measurement in any orthonormal basis containing ∣s⟩ distinguishes them with success 1−1/(2d): guess the pure-state action on its basis label and the reset action on the remaining d−1 labels. The first action succeeds with probability one and the second with probability (d−1)/d.
The Holevo–Helstrom theorem also proves optimality for these returned states. The Hermitian difference ∣s⟩⟨s∣−I/d has eigenvalues 1−1/d and −1/d with multiplicity d−1, hence trace norm 2(1−1/d). Its optimal equal-prior discrimination success is 1/2+∥ρ0​−ρ1​∥1​/4. Random computational-basis permutations fix the uniform superposition, so they supply an example of the invariant action. This compares the returned states in the stated unassisted protocol; it makes no assertion about a more general channel-discrimination problem with an entangled ancillary probe.[ref]
