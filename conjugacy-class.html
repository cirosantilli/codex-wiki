Conjugacy class (ClG​(x))

 ... Mathematics Area of mathematics Algebra Group theory Group action Conjugation action
OurBigBook.com Words: 1k Articles: 13
For an element x of a group G, its conjugacy class is its orbit of a group action under the conjugation action:
ClG​(x)={gxg−1:g∈G}.
(17)
Thus conjugate group elements describe the same operation after a change of labels. In a symmetric group, conjugation renames the entries of every permutation cycle:
g(a1​a2​…ak​)g−1=(g(a1​)g(a2​)…g(ak​)).
(18)
For example, with g=(123) and the transposition t=(12), this gives gtg−1=(g(1)g(2))=(23). Products use function composition of permutations, with the rightmost factor applied first. The complete six-element example is conjugacy classes of the symmetric group on three letters.
The conjugacy classes form a partition of a set of G, since conjugacy is an equivalence relation. In a symmetric group, two permutations lie in the same conjugacy class precisely when they have the same cycle type: renaming preserves the lengths of permutation cycles, and matching equal-length permutation cycles constructs a renaming in the other direction. This compresses many labelled permutations into a few structural types. See McKernan, Conjugation in Sn, lemma 6.2 and proposition 6.6.
The stabilizer subgroup of x under the conjugation action is its centralizer CG​(x). Consequently the orbit-stabilizer theorem gives
∣ClG​(x)∣=[G:CG​(x)],∣ClG​(x)∣=∣CG​(x)∣∣G∣​if G is finite.
(19)
The counting mechanism is the bijection gCG​(x)↦gxg−1 from left cosets onto the conjugacy class: two choices of g give the same result exactly when they differ on the right by an element of the centralizer. A large centralizer means many choices of labels leave the operation unchanged, hence a small conjugacy class. A conjugacy class has size one precisely when its element belongs to the center of a group. The class equation counts these orbits of a group action together.
