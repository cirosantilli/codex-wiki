Tidal locking

 Home Physics Branches of physics Astrophysics Planetary science Planetary system dynamics
OurBigBook.com Words: 1k Articles: 6
Tidal locking settles a body's spin into a sustained relation with its orbital motion through tidal forces and tidal dissipation. The simplest case is synchronous rotation, a 1:1 spin-orbit resonance. On an ideal circular orbit, with spin axis perpendicular to the orbital plane, the body keeps the same face toward its companion, as the Moon approximately does toward Earth. [ref].
This article retains the broader sense of tidal locking as capture into a spin-orbit resonance; the term is also commonly used specifically for synchronous rotation. Tidally evolved rotation need not be 1:1: Mercury has a 3:2 spin-orbit resonance with the Sun, turning three times in two orbital periods. The distinction matters when inferring illumination or climate from a spin period. [ref].
