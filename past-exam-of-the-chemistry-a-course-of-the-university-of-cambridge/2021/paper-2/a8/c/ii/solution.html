For a frozen ortho-para mixture, the mole fractions do not change during the heat measurement, and the molar heat capacity is the composition-weighted average. A high-temperature composition retained on cooling has xo​=3/4, xp​=1/4, so
RCV,mrot​​=41​(1.425284)+43​(0.252645)≃0.54581,CV,m​≃2.04581R.​
(25)
For a mixture that continues to interconvert and remain in equilibrium near the specified temperature, use the combined rotational sum and differentiate that sum:
qeq​=qeven​+3qodd​=4.075,qeq′​=0.02668 K−1,qeq′′​=−5.66×10−5 K−2.
(26)
Consequently
RCV,m,eqrot​​=4.07522500(−5.66×10−5)​−22500(4.0750.02668​)2+4.075300(0.02668)​≃0.68716,CV,m,eq​≃2.18716R.​
(27)
The equilibrium composition itself is K=3(0.970)/1.165≃2.4979, giving xo​≃0.71411. Its changing composition supplies a further positive heat capacity: warming converts some lower-energy para molecules into higher-energy ortho molecules. One can isolate this term as
RCV,m,eqrot​​=xp​RCV,m,prot​​+xo​RCV,m,orot​​+T2xp​xo​(qodd​qodd′​​−qeven​qeven′​​)2.
(28)
For clarity, if a mixture is first equilibrated at this temperature and then its composition is frozen during measurement, the conversion term is absent: its rotational value is 0.58789R and its total value is 2.08789R. This is distinct from the equilibrium heat capacity of interconverting states even though the instantaneous composition is the same. An experiment must specify conversion activity and equilibration time; compare sufficiently slow temperature steps with a conversion catalyst against a composition-preserving sample, while keeping the same thermal background and checking that the sample has reached the stated temperature.
