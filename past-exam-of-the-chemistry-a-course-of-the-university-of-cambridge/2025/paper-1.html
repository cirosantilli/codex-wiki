Take Vψ=V(x)ψ with real V, and T=−(ℏ2/2m)d2/dx2. The operator domain matters: use sufficiently smooth wavefunctions for which the following boundary terms vanish at both infinities. A real multiplication operator gives
⟨f,Vg⟩=∫f∗Vgdx=∫(Vf)∗gdx=⟨Vf,g⟩.
(1)
Two applications of integration by parts give
⟨f,Tg⟩−⟨Tf,g⟩=−2mℏ2​[f∗g′−f′∗g]−∞∞​=0.
(2)
Thus the potential energy and kinetic energy operators obey the stated Hermitian operator identity on this common domain. For D=d/dx, one application of integration by parts instead gives
⟨f,Dg⟩=[f∗g]−∞∞​−⟨Df,g⟩=−⟨Df,g⟩.
(3)
The derivative is anti-Hermitian, rather than Hermitian; the minus sign cannot generally be removed. Multiplication by −iℏ makes the corresponding momentum operator Hermitian. These integrations establish the requested symmetry identities; full self-adjointness of an unbounded operator additionally requires equality of its domain with its adjoint's domain.

The quantum variational principle states that the energy expectation of an admissible trial wavefunction is at least the ground state energy:
⟨f,f⟩⟨f,Hf⟩​≥E0​.​
(10)
For the discrete, complete orthonormal basis assumed here, expand in Hamiltonian operator eigenfunctions, f=∑n​cn​ϕn​, with Hϕn​=En​ϕn​ and En​≥E0​. Then
⟨f,f⟩⟨f,Hf⟩​−E0​=∑n​∣cn​∣2∑n​∣cn​∣2(En​−E0​)​≥0.
(11)
Every summand is nonnegative. Equality holds precisely when all occupied components lie in the ground eigenspace. Thus minimizing trial energies supplies upper bounds on E0​, with exact equality if the trial family contains a ground state.

Let a=ϕ1s​ and b=ϕ2s​ be orthonormal atomic orbitals. The normalized spatial factors are
Φ±​(1,2)=2​a(1)b(2)±b(1)a(2)​.
(13)

The 3d and 4s atomic orbitals of chromium are close enough in energy that electron-electron terms can overturn the simple filling order. The configuration [Ar]3d54s1 permits five singly occupied 3d orbitals and a parallel-spin 4s electron. Relative to 3d44s2, it removes the 4s pair and permits more parallel-spin exchange interactions, lowering the Coulomb-and-exchange contribution enough to offset the orbital promotion cost.
As qualitative bookkeeping, six parallel electron spins give (26​)=15 same-spin pairs, compared with (25​)=10 in high-spin 3d44s2. The individual exchange integrals are unequal, so this is an explanation of the tendency, rather than a quantitative atomic energy calculation. The observed ground state configuration is [Ar]3d54s1​.[ref]

A hot band starts from a thermally populated vibrationally excited state, for example v=1→2. It can satisfy Δv=1 and need not have a small transition moment, but its lower-state population is reduced by the Boltzmann distribution:
N0​N1​​≃exp[−(E1​−E0​)/(kB​T)].
(23)
It is therefore usually weak at low temperature and grows relative to the fundamental on heating. Anharmonicity shifts its frequency away from the 0→1 transition; in a polyatomic molecule, excitation in another mode can also produce a hot-band sequence.

hcE(v,J)​=νe​(v+21​)−νe​xe​(v+21​)2+Bv​J(J+1),Bv​≃Be​−αe​(v+21​).​

This is a rovibrational spectrum: the vibrational excitation is accompanied by quantized molecular rotation. The high-wavenumber R branch has ΔJ=+1, and the low-wavenumber P branch has ΔJ=−1. In the simplest common-B approximation,
νR​(J)=ν0​+2B(J+1),νP​(J)=ν0​−2BJ.
(25)
Consecutive lines within either branch are therefore separated by roughly 2B, while the gap between P(1) and R(0) is roughly 4B. There is no Q branch: for this diatomic parallel Σ→Σ transition, the electric-dipole rotational selection rule allows ΔJ=±1, and the ΔJ=0 line strength vanishes.
The intensity envelope comes from rotational populations, approximately (2J+1)e−hcBJ(J+1)/(kB​T), together with the rotational line strengths. The paired peaks arise from the two chlorine isotopologues, H35Cl and H37Cl: their different reduced masses shift both rotational and vibrational spacings. The more abundant 35Cl gives the stronger member. The changing spacing away from the band centre reflects B1​=B0​ and, at higher J, centrifugal distortion; it is not evidence for additional vibrational fundamentals.

Let Qk​ be a vibrational normal mode coordinate with symmetry Γk​. The harmonic vibrational ground state is totally symmetric; the one-quantum state transforms as Qk​. Integration over a symmetry-invariant domain annihilates any integrand lacking the totally symmetric representation. Thus a fundamental requires
Γ(v′=1k​)⊗Γ(μα​)⊗Γ(v′′=0)⊃A1​
(30)
for some Cartesian component α. For the one-dimensional irreducible representations used here, this means Γk​=Γ(x), Γ(y) or Γ(z). Equivalently, expanding the electric dipole moment gives
μα​=μα,0​+(∂Qk​∂μα​​)0​Qk​+⋯;
(31)
the derivative can be nonzero only if Qk​ and μα​ have compatible symmetry. This is the symmetry criterion for vibrational infrared activity; it permits a transition without guaranteeing a large intensity.
