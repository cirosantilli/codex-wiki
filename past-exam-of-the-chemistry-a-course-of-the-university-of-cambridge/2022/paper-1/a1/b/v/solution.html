Project the first-order equation onto Ψj(0)​, with j=i. By orthonormality and the Hermitian operator property,
⟨Ψj(0)​∣(H^(0)−Ei(0)​)∣Ψi(1)​⟩⟨Ψj(0)​∣(H^(1)−Ei(1)​)∣Ψi(0)​⟩​=(Ej(0)​−Ei(0)​)cij​,=Hji​.​
(11)
Consequently
cij​=Ei(0)​−Ej(0)​Hji​​=Ei(0)​−Ej(0)​Hij∗​​(j=i),​
(12)
and
Ψi(1)​=j=i∑​Ei(0)​−Ej(0)​⟨Ψj(0)​∣H^(1)∣Ψi(0)​⟩​Ψj(0)​.​
(13)
The numerator order is important: the question defines Hij​ with i on the bra, whereas projecting the correction onto state j gives Hji​. The two coincide only when the relevant matrix element is real.
Excluding an i component chooses ⟨Ψi(0)​∣Ψi(1)​⟩=0, a permitted first-order phase and wavefunction normalization convention. A component parallel to Ψi(0)​ is not determined by the projected equation. Nondegeneracy ensures nonzero denominators; a small energy gap can still make the perturbative expansion inaccurate unless ∣λHji​∣≪∣Ei(0)​−Ej(0)​∣.
