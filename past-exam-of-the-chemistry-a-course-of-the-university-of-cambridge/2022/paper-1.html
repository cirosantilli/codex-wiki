With the physics convention ⟨Φ∣Ψ⟩=∫Φ∗Ψdτ, the adjoint operator is defined by
⟨Φ∣A^Ψ⟩=⟨A^†Φ∣Ψ⟩.​
(1)
This definition includes the operator domains and their boundary conditions. If A^ is a Hermitian operator on the common domain, then A^†Φ=A^Φ. Conjugate symmetry of the inner product consequently gives
⟨Φ∣A^Ψ⟩=⟨A^Φ∣Ψ⟩=⟨Ψ∣A^Φ⟩∗,
(2)
which is the required integral identity.
The boundary conditions must make the boundary form from integration by parts vanish for every pair of allowed functions. For a Schrödinger kinetic operator in one dimension, this means
[Φ∗Ψ′−(Φ′)∗Ψ]boundary​=0.
(3)
Sufficient examples are decay of the functions and derivatives at spatial infinity, homogeneous hard-wall conditions Φ=Ψ=0 at finite endpoints, or matching periodic conditions on both functions and their derivatives. The exact boundary form depends on A^; square integrability alone does not replace the domain requirement.

(14)

ΨS=0​=Φ+​2​α(1)β(2)−β(1)α(2)​.​

Ψ1,+1​Ψ1,0​Ψ1,−1​​=Φ−​α(1)α(2),=Φ−​2​α(1)β(2)+β(1)α(2)​,=Φ−​β(1)β(2).​​

An electron configuration specifies the occupied one-electron subshells and their populations, such as 1s22s22p2, without fixing how all orbital and spin angular momenta are coupled.
An atomic term groups states of a configuration with the same L and S. An atomic fine-structure level additionally specifies J; spin-orbit coupling can split one term into several such levels. In zero external field, a level retains 2J+1 magnetic substates.
The term symbol is
2S+1LJ​,​
(24)
with letters S,P,D,F,G,… denoting L=0,1,2,3,4,…. The superscript is the spin multiplicity. Without the J subscript, 2S+1L identifies an atomic term, rather than an individual fine-structure level.

Solution
Words: 189
iii OurBigBook.com
Closed subshells have L=S=0, so only the open p or d subshell determines each atomic term. Apply Hund's rules in order: maximize S, maximize L at that S, then choose the lowest J below half filling or the highest J above half filling.
For carbon, the two 2p electrons occupy different magnetic atomic orbitals with parallel spins, giving S=1. Their largest allowed ML​ at that spin is 1+0=1, so L=1, a P term. The p2 subshell is less than half full, giving J=∣1−1∣=0. The ground level is 3P0​.
For vanadium, three parallel-spin 3d electrons give S=3/2. The largest ML​ compatible with the Pauli exclusion principle is 2+1+0=3, hence L=3, an F term. The d3 subshell is less than half full, so J=3−3/2=3/2. The ground level is 4F3/2​.
For cobalt, the d7 subshell is equivalent to three d holes for the allowed L,S terms, so its maximum-spin, maximum-L term is again 4F. Explicitly, five spin-up electrons fill all ml​ values and two spin-down electrons can occupy ml​=2,1, giving MS​=3/2 and ML​=3. Since d7 is more than half full, J=3+3/2=9/2. The ground level is 4F9/2​.

i OurBigBook.com
The resolved lines arise from simultaneous changes of vibration and rotation in hydrogen cyanide. A vibrational fundamental connects many populated lower rotational levels to different upper rotational levels, giving the fine structure of a rovibrational spectrum.
The prominent central Q branch, together with flanking P branch and R branch lines, identifies a perpendicular band. For linear HCN, this is the doubly degenerate bending fundamental, not either stretching fundamental. Bending creates a transition-dipole component perpendicular to the molecular axis; its vibrational angular momentum permits ΔJ=0 as well as ΔJ=±1.
The two perpendicular bending coordinates share one vibrational frequency. Their degeneracy is different from the series of resolved rotational transitions superimposed on that fundamental. The stretches have their electric dipole transition moment along the molecular axis and would give the usual parallel-band P/R pattern without this strong central Q feature.

The uniform planar eight-electron ring has a partially filled frontier degeneracy. Bond-length alternation splits those levels, permitting the two frontier electrons to occupy the lowered orbital and opening a gap to the empty partner. This is the electronic mechanism for destabilizing the equal-bond planar geometry.
A controlled comparison keeps the mean resonance integral fixed. Put
βS​=β(1+δ),βL​=β(1−δ),β<0,0<δ<1.
(59)
Using part (i), the change from the uniform ring is
ΔEπ​=4βδ+42​β(1+δ2​−1)=4βδ+22​βδ2+O(δ4)<0.
(60)
Thus a small alternation lowers the electronic energy, with a leading gain linear in δ. An ordinary elastic cost starts quadratically, so the equal-bond planar state is unstable in this model. The equilibrium distortion requires those geometric and sigma-bond costs as well.
The eight π electrons constitute a 4n cyclic count rather than the closed-shell 4n+2 case associated with aromaticity. The result concerns the imposed planar geometry; it does not establish the unconstrained three-dimensional equilibrium shape of cyclooctatetraene.

Each hydrogen lies on the radial line through its attached carbon. Consequently every molecular symmetry operation fixes or permutes the hydrogen sites exactly as it does the carbon sites, and the Cartesian trace is the same. Thus ΓH​=ΓC​ and the complete benzene displacement group representation is Γ3N​=2ΓC​, of dimension 36.
Subtract translations A2u​+E1u​ and rotations A2g​+E1g​. The full set of normal modes is
Γvib​=​2A1g​+A2g​+2B2g​+E1g​+4E2g​+A2u​+2B1u​+2B2u​+3E1u​+2E2u​.​​

There are twenty distinct symmetry-labelled fundamentals before accidental coincidences, because each doubly degenerate pair has one frequency.
Subtracting translations and rotations directly from the carbon-only result would instead give twelve carbon-skeleton coordinates. That is a six-site mechanical model, not all the normal modes of C6​H6​. The hydrogen contribution is required for the actual molecule.

The electronic transition starts from A1g​, so the electric-dipole test reduces to whether the final electronic irreducible representation occurs among A2u​+E1u​. The excited E1u​ state matches the (x,y) dipole components and is therefore electric-dipole allowed.
The B2u​ state matches neither z nor (x,y) and is electric-dipole forbidden at the equilibrium geometry. Both excited states are singlets, so the difference is spatial symmetry rather than the spin selection rule. Inversion parity alone does not decide the result: both final states are ungerade, but only the matching vector representation is allowed.
