Treat each species as a dilute classical ideal gas: molecules of the same species are indistinguishable, while different species are distinguishable. Let Ni​ be the number of molecules of species i, qi​(T,V) its molecular partition function with energies measured from a chosen internal reference, and ε0,i that reference energy per molecule relative to a common chemical energy convention. Then the mixture's canonical partition function factorizes:
Q=∏i​Ni​!qiNi​​​exp[−βNi​ε0,i],β=kB​T1​.
(1)
The Ni​! corrects the overcounting of indistinguishable particles. The exponential restores the species-dependent reference energies that matter for reactions. Thus the Helmholtz free energy is
A=−kB​T∑i​[Ni​lnqi​−ln(Ni​!)]+∑i​Ni​ε0,i.
(2)
Using Stirling's approximation, ln(Ni​!)≃Ni​lnNi​−Ni​, and differentiating at fixed T,V,Nj=i​ yields
μi​=(∂Ni​∂A​)T,V,Nj=i​​=−kB​TlnNi​qi​​+ε0,i.​
(3)
The ideal-gas qi​ does not depend on the number of molecules, so no additional ∂qi​/∂Ni​ term appears. These are chemical potentials per molecule; multiply by the Avogadro constant for a molar chemical potential. The result assumes the thermodynamic, nondegenerate-gas limit.

The large negative activation entropy is consistent with a strongly organized bimolecular cycloaddition. Two separately translating and rotating molecules must approach with a restricted orientation to form a compact ring-forming transition state. Losing relative positional and orientational freedom lowers entropy.
Internal conformational restrictions and solvent rearrangement can contribute too. In a nonpolar solvent, a simple association picture is more plausible than assigning the whole effect to strong ionic solvation, but the numerical value is not a direct count of molecular orientations alone.

The activation enthalpy is obtained from the slope across the measured 1/T interval. The activation entropy comes from the intercept at 1/T=0, far outside that interval, so a small slope uncertainty can produce a much larger uncertainty in the extrapolated intercept. Slope and intercept errors are consequently strongly correlated.
The entropy is also sensitive to the assumed prefactor and standard concentration, whereas a constant multiplicative calibration error shifts the intercept without changing the slope. This explains why its inferred precision is often poorer. Since enthalpy and entropy have different units, a claim about which absolute numerical uncertainty is larger requires a stated comparison convention.

For an ordinary fixed field satisfying μB≪Δ, the ground manifold remains lowest. The magnetization therefore vanishes as T→0: the molecules are overwhelmingly in the stipulated nonmagnetic states. On warming, thermal excitation creates magnetic molecules and initially increases the response. At higher temperature, orientational randomization wins over the growing magnetic population, giving a maximum and then an inverse-temperature tail.
In the weak-field regime,
M(T)≃VkB​Nμ2B​T(1+eθ/T)1​.
(38)
Its maximum occurs near T=θ/1.2785≃135 K. At high temperature the excited fraction tends to one half, while each magnetic molecule's alignment decreases as 1/T, so M→0. The sketch assumes the simplified moments stated in the question. a OurBigBook.com
A direct band gap has its conduction band minimum and valence band maximum at the same crystal momentum. Near-edge absorption or emission can therefore conserve momentum with the electron and photon alone, since optical photon momentum is small on the Brillouin-zone scale. An indirect band gap has its extrema at different wavevectors and normally needs a phonon or another momentum-transfer process in addition to the photon.
Direct-gap materials are usually preferred for efficient light emission, because the radiative transition avoids the extra phonon-assisted process. Their stronger near-edge absorption also permits thinner photovoltaic absorbers. Indirect-gap materials can nevertheless make excellent solar cells: sufficiently thick optical paths, light trapping, long carrier lifetimes and suitable material quality can compensate for weaker absorption. The direct/indirect distinction alone does not rank every device.
This momentum distinction and the difference between bound exciton lines and the free-pair continuum are treated together in the primary effective-mass absorption theory.[ref]

E(k)≃α+6β−βa2(kx2​+ky2​+kz2​).
(57)
Only ∣k∣2 appears at quadratic order, so the band effective mass tensor is isotropic. Higher-order lattice terms need not be isotropic.
For the conduction band, βc​<0, so the curvature is positive and
mc∗​=2∣βc​∣a2ℏ2​=0.2397me​.​
(58)
Here a=5.42×10−10 m and both beta parameters must be converted from eV to joules. The band edges at the zone centre are
Ev​=−1.700+6(0.180)=−0.620 eV,Ec​=3.866−6(0.541)=0.620 eV,
(59)
so the model has a direct gap Eg​=1.240 eV​. The mass values describe curvature at these extrema; they are not constant masses throughout the full tight-binding bands.

The smaller ∣βv​∣ gives the valence edge a larger density-of-states prefactor than the conduction edge. If the Fermi level were exactly halfway across the gap, there would therefore be more thermally generated holes than electrons. Charge neutrality in an intrinsic semiconductor requires equal carrier densities, so the chemical potential moves upward: it suppresses holes while increasing electrons.
Explicitly, in the nondegenerate limit,
n=Nc​e−(Ec​−Ei​)/(kB​T),p=Nv​e−(Ei​−Ev​)/(kB​T),
(61)
and n=p gives the intrinsic Fermi level
Ei​=2Ec​+Ev​​+2kB​T​lnNc​Nv​​.​
(62)
Since Nv​>Nc​, the Fermi level lies in the upper half of the gap at finite temperature.
The supplied inverse-∣β∣ density-of-states rule gives the correct ordering. Strictly, an isotropic three-dimensional parabolic band has a prefactor proportional to (m∗)3/2∝∣β∣−3/2, rather than ∣β∣−1; this changes the size of the displacement but not its sign. At zero temperature the limiting intrinsic expression approaches midgap; a chemical potential in a completely empty gap is not uniquely fixed by carrier neutrality alone.

me​μ∗​=0.239733+0.720530(0.239733)(0.720530)​=0.17988.​

In a screened hydrogenic Wannier-Mott exciton, replace me​ by μ∗ and ε0​ by εr​ε0​ in the hydrogen binding-energy scale. Therefore
R∗=εr2​μ∗/me​​RH​=820.17988​(13.6057 eV)=0.03824 eV.​

The associated radius is about εr​(me​/μ∗)a0​=23.5 Å, several lattice spacings, consistent with using an extended-pair model as a first approximation rather than a tightly localized molecular exciton.

The Exciton Rydberg series lies below the unbound electron–hole threshold:
En​−Ev​=Eg​−n2R∗​.
(65)
Using Eg​=1.240 eV and R∗=0.038241 eV gives

Their positive exciton binding energies relative to the continuum are 38.24 meV and 9.56 meV, respectively.
These are optical electron–hole excitation energies for negligible centre-of-mass momentum, not new occupied one-electron states inside the valence band. The simple model predicts their locations; observing a particular line also requires a nonzero optical transition matrix element and adequate linewidth.

f OurBigBook.com
At cryogenic temperatures, the intrinsic carrier concentration is exponentially small, while a low phonon population permits relatively sharp band-edge and exciton spectral features. Near room temperature, thermal electron–hole generation increases strongly:
ni​=Nc​Nv​​e−Eg​/(2kB​T).
(67)
This generally raises intrinsic conductivity, although increased phonon scattering tends to lower the individual carrier mobilities. With a gap of 1.24 eV, the material remains far from a degenerate metal at room temperature.
For optical structure, compare kB​T≃25.9 meV at 300 K with the exciton binding energies. The n=2 binding of 9.56 meV is substantially smaller, so excited bound states are readily thermally disrupted and broadened. The ground binding of 38.24 meV is larger but only about 1.48kB​T: the ground exciton need not disappear completely, yet strong thermal ionization and broadening can weaken its sharp line. A comparison with kB​T is a useful scale argument, not an exact bound-pair fraction independent of density or lifetime.
Thermal lattice expansion and electron–phonon interactions can also shift a real material's gap and optical edge. The given fixed tight-binding parameters contain no temperature dependence, so they do not determine the magnitude or even a universal sign of that extra gap shift. Within the stated model, the dependable trends are more thermally generated carriers and less distinct excitonic structure on warming.
