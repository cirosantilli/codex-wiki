A polar diatomic molecule can absorb microwaves through its permanent electric dipole moment. In the linear rigid rotor approximation,
EJ​=hB0​J(J+1),J=0,1,2,…,B0​=8π2I0​h​.
(31)
The electric-dipole selection rule is ΔJ=+1 in absorption. It gives
νJ→J+1​=hEJ+1​−EJ​​=2B0​(J+1),
(32)
so adjacent lines are separated by 2B0​. Therefore
B0​=221.999​=10.9995 GHz.​
(33)
Assign a line near m×21.999 GHz to J=m−1→m for positive integer m. The first member is 0→1, the next 1→2, and so on; if the recorded window misses the lowest lines, their absolute frequencies fix the integer offset. At high temperature many rotational states are populated, so the strongest line need not be 0→1.
This is the leading rotational pattern, not a claim that real ZnF lacks spin or hyperfine structure. Fine splittings and centrifugal distortion can perturb the ideal lines; they do not explain the three systematically displaced progressions as economically as vibrational excitation.

The two extra progressions are rotational transitions within thermally populated v=1 and v=2 vibrational levels. Isotopic purity rules out attributing them to different isotope masses. Their rotational constants are

A vibrational fundamental is the transition v=0→1. A first vibrational overtone is v=0→2. A hot band starts from a thermally populated vibrationally excited level, such as v=1→2.
For a strictly harmonic potential and a dipole linear in displacement, the allowed transitions have Δv=±1. A fundamental and a 1→2 hot band then share the same frequency, while the overtone has zero intensity. Anharmonicity separates the adjacent gaps and can give overtone intensity; nonlinear displacement dependence of the dipole can also contribute. A hot band is defined by its lower state, not merely by being weak.

The molecular symmetry operations must be closed under composition. Write the proposed nonidentity operations as R=C2​ and S=σ. Their product RS cannot equal any proposed member:
RS=E⇒R=S−1=S,RS=R⇒S=E,RS=S⇒R=E.
(45)
Each conclusion contradicts the distinct operations stipulated. Therefore at least a fourth symmetry operation must exist. For a mirror plane containing the twofold axis, the product generates another mirror plane; for a plane perpendicular to it, the product is inversion. A tilted plane may generate still more operations, but cannot evade group closure.

A symmetry-closed orbital basis, often called a complete basis in this context, is a linearly independent set whose span is carried into itself by every group operation. No transformed basis function requires a function outside that span. This is completeness for the chosen group representation, not a claim that a small atomic-orbital set spans the full electronic Hilbert space.
A representation matrix D(g) specifies the linear transformation produced by a molecular symmetry operation g. With the column convention,
U^(g)ϕj​=∑i​ϕi​Dij​(g),D(gh)=D(g)D(h).
(48)
Its character of a representation is the matrix trace,
χ(g)=trD(g)=i∑​Dii​(g).​
(49)
The character is unchanged by a simultaneous change of basis and is constant on a conjugacy class, which is why character tables have one column per class.

The ideal eclipsed ion has D4h molecular point group symmetry. Put the Mo–Mo axis along z, with the metal atoms at (0,0,±c), and orient the four chloride positions around each metal along ±x,±y.

The eclipsed arrangement makes the two chloride squares coincide in projection along z. These labels refer to the ideal molecular geometry, rather than small crystal-packing distortions.

A ligand symmetry orbital can mix with a metal combination only if their irreps match. Since the donor levels lie below the metal levels, mixing produces a lower ligand-dominated bonding orbital and a higher metal-dominated antibonding orbital. In a two-level model with ϵd​>ϵL​,
Δϵd​≃ϵd​−ϵL​∣VdL​∣2​>0.
(63)
The requested metal-dominated levels are shown in the right-hand column; the lower ligand-dominated partners are deliberately outside this diagram.
The dx2−y2​ lobes point directly at the chlorides, so their b1g​ and b2u​ combinations are strongly destabilized. The dz2​ combinations a1g​,a2u​ can also interact through their equatorial density. The eu​,eg​ symmetries have ligand partners, so weaker shifts are symmetry-allowed; their local nearest-neighbour sigma overlap vanishes in the ideal planar geometry, making their shifts much smaller in that approximation. In particular the low b2g​(δ) level survives while the other delta bonding channel becomes strongly metal–ligand antibonding. The ligand field leaves one low sigma bond orbital, two low pi bond orbitals and one low delta bond orbital. Their closed-shell occupation is
(a1g​)2(eu​)4(b2g​)2=σ2π4δ2.​

It requires eight metal d electrons, or four per Mo, hence Mo(II). With eight Cl− ligands, charge balance gives
2(+2)+8(−1)=−4.​

Oxidizing removes an electron from a Mo–Mo bonding orbital; reducing populates a higher orbital with Mo–Mo antibonding or strong metal–ligand antibonding character. Thus the 4− state has the particularly favourable filled bonding configuration in this model. This is an orbital explanation of a preferred charge, not a claim that charge alone determines solution redox stability independently of counterions or solvent.
The eclipsed geometry also follows naturally from the occupied delta bond: it aligns the four-lobed dxy​ functions. Twisting the chloride squares toward a staggered geometry reduces this overlap and weakens that contribution to the quadruple bond.

Take the eight Mo–Cl bond-length changes as a stretching basis. These scalar coordinates have the same permutation representation as the eight sigma donors:
Γstretch​=A1g​+B1g​+Eg​+A2u​+B2u​+Eu​.
(66)
Infrared spectroscopy detects modes transforming as a dipole component: z is A2u​ and (x,y) is Eu​. There are therefore two IR fundamentals, one A2u​ and one doubly degenerate Eu​ frequency.
Raman spectroscopy detects modes transforming as polarizability components. The relevant modes are A1g​, B1g​ and Eg​, giving three Raman fundamentals, of which Eg​ is doubly degenerate. The remaining B2u​ stretch is silent in both ordinary spectra.
Thus the prediction is
NIR​=2,NRaman​=3,Ncoincident modes​=0.​
(67)
The zero follows from infrared and Raman mutual exclusion: the molecule has inversion symmetry, dipole components are ungerade, and polarizability components gerade. This counts resolved fundamental frequencies of the ideal ion, not separate lines for degenerate components, isotope splittings or accidental numerical overlaps between distinct modes.
