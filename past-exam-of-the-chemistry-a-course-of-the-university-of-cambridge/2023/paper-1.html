Denote the two complex spatial atomic orbitals by ψ+​ and ψ−​, retaining the same spin state. With the phases specified by the supplied spherical harmonics, choose
ψx​=2​ψ−​−ψ+​​,ψy​=2​i(ψ−​+ψ+​)​.​

Let {ϕj​} be a complete orthonormal eigenbasis of the Hamiltonian, with Hϕj​=Ej​ϕj​ and Ej​≥E0​. Expand a nonzero admissible trial wavefunction as Ψ=∑j​cj​ϕj​. Then
⟨Ψ∣Ψ⟩=∑j​∣cj​∣2,⟨Ψ∣H∣Ψ⟩=∑j​∣cj​∣2Ej​.
(14)
Consequently
E−E0​=∑j​∣cj​∣2∑j​∣cj​∣2(Ej​−E0​)​≥0,E≥E0​.​
(15)
Every numerator term is nonnegative and the denominator is positive. This is the quantum variational principle, and wavefunction normalization before calculation is optional because the quotient removes the overall scale.

For any α>0, the Gaussian function is the exact nodeless ground wavefunction of a quantum harmonic oscillator with
H^ho​(α)=−21​dx2d2​+21​α2x2,E0,ho​=2α​.​
(20)

A natural comparison uses the same optimized width as part (i), α=31/3. The harmonic ground energy is then 0.72112479, compared with the quartic Gaussian function expectation 0.54084359. For this common normalized trial,
⟨Vquartic​⟩=8α​,⟨Vho​⟩=4α​,
(21)
while the kinetic energy expectation value is α/4 in both cases. The quartic potential lies below this harmonic potential for 0<∣x∣<α and above it for ∣x∣>α; the difference weighted by the Gaussian function is negative, giving the lower expectation.

Crossing potential curves cannot be ordered globally; the comparison must specify the frequency or Gaussian function width used.

For n unpaired electrons, the spin-only magnetic moment is
μeff​=n(n+2)​μB​=2S(S+1)​μB​,​
(36)
where μB​ is the Bohr magneton and the second expression uses g≃2.

The D4h molecular point group contains an inversion centre, so infrared and Raman mutual exclusion applies. A normal mode of u parity can have vibrational infrared activity, whereas the quadratic polarizability tensor components responsible for Raman vibrational activity have g parity. No individual fundamental can be active in both spectra.
The Td molecular point group lacks an inversion centre. In particular, T2​ normal modes transform both as electric-dipole components and as off-diagonal polarizability tensor components, so they occur in both spectra. Coincident IR and Raman fundamentals therefore favour the tetrahedral structure; symmetry requires no such coincidences for the square. Weak or unresolved features can conceal coincidences experimentally, while accidental equality of different frequencies is not a violation of mutual exclusion.

There are four internal C–N stretching coordinates. A stretch length is unchanged in sign when a molecular symmetry operation carries a bond to an equivalent bond, so these coordinates have the same permutation group representation as the four ligand sigma functions.
For the square,
ΓCN​=A1g​+B1g​+Eu​.
(37)
Only Eu​∼(x,y) has vibrational infrared activity. The A1g​ and B1g​ stretches have Raman vibrational activity. Thus the ideal square gives one IR feature and two Raman features, with no common fundamental. The two components of Eu​ have one frequency, not two different lines.
For the tetrahedron,
ΓCN​=A1​+T2​.
(38)
The T2​ stretch is IR active, while both A1​ and T2​ are Raman active. Thus the tetrahedron also gives one IR feature and two Raman features, but one feature, T2​, is shared between the spectra. Its three components likewise describe three independent displacements at one frequency.
The separate line counts alone do not distinguish the geometries. Comparing their frequencies does: the tetrahedral IR C–N feature coincides with one Raman C–N feature, whereas the square has no symmetry-enforced coincidence. This assumes resolved fundamentals and excludes accidental equality of unrelated frequencies.

The nine-atom nonlinear ion has 3N−6=21 vibrational degrees of freedom. To construct its displacement group representation, only atoms left in place by an operation contribute to the character. Each such atom contributes the trace of the operation on (x,y,z):
χ3N​(R)=Nfixed​(R)trDxyz​(R).
(39)
For example, reflection in the square's molecular plane fixes all nine atoms and has trace 1, giving character 9; inversion fixes nickel alone and has trace −3, giving −3. Molecular character reduction uses
nλ​=∣G∣1​∑C​∣C∣χ3N​(C)χλ​(C)∗.
(40)
For the D4h molecular point group, with primed twofold axes along the Ni–C directions, the class order and displacement characters are
Cχ3N​​E27​2C4​1​C2​−1​2C2′​−5​2C2′′​−1​i−3​2S4​−1​σh​9​2σv​5​2σd​1​​

Reducing and removing translations A2u​+Eu​ and rotations A2g​+Eg​ gives
ΓvibD4h​​=2A1g​+A2g​+2B1g​+2B2g​+Eg​+2A2u​+2B2u​+4Eu​.​
(42)
The vibrational infrared activity belongs to 2A2u​+4Eu​, giving six features. The Raman vibrational activity belongs to 2A1g​+2B1g​+2B2g​+Eg​, giving seven features. The remaining A2g​+2B2u​ modes are silent. There are zero shared fundamentals, by infrared and Raman mutual exclusion. Counting dimensions gives 10 IR-active, 8 Raman-active and 3 silent vibrational coordinates, totaling 21.
For the Td molecular point group, the corresponding calculation starts from
Cχ3N​​E27​8C3​0​3C2​−1​6S4​−1​6σd​5​​

These are numbers of distinct ideal fundamental frequencies: each E or T multiplet contributes one feature, and different copies of the same irreducible representation generally have different frequencies. Accidental degeneracy, overtones and combination bands are outside this count.

All three bonding π molecular orbitals are filled and the antibonding levels are empty, giving the closed-shell stabilization associated with aromaticity. Pyrazine is aromatic: its planar cyclic conjugated system contains six π electrons and has a filled set of three bonding π molecular orbitals. Each ring nitrogen contributes one pz​ electron; its in-plane lone pair is outside this π system. The heteroatoms change orbital energies and coefficients without interrupting cyclic conjugation or the 4n+2 count. Aromaticity does not require the stabilization to equal benzene's.
