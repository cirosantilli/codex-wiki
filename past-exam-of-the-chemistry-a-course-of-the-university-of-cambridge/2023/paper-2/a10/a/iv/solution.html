Let D be the equal constant density of states per unit volume and energy in each band. In the nondegenerate semiconductor regime, the conduction-band occupation is
f(E)≃e−(E−μ)/(kB​T).
(67)
For bands broad compared with kB​T, integrating above the conduction edge gives
n=D∫Ec​∞​e−(E−μ)/(kB​T)dE=DkB​Te−(Ec​−μ)/(kB​T).
(68)
The valence-hole integral similarly gives p=DkB​Te−(μ−Ev​)/(kB​T). Midgap μ therefore implies
n=p≃DkB​Te−Eg​/(2kB​T)​,Eg​=Ec​−Ev​.
(69)
Inserting the carrier densities into the transport expression yields
σ≃σ0​(T)eDkB​T(ue​+uh​)​​e−Eg​/(2kB​T).
(70)
On treating the algebraic and mobility prefactor as approximately constant, the requested leading activation law is
σ∝e−Eg​/(2kB​T)​.
(71)
The factor 1/2 is essential: an intrinsic excitation creates an electron–hole pair, and charge neutrality places the chemical potential halfway across the symmetric gap. The conduction-electron population is governed by Ec​−μ=Eg​/2, not by replacing this separation with the full gap.
There is a qualification to the proportionality as printed. A literal constant-density-of-states intrinsic semiconductor with temperature-independent mobilities retains the additional factor T. Indeed, using the full Fermi-Dirac distribution gives
n=p=DkB​Tln(1+e−Eg​/(2kB​T)),
(72)
which reduces to the expression above when Eg​≫kB​T. Thus the pure exponential is an activation approximation, not an exact consequence of constant DOS alone. A mobility sum proportional to T−1 would cancel the leading algebraic factor.
