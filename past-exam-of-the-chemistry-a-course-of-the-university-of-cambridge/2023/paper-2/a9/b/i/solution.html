For direct-lattice points xn​=na, a lattice-periodic plane wave must have the same phase at every translated lattice point:
eiG(x+a)=eiGx,eiGa=1.
(51)
Thus Ga=2πm, with integer m, and the reciprocal lattice is
Gm​=a2πm​,m∈Z​.
(52)
Its spacing is 2π/a. These plane waves execute an integer number of complete cycles within a direct-lattice period, so their wavelength magnitudes are a/∣m∣ for m=0.
A general electronic Bloch wave need not itself be lattice-periodic: wavevectors differing by Gm​ have the same translation phase. The reciprocal lattice labels that equivalence, while a large finite specimen with periodic boundary conditions has a much finer grid of allowed electronic wavevectors.
