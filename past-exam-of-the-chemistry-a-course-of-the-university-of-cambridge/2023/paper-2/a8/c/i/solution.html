First construct the energy cost for heterolysis from the molecular potential minimum. Dissociation to neutral atoms costs De​, ionizing caesium costs I, and attachment of the electron to iodine releases EA. Therefore
Δεmin​=De​+I−EA=4.36eV.
(34)
For the ground-state reference convention used above, the initial molecule already has vibrational zero-point energy. Thus its ground-state dissociation energy is D0​=De​−21​kB​θvib​ and
EZP​=21​(8.61733×10−5)(172)eV=0.0074109eV,
(35)
Δε0​=D0​+I−EA=4.35259eV≃4.353eV​.
(36)
At 3000K, kB​T=0.258520eV, giving
e−Δε0​/(kB​T)=4.8750×10−8​.
(37)
Alternatively, retaining the molecular minimum as reference gives Δεmin​=4.36eV and a Boltzmann factor 4.7372×10−8. That convention must use the full vibrational partition function, including e−θvib​/(2T), in the next part. The ground-state convention instead uses qvib0​. Either consistent convention gives the same final equilibrium constant; using the ground-state reaction energy with the full zero-point factor would count the correction twice. The distinction between De​ and D0​ is a distinction between initial states, not a change in the dissociation products.[ref]
