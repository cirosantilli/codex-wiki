Because ΔK=0, the (C−B)K2 terms cancel between the initial and final states. The two positive-shift branches are consequently
ΔJ=+1:ΔJ=+2:​νS​=νL​−2B(J+1),νS​=νL​−(4J+6)B.​​
(26)
The +2 branch gives shifts 6B,10B,14B,…, as for the diatomic. The +1 branch fills in a series spaced by 2B, so the combined nonzero series is 4B,6B,8B,10B,….
There is a boundary qualification to the abbreviated rules printed in the question: for an ordinary symmetric polarizability tensor, ΔJ=±1 has zero strength when K=0. Thus J=0,K=0→J=1,K=0 does not produce a 2B line; the first +1 transition can start at J=1,∣K∣=1 and has shift 4B. This follows from the rank-two angular-momentum coupling: the all-zero-projection coupling for J and J+1 vanishes because J+2+(J+1) is odd. If one applies only the printed inequalities without this matrix-element restriction, 2B is a formal position, but its intensity is zero.
The lower panel of the figure in part (a)(ii) marks the two branches and their overlaps. The useful spacing is 2B, not the diatomic value 4B.
