The D4h molecular point group contains an inversion centre, so infrared and Raman mutual exclusion applies. A normal mode of u parity can have vibrational infrared activity, whereas the quadratic polarizability tensor components responsible for Raman vibrational activity have g parity. No individual fundamental can be active in both spectra.
The Td molecular point group lacks an inversion centre. In particular, T2​ normal modes transform both as electric-dipole components and as off-diagonal polarizability tensor components, so they occur in both spectra. Coincident IR and Raman fundamentals therefore favour the tetrahedral structure; symmetry requires no such coincidences for the square. Weak or unresolved features can conceal coincidences experimentally, while accidental equality of different frequencies is not a violation of mutual exclusion.
