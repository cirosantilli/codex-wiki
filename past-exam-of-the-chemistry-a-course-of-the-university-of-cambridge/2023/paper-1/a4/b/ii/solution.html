There are four internal C–N stretching coordinates. A stretch length is unchanged in sign when a molecular symmetry operation carries a bond to an equivalent bond, so these coordinates have the same permutation group representation as the four ligand sigma functions.
For the square,
ΓCN​=A1g​+B1g​+Eu​.
(37)
Only Eu​∼(x,y) has vibrational infrared activity. The A1g​ and B1g​ stretches have Raman vibrational activity. Thus the ideal square gives one IR feature and two Raman features, with no common fundamental. The two components of Eu​ have one frequency, not two different lines.
For the tetrahedron,
ΓCN​=A1​+T2​.
(38)
The T2​ stretch is IR active, while both A1​ and T2​ are Raman active. Thus the tetrahedron also gives one IR feature and two Raman features, but one feature, T2​, is shared between the spectra. Its three components likewise describe three independent displacements at one frequency.
The separate line counts alone do not distinguish the geometries. Comparing their frequencies does: the tetrahedral IR C–N feature coincides with one Raman C–N feature, whereas the square has no symmetry-enforced coincidence. This assumes resolved fundamentals and excludes accidental equality of unrelated frequencies.
