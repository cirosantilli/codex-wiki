Bridgman unsupported-area seal (q=pAp​/Aseal​)

 ... Statistical physics Thermodynamics Pressure Pressure measurement High-pressure experimental metrology Piston-cylinder pressure apparatus
OurBigBook.com Words: 326
Consider an ideal pressure-driven closure plug with a large chamber-facing projected area Ap​. Its opposite side seats against a smaller annular packing surface area Aseal​. The remaining projected part Au​=Ap​−Aseal​>0 is the unsupported area: it has no opposing packing contact in that plane. Here p is the chamber pressure relative to the exterior. Let q denote the mean axial compressive contact stress on the packing, not the chamber's hydrostatic stress.
Neglect friction, the plug's weight, and other axial reactions. The plug's Newton's second law in static balance is
pAp​−qAseal​=0,q=pAseal​Ap​​=p(1+Aseal​Au​​)>p.
(31)
The load is the same on both sides; its smaller supporting surface area raises the packing's mean contact stress. For a stipulated ratio Ap​/Aseal​=1.25 and p=1GPa, the ideal seating stress is q=1.25GPa. Its excess over chamber pressure is 0.25GPa. Reducing Au​ to zero removes this amplification. This is the elementary force balance behind self-energizing seating, consistent with the lecture's Figure 1 caption.[ref]
The relation is a model, not a universal sealing law. The mean axial contact stress need not equal the local contact stress along every possible leakage path. Confinement and plasticity in continuum mechanics determine whether the packing closes a gap or instead extrudes into it. Friction diverts load into other surfaces; material plasticity in continuum mechanics changes contact geometry; the vessel must carry its own stress. A finite initial seating load may also be needed before chamber pressure can develop. An arbitrarily small Aseal​ does not yield arbitrarily useful sealing: the assumptions fail when local deformation, extrusion or structural failure takes over.
