Radiant exitance (M=Me​=dΦemitted​/dA)

OurBigBook.com Words: 163
Radiant exitance is total outward emitted radiant flux per actual emitting area, measured in Wm−2. Integrate radiance over the outward hemisphere, keeping the projected area factor:
M=∫Ωoutward​​L(θ,φ)cosθdΩ.
(27)
If the radiance is direction-independent, the spherical coordinates element is dΩ=sinθdθdφ, so
M=L∫02π​dφ∫0π/2​cosθsinθdθ=πL.
(28)
The weighted solid angle is πsr, although the whole hemisphere covers 2πsr. Directions near grazing contribute less because of the projected area. This geometric factor is the bridge between Lambert's cosine law and the total output of a diffuse patch. [ref].
For the uniform patch with L=10Wm−2sr−1, radiant exitance is M=10πWm−2. Its area 0.020m2 gives total radiant flux Φ=MA=0.20πW≈0.628319W over the outward hemisphere. This total differs from its radiant intensity I(0)=0.20Wsr−1, which describes only one direction.
For an ideal blackbody, integrate Planck's law over frequency as well: M=Me​=j∗=σT4 by the Stefan–Boltzmann law. An ideal 6000K emitting patch of area 1mm2 emits about 73.4881W outward. Irradiance counts incoming radiation separately; for purely emitted output the net outward exchange per area is M−E. Reflected output must also be included if the surface reflects incident radiation.
