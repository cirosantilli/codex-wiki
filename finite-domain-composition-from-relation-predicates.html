Finite-domain composition from relation predicates ((S∘R)(x,z)⟺∃y∈Y: R(x,y)∧S(y,z))

OurBigBook.com Words: 97
If R:X×Y→{false,true} and S:Y×Z→{false,true} expose only membership predicates, composition requires finding whether some middle value satisfies both. A complete finite enumeration of Y makes the query decidable by scanning until a witness is found or all values fail. Without an enumeration or equivalent access operation, generic membership oracles do not supply enough information: a finite query transcript can agree with an all-false case yet omit a fresh middle witness that makes composition true. Known finiteness is not the same as an available traversal. A separate carrier argument preserves the variance of the base predicate interfaces.
