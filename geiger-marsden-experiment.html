Geiger-Marsden experiment

 Home Physics Atomic physics
OurBigBook.com Words: 291 Articles: 1
The Geiger-Marsden experiment probes the interior of a chemical atom by counting alpha particles deflected by metal foils. The surprising observation was a small population returning towards the incident side. Hans Geiger and Ernest Marsden performed the experiments under Ernest Rutherford's direction; Ernest Rutherford developed the Rutherford model in 1911. Observation and interpretation were distinct contributions.[ref]
In 1909, a radon source and its radioactive decay products in a conical glass tube supplied alpha particles through a mica window towards a reflector. Lead shielded a fixed zinc sulfide screen from direct hits; an optical microscope resolved individual particle scintillations. Reflectors occupied the same position. Reflector-out counts tested air scattering; stacked gold foils and bare glass tested thickness and support contributions.[ref]
Nearby source geometry blurred incidence angles. Extrapolating platinum counts uniformly over a hemisphere was an assumption, not a universal reflection probability.[ref]
