Cross-product Lie algebra ((R3,×))

 ... Mathematics Area of mathematics Algebra Diagonal dominance Lie theory Lie algebra
OurBigBook.com Words: 156
The vector space R3 with the cross product as Lie bracket, [x,y]=x×y. Bilinearity and x×x=0 give an Alternating Lie bracket; the vector triple product identity proves the Jacobi identity because the three expressions x×(y×z)=y(x⋅z)−z(x⋅y) cancel cyclically, using the symmetric dot product.
Its relation to the SO(3) Lie algebra is explicit. For a vector x=(x1​,x2​,x3​), define the skew-symmetric matrix
x=​0x3​−x2​​−x3​0x1​​x2​−x1​0​​,xv=x×v.
(3)
Then [x,y​]=x×y​: acting on v, the left side is x×(y×v)−y×(x×v)=(x×y)×v. Every real 3×3 skew-symmetric matrix occurs uniquely, so this hat map is a Lie algebra isomorphism. With the standard basis vectors e1​,e2​,e3​,
X=e1​​=​000​001​0−10​​,Y=e2​​=​00−1​000​100​​,Z=e3​​=​010​−100​000​​,
(4)
and their matrix commutators are [X,Y]=Z, [Y,Z]=X, [Z,X]=Y. With active rotation matrices acting on column vectors, exp(tx) rotates through angle t∥x∥ about the oriented axis x when x=0. In particular exp(tZ)e1​=(cost,sint,0), fixing the positive-angle convention used in the small rotation commutator.
The map x↦−(i/2)x⋅σ is also a Lie algebra isomorphism to the compact real SU(2) Lie algebra, using the Pauli matrix commutator identity. These two identifications concern Lie algebras; they do not identify the corresponding Lie groups globally.
