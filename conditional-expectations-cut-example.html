Conditional expectations cut example

OurBigBook.com Words: 430
Consider the following original five-vertex simple graph, where AB denotes the undirected edge {A,B}:
V={A,B,C,D,E},E={AB,BC,CD,DE,EA,AC,BD}.
(65)
Its seven edges give initial expected value q(∅)=7/2=3.5. Use vertex order A,B,C,D,E and choose zero whenever the two child conditional expectations tie.
The complete decision ledger is below. A prefix lists fixed labels in vertex order; all later labels remain hypothetically independent fair bits. Each candidate entry is the conditional expectation of the final cut of a graph, including the contribution of unresolved edges.

For example, after A=0,B=1, edge AB is fixed crossing and the other six edges are unresolved, giving 1+6/2=4. Setting C=0 fixes BC crossing and AC noncrossing, leaving four unresolved edges: 2+4/2=4. Setting C=1 instead fixes AC crossing and BC noncrossing, giving the same conditional expectation.
The final labels are (0,1,0,0,1), so the two sides are {A,C,D} and {B,E}. The five crossing edges are AB,BC,DE,EA,BD; AC,CD do not cross. This exceeds m/2=3.5, and hence also the rounded-up guarantee of four edges.
It is not a maximum cut. Labels (0,1,1,0,1) cross all edges except BC, giving six. Seven are impossible because the triangle in a graph ABC cannot have all three edges crossing a two-sided cut of a graph. The weighted sum is 112, so the average is 112/32=3.5, agreeing with linearity of expectation. Choosing C=0 in the earlier tie discards the remaining size-six completion while preserving the average of four. The method protects an expected value guarantee; it need not preserve the best possible completion.
