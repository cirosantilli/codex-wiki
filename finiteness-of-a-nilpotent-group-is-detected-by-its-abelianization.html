Finiteness of a nilpotent group is detected by its abelianization (∣G∣<∞⟺∣G/[G,G]∣<∞)

 ... Mathematics Area of mathematics Algebra Group theory Solvable group Nilpotent group
OurBigBook.com Words: 97
A nilpotent group is finite exactly when its abelianization is finite; finite generation is not a separate hypothesis. Induct on the nilpotency class c. The abelian case is immediate. Otherwise G/γc​(G) has smaller class and the same abelianization, so is finite. Let H=G, K=γc−1​(G). Their commutator subgroup [H,K]=γc​(G) is central. Hence its commutator map is a homomorphism in each variable. Both centralizer quotients are finite because their kernels contain γc​(G). Finitely many pairs of coset representatives therefore generate the abelian group [H,K], and its exponent divides the exponent of H/CH​(K). It is finite, completing the induction.
