A solvable infrared renormalization flow (dg/dℓ=ϵg−g2,ℓ=logb)

OurBigBook.com Words: 210
Take the following bounded analytic example for a dimensionless positive coupling g, with fixed ϵ>0 and g(0)=g0​>0:
dℓdg​=ϵg−g2,ℓ=logb=log(L/a).
(50)
Increasing ℓ means increasing observation length L, hence motion toward the infrared fixed point. This convention matters: if the inverse-length scale is μ=μ0​e−ℓ, the same trajectory has renormalization-group beta function βμ​(g)=μdg/dμ=−ϵg+g2. The two derivatives have opposite signs.
To solve the equation, set v=1/g. It obeys dv/dℓ=−ϵv+1, a linear ordinary differential equation. Therefore
g(ℓ)=1+(ϵ/g0​−1)e−ϵℓϵ​.
(51)
For ℓ≥0 its denominator is positive, and g stays between g0​ and ϵ. Thus the trajectory is bounded on this domain. The renormalization-group fixed points are g=0 and g∗​=ϵ. The derivative of the length-flow right-hand side is ϵ−2g: zero is repulsive from positive g, whereas g∗​ has eigenvalue −ϵ and attracts positive trajectories. Linearization gives g−g∗​∝e−ϵℓ=b−ϵ near g∗​. The inverse-length beta function has positive derivative at the same infrared-attractive point.
For the chosen ϵ=1/5, take two starting models with g0​=1/20 and g0​=3/5. At b=1024, e−ϵℓ=1/4, so their couplings are respectively 4/35 and 6/25. Both move toward 1/5, from opposite sides. They are still visibly different: slow decay of an irrelevant operator can leave substantial corrections at accessible lengths. The case g0​=0 stays exactly zero and is outside this positive-coupling basin.
