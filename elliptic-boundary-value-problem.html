Direct spherical-shell H2 estimate (∥u∥H2​≤C∥Δu∥2​)

Words: 81
Elliptic boundary value problem OurBigBook.com
On a fixed shell a<r<b with a>0, put T=∂r2​+2r−1∂r​ and A=ΔS2​. If u is smooth and zero on both boundary spheres, direct radial and spherical integration by parts gives
∥Δu∥22​=∥Tu∥22​+∥r−2Au∥22​+2∫ab​∫S2​∣∇S​ur​∣2−2∫ab​∫S2​r−2∣∇S​u∣2.
(1)
The last term is controlled by the first-order energy estimate. The spherical Hessian identity controls angular second derivatives, and the radial/mixed derivatives follow from T and ∇S​ur​. The polar orthonormal-frame formulas for the Cartesian Hessian matrix then prove the estimate. Zero boundary values eliminate the radial boundary remainders because every angular derivative of their traces is zero.

Dirichlet realization of an elliptic operator (AD​u=Lu,D(AD​)=H2(U)∩H01​(U))

Words: 72
Elliptic boundary value problem OurBigBook.com
A smooth uniformly elliptic differential expression with symmetric Dirichlet bilinear form has an unbounded self-adjoint operator realization AD​ on L2(U), whose domain is H2(U)∩H01​(U) on a smooth bounded domain. If the form is strictly positive, the Lax-Milgram theorem and elliptic regularity make AD−1​ a bounded map from L2 to H2∩H01​. Its action on L2 is compact by the Rellich-Kondrachov compactness theorem and self-adjoint by symmetry of the form. The spectral theorem for compact Hermitian operators therefore gives an orthonormal basis of smooth Dirichlet eigenfunctions with positive eigenvalues tending to infinity.

For a uniformly elliptic operator L on a bounded regular domain, exactly one of the following equivalent descriptions applies to its homogeneous Dirichlet problem. If kerL∗=0, every forcing has a unique solution. Otherwise Lu=f is solvable exactly when f is orthogonal to kerL∗, and any two solutions differ by an element of kerL. Moreover dimkerL=dimkerL∗.

Boundary compatibility in the self-adjoint Fredholm alternative

Quadratic ellipticity does not bound a nonsymmetric coefficient matrix (ξTAξ=ξT21​(A+AT)ξ)

Words: 105
Uniformly elliptic operator OurBigBook.com
The quadratic form of a matrix only sees its symmetric part. Thus λ∣ξ∣2≤ξTAξ≤Λ∣ξ∣2 need not bound the skew part of a measurable coefficient matrix. For A=(1−k​k1​) the quadratic form is exactly ∣ξ∣2, regardless of k. On a square, take k(x)=1/∣x1​∣ off the line x1​=0, with a finite arbitrary value on that line. Two compactly supported smooth functions that equal x2​ and x1​ near the origin have (A∇u)⋅∇v=k there; the purported energy pairing is not integrable. A Lax-Milgram theorem proof therefore requires bounded full coefficients or another hypothesis that makes the bilinear form bounded. Symmetry supplies the missing matrix bound from the quadratic upper bound.

Strict positivity implies coercivity for an elliptic Dirichlet form

Let B[u,v]=∫U​aij∂i​u∂j​v+cuv on H01​(U) for a bounded smooth domain, smooth symmetric uniformly positive coefficients, and bounded real c. Strong maximum principle for elliptic operators

Words: 110
Weak maximum principle for elliptic operators OurBigBook.com
For a locally uniformly elliptic operator L=aijDij​+biDi​+c with locally bounded coefficients and c≤0, a C2 function satisfying Lu≥0 on a connected domain cannot attain a nonnegative global maximum inside unless it is constant. If c=0, the maximum can have either sign. If the maximum set were proper, choose an interior ball in its complement tangent to that set. The Hopf boundary point lemma gives a nonzero normal derivative at the contact point, contradicting the vanishing gradient at an interior maximum.
The strong minimum principle for elliptic operators follows by applying this to −u. For nonnegative u with Lu≤0, replacing c by min(c,0) retains the inequality, so the strict positivity-or-zero conclusion does not require an original sign restriction on c.

Blow-up compactness proof of an interior Schauder estimate
Words: 156 Articles: 1
Interior Schauder estimate OurBigBook.com
A compactness proof of the interior Schauder estimate can magnify a pair of points where the Hölder seminorm of the Hessian matrix is nearly attained. If the proposed estimate fails, normalize that seminorm to one while lower norms and forcing tend to zero. The separation rj​ of the chosen pair tends to zero. Subtract the quadratic Taylor polynomial and divide by rj2+α​ after rescaling space by rj​.
The resulting functions have uniformly bounded Hessian Hölder seminorms on expanding domains and vanishing Taylor data at zero. Compact embedding of Hölder spaces yields an entire harmonic function whose second derivatives have finite global α-Hölder seminorms, with 0<α<1. The polynomial-growth Liouville theorem for harmonic functions makes those derivatives constant, contradicting the normalized nonzero oscillation. The Simon absorption lemma removes the residual small multiple of the larger-ball seminorm.
