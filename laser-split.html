Laser

 Home Physics Branches of physics Optics
OurBigBook.com Words: 8k Articles: 65
A laser generates amplified light through stimulated emission. Its name abbreviates “light amplification by stimulated emission of radiation”. Lasers supply the intense beams used in optical tweezers; optical coherence also makes them useful for wave interference measurements. [ref].
In the usual material picture, a gain medium stores energy supplied by pumping, for example by incident light or an electric current. Stimulated emission transfers that stored energy to a selected optical normal mode, while competing photon absorption removes light. Producing net optical gain requires a suitable population distribution, such as population inversion in the simple model below.
A familiar laser oscillator uses mirrors to return light through the gain medium. This optical feedback favours resonant normal modes; a partly transmitting mirror lets useful power escape. Feedback and mode selection have different implementations, including ring arrangements: every laser need not resemble the same two-mirror device. The laser threshold example treats just that device. [ref] [ref].
The useful combination is concentrated power and controlled optical normal modes. It gives optical tweezers a localized source of force and wave interference measurements a source with useful optical coherence. A laser can nevertheless emit several frequencies; “laser” alone does not guarantee a single frequency. [ref].
An original experimental landmark is T. H. Maiman's 1960 report of stimulated optical radiation from optically pumped ruby at about 694.3nm. His paper describes both the pumping path and the observed emission, connecting stored material energy with amplified light. [ref].
