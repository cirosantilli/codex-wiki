Aristarchus of Samos

Aristarchus of Samos used the half-lit Moon to estimate the relative distances of the Sun and Moon. In the spherical illumination model a half-lit disk means phase angle α=π/2, so the Sun–Moon–Earth triangle is right-angled at the Moon. Keeping the Sun at a finite distance, let dEM​ and dES​ be the distances from Earth to the Moon and Sun. Then elongation ϵ at Earth gives

The 87∘ elongation attributed to Aristarchus of Samos gives a ratio about 19.1, consistent with his conclusion that the Sun was between 18 and 20 times farther away. The example shows how lunar phase geometry becomes a distance measurement, but the ratio is extremely sensitive to an angle close to 90∘. Unlike the distant-Sun approximation, this finite triangle has elongation slightly less than 90∘ at half illumination. [ref].
