Radial free fall from rest (v(r)=2μ(1/r−1/r0​)​)

Let a test particle start at rest at radius r0​ outside a fixed spherically symmetric source. The spherical shell theorem gives the exterior Newtonian gravitational potential Φ(r)=−μ/r, where μ=GM is the source's standard gravitational parameter. Zero initial transverse velocity makes the motion radial. Conservation of mechanical energy per unit mass gives
2r˙2​−rμ​=−r0​μ​,r˙=−2μ(r1​−r0​1​)​.
(7)
The minus sign selects inward motion; the positive square root is its speed. For a fall from r0​=2R to the surface r=R, that speed is μ/R​. In the limit of release from rest infinitely far away, the familiar free-fall speed is 2μ/r​, equal in magnitude to the local escape velocity. A finite starting radius gives a smaller speed.
The arrival time follows from the same first integral. Set r=r0​cos2η. Then
t(η)=2μr03​​​(η+sinηcosη),η=arccosr0​r​​,0≤η<2π​.
(8)
For arrival at R>0 this is finite, and the field formula is used only outside the source. For r0​=2R, η=π/4 gives
tarrival​=(1+2π​)μR3​​≈2.570796μR3​​.
(9)
Keeping the smaller initial gravitational magnitude g(r0​)=μ/(4R2) constant would predict 8​R3/μ​, about 10.0% longer. The actual acceleration increases during the fall.
The curves compare a shallow fall, h/R=0.02, with a fall from r0​=2R, so h/R=1. Each comparison holds the release height and initial gravitational magnitude fixed. The constant-gravity model is close for the shallow fall and increasingly inaccurate for the larger one. This is also a practical use of nondimensionalization: the normalized trajectories depend on h/R, while R3/μ​ supplies the dimensional time scale.
