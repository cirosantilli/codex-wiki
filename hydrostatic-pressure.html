Hydrostatic pressure (dp/dz=−ρf​g)

 Home Physics Branches of physics Fluid mechanics
OurBigBook.com Words: 334 Articles: 5
Hydrostatic pressure is the pressure distribution in a resting fluid, balancing its body force from gravity. Take z upward and a uniform downward gravitational field g=−gez​, where g>0. The vertical pressure gradient satisfies
dzdp​=−ρf​g.
(72)
A short derivation balances the vertical forces on a thin horizontal layer of area A and thickness Δz: p(z)A−p(z+Δz)A−ρf​gAΔz=0. Dividing by AΔz and taking the derivative gives the equation above. For constant mass density ρf​, integration from the surface at zs​ gives
p(z)=psurface​+ρf​g(zs​−z)=psurface​+ρf​gh,h=zs​−z.
(73)
Thus pressure increases with depth h. For the stipulated water with mass density 1000 kgm−3 and gravitational acceleration 9.81 ms−2, depths 0.20 m and 0.30 m give gauge pressures 1962 Pa and 2943 Pa. Their difference produces the buoyancy of the example cube. [ref]
