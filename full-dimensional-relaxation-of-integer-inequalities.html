Full-dimensional relaxation of integer inequalities (Pε​={x:Ax≥b−ε1})

 Home Mathematics Area of mathematics Mathematical optimization Linear programming Ellipsoid method
OurBigBook.com Words: 85
For an integer linear polyhedron P={x∈Rn:Ax≥b} with coefficients bounded by U≥1, put D=[(n+1)U]n+1 and 0<ε<1/D. If x0​∈P, every h with ∥h∥2​≤ε/(nU) satisfies Ai​h≥−n​U∥h∥2​≥−ε. Thus Pε​ contains a positive-radius Euclidean ball and is a full-dimensional linear polyhedron. If P is empty, the normalized integer Farkas infeasibility gap gives λ≥0, ATλ=0, 1Tλ=1 and bTλ≥1/D. A point of Pε​ would give 0=λTAx≥bTλ−ε>0, a contradiction. Consequently this relaxation preserves emptiness and supplies the inner-volume bound needed by the ellipsoid method, even when P has empty interior.
