Free strain and blocked magnetostrictive stress (σ=E(ε−εmag))

 Home Physics Branches of physics Continuum mechanics Elasticity (physics) Magnetostriction
OurBigBook.com Words: 109
An eigenstrain is the deformation a material would prefer locally without applied stress. In a one-dimensional linear elasticity model, let E be Young's modulus, take tension as positive stress, and write σ=E(ε−εmag). A free rod has σ=0 and follows ε=εmag. A perfectly blocked rod has ε=0 and σ=−Eεmag. Thus positive preferred elongation produces compressive stress when blocked.
For stipulated E=100GPa, cross-sectional area A=1mm2, and εmag=20×10−6, blocking gives σ=−2MPa and axial force F=σA=−2N. This idealization ignores changes of magnetization caused by the load itself. A real magnetostrictive actuator therefore needs both magnetic and mechanical constitutive information: a free-length measurement alone does not determine its loaded motion.
