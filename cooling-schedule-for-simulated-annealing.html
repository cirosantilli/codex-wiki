Cooling schedule for simulated annealing (Tn​>0)

 Home Mathematics Area of mathematics Mathematical optimization Heuristic optimization Simulated annealing
OurBigBook.com Words: 200
A cooling schedule for simulated annealing specifies successive effective temperatures and how many proposals to attempt at each. For example, Tk​=T0​rk with 0<r<1, using the geometric sequence, can define stages of equal chosen length. Changing T changes the transition matrix: if the state probability distribution is μn​, then μn+1​=μn​PTn​​, which generally differs from πTn+1​​. Time spent at one temperature must be compared with its mixing time before equilibrium intuition is justified.
In the five-state barrier example for simulated annealing, fixed T∗​>0 gives a finite irreducible Markov chain, so 4 is eventually visited with probability one. That unbounded-time statement gives no fixed finite deadline. Over any finite number N of trials starting at 1, the probability of staying at 1 throughout is
∏n=0N−1​[1−21​e−1/Tn​−21​e−3/Tn​]>0.
(25)
Each factor is the rejection probability at 1. This is an explicit failure event even when every Tn​ is positive: the best visited objective function value remains 1, above the global minimum value 0. More exploratory proposals can help cross a barrier; cooling before those moves occur can preserve a poor local minimum. Successful asymptotic schedules need additional analysis of the allowed moves and barriers.
Historically, Scott Kirkpatrick, Charles Daniel Gelatt Jr. and Mario P. Vecchi connected statistical mechanics with mathematical optimization in 1983. They adapted the Metropolis–Hastings algorithm to cooling searches with symmetric proposals and illustrated computer-design problems.[ref]
