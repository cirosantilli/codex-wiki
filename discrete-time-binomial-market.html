Discrete-time binomial market (St+1​∈{uSt​,dSt​},Bt+1​=RBt​)

 Home Mathematics Area of mathematics Mathematical optimization Mathematical finance Binomial options pricing model
OurBigBook.com Words: 609 Articles: 6
At each node a positive stock price moves to uSt​ or dSt​, with 0<d<u, while a bank account grows by a fixed gross factor R>0. Assume both successors have positive physical probability, no dividends, a frictionless market with no trading costs, divisible holdings, unrestricted short selling, and borrowing and lending at the same fixed R. A self-financing portfolio receives no outside cash after its initial funding; subsequent rebalancing uses its own proceeds.
This binomial market excludes arbitrage exactly when d<R<u. If R≤d, borrowing S0​ to buy one share gives terminal gains S0​(u−R) or S0​(d−R); these are nonnegative and one is positive. If R≥u, short selling one share and lending its proceeds gives the analogous arbitrage. Strict inequalities allow a positive risk-neutral probability for each state. The two traded payoffs also span both successor states, giving market completeness relative to the information generated by this tree.
