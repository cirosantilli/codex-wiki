Exponential survival risk over an interval (P(a<T≤b∣T>a)=1−e−λ(b−a))

 Home Mathematics Area of mathematics Probability and statistics Survival analysis Constant hazard survival model
OurBigBook.com Words: 147
Let the survival time T have an exponential distribution with rate λ=0.2 per year. Its hazard function is h(t)=0.2 per year, its cumulative hazard is H(t)=0.2t for t in years, and its survival function is S(t)=e−0.2t. For a subject still event-free after five years, the conditional probability of an occurrence within the next two years is
P(5<T≤7∣T>5)​=1−S(5)S(7)​=1−e−0.4≈0.329680.​
(24)
The original population's probability of an occurrence between those same endpoints is instead S(5)−S(7)=e−1−e−1.4≈0.121282. Dividing by S(5)≈0.367879 recovers the conditional answer. The memoryless property makes the two-year conditional answer independent of the preceding five years.
For just Δ=0.01 year, the exact conditional event probability is 1−e−0.002≈0.001998, close to the instantaneous approximation h(5)Δ=0.002. For two years that approximation gives 0.4, whereas the exact answer is about 0.329680. At the single exact time five, the event probability is zero. These are three different quantities: an instantaneous hazard function with inverse-time units, a short-interval approximation, and an exact finite-interval probability.
