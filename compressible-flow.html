Transonic flow

Words: 27
Compressible flow OurBigBook.com
A compressible flow that passes between subsonic and supersonic regions. A smooth steady crossing of a sonic point requires the corresponding regularity condition; a discontinuous transition can instead involve a shock wave.

Stagnation temperature (Tstag​)

Words: 34
Compressible flow OurBigBook.com
The temperature reached by bringing a flow to rest adiabatically while converting its kinetic energy to enthalpy. For a calorically perfect gas, Tstag​=T+u2/(2cp​)=T[1+(γ−1)M2/2]. It is conserved across an adiabatic normal shock even though entropy increases and stagnation pressure decreases.

Choked flow

Words: 45
Compressible flow OurBigBook.com
Flow whose mass flux is limited by a sonic condition in a controlling section, for fixed upstream thermodynamic conditions. In a smooth isentropic converging-diverging nozzle, the throat reaches Mach number one. Lowering downstream pressure further cannot indefinitely increase that fixed-upstream throat mass flux; shocks and downstream structure can still change.

Quasi-one-dimensional flow

Words: 51
Compressible flow OurBigBook.com
A model representing a passage by its varying cross-sectional area and approximately uniform section-averaged flow variables. It neglects detailed transverse velocity and thermodynamic structure. In a smooth lossless nozzle, conservation laws then connect area to Mach number; this model does not by itself determine the curved sonic surface of an arbitrary multidimensional passage.

Isothermal flow through molecular hydrogen formation (ρ1​u1​=ρ2​u2​,p1​+ρ1​u12​=p2​+ρ2​u22​)

Words: 112
Compressible flow OurBigBook.com
Across a thin steady transition with negligible external impulse, mass conservation and momentum conservation give the displayed relations. If the gas stays isothermal while neutral atomic hydrogen becomes molecular hydrogen, the mean particle mass doubles and a22​=a12​/2 for its isothermal sound speed. Let J=u1​+a12​/u1​. The downstream subsonic branch has u2​=[J−J2−2a12​​]/2, ρ2​/ρ1​=u1​/u2​, and p2​−p1​=ρ1​u1​(u1​−u2​)>0.
For u1​≪a1​, the leading behavior is u2​≃u1​/2, ρ2​≃2ρ1​ and p2​≃p1​, at unchanged temperature. Radiation of the molecular binding energy prevents that released energy from necessarily heating the gas; it does not, by itself, establish an isothermal thermal closure. If temperature evolution is allowed, an energy balance and a cooling model are required to determine all four changes uniquely.

Self-similar blast wave

Words: 440 Articles: 6
Compressible flow OurBigBook.com
A self-similar blast wave is an expanding shock wave whose post-shock profiles retain their shape in a rescaled radius or distance. A fixed explosion energy, ambient mass density and geometry set the front scaling through conservation of total energy. The dimensionless coefficient requires the profiles and an energy-normalization convention.

Isothermal shock (ρ2​/ρ1​=M12​)

Words: 195 Articles: 2
Compressible flow OurBigBook.com
An isothermal shock joins two states at the same temperature and isothermal sound speed. Mass and momentum conservation imply u1​u2​=cs2​ for a nontrivial planar jump, so the compression ratio is the upstream isothermal Mach number squared. Heat exchange replaces adiabatic energy conservation across the gas alone.

Dissociating isothermal shock (2χ2−(M12​+1)χ+M12​=0)

Words: 114
Isothermal shock OurBigBook.com
Suppose molecular hydrogen is fully dissociated into atoms across a steady planar shock, with the same far-upstream and far-downstream temperature and negligible binding-energy mass correction. The upstream isothermal sound speed obeys a12​=kB​T/(2mH​), while a22​=2a12​. Conservation of mass gives χ=ρ2​/ρ1​=u1​/u2​. Momentum conservation then gives u12​+a12​=u12​/χ+2a12​χ, producing the displayed quadratic with M1​=u1​/a1​.
The high root approaches M12​/2 at large upstream Mach number. Its real compressive domain is M1​≥1+2​. At the endpoint the downstream flow is sonic; above it the high root is subsonic downstream. Completion of dissociation and maintenance of equal temperatures additionally require a consistent chemical and radiative energy budget. The two flux equations alone do not establish that physical realizability.

Mach number (M)

Words: 967 Articles: 5
Compressible flow OurBigBook.com
The Mach number is the ratio of fluid flow speed to local sound speed.
More precisely, the Mach number is the dimensionless quantity
M=c∣vbody​−ugas​∣​=cU​,
(11)
where U is the magnitude of the body's relative velocity through the surrounding gas, and c>0 is the local sound speed measured in the gas rest reference frame. For a fluid flow past a stationary body, the same Mach number uses the local fluid speed in the body's rest reference frame. A Mach number below, equal to, or above one means subsonic flow, sonic flow, or supersonic flow, respectively. The denominator is a property of the local gas: its sound speed depends on temperature and composition.[ref]
The relative velocity matters even when the body is at rest in a wind tunnel. A uniform change of reference frame subtracts the same velocity from body and gas, so their relative velocity and the Mach number stay unchanged under a Galilean transformation. Ground speed alone cannot supply U when there is wind. For an original collinear example, a body moving at 680ms−1 over the ground through gas moving in the same direction at 170ms−1 has U=510ms−1. With a stipulated sound speed c=340ms−1, its Mach number is 1.5, rather than 2. A moving observer changes apparent acoustic wave arrival times, as in the radial Mach number, without changing the body's Mach number relative to the gas.
Why is the threshold one so useful? Acoustic waves carry small pressure disturbances at the sound speed. In subsonic flow, such information can travel upstream relative to the body; in a uniform supersonic flow, it is swept downstream. The Mach cone turns this difference in communication into visible geometry. Matching Mach number between a wind tunnel and flight is therefore essential to matching compressible flow effects.[ref]
An actual supersonic wind tunnel experiment makes the distinction between wavefronts and shock waves concrete. In NASA Glenn's 1-by-1-foot wind tunnel, interchangeable converging-diverging nozzle blocks set the Mach number. A strut-mounted exhaust-nozzle model had a nosecone, an air plenum and transparent sidewalls for schlieren photography. Before installing the model, the researchers photographed the empty test section: two shock wave patterns originated at a wall joint after the nozzle block. This control guided model placement to keep reflected shock waves away from the plume-study region. The rig included internal total pressure and static pressure sensing and a traversing external static pressure probe. A baseline nozzle and previous pressure data supplied a planned validation comparison; the report distinguishes this plan from the observed empty-tunnel control. This establishes an apparatus origin for those visible shock waves, not a measurement of the Mach cone from an infinitesimal source. The reported finite shock wave angle cannot simply be inserted into the Mach angle formula to calibrate M: shock wave strength and geometry also matter.[ref]
In conventional schlieren photography, a bright slit source and two concave mirrors send parallel light rays through the wind tunnel and focus them at a knife edge before an optical camera. Spatial changes in mass density cause refraction; the knife edge converts the deflection into image contrast, making shock waves visible. This is the general optical principle, rather than a specification of every component in the preceding rig. A photograph projects a three-dimensional fluid flow into two dimensions; contrast alone does not provide a calibrated pressure jump or acoustic wave amplitude.[ref]

Moving-point acoustic wavefronts ((x−Uτ)2+y2=c2τ2,τ<0)

Words: 221
Mach number OurBigBook.com
Take a steady moving point source in uniform resting gas, with constant sound speed c and source velocity Uex​. Place the source at the origin at the current time t=0. In the linear acoustics model, an emission at an earlier time τ<0 has a spherical wavefront centered at (Uτ,0,0) with radius −cτ. An axial cross-section is the circle in the displayed equation. It is centered at the old release position, not the source's present position. The circles describe the geometry of weak acoustic waves, not a measured shock wave or its pressure jump.
For M=0.5, every old circle contains the present source because its center is U∣τ∣ from the origin while its radius is the larger distance c∣τ∣. It reaches ahead of the source. For M=2, the rightmost point of every old circle is (U−c)τ<0: the source outruns all of its old wavefronts. The continuously emitted wavefronts have a common geometric envelope, the Mach cone. The plotted discrete release times sample this continuous family; rotating the axial section about the motion axis recovers the three-dimensional construction.

The Mach angle is the half-angle between a Mach cone generator and its backward motion axis for a steady point source in a uniform three-dimensional medium with isotropy and constant sound speed. It obeys
sinμ=Uc​=M1​,0<μ<2π​,M>1.
(29)

The resulting right triangle has hypotenuse Us and opposite side cs, giving the displayed sine ratio. The emission age cancels: every wavefront has the same tangent directions, so a continuum of expanding spheres produces one simple Mach cone.
