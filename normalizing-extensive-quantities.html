Normalizing extensive quantities

OurBigBook.com Words: 655 Articles: 2
Dividing two extensive quantities with the same replication factor produces an intensive quantity:
λYλX​=YX​,Y>0.
(80)
Division by volume gives a density, such as mass density ρ=m/V or energy density U/V. Division by mass gives a specific quantity, such as specific internal energy u=U/m or specific volume v=V/m. Division by amount of substance gives a molar quantity, such as molar volume Vm​=V/n. Their units are respectively kgm−3, Jm−3, Jkg−1, m3kg−1 and m3mol−1. The denominator must be stated: “per particle”, “per mole” and “per unit mass” are different normalizations.
For a hypothetical uniform parcel with mass 2kg, volume 0.002m3 and internal energy 40kJ relative to a specified reference, u=20kJkg−1 and ρ=1000kgm−3. Three independent copies have 6kg, 0.006m3 and 120kJ; both ratios remain unchanged. This permits comparison of materials without confusing the amount tested with the state tested.
If X and Y are additive but the parcels have different ri​=Xi​/Yi​, their composite ratio is a weighted arithmetic mean:
rtotal​=∑i​Yi​∑i​Xi​​=∑i​∑j​Yj​Yi​​ri​.
(81)
The denominator supplies the weights: specific internal energy uses mass weights, while mass density uses volume weights. This identity is accounting for the specified subsystems; predicting a new state after mixing also needs the relevant equation of state.
