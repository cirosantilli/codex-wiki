Derivative along a curve (dtd​F(γ(t))=∇F(γ(t))⋅γ′(t))

 ... Analysis Real analysis Calculus Derivative Chain rule Multivariable chain rule
OurBigBook.com Words: 322 Articles: 1
If F:Rn→R is differentiable at the point γ(t) of a differentiable parametric curve, the multivariable chain rule gives
dtd​F(γ(t))=∇F(γ(t))⋅γ′(t).
(105)
The gradient supplies the local spatial rates; the velocity vector γ′(t) supplies the direction and rate of movement. Their dot product is the change in the sampled scalar field per unit parameter. If the parameter is time, this is a rate per unit time; a directional derivative per unit distance instead uses a unit vector.
Take F(x,y)=x2+2y2 and the unit circle path γ(t)=(cost,sint). Along the path,
F(γ(t))=1+sin2t,dtd​F(γ(t))=2sintcost=sin2t.
(106)
At t=π/4, the gradient is (2​,22​), the velocity vector is (−1/2​,1/2​), and their dot product is −1+2=1. The sampled value is 3/2 and increases at rate one, even though one coordinate is decreasing. This is why a moving observation must combine every coordinate's contribution.
For a level curve, F(γ(t)) is constant, so the derivative along a curve is zero and its velocity vector is orthogonal to the gradient. Along other paths, the same formula combines a field's geometry with the observer's motion. [ref].
