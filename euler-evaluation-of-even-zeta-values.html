Euler evaluation of even zeta values (ζ(2r)=(−1)r+1B2r​(2π)2r/(2(2r)!))

The Euler evaluation of even zeta values turns a family of series into exact expressions using Bernoulli numbers, factorials and powers of π. For every positive integer r,
n=1∑∞​n2r1​=ζ(2r)=2(2r)!(−1)r+1B2r​(2π)2r​​.

Here ζ is the Riemann zeta function, defined by its convergent series at these arguments, and the Bernoulli number convention is B1​=−1/2. The alternating sign combines with the sign of B2r​ to give a positive sum.[ref]
Substituting B2​=1/6, B4​=−1/30 and B6​=1/42 gives
ζ(2)=6π2​,ζ(4)=90π4​,ζ(6)=945π6​​.

The first identity answers the Basel problem. It is surprising because the original series only mentions reciprocal squares of integers, while its exact sum involves the circle constant π. The cotangent partial-fraction expansion supplies a connection between the two: trigonometric functions encode equally spaced integer poles, whose expansion brings the reciprocal powers into view.
For an original numerical comparison, the first ten terms give ∑n=110​n−2≈1.549767731, while π2/6≈1.644934067. The omitted positive terms still contribute approximately 0.095166336. For fourth powers, the ten-term partial sum is approximately 1.082036583, only 0.000286650 below π4/90≈1.082323234. Both are convergent series, but the second approaches its limit much faster.
Exact evaluation is useful beyond obtaining more digits. It supplies an independent check on a numerical summation, exposes how Bernoulli numbers organize infinitely many identities, and makes the remaining series truncation error measurable without knowing any omitted terms individually. The even integer condition is essential to this formula: putting r=3/2 into it is not a way to evaluate ζ(3), since the required Bernoulli number index and factorial expression here are indexed by positive integers.
Historically, Leonhard Euler announced reciprocal-square and reciprocal-fourth-power evaluations in §2 of On the sums of series of reciprocals by Leonhard Euler. That paper was presented on 5 December 1735 and published in 1740, as the English translation's first-page note records. His §§3–8 develop a route through the sine power series and relationships between roots and coefficients. The cotangent calculation below is a concise modern presentation of the connection.[ref]
