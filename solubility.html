Solubility

 Home Chemistry Chemical bond Ionic bonding Physical properties of salts
OurBigBook.com Words: 2k Articles: 19
Solubility specifies the amount that can remain dissolved at chemical equilibrium under stated conditions; it is different from how quickly a sample dissolves. Separating ions from an ionic crystal costs energy, but solvation establishes new interactions with the solvent. In water, hydration of ions couples charged ions to polar water molecules. The net enthalpy of dissolution can have either sign; entropy and concentration also enter the Gibbs free energy balance. Consequently, being a salt does not guarantee high solubility in water or any other solvent.[ref]
Low solubility must also be distinguished from weak electrolyte behavior. A small amount of an ionic compound can dissolve essentially completely as ions, giving few charge carriers because little material dissolved. Calling that a weak electrolyte solely because its chemical solution conducts poorly would conflate two different questions.[ref]
