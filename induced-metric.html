Induced metric (g=F∗h)

 ... Mathematics Area of mathematics Geometry and topology Differential geometry Riemannian geometry Riemannian manifold
OurBigBook.com Words: 137
An immersion F:M→N into a Riemannian manifold (N,h) inherits the Riemannian metric g=F∗h: measure two tangent vectors after mapping them into the surrounding tangent space. This is a pullback of a Riemannian metric, with
gp​(u,v)=hF(p)​(dFp​u,dFp​v).
(4)
The injective derivative of an immersion makes this inner product positive-definite. For a parametrized surface in Euclidean space, its coefficients are simply the scalar products of the coordinate tangent vectors: the first fundamental form.
For the circular cylinder F(θ,z)=(Rcosθ,Rsinθ,z), the tangent vectors Fθ​ and Fz​ are orthogonal, with squared norms R2 and 1. Consequently
ds2=R2dθ2+dz2.
(5)
The unrolled coordinate x=Rθ gives ds2=dx2+dz2. This calculation explains intrinsic flatness of a circular cylinder and makes Riemannian distance on a circular cylinder a planar problem with repeated endpoint lifts.
Preserving a Riemannian metric locally preserves the arc length of every mapped curve, but it need not preserve distances measured by unrestricted paths in the surrounding space. The great-circle distance between two sphere points, for example, exceeds their straight chord length unless they coincide. [ref].
