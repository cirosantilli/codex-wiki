Reduced mass (μ)

 Home Physics Branches of physics Classical mechanics Center of mass
OurBigBook.com Words: 1k Articles: 4
The reduced mass is the effective mass for the two-particle relative coordinate of two bodies with constant positive masses m1​,m2​:
μ=m1​+m2​m1​m2​​,μ1​=m1​1​+m2​1​.
(251)
Both bodies can respond to their mutual force, so their relative acceleration is greater than if one were fixed. Consequently 0<μ<min(m1​,m2​). This explains why using either individual mass, or their sum, generally gives the wrong internal oscillation rate. The same reduced mass describes a two-body problem, a free two-mass spring, and the approximate molecular vibration of a diatomic molecule.[ref]
For equal masses m1​=m2​=m, μ=m/2. Two 1kg bodies therefore have μ=0.5kg: in their stationary centre of mass frame a relative speed 2m/s means individual speeds 1m/s and total kinetic energy 1J. With m1​=1kg and m2​=100kg, instead μ=100/101kg≈0.990099kg. At the same relative speed, the individual speeds are 200/101 and 2/101m/s, and the total kinetic energy is 200/101J. These are calculations for stipulated masses and velocities.
For fixed m1​ and q=m2​/m1​>0, μ/m1​=q/(1+q). The heavy-partner limit q→∞ gives μ→m1​ from below; the relative error incurred by replacing μ with m1​ is (m1​−μ)/μ=1/q. At q=100 this is 1%. Treating the heavy body as fixed is thus a controlled approximation to internal motion, rather than an exact consequence of its being heavier.
