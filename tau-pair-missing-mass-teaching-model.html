Tau-pair missing-mass teaching model

Choose mτ​=2GeV and s​=6GeV purely for illustration. Then each parent has Eτ​=3GeV, ∣pτ​∣=5​GeV and β=5​/3. Treat all six daughters as massless, including the charged electron and antimuon, and generate each three-body particle decay with constant squared decay amplitude. Choose the parent direction isotropically and the two decays independently; this omits the physical production angular law and spin correlations.
The Lorentz-invariant phase space factorizes through t=q2 into a two-body decay to ℓ+q, followed by a two-body decay of q:
dΦ3​=2πdt​dΦ2​(Pτ​;pℓ​,q)dΦ2​(q;q1​,q2​).
(45)
After angular integration the first factor contributes 1−t/mτ2​=x, while the massless second factor is constant. Since ∣dt∣=mτ2​dx, the normalized chosen density is f(x)=2x. Drawing x=U​ with U uniform on [0,1] implements this density. Isotropic directions, followed by a Lorentz boost for the invisible pair and another for the parent, conserve the complete four-momentum of each generated event.
Define transverse acoplanarity by α=π−Δϕ, where Δϕ∈[0,π] is the angle between the two visible momentum projections perpendicular to the beam. A fully visible two-body event with zero initial transverse momentum has α=0 and Mmiss2​=0. The toy tau lepton events spread away from that point. These observables are complementary: an angular imbalance asks about directions, while the missing invariant mass also uses the visible energies and longitudinal momenta.
A Lorentz-invariant phase space density supplies allowed configurations and their geometric measure. A physical weak interaction decay amplitude reweights them and correlates spins. Consequently the plotted density is a lesson about conservation and available configurations, not a predicted experimental rate or a fit to the discovery sample. The massless charged-daughter approximation is also unsuitable near the muon rest-energy threshold.
