Quadratic minimization with a moving upper bound (x∗​(b)=min(2,b))

Consider the minimization problem
V(b)=minx≤b​(x−2)2,b∈R.
(73)
The objective function is a strictly convex function with its unconstrained optimization minimum at x=2. If b<2, it decreases throughout the feasible set until the bound x=b is reached. If b≥2, the feasible set already contains 2. Hence
x∗​(b)=min(2,b),V(b)=(max(2−b,0))2.
(74)
With g(x)=x−b, the optimization Lagrangian and KKT conditions are
L=(x−2)2+λ(x−b),x≤b,λ≥0,2(x−2)+λ=0,λ(x−b)=0.
(75)
Substituting x∗​(b) gives the unique inequality Lagrange multiplier
λ∗​(b)=2max(2−b,0).
(76)
For b<2, the active constraint has λ∗​>0. For b>2, there is strict slack b−x∗​=b−2 and λ∗​=0. At b=2, the active constraint has zero Lagrange multiplier: the boundary happens to pass through the unconstrained optimum. Feasibility and complementary slackness must both be checked; solving only 2(x−2)+λ=0 would allow infeasible candidates.
An independent certificate follows by completing the square:
L=(x−2+2λ​)2+λ(2−b)−4λ2​,db​(λ)=infx∈R​L=λ(2−b)−4λ2​.
(77)
The Lagrange dual function is a concave function of λ. Its maximum over λ≥0 occurs at λ∗​(b) and equals V(b). Thus every case has an explicit lower bound attained by a feasible point; the KKT conditions provide a certificate that can be checked without searching all real numbers.
