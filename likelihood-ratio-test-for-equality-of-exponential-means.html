Likelihood-ratio test for equality of exponential means (Λ=nnmm(n+m)n+m​Tn(1−T)m)

 ... Area of mathematics Probability and statistics Statistical model Statistical modelling Statistical hypothesis test Likelihood-ratio test
OurBigBook.com Words: 113
For two independent statistical samples from exponential distributions with positive means θ and μ, write A=∑i=1n​Xi​, B=∑j=1m​Yj​ and T=A/(A+B). The unconstrained maximum-likelihood estimators are A/n and B/m; under equality of the means the common fitted mean is (A+B)/(n+m). Dividing the constrained maximum likelihood function by its unrestricted maximum gives the displayed likelihood ratio. Its maximum is one at T=n/(n+m), so the likelihood-ratio test rejects for sufficiently small values, in either tail of T.
Under the null hypothesis, the sums have independent gamma distributions with a common rate, so T∼Beta(n,m) by beta sampling by gamma ratios. This permits exact calibration. For large samples with n/(n+m)→ρ∈(0,1), the Wilks theorem gives −2logΛd​χ12​. Its upper-tail probability is the asymptotic p-value. The one statistical degree of freedom is the difference between the unrestricted and constrained parameter dimensions.
