The AC Josephson effect is V=(Φ0​/2π)δ˙. Since the imposed flux is held fixed when defining a small-signal inductance, differentiate c to obtain
I˙=2IC​cosϕcosδδ˙,L(δ,Φ)=I˙V​=4πIC​cosϕcosδΦ0​​.​
(55)
On the stable small-current branch with cosϕ>0, δ≃0 and the result reduces to the printed expression,
Leff​=4πIC​cos(πΦ/Φ0​)Φ0​​.​
(56)
The stable small-signal inductance of a symmetric DC SQUID requires ∣I∣≪2IC​∣cosϕ∣, stronger than I≪IC​ near a half-integer flux quantum. If cosϕ<0, the stable zero-current phase is δ≃π and cosδ≃−1. Its inductance is positive:
Lstable​=4πIC​∣cos(πΦ/Φ0​)∣Φ0​​.​
(57)
For example, Φ=Φ0​ makes the printed signed formula negative when expanded about δ=0, but that phase is an energy maximum. Expanding about the stable δ=π corrects the sign. At ideal half-integer flux the critical current vanishes and the linear inductance formally diverges; a finite transport current cannot satisfy the small-current approximation there. Geometric inductance and other nonidealities then have to be retained instead of extending the simple formula through its pole.
