Cool a thick superconducting ring through its transition in a controlled external flux, remove or change the applied field while keeping the ring superconducting, and measure the persistent trapped flux using a calibrated pickup coil and sensitive magnetometer. Repeating while varying the cooling flux selects successive winding sectors. The measured flux is close to steps separated by Φ0​ when the kinetic-inductance correction is negligible. A SQUID-based flux readout can resolve that spacing; scanning local magnetic imaging offers another way to observe the trapped flux without identifying the ring current with an ordinary dissipative transport current.
The experiment tests the phase-winding/fluxoid relation in a. Self-inductance, London penetration depth and the size of the ring determine how nearly the measured geometric flux follows integer steps. A periodic response alone should not be used to infer an absolute trapped flux without calibration and the relevant inductive corrections.
