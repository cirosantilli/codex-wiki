The sharp solid curve is the 4.2 K spectrum; the broadened dashed curve is the 77 K spectrum. At low temperature, a strong discrete exciton transition appears just below the continuum edge, with weaker higher members converging toward the band gap. Heating broadens those resonances through scattering, reduces their spectral resolution, and shifts the band edge to lower photon energy. The lower-energy broad dashed maximum is therefore not evidence for a second unrelated low-temperature band gap.
Read approximate energies from the native figure: the main low-temperature peak is near 1.610 eV and a weaker feature is near 1.617–1.618 eV, with the continuum edge around 1.620 eV. A three-dimensional hydrogenic series En​=Eg​−R∗/n2 then gives
Eg​≃1.62 eV,R∗≃10 meV.​
(45)
For example, E2​−E1​=3R∗/4 predicts about 7.5 meV, consistent with those feature spacings. These are graphical estimates at roughly meV precision, not extra digits extracted from the line widths. The strong direct interband onset and subgap series support a direct-gap Wannier-Mott interpretation. The continuum absorption scale is of order 106 m−1, corresponding to an optical absorption length of order a micrometre. At 77 K, kB​T≃6.6 meV is already comparable with the inferred binding scale, explaining why a sharp Rydberg series is poorly resolved; at 4.2 K, kB​T≃0.36 meV.
