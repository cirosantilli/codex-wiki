Subtract the reciprocal lattice vector (0,0,8), leaving a reduced wave vector (0.2,0,0) along a. A linear-acoustic estimate gives
q=0.20.353 nm2π​=3.560×109 m−1,vg​≃ℏqE​=2.99×103 ms−1.
(16)
Interpret the quoted intrinsic width as the full width at half maximum in energy, Γ=0.1 meV. The corresponding energy-relaxation mean lifetime is τ=ℏ/Γ=6.58×10−12 s, giving mean free path vg​τ≃19.7 nm. If the given width instead denotes a half width, the inferred mean lifetime differs by a factor of two; the convention must be specified.
Approximating the thermal average by this velocity and mean lifetime, and using the isotropic angular factor ⟨va2​⟩=vg2​/3, kinetic theory gives
κph,a​≃31​CV​vg2​τ=0.72 Wm−1K−1.​
(17)
Using a single purely a-directed effective channel without angular averaging gives 2.17 W m−1 K−1, still far below the observed total 39 W m−1 K−1. The isotropic estimate is roughly 54 times smaller than the measured total.
The calculation is not a complete thermal-transport average. Electrons in this metallic compound can carry substantial heat, the supplied total heat capacity need not be entirely phononic, and one 7 meV phonon need not represent all thermally active branches and mean lifetimes. At 20 K, kB​T≃1.72 meV, so that measured mode is relatively high in the thermal distribution; lower-energy modes can have much longer mean lifetimes. A measured phase velocity is also only an estimate of the group velocity if the branch is nonlinear.
electron-phonon interaction is a plausible damping mechanism in a phonon-mediated superconductor, but the measurement is above its 15 K transition and probes the normal state. It cannot be attributed directly to decay into an already established superconducting condensate. Below the transition an energy gap can modify electron-hole damping. The weak-coupling scale 2Δ≃3.52kB​Tc​≃4.55 meV is below the 7 meV phonon energy, so even superconductivity need not prohibit all pair-breaking damping of this mode. Temperature-dependent spectra would distinguish that mechanism from defects or anharmonic phonon decay.
