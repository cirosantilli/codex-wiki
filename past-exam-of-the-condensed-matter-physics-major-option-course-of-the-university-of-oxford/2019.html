Infrared spectroscopy measures absorption as the incident photon frequency is swept through molecular normal mode frequencies. In the electric-dipole approximation the interaction is −p⋅E. Expanding the molecular electric dipole moment in a normal coordinate Q gives
p(Q)=p(0)+(∂Q∂p​)0​Q+⋯.
(1)
The fundamental one-quantum vibrational transition dipole moment is proportional to the derivative multiplying Q. Thus
(∂p/∂Q)0​=0​
(2)
is the criterion for vibrational infrared activity; the derivative must also have a component along the incident polarization of an electromagnetic wave. A permanent molecular electric dipole moment is not necessary. Absorption occurs near ℏω=Ev+1​−Ev​, with broadened lines reflecting finite lifetimes and the molecular environment.

ii OurBigBook.com
Use the indicated out-of-plane displacements and the equivalence of the four oxygen sites and of the two carbon sites in the isolated planar oxalate ion. In V1​, the two oxygen atoms on one side move oppositely to those on the other side, and the two carbon atoms also move oppositely. Their dipole changes cancel. The mode is even under molecular inversion symmetry, which independently forbids an electric-dipole transition.
In V2​, all four oxygen atoms move together out of the plane while both carbon atoms move in the opposite direction. The positive and negative charge distributions acquire a relative out-of-plane displacement, producing a nonzero derivative of the molecular electric dipole moment.
In V3​, two oxygen atoms move out and two move in, in a checkerboard arrangement, while the carbon atoms are stationary. The equivalent oxygen contributions cancel. This mode illustrates that being odd under inversion symmetry is necessary but not sufficient for vibrational infrared activity: its full symmetry does not transform like any component of an electric dipole moment.
V2​ is IR active;V1​,V3​ are IR inactive.​
(3)
These conclusions concern the isolated-molecule displacements drawn in the source; a different crystal environment can alter selection rules through additional symmetry breaking.

The primitive unit cell contains two carbon atoms and four edge-shared oxygen atoms, each contributing one half: n=2+4/2=4 atoms. Equivalently, one molecular repeat contributes its two carbon atoms but only half of each of its four shared oxygen atoms. There are therefore 3n=12 phonon branches.
At the Brillouin zone centre, three rigid translations give three acoustic phonon branches; the remaining 3n−3 are optical phonon branches:
3 acoustic+9 optical=12 phonon branches.​
(5)
A free layer has two in-plane acoustic modes and an out-of-plane flexural acoustic mode. A two-dimensional lattice does not imply that its atoms can move only in two directions. Artificially suppressing all out-of-plane motion would instead define a different model with two acoustic and six optical branches.

ii OurBigBook.com
For ordinary one-phonon infrared optical absorption, both an energy and a momentum selection rule must hold. The optical photon has negligible crystal momentum on the scale of the Brillouin zone, so crystal momentum conservation selects q≃0 modulo a genuine reciprocal lattice vector. In addition, the mode must change the macroscopic electric polarization, with a component along the incident polarization of an electromagnetic wave.
In terms of atomic displacements uj​ and Born effective charge tensors Zj∗​, the relevant derivative is proportional to ∑j​Zj∗​uj​ per primitive unit cell. Thus an optical phonon at q=0 is not automatically infrared active: its dipole contributions may cancel. Disorder-assisted and multiphonon processes relax these simple first-order selection rules.

a OurBigBook.com
For a free transition-metal ion, degeneracy among different orbital states can support an orbital contribution to its magnetic moment. In a crystal, the electric fields and bonding of the surrounding ligands split those states. If the orbital ground state becomes nondegenerate and is separated from excited orbital states, its wavefunction can usually be chosen real in zero field. Since the orbital angular momentum operator is imaginary in a real-orbital basis, its expectation value then vanishes.
This suppression is orbital quenching. The remaining leading magnetic moment is the spin contribution, approximately gμB​S with g≃2. Spin-orbit coupling admixes excited orbital states and produces residual anisotropy and shifts in the g-factor; quenching does not mean that every orbital matrix element or every higher-order orbital response vanishes. A degenerate ground orbital multiplet need not be quenched until a further distortion removes its degeneracy.

For the regular octahedron, repulsion from the six oxygen ligands raises the two eg​ orbitals, dz2​ and dx2−y2​, above the three t2g​ orbitals, dxy​,dxz​,dyz​. The latter point between the metal-ligand axes and experience less direct repulsion. The crystal field splitting is therefore a lower triplet and an upper doublet.
For the 3d2 V3+ ion, Hund's rules put the two electrons into different t2g​ orbitals with parallel spins, giving S=1. There are three choices for the unoccupied t2g​ orbital, so the orbital ground state is triply degenerate. Its spin degeneracy is separate. This orbital degeneracy makes a symmetry-lowering Jahn-Teller effect possible.

Solution

ii OurBigBook.com
Take the elongation axis as z. The axial oxygen ligands move farther away while the equatorial ones move closer, at fixed mean bond length. Orbitals containing z are stabilized relative to their in-plane partners. Thus dxz​,dyz​ move below dxy​ within t2g​, while dz2​ moves below dx2−y2​ within eg​. The schematic level ordering appears in the figure below; the octahedral gap remains open for a small distortion.
The two parallel-spin electrons occupy dxz​ and dyz​, one each. Filling the entire lower doublet gives a unique orbital configuration, so its orbital degeneracy is one and the spin is still S=1. This is the orbitally quenched configuration relevant to the later spin Hamiltonian.
The energetic preference can be made explicit in a traceless linear splitting model. For an elongation coordinate Q>0, write the t2g​ levels relative to their barycentre as (−aQ,−aQ,2aQ) with a>0. Their two-electron ground energy decreases by 2aQ. For an equal-magnitude compression, the levels are (−2aQ,aQ,aQ) and the two-electron energy decreases only by aQ. With the same quadratic elastic cost, minimizing −2aQ+kQ2/2 gives four times the stabilization of minimizing −aQ+kQ2/2. Thus the small-distortion Jahn-Teller effect favors elongation for this high-spin d2 filling. The argument assumes the linear coupling and harmonic elastic model, rather than claiming that symmetry alone fixes all real-material distortions.

For an x-elongated A site and a y-elongated B site, Ψ=ϵ, with the opposite sign for the domain interchanging the pattern. The electron paramagnetic resonance data determine its magnitude from
∣Ψ∣=γ−1+δ−1(hν/μB​)(B∥−1​−B⊥−1​)​.​
(35)
For small splitting this is equivalently proportional to B⊥​−B∥​, but the reciprocal-field formula follows directly from the stated g-factors. It does not fix the sign of the structural domain. In a continuous transition ∣Ψ∣ tends continuously to zero at TJT​ and grows below it, eventually saturating. A mean-field sketch has ∣Ψ∣∝(TJT​−T)1/2 close to the transition; continuity alone does not determine that exponent. The last panel of the original orbital figure shows this illustrative behavior.

Choose the four basis-spin signs as (+,−,−,+) in the order of positions listed in the source; reversing all signs changes no diffraction intensity. Apart from the common magnetic form factor and perpendicular-moment factor, the magnetic structure factor is
FM​(hkl)​=m[eiπh−eiπk−eiπ(h+l)+eiπ(k+l)]=m[(−1)h−(−1)k][1−(−1)l].​

The first factor is nonzero exactly when h and k have opposite parity, and the second exactly when l is odd. Thus the magnetic Bragg peaks require
h+k=2n+1,l=2m+1.​
(37)
When both conditions hold, ∣FM​∣=4∣m∣ before geometric and magnetic-form-factor weights. These are magnetic selection rules for the antiferromagnetic component, not conditions forbidding all nuclear diffraction at other indices.

Measure several allowed magnetic reflections from an oriented single crystal by magnetic neutron diffraction, preferably with polarization analysis. A neutron couples to the component of the ordered magnetic moment perpendicular to the scattering vector Q. For a collinear arrangement, the geometric intensity factor is
1−(Q​⋅m)2.
(38)
Compare reflections whose Q point in different crystal directions, correcting for the magnetic form factor and known experimental geometry. Their relative intensities distinguish candidate spin directions; a moment parallel to Q gives no magnetic signal from that component. Polarized neutrons help separate nuclear and magnetic contributions. Observing only peak positions establishes periodicity, not the spin direction.

The canted pattern has opposite x components but a common z component. Write neighboring spins as SA​=S(cosθ,0,sinθ) and SB​=S(−cosθ,0,sinθ). Their scalar product is −S2cos2θ, and the cross-product magnitude is S2∣sin2θ∣.
There are three exchange bonds per spin, two eligible xy bonds per spin for the D term, and one single-ion term per spin. Hence
E=−S2[3Jcos2θ+Kcos2θ+2D∣sin2θ∣].

a OurBigBook.com
In a conventional superconductor, an attractive pairing interaction forms Cooper pairs and a phase-coherent condensate. The elementary fermionic excitations have a superconducting energy gap Δ; breaking a pair into two excitations costs at least 2Δ. The density of thermally excited quasiparticles is exponentially small for kB​T≪Δ. Nevertheless the vanishing DC electrical resistivity below the critical temperature is a property of the coherent supercurrent, not a claim that all thermal quasiparticles disappear. Below the relevant critical-current and critical-field limits, a homogeneous bulk sample carries dissipationless current; above the superconducting transition the normal resistivity returns.
In a semiconductor, the band gap separates filled valence states from empty conduction states because of the periodic crystal potential and orbital hybridization. Thermally exciting carriers across the gap produces both conduction electrons and valence holes. For an intrinsic sample,
n=p∝T3/2e−Eg​/(2kB​T),σ=e(nμe​+pμh​).
(44)
Thus its electrical resistivity generally rises rapidly on cooling, with an activated factor eEg​/(2kB​T) up to mobility and density-of-states prefactors. This differs from the zero-resistivity condensate response. Dopant freeze-out and extrinsic-carrier regimes require their own activation scales; the intrinsic formula is not universal for every doped sample.

Use a normal-metal--insulator--superconductor junction with a weak tunnelling barrier. For a nearly constant normal-metal density of states, the differential conductance probes the thermally broadened superconducting quasiparticle density of states. At sufficiently low temperature, the ideal BCS theory result is
Ns​(E)/N0​=ReE2−Δ2​∣E∣​,RN​dVdI​=Ns​(eV)/N0​.
(45)
There is very little single-electron tunnelling for ∣eV∣<Δ, while a sharp onset occurs at ∣eV∣=Δ. The gap can therefore be obtained from the two symmetric onset voltages, Δ=e∣Vonset​∣. For this one-superconductor junction the threshold is Δ/e, not 2Δ/e.
Integrating at zero temperature gives the useful current sketch
I(V)=eRN​sgnV​(eV)2−Δ2​Θ(∣eV∣−Δ).​
(46)
The curve is odd, nearly flat inside the gap, and approaches the normal-state line V/RN​ at large ∣V∣. The ideal gap-edge singularity is in the slope, not in the current itself. At finite temperature below the transition the onset rounds and thermally activated subgap current appears. Measuring several temperatures distinguishes this rounding from the closing of Δ(T).

Rearrange the source's condensate-current expression and integrate around a closed path lying in the superconducting material:
ℏ∮∇θ⋅dl=q∮A⋅dl+ns​qm​∮Js​⋅dl.
(47)
Single-valuedness of the superconducting wavefunction requires ∮∇θ⋅dl=2πn, and Stokes theorem gives ∮A⋅dl=Φ. Thus the exact result is fluxoid and flux quantization in a superconductor:
Φ+ns​q2m​∮Js​⋅dl=qnh​.​
(48)
Reversing the winding orientation absorbs the sign of q. Since a Cooper pair has ∣q∣=2e, the positive superconducting flux quantum is
Φ0​=2eh​≃2.068×10−15Wb.​
(49)
For a thick ring, choose the path deep enough that the supercurrent is negligible; then the enclosed magnetic flux is nΦ0​. This is the flux quantization usually intended in the question.
Qualification of the unqualified flux claim: a thin ring can have a nonzero kinetic contribution around every available path, so it is the fluxoid that is exactly quantized. For example, with zero applied flux and geometric and kinetic inductances Lg​=Lk​, a metastable winding-1 state has (Lg​+Lk​)I=Φ0​ but Φ=Lg​I=Φ0​/2. This concrete counterexample satisfies the given current law while its magnetic flux is not an integer multiple of Φ0​.
