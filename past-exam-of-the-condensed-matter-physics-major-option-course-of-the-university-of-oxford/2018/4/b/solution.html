At normal incidence from vacuum the reflectivity is
R=​ϵr​​+1ϵr​​−1​​2,
(33)
with the passive branch of the complex refractive index. Below the optical resonance the real permittivity rises towards its pole and reflectance increases. Between the TO and LO frequencies, the lossless permittivity is negative; the wave is evanescent and the reflectivity is unity in that idealization. Finite damping rounds and reduces this reststrahlen band. Above the LO edge, positive permittivity returns and reflectivity can become very small when the refractive index is near one, before rising towards its electronic-background value.
Reading the surviving source graph to its available precision gives
ℏωTO​≃45meV,ℏωLO​≃50meV,ωTO​≃6.84×1013s−1,ωLO​≃7.60×1013s−1.​
(34)
These are band-edge estimates, not a high-precision damping fit. The minimum immediately above the band is not exactly the LO zero: for zero damping ϵr​=1 occurs at ω2=(ϵ∞​ωLO2​−ωTO2​)/(ϵ∞​−1)>ωLO2​.
The high-energy plotted tail near 90meV has R≃0.22. Ignoring residual phonon dispersion gives n≃(1+R​)/(1−R​)=2.77 and ϵ≃7.65. Correcting with the finite-energy oscillator ratio gives
