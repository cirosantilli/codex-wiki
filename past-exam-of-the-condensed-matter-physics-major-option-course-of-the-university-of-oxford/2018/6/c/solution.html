As magnetic field grows from zero, a clean two-dimensional electron gas first has an ordinary finite longitudinal resistivity, then develops Shubnikov–de Haas oscillations as broadened Landau levels pass the chemical potential. Thermal and disorder broadening obscure the low-field oscillations; their visibility grows when the level spacing exceeds those broadening scales. In a well-developed integer quantum Hall regime, ρxx​ has minima close to zero on Hall plateaux and maxima during transitions between them, when extended states cross the chemical potential. Detailed peak heights and envelopes depend on disorder and scattering and are not fixed by quantization alone.
At fixed density the nominal filling is ν(B)=NS​h/(eB), so level-filling features are approximately periodic in 1/B, with period e/(NS​h) for resolved spin levels or 2e/(NS​h) for an unresolved spin pair. Increasing B lowers the filling; spin splitting can create additional odd-index features. The original schematic below shows these relations without pretending to reconstruct the missing data.
