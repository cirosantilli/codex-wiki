Use the explicit convention H=J1​∑⟨ij⟩​Si​⋅Sj​+J2​∑⟨⟨ij⟩⟩​Si​⋅Sj​, counting every bond once and taking dimensionless spins of length S. On the simple cubic lattice there are six nearest and twelve face-diagonal next-nearest neighbours.
For type I all six nearest bonds are antiparallel and all twelve next-nearest bonds parallel, so EI​/N=S2(−3J1​+6J2​). Choose the parallel direction of type II as z. It has two parallel and four antiparallel nearest bonds, and four parallel xy face diagonals but eight antiparallel xz,yz diagonals. Thus EII​/N=S2(−J1​−2J2​) and
NEII​−EI​​=2S2(J1​−4J2​).
(20)
Consequently, for the stated antiferromagnetic exchanges,
type I: J2​/J1​<1/4;type II: J2​/J1​>1/4;degenerate at 1/4.​
(21)
This comparison is also consistent with the full classical spiral energy. With ui​=cos(qi​a),
E(q)/(NS2)=J1​(ux​+uy​+uz​)+2J2​(ux​uy​+uy​uz​+uz​ux​).
(22)
It is affine in each ui​∈[−1,1], so some minimum occurs at a cube vertex, giving the competing collinear states above. At the boundary, the coefficient of uz​ vanishes for ux​=uy​=−1, producing the larger degeneracy (π/a,π/a,qz​) and permutations. It is not an isolated two-state crossing.
