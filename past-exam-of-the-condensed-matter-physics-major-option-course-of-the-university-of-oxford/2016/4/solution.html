For competing exchanges on a body-centred cubic antiferromagnet, use the Heisenberg model convention H=∑⟨ij⟩​Jij​Si​⋅Sj​, with each bond counted once, dimensionless spins, and positive antiferromagnetic J. There are eight nearest and six next-nearest neighbours. Bond counting in the two classical collinear structures gives
NEI​​=S2(−4J1​+3J2​),NEII​​=−3J2​S2.
(22)
For type II the eight J1​ contributions cancel, while all six J2​ bonds are antiparallel. Their crossing yields c=2/3. One can also exclude a lower spiral within the classical bilinear model: writing ui​=cos(qi​a/2) gives
J(q)=8J1​ux​uy​uz​+4J2​(ux2​+uy2​+uz2​)−6J2​.
(23)
For a favourable sign of the product, ux2​+uy2​+uz2​≥3∣ux​uy​uz​∣2/3≥3∣ux​uy​uz​∣. Hence when J2​≥2J1​/3 its minimum is at ux​=uy​=uz​=0, the type-II ordering vectors. When J2​≤2J1​/3, compare with the type-I value and use 3−∑ui2​≤3(1−∣ux​uy​uz​∣) to obtain the complementary bound. Thus
J2​/J1​>2/3 ⟹ classical type-II order​.
(24)
This is a classical/mean-field energy comparison; an exact quantum ground state is not generally a collinear product state.
For type I a magnetic cell is the simple cube of side a, with opposite moments at 0 and a(1,1,1)/2. Its magnetic structure factor is proportional to 1−eiπ(h+k+l). Allowed magnetic reflections have integer h,k,l with h+k+l odd. The first two families are (100) and (111), with symmetry-equivalent members understood.
For type II choose magnetic primitive translations a(1,1,0), a(1,0,1) and a(0,1,1), enclosing four spins in volume 2a3. Equivalently, a side-2a cube is a convenient nonprimitive magnetic cell. On each original simple-cubic sublattice the moment at an is proportional to (−1)nx​+ny​+nz​. Accordingly,
(h,k,l)=(p+1/2,q+1/2,r+1/2),p,q,r∈Z.
(25)
The translated body-centre basis gives 1+eiπ(h+k+l)=1±i, so there is no further extinction. The first two families are (1/2,1/2,1/2) and (3/2,1/2,1/2). The neutron polarization factor may additionally suppress a reflection for a particular moment direction; the stated rules are the positional selection rules.
Each sublattice has number density nA​=1/a3 and saturation magnetization Ms​=gμB​S/a3. Replacing neighbouring spins by their averages makes their exchange energy equivalent to a molecular magnetic field. Its minus sign expresses antiferromagnetic exchange, not an externally applied field. Since ⟨SB​⟩=MB​/(nA​gμB​),
λAB​=g2μB2​8J1​a3​,λAA​=g2μB2​6J2​a3​​.
(26)
For MB​=−MA​, Bmf​=(λAB​−λAA​)MA​. Linearizing the Brillouin magnetization formula gives
kB​TN​=3S(S+1)​(8J1​−6J2​)​.
(27)
The g-factor cancels because exchange is specified in energy units. A convention with 2JSi​⋅Sj​ doubles these exchange energies and TN​.
Let m=MA​/Ms​, A=(S+1)/(3S) and let bB​>0 denote the cubic coefficient of the Brillouin magnetization formula. The argument is y=(TN​/T)m/A. Thus m=Ay−bB​y3 implies
m2=bB​A3​(TN​T​)3(TTN​​−1)∼bB​A3​TN​TN​−T​.
(28)
Consequently β=1/2​ in mean-field theory. The positive branch falls from saturation at zero temperature to zero at TN​ and remains zero above it. An elastic magnetic neutron-reflection intensity scales as I∝MA2​; a log–log fit against TN​−T has slope 2β. Background subtraction and a suitable critical interval are needed. Fluctuations can change the measured exponent from its mean-field value; uniform bulk magnetization vanishes for a compensated antiferromagnet and is not itself this order parameter.
