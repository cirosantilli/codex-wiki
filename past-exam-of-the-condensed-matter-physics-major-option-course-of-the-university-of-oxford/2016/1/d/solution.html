For l=0, the transformation in part c maps (h,k,0) onto its Friedel pair with only a phase factor. Cyclic permutations give the same result when either other index vanishes. Friedel's law therefore survives for every such reflection, including any systematic absences.

Sx​(111)=e3it+3e−it=4(cos3t−isin3t).
(6)
Since the purely positional sums obey Sx​(−h)=Sx​(h)∗, the scalar resonant Friedel-pair intensity difference is
I(h)−I(−h)=4fSi​f′′Im(AB∗)​

up to the same positive experimental scale factor. In particular,
Im(AB∗)=16(cos3tSi​sin3tMn​−sin3tSi​cos3tMn​).
(8)
Different positional phases can therefore produce a nonzero resonant intensity difference at (111), allowing the opposite structural handednesses to be distinguished. The claimed violation is generic, not an unconditional identity: the source supplies no numerical internal coordinates. For a concrete noncentrosymmetric counterexample, take xSi​=1/8 and xMn​=5/8. Their orbit sums at (111) are 2​(1−i) and −2​(1−i), so the difference vanishes even if f′′=0; each generic chemical orbit is noncentrosymmetric, so this cancellation does not require inversion symmetry. Accidental cancellation, or f′′=0, must be excluded before asserting a violation for a particular specimen.
