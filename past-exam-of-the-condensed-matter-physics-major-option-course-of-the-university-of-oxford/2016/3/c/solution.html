Take dimensionless spin operators and H=DSz2​+gμB​BSz​, with g>0. The four wavefunctions are ∣m⟩, m=−3/2,−1/2,1/2,3/2, with
Em​=Dm2+gμB​Bm​.
(17)
At zero field they form doublets at D/4 and 9D/4. A transverse microwave magnetic field drives Δm=±1 transitions. When hν>2D, their positive resonance fields in ascending order are
B1​=gμB​hν−2D​,B2​=gμB​hν​,B3​=gμB​hν+2D​.
(18)
They connect (1/2,3/2), (−1/2,1/2), and (−3/2,−1/2), respectively. Thus
g=2.382,D=0.425hν=2.82×10−24 J=0.0176 meV,B3​=0.555 T​.
(19)
Here D/h=4.25 GHz and the zero-field doublet gap is 2D.
At lower frequency it is essential to use absolute energy gaps, not discard a transition after its levels cross. For 0<hν′<2D, the distinct candidate positive fields are
gμB​hν′​,gμB​2D−hν′​,gμB​2D+hν′​.
(20)
The first two coincide when
ν′=D/h=4.25 GHz​.
(21)
This axial spin-three-halves resonance coincidence leaves two distinct resonance positions, at 0.1275 and 0.3825 T, with two transitions superposed at the lower position. At ν′=2D/h there are also only two strictly positive-field lines, but an additional zero-field resonance remains; this is a different counting convention. Observable intensities also require thermal occupation of the relevant lower levels.
