For a periodic single-electron Hamiltonian operator, Bloch's theorem gives
ψbk​(r)=eik⋅rubk​(r),ubk​(r+R)=ubk​(r),ψbk​(r+R)=eik⋅Rψbk​(r).
(29)
Two bands at the same k have the same translation eigenvalues but different cell-periodic eigenfunctions, generally different energies and different orbital/nodal character. They may be chosen orthogonal even at a band degeneracy. The basis choices are treated in the two scoped branches below.
For a slowly varying external potential U(r), the semiclassical wavepacket Hamiltonian is ϵb​(k)+U(r). Hamilton's equation for the conjugate variables r,ℏk gives ℏk˙=−∇U=F. Equivalently the electric perturbation changes the translation phase at the rate qE⋅R/ℏ. This is the single-band acceleration theorem, requiring a slowly varying field and negligible interband tunnelling/scattering; it refers to crystal momentum, not free-space mechanical momentum.
Let e>0 and K=eE0​/(ℏω). Integrating from the specified initial condition k(0)=0 gives, with the first field alone,
kx​=K(cosωt−1),ky​=0​.
(30)
With both components switched on,
kx​=K(cosωt−1),ky​=−Ksinωt​.
(31)
This is a circle centred at (−K,0), not at the band minimum. Since vi​=ℏki​/mi∗​, integration gives
x−x0​=mx∗​ω2eE0​​(sinωt−ωt),y−y0​=my∗​ω2eE0​​(cosωt−1)​.
(32)
The switch-on drift in a circularly driven parabolic band accompanies unequal transverse scales: it is a stretched cycloid. In a frame moving with that drift, the oscillation is an ellipse with semiaxis ratio my∗​/mx∗​≫1. A closed real-space ellipse would require different initial momentum. Indeed, after one period x−x0​=−2πeE0​/(mx∗​ω2)=0, an explicit check against that interpretation.
Writing θ=ωt, the kinetic energy above the band minimum is
ϵ−ϵΓ​=2ω2e2E02​​[mx∗​(1−cosθ)2​+my∗​sin2θ​].
(33)
For mx∗​<my∗​ it is largest at θ=π, with value 2e2E02​/(mx∗​ω2). Equating this to the optical-phonon energy gives the emission threshold estimate
E0,max​≃eω​2mx∗​ℏωLO​​​=5.80×105 Vm−1​.
(34)
This treats the optical branch as approximately dispersionless and assumes a momentum-allowed final state, a cold initial electron, and negligible phonon absorption. It is the amplitude of each Cartesian field component; the circular field has constant magnitude E0​. A steady periodic orbit centred at k=0 would instead give twice this amplitude, but fails the stated switch-on condition.
