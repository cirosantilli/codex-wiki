For the electron-doped sample, sketch six closed ellipsoids centred on the Γ--X axes, just inside the six square faces of the face-centred-cubic first Brillouin zone, with long axes pointing radially toward X. In the parabolic approximation,
2m1​(μ−Ec​)/ℏ2δk⊥2​​+2m2​(μ−Ec​)/ℏ2δk∥2​​=1.
(20)
The radial-to-transverse semiaxis ratio is m2​/m1​​≃2.2. The readable conduction minimum near X and its crossing of μ support these electron Fermi surface pockets. The two [001] cyclotron resonance fields arise from the two distinct orientations of the six ellipsoids, rather than two unrelated carrier species. The left panel of the original sketch includes axes, Γ, and the three positive-axis X points; the negative-axis X points are symmetry-equivalent.
The surviving valence maximum lies roughly 1.2eV below the original μ. Lowering μ by 1.5eV therefore places it in the upper valence band in the conventional silicon band interpretation. The electron pockets disappear and one sketches a hole pocket centred at Γ, as in the middle panel. Detailed valence-band warping and the number of spin-orbit-resolved sheets cannot be extracted from the damaged figure, and the single drawn surface is schematic rather than a claim that all such sheets coincide.
A discriminating physical property is the Hall coefficient: electron-dominated transport gives a negative value, whereas hole-dominated transport gives a positive value in the ordinary single-dominant-carrier regime. One should not infer a numerical resistivity change without the carrier densities and mobilities.
