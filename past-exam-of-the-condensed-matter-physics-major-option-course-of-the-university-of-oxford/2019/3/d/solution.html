A possible assignment is a zone-centre transverse optical phonon of gallium arsenide: the two atomic sublattices oscillate oppositely, producing a narrow lattice Raman scattering line near 270cm−1. Its energy is hcν≃33.5meV, a reasonable optical-phonon scale. Unlike the broad carrier feature, its frequency is set mainly by interatomic force constants and ionic masses.
Whether this transverse optical phonon is allowed in an ideal first-order Raman scattering geometry depends on the surface orientation and incident and scattered polarizations; disorder or imperfect geometry can relax a forbidden selection rule. Those experimental details are unspecified, so this is a possible explanation rather than an exclusive assignment.
