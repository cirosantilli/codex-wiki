A local derivation makes the acceleration sign transparent. Normalize a(t0​)=1 and put τ=t0​−te​. A Taylor series gives
a(t0​−τ)=1−H0​τ−21​q0​H02​τ2+O(τ3),z=H0​τ+(1+q0​/2)H02​τ2+O(τ3).
(72)
The radial null geodesic in FLRW spacetime gives DC​=c∫te​t0​​dt/a(t)=c[τ+H0​τ2/2+O(τ3)]. Invert the redshift series, use dL​=(1+z)DC​ through second order, and obtain the displayed luminosity distance. Spatial curvature of an FLRW universe first changes this relation at third order. Lower q0​ therefore means larger dL​ at a fixed sufficiently small positive redshift and common H0​.
At z=0.05, the quadratic approximation gives 222.4353Mpc for q0​=−0.55, within 0.07% of the full flat-model value 222.2891Mpc. At z=0.5 it gives 2971.1574Mpc, about 4.9% too large. A useful sign argument at low redshift is not a precision replacement for the full distance integral in a distant supernova analysis.
