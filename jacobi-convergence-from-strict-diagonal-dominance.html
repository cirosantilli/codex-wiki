Jacobi convergence from strict diagonal dominance (∥B∥∞​=maxi​∑j=i​∣aij​/aii​∣<1)

A strictly diagonally dominant matrix guarantees convergence of the Jacobi method for every initial vector.[ref]
Indeed, using the maximum norm ∥v∥∞​=maxi​∣vi​∣, the induced operator norm is the largest absolute row sum. The Jacobi method has bii​=0 and bij​=−aij​/aii​ for j=i, so
ρ(B)≤∥B∥∞​=maxi​∣aii​∣∑j=i​∣aij​∣​<1,∥e(k)∥∞​≤∥B∥∞k​∥e(0)∥∞​.
(127)
This gives a cheap sufficient check without computing any eigenvalues. For the Jacobi iteration for a three-variable tridiagonal system, the absolute row sums of B are 1/4,1/2,1/4, giving ∥B∥∞​=1/2. The true spectral radius 2​/4 gives a sharper eventual rate.
Strict diagonal dominance is sufficient, not necessary: A=(11/10​21​) violates it in the first row, but its Jacobi method has eigenvalues ±1/5​ and converges. Conversely, being a positive-definite matrix alone is insufficient: the Jacobi convergence for a three-by-three equicorrelation matrix with α=3/4 has positive eigenvalues 5/2,1/4,1/4 for A, but spectral radius 3/2 for its iteration matrix. The extra tridiagonal matrix structure in Jacobi convergence for a symmetric positive-definite tridiagonal matrix is therefore substantive.
