Siegbahn photoelectron spectroscopy experiments

 Home Chemistry Physical chemistry Photoemission spectroscopy X-ray photoelectron spectroscopy
OurBigBook.com Words: 140
Kai Siegbahn's early instrument used iron-free magnetic double focusing and a thin-window Geiger-Müller counter. His later instrument, compatible with ultra-high vacuum, used a spherical electrostatic electron energy analyzer and monochromatic aluminum X-rays. Argon mixed with methane supplied simultaneous 2p reference lines.[ref]
Two sulfur lines in sodium thiosulfate distinguished chemical environments within one compound. A separate gold comparison showed a fingerprint obscuring substrate lines.[ref]
For an unknown sample, first document its preparation and transfer, then compare survey and detailed scans under reproducible illumination, contact and acquisition conditions. Repeat a region to test change during exposure. If a cleaning treatment is used, compare before and after rather than assuming that cleaning preserves the original chemical bonds. Report the photoelectron binding energy reference, reference lines, charge-compensation conditions and instrumental settings with the inferred chemical shift in photoelectron spectroscopy. A chemical interpretation should survive these checks as well as explain the line separation.
