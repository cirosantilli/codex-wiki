Cyclic group of order four and Klein four-group (C4​≅C2​×C2​)

 ... Area of mathematics Algebra Group theory Group Abelian group Finite abelian group
OurBigBook.com Words: 229
The finite cyclic group C4​=Z/4Z and the Klein four-group V4​=C2​×C2​ are both abelian groups of order four. In C4​, composition is addition modulo four. In V4​, label the elements 00,01,10,11 and add each coordinate modulo two. For example, 01+10=11 and 11+11=00.
The order of a group element is preserved by a group isomorphism: an equation gm=e holds exactly when its image satisfies the same equation. In C4​, both 1 and 3 have order four. In V4​, each nonidentity element has order two. Hence these groups are not isomorphic, despite having the same order of a finite group. No relabelling can repair this discrepancy.
For a geometric model of V4​, take the symmetries of a non-square rectangle: the identity element, reflection in its horizontal axis, reflection in its vertical axis, and rotation through 180∘. The two reflections commute, and their composite is the half-turn. Every nonidentity motion is its own inverse element. This makes the two binary switches concrete: choosing whether to apply each reflection specifies all four motions. The case of abelian groups of prime-square order generalizes this distinction from p=2 to any prime p.
