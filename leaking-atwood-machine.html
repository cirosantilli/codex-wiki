Leaking Atwood machine (vempty​=αgM0​​[−ϵ−2log(1−ϵ/2)])

 Home Physics Branches of physics Classical mechanics Lagrangian mechanics Atwood machine
OurBigBook.com Words: 123
Two initially equal buckets of total mass M0​ share an ideal Atwood machine. If one loses sand at constant rate α and the sand leaves with zero initial velocity relative to that bucket, the leakage supplies no rocket thrust. The remaining masses satisfy (M+M0​)v˙=g(M0​−M) for upward velocity of the leaking side. Using M˙=−α and integrating from rest gives v=(g/α)[M−M0​−2M0​log((M+M0​)/(2M0​))].
If the initial sand fraction is ϵ, emptying occurs at M=(1−ϵ)M0​. This gives the displayed speed, which tends to zero for ϵ→0 and to (gM0​/α)(2log2−1) for ϵ→1−. The latter is a finite limiting result, not a physical claim about an exactly massless empty bucket. The derivation is an application of a variable-mass system, rather than of d(Mv)/dt=F for a closed body.
