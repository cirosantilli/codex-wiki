Reversible receptor phosphorylation model

 Home Biology Protein Receptor (biochemistry) Cell-surface receptor Receptor tyrosine kinase
OurBigBook.com Words: 171
Protein kinase activity adds modification, while protein tyrosine phosphatase activity can remove it. An original one-site model shows why a receptor phosphorylation assay cannot determine either process from a single modified fraction. Let f(t)∈[0,1] be the fraction of a fixed receptor population carrying one selected phosphotyrosine residue. Stipulate constant effective first-order rate constants k+​,k−​>0, with ATP availability absorbed into k+​, and no receptor synthesis, degradation or trafficking:
dtdf​=k+​(1−f)−k−​f,f∗​=k+​+k−​k+​​,τ=k+​+k−​1​.
(11)
Here f∗​ is the steady state fraction and τ the time constant. The ordinary differential equation has the exponential function solution
f(t)=f∗​+(f(0)−f∗​)e−t/τ.
(12)
The balance equation and a single relaxation step give this result; it is an illustrative model, rather than a fit or a claim about every RTK.
With stipulated rate constants (k+​,k−​)=(2,1)min−1, f∗​=2/3 and τ=1/3min. Doubling both rate constants leaves f∗​=2/3 but changes τ to 1/6min. The balanced phosphate turnover per site,
J∗​=k+​(1−f∗​)=k−​f∗​=k+​+k−​k+​k−​​,
(13)
doubles from 2/3 to 4/3min−1. Identical steady state modification therefore permits different catalytic fluxes. Removing the addition term instead gives f(t)=f(0)e−k−​t; this illustrates why a perturbation that stops protein kinase activity can expose removal dynamics, subject to the model's unchanged-removal assumption. A maintained steady state with ongoing ATP consumption is not chemical equilibrium.
