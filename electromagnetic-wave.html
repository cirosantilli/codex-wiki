Electromagnetic wave

 Home Physics Branches of physics Electromagnetism
OurBigBook.com Words: 3k Articles: 54
An electromagnetic wave is a propagating disturbance of the electric field and magnetic field obeying the Maxwell equations. In source-free vacuum its speed is
c=μ0​ϵ0​​1​≃2.998×108m/s,
(277)
the speed of light. An electromagnetic wave can travel through vacuum: a conducting medium is unnecessary. A single traveling plane electromagnetic wave has mutually perpendicular electric field, magnetic field, and propagation direction. This statement applies to that wave; arbitrary near and far field configurations need not share these directions or the relation ∣B∣=∣E∣/c.
The visualization compares perpendicular components on a common scale: cB has the same units as E. The curves are field values along space, rather than paths traced by particles. Their common phase moves toward +z, while the Poynting vector records the associated energy flux.
The electromagnetic wave equation gives a propagation law, while the full Maxwell equations additionally enforce the field directions and relative amplitudes. This is why solving an independent scalar wave equation for each component is insufficient to construct an electromagnetic wave. [ref].
