Magnetic dipole torque (τ=m×Bext​)

 Home Physics Branches of physics Electromagnetism Magnetic vector potential Magnetic dipole moment
OurBigBook.com Words: 771 Articles: 2
An externally imposed magnetic field that is nearly uniform over a rigid current loop produces the torque
τ=m×Bext​,U=−m⋅Bext​=−mBcosθ.
(86)
The angle θ is measured between the magnetic dipole moment and the external magnetic field, not between the plane of the current loop and the magnetic field. The magnetic dipole potential energy uses a dot product, whereas the torque uses a cross product. The torque is largest when the magnetic dipole moment is perpendicular to the magnetic field; parallel alignment minimizes the potential energy.[ref]
For the sign convention Bext​=Bx and m=m(cosθx+sinθy​), positive rotation increases θ and
τz​=−mBsinθ=−dθdU​.
(87)
Thus at 0<θ<π the torque turns the magnetic dipole moment toward the external magnetic field. Both parallel and antiparallel alignment have zero torque, but they are respectively a stable equilibrium and an unstable equilibrium. With no damping, a freely released current loop can undergo oscillation around the stable equilibrium; settling requires damping.
In the twelve-turn example, take B=0.020T and θ=60°. The predicted torque is τz​=−2.35×10−4Nm and the magnetic dipole potential energy is U=−1.36×10−4J. The unit check is (Am2)T=Nm=J; torque and energy have the same physical dimensions but different physical meanings.
A short derivation starts with the magnetic force on a current-carrying wire, dF=Idr×Bext​. In an exactly uniform magnetic field, the net force is I(∮C​dr)×Bext​=0, but its torque need not vanish: opposite sides produce a turning couple. Integrating r×dF gives m×Bext​. Integrating dU=−τz​dθ at fixed m and B gives the orientation potential energy. This is the mechanical orientation potential energy for a maintained electric current, with the external source held fixed; the energy accounting for field and power supply is a separate problem. A varying external magnetic field requires corrections across a finite current loop, and can also give a net force.
