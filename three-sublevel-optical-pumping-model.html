Three-sublevel optical pumping model (F=1→F′=0)

 Home Physics Atomic physics Atomic measurement methods Optical pumping
OurBigBook.com Words: 375
Consider an original hypothetical atom with a lower angular momentum multiplet F=1, m=−1,0,+1, and one opposite-parity excited level F′=0,m′=0. Stipulate that all spontaneous emission returns to these three lower quantum states, with no other decay channel. This is an isolated four-state model, not the actual sodium or rubidium D-line structure. In particular, the physical rubidium cell above contains multiple hyperfine structure levels.
The electric-dipole selection rule permits ΔF=−1 here; σ+ absorption with Δm=+1 excites only m=−1. Decay from F′=0 has three equal integrated branching probabilities b−​=b0​=b+​=1/3: rotational symmetry gives equal squared Clebsch-Gordan coefficients. The emitted photons carry the three permitted angular momentum projections; equal integrated branching does not imply equal intensity in every observation direction. Thus m=0 and m=+1 are both dark. The chosen beam cannot prepare a unique pure m=+1 state.
Let R≥0 be the absorption rate of an atom in m=−1, and let Γ be the excited-state spontaneous emission rate, both in inverse seconds. Assume R,γ≪Γ, negligible stimulated emission, and no maintained quantum coherence between sublevels. Eliminating the short-lived excited population gives normalized lower populations p−​+p0​+p+​=1 to this approximation. At each scattering event the atom leaves m=−1 with probability 2/3; the remaining 1/3 simply returns it there.
Model depolarizing collisions and other population relaxation by resets to the unpolarized distribution at rate γ≥0. The rate equations are
p˙​−​=−32R​p−​+γ(31​−p−​),p˙​0​=3R​p−​+γ(31​−p0​),p˙​+​=3R​p−​+γ(31​−p+​).
(40)
This relaxation prescription assumes that Zeeman splitting is small compared with thermal energy, so the unpolarized distribution approximates thermal equilibrium. It is not an energy-independent relaxation law at arbitrarily low temperature.
Every transfer has a nonnegative rate: optical transfers −→0 and −→+ each have rate R/3, and relaxation transfers between each distinct pair have rate γ/3. These define a continuous-time Markov chain. The three derivatives sum to zero, and at pi​=0 the corresponding derivative cannot be negative. Hence the model conserves population and preserves nonnegative populations. Optical cycles that return to m=−1 contribute to scattering even though they cause no net population transfer.
