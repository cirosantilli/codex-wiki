Continuous dual space (X∗)

 Home Mathematics Area of mathematics Analysis Functional analysis
OurBigBook.com Words: 2k Articles: 34
The continuous dual space X∗ of a topological vector space is the vector space of its continuous linear functionals. Unlike the algebraic dual, it requires that a small change in an input produce a small change in its scalar measurement. For a normed vector space, this is equivalent to a finite bound
∣φ(x)∣≤C∥x∥
(1)
for all vectors x and some constant C≥0. The least such constant is the operator norm of φ.
This is the continuity-restricted subspace of the algebraic dual space; in infinite dimension the two can differ.
For the linear functional φ(x,y)=2x−y on R2 with its Euclidean norm, the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality gives
∣φ(x,y)∣≤5​x2+y2​.
(2)
Equality holds on the unit vector (2,−1)/5​, so its operator norm is exactly 5​. The same estimate controls measurement error: ∣φ(v+h)−φ(v)∣≤5​∥h∥2​. The linear functional diagram shows which changes are entirely invisible to this measurement; its kernel of a linear map consists of the changes with zero response. All linear functionals on a finite-dimensional real or complex normed vector space are continuous, but discontinuous functional detected by vanishing sequence tails shows why the restriction matters in infinite dimension.
