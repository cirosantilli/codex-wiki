All three active constraints force x1​=2 and x2​=4, which also satisfy x12​+x22​=20. For maximization use L=F+λTg, λ≥0. At x∗=(2,4),
∇F=(16,10),∇g1​=(−1,0),∇g2​=(−4,−8),∇g3​=(−4,1).
(2)
Stationarity gives the complete family
λ2​=t,λ3​=8t−10,λ1​=56−36t,45​≤t≤914​.​
(3)
In particular (λ1​,λ2​,λ3​)=(2,3/2,2) is strictly positive. Three gradients in two dimensions cannot be linearly independent, so the linear independence constraint qualification fails. But d=(−1,−1) gives ∇gi​⋅d=(1,12,3)>0, so the Mangasarian–Fromovitz constraint qualification holds.
For a nonzero linearized feasible direction d, each ∇gi​⋅d≥0, and
∇F⋅d=−2∇g1​⋅d−23​∇g2​⋅d−2∇g3​⋅d<0.
(4)
The inequality is strict because equality would require all three inner products to vanish, and the first and third gradients already span R2. This proves a strict local maximum: otherwise a sequence of nearby feasible points with no smaller objective would, after normalizing their differences from x∗ and taking a convergent subsequence, give a unit linearized feasible direction with nonnegative objective derivative, contradicting the displayed inequality. Thus
x∗=(2,4) is a strict local maximizer,F(x∗)=32.​
(5)
