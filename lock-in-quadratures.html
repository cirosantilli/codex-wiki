Lock-in quadratures (X=Acosϕ,Y=Asinϕ)

 ... Physics Branches of physics Electromagnetism Electrical circuit Electronic amplifier Lock-in amplifier
OurBigBook.com Words: 273
Let the input electric potential difference be s(t)=Acos(ωt+ϕ), with peak amplitude A≥0, angular frequency ω>0, and phase difference ϕ relative to cosωt. For an observation interval [0,T] containing an integer number N≥1 of cycles, each lasting one period of a function, T=2πN/ω, define the lock-in quadratures in a peak convention:
X=T2​∫0T​s(t)cosωtdt,Y=−T2​∫0T​s(t)sinωtdt.
(21)
These integrals average the products with dimensionless references 2cosωt and −2sinωt; X and Y have the input's units. The minus sign makes positive ϕ give positive Y. The trigonometric functions give
2s(t)cosωt−2s(t)sinωt​=Acosϕ+Acos(2ωt+ϕ),=Asinϕ−Asin(2ωt+ϕ).​
(22)
The oscillating terms have zero integral over the stated interval, proving X=Acosϕ and Y=Asinϕ. Their orthogonality turns an oscillating signal into two constant coordinates.
Represent those coordinates as a vector, or as the complex number Z=X+iY. Its modulus and complex argument recover the peak amplitude and phase difference:
R=X2+Y2​=A,ϕ=atan2(Y,X)(mod2π),R>0.
(23)
Here atan2(Y,X) selects the angle with the correct signs of both coordinates, rather than applying the arctangent to Y/X and losing the quadrant. When X=Y=0, the amplitude is zero and the phase difference is undefined. When ϕ=π/2, X=0 despite a nonzero signal; measuring Y prevents this blind spot. Rotating both references by a fixed angle rotates the coordinate vector but preserves R.
The factors of two above deliberately recover peak amplitude. The SR830 instead reports the selected sinusoidal component in root mean square units: in a calibrated, zero-offset display, Rrms​=R/2​. For R=0.4V this is 0.282843V, while the peak-to-peak span of that component is 0.8V. Display normalization, reference sign, gain and angle units must be checked for the particular instrument.[ref]
