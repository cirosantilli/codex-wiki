Two-stock binomial market without a supplied bond

 Home Mathematics Area of mathematics Mathematical optimization Mathematical finance Binomial options pricing model
OurBigBook.com Words: 74
Suppose two positive stock prices have joint one-period multipliers (u1​,u2​) or (d1​,d2​), with u1​>u2​. If d1​<d2​, the Arrow state prices are (d2​−d1​)/(u1​d2​−u2​d1​) and (u1​−u2​)/(u1​d2​−u2​d1​), both strictly positive. The two stocks therefore span both state payoffs, including a synthetic zero-coupon bond, and backward induction excludes arbitrage in the corresponding finite multiperiod market. If d1​≥d2​, equal initial currency exposures long the first stock and short the second give an arbitrage when both states have positive probability.
