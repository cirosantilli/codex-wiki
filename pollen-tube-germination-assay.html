Pollen tube germination assay

 ... Biology Reproduction Sexual reproduction Pollination Pollen Pollen tube
OurBigBook.com Words: 492
A pollen tube germination assay makes tube emergence visible outside a flower. The published Practical Biology experiment, Observing the growth of pollen tubes (Practical Biology), Technical note 3, lists florist lilies among successful materials without specifying a species. Record the actual identification; success with one sample cannot establish a universal growth rate.[ref]
Pearson's Core practical 4: Investigate the effect of sucrose concentration on pollen tube growth by Pearson Education Ltd, Teacher sheet, pp. 1–2, describes an inspectable experiment. Mix equal volumes of sucrose solution and mineral-salt medium on clean cavity slides. Dust in pollen from mature anthers with a mounted needle, record the addition time, and incubate without coverslips in covered dishes containing wet filter paper. The moist chamber reduces drying; the uncovered drops avoid oxygen limitation. Brief observations with an optical microscope at 100×, using an eyepiece graticule calibrated against a stage micrometer, reveal tube emergence and length. Return slides to the chambers and switch off the lamp between observations to reduce heating and drying. For comparisons of sucrose concentration, use the same flower across treatments to control maturity, then repeat with other flowers.[ref]
An additional proposed experimental design check is a medium-only slide: it tests whether debris is being mistaken for pollen tubes. Compare matched pollen samples at different final sucrose concentrations, keeping temperature, elapsed time and observation rules fixed. A sample known to produce pollen tubes in a previously successful medium would test whether an apparent failure comes from handling or the tested condition. These controls are questions to test, not guaranteed outcomes. A negative result cannot distinguish dead pollen from an unsuitable medium without further comparisons.
Two original calculations clarify the readout. First, take 0.40mL of 0.80molL−1 sucrose solution and 0.40mL of sucrose-free medium. Assuming additive volumes, conservation of the amount of substance gives

This dilution also halves the medium's original component concentrations. Label the final molar concentration, not the starting bottle.
Second, stipulate a toy field containing 40 pollen grains, of which 12 have emerged pollen tubes. Its emergence fraction is 12/40=0.30. If those twelve pollen tubes have total measured length 960μm, their arithmetic mean length is 80μm. Assigning length zero to the other grains instead gives 960/40=24μm per grain. These invented numbers show why emergence fraction and mean length among emerged tubes answer different questions. Specify the denominator and the emergence criterion before counting, and avoid selecting only conspicuous long pollen tubes.
Tube emergence in a drop demonstrates growth under that medium's conditions. It observes no stigma interaction, passage through a style, delivery into an ovule, gamete fusion, or seed development. Thus a positive pollen tube germination assay does not demonstrate successful fertilization in a living flower. Its beauty is making one otherwise hidden part of sexual reproduction directly observable.[ref]
