Keplerian radial-velocity curve (vr​(t)=γ+K[cos(ν+ω⋆​)+ecosω⋆​])

A repeating Doppler shift contains more than an amplitude. For an isolated two-body problem, let ν be the true anomaly, ω⋆​ the stellar argument of periapsis, τ the periapsis time and γ the constant system line-of-sight velocity. The companion's argument of periapsis differs by π from ω⋆​, so mixing the two conventions reverses the orbital signal. With positive velocity for recession, choose the stellar line-of-sight coordinate positive away from the observer. Kepler's equation supplies the clock:
M=P2π(t−τ)​=E−esinE,cosν=1−ecosEcosE−e​,sinν=1−ecosE1−e2​sinE​.
(24)
Here M is the mean anomaly and E the eccentric anomaly. The time coordinate is uniform in M, not in ν: an eccentric Kepler orbit runs fastest near periapsis.
To derive the velocity law without guessing a sinusoid, write the stellar orbital coordinates as X=a⋆​(cosE−e) and Y=a⋆​1−e2​sinE. Project to
z=(Xsinω⋆​+Ycosω⋆​)sini,dtdE​=1−ecosE2π/P​.
(25)
Differentiating z and substituting the true anomaly identities gives
vr​−γ=z˙=K[cos(ν+ω⋆​)+ecosω⋆​],K=P1−e2​2πa⋆​sini​.
(26)
The constant ecosω⋆​ term makes the time average of the orbital velocity zero: z returns to its original value after one orbital period. For e=0 this reduces to a sinusoid with an arbitrary phase origin; periapsis itself is then undefined. For e>0, the largest and smallest velocities are γ+K(ecosω⋆​±1), so K is still half the peak-to-peak range, although the extrema need not be symmetric about γ.
For an original worked example, choose M⋆​=1M⊙​, P=10 days, K=50ms−1, e=0.4 and ω⋆​=60∘. Taking one day as 86400s and G=6.67430×10−11m3kg−1s−2, the binary mass function is f=1.98270×1020kg. Its units check directly: [PK3/G]=kg. With adopted solar mass M⊙​=1.98847×1030kg and Jupiter mass MJ​=1.89813×1027kg, solving the exact binary mass function gives

All three systems have the same chosen P,K,e,ω⋆​,τ and therefore the same Doppler spectroscopy time series, despite different true masses. Exactly what remains fixed is Mp​sini/(M⋆​+Mp​)2/3; Mp​sini alone is only the small-companion approximation. The 90∘ value is the minimum mass. For this example the extrema are +60 and −40ms−1 relative to γ, and the periapsis value is +35ms−1. This is a chosen eccentric system, not a reconstruction of 51 Pegasi b.
