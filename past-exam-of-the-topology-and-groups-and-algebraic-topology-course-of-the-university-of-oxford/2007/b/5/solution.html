Choose an orientation for each simplex. The chain group Ck​ is the free abelian group on the oriented k-simplices, with orientation reversal changing sign. On an ordered simplex set
∂k​[v0​,…,vk​]=∑i=0k​(−1)i[v0​,…,vi​,…,vk​].
(7)
Its face maps respect the identifications in a Delta-complex. In ∂k−1​∂k​, deleting any pair of vertices occurs in the two possible orders with signs (−1)i+j−1 and (−1)i+j, which cancel. Hence ∂2=0. The groups of homological cycles and boundaries are Zk​=ker∂k​ and Bk​=im∂k+1​; the identity just proved gives Bk​⊆Zk​, so Hk​=Zk​/Bk​ is defined. Mod-two homology uses the same chain complex with coefficients reduced modulo two.
Here the edge identifications put all six vertices into one vertex class. Let the three boundary edge classes be a,b,c in cyclic order, and orient the internal diagonals as d=[v1​,v3​], e=[v3​,v5​], f=[v5​,v1​]. With the four face orientations listed in the source, the boundaries are
∂σ1​=2a−d,∂σ2​=2b−e,∂σ3​=2c−f,∂σ4​=d+e+f.
(8)
Thus C2​=Z4, C1​=Z6, C0​=Z, and ∂1​=0. The Euler characteristic is
χ=1−6+4=−1​.
(9)
The face-boundary map is injective over the integers: coefficients of a,b,c force the first three face coefficients to vanish, after which the diagonal coefficients force the fourth to vanish. Its cokernel eliminates d,e,f and leaves generators a,b,c with the single relation 2(a+b+c)=0. Since a+b+c is primitive in Z3, this cokernel is Z2⊕Z/2. Therefore
Hk​=⎩⎨⎧​Z,Z2⊕Z/2,0,​k=0,k=1,k≥2.​​
(10)
Modulo two the first three face-boundary columns are d,e,f, and the last is their sum. The rank is three, the kernel is one-dimensional, and the edge quotient has dimension three. Consequently
Hk​(−;Z/2)=⎩⎨⎧​Z/2,(Z/2)3,0,​k=0,2,k=1,k≥3.​​
(11)
The top mod-two class is the sum of all four faces. Integral homology has no such top class, reflecting nonorientability.
