The identifications yield one vertex, g one-cells and one two-cell, whose boundary word is a12​⋯ag2​. Thus the integral cellular chain complex, in degrees two, one and zero, has
C2​=Z,C1​=Zg,C0​=Z,∂2​(1)=(2,…,2),∂1​=0.

For g≥1 the first map is injective. The vector (1,…,1) is primitive and extends to an integral basis, so quotienting by twice that vector gives
Hk​(Ng​;Z)=⎩⎨⎧​Z,Zg−1⊕Z/2,0,​k=0,k=1,k≥2.​​
(18)
With F2​=Z/2Z, every boundary map vanishes. Hence
Hk​(Ng​;F2​)=⎩⎨⎧​F2​,F2g​,0,​k=0,2,k=1,k>2.​​
(19)
For cohomology, dualize the free cellular complex. The only nonzero possible coboundary over Z is δ1:Zg→Z, (v1​,…,vg​)↦2∑i​vi​. Its kernel is free of rank g−1 and its image is 2Z. Thus
Hk(Ng​;Z)=⎩⎨⎧​Z,Zg−1,Z/2,0,​k=0,k=1,k=2,k>2.​​
(20)
Over F2​ the cochain maps are zero too, giving H0=H2=F2​, H1=F2g​, and zero higher groups. In particular the integral top homology and top cohomology differ, as their explicit complexes show. Finally the cell count gives
χ(Ng​)=1−g+1=2−g​.
