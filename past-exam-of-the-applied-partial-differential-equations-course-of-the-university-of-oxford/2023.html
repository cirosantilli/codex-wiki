The supplied positive free-space kernel satisfies −ΔH=δ, so use the same sign convention for the Dirichlet Green function. For an interior source Q=(ξ,η,ζ), require
−ΔP​G(P;Q)=δQ​(P),G=0 on x=0 and z=0,G(P;Q)→0 as ∣P∣→∞.
(1)
Away from Q, the function is harmonic, and G−H is regular near Q. The method of images reflects the source in each coordinate plane, reversing its sign on each reflection. Therefore
G(P;Q)=4π1​i,j=0∑1​(x−(−1)iξ)2+(y−η)2+(z−(−1)jζ)2​(−1)i+j​​.
(2)
On either face, the terms cancel in pairs. Only the original source lies in the domain, so the other three terms are harmonic there; the required singularity and decay also follow. This is the Dirichlet Green function for a three-dimensional quarter-space. Using ΔG=δ instead would change the sign of the kernel and the representation formula together, as in sign conventions for a Dirichlet Green function.

The upward jump at −1/2 creates a rarefaction wave with u=(x+1/2)/t, bounded by the characteristic lines x=−1/2 and x=t−1/2. The downward jump at zero joins the states 1,0, so part (a) gives the shock position s(t)=t/2. The intervening plateau exists exactly while t−1/2<t/2, or t<1. The fan satisfies the Inviscid Burgers equation classically and matches the constant branches continuously; the shock satisfies the Rankine-Hugoniot condition and is causal because 1>1/2>0. Freeze the coefficients at a point. Characteristic normals (τ,ξ)=(0,0) satisfy the homogeneous quadratic matrix pencil equation det(τA+ξB)=0. In the elementary characteristic classification, two distinct real characteristic directions give a hyperbolic system, no real directions an elliptic system, and a repeated real direction the parabolic case. An identically zero determinant polynomial is a further degeneracy, not covered by that nondegenerate classification.
When A is invertible, write C=A−1B. The finite characteristic slopes solve det(B−λA)=0, or
λ2−(trC)λ+detC=0.
(19)
Its discriminant (trC)2−4detC is positive, negative, or zero in the three cases above. For the well-posed first-order evolution meaning of hyperbolicity, real eigenvalues must also admit a complete eigenbasis: a repeated but diagonalizable pair is non-strictly hyperbolic rather than a defective parabolic system. Distinct real roots suffice for strict hyperbolicity.
For the given shallow water equations, subtracting u times mass conservation from momentum conservation and dividing by h>0 gives the primitive-variable system
(hu​)t​+(u1​hu​)(hu​)x​=0.
(20)
The characteristic polynomial is (u−λ)2−h, so
λ−​=u−h​,λ+​=u+h​​.
(21)
They are real and distinct whenever h>0, proving strict hyperbolicity.

For a strictly hyperbolic two-component system of conservation laws with ordered speeds λ−​<λ+​, a compressive family-j shock has its family characteristics entering from both sides: λj​(UR​)<s<λj​(UL​). The other family crosses the interface with the same sign of relative speed on both sides. These are the Lax shock admissibility conditions and give three incoming characteristic directions for two conservation laws.
In the present problem,
λ±​(UR​)=±1,λ±​(UL​)=u1​±h1​​.
(24)
Since u1​=s−s/h1​, we always have u1​−h1​​<s. Hence a positive-speed shock of this form cannot be a family-minus shock. For a family-plus shock the remaining strict inequalities are
1<s<u1​+h1​​.

The first is equivalent to h1​(h1​+1)>2, or h1​>1. The second is equivalent to s<h13/2​, whose square reduces to h1​+1<2h12​, again equivalent to h1​>1 for positive h1​. The minus speed on the right is −1<s automatically. Thus all causal inequalities hold exactly when
