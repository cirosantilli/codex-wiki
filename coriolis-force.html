Coriolis force (−2mΩ×v)

 Home Physics Mathematical physics Reference frame Rotating reference frame
OurBigBook.com Words: 2k Articles: 11
The Coriolis force is the apparent force proportional to a particle's velocity relative to a rotating reference frame. For constant angular velocity Ω and particle mass m,
Fcor​=−2mΩ×vrot​,vrot​=(dtdr​)rot​.
(66)
Here v in the title formula means vrot​, not the velocity measured in an inertial frame. The Coriolis force belongs to the chosen reference frame: a particle stationary in that rotating reference frame has no Coriolis force, even though it can have a nonzero velocity in the laboratory. Other reference frames assign different angular velocities and relative velocities, and hence different Coriolis forces to the same motion.
The cross product makes the Coriolis force perpendicular to both Ω and vrot​, with magnitude 2mΩ∣vrot​∣sinα, where α is the angle between them. Apply the right-hand rule to Ω×vrot​ and then reverse its direction. Motion parallel to the rotation axis gives zero Coriolis force; reversing the relative velocity reverses the Coriolis force.
The appeal of this description is practical: axes attached to the Earth or a turning apparatus can be kept fixed on the page, provided Newton's second law includes the extra terms. A free particle in rotating coordinates shows exactly how a straight laboratory trajectory can become curved, while Coriolis acceleration in local Earth coordinates gives a useful terrestrial scale. The rotating-table demonstration of Coriolis deflection makes the two viewpoints visible in one experiment.
