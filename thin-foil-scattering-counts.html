Thin-foil scattering counts (μ=Ninc​ns​ηΔΩdΩdσ​)

 Home Physics Atomic physics Rutherford scattering
OurBigBook.com Words: 407 Articles: 1
Let Ninc​ be the number of projectiles actually incident on the foil during an exposure, ns​ its foil areal number density, and η the probability of detecting an accepted particle. For a sufficiently small detector patch of area A at distance R from the scattering point, with its normal at angle ψ to the line of sight,
ΔΩ≃R2Acosψ​,μ(θ)≃Ninc​ns​ηΔΩdΩdσ​(θ).
(70)
The product ns​(dσ/dΩ)ΔΩ is a dimensionless scattering probability in this approximation; μ is an expected value of counts, so it need not be an integer. The approximation requires a nearly constant differential scattering cross-section and efficiency across the patch and incident beam. For finite acceptance use μ=Ninc​ns​∫detector​η(Ω)(dσ/dΩ)dΩ, including the incident beam's spread if appreciable. A screen facing the foil has ψ=0.
For an original numerical model, stipulate monoenergetic alpha particles with z=2, E=5.00MeV, and a gold target with Z=79. Use the rounded model constant e2/(4πε0​)=1.440MeVfm, where 1MeV=106 electronvolts and 1fm=10−15m. Thus κ=227.52MeVfm and a=22.752fm. Choose Ninc​=1011, ns​=1021m−2, η=1, and ΔΩ=10−3sr. For example, A=1mm2 facing the foil at R≃31.6mm gives this small-patch solid angle.
At θ=90∘, b=a and dσ/dΩ=a2=517.654fm2sr−1=5.17654bsr−1, using 1 barn =100fm2=10−28m2. The expected signal is μ≃51.8 counts. At 60∘ it is four times larger, about 207 counts. Doubling foil areal number density or detector solid angle doubles these expected signals as long as the approximations continue to hold.
An observation also contains backgrounds. For a stable source and matched geometry, an estimator is Cnet​=Cfoil​−(Tfoil​/Tblank​)Cblank​, where T denotes exposure duration. If source activity changes, normalize by incident exposure as well. Background subtraction removes an estimated background; it does not make finite counting fluctuations disappear.
