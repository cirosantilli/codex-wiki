Spectral phase in pulse compression (ϕ(Ω)=ϕ0​+ϕ1​Ω+ϕ2​Ω2/2+⋯)

Use a complex envelope A(t) normalized so that ∣A(t)∣2=P(t); its units are W1/2. The optical carrier convention is e−iω0​t: the physical electric field is proportional to the real part of A(t)e−iω0​t for a fixed transverse mode. With angular frequency offset Ω=ω−ω0​, fix the Fourier transform convention
A(Ω)=∫−∞∞​A(t)eiΩtdt,A(t)=2π1​∫−∞∞​A(Ω)e−iΩtdΩ.
(24)
Write A=Beiϕ with real B≥0. A dispersive element adds spectral phase
ϕ(Ω)=ϕ0​+ϕ1​Ω+2ϕ2​​Ω2+6ϕ3​​Ω3+⋯.
(25)
Constant phase rotates the envelope; the linear term delays it by ϕ1​. The group delay is dϕ/dΩ. The quadratic coefficient ϕ2​ is group delay dispersion, abbreviated GDD, with units of time squared. Positive GDD makes higher frequencies arrive later with this convention. It is accumulated dispersion of a component; material group velocity dispersion is its corresponding coefficient per unit length.
To undo a quadratic stretcher spectral phase, the compressor must supply opposite GDD. Include the amplifier and all intervening optics in the sum:
ϕ2,total​=ϕ2,stretch​+ϕ2,amplifier​+ϕ2,compress​.
(26)
Setting this sum to zero does not cancel third-order dispersion, ϕ3​, or still higher orders. Residual spectral phase can produce asymmetry and satellite pulses even when the quadratic term vanishes. A diffraction grating system can make path length depend on frequency; matching the complete spectral phase over the occupied spectrum is more demanding than merely choosing opposite quadratic coefficients.
There is a useful proof of what phase correction can accomplish. By Parseval's identity,
U=∫∣A∣2dt=2π1​∫B2dΩ.
(27)
For a smooth decaying spectrum, the Fourier transform of tA is −idA/dΩ. Consequently the power-weighted mean arrival time and variance obey
tˉ=∫B2dΩ∫B2ϕ′dΩ​,σt2​=∫B2dΩ∫(B′)2dΩ​+∫B2dΩ∫B2(ϕ′−tˉ)2dΩ​.
(28)
At fixed spectral amplitude, constant group delay minimizes this root mean square duration. Such a pulse is a transform-limited pulse, up to a time shift and constant phase. The nonnegative second term measures temporal broadening from unequal arrival times; the first term remains even after perfect phase correction.
