Logarithmic scoring rule (S(p,y)=logp(y))

 ... Area of mathematics Probability and statistics Statistical inference Scoring rule Proper scoring rule Strictly proper scoring rule
OurBigBook.com Words: 236 Articles: 1
The logarithmic scoring rule rewards the natural logarithm of the reported probability mass or probability density at the observed outcome. The title uses the reward convention. For a binary report p, its negative is the logarithmic loss:
Llog​(p,y)=−ylnp−(1−y)ln(1−p),0<p<1.
(18)
For y=1 this is −lnp; for y=0 it is −ln(1−p). At an endpoint, use these outcome-specific forms: assigning zero probability to the outcome incurs infinite loss, while assigning it probability one gives zero. The natural logarithm therefore makes a confidently wrong binary forecast far more costly than the bounded Brier score does.
Under the stipulated Bernoulli distribution with q=0.7, the expected logarithmic loss is
E0.7​[Llog​(p,Y)]=−0.7lnp−0.3ln(1−p).
(19)
Its unique minimum is at p=0.7, where the expected loss is approximately 0.610864. At p=0.2 it is approximately 1.193550. More generally, the excess expected logarithmic loss over truthful reporting is the Kullback-Leibler divergence in the order true law first, reported law second:
Eq​[Llog​(p,Y)]−Eq​[Llog​(q,Y)]=qlnpq​+(1−q)ln1−p1−q​=DKL​(Bernoulli(q)∥Bernoulli(p)).
(20)
For 0<q<1, this Kullback-Leibler divergence is nonnegative and vanishes only at p=q, explaining why the logarithmic scoring rule is a strictly proper scoring rule. The same Kullback-Leibler divergence identity applies to probability density functions relative to a common measure when the truthful expected logarithmic loss is finite and the comparison expected value is well defined.[ref] For sequential forecasts, the existing prequential log score identity explains how logarithmic rewards add over observations.
