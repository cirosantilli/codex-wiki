Hyperbolic Pythagorean theorem (coshc=coshacoshb)

Words: 74
Hyperbolic geometry OurBigBook.com
For a right-angled geodesic triangle in curvature minus one, with legs a,b and hypotenuse c, the displayed identity replaces the Euclidean theorem. Normalize the right vertex to i in the upper half-plane, one endpoint to iy with y>1, and the other to s+it with s2+t2=1 and t>0. The Hyperbolic distance in the Poincare half-plane gives cosha=(y+y−1)/2, coshb=1/t and coshc=(1+y2)/(2yt), proving the identity. For small lengths its expansion gives c2=a2+b2 to leading order.

Tessellation

Words: 154 Articles: 2
Hyperbolic geometry OurBigBook.com
A tessellation is a covering by tiles with pairwise disjoint interiors; a locally finite tessellation meets any compact set in only finitely many tiles. In a tessellation by congruent compact hyperbolic polygons, the adjacency graph records side-crossing steps. The area growth AreaB(r)=2π(coshr−1) and bounded tile diameter imply exponential growth of the number of tiles within n steps: a unit geodesic segment meets a uniformly bounded number of tiles, by packing congruent interior balls, so a metric ball of radius proportional to n lies in the step ball. Conversely the step ball lies in a metric ball of radius proportional to n. The regular right-angled pentagons with four around every vertex give the tiling {5,4}.

Hyperbolic circle in the Poincare disc (dD​(p,z)=R)

Words: 85
Hyperbolic geometry OurBigBook.com
In the Poincare disc model, a hyperbolic circle in the Poincare disc consists of points at one fixed hyperbolic distance from a center. At center zero it is the Euclidean circle ∣z∣=tanh(R/2), since radial hyperbolic distance is 2artanh∣z∣. A Poincare disc automorphism sending a general center to zero transports this description. Conversely every Euclidean circle entirely inside the disc is such a circle: the product of inversion in that circle and unit-circle inversion has reciprocal fixed points, one inside; sending the inner fixed point to zero makes the circle concentric with the unit circle.

Hyperbolic space (Hn)

Words: 382 Articles: 5
Hyperbolic geometry OurBigBook.com
The complete simply connected Riemannian manifold of dimension n with constant sectional curvature −1 is hyperbolic space. In the upper half-space model its metric is ds2=(dx12​+⋯+dxn2​)/xn2​ for xn​>0. Its ideal boundary in dimension three is the Riemann sphere.

Bounded displacement sets of hyperbolic isometries are compact

Words: 91
Hyperbolic space OurBigBook.com
In the positive Hermitian determinant-one model of three-dimensional hyperbolic space, the base point is I and a matrix g∈SL(2,C) sends it to gg∗. The distance obeys 2coshd(I,gg∗)=tr(gg∗). Bounded displacement therefore bounds every matrix entry, and the determinant-one condition gives a closed bounded subset of a finite-dimensional real vector space. Passing to PSL(2,C) preserves compactness. Conjugating moves any chosen base point to I. A discrete subgroup meets each such compact set in finitely many elements, proving escape of distinct elements and proper discontinuity, including finite stabilizers.

Minimum enclosing ball in hyperbolic space

Words: 79
Hyperbolic space OurBigBook.com
For a nonempty finite set {p1​,…,pm​} in finite-dimensional hyperbolic space, the continuous function R(x)=maxj​d(x,pj​) attains its minimum. Indeed, a bounded candidate radius confines all possible improving centers to a closed bounded ball about p1​. Such a ball is compact in the Poincare ball model, since its points remain away from the ideal boundary. Minimizing R on it supplies a closed ball of smallest enclosing radius. More generally this argument works for any nonempty bounded set in a proper metric space when the supremum-distance function is finite.

Axis of a hyperbolic-space isometry

Words: 75
Hyperbolic space OurBigBook.com
An isometry of hyperbolic space with positive attained translation length has an invariant complete geodesic joining its two ideal fixed points. This is its axis. It translates along the axis by its translation length; in three dimensions it may additionally rotate around it. In upper half-space, (z,t)↦(kz,∣k∣t) with ∣k∣=1 has the vertical axis through zero and length ∣log∣k∣∣. Every connecting path has length at least the change in logt, and a vertical segment attains the bound.

The unit ball in Euclidean space with the displayed Riemannian metric models hyperbolic space of curvature minus one. Its ideal boundary is the unit sphere, and its geodesics are diameters or circle arcs orthogonal to that sphere. The metric is invariant under the conformal ball automorphisms.

Hyperbolic extension of a Möbius transformation

Words: 59
Poincare ball model OurBigBook.com
A Möbius transformation of the ideal two-sphere extends uniquely to an orientation-preserving isometry of three-dimensional hyperbolic space. In upper half-space, translations, complex similarities, and z↦−1/z extend respectively to (z,t)↦(z+b,t), (λz,∣λ∣t), and (−z,t)/(∣z∣2+t2). Each preserves (∣dz∣2+dt2)/t2; composing these extensions constructs the general case. Ball-origin stabilizers are ordinary rotations, represented on the boundary by SU(2) modulo its central signs.

Right-angled hyperbolic hexagon

A geodesic hexagon in the hyperbolic plane with all six interior angles π/2. Any three positive alternating side lengths determine it up to isometry. If those sides are ℓi​/2, the opposite seam satisfies coshsi​=(cosh(ℓi​/2)+cosh(ℓj​/2)cosh(ℓk​/2))/(sinh(ℓj​/2)sinh(ℓk​/2)). The Beltrami-Klein model realizes the hyperbolic plane inside an open Euclidean disk, with geodesics given by straight chords. Its metric differs from the Euclidean metric, and angles are generally distorted. The straight chords make geodesic convexity especially easy to see.

A surface with a complete Riemannian metric of constant Gaussian curvature −1. Its local model is the Poincaré half-plane model; the orientable case is a quotient by a free properly discontinuous group action of orientation-preserving isometries.

Hyperbolic holonomy representation (ρ:π1​(Σg​)→PSL2​(R))

For a marked closed hyperbolic surface, the representation of its fundamental group whose image acts as the deck group on the hyperbolic plane. It is discrete and faithful, determined up to conjugation by the marking and the metric, and gives X=H2/ρ(π1​Σg​). For a closed orientable surface it admits determinant-one lifts. Length spectrum ({ℓ(γ)})

Words: 394 Articles: 6
Hyperbolic surface OurBigBook.com
The unmarked length spectrum of a compact hyperbolic surface is the multiset of lengths of its closed geodesics, with a consistent convention for primitive curves and orientation. The marked version labels lengths by free homotopy classes. The Selberg trace formula relates the unmarked multiset to the Laplace-Beltrami operator spectrum.

Marked length spectrum ([γ]↦ℓ(γ))

Words: 50
Length spectrum OurBigBook.com
For a closed hyperbolic surface with a specified marking of its fundamental group, assign to each nontrivial conjugacy class the length of its unique closed geodesic. Retaining the group label is stronger than retaining only the unmarked length multiset: it also records which lengths belong to intersecting curves or powers of particular curves.

Huber spectral equivalence theorem

Words: 46
Length spectrum OurBigBook.com
For closed hyperbolic surfaces of curvature −1, equality of the Laplace-Beltrami operator spectra, including multiplicities, is equivalent to equality of the unmarked length spectra of closed geodesics. The Selberg trace formula proves the equivalence. Primitive lengths with multiplicities and the multiset including their iterates determine one another, provided the same orientation convention is used.

Uniform finiteness of short geodesic classes

Words: 38
Length spectrum OurBigBook.com
In a compact smooth family of marked closed hyperbolic surfaces, only finitely many free homotopy classes of closed geodesics have length below a fixed bound somewhere in the family. A uniform bilipschitz equivalence with a fixed metric reduces this to local finiteness of its length spectrum.

Buser finite length spectrum theorem (L=L(g,ε))

Words: 180 Articles: 1
Length spectrum OurBigBook.com
For fixed genus g≥2 and systole lower bound ε>0, a uniform finite length cutoff L determines the entire unmarked length spectrum of a closed hyperbolic surface in the thick part, including multiplicities. Equality up to L forces equality everywhere. This is a finite-determination statement for spectra and does not claim that all isospectral surfaces are isometric.
Finite spectral matching by polynomial invariants
Words: 127
Buser finite length spectrum theorem OurBigBook.com
Suppose a compact parameter set carries a locally finite countable multiset of positive quantities, and a fixed injective transform of each labelled quantity is a polynomial in finitely many ambient coordinates. Assume only finitely many labels can have bounded value anywhere in the compact set. Then a uniform finite initial multiset determines the entire multiset. For each source label there are finitely many possible matching labels. Equality for finitely many labels in both directions is a finite union of algebraic sets, obtained from injective matchings and polynomial equalities. These algebraic sets decrease as more labels are included, so they stabilize by the Hilbert basis theorem. A compatible infinite matching in each direction follows by the König infinity lemma, and local finiteness makes multiplicities equal. Squared traces of hyperbolic holonomy representations give the application to the Buser finite length spectrum theorem.

Selberg trace formula

Words: 38
Length spectrum OurBigBook.com
For a compact hyperbolic surface, the Selberg trace formula equates a sum over Laplace-Beltrami operator eigenvalues to an area term and a sum over primitive closed geodesics and their iterates. It proves that the Laplacian spectrum and the unmarked length spectrum, with multiplicities, determine each other.

An elementary move replaces one cuff inside the four-holed sphere or one-holed torus obtained by uniting its adjacent pants. The replacement is an essential curve meeting the old cuff minimally. Pants decomposition graph (V=2g−2, E=3g−3)

Words: 58
Pants decomposition OurBigBook.com
The graph of a pants decomposition has one vertex per pair of pants and one edge per glued cuff. It is a connected trivalent multigraph, possibly with loops. A closed surface of genus g gives 2g−2 vertices and 3g−3 edges, so its first Betti number is g. The graph records a decomposition rather than the homeomorphism class alone, and omits cuff lengths and twists.

For fixed g≥2, a constant Bg​ bounds all cuff lengths in some pants decomposition of every closed genus-g hyperbolic surface. Together with Fenchel–Nielsen coordinates this implies Mumford's compactness theorem.

For a fixed pants decomposition, the positive cuff lengths and real twist parameters give coordinates on Teichmüller space. With twists measured in length units, a full Dehn twist changes τi​ by ℓi​; with angle units it changes the twist by 2π.
Fenchel–Nielsen twist (τ)
Words: 65
Fenchel–Nielsen coordinates OurBigBook.com
After cutting a hyperbolic surface along a simple closed geodesic, reglue its two boundary copies after an arclength displacement τ. The boundary length remains fixed, while the Riemannian metric varies. In marked Fenchel–Nielsen coordinates, the twist is real-valued; changing it by one boundary length corresponds to a Dehn twist in the marking. This construction also applies to a hyperbolic pair of pants with a fixed cone angle and two geodesic boundaries.
Finite length coordinates for hyperbolic surfaces (9g−9 marked geodesic lengths)
Words: 85
Fenchel–Nielsen coordinates OurBigBook.com
For a fixed pants decomposition of a closed genus-g hyperbolic surface, its 3g−3 cuff lengths determine all its Y-pieces. Two suitably chosen transverse simple closed curves at each cuff, one obtained from the other by a Dehn twist, determine the corresponding twist: hyperbolic seam identities express their lengths through shifted hyperbolic cosines of that twist, removing the sign ambiguity of just one transversal. Thus finitely many marked lengths determine the Fenchel–Nielsen coordinates. The unmarked length spectrum lacks these labels, which is why this fact alone does not imply global spectral uniqueness.

Hyperbolic systole (syshyp​(X))

Words: 49
Hyperbolic surface OurBigBook.com
The infimum of nonconstant closed geodesic lengths in curvature −1, with value +∞ if there are none. On a closed surface with a hyperbolic metric this is a positive attained minimum, and a minimizing geodesic is simple. For fixed closed genus g≥2, a common positive lower bound gives relative compactness in the unmarked moduli space of Riemann surfaces by Mumford's compactness theorem.

Poincare metric on a Riemann surface (dsD​=2∣dz∣/(1−∣z∣2))

Words: 259 Articles: 4
Hyperbolic metric OurBigBook.com
A connected Riemann surface whose universal cover is the unit disc inherits the complete curvature-minus-one metric displayed on its cover. Möbius transformations preserving the disc are isometries, so deck transformations preserve the metric and it descends to the surface. This uniformization notion must be distinguished from the potential-theoretic existence of a Green function. Holomorphic maps between such surfaces contract their metric by lifting to Schwarz-Pick theorem on the covers.

Hyperbolic zero sequences on a punctured disk (zn​=e−n2,e−ρ(e−1,zn​)=n−2)

Words: 65
Hyperbolic metric on the punctured disk OurBigBook.com
The curvature-minus-one hyperbolic metric on the punctured disk assigns distance 2logn along the radial geodesic from e−1 to e−n2. Its exponential distances therefore sum to a finite value; the triangle inequality gives the same conclusion for any fixed basepoint. Nevertheless no nonzero bounded holomorphic function has these zeros: the Riemann removable singularity theorem extends it at zero, and the identity theorem then makes it identically zero. Simple connectivity is necessary in the hyperbolic characterization of bounded analytic zero sets.

Ideal hyperbolic quadrilateral

An ideal hyperbolic quadrilateral is the region bounded by four hyperbolic geodesics whose four consecutive endpoints are distinct points on the ideal boundary. The ideal vertices lie outside the hyperbolic plane. In the hyperbolic disk, the four vertices 1,i,−1,−i give a regular example: its sides are the interior arcs of the circles of radius one centered at ±1±i. Although the sides have infinite length, the quadrilateral has finite hyperbolic area 2π in curvature −1.

Horocycle

A horocycle in the Poincare disc model is a Euclidean circle internally tangent to the boundary circle. In the upper half-plane model, a horocycle is a Euclidean circle tangent to the real axis from above, or a horizontal line y=h>0. The common geometric feature is a single ideal centre of a horocycle.

Common orthogonal line of two horocycles

Words: 46
Horocycle OurBigBook.com
Two horocycles have a unique common orthogonal hyperbolic line when their ideal centres of horocycles differ: it is the line with those two ideal endpoints. If the centres agree, an isometry puts both horocycles on horizontal lines; every vertical hyperbolic line meets both orthogonally, so there are infinitely many. This classification remains valid for intersecting or tangent horocycles.

Ideal centre of a horocycle

Words: 51

The ideal centre of a horocycle is its point of tangency to the boundary at infinity; a horizontal horocycle in the upper half-plane model has centre infinity. Sending the centre to infinity by an isometry makes the horocycle horizontal. Orthogonal hyperbolic lines then become vertical, proving that a hyperbolic line meets a horocycle orthogonally exactly when it has the centre as an ideal endpoint.

Geodesic-cap and Carleson-box comparison

Conversely, for any fixed b>0, the region with angular projection in I and depth 1−∣z∣≤bℓ lies in Q(κI) for a fixed enlargement κ depending only on b, when I is sufficiently short. Every hyperbolic-plane isometry is a product of at most three reflections

Words: 80

A reflection in the perpendicular bisector of a point and its image corrects that point. A second bisector reflection through the corrected point can correct another point without moving the first. An isometry fixing two distinct points is either the identity or reflection in their joining geodesic: points with prescribed distances from both centres have at most two possibilities, exchanged by that reflection. Fixing a third noncollinear point then fixes every point. This constructs a factorization with at most three hyperbolic reflections.
