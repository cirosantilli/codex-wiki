Modified Arrhenius equation (k(T)=A∗​(T/T∗​)nexp[−E0​/(RT)])

The Modified Arrhenius equation allows a power of thermodynamic temperature in the pre-exponential factor:
k(T)=A∗​(T∗​T​)nexp(−RTE0​​),T∗​>0.
(118)
Here A∗​, E0​, n, and T∗​ are constant model parameters. Using the dimensionless quantity T/T∗​ leaves A∗​ with the same units as k; in the equivalent convention BTnexp[−E0​/(RT)], B instead has units of k multiplied by K−n. The power exponent n here is not an order of reaction. The IUPAC definition uses that equivalent BTn convention.[ref]
Applying the apparent activation energy definition yields the original short calculation
dTdln(k/kref​)​=Tn​+RT2E0​​,Ea,app​(T)=E0​+nRT.
(119)
For the orange synthetic Arrhenius plot, n=2, T∗​=300K, and E0​=60kJmol−1. Its pre-exponential factor changes by (310/300)2≈1.06778 from 300 to 310K; combined with the exponential function, k(310K)≈0.231991s−1, compared with 0.217266s−1 for the constant-pre-exponential factor model. The diagram uses those models only to reveal the difference between an exponent parameter and a slope-derived activation energy.
