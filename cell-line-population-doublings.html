Cell line population doublings (d=log2​(N1​/N0​))

 Home Biology Biological cell Cell line
OurBigBook.com Words: 148
For positive recoverable counts N0​,N1​ bracketing one growth interval, define its net cell line population doublings by
d=log2​(N0​N1​​)=ln2ln(N1​/N0​)​.
(2)
The formula counts how many factors of two produce the observed net factor. For exactly two divisions per original biological cell, with all daughters recovered, N1​=22N0​, giving d=2. Death or loss of biological cells makes this a net estimate of division.[ref]
Under constant-rate exponential growth, N(t)=N0​ert, a growth interval of duration Δt gives
r=Δtln(N1​/N0​)​,t2​=rln2​=dΔt​.
(3)
For the constructed fourfold increase over 48h, d=2 and the model doubling time is 24h. The proof follows by taking the natural logarithm and solving ert2​=2. This is a fitted property of that interval and model: it is not a universal HeLa doubling time, nor the interval between two divisions of each individual biological cell. Across successive growth intervals, add their d values; keep transfer losses separate as in cell culture passage accounting.
