Stokes shift (Δλ=λem,max​−λabs,max​)

 Home Physics Branches of physics Optics Physical optics Fluorescence
OurBigBook.com Words: 149
The Stokes shift compares the lowest absorption-band maximum with the fluorescence emission spectrum maximum. Relaxation often makes Δλ>0. This comparison differs from using an arbitrary excitation wavelength. A spectral-density maximum can move when changing from per unit wavelength to per unit spectroscopic wavenumber; specify the spectral convention.[ref]
An original illustrative energy budget makes the distinction concrete. Excitation at 450nm and emission at 520nm correspond to about 2.76 and 2.38eV per photon, using E=hc/λ with Planck constant h and speed of light c in vacuum. The difference, about 0.37eV, must go elsewhere in the complete energy balance. These chosen wavelengths are not measured band maxima for a particular fluorophore.
Anti-Stokes emission has shorter wavelength than excitation. Additional energy, for example from a thermally populated level of molecular vibration or multiple excitation photons, makes it possible. The one-photon, initially relaxed drawing does not cover these cases.[ref]
