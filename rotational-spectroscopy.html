Rotational spectroscopy

 Home Chemistry Molecule Molecular rotation
OurBigBook.com Words: 2k Articles: 10
Rotational spectroscopy measures transitions between molecular rotation quantum states, usually in low-pressure gaseous molecules. The measured spectral lines become a molecular fingerprint only after rotational spectral line assignment: identify which transitions belong together, determine their quantum numbers, fit a rotational Hamiltonian, and test additional predictions. Line positions constrain moments of inertia, while rotational state populations and transition coupling determine which lines can be detected.
The simplest ordinary microwave absorption ladder belongs to a polar linear molecule obeying pure rotational electric-dipole selection rules. A homonuclear diatomic molecule has no permanent electric dipole moment in its symmetric internal state, but rotational Raman spectroscopy can probe its rotation through anisotropic electric polarizability. The coupling mechanism must therefore be specified before interpreting a missing spectrum.
The existing linear rigid rotor, rotational constant, isotope shift of a linear rotor, and centrifugal distortion explain the forward model. Molecular structure from rotational constants asks the reverse question: which arrangements of chemical atoms could produce the fitted constants? The NIST trace-gas FTMW validation experiment makes the measurement chain concrete and separates identifying a species from measuring its concentration.
