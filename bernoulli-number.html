Bernoulli number (Bn​)

 Home Mathematics Area of mathematics Number theory
OurBigBook.com Words: 2k Articles: 9
The Bernoulli numbers are a sequence of rational numbers that turns integer power sums into exact polynomials. The same numbers describe corrections when a discrete summation is compared with an integral. For example, the sum of squares 12+⋯+N2 is N3/3+N2/2+N/6: the leading term is an integral, while the last two terms repair the difference between area and sampled values.
We use B1​=−21​ throughout. The defining exponential generating function is
et−1t​=∑n=0∞​Bn​n!tn​=1−2t​+12t2​−720t4​+30240t6​−⋯.
(1)
Here et is the exponential function and n! is a factorial; in particular, the coefficient of t2 is B2​/2!, rather than B2​. At t=0 the quotient has limiting value one. The identity can be read algebraically as a formal power series, or as a convergent series for ∣t∣<2π.[ref]
To compute the numbers, multiply by et−1 and compare coefficients. The resulting recurrence relation, with binomial coefficients, is
B0​=1,∑k=0m​(km+1​)Bk​=0(m≥1),Bm​=−m+11​∑k=0m−1​(km+1​)Bk​.
(2)
Thus 1+2B1​=0, 1+3B1​+3B2​=0, and 1+4B1​+6B2​+4B3​=0 give B1​=−1/2, B2​=1/6, and B3​=0. Continuing gives
nBn​​01​1−21​​261​​30​4−301​​50​6421​​70​8−301​​90​10665​​​
(3)
The recurrence relation agrees with equation 24.5.3 after replacing its index n by m+1.[ref]
Why do so many values vanish? Put F(t)=t/(et−1). Direct substitution gives F(−t)=F(t)+t, so F(t)+t/2 is an even function. Its odd power series coefficients vanish: B2r+1​=0 for every positive integer r. The exception B1​ matters. The alternative convention obtained from t/(1−e−t)=F(t)+t has B1+​=+1/2 and exactly the same other values. A formula copied between conventions must also retain its summation endpoints.
Historically, integer power sums were studied before the modern Bernoulli number notation. A historical study examines formulas published in 1631 and distinguishes those separate formulas from encoding all exponents using one sequence of constants. This is a useful motivation for the generating function, without assigning all discovery to a single moment. [ref]

Bernoulli polynomial (Bn​(x))

Words: 527 Articles: 2
Bernoulli number OurBigBook.com
The Bernoulli polynomials extend the Bernoulli numbers by inserting the exponential function ext into their exponential generating function:
et−1text​=∑n=0∞​Bn​(x)n!tn​,Bn​(x)=∑j=0n​(jn​)Bj​xn−j,Bn​(0)=Bn​.
(4)
For example, B0​(x)=1, B1​(x)=x−1/2, B2​(x)=x2−x+1/6, and B3​(x)=x3−3x2/2+x/2.[ref]
Their crucial property is a finite difference identity:
Bn​(x+1)−Bn​(x)=nxn−1(n≥1).
(5)
Indeed, subtracting the two exponential generating functions cancels et−1 and leaves text. Thus Bp+1​(x)/(p+1) plays for discrete summation the role that xp+1/(p+1) plays for integration: taking a unit finite difference recovers xp.[ref]

Integer power sum (Sp​(N)=∑k=0N−1​kp)

Words: 450 Articles: 1
Bernoulli polynomial OurBigBook.com
An integer power sum adds a fixed nonnegative integer power of consecutive integers. Let N be a nonnegative integer and p a nonnegative integer. For p=0, use the constant polynomial x0=1, including at zero, so S0​(N)=N. Summing the Bernoulli polynomial finite difference identity makes the intermediate terms cancel and gives
Sp​(N)=∑k=0N−1​kp=p+1Bp+1​(N)−Bp+1​(0)​=p+11​∑j=0p​(jp+1​)Bj​Np+1−j.
(6)
The sum is empty when N=0. For the endpoints 1,…,N, the formula valid also at p=0 is instead
∑k=1N​kp=p+1Bp+1​(N+1)−Bp+1​(1)​.
(7)
For p≥1, Bp+1​(1)=Bp+1​(0), and this becomes the standard positive-endpoint formula. The exception at p=0 is essential: B1​(1)−B1​(0)=1.[ref]
For p≥1, adding Np to Sp​(N) changes the B1​ contribution from −Np/2 to +Np/2. Equivalently,
∑k=1N​kp=p+11​∑j=0p​(jp+1​)Bj+​Np+1−j(p≥1),
(8)
where only B1+​ differs from our Bernoulli numbers. A quick endpoint check is p=1: the sums over 0,…,N−1 and 1,…,N are N(N−1)/2 and N(N+1)/2, respectively. The latter is a triangular number.

Bernoulli corrections for polynomial sums (∑k=1N​k4=5N5​+2N4​+3N3​−30N​)

For a concrete comparison, take f(x)=x2 on [0,6]. The integral is I=∫06​x2dx=72, whereas the unit-width left and right Riemann sums are L=55 and R=91. The exact integer power sum formula gives
L=I−262​+66​=55,R=I+262​+66​=91.
(9)
The endpoint choice explains the opposite signs of the 18 correction. The remaining correction is one in both cases.
The same method is useful far beyond squares of numbers. Substituting B0​, B1​, B2​ and B4​ into the integer power sum formula gives the exact fourth-power result
∑k=1N​k4=5N5​+2N4​+3N3​−30N​.
(10)
At N=10, this is
integral20000​​+endpoint5000​​+B2​ term31000​​​−B4​ term31​​​=25333.
(11)
Thus a finite polynomial calculation replaces ten additions, or a million additions if N is a million. Although the individual corrections can be rational numbers, the result is the exact integer sum.
There is also a useful numerical integration interpretation. The average of the left and right Riemann sums is the composite trapezoidal rule. For x2, the endpoint corrections cancel but its value is 73, one above the true integral. Subtracting the B2​ correction makes this particular polynomial example exact. For f(x)=x4, the two interior corrections above can be written B2​(f′(N)−f′(0))/2! and B4​(f′′′(N)−f′′′(0))/4!. These are exact identities for these polynomials; extending such corrections to general functions requires a separate error analysis.

Hyperbolic cotangent generating function for Bernoulli numbers (cothz=z−1+∑j≥1​(2j)!22jB2j​​z2j−1)

Words: 64
Bernoulli number OurBigBook.com
The defining generating function of the Bernoulli numbers, with B1​=−1/2, gives this identity of formal Laurent series by cothz=1+2/(e2z−1). Therefore acoth(az)−cothz is regular and odd. Integrating it gives the even formal logarithm of sinh(az)/(asinhz). Its even derivatives of order k at zero are 2k(ak−1)Bk​/k, while the odd derivatives vanish. This computes the cyclotomic higher logarithmic derivatives of symmetric cyclotomic units without introducing a spurious linear term.

Euler evaluation of even zeta values (ζ(2r)=(−1)r+1B2r​(2π)2r/(2(2r)!))

Words: 619 Articles: 2
Bernoulli number OurBigBook.com
The Euler evaluation of even zeta values turns a family of series into exact expressions using Bernoulli numbers, factorials and powers of π. For every positive integer r,
n=1∑∞​n2r1​=ζ(2r)=2(2r)!(−1)r+1B2r​(2π)2r​​.
(12)
Here ζ is the Riemann zeta function, defined by its convergent series at these arguments, and the Bernoulli number convention is B1​=−1/2. The alternating sign combines with the sign of B2r​ to give a positive sum.[ref]
Substituting B2​=1/6, B4​=−1/30 and B6​=1/42 gives
ζ(2)=6π2​,ζ(4)=90π4​,ζ(6)=945π6​​.
(13)
The first identity answers the Basel problem. It is surprising because the original series only mentions reciprocal squares of integers, while its exact sum involves the circle constant π. The cotangent partial-fraction expansion supplies a connection between the two: trigonometric functions encode equally spaced integer poles, whose expansion brings the reciprocal powers into view.
For an original numerical comparison, the first ten terms give ∑n=110​n−2≈1.549767731, while π2/6≈1.644934067. The omitted positive terms still contribute approximately 0.095166336. For fourth powers, the ten-term partial sum is approximately 1.082036583, only 0.000286650 below π4/90≈1.082323234. Both are convergent series, but the second approaches its limit much faster.
Exact evaluation is useful beyond obtaining more digits. It supplies an independent check on a numerical summation, exposes how Bernoulli numbers organize infinitely many identities, and makes the remaining series truncation error measurable without knowing any omitted terms individually. The even integer condition is essential to this formula: putting r=3/2 into it is not a way to evaluate ζ(3), since the required Bernoulli number index and factorial expression here are indexed by positive integers.
Historically, Leonhard Euler announced reciprocal-square and reciprocal-fourth-power evaluations in §2 of On the sums of series of reciprocals by Leonhard Euler. That paper was presented on 5 December 1735 and published in 1740, as the English translation's first-page note records. His §§3–8 develop a route through the sine power series and relationships between roots and coefficients. The cotangent calculation below is a concise modern presentation of the connection.[ref]

Zeta series truncation error (RN​(s)=ζ(s)−∑n=1N​n−s)

Words: 221
Euler evaluation of even zeta values OurBigBook.com
For a real exponent s>1 and a positive integer N, the series truncation error in the defining Riemann zeta function series is
RN​(s)=∑n=N+1∞​n−s>0.
(14)
The integral comparison for the positive decreasing function x−s gives the explicit bounds
s−1(N+1)1−s​≤RN​(s)≤s−1N1−s​​.
(15)
On each unit interval, the area under x−s lies between its endpoint heights. Summing from the first omitted term gives the lower integral from N+1 to infinity and the upper integral from N to infinity. This uses positivity and monotonicity, not an assumption that the last computed term equals the remaining error.
At N=10, the bounds are 1/11≤R10​(2)≤1/10, whereas 1/(3⋅113)≤R10​(4)≤1/(3⋅103). The errors above fit both intervals. Merely looking at the eleventh term would give 1/121≈0.008264 for squares, far smaller than the actual remaining 0.095166.
For a chosen absolute error target ε>0, the sufficient condition is N≥((s−1)ε)−1/(s−1), rounded upward to an integer. Thus ε=10−4 requires N=10000 by this bound for squares but only N=15 for fourth powers. This is a guarantee from an upper bound, not a claim that those are the smallest successful term counts.

Cotangent coefficient proof of the even zeta values

Euler evaluation of even zeta values OurBigBook.com
The cotangent partial-fraction expansion gives, on 0<∣z∣<1,
πcot(πz)=z−1−2∑r≥1​ζ(2r)z2r−1.
(16)
The Bernoulli number generating function gives the same Laurent series in another form:
πcot(πz)=πi+e2πiz−12πi​=z−1+∑r≥1​(2r)!B2r​(2πi)2r​z2r−1.
(17)
The B1​ term cancels the separate πi; the higher odd Bernoulli numbers vanish. Comparing Laurent coefficients therefore gives −2ζ(2r)=B2r​(2πi)2r/(2r)!, which is the stated real formula because i2r=(−1)r. The cotangent partial-fraction expansion justifies the convergent expansion before the coefficient comparison; a finite plot of partial sums alone would not prove the infinite identity.
