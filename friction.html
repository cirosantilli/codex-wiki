Friction

 Home Physics Branches of physics Classical mechanics Newtonian mechanics
OurBigBook.com Words: 843 Articles: 5
Friction is a contact force tangent to the surfaces. Kinetic friction opposes contact slip; static friction maintains contact without contact slip, including rolling without slipping. The perpendicular contact component is the normal force. These two components answer different questions: how strongly the surfaces press together, and how strongly they resist sliding past one another.
The ideal Coulomb dry-contact model combines a bound on static friction with a sliding law for kinetic friction. Its parameters are the coefficient of static friction and the coefficient of kinetic friction; constant coefficients are an empirical approximation, rather than a consequence of Newton's laws of motion. Surface condition matters, and the static coefficient need not universally exceed the kinetic coefficient. [ref]
The model's historical setting was practical machinery: Coulomb's Théorie des machines simples investigated friction and the stiffness of ropes. The Académie's bibliography records its 1781 prize and its appearance in volume 10, pages 161–332, of the Recueil des savants étrangers. [ref]
