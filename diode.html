Diode

 Home Physics Branches of physics Electromagnetism Electrical circuit
OurBigBook.com Words: 3k Articles: 24
A diode is a two-terminal component with an asymmetric current-voltage characteristic: reversing its terminal electric potential difference need not reverse its electric current with the same magnitude. Name the terminals diode anode A and diode cathode K. Define VD​=ϕA​−ϕK​ using their electric potentials, and take positive electric current ID​ from A to K. The bar in the diode symbol marks K. Positive VD​ is the forward direction; negative VD​ is the reverse direction.[ref]
The asymmetry makes a rectifier possible: a sinusoid can drive electric current through a resistor on one half of each cycle. An ordinary resistor obeying Ohm's law instead reverses its electric current when the applied electric potential difference reverses. The useful idea is directional selection; the ideal diode model captures it, while the Shockley diode equation describes a smooth exponential function rather than an abrupt switch.
