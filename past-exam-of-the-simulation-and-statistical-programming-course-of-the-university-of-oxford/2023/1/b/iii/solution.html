With a fixed budget of n independent proposal trials, the accepted count Kn​ has binomial distribution with success probability 1/M. Hence
EKn​=Mn​,Var(Kn​)=M2n(M−1)​.
(10)
For M>1, the central limit theorem gives the one-sided approximation
P(Kn​≥N)≈Φ(n(M−1)​n−MN​).
(11)
Use z=2.33, since now only the lower tail of the accepted count matters. Requiring n−MN≥zn(M−1)​ and solving the resulting quadratic equation in t=n​ gives the one-sided normal approximation for a rejection proposal budget
n0​=​(2zM−1​+z2(M−1)+4MN​​)2​.​
(12)
Equivalently, before rounding, this is MN+z2(M−1)/2+(z/2)(M−1)[z2(M−1)+4MN]​. For the value in (a)(iii),
n0​=​(22.33M−1​+5.4289(M−1)+266256M​​)2​.​
(13)
When M=1, every proposal is accepted and n0​=N=66,564​ exactly. Otherwise the positive safety margin above MN allows for random fluctuations in the number accepted. The average cost in (b)(ii) describes stopping at the Nth acceptance; this answer chooses a deterministic budget that reaches that acceptance with approximately 99% probability.
The approximation requires a sufficiently accurate normal distribution approximation to the binomial distribution. An exact 99% budget can instead be found from its tail probabilities. Also, a 99% chance of enough accepted draws and approximate 99% precision conditional on obtaining them are separate claims: their intersection is not automatically a 99% event.
