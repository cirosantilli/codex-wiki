Associator ring ((a)=(b)⟹a=ub for some u∈R×)

 Home Mathematics Area of mathematics Algebra Commutative algebra Ring
OurBigBook.com Words: 192
An associator ring is a ring in which equality of the principal ideals generated by two elements forces them to be associate elements: (a)=(b) implies a=ub for a unit in a ring u. The reverse implication always holds, since a=ub and b=u−1a give both ideal inclusions. The property separates equality of generated ideals from equality of generators up to an invertible factor.
Every integral domain is an associator ring. If a=0 and (a)=(b), write a=rb and b=sa. Then (1−rs)a=0, so cancellation gives rs=1 and a=rb with r a unit. The zero case is immediate. The converse fails: Z/4Z has generator classes {0}, {2} and {1,3} for its three ideals, and 1,3 differ by a unit, although 2 is a nonzero zero divisor.
Finite direct product of rings preserve this property. Equality of principal ideals is coordinatewise, and a tuple of units is a unit. Every quotient ring of a principal ideal domain is also an associator ring: first, modulo a prime power pk, every nonzero class is a power pe, e<k, times a unit, and distinct powers generate distinct ideals. Next factor a nonzero nonunit modulus into prime powers and apply the Chinese remainder theorem. The zero modulus gives the original integral domain, while a unit modulus gives the zero ring.
