Weak coupling condition for two NMR spins (∣ω1​−ω2​∣≫∣J∣)

 Home Chemistry Nuclear magnetic resonance NMR scalar coupling
OurBigBook.com Words: 175 Articles: 1
For the two-spin Hamiltonian operator H=(ω1​ZI+ω2​IZ+J(XX+YY+ZZ))/2, juxtaposition of two Pauli matrices denotes their tensor product. Put Δ=ω1​−ω2​. The exchange matrix element between ∣01⟩ and ∣10⟩ is J, whereas their diagonal separation is Δ. Nondegenerate perturbation theory therefore gives admixtures ±J/Δ and second-order frequency shifts ±J2/Δ. Neglecting the exchange terms is justified to leading order when ∣J/Δ∣≪1. A large common Zeeman energy is insufficient. To approximate accumulated phases by this leading-order Hamiltonian operator, one also needs J2t/∣Δ∣≪1 over the observation time, or must include the second-order shifts.
For fixed nonidentical chemical shifts, increasing the static magnetic field increases ∣Δ∣ in angular-frequency units while the NMR scalar coupling changes little. If Δ=0 remains exact, the approximation fails at every nonzero J.
