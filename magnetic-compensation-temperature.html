Magnetic compensation temperature (Tcomp​)

 Home Physics Branches of physics Electromagnetism Magnetism Ferrimagnetism
OurBigBook.com Words: 187
At a magnetic compensation temperature, MA​=MB​=0: an ordered ferrimagnet has zero net magnetization. Such cancellation need not occur in every material.[ref]
For an original stipulated example, let t=T/TC​, with 0≤t≤1, and prescribe sublattice magnitudes per common volume by
MA​(t)=M0​(1−t),MB​(t)=23​M0​(1−t)2,M0​>0.
(122)
Take A along +z and B along −z, retaining a fixed sublattice orientation convention. These are toy functions, not measured curves or a self-consistent mean-field approximation. Their difference factors exactly:
Mz​(t)=MA​−MB​=2M0​​(1−t)(3t−1).
(123)
At t=0, Mz​=−M0​/2. At t=1/3, both magnitudes equal 2M0​/3 and Mz​=0: this is compensation with finite order. At t=1/2, Mz​=M0​/8>0. At t=1, both magnitudes vanish: that endpoint represents the stipulated Curie temperature, not another compensation point. For t>1 stipulate both ordered magnitudes zero; do not extrapolate the polynomials into negative sublattice magnitudes.
The sign change refers to the net component relative to the fixed A direction. A freely reorienting specimen in an applied magnetic field need not display this signed branch directly. The example isolates the cancellation mechanism; it predicts neither a material's transition temperature nor its response to magnetic domains or an applied magnetic field.
