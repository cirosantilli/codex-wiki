Derived category of sheaves (D(X))

Words: 309 Articles: 5
Homological algebra OurBigBook.com
The derived category of sheaves is formed by localizing cochain complexes of sheaves at quasi-isomorphisms, equivalently localizing their homotopy category. The bounded, bounded below and bounded above variants are denoted Db(X), D+(X) and D−(X). Cohomology sheaves are invariant under quasi-isomorphism and therefore define functors on these categories. Constructible bounded complexes form a natural subcategory on which Verdier duality reverses arrows and, under the usual finiteness hypotheses, is an involution.

Ghost morphism in a derived category

Words: 60
Derived category of sheaves OurBigBook.com
A ghost is a morphism inducing zero on every cohomology sheaf, but it need not itself be zero. For the equator j:S1↪S2 and complement i:U↪S2, the connecting morphism j∗​QS1​→i!​QU​[1] is a ghost. Its source and target have cohomology sheaves in different degrees, but its induced map H1(S1)→Hc2​(U) is nonzero, proving that the derived morphism is nonzero.

Verdier duality (DX​)

Words: 48
Derived category of sheaves OurBigBook.com
On bounded constructible complexes over locally compact stratified spaces, Verdier duality is DX​E=RHom(E,ωX∙​), where ωX∙​ is the dualizing complex. For an oriented real N-manifold, DX​QX​≅QX​[N]. Proper direct image commutes with this duality, and ix!​DX​E≅D{x}​ix∗​E. These properties exchange stalk support conditions with costalk conditions.

Constructible complex of sheaves

Words: 29
Derived category of sheaves OurBigBook.com
A bounded complex is constructible for a given stratification when each of its cohomology sheaves restricts to a finite-rank local system on every stratum. Constructibility concerns cohomology sheaves, not an arbitrary choice of resolution.

Bounded below derived category of sheaves (D+(X))

Words: 103 Articles: 1
Derived category of sheaves OurBigBook.com
The category D+(X) is obtained from bounded below cochain complexes of sheaves by passing to cochain-homotopy classes of maps and then inverting quasi-isomorphisms. It can equally be modeled by injective resolutions. The cohomology-sheaf functors descend to this localization, and an object is zero exactly when all its cohomology sheaves vanish.

Exact triangle in a derived category

Words: 58
Bounded below derived category of sheaves OurBigBook.com
An exact, or distinguished, triangle is one isomorphic to Ef​F→Cone(f)→E[1]. For a degreewise short exact sequence 0→E→F→G→0, the map Cone(f)→G taking (v,e) to the image of v is a quasi-isomorphism: its kernel is the contractible cone of the identity on E. Consequently the short exact sequence defines such a triangle.

Left derived functor (Li​F(M)=Hi​(F(P∙​)))

Words: 114 Articles: 1
Homological algebra OurBigBook.com
For an additive right-exact functor on module categories, apply it to a projective resolution and take homology. Lifts of module maps exist by projectivity; any two lifts are chain homotopic, giving independence and functoriality. The zeroth derived functor is naturally F, positive degrees vanish on projective modules, and the horseshoe lemma produces a long exact homology sequence from each short exact sequence. A Tor functor is the left derived functor of a tensor product of modules functor.

Left derived functors on an abelian category (Ln​F(A)=Hn​(FP∙​))

Words: 48
Left derived functor OurBigBook.com
For a right-exact additive functor out of an abelian category with enough projectives, apply the functor to a projective resolution and take homology objects. The zeroth derived functor is F, positive derived functors vanish on projective objects, and the degreewise split horseshoe lemma produces a long exact sequence from every short exact sequence in an abelian category. The target can be any abelian category, including an opposite category of one.

Injective resolution (0→M→I0→I1→⋯)

Words: 63
Homological algebra OurBigBook.com
An exact augmented cochain complex with injective module terms. Module categories have enough injectives: coinduction J=HomZ​(A,Q/Z) is injective by its character adjunction, and the maps m↦(a↦ϕ(am)), over all additive characters ϕ of M, embed M into a product of copies of J. Products of injectives are injective by coordinatewise extension. Repeat on cokernels to obtain a resolution.

Right derived functor (RiF)

Words: 119 Articles: 1
Homological algebra OurBigBook.com
For an additive covariant left-exact functor on module categories, choose an injective resolution M→I∙ and set RiF(M)=Hi(F(I∙)). For a contravariant functor, use a projective resolution instead, as in contravariant right derived functor. Resolution comparison and homotopies give independence and functoriality; short exact sequences give long exact derived-functor sequences. The zeroth functor is naturally F.

Contravariant right derived functor (RiF(M)=Hi(F(P∙​)))

Words: 71
Right derived functor OurBigBook.com
For an additive contravariant left-exact functor, apply it to a projective resolution of M. Reversal of the arrows gives a cochain complex. Comparison maps lift using projectivity, and any two lifts are chain homotopic, so its cohomology is independent of the resolution. The functors are contravariant, vanish on projective modules in positive degrees, and send 0→M′→M→M′′→0 to 0→F(M′′)→F(M)→F(M′)→R1F(M′′)→⋯. Degreewise split resolutions from the horseshoe lemma yield this sequence.

Spectral sequence ((Erp,q​,dr​))

Words: 187 Articles: 4
Homological algebra OurBigBook.com
A succession of bigraded pages with Er+1​=H(Er​,dr​). In the cohomological convention, the differential has bidegree (r,1−r). A filtered complex produces such successive approximations to its cohomology.

Künneth spectral sequence (E2p,q​=Tor−pR​(HqP,M)⇒Hp+q(P⊗R​M))

Words: 88 Articles: 1
Spectral sequence OurBigBook.com
For a bounded complex P of projective right modules and a left module M of finite projective dimension, a finite projective resolution of M yields a bounded double cochain complex. Its two spectral sequences give the displayed abutment. Over a noncommutative ring, the handedness of the two tensor factors is essential.

Two-column degeneration of a Künneth spectral sequence (E2​=E∞​)

Words: 42
Künneth spectral sequence OurBigBook.com
If only the columns p=−1,0 are nonzero, a cohomological differential of bidegree (r,1−r) has no possible nonzero source-target pair for r≥2. Projective boundaries in a complex of projectives give this condition because every cohomology module has projective dimension at most one.

Convergence of a spectral sequence (E∞p,q​≅FpHp+q/Fp+1Hp+q)

Words: 71 Articles: 1
Spectral sequence OurBigBook.com
The stable page identifies the associated graded module of a filtration of the abutment. For a finite filtration this is an exhaustive, separated finite extension description. It does not itself determine a canonical splitting. Unbounded filtrations require further convergence hypotheses.

Bounded filtered-complex convergence theorem (E1p,q​=Hp+q(FpC/Fp+1C)⇒Hp+q(C))

Words: 34
Convergence of a spectral sequence OurBigBook.com
A finite filtration of a cochain complex produces a convergent spectral sequence. The induced abutment filtration is the image of Hn(FpC)→Hn(C). Finiteness in each degree suffices; a bounded range of cochain degrees is not required.

Tor functor (ToriR​(M,N))

Words: 155 Articles: 2
Homological algebra OurBigBook.com
The Tor functor is the homology of a tensor product of modules with a free resolution: ToriR​(M,N)=Hi​(F∙​⊗R​N) for a free resolution F∙​ of M. It measures the failure of tensoring to preserve exact sequences. For a commutative ring, one may resolve either argument; the double complex of the two resolutions proves that both computations agree.

Tor for a principal non-zero-divisor quotient (Tor0A​(A/(a),M)=M/aM,Tor1A​(A/(a),M)=ker(a:M→M))

Words: 58
Tor functor OurBigBook.com
For a non-zero-divisor a∈A, resolve A/(a) by 0→Aa​A→A/(a)→0. Tensoring with M gives the kernel and cokernel in the displayed formula; all higher Tor functors vanish. Resolving either argument gives the same homology by the two filtrations of the double complex of the two free resolutions. For M=A/(a), both degrees zero and one equal A/(a).

First Tor of a cyclic quotient (Tor1A​(A/I,M)=ker(I⊗A​M→M))

Words: 53
Tor functor OurBigBook.com
Start a free resolution of A/I with P0​=A and a free module P1​ surjecting onto I. The next differential presents the relations in I. Right exactness of the tensor product of modules identifies coker(P2​⊗A​M→P1​⊗A​M) with I⊗A​M. Taking degree-one homology then gives the displayed kernel formula. Hence vanishing for every ideal is equivalent to the flatness criterion using ideals.

Cochain complex ((C∙,d))

Words: 263 Articles: 10
Homological algebra OurBigBook.com
A cochain complex is a sequence of abelian groups or modules and maps dn:Cn→Cn+1 satisfying dn+1dn=0. Its cohomology is Hn(C)=kerdn/imdn−1.

Cochain map (dF​f=fdE​)

Words: 40
Cochain complex OurBigBook.com
A cochain map f:E→F consists of degreewise maps fi:Ei→Fi satisfying dFi​fi=fi+1dEi​. This identity sends cocycles to cocycles and coboundaries to coboundaries, hence induces maps on cohomology. For complexes of sheaves the same identity holds as an identity of sheaf morphisms.

Shift of a cochain complex (ΣE=E[1])

Words: 22
Cochain complex OurBigBook.com
The shift E[1] has E[1]i=Ei+1 and differential −dEi+1​. Thus Hi(E[1])=Hi+1(E). In general the differential of E[r] is (−1)rdE​.

Cycle module (Zq=kerdq)

The submodule of cocycles in degree q. A projective boundary module Bq+1 makes 0→Zq→Pq→Bq+1→0 split when Pq is projective, so Zq is then also projective.

Cocycle (z∈kerdq)

An element killed by the next differential of a cochain complex. Its class modulo coboundaries is a cohomology class.

Boundary module (Bq=imdq−1)

The submodule of coboundaries in degree q. The cohomology group is the corresponding cycle module modulo the boundary module.

Coboundary (b∈imdq−1)

Words: 22
Boundary module OurBigBook.com
An element in the image of the preceding differential of a cochain complex. Every coboundary is a cocycle, since consecutive differentials compose to zero.

Filtered cochain complex (⋯⊇FpC⊇Fp+1C⊇⋯)

Words: 34

A cochain complex with a decreasing filtration by cochain subcomplexes. A filtration finite in each degree is bounded in the degreewise sense; uniform bounds FaC=C and FbC=0 are sufficient. The associated spectral sequence starts with the graded quotient complexes.

Cochain subcomplex (d(Sn)⊆Sn+1)

Cochain complex OurBigBook.com
A sequence of submodules preserved by the differential of a cochain complex. Its inclusion is a map of complexes.

Short exact sequence of cochain complexes (0→A∗→B∗→C∗→0)

Cochain complex OurBigBook.com
A degreewise short exact sequence of cochain complexes induces a long exact sequence from a coefficient sequence in the coefficient case, and a long exact cohomology sequence generally. Its degree-one connecting homomorphism lifts a quotient cocycle, takes its coboundary, and identifies that result in the subcomplex.

A coboundary map is the degree-one differential dn:Cn→Cn+1 of a cochain complex.

Hom functor (HomR​(−,−))

Homological algebra OurBigBook.com
The Hom functor sends two R-modules to the abelian group of R-linear maps between them. It is contravariant in its first argument and covariant in its second.

Ext functor (ExtRn​(M,N))

Words: 647 Articles: 9
Hom functor OurBigBook.com
The Ext functors are the right derived functors of the Hom functor. They can be computed by applying HomR​(−,N) to a projective resolution of M and taking cohomology.

Ext from a principal non-zero-divisor quotient (ExtA0​(A/(a),M)=kera,ExtA1​(A/(a),M)=M/aM)

Words: 84
Ext functor OurBigBook.com
For a non-zero-divisor a∈A, the free resolution 0→Aa​A→A/(a)→0 gives degree-zero kernel and degree-one cokernel, and all higher Ext functors vanish. Directly in an injective resolution of the second variable, multiplication by a is surjective in every term: the map aA→I taking ax to xm extends to A→I. Thus 0→HomA​(A/(a),I∙)→I∙a​I∙→0 is a short exact sequence of complexes and gives the same result. The displayed Hom functor is covariant in M; changing it to HomA​(M,A/(a)) changes the variable that is resolved.

Nonzero Ext of the rationals with integer coefficients (ExtZ1​(Q,Z)=0)

Words: 78
Ext functor OurBigBook.com
A free resolution of Q has generators an​=1/n! and independent relations an​−(n+1)an+1​. Thus the Ext functor gives the cokernel of the map on integer sequence products
(un​)⟼(un​−(n+1)un+1​).
(1)
The constant sequence one is not in its image. A preimage would require u1​≡∑j=1N​j!(mod(N+1)!) for every N. For N≥3, the sum is below 2N!, tends to infinity, and its distance from (N+1)! also tends to infinity. No fixed integer satisfies all these congruences. Hence ExtZ1​(Q,Z)=0.

Long exact sequence of Ext groups

Words: 51
Ext functor OurBigBook.com
Applying the contravariant Hom functor HomR​(−,W) to 0→I→X→C→0 gives
0→Hom(C,W)→Hom(X,W)→Hom(I,W)→Ext1(C,W)→Ext1(X,W)→Ext1(I,W)→Ext2(C,W)→⋯.
(2)
The connecting map sends I→W to its pushout of a module extension. Its vanishing means that map extends to X→W. For a hereditary ring, Ext2(C,W)=0, making the indicated restriction on first extension groups surjective. The covariant variable has its corresponding long exact sequence.

Extension group (ExtRn​(M,N))

Words: 115 Articles: 1
Ext functor OurBigBook.com
An extension group is a value of an Ext functor. In degree one it classifies short exact sequences 0→N→E→M→0 up to isomorphisms fixing the endpoints. The zero class is the split extension; its addition is the Baer sum. Higher degrees can be represented by longer exact extensions, or computed from a projective resolution.
Baer sum
Words: 67
Extension group OurBigBook.com
To add two extensions of M by N, take their direct sum, form a pullback in a category along the diagonal M→M⊕M, and form a pushout in a category along addition N⊕N→N. This produces another extension of M by N and defines addition in ExtR1​(M,N). In a quiver representation, vertexwise splittings express the two extensions by off-diagonal arrow matrices, and the Baer sum adds those matrices modulo the extension complex of quiver representations coboundaries.

Localization of Ext over a Noetherian ring (S−1ExtRi​(N,M)≅ExtS−1Ri​(S−1N,S−1M))

Words: 52
Ext functor OurBigBook.com
Over a Noetherian ring, a finitely generated module N has a free resolution with every term finitely generated. Hom from a finite free module commutes with localization, and exactness of localization commutes with cohomology. Thus the displayed isomorphism holds for every i≥0 and arbitrary M. The finiteness condition is on the first argument; the second need not be finitely generated.

Ext groups between polynomial-ring residue modules (ExtZ[X]∗​(Z/p,Z/q))

Words: 93 Articles: 1
Ext functor OurBigBook.com
When X acts as zero, resolve the first module with the Koszul resolution on (p,X). Writing N=Z/q, the resulting groups are ker(p:N→N) in degree zero, (N/pN)⊕kerp in degree one, and N/pN in degree two, with zero higher groups. This works for composite as well as prime moduli.
Koszul self-Ext algebra (ExtR∗​(R/I,R/I)≅ΛR/I∗​((I/I2)∨))
Words: 43
Ext groups between polynomial-ring residue modules OurBigBook.com
For an ideal generated by a finite regular sequence in a commutative ring, the self-Ext functor algebra is exterior on the degree-one dual generators. Contraction chain maps on the Koszul resolution represent those generators, square to zero, and anticommute. The exterior square-zero relations also apply in characteristic two.

Ext separation of finite-length modules

Words: 149
Ext functor OurBigBook.com
Partition the isomorphism classes of simple modules into two sets C1​,C2​, with Ext1(S,T)=Ext1(T,S)=0 whenever S,T lie in different sets. Every module M of finite composition length then has a unique decomposition M=U1​⊕U2​ into its largest submodules whose Jordan–Hölder factors belong to the respective sets.
For a simple S∈C1​ and a finite-length N with factors in C2​, induction through the long exact sequence of the Ext functor gives Ext1(S,N)=0. Induct on the length of M: take 0→N→M→S→0, split N=N1​⊕N2​, and suppose S∈C1​. The quotient extension 0→N2​→M/N1​→S→0 splits. The inverse image of its S-summand complements N2​ in M and has only factors in C1​. Finally, Hom between modules with factors in different sets is zero, since any nonzero image would have a factor in both sets. Projecting any proposed submodule to the opposite summand therefore proves maximality and uniqueness.

Resolution by finitely generated free modules

Words: 68
Projective resolution OurBigBook.com
A finitely generated module over a Noetherian ring admits a possibly infinite free resolution whose every term is finitely generated. Surject a finite free module onto it; the kernel is finitely generated because a finite free module is Noetherian. Repeat this construction on the successive kernels. If the original module has finite projective dimension n, its nth syzygy is projective by dimension shifting of the Ext functor, giving a length-n resolution with finite projective terms.

Horseshoe lemma

Words: 88
Projective resolution OurBigBook.com
For a short exact sequence of modules with projective resolutions of its outer terms, there is a projective resolution of the middle term forming a degreewise split short exact sequence of resolutions. Lift the degree-zero projective cover of the quotient into the middle module and add the projective cover of the submodule; this surjects onto the middle module. Its kernel fits into the corresponding short exact sequence of kernels. Repeating this construction proves the claim. Additive contravariant functors preserve the degreewise split exact sequences with their direction reversed.

Set A=kQ, Pi​=Aei​, Xi​=ei​X. There is an exact sequence
0→⨁ρ:i→j​Pj​⊗k​Xi​d​⨁i​Pi​⊗k​Xi​→X→0,

Extension complex of quiver representations (C0δ​C1)

Words: 93 Articles: 1
Standard projective resolution of a quiver representation OurBigBook.com
Put C0=⨁i​Hom(Xi​,Yi​) and C1=⨁ρ:i→j​Hom(Xi​,Yj​). The differential has components gρ​hi​−hj​fρ​. Its kernel is HomQ​(X,Y) and its cokernel is ExtQ1​(X,Y). In a vertexwise splitting of an extension, the off-diagonal arrow blocks change by these coboundaries when the splitting changes.
Kernel-cokernel obstruction to splitting a quiver extension
Words: 51
Extension complex of quiver representations OurBigBook.com
Restriction at one arrow induces a surjection ExtQ1​(X,Y)↠Homk​(kerfρ​,cokergρ​). Coboundaries vanish on the kernel after quotienting by the image, while every map between these spaces can be lifted and extended. Nonzero kernel and cokernel therefore force a nonsplit extension. For X=Y, rigidity forces every arrow to have maximal rank.

A short exact sequence presenting a module as a quotient of a projective module. The kernel need not be projective. Continuing a projective presentation gives a projective resolution.

Koszul resolution

Words: 58
Projective resolution OurBigBook.com
For a regular sequence f1​,…,fr​ in a commutative ring R, its Koszul complex is the finite free projective resolution of R/(f1​,…,fr​) with terms ⋀jRr and boundary map contracting by (f1​,…,fr​). Exactness follows by induction: append fr​ using the mapping cone for multiplication by fr​, which is injective on the preceding quotient. For a polynomial ring viewed as a bimodule, use the regular sequence Xi,ℓ​−Xi,r​ in the enveloping algebra.

Free resolution

Words: 234 Articles: 4
Projective resolution OurBigBook.com
A free resolution is a projective resolution in which every resolving module is a free module.

Hilbert's syzygy theorem

Words: 42
Free resolution OurBigBook.com
Every finitely generated module with a grading over the polynomial ring S=k[X1​,…,Xn​] has a finite graded free resolution of length at most n. The Koszul resolution of k has length n, so ToriS​(M,k)=0 for i>n. In a minimal graded free resolution, this Tor functor is Fi​/(X1​,…,Xn​)Fi​; the graded Nakayama lemma forces Fi​=0 for i>n.

Minimal graded free resolution (F∙​)

Words: 59
Free resolution OurBigBook.com
Over S=k[X1​,…,Xn​] with its standard grading and m=(X1​,…,Xn​), a graded free resolution is minimal when di​(Fi​)⊆mFi−1​ for every i≥1. It is constructed by choosing minimal homogeneous generators for each successive syzygy module, using the graded Nakayama lemma. After tensoring with k=S/m, all its differentials vanish, so Fi​/mFi​≅ToriS​(M,k); these dimensions and degrees are intrinsic to M.

Syzygy module (ΩRi​(M))

Words: 53
Free resolution OurBigBook.com
Given a free resolution ⋯→F1​→F0​→M→0, its first syzygy module is ker(F0​→M), the module of relations among the chosen generators of M. Higher syzygy modules are successive kernels. They depend on the choice of resolution; a minimal graded free resolution gives canonical ranks and degrees of generators over a standard graded ring structure on a polynomial ring.

Bar resolution of an associative algebra (Bn​(A))

Words: 67
Free resolution OurBigBook.com
The bar resolution of an associative algebra A over a field has degree-n term A⊗(n+2) as an Ae-module, with the outer factors carrying the left and right actions. Its boundary multiplies each consecutive pair with alternating signs, and its augmentation is multiplication A⊗A→A. It is exact: the k-linear contracting homotopy inserts 1 at the left. Applying HomAe​(−,M) gives the Hochschild cochain complex of the bimodule M.
