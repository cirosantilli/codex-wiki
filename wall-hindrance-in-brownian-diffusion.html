Wall hindrance in Brownian diffusion

Buoyancy changes the effective gravitational mass; a nearby no-slip wall changes the hydrodynamic resistance matrix and therefore the diffusion coefficient. These are distinct corrections. Write the horizontal resistance as ζ∥​=6πηa/f, with 0<f<1 representing wall hindrance at a specified height. Applying the Stokes–Einstein relation with that resistance gives
D∥​=f6πηakB​T​,NA,true​NA,inferreddiff​​=f1​
(17)
if one mistakenly uses the bulk resistance in the inversion. A chosen f=0.8 would overestimate NA​ by 25%; this is a sensitivity example, not a correction measured in Jean Baptiste Perrin's apparatus. Normal and parallel coefficients of the hydrodynamic resistance matrix need not coincide. Stokes settling velocity near a wall can likewise bias a radius inferred using an unbounded-fluid formula.
For a passive dilute particle suspension at uniform temperature, a change of mobility alone does not change the Boltzmann distribution. In the absence of additional wall forces the correct local vertical flux is jz​=−Dz​(z)[dn/dz+mb​gn/(kB​T)], so zero flux still gives the same sedimentation scale height. Walls can nevertheless invalidate that simple inference through adsorption, additional interaction energies, or sampling of inaccessible grain-centre positions. Optical position errors and exposure averaging also affect displacement readout; varying distance from the wall, observation lag and optical depth tests different possible errors.
