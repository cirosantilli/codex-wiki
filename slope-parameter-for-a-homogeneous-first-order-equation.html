Slope parameter for a homogeneous first-order equation

 ... Mathematics Area of mathematics Analysis Differential equation Ordinary differential equation Homogeneous first-order differential equation
OurBigBook.com Words: 104
An implicit homogeneous first-order differential equation y/x=F(p), where p=dy/dx, can be reduced by treating the slope as an auxiliary variable. Differentiating y=xF(p) gives
p=F(p)+xF′(p)dxdp​,xdx​=p−F(p)F′(p)​dp.
(15)
Where the divisions are valid, this is a separable differential equation. Constant slopes lost when dividing by p−F(p) must be checked in the original equation.
For F(p)=2p/(1−p2), the reduced equation is 2xp′=−p(1−p2) and its nonconstant solutions obey xp2/(1−p2)=C. Substitution recovers the parabolas y2=4C(x+C), together with the constant solution y=0. Real graph branches must have p2=C/(x+C)≥0, and the original restrictions x=0, p=±1 still apply. The constant slopes p=±1 solve the reduced equation but are excluded from the original equation.
