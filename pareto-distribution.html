Pareto distribution (Pareto(α,β))

 ... Mathematics Area of mathematics Probability and statistics Probability theory Probability distribution Continuous probability distribution
OurBigBook.com Words: 1k Articles: 7
The Pareto distribution describes a positive random variable whose tail probability falls as a power law. In the shape–minimum convention used here, X∼Pareto(α,β) has shape parameter α>0 and minimum β>0, with probability support [β,∞). Its probability density function is
f(x)={αβαx−α−1,0,​x≥β,x<β.​
(16)
The minimum β has the same units as X; the shape parameter α is dimensionless. At fixed α, changing β gives a scale family, since X/β∼Pareto(α,1). This convention is also used in uniform-Pareto conjugacy.[ref]
The survival function and cumulative distribution function are
F(x)=P(X>x)={1,(β/x)α,​x<β,x≥β,​F(x)=1−F(x).
(17)
For 0<p<1, solving F(x)=p gives the quantile function
Q(p)=β(1−p)−1/α.
(18)
Its right-hand limit at p=0 is β; it tends to infinity as p↑1.[ref] There is no probability mass at β: changing a probability density function's value at that single point changes no probabilities.
Choose α=2 and β=10 as an original theoretical example. Then P(X>20)=1/4, P(X>100)=1/100, the median is 102​≃14.142, and the 0.99 quantile is Q(0.99)=100. These chosen values and all figures below describe mathematical probability distributions, not actual wealth observations.
On this log-log plot, write r=x/β to obtain logF(βr)=−αlogr. The slope is −α, whereas the probability density function has power exponent α+1. Multiplying a threshold by ten divides its tail probability by 10α. A larger α therefore concentrates the Pareto distribution nearer β; a smaller α makes extreme values more plausible. This exact scaling is useful when modelling large positive losses or sizes over several orders of magnitude.
The Pareto distribution is a heavy-tailed distribution: for every t>0, the exponential moment E[etX] diverges because etxx−α−1 eventually grows without bound. In contrast, the Laplace distribution has exponentially decreasing tails and some finite positive exponential moments. The Student's t-distribution also has power tails, but on both sides of the real line, with a smooth central region rather than a positive minimum.
