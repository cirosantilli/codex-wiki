Let X take integer values with probabilities pn​, and let Yσ​ be an independent centered normal distribution with positive standard deviation σ. Conditioning on X gives the probability density function of their sum as gσ​(z)=∑n​pn​φσ​(z−n), where φσ​ is the normal density. Nonnegativity and Tonelli's theorem show that this density integrates to one. For small σ, isolated atoms become narrow smooth peaks with their original probability masses.
For each fixed real t, dominated convergence gives
σgσ​(σt)=Eφ1​(t−X/σ)⟶φ1​(t)(σ→∞),

since X/σ→0 almost surely and φ1​ is bounded. Thus every fixed integer-valued distribution acquires a Gaussian central profile on this expanding scale, without any moment assumption on X. This is not a claim about uniform relative error in the tails or about finite moments. For example, if pn​=3/(π2n2) for n=0 and p0​=0, the expectation of ∣X∣ is infinite. Since E∣Yσ​∣<∞, the triangle inequality forces E∣X+Yσ​∣=∞ too. Symmetry then gives equal infinite positive and negative first moments, so there is no ordinary mean. The second moment is also infinite. A Gaussian-looking center can therefore coexist with a heavy-tailed distribution.
