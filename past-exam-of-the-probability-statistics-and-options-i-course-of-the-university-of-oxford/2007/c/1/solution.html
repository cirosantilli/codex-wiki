Euler's theorem states that if gcd(a,m)=1, then aϕ(m)≡1(modm), where ϕ is Euler's totient function. This includes Fermat's little theorem when m is prime.
For each prime-power factor piei​​, the integer ϕ(piei​​) divides the defined least common multiple λ(m). Euler's theorem modulo that prime power therefore gives
aλ(m)=(aϕ(piei​​))λ(m)/ϕ(piei​​)≡1(modpiei​​).
(6)
The prime powers are pairwise coprime, so their product divides aλ(m)−1. Equivalently the Chinese remainder theorem gives
aλ(m)≡1(modm).​
(7)
For the specified composite integer,
λ(561)=lcm(2,10,16)=80,560=7⋅80.
(8)
Raising a80≡1(mod561) to the seventh power proves
a560≡1(mod561)(gcd(a,561)=1).​
(9)
Thus 561 is a Carmichael number. We use exactly the source's definition of λ as the least common multiple of prime-power totients; its validity here does not require replacing it by a smaller exponent at powers of two.
