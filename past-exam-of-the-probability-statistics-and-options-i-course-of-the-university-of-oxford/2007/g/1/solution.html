Without assuming those classical derivatives initially, the same calculation with compactly supported variations states this equation weakly; sufficient regularity then gives the classical equation.
For the specific integrand, Fy​=−2y, Fy′​=0, Fy′′​=2y′′, so the equation is y(4)−y=0. Its weak solutions here are smooth, and its general classical solution is
y=Acosx+Bsinx+Ccoshx+Dsinhx.

Put ℓ=π/2, c=coshℓ, s=sinhℓ. The conditions at zero give C=−A and D=1−B. Those at ℓ give
B−Ac+(1−B)s=0,−A−As+(1−B)c=0.

Using c2−s2=1, solve to obtain A=c/2, B=(1−s)/2, C=−c/2, D=(1+s)/2. Thus an extremal is

Substitution checks all four boundary values. The additive term x2 in the integrand contributes no variation in y, so it does not alter this equation.
