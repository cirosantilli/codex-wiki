Take the curl of the Euler equations for an inviscid fluid. The gradient on the right has zero curl. Use the stated vector identity with F=ω, G=u:
∂t​ω+(u⋅∇)ω−(ω⋅∇)u+ω(∇⋅u)−u(∇⋅ω)=0.
(43)
incompressible flow gives ∇⋅u=0, while a curl always has zero divergence, so ∇⋅ω=0. The remaining terms give
DtDω​=(ω⋅∇)u.​
(44)
For a two-dimensional velocity independent of z, ω=(0,0,vx​−uy​) and (ω⋅∇)u=ωz​∂z​u=0. Thus the scalar vorticity is conserved following each fluid particle. It can still vary from particle to particle; this is advection, not a claim of spatially uniform vorticity.
