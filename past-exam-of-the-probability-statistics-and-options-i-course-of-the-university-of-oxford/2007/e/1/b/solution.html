A metric space is sequentially compact if every sequence in it has a subsequence converging to a point of the same space. The sequence xn​=1/(n+1) lies in (0,1), but every subsequence converges in R to zero. Uniqueness of limits excludes an alternative limit inside (0,1), so the open interval is not sequentially compact.
For the product, take any sequence (xn​,yn​). sequential compactness of X gives a subsequence xnk​​→x∈X. Apply sequential compactness of Y to the corresponding sequence ynk​​ to obtain a further subsequence ynkj​​​→y∈Y. Passing to a subsequence preserves the convergence of the first coordinate, and therefore
d((xnkj​​​,ynkj​​​),(x,y))=dX​(xnkj​​​,x)+dY​(ynkj​​​,y)→0.
(18)
Thus X×Y is sequentially compact with the stated sum metric. Taking the second coordinate subsequence inside the first one is essential; two unrelated subsequences would not suffice.
