The Markov property says that, conditional on the present state, the next state is independent of the earlier history:

Two states communicate if each is reachable from the other with positive probability in finitely many steps. The equivalence classes are communicating classes; a class is closed if no positive-probability transition leaves it.
The displayed probabilities require 0≤p≤1/2; negative values allowed by the printed one-sided inequality are not transition probabilities. For p=0, every state is an absorbing singleton, so all singleton classes are closed. For 0<p≤1/2, neighboring states communicate in both directions, so there is one closed irreducible class S and no open classes.

For 0<p<1/2, positive holding probabilities make the finite irreducible chain aperiodic, so its transition probabilities converge to πj​ from every initial state. At p=1/2, every step changes parity and the period is two; convergence from a fixed initial state fails. A stationary initial mixture still has distribution π at every time, but this does not give convergence of the transition probabilities asked for.
