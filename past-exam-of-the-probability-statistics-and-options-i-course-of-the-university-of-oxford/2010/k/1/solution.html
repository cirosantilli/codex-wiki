The momentum of a material volume V(t) is ∫V(t)​ρudV. Newton's second law equates its time derivative to the pressure force and gravity:
dtd​∫V(t)​ρudV=−∫∂V(t)​pndS+∫V(t)​ρgdV.
(29)
Apply the given Reynolds transport theorem componentwise to the left side, and the divergence theorem to the pressure term. Then
∫V(t)​[ρ(∂t​u+(u⋅∇)u)+∇p−ρg]dV=0.
(30)
Since this holds for arbitrary material volumes and the fields are continuous, the integrand vanishes pointwise. Dividing by positive density gives the Euler equations for an inviscid fluid
∂t​u+(u⋅∇)u=−ρ−1∇p+g.​
(31)
At a fixed physical time t0​, a streamline is a curve tangent to u(⋅,t0​), which may be parametrized by dx/ds=u(x(s),t0​). The chain rule gives df(x(s),t0​)/ds=u⋅∇f=0, proving constancy along it. The time is held fixed; a time-dependent streamline statement does not by itself assert material conservation along particle trajectories.
