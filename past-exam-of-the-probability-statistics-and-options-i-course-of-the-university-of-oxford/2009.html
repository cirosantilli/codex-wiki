A finite soluble group is one whose derived series G(0)=G, G(j+1)=[G(j),G(j)] reaches the trivial group. Equivalently it has a finite normal series with abelian factors. If H≤G, induction using commutator containment gives H(j)≤G(j) for every j. Thus termination for G implies termination for H, proving every subgroup of a soluble group is soluble.
For S4​, the chain
{1}◃V4​◃A4​◃S4​
(3)
has abelian factors V4​, C3​ and C2​. Here V4​ consists of the identity and the three double transpositions. It is normal because conjugation permutes those double transpositions. Hence S4​ is soluble.
An example neither soluble nor simple is S5​. Its subgroup A5​ is proper, nontrivial and normal, so S5​ is not simple. The group A5​ is nontrivial and perfect: its generating three-cycles are commutators of double transpositions, using two additional symbols, so [A5​,A5​]=A5​. For example the commutator of (12)(45) and (23)(45) is a three-cycle, and conjugation and inversion give all three-cycles. Thus A5​ is not soluble, and subgroup inheritance then proves S5​ is not soluble either.

The Lebesgue outer measure of E is the infimum of ∑j​∣Ij​∣ over countable coverings by open intervals. A set is a null set when this infimum is zero, equivalently when it has such a cover of total length less than every prescribed positive number.
The Cantor set is obtained from [0,1] by deleting the open middle third of every remaining interval at each stage. After n stages it lies inside 2n closed intervals of length 3−n. Slightly enlarging these finitely many intervals to open intervals shows its outer measure is at most (2/3)n for every n, hence zero.
For the modified construction, let Ln​ be the total length of the 2n intervals in Kn​. The removed fraction at stage n is exactly 1/(2n+2), so
Ln+1​=Ln​2n+22n+1​,L0​=1.
(7)
The product telescopes because 2n+2=2(2n−1+1), giving
Ln​=31​+3⋅2n2​.
(8)
Since K⊆Kn​, the same finite interval-cover argument gives m∗(K)≤Ln​ for every n. Taking the infimum proves
m∗(K)≤1/3.​
(9)
Only interval lengths and the definition of outer measure were used; the three numbered clauses in the source describe the construction rather than separate requests to prove additional statements.

A useful differentiation under the integral sign theorem is this: if f(⋅,y) is integrable, f is differentiable in y almost everywhere, and on every compact parameter interval ∣∂y​f(x,y)∣ is bounded by an integrable function of x, then F′(y)=∫I​∂y​f(x,y)dx, under the usual pointwise continuity hypotheses. 1+x4cos(x2y)​,−1+x4x2sin(x2y)​,
