Take an admissible variation u+ϵη with η=0 on the boundary. Differentiating the functional at an extremum gives
0=∫V​(Fu​η+Fux​​ηx​+Fuy​​ηy​+Fuz​​ηz​)dV.
(16)
Integration by parts removes the derivatives from η; the boundary term vanishes. The fundamental lemma of the calculus of variations yields
∂x​Fux​​+∂y​Fuy​​+∂z​Fuz​​−Fu​=0.​
(17)
For the specified squared-gradient energy this is 2Δu=0, so u is harmonic. The affine function ℓ=ax+by+cz is harmonic and satisfies the boundary data.
To verify a global minimum, write u=ℓ+v with zero boundary trace. Then
I[u]=I[ℓ]+2∫V​∇ℓ⋅∇vdV+∫V​∣∇v∣2dV.
(18)
The middle term vanishes by integration by parts, since Δℓ=0 and v=0 at the boundary. Hence I[u]≥I[ℓ], and
minI=(a2+b2+c2)volV.​
(19)
This proves minimality directly, rather than relying only on a necessary Euler-Lagrange equation.
