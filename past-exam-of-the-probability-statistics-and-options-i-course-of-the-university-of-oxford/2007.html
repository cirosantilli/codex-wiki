For a finite group action, Burnside's lemma gives
∣Ω/G∣=∣G∣1​∑g∈G​∣Fix(g)∣.
(1)
Pair each vertex with its opposite face. This correspondence is equivariant under every tetrahedral symmetry. Each pair has six possible combined color states: three face colors and two vertex colors. Thus we are coloring four positions with six symbols; the two kinds of coloring must be counted together.
The group of rotations is the alternating group A4​. The identity has four cycles and fixes 64 colorings. Its three half-turns have cycle type (2,2) and each fix 62. Its eight third-turns have cycle type (3,1) and each also fix 62. Consequently the rotation count is
1264+3⋅62+8⋅62​=141.​
(2)
Allowing mirror equivalence gives the full tetrahedral symmetry group S4​. Now all permutations of the four paired positions are allowed, so a coloring is determined by a multiset of four symbols chosen from six. stars and bars gives
(46+4−1​)=(49​)=126.​
(3)
For an independent Burnside check, the five cycle types of S4​ give numerator 64+6⋅63+3⋅62+8⋅62+6⋅6=3024, whose quotient by 24 is 126. This is paired face and vertex colorings of a tetrahedron.

The degree of a field extension [K:F] is the dimension of K as a vector space over F. The tower law states that for finite extensions F⊆E⊆K,
[K:F]=[K:E][E:F].
(4)
If the left side is prime, one factor is one. Degree one forces equality of the corresponding fields, so there is no proper intermediate field.
Let K have 32 elements. Its prime field is F2​, and its dimension over that field is five, since a dimension-d vector space over F2​ has 2d elements. Every element outside F2​ has minimal polynomial of degree five: its generated intermediate field has degree greater than one and dividing five.
Every element of K satisfies x32=x, by the multiplicative group of a field of order 31 for nonzero elements. Hence X32−X splits completely over K and has no repeated roots, since its derivative is minus one. Conversely any irreducible polynomial q over F2​ gives a quotient ring F2​[X]/(q) with 32 elements. Its defining element satisfies X32−X, so q divides that polynomial and therefore has all five distinct roots in K.
The 30 elements of K∖F2​ are thus partitioned into disjoint five-element root sets of the irreducible polynomials. Their number is
532−2​=6.​
(5)
No classification of the six individual polynomials is needed; the tower degree and separability already count them all.

A metric space is sequentially compact if every sequence in it has a subsequence converging to a point of the same space. The sequence xn​=1/(n+1) lies in (0,1), but every subsequence converges in R to zero. Uniqueness of limits excludes an alternative limit inside (0,1), so the open interval is not sequentially compact.
For the product, take any sequence (xn​,yn​). sequential compactness of X gives a subsequence xnk​​→x∈X. Apply sequential compactness of Y to the corresponding sequence ynk​​ to obtain a further subsequence ynkj​​​→y∈Y. Passing to a subsequence preserves the convergence of the first coordinate, and therefore
d((xnkj​​​,ynkj​​​),(x,y))=dX​(xnkj​​​,x)+dY​(ynkj​​​,y)→0.
(18)
Thus X×Y is sequentially compact with the stated sum metric. Taking the second coordinate subsequence inside the first one is essential; two unrelated subsequences would not suffice.

With integer capacities and an integer electric current flow, every residual capacity is an integer, so a path bottleneck is a positive integer.
