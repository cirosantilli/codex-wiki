Coupled-enzyme readout of glycolytic ATP formation

 Home Chemistry Biochemistry Metabolic pathway Glycolysis
OurBigBook.com Words: 346
The final substrate-level phosphorylation in glycolysis provides a useful experimental handle: pyruvate kinase transfers a phosphate group from phosphoenolpyruvate to ADP, producing pyruvate and ATP. Adding lactate dehydrogenase couples each pyruvate formed to consumption of one NADH:
phosphoenolpyruvate+ADP+H+pyruvate+NADH+H+​⟶pyruvate+ATP,⟶lactate+NAD+.​
(12)
These equations select free ionic forms consistently: phosphoenolpyruvate has charge −3, ADP −3, ATP −4, and pyruvate and lactate each −1; NAD+ has one more positive charge than NADH. Each line conserves charge. Cancelling pyruvate gives one NADH consumed per ATP formed in this coupled system. A whole-pathway NADH measurement has a different meaning, because the earlier glycolysis redox step produces NADH.
The published An Optimized Enzyme-Coupled Spectrophotometric Method for Measuring Pyruvate Kinase Kinetics reports recombinant pyruvate kinase prepared in Escherichia coli, purified by chromatography and size-exclusion chromatography. UV-transparent microplate wells contained ADP, phosphoenolpyruvate, NADH, lactate dehydrogenase and a buffer solution with magnesium ions. An Epoch plate reader used spectrophotometry to follow absorbance at 340nm every five to ten seconds for one minute at 25∘C. Its Figure 2 presents A0​−At​, increasing as NADH is consumed. Heat-inactivated pyruvate kinase gave minimal background; a validated active preparation was the positive control. These comparisons support an enzyme-dependent readout in the prepared system, rather than a whole-biological cell glycolysis measurement.[ref]
The same report uses 0.5cm for optical path length in Procedure B.2.i, but approximately 0.3cm in Data analysis §3. An absolute conversion requires the actual path length and sufficient coupling enzyme activity; no measured ATP rate is inferred here.[ref]
For an original dimensional example, stipulate a blank-corrected absorbance decline −dA340​/dt=0.024min−1, a calibrated path length ℓ=0.40cm and a chosen calibration coefficient ε=6000Lmol−1cm−1. These are invented teaching inputs, not the protocol's measurements or its quoted coefficient. The Beer-Lambert law gives
A340​=εℓcNADH​,−dtdcNADH​​=6000×0.400.024​=10−5molL−1min−1.
(13)
In a stipulated 0.20mL reaction volume, this is 2nmolmin−1 of NADH consumed and, conditionally, 2nmolmin−1 of ATP formed. The conditional inference requires rapid pyruvate consumption by the coupling enzyme, no other NADH-consuming chemical reactions, and a linear, correctly calibrated optical response. The readout measures this last glycolysis step; it cannot establish the full two-net-ATP-per-glucose balance by itself.
