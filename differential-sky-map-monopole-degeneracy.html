Differential sky map monopole degeneracy (dij​=Ti​−Tj​,A1=0)

 ... Branches of physics Cosmology Cosmic microwave background Cosmic microwave background anisotropy Differential Microwave Radiometer COBE DMR sky anisotropy experiment
OurBigBook.com Words: 266
An ideal differential measurement constrains two sky temperatures through dij​=Ti​−Tj​. Represent every observed pair by a row of a matrix A, with +1 in column i, −1 in column j and zeros elsewhere. Then d=AT and A1=0: adding any constant to the entire map changes no measurement. This null space is the angular angular monopole ambiguity, explaining why a directional comparison alone cannot measure the absolute blackbody temperature established by FIRAS.
For an original three-pixel example choose T=(30,−10,−20)μK relative to an unspecified mean. Measurements d12​=40μK and d23​=10μK determine the third difference d31​=−50μK. The zero-mean condition recovers the chosen map, but adding 100μK to every pixel gives identical differences. The closure sum d12​+d23​+d31​=0 checks consistency without establishing the mean.
More generally, the graph joining measured pixel pairs must be a connected graph. If AT=0, each edge forces equal endpoint temperatures; a path propagates that equality throughout each connected component. Thus a connected observation graph leaves exactly one undetermined constant, whereas disconnected coverage leaves one per component. Fixing the mean makes an ideal connected reconstruction unique; real noise calls for least-squares solution fitting and uncertainty analysis.

Expanding cos2ϕ=(1+cos2ϕ)/2 proves the formula; its peak magnitude is 453​/2≃38.9711μK. A constant sky gives zero. This deliberately restricted scan also misses some angular patterns, so rejecting the angular monopole is not a proof of complete sky reconstruction. Broad, overlapping coverage supplies the missing constraints; finite antenna beam patterns additionally smooth small angular features.
