a OurBigBook.com
A real singular value decomposition has the form A=UΣVT, where U∈Rm×m and V∈Rn×n are orthogonal matrices, and Σ is a rectangular diagonal matrix with entries σ1​≥⋯≥σmin(m,n)​≥0. These entries are the singular values. Their squares are the eigenvalues of ATA, omitting the additional zero eigenvalues when n>m. The positive singular values number exactly the matrix rank.

Use the singular value decomposition and the orthogonal invariance of the Euclidean norm. Put z=VTx and c=UTb. Since precisely σ1​,…,σr​ are positive,
∥Ax−b∥22​=∑j=1r​(σj​zj​−cj​)2+∑j=r+1m​cj2​.
(1)
The second sum is independent of x and cannot be removed. The first sum vanishes exactly when zj​=cj​/σj​ for 1≤j≤r, while zr+1​,…,zn​ are arbitrary. Thus every least-squares solution is
x=j=1∑r​σj​ujT​b​vj​+j=r+1∑n​αj​vj​,αj​∈R.​
(2)
The unavoidable linear-system residual is the component of b orthogonal to the column space of A:
xmin​∥Ax−b∥2​=(j=r+1∑m​∣ujT​b∣2)1/2.​
(3)
The minimum-norm least-squares solution sets all αj​=0 and equals A+b, where A+ is the Moore-Penrose inverse. The squared minimum, rather than the minimum itself, is the second sum in the first display.

Let uj​ and vj​ be the columns of the orthogonal matrices U and V in the singular value decomposition. Multiplying by the rectangular diagonal matrix retains its r nonzero diagonal entries:
A=UΣVT=j=1∑r​σj​uj​vjT​.​
(4)
The families (uj​) and (vj​) are orthonormal, hence in particular orthogonal. Each summand is a rank-one outer product, and the remaining terms vanish because their singular values are zero.

For the matrix's Euclidean operator norm, orthogonal invariance of the Euclidean norm gives
∥Ax∥2​=∥ΣVTx∥2​≤σ1​∥x∥2​.
(5)

The spectral theorem for real symmetric matrices gives A=QΛQT with an orthogonal matrix Q. Positive definiteness makes every eigenvalue λi​ positive. Define the matrix square root
A1/2=Qdiag(λ1​​,…,λn​​)QT.​
(10)
It is symmetric, its square is A, and for any nonzero vector x,
xTA1/2x=∑i​λi​​(QTx)i2​>0.
(11)
Thus it is also positive-definite, and its matrix inverse is A−1/2=Qdiag(λi−1/2​)QT.

The induced matrix norm associated with the given energy norm is a supremum of length ratios. The invertible change of variables y=A1/2x gives
∥B∥A​=supx=0​∥x∥A​∥Bx∥A​​=supy=0​∥y∥2​∥A1/2BA−1/2y∥2​​=∥A1/2BA−1/2∥2​​.

Write A=D+L+R, where D is the diagonal matrix of diagonal entries of A, and L,R are its strictly lower- and upper-triangular parts. Since A is symmetric, R=LT. Subtract the two factors in the SOR iteration:
(D+ωL)−[(1−ω)D−ωR]=ωA.
(13)
Therefore, for ω=0,
C(ω)=I−ω(D+ωL)−1A=I−B(ω)−1A,B(ω)=ω−1D+L.​
(14)
Positive definiteness makes each Dii​>0, so the triangular matrix B(ω) is an invertible matrix for every ω=0. The original formula gives C(0)=I; the expression for B(ω) itself is undefined there. The affine term ensuring that the desired solution is a fixed point is c=B(ω)−1b.

Apply the matrix norm induced by an energy norm and set T=A1/2C(ω)A−1/2=I−A1/2B−1A1/2. Multiplication gives
TTT=I−A1/2[B−1+B−T−B−TAB−1]A1/2.
(15)
The bracket factors as B−T(B+BT−A)B−1. Because B=D/ω+L and A=D+L+LT,
B+BT−A=(ω2​−1)D.
(16)
Thus TTT=M(ω) for the stated matrix. In particular M(ω) is a positive semidefinite matrix. The Euclidean operator norm squared is the largest eigenvalue of TTT, and all those eigenvalues are nonnegative. Consequently
∥C(ω)∥A​=∥T∥2​=ρ(M(ω))1/2.​
(17)
This identity is the algebraic step in the energy-norm proof of convergence of SOR; spectral radius equals the largest eigenvalue here because M(ω)=TTT.

For the quadratic function, write rj​=b−Axj​ and restrict to the full line xj​+αpj​. Symmetry of A gives
f(xj​+αpj​)=f(xj​)−αpjT​rj​+2α2​pjT​Apj​.
(20)
The coefficient pjT​Apj​ is positive by positive definiteness and pj​=0. The unique minimizer is therefore
αj​=pjT​Apj​pjT​rj​​,xj+1​=xj​+αj​pj​.​
(21)
Quadratic minimization along a line also gives the exact change
f(xj+1​)−f(xj​)=−2pjT​Apj​(pjT​rj​)2​.
(22)
Thus strict decrease holds exactly when pjT​rj​=0. The search is over the whole line, so αj​ can be negative; a positive step size would require the additional descent direction condition pjT​rj​>0.

b OurBigBook.com
The exact minimization gives rj+1​=rj​−αj​Apj​, with αj​=pjT​rj​/(pjT​Apj​). For steepest descent method, the next search direction is pj+1​=rj+1​. Hence
pjT​pj+1​=pjT​rj​−αj​pjT​Apj​=0.​
(23)
Thus consecutive nonzero search directions are orthogonal. This calculation even covers an arbitrary initial direction p1​, as in the generic algorithm: it only needs the exact preceding line minimization and the choice pj+1​=rj+1​. If the new linear-system residual is zero, the linear system is already solved. No pairwise orthogonality of all nonconsecutive directions is asserted.

Solution

Words: 115
c OurBigBook.com
Nonzero vectors p1​,…,pk​ are conjugate directions with respect to A when piT​Apj​=0 for i=j. They are linearly independent: if ∑i​ci​pi​=0, multiplication by pjT​A gives cj​pjT​Apj​=0, hence cj​=0 by positive definiteness. There are consequently at most n such directions.
The finite termination of conjugate directions follows from the residual orthogonality maintained by the exact line minimizations. At step j,
rj+1​=rj​−αj​Apj​,pjT​rj+1​=0.
(24)
For i<j, conjugacy gives piT​rj+1​=piT​rj​. By mathematical induction, rj+1​ is orthogonal to every p1​,…,pj​. After n nonzero conjugate directions they form a basis of Rn, forcing rn+1​=0. Thus the method reaches Ax=b in at most n steps, and positive definiteness makes that solution unique. The claim is in exact arithmetic; rounding errors need not preserve exact conjugacy.
