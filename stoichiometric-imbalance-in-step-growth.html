Stoichiometric imbalance in step growth (r=A0​/B0​)

 Home Chemistry Polymer Polymerization Step-growth polymerization Carothers equation
OurBigBook.com Words: 264
For a strictly bifunctional AA+BB feed, label the limiting functional groups A, set 0<r=A0​/B0​≤1, and define p=J/A0​ as in functional-group conversion in step growth. The initial molecule count is N0​=(A0​+B0​)/2, and each intermolecular join consumes one A and one B. Thus
N=2A0​+B0​​−pA0​,Xn​=1+r−2rp1+r​.
(8)
This version of the Carothers equation uses limiting-group conversion, not the fraction of all groups reacted.[ref]
As an original finite example, mix 95 AA monomer molecules with 100 BB monomer molecules. Then (N0​,A0​,B0​)=(195,190,200) and r=0.95. At p=0.90, 171 joins leave 24 molecules, giving Xn​=195/24=8.125. At p=1, 190 joins leave five chains and ten unreacted B groups; every chain has two B ends, and Xn​=39. Continuing to wait cannot remove the missing-A constraint within this model.
For r<1, complete limiting-group conversion gives the ceiling Xn,max​=(1+r)/(1−r). Reaching a target Xn​=K>1 therefore requires r≥(K−1)/(K+1), even before considering incomplete conversion. For K=200, r≥199/201≈0.99005. Equality requires p=1; any lower conversion demands closer balance. The imbalance 1−r is measured relative to the initial number of B groups, not relative to the total number of groups. A monofunctional impurity changes the initial groups-per-molecule count, so it cannot be inserted into this strictly bifunctional formula by retaining an unexamined r.
