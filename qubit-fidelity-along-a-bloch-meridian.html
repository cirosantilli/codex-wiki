Qubit fidelity along a Bloch meridian (F=cos(θ/2),Fsq​=cos2(θ/2),D=sin(θ/2))

 ... Physics Branches of physics Quantum theory Quantum information theory Fidelity of quantum states Pure-state trace distance and fidelity identity
OurBigBook.com Words: 163
Compare the qubit pure state
∣ψθ​⟩=cos(θ/2)∣0⟩+sin(θ/2)∣1⟩,0≤θ≤π,
(13)
with ∣0⟩, using the computational basis. Its Bloch vector is (sinθ,0,cosθ): it follows a meridian of the Bloch sphere from the north pole ∣0⟩ through ∣+⟩ to the south pole ∣1⟩. The inner product with ∣0⟩ is cos(θ/2), nonnegative on this interval. Consequently quantum fidelity, squared quantum fidelity, and trace distance are
F=cos(θ/2),Fsq​=cos2(θ/2),D=sin(θ/2).
(14)
At θ=0 the triple (F,Fsq​,D) is (1,1,0); at θ=π/2 it is (1/2​,1/2,1/2​); at θ=π it is (0,0,1). The angle between the Bloch vectors is θ, while the quantum fidelity uses its half-angle. The pure-state trace distance and fidelity identity becomes the familiar relation cos2(θ/2)+sin2(θ/2)=1.
For equal prior probabilities, the Holevo–Helstrom theorem gives optimal quantum state discrimination success Psucc​=(1+D)/2. Along this meridian it rises from 1/2 to one, with (1+1/2​)/2≈0.8536 at ∣+⟩. This connects the geometric trace distance to the best possible decision from a single preparation; it is a different measurement task from the target-acceptance probability defining squared quantum fidelity.
