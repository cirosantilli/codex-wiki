In a contractible region, the Maxwell equations ∇⋅B=0 and the Poincaré lemma imply the existence of a magnetic vector potential A such that B=∇×A. Faraday's law of induction then gives
∇×(E+∂t​A)=0.
(1)
The expression in parentheses is a gradient there, so a scalar electric potential ϕ can be chosen with
B=∇×A,E=−∇ϕ−∂t​A.​
(2)
These statements are local, or global when the domain has the required topology and the electromagnetic fields are regular. A nonzero flux through a topologically nontrivial closed surface can obstruct a single globally defined vector potential, even though local expressions remain valid. The electric potential and magnetic vector potential are not unique: A↦A+∇χ and ϕ↦ϕ−∂t​χ leave both electromagnetic fields unchanged.
For the Lorenz gauge condition ∇⋅A=−c−2∂t​ϕ, Gauss's law becomes
−∇2ϕ−∂t​(∇⋅A)=ϵ0​ρ​,(∇2−c21​∂t2​)ϕ=−ϵ0​ρ​.
(3)
The Ampere-Maxwell law gives
∇(∇⋅A)−∇2A=μ0​J−c21​∇∂t​ϕ−c21​∂t2​A.
(4)
Cancel the two gradient terms using the Lorenz gauge condition. Hence
(∇2−c21​∂t2​)ϕ=−ϵ0​ρ​,(∇2−c21​∂t2​)A=−μ0​J.​
(5)
Both potential equations have the same d'Alembert operator, with their respective charge density and current density sources. In a source-free region these are homogeneous wave equations with propagation speed c.
