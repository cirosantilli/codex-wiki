Two-electron Laughlin relative probability (pm​(u)=22m+1m!u2m+1e−u2/4​)

Consider the original two-electron example obtained by taking N=2 in the Laughlin wavefunction, with no additional polynomial for the centre of mass. Introduce Z=(z1​+z2​)/2, relative complex coordinate w=z1​−z2​, separation r=∣w∣, and dimensionless separation u=r/ℓB​. Since ∣z1​∣2+∣z2​∣2=2∣Z∣2+∣w∣2/2, the probability density factorizes:
∣Ψm​∣2∝e−∣Z∣2/ℓB2​r2me−r2/(4ℓB2​).
(39)
Integrating out Z leaves an isotropic relative probability density. Define Dm​(u) per unit dimensionless area and pm​(u) per unit dimensionless separation:
Dm​(u)=π4m+1m!u2me−u2/4​,pm​(u)=2πuDm​(u),∫0∞​pm​(u)du=1.
(40)
The physical areal probability density is Dm​(r/ℓB​)/ℓB2​. The probability of a separation between r and r+dr is pm​(r/ℓB​)dr/ℓB​. The extra u comes from the Jacobian determinant in polar coordinates: a thin annulus has area 2πrdr. Omitting that factor changes the question being answered.
Both wavefunction normalization and the peak shift can be checked exactly. The substitution t=u2/4 gives the Gamma function integral
∫0∞​u2m+1e−u2/4du=22m+1∫0∞​tme−tdt=22m+1m!.
(41)
For m>0, logarithmic differentiation gives
dud​logDm​=u2m​−2u​,dud​logpm​=u2m+1​−2u​.
(42)
Consequently the areal maximum lies at u=2m​, while the most probable separation lies farther out, at u=4m+2​. For m=1 these are 2 and 6​≃2.449; for m=3, 12​≃3.464 and 14​≃3.742. The same substitution gives ⟨u2⟩=4(m+1), so the root mean square separations are 8​ and 4 respectively. The distinction between a probability density maximum and a radial maximum therefore has a directly calculable geometric origin.
This two-electron model explains exchange and pair avoidance. It does not establish a nonzero many-particle spectral gap, an extended quantum Hall plateau, or the topological properties of a fluid in the thermodynamic limit. Those require additional many-electron reasoning and experimental tests.
