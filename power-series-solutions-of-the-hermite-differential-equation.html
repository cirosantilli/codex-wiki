Power series solutions of the Hermite differential equation (an+2​=2(n−λ)an​/[(n+2)(n+1)])

For y′′−2xy′+2λy=0, substitute a power series y=∑n≥0​an​xn to obtain the displayed recurrence relation. It separates into an even solution with a0​=1,a1​=0 and an odd solution with a0​=0,a1​=1:
ye​=1−λx2+6λ(λ−2)​x4+⋯,yo​=x+31−λ​x3+30(1−λ)(3−λ)​x5+⋯.
(108)
The ratio of successive terms within each parity tends to zero at every fixed complex x, giving infinite radius of convergence. The two initial-value vectors are independent, so these solutions span the solution space. A nonzero polynomial solution exists precisely when λ=N is a nonnegative integer: its highest power forces this value, and the recurrence terminates the matching parity. These terminating solutions are nonzero scalar multiples of the Hermite polynomials. Multiplication by e−x2/2 connects them to Hermite oscillator quantization.
