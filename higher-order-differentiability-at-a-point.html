Higher-order differentiability at a point (f(n)(a))

 Home Mathematics Area of mathematics Analysis Differentiable function Differentiability
OurBigBook.com Words: 76
For n=1, differentiability at a means that the difference quotient has a finite two-sided limit of a function. For n≥2, require derivatives through order n−1 throughout some open neighbourhood of a, and require the final difference quotient
f(n)(a)=limh→0​hf(n−1)(a+h)−f(n−1)(a)​
(79)
to exist as a finite real number. The last derivative need not exist elsewhere or be continuous at a. In particular, existence of f′(a) by itself does not justify taking a second derivative: the function f′ must first be defined in a neighbourhood of a.
