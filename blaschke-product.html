For zeros an​ in the unit disc satisfying ∑n​(1−∣an​∣)<∞, with m occurrences at zero, a Blaschke product is
B(z)=λzm∏an​=0​an​∣an​∣​1−an​​zan​−z​,∣λ∣=1.
(3)
For ∣z∣≤R<1 and a=0, the normalized factor ba​ satisfies ∣1−ba​(z)∣≤(1−∣a∣)(1+R)/(1−R). The product therefore converges locally uniformly; outside its prescribed zeros the logarithms of its tail factors converge absolutely, so it has no additional zeros. Every finite product has modulus at most one in the disc, and the limit does too. Since ∣ba​(w)∣ is pseudohyperbolic distance from w to a, its modulus can be estimated geometrically.
