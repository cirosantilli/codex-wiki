Adjacency list edge conventions

 Home Computer science Data structure Adjacency list
OurBigBook.com Words: 305
For a directed graph, store v in Adj[u] for the edge u→v. The reverse edge v→u is separate and is stored only if it exists. Consequently, list length is outdegree, not indegree, and each directed edge contributes one entry in total. An incoming-list index is additional storage.[ref]
Orient the seven edges of our example from earlier to later labels. The resulting directed graph has
Adj[A]Adj[D]​=(B,C),=(E,F),​Adj[B]Adj[E]​=(C),=(F),​Adj[C]Adj[F]​=(D),=().​
(3)
There are now seven entries, with outdegrees (2,1,1,2,1,0). In particular, F needs an empty list even though it is reached from D,E: incoming edges do not make F's outgoing list nonempty. Replacing each directed edge by an undirected graph connection would lose this distinction.
Our running simple graph excludes self-loops and repeated edges. If those restrictions are relaxed, specify how the adjacency list encodes them. Here an undirected self-loop at u contributes two copies of u to Adj[u], matching its contribution of two to the degree of a vertex. A directed self-loop contributes one copy, and one each to outdegree and indegree. Distinct parallel edges retain distinct entries; a multigraph's list length can therefore exceed its number of distinct neighbours.[ref][ref]
For example, add one undirected self-loop at A to the seven-edge example. Its list becomes (B,C,A,A), the vertex degree of A becomes four, and the total entry count becomes sixteen for eight edges. If an application instead stores a self-loop once, it must account for its doubled undirected vertex degree separately. In a weighted graph, an entry can be an ordered pair (v,w) meaning destination v and weight w; the weight belongs to the edge, not to the destination vertex.
