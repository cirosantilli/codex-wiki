Antiproton mass from momentum and velocity (m2c4=p2c2(β−2−1))

For a singly charged track perpendicular to a uniform magnetic field, its curvature gives magnetic rigidity p/∣q∣=Br, and the bend direction gives the sign of electric charge. The mass shell relation and four-momentum relation between energy and velocity give
E2=p2c2+m2c4,β=cv​=Epc​,m2c4=p2c2(β−2−1).
(4)
For a known path length L and constant velocity, time of flight t=L/(βc) therefore gives
m2c4=p2c2[(Lct​)2−1].
(5)
This measures rest mass under an assigned electric charge magnitude. Curvature alone measures p/∣q∣, so independent evidence for ∣q∣=e matters. In material, the measured delay is instead t=∫dl/v(l); matching a local Cherenkov detector velocity to an upstream momentum requires an energy-loss correction.

Both particles have the same selected momentum and negative electric charge, yet their time of flight separation is about 10.51ns. These are calculated values for a deliberately simplified example, not measured discovery events.
For example, the toy energy loss changes the central antiproton velocity from 0.7878c to 0.7745c. Directly substituting the slower downstream value together with the upstream momentum would overestimate the rest mass. The two panels thus describe different stages of a deliberately specified model; their numerical consistency depends on propagating energy between stages. Likewise, the scattered-pion markers represent an erroneous C2 response, not actual slow pions; an upstream C1 signal still rejects them.
