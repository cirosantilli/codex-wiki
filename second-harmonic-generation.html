Second-harmonic generation

 Home Physics Branches of physics Optics Nonlinear optics
OurBigBook.com Words: 850 Articles: 5
Second-harmonic generation converts an incident optical frequency ν into 2ν through a material's nonlinear response. In the photon description of an ideal lossless conversion, two input photons supply one output photon:
2hν=h(2ν),ω=2πν.
(60)
Here h is the Planck constant. The relation states conservation of energy; the material mediates the interaction. It does not say that each input photon independently splits its wavelength in half.
For vacuum wavelengths, λ0,ω​=c/ν and λ0,2ω​=c/(2ν), where c is the speed of light. Hence
λ0,2ω​=2λ0,ω​​,λmed,ω​λmed,2ω​​=2n2ω​nω​​.
(61)
The second relation uses the refractive index for each propagating mode and frequency. Within a medium with optical dispersion, the wavelength need not halve. For an invented example, λ0,ω​=800nm, nω​=1.5, and n2ω​=1.6 give 400nm in vacuum, but in the material the two wavelengths are 533.33nm and 250nm; their ratio is 0.46875.
