X-ray attenuation (I(x)=I0​e−μx)

 Home Physics Branches of physics Electromagnetism Electromagnetic radiation X-ray
OurBigBook.com Words: 800 Articles: 8
For a narrow beam of monoenergetic photons crossing homogeneous material of thickness x, the uncollided wave intensity obeys I(x)=I0​e−μx. Here μ is the linear attenuation coefficient, counting removal from the incident beam by photon absorption and scattering of light. It depends on photon energy and material; it is not universally independent of energy.[ref]
An original derivation makes the model explicit. If each thin layer removes fraction μdx of the surviving beam, with the same μ throughout, then
dxdI​=−μI,lnI0​I(x)​=−μx.
(22)
Thus equal added thicknesses multiply transmission by the same factor. The ordinary differential equation describes uncollided beam survival. To test it with a detector, keep source output, distance, selected photon energy band and detector response fixed. Use a collimator and suitable detector acceptance to exclude scattering of light into the detector. Subtract background, measure an empty-path reference, and keep the detector in its linear operating range. A changing source distance adds geometric dilution and is not an attenuation measurement at fixed geometry.
For a stipulated example, take μ=0.40mm−1 and x=2.5mm. Transmission is e−1≃0.368. If the empty-path detector rate is 1010s−1, including background b=10s−1, the model predicts sample rate 10+1000e−1≃378s−1. Recover transmission as (R−b)/(R0​−b); the raw ratio R/R0​≃0.374 is biased. These chosen values are a calculation, not measurements of a particular material or a historical apparatus.
