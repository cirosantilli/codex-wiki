For a nearly flat interface, the extra surface energy in the small-slope approximation is (γ/2)∫∣∇h∣2dA. Each nonzero Fourier mode therefore has thermal variance ⟨∣hq​∣2⟩=kB​T/(Aγq2) in a convention with height reconstructed as the sum of its mode amplitudes. Summing over a two-dimensional annulus of wavevectors gives
⟨h2⟩=(2π)2A​∫qmin​qmax​​2πqdqAγq2kB​T​=2πγkB​T​logqmin​qmax​​.
(15)
The smallest wavevector is set by the lateral size or pinning, and the largest by the breakdown of the continuum description, commonly a molecular length. A fixed mean height removes the zero mode. Gravity supplies an additional restoring term and changes the long-wavelength cutoff when it is retained. Roughness here means a height variance; its square root is the root mean square amplitude, even when the mean height fluctuation is zero.
