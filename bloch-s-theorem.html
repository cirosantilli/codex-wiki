State counting in a Bloch band

Words: 68
Bloch's theorem OurBigBook.com
A crystal with N primitive unit cells has N distinct wavevectors in one Brillouin zone. For a rectangular periodic sample, ki​=2πli​/Li​, and the product of the numbers of allowed values along the three directions equals the number of cells. Each orbital-derived energy band therefore holds N states per spin orientation, or 2N with twofold spin degeneracy. This means two states per primitive cell per spin-degenerate band, independently of how many atoms the cell contains.

Band edges of a repulsive delta-comb potential

Words: 92
Kronig-Penney model OurBigBook.com
For V(x)=α∑j​δ(x−ja) with α>0, put P=mαa/ℏ2 and x=ka. The Floquet discriminant of the delta-comb Kronig-Penney model is f(x)=cosx+Psinx/x. Extended Bloch states require ∣f(x)∣≤1. Since f(0)=1+P>1, there is a positive lower band edge; a repulsive potential does not support negative-energy states in this energy convention.
The jth positive-energy band ends at x=jπ. Its lower edge lies in ((j−1)π,jπ) and solves f(x)=(−1)j−1. Nonzero band gaps do not by themselves imply an insulator: the band filling must also place the Fermi energy in a gap.

Electronic band structure

Words: 2k Articles: 52
Bloch's theorem OurBigBook.com
Electronic band structure is the collection of allowed electron energy bands and gaps in a periodic medium, usually represented as energy versus crystal momentum. An energy band is one branch of that structure.

k·p perturbation theory (Hk​=H0​+ℏk⋅p/m0​+ℏ2k2/(2m0​))

Electronic band structure OurBigBook.com
For a local periodic scalar potential, writing a Bloch wave as eik⋅runk​ gives the displayed cell-periodic Hamiltonian before additional spin-orbit terms. Momentum matrix elements mix zone-centre bands when k is varied. Perturbatively eliminating remote bands changes the effective mass; a multiband retained subspace describes degeneracy, nonparabolicity and coupled hole bands. Spin-orbit coupling and the chosen reference wavevector must be included consistently.

Interband transition

Words: 261 Articles: 4
Electronic band structure OurBigBook.com
An interband transition moves an electron between distinct energy bands. A direct optical transition approximately conserves crystal momentum because optical photon momentum is small compared with a reciprocal lattice vector. A phonon-assisted transition can supply the momentum difference needed near an indirect band gap. Transition energies alone do not fix their optical intensities, which also depend on matrix elements and occupations.

Indirect optical transition

Words: 55
Interband transition OurBigBook.com
An optical interband transition that requires another momentum source, commonly a phonon, to connect initial and final electronic wavevectors. Near an indirect band gap, phonon emission gives a threshold photon energy Eg​+ℏΩ, while phonon absorption gives Eg​−ℏΩ and requires an occupied phonon mode. The temperature dependence of the absorption therefore contains phonon-population information in addition to electronic occupations.

Direct optical transition

Words: 110 Articles: 1
Interband transition OurBigBook.com
A photon-induced interband transition that approximately preserves the electron's crystal wavevector because the photon momentum is small compared with reciprocal-lattice momenta. Its strength depends on the dipole matrix element and the joint density of states. A direct band gap permits such a transition between the band-edge states, but optical selection rules can still suppress it.
Parabolic-band optical energy sharing (Ke​=(Eγ​−Eg​)mh∗​/(me∗​+mh∗​))
Words: 63
Direct optical transition OurBigBook.com
A vertical direct transition at a common wavevector puts an electron above the conduction edge and leaves a hole below the valence edge. Their parabolic kinetic energies are inversely proportional to their effective masses. The excess photon energy is therefore split as Ke​/Kh​=mh∗​/me∗​, rather than equally unless the masses match. The initial valence electron energy is −Kh​ relative to its band maximum; the final conduction energy is Eg​+Ke​.

Interband absorption

Words: 48
Interband transition OurBigBook.com
Absorption in which a photon excites an electron between different energy bands, requiring an occupied initial state, an available final state and a nonzero transition matrix element. Indirect band-edge transitions also need momentum transfer, commonly through a phonon. Such absorption can prevent optical transmission even above a metal's free-carrier plasma edge.

Superlattice

Words: 54
Electronic band structure OurBigBook.com
A superlattice is a spatial periodic structure with a repeat length larger than an underlying atomic lattice period. Its additional reciprocal lattice vectors fold electronic bands into a smaller Brillouin zone, and allowed couplings can open small gaps at the folded crossings. The gap sizes depend on potential Fourier components and symmetry, not on the geometric period alone.

Flat band

Words: 36
Electronic band structure OurBigBook.com
A flat band has energy independent of wavevector, and hence zero group velocity. A nearly flat band has small dispersion and, near a parabolic extremum, large band effective mass. Coupling a flat band to a dispersive band can produce an avoided crossing and a hybridization gap.

H(k)=(Ea​(k)α​αEb​(k)​),E±​(k)=2Ea​+Eb​±(Ea​−Eb​)2+4α2​​.
(11)

Band hybridization OurBigBook.com
Mix a parabolic energy band ϵk​=ℏ2k2/(2m) with a perfectly flat band at Ed​>0 through momentum-independent V. At zero coupling suppose the two orbital sectors have equal populations, with the dispersive sector filled to k0​=2mEd​​/ℏ. At fixed total density and sufficiently low temperature, nonzero coupling fills only the monotonic lower hybridized branch, whose Fermi wavevector is kF​=21/3k0​, provided the available momentum-space states accommodate it. Its small slope at that radius gives the displayed ratio to vF(1)​=ℏk0​/m for ∣V∣≪Ed​. The result uses the singular perfectly flat limit and an empty upper band; it is not implied by Fermi-volume conservation in two hybridized electron bands for arbitrary pairs of dispersive bands. The zero-temperature limit must resolve the small hybridization energy scale.

Fermi-volume conservation in two hybridized electron bands

kF,−3​+kF,+3​=2k03​.
(12)

(13)

Hybridization gap

Words: 80
Band hybridization OurBigBook.com
A hybridization gap is a separation in electronic band structure caused by mixing orbital-derived states. For Ea​=Ak2, Eb​=E0​>0, A>0 and constant coupling α>0, the lower eigenvalue is everywhere below E0​ and tends to it as k→∞. The upper eigenvalue has minimum (E0​+E02​+4α2​)/2. The unbounded model therefore has an indirect gap of width (E02​+4α2​−E0​)/2, approximately α2/E0​ at weak coupling. This differs from the direct separation 2α at the bare crossing. A finite Brillouin zone bounds the lower band farther below E0​.

Impurity level in a crystal

Words: 180 Articles: 1
Energy band OurBigBook.com
A spatially localized electronic level produced by a defect or foreign atom in a crystalline potential. Such levels can lie in a host band gap, where no extended host state has the same energy, while impurity resonances can also occur inside an energy band. Dilute localized levels are nearly independent; increasing overlap can broaden them into an impurity band. The location and existence of a bound level depend on the impurity potential.
Occupation of an interacting localized electron level (EN​=EN​−μN)
Words: 109
Impurity level in a crystal OurBigBook.com
A localized orbital with two electron spin states has energies E0​=0, E1​=ϵ and E2​=2ϵ+U. Its zero-temperature equilibrium occupation minimizes EN​=EN​−μN, where μ is the chemical potential. For repulsion U>0, the occupation is successively 0,1,2 as μ crosses ϵ and ϵ+U. For attraction U<0, the singly occupied state is never the minimum: the occupation jumps from 0 to 2 at μ=ϵ+U/2. Exactly at a crossing the ground-state occupation is not unique.
At positive temperature, its grand canonical partition function is Ξ=1+2e−β(ϵ−μ)+e−β(2ϵ+U−2μ), where β=1/(kB​T). The expected value of the occupation follows by differentiating: ⟨N⟩=kB​T∂μ​lnΞ. The factor two counts the two singly occupied microstates, not an additional pair state.

The Wigner-Seitz cell of a reciprocal lattice centered at the origin. It contains wavevectors at least as close to zero as to any other reciprocal lattice point. Boundary sharing conventions do not change its area or volume.

Zone folding in an equal-mass diatomic chain

Putting M=m in the diatomic-chain dispersion relation with equal springs gives ω−​=2K/m​∣sin(kd/2)∣ and ω+​=2K/m​∣cos(kd/2)∣ in the reduced zone ∣k∣≤π/(2d). The branches touch at its edge. If the sites are actually identical, the true primitive unit cell has length d; the apparent optical phonon branch is the outer part of the monatomic-chain dispersion relation folded by an unnecessarily doubled cell. A gap does not appear merely from redrawing the zone.

Bragg point

Words: 29
Brillouin zone OurBigBook.com
A Bragg point is a crystal momentum at which two plane waves differing by a reciprocal lattice vector are degenerate. A periodic potential mixes the waves there, producing an avoided crossing and opening a band gap.

Brillouin zone of a two-dimensional triangular lattice

Words: 42
Brillouin zone OurBigBook.com
The reciprocal lattice of a two-dimensional triangular Bravais lattice is triangular, so its Wigner-Seitz cell is a regular hexagon. Opposite boundary points are identified modulo reciprocal-lattice translations, and the six corners fall into two inequivalent classes of three corners each, conventionally denoted K and K′.
