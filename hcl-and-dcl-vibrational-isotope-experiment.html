HCl and DCl vibrational isotope experiment

OurBigBook.com Words: 305
An actual infrared spectroscopy route is to compare gaseous hydrogen chloride containing ordinary hydrogen with its deuterium counterpart. A documented teaching experiment uses a stopcock-equipped gas cell with potassium bromide windows and a Nicolet Avatar instrument for Fourier transform spectroscopy. A nitrogen-purged flask and drying tube supply the mixture; the sample also contains sulfur dioxide. An empty sample-chamber background precedes the filled-cell scan. The absorbance trace and peak-picked spectroscopic wavenumbers are the readout. The analysis assigns and indexes 1H35Cl and 2H35Cl transitions separately and propagates transition uncertainties. The instructions distinguish resolvable chlorine isotope peaks from adequate precision for the intended parameter analysis. [ref]
A separate published teaching dataset reports a Nicolet Nexus 670 instrument, a 10cm gas cell at 20Torr, and chlorine isotope peaks resolved to baseline. These apparatus details describe that dataset, not the preceding experiment. [ref]
The inference connects several models. Fit correctly assigned transitions to separate molecular rotation from molecular vibration, then compare the inferred vibrational scales using isotope scaling of diatomic vibration. Agreement supports the approximate separation between a nearly unchanged chemical bond stiffness and a changed nuclear mass. It does not make a real molecule an exact classical spring: anharmonicity shifts the fundamental transition away from the local harmonic oscillator scale, and coupling to molecular rotation shifts individual lines.
For an independent test, calculate the reduced masses from the specified isotopes and compare the inferred values k=μ(2πcνharm​)2. Agreement within uncertainty tests the common-curvature assumption; choosing separate k values just to fit each frequency would not test it. The documented background addresses instrument and ambient contributions, but does not by itself subtract window absorption, identify contaminating species, or calibrate the spectroscopic wavenumber scale. Those require separate controls. Finite spectral resolution, blended peaks, incorrect isotope assignments, and departure from the harmonic approximation near a potential minimum limit the comparison. No numerical measured line positions are assumed here.
