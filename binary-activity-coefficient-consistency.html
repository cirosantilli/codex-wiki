Binary activity-coefficient consistency (xdlnγA​+(1−x)dlnγB​=0)

 Home Physics Branches of physics Statistical physics Thermodynamics Gibbs-Duhem equation
OurBigBook.com Words: 238
For a nonideal chemical solution, write its thermodynamic activities on a mole fraction basis as aA​=xγA​ and aB​=(1−x)γB​. Use composition-independent pure-component reference chemical potentials at the same temperature and pressure:
μA​=μA∗​+RTln(xγA​),μB​=μB∗​+RTln[(1−x)γB​].
(95)
The activity coefficients describe departure from the ideal solution under that reference convention.[ref]
At fixed temperature and pressure, insert these expressions into the Gibbs-Duhem equation. The ideal solution terms cancel because xdlnx+(1−x)dln(1−x)=dx−dx=0. What remains is
xdlnγA​+(1−x)dlnγB​=0.
(96)
Thus two independent fits to measured activity coefficients can each look smooth while failing to describe one possible Gibbs free energy. This is a necessary consistency condition; satisfying it alone does not establish experimental accuracy or thermodynamic stability.
For an original algebraic example, suppose at one fixed temperature and pressure that lnγA​=α(1−x)2, with dimensionless constant α. The constraint requires dlnγB​/dx=2αx. With the pure-B normalization γB​(0)=1, integration gives
lnγB​=αx2.
(97)
For α=1/2 and x=1/4, the derivatives of the two logarithms are −3/4 and 1/4; their weighted sum is −3/16+3/16=0. Choosing lnγB​=αx instead would give a weighted sum 3/16 at that composition, contradicting the Gibbs-Duhem equation. These are invented model functions, with no claim to fit a particular mixture.
The fixed-temperature, fixed-pressure restriction matters. Along a path with changing temperature or pressure, the general equation contains SdT−Vdp, and the reference chemical potentials and RT also change. Across phase coexistence, apply the relation separately within each smooth thermodynamic phase; do not differentiate a discontinuous switch between phases as if it were a single composition curve.
