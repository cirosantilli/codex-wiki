Quartic surface with axes and a circle as its zero set (FR​(x,y)=xy(x2+y2−R2),R>0)

 Home Mathematics Area of mathematics Analysis Critical point
OurBigBook.com Words: 167
The zero level set of FR​ is the union of the coordinate axes and the circle of radius R. The gradient is
∇FR​=(y(3x2+y2−R2), x(x2+3y2−R2))T.
(57)
Its nine stationary points are the origin, the four axis points at distance R, and the four points with x2=y2=R2/4. The factored gradient proves completeness: a zero coordinate gives the five axis cases; two nonzero coordinates give a linear system in x2,y2.
The Hessian matrix has diagonal entries 6xy and off-diagonal entries 3(x2+y2)−R2. At the origin its eigenvalues are ±R2; at the four unit-direction axis points they are ±2R2. These are saddle points of a scalar function. At the two interior points with equal coordinate signs, the eigenvalues are R2,2R2, giving strict local minima of value −R4/8. Opposite coordinate signs give the negative eigenvalues and strict local maxima of value R4/8.
On the zero circle, ∇FR​=2xy(x,y)T. Hence its direction is radial outward for xy>0, inward for xy<0, and zero at the four axis crossings. This simple geometric sign rule links the zero level set to the gradient sketch without requiring numerical differentiation.
