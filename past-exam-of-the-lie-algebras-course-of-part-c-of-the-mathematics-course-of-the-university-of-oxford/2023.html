Since the general linear Lie algebra gl1​(k) has zero Lie bracket, identify it with kz. A central extension of a Lie algebra is a short exact sequence
0⟶kzι​g​π​g⟶0
(1)
of Lie algebra homomorphisms with ι(kz) contained in the center of a Lie algebra Z(g​). Thus π is surjective, its kernel is precisely the indicated line, and that line commutes with all of g​.
Choose a linear map s:g→g​ with πs=id. Such a section exists by lifting a basis. Identify g​ with the direct sum of vector spaces g⊕kz by (x,tz)↦s(x)+tι(z). The difference [s(x),s(y)]−s([x,y]) belongs to the kernel of π, so there is a unique bilinear map α:g×g→k such that
[s(x),s(y)]−s([x,y])=α(x,y)ι(z).
(2)
It is an alternating bilinear form: α(x,x)=0, equivalently α(x,y)=−α(y,x) in characteristic zero. Taking the central component of the Jacobi identity gives the Lie algebra two-cocycle condition
α([x,y],w)+α([y,w],x)+α([w,x],y)=0(x,y,w∈g).
(3)
Conversely, any alternating bilinear form satisfying this identity defines a Lie algebra by
[(x,tz),(y,uz)]=([x,y],α(x,y)z).​
(4)
Indeed its Lie bracket is an alternating multilinear map and a bilinear map, and the two components of its Jacobi identity are respectively the Jacobi identity in g and the displayed Lie algebra two-cocycle condition. The line kz is central, and projection to g gives the required central extension of a Lie algebra.
For later use, replacing s by s′(x)=s(x)+t(x)ι(z), where t∈g∗ is in the dual space, changes the Lie algebra two-cocycle to
α′(x,y)=α(x,y)−t([x,y]).​
(5)
Thus changes of section add a Lie algebra two-coboundary, up to the conventional sign. A section that is a Lie algebra homomorphism has α′=0 and gives a direct sum of Lie algebras.

Choose a basis x,y of the two-dimensional abelian Lie algebra a, and lifts with the same names. Every alternating bilinear form is determined by c=α(x,y), and every such form is a Lie algebra two-cocycle because every Lie bracket in a is zero. Thus every central extension of a Lie algebra has a basis x,y,z with
[x,y]=cz,[x,z]=[y,z]=0.
(6)
If c=0, all Lie brackets vanish, so this is the direct sum of Lie algebras a⊕kz≅k3. If c=0, replace the central basis vector by z′=cz. Then [x,y]=z′, which is the Heisenberg Lie algebra h3​(k). This change of basis shows that every nonzero c gives the same Lie algebra isomorphism class.
These two Lie algebras cannot be isomorphic: their derived algebras have dimensions zero and one, respectively, and a Lie algebra isomorphism preserves the derived algebra. Moreover, the Heisenberg Lie algebra cannot be a direct sum of Lie algebras kz⊕a, since that direct sum of Lie algebras would have zero Lie bracket. Therefore
a≅k3orh3​(k).​
(7)
Here “up to isomorphism” means the Lie algebra isomorphism class of the middle Lie algebra, allowing a change of the identifications at the ends of the short exact sequence. If instead equivalence of central extensions of a Lie algebra must induce the identity on both kz and a, the parameter c cannot change: the formula in part (a) has t([x,y])=0. That stricter notion has one class for each c∈k, rather than two. This distinction explains the convention required by the question.

p∙q=z,q∙p=−z,

For a perfect Lie algebra s, every element is a sum of Lie brackets. The Adjoint representation satisfies ad[x,y]=[adx,ady], and the trace of a commutator is zero. Therefore
tr(adx)=0(x∈s).
(18)
Some adx must have a nonzero eigenvalue. Otherwise, over the algebraically closed field k, every adx would be a nilpotent linear map, and the Engel theorem would imply that s is a nilpotent Lie algebra. This contradicts perfection: the lower central series of a Lie algebra γ1​=s, γr+1​=[s,γr​] would equal s=0 at every step instead of terminating.
Choose such an x. Since [x,x]=0, zero is an eigenvalue of adx. Its trace is zero and its dimension is three, so the remaining eigenvalues are λ,−λ with λ=0. These three eigenvalues are distinct in characteristic zero, hence adx is diagonalizable. Put h=2x/λ, and choose nonzero eigenvectors e,f with
[h,e]=2e,[h,f]=−2f.
(19)
The zero eigenspace is kh, so h,e,f form a basis. Applying the Jacobi identity gives
[h,[e,f]]=[[h,e],f]+[e,[h,f]]=2[e,f]−2[e,f]=0.
(20)
Thus [e,f]=ch for some c∈k. If c=0, all Lie brackets of basis vectors lie in ⟨e,f⟩, contradicting [s,s]=s. Therefore c=0, and replacing f by f/c gives [e,f]=h.
We have constructed an sl2 triple that is a basis. The linear map to the sl2 Lie algebra sending
e⟼(00​10​),h⟼(10​0−1​),f⟼(01​00​)
(21)
preserves all Lie brackets of basis vectors and is bijective. Hence
s≅sl2​(k).​
(22)
This is the classification of three-dimensional perfect Lie algebras without appealing to a classification of all simple Lie algebras.

L=sl2​(k)⋉k2,[(X,u),(Y,v)]=([X,Y],Xv−Yu),
(23)
where k2 is the defining representation of the sl2 Lie algebra. To see directly that this is a Lie algebra, realize (X,u) as the block matrix (X0​u0​). These matrices are closed under the operator commutator, whose Jacobi identity holds in every associative algebra.
Using the standard sl2 triple e,h,f, its Lie brackets show [sl2​,sl2​]=sl2​: indeed h=[e,f], e=[h,e]/2, and f=−[h,f]/2. Also h=diag(1,−1) is invertible on k2. For any u∈k2, put v=h−1u; then

(24)

(25)

Thus conjugacy of Cartan subalgebras of the symplectic Lie algebra follows by explicitly matching their symplectic bases. If n=0, the Lie algebra is zero and the same conclusion holds with g the identity on the zero vector space.
