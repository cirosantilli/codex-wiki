Use the standard structural fact that sp(V) is a semisimple Lie algebra over an algebraically closed field of characteristic zero. If h is a Cartan subalgebra, part (a)(ii) gives Csp(V)​(h)=h, and h is an abelian Lie algebra. Part (c) therefore shows that its weight spaces occur as n pairs of one-dimensional spaces, with no zero weight space.
Choose representatives λ1​,…,λn​ of its nonzero weights of a representation. Pick pi​=0 in Vλi​​ and qi​=0 in V−λi​​, scaling qi​ so that ω(pi​,qi​)=1. Part (b) shows that all other pairings among different pairs are zero. Each individual weight space is one-dimensional and the bilinear form is alternating, so the ordered basis
B=(p1​,…,pn​,q1​,…,qn​)
(46)
is a symplectic basis. Every h∈h is a diagonal matrix in this basis, with entries λ1​(h),…,λn​(h),−λ1​(h),…,−λn​(h).
Let DB​ be the Lie subalgebra consisting of all diagonal matrices
DB​={diag(t1​,…,tn​,−t1​,…,−tn​):ti​∈k}.
(47)
Each such matrix lies in the symplectic Lie algebra and commutes with h. Hence
h⊆DB​⊆Csp(V)​(h)=h,
(48)
and h=DB​. In particular, every Cartan subalgebra has dimension n; the λi​ are a basis of h∗.
Carry out the same construction for h′, obtaining a symplectic basis B′=(p1′​,…,pn′​,q1′​,…,qn′​) with h′=DB′​. The unique linear map g sending pi​ to pi′​ and qi​ to qi′​ is invertible and preserves all pairings of basis vectors. By bilinearity, it preserves ω on all of V, so g belongs to the symplectic group Sp(V). The change of basis gives
ghg−1=gDB​g−1=DB′​=h′,g∈Sp(V).​
(49)
Thus conjugacy of Cartan subalgebras of the symplectic Lie algebra follows by explicitly matching their symplectic bases. If n=0, the Lie algebra is zero and the same conclusion holds with g the identity on the zero vector space.
