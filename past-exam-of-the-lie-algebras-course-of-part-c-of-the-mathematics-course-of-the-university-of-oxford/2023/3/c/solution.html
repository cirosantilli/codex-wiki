Choose representatives λ1​,…,λr​ as in part (b). We use the symplectic centralizer decomposition
Csp(V)​(a)≅sp(V0​)⊕i=1⨁r​gl(Vλi​​).​

To prove it, a linear map commuting with every a∈a must preserve every weight space: if v∈Vλ​, then a(Tv)=T(av)=λ(a)Tv. Conversely, preserving the weight spaces makes a linear map commute with the scalar action of every a on each space.
On V0​, the symplectic Lie algebra condition says exactly that the restriction belongs to sp(V0​). On Vλi​​⊕V−λi​​, use the perfect pairing from part (b) to identify V−λi​​ with the dual space Vλi​∗​. The action Ai​ on Vλi​​ can be arbitrary, but its action on V−λi​​ must be −Ai∗​, where Ai∗​ is the dual map. Indeed,
ω(Ai​v,w)+ω(v,Bi​w)=0for all v,w
(44)
determines Bi​=−Ai∗​ uniquely. There are no additional conditions between different pairs because ω vanishes between different pairs. Every choice of these blocks therefore gives an element of the centralizer of a Lie subalgebra. The block correspondence preserves Lie brackets, proving the claimed direct sum of Lie algebras.
The general linear Lie algebra glm​(k) is an abelian Lie algebra exactly when m≤1: for m≥2, the matrix units satisfy [E11​,E12​]=E12​=0. A nonzero symplectic vector space has a symplectic basis and contains a two-dimensional symplectic subspace. The symplectic Lie algebra acting on that plane, extended by zero on its symplectic orthogonal complement, contains the matrices H=diag(1,−1) and E=E12​ with [H,E]=2E=0. Thus sp(V0​) is an abelian Lie algebra exactly when V0​=0.
Since different summands in a direct sum of Lie algebras commute, we obtain the precise criterion
Csp(V)​(a) is abelian⟺V0​=0,dimVλ​=1(0=λ∈Ψ(a)).​​
