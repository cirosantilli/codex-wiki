For the Lie algebra two-cocycle αc​ of part (i), the central extension of a Lie algebra has a basis x,y,z and Lie brackets
[x,y]=y+cz,[x,z]=[y,z]=0.
(12)
Put y′=y+cz. This is still a basis together with x,z, and
[x,y′]=[x,y]+c[x,z]=y+cz=y′.
(13)
Consequently ⟨x,y′⟩ is a Lie subalgebra isomorphic to the affine Lie algebra of the line. It commutes with kz and has zero intersection with kz, so both summands are ideals of a Lie algebra and
s≅s⊕kz:exactly one class, which is trivial.​
(14)
This also holds for the stricter equivalence of central extensions of a Lie algebra. Indeed, choose the linear functional t with t(x)=0, t(y)=c. Since [x,y]=y in s, the change of section in part (a) gives αc′​(x,y)=c−t(y)=0. Thus every Lie algebra two-cocycle here is a Lie algebra two-coboundary, so H2(s,k)=0 in Lie algebra cohomology. The explicit map from the split central extension of a Lie algebra sends x to x, y to y+cz, and z to z; it fixes the central line and induces the identity on the quotient Lie algebra.
