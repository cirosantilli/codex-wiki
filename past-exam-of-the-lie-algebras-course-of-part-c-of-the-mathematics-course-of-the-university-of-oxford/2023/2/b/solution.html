Take the semidirect product of a Lie algebra and a module
L=sl2​(k)⋉k2,[(X,u),(Y,v)]=([X,Y],Xv−Yu),
(23)
where k2 is the defining representation of the sl2 Lie algebra. To see directly that this is a Lie algebra, realize (X,u) as the block matrix (X0​u0​). These matrices are closed under the operator commutator, whose Jacobi identity holds in every associative algebra.
Using the standard sl2 triple e,h,f, its Lie brackets show [sl2​,sl2​]=sl2​: indeed h=[e,f], e=[h,e]/2, and f=−[h,f]/2. Also h=diag(1,−1) is invertible on k2. For any u∈k2, put v=h−1u; then
[(h,0),(0,v)]=(0,u).
(24)
It follows that the derived algebra contains both sl2​⊕0 and 0⊕k2, so [L,L]=L: this is a perfect Lie algebra.
However, 0⊕k2 is a nonzero ideal of a Lie algebra with zero Lie bracket. It is therefore a solvable Lie algebra and is contained in the solvable radical of L. A semisimple Lie algebra has zero solvable radical, so
[L,L]=L,0=k2⊆radL,dimL=5.​
(25)
This illustrates the perfect semidirect product criterion: the Lie brackets internal to the first summand generate it, while its action generates the second summand.
