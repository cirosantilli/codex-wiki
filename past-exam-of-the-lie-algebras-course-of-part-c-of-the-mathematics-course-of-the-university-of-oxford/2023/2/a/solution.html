For a perfect Lie algebra s, every element is a sum of Lie brackets. The Adjoint representation satisfies ad[x,y]=[adx,ady], and the trace of a commutator is zero. Therefore
tr(adx)=0(x∈s).
(18)
Some adx must have a nonzero eigenvalue. Otherwise, over the algebraically closed field k, every adx would be a nilpotent linear map, and the Engel theorem would imply that s is a nilpotent Lie algebra. This contradicts perfection: the lower central series of a Lie algebra γ1​=s, γr+1​=[s,γr​] would equal s=0 at every step instead of terminating.
Choose such an x. Since [x,x]=0, zero is an eigenvalue of adx. Its trace is zero and its dimension is three, so the remaining eigenvalues are λ,−λ with λ=0. These three eigenvalues are distinct in characteristic zero, hence adx is diagonalizable. Put h=2x/λ, and choose nonzero eigenvectors e,f with
[h,e]=2e,[h,f]=−2f.
(19)
The zero eigenspace is kh, so h,e,f form a basis. Applying the Jacobi identity gives
[h,[e,f]]=[[h,e],f]+[e,[h,f]]=2[e,f]−2[e,f]=0.
(20)
Thus [e,f]=ch for some c∈k. If c=0, all Lie brackets of basis vectors lie in ⟨e,f⟩, contradicting [s,s]=s. Therefore c=0, and replacing f by f/c gives [e,f]=h.
We have constructed an sl2 triple that is a basis. The linear map to the sl2 Lie algebra sending
e⟼(00​10​),h⟼(10​0−1​),f⟼(01​00​)
(21)
preserves all Lie brackets of basis vectors and is bijective. Hence
s≅sl2​(k).​
(22)
This is the classification of three-dimensional perfect Lie algebras without appealing to a classification of all simple Lie algebras.
