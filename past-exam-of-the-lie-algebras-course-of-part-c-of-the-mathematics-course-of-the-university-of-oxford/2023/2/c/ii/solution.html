Use the corrected Lie algebra representation E=−e, H=h, F=f from part (i). A vector subspace is invariant under E,H,F exactly when it is invariant under the printed e,h,f, since changing the sign of a linear map does not change its invariant subspaces. Define

This is a nonzero proper submodule: H preserves each kvi​, F increases the index, E(v1​)=0, and E(vi​)=−(i−1)vi−1​∈W for i≥2.
We must rule out submodules consisting only of mixtures of weight vectors. Let U=0 be any submodule, and choose u=∑j∈S​aj​vj​∈U with S finite and every aj​=0. The eigenvalues −2j of H are distinct. For i∈S, the Lagrange interpolation polynomial
pi​(t)=∏j∈Sj=i​​−2i+2jt+2j​

satisfies pi​(H)u=ai​vi​. Since U is invariant under H, it is invariant under every polynomial in H, so vi​∈U.
If i=0, repeated applications of F give Fmv0​=m!vm​, and U=V. If i≥1, repeated applications of E give a nonzero multiple of v1​, because the successive coefficients −(i−1),…,−1 are nonzero in characteristic zero. Repeated applications of F to v1​ then give every vj​ with j≥1, so W⊆U.
Finally, if such a U contains any vector outside W, that vector has a nonzero coefficient of v0​; the same polynomial interpolation extracts v0​, forcing U=V. Hence the complete list of submodules is 0,W,V. In particular, W is a simple module. The quotient module V/W has basis v0​; Ev0​=Hv0​=0 and Fv0​=v1​=0. It is the one-dimensional trivial Lie algebra representation.
The module extension does not split. If V=W⊕U for a submodule U, then U would be a one-dimensional trivial Lie algebra representation, since it maps isomorphically to V/W. But kerH=kv0​ and Fv0​=v1​=0, so no such U exists. Thus

Here semisimple module means a direct sum of modules that are simple modules, equivalently a module in which every submodule has a complementary submodule. This is the nonsplit zero-weight Verma extension for sl2: rescaling to wi​=i!vi​ gives Fwi​=wi+1​ and Ewi​=i(1−i)wi−1​. Its infinite-dimensional vector space is outside the hypotheses of the Weyl complete reducibility theorem.
