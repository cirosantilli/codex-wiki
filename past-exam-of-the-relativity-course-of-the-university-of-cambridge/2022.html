a OurBigBook.com
A fundamental observer follows the coarse-grained cosmological matter, with fixed comoving coordinates. The cosmological principle asserts spatial homogeneity and cosmological isotropy on large scales for this family of observers. A moving observer generally sees a preferred direction, for example a Doppler dipole in the cosmic microwave background, even when the underlying cosmology is isotropic.
In an FLRW metric, these worldlines are orthogonal to homogeneous spatial slices. Synchronize their clocks to define cosmic time t, which is their proper time. Along a fixed spatial label, ds2=c2dt2, giving gtt​=c2. Orthogonality of the spatial coordinate directions to the observers' four-velocity gives gti​=0. Thus, in the +−−− metric signature,
ds2=c2dt2+gij​(t,x)dxidxj,​
(6)
where the spatial matrix gij​ is negative definite. Spatial homogeneity and cosmological isotropy further restrict it to −a(t)2γij​(x), with γ a constant-curvature spatial metric.

ft˙=k,r2ϕ˙​=h.​

For h=0, the effective potential vanishes at r=2μ, rises to a maximum, and then falls to zero as r→∞. Its derivative is Veff′​=h2(3μ−r)/r4, so the only circular null geodesic has
r=3μ,Veff​=54μ2h2​.​

Measure α in the orthonormal tetrad of a static observer at r>2μ. This specifies the local frame needed to define an angle. If E∞​ is the conserved photon energy, gravitational redshift gives Eloc​=E∞​/1−2μ/r​. The tangential local momentum is Eloc​sinα/c, so the impact parameter is
b=1−2μ/r​rsinα​.
(22)
Inside the photon sphere, an outward ray must have b<33​μ to cross the potential barrier. The Schwarzschild photon escape cone therefore has critical half-angle
αcrit​(r)=arcsin[r33​μ​1−r2μ​​],0≤α<αcrit​.​
(23)
The upper bound is strict: a critical ray only approaches the circular orbit. At r=3μ, the critical half-angle is π/2, and exactly tangential light is circular. As r↓2μ, the cone shrinks to zero opening. No static observer exists exactly on the Schwarzschild event horizon, and no future-directed photon emitted there escapes to exterior infinity; the zero-angle result is an exterior limit.

a OurBigBook.com
At any event on a massive observer's worldline, choose an orthonormal tetrad with timelike basis vector e(0)​=U/c. Its components obey g(e(a)​,e(b)​)=ηab​=diag(1,−1,−1,−1), so locally ds2=ηab​dX(a)dX(b): the Minkowski metric. A local inertial frame can also remove the Christoffel symbols at that event. Curvature cannot be removed on a finite region, and a frame following an accelerated observer need not remain inertial in its neighbourhood.
In general coordinates, Xμ(τ) labels the trajectory, while the four-velocity and four-acceleration are
Uμ=dτdXμ​,Aμ=dτDUμ​=dτdUμ​+Γνρμ​UνUρ.
(24)
Coordinate values Xμ are not themselves a vector under arbitrary nonlinear coordinate changes; their tangent U is. For fixed rest mass m, the four-momentum is Pμ=mUμ and the four-force is fμ=DPμ/dτ=mAμ. With the +−−− metric signature,
UμUμ​=c2,UμAμ​=0,AμAμ​=−a2≤0,​
(25)
where a is the proper acceleration. The middle identity follows by differentiating the constant velocity norm; in the instantaneous rest frame, U=(c,0) and A=(0,a), proving the last identity.
