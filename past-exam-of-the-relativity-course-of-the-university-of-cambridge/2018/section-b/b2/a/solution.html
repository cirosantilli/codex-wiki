Let a=du/dt denote coordinate acceleration and use Aμ for four-acceleration, to distinguish these two objects. Applying the chain rule to the Lorentz factor gives
dtdγu​​=−21​(1−c2u⋅u​)−3/2(−c22u⋅a​)=c2γu3​u⋅a​​.
(12)
Since dt/dτ=γu​, differentiation of the four-velocity Uμ=γu​(c,u) with respect to proper time yields
Aμ=γu​dtd​[γu​(c,u)]=(cγu4​u⋅a​, γu2​a+c2γu4​(u⋅a)u​)​.
(13)
This is also γu2​[γu2​(u⋅a)/c, a+γu2​(u⋅a)u/c2], as required.
The invariant Minkowski norm of the four-velocity is UμUμ​=c2. Differentiating this identity gives UμAμ​=0. In the instantaneous rest frame, Uμ=(c,0), so A0=0 and the spatial components equal the physically experienced proper acceleration α. Therefore α2=−AμAμ​ in the +−−− metric signature.
For a direct calculation, put q=u⋅a and expand the temporal and spatial squares:
α2​=​γu2​a+c2γu4​qu​​2−c2γu8​q2​=γu4​a2+c22γu6​q2​+c2γu8​q2​(c2u2​−1)=γu4​a⋅a+c2γu6​(u⋅a)2​​.​
(14)
The last step uses 1−u2/c2=γu−2​. This proper acceleration from coordinate velocity and acceleration also reads α2=γu6​a∥2​+γu4​a⊥2​, providing the familiar one-dimensional limit α=γu3​∣a∥​∣. Each expression has units m2s−4.
