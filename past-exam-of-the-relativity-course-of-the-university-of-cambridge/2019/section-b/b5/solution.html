Such timelike circular orbits require R>3μ. Their coordinate angular velocity satisfies Ω2=μc2/R3, and their proper clock rate is dτs​/dt=A/k=1−3μ/Rs​​.
For the static North-Pole clock, dτE​/dt=1−2μ/RE​​. Consecutive overhead passages occur one coordinate orbital period apart, and the repeated Earth-satellite geometry gives equal signal travel delays. The comparison of circular-orbit and static Schwarzschild clocks therefore gives
τE​τs​​=1−2μ/RE​1−3μ/Rs​​​.​

This includes both gravitational and orbital-motion effects; a separate Newtonian time-dilation correction must not be added again.
