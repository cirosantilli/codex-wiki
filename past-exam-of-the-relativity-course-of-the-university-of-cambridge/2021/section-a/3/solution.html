Interpret the orbit in the Schwarzschild metric as an unforced circular timelike geodesic and choose its plane as θ=π/2. Put f=1−2μ/r. The radial geodesic equation at constant radius uses the Christoffel symbols
Γrtt​=r2c2μf​,Γrϕϕ​=−fr,
(9)
and gives the Angular velocity of a circular Schwarzschild geodesic
Ω2=(dtdϕ​)2=r3c2μ​=r3GM​.
(10)
The proper-time normalization then yields
(dtdτ​)2=f−c2r2Ω2​=1−r3μ​.
(11)
Over one revolution, the Proper period of a circular Schwarzschild geodesic is therefore
Δτ=2πGMr3​​1−c2r3GM​​,r>3GM/c2.​
(12)
Both the gravitational and orbital kinematic contributions to time dilation are included. Circular timelike geodesics exist for r>3μ; they are stable for r>6μ and unstable for 3μ<r<6μ, with marginal stability at 6μ.
If “circular orbit” instead means a forced trajectory with an externally chosen angular velocity, the radius alone does not fix the answer. Its elapsed time is Δτ=Δt1−2μ/r−r2Ω2/c2​, or 2π1−2μ/r−r2Ω2/c2​/∣Ω∣ per revolution. The geodesic interpretation supplies the missing angular velocity.
