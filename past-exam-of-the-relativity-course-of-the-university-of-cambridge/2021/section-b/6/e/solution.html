Let a>0 be the constant proper acceleration away from the emitter and set R=c2/a. Take the signal emission to be simultaneous, in the initial inertial frame, with the ship being at rest and its clock reading τ=0. Place that initial ship event at T=0, X=R. Its hyperbolic motion is
Xs​(τ)=Rcosh(aτ/c),cTs​(τ)=Rsinh(aτ/c).
(46)
A photon emitted from X=R−d at T=0 towards the departing ship follows Xγ​=R−d+cT. At reception,
R[cosh(aτ/c)−sinh(aτ/c)]=R−d,e−aτ/c=1−c2ad​.
(47)
Therefore light reception by a uniformly accelerated observer occurs after
τrec​=ac​log1−ad/c21​,0≤d<ac2​.​
(48)
The same result follows in Rindler coordinates: the ship stays at x=R, the emitter starts at x=R−d, and the right-moving null ray has x=(R−d)eαt. The onboard lapse dτ=αRdt/c converts its coordinate catch-up time to the expression above.
If d=c2/a, the ray lies on the limiting null horizon and never intersects it at finite proper time; if d>c2/a, it never reaches the ship. The reception time diverges as the emitter approaches this Rindler horizon. For ad/c2≪1, τrec​=d/c+ad2/(2c3)+⋯, recovering the inertial light-travel time. A different emission time or initial relative velocity would require specifying that extra datum; the formula uses the initial-rest simultaneous-emission convention stated above.
