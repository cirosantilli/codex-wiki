Use the Christoffel symbol formula with its first index raised:
Γijk​=21​giℓ(∂j​gℓk​+∂k​gℓj​−∂ℓ​gjk​).
(1)
The diagonal metric tensor has grr​=e2χ and gθθ​=r2e2χ, with inverse components grr=e−2χ and gθθ=r−2e−2χ. Since ∂r​χ=χ′ and all angular derivatives vanish,
Γrrr​=χ′,Γrθθ​=−r−r2χ′,Γθθr​=Γθrθ​=r1​+χ′.​
(2)
For example, Γrθθ​=−21​e−2χ∂r​(r2e2χ). The other components vanish. The formula supplied in the paper gives the first-kind coefficients Γijk​; multiplication by the inverse metric tensor is needed for the requested second-kind coefficients. These polar coordinates are understood away from r=0.

For a scalar field, ∇μ​ϕ=∂μ​ϕ. Apply the product rule to the stress-energy tensor, using metric compatibility to pass the covariant derivative through gμν:
∇μ​Tμν=​(□ϕ)∇νϕ+∇μϕ∇μ​∇νϕ−∇σ​ϕ∇ν∇σϕ+m2ϕ∇νϕ.​
(3)
The middle terms cancel because the Levi-Civita connection gives a symmetric Hessian of a scalar field. Thus, with the sign conventions of this question, the scalar stress-energy divergence identity becomes
∇μ​Tμν=(□ϕ+m2ϕ)∇νϕ=0.​
(4)
The last equality is the Klein-Gordon equation. This stress-energy conservation follows from the field equation and metric compatibility; it does not require imposing the Einstein field equations.

The three classic tests probe orbital motion, the propagation of light, and clock rates in a gravitational field.
Schwarzschild perihelion precession. For an orbit with semi-major axis a and orbital eccentricity e, the leading advance per revolution is
Δϖ=a(1−e2)c26πGM​.​
(9)
The relevant observation for Mercury is the residual advance after the much larger ordinary perturbations have been included, approximately 43 arcseconds per century.[ref] An orbital fit must account for the other planets and reference-frame effects before interpreting that residual as a relativistic contribution.
Schwarzschild light deflection. A ray with large impact parameter b passing a nearly spherical mass is deflected through
α≃bc24GM​.​
(10)
At the Sun's limb this is about 1.75 arcseconds. The Eddington experiment used the 1919 solar eclipse to photograph background stars near the obscured Sun, then compared their positions with reference photographs. The measured effect is an apparent outward displacement of the background stars, not a direct photograph of a curved light path. Telescope focus, plate scale and atmospheric effects must be controlled when extracting such small shifts.[ref]
Gravitational redshift. For a photon emitted lower in a weak static field and received a height H above it,
νem​νrec​−νem​​≃−c2gH​.​
(11)
The Pound-Rebka experiment used the narrow recoil-free nuclear resonance supplied by the Mössbauer effect. A controlled source motion supplies a small Doppler shift that compensates the gravitational shift, allowing a change in resonant absorption to reveal it across a laboratory tower.[ref] The leading weak-field redshift follows from the equivalence principle and therefore does not, by itself, distinguish every competing metric theory of gravity.

Nearby freely falling particles can accelerate relative to one another even though each follows a geodesic. A local inertial frame removes the common gravitational acceleration at one event; it cannot remove the variation across an extended laboratory. That variation is a tidal force, measured covariantly by geodesic deviation.
Let uμ be the reference four-velocity and ξμ an infinitesimal separation to a neighboring geodesic, with matching affine parameters. With the convention [∇β​,∇γ​]Vμ=Rμνβγ​Vν,
Dτ2D2ξμ​=Rμαβγ​uαuβξγ.​
(12)
This relates measured relative acceleration to the Riemann curvature tensor. Reversing the curvature-tensor convention reverses the displayed curvature sign, without changing the physical relative motion.
In a small freely falling frame outside a spherical mass, the weak-field tidal tensor has radial and transverse components proportional to 2,−1,−1. Thus
ξ¨​r​=r32GM​ξr​,ξ¨​⊥​=−r3GM​ξ⊥​.​
(13)
An initially spherical cloud stretches radially and compresses in both transverse directions. Two separated test masses, with their changing separation measured by light, form the basic idea of a gravity gradiometer; the common falling motion drops out. A uniform apparent acceleration in an accelerated laboratory can occur even in flat Minkowski spacetime, so it is the separation-dependent tidal response that diagnoses curvature.

Write rs​=2GM/c2 and f=1−rs​/r. The Schwarzschild tortoise coordinate satisfies dr∗​/dr=1/f and can be chosen as r∗​=r+rs​ln∣r/rs​−1∣. Define the advanced time v=t+r∗​/c. Substituting dt=dv−dr/(cf) into the Schwarzschild metric gives the ingoing Eddington-Finkelstein coordinates form
ds2=fc2dv2−2cdvdr−r2dΩ2.​
(14)
The coefficients are regular at r=rs​ and the radial-block determinant is −c2, so the exterior Schwarzschild-time divergence is a coordinate singularity at the future event horizon. The physical curvature singularity at r=0 remains.
The two families of radial null trajectories in ingoing Eddington-Finkelstein coordinates obey
v=constant(ingoing),dvdr​=2c​(1−rrs​​)(outgoing).​
(15)
Outside the event horizon, the outgoing family increases r; at the horizon it generates the horizon itself; inside, even the outgoing future-directed family decreases r. An infaller crosses the future horizon at finite v and finite proper time. The chart makes this causal geometry visible without mistaking Schwarzschild coordinate time for the infaller's clock.

Let xμ(τ) denote the spacetime coordinates, with dots meaning differentiation with respect to an affine parameter; for a massive particle take proper time τ. The metric tensor determines the quadratic geodesic Lagrangian L=gμν​x˙μx˙ν. The Euler-Lagrange equations are
dτd​∂x˙μ∂L​−∂xμ∂L​=0.
(16)
Symmetry of the metric tensor gives ∂L/∂x˙μ=2gμν​x˙ν. Hence the covariant four-momentum is pμ​=mgμν​x˙ν=(m/2)∂L/∂x˙μ. If xμ is a cyclic coordinate, its Euler-Lagrange equation immediately gives p˙​μ​=0.
For the rotating metric, t,ϕ,z are all cyclic. Thus
pt​pϕ​​=m[(c2−ω2r2)t˙−ωr2ϕ˙​],=−mr2(ϕ˙​+ωt˙),pz​=−mz˙.​​
(17)
Combining the first two equations cancels their r dependence:
t˙=mc2pt​−ωpϕ​​=constant.​
(18)
The radial Euler-Lagrange equation is −2r¨=−2r(ϕ˙​+ωt˙)2, so
r¨=r(ϕ˙​+ωt˙)2=m2r3pϕ2​​.​
(19)
This is the geodesic equation in a uniformly rotating Minkowski chart. With inertial angle Φ=ϕ+ωt, −pϕ​ is the inertial axial angular momentum. The outward radial-coordinate acceleration expresses the familiar centrifugal term associated with angular motion: the underlying free trajectory is a straight line in Minkowski spacetime. It does not imply an external radial force. Holding a particle at rest in the rotating reference frame instead requires a real inward force.

Let the inertial frame S′ move at speed v in the positive x direction relative to S, with parallel axes and coincident origins at zero time. The inverse Lorentz transformation gives
dt=γv​(dt′+vdx′/c2),dx=γv​(dx′+vdt′),dy=dy′,dz=dz′.
(20)
Here γv​=(1−v2/c2)−1/2 and u′=dx′/dt′ is the particle's velocity in S′. Dividing spatial differentials by dt gives the relativistic velocity addition law
ux​=1+vux′​/c2ux′​+v​,uy​=γv​(1+vux′​/c2)uy′​​,uz​=γv​(1+vux′​/c2)uz′​​.​
(21)
For the collision, set c=1 during the algebra. Let the incident projectile have energy E>m1​ and momentum p=E2−m12​​. The target is initially at rest. The squared invariant mass, boost speed and Lorentz factor of the center-of-momentum frame are
s=m12​+m22​+2m2​E,V=E+m2​p​,Γ=s​E+m2​​.
(22)
In that frame the projectile has p∗​=m2​p/s​ and E∗​=(m12​+m2​E)/s​. An elastic collision changes the direction of its momentum, through an angle φ, while preserving these magnitudes. Transforming the outgoing four-momentum back to the laboratory gives
tanθ=Γ(cosφ+B)sinφ​,B=p∗​VE∗​​=m2​(E+m2​)m12​+m2​E​>1.
(23)
The maximum occurs at cosφ=−1/B. Since
Γ2(B2−1)=m22​m12​−m22​​,
(24)
the maximum laboratory deflection in relativistic elastic scattering is
tanθmax​=m12​−m22​​m2​​,sinθ≤sinθmax​=m1​m2​​.​
(25)
The printed strict inequality needs correction: equality is kinematically attainable. For example, in c=1 units take m1​=2, m2​=1, and incident E=3. A projectile scattered through 30∘ with final energy 16/7 has momentum (35​/7,15​/7). The target then has energy 12/7 and momentum (45​/7,−15​/7); both outgoing particles satisfy their mass shells and conserve total four-momentum. This gives sinθ=1/2=m2​/m1​ exactly. The inequality is strict only away from the maximizing direction.

Curvature contractions. Use the index contraction stipulated in the question. In three dimensions,
Rjk​=gilK(gik​gjl​−gil​gjk​)=K(gjk​−3gjk​)=−2Kgjk​.
(26)
The same contraction in four dimensions gives
Rβγ​=−3Λ​(gβγ​−4gβγ​)=Λgβγ​.
(27)
Then the Ricci scalar is R=4Λ and the Einstein tensor is Gμν​=−Λgμν​. Thus Rμν​=Λgμν​ is precisely the vacuum Einstein field equations Gμν​+Λgμν​=0.
The static solution. For A=1+λ2r2, A′=2λ2r and A′′=2λ2. The given Ricci tensor components become
R00​=−3λ2A,R11​=A3λ2​,R22​=3λ2r2,R33​=3λ2r2sin2θ.
(28)
Each equals −3λ2 times the corresponding metric tensor component. Therefore
Λ=−3λ2.​
(29)
This is Anti-de Sitter spacetime in static coordinates on anti-de Sitter spacetime, with curvature radius 1/λ. The overall metric signature is opposite to the convention used in some descriptions of those coordinates; the geodesic constants below use the question's (+,−,−,−) convention.
Conserved quantities and radial motion. In the equatorial plane, the geodesic Lagrangian is L=At˙2−r˙2/A−r2ϕ˙​2. Its cyclic coordinates give
k=At˙,h=r2ϕ˙​.​
(30)
For rest mass m, mk=pt​ is the conserved Killing energy and mh=−pϕ​ is the axial angular momentum. Timelike normalization L=1 gives
r˙2=k2−A(1+r2h2​)=k2−1−λ2h2−λ2r2−r2h2​.​
(31)
For purely radial motion h=0, differentiating away from a turning point gives r¨=−λ2r, and continuity extends that equation through the turning point. An outward launch from the origin at τ=0 has
r(τ)=λk2−1​​sin(λτ),a=λk2−1​​,b=λ.​
(32)
This is the radial timelike geodesic in anti-de Sitter spacetime. Its first return to the origin is
τreturn​=λπ​,​
(33)
independent of the outward launch speed for every nontrivial launch k>1. The coordinate r is nonnegative, so this sine expression describes the excursion up to that return; subsequent passage through the origin requires changing the radial direction or using a signed diameter coordinate. The speed changes the amplitude, not the harmonic frequency.
Circular periods. The effective radial potential is Veff​=A(1+h2/r2). For a circle at r0​, Veff′​=0 gives h2=λ2r04​, and normalization gives k=A(r0​). Thus t˙=1 and ϕ˙​=±λ. A stable circular timelike geodesic in anti-de Sitter spacetime therefore has
Δt=Δτ=λ2π​,​
(34)
independently of radius. This is the same isochronous behavior as the central harmonic radial equation. A static clock placed at the orbit's radius instead measures 1+λ2r02​​Δt, which does depend on radius; it is a different clock from the orbiting particle's proper time.
