In general relativity, light follows null geodesics. A weak gravitational field outside an isolated spherical mass gives the Schwarzschild light deflection
α=bc24GM​​
(4)
for impact parameter b≫GM/c2. At the solar limb this is approximately 1.75 arcseconds. A Newtonian corpuscular calculation gives half this value; the full result depends on spatial curvature as well as the gravitational time dilation. The parametrized post-Newtonian formalism predicts α=2(1+γPPN​)GM/(bc2), with γPPN​=1 in general relativity.
The Eddington experiment compared photographic star positions near the eclipsed Sun with reference exposures taken without the Sun in that field. Rays bend toward the Sun, but their backward extrapolations place the apparent stellar images farther from it. Plate scale, focus changes, atmospheric refraction and comparison-field alignment must be separated from a radial displacement proportional to 1/b. The original report discusses the instruments, plate reductions and competing predicted deflections; the test is a quantitative comparison with uncertainty, not a unique proof of a gravitational theory.[ref]
radio interferometry permits an independent test without an eclipse. A 2005 Very Long Baseline Array experiment observed four compact sources at 43, 23 and 15 GHz. Differential astrometry suppressed common antenna errors, while a fit p(ν)=p0​+Iν−2 separated refraction in the solar corona from achromatic gravitational deflection. Tropospheric errors and radio-source structure also required calibration. The reported γPPN​=0.9998±0.0003 was consistent with general relativity.[ref]
