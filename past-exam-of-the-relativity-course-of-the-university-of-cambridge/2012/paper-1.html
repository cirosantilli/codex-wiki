In the rest frame, conservation of energy would require Eγ​=mc2, whereas conservation of momentum would require the photon momentum to vanish. These contradict ∣pγ​∣=Eγ​/c. More generally, four-momentum conservation would equate the initial massive four-momentum to a null four-momentum, although their Minkowski norms differ:
Pμ​Pμ=m2c2>0,pγμ​pγμ​=0.
(1)
An isolated massive particle cannot decay into one photon in any inertial frame. Directly, a laboratory speed u<c gives E=γmc2 and ∣p∣=γmu; matching a photon would require E=c∣p∣, hence u=c. Recoil against another object could change the kinematics, but would introduce an additional participant.

Write the four-velocity Uμ=γ(c,v) and four-acceleration Aμ=dUμ/dτ. Since Uμ​Aμ=0, spatial orthogonality U⋅A=0 implies U0A0=0. Thus A0=cdγ/dτ=0 and the speed is constant. If “acceleration” is read as the laboratory three-acceleration instead, γv⋅dv/dt=0 immediately gives the same conclusion.
For four-acceleration in uniform circular motion, the speed is r∣ω∣<c, so γ=(1−r2ω2/c2)−1/2 is constant. Differentiating the four-velocity using d/dτ=γd/dt gives
Aμ=(0,−γ2rω2cosωt,−γ2rω2sinωt,0).​
(2)
The proper acceleration is −Aμ​Aμ​=γ2rω2. The vanishing time component reflects constant energy; the spatial components describe the changing momentum direction.

The geodesics of the product-weighted diagonal plane metric follow from the geodesic Lagrangian L=(y2x˙2+x2y˙​2)/2. With dots denoting an affine parameter derivative, its Euler-Lagrange equations give
y2x¨+2yy˙​x˙−xy˙​2x2y¨​+2xx˙y˙​−yx˙2​=0,=0.​​
(3)
Substituting y=mx with constant nonzero m reduces both equations to xx¨+x˙2=0. Thus xx˙ is constant, and a suitable affine parametrization exists; explicitly, x2=C1​λ+C2​ on a segment of fixed sign.
These straight rays are geodesics only in the regular domain xy=0. The metric tensor determinant is x2y2. On the axes, including the origin, this is a degenerate metric tensor: the calculation does not establish a regular geodesic continuing through the origin. The slope-zero ray is outside the regular domain. Geometrically, on each quadrant the change ρ=∣xy∣/2​, ϑ=log∣y/x∣ gives ds2=dρ2+ρ2dϑ2. A fixed slope has constant ϑ and is therefore locally a radial geodesic of a flat metric tensor.

a OurBigBook.com
In general relativity, light follows null geodesics. A weak gravitational field outside an isolated spherical mass gives the Schwarzschild light deflection
α=bc24GM​​
(4)
for impact parameter b≫GM/c2. At the solar limb this is approximately 1.75 arcseconds. A Newtonian corpuscular calculation gives half this value; the full result depends on spatial curvature as well as the gravitational time dilation. The parametrized post-Newtonian formalism predicts α=2(1+γPPN​)GM/(bc2), with γPPN​=1 in general relativity.
The Eddington experiment compared photographic star positions near the eclipsed Sun with reference exposures taken without the Sun in that field. Rays bend toward the Sun, but their backward extrapolations place the apparent stellar images farther from it. Plate scale, focus changes, atmospheric refraction and comparison-field alignment must be separated from a radial displacement proportional to 1/b. The original report discusses the instruments, plate reductions and competing predicted deflections; the test is a quantitative comparison with uncertainty, not a unique proof of a gravitational theory.[ref]
radio interferometry permits an independent test without an eclipse. A 2005 Very Long Baseline Array experiment observed four compact sources at 43, 23 and 15 GHz. Differential astrometry suppressed common antenna errors, while a fit p(ν)=p0​+Iν−2 separated refraction in the solar corona from achromatic gravitational deflection. Tropospheric errors and radio-source structure also required calibration. The reported γPPN​=0.9998±0.0003 was consistent with general relativity.[ref]

tidal forces measure differences in gravitational acceleration across an extended region. Neighboring freely falling particles each have zero four-acceleration, but their separation need not accelerate uniformly. Let uμ be the tangent to a reference timelike geodesic and ξμ its infinitesimal separation from a neighboring one, chosen from an affinely parametrized geodesic congruence. Using the Riemann curvature tensor convention specified in Solution 3,
Dτ2D2ξα​=−Rαβγδ​uβuγξδ.
(5)
Changing the overall curvature convention changes this displayed sign. The covariant derivatives compare vectors by parallel transport, avoiding a coordinate-dependent subtraction of accelerations.
In the Newtonian limit, expand the force field at two nearby points:
ξ¨​i​=−∂i​∂j​Φξj​.
(6)
For Φ=−GM/r, a local orthonormal frame aligned with the radius gives
ξ¨​=r3GM​diag(2,−1,−1)ξ.​
(7)
There is radial stretching and transverse compression; the trace vanishes outside the mass. Two freely falling test masses and a measurement of their changing separation implement gravity gradiometry. Their common acceleration drops out, while a relative acceleration proportional to separation remains. A local inertial frame removes Christoffel symbols at a point, but cannot remove nonzero curvature over a finite region.

With Cartesian Minkowski spacetime coordinates xμ=(ct,x,y,z), introduce the four-current Jμ=(cρ,J) and electromagnetic four-potential Aμ=(ϕ/c,A). The antisymmetric electromagnetic field tensor Fμν=∂μAν−∂νAμ has components F0i=−Ei​/c and Fij=−ϵijkBk​. Maxwell equations become
∂μ​Fμν=μ0​Jν,∂μ​Fμν=0,​Fμν=21​ϵμνρσFρσ​,ϵ0123=+1.
(8)
The time and space components reproduce Gauss's law and the Ampère-Maxwell equation; the dual equation gives absence of magnetic monopoles and Faraday's law of induction. Antisymmetry gives charge conservation ∂ν​Jν=0. The relativistic Lorentz force is dpμ/dτ=qFμνuν​. Every displayed equation transforms tensorially under a Lorentz transformation; the electric field and magnetic field are different components of the same object.
Two independent algebraic electromagnetic field invariants are
Fμν​Fμν=2(B2−E2/c2),Fμν​Fμν=−4E⋅B/c.​
(9)
The second is a pseudoscalar: it is invariant under proper Lorentz transformations but changes sign under spatial reflection. For perpendicular fields, the sign of the first distinguishes a frame with purely magnetic field from one with purely electric field. Both invariants vanish for a plane electromagnetic wave with E⊥B and E=cB, although the field itself is nonzero.

The Einstein tensor and its contractions are
Gμν​=Rμν​−21​gμν​R,Rμν​=Rαμαν​,R=gμνRμν​.
(10)
To match the supplied Ricci tensor components, use the Riemann curvature tensor convention
Rρσμν​=∂ν​Γμσρ​−∂μ​Γνσρ​+Γνλρ​Γμσλ​−Γμλρ​Γνσλ​.
(11)
This convention also fixes the sign used for geodesic deviation above. In vacuum, tracing the Einstein field equations gives −R+4Λ=0. Substitution therefore yields Rμν​=Λgμν​.
Differentiate the angular field equation to obtain
−2A′−rA′′=2Λr,rA′​+2A′′​=−Λ.
(12)
Consequently R00​=ΛA=Λg00​ and R11​=−Λ/A=Λg11​. The other angular component follows from R33​=sin2θR22​. Thus the single angular equation implies all the displayed vacuum equations wherever this coordinate chart is regular.
Integrating (rA)′=1−Λr2 gives A=1+C/r−Λr2/3. Match gtt​≃1+2Φ with the Newtonian gravitational potential Φ=−GM/r in the weak-field overlap region to identify C=−2GM. Hence the Schwarzschild-de Sitter spacetime has
A(r)=1−r2GM​−3Λr2​(c=1).​
(13)
Negative Λ instead gives the Schwarzschild--anti-de Sitter black hole case. This matching fixes the normalization locally; a spacetime with nonzero Λ is not asymptotically flat.
For timelike geodesics in the orbital plane, take proper time as parameter and use L=(At˙2−r˙2/A−r2ϕ˙​2)/2. The cyclic coordinates give At˙=k and r2ϕ˙​=h; the angular canonical momentum is −h in this signature. The four-velocity normalization 2L=1 then gives
r˙2=k2−A(r)(1+r2h2​).​
(14)
Here k is conserved Killing energy per unit rest mass in the chosen time normalization, and h is conserved angular momentum per unit rest mass. There is no general interpretation of k as energy measured at an asymptotically flat infinity when Λ=0.
For radial motion h=0, differentiation gives 2r˙r¨=−A′r˙. The radial Euler-Lagrange equation extends the resulting expression through a turning point where division by r˙ would fail. Thus
r¨=−2A′​=−r2GM​+3Λr​(c=1).​
(15)
This is the proper-time second derivative of the areal radius, not a nonzero four-acceleration of the freely falling particle. Positive Λ contributes an outward term; negative Λ reinforces inward attraction. Restoring units in the slow-motion, weak-field Newtonian limit gives
dt2d2r​≃−r2GM​+3Λc2r​,Φ=−rGM​−6Λc2r2​.​
(16)
The extra term describes the gravitational effect of a cosmological constant, not drag or a contact force. The approximation requires GM/(rc2)≪1, ∣Λ∣r2≪1 and speed much less than c.

Put f=1−2μ/r and let dots now denote an affine parameter derivative. The null geodesic lagrangian in the orbital plane is L=(fc2t˙2−r˙2/f−r2ϕ˙​2)/2. The time and azimuthal Euler-Lagrange equations integrate once; the null normalization gives
ft˙=k,r2ϕ˙​=h,fc2t˙2−r˙2/f−r2ϕ˙​2=0.
(17)
For completeness, the differential radial geodesic equation is
r¨+2c2ff′​t˙2−2ff′​r˙2−frϕ˙​2=0;
(18)
the remaining differential equations are t¨+(f′/f)t˙r˙=0 and ϕ¨​+2r˙ϕ˙​/r=0. Spherical symmetry permits the equatorial choice.
Eliminating t˙,ϕ˙​ yields r˙2+h2f/r2=c2k2. For h=0, divide by h2:
h2r˙2​+Veff​(r)=b21​,Veff​=r21​−r32μ​,b=c∣k∣∣h∣​.​
(19)
The impact parameter is unchanged by a common rescaling of the affine parameter. At infinity it is c times angular momentum divided by energy. Radial photons have h=0 and obey the undivided equation r˙2=c2k2.
The effective potential is zero at r=2μ, positive outside, and tends to zero as r→∞. Its derivative is Veff′​=2(3μ−r)/r4. Thus
rcirc​=3μ,Veff​(3μ)=27μ21​,bcrit​=33​μ.​
(20)
The circular null geodesic lies at a maximum, since Veff′′​(3μ)=−2/(81μ4)<0. It is unstable: a small radial displacement away from the maximum permits motion away from the orbit. For incoming photons from infinity in the exterior of a black hole, an outer turning point exists when b>bcrit​; for b<bcrit​ the photon can cross the event horizon. For a material central body, the vacuum circular orbit is relevant only if 3μ lies outside its surface.
For the new coordinates, set q=2μ/r, so f=1−q. The geodesic Lagrangian is L′=[fc2t˙′2−2qct˙′r˙−(1+q)r˙2−r2ϕ˙​2]/2. Cyclic-coordinate conservation and the null condition give the required analogous equations:
ft˙′−cq​r˙fc2t˙′2−2qct˙′r˙−(1+q)r˙2−r2ϕ˙​2​=k,=0.​r2ϕ˙​​=h,​​
(21)
Here k denotes the same conserved time-translation quantity as before. The transformation is t′=t+(2μ/c)log∣r/(2μ)−1∣. In particular, the displayed time coordinate belongs to Ingoing Kerr-Schild coordinates for Schwarzschild spacetime; the usual advanced null coordinate of ingoing Eddington-Finkelstein coordinates is v=t′+r/c. Using a metric tensor with a vanishing dr2 coefficient while retaining the name t′ would therefore solve a different coordinate representation.
An elimination that stays finite at the horizon is especially simple:
c2k2−r˙2​=(fct˙′−qr˙)2−r˙2=f[fc2t˙′2−2qct˙′r˙−(1+q)r˙2]=fr2ϕ˙​2=r2fh2​.​
(22)
The middle equality uses q2+f(1+q)=1. Hence the radial energy equation and Veff​(r) are unchanged, including the horizon limit. The radial metric tensor block has determinant −c2 and is regular at r=2μ. Its radial null directions are dr/(cdt′)=−1 and dr/(cdt′)=f/(1+q), as shown in the original schematic.
This coordinate invariance of the Schwarzschild photon radial potential follows because the transformation changes the time slicing but preserves the areal radius and the stationary Killing vector field ∂t′​=∂t​. Consequently the conserved Killing energy, angular momentum and impact parameter are the same. photon capture, the circular orbit and the radial turning points cannot depend on coordinate labels; coordinate velocities can.
