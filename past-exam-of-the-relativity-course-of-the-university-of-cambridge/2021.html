Put κ=8πG/c4 and T=Tν​ν. The contracted Bianchi identity and metric compatibility give
(21​−α)∂μ​R=−κ∇ν​Tμ​ν.
(6)
Taking the trace of the field equation in four dimensions gives (1−4α)R=−κT. For α=1/4, eliminate R:
∇ν​Tμ​ν=1−4α21​−α​∂μ​T.​
(7)
Ordinary covariantly coupled matter obeys stress-energy conservation, ∇ν​Tμ​ν=0. Its trace need not be constant, so the trace coefficient fixed by stress-energy conservation is
α=21​.​
(8)
This restores the Einstein tensor and is the only coefficient compatible with arbitrary conventional matter without adding a new constraint or changing the matter conservation law. Other coefficients can work for specially restricted sources with constant trace, but do not give the same universal matter coupling.
The excluded case α=1/4 must be treated before dividing: its trace equation requires T=0. If stress-energy is also conserved, its divergence equation requires ∂μ​R=0. It can describe a restricted traceless-source sector with constant scalar curvature, not general matter such as ordinary dust. In particular it is not the general trace-free Einstein equation with source Tμν​−gμν​T/4, which is a different equation.

Interpret the orbit in the Schwarzschild metric as an unforced circular timelike geodesic and choose its plane as θ=π/2. Put f=1−2μ/r. The radial geodesic equation at constant radius uses the Christoffel symbols
Γrtt​=r2c2μf​,Γrϕϕ​=−fr,
(9)
and gives the Angular velocity of a circular Schwarzschild geodesic
Ω2=(dtdϕ​)2=r3c2μ​=r3GM​.
(10)
The proper-time normalization then yields
(dtdτ​)2=f−c2r2Ω2​=1−r3μ​.
(11)

Δτ=2πGMr3​​1−c2r3GM​​,r>3GM/c2.​
(12)
Both the gravitational and orbital kinematic contributions to time dilation are included. Circular timelike geodesics exist for r>3μ; they are stable for r>6μ and unstable for 3μ<r<6μ, with marginal stability at 6μ.

For the supplied plus polarization, the perturbation is traceless, so hμν​=ϕμν​. At a fixed longitudinal coordinate z0​, put H(t)=h⊕​cos[ω(t−z0​)]. With the +−−− convention chosen above, the transverse spatial metric measuring positive squared lengths is

Take α>0 and work in a connected patch with x=0. Setting the line element to zero gives
dtdx​=±αx,x(t)=Ce±αt,C=0.​
(35)
These are the two families of Rindler null geodesics. They remain on the same side of x=0 and approach it only at infinite coordinate time. x=0 itself is not an additional geodesic within this chart, since the metric is degenerate there.

More explicitly, the transformation in part (d) makes these paths straight null lines. The coordinate t is generally not an affine parameter.

Put rs​=2μ and f(r)=1−rs​/r. Radial null geodesics of the Schwarzschild metric obey
dtdr​=±cf(r),ct=±[r+rs​log​rs​r​−1​]+constant.
(36)
Near the horizon, δ=r−rs​ satisfies
δ(t)≃Ce±ct/rs​.​
(37)
C>0 describes the exterior and C<0 the interior. The exponential approach in Schwarzschild coordinate time resembles part (a), but δ is not a proper radial distance.
Outside, set ρ=2rs​δ​ and retain the leading near-horizon terms. The radial metric becomes the Rindler limit of a Schwarzschild horizon:
ds2≃(2rs​cρ​)2dt2−dρ2.
(38)
It agrees with part (a) for x=ρ, α=c/(2rs​). Accordingly ρ∝e±ct/(2rs​), while δ∝ρ2 explains the factor two in its exponent.
Inside, write r=rs​−ρ2/(4rs​). The leading radial metric is instead
ds2≃dρ2−(2rs​cρ​)2dt2.
(39)
Here the radial coordinate is timelike and the Schwarzschild t coordinate is spacelike. Thus x<0 in the Rindler metric does not represent the black-hole interior: gtt​=(αx)2 stays positive on both Rindler wedges. In the black-hole interior all future-directed radial light rays move towards decreasing r; the two signs of dr/dt do not mean opposite future radial directions because t is no longer time.
The horizon is a coordinate singularity in Schwarzschild coordinates and in the accelerated chart, not a curvature singularity. The full Schwarzschild geometry nevertheless has genuine tidal forces; the flat Rindler metric captures its leading local radial near-horizon structure.

Let a>0 be the constant proper acceleration away from the emitter and set R=c2/a. Take the signal emission to be simultaneous, in the initial inertial frame, with the ship being at rest and its clock reading τ=0. Place that initial ship event at T=0, X=R. Its hyperbolic motion is

R[cosh(aτ/c)−sinh(aτ/c)]=R−d,e−aτ/c=1−c2ad​.
(47)
Therefore light reception by a uniformly accelerated observer occurs after
τrec​=ac​log1−ad/c21​,0≤d<ac2​.​
(48)

If d=c2/a, the ray lies on the limiting null horizon and never intersects it at finite proper time; if d>c2/a, it never reaches the ship.
