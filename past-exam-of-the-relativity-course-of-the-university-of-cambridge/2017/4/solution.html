Curvature contractions. Use the index contraction stipulated in the question. In three dimensions,
Rjk​=gilK(gik​gjl​−gil​gjk​)=K(gjk​−3gjk​)=−2Kgjk​.
(26)
The same contraction in four dimensions gives
Rβγ​=−3Λ​(gβγ​−4gβγ​)=Λgβγ​.
(27)
Then the Ricci scalar is R=4Λ and the Einstein tensor is Gμν​=−Λgμν​. Thus Rμν​=Λgμν​ is precisely the vacuum Einstein field equations Gμν​+Λgμν​=0.
The static solution. For A=1+λ2r2, A′=2λ2r and A′′=2λ2. The given Ricci tensor components become
R00​=−3λ2A,R11​=A3λ2​,R22​=3λ2r2,R33​=3λ2r2sin2θ.
(28)
Each equals −3λ2 times the corresponding metric tensor component. Therefore
Λ=−3λ2.​
(29)
This is Anti-de Sitter spacetime in static coordinates on anti-de Sitter spacetime, with curvature radius 1/λ. The overall metric signature is opposite to the convention used in some descriptions of those coordinates; the geodesic constants below use the question's (+,−,−,−) convention.
Conserved quantities and radial motion. In the equatorial plane, the geodesic Lagrangian is L=At˙2−r˙2/A−r2ϕ˙​2. Its cyclic coordinates give
k=At˙,h=r2ϕ˙​.​
(30)
For rest mass m, mk=pt​ is the conserved Killing energy and mh=−pϕ​ is the axial angular momentum. Timelike normalization L=1 gives
r˙2=k2−A(1+r2h2​)=k2−1−λ2h2−λ2r2−r2h2​.​
(31)
For purely radial motion h=0, differentiating away from a turning point gives r¨=−λ2r, and continuity extends that equation through the turning point. An outward launch from the origin at τ=0 has
r(τ)=λk2−1​​sin(λτ),a=λk2−1​​,b=λ.​
(32)
This is the radial timelike geodesic in anti-de Sitter spacetime. Its first return to the origin is
τreturn​=λπ​,​
(33)
independent of the outward launch speed for every nontrivial launch k>1. The coordinate r is nonnegative, so this sine expression describes the excursion up to that return; subsequent passage through the origin requires changing the radial direction or using a signed diameter coordinate. The speed changes the amplitude, not the harmonic frequency.
Circular periods. The effective radial potential is Veff​=A(1+h2/r2). For a circle at r0​, Veff′​=0 gives h2=λ2r04​, and normalization gives k=A(r0​). Thus t˙=1 and ϕ˙​=±λ. A stable circular timelike geodesic in anti-de Sitter spacetime therefore has
Δt=Δτ=λ2π​,​
(34)
independently of radius. This is the same isochronous behavior as the central harmonic radial equation. A static clock placed at the orbit's radius instead measures 1+λ2r02​​Δt, which does depend on radius; it is a different clock from the orbiting particle's proper time.
