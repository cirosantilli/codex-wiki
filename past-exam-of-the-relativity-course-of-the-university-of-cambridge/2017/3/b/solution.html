Let the inertial frame S′ move at speed v in the positive x direction relative to S, with parallel axes and coincident origins at zero time. The inverse Lorentz transformation gives
dt=γv​(dt′+vdx′/c2),dx=γv​(dx′+vdt′),dy=dy′,dz=dz′.
(20)
Here γv​=(1−v2/c2)−1/2 and u′=dx′/dt′ is the particle's velocity in S′. Dividing spatial differentials by dt gives the relativistic velocity addition law
ux​=1+vux′​/c2ux′​+v​,uy​=γv​(1+vux′​/c2)uy′​​,uz​=γv​(1+vux′​/c2)uz′​​.​

For the collision, set c=1 during the algebra. Let the incident projectile have energy E>m1​ and momentum p=E2−m12​​. The target is initially at rest. The squared invariant mass, boost speed and Lorentz factor of the center-of-momentum frame are
s=m12​+m22​+2m2​E,V=E+m2​p​,Γ=s​E+m2​​.
(22)
In that frame the projectile has p∗​=m2​p/s​ and E∗​=(m12​+m2​E)/s​. An elastic collision changes the direction of its momentum, through an angle φ, while preserving these magnitudes. Transforming the outgoing four-momentum back to the laboratory gives
tanθ=Γ(cosφ+B)sinφ​,B=p∗​VE∗​​=m2​(E+m2​)m12​+m2​E​>1.
(23)
The maximum occurs at cosφ=−1/B. Since
Γ2(B2−1)=m22​m12​−m22​​,
(24)
the maximum laboratory deflection in relativistic elastic scattering is
tanθmax​=m12​−m22​​m2​​,sinθ≤sinθmax​=m1​m2​​.​
(25)
The printed strict inequality needs correction: equality is kinematically attainable. For example, in c=1 units take m1​=2, m2​=1, and incident E=3. A projectile scattered through 30∘ with final energy 16/7 has momentum (35​/7,15​/7). The target then has energy 12/7 and momentum (45​/7,−15​/7); both outgoing particles satisfy their mass shells and conserve total four-momentum. This gives sinθ=1/2=m2​/m1​ exactly. The inequality is strict only away from the maximizing direction.
