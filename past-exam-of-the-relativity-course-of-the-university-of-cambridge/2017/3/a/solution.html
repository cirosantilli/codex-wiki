Let xμ(τ) denote the spacetime coordinates, with dots meaning differentiation with respect to an affine parameter; for a massive particle take proper time τ. The metric tensor determines the quadratic geodesic Lagrangian L=gμν​x˙μx˙ν. The Euler-Lagrange equations are
dτd​∂x˙μ∂L​−∂xμ∂L​=0.
(16)
Symmetry of the metric tensor gives ∂L/∂x˙μ=2gμν​x˙ν. Hence the covariant four-momentum is pμ​=mgμν​x˙ν=(m/2)∂L/∂x˙μ. If xμ is a cyclic coordinate, its Euler-Lagrange equation immediately gives p˙​μ​=0.
For the rotating metric, t,ϕ,z are all cyclic. Thus
pt​pϕ​​=m[(c2−ω2r2)t˙−ωr2ϕ˙​],=−mr2(ϕ˙​+ωt˙),pz​=−mz˙.​​
(17)
Combining the first two equations cancels their r dependence:
t˙=mc2pt​−ωpϕ​​=constant.​
(18)
The radial Euler-Lagrange equation is −2r¨=−2r(ϕ˙​+ωt˙)2, so
r¨=r(ϕ˙​+ωt˙)2=m2r3pϕ2​​.​
(19)
This is the geodesic equation in a uniformly rotating Minkowski chart. With inertial angle Φ=ϕ+ωt, −pϕ​ is the inertial axial angular momentum. The outward radial-coordinate acceleration expresses the familiar centrifugal term associated with angular motion: the underlying free trajectory is a straight line in Minkowski spacetime. It does not imply an external radial force. Holding a particle at rest in the rotating reference frame instead requires a real inward force.
