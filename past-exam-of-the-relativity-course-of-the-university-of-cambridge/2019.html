The conserved initial four-momentum of a particle with positive rest mass satisfies Pμ​Pμ=m2c2>0. A single photon instead has null four-momentum, Kμ​Kμ=0. Four-momentum conservation for an isolated one-body decay would require Pμ=Kμ, contradicting these Lorentz invariants. Thus decay into a single photon is impossible in every inertial frame.
Equivalently, the incoming energy and momentum obey E2−c2∣p∣2=m2c4, whereas a single outgoing photon obeys E=c∣p∣. Changing the laboratory speed cannot change this mismatch of the invariant mass shell.

For motion along one spatial axis, introduce the rapidity η so that the four-velocity is uμ=c(coshη,sinhη,0,0). Its four-acceleration is
aμ=cdτdη​(sinhη,coshη,0,0),aμ​aμ=−c2(dτdη​)2.
(1)
A constant signed proper acceleration g therefore gives dη/dτ=g/c, hence η=η0​+gτ/c. Differentiating the four-velocity twice yields
dτ2d2uμ​=c2g2​uμ.​
(2)
The zero transverse components also satisfy this equation. The constant acceleration measured on board is the proper acceleration, rather than a constant coordinate acceleration in the laboratory.

Use signature (+−−−) and local inertial coordinates x0=ct. Write uμ=γ(c,v) and w=ρc2+p, where ρ is rest mass density. The perfect fluid in general relativity has components
T00=wγ2−p,T0i=Ti0=cwγ2vi​​,Tij=c2wγ2vi​vj​​+pδij​.
(5)
Here T00 is energy density, cTi0 is energy flux, T0i/c is momentum density, and Tij is the flux of the jth momentum component through a surface normal to i. In the local rest frame these reduce to energy density ρc2, zero energy and momentum flow, and isotropic pressure p. The equation ∇μ​Tμν=0 expresses local stress-energy conservation. Its temporal and spatial components give energy and momentum balance; in flat coordinates, for example, ∂t​T00+∂i​(cTi0)=0.
For relativistic dust, Tμν=ρuμuν. Contracting its divergence with uν​, and using metric compatibility together with uν​uν=c2, gives
0=uν​∇μ​(ρuμuν)=c2∇μ​(ρuμ)+2ρ​uμ∇μ​(uν​uν)=c2∇μ​(ρuμ).
(6)
Consequently ∇μ​(ρuμ)=0. Substituting this continuity equation back into the divergence leaves ρuμ∇μ​uν=0. Wherever matter is present,
uμ∇μ​uν=0,​
(7)
so the individual particles follow geodesics.
The electromagnetic four-force law fμ=qFμνuν​ must preserve the particle's rest mass. Thus uμ​fμ=0, and requiring this for arbitrary timelike four-velocities makes the electromagnetic field tensor antisymmetric. Its six independent components comprise the electric field and magnetic field. With the stated signature and x0=ct, one consistent convention is
Fi0=Ei​/c,F0i=−Ei​/c,Fij=−ϵijk​Bk​.
(8)
It gives the spatial force dp/dt=q(E+v×B) and energy transfer dE/dt=qE⋅v. Under a Lorentz transformation, Fμν transforms as a rank-two contravariant tensor; its electric and magnetic components consequently mix. A four-potential supplies Fμν​=∇μ​Aν​−∇ν​Aμ​, unchanged by Aμ​↦Aμ​+∇μ​χ. The Maxwell equations are
∇μ​Fμν=μ0​jν,∇α​Fβγ​+∇β​Fγα​+∇γ​Fαβ​=0.
(9)
These combine the source equations and the homogeneous equations into covariant tensor statements; jμ is the four-current.
For charged relativistic dust, the matter tensor exchanges four-momentum with the electromagnetic field: ∇μ​Tmμν​=Fναjα​, while their total stress-energy tensor is conserved. Identical particles of rest mass m and electric charge q have jα=(q/m)ρuα. Contraction with uν​ again gives mass continuity because uν​Fναuα​=0. Removing that term gives
uμ∇μ​uν=mq​Fναuα​.​
(10)
Using zero divergence for the matter tensor alone in this last step would omit the electromagnetic Lorentz force.

Write A(r)=1−2μ/r, with μ=GM/c2. On the equatorial plane, the geodesic Lagrangian is
L=21​(c2At˙2−A−1r˙2−r2ϕ˙​2).
(11)
The coordinates t and ϕ are cyclic, giving At˙=k,r2ϕ˙​=h​. The conserved physical energy per unit rest mass is c2k, while h is the signed angular momentum per unit rest mass. The radial Euler-Lagrange equation, with A′=2μ/r2, is
A−1r¨+r2μc2​t˙2−r2A2μ​r˙2−rϕ˙​2=0.​
(12)
These are the requested geodesic equations. The equatorial condition is consistent with the polar equation by the spherical symmetry of the Schwarzschild metric.
The normalization of the four-velocity is c2=c2At˙2−A−1r˙2−r2ϕ˙​2. Insert the two conserved quantities and multiply by A to obtain
r˙2+r2h2​(1−r2μ​)−r2μc2​=c2(k2−1).​
(13)
This is a one-dimensional radial energy equation. Half of its left side is radial kinetic energy per unit mass plus an effective potential: the Newtonian centrifugal and gravitational terms acquire the extra attractive contribution −μh2/r3.
A timelike circular Schwarzschild geodesic requires r˙=r¨=0. Equivalently, set the derivative of V(r)=h2/r2−2μh2/r3−2μc2/r to zero. At r=R this gives h2(R−3μ)=μc2R2, and substitution in the radial energy equation yields
h2=R−3μμc2R2​,k2=R(R−3μ)(R−2μ)2​.​
(14)
Such timelike circular orbits require R>3μ. Their coordinate angular velocity satisfies Ω2=μc2/R3, and their proper clock rate is dτs​/dt=A/k=1−3μ/Rs​​.
For the static North-Pole clock, dτE​/dt=1−2μ/RE​​. Consecutive overhead passages occur one coordinate orbital period apart, and the repeated Earth-satellite geometry gives equal signal travel delays. The comparison of circular-orbit and static Schwarzschild clocks therefore gives
τE​τs​​=1−2μ/RE​1−3μ/Rs​​​.​
(15)
This includes both gravitational and orbital-motion effects; a separate Newtonian time-dilation correction must not be added again.
For an estimate, Kepler's third law gives Rs​=42100km(3/24)2/3=10525km. The same approximate geostationary radius and period give μ=4π2Rgeo3​/(c2Tgeo2​)≃4.4mm. Expanding the ratio to first order,
τE​τs​​≃1+RE​μ​−2Rs​3μ​≃1+6.4×10−11​.
(16)
The satellite clock consequently gains about 0.7μs over a three-hour orbit in this approximation. The surface observer is taken to be static, and the spacetime model is the spherical Schwarzschild metric specified in the problem.

A fundamental observer follows the mean cosmic matter flow and is at rest with respect to the homogeneous cosmological frame. Cosmological isotropy permits no preferred spatial acceleration, rotation vector or anisotropic expansion for this flow: the congruence is geodesic, irrotational and shear-free, with isotropic expansion. Its orthogonal homogeneous spatial slices define simultaneity. Choosing fixed spatial labels along these worldlines and their common proper time as t gives synchronous coordinates, gtt​=c2 and gti​=0.
In the Friedmann-Lemaître-Robertson-Walker metric, t is this cosmic proper time and χ is radial comoving coordinate. On a constant-t radial slice, the proper distance in cosmology from 0 to a fixed χ0​ is D(t)=a(t)χ0​. Therefore
D˙=H(t)D,H(t)=a(t)a˙(t)​.​
(17)
This is the Hubble parameter. The metric describes expansion when a˙>0; its form alone also permits static or contracting histories, so expansion additionally requires that sign of the scale factor derivative.
For a radial photon, the metric components relevant to its motion are gtt​=c2 and gχχ​=−a2(t), both independent of χ. The angular momentum components vanish. The covariant-momentum form of the geodesic equation therefore gives
dλdpχ​​=21​∂χ​gνρ​pνpρ=0.​
(18)
Only this radial contraction is needed: angular metric coefficients can depend on χ, but multiply zero angular components. The conserved quantity is the lower-index p1​=pχ​, rather than the contravariant component p1.
For outward travel put P=−pχ​>0. The null condition c2(pt)2−a2(pχ)2=0 gives
pχ=a2P​,pt=acP​,p0​=pt​=c2pt=a(t)cP​.​
(19)
Here the time coordinate is t, rather than ct. A comoving observer has ut=1 and measures photon energy E=pμ​uμ=pt​. Thus energy and frequency decrease as a−1, giving the cosmological redshift
1+z=νR​νE​​=a(tE​)a(tR​)​.​
(20)
The reciprocal relation for wavelengths follows from their common local propagation speed c.
Finally center the spatial coordinates on the receiving comoving observer. At the source, the transverse spatial line element is dl=a(tE​)SK​(χ)dθ. For a short transverse rod and a small observed angle Δθ,
l=a(tE​)SK​(χ)Δθ=1+za(tR​)SK​(χ)​Δθ.​
(21)
Thus the angular diameter distance is dA​=a(tE​)SK​(χ). Writing the reception epoch as t0​=tR​ gives the displayed formula in the question. The length is the proper transverse length at emission; t0​ cannot be replaced by an unrelated epoch while retaining that expression.
