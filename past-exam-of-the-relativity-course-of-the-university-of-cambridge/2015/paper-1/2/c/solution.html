Ordinary component differences at neighboring points depend on how the local bases vary. The covariant derivative corrects for that variation:
∇b​Va=∂b​Va+Γbca​Vc,∇b​Wa​=∂b​Wa​−Γbac​Wc​.​
(21)
Each upper tensor index adds a connection term and each lower index subtracts one. A scalar has ∇a​f=∂a​f, while the derivative of a tensor transforms as a tensor with one additional covariant index. The Christoffel symbols themselves are not tensor components; their inhomogeneous transformation cancels that of the ordinary derivative.
For the Levi-Civita connection,
Γbca​=21​gad(∂b​gdc​+∂c​gbd​−∂d​gbc​),∇c​gab​=0,Γbca​=Γcba​.
(22)
These express metric compatibility and zero torsion. Along xa(λ), parallel transport is the initial value problem
dλDVa​=dλdVa​+Γbca​dλdxb​Vc=0.​
(23)
It preserves inner products for the Levi-Civita connection. Parallel transport of the tangent itself gives the affinely parametrized geodesic equation, x¨a+Γbca​x˙bx˙c=0.
Transport generally depends on the path. On a round sphere, transport a tangent vector around the triangle bounded by two meridians 90 degrees apart and the intervening equatorial arc. All three sides are geodesics; the enclosed spherical area is πa2/2. The returning vector is rotated by 90 degrees, with sign set by traversal orientation. Locally this holonomy is controlled by curvature, even though the connection can be made zero at a single point. A vector can therefore have changing coordinate components while remaining parallel, and constant components do not generally mean parallel transport.
This construction compares directions at separate points through a chosen connection, rather than assuming that a curved manifold supplies a path-independent comparison.[ref]
