Lagrange cardinal polynomial (ℓi​)

 ... Area of mathematics Analysis Numerical analysis Interpolation Polynomial interpolation Lagrange polynomial
OurBigBook.com Words: 341 Articles: 2
For distinct interpolation nodes x0​,…,xn​, the Lagrange cardinal polynomial ℓi​ has value one at its own interpolation node and zero at every other interpolation node:
ℓi​(x)=∏j=i​xi​−xj​x−xj​​,ℓi​(xj​)=δij​={1,0,​i=j,i=j.​
(9)
Here δij​ is the Kronecker delta. These Lagrange cardinal polynomials form a basis of the vector space of polynomials of polynomial degree at most n: every such polynomial has the unique expansion p(x)=∑i​p(xi​)ℓi​(x). The nodal evaluation linear functionals Ej​(p)=p(xj​) form the dual basis, since Ej​(ℓi​)=δij​.
For the interpolation nodes 0,1,2, the three Lagrange cardinal polynomials are
ℓ0​(x)=2(x−1)(x−2)​,ℓ1​(x)=−x(x−2),ℓ2​(x)=2x(x−1)​.
(10)
Their values on the interpolation nodes are the rows of the identity matrix. In the figure, each colored curve passes through its own unit-height marker and through zero at the other two interpolation nodes. Multiplying the curves by the data 1,2,5 and adding gives the Lagrange polynomial x2+1.
Since the constant polynomial one interpolates data values all equal to one, uniqueness gives ∑i​ℓi​(x)=1. Nevertheless, ℓ2​(1/2)=−1/8, so Lagrange cardinal polynomials need not stay between zero and one. If a single data value yi​ changes by ε, the interpolation polynomial changes by exactly εℓi​(x). Thus a Lagrange cardinal polynomial describes how one observation influences the entire graph, and ∑i​∣ℓi​(x)∣ controls the worst amplification of simultaneous data errors at x; its maximum is the Lebesgue constant of interpolation.
