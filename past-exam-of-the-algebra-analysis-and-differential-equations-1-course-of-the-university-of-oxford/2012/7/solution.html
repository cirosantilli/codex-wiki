For every fixed real x<1, these are partial sums of the exponential series evaluated at the finite real number −log(1−x). Therefore
n→∞lim​yn​(x)=exp(−log(1−x))=1−x1​for every x<1.​

This range is larger than ∣x∣<1: the expansion is in powers of log(1−x), not a Taylor series in x. For example it converges at x=−2. It also converges uniformly on every compact interval contained in (−∞,1), because ∣log(1−x)∣ is bounded there and the exponential series supplies a uniform summable bound. At x=1 the approximations are undefined, while for x>1 the real integral from zero crosses the singularity. This is Picard iteration for a simple-pole linear differential equation.
