In the finite field F5​=Z/5Z, the characteristic polynomial becomes
χA​(λ)=(λ−1)(λ−3)2,
(13)
because (λ−3)2=λ2−λ−1 modulo 5. Splitting into linear factors does not by itself guarantee diagonalizability: the repeated eigenvalue needs enough eigenvectors.
Solving (A−3I)(x,y,z)T=0 gives
2x+y=0,x+3y=0,y+3z=0,
(14)
so y=3x and z=4x. Hence
ker(A−3I)=spanF5​​{(1,3,4)T}
(15)
has dimension one, although 3 has algebraic multiplicity two. The geometric multiplicity is too small, and A is not diagonalizable over F5​. This illustrates diagonalizability depends on the field: characteristic zero gave three distinct eigenvalues, while reduction modulo 5 merges two of them.
