The annihilator of a vector subspace is the vector subspace of the dual space consisting of the linear functionals that vanish on U:
U∘={λ∈V′:λ(u)=0 for every u∈U}.​
(1)
It is a vector subspace because every linear combination of functionals vanishing on U still vanishes there.

The Gram-Schmidt process subtracts projections onto directions already constructed and then normalizes. Define recursively
wk​=vk​−∑j<k​⟨vk​,ej​⟩ej​,ek​=∥wk​∥wk​​,k=1,…,n,
(8)
where the empty sum for k=1 is zero. Assuming the preceding ej​ are orthonormal, taking the inner product of wk​ with ei​, i<k, gives zero. Moreover wk​=0: otherwise vk​ would belong to the span of v1​,…,vk−1​, contrary to linear independence. Normalization is therefore legitimate and produces an orthonormal basis. At each stage the first k constructed vectors have the same span as the first k original vectors.

The Chinese remainder theorem for two comaximal two-sided ideals I,J of a ring states that the natural ring homomorphism induces
R/(I∩J)≅R/I×R/J(I+J=R).​
(12)
Indeed, the map r↦(r+I,r+J) has kernel of a ring homomorphism I∩J. Choose i∈I, j∈J with i+j=1. Given target representatives a,b∈R, the element aj+bi is congruent to a modulo I and to b modulo J, proving surjectivity. The first isomorphism theorem for rings gives the result. For a commutative ring, I∩J=IJ: if x∈I∩J, then x=xi+xj∈IJ, while the reverse inclusion follows from the ideal properties.

Let N count everyone present, including the host. The grouping, seating and departure conditions give the modular congruences
N≡4(mod5),N≡3(mod9),N≡1(mod2).
(14)
The first two imply N=39+45k, and oddness forces k even. By the Chinese remainder theorem, the complete solution is
N≡39(mod90).​
(15)
Because not every invitee attended, at most y−1 guests came, so N≤y. For y=120, the only positive possibility is 39 people. It is feasible: seven groups of five and one of four; three tables of ten and one of nine; nineteen departing pairs and the host.
For y=150, both 39 and 129 people are possible. The latter uses twenty-five groups of five and one of four, three tables of ten and eleven of nine, and sixty-four departing pairs. Thus the larger invitation count destroys uniqueness; it does not force a different attendance.

In the subspace topology on S, a subset A is open in S precisely when A=S∩O for some open set O⊆C. A subset B is closed in S precisely when S∖B is open in S, equivalently B=S∩F for some closed set F⊆C.
A compact space is one for which every open cover has a finite subcover. Here the cover may use relatively open subsets of S, or equivalently ambient open subsets covering it. The Heine-Borel theorem states that
S⊆C is compact⟺S is closed and bounded.​
(16)
Closedness and boundedness here refer to the ambient complex plane, not merely closedness in S itself.

A connected space S cannot be expressed as S=A∪B with A,B disjoint nonempty relatively open sets. Equivalently, the only subsets simultaneously relatively open and closed are ∅ and S. A decomposition into such A,B is a separation of the topological space.

A path-connected space S has, for every pair z0​,z1​∈S, a continuous path γ:[0,1]→S with γ(0)=z0​ and γ(1)=z1​. Every path-connected space is a connected space, because a separation of the target would pull a joining path apart and contradict connectedness of an interval.

The integers are unbounded in the complex plane, so the Heine-Borel theorem shows they are not compact. They are also not connected: {0} and Z∖{0} are disjoint nonempty relatively open sets covering Z. Each integer is isolated, so the induced subspace topology is discrete.

The disc is a closed set and is bounded in the complex plane, so it is compact by the Heine-Borel theorem. It is convex: the triangle inequality gives ∣(1−t)z0​+tz1​∣≤2 for all t∈[0,1] when both endpoints lie in it. Straight segments therefore supply paths, so it is path-connected and hence connected.

The union is bounded but is not a closed set: the points −1+1/n, n≥2, belong to the open disc and tend to −1, which lies in neither disc. Thus it is not compact by the Heine-Borel theorem.
Nevertheless it is connected, indeed path-connected. Every point of the open disc joins 0 by a straight segment. The segment from 0 to 1 stays in the union, since its points before the endpoint belong to the open disc and the endpoint 1 belongs to the closed disc. Every point of that closed disc joins 1 inside it by convexity. Concatenating these paths joins any two points of the union. The fact that the two discs are disjoint does not imply disconnection: their contact point belongs to one of them. This illustrates connectedness at a one-sided contact point.

An isolated singularity at a means that the function is holomorphic on some punctured disc 0<∣z−a∣<r, while it is undefined or fails to be holomorphic at a. The punctured neighbourhood contains no other singularities; this isolation is needed before applying the usual removable/pole/essential classification.

A removable singularity at a is an isolated singularity for which one can assign a value at a to obtain a holomorphic function on the whole disc. Equivalently its Laurent series has no negative powers. A finite limit L supplies the extension value f(a)=L.

Introduce a characteristic parameter s and label each point of the initial segment by r∈[0,1]. The method of characteristics gives
dsdx​=x−2,dsdy​=y,dsdz​=y,(x,y,z)∣s=0​=(1,r,1).
(37)
Integrating gives the parametric solution
x=2−es,y=res,z=1+r(es−1),s∈R,0≤r≤1.​
(38)
The projected characteristic curves satisfy y=r(2−x), so each lies on a straight line through (2,0). Its branch from the data always has x<2; the common vertex is approached only as s→−∞.
Eliminating s,r using es=2−x and r=y/(2−x) yields
z(x,y)=1+y−2−xy​=1+2−xy(1−x)​.​
(39)
Its derivatives give (x−2)zx​+yzy​=y, and substitution of x=1 gives the prescribed value one.
The Jacobian determinant of (s,r)↦(x,y) is −e2s=0, and the inverse just displayed is unique. Hence the data determine the solution exactly on
{(x,y):x<2, 0≤y≤2−x}.​
(40)
This is the domain determined by a finite segment under a radial characteristic flow. Its interior is x<2, 0<y<2−x; the two boundary rays come from the endpoints of the initial segment. Every point there lies on exactly one characteristic from the data. Outside this sector, a characteristic either misses the initial segment or lies on the branch x>2, which cannot reach it. Thus the formula may be extended beyond this sector, but such an extension is not uniquely specified by the given data.
