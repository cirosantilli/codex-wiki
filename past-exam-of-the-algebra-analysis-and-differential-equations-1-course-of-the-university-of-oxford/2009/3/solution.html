A cubic over a field is reducible exactly when it has a root in that field. For p(X)=X3+2X2+1 modulo five, direct evaluation gives
(p(0),p(1),p(2),p(3),p(4))=(1,4,2,1,2).
(2)
No value is zero, so p is irreducible in Z5​[X].
The integer polynomial in the second part reduces to precisely p, because −2008≡2, 2010≡0 and −2009≡1(mod5). It is monic, hence primitive, and its degree is preserved by reduction. The irreducibility by degree-preserving reduction modulo a prime criterion states that a primitive integer polynomial with irreducible reduction of the same degree is irreducible over the rationals. This follows from Gauss's lemma: a rational factorization would give integer factors whose leading coefficients remain nonzero modulo five, contradicting the irreducible reduction. Thus
X3−2008X2+2010X−2009 is irreducible in Q[X].​
