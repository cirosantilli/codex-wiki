Use the monomial basis ek​=xn−kyk, 0≤k≤n. Direct differentiation gives
Pek​=kek−1​,Qek​=(n−k)ek+1​,
(5)
with the endpoint terms zero. Repeated application therefore gives Pn+1=Qn+1=0, while Pnen​=n!e0​=0 and Qne0​=n!en​=0. Each nilpotency index is exactly n+1, so
mP​(t)=mQ​(t)=tn+1.​
(6)
Next
PQek​=(n−k)(k+1)ek​,QPek​=k(n−k+1)ek​,
(7)
and hence (PQ−QP)ek​=(n−2k)ek​. These n+1 real eigenvalues are distinct. A polynomial annihilates this operator exactly when it vanishes at all of them, giving
mPQ−QP​(t)=k=0∏n​(t−(n−2k)).​
(8)
The commutator is diagonal in the displayed monomial basis. Since n≥1, P,Q are nonzero nilpotent linear map; a diagonalizable nilpotent map would have a diagonal matrix with all eigenvalues zero and thus be the zero map. Therefore only PQ−QP is diagonalizable. The calculation realizes raising and lowering on binary homogeneous polynomials.
