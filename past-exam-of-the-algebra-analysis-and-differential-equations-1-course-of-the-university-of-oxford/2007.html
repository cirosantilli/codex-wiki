The weight is strictly positive and all its polynomial moments are finite. Thus the given integral is a inner product: for a nonzero polynomial, its square is positive on a nonempty interval, so its weighted integral is strictly positive.
In the finite-dimensional vector space Vn​, subtract the orthogonal projection onto a finite-dimensional subspace of xn onto Vn−1​:
ϕn​=xn−projVn−1​​xn.
(1)
This is monic, since the subtracted polynomial has lower degree, and is orthogonal to every lower-degree polynomial. If ϕn​,ψn​ were two such monic polynomials, their difference would lie in Vn−1​ and be orthogonal to that space. Taking its inner product with itself gives zero, hence the difference vanishes. This proves existence and uniqueness for every n, with ϕ0​=1.
Odd moments vanish because the weight is even; the even moments are
∫R​e−∣x∣x2kdx=2∫0∞​e−xx2kdx=2(2k)!.
(2)
Consequently ϕ1​=x, while a monic quadratic orthogonal to x has no linear term, and orthogonality to one gives its constant term −4/2=−2. Thus
ϕ0​=1,ϕ1​=x,ϕ2​=x2−2.​
(3)
Finally (−1)nϕn​(−x) is also monic and orthogonal to every lower-degree polynomial, by the change of variable x↦−x. Uniqueness implies ϕn​(−x)=(−1)nϕn​(x). In particular ϕ2007​ is odd and

(4)
This is parity of monic orthogonal polynomials for an even weight.

The topology on A is the subspace topology inherited from the complex plane. A subset B is open in A if B=A∩U for some open subset U⊆C. Equivalently, every b∈B has a radius ϵ>0 such that A∩{z:∣z−b∣<ϵ}⊆B.
A subset B is closed in A if A∖B is open in A, equivalently if B=A∩F for a closed subset F of the complex plane. These are relative properties: the same subset need not be open or closed in the ambient plane.

b OurBigBook.com
The set A is disconnected space if it has a separation of a topological space: two nonempty disjoint subsets U,V open in A with A=U∪V. Each is then also closed in A, being the other's complement.
The set A is connected if no such separation of a topological space exists. Equivalently its only subsets that are both open and closed are ∅ and A. A separation of a topological space uses openness in A rather than necessarily in the entire complex plane.

The complex exponential function gives the natural onto map:
w=ez,e1/4<∣w∣<e1/2.​
(23)
Its derivative is never zero, so it preserves angles locally. Conversely any point in this annulus has a logarithm with real part in (1/4,1/2), proving surjectivity.
There is a terminology qualification. This map is not injective, since ez+2πi=ez and the strip contains both points. If “conformal mapping onto” requires a biholomorphism, no such map exists: the strip is convex and simply connected space, whereas the annulus contains a loop of winding number one around zero that cannot contract inside it. A biholomorphism would be a homeomorphism and preserve this property.
Thus the complex exponential function is a locally conformal covering; a biholomorphism is impossible. It identifies imaginary translates by 2πi. The printed request is satisfied under its local-angle-preserving convention, with the injectivity distinction made explicit. This is exponential covering of an annulus by a vertical strip.

The second equilibrium point equation gives y=−(x+1)/2. Substituting into the first gives x+e(x−1)/2=0, so x<0. Put x=−a, with a>0. Then
a=e−(a+1)/2⟺ae(a+1)/2=1,y=(a−1)/2.
(35)
The function h(a)=ae(a+1)/2 is strictly increasing on a≥0, since h′(a)=e(a+1)/2(1+a/2)>0. It starts at zero and tends to infinity, so it takes the value one exactly once. Moreover h(1)=e>1, so 0<a<1. This proves uniqueness of the stated equilibrium point.
At that point ex+y=a, and the Jacobian matrix is
J=(1+a1​a2​).
(36)
Its trace is 3+a and determinant 2+a. The characteristic discriminant is (3+a)2−4(2+a)=(a+1)2, so its two eigenvalues are
λ1​=1,λ2​=2+a.​
(37)
They are distinct and positive, with eigenvectors (1,−1) and (a,1) respectively. Thus the linearized critical point is an unstable node. It is a hyperbolic equilibrium, so the original nonlinear equilibrium point is also locally repelling; this last observation is stronger than the requested linearized classification.
