The quantitative Picard-Lindelöf theorem applies on a rectangle ∣x−x0​∣≤a, ∥Z−B∥∞​≤b. Suppose F is continuous there, bounded in maximum norm by M, and uniformly Lipschitz continuous in Z. Then the initial value problem Y′=F(x,Y), Y(x0​)=B, has a unique solution on
∣x−x0​∣≤h,h=min{a,b/M},​
(53)
with the usual interpretation b/M=∞ if M=0. The solution remains in the dependent-variable ball. Its endpoints are included when F is continuous on the closed rectangle, as here. Equivalently, existence first on every smaller interval and the derivative bound give extension to those endpoints.
For the given cube, x0​=0 and a=b=1. Thus the theorem gives h=min{1,1/M} once a bound M and a uniform Lipschitz constant have been established. The theorem does not require Lh<1: convergence of Picard iteration can be proved by factorial estimates or by using successive shorter intervals.
