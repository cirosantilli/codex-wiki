An ideal is an additive subgroup of a ring closed under multiplication by arbitrary ring elements; in a potentially noncommutative ring, a two-sided ideal must absorb multiplication on both sides. For a ring homomorphism ϕ, its kernel of a ring homomorphism K={r:ϕ(r)=0} contains zero, is closed under subtraction, and satisfies ϕ(rk)=ϕ(r)ϕ(k)=0 and ϕ(kr)=0. Thus K is an ideal.
Define ϕ​:R/K→S by r+K↦ϕ(r). It is well-defined because two representatives differ by an element of K, preserves addition and multiplication, is surjective by hypothesis, and is injective because its zero class is precisely K. Therefore R/kerϕ≅S​, the first isomorphism theorem for rings.
Apply this to the evaluation homomorphism C[z]→C, p↦p(1). Constants prove surjectivity, and division by z−1 shows that its kernel of a ring homomorphism is (z−1). Hence C[z]/(z−1)≅C​.
