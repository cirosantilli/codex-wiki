Since the exponential factor is positive, equilibria must have y=0 and x4=1. Thus (x,y)=(−1,0), (1,0)​. At either point the Jacobian matrix is (0−4x3​10​). At (−1,0) its eigenvalues are ±2, so this is a saddle point. At (1,0) they are ±2i: the linearization has a centre equilibrium.
The requested first-order approximation follows by putting x=1+u, y=v: u˙=v+O(∥(u,v)∥2) and v˙=−4u+O(∥(u,v)∥2). The linearized system conserves 4u2+v2, giving the approximate ellipses
4(x−1)2+y2=constant.​
(11)
However, the printed nonlinear system has a weak repelling focus at (1,0), rather than a centre. A centre in the linearization need not be a nonlinear centre. Here that distinction can be proved explicitly, without relying on the numerical drift in the figure.
Put X=2(x−1), Y=y, τ=2t and r2=X2+Y2. The weak focus of an exponential polynomial planar system has the local positive function
H=2X2+Y2​+127​X3+21​XY2+31​Y3+41​X4−163​X3Y+163​XY3.
(12)
Expanding the vector field through cubic order and differentiating gives
dτdH​=163​r4+O(r5)>0for sufficiently small r>0.​
(13)
The cubic correction cancels the cubic terms in the derivative of H, and the quartic correction leaves the positive radial term 3r4/16. Since H=r2/2+O(r3) is positive and the angular velocity stays near −1, small nonzero trajectories spiral outward. Equivalently the reversed system has a strict local Lyapunov function and an asymptotically stable equilibrium. In particular, a periodic orbit sufficiently near the equilibrium is impossible, because H would have to increase strictly and return to its starting value. This proves the failure of the printed centre assertion while retaining its correct linearized ellipse calculation.
