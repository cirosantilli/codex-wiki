Reduction modulo the prime p preserves integer addition, multiplication, and one:
a+b​=aˉ+bˉ,ab=aˉbˉ.

Every congruence class has an integer representative, so the ring homomorphism is onto. Its kernel is exactly pZ=(p). The quotient is a field because every class not divisible by p has a multiplicative inverse by Bézout's identity.
For the coefficientwise extension to the polynomial ring, addition is preserved term by term. If f=∑i​ai​xi and g=∑j​bj​xj, the coefficient of xk in their product is ∑i+j=k​ai​bj​. Reducing it modulo p gives ∑i+j=k​aˉi​bˉj​, the coefficient in ϕ^​(f)ϕ^​(g). Thus multiplication and one are preserved as well. Lift each coefficient of a polynomial over Zp​ to an integer to prove surjectivity. The kernel is
kerϕ^​=pZ[x]=(p).​

Indeed, its elements are precisely the polynomials whose coefficients are all divisible by p.
In the binomial theorem expansion of (x−1)p, every intermediate coefficient (jp​) is divisible by p: the numerator contains the prime factor p, while j!(p−j)! does not for 1≤j<p. Hence
(x−1)p=xp+(−1)p=xp−1in Zp​[x].
