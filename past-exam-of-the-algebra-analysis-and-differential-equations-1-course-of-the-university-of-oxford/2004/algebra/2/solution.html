Self-adjointness means ⟨Tv,w⟩=⟨v,Tw⟩ for all v,w∈V, or T=T∗. Take the inner product linear in its first variable. If Tv=λv with v=0, then
λ∥v∥2=⟨Tv,v⟩=⟨v,Tv⟩=λ∥v∥2.
(4)
Therefore every eigenvalue is real.
If U is invariant and w∈U⊥, then for every u∈U, Tu∈U and ⟨Tw,u⟩=⟨w,Tu⟩=0. Hence Tw∈U⊥, proving invariance of the orthogonal complement.
Induct on d=dimV. The zero-dimensional case is empty. For d>0, choose an eigenvector and normalize it to a unit vector e. Its span is invariant, so its orthogonal complement is invariant as just proved. The restriction of T to that complement is again self-adjoint. Its dimension is d−1, so induction supplies an orthonormal basis of eigenvectors there. Adjoining e gives an orthonormal eigenbasis of V. This proves the finite-dimensional spectral theorem without assuming it in the argument.
