Every vector decomposes as v=Ev+(v−Ev), where Ev∈imE and E(v−Ev)=0. If w lies in both the image and kernel, write w=Eu to obtain w=E2u=Ew=0. Thus
V=imE⊕kerE,

detE=0.​

First extract the polynomial content from f:
f(X)=2(X4−2X3+4X2−2X+2).​
(10)
The primitive quartic is Eisenstein at the prime 2: every nonleading coefficient is divisible by 2, while the constant term is not divisible by 4. Eisenstein's criterion makes it irreducible over Q, and Gauss's lemma makes it irreducible in Z[X]. The constant factor 2 is itself irreducible in Z[X].
The polynomial g is already irreducible over Q: it is Eisenstein at 3. Its nonleading coefficients, including the missing zero coefficients, are divisible by 3, and −21 is not divisible by 9. Thus g is its own sole nonconstant irreducible factor over Q.
Over the field Z3​, grouping terms gives
h(X)=X(X+2)(X2+1).​
(11)
The first two factors are linear and therefore irreducible. The quadratic takes the values 1,2,2 at 0,1,2 respectively, so it has no root in Z3​. A degree-two polynomial over a field is reducible exactly when it has a root, proving its irreducibility.
All three factorizations are unique up to order and multiplication by units. The ring Z is a unique factorization domain, hence so is Z[X] by Gauss's lemma. A polynomial ring over a field is a Euclidean domain, so both Q[X] and Z3​[X] have unique factorization. The units are ±1 in the first ring, nonzero rational constants in the second, and 1,2 in the third.

In the subspace topology, B is relatively open in A if B=A∩U for an open subset U of the ambient Euclidean space. Equivalently, every b∈B has a radius ε>0 with A∩Bε​(b)⊆B. It is relatively closed if its complement A∖B is relatively open, equivalently B=A∩F for an ambient closed set F.
For the upper half of the open disk, B is closed in A, but not open in A. It is the intersection with the closed half-plane Imz≥0. A relative neighborhood of zero contains points of negative imaginary part, so relative openness fails.

The rational cut is both open and closed in Q. It is relatively open as Q∩(−∞,2​). Since 2​ is irrational, its relative complement is Q∩(2​,∞), also relatively open. Thus this cut is a clopen set in the subspace topology of the rationals.

Solution

ii OurBigBook.com
A set is compact if every open cover of it has a finite subcover. Let K1​,…,Km​ be compact and let U cover their union. For each j, the same family covers Kj​, so compactness supplies a finite subfamily Uj​ covering Kj​. The union of these finitely many finite subfamilies is finite and covers ⋃j​Kj​. Hence a finite union of compact sets is compact.
An arbitrary union need not be compact. Each singleton {n}, n≥1, is compact, but their union N has the open cover {(n−1/3,n+1/3):n≥1} with no finite subcover. Every member covers only one positive integer, so finitely many leave infinitely many integers uncovered.

Suppose, for a contradiction, that f has no zero in the open disk. The strict boundary inequality implies no boundary zero either. Thus 1/f is holomorphic on a neighborhood of the closed disk: compactness and continuity keep f nonzero in a sufficiently small neighborhood. Apply the k=0 Cauchy estimate to 1/f:
∣f(z0​)∣1​≤max∣z−z0​∣=r​∣f(z)∣1​=min∣z−z0​∣=r​∣f(z)∣1​.

Here a periodic solution must mean a nonconstant periodic orbit: constant equilibria are not excluded by the stated divergence hypothesis. Suppose such an orbit exists. Since the vector field is continuously differentiable, uniqueness for the autonomous differential equation makes its trajectory a simple closed curve C, bounding a region D by the Jordan curve theorem. The Green theorem gives
∬D​[∂x​(ρf)+∂y​(ρg)]dxdy=∮C​ρfdy−ρgdx.
(23)
Along the orbit, dx=fdt and dy=gdt, so the line integral is identically zero. Reversing orientation if necessary does not change this conclusion. The area integral is strictly positive because its continuous integrand is positive everywhere and D has positive area. This contradiction proves the Bendixson-Dulac criterion in this case.
For the second-order equation set y=x′, giving x′=y, y′=−y−εy3−sinx. Choose the Dulac function ρ=−1. Then
∂x​(ρf)+∂y​(ρg)=1+3εy2>0.
(24)
Thus there is no nonconstant periodic solution for ε>0. The equilibria x(t)=kπ do remain solutions. As a direct check, the mechanical energy H=y2/2+1−cosx obeys H′=−y2−εy4, whose integral over a period could vanish only if y≡0.
