Topological tripod

 Home Mathematics Area of mathematics Geometry and topology Topology Topological manifold
OurBigBook.com Words: 388
A topological tripod is three copies of the closed interval [0,1] with their zero endpoints identified to a single junction o. Equivalently, embed three line segments with distinct directions in the Euclidean plane and use the subspace topology. It is a compact, connected metric space, and is Hausdorff, second countable, and paracompact. Every interior arm point has a coordinate chart to an open interval, and each outer endpoint has a half-interval chart as in a topological manifold with boundary. The junction has neither kind of chart.
Here is a precise local obstruction. Let S be a small open neighbourhood of o consisting of initial segments of the three arms, excluding their outer endpoints. Every connected open neighbourhood N of o contained in S contains an initial segment on every arm, because N is open at o. Its intersection with each arm has no gaps: if a point between o and a point of N were missing, cutting there would separate the part beyond it from the rest of N, contradicting connectedness. Thus N∖{o} has exactly three connected components, one interval per arm.
Suppose a coordinate chart h to the real line existed at o. Choose a small open interval I about h(o) lying inside h(S∩domain(h)), and restrict the chart to N=h−1(I). This is a connected open neighbourhood of o contained in S. Removing o leaves three connected components in N, while removing h(o) leaves two in I. A homeomorphism preserves connected components, so this is impossible. This restriction step is essential: the count is proved for any sufficiently small connected chart neighborhood, not merely one chosen drawing.
A higher dimension of a manifold cannot repair the junction. For n≥2, repeat the argument with an open ball about h(o): a punctured open ball in Rn is path-connected, whereas N∖{o} still has three connected components. A zero-dimensional chart would make o an isolated point, which it is not. A chart at a boundary point of a topological manifold with boundary similarly gives a punctured relative half-ball: one connected component when n=1, and a path-connected space when n≥2. Consequently the topological tripod fails the local definition even when intrinsic boundary is allowed.
The circle provides a useful warning about scale: deleting a point from the whole circle leaves a connected space, but deleting it from a sufficiently small connected arc leaves two connected components. Local chart tests use the latter. The circle and interval are not homeomorphic article uses deletion from the whole spaces for a different, global obstruction.
