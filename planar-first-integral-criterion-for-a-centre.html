Planar first integral criterion for a centre (F⋅∇H=0,D2H(p) definite)

 ... Branches of physics Dynamical systems Autonomous planar system Equilibrium point of a dynamical system Linear stability of a planar equilibrium Center equilibrium
OurBigBook.com Words: 136
Let F be a continuously differentiable planar vector field with an isolated equilibrium point p, and let the twice continuously differentiable first integral H satisfy F⋅∇H=0 and ∇H(p)=0. If the Hessian matrix D2H(p) is positive or negative definite, then p is a centre equilibrium.
For a positive-definite matrix, sufficiently short rays from p have strictly increasing H with radial derivative bounded below by a positive constant times distance. Every sufficiently small positive level difference therefore meets each ray exactly once, and the implicit function theorem makes these intersections a simple closed curve. These regular level sets shrink to p; use −H in the negative-definite case. The vector field is tangent and nonzero on each such compact curve. Its speed is bounded away from zero, so a traversal takes finite time and each curve is a periodic orbit. An arbitrary strict local extremum without these regularity and nondegeneracy conditions does not by itself supply this argument.
