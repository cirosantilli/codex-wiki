Newton's law of universal gravitation (F=−GMmr/∣r∣3)

 Home Physics Branches of physics Classical mechanics
OurBigBook.com Words: 8k Articles: 73
Newton's law of universal gravitation gives the attractive force between two point masses. With source mass M at the origin and relative position r of mass m, write r=∣r∣>0:
FM→m​=−r3GMm​r,∣FM→m​∣=r2GMm​.
(1)
Here G is the gravitational constant. The minus sign points toward M; the magnitude follows an inverse-square law, so doubling the separation quarters the force. The opposite force on M satisfies Newton's third law. [ref]
Both masses accelerate. Applying Newton's second law to each in the isolated two-body problem gives
r¨=−r3μ​r,μ=G(M+m).
(2)
The relative standard gravitational parameter includes both masses; replacing it by GM requires the test particle approximation m≪M.
Historically, Newton's Book III, Proposition VII argues that gravitational attraction applies to all bodies and scales with their masses; its second corollary states the inverse-square law for individual particles. [ref]
