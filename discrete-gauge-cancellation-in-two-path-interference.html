Discrete gauge cancellation in two-path interference (WC​=∏(i,j)∈C​Uij​)

A four-vertex model proves the essential gauge invariance without solving an electron scattering problem. Use two directed paths S→U→D and S→L→D. Assign an edge phase aij​ and a complex unit link Uij​=eiaij​ to each directed edge, with Uji​=Uij−1​. These links represent discretized magnetic line integrals multiplied by q/ℏ. Under a gauge transformation with arbitrary vertex phases χi​,
aij′​=aij​+χj​−χi​,P1​=USU​UUD​,P2​=USL​ULD​.
(7)
Both path products acquire the same factor ei(χD​−χS​). Their gauge-invariant ratio is the closed-loop holonomy
WC​=P1​P2∗​=exp(i[aSU​+aUD​−aSL​−aLD​])=eiδ.
(8)
Every intermediate vertex phase cancels. For any fixed nonnegative real amplitudes a,b, the interference quantity ∣aP1​+bP2​∣2 is unchanged too. A gauge-dependent open-path phase consequently cannot be interpreted as an observable by itself.
For an exact example, measure phases in units of π and order edges as (SU,UD,SL,LD). Choose edge phases (0,0,0,1/2) and vertex phases (χS​,χU​,χL​,χD​)=(1/3,−1/4,2/5,1/7)π. The transformed edge phases are
(aSU′​,aUD′​,aSL′​,aLD′​)=(−127​,2811​,151​,7017​)π.
(9)
The upper and lower sums become −4π/21 and 13π/42; their difference remains −π/2. With amplitudes a=1, b=1/2, the intensity remains 5/4. Sweeping one physical loop phase through 2π instead produces extrema 9/4 and 1/4, agreeing with the interferometric visibility example above.
