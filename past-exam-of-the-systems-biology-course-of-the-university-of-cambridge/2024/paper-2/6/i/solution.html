(a): two-photon excitation; (b): one-photon/confocal excitation.​ The compact emitting region in (a) indicates that excitation itself is strongly localized. In (b), the illuminated path generates out-of-focus fluorescence as well as fluorescence near the focus. A confocal detection pinhole can reject much of that unwanted emission from the recorded image; it does not prevent the dye solution from emitting it.
In ordinary one-photon excitation, the local fluorescence-generation rate is approximately proportional to excitation light intensity I. In unsaturated two-photon excitation microscopy, it is proportional to I2. Simultaneous absorption is appreciable only in the high-intensity focal region, generally produced using a pulsed laser. Two lower-energy photons jointly excite the fluorophore; the excitation wavelength is therefore typically substantially longer than in its one-photon experiment. This reduces out-of-focus excitation and permits detection without a confocal pinhole.[ref]
For a small detection pinhole, the effective confocal point spread function is approximately
hconf​(r)∝hexc​(r)hdet​(r)​.
(1)
Here each factor denotes an intensity response. Excitation and detection jointly select the focal region. Enlarging the pinhole increases collected signal but weakens axial rejection and changes the effective point spread function. In two-photon excitation microscopy with broad collection, the spatial response is dominated by
h2p​(r)∝[hexc,2p​(r)]2​.
(2)
The pinhole product and excitation square are different physical mechanisms even when simplified mathematical profiles resemble one another.[ref]
Compare both lateral and axial widths using the actual wavelengths and objective. For a diffraction-limited system, their characteristic scalings are
δxy​∼NAλ​,δz​∼NA2nmed​λ​,
(3)
where NA is numerical aperture and nmed​ is the medium's refractive index. Numerical prefactors depend on the width criterion and high-aperture optical model. Axial resolution is generally worse than lateral resolution. A small confocal pinhole improves axial sectioning and can narrow the lateral response, at the cost of photon throughput.
As a simple Gaussian function example, h(x)=exp[−x2/(2σ2)] has squared profile h(x)2=exp[−x2/σ2], so its width is reduced by 1/2​. However, the two-photon illumination spot itself is broader at its longer excitation wavelength. Two-photon excitation is not automatically higher-resolution than confocal microscopy, and ordinary two-photon sectioning is not intrinsically super-resolution. Compare measured point spread functions, pinhole settings, numerical apertures, scattering and optical aberrations, not just the fact that one profile is squared.
