Gauss-Seidel iteration for a two-variable positive-definite system (A=(41​13​))

OurBigBook.com Words: 360 Articles: 1
Consider the original linear system
A(xy​)=b,A=(41​13​),b=(12​),(x∗​y∗​​)=(1/117/11​).
(98)
Direct substitution verifies the solution. The matrix is a symmetric positive-definite matrix: completing the square in its quadratic form gives 4x2+2xy+3y2=4(x+y/4)2+11y2/4>0 for every nonzero vector. It is also a strictly diagonally dominant matrix.
Starting from (x(0),y(0))=(0,0), update the first row and then the second:
x(k+1)=41−y(k)​,y(k+1)=32−x(k+1)​.
(99)
The first sweep gives x(1)=1/4 and y(1)=(2−1/4)/3=7/12. The next gives x(2)=(1−7/12)/4=5/48 and y(2)=(2−5/48)/3=91/144. The second row uses 1/4 during the first sweep, even though the initial first coordinate was zero.
With a stale first coordinate, the Jacobi method would instead give (1/4,2/3) after the first sweep. Both methods have the same fixed point, but changing when coordinates become available changes the entire sequence of vectors.
