Expected generation size in a Galton-Watson process (E[Zn​]=μn)

 Home Mathematics Area of mathematics Probability and statistics Galton-Watson process
OurBigBook.com Words: 242
For a Galton-Watson process starting with one ancestor and finite offspring mean μ, the expected value of the generation size is E[Zn​]=μn. At n=0 it is one, including when μ=0; zero offspring mean forces every later generation to be empty. With m initial ancestors, the expected value is mμn.
For p0​=p1​=1/4, p2​=1/2, the offspring mean is 5/4. The exact calculations used in the generation probability mass function figure under Galton-Watson process give:

Nearly half the families are already empty at generation six, yet the expected value exceeds three. Since empty families contribute zero,
E[Zn​∣Zn​>0]=1−qn​μn​
(95)
whenever qn​<1. At generation six this conditional expectation is about 7.092969. Conditioning on being nonempty now differs from conditioning on eventual survival: some presently nonempty families will still die out. The eventual branching extinction probability is 1/2, even as the unconditional expected value grows without bound.
The expected value is useful for computing average population counts and, in the subcritical case, the expected value of the total progeny of a branching process. Its simple formula comes from one local calculation: linearity of expectation and the independent and identically distributed offspring counts give
E[Zn+1​∣Zn​]=μZn​,E[Zn+1​]=μE[Zn​].
(96)
The tower property of conditional expectation gives the second equality; starting from E[Z0​]=1 gives the formula. It describes an average over the generation probability distribution, not deterministic growth of an individual family. [ref]
