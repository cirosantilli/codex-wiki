Electron microscope

 Home Physics Branches of physics Optics Electron optics
OurBigBook.com Words: 3k Articles: 48
An electron microscope forms a spatially enlarged representation of a specimen using electrons, electron lenses, and a detector. Its appeal is access to structure too fine for an ordinary optical microscope, but a short de Broglie wavelength alone does not guarantee fine electron microscope resolution. Increasing magnification can enlarge an existing blur without revealing any additional structure.
In transmission electron microscopy, an electron gun illuminates a thin specimen; magnetic electron lenses form and enlarge its transmitted image on a fluorescent screen or detector. A vacuum reduces collisions with gas. Lens electric currents control focusing and magnification.[ref]
