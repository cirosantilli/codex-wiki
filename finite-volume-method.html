Finite volume method

 Home Mathematics Area of mathematics Analysis Numerical analysis
OurBigBook.com Words: 1k Articles: 11
The finite volume method follows the amount of a conserved quantity inside each control volume. Its unknowns are cell averages, rather than values at isolated points: a cell's width times its stored average approximates that cell's total. A numerical flux transfers an amount across a face. Using one shared transfer for both neighboring cells makes the loss from one exactly the gain of the other, as explained in shared face flux cancellation.[ref]
For a one-dimensional scalar conservation law without sources, let ut​+∂x​f(u)=0, with conservation law flux f. Fix cells Ij​=[xj−1/2​,xj+1/2​] of widths hj​>0. Define the exact cell average and its numerical approximation by
uj​(t)=hj​1​∫Ij​​u(x,t)dx,Ujn​≈uj​(tn​).
(1)
The integral balance over one cell and one time interval of length k>0 is
hj​(uj​(tn​+k)−uj​(tn​))=∫tn​tn​+k​[f(u(xj−1/2​,t))−f(u(xj+1/2​,t))]dt.
(2)
For a smooth solution this follows by integration of the differential equation; with jump discontinuities, it expresses the conservation law in integral form, using appropriate face traces when they exist. Replacing each time-averaged face conservation law flux by a numerical flux gives
Ujn+1​=Ujn​−hj​k​(Fj+1/2n​−Fj−1/2n​).
(3)
Thus the finite volume method implements a balance directly, without needing a pointwise derivative at a jump. The tube balance in Riemann Problems and Jupyter Solutions by David I. Ketcheson, Randall J. LeVeque, and Mauricio J. del Razo (Advection, §Conservation of mass) motivates this construction from conservation of mass.
This is useful for shock waves: their speed is constrained by the Rankine-Hugoniot condition, itself an integral balance. A nonconservative approximation can give a visibly plausible jump with the wrong speed. Conservation is one requirement; a nonlinear scalar conservation law also needs the correct entropy solution, and the chosen numerical flux and update need their own stability and accuracy analysis.[ref] The original periodic upwind finite volume advection example isolates the shared-transfer mechanism in a problem with a known translated solution.

Shared face flux cancellation (Mn+1−Mn=k(F1/2n​−FN+1/2n​))

Words: 223
Finite volume method OurBigBook.com
In a finite volume method, orient every face numerical flux to the right. For cells 1,…,N, write the discrete total as Mn=∑j=1N​hj​Ujn​. If u is a mass per unit length, this total approximates mass; for a different conserved field, it measures that field's integral. Multiplying the update by the cell width gives an amount balance, so adjacent cells contribute
hj​(Ujn+1​−Ujn​)hj+1​(Uj+1n+1​−Uj+1n​)​=kFj−1/2n​−kFj+1/2n​,=kFj+1/2n​−kFj+3/2n​.​
(4)
The interior face contributes −kFj+1/2n​+kFj+1/2n​=0. Summing all cells gives
Mn+1−Mn=k(F1/2n​−FN+1/2n​).
(5)
With periodic boundary conditions, the two boundary labels denote the same physical face and must use the same numerical flux; then Mn+1=Mn. On an open interval the total changes by the boundary transfers. Unequal widths require the weighted total ∑hj​Uj​, not the unweighted sum of cell averages.
The diagram uses two unit-width cells, k=1/4, cell averages (2,1) and right-oriented numerical fluxes (1,3,2) at their three faces. The updated cell averages are (3/2,5/4): the total changes from 3 to 11/4, exactly k(1−2)=−1/4. The shared interior transfer k⋅3=3/4 cancels. If the receiving cell instead used 3+ε for that same face, the total would acquire the spurious term kε. Conservation is therefore an algebraic property of sharing the transfer, even when the numerical flux is a poor approximation. In floating-point arithmetic, independently rounded cell updates may still leave small roundoff errors in the computed total.

Periodic upwind finite volume advection (Ujn+1​=(1−ν)Ujn​+νUj−1n​)

Words: 379
Finite volume method OurBigBook.com
Consider the advection equation ut​+aux​=0 with constant speed a>0, no sources, and periodic boundary conditions on [0,1]. Use N equal cells of width h=1/N, time step k>0, and indices modulo N. The exact solution is the periodic translation u(x,t)=u0​(x−at). At a face, the Riemann problem transports the left state to the right, so the two-state upwind numerical flux is F(L,R)=aL, with F(U,U)=aU.[ref]
The finite volume method with this numerical flux and an explicit Euler method step gives
Fj+1/2n​=aUjn​,ν=hak​,Ujn+1​=(1−ν)Ujn​+νUj−1n​.
(6)
Here Ujn​ represents a cell average; the recurrence also occurs in the upwind finite difference scheme. Impose the specific CFL condition 0<ν≤1. Each updated cell average is then a convex combination of two old ones. This proves preservation of their initial minimum and maximum, including nonnegativity for nonnegative data, and contraction of the maximum norm of the difference of two solutions on this periodic grid. These statements concern this constant-speed scalar update with this time step.
For an original synthetic example, take N=8, a=1, h=1/8, k=1/16, hence Courant number ν=1/2. Choose a function constant on each successive cell, with the following values, so these are exact initial cell averages. One step gives
jUj0​Uj1​​001/2​100​221​322​413/2​501/2​600​711/2​​M0=M1=81​⋅6=43​.
(7)
For example, cell zero receives from cell seven across the periodic face: U01​=(0+1)/2=1/2. All eight face transfers cancel in the total by shared face flux cancellation. More generally this periodic recurrence conserves the total algebraically for any ν, even though the CFL condition is needed for the convex combination argument. With the same initial data and ν=3/2, one step gives U21​=−1 and U41​=5/2, despite preserving the total.
After four steps, t=1/4, the exact translated profile has moved two cells to the right. Repeated averaging instead gives the explicit binomial coefficient formula
Uj4​=161​(Uj0​+4Uj−10​+6Uj−20​+4Uj−30​+Uj−40​),
(8)
with all indices modulo eight. The diagram compares these computed cell averages with the exact translated cell averages. Their integrals agree, but the numerical jumps spread across cells. The finite volume method is useful for jump discontinuities because it retains the integral balance even at a jump; conservation alone does not guarantee a sharp profile. At ν=1 this particular update is an exact one-cell shift, whereas for 0<ν<1 it mixes neighboring states. No accuracy conclusion at a jump follows just from the conserved total.

Monotone conservative scheme

Words: 351 Articles: 6
Finite volume method OurBigBook.com
A scalar finite-volume update is monotone if increasing any input state cannot decrease any output state. A consistent conservative update preserves constants and has an invariant interval by comparison. On a periodic grid, or an infinite grid with summable perturbations, conservation and monotonicity imply L1 contraction of a monotone conservative scheme. Translation invariance then implies the total variation diminishing scheme property.

Total variation diminishing scheme (TV(TU)≤TV(U))

Words: 39
Monotone conservative scheme OurBigBook.com
A translation-invariant conservative monotone update decreases discrete total variation. If S is the one-cell shift, TS=ST, and L1 contraction of a monotone conservative scheme applied to U,SU gives ∑j​∣(TU)j+1​−(TU)j​∣≤∑j​∣Uj+1​−Uj​∣. This prevents creation of new total variation, though it does not make every discontinuity sharply resolved.

L1 contraction of a monotone conservative scheme (∥TU−TV∥1​≤∥U−V∥1​)

Let T be a monotone conservative scalar update on a periodic grid. Since T(U∨V)≥TU,TV, one has (TU−TV)+​≤T(U∨V)−TV. Conservation makes the sum of the right side equal to ∑(U−V)+​. Reverse U,V and add to obtain ∥TU−TV∥1​≤∥U−V∥1​. On an infinite grid the same proof holds for summable differences with a Lipschitz flux on the state range.

Engquist-Osher method

Words: 199 Articles: 3
Monotone conservative scheme OurBigBook.com
The Engquist-Osher method is the finite volume method using the Engquist-Osher flux. Under (k/d)sup∣f′∣≤1 on the invariant state interval, its update is monotone. It preserves the state range, is contractive for summable perturbations in L1, and decreases discrete total variation.

CFL necessity for explicit Engquist-Osher stability (hk​sup∣f′∣≤1)

Words: 61
Engquist-Osher method OurBigBook.com
Monotonicity of the explicit update requires (k/h)sup[m,M]​∣f′∣≤1 on its invariant state interval. Convexity and a unique sonic point of a scalar flux do not remove this time-step restriction. For f(u)=u2/2, linearization about a positive constant U gives an upwind finite difference scheme for advection equation: vjn+1​=(1−rU)vjn​+rUvj−1n​, where r=k/h. At Fourier frequency π the multiplier is 1−2rU, whose modulus exceeds one when rU>1. Thus a CFL condition is essential to the claimed nonlinear stability.

Engquist-Osher flux (FEO​(a,b))

Words: 102 Articles: 1
Engquist-Osher method OurBigBook.com
The Engquist-Osher numerical flux is F(a,b)=f(0)+∫0a​max(f′(s),0)ds+∫0b​min(f′(s),0)ds. It sends positive-speed contributions from the left state and negative-speed contributions from the right state. Its consistency F(u,u)=f(u) and opposite monotonicities in the two arguments yield a monotone conservative scheme under the appropriate Courant–Friedrichs–Lewy condition. For the Inviscid Burgers equation, it is 21​max(a,0)2+21​min(b,0)2.
Sonic-point splitting of a convex Engquist-Osher flux (F(a,b)=f(s∗​)+f+(a)+f−(b))
Words: 59
Engquist-Osher flux OurBigBook.com
For a flux that is a differentiable convex function with unique minimum at s∗​, define f+(u)=f(u)−f(s∗​) for u>s∗​ and zero otherwise, and f−(u)=f(u)−f(s∗​) for u<s∗​ and zero otherwise. The Engquist-Osher flux is F(a,b)=f(s∗​)+f+(a)+f−(b). Thus transsonic rarefaction wave states a≤s∗​≤b give F=f(s∗​), while reversed transsonic states give F=f(a)+f(b)−f(s∗​). This follows by integrating the positive and negative parts of f′.

Numerical flux (F(a,b))

Words: 266 Articles: 1
Finite volume method OurBigBook.com
A numerical flux estimates the rate of transfer across a cell face from nearby states. In the finite volume method update, Fj+1/2n​ approximates the conservation law flux averaged over the time step; kFj+1/2n​ is the transferred amount. For a two-state numerical flux F(L,R) approximating a scalar conservation law with conservation law flux f, consistency of a numerical method requires F(U,U)=f(U). Thus equal states recover the physical transfer rate. With constant equal-width cells, this also preserves a constant state because its incoming and outgoing numerical fluxes agree.[ref]
Consistency and shared face flux cancellation answer different questions: matching f at equal states approximates the intended equation, while sharing one oriented face value preserves the total. The example periodic upwind finite volume advection uses F(L,R)=aL for a>0, so the arriving state comes from the left. The Godunov numerical flux uses an entropy solution of a local Riemann problem; the Engquist-Osher flux provides a different construction for a scalar conservation law. A monotone conservative scheme uses a numerical flux nondecreasing in its left state and nonincreasing in its right state, together with a time step that makes the complete update monotone. Neither two-state consistency nor conservation by itself supplies that time-step restriction or a general stability or order of a numerical method guarantee.

Godunov numerical flux (FG​(a,b))

Words: 97
Numerical flux OurBigBook.com
For a scalar conservation law whose flux is a convex function, the Godunov numerical flux is the physical flux at the origin of the entropy solution of the Riemann problem between states a,b. It is minu∈[a,b]​f(u) for a≤b and maxu∈[b,a]​f(u) for a>b. For a rarefaction wave, the characteristic speed is monotone across its states, so the origin sees either an endpoint or a zero-speed minimizing state. For a shock wave, its speed is (f(a)−f(b))/(a−b); the origin sees the left state if this speed is positive and the right state if negative, selecting the larger endpoint flux. Unlike the Godunov flux, the Engquist-Osher flux at reversed transsonic states may equal f(a)+f(b)−f(s∗​).
