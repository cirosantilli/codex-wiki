Chromatography

 Home Chemistry
OurBigBook.com Words: 3k Articles: 25
Chromatography separates components of a mixture through different migration rates in contact with a chromatographic stationary phase while a chromatographic mobile phase transports them. Liquid chromatography describes the physical state of the transporting phase; size-exclusion chromatography describes a separation mechanism. Classification by phase and classification by mechanism answer different questions. In ideal size-exclusion chromatography, differential access to liquid-filled pores replaces binding as the source of differential migration.
In many other forms of chromatography, a solute repeatedly leaves the chromatographic mobile phase and returns to it. Reversible adsorption holds it at a surface; partition distributes it between the chromatographic mobile phase and an immobilized liquid chromatographic stationary phase. Spending more time stationary means less progress with the moving fluid. Two initially overlapping components can therefore emerge as separate bands without either being permanently trapped. Chromatography is useful both for comparing a sample with reference substances and for collecting separated components; a visible band need not contain just one substance.
Relative retention belongs to the sample together with the chosen chromatographic stationary phase, chromatographic mobile phase, eluent composition, and temperature. Changing these conditions can change the separation order. Chemical polarity helps explain interactions in some systems but is not a universal ranking rule. In thin-layer chromatography, a spot's TLC retardation factor is consequently conditional evidence about identity, rather than a fixed identifier intrinsic to a molecule.

Chromatographic mobile phase

The chromatographic mobile phase carries solutes through the apparatus. In liquid chromatography it is a liquid solvent or chemical solution.
The eluent is the supplied chromatographic mobile phase. Its composition can alter both solute solubility and competition for adsorption sites, so changing the eluent can change retention even with the same chromatographic stationary phase.

Chromatographic stationary phase

Words: 57
Chromatography OurBigBook.com
The chromatographic stationary phase remains in place during separation. A porous packing in size-exclusion chromatography supplies internal liquid regions with size-dependent accessibility; ideal separation requires no adsorption of the solute to the packing.
A chromatographic stationary phase may instead expose a solid surface for adsorption, or retain an immobilized liquid into which solutes partition. In ordinary silica gel thin-layer chromatography, interactions with the surface delay migration. This mechanism differs from ideal SEC pore access.

Chromatographic partition coefficient (K=cs​/cm​)

Words: 271
Chromatography OurBigBook.com
For a dilute solute distributed between two liquid regions at local chemical equilibrium, define the chromatographic partition coefficient as
K=cm​cs​​,n=cm​Vm​+cs​Vs​=cm​(Vm​+KVs​),fm​=ncm​Vm​​=Vm​+KVs​Vm​​.
(1)
Here cm​,cs​ are concentrations expressed in the same units, Vm​,Vs​ specify accessible volume in the chromatographic mobile phase and immobilized liquid chromatographic stationary phase, respectively, n is total solute amount of substance, and fm​ is the fraction of that amount of substance in the chromatographic mobile phase. Assume a constant chromatographic partition coefficient, rapid exchange, unchanged phases and temperature, and no chemical reaction. A chemical equilibrium population still exchanges molecules; it is not permanently divided into two sets.
The amount of substance balance proves the expression for fm​. Increasing either K or Vs​/Vm​ reduces the mobile fraction, illustrating why a concentration ratio alone cannot determine retention. For surface adsorption, specify an adsorption storage law instead of assigning an arbitrary liquid volume to the solid.
An original discrete model makes the separation visible. In each identical cell, first establish local chemical equilibrium, then advance only the mobile fraction by one cell; let the stationary fraction wait. Repeat twelve times, without any additional spreading. With Vs​=Vm​, stipulated chromatographic partition coefficients KA​=1/3 and KB​=3 give fA​=3/4 and fB​=1/4. Each cycle contributes mean displacement fm​ cells, so the mean positions after twelve cycles are nine cells and three cells. Redistribution itself broadens each band: the cell positions have a binomial distribution with parameters 12,fm​. These dimensionless chosen values illustrate transport, not measurements of any particular substances.

Thin-layer chromatography

Words: 560 Articles: 1
Chromatography OurBigBook.com
Thin-layer chromatography spreads a thin chromatographic stationary phase on a supporting plate. A small sample spot starts on a pencil origin line. The lower edge contacts the eluent, whose level must stay below that origin; capillary action draws the chromatographic mobile phase upward. Components exchange with the chromatographic stationary phase as the wet region advances. The leading wet edge is the solvent front. Mark it after removing the plate, before solvent evaporation erases it.[ref]
Juniata College's Science in Motion protocol grinds vegetable tissue with about 5mL of acetone, transfers the extract, adds about 2mL of hexane, and specifies sampling the upper layer after separation. It spots a silica gel plate with a capillary tube, drying between repeated applications; a reference pigment occupies another lane. A closed jar contains about 0.5cm depth of 70:30 hexane–acetone eluent, below the pencil origin. The plate is removed before the front reaches the top; the front is marked immediately. Spot centres and front are measured from the origin.[ref]
The protocol's standard provides a comparison control. It specifies no added water in this extraction and reports no measured plate positions or temperature. Its tabulated pigment values are guidance, not observations from this run.[ref]
A useful additional control is an eluent blank, checking contamination. Compare sample and standard on the same plate, and optionally apply both together at a third origin position. Agreement under identical conditions supports a proposed identity but cannot prove it: different substances can migrate together. A second eluent giving different selectivity strengthens the comparison. One spot cannot establish purity, and a missing visible spot can reflect poor detection rather than absence. Overloading, broad spots, or an uneven solvent front also weaken the readout. These are interpretation checks, not extra observations claimed for the protocol.

TLC retardation factor (Rf​)

Words: 266
Thin-layer chromatography OurBigBook.com
For thin-layer chromatography, define the TLC retardation factor using distances along the development direction:
Rf​=dfront​dspot​​=yfront​−y0​yspot​−y0​​.
(2)
The origin coordinate is y0​; use the centre of a resolved spot and a front marked at the end of development. Both distances begin at the origin, never at the plate bottom. The ratio is dimensionless. An immobile spot has Rf​=0; a spot at the front has Rf​=1, where separation from other fast components may be poor. This distance ratio is distinct from the chromatographic partition coefficient and from a column retention time.
For an original stylized plate, choose absolute vertical coordinates y0​=1cm, yfront​=11cm, yA​=4cm and yB​=8cm above the plate bottom. The solvent distance is therefore 10cm, while the spot distances are 3cm and 7cm:
Rf,A​=11−14−1​=0.3,Rf,B​=11−18−1​=0.7.
(3)
Using 4/11 or 8/11 would wrongly include the unused centimetre below the origin. These chosen positions do not represent measured pigments or the protocol's plate dimensions.
The TLC retardation factor depends on the sample, chromatographic stationary phase, eluent composition, and temperature; plate handling and development conditions must also match when comparing runs. The moving wet front and possible surface adsorption require their own transport assumptions. The equilibrium mobile fraction fm​ in the chromatographic partition coefficient model is not unconditionally equal to Rf​; even that model depends on the phase volume ratio. Coincident TLC retardation factors alone never prove chemical identity.

Size-exclusion chromatography

Words: 2k Articles: 11
Liquid chromatography OurBigBook.com
Size-exclusion chromatography separates dissolved molecules by their hydrodynamic size in the chosen solvent. A larger species that cannot enter the packing's pores travels with the liquid between particles and elutes earlier. Smaller species explore more internal liquid and elute later. The column is not a sieve that permanently traps the large species: exclusion makes them leave first. “Gel filtration” is common for aqueous separations; “gel permeation chromatography” is common for organic-solvent analysis of polymers. SEC molar mass calibration translates size-dependent elution into a standard-relative estimate of molar mass, rather than a universal absolute measurement.
This makes SEC useful for separating proteins from small dissolved species, comparing protein aggregation, and characterizing dissolved polymer chains. The residual-group monitoring of polyurethane formation experiment already illustrates polymer calibration: its reported molar masses are relative to polystyrene standards. Size-exclusion characterization of chain polymers explains why an elution trace alone cannot count chains or establish their growth mechanism.

Size-exclusion chromatography with multi-angle light scattering

Words: 27
Size-exclusion chromatography OurBigBook.com
Size-exclusion chromatography with multi-angle light scattering combines separation by hydrodynamic behaviour with light-scattering and concentration measurements. With appropriate optical parameters it estimates molar mass across a peak, helping distinguish assembly from shape-dependent elution.

SEC pore access

Words: 167
Size-exclusion chromatography OurBigBook.com
In ideal SEC pore access, geometric exclusion changes the fraction of pore liquid a solute can occupy. Diffusion permits entry into and exit from accessible pores; the through-flow chiefly transports the sample between packing particles. A hydrodynamic radius is a useful size descriptor, but actual pore entry also depends on shape and pore geometry. A flexible polymer coil is not a rigid ball with a perfectly sharp entry diameter.
The extra delay is best understood as extra accessible storage, rather than a prescribed longer streamline. Pore visits are reversible and random. An entirely excluded species samples the SEC void volume; a sufficiently small nonbinding species samples the SEC total accessible liquid volume. Intermediate species lie between these limits. Larger hydrodynamic size generally means earlier elution within the column's SEC fractionation range.

SEC void volume (V0​)

Words: 48
Size-exclusion chromatography OurBigBook.com
The SEC void volume V0​ is the interparticle liquid volume, operationally measured by a sufficiently large excluded, nonbinding tracer. It excludes pore liquid. Choose a tracer that does not undergo adsorption or change the packing; correct the measured elution for tubing and detector delay before identifying it with a column-only V0​.

SEC total accessible liquid volume (Vt​=V0​+Vi​)

Words: 46
Size-exclusion chromatography OurBigBook.com
The SEC total accessible liquid volume is Vt​=V0​+Vi​: interparticle plus pore liquid. A fully included nonbinding tracer estimates it. Geometric bed volume instead includes solid matrix, Vc​=Vt​+Vmatrix​.[ref]

SEC partition coefficient (Kd​=(Ve​−V0​)/(Vt​−V0​))

Words: 254
Size-exclusion chromatography OurBigBook.com
For ideal SEC, define the SEC partition coefficient by
Kd​=cinterparticle​cpore​​,Ve​=V0​+Kd​Vi​,Kd​=Vt​−V0​Ve​−V0​​.
(4)
Here cpore​ averages solute concentration over all pore liquid, including inaccessible regions. Ve​ is column elution volume. Pure steric exclusion gives 0≤Kd​≤1; the endpoints mean exclusion and full access.[ref]
A storage argument explains the relation. Assume a dilute stable solute, unchanged packing and solvent, constant volumetric flow rate Q, rapid local chemical equilibrium between pore liquid and interparticle liquid, negligible binding, and negligible transport through the pores by bulk flow. At interparticle concentration c, the column holds an amount of substance n=cV0​+Kd​cVi​. Its exit transports Qc per unit time. The ratio n/(Qc) is the mean residence time in this equilibrium transport model; multiplying by Q gives Ve​=V0​+Kd​Vi​. For a narrow symmetric SEC chromatogram peak, the peak maximum approximates that mean; a strongly asymmetric peak needs an explicitly stated location convention.
The handbook also uses an operational normalization
Kav​=Vc​−V0​Ve​−V0​​=Kd​Vi​+Vmatrix​Vi​​.
(5)
This denominator includes solid matrix, so full access gives Kav​<1 when Vmatrix​>0. It differs from the pore-liquid SEC partition coefficient. Read each source's volume convention before comparing coefficients.[ref]
For an original numerical example, take V0​=5mL, Vi​=8mL, and Vmatrix​=3mL. Then Vt​=13mL and Vc​=16mL. Elution at 9mL gives Kd​=1/2 but Kav​=4/11. Even a fully included tracer gives Kav​=8/11, not 1. These are stipulated volumes, not experimental measurements.

SEC fractionation range

Words: 56
Size-exclusion chromatography OurBigBook.com
The SEC fractionation range has differential SEC pore access. Larger excluded species share Ve​≃V0​; fully included species share Ve​≃Vt​. Neither group can be size-ranked by elution. A broader pore distribution can trade separation per size increment for range.[ref]

SEC molar mass calibration

Words: 242
Size-exclusion chromatography OurBigBook.com
SEC molar mass calibration measures standards under the same column, solvent, and temperature conditions as the unknown. Over a suitable fractionation interval, a common empirical approximation is
log10​(Mref​Mapp​​)=a−bVe​,b>0,
(6)
where Mref​ specifies the molar mass unit and b has inverse-volume units. Interpolation is conditional on the standards' size–molar mass relationship. SEC measures access associated with hydrodynamic size, so a branched polymer, an extended chain, and a compact protein need not share that relationship.
In this model the 80,000, 40,000, and 20,000gmol−1 standards have hydrodynamic radii 22​, 2, and 2​nm and elute at 7, 9, and 11mL. An unknown at 8mL would be assigned Mapp​=40,0002​gmol−1 by this calibration. But a 40,000gmol−1 species that expands from 2 to 22​nm elutes at 7mL and is assigned 80,000gmol−1 without gaining any mass. The numbers demonstrate ambiguity, rather than predict a real unfolding transition.
Changes in conformational isomerism, protein folding, protein denaturation, and protein aggregation can therefore shift peaks. Association usually increases size; compaction can reduce size at unchanged molar mass. Adsorption creates a different ambiguity: delayed elution need not indicate a smaller species. A different solvent can change both polymer coil dimensions and interactions with the packing. The SEC protein conformation experiment tests these alternatives with additional detectors.

SEC chromatogram

Words: 173 Articles: 1
Size-exclusion chromatography OurBigBook.com
A SEC chromatogram plots a detector signal against elapsed time or elution volume. At constant volumetric flow rate Q, the volume delivered after injection is Q(t−tinjection​); subtraction of extra-column transport volume is needed for a column-only Ve​. Real peak widths include injection volume, column dispersion, and tubing/detector spreading. Peak position, width, and area answer different questions.
SEC detector response
Words: 118
SEC chromatogram OurBigBook.com
A SEC detector response must be specified before interpreting a peak area. Ultraviolet absorbance depends on a species' response factor and concentration, as described by the Beer-Lambert law; differential refractive index detection depends on how the dissolved species changes refractive index. Equal amounts need not produce equal signal areas across unlike species.
Even a calibrated mass-proportional trace is not a chain-count probability distribution. In an original two-population example, equal recovered masses of chains with molar masses 20,000 and 80,000gmol−1 contain four times as many chains in the lighter population. Thus equal signal areas, assuming equal response per unit mass, correspond to number fractions 4/5 and 1/5: their number-average molar mass is 32,000gmol−1, while their weight-average molar mass is 50,000gmol−1. Converting a continuous trace additionally requires response correction and the appropriate change of variables between elution and molar mass bins.

SEC light-scattering detection

Words: 529 Articles: 1
Size-exclusion chromatography OurBigBook.com
SEC light-scattering detection adds information from scattering of light to the separation. Static scattering with a concentration readout can infer a weight-average molar mass for material in an eluting slice without the standard-family retention calibration. Dynamic scattering estimates a diffusion coefficient and hence a hydrodynamic radius. “Absolute” here means independent of a retention-to-molar mass standard curve; it still needs calibrated detectors, optical response parameters, and a valid sample model. Coeluting species produce a mixture-weighted result, rather than individually identified molecules.
A concrete manufacturer test used a Bio SEC-3 300A˚ column, 7.8×300mm with 3μm particles, an Agilent 1260 Infinity G5611A quaternary pump and G5667A autosampler, a 10mm diode-array flow cell, and dual-angle/dynamic scattering detectors. A buffer solution at pH 7.4 flowed at 0.75mLmin−1; column and scattering detector were at 30∘C. Absorbance at 280nm supplied a concentration-sensitive readout. Dilution and loading comparisons tested response: seventeen ovalbumin injections spanning 2–960μg gave a reported average 47,198gmol−1 with 2% relative standard deviation. The note reports poorer separation of thyroglobulin from aggregates and an elevated mass estimate, illustrating the coelution limit. Its 0.2μm sample filtration also limits interpretation of larger protein aggregation products removed before injection.[ref]
SEC protein conformation experiment
Words: 317
SEC light-scattering detection OurBigBook.com
An authored manufacturer experiment used an Agilent 1260 Infinity Bio-inert Quaternary LC with Bio SEC-3 300A˚ columns, 7.8mm diameter and 150 or 300mm length. Four pump channels mixed water, sodium chloride, and two phosphate solutions into 20mmolL−1 phosphate at pH 7.0, varying salt from 0.2 to 0.6molL−1. At 1.0mLmin−1 and 30∘C, the readouts were diode-array absorbance, differential refractive index, and scattering at 15∘ and 90∘. A supplied protein standard mixture calibrated retention; bovine serum albumin calibrated scattering constants and inter-detector delay. Increasing salt improved separation of two α-chymotrypsinogen A peaks; two 300mm columns also resolved peaks that one column did not. At 0.5molL−1 salt, the two scattering-derived estimates were about 26,100 and 25,800gmol−1. The author inferred a possible size-changing conformation difference, rather than doubling of molar mass. The same poster shows delayed, tailing lysozyme peaks from unwanted interactions.[ref]
The salt comparison and the independent mass readout constrain interpretation, but do not uniquely prove a particular protein structure. Salt can change protein folding, protein aggregation, and packing interactions together. In ideal SEC there is no adsorption; attraction from the hydrophobic effect or forces described by electrostatics can cause additional retention, tailing, and loss of recovered sample. Repulsion can instead reduce pore entry. A peak beyond Vt​ contradicts the simple nonbinding model, rather than extending its size calibration.
Useful proposed controls compare a solvent blank, fresh calibration standards bracketing the sample, several injection amounts and volumes, and recovered sample quantity with injected quantity using an appropriately calibrated detector. Keep temperature and volumetric flow rate fixed while testing pH or ionic strength. Compare an independent mass readout before declaring an early peak an aggregate. Loss of an insoluble species during filtration, on-column association or dissociation, overloading, and broad unresolved peaks can all hide the original sample population. These are interpretation checks and limitations, not measurements invented for the figures.
