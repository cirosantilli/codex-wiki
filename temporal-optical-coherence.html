Temporal optical coherence (g(1)(τ))

 ... Physics Branches of physics Optics Physical optics Optical coherence First-order optical coherence
OurBigBook.com Words: 1k Articles: 3
Temporal optical coherence compares a field at one position with a delayed copy. In a Michelson interferometer, unequal round-trip lengths supply this delay. Even a source with rapidly changing wave phase can give high interferometric visibility close to zero delay, because both arms then deliver nearly the same source history. A source narrowed in optical spectrum generally retains correlation over larger delays; a single width does not describe sources with several separated lines. [ref].
For a stationary scalar field, choose the carrier convention e−i2πνt, with frequency ν in cycles per second. Let S(ν)≥0 be its spectral density per unit frequency, with 0<∫S(ν)dν<∞. Its normalized field correlation is the Fourier transform
g(1)(τ)=∫S(ν)dν∫S(ν)e−i2πντdν​.
(81)
The optical spectrum weights the rotating contributions; a broad spread of frequencies allows them to cancel at short delays. This is the stationary field correlation–spectrum relation. The relevant spectrum is that of the optical field in the detected mode, rather than the low-frequency fluctuations of a detector's light intensity. [ref].
Here are two stipulated line profiles, each centered at ν0​ with full width at half maximum Δν>0. Write f=ν−ν0​ and use normalized profiles on the whole detuning line:
pL​(f)=π[f2+(Δν/2)2]Δν/2​,pG​(f)=π​Δν2ln2​​exp(−4ln2Δν2f2​).
(82)
The first is a Lorentzian, mathematically a Cauchy distribution; the second is a Gaussian function. Taking their Fourier transforms gives, with the carrier factor e−i2πν0​τ omitted from the magnitudes,
∣gL(1)​(τ)∣=e−πΔν∣τ∣,∣gG(1)​(τ)∣=exp(−4ln2π2Δν2τ2​).
(83)
These are calculated model results, not measured line profiles. The detuning approximation treats the narrow line as extending to arbitrarily large positive and negative f; for optical applications require Δν≪ν0​. Exact spectra of the complex positive-frequency field have no negative absolute frequencies. Changing to angular frequency replaces Δν by Δω/(2π); it must not silently preserve the same numerical constant.
Equal detected single-arm light intensities give interferometric visibility equal to the correlation magnitude. Doubling the Gaussian full width at half maximum from 1 to 2GHz halves every fixed-contrast delay. At τ=0.5ns the model gives Gaussian interferometric visibility approximately 0.410686 at 1GHz and 0.0284471 at 2GHz; the 1GHz Lorentzian gives 0.207880. The dashed curve instead keeps the 1GHz Gaussian correlation and changes I2​/I1​ to 1/4, multiplying all interferometric visibilities by 0.8. Its lower contrast at zero delay therefore comes entirely from intensity imbalance.
