Fisher information in an exponential survival snapshot (IT​(λ)=nT2/(eλT−1))

 ... Area of mathematics Probability and statistics Statistical model Statistical modelling Informant function Fisher information matrix
OurBigBook.com Words: 187
For n independent exponential lifetimes with rate λ, recording all lifetimes gives Fisher information n/λ2. Recording only the count alive at a fixed time T>0 gives a binomial distribution with success probability s=e−λT. Its information in s is n/[s(1−s)], and the change of parameter ds/dλ=−Ts gives the displayed information.
The retained fraction is R(z)=z2/(ez−1), z=λT. It is strictly below one, so every single snapshot loses information. The inequality follows, for instance, from h(z)=ez−1−z2: its derivative is ez−2z>0 for positive z, because that derivative has its minimum at z=log2 with value 2−2log2>0. Also R(z) tends to zero as z↓0 and as z→∞.
Differentiating R shows that the best snapshot time solves 2(1−e−z)=z. The difference of the two sides is strictly concave, starts at zero with positive derivative, and eventually becomes negative, so it has exactly one positive root, approximately 1.59362. The largest retained fraction is about 0.64761. Thus even an optimally timed snapshot keeps less than two thirds of the full lifetime information. This is a local design result: choosing that time requires a nominal rate or a preceding estimate.
