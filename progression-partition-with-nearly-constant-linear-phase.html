Progression partition with nearly constant linear phase

 ... Mathematics Area of mathematics Combinatorics Additive combinatorics Normalized Fourier analysis on a finite abelian group Linear phase
OurBigBook.com Words: 108
For a linear phase e2πiθx on [n], set Q=⌊n​⌋. The Dirichlet approximation theorem gives 1≤q≤Q with ∥qθ∥R/Z​≤Q−1. If 1≤L and 2L≤Q, split each residue chain modulo q into arithmetic progressions of lengths between L and 2L−1, merging the final remainder into the preceding block. Each chain has at least Q points, so this is possible. On each resulting arithmetic progression, the linear phase varies from its first value by at most 4πL/Q. This converts a large Fourier coefficient of a zero-sum real function into positive mean on one block: freezing the linear phase controls the weighted block sums, and their positive and negative total masses agree.
