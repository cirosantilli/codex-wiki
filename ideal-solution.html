Ideal solution

 Home Chemistry Mixture Solution (chemistry)
OurBigBook.com Words: 1k Articles: 6
An ideal solution is a chemical solution whose component chemical potentials depend on composition through
μi​(T,P,{xj​})=μi∗​(T,P)+RTlnxi​,xi​=∑j​nj​ni​​.
(8)
Here ni​ is an amount of substance, xi​ its mole fraction, R the molar gas constant, and μi∗​ the pure-component liquid reference at the same temperature T and pressure P. This model applies to every component across the composition range; it is stronger than an approximation valid only near a pure solvent.[ref]
With this pure-liquid standard state convention, the thermodynamic activity satisfies μi​=μi∗​+RTlnai​, so an ideal solution has ai​=xi​, or activity coefficient γi​=ai​/xi​=1.[ref] This statement concerns the liquid. Assuming that a coexisting vapor is an ideal gas mixture is an additional approximation. Its mole fractions yi​ generally differ from the liquid mole fractions xi​.
For an original two-component example, stipulate T=300K and pure-component vapor pressures pA∗​=80kPa and pB∗​=20kPa. A and B are invented distinguishable components; these numbers are chosen model inputs, not material data. Combine an ideal solution with an ideal gas vapor, neglect the pure-liquid reference's pressure correction, and exclude other gases. At liquid mole fractions xA​=0.4 and xB​=0.6, Raoult's law and addition of partial pressures give
pA​=0.4(80)=32kPa,pB​=0.6(20)=12kPa,P=pA​+pB​=44kPa,
(9)
yA​=PpA​​=4432​=118​,yB​=113​.
(10)
Thus the coexisting vapor contains about 72.73% A by amount of substance, although the liquid contains only 40%. The total pressure is neither pure-component vapor pressure, and xA​ cannot be substituted for yA​. This enrichment is the basis of distillation.
The usefulness of the ideal solution is that one composition law connects chemical potentials, vapor-liquid equilibrium, entropy of mixing and Gibbs free energy of mixing. Its liquid components can interact strongly: the defining condition is their composition dependence, rather than the absence of all interactions as in an ideal gas model.
