Vector (v∈V)

 Home Mathematics Area of mathematics Algebra Linear algebra Vector space
OurBigBook.com Words: 1k Articles: 12
A vector is an element of a vector space. Its defining operations are vector addition and scalar multiplication. An arrow in a Euclidean space is one example; a polynomial or a function can also be a vector when the chosen vector space uses the corresponding addition and scalar multiplication.
For instance, the polynomials p(x)=1+x and q(x)=x−x2 belong to the real vector space of polynomials of degree at most two. Their vector addition gives (p+q)(x)=1+2x−x2, and scalar multiplication gives (2p)(x)=2+2x. In the monomial basis (1,x,x2) these are the coordinate vectors (1,1,0), (0,1,−1), (1,2,−1) and (2,2,0) respectively. The vectors are the polynomials; these tuples are their representations in a chosen basis.
A general vector space does not itself prescribe lengths or angles. A norm supplies a size, while an inner product also supplies angle information and orthogonality. The familiar arrow geometry below belongs to Euclidean vectors, where this extra structure is available.
