Let a<i<b and a<j<b be integers. A simple symmetric random walk started at i exits the integer interval at τ=inf{n≥0:Sn​∈{a,b}}. Its expected occupation of j before exit, including its initial state, is
Ga,b​(i,j)=Ei​∑k=0τ−1​1{Sk​=j}​=b−a2((i∧j)−a)(b−(i∨j))​.
(19)
A block of b−a consecutive positive steps forces exit, so the geometric tail bound from a uniform escape probability proves τ<∞ almost surely. Optional stopping at the bounded stopping time n∧τ, followed by the bounded convergence theorem for Sn∧τ​∈[a,b], gives the gambler's ruin probabilities
Pi​(Sτ​=a)=b−ab−i​,Pi​(Sτ​=b)=b−ai−a​.
(20)
Translate the absolute value martingale of a simple symmetric random walk from zero to j. Bounded-time optional stopping yields
Ei​∑k<n∧τ​1{Sk​=j}​=Ei​∣Sn∧τ​−j∣−∣i−j∣.
(21)
Use monotone convergence on the increasing visit count and the bounded convergence theorem on the right. Substituting the exit probabilities gives
Ga,b​(i,j)=b−a(b−i)(j−a)+(i−a)(b−j)​−∣i−j∣,
(22)
which simplifies to the displayed expression by considering i≤j and i≥j. In particular, Ga,b​(0,0)=−2ab/(b−a) when a<0<b.
The proof establishes finite expected value of the visit count. Therefore each translated stopped absolute-value martingale is dominated in absolute value by a constant plus that total count, an integrable random variable; its family of values is uniformly integrable. This supplies the limit justification without assuming uniform integrability in advance.
