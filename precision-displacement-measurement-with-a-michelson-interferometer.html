Precision displacement measurement with a Michelson interferometer

 ... Optics Physical optics Optical coherence First-order optical coherence Temporal optical coherence Michelson interferometer
OurBigBook.com Words: 650
A Michelson interferometer turns displacement into a count of interference fringes: a moving mirror changes both the outward and return paths. This lets a wavelength act as a ruler whose divisions need no engraving. Assume normal incidence, a fixed reference arm, stable refractive index n along the moving arm, and fixed vacuum wavelength λ0​. Write λ=λ0​/n for the wavelength in that medium and take ΔL positive away from the beam splitter. The change in optical path difference, the accumulated phase difference, and the signed number of complete interference fringe cycles are
ΔD=2nΔL,Δϕ=λ0​2πΔD​=λ4πΔL​,ΔN=2πΔϕ​=λ2ΔL​,ΔL=2λΔN​.
(55)
The factor two comes from propagation geometry: moving the mirror by ΔL adds ΔL before reflection and ΔL afterward. One bright-to-bright cycle means mirror travel λ/2; bright-to-dark means λ/4, half a cycle. All lengths must use compatible units, and ΔN is dimensionless. A constant reflection phase difference changes the initial fringe position but cancels from this change formula.
For an original hypothetical example, suppose λ=600nm in the actual surrounding medium and a continuous scan gives 40.25 cycles, including the final fractional cycle. Then ΔL=(600nm)(40.25)/2=12075nm=12.075μm. With the wavelength treated as exact, a fringe-reading measurement uncertainty of 0.02 cycle contributes 6nm to the displacement measurement uncertainty. An uncertainty of 0.01rad in a local phase difference instead corresponds to 600(0.01)/(4π)nm≈0.477nm. This illustrates measurement resolution, not a complete claim of accuracy: wavelength calibration, air changes, mirror tilt and missed cycles can dominate.
To separate the oscillating readout from its optical coherence envelope, use the local model
I(D)=Iˉ[1+V(D)cos(λ0​2πD​+ϕb​)],D=2(n2​L2​−n1​L1​).
(56)
Here I is detected light intensity, Iˉ is the mean level, ϕb​ is a fixed bias wave phase, and V is interferometric visibility. A fine scan of D measures the carrier phase difference; a coarse scan can map the temporal optical coherence envelope through V(D). The envelope maximum locates matched delays only under the source and apparatus assumptions; it is not a bright fringe identifying an arbitrary mirror position. Even with known V, one intensity reading leaves the cosine sign and whole-cycle ambiguities. Continuous motion of known direction, or an independently obtained pair of signals in phase quadrature, is needed to recover signed travel through reversals.
The figure's original control example stipulates equal arm light intensities, matching linear polarizations, and a Lorentzian optical spectrum giving V(D)=exp(−∣D∣/Lc​) with Lc​=1mm. In vacuum, arm mismatches ∣L2​−L1​∣=0.60mm and 0.10mm give V=e−1.2≈0.301 and e−0.2≈0.819. Matching the arms improves the phase difference readout without changing its λ0​/2 travel per cycle. A fine mirror scan of one cycle changes D by only 600nm for the stipulated 600nm source, much less than Lc​. These are calculated values, not historical data.
A proposed measurement sequence follows from this model. Overlap the returning beams, match their linear polarizations, find a high-contrast delay and use a small calibrated mirror scan to determine the sign and fringe period. Read a fixed spatial sample of the interference fringes with a photodiode; record each arm separately and the dark signal, avoid detector saturation, and retain enough time resolution to count every cycle. Approach mechanical settings consistently and repeat the return scan to expose lost motion or drift. For very small changes, bias near phase quadrature, where ∣dI/dϕ∣=IˉV is largest; an intensity extremum has zero first-order sensitivity. A known drive voltage alone does not provide measurement calibration for mirror travel: compare the actual travel with the fringe count using an independently known wavelength.
Control temperature, pressure, vibration and air currents because the instrument measures optical path difference, not mirror motion alone. For a fixed reference arm and fixed λ0​, the first-order change of the moving arm's round-trip path is ΔD≈2nΔL+2LΔn. Thus an unrecognized Δn mimics ΔLapp​=LΔn/n. A hypothetical L=0.10m, n≈1, and Δn=10−7 give 10nm apparent travel. Holding the mirror still while varying or monitoring the air conditions tests this confounder. Separate checks of alignment, wavelength stability and thermal expansion are necessary before sub-fringe measurement resolution becomes a defensible measurement uncertainty.
