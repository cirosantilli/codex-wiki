Passive rotation of coordinates ([v]new​=RθT​[v]old​)

 Home Mathematics Area of mathematics Algebra Linear algebra Rotation matrix
OurBigBook.com Words: 144
Rotate an orthonormal basis counterclockwise by θ while keeping the vector v fixed. The new basis vectors, written in the old basis, are the columns of P=Rθ​. The change-of-basis matrix convention therefore gives
[v]old​=Rθ​[v]new​,[v]new​=Rθ−1​[v]old​=RθT​[v]old​=R−θ​[v]old​.
(113)
For example, the fixed vector with old coordinate vector (2,0)T has new coordinate vector (1,−3​)T after the basis turns by π/3 radians. Its negative second coordinate means that the arrow lies below the new first axis. By contrast, an active planar rotation through that same angle moves the arrow to old coordinates (1,3​)T. The sign difference follows from which object moves. The passive component formulas are x′=xcosθ+ysinθ, y′=−xsinθ+ycosθ.[ref]
If coordinate vectors are instead written as rows, transposing the active equation gives (v′)T=vTRθT​. Switching to rows changes the side and the transpose; it does not by itself turn an active planar rotation into passive rotation of coordinates.
