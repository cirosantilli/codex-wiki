Saturated isotropic magnetostrictive strain (εijmag​=23λs​​(mi​mj​−δij​/3))

 Home Physics Branches of physics Continuum mechanics Elasticity (physics) Magnetostriction
OurBigBook.com Words: 443 Articles: 1
Consider a mechanically free isotropic model of a saturated ferromagnet, at fixed temperature and fixed magnitude of magnetization. Let m=M/Ms​ be a unit vector, where Ms​ is the saturation magnetization. The magnetostrictive strain is an infinitesimal strain tensor, measured relative to a reference with randomly oriented magnetic domains. This reference convention matters: it sets the orientational average of the preferred strain to zero. Define λs​, the saturation magnetostriction, as the longitudinal relative length change in this model.
Rotational symmetry permits a symmetric second-rank tensor made from m of the form aδij​+bmi​mj​, where δij​ is the Kronecker delta. If we restrict attention to a change of shape with zero first-order volume change, its trace gives 3a+b=0. Requiring the longitudinal component to equal λs​ gives a+b=λs​. Hence
εijmag​=23λs​​(mi​mj​−3δij​​),ε∥​=λs​,ε⊥​=−2λs​​.
(1)
The tensor product m⊗m is symmetric and unchanged under m↦−m. It uses two copies of the magnetic direction, so the third-rank isotropic tensor obstruction in isotropic linear magnetostriction vanishes does not apply. More generally, a small-magnetization expansion can contain aM2δij​+bMi​Mj​; isotropy alone does not require its trace to vanish. The displayed saturated formula isolates the volume-preserving orientational contribution, rather than asserting that every material has zero volume magnetostriction.
Along a measuring unit vector n, with n⋅m=cosθ, the fractional length change is
LΔLn​​=ni​εijmag​nj​=2λs​​(3cos2θ−1).
(2)
For a stipulated λs​=20×10−6 and reference length L=100mm, alignment gives +2μm and a perpendicular orientation gives −1μm. Rotating from perpendicular to parallel therefore changes the length by 3μm, even though the saturated longitudinal value relative to the random reference is 2μm. A negative λs​ reverses these elongation and contraction signs.
The eigenvalues of this infinitesimal strain tensor are λs​,−λs​/2,−λs​/2, so ΔV/V=trεmag=0 to first order. This is not exact finite incompressibility: using the illustrative deformation gradient F=I+εmag gives
detF=(1+λs​)(1−λs​/2)2=1−43​λs2​+41​λs3​.
(3)
A finite-deformation law would need higher-order corrections if exact volume conservation were required.
