A character of an algebra is a nonzero multiplicative complex-linear map χ:A→C. A maximal proper ideal is a proper ideal contained in no larger proper ideal. For a character, χ(1)=1 and χ(a)∈σ(a): otherwise a−χ(a)1 would be invertible although its character value is zero. The spectral bound gives ∣χ(a)∣≤∥a∥, so the character is continuous. It is onto, and its kernel is a closed maximal ideal because A/kerχ≅C.
Conversely let M be maximal. Its closure is an ideal and remains proper: otherwise an element of M would lie within distance one of the unit, hence would be invertible by the Neumann series, forcing 1∈M. Maximality then gives M=M. The Banach algebra A/M is a field. For any b in this quotient choose λ∈σ(b), using nonemptiness of the complex spectrum. In a field only zero is noninvertible, so b−λ1=0. Hence every quotient element is a scalar multiple of the unit. The quotient map therefore determines a character χM​, uniquely normalized by χM​(1)=1. These constructions are inverse:
Δ(A)⟷M(A),χ⟼kerχ.​

For the convolution algebra, write ej​ for the sequence supported at j with value one. Then ej​ek​=ej+k​, e0​=1, and e1​e−1​=1. If z=χ(e1​), continuity and ∥e±1​∥1​=1 imply ∣z∣≤1 and ∣z−1∣≤1. Thus ∣z∣=1, so uniquely z=eit for 0≤t<2π, and χ(ej​)=eijt for every integer j. Absolute convergence in ℓ1 now gives
χt​(α)=j∈Z∑​αj​eijt.​

Conversely each displayed map is bounded, nonzero, and multiplicative, since the absolutely convergent double sum can be rearranged along k+l=j. Thus these are all characters, and all maximal proper ideals are
Mt​={α∈ℓ1(Z):j∑​αj​eijt=0},0≤t<2π.​
(13)
They are distinct because the character is uniquely recovered from its kernel and its value on the unit.
