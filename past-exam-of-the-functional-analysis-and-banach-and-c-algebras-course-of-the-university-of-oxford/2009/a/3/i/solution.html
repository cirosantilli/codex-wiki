The Baire category theorem says that a nonempty complete metric space is not a countable union of closed sets with empty interior; equivalently, every countable intersection of open dense sets is dense. To prove the latter, start with any nonempty open set V and open dense sets Gn​. Choose closed balls of positive radii with

Each choice is possible by density and openness. Their centers form a Cauchy sequence; its limit exists by completeness and belongs to every closed ball, hence to V∩⋂Gn​. This proves density and the theorem.
The Uniform boundedness principle says that a pointwise bounded family T of bounded linear maps from a Banach space X to a normed space Z has uniformly bounded operator norms. Put Em​={x:supT∈T​∥Tx∥≤m}. These sets are closed and cover X. Baire gives an open ball B(x0​,r)⊂Em​. For ∥h∥<r, the triangle inequality applied to T(x0​+h)−Tx0​ gives ∥Th∥≤2m.
