The weak topology σ(X,X∗) is the coarsest topology making every f∈X∗ continuous. Its basic neighbourhoods impose finitely many inequalities ∣fj​(x−x0​)∣<ε. The weak-star topology σ(X∗,X) is the coarsest topology making each evaluation f↦f(x) continuous, for x∈X.
The canonical embedding into the bidual is
JX​x∈X∗∗,(JX​x)(f)=f(x).
(18)
It is linear and ∥JX​x∥≤∥x∥. For x=0, the Hahn-Banach theorem supplies a norming functional f with ∥f∥=1 and f(x)=∥x∥, proving equality. Thus JX​ is an isometric embedding. The coordinates defining the weak-star topology on X∗∗, restricted to JX​(X), are precisely JX​x↦f(x) for f∈X∗. These are exactly the coordinates defining the weak topology on X, so JX​ is also a topological embedding for these topologies.
A normed vector space is a reflexive space if JX​ is onto X∗∗. Such a space is automatically a Banach space, since it is isometric to the complete continuous dual space X∗∗. The Banach-Alaoglu theorem states that the closed unit ball of a continuous dual space is compact for its weak-star topology. The Goldstine theorem states that JX​(BX​) is dense in the weak-star topology in BX∗∗​. Consequently
X is reflexive⟺BX​ is weakly compact.​
(19)
If JX​ is onto, its weak-to-weak-star homeomorphism carries BX​ onto the Banach-Alaoglu theorem's compact ball BX∗∗​. Conversely, if BX​ is weakly compact, JX​(BX​) is compact and hence closed in the Hausdorff weak-star topology. Its density from the Goldstine theorem makes it all of BX∗∗​, and scaling gives surjectivity of JX​.
Now suppose X is separable and nonzero. Choose a countable dense set (un​) in its unit sphere and, using the Hahn-Banach theorem, choose norming functionals fn​ with ∥fn​∥=1 and fn​(un​)=1. These continuous linear functionals separate points: for x=0, choose ∥un​−x/∥x∥∥<1/2 to get fn​(x)>∥x∥/2. Define
d(x,x′)=∑n≥1​min{2−n,∣fn​(x−x′)∣}.
(20)
The series converges uniformly, separates distinct points, and obeys the triangle inequality because min(c,a+b)≤min(c,a)+min(c,b). Thus d is a metric. Its topology is coarser than the weak topology: a finite set of coordinate controls makes the finite initial sum small, while the remaining sum is at most ∑n>N​2−n=2−N. On a weakly compact set K, the identity (K,weak)→(K,d) is therefore a continuous bijection from a compact space into a Hausdorff space, hence a homeomorphism. This proves that K is metrizable in its weak topology, an instance of countable separating family metrizes a weakly compact set.
If X is also a reflexive space, its closed unit ball is weakly compact and therefore metrizable by this construction. Compactness in a metric space implies sequential compactness, so every sequence in BX​ has a weakly convergent subsequence. Scaling proves the claim for every bounded sequence.
For the converse, assume every bounded sequence in X has a weakly convergent subsequence. Use the same metric d on BX​. A weak limit of points of BX​ remains in BX​: ∣f(x)∣≤1 for every ∥f∥≤1, and the Hahn-Banach theorem recovers ∥x∥≤1. Thus every sequence in BX​ has a subsequence convergent in d to a point of BX​, so (BX​,d) is sequentially compact and hence compact.
We must still recover the entire weak topology, rather than just the selected coordinates. Suppose xn​→x in d but not weakly. There is a neighbourhood in the weak topology U of x and a subsequence outside U. By hypothesis that subsequence has a further subsequence converging weakly to y∈BX​. It then converges in d to y, so uniqueness of metric limits gives y=x, contradicting eventual membership in U. Hence convergence in d implies weak convergence. Every f∈X∗ is therefore sequentially continuous on (BX​,d) and hence continuous, since the domain is a metric space. The weak topology and d-topology on BX​ coincide. The ball is weakly compact, so the criterion above proves
X separable:X reflexive⟺every bounded sequence has a weakly convergent subsequence.​
(21)
The zero space satisfies all statements directly. The converse did not assume completeness, nor use the full Eberlein-Šmulian theorem.
