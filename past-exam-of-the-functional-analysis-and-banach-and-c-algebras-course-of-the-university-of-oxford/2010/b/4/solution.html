A unital C-star algebra is a complex Banach algebra A with a multiplicative identity 1A​ and a conjugate-linear involution a↦a∗ satisfying (ab)∗=b∗a∗ and the C-star identity ∥a∗a∥=∥a∥2. A C-star isomorphism Φ:A→B is a bijective complex-linear map preserving products and the involution. It carries 1A​ to 1B​ and is automatically isometric.
For a commutative unital C-star algebra, let Ω be its maximal ideal space, equivalently the space of Gelfand characters, with the weak-star topology. A Gelfand character has χ(1)=1 and ∥χ∥=1. The equations χ(ab)=χ(a)χ(b) and χ(1)=1 define a closed in the weak-star topology subset of the dual unit ball. The Banach-Alaoglu theorem makes Ω a compact Hausdorff space. Maximal ideals give characters by the Gelfand-Mazur theorem, and the Banach algebra spectrum satisfies σ(a)={χ(a):χ∈Ω}.
The Gelfand transform is a(χ)=χ(a). It preserves products, constants and the involution; the latter uses the fact that characters of a C-star algebra respect the involution. Every element of a commutative C-star algebra is normal, so the C-star identity and the spectral radius formula give
∥a∥∞​=supχ∈Ω​∣χ(a)∣=r(a)=∥a∥.
(22)
Thus its image is a closed self-adjoint subalgebra of C(Ω), contains the constants, and separates characters. The complex Stone-Weierstrass theorem makes that image dense and therefore all of C(Ω). This is the Commutative Gelfand--Naimark theorem.
For the convergent sequence space c, coordinatewise multiplication satisfies ∥αβ∥∞​≤∥α∥∞​∥β∥∞​, and coordinatewise complex conjugation is an involution. The constant sequence 1 is the identity, and
∥α∗α∥∞​=supn​∣αn​∣2=∥α∥∞2​.
(23)
It is complete: a uniform limit of convergent sequences is convergent, so c is a closed linear subspace of the Banach space ℓ∞​. Hence it is a commutative unital C-star algebra.
Take
Ω=N∪{∞},the one-point compactification of discrete N.​
(24)
Every finite point is isolated, and the neighbourhoods of ∞ contain all but finitely many integers. This is a compact Hausdorff space: an open cover has a set containing ∞, leaving only finitely many points to cover. Define
Φ(α)(n)=αn​,Φ(α)(∞)=limn→∞​αn​.
(25)
Continuity at ∞ is exactly convergence of the sequence. Thus Φ is onto C(Ω), and pointwise operations show it is a C-star isomorphism with ∥Φα∥∞​=∥α∥∞​. Explicitly, these are all Gelfand characters: each χ(en​) is 0 or 1 and at most one is 1. If χ(en​)=1, multiplication by en​ gives χ(α)=αn​. If all vanish, density of finitely supported sequences in l-p in c0​ gives χ∣c0​​=0, so χ(α)=limαn​.
