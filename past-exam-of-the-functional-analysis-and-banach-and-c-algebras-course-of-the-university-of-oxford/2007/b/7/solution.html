The continuous functional calculus for a Hermitian element of a C-star algebra identifies its generated unital C-star algebra with continuous functions on its real spectrum, preserves the involution and norm, and sends functions to the corresponding spectral values by spectral mapping. Consequently an element is a square of a Hermitian element of a C-star algebra exactly when it is a Hermitian element of a C-star algebra with nonnegative spectrum: the reverse direction uses the continuous square root on that spectrum.
Nonnegative scalar multiples preserve this property directly. For positive a,b put C=∥a∥+∥b∥. Functional calculus gives ∥∥a∥1−a∥≤∥a∥ and similarly for b, so ∥C1−(a+b)∥≤C. The sum is a Hermitian element of a C-star algebra, and spectral mapping now places its spectrum in [0,2C]. It therefore has a positive square root in a C-star algebra. If C=0 both elements are zero. Thus A+​ is a cone. It is pointed because an element positive with its negative has spectrum contained in {0} and hence is zero by the self-adjoint norm formula. It is closed as well: for a converging sequence of Positive elements of a C-star algebra use a common norm bound C and pass to the limit in ∥C1−an​∥≤C.
For the two-by-two matrix the exact criterion is
a11​,a22​∈[0,∞),a21​=a12​​,a11​a22​≥∣a12​∣2.​

A positive matrix is Hermitian with nonnegative eigenvalues; evaluating its quadratic form at the coordinate vectors gives the diagonal conditions, and its determinant, the eigenvalue product, gives the final inequality. Conversely if a11​>0, completing the square writes the quadratic form as a11​∣z1​+(a12​/a11​)z2​∣2+(a22​−∣a12​∣2/a11​)∣z2​∣2, which is nonnegative. If a11​=0, the determinant inequality forces a12​=0 and the conclusion is immediate. The spectral theorem then gives nonnegative eigenvalues and their Hermitian square root, proving positivity in the question's sense.
