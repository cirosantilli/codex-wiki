A linear operator has closed graph when {(x,Tx):x∈X} is closed in X×Y. The closed graph theorem states that an everywhere-defined linear operator between Banach spaces is bounded if and only if it has closed graph. Boundedness gives the closed-graph property by continuity. Conversely a closed graph is Banach in the product norm ∥(x,y)∥=∥x∥+∥y∥. Projection of the graph onto X is a bounded linear bijection, so the open mapping theorem makes its inverse bounded. Composing that inverse with the second-coordinate projection proves T is bounded.
For the composition assertion, suppose xn​→x and Txn​→y. Boundedness of S gives STxn​→Sy, and boundedness of ST gives STxn​→STx. Injectivity of S forces y=Tx. Hence T has closed graph and is bounded by the theorem.
For the derivative bound take Y=C1[0,1] with norm ∥v∥C1​=∥v∥∞​+∥v′∥∞​, and Z=C[0,1] with its supremum norm; both are Banach. Regard T(x)=Ux as a map into Y, and let S:Y→Z be inclusion. This inclusion is bounded and injective, and ST=U is bounded by hypothesis. The preceding result gives a constant c with ∥Ux∥C1​≤c∥x∥. In particular ∣(Ux)′(t)∣≤c∥x∥ uniformly in t.
