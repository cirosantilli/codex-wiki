Euler relation in thermodynamics (E=TS−PV+μN)

 ... Physics Branches of physics Statistical physics Thermodynamics Intensive and extensive thermodynamic quantities Extensive quantity
OurBigBook.com Words: 85
For a single-component equilibrium system with extensive internal energy E(S,V,N), scaling its entropy, volume and particle number together gives E(λS,λV,λN)=λE(S,V,N). The Euler theorem for homogeneous functions therefore gives E=S∂S​E+V∂V​E+N∂N​E. The first law of thermodynamics identifies these derivatives as T,−P,μ, respectively, proving E=TS−PV+μN. Dividing by volume yields the entropy density formula s=(ρ+P−μn)/T. This step fixes the entropy normalization rather than merely identifying its differential. Differentiating the Euler relation and subtracting dE=TdS−PdV+μdN gives the Gibbs-Duhem equation SdT−VdP+Ndμ=0. The argument requires extensivity; it is not an assumption about arbitrary nonadditive systems.
