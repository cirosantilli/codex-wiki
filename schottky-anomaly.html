Schottky anomaly

 ... Physics Branches of physics Statistical physics Thermodynamics Heat Heat capacity
OurBigBook.com Words: 141 Articles: 1
A Schottky anomaly is a peak in heat capacity caused by thermal occupation of a small set of separated energy levels. For N independent two-level units with equal degeneracies and gap Δ>0, write x=Δ/(kB​T); their heat capacity is C=NkB​x2e−x/(1+e−x)2, with Boltzmann constant kB​. It vanishes at both low and high temperature and can spoil a lattice T3 fit in between. Localized electronic or nuclear levels can contribute, including in calorimeter addenda; their presence must be checked rather than attributed automatically to phonons.
