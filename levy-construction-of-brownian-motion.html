Lévy construction of Brownian motion (B(u+v)/2​=(Bu​+Bv​)/2+v−u​Z/2)

 ... Mathematics Area of mathematics Probability and statistics Probability theory Stochastic process Brownian motion
OurBigBook.com Words: 197 Articles: 1
The Lévy construction builds Brownian motion on [0,1] by successive independent midpoint displacements on dyadic intervals. Start with B0​=0 and B1​=Z0​, where Z0​ has the standard normal distribution. On each dyadic interval [u,v], choose a fresh independent standard normal random variable Z and use the displayed midpoint rule. Conditional on the endpoints, this is the correct Brownian bridge midpoint law.
To verify the increment law, if D=Bv​−Bu​∼N(0,ℓ) with ℓ=v−u, the new increments are D/2±ℓ​Z/2. They are Jointly Gaussian, each has variance ℓ/2, and their covariance is zero, hence they are independent. Induction gives all dyadic Brownian increments. The change between consecutive linear interpolants has uniform norm 2−n/2−1maxk<2n​∣Zn,k​∣. The Gaussian tail bound and union bound bound the chance that this maximum exceeds n by 2n+1e−n2/2. The First Borel-Cantelli lemma and ∑n​n2−n/2<∞ prove uniform convergence almost surely. The continuous limit has the required Brownian increments at arbitrary times by dyadic approximation.
