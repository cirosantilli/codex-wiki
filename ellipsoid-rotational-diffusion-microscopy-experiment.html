Ellipsoid rotational diffusion microscopy experiment

 ... Physics Branches of physics Statistical physics Thermodynamics Diffusion Rotational diffusion
OurBigBook.com Words: 229
In 2006, Yilong Han and colleagues dispersed plastic ellipsoids in water inside a thin glass cell, using a very dilute suspension to limit particle interactions. A camera on an optical microscope recorded 30 frames per second. Image analysis extracted centre positions and long-axis angles, with reported angular resolution about 1∘. Their shape supplied the observable axis. Reflected interference colours calibrated cell thickness; nearly constant particle interference colour checked vertical wandering. One tracked particle had dimensions 2.4×0.3×0.3μm in an 846nm gap. Angular displacement variance grew linearly with lag; displacement resolved along the particle's axes and in the laboratory showed how turning washes out translational anisotropy. These are observations of confined particles: walls alter drag, and slight out-of-plane tumbling affected positional resolution. They do not establish the bulk sphere formula. The paper's Figure 1A–B shows simulations; Figure 1F shows a measured trajectory.[ref]
For interpreting such images, the director distinction is essential: identical ends give an angle modulo π. A continuously unwrapped trace can follow small successive turns, but it does not make the ends physically distinguishable. The invariant planar axis autocorrelation is ⟨cos(2Δϕ)⟩, and its decay determines Dr​ through e−4Dr​t. Separately measuring position and rigid body orientation is what makes the connection between translational anisotropy and loss of angular memory experimentally accessible.
