Termwise differentiation of a power series (f′(z)=∑n≥1​nan​(z−c)n−1)

 ... Mathematics Area of mathematics Analysis Real analysis Sequence and series Power series
OurBigBook.com Words: 119
Inside its radius of convergence, a power series f(z)=∑n≥0​an​(z−c)n can be differentiated term by term:
f′(z)=∑n≥1​nan​(z−c)n−1.
(113)
The differentiated series has the same radius of convergence. The conclusion concerns the interior, including uniform convergence on smaller closed disks; it does not assert that differentiated boundary series converge wherever the original did. For example, ∑n≥1​zn/n converges conditionally at z=−1, but its differentiated series ∑n≥1​zn−1 does not.
Applied to the geometric series, it gives the useful identity
(1−z)21​=∑n=1∞​nzn−1,∣z∣<1.
(114)
Differentiation turns the plain coefficients into integer weights. This is one way generating functions translate a weighted counting question into an operation on a known series. A proof controls the original and differentiated tails on smaller disks; the Weierstrass M-test provides the needed uniform bounds. See Lebl, Basic Analysis, §11.3: power-series differentiation and analyticity.
