Integers whose totient divides them (φ(n)∣n⟺n=1 or n=2c3d, c≥1, d≥0)

 Home Mathematics Area of mathematics Number theory Euler totient function
OurBigBook.com Words: 96
If n>1 is odd, its Euler totient function is even and cannot divide n. Otherwise write n=2c∏j=1r​pjaj​​ with odd primes pj​ and c≥1. Comparing powers of two in φ(n)∣n gives ∑j​v2​(pj​−1)≤1. Thus there is at most one odd prime p, with v2​(p−1)=1. Since p−1 divides φ(n), it divides n, and is coprime to p; with only the prime factors 2,p available it must be a power of two. Hence p−1=2 and p=3. Conversely, for c≥1 the prime-power totient formula verifies divisibility for 2c3d, and n=1 is immediate.
