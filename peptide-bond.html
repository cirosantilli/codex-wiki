Peptide bond (−C(=O)−NH−)

 Home Biology Protein Polypeptide
OurBigBook.com Words: 70
A peptide bond is the covalent bond between the carboxyl carbon of one amino acid residue and the amino nitrogen of the next. The displayed group shows the usual linkage, with the central carbon–nitrogen connection being the peptide bond. In an intact unbranched chain of n amino acid residues, adjacent positions supply n−1 peptide bonds. Cutting one internal peptide bond produces two fragments; merely changing the chain's shape need not cut it. See Clark, Douglas and Choi, Biology 2e: peptide bonds and protein structure.
