Achromatic lens

 ... Physics Branches of physics Optics Geometrical optics Optical aberration Chromatic aberration
OurBigBook.com Words: 884 Articles: 3
An achromatic lens combines optical lens elements with different optical dispersion so that two specified wavelengths have the same focal length in the paraxial approximation. This brings their parallel incoming light rays to a common optical focus, helping an optical camera form a sharper image under mixed-colour illumination. A common implementation is an achromatic doublet: a positive crown glass element paired with a negative flint glass element. [ref].
The achromatic doublet does not require either material to have zero optical dispersion. Instead, the positive element gains positive optical power toward blue, while the negative element becomes more negative. Suitable Abbe numbers and signed optical powers let these changes cancel even though their reference optical powers do not cancel. For example, the achromatic doublet power balance calculation below combines +10D with −5D to obtain +5D, or focal length 0.2m, with zero primary F–C optical power difference. The smaller Abbe number of the negative element makes its weaker reference optical power sufficient to oppose the positive element's colour change.
An achromatic lens is corrected at two chosen wavelengths, not necessarily at every wavelength between them. The remaining focal length variation is the secondary spectrum. Correcting axial chromatic aberration also does not automatically remove lateral chromatic aberration throughout an imaging system, spherical aberration, or diffraction. Actual designs therefore need ray tracing with the intended wavelengths, element thicknesses, separations and apertures. [ref]; [ref].
