Matrix similarity invariants (χB​(t)=χA​(t))

 Home Mathematics Area of mathematics Algebra Linear algebra Matrix similarity
OurBigBook.com Words: 207
Matrix similarity invariants are quantities unchanged by replacing A with S−1AS. Important examples are the characteristic polynomial, trace, determinant, matrix rank, and eigenvalues with their algebraic multiplicities. The characteristic polynomial convention here is χA​(t)=det(tI−A), where I is the identity matrix.[ref]
The short determinant proof works over any field: because tI−B=S−1(tI−A)S, multiplicativity of the determinant gives
χB​(t)=det(S−1)det(tI−A)det(S)=χA​(t).
(79)
Thus its roots, the eigenvalues, retain their algebraic multiplicities in any field extension where the polynomial splits. Also detB=detA. The cyclic property of the trace gives tr(S−1AS)=tr(ASS−1)=trA; equivalently, the trace is determined by the coefficient of tn−1 in the characteristic polynomial for an n-by-n matrix.[ref]
For the two matrices in the opening example,
χA​(t)=χB​(t)=(t−2)(t−5)=t2−7t+10,trA=trB=7,detA=detB=10.
(80)
Their entries differ, yet these quantities agree. Agreement is a useful necessary test for matrix similarity; it is not sufficient in general, as same eigenvalues do not imply similarity illustrates.
The relation also preserves repeated application. Adjacent factors SS−1 cancel to give
Bk=S−1AkS(k≥0).
(81)
For the opening example, matrix powers therefore have the explicit form
Bk=(2k0​2k−5k5k​).
(82)
At k=2, this is (40​−2125​). Acting on y=(1,1)T gives (−17,25)T, which reconstructs as S(−17,25)T=(8,25)T=A2x. More generally q(B)=S−1q(A)S for every polynomial q, so the minimal polynomial is also unchanged. Multiplication by invertible matrices preserves matrix rank, giving another invariant without finding eigenvalues.
