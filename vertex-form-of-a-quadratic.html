Write the quadratic polynomial as
p(x)=a(x−h)2+k,h=−2ab​,k=c−4ab2​.
(22)
Its function graph is a parabola with vertex of a parabola (h,k). Equal offsets from h give equal values, since p(h+t)=p(h−t)=at2+k. If a>0, the minimum is k; if a<0, the maximum is k. In both cases it is attained only at x=h.
For the nonmonic quadratic polynomial
−2x2+8x−5=−2(x2−4x)−5=−2(x−2)2+3​,
(23)
the negative leading coefficient reverses the inequality: −2(x−2)2≤0. Its maximum is 3 at x=2. Its coefficients are different from those of the positive example, but the same form exposes the shape of both function graphs.
Completing the square also simplifies an integral containing the exponential function:
∫−∞∞​e−(x2+6x+13)dx=e−4∫−∞∞​e−(x+3)2dx=e−4π​.
(24)
The substitution u=x+3 removes the linear term and leaves the standard Gaussian integral. The same shift that locates the minimum makes this integral recognizable.
