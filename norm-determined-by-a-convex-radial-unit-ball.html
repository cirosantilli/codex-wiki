Norm determined by a convex radial unit ball (pB​(x)=inf{t>0:x∈tB})

 ... Mathematics Area of mathematics Analysis Functional analysis Normed vector space Norm
OurBigBook.com Words: 90
Suppose B contains zero, is convex, and meets every line Rv, v=0, in the finite symmetric open segment {tv:∣t∣<λ(v)} with λ(v)>0. Then its Minkowski functional is a norm, with pB​(v)=1/λ(v) and B={x:pB​(x)<1}. Absolute homogeneity follows by rescaling the segment. If s>pB​(x) and t>pB​(y), convexity puts (x+y)/(s+t) in B, giving pB​(x+y)<s+t; passing to the infima proves the triangle inequality. Conversely the open unit ball uniquely determines any norm by the displayed formula. No prior topology on the vector space is needed for these radial hypotheses.
