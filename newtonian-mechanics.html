Newtonian mechanics (F=ma)

 Home Physics Branches of physics Classical mechanics
OurBigBook.com Words: 3k Articles: 24
Newtonian mechanics describes motion using forces, masses and acceleration in inertial frames with absolute time. It is the low-speed limit of special relativity; for constant proper acceleration with ∣at∣≪c, relativistic hyperbolic motion reduces to x≃at2/2.

Galilean spacetime ((M,ta​,hab,∇))

Words: 84
Newtonian mechanics OurBigBook.com
A geometric background for Newtonian mechanics consisting of a four-dimensional affine space, an absolute time covector ta​, a rank-three positive spatial inverse metric tensor hab with ta​hab=0, and a flat torsion-free affine connection preserving both. A trajectory parametrized by absolute time has tangent u with ta​ua=1. Its absolute acceleration is ∇u​u; an unforced particle obeys ∇u​u=0. The structure distinguishes inertial motion without selecting an absolute state of uniform rest. Additional force laws, including a Newtonian gravitational force when wanted, specify a dynamical theory on this background.

Small-slope particle motion on a height graph (r¨≃−g∇h)

Words: 141 Articles: 1
Newtonian mechanics OurBigBook.com
A particle constrained to the graph z=h(x,y) has kinetic energy m[∣r˙∣2+(∇h⋅r˙)2]/2 and potential energy mgh. For a gently varying graph and velocities for which its geometric kinetic correction is higher order, the leading Euler-Lagrange equations are the displayed planar gradient-force equations. Small slope alone at arbitrarily large speed or curvature does not justify discarding the normal inertial acceleration. For a radial height, the leading planar angular momentum is conserved, allowing an effective potential reduction.

Radial turning points for an inverse-square height well (r˙2=2E+2g/r−(L2+2g)/r2)

Words: 73
Small-slope particle motion on a height graph OurBigBook.com
For the height profile h(r)=r−2−r−1, set A=L2+2g and use the planar small-slope model. Its effective potential per unit mass is Veff​=A/(2r2)−g/r. Release with negligible radial speed at r0​ gives E=A/(2r02​)−g/r0​ and the displayed radial-speed law. A bounded trajectory requires E<0, equivalently r0​>A/(2g). The two positive turning radii are r0​ and Ar0​/(2gr0​−A); they coincide at the circular solution r0​=A/g. Zero energy is marginal escape, not bounded motion.

Tangentially driven motion on an axisymmetric surface (ϕ˙​2=r+2rϕ2​/r−rϕϕ​gh′(r)​)

Words: 63
Newtonian mechanics OurBigBook.com
Neglecting vertical acceleration and the vertical driving-force component, the radial normal reaction is −Mgh′(r). A drive parallel to the horizontal velocity cancels when the radial acceleration equation is combined with rϕ​/r times the azimuthal equation. For a prescribed path r(ϕ) this gives the displayed speed constraint. It describes the projected approximation, not arbitrary trajectories with substantial vertical motion.

Friction

Words: 2k Articles: 16
Newtonian mechanics OurBigBook.com
Friction is a contact force tangent to the surfaces. Kinetic friction opposes contact slip; static friction maintains contact without contact slip, including rolling without slipping. The perpendicular contact component is the normal force. These two components answer different questions: how strongly the surfaces press together, and how strongly they resist sliding past one another.
The ideal Coulomb dry-contact model combines a bound on static friction with a sliding law for kinetic friction. Its parameters are the coefficient of static friction and the coefficient of kinetic friction; constant coefficients are an empirical approximation, rather than a consequence of Newton's laws of motion. Surface condition matters, and the static coefficient need not universally exceed the kinetic coefficient. [ref]
The model's historical setting was practical machinery: Coulomb's Théorie des machines simples investigated friction and the stiffness of ropes. The Académie's bibliography records its 1781 prize and its appearance in volume 10, pages 161–332, of the Recueil des savants étrangers. [ref]

Kinetic friction (∣Ffr​∣=μN)

Words: 2k Articles: 7
Friction OurBigBook.com
Kinetic friction is friction during nonzero contact slip. For one-dimensional motion along a surface, choose right as positive and write v for the body's contact-point velocity and U for the surface's contact-point velocity, both measured in the same inertial frame. The signed relative velocity is w=v−U. The ideal Coulomb law for the tangential force on the body is
fk​=−μk​Nsgn(v−U),v−U=0.
(1)
Here N≥0 is the magnitude of the normal force, μk​≥0 is the coefficient of kinetic friction, and sgn is the sign function. Thus kinetic friction opposes contact slip, which can differ from the body's direction of motion. In several tangential directions the same law reads fk​=−μk​Nw/∣w∣ for a nonzero tangential relative velocity w. The magnitude law is documented in Ling, Sanny and Moebs, OpenStax University Physics 1, §6.2: static and kinetic friction.
For a stipulated toy model, take a nonrotating box with mass m=1kg, normal force N=10N, coefficient of static friction μs​=0.5, and coefficient of kinetic friction μk​=0.3. These are assumed model parameters, rather than measured properties of any particular pair of materials; the prescribed normal force is consistent with horizontal support under g=10m/s2 and no other vertical forces. On stationary ground, a box already sliding right has fk​=−3N. With an applied horizontal force F=6N to the right, Newton's second law gives a=(F+fk​)/m=3m/s2, for as long as rightward contact slip persists.
Direction is clearer by comparing two instantaneous states of this same model. If the box has v=1m/s on stationary ground, U=0 and w=+1m/s, so kinetic friction acts left with magnitude 3N. If the box still has v=1m/s but a belt beneath it has U=2m/s, then w=−1m/s: kinetic friction acts right with magnitude 3N, even while the box moves right.
In the belt case, the power delivered to the box is Pbox​=fk​v=+3W. With no other horizontal force, its kinetic energy therefore increases by the work-energy theorem. By Newton's third law, the box exerts −fk​ on the belt, so the contact power delivered to the belt is Pbelt​=−fk​U=−6W. Their sum is −3W: frictional heating produces thermal internal energy at 3W. An ideal motor maintaining the prescribed belt velocity supplies +6W against this contact load, neglecting other losses, so its input divides into 3W of box kinetic energy and 3W of frictional heating. These are instantaneous rates, since the box's velocity subsequently changes. This balance follows directly from power as force times velocity and conservation of energy; the power law is given in Ling, Sanny and Moebs, OpenStax University Physics 1, §7.4: power.
When contact slip vanishes, including a transition to rolling without slipping, the nonzero-slip law no longer applies. Determine the required static friction from the no-slip condition and the equations of motion, then test its bound. Substituting sgn(0)=0 into the sliding law would incorrectly exclude a nonzero static friction.

A cylindrical plug of radius a, length l and density ρ is pushed outward by gauge pressure Pg​. At the marginal initial condition πa2Pg​=2πalμPc​, the net outward force becomes πa2Pg​x/l. With mass ρπa2l,
x¨=b2x,b=l1​ρPg​​​,x(t)=bv0​​sinh(bt),x˙2=v02​+b2x2.
(2)
Thus the exit speed at x=l tends to Pg​/ρ​ as v0​→0+. Exactly v0​=0 leaves the marginal configuration stationary, and the exit time b−1arsinh(bl/v0​) diverges in that limit. Uniform-pressure friction torque on a circular contact (τf​=2μNa/3)

A flat circular contact of radius a carrying total normal force N has uniform pressure p=N/(πa2). Coefficient of kinetic friction (μk​)

Words: 1k Articles: 3
Kinetic friction OurBigBook.com
The coefficient of kinetic friction is the dimensionless quantity μk​=∣fk​∣/N for sliding contact with positive normal force N in the ideal kinetic friction model. It characterizes the pair of contacting surfaces and their condition. A constant μk​ means the model neglects dependence on contact slip speed; unlike the coefficient of static friction, it specifies a sliding force magnitude rather than a no-slip upper bound.
The ratio compares the tangential kinetic friction magnitude with the perpendicular normal force:
μk​=N∣fk​∣​,N>0,∣w∣>0.
(3)
Here w is the tangential relative velocity defining contact slip. Both numerator and denominator have the same physical dimension, so the coefficient of kinetic friction has no units. It belongs to a contact pair under specified conditions, rather than to one material alone. The coefficient of static friction instead describes the threshold ∣fs​∣max​/N before contact slip; a pull required to start motion cannot be substituted for a sliding measurement.[ref]
As an original model example, stipulate μk​=0.30 and gravitational acceleration g=9.8m/s2. An already sliding, nonrotating block of total mass m=2kg is pulled horizontally across a stationary horizontal surface at constant velocity. With no other vertical force, Newton's second law gives N=mg=19.6N. Zero horizontal acceleration then requires a pulling force F=∣fk​∣=μk​mg=5.88N. Doubling the total mass to 4kg gives N=39.2N and F=11.76N. The force doubles while the coefficient of kinetic friction stays 0.30 by assumption. These numbers are model predictions, not observations or a calibration for a particular material pair.
A constant coefficient of kinetic friction is useful because one ratio can summarize many loads, as tested by load dependence of kinetic friction. It is an empirical approximation over a stated range of normal forces, speeds and surface conditions. It implies no universal independence from speed or apparent contact area: a change of face or sliding regime can change the result. Temperature, wear and lubrication are further conditions to report. Newton's second law still applies when the constant-coefficient approximation fails.[ref]
Constant-speed measurement of kinetic friction (μk​=F/(mg))
Words: 297
Coefficient of kinetic friction OurBigBook.com
A force gauge reads the pulling force. For horizontal straight-line sliding at constant velocity, its reading equals the opposing kinetic friction magnitude, provided these are the only horizontal forces. For total supported mass m, including added loads, N=mg, so a mean sliding reading F gives μk​=F/(mg). The important readout is the sustained sliding force after the starting peak; the peak belongs to the static friction threshold.
The University of Mississippi's Experiment 6 uses a wooden block on a wooden plane, a triple-beam balance, added masses, and a dual-range 50 N force probe connected through LabPro to Logger Pro. Its procedure calibrates the probe with a 0.5kg load on a hanger, entering the corresponding force in newtons, and measures the block's mass separately. With the plane horizontal, it starts with 0.5kg on the block and pulls horizontally at constant speed. The recorded force trace has a starting peak; the procedure selects the subsequent constant-force interval and takes its mean. It repeats with 0.5kg increments up to 3kg added, then plots sliding force against normal force to estimate the coefficient of kinetic friction from the slope.[ref]
For a replication, check the gauge's zero with the string slack and its response to known gravitational weights; zeroing alone does not check its scale. Keep the plane level, the pull parallel to it, and the block face and track region consistent. Specify the contacting materials and whether the surfaces are dry, cleaned or lubricated; repeat runs to see whether debris or wear changes the sliding force. Verify constant velocity independently from position versus time, rather than assuming that a flat force trace proves zero acceleration. These are proposed measurement controls, not additional observations from the laboratory writeup. Record the selected interval, variation between runs and gauge resolution as sources of measurement error.
Load dependence of kinetic friction (∣fk​∣=μk​N)
Words: 191
Coefficient of kinetic friction OurBigBook.com
Changing the added mass while retaining the same contact pair and sliding speed tests whether a constant coefficient of kinetic friction is a useful model. Compute N=(mblock​+madded​)g for each level horizontal run, and plot the mean sliding force vertically against normal force horizontally. The constant-coefficient prediction is a straight line through the origin; its slope is μk​. This extracts a ratio from several loads rather than relying on one reading.
The apparatus sketch identifies the contact pair and horizontal pull used by the cited laboratory procedure. The plotted line and points are the original stipulated μk​=0.30 model from coefficient of kinetic friction, not laboratory data. The two model points give
ΔNΔ∣fk​∣​=39.2−19.611.76−5.88​=0.30.
(4)
In actual measurements, a linear regression F=b+μk​N can expose an unexplained intercept b. A nonzero intercept calls for checking the gauge zero, other resisting forces and the contact model; forcing the line through zero can hide those problems. Conversely, constant ratios within measurement error support the approximation only over the loads, speeds and surface conditions tested. A load sweep alone cannot establish independence from contact area or sliding speed; those require separate controlled variations.
Corrections to a pulling measurement of kinetic friction (μk​=(Fcosα−ma)/(mg−Fsinα))
Words: 221
Coefficient of kinetic friction OurBigBook.com
For a block sliding right on a stationary horizontal plane, take rightward acceleration a as positive. A gauge reading F for a pull at angle α above horizontal has horizontal component Fcosα and upward component Fsinα. With no vertical acceleration or other forces, Newton's second law gives
N=mg−Fsinα,Fcosα−∣fk​∣=ma,
(5)
and therefore
μk​=mg−FsinαFcosα−ma​.
(6)
This inference requires continued contact with N>0 and rightward contact slip, so its numerator must be nonnegative. An upward pull reduces normal force; a downward pull, represented by α<0, increases it. The horizontal constant-velocity formula is the special case α=0, a=0.
For the original 2kg, μk​=0.30 model, an upward pull at α=30∘ maintaining constant velocity needs
F=cosα+μk​sinαμk​mg​≃5.7873N,N≃16.7064N.
(7)
Dividing this gauge reading by mg would give about 0.2953, rather than 0.30: the gauge reads the full inclined force, and the pull has changed the normal force.
Even with a horizontal pull, an accelerating measurement needs the ma correction: μk​=(F−ma)/(mg). In the same model, a horizontal 7.88N pull produces a=1m/s2 while the block slides right; subtracting ma=2N recovers the 5.88N kinetic friction. Dividing the uncorrected reading by 19.6N instead gives about 0.4020. Measure acceleration in an inertial frame, along the sliding direction, over the same interval as the gauge reading. These corrections follow from the force balance, independently of whether the resulting coefficient of kinetic friction is constant across runs.

Two-stage stopping under linear drag and dry friction (t=λM​logu∗​U​+μgu∗​​,d=λM​(U−u∗​)+2μgu∗2​​)

Words: 76
Kinetic friction OurBigBook.com
For a body of mass M initially at speed U, suppose the total resistance is linear drag λu until speed u∗​>0, followed by kinetic friction μMg until rest. The first stage solves Mu˙=−λu, giving duration (M/λ)log(U/u∗​) and distance (M/λ)(U−u∗​). Constant deceleration in the second stage gives duration u∗​/(μg) and distance u∗2​/(2μg). The stopping distance is finite because the switch removes the infinite exponential stopping time of pure linear drag.

Contact slip (w)

Words: 46
Friction OurBigBook.com
Contact slip is the tangential relative velocity between the two material points instantaneously in contact. For a nonrotating box on a belt it is w=v−U; for a rigid body, use the velocity of its contact point, including the contribution from angular velocity. Zero contact slip defines rolling without slipping at a rolling contact, even when the center of mass moves.

Frictional heating (E˙thermal​=μk​N∣w∣)

Words: 75
Friction OurBigBook.com
In the ideal kinetic friction model, the loss of combined mechanical energy of the two contacting bodies becomes thermal internal energy at rate
E˙thermal​=−fk​(v−U)=μk​N∣v−U∣≥0.
(8)
This follows by adding the contact powers fk​v and −fk​U from Newton's third law. It depends on contact slip, whereas the contact power received by either body separately depends on its velocity in the chosen inertial frame. Thus positive frictional heating is compatible with increasing kinetic energy of one body, as the moving-belt example in kinetic friction shows. Heating need not cause a temperature rise if thermal energy escapes by heat transfer.

Newton–Cartan theory

Words: 139 Articles: 2
Newtonian mechanics OurBigBook.com
Newton–Cartan theory describes Newtonian mechanics geometrically using absolute time, a degenerate spatial inverse metric, and a compatible torsion-free affine connection. It is not a theory with a nondegenerate Lorentzian metric. Gravitationally accelerated particles are geodesics of that connection, whose curvature measures tidal gravity.

Newton–Cartan gravitational connection (Γi00​=∂i​Φ)

Words: 99 Articles: 1
Newton–Cartan theory OurBigBook.com
In Cartesian spatial coordinates and absolute time t, the gravitational potential Φ(t,x) determines an affine connection by the displayed coefficients, all others zero. The geodesic equation with affine time is d2xi/dt2=−∂i​Φ. It preserves dt and the spatial inverse metric hij=δij, reproducing freely falling Newtonian mechanics in a curved spacetime connection.

Using [∇c​,∇d​]Va=Rabcd​Vb, the Newton–Cartan gravitational connection has only the displayed Hessian components and their antisymmetric partners in the last two indices. The geodesic deviation equation gives relative acceleration −∂i​∂j​Φδxj. The Poisson equation for Newtonian gravity is therefore Rab​=4πGρ(dt)a​(dt)b​.
