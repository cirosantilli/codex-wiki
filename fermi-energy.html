Fermi energy (EF​)

 ... Physics Branches of physics Statistical physics Quantum ideal-gas statistics Fermi-Dirac distribution Fermi gas
OurBigBook.com Words: 1k Articles: 10
The Fermi energy is the highest occupied one-particle energy in the ground state of a noninteracting Fermi gas at zero temperature, measured here relative to the lowest one-particle energy. The Pauli exclusion principle allows only one fermion in each complete one-particle quantum state, including its spin, so adding more particles fills progressively higher energy eigenstates. A cold gas can therefore have substantial kinetic energy even when its temperature is zero. In the thermodynamic limit, with the same energy reference, EF​=μ(T=0) where μ is the chemical potential.[ref]
For a uniform, three-dimensional gas of noninteracting electrons, with spin one-half, equal populations of the two spin states, no applied magnetic field, and nonrelativistic particles of mass m=me​,
n=VN​,pF​=ℏ(3π2n)1/3,EF​=2mpF2​​=2mℏ2​(3π2n)2/3,TF​=kB​EF​​.
(17)
Here n is the total number density, V the volume, pF​ the Fermi momentum, ℏ=h/(2π) the reduced Planck constant, and kB​ the Boltzmann constant. The electron Fermi temperature TF​ expresses the same energy scale in kelvin; it is not the actual gas temperature. Strong degeneracy of a degenerate electron gas means T≪TF​. The momentum formula counts states and survives changing the energy–momentum relation, whereas EF​=pF2​/(2m) requires pF​≪mc.[ref]
The reference matters: the displayed EF​ excludes rest energy and sets the zero at p=0. Including rest energy shifts both the one-particle energy and the zero-temperature chemical potential by mc2, leaving occupations unchanged. More generally, adding a constant C to every one-particle energy also adds C to μ; the Fermi-Dirac distribution depends only on their difference. In a finite quantum system the chemical potential at zero temperature can lie between the last occupied and first empty energy eigenstates; its equality with the filling edge is a continuum statement.
At positive temperature, EF​ remains the zero-temperature scale associated with the specified number density. The Fermi level μ(T) must instead satisfy the particle-number condition
N=∫0∞​exp[(E−μ(T))/(kB​T)]+1g(E)​dE,
(18)
where g(E) is the extensive density of states, including both spin states. Smearing the Fermi-Dirac distribution while arbitrarily holding μ=EF​ generally changes N. This distinction matters when interpreting electron heat capacity and any experiment that infers temperature from an occupation profile.[ref]
