DC Josephson effect (V=0,∣Is​∣≤Ic​)

 ... Physics Branches of physics Statistical physics Superconductivity Josephson junction Josephson effect
OurBigBook.com Words: 129
At zero electric potential difference, ϕ˙​=0 permits a nonzero constant quantum phase and hence a steady pair electric current. Under an imposed electric current Ib​ with ∣Ib​∣<Ic​, a static solution satisfies sinϕ=Ib​/Ic​. The low-energy branch has cosϕ>0. For example, a stipulated Ib​=Ic​/2 gives ϕ=π/6 modulo 2π; it does not require a voltage drop. In the ideal current-biased energy −EJ​cosϕ−(ℏIb​/2e)ϕ, the positive second derivative on that branch explains its local stability.
The superconducting critical current Ic​ is a maximum available static pair electric current, not the inevitable electric current under a voltage bias. At V=0 the initial state and circuit select a phase and a current. Above Ic​ this sinusoidal model has no static phase solution, but its two relations alone do not predict the complete resistive current-voltage characteristic, switching dynamics or hysteresis. Those require the circuit and dissipative channels.
