Connected component (Cx​)

 Home Mathematics Area of mathematics Geometry and topology Connected space
OurBigBook.com Words: 410 Articles: 3
A connected component of a topological space X is a nonempty connected subset that is maximal under subset inclusion. If C is a connected component and C⊆D⊆X with D a connected subset, then D=C. Maximality means that no larger connected subset contains C; it does not mean largest cardinality among all connected subsets.
For example, the connected components of [−2,2]∖{0} in the usual subspace topology of the real line are [−2,0) and (0,2]. Each is a real interval, and no connected subset of the real line can meet both without containing the missing point 0. For the finite set {−2,0,2} in the same subspace topology, the three connected components are its singleton sets.
Every point x∈X lies in exactly one connected component, constructed by taking the union
Cx​=⋃{A⊆X:x∈A and A is connected}.
(35)
All members contain x, so their union is connected by connected union with a connected hub. The union includes every connected subset through x, so it is maximal. Equivalently, define
x∼y⟺some connected subset of X contains both x and y.
(36)
This is an equivalence relation: singleton sets give reflexivity, symmetry is immediate, and two connected subsets sharing the middle point have a connected union, giving transitivity. The equivalence classes are precisely the connected components. They form a partition of a set: they cover X and any two are either equal or disjoint.[ref]
Every nonempty connected subset lies within one connected component. Moreover, every connected component is closed in X: closure of a connected set makes its closure connected, and maximality forces that closure to equal the original connected component. A connected component need not be open. For example, in S={0}∪{1/n:n=1,2,…} with its usual subspace topology from the real line, all connected components are singleton sets, but {0} is not open: every neighbourhood of 0 in S contains some 1/n.[ref]
