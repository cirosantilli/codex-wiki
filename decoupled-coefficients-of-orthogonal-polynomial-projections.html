Decoupled coefficients of orthogonal polynomial projections (cn​=⟨f,pn​⟩w​/hn​)

 Home Mathematics Area of mathematics Analysis Numerical analysis Orthogonal polynomial
OurBigBook.com Words: 484 Articles: 1
Let f be a real function with ∥f∥w2​=∫ab​f(x)2w(x)dx<∞, and let Vm​ be the vector space of real polynomials of degree at most m. Its orthogonal projection is the least-squares polynomial in an orthogonal-polynomial basis
qm​=∑n=0m​cn​pn​,cn​=hn​⟨f,pn​⟩w​​,hn​=⟨pn​,pn​⟩w​.
(5)
Each coefficient depends on just one weighted inner product with f and one squared norm. In particular, the coefficients c0​,…,cm​ stay the same when the allowed polynomial degree increases. This statement assumes a fixed weight, interval and normalization of the orthogonal polynomials.
To see why the least-squares normal equations decouple, first write a trial polynomial as q=∑j=0m​aj​ϕj​ in any basis of Vm​. The residual must be orthogonal to each permitted direction, giving
∑j=0m​Gij​aj​=bi​,Gij​=⟨ϕj​,ϕi​⟩w​,bi​=⟨f,ϕi​⟩w​.
(6)
The Gram matrix usually couples the unknown coefficients. With ϕj​=pj​, it becomes Gij​=hi​δij​, leaving the separate equations hi​ai​=bi​. For the monomial basis on [−1,1] with w=1, by contrast, Gij​ is zero when i+j is odd and 2/(i+j+1) when i+j is even. Distinct even powers, or distinct odd powers, remain coupled.
There is also a proof of the minimum that uses no differentiation. The defining coefficients give ⟨f−qm​,pn​⟩w​=0 for 0≤n≤m. Thus f−qm​ is orthogonal to all of Vm​. For every v∈Vm​, the Pythagorean theorem in an inner-product space gives
∥f−v∥w2​=∥f−qm​∥w2​+∥qm​−v∥w2​.
(7)
The second term is nonnegative and vanishes exactly when v=qm​, proving that the least-squares solution is unique. This is an integrated squared-error optimum; it does not assert a smallest error at every point or a smallest uniform norm.
For N>m, the nested orthogonal projections satisfy the exact residual budget
∥f−qm​∥w2​=∥f−qN​∥w2​+∑n=m+1N​cn2​hn​.
(8)
Indeed, qN​−qm​ is a linear combination of mutually orthogonal polynomials, and f−qN​ is orthogonal to it. Every newly retained component removes exactly cn2​hn​ from the squared error. No infinite expansion or completeness assumption is needed for this finite identity. The Legendre least-squares projections of a cubic make every term explicit.
