Euler method (yn+1​=yn​+hf(tn​,yn​))

 Home Mathematics Area of mathematics Analysis Numerical analysis Linear stability domain
OurBigBook.com Words: 284
The Euler method approximates an initial value problem y′=f(t,y), y(t0​)=y0​, by evaluating the derivative at the current point and moving along that tangent line for a time h. With tn​=t0​+nh, its displayed update needs one evaluation of f per step. This simple rule turns local slope information into a whole approximate trajectory. MIT's course notes derive it by dropping the second-order Taylor remainder.[ref]
Take y′=−2y, y(0)=1, whose exact solution is the exponential function y(t)=e−2t. The Euler method gives
yn+1​=(1−2h)yn​,yn​=(1−2h)n.
(305)
At h=0.2, the first values are 1,0.6,0.36,0.216. At t=1, five steps give 0.65=0.07776, compared with e−2≈0.135335. The result decays but is too small: absolute stability alone does not ensure accuracy. At h=1.2, the first values are 1,−1.4,1.96,−2.744, and at t=6 five steps give −5.37824, while the exact solution is about 0.00000614421.
For the Dahlquist test equation y′=λy, the Euler method has stability function R(z)=1+z with z=hλ. Its absolute stability region is ∣1+z∣≤1, the closed disk centred at −1 with radius one. For the present real decay problem and h>0, bounded numerical values require 0<h≤1; strict decay requires 0<h<1. At h=1 the values alternate 1,−1,1,… without decay. These conditions follow directly from the amplification factor 1−2h, matching MIT's criterion for y′=−ay.[ref]
The Euler method is first order: with bounded second derivative and suitable Lipschitz continuity, its global error is O(h) on a fixed interval. In this example the logarithm gives log(1−2h)=−2h−2h2+O(h3) for small positive h. At t=nh, this means yn​=e−2t(1−2th+O(h2)). It explains both the downward bias and why halving the step roughly halves the error once h is sufficiently small.
