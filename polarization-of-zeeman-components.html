Polarization of Zeeman components

 Home Physics Atomic physics Zeeman splitting
OurBigBook.com Words: 279
Viewed perpendicular to the magnetic field, the normal Zeeman effect has a π component with linear polarization parallel to the magnetic field and two σ components polarized perpendicular to it. Along the magnetic field, π vanishes and the σ pair has opposite circular polarizations. Pieter Zeeman described these geometry-dependent patterns in his lecture.[ref]
The geometry can be derived from electric dipole radiation. Let the observation unit vector be n=(sinθ,0,cosθ), with θ measured from the magnetic field. Radiation retains only the transverse part of the oscillating electric dipole moment d:
Erad​ ∝ d−n(n⋅d).
(8)
For π, take d∥z^; its angular light intensity factor is sin2θ, so it vanishes on axis. For σ±, use normalized complex dipoles (x^±iy^​)/2​. Their angular factors are (1+cos2θ)/2. Projection gives circles on axis, lines at θ=π/2, and generally elliptical polarization between. These factors assume equal dipole magnitudes; actual line light intensities also depend on initial quantum state populations, transition strengths and collection geometry. No absolute handedness name is needed: it depends on the viewing and phase conventions.
A transverse polarizer can separate π from σ even before the spectral line components are fully resolved. Along the magnetic field, a quarter-wave plate followed by a polarizer converts the two opposite circular polarizations into distinguishable linear settings. This explains why polarization analysis carried information beyond a small increase in line width.
