Equality in Hölder's inequality

 ... Mathematics Area of mathematics Analysis Real analysis Lp norm Hölder's inequality
OurBigBook.com Words: 198
For 1<p,q<∞ and nonzero factors in their respective Lp spaces, equality in the product-integral bound holds precisely when
∥f∥pp​∣f∣p​=∥g∥qq​∣g∣q​almost everywhere.
(77)
In other words, the normalized powers match. Zero factors give the trivial equality separately. For real vectors, equality for the signed finite sum also needs the nonzero coordinate products to have a common sign; otherwise the preceding triangle inequality loses information.
For f(t)=t and g(t)=1−t on [0,1], with p=3, q=3/2,
∫01​f(t)g(t)dt=61​,∥f∥3​∥g∥3/2​=(41​)1/3(52​)2/3=25−1/3≈0.341995.
(78)
Changing only g to t2 gives f3=g3/2=t3, and both sides become 1/4. The factors need not be equal: their conjugate powers are what must agree after normalization.
The proof outline makes the equality condition natural. Normalize u=∣f∣/∥f∥p​ and v=∣g∣/∥g∥q​. The Young inequality gives uv≤up/p+vq/q; integration yields ∫uv≤1/p+1/q=1. Equality requires up=vq almost everywhere. The endpoint proof uses the essential supremum directly, so it does not need division by p−1 at p=1.
