The cosmic microwave background consists of relic photons from the early hot, nearly thermal photon-baryon fluid. Before cosmological recombination, abundant free electrons repeatedly scatter photons by Thomson scattering. Their mean free path λγ​=(ne​σT​)−1 is short and their Thomson scattering rate ne​σT​c is much greater than the Hubble parameter. An earlier photon therefore cannot stream directly to us: subsequent scattering repeatedly changes its direction.
As cosmic expansion cools the plasma to about T≃3000K, electrons and protons combine into neutral hydrogen. The free-electron abundance drops rapidly, the scattering optical depth to the observer becomes of order one, and photon decoupling occurs. Since the cosmic microwave background temperature scales as T=T0​(1+z), with T0​≃2.725K, this occurs at z≃1100. The small baryon-to-photon ratio delays neutralization to well below 13.6eV/kB​: the high-energy tail contains enough photons to ionize hydrogen until the temperature is much lower than the Hydrogen binding energy expressed as a temperature. The observed cosmic microwave background is primarily this pre-existing radiation undergoing its last scattering; its nearly blackbody spectrum is then preserved by cosmological redshift.

The angular diameter of a patch of that radius is about 3.56∘. The factor of two is a radius-versus-diameter convention; either gives a horizon scale of only a few degrees. This is a particle horizon, rather than the smaller sound horizon. Including early radiation in cosmology changes the numerical estimate but preserves its order of magnitude.
The horizon problem is that the cosmic microwave background has almost the same temperature across the whole sky, including pairs of regions whose past light cones do not overlap in this noninflating model. Local thermal equilibration cannot explain that observation within this history. A sufficiently long period of cosmic inflation makes the comoving Hubble radius decrease: a region once small enough to share a causal past can expand to encompass our entire observed Cosmic microwave background last-scattering surface. After reheating, it can be homogeneous across many later particle horizon patches. The matter-only estimate integrates from a decelerating Big Bang; extending the earlier history by cosmic inflation changes that causal conclusion.
