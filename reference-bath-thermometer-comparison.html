Reference-bath thermometer comparison

 ... Branches of physics Statistical physics Thermodynamics Laws of thermodynamics Zeroth law of thermodynamics Zeroth-law thermometer comparison
OurBigBook.com Words: 291
A motivating real procedure is NIST's ambient thermometer verification. Its apparatus is a deep beaker of water, a calibrated reference thermometer, a test thermometer, and a magnetic stirrer with any heater switched off. Let the water approach room temperature, stir gently, immerse both sensing tips well below the surface, and wait before reading. NIST specifies about 20cm of water, 10–15cm immersion where possible, and an initial five-minute wait. Record test then reference, repeat in reverse order, and apply the reference's certificate correction. Subtract corrected reference from test: the difference estimates the test instrument's error at this bath temperature. Agreement between the two reading orders checks repeatability. [ref]
For example, suppose a test reads 24.7∘C, a reference reads 24.4∘C, and its certificate says to add 0.1∘C. The corrected reference is 24.5∘C, so the test error is +0.2∘C: it reads high. These are illustrative readings. This verification tests a previously calibrated instrument at one point; it does not establish the two-point response used in two-point thermometer calibration example.
Additional controls follow from the thermal equilibrium premise. Keep tips away from walls and bottom, interchange probe positions to test a temperature gradient, and check that longer waits and modest immersion changes leave agreement unchanged within the required tolerance. A flat trace alone can be a steady state maintained by competing heat transfer paths. Five minutes is a procedural starting point, not a universal equilibration time. Excessive stirring can supply work and warm the bath, while probe stems can exchange heat with the room. These controls test whether both probes sample the same state; neither one-point agreement nor this experiment proves the zeroth law of thermodynamics for every material and condition.
