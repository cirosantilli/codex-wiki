Blackett-Occhialini triggered cloud-chamber experiment

 ... Physics Particle detection Particle detector Cloud chamber Expansion cloud chamber Triggered cloud chamber
OurBigBook.com Words: 390
Patrick Maynard Stuart Blackett and Giuseppe Occhialini began their cosmic-ray collaboration in 1931. They used the coincidence methods of Walther Bothe and Bruno Rossi to make a particle initiate its own recording. Blackett later reported tracks on about 80% of their counter-controlled photographs, compared with roughly 2–5% for random expansions in small chambers. Those figures describe the reported apparatus and conditions, not a universal detection efficiency.[ref]
In their 1933 account, Geiger counters above and below the chamber fed a coincidence circuit. Its pulse operated a thyratron, magnet and spring-release valve, letting a light piston expand the gas. A mercury-vapor lamp then illuminated the drops. A water-cooled solenoid supplied the magnetic field; two oblique cameras allowed track reconstruction. Moving the lower counter changed which trajectories could be recorded.[ref]
Two timing problems must be separated. The coincidence circuit's window distinguishes nearly simultaneous counter signals. The mechanical expansion latency determines how much the existing ionization trail spreads before droplets form. For a chosen one-coordinate diffusion model with initial variance σ02​, diffusion coefficient D and expansion delay te​,
σtrack2​=σ02​+2Dte​.
(27)
Taking σ0​=0, D=2.0×10−6m2s−1 and te​=20ms gives σtrack​≃0.283mm. This is an original illustrative calculation, not the historical track width. It shows why a fast electronic pulse does not by itself guarantee a sharp photograph: the subsequent mechanical response also matters.
The photographs revealed groups containing positive and negative electron-like tracks, complementing the Anderson positron track experiment. Their interpretation depended on track curvature, direction, ionization and particle range, not simply on the presence of a coincidence.[ref] Blackett explicitly noted that the counter control favored larger particle showers.[ref] The multiplicity bias from a detector trigger gives a small model of that effect: a recording method can improve the study of rare interactions while making its photographs unrepresentative of the incident population.
