Connected sum of surfaces (M#N)

Words: 166 Articles: 1
Topological surface OurBigBook.com
For two connected closed surfaces, remove an open disc from each and identify the resulting boundary circles by a homeomorphism. The resulting closed surface is their connected sum, defined up to homeomorphism independently of these choices. A triangulation version removes one triangular face from each and glues their boundary three-cycles by a simplicial homeomorphism.
At a seam vertex the two vertex links of a simplex are paths; gluing their matching endpoints produces a cycle graph, proving the local surface condition. Removing a triangular face subtracts one from each Euler characteristic, while boundary gluing reduces both the vertex count and the edge count by three. Therefore χ(M#N)=χ(M)+χ(N)−2. If the result were orientable, either punctured summand would inherit consistent choices of orientation of a simplex, and these would extend over its capping face. Thus a nonorientable summand forces a nonorientable surface result. The classification theorem for closed combinatorial surfaces then gives T2#RP2≅RP2#RP2#RP2.

Failure of cancellation for connected sums of surfaces (T2#RP2≅#3RP2)

Words: 38
Connected sum of surfaces OurBigBook.com
The torus and Klein bottle are not homeomorphic because only the former is orientable. Adding a projective plane to either by connected sum of surfaces makes a nonorientable surface with Euler characteristic minus one. The classification of compact surfaces identifies both with three projective planes. Thus connected-sum cancellation can fail even for closed connected surfaces.

Closed combinatorial surface

Words: 121
Topological surface OurBigBook.com
A closed combinatorial surface is a finite connected simplicial complex in which every simplex is a face of a triangle and the link of a simplex at every vertex is a cycle graph. Every edge then lies in exactly two triangles, and the triangles at a vertex form a single cyclic fan. The edge-incidence condition alone is insufficient: gluing two triangulated spheres only at one vertex gives two disjoint circles in that vertex's link.
Its geometric realization is a compact surface without boundary. Triangle interiors have disc neighbourhoods, edge interiors have such neighbourhoods from the two incident triangles, and the cyclic fan gives one at each vertex. A finite realization is a Hausdorff space with a countable basis of a topology, being a finite union of geometric simplexes in Euclidean space. If connectedness is omitted by convention, require these properties separately on each connected component.

Classification of compact surfaces

Words: 124 Articles: 1
Topological surface OurBigBook.com
A connected compact surface is determined by its orientability, number b of boundary circles and genus. An orientable surface of genus g has Euler characteristic 2−2g−b; a surface which is not orientable with r crosscaps has Euler characteristic 2−r−b. In particular, a closed connected surface with vanishing first mod-two homology is a sphere, and a connected closed orientable surface of Euler characteristic zero is a torus.

Classification theorem for closed combinatorial surfaces

Words: 77
Classification of compact surfaces OurBigBook.com
Every closed combinatorial surface has geometric realization homeomorphic to exactly one member of the following list: the sphere S2; the connected sum of surfaces of g≥1 tori; or the connected sum of surfaces of k≥1 real projective planes. Every member admits a finite triangulation. The orientable family has Euler characteristic 2−2g, including g=0 for the sphere; the nonorientable surface family has Euler characteristic 2−k. Thus orientability and Euler characteristic determine the homeomorphism type. The first two nonorientable cases are the real projective plane and the Klein bottle. For a finite disconnected simplicial complex satisfying the same local conditions, the unordered list of its connected components' types determines its type.

Thrice-punctured sphere

Words: 73
Topological surface OurBigBook.com
The sphere with three points removed is an orientable noncompact surface of genus zero with three ends. It can be realized by pairing the two sides at each of two opposite ideal vertices of an ideal hyperbolic quadrilateral in the hyperbolic disk. The four ideal vertices fall into three equivalence classes; after adding these three points the cell structure has one face, two edges and three vertices, hence Euler characteristic two. Removing these added vertices recovers the original quotient.

Surface with boundary

Boundary component of a surface (b=#π0​(∂M))

Words: 38
Surface with boundary OurBigBook.com
A boundary component of a surface with boundary is a connected component of its manifold boundary. For a compact surface with boundary, these are finitely many circles. Their number b enters the classification of compact surfaces: χ=2−2g−b in the orientable case and χ=2−k−b in the nonorientable surface case, where k is the nonorientable genus.

Topology of disk tiles glued along disjoint boundary arcs (χ=n−e,π1​≅Fe−n+1​)

Words: 165
Surface with boundary OurBigBook.com
Consider a connected assembly of n closed discs formed by identifying e pairs of boundary arcs, each arc used once and all arcs in a given tile having disjoint endpoints. Its gluing graph has one vertex per tile and one edge per paired arc. A spanning tree joins the initial discs into a single closed disc; each of the remaining e−n+1 pairings attaches a band. This is a thickening of the gluing graph, so it is homotopy equivalent to that graph. Thus its Euler characteristic is n−e and its fundamental group is the free group Fe−n+1​. If the gluing graph is a tree, the assembled surface is itself a closed disc.
Suppose each pairing preserves the positive boundary parameter of the reference discs. A global orientation must then assign opposite orientation signs to adjacent tiles, because their induced boundary orientations on an interior seam must oppose one another. Such signs exist exactly when the gluing graph is bipartite. An odd gluing cycle therefore proves that the assembly is a nonorientable surface. A Dirichlet boundary condition or a Neumann boundary condition on an unpaired arc has no effect on this topological calculation.

The pair of pants is the compact genus-zero surface with boundary having three boundary components. It is obtained from the sphere by deleting the interiors of three disjoint discs and is homotopy equivalent to a wedge of two circles.

Hyperbolic Y-piece (Y(ℓ1​,ℓ2​,ℓ3​))

Words: 147 Articles: 1
Pair of pants (mathematics) OurBigBook.com
A Y-piece is a compact curvature-−1 hyperbolic surface homeomorphic to a pair of pants, with three geodesic boundary curves. Its labelled boundary lengths ℓ1​,ℓ2​,ℓ3​>0 determine it up to isometry. Cutting along the three mutually perpendicular seams produces two congruent right-angled hyperbolic hexagons with alternating sides ℓi​/2. Conversely, doubling the hexagon along its other three sides constructs the Y-piece for arbitrary positive lengths.
Hyperbolic X-piece (X(ℓ1​,ℓ2​,ℓ3​,ℓ4​;s,τ))
Words: 92
Hyperbolic Y-piece OurBigBook.com
An X-piece is a curvature-−1 four-holed sphere with geodesic boundary, formed by gluing two Y-pieces along equal-length cuffs. For a specified separating cuff and labelled outer boundaries, four outer lengths, the internal cuff length s>0, and a twist τ determine the metric. The gluing is orientation-reversing on boundary circles, with relative arclength displacement τ measured from chosen seam endpoints. Without markings, the gluing displacement is taken modulo s; with a marking, τ∈R records Dehn twists as well. Changing the chosen separating curve can give different parameter descriptions of the same unmarked surface.

Genus of a finite cover of a closed orientable surface (g′=1+n(g−1))

Words: 70
Closed orientable surface OurBigBook.com
A connected n-sheeted covering space of a closed orientable surface of genus g has genus 1+n(g−1). Lifting a finite cell decomposition gives n cells above every base cell, so its Euler characteristic is n(2−2g). Equating this with 2−2g′ gives the formula. For g≥1, every positive n occurs: send one handle generator of the fundamental group to 1 in Z/n and all remaining generators to zero, then use the classification of connected covering spaces for the kernel of that surjection.

Jordan separation from the exponential quotient (C(J,C∗)/expC(J)≅Z)

Words: 107
Jordan curve theorem OurBigBook.com
For a compact set J homeomorphic to a circle, lifting an argument along its parametrization identifies the quotient by continuous exponentials with Z. By planar logarithm factorization it is also free abelian on the bounded complementary components, so exactly one exists. A proper closed arc has trivial exponential quotient, and therefore connected complement. Given a small open arc of J inside any neighborhood of a point, join points from the two complementary regions by a path avoiding the remaining closed arc. The first and last intersections with J occur in the small arc, proving that the neighborhood meets both regions. Thus J is the common boundary of its unique bounded and unbounded regions.

Jordan domain

Words: 47
Jordan curve theorem OurBigBook.com
A Jordan domain is the bounded complementary component of a closed simple curve in the plane. The Jordan curve theorem separates the plane into this inside component and one unbounded outside component. A conformal bijection from the disc or complex upper half-plane to this simply connected domain extends to a homeomorphism of their closed conformal boundaries by the Caratheodory boundary extension theorem.

Schoenflies theorem

Words: 48
Jordan curve theorem OurBigBook.com
A Jordan curve in the two-sphere is carried to an equator by a homeomorphism of the sphere; equivalently, the closures of its two complementary regions are discs. This strengthens the separation assertion of the Jordan curve theorem and controls the boundary degrees of characteristic maps for cell decompositions of a surface.

Outer boundary of a planar compact set (∂C∞​(K))

Words: 70
Jordan curve theorem OurBigBook.com
The outer boundary of a compact planar set K is the boundary of the unbounded component C∞​(K) of its complement. Its filled hull is C∖C∞​(K). When the outer boundary is a simple closed curve, the hull is its closed interior. For a simply connected open domain U, containment of a compact trace and of its filled hull are equivalent. This lets outer-boundary pushforwards retain simply connected domain-containment information.

Moore triod theorem

Words: 73
Topological surface OurBigBook.com
A simple triod is the union of three arcs that share one endpoint and are otherwise disjoint. The Moore triod theorem states that every pairwise disjoint family of simple triods in the plane is countable. One proof chooses data from a countable basis of open disks around each branch point; the Jordan curve theorem shows that two disjoint triods cannot determine the same finite basis data, giving an injection into a countable set of finite tuples.

Crosscap extraction move for a polygonal word

Words: 155
Polygonal schema OurBigBook.com
For a polygonal schema with all its sides paired and two same-direction occurrences of an edge label x, the words xAxB, xxA−1B, and A−1xxB describe homeomorphic quotient spaces. Here inversion of a string reverses its order and inverts each letter. Cut from the start of the first x side to the start of the second, using a diagonal d. The pieces have boundary words xAd−1 and dxB. Gluing the old x sides gives outside word Ad−1d−1B−1; reversing the whole boundary and choosing a new starting point gives ddA−1B. Alternatively cut from the end of the first x side to the end of the second. The pieces are xdB and Axd−1, and gluing their old x sides gives dBA−1d, cyclically A−1ddB. Renaming the new paired side gives both asserted forms. These are actual cutting and gluing homeomorphisms, so they preserve all the other side identifications as well as the Euler characteristic.

Polygon with a complete set of side-identifications

Words: 116
Polygonal schema OurBigBook.com
Start with a polygonal disk and pair all its sides, specifying for each pair a homeomorphism matching endpoints. Arrow labels encode whether each occurrence is traversed with or against its assigned direction. Form the quotient topology using those identifications and no extra identifications in the interior. Matched edge interior points have disk neighbourhoods formed from two half-disks; the corner sectors around each identified vertex form a circular link. The quotient is therefore a closed surface. A boundary word records each edge label exactly twice. Its endpoint identifications determine the vertex count, while its signs determine orientability. This encoding is distinct from treating a side-paired polygon as an abstract simplicial complex; a compatible subdivision is needed for a simplicial triangulation.

Standard polygon words for closed surfaces

Words: 149
Polygonal schema OurBigBook.com
Read a boundary word counterclockwise, giving every paired edge a label. A positive exponent means the edge arrow agrees with the boundary traversal, and an inverse means it disagrees. Identify equally labelled edges in their arrow directions. The sphere has a square word abb−1a−1; the vertex count is three, giving Euler characteristic 3−2+1=2. A closed orientable surface of genus g≥1 has word
∏j=1g​aj​bj​aj−1​bj−1​.
(2)
A nonorientable surface which is the connected sum of surfaces of k real projective planes has word c1​c1​⋯ck​ck​. For k=1, this is a digon; the square word abab, identifying antipodal boundary points, is another model of the real projective plane. The Klein bottle has square word aba−1b, representing the nonorientable case k=2. The classification theorem for closed combinatorial surfaces says that these orientable and nonorientable families exhaust the connected closed cases.

Triangulation of a polygon with paired sides

Words: 115
Polygonal schema OurBigBook.com
Triangulate a closed polygon by joining an interior point to every side, using matching parametrizations on paired sides. Orient every radial edge away from the interior point and choose consistent orientations on paired boundary edges. Each triangle then has a local vertex ordering with the centre first and its boundary edge in the chosen direction. These local orderings agree along shared edges. The quotient triangles and their affine face identifications form a finite delta-complex, even if some triangle vertices become identified. The second barycentric subdivision of a delta-complex removes these identifications at the simplicial level without changing the underlying quotient space. Thus every polygonal schema with a complete pairing has a finite triangulation. A single subdivision need not suffice to give an abstract complex.

Vertex link in a polygonal schema (Lk(v))

Words: 122
Polygonal schema OurBigBook.com
Replace each corner of a polygonal schema by a small closed interval cutting across that corner. The endpoint identifications induced by paired polygon edges glue these intervals into a graph at each vertex equivalence class. This is its vertex link. A circle link gives a disc neighbourhood; a closed interval link gives a half-disc neighbourhood in a surface with boundary.
For a complete pairing of the sides of a single polygon, every link endpoint is paired exactly once. In each vertex equivalence class the link graph is connected, since the corner endpoint identifications generate precisely that class, and all its vertices have degree two. It is therefore a circle. For partial pairings, a connected link can instead be a closed interval when it has two unpaired endpoints. Counting free polygon edges alone does not count boundary components of a surface; their endpoints must first be identified.

Polygonal presentation of a punctured surface

Words: 154
Polygonal schema OurBigBook.com
Start with a polygonal schema whose outer boundary has m sides, and remove a triangular disc strictly inside its polygon. Cut the resulting annulus along an arc from an outer vertex to an inner vertex. The cut space is a closed disc with m+5 marked sides: the original m outer sides, two banks of the cut, and three inner sides. Parametrize it by a convex polygon with that many sides. A boundary homeomorphism carrying the marked vertices in cyclic order extends over the disc by radial extension after identifying both discs with a standard closed disc.
Transport the original side identifications to this polygon and identify the two cut banks, leaving the three inner sides unpaired. The quotient space is the original surface with an open triangular disc removed. In boundary-word notation, if w is the original word, the new word is wcdefc−1, with d,e,f unpaired. The torus has w=aba−1b−1; a square model of the real projective plane has w=abab, pairing antipodal points of the square's boundary.

Mapping class

Words: 29
Mapping class group OurBigBook.com
A mapping class is an isotopy class of self-homeomorphisms of a surface. Orientation-preserving classes form the mapping class group. Acting on markings changes a point of Teichmüller space while preserving the underlying unmarked Riemann surface.

Injection of a finite hyperbolic isometry group into a mapping class group

Words: 62
Mapping class group OurBigBook.com
If a finite group G acts faithfully by orientation-preserving isometries on a closed hyperbolic surface S of genus at least two, then G↪Mod(S). An isometry isotopic to the identity induces an inner automorphism of the surface group; its suitable lift commutes with every deck transformation and fixes their limit set, forcing the lift and the original isometry to be the identity.

Realization of a finite group as a surface deck group

Words: 55
Mapping class group OurBigBook.com
Every finite group G is the deck group of a connected finite regular covering of some closed orientable surface of genus at least two. Choose a surface group that surjects onto G and take the covering corresponding to the kernel. The resulting free action embeds G into the mapping class group of the covering surface.

Dehn twist

A Dehn twist Tγ​ cuts an oriented surface along a simple closed curve γ, rotates one side once, and glues it back. If a curve or arc α has nonzero geometric intersection with γ, the iterates Tγn​(α) usually represent distinct isotopy classes, with intersection numbers growing linearly in ∣n∣.

Lickorish-Dehn theorem

Words: 55
Dehn twist OurBigBook.com
The mapping class group of a compact connected oriented topological surface, for orientation-preserving maps fixing its boundary pointwise and isotopies relative to that boundary, is generated by Dehn twists about simple closed curves. Boundary-parallel twists are allowed. For a closed surface of positive genus a finite generating collection exists. Permuting boundary components requires additional generators if that larger group is used.

If γ⋅u uses the ordered-tangent orientation convention for algebraic intersection number of curves on an oriented surface, a right-handed Dehn twist takes u to u+([γ]⋅u)[γ] on first homology. In particular a separating-curve twist acts trivially there.

In orientation-preserving annular coordinates (θ,r) with orientation dθ∧dr, the twist is (θ,r)↦(θ+2πf(r),r), where f increases from zero to one. The map is the identity outside this annulus and near its boundary. The orientation of the ambient topological surface, rather than the orientation assigned to the curve, fixes the handedness.

Algebraic intersection number of curves on an oriented surface (⟨α,β⟩)

For transverse oriented curves on an oriented surface, the algebraic intersection number is the sum of the local signs of their crossings. It depends only on their oriented homology classes and is antisymmetric:
⟨α,β⟩=−⟨β,α⟩.
(4)

During a generic isotopy of one transverse curve, crossings with a fixed curve can only be created or removed in pairs of opposite local sign. Their signed sum, and hence the algebraic intersection number, is therefore invariant.

The three-punctured sphere has six arc classes: one joining each unordered pair of distinct punctures and one returning to each puncture. Torus

Normals through a point on a ring torus (P=(a,R/2,0),Q=(a,0,0))

Words: 109
Torus OurBigBook.com
For the surface of revolution of the circle with center (a,R) and radius r<R in the upper half-plane, use X(u,v)=(a+rcosu,(R+rsinu)cosv,(R+rsinu)sinv) and unit normal n=(cosu,sinucosv,sinusinv). Exactly four surface normals pass through the displayed P. The equation P−X=tn forces (t+r)cosu=0. The case t=−r would put P at radial distance R, so cosu=0; its nonzero radial coordinate then forces v=0 or π. All four combinations work. By contrast, infinitely many normals pass through Q: both complete circles with u=π/2 or 3π/2 have radial normals through that point. This is a concrete illustration of degenerate distance-critical sets versus isolated critical points.

Three-critical-point function on a torus (h(u,v)=sinusinvsin(u−v))

Words: 77
Torus OurBigBook.com
Take u,v modulo π; shifting either coordinate by π leaves h unchanged. Its partial derivatives are hu​=sinvsin(2u−v) and hv​=sinusin(u−2v). The only critical points are (0,0), (π/3,2π/3) and (2π/3,π/3). The latter are a nondegenerate minimum and maximum, with values ∓33​/8. At the origin the leading cubic is uv(u−v), linearly equivalent to a monkey saddle; its critical point is isolated but degenerate. Thus three smooth critical points coexist with the Morse lower bound of four for the torus.

Pinched torus (T2/S1≃S1∨S2)

Words: 97
Torus OurBigBook.com
Collapsing an essential meridian circle of a torus to one point gives the pinched torus. In the torus's CW structure, the two-cell attaches to two circles by the commutator. Collapsing one circle leaves one one-cell and a two-cell attached by a null-homotopic word. Thus the quotient is homotopy equivalent to S1∨S2. Its cellular chain groups are Z in degrees zero, one and two, with zero differentials, so its integral homology groups are H0​=H1​=H2​=Z and vanish in all higher degrees. This illustrates that collapsing a circle can preserve a top-dimensional homology class even while reducing the first homology rank.

Cohomology ring of a torus (H∗(Tr;R)≅ΛR​(x1​,…,xr​),∣xi​∣=1)

Words: 42
Torus OurBigBook.com
Since Tr is a product of circles, the Künneth theorem identifies its cohomology ring with the exterior algebra on the coordinate degree-one classes. Inversion negates each integral degree-one class, so it acts as (−1)q in degree q. Even in characteristic two, each degree-one class has square zero.

Homology of an integer torus endomorphism ((fA​)∗​∣Hq​​=⋀qA)

Words: 79
Torus OurBigBook.com
On the torus Rd/Zd, an integer matrix A defines an endomorphism. The coordinate circles give a basis of the first homology group, and the induced map on that group is A. The Künneth theorem identifies higher integral homology with the exterior powers of Zd, so the degree-q map is ⋀qA. In top degree it is multiplication by detA. The map is a homeomorphism precisely when A is a unimodular matrix; a merely nonsingular integer matrix need not have an integer inverse.

Möbius band

Words: 633 Articles: 3
Topological surface OurBigBook.com
The Möbius band is the topological surface obtained by gluing the ends of a rectangle with a half-turn. Precisely, it is the quotient space
M=([0,1]×[−1,1])/((0,v)∼(1,−v)).
(15)
The twist identifies the two apparent long edges into one boundary circle. Following the core circle of a Möbius band reverses local handedness, so this surface is not orientable. An untwisted untwisted band has two boundary circles and is orientable: the distinction survives stretching and bending because orientability is a topological invariant.
To explore the construction with paper, mark the two long edges in different colours, join the short ends after a half-turn, and trace an edge. The trace passes from one marked edge to the other without ever leaving the boundary. Separately, trace the centre line and track a local transverse arrow: it comes back reversed after one circuit. The boundary closes after twice the travel around the centre, while the transverse arrow needs two circuits to return to its original direction.
This original mathematical model has central radius R=2 and half-width a=0.55:
F(θ,w)=((R+wcos(θ/2))cosθ, (R+wcos(θ/2))sinθ, wsin(θ/2)),0≤θ≤2π,−a≤w≤a.
(16)
Its seam obeys F(2π,w)=F(0,−w). The dotted circle is the core circle of a Möbius band; the teal curve follows the single boundary through 0≤θ≤4π. At the same point on the core, the arrows for θ=0 and θ=2π are opposite. The graph compares a continuously followed unit normal with its starting value in the ambient space; the endpoint reversal, rather than any intermediate zero of the graph, establishes the obstruction. It is not a statement about parallel transport.
The Möbius band has the homotopy type of a circle: a deformation retraction shrinks each width coordinate to zero. Nevertheless its boundary represents twice a generator of its fundamental group. The boundary of a Möbius band winds twice around its core, so with compatible generator choices the boundary generator maps to c2 in π1​(M)≅Z.
The historical account by Julyan H. E. Cartwright and Diego L. González places the mathematical discovery by August Möbius and Johann Listing in 1858, while investigating representations of twisted bands predating that work. A familiar-looking picture and a recognition of the underlying topological invariant are different kinds of evidence. [ref]

Core circle of a Möbius band (C={[u,0]:u∈[0,1]})

Words: 68
Möbius band OurBigBook.com
The core circle is the image of the centre line in the rectangle defining a Möbius band. Its endpoints are identified, so it is a circle. The formula
Ht​([u,v])=[u,(1−t)v],0≤t≤1,
(17)
defines a deformation retraction onto that circle: it respects (0,v)∼(1,−v), starts at the identity map, and fixes the core throughout. Consequently the Möbius band has fundamental group isomorphic to Z, just like a circle; this alone cannot distinguish it from an annulus.

Normal reversal around the Möbius core (n(2π)=−n(0))

Words: 130
Möbius band OurBigBook.com
For the model F of the Möbius band, the tangent vectors along its core circle of a Möbius band are
Fθ​(θ,0)=(−Rsinθ,Rcosθ,0),Fw​(θ,0)=(cos(θ/2)cosθ,cos(θ/2)sinθ,sin(θ/2)).
(18)
Their cross product, divided by its norm R, gives the continuous unit normal
n(θ)=(cosθsin(θ/2), sinθsin(θ/2), −cos(θ/2)).
(19)
Although F(2π,0)=F(0,0), we have n(2π)=−n(0). Any other continuous choice of unit normal along this parametrized interval is either n everywhere or −n everywhere: its relative sign is a continuous map into the discrete space {+1,−1}, and the interval is connected. Thus no choice can agree at the identified endpoints. This short calculation proves that the Möbius band has no global continuous unit normal and is not an orientable surface.
The plotted quantity is n(θ)⋅n(0)=cos(θ/2). It compares ambient vectors at generally different points; after one circuit it is −1 at the starting point, and after two circuits it is +1 there. Only these endpoint comparisons are needed for the orientability argument.

Boundary of a Möbius band winds twice around its core (deg(∂M→C)=2)

Words: 99
Möbius band OurBigBook.com
In the quotient space description of the Möbius band, follow v=+1 once along the rectangle. The seam sends its endpoint to the start of v=−1, which must also be traversed before returning to the original point. Under the deformation retraction onto the core circle of a Möbius band, these two stretches each travel once around the same circle. Orienting both circles by increasing longitudinal parameter gives a map of topological degree two. Equivalently, the group homomorphism on fundamental groups induced by inclusion of the boundary sends a chosen generator to twice the core generator, or to c2 in multiplicative notation. Reversing the chosen boundary generator changes the sign, not the fact that it winds twice.

Untwisted band (S1×[−1,1])

Words: 75
Topological surface OurBigBook.com
An untwisted band is the topological surface obtained from a rectangle by identifying (0,v) with (1,v). It is the Cartesian product of a circle and a closed interval, and is homeomorphic to a closed annulus. A finite portion of the lateral surface of a circular cylinder realizes it in three-dimensional space. Its two boundary circles are S1×{−1} and S1×{1}, and the matching seam directions make it an orientable surface. The Möbius band changes just one identification sign and loses both this two-component boundary and its orientability.

Gluing annuli with one reflected boundary

Words: 196
Klein bottle OurBigBook.com
Glue the outer circles of two closed annuli by the identity, and their inner circles by complex conjugation. The result is a Klein bottle. To see the gluing explicitly, let r∈[1/2,1] and z∈S1. Parametrize the first annulus by (z,τ)=(z,2r−1), with 0≤τ≤1, and the second by (z,τ)=(z,3−2r), with 1≤τ≤2. The outer-circle gluing identifies their common level τ=1. The remaining gluing is
(z,0)∼(z,2).
(20)
This is the mapping torus of reflection of the circle. The coordinate maps and their piecewise inverses agree on every identified boundary, so the universal property of the quotient topology gives inverse continuous maps between the two quotient descriptions. Gluing collars by the reflection supplies disc charts at the seam; thus it is a closed surface. Cutting the circle coordinate at one point gives a rectangle with one matching and one reversed edge pairing, the usual Klein-bottle model. Its Euler characteristic is zero and it is a nonorientable surface, so the classification theorem identifies it with the connected sum of two real projective planes.
