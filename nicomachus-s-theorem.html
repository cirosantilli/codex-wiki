Nicomachus's theorem (∑k=1n​k3=Tn2​)

 ... Mathematics Area of mathematics Arithmetic Figurate number Triangular number Squared triangular number
OurBigBook.com Words: 66
For every nonnegative integer n,
∑k=1n​k3=(2n(n+1)​)2=Tn2​.
(5)
For example, 13+23+33+43=1+8+27+64=100=T42​. A short proof uses the triangular number recurrence and the triangular number square-complement identity:
Tk2​−Tk−12​=(Tk​−Tk−1​)(Tk​+Tk−1​)=k⋅k2=k3.
(6)
Summing this difference of squares identity gives a telescoping sum; all intermediate squared triangular numbers cancel. Nicomachus describes a related grouping of the odd integers: take one, then the next two, then the next three, producing 1,3+5,7+9+11,…, which are 13,23,33,…. This historical grouping is recorded in Introduction to Arithmetic by Nicomachus (Hahn teacher edition) (Book II, Chapter 20, §5, printed p. 75).
