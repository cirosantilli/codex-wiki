Voltage divider (Vout​=Vs​Rb​/(Ra​+Rb​))

 Home Electrical engineering
OurBigBook.com Words: 1k Articles: 3
A voltage divider uses two resistors connected in series to obtain a fraction of an applied electric potential difference. Let the upper electrical resistance be R1​>0, the lower one R2​>0, and the input be Vin​=Vs​ relative to a common electrical reference potential. The output is the junction's electric potential difference relative to that same reference, across R2​. The familiar ratio assumes zero output electric current: no external electrical load draws from the junction. In a steady direct current model with fixed resistors obeying Ohm's law and an ideal voltage source,
I=R1​+R2​Vin​​,V0​=IR2​=Vin​R1​+R2​R2​​.
(26)
Here V0​ is the open-circuit voltage; R1​,R2​ correspond to Ra​,Rb​ in the header formula. With no junction branch, Kirchhoff's current law makes the same electric current pass through both resistors, and Ohm's law gives the two voltage drops. For Vs​=0, division gives V0​/Vs​=R2​/(R1​+R2​); if Vs​=0, the predicted output is zero but this ratio is undefined.
An original stipulated example has R1​=R2​=10kΩ and Vin​=10V. With zero output electric current, I=0.5mA and V0​=5V. An attached 10kΩ electrical load instead gives Vout​=10/3V; a 1MΩ load gives Vout​=1000/201V=4.975124378…V. These are exact model predictions, not measured readings or specifications of a particular multimeter or oscilloscope.
The divider is useful for scaling a voltage or converting a change in a sensor's electrical resistance into an electric potential difference. Its beauty is also its trap: multiplying both electrical resistances by the same positive factor preserves the unloaded ratio, but changes the effect of any fixed electrical load. A divider therefore supplies a predictable ratio under specified loading conditions; it is not a source that maintains that voltage for arbitrary output electric currents. A Wheatstone bridge compares two divider junctions, while Thévenin's theorem describes the divider's response to many different loads.
