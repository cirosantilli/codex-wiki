Conditional entropy (H(Y∣X))

 Home Mathematics Area of mathematics Probability and statistics Information theory
OurBigBook.com Words: 2k Articles: 12
For finite discrete random variables, conditional entropy is the average remaining information entropy of Y after the value of X is known:
H(Y∣X)=∑x:pX​(x)>0​pX​(x)H(Y∣X=x).
(3)
Each observation X=x changes the probability distribution used to describe Y; the average weights that uncertainty by how often the observation occurs. An observation can increase uncertainty for one particular outcome, even though conditioning reduces entropy on average for finite variables.
For example, let Y choose A,B,C,D with probabilities 1/2,1/4,1/8,1/8, and let X report whether Y=A. If it reports yes, there is no remaining uncertainty. Otherwise the conditional probabilities are 1/2,1/4,1/4, whose information entropy is 3/2 bits. The two reports each occur with probability 1/2, so H(Y∣X)=3/4 of a bit. The original uncertainty was 7/4 bits: this observation removes exactly one bit on average.
If Y is a function of X, then H(Y∣X)=0: the observation determines the answer. If X and Y are independent random variables, then H(Y∣X)=H(Y): the observation gives no improvement. These endpoints make conditional entropy useful for separating relevant observations from irrelevant ones. They also lead to the chain rule for information entropy, H(X,Y)=H(X)+H(Y∣X), which counts the first uncertainty and the remaining uncertainty without double counting.
