Probability plot

 Home Mathematics Area of mathematics Probability and statistics Statistics
OurBigBook.com Words: 95
A probability plot compares order statistics with plotting positions or model quantiles to diagnose whether a proposed probability distribution describes data. If the model can be transformed into a linear identity T(F(x))=a+bS(x), plot T(i/(n+1)) against S(X(i)​). Approximate linearity supports that model's shape; systematic curvature suggests a mismatch.
The plotting position i/(n+1) comes from the exact mean of the ith uniform order statistic, by the probability integral transform. It does not assert equality between the expectation of a nonlinear transformation and that transformation of the expectation. Fitted slope and intercept can estimate model parameters, but the visual plot alone is not a calibrated hypothesis test.
