Metropolis sampling on fixed-cardinality subsets (q(v∣u)=1{v∼u}​/[k(n−k)])

 ... Area of mathematics Probability and statistics Statistical inference Bayesian statistics Markov chain Monte Carlo Metropolis–Hastings algorithm
OurBigBook.com Words: 117
Represent a size-k subset of n objects by a binary vector with coordinate sum k, where 0<k<n. Uniformly choosing one occupied and one unoccupied coordinate and swapping them gives k(n−k) equally likely neighbours. This proposal distribution is symmetric, so its forward and reverse factors cancel in the Metropolis–Hastings acceptance probability. Every state is connected to every other by a sequence of swaps, establishing an irreducible Markov chain when the target masses are positive. Symmetry and the acceptance rule give detailed balance with that target.
For a discrete uniform distribution as the prior distribution and a multivariate normal distribution for observations with covariance matrix Σ and mean u, the posterior distribution is proportional to exp[−(x−u)TΣ−1(x−u)/2]. If d=v−u, expanding the quadratic forms gives log acceptance ratio dTΣ−1(x−u)−dTΣ−1d/2, and the log acceptance probability is its minimum with zero.
