Kolmogorov's zero–one law

 ... Mathematics Area of mathematics Probability and statistics Probability theory Independent sigma-algebras Tail sigma-algebra
OurBigBook.com Words: 371 Articles: 1
For mutually independent random variables, every tail event has P(A)∈{0,1}. Identical probability distributions are unnecessary. The tail sigma-algebra is trivial in probability: its events have probability zero or one, though it can contain nonempty proper sets.[ref]
For a proof, set Fm​=σ(X1​,…,Xm​) and FX​=σ(X1​,X2​,…). Independence extended from generating pi-systems makes Fm​ independent of σ(Xm+1​,Xm+2​,…), hence of each tail event A. The increasing union ⋃m​Fm​ is a generating pi-system for FX​. For fixed A, the events B satisfying P(A∩B)=P(A)P(B) form a Dynkin system. The pi-lambda theorem extends this equality to all B∈FX​. Taking B=A gives
P(A)=P(A∩A)=P(A)2,P(A)(1−P(A))=0.
(33)
Thus A is independent of itself, forcing the two stated values.[ref]
The useful feature is the absence of intermediate probabilities for infinite properties that ignore every finite prefix. It reduces some calculations to showing that a tail event has positive probability, or that its complement does. The theorem supplies no finite observation deadline, and an extra argument such as the calculation in infinitely many heads as a tail event is needed to decide which value occurs.
