Laplacian matrix (L)

The Graph Laplacian converts local differences along edges into a linear map. Throughout this section, G=(V,E) is a finite undirected graph with no self-loops or repeated edges, and each edge has weight one. In a fixed order of vertices, let A be the adjacency matrix and D the degree matrix. The combinatorial Graph Laplacian is
L=D−A,(Lx)i​=∑j:{i,j}∈E​(xi​−xj​).
(43)
Thus the value at a vertex is compared with its neighbours. Every row sums to zero, so L1=0: a constant vector produces no differences. This definition and the associated Graph Laplacian quadratic form are developed in Spielman's authored survey.[ref]
For the three-vertex path graph A−B−C, the vertex degrees are 1,2,1, and in that order
A=​010​101​010​​,D=​100​020​001​​,L=​1−10​−12−1​0−11​​.
(44)
Direct multiplication gives three eigenvectors and their eigenvalues:
L​111​​=0,L​10−1​​=​10−1​​,L​1−21​​=3​1−21​​.
(45)
The constant eigenvector has eigenvalue zero; the other two describe progressively stronger neighbour disagreement. Dividing these eigenvectors by 3​,2​,6​, respectively, gives an orthonormal basis. The four-vertex cycle graph is already worked out under circulant matrix, where Fourier diagonalization of a circulant matrix gives its eigenvalues.
Every formula here uses L=D−A without degree normalization. For comparison, Chung's treatment uses the degree-scaled matrix D−1/2LD−1/2, with a convention for isolated vertices; its eigenvalues must not be substituted into the examples here.[ref]
