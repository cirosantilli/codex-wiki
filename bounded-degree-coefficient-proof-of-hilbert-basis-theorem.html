Bounded-degree coefficient proof of Hilbert basis theorem

 Home Mathematics Area of mathematics Algebra Noetherian ring Hilbert basis theorem
OurBigBook.com Words: 121
For an ideal I in R[x], let Jn​ contain the coefficients of xn in its elements of degree at most n. These are ideals of R and form an ascending chain, since multiplying by x embeds one into the next. If R is a Noetherian ring, the chain stabilizes at N, and each Jn​ for 0≤n≤N has finitely many generators. Choose corresponding polynomials in I. Their union generates I: for an element of degree d, subtract a combination of the chosen polynomials for Jd​ if d<N, or the polynomials for JN​ multiplied by xd−N otherwise. This cancels its leading coefficient and lowers its degree. Induction completes the proof without assuming that R is an integral domain.
