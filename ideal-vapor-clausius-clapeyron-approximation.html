Ideal-vapor Clausius-Clapeyron approximation (dlnp/dT≃Lm​/(RT2))

 ... Thermodynamic potential Thermodynamic free energy Gibbs free energy Phase coexistence curve Latent heat Clausius-Clapeyron relation
OurBigBook.com Words: 447 Articles: 3
For liquid–gas phase coexistence, make two distinct approximations: the gas obeys the ideal gas law, vm,g​=RT/p, and its molar volume greatly exceeds the liquid molar volume, vm,g​≫vm,l​. Here R is the molar gas constant and p is the saturation pressure of the pure substance. Substitution into the Clapeyron equation gives
dTdp​≃RT2Lm​(T)p​,dTdln(p/p∗​)​≃RT2Lm​(T)​,
(191)
where p∗​>0 is any fixed reference pressure, making the logarithm dimensionless. Constant latent heat is a further approximation, needed for the elementary Integrated Clausius-Clapeyron relation below.[ref]
The neglected liquid molar volume has a transparent error estimate. If the gas is ideal but ϵ=vm,l​/vm,g​ is retained, then
(dTdp​)full​=RT2(1−ϵ)Lm​p​,(dp/dT)full​(dp/dT)approx​​=1−ϵ.
(192)
Thus ignoring the liquid molar volume underestimates this slope by a relative amount ϵ. This estimate does not bound errors from treating a real gas as ideal or from freezing Lm​(T). Near a thermodynamic critical point, the gas and liquid molar volumes approach each other and these dilute-vapor assumptions fail.
