Geometrical optics

 Home Physics Branches of physics Optics
OurBigBook.com Words: 6k Articles: 49
Geometrical optics describes propagation by rays and Snell's law, neglecting diffraction on scales much larger than the wavelength.
A light ray is a path in an approximation, not a physical thread of zero width. In a transparent, homogeneous, isotropic medium it is straight and perpendicular to a wavefront; boundaries redirect it by the law of reflection or Snell's law. The approximation is useful when the wavelength is small compared with relevant apertures, surface curvature scales, and distances over which the refractive index changes. For an illustrative wavelength of 500nm and a 10mm aperture, their ratio is 5×10−5. That small ratio supports ray tracing for image location, but does not eliminate diffraction when calculating the fine structure of a focused spot. At a wave caustic, geometrical optics can fail despite large optical elements. [ref].
The mechanism of refraction is a change in phase velocity. For transparent isotropic media at the chosen frequency, the refractive index is
n=vphase​c​,n1​sinθ1​=n2​sinθ2​,
(109)
where c is the vacuum speed of light and the angles are measured from the interface normal vector. At a stationary planar interface the frequency and tangential wavevector match across the boundary. Since the wavenumber is k=nω/c, that matching gives Snell's law. The transmitted wavelength changes while the frequency stays fixed. Here n uses phase velocity, which need not equal group velocity. [ref]. [ref].
Choose model air with n1​=1 and model glass with n2​=1.5, without claiming these are exact values for every sample or wavelength. At θ1​=45∘, Snell's law gives
θ2​=arcsin(1.5sin45∘​)≈28.1255∘.
(110)
The transmitted light ray bends toward the normal vector; its phase velocity is c/1.5, and its wavelength is two thirds of the air value. By reversibility of an optical ray, incidence from glass at 28.1255∘ returns the outgoing air angle 45∘.
Incidence from glass at 45∘ is a different experiment. It would require sinθ2​=1.5sin45∘≈1.06066, so there is no real transmitted angle. The critical angle for total internal reflection is
θc​=arcsin(1/1.5)≈41.8103∘.
(111)
Above this angle, an ideal lossless glass-air boundary produces total internal reflection. The physical optics description still permits an evanescent wave in the air, although no propagating transmitted light ray crosses the boundary. [ref]. [ref].
The usefulness of geometrical optics is that complicated instruments can be assembled from these local rules. Applying Snell's law at successive curved surfaces changes a spreading bundle into a converging one. The Fermat principle unifies the construction through stationary optical path length; stationarity does not require a global minimum. [ref]. Fang's MIT notes explicitly connect this principle to specular reflection, refraction, and thin lens imaging, providing a route from individual paths to complete instruments. [ref].
