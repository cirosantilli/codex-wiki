Material culture of science

Words: 35
History of science OurBigBook.com
The scientific instruments, specimens, buildings, records and embodied skills through which scientific work is performed and communicated. Explaining a result may require reconstructing apparatus dimensions, maintenance, preparation, authorship and access, as well as the published argument.

The French royal contracted tax-collection institution. It provides relevant context for Antoine Lavoisier's financial and official position and the prosecution of its former members.

Historiography of science

Words: 68 Articles: 2
History of science OurBigBook.com
Study of how accounts of the scientific past are selected, organized and justified. Explaining why an investigator accepted a claim is distinct from endorsing that claim.

Whig history of science

Words: 29
Historiography of science OurBigBook.com
A retrospective account organized around the apparent inevitability of later scientific success. The problem is explanatory selection, not the mere recognition that some theories explain evidence better than others.

Historical presentism

Words: 13
Historiography of science OurBigBook.com
Interpreting past inquiry primarily through later categories, problems or standards. Distinct from presentism.

Mixed mathematics

Words: 37
Natural philosophy OurBigBook.com
Early modern mathematical disciplines applied to physical or practical objects, including astronomy, optics and mechanics. The social standing and explanatory claims of practitioners varied; this is not simply a synonym for the present administrative category of applied mathematics.

Natural philosopher

Words: 20
Natural philosophy OurBigBook.com
An investigator of nature within historical traditions of natural philosophy, rather than necessarily a member of the later profession of physics.

Demonstration in early modern natural philosophy

Words: 36
Natural philosophy OurBigBook.com
A proposed proof of a physical claim, as distinguished in a particular controversy from a calculation that succeeds under a supposition. Historical standards of demonstration and the authority to judge one must be reconstructed from the participants' arguments.

Scholastic natural philosophy

Words: 24
Natural philosophy OurBigBook.com
University traditions that investigated nature through authoritative texts, commentary and disputed questions. Acceptance of a textual framework could coexist with disagreement about particular explanations.

Aristotelian natural philosophy

Words: 103 Articles: 2
Natural philosophy OurBigBook.com
A tradition of explanations of natural change drawing on Aristotle's distinctions among matter, form, capacities and causes. Its medieval and early modern versions were interpreted traditions, not identical endorsements of every ancient conclusion.[ref]

Four causes

Words: 66 Articles: 1
Aristotelian natural philosophy OurBigBook.com
Aristotle's four explanatory questions concern what something is made of, its form, the source of change and its end. The final cause is the last of these; this is not a list of four modern interacting forces.[ref]
Final cause
Words: 27
Four causes OurBigBook.com
The end for the sake of which a process or organized activity is explained in Aristotelian natural philosophy. Such an explanation should be distinguished from a modern dynamical force law.
