Energy density and flux of a plane electromagnetic wave

OurBigBook.com Words: 150
For a single traveling vacuum plane electromagnetic wave, B=k×E/c gives equal electric and magnetic contributions to electromagnetic energy:
uE​=uB​=21​ϵ0​∣E∣2,u=ϵ0​∣E∣2,S=cuk.
(289)
For the fields with linear polarization along x and magnetic orientation along y specified above,
S(z,t)=ϵ0​cE02​cos2(kz−ωt)z,⟨S⟩=2ϵ0​cE02​​z.
(290)
Angle brackets mean an average over one period at fixed position. The factor 1/2 comes from averaging squared cosine; E0​ is the peak amplitude, whereas the root mean square field is Erms​=E0​/2​. The equal energy contributions oscillate together, rather than trading energy as in an RLC circuit. [ref].
For E0​=100V/m, using c=299792458m/s and ϵ0​≃8.854×10−12F/m,
B0​=c100​V/m≃3.34×10−7T,⟨Sz​⟩≃13.3W/m2,⟨u⟩≃4.43×10−8J/m3.
(291)
A 0.010m2 surface normal to +z therefore has average crossing power 0.133W. This is incident power; the amount absorbed depends on the surface. Doubling the field amplitude quadruples the average flux.
