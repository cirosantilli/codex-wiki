Well-founded progress in recursion (μ(child)<μ(parent))

 Home Computer science Algorithm Recursion (computer science)
OurBigBook.com Words: 137
A base case and a decreasing natural number measure work together. Check that every invocation satisfies its precondition, each base case finishes with the correct output, and every other invocation makes finitely many recursive calls with strictly smaller measure. All local work must also terminate. By well-founded induction, the children finish, so their caller finishes; if their answers are correct and the combination rule preserves the postcondition, the caller is correct too. This proves total correctness, not just correctness conditional on return.
Strict decrease without a lower bound is insufficient: subtracting one forever from a negative integer never reaches zero. Even a positive real-valued measure can decrease indefinitely, as 1,1/2,1/4,… shows. A well-founded relation rules out that descent. In structural recursion, a proper child subtree of a finite binary tree supplies progress without numeric arguments. The proof obligation is the relation between parent and child instances, together with terminating local work and complete base cases.
