The Bessel process is stopped in the compact interval [α,β], where its drift and diffusion coefficients are bounded. In particular its stopped stochastic integral has expectation zero on every finite time interval.
For k>1, taking expectations in the stopped equation gives
EYt​−1=2k−1​E∫0t∧τ​ys​ds​≥2βk−1​E(t∧τ).
(1)
Since Yt​≤β, this bounds E(t∧τ) uniformly in t. For k<1, the same argument with 1−EYt​ gives E(t∧τ)≤2β(1−α)/(1−k).
If k=1, the stopped process is a stopped Brownian motion. Itô's formula gives E(Yt​−1)2=E(t∧τ), which is at most max{(1−α)2,(β−1)2}. Thus in every case the monotone convergence theorem gives Eτ<∞, so τ<∞ almost surely. By path continuity its value at exit is one of the two endpoints. They are disjoint possibilities, hence
P(yτ​=α)+P(yτ​=β)=1.​
(2)

Let p=P(yτ​=α). For k=2, the bounded stopped martingale from part (b) converges to yτ2−k​, so the dominated convergence theorem gives
1=pα2−k+(1−p)β2−k.
(5)
Therefore the boundary hitting probability from a diffusion scale function is
p=α2−k−β2−k1−β2−k​(k=2).​
(6)
For k=2, applying Itô's formula to logYt​ gives a bounded stopped martingale instead, yielding
p=logβ−logαlogβ​(k=2).​
(7)
If k>2 and β>1 is fixed, the first expression tends to zero as α↓0. A continuous path that reaches zero before it reaches β must first reach every such α before β, so that event has probability zero. Any path hitting zero in finite time has a finite maximum up to that time, and therefore belongs to one of these zero-probability events with an integer upper boundary β>1. Their countable union still has probability zero. Thus the process does not hit zero almost surely. This is the relevant part of the Hitting-zero classification for a Bessel process.

Set An​={sups≤t​∣Ms(n)​∣≥2−(n−1)/2}. Part (a) gives ∑n​P(An​)<∞. The Borel-Cantelli first lemma implies that, almost surely, only finitely many An​ occur. On the complementary full-probability event, all sufficiently late terms satisfy
sups≤t​∣Ms(n)​∣<2−(n−1)/2.
(10)
These deterministic bounds form a convergent geometric series. The finitely many earlier suprema are finite by continuity on the compact time interval. Hence
n∑​s≤tsup​∣Ms(n)​∣<∞almost surely.​
(11)
The series therefore converges absolutely and uniformly on [0,t] by the Weierstrass M-test, and its sample-path limit is continuous.

Work modulo indistinguishability of stochastic processes, with norm ∥M∥=(E∣Mt​∣2)1/2. It is a genuine norm: zero terminal value implies Ms​=E[Mt​∣Fs​]=0 at every fixed time, and continuity extends equality from countably many rational times to all times simultaneously.
Let Mj be a Cauchy sequence in this norm. Choose a subsequence with ∥Mjn+1​−Mjn​∥<2−n. Part (b) applies to these difference martingales, so their telescoping series converges uniformly almost surely. Its limit has continuous paths. At each time s, conditional-expectation contractivity gives
∥Msj​−Msk​∥2​≤∥Mtj​−Mtk​∥2​,
(12)
so the fixed-time limit also exists in L2 and agrees with the pathwise limit. It has an adapted version obtained by taking the finite pointwise subsequence limit when it exists, and zero otherwise. Each such definition is measurable at its own time, and the almost surely uniform limit gives continuous paths. Passing to the L2 limit in E[Mvj​∣Fs​]=Msj​ shows that the limit is a martingale. The original Cauchy sequence converges to it in terminal norm. Thus the space is complete.
Part (b) supplies what fixed-time L2 completeness alone does not: a single full-probability set on which a subsequence converges uniformly in time, preserving path continuity. This is completeness of continuous square-integrable martingales.

The initial value can first be subtracted: it is F0​-measurable and has the same integrability under both measures, since the density has conditional expectation one on F0​. Put N=M−⟨M,X⟩. Since dZ=ZdX, the Itô product rule and the quadratic covariation identity d⟨M,Z⟩=Zd⟨M,X⟩ give
d(ZN)=NdZ+ZdM−Zd⟨M,X⟩+d⟨M,Z⟩=NdZ+ZdM.
(19)
This is a local P-martingale. Stop N at increasing bounded levels; the resulting products with Z are true martingales, since they are local martingales dominated at stopping times by a constant times the uniformly integrable density process. Part (c) then shows that the stopped N processes are Q-martingales. Consequently
M−⟨M,X⟩ is a continuous local Q-martingale.​
(20)
The unqualified true-martingale assertion printed in this part needs an additional integrability hypothesis. One sufficient version is a square-integrable P-martingale M. Indeed Zt​∈L2(P) by boundedness of Y, and the covariation bound gives
sups≤t​∣⟨M,X⟩s​∣≤Ct​⟨M⟩t1/2​.
