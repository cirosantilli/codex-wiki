If k>2 and β>1 is fixed, the first expression tends to zero as α↓0. A continuous path that reaches zero before it reaches β must first reach every such α before β, so that event has probability zero. Any path hitting zero in finite time has a finite maximum up to that time, and therefore belongs to one of these zero-probability events with an integer upper boundary β>1. Their countable union still has probability zero. Thus the process does not hit zero almost surely. This is the relevant part of the Hitting-zero classification for a Bessel process.
