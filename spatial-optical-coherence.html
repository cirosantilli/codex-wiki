Spatial optical coherence (g12(1)​(0))

 ... Physics Branches of physics Optics Physical optics Optical coherence First-order optical coherence
OurBigBook.com Words: 498
Spatial optical coherence compares field samples at different positions, usually across a transverse plane at equal time. Two small openings separated by d sample this correlation: recombine their light with nearly matched travel times and measure interferometric visibility near the central interference fringes. A narrow opening in front of a broad-spectrum lamp can improve transverse spatial optical coherence while leaving its short temporal optical coherence. These are separate controls: narrow spectral selection by itself need not make an extended source spatially coherent. MIT's lecture notes illustrate the two-opening test and these contrasting source preparations. [ref].
To isolate source width, stipulate a distant, narrowband source with uniform angular brightness on −Θ/2≤α≤Θ/2, and mutually uncorrelated emission from distinct source directions. Here Θ≪1 is the full angular width in radians, λ the common wavelength, and the two openings are separated by d along the same transverse direction as the source extent. Neglect amplitude differences across the openings. Each source direction contributes a phase difference approximately 2πdα/λ. Averaging its complex cross term gives

The sinc function is extended continuously to one at q=0. The sign convention in the exponential depends on which opening is numbered first; the symmetric model gives the same real result either way. Equal detected single-opening light intensities make the magnitude equal to interferometric visibility. A displaced source center adds a deterministic wave phase, shifting the interference fringes without changing their contrast.
The strips show I/(I1​+I2​)=1+g12(1)​(0)cosϕ with equal single-opening light intensities, a common phase difference coordinate ϕ and no single-slit diffraction envelope. At q=1/2, contrast is 2/π≈0.636620; at q=1, it vanishes. At q=3/2, the correlation is −2/(3π)≈−0.212207: weak fringes return with bright and dark positions exchanged. For λ=500nm and d=0.5mm, the first zero occurs at Θ=1mrad. This is a calculated example, not an experimental measurement or a universal source-width law.
A proposed source-width control follows directly from this model: illuminate two fixed openings with a distant adjustable luminous slit of full width w at distance R, so Θ≈w/R under the small-angle approximation, and hold d and the spectral selection fixed. Increasing w overlays more mutually incoherent, displaced interference fringe patterns. Record profiles over several near-central fringes, measure each opening separately and correct intensity imbalance as in first-order optical coherence. Restrict the recorded region so the path difference stays small compared with the optical coherence length, and resolve the fringes with the detector. Real source nonuniformity, finite opening widths and spectral spread modify the stipulated sinc function law. This protocol is a model-based extension of the verified two-opening method, not a claim that the plotted curves were measured.
Thus an extended incoherent source can give locally excellent wave interference when the sampled separation is small, and almost none when it is larger. The useful scale dΘ∼λ expresses how source extent limits the aperture over which coherent addition is available. The same angular averaging explains why narrowing a source opening can recover visible interference fringes, even without narrowing its optical spectrum.
