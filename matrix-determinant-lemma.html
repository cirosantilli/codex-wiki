Matrix determinant lemma (det(A+uvT)=det(A)(1+vTA−1u))

 Home Mathematics Area of mathematics Algebra Linear algebra
OurBigBook.com Words: 267
For an invertible matrix A∈Rn×n and real vectors u,v∈Rn, the matrix determinant lemma states
det(A+uvT)=det(A)(1+vTA−1u).
(121)
The expression vTA−1u is a scalar, so a rank-one matrix update changes the determinant by a single multiplicative factor. One need not expand the updated determinant entry by entry.
For the preceding example with A=I, the dot product is vTu=3−2=1. Hence the updated determinant is 1+1=2. Direct evaluation confirms
det(46​−1−1​)=4(−1)−(−1)6=2.
(122)
More generally, let B(t)=I+tuvT for a real scalar t. The same calculation gives detB(t)=1+t. The determinant is one at t=0, two at t=1, and zero at t=−1.
A compact proof explains the scalar reduction. Put w=A−1u and factor A+uvT=A(I+wvT). If w=0, choose a basis starting with w. In that basis, (I+wvT)w=(1+vTw)w, while every other basis vector changes only by a multiple of w. Its matrix is triangular with diagonal 1+vTw,1,…,1, giving the required determinant. If w=0, the correction vanishes. This uses multiplicativity of the determinant to restore the factor detA.
The denominator in the Sherman–Morrison formula is exactly this factor. The rank-one update singularity criterion therefore identifies when the updated matrix inverse ceases to exist. Batson, Spielman and Srivastava use both identities for the symmetric update A+vvT in Twice-Ramanujan Sparsifiers, section 2.3, Lemmas 2.1–2.2.
