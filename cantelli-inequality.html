Cantelli inequality (P(X−EX≥a)≤σ2/(σ2+a2))

 ... Mathematics Area of mathematics Probability and statistics Probability theory Probability inequality Chebyshev's inequality
OurBigBook.com Words: 447 Articles: 1
Cantelli's inequality uses a mean and variance to control deviations in one specified direction. For a real random variable X with expected value μ, finite variance σ2, and a>0,
P(X−μ≥a)≤σ2+a2σ2​.​
(6)
Applying the same statement to −X gives the lower tail probability bound P(X−μ≤−a)≤σ2/(σ2+a2). No independence or particular probability distribution is required. If σ2=0, the random variable equals μ almost surely and both positive-threshold tail probabilities are zero.
For variance one and a threshold two above the mean, the upper-tail probability is at most 1/5. Applying the two-sided Chebyshev's inequality to contain that upper event would give the weaker bound 1/4. For the standard normal distribution, the actual upper tail probability at two is about 0.0227501, much smaller than either bound. Yet the two-point probability distribution in equality in Cantelli's inequality attains 1/5, showing why the smaller Gaussian value cannot be promised from these two moments alone.
A short explanation is to centre Y=X−μ and choose a nonnegative shift b. On Y≥a, the square (Y+b)2 is at least (a+b)2, so Markov's inequality gives
P(Y≥a)≤(a+b)2E(Y+b)2​=(a+b)2σ2+b2​.
(7)
The best shift is b=σ2/a, giving the displayed bound. Choosing the shift before squaring exploits the direction of the deviation; squaring the unshifted random variable treats both directions together. This shifted-square proof is also the strategy requested in the University of Utah's 2005 qualifying problem on Cantelli's inequality. [ref].
The one-sided improvement should be used for a one-sided question. Adding upper and lower bounds by the union bound gives 2σ2/(σ2+a2) for ∣X−μ∣≥a. When a≥σ>0, that is at least the Chebyshev's inequality bound σ2/a2; when a<σ, both two-sided expressions exceed one and must be capped. The separate one-sided bounds generally cannot be attained simultaneously by the same probability distribution.
