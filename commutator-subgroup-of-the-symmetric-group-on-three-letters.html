Commutator subgroup of the symmetric group on three letters (S3′​=A3​)

 Home Mathematics Area of mathematics Algebra Group theory Commutator subgroup
OurBigBook.com Words: 163
For the symmetric group S3​, take the transpositions x=(12) and y=(23). Products of permutations apply the rightmost factor first. Then
xy=(123),yx=(132),[x,y]=(12)(23)(12)(23)=(132).
(14)
The last permutation sends 1↦3, 3↦2, and 2↦1: two swaps fail to commute by an entire three-cycle.
Every group commutator has sign of a permutation +1, so S3′​⊆A3​, the alternating group of even permutations. Conversely, the displayed group commutator generates A3​={e,(123),(132)}. Therefore S3′​=A3​. The two cosets are the even permutations and the odd permutations, and their multiplication is just multiplication of signs:
S3ab​=S3​/A3​≅{+1,−1}≅C2​.
(15)
This is the first isomorphism theorem applied to the sign of a permutation. Once commutativity is forced, all three transpositions become indistinguishable, and both three-cycles become the identity element. The surviving information is exactly whether the permutation is even or odd. Compare the very different center of a group Z(S3​)={e}.
