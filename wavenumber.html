Wavenumber (k)

 Home Mathematics Area of mathematics Analysis Partial differential equation Wave equation
OurBigBook.com Words: 2k Articles: 19
A wavenumber measures how rapidly a wave phase changes with distance. Two conventions coexist: angular wavenumber counts radians per unit length, while ordinary spatial frequency counts cycles per unit length. For a monochromatic wave with wavelength λ>0,
K=λ2π​,νx​=λ1​,K=2πνx​.
(64)
Here K≥0 is the magnitude of the angular wavenumber. The symbol k can instead denote a signed one-dimensional angular wavenumber: a factor eikx has wavelength 2π/∣k∣ for real k=0. Both conventions have dimensions of inverse length; writing radm−1 or “cycles per metre” exposes the convention. A quoted value in m−1 alone does not resolve the factor of 2π. Spectroscopic wavenumber usually uses the cycles-per-length convention.[ref]
For a real monochromatic plane wave, fix the convention
u(x,t)=Acosϕ(x,t),ϕ=k⋅x−ωt+ϕ0​,K=∣k∣.
(65)
The wavevector k supplies both the spatial wave phase slope and the normal to the wavefronts; its dot product with position makes the wave phase constant on parallel planes. The angular frequency is ω=2πf, with f>0 the ordinary frequency. Moving a distance λ along k changes the wave phase by 2π. Holding a wave crest's wave phase fixed gives its phase velocity ωk/K2 and phase speed
vp​=Kω​=fλ.
(66)
In one dimension the signed phase velocity is ω/k. This identifies a moving pattern; it does not identify the motion of the medium's particles or a wave packet's group velocity.
For the synthetic plane wave below, choose λ=0.40m and f=5Hz. Then K=5πradm−1, νx​=2.5 cycles per metre, ω=10πrads−1, and vp​=2ms−1. The wave crests are 0.40m apart at one instant; after a quarter oscillation period, each has advanced 0.10m. The lower panel retains whole turns of wave phase, making its slope visible instead of reducing it modulo 2π.
