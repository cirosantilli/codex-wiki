Appleton-Barnett radio interference experiment

 Home Physics Branches of physics Geophysics Upper atmosphere Ionosphere
OurBigBook.com Words: 725 Articles: 1
On 11 December 1924, Edward Victor Appleton and Miles Aylmer Fulton Barnett perform an experiment: the Bournemouth broadcasting radio transmitter slowly changes wavelength; an Oxford radio receiver records alternating maxima and minima from ground wave-skywave wave interference. The inferred equivalent height is about 90km. A separate comparison of simultaneous loop and vertical antenna signals tests the downward arrival direction. The E layer is established first; the higher F layer is found in winter 1926–27. These dates describe different findings.[ref]
Why change the wavelength deliberately? Ordinary fading alone could arise from changing emission, receiver drift or a moving propagation path. A controlled sweep predicts a specific progression of phase difference between two arrivals. The directional antenna comparison supplies an independent kind of evidence for an overhead contribution. Agreement supports an atmospheric return; fitting a distance alone would not establish a unique electron density profile. In the model below, comparable received amplitudes make the interference fringes easier to see. A practical repetition would also calibrate the frequency sweep, track radio transmitter output and receiver gain, and compare repeated sweeps with fixed-frequency intervals to detect time drift. These are controls inferred from the measurement model, rather than additional procedures attributed to the historical account.
The radio receiver does not need to resolve two pulses to detect two paths. If one path takes longer, the two contributions reaching the receiver during a slow frequency sweep carry slightly different emission frequencies and can produce a slow beat in the detected amplitude. At strictly fixed frequency with stationary paths, the arrivals instead have a fixed phase difference: there is no continuing beat merely because one path is longer. This distinction connects the historical observation to ionospheric interference fringe spacing.
