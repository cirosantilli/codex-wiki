Adiabatic vertical modes of a Gaussian atmosphere (σn2​=Ω2[1+γ(2n+1)])

 Home Physics Branches of physics Fluid mechanics Acoustic wave
OurBigBook.com Words: 80
A plane-parallel atmosphere with isothermal sound speed cs​ in gravity −Ω2z has mass density ρ0​e−z2/(2H2), H=cs​/Ω. Above a rigid base, vertically directed adiabatic fluid perturbations satisfy w′′−zw′/H2+(σ2/Ω2−1)w/(γH2)=0 and w(0)=0. Set x=z/H and λ=(σ2/Ω2−1)/γ. The substitution y=e−x2/4w converts finite Gaussian-weighted kinetic energy into square integrability for −y′′+x2y/4=(λ+1/2)y. Odd extension across the base and the oscillator lowering operator force λ to be an odd positive integer. Thus wn​ is proportional to the Probabilists' Hermite polynomial He2n+1​(z/H). These are vertical acoustic waves; a general stably stratified atmosphere also admits internal gravity waves with horizontal structure.
