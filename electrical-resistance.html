Electrical resistance (R)

 ... Physics Branches of physics Electromagnetism Electric charge Electric current Electrical resistance and conductance
OurBigBook.com Words: 4k Articles: 24
Electrical resistance measures the electric potential difference needed to drive a given electric current through a component. For an Ohmic conductor under fixed material conditions, R=V/I is constant and Ohm's law gives V=IR. The SI unit is the ohm, 1Ω=1V/A. At a fixed positive electric potential difference, increasing electrical resistance reduces electric current. Electrical conductance G=1/R expresses the same response reciprocally. [ref].
For a straight uniform wire at rest, with constant electrical conductivity σ>0, length L and wire cross-sectional area A, the uniform Ohmic conductor model gives
R=σAL​=ρel​AL​,ρel​=σ1​.
(77)
The electrical resistivity ρel​ characterizes the material; electrical resistance also includes geometry. Doubling length doubles R, while doubling wire cross-sectional area halves R, with material and temperature fixed. The uniform-field assumption includes insulated sides and equipotential end faces, as explained in uniform current in a straight homogeneous wire. [ref].
As a defined wire example, take L=1m, A=1mm2 and σ=5×105(Ωm)−1. Then R=2Ω; applying electric potential difference 5V drives electric current 2.5A. With the same length and material, a second wire of wire cross-sectional area 2mm2 has R=1Ω and carries 5A at that same electric potential difference. Fixing electric potential difference here differs from fixing electric current in the current density comparison: stating what is held fixed is essential.
