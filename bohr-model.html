Bohr model

 Home Physics Mathematical physics Hydrogen atom
OurBigBook.com Words: 965 Articles: 3
The Bohr model assumes circular electron orbits with quantized orbital angular momentum Nℏ. It reproduces the hydrogen energy levels, but wavefunctions in quantum mechanics describe spatial probability distributions rather than definite trajectories. In particular, spherically symmetric Coulomb bound states have zero orbital angular momentum.
The puzzle is the coexistence of reproducible spectral lines and stable chemical atoms. A classical circular orbit in a Coulomb potential can have any negative energy: Coulomb's law alone selects no special radius or emitted frequency. Moreover, its electron has centripetal acceleration. The Larmor formula then predicts positive radiated power, so conservation of energy makes the orbit shrink. Simply drawing concentric circles explains neither stability nor a discrete spectrum.
Niels Bohr postulated nonradiating stationary states and emission with hν=Ei​−Ef​. His circular-orbit condition is L=nℏ. These assumptions override classical radiation; the emitted frequency need not equal an orbital frequency.[ref]
