Discrete sampling inner product on polynomial spaces (⟨f,g⟩=∑j=0n​wj​f(tj​)g(tj​))

 Home Mathematics Area of mathematics Algebra Linear algebra Inner product
OurBigBook.com Words: 137
Let Pn​(R) be the vector space of real polynomials of degree of a polynomial at most n. Choose n+1 distinct real sample points t0​,…,tn​ and positive real weights w0​,…,wn​. Then
⟨f,g⟩=∑j=0n​wj​f(tj​)g(tj​)
(2)
is an inner product. Symmetry and bilinearity follow from linearity of polynomial evaluation. Its value at (f,f) is nonnegative, and equality forces all f(tj​)=0. By the root bound for a polynomial, a nonzero polynomial of degree of a polynomial at most n cannot vanish at n+1 distinct points. Thus the form has positive definiteness.
Equivalently, the polynomial evaluation map on a finite point set identifies Pn​(R) with Rn+1, equipped with a weighted Euclidean inner product. Too few sample points destroy positive definiteness: if the distinct points number at most n, the nonzero polynomial ∏j​(x−tj​) lies in Pn​(R) and has zero sampled squared norm.
For three equally weighted samples at −1,0,1, an orthonormal basis is
1,23​​x,2​3x2−2​.
(3)
This construction gives a finite sampling version of orthogonal polynomials and permits exact Gram-Schmidt orthogonalization using only finitely many evaluations.
