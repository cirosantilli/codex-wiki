Why the 2-core is unique

 Home Mathematics Area of mathematics Foundations of mathematics Graph theory 2-core of a graph
OurBigBook.com Words: 155
Suppose T is any vertex set whose induced subgraph has every degree of a vertex at least two. No vertex of T can be the first one deleted from T: before such a deletion it still has its two graph neighbours within T, contradicting eligibility for deletion. Consequently every qualifying T survives every legal deletion sequence. Once deletion stops, its remainder R itself qualifies, because every surviving vertex has at least two surviving graph neighbours. Therefore T⊆R for every qualifying T: G[R] is the unique maximum and the final remainder is independent of deletion order. The intermediate FIFO queues and deletion orders can differ.
There is also a direct existence argument. If A and B qualify, then A∪B qualifies: each vertex retains at least the two graph neighbours it already had in A or in B. Taking the union of all qualifying vertex sets produces the maximum. This rules out a collection of competing, incomparable inclusion-maximal answers. It also explains why restricting the search to connected subgraphs would change the question.
