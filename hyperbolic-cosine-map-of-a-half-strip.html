Hyperbolic-cosine map of a half-strip (ζ=cosh(πz/a))

 Home Mathematics Area of mathematics Geometry and topology Conformal map
OurBigBook.com Words: 110
For a>0, this conformal map takes Rez>0, 0<Imz<a bijectively onto the upper half-plane. Its imaginary part is sinh(πx/a)sin(πy/a)>0, and its derivative is nonzero in the interior. The end wall maps to [−1,1] and the two long walls to the complementary real rays. To prove the map is onto and injective, first put s=eπz/a, which maps onto ∣s∣>1, Ims>0. The equation s+s−1=2ζ has exactly one root outside the unit circle when Imζ>0: the roots have product one and neither has unit modulus. The identity Imζ=(1−∣s∣−2)Ims/2 puts that root in the correct half-plane. This permits planar potential flow problems in a closed-end channel to be solved by the method of images.
