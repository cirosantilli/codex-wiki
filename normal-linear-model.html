Variance of a fitted regression mean (Var(vTβ​)=σ2vT(XTX)−1v)

Words: 60
Normal linear model OurBigBook.com
For a full-column-rank design matrix in a normal linear model, a fitted mean at covariate vector v is vTβ​. The covariance matrix of the least-squares estimator immediately gives the displayed variance. Replacing σ2 by the independent unbiased residual variance gives its squared standard error and a Student t confidence interval on n−p statistical degrees of freedom. A future response instead contributes an additional error variance σ2 when its error is independent of the fitted data.

Analysis of covariance (E(Yi​)=α+βxi​+τGi​​)

Words: 70
Normal linear model OurBigBook.com
Analysis of covariance combines a categorical treatment and a quantitative covariate in a normal linear model. A common-slope model is Yi​=α+βxi​+∑g=2G​τg​1{Gi​=g}​+εi​, with independent N(0,σ2) errors. Treatment differences compare fitted means at the same covariate value. Lack of fit is a mismatch between a regression model's mean function and the true mean relationship. Replicated predictor configurations can separate its sum of squares from pure error. Lack-of-fit F-test (F=SSPE​/(n−g)(RSS−SSPE​)/(g−p)​)

Words: 75
Lack of fit OurBigBook.com
For n independent normal responses of common variance at g distinct predictor settings, a rank-p normal linear model has residual degrees n−p. The saturated setting-mean model has g parameters and pure error degrees n−g. Splitting orthogonal residual spaces gives lack of fit degrees g−p. Under the fitted mean model, the two squared lengths divided by the common variance are independent chi-squared variables; their ratio of mean squares has the F-distribution shown. This test is unavailable without repeated settings or positive lack-of-fit degrees.

Normal regression with independent coefficient and variance priors (b∼N(b0​,D),s∼InvGamma(a0​,c0​),b⊥s)

Words: 118
Gaussian conjugacy for a normal linear model OurBigBook.com
In a normal linear model Y∣b,s∼N(Xb,sI), assign the coefficient vector b a proper normal distribution with fixed positive-definite covariance matrix D, independently of the variance s, whose prior distribution is inverse-gamma distribution. Completing the square gives
V(s)=(D−1+XTX/s)−1,m(s)=V(s)(D−1b0​+XTy/s),b∣s,y∼N(m(s),V(s)).
(2)
Multiplying the residual likelihood function by the inverse-gamma distribution density also gives
s∣b,y∼InvGamma(a0​+n/2,c0​+∥y−Xb∥2/2).
(3)
These full conditional distributions permit a Gibbs sampler. The marginal coefficient posterior distribution is obtained by marginalization over s. Its conditional mean and covariance matrix both depend on s, so it is generally not a Student's t-distribution. Even if the columns of X and the prior coordinates make the conditional coefficient components independent, averaging over a shared unknown s need not preserve that independence.
A normal-inverse-gamma prior instead scales the conditional coefficient prior covariance matrix by s, producing a multivariate Student's t-distribution on marginalization. It is a different joint prior distribution.

Normal-gamma posterior with a flat prior (π(u,γ∣m))

Gaussian conjugacy for a normal linear model OurBigBook.com
Let m∣u,γ∼N(Au,γ−1Ik​), let the prior distribution of the precision be the shape-rate Gamma distribution Gamma(α,β), and use a constant improper prior for u∈Rd. Assume A has zero null space. Write B=ATA, u∗​=B−1ATm, a=α+k/2, b0​=β+∥m−Au∗​∥2/2, and s=α+(k−d)/2>0. The proper joint posterior density is proportional to
γa−1exp[−γ(b0​+21​(u−u∗​)TB(u−u∗​))].
(4)
Its conditional distributions are u∣γ,m∼N(u∗​,γ−1B−1) and γ∣u,m∼Gamma(a,β+∥m−Au∥2/2). Its marginal precision law is Gamma(s,b0​). Integration of the displayed kernel gives (2π)d/2Γ(s)/(detB​b0s​), proving propriety despite the improper prior.
A joint maximum a posteriori estimate relative to dudγ exists exactly when a>1, and is (u∗​,(a−1)/b0​). If a=1, the supremum is approached at γ=0 but not attained; if a<1, the density is unbounded there. Separate marginal maximization gives u∗​ for the unknown and (s−1)/b0​ for the precision when s>1. This illustrates that joint and marginal modes need not agree, and that a proper posterior distribution need not have a mode in its open parameter space.

Gaussian posterior in the zero-noise limit

Words: 69
Gaussian conjugacy for a normal linear model OurBigBook.com
For m=Au+η, with independent u∼N(0,Id​) and η∼N(0,δ2Ik​), the multivariate normal distribution of the Bayesian posterior has posterior mean bδ​=(ATA+δ2Id​)−1ATm and covariance matrix Cδ​=δ2(ATA+δ2Id​)−1. The singular value decomposition shows that the variance on a right singular vector of nonzero singular value s is δ2/(s2+δ2), while the variance along the null space is one. Thus, for fixed data, the mean tends to the minimum-norm least-squares solution and the covariance tends to the orthogonal projection onto the null space. Unobserved components retain their prior distribution even as the noise vanishes.

Rank-sum form of Cochran's theorem (∑i​Ai​=I,∑i​rankAi​=n)

Words: 114
Cochran's theorem OurBigBook.com
Let Ai​ be real symmetric matrices with sum In​. Their matrix ranks sum to at least n, because their ranges span Rn. If the ranks sum to n, those ranges form a direct sum. For y in the range of Ai​, the identity y=∑j​Aj​y and uniqueness of its range decomposition imply Aj​y=δij​y. Thus Ai2​=Ai​ and Ai​Aj​=0 for i=j: the matrices are mutually orthogonal orthogonal projections. In a combined orthonormal basis, the forms ZTAi​Z for Z∼Nn​(0,In​) use disjoint sets of independent standard normal coordinates. They are independent chi-squared distributions with degrees of freedom equal to the ranks. Conversely, if all these forms have those chi-squared laws, their expectations and their sum give ∑i​rankAi​=n.

Nonidentifiability prevents unbiased coefficient estimation

If a design matrix X has a nonzero null vector z, the normal linear model has identical observation distributions at β and β+z. Admissible polynomials for a singular inverse-linear weight

Words: 147
Weighted least squares OurBigBook.com
Consider the squared error of approximating xn, n≥1, by a polynomial q under a measure containing dx/x on (0,1). This error is finite exactly when q(0)=0. A nonzero constant term makes the integrand asymptotic to q(0)2/x, which is not Lebesgue integrable; a vanishing constant term makes xn−q(x)=O(x), giving an integrable O(x) integrand.
Consequently the full space of polynomials of degree at most n−1 is not contained in the weighted L2 space. Its admissible subspace is span{x,…,xn−1}, or zero when n=1. This finite-dimensional subspace has a unique orthogonal projection of xn, hence a unique best approximation. Indeed its Gram matrix is positive definite: a nonzero polynomial cannot vanish on an interval, so its squared norm is positive. Thus a singular weight can invalidate the proposed full approximation space while still allowing a unique finite-error minimizer in its admissible part. As usual, weighted L2 spaces identify functions equal almost everywhere.

As n increases it concentrates at the endpoints, where x2=1, explaining the limiting best constant. Although the weight vanishes at zero, its integral against the square of a nonzero continuous function is positive, so the associated weighted inner product is positive definite.

Equal-cell replication variance formula (Cov(μ​)=σ2I/m)

Words: 81
Cell-means parametrization OurBigBook.com
Suppose a normal linear model gives an unrestricted mean to each cell and has m independent observations per cell, with common error variance σ2. Each fitted cell mean is its sample mean, with variance σ2/m; different fitted cell means are independent. Estimate σ2 by the pooled within-cell residual sum of squares divided by n−q, where q is the number of cells. Thus a cell mean has standard error s/m​, a difference of two cell means has standard error s2/m​, and a two-by-two difference-of-differences has standard error 2s/m​.
