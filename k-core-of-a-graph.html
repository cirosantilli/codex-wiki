k-core of a graph (Ck​(G))

 Home Mathematics Area of mathematics Foundations of mathematics Graph theory
OurBigBook.com Words: 103
For a nonnegative integer k, the k-core of a graph is the unique maximum by set inclusion induced subgraph in which every degree of a vertex is at least k, allowing the empty graph. Repeatedly delete vertices whose current degree of a vertex is less than k. These induced subgraphs are nested: Ck+1​(G)⊆Ck​(G); the case k=2 is the 2-core. In their 2003 preprint, Vladimir Batagelj and Matjaž Zaveršnik give an algorithm for the entire core decomposition, using degree buckets and constant-time position updates. Their general bound is O(∣V∣+∣E∣); the title's O(m) form applies to connected graphs with at least two vertices, where m=∣E∣≥∣V∣−1. [ref]
