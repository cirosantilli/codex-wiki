Nuclear elastic electron scattering kinematics (E′=E/[1+2Esin2(θ/2)/(Mc2)])

 Home Physics Nuclear physics Atomic nucleus Nuclear recoil
OurBigBook.com Words: 121
An elastic scattering event leaves the nuclear internal quantum state unchanged while transferring energy to its motion. Let a target of mass M initially be at rest. Neglect the electron rest mass, but retain relativistic target recoil. Conservation of momentum and conservation of relativistic energy give
ℏ2c2q2=E2+E′2−2EE′cosθ,(Mc2+E−E′)2=M2c4+ℏ2c2q2.
(1)
Eliminating q yields Mc2(E−E′)=EE′(1−cosθ) and hence
E′=1+Mc22E​sin2(θ/2)E​,TR​=E−E′.
(2)
The static relation q=2Esin(θ/2)/(ℏc) follows only when E/(Mc2) is negligible. For small recoil, TR​≃ℏ2q2/(2M); this last approximation is nonrelativistic for the recoiling nucleus, not for the incident electron. The invariant spacelike transfer, expressed in energy-squared units, is
Q2=ℏ2c2q2−(E−E′)2=4EE′sin2(θ/2),
(3)
so Q/(ℏc) and the spatial inverse length q coincide only in the static limit. Recoil kinematics alone does not correct the static differential scattering cross-section: a finite-mass target also changes its kinematic prefactor and the appropriate current matrix elements.
