Dyadic conditional averages recover integrable functions (fn​→f almost everywhere and in L1)

 ... Mathematics Area of mathematics Analysis Real analysis Measure theory Conditional expectation
OurBigBook.com Words: 134
For an integrable function on [0,1], average f on each dyadic interval of length 2−n. The resulting step function fn​ converges to f almost everywhere and in the L1 norm. If U has the uniform distribution on [0,1], then fn​(U)=E[f(U)∣σ(bn​(U))], where bn​ rounds down to a dyadic endpoint. Refinement makes these conditional expectations a uniformly integrable martingale. The uniformly integrable martingale convergence theorem gives almost-sure and convergence in L1 to a limit Y.
Since bn​(U)→U, the generated limiting sigma-algebra contains U up to null sets. For every event A in any finite-stage sigma-algebra, convergence in the L1 norm gives E[Y1A​]=E[f(U)1A​]. The pi-lambda theorem extends this equality to the limiting sigma-algebra, identifying Y=f(U). The uniform distribution of U translates the two convergence conclusions into the assertions for fn​. Choosing a Borel representative of f and assigning arbitrary values at the endpoint one makes all random variables well defined without changing either conclusion.
