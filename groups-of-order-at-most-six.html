Groups of order at most six (C1​,C2​,C3​,C4​,C2​×C2​,C5​,C6​,S3​)

 Home Mathematics Area of mathematics Algebra Group theory Finite group theory
OurBigBook.com Words: 186
Up to group isomorphism, the finite groups with at most six elements are the trivial group, the cyclic groups C2​,C3​,C4​,C5​,C6​, the Klein four-group C2​×C2​, and the symmetric group S3​.
A group of prime cardinality is a cyclic group, since Lagrange's theorem forces any nonidentity element to generate it. For four elements, either an element has order of a group element four, or every nonidentity element is an involution. In the latter case the group of exponent two is abelian identity shows that two distinct nonidentity elements a,b generate {e,a,b,ab}≅C2​×C2​.
For a group of order six, either an element generates it or some element a has order of a group element three: otherwise all nonidentity elements would be involutions, producing a four-element subgroup in contradiction with Lagrange's theorem. Pairing each element with its inverse element gives an involution b outside ⟨a⟩. The subgroup ⟨a⟩ has index of a subgroup two and is a normal subgroup, so bab−1=a or a−1. The commuting case makes ab have order of a group element six, whereas the second case gives the multiplication rules of S3​ on the six distinct elements ai,aib, 0≤i<3.
All these groups embed in S4​ except C5​ and C6​. The former is excluded by Lagrange's theorem; the latter is excluded because the disjoint cycle decomposition of a permutation of four letters cannot have least common multiple of cycle lengths equal to six.
