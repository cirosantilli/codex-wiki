Set ∂t​u=0 and ν=0 in the Navier-Stokes equation. Dotting the resulting Euler equation with a displacement dr tangent to a streamline gives
d(2∣u∣2​)+ρdp​+gdz=0,2∣u∣2​+ρp​+gz=B.​
(1)
Here mass density ρ is constant, and B is constant along each streamline. The terms are kinetic energy per unit mass, the pressure work per unit mass available from pushing a parcel through the flow, and gravitational potential energy per unit mass. Their sum is conserved mechanical energy per unit mass; p/ρ is not an additional independently conserved parcel energy. Different streamlines can have different B unless, for example, the flow is also irrotational in a connected region.

For planar incompressible flow, ux​+wz​=0. Locally this permits a streamfunction with u=ψz​, w=−ψx​, because equality of the mixed derivatives enforces incompressibility. The remaining component of the curl is uz​−wx​=ψzz​+ψxx​, so irrotational flow requires Laplace's equation for ψ.
Put K=π/b. The sinusoidal contribution to the supplied streamfunction has ψxx​=U0​aK2cos(Kx)e−Kz and ψzz​=−U0​aK2cos(Kx)e−Kz; the linear term is also harmonic. Hence
u=U0​[1+aKcos(Kx)e−Kz],w=−U0​aKsin(Kx)e−Kz.
(2)
As z→∞, these approach the specified uniform flow, (u,w)=(U0​,0).
The impermeability kinematic boundary condition is w=uzs′​(x) on z=zs​(x), equivalently ψ is constant on the wall. For aK≪1, evaluating the perturbed velocities at z=0 gives w=−U0​aKsin(Kx)=U0​zs′​ to first order. The residual is of order U0​(aK)2. Equivalently ψ(x,zs​)=U0​acos(Kx)[1−e−Kacos(Kx)]=O(U0​Ka2). A parcel at fixed z spends time a/u(z) in the light. With no thermal conduction or thermal expansion, its energy balance is ρcdT/dt=qR​. Equivalently the steady advection equation is ρcu(z)Tx​=qR​(z) with T(0,z)=T0​. Therefore
T(a,z)=T0​+cu(z)aF0​k​e−kρz=T0​+cf2μaF0​k​z(b−z)e−kρz​.​
(12)
The no-slip boundary condition makes residence time diverge as either wall is approached. The resulting unbounded temperature is a failure of the approximations, rather than a physically steady wall prediction. Even small thermal conductivity transfers heat away from these layers; conduction into real perspex adds another heat sink. Thermal expansion can alter mass density, create buoyancy-driven motion and change the background profile, while temperature-dependent dynamic viscosity changes the assumed residence time. With exactly insulating walls, finite fluid thermal conductivity still regularizes the transverse profile; an entrance region and, sufficiently near a stagnant wall, longitudinal conduction also matter.

An equilibrium point of a dynamical system is a state x∗​ with f(x∗​)=0, so the trajectory stays there. In a planar phase portrait, an unstable spiral has a complex-conjugate pair of Jacobian matrix eigenvalues with positive real part: nearby trajectories rotate while moving outward. An attractor is an invariant set approached by trajectories from a neighborhood, its basin of attraction. It can be an equilibrium point, a limit cycle, or a more complicated set; outward spiralling near one equilibrium point does not preclude attraction to a larger set.

The Jacobian matrix of the Rössler system is
J=​01z​−1a0​−10x−c​​.​
(16)
At the origin the vertical homogeneous perturbation obeys δz˙=−cδz, so it decays as e−ct. Nearby, z˙≃a−cz also relaxes the trajectory toward z≃a/c, close to the x–y plane. The small fixed-point value of z weakly couples planar errors back into z; this is neglected in the requested approximation.
The planar block has trace a and determinant 1, with
λ1,2​=2a​±i1−4a2​​.​
(17)
For 0<a≪1, this is an unstable spiral. Writing X=x−x−​ and Y=y−y−​, its linearized trajectories have an envelope proportional to eat/2 and angular frequency approximately 1. They rotate counterclockwise: at X>0,Y=0, Y˙=X>0. Fast vertical contraction occurs on scale 1/c, much shorter than the planar growth scale 2/a.
Confinement weakens as x approaches c from below; the approximate vertical relaxation time is 1/(c−x), and the quasi-steady value a/(c−x) ceases to be small. For x>c, the coefficient of z becomes positive, so vertical departures are amplified. The plane z=0 is never exactly invariant because z˙=a there.

For a head sampling phases uniformly during steady sliding, its mean force is f0​ times the phase-average bound probability. In the B≪1 limit of spatially gated myosin attachment, integrating the three intervals gives
f0​Δ⟨f⟩​≃x1​−k1​u​(1−A)+(x2​−x1​)(1−A)+k2​u​(1−A)(1−B).

Covered by the official notes: comparison with a 200 keV neutrino mass (PDF p. 12; handwritten p. 7).[ref]
The notes stop at “no change”; the experimental implication is that this is a poorly conditioned neutrino mass measurement. In relativistic two-body decay, the corrections to the rest-frame energy and momentum enter through the square of the daughter rest mass, while the Lorentz transformation boosts both the massless value and its small correction. A measured laboratory energy or angle must therefore resolve a very small change against beam-energy spread, decay-angle uncertainty and detector resolution. No visible change at the quoted precision does not establish zero neutrino mass.

Covered by the official notes: definitions of four-force and four-velocity and the vanishing antisymmetric contraction (PDF p. 15; handwritten p. 10).[ref]
To make the necessity statement precise, mass preservation must hold for every allowed timelike four-velocity, with the same field tensor and nonzero charge. It is not necessary for one prescribed trajectory alone. Use metric signature (−,+,+,+), so Ua​Fa=−c2dm/dτ. Put Hab=(Fab+Fba)/2. The mass-preservation condition requires the quadratic form HabUa​Ub​=0 for every timelike U. Every timelike vector can be rescaled to a physical four-velocity; by homogeneity the quadratic form vanishes on the open timelike cone. A polynomial vanishing on an open set is identically zero, so Hab=0. Thus the electromagnetic field tensor must be antisymmetric under this universal quantifier. For a single velocity, the scalar contraction can vanish even for a nonzero symmetric part, which is why the quantifier is essential.
