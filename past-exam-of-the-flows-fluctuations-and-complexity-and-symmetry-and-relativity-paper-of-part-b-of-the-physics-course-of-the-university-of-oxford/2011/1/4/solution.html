Take positive circulation to mean counterclockwise in the (x,z) diagram, with x right and z up. The upper surface is then traversed right to left. To first order in a/b,
Γ=∮u⋅dl=∫−b/2b/2​(U0​−us​)dx=−2U0​a.​

Thus the bound circulation is clockwise. Any exterior contour enclosing the hydrofoil has the same circulation in an irrotational flow; a globally completed two-dimensional flow must contain a circulation component decaying as ∣Γ∣/(2πr) at large distance. The simple local upper/lower velocity prescription cannot describe the entire exterior. For a start from rest, Kelvin's circulation theorem requires compensating shed vorticity, represented by a starting vortex in a physical wake.

At this large Reynolds number, smooth laminar flow over the whole body is unlikely in ordinary conditions; wall layers may undergo transition and separate. A thin-layer inviscid flow approximation outside them can remain useful, but does not justify globally laminar flow.
