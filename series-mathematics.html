Series (mathematics, ∑n=1∞​an​)

 Home Mathematics Area of mathematics Analysis Real analysis Sequence and series
OurBigBook.com Words: 3k Articles: 32
A series adds the terms of a sequence in their specified order. Its infinite-sum notation is defined through finite partial sums:
SN​=∑n=1N​an​,∑n=1∞​an​=limN→∞​SN​
(19)
when the displayed limit of a sequence exists as a finite number. Otherwise the series is not a convergent series in the ordinary sense. Writing an infinite list of additions does not by itself assign it a finite sum. Cesàro summation is a different prescription, with its own definition.
Two positive series make the distinction concrete. For an​=1/n, the individual terms tend to zero but the harmonic series has unbounded partial sums. For an​=1/[n(n+1)], partial fraction decomposition reveals a telescoping series whose partial sums tend to one. The useful question is how much all the remaining terms can contribute together, rather than only how small the next term looks.
