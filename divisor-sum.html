Divisor sum (F(n)=∑d∣n​f(d))

 Home Mathematics Area of mathematics Number theory Arithmetic function Multiplicative function
OurBigBook.com Words: 516 Articles: 2
The divisor sum of an arithmetic function f assigns to each positive integer n the sum
F(n)=∑d∣n​f(d).
(13)
Only positive integer divisors are included, so F(1)=f(1). The weights f(d) can count the integer divisors, measure their sizes, or record other arithmetic information: the constant-one arithmetic function gives the divisor function, whereas the identity arithmetic function gives the sum-of-divisors function.
Equivalently, F=f∗1, where ∗ is Dirichlet convolution and 1 is the constant-one arithmetic function. If f is a multiplicative function, then F is a multiplicative function by Multiplicativity of Dirichlet convolution. [ref]
Here is the mechanism. For positive coprime integers m,n, every positive integer divisor of mn has a unique form ab with a∣m and b∣n; moreover, a,b are coprime integers. Thus a multiplicative function satisfies
F(mn)=∑a∣m​∑b∣n​f(a)f(b)=F(m)F(n).
(14)
At a prime power, the divisor sum becomes F(pa)=f(1)+f(p)+⋯+f(pa). This turns a calculation over many integer divisors into short local calculations at prime powers.
