Standing wave node spacing (Δx=λ/2)

 Home Physics Mathematical physics Standing wave
OurBigBook.com Words: 136
A node of a standing wave must have y(x,t)=0 for every time, not merely at one snapshot. For y=2asin(kx)cos(ωt), with a,k,ω>0, set t=0 to see that this requires sin(kx)=0. Thus
xj​=kjπ​=2jλ​,xj+1​−xj​=2λ​,j∈Z.
(107)
An antinode instead maximizes the oscillation amplitude 2a∣sin(kx)∣ and lies halfway between successive nodes of a standing wave, at x=(2j+1)λ/4. Neighbouring antinodes are also separated by λ/2; the distance from a node of a standing wave to its nearest antinode is λ/4. These distances refer to the string's longitudinal coordinate, not its curved arc length.
Within one interval between nodes of a standing wave, all moving points have the same oscillation phase. Adjacent intervals have opposite signs of sin(kx), so their phase difference is π: one loop rises while the next falls. The oscillation phase at an exact node of a standing wave is undefined because its amplitude is zero. A ruler measures the spacing of the fixed pattern; it does not track a moving wave crest.
