Spectral radiance (Lν​, Lλ​)

 Home Physics Branches of physics Optics Radiometry Radiance
OurBigBook.com Words: 2k Articles: 8
Spectral radiance is radiance per unit frequency or wavelength interval. Its astronomical synonym specific intensity is usually written Iν​=Lν​ or Iλ​=Lλ​; this Iν​ is a spectral radiance, distinct from the radiant intensity I in radiometry. For positive small intervals,
d3Φ=Lν​cosθdAdΩdν=Lλ​cosθdAdΩdλ.
(11)
The two expressions refer to the same spectral slice, with the positive widths related through frequency ν and wavelength λ. For frequency measured in hertz, Hz=s−1, the units are [Lν​]=Wm−2sr−1Hz−1. For wavelength measured in metres, [Lλ​]=Wm−2sr−1m−1. Integrating over a spectral band gives its radiance, and then multiplying by the collecting projected area and accepted solid angle gives a small uniform beam's radiant flux. A spectral measurement therefore retains information that the band-integrated power alone loses.
For an ideal blackbody, Planck's law gives Lν​=Bν​(T) and Lλ​=Bλ​(T) at temperature T, independent of outgoing direction. Its existing frequency and wavelength peaks of Planck's law illustrate why the coordinate matters.
