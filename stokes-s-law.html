Stokes's law (FD​=−6πμaU)

 ... Physics Branches of physics Fluid mechanics Viscous fluid flow Stokes flow Surface traction in translating-sphere Stokes flow
OurBigBook.com Words: 467 Articles: 4
For an isolated rigid sphere of radius a moving at velocity U relative to a distant uniform Newtonian fluid, the drag force exerted by the fluid is
FD​=−6πμaU,∣FD​∣=6πμaU.
(27)
The assumptions are steady incompressible flow, constant dynamic viscosity μ, an unbounded surrounding fluid, the no-slip boundary condition, and a small diameter-based particle Reynolds number, Red​=2aρf​U/μ≪1. Integrating the surface traction in translating-sphere Stokes flow gives the factor 6π: the disturbance pressure contributes 2πμaU to the drag magnitude and the viscous shear stress contributes 4πμaU. See Richard Fitzpatrick, Axisymmetric Stokes Flow Around a Solid Sphere, equations 10.120–10.129.
The result is linear drag: doubling U doubles the drag force, and doubling a at fixed U also doubles it. The required power is 6πμaU2, all supplied to viscous dissipation. Density enters the check of the particle Reynolds number, although it does not appear in the leading drag formula.
Nearby walls or other particles change the hydrodynamic resistance matrix and invalidate the isolated-sphere coefficient, even at very small Reynolds number. Finite Reynolds number introduces inertia corrections; the Oseen approximation resolves the distant region where the leading Stokes flow approximation ceases to be uniform. A clean gas bubble has tangential slip and instead obeys clean-bubble Stokes drag, with coefficient 4πμa in its spherical, negligible-internal-viscosity limit. See H. K. Moffatt, Six Lectures on General Fluid Dynamics, section 3.4 for the sphere solution and its small-Reynolds number limitation. The continuum mechanics and boundary assumptions must also remain valid as the particle becomes very small.
Balancing this Stokes drag law against gravity and buoyancy gives the Stokes settling velocity of an isolated rigid sphere.
