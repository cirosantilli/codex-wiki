Fourier response of a first-order linear differential equation (y​n​=g​n​/(a+in))

For y′+ay=g with real a>0 and 2π-periodic forcing, comparing Fourier coefficients gives (a+in)y​n​=g​n​. There is no vanishing denominator, including the constant mode. With real cosine and sine coefficients an​,bn​ for g and An​,Bn​ for y, the equivalent equations give
An​=a2+n2aan​−nbn​​,Bn​=a2+n2nan​+abn​​.
(1)
Whenever termwise differentiation is justified, these formulas construct a periodic particular solution. For a forcing series with coefficients O(n−2), the differentiated response coefficients are also summable, which supplies such a justification by uniform convergence. All other solutions add the homogeneous solution Ce−ax. Since this exponential cannot be periodic unless C=0, the periodic solution is unique. This is also a simple example of how a linear equation changes the amplitude and phase of each harmonic independently.
