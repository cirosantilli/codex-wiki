Unramified division torsors at good primes (v∤m,E good at v)

 ... Genus one curve Elliptic curve Mordell-Weil group Weak Mordell-Weil theorem Kummer-theoretic proof of the weak Mordell-Weil theorem Kummer exact sequence of an elliptic curve
OurBigBook.com Words: 167
At a prime of good reduction of an elliptic curve with residue characteristic not dividing m, multiplication by m extends to a finite étale map of the smooth proper elliptic group scheme over the valuation ring. A local rational point extends to a section by properness. Its division fibre is therefore a finite étale torsor, so the Kummer map of an elliptic curve gives an unramified cohomology class. This bounds ramification in the Weak Mordell-Weil theorem.
A direct proof describes the coordinates, not only the cohomology class. Reduce P by properness and choose a point Q​ with [m]Q​=P over a finite extension of the residue field. Over the corresponding unramified extension of the complete local field, lift Q​ by the Hensel lemma. The error [m]Q0​−P is in the kernel of reduction of an elliptic curve; prime-to-residue-characteristic multiplication on a formal group corrects it uniquely. There is no m-torsion in that kernel, even after a ramified field extension, so every division point with that reduction equals this unramified lift. Consequently all division points of an elliptic curve above P have unramified coordinate fields. The torsion case is discussed in J. S. Milne's Elliptic Curves, Chapter V, Proposition 8.1.
