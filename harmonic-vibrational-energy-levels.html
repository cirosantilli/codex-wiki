Harmonic vibrational energy levels (Ev​=V0​+ℏω(v+1/2))

OurBigBook.com Words: 216
Quantizing the stretching coordinate gives a quantum harmonic oscillator with reduced mass μ:
H=V0​−2μℏ2​dq2d2​+21​kq2,Ev​=V0​+ℏω(v+21​),v=0,1,2,….
(28)
Here v is the vibrational quantum number and ℏ=h/(2π) the reduced Planck constant. Consecutive energy eigenvalues have gap ℏω; the ground state has zero-point energy ℏω/2 above the well minimum. The offset V0​ cancels from every transition energy.
A short proof uses creation and annihilation operators: with ℓ=ℏ/(μω)​, write q=ℓ(a+a†)/2​. The Quantum Hamiltonian becomes H−V0​=ℏω(a†a+1/2). Nonnegativity of a†a gives the lowest level; repeated application of a† raises its energy by ℏω each time. This also gives ⟨q⟩v​=0 and
⟨q2⟩v​=μωℏ​(v+21​).
(29)
The ground state therefore has a nonzero spread of bond lengths, even though its mean stretching displacement vanishes. A stationary energy eigenstate has a time-independent position probability density; the classical oscillation curve is not a trajectory of an atomic nucleus in that state.
The parabolic model formally extends q to the whole real line, whereas an actual bond length satisfies r>0. It is useful for low levels concentrated near re​, with typical stretching displacements much smaller than re​. It fails near breakup of the chemical bond and when neglected anharmonicity becomes substantial.
