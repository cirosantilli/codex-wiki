Exact conditional Pearson test for a binary contingency table (P(X=x∣K)=(KN​)∏i​(xi​ni​​)​)

Consider independent binomial counts Xi​∼Bin(ni​,p) under the common-probability null hypothesis, with fixed positive row sizes ni​. Let N=∑i​ni​, K=∑i​Xi​ and assume 0<K<N. Conditional on K, the probability mass function is the displayed multivariate hypergeometric distribution: in the joint likelihood, the factor pK(1−p)N−K is common to all admissible tables and cancels. The Vandermonde identity gives the denominator, so the conditional distribution is free of the unknown nuisance parameter p.
For a table x, its Pearson chi-squared statistic is
Q(x)=∑i​ni​K(N−K)(Nxi​−ni​K)2​.
(1)
An exact conditional p-value sums the displayed probabilities over all tables with the same margins and Q(x)≥Q(xobs​). The ordering is by the Pearson chi-squared statistic, rather than by the probability of an individual table; different exact-test orderings can give different answers. Enumeration is practical when K and the number of rows are small. Monte Carlo tests can approximate the same tail by sampling tables with fixed margins.[ref]
An exact arithmetic comparison is easy too. Let M be the least common multiple of the row sizes. Compare the integer
A(x)=∑i​(Nxi​−ni​K)2ni​M​,
(2)
since every table has the same positive denominator MK(N−K). This includes boundary ties without a floating-point tolerance. Conditional p-values are discrete and can be conservative; “exact” concerns their null calibration, not equality of rejection probability with every chosen significance level.
