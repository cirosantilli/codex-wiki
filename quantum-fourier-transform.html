Dyadic quantum Fourier transform circuit (QFT2n​)

Words: 81
Quantum Fourier transform OurBigBook.com
In most-significant-bit-first order, let x=x1​⋯xn​ and 0.xj​⋯xn​=∑l=jn​xl​2−(l−j+1). On a computational-basis state, process wire j with a Hadamard gate, then controlled phase gates Rl−j+1​ controlled by unprocessed wire l for each l>j. Wire j becomes (∣0⟩+e2πi0.xj​⋯xn​∣1⟩)/2​. Reversing the final wire order yields the positive-exponent quantum Fourier transform. There are n Hadamard gates and n(n−1)/2 controlled phase gates. Physical reversal costs ⌊n/2⌋ swap operators, each expressible as three controlled-NOT gates, themselves implemented with Hadamard gates and a Controlled-Z gate. Thus the entire construction uses O(n2) gates from the specified family.

Fourier sample (Pr(c)=∣⟨c∣F∣ψ⟩∣2)

Words: 62
Quantum Fourier transform OurBigBook.com
A Fourier sample is a computational-basis measurement outcome after applying a quantum Fourier transform F to a quantum state ∣ψ⟩. Its distribution is given by the squared Fourier amplitudes. For a uniform periodic coset of spacing r∣N, samples are the multiples sN/r with uniform s∈Zr​. Samples need not individually reveal the whole period: a greatest common divisor can remove factors from a single reduced denominator, motivating two-sample exact period recovery.

Two-sample exact period recovery (r=N/gcd(N,c1​,c2​))

Words: 80
Exact period recovery from a Fourier sample OurBigBook.com
Suppose a function has least period r∣N and distinct values on distinct period cosets. Two independent Fourier samples have the form cj​=sj​N/r with uniform sj​∈Zr​. The displayed candidate is r/gcd(r,s1​,s2​), so exact recovery occurs when no prime divisor of r divides both residues. The Chinese remainder theorem gives probability ∏ℓ∣r​(1−ℓ−2)≥1/ζ(2)=6/π2, using the Euler product. Unlike a single-sample coprimality probability, this bound is uniformly greater than one half. A candidate-period test makes successful recovery heralded when such a test is available.

Heralded exact quantum period finding when the period divides the register size

Words: 113 Articles: 1
Exact period recovery from a Fourier sample OurBigBook.com
Suppose a function on ZN​ has least period r∣N and is injective on each period. A Fourier sample c=sN/r gives the candidate denominator q=r/gcd(s,r). If equality of function values can be tested efficiently, then q is the full period exactly when f(q)=f(0), so each successful run is certified. A sample succeeds with probability φ(r)/r, and an inverse-polylogarithmic lower bound for this ratio permits amplification to constant success probability with polynomially many repetitions.

Hidden subgroup problem

The hidden subgroup problem asks for a subgroup H≤G given an oracle f:G→X that is constant on each left coset of H and takes different values on different cosets. Abelian instances are solved by preparing coset states and applying a group quantum Fourier transform.

Measuring the function register gives its normalized coset state. Applying the positive-exponent quantum Fourier transform to both input registers gives amplitude
MM​e2πikc1​/M​∑b=0M−1​e2πib(yc1​+c2​)/M.
(7)

Linear congruence from a discrete-logarithm Fourier sample (c1​y≡−c2​(modM))

Simon's problem

Words: 410 Articles: 7
Hidden subgroup problem OurBigBook.com
Simon's problem asks for the hidden string p∈{0,1}n promised by f(x)=f(y) exactly when x⊕y∈{0n,p}. It is the hidden subgroup problem on (Z2​)n with hidden subgroup {0n,p}.

Classical collision query bound for Simon's problem (Θ(2n/2))

Words: 74
Simon's problem OurBigBook.com
For an unknown nonzero period, a collision f(x)=f(y) reveals the period x⊕y. Birthday problem sampling finds one using O(2n/2) queries with bounded error. A matching lower bound follows by choosing the period and output labels randomly: without a collision, q queries rule out at most (2q​) candidate periods. For q=o(2n/2), almost all periods remain consistent with the transcript. The exponential separation is in query complexity, compared with Simon's algorithm's linear quantum query count.

Simon's algorithm

Words: 309 Articles: 5
Simon's problem OurBigBook.com
Simon's algorithm prepares hidden-subgroup coset states, applies the quantum Fourier transform over (Z2​)n, and samples vectors y satisfying y⋅p=0 over F2​. Repeated samples and Gaussian elimination recover p with O(n) oracle queries and polynomial computation.
To solve Simon's problem, prepare a uniform quantum superposition, evaluate the function coherently into a second quantum register, and perform Simon Fourier sampling on the first. Each run produces a uniform binary string z with z⋅s=0 over the two-element field. Once the observed rows have matrix rank n−1, Gaussian elimination recovers s as the unique nonzero element of their kernel. The random spanning of a binary vector space bounds the required number of runs.
Simon Fourier sampling (Pr(z)=21−n1z⋅s=0​)

A computational-basis measurement therefore samples the kernel s⊥ uniformly, independently of the representative x.

Simon's algorithm OurBigBook.com
In Simon's algorithm, conditioning on a function value leaves the first quantum register in an equal coherent sum of its two preimages. Expected query count for Simon sampling (ET=∑k=0n−2​(1−2k−(n−1))−1)
Words: 61
Simon's algorithm OurBigBook.com
With a nonzero hidden period, Simon's algorithm samples uniformly from its (n−1)-dimensional orthogonal complement over the binary field. At rank k, a new sample raises the rank with probability 1−2k−(n−1). Summing the geometric waiting times gives an expected n−1+∑j=1n−1​(2j−1)−1<n+1 queries. After n−1+c samples, the probability of insufficient rank is less than 2−c, by a union bound over the nonzero linear functionals that could annihilate all samples.

Quantum phase estimation

Given controlled powers of a unitary U and an eigenstate U∣ψ⟩=e2πiϕ∣ψ⟩, quantum phase estimation outputs an approximation to ϕ. If the input is a superposition of eigenstates, measurement selects an eigenphase with probability equal to the squared magnitude of its coefficient.

Quantum phase estimation tail bound (Pr(distZ/LZ​(J,j0​)≥m)≤[2(m−1)]−1)

Words: 94
Quantum phase estimation OurBigBook.com
Let L=2t, let j0​ be the nearest label to Lϕ, and let m≥2. The exact quantum phase estimation amplitude is L−1∑a=0L−1​e2πia(ϕ−j/L). The sine bound on circular distances gives probability at most 1/(4dj2​), where dj​ is the centered distance from j to Lϕ. At label distance ℓ from j0​, ∣dj​∣≥ℓ−1/2, with at most two labels for each ℓ. Since (ℓ−1/2)−2≤[(ℓ−1)ℓ]−1, the tail telescopes to the displayed bound. Adding u control qubits beyond b desired phase bits gives circular accuracy below 2−b with failure probability at most [2(2u−1)]−1.

Parallel phase multiplication by coherent fanout (Uϕ⊗r​: ∣0r⟩+∣1r⟩↦∣0r⟩+eirϕ∣1r⟩)

Words: 67
Quantum phase estimation OurBigBook.com
Use CNOT gates to encode one logical qubit and r−1 zero ancillas into the repetition subspace spanned by ∣0r⟩,∣1r⟩. Apply the phase gate in parallel to all r physical qubits, then undo the encoding. The logical phase is multiplied by r in one oracle-time layer. This copies computational-basis labels coherently rather than cloning an arbitrary quantum state. Blocks of sizes 1,2,4,…,2n−1 prepare a dyadic Fourier phase state using 2n−1 parallel oracle applications.

Quantum phase estimation with a maximally mixed target (p(m)=d−1∑j​∣am​(ϕj​)∣2)

Words: 80
Quantum phase estimation OurBigBook.com
For a d-dimensional unitary operator V=∑j=1d​eiϕj​∣vj​⟩⟨vj​∣, a maximally mixed state I/d on its target is the equal mixture of these orthonormal eigenvectors. Therefore quantum phase estimation with register size L produces label m with probability
p(m)=d1​∑j=1d​∣am​(ϕj​)∣2,am​(ϕ)=L1​∑x=0L−1​eix(ϕ−2πm/L).
(14)
This follows by linearity of unitary time evolution and the Born rule on the spectral mixture. If every eigenphase has an exact L-grid label, that label has probability equal to its multiplicity divided by d. Otherwise the exact distribution has tails, and the nearest labels are guaranteed only the nearest-integer success bound for quantum phase estimation weighted by the corresponding spectral probabilities.

Nearest-integer success bound for quantum phase estimation (Pr(nearest phase label)≥4/π2)

Words: 85
Quantum phase estimation OurBigBook.com
For a unitary operator eigenphase ϕ, an n-qubit quantum phase estimation register of size L=2n has output probability amplitude
am​(ϕ)=L1​∑x=0L−1​eix(ϕ−2πm/L).
(15)
Choose the nearest integer label m to Lϕ/(2π), with labels understood modulo L, and put d=Lϕ/(2π)−m, taking the representative ∣d∣≤1/2. The finite geometric series gives ∣am​∣=∣sin(πd)∣/[L∣sin(πd/L)∣]. For 0<∣d∣≤1/2, concavity of sine gives ∣sin(πd)∣≥2∣d∣, while ∣sin(πd/L)∣≤π∣d∣/L. Thus the probability of the nearest label is at least 4/π2. At d=0 it is exactly one. If two labels tie for nearest, each satisfies the bound.

Dyadic phase-gate identification (FN†​N−1/2∑x​e2πiax/N∣x⟩=∣a⟩,N=2n)

Words: 80
Quantum phase estimation OurBigBook.com
For the phase gate Ra​=diag(1,e2πia/2n), apply powers Ra2n−1​,…,Ra​ to a uniform quantum superposition on n qubits. Binary positional weights multiply the phases into e2πiax/2n on basis vector ∣x⟩. The inverse quantum Fourier transform returns ∣a⟩ exactly, by orthogonality of roots of unity. Preparing the powers by repeated applications uses 2n−1 copies of Ra​; this does not claim a number of gate calls polynomial in n. If a is odd, its powers generate the entire set of dyadic phase gates because a has a modular inverse modulo 2n.

Tensor-product eigenphase amplification (U⊗n∣ψ⟩⊗n=e2πinϕ∣ψ⟩⊗n)

Words: 68
Quantum phase estimation OurBigBook.com
An eigenstate with phase ϕ becomes an eigenstate with phase nϕ under the tensor product of n copies of the unitary operator. A promise 0≤ϕ<1/n prevents modulo-one aliasing. For n=2s and dyadic ϕ=x/2m, exact quantum phase estimation then needs m−s phase bits and 2m−s−1 queries to a supplied joint controlled oracle. This is a saving in joint-oracle calls, not in the total number of individual oracle invocations needed to build that joint operation.

Positive-phase fractional power of a unitary operator (U+1/M​=∑j​e2πiϕj​/M∣vj​⟩⟨vj​∣)

Words: 100
Quantum phase estimation OurBigBook.com
Choose eigenphase representatives 0≤ϕj​<1 for a unitary operator U and a positive integer M. Its positive-phase fractional power acts on each eigenstate by e2πiϕj​/M. This branch uses arguments in [0,2π) and can differ from the usual complex principal branch using (−π,π]. For exactly dyadic phases, exact quantum phase estimation stores each label in a coherent phase register; bitwise phase gates multiply it by the required phase, and inverse phase estimation removes the labels. Keeping the phase register unmeasured preserves arbitrary eigenstate superpositions and resets the ancilla qubits. With only controlled-U and controlled-U−1 primitives, the direct method uses 2(2n−1) oracle calls for n exact phase bits.

Quantum spectral filtering (h(A))

Words: 239 Articles: 2
Quantum phase estimation OurBigBook.com
Let a Hermitian operator A have dyadic eigenvalues λj​=cj​/2t in [0,1) for known t, with controlled access to the unitary operator U=e2πiA. This interval ensures that different eigenvalues have different eigenphases; exact representability alone would not exclude phase aliasing, as 0 and 1 both give phase zero. Let a real function h obey ∣h(λj​)∣≤1, and assume the required quantum variable rotations are available. Coherent exact quantum phase estimation, a quantum variable rotation and uncomputation implement
∣uj​⟩∣0⟩⟼∣uj​⟩(1−h(λj​)2​∣0⟩+h(λj​)∣1⟩).
(16)
Postselection on flag one gives h(A)∣b⟩ normalized, with probability ∥h(A)∣b⟩∥2, provided this vector is nonzero. Erasing the eigenvalue label by uncomputation is essential to preserve coherence between different eigenvectors. On this dyadic spectrum, U2t=I gives U−1=U2t−1, so reversing the phase-estimation gates is possible using forward controlled-U calls. The choice h(λ)=λ multiplies by A; a scaled reciprocal gives the different filter used in the HHL algorithm.

Singular obstruction to normalized quantum matrix multiplication

Quantum spectral filtering OurBigBook.com
If ∣b⟩ lies in the kernel of A, then A∣b⟩=0 has no normalized quantum state. A request to produce that normalized vector with positive probability is consequently undefined. For example, A=diag(0,1/2) on one qubit has distinct dyadic eigenvalues, but annihilates ∣0⟩. The corrected filtering assertion requires A∣b⟩=0; it cannot be repaired by assigning a positive success probability to this input.

Success probability of positive quantum spectral filtering (P=∥A∣b⟩∥2)

P=∑j​∣βj​∣2λj2​≥λmin2​.
(17)
