Equilibrium constant (K)

 ... Physics Branches of physics Statistical physics Thermodynamics Chemical potential Chemical equilibrium
OurBigBook.com Words: 235 Articles: 3
For a specified chemical reaction and standard state convention, the dimensionless equilibrium constant is the equilibrium value of the reaction quotient Q=∏i​aiνi​​, where ai​ denotes thermodynamic activity and νi​ a signed stoichiometric vector entry. Thermodynamic activities incorporate the reference state and departures from ideal behavior. See IUPAC Gold Book, equilibrium constant.
In the ideal concentration model for A converting to B, use aA​=[A]/c∘ and aB​=[B]/c∘ with the same reference c∘=1M. Their ratio gives Q=[B]/[A] and K=0.75/0.25=3. Equality K=kf​/kr​ applies to this elementary first-order pair; it is not a universal ratio of two measured rate constants for an arbitrary multistep chemical reaction.
At a specified constant temperature, with consistent standard states, the standard molar reaction Gibbs free energy is
Δr​G∘=∑i​νi​μi∘​=−RTlnK,Δr​G=Δr​G∘+RTlnQ=RTln(Q/K).
(18)
Here Δr​G is the local slope per mole of extent of reaction at fixed temperature and pressure, rather than the finite total Gibbs free energy change between two mixtures. At equilibrium Δr​G=0, while Δr​G∘ generally remains nonzero. For K=3 at 298K, it is about −2.72kJ/mol. For positive activities, Q<K favors forward conversion and Q>K favors reverse conversion; neither sign supplies a reaction rate. See MIT 5.111, Lecture 18, reaction free energy and equilibrium.
