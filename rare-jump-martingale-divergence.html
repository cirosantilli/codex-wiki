Rare-jump martingale divergence (Xn​=∑k=1n​(1−2kYk​))

 Home Mathematics Area of mathematics Probability and statistics Probability theory Martingale
OurBigBook.com Words: 53
A martingale can diverge to +∞ almost surely when its increments are finite but not uniformly bounded. For independent Bernoulli random variables with P(Yn​=1)=2−n,
Xn​=∑k=1n​(1−2kYk​)
(2)
has centered integrable increments. The First Borel-Cantelli lemma makes the rare negative jumps occur only finitely often, after which every increment equals one. The expectation remains zero at every finite time despite this pathwise divergence.
