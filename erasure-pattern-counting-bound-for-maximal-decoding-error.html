Erasure-pattern counting bound for maximal decoding error ((1−εmax​)(log2​m−1)≤n(1−q))

 ... Mathematics Area of mathematics Algebra Coding theory Discrete memoryless channel Binary erasure channel
OurBigBook.com Words: 180
For a deterministic length-n channel code with m>2 messages on a binary erasure channel of erasure probability q, exclude the message decoded from the all-erased output. Under a uniform choice of the other m−1 messages, success probability is at least 1−εmax​. Conditional on an erasure pattern with k≥1 unerased positions, there are at most 2k output words, so average success is at most min{1,2k/(m−1)}. With no unerased positions it is zero.
Put L=log2​m−1. The scalar bound min{1,2k/(m−1)}≤k/L is immediate for k≥L. Otherwise set r=m/2k>2. Since log2​r≤r−1 for r≥2,
L=k−1+log2​r≤k+r−2≤k(r−1)<k(m−1)/2k.
(8)
The inequality log2​r≤r−1 follows because equality holds at r=2 and the derivative of r−1−log2​r is 1−1/(rln2)>0 for r≥2. Averaging the scalar bound over the number K of unerased positions, with EK=n(1−q), proves the displayed claim. Thus εmax​≤δ<1 implies code rate R≤(1−q)/(1−δ)+1/n. The cases m≤2 satisfy this rate bound directly.
Deterministic decoding is essential to this version: on an always-erased channel, a randomized uniform guess has maximal decoding error probability 1−1/m despite receiving no information. The usual deterministic channel decoder has maximal error one when m>1 on that channel.
