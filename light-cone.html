Light cone (∣x∣2=c2t2)

 Home Physics Branches of physics Special relativity Minkowski spacetime Minkowski metric
OurBigBook.com Words: 504 Articles: 5
The light cone of a spacetime event O consists of O together with the spacetime events having null separation from it. In the coordinates of an inertial frame centered on O, it obeys
∣x∣2=c2t2.
(31)
For t>0 it traces an expanding spherical flash of light, with radius ct at time t; for t<0 it describes possible incoming light rays. A spacetime diagram with one spatial coordinate shows the two lines x=±ct. The apparent 45∘ angle uses equal scales for x and ct and is not a separate physical angle.
The light cone is the boundary, while its interior has timelike separation from O. An outgoing light ray follows the boundary; a future-directed massive observer's world line remains inside it. The exterior has spacelike separation: no signal travelling at or below the speed of light can connect its spacetime events directly to O. This separates possible causal influence from merely being assigned a later coordinate time.
In the diagram, A=(4s,3ls) is inside the future boundary, N=(3s,3ls) is on it, and B=(2s,3ls) is outside. Their squared Minkowski intervals are respectively −7,0,5 in ls2. Thus B occurs later than O in the displayed inertial frame while still being unable to receive a light-speed-or-slower signal sent from O.
The usefulness is immediate for an experiment: a detector's reading can depend on a source event only if a causal influence can reach it. A Lorentz boost changes coordinates but preserves this classification. For a boost with β=0.8 and Lorentz factor γ=5/3, applying the Lorentz transformation gives, in light-second length units,
(ct,x)A​=(4,3)(ct,x)N​=(3,3)(ct,x)B​=(2,3)​⟼(ct′,x′)A​=(8/3,−1/3),⟼(ct′,x′)N​=(1,1),⟼(ct′,x′)B​=(−2/3,7/3).​
(32)
The corresponding squared Minkowski intervals remain −7,0,5. In particular B is now earlier than O, which is allowed for spacelike separation; A remains later.
