Additive energy (E(A))

 Home Mathematics Area of mathematics Combinatorics Additive combinatorics
OurBigBook.com Words: 918 Articles: 11
The additive energy of a finite set S⊆G in an abelian group G counts additive quadruples, with order and repeated members allowed:
E(S)=​{(a,b,c,d)∈S4:a+b=c+d}​=∑s∈G​rS+S​(s)2.
(33)
At a fixed sum s, independently choosing two of its rS+S​(s) ordered pairs gives rS+S​(s)2 additive quadruples. Thus additive energy weights heavily represented sums more strongly than the cardinality of the sumset does.
For A={0,1,2,3} and B={0,1,4,10}, the sumset representation functions give
E(A)E(B)​=12+22+32+42+32+22+12=44,=4⋅12+6⋅22=28.​
(34)
The arithmetic progression has additional coincidences such as 0+3=1+2. The set B still has coincidences forced by commutativity, including 0+1=1+0, so its additive energy is not merely the sixteen identical-pair comparisons.
The same additive energy can be counted using the difference set. Define rS−S​(t)=∣{(a,b)∈S×S:a−b=t}∣. The identity a+b=c+d is equivalent to a−c=d−b, and the coordinate rearrangement (a,b,c,d)↦(a,c,d,b) is a bijection. Consequently
E(S)=∑t∈G​rS−S​(t)2.
(35)
For A, the difference counts from −3 through 3 are again 1,2,3,4,3,2,1. For B, the zero difference has four representations and each of the twelve nonzero differences has one, giving 42+12⋅12=28. The sumset representation functions for sums and differences can look different while their squared totals agree.
For every nonempty finite set S⊆G in an abelian group G,
∣S+S∣∣S∣4​≤E(S)≤∣S∣3.
(36)
The lower bound follows from the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality and the total sumset representation function count:
∣S∣4=(∑s∈S+S​rS+S​(s))2≤∣S+S∣∑s∈S+S​rS+S​(s)2.
(37)
For the upper bound, any three members a,b,c determine at most one fourth member d=a+b−c of an additive quadruple. See Yufei Zhao's MIT lecture 25, pages 1-3, for representation counts and the energy bounds.
In the two integer examples, the lower bounds are 256/7 and 256/10, and the upper bound is 64. For the subgroup H={0,2,4} of Z/6Z, each element of H has three sum representations, so E(H)=3⋅32=27 and both bounds are equalities.
For nonempty S, dividing additive energy by ∣S∣3 gives a relative measure between 1/K(S) and one. Small doubling constant therefore forces relatively large additive energy. A converse requires care: the Balog-Szemerédi-Gowers theorem extracts a large subset with small doubling constant, rather than asserting that all of S has small doubling constant.
