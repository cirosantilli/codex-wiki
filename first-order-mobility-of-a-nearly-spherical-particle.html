First-order mobility of a nearly spherical particle (U1​=8πa33​∫r=a​f(n)(nn−I)U0​dS)

A force-driven rigid particle with surface r=a+εf(n) has a leading translating sphere in Stokes flow with U0​=F/(6πμa). Expanding the no-slip boundary condition gives the first-order surface velocity u1​=U1​+Ω1​×x+g, where g=3f(I−nn)U0​/(2a). The first-order resultant force and torque vanish when the applied load is fixed. The Lorentz reciprocal theorem for Stokes flow, using the uniform surface traction in translating-sphere Stokes flow, therefore gives U1​=−∫gdS/(4πa2). For f=an⋅Dn with symmetric traceless D, the isotropic fourth moment gives U1​=DU0​/5. A rotating-sphere test similarly gives Ω1​=−3∫n×gdS/(8πa3), which vanishes for an inversion-symmetric deformation.
