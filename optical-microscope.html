Optical microscope

 Home Physics Branches of physics Optics Optical instrument
OurBigBook.com Words: 2k Articles: 27
An optical microscope uses light and optical lens assemblies to reveal small structures, such as the boundaries of biological cells or defects in a manufactured surface. In a compound optical microscope, the microscope objective supplies an enlarged real image; the microscope eyepiece makes that intermediate microscope image subtend a larger viewing angle. The transverse magnification of the first stage and the angular magnification of the second answer different questions.[ref]
Consider an original ideal finite-conjugate microscope in air, with both optical lens assemblies treated as converging thin lens elements in the paraxial approximation. For instance, a hypothetical object height ho​=0.020mm becomes hi​=−0.100mm at the intermediate microscope image. The signed viewing angles are approximately hi​/fe​=−0.004rad with the instrument and ho​/D=0.00008rad unaided. Their ratio is −50: enlarged and inverted. These are stipulated model dimensions, not measurements or specifications of a modern commercial optical microscope.
The attraction of an optical microscope is that seeing can become measuring: a stage micrometer supplies a known object-space length, and microscope scale calibration converts an image readout into a specimen length. This also separates a large-looking image from a trustworthy size estimate. Meanwhile, diffraction-limited resolution depends on numerical aperture and wavelength; empty magnification enlarges an existing blur without revealing additional structure.
