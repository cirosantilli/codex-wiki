Von Mangoldt divisor identity (∑d∣n​Λ(d)=logn)

 Home Mathematics Area of mathematics Number theory Arithmetic function Von Mangoldt function
OurBigBook.com Words: 189
For every positive integer n, the divisor sum of its Von Mangoldt function weights recovers its natural logarithm:
∑d∣n​Λ(d)=logn.
(75)
The sum runs over the positive integer divisors of n. For n=1, its only term is Λ(1)=0=log1. Equivalently, if L(n)=logn and 1 is the constant-one arithmetic function, then 1∗Λ=L, where ∗ is Dirichlet convolution. [ref]
For 12=22⋅3, the positive integer divisors are 1,2,3,4,6,12. Only 2,3,4 contribute:
Λ(1)+Λ(2)+Λ(3)+Λ(4)+Λ(6)+Λ(12)=0+log2+log3+log2+0+0=log12.
(76)
Thus the vanishing of Λ(12) loses no information here: the divisor sum retrieves both copies of the prime factor 2 from the integer divisors 2 and 4.
Proof outline: by the fundamental theorem of arithmetic, write n=∏i=1r​piai​​ with distinct prime numbers pi​ and positive exponents ai​. Among the positive integer divisors, the ones with nonzero Von Mangoldt function weights are exactly pi​,pi2​,…,piai​​. Consequently
∑d∣n​Λ(d)=∑i=1r​∑j=1ai​​logpi​=∑i=1r​ai​logpi​=log(∏i=1r​piai​​)=logn.
(77)
The natural logarithm turns the prime factorization product into a sum, and the prime powers greater than one supply exactly the required multiplicities.
