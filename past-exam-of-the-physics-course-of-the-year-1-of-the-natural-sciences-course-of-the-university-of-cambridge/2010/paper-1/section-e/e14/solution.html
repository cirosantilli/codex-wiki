The closed surface integral sums the outward electric flux: E is the electric field, dS its outward vector area, Q the total enclosed charge and ϵ0​ the vacuum permittivity. Gauss's law relates that flux to enclosed charge for any closed surface. High symmetry can make the field's magnitude constant on a suitable Gaussian surface and fix its direction; without symmetry, flux alone does not determine the local field.

The electrostatic energy increase on equal-drop coalescence is positive. It can be supplied by the decrease in surface energy during coalescence and/or by external mechanical work bringing like charges together. Remaining energy can become motion and heat. If the charge is too large, surface-energy release alone need not suffice to make merging spontaneous; conservation does not assert that it will. Keeping the drops connected to a voltage supply would instead be a different charge-balance problem.
