The displacement is along x, transverse to propagation in the membrane's yz plane. Write A=∣A∣eiϕ; then d=∣A∣cos(ωt−ky​y−kz​z+ϕ). Here ∣A∣ is the displacement amplitude, ϕ the initial phase, ω the angular frequency and k the wavevector normal to constant-phase lines. Its magnitude is 2π/λ, and its direction specifies phase propagation for the chosen sign convention.
For equal-phase two-wave membrane modes, two equal complex amplitudes with opposite ky​ sum to
d=2Re{Acos(ky​y)ei(ωt−kz​z)}.
(37)
The edge condition gives cos(ky​a/2)=0, hence ky​=(2j+1)π/a, j=0,1,…, for this even-in-y family. Opposite relative phase gives the sine family; together they yield all Dirichlet transverse modes ky​=nπ/a, n≥1. For the equal-phase family,
kz​=(ω/c)2−[(2j+1)π/a]2​.​
(38)
The lowest mode has ky​=π/a. Therefore
vz​=kz​ω​=1−(λ/2a)2​c​,0<λ<2a.​
(39)
It approaches c for λ→0 and diverges as λ→2a−; there is no real travelling-mode kz​ beyond the cutoff. This is an axial phase velocity, not the group velocity: vg​=c1−(λ/2a)2​<c. Here c denotes the membrane wave speed, not the speed of light.
For the evanescent membrane response below cutoff, below fc​=c/(2a), put kz​=−iκ for decay along positive z, with κ=(π/a)2−(ω/c)2​. A localized central drive creates an evanescent, sinusoidally oscillating deformation decaying away in both axial directions. Far enough from the drive the leading profile is proportional to cos(πy/a)e−κ∣z−z0​∣cos(ωt+ϕ0​). Higher evanescent modes contribute near the point source. The pattern has no steadily advancing axial phase front or far-field propagating wave; decreasing frequency increases its spatial localization.
