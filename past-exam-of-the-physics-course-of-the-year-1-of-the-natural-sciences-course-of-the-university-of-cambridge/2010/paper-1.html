For a proper lifetime inferred from a delayed decay flash, the measured interval ends when the light returns to the detector, not when the muon decays. Put passage at (t,x)=(0,0) in the detector frame. If decay occurs at td​, its distance is vtd​, and the backwards photon takes another vtd​/c to arrive. Therefore
trecord​=td​(1+v/c),td​=1+1/210ns​=320​ns.
(9)

(10)

For background-subtracted radiative temperature uncertainty, assume the two quoted precisions are independent standard uncertainties, and treat the radiative proportionality factor as known. Write PB​=B and PT​=10B. Then PS​=9B, while both absolute input uncertainties equal 0.5B. Consequently
σS​=(0.05×10B)2+(0.50B)2​=2​B​,TσT​​=41​PS​σS​​.
(11)
Thus the temperature precision is about 1.96%, or 2%. Subtraction amplifies fractional uncertainty even though PT​ itself is precise.
Independence is a measurement assumption, not a consequence of the two percentages. For correlation coefficient rc​, the result is σT​/T=2(1−rc​)​/72. If the percentages are strict error bounds combined pessimistically, the corresponding bound is 2.78%. Unknown calibration correlations cannot be inferred from the source.

For collinear inelastic capture followed by two photons, each particle has p=γmu and E=γmc2, with γ=(1−u2/c2)−1/2. An isolated collision conserves the vector sum of momentum and total energy, including rest energy, internal excitation and radiation. It need not conserve the sum of individual rest masses. At low speed,
E=mc2+21​mu2+O(mu4/c2),
(22)
recovering the classical kinetic energy after subtracting mc2.
Here the incoming Lorentz factors are 5/3 and 5/4, so
E1​=E2​=5mc2,p1x​=4mc,p2x​=−3mc.
(23)
The composite therefore has
v=Epc2​=10c​ in the +x direction,M=c2E2−p2c2​​=99​m=311​m.​
(24)
Its rest mass exceeds 7m because relative kinetic energy has become internal rest energy.
For the subsequent collinear photon pair, E+​+E−​=10mc2 and (E+​−E−​)/c=mc. Thus E+​=11mc2/2, E−​=9mc2/2, and
ν+​/ν−​=11/9.​
(25)

Fy​=−mω02​y,ω02​=34πGρ​.​
(26)

y¨​+ω02​y=0,y(t)=ys​cosω0​t.​

The far end is −ys​, so ttransit​=π/ω0​≈2530s=42.2min, independent of chord offset in this ideal model. Here ρ=5515kgm−3 and G=6.67430×10−11m3kg−1s−2 give ω0​=1.24171×10−3s−1.

For the series-fed parallel LC voltage response, use phasors with time dependence eiωt. The parallel branch has impedance iX, where X=ωL/(1−ω2LC). Therefore
Z=R+iX,∣Z∣=R2+X2​,argZ=atan2(X,R).​
(32)
At the ideal parallel resonance ω0​=1/LC​ its input impedance diverges. The input-current phase changes sign on crossing this pole; expressions there are interpreted by limits.
The capacitor voltage equals the parallel-branch voltage. Define q=R(1−ω2LC)/(ωL). Then
H(ω)=Vi​VC​​=R+iXiX​=1−iq1​,∣H∣=(1+q2)−1/2,argH=arctanq.​

At low frequency H∼iωL/R; at high frequency H∼−i/(ωRC); at resonance H=1. Thus it is a band-pass voltage filter or resonant frequency selector. Its phase decreases continuously from +π/2 through zero to −π/2. Half-power points satisfy ∣q∣=1 and
ω±​=2(RC)−2+4/(LC)​±(RC)−1​,ω+​−ω−​=RC1​.

For the supplied component values and ω=2π(50)s−1, X=421.745Ω and VC,pk​=99.3046V. The element peak currents are

D12 OurBigBook.com
The displacement is along x, transverse to propagation in the membrane's yz plane. Write A=∣A∣eiϕ; then d=∣A∣cos(ωt−ky​y−kz​z+ϕ). Here ∣A∣ is the displacement amplitude, ϕ the initial phase, ω the angular frequency and k the wavevector normal to constant-phase lines. Its magnitude is 2π/λ, and its direction specifies phase propagation for the chosen sign convention.
For equal-phase two-wave membrane modes, two equal complex amplitudes with opposite ky​ sum to
d=2Re{Acos(ky​y)ei(ωt−kz​z)}.
(37)
The edge condition gives cos(ky​a/2)=0, hence ky​=(2j+1)π/a, j=0,1,…, for this even-in-y family. Opposite relative phase gives the sine family; together they yield all Dirichlet transverse modes ky​=nπ/a, n≥1. For the equal-phase family,
kz​=(ω/c)2−[(2j+1)π/a]2​.​
(38)
The lowest mode has ky​=π/a. Therefore
vz​=kz​ω​=1−(λ/2a)2​c​,0<λ<2a.​
(39)
It approaches c for λ→0 and diverges as λ→2a−; there is no real travelling-mode kz​ beyond the cutoff. This is an axial phase velocity, not the group velocity: vg​=c1−(λ/2a)2​<c. Here c denotes the membrane wave speed, not the speed of light.
For the evanescent membrane response below cutoff, below fc​=c/(2a), put kz​=−iκ for decay along positive z, with κ=(π/a)2−(ω/c)2​. A localized central drive creates an evanescent, sinusoidally oscillating deformation decaying away in both axial directions. Far enough from the drive the leading profile is proportional to cos(πy/a)e−κ∣z−z0​∣cos(ωt+ϕ0​). Higher evanescent modes contribute near the point source. The pattern has no steadily advancing axial phase front or far-field propagating wave; decreasing frequency increases its spatial localization.

The closed surface integral sums the outward electric flux: E is the electric field, dS its outward vector area, Q the total enclosed charge and ϵ0​ the vacuum permittivity. Gauss's law relates that flux to enclosed charge for any closed surface. High symmetry can make the field's magnitude constant on a suitable Gaussian surface and fix its direction; without symmetry, flux alone does not determine the local field.
For an isolated conducting sphere, charges reside at its surface and the exterior field is radial. Thus
E(r)=4πϵ0​r2Q​r(r>a),V(a)=∫a∞​E(r)dr=4πϵ0​aQ​,C=4πϵ0​a.​
(43)
The conductor has zero interior field. Integrating exterior field energy gives
U=∫a∞​21​ϵ0​(4πϵ0​r2Q​)24πr2dr=8πϵ0​aQ2​​.
(44)
Let the initial charge per drop be q=4πϵ0​aV1​. After disconnecting the charging supply, charge is conserved. Volume conservation gives final radius A=21/3a, total charge 2q and
Vf​=4πϵ0​A2q​=22/3V1​.​
(45)
Initial mutual energy is negligible at the stipulated large separation. Hence
Ui​=4πϵ0​aq2​,Uf​=8πϵ0​21/3a4q2​=22/3Ui​,Ui​/Uf​=2−2/3.​
(46)
The electrostatic energy increase on equal-drop coalescence is positive. It can be supplied by the decrease in surface energy during coalescence and/or by external mechanical work bringing like charges together. Remaining energy can become motion and heat. If the charge is too large, surface-energy release alone need not suffice to make merging spontaneous; conservation does not assert that it will. Keeping the drops connected to a voltage supply would instead be a different charge-balance problem.

For point masses, Newton's force is attractive along their separation with magnitude Gm1​m2​/r2. Treat the narrow rod as uniform, with linear mass density μ=M/(2a); a mass profile would otherwise be needed beyond total mass and length.
For axial and transverse gravity of a uniform rod, on the axis at b>a, integrating the element force gives
F1​=Gmμ∫−aa​(b−x)2dx​=b2−a2GMm​,​
(47)
directed toward the rod. On the perpendicular bisector, axial components cancel and transverse components add:
F2​=Gmμr∫−aa​(x2+r2)3/2dx​=rr2+a2​GMm​.​
(48)
At r=b≫a,
F1​=b2GMm​[1+b2a2​+O(a4/b4)],F2​=b2GMm​[1−2b2a2​+O(a4/b4)].
(49)
Both approach the point-mass force, but F1​/F2​=1+3a2/(2b2)+O(a4/b4), so the axial attraction is slightly larger.
For a circular orbit perpendicular to a uniform rod, rotational symmetry about the rod makes a centred circular orbit possible in its perpendicular-bisector plane. With m≪M, centrifugal balance gives
v(r)=r2+a2​GM​​=aGM​​[1+(r/a)2]−1/4.​
(50)
For r≫a, it is Keplerian, v∼GM/r​. For rod thickness ≪r≪a, the force approximates that of an infinite line and v→GM/a​, a flat speed limit rather than a divergence.
