Measure the mass's displacement x from its static equilibrium height. This removes the constant spring extension supporting its weight. If the spring's top moves with the box, its incremental extension is y2​−x, so its incremental upward force is k(y2​−x). The specified damping force is against the mass's velocity in the static frame. Newton's second law therefore gives
mx¨+bx˙+kx=ky2​=kas​cosωt.​
(41)
In absolute height coordinates an additional constant on the right expresses the spring's rest length, its fixed attachment height and gravity; translating to static equilibrium removes it. Replacing the given damping by −b(x˙−y˙​2​) would describe a different base-excited oscillator.
After the decaying transient, write the steady-state response as x=A(ω)cos(ωt−Φ). Using a complex sinusoid gives
x=k−mω2+ibωkas​​,
(42)
so
A(ω)=(k−mω2)2+b2ω2​kas​​,Φ(ω)=atan2(bω,k−mω2).​
(43)
For positive frequency, the phase lag lies between zero and π, passing through π/2 at ω0​=k/m​. For small b, the frequency response begins at as​, develops a narrow resonance near ω0​, and falls as
A(ω)∼mω2kas​​(ω≫ω0​).
(44)
The precise amplitude-peak frequency is k/m−b2/(2m2)​ when that expression is positive.
The pen records the relative displacement z=x−y2​. Its complex transfer function is
as​z​=k−mω2+ibωmω2−ibω​.
(45)
At high frequency, x is small while z≃−y2​. The inertial mass then stays nearly stationary and the recorded relative motion reproduces the ground displacement, with reversed sign. At low frequency the mass follows the box and their relative motion is small; near resonance the response is strongly frequency-dependent. Here “high” means compared with the suspension's natural frequency, rather than an absolute seismic-frequency threshold.
