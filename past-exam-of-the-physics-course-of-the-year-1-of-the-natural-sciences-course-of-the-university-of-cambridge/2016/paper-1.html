The ground-state energies are 1.50 eV and 0.376 eV, respectively. For a particle in a box of width L, the wave function must vanish at both infinite walls. The lowest nonzero standing wave therefore has wavelength 2L, giving the kinetic energy eigenvalue
E1​=2me​L2π2ℏ2​=8me​L2h2​.
(1)
Using the electron mass and converting joules to electronvolts gives
E1​(0.5 nm)≃1.504 eV,E1​(1 nm)≃0.3760 eV.​
(2)
Doubling the confinement length divides this kinetic energy by four. A zero potential energy inside the box does not imply zero total energy: a constant, zero-momentum wave function cannot satisfy both wall conditions unless it vanishes everywhere. This is a concrete manifestation of quantum confinement and the Heisenberg uncertainty principle.

The Michelson-Morley experiment splits coherent light into two perpendicular arms, reflects the beams and recombines them to observe interference fringes. Rotating the apparatus interchanges each arm's orientation relative to a proposed stationary luminiferous aether. The historical experiment used multiple reflections to increase the effective optical path and a rotatable support to compare orientations.[ref]
For equal one-way effective arm lengths ℓ, a classical stationary-aether model gives
t∥​=c−vℓ​+c+vℓ​≃c2ℓ​(1+c2v2​),t⊥​=c2−v2​2ℓ​≃c2ℓ​(1+2c2v2​).
(28)
A right-angle rotation should reverse their difference, producing a fringe shift of approximately 2ℓv2/(λc2). The observed orientation-dependent shift was much smaller than the stationary-aether prediction. In special relativity, the apparatus rest frame has the same vacuum speed of light along both arms, so no such orientation-dependent shift is expected. The result supports special relativity but does not, by itself, uniquely establish every postulate or exclude every modified aether theory.
For the rocket, take its tail at x′=0 and nose at x′=L0​, with coincident frame origins when t=t′=0. Denote emission, reflection and return by A,B,C. In the rocket's inertial frame, their coordinates (t′,x′) are
A:(0,L0​),B:(L0​/c,0),C:(2L0​/c,L0​).​
(29)
The inverse Lorentz transformation is x=Γ(x′+ut′), t=Γ(t′+ux′/c2), where Γ=(1−u2/c2)−1/2. Thus the same spacetime events, in coordinates (t,x), are
ABC​:(ΓuL0​/c2, ΓL0​),:(ΓL0​/c, ΓuL0​/c),:(Γ(2L0​/c+uL0​/c2), Γ(L0​+2uL0​/c)).​​

The nose-to-tail travel time is ΔtAB​=ΓL0​(1−u/c)/c. If L is the laboratory length measured at one laboratory time, a left-moving pulse and the right-moving tail close their initial separation at speed c+u. Therefore

(31)

The Huygens-Fresnel principle explains it by constructing the transmitted wavefront from secondary wavelets: during the same time interval, distances travelled in the two media are proportional to c/n1​ and c/n2​. (64)
Near the centre, the coils' magnetic field lines are nearly straight and parallel to the axis. On the axis rotational symmetry makes Bx​=By​=0 exactly; in the central plane the radial components from the two coils cancel. More quantitatively, in the current-free central volume, axisymmetry and ∇⋅B=0 imply

In a fixed point-mass Newtonian gravitational field, a test mass m experiences
F=−r2GMm​r.
(66)
Because this is a central force, its torque about the attracting centre vanishes and angular momentum is conserved. The orbit lies in a fixed plane and sweeps out equal areas in equal times. Since the force is also conservative, the specific orbital energy is constant:
ε=21​r˙2+2r2j2​−rGM​,j=r2φ˙​.
(67)
The last two terms form an effective potential. They show how the angular-momentum barrier competes with attraction and determines the turning radii. For nonradial motion the orbit is a conic section:
r=1+ecos(φ−φ0​)p​,p=GMj2​,e2=1+G2M22εj2​.​
(68)
Negative energy gives an ellipse, with e<1; zero energy gives a parabola; positive energy gives a hyperbola. The circular limit has e=0, vc​=GM/r​ and ε=−GM/(2r). For a bound ellipse of semimajor axis a, ε=−GM/(2a) and the orbital period is T=2πa3/(GM)​. The local escape speed is 2GM/r​. These are the point-mass, test-particle limits; extended mass distributions or noncentral perturbations change the orbit laws.

An electric dipole consists of opposite electric charges separated by a short displacement. Its electric dipole moment is p=qd, directed from negative to positive electric charge. At distances large compared with the separation, its leading electric potential and electric field in vacuum are
V(r)=4πϵ0​r2p⋅r​,E(r)=4πϵ0​r33(p⋅r)r−p​.​
(69)
The absence of net electric charge removes the monopole term; the dipole electric potential therefore falls as r−2 rather than r−1. On its axis the electric field is parallel to p with magnitude 2p/(4πϵ0​r3); on the equatorial plane it points oppositely with half that magnitude.
In a uniform applied electric field, the two forces cancel as a net translation but form a couple. The torque and potential energy are
τ=p×E,U=−p⋅E.​
(70)
Alignment parallel to the electric field is stable. A nonuniform electric field can also exert a net force, F=∇(p⋅E) for a fixed permanent electric dipole moment. An induced electric dipole instead depends on the applied electric field, so its energy must account for the work needed to create it.

Electromagnetic induction is the production of an electromotive force by changing magnetic flux or by moving a conductor through a magnetic field. With the loop and surface orientations related by the right-hand rule, Faraday's law of induction is
E=∮(E+v×B)⋅dℓ=−dtd​∫B⋅dS.​
(71)
Here v is the local wire velocity. For a stationary loop, the induced electric field can have a nonzero closed-path integral; its differential form is ∇×E=−∂B/∂t. For a straight rod moving perpendicularly to itself and to a uniform magnetic field, the motional electromotive force has magnitude Bℓv.
The minus sign expresses Lenz's law: the induced electric current produces a magnetic field that opposes the change in linked magnetic flux. Pulling a conducting loop through a magnetic field therefore requires work against an electromagnetic resisting force; that work supplies the resulting heat or stored energy.
For an inductor with constant self-inductance, E=−LdI/dt and its stored magnetic energy is LI2/2. A changing electric current in one coil can induce an electromotive force in another through mutual inductance, the operating principle of a electrical transformer. These examples connect the flux law, the direction of the response and conservation of energy.

The Lorentz force determines the motion of a charged particle:
dtdp​=q(E+v×B).​
(72)
At nonrelativistic speeds, p=mv. The electric field does work at rate qE⋅v, while the magnetic part of the Lorentz force is perpendicular to velocity and does no work. A uniform electric field gives constant acceleration qE/m; with a transverse initial velocity, the trajectory is a parabola.
In a uniform magnetic field, the parallel velocity is constant, while the perpendicular component undergoes circular motion. The angular cyclotron frequency, orbit radius and helical pitch are
Ωc​=m∣q∣B​,rL​=∣q∣Bmv⊥​​,phelix​=Ωc​2πv∥​​.​
(73)
The sign of q determines the sense of rotation. For perpendicular uniform electric fields and magnetic fields, the guiding centre has the electric drift velocity
vD​=B2E×B​.​
(74)
Indeed, E+vD​×B=0, so the remaining motion is cyclotron rotation about that drifting centre, with possible motion along the magnetic field. The drift is independent of electric charge sign and mass. At relativistic energies, retain p=Γmv and the corresponding relativistic orbit radius rather than assuming constant nonrelativistic acceleration.
