Classically, a particle with 0<E<V0​ is completely reflected and never enters the raised region. In quantum mechanics the wavefunction penetrates the classically forbidden side as an evanescent wave. Define the tunnelling decay constant
κ=ℏ2m(V0​−E)​​.
(68)
Boundedness at positive infinity excludes e+κx, so
ψ(x)={Aeikx+Be−ikx,Ce−κx,​x≤0,x>0.​
(69)
Matching the wavefunction and its derivative gives
AB​=k+iκk−iκ​,AC​=k+iκ2k​.

The reflected probability amplitude has unit modulus, hence quantum reflection probability one. The decaying tail carries zero probability current: this semi-infinite potential step has no transmitted beam at infinity, unlike a finite potential barrier. The sketch shows an exact matched example rather than attempting to assign a single real value to a complex wavefunction.
Because the probability density decays as ∣C∣2e−2κx, the requested 1/e penetration distance is
d=2κ1​=22m(V0​−E)​ℏ​.​
(71)
For a proton, take mp​=1.67262×10−27kg, V0​−E=10MeV=1.60218×10−12J and ℏ=1.05457×10−34Js. Then
d=7.20×10−16m=0.720fm.​
(72)
The wavefunction amplitude decay length 1/κ=1.44fm is twice this value. The nonrelativistic equation supplied in the paper is appropriate for this estimate since 10MeV is small compared with the proton rest energy, approximately 938MeV. If the original PDF's unbarred h is instead read literally as the Planck constant, the displayed differential equation gives dliteral​=2πd=4.53fm. This is a valid calculation for that literal coefficient, but 0.720fm is the physical estimate using the standard Schrödinger equation.
