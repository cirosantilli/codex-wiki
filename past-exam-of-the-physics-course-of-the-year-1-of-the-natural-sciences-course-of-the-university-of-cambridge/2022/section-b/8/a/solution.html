For electric potential difference and electric current varying as sinusoids, choose the phasor convention
V(t)=Re(Veiωt),I(t)=Re(Ieiωt).
(48)
The electrical impedance is
Z(ω)=IV​,[Z]=Ω.​
(49)
Its modulus gives the voltage-to-current amplitude ratio, and its complex argument gives the voltage-minus-current phase difference. For ideal components,
ZR​=R,ZC​=iωC1​=−ωCi​,ZL​=iωL.​
(50)
These follow from Ohm's law V=RI, the capacitor law I=CdV/dt, and the inductor law V=LdI/dt. In phasor notation a time derivative becomes multiplication by iω, turning the linear ordinary differential equations of the electrical circuit into algebraic equations. Electrical impedances add in a series circuit; their reciprocals, the electrical admittances, add in a parallel circuit. This computes both amplitudes and phase differences in the steady response to sinusoids.
