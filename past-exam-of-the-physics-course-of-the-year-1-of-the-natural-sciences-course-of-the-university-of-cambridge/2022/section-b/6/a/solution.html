OurBigBook.com Words: 105
Choose three mutually perpendicular axes through the same point, and label coordinates x,y,z. The moments of inertia of any rigid body are
Ix​=∫(y2+z2)dm,Iy​=∫(z2+x2)dm,Iz​=∫(x2+y2)dm.
(16)
Subtracting gives
Ix​+Iy​−Iz​=2∫z2dm≥0.
(17)
For a thin planar lamina lying in z=0, the right-hand side vanishes, proving the perpendicular axis theorem:
Iz​=Ix​+Iy​.​
(18)
The two in-plane axes and the perpendicular axis must intersect at one point; this need not be the centre of mass. For an extended three-dimensional mass distribution, the equality generally fails. The general moment-of-inertia triangle inequalities are
Ix​+Iy​≥Iz​,Iy​+Iz​≥Ix​,Iz​+Ix​≥Iy​,​
(19)
with equality in the first precisely when all mass lies in the plane perpendicular to its z axis, and cyclically for the others. None of these identities requires principal axes.
