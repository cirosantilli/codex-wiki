Take +x along the upper ship's initial velocity, and take +y from the lower ship to the upper ship at closest approach. The upper and lower ships then have initial velocities v1​x and −v2​x. Their equal masses put each at distance l/2 from the centre of mass, so
ICM​=2m(l/2)2=21​ml2.​
(21)
By momentum conservation, the centre of mass keeps moving with constant velocity
VCM​=2v1​−v2​​x.​
(22)
In that frame the ships have opposite velocities of magnitude u=(v1​+v2​)/2. Their initial angular momentum about the centre of mass is nonzero even though their trajectories were straight:
L=−ml2v1​+v2​​z,L=2ml(v1​+v2​)​.
(23)
The tether exerts central internal forces, so conservation of angular momentum gives
ω=ICM​L​=lv1​+v2​​.​
(24)
Each ship follows uniform circular motion of radius l/2 around the translating centre of mass, clockwise viewed from +z. More explicitly, put the initial centre at the origin and define
n(t)=sin(ωt)x+cos(ωt)y​,r1,2​(t)=VCM​t±2l​n(t).
(25)
The kinetic-energy decomposition about the centre of mass verifies conservation of mechanical energy directly:
Eafter​​=21​(2m)VCM2​+21​ICM​ω2=4m​(v1​−v2​)2+4m​(v1​+v2​)2=21​mv12​+21​mv22​=Ebefore​.​​
(26)
At closest approach the relative radial velocity is already zero, so connecting the tether requires no dissipative radial impulse. Subsequently its tension is perpendicular to each ship's velocity relative to the centre of mass and does no net work on the pair at fixed l.
