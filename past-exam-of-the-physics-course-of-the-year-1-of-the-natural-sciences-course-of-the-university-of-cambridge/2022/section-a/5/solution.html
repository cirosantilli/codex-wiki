Write a=0.020m for the side length. In an equilateral triangle, the distance from centre to a vertex is a/3​; it is two thirds of the altitude 3​a/2. By the superposition principle, the electric potential at the centre due to the three fixed electrons is
Φ(0)=−4πε0​(a/3​)3e​.
(12)
Here e>0 is the elementary charge and ε0​ the vacuum permittivity. The approaching electron has electric charge −e, so its potential energy, measured relative to infinity, is positive:
U(0)=(−e)Φ(0)=4πε0​a33​e2​.
(13)
The fixed sources do not move, and radiation is excluded. Hence conservation of mechanical energy gives
21​me​v∞2​=U(0),v∞​=4πε0​me​a63​e2​​.​
(14)
Using e=1.60218×10−19C, me​=9.10938×10−31kg for the electron mass, and (4πε0​)−1=8.98755×109Nm2C−2 gives
U(0)=5.99×10−26J=3.74×10−7eV,v∞​≃3.63×102ms−1.​
(15)
This speed is small enough to justify classical kinetic energy. There is an idealization in the word “stops”: along the line normal to the triangle through its centre, this threshold energy makes the electron approach the central equilibrium asymptotically. A smooth, conservative trajectory cannot reach an equilibrium with exactly zero velocity in finite time unless it was already there. The energy threshold, and therefore the requested initial speed, is nevertheless unambiguous.
