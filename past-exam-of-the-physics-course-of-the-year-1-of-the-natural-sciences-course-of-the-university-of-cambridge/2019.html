The Huygens-Fresnel principle constructs a new wavefront as the envelope of secondary wavelets emitted by points on an earlier wavefront. Let the phase velocities in the two media be v1​=c/n1​ and v2​=c/n2​, with refractive indices evaluated at the light's fixed frequency.
For refraction from secondary wavelets, let point A of the incident wavefront reach the interface first. A second point B reaches the interface at C after time Δt, so BC=v1​Δt. During the same interval, the secondary wavelet centered on A advances radius AD=v2​Δt into the second medium. The refracted wavefront CD is tangent to this wavelet, hence AD⊥CD; also BC⊥AB because a ray is normal to its wavefront in these isotropic media.
Let θ1​,θ2​ be the incident and refracted ray angles from the interface normal. They also equal the corresponding acute angles between the wavefronts and the interface. The two right triangles have common hypotenuse AC, giving
sinθ1​=ACBC​=ACv1​Δt​,sinθ2​=ACAD​=ACv2​Δt​.
(12)
Their ratio eliminates both AC and Δt:
sinθ2​sinθ1​​=v2​v1​​=n1​n2​​,n1​sinθ1​=n2​sinθ2​.​
(13)
This is Snell's law. In particular, entering a medium of larger refractive index reduces the ray's angle to the normal. The construction assumes a stationary interface and homogeneous isotropic media with a propagating refracted wave.

Experimental evidence. Tests of special relativity check linked predictions about light propagation, clocks and four-momentum. Several independent kinds of experiment support those predictions.
  • In the Michelson-Morley experiment, light follows perpendicular paths in an interferometer. Rotating the apparatus would change their round-trip travel-time difference in a classical stationary-aether model. The expected orientation-dependent interference fringe shift was absent at the predicted scale, consistent with isotropy of the round-trip speed of light in special relativity. This experiment alone does not distinguish special relativity from every alternative theory.[ref]
  • The Fizeau experiment compares light propagation with and against flowing water using an interference fringe displacement. Neglecting optical dispersion, the velocity-addition formula predicts
vlab​=1+u/(nc)c/n+u​≃nc​+u(1−n21​).
(58)
The observed partial-drag coefficient agrees with this first-order prediction rather than simple Galilean addition of u. It tests a different consequence of the Lorentz transformation from a particle-lifetime measurement.[ref]
  • A controlled muon storage-ring time-dilation experiment measured circulating muons at γ=29.33. Its measured mean lifetimes were about 64.4μs, compared with about 2.2μs at rest, consistent with τlab​=γτ0​. This tests time dilation with accelerator-selected particles.[ref]
Atmospheric muons and the two frame descriptions. Atmospheric cosmic rays create unstable particles whose decay chains produce muons. A stopped-muon experiment measures the time between a stopping signal and the subsequent decay electron, obtaining the proper mean lifetime τ0​≃2.2μs. The associated light-travel distance is only cτ0​≃660m, yet substantial numbers of energetic atmospheric muons reach detectors after travelling several kilometres.
For an ideal selected cohort produced height h above a detector and moving downward at constant speed v, the flight time in the terrestrial frame is h/v. Time dilation predicts a laboratory mean lifetime γτ0​, hence survival fraction
N(h)N(h→0)​=exp(−γvτ0​h​),γ=1−v2/c2​1​.​
(59)
Without time dilation, the predicted exponent would be −h/(vτ0​) and the surviving population much smaller. Altitude comparisons constitute a cosmic-ray muon time-dilation experiment: select comparable momenta or energies and account for detector acceptance, atmospheric energy loss and backgrounds before interpreting the count ratio.
For illustration, not as a claimed measurement, take h=10km and v=0.998c. Then γ=15.82, the laboratory flight time is 33.4μs, and the predicted surviving fraction is about 0.38. Omitting time dilation would give only about 2.5×10−7 on the same idealized constant-speed model.
In the muon rest frame, its decay still follows e−Δτ/τ0​. The atmosphere and ground move upward. Their separation, measured simultaneously in that frame, is shortened by length contraction:
h′=γh​,Δτ=vh′​=γvh​.
(60)
The same survival fraction follows. The terrestrial description uses a longer-lived moving particle; the muon-frame description uses a shorter atmospheric flight path. They describe the same production and detection events. This consistency illustrates both time dilation and length contraction, while the survival measurement is not two independent experimental tests merely because it can be described in two frames.

(69)
It points outward for positive Q and inward for negative Q. For several point charges, the superposition principle adds their fields as vectors:
E(r)=4πϵ0​1​∑i​qi​∣r−ri​∣3r−ri​​.
(70)
This formula excludes the source points themselves, where the ideal point-charge field is singular.
Exact field components of the pair. Put
R+​=(x−a)2+y2​,R−​=(x+a)2+y2​.
(71)
The displacement vectors from the positive and negative charges to the observation point are (x−a)i^+yj^​ and (x+a)i^+yj^​. Including the signs of the charges gives
E(x,y)=4πϵ0​q​[R+3​(x−a)i^+yj^​​−R−3​(x+a)i^+yj^​​].
(72)
Reading off both components,
Ex​=4πϵ0​q​[((x−a)2+y2)3/2x−a​−((x+a)2+y2)3/2x+a​],​
(73)
Ey​=4πϵ0​qy​[((x−a)2+y2)3/21​−((x+a)2+y2)3/21​].​
(74)
For example, on the perpendicular bisector x=0, the two y components cancel and the x components add toward the negative charge, checking both relative signs.
Far-field polar components. The pair's electric dipole moment points from negative to positive charge:
p=2qai^,p=2qa.
(75)
Use polar coordinates x=rcosθ, y=rsinθ, with θ measured from that positive dipole axis. The supplied leading electric dipole field becomes
Ex​=4πϵ0​r3p​(3cos2θ−1),Ey​=4πϵ0​r3p​(3cosθsinθ).
(76)
Taking the dot products with the radial and angular unit vectors gives
Er​Eθ​​=Ex​cosθ+Ey​sinθ=4πϵ0​r3p​[3cosθ(cos2θ+sin2θ)−cosθ],=−Ex​sinθ+Ey​cosθ=4πϵ0​r3p​sinθ.​
(77)
Therefore
Er​=4πϵ0​r32pcosθ​,Eθ​=4πϵ0​r3psinθ​.​
(78)
The unit vectors for polar coordinates are orthonormal, so the magnitude is
∣E(r,θ)∣=Er2​+Eθ2​​=4πϵ0​r3p​1+3cos2θ​,r≫a.​
(79)
The units are NC−1, since the electric dipole moment has units Cm.
Angular sketch and comparison. At fixed large r, the magnitude has period π. It is largest on the dipole axis, θ=0,π, with value 2p/(4πϵ0​r3), and smallest on the perpendicular directions, θ=π/2,3π/2, with value p/(4πϵ0​r3). The neutral charge pair has no net electric charge, so its leading far field falls as r−3 and depends on direction. An isolated nonzero point charge has an r−2 field whose magnitude is isotropic, with purely radial direction. The dipole also generally has a nonzero Eθ​ component.
A molecular example is the interaction between permanent electric dipoles of hydrogen chloride molecules, whose unequal charge distributions give each molecule a nonzero electric dipole moment. The electric dipole-dipole interaction depends on their relative orientations:
U=4πϵ0​r3p1​⋅p2​−3(p1​⋅r^)(p2​⋅r^)​.
(80)
For two parallel dipoles pointing along their line of separation, U=−2p1​p2​/(4πϵ0​r3)<0, illustrating an attractive molecular configuration.
