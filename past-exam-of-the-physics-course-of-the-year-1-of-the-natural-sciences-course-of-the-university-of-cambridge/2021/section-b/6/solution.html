Translation and rotation. For particles constituting a rigid body, the total linear momentum is
P=∑i​mi​vi​=MVCM​,dtdP​=∑i​Fi,ext​.
(21)
Thus the total external force governs the motion of the centre of mass. About that centre of mass, the angular momentum and applied torque are
LCM​=∑i​ri′​×mi​vi′​,GCM​=∑i​ri′​×Fi,ext​,dtdLCM​​=GCM​,
(22)
where primes denote relative positions and velocities. The derivative of the vector is taken relative to nonrotating axes. The inertia tensor relates angular momentum to angular velocity, LCM​=Iω. For rotation about a fixed unit direction n, the moment of inertia is In​=∫r⊥2​dm=n⋅In; it measures the resistance to changing that rotation and gives rotational kinetic energy In​ω2/2. The simpler vector relation L=Iω applies when the rotation axis is a principal axis, not for every orientation of an arbitrary rigid body. These translational and rotational balances must be applied together; a nonzero torque does not replace the net-force equation.
Gyroscope precession. A rapidly spinning gyroscope has a large angular momentum approximately along its spin axis. The torque component parallel to this vector changes its magnitude, while the perpendicular component turns its direction. If the vector undergoes precession with angular velocity Ω, then over dt its change is the infinitesimal rotation dL=(Ωdt)×L. Hence
G=dtdL​=Ω×L.​
(23)
This describes direction-changing precession at constant ∣L∣; any spin-up contribution must be added separately. For perpendicular torque and angular momentum, the precessional component perpendicular to L is Ω⊥​=L×G/L2. The familiar tendency of a supported gyroscope to turn sideways is this vector balance, not the absence of gravitational force.
Wheel-lift threshold without the flywheel. Let h=0.50m be the centre of mass height, a=1.0m half the wheel separation, and Rb​=10m the bend radius. The required inward force is F=Mv2/Rb​, provided by static friction. Let No​,Ni​ be the summed outer and inner wheel normal forces. Vertical balance gives No​+Ni​=Mg. Moment balance about the centre of mass gives
a(No​−Ni​)=hRb​Mv2​,Ni​=2M​(g−aRb​hv2​).
(24)
The inner wheels lift when Ni​=0. Thus the vehicle rollover threshold is
vmax​=hgaRb​​​=14.01ms−1=50.4kmh−1.​
(25)
The stated mass cancels. This assumes the rigid geometry and sufficient static friction specified in the paper; the threshold is set by loss of contact, not by sliding.

ωf​=4000602π​=418.88rads−1,Lf​=If​ωf​=1675.52kgm2s−1,Ω=1040/3.6​=1.1111rads−1.

The transverse flywheel axis is perpendicular to the vertical turn axis, so
G=ΩLf​=1.862×103Nm.​
(28)
To specify its direction, use right-handed axes +x forward, +y to the car's left, and +z upward, and consider a left turn. Then Ω=+Ωz. Choose the flywheel angular momentum to the car's right, Lf​=−Lf​y​. The car must apply
Gon flywheel​=+ΩLf​x,Gon car​=−ΩLf​x​
(29)
to obtain the required precession. This gyroscopic roll torque in a turning vehicle opposes the outward roll tendency in a left turn. By the right-hand rule, the flywheel must turn counterclockwise viewed from the car's right side toward its centre, opposite to the rotation of wheels rolling forward. Reversing flywheel spin would aggravate the outward roll. Reversing the turn reverses both roll and gyroscopic torques, so the same flywheel spin remains helpful.
For comparison, the outward inertial roll moment at the stated speed is Mhv2/Rb​=6173Nm, and the limiting gravitational restoring moment is Mga=9810Nm. The gyroscopic reaction is about 30% of the former and 19% of the latter, so it is appreciable for this large flywheel. If this ideal model is extended to include the favorable gyroscopic reaction in the wheel-lift condition, it gives Mhv2/Rb​−Lf​v/Rb​=Mga, or about 56.8kmh−1 rather than 50.4kmh−1. This quantifies a useful but finite increase in the rollover threshold.
