Put δ=ϕ2​−ϕ1​. If both coordinate amplitudes are nonzero, eliminate the common phase to obtain
(x0​x​)2+(y0​y​)2−2x0​y0​xy​cosδ=sin2δ.​
(42)
For sinδ=0, this is an ellipse centred at the origin. The following special cases exhaust the possible nontrivial shapes:
  • δ=0 or π gives straight-line simple harmonic motion, including an axis-aligned line if one coordinate amplitude vanishes.
  • δ=±π/2 gives an axis-aligned ellipse; if additionally x0​=y0​, it is a circle traversed in one of the two orientations.
  • Other phase differences give tilted ellipses.
The degenerate zero-amplitude solution is the stationary origin. There are no general multi-loop patterns here because the two angular frequencies are equal. Another useful description is r=acosωt+bsinωt, the image of a circle under a linear map.
