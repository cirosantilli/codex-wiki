Take q=x¨ positive when the hanging mass descends and the other mass moves uphill. For the sliding block, Newton's second law gives mg−T=mq and T−Mgsinθ=Mq. Therefore
q=m+Mg(m−Msinθ)​,T=m+MmMg(1+sinθ)​.​
(9)
For the pulley coupled to a rolling disc, tension and weight act through its centre; the contact normal is perpendicular to the slope. Static friction provides the torque required for rolling without slipping. Define signed F down the slope, so F>0 for uphill acceleration.
The rotational equation and rolling constraint give Fa=Iq/a, hence F=Mq/2. The translational equations become T−Mgsinθ−F=Mq and mg−T=mq, giving
q=2m+3M2g(m−Msinθ)​,T=2m+3MmMg(3+2sinθ)​,F=2m+3MMg(m−Msinθ)​.​
(10)
Thus the threshold remains m>Msinθ: the moment of inertia increases the effective mass but does not change the sign of the driving force. For downhill acceleration F reverses. The normal force is Mgcosθ; static friction does no work at the stationary contact point and sufficient roughness is assumed.

In standard configuration the frames are inertial, their corresponding axes are parallel, S′ moves at v along the positive x axis of S, and their origins coincide at t=t′=0. Here (t,x,y,z) and (t′,x′,y′,z′) label the same event, c is the speed of light and γ=(1−v2/c2)−1/2.
A clock fixed in S′ has Δx′=0. The inverse time transformation gives
Δt=γΔt′=γΔτ,​
(11)
so the moving clock accumulates less proper time between its two ticks than the elapsed S-coordinate time.
Without time dilation the flight time from the given altitude would be 66.7μs, about 30.3 rest-frame mean lifetimes, giving survival fraction only e−30.3≃6.8×10−14 even at speed c. Relativistic muons can instead reach the ground with appreciable survival. Momentum-selected decay measurements test this clock-rate dependence experimentally.[ref]
A mean-lifetime survival energy estimate requires a mean flight time no greater than the dilated mean lifetime:
γτ0​≳cH​,γ≳30.3,E=γmμ​c2≳3.21GeV.​
(12)
This corresponds to survival probability at least about e−1. There is no sharp minimum energy for an individual random decay. For a required probability p, use exp[−H/(γvτ0​)]≥p. Energy loss in the atmosphere is neglected in this estimate. In the muon's frame the atmospheric path is length-contracted to H/γ≃660m; the ground reaches it in about τ0​, giving the same survival probability.
For the two-body decay, use the rest frame of A. Momentum conservation makes B and the photon oppositely directed with equal momentum magnitude p. Energy conservation and the energy–momentum relation give
mA​c2=EB​+pc,EB2​=p2c2+mB2​c4.
(13)
Substitution and cancellation of EB2​ give
EB​=2mA​(mA2​+mB2​)c2​,Eγ​=2mA​(mA2​−mB2​)c2​.​
(14)
A nonzero emitted photon requires mA​>mB​.

C10 OurBigBook.com
For sinusoidal steady state with convention eiωt, complex impedance is the voltage phasor divided by the current phasor. Its modulus is the amplitude ratio and its argument is the voltage-current phase difference. Since VL​=LdI/dt,
ZL​=iωL,ZC​=iωC1​,ZR​=R.
(19)
The original circuit is a series RLC circuit with output across the inductor. Its transfer function is
H(ω)=V1​V2​​=R+i(ωL−1/(ωC))iωL​,∣V2​∣=R2+(ωL−1/(ωC))2​∣V1​∣ωL​.​
(20)
For the original capacitance, the outputs are 10.15mV at 9MHz and 4.65mV at 10MHz, a ratio 0.458. The current resonance is f0​=1/(2πLC​)=8.967MHz, so the circuit favors the first transmitter.
The resistor voltage is in phase with the current; the inductor voltage leads it by π/2 and the capacitor voltage lags by π/2. Large reactive voltages therefore nearly cancel in their phasor sum. A large inductor voltage is consistent with a small supply voltage: it is inductor voltage magnification, not power gain in a passive circuit.
Reducing the capacitance raises f0​ to 9.452MHz, between the two transmitters. Direct evaluation gives 7.02mV and 7.22mV, respectively. Their similar detunings give approximately equal amplitudes; they are not exactly equal, partly because the numerator of H also depends on frequency.
The two-tone output of a linear resonant circuit, for coherent monochromatic sources of equal input amplitude and arbitrary fixed input phases, is
Vout​(t)=Acos(2πfA​t+ϕA​)+Bcos(2πfB​t+ϕB​).
(21)
With A≃B=Vo​, this is approximately
Vout​≃2Vo​cos[π(fB​−fA​)t+2ϕB​−ϕA​​]cos[2π2fA​+fB​​t+2ϕA​+ϕB​​].​
(22)
Thus a 9.5MHz rapid oscillation has a beat envelope repeating in magnitude at 1MHz. The signed envelope cosine has frequency 0.5MHz. Unequal amplitudes leave a small nonzero envelope minimum; independently drifting transmitter phases make the beating drift. No new frequency is generated by this ideal linear circuit.

A travelling wave, for example Acos(kx−ωt), has moving constant-phase surfaces. A standing wave, for example 2Asin(kx)cos(ωt), has stationary nodes and can be formed from two equal counterpropagating waves.
For the half-line finite well with E>V0​, define k=2mE​/ℏ and q=2m(E−V0​)​/ℏ. The hard wall requires ψ=0 for x<0 and ψ(0)=0, so the general allowed forms are
ψ(x)=⎩⎨⎧​0,Asin(kx),Beiq(x−a)+Ce−iq(x−a),​x<0,0<x<a,x>a.​​
(29)
At the finite step both ψ and ψ′ are continuous:
Asin(ka)=B+C,Akcos(ka)=iq(B−C).
(30)
There is no requirement to match the derivative to zero across the infinite wall.
For a bound state 0<E<V0​, put κ=2m(V0​−E)​/ℏ>0. Normalizability excludes the growing exponential, leaving Asin(kx) inside and De−κ(x−a) outside. Matching their logarithmic derivatives gives
kcot(ka)=−κ,tan(ka)=−k/κ.​
(31)
The exterior expression is equivalent to Ce−κx by rescaling its constant. Negative cotangent first occurs for π/2<ka<π; all higher allowed intervals also have ka>π/2. Thus λ1​=2π/k<4a. Since k<2mV0​​/ℏ, a normalizable bound state requires
V0​>8ma2π2ℏ2​.​
(32)
This is also sufficient: on the lowest negative-cotangent branch, the two matching sides cross whenever a2mV0​​/ℏ>π/2. The printed non-strict inequality includes the threshold limit. At equality E=V0​ and the exterior is not square-integrable, so it is not itself a bound state.
For the helium mass and energy scale given, the limiting width is
ath​=8mHe​V0​​πℏ​=1.14×10−10m=0.114nm.​
(33)
Actual binding requires a width just larger than this threshold in the stated ideal potential.

Represent each wave by a complex phasor. Add phasors before squaring: two amplitudes give I∝∣A1​+A2​eiδ∣2=A12​+A22​+2A1​A2​cosδ. For N equally illuminated narrow slits, adjacent phasors differ by δ=2πdsinθ/λ, so
SN​=∑n=0N−1​einδ=ei(N−1)δ/2sin(δ/2)sin(Nδ/2)​.
(34)
All phasors align when δ=2πm, giving the grating equation
dsinθm​=mλ,Iprincipal​=N2I1​.​
(35)
The nearest zero has adjacent-phase increment 2π/N: the phasor polygon closes once. Thus dcosθm​Δθ≃λ/N, giving
Δθ≃Ndcosθm​λ​=Wcosθm​λ​.​
(36)
The angular linearization is valid away from grazing directions; the underlying exact first-zero condition is d(sinθzero​−sinθm​)=±λ/N.
For the first subsidiary maximum of a large slit array, use the stipulated 1.5 phasor wraps: Nδ/2≃3π/2 relative to a principal maximum. Its amplitude is approximately 2N/(3π) times one slit's amplitude. Therefore
Isubsidiary​Iprincipal​​≃49π2​=22.2.​
(37)
This uses the question's approximate maximum position, not the exact extremum of the slit-array intensity.
The Rayleigh criterion places one line's principal maximum at the other's first minimum. Grating dispersion then gives ∣m∣N≳λ/δλ=1.674×106. Combining with normal incidence, ∣m∣λ=d∣sinθ∣, gives the normal-incidence grating width bound:
W≳δλ∣sinθ∣λ2​≥δλλ2​=1.289m.​
(38)
The 1.29m value is an ideal lower bound approached near grazing diffraction. A usable finite angle needs greater width; for example θ=60∘ requires about 1.49m. The exact sine first-zero relation still gives the lower bound when the local angular approximation fails near grazing.
Such a coherently illuminated grating is very large and needs accurate groove placement and suitable collection optics; an unaided far-field screen would require distances much greater than W2/λ∼2×106m. Focusing optics avoid that screen distance but not the required resolving power of a diffraction grating. Real linewidth, slit width and aberrations can require more resolution. The unequal isotope abundances also make this ideal Rayleigh estimate less decisive than for equally bright lines.

An inverse-square field spreads through area proportional to squared distance. More generally the flux contribution of a point source is proportional to the solid angle subtended by a surface element; the oriented solid angles sum to 4π for an enclosed source and zero for an exterior one. Superposition extends this to a mass distribution.
Newton's law of universal gravitation gives g=−GMr^/r2 for a point mass. A surrounding sphere therefore has flux −4πGM, so
C=4πG,∮g⋅dS=−4πGMenc​.​
(39)
For the tunnel, approximate its water as an infinite uniform cylinder with R=3.5m and density ρ=1000kgm−3. The bob is at radial distance r=R+1.5m=5.0m. A coaxial Gaussian surface gives
2πrLc​gr​=−4πG(πR2ρLc​),∣gr​∣=r2πGρR2​.
(40)
For the plumb-line deflection from cylindrical gravity, horizontal equilibrium gives d/L≃∣gr​∣/g. Draining removes the attraction toward the tunnel, so the bob shifts away by
d≃rg2πGρR2L​=5.23×10−7m=0.523μm.​
(41)
The unchanged rock and ordinary gravitational field cancel in the before/after comparison; end effects and mechanical disturbances are neglected.
For the tidal water loading of a spring balance, approximate the extra water at high tide as an infinite plane slab of thickness h=8m, with the suspended mass above it. The gravitational field of an infinite plane sheet, derived with a pillbox Gaussian surface, gives added downward field Δg=2πGρh. With initial spring extension x0​=mg/k=1m,
Δx=km​Δg=x0​g2πGρh​=3.42×10−7m=0.342μm.​
(42)
The extension increases at high tide. These estimates use g=9.81ms−2 and the stated water-density approximation; salinity, finite estuary geometry and terrestrial tidal deformation alter a real measurement.

a OurBigBook.com
A field assigns a physical quantity to each point. Gravitational field is force per unit test mass; electric field is force per unit test charge. In a conservative, time-independent field,
F=−∇U,ΔU=−∫F⋅dr.​
(43)
Write U=mΦ for gravitational potential Φ, or U=qϕ for electric potential ϕ, giving g=−∇Φ and E=−∇ϕ. A point mass has U=−GMm/r with zero at infinity, while two point charges have U=Qq/(4πϵ0​r). Only potential-energy differences matter; a constant can be added freely. The mechanical energy K+U is conserved if no nonconservative forces do work. A magnetic Lorentz force qv×B does no work on a point charge and generally cannot be represented by a position-only scalar potential energy.

Faraday's law relates induced electromotive force to the rate of change of magnetic flux linkage. For a coil with N turns and a consistent right-hand orientation,
E=−dtd(NΦB​)​.​
(44)
Flux can change through field variation or circuit motion. Lenz's law is the minus sign: the induced current's field opposes the flux change, consistent with energy conservation. It opposes the change rather than necessarily opposing the original field.
For a linear fixed-geometry inductor, NΦB​=LI and the self-induced electromotive force is −LdI/dt. The passive terminal voltage drop is instead V=LdI/dt. The stored magnetic energy is
UB​=∫0I​LI′dI′=21​LI2.​
(45)
Inductance is measured in henries. Transformers use mutual inductance: changing one winding's current induces an electromotive force in another.

For steady currents in vacuum, the Biot-Savart law sums the contributions of current elements:
B(r)=4πμ0​I​∫∣r−r′∣3dℓ′×(r−r′)​.​

For a test mass moving around a fixed central mass M, gravity is central and conservative. Specific angular momentum h=∣r×v∣ and specific orbital energy ϵ=v2/2−GM/r are constant. The motion is planar and sweeps equal areas in equal times, dA/dt=h/2.
For a nonradial orbit, the inverse-square force gives a conic section,
r=1+ecosθh2/(GM)​,e2=1+(GM)22ϵh2​.​
(48)
Negative energy gives an ellipse, zero energy a parabola and positive energy a hyperbola. For an ellipse of semimajor axis a,
ϵ=−2aGM​,P2=GM4π2a3​,v2=GM(r2​−a1​).​
(49)
A circular orbit is the e=0 case, with v=GM/r​. Escape from radius r requires v≥2GM/r​ in the absence of further forces. For two comparable masses, apply the relative two-body equation with GM replaced by G(M+m), rather than treating one body as fixed.
