A time-independent conservative force defines a conservative vector field on a domain when the work between two points is independent of path, equivalently when every closed-path work integral vanishes. Define a potential energy U by F=−∇U​ and U(B)−U(A)=−∫AB​F⋅dr. The additive constant is arbitrary; only differences affect work. A vanishing curl is necessary, and is sufficient on a simply connected domain under the usual smoothness assumptions. Local zero curl by itself need not guarantee a single-valued global potential on a domain with holes.
For a test mass outside a point or spherical mass, U=−GMm/r with zero at infinity reproduces the inward gravitational force. Near a surface with approximately uniform downward gravity, U=mgz when z increases upward. A linear spring has U=kx2/2. In electrostatics the electric potential is energy per unit charge, so U=qV and E=−∇V; potential and potential energy have different units and roles.
For motion under a time-independent conservative force, d(K+U)/dt=0. nonconservative forces change the mechanical energy by their work, and an explicitly time-dependent potential can exchange energy with the external agency changing it. A time-varying magnetic field can give a circulating electric field, so not every electric field is electrostatic or conservative. Conversely, zero magnetic work on a charge does not turn a velocity-dependent magnetic force into an ordinary position-potential force.
