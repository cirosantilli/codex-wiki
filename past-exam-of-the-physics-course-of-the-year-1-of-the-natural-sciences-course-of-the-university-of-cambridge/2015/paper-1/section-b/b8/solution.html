Newton's first law identifies inertial frames in which a force-free body maintains constant velocity. The second is P˙=Fnet​ for a closed system; for fixed mass it becomes ma=F. The third gives equal and opposite pair forces. Summing the second law over a closed collection cancels internal force pairs, so total momentum is conserved when the resultant external force vanishes. A rocket alone is an open variable-mass subsystem, so one must include the momentum carried by exhaust before applying this reasoning.
Choose downward velocity as positive and write M(t)=M0​−αt. During dt, exhaust mass dm=αdt leaves downward with speed v+vE​ in the surface frame. To first order, momentum balance for rocket plus this exhaust gives
Mv+Mgdt=(M−dm)(v+dv)+dm(v+vE​),v˙=g−M0​−αtαvE​​.
(13)
Integration, with v(0)=v0​, yields
v(t)=v0​+gt+vE​logM0​M0​−αt​.​
(14)
Setting g=0 gives the gravity-free result. This assumes a constant downward relative exhaust speed, constant mass-loss rate and M0​−αt>0 throughout the burn; the negative logarithm supplies the upward thrust effect.
Height satisfies h˙=−v. Put u(t)=1−αt/M0​. Integrating the logarithm and choosing h(0)=h0​ gives
h(t)=h0​−v0​t−21​gt2+αM0​vE​​[ulogu−u+1].​
(15)
Equivalently, the last term is vE​[t+(M0​−αt)logu/α]. Differentiating either form recovers −v and checks the sign.
For this constant-rate retro-rocket landing, at touchdown uL​=3/4 and vL​=0, hence
tL​=gvE​log(4/3)−v0​​=12.481s.​
(16)
The fuel condition gives α=M0​/(4tL​)=200.30 kg/s. Requiring h(tL​)=0 then gives
h0​=v0​tL​+21​gtL2​−vE​tL​[1+3log(3/4)]=275.4m.​
(17)
In this burn the downward speed decreases monotonically to zero: the initial thrust acceleration already exceeds gravity and increases as fuel is consumed. The result is the idealized vertical landing, without atmospheric drag, changing gravity or a separate terminal burn phase.
