For a flexible uniform string with linear mass density ρ and tension T, dimensional analysis gives [T/ρ]=L2T−2 and hence c∝T/ρ​. The small-slope force balance ρψtt​=Tψxx​ fixes the factor to one:
c=T/ρ​,ω=ck.​
(34)
Here ρ has units kg/m, not the volume-density units used for a bulk fluid. The phase moves along the string at c, while a material point moves transversely with ψt​=Aωsin(kx−ωt). Its maximum speed is Aω, which is not the phase velocity. Small slope Ak≪1 makes Aω≪c in this model.
The kinetic energy and extra elastic energy of a segment are
K=21​∫ρψt2​dx,U=21​∫Tψx2​dx.
(35)
The second expression follows from extra length 1+ψx2​​dx−dx≃ψx2​dx/2 against the pre-existing constant tension. Time averaging a harmonic wave gives ⟨EK​⟩=ρA2ω2/4 and ⟨EU​⟩=TA2k2/4=ρA2ω2/4. Therefore
εav​=21​ρA2ω2.​
(36)
At the driven endpoint the string pulls along its local tangent into x>0, with vertical component Tψx​ for small slope. The point exerts the opposite force on the string, so the driven-string endpoint power is
P(0,t)=−Tψx​(0,t)ψt​(0,t).​
(37)
For the outgoing wave ψ=Acos(kx−ωt), at x=0 the derivatives are ψx​=Aksinωt and ψt​=−Aωsinωt. Thus P=TA2kωsin2ωt and
Pav​=21​TA2kω=cεav​.​
(38)
The energy flux of a transverse string wave follows by combining the energy density E=(ρψt2​+Tψx2​)/2 and flux P=−Tψx​ψt​, which satisfy ∂t​E+∂x​P=0. For a pure right-going nondispersive wave, P=cE even instantaneously. energy is transported with the wave; a material string element merely oscillates about its unperturbed position.
