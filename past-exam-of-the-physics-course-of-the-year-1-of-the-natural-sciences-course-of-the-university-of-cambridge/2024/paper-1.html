V(x) is the particle's potential energy as a function of position, and E is its total energy eigenvalue in this time-independent Schrödinger equation. Solving it with the boundary conditions determines the allowed stationary states and energy levels.
The wavefunction is a probability amplitude: the Born rule gives position probability density ∣ψ(x)∣2, normalized to unit integral for one particle. ψ itself need not be real or positive and is not a classical matter-density profile. The full stationary-state time dependence is Ψ(x,t)=ψ(x)e−iEt/ℏ, leaving ∣Ψ∣2 time independent.

In magnetostatics, Ampère's circuital law is
∮C​B⋅dℓ=μ0​Ienclosed​.
(46)
It relates magnetic circulation around a closed curve to the steady current through a spanning surface. It is especially useful when symmetry makes the tangential field constant on a chosen path: an infinite straight wire gives B=μ0​I/(2πr), while an ideal long solenoid gives approximately B=μ0​nI inside.
The Biot-Savart law gives the field contribution from the actual current geometry:
B(r)=4πμ0​I​∫∣r−r′∣3dℓ′×(r−r′)​.
(47)
The cross product determines the direction by the right-hand rule. Integrating around a circular loop of radius a gives B=μ0​I/(2a) at its centre. A finite wire segment can also be integrated directly, even though it lacks enough symmetry for an Ampère-loop shortcut. The full steady-current circuit includes a return path; a segment calculation specifies that segment's contribution.
