In vacuum the electric and magnetic field energy densities are
uE​=2ϵ0​E2​,uB​=2μ0​B2​.​
(59)
For example, charging a capacitor requires U=∫0Q​(q/C)dq=Q2/(2C)=CV2/2. A parallel-plate capacitor with C=ϵ0​A/d and E=V/d then gives U=ϵ0​E2Ad/2, identifying the electric field energy density. Similarly an inductor stores LI2/2; applying this to a long solenoid yields B2/(2μ0​) times its interior volume.
Electromagnetic energy is transported by the Poynting vector S=E×B/μ0​. Poynting's theorem,
∂t∂(uE​+uB​)​+∇⋅S=−J⋅E,
(60)
expresses local energy conservation and transfer to matter. In a vacuum plane wave E=cB, so the two energy densities are equal and the energy flux has magnitude c(uE​+uB​). For nondispersive linear media the corresponding reversible expressions are E⋅D/2 and B⋅H/2; they must not be applied unchanged to arbitrary dispersive or hysteretic materials.
