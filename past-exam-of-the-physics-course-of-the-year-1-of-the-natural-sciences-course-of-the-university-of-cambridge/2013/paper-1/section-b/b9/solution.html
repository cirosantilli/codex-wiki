For a fixed rotation axis with constant moment of inertia, the axial torque obeys τ=Iθ¨, with τ=∑(r×F)⋅eaxis​. For a point mass at radius l, the tangential equation is Ft​=mlθ¨; multiplying by l gives lFt​=ml2θ¨, as required. Radial forces have zero moment about the axis.
Take increasing θ in the direction of the clapper's initial fall and let the contact force N oppose that tangential motion. Initial equilibrium of the clapper gives
Nl=mglsinβ,Ninitial​=mgsinβ.​
(25)
While contact persists the bell and clapper rotate together. Their respective offsets from the upward vertical are θ−β and θ. Summing external gravitational moments eliminates their internal contact force:
θ¨=Mk2+ml2Mghsin(θ−β)+mglsinθ​.​
(26)
The clapper alone satisfies ml2θ¨=mglsinθ−Nl. Hence for m≪M,
N≃mg[sinθ−k2lh​sin(θ−β)].​
(27)
A nonadhesive contact cannot exert tension. The first zero of this expression on the released trajectory is the loss-of-contact angle. Write q=lh/k2=0.9375. Then
(1−qcosβ)sinθ0​+qsinβcosθ0​=0,θ0​=111.9∘.​
(28)
The second-quadrant root is reached from θ=β=30∘; selecting the principal negative arctangent would choose the wrong branch. Indeed the exact contact force before taking m/M→0 is mMg[k2sinθ−lhsin(θ−β)]/(Mk2+ml2), so its zero is unchanged.
