Across a uniformly illuminated slit, the phase difference between its edges is 2πasinθ/λ. Integrating the complex amplitude across the slit gives the single-slit diffraction factor
I(0)I(θ)​=[qsinq​]2,q=λπasinθ​≃λπaθ​.
(39)
Thus β=πa/λ in the small-angle amplitude expression. The first noncentral zero has q=π, so θ0​≃λ/a. At the stated approximate subsidiary maximum,
I(0)I(1.5λ/a)​≃9π24​=0.0450.​
(40)
The exact first side maximum solves tanq=q, giving q≃4.493 and intensity ratio 0.0472; the requested approximation need not be mistaken for the exact maximum.
A virtual object is a point toward which incident rays are already converging and would meet if the lens were removed. With real-is-positive object/image distances it has u<0. The thin-lens equation gives 1/v=1/f−1/u; as u→−∞, v→f, so a distant virtual object images in the rear focal plane.
Without the lens, y≃Lθ puts the central maximum at zero and the first dark fringes at y=±Lλ0​/a=±0.100m. Subsequent zeros are integer multiples, and weaker subsidiary maxima lie between them.
For the finite-distance pattern compression, take the old screen as the prospective image plane at L=6m, with u=−L, and the new screen at d=0.10m. Then
f1​=d1​−L1​,f=L−dLd​=0.101695m,R=(n−1)f=0.0610m.​
(41)
The plane surface has infinite radius of curvature. The transverse scale is d/L=1/60, yielding
y0,new​=Ld​y0,old​=adλ0​​=1.67mm.​
(42)
The finite-distance scaling can also be checked without treating an intensity pattern as a collection of geometrical point objects: the slit-to-screen Fresnel integral after a thin lens has quadratic coefficient 1/d−1/f=1/L and linear term −y/d. It therefore reproduces the old diffraction pattern at transverse coordinate Y=Ly/d, up to an overall amplitude and phase. If the old far-field plane is treated as infinitely distant, the equally useful leading approximation is f=d, R=6.0cm; the finite-distance correction is only 1.7%.
