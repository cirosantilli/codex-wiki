For sinusoidal steady state with the eiωt convention, complex impedance is the phasor ratio Z=V/I, encoding both amplitude ratio and phase. From V=LdI/dt, an ideal inductor has ZL​=iωL; from I=CdV/dt, a capacitor has ZC​=(iωC)−1.
The original diagram has R1​,L1​ in series, and R4​,C4​ in parallel. Thus
Z1​=R1​+iωL1​,Z4​=1+iωR4​C4​R4​​.
(24)
With the right supply node as the reference, the two unloaded voltage divider outputs are VA​=V0​R2​/(Z1​+R2​) and VB​=V0​Z4​/(R3​+Z4​). Subtracting gives
VAB​=V0​[Z1​+R2​R2​​−R3​+Z4​Z4​​].​
(25)
The series-RL parallel-RC bridge balance is Z1​Z4​=R2​R3​. Equating its real and imaginary coefficients gives
R1​=R4​R2​R3​​=25Ω,L1​=R2​R3​C4​=0.250H.​
(26)
These ideal components balance the bridge at any frequency, not just the test frequency.
At ω=200rads−1, the current amplitude through R2​ is 5/∣50+50i∣=0.0707A. Using peak rather than RMS supply amplitude,
PR2​​=21​∣I∣2R2​=0.0625W.​
(27)
In the DC steady state the inductor is a short and the capacitor is open. Both dividers have equal resistors, so each midpoint is at2.5V and the bridge stays balanced. The upper-branch current is0.100A, giving PR2​,DC​=0.250W​.
For the high-input-resistance voltmeter, hold the other components fixed. At the DC balance,
dR1​dVAB​​=−(R1​+R2​)2V0​R2​​=−0.0500VΩ−1,δR1​≃0.05000.001​=0.020Ω.​
(28)
The DC bridge resistance resolution is a local readout resolution, or0.08% of R1​, not a total uncertainty allowing for supply, component calibration or voltmeter loading. [ref]
