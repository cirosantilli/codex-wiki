Use the PDF's notation f0​ for the source-frame pulse frequency, and set β=v/c, γ=(1−β2)−1/2. Successive emissions occur at the same train-frame position and are separated by proper time 1/f0​. The last observation requires a transverse-geometry convention. The general observer-frame ray formula is
λ=γλ3′​(1−βcosθ),​
(30)
where θ is the angle between source velocity and photon propagation in the platform frame at emission. Under the usual transverse-ray interpretation, θ=π/2, so for β=0.6 and γ=1.25,
λ=γλ3′​=500nmfor a platform-frame transverse ray.​
(31)
This is the standard transverse redshift from time dilation.
There is a genuine distinction between emission and reception at the instant of passing. A side-mounted source emitting perpendicularly in its own frame instead has cosθ=β by aberration of light, giving λ=λ3′​/γ=320nm. The same blue result occurs if “at the exact moment” means reception when the source's current position is at closest approach, with a small finite transverse offset. For offset d, reception and emission satisfy tr​=te​+v2te2​+d2​/c. Setting tr​=0 gives te​=−γd/c and cosθ=β; setting te​=0 instead gives the transverse ray received at tr​=d/c. These limits remain different as d→0.
Thus 500 nm requires the transverse ray in the platform frame; 320 nm applies to reception at closest approach or transverse emission in the train frame. The wording does not distinguish these event/frame choices, so merely neglecting the source-observer separation is insufficient to select between them. The reusable derivation is transverse Doppler shift and observation time.
