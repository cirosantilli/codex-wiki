Expand the cloud's potential energy about a stable equilibrium: U(z)=U(0)+U′(0)z+U′′(0)z2/2+⋯. Equilibrium gives U′(0)=0, stability gives U′′(0)=k>0, and reflection symmetry also removes odd terms. For small displacements, U−U(0)≃kz2/2 and F=−dU/dz≃−kz.

Define the nonnegative amplitude z0​=∣z∣ and δ=atan2(2γω,ω02​−ω2). Then
z0​=(ω02​−ω2)2+4γ2ω2​∣q∣E0​/m​,ϕ=argq−δ(mod2π).​

For positive q, ϕ=−δ. For electrons q<0, the phase of a negatively charged driven oscillator is ϕ=π−δ: the low-frequency displacement is opposite to the field, rather than in phase with it. A signed amplitude could absorb this extra π, but the phase convention must then be stated. The induced dipole p=qz has phase −δ for either charge sign. Transient free oscillations have been omitted because the question asks for steady state.
For the electron cloud, q/m=−e/me​ and
ω0​=me​k/n​​=9.109×10−31100​​=1.05×1016rads−1.
(35)
The visible range corresponds to ω=2πc/λ between 2.35×1015 and 4.71×1015rads−1, below this resonance. The resonant wavelength is about 180nm, in the ultraviolet.
The acceleration amplitude is ω2z0​. At fixed incident field amplitude, the scattered intensity is therefore proportional to
Isc​(ω)∝(ω02​−ω2)2+4γ2ω2ω4​.​
(36)
Well below resonance and outside strong damping corrections, z0​ is nearly frequency-independent and this becomes the Rayleigh scattering wavelength dependence Isc​∝ω4∝λ−4. The shortest visible wavelengths are consequently scattered much more strongly into directions away from the direct solar beam than the longest ones, producing a blue sky. The simple fourth-power approximation gives a factor 16 between 400 and 800 nm at equal incident intensity. Retaining the undamped denominator with the given resonance raises that factor to about 22.7, since the upper end is not extremely far below resonance. The exact ratio needs the unspecified damping and incident solar spectrum; the stronger scattering of the blue end is the robust conclusion here.
The model describes frequency-dependent scattering, not the detailed perceived color after solar-spectrum weighting and human visual response. It also explains why treating the electrons as free at visible frequencies would miss the bound-charge fourth-power limit.
