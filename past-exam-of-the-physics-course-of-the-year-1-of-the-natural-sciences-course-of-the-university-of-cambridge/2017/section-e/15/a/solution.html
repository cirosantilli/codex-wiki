A charge q moving nonrelativistically in prescribed fields obeys the Lorentz force law
mdtdv​=q(E+v×B).​
(62)
The magnetic part is perpendicular to velocity, so d(mv2/2)/dt=qE⋅v: the electric field can change kinetic energy, while the magnetic field alone changes direction without doing work.
In a uniform electric field with no magnetic field, acceleration is constant: v=v0​+(qE/m)t and r=r0​+v0​t+qEt2/(2m). The trajectory is a parabola when there is initial motion perpendicular to the field, and a line when motion is purely along it. Reversing the charge reverses the electric acceleration.
In a uniform magnetic field with no electric field, resolve velocity into parallel and perpendicular components. The parallel component is constant; the perpendicular component rotates at the cyclotron frequency
ωc​=m∣q∣B​,rL​=∣q∣Bmv⊥​​.​
(63)
The resulting path is a helix, or a circle if v∥​=0, with pitch 2πv∥​/ωc​. Charge sign determines its sense of rotation. These relations underlie magnetic bending and mass/charge analysis. At relativistic energies, use momentum p=γmv; at fixed energy the cyclotron frequency becomes ∣q∣B/(γm).
For uniform crossed fields E⊥B, the velocity vD​=E×B/B2 satisfies E+vD​×B=0. The orbit is a gyromotion about a guiding center drifting with
vD​=B2E×B​,​
(64)
independent of mass and charge sign. A velocity selector transmits particles with the appropriate direction and speed E/B without deflection. If the electric field has a component along the magnetic field, that component accelerates the parallel motion instead. Nonuniform fields can add other drifts; the simple helix and uniform crossed-field expressions require the stated uniform-field assumptions.
