Equation and interpretation. The supplied equation is the time-independent Schrödinger equation for a nonrelativistic particle with mass m in a prescribed potential energy V(x). Equivalently,
Hψ=Eψ,H=−2mℏ2​dx2d2​+V(x).
(70)
It is an eigenvalue problem for the Hamiltonian operator. A stationary energy state has full wavefunction Ψ(x,t)=ψ(x)e−iEt/ℏ. By the Born rule, ∣ψ(x)∣2dx is the probability of finding the particle in a small interval dx, with wavefunction normalization ∫−∞∞​∣ψ∣2dx=1.
Infinite barriers. Infinite exterior potential energy excludes any finite-energy probability outside the interval. The infinite square well consequently has ψ=0 outside and the hard-wall boundary conditions
ψ(0)=ψ(a)=0.​
(71)
The interior wavefunction must meet the zero exterior amplitude continuously. Its derivative need not be continuous across an infinite wall; imposing both zero value and zero derivative would eliminate every nonzero interior solution.
Ground state. Inside the well V=0, so ψ′′+k2ψ=0, with k=2mE​/ℏ. Its general solution is Asinkx+Bcoskx. The left wall gives B=0, and the right wall gives ka=nπ for n=1,2,…. Thus the discrete energy eigenvalues are En​=n2π2ℏ2/(2ma2). The smallest, n=1, is the ground state. Since
∫0a​sin2aπx​dx=2a​,
(72)
its normalized wavefunction, choosing an irrelevant overall phase to make it positive inside, is
ψ1​(x)={2/a​sin(πx/a),0,​0<x<a,x≤0 or x≥a,​E1​=2ma2π2ℏ2​.​
(73)
Finite barriers. For a finite step and constant particle mass, both ψ and ψ′ are continuous at x=0,a. Wavefunction matching at a finite potential step follows by integrating the Schrödinger equation across a vanishingly small neighborhood of the step: a bounded potential cannot generate a finite jump in ψ′. The bound-state exterior solutions decay rather than vanish identically:
ψ(x)∝eκx(x<0),ψ(x)∝e−κ(x−a)(x>a),κ=ℏ2m(Vw​−E)​​.
(74)
Normalizability excludes the growing exterior exponentials. The equivalent interior boundary conditions are ψ′(0)=κψ(0) and ψ′(a)=−κψ(a); neither boundary value is zero for the nontrivial ground state.
The ground state of a finite square well remains symmetric about a/2 and has no nodes. It bends smoothly into exponential tails at both walls, extending over a larger region than the infinite-well state. Its maximum after normalization is lower for the deep well considered here. When Vw​≫E, the penetration length 1/κ is short, so the two shapes are close except near the walls and in the exterior tails.
The finite curve is an illustrative exact matched solution with Vw​=10E1,∞​; no numerical wall height is specified by the question.
Energy comparison. The finite barriers relax the confinement, allowing a less rapidly varying interior wavefunction and therefore smaller kinetic energy. More explicitly, the even interior solution is proportional to cos[k(x−a/2)], and matching to the tails gives ktan(ka/2)=κ. The lowest root lies at 0<ka<π, while the infinite well has ka=π. Hence
E1,finite​<2ma2π2ℏ2​=E1,∞​.​
(75)
As Vw​→∞, the tails disappear and the finite-well energy approaches the infinite-well value from below. This comparison uses the same zero of potential energy inside both wells.
