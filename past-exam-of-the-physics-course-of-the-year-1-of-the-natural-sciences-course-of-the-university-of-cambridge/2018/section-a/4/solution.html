First intensity minimum. In Fraunhofer diffraction, the path difference between adjacent slits is dsinθ, so the relative wave phase is δ=2πdsinθ/λ1​. Summing the N equal complex amplitudes as a geometric series gives the diffraction grating interference factor
I(θ)∝​∑j=0N−1​eijδ​2=sin2(δ/2)sin2(Nδ/2)​.
(11)
The central maximum has δ=0. Its nearest zero occurs at Nδ/2=π, where the denominator is nonzero. Thus
sinθmin​=Ndλ1​​=10(80×10−6)640×10−9​=8.00×10−4,∣θmin​∣=8.00×10−4rad=0.0458∘.​
(12)
There is a symmetric first zero on each side of the incident direction. The narrow slits are treated as having a sufficiently broad single-slit diffraction envelope.
Wavelength resolution and the missing order specification. Adopt the Rayleigh criterion: the principal maximum of one wavelength lies at the adjacent minimum of the other in the same nonzero diffraction order m. For λ2​>λ1​ and m>0, those directions obey
dsinθ=mλ2​=(m+N1​)λ1​.
(13)
Consequently the ideal resolving power of a diffraction grating is λ1​/Δλ=mN, giving
Δλmin,m​=mNλ1​​=m64​nm.​
(14)
The usual first-order reading therefore gives 64nm. Zero diffraction order cannot separate the two wavelengths, because both central maxima coincide.
The paper does not specify which diffraction order is observed, so 64nm is not an order-independent minimum. In the ideal infinitely narrow-slit model, the largest usable order for this positive wavelength separation must satisfy (m+1/N)λ1​≤d. Since d/λ1​=125, this permits m≤124, giving the formal all-order result
Δλmin,124​=0.516nm.​
(15)
That pair meets the criterion at θ≃83.1∘. Order 125 of λ1​ is at grazing incidence and the corresponding longer-wavelength peak cannot propagate. In a real device, finite slit width, weak high-order intensity and screen acceptance may prevent such an extreme order from being used; those data are absent. Thus the specified-order expression, and 64nm when first order is intended, are the defensible resolution answers under the stated criterion.
