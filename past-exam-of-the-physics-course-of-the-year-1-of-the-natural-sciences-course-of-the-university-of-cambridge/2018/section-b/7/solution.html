Powered vertical flight. Take upward velocity v as positive, and let ve​>0 be the downward exhaust speed relative to the rocket. During dt the rocket's mass changes by dm<0, and exhaust mass −dm leaves with Earth-frame velocity v−ve​. Compare the momentum of the rocket plus this newly expelled exhaust before and after the interval:
(m+dm)(v+dv)+(−dm)(v−ve​)−mv=−mgdt.
(22)
Discard the second-order product dmdv. The result is mdv+ve​dm=−mgdt, or
mdtdv​=−ve​dtdm​−mg.
(23)
The positive rocket thrust is −ve​m˙. Integrating with v(0)=0 and m(0)=m0​ gives the rocket equation with gravitational loss:
v(t)=ve​lnm(t)m0​​−gt.​
(24)
Using d(mv)/dt for the rocket alone without the exhaust momentum flux would give the wrong equation for this open system.
Orbital speed under the stated gravity assumption. A circular orbit of radius ro​=RE​+ho​=7.90×106m requires centripetal acceleration vo2​/ro​. If the magnitude of gravitational acceleration is the stipulated constant g=9.8ms−2 even at the orbit,
ro​vo2​​=g,vo​=gro​​=8.80×103ms−1.​
(25)
This treats gravity as centrally directed for the orbital calculation, while retaining its constant magnitude. The subsequent flat-surface approximation treats its direction as locally vertical.
Burnout height. During the unpowered projectile motion, horizontal velocity is constant. At the top of the trajectory the vertical component vanishes, so the horizontal component must equal the orbital speed vo​. At burnout, therefore,
vy,b​=vo​tan30∘,ho​−hb​=2gvy,b2​​=6gvo2​​.
(26)
These follow either by integrating y¨​=−g or by the vertical work-energy theorem. Substituting the preceding constant-g orbital result gives
hb​=ho​−6RE​+ho​​=1.83×105m≃183km.​
(27)
The vertical component is initially positive, as required to reach the orbit after burnout.
Alternative inverse-square interpretation. A real Earth's exterior Newtonian gravitational field is not constant over a 1500km altitude change. If 9.8ms−2 instead means the surface value g0​, then Newton's law of universal gravitation gives GM=g0​RE2​ and
vo​=RE​+ho​g0​RE2​​​=7.13×103ms−1.
(28)
Using this orbital speed but the paper's constant-g flat projectile motion approximation for the coast would give
hb​=1.50×106−6(RE​+ho​)RE2​​=6.36×105m≃636km.​
(29)
This alternative changes the global constant-gravity assumption; it is not the same model as the 183km result. An exact curved Kepler orbit coast would also abandon the prescribed flat trajectory. The printed assumptions support the constant-magnitude answers above, while the inverse-square values explain the different answer obtained from the usual physical Earth-orbit formula.
