Directions. Choose +x along the horizontal spindle from the pivot to the disk, and +z vertically upward. With lever arm r=lx and weight Fg​=−mgz, the torque about the pivot is
τ=r×Fg​=mgly​.
(30)
Clockwise precession viewed from above has Ω=−Ωz. The relation τ=Ω×L therefore requires the spin angular momentum to point along −x, from the disk toward the pivot. The disk spins clockwise when viewed from its outer face looking along the spindle toward the pivot. Looking from the pivot reverses that apparent rotation. The right-hand rule fixes these signs unambiguously.
The drawing shows an instantaneous top view; the disk's angular momentum and the horizontal spindle turn clockwise together. The pivot force has zero torque about the pivot.
Rate of precession. For steady horizontal motion the spin contribution has magnitude L=Iω and turns through dϕ=Ωdt in time dt. Its change is perpendicular to itself and has magnitude dL=Ldϕ. Hence
∣τ∣=​dtdL​​=ΩIω=mgl,Ω=Iωmgl​.​
(31)
This is the usual slow steady gyroscope precession relation. Any constant vertical angular momentum associated with steady precession has zero time derivative; the horizontal spin contribution supplies the changing component here. Transient nutation is excluded by the prescribed horizontal steady motion.
Clock design. Convert both rotation rates to angular frequencies:
Ω=602π​rads−1,ω=2π(300)rads−1.
(32)
For the moment of inertia of a uniform disc, I=mR2/2, so the mass cancels:
l=2gΩR2ω​=2(9.8)(2π/60)(0.10)2(600π)​=0.1007m≃10.1cm.​
(33)
Spin friction. At fixed geometry, reducing ω increases Ω∝1/ω. The precession period, Tp​=2π/Ω, therefore decreases. This trend holds while slow steady precession remains a suitable description; it cannot be extrapolated to a stopped disk.
Counterweight. To oppose the disk's gravitational torque, place the counterweight on the other side of the pivot, at −lc​x. The net torque is
τ=g(ml−mc​lc​)y​.
(34)
A horizontal axle can remain fixed when this vanishes, giving
lc​=mc​ml​on the opposite side of the pivot.​
(35)
For the fixed spin direction already found, counterbalanced gyroscope precession has signed rate
Ωz​=−Iωg(ml−mc​lc​)​.
(36)
Moving the counterweight outward from the pivot first slows the clockwise motion, eliminates its driving torque at balance, and then reverses the steady motion to counterclockwise. A counterweight on the disk's side would add to the gravitational torque. These statements neglect axial spin moment of inertia of the counterweight and assume the stated horizontal motion, without a separately imposed precession at balance.
