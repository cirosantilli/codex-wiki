Axial moment of inertia. In the original diagram R1​ is the outer radius and R2​ the inner radius. For uniform mass density ρ, a coaxial annulus of thickness dr has dm=2πρLrdr. Therefore the axial moment of inertia of a hollow cylinder is
I=∫R2​R1​​r2dm=2πρL∫R2​R1​​r3dr=2πρL​(R14​−R24​).
(44)
Since M=πρL(R12​−R22​), factorizing the fourth-power difference gives
I=21​M(R12​+R22​).​
(45)
For a solid cylinder R2​→0, giving I=MR12​/2. For a thin cylindrical shell with fixed mass and R1​,R2​→R, I=MR2. The latter limit agrees with every mass element being at radius R.
Undamped rolling oscillations. Let x be axle displacement from the spring equilibrium. Rolling without slipping imposes ∣ωroll​∣=∣x˙∣/R. The sum of translational and rotational kinetic energy is
T=21​mx˙2+21​IR2x˙2​=21​(m+R2I​)x˙2.
(46)
The potential energy is U=kx2/2. Differentiating T+U in the absence of damping, or applying the force and torque equations below, gives
(m+R2I​)x¨+kx=0.
(47)
For the thin shell I=mR2, the effective inertia is 2m, and the angular frequency of the simple harmonic motion is
ω0​=2mk​​.​
(48)
Static contact friction supplies the required torque but does no work when the contact point is instantaneously stationary.
Damped force balance. Take rightward force as positive and counterclockwise rotation as positive. Write f for the signed static friction at the bottom contact. Then
mx¨=−kx−bx˙+f,Iα=fR,α=−Rx¨​.
(49)
Eliminate f=−Ix¨/R2 and use I=mR2:
2mx¨+bx˙+kx=0,x¨+Bx˙+Cx=0,B=2mb​,C=2mk​.​
(50)
Here b has units kgs−1, so B has units s−1 and C units s−2. The stated drag force is interpreted as acting through the axle, with no additional viscous torque. An oil bath could also exert such a torque physically, but its magnitude is not specified by this force-only model.
Light damping. Substitution of z=Ae−pt into the differential equation gives p2−Bp+C=0. In the underdamped regime B2<4C,
p=2B​±iC−4B2​​=4mb​±iωd​,ωd​=2mk​−16m2b2​​.
(51)
Taking real linear combinations gives the general damped harmonic oscillator displacement
x(t)=e−bt/(4m)[A1​cos(ωd​t)+A2​sin(ωd​t)].​
(52)
The constants are fixed by the initial displacement and velocity. For example, release from rest at x0​ gives A1​=x0​ and A2​=bx0​/(4mωd​). Light damping means b2≪8mk, in which case ωd​≃ω0​ and the envelope decays slowly compared with an oscillation.
