Complex impedance. In sinusoidal steady state write physical voltage and electric current as real parts of Veiωt and Ieiωt. The electrical impedance Z=V/I is a ratio of phasors; its magnitude gives the voltage-to-current amplitude ratio and its argument gives their phase difference. It is measured in ohms.
For an ideal capacitor, q=Cv and i=dq/dt=Cdv/dt. Consequently I=iωCV, giving
ZC​=iωC1​=−ωCi​.​
(53)
With this time convention the voltage lags the electric current by π/2.
Bridge output. The original diagram has R1​ and C1​ in parallel in the upper-left arm, and R2​ and C2​ in series in the lower-left arm. The point labelled C is connected to the upper supply node. Set the lower supply node to zero, so VC​=Vi​. Assuming the measured output draws negligible electric current, the two voltage dividers give
VA​=Vi​Z1​+Z2​Z2​​,VB​=Vi​R3​+R4​R4​​.
(54)
Their difference is therefore
Vi​VAB​​=Z1​+Z2​Z2​​−R3​+R4​R4​​.​
(55)
The reactive-arm electrical impedances are
Z1​=(R1​1​+iωC1​)−1,Z2​=R2​+iωC2​1​.
(56)
Balance. Setting VAB​=0 and cross-multiplying gives R3​Z2​=R4​Z1​. For this Wien bridge,
Z1​Z2​​=(R2​+iωC2​1​)(R1​1​+iωC1​)=R1​R2​​+C2​C1​​+i(ωR2​C1​−ωR1​C2​1​).
(57)
The right-hand side must be the real number R4​/R3​. Equating imaginary and real parts gives
ω=R1​R2​C1​C2​​1​,C2​C1​​=R3​R4​​−R1​R2​​.​
(58)
Positive capacitances require R4​/R3​>R2​/R1​. In the equal-component case R1​=R2​=R and C1​=C2​=C, this reduces to
ω=RC1​,R4​=2R3​.​
(59)
Band-pass output and phase. Preserve the requested voltage polarity: VAC​=VA​−VC​. Since VC​=Vi​,
Vi​VAC​​=Z1​+Z2​Z2​​−1=−Z1​+Z2​Z1​​.
(60)
Put u=ωRC>0. The equal-component ratio is Z2​/Z1​=2+i(u−u−1), so the Wien bridge band-pass response is
H(ω)=Vi​VAC​​=−3+i(u−u−1)1​=−1−u2+3iuiu​.​
(61)
At finite positive frequency, its imaginary part vanishes exactly when u−u−1=0. Thus
ωr​=RC1​,H(ωr​)=−31​.​
(62)
The magnitude ∣H∣=[9+(u−u−1)2]−1/2 is maximal there and tends to zero at both frequency extremes, as expected for a band-pass filter.
A convenient continuous, unwrapped wave phase is
ϕ(ω)=−π−arctan(3u−u−1​).​
(63)
It decreases from −π/2 as ω→0+, passes through −π at ωr​, and tends to −3π/2 as ω→∞. A principal-value plot instead jumps from −π to +π at resonance; both represent the same phasor. Reversing the output to VCA​ would shift the phase by π and make the resonant ratio +1/3, but that is a different output polarity from the one printed.
