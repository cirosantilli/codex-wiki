Faraday's law of induction relates an induced electromotive force to the rate of change of magnetic flux. Choose a positive orientation around a loop and its associated surface normal by the right-hand rule. For a stationary conducting loop,
ΦB​=∫S​B⋅dS,E=∮E⋅dl=−dtdΦB​​.​
(91)
Unlike an electrostatic field, the induced electric field generally has nonzero curl; the differential form is ∇×E=−∂B/∂t. For a moving circuit, magnetic flux transport for a moving circuit includes the magnetic Lorentz force on the charges:
E=∮(E+u×B)⋅dl=−dtdΦB​​,
(92)
where u is the local wire velocity. Thus changing the field or moving a conductor can produce induction.
The minus sign is Lenz's law: an induced electric current, when a closed conducting path permits it, produces a magnetic field opposing the change of linked flux. It need not oppose the original field itself. For example, increasing flux through a loop produces a current tending to reduce that increase; decreasing the same flux reverses the current. The opposition is consistent with conservation of energy: external mechanical or electrical work is needed to maintain a change against its inductive reaction.

(93)
The corresponding terminal voltage drop, with the passive sign convention, is VL​=LdI/dt. It opposes changes in electric current; it does not require the current itself to vanish.
