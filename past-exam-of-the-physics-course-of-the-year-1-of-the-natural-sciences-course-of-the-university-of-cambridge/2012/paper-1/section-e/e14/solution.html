For steady currents in vacuum, Ampère's law is ∮B⋅dl=μ0​Ienclosed​. A rectangular loop with one long side inside an ideal long solenoid and the other outside gives Bℓ=μ0​(nℓ)I, because the exterior field is negligible and the perpendicular sides contribute zero. Thus B=μ0​nI, approximately uniform away from the ends.
Write the electron electric charge as qe​=−e, with e>0. The Lorentz force is F=−ev×B; it does no work. For perpendicular motion, mv2/rL​=evB gives
ωc​=meB​,rL​=eBmv​.​
(47)
Here ωc​ is the positive angular speed; the rotation sense is determined by the negative electron electric charge.
The parallel velocity is unaffected, while the perpendicular component rotates. Hence the path is a helix with v∥​=vcosθ and rL​=mvsinθ/(eB). For on-axis entry into a solenoidal tube, the transverse circle passes through that axis: its guiding centre is displaced by rL​, and its maximum distance from the tube axis is 2rL​, not rL​.
Using the nonrelativistic approximation appropriate to 1keV,
v=m2E0​​​=1.876×107ms−1,t=vcos3∘l​=4.27ns.​
(48)
The cyclotron period is 2π/ωc​=0.893ns, so the flight lasts 4.783 revolutions. The electron therefore crosses the axis again four times before leaving. At 3∘, 2rL​=0.279mm<a. Wall clearance requires 2mvsinθ/(eB)≤a, giving
θm​=sin−1(2mveBa​)=5.38∘.​
(49)
The transit is longer than a full orbit, so the maximum transverse excursion really is sampled before exit.
For the solenoidal time-of-flight energy window, the prescribed time fixes v∥​=l/t0​=v0​=2E0​/m​, while 0≤v⊥​≤eBa/(2m). Therefore
Emin​=E0​=1.000keV,Emax​=E0​+8me2B2a2​=1.0088keV.​
(50)
These are total kinetic energies. The upper value is the ideal grazing limit; if touching the wall counts as rejection, it is a supremum approached from below.
