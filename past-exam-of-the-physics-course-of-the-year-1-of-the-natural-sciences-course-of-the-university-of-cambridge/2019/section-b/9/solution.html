Experimental evidence. Tests of special relativity check linked predictions about light propagation, clocks and four-momentum. Several independent kinds of experiment support those predictions.
  • In the Michelson-Morley experiment, light follows perpendicular paths in an interferometer. Rotating the apparatus would change their round-trip travel-time difference in a classical stationary-aether model. The expected orientation-dependent interference fringe shift was absent at the predicted scale, consistent with isotropy of the round-trip speed of light in special relativity. This experiment alone does not distinguish special relativity from every alternative theory.[ref]
  • The Fizeau experiment compares light propagation with and against flowing water using an interference fringe displacement. Neglecting optical dispersion, the velocity-addition formula predicts
vlab​=1+u/(nc)c/n+u​≃nc​+u(1−n21​).
(58)
The observed partial-drag coefficient agrees with this first-order prediction rather than simple Galilean addition of u. It tests a different consequence of the Lorentz transformation from a particle-lifetime measurement.[ref]
  • A controlled muon storage-ring time-dilation experiment measured circulating muons at γ=29.33. Its measured mean lifetimes were about 64.4μs, compared with about 2.2μs at rest, consistent with τlab​=γτ0​. This tests time dilation with accelerator-selected particles.[ref]
Atmospheric muons and the two frame descriptions. Atmospheric cosmic rays create unstable particles whose decay chains produce muons. A stopped-muon experiment measures the time between a stopping signal and the subsequent decay electron, obtaining the proper mean lifetime τ0​≃2.2μs. The associated light-travel distance is only cτ0​≃660m, yet substantial numbers of energetic atmospheric muons reach detectors after travelling several kilometres.
For an ideal selected cohort produced height h above a detector and moving downward at constant speed v, the flight time in the terrestrial frame is h/v. Time dilation predicts a laboratory mean lifetime γτ0​, hence survival fraction
N(h)N(h→0)​=exp(−γvτ0​h​),γ=1−v2/c2​1​.​
(59)
Without time dilation, the predicted exponent would be −h/(vτ0​) and the surviving population much smaller. Altitude comparisons constitute a cosmic-ray muon time-dilation experiment: select comparable momenta or energies and account for detector acceptance, atmospheric energy loss and backgrounds before interpreting the count ratio.
For illustration, not as a claimed measurement, take h=10km and v=0.998c. Then γ=15.82, the laboratory flight time is 33.4μs, and the predicted surviving fraction is about 0.38. Omitting time dilation would give only about 2.5×10−7 on the same idealized constant-speed model.
In the muon rest frame, its decay still follows e−Δτ/τ0​. The atmosphere and ground move upward. Their separation, measured simultaneously in that frame, is shortened by length contraction:
h′=γh​,Δτ=vh′​=γvh​.
(60)
The same survival fraction follows. The terrestrial description uses a longer-lived moving particle; the muon-frame description uses a shorter atmospheric flight path. They describe the same production and detection events. This consistency illustrates both time dilation and length contraction, while the survival measurement is not two independent experimental tests merely because it can be described in two frames.
Energy-momentum invariant. For a particle with rest mass m,
E2−p2c2=m2c4.​
(61)
Since E=mc2+K, substitution and cancellation of m2c4 give
(mc2+K)2−p2c2=m2c4,K2+2mKc2=p2c2.​

This relativistic energy-momentum relation reduces to K≃p2/(2m) when K≪mc2.
Laboratory beam energies. If both final muons are at rest in the laboratory, their total momentum is zero and their total energy is 2mμ​c2. Four-momentum conservation therefore requires equal and opposite incoming momenta. Because the electron and Positron have equal rest masses, they then have equal total energies:
E−​=E+​=mμ​c2.
(63)
Subtract each particle's own rest energy:
K−​=K+​=(mμ​−me​)c2.​
(64)
Using the usual handbook rest energies gives approximately 105.1MeV for each beam. These are the ideal kinematic threshold values. Just above threshold, the pair has small, equal and opposite momenta in this laboratory frame.
Positron momentum. Apply the relativistic energy-momentum relation to E+​=mμ​c2 and use its stipulated negative-x direction:
px2​c2=mμ2​c4−me2​c4,px​=−cmμ2​−me2​​.​
(65)
The electron has the opposite momentum, pe,x​=+p0​ with p0​=cmμ2​−me2​​.
Boost to the electron rest frame. The electron's velocity is v=pe,x​c2/E−​=p0​/mμ​>0. Thus S′ moves in the positive-x direction, and its Lorentz factor satisfies
γ=me​c2E−​​=me​mμ​​.
(66)
