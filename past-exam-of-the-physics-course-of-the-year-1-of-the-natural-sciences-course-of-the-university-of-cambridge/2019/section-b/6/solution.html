Definitions. The torque of a force F about an origin is τ=r×F, where r runs from that origin to the force's point of application. Its component about a fixed axis changes angular momentum about that axis. The moment of inertia about that axis is
I=∫s2dm,​
(20)
where s is perpendicular distance to the axis. It measures resistance to angular acceleration; for fixed-axis rigid rotation, τnet​=Iα. The units are Nm for torque and kgm2 for moment of inertia.
Mass and axial inertia. Slice the shaft perpendicular to its common axis. A slice of radius r(z) has mass dm=ρπr(z)2dz and moment of inertia dI=21​r(z)2dm, using the moment of inertia of a uniform disc. Thus the axial moment of inertia of a solid of revolution uses the second and fourth powers of its radius:
M=ρπ∫r(z)2dz,I=2ρπ​∫r(z)4dz.
(21)
For the tapered section, take z from its narrow end toward its wide end, so r(z)=a(1+z/l) for 0≤z≤l. Its contributions are
Mtaper​=ρπa2l∫01​(1+u)2du=37​ρπa2l,Itaper​=2ρπa4l​∫01​(1+u)4du=1031​ρπa4l.
(22)
The two cylinders contribute 4ρπa2l and ρπa2l to mass, and 8ρπa4l and 21​ρπa4l to moment of inertia. Adding the three sections gives
M=(4+37​+1)ρπa2l=322​ρπa2l,​
(23)
I=(8+1031​+21​)ρπa4l=558​ρπa4l,I=22/358/5​Ma2=5587​Ma2.​
(24)
All slice rotation axes coincide with the shaft axis, so the moments add directly.
Frictional torque. The normal load on the narrow circular end is Mg. With the stipulated uniform pressure, pN​=Mg/(πa2). A contact annulus of radius s and width ds has area dA=2πsds, normal force pN​dA and tangential kinetic friction dF=μpN​dA. Multiplying by its lever arm s and integrating gives the uniform-pressure friction torque on a circular contact:
Gf​=∫0a​sμπa2Mg​2πsds=a22μMg​3a3​,Gf​=32Mμga​.​
(25)
Here Gf​ is the positive magnitude; its torque opposes the angular velocity.
Steady speed and stopping rotations. Mechanical power in rotation is P=τω. At steady angular velocity, the driving and frictional torques balance, so constant-power rotation against dry friction gives
ω∗​=Gf​P​=2Mμga3P​.​
(26)
Using g=9.81ms−2 and a=0.100m,
I=0.0316364kgm2,Gf​=0.3924Nm,ω∗​=25.4842rads−1≃25.5rads−1.​
(27)
Once the motor is removed, the speed-independent kinetic friction gives constant angular acceleration −Gf​/I. Equivalently, the initial rotational kinetic energy is dissipated as frictional work. The stopping angle under constant resisting torque is therefore
Gf​Δϕ=21​Iω∗2​,Δϕ=2Gf​Iω∗2​​=26.1800rad.
(28)
The total angular displacement corresponds to
N=2πΔϕ​=4πGf3​IP2​=4.16668 revolutions.
(29)
Hence the shaft completes
⌊N⌋=4 complete rotations,​
(30)
and then another 0.16668 of a rotation. Its stopping time is Iω∗​/Gf​=2.05461s. Using the rounded value g=9.8ms−2 changes the fractional rotation slightly but leaves four complete rotations.
