Cell-count formula for Euler characteristic (χ(X)=∑i=0d​(−1)ici​=∑i=0d​(−1)ibi​)

OurBigBook.com Words: 118
Let X be a finite CW complex, with ci​ topological cells of dimension i and none above d. Its Euler characteristic is the displayed alternating sum. For a finite simplicial complex, ci​ counts its i-dimensional simplexes, each counted once rather than once for each containing simplex. In dimension two this becomes V−E+F. A topological cell's interior is counted; identified parts of its boundary are counted in their own lower-dimensional topological cells.[ref]
The cellular homology theorem identifies cellular homology with singular homology, so the count equals the alternating rational Betti numbers. These are the free ranks of integral homology; torsion subgroups do not contribute to them. Over any field, the alternating homology dimensions still give the same Euler characteristic, although individual Betti numbers can depend on the field.
