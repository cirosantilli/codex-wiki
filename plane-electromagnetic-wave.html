Plane electromagnetic wave (B=k×E/c)

 Home Physics Branches of physics Electromagnetism Electromagnetic wave
OurBigBook.com Words: 5k Articles: 50
A monochromatic vacuum plane electromagnetic wave has phase k⋅r−ωt, where k is the wavevector, ω>0 the angular frequency, and ω=c∣k∣. Its field amplitudes obey k⋅E0​=k⋅B0​=0 and B0​=k×E0​/ω.
For the linear polarization and orientation in the visualization, choose a right-handed Cartesian coordinate system and put
E(z,t)=E0​cos(kz−ωt)x,B(z,t)=cE0​​cos(kz−ωt)y​,k>0,ω=ck.
(21)
Constant phase means z=ct+constant, so propagation is along +z. The cross product x×y​=z fixes the positive Poynting vector. Both fields reverse together every half-period and are in phase. Reversing the propagation direction with the same electric orientation reverses the magnetic orientation.
These fields are spatially uniform across every transverse plane. An infinite plane electromagnetic wave is an idealization with infinite total electromagnetic energy; it is useful as a local approximation and as a building block for superposition. For frequency f=100MHz, ω=2πf and wavelength λ=c/f≃3.00m, independently of E0​.
