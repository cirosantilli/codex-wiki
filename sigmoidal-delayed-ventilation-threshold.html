For Kp˙​=M−pV(p(t−τ)) and a positive steady state, linearization gives z˙=−Az−Bz(t−τ), where A=V(p∗)/K and B=p∗V′(p∗)/K. Splitting the characteristic equation of a delay differential equation at an imaginary root yields ω2=B2−A2 and the displayed first critical delay. The root crosses into the unstable half-plane with real derivative ω2/[(1+Aτc​)2+ω2τc2​]>0. If A≪B, the period near onset is approximately 4τc​. For a Hill equation response, B/A=nθn/(θn+(p∗)n) identifies whether delayed gain can exceed instantaneous damping.
A longer circulation time increases the delay between gas exchange and its sensed feedback. The American Thoracic Society's documented case relates a change in breathing pattern during one night to a longer estimated circulation time; the simple scalar model isolates a possible feedback mechanism rather than representing every determinant of that observation.[ref]
