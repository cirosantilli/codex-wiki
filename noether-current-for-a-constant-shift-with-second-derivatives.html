Noether current for a constant shift with second derivatives (jμ=Lϕ,μ​​−∂ν​Lϕ,μν​​)

 Home Physics Branches of physics Quantum field theory Classical field theory Noether current
OurBigBook.com Words: 81
If a Lagrangian density depending on first and second derivatives has no undifferentiated field, the scalar-field shift symmetry δϕ=ϵ gives the displayed Noether current. Its divergence is minus the Euler-Lagrange field equation with second derivatives, so it is conserved on shell. The spatial integral of j0 is constant only if the boundary current vanishes. For L=ρϕt2​/2+αϕx2​/2−βϕxx2​/2, it gives j0=ρϕt​, jx=αϕx​+βϕxxx​. Fixed transverse endpoint positions break the global shift symmetry of the boundary problem; support forces can then change the total transverse momentum even though the bulk continuity equation still holds.
