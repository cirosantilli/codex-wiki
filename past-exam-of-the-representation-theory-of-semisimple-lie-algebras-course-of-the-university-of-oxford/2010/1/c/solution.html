Let V=0 be finite-dimensional. Choose an H-eigenvector and apply e repeatedly until the last nonzero vector v in that chain. Such a last vector exists because e raises the H-eigenvalue by two and H has only finitely many eigenvalues. Then ev=0 and Hv=lv for some l.
The nonzero f-chain cannot continue indefinitely, since its weights are distinct. Part (a), with its zero endpoint included, gives l∈Z≥0​ and the basis v,fv,…,flv. The raising formula in part (a) shows its span is a submodule isomorphic to Al​.
To prove Al​ irreducible, a nonzero submodule stable under diagonal H contains a weight vector: polynomial interpolation in the distinct eigenvalues projects onto individual representation weight lines. Repeated raising reaches v0​, and repeated lowering then reaches every vk​. Thus the submodule is all of Al​. Distinct l give different dimensions l+1, so the modules are nonisomorphic. Every finite-dimensional irreducible module contains one of these nonzero simple submodules and hence equals it. Therefore
{Al​:l≥0} is the complete list of finite-dimensional simple modules.​
