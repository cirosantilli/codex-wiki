Use the intended finite-dimensional irreducible highest weight module Aϕ, with dominant integral highest weight ϕ. Its highest weight multiplicity is one. An arbitrary direct sum having that same largest representation weight would not in general satisfy this formula. Set f=πAϕ​ and use the corrected denominator D of part (a).
Restriction to each simple root sl2​ decomposes into symmetric finite representation weight strings, so f is Weyl-invariant. Consequently Df is Weyl-antisymmetric. We prove that no lower Weyl alternants occur, rather than assuming the desired character formula.
First establish the denominator identity itself. The finite sum D has support in ρ−Q+​ and coefficient one at ρ. Antisymmetry kills all singular representation weight coefficients, since a reflection fixing the representation weight reverses their sign. Every remaining Weyl orbit has a unique regular dominant representative η. If it occurs in D, then η≤ρ. Since η is integral and regular dominant, η−ρ is dominant integral; it also belongs to −Q+​.
