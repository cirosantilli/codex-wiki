Multinomial distribution (Mult(n;p1​,…,pk​))

 ... Mathematics Area of mathematics Probability and statistics Probability theory Probability distribution Discrete probability distribution
OurBigBook.com Words: 1k Articles: 10
A multinomial distribution describes category counts from n independent trials, each choosing exactly one of k categories with the same probabilities p1​,…,pk​. Require n≥0 to be an integer, pi​≥0, and ∑i​pi​=1. If Ni​ counts the trials in category i, then ∑i​Ni​=n and
P(N1​=n1​,…,Nk​=nk​)=n1​!⋯nk​!n!​∏i=1k​pini​​,ni​≥0,∑i​ni​=n.
(33)
The ni​ are integers, and the mass is zero for other count patterns. A category with pi​=0 has count zero almost surely, with 00=1 in this formula. For a specified ordered trial sequence, independence gives the product of numbers ∏i​pini​​; the multinomial coefficient counts the different sequences with that same count pattern.
For four trials with category probabilities (1/2,3/10,1/5), the count pattern (2,1,1) can occur in 4!/(2!1!1!)=12 orders, each of probability (1/2)2(3/10)(1/5)=0.015. Thus its total probability is 12(0.015)=0.18. This is a constructed independent-trial example, distinct from drawing four objects without replacement from a ten-object population of composition (5,3,2), where the multivariate hypergeometric distribution gives 2/7.
The diagram shows all fifteen possible count patterns. Coordinates (N1​,N2​) determine N3​=4−N1​−N2​; the triangle is a constraint on integer counts, not a continuous probability density. The mode is (N1​,N2​,N3​)=(2,1,1), where the probability mass function is largest, but the expected values (2,1.2,0.8) are not a possible count pattern.
Each single count has a binomial distribution, because recording category i versus all other categories reduces every trial to a Bernoulli trial. Consequently,
ENi​=npi​,Var(Ni​)=npi​(1−pi​),Cov(Ni​,Nj​)=−npi​pj​(i=j).
(34)
These statements about the joint count model are summarized in Aditya Guntuboyina's lecture notes.[ref]
Independent trials do not make the resulting counts independent random variables. Within one trial the indicator random variables for two distinct categories cannot both equal one, so their product is zero and their covariance is −pi​pj​. Summing over independent trials gives −npi​pj​. In the four-trial example, the variances are (1,0.84,0.64) and the pairwise covariances are −0.6,−0.4,−0.24. Knowing that all four trials were in the first category fixes the other two counts at zero.
The attraction is that one formula describes any fixed-total list of category counts while preserving the constraint that they add up to n. It underlies multinomial likelihood calculations and uniform balls-in-bins allocation. The multinomial aggregation rule lets us merge categories without leaving this family of probability distributions. Allowing independent category counts with an unfixed total instead leads to a different model, related through Poisson-multinomial conditioning.
