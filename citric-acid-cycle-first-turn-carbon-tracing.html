Citric acid cycle first-turn carbon tracing

 Home Chemistry Biochemistry Metabolic pathway Citric acid cycle
OurBigBook.com Words: 314
Net stoichiometry does not identify individual chemical atoms. In the ordinary forward citric acid cycle, neither entering acetyl group carbon is released in that same first turn: both released carbon dioxide carbon chemical atoms came from the starting oxaloacetate. The entering carbon remains in regenerated intermediates.[ref]
Consider a chosen ideal first-turn experiment, with negligible natural carbon-13 abundance and no exchange, side pathways, repeated turns or isotope effects. Supply 10μmol of initially unlabelled oxaloacetate and 10μmol of acetyl-CoA, of which 6μmol has both acetyl group carbon positions labelled with carbon-13. The other 4μmol has neither position labelled. The label refers only to the acetyl group, not to coenzyme A.
The input contains 2×6=12μmol of labelled carbon chemical atoms. The 10μmol of citrate initially formed contains 60μmol of carbon chemical atoms, so its labelled-carbon fraction is 12/60=20%. After exactly one forward traversal per starting oxaloacetate, the 20μmol of released carbon dioxide has zero label, while the regenerated 10μmol of oxaloacetate contains all 12μmol of labelled carbon chemical atoms among 40μmol total: 30%.
The conservation check is 60=20+40 for total carbon and 12=0+12 for label. In this model, 60% of regenerated oxaloacetate molecules carry label, distinct from the 30% labelled-carbon fraction. The regenerated oxaloacetate is the same chemical species and the same amount of substance as the initial acceptor pool, but its chemical atom identities have changed. Saying that the cycle oxidizes an acetyl group to two carbon dioxide is a net ledger, not a claim that those exact two incoming chemical atoms immediately leave. Later-turn label release requires positional tracking and mixing assumptions; the first-turn balance alone does not determine it.
