Roth density-increment step

 Home Mathematics Area of mathematics Combinatorics Additive combinatorics Density increment
OurBigBook.com Words: 185
There is an absolute constant c>0 such that, for every δ>0 and all sufficiently large N, a subset A⊆[N] whose density of a finite subset is δ and which has no nonconstant three-term arithmetic progression has an arithmetic progression P⊆[N] satisfying
∣P∣≥η(δ)N​,∣P∣∣A∩P∣​≥δ+cδ2.
(29)
The Fourier transform of the balanced function f=1A​−δ1[N]​ has a coefficient of magnitude at least c0​δ2N. Indeed, the trilinear count
Λ(g1​,g2​,g3​)=∑x+z=2y​g1​(x)g2​(y)g3​(z)
(30)
satisfies Λ(1A​,1A​,1A​)=∣A∣, whereas Λ(1[N]​,1[N]​,1[N]​)≫N2. Expanding 1A​=δ1[N]​+f and using the Parseval identity and the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality to bound every error term forces ∥f​∥∞​≫δ2N once N is sufficiently large in terms of δ.
Choose θ at which this large coefficient occurs. The Dirichlet approximation theorem gives 1≤d≤N​ with ∥dθ∥R/Z​≤N−1/2. Partition [N] into progressions of common difference d and lengths between ηN​ and 2ηN​, where η>0 is a sufficiently small multiple of δ2. The linear phase e(θx) varies by O(η) on each cell. The large Fourier coefficient then gives
∑P​​∑x∈P​f(x)​≫δ2N.
(31)
Since ∑x∈[N]​f(x)=0, the total positive discrepancy is half the total absolute discrepancy, so at least one cell has average of f at least cδ2. This is the required density increment.
