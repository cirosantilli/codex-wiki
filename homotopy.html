Homotopy (H:X×[0,1]→Y)

 Home Mathematics Area of mathematics Geometry and topology Algebraic topology
OurBigBook.com Words: 6k Articles: 136
A homotopy between continuous maps f,g:X→Y is a continuous map H:X×[0,1]→Y satisfying H(x,0)=f(x) and H(x,1)=g(x). Think of t as time: continuity is required jointly in position and time. The target Y specifies where every intermediate point is allowed to go. See Hatcher, Algebraic Topology, Chapter 0, pp. 3–4.
For the filled disk D2={x∈R2:∥x∥≤1}, the formula
H(x,t)=(1−t)x
(1)
is a contraction of a topological space: it starts at the identity map and ends at the constant map to zero. The Euclidean norm obeys ∥H(x,t)∥=(1−t)∥x∥≤1, so the formula never leaves the disk. For x=(3/5,4/5), the midpoint of the deformation is (3/10,2/5), whose Euclidean norm is 1/2.
On the circle S1={x:∥x∥=1}, the same formula fails: at t=1/2 every image has Euclidean norm 1/2, so lies outside S1. This checks one failed candidate; the nonzero winding number of a once-around loop proves that no contraction within the circle exists. The distinction is between allowing the filling and requiring the deformation to stay on its boundary.

Homotopy category

Words: 312 Articles: 5
Homotopy OurBigBook.com
The homotopy category of based CW complexes has spaces as objects and based homotopy classes as morphisms. Composition is well-defined because composition with continuous maps preserves homotopies. A based homotopy equivalence becomes an isomorphism. Rational localization instead inverts rational homotopy equivalences, producing a category represented by rational spaces. More general model categories have analogous homotopy categories obtained by inverting their weak equivalences.

Homotopy pushout

Words: 75
Homotopy category OurBigBook.com
For maps A→X and A→Y, the double mapping cylinder X⊔(A×I)⊔Y, with each endpoint attached by its given map, models the homotopy pushout. A map out of this model is a pair of maps on X and Y together with a homotopy between their restrictions to A. This is why compatible homotopy classes extend over a homotopy pushout. For a CW pair, the inclusion is a cofibration and the ordinary pushout gives the same homotopy type.

H-space (m:Y×Y→Y)

Words: 82 Articles: 1
Homotopy category OurBigBook.com
A based topological space with a continuous map m whose left and right restrictions at the basepoint are homotopic to the identity. Associativity and inverses up to homotopy are additional conditions, not part of the bare definition.

H-group

Words: 50
H-space OurBigBook.com
An H-space whose multiplication is homotopy associative and admits a homotopy inverse. These operations give [X,Y]∗​ a group structure for every based CW complex X. A loop space is the basic example: concatenate paths, use reversal for inversion, and use reparametrization for homotopy associativity. A bare H-space should not automatically be treated as a group object.

Topological half-exact functor

Words: 93 Articles: 1
Homotopy category OurBigBook.com
For a contravariant pointed functor on based CW homotopy types, half-exactness on a cofibration means that F(X/A)→F(X)→F(A) is exact at F(X): the image is the inverse image of the distinguished point. This does not demand injectivity or surjectivity at the ends. Set-valued exactness and group-valued exactness must be distinguished.

Brown's representability theorem

Words: 44
Topological half-exact functor OurBigBook.com
A reduced contravariant homotopy functor on based CW complexes satisfying the wedge-to-product axiom and the Mayer-Vietoris weak-pullback gluing axiom is represented by [X,Y]∗​ for a CW complex Y. Half-exactness alone is not the full hypothesis. A reduced generalized cohomology theory supplies these axioms; suspension compatibility assembles its representing spaces into an Omega-spectrum.

Homotopy inverse limit of a tower (holimn​Xn​)

Words: 76
Homotopy OurBigBook.com
For a sequence of maps Xn​→Xn−1​, its homotopy inverse limit records points xn​ and paths from the image of xn​ to xn−1​, with the usual homotopy-coherent compatibility. A fibrant replacement tower computes it by an ordinary inverse limit. For a tower whose homotopy groups are eventually constant in each degree, the limit has those stable groups: the Milnor inverse-limit exact sequence has zero derived-limit term. In particular X→holimPn​X is a weak equivalence for the Postnikov tower of a connected CW complex.

Maps to a sphere above the dimension of a compact smooth manifold are null-homotopic (dimX<q,q≥1⟹[X,Sq]={∗})

Words: 62
Homotopy OurBigBook.com
Approximate a continuous sphere-valued map by smooth real coordinate functions, then normalize. A sufficiently close approximation is homotopic to the original map by normalized straight interpolation. Sard theorem implies the smooth map misses a point when its source dimension is smaller than the sphere dimension. The punctured sphere is contractible, so the map is null-homotopic, even if the source is disconnected.

Rational homotopy theory (π∗​(X)⊗Q)

Words: 935 Articles: 15
Homotopy OurBigBook.com
Study homotopy after killing torsion, especially for simply connected spaces of finite type. The rational Whitehead theorem detects rational homotopy equivalences by rational homology, while Sullivan minimal models encode rational homotopy generators and cohomological relations.

Nilpotent space

Words: 65
Rational homotopy theory OurBigBook.com
A connected space is nilpotent when its fundamental group is nilpotent and its action on each higher homotopy group is nilpotent: the module has a finite filtration whose successive quotients have trivial group action. Simply connected spaces satisfy these conditions automatically. They are the natural setting for localization and rationalization, since fibrewise coefficient localization is compatible with the successive untwisted stages of such a filtration.

Rational space

Words: 200 Articles: 3
Rational homotopy theory OurBigBook.com
In the simply connected homotopy category, a rational space is a simply connected CW complex whose positive homotopy groups are vector spaces over Q. Multiplication by every nonzero integer on these groups is invertible. More generally one uses nilpotent spaces with localized fundamental group and higher homotopy groups; the simply connected version avoids local-coefficient twisting.

Rationalization of a topological space (r:X→XQ​)

Words: 161 Articles: 2
Rational space OurBigBook.com
For simply connected X, rationalization of a topological space is a map r:X→XQ​ to a rational space inducing πn​(XQ​)=πn​(X)⊗Q. It is also a rational homology equivalence. Its universal property is the bijection r∗:[XQ​,Z]→[X,Z] for every rational space Z. A Postnikov tower constructs it by replacing each fibre group with its rational tensor product and transporting the Postnikov invariant through the resulting cohomology isomorphism, then taking the homotopy inverse limit.
Rational homotopy equivalence
Words: 106 Articles: 1
Rationalization of a topological space OurBigBook.com
A map between simply connected spaces is a rational homotopy equivalence when it induces isomorphisms πn​(X)⊗Q→πn​(Y)⊗Q in every degree. Equivalently it induces an equivalence of their rationalizations, or a rational homology isomorphism by the rational Whitehead theorem. Spaces have the same rational homotopy type when they are connected by a zigzag of such maps. This is distinct from rational homotopy equivalence of algebraic cycles.
Rational homotopy type
Words: 55
Rational homotopy equivalence OurBigBook.com
The rational homotopy type of a simply connected space is its equivalence class under zigzags of rational homotopy equivalences, or equivalently the homotopy type of its rationalization of a topological space. For the usual finite-type spaces, it is encoded contravariantly by the isomorphism class of the Sullivan minimal model. Isomorphic cohomology rings determine this type only when additional information, such as formality of a topological space, makes the cochain algebra equivalent to the cohomology algebra.

Sullivan minimal model ((ΛV,d))

Words: 646 Articles: 9
Rational homotopy theory OurBigBook.com
A free graded-commutative rational differential graded algebra with decomposable differentials and a quasi-isomorphism to the rational polynomial forms of a space. For simply connected finite-type spaces, Vi is dual to πi​(X)⊗Q. A proposed finite model must have its cohomology and representing quasi-isomorphism checked; matching a list of relations alone is insufficient.

Relative Sullivan algebra

Words: 76
Sullivan minimal model OurBigBook.com
A relative Sullivan algebra is an inclusion (A,d)→(A⊗ΛW,D) with a well-ordered homogeneous basis of W such that the differential of a generator lies in the algebra on A and earlier generators. It need not be minimal: a differential can have linear terms from earlier generators. Such extensions supply cofibrant replacements for commutative differential graded algebras. A relative acyclic extension of a sphere model represents its path-space fibration and computes a homotopy-fibre model after base change.

Sullivan fibre-model theorem

Words: 75
Sullivan minimal model OurBigBook.com
For a map f:E→B with simply connected base and the usual finite-type hypotheses, choose a relative Sullivan algebra M(B)→M(B)⊗ΛW modelling the path-space fibration, with acyclic total algebra. Tensor it over M(B) with a model of E via f∗. The resulting algebra models the homotopy fibre. Relative Sullivan extensions are cofibrant replacements, so this tensor product computes the derived algebraic pushout, contravariantly corresponding to the topological homotopy pullback. Linear differential pairs must be cancelled before reading rational homotopy groups from generators.

Sullivan minimal-model classification

Words: 52
Sullivan minimal model OurBigBook.com
For simply connected rational spaces of finite type, isomorphic minimal Sullivan models are equivalent to the same rational homotopy type. A quasi-isomorphism between minimal models is an isomorphism, so a zigzag of cochain quasi-isomorphisms produces the same minimal model. Existence and uniqueness come from adjoining closed generators to represent missing cohomology and generators with prescribed decomposable differential to kill kernel classes, degree by degree.

Rational polynomial differential forms (APL​(X))

Words: 70
Sullivan minimal model OurBigBook.com
For the standard n-simplex use Ωn​=Q[t0​,…,tn​,dt0​,…,dtn​]/(∑ti​−1,∑dti​), with ∣ti​∣=0, ∣dti​∣=1, and d(ti​)=dti​. Compatible families of these forms on the simplices of a simplicial model of X form the commutative differential graded algebra APL​(X). Its cohomology is H∗(X;Q): polynomial contraction proves the simplex Poincaré lemma, and extension across faces gives the simplicial de Rham comparison. The contravariant functor takes simplicial pushouts to pullbacks of algebras.

Minimal model of the fibre of the projective-space collapse map

Words: 71
Sullivan minimal model OurBigBook.com
For a degree-one map f:CPn→S2n with n≥2, a minimal model of its homotopy fibre is (Λ(x2​,a2n−1​,z2n+1​,b4n−2​),d) with dx=dz=0, da=xn, db=xn−1z. Adjoin suspended sphere generators to the projective-space model to model the path pullback, then put z=y−xa; the differential becomes the displayed decomposable one. The rational homotopy groups are one-dimensional in those four generator degrees and zero otherwise. At n=1 the map is a rational homotopy equivalence and the minimal fibre model is Q.

Rational model of a collapsed complex projective subspace

Words: 77
Sullivan minimal model OurBigBook.com
The quotient CP5/CP2 is five-connected and has cohomology only in degrees 0,6,8,10, with positive products zero. Its minimal model therefore starts with closed a6​,b8​,c10​; no decomposable differential can occur below degree twelve. Sending these three generators to the cohomology basis and all later generators to zero is a chain map and a quasi-isomorphism. Thus its full minimal model is the same as that of S6∨S8∨S10. It starts with generators u11​,v13​,w15​,t15​ satisfying du=a2,dv=ab,dw=ac,dt=b2, followed by generators killing remaining products and syzygies.

Minimal model of a wedge of simply connected spheres

Words: 86
Sullivan minimal model OurBigBook.com
For a finite wedge sum of spheres of dimensions ni​≥2, let L=L(ei​) with ∣ei​∣=ni​−1 and zero differential. Its Chevalley–Eilenberg cochains of a graded Lie algebra are a minimal commutative differential graded algebra: one generator dual to each Lie basis word has degree one more than that word, and its differential is dual to the Lie bracket. Its cohomology is Q⊕⨁i​Qani​​ with all positive products zero. One way to compute this is U(L)=T(ei​); the augmentation module has a length-one free resolution, so the corresponding Ext has only the unit and the dual generating classes.

Formal space

Words: 52
Sullivan minimal model OurBigBook.com
A space is formal over Q if its rational cochain commutative differential graded algebra is joined to (H∗(X;Q),0) by quasi-isomorphisms. Equivalently in the usual simply connected Sullivan-model setting, its minimal model has a quasi-isomorphism to its cohomology algebra with zero differential. This says its rational homotopy type can be recovered from the cohomology algebra; zero cup products alone do not imply formality of a topological space without an additional argument.

The based suspension collapses both ends and the basepoint cylinder of X×I to one basepoint. The loop space and reduced suspension are adjoint in based homotopy theory. For a connected based CW complex, the James reduced product models the loop space of its reduced suspension.

Suspension-loop adjunction ([ΣX,Y]≅[X,ΩY])

Words: 60
Reduced suspension OurBigBook.com
For based spaces there is a natural bijection [ΣX,Y]≅[X,ΩY]. A map on the suspension sends a point x and suspension coordinate t to a loop t↦f(x,t); the collapsed ends and basepoint give based loops. The reverse construction evaluates such loops. The two constructions commute with based homotopies. Applied to spheres it gives πn+1​Y≅πn​ΩY.

Free homotopy

Words: 58
Homotopy OurBigBook.com
A free homotopy between maps is an ordinary homotopy with no specified basepoint held fixed. Two loops at the same basepoint are freely homotopic precisely when their elements of the fundamental group are conjugate: a homotopy traces a connecting basepoint path, and the boundary of its parameter square gives conjugation. Conversely that connecting path can be slid around the loop to realize the conjugation.

Homology obstruction to carrying surface curves onto one another

Words: 59
Homotopy equivalence OurBigBook.com
A homotopy equivalence of a closed surface cannot carry a simple closed curve of nonzero integral homology class onto a contractible simple closed curve. Any map into the latter factors through a circle whose inclusion induces zero on first homology; the former class would be killed by an isomorphism. Such a carrying map can nevertheless exist: project a handle generator onto a circle and compose with the contractible embedded circle.

Separate homotopy inverses combine into a homotopy equivalence

If f:X→Y has maps g,h:Y→X with fg≃1Y​ and hf≃1X​, then h≃hfg≃g, so g is a two-sided homotopy inverse. More generally, if fg and hf are homotopy equivalences, composing g and h with their respective homotopy inverses supplies a right and a left homotopy inverse of f, reducing to the first case.

Deformation retraction (H:X×[0,1]→X)

Words: 191
Retract OurBigBook.com
A deformation retraction of X onto a subspace A is a homotopy H:X×[0,1]→X such that H(x,0)=x, H(x,1)=r(x)∈A, and H(a,t)=a for every a∈A and every t. Thus the retract is fixed pointwise throughout. With inclusion i:A↪X, the equations r∘i=idA​ and i∘r≃idX​ exhibit a homotopy equivalence. See Hatcher, Algebraic Topology, Chapter 0, pp. 2–3.
Take the closed annulus
A={x∈R2:1≤∥x∥≤2}.
(3)
An exact deformation retraction onto its inner circle is
H(x,t)=((1−t)+∥x∥t​)x,r(x)=∥x∥x​.
(4)
The denominator is nonzero everywhere on the annulus. If the starting radius is ρ=∥x∥, its radius at time t is (1−t)ρ+t. This lies between 1 and ρ≤2, verifying that the entire homotopy stays in the original annulus. When ρ=1, the point is stationary, as required.
For x=(0,2), times 0,1/2,1 give (0,2),(0,3/2),(0,1). A radius-3/2 loop similarly becomes a radius-5/4 loop halfway through, then the unit circle. Its angle and winding number are preserved. This is useful because computing the fundamental group of the annulus reduces to computing that of the circle; the explicit homotopy equivalence establishes why the answer transfers.

The geometric Whitehead product of based maps α:Sp→Y, β:Sq→Y is their wedge sum map composed with the attaching map Sp+q−1→Sp∨Sq of the top cell of Sp×Sq. A sphere cannot fibre over a lower-dimensional odd sphere

Relative homotopy of a Serre fibration (πn​(E,F)≅πn​(B))

Words: 88
Serre fibration OurBigBook.com
For a based Serre fibration with fibre F over the basepoint, lift a based cube in B from its constant bottom face, keeping the side faces fixed. Its final face lies in F, giving surjectivity of the relative map. Homotopies in B lift; two lifts of the same cube are relatively homotopic by lifting with both side liftings prescribed. This gives injectivity. For n≥2 this is a group isomorphism; at n=1 it is initially a bijection of pointed sets, unless the relative set is endowed with the group law transported through the fibration.
