Ideal weak electrolyte dilution law (Ka​=cα2/(1−α))

 Home Chemistry Electrochemistry Electrolyte Weak electrolyte
OurBigBook.com Words: 391
Consider a neutral monoprotic acid HA dissolved in water, with no added salts or other acids. Assume dilute ideal thermodynamic activities, fixed temperature, water thermodynamic activity one, and negligible self-ionization of water. Write CT​ for its analytical concentration, c=CT​/c∘ with c∘=1molL−1, and α for its weak acid dissociation fraction. Conservation of acid units and electric charge gives
[HA]=CT​(1−α),[A−]=[H3​O+]=CT​α.
(17)
Here A− is the conjugate base, and H+ is conventional shorthand for hydrated hydrogen ions, represented by hydronium in this model. Substitution into the dimensionless acid dissociation constant gives
Ka​=c∘[HA][H3​O+][A−]​=1−αcα2​,cα2+Ka​α−Ka​=0.
(18)
Solving this quadratic equation and rejecting its negative root of a polynomial gives an exact result within the stated model:
α=2cKa2​+4Ka​c​−Ka​​=Ka​+Ka2​+4Ka​c​2Ka​​.
(19)
The second form avoids subtracting nearly equal numbers. Unlike the approximation α≈Ka​/c​, it retains depletion of neutral weak acid and always satisfies 0<α<1 for positive Ka​,c. The broader weak acid mass and charge balances restore self-ionization of water when that omitted contribution matters.
For an original worked example, stipulate Ka​=10−5 without identifying a real substance. At CT​=10−2molL−1, the weak acid dissociation fraction is approximately 0.0311267, or 3.11267%, and [H3​O+]=3.11267×10−4molL−1. A hundredfold dilution to CT​=10−4molL−1 raises the fraction to 0.270156, or 27.0156%, but lowers [H3​O+] to 2.70156×10−5molL−1. A completely ionized strong acid at CT​=10−4molL−1 would have [H3​O+]≈10−4molL−1: less than the more concentrated weak acid in the first calculation, despite its greater converted fraction.
The directions of both changes follow without relying on the plot. Put x=[H3​O+]/c∘=cα. Rearranging the chemical equilibrium relation gives
c=x+Ka​x2​,dxdc​=1+Ka​2x​>0,α=Ka​+xKa​​.
(20)
Thus smaller c forces smaller x, which in turn makes α larger. The derivative proves that total hydronium concentration and converted fraction move in opposite directions during dilution. In this model, adding solvent also makes additional acid units ionize at fixed total acid amount of substance, even though their ions are spread over more volume. That distinction explains why “more ionized” alone does not predict greater electrical conductivity.
Do not extend the no-background model to unlimited dilution: its prediction [H3​O+]→0 omits water's own ions. The weak acid mass and charge balances include that background. Added salts, other acids or bases, and nonideal activity coefficients also change the relation between analytical concentration, converted fraction and hydronium concentration.
