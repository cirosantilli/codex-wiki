Cyclic group (Cn​)

 Home Mathematics Area of mathematics Algebra Group theory Group
OurBigBook.com Words: 2k Articles: 19
A cyclic group is a group that can be generated by repeating a single group element g and its inverse element:
G=⟨g⟩={gk:k∈Z}.
(5)
For an additive group, the same expression is written {kg:k∈Z}: kg means repeated addition, including subtraction when k is negative. A finite cyclic group has a repeating cycle, while an infinite cyclic group is isomorphic to the additive integers. The notation Cn​ refers to a finite cyclic group of order n.
This definition compresses a whole composition rule into one repeatable operation. Once g is chosen, gagb=ga+b, so working with group elements becomes working with integer exponents. In the finite case those exponents are reduced using modular arithmetic. It also proves that every cyclic group is an abelian group, because a+b=b+a. A finite cyclic group of order n has exactly d solutions of xd=e for every positive integer divisor d of n: in exponents, n∣dk has exactly d solutions modulo n. The standard finite and infinite models are described in Group Theory (v4.01) by James S. Milne (section 1.16).
