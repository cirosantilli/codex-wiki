ER signal sequence

 Home Biology Biological cell Organelle Endoplasmic reticulum Rough endoplasmic reticulum
OurBigBook.com Words: 161
An ER signal sequence is polypeptide chain targeting information recognized by machinery delivering a protein to the endoplasmic reticulum. Many soluble cargoes have a removable N-terminus signal; membrane insertion can use internal signals. An ER signal sequence alone does not specify the final destination.[ref]
For an original counting example, stipulate an unbranched precursor polypeptide chain of 180 amino acid residues whose first 20 amino acid residues form a cleaved ER signal sequence. With no further processing, the remaining chain has 180−20=160 amino acid residues and 159 peptide bonds. The initial chain had 179 peptide bonds; the detached signal has 19, so 159+19=178 remain after one bond is cut. The check counts adjacent positions: an unbranched chain of n amino acid residues has n−1 peptide bonds. These stipulated lengths are not a natural sequence, a measured size or evidence that a particular sequence functions as an ER signal sequence.
