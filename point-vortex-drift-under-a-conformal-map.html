Point-vortex drift under a conformal map (c˙=2πiΓ​2g′(c)g′′(c)​+g′(c)R′(g(c)))

Let ζ=g(z) be a conformal map, and let a point vortex of circulation Γ lie at z=c. Suppose the mapped complex potential of a two-dimensional potential flow is
W(ζ)=2πiΓ​log(ζ−g(c))+R(ζ),
(6)
where R is a holomorphic function near g(c) and represents the other point vortices, boundaries, and any imposed flow. The physical complex velocity is w′=g′W′. The point vortex follows its regular velocity, found by removing Γ/[2πi(z−c)] before taking the limit. A Taylor expansion proves
g(z)−g(c)g′(z)​=z−c1​+2g′(c)g′′(c)​+O(z−c),
(7)
which gives the title formula. The complex conjugate occurs because the complex velocity convention is w′=u−iv, whereas c˙=u+iv.
For a mapped upper half-plane with no imposed flow, the method of images gives R(ζ)=−Γlog(ζ−g(c)​)/(2πi), hence
c˙=2πiΓ​[2g′(c)g′′(c)​−g(c)−g(c)​g′(c)​].
(8)
The first term is essential: excluding a mapped singularity and then transforming its regular velocity does not in general equal subtracting the physical singularity after transformation.
