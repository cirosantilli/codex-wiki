Uncountability obstruction to enumerating infinite Cartesian products ({0,1}N is uncountable)

 Home Mathematics Area of mathematics Foundations of mathematics Set theory Cartesian product
OurBigBook.com Words: 80
An infinite Cartesian product of finite sets need not admit any complete list enumeration. For the repeated binary factor, suppose the output lists all sequences as z0​,z1​,…. Define a sequence by bi​=1−zi​(i). It is a valid binary sequence but differs from the i-th output at coordinate i, a contradiction. This is Cantor's diagonal argument. Consequently fair binary products can recursively enumerate the choices from finitely many possibly infinite lists, but cannot solve the unrestricted infinite-family specification. A finite-family algorithm's restriction must be explicit.
