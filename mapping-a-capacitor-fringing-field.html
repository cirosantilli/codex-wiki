Mapping a capacitor fringing field

 ... Physics Branches of physics Electromagnetism Capacitor Parallel-plate capacitor Fringing field of a capacitor
OurBigBook.com Words: 415
The Field Mapper Kit (PK-9023), Product Manual 012-04346D by PASCO scientific (p. 3, Parallel plate capacitor) proposes a direct investigation of the plate-length-to-gap ratio using conductive-paper electric potential mapping. Its finite straight electrodes represent a cross-section of the plates. The apparatus is a gridded resistive sheet on a corkboard, silver-ink electrodes, pushpin electrical contacts, a 5–20V direct supply capable of 25mA, and a voltmeter with input electrical resistance at least 10MΩ. The manual specifies waiting 20 minutes for the ink to reach its maximum electrical conductivity, then checking each electrode: its internal voltage drop should be no more than 1% of the applied electric potential difference.[ref]
For the readout, keep one voltmeter lead on the reference electrode and scan the other across the sheet. Mark points having the same reading to trace an equipotential. A second method holds the two probe tips at a fixed separation and pivots them to find the largest electric potential difference; a suggested separation is one centimetre. Avoid printed grid marks when making contact.[ref]
The inference follows from the measured electric potential, rather than assuming straight electric field lines. With probe separation Δs and direction t, a local finite difference gives

To investigate the fringing field of a capacitor, compare contours well inside the gap with contours near and beyond the ends, then repeat at a changed gap while keeping the sheet boundary and plate length fixed. Curving contours and an outside gradient establish nonuniformity in this planar setup. Compare −Ey​d/V at recorded positions to distinguish simple voltage rescaling from geometric changes; no measured values are supplied here.
Additional useful controls are repeat reference readings to detect drift, reversal of supply polarity, and repeating selected readings with reduced probe spacing. Keep contact quality and sheet edges fixed during the aspect-ratio comparison. Finite probe separation averages the gradient; reducing it improves spatial resolution but magnifies the effect of voltage uncertainty. Voltmeter loading, imperfect ink electrodes, and inhomogeneous sheet electrical conductivity can mimic distortions. These proposed checks supplement the manufacturer's electrode check, rather than claiming a completed experiment.
The sheet carries a steady electric current. For uniform electrical conductivity, Ohm's law and electric charge conservation give the two-dimensional Laplace equation away from electrodes, as explained in conductive-paper electric potential mapping. Uncontacted sheet edges have zero normal electric current, whereas the diagram model fixes zero outer electric potential. These distinct boundary conditions must be matched before a quantitative comparison. The apparatus reveals a planar electric potential map; it does not directly measure the free-space three-dimensional capacitance of metal plates.
