Totient divisor-sum identity (∑d∣n​φ(d)=n)

 Home Mathematics Area of mathematics Number theory Euler totient function
OurBigBook.com Words: 103
The divisor sum of Euler's totient function is the identity arithmetic function:
∑d∣n​φ(d)=n,n>0.
(254)
Equivalently, φ∗1=id under Dirichlet convolution, with 1 the constant-one arithmetic function. [ref]
To see why, group the integers j=1,…,n by h=gcd(j,n). Each h is a positive integer divisor of n. Within its group, j=ha with 1≤a≤n/h and gcd(a,n/h)=1, by the scaling identity for greatest common divisors. Dividing by h gives a bijection with the integers counted by φ(n/h). The groups form a partition of a set, so
n=∑h∣n​φ(n/h)=∑d∣n​φ(d).
(255)
For n=12, the groups with greatest common divisor h=1,2,3,4,6,12 are respectively {1,5,7,11},{2,10},{3,9},{4,8},{6},{12}. Their sizes are 4,2,2,2,1,1, totaling 12. Möbius inversion reverses this divisor sum to recover Euler's totient function from g(n)=n.
