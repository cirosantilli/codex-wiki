Uniform integrability gives uniform compactness of primitives (vj​⇀v in L1,uj​(x)=α+∫ax​vj​⟹uj​→u uniformly)

OurBigBook.com Words: 105
For a uniformly integrable bounded sequence of derivatives on a compact interval and a fixed initial value, its primitives are uniformly bounded and equicontinuous. The increment over any short interval is bounded by the integral of the absolute derivative on that interval. The Arzelà-Ascoli theorem gives relative uniform compactness. If the derivatives converge weakly in L1, tests by interval indicators identify every pointwise limit with u(x)=α+∫ax​v. Hence every convergent subsequence has this same limit and the primitives converge uniformly. The limit belongs to W1,1 and retains any common terminal value. Uniform integrability alone gives no essential-supremum bound on its derivative, so this compactness statement does not assert a Lipschitz limit.
