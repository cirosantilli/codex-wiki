Ruby calibration against sodium chloride

 ... Thermodynamics Pressure Pressure measurement High-pressure experimental metrology Diamond anvil cell Ruby pressure scale
OurBigBook.com Words: 187
The 1975 experiment mixed powdered sodium chloride and 0.5%-chromium ruby in a 4:1 volume ratio. An Inconel gasket, initially 0.15mm thick with a 0.3mm hole, held the powder and 4:1 methanol–ethanol. At 25∘C, X-ray diffraction rings on film 12cm away gave salt compression; a reference equation of state assigned pressure. After decreasing load, they waited at least five hours and bracketed diffraction exposures with R1​ readings to check drift.[ref]
The recommended linear slope was 2.740GPanm−1, with a 95% fit confidence interval of ±0.016GPanm−1, to 19.5GPa. Reference-scale systematic measurement uncertainty remained separate. Above about 10.4GPa the medium was only quasihydrostatic; separate range fits checked consistency.[ref]
The logical chain is compression to reference pressure, paired with optical shift, then a fitted response. X-ray diffraction supplies the reference comparison during calibration; subsequent ruby readings transfer that scale to other samples. The reference equation of state is therefore part of the meaning of the reported pressure. Changing it can move all calibrated readings together, even if the optical measurements repeat perfectly. A regression residual tests agreement with the chosen reference within the sampled conditions; it cannot independently certify that reference.
