Universal stiffness jump (ρs​(TBKT−​)=2kB​TBKT​/π)

 ... Physics Branches of physics Statistical physics Critical phenomenon Lower critical dimension Berezinskii–Kosterlitz–Thouless transition
OurBigBook.com Words: 163
The renormalized phase stiffness has this discontinuity at the Berezinskii–Kosterlitz–Thouless transition. The algebraic correlation function has exponent 1/4 at the transition from the ordered side.
The stiffness here is an energy in two dimensions, not an areal mass density. For a neutral superfluid of particles of mass m, let ns​ be the superfluid number density per unit area. Substituting the superfluid velocity vs​=(ℏ/m)∇θ into the kinetic energy gives ρs​=ℏ2ns​/m. Consequently the jump can also be written
ns​(TBKT−​)λT2​=4,λT2​=mkB​T2πℏ2​,
(6)
where λT​ is the thermal wavelength. It is the large-distance superfluid number density, rather than the total density or a locally inferred condensate density, that enters this relation.
Within the vortex-pair renormalization flow, y changes from irrelevant to relevant at πKR​=2, with KR​ the large-distance dimensionless phase stiffness. Combining this with the spin wave result η=1/(2πKR​) gives η=1/4. Above the transition the homogeneous equilibrium phase stiffness vanishes at infinite observation scale. A discontinuous response coefficient is compatible with a diverging correlation length; it does not imply a latent heat.[ref]
