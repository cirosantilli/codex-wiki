Thermodynamic limit

 Home Physics Branches of physics Statistical physics
OurBigBook.com Words: 2k Articles: 9
The thermodynamic limit describes a sequence of larger systems, not a single enormous specimen: particle number N and volume V tend to infinity with number density n=N/V fixed. Hold the chosen intensive and extensive thermodynamic quantities such as temperature T and an applied magnetic field fixed along that sequence. In the canonical ensemble the controls are T,N,V; in the grand canonical ensemble one instead fixes T and chemical potential, and the limiting number density is a result, possibly with a jump at phase coexistence. These are different specifications of a limiting experiment.
The useful limit is usually an intensive and extensive thermodynamic quantities normalization of the Helmholtz free energy, not a finite limit of the total extensive quantity itself:
FN,V​=−kB​TlogZN,V​,f(T,n)=limN,V→∞N/V=n​​VFN,V​​,a(T,n)=limN,V→∞N/V=n​​NFN,V​​=nf(T,n)​.
(34)
Here ZN,V​ is the canonical partition function, kB​ is the Boltzmann constant, f is free-energy density, and a is Helmholtz free energy per particle. Thus FN,V​=Vf+o(V) when the limit exists; finite systems need not be exactly extensive quantities because of the surface correction to thermodynamic free energy.
Specify the interactions and the growing containers too. For a conventional fluid, use a fixed stable pair potential with a tempered pair potential tail, and containers whose boundary layer of any fixed thickness occupies a vanishing fraction of the volume; uniformly enlarged cubes work, a tube of fixed cross-section does not give the same bulk limit. For a finite-range Ising model, keep the lattice spacing and bond coupling fixed while enlarging all intended spatial directions. Ordinary bounded wall interactions or periodic boundary conditions can then give the same bulk free-energy density, although boundary conditions can select different spontaneous symmetry breaking states. This is a set of hypotheses, not a theorem that every interaction has a boundary-independent limit.[ref]
The Curie–Weiss model below deliberately uses a different limiting prescription: its all-to-all coupling is divided by N. Without that factor its interaction energy would grow as N2, rather than as an extensive quantity. The model has no spatial wall or correlation length to compare with a container; it is an exactly summable illustration of finite-size rounding, not a quantitative model of the fluid experiment below.
