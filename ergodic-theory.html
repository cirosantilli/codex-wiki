Generic point for an invariant measure (n1​∑k=0n−1​φ(fkx)⟶∫φdμ)

Words: 76
Ergodic theory OurBigBook.com
A point is generic for an invariant measure μ of a continuous map on a compact metric space if the displayed convergence holds for every continuous function φ. Its empirical probability measures converge against continuous test functions to μ. The same test-function condition may be used before invariance is known: the telescoping identity for φ∘f−φ then proves that the limiting Borel probability measure must be invariant. If almost every point is generic for the same measure, continuous approximation of invariant-set indicators proves that this measure is ergodic.

Invariant measure (T∗​μ=μ)

Words: 129 Articles: 2
Ergodic theory OurBigBook.com
For a measurable transformation T, a measure is invariant if the pushforward measure equals the original measure, or equivalently μ(T−1A)=μ(A) for every measurable A. This is the measure used in a measure-preserving system.

Invariant probability measure for a semigroup (∫Pt​fdμ=∫fdμ)

Words: 99 Articles: 1
Invariant measure OurBigBook.com
A Borel probability measure is invariant for a Markov kernel semigroup if every transition preserves it. For a Feller semigroup on a compact metric space, it suffices to test the displayed identity on continuous functions. Kernel Jensen inequality gives contraction on Lp(μ), and sup-norm strong continuity followed by density gives strong continuity there for 1≤p<∞.

Krylov–Bogolyubov theorem

Words: 53
Invariant probability measure for a semigroup OurBigBook.com
For a Feller semigroup on a compact metric space, time-averaged transition laws have weakly convergent subsequences by compactness of probability measures on a compact metric space. Shifting a time interval of length n by t changes a bounded test-function average by at most 2t∥f∥∞​/n. A weak subsequential limit is therefore an invariant probability measure for a semigroup. More generally, tightness of the time averages supplies the required compactness.

Cutting and stacking

Words: 129 Articles: 1
Ergodic theory OurBigBook.com
Cutting and stacking constructs measure-preserving transformations from towers of equal-measure levels. Cut a tower into equal-width subcolumns, add new spacer levels, and stack the resulting columns. Map each level to the one above by a measure-preserving identification; subsequent stages extend the partial map. When uncovered and top-level measures tend to zero, the limiting map is defined modulo null sets.

Chacon transformation

Words: 72
Cutting and stacking OurBigBook.com
The standard three-cut Chacon transformation is an invertible measure-preserving transformation built by cutting and stacking with spacer vector (0,1,0) at every stage. Cut the current tower into three equal subcolumns, place one spacer above the middle, and stack left to right. With initial width 2/3 and height one on a unit interval, level counts and widths are hj​=(3j+1−1)/2 and wj​=2/3j+1. Tower measure tends to one, using total spacer measure 1/3. Tower words obey Wj+1​=Wj​Wj​1Wj​.

Unique ergodicity

Words: 98 Articles: 1
Ergodic theory OurBigBook.com
A continuous self-map of a compact metric space is uniquely ergodic when it has exactly one invariant Borel probability measure. An irrational rotation of the circle is uniquely ergodic: invariance multiplies its nonzero-index Fourier coefficients by nontrivial phases, forcing those coefficients to vanish. The Stone-Weierstrass theorem then identifies the invariant measure as normalized Lebesgue measure.

Uniform ergodic convergence for uniquely ergodic systems

Words: 57
Unique ergodicity OurBigBook.com
For a uniquely ergodic continuous map T of a compact metric space, every continuous f satisfies N−1∑j=0N−1​f(Tjx)→∫fdμ uniformly in x. Otherwise choose increasingly long orbit empirical measures with a fixed discrepancy. Compactness provides a weak limit; the telescoping identity makes it invariant, so it must be the unique measure μ, contradicting that discrepancy. Compactness and continuity are essential hypotheses.

Van der Corput lemma (Hilbert space sequences)

Words: 122 Articles: 1
Ergodic theory OurBigBook.com
For a bounded sequence (un​) in a Hilbert space, put ch​=limsupN→∞​∣N−1∑n=0N−h−1​⟨un+h​,un​⟩∣. If H−1∑h=1H​ch​→0, then N−1∑n=0N−1​un​→0 in norm. Since the ch​ are bounded, it suffices that (ch​) has convergence in density of a sequence to zero. The proof applies the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality to an average of shifted sums.

Van der Corput inequality for finite scalar sequences (∣N−1∑n​un​∣2)

Words: 81
Van der Corput lemma (Hilbert space sequences) OurBigBook.com
For complex numbers ∣un​∣≤1, 0≤n<N, and 1≤H≤N, put Ch​=∑n=0N−h−1​un+h​un​​. Then
​N1​∑n=0N−1​un​​2≤NHN+H−1​(1+2∑h=1H−1​(1−Hh​)N∣Ch​∣​).
(1)
Extend the sequence by zero, count each summand in H consecutive windows, and apply the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality to their sum. Expansion of the squared window sums gives the stated coefficients. Fixing H and making each normalized correlation tend to zero bounds the limiting squared average by 1/H; then let H tend to infinity. This is the scalar finite form underlying the Van der Corput lemma.

Equidistributed sequence

Words: 326 Articles: 6
Ergodic theory OurBigBook.com
A sequence (xn​) in R/Z is equidistributed if the limiting frequency in every half-open interval equals that interval's length. Equivalently, its empirical probability measures converge to normalized Lebesgue measure when tested against every continuous function.

Equidistribution criterion for a torus translation (ku+ℓv∈/2πZ)

Words: 77
Equidistributed sequence OurBigBook.com
The sequence r(u,v) is equidistributed in T2 precisely when ku+ℓv∈/2πZ for every nonzero integer pair (k,ℓ). Equivalently 1,u/(2π),v/(2π) are rationally independent. Under that condition, nonconstant character averages tend to zero by the geometric-series formula. Uniform approximation by trigonometric polynomials gives convergence for continuous functions, and continuous upper and lower bounds give convergence for rectangles. If a character is resonant, the orbit lies in its proper closed kernel and misses a positive-area rectangle, so equidistribution fails.

Interval discrepancy (DN​(I)=∑j=1N​1I​(uj​)−N∣I∣)

Words: 48
Equidistributed sequence OurBigBook.com
Interval discrepancy measures the difference between empirical counts and the expected count for normalized Lebesgue measure. Convolving an enlarged interval indicator function with a narrow unit-integral indicator function gives a continuous majorant. Its Fourier coefficients decay as O(1/(δk2)), converting the count into a constant term, finite exponential sums and a controlled absolute tail.

Weyl criterion

Words: 166 Articles: 3
Equidistributed sequence OurBigBook.com
A sequence (xn​) in R/Z is an equidistributed sequence if and only if N−1∑n=0N−1​e2πimxn​→0 for every nonzero integer m. Approximation of continuous functions by trigonometric polynomials proves the criterion.

Differencing obstruction to equidistribution

Words: 38
Weyl criterion OurBigBook.com
If a circle-valued sequence is not an equidistributed sequence, some positive-shift difference is not equidistributed either. The contrapositive follows by applying the Van der Corput inequality for finite scalar sequences to every nonzero integer exponential sum. Thus cancellation for all fixed nonzero differences implies equidistribution of the original sequence.

Equidistribution of a quadratic polynomial with irrational leading coefficient

Words: 97 Articles: 1
Weyl criterion OurBigBook.com
If a is irrational and b,c are real, the fractional parts of an2+bn+c form an equidistributed sequence. In the Van der Corput lemma, every nonzero-lag correlation of e2πim(an2+bn+c) is a geometric series with irrational frequency 2mah and tends to zero. The Weyl criterion then applies.
Uniform square recurrence on the circle (∀ϵ>0 ∃N ∀a ∃1≤m≤N:∥am2∥<ϵ)
Words: 59
Equidistribution of a quadratic polynomial with irrational leading coefficient OurBigBook.com
For rational a, a multiple of its denominator gives exact integer recurrence. For irrational a, Weyl differencing and the Weyl criterion give an equidistributed sequence am2 modulo one. Thus every parameter belongs to one of the open sets {a:∥am2∥<ϵ}. Compactness of the circle group gives a finite subcover, hence a bound N uniform in a; no explicit rate follows from this compactness step.

Spectral measure of a Koopman observable (⟨Unf,f⟩=∫e2πintdσf​(t))

Words: 57
Koopman operator OurBigBook.com
The finite positive measure representing an observable's autocorrelation sequence by the Bochner–Herglotz theorem. For a probability measure-preserving system, the Koopman operator is an isometry, so its forward autocorrelations, extended by complex conjugation to negative indices, form a positive-definite function on Z. The measure has mass ∥f∥22​, and its atoms are squared norms of projections onto Koopman operator eigenspaces, including for noninvertible transformations.

Almost periodic observable ({UTn​f:n∈Z})

Words: 149 Articles: 2
Koopman operator OurBigBook.com
An L2 observable of an invertible probability measure-preserving system is almost periodic when its Koopman operator orbit is totally bounded in the global L2 norm. A noninvertible system uses its forward orbit. This is the Hilbert-space dynamical definition, distinguished from uniform almost periodicity for continuous functions of a real variable.

Syndetic near returns of an almost periodic observable (Rε​={n≥1:∥Unf−f∥2​<ε})

If the forward orbit of f under an isometry U is totally bounded, choose a finite net whose centers are Unj​f and let M=maxj​nj​. For every sufficiently large n, some center is within ε of Unf, so cancellation gives n−nj​∈Rε​. Thus Rε​ is a syndetic set. Telescoping also gives ∥Uknf−f∥2​≤k∥Unf−f∥2​.

Compact measure-preserving system

Words: 48
Almost periodic observable OurBigBook.com
A probability measure-preserving system is compact if every L2 observable has a relatively compact Koopman operator orbit in the global L2 norm. For an invertible system one may use all integer times; for a general transformation use nonnegative times. This is a measure-theoretic definition, stronger than compactness of the underlying topological space.

Decay of autocorrelation implies weak convergence of an observable

Words: 60
Koopman operator OurBigBook.com
For a probability measure-preserving system, if ⟨UTn​f,f⟩→∣∫fdμ∣2, then UTn​f converges weakly in L2 to (∫fdμ)1. Centre v=f−(∫f)1; its autocorrelations tend to zero. The isometry identity gives the same decay against each UTk​v, hence by approximation against their closed forward span. Against its orthogonal complement the correlations are exactly zero. Orthogonal decomposition by a closed subspace finishes the proof without invertibility.

Invariance from integral duality for a measure-preserving transformation (E[XZ]=E[X(Z∘T)] ∀Z bounded⟹X=X∘T a.s.)

Words: 62
Measure-preserving transformation OurBigBook.com
Let T be a measure-preserving transformation of a probability space and X an integrable random variable. If the displayed duality holds for every bounded random variable Z, test it on indicator functions to identify E[X∣T−1(F)]=X∘T. The measure-preserving transformation gives the same probability distribution to X and X∘T. The principle of conditional expectation preserving a distribution then forces equality almost surely. This does not require an inverse for T or require X to be square-integrable.

Ergodic invariant probability on a countable state space has finite cyclic support

Words: 74
Ergodicity OurBigBook.com
Let T be an ergodic transformation preserving a probability measure μ on a countable set. Some state x has μ({x})>0. Along its forward orbit,
μ({Tn+1x})=μ(T−1{Tn+1x})≥μ({Tnx}).
(4)
An infinite sequence of distinct states with masses bounded below by μ({x}) is impossible, so the orbit eventually reaches a finite cycle Y. Since Y⊆T−1Y and invariance gives μ(T−1Y)=μ(Y)>0, the difference is null. The cycle is invariant modulo null sets, and ergodicity therefore gives μ(Y)=1.
