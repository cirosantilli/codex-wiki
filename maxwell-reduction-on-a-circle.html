Maxwell reduction on a circle (mn​=∣n∣/r)

 Home Physics Mathematical physics Compactification (physics) Kaluza-Klein theory
OurBigBook.com Words: 98
A real five-dimensional Maxwell field on a circle of radius r has a Fourier series with coefficients AM(n)​, AM(−n)​=(AM(n)​)∗. Its mixed field-strength component is Fμ5(n)​=∂μ​A5(n)​−inAμ(n)​/r. The nonzero modes transform as δAμ(n)​=∂μ​Λ(n), δA5(n)​=inΛ(n)/r. Thus A5(n)​ is a Stueckelberg mechanism field and the gauge-invariant massive vector is Aμ(n)​−r∂μ​A5(n)​/(in). Its mass is ∣n∣/r and it has three physical polarizations. The zero modes instead consist of a massless four-dimensional Maxwell field and a real massless scalar field; the latter cannot be removed by a periodic infinitesimal gauge transformation. A positive Fourier level and its negative level are conjugate, not independent real fields.
