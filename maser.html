Maser

 Home Physics Branches of physics Optics
OurBigBook.com Words: 1k Articles: 9
A maser uses stimulated emission to amplify microwaves. With population inversion and feedback it can sustain a coherent wave field as an oscillator. The name abbreviates "microwave amplification by stimulated emission of radiation".[ref]
Why make molecules supply an oscillator? Their energy levels offer a reproducible frequency reference, while stimulated emission lets successive molecules reinforce one coherent wave field. In his 1964 lecture, Charles Hard Townes traced his motivation to microwave spectroscopy: resolving molecular structure required useful coherent sources.[ref] The interesting balance is between molecular amplification and escaping energy: even a beam with population inversion fails to oscillate if cavity losses win. This is the microwave counterpart of laser threshold.

Ammonia maser

Words: 1k Articles: 6
Maser OurBigBook.com
An ammonia maser uses an ammonia molecular beam, maser state selection and a maser cavity to obtain amplification or sustained microwave oscillation. In the 1954 experiment by James Power Gordon, Herbert Jack Zeiger and Charles Hard Townes, a quadrupolar static electric field focused upper-state molecules toward the cavity and deflected lower-state molecules away. The reported J=K=3 ammonia inversion transition lies near 23.87GHz.[ref]

Ammonia inversion transition (hν0​)

Words: 128
Ammonia maser OurBigBook.com
In ammonia, the nitrogen chemical atom can lie on either side of the plane of the three hydrogen chemical atoms. Quantum tunnelling couples these configurations; the resulting pair of energy levels permits an electric dipole transition. This geometrical inversion is distinct from population inversion, which concerns how many molecules occupy each level.[ref]
For an idealized pair of energy levels with equal quantum degeneracy, the Boltzmann distribution gives Nu​/Nl​=exp[−hν0​/(kB​T)]<1 at positive temperature T. A warm source therefore supplies upper-state molecules together with an unwanted lower-state population. Selecting the former can produce population inversion in the transmitted beam without exciting each transmitted molecule during selection. The energy per net downward transition is
ϵ=hν0​.
(1)
Here h is the Planck constant. Taking the reported ν0​=23.87GHz as a rounded input gives ϵ≈1.582×10−23J. This calculation is a conversion of the reported frequency, not a new measurement of the ammonia inversion transition.

Maser state selection

Words: 169
Ammonia maser OurBigBook.com
Maser state selection separates paths according to the Stark effect, the dependence of a molecule's energy levels on an electric field. It prepares a transmitted population by sorting. Optical pumping, by contrast, changes populations through absorption of light and subsequent transitions. Both can produce a useful population imbalance, but they are different preparation mechanisms.
A simple two-level system explains the sign of the focusing force. Ignore additional splittings and let the magnitude of the relevant projected electric dipole moment be d. Model the two local energy levels in an electric field of magnitude E by
W±​(E)=±(hν0​/2)2+d2E2​.
(2)
For an ideal quadrupolar electrostatic potential Φ=A(x2−y2), the electric field magnitude is E=2∣A∣r, where r=x2+y2​. Since W+​ increases with r, the force F+​=−∇W+​ points inward. The lower branch has the opposite sign and is defocused. The upper branch is thus low-field seeking. This argument uses the gradient of internal energy, rather than a net electric charge on the molecule. The square-root splitting is the usual two-state description.[ref] The idealized two-level system omits the actual rotational and hyperfine structure sublevels and their different transmission efficiencies.

Maser cavity

Words: 729 Articles: 2
Ammonia maser OurBigBook.com
The maser cavity stores a resonant electromagnetic wave so that an arriving molecule encounters a field left by earlier molecules. An output coupling lets part of that stored energy reach a detector. The same coupling also removes energy, so increasing extraction can prevent startup. The cavity provides feedback; the selected molecular beam supplies the useful internal energy.
Let U be stored field energy, ω=2πν0​, and κ=κint​+κout​ the total passive energy decay rate. Internal dissipation and output extraction contribute κint​U and κout​U to lost power. With this energy convention, the loaded quality factor is
QL​=κUωU​=κω​,QL​1​=Qint​1​+Qout​1​.
(3)
The field amplitude decays at κ/2; confusing amplitude and energy decay introduces a factor of two into a maser oscillation threshold calculation.

Maser oscillation threshold (GN0​>κ)

Words: 617 Articles: 1
Maser cavity OurBigBook.com
The following original model isolates amplification by inverted electric dipoles. Let N=Nu​−Nl​ be the excess number in the upper member of one equally degenerate resonant pair. Let a be a slowly varying cavity amplitude with photon number n=∣a∣2, and let p describe the collective molecular dipole component in the amplifying phase convention. On exact resonance, stipulate the linear equations
a˙=−2κ​a+g0​p,p˙​=−Γp+g0​Na,Γ>0.
(4)
The dipole relaxation rate is Γ, and g0​=∣dul​Evac​∣/ℏ is a single-molecule coupling in the chosen mode normalization. Here dul​ is an electric dipole moment matrix element, Evac​ is the field's zero-point amplitude, and ℏ=h/(2π). Field overlap and dipole orientation enter this coupling. The characteristic polynomial for growth rate λ is
(λ+κ/2)(λ+Γ)−g02​N=0.
(5)
For N>0, one root becomes positive precisely when g02​N>κΓ/2. If dipole relaxation is fast enough to eliminate p, then p≈g0​Na/Γ and
n˙=(GN−κ)n,G=Γ2g02​​.
(6)
Thus positive population inversion alone is insufficient: the gain rate GN must exceed the passive decay rate. This reduction describes the onset near resonance; it does not predict output phase noise, actual transit dynamics or an exact historical threshold. Nikolay Basov and Aleksandr Prokhorov's molecular-generator theory likewise treats self-excitation as competition between molecular gain and resonator-plus-load losses.[ref]
To include replenishment, suppose upper-state molecules enter at rate F and both levels leave with residence rate γ. A deliberately simple ordinary differential equation model is
N˙u​=F−γNu​−GNn,N˙l​=−γNl​+GNn,n˙=(GN−κ)n.
(7)
One downward transition removes two units of the difference Nu​−Nl​, so N˙=F−γN−2GNn. Below onset the unsaturated inversion is N0​=F/γ, giving
GN0​>κ,F>Fth​=Gγκ​.
(8)
For fixed mode volume V, the equivalent number density condition is N0​/V>κ/(GV). Raising selected beam intensity raises gain; increasing quality factor lowers the loss rate at fixed frequency. These statements hold with G, residence time and mode overlap fixed.
Above onset, the nonzero steady state has
Nss​=Gκ​,nss​=2κF−γκ/G​,Pout​=κout​hν0​nss​.
(9)
The inversion is clamped while excess supply supports output. A finite initial seed is understood: the noiseless equations leave n=0 exactly unchanged even above onset. Actual startup has spontaneous emission and field fluctuations.
Maser microwave energy budget
Words: 203
Maser oscillation threshold OurBigBook.com
Multiplying the upper-level and photon equations by hν0​ makes the cancellation of internal transfer visible:
dtd​[hν0​(Nu​+n)]=hν0​F−γhν0​Nu​−κint​hν0​n−κout​hν0​n.
(10)
Incoming excitation becomes unused excitation carried away, internal dissipation, useful output, or a change in stored energy. Translational kinetic energy, the source's other internal states and preparation losses lie outside this two-level budget. The output cannot exceed the incoming excitation power hν0​F.
Choose G=10−4s−1 per unit inversion, γ=104s−1, κint​=κout​=106s−1, and F=4×1014s−1. These are stipulated teaching parameters. Then N0​=4×1010, Fth​=2×1014s−1, Nss​=2×1010 and nss​=5×107. Using the rounded ammonia inversion transition frequency, the loaded quality factor is approximately 7.50×104. The incoming excitation power is 6.327nW: 4.745nW leaves unused, 0.791nW becomes internal dissipation and 0.791nW is useful output, with rounding in these displayed values.
There is an instructive extraction optimum within this model. With the same F, G and γ, put ρ=κout​/κint​ and p=FG/(γκint​)=4. For 0≤ρ<p−1,
Pout​=2Ghν0​γκint​​1+ρρ(p−1−ρ)​.
(11)
Differentiation gives ρopt​=p​−1=1. At zero coupling no power is extracted; at ρ=3 the device reaches onset and the nonzero branch disappears. The optimum balances extraction against the inversion required to compensate that extraction. Equal residence rates and loss of molecular phase memory are simplifying assumptions; the calculated efficiency is not a universal bound for beam masers.

Maser discovery experiment

Words: 147
Ammonia maser OurBigBook.com
Ammonia gas entered a vacuum chamber through fine channels; the selected beam entered a cavity with input and output waveguides.[ref] With external microwaves swept across a molecular resonance, the 1954 experiment read an increase in transmitted power; with sufficient beam emission, it sustained oscillation.[ref]
The 1955 readout mixed two maser signals with a local oscillator in a crystal detector, amplified them at an intermediate frequency, and combined them in a diode detector. An oscilloscope showed their beat frequency. Changing cavity tuning, source pressure or focuser field tested sensitivity; excessive detuning stopped oscillation.[ref] The beat tests relative frequency stability; it does not by itself establish an absolute frequency or an intrinsic linewidth independent of detector noise. Detuning also changes the field–molecule interaction, so the threshold model's constant G cannot describe arbitrary tuning scans.

Maser history

The 1955 paper by James Power Gordon, Herbert Jack Zeiger and Charles Hard Townes acknowledged Nikolay Basov and Aleksandr Prokhorov's independent proposal.[ref] The latter authors' theory paper states that they presented the molecular-generator possibility in May 1952, cites their 1954 publication, and separately cites the Gordon–Zeiger–Townes experimental realization. These are contributions from independent groups.[ref]
