The amplification factor is R=1−λ/N. To reproduce decay, the forward Euler method requires ∣R∣<1, equivalently
−1<1−Nλ​<1⟺N>λ/2.​
(73)
At N=λ/2, R=−1 and the approximations alternate with constant magnitude 2; for N<λ/2, ∣R∣>1 and the magnitude grows. This is the condition of absolute stability for this decaying differential equation.
Stability is only a minimum requirement. Values just inside the bound can still give a poor endpoint approximation; resolving the decay accurately generally requires a smaller step size. If positivity at every step is desired, the stronger condition N≥λ ensures R≥0. The figure shows the difference between decay of the exact solution and numerical oscillation.
