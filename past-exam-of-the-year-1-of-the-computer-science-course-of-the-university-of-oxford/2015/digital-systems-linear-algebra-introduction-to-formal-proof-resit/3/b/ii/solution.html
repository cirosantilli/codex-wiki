A writing MIPS instruction distinguishes a line stuck at zero. With $t0=7, $t1=5, $t2=99, execute
add $t2, $t0, $t1
A healthy line or a line stuck at one produces $t2=12; a line stuck at zero leaves $t2=99.
To distinguish normal operation from stuck at one, execute a normally nonwriting MIPS instruction. The following functional test uses the usual concrete controller choice MemToReg=0 during sw, selecting the ALU result on the write-data bus. Prepare $t0=37 and $s0=L-16384, for an aligned writable address L=37, then execute
sw $t0, 16384($s0)
The ALU computes L. Here rt is register 8 ($t0), and immediate 16384=0x4000 also puts 8 into bits 15…11. Thus either value of the otherwise irrelevant RegDst selects $t0. With a healthy RegWrite, $t0 remains 37. With RegWrite stuck at one and the specified write-data selection, the unwanted register file write changes $t0 to L. Combining the two independent observations gives
RegWrite statenormalstuck at 0stuck at 1​(t2 after add,t0 after store)(12,37)(99,37)(12,L)​​​

Controller qualification. The source diagram does not specify values of otherwise irrelevant control outputs during a store. MemToReg is a don't-care condition when RegWrite=0; its chosen implementation matters when that enable line is faulty. The functional test above explicitly fixes this convention instead of assuming that all implementations resolve it identically. A manufacturing fixture can also observe the register file write-enable directly while executing add followed by sw: the expected pairs are (1,0) normally, (0,0) stuck at zero, and (1,1) stuck at one. This second observation does not depend on unused write-data controls or on whether an unwanted write happens to repeat an old value.
