A more economical static CMOS design follows from factoring the condition for z=0. The output is low exactly when both input pairs disagree:
¬z=(a⊕c)∧(b⊕d)=(a¬c∨¬ac)(b¬d∨¬bd).
(11)
Generate ¬a,¬b,¬c,¬d with four CMOS inverters. Write n(x) or p(x) for an NMOS transistor or PMOS transistor whose gate receives x. In the following network expressions, a dash means a series connection and ∥ means a parallel connection.
Connect this eight-device pull-down network between z and ground:
[(n(a)−n(¬c))∥(n(¬a)−n(c))]−[(n(b)−n(¬d))∥(n(¬b)−n(d))].​
(12)
The first parallel module conducts exactly when a=c and the second exactly when b=d. Their series connection therefore conducts precisely when z should be zero.
By complementary CMOS network duality, connect this eight-device pull-up network between VDD​ and z:
[(p(a)∥p(¬c))−(p(¬a)∥p(c))]∥[(p(b)∥p(¬d))−(p(¬b)∥p(d))].​
(13)
A PMOS transistor conducts when its gate is zero. Consequently the first upper branch conducts when (¬a∨c)∧(a∨¬c), namely a=c; the second conducts when b=d. Their parallel connection gives the required logical disjunction. For every stable binary input, exactly one of the pull-up network and pull-down network conducts.
