In A, the Scala trait linearization sends insert('a',0) first to CapitalisePlugIn, then to CharTrackPlugIn, then to DocImpl. The counter trait receives 'A', so it does not increment the lowercase 'a' count. The stored document is A.
In B, the counter trait receives 'a', delegates through CapitalisePlugIn, and increments its original argument's counter on return. The stored document is again A, but the counter records one lowercase insertion:
d1.getCount(′a′)=0,d2.getCount(′a′)=1.​
(35)
This demonstrates a composition that violates the count-of-current-document representation invariant for B: an earlier counter cannot assume a later transforming trait inserts its argument unchanged. Subsequent deletion also receives uppercase 'A' and would leave that stale lowercase count. A different valid design could inspect read(pos) after insertion to count the actual stored character; that design would produce 0,0. The stated 0,1 follows the explicit implementation in part (b), whose correctness assumption has now failed in the second composition.
