Let s(T) be the number of nodes and let the left-size potential of a binary tree be
L(∅)=0,L(T)=s(T.left)+L(T.right).
(7)
Equivalently L(T) counts nodes outside the right spine, so 0≤L(T)≤s(T); for a nonempty binary tree of n nodes, L(T)≤n−1.
For the right tree rotation in part (f), write the old shape as ((A,x,B),y,C). Before and after,
Lbefore​=s(A)+1+s(B)+L(C),Lafter​=s(A)+s(B)+L(C).
(8)
Thus each inner iteration decreases L by exactly one, making L an inner loop variant. Removing a root with empty left subtree leaves L unchanged and decreases s by one. Therefore s+L decreases on every rotation or visit, while s decreases on every completed outer iteration. These are bounds used for proof; the program never computes them.
There are exactly n visits and exactly L(T0​) rotations, since the final empty binary tree has potential zero. Counting constant-cost p,
rotations+visits=n+L(T0​)≤2n−1(n>0),T(n)=Θ(n+1).​
(9)
The nested loops therefore have linear total running time, despite one outer iteration potentially making many rotations.
