For an m×n matrix A with real entries, its row space is the linear span of its rows, a linear subspace of Rn. Its column space is the linear span of its columns, a linear subspace of Rm and the image of a linear map x↦Ax. Its null space is
kerA={x∈Rn:Ax=0},
(12)
a linear subspace of Rn. Finally,
rankA=dimColA=dimRowA.​
(13)
Thus the rank of a matrix counts the number of linearly independent columns, equivalently rows. The nullity is dimkerA=n−rankA by the rank-nullity theorem.

Let U,V be n×n upper triangular matrices. For an entry below the diagonal, i>j, matrix multiplication gives
(UV)ij​=∑k=1n​Uik​Vkj​.
(23)
If k<i, then Uik​=0. If k≥i, then k>j, so Vkj​=0. Every summand is zero, giving
i>j⟹(UV)ij​=0.​
(24)
Thus UV is an upper triangular matrix. Equivalently, a potentially nonzero summand would require i≤k≤j, impossible below the diagonal.

Suppose an invertible matrix has two LU factorizations, A=L1​U1​=L2​U2​, with L1​,L2​ unit lower triangular matrices and U1​,U2​ upper triangular matrices. All four factors are invertible matrices, since their square products are invertible. Multiplication by the appropriate inverse matrices gives
L2−1​L1​=U2​U1−1​.
(25)
By the supplied closure properties, the left side is a unit lower triangular matrix and the right side is an upper triangular matrix. A matrix of both types is diagonal; its diagonal entries are all one. Thus both sides are the identity matrix, whence
L1​=L2​,U1​=U2​.​
(26)
Qualification of the printed wording: this establishes uniqueness if the specified LU factorization exists. Invertibility alone does not guarantee an unpivoted LU factorization. For example (01​10​) is invertible, but in a product (1ℓ​01​)(u11​0​u12​u22​​), the zero top-left entry forces u11​=0, making the bottom-left entry ℓu11​=0 as well, contrary to the required value one. A permutation matrix is needed in general, as in PLU factorization.

Use three positive-edge-triggered D flip-flops, all driven by the same clock signal CLK. Write the current binary representation as B2B1B0, with B0 the least significant bit. The next state is the current value plus one when A=1 and minus one when A=0, in each case modulo eight. A bit toggles under exclusive or with a one.
For upward counting, B1 toggles when B0=1, while B2 toggles when B1=B0=1, the respective carry conditions. For downward counting the corresponding borrow conditions are B0=0 and B1=B0=0. Define the shared inverted direction N=¬A and the two direction-match signals
T0​=B0⊕N,T1​=B1⊕N.
(30)
Each Ti​ is one exactly when Bi=A: it equals Bi for upward counting and ¬Bi for downward counting. Thus the Boolean functions for the three D inputs are
D0​=¬B0,D1​=B1⊕T0​,D2​=B2⊕(T0​∧T1​).​
(31)
Implement this with two NOT gates for N,D0​, four two-input exclusive or gates for T0​,T1​,D1​,D2​, and one two-input AND gate. Connect Di​ to the D flip-flop whose output is Bi; feed the three outputs back into the indicated gates. All three flip-flops capture simultaneously, so each equation uses the old state.

For an estimate, use the usual equal-device CMOS series-stack model: a k-input NAND gate or NOR gate has a worst-case propagation delay approximately k times that of an inverter, because a conducting path contains k series transistors. An AND gate or OR gate adds an output inverter. With a 1 ns inverter this gives, for k≥2,
tAND,k​≃tOR,k​≃(k+1)ns.​
(32)
For k=2 it reproduces the stated 3 ns. This is a technology estimate under equal sizing and comparable loading, not a unique physical law inferable from a single measured delay. Actual propagation delay also depends on capacitance, fan-out and transistor sizing. If instead only the supplied two-input gates were allowed, a balanced tree would have worst-case depth ⌈log2​k⌉ and delay 3⌈log2​k⌉ ns; that is a different construction from a single k-input gate.
For the explicit circuit in part (a), the longest combinational logic path starts at A, passes through the shared inverter, either direction-match exclusive or, the AND gate, and the final exclusive or feeding D2​:
tmax​=1+2+3+2=8ns.​
(33)
The path A→D1​ takes 5 ns, the paths from B0 or B1 to D2​ take at most 7 ns, and B0→D0​ takes 1 ns. Both direction-match exclusive or gates act in parallel; their delays are not added together. The 8 ns excludes clock-to-Q delay, setup time, hold time and clock-distribution effects, because the question asks only for the combinational logic delay.

A zero-address instruction has no explicit operand addresses, so its operands and result location must be implicit in the instruction set architecture. A stack machine supplies these naturally: operations remove their inputs from the top of an operand stack and push the result. A fixed-processor register design is logically possible, so “almost certainly” is appropriately weaker than a proof that only a stack machine could work.
Write the stack from bottom to top, with its top on the right. Let S stand for any unchanged prefix. Suitable effects are
add:S,u,v↦S,u+v,const c:S↦S,c.​
(44)
Thus add pops two operands and pushes one sum, reducing stack occupancy by one. The special const c instruction pushes its immediate constant and increases occupancy by one. Only const needs an explicit value in its encoding.

Let de​=depth(e) and df​=depth(f) measure the extra operand stack space required from the current base, with each evaluation leaving one result. Evaluating e first uses de​ slots. Its result occupies one slot while evaluating f, so that stage uses 1+df​ slots. The final add reduces two slots to one. Thus
de first​=max(de​,1+df​),df first​=max(df​,1+de​).​
(49)
By commutativity of addition, choose the smaller of these two bounds. Evaluate the child of larger depth first. The optimum recurrence on a binary expression tree is
depth(e+f)={max(de​,df​),de​+1,​de​=df​,de​=df​.​​
(50)
Assign depth one to a literal or a simple already-addressable value, and compute the child depths recursively. If de​>df​, the depths are integers, so de​≥1+df​; evaluating e first therefore attains de​, while reversing the order needs de​+1. If the depths are equal, whichever child goes second needs the extra retained-result slot. These lower bounds prove the recurrence is optimal for evaluating each child subtree without interleaving or spilling. Apply it at every node to minimize peak operand stack occupancy for the given binary expression tree; this is optimal stack evaluation of a binary expression tree.
This changes child evaluation order, not the parenthesization. For example, a chain-shaped binary expression tree can have depth two, whereas a balanced tree of eight leaves has depth four under this model. The reordering assumes expressions have no order-sensitive side effects or observable exceptions. Reassociating floating-point addition would require additional care because it can change floating-point rounding.

Fix sub to mean S,u,v↦S,u−v. If the depth-minimizing order evaluates the right operand f before the left operand e, the stack ends in S,f,e, so ordinary sub would compute the wrong sign. Two possible instruction set architecture extensions are:
  • Add swap, with S,u,v↦S,v,u. After computing f and then e, use swap; sub to obtain e−f. One general zero-address instruction handles every noncommutative binary operation and is useful elsewhere, but each reversed evaluation needs an extra instruction and extra execution time. It can be implemented without an additional operand stack slot.
  • Add reverse-operand variants, such as rsub with S,u,v↦S,v−u, and analogously reverse division and other required operations. After computing f and then e, a single rsub obtains e−f. This avoids the extra executed swap, but requires more opcodes and decoder support, usually one reverse form for each affected operation.
Both preserve the same peak operand stack bound:
deeper child first; use either operand swapping or a reverse-operand opcode.​
(51)
The first trades extra dynamic instructions for a smaller instruction set; the second trades a larger instruction set for fewer executed instructions. Neither extension makes order-sensitive side effects safe to reorder.

Disjunction elimination takes ϕ∨ψ and two subproofs of the same conclusion κ, one assuming ϕ and the other assuming ψ, and concludes κ. Implication introduction takes a subproof deriving ψ from a temporary assumption ϕ, and concludes ϕ→ψ.
The rectangular boxes mark the scopes of temporary assumptions. The disjunction elimination rule discharges its two case assumptions, while the implication introduction rule discharges the antecedent assumption. Other surrounding assumptions remain in force; intermediate results depending on the boxed assumptions cannot be exported arbitrarily. Schematically,

(52)
