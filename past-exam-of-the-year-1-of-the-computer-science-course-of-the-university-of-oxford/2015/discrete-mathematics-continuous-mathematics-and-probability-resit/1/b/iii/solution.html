With the convention N={0,1,2,…}, the even inputs 2j map to j for j≥0, while the odd inputs 2j+1 map to −(j+1) for j≥0. These two images of a function are disjoint and together equal Z. Within either class there are no repeated outputs. More explicitly the inverse function is
f−1(z)={2z,−2z−1,​z≥0,z<0.​​
(5)
Consequently f is both injective and surjective, hence a bijection. If instead N={1,2,…}, the input zero is absent: f remains injective but its image of a function is Z∖{0}, so it is not onto. The printed paper does not specify this convention; the zero-based convention gives the apparent intended bijection.
