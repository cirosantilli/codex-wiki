The standard three-case master theorem concerns
T(n)=aT(n/b)+f(n),a≥1,b>1,
(2)
with constant base-case cost and eventually nonnegative f. Put d=logb​a.
  • If f(n)=O(nd−ϵ) for some ϵ>0, then T(n)=Θ(nd).
  • If f(n)=Θ(nd), then T(n)=Θ(ndlogn).
  • If f(n)=Ω(nd+ϵ) for some ϵ>0 and af(n/b)≤cf(n) eventually for a constant c<1, then T(n)=Θ(f(n)).
In a divide-and-conquer algorithm, a counts recursive subproblems, n/b is their size, and f accounts for splitting and combining. The comparison with nlogb​a identifies whether leaves, all levels, or the upper levels dominate the running time. The polynomial gap and the third case's regularity condition are part of this version of the theorem.
