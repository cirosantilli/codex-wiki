The first value is the tournament winner M. Find the second value s by taking the maximum of the direct-loser set second(S), retaining its associated tournament record.
The third-largest value must have lost directly to either M or s: any other value is smaller and could not defeat it. Therefore it belongs to
C=(second(S)∖{s}) ∪ {r.key:r∈s.losers}.
(4)
Conversely, every value in C is distinct from M,s. Thus the third-largest value is maxC. This is tournament selection of the three largest elements; it uses the retained defeat records while computing the requested three-part partition.
Three largest elements exist only for n≥3. With the paper's power-of-two assumption this means n≥4. The partition in part (b) remains meaningful for n=2, but a third output cannot be supplied in that case.
