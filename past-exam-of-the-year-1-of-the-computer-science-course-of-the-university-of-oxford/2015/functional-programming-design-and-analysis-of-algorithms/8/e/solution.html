Process outgoing edges in topological order:
order = topological_sort(G)
for v in V:
    distance[v] = infinity
    predecessor[v] = none
distance[s] = 0

for u in order:
    if distance[u] != infinity:
        for edge (u,v) of weight w:
            if distance[u] + w < distance[v]:
                distance[v] = distance[u] + w
                predecessor[v] = u
For correctness, argue by induction through the topological sort. Every path ending at a vertex v=s reaches it through some incoming edge uv. All such predecessors u occur earlier, so their finalized shortest-path distances have already been considered when v is processed. Taking the minimum of distance(u)+w(u,v) over these edges therefore gives exactly the shortest-path distance to v. Unreachable vertices retain infinity. Negative edge weights cause no difficulty because a later vertex cannot lead back to an earlier one.
This is the DAG shortest-path algorithm. Topological sorting and the single pass of edge relaxation each take O(∣V∣+∣E∣) time with adjacency lists; distances, predecessors and the order use O(∣V∣) auxiliary space. Following predecessors reconstructs a shortest path to any reachable target.
