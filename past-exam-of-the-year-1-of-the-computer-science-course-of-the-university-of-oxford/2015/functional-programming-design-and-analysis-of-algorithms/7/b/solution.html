In a bipartite graph, successive vertices along an edge lie in opposite parts. Following a cycle alternates parts at each step, so returning to the starting part requires an even number of edges. Thus a bipartite graph contains no odd cycle.
Conversely, choose a rooted spanning forest and colour each vertex by the parity of its depth in its component's tree. Suppose an edge uv had equally coloured endpoints. The unique tree path from u to v has length
depth(u)+depth(v)−2depth(LCA(u,v)),
(9)
which is even. Adjoining uv makes an odd cycle. Therefore, if there are no odd cycles, every edge joins opposite colours, which supplies the required bipartition.
G is bipartite⟺G has no odd cycle.​

This is the odd-cycle characterization of bipartite graphs. The argument applies separately to every connected component.
