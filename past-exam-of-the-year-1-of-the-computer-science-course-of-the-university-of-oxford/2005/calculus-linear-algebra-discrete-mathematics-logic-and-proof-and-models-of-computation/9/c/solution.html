A context-free grammar is a quadruple (N,Σ,P,S): N is a finite set of nonterminal symbols, Σ is a disjoint finite alphabet of terminal symbols, S∈N is the start symbol, and P is a finite set of productions A→α, with A∈N and α∈(N∪Σ)∗. Its formal language consists of the terminal strings derivable from S.
For the even-length palindromes in (b)(ii), take terminal alphabet {0,1} and the context-free grammar
S→0S0∣1S1∣ε.​
(66)
Every production adds identical symbols at the two ends, so every generated string is an even-length palindrome. Conversely, remove the matching outer pair of any nonempty even-length palindrome and repeat; this constructs its derivation, ending with ε.
For the unequal-block formal language in (b)(iv), use
SAB​→A∣B,→0A1∣Z,→0B1∣O,​ZO​→0Z∣0,→1O∣1.​​
(67)
After k uses of A→0A1, and j≥1 zeros from Z, the resulting string is 0k+j1k, so its zero count is larger. Every such unequal pair with m>n occurs by choosing k=n,j=m−n. Similarly B generates exactly 0k1k+j with j≥1, covering precisely m<n. The start alternatives cover both possibilities and never equal counts. If positive exponents are required, replace the start productions by S→0A1∣0B1, which adds one matched pair and ensures both counts are positive.
