A particularly transparent design uses six D flip-flops in a one-hot encoding. Let Qi​=1 mean that face i is displayed. Initialize Q1​=1 and Q2​=⋯=Q6​=0, and wire the next-state inputs as a ring:
D1​=Q6​,D2​=Q1​,D3​=Q2​,D4​=Q3​,D5​=Q4​,D6​=Q5​.​
(39)
All six D flip-flops share the same active clock edge. Each pulse moves the single stored 1 to the next position, giving the state-transition diagram cycle 1→2→3→4→5→6→1. The representation invariant “exactly one Qi​ is high” holds after initialization and is preserved by rotation. Six D flip-flops are more than the three-bit theoretical minimum, but their simple wiring gives a complete implementation without requiring that minimum.
Decode the seven lamps by their positions, with T,M,B denoting top, middle and bottom, and L,R denoting left and right. Let LC​ be the center lamp. In the original figure, face 2 uses top-left and bottom-right, while face 3 uses top-right and bottom-left plus center; the two diagonals must therefore have different controls.[ref] The required OR gate decoder is
LTL​=LBR​LTR​=LBL​LML​=LMR​LC​​=Q2​∨Q4​∨Q5​∨Q6​,=Q3​∨Q4​∨Q5​∨Q6​,=Q6​,=Q1​∨Q3​∨Q5​.​​
(40)
For example, only the center is high in state Q1​, the first diagonal is high in state Q2​, and in state Q6​ both outer columns are high while the center is low. This verifies all six layouts directly.
To implement the switch behavior, use a rapid common clock signal and a sampled enable S, high while the switch is pressed. Each next-state input is then selected between its advancing value and its own stored value:
Di​=(S∧Qprev(i)​)∨(S∧Qi​),prev(1)=6,prev(i)=i−1 (i>1).​
(41)
One multiplexer per D flip-flop implements that selection. While enabled the electronic dice advances; otherwise every D flip-flop reloads its own value and the display holds. The enable supplied to this synchronous design must be stable at sampling edges. A reset or preset facility must establish the specified one-hot initial state: an all-zero ring does not start itself.
With a rapid clock signal, uncertainty in human release timing makes the final face practically unpredictable. The sequential circuit itself is deterministic; this observation alone is not a proof that its six outcomes have equal probabilities.
