A D flip-flop stores one bit. At its specified active clock edge, it samples D and, after its clock-to-Q delay, makes Q equal to that sampled value. Between active clock edges it retains the stored value. The input must meet the setup and hold time requirements around the sampling edge; a level-sensitive latch would have different behavior.
For a finite-state sequential circuit, encode the present state in a bank of D flip-flops. Feed their outputs q and the external inputs u to combinational logic computing the next-state function F(q,u), and connect those outputs to the flip-flop D inputs. A common clock signal then performs the simultaneous update
q+=F(q,u).​
(38)
Output combinational logic computes H(q) for a Moore machine or H(q,u) for a Mealy machine. Since any finite next-state and output table is a Boolean function, it has a Boolean circuit implementation. Initialization specifies the starting state, and unused state encodings require either a recovery rule or an initialization assumption. The clock period must accommodate propagation through the D flip-flops and intervening combinational logic before the next sampling edge.
