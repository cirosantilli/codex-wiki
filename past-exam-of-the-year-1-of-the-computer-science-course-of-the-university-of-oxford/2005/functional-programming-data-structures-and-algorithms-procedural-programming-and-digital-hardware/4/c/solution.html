In the coin change problem, take denominations {1,3,4} and target amount 6. The natural greedy algorithm repeatedly chooses the largest denomination not exceeding the remaining amount. It chooses 4, then 1, then 1, for three coins. Yet 3+3=6 uses only two.
Two is optimal: one coin cannot make 6 because denomination 6 is absent, and the displayed pair attains the lower bound. Therefore
greedy: 4+1+1 (3 coins);optimal: 3+3 (2 coins).​
(15)
The locally largest first coin loses the possibility of using the better pair of threes. This supplies a concrete failure of the greedy-choice property; optimal substructure alone does not justify the greedy choice. The dynamic programming recurrence from part (b) would compare all feasible first or last denominations and find the pair.
