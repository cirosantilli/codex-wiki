Under the same scope convention, g(t,z) is not free for substitution for y. The occurrence of y inside Q(f(x,y),f(z,z)) is free but lies under ∃t, so its replacement would capture the new t. Rename this binder to fresh s. Substitute only for the free y occurrences in the first conjunct:
∀x(P(x,g(t,z),z)∨∃s(Q(z,s)∧Q(f(x,g(t,z)),f(z,z))))∧∃yQ(y,y).​
(51)
The two occurrences of y in the final conjunct are bound variables and remain unchanged. The inserted t,z remain free.
If instead one gives the printed ∃t only the scope Q(z,t), this part's free-for-substitution answer becomes yes, because neither free y occurrence then lies under that binder. Under that alternative parsing, the result is explicitly
∀x(P(x,g(t,z),z)∨((∃tQ(z,t))∧Q(f(x,g(t,z)),f(z,z))))∧∃yQ(y,y).
(52)
The narrower reading does not change the negative answer in (1): its occurrence of z inside Q(z,t) still requires renaming that binder before substitution. The broad-scope answers above give a single consistent parsing across the paper.
