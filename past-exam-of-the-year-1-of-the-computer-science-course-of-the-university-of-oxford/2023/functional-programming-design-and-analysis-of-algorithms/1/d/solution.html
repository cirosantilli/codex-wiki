tails is not a direct list fold. Every foldr c z is strict in its list argument, but tails undefined = undefined : undefined has a defined outer constructor. Applying null distinguishes this result from ⊥.
ptails is not a direct list fold either. On a type with distinct values u and v,
ptails[u]=[[]]=ptails[v].
(2)
A fold step given the same new head x and these equal folded tails must return the same result. However,
ptails[x,u]=[[u],[]]=[[v],[]]=ptails[x,v].
(3)
Thus the tail information needed by the next step does not factor through ptails; this violates the fold factorisation criterion even on finite total lists.
ntails is a direct list fold:
ntailsFold :: [a] -> [[a]]
ntailsFold = foldr step []
  where
    step x yss = (x : original yss) : yss
    original [] = []
    original (ys:_) = ys
When the tail is nonempty, its first nonempty suffix of a list is the tail itself; otherwise it is recovered as []. Hence original (ntailsFold xs) = xs. The inner case distinction is delayed beneath constructors, so the definition also preserves the required behaviour on partial and infinite lazy lists. Building a suffix head does not first demand the whole folded tail.
