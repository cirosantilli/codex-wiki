The Euclidean algorithm repeatedly replaces (ri−1​,ri​) by (ri​,ri−1​−qi​ri​), where the new remainder lies between 0 and ri​−1. Common divisors are unchanged by this subtraction, and decreasing positive remainders ensure termination. The last nonzero remainder is the greatest common divisor.
The extended Euclidean algorithm also tracks coefficients ri​=ui​a+vi​b, beginning with coefficient pairs (1,0) and (0,1) and applying the same subtraction to the pairs. This invariant produces Bézout coefficients for the last nonzero remainder. Here the divisions are
580=2⋅252+76,252=3⋅76+24,76=3⋅24+4,24=6⋅4.
(6)
Back substitution gives
4=76−3⋅24=10⋅76−3⋅252=10⋅580−23⋅252.
(7)
Thus gcd(580,252)=4, with m=10 and n=−23 in Bézout's identity.
