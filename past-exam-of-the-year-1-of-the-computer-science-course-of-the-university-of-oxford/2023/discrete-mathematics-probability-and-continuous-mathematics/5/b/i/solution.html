Interpret the norms as Euclidean norms and set B=ATA. This is a real symmetric matrix with xTBx=∥Ax∥2≥0, hence a positive semidefinite matrix. Squaring the nonnegative objective does not change its argmin. Let λmin​ be the smallest eigenvalue of B and Emin​ its eigenspace.
First minimize on the unit sphere. The Lagrange multiplier function L(x,λ)=xTBx−λ(xTx−1) has stationarity condition Bx=λx. Multiplication by xT shows that the squared objective at such a point is λ. The spectral theorem for real symmetric matrices also gives, for every unit vector, xTBx≥λmin​, with equality exactly in Emin​. This proves global minimality, rather than merely finding stationary candidates.
If λmin​>0, every point with ∥x∥>1 has a strictly smaller objective after rescaling to unit length. Therefore
∥x∥≥1argmin​∥Ax∥={x∈Emin​:∥x∥=1},min=λmin​​.​
(14)
If λmin​=0, the kernel of a linear map satisfies kerB=kerA, since Bx=0 implies ∥Ax∥2=0, and the converse is immediate. The minimum is then zero, attained precisely at
{x∈kerA:∥x∥≥1}.​
(15)
In this case minimizers need not have unit length. This distinction is essential for a rank-deficient A, including any real m×n matrix with m<n. The result is the minimum image norm outside a Euclidean unit ball, governed by the least singular value.
