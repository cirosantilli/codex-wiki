Let Ai​ be the event that candidate i succeeds. The countable additivity axiom adds probabilities only for mutually disjoint events. Successes of different candidates are not mutually exclusive, so replacing the probability of their union by ∑i​P(Ai​)=1 misuses that axiom. Without disjointness one obtains only the union bound, not certainty. The same sum is the expected value of the number of successes, which also need not imply at least one.
If the ten outcomes are mutually independent events, with each success probability 0.1, all fail with probability 0.910. Hence
P(at least one success)=1−0.910=0.6513215599….​

The independence assumption is essential. The marginal probabilities alone allow any union probability from 0.1 up to 1; perfectly overlapping success events give the lower endpoint and disjoint successes give the upper endpoint.
