Conditioning on Z=z has positive probability exactly when z=2r+1 for an integer r≥0. This is the same event as X+Y=r. The addition of independent Poisson random variables gives a Poisson distribution with parameter λ+μ for the sum. Therefore, for 0≤t≤r,
P(X=t∣Z=2r+1)​=e−(λ+μ)(λ+μ)r/r!e−(λ+μ)λtμr−t/[t!(r−t)!]​=(tr​)(λ+μλ​)t(λ+μμ​)r−t.​
(53)
Thus X∣{Z=2r+1}∼Binomial(r,λ/(λ+μ)). The case r=0 is a constant zero random variable. For even, nonpositive, or noninteger z, the conditioning event has zero probability, so an event-conditional probability distribution is not specified by the quotient formula. This is a two-component case of Poisson-multinomial conditioning.
