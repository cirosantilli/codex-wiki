Let d=gcd(c,n), c=dc′, and n=dn′. The preceding part gives gcd(c′,n′)=1. The integer congruence on the left is equivalent to
n∣c(a−b)⟺n′∣c′(a−b).
(6)
By Euclid's lemma, coprimality of c′ and n′ makes this equivalent to n′∣a−b. The reverse implication is also immediate by multiplying a divisibility relation by dc′. Therefore
ac≡bc(modn)⟺a≡b(modn/gcd(c,n)).​
(7)
This is cancellation of a common factor in a congruence. The printed equality sign in the right-hand expression is interpreted as equality of residue classes, hence the displayed integer congruence. Ordinary equality of the integers a,b would be false: the following example has a=b.
