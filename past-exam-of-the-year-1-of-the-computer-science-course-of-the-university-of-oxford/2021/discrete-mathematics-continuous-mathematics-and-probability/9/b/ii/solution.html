Let ti​=Ei​[X]. Time is accumulated in each transient chamber before the next move; arrival at either terminal state ends the trial immediately. Thus t0​=tk​=0. A block of k identical rightward choices forces absorption, so the geometric tail bound from a uniform escape probability gives finite expected value of X.
First-step analysis now gives
ti​=1+21​ti−1​+21​ti+1​,1≤i≤k−1.
(72)
The quadratic ti​=i(k−i) has the correct boundary values and second difference ti+1​−2ti​+ti−1​=−2, so it solves this system. The difference of two solutions would be linear in i with zero values at both endpoints, hence zero. Therefore
Ei​[X]=i(k−i) minutes.​
(73)
This is the expected duration of symmetric gambler's ruin. It includes the first minute in the starting transient chamber and does not add a minute after absorption.
