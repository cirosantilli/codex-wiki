For a remaining tree T, define the potential
L(∅)=0,L(T)=∣T.left∣+L(T.right).
(10)
This counts the nodes not on the right spine, so 0≤L(T)≤∣T∣. Consider a right rotation with old root y, left child x, and three untouched subtrees A,B,C. Before the rotation the potential is
1+∣A∣+∣B∣+L(C).
(11)
After the rotation it is ∣A∣+∣B∣+L(C), so each rotation decreases L by exactly one while preserving the number of remaining nodes. This supplies a nonnegative decreasing bound for the inner loop.
When the root has no left child, printing it and advancing to its right child leaves L unchanged and decreases the remaining node count by one. Thus ∣T∣+L(T) decreases on every rotation and every removal, proving termination of the whole procedure. Over the entire execution there are exactly N printed nodes and exactly L(T0​) rotations, since the final potential is zero. Both quantities are at most N, so the running time is Θ(N) for a nonempty tree, with constant auxiliary space. The nested syntax does not imply quadratic time: it is the total potential decrease across all iterations that bounds the inner work. The potential is a proof device and is not computed by the running program.
