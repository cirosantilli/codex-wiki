 ... Past exam of the year 1 of the computer science course of the University of Oxford 2021 Digital systems, linear algebra, introduction to formal proof 7 b ii
OurBigBook.com Words: 208
Write D(ϕ,ψ)=¬(¬ϕ∧¬ψ). We derive the two disjunction introduction rules and the disjunction elimination rule without using any disjunction rule inside the derivations.
For left introduction, start with ϕ and temporarily assume ¬ϕ∧¬ψ. The conjunction elimination rule gives ¬ϕ, and the negation elimination rule gives ⊥. Discharge the temporary conjunction by the negation introduction rule, concluding D(ϕ,ψ). For right introduction, start with ψ and use the right conjunction elimination rule to obtain ¬ψ instead. Thus either input derives the required encoded disjunction.
For elimination, suppose D(ϕ,ψ) is available, together with a derivation of κ from an additional assumption ϕ and a derivation of κ from an additional assumption ψ. Keep their common external context fixed. Temporarily assume ¬κ under label a. Temporarily assume ϕ under label b and insert the supplied first branch derivation to obtain κ. Negation elimination rule gives ⊥, so negation introduction rule discharges b and gives ¬ϕ. Repeat with a temporary assumption ψ under label c and the supplied second branch derivation, discharging c to obtain ¬ψ.
Conjunction introduction rule now gives ¬ϕ∧¬ψ. Its negation D(ϕ,ψ) gives ⊥ by negation elimination rule. The supplied reductio ad absurdum rule discharges a and yields κ. This discharges the two branch assumptions as required and proves the encoded elimination rule. Its last step uses classical propositional logic; the same encoded elimination principle is not generally available in Intuitionistic propositional logic.
