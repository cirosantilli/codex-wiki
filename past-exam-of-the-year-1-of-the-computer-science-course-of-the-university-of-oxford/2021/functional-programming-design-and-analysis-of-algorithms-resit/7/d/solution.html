Take reachability to include a path of length zero. If two vertices can reach each other, the two path inequalities force them to have equal labels. Conversely, equal labels force mutual reachability: a missing path in either direction would require a strict inequality. Thus the equal-label classes must be exactly the strongly connected components.
Two different components must also be comparable by reachability. If neither could reach the other, applying the no-path condition in both directions would require both ℓ(a)>ℓ(b) and ℓ(b)>ℓ(a). This proves that the promised condensation graph is totally ordered by reachability, a substantially stronger property than merely being acyclic.

A path goes within one component or forward in this order, so its labels are nondecreasing. Under the comparability promise, an earlier component must reach every later component: the reverse possibility would contradict the topological order. Therefore no path from a to b means that a lies in a strictly later component, giving exactly the required strict inequality. This proves both parts of the specification.
For a new example, use vertices u,v,w,x,y, edges u→v,v→u,v→w,w→x,x→y,y→x. Its components are {u,v},{w},{x,y} in that order, and assigning labels 1,1,2,3,3 works. Every earlier component reaches each later one through the displayed chain.
Component computation, scanning original edges to build the condensation graph, topological sorting, and expanding component labels all take O(∣V∣+∣E∣). Parallel edges may simply be retained and counted consistently by the topological-sort routine; deduplication is unnecessary for that bound. An empty graph returns the empty mapping.
