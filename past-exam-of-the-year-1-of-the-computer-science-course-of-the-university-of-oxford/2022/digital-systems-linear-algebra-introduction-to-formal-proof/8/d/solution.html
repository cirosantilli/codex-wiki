Use the intended first-order structure of real arithmetic, with Int selecting the integers; z is the parameter in ϕz​. A suitable first-order formula is
ϕz​=∃r(r∗r=(1+1)∗z ∧ ¬∃p∃q(Int(p)∧Int(q)∧¬(q=0)∧q∗r=p)).​
(36)
The first condition requires a real square root of 2z. The second excludes every representation as a rational number p/q with a nonzero integer denominator. This uses only the listed function and constant symbols, the Int predicate and logical equality; in particular, 2 is written 1+1 and there is no extra division symbol. A square root exists exactly when z≥0, and either sign of a root is a rational number exactly when the other sign is a rational number, so the absence of a positivity condition on r does not affect the claim about the principal square root. At z=0 the formula is false; for z<0 there is no real witness. The meanings of + and ∗ as real arithmetic, not just the meaning of Int, are part of this interpretation.
