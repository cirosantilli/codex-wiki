Suppose first that f=O(g). Choose C>0 and N so that ∣f(n)∣≤C∣g(n)∣ for n≥N. There are only finitely many natural numbers below N. Because g(n)=0 at each of them, all ratios ∣f(n)∣/∣g(n)∣ in this finite prefix are finite. Let
c=max({C}∪{∣g(n)∣∣f(n)∣​:n<N}).
(7)
Then ∣f(n)∣≤c∣g(n)∣ both in the prefix and in the tail, hence on the entire domain of a function. This proves the finite-prefix extension of a big O bound.
Conversely, a bound valid for every natural number is certainly valid eventually. The purported real constant cannot be negative because g never vanishes; if it is zero, enlarge it to a positive constant. The definition of Big O notation then applies. The nonvanishing assumption is what permits the finite exceptions to be absorbed into one constant.
