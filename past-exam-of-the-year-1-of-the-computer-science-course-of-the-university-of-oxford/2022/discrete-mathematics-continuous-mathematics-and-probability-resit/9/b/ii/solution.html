The same integral works with both powers shifted:
E[Xs(1−X)t]​=Γ(α)Γ(β)Γ(α+β)​∫01​xα+s−1(1−x)β+t−1dx=Γ(α)Γ(β)Γ(α+β+s+t)Γ(α+β)Γ(α+s)Γ(β+t)​​.​

The assumptions s>−α and t>−β say precisely that the exponents at each endpoint are greater than −1, ensuring integrability there. Values of a negative power at the endpoints themselves can be assigned arbitrarily, because those endpoints have probability zero. This establishes the mixed power moments of a beta distribution without requiring s and t to be integers.[ref]
