Describe a branching vertex by the first-order logic sentence
β=∃x∃y∃z[E(x,y)∧E(x,z)∧y=z],
(31)
and the existence of a sink by δ=∃x∀y¬E(x,y). The directed path has sinks but no branching vertex; the directed cycle has neither sinks nor branching vertices; the directed arborescence has a branching vertex. Thus the following three sentences distinguish the named structures in order:
φ1​=δ∧¬β,φ2​=∀x∃yE(x,y),φ3​=β.​
(32)
Here β,δ are abbreviations for the displayed formulas; replacing them gives sentences in the single-relation first-order signature {E}. Explicitly, φ1​ says that some vertex has no successor and that no vertex has two distinct successors. The truth table across the three structures is:

The conditions distinguish these three finite structures; they are not claimed to characterize all directed paths, cycles or trees among arbitrary directed graphs. Only local out-degrees and the presence of a sink are needed, without quantifying over paths of unbounded length.
