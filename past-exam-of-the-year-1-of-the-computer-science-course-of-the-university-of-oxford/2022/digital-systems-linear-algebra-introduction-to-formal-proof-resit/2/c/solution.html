A 32-bit free-running timer operates modulo M=232. If its previous reading is p, and the actual elapsed count is d with 0≤d<M, the new reading is (p+d)modM. The modulo-counter elapsed-time identity therefore gives
(uint32_t)(now−previous_tick)=d.​
(1)
Unsigned integer arithmetic in C reduces the subtraction modulo 232, so the identity remains valid when a wrap occurs between pulses. For example, readings 232−8000 and 8000 give 16,000 ticks, hence one millisecond, rather than a negative interval.[ref]
At the specified clock rate, the true wrap period is 232/16,000,000=268.435456 seconds. The printed 256 seconds is inconsistent with the other two specifications. The implementation uses the stated 32-bit width and 16 MHz rate. Assuming consecutive handled pulses are less than 256 seconds apart is a sufficient conservative bound; the exact allowable bound is less than 268.435456 seconds.
This calculation requires less than one entire timer modulus of elapsed ticks between successive stored timestamps. If a whole wrap or several wraps elapse, identical register readings cannot reveal how many occurred; an overflow counter or an independent longer-range clock would be needed. Also assume pulse events are not lost and interrupt latency is constant or sufficiently small for the required accuracy: timestamps read in software measure handler-to-handler intervals, not perfect hardware capture times. Conversion to milliseconds occurs after modular subtraction, so separately rounded absolute timestamps cannot corrupt the wrap calculation.
