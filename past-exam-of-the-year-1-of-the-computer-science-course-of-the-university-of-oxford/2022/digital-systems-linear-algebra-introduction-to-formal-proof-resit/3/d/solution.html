Use a synchronous sequential circuit with four Moore machine states. R is ready, P is the one-cycle pulse state, D is disabled with no qualifying final zero, and Z is disabled after one sampled zero. Reset into R, so an initial sampled high generates a pulse immediately. The complete transition/output table is:

Both D flip-flops share the clock; their outputs feed the displayed logic gates. The output depends only on the stored state, not combinationally on the external input. At each edge, evaluate the right-hand sides using the old state and the sampled input, then update both flip-flops together. The resulting z applies throughout the following clock period.
From R, a high enters P. The next edge always leaves P, so the pulse lasts exactly one period. In the disabled states, a high leads to D and erases progress towards rearming. A zero enters Z, and a second successive zero enters R. A later high can then enter P. This proves both the two-zero requirement and the startup exception. For example, sampled inputs 1,1,0,1,0,0,1,0,0,1 yield outputs 1,0,0,0,0,0,1,0,0,1.
The construction is a synchronous pulse detector with two-zero rearming. Assume a meets setup/hold requirements at the sampling edges. A genuinely asynchronous input would first require synchronization, with its associated latency; that is separate from this sampled-input specification.
