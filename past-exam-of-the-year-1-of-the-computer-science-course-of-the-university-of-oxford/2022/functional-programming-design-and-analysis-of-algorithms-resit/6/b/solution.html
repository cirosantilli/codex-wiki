Capacity alone cannot say which items have already been used. For the 0-1 knapsack problem, let D(i,c) use only the first i items, each at most once. Then
D(0,c)=0,D(i,c)={D(i−1,c),max{D(i−1,c), vi​+D(i−1,c−wi​)},​wi​>c,wi​≤c.​​
(7)
The two alternatives omit item i or take it. In the latter case its remaining companions must solve the smaller problem using the first i−1 items; otherwise replacing them would improve the whole selection. Both branches reduce the item index, so taking item i cannot take it again. This establishes the recurrence relation and optimal substructure.
Fill rows in increasing i and return D(n,W). There are O(nW) entries with constant work each. Store the decisions for reconstruction; value-only computation uses O(W) space by keeping two rows, or by scanning capacities downwards in a single row. A forward scan in a single row would instead allow repetitions. Nonnegative integral weights suffice for this version, including zero-weight items, because the item index always decreases.
