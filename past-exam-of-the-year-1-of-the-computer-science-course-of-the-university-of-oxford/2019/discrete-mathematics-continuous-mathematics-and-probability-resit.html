Solution
Words: 239
ii OurBigBook.com
Write P for the prime numbers in A and C=A∖P for its nonprime elements. Since n≥4, both antichains have at least two elements: 2,3∈P and 1,4∈C. Every element of P is below every element of C. We can classify a subset S by its intersection with these two levels of the two-level partial order.
  • If S=∅, every element of A is an upper bound. There is no maximum, because S has no elements, and no least upper bound, because the distinct elements 2,3 are incomparable minimal elements of A.
  • If S={p} with p∈P, its upper bounds are {p}∪C; its maximum and least upper bound are both p.
  • If S⊆P has at least two elements, its upper bounds are exactly C. There is no maximum in S and no least upper bound, because C has at least two incomparable elements.
  • If S∩C={c}, its only upper bound is c: no different element is above this nonprime element. Since every remaining element of S is a prime number, c belongs to S and dominates all of it. Thus c is both its maximum and its least upper bound.
  • If S contains at least two elements of C, these distinct nonprime elements have no common upper bound, and S has neither a maximum nor a least upper bound.
Consequently an upper bound exists exactly when ∣S∩C∣≤1; a maximum or least upper bound exists exactly when ∣S∩C∣=1 or S is a singleton subset of P. The exhaustive cases also justify why the last two existence criteria coincide here.

For iterates approaching x∗​, write ek​=∥xk​−x∗​∥. Exact order of convergence p means ek+1​/ekp​→C for a finite constant C>0, with nonzero errors. Thus quadratic convergence has p=2, and cubic convergence has p=3. The weaker eventual bound ek+1​≤Cekp​ means at least order p. Finite termination is a separate case.[ref]
The assertion about being always faster is false. For example, the fixed-point iterations with errors ek+1​=ek2​ and dk+1​=50dk3​ have exact quadratic convergence and cubic convergence, respectively. Starting both at 0.1, their next errors are 0.01 and 0.05: the cubic convergence method is worse on that step, although both converge. Error constants, starting points, and work per step all matter. The higher order of convergence describes asymptotic behavior within a convergence region, not a uniform comparison of all computations.
