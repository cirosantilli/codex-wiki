Put J={k∈N:k≤100}, avoiding any dependence on whether the convention includes 0 among the natural numbers. Under set inclusion, the results are
FamilyMaximumLeast upper bound in A
S1​NoneNone
S2​JJ
S1​∩S2​NoneJ
The set union of all three-element subsets of N is N. An upper bound for S1​ under set inclusion would have to contain this entire infinite set, whereas members of A are finite. Thus S1​ has no upper bound and hence no maximum or least upper bound.
For S2​, every member is a subset of the finite set J, and J itself belongs to S2​, making it both maximum and least upper bound. The union of the three-element subsets of J is again J, so J is the least upper bound of S1​∩S2​. It is not a member of that family, and no three-element member contains all the others, so there is no maximum.
