There are six functions: the four power maps x↦xk for k=1,2,3,4, and the two constant maps 0,1. All calculations in this part are in the finite field Z5​.
Put e=f(1). Multiplicativity gives e2=e, so the absence of zero divisors forces e=0 or e=1. If e=0, then f(x)=f(x)f(1)=0 for every x, giving only the constant-zero map.
Suppose e=1. For nonzero x, the equality f(x)f(x−1)=1 shows f(x)=0. The nonzero elements form a cyclic group generated by 2, in the order 1,2,4,3. This is the special case with five elements of multiplicative self-maps of a finite field.

Solution

ii OurBigBook.com
The initial values are d1​=0 and d2​=1: one element cannot move, while two elements must be swapped.
For n≥3, fix one distinguished element, called n, and choose its image j=n, in n−1 ways. If n,j form a two-cycle, deleting them leaves an arbitrary derangement on the other n−2 elements, giving dn−2​ choices.
Otherwise the permutation cycle containing n has length at least three. Remove n from that cycle, joining its predecessor directly to j. No fixed point is created because the predecessor is not j. This gives a derangement on n−1 elements. Conversely, any such derangement uniquely recovers the original one by inserting n immediately before j in its cycle. This case contributes dn−1​ choices for each j.
The two cases are disjoint and exhaustive, so the derangement recurrence is
dn​=(n−1)(dn−1​+dn−2​).​
(11)

An upper bound of S is an element u∈A with s⪯u for every s∈S. It need not belong to S. A maximum, or greatest element, is an m∈S with that same property. A least upper bound is an upper bound ℓ∈A satisfying ℓ⪯u for every upper bound u.
A maximum belongs to S; a least upper bound need not. In a partially ordered set, an existing maximum is automatically the least upper bound, and the antisymmetric relation condition makes each unique.
