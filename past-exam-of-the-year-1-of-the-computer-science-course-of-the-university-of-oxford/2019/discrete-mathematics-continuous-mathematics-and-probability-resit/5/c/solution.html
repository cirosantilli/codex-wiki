Choose equation (1), evaluated by refined composite Simpson's rule. It gives y directly as a one-dimensional definite integral with fixed endpoints and a smooth integrand. It therefore avoids root finding altogether. Before evaluating, use the trigonometric identity
sin(x−acosx)+sin(x+acosx)=2sinxcos(acosx),a=π/4.
(25)
This equivalent integrand is nonnegative throughout [0,π/2], avoiding cancellation between the original two terms. Equation (2) instead subtracts two potentially large exponential functions, while equation (3) would ordinarily put a numerical integration inside a root finding loop.
For even n, let h=(π/2)/n, ϕ(x)=2sinxcos((π/4)cosx), and compute
Yn​=4π​3h​[ϕ(0)+ϕ(π/2)+4∑1≤j<nj odd​​ϕ(jh)+2∑2≤j<nj even​​ϕ(jh)].
(26)
Then double n. The integrand has bounded derivatives of every fixed order on the integration interval; in the fourth-order error regime, the estimated remaining truncation error in Y2n​ is ∣Y2n​−Yn​∣/15. The factor 15 follows by eliminating the leading term Ch4 between the two mesh widths.[ref]
Stop when this estimate is comparable to εmach​max(1,∣Y2n​∣) and further refinement shows stagnation at the roundoff error scale. Check that the differences previously decreased consistently with fourth-order convergence; differences alone are not a rigorous error certificate if cancellation or roundoff error dominates. Compensated summation helps keep the summation error near the precision limit. Refining indefinitely after this point increases work without reliably adding meaningful digits. This answers the requested choice and termination strategy without requiring an explicit value of y.
