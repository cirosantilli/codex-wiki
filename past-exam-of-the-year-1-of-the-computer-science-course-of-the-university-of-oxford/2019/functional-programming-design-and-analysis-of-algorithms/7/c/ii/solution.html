Use the multiplicative score exactly as defined in the paper. Let Pt​(v) be the largest score of a matching length-t route ending at v. Initialize P0​(v0​)=1 and mark all other entries unreachable. For each stage, clear the new layer and inspect matching edges from reachable entries:
Pt​(v)=max(u,v)∈E, σ(u,v)=xt​u reachable at stage t−1​​Pt−1​(u)p(u,v).
(8)
An empty candidate set is marked unreachable, rather than given score zero: a genuine route may have score zero. A Boolean reachability table or a sentinel −1 with a guard before multiplication distinguishes the cases. Store the maximizing predecessor edge at each reachable layer entry, retaining one on a tie.
Multiplication by a nonnegative edge factor preserves order, so replacing a prefix by a larger-scoring matching prefix cannot worsen its extension. Induction proves this maximum-product edge-labelled walk recurrence correct. Choose a reachable final entry of greatest score and follow its layer-indexed predecessors backwards to reconstruct the answer. If no final entry is reachable, report that no matching route exists, even though all numeric scores might otherwise look like zero.
The running time is O(k(∣V∣+∣E∣)), and rolling scores have space complexity O(∣V∣), while retaining all reconstruction pointers gives space complexity O(k∣V∣). For the vertex-simple interpretation, retain the visited set in each state as in part i, prohibit revisits and maximize the same products over those states. This exact exponential-state variant preserves both the zero-score distinction and predecessor reconstruction. If the starting vertex is unrestricted rather than inherited from part i, initialize P0​(v)=1 for every vertex. The calculation needs no independence assumption beyond the paper's definition of a product score; repeated physical edge-activation events would require a separate probabilistic model.
