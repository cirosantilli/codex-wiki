If n=1, return its sole index without a query. Otherwise first evaluate f(t,t,…,t) for t=1,…,d until two different outputs are found. Part i says these outputs are precisely fi​(t), so two values a,b with fi​(a)=fi​(b) must exist. This uses at most d calls to the function-evaluation oracle.
Maintain a candidate index set S, initially all indices. Split S into two nearly equal pieces and choose one, H. Query the input that is b on H and a everywhere else. Its output equals fi​(b) exactly when i∈H, and otherwise equals fi​(a). Retain the indicated half and repeat until one index remains.
The loop invariant is i∈S, and the witness outputs keep each test unambiguous. There are at most ⌈log2​n⌉ splitting queries. The total is at most d+⌈log2​n⌉ evaluations, which is O(dlogn) for n≥2, and in fact a stronger additive bound. Only oracle calls are being counted; explicitly constructing each query vector can require O(n) ordinary work. This is identification of a unique essential variable.
