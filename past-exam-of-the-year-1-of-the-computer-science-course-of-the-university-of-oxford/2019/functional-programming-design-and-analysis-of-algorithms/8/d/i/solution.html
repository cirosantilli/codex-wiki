OurBigBook.com Words: 107
Sort the two dimension vectors separately into ascending order, a1​≤⋯≤ad​ and b1​≤⋯≤bd​. Then return true exactly when ai​<bi​ for every i. Sorting costs O(dlogd) and the coordinate comparisons cost O(d).
Sufficiency follows by matching corresponding ranks and translating that rank matching back to the original axes. For necessity, suppose ak​≥bk​ for some k. The d−k+1 dimensions ak​,…,ad​ must all be assigned to strictly larger target dimensions. None of the first k sorted target dimensions is large enough, leaving at most d−k eligible slots. The pigeonhole principle gives a contradiction. This proves the sorted-coordinate criterion for box containment, including ties: equality fails the required strict fit.
