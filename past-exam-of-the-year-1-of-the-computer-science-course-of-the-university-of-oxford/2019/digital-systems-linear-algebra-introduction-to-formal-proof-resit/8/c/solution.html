For readability write R(s,t) for the uninterpreted relation s∋t. In the first proof, introduce the universal quantifier inside the existential witness scope:
LineFormulaJustification
1∃y∀xR(x,y)Premise
2∀xR(x,w)Assume with fresh witness name w
3R(u,w)Universal quantifier elimination rule, 2; choose fresh arbitrary u
4∃yR(u,y)Existential quantifier introduction rule, 3
5∀x∃yR(x,y)Universal quantifier introduction rule, 4; u is absent from open assumptions
6∀x∃yR(x,y)Existential quantifier elimination rule, 1 and 2–5
In the second proof, first close the witness scope and then generalize the arbitrary object:
LineFormulaJustification
1∃y∀xR(x,y)Premise; choose u arbitrary and fresh for this premise
2∀xR(x,w)Assume with fresh w, also different from u
3R(u,w)Universal quantifier elimination rule, 2
4∃yR(u,y)Existential quantifier introduction rule, 3
5∃yR(u,y)Existential quantifier elimination rule, 1 and 2–4
6∀x∃yR(x,y)Universal quantifier introduction rule, 5
These are distinct proof trees: the final two rule applications are interchanged, and their freshness conditions have been checked in their respective contexts. They express the quantifier order and uniform witness principle: a single witness working for every object also supplies a witness for each object individually.
The converse is not valid. In a domain {0,1} interpret R(s,t) as equality of its arguments. Every object has a witness, namely itself, but no one object equals both 0 and 1. The premise of the converse is true and its conclusion false. The symbol ∋ is an arbitrary binary relation here; no set-membership axioms may be imposed on it without an additional premise.
