Let Xj​ be the indicator random variable that coin j shows a head. Each expectation is EXj​=1/2, so linearity of expectation gives the expected number of heads:
E[j=1∑n​Xj​]=2n​.​
(41)
For the game, write pi​ for the probability that H eventually holds all n coins when starting with i. The states 0,n are absorbing, so p0​=0 and pn​=1. Conditioning on the first toss and using the law of total probability gives
pi​=21​pi−1​+21​pi+1​,1≤i≤n−1.
(42)
Thus pi+1​−pi​=pi​−pi−1​: successive differences are constant, and pi​=A+Bi. The boundary values give A=0, B=1/n, hence the gambler's ruin answer is
pi​=ni​.​
(43)
For completeness, the game terminates with probability one. From any nonabsorbing state, a run of n consecutive heads would reach n within the next n tosses, and that run has probability 2−n. Conditional on survival, the same bound applies to the next block. Therefore the probability of surviving kn tosses is at most (1−2−n)k, which tends to zero.
