If a square matrix A is an invertible matrix, multiplicativity of the determinant gives
1=detI=det(AA−1)=(detA)(detA−1),
(31)
so detA=0.
Conversely, suppose detA=0. Use Gaussian elimination to reduce A to row echelon form U. Each elementary row operation multiplies the determinant by a nonzero factor, so detU=0. If this square matrix had fewer than n pivots, its row echelon form would have a zero row and hence zero determinant, a contradiction. Therefore it has a pivot in every row and column, and further elementary row operations reduce it to I.
Each elementary row operation is multiplication on the left by an invertible matrix, its inverse being the reverse operation. Thus Ek​⋯E1​A=I implies A=E1−1​⋯Ek−1​, a product of invertible matrices. Hence
A is invertible⟺detA=0.​
