Write z(x,y)=Z(r,s) in the new coordinates. Since
∂x​=∂r​−∂s​,∂y​=∂r​+∂s​,
(16)
the partial differential equation becomes
zxx​−zyy​=−4Zrs​=0.
(17)
For a twice continuously differentiable solution of a differential equation, Zr​ is independent of s, so integration first in r and then in s gives
z(x,y)=ϕ(y+x)+ψ(y−x).​
(18)
This is the decomposition of the one-dimensional wave equation into waves traveling in opposite directions.
The first initial condition gives ϕ(y)+ψ(y)=cosy. The second initial condition gives ϕ′(y)−ψ′(y)=0. Differentiating the first relation and combining the two relations yields ϕ′=psi′=−21​siny. Thus ϕ(y)=21​cosy+C and ψ(y)=21​cosy−C. The constants cancel, and the cosine addition formula gives
z(x,y)=2cos(y+x)+cos(y−x)​=cosxcosy.​
(19)
Both initial conditions and the partial differential equation follow directly by differentiation.
