The rank-nullity theorem states that a linear map T:V→W with finite-dimensional V satisfies
dimV=nullityT+rankT.​
(22)
Choose a basis k1​,…,kr​ of kerT and use basis extension to obtain a basis k1​,…,kr​,u1​,…,us​ of V. We prove that Tu1​,…,Tus​ is a basis of imT.

For linear independence, if ∑j​bj​Tuj​=0, then ∑j​bj​uj​ belongs to the kernel of a linear map. Hence ∑j​bj​uj​=∑i​ai​ki​ for some scalars ai​. The linear independence of the extended basis forces every ai​ and bj​ to vanish. Consequently rankT=s and nullityT=r, while dimV=r+s, proving the theorem.

DP​={p:∃q(p,q)∈D},DQ​={q:∃p(p,q)∈D}
(60)
are directed sets. For example, if p1​,p2​∈DP​, choose (p1​,q1​),(p2​,q2​)∈D. Directedness supplies (p3​,q3​)∈D above both, so p3​∈DP​ is a common upper bound of p1​,p2​ inside DP​. The argument for DQ​ is identical.
By the assumed directed-complete partial order property, p∗​=supDP​ and q∗​=supDQ​ exist. Every (p,q)∈D satisfies p≤P​p∗​ and q≤Q​q∗​, so (p∗​,q∗​) is an upper bound of D. If (u,v) is any other upper bound, then u bounds DP​ and v bounds DQ​. Hence p∗​≤P​u, q∗​≤Q​v, proving leastness. Thus
supD=(supDP​,supDQ​),P×Q is directed-complete.​
(61)
The printed definition of a directed set also permits D=∅.[ref] Under that convention, the hypotheses require P,Q to have least elements, namely the least upper bounds of their empty subsets. Their pair is the least element of the product and is sup∅ there. This handles the empty case as well; under the convention restricting directed sets to nonempty sets, only the preceding nonempty argument is needed.

Take the five-element partially ordered set P={⊥,a,b,u,v}. Its strict comparisons are ⊥<a, ⊥<b, and a<u, a<v, b<u, b<v, together with their transitive consequences. The elements a,b are incomparable, as are u,v.
A finite directed set has a greatest element whenever it is nonempty: list its elements and repeatedly use directedness to find an internal upper bound of the previously chosen element and the next one. The final element is above every member of the directed set, and is its least upper bound. Thus every nonempty directed set in P has a least upper bound. The empty subset also has a least upper bound, namely ⊥. Therefore P satisfies the paper's completeness condition under either convention.
However, {a,b} is bounded above by both u and v. A least upper bound would have to be an upper bound lying below both u and v. Neither u nor v has that property, and none of ⊥,a,b is an upper bound of {a,b}. Hence
P is directed-complete but not bounded-complete.​
(62)

In the language of first-order logic, define
Bottom(b)≡∀yR(b,y),Minimal(b)≡∀y(R(y,b)⇒y=b).​
(67)
Thus a least element lies below every element, whereas a minimal element merely has no distinct element below it. The latter definition allows multiple incomparable minimal elements.
Here is a formal natural deduction proof, with the antisymmetry axiom available as a premise. Assume Bottom(b) and take a fresh arbitrary variable t. Assume R(t,b). By universal elimination applied to Bottom(b), obtain R(b,t). By conjunction introduction, obtain R(t,b)∧R(b,t). Instantiate antisymmetry at t,b, and use modus ponens rule to obtain t=b. Discharge the assumption R(t,b) by implication introduction, obtaining R(t,b)⇒t=b. Since t was arbitrary and is free in no undischarged assumption, universal introduction gives Minimal(b). Discharge the initial assumption and universally quantify b to conclude
∀b(Bottom(b)⇒Minimal(b)).​
(68)
Only antisymmetry was needed for this implication; reflexivity and transitivity are part of the ambient partial order axioms.

A string belongs to the desired formal language precisely when its reversal has no string prefix with more ones than zeros. Associate a path with each reversed string, counting a zero as a step +1 and a one as a step −1.
The nonterminal D generates exactly the paths that stay nonnegative and finish at zero. The production 0D1D consists of an initial up-step, a balanced path one level higher, a down-step to zero, and another balanced path. A context-free grammar is an ambiguous grammar if some terminal string has two distinct parse trees, equivalently two distinct leftmost derivations. Different orders of expanding independent nonterminals do not by themselves constitute ambiguity.
The context-free grammar just given is unambiguous. It suffices to prove this for the reversed productions, because reversing a parse tree preserves whether two parse trees are distinct.
For D, the empty string forces the empty production. A nonempty string must use D→0D1D. In any resulting nonnegative balanced path, the displayed one is the unique first return to zero. It therefore uniquely determines the two shorter substrings generated by D. Mathematical induction on length makes both smaller parse trees unique.
For U, the empty string again forces the empty production. A nonempty path with no return to zero must use U→0U, since 0D1U always returns to zero. A path with a return must use U→0D1U, since 0U stays positive after its first step. In the latter case, the first return uniquely determines the D substring and the remaining U substring. Applying mathematical induction to these shorter substrings proves uniqueness throughout. Hence
The grammar in part (b)(i) is unambiguous.​
