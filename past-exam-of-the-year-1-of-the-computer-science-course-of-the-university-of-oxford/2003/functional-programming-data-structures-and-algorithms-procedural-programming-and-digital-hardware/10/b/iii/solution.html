Use a three-state Moore machine for a rearmable one-cycle pulse generator. Call the states Ready, Pulse, and Wait. Their outputs are respectively z=0,1,0, and initialize in Ready. Labels below are the sampled input pair ab; because simultaneous ones are unspecified, choose reset priority for 11.
The complete chosen state transition table is
Statezab=00ab=01ab=10ab=11
Ready0ReadyReadyPulseReady
Pulse1WaitReadyWaitReady
Wait0WaitReadyWaitReady
A sampled trigger 10 causes a pulse only in Ready. Leaving Pulse unconditionally on the next edge keeps its output high for exactly one clock period, even if a remains high. In Wait, further triggers are ignored until a sampled reset rearms the finite-state machine.
The crucial transition is Pulse --01--> Ready. If reset occurs in the cycle immediately following a trigger, this transition both ends the current pulse and rearms the circuit. The next trigger then produces a pulse after just one intervening low-output cycle. Sending every exit from Pulse to Wait would lose that reset and fail the alternate-cycle requirement. This behavior is explicitly visible in the last three pulses of the original timing diagram.[ref]
For the sixteen sampled pairs in that diagram, numbering the first edge as 1, z is high just after edges 2,10,13,15 and low after every other edge. Equivalently,
zk​=1⟺sk​=Pulse,Pulseb=1​Ready,Readya=1,b=0​Pulse.​

The two low-output states cannot be merged: the same future trigger must produce a pulse from Ready and be ignored from Wait. A separate high-output state is needed to hold the one-cycle pulse, explaining the three-state design.
