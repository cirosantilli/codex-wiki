Past exam of the year 1 of the computer science course of the University of Oxford / 2003 / Functional programming, data structures and algorithms, procedural programming, and digital hardware / 9 / e / Solution

Arrange each four-variable Karnaugh map in Gray code order LG=00,01,11,10 down the rows and ab=00,01,11,10 across the columns. For L+ the row strings are 0100, 0100, ----, 1110; for G+ they are 0001, 1011, ----, 0001. The maps are drawn alongside the state-transition diagram above.
In the L+ map, group the whole ab=01 column to obtain ¬a∧b. Group rows 11,10 with columns 00,01 to obtain L∧¬a. Group those same rows with columns 01,11 to obtain L∧b. The unused-state row permits these larger groups. Symmetrically, in the G+ map group column ab=10, rows 01,11 with columns 11,10, and those same rows with columns 10,00 (wrapping around the map). This gives
L+G+​=(¬a∧b)∨(L∧¬a)∨(L∧b),=(a∧¬b)∨(G∧a)∨(G∧¬b).​​
(42)
These expressions drive the D inputs of the two D flip-flops. With a=b, both copy their stored bit. With ab=01, they force Lt; with ab=10, they force Gt. The equations also specify a harmless extension at unused code 11: equal inputs retain it and unequal inputs recover to a valid comparison state. Correct reset is still required. This extension was chosen using don't-cares; the circuit is not claimed to recover from every invalid state without reset.
