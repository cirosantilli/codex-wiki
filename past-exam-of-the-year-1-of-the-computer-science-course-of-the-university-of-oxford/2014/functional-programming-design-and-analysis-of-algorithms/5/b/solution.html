The scan permits an arbitrary signed integer but prints log2​m. That expression is undefined for m≤0. The printed bound is proved below for positive m; for the stated signed domain the corresponding bound uses log2​max(2,∣m∣). This is a source qualification, rather than an unreadable part of the question.[ref]
Choose a fixed positive integer k with 1/k≤ϵ, and put B=2k. For positive m, use coarse exponential bracketing: compare against B,B2,B3,… until the first Bj≥m. Equality determines m immediately. Otherwise, Bj−1<m<Bj, except in the first interval where the lower bound is zero. Apply binary search to the remaining integer candidates.
There are at most j bracketing comparisons and at most kj+O(1) subsequent comparisons, because the interval width is at most Bj=2kj. Minimality of j gives j≤(log2​m)/k+1. Hence
C(m)≤j+kj+O(1)≤(1+k1​)log2​m+k+O(1)≤(1+ϵ)log2​m+Oϵ​(1).
(13)
The additive constant may depend on the fixed ϵ, but is independent of m. Taking larger jumps in the bracketing phase reduces its leading comparison cost; the overshoot costs only the fixed number k of extra binary search levels.
For signed inputs, first compare with zero. If negative, set u=∣m∣ conceptually and perform the same search for u: comparing m with −t yields the reversed outcome of comparing u with t. Thus every test is still a permitted test of the sign of m−x. Return zero immediately on equality in the initial test. The corrected all-input bound is
C(m)≤(1+ϵ)log2​max(2,∣m∣)+Oϵ​(1).​
(14)
All thresholds are exact integers, generated by repeated multiplication by B. Their arithmetic and storage costs are outside the requested comparison model; bounded machine arithmetic would not suffice for arbitrarily large ∣m∣.
