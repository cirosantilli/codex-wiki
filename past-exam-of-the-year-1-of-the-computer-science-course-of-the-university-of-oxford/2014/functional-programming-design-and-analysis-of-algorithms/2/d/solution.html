Maintain all pairs discovered so far. Extend every discovered path by one original binary relation edge, keeping the old pairs first:
transclose :: Eq a => [(a, a)] -> [(a, a)]
transclose r = grow (removedup r)
  where
    grow s =
      let t = removedup (s ++ relcomp r s)
      in if length t == length s then s else grow t
After j extensions, the represented binary relation is R∪R2∪⋯∪Rj+1. This loop invariant follows by distributivity of composition of relations over a union. The old list s is a duplicate-free prefix of t; therefore equality of lengths is equivalent to equality of the represented sets and signals a fixed point.
Let V be the finite set of endpoints appearing in the input. No extension introduces a new endpoint, so there are at most ∣V∣2 distinct pairs. Every nonfinal iteration adds at least one pair, proving algorithm termination. At the fixed point S, we have R⊆S and R;S⊆S. Mathematical induction gives Ri⊆S for every positive i, while the loop invariant says that each discovered pair belongs to some Ri. Hence
transclose(R)=R+=i≥1⋃​Ri.​
(5)
This is the transitive closure of a relation, rather than a reflexive closure: diagonal pairs appear only when supported by nonempty cycles. The empty input is handled immediately.
