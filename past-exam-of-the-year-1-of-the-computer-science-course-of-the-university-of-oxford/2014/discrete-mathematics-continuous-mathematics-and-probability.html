By De Morgan's laws, taking a set difference from a union requires exclusion from both sets, while taking a set difference from an intersection requires exclusion from at least one:
A∖(B∪C)=(A∖B)∩(A∖C),A∖(B∩C)=(A∖B)∪(A∖C).​
(1)
For example, x belongs to the first left-hand side precisely when x∈A, x∈/B and x∈/C; these are exactly the membership conditions on the first right-hand side. For the second identity, replace the two exclusions by their logical disjunction.

Function composition is not always commutative. The reversed function composition may be undefined when the necessary domain of a function and codomain do not match. A counterexample using functions R→R avoids that issue: let f(x)=x+1 and g(x)=2x. Then
