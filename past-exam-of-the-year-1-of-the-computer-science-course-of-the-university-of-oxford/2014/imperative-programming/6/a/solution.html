Past exam of the year 1 of the computer science course of the University of Oxford / 2014 / Imperative programming / 6 / a / Solution

 ... Year 1 of the computer science course of the University of Oxford Past exam of the year 1 of the computer science course of the University of Oxford 2014 Imperative programming 6 a
OurBigBook.com Words: 227
Apply overload selection followed by dynamic dispatch: the receiver's static type determines visible signatures, and argument static types choose the overload. The receiver's runtime type then selects its override. A super call starts at the inherited implementation instead of redispatching to the override just entered.
For c.f1(y), the Y overload is visible on C, so its body in C runs. For a.f2(x), the X signature is selected and its override in C calls C.f1(X). For a.f2(y), the Y signature is selected; its body in C calls super.f1(y). The superclass B has only f1(X), so that call uses B.f1(X).
For b.f3(y), the static type B exposes only f3(X). Its override in C calls super.f2(x), entering B.f2(X). The unqualified f1(x) inside that body still uses dynamic dispatch on the original C instance, so it calls C.f1(X).
Finally c.f4(x) invokes the companion object C, which is a separate receiver extending B. Its f3(X) calls inherited B.f2(X), whose f1 call dispatches to ObjC.f1(X).[ref]
The exact five output lines, including the final comma and space on each line, are:
f1(y:Y) in C, 
f1(x:X) in C, f2(x:X) in C, 
f1(x:X) in B, f2(y:Y) in C, 
f1(x:X) in C, f2(x:X) in B, f3(x:X) in C, 
f1(x:X) in ObjC, f2(x:X) in B, f3(x:X) in ObjC, f4(x:X) in C,
Select the signature statically, then dispatch that signature dynamically.​
