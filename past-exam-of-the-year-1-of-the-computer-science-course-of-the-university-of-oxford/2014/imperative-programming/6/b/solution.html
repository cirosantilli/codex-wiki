In Scala trait linearization, the rightmost mixed-in trait takes priority. Merge inherited linearizations while retaining a shared ancestor only at its final position; super advances to the next implementation in the resulting chain. Ignoring predefined bases, the lookup orders are
Life1, Exercise, Work, Life
Life2, Dance, Exercise, Program, Study, Work, Fun, Life
Life3, Program, Study, Work, Dance, Fun, Exercise, Life
For example, Dance alone has order Dance, Fun, Exercise, Life. In Life2, the additional Program branch already contains Fun before Life; keeping that shared ancestry in its later position moves Fun after Work, while Dance still takes priority. Reversing the mixins yields the third chain above.
Each describe first delegates and then prints, so the lines appear in the reverse of the method lookup order. The exact output, including the two blank lines, is
I live.
I work.
I exercise.

I live.
I have fun.
I work.
I study.
I program.
I exercise.
I dance.

I live.
I exercise.
I have fun.
I dance.
I work.
I study.
I program.
Lookup follows the linearization; these prints occur while the calls return.​
(31)
The super inside a trait therefore need not call that trait's isolated textual parent; the receiving object's full Scala trait linearization controls the next body.
