Choose the reciprocal-factorial summation eb for asymptotically high accuracy, counting multiplications and divisions as arithmetic work. For tolerance ϵ, linear powering in part (a)(i) takes O(ϵ−1) multiplications; binary exponentiation in part (a)(ii) reduces this to O(log(1/ϵ)). Part (c) also has O(log(1/ϵ)) arithmetic work, since its required n grows like ϵ−1/2, though its constants and cancellation error differ.

Thus the factorial denominator grows much faster than an exponential in the number of iterations. For d decimal places, setting ϵ of order 10−d gives n=Θ(d/logd) by Stirling's approximation, rather than Θ(d) binary-power arithmetic steps. This is a comparison in an exact-arithmetic operation-count model; the stated Big O notation does not determine the exact winner for every small tolerance. Taken literally, eb uses zero multiplications and only divisions. Fixed double-precision floating-point format cannot deliver arbitrarily many correct decimal places, and the precision-dependent cost of arbitrary-precision arithmetic is a separate issue.
