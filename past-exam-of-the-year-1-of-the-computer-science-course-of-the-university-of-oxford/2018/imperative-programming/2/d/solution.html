Black-box testing chooses inputs and expected outputs from the public specification without using the implementation's control structure. Equivalence class testing partitions the input space into classes expected to exercise similar specified behavior, then chooses representatives; boundary cases test where that behavior changes. For equal-range search, use these concrete representatives:
  • Empty array: ([], 4) gives (0,0).
  • One entry, with the target below, equal, and above it: ([4],3), ([4],4), ([4],5) give (0,0), (0,1), (1,1).
  • An absent interior value: ([1,3,5],4) gives (2,2).
  • One interior match: ([1,3,5],3) gives (1,2).
  • A repeated interior block: ([1,3,3,3,5],3) gives (1,4).
  • A repeated block touching the left or right boundary: ([3,3,5],3) and ([1,3,3],3) give (0,2) and (1,3).
  • Every entry equal: ([3,3,3],3) gives (0,3); targets two and four give (0,0) and (3,3).
  • Extreme keys: ([Int.MinValue,0,Int.MaxValue],Int.MaxValue) gives (2,3), and searching for Int.MinValue gives (0,1).
For each result independently check 0≤i≤j≤n, the strict inequalities outside the returned interval, and equality inside it. Unsorted inputs violate the precondition; they are not ordinary members of the specified valid-input classes. Software testing supplies evidence about implementations, while the loop invariant proof explains correctness for every valid input.
