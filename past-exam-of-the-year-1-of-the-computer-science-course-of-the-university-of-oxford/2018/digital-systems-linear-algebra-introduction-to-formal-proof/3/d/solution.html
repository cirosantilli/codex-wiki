Use the stipulated MIPS I-type instruction encoding, whose unused rt field is zero, so register-file output rd1​ supplies the hardwired zero register. With ALUSrc=0 and subtraction selected, the arithmetic logic unit computes R[rs]−0. Its zero flag detects R[rs]=0, while bit 31 of rd0​ is the sign in two's complement. Therefore the required signed test is
R[rs]>0⟺¬Zero∧¬rd0​[31].

Introduce one new control bit GT, and replace the zero-flag input to the existing branch XOR by
Z∗=Zero∨(GT∧rd0​[31]).
(16)
This needs an AND gate, an OR gate and a tap of the sign bit. With GT=0, existing branch behavior is unchanged. With GT=1 and Branch=11, the existing branch-taken circuit gives ¬Z∗, exactly the positive test. Its output still selects between pc+4 and the usual sign-extended branch target. The complete control row is

Set GT=0 for each instruction in parts (a)–(c). Registers and data memory remain unchanged. The available signed slt operation could support a different design; the sign-and-zero design above avoids adding an ALU-input swap and explicitly distinguishes zero from positive values.
