d OurBigBook.com
Removing logic hazards prevents transient false control pulses, incorrect triggering of asynchronous state changes and avoidable switching activity. It also makes behavior less sensitive to unequal propagation delays. These benefits matter especially when an output directly controls another circuit rather than being sampled only after settling.
The usual costs are redundant logic gates, larger area and capacitance, more wiring and possibly a longer critical path. Power can rise from extra hardware even when glitch power falls. A synchronous design may instead sample after a sufficiently long clock period, trading settling time against additional hazard-correction logic. A circuit proved free of single-input static hazards need not be safe under arbitrary simultaneous input changes; metastability at an asynchronous input is a different problem.

Each elementary matrix is obtained by applying one invertible elementary row operation to the identity matrix. Its inverse is the elementary matrix for the reverse operation. A product of elementary matrices is therefore invertible and has full rank n.
Conversely, if rankA=n, Gaussian elimination has a pivot in every column and reduces A to the identity. Writing the successive row operations as left multiplication gives Er​⋯E1​A=I. Hence A=E1−1​⋯Er−1​, a product of elementary matrices. This proves both directions of the elementary-matrix factorization theorem.

A fresh variable is an arbitrary eigenvariable whose value is not constrained by the undischarged assumptions. For universal introduction, it must not occur free in those assumptions; otherwise a fact about one constrained object could be incorrectly generalized to all objects. For existential elimination, its temporary witness must additionally not occur free in the conclusion or in other undischarged assumptions outside the witness subproof. Choosing a completely unused variable is a sufficient convention. These are the eigenvariable conditions, and concern free occurrences rather than harmless reuse of bound names.
