An element u∈A is a least upper bound of S if it is an upper bound, meaning s≤u for every s∈S, and u≤v for every upper bound v∈A of S. The candidate need not belong to S. Because a partial order is an antisymmetric relation, two least upper bounds must be equal. A least upper bound is also called the supremum.

Under the product order, every point of the open unit disc has both coordinates below one, so (1,1) is an upper bound. Any upper bound (u,v) must have u≥1 and v≥1, since the two coordinate projections of the open unit disc are (−1,1). Therefore (1,1)≤P​(u,v), proving sup≤P​​S=(1,1).
Under the lexicographic order, every point (1,v), with arbitrary real v, is an upper bound, because every point of S has first coordinate strictly below one. Conversely, an upper bound cannot have first coordinate u<1: a point (x,0)∈S with x>u would exceed it. If an upper bound has u>1, the upper bound (1,0) is strictly smaller. If it is (1,v), the upper bound (1,v−1) is strictly smaller. Hence there is no least upper bound under the lexicographic order. This illustrates the supremum of an open disc under product and lexicographic orders.
