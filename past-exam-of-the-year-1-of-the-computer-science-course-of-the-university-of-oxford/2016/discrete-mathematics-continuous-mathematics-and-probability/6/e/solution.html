Put h=(b−a)/n, gi​=g(xi​), and z0​=0. Under the endpoint-slope convention suggested by the requested connection, the recurrence is zi+1​=zi​+2h​(gi​+gi+1​). Summing telescopes, so
zn​=nb−a​[2g0​+gn​​+i=1∑n−1​gi​]=Tn​(g).​
(49)
Since G(b)=∫ab​g(x)dx by the fundamental theorem of calculus, this recurrence gives exactly the composite trapezoidal rule. It replaces the average derivative over each strip by the arithmetic mean of its endpoint derivatives.
For the standard midpoint method, the literal endpoint-only request is not valid for general g. Both the explicit and implicit one-step midpoint formulas here give instead
zn​=hi=0∑n−1​g(xi​+h/2)=Mn​(g),​
(50)
the composite midpoint quadrature rule. It requires half-grid samples. These samples cannot be recovered exactly from g0​,…,gn​ for arbitrary smooth g: the zero function and g(x)=∏j=0n​(x−xj​)2 have identical grid values but different midpoint sums. For a smooth g, replacing each half-grid sample by (gi​+gi+1​)/2 is an additional approximation, giving Tn​ with an O(h2) total difference, rather than an identity. A precise useful relation, obtained by splitting the finer trapezoidal sum into old nodes and midpoints, is
T2n​(g)=21​[Tn​(g)+Mn​(g)],Mn​(g)=2T2n​(g)−Tn​(g).​
(51)
Here T2n​ uses the finer half-grid, so it does not eliminate the missing data.
If “Mid-Point Method” means the two-step explicit midpoint linear multistep method, the recurrence is zi+1​=zi−1​+2hgi​. It gives z2r​=2h∑j=1r​g2j−1​ and z2r+1​=z1​+2h∑j=1r​g2j​, so an odd final index additionally needs a starting value z1​. For even n=2r, the result is the midpoint quadrature on r double-width strips; equivalently zn​=2Tn​−Tn/2​. The question specifies neither even n nor a starter. Thus the trapezoidal expression above is the natural endpoint-average interpretation, while the other common midpoint meanings require the qualifications just given.
