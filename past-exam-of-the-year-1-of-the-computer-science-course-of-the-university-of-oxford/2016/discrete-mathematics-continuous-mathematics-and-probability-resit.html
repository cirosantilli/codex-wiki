A function f:A→B is onto, or a surjective function, when every b∈B has a preimage: ∀b∈B ∃a∈A: f(a)=b. It is one-to-one, or an injective function, when f(a)=f(a′) implies a=a′ for all a,a′∈A. It is bijective, a bijection, when both properties hold.
An inverse function is a function f−1:B→A satisfying
f−1∘f=idA​,f∘f−1=idB​​.
(5)
For a bijection, f−1(b) is the unique a with f(a)=b: existence follows from surjectivity and uniqueness from injectivity. This is a two-sided inverse function, rather than just a left or right inverse.

A preorder is a binary relation that is reflexive and transitive: a⪯a, and a⪯b⪯c implies a⪯c.
A partial order is a preorder that is also an antisymmetric relation: a⪯b and b⪯a together imply a=b.
A linear order, also called a total order, is a partial order in which every two elements are comparable: a⪯b or b⪯a for all a,b∈A.
