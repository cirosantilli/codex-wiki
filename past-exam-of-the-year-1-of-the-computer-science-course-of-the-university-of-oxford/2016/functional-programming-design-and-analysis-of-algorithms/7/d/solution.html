Dijkstra's algorithm solves the single-source shortest path problem in a finite weighted graph with nonnegative edge weights. Given a source s, it computes for every vertex v the minimum sum of edge weights on a path from s to v, reporting +∞ if v is unreachable. Keeping a shortest-path predecessor also recovers the corresponding paths. Directed graphs are allowed; an undirected graph can be represented by two oppositely directed edges. Negative edge weights are outside the algorithm's standard correctness assumptions, while zero weights are allowed.
