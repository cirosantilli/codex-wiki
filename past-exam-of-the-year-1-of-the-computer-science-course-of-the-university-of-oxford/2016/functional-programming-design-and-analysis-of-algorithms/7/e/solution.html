Use an indexed priority queue of unsettled vertices, keyed by their tentative distances. Initialize the source to zero and other keys to +∞. Each ExtractMin settles the currently closest vertex. Scan its outgoing edges; whenever edge relaxation improves a neighbour's tentative distance, update that neighbour with DecreaseKey. Already settled vertices need no further update, by the correctness of Dijkstra's algorithm for nonnegative weights.
There are at most ∣V∣ extractions and at most ∣E∣ successful decreases. The indexed priority queue maintains each vertex's current array position and updates this index table on every swap, so locating a key for DecreaseKey costs O(1). Preallocate capacity ∣V∣. Repeated insertion costs O(∣V∣(1+logd​∣V∣)), or bottom-up heap construction initializes the queue in O(∣V∣) time. Scanning adjacency lists costs O(∣V∣+∣E∣) in total.
For ∣V∣≥2 and 2≤d≤∣V∣, the heap-operation contributions are therefore
O(∣V∣dlogd​∣V∣)andO(∣E∣logd​∣V∣),

and the overall bound is
O((d∣V∣+∣E∣)logd​∣V∣)​.

A uniform bound including larger arity and tiny graphs is O((d∣V∣+∣E∣)(1+logd​(max(2,∣V∣)))). Arity greater than the number of vertices offers no useful extra heap levels. These bounds assume adjacency lists; with an adjacency matrix, scanning neighbours contributes O(∣V∣2) instead. No rebuilding of the heap is required after each decrease.
