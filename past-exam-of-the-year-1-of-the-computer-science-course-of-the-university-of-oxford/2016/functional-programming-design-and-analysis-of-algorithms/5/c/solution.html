Put α=logb​a. With a nonnegative toll bounded by O(nd), the Master theorem gives the upper bounds
T(n)=⎩⎨⎧​O(nα),O(ndlogn),O(nd),​d<α,d=α,d>α.​​

If the base cost is Θ(1) and the toll is Θ(nd), these become the usual tight Θ bounds. The printed O toll by itself does not imply those lower bounds; for example, the toll could vanish.
For the two requested proofs, let n=bk as permitted. Expanding the recurrence relation through k levels gives
T(n)=akT(1)+∑j=0k−1​ajO((n/bj)d)=O(nα)+O(nd∑j=0k−1​(a/bd)j).
(10)
When d=α, the ratio is one. The sum has k=logb​n terms, so T(n)=O(ndlogn). With matching positive lower bounds on each level's work, the same expansion gives Θ(ndlogn).
When d>α, the ratio r=a/bd is strictly below one. The geometric series is at most 1/(1−r), so the internal work is O(nd); the leaf term O(nα) is also O(nd). A Θ(nd) root toll supplies the matching lower bound in the tight version. This proves both requested cases.
There is a small termination qualification in the printed generality: if 1<b<2, the literal base case only at n=1 may leave ceil(n/b) = n at a small integer, for example n=2,b=3/2. The standard rounded Master theorem assumes a sufficiently large fixed base-case cutoff so that recursive sizes decrease. The allowed unrounded powers-of-b proof above does not use that problematic rounded step. Also, a literal number of recursive calls is integral, though the algebraic recurrence expansion remains meaningful for positive real a.
