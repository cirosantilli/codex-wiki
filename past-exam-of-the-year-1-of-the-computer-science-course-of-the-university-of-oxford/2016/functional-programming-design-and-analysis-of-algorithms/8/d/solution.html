Let Ii​=[si​,ti​] be an interval whose earliest end triggers greedy meeting i. After that meeting, every remaining interval starts strictly after ti​: its end is at least ti​, and otherwise it would contain ti​ and be removed. Hence
si+1​>ti​,t1​<t2​<⋯<tk​.
(18)
The witness intervals I1​,…,Ik​ are therefore pairwise disjoint. Every feasible schedule needs a distinct meeting in each one, yielding a lower bound of k. The greedy schedule uses exactly k meetings and covers everyone, so it is optimal. This is an optimality certificate for the interval stabbing problem.
To follow the source's suggested contradiction explicitly, suppose a schedule u1​<⋯<uj​ with j<k covered everyone. Since I1​ ends at t1​, some uℓ​≤t1​, so u1​≤t1​. If ui​≤ti​ and i<j, then none of the first i alternative meetings can hit Ii+1​, which starts after ti​. A later meeting must hit it by its end ti+1​, implying ui+1​≤ti+1​. Mathematical induction gives ui​≤ti​ for 1≤i≤j. But Ij+1​ starts after tj​≥uj​, later than every alternative meeting. It is uncovered, a contradiction. For j=0 a nonempty family is immediately uncovered. The source's hint starts at index zero without defining u0​,t0​; the proof above starts at the actual first meetings instead.
