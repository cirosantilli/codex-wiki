Past exam of the year 1 of the computer science course of the University of Oxford / 2016 / Functional programming, design and analysis of algorithms / 8 / e / Solution

OurBigBook.com Words: 162
Sort once by nondecreasing end time, then retain the most recent meeting:
Meetings(intervals):
    sort intervals by nondecreasing end
    result = []
    haveMeeting = false
    for (s,e) in intervals:
        if not haveMeeting or s > lastMeeting:
            lastMeeting = e
            append e to result
            haveMeeting = true
    return result
When an interval is inspected, lastMeeting≤e because of the sorted end order. Therefore the interval is already covered exactly when s≤lastMeeting; otherwise it is the earliest-ending uncovered interval and the algorithm chooses its end. This proves that the scan implements greedy strategy 2. The strict test s > lastMeeting correctly treats equality as covered for closed intervals.
The sorting costs O(nlogn) comparisons, and the scan costs O(n), with O(k) space for the output and constant scan state. Including an empty input, the overall bound is O(nlog(n+1)) time, conventionally O(nlogn) for n≥2. Additional sorting space depends on the selected sorting algorithm. Part (d) proves that the returned number of meetings is minimum.
