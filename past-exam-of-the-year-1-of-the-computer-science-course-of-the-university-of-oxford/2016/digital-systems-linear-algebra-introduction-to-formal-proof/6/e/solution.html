For A, the triangular matrix formula gives the characteristic polynomial
det(tI−A)=(t−1)(t−3)(t−2).
(28)
The three eigenvalues are distinct, so part (d) already proves the existence of an eigenvector basis. Explicit choices, ordered by eigenvalues 1,3,2, are
(1,0,0)T,(1,1,0)T,(2,−1,1)T.​
(29)
Their column matrix has determinant one, and direct multiplication gives respectively one, three and two times the indicated vector.
For B, expansion along the last row gives
det(tI−B)=(t−3)((t−3)2−1)=(t−3)(t−4)(t−2).
(30)
Again the three eigenvalues are distinct. An explicit eigenvector basis, ordered by eigenvalues 4,2,3, is
(1,1,0)T,(1,−1,0)T,(0,−1,1)T.​
(31)
Its column matrix has determinant −2=0. In particular, B's diagonal entries are not its eigenvalues: it is not a triangular matrix.
Finally C=I3​, so every nonzero vector is an eigenvector with eigenvalue one. Choose the standard basis e1​,e2​,e3​. These are linearly independent even though their eigenvalues coincide. Distinct eigenvalues are sufficient for an eigenvector basis, and are not necessary.
