 ... Past exam of the year 1 of the computer science course of the University of Oxford 2016 Digital systems, linear algebra, introduction to formal proof 7 d ii
OurBigBook.com Words: 140
First derive A by double-negation elimination, then derive ¬B by negation introduction. The logical falsity elimination rule is used only inside the attempt to derive A.
1  ¬(A → B)  premise
 2  | ¬A     assumption
 3  | | A    assumption
 4  | | ⊥    ¬-elim 3,2
 5  | | B    ⊥-elim 4
 6  | A → B  →-intro 3–5
 7  | ⊥      ¬-elim 6,1
 8  ¬¬A      ¬-intro 2–7
 9  A        ¬¬-elim 8
10  | B      assumption
11  | | A    assumption
12  | | B    reiteration 10
13  | A → B  →-intro 11–12
14  | ⊥      ¬-elim 13,1
15  ¬B       ¬-intro 10–14
16  A ∧ ¬B   ∧-intro 9,15
Reiteration at line 12 merely uses an assumption that remains in scope. The final A∧¬B follows by conjunction introduction. This explicitly supplies the classical step needed to obtain A rather than just ¬¬A.
