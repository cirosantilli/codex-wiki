Past exam of the year 1 of the computer science course of the University of Oxford / 2016 / Digital systems, linear algebra, introduction to formal proof / 2 / b / Solution

Split the four-bit unsigned inputs into two-bit halves: X=4XH​+XL​ and Y=4YH​+YL​. Use four copies of the two-bit binary multiplier to compute PLL​=XL​YL​, PHL​=XH​YL​, PLH​=XL​YH​, and PHH​=XH​YH​. Then
XY=PLL​+4PHL​+4PLH​+16PHH​.​
(8)
The extreme terms occupy disjoint output positions: PLL​ uses positions zero to three and 16PHH​ uses positions four to seven. Form W by concatenating their four-bit outputs, with PHH​ in the upper half and PLL​ in the lower half. This needs wiring alone, so W=16PHH​+PLL​ without an adder.
Use the first eight-bit adder to form R=PHL​+PLH​ after zero-extension, and the second to form Z=W+(R≪2). Thus four two-bit binary multipliers and two eight-bit adders suffice. The fixed left bit shift is also wiring alone.
The middle sum can reach 9+9=18, so preserve all five of its potentially nonzero bits; retaining only four would be wrong. Its shifted value is at most 72. The final result is at most 15×15=225, so both additions fit in eight bits. Concatenating the disjoint extreme terms is what saves an addition.
