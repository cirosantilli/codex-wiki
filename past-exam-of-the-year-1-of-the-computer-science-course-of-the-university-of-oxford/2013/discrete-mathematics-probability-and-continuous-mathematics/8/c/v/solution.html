First restrict to integers in the marginal supports: 2≤i≤12 and −5≤j≤5. Their marginal probabilities are positive. By part (i), a feasible joint pair identifies a unique ordered pair of rolls, so its joint probability is 1/36; an infeasible pair has joint probability zero.
Put ai​=6−∣i−7∣ and bj​=6−∣j∣. If the two events were independent, a feasible pair would require
361​=36ai​​36bj​​,so ai​bj​=36.
(115)
Since 1≤ai​,bj​≤6, equality forces ai​=bj​=6, hence (i,j)=(7,0). But that pair is infeasible. An infeasible pair cannot give independence either, since its joint probability is zero while the product of its positive marginal probabilities is positive. Thus
There are no independent pairs of these events within the two marginal supports.​
(116)
If all real values of i,j are allowed literally, an impossible event is independent of every event. The complete answer without a support restriction is therefore
{X=i} and {Y=j} are independent iff i∈/{2,…,12} or j∈/{−5,…,5}.​
(117)
