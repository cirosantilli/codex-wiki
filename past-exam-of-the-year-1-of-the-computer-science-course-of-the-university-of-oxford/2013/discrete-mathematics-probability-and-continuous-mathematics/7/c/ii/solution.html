Let B count the red balls among the n added balls. By independence of random variables, B has a binomial distribution with parameters n,1/2. The total red count is R=1+B, and the box has n+1 balls. Conditional on R=r, sampling without replacement gives
P(D1​∣R=r)=n+1r​,P(D1​∩D2​∣R=r)=n(n+1)r(r−1)​,
(97)
where D1​,D2​ denote red first and second draws. Averaging these conditional probabilities and taking their ratio gives
P(D2​∣D1​)=nE[R]E[R(R−1)]​.
(98)
The binomial distribution moments give E[B]=n/2 and E[B2]=n/4+n2/4. Consequently
E[R]=2n+2​,E[R(R−1)]=E[B2+B]=4n(n+3)​.
(99)
Therefore the red-ball prediction with one certain red ball is
P(D2​∣D1​)=2(n+2)n+3​,n≥1.​
(100)
It reduces to 2/3 at n=1 and approaches 1/2 as the number of randomly coloured extra balls grows. Taking an unweighted average of the remaining red fractions would miss the change in weights caused by taking a conditional probability given the first red draw.
