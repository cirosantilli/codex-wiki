Represent Pascal's triangle by an infinite outer lazy list, each of whose elements is one finite row. Pad the preceding row with zero on its left and on its right, then add corresponding entries:
pascal :: [[Integer]]
pascal = iterate next [1]
  where
    -- Padding supplies the two boundary entries as well as the interior sums.
    next :: [Integer] -> [Integer]
    next row = zipWith (+) (0 : row) (row ++ [0])
Here iterate next [1] begins [[1],[1,1],[1,2,1],[1,3,3,1],...]. Every call to next receives a finite row, so its final zero is reachable. Lazy evaluation produces any requested finite prefix without trying to finish the infinite outer list.
Indexing rows and columns from zero, Pascal's rule says
pascaln​=[(0n​),(1n​),…,(nn​)],(jn+1​)=(j−1n​)+(jn​).​

Take out-of-range binomial coefficients to be zero. The seed is the zeroth row, and the padded addition implements this identity at every position, including the two boundaries. Mathematical induction therefore proves every generated row correct. Using unbounded integers prevents overflow. Generating rows zero through r takes Θ(r2) arithmetic operations, with additional bit costs as the binomial coefficients grow.
