Every finite distance label is the weight of a discovered directed walk, so it can never underestimate a finite shortest-path distance. After k complete passes, each label is at most the cost of every source-to-vertex path using at most k edges. To prove that assertion, consider such a path's last edge u→v: after the previous pass, u already has a label no greater than the prefix cost, and the current pass relaxes that last edge. In-place improvements can only make the label smaller, preserving the assertion.
When no negative-weight cycle is reachable, any repeated-vertex portion of an optimal walk can be removed without increasing its cost. Some shortest path therefore has at most ∣V∣−1 edges. The two bounds meet after that many passes, proving correctness. If a complete pass makes no change, all edge relaxation inequalities already hold and further passes cannot improve anything. A further strict relaxation after ∣V∣−1 passes implies a reachable negative cycle by the same simple-path bound.
