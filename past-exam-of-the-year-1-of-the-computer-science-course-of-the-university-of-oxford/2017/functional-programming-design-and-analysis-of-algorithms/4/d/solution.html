Prove the natural-number fold fusion law pointwise. At Zero,
f (foldNat g a Zero) = f a = b = foldNat h b Zero
Assuming the equation at m, the Succ m calculation is:
f (foldNat g a (Succ m))
  = f (g (foldNat g a m))
  = h (f (foldNat g a m))
  = h (foldNat h b m)
  = foldNat h b (Succ m)
The compatibility premise f . g = h . f justifies the middle rewrite. This proves the theorem for finite total constructor values by structural induction.
For the full lazy Haskell interpretation, also handle the bottom value: foldNat g a is strict in its outer Natural constructor, so its value on bottom is bottom; the assumed strict function property of f makes the left side bottom too. The same zero/successor induction now covers finite partial constructor chains. Pure definable functions preserve the limits of these information approximations, extending equality to infinite Succ chains. This explains the strictness premise; finite total induction alone would not use it.[ref]
