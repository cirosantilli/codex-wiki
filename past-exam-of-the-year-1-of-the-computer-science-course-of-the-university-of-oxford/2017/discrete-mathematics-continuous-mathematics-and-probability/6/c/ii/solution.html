Define the one-step local truncation error as the defect obtained by inserting the exact solution into an update:
δn+1​=y(xn​+h)−y(xn​)−hf(xn​,y(xn​)).
(38)
For a smooth solution, Taylor's theorem and y′=f(x,y) give
δn+1​=2h2​y′′(xn​)+O(h3)=O(h2).​
(39)
Thus m=2 for the one-step defect convention. The leading derivative can also be written y′′=fx​+fy​f by the chain rule.
Some conventions instead call τn+1​=δn+1​/h the truncation error. That normalized local truncation error is O(h), so m=1 under the normalized convention. Both describe the same Euler one-step defect; neither should be confused with an unqualified global error claim.
