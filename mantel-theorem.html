Mantel theorem (ex(n,K3​)=⌊n2/4⌋)

 ... Mathematics Area of mathematics Foundations of mathematics Graph theory Extremal graph theory Extremal number
OurBigBook.com Words: 442 Articles: 1
The complete bipartite graph K3,3​ has vertex sets A={a1​,a2​,a3​} and B={b1​,b2​,b3​}, with every edge between A and B and none inside either part. Its six vertices support 3⋅3=9 edges and no triangles in a graph: among any three vertices, two belong to the same independent set and have no joining edge.
Add the single edge a1​a2​. Each bj​ is a common neighbour of its endpoints, producing the triangle in a graph a1​a2​bj​. These are exactly the three new triangles in a graph, because every triangle in a graph must use the only edge inside a part. The new count is ten edges; nine was the last possible count without a triangle in a graph on six vertices.
In general, every simple graph with a finite set of n≥0 vertices that is triangle-free has at most
m≤⌊4n2​⌋
(3)
edges. This is the r=2 case of Turán's theorem, with forbidden clique K3​. Equality holds exactly for the balanced complete bipartite graph K⌊n/2⌋,⌈n/2⌉​, up to graph isomorphism; empty parts are allowed for n<2.[ref]
The floor function gives the exact integer bound. Splitting n vertices into two independent sets of sizes a and n−a, and putting in every cross edge, gives
a(n−a)=4n2​−(a−2n​)2.
(4)
The count is largest when a is an integer nearest n/2. For n=2k it is k2; for n=2k+1 it is k(k+1). Both equal ⌊n2/4⌋. This proves attainability for every n, including the empty graph.
A short proof of the upper bound uses degrees of vertices. In a triangle-free graph, the endpoints u,v of an edge have disjoint vertex neighbourhoods: a common neighbour would complete a triangle in a graph. Thus d(u)+d(v)≤n. Summing over edges counts d(v) once for each of the d(v) edges incident to v, giving
∑v∈V​d(v)2=∑uv∈E​(d(u)+d(v))≤nm.
(5)
For n>0, the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality and handshaking lemma yield
4m2=(∑v∈V​d(v))2≤n∑v∈V​d(v)2≤n2m.
(6)
If m>0, division gives m≤n2/4; m=0 is immediate. Since m is an integer, apply the floor function. The case n=0 is immediate too. This proves the bound; the full equality characterization follows from Turán's theorem.
The useful reversal is that more than ⌊n2/4⌋ edges force a triangle in a graph, whatever their placement. A global count certifies a local pattern. The extremizer is bipartite, but a triangle-free graph need not be bipartite: a five-vertex graph cycle has five edges, below the bound of six. Historically, Willem Mantel posed the triangle-exclusion question in 1907.[ref]
