Positional notation (N=∑j=0n​dj​bj)

 Home Mathematics Area of mathematics Number theory Integer
OurBigBook.com Words: 2k Articles: 34
In positional notation, a numeral digit contributes its value multiplied by a weight determined by its position. Fix an integer radix b≥2. A finite string of numeral digits dn​dn−1​⋯d0​, with 0≤dj​<b, represents the nonnegative integer
(dn​dn−1​⋯d0​)b​=∑j=0n​dj​bj.
(2)
The rightmost position has weight b0=1; each move left multiplies the weight by b. This weight is the place value. For a nonzero integer, the canonical string has dn​=0. Zero is written with the single numeral digit 0. Leading zeros are permitted in a fixed-width display but omitted in the canonical form: 0020510​=20510​. A negative integer is written with a separate minus sign and the positional notation for its absolute value; the minus sign is not a numeral digit.[ref]
For example,
20510​=2⋅102+0⋅101+5⋅100=200+0+5.
(3)
The zero is essential inside this string: 2510​=25, not 205. It records that the tens position contributes nothing while keeping the hundreds and units in their proper positions. The same integer has binary representation
110011012​=27+26+23+22+20=128+64+8+4+1=20510​.
(4)
Repeated Euclidean division constructs the representation exactly. Set N0​=N. At each step write
Nj​=bNj+1​+dj​,0≤dj​<b.
(5)
Keep the remainder dj​ and continue with the integer quotient Nj+1​. A positive quotient strictly decreases, so eventually it is zero. Substituting the successive equations back into the first gives N=d0​+bd1​+b2d2​+⋯: the numeral digits are obtained from right to left. The remainder modulo b uniquely fixes d0​, and then the quotient uniquely fixes the remaining string by the same reasoning. This proves existence and uniqueness once leading zeros are excluded. The needed unique quotient and remainder are precisely the conclusion of the Euclidean division theorem.[ref]
This finite expansion concerns nonnegative integers. Extending positional notation to fractional positions uses weights b−1,b−2,… to the right of a point; an arbitrary real number need not have a finite digit string. In particular, the decimal representation 0.1 represents 1/10, while the base-two string 0.12​ represents 1/2. The radix is part of the interpretation.
The benefit is compact arithmetic: k positions can encode every nonnegative integer below bk. To see the upper endpoint, fill all positions with b−1; the finite geometric series gives (b−1)(1+b+⋯+bk−1)=bk−1. Carrying in addition exchanges b units of one place value for one unit of the next. Thus a short string stores a large count and also supplies an algorithm for computing with it.
