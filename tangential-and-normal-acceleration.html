Tangential and normal acceleration (a=v˙T+κv2N)

 ... Area of mathematics Geometry and topology Differential geometry Normal vector Unit normal Normal and tangential components
OurBigBook.com Words: 153
For a twice continuously differentiable regular curve describing motion, let v=∥r′(t)∥>0, T=r′/v, and let N be the principal normal vector where the curvature κ is positive. Differentiating the velocity vector r′=vT gives
a=r′′=v˙T+κv2N.​
(50)
The tangential component changes the speed; the normal component changes the direction of the velocity vector. A particle can therefore have nonzero acceleration at constant speed, as in circular motion with constant speed. The formula follows from dT/dt=(dT/ds)(ds/dt)=κvN and the product rule. When κ=0, the normal contribution vanishes even though the principal normal vector is undefined there. [ref]
In dimensionless coordinates, take r(t)=(t,t2). At t=1,
v=5​,v˙=5​4​,κ=55​2​,T=5​(1,2)T​,N=5​(−2,1)T​.
(51)
The resulting vector components are
aT​=(54​,58​)T,aN​=(−54​,52​)T,aT​+aN​=(0,2)T=r′′(1).​
(52)
Both increasing speed and turning contribute to the same acceleration.
