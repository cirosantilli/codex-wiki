Transition frequencies and matrix multiplication (ωmn​=(Em​−En​)/ℏ)

 ... Physics Branches of physics Quantum mechanics Operator formalism Matrix mechanics Observable matrices in an energy basis
OurBigBook.com Words: 147
In the Heisenberg picture, an observable without explicit time dependence has
AH​(t)=eiHt/ℏAe−iHt/ℏ,(AH​)mn​(t)=Amn​eiωmn​t,ωmn​=ℏEm​−En​​.
(5)
Here ℏ is the reduced Planck constant. An emitted photon on a transition n→m with En​>Em​ has positive angular frequency (En​−Em​)/ℏ=∣ωmn​∣. The sign of the array's quantum phase follows its index convention; it is not a negative measured photon frequency. The frequencies involve differences of energy levels, rather than the absolute values En​/ℏ. Adding a constant to H leaves these frequencies unchanged.
Insert the complete orthonormal basis between two linear operators:
(AB)mn​=∑k​Amk​Bkn​,ωmk​+ωkn​=ωmn​.
(6)
The sum runs over intermediate states. The second identity follows by cancellation of Ek​; it makes every term in the product acquire the same time factor eiωmn​t. This is a useful reason for matrix multiplication: it composes the two indexed arrays consistently with the addition of transition frequencies. It does not multiply matching entries independently. Swapping A and B generally changes the intermediate-state sum and hence the result.
