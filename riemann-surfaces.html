Doubly connected domain (∣π0​(C∖Ω)∣=2)

Words: 44
Riemann surfaces OurBigBook.com
A planar domain whose complement in the Riemann sphere has exactly two connected components. An annulus is the standard example. If neither complementary component is a single point, the domain is conformally equivalent to a circular annulus with finite positive conformal modulus of an annulus; punctured-disc and punctured-plane cases account for degenerate complementary components.

Conformal quadrilateral ((Ω;a,b,c,d))

Words: 322 Articles: 2
Riemann surfaces OurBigBook.com
A Jordan domain together with four distinct boundary points in cyclic order, regarded up to a conformal map preserving those marked points. Such a domain maps to a rectangle with its marked points at the four corners. The opposite boundary-arc pairs specify two dual curve-joining problems whose extremal lengths are reciprocal.

Conformal modulus of a quadrilateral (M=w/h)

Words: 277 Articles: 1
Conformal quadrilateral OurBigBook.com
Map a conformal quadrilateral to a rectangle of width w and height h, with the chosen opposite boundary arcs mapped to its vertical sides. Their extremal length is M=w/h; the other pair has extremal length 1/M. The constant conformal metric is extremal. For an arbitrary density, average horizontal joining lengths over height and apply the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality to obtain Lρ2​h≤wAρ​, proving the upper bound. Vertical joining segments prove the dual formula. Conformal invariance of extremal length transports both equalities back to the quadrilateral.

Doubled rectangle extremal-length duality (λ(Γ)=2w/h,λ(Γ∗)=2h/w)

Words: 204
Conformal modulus of a quadrilateral OurBigBook.com
Glue two copies of [0,w]×[0,h] along their left, bottom and right sides. Their unglued top sides form the outer boundary of a disk; the two bottom corners are marked interior points. Let Γ be the loops surrounding both points, and Γ∗ the boundary-to-boundary crosscuts separating them. Then λ(Γ)λ(Γ∗)=4.
Each vertical seam joins a marked point to the outer boundary, so the Jordan curve theorem forces a loop surrounding both points to meet both seams. In the flat conformal metric, its total horizontal travel is therefore at least 2w. Doubled horizontal segments attain this length. Each separating crosscut meets the bottom seam and starts and ends at height h, so its total vertical travel is at least 2h; doubled vertical segments attain it. The total area is 2wh.
For an arbitrary density, integrate the lengths of the doubled horizontal segments over their height. The Cauchy-Schwarz inequality yields Lρ​(Γ)2h≤2wAρ​. Doubled vertical segments similarly give Lρ​(Γ∗)2w≤2hAρ​. These upper bounds equal the flat-metric ratios, proving both displayed formulas. Mapping the upper and lower halves of a disc to these rectangles gives the corresponding duality for a simply connected domain with two marked interior points.

Meromorphic differential on a Riemann surface (ω=f(z)dz)

Words: 90
Riemann surfaces OurBigBook.com
A meromorphic differential is specified in each holomorphic chart by a meromorphic coefficient f(z), with the coefficients transforming by the coordinate derivative so that the one-form agrees on overlaps. The derivative of a meromorphic function gives such a differential, and the ratio of two nonzero differentials is a meromorphic function. Orders of zeros and poles are independent of chart because coordinate derivatives are nonvanishing holomorphic factors. On a compact connected surface the sum of orders of a nonzero differential is 2g−2, allowing its divisor to determine the genus.

Hyperbolic Riemann surface in potential theory

Words: 88
Riemann surfaces OurBigBook.com
A noncompact Riemann surface is hyperbolic in potential theory when its Green-function exhaustion has a finite limit, equivalently when it admits a positive Green function on a Riemann surface. This definition does not assume a disk uniformization. The unit disk is an example, since −log∣z∣ is its positive Green function with pole zero. For simply connected surfaces this is equivalent to being conformally equivalent to the disk. For general surfaces, potential-theoretic hyperbolicity should not be conflated with the condition that the universal cover is the disk: some surfaces with disk universal cover have no positive Green function.

Compact Riemann surface

Words: 44
Riemann surfaces OurBigBook.com
A Riemann surface which is compact as a topological space. For a connected one of genus g, the Riemann-Roch theorem gives g independent holomorphic differential forms. The Riemann bilinear relations for a compact surface make their normalized periods a full lattice in Cg, and the Abel-Jacobi map of a compact Riemann surface turns this lattice into a complex torus encoding divisor classes.

Abel theorem for divisors

Words: 92
Abel-Jacobi map of a compact Riemann surface OurBigBook.com
A degree-zero complex analytic divisor E on a compact Riemann surface is principal exactly when its Abel-Jacobi map of a compact Riemann surface is zero. Normalize a differential of the third kind with residues equal to the coefficients of E. The Riemann bilinear relations for a compact surface express its b-periods as 2πi times the Abel integrals. If those integrals are in the period lattice, subtract a suitable integral multiple of the normalized holomorphic differential forms; all periods then lie in 2πiZ, so exponentiation gives the required meromorphic function. Conversely, the logarithmic differential of a meromorphic function has integer residues and periods in 2πiZ, giving a zero Abel class by the same calculation.

Differential of the third kind

Words: 74
Riemann surfaces OurBigBook.com
A meromorphic one-form on a compact Riemann surface with only simple poles is a differential of the third kind. Prescribed residues can be realized exactly when their sum is zero: in the residue exact sequence for KC​(S), the obstruction in H1(C,KC​)≅C is the sum of residues. Subtracting a holomorphic differential form normalizes its a-periods. Integer residues and periods in 2πiZ make the exponential of its integral a single-valued meromorphic function with those orders of zeros and poles.

Three-branch-point regular surface cover (χ(N)=∣T∣(1/m+1/n+1/r−1))

Words: 158 Articles: 1
Riemann surfaces OurBigBook.com
For a finite group generated by A,B, glue sheets labeled by the group over the three-punctured Riemann sphere using left monodromy multiplication. Fill the punctures by the local power charts dictated by the orders m,n,r of A,B,AB. Right multiplication by inverses defines the commuting deck action. Its nontrivial stabilizers are conjugates of the corresponding cyclic subgroups. The Riemann-Hurwitz formula gives the displayed Euler characteristic. A compatible metric can be averaged over the group; in genus at least two the invariant hyperbolic metric is canonical by the uniformization theorem.

Three-branch-point regular surface cover OurBigBook.com
For covers branched over three real points with a real base point, choose the two generating loops so complex conjugation reverses each. A sheet permutation satisfying the displayed identities lifts that conjugation to an anticonformal equivalence of the covers, including the filled branch points. For closed hyperbolic Riemann surfaces this is a Riemannian isometry. Exhaust a simply connected hyperbolic Riemann surface in potential theory by compact bordered Jordan domains Rn​. The Schwarz lemma makes F′(p) extremal among normalized disk-valued maps. If F omitted a=0, taking a global holomorphic square root of a disk automorphism applied to F, then renormalizing at p, would multiply its derivative by (1+∣a∣)/(2∣a∣​)>1. Thus F is onto and gives the desired biholomorphism.

On the unit sphere, use ζ=(x+iy)/(1−z) away from the north pole and η=(x−iy)/(1+z) away from the south pole. z=1−ZX+iY​on S2∖{N},w=1+ZX−iY​on S2∖{S}.

The reciprocal chart makes infinity an ordinary coordinate origin. Its domain is the Riemann sphere minus the point z=0; w(∞)=0 and w(z)=1/z elsewhere. A neighbourhood UR​ of infinity becomes the open disc ∣w∣<1/R. In sphere coordinates this is exactly the cap
Z>R2+1R2−1​.

The point z=2+i lies in this neighbourhood because ∣z∣=5​>2. Its reciprocal coordinate is w=(2−i)/5, with ∣w∣=1/5​<1/2, and its sphere height is Z=2/3>3/5. The negative imaginary part of w is the axis reversal required by the holomorphic stereographic atlas of the sphere.
