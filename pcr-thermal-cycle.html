PCR thermal cycle

 Home Biology Genetics Molecular genetics Polymerase chain reaction
OurBigBook.com Words: 1k Articles: 3
A PCR thermal cycle separates three jobs. During PCR denaturation, heating separates the paired DNA strands without intentionally cutting their phosphodiester bonds. During PCR annealing, cooling permits base pairing between PCR primers and templates. During PCR extension, the chosen interval supports DNA polymerase synthesis from the paired 3′ ends. Each new nucleotide is supplied as a deoxynucleoside triphosphate; its incorporation lengthens the strand. The DNA polymerase is a catalyst, while these nucleotides are consumed substrates.[ref]
Taq polymerase withstands repeated high-temperature strand separation better than a heat-sensitive DNA polymerase. Its extension activity still depends on temperature, buffer solution and magnesium ions. Heat resistance does not mean constant activity at every stage: the separation temperature serves the DNA, whereas the extension temperature serves the copying enzyme. As one institutional example, Addgene's Basic PCR Program specifies 94∘C separation, 55∘C primer binding and 72∘C extension. These are example settings, not universal properties of all PCR primers or DNA polymerases.[ref]
For timing arithmetic alone, those stipulated holds give 20+25+45=90s per PCR thermal cycle, and three cycles give 270s. Adding an assumed 90s initial separation and 180s final extension gives 540s=9min of holds. A real thermal cycler also takes time to move between temperatures. The schedule tells us when conditions favour PCR denaturation, PCR annealing or PCR extension; it does not measure the fraction of DNA copied.
An actual institutional protocol makes the distinction concrete. New England Biolabs specifies mixing templates and PCR primers with its OneTaq master mix in a thin-walled tube; a quick spin collects liquid at the bottom. Its 25μL preparation uses 12.5μL of the twice-concentrated mix and 0.5μL of each 10μM PCR primer stock, with water completing the volume. Tubes enter a thermal cycler preheated to 94∘C; mineral oil is specified if there is no heated lid. The program gives thirty cycles of 94∘C separation, 45–68∘C binding and 68∘C extension, with extension time scaled to product length. The binding setting must be selected for the PCR primers; the range is not a continuously swept setting.[ref]
The dilution can be checked independently: each PCR primer has final concentration 10×0.5/25=0.2μM, while the mix is diluted twofold. For an endpoint readout, Addgene specifies examining a product aliquot by gel electrophoresis.[ref] Pair that size comparison with the existing PCR controls: a known template and a no-template tube test different explanations of success or failure. Those comparisons are an analytical addition, not controls documented in the OneTaq schedule. An expected-sized band supports production of a length-compatible amplicon; it does not establish its nucleotide sequence, ideal PCR efficiency or successful separation during every cycle. Neither protocol supplies a measured temperature or yield curve for the schematic above.
