Nonlinear optics

 Home Physics Branches of physics Optics
OurBigBook.com Words: 3k Articles: 53
Nonlinear optics studies the response of matter to light when the induced electric polarization is not proportional to the driving electric field. An intense laser can make these additional terms observable. The striking consequence is that a material can emit light at a frequency absent from the incident beam: second-harmonic generation doubles it. This extends a laser's usable optical spectrum without requiring a second lasing transition.
Franken and colleagues frequency-doubling experiment established an optical harmonic in 1961. Nicolaas Bloembergen's theoretical work developed the description of interacting light waves in nonlinear matter. His 1981 lecture connects a nonlinear constitutive relation with Maxwell's equations and explains compensation of optical dispersion by birefringence.[ref]
