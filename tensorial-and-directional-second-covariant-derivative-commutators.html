Tensorial and directional second covariant derivative commutators

 Home Physics Branches of physics General relativity Riemann curvature tensor Commutator of covariant derivatives
OurBigBook.com Words: 85
For a vector field V, the tensorial second derivative is ∇2V(X,Y)=∇X​∇Y​V−∇∇X​Y​V. With R(X,Y)V=∇X​∇Y​V−∇Y​∇X​V−∇[X,Y]​V and geometric torsion tensor T, antisymmetrization gives
∇2V(X,Y)−∇2V(Y,X)=R(X,Y)V−∇T(X,Y)​V.
(1)
This follows by subtracting the two definitions and using ∇X​Y−∇Y​X=[X,Y]+T(X,Y). By contrast, the directional operator commutator subtracts no derivative-index connection and obeys [∇X​,∇Y​]V=R(X,Y)V+∇[X,Y]​V. On scalars the directional commutator is [X,Y]f, while the tensorial Hessian commutator is −T(X,Y)f. Distinguishing these objects prevents an apparent contradiction when a scalar Hessian has an antisymmetric part despite commuting coordinate vector fields.
