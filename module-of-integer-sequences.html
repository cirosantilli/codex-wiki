Module of integer sequences (∏j∈N0​​Z)

OurBigBook.com Words: 130
The module of integer sequences consists of all functions N0​→Z, with pointwise addition and integer scalar multiplication. Its submodule of finitely supported sequences is the direct sum of modules ⨁j≥0​Z, a free module with coordinate basis of a module (ej​). The full product is not a free module.
To prove this, suppose it had a basis of a module E. The countable basis support lemma places every finitely supported sequence in a submodule C generated by a countable subset of E. The basis partition gives a module direct sum lemma supplies a projection π onto its complementary direct summand, with kernel C. By countability of countably generated abelian groups, C is countable. The Cantor diagonal argument makes the family of all sequences w(j)=±2j uncountable, so choose one with π(w)=0. Truncating w before coordinate i writes w=fi​+2igi​, where fi​ is finitely supported and gi​ is an integer sequence. Thus π(w)=2iπ(gi​) for every i. The vanishing of infinitely divisible integer sequences forces π(w)=0, a contradiction.
