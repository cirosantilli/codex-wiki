Fermi sea

 ... Branches of physics Statistical physics Quantum ideal-gas statistics Fermi-Dirac distribution Fermi gas Fermi energy
OurBigBook.com Words: 346 Articles: 1
The Fermi sea is the zero-temperature many-fermion quantum state with every one-particle state below the Fermi energy occupied and every state above it empty. For a uniform isotropic Fermi gas, the occupied region in momentum space is a solid sphere; its boundary is the Fermi surface. These are momenta, not positions: filling the sphere does not put the particles in a ball in ordinary space. Opposite momentum contributions cancel, so the gas at rest has zero total momentum even though its total kinetic energy is positive.
The filled states are forced upward in energy by the Pauli exclusion principle. This is different from the zero-point energy of one particle confined by a boundary. At fixed number density, the Fermi energy remains positive as the box grows to the thermodynamic limit, while the lowest box confinement energy tends to zero. Low-energy excitations move particles from occupied states near the Fermi surface into nearby empty states; deep states have no nearby available destination. This explains why only a small fraction of electrons contribute to the low-temperature electron heat capacity.[ref]
