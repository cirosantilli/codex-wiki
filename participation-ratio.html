For an eigenvector normalized by ∑n=1N​∣ψn​∣2=1, the participation ratio in its site basis is
P=∑n=1N​∣ψn​∣41​,1≤P≤N.
(14)
It estimates how many sites carry appreciable probability. Equal weight on K sites and zero elsewhere gives P=K, irrespective of their wave phases. A single occupied site gives P=1; uniform probability on all sites gives P=N. These are exact constructed examples, not claims about particular Hamiltonian operators.
To prove the bounds, write pn​=∣ψn​∣2. Since 0≤pn​≤1, ∑n​pn2​≤∑n​pn​=1, giving P≥1. The Cauchy-Schwarz inequality gives 1=(∑n​pn​)2≤N∑n​pn2​, giving P≤N. Thus the measure has useful limits without assuming an exponential shape. Comparing P as N increases helps distinguish a state filling the sample from one occupying a fixed region, provided energy selection and disorder statistics are controlled.
