For the degree-N partial sum PN​(z)=1+z+⋯+zN of the geometric series, the finite identity (1−z)PN​(z)=1−zN+1 gives
1−z1​−PN​(z)=1−zzN+1​,z=1.
(55)
For any fixed 0<r<1, the triangle inequality then gives the uniform error bound
sup∣z∣≤r​​1−z1​−PN​(z)​≤1−rrN+1​.
(56)
Equality is attained at the positive real point z=r, so this is the exact worst-case error on that closed disk. It also proves uniform convergence on each such disk, not just pointwise convergence.
Given a positive tolerance ε, choosing N with rN+1/(1−r)≤ε turns a convergence statement into a finite stopping rule. For ε=10−3, degree ten suffices throughout ∣z∣≤1/2. Nearer the boundary, r=0.9 requires degree 87 and r=0.99 requires degree 1145. The required degree depends on both the tolerance and the region, not just on the name of the function.
