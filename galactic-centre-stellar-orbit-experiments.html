Galactic-centre stellar-orbit experiments (2002–2008)

 ... Branches of physics Astrophysics Astrophysical black hole Supermassive black hole Sagittarius A* Stellar orbits at the Galactic centre
OurBigBook.com Words: 134
Rainer Schödel, Reinhard Genzel and collaborators combined NTT imaging with VLT adaptive-optics infrared observations. Silicon-monoxide masers registered stellar images to the radio position of Sagittarius A*. Their 2002 observations included S2's closest approach; a fitted Kepler orbit constrained a concentrated central mass. Rejecting a particular extended cluster also required its density and survival assumptions.[ref]
Andrea Ghez and collaborators combined Keck speckle imaging and laser-guide-star adaptive optics with infrared spectra. Image fitting supplied angular positions; spectral lines supplied velocities. The 2008 analysis treated optical distortion, coordinate alignment and blended sources: a shifting photocentre can imitate motion. Joint angular and spectral fitting constrained both distance and mass, with assumptions about central radial motion affecting the result. Apparatus calibration and confusion tests are therefore part of the dynamical inference.[ref]
