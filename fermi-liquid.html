Fermi liquid

 Home Physics Branches of physics Statistical physics
OurBigBook.com Words: 5k Articles: 62
Why can a strongly interacting liquid resemble a gas? Its low-energy excitations can be long-lived Landau quasiparticles near a Fermi surface. Normal liquid helium-3 motivates this Fermi liquid description: interactions survive in effective excitation energies and interactions.[ref]
Interacting one-dimensional fermion systems often form Luttinger liquids; non-Fermi liquids also occur in higher dimensions. A Cooper instability can produce a paired phase.[ref]
The Fermi-surface renormalization-group scaling below organizes the low-energy interactions; zero sound tests whether their collective dynamics survives even when collisions are rare.

Fermi surface

Words: 482 Articles: 9
Fermi liquid OurBigBook.com
A Fermi surface is the codimension-one surface in momentum space separating occupied and unoccupied zero-temperature states of a fermionic system.

Free-electron Fermi circle in a square lattice (kF​a=2πz​)

Words: 63
Fermi surface OurBigBook.com
For a two-dimensional free electron gas with two spin states and z electrons per square cell of side a, counting occupied wavevectors gives z/a2=2πkF2​/(2π)2. The square first Brillouin zone has half-width π/a. A monovalent circle lies inside its faces; a divalent circle crosses its faces but not its corners. A real periodic potential can deform and reconnect the crossings, as in Fermi-surface reconstruction in a weak square potential; valence alone is insufficient to prove insulation.

Fermi-surface reconstruction in a weak square potential

Words: 103 Articles: 1
Fermi surface OurBigBook.com
For a free circle of radius R and square reciprocal period Q with 2​R<Q<2R, the circle cuts the faces but not the corners of the first zone. Weak Bragg gaps reconnect the folded branches into two inequivalent edge-centred electron pockets and one corner-centred hole pocket. The zero-potential limit retains band crossings instead of avoided crossings.

Circular-cap carrier count in a square Brillouin zone

Words: 50
Fermi-surface reconstruction in a weak square potential OurBigBook.com
For s=Q/(2R), each face cap has area C=R2[arccoss−s1−s2​]. The total second-band electron area is 4C; the first-band hole area is Q2−πR2+4C. Multiplying either area by the spin degeneracy two and dividing by (2π)2 gives the carrier densities. This is an infinitesimal-potential count, not an exact finite-gap formula.

Hole pocket

Words: 45
Fermi surface OurBigBook.com
A hole pocket surrounds unoccupied states near a band maximum. Its hole carrier density counts missing states in the nearly filled band, not all occupied electrons. In two dimensions the count is gAh​/(2π)2. Four corner pieces of a square-zone plot can be one pocket on the periodically identified zone.

Electron pocket

Words: 42
Fermi surface OurBigBook.com
An electron pocket surrounds occupied states near a conduction-band minimum. In two dimensions its carrier density is gA/(2π)2, where A is its total reciprocal-space area and g the unresolved degeneracy. Portions drawn at opposite zone faces can belong to the same pocket after periodic identification.

Fermi sphere (∣k∣≤kF​)

Words: 45
Fermi surface OurBigBook.com
For an isotropic three-dimensional free electron gas at zero temperature, occupied wavevectors fill a sphere of radius kF​. Including two spin states, n=kF3​/(3π2). The volume in reciprocal space counts occupied states; folding an extended-zone sphere into a first Brillouin zone does not remove states outside that first zone.

Fermi point (±kF​)

Words: 50
Fermi surface OurBigBook.com
A Fermi point is a point of the zero-temperature Fermi surface in one-dimensional momentum space. For a spin-degenerate band with one occupied interval, its two boundaries are usually −kF​ and +kF​. Low-energy excitations near them form left- and right-moving branches. A perturbation at wavevector 2kF​ can mix those branches, as in a Peierls distortion.

Fermi contour (En​(k)=EF​)

Words: 114 Articles: 1
Fermi surface OurBigBook.com
For a two-dimensional energy band, a Fermi contour is the set of wavevectors at the Fermi energy. It is the two-dimensional counterpart of a Fermi surface. At a regular value it consists locally of curves; at a band extremum or a crossing it can acquire isolated points or intersecting branches. At zero temperature, each band's occupied region satisfies En​(k)<EF​, so the contours must be labelled by band when determining occupations. The Fermi contours of an isotropic Rashba band illustrate why two contours need not belong to two different bands.

Fermi circle (kx2​+ky2​=kF2​)

Words: 34
Fermi contour OurBigBook.com
The circular zero-temperature occupation boundary of an isotropic two-dimensional electron gas. The occupied disk has area πkF2​; converting its area to particle number requires the reciprocal-space state density and a separately specified spin degeneracy.

Fermi-surface renormalization-group scaling

Words: 32
Fermi liquid OurBigBook.com
Fermi-surface renormalization-group scaling sends frequency and normal displacement from the Fermi surface to zero while leaving tangential momentum fixed. Generic interactions are irrelevant, while forward-scattering and opposite-momentum Cooper channels can remain marginal.

BCS theory

Words: 3k Articles: 37
Fermi liquid OurBigBook.com
BCS theory describes a superconducting instability caused by attractive interactions between opposite-momentum fermions near a Fermi surface.
John Bardeen, Leon Neil Cooper and John Robert Schrieffer developed BCS theory in 1957, using attraction mediated by phonons.[ref]
Weak-coupling Cooper pairs overlap. The Cooper instability reorganizes an unpaired Fermi surface; it is not Bose-Einstein condensation of compact, pre-existing molecules. The original paper explicitly distinguishes these pictures.[ref]
Two complementary clues made the microscopic problem concrete. The superconducting isotope effect connected the transition to phonons.[ref]
A 1957 transmission experiment used thin lead and tin films as samples in far-infrared and millimeter-microwave measurements. The measured transmission was converted to complex electrical conductivity, normalized to the normal-state value. Comparing film thickness, resistance, annealing and the two metals tested the response's reproducibility. Weak low-photon-energy absorption followed by an onset supported a superconducting energy gap; its extraction required a thin-film electrodynamic model.[ref]
The later Giaever superconducting tunnelling experiment provided another way to resolve the superconducting energy gap through quantum tunnelling.[ref] The derivations below explain what the model predicts, including the distinction between a single quasiparticle's minimum energy and the threshold for making two of them.

Fermionic Bogoliubov quasiparticle (Ek​=ξk2​+∣Δ∣2​)

Words: 52
BCS theory OurBigBook.com
A fermionic excitation obtained by a canonical particle–hole Bogoliubov transformation of a paired mean-field Hamiltonian. The uniform spin-singlet problem has the displayed positive energy and weights ∣u∣2=(1+ξ/E)/2, ∣v∣2=(1−ξ/E)/2. It interpolates between particle-like and hole-like excitations across the Fermi surface. These single-particle excitations differ from collective phase and amplitude modes of the order parameter.

BCS-BEC crossover

Words: 45
BCS theory OurBigBook.com
The continuous evolution, in suitable attractive Fermi systems, from weakly paired overlapping Cooper pairs to tightly bound composite bosons undergoing Bose-Einstein condensation. Pair formation, phase coherence and the chemical potential must be determined consistently across the crossover. A weak-coupling gap-temperature formula with fixed density of states is not uniformly valid through the molecular limit.

Asymmetric-cutoff BCS transition temperature (kB​Tc​≃1.13θσ​e−1/λ)

Words: 146 Articles: 1
BCS theory OurBigBook.com
For a constant effective density of states and attractive coupling λ, let the linearized BCS theory gap equation integrate tanh(ξ/(2kB​Tc​))/(2ξ) over −σ<ξ<θ, with positive cutoffs. Its even integrand makes the result the mean of two positive-cutoff logarithms. When kB​Tc​≪σ,θ, 1/λ≃21​ln(1.13θ/(kB​Tc​))+21​ln(1.13σ/(kB​Tc​)), giving the displayed geometric mean of cutoffs.
If a band bottom truncates the lower side, σ=min(μ,θ) for positive chemical potential μ. The weak-coupling limit at fixed μ/θ>0 must not be interchanged with an uncontrolled μ→0 extrapolation: the lower logarithm requires μ≫kB​Tc​ when μ<θ.

Density crossover of a fixed-coupling BCS cutoff model (nθ​=(2mθ/ℏ2)3/2/(3π2))

Words: 63
Asymmetric-cutoff BCS transition temperature OurBigBook.com
Set the chemical potential equal to the degenerate three-dimensional spin-degenerate Fermi energy, proportional to n2/3, and keep the effective coupling and upper cutoff fixed. The asymmetric-cutoff BCS transition temperature scales as n1/3 below nθ​ and saturates above it. At low density the predicted kB​Tc​/μ instead grows as n−1/3, eventually invalidating the degenerate and logarithmic approximations. This is a prescribed cutoff model, not a self-consistent treatment of the BCS-BEC crossover.

BCS pairing-field effective action (Seff​[Δ]=∫∣Δ∣2/λ−TrlnG−1[Δ])

Words: 191 Articles: 2
BCS theory OurBigBook.com
An attractive local pairing interaction can be decoupled with a complex Hubbard–Stratonovich transformation. Integrating the fermionic Grassmann variables gives an effective action for the pair field. Its uniform saddle is the BCS gap equation, and its slow-field expansion near the transition gives the Ginzburg-Landau theory of superconductivity. A trace over a fully doubled particle-hole basis requires the corresponding factor of one half; the displayed formula uses one independent pairing block.

Subtracted grand potential of the reduced BCS model (δΩ=V∣Δ∣2/λ−β−1∑kn​ln[(ωn2​+Ek2​)/(ωn2​+ξk2​)])

Words: 72
BCS pairing-field effective action OurBigBook.com
Subtracting the normal-state grand potential fixes the reference constant in the uniform BCS pairing-field effective action. Here ωn​=(2n+1)π/β has energy units and Ek2​=ξk2​+∣Δ∣2. The ratio cancels a field-independent determinant phase and produces a convergent high-frequency sum under the same momentum regulator. Differentiation gives the BCS gap equation. For a fixed attractive pairing shell, this is a finite regulated model; an unrestricted continuum contact interaction additionally needs ultraviolet renormalization. Its weak-coupling condensation density −νF​Δ2/2 uses a single-spin density of states per volume.

Fermionic pairing-block determinant normalization

Words: 60
BCS pairing-field effective action OurBigBook.com
A two-component Nambu spinor (ψk↑​,ψˉ​−k↓​) for every k accounts for both spin species once after relabelling. Its Grassmann integral contributes one functional determinant. A fully doubled spin-particle-hole basis instead has redundancy and requires one half in the trace logarithm or a corresponding Pfaffian convention. A reliable check is the zero-gap limit: the grand potential must become −2β−1∑k​ln(1+e−βξk​), with exactly two spin states and no invented vacuum offset.

Weak-coupling BCS thermodynamic ratios (2Δ(0)/(kB​Tc​)=2πe−γE​,ΔC/(γn​Tc​)=12/[7ζ(3)])

Words: 50
BCS theory OurBigBook.com
The isotropic weak-coupling BCS theory predicts the gap ratio approximately 3.528 and the electronic heat-capacity jump approximately 1.426 in units of the normal-state heat capacity at the transition. These compare distinct measurements of the same paired state. They are conditional predictions of the weak-coupling fully gapped model, rather than universal numbers for all superconductors.

The gap feature in a superconducting single-particle excitation spectrum. In a simple BCS model, excitations have Ek​=ξk2​+∣Δ(k)∣2​. The superconducting gap function specifies the pairing amplitude and its phase; its magnitude need not be constant, and nodes can leave gapless excitations.

A complex momentum-dependent pairing amplitude. Its crystal-symmetry transformation, nodes and relative signs distinguish candidate superconducting states. Actual enclosed flux also depends on loop inductance and junction currents; the measurement requires controls against ordinary trapped flux.[ref]

Cooper pair

Words: 248 Articles: 4
BCS theory OurBigBook.com
A Cooper pair is a correlated pair of fermions associated with superconductivity. The conventional BCS ground state uses opposite-momentum, opposite-spin pair creation operators ck↑†​c−k↓†​. Their total momentum is zero for every relative momentum k; zero total momentum does not restrict k to zero.

Cooper problem

Words: 128 Articles: 2
Cooper pair OurBigBook.com
Two added electrons move above a frozen filled Fermi sea and attract within a thin energy shell. The Pauli-blocked continuum has a logarithmic pair susceptibility, so an arbitrarily weak net attractive constant interaction produces a state below the two-particle threshold at zero temperature. This two-added-particle result is not by itself a full many-electron superconducting state.
Cooper pair binding energy (B=−E)
Words: 36
Cooper problem OurBigBook.com
For shell width D, attraction g>0 and constant single-spin density of states ρ, 1=(gρ/2)log[(2D+B)/B] gives B=2D/[exp(2/(gρ))−1]. The pair energy relative to the free threshold is −B. At weak coupling this scale is nonanalytic in g.
Cooper shell projection
Words: 41
Cooper problem OurBigBook.com
The pairing Hamiltonian is projected onto empty states in a specified shell above the inert Fermi sea. A state restricted to that shell is an eigenstate of this projected model, not necessarily of a contact interaction that remains unrestricted at all momenta.

Pairing gap (Δ)

Words: 78
Cooper pair OurBigBook.com
A pairing gap is an energy scale for fermionic excitations above a paired superfluid or superconductor ground state. In a simple isotropic paired system the quasiparticle dispersion has the form Ek​=ξk2​+∣Δ∣2​, where ξk​ measures the unpaired single-particle energy relative to the chemical potential. A nonzero minimum gap gives exponentially small fermionic thermal occupations when kB​T≪∣Δ∣. If the gap varies with direction and has nodes, gapless directions can instead produce power-law thermodynamics. Collective excitations must be counted separately from the gapped fermionic quasiparticles.

BCS ground state (∏k​(uk​+vk​ck↑†​c−k↓†​)∣0⟩)

Words: 114 Articles: 1
BCS theory OurBigBook.com
The BCS ground state is the normalized product of empty and occupied pair states ∏k​(uk​+vk​bk†​)∣0⟩. For uk2​+vk2​=1, each pair factor is normalized. The inverse Bogoliubov transformation annihilates it, so it is the vacuum of positive-energy quasiparticles. Although individual factors use opposite momenta, k runs over the relative pair momenta; it is not restricted to k=0.

Cooper-pair pseudospin coherent state

Words: 58
BCS ground state OurBigBook.com
The empty and doubly occupied states of a paired momentum block form a two-level space. Define Sk+​=ak↑†​a−k↓†​, Sk−​=a−k↓​ak↑​ and Skz​=(nk↑​+n−k↓​−1)/2. They obey the spin algebra on that space. The normalized factor uk​∣0⟩+vk​∣pair⟩ is a spin-one-half coherent state; the product over blocks is the BCS ground state. Pauli exclusion prevents interpreting a block as an unrestricted harmonic-oscillator pair mode.

BCS coherence factors (uk2​+vk2​=1)

Words: 244 Articles: 2
BCS theory OurBigBook.com
For real gap and the particle-hole block (ξk​−Δk​​−Δk​−ξk​​), the BCS coherence factors obey uk2​=(1+ξk​/Ek​)/2, vk2​=(1−ξk​/Ek​)/2 and uk​vk​=Δk​/(2Ek​), where Ek​=ξk2​+Δk2​​. They diagonalize the Bogoliubov--de Gennes Hamiltonian and give the empty/pair amplitudes of the BCS ground state. Their relative sign depends on the pairing convention.
For an original check, write x=ξk​/Δ with constant real Δ>0. At x=0, the BCS coherence factors give uk2​=vk2​=1/2. At x=1, they give (uk2​,vk2​)=(0.853553,0.146447); at x=−1 these values exchange places. Thus the zero-temperature occupation vk2​ rounds the normal Fermi sea's step over an energy interval of order Δ. This mixing is strongest at the Fermi surface, where the BCS quasiparticle spectrum has its minimum.

BCS population above the normal-state Fermi surface (ΔN/N≃3Δ/(4μ))

Words: 82
BCS coherence factors OurBigBook.com
For a uniform isotropic weak-gap BCS state, the zero-temperature electron occupation is n(ξ)=(1−ξ/ξ2+Δ2​)/2. The number above the normal-state Fermi surface is approximately D(μ)∫0∞​n(ξ)dξ. The integral equals Δ/2, while a three-dimensional parabolic band including both spins has D(μ)=3N/(2μ). This gives the displayed leading fraction.
The result measures redistribution relative to a normal Fermi sea, not bosonic single-mode condensate depletion or quasiparticle occupation. A different band dimension or anisotropic gap requires its actual density-of-states or momentum integral. Material estimates need specified gap and chemical-potential scales.

Hebel-Slichter coherence peak

Words: 60
BCS coherence factors OurBigBook.com
In a conventional isotropic spin-singlet superconductor, the nuclear spin-lattice relaxation rate can rise just below the transition before falling at lower temperature. The BCS coherence factors and enhanced near-gap density of states produce the coherence enhancement. Nuclear relaxation and ultrasonic attenuation have different coherence factors, so their contrasting temperature dependences test pairing structure rather than only the existence of a gap.[ref]

The BCS anomalous average is the pair expectation ⟨bk​⟩=⟨c−k↓​ck↑​⟩. In the BCS ground state it is uk​vk​=Δk​/(2Ek​) for real gap at zero temperature. At temperature T it becomes Δk​tanh(Ek​/(2kB​T))/(2Ek​). Substitution into the pairing-field definition gives the BCS gap equation.

Mass-anisotropy enhancement of a weak-coupling BCS gap (Δ≃2εc​e−1/(λ0​γ1/3))

For a fixed-density balanced two-spin Fermi gas with masses (m,m,γm), the Fermi energy scales as γ−1/3 while the single-spin Fermi-level density of states scales as γ1/3. Constant-shell BCS gap equation (Δ=ΩD​/sinh(1/(νs​V)))

Words: 422 Articles: 1
BCS gap equation OurBigBook.com
An attractive interaction Vk,k′​=−V/V within ∣ξk​∣,∣ξk′​∣<ΩD​ gives a constant gap inside that energy shell and zero outside. With approximately constant single-spin density of states νs​ per volume,
1=νs​V∫0ΩD​​ξ2+Δ2​dξ​=νs​Varsinh(ΩD​/Δ),Δ=sinh(1/(νs​V))ΩD​​.
(2)
The weak coupling limit is Δ≃2ΩD​e−1/(νs​V). A density counting both spin species is twice νs​ and must be divided by two. The shell cutoff is a relative-energy condition, even when every Cooper pair has zero total momentum.
BCS dimensionless coupling (λ=N0​V)
Words: 362
Constant-shell BCS gap equation OurBigBook.com
Use N0​=νs​ for the normal-state single-spin density of states per unit energy and volume at the Fermi surface. Write the attraction as −V/V, with V>0, and define λ=N0​V.[ref]
Since V/V has units of energy, V has units of energy times volume, making λ dimensionless. Set ΩD​=ℏωD​, an energy cutoff using the Debye frequency ωD​, rather than a frequency itself. These conventions also fix the meaning of the earlier constant-shell BCS gap equation.
Here is the reduction of the BCS gap equation in these conventions. Substituting the zero-temperature BCS anomalous average gives
Δ=VV​∑∣ξk​∣<ΩD​​2ξk2​+Δ2​Δ​.
(3)
The k sum counts one spin species. For Δ>0, cancelling Δ and replacing this sum by an integral with constant N0​ gives
1=2N0​V​∫−ΩD​ΩD​​ξ2+Δ2​dξ​=λ∫0ΩD​​ξ2+Δ2​dξ​.
(4)
The factor two from the symmetric integral cancels the 1/2 in the BCS anomalous average; it is not an extra spin degeneracy. Substituting ξ=Δsinht evaluates the integral as arsinh(ΩD​/Δ). Solving gives
Δ(0)=sinh(1/λ)ℏωD​​=1−e−2/λ2ℏωD​e−1/λ​≃2ℏωD​exp[−N0​V1​],λ≪1.
(5)
The first equality is exact within the constant-density of states, sharp-cutoff mean-field approximation; the last is the weak coupling asymptote. Extending the first equality to large λ does not make this simplified model an accurate description of a strongly coupled material.
For an original numerical example, choose ℏωD​=20 meV and λ=0.25. The exact constant-shell BCS gap equation gives Δ(0)=0.732871 meV; the weak coupling formula gives 0.732626 meV. Its fractional underestimate, measured against the exact result, is exactly e−2/λ=e−8≃0.03355%. At λ=1 that error is e−2≃13.53%, so the two formulas are no longer interchangeable. No material parameters have been fitted here.
At nonzero temperature, substitute the finite-temperature BCS anomalous average instead. The same cancellation yields
1=λ∫0ΩD​​ξ2+Δ(T)2​tanh(ξ2+Δ(T)2​/(2kB​T))​dξ.
(6)
At the superconducting transition temperature take the continuous Δ→0 limit. The integrand at ξ=0 then has the finite limiting value 1/(2kB​Tc​), so no divergent endpoint needs to be inserted into a numerical quadrature. For kB​Tc​≪ΩD​, the resulting integral gives kB​Tc​≃1.134ΩD​e−1/λ and 2Δ(0)/(kB​Tc​)≃3.528. These ratios concern the isotropic weak coupling model.[ref]

BCS quasiparticle spectrum (Ek​=ξk2​+∣Δ∣2​)

Words: 510 Articles: 3
BCS theory OurBigBook.com
The BCS quasiparticle spectrum is the positive excitation energy obtained by diagonalizing the paired particle-hole block. Here the weak coupling shell model has Δ≪ΩD​: the minimum lies inside the pairing shell, and states outside it cost more. To derive it for a uniform isotropic real Δ>0, measure the normal-state energy from the chemical potential, ξk​=ϵk​−μ, and use the same sign convention as the BCS coherence factors:
Hk​=(ξk​−Δ​−Δ−ξk​​),det(Hk​−εI)=ε2−ξk2​−Δ2=0.
(7)
Its eigenvalues are ε±​=±Ek​, where Ek​=ξk2​+Δ2​. The positive Ek​ is the cost of creating a quasiparticle above the BCS ground state. The lower branch −Ek​ is its particle-hole partner in this doubled matrix description, not an excitation that lowers the ground state energy without bound.
The location and value of the minimum follow directly:
dξdE​=ξ2+Δ2​ξ​,dξ2d2E​=(ξ2+Δ2)3/2Δ2​>0.
(8)
The unique minimum as a function of ξ is E(0)=Δ, at the normal-state Fermi surface. On an isotropic three-dimensional Fermi surface it occurs for every direction of k, rather than at a unique point of momentum space. Near that minimum,
E(ξ)=Δ+2Δξ2​+O(ξ4/Δ3).
(9)
Farther away, E(ξ)≃∣ξ∣ when Δ≪∣ξ∣≪ΩD​. For the illustrative λ=0.25 example, a state at ξ=Δ costs 2​Δ≃1.03644 meV rather than the minimum 0.732871 meV.

An elementary excitation of the paired mean-field state whose electron operator has mixed creation and annihilation components. Its positive excitation energy is Ek​=ξk2​+∣Δk​∣2​. At zero temperature the BCS ground state has zero occupation of these positive-energy modes, even though physical electrons have a smooth nontrivial momentum occupation.

BCS electronic density of states (Ns​(E)=gF​∣E∣/E2−Δ2​)

Words: 94
BCS quasiparticle spectrum OurBigBook.com
For a particle-hole-symmetric normal density of states gF​ and isotropic superconducting energy gap Δ>0, the electron spectral density is zero for ∣E∣<Δ and has the displayed form outside the gap. The BCS coherence factors weight the two roots ξ=±E2−Δ2​, where ξ is normal-state energy relative to the Fermi energy. At positive E, their electron weights (1+ξ/E)/2 sum to one. Multiplying their sum by the Jacobian ∣dξ/dE∣=E/E2−Δ2​ proves the formula. This electron spectral density differs from the unweighted count of all positive-energy quasiparticles, which receives both roots. Its gap-edge divergence is integrable and approaches the normal electronic density at large ∣E∣.

Superconducting energy gap (Δ,2Δ)

Words: 133
BCS quasiparticle spectrum OurBigBook.com
In this fully gapped isotropic BCS quasiparticle spectrum, Δ is the minimum single-quasiparticle excitation energy. The interval between −Δ and +Δ has width 2Δ. A process that creates two quasiparticles from the BCS ground state has an energetic threshold 2Δ, subject also to its transition-selection rules.[ref]
For example, two independently created excitations with ξ1​=ξ2​=0 cost E1​+E2​=2Δ. With the chosen cutoff example this is 1.46574 meV, while adding one excitation costs at least 0.732871 meV. This factor of two matters when comparing photon absorption with the Giaever superconducting tunnelling experiment. Quantum tunnelling from a normal metal can add one excitation to a sample exhibiting superconductivity; with two gapped electrodes, their excitation costs add. A minimum excitation cost is an energetic condition; the measured onset can additionally depend on temperature and on the available transitions.

BCS coherence length (ξ0​=ℏvF​/(πΔ(0)))

Words: 84
BCS theory OurBigBook.com
The clean isotropic weak-coupling BCS coherence length is conventionally ξ0​=ℏvF​/(πΔ(0)), with Fermi velocity vF​. It characterizes the spatial extent of pairing correlations.[ref]
The scale follows from the same spectral calculation. Linearizing the normal energy near the Fermi surface gives ξk​≃ℏvF​(k−kF​). The region ∣ξk​∣≲Δ therefore has radial width δk∼Δ/(ℏvF​). Fourier analysis associates this width with a distance ℓ∼1/δk∼ℏvF​/Δ. This argument establishes the scale, not the conventional factor 1/π. A small superconducting energy gap consequently permits pairing correlations over many interparticle spacings.

Superconducting isotope effect (α=−dlogTc​/dlogM)

The superconducting isotope effect is the dependence of the superconducting transition temperature on isotopic mass M at fixed chemical composition. Its discovery in 1950 was a crucial clue that phonons participate in conventional superconductivity.[ref]
The model prediction can be derived from two assumptions. For harmonic motion with a fixed restoring-force constant K, a phonon frequency scales as ω∝K/M​. If the BCS dimensionless coupling is independent of M, the weak coupling result kB​Tc​≃1.134ℏωD​e−1/λ then gives Tc​∝M−1/2 and α=1/2. Changes in the effective interaction can change this exponent; it is not a universal law for every material exhibiting superconductivity. For a stipulated mass ratio M2​/M1​=1.04, the ideal model predicts Tc,2​/Tc,1​=1/1.04​≃0.98058. This is an original scaling example, not a pair of measured transition temperatures.

Landau quasiparticle (ξp​)

A Landau quasiparticle is a fermionic excitation connected continuously to a Fermi gas particle when interactions preserve the phase. Near an isotropic Fermi surface, its energy relative to the chemical potential is ξp​=ϵp​−μ≃vF​(p−pF​), with Fermi momentum pF​ and Fermi velocity vF​.[ref]
The Pauli exclusion principle restricts decay. For a three-dimensional normal Fermi liquid with regular short-range interactions, a representative low-energy lifetime model is
τp​ℏ​=AE∗​ξp2​+(πkB​T)2​,A>0,
(10)
Here E∗​ is an energy scale and A is dimensionless; sharpness requires ℏ/τp​≪∣ξp​∣ at zero temperature.[ref]
For an original example, stipulate A=1 and T=0. At ∣ξp​∣/E∗​=0.02, the decay energy is 0.0004E∗​, just 2% of the excitation energy; halving ∣ξp​∣ reduces that ratio to 1%. Approaching the Fermi surface improves the particle description even though the microscopic interaction has not weakened. This quadratic illustration is three-dimensional; low-dimensional corrections require separate analysis.

Low-energy Fermi-liquid quasiparticle lifetime (τ−1∝(ϵ−μ)2+(πkB​T)2)

Landau quasiparticle OurBigBook.com
The Pauli exclusion principle restricts final states available to a low-energy Landau quasiparticle and the particle–hole pair it excites. In a regular three-dimensional Fermi liquid, this phase-space restriction makes the decay rate quadratic in excitation energy or temperature. The excitation's width divided by its energy therefore vanishes at the Fermi surface, allowing an asymptotically sharp quasiparticle despite interactions.

Zero sound (c0​)

Words: 470 Articles: 3
Fermi liquid OurBigBook.com
Zero sound is a collisionless collective Fermi surface deformation sustained by Landau quasiparticle interactions.[ref]
A useful analogy is a crowd whose members respond to their neighbors' density without repeatedly bumping into them: the restoring interaction can remain effective when collisions cannot relax the distribution within one cycle. Zero sound is collective; it is not a single Landau quasiparticle outrunning every other excitation.

Isotropic zero-sound model (1+F0s​[1−2s​logs−1s+1​]=0)

Consider a neutral, isotropic, unpolarized three-dimensional spin-one-half Fermi liquid, with only F0s​>0 nonzero. A longitudinal density wave with angular frequency ω and wavevector magnitude q has s=ω/(qvF​)=c0​/vF​. The collisionless angular equation for the energy displacement ν(u) of the Fermi surface is
(s−u)ν(u)=uF0s​⟨ν⟩,⟨ν⟩=21​∫−11​ν(u)du,u=cosθ.
(15)
Averaging gives
1=2F0s​​∫−11​s−uu​du=F0s​[2s​logs−1s+1​−1].
(16)
The bracket decreases from infinity to zero on 1<s<∞: each F0s​>0 gives one undamped root above the small-q particle-hole continuum.[ref]
For an original example, F0s​=2 gives s=1.16474145. Since F1s​=0 here, the Principle of Galilean relativity gives m∗=m and taking the hydrodynamic limit before T→0 gives c1​/vF​=(1+F0s​)/3​=1. Stipulating vF​=50 ms−1 gives c0​≃58.24 ms−1 and c1​=50 ms−1. These are model choices, not helium-3 measurements. At large F0s​, expanding the logarithm gives s∼F0s​/3​; for small positive F0s​, the root approaches 1 exponentially closely, so numerical resolution matters. A charged electron liquid needs its long-range interaction included; this neutral model is not its long-wavelength density mode.

First-to-zero-sound crossover (ωτ)

Words: 84
Zero sound OurBigBook.com
Angular-relaxation collision time τ distinguishes first sound (ωτ≪1) from zero sound (ωτ≫1). Collisions establish local thermodynamic equilibrium in the former.[ref]
For the figure's stipulated law ωτ=(T/T×​)−2, cooling from T=5T×​ to T=T×​/5 changes ωτ from 0.04 to 25, without changing the driving angular frequency. The crossover temperature is defined by ωτ(T×​)=1. Calling zero sound a high-frequency mode therefore means high relative to the collision rate 1/τ, while its excitation energy must still remain within the low-energy domain of the Fermi liquid.

Abel-Anderson-Wheatley zero-sound experiment

Words: 135
Zero sound OurBigBook.com
In 1966, William Russell Abel, Ansel Cochran Anderson and John Charles Wheatley sent acoustic waves through bulk liquid helium-3 at 0.32 atm and frequencies 15.4 and 45.5 MHz. A quartz delay line and receiving acoustic transducer bounded a 0.138 mm liquid gap; shielding reduced electrical pickup. Powdered cerium magnesium nitrate provided magnetic thermometry during a slow warm-up. Pulse amplitudes measured acoustic attenuation; phase difference comparison measured velocity. Calibration pulses checked gain; early received cycles avoided multiple reflections. On cooling through a maximum, acoustic attenuation changed from α∝ω2/T∗2 to α∝T∗2 with a velocity shift supporting zero sound; T∗ is their magnetic temperature scale. Weak acoustic attenuation was harder to resolve.[ref]
This 1966 test followed Lev Landau's 1962 Nobel Prize in Physics.[ref]
The experiment concerned normal liquid helium-3, distinct from the later discovery of superfluid helium-3.[ref] Landau's original papers are theoretical calculations, not reports of an apparatus.
