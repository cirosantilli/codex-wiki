Optical gain (g)

 Home Physics Branches of physics Optics Laser
OurBigBook.com Words: 625 Articles: 7
Optical gain is amplification of light by a medium. Here the small-signal intensity gain coefficient g has units m−1: for fixed populations, a weak beam obeys
dzdI​=gI,I(z)=I(0)egz,g=σ(N2​−N1​)
(10)
in the nondegenerate resonant two-level model, with common transition cross-section σ. Thus g includes the competition between stimulated emission and photon absorption on that transition. Separate passive intensity loss α gives I(z)=I(0)e(g−α)z. Both coefficients describe light intensity, not wave amplitude; the corresponding field-amplitude exponent is half as large. [ref].
For g=0.30m−1, α=0.10m−1 and z=0.50m, the single-pass intensity multiplier is e0.10≈1.10517. This is amplification during one passage, before mirror losses. Population inversion can therefore yield positive optical gain while the complete oscillator remains below laser threshold.
