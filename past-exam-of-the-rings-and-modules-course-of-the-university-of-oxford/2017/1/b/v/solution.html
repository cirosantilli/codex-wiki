OurBigBook.com Words: 138
No field has six elements. A finite field has positive characteristic: the sequence 0,1,2⋅1,… must repeat. By part (a), this characteristic is a prime number p. Translation by 1 partitions the field into cycles of length p, since returning to a starting point after k steps is equivalent to k1=0. Thus p divides six and p=2 or p=3.
The multiples of 1 form a copy of the prime field Fp​. For completeness, the inverse of a nonzero residue a lies among these multiples: the integer Bezout identity ab+cp=1 gives (a1)(b1)=1. The entire field is a vector space over this subfield. A maximal linearly independent set is finite and spans, so it is a basis of some positive size d. Unique basis coordinates give exactly pd elements. This proves finite field cardinality is a prime power here. But six is neither a power of two nor a power of three, a contradiction.
