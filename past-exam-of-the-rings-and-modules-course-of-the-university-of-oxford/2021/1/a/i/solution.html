We prove the stated Eisenstein criterion directly. Assume n≥1. Suppose f=gh in the polynomial ring R[X]. Since the principal ideal domain R is an integral domain, polynomial degree is additive under polynomial multiplication, and the product of the leading coefficients of g,h is the leading coefficient 1 of f. These two coefficients are therefore units. Multiplying g and h by inverse units, without changing their product, makes both factors monic polynomials.
If either factor is a constant polynomial, that factor is now 1, so the original factor was a unit. It remains to rule out r=degg>0 and s=degh>0.
The prime element p generates a maximal ideal in the principal ideal domain R. Indeed, if (p)⊆(d), then p=de, and the irreducible element property of p forces either (d)=R or (d)=(p). Consequently k=R/(p) is a field. Apply reduction modulo an ideal to every coefficient. The monic polynomial factors retain their polynomial degrees, and the hypotheses give
g​h=f​=Xnin k[X].

The polynomial ring over a field is a unique factorization domain, and X is an irreducible polynomial. Thus its only monic polynomial divisors in Xn are powers of X. Hence g​=Xr and h=Xs. Both have zero constant term, so p∣g(0) and p∣h(0). This gives p2∣g(0)h(0)=a0​, a contradiction.
The monic polynomial f has positive polynomial degree, so it is a nonzero nonunit. Every factorization therefore has a unit factor, proving
f is irreducible in R[X]​.
