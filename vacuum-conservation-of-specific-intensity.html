Vacuum conservation of specific intensity

 ... Physics Branches of physics Optics Radiometry Radiance Spectral radiance
OurBigBook.com Words: 227
In a transparent, stationary vacuum with Euclidean geometry and observers at rest in the same frame, the radiative transfer equation has zero absorption and emission, so dIν​/ds=0 along a light ray. The same specific intensity reaches the observer without geometric dilution. More generally, Iν​/ν3 is a Lorentz invariant; collisionless propagation preserves this ratio along a ray even with gravitational or cosmological redshift, rather than preserving Iν​ at shifted frequencies. [ref].
The elementary geometry is instructive. Two facing surface elements separated by distance s exchange spectral radiant flux
d3Φ=Iν​s2cosθ1​cosθ2​​dA1​dA2​dν.
(17)
The receiver subtends dΩ2​=cosθ2​dA2​/s2 at the source. Describing that same traveling beam at the receiver instead uses dΩ1​=cosθ1​dA1​/s2. Both descriptions have identical geometric factors, so conservation of its radiant flux gives equal specific intensities at the two ends. This compares the same propagation direction at both ends, not emission in the opposite direction.
A uniform source remains equally bright per resolved direction under these assumptions, but occupies a smaller apparent solid angle farther away. For a small source of projected area A⊥​ at distance d and a small receiver facing it, irradiance is approximately E=LΩsource​≃LA⊥​/d2. The radiant flux intercepted by receiver area Ad​ is approximately EAd​. With L=10Wm−2sr−1, A⊥​=0.020m2 and Ad​=10−4m2, distances 10 and 20m give E≃0.002 and 0.0005Wm−2, and received radiant fluxes of 0.20 and 0.050μW. The inverse-square law concerns collected radiation from the whole source; L remains 10 at either distance.
