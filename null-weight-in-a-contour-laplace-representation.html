Null weight in a contour Laplace representation (∫Γ​h(ζ)ezζdζ=0)

 ... Area of mathematics Analysis Asymptotic expansion Laplace's method Laplace integral Laplace integral solution
OurBigBook.com Words: 187
A null weight contributes zero to a Laplace integral on its domain of convergence. It can be added to a weight without changing the represented function, so an integration-by-parts differential equation for the weight is not an unconditional uniqueness theorem.
For example, let Γ enter along angle π/3 from infinity to zero and leave along angle 2π/3 to infinity. The entire weight h(ζ)=eζ2 is null for every complex z. Throughout the wedge between those rays, Re(ζ2)≤−r2/2; the factor ezζ is dominated by this quadratic decay. Closing the contour by a large circular arc and applying the Cauchy integral theorem therefore proves the zero integral. All derivatives with respect to z converge locally uniformly as well.
On the same contour, the quartic weight eζ4/4 gives a nontrivial entire solution. Its rays can be deformed within the quartic decay sectors for a contour integral to angles π/4 and 3π/4, where the value at z=0 is −2​4−3/4Γ(1/4)=0. Adding the null weight changes neither this integral nor its vanishing endpoint terms, but the sum does not satisfy g′=ζ3g. Thus the usual Laplace method selects a convenient weight by cancelling the interior integrand separately; a necessity claim needs additional injectivity assumptions.
