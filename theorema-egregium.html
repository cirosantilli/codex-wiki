Theorema Egregium (KN​∘f=KM​)

 Home Mathematics Area of mathematics Geometry and topology Differential geometry
OurBigBook.com Words: 265
For smooth surfaces, Gaussian curvature depends only on the first fundamental form and its first two coordinate derivatives. Consequently any smooth local isometry f:M→N satisfies KN​(f(p))=KM​(p). The surprise is that K is defined using the shape operator, which describes bending in space, yet its value can be recovered from measurements made entirely within the surface.
In his 1827 paper, Carl Friedrich Gauss expressed Gaussian curvature through the metric coefficients and their partial derivatives in Article 11, then stated its invariance under development in Article 12. Article 13 explicitly compares a plane with surfaces developable onto it, including cylindrical surfaces. The paper was presented on 8 October 1827 and printed in 1828; the Morehead–Hiltebeitel English translation appeared in 1902. [ref]
A plane and a circular cylinder can have different principal curvatures while both have K=0. Their matching first fundamental forms exhibit the actual local isometry. In contrast, no nonempty open patch of a sphere of radius R can be flattened smoothly into a plane while preserving its first fundamental form: 1/R2 cannot become zero. Making the patch small reduces practical distortion, but never produces an exact local isometry. This is a geometric obstruction to a perfectly distance-preserving flat map of a spherical world, independent of where one places a seam.
The following proof outline connects the two descriptions of Gaussian curvature. For a parametric surface with metric coefficients gij​, decompose its second derivatives by the Gauss formula
Xij​=Γijk​Xk​+hij​N.
(67)
Taking tangent vector inner products determines the lowered Christoffel symbols solely from the Riemannian metric:
Γijk​=21​(∂i​gjk​+∂j​gik​−∂k​gij​).
(68)
Comparing Xijk​ and Xikj​ gives the Gauss equation
R1212​=h11​h22​−h122​.
(69)
Consequently
K=det(gij​)h11​h22​−h122​​=det(gij​)R1212​​,
(70)
where R1212​ is the corresponding component of the intrinsic Riemann curvature tensor, with the sign convention in the displayed Gauss equation. The final expression uses only gij​ and its first two derivatives.
