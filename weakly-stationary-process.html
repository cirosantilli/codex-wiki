Weakly stationary process

 Home Mathematics Area of mathematics Probability and statistics Time series Stationary process
OurBigBook.com Words: 2k Articles: 39
A real stochastic process (Xt​)t∈Z​ is a weakly stationary process if it has finite second moments, a constant expected value μ, and an autocovariance depending only on the lag of a time series:
E[Xt​]=μ,E[Xt2​]<∞,Cov(Xt+h​,Xt​)=γ(h)for all t,h∈Z.
(4)
In particular its variance is the same at every time, Var(Xt​)=γ(0). This is often called second-order stationarity. It makes observations from different parts of a record comparable through the same autocorrelation function. Bartlett uses this second-order meaning of stationarity in Introduction to Time Series Analysis, lecture 2: mean, covariance, and stationarity.
For a worked contrast, let εt​ be centered white noise of variance σ2>0. The process Xt​=5+εt​ has mean 5, variance σ2, and zero autocovariance at nonzero lags, so it is weakly stationary. The process Yt​=t+εt​ has exactly the same covariance structure, but its mean is t, so it is not weakly stationary. Constant variance alone is insufficient.
Weak stationarity need not imply strict stationarity. For example, choose independent random variables at all integer times, using a standard normal distribution at even times and a Rademacher distribution at odd times. Every coordinate has zero mean and unit variance, and distinct coordinates have zero covariance. Nevertheless a shift by one changes the marginal probability distribution from continuous to two-point. This also illustrates why weak white noise need not be identically distributed.
