Graphene

 Home Chemistry Chemical substance Chemical element Carbon
OurBigBook.com Words: 1k Articles: 13
Graphene is a single layer of carbon chemical atoms arranged in a honeycomb lattice. A nearby gate can select predominantly electron or electron hole conduction, as demonstrated by the graphene exfoliation and field-effect experiments.[ref]
A useful question is how geometry changes motion. The nearest-neighbour model of graphene starts with three equivalent bonds from each site and two sites per primitive cell. Interference between those three hopping paths makes two energy bands meet; near their meeting, energy grows linearly with distance in wave vector. The resulting Dirac cones explain why an atomic sheet can imitate one aspect of a relativistic particle while remaining an ordinary material with contacts, defects and boundaries.
