Convective adjustment

 ... Physics Branches of physics Geophysics Climate change Climate model Radiative-convective climate column
OurBigBook.com Words: 183
Convective adjustment replaces an excessively steep vertical temperature gradient by an allowed profile while respecting the selected energy budget. It is a closure for unresolved convection, not a direct simulation of individual rising parcels. A prescribed critical atmospheric lapse rate is a modeling assumption; it need not equal the dry adiabatic lapse rate.
For an original dry two-slab example, take fixed equal heat capacities and slab centers separated by Δz=2km. Initially T1​=300K below and T2​=280K above. Stipulate a permitted atmospheric lapse rate Γc​=6Kkm−1. The initial difference 20K exceeds the allowed 12K. Conservation of the sum of the two sensible energies requires T1′​+T2′​=580K, while the adjusted gradient requires T1′​−T2′​=12K. Hence T1′​=296K and T2′​=284K. Heat removed below equals heat added above. Simply holding the lower slab fixed and raising the upper slab to 288K would introduce external energy.
For unequal constant heat capacities C1​,C2​, the same two constraints instead give
T1′​=C1​+C2​C1​T1​+C2​T2​+C2​Γc​Δz​,T2′​=T1′​−Γc​Δz.
(11)
Apply this adjustment only when the original gradient exceeds the threshold. A moist adjustment also has to account for latent heat and changing composition; conservation of this dry sensible-energy expression alone is then insufficient.
