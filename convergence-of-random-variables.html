Contiguity of probability measures (Qn​◃Pn​)

Words: 125 Articles: 2
Convergence of random variables OurBigBook.com
A sequence Qn​ is contiguous with respect to Pn​ if Pn​(An​)→0 implies Qn​(An​)→0 for every measurable sequence of events. It transfers negligible-event assertions, including consistency, between changing sampling laws. It need not imply small total variation distance.

Le Cam's first lemma

Words: 68
Contiguity of probability measures OurBigBook.com
Let Ln​ be the probability density function of the absolutely continuous part of Qn​ relative to Pn​. If Ln​ converges in distribution under Pn​ to L>0 almost surely with EL=1, then Pn​,Qn​ are mutually contiguous. Indeed, ELn​≤1 and convergence to a mean-one limit imply uniform integrability and vanishing singular mass, giving forward contiguity. For reverse contiguity, Pn​(An​)≤Pn​(Ln​≤c)+Qn​(An​)/c, and one first takes n→∞ and then c↓0.

Mutual contiguity (Pn​◃Qn​,Qn​◃Pn​)

Words: 22
Contiguity of probability measures OurBigBook.com
Two sequences of probability laws are mutually contiguous when contiguity of probability measures holds in both directions. A positive mean-one limit of their likelihood ratio is a standard sufficient criterion.

Convergence in L1 means that the expected value of the absolute error tends to zero. It implies convergence in probability and convergence of expected values.

Scheffé lemma (fn​→f a.e., ∫fn​→∫f ⟹ ∥fn​−f∥1​→0(fn​,f≥0))

Words: 44
Convergence in L1 OurBigBook.com
For nonnegative integrable functions converging almost everywhere to an integrable f, convergence of their integrals implies L1 convergence. Indeed min(fn​,f)→f almost everywhere and is dominated by f, so the dominated convergence theorem gives convergence of its integral. Use ∣fn​−f∣=fn​+f−2min(fn​,f). This applies to probability densities, giving convergence in total variation distance.

If square-integrable random variables satisfy Xn​→X almost surely, then Xn​→X in L2 exactly when E[Xn2​]→E[X2]. Boundedness in L2 and almost-sure convergence first give weak convergence in the Hilbert space L2; weak convergence together with convergence of norms gives strong convergence.

Atoms obstruct a continuous distributional limit

Words: 73
Convergence in distribution OurBigBook.com
If Zn​ has probability at least p at zn​, with p>0 and zn​→z, every weak limit has probability at least p at z. Apply the closed-set bound of the Portmanteau theorem to arbitrarily small closed intervals containing z and eventually zn​, then decrease their radii. Thus a persistent atom prevents convergence to a continuous law, including a chi-squared law. This provides a direct check when bounded-information estimation fails standard asymptotic-normal theory.

Marginal weak convergence does not control sums

Words: 69
Convergence in distribution OurBigBook.com
Let Z take values 1,−1 with equal probabilities. Set Xn​=Z for every n, and Yn​=Z for even n, Yn​=−Z for odd n. Both marginal laws are constant, but Xn​+Yn​ alternates between the law of 2Z and a point mass at zero. Thus separate weak convergence of random variables does not determine a joint limit or a sum limit. The obstruction disappears when one marginal limit is constant, as in the Slutsky theorem.

Skorokhod representation theorem

Words: 55
Convergence in distribution OurBigBook.com
For convergence in distribution of random elements in a separable complete metric space, one can construct copies of the sequence and its limit on one probability space which converge almost surely. The copies preserve the individual laws; they need not preserve the original dependence between sequence elements. Suitable more general versions permit a separably supported limit in a metric space.

Method of moments (probability theory)

If all moments of real random variables converge to those of a moment-determinate limit law, then the variables converge to that law in the sense of convergence in distribution. Moment determinacy is essential; the standard normal distribution meets this condition.

Probability measures μn​ on a metric space converge weakly to μ when ∫fdμn​→∫fdμ for every bounded continuous real function f.

Compact containment (limR→∞​supn​P(supt≤T​∣Xtn​∣>R)=0)

Words: 76
Weak convergence of probability measures OurBigBook.com
A family of path laws satisfies compact containment when for every finite horizon and every error tolerance there is a compact set which contains the whole path up to that horizon with at least the complementary probability, uniformly over the family. In Euclidean state space this is the displayed condition. It prevents escape to distant regions where local coefficient approximations give no control. A nonnegative Lyapunov function with uniformly bounded generator growth often proves it by the optional stopping theorem.

Scaling limit of a random curve

Words: 39
Weak convergence of probability measures OurBigBook.com
A scaling limit of random curves is a weak convergence of probability measures after spatial rescaling, usually modulo monotone reparameterization. Tightness specifies the topology and yields subsequential limits; identification of their laws gives a unique continuum model. SLE is a central family of such limiting curve laws.

Skorokhod J1 topology (J1​)

Words: 66
Weak convergence of probability measures OurBigBook.com
On càdlàg paths on a compact time interval, convergence in this topology means that increasing continuous bijections of the time interval can be chosen converging uniformly to the identity, while the corresponding time-changed paths converge uniformly to the limit. At a continuous limiting path this reduces to uniform convergence. It is a standard topology for weak limits of accelerated discrete-time chains, whose paths are step functions.

This is the coarsest topology making all displayed bounded-continuous test integrals continuous. On a separable metric space it is metrized by the bounded-Lipschitz metric. This probability-measure topology should be distinguished from the weak topology of a Banach space.
Bounded-Lipschitz metric (β(μ,ν)=sup∥h∥∞​+Lip(h)≤1​∣μ(h)−ν(h)∣)
Words: 43
Weak topology of probability measures OurBigBook.com
The bounded-Lipschitz metric tests probability measures against a uniformly bounded Lipschitz class. It metrizes weak convergence of probability measures on separable metric spaces. With a complete compatible base metric, it is complete on the probability measures of a Polish space. Replacing the sum norm by the maximum norm changes the metric only by uniform constant factors.

Prokhorov's theorem

On a Polish space, a family of probability measures is relatively compact for weak convergence exactly when it is tight. For finite measures, one additionally requires uniformly bounded total masses.
Closed uniformly tight compactness criterion
Words: 51
Prokhorov's theorem OurBigBook.com
A weakly closed uniformly tight family of Borel probability measures on a Polish space is weakly compact. Embed the underlying space into a compact metric closure, take subsequential pushforward limits, and use the closed-set Portmanteau theorem on the common tightness compact sets. The limit retains full mass in the original space; inverse continuity of the probability pushforward embedding theorem then returns the weak limit.
Compactness of probability measures on a compact metric space
Words: 57
Prokhorov's theorem OurBigBook.com
On a compact metric space, every sequence of Borel probability measures has a subsequence with weak convergence of probability measures to a Borel probability measure. To see this, choose a countable uniformly dense subset of continuous functions, extract a diagonal subsequence of their bounded integrals, and extend the resulting positive normalized functional to all continuous functions. The Riesz representation theorem supplies its Borel probability measure. This is useful for constructing an invariant measure from orbit empirical measures.

Bounded moment criterion for weak convergence

For random variables supported in one fixed compact interval, convergence of every moment is equivalent to weak convergence. One direction tests monomials; the other follows by uniformly approximating continuous functions by polynomials.

Moment determinacy on a compact interval

Words: 34
Bounded moment criterion for weak convergence OurBigBook.com
Two probability measures supported on one compact interval are equal if all their moments are equal. Indeed, equality on monomials gives equality on polynomials, the Weierstrass approximation theorem extends it to every continuous function on the interval, and compactly supported continuous functions determine a finite Borel measure.

Complete convergence of random variables

Words: 69
Almost sure convergence OurBigBook.com
A sequence converges completely to X when ∑n​Pr(∣Xn​−X∣>ϵ)<∞ for every ϵ>0. The first Borel-Cantelli lemma implies almost sure convergence. For independent Xn​, any limit in probability is almost surely constant; the second Borel-Cantelli lemma then gives the converse from almost sure to complete convergence. Independence is essential: Xn​=1{U≤1/n}​ for one uniform random variable U converges almost surely to zero but its exceedance probabilities have a divergent sum.

Weak law without an absolute first moment

Words: 92
Weak law of large numbers OurBigBook.com
Let X have a symmetric probability distribution with P(∣X∣>x)=1/(xlogx) for x≥e, mass 1−1/e at zero, and no mass with 0<∣X∣<e. Then E∣X∣=∞, but independent copies satisfy the weak law of large numbers with limit zero. Indeed nP(∣X∣>n)=1/logn→0. Their symmetric truncations Yn,j​=Xj​1{∣Xj​∣≤n}​ have mean zero and second moment O(n/logn), so Chebyshev's inequality gives n−1∑j≤n​Yn,j​→0 in probability. A union bound shows that the original and truncated sums differ with probability tending to zero. This also gives a differentiable characteristic function at zero whose derivative cannot be interpreted as an absolutely convergent expectation.

Weak law forces a characteristic-function derivative (Sn​/n→a in probability⟹ϕ′(0)=ia)

Words: 86
Weak law of large numbers OurBigBook.com
For independent and identically distributed random variables with characteristic function ϕ, suppose their sample mean converges in probability to a finite constant a. Bounded exponential tests give e−iauϕ(u/n)n→1 uniformly on bounded u intervals: the error is at most Tδ+2P(∣Sn​/n−a∣>δ) on ∣u∣≤T. Taking the continuous complex logarithm vanishing at zero shows nlogϕ(u/n)−iau→0 uniformly; an integer-valued branch difference is continuous and therefore zero. For arbitrary h→0, put n=⌊1/∣h∣⌋ and u=nh, so 1/2≤∣u∣≤1. Dividing the uniform limit by u proves logϕ(h)/h→ia, and thus ϕ′(0)=ia. No absolute first moment is assumed.

Pointwise separable function class
Words: 80
Uniform law of large numbers OurBigBook.com
A measurable-function class is pointwise separable if it has a countable subclass such that every member is the pointwise limit of a sequence from that subclass. With an integrable common envelope, dominated convergence theorem makes both empirical averages and population expectations converge along each such sequence. Thus the supremum of the absolute empirical errors equals the supremum over the countable subclass and is measurable. A finite integrable bracketing cover provides an integrable envelope by taking the maximum absolute value of all its endpoints.
Bracketing of a function class ([ℓ,u],∥u−ℓ∥L1(P)​)
Words: 223 Articles: 3
Uniform law of large numbers OurBigBook.com
A bracket [ℓ,u] contains every measurable function h satisfying ℓ≤h≤u pointwise. For a probability measure P, its L1(P) width is P(u−ℓ), with integrable endpoints. Finite bracketing at every positive width is a sufficient complexity condition for a uniform law of large numbers. Pointwise inequalities, or inequalities outside a single common null set, allow empirical inequalities to hold simultaneously throughout the class.

Uniform strong law from finite L1 bracketing (suph​∣Pn​h−Ph∣a.s.​0)
Words: 127 Articles: 1
Bracketing of a function class OurBigBook.com
Suppose an integrable measurable-function class can be covered by finitely many function brackets of every positive L1(P) width. For an independent sample with common law P, the empirical measure satisfies suph​∣Pn​h−Ph∣→0 on a common probability-one event. For one finite ε-cover, the supremum is bounded by ε plus the largest empirical error among its endpoints. The strong law of large numbers makes that finite maximum vanish. Taking a countable sequence of widths decreasing to zero proves the assertion. A measurable supremum can be obtained from a pointwise separable function class; otherwise the probability-one-event formulation expresses the same pathwise conclusion.

Quantile proof of the Glivenko-Cantelli theorem
Words: 107
Glivenko-Cantelli theorem OurBigBook.com
For a cumulative distribution function G, let Q(u)=inf{x:G(x)≥u}, 0<u<1. Right continuity gives Q(u)≤x exactly when u≤G(x), including at atoms. Thus applying Q to independent uniform variables Ui​ constructs an independent sample with distribution function G. Its empirical distribution function is Gn​(x)=Fn​(G(x)), where Fn​ is the uniform-sample empirical distribution function. Consequently supx​∣Gn​(x)−G(x)∣≤supt∈[0,1]​∣Fn​(t)−t∣. For the uniform sample, monotonicity and a grid of mesh 1/m bound the last supremum by the largest error at grid points plus 1/m. The strong law of large numbers on the countable union of all such grids proves the almost-sure limit. This construction proves the theorem for distributions with atoms as well as continuous distributions.

The Brownian reflection principle then gives upper-tail limits 2(1−Φ(a)) at all a>0, since the limiting maximum has no atom there.

For a random walk with independent identically distributed steps of mean zero and variance one, the Donsker invariance principle and the continuous mapping theorem for integration give this limit. The integral of the polygonal interpolation differs from the right-endpoint sum by Sn​/(2n3/2), whose squared expectation is 1/(4n2). The Slutsky theorem removes that error. The limiting variable is the time-one value of integrated Brownian motion, with law N(0,1/3).
