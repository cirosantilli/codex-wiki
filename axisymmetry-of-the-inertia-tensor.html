Axisymmetry of the inertia tensor (I=diag(I⊥​,I⊥​,I∥​))

 ... Physics Branches of physics Classical mechanics Rigid body dynamics Moment of inertia Inertia tensor
OurBigBook.com Words: 105
If a rigid body's mass density is invariant under every rotation about an axis through its centre of mass, its inertia tensor in any orthonormal basis with third vector on that axis has the displayed form. In particular all transverse directions are principal axes with equal principal moments of inertia.
For a proof, let that axis be z. In the defining integrals for the inertia tensor, a half-turn (x,y,z)↦(−x,−y,z) makes the xz and yz products change sign, so their integrals vanish. A quarter-turn (x,y,z)↦(−y,x,z) makes the xy integral vanish and equates the integrals of x2 and y2. Therefore all off-diagonal components vanish and I11​=I22​. The values are I⊥​=∫ρ[(x2+y2)/2+z2]d3r and I∥​=∫ρ(x2+y2)d3r. No reflection symmetry in z is needed.
