Acoustic wavelength measurement (λ=2d)

 ... Area of mathematics Analysis Partial differential equation Wave equation Wavenumber Wavelength
OurBigBook.com Words: 1k Articles: 13
An acoustic wavelength measurement makes an invisible spatial pattern readable with a ruler. The authored guide Interesting and inexpensive experiments for high school physics by Joe Wolfe places a loudspeaker near a pipe's open end, drives it with an oscillator, and slides a microphone attached to a metre rule through the pipe. An oscilloscope or computer displays the microphone signal. Its oscillation amplitude rises and falls with position; adjacent minima locate pressure nodes of a standing wave a distance d=λ/2 apart. A cap gives a pressure antinode at the closed end; an open end has a pressure node just outside it. These are pressure patterns, distinct from displacement patterns. The guide advises wavelengths longer than the pipe diameter, sufficient pipe length to locate both a node and an antinode, and frequency spectrum analysis to distinguish the intended tone from other sounds.[ref]
The inference follows from superposition of two oppositely directed acoustic plane waves. In an ideal uniform, lossless pipe, taking equal pressure amplitudes P and a rigid cap at x=0 gives
p′(x,t)=Pcos(kx−ωt)+Pcos(kx+ωt)=2Pcos(kx)cos(ωt).
(71)
The time-varying pressure perturbation p′ has local oscillation amplitude 2∣Pcos(kx)∣. Its zeros are separated by π/∣k∣=λ/2. Unequal incident and reflected amplitudes leave nonzero minima, but their spacing remains λ/2 in this ideal model. A microphone detects that pressure variation through a changing electrical signal, rather than watching a wave crest move. Record the tone's frequency too, then infer phase speed vp​=2fd.
For proposed controls, keep the drive frequency and amplitude fixed during a scan, verify the frequency on the recorded signal, and repeat the scan in both directions. Fit several minimum positions xj​=a+jd using linear regression to reduce dependence on one pair of positions. Check the ruler scale and use one consistent reference point on the microphone. Compare with the source switched off to locate background measurement noise; use frequency spectrum analysis to check for additional tones, and reduce drive if the trace ceases to resemble a sinusoid. Record air temperature and avoid strong air motion. These are suggested controls, not additional procedures or observations attributed to the guide.
The pipe must support approximately plane waves over the measured region: transverse structure at shorter wavelengths, the microphone's finite size, and disturbance by the ruler can compromise the interpretation. Weak reflection gives shallow minima, while damping and other reflections can shift the extrema of the simple two-wave model. Measure interior spacings rather than assuming the first minimum is exactly at the pipe lip. A short scan may not include enough minima to estimate d reliably, and drift during sequential scanning can imitate spatial variation.
For an explicitly hypothetical calculation, f=1000Hz and d=0.1715m give λ=0.3430m, K≃18.3183radm−1, and vp​=343.0ms−1. No such measurements are claimed here. If independent small measurement errors in f and fitted d have standard deviations σf​ and σd​, first-order error propagation gives σvp​​/vp​≃(σf​/f)2+(σd​/d)2​. A systematic scale error or changing air conditions requires separate assessment.
