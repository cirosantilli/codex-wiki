Write a=[A], b=[B] and c=[C] for the three concentrations. The letters denote chemical species; the forward arrow represents association of one molecule of each reactant into one complex, and the reverse arrow represents dissociation. The positive constants k+​ and k−​ are the respective reaction rate constants. For an elementary chemical reaction, the law of mass action gives forward and reverse reaction rates
R+​=k+​ab,R−​=k−​c.
(1)
Every forward event removes one molecule of each reactant and adds one complex; a reverse event reverses those changes. Thus the reaction-rate equations are
a˙=−k+​ab+k−​c,b˙=−k+​ab+k−​c,c˙=k+​ab−k−​c.​
(2)
The units of k+​ are inverse concentration per unit time, whereas k−​ has units of inverse time. Adding the equations gives the independent conservation laws
a+c=a(0)+c(0),b+c=b(0)+c(0).​
(3)
In particular a−b is constant, but this is a consequence of those two conservation laws. The total a+b+c need not be constant: association changes the number of separate molecules.
For the general stoichiometry, take p,q to be positive integers. The net reaction rate and stoichiometric vector are
R=k+​apbq−k−​c,ν=(−p,−q,1)T.
(4)
Consequently
a˙=−pR,b˙=−qR,c˙=R.​
(5)
The factors p,q outside R count molecules consumed per event; the powers inside R express the law of mass action. The corresponding conservation laws are a+pc=constant and b+qc=constant. No factorial is required in this deterministic convention for the reaction rate constant.
