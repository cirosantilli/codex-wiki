Assume ε>0. At an equilibrium, w=γv=f(v), so
v[(a−v)(v−1)−γ]=0.
(22)
The nonzero roots are
v±​=21+a±(1−a)2−4γ​​,w±​=γv±​.
(23)
Consequently, with γc​=(1−a)2/4, there are three distinct equilibria for 0<γ<γc​, two distinct equilibria for γ=γc​, and only the origin for γ>γc​. At equality the two nonzero equilibria coincide; for 0<γ<γc​ they both have a<v<1. Geometrically, γc​ is the maximum of the concave quadratic (a−v)(v−1).
Since f′(0)=−a, the Jacobian matrix at the origin is
J0​=(−a/εγ​−1/ε−1​),trJ0​=−εa​−1<0,detJ0​=εa+γ​>0.
(24)
Thus the origin is always asymptotically stable. Its eigenvalues are
λ±​=2ε−(a+ε)±(a−ε)2−4εγ​​.
(25)
Writing γN​=(a−ε)2/(4ε), the origin is a stable node for 0<γ<γN​ and a stable focus for γ>γN​. At γ=γN​>0 it is a degenerate node: the repeated negative eigenvalue has only one independent eigenvector, since the nonzero off-diagonal entry prevents J0​ being a scalar matrix. If a=ε, then γN​=0 and every permitted γ>0 gives a stable focus. This change from node to focus does not change stability and is not a Hopf bifurcation.
