One-dimensional wave equation (utt​−c2uxx​=0)

 Home Mathematics Area of mathematics Analysis Partial differential equation Wave equation
OurBigBook.com Words: 412 Articles: 3
On the whole line, the homogeneous one-dimensional wave equation with constant wave speed c>0 is
utt​−c2uxx​=0,u(x,0)=f(x),ut​(x,0)=g(x).
(8)
Here u is displacement in the string interpretation; f is initial displacement and g is initial velocity. Its two directions of travel give
u(x,t)=F(x−ct)+G(x+ct)=2f(x−ct)+f(x+ct)​+2c1​∫x−ctx+ct​g(s)ds.
(9)
This is D'Alembert's formula, including both Cauchy data. The countertravelling decomposition of the one-dimensional wave equation identifies F(x−ct) as the right-moving travelling wave and G(x+ct) as the left-moving travelling wave. Their sum is permitted by the superposition principle. For f∈C2 and g∈C1, it is a classical solution. More generally, travelling waves with profiles F and G that are locally integrable functions solve the equation as distributions, with derivatives interpreted distributionally.[ref]
The synthetic diagram uses the smooth bump function b defined in D'Alembert's formula, with f(x)=b(x) and c=1. Releasing this displacement from rest, g=0, gives F=G=f/2. Each emerging travelling wave has half the original peak. Launching the identical initial displacement solely to the right instead requires g=−cf′ and gives F=f, G=0. At t=1.5, the released profiles have peaks 1/2 at x=±1.5, while the launched profile has peak 1 at x=1.5. These are calculated model values. A purely left-moving launch requires g=cf′. The initial velocity therefore selects the balance of directions; the initial shape alone does not.
