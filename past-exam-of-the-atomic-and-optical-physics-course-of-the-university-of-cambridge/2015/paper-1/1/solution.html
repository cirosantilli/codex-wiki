Use the ordered basis (∣1⟩,∣2⟩) and σz​=diag(1,−1), with
ρ=21​(I+R⋅σ),Rz​=p1​−p2​,p2​=21−Rz​​.
(1)
A pure quantum superposition ∣+x⟩=(∣1⟩+∣2⟩)/2​ has R=(1,0,0) on the Bloch sphere. In contrast, a mixed state ρ=p∣1⟩⟨1∣+(1−p)∣2⟩⟨2∣ has R=(0,0,2p−1), inside the sphere for 0<p<1. At equal populations its vector is zero. Equal populations alone therefore do not distinguish a coherent superposition from a mixture; their off-diagonal density matrix elements differ.
Choose the drive phase so the rotating-frame Hamiltonian is Hrot​=ℏ(Ωσx​+δσz​)/2, where δ=ω−ω0​ as specified. The Rabi vector is the angular-velocity vector
W=(Ω,0,δ),R˙=W×R.​
(2)
Here Ω is the on-resonance population oscillation angular frequency, not the unhalved coefficient of a cosine coupling. From R(0)=z^, rotation about W at W=Ω2+δ2​ gives
R(t)=(W2Ωδ​[1−cosWt],−WΩ​sinWt,W2δ2+Ω2cosWt​),
(3)
p2​(t)=Ω2+δ2Ω2​sin2(2Ω2+δ2​t​).​
(4)
For Ω≫∣δ∣, the rotation axis is nearly transverse, the trajectory nearly a great circle, and transfer is almost complete. For Ω=δ>0, the axis bisects +x and +z, the orbit lies in Rx​+Rz​=1, and p2​=21​sin2(2​Ωt/2) never exceeds 1/2. Each member remains a pure state in this coherent evolution.
For the Ramsey free interval, let atom j have detuning δj​=δ+ηj​. Instantaneous ideal pulses give the Ramsey ensemble coherence factor χ(T)=⟨eiηj​T⟩. Averaging the free-evolution vector obtained in step (ii) gives
R(T)=(Im[eiδTχ(T)],−Re[eiδTχ(T)],0),p​2​(T)=21​{1+Re[eiδTχ(T)]}.​
(5)
Different atoms fan out around the equator, shortening the ensemble Bloch vector even though every individual's vector remains unit length. A broad smooth detuning distribution usually makes the average spiral towards the centre and damps the Ramsey fringes towards 1/2. The dephasing time is of order 1/Δω0​∼5/δ. inhomogeneous ensemble dephasing is not necessarily irreversible decoherence of individual atoms; an appropriate spin echo can rephase static frequency offsets.
The given spread does not specify a probability distribution or whether it denotes an RMS width. An exact envelope is therefore not determined. The figure illustrates, rather than infers, a Gaussian distribution with RMS σδ​=0.2δ, for which χ=e−σδ2​T2/2. A uniform distribution would instead give a sinc envelope and side lobes. Both have the same qualitative initial loss of contrast on the quoted inverse-spread scale.
In the rubidium-87 ground-state hyperfine structure, J=1/2 and I=3/2, so F=1,2. Taking Hhf​=AI⋅J/ℏ2 gives
EF​=2A​[F(F+1)−I(I+1)−J(J+1)],E1​=−45​A,E2​=43​A.
(6)
The zero-field splitting is Δhf​=2A≃h×6.8GHz. Ignoring the small nuclear magnetic term, the weak-field Landé g-factors are gF=1​≃−1/2 and gF=2​≃+1/2. The diagram labels all three F=1 and five F=2 substates, with linear shifts gF​μB​mF​B.
The pair ∣1⟩=∣F=1,mF​=0⟩ and ∣2⟩=∣F=2,mF​=0⟩ is connected by a magnetic dipole transition with ΔF=1, ΔmF​=0, driven with its oscillating magnetic component along the quantization axis. Both first-order shifts vanish, giving the rubidium-87 clock transition first-order insensitivity near zero field. Its insensitivity does not imply absence of quadratic shifts.
For these two mF​=0 levels the Breit-Rabi formula gives the quadratic Zeeman shift of a clock transition:
ΔE(B)=(2A)2+[(gJ​−gI​)μB​B]2​≃2A+AμB2​B2​,
(7)
where the last expression neglects the nuclear term and sets gJ​≃2. Hence
∂B∂ΔE​≃A2μB2​B​,μB​∣μ2​−μ1​∣​≃A2μB​B​≃8.2×10−3(B=10G).​
(8)
This uses μB​/h≃1.40MHz/G and the supplied A/h≃3.4GHz. With physical moments defined by μi​=−∂Ei​/∂B, the differential magnetic moment is μ2​−μ1​≃−8.2×10−3μB​. If effective magnetic moment denotes the positive energy slope instead, that slope difference is positive. The magnitude and physical frequency sensitivity are the same. Retaining the full atomic factors gives the familiar quadratic clock-frequency coefficient about 575Hz/G2; the simplified supplied parameters give about 576Hz/G2.[ref]
Use a far-detuned optical dipole trap, such as a red-detuned focused-beam trap, to confine both clock states. Their first-order magnetic moments vanish; their residual quadratic magnetic slopes are opposite, so a simple static magnetic-field minimum does not confine both in the same way. An optical potential acts through the electronic polarizability and can trap both ground hyperfine states. It is not perfectly state independent: differential AC Stark shifts, intensity noise and residual magnetic gradients still need control during Ramsey evolution.
