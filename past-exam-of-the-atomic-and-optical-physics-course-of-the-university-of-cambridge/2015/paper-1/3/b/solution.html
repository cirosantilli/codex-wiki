A two-photon transition is a second-order atom-field process. For two-photon absorption, energy conservation requires Ef​−Eg​=ℏ(ω1​+ω2​). A virtual intermediate state in two-photon absorption need not be populated as a real stationary state. In the electric dipole approximation, the two time orderings give an amplitude proportional to
Mfg​=∑n​[Eg​+ℏω1​−En​⟨f∣d⋅ϵ2​∣n⟩⟨n∣d⋅ϵ1​∣g⟩​+Eg​+ℏω2​−En​⟨f∣d⋅ϵ1​∣n⟩⟨n∣d⋅ϵ2​∣g⟩​].
(20)
The electric-field amplitudes multiply this expression. Far from intermediate resonance, the denominators are large relative to linewidths and one-photon couplings, keeping real intermediate occupation small. Near an intermediate resonance, sequential absorption, linewidths and saturation require a fuller treatment. For weak unsaturated absorption the rate scales as I1​I2​, or I2 when both photons come from one field. Absorption followed by stimulated emission instead gives a difference-frequency Raman transition; it is not two-photon absorption at the sum frequency.
Two electric dipole interactions change parity twice, so initial and final states have the same parity. For a one-electron orbital, the usual changes are Δℓ=0,±2, subject to angular-momentum and polarization constraints. For identical equal-frequency photons, exchange symmetry removes the antisymmetric rank-one tensor: ranks zero and two survive. Thus J=0→0 is possible, whereas J=0→1 is forbidden in this degenerate electric-dipole approximation. It would be too strong to prohibit every ΔJ=1 transition between nonzero angular momenta, since a rank-two tensor can connect some such pairs. Spin remains unchanged to leading electric-dipole order.
A central example is hydrogen 1S→2S, forbidden as a one-photon electric dipole transition but accessible with two photons near 243nm. Absorption of one photon from each of equal-frequency counterpropagating beams makes the first-order Doppler shift (k1​+k2​)⋅v vanish and suppresses net recoil. The two same-direction absorption paths can still give a broad Doppler background. A primary demonstration used a frequency-doubled pulsed dye laser near this wavelength and compared with a simultaneously recorded Balmer reference.[ref]
Experimentally, scan the sum frequency and detect the excited state, its subsequent fluorescence or its ionization. Counterpropagating and single-beam spectra distinguish the narrow Doppler-free contribution from its background. Varying intensity tests weak-field scaling and extrapolates AC Stark shifts and power broadening; frequency calibration, beam overlap, residual angle and second-order Doppler shifts still matter. “Doppler free” here cancels the leading velocity term, not every systematic shift.
