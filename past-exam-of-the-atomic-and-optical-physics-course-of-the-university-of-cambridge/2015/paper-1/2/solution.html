Interpret the box as a hard-wall cube. Its one-particle levels are ϵnx​ny​nz​​=π2ℏ2(nx2​+ny2​+nz2​)/(2mL2) for positive integers, giving E0​=3π2ℏ2/(2mL2). Counting lattice points in a positive octant and differentiating the large-radius count gives the supplied continuum density of states. Expressing its prefactor through E0​ yields
g(ϵ)=AE0−3/2​ϵ​,A=433​π​.​
(12)
Here A denotes the dimensionless constant requested in this question, to distinguish it from the unrelated hyperfine constant of question 1. Periodic boundary conditions would have zero ground energy and would not permit this parametrization by a nonzero E0​.
Separate the ground-state occupation from excited particles. The finite-box ground-energy convention in Bose condensation makes the chemical potential approach the ground energy from below as that occupation becomes macroscopic. In the thermodynamic continuum approximation one shifts the energy zero to the ground state and sets the limiting chemical potential to zero in the excited-state integral. The difference between the exact shifted density and its ϵ​ bulk form gives finite-size corrections; at the transition kB​Tc​/E0​∼N2/3≫1. Thus
Nmax′​(T)=∫0∞​eϵ/(kB​T)−1AE0−3/2​ϵ​​dϵ=AΓ(3/2)ζ(3/2)(E0​kB​T​)3/2.
(13)
The identity follows by expanding (ex−1)−1=∑r≥1​e−rx and integrating each term. The excited states have a finite saturation capacity. Setting that capacity equal to N determines condensation:
kB​Tc​=BE0​N2/3,B=[AΓ(3/2)ζ(3/2)]−2/3=3πζ(3/2)2/34​≃0.22375.​
(14)
Equivalently kB​Tc​=(2πℏ2/m)[n/ζ(3/2)]2/3, with n=N/L3.
At fixed N, below Tc​ the chemical potential is pinned to its limiting value and
N′=N(T/Tc​)3/2,N0​=N[1−(T/Tc​)3/2].​
(15)
Above Tc​, N′≃N and there is no macroscopic N0​; the chemical potential adjusts rather than allowing the saturation expression to exceed the available particles. At fixed T, put Nc​=Nmax′​(T). For N<Nc​, N′≃N and N0​ is not macroscopic; for N>Nc​, N′=Nc​ saturates while N0​=N−Nc​ grows linearly. This is particle saturation in a homogeneous Bose gas; the sharp threshold is a thermodynamic-limit approximation to finite-box occupations.
The interaction scale compares differently with confinement and transition energies:
E0​EI​​∼LNa​≫1,kB​Tc​EI​​∼N1/3La​≪1.
(16)
Thus the stated range makes interactions dominate the ground-state confinement energy while remaining small on the thermal scale controlling Tc​. This is an interaction-dominated ground state with nearly ideal condensation temperature. It also gives the dilute-gas parameter na3=N(a/L)3≪1. A contact model has g=4πℏ2a/m and μ≃gn; the question's EI​ suppresses order-one factors, and the uniform ground energy per particle is gn/2, rather than exactly gn. Weak interactions can shift Tc​ slightly, but their dimensionless strength an1/3≪1 makes that a small relative effect. In a hard-wall box, boundary healing layers do not overturn bulk interaction dominance when their thickness is small compared with L.
At kB​T≪μ, thermally occupied Bogoliubov modes have Ek​≃ℏcs​k, with cs​=μ/m​. There is one acoustic branch for this spinless gas. Its thermal energy determines the phonon heat capacity of a dilute Bose gas:
Uph​=2π2V​∫0∞​eℏcs​k/(kB​T)−1ℏcs​k3​dk=30π2V​ℏ3cs3​(kB​T)4​,
(17)
CV,ph​=152π2​VkB​(ℏcs​kB​T​)3∝T3.​
(18)
The zero-temperature interaction energy does not itself contribute to this leading temperature derivative at fixed N,V. This bulk power law also requires kB​T≫ℏcs​/L so that the phonon spectrum may be treated as continuous; a strictly finite box eventually has discrete-level activated behaviour.
For the noninteracting condensed Bose gas, the ideal Bose-gas heat capacity below condensation follows by integrating ϵg(ϵ)f(ϵ):
U=23​NkB​Tζ(3/2)ζ(5/2)​(T/Tc​)3/2,CV,ideal​=415​NkB​ζ(3/2)ζ(5/2)​(T/Tc​)3/2∝T3/2.​
(19)
Its limiting value at Tc−​ is about 1.926NkB​. At very low temperature the interacting phonon curve is suppressed relative to the ideal curve, has slope three on logarithmic axes, and crosses over towards particle-like T3/2 behaviour around kB​T∼μ. Since μ≪kB​Tc​, most of the interval below Tc​ is approximately ideal at leading weak-coupling order. The figure marks a qualitative extrapolation near the transition: a zero-temperature Bogoliubov spectrum does not determine precise interacting critical heat capacity or its small transition shift. The illustrative fixed-μ interpolation is not claimed as a controlled critical-region calculation.
