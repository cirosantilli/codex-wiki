Fix the qubit polarization convention by ρ=(I+P⋅σ)/2, where the Pauli matrices are ordered in the basis (∣1⟩,∣2⟩) and σz​=∣1⟩⟨1∣−∣2⟩⟨2∣. Thus the ground state is the north pole of the Bloch sphere. In a rotating frame and within the rotating-wave approximation, choose the drive phase and detuning sign so that
HR​=2ℏ​(Ωσx​+Δσz​),P˙=(Ω,0,Δ)×P.
(9)
For example, with this ground-state convention one may take Δ=ωL​−ω0​ and choose the transverse coupling positive. The Bloch vector precesses around the effective field (Ω,0,Δ) at angular frequency ΩR​=Ω2+Δ2​, tracing a circle at fixed projection on that axis. Its length is conserved under coherent unitary time evolution. On resonance the axis is transverse and the Rabi oscillation can fully invert an initially ground-state atom. Away from resonance the axis tilts, and
P1→2​(t)=Ω2+Δ2Ω2​sin2(2ΩR​t​).
(10)
There is no relaxation towards an equilibrium Bloch vector in this ideal model. An inhomogeneous ensemble can nevertheless lose its averaged qubit polarization through dephasing even without spontaneous emission; the circle description assumes a common drive and detuning.
For ∣a∣2+∣b∣2=1, the stated quantum state gives
ρ=∣Ψ⟩⟨Ψ∣=(∣a∣2a∗b​ab∗∣b∣2​),P=(2Re(a∗b),2Im(a∗b),∣a∣2−∣b∣2).
(11)
The diagonal elements are the two state populations. The off-diagonal elements are quantum coherences: they retain the relative quantum phase and determine interference under a subsequent pulse. This is a pure state, even if both populations are nonzero: ρ2=ρ, Trρ2=1, and ∣P∣=1. A mixed state averages projectors over preparation probabilities and generally has Trρ2<1. Equal populations alone do not distinguish an equal coherent superposition from an unpolarized mixture.
For use in all three cases, multiplication of the gates gives the Hadamard-phase-Hadamard qubit rotation
U=HPα​H=21​(1+eiα1−eiα​1−eiα1+eiα​)=eiα/2exp(−iασx​/2).
(12)
The global phase cancels in ρ′=UρU†. Thus the Bloch vector map is
(Px​,Py​,Pz​)⟼(Px​,Py​cosα−Pz​sinα,Py​sinα+Pz​cosα).​
(13)
The final unlabelled Ramsey spectroscopy comparison applies to the three cases as follows. In an atomic clock, an initial coherent pulse prepares a superposition, a free interval T accumulates a relative quantum phase proportional to detuning times T, and a final coherent pulse converts that phase to a population. Here α plays the role of that accumulated phase, with sign and fringe offset set by pulse conventions. In (a), the first Hadamard gate creates the needed superposition and the final gate reveals full-contrast Ramsey interferometry. In (b), the first Hadamard gate instead puts the state in ∣1⟩, so the intervening phase gate has no relative quantum phase to imprint: the output is independent of α. In (c), the unpolarized density matrix remains unchanged under every gate, so the ensemble has no Ramsey interferometry contrast. A coherent unitary gate alone cannot initialize a maximally mixed ensemble into a pure clock state.
The Ramsey interferometry analogy concerns preparation, phase accumulation, and readout; a Hadamard gate is not literally a transverse π/2 rotation on every input. In this basis H=Ry​(π/2)σz​, and its Bloch sphere action is a π rotation about (x^+z^)/2​, up to a global phase. The phase conventions of physical Ramsey spectroscopy pulses must be specified to compare their outputs for arbitrary initial states.
