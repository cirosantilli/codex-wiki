The two-level Doppler limit, kB​TD​=ℏΓ/2, balances velocity-dependent scattering friction with momentum diffusion. Multilevel atoms have additional, slower internal dynamics. Counterpropagating beams with orthogonal linear polarizations create a spatially varying polarization, giving different position-dependent light shifts for ground-state magnetic sublevels. A moving atom climbs a light-shift hill, loses kinetic energy and is optically pumped into a lower-energy sublevel: this repeated Sisyphus cooling cycle dissipates motion through emitted light.
Optical pumping responds with a finite delay. Its rate, rather than only the excited-state linewidth, sets the low-velocity response scale. In the opposite-circular-polarization arrangement, motion instead produces unequal sublevel populations and unbalanced radiation pressure. Both mechanisms can cool below the two-level Doppler temperature; diffusion heating still balances the cooling, and recoil matters at the lowest temperatures.[ref]
Ground-state substructure, optical pumping and polarization gradients enable the extra cooling. Magnetic fields that disturb the required sublevel dynamics can suppress it. Optical molasses damps velocity but does not by itself provide position-restoring confinement.
