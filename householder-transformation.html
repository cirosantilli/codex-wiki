Householder transformation (H=I−2uuT)

 Home Mathematics Area of mathematics Algebra Linear algebra
OurBigBook.com Words: 624 Articles: 2
A Householder transformation is a reflection that leaves every direction in a chosen hyperplane fixed and reverses its normal direction. For a real unit vector u, its matrix is
H=I−2uuT.​
(59)
To understand the formula, decompose any vector into its orthogonal projection onto the normal and a perpendicular component:
x=x∥​+x⊥​,x∥​=(uTx)u,uTx⊥​=0.
(60)
Then Hx=x⊥​−x∥​. Subtracting twice the normal component changes its sign while retaining everything parallel to the mirror. A nonzero normal v gives the equivalent formula H=I−2vvT/(vTv), so normalization can be deferred.
For example, take u=(2,1)T/5​. Its mirror is the straight line 2x1​+x2​=0, and
H=(−3/5−4/5​−4/53/5​),H(34​)=(−50​).
(61)
The midpoint of these two vectors is (−1,2)T, which lies on the mirror; their connecting segment is parallel to its normal. Both vectors have Euclidean norm five.
Several useful properties follow from this decomposition. The Householder reflection is a symmetric matrix and an orthogonal matrix:
HT=H,H2=I,HTH=I.
(62)
Reflecting twice restores the original vector; Euclidean norms and inner products are preserved. The eigenvalue is −1 along u and +1 on u⊥, so detH=−1. A reflection reverses orientation even though it preserves lengths.
The computational payoff is that the mirror can be chosen to send a whole vector onto one coordinate axis, eliminating several entries at once. Householder QR decomposition repeats this operation on the remaining parts of successive columns. For a real symmetric matrix, applying the same Householder reflection on both sides gives a matrix similarity HAH that preserves symmetry and eigenvalues; this is the basis of Householder tridiagonalization.
Alston S. Householder's 1958 paper Unitary Triangularization of a Nonsymmetric Matrix develops this elimination method. Its motivation includes reducing the arithmetic needed for triangularization while retaining the conditioning advantages of orthogonal transformations. [ref] Over the complex numbers, I−2uu∗ for a unit u is Hermitian and unitary, with the star denoting the conjugate transpose; the coordinate-axis construction below is stated for real vectors.
