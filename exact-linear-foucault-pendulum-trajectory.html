A bob released from rest therefore does not describe a perfectly straight line even in this ideal linear model. Replacing the bracket by cosω0​t gives the familiar leading approximation but introduces a spurious initial transverse velocity −iαA. The exact expression keeps the initial condition and separates that tiny orbit width from the slow rotation of its long axis.
For the illustrative ℓ=20m and g=9.81m/s2, the fast period 2π/β is approximately 8.9714s. At 45∘ north, ∣α∣/β≃7.3624×10−5: the transverse semiaxis is too small to distinguish at the figure's scale. Nevertheless the long-axis orientation changes by about −63.8138∘ in six hours. Changing the sign of geographical latitude conjugates the entire solution, reflecting the trajectory across the eastward axis; setting α=0 gives z=Acosω0​t exactly. These are stronger checks than merely drawing a rotating line.
