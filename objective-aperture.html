Objective aperture

 ... Optics Electron optics Electron microscope Electron microscopy Transmission electron microscopy Transmission electron microscopy contrast
OurBigBook.com Words: 174 Articles: 2
An objective aperture selects outgoing electron directions in the objective lens's back focal plane. Passing the central transmitted beam gives bright-field transmission electron microscopy; selecting a separated diffracted beam gives dark-field transmission electron microscopy.[ref]
In a paraxial approximation, an outgoing angle θ maps approximately to position r=fθ in this focal plane, where f is the objective focal length. A centred opening of radius a therefore admits ∣θ∣≲a/f. Selecting a direction selects a component of the outgoing wavefunction; the subsequent image still assigns intensity to specimen positions. The opening is not a hole cut into a real-space specimen image. Its angular selection must be distinguished from selecting a specimen region for electron diffraction.
