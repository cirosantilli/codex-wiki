Deep inelastic scattering

 Home Physics Branches of physics Quantum field theory Standard Model Quantum chromodynamics
OurBigBook.com Words: 1k Articles: 21
Scattering that resolves the constituents of a hadron at large spacelike momentum transfer, with an inclusive sum over final hadron states.
In electron scattering from a proton, write the reaction as e−p→e−X: the outgoing electron is measured, while X denotes all allowed final hadron states. Large momentum transfer supplies a short-distance probe; a large final invariant mass separates the deep continuum from elastic scattering and individual particle resonances. The useful surprise is that an extended proton can have a response characteristic of much smaller constituents. Deep inelastic scattering turns measurements outside the proton into constraints on its internal parton distribution functions.
The SLAC-MIT deep inelastic electron scattering experiment connects this definition to an actual measurement. Its evidence came from distributions of outgoing electron energies and scattering angles, rather than a photograph of a quark. Bjorken scaling concerns deep-inelastic structure functions after known scattering factors have been accounted for; it does not say that the raw differential scattering cross-section is constant.
