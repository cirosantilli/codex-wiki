Even reflection across a homogeneous Neumann boundary (u(x,y)=u(∣x∣,y))

 ... Area of mathematics Analysis Differential equation Ordinary differential equation Boundary condition Neumann boundary condition
OurBigBook.com Words: 131
If a harmonic function on one side of a straight boundary has zero normal derivative there, even reflection extends it harmonically across the boundary. Locally take the boundary to be x=0 and assume u and its first derivatives extend continuously to its open segment. Define u(x,y)=u(∣x∣,y). The zero value of ux​ makes the first derivatives match across the join. Applying Green's first identity on the two sides against a smooth compactly supported test function leaves no interface term, so the reflected function is weakly harmonic and hence harmonic by Weyl lemma.
Reflecting across both edges of a right-angle corner gives a harmonic function on a punctured neighbourhood when both normal derivatives vanish. If the original function is bounded at the corner, the removable singularity for a bounded harmonic function makes the extension harmonic there too. Thus a maximum on such an edge or corner can be tested using the interior strong maximum principle for harmonic functions.
