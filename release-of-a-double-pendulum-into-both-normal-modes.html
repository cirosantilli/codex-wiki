Release of a double pendulum into both normal modes

Release the upper rod at θ1​(0)=α=0.06 radians while keeping the lower rod vertical, θ2​(0)=0, with both angular velocities zero. The initial condition is the sum θ(0)=(α/2)a−​+(α/2)a+​. The solution from linearization is therefore
θ1​(t)θ2​(t)​=2α​[cos(ω−​t)+cos(ω+​t)],=2​α​[cos(ω−​t)−cos(ω+​t)].​
(39)
At release the lower angle vanishes by cancellation between the two normal modes. Their different angular frequencies then destroy that cancellation. Differentiating twice gives θ¨1​(0)=−2gα/ℓ and θ¨2​(0)=2gα/ℓ, agreeing with the small-angle limit of the exact double pendulum equations of motion.
The quadratic mechanical energy at release is E2​=mgℓα2. Each normal mode contributes half, because (4+22​)(2−2​)=(4−22​)(2+2​)=4. Equal coordinate amplitudes need not mean equal energies in general; here the modal inertias and squared angular frequencies happen to compensate. Releasing instead along θ(0)=αa−​ or αa+​, with zero angular velocities, excites just one normal mode.
