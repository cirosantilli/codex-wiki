Fine-grained Gibbs entropy (Sfg​=−kB​∫ρlog(ρ/ρref​)dΓ)

 Home Physics Branches of physics Statistical physics Gibbs entropy
OurBigBook.com Words: 87
For a normalized probability density ρ on classical phase space with canonical volume element dΓ, the fine-grained Gibbs entropy is −kB​∫ρlog(ρ/ρref​)dΓ, whenever the integral exists. Here ρref​>0 is a constant reference density fixing the units inside the logarithm, and kB​ is the Boltzmann constant. No averaging into finite cells is performed.
For a regular Hamiltonian flow Φt​, Liouville theorem in Hamiltonian mechanics gives dΓ(Φt​z)=dΓ(z) and ρt​(Φt​z)=ρ0​(z). Substitution directly proves
Sfg​[ρt​]=−kB​∫ρ0​(z)log(ρ0​(z)/ρref​)dΓ(z)=Sfg​[ρ0​].
(82)
An irreversible macroscopic entropy increase therefore requires a different description or additional assumptions; it is not a failure of this volume-preservation argument.
