Kapitza thermal boundary experiment (1941)

 ... Statistical physics Thermodynamics Temperature Cryogenics Thermal boundary resistance Kapitza resistance
OurBigBook.com Words: 236
Pyotr Leonidovich Kapitza's 1941 study suspended cleaned, annealed copper blocks in liquid helium. Sealed, gas-filled cavities held a heater and a bronze resistance thermometer. Electrical readout tracked the heating-induced temperature rise; the earlier readout method assumed a stable bath without a separate bath thermometer. A nearby unheated block warmed much less, localizing the drop near the heated surface. Gold plating and coatings tested surface effects; tight cavities prevented liquid ingress. These were solid-body readings, not probes resolving the microscopic interfacial layer. [ref]
The measurement logic suggests a modern control sequence: calibrate both temperature readouts, establish their zero-load difference, increase heater power, wait for a steady state, and repeat while monitoring the bath. Determine lead losses and the bulk temperature gradients before using rB​=(Ts​−Tl​)/q. These are proposed controls derived from the energy balance, rather than additional reported steps in the 1941 apparatus. A nearby unheated solid also has its own interface: its reading need not equal the liquid's local temperature exactly. The experiment supports a drop concentrated near the surface, while a microscopic thickness and a universal interfacial law require further evidence.
