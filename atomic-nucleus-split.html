Atomic nucleus (A=Z+N)

 Home Physics Nuclear physics
OurBigBook.com Words: 7k Articles: 77
The central system of protons and neutrons in an atom. Its mass number A is its baryon number in ordinary nuclear states; Z is the proton number and N the neutron number.
The nucleus contains almost all of an atom's mass and carries positive electric charge Ze, where e is the elementary charge. The atomic number Z identifies the chemical element; changing N at fixed Z gives another isotope. The mass number A=Z+N counts nucleons, rather than specifying the exact nuclear mass: nuclear binding energy lowers the mass of a bound system relative to its separated constituents. The simplest nucleus is a single proton; other atomic nuclei can contain many protons and neutrons bound by the nuclear force.
A nuclear quantum state has properties such as spin and a spatial nuclear charge distribution. These are different questions from merely counting nucleons. A small-angle elastic scattering measurement can reveal a nuclear charge radius, while a wider range of momentum transfer tests the distribution's shape. The Hofstadter nuclear electron scattering experiment made this connection between scattering patterns and nuclear structure experimentally concrete.
