Energy profile (chemistry)

 Home Chemistry Chemical reaction Chemical kinetics Reaction mechanism Reaction coordinate
OurBigBook.com Words: 333 Articles: 3
An energy profile plots an explicitly specified energy against a reaction coordinate. Identify whether the vertical axis is potential energy, enthalpy, or Gibbs free energy; these descriptions are related but not interchangeable. A potential-energy profile describes a path through configurations, while a free-energy description also accounts for the relevant thermodynamic conditions.
For the two chosen profiles, the reaction energy barriers from the reactants are 80 and 50kJmol−1. From the reaction products, the same maxima are 100 and 70kJmol−1 higher. Both paths have endpoint difference −20kJmol−1. The barrier is measured from the relevant starting endpoint, not from an arbitrary zero of potential energy.
The appeal of the diagram is that it separates two useful questions: which endpoint is lower, and which path is easier to traverse? Catalysis changes the route through transition states while retaining the endpoint thermodynamics of the same overall chemical reaction. A real catalytic route may have several steps and reaction intermediates; the two simple curves do not specify such a reaction mechanism. The plotted potential-energy difference alone is not an equilibrium constant or a calculated rate constant. See Chemistry 2e, 12.7: alternative reaction routes and catalysis.
