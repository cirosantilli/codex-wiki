Box plot

 Home Mathematics Area of mathematics Probability and statistics Descriptive statistics
OurBigBook.com Words: 478
A box plot compresses a statistical sample into a box spanning its lower and upper quartiles Q1​,Q3​, a line at its median Q2​, and two whiskers. It makes location and central spread easy to compare across groups on a common value axis. The box represents the central half in terms of quantile levels; with ties or interpolated quartiles, it need not contain exactly half the observations. Whisker endpoints require a stated convention: they can be the observed extremes, or they can stop short of separately marked observations. [ref]
Here is one explicitly toy dataset, reused under histogram: twelve invented, unitless values, with repeated values counted separately, already arranged as order statistics:
(x(1)​,…,x(12)​)=(1,2,2,3,4,4,5,6,6,7,8,20).
(1)
For this box plot we choose the interpolated quartiles defined under quartile, using R's type-7 rule. This gives
Q1​=2+43​(3−2)=2.75,Q2​=4+21​(5−4)=4.5,Q3​=6+41​(7−6)=6.25.
(2)
The interquartile range is I=Q3​−Q1​=3.5. Our whisker convention uses the fences
L=Q1​−1.5I=−2.5,U=Q3​+1.5I=11.5.
(3)
The whiskers end at the smallest and largest observations in the closed interval [L,U]: here 1 and 8. Values strictly outside the fences receive separate outlier markers: here only 20. An observation exactly on a fence is retained within the whisker under this convention. The fences are thresholds, not observed whisker endpoints; the upper whisker is at 8, not 11.5. R's documented whisker rule likewise includes observations no more than 1.5 box lengths away, although its box uses hinges, which can differ from our type-7 quartiles. [ref]
A separate outlier marker is a prompt to investigate, not a finding that the value is bad data and not an instruction to delete it. A distant observation can be a recording error, a legitimate unusual outcome, or evidence that the assumed statistical model misses some behavior. Check its provenance and context before deciding how to analyze it. No measurement-error claim is possible for our invented 20: we deliberately put it there to demonstrate the rule. [ref]
The beauty of a box plot is its compact comparison of ordered positions rather than every observation. That compression also loses information: many different statistical samples have the same quartiles and whiskers, so the box alone cannot reveal every cluster or gap that a histogram might show. For example, replacing the two invented 6 values by 5.5 and 6 leaves the type-7 quartiles, whiskers and separate marker unchanged. Displaying the individual values alongside the box plot is especially helpful for such a small statistical sample. Always state both the quartile rule and the whisker rule; changing either can change which values are marked.
