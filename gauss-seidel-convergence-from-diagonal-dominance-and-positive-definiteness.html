Gauss-Seidel convergence from diagonal dominance and positive definiteness (ρ(H)<1)

A strictly diagonally dominant matrix or a real symmetric positive-definite matrix guarantees convergence of the Gauss-Seidel method from every initial vector. These are sufficient conditions.[ref]

Induct through a sweep. Old coordinates have absolute error at most E; previously updated coordinates have absolute error at most qE≤E. The component update and the triangle inequality therefore bound the new error by qE. This proves ∥e(k+1)∥∞​≤q∥e(k)∥∞​, a maximum norm contraction.
For a symmetric positive-definite matrix, write A=D+L+LT. The Householder-John theorem applies to M=D+L and N=−LT, because MT+N=D is a positive-definite matrix. Hence the iteration matrix has spectral radius less than one, which gives the convergence criterion for a stationary iteration.
There is also a geometric reason fresh updates help: the Gauss-Seidel method is exact coordinate descent on the convex quadratic function

Holding the other coordinates fixed, minimizing in coordinate i gives the corresponding row update. A change δi​ lowers F by exactly aii​δi2​/2. Each fresh coordinate is therefore the minimizer for the current vector, and a complete sweep lowers the quadratic form error unless every coordinate already solves its row. This explains the method's beauty without implying monotone decrease in every vector norm.
