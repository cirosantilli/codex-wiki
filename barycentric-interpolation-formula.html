The barycentric interpolation formula evaluates the same Lagrange polynomial using weights that depend only on the distinct interpolation nodes:

(8)
At x=xi​, return yi​ directly, before dividing; the formula has removable singularities at the interpolation nodes. Multiplying every wi​ by the same nonzero constant leaves the answer unchanged. Driscoll and Braun derive this formula as a more efficient, numerically stable way to evaluate polynomial interpolation.[ref]
For the interpolation nodes 0,1,2, the weights are (1/2,−1,1/2), or equivalently (1,−2,1). With data (1,2,5), at x=1/2 the denominator using the latter weights is 2+4−2/3=16/3 and the numerator is 2+8−10/3=20/3, giving p2​(1/2)=5/4 again. For eleven interpolation nodes in the Runge phenomenon figure, the generator computes the weights by the direct products above and returns stored values exactly at every interpolation node.
The direct products require O(n2) arithmetic operations once; each subsequent value uses O(n) arithmetic operations. Here O denotes Big O notation. At large polynomial degree or on poorly scaled intervals, forming these products can exceed the range of floating-point arithmetic; normalization or specialized weight formulas may be needed. Stable evaluation also cannot cure sensitivity intrinsic to the chosen interpolation nodes, measured by the Lebesgue constant of interpolation, or the approximation failure in the Runge phenomenon.
