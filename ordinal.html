Finite ordinal (n<ω)

Words: 70
Ordinal OurBigBook.com
A finite ordinal is the order type of a finite well-order; in the von Neumann representation it is one of 0,1,2,…. They are exactly the ordinals x such that x and each member of x are either zero or successor ordinals. If an ordinal is infinite, it either is the nonzero limit ω or contains ω as a member, so it fails this test. Thus the finite ordinals give the usual representation of the natural numbers.

Bounded successor characterization of finite ordinals (x∈ω⟺Ord(x)∧Succ0​(x)∧∀y∈x Succ0​(y))

Words: 82
Ordinal OurBigBook.com
A Von Neumann ordinal x is finite exactly when x and every member of x are either zero or successor ordinals. Every finite ordinal has that property. Conversely, an infinite ordinal is either ω, which is a nonzero limit, or is greater than ω and contains that nonzero limit ordinal. The test can be expressed by a bounded first-order formula: ordinality uses transitivity and membership comparability, and a nonempty ordinal is a successor exactly when it has a greatest member. This defines the class of natural numbers without quantifying over arbitrary infinite inductive sets.

Club set

Words: 1k Articles: 33
Ordinal OurBigBook.com
For a limit ordinal κ, a subset C⊆κ is club if it is unbounded and contains each limit below κ of an increasing sequence from C. At an uncountable regular cardinal κ, intersections of fewer than κ club sets are club: above a starting point cycle through the sets and take suprema, using regularity to stay below κ; closure puts the resulting limit in all of them. Club sets provide the reflecting ranks in worldly cardinals below an inaccessible cardinal.

Club of closure points for countable set-valued functions (D={δ:(∀α<δ) B(α)⊆δ})

Words: 48
Club set OurBigBook.com
For a regular uncountable cardinal λ and B:λ→[λ]ω, the limit ordinals closed under all earlier values of B form a club set. Countably iterate bounds for ⋃α<γ​B(α) to obtain unboundedly many closure points. Regularity keeps each bound and the countable supremum below λ. Limits of closure points remain closure points.

Square principle (□κ​)

Words: 72
Club sequence OurBigBook.com
For an infinite cardinal κ, the square principle supplies club sets Cζ​⊆ζ at limit ζ<κ+, with order type at most κ, coherent at limit points: Cγ​=Cζ​∩γ whenever γ is a limit point of Cζ​. The bound precludes a single club set threading the entire sequence. For regular uncountable κ, coherence together with the weaker-looking size clause cf(ζ)<κ⇒∣Cζ​∣<κ implies the order-type bound. At κ=ω, that clause alone is vacuous and cannot replace the bound.

Stationary set (S∩C=∅ for every club C)

Words: 934 Articles: 20
Club set OurBigBook.com
A subset of a regular uncountable cardinal number κ is stationary if it meets every club set in κ. Intersecting a stationary set with a club set preserves stationarity. Every member of the club filter is stationary. At a regular infinite θ<κ, the stationarity of ordinals of prescribed cofinality supplies important examples.

Stationary partition at a successor cardinal (S=⨆ξ<κ​Sξ​)

Words: 72
Stationary set OurBigBook.com
Every stationary subset of a successor cardinal λ+ can be partitioned into λ+ disjoint stationary subsets. For each β∈[λ,λ+) choose a bijection eβ​:λ→β. For each ξ<λ+, the function β↦eβ−1​(ξ) is regressive on the stationary tail with β>ξ. The Fodor lemma gives a stationary fiber with one value iξ​<λ. Some value occurs for λ+ many indices, whose fibers are pairwise disjoint. Assign any leftover part of S to one fiber.

Stationary set OurBigBook.com
For any nonzero limit ordinal δ, a club set is an unbounded subset closed under limit points below δ. A subset is stationary if it meets every such club set. Statements concerning the completeness of the club filter or Fodor lemma require regular uncountable height; they should not be transferred to arbitrary singular or countable limit ordinals without checking their hypotheses.

Stationary partition by cofinal-sequence fibers (κ=⨆ξ<κ​Sξ​)

Words: 70
Stationary set OurBigBook.com
For an uncountable regular cardinal κ, choose cofinal sequences cα​:ω→α for the stationary set of ordinals of cofinality ω. Above any bound, some fixed coordinate exceeds the bound on a stationary subset; Fodor lemma makes that coordinate constant on a stationary subset. Thus the stationary constant fibers, over all coordinates, have unboundedly many values. Regularity makes one coordinate have κ such values. Its fibers are disjoint stationary sets; adding all leftover ordinals to one piece partitions κ.

Disjoint stationary subsets of omega-one ((Sξ​:ξ<ω1​))
Words: 59
Ulam matrix on omega-one OurBigBook.com
There are ℵ1​ pairwise disjoint stationary subsets of the nonzero countable limit ordinals. In a Ulam matrix on omega-one, each row partitions a stationary tail into countably many cells, so some cell is stationary. One column contains stationary cells in uncountably many rows, and the column cells are disjoint. Unions indexed by different subsets of ω1​ then differ on a stationary set.

CCC forcing cannot create diamond (M[G]⊨◊ ⟹ M⊨◊)
Words: 73
Diamond principle OurBigBook.com
For each index, collect the ground subsets which some condition forces to be the corresponding diamond guess. Distinct forced values have incompatible witnessing conditions, so the countable chain condition for forcing makes this a countable family. Every ground subset is guessed by these families on a ground stationary set, by testing each ground club set. The countable-family diamond equivalence then yields diamond in the ground model. A generic guess need not itself be a ground subset; only values forced equal to one are collected.

Countable-family diamond equivalence (◊S′​⟺◊S​)
Words: 75
Countable-family diamond principle OurBigBook.com
Enumerate each countable guessing family and use a bijection π:λ×ω→λ with a club set of prefix-closure points. The nth candidate sequence decodes the nth component of the nth family entry. If all candidates fail, choose counterexample sets Xn​ and club sets witnessing failure, then code the Xn​ together using π. A correct family guess on the common closure club set and all failure club sets decodes to one of the forbidden correct guesses. The singleton-family implication supplies the reverse direction.

Club filter completeness (Dκ​ is κ-complete)

The club filter on a regular uncountable cardinal number κ is κ-complete. Intersections of fewer than κ club sets are closed. For unboundedness, repeatedly step past a point of each club and take a countable supremum; regularity keeps all suprema below κ, and closure puts the final supremum in every club.

Generalized Cantor normal form (α=∑i<m​ρβi​ξi​)

Words: 70
Cantor normal form OurBigBook.com
For every ordinal base ρ>1, a nonzero ordinal has a unique finite ordinal addition expansion with strictly decreasing exponents βi​ and coefficients 0<ξi​<ρ. Choose the largest power at most the remaining ordinal and apply the ordinal division algorithm. The next remainder has smaller leading exponent, so well-foundedness forces termination. Each lower tail is smaller than its preceding power; uniqueness of division consequently fixes the exponent, coefficient and tail successively. For ρ=ω this is Cantor normal form.

Commensurable ordinals

Words: 65
Cantor normal form OurBigBook.com
Two ordinals are commensurable when neither is absorbed by adding the other on its left. For positive ordinals, this is equivalent to equality of their leading Cantor normal form exponent. If leading exponents differ, the lower-leading ordinal is absorbed by the higher power of ω. If they agree, either sum adds the two positive leading coefficients and therefore exceeds both original ordinals. Zero is commensurable with no ordinal.

Computable Cantor normal form notation

Words: 124 Articles: 1
Cantor normal form OurBigBook.com
Finite canonical terms built from zero, ordinal sums and powers of omega describe exactly the ordinals below epsilon zero. Syntactic validity and comparison are decidable by recursion through the exponent subterms. Enumerating valid term codes transfers their order to a computable well-order on all natural numbers.

Slow well-ordering below epsilon zero (∀k ∃N ∀(αi​)i<N​ (∥αi​∥≤k+i⇒∃i<j αi​≤αj​))

Words: 84
Computable Cantor normal form notation OurBigBook.com
Use the unary Cantor normal form tree norm, counting one root and recursively counting all repeated exponent subterms. There are only finitely many notations of bounded norm. This finite principle bounds all descending sequences whose norms grow at most linearly with the position. Friedman's finite form of Kruskal's theorem implies it through the natural-sum ordinal rank of a finite rooted tree. The Friedman–Smith miniaturization theorem turns this controlled termination principle for standard epsilon-zero notations into the formal consistency statement for Peano arithmetic; the technical ordinal-analysis result is needed in addition to the tree coding. Section 2.2 of formal.hknu.ac.kr/Publi/kruskalJKMS.pdf records the general miniaturization and soundness theorem.

α=ωβn+γ
(8)
for a positive finite integer n and γ<ωβ.

Leading exponent of an ordinal product

Leading term of an ordinal OurBigBook.com
Let nonzero ordinals ξ,η have leading exponents ρ,σ. If η is finite, then ξη has leading exponent ρ. If η is infinite, so that σ>0, then ξη has leading exponent ρ+σ. This follows by multiplying the leading terms in Cantor normal form and using continuity of ordinal multiplication at limit ordinals.

Additively closed ordinal

Additively indecomposable ordinal OurBigBook.com
A nonzero ordinal δ is additively closed when β+γ<δ for all β,γ<δ. These are exactly the ordinals ωα: sufficiency follows from Cantor normal form, while necessity follows by splitting any leading coefficient or nonzero tail below δ into two smaller summands.

Ordinal arithmetic

Words: 296 Articles: 6
Ordinal OurBigBook.com
Ordinal arithmetic defines addition, multiplication and exponentiation of ordinals through order types or transfinite recursion. The usual operations need not be commutative. Natural sum and natural product instead combine Cantor normal forms and are commutative; the Hessenberg natural sum is the natural addition operation.

Fundamental sequence of a limit ordinal (λ[0]<λ[1]<⋯<λ,supn​λ[n]=λ)

Words: 60
Ordinal arithmetic OurBigBook.com
For a countable limit ordinal, a fundamental sequence is an increasing cofinal sequence of smaller ordinals. Effective choices allow a fast-growing hierarchy to turn limit recursion into recursion at a finite input. Below epsilon zero, write the final term as γ+ωβ. For successor β=δ+1, take γ+ωδ(n+1); for nonzero limit β, take γ+ωβ[n]. Earlier terms in the Cantor normal form remain fixed.

Fast-growing hierarchy (F0​(n)=n+1,Fα+1​(n)=Fαn+1​(n),Fλ​(n)=Fλ[n]​(n))

Words: 120 Articles: 1
Ordinal arithmetic OurBigBook.com
A fast-growing hierarchy indexes number-theoretic functions by an effective system of ordinal notations. Successor stages iterate the preceding function, while limit stages diagonalize along a chosen fundamental sequence of a limit ordinal. These choices are part of the definition. With the standard Cantor normal form sequences below epsilon zero, each fixed Fα​ for α<ε0​ is provably total in Peano arithmetic, but the uniform endpoint Fε0​​ is not. Every function provably total in Peano arithmetic is eventually bounded by some lower level. These classification results are proved in epub.ub.uni-muenchen.de/3843/1/3843.pdf .

Eventual dominance in a fast-growing hierarchy (α<β⟹Fα​(n)<Fβ​(n) eventually)

For the standard fundamental sequences of limit ordinals below epsilon zero, the index order corresponds to eventual, rather than necessarily pointwise, growth. For example, under ω[n]=n+1 and successor iteration n+1 times, Fω​(1)=F2​(1)=7 whereas F3​(1)=2047. Limit stages are diagonal choices, so a larger index can give a smaller value at small arguments.
