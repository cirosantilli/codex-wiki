By irreducible traceless matrix Lie algebra is semisimple, let R be the solvable radical. Lie's theorem triangularizes its action, with finitely many diagonal characters λi​:R→C, and supplies a nonzero common eigenspace for one of them. For x∈L, conjugation by etx preserves R and gives an equivalent representation of it. Thus the multiset of characters λi​∘etadx is constant. Each character varies continuously in t and has values in that finite set of characters, so it is individually constant. Differentiating at zero gives λi​([x,r])=0 for all r∈R.

Thus Vλ​ is L-invariant, so irreducibility makes it all of Cn. Every element of R acts as a scalar. Its trace is zero because L⊆sln​, so that scalar is zero. Faithfulness of the matrix inclusion gives R=0. Hence L is semisimple​.
One sufficient diagonal-matrix condition for simplicity is that L contain all traceless diagonal matrices. Indeed invariance under their adjoint actions then decomposes L into that diagonal algebra and some matrix-unit spaces Ceij​. Put a directed edge j→i when eij​∈L. Irreducibility makes the directed graph strongly connected; otherwise a terminal component spans a proper invariant coordinate subspace. Bracketing matrix units along a simple directed path supplies the matrix unit between its endpoints. Strong connectivity therefore supplies every off-diagonal matrix unit, so L=sln​, which is simple. The mere existence of a diagonal matrix with distinct diagonal entries would not be a sufficient condition.
For the specified construction, H has dimension two. Its diagonal adjoint action and the twelve distinct nonzero weights give a direct sum L=H⊕⨁i=112​Cgi​, of dimension fourteen. The stated bracket condition and the weight-vector relations make it a Lie algebra. A subspace invariant under it is invariant under all the gi​ and hence their associative algebra, so it acts irreducibly on C7. The preceding result makes it semisimple.
Use prime-dimensional faithful irreducible module forces simplicity. To see this, decompose the algebra into simple ideals. Complete reducibility for one ideal and commutation with the others make its isotypic component invariant under the whole algebra, hence all of the representation. Writing that component as W⊗Hom(W,V) and repeating produces a tensor product of irreducibles for the simple ideals. Faithfulness makes every factor nontrivial, of dimension at least two. Their dimensions multiply to seven, so only one factor is possible. Thus the constructed L is simple.
The diagonal algebra H is toral, and its centralizer inside L is just H, since no gi​ has zero weight. A maximal toral subalgebra containing H lies in that centralizer, so equals H; it is a Cartan subalgebra. Thus the rank is two and the twelve weights are the roots. Pair them as αi+6​=−αi​. The opposite-root brackets have weight zero and belong to H. For distinct i,j≤6, the sum cannot be zero, and the supplied root-bracket result says their bracket is nonzero exactly when αi​+αj​ is a root. This is equivalent to −αi​−αj​ being a root, proving the requested equivalence for the paired brackets.
