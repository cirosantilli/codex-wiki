For shape and type both μ, column strictness makes every entry in row r at least r. All μ1​ entries equal to one must therefore be in the first row, which has exactly that length; it is filled with ones. Remove that row and repeat after subtracting one from the remaining labels. Induction gives the unique filling whose ith row consists of i's. Thus Kμμ​=1, and Young's rule proves that Sμ occurs exactly once.
More generally, if a semistandard filling of shape λ and type μ exists, its entries at most k must lie in the first k rows. Counting them gives
∑i=1k​μi​≤∑i=1k​λi​for every k.
(29)
These are the inequalities defining the dominance order on partitions. Hence Kλμ​>0 implies λ≥μ​, and the asserted restriction on summands follows from Young's rule.
