Use induction on dimV. By part (b), there is a one-dimensional invariant linear subspace U=Kv on which L acts as zero. The induced operators on V/U remain nilpotent, so induction gives a simultaneous strictly upper triangular basis there. Part (c) lifts it together with v to such a basis of V.
Thus all elements strictly lower one common complete flag. A nested Lie bracket of sufficiently many flag-lowering operators lowers it past zero. Consequently the lower central series of L terminates, proving that the Lie algebra is nilpotent. In fact its nilpotency class is at most n−1 for n>1; the zero algebra covers the one-dimensional case.
