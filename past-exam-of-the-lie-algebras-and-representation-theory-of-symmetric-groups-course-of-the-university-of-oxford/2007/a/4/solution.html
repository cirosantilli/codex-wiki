A reduced crystallographic root system in a real Euclidean space is a finite spanning subset Φ not containing zero, with Φ∩Rα={α,−α}, closed under sα​(v)=v−2(v,α)α/(α,α), and satisfying 2(β,α)/(α,α)∈Z for all roots. A base of a root system is a vector-space basis Δ⊆Φ such that every root has integer coefficients of one sign in that basis.
Choose h not orthogonal to any root and call roots with (h,α)>0 positive. Let Δ be the positive roots that cannot be written as a sum of two positive roots. Repeated decomposition terminates because each summand has strictly smaller positive h-value in a finite set. Thus every positive root is a nonnegative integer combination of Δ.
For distinct α,β∈Δ, (α,β)≤0: otherwise the allowed difference-root lemma would make either α−β or β−α positive, decomposing one of them. In a real linear relation among Δ, move negative coefficients to the other side, obtaining u=v as positive combinations with disjoint supports. Then ∥u∥2=(u,v)≤0, so u=0. But a nonempty positive combination has (h,u)>0, a contradiction. Thus the elements are linearly independent. They span every root and hence E, proving existence of a base of a root system. The difference-root lemma is used only for distinct roots; that qualification excludes the zero difference.
For the displayed set, there are eight coordinate roots, twenty-four roots with two nonzero unit entries, and sixteen half-coordinate roots: forty-eight in total. It spans R4, is reduced, and has squared root lengths one and two. Signed coordinate permutations preserve it. Reflections in coordinate roots are sign changes, and reflections in roots ei​±ej​ are signed coordinate permutations.
