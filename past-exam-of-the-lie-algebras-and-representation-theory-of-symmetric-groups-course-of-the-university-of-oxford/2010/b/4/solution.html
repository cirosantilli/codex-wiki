For a Young diagram cell (i,j), its hook length is one plus the number of cells to its right and below. The hook-length formula is
fλ=∏(i,j)∈λ​hij​n!​.
(22)
Applying it to the two shapes gives
f(n−2,1,1)=2(n−1)(n−2)​,f(n−2,2)=2n(n−3)​.​
(23)
The shapes here require n≥4.
A hook tabloid remembers its first-row set and the ordered singleton entries below it. The proposed map keeps that first-row set and merges the remaining entries into one unordered row. Thus it is well-defined on tabloids. Permuting entries before merging gives the same tabloid as merging before permuting, so it is a module homomorphism. Every target tabloid is reached by putting its second-row entries, in any order, into the singleton rows. Therefore it is surjective.
For r=2, the strictly dominating partitions of λ=(n−2,1,1) are exactly (n),(n−1,1),(n−2,2). Indeed dominance forces the first part to be at least n−2, and the possibilities for the remaining sum give precisely those shapes or λ itself.
In characteristic zero, Maschke's theorem makes the permutation module semisimple, and Young's rule gives one copy of Sλ and only strictly dominating additional shapes. The trivial multiplicity is one, since a transitive permutation module has a one-dimensional invariant space. For the standard representation S(n−1,1), Frobenius reciprocity identifies its multiplicity with the dimension of fixed vectors under the row stabilizer Sn−2​. In its realization {(z1​,…,zn​):∑zi​=0}, those fixed vectors have the form (c,…,c,a,b) with (n−2)c+a+b=0, a two-dimensional space. Thus its multiplicity is two.
Finally dimMλ=n(n−1). Subtracting the dimensions of one Sλ, one trivial module and two standard modules leaves n(n−3)/2, exactly one S(n−2,2). Therefore
M(n−2,1,1)≅S(n−2,1,1)⊕S(n)⊕2S(n−1,1)⊕S(n−2,2).​
(24)
The additional multiplicities are 1,2,1 respectively. If the printed weak summation index is read literally alongside its separate Sλ, then mλ,λ​=0; the total multiplicity of Sλ is one.
