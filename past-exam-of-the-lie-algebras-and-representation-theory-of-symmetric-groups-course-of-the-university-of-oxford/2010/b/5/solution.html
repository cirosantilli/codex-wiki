A tabloid is the row-equivalence class {t} of a tableau filled bijectively with 1,…,n: order inside each row is forgotten, but the rows remain distinguished. The permutation module Mλ has these tabloids as an F-basis, with Σn​ permuting their entries. Let Ct​ be the column stabilizer of t and define
κt​=∑σ∈Ct​​sgn(σ)σ,et​=κt​{t}.
(25)
The Specht module Sλ is the submodule spanned by all polytabloids et​.
If a module homomorphism ϕ:Mλ→Mμ is nonzero on Sλ, it is nonzero on some spanning et​. Equivariance gives 0=ϕ(et​)=κt​ϕ({t}), so κt​Mμ=0. The allowed criterion yields λ≥μ.
In characteristic zero, Maschke's theorem gives an equivariant projection Mλ→Sλ. If Sλ≅Sμ, compose that projection with the isomorphism and the inclusion into Mμ. The preceding argument gives λ≥μ. Reversing the roles gives μ≥λ, so antisymmetry of dominance gives
λ=μ.​
(26)
For a map defined only on a Specht module in arbitrary characteristic, dominance need not hold. Take F=F2​, n=2, λ=(1,1) and μ=(2). The first Specht module is the one-dimensional span of
e={1∣2}−{2∣1}={1∣2}+{2∣1}.
(27)
The transposition fixes e, so this is the trivial module. The one-row permutation module M(2) is also trivial and one-dimensional. Sending e to its single tabloid is a nonzero equivariant map, yet (1,1)≥(2). In this characteristic such a map need not extend to the surrounding permutation module; that is exactly where the earlier proof ceases to apply.
