The hook formula gives v2​(fλ)=v2​(n!)=a, since its denominator is odd. Thus 2a divides fλ. Conversely 2a∣fγ implies v2​(∏hγ​)=a−v2​(fγ)≤0. The product is an integer, so its valuation is zero and all its hooks are odd. By the equivalence γ is a staircase; its size uniquely determines its length, so γ=λ.
For the character assertion, every removable rim hook has length equal to some hook length of the diagram. Here is a direct justification. If a border strip λ/μ occupies rows i through j, connectedness and absence of a two-by-two block make adjacent strips of boxes overlap in exactly one column. Hence μl​=λl+1​−1 for i≤l<j, so its size is λi​−μj​+j−i. The column c=μj​+1 has height j, giving exactly hλ​(i,c)=λi​−μj​+j−i. Therefore every removable rim hook of the staircase has odd length.
A fixed-point-free permutation of power-of-two order has only even cycles. Apply the Murnaghan–Nakayama rule starting with any cycle. It would require removal of a rim hook of that even length, but none exists. The sum is empty, so
χλ(g)=0.​
(15)
Finally suppose γ has distinct parts and is not λ. It cannot be another staircase of the same size. Thus some gap between successive rows is at least two, or the last row has length at least two; in either case a horizontal rim domino is removable. No vertical rim domino is removable, since that would require two equal adjacent row lengths. Murnaghan-Nakayama on a transposition consequently gives a nonempty sum
χγ(τ)=∑removable horizontal dominoes​fγ∖domino>0,
(16)
all signs being positive and all tableau counts positive. Thus the character is nonzero at every transposition, proving the requested existence.
