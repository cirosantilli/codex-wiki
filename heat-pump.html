Heat pump

 Home Physics Branches of physics Statistical physics Thermodynamics
OurBigBook.com Words: 1k Articles: 2
A heat pump uses work to deliver heat to the region we want to keep warm. Its useful output is the hot-side delivery Qh​. The same operating machine can be described as a refrigerator when the cold-side extraction Qc​ is the objective: which output is useful changes the coefficient of performance, while the energy balance stays Qh​=Qc​+W. Heating a water bath by taking heat from a colder water bath makes this distinction visible in the heat-pump calorimetry experiment below. [ref]
In the illustrative budget from refrigerator, supplying 1kJ of work delivers 4kJ of useful heat, giving a heating coefficient of performance of 4. The additional 3kJ came from the colder region. Compare an ideal electrical heater whose entire input work becomes heat in the target region: it delivers 1kJ for that input. The appeal of a heat pump is this ability to move existing energy, while the Second law of thermodynamics limits the work saving for a given pair of temperatures.
