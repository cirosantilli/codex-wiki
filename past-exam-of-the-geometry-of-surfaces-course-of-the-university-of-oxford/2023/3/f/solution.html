For 0<b−a<2π, let D=r([a,b]×[c,d]) and use the orientation of a surface determined by N=ru​×rv​/∣ru​×rv​∣. Its positive boundary orientation follows the bottom side in increasing u, the right side in increasing v, the top side in decreasing u, and the left side in decreasing v. The Gauss-Bonnet theorem for this disc with four corners reads
∫D​κdA+∫∂D​κg​ds+∑j=14​εj​=2π,
(44)
where the εj​ are the signed exterior angles.
The bottom and top sides have cancelling geodesic curvature integrals: a screw motion takes one to the other with opposite boundary orientation. Explicitly, along increasing u at fixed v, the speed is one and the signed geodesic curvature is −sinu/1+sin2u​ on both sides. The right and left sides contribute, respectively, the integral along γb​ and minus the integral along γa​.
Although the coordinate sides are generally not perpendicular, the corner contributions still sum to 2π. Indeed, let θ(u) be the angle between ru​ and rv​, so
cosθ(u)=3+2cosu​1+cosu​.
(45)
Starting at the bottom-left corner, the exterior angles are π−θ(a), θ(b), π−θ(b) and θ(a). Their sum is 2π. Cancelling this from the Gauss-Bonnet theorem leaves
∫D​κdA=∫γa​​κg​ds−∫γb​​κg​ds,
(46)
which is the identity in part (e). For a rectangle covering more than one turn, apply the same argument on the parameter rectangle with its induced Riemannian metric, or partition it into narrower rectangles and cancel the artificial internal boundary contributions. This gives the version counting multiplicity, as required by the correction in part (e).
