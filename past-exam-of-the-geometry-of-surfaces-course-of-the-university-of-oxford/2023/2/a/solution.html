Let x∈X and choose holomorphic coordinate charts centred at x and f(x). A nonconstant holomorphic map has a local expansion w∘f(z)=czm+O(zm+1), where c=0 and m≥1. Taking a local holomorphic mth root of the nonvanishing factor and changing the source coordinate gives the exact local form w=zm. The integer
νf​(x)=m​
(10)
is the ramification index of a holomorphic map. A ramification point of a holomorphic map is a source point with νf​(x)>1, equivalently where its derivative vanishes; a branch value of a holomorphic map, also called a branch point here, is the image of such a point in Y. These are different sets, one in the source and one in the target.
Every fiber of a function is finite: locally it is the zero set of a nonzero holomorphic function, so its points are isolated, and compactness prevents infinitely many such zeros without an accumulation point. The same argument applied to the local derivatives makes the set of ramification points of a holomorphic map finite. The open mapping theorem and compactness show that f(X) is both open and closed in the connected target Y, hence f is surjective.
For any y∈Y, the degree of a holomorphic map is
d=degf=x∈f−1(y)∑​νf​(x).​
(11)
To see independence of y, choose disjoint small coordinate charts about its finitely many preimages. The local form z↦zνf​(x) shows that nearby values have νf​(x) preimages counted with multiplicity in the corresponding chart. Compactness excludes additional preimages outside these charts for sufficiently nearby values: a sequence of such preimages would have a limit in f−1(y). The displayed sum is therefore locally constant, hence constant on connected Y. Away from branch values of a holomorphic map it is the ordinary number of preimages, and f is a d-sheeted covering map there.
The Riemann-Hurwitz formula is
χ(X)=dχ(Y)−x∈X∑​(νf​(x)−1),​
(12)
or, in terms of the two genera,
2gX​−2=d(2gY​−2)+x∈X∑​(νf​(x)−1).​
(13)
For its proof, choose a triangulation of a surface on Y containing every branch value of a holomorphic map among its vertices. Every open edge and open face has d lifts, since its interior avoids branch values of a holomorphic map. The lifted cells give a finite CW complex structure on X. If VY​,EY​,FY​ are the target counts and R=∑x​(νf​(x)−1), the source counts are
EX​=dEY​,FX​=dFY​,VX​=∑y vertex​#f−1(y)=dVY​−R.
(14)
Indeed, at a target vertex the deficit from d distinct preimages is precisely ∑x↦y​(νf​(x)−1), and all nonzero contributions occur over vertices. Taking the alternating sum gives χ(X)=d(VY​−EY​+FY​)−R, as required. This is the Triangulation proof of the Riemann-Hurwitz formula.
