Every nonidentity biholomorphism in the finite group G has only finitely many fixed points, by the identity theorem on a Riemann surface and compactness. Because the group action is a faithful group action, the union of these finite sets does not exhaust X. A point outside this union has trivial stabilizer subgroup, so its group orbit has ∣G∣ points by the orbit-stabilizer theorem. The generic fiber of a function of the projection is such an group orbit, giving
degf=∣G∣.​
(15)
More precisely, put H=Gx​ and m=∣H∣. Differentiation at x gives a group homomorphism H→C×. It is injective: if a nonidentity finite-order germ had the form h(z)=z+ak​zk+O(zk+1) with k≥2 and ak​=0, then its qth iterate would begin z+qak​zk, so no positive iterate could be the identity. A germ equal to the identity gives the identity on X by the identity theorem on a Riemann surface. Thus H is a cyclic group, with derivative image the mth roots of unity.
For completeness, an explicit local coordinate linearizing H is
w(p)=m1​∑h∈H​h′(x)−1z(hp).
(16)
It has w′(x)=1, and reindexing the sum gives w(hp)=h′(x)w(p). Therefore a coordinate on the local quotient space is wm. Other translates of a sufficiently small neighbourhood of x are disjoint unless their translating element lies in H, so this local quotient space is also a chart for X/G. The local cyclic quotient of a Riemann surface consequently gives
νf​(x)=∣Gx​∣.​
(17)
