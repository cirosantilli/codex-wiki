Generalized-weight decomposition for a nilpotent Lie algebra (V=⨁λ​Vλ)

 ... Lie algebra Semisimple Lie algebra Highest-weight representation Weight vector Weight space Generalized weight space of a Lie algebra representation
OurBigBook.com Words: 222 Articles: 1
Every finite-dimensional complex Lie algebra representation of a nilpotent Lie algebra decomposes into invariant generalized weight spaces. The nilpotence assumption is essential: a general solvable action can have extensions between distinct weights.
Here is a proof. For h,k∈H, nilpotence gives (adh)Nk=0, hence (adρ(h))Nρ(k)=0. By the nilpotent commutator preserves generalized eigenspaces lemma, every generalized eigenspace of ρ(h) is invariant under every ρ(k). Split successively by the acting operators of a basis of H. Each resulting block is invariant under H, and each basis operator has only one eigenvalue on it. The Lie theorem triangularizes the action on each block; its diagonal entries therefore give one and the same linear functional λ on every basis vector of that block. Every ρ(h)−λ(h)I is strictly upper triangular there, proving that the block is Vλ. Distinct blocks have distinct weights, so this is a direct sum. On the zero generalized weight space, every acting operator is a nilpotent endomorphism, so the Engel theorem supplies a nonzero common annihilated vector whenever that space is nonzero.
