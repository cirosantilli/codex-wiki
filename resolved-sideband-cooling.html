Resolved sideband cooling

 Home Physics Atomic physics Atomic measurement methods Ion trap Trapped-ion quantum control
OurBigBook.com Words: 248 Articles: 1
Resolved sideband cooling removes motional quanta by selectively driving a red motional sideband and then resetting the internal quantum state through spontaneous emission or optical pumping. Spectral resolution requires the effective cooling-transition width smaller than the motional angular frequency in the same units. A narrow Raman transition can meet that requirement even when the optical transition used for Doppler cooling is broad.
The reset is essential: the coherent red motional sideband merely exchanges internal and motional excitation reversibly. To see the distinction, an ideal pulse sends ∣g,1⟩ to −i∣e,0⟩; the same interaction can send it back. An ideal dissipative reset instead takes ∣e,0⟩ to ∣g,0⟩, with information and energy carried into the environment. Repeating this cycle accumulates population near the motional ground state. Recoil during the reset can add motion, and off-resonant excitation can disturb ∣g,0⟩; neither is included in the ideal pulse below.
