Global hydrodynamic mode

Words: 35
Hydrodynamic stability OurBigBook.com
A global hydrodynamic mode is a separated solution a(x)e−iωt satisfying the complete spatial boundary conditions of a linearized flow problem. Its eigenvalue depends on the spatial domain and variable coefficients, rather than solely on a local dispersion relation.

Global hydrodynamic instability

Words: 100 Articles: 1
Hydrodynamic stability OurBigBook.com
A spatially nonuniform system is globally unstable when a normal mode satisfying its global spatial boundary conditions has positive temporal growth rate. This differs from purely local temporal amplification or convective wave-packet instability. A locally absolutely unstable region can be necessary without being sufficient for a growing global mode of finite spatial extent.

For the whole-line linear complex Ginzburg-Landau equation with real positive diffusion γ and μ(x)=μ0​−νϵ2x2, removing drift and scaling ξ=ϵ​(ν/γ)1/4x gives the Hermite differential equation. Critical layer in a shear flow (U(yc​)=c0​)

Words: 281 Articles: 4
Hydrodynamic stability OurBigBook.com
A critical layer is a region where the leading wave phase speed coincides with the local shear-flow velocity and an outer approximation degenerates. Small viscosity or other corrections must then be retained on a distinguished transverse scale. The width depends on the order of vanishing of U−c0​ and on the governing mode equation; simple-shear and stationary-profile layers have different balances.

A stationary point in a non-monotone velocity profile can support a quarter-power critical layer whose phase-speed correction has positive imaginary part. Critical level of a shear-flow wave (U(zc​)=c)

Words: 64
Hydrodynamic stability OurBigBook.com
For a wave with zonal phase velocity c in a basic shear U(z), a critical level is a height zc​ at which U(zc​)=c. The Doppler-shifted material frequency then vanishes. This means equality of the laboratory velocities; the U and −c terms cancel in the wave frame. It does not mean U=−c. A semi-infinite Eady edge wave with zero interior potential vorticity anomaly can remain regular through its steering height.

Baroclinic instability

Words: 42
Hydrodynamic stability OurBigBook.com
Baroclinic instability converts potential energy of a stratified sheared basic flow into growing disturbances. In the Eady model, coupled Boundary Rossby waves provide a useful mechanism: their induced velocities displace boundary buoyancy anomalies, and suitable wavelengths permit phase locking. A single neutral boundary wave does not by itself imply exponential instability.

Convective hydrodynamic instability

Words: 22
Hydrodynamic stability OurBigBook.com
A convective hydrodynamic instability amplifies an advected localized disturbance while its amplitude decays at any fixed position. The distinction from absolute hydrodynamic instability depends on the observation frame.

Absolute hydrodynamic instability

Words: 281 Articles: 5
Hydrodynamic stability OurBigBook.com
An absolute hydrodynamic instability makes the response to a localized impulse grow at a fixed spatial point in a specified frame. This differs from convective hydrodynamic instability, which amplifies a moving wave packet while its response decays at fixed positions. For analytic dispersion relations, the relevant absolute frequency is associated with an accessible zero-group velocity saddle, with the spatial-branch selection checked rather than assumed for an arbitrary dispersion relation.

Anisotropic absolute-instability threshold for positive diffusion (μa​=41​cTΓ−1c)

Absolute hydrodynamic instability OurBigBook.com
For ηt​+c⋅∇η=μη+∇⋅(Γ∇η) with symmetric positive definite Γ, the dispersion relation is ω=c⋅k+i(μ−kTΓk). Its zero-group velocity saddle is k0​=−iΓ−1c/2. The anisotropic heat kernel has fixed-position exponential rate μ−cTΓ−1c/4, so this accessible saddle gives the actual threshold. The absolute frequency is the generally complex angular frequency at the physically selected absolute wavenumber. Its imaginary part is the fixed-position exponential growth rate under the convention e−iωt. Marginal zero exponential growth may still carry an algebraic prefactor.
