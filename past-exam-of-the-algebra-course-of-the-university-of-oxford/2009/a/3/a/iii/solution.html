OurBigBook.com Words: 156
A semisimple module is a direct sum of simple modules. An algebra is semisimple when all its modules are semisimple. Suppose the left regular module A is semisimple. Every module M is a quotient of a free module ⨁j∈J​A, by sending its basis elements to a generating set of M. This free module is a direct sum of simple modules.
A quotient of a direct sum of simple modules is semisimple: the images of its simple summands are simple or zero and span the quotient. Choose a maximal direct sum among these simple images, allowing all simple submodules in the choice. If it does not span the whole quotient, some simple image is not contained in it. Its intersection with that direct sum is zero by simplicity, so it could be added, contradicting maximality. For infinite index sets, Zorn's lemma supplies the maximal direct sum; in finite dimension the construction terminates directly.
Hence every M is semisimple, and A is a semisimple algebra. This proves semisimplicity from the regular module.
