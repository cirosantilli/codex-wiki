Choose s=0 in the simple module S. The map ϕ:A→S, a↦as, is an A-module homomorphism whose image is the nonzero submodule As, hence all of S. Thus the first isomorphism theorem for modules gives
S≅A/kerϕ.​
(11)
The kernel is a maximal left ideal because the quotient is simple. Whenever the regular module has finite length, as it does for a finite-dimensional algebra, take a composition series of the kernel and append kerϕ<A. Its last factor is S, so S occurs as a composition factor of the regular module; the Jordan–Hölder theorem for modules makes that independent of the chosen series.
The quotient conclusion holds for every unital algebra. If an arbitrary infinite-dimensional regular module has no finite composition series, the universally valid statement is that S is a simple subquotient; a claim about the factors of a finite series requires finite length.
