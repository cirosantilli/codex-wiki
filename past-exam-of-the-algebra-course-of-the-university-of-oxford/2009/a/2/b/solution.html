The class sizes sum to ∣G∣=36. A degree-one character is a homomorphism into C×. The supplied χ2​ has order four and χ22​=χ1​, so 1,χ2​,χ1​,χˉ​2​ are four distinct irreducible characters. A finite group has as many complex irreducible characters as conjugacy classes, so there are exactly two more. Their degrees d,e satisfy d2+e2=36−4=32, whose only positive-integer solution is d=e=4.
The column orthogonality give ∑χ​∣χ(g)∣2=∣CG​(g)∣. Each of the last three classes has centralizer order 36/9=4, already exhausted by the four degree-one rows. Thus both new rows vanish on C4​,C5​,C6​.
To justify that their remaining values are real, put P=kerχ2​. The supplied values show P=C1​∪C2​∪C3​, a normal subgroup of order nine, with G/P≅C4​. A group of order nine is Abelian, by the standard p-group center argument and the fact that a cyclic quotient by the center forces the group to be Abelian. If P were cyclic of order nine, conjugation through G/P would act by an automorphism of order dividing both four and ∣Aut(C9​)∣=6, hence dividing two. Its nontrivial conjugacy classes could not have size four. Therefore P≅C32​.
Let h map to a generator of G/P, and let A be its conjugation action on the two-dimensional vector space P over F3​. The two nonzero orbits have length four, so A2 fixes no nonzero vector, while A4=I. Since t2−1 has distinct roots in characteristic three, the involution A2 is diagonalizable; the absence of a +1 eigenvector gives A2=−I. Thus conjugation by h2 inverts every element of P. Every element in C2​,C3​ is therefore conjugate to its inverse, so every character value there is real.
Write one new row as (4,a,b,0,0,0). Its orthogonality with the trivial row gives 4+4a+4b=0, so a+b=−1. Its squared character inner product one gives 16+4a2+4b2=36, so a2+b2=5 and ab=−2. Thus a,b are 1,−2 in some order. Column orthogonality with the identity column forces the other row's entries to be interchanged.
