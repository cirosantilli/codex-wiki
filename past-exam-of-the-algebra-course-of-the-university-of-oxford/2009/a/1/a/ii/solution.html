In the finite-dimensional setting of the preceding part, let S be a simple module. Each action map s↦as is an A-module endomorphism because A is commutative. The complex Schur's lemma makes every such action a scalar. Therefore every linear subspace of S is an submodule; simplicity forces
dimC​S=1.​
(2)
Equivalently, a simple module over a commutative ring is a quotient by a maximal ideal, and a finite-dimensional field extension of C is just C.
The finite-dimensional context matters. If the clause is read as allowing an arbitrary commutative complex algebra, the unrestricted assertion is false: A=C(t) is simple as a module over itself and is infinite-dimensional over C. This is simple module of a finite-dimensional commutative complex algebra. Thus the one-dimensional conclusion uses the finite-dimensional algebra context, or an alternative hypothesis such as finite dimensionality of the simple module.
