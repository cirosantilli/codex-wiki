The commutator subgroup is G′=⟨xyx−1y−1:x,y∈G⟩. It is characteristic and hence normal, because every automorphism sends a commutator to a commutator. If G′≤H, then ghg−1h−1∈H for every g∈G,h∈H, so H is normal. Every commutator becomes trivial in G/H, making that quotient Abelian. Conversely, the quotient homomorphism to an Abelian group kills each commutator, and hence kills G′. Therefore
G′≤H⟺H◃G and G/H is Abelian.​
(20)
Set G(0)=G, G(i+1)=(G(i))′. The group is soluble exactly when this derived series reaches the identity after finitely many steps.
If a nontrivial simple group is soluble, its normal subgroup G′ is either 1 or G. The latter would make the derived series constant and nontrivial, so G′=1 and G is Abelian. Every nonidentity element then generates G, since its cyclic subgroup is normal. An infinite cyclic group is not simple, and a finite cyclic group is simple exactly when its order is prime. This proves soluble simple group has prime order.
