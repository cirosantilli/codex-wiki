By Cauchy theorem for groups, choose a of order p and b of order two. The subgroup P=⟨a⟩ has index two, hence is normal, and b∈/P. Thus a,b generate G. Conjugation by b has bab−1=ar with r≡0(modp) and r2≡1(modp). Since p is prime, r≡1 or −1.
If r=1, the two generators commute and ab has order 2p, so G is cyclic. If r=−1, then (ab)2=e and ab=b. The two involutions b,ab generate G, because (ab)b=a. Hence G is dihedral in the stated convention. Thus
G≅C2p​orD2p​,​

where D2p​ denotes the dihedral group of order 2p. In both cases P is a proper nontrivial normal subgroup, so G is not simple.
