For a finite group with ∣G∣=pam and p∤m, a Sylow p-subgroup has order pa. The Sylow theorems assert existence, containment of every p-subgroup in a Sylow subgroup, conjugacy of all Sylow subgroups, and np​∣m, np​≡1(modp).
For Sylow subgroups of A6, ∣A6​∣=6!/2=360=23⋅32⋅5. Explicit choices are
P5​=⟨(12345)⟩≅C5​,P3​=⟨(123),(456)⟩≅C3​×C3​.​
(17)
Their elements are even permutations, and their orders are the largest powers of five and three dividing 360.
Every nonidentity element of P5​ is a five-cycle. There are (56​)4!=144 five-cycles, and distinct subgroups of order five meet only at the identity. Each contributes four such elements, giving
n5​=144/4=36.​
(18)
For P3​, each unordered partition of the six letters into two triples gives the subgroup generated by the two triple-cycles. It is independent of the chosen orientation of either cycle. There are 21​(36​)=10 such partitions. Different partitions give different subgroups, since the four single three-cycles in a subgroup identify the two triples. Every Sylow subgroup of order nine is conjugate to the displayed P3​, so every one arises from a partition in this way. Hence
n3​=10.​
(19)
The normalizer is NG​(H)={g∈G:gHg−1=H}. Its definition makes H normal in NG​(H). If H is a Sylow subgroup of G, it is also a Sylow subgroup of NG​(H), since H≤NG​(H)≤G leaves no room for a larger power of p. The Sylow theorems make all Sylow subgroups of the normalizer conjugate there, and normality of H makes every such conjugate equal to H. Thus H is the unique Sylow subgroup of its normalizer.
