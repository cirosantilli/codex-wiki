For any idempotent e, the set Ae is closed under multiplication and e acts as its identity. With f=1−e the map a↦(ae,af) is an algebra isomorphism A→Ae×Af: multiplication is componentwise because ef=0, and its inverse sends (u,v) to u+v.
The A-submodules of Ae are exactly its ideals as a unital algebra, since acting by a on Ae is the same as acting by ae. A nonzero commutative unital algebra is a simple module over itself exactly when every nonzero principal ideal is the whole algebra, which is equivalent to every nonzero element having an inverse. Thus a nonzero component Ae is simple exactly when it is a field. If e=0, both properties fail under the nonzero-field convention.
A semisimple regular module is a finite direct sum of simple ideals. Applying the preceding decomposition gives a finite direct product of fields. Conversely the coordinate ideals of a finite product of fields are simple modules, so its regular module is semisimple. The fields are finite extensions of K, because A has finite K-dimension.
For a generator g of the cyclic group of order three, evaluation X↦g gives a surjection Q[X]→QG with kernel (X3−1), by division and independence of 1,g,g2. The factors X−1 and X2+X+1 are coprime, and the quadratic is irreducible over Q. The Chinese remainder theorem yields
QG≅(X3−1)Q[X]​≅Q×Q(−3​).​
