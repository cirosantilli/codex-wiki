A composition series is a chain 1=G0​◃G1​◃⋯◃Gr​=G with simple nontrivial factors. Every finite group has one, by recursively choosing a maximal proper normal subgroup. The Jordan–Hölder theorem states that any two such series have the same length and the same multiset of simple factors up to isomorphism.
For a proof induct on ∣G∣. Let M,N be the penultimate terms in two series. If M=N, apply induction there. Otherwise their maximal normality gives MN=G. Set D=M∩N. The second isomorphism theorem gives
M/D≅G/N,N/D≅G/M,
(8)
so these quotients are simple. Choose a composition series of D and extend it once to M and once to N. Induction in the smaller groups M,N compares these with the respective original prefixes. Appending G/M or G/N gives in both cases the factors of D together with G/M,G/N. This completes the proof, including the case D=1.
Exactly one: take S5​. Its only nontrivial proper normal subgroup is A5​. Indeed a normal N has N∩A5​ either 1 or A5​ by the supplied simplicity. In the first case N injects into S5​/A5​≅C2​, so a nontrivial N would be a normal subgroup of order two and hence central. But S5​ has trivial center: conjugating every transposition trivially forces preservation of each unordered pair, and therefore of every point. Thus the unique series is 1◃A5​◃S5​.
Exactly two: take A5​×C2​. A normal subgroup containing A5​×1 is that subgroup or the whole product. For a normal subgroup N meeting A5​×1 trivially, commutators of its elements with A5​×1 vanish; its first coordinates therefore lie in Z(A5​)=1. Hence N⊆1×C2​. The two nontrivial proper normal subgroups are exactly the two factors, each simple, so the two series pass respectively through A5​×1 and 1×C2​.
More than two: take C120​. A cyclic group has exactly one subgroup of each divisor order, and composition steps have prime indices. The orders of those indices are the distinct permutations of 2,2,2,3,5, giving
5!/3!=20 composition series.​
(9)
All three example groups have order 120.
