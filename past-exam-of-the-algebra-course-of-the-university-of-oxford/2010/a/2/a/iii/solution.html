The general Schur's lemma says that a homomorphism f:M→N between simple modules over any unital ring is either zero or an isomorphism. Indeed, kerf is a submodule of M and imf is a submodule of N. If f=0, simplicity gives kerf=0 and imf=N. In particular, EndR​(M) is a division ring.
For a complex algebra and a finite-dimensional simple module M, the stronger conclusion is
EndR​(M)=CidM​.​
(10)
For f∈EndR​(M), the fundamental theorem of algebra gives an eigenvalue λ∈C. The endomorphism f−λI has nonzero kernel, so the general Schur's lemma forces it to be zero. Consequently a homomorphism between two such simple modules is zero if they are nonisomorphic, and their homomorphism space is one-dimensional if they are isomorphic.
The finite-dimensional qualification is essential for this scalar conclusion. For example, the field C(t) is a complex algebra, simple as a module over itself, and its endomorphisms include multiplication by t, which is not a complex scalar.
