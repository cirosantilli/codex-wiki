By the Maschke theorem, the restricted representation is a direct sum of irreducible H-modules. The row orthogonality relations for a character table identify the multiplicity of ψi​ as di​=⟨χ↓H​,ψi​⟩H​. Therefore
χ↓H​=∑i​di​ψi​,di​∈Z≥0​.
(34)
Using the preceding character inner product identity and the irreducibility of χ,
∑i​di2​=∣H∣1​∑h∈H​∣χ(h)∣2≤∣H∣1​∑g∈G​∣χ(g)∣2=∣H∣∣G∣​.
(35)
This is the character restriction norm bound. When [G:H]=2, the nonzero sum of squares of integer multiplicities is either one or two. Hence the restriction is irreducible, or is the sum of two distinct irreducible characters, each with multiplicity one. This proves the requested index-two character restriction dichotomy directly; normality of an index-two subgroup is a general group theory fact.
