Use the column order 1,(123),(12)(34),(12345),(13452) in A5​. The two restrictions of degree four coincide and equal θ4​=(4,1,0,−1,−1); those of degree five coincide and equal θ5​=(5,−1,1,0,0). The centralizer orders give
⟨θ4​,θ4​⟩=6016​+31​+51​+51​=1,⟨θ5​,θ5​⟩=6025​+31​+41​=1.
(36)
Thus both are irreducible characters. The remaining given restriction is η=(6,0,−2,1,1), and
⟨η,η⟩=6036​+44​+51​+51​=2.
(37)
It splits as two distinct irreducible characters. Neither is 1, θ4​ or θ5​, since their character inner products with η are zero. A finite group has as many complex irreducible characters as conjugacy classes, so these five exhaust them. The sum of squares of irreducible degrees is ∣A5​∣=60. The first three degrees contribute 1+16+25=42, leaving 18 for the two constituents, whose degrees sum to six. Hence both have degree three.
Write one constituent as θ=(3,x,y,u,v). All five conjugacy classes are closed under inversion, so all its values are real: χ(g−1)=χ(g)​. Orthogonality with 1, θ4​ and θ5​ gives
603​+3x​+4y​+5u+v​=0,6012​+3x​−5u+v​=0,6015​−3x​+4y​=0.
(38)
Solving gives x=0, y=−1 and u+v=1. The squared norm one gives 9/60+1/4+(u2+v2)/5=1, so u2+v2=3 and uv=−1. Thus u,v are the roots of t2−t−1, namely φ=(1+5​)/2 and φˉ​=(1−5​)/2. Here the bar labels the other real root, rather than complex conjugation. The other constituent is η−θ, so the two last values are interchanged.
The Character table of the alternating group A5 is therefore
1θ3​θ3′​θ4​θ5​​113345​(123)1001−1​(12)(34)1−1−101​(12345)1φφˉ​−10​(13452)1φˉ​φ−10​​.​
(39)
The conjugacy class sizes are 1,20,15,12,12. A normal subgroup is a union of entire conjugacy classes containing the identity, and its order divides 60 by Lagrange's theorem. A nontrivial such union has size at least 13; the only proper divisors of 60 at least 13 are 15,20,30. Subtracting the identity would require a sum of some of 20,15,12,12 equal to 14,19,29, respectively, none of which is possible. Thus A5​ is simple.
