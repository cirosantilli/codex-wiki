Indeed A1​ is diagonal. In A2​ and A5​ the two off-diagonal generators have all products zero, so multiplication has the form (x,u)(x′,u′)=(xx′,xu′+x′u). The diagonal entries of a square force x=0, after which the off-diagonal part squares to zero. In A3​=K[N], a square-zero element first has scalar coefficient zero and then (yN+zN2)2=y2N2, forcing y=0. This argument works also in characteristic two. In A4​, a square first forces both diagonal parameters to vanish, leaving precisely KE12​. Noncommutativity follows from E22​E12​=0 but E12​E22​=E12​.
The coordinate map xI+yE12​+zE13​↦xI+yE13​+zE23​ is an isomorphism A2​→A5​, by the common multiplication formula. Every other pair is nonisomorphic: A4​ is the only noncommutative member, and among the commutative members the dimensions of the square-zero sets are respectively zero, two, one, and two. These sets are vector spaces in these examples, and an algebra isomorphism must carry one onto the other. Thus the only distinct isomorphic pair is A2​≅A5​.
