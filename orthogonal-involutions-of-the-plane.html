Orthogonal involutions of the plane (ATA=I,A2=I)

 ... Mathematics Area of mathematics Algebra Linear algebra Orthogonal group Orthogonal matrix
OurBigBook.com Words: 84
For a real orthogonal involution, A−1=A=AT, so it is a symmetric matrix. Its eigenvalues are ±1. In two dimensions the possibilities are the identity, the half-turn −I, and reflections
A=(cosθsinθ​sinθ−cosθ​).
(124)
The reflection fixes the line making angle θ/2 with the first coordinate axis and negates its perpendicular direction. This follows either from the two corresponding eigenvectors or by rotating diag(1,−1). A nonidentity element of order two in a faithful representation must be either the half-turn or a reflection; the identity matrix is excluded by faithfulness.
