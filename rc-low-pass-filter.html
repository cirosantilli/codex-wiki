RC low-pass filter (τ=RC)

 Home Electrical engineering Signal processing Linear filter Low-pass filter
OurBigBook.com Words: 1k Articles: 5
An RC low-pass filter places a resistor R>0 between input and output, with a capacitor C>0 from output to their common return. The ideal model prescribes the input electric potential difference and leaves the output unloaded. It is a first-order low-pass filter with unit eventual gain.[ref]
For electrical resistance R and capacitance C, Ohm's law gives the electric current through the resistor as (vin​−vout​)/R. The capacitor relation gives Cdvout​/dt. Equating them by Kirchhoff's current law yields the ordinary differential equation
RCdtdvout​​+vout​=vin​,τ=RC.
(72)
The time constant τ has units of time. A rapid change in electric potential difference requires a large electric current into the capacitor; the finite electrical resistance limits that rate. A slowly maintained voltage instead lets the capacitor approach the input voltage. The same equation therefore predicts both smoothing of sustained oscillations and a rounded response to a sudden change.
For the original constructed example throughout this article, take
R=2.2kΩ,C=47nF,τ=103.4μs.
(73)
These stipulated ideal values specify an analytical model, rather than measurements of particular components.
