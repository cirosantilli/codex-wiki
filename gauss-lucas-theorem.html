Gauss-Lucas theorem

 Home Mathematics Area of mathematics Algebra Polynomial
OurBigBook.com Words: 88
Every root of the derivative of a nonconstant complex polynomial lies in the convex hull of its roots. If a critical point β is itself a root, this is immediate. Otherwise factorization and the product rule give
0=Q(β)Q′(β)​=∑k​β−αk​1​.
(3)
After taking complex conjugates, set wk​=∣β−αk​∣−2>0 to obtain β=∑k​wk​αk​/∑k​wk​. This is a convex combination, proving the theorem, with roots counted according to their multiplicities. The same formula shows that roots strictly inside an open half-plane or a convex set that is open keep all critical points there. For a degree-one polynomial the derivative has no zeros and the assertion is vacuous.
