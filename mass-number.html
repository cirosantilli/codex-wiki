Mass number (A=Z+N)

 Home Chemistry Chemical substance Chemical element Isotope
OurBigBook.com Words: 115 Articles: 1
The mass number A is the total number of nucleons, namely protons plus neutrons, in an atomic nucleus:
A=Z+N,N=A−Z.
(197)
It is an exact positive integer with no units, rather than a measured mass. Carbon-12 has A=12 because its atomic nucleus contains six protons and six neutrons. No improvement in a mass measurement can turn that count into 11.999 or 12.001.[ref]
The left superscript in 1022​Ne denotes mass number 22, not an atomic mass of exactly 22u. For neon-22, the displayed reference atomic mass is about 21.991385114u. Even a weighted mean of mass numbers, such as 20.1877 for the illustrative neon population below, is a population average, not the mass number of an individual atomic nucleus.[ref]
