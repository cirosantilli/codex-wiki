Ground-state degeneracy

 Home Physics Branches of physics Quantum mechanics Ground state
OurBigBook.com Words: 277
When a ground state exists, its ground-state degeneracy is the dimension of the ground-state subspace:
g0​=dimker(H−E0​I),E0​=infσ(H).
(27)
Here I is the identity operator, and the kernel of a linear map is taken within the operator domain of the Hamiltonian operator H. The count concerns linearly independent energy eigenstates, rather than every normalized quantum superposition or every choice of global phase. Even a single energy eigenspace contains infinitely many representing vectors; its dimension measures independent freedom. Ground-state degeneracy is the lowest-energy instance of degenerate energy levels.
For an exact finite model with energy unit ε>0, let the Hamiltonian operator be the diagonal matrix
Htoy​=εdiag(0,0,2,5).
(28)
Its ground-state energy is zero and g0​=2: the first two coordinate unit vectors e1​,e2​ span the ground-state subspace. Every normalized ae1​+be2​, with ∣a∣2+∣b∣2=1, is a ground state, including (e1​+e2​)/2​. Its energy measurement is certain to return zero, even though a measurement distinguishing e1​ from e2​ need not have a definite outcome. Under the unchanged Hamiltonian operator, this quantum superposition is a stationary state because both components acquire the same global phase.
Ground-state degeneracy matters when an interaction selects or splits previously equal energy eigenvalues. In the finite model, adding εdiag(δ,−δ,0,0) with 0<δ<2 gives the formerly lowest pair the distinct energies ±εδ, leaving a unique ground state. This direct calculation illustrates why degenerate perturbation theory first examines the perturbation inside the whole ground-state subspace.
A classical continuous-symmetry model can instead have a manifold of minimizing configurations, such as the sphere of orientations of a Néel state in an isotropic Heisenberg antiferromagnet. That geometric description does not by itself count the dimension of a finite quantum ground-state subspace.
