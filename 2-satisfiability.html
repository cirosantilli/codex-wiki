2-satisfiability (2-SAT)

 ... Computational complexity theory Polynomial-time reduction Polynomial-time many-one reduction NP-hardness NP-completeness Boolean satisfiability problem
OurBigBook.com Words: 139
2-satisfiability restricts SAT to CNF in which each Boolean clause has at most two Boolean literals. The example in Boolean satisfiability problem is therefore a 2-SAT instance. Unlike general SAT and 3-SAT, 2-SAT has a polynomial-time algorithm; its tractability concerns finding a satisfying assignment or deciding that none exists, not merely checking a candidate. 3-SAT allows up to three Boolean literals per Boolean clause and is NP-complete, even though it includes many individually easy instances such as this one.[ref]
The key logical equivalence is
a∨b≡(¬a→b)∧(¬b→a),
(27)
where a,b are Boolean literals. It expresses a two-Boolean literal Boolean clause as two logical implications. Representing these logical implications as edges in a directed graph gives a route to the polynomial-time algorithm. In the example, C2​ says both x→z and ¬z→¬x: enabling the feature requires its component, and an absent component excludes the feature.
