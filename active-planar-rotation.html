Active planar rotation (v′=Rθ​v)

 ... Mathematics Area of mathematics Algebra Linear algebra Rotation matrix Planar rotation
OurBigBook.com Words: 120
For column coordinate vectors in the fixed Cartesian basis, an active planar rotation sends (x,y)T to (xcosθ−ysinθ,xsinθ+ycosθ)T. For θ=π/3 radians,
Rπ/3​=(1/23​/2​−3​/21/2​),Rπ/3​(20​)=(13​​).
(119)
The endpoint travels counterclockwise through 60∘ along a circle of radius 2. Its Euclidean norm stays 12+(3​)2​=2. The second Cartesian basis direction becomes Rπ/3​(0,1)T=(−3​/2,1/2)T, whose dot product with (1,3​)T is still zero.
A quick sign check is to look at the first column: Rθ​(1,0)T=(cosθ,sinθ)T must point above the horizontal axis for 0<θ<π. The columns have unit Euclidean norm and zero dot product, so RθT​Rθ​=I; the determinant is cos2θ+sin2θ=1. Finally, the transpose negates the off-diagonal entries, giving RθT​=R−θ​: turning back reverses the action. This is the same matrix used for passive rotation of coordinates, though that interpretation keeps the vector fixed.
