Lock-in amplifier

 Home Physics Branches of physics Electromagnetism Electrical circuit Electronic amplifier
OurBigBook.com Words: 1k Articles: 5
A lock-in amplifier measures the component of an input signal synchronized with a reference at a chosen frequency. It multiplies the input by a reference sinusoid, then uses a low-pass filter to retain the slowly varying product. Two references in phase quadrature recover both amplitude and phase difference. A shared timing source keeps the relative wave phase stable: this is coherent detection.[ref]
The useful trick is to move the wanted frequency to zero before averaging. An electronic amplifier alone enlarges both the wanted signal and unwanted noise; a lock-in amplifier instead selects a component by its timing. A photodiode's electric current can first be converted to an electric potential difference, so the same calculation can measure weak modulated light. The chopped-light lock-in experiment makes this selection tangible.
