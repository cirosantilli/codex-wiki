For a dividend-free Black-Scholes model with r>0, σ>0, K>0 and positive integer n, the optimal stopping value of the power put option is
V(S)={(K−S)n,(K−b)n(S/b)−γ,​0≤S≤b,S>b,​γ=σ22r​,b=γ+nγK​.
(10)
In the continuation region the stationary Black-Scholes equation has powers S and S−γ. Boundedness at infinity removes S. Value matching gives the coefficient (K−b)nbγ, maximized at the displayed b; equivalently this is the smooth fit boundary.
For a verification beyond constant-trigger policies, let g(S)=(K−S)+n​. The maximum property of bγ(K−b)n gives V≥g. For the infinitesimal generator L=rS∂S​+σ2S2∂SS​/2, (L−r)V=0 above b. Below b, set y=S/(K−S)≤γ/n; then
g(L−r)g​=21​σ2[n(n−1)y2−γny−γ]≤−r(1+y)<0.
(11)
The candidate is continuously differentiable across b, so the generalized Itô formula has no derivative-jump local time of a semimartingale term. The discounted candidate is a nonnegative supermartingale, bounding every stopping reward by optional stopping. First hitting of b attains the bound; the remainder at infinite time vanishes since V≤Kn and r>0. For n=1 this recovers the perpetual put option. At r=0 the value is Kn for S>0, approached but not attained at a finite exercise time. At r<0 the perpetual value is infinite, because exercise at deterministic times tending to infinity has unbounded discounted expected value.
