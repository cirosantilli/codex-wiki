Trade obstruction to weighted voting

 ... Area of mathematics Mathematical optimization Game theory Cooperative game theory Transferable utility game Weighted voting game
OurBigBook.com Words: 103
Suppose winning coalitions W1​,…,Wk​ and losing coalitions L1​,…,Lk​ contain each player the same total number of times:
∑j=1k​1{i∈Wj​}​=∑j=1k​1{i∈Lj​}​for every player i.
(23)
No real-weight, weak-quota weighted voting game represents those outcomes. Summing weights over the winning family gives at least kq, while summing over the losing family gives strictly less than kq. Equal player multiplicities make the two sums identical, a contradiction. This certificate works without assuming nonnegative weights.
For example, the winners {1,2,3},{1,4,5} and losers {1,2,4},{1,3,5} form a two-against-two obstruction. A monotone game whose minimal winning coalitions are the first two sets realizes this certificate, even though every winner contains player 1.
