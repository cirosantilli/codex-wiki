Slotted-line waveguide measurement

 Home Physics Branches of physics Electromagnetism Electromagnetic wave Rectangular electromagnetic waveguide
OurBigBook.com Words: 267
MIT's documented slotted-line waveguide measurement shorts one end of a rectangular electromagnetic waveguide and excites it through a probe coupled by capacitance. A second such probe moves through a longitudinal slot, aligned with the wall electric current to reduce disturbance. Its output locates axial standing wave nulls. The book's standing-wave setup chooses a frequency above the first waveguide cutoff frequency and below higher-mode cutoffs. Lowering it below the first cutoff instead reveals spatial decay away from the source. The text supplies apparatus and an observable comparison, rather than a table of measured null positions.[ref]
The inference can be checked without assuming that detector output is proportional to electric field amplitude. At fixed measured frequency, record several minima at coordinates z0​,…,zN​; then estimate λg​=2(zN​−z0​)/N when the minima are consecutive. This uses positions alone. Compare the result with the guided wavelength prediction using independently measured a and f. The 40mm spacing above is a synthetic example, not a reading from the MIT apparatus.
Additional proposed controls are to keep probe insertion fixed, repeat the scan in both directions, recheck source frequency, and compare shallow probe settings for shifts in the minima. These test probe loading, mechanical backlash and drift. A deliberately reflecting termination supplies the standing wave; varying its position should translate the pattern without changing adjacent-null spacing in a uniform single-mode region. Source-adjacent evanescent waves, unintended reflections, additional propagating normal modes, detector noise and finite positional resolution can spoil that inference. A disappearing remote signal alone does not distinguish below-cutoff decay from loss or poor excitation, and a scan at one frequency does not measure a pulse's group velocity.
