Elitzur-Vaidman bomb tester

 Home Physics Branches of physics Quantum theory Quantum measurement
OurBigBook.com Words: 323 Articles: 2
A photon in a quantum superposition of a safe path and an absorbing path can sometimes certify a live absorber without being absorbed. Recombination is chosen so that one output is impossible if the absorber is absent. A detection at that output is then an unambiguous successful test. The measurement back-action of a surviving live-absorber trial changes the interference pattern.
In 1993, Avshalom Elitzur and Lev Vaidman asked how to identify a working bomb whose sensor would be triggered by even one photon. Their theoretical proposal places the sensor in one arm of a Mach-Zehnder interferometer, adjusted so that destructive interference keeps one detector dark when both arms are unobstructed. A click there can identify a working bomb while leaving it unexploded. The motivation is to obtain information about a fragile object without destroying it; the bomb makes that operational claim explicit without committing to an interpretation of quantum mechanics.[ref]
