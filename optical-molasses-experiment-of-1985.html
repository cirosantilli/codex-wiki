Optical molasses experiment of 1985

 Home Physics Atomic physics Atomic measurement methods Laser cooling Optical molasses
OurBigBook.com Words: 181
Steven Chu's Bell Laboratories team prepared sodium atoms by pulsed-laser evaporation of a pellet. A frequency-swept opposing beam slowed them before six crossed laser beams supplied optical molasses. The vacuum chamber used a shield coated with graphite and cooled with liquid nitrogen as a pump. Fluorescence provided the readout; the initial beams were aligned as closely counterpropagating as possible. [ref].
After equilibration, the team switched off the cooling light for a variable interval, then restored it. Faster atoms escaped during the dark interval; slower atoms were recaptured. The recaptured fraction versus delay constrained the velocity distribution and hence temperature. The inference depended on the initial cloud shape and recapture volume: Steven Chu later acknowledged adjusting a geometry correction towards the expected Doppler cooling limit. Delay was the scanned variable, and the cooling beams themselves supplied the release/recapture control; visible fluorescence alone could not establish temperature. [ref].
