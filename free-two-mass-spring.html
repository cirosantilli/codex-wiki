Free two-mass spring (ω=k/μ​)

 Home Physics Branches of physics Classical mechanics Center of mass Reduced mass
OurBigBook.com Words: 197
Take two point masses joined by a massless spring of spring constant k>0 and equilibrium length ℓ>0. Assume one-dimensional motion along the spring, no external force, no damping, and an extension s=x2​−x1​−ℓ satisfying Hooke's law; keep x2​>x1​. For a real spring this is a model within its elastic range. Its potential energy is U=ks2/2, so
μs¨=−ks,s(t)=Acos(ωt+ϕ),ω=μk​​=k(m1​1​+m2​1​)​,ν=2πω​.
(60)
Here ω is angular frequency and ν is frequency. Choose ∣A∣<ℓ so the model bodies do not cross. The internal normal mode has displacements u1​=−m2​s/M and u2​=m1​s/M about equilibrium offsets from the centre of mass; its amplitude ratio is ∣u1​∣/∣u2​∣=m2​/m1​. The other normal mode is collective translation with zero frequency and no spring extension. This free pair has different boundary conditions from normal modes of two equal masses between three springs.
For k=10N/m and two 1kg masses, ω=20​s−1≈4.47214s−1 and ν≈0.711763Hz. Replacing the second mass by 100kg gives ω=10.1​s−1≈3.17805s−1 and ν≈0.505802Hz. Fixing the second endpoint instead would give 10​s−1: the free heavy partner still moves. The free pair's angular frequency exceeds that fixed-end limit by the factor 1+1/q​, about 1.004988 at q=100.
