Union of two matchings

 Home Mathematics Area of mathematics Foundations of mathematics Graph theory Matching (graph theory)
OurBigBook.com Words: 148
In the spanning subgraph formed by the union of two matchings in a graph M1​,M2​, every vertex has vertex degree at most two. A common edge is an isolated two-vertex connected component of a graph. Every other nontrivial connected component of a graph is a graph path or an even graph cycle, with the edges alternating between the two matchings in a graph.
Suppose the original graph is a bipartite graph with vertex sets A,B, M1​ covers a specified subset X⊆A, and M2​ covers a specified subset Y⊆B. Their union contains a matching in a graph covering X∪Y. On an even graph cycle, either alternating choice covers every vertex. On an odd-length graph path, the larger alternating choice covers every vertex. On an even-length graph path, both endpoints lie in the same side: choose M1​ if they lie in A, or M2​ if they lie in B. Every required endpoint is covered by that choice, and every internal vertex meets both choices. Select each common edge as well; isolated vertices are not in X∪Y. Different connected components of a graph do not interfere.
