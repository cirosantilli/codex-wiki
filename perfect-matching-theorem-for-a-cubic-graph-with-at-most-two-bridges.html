Perfect matching theorem for a cubic graph with at most two bridges

 ... Mathematics Area of mathematics Foundations of mathematics Graph theory Graph Cubic graph
OurBigBook.com Words: 118
Every finite connected cubic graph with at most two bridges has a perfect matching.
Proof. The handshaking lemma implies that the number n of vertices is an even integer. Fix a nonempty subset S of the vertex set, and let q count the components of G−S of odd integer order. For such a component C, counting vertex degrees gives 3∣C∣=2∣E(C)∣+d(C), where d(C) counts its edges to S. Thus d(C) is a positive odd integer. If it is one, the unique outgoing edge is a bridge; this happens for at most two components. For all others d(C)≥3. Therefore 3q−4≤3∣S∣. Meanwhile q≡n−∣S∣≡∣S∣(mod2). If q>∣S∣, the same parity would force q≥∣S∣+2, contradicting the preceding bound. For S=∅ there is one component of even integer order and hence q=0. Apply Tutte's theorem on perfect matchings.
