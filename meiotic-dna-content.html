Meiotic DNA content ((2n,2C)→(2n,4C)→(n,2C)→(n,C))

 Home Biology Genetics Meiosis
OurBigBook.com Words: 128
Ploidy counts sets of chromosomes; DNA amount counts material. Let n be the number of chromosomes in one complete set and C the DNA amount of one unreplicated set. For the ordinary diploid model, read each row per completed cell nucleus, not as a total across all products:
StagePloidyDNA amountChromosome organization
Before DNA replication2n2CTwo unreplicated sets
After DNA replication2n4CTwo sets; each chromosome has two sister chromatids
After meiosis In2COne replicated set
After meiosis IInCOne unreplicated set
In the illustrated n=2 example, the starting biological cell has four chromosomes. DNA replication gives eight chromatids, organized into four replicated chromosomes. Each product of meiosis I has two replicated chromosomes, hence four chromatids; each final product has two unreplicated chromosomes. Across completed products, 2(2C)=4C after meiosis I and 4(C)=4C after meiosis II: division redistributes the replicated DNA. A haploid cell nucleus can therefore have 2C without becoming diploid.
