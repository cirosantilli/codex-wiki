Cross-correlation ((f⋆g)(x)=∫R​f(y)​g(x+y)dy)

 Home Mathematics Area of mathematics Analysis Fourier analysis
OurBigBook.com Words: 185 Articles: 2
For this sign convention, cross-correlation compares a function with translated copies of another, conjugating the first factor. If f,g∈L1(R), Fubini's theorem gives Fourier transform f⋆g​(k)=f​(k)​g​(k). Indeed, the substitution s=x+y factors the double integral into ∫f(y)​eikydy and ∫g(s)e−iksds. The zero-lag value for f=g∈L2 is the squared L2 norm. Fourier inversion applied to this correlation proves the Parseval identity, first for Schwartz functions and then by density for L2 space.
