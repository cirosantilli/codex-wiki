Boundary-matrix construction of an ordinary differential Green function (G=G0​−hTM−1b)

 Home Mathematics Area of mathematics Analysis Green's function
OurBigBook.com Words: 112
Let h1​,…,hn​ be a basis of homogeneous solutions of a linear ordinary differential operator L of order n. Let B1​,…,Bn​ be independent homogeneous linear boundary conditions, which may be coupled. Suppose a fundamental solution G0​(x,ξ) satisfies Lx​G0​=δ(x−ξ). Define the boundary matrix Mij​=Bi​hj​ and bi​(ξ)=Bi​G0​(⋅,ξ). If M is invertible, then
G(x,ξ)=G0​(x,ξ)−∑j=1n​hj​(x)[M−1b(ξ)]j​.
(6)
The added terms are homogeneous solutions, so the Dirac delta source is preserved. Applying the boundary operators gives b−MM−1b=0. Any two such Green functions differ, for fixed source position, by a homogeneous solution with coefficient vector in the null space of M; invertibility therefore proves uniqueness. A singular boundary matrix signals a nontrivial homogeneous solution satisfying the endpoint conditions, where a compatibility condition and a nonunique inverse may arise. For L=D2, one convenient fundamental solution is (x−ξ)H(x−ξ).
