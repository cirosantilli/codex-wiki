Viscous dissipation potential (S∈∂Ω(D))

Words: 130 Articles: 1
Rheology OurBigBook.com
A convex scalar function Ω(D) on symmetric rate-of-strain tensors generates stress by S=∂Ω/∂D. For incompressible flow, its argument is trace free and a pressure reaction must be added to the stress. At nondifferentiable points the law is S∈∂Ω(D), where the subdifferential is defined by Ω(E)−Ω(D)≥S:(E−D) for every admissible E. This is a strain-rate potential, in contrast with potentials parameterized by thermodynamic forces.

Convex viscous potential minimum principle

Words: 69
Viscous dissipation potential OurBigBook.com
An inertialess fluid flow with symmetric stress generated by a convex viscous dissipation potential minimizes ∫Ω(D)dx−∫b⋅vdx−∫St​​t⋅vdS among fields satisfying the prescribed velocities, and any incompressibility constraint. The supporting-hyperplane inequality bounds the difference of two functional values below by ∫S:∇w−∫b⋅w−∫St​​t⋅w. Integration by parts, equilibrium divS+b=0 and w=0 on the velocity boundary make this lower bound zero. A pressure reaction has zero contraction with divergence-free variations. Convexity proves global minimality without asserting uniqueness.

A small-angle cone rotating above a stationary plate imposes approximately uniform shear rate, since local gap and wall speed both scale with radius. Non-Newtonian fluid

Words: 4k Articles: 97
Rheology OurBigBook.com
A non-Newtonian fluid has a stress--strain-rate relation with nonlinear, time-dependent, history-dependent, or microstructure-dependent behavior.

Simple fluid

Words: 104 Articles: 1
Non-Newtonian fluid OurBigBook.com
A simple fluid has local stress determined by the history of deformation of a material element, rather than by spatial derivatives of that history. An isotropic incompressible simple fluid has an arbitrary isotropic pressure and an objective constitutive functional of the relative deformation gradients. Internal-variable equations can represent such a functional when the initial material state is fixed by the past history.

Viscometric flow

Words: 45
Simple fluid OurBigBook.com
A viscometric flow has the local relative deformation history of steady simple shear flow, up to rigid changes of frame. For an isotropic simple fluid, symmetry leaves one shear stress and two independent normal-stress differences. The corresponding viscometric functions depend on the magnitude of the shear rate, while pressure supplies the isotropic part.

Shear banding

Words: 70
Non-Newtonian fluid OurBigBook.com
Shear banding is spatial separation into regions with different local shear rates. In simple inertialess planar shear, equal shear stress can coexist with different rates when the homogeneous constitutive curve is nonmonotonic. Gradient terms and boundary conditions can determine the interface and selected stress; a local multivalued flow curve alone does not provide that selection. Shear banding need not be confused with the ordinary smooth shear-rate variation in a pressure-driven channel.

Weissenberg number (Wi=τγ˙​)

Words: 62
Viscoelasticity OurBigBook.com
The Weissenberg number measures deformation accumulated over a viscoelastic relaxation time, often as the product of that time and a shear rate or an extension rate. Its precise rate convention should be stated. Unlike a Deborah number based on externally imposed temporal variation, it can remain nonzero in a steady flow. A weak-flow constitutive expansion generally requires both slow temporal change and small deformation over the memory time.

Deborah number (De=τ/tprocess​)

Words: 52
Viscoelasticity OurBigBook.com
The Deborah number compares a material's viscoelastic relaxation time with the time scale of an imposed process. A small value allows substantial stress relaxation during that process; a large value can reveal stored elasticity. In small-amplitude oscillatory shear, one commonly uses De=ωτ. Large frequency does not by itself invalidate a linear viscoelastic fluid approximation if the deformation amplitude remains sufficiently small.

Stress relaxation is decay of stored stress after a deformation is imposed and then held fixed. It probes a material's memory through its relaxation modulus. An instantaneous generalized Newtonian fluid has no stored shear stress once the shear rate is zero and cannot describe a nontrivial relaxation tail.
