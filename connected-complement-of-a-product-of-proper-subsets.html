Connected complement of a product of proper subsets ((X×Y)∖(A×B))

 Home Mathematics Area of mathematics Geometry and topology Connected space
OurBigBook.com Words: 138
Suppose X,Y are nonempty connected spaces, and A⊊X, B⊊Y are proper subsets. Then C=(X×Y)∖(A×B), with its subspace topology from the product topology, is a connected space. No closed set or open set hypothesis on A,B is needed.
Choose x0​∈X∖A, y0​∈Y∖B. The two topological subspaces {x0​}×Y and X×{y0​} are homeomorphic to the factors, intersect at (x0​,y0​), and lie in C. Their union is a connected subset H. Every (x,y)∈C has x∈/A or y∈/B. In the first case its entire vertical copy {x}×Y lies in C and meets H; in the second case the horizontal copy X×{y} does so. Thus C is a connected union with a connected hub.
Both properness assumptions are needed for this general result. Taking X=Y=R, A=X and B={0} gives the two disjoint open half-planes y<0 and y>0, a separation of a topological space of the complement.
