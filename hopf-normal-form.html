Hopf normal form (z˙=(μ+iω)z+ℓz∣z∣2+⋯)

 Home Physics Branches of physics Dynamical systems Bifurcation theory Hopf bifurcation
OurBigBook.com Words: 299
The Hopf normal form is a local normal form of a dynamical system on the critical two-dimensional centre manifold. After shifting the equilibrium point and changing coordinates, its cubic truncation can be written
z˙=(μ+iω(μ))z+(a+ib)z∣z∣2,r˙=μr+ar3,θ˙=ω(μ)+br2,
(9)
where μ is the critical eigenvalue real part, a=Reℓ(0), b=Imℓ(0), and ω(0)>0. Smooth parameter dependence of the cubic coefficients and higher-order terms are omitted in this truncation. The real coefficient a controls radial saturation; the imaginary coefficient b changes the angular frequency with amplitude. This finite-order reduction, rather than literal equality to a cubic equation everywhere, is the meaning of the Hopf normal form for a general system.[ref]
In the cubic truncation, r∗2​=−μ/a whenever μa<0. Substituting into the angular equation gives Ω=ω(μ)−bμ/a. For the full smooth system, the leading normal form radius satisfies r∗2​=−μ/a+o(∣μ∣) and the period tends to 2π/ω(0). The exact circle and exactly constant angular frequency of the special model a=−1, b=0, ω(μ)=ω are conveniences of that model. In the original coordinates the small periodic orbit need not be a circle. Radial scaling can normalize a nonzero a to −1 or +1, so ∣μ∣​ is a normalized leading amplitude, not a universal physical radius.
