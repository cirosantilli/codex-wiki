Solid angle of a circular cone (Ω=2π(1−cosθ))

 ... Mathematics Area of mathematics Geometry and topology Euclidean geometry Sphere Solid angle
OurBigBook.com Words: 218
A right circular cone with vertex O selects all directions within a half-opening angle θ of its axis. Here θ is measured from the axis to a boundary direction, so the full opening in a meridian plane is 2θ. For 0≤θ≤π, interpret the selected directions as a spherical cap; for θ>π/2, this is a region larger than a hemisphere.
The spherical coordinate system gives the area element dA=R2sinαdαdϕ on the sphere. Dividing by R2 and integrating over the cap yields
dΩ=sinαdαdϕ,Ω=∫02π​∫0θ​sinαdαdϕ=2π(1−cosθ).
(19)
The factor sinα matters: a thin angular strip near a pole contains fewer directions than an equally wide strip near the equator. Treating equal intervals of both spherical coordinates as equal solid angles would overcount the polar directions.
At θ=45∘, the solid angle is π(2−2​)≈1.8403 steradians, or about 14.64% of all directions. At θ=90∘ it is 2π steradians, and at 180∘ it is 4π steradians. For a small θ measured in radians, the Taylor series cosθ=1−θ2/2+O(θ4) gives the small-angle approximation Ω≈πθ2. Doubling a narrow half-opening angle therefore approximately quadruples its coverage.
