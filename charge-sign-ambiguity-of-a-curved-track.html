Charge-sign ambiguity of a curved track

 ... Relativistic electromagnetism Electromagnetic four-potential Electromagnetic field tensor Lorentz force Magnetic rigidity Anderson positron track experiment
OurBigBook.com Words: 287
A photograph records a path but supplies no arrow of time. The Lorentz force depends on qv×B: reversing both electric charge and direction of travel leaves the spatial bending compatible with the same path. Thus a particular arc can represent a Positron travelling one way or an electron travelling the other. Knowing the magnetic field's polarity is necessary but insufficient. Gravity does not justify assuming that every cosmic ray track travels downward.
The lead plate adds a physical ordering. For a single particle crossing a passive absorber without an accelerating interaction, the outgoing momentum is smaller, so the outgoing gyroradius is smaller in the same magnetic field. The less curved segment comes first. Once this direction is assigned, the cross product in the Lorentz force determines the sign of electric charge. For the convention below, B=Bz points out of the page; at upward velocity v=vy​, v×B=vBx. Bending to the right therefore means positive electric charge.
Magnetic forces do no work: the change of kinetic energy occurs through interaction with the material, not through the bending magnetic field. The ideal construction suppresses multiple scattering, kinetic energy fluctuations, and loss of kinetic energy in the gas. In an actual analysis one compares arcs away from the plate and allows a change of tangent from multiple scattering; one cannot interpret every kink as a change of electric charge. Short visible arcs, uncertain projection and small bending also limit a determination of magnetic rigidity.
