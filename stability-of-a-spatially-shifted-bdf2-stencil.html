the exact von Neumann stability analysis range at fixed μ>0 is μ≥3. The Fourier symbol gives A(θ)ξ2−4ξ+1=0, A=3e2iθ+8μsin2(θ/2). The root near one obeys ∣ξ∣2=1+(6−2μ)θ2+O(θ4), proving instability for μ<3.
For μ≥3, ReA−3=6(cosθ−1)2+4(μ−3)(1−cosθ)≥0. Writing the modal recurrence as 3vn+2−4vn+1+vn=−(A−3)vn+2, the BDF2 discrete energy identity proves nonincrease of ∣vn+1∣2+∣2vn+1−vn∣2. Summing modes proves stability uniformly even when μ varies within [3,∞).
This shifted scheme is not a consistent standard heat discretization under k=μh2: dividing its extra spatial-shift defect by 2k/3 produces 3hux​/k+O(h2/k). On a finite interval with Dirichlet boundary conditions it also requires an extra boundary closure. Thus the periodic stability theorem is not a theorem of convergence to the heat equation or of stability for an unspecified boundary treatment.
