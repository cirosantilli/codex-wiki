Mitosis

 Home Biology Biological cell Cell cycle M phase
OurBigBook.com Words: 970 Articles: 12
Mitosis allocates copied chromosomes to daughter cell nuclei. The spindle apparatus aligns sister chromatids before separation in anaphase.
The copying has already occurred during S phase, before mitosis. Nuclear chromosome segregation and cytokinesis, the partition of the cytoplasm, are distinct processes: two daughter cell nuclei can form inside one undivided biological cell.[ref]
The original cartoon follows a hypothetical diploid animal biological cell with 2n=4. Its two homologous chromosome pairs are A,a and B,b; upper- and lower-case letters identify homologues, not dominant and recessive alleles. In prophase and metaphase, each replicated chromosome contains two sister chromatids: four replicated chromosomes contain eight chromatids, rather than eight replicated chromosomes. The X shape depicts two associated copies, not the shape of every chromosome throughout the cell cycle.
This open mitosis cartoon shows nuclear envelope breakdown and reassembly. Closed mitosis in some yeasts retains the nuclear envelope while daughter chromosomes separate inside it; the cartoon is therefore not a universal cellular architecture.[ref]
