polysulfur has a chain of S–S sigma bonds with filled lone-pair orbitals. Its occupied states do not supply a partly filled delocalized band, so the chain is an electrical insulator.
In polythiazyl, an approximately planar S–N chain permits overlap of orbitals perpendicular to the backbone. In an elementary pi bond electron picture, nitrogen supplies one electron and sulfur two to this manifold: three pi electrons per SN unit, rather than a completely filled, isolated two-centre bonding pair. The extended states are therefore not all fully occupied. More realistic descriptions include the crystallographic repeat, interchain interactions and overlapping bands crossing the Fermi level; the odd electron count of a chosen chemical repeat is suggestive, not by itself a proof of metallicity.
The essential distinction is occupied, localized bonding/lone-pair states in sulfur chains versus available extended states at the Fermi level in crystalline sulfur nitride. The measured high conductivity along the polymer fibres and its metallic temperature dependence provide the experimental basis, alongside the structural evidence for the chain. Conductivity depends on order and defects as well as chemical composition.[ref]
