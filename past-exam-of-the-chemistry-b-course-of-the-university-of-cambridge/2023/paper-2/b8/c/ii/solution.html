The terminal nitrosyls are linear in both proposed electron-precise structures; the formal M–M bond orders are V–V double and Fe–Fe single. Use three-electron terminal linear NO donors, three-electron μ2​-NO bridges, and five-electron neutral Cp donors in covalent counting.
For the vanadium dimer the two metal fragments together contain
Nfrag​=2(5)V​+2(5)Cp​+2(3)terminal NO​+2(3)μNO​=32.
(13)
A metal–metal bond of order b adds b to each local count, so 32+2b=36 gives b=2 and 18 electrons per V. For an ionic cross-check, Cp is −1, each linear terminal NO is conventionally NO+, and each bridge is represented as NO−. Thus
2x−2+2−2=0,xV​=+1,d4.​
(14)
Each V then has 4+6Cp​+2terminal NO​+4two bridges​+2V=V​=18 electrons.
The iron species is monoanionic, and L− is a pyrazolate bridge binding through its two nitrogens. This is a four-electron donor overall in ionic counting, or three overall in neutral counting. The carbonyl bridge supplies two electrons overall. The covalent fragment total is consequently
Nfrag​=2(8)Fe​+4(3)terminal NO​+2μCO​+3μL​+1anion​=34.
(15)
Therefore 34+2b=36 gives b=1. In the formal NO+ description,
2x+4−1=−1,xFe​=−2,d10.​
(16)
Each Fe receives 10+4two NO​+2pyrazolate N​+1μCO​+1Fe−Fe​=18 electrons. The negative formal oxidation state follows from the chosen nitrosyl assignment and the overall charge; omitting that charge would change the count.
Bent terminal NO ligands would supply fewer neutral-count electrons and demand much higher formal M–M bond orders to attain 18-electron counts. The linear assignments give the ordinary double and single bond solutions. Since NO is a non-innocent ligand, these oxidation states are formal bookkeeping limits, not measured metal charges; actual nitrosyl bending and M–M bond strengths require structural and spectroscopic evidence.[ref]
