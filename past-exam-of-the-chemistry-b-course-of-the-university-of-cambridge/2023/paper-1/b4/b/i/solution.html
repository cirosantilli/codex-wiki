The lactol is a cyclic hemiacetal and can open to HOCH2​CH2​−CH(OBn)−CHO, retaining its pre-existing alkoxy-bearing stereocentre. Its two anomers lose their anomeric configuration on opening to the planar aldehyde, so both can feed the same addition pathway. A first equivalent of decylmagnesium bromide can be consumed by the free OH, producing decane and a magnesium alkoxide; the excess reagent supplies the carbon nucleophile for addition.
magnesium can coordinate both the aldehyde oxygen and the adjacent benzyloxy oxygen. This produces a five-membered chelate, with ring sequence Mg–O(carbonyl)–C(aldehyde)–C(alpha)–O(benzyloxy), that restricts rotation about the aldehyde–alpha-carbon bond. Decyl attack on the less crowded face, away from the alpha-carbon's hydroxyethyl chain in the chelated arrangement, forms the new C–C bond and a magnesium alkoxide at the former aldehyde carbon. Acidic workup protonates both alkoxides to give G. The OBn bond is retained; it is not displaced by the Grignard reagent.
The product configuration is the one with OBn and the newly formed OH both projecting forward in the specified zigzag chain, while the opposite configuration at the new alcohol carbon is disfavoured. This is the Cram chelation model for an alpha-alkoxy aldehyde, rather than stereochemical memory of the starting lactol OH. Coordination is a mechanistic explanation consistent with MgBr2 and the product; the scheme does not quantify individual organomagnesium aggregates.[ref] The relevant primary synthetic sequence is documented independently.[ref]
