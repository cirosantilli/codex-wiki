Past exam of the chemistry B course of the University of Cambridge / 2023 / Paper 1 / B4 / b / ii / Solution

 ... Past exam of the chemistry B course of the University of Cambridge 2023 Paper 1 B4 b ii
OurBigBook.com Words: 205
Write the reacting terminus as R−CH2​−CH=CH2​, where R includes the pre-existing stereocentres and the intact OCH2​C(=O)NMe2​ substituent. ozone undergoes 1,3-dipolar cycloaddition across the terminal alkene to give a molozonide. In a resonance description of ozone as O−−O+=O, the alkene pi pair bonds to one terminal oxygen while the other terminal oxygen forms the second C–O bond and the ozone pi bond shifts onto its central oxygen. Cleavage of this unstable primary ozonide produces a carbonyl compound and a carbonyl oxide, which recombine to form the secondary ozonide. Reductive workup breaks the peroxide bonds and gives the carbonyl fragments R−CH2​CHO and formaldehyde.
Excess sodium borohydride also reduces the newly formed aldehyde: hydride attacks its carbonyl carbon, shifting the pi electrons onto oxygen, and the final acidic workup protonates the alkoxide. Thus the retained chain becomes R−CH2​CH2​OH. The terminal alkene carbon becomes formaldehyde and can be reduced to methanol; it is no longer part of the main product. This accounts for the one-carbon shortening of the retained skeleton.
The original amide carbonyl is present in both H and I and is not reduced under the ordinary borohydride workup. The existing stereocenters and benzyl ether are retained. Stopping the mechanism at an aldehyde, or deleting the amide C=O when reading the smaller printed scheme, would give the wrong product. The new terminal alcohol carbon is CH2 and therefore adds no stereocenter.
