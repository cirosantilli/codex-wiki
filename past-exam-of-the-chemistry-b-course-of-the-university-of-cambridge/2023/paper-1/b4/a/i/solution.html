B and C are the two geometrical ketoxime isomers, formed by replacing the reactive ring C=O with C=N–OH. The separate spiroacetal remains intact. Define K as the original ketone carbon, Ra​ as the adjacent ring path towards the aromatic ring, and Rs​ as the adjacent path towards the spiroacetal-bearing junction. These are the two carbon substituents directly bonded to K, not detachable independent fragments.
In B, N–OH is anti to the K−Ra​ bond across C=N; in C, N–OH is anti to K−Rs​. Those geometries predict exactly the respective nitrogen insertions in D and E. Both products have formula C18​H21​NO5​: condensation adds NH2OH to C18​H20​O5​ and removes H2O. An alternative ring regioisomer or reaction at the protected ketone would not represent the pair of oxime geometrical isomers.
