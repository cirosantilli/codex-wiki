Both permitted alternatives follow the same condensation–activation–migration sequence. sodium acetate liberates a useful concentration of hydroxylamine from its hydrochloride while providing a buffered medium. nitrogen of NH2OH attacks the ketone carbonyl; proton transfer gives a carbinolamine. protonation of its carbon-derived OH and loss of water, with nitrogen lone-pair donation, then gives the oxime after deprotonation. The two C=N geometries can be separated.
For the B-to-D route, oxime O-tosylation begins when oxime oxygen attacks sulfur of tosyl chloride and chloride leaves; triethylamine accepts the released proton. The resulting O-tosyl oxime has an excellent leaving group. The K−Ra​ bond, anti to N–OTs, migrates to nitrogen while N–O cleaves, giving a ring-expanded nitrilium ion. Its local connectivity is Rs​−K≡N+−Ra​, with the existing scaffold still joining the two paths. water attacks the nitrilium carbon K. proton transfers and imidic-acid/amide tautomerization produce D, with nitrogen inserted on the aromatic side of the old carbonyl.
For the C-to-E route, the same O-tosylation activates the other oxime geometry. Now the anti bond is K−Rs​; its migration gives Ra​−K≡N+−Rs​, and aqueous trapping produces E, with nitrogen inserted on the spiro-junction side. Each Beckmann rearrangement expands the ketone's six-membered ring to a seven-membered lactam. The original carbonyl carbon remains the amide carbonyl carbon; nitrogen is the inserted atom. In this mechanism the ketone oxygen is lost during oxime condensation, the oxime oxygen leaves with the sulfonate group, and water supplies the new amide oxygen.
