The first reagent supplies a vinyl nucleophile, CH2​=CH−, rather than an allyl group. Two successive conjugate additions install vinyl at C2 and a 2-formylethyl chain at C1. Number the original nitro-bearing alkene carbon C1. Vinylmagnesium bromide attacks the beta carbon C2 of the nitroalkene, shifting the original C1=C2 electrons into a magnesium nitronate. The new C2–vinyl bond remains intact during the second stage; its terminal double bond is not the electrophilic site in that stage.
The carbon end of the nitronate at C1 then attacks the terminal beta carbon of acrolein. Move the acrolein C=C electrons towards its carbonyl and the C=O electrons onto oxygen. This forms the C1–CH2CH2CHO connectivity through an aldehyde enolate; protonation by methanol and tautomerization restore the aldehyde. The nitronate returns to a neutral nitro group as it makes the second C–C bond. Thus the chain is attached through the carbon formerly beta to the acrolein carbonyl, rather than by a direct addition to its aldehyde carbon.
After the first addition, C2 bears the vinyl group while the nitronate carbon C1 remains approximately planar. Approach of the second electrophile to the less crowded face, opposite the adjacent vinyl substituent, gives the new formylethyl chain trans to vinyl. Consequently nitro and vinyl are cis, as depicted: at C1 the chain and nitro group occupy opposite faces. This is a substrate-controlled diastereoselective tandem Michael addition. The reagents and starting material contain no chiral source, so the depicted product represents a racemic pair rather than an enantioselective synthesis. Steric facial selection explains the relative configuration without requiring a uniquely established magnesium aggregate or transition-state conformation.
