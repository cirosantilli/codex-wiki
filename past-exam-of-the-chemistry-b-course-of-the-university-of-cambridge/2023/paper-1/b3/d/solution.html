With triethylamine present, proton transfer from thiophenol produces a more nucleophilic thiolate population. sulfur attacks the anomeric epoxide carbon C1 from the face opposite its epoxide C–O bond. Displacement by backside attack opens the epoxide, breaks C1–O, and leaves the former epoxide oxygen attached to C2. protonation of that alkoxide by thiophenol or triethylammonium gives G: SPh at C1 on the front face and OH at C2 on the back face, a trans relationship. This is inversion at C1 with retention of the C2 C–O bond; there is no need to invert every stereocentre. Rapid protonation also suppresses competing silyl transfer by the alkoxide.
Without added base, neutral thiophenol is less nucleophilic. acid-assisted cleavage at the anomeric epoxide bond can instead be accompanied by donation from the ring oxygen, giving an oxocarbenium ion with charge shared with the ring oxygen. thiophenol capture from the face that puts SPh back on the original epoxide face, followed by deprotonation, gives H with SPh and C2–OH both on the back face, the net-retention pathway. An ion-pair or highly asynchronous opening can provide this outcome; the drawing does not establish a long-lived free carbocation.
The distinction is configuration, not a mandatory fixed chair. For the closely matching trisilylated substrate, the primary study obtained both anomers with thiophenol alone, and established an unusual inverted chair for the alpha thioglycoside by NMR and crystallography. Thus H should be explained as an accessible product, not asserted to be the sole product or necessarily to have axial SPh in its preferred conformation.[ref]
