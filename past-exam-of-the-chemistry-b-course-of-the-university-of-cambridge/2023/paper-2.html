i OurBigBook.com
ammonia and cyclohexylamine are sigma-donor ligands; chloride and acetate are sigma- and pi-donor ligands. The nitrogen lone pair of each amine points toward the metal and forms the donor interaction. These saturated amines have no low-lying conjugated acceptor system that would make them conventional pi-acceptor ligands.
chloride uses a lone pair for sigma donation and has additional occupied p orbitals perpendicular to the bond that can donate with pi symmetry. An oxygen-bound acetate similarly has occupied oxygen-centred orbitals available for sigma and pi donation, although its carboxylate resonance in chemistry changes their energies and distribution. Neither should be classified as a pi acceptor merely because its bonding can be drawn with multiple bonds or resonance in chemistry contributors. None of these four is a standard sigma-donor/pi-acceptor ligand.

The average Pt oxidation state is +3, since six bromides balance the two metal atoms. A natural mixed valence assignment is alternating Pt(II), d8, and Pt(IV), d6, rather than two equivalent Pt(III) sites. Use neutral square-planar PtII(NH3​)2​Br2​ units and octahedral PtIV(NH3​)2​Br4​ units. At every site place two ammonia and two bromide ligands in an equatorial plane; the Pt(IV) sites have two additional axial bromides pointing toward the adjacent Pt(II) sites.
Along the chain the connectivity is
⋯PtII ⋯Br−PtIV−Br⋯ PtII ⋯Br−PtIV−Br⋯.
(6)
Every Pt–Br–Pt bridge is asymmetric: short to Pt(IV), long to Pt(II). The latter contact is a secondary axial interaction, consistent with the preference of Pt(II) for a square-planar primary coordination sphere. The Pt(IV) site is smaller and more strongly attracts the bridging bromides. A drawing with all axial distances identical would obscure the distinct local environments.
There are four equatorial bromides and two axial bridging bromides per two-Pt repeat, giving precisely Pt2​(NH3​)4​Br6​. A direct Pt–Pt bond is not required. This is a proposed structure consistent with the stated composition and two environments; the formula alone does not determine every equatorial cis/trans arrangement or numerical bond length.

The more radially extended Pt 5d orbitals give strong metal–ligand overlap and covalent bonding. B is oxidative addition; D is alkyne insertion into Ni–aryl followed by carbonyl-oxygen coordination; E is carbonyl insertion into Ni–alkenyl. The distinction is visible from the bonds made and from consistent electron counting.
For B the depicted Ni(0) starting complex has two phosphine and two MeCN donors, giving 10+4+4=18 electrons. Addition across the aryl C–Br bond produces two X-type ligands and Ni(II). The drawn product has lost the two solvent donors, giving 8+4+2+2=16 electrons. Intrinsic oxidative addition to the unsolvated 14-electron Ni0(dppe) fragment raises its count to 16; the overall drawn 18→16 change also includes loss of four solvent-donor electrons. A falling displayed count does not turn this oxidative addition into a reductive elimination.
Before D, the aryl–alkyne intermediate is Ni(I), d9, with 9+4P,P​+2aryl​+2alkyne​=17 electrons. Syn migration couples aryl carbon to one alkyne carbon and leaves a Ni–alkenyl bond. Insertion alone removes the separate two-electron alkyne donor and would give a 15-electron alkenyl fragment. The ester carbonyl oxygen then binds, as drawn, restoring the count to 9+4+2alkenyl​+2O​=17. Ni remains +1.
In E the Ni-bound alkenyl carbon attacks the coordinated ester carbonyl carbon, forming the new ring-closing C–C bond. The C=O pi electrons move to oxygen, and an Ni–O alkoxide bond replaces Ni–C. This is carbonyl migratory insertion, equivalently intramolecular addition to a coordinated carbonyl. A neutral O donor plus an alkenyl X ligand become one alkoxide X ligand, so the drawn count falls 17→15, while Ni remains +1. A solvating molecule could fill the resulting vacancy, but it is not shown and should not be silently added to the stated count.

i OurBigBook.com
The Dewar-Chatt-Duncanson model has two complementary interactions. The filled alkene π orbital donates to an empty metal orbital, and an occupied metal dπ​ orbital back-donates into the empty alkene π∗ orbital. The metal acceptor has the symmetry of the sum of the two ligand lobes facing it; the back-donor has opposite phases at the two carbon positions and matches the antisymmetric π∗ combination.
π(C=C)⟶M,M(dπ​)⟶π∗(C=C).
(11)
Both channels strengthen metal–alkene attachment. Donation removes some electron density from the internal bonding orbital, while back-donation populates its antibonding partner, so coordination generally reduces C–C bond order. The schematic shows orbital phase signs, not positive and negative electric charges.

The metal interacts with the extra in-plane bonding system of benzyne, not by replacing the ordinary out-of-plane aromatic pi system. Take the ring and metal to lie in the xz plane, with the metal-to-C–C-midpoint direction along z and C1–C2 along x. The carbon orbitals directed toward the metal form an occupied symmetric combination and an empty antisymmetric combination.
The occupied in-plane π combination donates into an empty metal orbital of matching symmetry. An occupied metal dxz​-type orbital overlaps with the in-plane π∗ combination, providing back-donation. These are the same symmetry principles as alkene binding, rotated into the plane of the ring. The carbon py​ orbitals perpendicular to the ring continue to participate in its aromatic system. The strained formal triple bond therefore does not supply two independent, ordinary out-of-plane alkene interactions.
Strong population of the in-plane acceptor can be described as reduction of the aryne to a dianionic, metallacyclopropene-like ligand. That description emphasizes two M–C sigma bonds and loss of the extra C–C pi bond. It is particularly useful for early-metal aryne complexes. xZr​−2−2=0,xTa​−1−2−2=0.

A neutral, simple μ2​-NO contributes three electrons to the two-metal unit overall. In neutral bookkeeping it is an LX-type bridge: a two-electron donor component plus the odd electron of neutral NO. i OurBigBook.com
The unequal P–N distances reflect unequal local bonding interactions in the alternating-chain conformation, rather than an extended conducting P=N pi system. nitrogen is much more electronegative than phosphorus, so the backbone sigma bonds are strongly polar. nitrogen lone-pair density can also donate into appropriately oriented antibonding P–substituent orbitals: negative hyperconjugation, schematically
n(N)⟶σ∗(P−R).
(17)
The strengths of these interactions depend on the orientation of the donor and acceptor orbitals. In a planar cis–trans backbone, the P–N links are not all geometrically equivalent. Different overlap and charge redistribution give alternating greater and smaller effective P–N bond orders, hence shorter and longer bonds. A formal alternating P=N/P–N drawing is a useful shorthand for this inequality, but does not imply that isolated textbook single and double bonds are present with no electronic communication.
The historical island picture captures the limitation of pi interaction to local P–N–P regions rather than a continuous band along the whole chain. A modern description need not invoke strong phosphorus 3d–nitrogen 2p bonding; polar bonding and donation into sigma-star orbitals provide the useful explanation. Calculations on oligomers found bond alternation for planar cis–trans conformations but not for optimized helices. Bond alternation is therefore conformation dependent, not a universal consequence of the repeat formula alone.[ref]

polysulfur has a chain of S–S sigma bonds with filled lone-pair orbitals. Its occupied states do not supply a partly filled delocalized band, so the chain is an electrical insulator.
In polythiazyl, an approximately planar S–N chain permits overlap of orbitals perpendicular to the backbone. The target is poly[bis(ethoxy)phosphazene]. The final step is nucleophilic substitution with an ethoxide salt in a compatible dry medium, replacing two chlorides per phosphorus. Polymerize the chlorinated ring first, then substitute the linear polymer: complete ethoxylation of the isolated ring does not by itself produce the requested high polymer. The primary macromolecular-substitution work explicitly includes the ethoxy derivative.[ref]

The chemical requirement for permanence is retention of the backbone and network in a wet environment. A suitably prepared polysiloxane has strong Si–O bonding, relatively slow backbone cleavage under mild aqueous conditions, and flexible chains that can be retained in a cross-linked elastic network. In the comparison intended here, hydrolysis of susceptible phosphazene substituents and P–N linkages leads to chain scission, reduced molar mass and loss of mechanical integrity, which conflicts with permanence.
This is a comparison of chemical durability, not a universal statement that every polyphosphazene degrades rapidly or that every polysiloxane is indefinitely inert. phosphazene side groups can strongly change access of water and hydrolysis rates; siloxanes can also be cleaved under sufficiently acidic or basic conditions. Those structural and kinetic qualifications matter even before any biological suitability is considered.
