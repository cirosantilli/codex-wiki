The beta-carbon bond-forming transition state retains the same basic connectivity across this pH range, but the predominant proton-transfer route and enzyme protonation change. Near the acidic end, protonated glutamate can act as a general acid, donating a proton or a strong hydrogen bond to carbonyl oxygen and making C3 more electrophilic; it is less available as the carboxylate general base that activates water. Above its local pKa​, carboxylate-mediated water activation is favored. Acid catalysis therefore shifts positive-charge stabilization toward a protonated carbonyl, whereas base-assisted attack reduces positive-charge buildup on incoming water. Neither a unique transition-state geometry nor a numerical pH optimum follows from pH alone.
Using a free-solution glutamate side-chain pKa​≃4.2 only as an illustration, the deprotonated fraction is [1+10pKa​−pH]−1: about 0.059 at pH 3 and 0.998 at pH 7. An enzyme environment can shift that pKa​ substantially. The glutamine amide and the hydrocarbon side chains remain in their ordinary forms from pH 3 to 10; arginine guanidinium, with a typical free side-chain pKa​ around 12.5, remains predominantly positive even at pH 10. Thus the major side-chain switch among those listed is glutamate. At the alkaline end, bulk hydroxide can also contribute to nonenzymatic conjugate addition. A measured pH-rate profile must control substrate stability and enzyme folding before assigning every loss of activity to an active-site titration.
