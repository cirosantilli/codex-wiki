OurBigBook.com Words: 201
B=N3​P3​(NHCy)6​, with two cyclohexylamino substituents on each original phosphorus. All six P–Cl bonds have been replaced; the six N–H bonds of those substituents remain. Each equivalent of triethylamine traps one equivalent of HCl.
C contains three four-membered PV−N(Cy)−PIII(Cl)−N(Cy) spiro rings, one at each original ring phosphorus. Each PCl3​ joins the two geminal NHCy groups: replacement of two of its chlorides forms two new P–N bonds and releases two HCl, consuming both N–H protons. The new phosphorus retains one chloride and a lone pair, making it three-coordinate P(III); the original six-membered P3​N3​ ring retains P(V). The product formula is N3​P3​{(NCy)2​PCl}3​. For each spiro ring, the original phosphorus is bonded to both exocyclic nitrogens, and each of those nitrogens is bonded to the same new PCl center; the braces do not indicate a simple linear substituent.
The three new P–Cl directions permit an all-cis isomer and a cis,trans isomer. Relative to the central ring, write these as (+,+,+) and (+,+,−), with cyclic relabeling giving equivalent choices. Reversing all signs does not create a third relative configuration. This describes the configuration pattern; puckering and substituent conformations are additional structural details.
