Past exam of the chemistry B course of the University of Cambridge / 2025 / Paper 2 / B9 / d / Solution

 ... Chemistry B course of the University of Cambridge Past exam of the chemistry B course of the University of Cambridge 2025 Paper 2 B9 d
OurBigBook.com Words: 343
Replace the two chlorides on one ring phosphorus P1​ with the two ends of the diiron unit. D has a spiro P1​Fe2​ triangle, with an Fe–Fe bond, a P1​–Fe bond to each metal and four terminal CO ligands on each Fe. The other two phosphorus atoms retain their PCl2​ groups. Its composition is N3​P3​Cl4​Fe2​(CO)8​. In neutral electron counting, each metal has 8Fe​+84CO​+1Fe−P​+1Fe−Fe​=18.
E has a triangular Fe3​ cluster, one bridging CO, and an adjacent ring N→Fe bond. The two original metals remain bonded to P1​ and to one another, and each bonds to the added third Fe. Each Fe bears three terminal CO ligands; one additional bridging carbonyl ligand spans the original Fe–Fe edge. A nitrogen directly adjacent to P1​ donates its lone pair to the third Fe. The formula is therefore N3​P3​Cl4​Fe3​(CO)10​, satisfying 8+5−3=10 retained CO ligands. The original diagram above gives all metal–metal and metal–ring connections.
The counts are an especially useful structural check. Each original Fe has 8+63CO​+1μCO​+1P​+22Fe−Fe​=18. The added Fe has 8+63CO​+2N​+22Fe−Fe​=18. Omitting nitrogen coordination leaves the third Fe two electrons short; omitting the bridging CO leaves both original metals one electron short.
In D, the two untouched phosphorus sites are equivalent: an A2​X phosphorus-31 nuclear magnetic resonance system gives their doublet with integral 2, and the unique P1​ triplet with integral 1. The two equivalent nuclei do not split each other. In E, coordination of one adjacent nitrogen distinguishes the two sides of the ring, producing three inequivalent phosphorus sites. Each couples to the other two and gives a doublet of doublets in the first-order limit; equal couplings or strong coupling could distort this simple pattern.
The high-frequency infrared spectroscopy bands belong to terminal carbonyl stretching modes. The extra low-frequency band is diagnostic of a strongly backbonded bridging carbonyl ligand. The number of IR bands counts allowed, possibly overlapping normal modes, not individual CO ligands. Formula, 18-electron rule, NMR equivalence and IR together select this model; the historical experimental work independently established diiron spiro rings and triiron products containing both P–Fe and N–Fe bonds.[ref]
