P is H2, the acetal methine between two oxygens; its only resolved coupling is to the attached methyl, giving the quartet. S is that C2 methyl, whose doublet has the same 6.9 Hz coupling. T is tert-butyl, integrating to nine equivalent hydrogens with no adjacent carbon-bound methine proton across its quaternary attachment.
U is equatorial H5, coupled to the four hydrogens at C4 and C6. The two equivalent equatorial methylene hydrogens are Q, each a doublet of doublets with a 12.3 Hz geminal coupling to its axial partner and a 1.3 Hz coupling to H5. The two equivalent axial methylene hydrogens are R, each with the same 12.3 Hz geminal coupling and a 3.7 Hz vicinal coupling to H5. Equivalence between C4 and C6 makes two pairs rather than four separately shifted methylene hydrogens.

Coupling U to two equivalent R hydrogens and two equivalent Q hydrogens produces its triplet of triplets. The different small vicinal couplings are consistent with oblique H–C–C–H torsions for equatorial H5; the 12.3 Hz value is geminal and must not be mistaken for a large vicinal trans-diaxial coupling.
