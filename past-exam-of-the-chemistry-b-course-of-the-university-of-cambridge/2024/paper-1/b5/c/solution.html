View the backbone along N–Cα​ to display ϕ=∠(Ci−1′​,Ni​,Cαi​,Ci′​) in a Newman projection. The front amide nitrogen carries the preceding carbonyl and H, while the back Cα​ carries the next carbonyl, H and side chain. Rotation changes the contacts among these groups. A corresponding view along Cα​–C′ displays ψ. The peptide C′–N bond itself is approximately planar and is not the freely rotating bond in either definition.
glycine replaces the side chain by a second hydrogen. Removing Cβ​ removes many contacts that exclude backbone arrangements for other residues, so its allowed ϕ,ψ region is much broader and includes substantial positive-ϕ space. Its alpha carbon is also achiral, so the steric map is approximately mirror-symmetric between positive and negative torsions in the simple isolated-residue model.
proline closes a five-membered ring from Cα​ through three methylenes back to nitrogen. In the N–Cα​ projection, the nitrogen's side-chain arm and Cα​'s Cβ​ arm are tied together, preventing unrestricted relative rotation. Its ϕ is consequently confined near the usual negative-60∘ region rather than exploring the broad range of an acyclic amino acid. proline also lacks a backbone N–H donor when incorporated into an ordinary internal peptide. Ring closure restricts ϕ strongly, but does not freeze every backbone or ring degree of freedom. These differences are naturally represented by distinct Ramachandran plots; the original torsion/steric analysis established the basis for such comparisons.[ref]
