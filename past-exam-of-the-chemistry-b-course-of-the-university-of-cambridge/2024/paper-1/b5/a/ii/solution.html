OurBigBook.com Words: 205
4-Fluorophenol probes acceptor strength because it donates its OH hydrogen. The qualitative order is C > D > B > A: the electron-rich lactam oxygen is the strongest acceptor here, the secondary amine is next, pyridine nitrogen is intermediate, and aldehyde oxygen is weakest. Resonance donation from amide nitrogen increases the lactam oxygen's basicity relative to an ordinary aldehyde or ketone oxygen. The N-methylpyrrole site in A does not add an independent amine-like acceptor.
N-Methylpyrrolidin-2-one is a carbonyl acceptor without N–H, so it instead probes the solute's donor ability. B > C ≫ A and D: B's phenolic OH is a strong donor, the lactam N–H of C is appreciable, A lacks a conventional donor, and D's neutral secondary-amine N–H is weak. The amine can be a good acceptor and a poor donor at the same time; the two properties are not opposites on one scale.
These are association equilibria in cyclohexane, where solvent competition for hydrogen bonds is weak. The quoted constants should be compared within each probe series, not treated as solvent-independent measures. At 298 K, as an illustrative common temperature, a tenfold increase in Ka​ corresponds to a binding free-energy advantage RTln10≃5.7 kJmol−1. This does not imply the experiment's temperature was specified, nor that every difference is the energy of one isolated hydrogen bond.
