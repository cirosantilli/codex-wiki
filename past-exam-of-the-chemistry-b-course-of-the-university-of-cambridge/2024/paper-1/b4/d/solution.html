 ... Chemistry B course of the University of Cambridge Past exam of the chemistry B course of the University of Cambridge 2024 Paper 1 B4 d
OurBigBook.com Words: 162
The free propargylic OH first reacts with an aluminium hydride, releasing hydrogen and forming an O–Al bond. Coordination of the nearby alkyne to this aluminium center organizes a five-membered O–C–C–C–Al unit. Hydride addition from the opposite side of the activated alkyne gives a cyclic alkenyl aluminate: aluminium bonds to the distal alkyne carbon, while hydride is delivered to the carbon nearer CH2​O.
The key step is directed trans hydroalumination. The new C–H and C–Al bonds are anti. Aqueous workup protonolyzes C–Al with retention of its alkene geometry and hydrolyzes the O–Al attachment. Replacing that C–Al bond by C–H therefore produces the E allylic alcohol, with CH2​OH and the rest of the chain on opposite alkene sides. A generic syn hydroalumination drawing followed by simple protonation would predict the wrong geometry.
The free OH provides the directing tether; the silyl-protected oxygen is not equivalently available. Primary electrophile-trapping studies of Red-Al-treated propargylic alcohols support the alkenyl-alanate description.[ref]
