A ring-retaining bromination replaces the three hydridic B–H positions while preserving N–H. Its net stoichiometry is
B3​H3​N3​H3​+3Br2​⟶B3​Br3​N3​H3​+3HBr.​
(12)
Use controlled bromination and conditions allowing elimination of hydrogen bromide from addition intermediates, rather than identifying the first bromine adduct as the final ring product. Boron-bound hydrogen has hydride-like character because boron is less electronegative than hydrogen; its replacement is consistent with electrophilic attack by bromine. The primary preparative work reports B-tribromoborazines obtained by bromination of borazine and its N-methyl analogue.[ref]
The product must retain the unsaturated B3​N3​ ring. Merely adding three HBr molecules to borazine initially gives a saturated hydrogen-halide addition product, not the required formula. Addition followed by suitable elimination is distinct from simply asserting direct B–H protonolysis by HBr.
