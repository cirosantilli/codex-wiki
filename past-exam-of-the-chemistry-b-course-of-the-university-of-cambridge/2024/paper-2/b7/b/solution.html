For comparable octahedral environments with the same donor set, the expected qualitative order is
Mn2+<Fe2+<Fe3+<Mo3+<Rh3+<Pt4+.​
(6)
Across comparable first-row ions, the increasing effective attraction and changing metal–ligand distances tend to increase octahedral splitting, giving Mn(II) below Fe(II). Raising the charge on the same metal gives Fe(III) above Fe(II), because the ligand interaction generally strengthens and metal–ligand distances contract. Moving from 3d to more radially extended 4d orbitals gives larger metal–ligand overlap, placing Mo(III) above Fe(III); Rh(III) lies further along the same 4d series and typically gives the larger splitting. The 5d ion Pt(IV) combines the heavier row with high charge and gives the largest splitting here.
This is a matched-environment trend, not an intrinsic list of constants for bare ions. Actual Δ also depends strongly on donor identity, geometry and distance. It would be meaningless to compare an unspecified weak-field tetrahedral compound with a strong-field octahedral compound solely through this ordering.
