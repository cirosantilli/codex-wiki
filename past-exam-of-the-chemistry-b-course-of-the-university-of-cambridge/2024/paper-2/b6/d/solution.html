For the doubly bridged tetracarbonyl, each iron has one terminal carbonyl ligand, two bridging carbonyl ligands and one cyclopentadienyl ligand. Neutral electron counting gives 8+5+2+2=17 before direct bonding, so a single Fe–Fe bond gives 18 at each iron. For the triply bridged tricarbonyl, each iron instead has 8+5+3=16 before direct bonding; formal completion to 18 requires a double Fe–Fe bond. Equivalently, the total fragment counts before metal–metal bonding are 34 and 32 respectively.

The increased direct bond order explains the substantial contraction. The pentamethylcyclopentadienyl ligands also alter steric and electronic effects, and bridge geometry changes, so this is not a controlled measurement of bond order alone. Nevertheless, the markedly shorter distance in the less carbonyl-saturated dimer is consistent with stronger direct Fe–Fe bonding. The isolated triply bridged compound was characterized structurally and magnetically in the primary work.[ref]
