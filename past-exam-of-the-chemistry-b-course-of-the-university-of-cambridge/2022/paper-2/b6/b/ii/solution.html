A Co(III) centre has d6, and charge balance requires one closed-shell catecholate ligand and one S=1/2 semiquinonate ligand. For low-spin Co(III), t2g6​(eg∗​)0, the metal has S=0, giving an independent-spin magnetic moment of
μeff​/μB​=20+21​(21​+1)​=3​=1.732.
(3)
Thus A1 contains low-spin Co(III), SCo​=0, with its observed moment residing on the free radical ligand. High-spin Co(III), t2g4​(eg∗​)2, would have S=2 and give 27​=5.20 under the stated independent-spin approximation, so it is inconsistent with this measurement.
For Co(II), d7, charge balance instead requires two S=1/2 semiquinonate ligands. High-spin Co(II) has t2g5​(eg∗​)2 and S=3/2, so
μeff​/μB​=223​25​+2(21​23​)​=21​=4.583.
(4)
Therefore A2 contains high-spin Co(II), SCo​=3/2. Low-spin Co(II), t2g6​(eg∗​)1, would give 9​=3.00 when the two ligand spins are included. These four checks distinguish both oxidation state and metal spin.
The larger octahedral splitting at Co(III) exceeds the effective pairing energy and favours a low-spin complex; the smaller octahedral splitting at Co(II) favours a high-spin complex with occupation of both eg∗​ orbitals before further pairing. Interconversion also transfers an electron between cobalt and a dioxolene ligand, so this is valence tautomerism, rather than a metal-only spin change. Squared moments add here because the supplied model treats the centres as independent; adding the three spins first would be a different, exchange-coupled model.
