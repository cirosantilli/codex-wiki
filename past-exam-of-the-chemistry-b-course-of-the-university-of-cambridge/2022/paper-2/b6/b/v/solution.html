The Co(III) centre in A1 has t2g6​(eg∗​)0: every occupied d subset is orbitally balanced. There is no first-order metal-centred Jahn–Teller driving force in A1. This does not require all six experimental bond lengths to be equal, since pyridine and dioxolene are different donors.
The Co(II) centre in A2 has t2g5​(eg∗​)2. Its two eg∗​ orbitals each contain one electron, so that subset gives no first-order preference. The t2g​ hole does give a weaker Jahn–Teller instability. To compare tetragonal distortions, take a positive small splitting parameter δ and retain each subset's orbital energy barycentre. On axial elongation let dxz​,dyz​ shift by −δ and dxy​ by +2δ. Five electrons then give
ΔEelong​=4(−δ)+1(2δ)=−2δ.
(5)
For equal-magnitude compression the shifts reverse, and filling the lower dxy​ orbital first gives
ΔEcompress​=2(−2δ)+3(δ)=−δ.
(6)
The two singly occupied eg∗​ orbitals contribute zero net shift in either case. Thus the elementary tetragonal splitting model favours elongation for high-spin d7, rather than equal electronic stabilization by the two signs. In a strictly sigma-only octahedral molecular orbital model t2g​ is nonbonding, so this weak effect is absent; it arises when the electrostatic and non-sigma interactions neglected in that diagram are restored. spin-orbit coupling, unequal donors and chelate constraints can modify the actual distortion, so the argument does not establish which experimental Co–donor bonds must lengthen.
