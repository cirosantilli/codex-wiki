OP(OMe)3​ also has approximately tetrahedral molecular geometry at phosphorus, now with three P–O–C sigma linkages and a terminal phosphoryl O. Only the latter is being compared with the terminal O of Me3​PO; the three P–O–C bonds are chemically distinct.
The electronegative alkoxy substituents withdraw electron density from P and improve its capacity to accept donation from terminal O into the surrounding antibonding framework. The elementary substituent argument therefore predicts a stronger, stiffer terminal P–O bond in OP(OMe)3​ than in Me3​PO. A description invoking polar bonding and negative hyperconjugation avoids assigning a literal localized d-orbital double bond solely because the formula is written P=O.
Measure the terminal P–O stretching vibration by infrared or Raman spectroscopy under comparable, non-hydrogen-bonding conditions. In an approximate local-mode comparison the reduced mass is the same, so a larger terminal stretching wavenumber indicates a larger force constant, k∝ν2. Mode assignment can be checked by 18O substitution at the terminal oxygen or by a normal-mode calculation; otherwise an alkoxy P–O–C vibration could be compared by mistake. X-ray diffraction can supply a supporting bond-length comparison, but neither bond length nor a single coupled vibrational frequency directly measures an isolated bond-dissociation enthalpy.
