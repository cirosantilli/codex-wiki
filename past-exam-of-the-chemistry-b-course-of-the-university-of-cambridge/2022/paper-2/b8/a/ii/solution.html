Both coordinated frequencies are substantially lower than that of free N2​. In a local harmonic model,
ν=2πc1​μk​​,kfree​kA​​≃(23592021​)2=0.734,kfree​kB​​≃(23592010​)2=0.726.​

Thus coordination lowers the approximate N–N stretching force constant by about 27%, with B marginally more activated than A by this measure. Donation into N2​ pi-antibonding orbitals provides the natural explanation: the M–N2​ bond has an important backbonding component and N–N loses some multiple-bond character.
The same qualitative red shift occurs for CO when metal-to-CO backbonding populates CO π∗ orbitals. N2​ is generally a poorer pi acceptor than CO, so low-valent electron-rich metal fragments are particularly helpful for stabilizing its complexes. A numerical claim that N2​ always shifts more or less than CO would require a matched metal/ligand comparison. Neither the two frequencies nor the local-force approximation establish integer N–N bond orders, complete N–N cleavage, or catalytic nitrogen conversion; they establish significant weakening of intact coordinated N2​.
