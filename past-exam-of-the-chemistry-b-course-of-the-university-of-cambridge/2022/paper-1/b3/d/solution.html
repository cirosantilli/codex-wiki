Past exam of the chemistry B course of the University of Cambridge / 2022 / Paper 1 / B3 / d / Solution

 ... Chemistry B course of the University of Cambridge Past exam of the chemistry B course of the University of Cambridge 2022 Paper 1 B3 d
OurBigBook.com Words: 203
Distinguish the original thiirane sulfur So​ from the sulfur Sn​ supplied by thiocyanate. sulfur attack by −Sn​−C≡N at one thiirane carbon opens its C–So​ bond with inversion. This produces a thiolate at the neighbouring carbon and a C–Sn​–CN group at the attacked carbon. The OH-bearing remote carbon is unaffected.
The original thiolate So−​ now attacks the nitrile carbon intramolecularly. Shift one C≡N pi pair onto nitrogen. The resulting cyclic imidodithiocarbonate has the five-membered sequence ring carbon–So​–C(=N−)–Sn​–adjacent ring carbon; its two carbon atoms are also part of the original six-membered carbocycle, exactly giving the hinted bicyclic intermediate.
Collapse can reform C≡N while breaking the nitrile-carbon–Sn​ bond. This transfers the CN group to So​ and generates Sn−​ as the thiolate on the other ring carbon. That thiolate attacks the remaining C–So​ bond from the back, expelling −So​−C≡N and reclosing the thiirane. Each thiirane carbon has undergone one inversion, so the new sulfur bridge lies on the opposite face; the OH configuration is retained. The thiocyanate sulfur has entered the ring and the original ring sulfur has left as thiocyanate. This atom-labelled exchange mechanism explains how the same elemental formula can conceal sulfur replacement; isotope labelling would be needed to test that exchange experimentally. A single epimerization at the OH carbon would give the wrong stereochemical outcome.
