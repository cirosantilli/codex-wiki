The five-carbon unit is initially an open chain, not a completed carbocycle. Label it P1​−P2​−P3​−P4​−P5​, with phenyl-bearing P1​, carbonyl P3​, amide-substituted P4​, and the two ethylthio groups on P5​. Its double bonds are initially P1​=P2​ and P4​=P5​. Let X be the nitro-bearing carbon of nitroethane; its methyl remains a substituent throughout.
DBU removes an alpha proton from nitroethane to give a nitronate. carbon attack at the Michael acceptor P1​ forms X−P1​, with electron redistribution through P2​−P3​ onto the carbonyl oxygen. proton transfer restores the ketone. Removing the remaining X–H proton gives another carbon-nucleophilic nitronate. It attacks the other conjugated end, P5​, intramolecularly, closing the six-membered circuit P1​−P2​−P3​−P4​−P5​−X. Addition to the ketene dithioacetal and ethylthiolate elimination restore the P4​=P5​ bond, leaving one SEt group at P5​.
base-assisted loss of the proton at P1​, with C–NO2 cleavage at X, creates P1​=X and expels nitrite. Enolization at P2​−P3​ supplies the third aromatic double bond and the phenolic OH. proton transfers to the leaving anions complete the acid–base balance. nitroethane supplies the new ring carbon X and its methyl substituent; its nitro group is eliminated. Aromatization, rather than a separate oxidation of an intact six-membered starting ring, provides the final driving force. This is a proposed stepwise nitroalkane benzannulation; related DBU-promoted tandem Michael addition/cyclization/aromatization has direct primary precedent.[ref]
