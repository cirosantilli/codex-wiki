Label the enamine carbon bonded to nitrogen C1 and its alkene partner C2; C6 is the other carbon next to C1. B is the C-alkylated iminium ion bromide. Its new substituent at C2 is −CH2​−C(CO2​Et)=CH2​, while C1 is double-bonded to the pyrrolidine nitrogen, which bears positive charge. bromide is the counterion, accounting for Br in the empirical formula without requiring a retained C–Br bond.
The C2 end of the enamine attacks the bromomethyl carbon by SN​2 displacement. Simultaneously, donation of the nitrogen lone pair creates the C1=N bond. This is ordinary enamine C-alkylation, not N-alkylation.
proton transfer removes an alpha proton at C6, converting the iminium ion into the enamine regioisomer C1=C6. That enamine attacks the terminal CH2​ of the tethered acrylate in an intramolecular Michael addition. nitrogen donation restores the iminium ion, and protonation of the ester enolate completes the sequence.
The new bridge is C2−CH2​−CH(CO2​Et)−CH2​−C6. Together with the original ring and its C1 bridge this produces the bicyclo[3.3.1]nonane iminium ion skeleton in C. It retains the pyrrolidine group and the ester; hydrolysis to a ketone is not part of the displayed reaction. The second panel of the original annulation figure shows the chemical atom connectivity and distinguishes ionic bromide in B from a covalent bromine substituent.
