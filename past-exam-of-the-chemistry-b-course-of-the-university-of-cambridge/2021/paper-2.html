Solution

a OurBigBook.com
Each thiocyanate is a three-metal bridge, μ3​-κN:κS:κS: its nitrogen donates to one metal and its sulfur donates to two other metals. Thus the coordination ligand is ambidentate, but the structure uses both ends and shares the sulfur donor. Each metal receives two nitrogen and four sulfur donors, giving an approximately octahedral MN2​S4​ coordination sphere. Six donor contacts per metal are consistent with two coordination ligands per metal because each coordination ligand contributes three contacts across the lattice. Sharing these contacts produces a coordination polymer within each layer; the interlayer Van der Waals force does not add coordination bonds. The index μ3​ counts metal centres, whereas a hapticity label would count adjacent coordinated coordination ligand atoms and would not describe this connectivity correctly.[ref]

Charge balance fixes each metal as MII. In an octahedral crystal field the lower t2g​ set is (dxy​,dxz​,dyz​) and the upper eg​ set is (dz2​,dx2−y2​). The measured spin-only magnetic moments select the following occupations:

The result follows from S=nunpaired​/2 and 2S(S+1)​=nunpaired​(nunpaired​+2)​. Manganese(II) and iron(II) are high-spin complexes: their octahedral splitting is too small to compensate the additional pairing cost of their low-spin alternatives. Copper(II) is d9, with one unpaired electron regardless of which member of the upper pair is singly occupied. spin-orbit coupling and exchange interactions can produce deviations from the spin-only estimate in real measurements; no such correction is required to explain the supplied values.

c OurBigBook.com
At fixed +2 oxidation state, increasing effective nuclear charge across the first transition series generally contracts the ionic radius. The first expectation is therefore shorter average metal–nitrogen and metal–sulfur distances toward copper. This is a trend in comparable coordination environments, rather than a requirement that every individual contact shorten monotonically. The occupation of metal–coordination ligand antibonding orbitals, donor identity and lattice distortion all affect the observed distances.
The high-spin d5 and d6 ions both have two electrons in the strongly sigma-antibonding eg​ set, so adding the sixth electron to t2g​ does not itself introduce another sigma-antibonding electron. Copper(II), however, has eg3​ occupation and is subject to the Jahn-Teller effect. A tetragonal Jahn-Teller distortion commonly gives four shorter and two longer contacts, so an elongated copper contact can oppose the overall size trend. Here the nitrogen and sulfur donors are already chemically inequivalent; the supplied schematic alone does not determine which trans pair becomes longest. One should compare actual M–N and M–S sets separately, rather than infer a unique axis or a numerical distance from the magnetic moments. The experimentally determined structures illustrate why the donor geometry and individual contacts matter.[ref]

For A, linear nitrogen coordination with predominantly nitrile-like bonding is consistent with all three measurements:
M←N≡C−S−.
(2)
The short C–N and long C–S distances correspond approximately to a triple bond and a single bond. An approximately sp nitrogen donor has its donor lone pair directed along the N–C axis, explaining the linear M–N–C angle.
For B, bent nitrogen coordination with increased −N=C=S chemical resonance character accounts for the longer C–N and shorter C–S bonds. In this limiting description both internal bonds have substantial double-bond character and nitrogen is approximately sp2. One nitrogen lone pair can bind a metal with an M–N–C angle near 120∘; alternatively, donation of both nitrogen lone pairs can give a trigonal, nitrogen-bridging unit:
M╲N=C=S╱M′​
(3)
The nitrogen-bridging drawing is a compatible proposed bonding mode, but the listed internal distances and one angle alone do not prove the number of attached metals. In particular, B cannot be identified simply as terminal sulfur coordination: the specified angle explicitly contains an M–N bond. resonance structures explain relative bond orders; they do not imply an isolated coordination ligand with permanently localized integer bonds.

Solution

a OurBigBook.com
Take the M–C–O direction as z. carbon monoxide binds through carbon because its occupied 5σ donor orbital has a substantial carbon-end lobe. End-on overlap with an empty metal orbital of sigma symmetry gives CO-to-metal sigma donation. At the same time, occupied metal dxz​ and dyz​ orbitals overlap sideways with the two corresponding empty CO 2π∗ orbitals, giving metal-to-CO pi backbonding. Both channels strengthen the metal–carbon bond, while backbonding lowers the internal C–O bond order. The processes are synergic: sigma donation helps supply metal electron density for backbonding, while backbonding relieves electron accumulation at the metal.
The original orbital sketch shows one of the two perpendicular pi channels. It is the occupied metal orbital, rather than an empty metal orbital, that supplies electrons to CO antibonding orbitals.

c OurBigBook.com
The methoxy substituent donates electron density into the coordinated arene by chemical resonance. This increases the electron density available at chromium for pi backbonding to CO, weakening C–O and lowering its stretching frequencies. The ester substituent withdraws electron density from the arene, reducing pi backbonding and raising the carbonyl stretching frequencies. For both observed bands, the methoxy-substituted compound has the lowest carbonyl frequency, the unsubstituted compound is intermediate, and the ester-substituted compound has the highest.
In the harmonic oscillator approximation,
ν=2πc1​μk​​,
(5)
so the trend reflects an increasing effective C–O bond force constant rather than a change in the CO reduced mass. For example, comparison of the higher-frequency bands gives kester​/kmethoxy​≈(1992/1983)2=1.0091. This is only an approximate local-bond interpretation because the observed normal modes couple the three CO oscillators. In the idealized local C3v​ geometry they span A1​+E, giving two infrared-active frequencies. A substituted arene can lower the exact molecular symmetry and split the E pair, even if that splitting is unresolved experimentally.

d OurBigBook.com
triphenylphosphine is a good sigma donor and a substantially poorer pi acceptor than CO. Replacing CO by this phosphine ligand increases electron density at chromium and reduces competition among carbonyl ligands for pi backbonding. Backbonding into each remaining CO therefore strengthens, their internal bond force constants fall, and both remaining carbonyl stretching frequencies are expected to shift downward relative to the parent tricarbonyl.
The two remaining carbonyl ligands have coupled approximately symmetric and antisymmetric stretches; both are generally infrared active in this geometry. One should not treat the spectral change as simply deleting one of the original two bands. The substituted complex still has 6+6+2(2)+2=18 valence electrons. No numerical frequency shift follows from electron counting alone.

e OurBigBook.com
Photoinduced CO loss gives a 16-electron fragment. In the ester-substituted complex the pendant ester carbonyl oxygen can reach chromium and capture that vacant site. A suitable structure is Cr(η6-C6​H5​CO2​Me)(CO)2​(κO-ester), retaining the coordinated arene and adding intramolecular coordination of its ester oxygen. The added oxygen lone-pair donation restores an 18-electron count. The gaseous by-product is carbon monoxide.
heptane is a poor donor, but intramolecular ligand trapping avoids needing a donor solvent. Benzene has no pendant donor. Although the methoxy group has oxygen lone pairs, its directly attached oxygen does not have the same reach and favourable geometry as the projecting ester carbonyl oxygen; possession of a lone pair alone is insufficient. For these two substrates, acetonitrile can trap the unsaturated fragment through nitrogen to give Cr(η6-arene)(CO)2​(NCMe). Without a suitable trap, CO recombination can reverse the initial photochemical dissociation.

Solution

Words: 212
a OurBigBook.com
Let z be normal to the Cp ring through its centre. The five carbon pz​ orbitals form symmetry-adapted linear combinations with angular patterns m=0, ∣m∣=1, and ∣m∣=2. The all-in-phase a1​ combination has sigma symmetry about this axis and can interact with dz2​. The e1​ pair matches (dxz​,dyz​) by pi symmetry, while the e2​ pair matches (dxy​,dx2−y2​) by delta symmetry. Rotating within either degenerate pair changes the drawing but not the physical interaction.
For the usual eta-five geometry, the metal d interactions are ranked qualitatively pi > delta > sigma. The dxz​,dyz​ lobes overlap effectively with the occupied ring combinations. The delta interaction is weaker and involves the higher, initially unoccupied Cp combinations; the dz2​ interaction with the ring is particularly weak because its axial and equatorial parts contribute little net overlap in this geometry. Thus the statement that a sigma bond is always stronger than a pi bond cannot be transferred indiscriminately to this delocalized ring–metal overlap problem. metal s and pz​ orbitals also have the symmetry of the a1​ coordination ligand combination and contribute to overall bonding, so weak dz2​ overlap does not mean the entire sigma-symmetry channel is absent.
In ionic electron counting, Cp is C5​H5−​ with six pi electrons in its three occupied ring combinations, hence a six-electron donor. The same coordination ligand is assigned five electrons in neutral counting.

b OurBigBook.com
In a metallocene, several orbitals with substantial metal d character are nonbonding molecular orbitals or only weakly bonding or antibonding with respect to the strong Cp–metal framework. Adding or removing a small number of electrons from them can therefore change oxidation state or spin without destroying the principal ring–metal bonds. This allows stable compounds on either side of the 18-electron count: for example, the usual neutral counts for chromocene, ferrocene, cobaltocene and nickelocene are respectively 16, 18, 19 and 20. The 18-electron rule remains a useful reference, but occupation of a weakly antibonding orbital is much less disruptive than occupation of a strongly antibonding orbital. Redox stability also depends on geometry, substituents and reaction conditions, so this is a bonding explanation of the observed flexibility rather than a guarantee of equal stability for all counts.

c OurBigBook.com
Use neutral electron counting consistently: each terminal Cp supplies five electrons, while the eight pi electrons of neutral pentalene must be allocated across the shared fused framework rather than counted twice as two independent Cp coordination ligands. One set of 18-electron-consistent proposals is:
  • Iron: syn μ-η5:η5 pentalene with an Fe–Fe single bond. The two iron–Cp fragments lie on the same face. Assigning four pentalene electrons to each iron gives 8+5+4=17 before direct bonding; the Fe–Fe bond contributes one electron to each count, giving 18. Its direct bond naturally accounts for the especially short metal–metal separation. An anti arrangement cannot retain this short same-face bond.
  • Cobalt: μ-η5:η5 pentalene without a Co–Co bond. The count is 9+5+4=18 at each metal. Syn and anti arrangements are possible at this formal level because there is no required direct bond. Removal of the bond relative to iron accounts for a larger separation.
  • Nickel: slipped μ-η3:η3 coordination to the two outer allylic portions. The fusion bond remains an uncoordinated alkene in this limiting bonding picture, while each outer three-carbon portion contributes three electrons in neutral counting: 10+5+3=18. Equivalent ionic counting gives a nickel(II) d8 centre, a six-electron Cp and a four-electron allyl ligand. Moving the metal binding sites toward the outer three-carbon portions increases the separation further. Syn and anti arrangements can again be drawn.
The original schematic shows the donor regions and the required iron–iron bond; dashed metal-to-region lines denote delocalized coordination rather than localized carbon–metal single bonds.
These proposals explain the requested electron counts and distance ordering. electron counting alone does not establish the unique ground-state structure: orbital calculations on related pentalene complexes also identify stable formally electron-deficient arrangements. In particular, the formal requirement of a metal–metal bond in the proposed 18-electron iron model should not be converted into a claim that every known iron pentalene complex adopts that model.[ref]

One equivalent of butyllithium removes one proton per hydrocarbon, giving the hydropentalenyl monoanion C8​H7−​. It does not give the doubly deprotonated pentalenide. salt metathesis with half an equivalent of iron(II) chloride therefore gives the sandwich complex
Fe(η5-C8​H7​)2​,d6+6+6=18.​
(6)
Each coordination ligand binds through its aromatic five-membered portion and retains an uncoordinated double bond in the fused ring. The orientation of this unsymmetrical fused appendage makes the two coordinated coordination ligand faces distinguishable. Pairing matching and opposite planar configurations gives a racemic form, (Rp​,Rp​)/(Sp​,Sp​), and a meso form, (Rp​,Sp​). This describes the two stereoisomeric classes, rather than assigning the two easily interconverting rotations of ordinary ferrocene as separate configurational isomers. The molecular structure and double-bond disorder of bis(hydropentalenyl)iron have been examined crystallographically.[ref]

ii OurBigBook.com
Cleavage of the disulfide supplies one methylthiolate per cobalt. A monomer CpCo(SMe) would have only 7+6+2=15 electrons by ionic counting. Dimerization with two bridging thiolates supplies a second sulfur interaction at each cobalt, and a direct Co–Co bond completes each count. Thus a suitable structure is [CpCo(μ-SMe)]2​ with a Co–Co single bond, containing an Co2​S2​ diamond:
Co:d7+6 (Cp)+2+2 (two S donors)+1 (Co−Co)=18.
(7)
Each cobalt is formally +2: Cp and methylthiolate each carry one negative charge. The stoichiometry is
2CpCo(CO)2​+MeSSMe⟶[CpCo(μ-SMe)]2​+4CO.
(8)
The molecular formula C12​H16​Co2​S2​ reduces to the required empirical formula. The sulfur–sulfur bond has been cleaved, both sulfur atoms now bridge the metals, and the loss of two CO molecules per cobalt is balanced. Formally cobalt(I) is oxidized to cobalt(II), while the disulfide is reduced to thiolate.

Solution

Words: 237
b OurBigBook.com
In a boroxine, each boron has three sigma bonds and is approximately trigonal planar, with an sp2 description for its sigma framework and an available perpendicular 2p orbital. oxygen can be described approximately as sp2: two in-plane hybrids form B–O sigma bonds, one contains an in-plane lone pair, and a perpendicular 2p lone pair overlaps with neighbouring boron 2p orbitals. These three oxygen donor pairs supply a six-electron pi system around the alternating ring. The combination of good same-shell overlap and a low-strain six-membered ring favours planarity and partial B–O multiple-bond character. The bonds remain polar; a six-electron diagram alone does not establish benzene-like aromatic stabilization quantitatively.
In a siloxane, each silicon instead has four sigma bonds, two to oxygen and two to its organic groups, giving an approximately tetrahedral sp3 sigma framework. oxygen has two sigma bonds and two lone pairs, so an approximately sp3 localized description is a useful starting point, although actual Si–O–Si angles and lone-pair hybrids vary considerably. There is no comparable ring of vacant silicon 2p orbitals. A description based on strong oxygen-2p to silicon-3d pi bonds is also misleading: those orbitals have poor energy and radial matching. Strongly polar Si–O sigma bonding, supplemented by negative hyperconjugation from oxygen lone pairs into appropriate Si–R antibonding orbitals, gives a better account. The ring can therefore pucker rather than requiring continuous planar pi overlap. This follows the broader conclusion that strong main-group bonds and apparently expanded valence do not imply dominant valence d-orbital participation.[ref]

Solution

Words: 161
c OurBigBook.com
boron is sufficiently small for effective oxygen-2p/boron-2p overlap, and three-coordinate planar boron benefits from the pi donation just described. A six-membered boroxine ring provides this overlap without severe angle strain. The larger aluminium centre has poorer oxygen-2p/aluminium-3p matching and remains strongly Lewis acidic. It can lower its electron deficiency by accepting additional oxygen coordination, making a three-dimensional aluminoxane cage favourable. Four-coordinate aluminium does not require a boroxine-like planar pi system.
An appropriate hexamer model is an alternating Al/O hexagonal prism, with six aluminium and six oxygen vertices. Colour the twelve-vertex prism alternately Al and O: each aluminium then has three oxygen neighbours and an outward organic substituent, making it four-coordinate, while each oxygen bridges three aluminium centres. The graph has 18 Al–O edges, hence six Al centres with three Al–O bonds each. Different polyhedral connectivities and sizes can meet similar local coordination requirements, so the hexamer is a proposal rather than a claim that the empirical repeat unit fixes a unique cage.

d OurBigBook.com
Ring cleavage of 1,3,5-Trioxane regenerates formaldehyde, whose strong carbon–oxygen pi bond stabilizes the monomer; in water much of it is further stabilized as its hydrate. acetal hydrolysis, particularly under acid catalysis, provides a route between the cyclic trimer and these monomeric forms.
The corresponding formal depolymerization of a siloxane would create R2​Si=O silanone units. Such ordinary small-substituent silanones have much less favourable pi bonding and are very reactive toward forming new Si–O–Si linkages. The connected siloxane is consequently preferred over a persistent aqueous solution of silanone monomers. This explains the intended contrast, but aqueous stability is conditional: acid or base can catalyse siloxane cleavage to silanols, and those silanols can condense again. Neither neutral-water inertness of every siloxane nor instantaneous neutral-water dissociation of every trioxane should be inferred.

First count the organic groups and then the unsatisfied oxygen valences. A is the fully condensed T8 silsesquioxane cage, Cy8​Si8​O12​, with silicon at the eight vertices of a cube and bridging oxygen on its twelve edges. Each silicon has one cyclohexyl group and three siloxane bridges. Its eight cyclohexyl groups supply exactly C48​H88​.
B is a fully condensed T10 silsesquioxane cage, Cy10​Si10​O15​, for which a pentagonal-prismatic silicon skeleton with an oxygen on every edge is a standard compatible structure. Its ten groups give C60​H110​. The formula establishes full condensation but does not, without more structural data, rule out every other possible ten-vertex cage connectivity.
C is a corner-deficient T7 trisilanol cage, Cy7​Si7​O9​(OH)3​. Seven cyclohexyl groups account for C42​H77​, leaving three hydrogen atoms as Si–OH groups. Remove one silicon corner of the T8 cube and replace the three severed connections by terminal hydroxyl groups. Nine oxygen atoms remain as bridges and three become terminal silanol oxygen atoms, giving precisely twelve oxygen atoms. There are three silicon sites bearing OH, three further equivalent fully condensed sites, and one opposite fully condensed site, consistent with three silicon environments in a 3:3:1 site-count ratio. The less negative resonance is naturally associated with the three silanol-bearing sites; the remaining two resonances belong to the two fully condensed classes. Peak heights alone are not quantitative integrations.
The source figure appears to label the spectrum 27Si. The chemically appropriate routine experiment here is silicon-29 NMR, using the spin-one-half isotope 29Si; the figure label is treated as an apparent isotope-label typographical error, not as evidence for another cage.

W{Cy7​Si7​O9​(O)3​}2​​

i OurBigBook.com
Treat the reacting carbon skeletons as an activated glycosyl donor and a 3-substituted indole. The new linkage is an anomeric-carbon–indole-C2 carbon–carbon bond, not a bond to indole nitrogen. A plausible purely chemical pathway is a dissociative electrophilic aromatic substitution:
  • Activation of the anomeric diphosphate leaving group, for example by acid, permits cleavage of its bond to the anomeric carbon. The ring oxygen stabilizes the resulting glycosyloxocarbenium ion, represented by the resonance pair Oring​−C1+​↔Oring+​=C1​. A nearby diphosphate counterion may remain in a contact ion pair.
  • The indole C2–C3 pi bond attacks C1 through C2. This makes the new C1–C2 bond and gives an arenium ion, with C2 temporarily tetrahedral and still bearing its original hydrogen. Positive charge can be delocalized over the pyrrolic portion, including an indolium resonance contributor.
  • A base removes that C2 hydrogen. The C2–H bond electrons restore the C2–C3 pi bond and aromaticity, releasing the C-glycosylation product while retaining N–H.
The drawn donor and product have opposite anomeric faces: the anomeric leaving group is on the same face as the hydroxymethyl substituent, while the new carbon substituent is on the opposite face. Thus the target has net inversion at the anomeric carbon. A tight ion pair with the departing group shielding its original face can rationalize opposite-face capture in this proposed pathway. A freely solvated planar oxocarbenium ion would not by itself guarantee exclusive inversion; competing faces, hydrolysis and other products remain possible without an experimentally specified chemical promoter. This is a plausible non-enzymatic mechanism, not a claim that the uncatalysed reaction is rapid or clean in neutral water.

ii OurBigBook.com
For the three elementary transformations just proposed, a qualitative free-energy profile has three transition states and two intermediates. The sugar substituents and indole C3 substituent stay unchanged, so the structural shorthand below suppresses them while retaining the reacting atoms.
The first transition state has a partially broken anomeric C1–O(leaving group) bond and developing oxocarbenium character, [Oring​−C1δ+​⋯OLG​]‡. Its following minimum is the glycosyloxocarbenium ion plus counterion. The second transition state has a forming C1–C2 bond, [Oring​−C1​⋯C2​(H)ind​]‡, and developing positive charge in the indole. Its following minimum is the covalently joined arenium ion with an intact C2–H bond. The third transition state has simultaneous C2–H cleavage, B–H formation and restoration of the indole pi bond, [B⋯H⋯C2​−C3​]‡. It leads to the aromatic C-linked product and protonated base.
The vertical separations are illustrative because no activation free energies or equilibrium constants are supplied. In particular, the highest barrier cannot be identified experimentally from this drawing alone. protonation of the leaving group is treated as an initial rapid equilibrium in the chosen mechanism; explicitly resolving it would add the corresponding proton-transfer state and intermediate.
