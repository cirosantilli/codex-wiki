ii OurBigBook.com
protonation of the alcohol converts its OH group into a water leaving group. Departure of water initially gives the secondary carbocation CH3​−CH+−CH(CH3​)2​. A 1,2-hydride shift from the neighboring methine group transfers its C–H bonding pair to the electron-deficient carbon:
CH3​−CH+−CH(CH3​)2​⟶CH3​CH2​−C+(CH3​)2​.
(1)
The resulting tertiary carbocation is the tert-amyl electrophile. An aromatic π bond of the dimethoxybenzene attacks that carbon, producing a Wheland intermediate. Removal of the proton from the attacked ring carbon restores aromaticity and regenerates acid. Repeating this Friedel-Crafts alkylation at the symmetry-related second site gives the disubstituted product.
The hydride migration explains the new ethyl group and the absence of a benzylic methine group proton. Simply attaching the original alcohol skeleton at its OH-bearing carbon would not explain the twelve-proton NMR singlet.

iii OurBigBook.com
The methoxy groups activate the aromatic ring by electron donation through chemical resonance and direct electrophilic substitution ortho and para. Their para positions are already occupied by one another, so the first alkylation occurs at an available ortho position; the four initially vacant positions are equivalent by symmetry.
After placing the first tert-amyl group at position 2, position 5 is favored for the second substitution. It is para to that bulky group and ortho to the other methoxy group. Position 3 is crowded between methoxy and tert-amyl substituents, while position 6 is near the first tert-amyl group across the intervening methoxy-bearing carbon. The 2,5 arrangement distributes the bulky groups on opposite parts of the ring and preserves the equivalence seen in the spectrum.
Alkyl groups do not electronically deactivate the ring, so the stopping point needs a steric explanation. After two such substitutions the remaining positions are much more congested, each adjacent to a bulky tert-amyl group and a methoxy group. Two large substituents are accommodated much more readily than a third, despite excess alcohol. The formula and NMR identify this sterically favored disubstitution; they do not imply that a Friedel-Crafts reaction always stops after two alkylations.

The new ring is formed by intramolecular Friedel-Crafts addition to the ketone. titanium tetrachloride coordinates the ketone oxygen of the cyclic beta-ketoester, increasing electrophilicity at that carbonyl carbon. The nearby electron-rich aryl ring attacks this activated ketone at the available position ortho to its ethyl tether.
The electron-flow sequence is: an aromatic π bond forms the new aryl–carbonyl-carbon bond, the ketone π bond moves onto oxygen, and loss of the aromatic proton restores aromaticity. The two-carbon tether and the shared quaternary carbon make the newly closed carbocycle six-membered. Hydrolytic work-up converts the titanium-bound alkoxide into the tertiary alcohol.
The lactone carbonyl survives. This is an addition to the ketone with retention of its oxygen as OH, rather than an acyl substitution that removes that oxygen. The pendant terminal alkene is not involved. These bond changes account for the fused aromatic ring system and the alcohol in C without needing to assign the new stereochemistry.

For an amine-first pathway, use triethylamine as a nucleophile in a reversible conjugate addition. Its lone pair attacks C3 of the enone; electron shifts through C2 and the carbonyl give a zwitterionic enolate with a C3–NEt3+​ bond.
The C2 nucleophilic site of that enolate attacks Br2​, installing C2–Br and releasing bromide. Removal of C2–H by base then reforms C2=C3 while the C3–N bond breaks back to neutral triethylamine. This addition–bromination–elimination sequence gives the same C2-brominated enone and regenerates the nucleophilic amine, with another base equivalent accepting the eliminated proton.
These are alternative formal sequences requested by the question, not a kinetic determination that one must dominate under unspecified conditions. In particular, the amine-first sequence does not require triethylamine to generate a high concentration of a vinylic carbanion by direct deprotonation.

C2−C3−S−CH2​−C(=O)−C2​.
(3)
The original C1 ketone remains, and the former methyl-ester carbonyl becomes the second ketone. The product is drawn in the first panel below.
methoxide first deprotonates the thiol. The soft thiolate attacks the beta carbon C3 in a Michael addition, shifting the enone electrons onto oxygen. The resulting enolate is nucleophilic at C2. It then attacks its tethered ester carbonyl intramolecularly, forming a five-membered ring through a tetrahedral intermediate. Collapse expels methoxide. proton transfers and work-up give the neutral bicyclic beta-diketone.

a OurBigBook.com
There are two, two and four stereoisomers, respectively; all eight individual stereoisomers are chiral. The drawings below give the mirror pairs. Solid and hashed wedges distinguish opposite faces; ordinary changes between conformers are not counted again.
  • The benzyl-substituted alkylidenecyclohexane has an axial stereogenic element. At one end the exocyclic alkene presents Ph and H; at the other end the substituted ring carbon presents benzyl and H. The ring constrains the intervening paths, so exchanging one end pair gives the nonsuperposable mirror image. There is one enantiomeric pair, rather than an independent ordinary E/Z pair multiplied by a carbon-center count.
  • The diphenylspiro compound also has one axial chirality. The two four-membered rings occupy different, approximately perpendicular planes. Their remote Ph/H substitutions distinguish the two handed arrangements. It gives one enantiomeric pair and no meso form. axial chirality of this spiro framework is experimentally established, for example by resolution of its 2,6-dicarboxylic-acid derivatives.[ref]
  • The phospholane has a stereogenic carbon at the COOH-bearing position and a pyramidal phosphorus stereocenter. The phosphorus has three different carbon ligands and a lone pair: its two ring paths differ because only one encounters the substituted carbon immediately. The four configurations are (RP​,RC​), (RP​,SC​), (SP​,RC​) and (SP​,SC​). The first and last form a mirror pair, as do the middle two. carbon and phosphorus cannot be interchanged by a symmetry operation, so there is no meso reduction in the count.
Unlike the usual rapidly inverting trialkylamine, a pyramidal phosphine can retain a phosphorus configuration on an ordinary observational timescale; its two phosphorus configurations must therefore be included.

b OurBigBook.com
Both reactions are E2 eliminations requiring an axial chloride and an axial beta hydrogen in an antiperiplanar conformation relative to the C–Cl bond. The reacting C–H bond supplies the alkene π bond as the C–Cl bond breaks; hydroxide accepts the proton.
For the trans-1,2 substrate, the low-energy chair has methyl and chlorine both equatorial. ring inversion is needed to place chlorine axial, but this also places methyl axial. The population of the reactive chair is consequently small, explaining the slow reaction. At the methyl-bearing neighboring carbon the axial position is occupied by methyl, not hydrogen. Only the other beta carbon has the needed anti axial H, so elimination gives 3-methylcyclohexene rather than the more substituted alkene.
For the cis substrate, a favorable chair has methyl equatorial and chlorine axial. It can react without paying the axial-methyl penalty, explaining the faster rate. Both adjacent carbons now supply an anti axial hydrogen. Removing the H on the methyl-bearing carbon gives 1-methylcyclohexene, the major product; removing the other gives 3-methylcyclohexene, the minor product. The former has the more substituted double bond.
Thus alkene stability selects between accessible pathways in the cis case, but it cannot create the missing antiperiplanar conformation H in the trans case.

Number the nitrogen N1, the phenyl-bearing carbon C2, and then continue through C3–C6 around the piperidine. Call the solid-wedge epoxide face up; the phenyl group is down in both starting structures. The bulky phenyl group favors a pseudoequatorial orientation.
epoxide opening follows the Fürst-Plattner preference for a chair-like, trans-diaxial opening trajectory. Attack at the two epoxide carbons is not equivalent: the favored route evolves toward an ordinary chair, whereas the alternative demands a less favorable twist/boat-like distortion. Such conformational control of epoxy-2-arylpiperidine opening is supported by synthetic studies.[ref]
For the first epoxide, azide attacks C5 from down, breaking the C5–O bond. The residual C4–O bond is retained, so work-up gives C5–N3 down and C4–OH up, with the original C2–Ph down. For the second, down-face epoxide, azide attacks C4 from up, leaving C4–N3 up and C5–OH down. These assignments are shown in the middle row of the original stereochemical map.
In each mechanism an azide terminal nitrogen attacks the epoxide carbon, the C–O bonding pair goes to oxygen, and protonation gives the alcohol. Each attacked center inverts, and the azide and OH are trans. The phenyl-bearing center is unchanged. Reversing the epoxide face changes which carbon has the favored axial attack trajectory; a steric rule based only on distance from phenyl misses that conformational effect.

sulfide first opens one epoxide by backside attack, giving a carbon-bound thiolate and an alkoxide. Using the same numbering, take this first attack at C2: the C2–S bond is down and C3–O remains up.
The attached sulfur still bears a nucleophilic lone pair and negative charge. It attacks C5 of the second epoxide intramolecularly, breaking C5–O and closing a bridge between C2 and C5. Work-up protonates the oxygen anions at C3 and C6. Thus B is 7-thiabicyclo[2.2.1]heptane-2,5-diol, with both OH groups on the face opposite the sulfur bridge.
Both C–S bonds are formed by inversion at their attacked epoxide carbons. Because the original epoxides are cis, both new C–S bonds point to the same opposite face and one sulfur chemical atom can connect them. The 1,4 bridge gives two five-membered sulfur-containing circuits, unlike the more strained closure between nearby 1,3 positions.
The sulfur therefore replaces two separate external nucleophiles: two epoxides are opened but only one sulfur chemical atom is incorporated. Adding that sulfur and two work-up protons gives C6​H10​O2​S, consistent with B.

The alcohol oxygen attacks sulfur of methanesulfonyl chloride, chloride leaves, and triethylamine removes the O–H proton. This gives an O-mesylate with retention of the carbon configuration: the original carbon–oxygen bond has not been displaced.
An accessible chair places the mesylate axial. triethylamine then removes the anti axial hydrogen on the carbon between the ketone and mesylate. Its C–H bond forms the new C=C bond while the C–O bond expels mesylate. The result is the conjugated cyclohexenone.
The neighboring carbon on the acetonide side has its relevant axial position occupied by its oxygen substituent, rather than an anti axial hydrogen. The elimination toward the ketone is therefore favored both by the trans-diaxial geometry and by conjugation of the resulting alkene with C=O. The acetonide remains intact. No inversion of the original alcohol carbon is required during mesylation, and it becomes planar during elimination.

The first step is a vinylogous aldol condensation at C5 of the unsaturated lactam. Number nitrogen N1, the lactam carbonyl C2, and then the methoxy-substituted alkene C3=C4 and the methylene group C5.
hydroxide reversibly removes a C5 proton. The resulting vinylogous enolate is delocalized through C5–C4–C3–C2–O: it is not necessary to create an unstabilized isolated carbanion beside nitrogen. The hydroxyl group first binds aluminium, normally by deprotonation to an aluminium alkoxide with release of hydrogen. Coordination also brings aluminium close to the alkyne. directed trans-hydroalumination then puts hydride on the alkyne carbon proximal to CH2O and aluminium on the distal carbon.
One useful mechanistic description has external hydride attacking the coordinated alkyne opposite the aluminium-containing face. The resulting vinylaluminate is stabilized by a five-membered O–Al–C–C–CH2 chelate. This is not the ordinary undirected syn addition of a neutral dialkylaluminium hydride. Propargylic-alcohol-directed trans-hydroalumination with Red-Al gives vinylaluminate intermediates that can be intercepted by electrophiles.[ref]
iodine replaces the distal C–Al bond with C–I without scrambling the alkene geometry; aqueous work-up releases the alcohol. hydride and iodine are consequently trans across the alkene, while iodine and the CH2OH group are on the same side.
By Cahn-Ingold-Prelog priority rules priority the product is therefore Z: CH2OH outranks H at one alkene carbon and I outranks CH2R' at the other. Describing the two carbon chains as trans would not make this an E alkene, because the iodine changes which substituent has higher priority.

The strongest donor belongs to the acid, α1​=3.5, and the strongest acceptor to the aminopyridine, β2​=8.5. The cocrystal pairs the strongest donor with the strongest acceptor, while the reciprocal amino-NH···carbonyl contact closes a complementary two-contact motif.
Using the simple favorable-contact score αβ, one heterodimer gives
Shetero​=α1​β2​+α2​β1​=(3.5)(8.5)+(3.0)(5.5)=46.25.
(4)
The self-associated comparison, normalized to the same two molecules and two hydrogen bonds, is half an acid dimer plus half an aminopyridine dimer:
Sself​=α1​β1​+α2​β2​=(3.5)(5.5)+(3.0)(8.5)=44.75.
(5)
Thus
ΔS=1.50=(α1​−α2​)(β2​−β1​)>0​.
(6)
In a model where bond free energy contains −αβ, this is a favorable energy change. No bonds have been artificially added to one side of the comparison: both alternatives contain two contacts per acid/aminopyridine pair.
