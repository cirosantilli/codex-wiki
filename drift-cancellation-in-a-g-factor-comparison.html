Drift cancellation in a g-factor comparison

 ... Branches of physics Electromagnetism Magnetic vector potential Magnetic dipole moment Electron magnetic moment Spin-orbit g-factor ratio from two P states
OurBigBook.com Words: 250
Even a shared field does not cancel if it is sampled at different times. Write fA​(t)=gA​C(t) and fB​(t)=gB​C(t), where C(t)=μB​B(t)/h. As an original proposed diagnostic, bracket the B-state reading at t=0 with A-state readings at t=±τ:
Rbracket​=21​[fA​(−τ)+fA​(+τ)]fB​(0)​.
(95)
If C(t)=C0​(1+bt), the denominator is exactly gA​C0​, so Rbracket​=gB​/gA​. A single earlier A reading instead gives Runpaired​=(gB​/gA​)/(1−bτ). For the preceding numerical model and bτ=0.001, this unpaired comparison falsely gives approximately ae​=0.00186649; interpolation recovers 0.0012. The sequence illustrates cancellation and is not asserted to be Kusch and Foley's exact historical timing protocol.
For a curved field C(t)=C0​(1+bt+ct2), the same calculation gives
Rbracket​=1+cτ2gB​/gA​​.
(96)
The linear drift cancels but the curvature remains. Different fields sampled by the two beams, additive frequency offsets and incorrect atomic corrections also survive. For a small ratio error δR, direct differentiation of the inverse relation gives
δx≃(R+1)26​δR,δae​≃(R+1)23​δR(gL​=1).
(97)
Repeated readings can reduce random measurement uncertainty; agreement among genuinely different state comparisons is what tests shared systematic errors in their interpretation.
