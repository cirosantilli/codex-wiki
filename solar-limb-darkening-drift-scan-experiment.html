Solar limb-darkening drift-scan experiment

 ... Branches of physics Optics Radiometry Radiance Spectral radiance Limb darkening
OurBigBook.com Words: 185
A motivating measurement used the McMath solar optical telescope at Kitt Peak. The Sun's image drifted across a 10mm-high, 0.2mm-wide optical slit; a double-pass spectrograph selected wavelength intervals from 0.0015nm wide at 303nm to 0.007nm at 1099nm. An EMI 9750 QB photodetector or silicon photodiode supplied a digitized signal: 1kHz samples were summed into 4096 positions per crossing, including dark and off-limb sky readings. [ref]
Controls included daily realignment of optical telescope mirrors, wavelength settings between spectral lines insensitive to the rotational Doppler shift, selection of detectors without detectable hysteresis, and measured electronic nonlinearity corrections below 0.1% of maximum signal. Limb-position calibration combined centres of gravity of the edge derivative profiles with almanac semidiameters; the preliminary edge shifts were consistent with astronomical seeing of approximately 5 arcseconds full width at half maximum. [ref]
The decreasing centre-to-limb readout gives a specific intensity profile, constraining stratification of the photosphere under a radiative transfer model. Astronomical seeing, instrumental scattering of light, and solar granulation limit this inference; brightness alone does not uniquely determine temperature versus geometric height. These observations need not follow the stipulated linear limb-darkening law.
