Quadratic residue (a≡x2(modp))

 Home Mathematics Area of mathematics Number theory
OurBigBook.com Words: 2k Articles: 24
For an odd prime p, a nonzero congruence class a is a quadratic residue when a≡x2(modp) for some integer x. Here "quadratic residue" excludes zero: zero is a square of a number, but is neither a quadratic residue nor a quadratic nonresidue under this convention. Some treatments include zero among quadratic residues; the distinction matters when counting them or discussing their subgroup.[ref]
Modulo seven, squaring the six nonzero congruence classes gives
xx2mod7​11​24​32​42​54​61​​
(1)
Thus the set of quadratic residues is Q7​={1,2,4}, and the quadratic nonresidues are {3,5,6}. In particular, x2≡2(mod7) has exactly the two solutions x≡3,4(mod7): 32=9≡2 and 42=16≡2. Modulo eleven the quadratic residues are instead Q11​={1,3,4,5,9}. Two is therefore a quadratic residue modulo seven and a quadratic nonresidue modulo eleven: being a quadratic residue depends on the modulus.
Why do the square roots come in pairs? If x2≡y2(modp), the prime number p divides (x−y)(x+y), so it divides one factor: y≡x or y≡−x(modp). For nonzero x these are distinct, because p is an odd prime. Every nonempty nonzero fiber of a function of s(x)=x2 therefore contains exactly two inputs. Pairing the p−1 nonzero congruence classes gives exactly (p−1)/2 quadratic residues and (p−1)/2 quadratic nonresidues. Including zero gives (p+1)/2 different squares, with one rather than two square roots for zero.
The quadratic residues form a subgroup of the multiplicative group of a finite field Fp×​, with index of a subgroup two. Indeed, one is a square, (u2)(v2)=(uv)2, and (u2)−1=(u−1)2; the inverses exist because the inputs are nonzero in the prime field. Equivalently, the squaring group homomorphism has kernel of a group homomorphism {1,−1} and image of a function Qp​. The apparent loss of a sign in squaring becomes a precise division of the nonzero arithmetic into two equal parts.
This structure turns a search for square roots into a yes-or-no test: Euler's criterion detects quadratic residues through modular exponentiation, and the Legendre symbol records the result as a sign. For a general integer modulus, the modular congruence x2≡a(modn) still makes sense, but the two-root count and the prime field argument need not survive. For example, x2≡1(mod15) has four solutions 1,4,11,14; the Chinese remainder theorem separates the sign choices modulo three and five.
