For a homogeneous canonical scalar field, ρϕ​=ϕ˙​2/2+V and Pϕ​=ϕ˙​2/2−V. Potential-energy domination gives Pϕ​≃−ρϕ​, accelerated expansion and a decreasing comoving Hubble radius. Hubble friction can permit slow roll down a sufficiently flat potential. Enough inflation stretches an initially small causally connected region to encompass our observable universe, suppresses the relative spatial-curvature term and dilutes unwanted massive relics. quantum fluctuations supply possible seeds for cosmological structure; a successful model must also end inflation and create the hot post-inflationary universe. These statements do not imply that arbitrary initial conditions inevitably inflate.

reheating requires interactions beyond the isolated field equations above. The oscillating inflaton can decay into other particles, whose scattering and further decays establish a thermal bath. The perturbative inflaton reheating equations are
ρ˙​ϕ​+3Hρϕ​=−Γρϕ​,ρ˙​r​+4Hρr​=Γρϕ​.

Expansion redshifts the components differently; transfer becomes efficient around H∼Γ, and radiation domination follows when the transferred relativistic energy dominates. For suitable couplings, nonperturbative preheating by parametric resonance can precede thermalization, with backreaction and rescattering affecting its completion. Neither the decay rate nor thermalization is specified by m and V alone. Instantaneous reheating idealizes all this as immediate conversion of the end-of-inflation energy to radiation.[ref]
For instantaneous-reheating horizon matching, specify the comparison: take the initial patch to be of order Hi−1​, compare the same-shaped inflationary history with approximately fixed Hi​/Hend​, and assume instantaneous reheating followed by adiabatic expansion. Ignoring changes in relativistic degrees of freedom, a0​/areh​∝Treh​, while energy conservation and the Friedmann equation give Hend​∝Treh2​. The present size of this inflated patch therefore scales as
ℓ0​∼Hi−1​eNareh​a0​​∝Treh​eN​.
(14)
At fixed present Hubble radius, a higher instantaneous reheating scale requires more e-folds in this standard comparison, approximately ΔN≃ln(T2​/T1​), up to slow scale and entropy-degree corrections. Hotter reheating gives more subsequent expansion, but the associated initial Hubble patch is smaller by a stronger power of temperature. If instead one artificially holds the initial physical patch or Hi​ fixed while varying the end energy independently, the post-inflationary geometric effect alone would give the opposite trend. temperature without an inflation-scale comparison convention does not fix that alternative problem; this is why the matching assumptions must be stated.[ref]
