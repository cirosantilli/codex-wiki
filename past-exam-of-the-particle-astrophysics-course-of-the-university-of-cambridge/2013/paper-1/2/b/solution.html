supersymmetry pairs bosonic and fermionic degrees of freedom; broken supersymmetry permits partners heavier than known particles. At the Large Hadron Collider, strong production of squarks and gluinos is an important channel. If R-parity is conserved, partners are produced in pairs and their cascades end in a stable lightest supersymmetric particle. A neutral weakly interacting lightest supersymmetric particle escapes, giving missing transverse momentum together with jets and possibly leptons. Conservation is tested transversely because the initial parton longitudinal momenta are unknown.
The experimental observable is
pTmiss​=−∑reconstructed​pT​.

neutrinos produce genuine missing transverse momentum, and calorimeter mismeasurement or detector defects can imitate it. Searches use jet multiplicity, transverse momenta, missing momentum, angular separation cuts and a collider effective mass such as meff​=pTmiss​+∑pTjet​. Isolated-lepton selections explore complementary cascades. Trigger efficiency, jet energy scale, collision pileup and event cleaning affect acceptance.
A credible search estimates backgrounds from particle-physics control regions before inspecting the signal region. Invisible Z→ννˉ plus jets can be constrained using photon-plus-jets or visible Z→ℓ+ℓ− samples with control-region transfer factors. Lepton-enriched samples constrain W and top backgrounds with lost leptons; multijet samples constrain fake missing momentum. A joint likelihood includes these observations and nuisance parameters for systematic uncertainties. Agreement with background gives upper bounds on signal rate and conditional mass exclusions. The pre-examination ATLAS 7 TeV analysis is a concrete example of this strategy and reported no significant excess.[ref]
Electroweak production motivates multilepton searches; compressed spectra can give soft visible decay products and require special selections such as recoil against initial-state radiation. R-parity violation can instead remove the stable missing-particle signature, requiring other searches. An exclusion always depends on masses, branching fractions, production rates and decay assumptions; a null jets-plus-missing-momentum result does not exclude supersymmetry as a whole.
