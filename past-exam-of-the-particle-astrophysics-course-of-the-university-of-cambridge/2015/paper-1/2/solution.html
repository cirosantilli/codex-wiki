Particle candidate. A stable, electrically neutral weakly interacting massive particle can become nonrelativistic before cosmological particle freeze-out. Its annihilation rate per particle Γ=n⟨σv⟩ eventually falls below the Hubble parameter; the surviving comoving number then remains approximately fixed. A weak-scale annihilation cross section can give a cosmologically significant thermal relic. This is a mechanism and a parameter-scale motivation, not a guarantee that every weakly interacting particle has the right abundance.
supersymmetry supplies neutralinos, mixtures of neutral electroweak gauginos and higgsinos. If a neutralino is the lightest supersymmetric particle and R-parity is conserved, it cannot decay solely into Standard Model particles and is stable. Its electroweak interactions can make it a WIMP. Conservation of R-parity and a neutral lightest state are additional assumptions; R-parity violation, coannihilation, composition and resonant annihilation can substantially change the conclusion.
An underground test. XENON100 at Gran Sasso used a liquid-xenon target in a two-phase time projection chamber. A WIMP scattering elastically from a xenon nucleus could produce a low-energy nuclear recoil. The interaction makes prompt particle scintillation, S1, and ionization electrons. An electric field drifts the electrons upward; their extraction into xenon gas produces delayed electroluminescence, S2. The delay measures depth, while the S2 light pattern determines the transverse position. An inner fiducial volume suppresses surface backgrounds through xenon self-shielding; underground overburden reduces cosmic-ray muons, and shielding and material selection reduce radioactivity.[ref]
The S2/S1 ratio separates most electron recoils from nuclear recoils. Gamma-source calibration maps the electron-recoil leakage; an americium–beryllium neutron source calibrates nuclear-recoil response and cut acceptance. Purity and position corrections must be measured, and selection fixed before unblinding the search region. neutrons can themselves produce single nuclear recoils, so discrimination is not perfect; material assays and transport simulations constrain this background. In the 224.6-live-day, 34-kg analysis, two candidate events were compatible with the expected 1.0±0.2 background events. The result constrained scattering cross sections rather than establishing a discovery.[ref]
A predicted count also depends on the local dark matter halo density, distribution of velocities, recoil threshold and nuclear response. Thus a null result constrains a particle model under stated astrophysical assumptions; it does not exclude all dark matter. A collider missing-momentum signal would likewise need independent evidence for a cosmological lifetime.
