When a cosmological phase transition breaks a symmetry G to a subgroup H, the degenerate vacuum configurations form a vacuum manifold M=G/H. Causally separated regions need not choose the same vacuum orientation. The Kibble mechanism explains how joining those choices can leave a field configuration that cannot be continuously deformed to a uniform vacuum without leaving M. The field then stores energy in a defect core, gradients or gauge fields.[ref]

A domain wall separates different disconnected vacua. A cosmic string has nonzero winding number on a loop around its core. A magnetic monopole has a nontrivial vacuum map on an enclosing sphere. Nontrivial π3​(M) can instead support cosmological texture configurations extending over a volume; cosmological textures can unwind dynamically by leaving the vacuum manifold and need not possess a permanently localized stable core. Nontrivial topology permits a defect; formation efficiency, interactions and the cosmological history still determine its abundance.
For example, a complex scalar with a circular set of minima can wind by 2πn around a cosmic string. Continuity forces the field away from its vacuum value near the core. A local gauge symmetry allows gauge fields to screen much of the exterior gradient and trap flux, whereas a global symmetry leaves long-range Goldstone-field gradients. Thus local and global cosmic strings have different energy-loss channels; they should not be treated as identical networks.
Relic defects can be tightly constrained because their energy dilutes differently from radiation. Nonrelativistic magnetic monopoles approximately dilute as a−3, so excessive production causes the cosmological monopole problem. A roughly frozen wall network has energy density proportional to a−1, even slower dilution. An evolving cosmic string network can approach a scaling regime with characteristic separation proportional to cosmic time and density of order μ/t2 in c=1 units, losing energy into loops and radiation. That is a dynamical network statement, not the dilution law of a fixed collection of cosmic strings.
cosmic inflation can dilute defects made before it; reheating and later symmetry breaking must not regenerate an unacceptable abundance. Observational tests include microwave-background anisotropy, gravitational lensing by cosmic strings, gravitational radiation from string loops, and searches for magnetic monopoles. Each is sensitive to model-dependent tensions, couplings and evolution. Defects are possible relics of symmetry breaking, not an inevitable dominant dark-matter population. Their absence constrains a transition and its subsequent history rather than every theory possessing a nontrivial vacuum topology.
