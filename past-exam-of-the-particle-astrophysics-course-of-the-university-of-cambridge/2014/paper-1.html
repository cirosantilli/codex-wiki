For a fixed comoving cell, take physical volume V=R3 and energy density u=ρc2. With negligible chemical potentials and fixed relativistic species, u=AT4 and P=u/3. The first law of thermodynamics gives

Choosing zero entropy at zero temperature yields S=4R3u/(3T). Reversible expansion conserves this entropy; when species disappear, the effective entropy degrees of freedom must be changed rather than keeping A fixed.

This assumes instantaneous decoupling, negligible neutrino masses/chemical potentials and complete electromagnetic thermalization. Small noninstantaneous corrections are outside this approximation.
In the Pati-Salam model, right-handed quarks and leptons fill a (4,1,2) multiplet. The neutrino component has no colour, TL​=0 and hypercharge Y=T3R​+(B−L)/2=1/2−1/2=0. Hence νR​ is a Standard Model gauge singlet neutrino, with no ordinary weak or electromagnetic gauge coupling. It is sterile under the low-energy Standard Model, not a singlet of the full unbroken Pati-Salam group. A heavy Majorana mass can arise after symmetry breaking. The representation alone fixes neither its mass nor the stability, mixing or relic abundance needed for dark matter.[ref]
Space example: AMS-02, a magnetic spectrometer on the International Space Station. Tracker curvature determines rigidity and charge sign; transition-radiation detection and an electromagnetic calorimeter distinguish electrons/positrons from the much larger proton background. It measures charged cosmic-ray spectra, including positrons that could arise from halo-particle annihilation. This is an indirect dark-matter search, despite directly recording the charged products: it does not measure scattering of neutral halo WIMPs, and astrophysical sources can mimic a positron excess. Calling it direct WIMP detection would misstate the technique.[ref]
Underground example: XENON100, a dual-phase xenon detector. A candidate WIMP scatters from a xenon nucleus, producing prompt liquid scintillation S1 and ionization electrons. Drift and extraction into gas produce delayed proportional scintillation S2. Their delay and light patterns reconstruct the interaction position; S2/S1 discriminates nuclear recoils from electron-recoil backgrounds. Underground shielding, fiducial cuts and calibration reduce backgrounds, though neutrons can imitate nuclear recoils. This is direct recoil detection; a null excess constrains cross sections for an assumed halo and interaction model.[ref][ref]

Type Ia supernovae are standardizable candles: light-curve shape and colour calibrate their luminosity. Distant events are fainter, hence have larger inferred luminosity distances, than appropriate matter-only decelerating models predict. Their redshift–distance relation favours recent accelerated expansion. In general relativity, sufficiently negative pressure, ρ+3P/c2<0, drives acceleration. Dust, evolution, calibration and selection are checked as possible systematics; a single faint supernova is not the argument.[ref]
An independent complementary test combines the cosmic microwave background acoustic distance/near-flat geometry with matter-density and baryon acoustic oscillation constraints. Matter alone does not supply the required late-time expansion history; a dark-energy component fits the joint data. CMB geometry alone has curvature/expansion degeneracies and is not an assumption-free detection of a cosmological constant.[ref]

a OurBigBook.com
Supersymmetry relates bosons and fermions whose spins differ by one-half. Broken supersymmetry can soften Higgs-mass sensitivity and improve gauge-coupling unification in suitable spectra; neither feature establishes that nature realizes it. Collider searches look for squarks/gluinos or electroweak superpartners through jets/leptons, missing transverse momentum and appropriate cascade-decay kinematics. Null searches constrain specified masses and branching fractions, with weaker reach for compressed or nonstandard spectra.
With conserved R-parity, superpartners are pair-produced and the lightest supersymmetric particle is stable. A neutral weakly coupled lightest state, such as a neutralino, can be a dark-matter candidate. Its thermal abundance follows annihilation freeze-out when nχ​⟨σv⟩∼H; later expansion dilutes number density while leaving the comoving relic abundance approximately fixed. Coannihilation and resonances can change the result. Recoil searches and cosmic-ray/photon searches test complementary couplings.
Cosmology also constrains unstable long-lived relics through nucleosynthesis and reheating; not every supersymmetric spectrum gives the observed dark matter. R-parity is an additional assumption: supersymmetry by itself does not guarantee a stable neutral relic. Laboratory discovery and a matching cosmological abundance would both be needed.

Quantum fluctuations stretched to cosmological scales seed density perturbations. Tests include the scalar spectral tilt, acoustic coherence, spatial curvature, non-Gaussianity and tensor-induced CMB B-mode polarization. Approximate flatness and near-scale-invariant fluctuations support the framework but do not uniquely determine a potential or prove an exact de Sitter phase.

c OurBigBook.com
Baryogenesis dynamically creates a baryon–antibaryon imbalance rather than assuming it as an initial condition. Under the usual local relativistic assumptions, the Sakharov conditions are baryon-number violation, C and CP violation, and departure from thermal equilibrium. Without the last condition, inverse reactions and equilibrium constraints cancel a net asymmetry; CP-conjugate rates must differ in a channel with the required number violation.
In heavy-boson decay baryogenesis, heavy particles decay through baryon-violating channels with CP-asymmetric branching. Expansion makes decays/inverse decays fall out of equilibrium; washout reactions must then become ineffective. Inflation would dilute an earlier asymmetry, so the generation/reheating history matters.
In leptogenesis, out-of-equilibrium Majorana-neutrino decays first produce lepton number. Electroweak sphalerons violate B+L while preserving B−L and convert part of the surviving asymmetry to baryon number. In the minimal Standard Model chemical-equilibrium limit, B=28(B−L)/79; the coefficient depends on particle content and equilibrated interactions. Electroweak baryogenesis is another possibility if CP violation and a sufficiently strong nonequilibrium phase transition are supplied. The Standard Model's observed parameters do not by themselves furnish an adequate conventional electroweak scenario. Each proposal must quantitatively match the relic asymmetry and avoid washout.
