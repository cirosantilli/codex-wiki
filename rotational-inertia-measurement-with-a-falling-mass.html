Rotational inertia measurement with a falling mass

OurBigBook.com Words: 498 Articles: 1
PASCO's PASPORT Rotary Motion Sensor Experiment Guide, Experiment 1, specifies a PS-2120A sensor, a rod carrying two equal-radius weights, a three-step drive wheel, a thread and hanging mass, a clamp-on guide wheel, a support, a balance and calipers. The thread leaves the drive wheel tangentially. The sensor records angular velocity against time; a linear regression over the accelerating interval supplies angular acceleration α. The guide measures the drive radius with calipers and repeats the run with the weights removed to subtract the apparatus's own moment of inertia. Its tables are blank: it prescribes a measurement and does not report an observed numerical agreement. [ref]
With hanging mass mh​ including its hanger, effective drive radius b, no thread slip and negligible guide-wheel inertia, Newton's second law gives thread tension F=mh​(g−bα). The axial torque is bF. Under negligible friction, the inference is therefore
Iloaded​=αmh​gb​−mh​b2.
(28)
Repeat the inference for the unloaded assembly and subtract: Iweights​=Iloaded​−Iempty​. Omitting the mh​b2 term incorrectly treats thread tension as the hanging weight. Comparing with (M1​+M2​)R2 tests the point mass approximation; finite-sized weights also have their own moments of inertia about their centres of mass. [ref]
Useful proposed checks go beyond that ideal inference. Verify the rotary encoder's angle scale by turning the shaft through a marked full revolution; check the recorded time against an independent clock and the balance against a known mass. Measure b at the thread's running surface, including thread thickness; avoid overlapping wraps that change b. Release without a push, inspect the angular velocity record for a straight interval, and stop before the hanger lands or the thread runs out. Repeat with several hanging masses, weight radii and rotation directions. A consistent inferred moment of inertia across drive torques supports the model; curvature, direction dependence or drift indicates a limitation rather than a new principal moment of inertia.
For approximately constant resisting torque τf​≥0 in one rotation direction, an original calibration model is
mh​gb−mh​b2α=Iα+τf​.
(29)
Fit the left-hand quantity against α across several drives: the slope estimates the axial moment of inertia and the intercept estimates τf​. This calibration is conditional on friction remaining constant over the fitted angular speeds; a freely slowing run can expose speed dependence. Sensor differentiation noise, guide-wheel moment of inertia, thread slip, flexing connectors and uncertain weight radii limit the inference. Removing weights can also change bearing loads, so empty-apparatus subtraction does not automatically cancel friction. These are proposed controls and a model extension, not results claimed by PASCO.
A fixed-axis readout measures nTIO​n, not the full inertia tensor. In a proposed generalization, repeat about x, y, z and the three diagonal directions (ex​+ey​)/2​, (ex​+ez​)/2​, (ey​+ez​)/2​, all through the same O. For example,
Ixy​=IO,(ex​+ey​)/2​​−2Ixx​+Iyy​​.
(30)
Six independent scalar readings then determine the six entries of a symmetric matrix. Each mounting needs its own apparatus correction, and different reference points require the tensor parallel-axis theorem before the readings can be combined.
