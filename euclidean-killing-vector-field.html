Euclidean Killing vector field (K(x)=A+Bx,BT=−B)

 Home Physics Branches of physics General relativity Killing vector field
OurBigBook.com Words: 229 Articles: 1
On Euclidean space, every Killing vector field is a constant translation generator plus a constant infinitesimal rotation generator:
K(x)=A+Bx,BT=−B.
(5)
Here A is a constant vector and B is an antisymmetric matrix. The second covariant derivative of a Killing vector vanishes for the Euclidean metric, so the components are affine functions of Cartesian coordinates; the Killing equation makes the linear coefficient antisymmetric.
In the Euclidean plane with metric tensor g=dx2+dy2, two useful examples are
Ktr​=∂x​,Krot​=−y∂x​+x∂y​.
(6)
The notation ∂x​ denotes a coordinate basis vector; dx denotes a differential one-form. The corresponding flow of a vector field maps are
Φstr​(x,y)=(x+s,y),Φsrot​(x,y)=(xcoss−ysins, xsins+ycoss).
(7)
For translation, all arrows have the same direction and length. For rotation, arrows are tangent to circles, have length x2+y2​, and vanish at the origin. The rotation flow keeps x2+y2 fixed, and its nonstationary integral curves of a vector field are circles. Both flows preserve the Euclidean metric. See Carroll's original notes, section 5, equations (5.45)-(5.46).
The circular integral curves of a vector field are symmetry orbits, whereas geodesics of the Euclidean plane are straight lines. A Killing vector field does not require its own orbits to be geodesics. The geodesic conserved quantity from a Killing vector pairs the field with the velocity of a separate geodesic; it does not require the velocity to equal the Killing vector field.
