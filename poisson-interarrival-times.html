Poisson interarrival times (Wn​=Tn​−Tn−1​∼Exp(λ))

 Home Mathematics Area of mathematics Probability and statistics Probability theory Poisson process
OurBigBook.com Words: 202
Let Tn​=inf{t≥0:Nt​≥n} be the nth arrival time, and set T0​=0. In a Homogeneous Poisson process of rate λ>0, the gaps Wn​=Tn​−Tn−1​ are independent and identically distributed random variables with an exponential distribution:
P(Wn​>w)=e−λw,fWn​​(w)=λe−λw,w≥0;E[Wn​]=λ1​,Var(Wn​)=λ21​.
(3)
Here Exp(λ) uses the rate convention. Under the scale convention θ=1/λ, the same probability density function is θ−1e−w/θ; a simulation function accepting a scale must receive 1/λ. Conversely, summing independent rate-λ exponential random variables to form Tn​ and counting those sums constructs a Homogeneous Poisson process.[ref]
The first-gap formula follows immediately from {W1​>w}={Nw​=0} and the Poisson distribution. Independence of all successive gaps is the additional interval-theorem assertion; deterministic-time independent increments alone should not simply be substituted at random arrival times without justification. The memorylessness of the exponential distribution gives
P(Wn​>a+w∣Wn​>a)=e−λw,a,w≥0.
(4)
At the toy rate of two per minute, the mean gap is half a minute, the median is (log2)/2≈0.346574 minutes, and the probability of waiting longer than half a minute is e−1≈0.367879. Here log is the natural logarithm. Already having waited a minute without an arrival leaves this same conditional probability of waiting another half minute. The exponential distribution describes a model with no aging of the remaining wait; a scheduled arrival with a fixed gap has no such property.
