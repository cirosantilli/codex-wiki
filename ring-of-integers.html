Ring of integers (OK​)

 Home Mathematics Area of mathematics Algebra Algebraic number theory Algebraic integer
OurBigBook.com Words: 748 Articles: 9
The ring of integers of a number field K is
OK​={α∈K:α is an algebraic integer}.
(9)
It is the integral closure of Z in K. Its additive group is a free abelian group of rank [K:Q], so an integral basis ω1​,…,ωn​ writes every element uniquely as ∑j​aj​ωj​ with aj​∈Z. Its field of fractions is K.[ref]
For K=Q, this gives OK​=Z; for K=Q(2​), it gives Z[2​]. The Gaussian integers give the corresponding example inside Q(i). The ring of integers is determined by the field, and need not equal Z[θ] for a chosen algebraic integer generator: in Q(5​), the larger ring Z[(1+5​)/2] contains Z[5​] with index two.
This is the setting for divisibility, global units, and prime ideal factorization. A ring of integers is a Dedekind domain, so every nonzero proper ideal factors uniquely into nonzero prime ideals, even when unique factorization of elements fails.[ref]
