Ising ring bond correlation (c1​=⟨σi​σi+1​⟩)

The Ising ring bond correlation is the neighboring two-point correlation function c1​=⟨σi​σi+1​⟩ in the four-spin Ising ring. All four edges are equivalent. At zero field each mean Ising spin is zero by spin inversion symmetry, so c1​ also equals the connected correlation function. Agreement gives bond product +1 and disagreement −1; thus P(σi​=σi+1​)=(1+c1​)/2.
Differentiating the finite partition function with respect to K=βJ inserts the sum of four bond products. Therefore
c1​=41​dKdlogZ​=cosh(4K)+3sinh(4K)​,U=⟨H⟩=−4Jc1​.
(8)
Here U is internal energy. At K=(ln2)/4, the class-weight calculation gives c1​=(4−1)/17=3/17 and U=−12J/17. The agreement probability for a selected bond is 10/17, despite the exactly zero mean spin magnetization.
A second derivative gives the energy variance, and hence the heat capacity CH​=dU/dT at fixed J and h=0:
J2Var(H)​=dK2d2logZ​=[cosh(4K)+3]216[1+3cosh(4K)]​,kB​CH​​=K2J2Var(H)​.
(9)
To see the variance identity directly, a derivative of an expected value under weights eKS inserts S−⟨S⟩, where S=∑i​σi​σi+1​=−H/J. The factor K2 follows from dK/dT=−K/T. At the same exact example, Var(H)/J2=1216/289. The heat capacity vanishes both as K→0 and as K→∞: at high temperature its dimensionless factor K2 vanishes, while at low temperature excited microstates become exponentially rare.
