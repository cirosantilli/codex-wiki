Graph subdivision (TH)

 Home Mathematics Area of mathematics Foundations of mathematics Graph theory
OurBigBook.com Words: 301 Articles: 3
A graph subdivision TH replaces each edge uv of a simple graph H by a graph path of at least one edge joining its original endpoints. The original vertices are branch vertices of a graph subdivision. The replacement graph paths have pairwise disjoint interiors, contain no branch vertices of a graph subdivision internally, and together make up the entire new graph. Leaving an edge unchanged is allowed.[ref]
For an explicit example, take H with vertex set {A,B,C,D} and edges AB,BC,CA,AD: a triangle in a graph with one attached leaf of a graph. Replace its edges by the following branch paths of a graph subdivision:
PAB​=(A,x,B),PBC​=(B,y,z,C),PCA​=(C,A),PAD​=(A,D).
(128)
The resulting graph subdivision S has vertex set {A,B,C,D,x,y,z} and edges Ax,xB,By,yz,zC,CA,AD. The internal vertex sets are {x},{y,z},∅,∅, so disjointness is explicit. The degrees of vertices of A,B,C,D remain 3,2,2,1, respectively; the three new internal vertices each have degree of a vertex two.
Adding m internal vertices to a finite graph increases both its vertex count and its edge count by m. Here m=3, so four vertices and four edges become seven of each; the graph excess is unchanged. Each replacement graph path remains a route between the same junctions. Thus graph subdivision preserves whether a graph is a planar graph: insert intermediate vertices along a crossing-free drawing, or trace each replacement graph path as one edge to recover a drawing of the original graph. Travel times or capacities are separate modelling choices and are not determined by this adjacency construction.
