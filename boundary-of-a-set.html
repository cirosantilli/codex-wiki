Boundary of a set (∂A=A∖A∘)

 Home Mathematics Area of mathematics Geometry and topology Topology Topological space
OurBigBook.com Words: 308 Articles: 3
The boundary of a subset A of a topological space X consists of the points whose every neighbourhood in X meets both A and its complement X∖A. Equivalently,
∂X​A=clX​A∖intX​A=clX​A∩clX​(X∖A).
(6)
Here the two operations are closure and interior. The second formula shows that the boundary is a closed set and that A and its complement have the same boundary. It locates precisely the points where membership cannot be decided from a small neighbourhood alone. [ref]
For A=[0,1) in the usual real line, the interior is (0,1), the closure is [0,1], and the boundary is {0,1}. In particular, 0 belongs both to A and to its boundary, while 1 belongs to the boundary but not to A. A boundary point need not be excluded from the original set: open sets exclude all their boundary points, closed sets contain all theirs, and this half-open interval does one of each.
The upper panel visualizes the relative example from open set: for X=[0,1] and A=[0,1/2), an open ball about zero in X is BX​(0,r)=X∩(−r,r)=[0,r) when 0<r<1/2. This open ball stays in A; points outside X are not required. The middle panel shows that X is clopen in itself, with interior and closure both X and empty boundary, although its boundary in the real line is {0,1}. The lower panel returns to ambient space R and separates the interior, closure, and boundary of [0,1); both marked boundary points are included in the boundary itself.
