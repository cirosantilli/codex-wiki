The Identity for Dirichlet convolution is the arithmetic function

For every arithmetic function f, it satisfies f∗ε=ε∗f=f. Indeed, in (f∗ε)(n)=∑d∣n​f(d)ε(n/d), only d=n survives. For example,
(f∗ε)(12)=f(1)0+f(2)0+f(3)0+f(4)0+f(6)0+f(12)1=f(12).
(62)
At n=1 the sole term is f(1)ε(1)=f(1). This gives a useful way to check Dirichlet convolution calculations: inserting ε leaves every value unchanged. The three arithmetic functions 1, id, and ε all take value 1 at 1, but at 12 the constant-one arithmetic function, the identity arithmetic function, and the Identity for Dirichlet convolution take values 1, 12, and 0, respectively. The Möbius function is the inverse of the constant-one arithmetic function under Dirichlet convolution, meaning μ∗1=1∗μ=ε. The Möbius divisor-sum identity gives a short proof: for n>1 the nonzero terms expand ∏p∣n​(1−1)=0, while at 1 the sum is μ(1)=1. At 12, the computation is
(μ∗1)(12)=μ(1)+μ(2)+μ(3)+μ(4)+μ(6)+μ(12)=1−1−1+0+1+0=0=ε(12).
(63)
The zeros occur because 4 and 12 are divisible by the square of the prime number 2. Consequently, if F=f∗1 is a divisor sum, then associativity gives F∗μ=f∗(1∗μ)=f∗ε=f: this is Möbius inversion.
