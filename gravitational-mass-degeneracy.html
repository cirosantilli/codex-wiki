Gravitational mass degeneracy (μ=GM)

 ... Physics Branches of physics Classical mechanics Newton's law of universal gravitation Newtonian gravitational field Gravitational constant
OurBigBook.com Words: 177
An orbit around an isolated source constrains its standard gravitational parameter μ=GM rather than independently measuring the source's mass and the gravitational constant. In the test particle approximation, a circular orbit of radius a and orbital period T satisfies
T24π2a​=a2GM​,μ=T24π2a3​.
(24)
The left equation equates the required centripetal acceleration to the gravitational acceleration. More generally, a Kepler orbit depends on the same μ. In the exact relative two-body problem, replace GM by G(M+m).
For any positive dimensionless number q, replacing G by qG and M by M/q leaves GM and every predicted test particle trajectory unchanged. This is the gravitational mass degeneracy: more observations of the same orbit can refine GM without separating its factors. A laboratory source whose mass is calibrated independently supplies the missing information.
Consequently an astronomical mass expressed in kilograms as M=μ/G inherits measurement uncertainty from G, even if μ is known much more precisely. To first order,
MδM​=μδμ​−GδG​.
(25)
When the relative standard uncertainty of μ is negligible, that of M is approximately u(G)/G. Conversely, the ratio of two source masses inferred using the same G is M1​/M2​=μ1​/μ2​, so this common factor cancels. That distinction explains how excellent orbit determination can coexist with less precise absolute astronomical masses.
