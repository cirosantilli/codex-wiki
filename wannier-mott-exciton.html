Wannier-Mott exciton (aB∗​,R∗)

 Home Physics Branches of physics Quantum mechanics Quasiparticle Exciton
OurBigBook.com Words: 331 Articles: 2
A Wannier-Mott exciton has an internal wave function extending over many unit cells. Its simplest model assumes a three-dimensional bulk crystal, approximately parabolic valence band and conduction band, positive isotropic scalar effective masses me∗​,mh∗​, a constant scalar relative permittivity ϵr​, and a dilute pair population. Replace microscopic crystal forces by those effective masses and dielectric screening, and neglect exchange corrections and coupling to phonons. These assumptions must hold on the spatial and energy scales of the particular exciton.
With relative coordinate r=re​−rh​, the internal Hamiltonian operator is the three-dimensional hydrogen atom problem with a reduced mass and a screened Coulomb potential:
Hrel​=−2μℏ2​∇r2​−4πϵ0​ϵr​re2​,μ=me∗​+mh∗​me∗​mh∗​​,M=me∗​+mh∗​.
(1)
Here e is the elementary charge, ϵ0​ the vacuum permittivity, and ℏ the reduced Planck constant. The internal energy eigenvalues, radius scale, and exciton binding energies are
aB∗​=μe24πϵ0​ϵr​ℏ2​,R∗=2(4πϵ0​ϵr​)2ℏ2μe4​,Erel,n​=−n2R∗​,EB,n​=n2R∗​.
(2)
The principal quantum number is n=1,2,…. This reduction and these scales are derived in Excitons in Semiconductor Quantum Dots by Matjaž Humar (§3, printed pp. 5–6, equations (13)–(26)).
To check the scales by dimensional analysis, write C=e2/(4πϵ0​ϵr​), which has units of energy times length. Then aB∗​=ℏ2/(μC) is a length and R∗=μC2/(2ℏ2) is an energy. Rescaling r=aB∗​ρ makes Hrel​/R∗=−∇ρ2​−2/ρ, the dimensionless hydrogen atom operator. The Bohr radius aB∗​ is a wave function scale, not the radius of a classical orbit; for this model's lowest internal bound state, the mean separation is 3aB∗​/2.
For the diagrams' hypothetical inputs me∗​=0.10m0​, mh∗​=0.40m0​, ϵr​=10 and Eg​=1.500eV, where m0​ is the free Electron mass, independent substitution gives μ=0.080m0​, aB∗​≃6.615nm and R∗≃10.885meV. Here electronvolts express energy. The exciton binding energies for n=1,2,3 are approximately 10.885, 2.721 and 1.209meV. These are model calculations, not measured material parameters. The large-radius approximation additionally requires aB∗​ to be much larger than the crystal's unit cell length scale. In a thin layer, with anisotropic effective masses, or with spatially varying dielectric screening, this three-dimensional formula is not generally valid.
