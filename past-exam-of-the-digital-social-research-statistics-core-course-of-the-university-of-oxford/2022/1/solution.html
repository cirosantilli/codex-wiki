Let μU​ and μV​ be the population means of the newspaper trust score for the unvaccinated and vaccinated groups. A two-sided hypothesis test compares
H0​:μU​−μV​=0,H1​:μU​−μV​=0.
(1)
The observed difference of sample means is 5.5−6.5=−1: the vaccinated group has a one-unit higher average trust score. The negative test statistic agrees with subtraction in this order. A Student's t-test scales the estimated difference by its estimated standard error; t=−3.6 means that the observed difference is 3.6 estimated standard errors below the equal-mean null hypothesis.
Using the reported reference Student t-distribution with 747 statistical degrees of freedom,
p=2P(T747​≥3.6)≈0.000339.​
(2)
Thus the equal-mean null hypothesis is rejected at a prespecified 5%, 1% or 0.1% significance level, subject to the test's assumptions. A p-value is a probability of a result at least this extreme under the specified null hypothesis and sampling model. It is neither the probability that the null hypothesis is true nor the probability that this particular conclusion is mistaken. A chosen significance level controls the procedure's type I error probability under that model.
The estimated difference is more informative than statistical significance alone. If the reported statistic uses this one-unit contrast, its estimated standard error is 1/3.6≈0.2778. The corresponding approximate 95% confidence interval is
−1±t747,0.975​(0.2778)≈[−1.545,−0.455].
(3)
This interval concerns a population mean difference under repeated sampling; it is not a range containing 95% of individual trust scores. Its practical interpretation also depends on the trust scale and the importance of a one-unit difference.
The groups are disjoint, so an independent-samples comparison is appropriate if the sampled observations are independent. The exact pooled Student's t-test additionally assumes common population variance and normal distributions; Welch's t-test permits unequal variances and uses an approximate reference distribution. A bounded or ordinal rating need not be exactly normal, so treating it numerically and using a large-sample approximation also requires judgment about group sizes and the score's meaning.
There is a bookkeeping qualification. The later outputs use 748 observations. If these are also the complete observations for this two-group comparison, the pooled test has 748−2=746 statistical degrees of freedom, and the Welch degrees of freedom upper bound is also 746. The printed 747 therefore needs the original test call or a different sample count to reconcile it. The reported-calibration calculation above answers the stated test; it does not invent group sizes or silently change the reference distribution.
The evidence supports an association between higher newspaper trust and recorded vaccination, rather than a causal effect of trust. An observational study does not rule out confounding, selection bias or reverse causation. A statistical hypothesis test cannot supply those missing causal assumptions.
