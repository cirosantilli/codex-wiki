Greater technology distrust is associated with lower efficacy in the overall sample. The omnibus evidence against independence is strong, while the unsigned association magnitude is modest: X2≈107.727 on 6 statistical degrees of freedom, p≈6.10×10−21 and Cramér's V about 0.148. The observational study does not establish that changing trust would cause efficacy to change.
Rows identify the technology response and columns identify efficacy. Each body percentage is a within-row conditional probability estimate, not a column percentage. For example, 166/336=49.40% of those who strongly disagree with the distrust statement have High efficacy, whereas 199/718=27.72% of those who agree do. The difference is about 21.69 percentage points. High efficacy decreases across all four rows: 49.40%, 42.41%, 31.02%, 27.72%. Low efficacy is 20.54% in the strongly-disagree row, 18.00% in Disagree, 21.43% in Neutral and 34.26% in Agree. Thus the overall pattern is clear without claiming that every category changes monotonically: the low-efficacy proportion initially falls, and the Medium proportion peaks at 47.55% among neutral respondents.
For the Pearson chi-squared test of independence, the null hypothesis says that the two categorical variables are independent, equivalently that the efficacy distribution is the same in every technology-response row. With row totals ni+​ and column totals n+j​, the expected frequency under that hypothesis is Eij​=ni+​n+j​/N, and
X2=∑i,j​Eij​(Oij​−Eij​)2​,df=(4−1)(3−1)=6.

All twelve expected frequencies exceed 79, with minimum about 79.844, so sparse expected cells are not the concern here. Under independent sampling and the usual large-sample calibration, the very small p-value rejects independence at conventional significance levels. This is an omnibus finding: it does not provide a separate adjusted significance test for every pair of row percentages.[ref]
The likelihood-ratio test of independence in a contingency table uses G2=2∑Oij​>0​Oij​log(Oij​/Eij​). The supplied G2≈105.146 with 6 statistical degrees of freedom gives p≈2.11×10−20. Its displayed 0.000 is rounded, not an exact zero. It corroborates the omnibus finding. The generalized phi coefficient is X2/N​≈0.209, the contingency coefficient is about 0.205, and Cramér's V is X2/[Nmin(3,2)]​≈0.148. These are unsigned measures of association strength rather than three additional significance tests; they do not describe causal effects or percentages of variance explained. Their magnitude and the small p-value answer different questions, especially with a large sample size.
The ordinal measures use the displayed order: increasing row index means increasing distrust, while increasing column index means decreasing efficacy. There are 882033 concordant pairs and 552543 discordant pairs. Hence Goodman and Kruskal's gamma is
γ=882033+552543882033−552543​≈0.230.
(5)
Its positive sign therefore means that increasing distrust tends to accompany movement toward lower efficacy. Under an ascending efficacy order its sign would be negative, by sign reversal under reversal of one ordinal ordering. Goodman and Kruskal's gamma ignores tied pairs; it is not a claim that 23% of efficacy variation is explained. The displayed pair counts partition all (22466​)=3039345 unordered pairs correctly, including ties. Somers' D for efficacy given technology is about 0.151, Kendall's tau-b about 0.158, and Stuart's tau-c about 0.163. Their different denominators handle ties or table dimensions differently, so their differing magnitudes do not constitute disagreement about the direction.[ref][ref]
Goodman and Kruskal's lambda concerns the proportional reduction in errors when predicting a most frequent category, not ordinal movement. Without technology information the modal efficacy category is Medium, producing 2466−972=1494 errors. Choosing a separate modal efficacy category in each technology row yields 2466−(166+391+233+273)=1403 errors. Thus λefficacy∣technology​=91/1494≈0.061. In the reverse prediction direction the reduction is 80/1544≈0.052, matching the other printed lambda. The improvement in modal classification is small even though independence is decisively rejected. Both the conditional percentages and these distinct summaries support a real sample association; ethnicity and other possible confounders still require attention before any causal account.
