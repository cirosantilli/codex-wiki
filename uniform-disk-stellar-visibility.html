Uniform-disk stellar visibility (γ(B)=2J1​(x)/x,x=πBθ/λ)

 ... Physics Branches of physics Quantum theory Quantum optics Hanbury Brown and Twiss effect Stellar intensity interferometry
OurBigBook.com Words: 410 Articles: 1
Stipulate a distant circular source of uniform angular brightness, full angular diameter θ≪1 in radians, and narrow optical bandwidth around wavelength λ. Distinct source patches emit independently. Assume each collector is small enough not to resolve the disk individually. For a sky offset α from the disk centre, the baseline-dependent phase difference is 2πb⊥​⋅α/λ, up to channel-sign convention. Summing independent patch contributions makes the equal-delay first-order optical coherence the normalized Fourier transform of angular brightness:
γ(B)=πa21​∫∣α∣≤a​ei2πb⊥​⋅α/λd2α,a=2θ​.
(15)
Choose the horizontal coordinate along b⊥​, put k=2πB/λ, and integrate along vertical chords of the disk:
γ(B)=πa22​∫−aa​a2−u2​eikudu=ka2J1​(ka)​=x2J1​(x)​,x=λπBθ​.
(16)
The imaginary part cancels between opposite chords. Here J1​ is the order-one Bessel function of the first kind. One way to obtain the last equality is to integrate the exponential's power series term by term on the finite disk. Equivalently the result is specified by the convergent series
J1​(x)=∑m=0∞​m!(m+1)!(−1)m(x/2)2m+1​,γ(B)=1−8x2​+192x4​+O(x6).
(17)
At B=0 take the continuous limit γ=1; V2=∣γ∣2 starts as 1−x2/4+O(x4). This is why very short interferometric baselines give little leverage on angular diameter. Larger disks lose spatial optical coherence at shorter baselines.
The first positive zero of J1​ is x1​≈3.831705970, so
B0​=πθx1​λ​≈θ1.21967λ​,θ≈B0​1.21967λ​.
(18)
This is a full diameter convention. The angle must be converted before substitution: one arcsecond is π/648000 radians, and one milliarcsecond, abbreviated mas, is 10−3 arcseconds. For the stipulated θ=5mas and λ=500nm, θ=2.4240684×10−8rad and B0​≈25.1575m. At B=10m, the model gives x≈1.52309 and V2≈0.542783.
Beyond B0​, γ changes sign but V2 stays nonnegative and develops weak further lobes. A single measured squared visibility can therefore match several angular diameters; sampling several interferometric baselines constrains the fit. Limb darkening, spectral averaging, or resolved collector apertures change the uniform-disk prediction. A uniform-disk diameter is a parameter of that brightness model; converting it to a physical stellar radius also requires distance and an appropriate brightness profile.
