Order of an element of a finite cyclic group (ord(k)=n/gcd(n,k))

 ... Area of mathematics Algebra Group theory Group Cyclic group Finite cyclic group
OurBigBook.com Words: 127
For a positive integer n and a congruence class k in the additive finite cyclic group Z/nZ, the order of a group element is
ord(k)=gcd(n,k)n​.
(6)
Indeed, write d=gcd(n,k), n=dn′ and k=dk′. Returning to zero after m steps means n∣mk, equivalently n′∣mk′. Since n′ and k′ are coprime, this holds exactly when n′∣m; the least positive such m is n′. This argument also covers k=0, since gcd(n,0)=n.
For n=6, the orders of 0,1,2,3,4,5 are respectively 1,6,3,2,3,6. The translation in a group x↦x+k has the same period from every starting element. Consequently its cycles each have length n/d and there are d of them. They are exactly the cosets of the cyclic subgroup generated by k, which explains both the lengths and the number of components in the picture.
