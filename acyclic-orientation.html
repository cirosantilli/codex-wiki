Acyclic orientation

 Home Mathematics Area of mathematics Foundations of mathematics Graph theory Orientation of a graph
OurBigBook.com Words: 359 Articles: 1
An acyclic orientation is an orientation of a graph whose resulting directed graph has no directed cycle, hence is a Directed acyclic graph. A directed cycle must follow every arrow around its closed route. A cycle in a graph in the skeleton of a directed graph only requires the corresponding undirected edges; their arrows need not agree around the route.
For the same triangle in a graph with vertex set {A,B,C} and edges AB,BC,AC, compare
D1​: A→B, B→C, A→C,D2​: A→B, B→C, C→A.
(10)
Both have an undirected cycle in a graph in their skeleton of a directed graph. Only D2​ has a directed cycle. In D1​, C has no outgoing directed edge, so a route cannot continue from C back to A.
Thus an acyclic orientation does not require its underlying undirected graph to be a forest. Every orientation of a graph of a forest is acyclic, because a directed cycle would give an undirected cycle in a graph. Conversely, every finite simple graph, including one with many cycles in a graph, admits at least one acyclic orientation by the following ordering construction. On this triangle in a graph, six of the eight orientations of a graph are acyclic; the other two follow the directed cycle in opposite senses.
