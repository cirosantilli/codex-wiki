Hardy's paradox

 Home Physics Branches of physics Quantum theory Foundations of quantum mechanics Bell theorem
OurBigBook.com Words: 76
A two-setting nonlocality argument in which several zero-probability events forbid a further coincidence in any deterministic local hidden-variable model, but quantum theory assigns that coincidence positive probability. For binary indicators, suppose C+R​C−R​=0, D+I​=1⇒C−R​=1 and D−I​=1⇒C+R​=1. Any assignment with D+I​D−I​=1 would violate the first relation. A quantum implementation can nevertheless give such a coincidence. Real imperfect experiments require an inequality that accounts for their nonzero error probabilities. Lucien Hardy gave a two-particle inequality-free argument in 1992.[ref]
