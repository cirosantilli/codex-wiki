Trace distance between qubit states (D(ρ,σ)=∣r−s∣/2)

 Home Physics Branches of physics Quantum theory Density matrix Trace distance
OurBigBook.com Words: 173
For qubit density operators, the trace distance has a particularly simple geometry. Write their Bloch vectors as r,s in the Bloch ball:
ρ=2I+r⋅σ​,σ=2I+s⋅σ​,Δ=ρ−σ=2(r−s)⋅σ​,
(12)
where I is the identity operator and σ=(σx​,σy​,σz​) denotes the Pauli matrices. This Bloch vector representation includes both pure states on the Bloch sphere and mixed states in the interior of the Bloch ball. [ref]
The Pauli matrix multiplication law gives [(r−s)⋅σ]2=∣r−s∣2I. Hence the two eigenvalues of Δ are ±∣r−s∣/2, and their absolute sum gives
D(ρ,σ)=2∣r−s∣​.
(13)
The trace distance is half the straight-line Euclidean distance between the Bloch vectors, not the angular or surface distance along the Bloch sphere. Opposite Bloch vectors have trace distance one; the centre I/2 is at trace distance 1/2 from every pure state.
For the pure states ∣0⟩ and ∣+⟩=(∣0⟩+∣1⟩)/2​, the Bloch vectors are r=(0,0,1) and s=(1,0,0). Their straight-line separation is 2​, so
D(∣0⟩⟨0∣,∣+⟩⟨+∣)=2​1​.
(14)
These Bloch vectors are perpendicular, but the pure states are not orthogonal: ∣⟨0∣+⟩∣=1/2​. The Helstrom measurement for the zero and plus states measures along the difference direction n=(r−s)/2​=(−1,0,1)/2​, rather than along either input Bloch vector.
