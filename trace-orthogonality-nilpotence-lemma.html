Trace orthogonality nilpotence lemma (tr(αM)=0 ⟹ α nilpotent)

 Home Mathematics Area of mathematics Algebra Linear algebra Matrix trace
OurBigBook.com Words: 95
Let U⊆W⊆EndC​(V) be vector subspaces and M={T:[T,W]⊆U}. If α∈M and tr(αβ)=0 for all β∈M, then α is a nilpotent endomorphism.
To prove it, let β act by λ on the generalized eigenspace of α of eigenvalue λ. Polynomial interpolation on eigenvalue differences, together with adjoint compatibility of additive Jordan decomposition, expresses adβ as a polynomial in adα with zero constant term. Since adα maps W into U and preserves U, this gives β∈M. Taking the matrix trace on each generalized eigenspace yields 0=∑λ​dim(Vλ​)∣λ∣2, so every eigenvalue is zero. This is the linear-algebra step behind the Cartan solvability criterion; U,W need not be Lie subalgebras.
