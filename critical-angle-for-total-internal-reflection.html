Critical angle for total internal reflection (θc​)

For refractive indices n−​>n+​, the critical angle for total internal reflection satisfies sinθc​=n+​/n−​. Above it, the transmitted normal wavenumber is imaginary and the transmitted field is an evanescent wave.
The critical angle for total internal reflection is the boundary between outgoing refraction and an evanescent wave, rather than an angle at which the external electric field abruptly disappears. Write n1​=n−​ and n2​=n+​. At the threshold, Snell law for electromagnetic waves has θt​=90∘, hence
θc​=arcsin(n1​n2​​),0<n2​<n1​.
(30)
Here inverse sine selects 0<θc​<90∘. For n1​=1.5 and n2​=1, sinθc​=2/3 and θc​≈41.8103149∘. The threshold is independent of polarization of an electromagnetic wave under the stated assumptions.
A short proof using phase matching at a planar wave interface reveals all three cases at once. Choose a Cartesian coordinate system with boundary z=0, incident medium z<0, and transmitted medium z>0. With vacuum wavenumber k0​=2π/λ0​, the shared tangential wavevector component is q=n1​k0​sinθ. The dispersion relation in medium two gives
k2z2​=n22​k02​−q2=k02​(n22​−n12​sin2θ).
(31)
Below θc​, choose the positive real square root for an outgoing plane electromagnetic wave. At θc​, k2z​=0: there is a grazing wave, with zero normal average power but no exponential confinement in z. Above θc​, choose k2z​=iκ with κ>0, so the wave amplitude decays into the second half-space.
If n1​≤n2​, no nongrazing angle of incidence produces this threshold. As n2​/n1​→1 from below, θc​→90∘; as that ratio tends to zero, θc​→0. These limits explain why a larger refractive index contrast permits confinement over a wider range of internal angles.
