Shape operator (Sp​)

 Home Mathematics Area of mathematics Geometry and topology Differential geometry Second fundamental form
OurBigBook.com Words: 713 Articles: 6
The shape operator measures how the unit normal turns as we move along a smooth surface. A plane has a constant unit normal, whereas the outward unit normal of a sphere turns in every direction in the tangent plane. Keeping the direction of this change, rather than just its magnitude, lets one recover the principal curvatures and their principal directions.
Choose an orientation of a surface M⊂R3, with Gauss map N:M→S2. The shape operator at p is the linear map
Sp​=−dNp​:Tp​M→Tp​M.
(15)
Here dNp​ is the differential of the Gauss map. Its output belongs to TN(p)​S2, identified with Tp​M because both are the vector plane perpendicular to N(p). The minus sign is our convention throughout this branch. [ref]
The shape operator packages the second fundamental form as an operator using the inner product supplied by the first fundamental form:
II(v,w)=I(Sp​v,w).
(16)
The symmetry of the second fundamental form makes the shape operator a self-adjoint operator for this inner product. Thus an orthonormal basis of principal directions diagonalizes it. In a coordinate basis Xu​,Xv​ of a parametric surface, write
A=(EF​FG​),B=(ef​fg​),[S]=A−1B.
(17)
The matrix [S] need not be a symmetric matrix in this coordinate basis: its self-adjointness means A[S]=[S]TA=B. Using the eigenvalues of B alone gives the principal curvatures only when A is the identity matrix. [ref]
For a worked example, parametrize the lateral surface of an outward-oriented circular cylinder of radius R>0 using circumferential arc length u and axial coordinate v:
X(u,v)=(Rcos(u/R),Rsin(u/R),v),N(u,v)=(cos(u/R),sin(u/R),0).
(18)
The coordinate tangent vectors are an orthonormal basis. Since Nu​=Xu​/R and Nv​=0, the shape operator satisfies
S(Xu​)=−R1​Xu​,S(Xv​)=0,A=(10​01​),B=(−1/R0​00​).
(19)
Changing to angular coordinate φ=u/R gives A=diag(R2,1) and B=diag(−R,0); A−1B still has eigenvalues −1/R,0. This is why the first fundamental form must participate in the calculation.
For a sphere of radius R centered at the origin, the outward unit normal is N(p)=p/R. Hence Sp​v=−v/R, so both principal curvatures are −1/R. For a plane, dN=0 and the shape operator vanishes. Reversing the unit normal negates the shape operator and both principal curvatures; the Gaussian curvature remains unchanged while the mean curvature changes sign, as in orientation reversal of surface curvature.
