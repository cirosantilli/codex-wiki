Liquefaction enthalpy budget (y=(hg​−hin​−q)/(hg​−hl​))

 ... Branches of physics Statistical physics Thermodynamics Temperature Cryogenics Helium liquefaction
OurBigBook.com Words: 465 Articles: 1
Consider an original model of a cold section enclosing a throttle and collecting region, with the precooling heat exchanger outside its boundary. The inlet has mass flow rate m˙>0 and specific enthalpy hin​. The outlets are equilibrium liquid and vapor at one fixed pressure, with specific enthalpies hl​ and hg​. Let y be the outlet liquid mass fraction, and let q=Q˙​/m˙≥0 be incoming unwanted heat per unit incoming mass. Assume steady flow, no shaft work, and negligible bulk kinetic energy and gravitational potential energy changes. The steady-flow energy balance gives
hin​+q=yhl​+(1−y)hg​,L=hg​−hl​>0,y=Lhg​−hin​−q​.
(30)
Here L is the latent heat per unit mass at the chosen outlet pressure. The mass flow rates of the outlets are ym˙ and (1−y)m˙. The formula describes a flowing cold section; a filling vessel with appreciable energy or mass accumulation requires storage terms.
For a deliberately dimensionless example, choose
η=Lhin​−hl​​=0.70,ℓ=Lq​=0.05,y=1−η−ℓ=0.25.
(31)
Per unit incoming mass, one quarter leaves as liquid and three quarters as vapor. With zero incoming heat, the same inlet would give y=0.30. Increasing q/L to 0.20 reduces y to 0.10, and q/L=0.30 reaches the no-liquid boundary. These are chosen model values, not helium property data or a historical yield. In normalized enthalpy units with hl​=0 and hg​=1, conservation checks directly: 0.70+0.05=0.25×0+0.75×1.
The admissible interval is hl​≤hin​+q≤hg​, or 0≤y≤1. Outside it, the assumed equilibrium two-phase outlet fails: an enthalpy above hg​ requires a hotter single-vapor outlet under these assumptions, rather than a negative amount of liquid. Below hl​, a colder liquid or a different thermodynamic phase must be considered. The balance does not predict its own outlet pressure, temperature or helium property functions.
At fixed outlet pressure, hence fixed L, the derivatives are ∂y/∂hin​=−1/L and ∂y/∂q=−1/L. Lowering inlet specific enthalpy by regeneration and reducing incoming heat therefore buy the same increase in liquid mass fraction per unit energy per unit mass in this model. Adding any constant to all three specific enthalpies leaves y unchanged. This independence of the arbitrary enthalpy reference makes the balance useful even when property tables choose different zero points.
