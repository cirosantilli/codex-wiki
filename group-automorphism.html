Group automorphism (α:G∼​G)

 Home Mathematics Area of mathematics Algebra Morphism Automorphism
OurBigBook.com Words: 345 Articles: 2
A group automorphism is a bijective group homomorphism α:G→G. It preserves the group operation, the identity element, and inverse elements; its inverse is another group homomorphism. Thus it is a group isomorphism with identical source and target. These maps form the automorphism group Aut(G) under function composition.[ref]
For the additive finite cyclic group C6​, take f5​(k)=5k(mod6). Its output list on 0,1,2,3,4,5 is 0,5,4,3,2,1, a permutation of all six elements. It preserves addition because 5(a+b)=5a+5b(mod6), and applying it twice gives 25k=k(mod6). Hence it is a group automorphism and its own inverse function. Compare the collapsing f2​ in the figure under endomorphism. The general criterion under endomorphism of a finite cyclic group says exactly when such a map is reversible: its multiplier must be coprime to the size of the finite cyclic group.
A permutation need not be a group automorphism, even if it fixes the identity element. Let π exchange 1 and 2 in C6​ and fix 0,3,4,5. It is a bijection, and π(0)=0, but
π(1+1)=π(2)=1,π(1)+π(1)=2+2=4(mod6).
(10)
Thus it fails to preserve addition and is not even a group homomorphism. Reversibility alone does not preserve the group operation.
A group automorphism carries each cyclic subgroup onto the cyclic subgroup generated by the image of its defining element, preserving the order of a group element. In particular, it carries a generator of a group to another generator of a group. For C6​, the existing calculation of generators of a finite cyclic group leaves only the images 1 and 5 for 1, so its automorphism group consists exactly of the identity function and f5​. This turns the problem of compatible relabeling into a much smaller choice than enumerating arbitrary permutations.
