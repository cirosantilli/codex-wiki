Optical pumping preparation and readout

 Home Physics Atomic physics Atomic measurement methods Optical pumping
OurBigBook.com Words: 133
Circular polarization selects the sign of the absorbed photon's angular momentum along the beam. Choose that direction as z and define σ+ by the absorption rule m′=m+1, where m is the magnetic quantum number. Spontaneous emission can return an atom to a different magnetic sublevel, so absorption followed by emission is a population-transfer cycle. A sublevel that the chosen beam cannot excite is dark to that beam. Darkness is relative to the selected transition and electromagnetic polarization, rather than a claim that the atom cannot interact with any light.
A static magnetic field along z defines a stable axis and gives Zeeman splitting. A transverse oscillating magnetic field can redistribute magnetic sublevel populations when its frequency matches an allowed splitting. With the appropriate optical transition, that redistribution restores photon absorption: transmitted intensity decreases, or collected fluorescence increases. These are alternative readouts whose detailed sensitivity depends on the transition, viewing direction and electromagnetic polarization.
