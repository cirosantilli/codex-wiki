In the ideal electric-dipole interaction, a vibrational transition needs a nonzero matrix element of the electric dipole moment. For a diatomic bond stretch, expand the body-axis dipole component as d(q)=de​+de′​q+⋯. A changing electric dipole moment, rather than a permanent value de​ alone, permits the harmonic fundamental. Keeping only the linear term and harmonic wavefunctions gives Δv=±1.[ref]
The derivation is sharper than a picture of a shaking molecule. Orthogonality removes the constant term for v′=v, and creation and annihilation operators give
⟨v′∣q∣v⟩=2μωℏ​​(v+1​δv′,v+1​+v​δv′,v−1​).
(31)
Here δ is the Kronecker delta. Thus absorption raises v by one when the dipole derivative and angular factor are nonzero, and its photon satisfies
hνγ​=Ev+1​−Ev​=ℏω,νγ​=hcEv+1​−Ev​​=νharm​.
(32)
A nonzero vibrational matrix element is only one factor: rotational electric-dipole selection rules, initial populations, and spectral resolution also constrain visibility.
For an isolated diatomic molecule with identical atomic nuclei, such as molecular hydrogen in its symmetric electronic ground state, stretching preserves zero electric dipole moment. By symmetry, this homonuclear stretch is infrared inactive in the ideal dipole model, although it still vibrates. Other interactions or environments can permit detection.[ref]
