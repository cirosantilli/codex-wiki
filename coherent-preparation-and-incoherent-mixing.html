Coherent preparation and incoherent mixing

Choose the qubit computational basis ∣0⟩,∣1⟩, also called the Z basis, and the Hadamard basis ∣±⟩=(∣0⟩±∣1⟩)/2​, also called the X basis. Compare a coherent preparation of ∣+⟩ with choosing ∣0⟩ or ∣1⟩ at random with equal probabilities and discarding that label:
ρ+​=21​(11​11​),ρmix​=21​∣0⟩⟨0∣+21​∣1⟩⟨1∣=21​(10​01​).
(6)
These are original ideal preparation models. Their Z-basis probabilities are both (1/2,1/2), because their quantum state populations agree. Their X-basis probabilities are respectively (1,0) and (1/2,1/2). Indeed ⟨+∣ρ+​∣+⟩=1, whereas ⟨+∣I/2∣+⟩=1/2. The missing off-diagonal quantum coherence therefore changes a prediction that the common populations cannot determine.
For a relative quantum phase ϕ, the coherent family instead has
∣ψϕ​⟩=2​∣0⟩+eiϕ∣1⟩​,ρϕ​=21​(1eiϕ​e−iϕ1​),PX​(±)=21±cosϕ​.
(7)
The last formula follows by adding or subtracting the two probability amplitudes before taking the squared modulus. At ϕ=π, the state is ∣−⟩ and the X outcomes switch to (0,1), while the Z outcomes remain (1/2,1/2). In contrast, −∣+⟩ changes both components by the same global phase and retains the X outcomes (1,0). In photon polarization, the identification ∣0⟩=∣H⟩, ∣1⟩=∣V⟩ makes ∣+⟩ and ∣−⟩ the two orthogonal diagonal linear polarizations; these labels describe measurement bases, not spatial axes for photon spin.
Even perfect statistics in one basis cannot certify a preparation. The above Z data fit both density matrices. Conversely, distinct preparation histories can yield the same quantum state:
21​∣0⟩⟨0∣+21​∣1⟩⟨1∣=21​∣+⟩⟨+∣+21​∣−⟩⟨−∣=2I​.
(8)
The off-diagonal terms cancel in the right-hand ensemble. Every measurement on the qubit alone then has identical probabilities for those two histories. A retained classical label would allow conditioning on different members of the quantum state ensemble; it is extra information beyond the unlabelled density operator.
