Constructible point

 Home Mathematics Area of mathematics Algebra Galois theory Constructible number
OurBigBook.com Words: 223 Articles: 2
A point of the Euclidean plane is constructible if it can be obtained from (0,0) and (1,0) by finitely many straightedge-and-compass operations: draw a line through two obtained distinct points, draw a circle with obtained centre and radius equal to an obtained segment length, and retain isolated intersection points. Its two coordinates are constructible numbers. Conversely, constructible real coordinates can be placed on perpendicular axes and their coordinate lines intersected, giving the point. Coordinates created at each isolated intersection require only solving linear or quadratic equations over the current coordinate field; hence they lie in a real field extension obtained by a finite tower of quadratic extensions. Coincident curves supply no isolated new intersection points.
