Fe-Cr giant magnetoresistance experiments (1988–1989)

 ... Electric charge Electric current Electrical resistance and conductance Electrical resistance Magnetoresistance Giant magnetoresistance
OurBigBook.com Words: 233
The 1988 study grew alternating iron/chromium layers by molecular beam epitaxy on gallium arsenide. Magnetization and torque measurements tested the magnetic configuration. An AC electrical resistance measurement passed electric current along the layers while the applied magnetic field changed. Comparing field directions and spacer thicknesses linked the resistance decrease to magnetic saturation. Electron microscopy and surface analysis checked layer formation, while leaving atomic-scale interface roughness unresolved.[ref]
The independent Jülich study used four-terminal sensing on epitaxial Fe/Cr/Fe strips. A single iron film supplied an anisotropic magnetoresistance comparison. Magneto-optic Kerr effect signals and Brillouin light scattering from spin waves identified the layer alignments, rather than inferring them from resistance alone. Field scans along different magnetic axes connected the optical signatures to the resistance changes.[ref]
Peter Andreas Grünberg's retrospective explains why the preceding interlayer exchange coupling measurements mattered: coupled spin wave modes supplied signatures of magnetic alignment before that alignment became an electrical signal.[ref]
A resistance curve alone does not reveal the microscopic direction of every magnetic layer. The optical measurements add a different observable, while the reference film tests an orientation-dependent response that can occur without a multilayer. Sample preparation and cross-checks therefore belong to the physical explanation.
