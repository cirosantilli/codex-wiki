Cotranslational protein translocation

 Home Biology Protein Protein targeting Organelle protein import
OurBigBook.com Words: 784 Articles: 10
Cotranslational protein translocation is passage of a growing polypeptide chain across a membrane during biological translation. SRP recognizes an exposed hydrophobic ER signal sequence. The SRP receptor enables handoff to the Sec61 complex. Biological translation can slow during delivery; the ribosome remains on the cytosol side.[ref]
The useful engineering feature is coordination: a targeting instruction can become accessible while the rest of its cargo is still being made. Recognition, docking and crossing remain distinct operations. A defect in any one can prevent successful import even when the protein primary structure contains an appropriate signal.
This route is not universal. Many tail-anchored proteins use delivery after biological translation, and their membrane-spanning segment lies near the C-terminus. Thus protein targeting signals need not be at the N-terminus.[ref]
