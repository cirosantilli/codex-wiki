Newtonian potential of a point mass (Φ(r)=−GM/r)

 ... Physics Branches of physics Classical mechanics Newton's law of universal gravitation Newtonian gravitational field Newtonian gravitational potential
OurBigBook.com Words: 169
The Newtonian potential of a point mass M at the origin is
Φ(r)=−rGM​,r>0,Φ(∞)=0.
(36)
It is the free-space Green function of the Laplacian with the normalization of the Poisson equation for Newtonian gravity. Differentiating gives inward gravitational acceleration −GM/r2. With z=r/r0​, the normalized Newtonian gravitational potential is Φ/(GM/r0​)=−1/z; at z=1,2,4 it equals −1,−1/2,−1/4.
For a radially outward launch from r0​, conservation of mechanical energy in the fixed-source test particle approximation gives the minimum escape velocity:
mE​=2v02​​−r0​GM​=0⟹vesc​=r0​2GM​​.
(37)
At this threshold the speed tends to zero only at infinite separation. The model neglects air resistance, external tidal forces, and general relativity. [ref]
A circular orbit at r0​ instead requires tangential speed vc​=GM/r0​​. Choosing units with G=M=r0​=1 gives g0​=1, vc​=1, and vesc​=2​≃1.41421. A radial launch at speed 1 reaches r=2 and returns; the same speed directed tangentially gives a circular orbit. [ref]
