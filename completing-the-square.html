Completing the square (a(x−h)2+k)

 Home Mathematics Area of mathematics Algebra Polynomial
OurBigBook.com Words: 649 Articles: 4
Completing the square rewrites a quadratic polynomial so that its minimum or maximum and its roots of a polynomial become easy to read. The linear term is absorbed into a shift of the variable; the resulting polynomial identity changes the description, rather than the values of the polynomial.
For coefficients a,b,c in the real numbers, with a=0,
ax2+bx+c=a(x+2ab​)2+c−4ab2​.​
(20)
First factor out the leading coefficient from ax2+bx, then add and subtract (b/(2a))2 inside the bracket. The cross term in (x+b/(2a))2 is (b/a)x, exactly the term needed. Multiplication by a restores bx. Thus the half-coefficient is b/(2a), rather than b/2 unless the quadratic polynomial is a monic polynomial. Expanding the right-hand side verifies the polynomial identity directly.[ref]
For an original example,
x2+6x+13=(x2+6x+9)+4=(x+3)2+4​.
(21)
For every x in the real numbers, (x+3)2≥0, with equality only at x=−3. The minimum is therefore 4, reached at −3; no calculus is needed, and there are no roots of a polynomial in the real numbers. The expanded coefficients conceal these conclusions more effectively than the completed square does.
