Molecular vibration

 Home Chemistry Molecule
OurBigBook.com Words: 2k Articles: 25
Molecular vibration changes internal separations and bond angles, unlike the fixed internal molecular geometry of a rigid rotor. Its coupling to molecular rotation makes measured rotational constants depend on the vibrational quantum state.
A diatomic molecule has one internal stretching coordinate: its two atomic nuclei approach and separate about an equilibrium bond length. The diatomic bond stretch turns this motion into a particularly useful model. The local potential energy curvature supplies the restoring force, while the reduced mass accounts for both moving partners. Thus the same chemical bond can vibrate more slowly after an isotope substitution even when its electronic structure is nearly unchanged.
This separation makes infrared spectroscopy informative: transition frequencies constrain chemical bond stiffness, and isotope scaling of diatomic vibration tests the mass dependence. The University of Florida HCl and DCl FTIR experiment illustrates the measurement. Detectability also requires vibrational infrared activity; having vibrational energy eigenvalues alone does not guarantee an infrared absorption line.
