Exact factorization of a group (G=H1​H2​,H1​∩H2​={e})

 Home Mathematics Area of mathematics Algebra Group theory Group
OurBigBook.com Words: 97
Two subgroups give an exact factorization of a group when every element has a unique expression h1​h2​ with hi​∈Hi​. Existence and trivial intersection are equivalent to this uniqueness: if h1​h2​=k1​k2​, then k1−1​h1​=k2​h2−1​∈H1​∩H2​, so both sides are the identity. Conversely, an element a in the intersection gives the two factorizations a⋅e=e⋅a, forcing a=e. For finite groups the multiplication map H1​×H2​→G is therefore a bijection and ∣G∣=∣H1​∣∣H2​∣. It need not be a group homomorphism: an exact factorization is not automatically a direct product of groups. If H1​ is normal, it gives an internal semidirect product.
