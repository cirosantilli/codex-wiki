Western blot transfer

 Home Biology Protein Western blot
OurBigBook.com Words: 220 Articles: 2
Western blot transfer moves separated proteins out of the gel onto a Western blot membrane, using an electric field across the gel thickness. Maintaining close contact preserves the lane pattern. Contact gaps, incomplete removal from the gel and passage through the receiving sheet can change how much protein is retained.
The original 1979 experiment pressed a wetted, 0.45μm-pore nitrocellulose Western blot membrane against the gel between scouring pads and plastic pipette-tray grids, held by elastic bands. The urea-gel assembly faced the cathode in acidic solution, with 6V/cm applied for one hour; the SDS-PAGE variant reversed polarity and used a glycine/methanol buffer solution. Stained gel/blot pairs checked pattern preservation. Corresponding labelled ribosome protein spots from two gels were compared through radioactive decay readouts after combustion. The stained gel remaining after transfer supplied a removal check; a second receiving sheet revealed breakthrough on overloading. These tests supported recovery for the examined urea-gel proteins, within experimental variation, while the SDS-PAGE results showed unequal staining recovery. Preserved band positions therefore did not establish universal quantitative transfer. [ref]
