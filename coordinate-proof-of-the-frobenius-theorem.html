Coordinate proof of the Frobenius theorem

 ... Area of mathematics Geometry and topology Differential geometry Distribution (differential geometry) Integrable distribution Frobenius theorem
OurBigBook.com Words: 104
For an involutive distribution of constant rank r, induct on r. Straighten a nonzero section by the flow-box theorem, and choose a frame ∂1​,Y2​,…,Yr​ with no ∂1​ component in the remaining fields. Involutivity implies ∂1​Y=AY for their column Y. The invertible solution of ∂1​B=−BA, initialized by B=I on x1​=0, makes Z=BY independent of x1​. Its restriction to the transverse slice is involutive of rank r−1, so induction provides coordinates spanning it. Extending these coordinates independently of x1​ gives D=span(∂1​,…,∂r​). Conversely, fields tangent to an integral manifold have tangent brackets because they preserve the ideal of smooth functions vanishing on it.
