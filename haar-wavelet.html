Haar wavelet (ψ=χ[0,1/2)​−χ[1/2,1)​)

 ... Mathematics Area of mathematics Analysis Fourier analysis Wavelet Orthonormal wavelet
OurBigBook.com Words: 1k Articles: 9
The Haar wavelet is a signed contrast between two adjacent half-intervals:
ψ(x)=⎩⎨⎧​1,−1,0,​0≤x<1/2,1/2≤x<1,otherwise.​
(45)
It has compact support, zero integral, and unit L2 norm: ∫ψ=1/2−1/2=0 and ∫∣ψ∣2=1. The zero integral is its one vanishing moment. Its Haar scaling function is ϕ=1[0,1)​, which records a coarse value rather than a contrast. Endpoint conventions do not alter the L2 inner products.
The normalized copies ψj,k​(x)=2j/2ψ(2jx−k) compare the halves of the dyadic interval [k2−j,(k+1)2−j). Narrower copies are taller so that their L2 norms stay one. Thus a coefficient identifies both a location, through k, and a scale, through j.
For a simple step function f equal to 2 on [0,1/2) and 6 on [1/2,1), its coarse coefficient is ⟨f,ϕ⟩=4, while its first contrast is ⟨f,ψ⟩=−2. Thus f=4ϕ−2ψ. The negative coefficient raises the second half and lowers the first. Finer contrasts vanish because f is constant on each half. A jump aligned with this dyadic interval boundary therefore needs only two nonzero coefficients; jumps at other positions can require details at many scales.
Its jump discontinuities make the Haar wavelet particularly transparent for changes between neighbouring regions, while limiting approximation of smooth variation. A Daubechies wavelet uses a more elaborate pattern to obtain additional vanishing moments and, at sufficiently high order, smoother basis functions. The elementary Haar refinement identity has coefficients h0​=h1​=1/2​ and explains how the coarse and fine descriptions fit together.
The historical source is Alfred Haar's [ref]. Chapter III, beginning on page 361, constructs the constant function and these localized piecewise-constant orthogonal functions. Its original endpoint assignments differ from the half-open convention used here, without changing L2 inner products.
