Ginzburg-Landau theory of superconductivity

 Home Physics Branches of physics Statistical physics Superconductivity
OurBigBook.com Words: 621 Articles: 8
Vitaly Lazarevich Ginzburg and Lev Landau applied an order parameter description to superconductivity in 1950. A spatially varying complex amplitude extends the fixed-amplitude description of the London equations. Their initial charge assumption preceded the later identification of the universal pair charge.[ref]
Consider the following isotropic, local SI Helmholtz free energy model near a continuous superconducting transition temperature:
F[ψ,A]=∫d3x[α∣ψ∣2+2β​∣ψ∣4+2m∗​∣(−iℏ∇−qA)ψ∣2​+2μ0​∣B∣2​],B=∇×A.
(14)
Here β>0, m∗​>0 is a phenomenological mass parameter, and q=−2e fixes the electron-pair convention. The uniform superconducting minimum for α<0 has ∣ψ0​∣2=−α/β. The gradient term penalizes changes of the amplitude and the gauge-invariant quantum phase; the magnetic term penalizes magnetic field penetration. The gauge-coupling sign in Ginzburg-Landau superconductivity keeps the electric current and field equation consistent. This model omits material anisotropy, microscopic nonlocality and critical fluctuations.
