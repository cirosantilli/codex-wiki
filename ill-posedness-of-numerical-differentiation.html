Ill-posedness of numerical differentiation (∥fk​∥∞​→0 need not imply ∥fk′​∥∞​→0)

 Home Mathematics Area of mathematics Analysis Numerical analysis Numerical differentiation
OurBigBook.com Words: 218 Articles: 1
Recovering a derivative from values controlled only in the uniform norm is an ill-posed problem: the differentiation map is not continuous in those norms. On [0,1], take fk​(x)=sin(kx)/k. Then ∥fk​∥∞​≤1/k→0, while fk′​(x)=cos(kx) has uniform norm one for every k. Arbitrarily small value perturbations can therefore conceal nonvanishing derivative changes.
Numerical differentiation can still be useful when additional smoothness bounds, a suitable sampling scale, or a regularization method control the admissible changes. The basic noise amplification by differentiation of a linear interpolant gives a finite-data version: endpoint uncertainty of size δ produces derivative uncertainty of order δ/h at spacing h.
