Graph kernel (K(G))

 Home Mathematics Area of mathematics Foundations of mathematics Graph theory 2-core of a graph
OurBigBook.com Words: 135
For a connected graph whose 2-core contains a vertex of degree of a vertex at least three, suppress each maximal graph path with degree-two internal vertices to a single edge, allowing its two endpoints to coincide. Here suppression is the reverse of graph subdivision. The resulting multigraph, retaining self-loops and parallel edges, is its graph kernel. Each suppressed internal vertex removes one vertex and one edge, preserving graph excess; counting a self-loop twice toward degree of a vertex, every remaining vertex has degree of a vertex at least three. A purely cyclic 2-core is a separate unicyclic case: it has no branching vertex to anchor this construction.
For the barbell in bridges and connectivity in the 2-core, the graph kernel has two vertices c,d, one self-loop at each, and a single joining edge cd. Suppressing x,y contracts the joining graph path to that bridge in a graph; suppressing the two other vertices on each triangle turns the triangle into a self-loop. The graph excess stays 9−8=3−2=1. Thus forming a graph kernel compresses degree-two chains rather than deleting everything outside graph cycles.
