Simultaneous rendezvous distance (⌈d(p,q)/2⌉)

 Home Computer science Theoretical computer science Computational complexity theory Computational problem Search problem
OurBigBook.com Words: 112
Two controlled walkers on the same connected undirected graph can each traverse one edge or wait in each unit-cost step. If they meet only by occupying the same vertex at the same time, their minimum meeting time is
⌈2d(p,q)​⌉,
(21)
where d is the shortest path distance. A common meeting point after t steps implies d(p,q)≤2t by the triangle inequality. Conversely, choose a middle vertex of a shortest connecting graph path; both walkers reach it within ⌈d/2⌉ steps, and the earlier arrival waits. The wait is essential when the path length is an odd integer. This distance on the joint Cartesian product state space is a consistent heuristic: each joint move changes the walkers’ separation by at most two. Replacing d by an obstacle-free Manhattan distance preserves admissible heuristic and consistent heuristic guarantees.
