Linear combination (∑i=1n​ai​vi​)

 Home Mathematics Area of mathematics Algebra Linear algebra Vector space
OurBigBook.com Words: 316 Articles: 2
Let V be a vector space over a specified field F, and let v1​,…,vn​∈V. A linear combination is a finite sum
w=∑i=1n​ai​vi​,ai​∈F.
(4)
The coefficients ai​ are scalars from that same field; each term uses scalar multiplication, and the terms are joined by vector addition. For coefficients in the real numbers, negative values reverse arrows and fractional values scale them by fractional amounts. The collection of attainable vectors is the linear span. The column picture in Strang's MIT notes interprets solving a linear system as choosing coefficients to reach a target vector.[ref]
For a concrete calculation over the real numbers, take
v1​=(1,1),v2​=(1,−1),w=(3,1).
(5)
Then 2v1​+v2​=(2,2)+(1,−1)=(3,1)=w. More generally, solving a+b=x and a−b=y gives
(x,y)=2x+y​v1​+2x−y​v2​.
(6)
Every vector in the Euclidean plane is therefore a linear combination of v1​,v2​, so their linear span is all of R2. The target (3,1) has coefficients (2,1); the target (0,2) has coefficients (1,−1), since (1,1)−(1,−1)=(0,2). The displayed formula also makes the coefficients unique. Consequently (v1​,v2​) is an ordered basis, with coordinate vector [(x,y)](v1​,v2​)​=((x+y)/2,(x−y)/2)T.
The payoff is a reusable language for synthesis: linear combinations build vectors from chosen ingredients, linear span decides which targets are reachable, and linear independence decides whether the coefficients are unique. The ingredients can also be polynomials or functions, as long as they belong to a vector space over the specified field.
