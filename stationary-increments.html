Stationary increments

 Home Mathematics Area of mathematics Probability and statistics Probability theory Stochastic process
OurBigBook.com Words: 66
A stochastic process has stationary increments when the probability distribution of Xt+h​−Xt​ depends only on the duration h, whenever both times belong to the index set. The clock origin then does not change the increment law. For Brownian motion, that law is N(0,h); for a rate-λ Poisson process, it is a Poisson distribution with expected value λh. Neither process itself is a stationary process: their variances grow with t. Stationary increments and a stationary process describe different properties.
