Point-mass gravitational lens (β=θ−θE2​θ/∣θ∣2)

A point-mass gravitational lens is the ideal gravitational lens obtained by concentrating a positive mass M at one location. Use geometrical optics, the weak-field approximation, small angles in radians, and a stationary lens whose deflecting region is short compared with the observer-lens and lens-source distances. At impact parameter b, require b≫rs​=2GM/c2, the Schwarzschild radius, and a ray passing outside any opaque body. The physical bend has magnitude α=4GM/(c2b) from Schwarzschild light deflection; G is the gravitational constant and c the speed of light. Multiple images can occur within this weak-field approximation: strong gravitational lensing does not require a ray to approach a black hole closely.[ref]
Let Dl​, Ds​ and Dls​ be angular diameter distances from observer to lens, observer to source, and lens to source, respectively. They refer to the background geometry used in the thin gravitational lens equation. In nearby Euclidean geometry, Dls​=Ds​−Dl​; in cosmology, do not make that subtraction. The angular source coordinate β is the direction the source would have without this lens; the observed image coordinate is θ. Both are measured relative to the observer-lens optical axis.[ref]
For the flat sketch below, extend the observer-side light ray backwards to the source plane. Its transverse position would be Ds​θ, whereas the actual source is at Ds​β. Their separation is Dls​α.
