Invertible fractional ideal (II−1=A)

 ... Mathematics Area of mathematics Algebra Commutative algebra Integral domain Fractional ideal
OurBigBook.com Words: 103
Over an integral domain A with fraction field K, an invertible fractional ideal is a finitely generated nonzero A-submodule I⊆K for which another fractional ideal J satisfies IJ=A. Here I−1={x∈K:xI⊆A} equals J. Choose 1=∑ui​vi​ with ui​∈I, vi​∈J. On the distinguished open where ui​vi​ is invertible, I is generated by ui​, since x/ui​=(xvi​)/(ui​vi​). The maps x↦vi​x form a finite dual basis and prove that I is a rank-one projective module. Conversely, a finitely generated rank-one projective module embeds in K after choosing a basis over K; its dual identifies with I−1, and local evaluation shows II−1=A.
