Cayley-Hamilton theorem (χA​(A)=0)

 ... Mathematics Area of mathematics Algebra Linear algebra Operator theory Minimal polynomial
OurBigBook.com Words: 728 Articles: 6
Every square matrix satisfies its own characteristic polynomial. More explicitly, for an n-by-n matrix A over a commutative ring with identity, form
χA​(t)=det(tI−A)=tn+cn−1​tn−1+⋯+c1​t+c0​.
(1)
Then
An+cn−1​An−1+⋯+c1​A+c0​I=0.​
(2)
Here 0 is the zero matrix, and the constant term multiplies the identity matrix. This is a polynomial evaluated at a matrix; it is stronger than saying that each eigenvalue is a root of a polynomial. Neither diagonalizability nor distinct eigenvalues is required.[ref]
The practical payoff is that arbitrarily high matrix powers can be reduced to a combination of I,A,…,An−1. These powers arise when repeatedly applying a linear map, solving a linear recurrence relation, or evolving a finite state space model. The characteristic polynomial turns that repeated action into a fixed-order scalar recurrence. The minimal polynomial may reduce the number of required powers further.
