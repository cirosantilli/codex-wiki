Lattice in toric geometry (N≅Zn,M=Hom(N,Z))

Words: 104 Articles: 1
Toric geometry OurBigBook.com
A toric lattice is a finite-rank free abelian group N, embedded as a discrete full-rank subgroup of NR​=N⊗Z​R. Its dual is M=Hom(N,Z). This lattice of integral vectors is distinct from an order-theoretic lattice. It supplies the integral structure for toric cones and their dual monomials.

Basis of a toric lattice

Words: 57
Lattice in toric geometry OurBigBook.com
A lattice basis v1​,…,vn​ expresses each vector of the toric lattice uniquely as ∑i​ai​vi​ with ai​∈Z. Relative to any fixed integral basis, vectors form another lattice basis exactly when their determinant is ±1. A primitive vector is one whose integer span is saturated; it can be completed to such a basis by the Euclidean algorithm.

Toric scheme over a base ring (XΣ,A​)

Words: 239 Articles: 2
Toric geometry OurBigBook.com
Given a dual pair of free lattices N,M and a rational fan, glue SpecA[σ∨∩M] over common-face charts. The monoid rings are semigroup algebras. A lattice map respecting cones gives a toric morphism. This construction works over a commutative ring A, including Z, and also for an infinite fan in toric geometry.

Semistable infinite chain from a toric fan (Ui​=SpecZ[xi​,yi​],q=xi​yi​)

Words: 190 Articles: 1
Toric scheme over a base ring OurBigBook.com
Take rays (i,1) and cones generated by consecutive rays in Z2. Their dual generators (1,−i) and (−1,i+1) give xi​=tq−i, yi​=t−1qi+1. Adjacent charts glue by xi+1​=yi−1​, yi+1​=xi​yi2​. The fibre q=0 is a chain of projective lines: each component has two affine-line charts joined by reciprocal coordinates, and consecutive components meet at one node. Inverting q identifies all charts with the multiplicative group scheme over Z[q,q−1]. The local node algebra is free over Z[q], with basis 1,xia​,yib​ for a,b≥1, so the morphism is flat.

Cyclic formal quotient of the infinite toric chain (X/⟨i↦i+m⟩)

Words: 98
Semistable infinite chain from a toric fan OurBigBook.com
The lattice shear (a,b)↦(a+mb,b) shifts the chain charts by m and preserves q. On the dense torus it acts as (t,q)↦(qmt,q). For m≥3, translates of each chart of the formal completion of a scheme along q=0 are disjoint. Glue m representative formal charts cyclically by the adjacent-chart formulas to obtain a formal scheme quotient. The two branch opens of each chart are disjoint because D(xi​)∩D(yi​)=D(q) is empty on the formal support. The quotient fibre is an m-gon of projective lines, and the quotient map is locally an isomorphism on each translated chart. Invariant morphisms descend uniquely through this gluing.

Algebraic torus

An algebraic torus of dimension n is an algebraic group isomorphic over the base field to (Gm​)n. Its character and cocharacter lattices are dual free abelian groups M and N.

Maximal algebraic torus (T)

Words: 115 Articles: 2
Algebraic torus OurBigBook.com
A maximal algebraic torus is an algebraic torus maximal among the tori contained in an algebraic group. In a connected reductive algebraic group, all maximal algebraic tori are conjugate. A Borel subgroup contains maximal algebraic tori, and its unipotent radical gives a decomposition B=T⋉U.

Rational maximal torus (F(T)=T)

Words: 78 Articles: 1
Maximal algebraic torus OurBigBook.com
A maximal algebraic torus T is rational for F when F(T)=T. Rationality does not require every point of T to be fixed: the finite group TF is its rational-point group. Similarly a rational Borel subgroup or parabolic subgroup means an F-stable algebraic subgroup.
Elliptic maximal torus

A rational maximal torus is elliptic when it is contained in no proper rational parabolic subgroup. This definition allows the split central torus of the ambient group; an absolute absence of every split subtorus is not the right condition when the centre is nontrivial.

Ample divisor after blowing up a torus-fixed point (cπ∗D−E)
Words: 71
Toric blowup at a torus-fixed point OurBigBook.com
For an ample invariant divisor D and the blowup π of a torus-fixed smooth point in dimension at least two, cπ∗D−E is ample for sufficiently large integers c. In the subdivided cone, its local characters are cmσ​+ui​, where ui​ is dual to the omitted ray generator. The new strict inequalities have gap one on the omitted ray and grow linearly with c elsewhere, proving the claim by the toric ampleness criterion.

Two minimal toric resolutions of a four-ray threefold cone (a=(1,0,0), b=(1,1,1), c=(0,2,1), d=(1,1,0))
Words: 90
Toric resolution of singularities OurBigBook.com
For this toric cone, one smooth fan subdivision has maximal cones abc,acd and no exceptional divisors. Another has abd,bce,cde,dbe, with e=a+c=(b+c+d)/2. Its only exceptional divisor is a projective plane, since the quotient fan at e has the three rays bˉ,cˉ,dˉ with sum zero and unimodular neighboring cones. Both subdivisions admit no proper smooth coarsening. Their common smooth refinement is obtained from the first by adding e in ac and then w=a+e in ae, and from the second by adding w=b+d in bd. Each step is a toric blowup along an orbit closure.
Multiplicity descent in toric desingularization (mult(σ)=[N∩spanR​σ:∑i​Zvi​])
Words: 98
Toric resolution of singularities OurBigBook.com
First make a toric fan simplicial by compatible rational subdivisions. In a nonregular simplicial cone, choose a nonregular face minimal under inclusion. Its half-open fundamental parallelepiped contains a nonzero point of the toric lattice w=∑i​αi​vi​, with every 0<αi​<1: a zero coefficient would put it in a regular proper face and force all coefficients to be integers. A star subdivision at the primitive point on this ray replaces a generator by w and multiplies the affected lattice determinant by a number strictly between zero and one. The maximum multiplicity and the number of maximal cones realizing it decrease lexicographically, giving a finite toric resolution of singularities.
Hirzebruch–Jung resolution

The last vector is (1,0), each consecutive pair is a lattice basis, and subdivision by these rays is a resolution. Its exceptional chain has self-intersections −bi​. Complete three-ray toric surfaces with at most one singular point (P(1,1,r),r≥1)

Words: 56

Four-ray completion criterion for a cyclic quotient surface singularity (s∣r−1,s≡±q(modr))
Words: 93
Cyclic quotient surface singularity OurBigBook.com
The cyclic quotient surface singularity r1​(q,1) is the only singular point of a complete four-ray toric surface exactly under the displayed condition. Normalize the three smooth cones to the consecutive rays (a,−1),(1,0),(0,1),(−1,b). The singular cone has determinant 1−ab=r, so ab=1−r. In the basis (−1,b),(0,−1), its other generator is −a(−1,b)+r(0,−1), giving quotient parameter amodr. Thus a=±s for a positive divisor s of r−1. Conversely these rays construct the surface. Exchanging the two axes replaces q by q−1; replacing a factor by its complementary factor preserves the criterion under this equivalence.
