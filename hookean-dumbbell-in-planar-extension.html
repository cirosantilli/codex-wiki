Hookean dumbbell in planar extension (C˙=AC+CAT−(C−I)/λ)

 Home Chemistry Polymer Polymer physics Coil-stretch transition

Represent one polymer by two beads joined by an ideal linear entropic spring, and stipulate a dimensionless conformation tensor C with equilibrium value I. The displayed model uses velocity gradient A=diag(ϵ˙,−ϵ˙,0) and positive polymer relaxation time λ. It omits finite chain extensibility, interactions with other chains, and feedback from the polymer onto the flow. It is a teaching model, not a fitted description of Keller's apparatus.
Define the Weissenberg number for this model as Wi=λϵ˙ and dimensionless time u=t/λ. With an initially isotropic C=I, the diagonal entries obey
dudCxx​​=(2Wi−1)Cxx​+1,dudCyy​​=−(2Wi+1)Cyy​+1,Czz​=1.
(2)
Below Wi=1/2, both transverse and longitudinal responses approach positive finite steady values:
Cxx​=1−2Wi1​,Cyy​=1+2Wi1​,Czz​=1.​
(3)
For a chosen Wi=0.4, Cxx​=5 and Cyy​=5/9: the longitudinal mean-square separation is five times its quiescent value, while the transverse separation contracts. A root-mean-square length grows as the square root of a variance, so the longitudinal length factor is 5​, not five. The anisotropy Cxx​−Cyy​ is an orientational indicator; converting it to measured birefringence requires an additional calibrated optical relation.
At Wi=1/2, Cxx​=1+u. Above that value, the growing homogeneous solution prevents a positive finite steady state. For instance, Wi=0.6 gives Cxx​=6eu/5−5. Real chains have finite contour lengths, so this growth identifies the failure of the linear spring model. It does not establish a universal threshold for arbitrary flow histories, finite chains or other definitions of polymer relaxation time. Combining the growth equation with residence time in planar extension also explains why a large instantaneous Weissenberg number alone does not guarantee a strongly stretched exiting chain.
