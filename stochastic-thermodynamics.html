Local detailed balance

Words: 101 Articles: 1
Stochastic thermodynamics OurBigBook.com
Local detailed balance states that the logarithm of the probability ratio between a trajectory and its time reverse equals the entropy delivered to the environment in units of kB​. For one heat bath, log(PF​/PB​)=ΔQ/(kB​T) when positive ΔQ flows into the bath.

Microscopic reversibility

Words: 57
Local detailed balance OurBigBook.com
For an equilibrium process, a path and its physically time-reversed path have equal probability after including their equilibrium starting weights. Conditional on their endpoints, this means
PB​PF​​=exp[−(F2​−F1​)/(kB​T)].
(1)
This is the path version of detailed balance. A time-even order parameter is read backward without changing sign, while a transport flux changes sign. The Onsager--Machlup path probability implements this reversal in a coarse-grained stochastic description.

Entropy production (ΔStot​)

Words: 2k Articles: 11
Stochastic thermodynamics OurBigBook.com
Entropy production is the sum of the entropy changes of a system and its environment. Under local detailed balance it is represented by a forward-to-reversed path-probability logarithm and has nonnegative expectation.

Entropy gain from impact and thermal equilibration

Words: 55
Entropy production OurBigBook.com
A body of mass m, heat capacity c, temperature Ti​ and speed v that stops in a large reservoir at TL​ produces entropy mc[log(TL​/Ti​)+Ti​/TL​−1]+mv2/(2TL​), assuming constant heat capacity and no phase changes. The first term follows by adding the body's logarithmic entropy change to the reservoir's heat gain; the second is kinetic-energy dissipation. A numerical answer needs the reservoir temperature.

Thermodynamic entropy flux (jη​=q/θ)

Local equilibrium associates the outgoing heat flux q with outgoing entropy flux q/θ at positive temperature θ. Its divergence appears in the Lagrangian entropy inequality, with the appropriate nominal flux in reference coordinates.

Fluctuation theorem

Words: 1k Articles: 8
Entropy production OurBigBook.com
A fluctuation theorem compares the probability of a fluctuating sample trajectory with that of its reverse. Positive mean entropy production permits negative individual realizations: what is constrained is their relative probability, not a prohibition on every reversal. The finite statement below compares specified forward and reverse probability laws; replacing the reverse law by the forward law requires an additional symmetry. The biased-step entropy production model exhibits that symmetry exactly, while the Crooks fluctuation theorem compares two different driving protocols.

Finite trajectory entropy production (Σ=ΔStot​/kB​)

Words: 345 Articles: 1
Fluctuation theorem OurBigBook.com
Consider a finite-duration Markov process with discrete, time-even states coupled to one thermal reservoir at positive temperature T. Let PF​[γ] include the initial probability ρ0​(x0​) of the sample trajectory γ. Define PR​ by reversing the control protocol and initializing it with the actual forward final probability distribution ρτ​. Write Θγ for the same states visited in reverse time order. Require normalized path laws with matching probability support: a path has positive forward weight exactly when its reverse has positive reverse weight. This excludes irreversible forbidden transitions and missing initial states.
Under local detailed balance, with positive heat Qbath​ delivered to the thermal reservoir, the full path-weight logarithm is
ΣF​[γ]=lnPR​[Θγ]PF​[γ]​=kB​TQbath​[γ]​+lnρτ​(xτ​)ρ0​(x0​)​=kB​ΔStot​[γ]​.
(2)
The endpoint term is the change in stochastic system entropy; the conditional path ratio alone accounts for the thermal reservoir. Here kB​ is the Boltzmann constant. Both terms matter even for a short observation. [ref]
Define the conjugate reverse functional by ΣR​[η]=ln(PR​[η]/PF​[Θη]), so ΣR​[Θγ]=−ΣF​[γ]. Summing the path identity over paths with a given value gives the exact finite fluctuation theorem
pR​(−s)pF​(s)​=es,
(3)
for nonzero denominator, using probability masses or corresponding probability density functions. This ΣR​ is defined by the paired laws: it need not be the total entropy production obtained by independently restarting the thermodynamic construction for the reverse experiment. A generic driven experiment therefore does not imply pF​(s)/pF​(−s)=es.
Integral fluctuation theorem (⟨e−ΣF​⟩F​=1)
Words: 128
Finite trajectory entropy production OurBigBook.com
Normalization of the reverse law gives an exact finite identity:
⟨e−ΣF​⟩F​=∑γ​PF​[γ]PF​[γ]PR​[Θγ]​=1.
(4)
The sum notation describes discrete paths; the corresponding path integral has the same change-of-variable argument. Jensen's inequality for the exponential function then gives e−⟨ΣF​⟩F​≤1, hence ⟨ΣF​⟩F​≥0. Equivalently this mean is the Kullback-Leibler divergence between the forward path law and the reverse path law pulled back by Θ. Equality requires those two laws to agree. [ref]
Rare negative entropy production contributes disproportionately to this expectation through e−ΣF​. Missing such events in a finite sample can spoil an estimate of the identity. Matching probability support is also essential: reverse paths absent from the forward law cannot be recovered by cancelling forward weights. This Second law of thermodynamics statement concerns an ensemble average; an individual negative value is compatible with it.

Biased-step entropy production model (Sn​=a(2K−n), a=ln(p/q))

Words: 400 Articles: 1
Fluctuation theorem OurBigBook.com
Take n independent steps, each +1 with probability p>1/2 and −1 with probability q=1−p>0. Reverse a step sequence by reading it backward and changing each sign; use the same biased law for both experiments. With K positive steps, the forward sequence weight is pKqn−K and its reversed weight is pn−KqK. Their logarithm ratio is
Sn​=(2K−n)a,a=ln(p/q)>0,K∼Binomial(n,p).
(5)
Here Sn​ is dimensionless entropy production, not an entropy in joules per kelvin. A Markov chain on a three-state ring gives a state-space realization: start uniformly, take a clockwise step with probability p and a counterclockwise step with probability q. The uniform stationary distribution makes endpoint weights cancel. A physical interpretation as exchanged heat requires local detailed balance with bath entropy ±kB​a per directed step; the abstract model alone specifies path probabilities.
For an allowed value s=a(2k−n), the binomial distribution gives
pn​(s)=(kn​)pkqn−k,pn​(−s)pn​(s)​=(n−kn​)pn−kqk(kn​)pkqn−k​=(qp​)2k−n=es.
(6)
This is an exact same-law finite fluctuation theorem, with no long-time approximation. For p=3/4, n=9 and k=6, s=3ln3 and pn​(s)/pn​(−s)=27. The positive mean is ⟨Sn​⟩=na(p−q), although the all-negative path has nonzero probability qn. Directly, ⟨e−Sn​⟩=(pe−a+qea)n=(q+p)n=1.
The left panel shows exact probability masses for p=3/4, with negative values highlighted; the right panel tests the logarithm ratio against s. These are model values, without experimental measurements or simulated measurement noise.
Negative entropy production over finite durations (Pr(Sn​<0))
Words: 182
Biased-step entropy production model OurBigBook.com
A single step has negative entropy production with probability q, independent of how long an observation lasts. A whole window is negative only if negative steps outnumber positive ones:
Pr(Sn​<0)=∑k=0⌊(n−1)/2⌋​(kn​)pkqn−k.
(7)
For even n, the tie K=n/2 has zero entropy production and is excluded. At p=3/4, the negative-window probabilities for n=1,9,21 are approximately 0.25, 0.04893, and 0.00642. Longer windows still contain negative increments: their expected number is nq. Their total is usually positive because the expected number of positive increments is np.
Only odd window lengths are plotted to avoid alternating tie effects. The left panel gives the exact negative-window probability and the upper bound (2pq​)n; the right gives expected counts np and nq. For steps of fixed duration δt, physical duration is nδt. These are counts and probabilities predicted by the model, not bead traces. The bound follows from Pr(Sn​<0)≤⟨e−Sn​/2⟩=(2pq​)n. Since 2pq​<1 for p=q, negative totals are exponentially suppressed even though the negative-step fraction stays q.

Steady-state asymptotic fluctuation theorem (I(−s)−I(s)=s)

Words: 162
Fluctuation theorem OurBigBook.com
A steady state does not by itself give every fluctuating observable an entropy production symmetry. If the dimensionless total entropy production Σt​ has the same-law finite relation and a large deviation principle with rate function I for Σt​/t, then, on its common domain,
pt​(Σt​/t≈s)≍e−tI(s),I(−s)−I(s)=s.
(8)
The first expression denotes leading exponential scaling; the second follows by taking the scaled logarithm of the probability ratio. A finite-state, autonomous Markov process with time-even states, positive bidirectional rates and a positive stationary distribution supplies a standard setting for a finite total-entropy production relation. The stochastic system entropy is then bounded, so its endpoint contribution does not change the long-time rate function of bath entropy production. [ref]
Unbounded state spaces can have endpoint fluctuations on the scale relevant to a large deviation principle. Dropping system entropy, changing the measured observable to heat, or changing the reverse preparation therefore requires a fresh argument. The exact finite integral fluctuation theorem, a same-law finite relation, and an asymptotic rate function symmetry are distinct claims.

Crooks fluctuation theorem (PF​(W)/PR​(−W)=eβ(W−ΔF))

Words: 186 Articles: 1
Fluctuation theorem OurBigBook.com
For a system coupled to a thermal reservoir at fixed temperature T, prepare the forward process in the initial canonical ensemble and the reverse process in the final control parameter's canonical ensemble. Reverse the protocol, assume microscopic reversibility and matching path probability support, and count W as work done on the system. With Helmholtz free energy difference ΔF=Ffinal​−Finitial​ and β=1/(kB​T),
PR​(−W)PF​(W)​=eβ(W−ΔF).
(9)
The final forward distribution need not already be in thermal equilibrium. Its reverse preparation is prescribed equilibrium preparation, rather than the actual forward final distribution used in finite trajectory entropy production. Thus the paired path logarithm β(W−ΔF) is dissipated work in thermal units, not automatically the instantaneous total stochastic entropy production at the end of an arbitrary driven protocol. Jarzynski equality (⟨e−βW⟩F​=e−βΔF)
Words: 55
Crooks fluctuation theorem OurBigBook.com
Integrating the Crooks fluctuation theorem over work and using normalization of the reverse probability distribution gives
⟨e−βW⟩F​=e−βΔF.
(10)
Consequently Jensen's inequality gives ⟨W⟩F​≥ΔF. Some realizations may nevertheless have W<ΔF. This is a work relation with equilibrium endpoint preparations, while the integral fluctuation theorem for total entropy production uses the actual final forward distribution. [ref]

Dragged-bead fluctuation theorem experiment

Words: 260
Fluctuation theorem OurBigBook.com
The 2002 experiment trapped a 6.3μm bead in water with a 980nm laser, a 100×, numerical aperture 1.3 objective and a piezo-driven microscope stage. A quadrant photodiode tracked bead position at 1kHz with approximately 500Hz bandwidth and reported 15nm resolution. Stage translation drove water past the fixed optical trap at 1.25μm/s. Weak trapping prolonged the transient; stationary recording before translation established the position reference. Stokes drag law calibration was checked against the bead's measured diffusion coefficient. [ref]
From calibrated displacement and calibrated harmonic optical trap force, the experimenters evaluated the dimensionless driven-bead dissipation functional
Σtdrive​=kB​T1​∫0t​vopt​⋅Fopt​(u)du.
(11)
Among 540 trajectories, negative values became less frequent with duration. Figure 2 compared the negative/positive count ratio with ⟨e−Σtdrive​⟩Σtdrive​>0​; its agreement tested the paper's integrated transient relation. The detailed log-ratio plot in Figure 4 used simulated particle sample trajectories, not bead measurements. Near-zero sign discrimination and stage-start transients limited the earliest points. [ref]
For any continuous probability distribution satisfying the same-law relation, integration over s>0 yields
Pr(Σ>0)Pr(Σ<0)​=⟨e−Σ⟩Σ>0​.
(12)
This conditional relation explains the comparison without equating it to the unconditional integral fluctuation theorem. The experiment supports a specified driven-system relation; it does not establish a universal same-law symmetry for any definition of heat or entropy production. In particular, a transient work-like functional should not silently acquire the actual-distribution endpoint term in finite trajectory entropy production. Negative realizations are compatible with positive mean dissipation and the Second law of thermodynamics; they do not provide a cyclic machine with reliable work output from a single thermal reservoir.

Vector noise normalization in inertial Langevin dynamics (⟨A(t)⋅A(s)⟩=(6γkB​T/m2)δ(t−s))

Words: 53
Fluctuation-dissipation relation for a Langevin particle OurBigBook.com
Divide the inertial Langevin equation by mass and let ζ=γ/m. An isotropic three-dimensional equilibrium velocity has ⟨∣u∣2⟩=3kB​T/m by the equipartition theorem. Its white acceleration dot-covariance amplitude is therefore 6ζkB​T/m, while the amplitude per Cartesian component is 2ζkB​T/m. A scalar moment formula must use the component variance rather than the trace.
