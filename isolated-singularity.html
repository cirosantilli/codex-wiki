Classification of isolated singularities

Words: 3k Articles: 56
Isolated singularity OurBigBook.com
An isolated singularity is removable, a pole, or essential according as its Laurent principal part has zero, finitely many nonzero, or infinitely many nonzero terms.

Removable singularity

Words: 103 Articles: 3
Classification of isolated singularities OurBigBook.com
An isolated singularity is removable when the function extends holomorphically across it, equivalently when its Laurent series has no negative-power terms.

Riemann removable singularity theorem

Finite spherical area criterion for an isolated singularity
Words: 81
Spherical derivative of a meromorphic function OurBigBook.com
A meromorphic function on a punctured disk has finite pulled-back spherical area precisely when it extends to the Riemann sphere at the puncture. A removable singularity or pole gives a smooth spherical extension and finite area. At an essential singularity, the meromorphic version of the Great Picard theorem gives infinitely many preimages of all but at most two spherical targets. The area formula then makes the area infinite. The logarithmically weighted annular area divided by log(1/r) tends to the total unweighted area divided by 4π, by averaging a nondecreasing area function.
Algebraic addition theorem
Words: 158 Articles: 1
Meromorphic function OurBigBook.com
A meromorphic function f has an algebraic addition theorem if some nonzero polynomial P∈C[U,V,W] satisfies P(f(z),f(w),f(z+w))=0 wherever the three values are finite. For a polynomial f of degree d>0, take roots zi​ of f(z)−U and wj​ of f(w)−V; the product ∏i,j​(W−f(zi​+wj​)) has polynomial coefficients in U,V by the Fundamental theorem of symmetric polynomials, is monic in W, and supplies an addition relation. An elliptic function also has such a relation, by finite algebraic extension of its function field and the Weierstrass elliptic function addition formula.
Simply periodic entire function without an algebraic addition theorem (f(z)=eez)
Words: 90
Algebraic addition theorem OurBigBook.com
The displayed entire function has exactly the periods 2πiZ: a period c would make ez(ec−1) a constant integer multiple of 2πi, forcing ec=1. If a polynomial addition relation existed, specialize its middle argument at a point with ec=α>0 irrational, avoiding the finitely many specializations making the entire polynomial vanish. With t=ez, the relation becomes a nonzero finite sum of exponentials e(m+αn)t. These real exponents are distinct, so the largest one dominates as t→+∞, a contradiction. This demonstrates that an algebraic addition theorem is stronger than simple periodicity.
Meromorphic functions on the sphere are rational
Words: 62
Meromorphic function OurBigBook.com
Poles are isolated, including in the coordinate w=1/z around infinity. Compactness of the Riemann sphere therefore leaves only finitely many poles. Subtract the finite principal parts at finite poles and the polynomial principal part at infinity. The remainder is entire with a finite limit at infinity, hence bounded and constant by Liouville's theorem. The original function is a sum of a polynomial and finitely many partial fractions, so is rational.
Mittag-Leffler's theorem
Words: 65
Meromorphic function OurBigBook.com
Mittag-Leffler's theorem prescribes the principal parts of a meromorphic function. Given an open subset D of the complex plane, a subset A having no accumulation point in D, and a finite sum of negative powers Pa​(z)=∑j=1ma​​ca,j​(z−a)−j for each a∈A, there is a meromorphic function on D with exactly these principal parts and no other poles. The difference of any two such meromorphic functions is a holomorphic function on D, because their principal parts cancel at every prescribed pole.
Polynomial growth forces a meromorphic function to be rational
Words: 68
Meromorphic function OurBigBook.com
A meromorphic function on the plane with ∣f(z)∣≤C∣z∣n outside a disc has no poles there and only finitely many poles inside. Subtract their finite principal parts to obtain an entire function. For n≥0, Cauchy estimates make every derivative of order above n vanish, so the remainder is a polynomial. For n<0 the remainder tends to zero at infinity and is zero by Liouville theorem. Adding back the principal parts gives a rational function in either case.

Nevanlinna theory studies the growth and value distribution of meromorphic functions through their integrated pole counts and logarithmic proximity to target values. The Nevanlinna characteristic combines both quantities, while the Nevanlinna first main theorem compares it with the corresponding counts and proximity for any fixed target.
Ahlfors covering surface theory
Words: 403 Articles: 6
Nevanlinna theory OurBigBook.com
This theory compares the area, boundary length and Euler characteristic of a surface mapped holomorphically to another surface, using the pulled-back metric. It extends covering-degree and Riemann-Hurwitz formula estimates when some boundary maps into the interior of the target. The Ahlfors second fundamental theorem and Ahlfors five islands theorem are important consequences.

Words: 159 Articles: 1
Ahlfors covering surface theory OurBigBook.com
For a finite simply connected covering surface over the Riemann sphere and q≥3 fixed separated Jordan domains, let nj​ count its simply connected islands over the jth domain once each, regardless of mapping degree. Let S be its average sheet number and L its spherical boundary length. The displayed inequality holds with h depending only on the target domains. For regular bordered surfaces f:X→Y, a general formulation is max{−χ(X),0}≥−Sχ(Y)−hL, where L counts only the boundary mapped into the interior of Y and χ is the modern Euler characteristic. The boundary error disappears for an actual branched covering. Ahlfors five islands theorem
Words: 46

Every nonconstant meromorphic function on the complex plane has a simple island over at least one of any five Jordan domains on the Riemann sphere with disjoint closures. The Ahlfors second fundamental theorem, area comparison over target domains, and a length-area exhaustion of the complex plane prove this: without a simple island every counted island has degree at least two, forcing 3S≤(5/2)S+O(L).
Island of a meromorphic function
Words: 92 Articles: 1
Ahlfors covering surface theory OurBigBook.com
An island over a Jordan domain D is a relatively compact component U of its inverse image on which the meromorphic function is a proper map onto D. A component reaching the boundary of the source is instead a peninsula. The island has an integer mapping degree; degree one means that the map is a biholomorphism. A simply connected island can have larger degree and need not be a simple island.

Rotational symmetry of a rational map (R(gz)=hR(z))
Words: 94 Articles: 1
Rational map (complex analysis) OurBigBook.com
Rotations of the domain and target Riemann spheres act as Möbius transformations represented by SU(2) matrices. A combined symmetry requires the equivariance identity R(gz)=hR(z) for the corresponding transformations. Symmetry of the Wronskian of a rational map alone is insufficient: it tests the ramification directions, not the entire map. Near an isolated essential singularity, a holomorphic function has dense image in the complex plane. Reciprocal cosine with accumulating poles

Words: 28
Non-isolated singularity OurBigBook.com
The function 1/cos(1/z) has simple poles at z=1/[π(n+1/2)], accumulating at zero. Its origin is therefore a non-isolated singularity, despite the underlying cosine of 1/z having an isolated essential singularity.

Zeros and poles

Words: 46
Isolated singularity OurBigBook.com
Zeros and poles describe the positive and negative local orders of a meromorphic function. Locally f(z)=(z−a)mg(z) with g holomorphic and nonzero at a: m>0 is a zero, m<0 is a pole, and m=0 is a regular nonzero value. A simple pole has m=−1, and the order of vanishing records m.
