The Neyman-Pearson lemma states that a test which rejects where the joint likelihood ratio L1​/L0​ is largest is a most powerful test of its size: its power is at least that of any test with no larger size. More precisely, choose a threshold k and reject if L1​/L0​>k, accept if it is smaller, and if necessary randomize on equality to attain the specified size.
For an independent and identically distributed statistical sample, take
Yi​=logf1​(Xi​)f0​(Xi​)​.
(12)
The Yi​ are independent and identically distributed random variables under either simple hypothesis, and the log joint ratio is their sum. Thus the nonrandomized threshold form can be written
i=1∑n​Yi​≤c​
(13)
with c=−logk, using either strict or non-strict inequality when the boundary has probability zero. The usual finite-log expression assumes positive common support; zero densities can instead be handled by the extended likelihood ratio on the relevant supports. Continuous observations alone do not ensure a continuous likelihood-ratio statistic: ties may require the boundary randomization in the lemma. This qualification preserves the exact-size formulation.
