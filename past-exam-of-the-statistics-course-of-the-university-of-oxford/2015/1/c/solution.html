At a fixed confidence level, a confidence interval based on the sample mean has a leading half-width smaller by the factor 2/π​. With known σ, X±z1−α/2​σ/n​ also has exact normal coverage. Prefer the sample mean under the normal model.
For the Laplace distribution, symmetry puts both the mean and median at θ. Its density there is 1/(2σ), and
Var(Xi​)=σ1​∫0∞​y2e−y/σdy=2σ2.

Consequently the sample mean has variance 2σ2/n, while the sample median has leading variance σ2/n. Hence
ARE(MX​,X)=2for the Laplace model.​
(15)
A large-sample confidence interval based on the sample median has leading half-width smaller by 1/2​; with known scale it is MX​±z1−α/2​σ/n​. Prefer the sample median under the Laplace model. Consistent estimation of an unknown scale preserves these leading comparisons. The reason for the reversal is quantitative: the median's precision depends on the central density f(θ), while the sample mean's precision depends on the whole population variance.
