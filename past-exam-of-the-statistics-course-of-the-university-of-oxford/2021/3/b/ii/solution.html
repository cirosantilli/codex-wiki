Put M0​=α+x0​β. As a linear combination of independent normal random variables under the posterior distribution,
M0​∣y∼N(m0​,v0​),m0​=mα​+x0​mβ​,v0​=vα​+x02​vβ​.
(47)
The shortest 95% credible interval is
[mα​+x0​mβ​−Φ−1(0.975)vα​+x02​vβ​​,mα​+x0​mβ​+Φ−1(0.975)vα​+x02​vβ​​]​.
(48)
Here Φ is the standard normal distribution cumulative distribution function. To justify shortest length, consider all intervals of a fixed length d. If f is this normal distribution posterior density, the interval probability ∫aa+d​f(t)dt has derivative f(a+d)−f(a) with respect to a. The normal distribution is symmetric and its density strictly decreases with distance from its mean, so this probability is uniquely maximized when the midpoint is m0​. The centered interval can therefore attain 95% posterior probability with the least length. It is both an equal-tailed credible interval and a highest posterior density interval.
The target is the experiment's mean at x0​. A posterior predictive distribution for a new noisy observation would instead have variance v0​+σ2; that extra observation variance does not belong in the requested interval.
