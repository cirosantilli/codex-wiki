The prior mean 1/(β+1) decreases as β increases. More strongly, its cumulative distribution function and upper-tail probability are
Fβ​(t)=1−(1−t)β,Pβ​(θ>t)=(1−t)β,0<t<1.
(39)
For every fixed t, that upper-tail probability decreases with β. The statistician increasingly favors smaller values of θ; as β→∞, the prior distribution concentrates near zero. For β>1, its variance β/[(β+1)2(β+2)] also decreases. This is concentration of a beta prior near zero.
With the data fixed, the derivatives are
dβdλ​=(N+β+1)2N​>0​,dβdθ1​​=−(N+β+1)2S+1​<0​.
(40)
Thus the prior weight increases and the posterior mean decreases. Their limits are λ→1 and θ1​→0. Both the changing prior mean and the increasing weight contribute to the change in the posterior mean.
