Condition on the total of 400 complaints. Assume each complaint independently has the same probability p of being received on Monday; in particular, repeated complaints from one incident must not create material dependence. The Monday count S then has a binomial distribution with parameters (400,p). Test H0​:p=0.2 against H1​:p>0.2, rejecting for large S as suggested by the monotone likelihood ratio.
Under the null hypothesis, E0​S=80 and Var0​(S)=400(0.2)(0.8)=64. Both expected successes and expected failures are large, so the central limit theorem gives a suitable normal approximation:
Z=8S−80​ ∼˙ N(0,1),zobs​=8100−80​=2.5​.
(27)
Using the paper's supplied rounded table, the upper-tail p-value is approximately
papprox​=1−Φ(2.5)≈0.005​.
(28)
A more accurate evaluation of the standard normal distribution cumulative distribution function gives about 0.0062. For example, choose significance level 0.05 before examining the data; the supplied upper 5% standard normal quantile is approximately 1.6, and 2.5>1.6. Reject H0​ at 5%, and also at 1%, using this approximation. The observed binomial proportion is 100/400=0.25, providing evidence that the Monday probability exceeds 0.2 under the sampling assumptions.
If applying a continuity correction, approximate P0​(S≥100) by 1−Φ((99.5−80)/8)=1−Φ(2.4375)≈0.0074; this gives the same 5% and 1% conclusions. The uncorrected value 0.005 is approximate, so a decision exactly at the 0.5% boundary is not warranted by the supplied rounded table.
