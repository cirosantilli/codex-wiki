For a sufficiently regular solution, put w=−Δu, with Δ the Laplacian. Then −Δu−w=0 and u−Δw=u+Δ2u=f. The zero Dirichlet boundary conditions on u and Δu become zero Dirichlet boundary conditions on u and w. Conversely, eliminating w from these two equations recovers the fourth-order equation with the biharmonic operator. For the weak solution we will construct, these interior identities hold as distributional identities; zero Sobolev traces are imposed on u and w, without assuming classical fourth derivatives of u.
Let V=H01​(Ω)×H01​(Ω), the Cartesian product of two zero-boundary Sobolev spaces, with norm
∥(p,q)∥V2​=∥p∥12​+∥q∥12​.
(1)
This is a Hilbert space. Applying integration by parts separately to the two equations gives the weak formulation
(∇u,∇v)0​−(w,v)0​(u,z)0​+(∇w,∇z)0​​=0=(f,z)0​​​for every v∈H01​(Ω),for every z∈H01​(Ω).​​
(2)
Equivalently, for U=(u,w) and T=(v,z), define the bilinear form and linear functional
b((p,q),(v,z))F(v,z)​=(∇p,∇v)0​+(∇q,∇z)0​−(q,v)0​+(p,z)0​,=(f,z)0​.​
(3)
We seek U∈V with b(U,T)=F(T) for every T∈V.
The skew-coupled elliptic system has a useful bounded bilinear form estimate with constant one. Indeed, write the integrand as the dot product of (∇p,∇q,−q,p) and (∇v,∇z,v,z). The Cauchy-Schwarz inequality gives
∣b((p,q),(v,z))∣≤∥(p,q)∥V​∥(v,z)∥V​,∣F(v,z)∣≤∥f∥0​∥(v,z)∥V​.
(4)
For equal arguments, the two zeroth-order coupling terms cancel. If K is the constant in the supplied Poincaré inequality, then
b((p,q),(p,q))=∥∇p∥02​+∥∇q∥02​≥1+K1​∥(p,q)∥V2​.
(5)
Thus b is a coercive bilinear form, although it is not a symmetric bilinear form. The Lax-Milgram theorem applies and yields
There is exactly one (u,w)∈H01​(Ω)×H01​(Ω) solving the weak system.​
(6)
In particular, ∥(u,w)∥V​≤(1+K)∥f∥0​. Testing with compactly supported smooth test functions identifies −Δu=w and −Δw=f−u as distributional identities. This gives the asserted relation to the original boundary value problem at the available regularity.

(8)

Use the continuous piecewise-linear nodal interpolants Ih​u and Ih​w on the shape-regular mesh. (SM​−MS​)(UW​)=(0F​),Sij​=∫Ω​∇ϕj​⋅∇ϕi​,Mij​=∫Ω​ϕj​ϕi​,Fi​=∫Ω​fϕi​.​

Here S is the diffusion stiffness matrix and M the mass matrix. Their entries are symmetric in i,j, so both are symmetric matrices.
For c∈RN, put vc​=∑j​cj​ϕj​. The two quadratic forms are
cTMc=∥vc​∥02​,cTSc=∥∇vc​∥02​.
(18)
If c=0, the linear independence of the basis ensures vc​=0, so cTMc>0. If cTSc=0, the Poincaré inequality and zero Dirichlet boundary condition force vc​=0, contradicting c=0. Thus both S and M are positive-definite matrices.
For X=(p,q)∈RN×RN, symmetry of M cancels the cross terms:
XTAX​=pTSp−pTMq+qTMp+qTSq=pTSp+qTSq.​
(19)
Therefore

Label the polygon vertices of the nondegenerate triangle K as a1​,a2​,a3​, and put mij​=(ai​+aj​)/2 for its three edge midpoints. The six monomials 1,x1​,x2​,x12​,x1​x2​,x22​ form a basis of P2​(K), so its dimension is six. Nondegeneracy matters: K has nonempty interior, and a polynomial vanishing on that interior is the zero polynomial.
Let λ1​,λ2​,λ3​ be the affine functions of the barycentric coordinate system, characterized by λi​(aj​)=δij​ and ∑i​λi​=1. Define six quadratic polynomials
Ni​=λi​(2λi​−1)(i=1,2,3),Nij​=4λi​λj​(1≤i<j≤3).
(21)
At ak​, Ni​(ak​)=δik​ and all Nij​(ak​) vanish. At mkl​, exactly λk​ and λl​ equal 1/2, while the third is zero. Thus every Ni​ vanishes at all three edge midpoints, and Nij​(mkl​) is one for the matching unordered pair and zero otherwise.
These six polynomials have the cardinal property for the six point evaluation functionals. Evaluating any vanishing linear combination at the nodes forces each of its coefficients to vanish. They are therefore six linearly independent members of a six-dimensional vector space, hence a basis.
In particular, every p∈P2​(K) has the unique expansion
p=i=1∑3​p(ai​)λi​(2λi​−1)+1≤i<j≤3∑​p(mij​)4λi​λj​.​
(22)
This proves both existence and uniqueness for arbitrary six prescribed nodal values, which is precisely an unisolvent finite element. It also identifies the finite element degrees of freedom and shape functions of the quadratic Lagrange triangle explicitly.

Use the Sobolev space with a partial Dirichlet condition
V={v∈H1(Ω):Tv=0 almost everywhere on ΓD​},​
(23)
where T is the Sobolev trace operator. The restricted Sobolev trace operator is continuous into L2(ΓD​); its kernel is consequently a closed vector subspace of H1(Ω). Thus V, with the first-order Sobolev norm, is a Hilbert space. The endpoint convention for ΓD​ and ΓN​ does not affect this definition: finitely many boundary points have zero boundary length. It would be inappropriate to impose pointwise boundary values on arbitrary H1 functions in two dimensions.
For a smooth solution, Green's first identity gives
∫Ω​(−Δu+u)v=∫Ω​(∇u⋅∇v+uv)−∫∂Ω​∂n​uTv.
(24)
The boundary term vanishes because Tv=0 on ΓD​ and the normal derivative is zero on ΓN​. This suggests the weak formulation
u∈V,a(u,v)=ℓ(v)(v∈V),a(p,v)=(∇p,∇v)0​+(p,v)0​,ℓ(v)=(f,v)0​.​
(25)
Here a is exactly the first-order Sobolev inner product. In particular the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality gives
∣a(p,v)∣≤∥p∥1​∥v∥1​,a(v,v)=∥v∥12​,∣ℓ(v)∣≤∥f∥0​∥v∥1​.
(26)
Thus a is a bounded bilinear form and a coercive bilinear form, both with constant one. The Lax-Milgram theorem proves existence and uniqueness, together with
∥u∥1​≤∥f∥0​.​
(27)
The positive reaction term already controls the entire Sobolev norm, so no Poincaré inequality is needed here. The zero Neumann boundary condition is the natural part of the weak formulation; it is not an additional restriction on the test space. For this weak solution, −Δu+u=f holds as a distributional identity, and the zero normal derivative on ΓN​ is understood through the corresponding Green's first identity.

For a conforming triangular finite element mesh, take
Vh​={vh​∈C0(Ω):vh​∣K​∈P2​(K) for every K∈Mh​,vh​∣ΓD​​=0}.
(28)
This is the quadratic conforming finite element space. Across a common edge of a polygon, the restrictions from neighboring triangles are univariate quadratic polynomials. Their agreement at the two endpoints and the midpoint makes their difference a degree-at-most-two polynomial with three distinct roots of a polynomial, so it vanishes on the entire common edge of a polygon. Thus matching the local finite element degrees of freedom produces the required continuity. Similarly, setting the three nodal values on every boundary edge carrying the Dirichlet boundary condition to zero makes its entire restriction vanish.
Continuity across element interfaces means the piecewise gradients give the global weak gradient, with no jump contribution, so Vh​⊂V. There are finitely many local finite element degrees of freedom, hence Vh​ is finite-dimensional. The Galerkin method seeks
uh​∈Vh​,(∇uh​,∇vh​)0​+(uh​,vh​)0​=(f,vh​)0​(vh​∈Vh​).​
(29)
The bounded bilinear form and coercive bilinear form estimates from part (b) remain valid on Vh​. Since a finite-dimensional subspace is closed, the Lax-Milgram theorem gives exactly one uh​. Alternatively, the stiffness matrix plus the mass matrix is a positive-definite matrix in any basis of Vh​. The same existence and uniqueness conclusion holds for any finite-dimensional vector subspace of this full space.

(58)
The Cauchy-Schwarz inequality for this inner product follows directly by expanding a(p−tq,p−tq)≥0 and minimizing over t when q=0. Hence
∥p+q∥a2​​=∥p∥a2​+2a(p,q)+∥q∥a2​≤(∥p∥a​+∥q∥a​)2,​
(59)
which proves the triangle inequality. This verifies every norm axiom. In fact, the estimates from part (b) give the equivalent norms
α​∥v∥1​≤∥v∥a​≤2μ+3λ​∥v∥1​.​
(60)
Subtract the discrete weak formulation from the continuous one to get Galerkin orthogonality,
a(u−uh​,vh​)=0(vh​∈Vh​).
(61)
For any vh​∈Vh​, write u−vh​=(u−uh​)+(uh​−vh​). The second summand is in Vh​, so expansion and Galerkin orthogonality give the Pythagorean theorem in the energy norm:
∥u−vh​∥a2​=∥u−uh​∥a2​+∥uh​−vh​∥a2​.​
(62)
The last term is nonnegative and vanishes exactly when vh​=uh​. Choosing vh​=uh​ attains the lower bound, proving
∥u−uh​∥a​=vh​∈Vh​min​∥u−vh​∥a​.​
(63)
Thus uh​ is the unique best approximation; the map u↦uh​ is the orthogonal projector onto Vh​ for the linear elasticity energy norm.
