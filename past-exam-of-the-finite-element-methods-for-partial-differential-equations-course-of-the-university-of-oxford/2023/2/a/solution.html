Label the polygon vertices of the nondegenerate triangle K as a1​,a2​,a3​, and put mij​=(ai​+aj​)/2 for its three edge midpoints. The six monomials 1,x1​,x2​,x12​,x1​x2​,x22​ form a basis of P2​(K), so its dimension is six. Nondegeneracy matters: K has nonempty interior, and a polynomial vanishing on that interior is the zero polynomial.
Let λ1​,λ2​,λ3​ be the affine functions of the barycentric coordinate system, characterized by λi​(aj​)=δij​ and ∑i​λi​=1. Define six quadratic polynomials
Ni​=λi​(2λi​−1)(i=1,2,3),Nij​=4λi​λj​(1≤i<j≤3).
(21)
At ak​, Ni​(ak​)=δik​ and all Nij​(ak​) vanish. At mkl​, exactly λk​ and λl​ equal 1/2, while the third is zero. Thus every Ni​ vanishes at all three edge midpoints, and Nij​(mkl​) is one for the matching unordered pair and zero otherwise.
These six polynomials have the cardinal property for the six point evaluation functionals. Evaluating any vanishing linear combination at the nodes forces each of its coefficients to vanish. They are therefore six linearly independent members of a six-dimensional vector space, hence a basis.
In particular, every p∈P2​(K) has the unique expansion
p=i=1∑3​p(ai​)λi​(2λi​−1)+1≤i<j≤3∑​p(mij​)4λi​λj​.​
(22)
This proves both existence and uniqueness for arbitrary six prescribed nodal values, which is precisely an unisolvent finite element. It also identifies the finite element degrees of freedom and shape functions of the quadratic Lagrange triangle explicitly.
