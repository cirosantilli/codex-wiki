Use the continuous piecewise-linear nodal interpolants Ih​u and Ih​w on the shape-regular mesh. Since u,w∈H2(Ω) in two dimensions, the Sobolev embedding theorem supplies continuous representatives, so the nodal values are well-defined. Their zero Sobolev traces imply zero boundary nodal values. Hence Ih​u∈Uh​ and Ih​w∈Wh​ for the usual full conforming finite element spaces.
The permitted finite element interpolation estimates give constants Cu​,Cw​ determined by the shape regularity and Ω, independent of h, such that
∥u−Ih​u∥1​≤Cu​h∥u∥H2(Ω)​,∥w−Ih​w∥1​≤Cw​h∥w∥H2(Ω)​.
(14)
Substitute these choices in the best approximation bound from part (b):
(∥u−uh​∥12​+∥w−wh​∥12​)1/2C∗​​≤C∗​h,=(1+K)(Cu2​∥u∥H2(Ω)2​+Cw2​∥w∥H2(Ω)2​)1/2.​​
(15)
For fixed u,w, this constant is independent of h. A shape-regular mesh suffices; no uniformity of the individual element sizes is needed, since h is their maximum diameter.
The inclusion of these interpolants is an essential assumption. If the wording “consist of” were read as permitting arbitrary smaller vector subspaces, taking Uh​=Wh​={0} for every h would prevent the claimed convergence for a nonzero solution. The full conforming finite element space interpretation is also needed for the dimension assertion in part (d).
