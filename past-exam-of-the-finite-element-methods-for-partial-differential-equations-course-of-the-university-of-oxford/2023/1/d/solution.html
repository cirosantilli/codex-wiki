Let x1​,…,xN​ be the interior nodes, and let ϕ1​,…,ϕN​ be the nodal basis of the common conforming finite element space, with ϕj​(xi​)=δij​ and zero boundary values. These piecewise-linear hat functions give the expansions
uh​=∑j=1N​Uj​ϕj​,wh​=∑j=1N​Wj​ϕj​.
(16)
Testing the first equation with ϕi​ and then the second equation with ϕi​ gives the linear system
(SM​−MS​)(UW​)=(0F​),Sij​=∫Ω​∇ϕj​⋅∇ϕi​,Mij​=∫Ω​ϕj​ϕi​,Fi​=∫Ω​fϕi​.​
(17)
Here S is the diffusion stiffness matrix and M the mass matrix. Their entries are symmetric in i,j, so both are symmetric matrices.
For c∈RN, put vc​=∑j​cj​ϕj​. The two quadratic forms are
cTMc=∥vc​∥02​,cTSc=∥∇vc​∥02​.
(18)
If c=0, the linear independence of the basis ensures vc​=0, so cTMc>0. If cTSc=0, the Poincaré inequality and zero Dirichlet boundary condition force vc​=0, contradicting c=0. Thus both S and M are positive-definite matrices.
For X=(p,q)∈RN×RN, symmetry of M cancels the cross terms:
XTAX​=pTSp−pTMq+qTMp+qTSq=pTSp+qTSq.​
(19)
Therefore
XTAX>0(X=0),2A+AT​=(S0​0S​).​
(20)
This is the positivity of a quadratic form intended by the last sentence of the question. For N>0, A is a nonsymmetric matrix, since M=0. Under the convention that “positive-definite matrix” includes symmetry, that last sentence needs this qualification: the symmetric part of a matrix is positive definite. The quadratic lower bound for a nonsymmetric matrix still implies that A is an invertible matrix: AX=0 would give XTAX=0, hence X=0.
