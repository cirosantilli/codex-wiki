Since a finite-dimensional subspace is closed, Vh​ is itself a Hilbert space. The bounded bilinear form and coercive bilinear form estimates in part (a) restrict to Vh​ with the same constants. The Lax-Milgram theorem therefore gives exactly one Uh​ for every h.
Put E=U−Uh​=(eu​,ew​). Subtracting the continuous and discrete weak formulations gives Galerkin orthogonality:
b(E,Th​)=0(Th​∈Vh​).
(8)
For any Yh​=(vh​,zh​)∈Vh​, the difference Yh​−Uh​ also belongs to Vh​, so
∥∇eu​∥02​+∥∇ew​∥02​​=b(E,E)=b(E,U−Yh​)+b(E,Yh​−Uh​)=b(E,U−Yh​)≤∥E∥V​∥U−Yh​∥V​.​
(9)
The last step uses the constant-one Cauchy-Schwarz inequality estimate from part (a). Bounding the four terms separately by a sum would obscure the precise constant requested here.
The two minimization problems over Uh​ and Wh​ are independent. Each has a best approximation because its vector subspace is finite-dimensional. Consequently, if
Dh2​=minvh​∈Uh​​∥u−vh​∥12​+minzh​∈Wh​​∥w−zh​∥12​,
(10)
then the preceding inequality gives exactly
∥∇(u−uh​)∥02​+∥∇(w−wh​)∥02​≤∥U−Uh​∥V​Dh​.​
(11)
Both errors belong to the zero-boundary Sobolev space. Applying the Poincaré inequality componentwise now yields
1+K1​∥U−Uh​∥V2​≤∥U−Uh​∥V​Dh​.
(12)
When U=Uh​ the desired estimate is automatic; otherwise divide by the nonzero norm. Thus
(∥u−uh​∥12​+∥w−wh​∥12​)1/2≤(1+K)(vh​∈Uh​min​∥u−vh​∥12​+zh​∈Wh​min​∥w−zh​∥12​)1/2.​
(13)
The constant C=1+K is independent of h. This is the Céa lemma estimate for this particular skew-coupled elliptic system.
