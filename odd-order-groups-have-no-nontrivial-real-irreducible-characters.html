Odd-order groups have no nontrivial real irreducible characters

 ... Area of mathematics Algebra Representation theory Character theory Character of a representation Irreducible character
OurBigBook.com Words: 47
For a real-valued irreducible character of a finite odd-order group, pairing g with g−1 gives ⟨χ,1G​⟩=(χ(1)+2A)/∣G∣, with A an algebraic integer. If this inner product were zero, χ(1) would be even, since a rational algebraic integer is an integer. But an irreducible character degree divides the group order, so it is odd. Therefore the character is trivial.
