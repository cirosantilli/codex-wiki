Fabry–Pérot resonance spacing (ΔνFSR​=c/(2nd))

 Home Physics Branches of physics Optics Optical instrument Fabry–Pérot interferometer
OurBigBook.com Words: 196
At normal incidence, with no optical dispersion and zero round-trip reflection wave phase modulo 2π, the resonance condition in Fabry–Pérot transmission from multiple reflections is
2nd=mλm​,νm​=2ndmc​,ΔνFSR​=νm+1​−νm​=2ndc​.
(7)
Here ν is frequency and c is the speed of light in vacuum. A change of ν by one free spectral range advances the round-trip wave phase by 2π. At fixed internal angle of incidence θ, the same nondispersive model gives c/(2ndcosθ). Holding the external angle of incidence fixed in a dispersive medium need not hold the internal θ fixed, because of Snell's law.
For stipulated n=1 and d=10mm, the free spectral range is 14.9896229GHz using c=299792458ms−1. A frequency difference of 0.10ΔνFSR​ is therefore 1.49896229GHz. Transmission repeats every free spectral range: one isolated scan cannot distinguish that separation from 1.10ΔνFSR​. Independent knowledge of the optical spectrum, an additional filter, or a changed mirror separation is needed to assign orders.
Equal spacing in frequency does not imply equal spacing in wavelength. At normal incidence the exact adjacent resonance wavelengths obey
λm​−λm+1​=m(m+1)2nd​=m+1λm​​≃2ndλm2​​(m≫1).
(8)
For example, choosing 2nd=10λ10​ makes the exact gap to order 11 equal to λ10​/11, while the high-order estimate gives λ10​/10. The approximation is already ten percent larger at order 10. With optical dispersion or frequency-dependent reflection wave phase, obtain local spacings from the total phase difference instead: Δν≃2π/(dδ/dν) when its derivative varies little over one order.
