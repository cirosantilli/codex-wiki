Conditional entropy rate (limn→∞​H(Xn​∣X1​,…,Xn−1​))

 Home Mathematics Area of mathematics Probability and statistics Information theory Entropy rate
OurBigBook.com Words: 100
For a strictly stationary process on a finite alphabet, put a1​=H(X1​) and an​=H(Xn​∣X1​,…,Xn−1​) for n≥2. Each an​ is the average remaining uncertainty in the next symbol after its history is known. More history can improve prediction, so this sequence is nonincreasing:
an+1​=H(Xn+1​∣X1​,…,Xn​)≤H(Xn+1​∣X2​,…,Xn​)=an​.
(118)
The inequality uses conditioning reduces entropy; the last equality uses strict stationarity. Nonnegative an​ therefore have a limit. By the chain rule for information entropy,
nH(X1​,…,Xn​)​=n1​∑j=1n​aj​.
(119)
The Cesàro mean converges to the same limit, proving the equality with the entropy rate. This is a statement about average information entropy. A constant almost-sure limit of the realized information content requires the further hypotheses in the Shannon-McMillan-Breiman theorem; stationarity without ergodicity in an information source explains the distinction.
