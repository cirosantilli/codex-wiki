Hess's law equation cancellation

 Home Chemistry Thermochemistry Hess's law
OurBigBook.com Words: 166
Keep the stoichiometry attached to the enthalpy. Use the same abstract A,B,C and suppose each of two entries fixes an amount of substance for every species:
R1​:R2​:​2A⟶2B,C⟶B,​​ΔH1​=+36kJ,ΔH2​=+25kJ.​
(23)
Here each unit coefficient represents one mole on the common basis. To obtain A→C, halve R1​ and reverse R2​. Adding those transformed chemical equations gives
21​R1​:−R2​:sum:​A⟶B,B⟶C,A+B⟶B+C,​​21​ΔH1​=+18kJ,−ΔH2​=−25kJ,ΔH=−7kJ.​
(24)
The equal amounts of B on opposite sides cancel, leaving A→C. Doubling this final chemical equation would give 2A→2C and ΔH=−14kJ; its change per mole of A would remain −7kJmol−1.
More generally, write each chemical equation as a stoichiometric vector νj​, with negative entries for reactants and positive entries for reaction products. Finding coefficients aj​ with νtarget​=∑j​aj​νj​ also gives ΔHtarget​=∑j​aj​ΔHj​, using compatible state and amount conventions. An identical chemical species cancels only with the same thermodynamic phase and composition conditions. A leftover water or dilution step is a real part of the balance.
