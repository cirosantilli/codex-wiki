Completeness of the real numbers

 ... Mathematics Area of mathematics Analysis Real analysis Sequence and series Cauchy sequence
OurBigBook.com Words: 478 Articles: 2
Every Cauchy sequence of real numbers converges to a real number. Explicitly, the condition
∀ε>0∃N∈N>0​∀m,n≥N:∣xm​−xn​∣<ε
(96)
implies the existence of L∈R with xn​→L. The hypothesis compares approximations with one another, without requiring a candidate answer. This makes completeness of the real numbers an existence principle: sufficiently consistent approximations cannot approach a missing destination. [ref].
The equivalent least-upper-bound property says that every nonempty set A⊆R bounded above has a supremum s∈R. Precisely, every x∈A satisfies x≤s, and for every ε>0 some x∈A satisfies s−ε<x. The second condition excludes any smaller upper bound. No largest element of A is required.
The rational numbers lack this property. The set A={q∈Q:q≥0, q2<2} contains one and has upper bound two, but has no supremum in Q. A proposed rational supremum s would have to satisfy s2=2: if s2<2, a sufficiently small rational increase still lies in A; if s2>2, a sufficiently small rational decrease remains an upper bound. The irrationality of the square root of two excludes equality. In R, its supremum is 2​. [ref].
Construct this square root by the bisection method, starting with rational bounds [l0​,u0​]=[1,2]. At each step square the midpoint and retain the half whose endpoints bracket two after squaring. The first four resulting intervals are [1,3/2], [5/4,3/2], [11/8,3/2], and [11/8,23/16]. After k steps, uk​−lk​=2−k and lk2​<2<uk2​. Both endpoint sequences are Cauchy sequences because every later endpoint remains in the current interval. Completeness of the real numbers gives a common limit of a sequence L; continuity of squaring gives L2=2. The midpoint approximates L with error at most 2−(k+1). Thus exact rational arithmetic produces certified accuracy while completeness of the real numbers supplies the exact answer.
Richard Dedekind approached completeness of the real numbers by filling gaps in the rational numbers with Dedekind cuts. In the preface to his 1872 Continuity and Irrational Numbers, he traced the problem to teaching differential calculus in 1858 and wanting arithmetic foundations for limits. His construction makes a missing boundary, such as 2​, into a number defined by the separation it produces among rational numbers. [ref].
