Liouville rigidity from a bounded holomorphic embedding

 Home Mathematics Area of mathematics Analysis Complex analysis Liouville theorem
OurBigBook.com Words: 97
Suppose D⊆C is open and admits an injective holomorphic map Φ:D→C whose image is bounded. Every entire function f:C→D is constant: the function composition Φ∘f is a bounded entire function, hence constant by Liouville theorem, and injectivity of Φ then forces f to be constant. Surjectivity of Φ onto a disk is unnecessary. For example z↦z/(1−z) is a conformal map from C∖[0,1] onto (C∖[0,∞))∖{−1}. A square-root branch with argument in (0,2π) followed by the Cayley transform between the half-plane and disk maps this to the punctured unit disk. It therefore supplies a bounded injective holomorphic coordinate on the interval complement, giving the same rigidity even though that domain is not simply connected.
