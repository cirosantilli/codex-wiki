Write w=u−v and r=f−g. Subtraction of the two boundary value problems gives −w′′+w+u3−v3=r, with w(0)=w(1)=0. Multiply by w and apply integration by parts:
∫01​(−w′′)wdx=−[w′w]01​+∫01​(w′)2dx=∫01​(w′)2dx.
(1)
Consequently the required energy identity is
∫01​((w′)2+w2+(u3−v3)w)dx=∫01​rwdx.
(2)
The nonlinear term is nonnegative pointwise, since
(u3−v3)(u−v)=(u−v)2(u2+uv+v2),u2+uv+v2=(u+v/2)2+3v2/4≥0.
(3)
This is the monotonicity energy estimate for a semilinear Dirichlet problem for the monotone function s↦s3. Apply Young's inequality for products, rw≤(r2+w2)/2, inside the last integral and discard the nonnegative nonlinear term. Moving 21​∫01​w2dx to the left gives
∫01​(u′−v′)2dx+21​∫01​(u−v)2dx≤21​∫01​(f−g)2dx.
(4)
In particular, equal forcing gives w=0, proving uniqueness and a quantitative continuous dependence on forcing estimate for this boundary value problem.

Let fi​=f(ih) and gi​=g(ih), and define the scaled forward difference operator, backward difference operator and second-order central difference by
Dx+​Wi​=hWi+1​−Wi​​,Dx−​Wi​=hWi​−Wi−1​​,Dx+​Dx−​Wi​=h2Wi+1​−2Wi​+Wi−1​​.

The two finite difference methods are the nonlinear systems
−Dx+​Dx−​Ui​+Ui​+Ui3​=fi​,−Dx+​Dx−​Vi​+Vi​+Vi3​=gi​,1≤i≤N−1,​
(6)
with the prescribed Dirichlet boundary conditions U0​=V0​=A and UN​=VN​=B. These equations determine the interior nodal values.
For clarity distinguish the interior and edge inner products and their norms:
(P,Q)h​=h∑i=1N−1​Pi​Qi​,(P,Q]h​=h∑i=1N​Pi​Qi​,∥P∥h2​=(P,P)h​,∥P]∥h2​=(P,P]h​.

(−Dx+​Dx−​W,W)h​=−∑i=1N−1​(qi+1​−qi​)Wi​=∑i=1N​qi​(Wi​−Wi−1​)=h∑i=1N​qi2​.

∥Dx−​U−Dx−​V]∥h2​+21​∥U−V∥h2​≤21​∥f−g∥h2​.​
(10)

(12)

On the vector space of nodal values with zero endpoints define the discrete Sobolev norm
∥W∥1,h​=(∥W∥h2​+∥Dx−​W]∥h2​)1/2=(hi=1∑N−1​Wi2​+hi=1∑N​(hWi​−Wi−1​​)2)1/2.​
(16)
It is a norm because it is the Euclidean norm of the scaled list of nodal values and first finite differences, and vanishes only for the zero list. It is the discrete counterpart of the Sobolev space H1 norm. Here W=U−u has zero endpoints, so this definition applies directly.
Set M4​=max0≤x≤1​∣u(4)(x)∣, finite by the assumed continuity. Taylor's theorem with fourth-order remainders gives, for suitable ξi,+​∈(xi​,xi​+h) and ξi,−​∈(xi​−h,xi​),
u(xi​±h)=ui​±hui′​+2h2​ui′′​±6h3​ui′′′​+24h4​u(4)(ξi,±​).
(17)
Adding the two expansions cancels the odd derivatives, so
φi​=24h2​(u(4)(ξi,+​)+u(4)(ξi,−​)),∣φi​∣≤12M4​h2​.
(18)
Consequently ∥φ∥h2​≤h(N−1)M42​h4/144≤M42​h4/144. Doubling the bound in (c) gives 2∥Dx−​e]∥h2​+∥e∥h2​≤∥φ∥h2​, and therefore
∥U−u∥1,h​≤∥φ∥h​≤12M4​​h2.​
(19)
Thus M4​/12 is a valid nonnegative constant. To satisfy the request for a strictly positive constant even when u(4)≡0, take C=max{1,M4​}/12, independent of h. This proves second-order convergence of a numerical method in a discrete Sobolev norm, including control of the backward difference operator applied to the error.

For u(m,n)(x,y)=sin(mπx)sin(nπy), direct differentiation yields
∂xx​u(m,n)=−(mπ)2u(m,n),∂yy​u(m,n)=−(nπ)2u(m,n).
(20)
Since the planar Laplacian is Δ=∂xx​+∂yy​, the required eigenvalue of −Δ+I is
λm,n​=1+π2(m2+n2)>0.​
(21)
On the sides x=0,1 the first sine factor vanishes, and on y=0,1 the second sine factor vanishes. Thus the homogeneous Dirichlet boundary condition holds on the whole boundary. The eigenfunction is nonzero, for instance at (x,y)=(1/(2m),1/(2n)), where its value is one.

The five-point Laplacian on the square grid is
Δh​Wi,j​=h2Wi+1,j​+Wi−1,j​+Wi,j+1​+Wi,j−1​−4Wi,j​​,1≤i,j≤N−1.​
(22)
It adds the second-order central differences in the two coordinate directions and uses the given zero Dirichlet boundary conditions when a neighbouring node lies on the boundary.
The sine addition formula implies
sin((i+1)α)+sin((i−1)α)−2sin(iα)=2(cosα−1)sin(iα)=−4sin2(α/2)sin(iα).
(23)
Apply this identity with α=mπh in the x direction and with α=nπh in the y direction. For Ui,j(m,n)​=sin(mπih)sin(nπjh), it gives
−Δh​Ui,j(m,n)​+Ui,j(m,n)​=[1+h24​(sin22mπh​+sin22nπh​)]Ui,j(m,n)​.
(24)
Hence the discrete eigenvalues are
Λm,n​=1+h24​(sin22mπh​+sin22nπh​)>0,1≤m,n≤N−1.​
(25)
Both sine factors vanish on their respective boundary sides. Also U1,1(m,n)​>0, since mπ/N and nπ/N lie strictly between zero and π. Thus each array is a nonzero discrete eigenvector, rather than merely the zero solution of the homogeneous system. The separated structure is also described by the five-point Dirichlet Laplacian as a Kronecker sum.

For every admissible m,n, the trigonometric inequality sinz≤z for z≥0 gives
h24​sin22mπh​≤m2π2,h24​sin22nπh​≤n2π2.
(30)
Thus Λm,n​≤λm,n​, in particular for m=n=N−1 and every N≥3.
For these highest-index eigenvalues, use h=1/N and sin((N−1)π/(2N))=cos(π/(2N)) to obtain
λN−1,N−1​−ΛN−1,N−1​=2π2(N−1)2−8N2cos22Nπ​.
(31)
Divide by N2 and take the limit:
N→∞lim​N2λN−1,N−1​−ΛN−1,N−1​​=2π2−8>0.​
(32)
For example, eventually the normalized difference exceeds (2π2−8)/2, so the unnormalized difference is at least (π2−4)N2 and tends to +∞. This does not contradict (c): there the indices stay fixed as the grid is refined; here the eigenfunctions oscillate increasingly quickly, with wavelength comparable to the mesh spacing. It illustrates the high-frequency eigenvalue error of the discrete Laplacian.

Use the Backward Euler method in time and the prescribed central finite differences in space. With j∈Z and m≥0, the implicit finite difference method for the advection-diffusion equation is
ΔtUjm+1​−Ujm​​+2ΔxUj+1m+1​−Uj−1m+1​​=a(Δx)2Uj+1m+1​−2Ujm+1​+Uj−1m+1​​,Uj0​=u0​(jΔx).​
(33)
Equivalently, for ν=Δt/Δx and μ=Δt/(Δx)2, the new time level solves
(ν/2−aμ)Uj+1m+1​+(1+2aμ)Ujm+1​+(−ν/2−aμ)Uj−1m+1​=Ujm​.
(34)
The spatial terms are evaluated at the new time level, making this an implicit scheme. On the whole line there are no finite-endpoint boundary conditions; in the next part its solution is chosen in the l2 sequence space.

For a grid sequence define its semidiscrete Fourier transform in the dimensionless frequency variable θ∈[−π,π] by
W(θ)=∑j∈Z​Wj​e−ijθ,Wj​=2π1​∫−ππ​W(θ)eijθdθ.
(35)
For general square-summable data the transform is interpreted as an limit in the L2 norm, rather than an absolutely convergent pointwise sum. The Parseval identity in this normalization is
∥W∥ℓ2​2​=Δx∑j∈Z​∣Wj​∣2=2πΔx​∫−ππ​∣W(θ)∣2dθ.
(36)
The Fourier symbols of Dx0​ and Dx+​Dx−​ are isinθ/Δx and −4sin2(θ/2)/(Δx)2. Transforming the implicit equation gives
(1+4aμsin2(θ/2)+iνsinθ)Um+1=Um.
(37)
Its amplification factor is consequently
GI​(θ)=1+4aμsin2(θ/2)+iνsinθ1​.
(38)
The denominator has real part at least one because a≥0. It never vanishes, so multiplication by GI​ defines a unique next value in the l2 sequence space, and
∣GI​(θ)∣2=(1+4aμsin2(θ/2))2+ν2sin2θ1​≤1.
(39)
The Parseval identity now gives
∥Um+1∥ℓ2​​≤∥Um∥ℓ2​​≤∥U0∥ℓ2​​for every m≥0, Δx,Δt>0.​
(40)
This is unconditional practical stability of a finite difference scheme: in the course's terminology the numerical evolution does not increase this norm. Linearity gives the same estimate for perturbations of the initial data. This contraction convention is stronger than a finite-time mesh-uniform bound allowing physical exponential growth.[ref]

Use the forward Euler method in time and evaluate both spatial finite difference operators at the old time level:
ΔtUjm+1​−Ujm​​+2ΔxUj+1m​−Uj−1m​​=a(Δx)2Uj+1m​−2Ujm​+Uj−1m​​,Uj0​=u0​(jΔx).​
(41)
With ν=Δt/Δx and μ=Δt/(Δx)2, the explicit update is
Ujm+1​=(aμ+ν/2)Uj−1m​+(1−2aμ)Ujm​+(aμ−ν/2)Uj+1m​.​
(42)
Every value on the new time level is computed directly from three old values. The sum of the stencil weights is one, as required to preserve constant solutions of the advection-diffusion equation.

Using the semidiscrete Fourier transform from (b), the explicit scheme has amplification factor
GE​(θ)=1−4aμsin2(θ/2)−iνsinθ.
(43)
Set r=aμ and s=sin2(θ/2)∈[0,1], so sin2θ=4s(1−s). Direct expansion gives
∣GE​∣2=(1−4rs)2+4ν2s(1−s),∣GE​∣2−1=4s[(ν2−2r)+(4r2−ν2)s].
(44)
The expression in square brackets is an affine function of s. At the two endpoints its values are
ν2−2r≤0,4r2−2r=2r(2r−1)≤0,
(45)
using precisely ν2≤2r≤1. Every intermediate value is a convex combination of these endpoints, so ∣GE​∣2≤1 for every frequency. The Parseval identity proves
∥Um∥ℓ2​​≤∥U0∥ℓ2​​(m≥0),ν2≤2aμ≤1.​
(46)
Thus the scheme is practically stable under the given condition. For a>0 it is equivalent to Δt≤2a and Δt≤(Δx)2/(2a). This is the Forward Euler stability for centered advection-diffusion calculation with the diffusion coefficient retained. The proof does not require all three stencil weights to be nonnegative.

When a=0, the explicit amplification factor becomes
GE​(θ)=1−iνsinθ,∣GE​(θ)∣2=1+ν2sin2θ.
(47)
A single Fourier mode has constant modulus in space and is not in the l2 sequence space. Instead choose a whole interval of frequencies on which there is definite growth. Let
E=[π/4,3π/4]∪[−3π/4,−π/4],U0(θ)=1E​(θ).
(48)
The inverse Fourier transform here gives the real sequence
Uj0​=⎩⎨⎧​πjsin(3πj/4)−sin(πj/4)​,21​,​j=0,j=0.​
(49)
By the Parseval identity, its squared norm is ∥U0∥ℓ2​2​=Δx∣E∣/(2π)=Δx/2, finite and strictly positive. These data are also compatible with sampling a bounded continuous function: use
u0​(x)=⎩⎨⎧​πx/Δxsin(3πx/(4Δx))−sin(πx/(4Δx))​,21​,​x=0,x=0.​
(50)
The value at zero is the limit of the displayed quotient, and u0​(jΔx)=Uj0​.
On E we have sin2θ≥1/2. Therefore the transformed solution and the Parseval identity give
∥Um∥ℓ2​2​​=2πΔx​∫E​(1+ν2sin2θ)mdθ≥(1+ν2/2)m∥U0∥ℓ2​2​.​
(51)
For each fixed pair Δx,Δt>0, take
c(Δx,Δt)=1+21​(ΔxΔt​)2​−1>0.​
(52)
Taking square roots proves ∥Um∥ℓ2​​≥(1+c)m∥U0∥ℓ2​​ for every m≥1. In particular the norm already increases at the first step, so no positive mesh pair gives practical stability in the contraction sense of (b).
The native PDF places the existence of c>0 before the universal quantifier over mesh pairs. If this means a single positive c independent of Δx,Δt, that assertion is false for nonzero data. Indeed, for every datum the same Fourier symbol gives
∥U1∥ℓ2​​≤1+ν2​∥U0∥ℓ2​​.
(53)
For any proposed fixed c>0, choose 0<ν<(1+c)2−1​. The upper bound then contradicts the requested lower bound at m=1. The correct quantifiers are ∀Δx,Δt>0 ∃c=c(Δx,Δt)>0, with nonzero square-summable data as constructed above.
Finally, failure of this stronger practical stability of a finite difference scheme should not be confused with every possible refinement notion of numerical stability. If Δt/(Δx)2≤K, then mΔt≤T gives
∣GE​(θ)∣m≤exp(mν2/2)≤exp(KT/2).
(54)
Thus a finite-time mesh-uniform bound can hold under this severe refinement even though contraction fails on every positive mesh. This is the distinction recorded in centered-advection refinement-dependent stability.
