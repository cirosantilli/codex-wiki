For a grid sequence define its semidiscrete Fourier transform in the dimensionless frequency variable θ∈[−π,π] by
W(θ)=∑j∈Z​Wj​e−ijθ,Wj​=2π1​∫−ππ​W(θ)eijθdθ.
(35)
For general square-summable data the transform is interpreted as an limit in the L2 norm, rather than an absolutely convergent pointwise sum. The Parseval identity in this normalization is
∥W∥ℓ2​2​=Δx∑j∈Z​∣Wj​∣2=2πΔx​∫−ππ​∣W(θ)∣2dθ.
(36)
The Fourier symbols of Dx0​ and Dx+​Dx−​ are isinθ/Δx and −4sin2(θ/2)/(Δx)2. Transforming the implicit equation gives
(1+4aμsin2(θ/2)+iνsinθ)Um+1=Um.
(37)
Its amplification factor is consequently
GI​(θ)=1+4aμsin2(θ/2)+iνsinθ1​.
(38)
The denominator has real part at least one because a≥0. It never vanishes, so multiplication by GI​ defines a unique next value in the l2 sequence space, and
∣GI​(θ)∣2=(1+4aμsin2(θ/2))2+ν2sin2θ1​≤1.
(39)
The Parseval identity now gives
∥Um+1∥ℓ2​​≤∥Um∥ℓ2​​≤∥U0∥ℓ2​​for every m≥0, Δx,Δt>0.​
(40)
This is unconditional practical stability of a finite difference scheme: in the course's terminology the numerical evolution does not increase this norm. Linearity gives the same estimate for perturbations of the initial data. This contraction convention is stronger than a finite-time mesh-uniform bound allowing physical exponential growth.[ref]
