Evanescent field in total internal reflection (k2z​=iκ)

The evanescent field in total internal reflection oscillates and advances in wave phase along the boundary while its wave amplitude decays away from it. With the coordinates above and time dependence e−iωt, a TE polarization has complex amplitude
E(x,z)=Aeiqx−κzy​,κ=k0​n12​sin2θ−n22​​,θ>θc​.
(32)
The physical electric field is Re[E(x,z)e−iωt]. The decaying sign excludes a field that grows without bound at z→∞. Dielectric-interface boundary conditions determine A; for nongrazing incidence it is generally nonzero.[ref]
Zero normal energy flux follows directly from Maxwell equations. The wavevector is (q,0,iκ); the relation H=(k×E)/(ωμ0​) gives
Hx​=−ωμ0​iκ​Ey​,Hz​=ωμ0​q​Ey​.
(33)
Consequently, the cycle-averaged Poynting vector is
⟨S⟩=21​Re(E×H∗)=2ωμ0​q∣A∣2​e−2κzx,⟨Sz​⟩=0.
(34)
The electric field and Hx​ are a quarter-cycle apart, so their product has zero cycle average. Tangential energy flux survives. This is a local calculation for the single decaying evanescent wave in the infinite second half-space.
To check reflected power independently, put a=n1​k0​cosθ>0. The existing transverse-electric Fresnel reflection coefficient becomes r=(a−iκ)/(a+iκ). Its numerator and denominator are complex conjugates, so ∣r∣2=1, while 1+r=2a/(a+iκ)=0 supplies the surface electric field relative to the incident wave amplitude. Thus a unit optical reflectance does not require a zero transmitted field. The same normal power balance holds for TM polarization; its reflected wave phase generally differs from the TE polarization phase. A mixture can therefore change its polarization of an electromagnetic wave on reflection even though all normal incident power returns.
