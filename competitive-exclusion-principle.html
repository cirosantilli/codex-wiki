Competitive exclusion principle

 Home Physics Branches of physics Mathematical biology Food web
OurBigBook.com Words: 73
Two consumers with different break-even requirements cannot both have bounded persistent abundance on a single well-mixed limiting resource when their per-capita growth is linear in that resource. For Y˙/Y=cX−d and Z˙/Z=eX−f, differentiation of logY/c−logZ/e gives the nonzero constant −d/c+f/e when their break-even levels differ. This excludes simultaneous bounded persistence without assuming convergence to an equilibrium. Additional resources, environmental variation or different interactions can invalidate the simple one-resource argument.
