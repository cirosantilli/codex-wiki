Vector projection (projc​a=(a⋅c)c/∥c∥2)

 ... Mathematics Area of mathematics Algebra Linear algebra Inner product Dot product
OurBigBook.com Words: 132
The vector projection multiplies the scalar projection by the chosen unit vector, giving the orthogonal projection onto the linear span of a nonzero real Euclidean vector c:
p=projc​a=c⋅ca⋅c​c,r=a−p,r⋅c=0.
(11)
For a=(3,4) and c=(2,0), this gives p=(6/4)(2,0)=(3,0) and r=(0,4); the dot product r⋅c=0 checks the orthogonal projection residual. Replacing c by (−2,0) gives the same p=(−6/4)(−2,0)=(3,0): reversing direction changes the scalar projection, but preserves the vector projection onto the line.
For a short proof of the nearest-point property, any vector z on this line satisfies ∥a−z∥2=∥r∥2+∥p−z∥2 by the Pythagorean identity. The second term is uniquely minimized at z=p. This connects a directional measurement to the orthogonal projection used in approximation.[ref]
