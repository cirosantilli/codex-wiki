Nuclear quadrupole vibration (En​−E0​=nℏω)

 Home Physics Nuclear physics Atomic nucleus Collective nuclear model
OurBigBook.com Words: 217 Articles: 1
A collective oscillation of the quadrupole shape coordinates of an atomic nucleus. Near spherical equilibrium, the ideal harmonic description has five components, corresponding to the five magnetic substates of angular momentum two. Each quantum carries Iπ=2+; it is an excitation of the many-nucleon system rather than an extra constituent. Its frequency is ω, and its energy spacing ℏω follows the quantum harmonic oscillator.
One quantum produces a 2+ level at ℏω. Two identical quadrupole quanta can couple to I=0,2,4 in a symmetric state, giving a degenerate 0+,2+,4+ multiplet at 2ℏω in the ideal model. Odd total I would be antisymmetric under their exchange. Thus the first 4+ to first 2+ excitation ratio is 2, rather than the nuclear rotational band value 10/3. Real nuclear quadrupole vibrations can be anharmonic and their multiplets split: the ratio is a diagnostic of a limiting model, not a universal classification rule.
There is a useful state-counting check. Two identical quanta distributed among five substates span 5(5+1)/2=15 symmetric states. The proposed multiplet contains (2⋅0+1)+(2⋅2+1)+(2⋅4+1)=1+5+9=15. This verifies the angular-state bookkeeping independently of the chosen energy scale.
