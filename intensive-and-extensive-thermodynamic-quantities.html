Intensive and extensive thermodynamic quantities

 Home Physics Branches of physics Statistical physics Thermodynamics
OurBigBook.com Words: 1k Articles: 6
An extensive quantity measures how much of a thermodynamic system there is; an intensive quantity describes its state independently of that amount. The clean comparison is replication: take independent identical systems at the same thermodynamic equilibrium, with the same substance, composition and thermodynamic phase. Combining their accounting boundaries multiplies internal energy U, entropy S, volume V, mass m and amount of substance n, while leaving temperature T, pressure p and chemical potential μ unchanged.[ref]
For λ copies the ledger is
(U,S,V,m,n)⟼λ(U,S,V,m,n),(T,p,μ)⟼(T,p,μ).
(74)
An integer λ counts actual copies; a positive real λ describes the corresponding bulk scaling law. Copies can remain separated: replication need not involve removing a partition, changing a container or performing any work. Distinguish n, the amount of substance measured in moles, from N=nNA​, the dimensionless count of specified entities, where NA​ is the Avogadro constant in mol−1. For example, 2mol contains 1.204428152×1024 specified entities; tripling it gives 6mol and 3.613284456×1024 entities. This uses the exact Avogadro constant NA​=6.02214076×1023mol−1, which does not triple. In the differential dU=TdS−pdV+μdn, μ is a molar chemical potential in Jmol−1; the coefficient of dN instead has units of energy per entity.
As a direct check, the ideal gas law pV=nRT gives
p′=λV(λn)RT​=p.
(75)
For a monatomic ideal gas with only translational degrees of freedom active, internal energy of an ideal gas gives U=3nRT/2, so U′=λU at unchanged temperature. Here R is the molar gas constant. The scaling distinction lets a small sample supply an equation of state for a much larger amount: material state and inventory become separate questions.
Replication does not mean multiplying every linear dimension by λ: a geometrically similar container then has λ3 times the volume. Nor does it mean combining samples at different states. Equal temperature establishes thermal equilibrium but does not imply equal pressure, composition or mass density. Density of a composite system makes this distinction concrete.
