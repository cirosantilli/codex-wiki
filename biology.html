Complex cell

Words: 169 Articles: 2
Primary visual cortex OurBigBook.com
A complex cell typically responds selectively to an oriented edge or bar over a finite spatial region with less dependence on spatial phase and contrast polarity than a simple cell. It need not have the simple cell's separated linear ON and OFF subregions. Phase tolerance is local, not invariance to arbitrary displacement outside its receptive field.
Quadrature energy model of a complex cell (E=(ke​∗I)2+(ko​∗I)2)
Words: 70
Complex cell OurBigBook.com
Two oriented linear filters in quadrature have responses Acosφ and Asinφ to a suitably matched narrowband grating. Their squared sum is A2, independent of phase φ, while orientation and spatial-frequency selectivity remain. A nonlinear output function or normalization can transform this energy into firing rate. Ideal quadrature gives an exact phase identity for the matched stimulus; finite approximate filter pairs need not have perfect invariance for arbitrary images.
Pooling model of a complex cell
Words: 47
Complex cell OurBigBook.com
Rectify outputs of simple cells with similar orientations and varying phases or positions, then sum or pool them. Opposite-polarity subunits allow response to both light and dark features. Pooling gives approximate phase and position tolerance over the subunit coverage; its extent and contrast dependence depend on the pooling rule.

Dynamic clamp

Words: 65
Computational neuroscience OurBigBook.com
A dynamic clamp measures a real neuron's voltage, updates a conductance model in real time and injects the resulting current, for example I=g(t)(E−V). It implements a virtual conductance without holding the voltage fixed. It can add artificial synapses, couple real neurons into a hybrid network or test how a voltage-dependent conductance affects excitability. Sampling delay and the injection site limit fidelity to spatially distributed biological channels.

A conductance-based model couples membrane potential to voltage-dependent channel-gating variables. It explains generation of action potentials through regenerative and opposing ionic currents, while its parameters and channel assumptions determine its biological domain of validity.

Words: 72
Computational neuroscience OurBigBook.com
The symmetric two-neuron rate model has nonnegative activity and saturating recurrent input. Every equilibrium has x=y: otherwise monotonicity of g gives a contradiction. With positive parameters it has three distinct equilibria exactly when am>2σ, namely zero and z±​=(m±m2−4σ2/a2​)/2. Spike-triggered average (k(u)=K−1∑i​s(ti​−u))

Words: 120 Articles: 1
Computational neuroscience OurBigBook.com
Average the stimulus history preceding each observed spike, after removing the stimulus mean. With white Gaussian stimulation and a suitable one-filter encoding model, the result is proportional to the relevant linear filter. An even nonlinearity can make the average vanish despite strong stimulus dependence, so averaging alone is not universally sufficient.
Stimulus-correlated spike-triggered averaging
Words: 69
Spike-triggered average OurBigBook.com
For a single linear filter followed by a rate nonlinearity and a Gaussian stimulus-history vector s with covariance C, Gaussian integration by parts gives E[sg(kTs)]∝Ck. Thus a spike-triggered average estimates Ck, rather than k directly, when the stimulus is correlated. Covariance correction, with regularization if necessary, can recover the filter direction. For an even nonlinearity the proportionality factor can vanish; then a one-filter average is insufficient.

Underdominance means that the heterozygote has lower genotype fitness than both corresponding homozygotes. In a deterministic randomly mating biallelic population, it produces an unstable interior allele-frequency threshold separating the two fixation outcomes, unlike heterozygote advantage.

Ancestral recombination graph

Words: 105
Population genetics OurBigBook.com
An ancestral recombination graph records ancestral coalescences and genetic recombination events for sampled chromosomal material. Backwards in time, each pair of ancestral lineages merges at rate one in standard diploid coalescent units, while a lineage splits at a recombination breakpoint at rate ρ/2 times the genetic span of the material it carries. The breakpoint splits that lineage's ancestral material into two parental contributions. Waiting times are exponential with the sum of the current event rates. At each genomic position, retaining the ancestry at that position gives a marginal phylogenetic tree with the neutral Kingman's coalescent distribution. Different positions have dependent trees because they share parts of the graph.
