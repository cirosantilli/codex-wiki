Biology studies living systems: how biological cells maintain themselves, how organisms develop and reproduce, and how evolution generates diversity. Its scales connect molecules, genes, biological cells, organisms and the interactions studied by ecology. These are complementary viewpoints: a protein's chemistry can explain a cellular mechanism, while evolution explains why inherited variants of that mechanism persist. See Clark, Douglas and Choi, Biology 2e: the science of biology.
Two organizing ideas make this immense subject navigable: shared cellular organization and descent with inherited modification. A neuron and a gliding bacterium differ dramatically in shape and behavior, yet both depend on a cell membrane and metabolism. Their differences become intelligible through specialization and evolution.
Keep observations separate from the mathematical models used to explain them. A measured membrane potential is evidence; an idealized two-state ion-channel gating equation proposes a mechanism. A mathematical model earns trust through predictions and comparisons with measurements. Its assumptions delimit its usefulness, and disagreement can reveal missing biology rather than a faulty measurement.

Facilitated target search

Words: 121 Articles: 1
Diffusion and capture OurBigBook.com
A facilitated target search combines bulk diffusion with lower-dimensional motion on a substrate, such as sliding on DNA. In the scaling model τ=A/λ+Bλ, positive constants A,B balance relocation and local exploration. Differentiation gives λ∗​=A/B​ and τmin​=2AB​. These expressions require an accessible optimum within the physically allowed range of sliding lengths.
Sliding diffusivity with per-step dissociation (τb​≃a2/(2DP),ℓrms​≃a/P​)
Words: 70
Facilitated target search OurBigBook.com
For an unbiased one-dimensional walk with step size a, diffusion coefficient D fixes step interval a2/(2D). A small dissociation probability P per step gives about 1/P steps before release. Thus lifetime depends on D, while the root-mean-square net displacement before release is a/P​. Net displacement is not the cumulative distance along all backtracking steps. Physically impossible printed diffusivities should not be silently replaced by a guessed exponent.

Smoluchowski association rate (k=4πDrel​a)

Words: 109 Articles: 1
Diffusion and capture OurBigBook.com
For an absorbing spherical target of radius a in an infinite uniform reservoir, steady diffusion gives c(r)=c∞​(1−a/r). Integrating the inward flux yields k=4πDrel​a, where Drel​=DA​+DB​ if both partners diffuse. Here k multiplies number densities; its units are volume per time. Molecular orientation, finite-domain transients and imperfect capture require corrections.
Earliest diffusive target encounter among independent searchers (τ≃V/(Nka​))
Words: 57
Smoluchowski association rate OurBigBook.com
If N independent well-mixed searchers each encounter a small absorbing target with approximate exponential hazard ka​/V, their minimum waiting time has hazard Nka​/V. The Smoluchowski association rate is ka​=4πDrel​r0​. Cell geometry, orientational capture and nonequilibrium spatial distributions can change this idealized estimate; an arbitrary nonexponential first-passage mean does not in general divide by N exactly.

Model organism

Words: 896 Articles: 4
Biology OurBigBook.com
A model organism is an organism used to investigate biological processes through accessible, repeatable experiments. Its value combines experimental tractability with a carefully delimited relationship to other organisms. For example, a gene deletion in Saccharomyces cerevisiae can test whether a gene is required for growth under specified conditions; adding another gene can test functional complementation. Such interventions support causal inference more directly than merely noticing that a gene and a phenotype occur together. [ref]
The striking opportunity is to ask two experimentally separate questions: what does this component do here, and which part of that function survives a change of species or conditions? The yeast cross-species complementation experiment makes both questions concrete. Model organism evidence transfer requires specifying the function being compared; a useful model organism need not reproduce every feature of another organism.

Experimental tractability

Words: 123
Model organism OurBigBook.com
Experimental tractability is the practical ability to cultivate a system, manipulate the variables of interest, and obtain an interpretable readout with appropriate scientific controls. In Saccharomyces cerevisiae, inexpensive cultivation, accessible haploid and diploid states, and tools for introducing DNA make controlled changes of genotype feasible. A haploid biological strain exposes a changed allele without a second copy at that genetic locus, while crosses and meiosis permit tests of inheritance. [ref]
An accessible readout is not necessarily a direct measurement of the mechanism. Growth of a biological colony integrates many cellular functions. Failure to grow could reflect the intended gene deletion, unsuitable conditions, failed genetic transformation, or another mutation. Matching the genetic background and using scientific controls narrows these alternatives. Ease of doing an experiment and strength of its inference are distinct advantages.

Yeast cross-species complementation experiment

Words: 433
Model organism OurBigBook.com
Can a gene from Schizosaccharomyces pombe replace a missing function in Saccharomyces cerevisiae? A modern laboratory project tested functional complementation of methionine synthesis. For each tested gene deletion, the investigators compared plasmids carrying the corresponding Saccharomyces cerevisiae gene, a candidate Schizosaccharomyces pombe gene, and an unrelated gene. The first was a positive scientific control; the last was a negative scientific control. The paper describes this design rather than publishing a complete set of individual growth measurements. [ref]
The recipient biological strains also lacked the ability to make uracil. The pYES2.1/V5-His plasmids carried URA3, a gene restoring that ability, and used the GAL1 gene promoter to control the inserted gene. Initial plates lacked uracil but supplied methionine: this selected biological colonies containing plasmids without requiring successful methionine synthesis. Subsequent plates lacking methionine tested the missing function, comparing galactose induction with glucose conditions. Thus selecting for a plasmid and testing its functional contribution were separate operations. [ref]
The apparatus can be simple and informative. In the associated protocol, replica plating uses a block covered with velveteen to transfer small samples of biological colonies from a master plate onto plates with different nutrient conditions. Orientation marks preserve correspondence between locations; gentle contact limits the transferred material. The specified replicas omit uracil and methionine with either inducing sugar or glucose, and include a fresh glucose plate supplying methionine. The readout is growth at corresponding locations, not a direct measurement of enzyme activity. [ref]
The scientific controls answer different objections. Growth on the methionine-supplied replica checks survival and transfer. Rescue by the native gene checks that this biological strain and assay can reveal functional complementation. The unrelated gene tests whether carrying a plasmid alone is sufficient. Comparing induction conditions checks dependence on gene expression, although changing the sugar also changes metabolism: it is not an isolated change of gene expression in every other respect.
If the candidate gene restores growth while those comparisons behave as intended, it supplies a function sufficient for the tested growth requirement in this recipient. Failure to rescue is less decisive: the candidate protein might not be produced, might be altered by its attached tag, or might not work in this cellular context. The investigators therefore also used Western blots to examine protein production. A detected protein still does not establish correct protein folding or catalytic activity. [ref]

Model organism evidence transfer

Words: 206
Model organism OurBigBook.com
Evidence transfers at a stated level. Sequence alignment can suggest a candidate relationship between genes; functional complementation tests whether a candidate can supply a particular function in a chosen recipient. Neither alone establishes identical native gene expression, identical signal transduction, or identical consequences in every species. The yeast cross-species complementation experiment is especially useful because it tests a conserved function by intervention rather than assuming it from resemblance. [ref]
Even successful functional complementation occurs within a specific genetic background, nutrient supply and imposed gene expression. The enzyme may work with the recipient’s available substrates and partner proteins at an experimentally imposed abundance. Establishing the same role in the donor species calls for experiments there; establishing behavior under another condition calls for experiments under that condition. A conserved component can therefore coexist with context-dependent behavior of an entire pathway. This separation makes model organisms powerful: an accessible experiment supplies a precise mechanistic foothold and a better question for the next system.

Three-domain system

Words: 87
Domain (biology) OurBigBook.com
The three-domain system recognizes bacteria, archaea and eukaryotes as three domains. In 1990, Woese, Kandler and Wheelis proposed the domain rank and the names Bacteria, Archaea and Eucarya, using molecular comparisons to argue that the older prokaryote/eukaryote division hid important differences. See Woese, Kandler and Wheelis, 1990: Towards a natural system of organisms.
The associated three-domain ancestry model places the nuclear lineage of eukaryotes alongside archaea, rather than within them. This historically influential interpretation should be distinguished from the two-domain system; listing three familiar groups does not establish three independent primary branches.

Two-domain system

Words: 127
Domain (biology) OurBigBook.com
The two-domain system recognizes bacteria and archaea as the two primary lineages, placing the nuclear ancestry of eukaryotes within archaea. In this model, archaea as a complete clade includes that eukaryotic lineage; restricting the name to prokaryotic members describes a different grouping. Williams and colleagues' study, published online in 2019 and in a 2020 journal issue, found support for this model and a close relationship to Asgard archaea. See Williams et al., 2020: Phylogenomics provides robust support for a two-domains tree of life.
This updates the interpretation of the 1977 and 1990 classifications rather than undoing their discovery of deep differences between bacteria and archaea. Deep phylogenetic trees depend on the sampled genes, organisms and inference model; the precise closest archaeal relatives and early sequence of events require further evidence. Bacterial ancestry of mitochondria through endosymbiosis is a separate part of eukaryotic history.

Biological species concept

It can be applied to preserved specimens and fossils when mating cannot be observed. Similar appearance need not imply the same species, and different appearance need not imply different species. [ref]

Gel electrophoresis

Words: 1k Articles: 10
Biology OurBigBook.com
Separation of charged molecules through a gel in an electric field. Denaturing conditions suppress folded structures, helping compare RNA lengths; matching migration alone does not establish an identical nucleotide sequence or identical covalent bonds. In the ribozyme experiments, labelled RNA was detected after separation by exposing photographic film.
In DNA agarose gel electrophoresis, the backbone phosphate groups carry negative electric charge under the usual mildly alkaline buffer solution conditions. DNA therefore moves toward the positive anode, away from the negative cathode.[ref]
The electric field points the other way: the electric force F=qE reverses direction when q<0. The apparatus is driven by an external source of electric potential difference; the positive anode convention here belongs to that operating mode. Direction follows electric charge, so it is not a rule that all molecules move toward the same electrode.
The useful result is a spatial comparison: a mixture becomes separated gel bands, which can be compared with a DNA ladder or recovered for subsequent analysis. Agarose gel electrophoresis of linear double-stranded DNA, gel electrophoresis of denatured RNA, and SDS-PAGE of proteins use different conditions to make migration informative about chain size. An electric field supplies motion; the gel supplies a porous obstacle course.

Gel band

Words: 197
Gel electrophoresis OurBigBook.com
A gel band is a localized population of stained or otherwise detected molecules with sufficiently similar migration in one sample lane. One visible gel band need not contain only one nucleotide sequence. Two different linear DNA fragments of 375 base pairs can occupy the same unresolved gel band; equal migration supplies size evidence under suitable conditions, not a sequence determination.
Fluorescence from a bound stain can support an estimate of DNA quantity, but suitable staining and detector response matter.[ref]
For a quantitative model with equal staining response per unit mass, background-subtracted, integrated fluorescence I satisfies I=km. This requires an unsaturated detector and a common proportionality constant k; visual peak brightness alone does not supply I.
The amount of substance is n=m/M, where M is molar mass. For double-stranded DNA with approximately equal average mass per base pair, M is proportional to length L, so n is proportional to I/L. In a constructed example, equal integrated signals from 250-base pair and 1000-base pair gel bands imply approximately equal masses but four times as many short DNA molecules. Conversely, equal numbers of those DNA molecules would give about four times the signal for the longer fragments in this model. Neither conclusion follows from darkness in a schematic drawing.

Agarose gel electrophoresis

Words: 620 Articles: 2
Gel electrophoresis OurBigBook.com
Agarose gel electrophoresis uses a porous agarose polymer network. For linear double-stranded DNA within a suitable separation range, shorter fragments generally move farther in a fixed run. Raising the agarose concentration changes the mesh and the range of sizes that can be resolved.[ref] A compact circular DNA molecule need not migrate like linear DNA of the same length: geometry affects passage through the mesh.[ref]
A concrete laboratory example is Addgene's documented DNA separation protocol. Its standard 1% gel contains 1 g of agarose in 100 mL of TAE buffer. Dissolved agarose sets in a casting tray around a comb; removing the comb leaves sample wells. The gel is covered with the same buffer solution in a box connected to a voltage source. Loading buffer contains glycerol to increase sample density and a tracking dye to show run progress. A DNA ladder occupies a separate lane beside the samples. With wells near the negative cathode, DNA migrates toward the positive anode. After separation, bound stain makes the DNA visible by fluorescence under a UV transilluminator. The DNA ladder supplies the size reference; the tracking dye monitors migration but does not identify sample DNA.[ref]
In the manufacturer's controlled comparison, the same DNA ladder runs in 1%, 2%, and 3% agarose gels with the same run time and other stated conditions. The changed gel band spacing shows why a calibration belongs to its particular gel conditions, rather than establishing a universal distance law.[ref]
In interpreting an unknown sample, a blank lane tests for detectable background or carryover in that blank, while the DNA ladder checks migration and detection of its known fragments. An empty sample lane cannot by itself distinguish absent DNA from unsuccessful loading or insufficient detection. These controls answer different questions; neither makes matching migration a proof of matching nucleotide sequence.

Gel electrophoresis size calibration (d=a−blog10​(L/L0​))

Words: 233
Agarose gel electrophoresis OurBigBook.com
A gel electrophoresis size calibration relates migration distance to known fragment lengths on the same gel. Here is an explicitly chosen model for linear double-stranded DNA, not measured data or a universal law. Let L be the numerical length in base pairs and d the numerical migration distance in millimetres from the wells:
d=55−12log10​(L/100).
(1)
The logarithm has a dimensionless argument: L/100 is the length divided by 100 base pairs. The chosen DNA ladder fragments L=100,250,500,1000 give d=55.00,50.22,46.61,43.00, respectively. The gel bands therefore appear in the reverse order of length along the direction of migration.
To invert the calibration, rearrange the logarithm:
L=10010(55−d)/12.
(2)
Our chosen unknown L=375 gives d=48.1116248…, between the 250-base pair and 500-base pair references. Inserting that distance into the inverse recovers 375; retaining only d=48.11 gives about 375.12 base pairs. The extra digits reflect rounding in this model, not the resolving power of a real gel.
For linear interpolation between those two reference gel bands, use the logarithm of length. Here the fraction of the distance from the 500-base pair reference toward the 250-base pair reference is t=log10​(500/375)/log10​2≈0.4150, giving L=500(250/500)t=375. Direct linear interpolation of lengths instead gives approximately 396.24 base pairs. This difference explains why the choice of calibration coordinates matters. A real calibration must be checked over its usable range; folded RNA or circular DNA does not automatically share the linear double-stranded DNA calibration.
