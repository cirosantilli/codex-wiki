Stable minimal surface (δ2Area[f]≥0)

 ... Area of mathematics Geometry and topology Differential geometry Second fundamental form Mean curvature Minimal surface
OurBigBook.com Words: 124
A stable minimal surface has nonnegative second variation of surface area for every admissible normal variation fN fixing the boundary. For a two-sided minimal surface in three-dimensional Euclidean space, the quadratic form is
δ2Area[f]=∫M​(∣∇M​f∣2−(k12​+k22​)f2)dA.
(35)
Here ∇M​f is the gradient for the induced Riemannian metric. The gradient term penalizes rapid changes in the displacement; the principal curvature term can make the second variation negative. A plane has only the nonnegative gradient term. Nonnegativity alone, especially with zero modes, does not prove a strict local minimum or a global minimum.
For a finite catenoid between fixed rings, the answer depends on the ring separation, waist radius, and allowed variations. The axisymmetric second variation of a catenoid tests a specified restricted class. Comparing with two disconnected disks tests a different class of competitors, including a change in topology. Consequently, a stationary catenoid can have greater surface area than two disks without immediately collapsing under every small perturbation.
