Inclined plane

 Home Physics Branches of physics Classical mechanics
OurBigBook.com Words: 1k Articles: 3
An inclined plane is a flat surface tilted through an angle θ above horizontal. A ramp exchanges a smaller lifting force for a longer travel distance; for a released body, it reduces the downhill component of gravitational weight and makes the resulting acceleration easier to measure. Its simplest dynamical model is a nonrotating block on a stationary, rigid surface, with constant mass m>0, uniform gravitational acceleration of magnitude g>0, and 0≤θ<π/2. Assume continued contact, negligible drag force, no tipping, and no applied pull. The only forces are gravitational weight, the normal force, and any tangential friction.
The central distinction is between a block resting on the inclined plane and one already sliding. Static friction supplies the force needed to maintain rest, up to its bound; kinetic friction has a prescribed magnitude in the ideal constant-coefficient model. These laws are empirical approximations whose range depends on the contacting surfaces.[ref]
