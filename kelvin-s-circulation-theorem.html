Kelvin's circulation theorem (dΓ/dt=0)

 Home Physics Branches of physics Fluid mechanics Vorticity Circulation (physics)
OurBigBook.com Words: 152 Articles: 1
In an inviscid barotropic fluid subject to conservative body force, the circulation of a material loop is constant. For constant density, parameterize that loop by r(s,t) with rt​=u. Differentiating Γ=∫u⋅rs​ds gives an integral of (Du/Dt)⋅dr plus ∫∂s​(∣u∣2/2)ds. The latter vanishes on a closed loop, and the Euler equations make the former the integral of a gradient, also zero. For a barotropic density, ∇p/ρ is still a gradient of specific enthalpy. Constant circulation is consistent with changing local vorticity when a vortex tube changes its cross-sectional area.
