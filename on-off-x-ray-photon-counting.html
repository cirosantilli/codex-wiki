On-off X-ray photon counting (S=Non​−αNoff​)

OurBigBook.com Words: 343
Background subtraction must account for uncertainty in the background measurement itself. In an original idealized experiment, an on-source exposure contains source and background, while an independent off-source exposure contains background alone. Assume stationary rates and identical background acceptance per unit live time. With α=ton​/toff​, let independent counts obey Poisson distributions:
Non​∼Poisson(S+B),Noff​∼Poisson(B/α).
(49)
Then linearity of expectation and addition of variances for independent random variables give
S=Non​−αNoff​,ES=S,Var(S)=S+(1+α)B.
(50)
The result concerns accepted source counts S, after all efficiencies. For large counts the normal approximation gives the descriptive signal-to-noise ratio in photon counting
Zapprox​=Non​+α2Noff​​S​.
(51)
This plug-in ratio is not an exact null-hypothesis significance. Small counts require the discrete Poisson model; searching many sky directions also changes a detection claim's p-value.
Choose ton​=100s and toff​=200s, with observed counts Non​=1000 and Noff​=800. Thus α=1/2, S=600, and the estimated standard deviation is 1200​=34.64 counts. The inferred F=S/(Aeff​ton​)=0.300±0.0173photonscm−2s−1 uses the preceding Aeff​=20cm2 and includes counting uncertainty only. The descriptive ratio is 17.32. Treating the scaled off-source background as exact would omit 200 counts squared from the estimated variance.
For a selection-design comparison, suppose before a veto the expected source contribution is S0​=750, the on-source background is B0​=2000, and α=1/2. Let source acceptance be ϵs​=0.8 and background acceptance be ϵb​=0.2. Then S=600, B=400, and S/S+(1+α)B​=17.32, whereas omitting the veto gives 750/750+3000​=12.25. Rejection helps because the background is reduced enough to compensate for lost source events. A different veto can make matters worse; source efficiency and background rejection are separate probabilities. This comparison is an independent teaching example, not the efficiency calibration used above.
If both exposure times grow with fixed α and stationary rates, S=ston​ and B=bton​ imply Z≃ston​​/s+(1+α)b​. Four times the live time doubles this ideal ratio. Misestimated background rates, uncertain X-ray effective area, and source variability can defeat that scaling; longer exposure does not remove systematic error.
