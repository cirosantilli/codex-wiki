For the wave equation ψtt​=c2ψxx​ on 0<x<L, impose homogeneous Neumann boundary conditions ψx​(0,t)=ψx​(L,t)=0, where c,L>0. Separation of variables gives X′′+λX=0 and T′′+c2λT=0. Integration by parts gives ∫0L​(X′)2dx=λ∫0L​X2dx, so every nontrivial spatial eigenfunction has λ≥0.
For λ=0, X is constant and T=A0​+B0​t. For λ>0, the boundary conditions select λn​=(nπ/L)2 and Xn​=cos(nπx/L). The superposition principle gives the displayed expansion when the initial data have sufficient regularity. The constant zero mode must be retained: homogeneous Neumann boundary conditions do not force the spatial average of the displacement to vanish.
With zero initial velocity, all Bn​ vanish, including B0​. Initial displacement a+bcos(nπx/L) then evolves as a+bcos(cnπt/L)cos(nπx/L), a standing wave above a constant offset.
