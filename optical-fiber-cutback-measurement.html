Optical fiber cutback measurement (αdB​=10log10​(Ps​/Pℓ​)/(Lℓ​−Ls​))

 ... Physics Branches of physics Optics Optical instrument Optical fiber Optical fiber attenuation
OurBigBook.com Words: 309
An optical fiber cutback measurement estimates distributed optical fiber attenuation by comparing a long guide with the short input portion left after cutting the output end back. Keep the launch untouched. For the following original functional model, fix wavelength, source output, input alignment and collected output cone; subtract a dark reading and keep the photodetector in its linear range. Renewed end-face quality and changed normal mode populations can still spoil the comparison. The diagram describes a measurement principle, not Kao's historical short-glass apparatus.
Let Lℓ​>Ls​ be lengths in kilometres, Pℓ​,Ps​ the corresponding positive dark-subtracted readings, and C the same launch and detection factor. A uniform guide obeys
Pℓ​=C10−αdB​Lℓ​/10,Ps​=C10−αdB​Ls​/10,αdB​=Lℓ​−Ls​10​log10​Pℓ​Ps​​.
(37)
Here αdB​ and its estimate are numerical values in dB/km. Dividing eliminates C; taking a logarithm proves the formula. A changed C instead adds an error 10log10​(Cs​/Cℓ​)/(Lℓ​−Ls​). Reconnecting the launch between readings defeats this cancellation.
For original chosen values Ls​=0.1km, Lℓ​=2.1km, Ps​=100μW and αdB​=0.5dB/km, the predicted long reading is Pℓ​=10010−0.1≃79.4328μW. The ratio gives 1dB for the removed 2km and hence 0.5dB/km.
Suppose an unremoved additive detector background contributes b=5μW to both readings. Then the apparent coefficient is
αdB​=210​log10​79.4328+5100+5​≃0.47339dB/km.
(38)
This is a bias of an estimator: a common additive background pushes the ratio toward one, whereas a common multiplicative factor cancels. For independent small zero-mean errors with standard deviations σs​,σℓ​ on the dark-subtracted readings, a first-order Taylor expansion gives
σα​≃(Lℓ​−Ls​)ln1010​(Ps​σs​​)2+(Pℓ​σℓ​​)2​.
(39)
Choosing both standard deviations as 1μW gives σα​≃0.03491dB/km. A shared estimated dark offset introduces covariance, so it requires a different uncertainty calculation. Near zero signal, the logarithm and this linear approximation become unreliable.
