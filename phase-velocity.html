Phase velocity (vp​=ω/k)

 ... Area of mathematics Analysis Partial differential equation Wave equation Dispersion relation Phase velocity and group velocity
OurBigBook.com Words: 513 Articles: 2
The phase velocity is the motion of a constant-wave phase surface. For a one-dimensional plane wave u=acos(kx−ωt+ϕ0​) with real k=0 and real ω, fixing kx−ωt+ϕ0​ gives
kdtdx​−ω=0,cp​=kω​.
(10)
Each wave crest of this monochromatic travelling wave advances at that signed phase velocity. Its magnitude is the phase speed ∣cp​∣=λ/T, where λ=2π/∣k∣ is the wavelength and T=2π/∣ω∣ is the temporal period when ω=0. Choosing ω>0 makes positive k mean rightward phase velocity and negative k mean leftward phase velocity. Reversing both signs leaves the real plane wave and its phase velocity unchanged. At k=0 there are no spatial crests to track, so ω/k is undefined; a finite limit as k→0 is a limiting phase speed, not the speed of a spatially uniform oscillation.
In several dimensions, a plane wave has wave phase k⋅x−ωt+ϕ0​. Constant-wave phase planes obey k⋅v=ω. Their normal phase velocity is
cp​=∣k∣2ωk​(k=0).
(11)
Tangential motion within a plane does not move that wavefront, which is why the normal direction fixes the vector convention. In an anisotropic medium the group velocity need not point along this wavevector. Likewise the horizontal phase velocity of a tilted wavefront is its crossing speed along a horizontal line, rather than a component of the normal phase velocity.
For the nondispersive wave branch ω=ck, a wave crest and a wave envelope both move at c. For deep-water surface gravity waves, ω=gk​ gives cp​=g/k​ and group velocity cg​=cp​/2. With the illustrative choices g=9.81ms−2 and λ=1m, the model predicts cp​≃1.25ms−1 and cg​≃0.625ms−1. These are calculated values for negligible surface tension and sufficiently large kh, not measurements. A wave crest moves forward through the wave packet, so timing a crest does not time the packet's journey.
There is a direct laboratory meaning to this distinction. MIT's 2005 surface-wave lab uses a long water tank, a controllable hydraulic paddle, and an absorbing beach. Resistance probes convert submerged length into voltage; calibration converts their readouts into free surface elevation versus time. Two probes a known distance apart, backed by a ruler and stopwatch, let students time a wave crest and separately time a feature of the wave packet. The lab records depth and varies frequency and wave amplitude.[ref]
For a clean measurement of phase velocity, fit the periodic portion of both records or follow one identifiable wave crest. A phase difference is only known modulo 2π: probe spacing below half the shortest relevant wavelength, or continuity while sweeping frequency, avoids assigning the wrong whole-cycle delay. Then compare the timed phase velocity with λ/T. Tracking pepper grains instead measures fluid-particle motion, which has a different meaning from either phase velocity or group velocity.
The Klein-Gordon packet velocities example makes the distinction especially sharp: its phase velocity exceeds c, yet its narrow wave envelope moves below c. A carrier wave labels successive oscillations; its speed alone does not establish information velocity. The front velocity depends on the full causal model, including angular frequencies far outside the carrier's narrow range.
