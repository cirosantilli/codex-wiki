Big theta notation (f=Θ(g))

 Home Mathematics Area of mathematics Analysis Real analysis Big O notation
OurBigBook.com Words: 165
At the specified limit, big theta notation means that two positive constants give both-sided bounds:
c∣g∣≤∣f∣≤C∣g∣(0<c≤C)​
(36)
sufficiently near the limit. Equivalently, f=O(g) and g=O(f) in big O notation. In operation-count analysis, 3n2+7n+4=Θ(n2) for n≥1, using c=3 and C=14. This supplies a tight scale without asserting that the normalized ratio tends to one.
For an example with no limiting ratio, let f(n)=n(2+(−1)n) for positive integers n. Then n≤f(n)≤3n, so f(n)=Θ(n), but f(n)/n alternates between one and three. A tight growth order is therefore weaker than asymptotic equivalence. The constant bounds also do not fix an exact factor by which a function changes when its argument doubles.
Donald E. Knuth's 1976 paper proposes using Θ for two-sided constant bounds in algorithm analysis. He credits Bob Tarjan and Mike Paterson with independently suggesting the notation. His discussion gives a concrete motivation for distinguishing an eventual upper estimate from a tight comparison. [ref]
