Bi-Lipschitz distortion (dist(f)=b/a)

 Home Mathematics Area of mathematics Analysis Functional analysis Metric embedding
OurBigBook.com Words: 492 Articles: 1
For an injective function between metric spaces with at least two source points, consider all distance ratios
rf​(x,y)=dX​(x,y)dY​(f(x),f(y))​,x=y.
(1)
If constants 0<a≤b<∞ satisfy
a≤rf​(x,y)≤b(x=y),
(2)
then f is a bi-Lipschitz map: both f and its inverse on f(X) are Lipschitz maps. The bound b/a compares the most expanded pair with the most contracted pair. To define the bi-Lipschitz distortion of this particular metric embedding, use the sharp bounds a=infx=y​rf​(x,y) and b=supx=y​rf​(x,y):
dist(f)=ab​=Lip(f)Lip(f−1)≥1.
(3)
Here the Lipschitz constant of f−1:f(X)→X is 1/a. If no positive finite bounds exist, the bi-Lipschitz distortion is infinite. For a finite source, every injective function has finite bi-Lipschitz distortion, but its size can depend on the source. Matoušek gives the equivalent definition using one scale factor and a multiplicative error bound. [ref]
Changing units is harmless: replacing dY​ by λdY​, with λ>0, multiplies both a and b by λ and leaves b/a unchanged. In a normed vector space this can be achieved by multiplying every output vector by λ. Thus bi-Lipschitz distortion one means that all distances change by the same factor; an isometric embedding additionally requires that factor to be one. A stretch factor with a specified normalization is a related quantity; when comparing sources, check which overall scale has been fixed.
Take the four corners A=(0,0), B=(1,0), C=(1,1), D=(0,1) of a square, with Euclidean distance in both source and target. For the linear map f(x,y)=(2x,y), the six unordered pairs exhaust the possibilities: the two vertical sides AD,BC have ratio 1, the two horizontal sides AB,CD have ratio 2, and the two diagonals AC,BD have ratio 5​/2​=5/2​. Consequently a=1, b=2, and dist(f)=2. In contrast, g(x,y)=(3x,3y), three times the identity function, gives ratio 3 for every pair and has bi-Lipschitz distortion 3/3=1.
The ratio chart makes the reason visible: a common multiplier moves every ratio together, while unequal coordinate scaling spreads the ratios apart. More generally, in a normed vector space, the ratio bounds give
b/a1​dX​(x,y)≤​bf(x)​−bf(y)​​≤dX​(x,y).
(4)
This normalization gives Lipschitz constant one and inverse Lipschitz constant b/a. It is only a change of scale, so it cannot improve the bi-Lipschitz distortion.
The best possible bi-Lipschitz distortion of a source into a specified target is a separate quantity:
cY​(X)=inff:X↪Y​dist(f).
(5)
The infimum runs over all injective functions, and need not be attained. The notation cp​(X) means the analogous infimum over metric embeddings into an Lp space. A calculation for one chosen f gives an upper bound for cY​(X); proving optimality also requires a lower bound for every possible f.
