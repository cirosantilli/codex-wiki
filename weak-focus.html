Weak focus

 Home Physics Branches of physics Dynamical systems Nonhyperbolic equilibrium
OurBigBook.com Words: 441 Articles: 2
A weak focus is an isolated planar equilibrium point with nonzero imaginary eigenvalues and nearby trajectories that spiral toward or away from it because of nonlinear terms. Its linearization is a center equilibrium, so linear analysis alone does not settle stability. The first nonzero radial drift in its Poincaré return map decides the direction.
For example, if the radial return map is P(r)=r+κr2m+1+O(r2m+2) with κ=0, sufficiently small positive r decreases under successive returns when κ<0 and increases when κ>0. The first case gives asymptotic stability; the second gives instability. Equivalently, if a positive local function W=r2+O(r3) satisfies W˙=cr2m+2+O(r2m+3) and the angular velocity stays positive, then the sign of c settles the same question. A quadratic planar system can have its cubic radial drift vanish and still have a nonzero fifth-order radial drift.
