Closest point on a plane (q=p−(n⋅p−d)n/∥n∥2)

 Home Mathematics Area of mathematics Geometry and topology Distance from a point to a plane
OurBigBook.com Words: 292 Articles: 1
For a nonzero normal vector n and the plane equation n⋅x=d, the closest point on a plane to p is
q=p−n⋅nn⋅p−d​n.​
(43)
It subtracts only the normal displacement. Substituting gives n⋅q=d, so q lies on the plane; the difference p−q is a scalar multiple of its normal vector. These two checks identify the orthogonal projection onto a plane without solving a three-variable minimization problem.
For n=(2,−1,2), d=3, and p=(4,1,2), the residual 8 gives
q=(4,1,2)−98​(2,−1,2)=(920​,917​,92​).​
(44)
Indeed, (40−17+4)/9=3, and ∥p−q∥=(8/9)⋅3=8/3. Choosing the plane point (1,1,1) instead gives distance 10​>8/3: being on the plane does not make a point the nearest one.
As a function of p, this projection is
p⟼(I−nTnnnT​)p+nTnd​n.
(45)
It is an affine map, becoming a linear map when d=0. Projecting a second time changes nothing. It preserves displacements along the plane and removes the normal displacement, so it is a concrete example of an idempotent map.
