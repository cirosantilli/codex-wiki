Normed vector space ((X,∥⋅∥))

 Home Mathematics Area of mathematics Analysis Functional analysis
OurBigBook.com Words: 7k Articles: 114
A normed vector space is a vector space equipped with a norm, whose induced metric is d(x,y)=∥x−y∥. The norm must be positive on nonzero vectors, scale by the absolute value of a scalar, and satisfy the triangle inequality. These conditions make distance compatible with addition and scaling.
On C([0,1]), both the supremum norm ∥f∥∞​=max0≤x≤1​∣f(x)∣ and the L2 norm ∥f∥2​=(∫01​∣f(x)∣2dx)1/2 are genuine norms. A nonzero continuous function stays nonzero on an interval of positive length, so its squared integral cannot vanish. For f(x)=x, they give 1 and 1/3​, respectively. Always ∥f∥2​≤∥f∥∞​, but the shrinking peaks below show that no uniform reverse bound exists. Unlike the finite-dimensional equivalence of norms, infinite-dimensional vector spaces can support inequivalent norm topologies.

Minimal total family in a normed space (span​{xi​:i∈I}=X,xi​∈/span​{xj​:j=i})

Words: 106
Normed vector space OurBigBook.com
A total family has dense linear span; it is minimal when no vector lies in the closed span of all the others. Equivalently, no proper subfamily remains total. Such a family has unique bounded coordinate functionals making a biorthogonal system. Indeed put Mi​=span​{xj​:j=i} and di​=dist(xi​,Mi​)>0. The functional m+txi​↦t on Mi​+Rxi​ is bounded by 1/di​, so the Hahn-Banach theorem extends it to X. Its norm is exactly 1/di​, by the distance infimum. Uniqueness follows because its values on the total family determine it on a dense subspace. For a countable family, minimality and totality alone do not ensure norm-convergent expansions; the uniform partial-sum criterion for a Schauder basis supplies the additional condition.

Interior and closure of norm balls (intB(0,1)=B(0,1),B(0,1)​=B(0,1))

Words: 76
Normed vector space OurBigBook.com
In a nonzero normed vector space, a unit vector x is approached from outside the unit ball by (1+ε)x, so no boundary point is interior. It is approached from inside by (1−ε)x, so every boundary point is in the closure of the open ball. The zero space satisfies the same identities trivially. These statements need the radial structure of a normed space; a two-point discrete metric space with interpoint distance one disproves both analogous claims for general metric balls.

Uniformly convex space

Words: 36

A normed vector space is uniformly convex if, for each ε>0, there is δ>0 such that unit-ball vectors x,y with ∥x−y∥≥ε satisfy ∥(x+y)/2∥≤1−δ. Completeness is not part of this definition; a complete example is a uniformly convex Banach space.

Uniqueness of best approximation in a strictly convex space

Words: 132 Articles: 1
Strictly convex normed space OurBigBook.com
If two distinct elements u,v of a linear subspace both minimize distance to f, normalize their residuals by the common positive minimum d. Strictly convex normed space geometry gives ∥f−(u+v)/2∥<d, a contradiction. If d=0, both elements equal f. This proves uniqueness when a best approximant exists, without asserting existence for a nonclosed subspace.

Strict convexity separates uniqueness from existence in best approximation

A strictly convex norm permits at most one best approximation from a linear subspace, but does not force the distance infimum to be attained. In the Hilbert space ℓ2, the finite-support vector subspace c00​ is dense and omits u=(2−j)j≥1​. Its distance from u is zero and no member attains that distance. Finite-dimensionality restores existence by compactness of bounded sets in the vector subspace. If Y is a bounded projection onto a vector subspace, the estimate ∥u−Yu∥≤(1+∥Y∥)infq​∥u−q∥ follows directly by writing u−Yu=(I−Y)(u−q) for each candidate q, even when the infimum is not attained.

Best approximation in a normed space (EU​(f)=infp∈U​∥f−p∥)

Words: 190 Articles: 2
Normed vector space OurBigBook.com
Given f in a normed vector space and a vector subspace U, an element p∗​∈U is a best approximation if ∥f−p∗​∥=infp∈U​∥f−p∥. Existence is automatic for a finite-dimensional vector subspace: a minimizing sequence can be bounded by the triangle inequality, and a subsequence converges by compactness in finite dimension. An arbitrary infinite-dimensional vector subspace need not contain a minimizer. The norm matters: best uniform approximation uses the supremum norm, while L1 approximation minimizes integrated absolute error.

Orthogonal splitting with bounded volume ratio (L≤32vr(E)2)

To see this, take ε-nets in two orthogonal half-dimensional unit spheres, each of size at most (1+2/ε)k, and rotate them uniformly together. Point-evaluation kernel is not closed in the integral norm

Words: 231
Normed vector space OurBigBook.com
On continuous functions on a compact interval, evaluation at an interior point is not continuous in the integral norm. Narrow triangular peaks have fixed point value and integral tending to zero. Subtracting such a peak from the constant function one gives functions vanishing at the point and converging in the integral norm to one. Thus the evaluation kernel is not closed, although it is closed in the uniform norm.
Here is a fully computed endpoint version, now using the L2 norm on the actual continuous functions C([0,1]). For each integer n≥1, set fn​(x)=max(1−nx,0). Its point evaluation at zero is E(fn​)=fn​(0)=1, while
∥fn​∥22​=∫01/n​(1−nx)2dx=3n1​,F(fn​)=∫01/n​(1−nx)dx=2n1​.
(1)
Consequently fn​→0 in the L2 norm but E(fn​)→0: point evaluation is discontinuous on this normed space. Quantitatively ∣E(fn​)∣/∥fn​∥2​=3n​ is unbounded. At n=100, the L2 norm is approximately 0.057735, the integral is 0.005, and the point value is still 1. In the supremum norm, ∥fn​∥∞​=1 and ∣E(f)∣≤∥f∥∞​, so E has operator norm one instead.
Set gn​=1−fn​. Then E(gn​)=0 and ∥gn​−1∥2​=1/3n​→0, although E(1)=1. Thus the kernel of E fails to be a closed set. On the full L2 space, a further distinction is essential: its elements are equivalence classes under equality almost everywhere, and changing a representative at zero does not change its class. Arbitrary point evaluation is therefore not even well-defined there. Restricting first to continuous functions makes the discontinuity statement precise.[ref]

Completion of a normed space (X↪X)

Words: 136
Normed vector space OurBigBook.com
The completion of a normed vector space X is a Banach space containing an isometric dense copy of X. Construct it from Cauchy sequences modulo sequences whose difference tends to zero; define the norm by ∥[(xn​)]∥=limn​∥xn​∥. Equivalently, the canonical evaluation map into the bidual space is an isometry by the Hahn-Banach theorem, and the closure of its image supplies a completion.
A Banach space is precisely a normed vector space in which every Cauchy sequence converges in norm to an element of the same space. Completion of a normed space fills missing limits without changing distances between existing elements. For example, C([0,1]) with the L2 norm completes to L2 space on the interval with Lebesgue measure. The bounded integral functional extends uniquely to that completion of a normed space: define F(f)=limk​F(fk​) for any approximating sequence of continuous functions. Its bound makes this independent of the approximation. The unbounded point evaluation has no continuous extension agreeing with its values on all continuous functions.[ref]

Dual norm (∥p∥∗​=sup∥v∥≤1​∣⟨p,v⟩∣)

Words: 625 Articles: 2
Norm OurBigBook.com
A dual norm measures how strongly a bounded linear functional can respond to a unit-sized input. If p is a bounded linear functional on a normed vector space X, write ⟨p,x⟩=p(x) and define
∥p∥∗​=sup∥x∥≤1​∣p(x)∣.
(2)
It is the operator norm of p when its scalar output is measured by absolute value. On Rn, represent p(x)=aTx using a coefficient vector a. The unit ball is symmetric, so maximizing ∣aTx∣ gives the same value as maximizing aTx: negate an input whenever its output is negative.[ref]
For an original two-coordinate example, fix a=(2,−1)T, so p(x)=2x1​−x2​. The input norm changes the allowable unit ball and hence the dual norm:
∥x∥1​≤1∥x∥2​≤1∥x∥∞​≤1​:∥p∥∗​=2,:∥p∥∗​=5​,:∥p∥∗​=3,​​x∗​=(1,0)T,x∗​=(2,−1)T/5​,x∗​=(1,−1)T.​
(3)
Under the L1 norm, ∣2x1​−x2​∣≤2(∣x1​∣+∣x2​∣)≤2, and (1,0)T attains the bound. Under the Euclidean norm, the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality gives ∣aTx∣≤∥a∥2​∥x∥2​≤5​, with equality at the displayed normalized vector. Under the maximum norm, each coordinate can reach unit absolute value, so ∣2x1​−x2​∣≤2+1=3; (1,−1)T attains it. Thus the dual norms of these three vector norms are respectively the maximum norm, the Euclidean norm, and the L1 norm. For 1≤p≤∞, Lp norms on a finite coordinate space pair with their Holder conjugate exponents.[ref]
This geometry explains the usefulness of a dual norm: it translates a bound on inputs into the sharp bound on a linear output. The support function of the unit ball gives its value in the coefficient direction a. Changing the input budget changes the set of possible inputs; the three different answers do not conflict. In finite dimensions a closed unit ball is compact, so a continuous linear functional achieves its maximum absolute value. In an infinite-dimensional normed vector space, the supremum remains the definition and need not be achieved.

Dual norm as a worst-case linear error bound (∣aTδx∣≤∥a∥∗​ε)

Words: 236
Dual norm OurBigBook.com
Suppose a perturbation δx satisfies ∥δx∥≤ε, with ε≥0. Absolute homogeneity of a norm and the dual norm definition give
∣p(x+δx)−p(x)∣=∣p(δx)∣≤∥p∥∗​∥δx∥≤ε∥p∥∗​.
(4)
For nonzero δx, divide it by its norm to obtain an input in the unit ball; for zero perturbation the inequality is immediate. This is an exact error bound for a linear functional, for every perturbation in the stated input set.
For p(x)=2x1​−x2​ and a budget ε=0.1, the sharp output-error bounds are 0.2 for ∥δx∥1​≤0.1, 5​/10≃0.224 for ∥δx∥2​≤0.1, and 0.3 for ∥δx∥∞​≤0.1. Scale the maximizing vectors from the preceding example by 0.1 to attain the positive endpoints; their negatives attain the negative endpoints. The L1 norm budget constrains a total absolute error; the maximum norm budget permits each coordinate to have that error separately. These budgets define nested sets and carry different information.
The coordinates must use the stated scale and units. Rescaling one coordinate changes both its coefficient in a and the meaning of its input budget. For a nonlinear differentiable function with scalar values, its Fréchet derivative at the base point is a linear functional governing the first-order response. An exact finite-perturbation error bound additionally needs control of the Taylor remainder or of the Fréchet derivative along the path.

Weighted one-norm on l2 (Jw​(u)=∑k​wk​∣uk​∣)

Unitary variational formula for the trace norm (∥X∥1​=maxU​∣Tr(XU)∣)

For a finite square matrix X, maximize over unitary operators. Write its singular value decomposition as X=PDQ†. Then ∣Tr(XU)∣=∣Tr(DQ†UP)∣≤∑j​Djj​, since every diagonal entry of a unitary has modulus at most one. Choosing U=QP† attains equality, proving the formula for the trace norm.

Orthogonal invariance of the Euclidean norm (∥Qx∥2​=∥x∥2​)

Hessian of the Euclidean norm (D2∣x∣=∣x∣−1(I−x^x^T))

Words: 87

For a nonzero vector x∈Rn, put r=∣x∣ and x^=x/r. The gradient of the Euclidean norm is ∇r=x^. Differentiate its components to obtain the Hessian matrix
∂i​∂j​r=rδij​​−r3xi​xj​​,D2r=r1​(I−x^x^T).
(12)
The factor I−x^x^T is the orthogonal projection matrix onto the orthogonal complement of the radial direction. Thus the Hessian matrix has eigenvalue zero radially and 1/r on each tangential direction, explaining the convexity of the Euclidean norm. Taking the trace gives Δr=(n−1)/r away from the origin. The Euclidean norm is not differentiable at the origin: its restriction to any line is a nonzero multiple of the absolute value.

Completeness of a continuous weighted uniform norm

Words: 80
Supremum norm OurBigBook.com
Let K be a nonempty compact space and let α:K→R be continuous and nowhere zero. On C(K), define ∥f∥α​=supx∈K​∣α(x)f(x)∣. Multiplication by α is an isometry onto C(K) with its ordinary uniform norm, because division by the continuous nonvanishing α provides the inverse. Thus the weighted space is a Banach space. Equivalently, the extreme value theorem gives 0<m=min∣α∣≤max∣α∣=M<∞, and m∥f∥∞​≤∥f∥α​≤M∥f∥∞​. A weighted Cauchy sequence is uniformly Cauchy, has a continuous uniform limit, and the upper bound gives convergence also in the weighted norm.

Metric extraction of a midpoint by shrinking diameters

Words: 59
Mazur-Ulam theorem OurBigBook.com
For v,w in a real normed space, start with the points at distance ∥v−w∥/2 from both endpoints and repeatedly retain those within half the previous diameter of every retained point. Reflection about (v+w)/2 preserves every stage, the midpoint belongs to every stage, and the diameters decrease by a factor at least two. Their intersection is therefore exactly the midpoint.

(17)

Topology determines norm equivalence

Words: 67
Equivalent norms OurBigBook.com
Two norms p,q on a vector space give the same topology if and only if cp(x)≤q(x)≤Cp(x) for some positive constants. Indeed, continuity of the identity at zero gives p(x)<δ⇒q(x)<1. For x=0, apply this to δx/(2p(x)) to obtain q(x)<2p(x)/δ. Reverse the roles of the norms for the lower bound. Conversely the two inequalities compare every translated open ball. This proof uses absolute homogeneity of a norm, not finite dimension.

Signed interval projection norm (qF​(x)=sup∥∑i​εi​PEi​​x∥)

Words: 114
Equivalent norms OurBigBook.com
Relative to a Schauder basis, take the supremum over successive finite intervals whose minima belong to a specified hereditary family F, and signs εi​∈{−1,1}. If this supremum is bounded above by a multiple of the original norm and the family contains all singletons, it gives an equivalent norm. Composing two admitted signed interval projections costs at most a factor of two in this norm: split their nonempty intersections according to which interval supplies the later left endpoint. In each of the two resulting families the minima form a subset of one original list, so heredity preserves admissibility. This estimate lets bounded distortion of a Banach space give a uniform bound independent of the complexity of F.

Bounded distortion of a Banach space

Words: 60
Equivalent norms OurBigBook.com
Bounded distortion means there is one finite bound on the ratio between the largest and smallest relative sizes of any equivalent norm after restriction to a further infinite-dimensional block subspace. The comparison is invariant under scalar multiplication of the new norm. A literal two-sided bound without an allowed rescaling would fail even for a scalar multiple of the original norm.

Banach norm rigidity from continuous point evaluations

Words: 68
Equivalent norms OurBigBook.com
On C(X) for compact Hausdorff X, any complete norm making every point evaluation functional continuous is equivalent to the uniform norm. If fn​ converges in the new norm to f and uniformly to g, evaluation gives f(x)=g(x) for every x, so the identity map between the two Banach spaces has closed graph. The closed graph theorem and open mapping theorem make the identity and its inverse bounded. Completeness of the proposed norm is essential to this argument.

Any two norms on a finite-dimensional real or complex vector space are equivalent. Relative to a fixed basis, continuity and positivity of a norm on the compact Euclidean unit sphere give uniform positive lower and finite upper bounds.
