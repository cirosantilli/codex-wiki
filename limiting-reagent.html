Limiting reagent

 Home Chemistry Chemical reaction Stoichiometry
OurBigBook.com Words: 198
The limiting reagent is the reactant exhausted first if the specified chemical reaction proceeds to the maximum conversion permitted by stoichiometry. With initial values of amount of substance ni,0​ for the reactants and positive consumption coefficients ai​, the largest forward extent of reaction is ξmax​=mini​(ni,0​/ai​). Compare these quotients, rather than simply choosing the smallest initial quantity. This calculation predicts the maximum reaction product quantity and identifies which reactant supply must increase to raise it. See Flowers et al., Chemistry 2e, limiting reactants.
Start with 3mol of molecular hydrogen, 1mol of molecular oxygen and no water. Assume complete conversion by the displayed chemical reaction, without competing reactions. Then
ξmax​=min(23mol​,11mol​)=1mol.
(40)
The molecular oxygen is the limiting reagent. Consumption of 2mol of molecular hydrogen and 1mol of molecular oxygen forms 2mol of water, leaving 1mol of molecular hydrogen. Initially there are 1.806642228×1024 molecular hydrogen molecules and 6.02214076×1023 molecular oxygen molecules; after conversion there are 1.204428152×1024 water molecules and 6.02214076×1023 leftover molecular hydrogen molecules.
As a check, the initial inventory has 6mol of hydrogen chemical atoms and 2mol of oxygen chemical atoms. The final water contains 4mol of hydrogen chemical atoms, and leftover molecular hydrogen supplies the remaining 2mol. The atom totals balance even though the total amount of substance of molecules falls from four to three moles.
