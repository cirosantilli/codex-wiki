Flux of a vector field with an integrable tangential singularity

 ... Analysis Real analysis Calculus Multivariable calculus Vector calculus Divergence theorem
OurBigBook.com Words: 152
Consider the vector field F=(p(x),q(x),r(z)) in the open unit cube, where p and r extend to continuously differentiable functions on [0,1], and q is continuous on (0,1] with ∫01​∣q(x)∣dx<∞. Even if q is unbounded as x↓0, the normal component on an x-face has the finite trace ±p(x). The two y-faces have finite improper integral fluxes ±∫01​q(x)dx and therefore cancel. The remaining face contributions give
outward flux=p(1)−p(0)+r(1)−r(0)​.
(66)
To justify this use of the divergence theorem, first apply it to [ε,1]×[0,1]2, on which the field is regular. Its divergence is p′(x)+r′(z), independent of q. Its outward flux is p(1)−p(ε)+(1−ε)[r(1)−r(0)]. Taking ε↓0 yields the displayed answer, and absolute convergence makes the two y-face limits finite separately. For example, q(x)=1/[x(1+(lnx)2)] diverges at zero but
∫01​q(x)dx=[arctan(lnx)]0+1​=2π​.
(67)
This argument defines the normal traces and the face integrals explicitly; it does not assert that the whole vector field has a finite value on the singular face.
