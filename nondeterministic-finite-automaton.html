Nondeterministic finite automaton

 Home Mathematics Area of mathematics Foundations of mathematics Formal language theory Finite-state machine
OurBigBook.com Words: 599 Articles: 8
A nondeterministic finite automaton allows a set of possible destinations for each automaton state and input symbol. Without the extra moves permitted in an Epsilon-NFA, replace the DFA function by Δ:Q×Σ→P(Q), where P(Q) is the power set. A word is accepted when at least one path consumes all its symbols and ends in an accepting automaton state. These choices express existential quantification, not probability: no chance of choosing a particular path is assigned. One successful path suffices even if other paths fail.
Consider a three-state NFA accepting exactly the words of binary digits ending in 01. Its initial automaton state q0​ loops on both 0 and 1; it also has an automaton transition q0​0​q1​, followed by q1​1​q2​. Only q2​ is accepting, and all unlisted automaton transitions are absent. Staying at q0​ skips an arbitrary beginning; leaving it guesses that the current zero starts the final two symbols.
The word 01 accepts along q0​→q1​→q2​. The word 1101 accepts along q0​→q0​→q0​→q1​→q2​. The word 010 rejects: a path reaching q2​ after 01 cannot consume the remaining zero, while the other paths end outside q2​. Thus finding 01 somewhere is insufficient; it must occur at the end.
To simulate the NFA deterministically, retain the set S of all currently possible automaton states. The powerset construction updates S by taking the union of the destinations of its members. Here only {q0​}, {q0​,q1​}, and {q0​,q2​} are reachable, although the full power set has eight members. Reading 010 gives {q0​}→{q0​,q1​}→{q0​,q2​}→{q0​,q1​}, which rejects. In general, an N-state NFA yields a DFA with at most 2N automaton states. Nondeterminism can simplify a description, while the powerset construction keeps the memory finite.
The second generator checks all 511 words of up to eight binary digits against the direct test of whether their final two symbols are 01. Exactly 127 accept, and only the three listed sets of automaton states occur. In particular, the empty word rejects for this NFA.
Michael O. Rabin and Dana Scott's 1959 paper studied finite-state machines as recognizers with finite internal memory. Its section 5 defines NFAs through successful paths, and theorem 11 proves their equivalence to DFAs by the powerset construction.
