KHP standardization protocol

Columbia University's institutional laboratory manual, Experiment 1, Glassware Calibration, Primary and Secondary Standards, and Manual Titrations, Part 3, specifies two weighed 0.2–0.3g portions of potassium hydrogen phthalate, transferred completely into separate 250mL Erlenmeyer flasks. Dissolve each in approximately 100mL distilled water; any warmed sample cools to room temperature before titration. Add two or three drops of phenolphthalein. Rinse and condition a clamped burette with the diluted sodium hydroxide, then titrate while swirling to faint pink lasting about 20 seconds. Calculate concentration from each portion's own mass and delivered titre; repeat if results scatter. Part 1 identifies a Mettler balance, and Part 2 includes paired weighings of exposed sodium hydroxide and potassium hydrogen phthalate. These are prescribed procedures, not reported measurements.[ref]
The same manual directs keeping the diluted sodium hydroxide covered and limiting air exposure after titrant standardization.[ref] The MIT handout explains the associated carbon dioxide problem: carbonate contamination of sodium hydroxide is controlled during preparation, bottles are promptly capped, and atmospheric carbon dioxide makes the pink phenolphthalein endpoint fade.[ref] Consequently, a standardized concentration applies to the stored chemical solution under its handling conditions; continued uptake of carbon dioxide can change the reacting inventory per delivered volume.
The color is the readout, and stoichiometry supplies the inference. Phenolphthalein indicates a pH range, so its first reproducible pale pink is an operational titration endpoint, rather than a direct observation of equal reacting inventories. A potentiometric titration can locate an endpoint from a pH versus volume curve, but glass electrode calibration alone still does not determine sodium hydroxide concentration. Repeat portions test scatter; incomplete dissolution, an unmatched titration blank, or a shared endpoint error can bias all repeats together.
