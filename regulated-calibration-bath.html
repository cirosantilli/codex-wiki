Regulated calibration bath

 ... Physics Branches of physics Statistical physics Thermodynamics Thermal equilibrium Heat bath
OurBigBook.com Words: 229
A regulated bath holds its temperature near a chosen setting through feedback control, while stirring reduces internal temperature gradients. Its finite heat capacity buffers disturbances; external heating and cooling replace the ideal heat bath's unlimited capacity. This is useful for thermometer calibration and for maintaining a blackbody calibration source at a known temperature.
Joel B. Fowler's A Third Generation Water Bath Based Blackbody Source reports a NIST test of a stirred water bath containing a copper blackbody cavity. A bottom plate supplied heating and cooling; a double stirrer circulated water. Electronic feedback control regulated heating. An electronic thermometer with a thermistor probe provided the readout. The preparation was to approach a selected setting, wait for stabilization, adjust against the measured temperature and then log continuously.[ref]
To test spatial uniformity, one thermistor stayed near the cavity tip while another visited ten locations at three heights. The simultaneous reference distinguished spatial differences from temporal drift. An insulated dome reduced thermal conduction along probe casings. Table 1 reports nonzero departures at several settings: circulation did not make every location identical. The measurement uncertainty analysis separately included uniformity, stability, setting, immersion and readout contributions. These tests supported a bounded temperature assignment around the cavity, rather than infinite heat capacity or perfect thermal equilibrium throughout the apparatus.[ref]
