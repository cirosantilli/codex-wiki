Overfitting

 ... Mathematics Area of mathematics Probability and statistics Statistical model Statistical modelling Statistical learning
OurBigBook.com Words: 230 Articles: 1
Overfitting occurs when further adaptation improves training error while degrading performance on new data, for example by fitting incidental disturbances instead of a stable regression function. A gap between training error and test error alone does not prove that a simpler rule would generalize better. Compare candidates using cross-validation or a validation set; reserve the test set for assessment. Regularization and early stopping can limit adaptation. [ref]
Evaluate the training error example at zj​=−1+2(j+1/2)/400, j=0,…,399, none coinciding with a training input. The mean squared discrepancy 400−1∑j​(f​(zj​)−f(zj​))2 is 0.373259, 0.000255, and 0.006319 for degrees 1, 2, and 7. Thus degree 7 improves training error but worsens recovery of the target relative to degree 2. Degree 1 misses the target's curvature, illustrating underfitting.
These are errors against a known noiseless target, not test errors against fresh noisy responses. In a separate noiseless control, degrees 2 and 7 both recover this target exactly; their numerical held-out errors are approximately 3.77×10−31 and 3.72×10−31. The fixed-disturbance simulation illustrates a mechanism, not a universal numerical benchmark or a claim that higher polynomial degree always harms generalization.
