Green theorem (∮∂D​(Pdx+Qdy)=∬D​(Qx​−Py​)dA)

 ... Area of mathematics Analysis Real analysis Calculus Multivariable calculus Vector calculus
OurBigBook.com Words: 850 Articles: 3
Green’s theorem converts circulation around a positively oriented planar boundary into the area integral of scalar curl.
Let D be a bounded region in the Euclidean plane whose boundary is a finite union of disjoint, piecewise smooth simple closed curves. Suppose the vector field F=(P,Q) is continuously differentiable on an open set containing the whole closed region. With positive boundary orientation,
∮∂D​F⋅dr=∮∂D​(Pdx+Qdy)=∬D​(∂x∂Q​−∂y∂P​)dA.
(70)
The partial derivatives Qx​−Py​ give the scalar curl: the third component of the curl of (P,Q,0). For a parametric curve γ(t)=(x(t),y(t)), the boundary line integral has integrand P(γ(t))x′(t)+Q(γ(t))y′(t).[ref]
Positive boundary orientation keeps D on the left as one walks along each boundary component: the outer curve is counterclockwise, and curves around holes are clockwise. Every component belongs in the line integral. The vector field must be regular throughout D, but need not extend into a hole.[ref]
The useful tradeoff is between boundary and interior: integrate tangential components along a curve, or integrate a partial derivative over a region, whichever is simpler. The boundary formula for planar area uses this freedom in the opposite direction, recovering area from a line integral. As a planar case of Stokes theorem, Green theorem also explains why a local curl can determine a complete boundary measurement while leaving room for the global obstruction in Green theorem with holes.
A short proof starts with a rectangle D=[a,b]×[c,d]. Add its four positively oriented edge line integrals:
∮∂D​(Pdx+Qdy)=∫ab​(P(x,c)−P(x,d))dx+∫cd​(Q(b,y)−Q(a,y))dy.
(71)
The fundamental theorem of calculus turns the differences into integrals of −Py​ and Qx​; Fubini's theorem then gives the required double integral. On subdividing a region, adjacent edge line integrals cancel because their directions oppose. For curved boundaries, the proof uses pieces bounded by graphs and double integrals; cancellation works the same way. This is the geometric reason only the region's complete boundary survives.[ref]
