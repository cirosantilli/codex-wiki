Turán graph (Tr​(n))

 ... Mathematics Area of mathematics Foundations of mathematics Graph theory Extremal graph theory Turán's theorem
OurBigBook.com Words: 355 Articles: 1
The Turán graph Tr​(n) is a complete multipartite graph with r independent sets of sizes differing by at most one, with every cross edge present. Empty parts are allowed when r>n.[ref]
In this example, the independent sets are A={a1​,a2​,a3​}, B={b1​,b2​,b3​} and C={c1​,c2​}. Count the edges between each pair of parts:
t3​(8)=3⋅3+3⋅2+3⋅2=9+6+6=21.
(8)
Any four vertices put at least two in the same independent set, by the pigeonhole principle, so they cannot form K4​. Conversely, every choice of one vertex from each part is a triangle in a graph, giving 3⋅3⋅2=18 triangles in a graph. Clique exclusion here does not mean triangle-free.
For the exact count, Euclidean division writes n=rq+s, where q=⌊n/r⌋ uses the floor function and s=nmodr is the remainder, with 0≤s<r. There are s parts of size q+1 and r−s of size q. For any part sizes a1​,…,ar​ summing to n, count cross edges by expanding the square:
e(Ka1​,…,ar​​)=∑i<j​ai​aj​=21​(n2−∑i=1r​ai2​).
(9)
Therefore
tr​(n)=21​[n2−s(q+1)2−(r−s)q2]=(1−r1​)2n2​−2rs(r−s)​.
(10)
For n=8,r=3, we have q=2,s=2, giving (64−2⋅9−1⋅4)/2=21. The Quadratic Turan edge bound gives 64/3 instead: the exact correction subtracts 1/3.
Balancing is an integer optimization with a simple reason. If two part sizes satisfy a≥b+2, moving one vertex from the larger part to the smaller decreases their contribution to the sum of squares by
a2+b2−(a−1)2−(b+1)2=2(a−b−1)>0,
(11)
so it increases the edge count by a−b−1. For eight vertices, parts 4,2,2 give 20 edges; moving one vertex produces 3,3,2 and 21 edges. The most symmetric permitted sizes pack the most cross edges, which explains the sharp construction in Turán's theorem.
