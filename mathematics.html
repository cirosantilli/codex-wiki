Walras's law (p⋅z(p)=0)

Words: 40
General equilibrium theory OurBigBook.com
At any price vector, the total value of aggregate excess demand is zero when consumers exhaust their budgets. After normalization to a price simplex, choosing a price that maximizes the value of a candidate excess-demand vector is a useful Kakutani fixed-point theorem construction.

Exchange economy

Words: 453 Articles: 10
General equilibrium theory OurBigBook.com
An exchange economy specifies consumers, their preference relations, their consumption sets and their initial endowments. A consumption allocation is feasible when aggregate consumption equals aggregate endowment; free-disposal models instead allow a coordinatewise inequality. These conventions must be distinguished when specifying supporting prices.
Regular exchange economy
Words: 95 Articles: 1
Exchange economy OurBigBook.com
An exchange economy is regular when the derivative of its reduced aggregate excess demand has full rank at every equilibrium. For two goods and normalized prices (p,1), this means Zp​(p)=0 at every zero of first-good excess demand. Regular equilibria are locally isolated and persist smoothly under small parameter changes.
Parametric regularity of exchange equilibria (D(p,t)​Z=0 on Z−1(0))
Words: 50
Regular exchange economy OurBigBook.com
If zero is a regular value of the joint smooth aggregate excess demand Z(p,t), almost every parameter gives a regular economy. The regular level set theorem makes the zero set a manifold. Its projection to parameter space fails to have full rank exactly where the derivative with respect to prices is singular; Sard theorem makes these exceptional parameter values a null set.
Aggregate excess demand (z(p)=∑i​xi​(p,p⋅ωi​)−∑i​ωi​)
Words: 24
Exchange economy OurBigBook.com
Aggregate excess demand subtracts total endowment from the sum of consumers' demand correspondences. A market-clearing price satisfies z(p)=0. Under Walras law, one equation is redundant after price normalization.
Price quasi-equilibrium with transfers (y≻a​f(a)⟹p⋅y≥p⋅f(a))
Words: 119 Articles: 1
Exchange economy OurBigBook.com
A price quasi-equilibrium with transfers is an exactly feasible consumption allocation f and a nonzero price vector p for which, almost everywhere, every strictly preferred bundle has cost at least p⋅f(a). The transfer-adjusted wealth is p⋅f(a); transfers p⋅(f(a)−ω(a)) sum to zero. Equal-cost strict improvements may remain, which distinguishes a quasi-equilibrium from a utility-maximizing equilibrium. Nonnegative prices need extra assumptions if goods may be undesirable.
Atomless support of a Pareto allocation
Words: 57
Price quasi-equilibrium with transfers OurBigBook.com
For an atomless measure of consumers with measurable, complete, transitive and continuous preference relations, every exactly feasible Pareto efficient consumption allocation admits nonzero quasi-equilibrium supporting prices. Countably many rational strict-improvement directions remove all exceptional null sets simultaneously. If their convex hull contained zero, sufficiently small disjoint cohorts could make those improvements in exactly balancing proportions, violating Pareto efficiency. A supporting hyperplane theorem then supplies the prices. Individual convexity of preferences is unnecessary.
Demand correspondence (x(p,w))
Words: 155 Articles: 3
Exchange economy OurBigBook.com
A demand correspondence assigns a nonempty set of chosen consumption bundles to each price and wealth pair. Every chosen bundle satisfies the budget constraint. A utility function rationalizes the demand correspondence when the chosen bundles are exactly its maximizers over the budget set.
Revealed preference
Words: 119 Articles: 2
Demand correspondence OurBigBook.com
Revealed preference infers rankings from choices: a chosen bundle is directly revealed at least as desirable as any bundle affordable in the same budget. Indirect comparisons use chains of such observations.
Indirect revealed preference (R=(RD​∪Δ)∗)
Words: 90 Articles: 1
Revealed preference OurBigBook.com
The weak indirect revealed preference is the reflexive closure of the transitive closure of a relation of direct revealed comparisons. Equivalently, it consists of finite chains, including chains of length zero. The diagonal convention is essential if the relation is to be an intersection of complete weak preference relations on all alternatives.
Congruence axiom
Words: 50
Indirect revealed preference OurBigBook.com
The congruence axiom says that an affordable alternative indirectly revealed at least as desirable as a chosen alternative must also be chosen. For a chosen bundle x and an affordable unchosen bundle y, the axiom rules out yRx; because xRy, every strict-part-preserving completion ranks x strictly above y.

Cayley-Hamilton theorem from cyclic subspaces (χT​(T)=0)

Words: 64
Cayley-Hamilton theorem OurBigBook.com
A cyclic vector gives a companion matrix, whose characteristic relation annihilates its generator and hence every iterate. If there is no cyclic vector spanning the whole space, a nonzero proper cyclic subspace is invariant. Induction annihilates the restriction and the quotient; the quotient polynomial first sends the whole space into the invariant subspace, and the restriction polynomial then kills it. The block-triangular characteristic polynomial is the product of these two polynomials.

Z/(m1​⋯mr​)Z≅∏j=1r​Z/mj​Z.
(1)
Equivalently, every list of residue classes modulo the mj​ has a unique simultaneous residue class modulo their product.

Chinese remainder theorem for ideals (R/(I1​⋯Ir​)≅∏i​R/Ii​)

Words: 60
Chinese remainder theorem OurBigBook.com
For pairwise comaximal ideals in a commutative ring, reduction has the displayed ring isomorphism. The kernel is their intersection, equal to their product. Choose ei​∈∏j=i​Ij​ with ei​≡1(modIi​); then ∑i​ai​ei​ lifts any prescribed residue tuple. The equality between intersection and product follows from 1=u+v, u∈I, v∈J, which writes each x∈I∩J as xu+xv∈IJ.

Unfolding a reflected interval trajectory

Words: 73
Method of images OurBigBook.com
Reflecting an interval repeatedly across its endpoints maps a reflected path to a free path in an image interval. A return trajectory from q∈(0,L) ends at q+2rL after an even number of wall reflections, or −q+2rL after an odd number. An infinite square well has reflection amplitude −1, so the corresponding Dirichlet heat kernel on an interval is the difference of direct and mirrored free kernels. Individual straight paths illustrate the geometry; the path integral sums all time-sliced paths with the same endpoints.

Words: 105 Articles: 2
Mathematics OurBigBook.com

Compact Nakayama lemma (M/(p,T)M finite⟹M finitely generated over Λ)

Words: 55
Nakayama lemma OurBigBook.com
For a compact continuous module over the Iwasawa algebra of a Zp-extension, lifts of a basis of M/(p,T)M generate M. Their generated image is compact. The quotient Q satisfies Q=(p,T)Q; in every finite continuous quotient, (p,T) acts nilpotently, since the group action is pro-p. Thus all finite quotients of Q vanish, and compactness gives Q=0.

Here w is a finite word over an alphabet, every letter of the word has positive probability, and B(w) is the set of lengths of its borders of a word, including its full length. Start a unit-capital gambler before every draw, betting successively on the word's letters and dividing surviving capital by the required letter probability. Total capital minus the number of entrants is a martingale. At the first full match, the surviving gamblers correspond exactly to the borders, giving the displayed formula by optional stopping. Suppose D has n distinct eigenvalues over a field and C commutes with D. Each eigenspace of D is one-dimensional. Since commuting maps preserve eigenspaces, C acts as a scalar on each of them. For a holomorphic function near the spectrum, the spectrum of its functional-calculus value is exactly the image of the original spectrum. On [0,1], use reduced representations of rational numbers with positive denominator. This reverses the nonzero values of the Thomae function, while leaving its values at irrational numbers zero. Every interval of positive length contains rational numbers of arbitrarily large reduced denominator: there are only finitely many fractions in [0,1] with denominator at most a fixed M, whereas any open interval contains infinitely many rational numbers.
