Fluid mechanics

 Home Physics Branches of physics
OurBigBook.com Words: 136k Articles: 2k
Fluid mechanics studies the motion and forces of liquids and gases. Its central simplification is the continuum approximation: replace individual molecules by bulk properties defined at every position and time. The averaging region must contain many molecules while remaining small compared with the distances over which those properties vary. A fluid element is such a small material parcel, rather than a single molecule. This connects fluid mechanics to continuum mechanics and makes calculus useful for describing flow. [ref]
Three basic fields are the velocity field u(x,t), the mass density ρ(x,t) and the pressure p(x,t). The first gives a parcel's instantaneous velocity; the second gives mass per unit volume. The pressure contributes an inward normal traction −pn across a surface with outward unit normal n. Thus pressure is a stress, measured in Pa=Nm−2, and may vary with position even in incompressible flow. Additional viscous stresses can act tangentially. [ref]
The guiding questions are what must be conserved and which forces matter. Mass conservation constrains the velocity field; momentum conservation determines its response to pressure gradients, viscosity and body forces. An incompressible flow model, a Reynolds number estimate and Bernoulli's principle answer different parts of a problem. Their appeal is that a few carefully stated assumptions can determine useful quantities before the entire velocity field is known.
