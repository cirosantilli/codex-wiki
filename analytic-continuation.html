Analytic continuation

 Home Mathematics Area of mathematics Analysis Complex analysis Holomorphic function
OurBigBook.com Words: 946 Articles: 13
An analytic continuation extends a holomorphic function beyond its original domain without changing its existing values. Formally, if U⊂V are domains and f is holomorphic on U, a holomorphic function F on V with F∣U​=f is an analytic continuation. More locally, holomorphic functions on overlapping open sets continue one another when they agree on the overlap, allowing a chain of patches along a path. The identity theorem for holomorphic functions forces agreement throughout a connected overlap once they agree on a nonempty open set in it. It also makes an extension to a fixed connected domain unique. [ref]
The geometric series illustrates why a formula can reach farther than the power series that first represents it. For z=1, its partial sum is
SN​(z)=∑n=0N​zn=1−z1−zN+1​.
(35)
When ∣z∣<1, zN+1→0, so the series converges to f(z)=1/(1−z). The rational function F(z)=1/(1−z) is holomorphic on C∖{1} and agrees there with the sum on the unit disc. It therefore continues f to every point except its simple pole at 1. In particular,
f(1/2)=2,F(2)=−1,SN​(2)=2N+1−1⟶+∞.
(36)
Thus analytic continuation gives a value at 2 without making the original geometric series converge there. Its radius of convergence remains 1: on ∣z∣≥1 the terms zn do not even tend to zero.
The pole at 1 limits the original Taylor series, although most points of its convergence circle are regular for F. Recentring at −1 gives another genuinely convergent power series,
F(z)=2−(z+1)1​=21​∑n=0∞​(2z+1​)n,∣z+1∣<2.
(37)
At z=−3/2 this yields 21​∑n≥0​(−1/4)n=2/5, even though the original powers of −3/2 grow. This is the useful idea: overlapping local descriptions can represent one holomorphic function on a larger region.
Uniqueness on a connected overlap does not imply that every path leads to the same terminal germ of a holomorphic function. Continuing a complex logarithm around 0 changes its value by 2πi, giving monodromy; the return patch can carry a different branch of a multivalued function. If two patches have a disconnected overlap, agreement on one component imposes no agreement on the others. [ref] Continuation using the rational function 1/(1−z) is single-valued: any path avoiding 1 gives the same terminal germ of a holomorphic function, even if it winds around the pole.
