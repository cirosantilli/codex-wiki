Mayer f-function (f(r)=e−u(r)/(kB​T)−1)

 Home Physics Branches of physics Statistical physics Pair potential
OurBigBook.com Words: 172
The Mayer f-function measures how a pair potential changes a pair's Boltzmann factor relative to the noninteracting value one:
f(r)=e−βu(r)−1,β=kB​T1​.
(36)
It vanishes where u=0, lies between −1 and 0 for repulsion, and is positive for attraction. Even an infinite hard-core potential energy produces the finite value f=−1. This is why expanding in Mayer f-functions handles a hard-sphere gas more naturally than a Taylor series in u. [ref]
For pairwise-additive potential energy U=∑i<j​u(rij​), the exact identity
e−βU=∏i<j​[1+f(rij​)]
(37)
turns the configurational part of the canonical partition function into a sum of products associated with pairs. Drawing one edge for each chosen factor f(rij​) gives the graphs used in the cluster expansion. The second virial coefficient uses just one pair's relative-position integral, B2​=−21​∫fd3r.
For the square-well toy pair potential in second virial coefficient, f=−1 in the core, f=e1/T∗−1 in the attractive shell, and f=0 beyond it. Its positive plateau need not be small: low number density, rather than small f at every separation, controls the dilute-gas approximation. The integral weights each plateau by spherical-shell volume; equal-looking unweighted areas on a plot of f versus r need not cancel in B2​.
