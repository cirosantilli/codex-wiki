Weakly mixing measure-preserving transformation

 ... Analysis Real analysis Measure theory Ergodic theory Measure-preserving transformation Ergodicity
OurBigBook.com Words: 381 Articles: 7
A probability measure-preserving transformation T is weakly mixing when, for every pair of measurable sets A,B, the average absolute correlation discrepancy tends to zero:
N1​∑n=0N−1​∣μ(T−nA∩B)−μ(A)μ(B)∣⟶0.
(24)
Equivalently, the correlations converge to their products in convergence in density of a sequence, its Cartesian product with itself is an ergodic transformation, or its product with every ergodic transformation is ergodic. The absolute values are essential: signed Cesaro convergence of a sequence of the correlations to their products characterizes ergodic transformations and does not imply weak mixing. For example, the transformation interchanging two equally weighted points has discrepancy (−1)n/4 for a singleton A=B: its signed average tends to zero while its average absolute discrepancy stays 1/4.
