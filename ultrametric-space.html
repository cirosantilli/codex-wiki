Ultrametric space ((X,d),d(x,z)≤max{d(x,y),d(y,z)})

An ultrametric space is a metric space whose metric satisfies the ultrametric inequality. The rational numbers with dp​(x,y)=∣x−y∣p​, and their P-adic number completion, are examples. Their geometry organizes points into nested clusters rather than measuring separation along a number line.
For integers a,b and k≥0, the definition of the P-adic valuation gives
dp​(a,b)≤p−k⟺a≡b(modpk).
(34)
Thus a closed ball of radius p−k in the integers is a congruence class modulo pk. In the p-adic integers the same closed ball is a+pkZp​. More shared low-order digits mean a smaller p-adic absolute value of the difference.
The binary tree first splits the sixteen integers into even and odd congruence classes, then refines modulo 4, 8, and 16. Each node fixes one more low-order digit. The leaves 5 and 13 share the class 5 modulo 8, then separate modulo 16, so their distance is exactly 1/8. The leaves are deliberately ordered by their low-order digits; horizontal spacing and edge lengths are layout choices, not the metric. The singleton leaves describe this finite sample; a congruence class modulo 16 in all p-adic integers remains infinite.
The ultrametric inequality also explains the unusual behavior of balls. Every point of an open ball can serve as its center: if d(a,b)<r, then d(a,x)<r if and only if d(b,x)<r. Two open balls that intersect are nested, since a point of their intersection serves as a common center and their radii can be compared. The same nesting rule holds for closed balls and for a pair consisting of one open ball and one closed ball.
Every open ball is a clopen set. For a point y outside B(a,r), the open ball B(y,d(a,y)) stays outside B(a,r): if d(y,z)<d(a,y), the ultrametric inequality forces d(a,z)=d(a,y)≥r. Hence the complement is an open set. Also every closed ball of positive radius is an open set, because B(b,r) lies in the closed ball of radius r around a whenever d(a,b)≤r. A radius-zero closed ball is a singleton and need not be an open set. Distinct points can therefore be separated by a clopen set, proving that an ultrametric space is totally disconnected. These ball properties turn distance thresholds into a hierarchy of compatible clusters. [ref]
