One-dimensional Anderson chain (H=−t∑n​(∣n⟩⟨n+1∣+∣n+1⟩⟨n∣)+∑n​ϵn​∣n⟩⟨n∣)

Consider a noninteracting electron in a finite tight-binding model with open ends:
H=−t∑n=1N−1​(∣n⟩⟨n+1∣+∣n+1⟩⟨n∣)+∑n=1N​ϵn​∣n⟩⟨n∣,t>0.
(15)
The vectors ∣n⟩ form an orthonormal basis of site states. The hopping energy is t; it is not the time coordinate. Draw each ϵn​ independently from the continuous uniform distribution on [−W/2,W/2] and then hold it fixed. The disorder strength W/t is dimensionless. Open ends mean no hopping bond connects sites N and 1; equivalently set ψ0​=ψN+1​=0. There are no electron interactions, phonons or wave absorption in this model.
The eigenvalue equation in the interior is
−t(ψn−1​+ψn+1​)+ϵn​ψn​=Eψn​.
(16)
As a simple local calculation, if a state's peak is at n0​, a neighbouring site with ∣E−ϵn​∣≫t receives a small leading contribution ψn​≃−tψn0​​/(E−ϵn​). Several successive large mismatches multiply small factors. This explains why large disorder can restrict spreading, but is not a proof of Anderson localization: near-resonant denominators and alternative interference paths prevent treating this expansion as uniformly valid.
For the first original calculation, take N=80, t=1, W/t=6 and the fixed random seed 20261015. Diagonalizing the resulting real symmetric matrix with numpy.linalg.eigh and selecting the eigenvalue closest to zero gives E/t≃−0.05962, a probability peak at site 29, and P≃6.0031. The selected eigenvector has total probability one. This is one chosen realization, not a disorder average or a measured specimen.
The clean comparison needs care. With W=0, the open chain has standing waves
ψn(m)​=N+12​​sinN+1mπn​,Em​=−2tcosN+1mπ​,m=1,…,N.
(17)
For even N, the identity sin4x=(3−4cos2x+cos4x)/8 and finite trigonometric sums give ∑n​∣ψn(m)​∣4=3/[2(N+1)], hence P=2(N+1)/3. Thus the clean 80-site comparison has P=54, although it extends across the chain. Extended does not mean uniform probability. For odd N, the special central mode m=(N+1)/2 has zeros on alternating sites and instead P=(N+1)/2; the even-size assumption avoids that exception.
The second original calculation compares N=40 and 80 over W/t=0,0.5,1,2,3,4,6,8. For each size, draw 24 independent site patterns using seed 20261016, with the size included in the random-seed construction. Scale each fixed pattern across disorder strengths. At every point, average P over the four energy eigenstates closest to E=0. The solid line is the median across these sample averages; shading spans their 10th–90th percentiles. The selected states can change as W/t varies: lines connect one sample's selections rather than tracking a single mode.
At W/t=0 the two clean values are 2731​ and 54. At W/t=8, the computed median sample averages are approximately 3.17 and 3.01. In the latter regime, doubling N hardly changes the typical occupied site count, so P/N falls. At weak disorder the larger chain permits more spatial extent. Neither a single curve nor the overlap of finite-size bands proves the infinite-system statement under mobility edge; a long localization length can hide confinement in either size.
