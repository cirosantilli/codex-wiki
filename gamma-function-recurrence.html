Gamma function recurrence (Γ(z+1)=zΓ(z))

 Home Mathematics Area of mathematics Analysis Complex analysis Gamma function
OurBigBook.com Words: 103
The Gamma function obeys the recurrence relation
Γ(z+1)=zΓ(z).
(59)
Initially, for Rez>0, integration by parts in the Gamma integral gives the short proof
Γ(z+1)=∫0∞​tze−tdt=[−tze−t]0∞​+z∫0∞​tz−1e−tdt=zΓ(z).
(60)
The boundary term vanishes at zero because ∣tz∣=tRez→0, and at infinity because of exponential decay. Together with Γ(1)=∫0∞​e−tdt=1, the Gamma function recurrence immediately gives Γ(n+1)=n! for every nonnegative integer n.[ref]
Read backwards, the Gamma function recurrence defines
Γ(z)=z(z+1)⋯(z+m−1)Γ(z+m)​,Re(z+m)>0,
(61)
where m is a positive integer. These overlapping formulas agree by the Gamma function recurrence, giving the meromorphic continuation. Since Γ(1)=1, they give a simple pole at zero and then at every negative integer; Gamma function residue at a nonpositive integer records the residues. The recurrence relation therefore holds throughout the continuation as an identity of meromorphic functions.
