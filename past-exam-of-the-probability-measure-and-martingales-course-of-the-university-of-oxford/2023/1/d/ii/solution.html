Both Xn​ and An​ are increasing, so each has a limit of a sequence in [0,∞]. Apply part (c)(ii) to the martingale Mn​=Xn​−An​, whose bounded increments were verified in part (i).
On {X∞​<∞, A∞​=∞} we would have Mn​→−∞. On {X∞​=∞, A∞​<∞} we would have Mn​→+∞. Both events have probability zero by the convergence-or-oscillation conclusion. Hence the two divergence events agree up to a null set. Since X∞​=∞ means precisely that infinitely many Bn​ occur,
{Bn​ infinitely often}={k=1∑∞​E[1Bk​​∣Fk−1​]=∞}almost surely.​

Taking probabilities proves the displayed equality in the question. This stronger agreement of events is the Conditional Borel-Cantelli lemma.
