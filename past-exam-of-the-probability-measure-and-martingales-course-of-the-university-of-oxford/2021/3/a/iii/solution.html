Consider the Borel set
D={(x,y):a<x≤y≤b}.
(40)
Its indicator function is nonnegative and measurable, so the Tonelli theorem applies to the product measure, without needing any separate absolute-integrability argument. At a fixed y∈(a,b], its section in the first coordinate is (a,y]; outside (a,b] that section is empty. Therefore
(μ⊗ν)(D)​=∫R​(∫R​1D​(x,y)μ(dx))ν(dy)=∫(a,b]​μ((a,y])ν(dy)=∫(a,b]​(Fμ​(y)−Fμ​(a))ν(dy).​
(41)
The last equality uses the definition of a cumulative distribution function on the half-closed interval. It retains all point atoms of a measure on the diagonal x=y, excludes a possible point atom of a measure at x=a, and includes y=b, exactly as required by the set D. If a=b, both sides vanish. This verifies the identity even when the probability measures are not non-atomic measures.[ref]
