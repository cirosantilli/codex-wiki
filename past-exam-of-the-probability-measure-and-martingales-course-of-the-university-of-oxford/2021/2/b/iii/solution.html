For each fixed r, the preceding event equality gives
A=⋂N≥r​⋃n≥N​{Xn​>an​}.

Every event on the right is determined by coordinates with index at least r. Therefore A∈σ(Xr​,Xr+1​,…) for every r, and A∈T. The random variables Xn​ are independent, so Kolmogorov's zero–one law applies: every tail event has probability zero or one. Hence P(A)∈{0,1}.
The role of independence can also be seen directly. A tail event is independent of each finite initial sigma-algebra σ(X1​,…,Xm​). The Monotone class theorem extends this independence to σ(X1​,X2​,…); since A itself lies there, it is independent of itself. Thus P(A)=P(A)2.[ref]
