Factorial cosine iterated limit detects rationality (limm→∞​limn→∞​cos2n(m!πx)=1Q​(x))

 ... Mathematics Area of mathematics Analysis Real analysis Sequence and series Pointwise convergence
OurBigBook.com Words: 84
For fixed m, the inner limit is one precisely when m!x is an integer, and zero otherwise, because ∣cos(πy)∣=1 exactly at integers and powers of a number with modulus below one tend to zero. If x=p/q is a rational number, q divides m! for every m≥q, making the inner limit eventually one. If x is an irrational number, multiplication by a nonzero integer cannot make it integral, making every inner limit zero. The order of the two limits is essential to this argument.
