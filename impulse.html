Impulse (J)

 Home Physics Branches of physics Classical mechanics Particle dynamics Collision mechanics
OurBigBook.com Words: 286 Articles: 1
The impulse of a force over a time interval is its vector integral:
J=∫ti​tf​​F(t)dt.
(166)
Its International System of Units unit is Ns=kgm/s. The impulse-momentum theorem equates the net impulse to the change in momentum. For one fixed-mass point mass, integrate its net force; for a closed collection obeying Newton's third law, integrate the sum of external forces. In one dimension, the signed area under the force versus time graph gives the impulse; negative force contributes negative area. [ref]
Three stipulated rightward net force pulses illustrate the same J=6Ns: constant 12N for 0.5s, a triangle rising from zero to 24N at 0.25s and falling to zero at 0.5s, and constant 3N for 2s. Their areas are
(12N)(0.5s)=21​(24N)(0.5s)=(3N)(2s)=6Ns.
(167)
Applied separately to the same fixed mass m=2kg, each gives Δv=J/m=3m/s. Their peak forces differ: 12, 24, and 3N. Thus impulse fixes the final momentum change, while the force history determines how abruptly it occurs.
The mean net force over the chosen interval is F=J/(tf​−ti​). Extending the interval for a fixed impulse reduces this mean force. A lower peak force additionally depends on the pulse shape: the two short pulses above have the same mean 12N but different peaks. This explains why controlling the duration and shape of a stopping impulse matters in cushioning a collision.
