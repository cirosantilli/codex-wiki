Quantile function (Q(p)=inf{x:F(x)≥p})

 Home Mathematics Area of mathematics Probability and statistics Probability theory Probability distribution
OurBigBook.com Words: 833 Articles: 10
The quantile function converts a cumulative probability level into a value. For a cumulative distribution function F and 0<p<1, use the generalized inverse
Q(p)=inf{x:F(x)≥p}.
(56)
It selects the first threshold reaching the requested level. The infimum handles jumps and flat portions, so this construction does not require an ordinary inverse function. Other endpoint conventions are possible at p=0 or p=1; the open probability interval avoids those issues. NIST introduces the inverse-direction question as the percent point function. [ref].
For the finite example under probability distribution,
QX​(p)=⎩⎨⎧​0,1,3,​0<p≤0.2,0.2<p≤0.7,0.7<p<1.​
(57)
Thus QX​(0.1)=0, QX​(0.5)=1, QX​(0.9)=3. The value at p=0.2 is still zero, because FX​(0)=0.2 already reaches that level. For FY​(y)=y2 on [0,1], the quantile function is QY​(p)=p​, giving QY​(0.5)=1/2​≈0.70710678.
A median is a central threshold; Q(1/2) supplies one, though a median need not be unique. The same quantile function becomes a sampling algorithm through inverse transform sampling. Jumps in F become flat bands in Q, precisely the mechanism needed to assign positive probability to one outcome.
