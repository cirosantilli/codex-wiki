Shot noise photocurrent measurement

OurBigBook.com Words: 509
MIT's guide uses an illuminated vacuum photodiode, with collecting anode positive relative to the emitting cathode. A transimpedance amplifier gives V1​=RF​I; a multimeter reads its mean. The fluctuation path rejects below 100Hz, adds roughly tenfold voltage gain, then nominal hundredfold preamplification and a 1–50kHz band-pass filter; the readout measures root mean square voltage. With diode bias off, a sinusoid at the test port calibrates g(f). Restore bias, retain gain settings, vary lamp brightness, and repeat both readings. The guide warns about overload; it supplies a procedure, not measured results.[ref]
The inference can be expressed without relying on nominal passband edges. Let g(f) denote the calibrated voltage transfer after the RF​ conversion, so K(f)=RF​g(f) and define K=∫0∞​∣K(f)∣2df. Under independent arrivals and an unchanged additive background,
σv,measured2​(I)=2qKI+σv,background2​,q=2K1​dIdσv,measured2​​.
(55)
The slope of voltage variance against mean electric current can therefore estimate q. A free intercept accounts for a constant background; forcing the line through zero generally biases the slope. The root mean square readings must have their mean removed and then be squared. Subtracting two root mean square amplitudes does not subtract independent variances. Estimate the variance from successive records; averaging the waveforms first would suppress the fluctuation being measured.
Additional controls sharpen this interpretation. Block the light with detector bias, temperature, and gain unchanged; this preserves dark current and electronics while removing photocurrent. Repeat this dark comparison between illumination settings to check drift. If independent dark arrivals have mean magnitude Id​ and illuminated arrivals add Iph​, their spectra add: 2q(Id​+Iph​). Subtracting the unchanged dark variance isolates 2qIph​K. This subtraction model assumes unchanged transfers and independence; switching off bias for gain calibration is a different control and need not preserve the dark background.
Vary measured Iph​ rather than assuming lamp knob position is proportional to photocurrent. Check collection versus bias to identify a range where incomplete collection is negligible; a space charge limited tube can correlate transfers. Verify that the transimpedance amplifier remains linear and that its frequency response is unchanged over the illumination range. Changing signal bandwidth should change the excess variance through K; compare inferred input spectra in several bands. Correlated fluctuations of the light source can add a contribution scaling approximately as Iph2​, while an electronics floor can hide the linear term at small electric currents. A fitted line over a short range alone does not establish independent transfers.
The gain calibration must include the same detector-to-readout path used for noise, or explicitly supply any omitted transfer. Detector capacitance, finite collection time, amplifier voltage and current noise, thermal electrical noise, pickup, clipping, and finite recording precision limit the inference. Current-dependent electrical impedance or heating can make a background current-dependent too. Since K contains squared gain, a small fractional error in gain produces approximately twice that fractional error in the predicted variance scale. Knowing a nominal gain and two cutoff settings is insufficient.
