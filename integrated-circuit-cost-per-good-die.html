Integrated circuit cost per good die (Cgood​=Cw​/(Ng​Y))

 ... Physics Branches of physics Electromagnetism Electrical circuit Integrated circuit Poisson defect yield
OurBigBook.com Words: 329
Let a circular semiconductor wafer have diameter d and processed cost Cw​. For square semiconductor dies of area A, ignoring all edge losses gives the ideal gross count N0​=πd2/(4A). For illustration adopt the geometric edge approximation
Ng​≈4Aπd2​−2A​πd​,E[G]≈Ng​e−DA,Cgood​≈Ng​e−DACw​​.
(80)
Here G is the good-die count. The correction scales as wafer perimeter divided by die side length: only a boundary strip can lose partially fitting squares. Its coefficient is an approximation depending on layout; the expression is not an exact packing theorem and is useful only when A​≪d. Counts from it need not be integers. Actual layouts, edge exclusion and separation lanes determine an integer gross count. The expected value of G follows by linearity of expectation; independence between semiconductor dies is not required for that step if each has the same yield.
For the original example d=200mm, A=100mm2, D=0.005mm−2 and Cw​=1000 cost units, N0​=314.159 and Ng​≈269.730. The predicted good count is 163.600 per wafer, and the processed cost is 6.11248 units per good die. This is total processed cost divided by expected output, appropriate to a large repeated batch; it is not the expected value of a single-wafer random ratio Cw​/G, which is undefined when G=0. Test, packaging, design and equipment costs are excluded.
A hypothetical test readout makes the inference explicit. Suppose 600 of 1000 tested semiconductor dies, each of area 100mm2, pass a specified functional test. These are invented counts for calculation, not reported measurements. The pass fraction is Y=0.600; within the Poisson defect yield assumptions it implies
D=−AlnY​=0.00510826mm−2.
(81)
If pass indicators are independent random variables with common success probability Y, their sum has a binomial distribution. The fitted standard deviation of the pass fraction is 0.6(1−0.6)/1000​=0.01549. A test readout estimates pass probability for its tested functions and conditions; it cannot alone establish spatially independent defects, detect every latent failure or distinguish fabrication losses from a faulty test. The logarithm only translates the observed fraction into a defect intensity after those additional assumptions are adopted.
