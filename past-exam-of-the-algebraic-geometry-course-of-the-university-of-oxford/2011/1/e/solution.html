The parametrization lands on the curve because (t3)4=(t4)3. Its pullback has image
imF#=k[t3,t4]⊂k[t]=A(A1).
(9)
Every monomial in this subalgebra has exponent 3a+4b for nonnegative integers a,b. No such exponent is one, so no linear combination of these monomials is t. Thus F# is not surjective and F is not an isomorphism.
It is nevertheless bijective on points. Away from the origin, x=0 and t=y/x gives t3=x and t4=y using x4=y3; the origin comes only from t=0. The rational inverse cannot extend regularly at the origin: if t=P(t3,t4)/Q(t3,t4) with Q(0)=0, then tQ(t3,t4) has a nonzero degree-one term, whereas P(t3,t4) has none.
F is bijective, but its inverse is not regular at the cusp.​
(10)
This distinguishes a set-theoretic bijection from an isomorphism of varieties in every characteristic.
This is a bijective monomial cusp parametrization with nonregular inverse.
