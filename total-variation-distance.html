Total variation distance (∥μ−ν∥TV​)

 Home Mathematics Area of mathematics Probability and statistics
OurBigBook.com Words: 952 Articles: 6
Total variation distance asks how much two probability distributions can disagree about the same event. For probability measures P,Q on one measurable space (S,F), its definition is
dTV​(P,Q)=∥P−Q∥TV​=supA∈F​∣P(A)−Q(A)∣.
(36)
The supremum ranges over all measurable sets, so dTV​(P,Q)≤ε guarantees that every yes-or-no prediction has probability error at most ε. This metric takes values between zero and one; it is zero exactly when the two probability measures agree. This makes it useful for comparing a sampling algorithm's output with its target probability distribution, and for defining the mixing time of a Markov chain.
On a finite set, add the excess probability mass wherever P exceeds Q. That excess is also half the sum of all absolute differences:
dTV​(P,Q)=21​∑x∈S​∣P(x)−Q(x)∣=1−∑x∈S​min{P(x),Q(x)}.
(37)
The last expression reveals the common probability mass that the two probability distributions can share.[ref] The coupling characterization of total variation distance turns this overlap into an actual joint construction; minimum sum of errors in a simple hypothesis test gives it a statistical interpretation. One quantity connects probability bookkeeping, comparison of sampling methods, and the limits of a statistical test.
