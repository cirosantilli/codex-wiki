Graph adjacency

Words: 43
Graph OurBigBook.com
Two vertices of a graph are adjacent if an edge joins them. A graph automorphism preserves this relation in both directions. In an intersection graph, adjacency can be defined by a prescribed dimension or cardinality of an intersection, so any ambient symmetry preserving that statistic acts by graph automorphisms.

Simple graph

Words: 24
Graph OurBigBook.com
A simple graph has no self-loops and at most one edge between each pair of distinct vertices. An undirected simple graph is determined by a set of unordered vertex pairs.

Loop (graph theory)

Words: 40
Graph OurBigBook.com
A self-loop is an edge whose two endpoints are the same vertex. In undirected total-degree counting it contributes two to the degree of a vertex, since both endpoints are incident to that vertex. Simple graphs exclude self-loops; the linearized chord diagram model retains them and starts from one self-loop.

Torus graph (TN​=CN​□CN​)

Words: 62
Graph OurBigBook.com
For N≥3, the square torus graph has vertex set (Z/NZ)2 and joins vertices differing by one in exactly one coordinate. It has N2 vertices and 2N2 bonds. Translations act transitively on vertices; translations together with a quarter turn act transitively on bonds. A rectangle of side lengths at most N−2 has the same induced subgraph as its planar square lattice counterpart.

Dual graph

Words: 49
Graph OurBigBook.com
For a graph with a cellular embedding on a surface, the dual has one vertex for each face and an edge crossing each original edge between its adjacent faces. Each edge is counted, including a loop when the two incident faces coincide. The planar dual graph is the case of a planar embedding.

Cubic graph

Words: 91 Articles: 2
Graph OurBigBook.com
A graph whose every vertex has degree of a vertex three.

Heawood graph

Words: 86 Articles: 1
Cubic graph OurBigBook.com
The bipartite cubic graph with vertices Ai​,Bi​, i∈Z/7Z, and edges Ai​Bi​,Ai​Bi+1​,Ai​Bi−2​. The three offsets give a Tait coloring.

Heawood torus map

Words: 66
Heawood graph OurBigBook.com
Triangulate a torus using the seven vertices of the complete graph K7​ and oriented triangles (i,i+1,i+3) and (i,i+3,i+2), with indices modulo seven. Every edge occurs twice with opposite orientations; each vertex has a cyclic link. The Euler characteristic is 7−21+14=0. Its dual graph is the Heawood graph. This dual map has seven disc faces, each adjacent to all the others, so it needs seven face colors although its edges have a Tait coloring.

Strong product of graphs (G⊠H)

Words: 406 Articles: 7
Graph OurBigBook.com
Its vertices are pairs of vertices. Two distinct pairs are adjacent exactly when, in each coordinate, their entries are equal or adjacent in the corresponding graph. Cartesian products of independent sets are independent, giving supermultiplicativity of the independence number.

Strong graph power (G⊠m)

Words: 368 Articles: 6
Strong product of graphs OurBigBook.com
An iterated strong graph product has vertices that are words of length m in the graph's vertex set. Distinct words are nonadjacent exactly when some coordinate contains distinct nonadjacent entries. This differs from the distance-based graph power.

Shannon capacity of a graph (c(G)=limm​α(G⊠m)1/m)

Words: 335 Articles: 5
Strong graph power OurBigBook.com
The capacity is the exponential growth rate of the independence numbers of strong graph powers. Products of independent sets make these numbers supermultiplicative, and Fekete lemma gives the limit and its equality to the supremum. Passing to every second power gives c(G⊠G)=c(G)2.
Haemers rank bound (c(G)≤rankF​A)
Words: 108 Articles: 1
Shannon capacity of a graph OurBigBook.com
A fitting matrix over a field has nonzero diagonal entries and zero entries at distinct nonadjacent vertices. Its tensor powers fit strong graph powers. On an independent set the restricted matrix is diagonal and nonsingular, so its cardinality is at most the matrix rank. This yields the capacity upper bound over every field.
Polynomial representation of a graph
Words: 55
Haemers rank bound OurBigBook.com
Choose points av​∈Fr and polynomials fv​ in a finite-dimensional polynomial space M, with fv​(av​)=0 and fv​(aw​)=0 for distinct nonadjacent vertices. Evaluation produces a fitting matrix of rank at most dimF​M. Equivalently, tensoring the polynomial spaces gives linearly independent polynomials for every independent code in a strong power, proving c(G)≤dimF​M.
Complement-pair capacity gain (c(G⊔Gˉ)≥2∣V(G)∣​)
Words: 141 Articles: 1
Shannon capacity of a graph OurBigBook.com
In each word pattern with equally many G and complement coordinates, pair the coordinates of the two types and put the same vertex label in each pair. Distinct labels are separated in one of the two graphs, making an independent code of size nm for a pattern of length 2m. Distinct patterns lie in different graph components, so their codes combine to an independent set of size (m2m​)nm. Taking 2mth roots gives the displayed bound.
Nonadditivity of Shannon capacity
Words: 62
Complement-pair capacity gain OurBigBook.com
Capacity of a disjoint union can strictly exceed the sum of the capacities. On the five-element subsets of a twenty-point set, join distinct sets when their intersection is odd. Linear polynomials over F2​ bound its capacity by twenty; squarefree quadratic polynomials over F3​ bound its complement's capacity by (220​)=190. The complement-pair lower bound is 2(520​)​>210, establishing strict nonadditivity.
Pentagon capacity code ({(j,2j):j∈Z/5Z})
Words: 44
Shannon capacity of a graph OurBigBook.com
These five words are independent in the strong square of the cycle graph C5​: a nonzero difference is a nonedge in the first or the second coordinate. A three-dimensional orthonormal representation with common vertical squared component 1/5​ proves the matching upper bound. Consequently c(C5​)=ϑ(C5​)=5​.

Incidence matrix (Bve​)

Words: 62
Graph OurBigBook.com
A vertex-edge incidence matrix records which vertices are endpoints of each edge of a graph. The unsigned convention uses entries one at the endpoints, with a convention for loops. Choosing an orientation gives the oriented incidence matrix, with minus one at the initial endpoint and plus one at the terminal endpoint. These are different conventions and should be specified when using the matrix in a computation.

Amenable graph

Words: 106 Articles: 1
Graph OurBigBook.com
A bounded-degree infinite graph is amenable when there are finite nonempty vertex sets Wn​ such that ∣∂+Wn​∣/∣Wn​∣→0, where ∂+Wn​ is the set of vertices outside Wn​ adjacent to it. These are graph Følner sets. Amenability makes surface contributions negligible compared with volume. The square lattice and any periodic finite decoration of it are examples.

Følner set (Wn​)

Words: 53
Amenable graph OurBigBook.com
A finite nonempty vertex set in an amenable graph is a Følner set with tolerance ε if its external boundary has size at most ε times its volume. A graph Følner sequence consists of such sets with tolerances tending to zero. For a bounded-degree graph, every fixed-thickness boundary layer also has negligible relative volume along this sequence.

Multigraph

Words: 85 Articles: 1
Graph OurBigBook.com
A multigraph permits distinct edges with the same pair of endpoints. Whether loops are also allowed is a convention that must be specified for a particular result. Counting edges with multiplicity differs from counting adjacent vertices.

Bouquet graph (Bj​)

Words: 50
Multigraph OurBigBook.com
A bouquet graph consists of one vertex and j self-loops. Its vertex has degree 2j, and its graph excess is j−1. The two-loop bouquet is one of the three possible kernels of a bicyclic graph; in a simple subdivision, each loop must expand to a cycle of length at least three.

Distance-transitive graph

Words: 161 Articles: 2
Graph OurBigBook.com
An automorphism group is transitive on ordered pairs of vertices at every fixed distance. Grassmann graphs provide examples with the finite general linear group as a distance-transitive automorphism group.

Distance-transitive action on a graph

Words: 64
Distance-transitive graph OurBigBook.com
A subgroup of the graph automorphism group acts distance-transitively when it is transitive on the ordered pairs of vertices at each fixed graph distance. Thus two vertex pairs at the same distance can be matched by one automorphism from that subgroup. Existence of such a subgroup implies that the full automorphism group has the same property, making the graph a distance-transitive graph. The specified subgroup need not be the entire automorphism group.

Symplectic dual polar graph (d(U,W)=m−dim(U∩W))

Words: 70
Distance-transitive graph OurBigBook.com
Vertices are Lagrangian subspaces of a 2m-dimensional symplectic vector space, joined when they intersect in codimension one. A pair with intersection dimension m−r admits a symplectic basis in which U=⟨e1​,…,em​⟩ and W=⟨f1​,…,fr​,er+1​,…,em​⟩. Replacing ei​ successively by fi​ gives an isotropic path of length r, while one edge can increase intersection dimension with a fixed target by at most one. Equal-distance ordered pairs have the same adapted form, so the symplectic group acts distance-transitively.

Vertex-transitive graph

Words: 30
Graph OurBigBook.com
A graph whose automorphisms act transitively on its vertices: any chosen vertex of a graph can be carried to any other by a symmetry preserving adjacency. Consequently rooted self-avoiding walk counts are independent of the chosen root.

Triangular lattice

Words: 26
Graph OurBigBook.com
The planar graph with vertices {a(1,0)+b(1/2,3​/2):a,b∈Z} and edges between vertices at Euclidean distance one. Every vertex of a graph has six neighbors. Its site percolation model is a particularly tractable setting for conformal invariance of planar percolation.

False twin (N(u)=N(v))

Words: 87 Articles: 1
Graph OurBigBook.com
Distinct vertices with equal open neighbourhoods are false twins. They are nonadjacent: adjacency would put one vertex in the other's open neighbourhood but not its own. Equal open neighbourhoods define false-twin classes, which are independent sets and have uniform adjacency to every other such class.

False-twin class ([v]={u:N(u)=N(v)})

Words: 45
False twin OurBigBook.com
A false-twin class is an equivalence class of vertices with identical open neighbourhoods. In edge-triangle symmetrization, merging nonadjacent false-twin classes by cloning the better local score never decreases the objective. A secondary maximization of squared class sizes forces all distinct classes to be adjacent, giving a complete multipartite graph.

Graph power (Gj)

Words: 85 Articles: 1
Graph OurBigBook.com
The jth power of a graph joins distinct vertices whenever their distance in the original graph is at most j. For example, the square of a cycle adds edges connecting vertices two steps apart. Graph powers turn constraints on short paths into adjacency constraints.

Square of a cycle (Ck2​)

Words: 48
Graph power OurBigBook.com
In a cycle square, each triple of consecutive vertices is a clique. A proper three-colouring therefore repeats with period three and closes exactly when the length is divisible by three. For all other cycle lengths at least four colours are needed; the five-cycle square is a complete five-vertex graph.

Graph cut (∂S={{i,j}∈E:i∈S,j∈/S})

Words: 131 Articles: 1
Graph OurBigBook.com
A graph cut consists of the edges joining a vertex subset to its complement. In a two-group stochastic block model, crossing and within-group edges have different Bernoulli distributions. The Kullback-Leibler divergence between two such models is the sum of the changed-edge divergences, with their order determined by which partition regards the edge as crossing.

Balanced component cut (max(∣V1​∣,∣V2​∣)≤(k+1)n/(2k+1))

Words: 85
Graph cut OurBigBook.com
If every graph component has at most (k+1)n/[k(2k+1)] vertices, assigning whole graph components to two sides gives an empty graph cut with the displayed bound. To prove it, maximize the smaller side's weight S≤n/2. If S<kn/(2k+1), every component on the larger side has weight at least n−2S, because moving a smaller one would improve the smaller side. There are at least k+1 such components, since their total exceeds k times the permitted maximum weight. Thus n−S≥(k+1)(n−2S), a contradiction. The proof also works for arbitrary positive weights.

Oriented incidence matrix (De,i​=1,De,j​=−1)

Words: 101 Articles: 1
Graph OurBigBook.com
An oriented incidence matrix records an arbitrarily chosen orientation of each edge of a graph. Use one row per edge, with +1 and −1 at its endpoints and zeros elsewhere; the transpose convention is also common. The product Dθ records endpoint differences. For a connected graph, kerD=span{1}, since zero difference propagates along every graph path.

Incidence pseudoinverse decomposition (D†D=I−11⊤/n)

Words: 47
Oriented incidence matrix OurBigBook.com
For a connected graph, the Moore-Penrose inverse of its oriented incidence matrix satisfies D†D=I−Π1​, because this product is the orthogonal projection onto (kerD)⊥. Thus every signal decomposes into its constant mean and D†Dθ. Writing L=maxe​∥(D†)⋅,e​∥2​, a sub-Gaussian random vector noise gives a simultaneous bound on ∥(D†)⊤z∥∞​ with scale Llog(m/δ)​.

Cartesian product of graphs (G□H)

Words: 46
Graph OurBigBook.com
For two graphs G,H, their Cartesian product of graphs has graph vertices (g,h)∈V(G)×V(H). Two graph vertices are adjacent if either their G coordinates agree and their H coordinates are adjacent, or conversely their H coordinates agree and their G coordinates are adjacent. The ladder graph is the product of a graph path with K2​.

Ladder graph

Words: 44 Articles: 1
Graph OurBigBook.com
A ladder graph is the Cartesian product of graphs of a graph path with the two-vertex complete graph K2​. Its two parallel rails are joined by a rung at each position. The doubly infinite ladder graph uses an infinite rail indexed by Z.

Doubly infinite ladder graph (Z□K2​)

Words: 12
Ladder graph OurBigBook.com
The doubly infinite ladder graph has graph vertices Z×{0,1}, horizontal edges {(j,s),(j+1,s)}, and vertical edges {(j,0),(j,1)}.

Cubic lattice (Zd)

Words: 85 Articles: 1
Graph OurBigBook.com
The cubic lattice in dimension d has graph vertex set Zd and an edge between x,y exactly when ∑i​∣xi​−yi​∣=1. For d=2 it is the square lattice. Every graph vertex has 2d graph neighbours, so it is a locally finite graph.

Amenable boundary growth of lattice boxes (∣Bn​∣∣∂+Bn​∣​=2n+12d​→0)

Words: 55
Cubic lattice OurBigBook.com
For the nearest-neighbour graph on Zd, a box Bn​=[−n,n]d∩Zd has (2n+1)d graph vertices and outer graph vertex boundary of size 2d(2n+1)d−1. Thus ∣∂+Bn​∣/∣Bn​∣→0. This boundary-to-volume property is the geometric mechanism that contradicts a positive volume density of trifurcation points in the uniqueness proof. It realizes the boundary-growth property of the translation amenable group on the lattice.

Vertex set (V(G))

Words: 29
Graph OurBigBook.com
The vertex set is the underlying set V of a graph or hypergraph. In a hypergraph, its hyperedges are subsets of this set; in a simple graph, edges are two-element subsets.

Graph blow-up (H(t))

Words: 153 Articles: 2
Graph OurBigBook.com
A blow-up replaces each vertex of a graph by an independent set, and each original edge by all edges between the corresponding two sets. Replacing every vertex of Kr​ by t vertices gives the balanced complete multipartite blow-up Kr​(t).

Dense clique family blow-up lemma (∣M∣≥ηns⟹Ks​(⌊alogn⌋)⊆G)

Words: 70
Graph blow-up OurBigBook.com
A family of positively many s-cliques forces a logarithmic balanced graph blow-up, which can also contain a matching of that many members of the family. To induct on s, prune faces with few extensions, apply induction to the remaining (s−1)-faces, and use a matching of those faces as one side of a bipartite incidence graph. The common neighbourhood from bipartite density estimate selects logarithmically many disjoint faces with polynomially many common extension vertices.

Vertex cloning in a graph

Words: 49
Graph blow-up OurBigBook.com
Cloning a vertex replaces it by several nonadjacent vertices with identical neighbourhoods. In homomorphism density, conditioning on the images of all other vertices reduces cloning to the inequality Ept≥(Ep)t from the Jensen inequality. Iterated cloning shows that positive clique homomorphism density forces any fixed complete multipartite blow-up.

Multipartite graph (V=V1​⊔⋯⊔Vr​)

Words: 36
Graph OurBigBook.com
A graph is r-partite when its vertices can be partitioned into r classes, each an independent set. Edges may be missing between different classes. If all possible edges between different classes occur, it is a complete multipartite graph.

Reachability in a graph

Words: 49
Graph OurBigBook.com
A vertex v is reachable from u if a finite path leads from u to v, respecting edge directions in a directed graph. For a finite graph, graph search decides reachability by maintaining the set of discovered vertices; every step discovers a new vertex or examines an edge, so the procedure terminates.

Graph search

Words: 49
Graph OurBigBook.com
A graph search explores vertices through incident edges while recording visited vertices. On a finite graph, depth-first or breadth-first exploration terminates and decides reachability; recording predecessors also gives a path. Restricting to vertices reachable from a start and able to reach an accepting vertex identifies the productive part of a deterministic finite automaton.

Tripartite graph

Words: 81 Articles: 1
Graph OurBigBook.com
A tripartite graph has a set partition of its vertices into three classes, with every edge joining different classes. Each pair of classes induces a bipartite graph, and every triangle in a graph uses one vertex from each class.

Tripartite graph encoding of a grid (z=x+y)

Words: 53
Tripartite graph OurBigBook.com
For A⊆[n]2, use three disjoint labelled vertex classes X=[n], Y=[n], Z=[2n]. Each (x,y)∈A inserts the three edges xy,x(x+y),y(x+y), producing pairwise edge-disjoint triangles. Every triangle in a graph with labels (x,y,z) corresponds to the three points (x,y),(x,z−x),(z−y,y) of A. It is canonical when z=x+y; otherwise it gives a corner in an integer grid with nonzero displacement z−x−y.

Graph automorphism

Words: 95 Articles: 1
Graph OurBigBook.com
A graph automorphism is a bijection of the vertex set that preserves adjacency. It preserves graph distances and transports simple random-walk hitting problems between vertices.

Quasi-transitive graph

Words: 70
Graph automorphism OurBigBook.com
A graph is quasi-transitive if some group of graph automorphisms has finitely many vertex orbits. This is also called a graph of finite type. A connected graph that is infinite and a locally finite graph then has uniformly bounded degrees. Every orbit is infinite: a finite invariant orbit would have finite neighborhoods, and finitely many orbit types would put the entire graph in one such neighborhood. This permits finite sets to be moved arbitrarily far apart by graph automorphisms.

Bridge (graph theory)

A bridge is an edge whose deletion increases the number of components of a graph. Equivalently, it belongs to no cycle; within its original component, deleting it makes the connected graph disconnected.

The d-dimensional hypercube graph has vertex set {0,1}d and joins two vertices exactly when they differ in one coordinate. It is a d-regular bipartite graph on 2d vertices.

Supermodularity of graph component count (k(S∩T)+k(S∪T)≥k(S)+k(T))
Words: 60
Component (graph theory) OurBigBook.com
For open-edge sets S,T in a fixed finite graph, including isolated graph vertices in k, one has k(S∩T)+k(S∪T)≥k(S)+k(T). Incidence-vector spans have rank r(S)=∣V∣−k(S); their union span is the sum of the two spans, while the intersection-edge span is contained in the intersection of spans. The vector-space dimension formula for a sum of subspaces proves submodularity of r, equivalently supermodularity of k.

A family of triangles in a graph is edge-disjoint if no edge belongs to two members. Any deletion of edges producing a triangle-free graph must remove at least one distinct edge for each member. The triangles in a graph may share vertices.

Triangle-free chromatic lower bound at bounded maximum degree (χ=Ω(Δ/logΔ))

Words: 75
Triangle-free graph OurBigBook.com
Choose n=d7 and a binomial random graph of edge probability d/n. With high probability its maximum degree is at most 2d, it has o(n) triangles, and every independent set has size at most 4nlogd/d. Delete one vertex per triangle. The resulting triangle-free graph has chromatic number at least d/(8logd). Appending a disjoint star of degree 2d, if necessary, makes the maximum degree exactly 2d without lowering the chromatic number. Thus the order Δ/logΔ is unavoidable.

Edge-triangle incidence bound

If every edge of a graph lies in at least one triangle, then the number of triangles is at least one third of the number of edges. Count edge-triangle incidences: each edge contributes at least one and each triangle contributes three.
