The neutron has valence content udd and total spin J=1/2; its ground-state parity is positive. The two down quarks contribute charge −2/3 and the up quark contributes +2/3, giving net charge zero. “Three valence quarks” specifies the net flavor quantum numbers, rather than excluding gluons or additional sea quark–antiquark components from the full Quantum chromodynamics state.
In the approximate light-flavor SU(3) classification, the neutron belongs to the ground-state baryon octet, containing eight hadrons:
p,n;Σ+,Σ0,Σ−;Λ0;Ξ0,Ξ−.​
(1)
These are eight particle species, without counting their antiparticles again as extra members of the same octet. The corresponding antibaryons form the conjugate particle set. Within the smaller SU(2) isospin classification, the neutron instead belongs to the two-member nucleon doublet (p,n). Stating the symmetry distinguishes the usual eight-member flavor multiplet answer from the also valid two-member isospin multiplet. The baryon decuplet has different ground-state spin and is not the neutron's octet.

Uniform probability per volume does not give a uniform radial distribution. A shell of radius r and thickness dr occupies volume 4πr2dr, so the radial probability density function is
f(r)=R33r2​,0≤r≤R.
(2)
It integrates to one. The second radial moment is
⟨r2⟩=∫0R​r2R33r2​dr=53​R2,rrms​=53​​R=1.162 fm(R=1.5 fm).​
(3)
The radial uncertainty scale in a uniform sphere supplies the position-uncertainty scale as instructed by the toy model. It is not literally the standard deviation of the scalar radius: ⟨r⟩=3R/4 and ⟨r2⟩−⟨r⟩2​=R3/80​. Nor is a uniform spatial probability density sufficient to determine a unique momentum space wavefunction. This explicit distinction keeps the order-of-magnitude assumption separate from a fully specified quantum bound-state calculation. Replacing the radial density by a uniform density in r would give a different second moment because it omits the spherical volume element.

Adopt the lower-bound scale of the Heisenberg uncertainty principle, ΔrΔp∼ℏ/2, with the prescribed radial scale from a. Using ℏc=197.327 MeV fm,
Δpc∼2rrms​ℏc​=84.92 MeV.​

Eq​∼(Δpc)2+(mq​c2)2​=(84.92)2+52​ MeV=85.06 MeV.​

The charged weak current changes one neutron down quark into an up quark:
d→u+W−∗,W−∗→e−+νˉe​,n(udd)→p(uud)+e−+νˉe​.​
(7)
The other two valence quarks are spectators in this tree-level Feynman diagram. The intermediate W boson is highly virtual; the available neutron decay energy is far below its on-shell rest energy. Charge is conserved at each vertex, and the electron's lepton number is balanced by the electron antineutrino.
The reversed quark-level charged current is allowed as an interaction, but a free proton cannot decay as p→n+e++νe​ because mp​<mn​+me​. Four-momentum conservation leaves no accessible final-state Lorentz-invariant phase space. The absence of this free-proton beta-plus decay is therefore kinematic, not an absence of a u→d weak vertex. Binding-energy differences in a nucleus, or incoming energy in a collision, can change whether the corresponding conversion is possible. Exotic baryon-number-violating proton decay is a separate question and is not generated by this ordinary charged-current diagram.

spν​=mn2​+me2​−2mn​Ee​,Ee​=2mn​mn2​+me2​−spν​​.

Ee,min​=me​c2,∣pp​∣=∣pν​∣=2(mn​−me​)(mn​−me​)2−mp2​​c,pp​=−pν​.​

Ee,max​=2mn​mn2​+me2​−mp2​​c2.​

These endpoint configurations lie on phase-space boundaries; their kinematic existence does not imply a nonzero event density exactly at the boundary.

The light pseudoscalar meson nonet has JP=0− and flavor structure 3⊗3ˉ=8⊕1. Its members are the three pions, four kaons, and the two neutral isospin-zero states eta meson and eta prime meson. For mesons the baryon number is zero, so the Gell-Mann--Nishijima formula reduces to
Q=I3​+S/2.​
(12)
Here S is strangeness, with an s quark carrying −1 and an antiquark sˉ carrying +1. Thus the upper row is K0=dsˉ at (I3​,S)=(−1/2,1) and K+=usˉ at (1/2,1). The lower row is K−=suˉ at (−1/2,−1) and Kˉ0=sdˉ at (1/2,−1). The charged pions lie at (±1,0), while π0,η,η′ share (0,0) as flavor-weight coordinates.
The neutral pion direction is (uuˉ−ddˉ)/2​. The octet and singlet isoscalar directions mix to make the physical eta and eta-prime states; counting three central states is essential to obtaining nine rather than seven particles. Horizontal lines in the diagram have constant S, while lines I3​+S/2=Q mark constant charge. Meson exchange describes part of the residual nuclear force; the underlying quark-level strong interaction is mediated by gluons, not by treating pions as elementary gauge bosons.

The charged meson decays proceed through a virtual W+:
π+(udˉ)→W+∗→μ+νμ​,K+(usˉ)→W+∗→μ+νμ​.

In the simple Cabibbo description these are approximately cosθC​ and sinθC​, so the strange amplitude carries a suppression tanθC​ relative to the nonstrange one, or tan2θC​≃0.053 in their corresponding squared weak-coupling factors.
For a charged pseudoscalar and a negligibly massive neutrino, the leading leptonic energy width has the form
Γ(P+→μ+νμ​)=8πGF2​fP2​∣Vqq′​∣2​mμ2​mP​(1−mP2​mμ2​​)2
(14)
in natural units. The helicity factor mμ2​ is common to these two muon channels, while the threshold factor is much less favorable for the pion. Using the usual masses, its value is about 0.182 for the pion and 0.910 for the charged kaon: the pion's threshold factor alone is only about one fifth as large. Including the explicit mP​ factor gives a kinematic ratio about 0.0564. The small pion–muon energy release suppresses the pion leptonic channel kinematically, whereas the strange weak vertex suppresses the kaon channels through Cabibbo suppression.

Interpret the displayed semi-empirical mass formula as an equation for mass energy, using c=1 for the mass terms. Its seven contributions are as follows.
The term Z(mp​+me​) is the rest energy of the Z protons and Z atomic electrons; (A−Z)mn​ is the rest energy of the neutrons. Including Zme​ makes this an atomic-mass approximation. Small atomic electronic binding energies are neglected.
The volume contribution −av​A represents the short-range attractive strong interaction. Saturation gives each nucleon roughly a fixed number of neighbors, so the nuclear binding energy is approximately proportional to A; binding lowers the mass. The surface contribution +as​A2/3 restores the binding missing from nucleons near the surface. Approximately constant nuclear density gives radius proportional to A1/3 and area proportional to A2/3, explaining that power and the positive mass correction.
The Coulomb potential energy contribution +ac​Z2/A1/3 is positive because proton charges repel. A sphere's electrostatic self-energy scales as Z2/R, and R∝A1/3. The more refined proton-pair count Z(Z−1) differs from the Z2 approximation explicitly used here.

By the Pauli exclusion principle, filling one species' higher-momentum states is energetically more expensive than balancing the two Fermi seas; nuclear interactions also contribute to the empirical coefficient. The stated convention is equivalent to 23.285(N−Z)2/A MeV, since Z−A/2=(Z−N)/2. It must not be combined with the usual coefficient near 23 MeV without this factor of four.

For the first generation the flavor-diagonal weak neutral current permits
Z⟶e−e+,νe​νˉe​,uuˉ,ddˉ.

W+⟶e+νe​, udˉ;W−⟶e−νˉe​, duˉ.
(39)
The two charges have equal partial decay widths to charge-conjugate channels to the accuracy needed here. Lepton universality and color multiplicity in a decay width give one unit of width for a massless lepton pair and three for a quark pair, apart from quark mixing and radiative corrections.

The Z boson has different vector and axial couplings for different fermions, so the same simple equal-unit rule cannot be transferred to all its partial decay widths.

mT​,mB​,mσ​,mN​≳2mZ​​≃45.6 GeV/c2.​
