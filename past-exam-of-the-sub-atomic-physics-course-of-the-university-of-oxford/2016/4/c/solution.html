An extra active generation with the same weak neutral current couplings would increase the Z boson total decay width if any new particle–antiparticle pair were light enough. A simple sufficient kinematic condition for closing each such two-body channel is
mT​,mB​,mσ​,mN​≳2mZ​​≃45.6 GeV/c2.​
(42)
The corresponding within-generation W boson channels close when
mT​+mB​≳mW​,mσ​+mN​≳mW​,mW​≃80.4 GeV/c2.​
(43)
If both members of each doublet already exceed mZ​/2, these two sum constraints follow automatically. Mixing with lighter generations can also open charged-current channels involving one heavy and one light particle, depending on the mixing amplitudes; that information is not specified.
These are threshold estimates, not precise confidence limits. Close to threshold, two-body decay phase space suppresses a new partial decay width, and a quantitative lower mass bound requires the experimental width uncertainties and the fermion's couplings. The given observations yield no upper mass bound. A sterile neutrino with no standard active coupling would not obey the stated Z-width inference, whereas the question specifies the same quantum numbers as the existing generations.
