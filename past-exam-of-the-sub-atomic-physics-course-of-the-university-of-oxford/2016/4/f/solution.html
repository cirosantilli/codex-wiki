For a measurement of the cross-section ratio with standard deviation 0.019R, the separation of the fourth-lepton prediction from the ordinary prediction is
nratio​=0.019R∣R′−R∣​=0.0190.0191107​=1.006≃1.0.​
(51)
Thus the peak-ratio model predicts only about a one-standard-deviation shift in this observable. In contrast, the direct Z boson width changes by 167+84=251 MeV, giving
nΓZ​​=2.3251​=109.1.​
(52)
Under these assumptions the direct width measurement is overwhelmingly more sensitive. These are separate expected displacements, using the supplied experimental uncertainties and fixed theoretical inputs; they are not a combined statistical significance. The 501/3 single-neutrino normalization and the peak-energy assumption are the same as in e. If the alternative literal 501 MeV per neutrino premise were used, the corresponding displacements would instead be 12.3 and 254 standard deviations, illustrating why that normalization must be stated.
