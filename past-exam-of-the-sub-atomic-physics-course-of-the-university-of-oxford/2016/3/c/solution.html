Interpret the displayed semi-empirical mass formula as an equation for mass energy, using c=1 for the mass terms. Its seven contributions are as follows.
The term Z(mp​+me​) is the rest energy of the Z protons and Z atomic electrons; (A−Z)mn​ is the rest energy of the neutrons. Including Zme​ makes this an atomic-mass approximation. Small atomic electronic binding energies are neglected.
The volume contribution −av​A represents the short-range attractive strong interaction. Saturation gives each nucleon roughly a fixed number of neighbors, so the nuclear binding energy is approximately proportional to A; binding lowers the mass. The surface contribution +as​A2/3 restores the binding missing from nucleons near the surface. Approximately constant nuclear density gives radius proportional to A1/3 and area proportional to A2/3, explaining that power and the positive mass correction.
The Coulomb potential energy contribution +ac​Z2/A1/3 is positive because proton charges repel. A sphere's electrostatic self-energy scales as Z2/R, and R∝A1/3. The more refined proton-pair count Z(Z−1) differs from the Z2 approximation explicitly used here.
The nuclear symmetry energy contribution +aa​(Z−A/2)2/A penalizes unequal neutron and proton populations. By the Pauli exclusion principle, filling one species' higher-momentum states is energetically more expensive than balancing the two Fermi seas; nuclear interactions also contribute to the empirical coefficient. The stated convention is equivalent to 23.285(N−Z)2/A MeV, since Z−A/2=(Z−N)/2. It must not be combined with the usual coefficient near 23 MeV without this factor of four.
Finally, nuclear pairing favors paired protons and paired neutrons. In the mass formula,
ΔMpair​c2=⎩⎨⎧​−ap​A−1/20+ap​A−1/2​Z,N both even,A odd,Z,N both odd.​​

The signs reverse when this correction is written as a contribution to positive nuclear binding energy. This smooth liquid-drop model omits detailed nuclear shell corrections.
