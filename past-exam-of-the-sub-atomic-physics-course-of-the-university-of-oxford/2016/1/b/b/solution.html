Adopt the lower-bound scale of the Heisenberg uncertainty principle, ΔrΔp∼ℏ/2, with the prescribed radial scale from a. Using ℏc=197.327 MeV fm,
Δpc∼2rrms​ℏc​=84.92 MeV.​
(4)
For a one-sided exponential distribution of a positive momentum magnitude, f(p)=p0−1​e−p/p0​, both its mean and its standard deviation are p0​. Under the additional identification made in this estimate, set the characteristic momentum to Δp. This identification concerns a positive magnitude; the mean of a signed momentum component in an isotropic stationary state would instead vanish.
Use the relativistic energy-momentum relation, not a nonrelativistic kinetic-energy approximation, because the momentum-energy scale is much larger than the stipulated quark rest energy:
Eq​∼(Δpc)2+(mq​c2)2​=(84.92)2+52​ MeV=85.06 MeV.​
(5)
This is a characteristic energy estimated at the characteristic momentum. It is not an exact evaluation of ⟨p2c2+mq2​c4​⟩ over a fully specified three-dimensional wavefunction. An order-of-magnitude convention Δp∼ℏ/Δr would give an answer about twice as large; the uncertainty principle by itself specifies a bound, not a unique confinement energy.
