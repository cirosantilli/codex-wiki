The charged meson decays proceed through a virtual W+:
π+(udˉ)→W+∗→μ+νμ​,K+(usˉ)→W+∗→μ+νμ​.
(13)
The first quark vertex has CKM factor Vud​, while the second has Vus​. In the simple Cabibbo description these are approximately cosθC​ and sinθC​, so the strange amplitude carries a suppression tanθC​ relative to the nonstrange one, or tan2θC​≃0.053 in their corresponding squared weak-coupling factors.
For a charged pseudoscalar and a negligibly massive neutrino, the leading leptonic energy width has the form
Γ(P+→μ+νμ​)=8πGF2​fP2​∣Vqq′​∣2​mμ2​mP​(1−mP2​mμ2​​)2
(14)
in natural units. The helicity factor mμ2​ is common to these two muon channels, while the threshold factor is much less favorable for the pion. Using the usual masses, its value is about 0.182 for the pion and 0.910 for the charged kaon: the pion's threshold factor alone is only about one fifth as large. Including the explicit mP​ factor gives a kinematic ratio about 0.0564. The pseudoscalar decay constants fP​ also matter, so Lorentz-invariant phase space and CKM factors alone do not determine the full lifetime ratio.[ref]
For K+→π+π0, one tree topology has the spectator u unchanged and sˉ→uˉ+W+∗ followed by W+∗→udˉ. The spectator u can combine with dˉ to form π+, and the uuˉ component supplies π0. This carries the strangeness-changing weak factor Vus∗​Vud​. Hadronization, other quark rearrangements and strong final-state interactions determine the hadronic amplitude; the diagram is not a complete precision width calculation. The small pion–muon energy release suppresses the pion leptonic channel kinematically, whereas the strange weak vertex suppresses the kaon channels through Cabibbo suppression.
