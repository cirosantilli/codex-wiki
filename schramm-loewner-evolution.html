Cardy boundary crossing formula (Iθ​(1/3,1/3))

Words: 114
Schramm–Loewner evolution OurBigBook.com
For critical planar percolation in a conformal quadrilateral, Cardy's boundary-crossing probability is Iθ​(1/3,1/3), where θ∈(0,1) is its conformal cross-ratio and I is the regularized incomplete beta function. This conformally invariant crossing law is associated with Schramm–Loewner evolution at parameter six.
The conformally invariant critical percolation crossing probability can be expressed in an appropriate ordered boundary cross ratio θ∈[0,1] as
∫01​[u(1−u)]−2/3du∫0θ​[u(1−u)]−2/3du​.
(1)
For the corresponding SLE exploration at parameter six, it arises from the beta integral for strict ordering of coupled Bessel lifetimes with a=1/3. In the upper-half-plane configuration 0<x<y, the strict-swallowing probability is this function at θ=1−x/y. Exchanging the two relevant boundary arcs exchanges the probability with its complement. This is a crossing probability, not the probability of visiting a fixed boundary point.

Scale-and-domain-Markov characterization of SLE

Words: 57
Schramm–Loewner evolution OurBigBook.com
Among Loewner chains with capacity 2t, continuous real driver and initial driver zero, scale invariance and the centered domain Markov property of a chordal Loewner chain characterize Schramm–Loewner evolution with some κ≥0. Driving-function reconstruction for a chordal Loewner chain turns the hull properties into stationary independent increments and Brownian scaling for the driver. The continuous Lévy process classification then gives Ut​=bt+κ​Wt​, and scaling eliminates b. Regularity and parametrization are essential hypotheses of this characterization.

Fixed-interior-point avoidance of SLE4 (P(Tz​=∞)=1)

Words: 65
Logarithmic martingale for SLE4 OurBigBook.com
Every fixed z∈H has infinite interior-point swallowing time for a Loewner chain under Schramm–Loewner evolution at κ=4. The centered flow is bounded on any finite horizon, making log∣Zt​∣ bounded above. The one-sided bound criterion for a martingale clock gives a finite logarithmic limit at any putative finite swallowing time. Thus ∣Zt​∣ stays away from zero and the imaginary coordinate remains positive, so the differential equation extends, a contradiction. In particular the trace misses each fixed interior point almost surely.

SLE4 angle martingale (θt​=arg(gt​(z)−Ut​))

Words: 89
SLE angle process OurBigBook.com
For a fixed interior point and parameter four, the SLE angle process has zero drift and satisfies θt​=argz+2∫0t​Ys​∣Zs​∣−2dBs​. Its values in (0,π) make this continuous local martingale a true bounded martingale. The identity log(Υt​/Υ0​)=−[θ]t​, together with the Dambis-Dubins-Schwarz theorem, prevents its Loewner conformal radius from vanishing at a finite lifetime. Since the parameter-four trace is simple and does not meet the real boundary at positive times, finite swallowing would require a visit to the point and vanishing radius. Thus its Loewner swallowing time is infinite almost surely. This gives a global continuous bounded martingale, not just one defined before swallowing.

Monotonicity of boundary derivatives of mapping-out functions (A⊆B⟹gB′​(x)≤gA′​(x)≤1)

Words: 65
Mapping-out function of a compact H-hull OurBigBook.com
For nested compact H-hulls whose closures avoid x∈R, the boundary derivative is an excursion avoidance probability. Avoiding the larger hull is a subset of avoiding the smaller one, which proves the derivative inequality. For a hull contained in {∣z∣≤R} and ∣x∣>R, comparison with the filled half-disc gives the sharp bound 1−R2/x2≤gA′​(x)≤1, since that half-disc has mapping-out function z+R2/z. The empty hull and filled half-disc attain the two bounds.

Let D=H∖K and stop planar Brownian motion from z∈D on reaching height R or leaving D. Then ImgK​(z)=limR→∞​RPz​(ImBτR​​=R). The stopped harmonic height is a bounded martingale. Height contraction of a hydrodynamically normalized mapping-out function (ImgK​(z)≤Imz)

For a compact H-hull, v(z)=ImgK​(z) is harmonic and satisfies 0<v(z)≤Imz. Local boundedness and the interior continuity of gK−1​ show that v tends to zero at every finite boundary point. Inverse-at-infinity criterion for local boundedness of a mapping-out function

Words: 62
Mapping-out function of a compact H-hull OurBigBook.com
The inverse of a hydrodynamically normalized mapping-out function has expansion f(w)=w+O(1/w) at infinity. If bounded-domain points zn​ had unbounded images wn​=g(zn​), then zn​=f(wn​)=wn​+O(1/wn​) would be unbounded, a contradiction. Thus g is bounded even near finite rough boundary points. Its own expansion at infinity then makes g(z)−z uniformly bounded throughout the domain. Reflection is needed only near infinity, not along the entire hull boundary.
