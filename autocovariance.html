Autocovariance (γ(h))

 ... Mathematics Area of mathematics Probability and statistics Time series Stationary process Weakly stationary process
OurBigBook.com Words: 634 Articles: 7
The autocovariance measures how two observations of the same stochastic process vary together. For a real weakly stationary process of mean μ, it is
γ(h)=Cov(Xt+h​,Xt​)=E[(Xt+h​−μ)(Xt​−μ)].
(7)
Weak stationarity removes dependence on the absolute time t. Without it, the covariance function generally needs two time arguments. Lag zero is the variance, γ(0)=Var(Xt​); symmetry of real covariance gives γ(−h)=γ(h). Positive values favor deviations from the mean in the same direction; negative values favor opposite directions. The units are the square of the observation units. See Bartlett, lecture 2: autocovariance examples.
For example, if Xt​=εt​+21​εt−1​ with centered unit-variance white noise, expanding the product leaves only matching noise times. Thus γ(0)=1+1/4=5/4, γ(1)=1/2, and γ(h)=0 for ∣h∣≥2. This MA(1) has a positive lag-one autocovariance because adjacent observations share one disturbance. Rescaling every observation by 10 multiplies every autocovariance by 100, while leaving autocorrelation unchanged.
The autocovariance is useful beyond comparing pairs: it determines the variance of any finite linear combination of observations. For the sample mean Xn​,
Var(Xn​)=n21​[nγ(0)+2∑h=1n−1​(n−h)γ(h)].
(8)
Each separation h occurs in n−h pairs. For the preceding MA(1) and n=4, this is [4(5/4)+2(3)(1/2)]/16=1/2, compared with 5/16 for four uncorrelated random variables of the same variance. This is the finite-record counterpart of the long-run variance of a stationary process.
