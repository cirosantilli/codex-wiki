Torsion constant (κ)

 Home Physics Branches of physics Classical mechanics Torsion balance
OurBigBook.com Words: 731 Articles: 1
The torsion constant, or torsional spring constant, is the positive restoring torque per unit twist angle of a complete suspension in its linear elasticity range:
τfiber​=−κθ,κ=−dθdτfiber​​>0.
(10)
Its SI unit is Nm/rad. The minus sign describes restoration toward the untwisted orientation. The derivative specifies a local stiffness if the response is not globally linear; using one constant over a finite range additionally requires a straight calibration curve. Return to the same zero after unloading tests recoverable elastic deformation; repeat loading in both directions to check whether the response depends on its previous loading. Staying below permanent deformation is necessary, but does not alone prove proportionality between torque and twist.
For an ideal uniform circular wire of length ℓ, radius r, and shear modulus Gs​, the filament torsional stiffness is C=Gs​Jp​, with polar second moment of area Jp​=πr4/2. Uniform twist gives θ/ℓ as the twist per unit length, so
κ=ℓC​=2ℓπGs​r4​,U=21​κθ2.
(11)
Here U is stored elastic energy. Doubling the wire's radius multiplies the ideal torsion constant by 16; doubling its length halves it. This explains why a long thin fiber is useful for weak torque measurement. The formula assumes linear elasticity with uniform material and circular section; it does not calibrate arbitrary ribbons, clamps, or a compound suspension. Within this ideal model, the equipartition theorem predicts an equilibrium thermal mean-square angular displacement ⟨θ2⟩=kB​T/κ, where kB​ is the Boltzmann constant and T the uniform absolute temperature. A softer suspension magnifies both the signal and its thermal fluctuations.
Static calibration supplies known torques without assuming the unknown interaction being measured. Apply a known perpendicular force F at lever arm a, record the change from the unloaded twist, and use
τcal​=aF,κ=Δθτcal​​.
(12)
Several positive and negative settings test proportionality; fit the slope of applied torque against twist and retain a possible intercept. A nonzero intercept must not be silently absorbed into the slope. Convert degrees to radians before reporting Nm/rad. PASCO's procedure turns the Coulomb balance on its side and adds small known masses to generate a gravitational weight at the sphere's lever arm, returning the rod to its index mark by turning the dial. Its tabulated coefficient is force per degree: multiply by the lever arm and 180/π to obtain our torsion constant. [ref]
The figure's ideal static calibration uses κ=1.0×10−8Nm/rad: an applied torque of 2.0×10−10Nm gives 0.020rad. Both signs lie on the same straight line. The plotted points are calculated examples, not experimental observations; they cannot demonstrate a real fiber's linearity.
Dynamic calibration instead measures the oscillation period T0​ with known total suspended moment of inertia I. For negligible damping, small free oscillations about a fixed reference obey simple harmonic motion:
Iθ¨+κθ=0,T0​=2πκI​​,κ=T02​4π2I​.
(13)
Include the rod, mirrors, and any attachments in I unless their contributions are demonstrably negligible. Keep the applied torque absent or independent of angle. If an external torque has derivative gτ​=dτext​/dθ at equilibrium, linearization gives effective stiffness κ−gτ​: timing alone then measures that combination, and stability requires κ−gτ​>0. A constant applied torque merely shifts equilibrium. Non-negligible damping changes the measured period; damped torsional oscillation gives a correction under a specified damping model. Agreement between static and dynamic calibrations is a useful check on the assumed moment of inertia, reference stability, and restoring law, rather than a reason to ignore their assumptions.
