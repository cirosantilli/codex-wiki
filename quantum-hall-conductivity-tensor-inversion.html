Quantum Hall conductivity tensor inversion (ρs​=σs−1​)

OurBigBook.com Words: 181
Choose conventional surface current density along +x, magnetic field along +z, and negative electron charge. An isotropic in-plane response has the following sheet conductivity and sheet resistivity tensors for s≥0, k>0:
K=he2​(sk​−ks​)E,E=RK​s2+k21​(s−k​ks​)K.
(36)
These are matrix inverses, rather than componentwise reciprocals. The sheet conductivity has units siemens, the sheet resistivity ohms. With Ky​=0, the sign is Ey​/Kx​<0, agreeing with the negative Hall coefficient of a single electron species. Contact polarity can reverse the measured voltage sign, so quote the Hall magnitude. For a homogeneous strip of width W and longitudinal probe separation L, I=WKx​ gives
I∣VH​∣​=RK​s2+k2k​,IVxx​​=RK​WL​s2+k2s​.​
(37)
At s=0, k=N, these reduce to RK​/N and zero. A nonzero transverse sheet resistivity creates no local Joule heating: the antisymmetric contribution cancels in E⋅K=(e2/h)s∣E∣2.
For an original nonideal example, choose k=2, s=0.01 and L/W=2. Then Vxx​/I≃129.061Ω, while the Hall magnitude is below RK​/2 by the relative amount s2/(k2+s2)≃25.0 parts per million. An unwanted admixture 0.02Vxx​ in the Hall readout would instead introduce approximately 2.58Ω. The chosen calculation shows why a small intrinsic tensor correction does not by itself guarantee an accurate contact measurement.
