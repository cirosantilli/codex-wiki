Correlation function (astronomy, ξ(r))

Words: 246 Articles: 3
Large-scale structure of the universe OurBigBook.com
The cosmological two-point correlation function measures excess pairing or correlated density fluctuations at a separation. For a statistically homogeneous and isotropic mean-zero density contrast, ξ(r)=⟨δ(x)δ(x+r)⟩. For distinct points in a discrete galaxy population it instead specifies the factorial pair density, nˉ2[1+ξ(r)]. The latter excludes self-pairs; an unsmoothed discrete-density covariance also contains a Dirac delta at zero separation. These definitions connect large-scale galaxy clustering with the matter power spectrum while keeping galaxy power spectrum shot noise separate.

Isotropic cosmological correlation-power-spectrum relation (ξ(r)=2π21​∫0∞​k2P(k)j0​(kr)dk)

Correlation function (astronomy) OurBigBook.com
For the Fourier transform convention δk​=∫d3xδ(x)eik⋅x and covariance ⟨δk​δk′​⟩=(2π)3P(k)δD​(k+k′), inversion gives ξ(r)=∫d3kP(k)eik⋅r/(2π)3. Isotropy reduces the angular integral to 4πj0​(kr), where j0​(x)=sinx/x is the order-zero Spherical Bessel function. At zero separation this gives the variance if the spectrum integral converges. A nonnegative spectrum can produce negative correlations away from the origin.

Galaxy two-point correlation function (dP12​=nˉ2[1+ξ(r)]dV1​dV2​)

Words: 117 Articles: 1
Correlation function (astronomy) OurBigBook.com
For a homogeneous isotropic point process of galaxies with mean number density nˉ, the galaxy two-point correlation function gives the excess pair probability at distinct positions. Dividing the factorial pair density by the probability of a central galaxy gives conditional neighbouring-galaxy density nˉ[1+ξ(r)]. Choosing a central location uniformly in volume does not perform this conditioning.

Galaxy cluster

Words: 264 Articles: 4
Large-scale structure of the universe OurBigBook.com
A galaxy cluster is a gravitationally bound collection of galaxies, diffuse gas and dark matter. Much of its ordinary matter can reside in hot intracluster gas, visible through thermal bremsstrahlung and the Sunyaev-Zeldovich effect. The two signals weight the electron density differently, making the cluster useful both as a structure-formation probe and, with a geometrical model, as a distance indicator.

Cluster gravitational-lensing mass estimate (κ=Σ/Σcrit​)

Words: 57
Galaxy cluster OurBigBook.com
Gravitational lensing constrains a galaxy cluster's projected mass independently of its light output or dynamical equilibrium. The lensing convergence is projected surface mass density divided by Σcrit​=c2Ds​/(4πGDd​Dds​), using the appropriate angular diameter distances. Strong gravitational lensing constrains the central mass through multiple-image geometry, and weak gravitational lensing constrains larger-scale projected mass through coherent image distortions. Converting these observations into a three-dimensional mass needs geometrical modelling and the relevant lensing degeneracies.

Cluster velocity-dispersion mass estimate (M∼Rσv2​/G)

Words: 53
Galaxy cluster OurBigBook.com
The virial theorem balances a relaxed galaxy cluster's orbital kinetic energy, of order Mσv2​, against its Newtonian gravitational potential energy, of order −GM2/R. Their ratio gives the displayed mass scale. The numerical coefficient depends on the density profile, radius definition and conversion from line-of-sight velocity dispersion to three-dimensional orbital motion. Membership contamination, anisotropy and nonequilibrium dynamics can bias the inference.

Intracluster medium

Words: 100 Articles: 1
Galaxy cluster OurBigBook.com
The intracluster medium is diffuse hot plasma between the galaxies of a galaxy cluster. Its X-ray radiation includes thermal bremsstrahlung and atomic line emission. Measured temperature and density profiles supply a cluster hydrostatic mass estimator when approximately thermal hydrostatic equilibrium holds. The medium supplies much of a cluster's baryonic mass, but this need not equal its total gravitating mass.

The density ρg​ is gas density, while M includes every gravitating component. A varying μ adds −dlogμ/dlogr inside the logarithmic bracket. Nonthermal pressure requires differentiating the total supporting pressure rather than thermal pressure alone.

Galaxy survey

Words: 169 Articles: 3
Large-scale structure of the universe OurBigBook.com
A galaxy survey measures positions and other properties of a selected galaxy sample. A spectroscopic survey uses cosmological redshift to estimate radial distance, with corrections for peculiar velocity. Its volume, selection, galaxy bias, and sampling determine which matter power spectrum modes can be inferred.

Redshift-space distortions

Words: 37
Galaxy survey OurBigBook.com
Redshift-space distortions arise because observed galaxy redshifts include peculiar velocity as well as cosmological redshift. Distances inferred solely from expansion therefore distort clustering along the line of sight. Coherent large-scale motion and small-scale velocity dispersion produce different effects on the inferred matter power spectrum.

Galaxy power spectrum shot noise (Pshot​=1/nˉ)

Words: 43
Galaxy survey OurBigBook.com
Sampling with a homogeneous Poisson point process of mean comoving number density nˉ adds a constant 1/nˉ to the dimensional galaxy power spectrum. Subtracting its expectation leaves sampling uncertainty. Correlated tracer populations and survey weights modify this simple formula; survey depth alone does not specify nˉ.

Survey window function (W(x))

Words: 52
Galaxy survey OurBigBook.com
A survey window function describes the finite spatial coverage and weights of a galaxy survey. Multiplication by this window in position space mixes Fourier modes by convolution in Fourier space. A dimension of extent L cannot robustly measure wavelengths much larger than L; the fundamental wavenumber of a periodic box of that size is 2π/L.

Cosmological structure formation

Words: 1k Articles: 24
Large-scale structure of the universe OurBigBook.com
Cosmological structure formation is the growth of primordial density perturbations into sheets, filaments, halos, galaxies, and clusters under gravity.

Hierarchical galaxy formation (σ(M) increases as M decreases)

Words: 702 Articles: 12
Cosmological structure formation OurBigBook.com
In hierarchical galaxy formation, low-mass dark-matter structures typically collapse before larger structures because the smoothed linear density variance is larger on smaller mass scales. Accretion and mergers assemble increasingly massive haloes. Cooling, feedback and angular momentum determine how baryons form galaxies within that halo hierarchy; the ordering is statistical rather than an absolute rule for every object.

Galactic angular-momentum catastrophe

Words: 53
Hierarchical galaxy formation OurBigBook.com
The galactic angular-momentum catastrophe is the excessive loss or redistribution of baryonic specific angular momentum in early simulated galaxy formation, producing overly compact disks or large central concentrations. Premature cooling, gravitational torques, interactions, and resolution contributed. Improved stellar feedback and gas accretion histories can mitigate it; it is a historical simulation problem rather than a theorem against cold dark matter.

Downsizing in galaxy formation

Words: 51
Hierarchical galaxy formation OurBigBook.com
Downsizing denotes the tendency for the characteristic mass of actively star-forming galaxies to decrease with time, together with related evidence that more massive quiescent systems formed their stars earlier or over shorter intervals. It does not assert that their dark-matter haloes assembled earlier: stellar formation and halo or stellar assembly are separate histories.

Monolithic galaxy formation

Words: 42
Hierarchical galaxy formation OurBigBook.com
Monolithic galaxy formation idealizes a galaxy as forming in one early rapid dissipative collapse, followed mainly by passive evolution. Rapid formation of the stars is distinct from early assembly of all those stars into their final object; hierarchical galaxy formation can assemble old stellar populations later.

The integrated halo star-formation efficiency is the fraction of a dark matter halo's reference baryonic mass converted into stars: ϵ∗​=M∗​/(fb​Mh​), with cosmic baryon fraction fb​. It is distinct from star-formation efficiency per free-fall time. Cooling, gas supply and retention, stellar feedback and active-galactic-nucleus feedback make it strongly dependent on dark matter halo mass. Changing this efficiency changes both the mapping and the logarithmic Jacobian between the dark matter halo mass function and the luminosity function.

Cooling criterion for galaxy formation (tcool​≲tdyn​)

Words: 173 Articles: 2
Hierarchical galaxy formation OurBigBook.com
Gas in a collapsing halo is heated toward a virial temperature. If its cooling time is short compared with the dynamical time, thermal support is removed and rapid condensation can form a galaxy. If cooling is slower, a hot atmosphere can persist or condense only gradually. This criterion links baryonic galaxy formation to the dark-matter assembly hierarchy without making every halo into one luminous object.
Atomic and molecular cooling thresholds for galaxy formation (Tatomic​∼104K,Tmolecular​∼102–103K)
Words: 51
Cooling criterion for galaxy formation OurBigBook.com
Primordial atomic line cooling becomes effective near the temperature where electronic excitations are accessible, of order ten thousand kelvin. Molecular hydrogen has lower-energy transitions and can cool colder gas if it forms and survives. The corresponding virial temperature thresholds translate into epoch-dependent halo mass thresholds. Radiation, molecular chemistry, metal-line cooling and gas density qualify a single universal minimum galaxy mass.
Upper galaxy mass from gas cooling (Mcool​ of order a galactic mass)
Words: 58
Cooling criterion for galaxy formation OurBigBook.com
In simple hierarchical models, increasing system mass raises the virial temperature and can carry primordial gas beyond its efficient atomic line-cooling interval. At assembly densities, the cooling-time constraint then distinguishes galaxy-sized condensations from larger hot groups or clusters. The resulting characteristic mass is approximate, depends on composition and structure, and does not prohibit subsequent mergers of already formed stellar systems.

Shell crossing

Shell crossing occurs when the Lagrangian-to-Eulerian map ceases to be one-to-one. Its Jacobian determinant vanishes, the pressureless single-stream density formally diverges, and several streams subsequently occupy the same position.
