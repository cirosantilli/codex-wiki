Stationary law of a reflecting nearest-neighbor walk on a finite path

 ... Area of mathematics Probability and statistics Probability theory Markov process Markov chain Stationary distribution
OurBigBook.com Words: 156
On states 0,…,m, take an interior step of +1 or −1 with probability p each and hold with probability 1−2p. At an endpoint take the inward step with probability 2p and hold with probability 1−2p. For m≥1 and 0<p≤1/2, the chain is irreducible and its stationary distribution has endpoint weights 1/(2m) and interior weights 1/m.
Detailed balance proves this: the doubled inward endpoint transition requires its stationary mass to be half that of an adjacent interior point, and all interior points have equal mass. The total unnormalized weight is 1+2(m−1)+1=2m. For m=1 there are just two equal endpoint masses.
When p<1/2, positive self-loops make the chain aperiodic, so its transition probabilities converge to this law. At p=1/2 every move changes parity and two-step returns are possible, giving period two and no such convergence from a fixed starting state. At p=0 every state is absorbing and uniqueness of the stationary law is lost.
