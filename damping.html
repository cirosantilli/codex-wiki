Damping

 Home Mathematics Area of mathematics Analysis Partial differential equation Wave equation
OurBigBook.com Words: 2k Articles: 16
Damping dissipates the energy of an oscillation, reducing its amplitude. A linearly damped string applies this mechanism to each normal mode.
Why can stronger damping make a return to equilibrium slower? A weak brake lets a spring exchange potential energy with kinetic energy many times; a very strong brake makes the final motion creep. Critical damping marks the boundary between these behaviours. The distinction depends on the force law: the classification below concerns a single damped harmonic oscillator with viscous damping, rather than every mechanism that removes energy.
Let x(t) be displacement from equilibrium, m>0 the moving mass, k>0 the spring constant, and b≥0 the constant coefficient of viscous damping. With no driving force, Newton's second law gives
mx¨+bx˙+kx=0,x(0)=x0​,x˙(0)=v0​.
(21)
The two initial conditions specify displacement and velocity. Define the undamped eigenfrequency as an angular frequency ω0​=k/m​ and the damping ratio as ζ=b/(2mk​). The ordinary differential equation becomes x¨+2ζω0​x˙+ω02​x=0. A constant weight in a vertical spring merely shifts equilibrium; it is already absorbed in the definition of x.[ref]
For example, the ideal parameters m=1kg and k=4Nm−1 give ω0​=2s−1 and critical damping at b=4kgs−1. Values b=1,4,8kgs−1 give damping ratios 0.25,1,2, respectively. These are model choices, not measured values. For release from rest with x0​>0, set X=x/x0​ and τ=ω0​t; then X′′+2ζX′+X=0, where primes now denote derivatives with respect to τ.
The left panel uses identical initial conditions X(0)=1, X′(0)=0. Underdamping produces repeated crossings of equilibrium; critical damping and overdamping give positive decreasing responses. The right panel keeps critical damping but changes initial velocity: X′(0)=−2 gives one crossing, although there is no oscillation. Both panels are exact model predictions.
