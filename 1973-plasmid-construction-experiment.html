1973 plasmid construction experiment

 Home Biology Genetics Molecular genetics Plasmid
OurBigBook.com Words: 206
In his account of the 1973 work, Stanley Norman Cohen describes purified R6-5 plasmid DNA cut with EcoRI, reassociated and joined by DNA ligase. The mixture entered calcium chloride-treated Escherichia coli; plates containing antibiotics selected biological colonies showing antibiotic resistance. The recovered pSC102 carried three R6-5 fragments and kanamycin antibiotic resistance. The team then joined material from pSC102 to the carrier pSC101 providing tetracycline antibiotic resistance, recovering pSC105 with both forms of antibiotic resistance.[ref]
Stained agarose gel electrophoresis, gradient centrifugation and electron microscope comparisons characterized recovered plasmids. Parental and mixture lanes in Figure 3 supplied fragment comparisons; pSC101 alone gave one EcoRI fragment, while pSC105 combined its carrier fragment with the resistance-bearing fragment. This supported joined, propagating genetic material beyond selection alone. The comparisons did not determine every nucleotide sequence or establish a universal construction efficiency.[ref]
The interesting inference combines two kinds of evidence: a recoverable phenotype identifies descendants worth examining, and the extracted DNA tests a structural explanation. The fragment comparisons are molecular scientific controls; they answer a different objection from a control for background growth. The hypothetical diagrams here illustrate that logic without reproducing the historical fragment lengths or biological colony counts.
