Frequency and wavelength peaks of Planck's law (λmax​T=b,νmax​/T=xν​kB​/h)

 Home Physics Branches of physics Statistical physics Planck's law
OurBigBook.com Words: 304
At a fixed temperature, the frequency maximizing Bν​ and the wavelength maximizing Bλ​ in Planck's law are different ways of describing the same black-body radiation. They are maxima of different densities. The inverse relation between frequency and wavelength does not preserve those maxima because its interval-conversion factor varies with wavelength.
For a short extremum calculation, substitute x=hν/(kB​T) into Bν​, leaving a positive constant times x3/(ex−1). Substitute x=hc/(λkB​T) into Bλ​, leaving a positive constant times x5/(ex−1). Setting each derivative to zero gives
3(1−e−xν​)=xν​,5(1−e−xλ​)=xλ​.
(71)
Select the positive roots: xν​=2.821439372… and xλ​=4.965114232…. Although x=0 solves the rearranged equations, it is an endpoint, not either peak. For m=3 or 5, the function m(1−e−x)−x initially increases, its derivative me−x−1 crosses zero once, and it eventually becomes negative. Hence it has exactly one positive root. Each density tends to zero at both ends, so this root gives its unique maximum. The bisection method brackets these roots in (1,3) and (1,5).
Consequently Wien's displacement law gives
λmax​T=kB​xλ​hc​=b≈2.897771955×10−3mK,Tνmax​​=hkB​xν​​≈5.878925758×1010HzK−1.
(72)
These constants agree with NIST's tabulation. [ref] The wavelength peaks at 3000, 4500, and 6000K are 965.924, 643.949, and 482.962nm. Doubling temperature halves this peak wavelength.
At 6000K, the frequency peak is νmax​≈352.736THz, whose converted wavelength is c/νmax​≈0.849907μm. The Bλ​ peak is instead 0.482962μm, corresponding to about 620.737THz. These numbers are compatible: equal-width frequency bins and equal-width wavelength bins partition the same radiation differently. Specifying which density was maximized is essential when using a peak to infer temperature.
