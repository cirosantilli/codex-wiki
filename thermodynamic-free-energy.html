Thermodynamic free energy

 Home Physics Branches of physics Statistical physics Thermodynamics Thermodynamic potential
OurBigBook.com Words: 5k Articles: 61
A free energy is a thermodynamic potential that compares possible states under specified environmental constraints. For a closed system of fixed composition, treated as a simple compressible system with only pressure-volume work, stable thermodynamic equilibrium minimizes Helmholtz free energy at fixed temperature and volume, or Gibbs free energy at fixed temperature and pressure. The choice follows the experiment: a rigid sealed vessel and a sealed cylinder with a freely moving loaded piston impose different constraints. Both may exchange heat with a thermal reservoir. See W. C. Carter, MIT thermodynamics notes, sections 2-1.14 and 2-1.19.
The useful insight is that internal energy alone cannot rank these alternatives: exchanging heat also changes the surroundings' entropy. Subtracting TS packages that accounting into a property of the chosen system. Adding pV additionally accounts for displacing the surroundings at fixed pressure. A free energy is a state function; supplied heat describes a transfer during a process. Under the stated constraints, supplied heat is Q=ΔU at fixed volume or Q=ΔH at fixed pressure, whereas neither ΔF nor ΔG generally equals Q.
A lower free energy identifies a thermodynamic preference, without specifying a timescale. A barrier can trap a system in a local minimum, producing metastability although a lower global minimum exists. The enzyme example illustrates the corresponding distinction for chemical reactions: changing the route can change the reaction rate while preserving the endpoint Gibbs free energy difference and chemical equilibrium.
