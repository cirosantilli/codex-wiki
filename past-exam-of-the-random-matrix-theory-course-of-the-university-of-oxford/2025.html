Gaudin's lemma is the projection kernel determinant integration identity. Suppose an integral kernel K satisfies
∫K(x,y)K(y,z)dμ(y)=K(x,z),∫K(x,x)dμ(x)=N,
(25)
with the relevant integrals absolutely convergent. Define Dk​(x1​,…,xk​)=det[K(xi​,xj​)]i,j=1k​ and D0​=1. Then
∫Dk​(x1​,…,xk​)dμ(xk​)=(N−k+1)Dk−1​(x1​,…,xk−1​).​
(26)
In particular, a finite-rank projection kernel of rank N has ∫DN​dμN=N!.
One can see the factor directly from the permutation expansion of the determinant. Terms whose permutation fixes k integrate to NDk−1​. Every permutation on the other k−1 indices admits k−1 insertions of k into a cycle. Each insertion reverses its sign, and the reproducing identity contracts its two adjacent kernel factors into one. These terms integrate to −(k−1)Dk−1​, proving the formula.

Use Haar sampling by Gaussian QR decomposition:
  • Generate an n-by-n real matrix G with independent entries having the standard normal distribution.
  • Compute a QR decomposition G=Q0​R0​ with Q0​ an orthogonal matrix and R0​ an upper triangular matrix.
  • Put S=diag(sgn(R0,11​),…,sgn(R0,nn​)) and return M=Q0​S​. Then G=MR with R=SR0​ having strictly positive diagonal.
The matrix G is invertible almost surely. Indeed, successively sampled Gaussian columns have probability zero of lying in the proper linear subspace spanned by the previous columns. Thus the diagonal signs used above exist with probability one. Equivalently, apply the Gram-Schmidt process to the columns, always dividing each residual by its positive Euclidean norm; this produces M directly.
To prove uniformity, fix H∈O(n). Each column of G has density proportional to e−∥x∥2/2, so orthogonal transformations preserve its probability distribution. Column independence therefore gives HG=dG.
The QR decomposition with positive diagonal is unique: if G=Q1​R1​=Q2​R2​, then Q2T​Q1​=R2​R1−1​ is both an orthogonal matrix and an upper triangular matrix, hence diagonal with entries ±1. Its diagonal entries are positive, so it is the identity. It follows that the orthogonal factor of HG=HMR is HM. Therefore
HM=dMfor every H∈O(n).​
(54)
By uniqueness of normalized Haar measure on the compact group O(n), the returned matrix has exactly the required probability distribution. Reflection invariance gives each determinant sign probability 1/2, so the algorithm samples both components of the orthogonal group. The positive-diagonal correction is essential when a numerical QR decomposition chooses its own signs.
