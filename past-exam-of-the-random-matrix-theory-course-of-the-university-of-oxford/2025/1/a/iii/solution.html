The probabilistic form of Wick's theorem is Isserlis's theorem. If (Y1​,…,Yk​) is a centered random vector with a multivariate Gaussian distribution and covariance matrix C, its odd product moments vanish, and, for even k,
Ea=1∏k​Ya​=π∈P2​(k)∑​{a,b}∈π∏​Cab​.​
(4)
Here P2​(k) is the set of perfect matchings of the k labelled positions. The formula allows repeated coordinates and singular covariance matrices. To see the pairing rule, differentiate the moment-generating function exp(tTCt/2) once in each coordinate and set t=0: only terms pairing all differentiated coordinates survive, each with the displayed product of covariances.
Take Ya​=Mia​ia+1​​, with ik+1​=i1​. These variables are linear combinations of the original independent normal random variables, hence are jointly Gaussian. They have zero means, so

(5)
