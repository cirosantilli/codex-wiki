Let gi​=(Xi1​,…,Xin​) be row i of X, viewed as a real vector. These are independent standard Gaussian random vectors, and (XXT)ij​=gi​⋅gj​. The trace expression for the empirical spectral measure gives
mk​(n)=n−k−1∑i1​,…,ik​​(gi1​​⋅gi2​​)⋯(gik​​⋅gi1​​).
(13)
We need the norm moments
E∥gi​∥2=n,E∥gi​∥4=n(n+2),E∥gi​∥6=n(n+2)(n+4).
(14)
For completeness, S=∥gi​∥2 has the chi-squared distribution with n degrees of freedom. By independence and the Gaussian integral, its moment-generating function is EesS=(1−2s)−n/2 for s<1/2. Differentiating at zero once, twice and three times proves these three identities.
For the first moment,
E[m1​(n)]=n−2i∑​E∥gi​∥2=1.​
(15)
For the second moment, if i=j, conditional expectation given gi​ gives E[(gi​⋅gj​)2∣gi​]=∥gi​∥2, hence an unconditional value n. Separating equal and unequal row indices yields
E[m2​(n)]=n−3{nn(n+2)+n(n−1)n}=2+n1​.​
(16)
For the third moment, the summand is (gi​⋅gj​)(gj​⋅gk​)(gk​⋅gi​). There are three disjoint cases.
  • If i,j,k are all different, average over gk​ first. Its covariance matrix is the identity, so the conditional expectation of the last two factors is gj​⋅gi​. The summand's expectation is therefore E(gi​⋅gj​)2=n. There are n(n−1)(n−2) such triples.
  • If exactly two indices agree, cyclic symmetry reduces the calculation to i=j=k. The product is ∥gi​∥2(gi​⋅gk​)2, whose conditional expectation given gi​ is ∥gi​∥4. Its expectation is n(n+2), and there are 3n(n−1) such triples.
  • If all three indices agree, the product is ∥gi​∥6, with expectation n(n+2)(n+4). There are n choices.
Consequently
E[m3​(n)]​=n−4{n2(n−1)(n−2)+3n2(n−1)(n+2)+n2(n+2)(n+4)}=5+n6​+n24​.​
(17)
The three limiting expected values are therefore
n→∞lim​E[mk​(n)]=⎩⎨⎧​1,2,5,​k=1,k=2,k=3.​​
(18)
