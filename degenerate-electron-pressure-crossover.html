Degenerate-electron pressure crossover (x=pF​/(me​c))

Consider an ideal degenerate electron gas at zero temperature. Counting two spin states per phase space cell gives number density ne​=pF3​/(3π2ℏ3). The isotropic momentum flux gives pressure

Here me​ is electron mass, c is the speed of light, ℏ=h/(2π) with Planck constant h, and special relativity gives v(p)=pc/p2+me2​c2​. The factor 1/3 averages the directional momentum flux, and the spin factor is already included. This normalization makes P∗​ a pressure, not an energy per electron.
For x≪1, expand the denominator: I(x)∼x5/5 and P≃ne​pF2​/(5me​). For x≫1, the integrand approaches t3: I(x)∼x4/4 and P≃ne​pF​c/4. At fixed composition, mass density is ρ=μe​mu​ne​, with μe​ the mean molecular weight per electron and mu​ the unified atomic mass unit. Since ne​∝x3, the logarithmic stiffness is
γeff​=dlogρdlogP​=31+x2​I(x)x5​,γeff​⟶{5/34/3​x≪1,x≫1.​
(34)
Pressure keeps rising; its fractional gain per fractional compression becomes smaller. Relativistic electrons approach a speed ceiling while their momenta keep increasing. Thus the high-density equation of state loses the extra compression leverage of a nonrelativistic white dwarf. The Chandrasekhar mass from central polytropic constants supplies the corresponding global mass calculation.
For a calculated local example with μe​=2, x=1 gives ρ≃1.95×109kgm−3, I(1)=0.1537384 and P≃7.38×1021Pa. Its stiffness is 1.53314, intermediate between the two limits. These are ideal equation of state values at one point, not a measured central mass density or a whole white dwarf model. Finite temperature and interactions are excluded.
