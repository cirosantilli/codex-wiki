Landau quasiparticle (ξp​)

 Home Physics Branches of physics Statistical physics Fermi liquid
OurBigBook.com Words: 742 Articles: 9
A Landau quasiparticle is a fermionic excitation connected continuously to a Fermi gas particle when interactions preserve the phase. Near an isotropic Fermi surface, its energy relative to the chemical potential is ξp​=ϵp​−μ≃vF​(p−pF​), with Fermi momentum pF​ and Fermi velocity vF​.[ref]
The Pauli exclusion principle restricts decay. For a three-dimensional normal Fermi liquid with regular short-range interactions, a representative low-energy lifetime model is
τp​ℏ​=AE∗​ξp2​+(πkB​T)2​,A>0,
(10)
Here E∗​ is an energy scale and A is dimensionless; sharpness requires ℏ/τp​≪∣ξp​∣ at zero temperature.[ref]
For an original example, stipulate A=1 and T=0. At ∣ξp​∣/E∗​=0.02, the decay energy is 0.0004E∗​, just 2% of the excitation energy; halving ∣ξp​∣ reduces that ratio to 1%. Approaching the Fermi surface improves the particle description even though the microscopic interaction has not weakened. This quadratic illustration is three-dimensional; low-dimensional corrections require separate analysis.
