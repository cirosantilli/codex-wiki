Phase matching in second-harmonic generation (Δk=0)

 Home Physics Branches of physics Optics Nonlinear optics Second-harmonic generation
OurBigBook.com Words: 492 Articles: 2
Phase matching makes wave amplitudes emitted at different depths add coherently. Consider collinear plane waves in a uniform transparent material of length L, with fixed polarization of waves and nonzero selected nonlinear optical susceptibility. Assume negligible pump depletion, negligible absorption, an initially absent harmonic, and a wide beam for which diffraction is negligible. The pump's wave amplitude is then constant throughout the interaction.
The pump electric field has spatial wave phase kω​z; its quadratic source has spatial wave phase 2kω​z. The freely propagating harmonic has wave number k2ω​. Define the second-harmonic phase mismatch by
Δk=k2ω​−2kω​,kω​=cnω​ω​,k2ω​=c2n2ω​ω​.
(63)
For the chosen complex-field convention, the outgoing harmonic wave amplitude, up to an overall constant and propagation phase, is proportional to
A(L)=∫0L​eiΔkzdz=LeiΔkL/2sinc(2ΔkL​),sincx={sinx/x,1,​x=0,x=0.​
(64)
Choosing the conjugate complex-field convention reverses the exponent's sign without changing the wave intensity. Taking the squared complex modulus gives
I2ω​(L)=CIω2​L2sinc2(2ΔkL​),C>0.
(65)
Here C is fixed for the chosen modes and material; its dimensions accommodate the stated wave intensity convention. The quadratic pump dependence follows because the source wave amplitude is proportional to E02​, whereas Iω​ is proportional to E02​. The sinc function comes from summing contributions along the whole material, just as a finite aperture produces diffraction through addition of complex amplitudes.
At Δk=0, the integral is L and the harmonic wave intensity grows as L2. At ΔkL=2π, all those contributions cancel. This cancellation needs no absorption: their wave phases wind through a full turn. At high conversion, the constant-pump assumption fails, so the L2 law cannot continue indefinitely. Tight focusing, finite optical pulses, absorption, and transverse separation of modes also require a more complete model.
Optical dispersion generally makes n2ω​=nω​. In a suitable crystal, birefringence allows modes with different polarization of waves to experience different refractive indices, making their selected values equal. The appropriate crystal direction and allowed nonlinear optical susceptibility component must both work.[ref]
For a worked calculation, choose L0​=1mm and Δk=πmm−1. At L=L0​, the harmonic wave intensity is 4/π2≃0.4053 of its perfectly matched value. At L=2L0​ it vanishes in this model. More material can therefore reduce the output. If instead Δk=0, doubling L multiplies output by four. The two curves retain that common normalization, so their different peak heights are meaningful.
