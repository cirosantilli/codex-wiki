Tail event

 ... Mathematics Area of mathematics Probability and statistics Probability theory Independent sigma-algebras Tail sigma-algebra
OurBigBook.com Words: 373 Articles: 1
A tail event is an event A∈T, where T is the tail sigma-algebra of the chosen sequence of random variables. It concerns information available arbitrarily far along the sequence. In the measurable coordinate models described in finite-prefix invariance of tail events, any finite edit preserves whether it occurs.
This is stronger than merely needing a long waiting time. On {0,1}N, with 1 denoting heads, let K=inf{j≥1:Xj​=1}, taking K=∞ if there is no head. The event
{K=3}={X1​=0,X2​=0,X3​=1}
(30)
is not a tail event: changing only X1​ from zero to one changes membership. Under fair independent Bernoulli trials its probability is 1/8. Even the event {K<∞}, of probability one in this model, is not a tail event as a literal set: the all-zero sequence and the sequence with just one initial head differ in one coordinate. A probability of zero or one does not characterize tail events.
