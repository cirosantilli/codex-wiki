Write H=ϕ1​ and P=ϕ2​, both Hermitian matrices. Through quadratic order around the chosen vacuum expectation value,
X=Φ†Φ=C21+2​CH+21​(H2+P2+i[H,P]).
(49)
The trace of a commutator vanishes. The needed matrix traces are therefore
trXtrX2(trX)2​=NC2+2​CtrH+21​tr(H2+P2),=NC4+22​C3trH+C2tr(3H2+P2)+O((H,P)3),=N2C4+22​NC3trH+NC2tr(H2+P2)+2C2(trH)2+O((H,P)3).​
(50)
Using m2=−(α+Nβ)C2, all linear terms cancel. The quadratic scalar potential reduces to
V(2)=αC2trH2+βC2(trH)2,​
(51)
with no P2 term. The kinetic term is
Lkin(2)​=21​tr[(∂H)2+(∂P)2],
(52)
since its mixed term is the trace of a commutator. A Hermitian matrix has N2 independent real components, so P describes exactly N2 massless scalar modes.
To determine the other masses, take T0​=1/N​ and traceless Hermitian TA​ with tr(TA​TB​)=δAB​, and expand H=h0​T0​+∑A=1N2−1​hA​TA​. These are canonically normalized real scalar fields. The scalar mass matrix is diagonal in this basis:
mh0​2​=2C2(α+Nβ)=−2m2,mhA​2​=2αC2(A=1,…,N2−1),mPA​2​=0(A=0,…,N2−1).​
(53)
Thus H contains one massive singlet and a degenerate multiplet of N2−1 massive fields in the Adjoint representation of the unbroken diagonal SU(N). Both squared masses are strictly positive for the specified parameters. The P fields are the N2 Goldstone bosons associated with the broken generators: N2−1 traceless relative left-right rotations and one overall phase rotation.
Goldstone's theorem guarantees those massless directions. It does not alone forbid additional accidental massless modes; the positive scalar mass matrix computed here supplies the further conclusion that every H component is massive. The fields describe a global symmetry, so these Goldstone bosons are physical rather than being absorbed into massive gauge bosons.
