For a Dirac field, the path integral is a Berezin integral over independent Grassmann fields ψ and ψˉ​:
Z=N∫Dψˉ​Dψexp[i∫d4xψˉ​(i∂−m)ψ].​
(1)
The vacuum boundary conditions and the Feynman i-epsilon prescription are understood; N fixes the vacuum normalization. This is the vacuum generating functional without sources, rather than a finite-temperature partition function.
The Dirac adjoint transforms as ψˉ​↦ψˉ​e−iα. For constant α, ∂μ​(eiαψ)=eiα∂μ​ψ. Both the kinetic term and the Dirac mass term therefore retain their values. This proves the global phase symmetry, whose group is the unitary group U(1).

For the local variation δψ=iα(x)ψ, δψˉ​=−iα(x)ψˉ​, the terms proportional to α cancel and the differentiated phase gives
δL​=i(−iαψˉ​)γμ∂μ​ψ+iψˉ​γμ∂μ​(iαψ)−mδ(ψˉ​ψ)=−ψˉ​γμψ∂μ​α.​
(2)

jμ=ψˉ​γμψ,δS=−∫d4xjμ∂μ​α=∫d4xα∂μ​jμ.​
(3)
This Dirac current is the Noether current in the convention δS=−∫jμ∂μ​α. Using the convention with a plus sign reverses the Noether current and the conserved charge together. There is one independent global symmetry in field theory, with four spacetime components of its Noether current. The associated conserved charge is Q=∫d3xj0, with vacuum subtraction when required.

An operator equation in a path integral O(x)=0 means that insertion of O(x) into every time-ordered correlation function vanishes when x is separated from the other insertion points. At coincident points, Ward identity contact terms can remain; an operator equation does not assert that these distributions vanish.
Let X be a product of local insertions. Make the local phase change of integration variables from part b. The functional measure has unit functional Jacobian: the opposite phases of ψ and ψˉ​ cancel in a vector-symmetry-preserving regularization in quantum field theory. There is no vector gauge anomaly. Invariance under a change of integration variables gives
0=⟨δα​X⟩+i∫d4xα(x)⟨T{∂μ​jμ(x)X}⟩.
(4)
If X=∏r​Or​(xr​) and δα​Or​=iqr​α(xr​)Or​, comparison of the coefficients of the arbitrary α(x) yields the Ward-Takahashi identity
⟨T{∂μ​jμ(x)X}⟩=−r∑​qr​δ(4)(x−xr​)⟨TX⟩.​
(5)
Consequently ∂μ​jμ=0 is an operator equation at separated points. This is stronger than just ⟨∂μ​jμ⟩=0. For example, X=ψ(y)ψˉ​(z) has qy​=1, qz​=−1, so its two Ward identity contact terms are [−δ(4)(x−y)+δ(4)(x−z)]⟨Tψ(y)ψˉ​(z)⟩; dropping them would give a false identity. Equivalently, the Dirac equation and the adjoint Dirac equation give ∂μ​(ψˉ​γμψ)=0, with the same coincident-point qualification in time-ordered correlation functions.

Couple a nondynamical background gauge field Bμ​ to the Dirac current, choosing a plus sign for the source term:
Z[B]=N∫Dψˉ​Dψexp{i∫d4x[ψˉ​(i∂−m)ψ+Bμ​ψˉ​γμψ]}.​
(6)
There is no integration over Bμ​. The gauge covariant derivative is ∂μ​−iBμ​, so the simultaneous transformations are
ψ′=eiα(x)ψ,ψˉ​′=ψˉ​e−iα(x),Bμ′​=Bμ​+∂μ​α.
(7)
The kinetic term changes by −jμ∂μ​α and the source term changes by +jμ∂μ​α. The action is therefore invariant, exactly for finite α as well as infinitesimally. Changing integration variables and using the invariant functional measure from part c proves the background gauge invariance of a current generating functional:
Z[B+∂α]=Z[B].​
(8)
The allowed transformations preserve the vacuum boundary conditions. Its infinitesimal version is ∂μ​δZ/δBμ​=0. Because Bμ​ is only a source, this statement needs no gauge fixing or photon kinetic term. The absence of a vector gauge anomaly is essential to the change-of-variables argument.

Collect the two Dirac fields into Ψ=(ψ1​,ψ2​)T, with a common nonzero mass m. Then
L=i=1∑2​ψˉ​i​(i∂−m)ψi​=Ψˉ(i∂−m12​)Ψ.​
(9)
Among complex-linear rotations of the Dirac fields, the internal symmetry of a classical field theory is Ψ↦UΨ, U∈U(2), with Ψˉ↦ΨˉU†. Unitarity preserves the kinetic term, and the mass matrix commutes with every U. This unitary flavour symmetry of degenerate Dirac fields has group (SU(2)×U(1))/Z2​, where the quotient avoids counting the common central element twice.
Choose the four Hermitian matrices T0=12​/2, Ta=σa/2 for a=1,2,3, with σa the Pauli matrices. Promoting the four parameters to functions gives δS=−∫d4x(∂μ​αA)ΨˉγμTAΨ. The four Noether currents are therefore
jAμ​=ΨˉγμTAΨ,A=0,1,2,3.​
(10)
Their divergences vanish by the equal-mass Dirac equations. The singlet is half the total Dirac current; the other three currents rotate the two species.
Free-theory qualification. If “global internal symmetry” allows mixing the Dirac fields with charge-conjugate fields, the completely free theory written here has a larger enhanced internal symmetry of free Dirac fields. Write ψi​=(λ2i−1​+iλ2i​)/2​ using four Majorana fermions. Up to a total derivative,
L=21​∑A=14​λˉA​(i∂−m)λA​.
(11)
The full orthogonal group O(4) acts on the four real species; its connected part is SO(4). Its six Noether currents can be chosen as JABμ​=iλˉA​γμλB​ for A<B, up to the common sign convention. The antisymmetry of this Majorana fermion vector bilinear makes these the six independent rotations. A concrete rotation outside the stated complex-linear U(2) is λ2​↦cosθλ2​+sinθλ3​, λ3​↦−sinθλ2​+cosθλ3​: it preserves the displayed Lagrangian density while mixing ψi​ with their charge-conjugate fields. The usual U(2) answer specifies the subgroup preserving the chosen common fermion number.

In the Weyl representation of the gamma matrices, write each Dirac field as ψi​=(ℓiα​,riα˙​)T, where ℓi​ and ri​ have opposite chirality. With the Weyl sigma matrices σμ=(1,σ) and σˉμ=(1,−σ),
L=i=1∑2​[iℓi†​σˉμ∂μ​ℓi​+iri†​σμ∂μ​ri​−m(ℓi†​ri​+ri†​ℓi​)].​
(12)
The Dirac mass term couples the two Weyl fields. Under ℓ↦UL​ℓ, r↦UR​r, its first term acquires UL†​UR​, so a common nonzero mass requires UL​=UR​. At zero mass there is no such constraint:
Gnumber preserving​=U(2)L​×U(2)R​.​
(13)
This is the chiral flavour symmetry of massless Dirac fields. Its eight Noether currents are jL,Aμ​=ℓ†σˉμTAℓ and jR,Aμ​=r†σμTAr. The sum jR​+jL​ is the vector current, and jR​−jL​ is the axial current, for each flavour generator. This statement concerns the free theory in flat spacetime; an interacting theory can have a chiral anomaly.
For completeness, allowing the extra transformations described in part e gives four left-handed Weyl fields, q=(ℓ1​,ℓ2​,r1c​,r2c​)T, where ric​ is the left-handed charge-conjugate field of ri​. After integration by parts their kinetic term is iq†σˉμ∂μ​q, whose maximal linear unitary group is U(4). The symmetric mass matrix for equal Dirac masses is
M=m(012​​12​0​),Lm​=−21​(qTMq+Hermitian conjugate),
(14)
with the antisymmetric spinor contraction implicit. A unitary change of basis makes M=m14​; preserving both the kinetic term and M requires V†V=VTV=14​, hence real V∈O(4). Thus the unrestricted free-theory enhancement is U(4) at zero mass and O(4) at equal nonzero mass; the familiar U(2)L​×U(2)R​ and diagonal U(2) are their common-fermion number-preserving subgroups.

The two one-loop Feynman diagrams are fermion loops with three photon interaction vertices. Fix vertex 1; the two cyclic orders are (1,2,3) and (1,3,2). A cyclic rotation does not produce another Feynman diagram. Keeping all three external labels in the same positions, these two orders can be represented by the same triangle with opposite fermion flow, as in the first two panels.

Use the source's charge-conjugation matrix convention CγμC−1=−(γμ)T. It implies
C−1(γμ)TC=−γμ,C−1K(p)TC=K(−p).
(16)
A trace equals the trace of its matrix transpose, which reverses the order of all factors. Inserting the displayed identities gives
TA​(ℓ)​=tr[γμK(ℓ+k1​)γνK(ℓ−k3​)γρK(ℓ)]=−tr[K(−ℓ)γρK(−ℓ+k3​)γνK(−ℓ−k1​)γμ]=−tr[γμK(−ℓ)γρK(−ℓ+k3​)γνK(−ℓ−k1​)].​
(17)
There is one minus sign from each of the three photon interaction vertices, and none from the transformed Dirac propagators. In the last line, change loop momentum to ℓ′=−ℓ−k1​. Since k1​+k3​=−k2​, its three Dirac propagators become K(ℓ′+k1​), K(ℓ′−k2​), K(ℓ′), exactly those of the reversed orientation. Hence
MAμνρ​=−MBμνρ​,Mone loopμνρ​=0.​
(18)
This is Furry's theorem. The regularization in quantum field theory matters: the individual Feynman integrals are superficially divergent, so an unregulated shift of a divergent integral is not a proof. A common dimensional regularization preserves charge conjugation and the vector Ward identity, making the change of variables legitimate. No chirality matrix occurs, so the axial triangle anomaly is not involved.

For an n-vertex fermion loop, reversing the fermion flow reverses the ordered product inside its trace. The same charge-conjugation matrix calculation transforms every Dirac propagator by p↦−p and contributes −1 for each vector photon interaction vertex. Relabel the loop momentum so that the external labels are fixed. The reversed Feynman diagram therefore obeys
Mreversed​=(−1)nMoriginal​.​
(19)
For odd n≥3, the (n−1)! cyclic orders pair into opposite orientations and cancel. For n=1, the single photon tadpole maps into itself with a minus sign and is zero. The common closed fermion loop sign is present on both sides and does not supply the factor (−1)n. This proves Furry's theorem for all the requested one-loop Feynman diagrams. The charge conjugation argument also gives vanishing odd-photon correlation functions to all orders in vacuum QED, provided no charge conjugation symmetry breaking background or charged external state is introduced.

At large loop momentum, each Dirac propagator scales as ℓ−1. One four-dimensional loop and four internal Dirac propagators therefore give the superficial degree of divergence
D=4−4=0.
(22)
Thus a possible ultraviolet divergence in the sum is logarithmic. By locality its divergent coefficient is a degree-zero polynomial in the external four-momenta, namely a constant Lorentz tensor,
Mdivμνρσ​=ϵ1​(agμνgρσ+bgμρgνσ+cgμσgνρ).
(23)
The photon Ward identities apply to the complete sum for arbitrary external four-momenta, including off shell ones: k1μ​Mμνρσ=0, and likewise for the other legs. A constant tensor with k1μ​Mdivμνρσ​=0 for every k1​ must be zero. Hence a=b=c=0 and
Mone loopμνρσ​ is ultraviolet finite.​
(24)
Equivalently, a logarithmic divergence would require a local dimension-four A4 counterterm, forbidden by Abelian gauge invariance. A gauge-invariant four-photon local operator starts with four gauge field strengths, for example (Fμν​Fμν)2, of mass dimension eight; it cannot be this power counting divergence. Individual boxes need not be finite; their forbidden divergent parts cancel in the sum. There are no divergent proper loop subgraphs at this loop order. With the massive Dirac field in the source, there is also no soft or collinear infrared divergence at generic external kinematics. The argument establishes ultraviolet finiteness, and does not claim the absence of physical threshold singularities.

Choose Dμ​=∂μ​−igAμa​ta on the Dirac field, ta=σa/2 in the fundamental representation of SU(2), and [ta,tb]=iϵabctc. Define the gauge field strength and the Adjoint representation gauge covariant derivative by
Fμνa​=∂μ​Aνa​−∂ν​Aμa​+gϵabcAμb​Aνc​,(Dμ​c)a=∂μ​ca+gϵabcAμb​cc.
(25)
The required gauge-fixed Yang-Mills Lagrangian in a covariant gauge, including the matter term, is
L=−41​Fμνa​Faμν+ψˉ​(iγμDμ​−m)ψ−2ξ1​(∂μAμa​)2−cˉa∂μ(Dμ​c)a.​
(26)
Here ca and cˉa are independent Grassmann fields in the Adjoint representation: the Faddeev-Popov ghost fields and antighost fields. Under an infinitesimal gauge transformation, δAμa​=(Dμ​ω)a; hence the Lorenz gauge function Ga=∂μAμa​ varies by ∂μDμab​ωb. Its Faddeev-Popov determinant is represented by the ghost term, with an irrelevant field-independent determinant sign absorbed into normalization. Integration by parts gives Lgh​=(∂μcˉa)(Dμ​c)a, explicitly showing the ghost-gluon vertex.
There is no scalar vacuum expectation value here, so R-xi gauge reduces to the linear covariant gauge displayed above, with no Goldstone boson term. At ξ=1 the Feynman gauge gauge boson propagator is −iδabgμν​/(k2+i0). With Fourier convention e−ikx and all three boson four-momenta incoming, this choice gives exactly the source's +gϵabc three-boson Feynman rule, and the matter interaction vertex is +igγμta. The sign of Dμ​ has been explicitly chosen here; the QED convention in question 2 instead has vertex −ieγμ.

The non-Abelian counterpart of the photon Ward identity is a Slavnov-Taylor identity, derived from BRST symmetry. A scattering amplitude with all other external states physical vanishes when one external massless gauge boson polarization vector is replaced by its four-momentum. An amputated tensor with the remaining indices open need not be transverse: part d gives its explicit nonzero longitudinal remainder.
Consequently, amplitudes involving several unphysical covariant polarization vectors need not vanish. For example, contracting the result of part d with a timelike vector nμ​ gives ig2ϵabc(Jc​⋅k2​)(n⋅k1​)/s, nonzero in the explicit configuration there for n=(1,0,0,0). Even with other states physical, the pure-gauge vector k is the relevant null combination; separate timelike and spatial longitudinal basis vectors are not themselves pure gauge, and their separate amplitudes need not be zero.
These auxiliary components do not define separately observable positive probabilities. Physical states are classes in BRST cohomology, and the S-matrix preserves that physical state space in this anomaly-free vectorlike gauge spectrum. There is no physical transition from a physical state to a nonphysical state. The incorrect stronger statement would be that every covariant component called “unphysical” has a zero matrix element.
For loop Feynman diagrams or unitarity cuts, summing covariant gauge indices with −gμν​ propagates those auxiliary components. In a Non-Abelian gauge theory, their cancellation requires the interacting Faddeev-Popov ghost fields as well as the gauge boson diagrams. Ghost contributions carry their Grassmann field loop signs and restore the Slavnov-Taylor identities and physical unitarity. One may instead use a formulation with only physical propagating polarization vectors, but one cannot simply discard the ghost diagrams while retaining the Feynman gauge rules from part a.
