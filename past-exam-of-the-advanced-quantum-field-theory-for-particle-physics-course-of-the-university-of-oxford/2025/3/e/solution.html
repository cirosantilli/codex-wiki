After the quadratic mixing cancellation, the vector inverse quadratic operator is
K=−(k2−mA2​)PT−(ξk2​−mA2​)PL.
(49)
Inverting its two orthogonal projectors and multiplying by i gives
Dμν(ξ)​(k)=k2−mA2​+i0−i​[gμν​−(1−ξ)k2−ξmA2​+i0kμ​kν​​].​
(50)
Equivalently D(ξ)=−iPT/(k2−mA2​+i0)−iξPL/(k2−ξmA2​+i0). At fixed finite ξ, both sectors fall as k−2 at large momentum in Euclidean space; ξ=1 gives −igμν​/(k2−mA2​+i0), and taking ξ→∞ at fixed k recovers unitary gauge. The latter limit is not uniform in ultraviolet momentum.
The expected power counting conclusion is that this k−2 behaviour, together with polynomial interactions of mass dimension at most four in Cartesian scalar variables and the gauge symmetry identities, permits renormalization of spontaneously broken gauge theories using a finite set of physical parameters. The propagator alone is not sufficient to establish that claim in the polar variables of this question. In fact, part b contains h(∂π)2/f and h2(∂π)2/(2f2), of dimensions five and six, and the nonlinear gauge fixing term contains −ξe2h4π2/(2f2), also of dimension six. Ordinary power counting is consequently not manifest in these variables.
For a representation where it is manifest, write ϕ=(f+H+iχ)/2​ and choose the usual polynomial R-xi gauge function ∂⋅A−ξefχ. It yields the same free vector propagator, cancels the quadratic mixing, and leaves only dimension-at-most-four interactions, including its Faddeev-Popov ghosts. This supplies the missing vertex condition in the source's proposed inference; the equivalence theorem for field redefinitions relates physical predictions near the vacuum. Good propagator falloff is the necessary ingredient illustrated by the requested formula, rather than a complete proof for arbitrary parametrizations or nonlinear choices of gauge fixing.
