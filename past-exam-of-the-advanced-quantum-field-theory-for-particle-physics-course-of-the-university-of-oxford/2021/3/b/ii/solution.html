For commuting real scalar fields, remove the closed fermion loop sign and replace the Dirac propagators by scalar propagators. There are no spinor indices, hence no matrix trace. With two independent real scalar fields χ1​,χ2​ and interaction gϕχ1​χ2​, the same normalization gives
Gc,χ1​χ2​(2)​=−g2∫d4xd4yD(xa​−x)D(ya​−y)G1​(x−y)G2​(y−x).​
(40)
The overall sign has reversed relative to part (i) because (ig)2 is now unaccompanied by a closed fermion loop sign. The same formula holds in the source's kernel convention, since there are again four propagators.
There is an additional convention to specify if the two symbols are intended to denote the same real scalar field. For the literal interaction gϕχ2, there are two identical connected Wick contractions between χ2(x) and χ2(y), so the coefficient becomes −2g2. If the conventional interaction is instead (g/2)ϕχ2, each vertex is ig and the loop has symmetry factor 1/2, giving coefficient −g2/2. In both interpretations the closed fermion loop sign and spinor matrix trace disappear; the differing numerical factor comes from the interaction normalization and field identity.
