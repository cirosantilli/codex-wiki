At a stationary symmetry-breaking vacuum of a gauge-invariant scalar potential, the angular field has zero potential curvature. Its free kinetic term is (∂b)2/2, while the displayed gauge-fixing term adds −ξg2v2b2/2. Hence
Db​(p)=p2−ξMA2​+i0i​,mb2​=ξMA2​.​
(40)
This is the would-be Goldstone boson, whose gauge-dependent pole accompanies the longitudinal pole in i; it is not an additional physical scalar particle of that mass. For MA​=0, fixed finite p and ξ→+∞,
Db​(p)∼−ξMA2​i​⟶0.
(41)
The angular field becomes infinitely heavy in this gauge and is absent from the unitary gauge propagating description. The qualification about fixed momentum matters: loop integrations probe arbitrarily large p, so one cannot automatically interchange the ξ→∞ limit with ultraviolet integration. If gv=0, there is no massive Higgs phase and the asserted heavy-field limit does not follow. A separate radial Higgs boson may remain; its mass depends on the unspecified potential and is not determined by this gauge-fixing term.
