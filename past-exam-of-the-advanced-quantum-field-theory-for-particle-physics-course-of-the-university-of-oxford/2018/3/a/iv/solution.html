The scalar potential is
V=−2μ2​(ϕ12​+ϕ22​)+4λ​(ϕ12​+ϕ22​)2=4λ​(ϕ12​+ϕ22​−λμ2​)2−4λμ4​.
(30)
Since μ2,λ>0, all minima form the circle ϕ12​+ϕ22​=v2, with v=μ/λ​>0. Choose the representative vacuum ⟨ϕ1​⟩=v, ⟨ϕ2​⟩=0, and write ϕ1​=v+h, ϕ2​=π. A nontrivial scalar rotation moves this vacuum to another minimum, so the continuous symmetry undergoes spontaneous symmetry breaking. The invariant action and the selected ground state have different stabilizers; the circle of equivalent minima is not evidence that the action lost its symmetry.

Comparison with the free Dirac equation gives
mψ​=gv as the signed mass coefficient,mphysical​=∣g∣v.​

For the usual choice g>0, this is simply gv. The absolute value matters if the real coupling is negative: the dispersion depends on mψ2​, and a field redefinition can change the mass coefficient's sign. There is no explicit symmetry-breaking fermion mass parameter in the original theory.
The scalar symmetry is broken completely. Because the spinor transformation uses a half angle, α=2π leaves the scalars fixed while sending ψ→−ψ; this discrete fermion parity is unbroken in the full combined transformation group. It does not remove the broken continuous generator or the associated Goldstone boson.
