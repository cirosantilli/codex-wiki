There are four topologies for the one-loop gluon self-energy, shown in the upper row of the figure:
  • A closed quark loop with the two external gluons attached through quark-gluon Feynman vertices, summed over active quark flavors.
  • A loop of two internal gluons joining two three-gluon Feynman vertices, each carrying one external gluon.
  • A closed Faddeev-Popov ghost field loop with two ghost-gluon Feynman vertices.
  • A single four-gluon Feynman vertex with two external gluons and its remaining two legs contracted into a tadpole diagram.
The two internal-gluon graphs have symmetry factor 1/2. The directed quark and ghost loops have symmetry factor one and a minus sign for a closed anticommuting loop. The four-gluon tadpole diagram must be included in the list even though its massless Feynman integral vanishes in dimensional regularization. Local counterterms are excluded here.

At the external gluon with indices (μ,a), let the incoming ghost carry colour c and the outgoing ghost carry colour d; the other Feynman vertex has the reverse order. (1)

iΠghμν,ab​=−gD2​CA​δabIμν,Iμν=∫(2π)DdDl​(l2+i0)((l+k)2+i0)(l+k)μlν​.

There is one four-gluon tadpole diagram. Temporarily replace its internal gluon propagator by −igρσ​δcd/(l2−mg2​+i0). Contract the supplied four-gluon Feynman vertex with gρσ​δcd. Its first colour factor vanishes because fcce=0. Each of the other two terms contributes CA​δab(D−1)gμν, so

iΠ4gμν,ab​=−gD2​CA​δab(D−1)gμνJ1​(mg2​).
(8)

J1​(mg2​)=−(4π)D/2i​Γ(1−D/2)(mg2​)D/2−1.

The Slavnov-Taylor identity implies transversality of the full perturbative gluon self-energy:
kμ​(Π3gμν,ab​+Π4gμν,ab​+Πghμν,ab​+Πqμν,ab​)=0.
(12)
The quark loop is separately transverse. This follows without evaluating its Feynman integral: contract its vector Feynman vertex with kμ​ and use k=(l+k−m)−(l−m) between its two Dirac propagators. One obtains a difference of two loop integrals that cancel after a shift of l in dimensional regularization. The colour factor is proportional to tr(TaTb) and does not obstruct this argument.
The four-gluon tadpole diagram vanishes after removal of mg​. Thus the nontransverse parts of the two-three-gluon-vertex loop and the ghost loop must cancel. Contracting the preceding ghost-loop pole of the gluon self-energy gives
kμ​Π3gμν,ab​​UV​=−kμ​Πghμν,ab​​UV​=−32π2ϵg2CA​δab​k2kν.​
(13)
For QCD, set CA​=3. No evaluation of the remaining three-gluon Feynman diagram is required. Its uncontracted transverse part is not fixed by this identity.

For the one-loop renormalization-group beta function, write t=logμ. Separating variables gives
∫αi​αf​​α2dα​=b0​∫logμi​logμf​​dt,α(μf​)=1−b0​α(μi​)log(μf​/μi​)α(μi​)​.​
(14)
This expression resums the leading logarithms of the one-loop running coupling, while omitting higher-order terms in the renormalization-group beta function. Assume a positive initial coupling.
For b0​<0, increasing the renormalization scale decreases the running coupling; the zero-coupling renormalization-group fixed point is attractive in the ultraviolet. This is asymptotic freedom. Toward lower scales the one-loop expression has a formal pole at μi​exp[1/(b0​αi​)]<μi​. The strong-coupling neighborhood of that pole lies outside perturbative control. QCD provides the main example; the minimal Standard Model SU(2) coupling is also asymptotically free at one loop.
For b0​>0, the running coupling grows with the renormalization scale and formally diverges at the ultraviolet Landau pole μi​exp[1/(b0​αi​)]>μi​. Toward the infrared it decreases to zero within this approximation. The Standard Model U(1) weak hypercharge coupling has this sign, as does QED in a fixed charged-particle regime. A perturbative Landau pole marks failure of the approximation, rather than proving that the exact theory has that divergence.
For b0​=0, the running coupling is constant at this order; higher-loop terms decide whether it runs. Vanishing of the leading coefficient alone does not establish exact scale invariance.
For a concrete common convention, define αi​=gi2​/(4π) with gY​ in its usual, non-unified weak hypercharge normalization. In the minimal Standard Model with three generations and one Higgs doublet, above its particle thresholds,
dlogμdαi​​=2πbi​​αi2​,(bY​,b2​,b3​)=(641​,−619​,−7).
(15)
Thus the question's b0​ is bi​/(2π), with the sign regimes just described. Crossing a mass threshold changes the active field content and requires the appropriate piecewise renormalization-group beta function.

For a real local parameter α(x), the Dirac adjoint varies as δψˉ​=ieαψˉ​. The matter kinetic term varies by
δ(ψˉ​iγμ∂μ​ψ)=e(∂μ​α)ψˉ​γμψ.
(16)
The QED interaction varies by −e(∂μ​α)ψˉ​γμψ, since the phases in ψˉ​γμψ cancel. These two variations cancel pointwise. The mass term is invariant for the same reason, and δFμν​=∂μ​∂ν​α−∂ν​∂μ​α=0.
Only the gauge fixing term varies. Put B=∂μ​Aμ; then δB=∂2α, giving
δLQED​=−ξ1​(∂μ​Aμ)∂2α.​
(17)
For arbitrary local α, the gauge-fixed action is therefore not invariant under this transformation of its dynamical fields. The restricted parameters satisfying ∂2α=0 give a residual invariance of the displayed density.

Let ϵ here be a constant infinitesimal transformation parameter, unrelated to the dimensional regularization parameter in question 1. The matter and Maxwell terms undergo an ordinary infinitesimal gauge transformation with α=ϵϕ, and their variations cancel as before. The gauge fixing contribution is
δLgf​=−ξϵ​B∂2ϕ.
(18)
The extra real scalar field kinetic term varies by
δ(21​∂μ​ϕ∂μϕ)=∂μ​ϕ∂μδϕ=−ξϵ​(∂μ​ϕ)(∂μB).
(19)
Adding the two gives a total derivative:
δL=−ξϵ​∂μ​(B∂μϕ),δS=0.​
(20)
The last equality uses vanishing boundary contributions, for example sufficiently fast decay of the fields. No field equations were used. This gauge-scalar global symmetry of covariantly fixed QED leaves the action invariant even though the density need not be invariant pointwise; the scalar's variation supplies exactly the missing boundary-term cancellation.

First derive the contracted gamma matrices identities from the Clifford algebra in four dimensions. Moving the outer gamma matrix through two factors gives
γρ​γα​γβ​γρ=4gαβ​1.
(21)
Moving it through three factors, and using this two-factor result, gives
γρ​γα​γβ​γγ​γρ​=2γβ​γγ​γα​−4gβγ​γα​=−2γγ​γβ​γα​.​
(22)
Consequently γνbγμ​cγν​=−2cγμ​b and γμ​bdγμ=4(b⋅d)1. Apply these in succession:
T​=tr(aγνbγμ​cγν​dγμ)=−2tr(acγμ​bdγμ)=−8(b⋅d)tr(ac)=−32(a⋅c)(b⋅d)​.​
(23)
The final step uses tr(γαγβ)=4gαβ. This is a genuinely four-dimensional instance of the gamma matrix trace identities; using it unchanged inside a D-dimensional loop calculation would require separate justification.

Label the four-momenta as e−(p1​)μ−(p2​)→e−(p3​)μ−(p4​). There is one tree-level Feynman diagram, the t-channel photon exchange shown in the lower-left panel. The two lepton species are distinct, so there is no exchanged-final-lepton diagram. With s=(p1​+p2​)2, t=(p1​−p3​)2 and u=(p1​−p4​)2, the Feynman rules give
iM=tie2​[uˉ(p3​)γμu(p1​)][uˉ(p4​)γμ​u(p2​)].
(24)
The massless fermion spin sum is ∑r​ur​(p)uˉr​(p)=p. Averaging the two initial spins of each lepton supplies 1/4, so
⟨∣M∣2⟩=4t2e4​tr(p3​γμp1​γν)tr(p4​γμ​p2​γν​).
(25)
The gamma matrix trace identities imply, for arbitrary null external four-momenta,
tr(aγμbγν)tr(cγμ​dγν​)=32[(a⋅c)(b⋅d)+(a⋅d)(b⋅c)].
(26)
To see the contraction, each trace is 4(aμbν+aνbμ−gμνa⋅b); the terms proportional to (a⋅b)(c⋅d) cancel in four dimensions, leaving the displayed two products. Here p1​⋅p2​=p3​⋅p4​=s/2 and p1​⋅p4​=p2​⋅p3​=−u/2. Therefore
⟨∣M∣2⟩=2e4t2s2+u2​.​
(27)
The requested proportionality constant is 2e4=32π2αem2​, where αem​ is the fine-structure constant. Both numerator terms are required by the spin sum; no final-state statistical factor enters a squared scattering amplitude for distinct leptons.

For Møller scattering, both the t-channel and u-channel photon exchange tree-level Feynman diagrams contribute, as shown in the two remaining lower panels. Exchanging the identical final electrons reverses the sign of the scattering amplitude, so the fermionic sign fixes
M=e2(tJt​​−uJu​​),Jt​=[uˉ3​γμu1​][uˉ4​γμ​u2​],Ju​=[uˉ4​γμu1​][uˉ3​γμ​u2​].

41​∑∣Jt​∣2=2(s2+u2),41​∑∣Ju​∣2=2(s2+t2).
(29)
The interference cannot be discarded because the final particles are identical. Summing their spins joins all four external Dirac spinors into a single trace:
∑Jt​Ju∗​​=tr(p3​γμp1​γνp4​γμ​p2​γν​)=−32(p3​⋅p4​)(p1​⋅p2​)=−8s2.​

−4tu2e4​Re∑Jt​Ju∗​=tu4e4s2​.
(31)
Thus
⟨∣M∣2⟩=2e4[t2s2+u2​+u2s2+t2​+tu2s2​].​

This is symmetric under t↔u, as required by the identical final electrons. There is no additional 1/2! in the squared scattering amplitude. After integration by parts, the quadratic Proca action with an external source is
S[A,J]=21​∫d4xAμ​KμνAν​+∫d4xJμAμ​,Kμν=gμν(∂2+M2)−∂μ∂ν.
(33)
Since M>0, this kernel is invertible with the Feynman i-epsilon prescription; the massless gauge redundancy is absent in this unitary gauge quadratic action. Complete the square by A↦A+KF−1​J in the Gaussian functional integral. Its source-independent determinant cancels in the normalized generating functional:
Z[0]Z[J]​=exp[−2i​∫d4xd4yJμ​(x)(KF−1​)μν(x−y)Jν​(y)].​
(34)
In Fourier space, the kernel is
Kμν(k)=(M2−k2)gμν+kμkν.
(35)
For k2=0, use the transverse projector of a vector field PTμν​=gμν−kμkν/k2 and the longitudinal projector of a vector field PLμν​=kμkν/k2. Their mixed-index products obey PT​PL​=0, so
K=(M2−k2)PT​+M2PL​,K−1=M2−k2PT​​+M2PL​​=−k2−M2gμν−kμkν/M2​.
(36)
The last expression also extends to k2=0; that point only obstructs the intermediate projector decomposition. Two source functional derivatives yield the gauge-boson propagator DF​=iKF−1​:
DFμν​(k)=k2−M2+i0−i​(gμν−M2kμkν​),⟨TAμ(x)Aν(y)⟩=∫(2π)4d4k​e−ik⋅(x−y)DFμν​(k).​
(37)
The pole residue is the physical massive-vector massive vector polarization sum. The longitudinal term is essential for inverting the quadratic kernel, and makes the naive M→0 limit of this unitary gauge Proca propagator singular.

Expand the matrix trace explicitly:
L=∑i,j=1N​[∂μ​Φij∗​∂μΦij​−m2Φij∗​Φij​].
(38)
Write Φij​=(xij​+iyij​)/2​ and collect the 2N2 real fields into a vector X. Then
L=21​∑A=12N2​[∂μ​XA​∂μXA​−m2XA2​].
(39)
Both quadratic forms are preserved by every constant orthogonal matrix R, acting as X↦RX. Thus the full linear internal symmetry contains O(2N2), with connected special orthogonal group SO(2N2) as requested. The enlargement beyond unitary transformations is possible because the real and imaginary components have identical kinetic and mass terms and no interactions distinguishing them.
For a Hermitian matrix Φ, there are N real diagonal entries and N(N−1)/2 independent complex off-diagonal entries, hence N2 real fields. Choose an orthonormal basis TA​ of Hermitian matrices for the trace inner product, tr(TA​TB​)=δAB​, and put Φ=∑A​XA​TA​/2​. The same calculation gives
SO(2N2) for a general complex matrix,SO(N2) for a Hermitian matrix,​
(40)
where these are the connected rotation groups; their full orthogonal extensions are O(2N2) and O(N2). A purely quadratic theory with m2<0 would have an unstable scalar potential, but this algebraic symmetry statement still holds.

Let X=Φ†Φ. Under the global transformation of the chiral matrix scalar model,
Φ′=eiθUL​ΦUR†​,X′=UR​XUR†​.
(41)
The phase and UL†​UL​ cancel, and UR†​UR​=1. The cyclic property of the trace implies
trX′=trX,tr(X′2)=tr(UR​X2UR†​)=trX2.
(42)
Therefore each of the three terms in the scalar potential is invariant separately. Since θ,UL​,UR​ are constant, ∂μ​Φ′=eiθUL​(∂μ​Φ)UR†​, giving invariance of the kinetic matrix trace by the same argument. Hence
G=SU(N)L​×SU(N)R​×U(1)​
(43)
is a global symmetry group of the Lagrangian density. Its action has a finite central kernel, which does not affect its Lie algebra or the broken-generator counting below. For special parameter choices the symmetry can be larger; the displayed group is a symmetry for the stated potential without such extra assumptions.

It is useful first to establish the global minimum, rather than only a stationary point. The positive semidefinite matrix X=Φ†Φ has nonnegative eigenvalues x1​,…,xN​. For fixed r=∑i​xi​, the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality gives ∑i​xi2​≥r2/N, with equality exactly when all xi​ are equal. Since α>0,
V≥21​(Nα​+β)r2+m2r.
(44)
For m2<0 and β>0, this lower bound has its minimum at
r=−α+NβNm2​,xi​=C2=−α+Nβm2​>0.
(45)
The bound is attained, so every minimum has Φ†Φ=C21, or Φ=CW with W a unitary matrix. A global transformation removes W by decomposing it into an overall phase and an SU(N) matrix. Thus Φ0​=C1 is a valid representative of the vacuum manifold.
Preservation of this chosen vacuum expectation value requires
eiθUL​UR†​=1.
(46)
Infinitesimally, with traceless Hermitian generators L,R, this reads θ1+L−R=0. Taking the trace forces θ=0, and then L=R. The surviving continuous transformations are consequently
Φ↦UΦU†,U∈SU(N),Hconnected​≃SU(N)diagonal​.​
(47)
All other independent continuous directions change the chosen vacuum expectation value. Thus the number of broken generators is
[2(N2−1)+1]−(N2−1)=N2.​
(48)
Globally, taking the determinant of the stabilizer equation permits the discrete phases eiNθ=1, paired with UL​=e−iθUR​. They induce the same conjugation action on Φ. The faithful unbroken conjugation group is SU(N)/ZN​; for example, at N=2, U=−1 acts exactly like U=1. Thus the question's description by SU(N) is correct as a parametrization and at the Lie algebra level, with this central redundancy understood. It leaves the number of broken generators unchanged.

Write H=ϕ1​ and P=ϕ2​, both Hermitian matrices. Through quadratic order around the chosen vacuum expectation value,
X=Φ†Φ=C21+2​CH+21​(H2+P2+i[H,P]).
(49)
The trace of a commutator vanishes. The needed matrix traces are therefore
trXtrX2(trX)2​=NC2+2​CtrH+21​tr(H2+P2),=NC4+22​C3trH+C2tr(3H2+P2)+O((H,P)3),=N2C4+22​NC3trH+NC2tr(H2+P2)+2C2(trH)2+O((H,P)3).​
(50)
Using m2=−(α+Nβ)C2, all linear terms cancel. The quadratic scalar potential reduces to
V(2)=αC2trH2+βC2(trH)2,​
(51)
with no P2 term. The kinetic term is
Lkin(2)​=21​tr[(∂H)2+(∂P)2],
(52)
since its mixed term is the trace of a commutator. A Hermitian matrix has N2 independent real components, so P describes exactly N2 massless scalar modes.
To determine the other masses, take T0​=1/N​ and traceless Hermitian TA​ with tr(TA​TB​)=δAB​, and expand H=h0​T0​+∑A=1N2−1​hA​TA​. These are canonically normalized real scalar fields. The scalar mass matrix is diagonal in this basis:
mh0​2​=2C2(α+Nβ)=−2m2,mhA​2​=2αC2(A=1,…,N2−1),mPA​2​=0(A=0,…,N2−1).​
(53)
Thus H contains one massive singlet and a degenerate multiplet of N2−1 massive fields in the Adjoint representation of the unbroken diagonal SU(N). Both squared masses are strictly positive for the specified parameters. The P fields are the N2 Goldstone bosons associated with the broken generators: N2−1 traceless relative left-right rotations and one overall phase rotation.
Goldstone's theorem guarantees those massless directions. It does not alone forbid additional accidental massless modes; the positive scalar mass matrix computed here supplies the further conclusion that every H component is massive. The fields describe a global symmetry, so these Goldstone bosons are physical rather than being absorbed into massive gauge bosons.
