The open-index tensor is generally nonzero: neither ϵabc nor Jc​⋅k2​ is constrained to vanish. For a concrete nonzero configuration, take centre-of-momentum frame kinematics p1​=(E,0,0,p), p2​=(E,0,0,−p), p=E2−m2​, k1​=(E,E,0,0), k2​=(E,−E,0,0). Standard Dirac spinors with two-component spin labels χ=(1,0)T for u and η=(0,1)T for v give the spinor current (0,2E,2iE,0) and its contraction with k2​ is 2E2. Choosing both colour spinors (1,0)T and (a,b,c)=(1,2,3) multiplies this by 1/2, still nonzero. This explicitly rules out an unconditional tensor identity k2ν​Mabμν=0.
For a physical polarization vector ε1μ∗​, however, k1​⋅ε1∗​=0, and therefore
ε1μ∗​k2ν​Mabμν=0.​
(34)
This is the required decoupling of a pure-gauge polarization vector on leg 2 when the other leg is physical. Interchanging the legs gives the corresponding identity for leg 1. The role of the three-boson diagram is explicit: it cancels the noncommuting colour-current term left by the two fermion exchanges.
