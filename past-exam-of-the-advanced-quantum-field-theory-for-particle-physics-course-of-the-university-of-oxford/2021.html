The superficial degree of divergence of this scalar electrodynamics three-point vertex is one. Its ultraviolet pole is consequently a local expression at most linear in the external four-momenta. The allowed counterterm Lagrangian, with the charge-conjugation and Hermiticity properties of scalar electrodynamics, contains a single one-derivative scalar electrodynamics three-point vertex:
Vctμ​(k,k′)=−ieδ1​(k+k′)μ.
(1)
Thus the coefficient δ1​ is independent of the chosen external four-momenta. Setting k′=0 leaves −ieδ1​kμ, so a nonzero generic k still determines that coefficient. It does not determine the full finite scattering amplitude at other four-momenta.
Keep m=0 and generic off shell k while extracting the ultraviolet pole. These choices avoid spurious infrared divergences; setting all scales to zero would instead produce scaleless integrals that can conceal the ultraviolet pole.

The scalar QED Ward identity equates the vertex renormalization and wave-function renormalization constants. Consequently, using part (iv),
Z2​=Z1​=1+16π2ϵe2(3−ξ)​+O(e4).​
(10)
The renormalization constants describe gauge-dependent fields and off shell vertices, so their dependence on ξ is permitted. A physical scattering amplitude includes all Feynman diagrams, the counterterms, and the external-state factors from LSZ reduction. The Ward identities ensure that the dependence on ξ cancels for physical external states. Infrared-sensitive probabilities also require the usual inclusive treatment of unobserved soft photons. Thus no dependence on ξ is implied for consistently calculated physical observables.

Let Σ=me2​+mt2​, Qe​=−1, Qt​=2/3 and Nc​=3. The single tree-level Feynman diagram is annihilation through a virtual photon. Up to an irrelevant common phase, its scattering amplitude is
Mγ​=se2Qe​Qt​​[vˉ(p2​)γμu(p1​)][uˉ(p3​)γμ​v(p4​)]δcd​.
(12)
There are two initial spin angular momentum states for each particle, so the spin average is 1/4. The final color charge sum gives ∑c,d​∣δcd​∣2=Nc​. There is no additional photon polarization average for a virtual photon and no separate polarization label beyond the Dirac spinor spin sums.
The spin sums and the gamma-matrix trace identities give
LμνHμν​​=tr[(p2​−me​)γμ(p1​+me​)γν]=4[p2μ​p1ν​+p2ν​p1μ​−2s​gμν],=tr[(p3​+mt​)γμ​(p4​−mt​)γν​]=4[p3μ​p4ν​+p3ν​p4μ​−2s​gμν​].​

Here p1​⋅p2​+me2​=p3​⋅p4​+mt2​=s/2. Contracting gives
41​LμνHμν​=8[(p1​⋅p3​)(p2​⋅p4​)+(p1​⋅p4​)(p2​⋅p3​)]+4sΣ.

Since 2p1​⋅p3​=2p2​⋅p4​=Σ−t and 2p1​⋅p4​=2p2​⋅p3​=Σ−u, this is the massive vector-current annihilation kernel
2[(Σ−t)2+(Σ−u)2+2sΣ]=2[t2+u2+4sΣ−2Σ2].

∣Mγ​∣2​=s22Nc​e4Qe2​Qt2​​[t2+u2+4sΣ−2Σ2]=3s28e4​[t2+u2+4sΣ−2Σ2].​

There are exactly four tree-level Feynman diagrams: one four-photon contact graph and one exchange graph for each of the three partitions 12∣34, 13∣24 and 14∣23 of the external legs. The exchange graphs contain two cubic Feynman vertices. A fermion loop or a closed Faddeev-Popov ghost line has positive loop order, so neither enters here.
Write p1​+p2​+p3​+p4​=0 and take physical photon polarization vectors εi​ with pi​⋅εi​=0 and pi2​=0. At the cubic Feynman vertex attached to legs i,j, the two terms containing pi​⋅εi​ or pj​⋅εj​ vanish. If q=pi​+pj​ flows into the internal line, the remaining factor at that end is
−2λqμ(εi​⋅εj​).
(23)
At the other end the internal incoming four-momentum is q rather than −q, giving +2λqν(εk​⋅εl​). Multiplication by the photon propagator gives
iMij∣kl​=(−2λqμ)q2−igμν​​(2λqν)(εi​⋅εj​)(εk​⋅εl​)=4iλ2(εi​⋅εj​)(εk​⋅εl​).
(24)
The cancellation holds first at generic nonzero channel Mandelstam invariants and then by continuation at degenerate kinematics. Summing the three exchange graphs produces 4iλ2P, where
P=(ε1​⋅ε2​)(ε3​⋅ε4​)+(ε1​⋅ε3​)(ε2​⋅ε4​)+(ε1​⋅ε4​)(ε2​⋅ε3​).
(25)
The quartic Feynman vertex contributes −4iλ2P. Therefore
Mγγ→γγtree​=0.​
(26)
This is a cancellation for physical on shell photon polarization vectors. The off shell four-photon correlation function need not vanish. Physical light-by-light scattering first occurs through charged-particle loop Feynman diagrams.

Put r2=ΦTΦ. The potential has its minimum at
drdV​=−m2r+4λr3=0,v2=4λm2​​.

Choose the vacuum expectation value Φ0​=(0,0,v)T. Since the weak hypercharge is zero, Q=T3 on this real scalar triplet, and T3Φ0​=0. Neither T1 nor T2 annihilates Φ0​. The stabilizer subgroup is consequently
SU(2)×U(1)Y​⟶U(1)T3​×U(1)Y​.​

This cannot be the sole symmetry-breaking sector of the Standard Model. Its gauge-boson mass matrix has two zero eigenvalues, so besides the electromagnetic photon it predicts an extra massless neutral gauge boson. Every linear combination of W3 and B, including the combination normally called Z, is massless. The observed massive Z boson is a concrete contradiction. Choosing g,g′,m,λ differently cannot change these two zero eigenvalues.
A real scalar triplet can occur as an additional sector, but a further vacuum expectation value, for example that of a Higgs doublet, is then necessary to obtain the observed neutral gauge boson mass pattern. This does not rescue the triplet-only model posed here.

Normalize the generating functional by its value with all sources zero, and take the connected part of the time-ordered correlation function. Source differentiation of the interaction exponential is equivalent to the Dyson series followed by Wick's theorem. Write D(x−y)=⟨Tϕ(x)ϕ(y)⟩0​ and Fij​(x−y)=⟨Tψi​(x)ψˉ​j​(y)⟩0​ temporarily, so that every contraction has an unambiguous meaning.
The second-order Dyson series contributes
2!(ig)2​∫d4xd4y⟨Tϕ(xa​)ϕ(ya​)ϕ(x)ϕ(y)[ψˉ​i​(x)ψi​(x)][ψˉ​j​(y)ψj​(y)]⟩0,c​.
(35)
There are two connected external scalar field attachments:
D(xa​−x)D(ya​−y)+D(xa​−y)D(ya​−x).
(36)
They become equal after exchanging the integration variables x,y, cancelling 2!. The connected fermion contractions have the closed fermion loop sign:
⟨T[ψˉ​i​(x)ψi​(x)][ψˉ​j​(y)ψj​(y)]⟩0,c​=−Fij​(x−y)Fji​(y−x)=−tr[F(x−y)F(y−x)].
(37)
The minus sign can also be checked directly: the pairing of the first with the fourth field and the second with the third has positive pairing parity, but ⟨Tψˉ​i​(x)ψj​(y)⟩=−Fji​(y−x). Multiplying it by (ig)2=−g2 leaves a positive result:
Gc(2)​(xa​,ya​)=g2∫d4xd4yD(xa​−x)D(ya​−y)tr[F(x−y)F(y−x)].
(38)
A contraction of the two external fields with each other times a vacuum bubble cancels against the normalization. Separate fermion tadpole integrals at x and y belong to a product of one-point functions, removed by taking the connected part.
To match the source's generating functional convention, distinguish its displayed kernels from contractions. The positive iJΔJ/2 exponent gives D=−iΔ. With the source insertion order in equation (5), the positive iηˉ​SF​η exponent similarly gives F=−iSF​. The two scalar propagators and two Dirac propagators supply a total phase (−i)4=1. Thus, writing S=SF​ as in the requested formula,
Gc(2)​(xa​,ya​)=g2∫d4xd4yΔ(xa​−x)Δ(ya​−y)tr[S(x−y)S(y−x)].​
(39)
The matrix trace closes the spinor indices; no extra factor of two or additional symmetry factor remains for a Dirac field with this Yukawa interaction.

For commuting real scalar fields, remove the closed fermion loop sign and replace the Dirac propagators by scalar propagators. There are no spinor indices, hence no matrix trace. With two independent real scalar fields χ1​,χ2​ and interaction gϕχ1​χ2​, the same normalization gives
Gc,χ1​χ2​(2)​=−g2∫d4xd4yD(xa​−x)D(ya​−y)G1​(x−y)G2​(y−x).​
(40)
The overall sign has reversed relative to part (i) because (ig)2 is now unaccompanied by a closed fermion loop sign. The same formula holds in the source's kernel convention, since there are again four propagators.
There is an additional convention to specify if the two symbols are intended to denote the same real scalar field. For the literal interaction gϕχ2, there are two identical connected Wick contractions between χ2(x) and χ2(y), so the coefficient becomes −2g2. If the conventional interaction is instead (g/2)ϕχ2, each vertex is ig and the loop has symmetry factor 1/2, giving coefficient −g2/2. In both interpretations the closed fermion loop sign and spinor matrix trace disappear; the differing numerical factor comes from the interaction normalization and field identity.
