a OurBigBook.com
Gauge invariance and power counting permit the same local operators as in the original renormalizable action. With renormalized fields, a convenient counterterm parametrization is
Lct​=−δZϕ​(Dμ​ϕ)†Dμϕ−δM2ϕ†ϕ−4μϵδλ​(ϕ†ϕ)2−4δZA​​Fμν​Fμν.​
(1)
Here Dμ​=∂μ​−iμϵ/2eAμ​. Expanding the first term supplies kinetic, scalar-scalar-photon, and seagull counterterms with the relative coefficients required by the Ward identity. Equivalently introduce separate vertex constants first and then impose that identity. For ϕ0​=Zϕ1/2​ϕ, A0​=ZA1/2​A, and e0​=μϵ/2Ze​e, it gives Ze​ZA1/2​=1. Thus charge renormalization can be represented through the photon field factor, rather than adding an independent gauge-noninvariant interaction.
The mass coefficient defined here is δM2=Zϕ​m02​−m2; it must be distinguished from m02​−m2. Gauge fixing may require the corresponding gauge-parameter redefinition, and vacuum diagrams can require an additive vacuum-energy counterterm. No photon mass counterterm is allowed by the unbroken gauge symmetry. The quartic scalar counterterm remains necessary for the full theory even at a scale where the renormalized λ is zero, because gauge interactions generate scalar four-point divergences.

b OurBigBook.com
The one-particle-irreducible scalar two-point function has three one-loop topologies: a scalar-photon exchange loop built from two cubic vertices; a photon tadpole at one seagull vertex; and a scalar tadpole at one quartic vertex. The last is absent when λ=0. Reducible chains of self-energy insertions belong to the Dyson expansion and are not additional one-loop self-energy graphs. The scalar-QED photon-tadpole symmetry factor is 1/2 because its two identical photon legs are joined; the oriented complex-scalar tadpole has no such identical-real-line factor.

Route the photon momentum as l and the internal scalar momentum as l+k. The transverse projector obeys Pαβ​lβ=0, so the two cubic vertices give
(2k+l)αPαβ​(l)(2k+l)β=4kαPαβ​(l)kβ.
(2)
Multiplying the two vertices and propagators therefore gives
iΠex​(k)=4e2μϵ∫(2π)dddl​l2[(l+k)2+m2]k2−(k⋅l)2/l2​.​
(3)
For the seagull, gαβPαβ​=d−1. Including the photon-loop symmetry factor gives instead
iΠsg​=21​(−2ie2)μϵ∫(2π)dddl​l2+mγ2​−i(d−1)​=−(d−1)e2μϵ∫(2π)dddl​l2+mγ2​1​.​
(4)
The printed seagull coefficient is twice this result. The vertex's factor two differentiates the two photon fields; closing them into one line requires the compensating symmetry factor. Direct expansion of the action gives the same check: the interaction density −e2A2ϕ†ϕ has one photon contraction, yielding coefficient −e2, not −2e2. The identical-photon origin of the vertex factor is also explicit in the standard scalar-electrodynamics rules.[ref] This discrepancy does not affect the massless-photon limit in part d or the ultraviolet pole in part f.

Apply the dimensionally regulated tadpole integral before removing the infrared regulator:
Iγ​(mγ​)=μϵ∫(2π)dddl​l2+mγ2​1​=(4π)2−ϵ/2iμϵ​Γ(−1+2ϵ​)(mγ2​)1−ϵ/2.
(5)
At fixed regulator 0<Reϵ<2 away from gamma-function poles, the power of mγ2​ has positive real part. Consequently
mγ​→0lim​Iγ​(mγ​)=0.​
(6)
Both the correctly weighted tadpole and the source's doubled version vanish. This is the near-four-dimensional limit appropriate to the question; the order of limits is important. Equivalently the exactly massless tadpole is a scaleless integral and is zero in dimensional regularization. The statement is not valid for every arbitrary fixed dimension: for example, ϵ=5/2 gives a nonzero prefactor and growth as mγ−1/2​. One must keep the regulator in its stated near-four-dimensional domain.

First remove the projector denominator explicitly:
iΠex​=4e2μϵ∫(2π)dddl​(l2)2[(l+k)2+m2]l2k2−(l⋅k)2​.
(7)
Let A=(l+k)2+m2 and B=l2. The Feynman parameter for a simple and a squared propagator follows by differentiating the elementary two-denominator Feynman formula with respect to B gives
AB21​=2∫01​dx[xA+(1−x)B]31−x​.​
(8)
Shift q=l+xk. Then xA+(1−x)B=q2+D, with D=x(1−x)k2+xm2. The numerator is unchanged by this parallel shift:
l2k2−(l⋅k)2=q2k2−(q⋅k)2.
(9)
Therefore the correctly combined integral is
iΠex​=8e2μϵ∫01​(1−x)dx∫(2π)dddq​(q2+D)3q2k2−(q⋅k)2​.​
(10)
The printed weight x/2 is not compatible with this denominator and prefactor. Exchanging x with 1−x changes the mass term in D as well and cannot supply the missing normalization. A concrete counterexample is m=0 with nonzero spacelike external momentum and 2<d<4: after Wick rotation, the remaining integral is nonzero and symmetric under x↔1−x. The source's 4e2∫(x/2)dx expression is then exactly one quarter of the correct expression. Thus the claimed equality cannot be imposed when computing the pole.

Lorentz-covariant tensor integration replaces qμ​qν​ by gμν​q2/d. The numerator consequently contributes (d−1)k2q2/d. The supplied master integral with powers a=1,b=3 gives
∫(2π)dddq​(q2+D)3q2​=(4π)d/2i​4d​Γ(ϵ/2)D−ϵ/2.
(11)
Inserting the corrected parameter integral gives
iΠex​=(4π)d/22i(d−1)e2μϵk2​Γ(ϵ/2)∫01​(1−x)D−ϵ/2dx.
(12)
The pole uses Γ(ϵ/2)=2/ϵ+O(1), D−ϵ/2=1+O(ϵ) and ∫01​(1−x)dx=1/2. Thus
iΠloop,div​=i8π2ϵ3e2​k2.​
(13)
There is no momentum-independent pole at λ=0 in this transverse gauge after removing the photon tadpole. The two-point counterterm insertion is −i(δZϕ​k2+δM2), so minimal subtraction requires
δZϕ​=8π2ϵ3e2​,δM2=0(d=4−ϵ).​
(14)
These coefficients cancel the actual one-loop insertion derived from the action. Applying the misprinted weight in part e would give a wave-function pole four times too small. In a d=4−2ε convention the same answer is δZϕ​=3e2/(16π2ε). Finally, δM2=0 does not mean that the bare mass parameter is unchanged: Zϕ​m02​=m2 gives m02​−m2=−m2δZϕ​+O(e4). Photon, charge and quartic counterterms require their respective Green functions and cannot be determined from this scalar two-point function alone.

ϕ ⟷ i1​δJδ​,ψˉ​ ⟷ iδςδ​,ψ ⟷ i1​δςˉ​δ​.​
(15)
The sign for ψˉ​ follows because a left derivative with respect to ς must cross ψˉ​. Keep the order ψˉ​γ5​ψϕ when replacing these fields. Expand eiSint​ in the original path integral; each field monomial can now be generated by these ordered derivatives acting on the free functional. Resumming gives
Z[J,ςˉ​,ς]=Nexp{i∫d4xLint​(i1​δJδ​, iδςδ​, i1​δςˉ​δ​)}Z0​[J,ςˉ​,ς].​

The arguments refer respectively to ϕ,ψˉ​,ψ at x. The free functional arises by performing the free Gaussian field integrals, and N may be chosen so that Z[0]=1.
No additional field-integration measure remains in this differential-operator formula. Such a measure is printed outside the operator in the source even though Z0​ has already integrated out those fields. At first order the Dyson expansion inserts iSint​=λ∫d4xψˉ​α​(x)(γ5​)αβ​ψβ​(x)ϕ(x). Contract the scalar field at the vertex with the external scalar. The four remaining fermion fields have two admissible pairings: both external fermions attach to the vertex, or the two vertex fermions contract with each other while the external pair contracts freely. Wick's theorem gives
⟨Tϕ(y)ψρ​(z)ψˉ​σ​(w)⟩=​λ(γ5​)αβ​∫d4xiΔF​(y−x)[iSF​(x−w)βσ​iSF​(z−x)ρα​−iSF​(0)βα​iSF​(z−w)ρσ​]+O(λ3).​​
(17)
The minus sign in the second term is the fermion sign of the closed contraction: ⟨Tψˉ​α​(x)ψβ​(x)⟩=−iSF​(0)βα​. The first term is connected; the second is a scalar-attached fermion tadpole multiplied by a disconnected free external fermion propagator. The full time-ordered expectation includes both topologies, whereas the connected generating functional retains only the first. There is no order-λ2 contribution in this pure Yukawa expansion because the total number of scalar fields would be odd.
For a parity-preserving free propagator, the tadpole coefficient is tr[γ5​SF​(0)], whose integrand contains only trγ5​ and tr(γ5​γμ). Both vanish. Thus the second term is a legitimate formal Wick contraction but is zero with a compatible regulator; normal ordering the interaction removes it from the outset.

i OurBigBook.com
The different-flavour quark annihilation into a massive pair has only an s-channel gluon graph at tree level when the two quark flavours differ. Its amplitude contains one incoming and one outgoing colour current, with common magnitude factor gs2​/s. On-shell current conservation removes the gauge-parameter-dependent longitudinal part of the gluon propagator. To display the Dirac signs transparently, evaluate spin traces with the auxiliary mostly-minus metric η=−g and {γμ,γν}=2ημν; the invariants s,t are then the usual positive-energy Mandelstam invariants, numerically identical to those defined in the paper.
The incoming colour average and outgoing colour sum give
Ccol​=321​∑a,b​tr(TaTb)tr(TaTb)=91​41​δabδab=92​.
(18)
Averaging over the two spins of each incident particle supplies an additional 1/4. The physical spin sums are ∑uuˉ=p+M and ∑vvˉ=p−M. Therefore
∣T∣2​=18s2gs4​​tr[γμ​(p2​−M)γα​(p1​+M)]tr[γμk1​γαk2​].​
(19)
The coefficient is equivalently 8π2αs2​/(9s2), with αs​=gs2​/(4π).
The two top projectors cannot both contain −M. The printed expression has that error. It is not repaired by changing the metric convention consistently: a particle and antiparticle still require the appropriate distinct projectors. In the auxiliary convention, its outgoing tensor would obey qμ​Hprintedμα​=8M2qα, rather than zero for q=p1​+p2​. At threshold it gives a zero contracted trace, while the correctly averaged vector-current production amplitude squared has a nonzero limit 4gs4​/9. The phase-space cross section still vanishes at threshold because the final velocity tends to zero. The colour and spin prefactor above is consequently the prefactor for the corrected trace; no overall constant can make the misprinted massive trace the physical answer for all kinematics.

The gauge-invariant functional integral counts every gauge orbit repeatedly. Insert a gauge-orbit identity
1=ΔFP​[A,ϕ]∫Dθδ(G[Aθ,ϕθ]−f),ΔFP​=det(δθ(y)δG(x)​).
(24)
Dividing by the gauge-group volume and averaging f with a Gaussian weight produces the term −G2/(2ξ). The determinant is represented by a pair of independent anticommuting scalar fields c,cˉ, giving the prescribed ghost density −cˉMc, where M=δG/δθ. The Grassmann integral gives a determinant rather than the inverse determinant of a commuting Gaussian integral; field-independent constants and overall determinant signs are absorbed in normalization.
Although the gauge group is Abelian, the determinant here is field dependent because the gauge condition contains the scalar fluctuation b. Thus Abelian ghosts need not decouple in a broken-phase Rξ​ gauge. The fields implement gauge-orbit counting and are not physical external particles.

For the ghost coupling in a linear Cartesian Abelian Higgs gauge, the common factor in the scalar parametrization cancels from the gauge transformation. Separating real and imaginary parts gives
δh=gθb,δb=−gθ(v+h),δ(∂⋅A)=−□θ.
(25)
Consequently
M=−□+ξg2v(v+h),Lgh​=cˉ□c−ξg2v2cˉc−ξg2vhcˉc.​
(26)
For the variables and gauge function actually printed, the ghost mass and Higgs-ghost vertex are therefore
mc2​=ξg2v2,Vhcˉc​=−iξg2v.​
(27)
For the usual nonnegative gauge parameter, mc​=ξ​g∣v∣. The momentum-space ghost propagator is −i/(k2+mc2​−i0) in this kinetic convention. There is no direct ghost-photon vertex or ghost-b vertex. At ξ=0 the ghost becomes massless and decouples.
The scalar prefactor 1/4 is incompatible with the stated canonical broken-vacuum interpretation. At h=b=0 it gives ∣ϕ∣2=v2/16, whereas the potential is minimized at ∣ϕ∣2=v2/2. In fact the printed parametrization gives
∂h∂V​​h=b=0​=−2567λv3​=0.
(28)
Its h,b kinetic terms have coefficient −1/16, not −1/2, and its vector mass parameter is g2v2/8. The given gauge function consequently does not cancel the vector-scalar mixing in those literal variables. The ghost operator computed above nevertheless follows algebraically from that gauge function without changing the printed fields.
With a canonical Abelian Higgs shift ϕ=(v+h+ib)/2​, the expansion is about a true minimum, the vector mass is mA​=g∣v∣, the gauge fixing cancels the mixing, and the same ghost formulas acquire the usual interpretation mc2​=ξmA2​. For the literal 1/4 parametrization, a canonical fluctuation is hcan​=h/8​, so its ghost vertex would instead be −i8​ξg2v; the non-vacuum background problem still remains. These two interpretations must not be silently mixed.

Gauge invariance of the scalar potential implies the infinitesimal identity
∂ϕi​∂V​(Ta)ij​ϕj​=0.
(29)
The irrelevant common factor i from the real representation's infinitesimal rotation has been divided out. Differentiate with respect to ϕk​ and evaluate at the stationary vacuum v:
∂ϕk​∂ϕi​∂2V​​v​(Ta)ij​vj​+∂ϕi​∂V​​v​(Ta)ik​=0.
(30)
The second term vanishes by stationarity, leaving
(Mϕ2​)ki​(Tav)i​=0.​
(31)
Whenever Tav=0, this is a nonzero vector in the kernel of the scalar mass-squared matrix, so it is a zero-eigenvalue direction. It is tangent to the degenerate vacuum orbit. In a global theory it is a Goldstone direction; in the gauged theory the corresponding would-be Goldstone field supplies a longitudinal polarization of a massive vector.
For the specific potential, stationarity at a nonzero vacuum gives v2=m2/(4λ), assuming m2>0 and λ>0. Its Hessian is
(Mϕ2​)ij​=(−m2+4λv2)δij​+8λvi​vj​=8λvi​vj​.
(32)
The antisymmetry of the triplet generators gives vi​(Tav)i​=0, verifying the claimed kernel directly.

Since the hypercharge is zero, electric neutrality means T3v=0. Choose the real-triplet vacuum vi​=vδi3​, with v2=m2/(4λ). With canonical real-scalar kinetic density −21​(Dμ​Φ)TDμΦ, insert this vacuum into the covariant derivative. The hypercharge field Bμ​ has no coupling to this scalar, while only T1v,T2v are nonzero. Thus
MV2​=g2v2diag(1,1,0,0)in (W1,W2,W3,B).​
(33)
The charged combinations W±=(W1∓iW2)/2​ have
mW±2​=g2v2=4λg2m2​,mW3​=mB​=0.​
(34)
Both T3 and the independent hypercharge generator annihilate the vacuum. The connected unbroken subgroup is therefore
U(1)T3​×U(1)Y​.​
(35)
The electromagnetic generator Q=T3+Y/2 belongs to it, but it is only one linear combination of two surviving neutral generators. This model has two massless neutral gauge fields, rather than the single massless photon and massive neutral vector of the usual scalar-doublet electroweak model. A neutral-field rotation does not create a massive eigenvalue from this zero block.
The two transverse triplet components are eaten, leaving one real, electrically neutral radial scalar H=ϕ3​−v. The radial eigenvalue of the Hessian found in part i is
mH2​=8λv2=2m2,mH​=2​m.​
(36)
Indeed the radial potential is a constant plus 4λv2H2+4λvH3+λH4, fixing the factor of two between its quadratic coefficient and its mass squared. The physical degree-of-freedom count is preserved: three real scalars plus four massless vectors give 3+4×2=11, while one scalar, two massive vectors and two massless vectors give 1+2×3+2×2=11.
