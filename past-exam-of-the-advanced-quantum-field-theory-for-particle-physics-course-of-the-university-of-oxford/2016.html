Choose the covariant derivative Dμ​=∂μ​+iμϵ/2eAμ​, so the interaction term is −μϵ/2eψˉ​γμAμ​ψ. The gauge transformation is Aμ​↦Aμ​+∂μ​α and ψ↦e−iμϵ/2eαψ. The derivative of this local phase cancels the added vector-potential term in Dμ​ψ, making iψˉ​γμDμ​ψ gauge-invariant.
For renormalized fields and parameters, a complete local QED counterterm parametrization is
L=Lct​=​iψˉ​∂ψ−mψˉ​ψ−41​Fμν​Fμν−μϵ/2eψˉ​γμAμ​ψ+Lct​,iδZ2​ψˉ​∂ψ−δmL​ψˉ​ψ−4δZ3​​Fμν​Fμν−μϵ/2eδZ1​ψˉ​γμAμ​ψ.​​
(1)
With ψ0​=Z2​​ψ, A0​=Z3​​A, the definitions are δZi​=Zi​−1, δmL​=Z2​m0​−m, and e0​=μϵ/2eZ1​/(Z2​Z3​​). In particular δmL​ is the coefficient in the Lagrangian density, not just m0​−m.
The Ward identity requires Z1​=Z2​ in a gauge-preserving renormalization scheme. The kinetic and vertex counterterms then combine into iδZ2​ψˉ​Dψ, so gauge invariance is explicit and e0​=μϵ/2e/Z3​​. A photon mass term is not an allowed counterterm in unbroken QED. For perturbative inversion one separately adds gauge fixing, Lgf​=−(∂μ​Aμ)2/(2ξ), taking ξ=1 in Feynman gauge. That quantization term is not itself part of the gauge-invariant density. The Abelian Faddeev-Popov determinant is field independent for this linear gauge and introduces no interacting ghost contribution.

At second order in the interaction term, attach the two external fermion fields to different vertices and contract the remaining two fermion fields with each other and the two vector fields with each other. The factor 1/2! in the expansion is cancelled by the two ways to assign the external ends. There is no closed fermion loop, and hence no extra closed-loop fermionic sign.
After removing the two external Dirac propagators, the internal momentum assignment l for the photon and p+l for the fermion gives the one-loop insertion
I(p)=(−ieμϵ/2)2∫(2π)dddl​γνS(p+l)γμΔμν​(l)=−e2μϵ∫(2π)dddl​γνS(p+l)γμΔμν​(l).​
(2)
Thus the insertion is proportional to exactly the propagator product specified in the source. Define I=−iΣ. With Fourier convention ψ(x)∼eip⋅x, the reduced free inverse kernel is K0​(p)=−p−m, since the printed Dirac propagator equals iK0−1​. Repeated insertions give
S=i(K0​−Σ)−1,Γ(2)=K0​−Σ+Kct​.
(3)
The factors of i and the sign of the named self-energy are convention dependent; the cancelling counterterm is fixed once this insertion convention is specified. The internal photon joins two vertices on one directed fermion line, as in the first panel of the figure in 1b.

Multiply the supplied Feynman gauge propagators before reducing the numerator. Their two factors −i and the source's Clifford algebra imply
γνS(p+l)γμΔμν​(l)=(l2−i0)[(p+l)2+m2−i0](d−2)(p+l)+dm​.
(4)
For example, contracting the numerator before the propagator factors gives γν[−(p+l)+m]γν​=−(d−2)(p+l)−dm; the extra minus from (−i)2 reverses both signs.
Use a Feynman parameter x on the massive denominator and shift q=l+xp. The combined denominator is
x[(p+l)2+m2]+(1−x)l2=q2+D(x),D(x)=xm2+x(1−x)p2.
(5)
The term odd in q integrates to zero in dimensional regularization. The pole can be evaluated away from on-shell infrared singularities; its local ultraviolet coefficient is independent of D. With the source's master integral,
IUV​(p)​=−8π2ϵie2​∫01​dx[2(1−x)p+4m]=−8π2ϵie2​(p+4m),ΣUV​(p)=8π2ϵe2​(p+4m).​
(6)
Terms proportional to d−4 multiply the pole into a finite term, so they do not change this result. The counterterm kernel from 1ai is Kct​=−δZ2​p−δmL​. Cancelling −ΣUV​+Kct​ therefore gives
δZ2​=−8π2ϵe2​,δmL​=−2π2ϵe2m​.​
(7)
The distinction between mass definitions matters. Since Z2​m0​=m+δmL​,
m0​−m=δmL​−mδZ2​=−8π2ϵ3e2m​,Zm​−1=−8π2ϵ3e2​(m0​=Zm​m).​
(8)
Equivalently the mass-dependent part of the counterterm density is +e2mψˉ​ψ/(2π2ϵ), because its definition includes the explicit minus sign in −δmL​ψˉ​ψ. In the alternative convention d=4−2ε, replace every 1/ϵ here by 1/(2ε). These are ultraviolet minimal-subtraction poles, not the finite on-shell mass correction. The Ward identity also fixes δZ1​=δZ2​; the photon coefficient δZ3​ requires its own two-point function and is not obtained from this graph.

In pure QCD, different incoming and outgoing quark flavours allow only the tree-level s-channel gluon graph. A quark-gluon vertex preserves flavour, so no flavour-changing t-channel exchange is available. Label the incoming momenta p1​,p2​ and outgoing momenta k1​,k2​. For transparent spin traces, use the auxiliary metric η=−g and its usual gamma matrices; the positive-energy Mandelstam variables are
s=(p1​+p2​)η2​,t=(p1​−k1​)η2​,u=(p1​−k2​)η2​,s+t+u=0.
(9)
This is a consistent change of metric notation, not a change of the physical invariants. Define the fixed-index colour tensor Cji;kl​=∑a​(Ta)ji​(Ta)kl​. Up to an irrelevant common phase,
M=sgs2​​Cji;kl​[vˉ(p2​)γμu(p1​)][uˉ(k1​)γμ​v(k2​)].
(10)
The indices i,j label incoming colours and k,l outgoing colours. Averaging over the two spins of each incoming particle and summing the outgoing spins gives
∣M∣2​spin​=4s2gs4​∣Cji;kl​∣2​tr(p2​γμp1​γν)tr(k1​γμ​k2​γν​).
(11)
The gamma-matrix trace identity makes the contraction
32[(p1​⋅k1​)(p2​⋅k2​)+(p1​⋅k2​)(p2​⋅k1​)]=8(t2+u2).
(12)
For instance p1​⋅k1​=p2​⋅k2​=−t/2 and the crossed products are −u/2. Therefore, before the remaining colour sum and average,
∣M∣2​spin​=2gs4​∣Cji;kl​∣2s2t2+u2​.​
(13)
Here the bar averages spins only; it does not silently include the colour average requested next.

Use the fundamental SU(3) normalization tr(TaTb)=δab/2, with Hermitian generators. The average over the 3×3 incoming colours and sum over the outgoing colours gives
Ccol​​=Nc2​1​i,j,k,l∑​​a∑​(Ta)ji​(Ta)kl​​2=Nc2​1​a,b∑​tr(TaTb)tr(TaTb)=4Nc2​Nc2​−1​=92​(Nc​=3).​
(14)
The complete spin and colour averaged result is consequently
∣M∣2​=94gs4​​s2t2+u2​=964π2αs2​​s2t2+u2​,αs​=4πgs2​​.​
(15)
For massless two-body centre-of-momentum kinematics, t=−s(1−cosθ)/2 and u=−s(1+cosθ)/2, so the same answer is 2gs4​(1+cos2θ)/9. This checks positivity, the symmetry under exchanging the outgoing directions, and the absence of a forward t-channel singularity in this particular process.

Work entirely with left-handed Weyl fermions; a right-handed field is counted through its left-handed charge conjugate. For a simple non-Abelian group G, define
trR​(TaTb)=T(R)δab,DRabc​=trR​[Ta{Tb,Tc}].
(16)
The two orientations of a chiral triangle supply the symmetrized trace. Spectator representation dimensions multiply each trace. The perturbative gauge anomaly conditions for G×U(1) are therefore
i∑​DRi​abc​=0for every a,b,c,i∑​Qi​T(Ri​)=0,i∑​dim(Ri​)Qi3​=0.​
(17)
The first concerns G3, the second G2U(1), and the third U(1)3. A GU(1)2 triangle has group factor ∑i​Qi2​trRi​​Ta=0, because non-Abelian generators of a semisimple group are traceless. If coupling to gravity is required, also impose the mixed gauge-gravitational condition ∑i​dim(Ri​)Qi​=0. Cancellation of these local triangles is not a universal substitute for checking global anomalies; the Witten SU(2) anomaly is the relevant additional test here.[ref]
For the Standard Model, one generation consists of QL​:(3,2)1/6​, uc:(3ˉ,1)−2/3​, dc:(3ˉ,1)1/3​, LL​:(1,2)−1/2​ and ec:(1,1)1​. These are precisely the all-left-handed representations; conjugating the singlets reverses their hypercharges relative to right-handed particles. Check the requested triangles separately:
For 3–3–3, normalize the cubic coefficient of a fundamental to +1. Its conjugate has −1, so the weak-doublet multiplicity gives
A333​=2−1−1=0.
(18)
For 2–2–2, the doublet generators obey {Tb,Tc}=δbcI/2, so tr(Ta{Tb,Tc})=0. Thus every doublet separately has zero local cubic weak anomaly. For the global Witten SU(2) anomaly, each generation has three coloured quark doublets and one lepton doublet, an even total of four. All three generations give twelve, so the global obstruction also cancels.
For 3–3–1, use T(3)=T(3ˉ)=1/2. The two conjugate triplets have the same quadratic index, not its negative:
A331​=2(61​)21​+(−32​)21​+(31​)21​=0.
(19)
For 2–2–1, the three quark colours and one lepton doublet give
A221​=3(61​)21​+(−21​)21​=0.
(20)
For 1–1–1, count every coloured and weak component:
A111​=6(61​)3+3(−32​)3+3(31​)3+2(−21​)3+1=361−32+4−9+36​=0.
(21)
The mixed gauge-gravitational coefficient also vanishes:
6(61​)+3(−32​)+3(31​)+2(−21​)+1=0.
(22)
Unlisted gauge combinations contain a single generator of at least one simple factor. For example, 3–2–1 has tr3​Tatr2​tbY, and 3–3–2 has tr3​(TaTb)tr2​tc; both vanish. The same reasoning covers 3–1–1, 2–1–1 and 3–2–2, including permutations of the legs. Scalars contribute no chiral triangle anomaly. Therefore
All the listed gauge anomalies cancel within each generation; three generations remain anomaly free.​
(23)

For λ>0, the scalar potential is minimized at ϕ†ϕ=v2/2. With the specified upper-component vacuum expectation value ϕ0​=(v,0)T/2​ and hypercharge Y=−I/2, the unbroken generator is
Q=T3+Y=(00​0−1​),Qϕ0​=0.
(24)
The other three independent generator directions act nontrivially on ϕ0​. Thus the connected unbroken gauge subgroup is U(1)em​, while three gauge directions are broken. The normalization here is Q=T3+Y, not the alternative convention in which hypercharge is doubled.
Apply the covariant derivative directly to the vacuum:
Dμ​ϕ0​=−22​iv​(g2​Aμ3​−g1​Bμ​g2​(Aμ1​+iAμ2​)​).
(25)
In the mostly-plus convention the canonical scalar kinetic term is −(Dμ​ϕ)†Dμϕ. Its quadratic vector terms are
Lmass​=−8v2​[g22​(Aμ1​A1μ+Aμ2​A2μ)+(g2​Aμ3​−g1​Bμ​)(g2​A3μ−g1​Bμ)].
(26)
Define sW​=sinθW​=g1​/g12​+g22​​ and cW​=cosθW​=g2​/g12​+g22​​. Diagonalization gives
Wμ±​=2​Aμ1​∓iAμ2​​,Zμ​=cW​Aμ3​−sW​Bμ​,Aμγ​=sW​Aμ3​+cW​Bμ​.​
(27)
The neutral combination Z lies along the nonzero eigenvector (g2​,−g1​); the orthogonal combination (g1​,g2​) is the massless photon. Comparing the mass terms with −mW2​W+⋅W−−mZ2​Z2/2 yields
mW​=2g2​v​,mZ​=2v​g12​+g22​​,mγ​=0,mZ​mW​​=cosθW​.​
(28)
Finally insert the inverse neutral rotation into the coupling:
g2​T3Aμ3​+g1​YBμ​=(g2​sW​T3+g1​cW​Y)Aμγ​+(g2​cW​T3−g1​sW​Y)Zμ​.
(29)
Both coefficients in the photon term agree, giving
e=g2​sinθW​=g1​cosθW​=g12​+g22​​g1​g2​​,Dμ​⊃−ieQAμγ​.​
(30)
The upper vacuum component is electrically neutral and the lower scalar component has charge −e, consistently with the source's negative hypercharge. This doublet is the conjugate convention to the commonly displayed positive-hypercharge doublet; it does not change the vector masses or the electromagnetic coupling. The four real scalar components supply three would-be Goldstone modes and one physical radial Higgs mode, preserving the total physical degrees of freedom.

For M>0, integrate the vector action by parts, dropping the boundary term, and write it as a quadratic functional:
S0​[A]=21​∫d4xAμ​KμνAν​,Kμν=(□−M2)gμν−∂μ∂ν.
(31)
The explicit mass makes this Proca kernel invertible: the displayed action alone has no gauge redundancy left to fix. It is interpreted as unitary gauge when obtained from a Higgs or Stückelberg completion with its would-be Goldstone field removed.
Introduce a source Jμ​ and complete the square, A=A′−K−1J. The normalized Gaussian path integral is
Z[0]Z[J]​=exp[−2i​∫d4xd4yJμ​(x)(K−1)μν(x,y)Jν​(y)],Dμν​=i(K−1)μν​.​
(32)
The source-independent functional determinant cancels in Z[J]/Z[0]. For Fourier momentum k, the mixed-index kernel is Kμν​=−(k2+M2)δμν​+kμkν​. For k2=0, use the projectors
PL​μν​=k2kμkν​​,PT​=I−PL​.
(33)
Their orthogonality gives
K=−(k2+M2)PT​−M2PL​,K−1=−k2+M2PT​​−M2PL​​.
(34)
Combining the projectors removes their apparent massless denominator. Adding the Feynman prescription gives the unitary-gauge propagator
Dμν​(k)=k2+M2−i0−i​(gμν​+M2kμ​kν​​).​
(35)
Multiplication by K verifies that it inverts the quadratic operator. On the physical pole k2=−M2, the numerator is transverse to k, as required for the three physical massive-vector polarizations.
The drawback is its off-shell ultraviolet behaviour: at large spacelike momentum the transverse term falls as 1/k2, but the longitudinal tensor term remains of order 1/M2. Naive loop power counting therefore looks worse and renormalizability is not manifest. In a finite Rξ​ gauge the corresponding expression is
Dξ​=−i[k2+M2−i0PT​​+k2+ξM2−i0ξPL​​],
(36)
which falls as 1/k2 at fixed finite ξ. Its ξ→∞ limit gives the unitary-gauge longitudinal term, so taking that limit before the ultraviolet limit changes power counting.
This is not a claim that a renormalizable Higgs theory ceases to be physically renormalizable in this gauge. Nor does it mean that the free Proca Gaussian integral is inconsistent. For conserved external sources k⋅J=0, the longitudinal numerator drops out of JμDμν​Jν and the tree exchange again falls as 1/k2. The difficulty concerns off-shell Green functions and manifest control of loop divergences, not the number of physical polarizations.
