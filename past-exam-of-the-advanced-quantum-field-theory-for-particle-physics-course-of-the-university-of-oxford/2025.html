The action is dimensionless, so in four dimensions the Lagrangian density has mass dimension four. Its kinetic term fixes [ϕ]=1 and [∂μ​]=1. An interaction gD​OD​ with [OD​]=D has [gD​]=4−D. Therefore
D≤4 is renormalizable by power counting;D>4 has a negative-dimensional coupling.​
(4)
In an effective field theory with heavy scale Λ, a higher-dimensional local operator enters as cD​OD​/ΛD−4. For characteristic scales E,m≪Λ and coefficients compatible with this expansion, its effects are suppressed by powers of the small ratios. Omitting these terms defines the leading approximation and its truncation error in effective field theory. It is not a fundamental prohibition: such terms must be retained if that accuracy is required. A continuum renormalizable quantum field theory can instead be defined by the closed set of counterterms of dimension at most four.
Up to total derivatives, all Lorentz scalar terms of dimension at most four built from one real scalar field are
1,ϕ,ϕ2,ϕ3,ϕ4,∂μ​ϕ∂μϕ.
(5)
The term ϕ□ϕ is equivalent to the kinetic term by integration by parts; □ϕ and □(ϕ2) contribute only at the boundary. The displayed Lagrangian density omits the linear and cubic interactions and a field-independent constant. The exact Z2 symmetry ϕ↦−ϕ forbids both odd interactions, and radiative corrections preserving this symmetry cannot generate them. The constant shifts the vacuum energy and the normalization of Z and can be dropped when gravity and absolute vacuum energy are not being studied. A constant vacuum counterterm may still be used when renormalizing unnormalized vacuum diagrams. The kinetic coefficient has already been fixed by field normalization.

For dimension-six operators of a real scalar field, let n count fields and r count partial derivatives. Mass dimension gives n+r=6. Lorentz invariance requires even r. Discard derivatives acting on the entire expression, but do not yet identify different expressions by integration by parts. A complete list of seven representatives is
O1​O2​O4​O6​​=ϕ6,=ϕ2∂μ​ϕ∂μϕ,=ϕ□2ϕ,=(□ϕ)2,​O3​O5​O7​​=ϕ3□ϕ,=∂μ​ϕ∂μ□ϕ,=(∂μ​∂ν​ϕ)(∂μ∂νϕ).​​
(6)
For (n,r)=(6,0) there is one choice; for (4,2) the derivative distribution is either 2 or 1+1; for (2,4) it is 4, 3+1, or 2+2, the last allowing two distinct metric contractions. Commuting partial derivatives identifies alternative orderings. Contractions with a Levi-Civita symbol vanish because an antisymmetric index pair necessarily contracts symmetric derivatives. The remaining case (0,6) gives no field-dependent expression. No independent odd-r Lorentz scalar exists.
The source's seven means this unreduced list. If “ignoring total derivative operators” were interpreted as already taking the full quotient by total derivatives, seven would be incorrect: the next part gives three representatives of that quotient. Dimensionful factors multiplying lower-dimensional terms do not count as new canonical-dimension-six field monomials.

In d=4−2ϵ, the modified minimal subtraction scheme subtracts ΔMS​=1/ϵ−γE​+log(4π) for each one-loop pole, with no further finite, kinematics-dependent subtraction. Equivalently, use a rescaled renormalization scale μ​2=4πe−γE​μR2​ and subtract only 1/ϵ in that convention. This differs from the minimal subtraction scheme by a fixed scale convention.
Let C=e2ΔMS​/(16π2). With the source's counterterm insertion signs, cancellation proceeds as follows. The vertex has −ieγμC from the loop and −ieγμδ1​ from its counterterm, giving δ1​=−C. The kinetic part of the fermion self-energy insertion is +ipC and its counterterm is +ipδ2​, giving δ2​=−C. Equivalently D(ξ)=−iPT/(k2−mA2​+i0)−iξPL/(k2−ξmA2​+i0). At fixed finite ξ, both sectors fall as k−2 at large momentum in Euclidean space; ξ=1 gives −igμν​/(k2−mA2​+i0), and taking ξ→∞ at fixed k recovers unitary gauge. The latter limit is not uniform in ultraviolet momentum.
The expected power counting conclusion is that this k−2 behaviour, together with polynomial interactions of mass dimension at most four in Cartesian scalar variables and the gauge symmetry identities, permits renormalization of spontaneously broken gauge theories using a finite set of physical parameters. The propagator alone is not sufficient to establish that claim in the polar variables of this question. In fact, part b contains h(∂π)2/f and h2(∂π)2/(2f2), of dimensions five and six, and the nonlinear gauge fixing term contains −ξe2h4π2/(2f2), also of dimension six. Ordinary power counting is consequently not manifest in these variables.
For a representation where it is manifest, write ϕ=(f+H+iχ)/2​ and choose the usual polynomial R-xi gauge function ∂⋅A−ξefχ. It yields the same free vector propagator, cancels the quadratic mixing, and leaves only dimension-at-most-four interactions, including its Faddeev-Popov ghosts. This supplies the missing vertex condition in the source's proposed inference; the equivalence theorem for field redefinitions relates physical predictions near the vacuum. Good propagator falloff is the necessary ingredient illustrated by the requested formula, rather than a complete proof for arbitrary parametrizations or nonlinear choices of gauge fixing.
