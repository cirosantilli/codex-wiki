Nonintegrability of a radial triangular Fourier cutoff in three dimensions (F−1[(1−∣ξ∣)+​]∈/L1(R3))

 Home Mathematics Area of mathematics Analysis Fourier transform Fourier multiplier
OurBigBook.com Words: 69
The inverse Fourier transform of (1−∣ξ∣)+​ in three dimensions, with a=2π∣x∣, is −4πsina/a3+8π(1−cosa)/a4. Its leading oscillatory ∣x∣−3 tail is not absolutely integrable against the radial volume element 4π∣x∣2d∣x∣. Multiplying by a Gaussian Fourier transform retains a nonzero boundary-derivative tail, so the radial cutoff need not map even Schwartz functions into L1. A tensor product of one-dimensional triangular cutoffs is a valid approximate identity, and must not be confused with this Euclidean radial cutoff.
