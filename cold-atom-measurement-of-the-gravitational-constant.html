Cold-atom measurement of the gravitational constant

 ... Physics Branches of physics Classical mechanics Newton's law of universal gravitation Newtonian gravitational field Gravitational constant
OurBigBook.com Words: 276
In 2014, Gabriele Rosi and colleagues used atom interferometry: two rubidium-87 clouds prepared by laser cooling in vacuum, 328mm apart, received shared three-pulse Raman transitions spaced by 160ms. Fluorescence readout and paired-fringe ellipse fitting yielded differential wave phase. Twenty-four tungsten alloy cylinders, about 516kg total, moved between C and F. Cloud subtraction rejected shared vibration; configuration subtraction isolated the source change.[ref]
Calibration used timed flight and light-sheet crossings; mirror tilt compensated Earth rotation. Wave vector reversal rejected unwanted shifts. Modeling included source Newtonian gravitational potential, atom trajectories, cloud distributions and moving supports. Source positioning, mass density variation and cloud geometry limited the reported combined relative standard uncertainty to 150 parts per million.[ref]
To understand the subtraction without reproducing that full analysis, consider an ideal atom interferometer in uniform vertical acceleration during each pulse sequence. Let keff​ be its signed effective vertical wave vector component and T the pulse spacing. Its acceleration-dependent wave phase is ϕ≃keff​gT2; labels U and L denote upper and lower clouds. The double difference is then
S=(ϕU​−ϕL​)C​−(ϕU​−ϕL​)F​≃keff​T2[(gU​−gL​)C​−(gU​−gL​)F​].
(29)
A background field identical in C and F cancels even if it differs between the two clouds. In a fixed-geometry, linear-response model, write S=GK, where K is calculated from the source mass density, calibrated pulse sequence and atom trajectories. Thus Ginferred​=S/K. Measuring wave phase accurately is one part of the task; calculating K accurately is another. The real nonuniform field and finite clouds require the trajectory-dependent analysis described above rather than this uniform-field approximation.
