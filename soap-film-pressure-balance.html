Soap film pressure balance (Δp=−2τH)

 ... Mathematics Area of mathematics Geometry and topology Differential geometry Second fundamental form Mean curvature
OurBigBook.com Words: 588 Articles: 1
For an ideal soap film with equal constant surface tension γ on both faces, the effective tension is τ=2γ. Choose a unit normal N from the minus side to the plus side, and Δp=p−​−p+​. Translating the Young–Laplace equation to S=−dN gives
Δp=−τ(k1​+k2​)=−2τH.
(36)
A single interface uses τ=γ. An outward-oriented spherical soap bubble has H=−1/R, giving Δp=4γ/R. The positive-convex curvature sum in the Young–Laplace equation article is −2H. [ref]
An open film with the same resting gas on both sides has zero pressure jump. Neglecting film weight, soap film pressure balance gives H=0: a minimal surface. A pressurized bubble instead has nonzero mean curvature. These statements assume equilibrium, constant surface tension, and negligible normal viscous stress. Surfactant gradients or motion require the fuller normal stress balance. [ref]
The variational outline is short. With fixed boundary, a normal displacement fN gives δA=−2∫M​HfdA by the first variation of area formula. For a closed interface oriented outward, δV=∫M​fdA, so stationarity of τA−ΔpV gives −2τH−Δp=0. This is a first variation calculation; testing for a stable minimal surface needs the second variation, and deciding a global minimum needs a comparison of competitors.
