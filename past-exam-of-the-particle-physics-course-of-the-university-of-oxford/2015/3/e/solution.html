The four allowed two-real-photon states are ηc​, ηc​(2S), χc0​ and χc2​. Two photons have C=+1, excluding J/ψ, ψ(2S) and hc​; the Landau-Yang theorem excludes χc1​ because J=1. Both scalar and pseudoscalar two-photon amplitudes exist, so 0++ and 0−+ are allowed. The J/psi meson was discovered first because its 1−− assignment permits direct, resonantly enhanced single-photon formation in e+e− and a clean dilepton peak in hadron production. The lighter ηc​ cannot be formed by one photon; it needs less favorable production channels and has a broader two-gluon decay.
A spin-zero ηc​ at rest has no preferred axis, so dΓ/dΩp​ is isotropic after summing final spins. A J/psi meson formed in unpolarized high-energy e+e− has transverse m=±1 alignment. Its ppˉ​ distribution can be written
dcosθp​dΓ​∝a(1+cos2θp​)+bsin2θp​,a,b≥0,

where a,b contain the two independent helicity amplitudes. It is generally anisotropic; the approximate massless, helicity-conserving limit has b→0. The tabulated branching fraction does not fix a/b. An ensemble equally populated in all three spin projections would instead be isotropic: alignment of the production ensemble matters.
