The Fermilab primary proton beam strikes a target, producing secondary pions and kaons. Horn focusing selects positive parents; their decays π+→μ+νμ​ and K+→μ+νμ​ produce a predominantly muon neutrino beam. A decay tunnel permits decay before the remaining charged secondaries encounter an absorber. A near detector constrains the flux and interaction spectrum, while the distant MINOS detector measures its change.
The alternating scintillation counters and magnetized iron give a sampling calorimeter and a magnetic tracking volume. A charged current νμ​N→μ−X event has a long negative muon track and a localized hadronic shower. Estimate Eμ​ by range if contained or magnetic curvature if exiting, and EX​ by the calibrated scintillator pulse-height sum. Reconstruct Eν​≃Eμ​+EX​, with corrections for nuclear binding, invisible energy and detector response. The muon charge separates most wrong-sign muon antineutrino events; short showers without the track are primarily neutral current candidates.
