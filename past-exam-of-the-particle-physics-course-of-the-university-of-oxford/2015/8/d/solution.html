The CKM matrix appears in the charged current of b. For a tree semileptonic transition, a schematic extraction is Γ=GF2​∣Vij​∣2×phase space×hadronic factor×(1+corrections). Measure an absolute rate or differential spectrum, calculate or independently determine the hadronic factor, and solve for the modulus. The process is not a direct count of quark flavours in a detector: form factors, decay constants, nuclear structure and radiation can limit the result.
  • ∣Vud​∣: superallowed nuclear 0+→0+ beta decay, with neutron and pion beta decay cross-checks; correct for nuclear and radiative effects.
  • ∣Vus​∣: K→πℓν spectra with the Kπ form factor; compare K→μν and π→μν using decay constants.
  • ∣Vub​∣ and ∣Vcb​∣: exclusive B→πℓν and B→D(∗)ℓν, or inclusive charmless/charmed semileptonic spectra; separate the rare charmless component and control the hadronic calculation.
  • ∣Vcd​∣ and ∣Vcs​∣: charm semileptonic or leptonic decays with form factors or decay constants, and charm production by neutrinos; charm-tagged W boson decays give a complementary constraint.
  • ∣Vtb​∣: single-top quark production and the ratio of t→Wb to all t→Wq decays, under stated assumptions about allowed interactions.
  • ∣Vtd​∣ and ∣Vts​∣: neutral B-meson mixing frequencies and rare loop-induced decays, with loop dynamics and hadronic inputs. Mixing-frequency ratios cancel some common uncertainties.
Rate moduli alone do not determine the complex phase. CP-asymmetric decay distributions and interference in neutral-meson systems, or tree-level B→DK interference, constrain the phase and the CKM unitarity triangle. Unitarity supplies consistency checks and indirect determinations, but a fit that imposes it must be distinguished from a direct measurement of an element.
