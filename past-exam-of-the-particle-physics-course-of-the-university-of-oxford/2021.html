In a relativistic two-body decay, four-momentum conservation in the parent's rest frame requires p1∗​=−p2∗​ and
M=p∗2+m12​​+p∗2+m22​​.
(1)
For fixed daughter masses, this fixes a unique p∗, independent of direction. In the Lorentz-invariant phase space integral, the Dirac delta distribution enforcing conservation of energy therefore performs the radial momentum integration. Only the solid angle remains to be integrated; the resulting p∗ can be taken outside that integral, even if the matrix element depends strongly on direction.
For three or more final particles, four-momentum conservation generally leaves continuously variable daughter energies and momenta, so the decay rate includes integrations over them, or equivalently over independent invariant masses in a Dalitz plot. Integrating over a variable daughter invariant mass is also necessary when an unstable daughter is treated with a finite decay width rather than a fixed mass.

Useful examples with no net quark flavor are the eta c meson ηc​=ccˉ and the eta b meson ηb​=bbˉ. Examples with nonzero quark flavor are the kaon K+=usˉ and the D meson D+=cdˉ. These are pseudoscalar mesons: in the lowest quark model configuration the constituent spins combine to S=0, the relative orbital angular momentum is L=0, and the total angular momentum and parity are JP=0−. For the self-conjugate examples, meson parity and charge conjugation also gives C=(−1)L+S=+1. Electrical neutrality alone would not imply zero net quark flavor; for example K0=dsˉ has strangeness.
Restoring reduced Planck constant, decay width and mean lifetime satisfy Γτ=ℏ. Thus the representative numbers correspond to
τ20MeV​=20 MeV6.582×10−22 MeVs​=3.29×10−23 s,Γ1ps​=6.582×10−10 MeV.
(2)
The mean lifetimes differ by at least 3.0×1010. Hidden-heavy-flavor quarkonium can undergo quark-antiquark annihilation into gluons, followed by hadronization. A 0−+ color-singlet state can annihilate into two gluons. A lightest open-flavor meson cannot simply annihilate through the strong interaction, which conserves net quark flavor. With no lighter accessible final state carrying that flavor, it must undergo a weak interaction changing a constituent's flavor. The small weak interaction amplitude, CKM matrix factors and available Lorentz-invariant phase space produce a much smaller decay width.
Qualification. A decay width of order tens of MeV is not a universal property of flavor-neutral pseudoscalar mesons. The neutral pion has Γ≃7.8 eV and decays predominantly by the electromagnetic interaction, a concrete counterexample. Its mass lies below the lightest allowed hadronic final state. The examples above illustrate the intended annihilation-versus-open-flavor comparison; the numerical scale depends on thresholds and selection rules.[ref]

Γμμ​Γττ​​≃0.388,Bττ​≃0.008×0.388=3.10×10−3=0.31%.​
(4)
The supplied total decay width also gives Γμμ​=2.352 keV and Γττ​≃0.91 keV. It cancels from the branching fraction ratio.
The decisive change at 3.770 GeV is crossing the open-charm D meson-pair threshold, approximately 3.73 GeV. The strong interaction can now create a light quark-antiquark pair and rearrange ccˉ into DDˉ, without annihilating the heavy pair. This increases the total decay width from hundreds of keV to tens of MeV. The leptonic decay partial decay width is additionally smaller because ψ(3770) is predominantly a 13D1​ state, whereas ψ(3686) is predominantly 23S1​; the short-distance annihilation overlap is suppressed for nonzero orbital angular momentum. A roughly hundredfold increase in total decay width together with an order-ten reduction in leptonic partial decay width explains the order-thousand reduction in branching fraction. Attributing all three orders of magnitude to the threshold alone would miss the second effect.[ref]

At an electron-positron collider tuned to the charmonium resonance, select the mixed leptonic tau lepton decays
τ−→e−νˉe​ντ​,τ+→μ+νμ​νˉτ​,
(5)
and their charge conjugates. Require exactly two well-reconstructed oppositely charged tracks, one identified electron and one identified muon, little extra visible activity, visible energy below the beam total, and significant missing momentum pointing into the instrumented region. The multiple neutrinos prevent full reconstruction of each tau lepton, but the known initial four-momentum, lepton spectra and missing energy support a statistical signal extraction. Off-resonance data constrain nonresonant electron-positron annihilation into a tau lepton pair; that continuum is present at both beam energies.[ref]
A resonant muon pair is one possible detector background: one muon can be misidentified as an electron, while radiation escaping along the beam or a badly measured track produces apparent missing energy. Particle-identification requirements and rejection of almost back-to-back tracks near the beam momentum suppress it. This background needs detector mismeasurement; an ideal measurement would distinguish it.
Above the open-charm threshold, ψ(3770)→DDˉ supplies an additional resonant background. Semileptonic decays of the two D mesons can give an electron, a muon and neutrinos; unreconstructed hadrons let some events pass the selection. This on-shell D meson-pair background is kinematically forbidden at ψ(3686).

With the four-momenta in a, q2=−2EE′(1−cosθ), p2​⋅q=mp​(E−E′), and p2​⋅p1​=mp​E. Thus the measured beam energy E, scattered electron energy E′ and scattering angle θ determine
Q2=4EE′sin22θ​,y=1−EE′​,x=mp​(E−E′)2EE′sin2(θ/2)​.​
(11)
Here Q2 measures the spacelike momentum transfer, y is the fractional energy transfer, and the Bjorken scaling variable x is the struck parton's longitudinal momentum fraction in the leading collinear description. The hadronic invariant mass satisfies W2=(p2​+q)2=mp2​+Q2(1/x−1); large Q2 and W2 select deep inelastic scattering.
A beam spectrometer measures E before a thin stationary hydrogen target. Tracking layers on both sides of a magnetic spectrometer measure the outgoing electron direction, charge and curvature; an electromagnetic calorimeter measures its energy. Matching the track to its particle shower, with downstream muon rejection where useful, separates the scattered electron from hadronic tracks. A downstream hadronic detector measures the hadronic activity and supports event-quality checks. The sketch in a shows these separate measurements.
The scattered electron provides a clean, single-particle reconstruction. Reconstructing the entire hadronic system requires summing many charged and neutral fragments, correcting unequal particle calorimeter response and losses through beam holes, and sometimes accounting for escaping neutrinos. Thus the leptonic definition is experimentally convenient. It is not a fundamental requirement of Lorentz invariance: exact reconstruction of pX​ gives the same q=pX​−p2​, and hadronic or combined reconstructions can be preferable in some kinematic regions.

At leading order in QED, the electron exchanges one spacelike virtual photon with a charged parton inside the proton.
The two Feynman vertices contribute e and eeq​, while the photon propagator contributes 1/q2=−1/Q2. Hence the scattering amplitude scales as e2eq​/Q2 and its squared absolute value as e4eq2​/Q4. Since the fine-structure constant is α=e2/(4π), the electromagnetic coupling dependence is α2/Q4. The numerical 4π in the stated differential scattering cross-section additionally uses the flux, Lorentz-invariant phase space, azimuthal integration and the convention defining the deep-inelastic structure function; it cannot be deduced from the propagator alone.
For each measured x,Q2 bin form the reduced quantity
F2​(x,Q2)=4πα2[1−y+y2/2]xQ4​dxdQ2d2σ​.​
(12)
Plot this against Q2 at several fixed x values, normally using a logarithmic Q2 axis. Bjorken scaling predicts an approximately horizontal curve at each x, or equivalently collapse of different-Q2 data onto one F2​(x) curve. Raw cross sections do not collapse because their explicit kinematic factors vary. QCD predicts logarithmic scaling violations, so exact independence is only the ideal parton model limit. The stated extraction also assumes negligible longitudinal deep-inelastic structure function, as in the leading massless spin-half parton approximation.

Subtracting the deep-inelastic structure functions in e gives
F2p​−F2n​=3x​(U−D).
(17)
Thus at moderate and large x, the charge-weighted up quark content of the proton exceeds its down quark content. In the valence quark dominated regime this is consistent with the uud valence structure, compared with udd for the neutron. The larger 2/3 up quark electric charge makes the proton scatter more strongly. This observation alone does not establish up(x)=2dp(x) pointwise: the valence-number sum rules constrain integrals, not every x bin.
If the small-x observation means equality to good relative accuracy, it supports dominance by approximately charge-symmetric sea quarks, with U≃D and negligible relative valence excess. Gluon splitting creates quark-antiquark pairs, and the leading strong interaction treats light quark flavors similarly. It does not follow that every sea quark flavor has exactly the same distribution at every x.
Logical qualification. Equality of the limiting values alone is weaker than equality of their ratio. For example U=2, D=1, S=0 near x=0 gives F2p​=x and F2n​=2x/3: both tend to zero, although U=D and F2n​/F2p​=2/3. The usual inference about a symmetric sea needs F2n​/F2p​→1 or equivalent information about relative differences, rather than merely coincident zero limits.

A mass eigenstate ∣νi​⟩ has a definite neutrino mass mi​ and acquires a definite propagation phase at fixed momentum. An interaction eigenstate is the neutrino flavor selected by a weak charged current: νe​ is produced or detected together with an electron, and νμ​ together with a muon. The two bases are related by a unitary matrix, here the real rotation matrix U.
Inverting the supplied transformation gives ∣νe​⟩=c∣ν1​⟩+s∣ν2​⟩ and ∣νμ​⟩=−s∣ν1​⟩+c∣ν2​⟩, where c=cosθ, s=sinθ. If θ=0, these bases coincide: each produced neutrino flavor propagates as one mass eigenstate, and there is no neutrino oscillation, even if the two masses differ. Conversely, equal masses also prevent vacuum neutrino oscillation, even when a nonzero basis-rotation angle is written down.

The chiral projectors PL,R​=(1∓γ5)/2 obey γμPL​=PR​γμ, while the Dirac adjoints are ψˉ​L​=ψˉ​PR​ and ψˉ​R​=ψˉ​PL​. Consequently the cross terms vanish and the vector current is
ψˉ​γμψ=ψˉ​L​γμψL​+ψˉ​R​γμψR​.
(27)
The vector interaction preserves fermion chirality at a vertex and couples equally to left and right components. This statement about the vertex does not assert that a fermion mass term preserves chirality. Under parity, left and right components are exchanged; their equal coefficients allow a parity-invariant contraction with a true vector field.
An axial vector must couple to the axial current to obtain the analogous parity-invariant interaction. Since γ5ψL​=−ψL​ and γ5ψR​=ψR​,

(28)

For a massless fermion, chirality is fixed by helicity. For a massless antifermion, the field-chirality label is opposite to its physical helicity: a left-chiral field creates negative-helicity particles and positive-helicity antiparticles. At E≫m, wrong-chirality amplitudes are suppressed by m/E, so chirality-preserving vector vertices conserve helicity along a physical fermion line up to these corrections.
For electron-positron annihilation into a muon pair through a vector mediator, each annihilating or created pair therefore has opposite physical helicities. Writing each pair in the order (negative particle, positive particle), the nonzero possibilities are
(L,R)e​→(L,R)μ​,(L,R)e​→(R,L)μ​,(R,L)e​→(L,R)μ​,(R,L)e​→(R,L)μ​.​
(29)
Here L,R refer to physical helicity along each particle's own momentum, not to the sign of its spin projected on a single common axis. The incoming pair has total spin projection m=±1 along the electron beam; the outgoing pair has m′=±1 along the muon direction. Same-helicity pairs vanish in the strictly massless vector-current limit.

Assume unpolarized beams, negligible electron and muon masses, a single scattering s-channel spin-one mediator with equal left and right vector couplings, and leading-order QED. Z boson exchange and radiative corrections are omitted. The angular factors are the Wigner D-matrix elements dmm′1​(θ⋆), with m,m′=±1. Up to common phases and normalization they are (1+cosθ⋆) for equal pair-spin projections and (1−cosθ⋆) for opposite projections. The four orthogonal helicity channels add incoherently, giving
2(1+cosθ⋆)2+2(1−cosθ⋆)2=4(1+cos2θ⋆).
(30)
With s the squared center-of-mass energy, the normalized differential scattering cross-sections are
dΩdσ​=4sα2​(1+cos2θ⋆),dθ⋆dσ​=2sπα2​sinθ⋆(1+cos2θ⋆).​
(31)
The second expression includes the solid angle Jacobian: a distribution per angle is different from one per cosθ⋆. Integration gives σ=4πα2/(3s).
Parity reverses all momenta but leaves the spin axial vector unchanged, so it interchanges L↔R. The LR→LR and RL→RL amplitudes have equal magnitudes, as do LR→RL and RL→LR. Thus every configuration has the same rate as its parity transform, proving parity conservation. The summed distribution is also forward-backward symmetric. Strictly, parity reverses both the beam and outgoing momenta, leaving their relative angle unchanged; forward-backward reflection by itself is not the definition of parity.

If ∣gl​/gr​∣=1, then a=b, so a forward-backward term survives. At the amplitude level, parity interchanges the gl2​ and gr2​ channels; their unequal squared magnitudes show directly that the interaction violates parity. Equivalently the chiral current contains both vector current and axial current terms, with coefficients (gr​+gl​)/2 and (gr​−gl​)/2; unequal magnitudes preclude a pure parity eigen-current.
The odd-in-z term is a useful signature within these assumptions, but it should not itself be called a parity-odd scalar: the unpolarized relative angle is unchanged when all momenta are reversed. If the two species have independent couplings, the coefficient is instead proportional to (ae​−be​)(aμ​−bμ​), and a parity-violating coupling at only one vertex need not produce an unpolarized forward-backward asymmetry.

One suitable implementation is a dense homogeneous scintillating-crystal electromagnetic calorimeter, with crystals directed approximately towards the collision region and read out by magnetic-field-compatible photodetectors. An incident electron emits bremsstrahlung photons; these produce electron-Positron pairs, which radiate again. This electromagnetic shower develops over successive radiation lengths until the daughter energies approach the critical energy of an electromagnetic shower, below which ionization dominates. The deposited energy creates scintillation light, and calibrated integrated detector signals estimate the incident energy.
A depth of roughly 25 radiation lengths contains most of a high-energy electromagnetic shower. Small transverse cells resolve its lateral profile, distinguish nearby deposits and permit position reconstruction. Crystal material, optical transmission, gain and electronics must remain stable under the LHC radiation exposure. Fast shaping, timing, segmentation and radiation-tolerant readout permit operation at high collision rates; collision pileup and particle showers from nearby particles require reconstruction and calibration. A dense crystal array offers good intrinsic energy resolution because it measures almost all the shower deposition rather than only the sampled fraction.

Independent fluctuations add as variances. A useful parametrization of the electromagnetic calorimeter energy resolution is
(EσE​​)2=E/GeVa2​+(E/GeV)2b2​+c2.​
(35)
The convention makes a,b,c dimensionless when E is inserted in GeV. The three terms describe different mechanisms.
The stochastic term comes from fluctuations in the electromagnetic shower, scintillation production and detected photoelectron counts. If the number of detected photoelectrons is N=kE, Poisson distribution gives σN​=N​, hence σE​/E=1/kE​∝E−1/2. A sampling electromagnetic calorimeter also has fluctuations in the fraction deposited in its active material, contributing to the same approximate scaling. A homogeneous detector suppresses that sampling contribution.
The noise term represents approximately fixed absolute electronics and dark-current noise for a chosen reconstruction window. Additional collision pileup fluctuations can behave similarly over a specified operating range. A nearly constant σE​ gives σE​/E∝E−1.
The constant term describes residual fractional cell-calibration errors, gain drifts, temperature dependence, nonuniform light collection, dead material, and imperfect correction for longitudinal and transverse leakage. These effects often scale approximately with the incident energy, so they approach a constant fractional error. For electrons, upstream bremsstrahlung and conversion of the radiated photons spread the deposit over multiple cells; material mapping and clustering are essential. Leakage and material losses can also produce non-Gaussian tails, so the three-term parametrization describes a resolution core rather than every feature of the response. Noise dominates at low energy, stochastic fluctuations at intermediate energy, and the constant term eventually limits performance.

Use a longitudinally segmented electromagnetic calorimeter with fine transverse position readout. A compact silicon-tungsten sampling electromagnetic calorimeter can serve as the small-angle luminosity monitor. Alternate dense tungsten absorber plates with finely segmented silicon sensor planes. Incident electrons and Positrons create electromagnetic showers in the absorber; their charged daughters create electron-hole pairs by ionization in depleted silicon. An electric field collects the charge, and the summed calibrated signal estimates shower energy.
Radial and azimuthal sensor segmentation gives precise shower positions and hence polar angles using the measured distance to the interaction point. Multiple layers measure shower development and improve the centroid estimate. Opposite-side coincidence, beam-time synchronization and an energy requirement reject beam backgrounds. Mechanical survey, alignment and edge-response calibration define the small-angle fiducial boundary: high position precision matters especially because the Bhabha scattering rate changes steeply with angle. The monitor's overall energy resolution need not match the central precision crystal detector; it must accurately reconstruct its own fiducial selection and efficiency.

Tag an energetic initial-state radiation photon in e+e−→γZ(∗)→γ+invisible, observing a single-photon signature with missing energy and missing momentum. At known squared center-of-mass energy s, the leading single-photon recoil invariant mass is
mrecoil2​=(pe+​+pe−​−pγ​)2=s−2s​Eγ​.​
(50)
Above the Z boson mass, radiative-return events cluster at mrecoil​≃MZ​. At or near the pole the full off-shell recoil spectrum is fitted instead. Folding the Breit-Wigner resonance cross section with the calculable QED radiation kernel gives a predicted tagged rate proportional to Γe​Γinv​ for fixed total decay width. A fit with the known luminosity and visible line-shape parameters therefore determines Γinv​ directly from invisible events, rather than subtracting visible widths. Comparing identically selected radiative-return events with visible Z boson decays can instead constrain Γinv​/Γvis​ while cancelling much of the luminosity normalization.
The detector needs efficient single-photon triggering, good photon energy and angular resolution, reliable timing, and nearly hermetic charged-particle and particle calorimeter coverage. Forward vetoes reject radiative Bhabha scattering with escaped electrons; additional-photon vetoes reject γγ events with a lost photon. Detector inefficiencies and beam holes must be measured, since visible final states with lost particles imitate the signal.
