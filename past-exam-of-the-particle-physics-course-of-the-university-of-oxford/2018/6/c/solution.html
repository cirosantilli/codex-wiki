For the Pole forward-backward asymmetry for charged leptons, integrating the normalized distribution over the two hemispheres cancels the even term in the numerator. Since ∫−11​(1+z2)dz=8/3 and ∫01​zdz−∫−10​zdz=1,
AFB​=43​(L2+R2L2−R2​)2=43​[1−4sin2θW​+8sin4θW​1−4sin2θW​​]2.​
(57)
Within this single-Z, tree-level model, a nonzero asymmetry requires unequal left/right coupling magnitudes, demonstrating parity violation in the vertices. For gL​=gR​ the odd angular term vanishes and AFB​=0; equal magnitudes with opposite signs also give zero. This inference is process-specific: a forward-backward asymmetry is not universally a parity-odd observable, since reversing both beam and outgoing momenta leaves their relative Angle unchanged. Purely electromagnetic Bhabha scattering is a concrete parity-conserving process with a forward-peaked, asymmetric distribution.
For the supplied sin2θW​=0.23153,
AFBpred​=0.01620.​
(58)
The measurement differs by 0.0188−0.01620=0.00260, or 0.79 times its quoted uncertainty of 0.0033, so it is consistent with the prediction. Statistical fluctuations alone preclude exact equality; photon exchange, initial-state radiation and electroweak radiative corrections also modify the idealized pure-resonance prediction and provide physical reasons for small differences.
