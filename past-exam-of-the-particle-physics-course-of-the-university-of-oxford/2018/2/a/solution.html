The valence quark distributions describe net quark content: ∫uv​(x)dx=2 and ∫dv​(x)dx=1 for a proton. Their momentum densities xuv​,xdv​ are small at very small x, peak at moderate momentum fractions, and vanish toward x=1, where one parton carrying almost all proton momentum is unlikely. The larger u distribution reflects two valence up quarks and its comparatively harder shape; its pointwise value need not be exactly twice the down distribution.
The sea quark distribution is generated largely by gluon radiation and g→qqˉ​ splitting. It grows strongly toward small x and falls at large x. The plotted sea curve is scaled by 0.05, so it must be multiplied by twenty before numerical use. The total up-quark PDF is
u(x)=uv​(x)+uˉ(x),​
(11)
using the valence-up distribution and the up-sea distribution. The summed sea curve alone does not fix uˉ without a flavour assumption. Under the later stated four-flavour symmetry, uˉ=Σ/8.
