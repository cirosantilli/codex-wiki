The parton model treats a fast proton as an ensemble of approximately collinear, nearly free quarks and antiquarks. A hard deep inelastic scattering collision resolves one parton on a time scale short compared with its binding motion. The probabilities are parton distribution functions; the simplest approximation neglects intrinsic transverse momentum, quark masses and the proton mass in the hard collision. Quantum chromodynamics introduces logarithmic violations of Bjorken scaling, so the parton distribution functions depend on a resolution scale as well as a parton momentum fraction.
Write the incident electron and proton four-momenta as k,P, the scattered electron four-momentum as k′, and set q=k−k′. With the (+−−−) Minkowski metric, the Bjorken scaling variable and the inelasticity are
Q2=−q2>0,y=P⋅kP⋅q​,xB​=2P⋅qQ2​=syQ2​,s≃4Ee​Ep​.
(1)
An incident parton with four-momentum ξP emerges on shell, giving (ξP+q)2≃2ξP⋅q−Q2=0. Thus ξ=xB​: the observable Bjorken scaling variable measures the struck parton momentum fraction in this approximation.
For clarity, measure the scattering angle ϑ from the incoming electron direction, and denote the outgoing electron energy by Ee′​. Direct four-momentum products give
Q2=4Ee​Ee′​sin22ϑ​,y=1−Ee​Ee′​​cos22ϑ​,xB​=Ee​Ep​[1−(Ee′​/Ee​)cos2(ϑ/2)]Ee​Ee′​sin2(ϑ/2)​​.
(2)
The expression uses the known beam energies and two measured quantities. An electromagnetic calorimeter measures Ee′​; a tracking detector, the collision interaction vertex and the position of the electromagnetic shower determine ϑ. Coverage close to the incoming electron direction matters at small Q2. A hadronic calorimeter supplies an independent consistency check and rejects backgrounds. If the angle is instead measured from the incoming proton, its sine and cosine half-angle factors interchange.

At the parton level, the dominant valence reactions are e−u→νe​d through W boson exchange and e+d→νˉe​u through W boson exchange. The first also acts on c, while the second acts on s with the appropriate Cabibbo-Kobayashi-Maskawa matrix factors; crossing supplies the antiquark reactions. The lines in the figure show fermion number flow; the Positron and antineutrino arrows point opposite to their momenta.
For unpolarized beams, the leading charged current cross sections, suppressing Cabibbo-Kobayashi-Maskawa matrix factors, have the form
dxdQ2d2σ(e−p)​dxdQ2d2σ(e+p)​​=2πGF2​​(MW2​+Q2MW2​​)2[u+c+(1−y)2(dˉ+sˉ)],=2πGF2​​(MW2​+Q2MW2​​)2[uˉ+cˉ+(1−y)2(d+s)].​
(3)
Here each parton distribution function is evaluated at (x,Q2). The proton has two valence up quarks and one valence down quark; moreover, its valence contribution in the Positron reaction carries the chiral angular factor (1−y)2. Both effects favour e−p. Increasing Q2 at fixed collider energy enforces x≥Q2/s, progressively suppressing the sea relative to the valence quarks; large y further suppresses the Positron valence term. There is no universal factor of two valid at every (x,y).
The escaping neutrino prevents direct reconstruction from a scattered charged lepton. Instead use the Jacquet-Blondel reconstruction. With the +z axis along the proton beam, measure the complete hadronic final state and form
δh​=∑h​(Eh​−pzh​),yh​=2Ee​δh​​,Qh2​=1−yh​∣∑h​pTh​∣2​,xh​=syh​Qh2​​​.
(4)
These relations follow from four-momentum conservation, with the unobserved neutrino transverse momentum opposite to the measured hadronic sum. A nearly hermetic hadronic calorimeter, supplemented by tracking detectors, must measure jets, their energies and their directions. Forward losses, initial-state radiation and small yh​ cause significant reconstruction uncertainties.

The photon propagator gives approximately dσNC​/dQ2∝1/(Q2)2, whereas a massive W boson gives dσCC​/dQ2∝1/(Q2+MW2​)2. Up to different coupling physics values, parton distribution functions and angular factors,
σNC​σCC​​≃C(Q2)(Q2+MW2​Q2​)2.
(5)
Consequently the low-Q2 charged current suppression disappears as Q2 approaches MW2​. The source graph shows comparable cross sections at Q2 of order 104 GeV2, so the relevant mass scale is of order 102 GeV.
A more quantitative, still rough reading uses the e−p ratio R1​≃0.006 near q1​=300 GeV2 and R2​≃0.17 near q2​=2000 GeV2, where qi​ denotes Q2, not Q. If C varies slowly over this interval, set κ=R2​/R1​​q1​/q2​. Then
κ=q2​+MW2​q1​+MW2​​,MW​=1−κκq2​−q1​​​≃80 GeV​.
(6)
The reading is compatible with roughly 70–100 GeV; it is not a precision mass determination. In particular, Z boson exchange contributes to the neutral current at high Q2, and the parton distribution functions do not cancel exactly. Equality of two inclusive cross sections alone does not define MW​.

An observable that reverses sign under parity but has a nonzero expectation in a suitably prepared experiment tests parity violation. A polar momentum reverses, whereas an axial spin angular momentum does not. Thus J⋅p and helicity are parity-odd; an apparatus asymmetry must be separated from a physical asymmetry by reversing the preparation or analyser.
In the Wu experiment, polarized cobalt-60 nuclei preferentially emit beta decay electrons in one direction relative to the nuclear spin angular momentum. The distribution contains AP⋅pe​/Ee​; its sign changes under spatial inversion. Reversing the nuclear polarization reverses the observed asymmetry.[ref]
In the Goldhaber experiment, the sign of the circular polarization of selected gamma rays determines the sign of the neutrino helicity. A preference for negative helicity is parity-odd because spatial inversion would produce the positive-helicity state. This provides a different experimental observable from the electron angular distribution.

The relevant sequence is electron capture followed by nuclear gamma decay:
63152m​Eu+eK−​⟶62152​Sm∗+νe​,152Sm∗⟶152Sm+γ.
(7)
The nuclear spin angular momenta are 0→1→0. The captured K-shell electron has total angular momentum 1/2, so the initial atom-plus-electron system cannot be treated as having zero angular momentum. The atomic vacancy also produces subsequent X-rays or Auger electrons; these are not the selected nuclear transition.
Choose the neutrino momentum along +z and the recoil momentum along −z. For a negative-helicity neutrino, sνz​=−1/2. With the two initial electron spin projections, angular momentum conservation allows the daughter to have mz​=0 or +1. A photon emitted exactly along the recoil direction is transverse, and the 1→0 transition selects the mz​=+1 component. Its spin angular momentum points along +z, opposite to its momentum.
Thus in the selected collinear geometry the daughter and photon have the same negative helicity as the neutrino, while their spin angular momenta point opposite to the neutrino spin. Reversing all these spin projections gives the positive-helicity alternative. In normalized notation,
hγ​=hSm∗​=hν​​,h=sS⋅p​​.
(8)
The actual eigenvalues of S⋅p​ are ±1/2 for the neutrino and ±1 for the photon; the equality concerns their normalized signs.

The nuclear gamma ray can be absorbed by ground-state samarium-152, excite the same level and be re-emitted: nuclear nuclear resonance fluorescence. Its energy in the absorber rest frame must overlap the nuclear absorption line within the relevant natural and thermal broadening. Atomic radiation from refilling the K shell does not have the required transition energy.
Let the excitation energy be E0​ and the nuclear mass be M. A free stationary emitter loses recoil energy while a free stationary absorber requires recoil energy:
Eem​≃E0​−2Mc2E02​​,Eabs​≃E0​+2Mc2E02​​,Eabs​−Eem​≃Mc2E02​​.
(9)
For E0​≃0.96 MeV the gap is about 6.5 eV. A photon emitted along the daughter recoil receives a positive Doppler shift of approximately E0​pν​/(Mc), whereas one emitted opposite the recoil is redshifted. Emission before the daughter slows down therefore selects photons roughly opposite to the neutrino. Comparable neutrino and photon energies nearly compensate the recoil mismatch; finite line widths and thermal motion permit overlap. Exact compensation need not hold for every atom, and this is not an assumption of recoil-free emission.
Magnetized iron supplies polarized electrons. Their Compton scattering cross section depends on the relative electron spin and photon helicity, so the iron attenuates the two circular polarizations differently. Reverse the magnetization and measure the resonance-selected count asymmetry A=(N+​−N−​)/(N+​+N−​). With a calibrated analysing power a and background correction, A=aPγ​ determines the sign and magnitude of the circular polarization. A scintillation counter such as NaI(Tl), coupled to a photomultiplier, determines the gamma ray energy from calibrated pulse height; a semiconductor detector could perform the same spectroscopic function.

The functional arrangement below separates the polarization analysis from the resonance selection. The shield prevents the direct source beam from overwhelming the detector; the off-axis detector accepts gamma rays re-emitted by the samarium scatterer.
The europium source emits into magnetized iron, whose magnetization is alternated. A samarium scatterer selects nuclear nuclear resonance fluorescence, favouring photons emitted along the daughter recoil. A shielded NaI(Tl) scintillation counter records the scattered nuclear line. Compare the background-subtracted line rates for the two iron magnetizations, and use the calibrated sign of the analysing power to identify the photon helicity.
The measured circular polarization was negative, compatible after angular acceptance corrections with negative neutrino helicity. By the angular momentum conservation argument, the selected photon helicity and neutrino helicity have the same sign. Thus the experiment concludes
Sν​⋅p​ν​=−21​​
(10)
for the emitted relativistic electron neutrino. Finite acceptance dilutes the measured polarization; it does not justify equating the raw count asymmetry to −1.[ref]

Strictly, it is the infinitesimal generators, not every unitary matrix with unit determinant, that are traceless. For a continuous one-parameter family U(ϵ)=exp(iϵT), unitarity requires the generator T to be Hermitian. The determinant condition gives
1=detU(ϵ)=exp(iϵtrT)for every real ϵ.
(11)
Differentiating at ϵ=0 yields trT=0. A Hermitian matrix has n real diagonal parameters and n(n−1) real off-diagonal parameters; one real trace constraint leaves n2−1 independent generators. Thus
dimSU(n)=n2−1​.
(12)
The identity matrix supplies a concrete counterexample to interpreting the claim as one about arbitrary group elements: In​∈SU(n) but trIn​=n. Also, eitrT=1 at a single parameter value only fixes the trace modulo 2π; the continuous neighbourhood is essential.

In neutral meson mixing, the mass eigenstates and flavor eigenstates differ. A state prepared as D0=cuˉ develops a Dˉ0=ucˉ component because the effective Hamiltonian operator has off-diagonal mass and decay width entries. In the CP symmetry limit implicit in the source, the propagating states are equal superpositions, with average mass M=(M1​+M2​)/2 and average decay width Γ=(Γ1​+Γ2​)/2.
To retain the source's positive isin convention for g−​, take M2​−M1​=ΔM and Γ2​−Γ1​=ΔΓ, with g±​=(e1​±e2​)/2, where ej​=exp(−iMj​t−Γj​t/2). Then x=ΔM/Γ and y=ΔΓ/(2Γ) reproduce the given functions. Reversing an eigenstate label changes signs in intermediate formulae; it must also change the phase conventions consistently.
For equal widths, the mixed probability is proportional to e−Γtsin2(ΔMt/2). Thus the angular angular frequency is ∣ΔM∣ in natural units, or ∣ΔM∣/ℏ with units restored; the period is 2πℏ/∣ΔM∣. A nonzero difference of decay widths adds hyperbolic time dependence.
The ordinary neutral flavour-carrying mesons that mix are K0, D0, Bd0​ and Bs0​ with their antiparticles. A self-conjugate π0 has no distinct antiparticle flavour to oscillate into. There are no long-lived top-flavoured mesons, since the top quark decays before hadronization.

A charged lepton in a semileptonic decay directly identifies the decaying charm quark flavour: ordinarily D0→K−μ+νμ​ and Dˉ0→K+μ−νˉμ​. With an independently tagged initial quark flavour, the wrong-sign charged lepton requires neutral meson mixing, rather than a competing doubly Cabibbo suppressed amplitude. That method measures only the quadratic combination x2+y2 at leading order, with no direct access to their signs. The missing neutrino also worsens kinematic and decay-time reconstruction. The fully reconstructed Kπ channel has substantial yield, a clear invariant-mass peak and a well-measured decay interaction vertex. Its cost is the unknown strong phase δ, so separating x and y needs further channels or an independent strong-phase measurement. At the Z boson resonance, a tau lepton pair produces two approximately opposite narrow hemispheres, usually with one or three charged tracks per decay, and missing energy from neutrinos. Multihadronic Z boson decays have much higher track multiplicity. Direct e+e− and μ+μ− events have two tracks but almost the full beam energy in an electromagnetic calorimeter or muon detector; the tau lepton daughters typically have smaller visible energy. Track directions, calorimetric energy, missing transverse momentum and particle identification together control these backgrounds.
Use a tag-and-probe method: select one hemisphere with an independently understood tau lepton tag, then classify the opposite hemisphere into mutually exclusive decay categories. Correct the category counts for efficiency, migration and background, including an explicit remainder for modes absent from the displayed table. Conditional on a genuine tag, the opposite-hemisphere probabilities sum to one. This avoids assuming that a raw event count equals the number of decaying tau leptons.
An absolute cross-check uses single and double tags. If Si​ counts tagged hemispheres and Dii​ counts events with both hemispheres in mode i, then
Si​=2Nττ​Bi​ϵi​,Dii​=Nττ​Bi2​ϵii​,Nττ​=4Dii​ϵi2​Si2​ϵii​​.
(29)
The double-tag efficiency need not factorize. Fit all categories with their efficiencies and correlations, imposing the inclusive normalization. The nine displayed entries sum to 96.18%, not 100%. Renormalizing those entries alone to 100% would bias every quoted branching fraction.

The isospin coupling 1⊗1→1, with third component −1, has the normalized state
∣M−⟩=2​∣ρ0π−⟩−∣ρ−π0⟩​.

A projection operator satisfies P2=P; an orthogonal projection operator also has P†=P. It extracts a subspace while leaving already projected states unchanged. Since (γ5)2=1 and (γ5)†=γ5, the chiral projectors are
PL​=21−γ5​,PR​=21+γ5​​,PL​PR​=0,PL​+PR​=1.
(38)
They separate the Dirac spinor into opposite fermion chirality components. The Standard Model weak charged current contains ψˉ​γμPL​ψ, selecting left-chiral particle fields and the corresponding right-helicity massless antiparticles; right-chiral charged fermions are weak-isospin singlets. The weak neutral current can couple to both fermion chirality components with different strengths. Chirality and helicity should not be equated for a massive fermion: a Lorentz boost can reverse its helicity, while the field representation defining chirality is a different notion.

Cherenkov radiation is coherent electromagnetic emission by a charged particle moving through a dielectric faster than the phase velocity of light in that medium. The particle polarizes the material along its path. Wavelets interfere constructively on a cone when
βn(ω)>1,cosθC​=βn(ω)1​​.
(45)
A simple wavefront construction compares a particle displacement vt with the light-wave displacement ct/n, giving the cone relation. The particle still travels slower than the vacuum speed of light; no violation of special relativity occurs. Dispersion makes the angle and threshold wavelength-dependent. The radiation has a continuous spectrum rather than the discrete line spectrum of an atomic transition.

Collider luminosity is the proportionality factor between a process cross section and its interaction rate: dN/dt=Lσ before efficiency corrections. It has units of inverse area per time. Integrated collider luminosity is Lint​=∫L(t)dt, with inverse-area units, and gives N=σLint​ for a constant cross section and unit efficiency.
At an e+e− collider, count small-angle Bhabha scattering in a dedicated forward detector. Its calculable quantum electrodynamics cross section in a precisely defined angular acceptance provides L=N/(ϵσΔt), after background and radiative corrections. The inner angular boundary is especially important because the rate is sharply forward-peaked.
At a collider with two proton beams, a van der Meer scan calibrates a visible interaction monitor by separating the beams horizontally and vertically while recording the overlap-dependent rate. Known bunch populations and the measured overlap give an absolute collider luminosity and hence the monitor's visible cross section. Its rate then tracks collider luminosity during normal running. Correlations between transverse coordinates require a nonfactorizing scan analysis rather than blindly multiplying two one-dimensional profiles.
