Fit radiatively corrected visible cross sections across the Z boson resonance. The line shape determines MZ​, the total decay width ΓZ​, and pole normalizations
σf0​=MZ2​12π​ΓZ2​Γe​Γf​​.
(45)
The first extraction uses the fitted total and visible partial decay widths. Assuming lepton universality, Γe​=Γμ​=Γτ​=Γℓ​, an absolute leptonic pole cross section gives Γℓ​=ΓZ​MZ2​σℓ0​/(12π)​. The hadronic-to-leptonic ratio gives Rℓ​=Γhad​/Γℓ​. Subtracting every visible channel then yields
Γinv​=ΓZ​−Γhad​−3Γℓ​.​
(46)
Without lepton universality, separately fitted visible leptonic widths replace 3Γℓ​, with sufficient independent information to disentangle their products with Γe​.
A second, useful peak-normalization extraction first determines the invisible-to-leptonic ratio. Because
σhad0​=MZ2​12π​Rℓ​(ΓZ​Γℓ​​)2,
(47)
one obtains
Rinv​≡Γℓ​Γinv​​=MZ2​σhad0​12πRℓ​​​−Rℓ​−3.​
(48)
This ratio extraction does not require the measured line width. Multiplication by an independently predicted leptonic partial decay width, or by ΓZ​/(Rinv​+Rℓ​+3) from the scan, converts it to Γinv​. Alternatively dividing Rinv​ by the predicted single-species ratio Γν​/Γℓ​ gives the number of light active neutrinos. These are two standard uses of line-shape information, with different choices of theoretical input; if both use the same fitted Γℓ​, they are algebraically equivalent and statistically correlated, not independent determinations.
Statistical uncertainties in the reference-process counts give fluctuations in the collider luminosity at individual scan points, propagating into normalization and fitted line width. They decrease with additional reference events. A common multiplicative luminosity-scale systematic largely cancels in Rℓ​ and the normalized line shape, but shifts every absolute peak cross section. It therefore does not cancel from either invisible-width extraction. For a fractional upward luminosity shift ε, σhad0​ shifts by −ε, and at fixed Rℓ​,MZ​,ΓZ​,
δRinv​≃2ε​(Rinv​+Rℓ​+3),δΓinv​≃2ε​(Γhad​+3Γℓ​).
(49)
Thus overestimating luminosity increases the inferred invisible contribution. Energy-dependent systematic errors can also distort the line shape and its total decay width. The covariance matrix must retain correlations between scan points and experiments, including common theoretical uncertainty in the Bhabha scattering calculation.
