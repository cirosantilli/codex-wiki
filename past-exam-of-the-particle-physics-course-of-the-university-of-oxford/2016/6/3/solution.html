Use the Dirac representation of the gamma matrices and commuting external-wavefunction components, rather than anticommuting quantum fields. Let A=γ0γ2, B=γ2γ0=−A, and Γα=γα(gV​−gA​γ5). Since (γ2)†=−γ2 and v=iγ2u∗,
vˉA​ΓαvB​=−uAT​BΓαγ2uB∗​.
(41)
Transpose this scalar and use (γ2)T=γ2, BT=A and uˉB​=uB†​γ0:
vˉA​ΓαvB​=uˉB​A(Γα)TBuA​.
(42)
Here A2=1 and A(γ5)TA=γ5. The supplied identity therefore yields
A(Γα)TB​=A(gV​−gA​(γ5)T)(γα)TB=(gV​−gA​γ5)γα=γα(gV​+gA​γ5).​
(43)
Hence
vˉA​(p)γα(gV​−gA​γ5)vB​(p′)=uˉB​(p′)γα(gV​+gA​γ5)uA​(p)​.
(44)
The external labels and momenta are exchanged; the scalar constants are transposed, not complex-conjugated. At the level of these numerical fermion bilinears, charge conjugation reverses the relative sign of the vector and axial terms, relating particle and antiparticle weak amplitudes. For gV​=gA​, the two displayed spinor chains have opposite chiral projectors. A physical charge-conjugation transformation of an operator current also includes the minus sign from reordering Dirac fields. In particular this numerical identity does not imply equal electric charges for a particle and its antiparticle.
