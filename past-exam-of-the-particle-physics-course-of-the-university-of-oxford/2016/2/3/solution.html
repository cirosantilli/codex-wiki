The nuclear gamma ray can be absorbed by ground-state samarium-152, excite the same level and be re-emitted: nuclear nuclear resonance fluorescence. Its energy in the absorber rest frame must overlap the nuclear absorption line within the relevant natural and thermal broadening. Atomic radiation from refilling the K shell does not have the required transition energy.
Let the excitation energy be E0​ and the nuclear mass be M. A free stationary emitter loses recoil energy while a free stationary absorber requires recoil energy:
Eem​≃E0​−2Mc2E02​​,Eabs​≃E0​+2Mc2E02​​,Eabs​−Eem​≃Mc2E02​​.
(9)
For E0​≃0.96 MeV the gap is about 6.5 eV. A photon emitted along the daughter recoil receives a positive Doppler shift of approximately E0​pν​/(Mc), whereas one emitted opposite the recoil is redshifted. Emission before the daughter slows down therefore selects photons roughly opposite to the neutrino. Comparable neutrino and photon energies nearly compensate the recoil mismatch; finite line widths and thermal motion permit overlap. Exact compensation need not hold for every atom, and this is not an assumption of recoil-free emission.
Magnetized iron supplies polarized electrons. Their Compton scattering cross section depends on the relative electron spin and photon helicity, so the iron attenuates the two circular polarizations differently. Reverse the magnetization and measure the resonance-selected count asymmetry A=(N+​−N−​)/(N+​+N−​). With a calibrated analysing power a and background correction, A=aPγ​ determines the sign and magnitude of the circular polarization. A scintillation counter such as NaI(Tl), coupled to a photomultiplier, determines the gamma ray energy from calibrated pulse height; a semiconductor detector could perform the same spectroscopic function.
