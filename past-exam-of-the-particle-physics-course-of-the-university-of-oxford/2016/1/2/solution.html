At the parton level, the dominant valence reactions are e−u→νe​d through W boson exchange and e+d→νˉe​u through W boson exchange. The first also acts on c, while the second acts on s with the appropriate Cabibbo-Kobayashi-Maskawa matrix factors; crossing supplies the antiquark reactions. The lines in the figure show fermion number flow; the Positron and antineutrino arrows point opposite to their momenta.
For unpolarized beams, the leading charged current cross sections, suppressing Cabibbo-Kobayashi-Maskawa matrix factors, have the form
dxdQ2d2σ(e−p)​dxdQ2d2σ(e+p)​​=2πGF2​​(MW2​+Q2MW2​​)2[u+c+(1−y)2(dˉ+sˉ)],=2πGF2​​(MW2​+Q2MW2​​)2[uˉ+cˉ+(1−y)2(d+s)].​
(3)
Here each parton distribution function is evaluated at (x,Q2). The proton has two valence up quarks and one valence down quark; moreover, its valence contribution in the Positron reaction carries the chiral angular factor (1−y)2. Both effects favour e−p. Increasing Q2 at fixed collider energy enforces x≥Q2/s, progressively suppressing the sea relative to the valence quarks; large y further suppresses the Positron valence term. There is no universal factor of two valid at every (x,y).
The escaping neutrino prevents direct reconstruction from a scattered charged lepton. Instead use the Jacquet-Blondel reconstruction. With the +z axis along the proton beam, measure the complete hadronic final state and form
δh​=∑h​(Eh​−pzh​),yh​=2Ee​δh​​,Qh2​=1−yh​∣∑h​pTh​∣2​,xh​=syh​Qh2​​​.
(4)
These relations follow from four-momentum conservation, with the unobserved neutrino transverse momentum opposite to the measured hadronic sum. A nearly hermetic hadronic calorimeter, supplemented by tracking detectors, must measure jets, their energies and their directions. Forward losses, initial-state radiation and small yh​ cause significant reconstruction uncertainties.
