Place a far detector near the first atmospheric disappearance maximum, approximately 1.2km for the source's monochromatic energy and splitting, and a nearly identical near detector a few hundred metres from the reactor complex. Their flux-corrected comparison largely cancels reactor power, emitted-spectrum and absolute-efficiency uncertainties. For a broad reactor spectrum one fits energy-dependent disappearance instead of treating that single baseline as a universal exact optimum.
Detect νˉe​p→e+n in a hydrogen-rich target, for example liquid scintillator with neutron-capture enhancement. The prompt Positron signal estimates neutrino energy; the delayed neutron-capture signal supplies a time-and-position coincidence that strongly reduces random backgrounds. Use sufficient target mass and exposure, low radioactivity, overburden and a muon veto to reduce cosmogenic backgrounds, calibrated relative energy response and efficiency, and monitored reactor powers and baselines. Account for multiple reactor cores, spent-fuel backgrounds and accidental coincidences. At kilometre baselines the slow solar phase is small, so the disappearance depth directly probes sin2(2θ13​) with little sensitivity to the CP phase or mass ordering.
