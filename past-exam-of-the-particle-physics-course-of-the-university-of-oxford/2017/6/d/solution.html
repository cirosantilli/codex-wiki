The literal t→qZγ final state has a three-vertex loop using the Standard Model quartic vertex W+W−Zγ. The two other vertices are t→di​W+ and di​W+→q, with q=u,c. The internal down-type quark joins those two vertices, while two charged-vector propagators join them through the quartic vertex emitting both neutral bosons. The bottom panel of the figure shows this topology, satisfying the three-vertex hint without dropping the photon. Its additional electromagnetic coupling is contained in the four-leg vertex; counting vertices alone does not determine coupling order.
The companion single-Z penguin diagram illustrates t→qZ: it instead attaches the neutral boson to the internal quark line and has three cubic vertices. These are different final states. A complete gauge-invariant amplitude requires all companion attachments; for qZγ these include photon radiation from charged lines as well as the quartic graph.

The same flavour preference applies to the separate two-body modes t→cZ and t→cγ. This estimates a relative flavour preference, not an absolute branching ratio. Loop factors and mass-difference suppression remain, so these channels are fully compatible with GIM. A resolved extra photon also introduces another electromagnetic coupling and three-body phase space; an experimental Zγ definition needs photon-energy and isolation cuts to distinguish hard radiation from the soft limit.
