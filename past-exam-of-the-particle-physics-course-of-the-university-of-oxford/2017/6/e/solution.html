For a t→cZ search, the cleanest neutral-boson reconstruction is Z→e+e− or μ+μ−: require an isolated opposite-sign same-flavour pair near the Z mass and a jet forming the top mass with that pair. In top-pair events, tagging the other top through its ordinary bW decay provides a bottom jet and, if its W is leptonic, another isolated lepton and missing transverse momentum. Kinematic reconstruction and heavy-flavour information help reject vector-boson-plus-jets and other backgrounds. Hadronic or invisible Z decays have higher rate but less distinctive backgrounds or incomplete mass reconstruction.
For the literal t→cZγ channel, additionally require a prompt isolated photon and reconstruct the top with Z+γ+jet. For the alternative t→cγ, use an isolated energetic photon and jet near the top mass, again exploiting the other top as a tag. These are search signatures; no present-day observation claim is needed to interpret the historical question.
