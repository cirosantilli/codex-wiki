Treat the electron and electron antineutrino as effectively massless particles in the centre-of-momentum frame, retain only the charged current, and use the Fermi interaction: me2​≪s≪MW2​. Take θ∗ between incoming and outgoing electron antineutrino directions. The V-A interaction selects a right-helicity electron antineutrino and a left-helicity electron. Their opposite incoming directions give total angular momentum projection one. The outgoing pair has the same projection along its own axis. The Wigner D-matrix element is d111​(θ∗)=(1+cosθ∗)/2, so the squared helicity amplitude is proportional to (1+cosθ∗)2. The inactive initial electron helicity is included in the unpolarized normalization supplied by the paper.
The boost from the centre-of-momentum frame to the laboratory frame has β=E/(E+me​), while the incoming electron antineutrino has E∗​=(s−me2​)/(2s​)=me​E/s​ and s=me2​+2me​E. Thus
EE′​=2E+me​E+me​+Ecosθ∗​,y=2E+me​E(1−cosθ∗)​≃21−cosθ∗​.
(1)

Strike a target with high-energy protons, collect negative pions and kaons with magnetic horns, and let them decay in a long evacuated decay tunnel: π−→μ−νˉμ​ and K−→μ−νˉμ​. Absorb residual hadrons and range out muons in shielding. Focusing selects the negative parent sign and therefore enriches muon antineutrinos; other parent decays leave a finite contamination. Monitor the parent flux and use a near detector to constrain the unoscillated spectrum.
A massive segmented calorimeter supplies interaction probability and measures the hadronic shower. Alternate dense absorber plates with scintillation counters or tracking planes, and magnetize the iron or add a downstream magnetic muon detector. The signal is a positive muon plus a hadronic shower; the sign rejects much of the muon neutrino contamination. For a contained muon, its range gives energy; for a penetrating track, curvature gives p⊥​[GeV]=0.2998B[T]R[m]. Multiple measurements and a field map determine the fitted momentum and sign. Summed calibrated shower response estimates the struck quark's visible hadronization energy, not the energy of a free observable quark. Correct for invisible nuclear losses, noncompensating response, leakage and acceptance. Neglecting target recoil corrections, Eν​≃Eμ​+Ehad​ and y≃Ehad​/Eν​.

At energies large compared with fermion masses, the gauge interactions preserve chirality on each fermion line. A photon couples to charged fermions with equal left and right coefficients eQf​. A gluon couples to either quark chirality with coefficient gs​Ta. A Z boson couples to both, with coefficients (g/cosθW​)(T3​−Qf​sin2θW​) on left-chiral fields and (g/cosθW​)(−Qf​sin2θW​) on right-chiral fields. A W boson couples only to left-chiral fermions and the associated right-helicity antifermions: W−→ℓL−​νˉR​ or dL​uˉR​, and the charge-conjugate W+ channels. For quarks the coefficient includes the CKM matrix. There is no Standard Model right-chiral neutrino gauge vertex.
For outgoing pairs at a neutral vertex, the allowed helicities are fL−​fˉ​R+​ and fR−​fˉ​L+​; the latter labels helicity, not the chirality of the corresponding antifield. Only the s-channel photon and Z boson diagrams contribute to e+e−→μ+μ− at tree level among these gauge exchanges. A W boson changes charged leptons into neutrinos, and gluons do not couple to leptons.

For a neutral ccˉ bound state, meson parity and charge conjugation give P=(−1)L+1 and C=(−1)L+S. In spectroscopic notation n2S+1LJ​, the integer n labels radial excitation, L is orbital angular momentum, S the total constituent spin, and J their total angular momentum. The four allowed two-real-photon states are ηc​, ηc​(2S), χc0​ and χc2​. Two photons have C=+1, excluding J/ψ, ψ(2S) and hc​; the Landau-Yang theorem excludes χc1​ because J=1. Both scalar and pseudoscalar two-photon amplitudes exist, so 0++ and 0−+ are allowed. The J/psi meson was discovered first because its 1−− assignment permits direct, resonantly enhanced single-photon formation in e+e− and a clean dilepton peak in hadron production. The lighter ηc​ cannot be formed by one photon; it needs less favorable production channels and has a broader two-gluon decay.
A spin-zero ηc​ at rest has no preferred axis, so dΓ/dΩp​ is isotropic after summing final spins. A J/psi meson formed in unpolarized high-energy e+e− has transverse m=±1 alignment. Its ppˉ​ distribution can be written
dcosθp​dΓ​∝a(1+cos2θp​)+bsin2θp​,a,b≥0,
(14)
where a,b contain the two independent helicity amplitudes. It is generally anisotropic; the approximate massless, helicity-conserving limit has b→0. The tabulated branching fraction does not fix a/b. An ensemble equally populated in all three spin projections would instead be isotropic: alignment of the production ensemble matters.

The tau-neutrino charged-current threshold requires specifying the stationary target and the lightest allowed final state. For a free neutron, the lowest charged current channel is ντ​n→τ−p. At threshold both final particles are at rest relative to one another in the centre-of-momentum frame, so
s=mn2​+2mn​Eν​=(mp​+mτ​)2,Eνthr​=2mn​(mp​+mτ​)2−mn2​​≃3.45GeV.

The customary 3.5GeV answer therefore presumes a nucleon target; nuclear thresholds and practical deep inelastic scattering thresholds differ.

In a dense beam dump, long-lived pions and kaons usually interact and lose energy before decaying. Their residual stopped decays yield low-energy neutrinos, below the useful high-energy tau-interaction sample. Prompt charmed mesons have much shorter lifetimes and can decay before reinteraction. This suppresses the conventional muon neutrino background relative to the prompt component. The production sequence is Quantum chromodynamics creation of ccˉ, principally gg→ccˉ with qqˉ​→g∗→ccˉ also allowed, followed by hadronization csˉ→Ds+​ and its charge conjugate. The gg process includes both quark-exchange diagrams and the three-gluon diagram. Fragmentation also produces other hadrons; the proton does not turn directly into one isolated Ds meson.
Ds+​(csˉ)→W+∗→τ+ντ​,τ+→νˉτ​W+∗→νˉτ​(ℓ+νℓ​ or udˉ,usˉ).

The charge-conjugate chain gives a prompt tau antineutrino and a secondary tau neutrino. Thus each leptonic Ds meson chain produces both tau-flavour signs. The corresponding Feynman diagrams are shown in a.
For the CKM matrix, diagonalizing the up/down quark mass matrices by UuL​,UdL​ leaves V=UuL†​UdL​ in the charged current:
Lcc​=−2​g​uˉLi​γμVij​dLj​Wμ+​+h.c.,V†V=I.

At fixed kinematics and hadronic matrix element, rates are proportional to ∣Vij​∣2; phase space, parent lifetimes, form factors and helicity suppression must also be included when comparing branching fractions.
For a charged pseudoscalar meson P+=(cqˉ​), leptonic pseudoscalar decay width gives
Γ(P+→τ+ντ​)=8πGF2​fP2​∣Vcq​∣2mP​mτ2​​(1−mP2​mτ2​​)2.

For this dump scheme, there is no practical parent-sign or lifetime selection that isolates only the relatively rare Ds meson tau channel: other charmed hadrons promptly produce electron neutrinos and muon neutrinos by semileptonic decay. Both electron and muon modes have the same charged current couplings and similar available phase space, so comparable νe​ and νμ​ fluxes are plausible. The tau lepton also decays into electron and muon channels. A rare tau-producing channel cannot remove the abundant prompt backgrounds, and its two-step chain emits both tau-flavour signs.
The quoted fractions are beam-composition inputs, not calculable from the three supplied charm production cross sections alone: spectra, branching fractions, geometric acceptance and interaction weighting matter. Impossibility of a pure tau source is a practical statement about this production method, not a fundamental prohibition on selecting tau flavour at an oscillation maximum.
Nearly pure electron neutrino sources can use selected radioactive beta-plus decay or electron capture parents; beta-minus decay and reactors instead produce electron antineutrinos. A boosted radioactive-ion beam gives directional electron-flavour flux. Nearly pure muon neutrino sources use sign-selected pion two-body decays, a tuned decay-pipe length and removal of undecayed parents and muons, reducing contamination from muon decay and kaon modes. Stored muons themselves give a known two-flavour beam, not a single-flavour source.

The Goldhaber experiment inferred neutrino helicity from the circular polarization of a nuclear gamma ray. A spin-zero metastable europium-152 nucleus captures an inner-shell electron, producing a spin-one excited samarium-152 daughter and an electron neutrino; the daughter promptly emits a roughly 960keV gamma ray to its spin-zero ground state. For a nearly stationary initial atom, the daughter recoils opposite the neutrino. Selecting photons emitted along that recoil relates the daughter spin projection, the photon helicity and the neutrino helicity.
The selection used resonant absorption and scattering in samarium. Nuclear recoil normally shifts an emitted gamma ray below the absorption resonance; the daughter recoil from electron capture supplies a compensating forward Doppler shift. Resonant scattering therefore preferentially selects forward photons, fixing their direction relative to the otherwise invisible neutrino. A samarium scatterer and scintillation counting identify this resonant component. Transmission through magnetized iron, whose Compton scattering depends on the relative electron and photon spins, then analyzes the circular polarization. Reversing the magnetization reverses the analyzing asymmetry and helps control instrumental offsets.
The selected photon and neutrino normalized helicities have the same sign: their momenta are opposite, while the daughter angular-momentum accounting gives opposite spin projections along the recoil axis. The observed negative photon helicity therefore establishes predominantly left-helicity neutrinos, consistent with the V-A interaction. It measures a helicity correlation, not a tiny neutrino mass or a literal observation of the neutral particle's trajectory.[ref]

A general-purpose collider detector surrounds the interaction region with complementary layers. The layout makes the measured signals useful together: tracks establish charged trajectories, calorimeters sum deposited energy, and outer detectors identify penetrating muons.
  • An inner vertex detector, usually fine silicon pixels or strips, measures precise hits and displaced decay vertices. A larger tracking volume follows charged trajectories through a magnetic field. Curvature supplies transverse momentum and charge sign; hit geometry and known field determine the track. Neutral particles leave no direct ionization track.
  • An electromagnetic calorimeter, using dense crystals or absorber/sampling layers, measures electrons and photons through electromagnetic particle showers. Electrons have matching tracks; unconverted photons ordinarily do not. Segmentation provides position and shower-shape discrimination.
  • A deeper hadronic calorimeter samples hadronic showers from hadrons and measures jet energy. Its larger depth contains nuclear cascades; invisible binding losses, leakage and differing electromagnetic/hadronic response require calibration.
  • An external muon detector, behind most of the absorber, records long penetrating tracks. Matching them to inner tracks identifies muons; magnetic instrumentation can give an additional momentum measurement. Hadron punch-through and decays in flight are backgrounds.
  • Dedicated particle identification uses specific ionization, time of flight, Cherenkov radiation or transition radiation to distinguish species over appropriate momentum ranges. The choice depends on the collider and physics goals.
  • Forward detectors and luminosity monitors cover small polar angles and constrain normalization. Fast trigger and data-acquisition systems select useful collisions within bandwidth limits while recording efficiencies and timing.
Near-hermetic coverage permits a missing transverse momentum estimate from the calibrated vector sum, signaling neutrinos or other invisible particles but also sensitive to detector failures and escaping jets. At a hadron collider the initial parton longitudinal momenta are unknown, so missing longitudinal energy cannot be inferred in the same way; an e+e− collider supplies stronger initial-state constraints. Every subsystem needs alignment, calibration and independent control samples for reliable combined reconstruction.
