The long-lived, electrically neutral kaon often traverses the particle detector without decaying into a reconstructable charged final state. Identifying it through a hadronic interaction or missing four-momentum is much less efficient than reconstructing the short-lived kaon through its nearby two-pion decay vertex. The efficiencies must be applied to the observed counts before taking their asymmetry:
NS​=0.931107​=1190.32,NL​=0.0549​=980,R=NS​+NL​NS​−NL​​=0.09691.
(13)
Using the leading aligned-amplitude approximation from c,
θC​=arctanR/2​=0.21667 rad=12.41∘.​
(14)
The exact aligned formula instead gives 12.43∘, an immaterial correction here. Treating the observed counts as independent Poisson random variables and the efficiencies as known, propagation of uncertainty gives
σS​=0.931107​​=35.78,σL​=0.0549​​=140,
(15)
σR2​=[(NS​+NL​)22NL​​]2σS2​+[(NS​+NL​)22NS​​]2σL2​,σR​=0.0723.​
(16)
The separate contributions to σR​ are 0.0149 and 0.0708; the small KL​ sample dominates. Linear propagation of uncertainty yields σ(θC​)≃4.49∘, although the nonlinear extraction and physical boundary make a symmetric confidence interval a rough summary. Unknown efficiency uncertainties, especially that of KL​, would be additional systematic contributions.
The supplied CKM matrix gives sinθC​≃0.224, hence θC​≃12.94∘ and R≃2(0.224/0.975)2=0.1056. The asymmetry difference is only about 0.12 statistical standard deviations, so the measurement is compatible. Without the phase assumption, these data measure tan2θC​cosδ to leading order and do not uniquely measure the Cabibbo angle.
