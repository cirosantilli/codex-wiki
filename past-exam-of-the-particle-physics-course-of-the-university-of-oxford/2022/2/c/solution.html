The stated magnitude assumption fixes ∣r∣=tan2θC​. Retaining the relative strong phase δ in the convention of b therefore gives
R=1+tan4θC​2tan2θC​cosδ​=2tan2θC​cosδ+O(tan6θC​).​
(12)
A magnitude assumption alone does not determine R. For example, r=itan2θC​ has the stipulated magnitude but gives R=0, whereas a real positive r gives a positive asymmetry. With the additional aligned-amplitude assumption δ=0, conventionally used for this idealised extraction, the intended leading result is R≃2tan2θC​​. The sign of r here refers to the complete amplitude after choosing the kaon phase convention, not to a bare CKM matrix product. A flavour-symmetry relation can supply further information, but is stronger than the printed magnitude assumption.
