Away from narrow resonances and quark thresholds, the QED annihilation cross section into each accessible quark flavour is its squared fractional electric charge times the muon-pair cross section, multiplied by the number Nc​ of distinguishable color charges. Thus the inclusive hadronic ratio is
Rhad​=σ(e+e−→μ+μ−)σ(e+e−→hadrons)​≃Nc​∑f​Qf2​.
(20)
With Nc​=3, the continuum predictions are Ruds​=2, Rudsc​=10/3 and Rudscb​=11/3 as successive charm quark and bottom quark channels become accessible. The measured normalization and steps support three color charges. At leading order these hadronic events form two back-to-back jets from the quark and antiquark; quantitative comparisons include QCD corrections and avoid the nonperturbative threshold region.
The extra resolved jet is explained by e+e−→γ∗→qqˉ​g, with gluon radiation from either the quark or antiquark. One possible color charge routing is initial qr​qˉ​rˉ​ followed by qr​→qb​+grbˉ​; the final qb​, qˉ​rˉ​ and grbˉ​ combine to a net colour singlet.
The three-jet cross section contains one additional strong interaction vertex in the amplitude, so its ratio to the two-jet rate is of order αs​=gs2​/(4π) times a resolution-dependent kinematic coefficient. A resolved fraction of order 15% is consistent with perturbative QCD and a strong coupling constant much larger than the fine-structure constant. It is not an equality αs​=0.15: the jet definition and cuts are needed. Photon radiation has a much smaller electromagnetic rate. The large hadronic third-jet rate supports gluon radiation; identifying the radiated object specifically as a colour-octet gluon also uses the QCD vertex and colour-flow description, not merely the existence of three visible jets.
