(ω2ϕ′)′=0,ω2ϕ′=C.

The conventional Courant-Snyder parameters normalize the phase so that C=1; any positive constant C can be absorbed into the scale assigned to β and ϵ. Explicitly, β​=β/C and ϵ=Cϵ preserve the orbit while making β​ϕ′=1. With this convention,
ϕ′=β1​,ω′′−ω31​+k(s)ω=0.​

The second expression is the Ermakov-Pinney equation for the beam envelope. It follows by substituting ϕ′=1/ω2 into the cosine coefficient.
The printed equation omits the factor ω multiplying k(s). For a concrete counterexample, take the constant periodic focusing strength k=1/16 and the constant envelope ω=2. Then ϕ′=1/4 and z=2ϵ​cos(s/4−ϕ0​) solve z′′+z/16=0. The correct envelope equation is 0−1/8+(1/16)2=0, whereas the printed equation gives −1/8+1/16=−1/16.
The associated invariant is
ϵ=γT​z2+2αT​zz′+βz′2,αT​=−β′/2,γT​=(1+αT2​)/β.

Thus ϵ fixes the phase-space ellipse of a trajectory; a beam's rms geometric emittance uses the corresponding second-moment convention. Factors of π depend on whether emittance means ellipse area or area divided by π and do not alter the envelope equation.
