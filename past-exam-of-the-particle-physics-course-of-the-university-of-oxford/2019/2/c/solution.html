For a beam with rms geometric emittance ϵy​, its vertical size at the collision point is σy⋆​=ϵy​βy⋆​​. Reducing βy⋆​ therefore concentrates the beam, increasing the number of particles encountering the opposite beam per transverse area. For identical Gaussian beams colliding head-on,
L=4πσx⋆​σy⋆​nb​frev​N1​N2​​.
(11)
Keep bunch populations, horizontal size, collision frequency and emittances fixed. If both vertical beta values are decreased by a factor f>1, meaning βy,new⋆​=βy,old⋆​/f, both vertical beam sizes decrease by 1/f​, and
Lold​Lnew​​=f​.​
(12)
The luminosity is inversely proportional to the common vertical size, not its square, because only one transverse dimension has changed. The same ratio follows from the unequal-beam overlap denominator σy12​+σy22​​.
A final-focus quadrupole system on either side of the interaction point forms a waist there. Strong quadrupole doublets or triplets, with suitable matching sections, focus both planes. At a symmetric waist β′=0 and the nearby drift gives β(s)=β⋆+s2/β⋆, so small β⋆ requires large beam envelopes and strong optical gradients in the adjacent magnets. Chromatic correction is needed for off-momentum particles. The formula assumes negligible crossing-angle and hourglass reductions; a bunch length comparable to β⋆ changes the effective overlap.
