For a paraxial orbit z′≃pz​/ps​, where pz​ is transverse momentum and ps​ longitudinal momentum. Acceleration mainly increases ps​, so a given transverse kick produces a smaller angular deviation. In an ideal matched accelerator without scattering or radiation, the conserved transverse canonical phase-space area becomes a smaller area in the coordinates (z,z′). This is adiabatic damping of geometric emittance, not loss of canonical phase-space volume.
The invariant normalized emittance is
ϵn​=βrel​γrel​ϵ=mps​​ϵ,ϵ∝ps−1​.​
(13)
Here βrel​ is speed in units of c, distinct from the lattice beta function. Neglecting dilution mechanisms, each acceleration stage thus supplies a beam with smaller angular spread and geometric emittance for injection into the next stage.
A chain of increasingly energetic particle accelerators lets each ring operate over a manageable range of magnetic rigidity and radio-frequency settings. It also avoids injecting a large-emittance low-energy beam directly into the small-aperture high-field main ring. Higher injection energy reduces space-charge effects and improves capture and transmission. Emittance dilution, mismatching and collective instabilities can oppose the ideal damping; acceleration alone does not reduce normalized emittance.
