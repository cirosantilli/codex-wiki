The observed dominance of kaons supports a predominantly ssˉ phi meson. In a connected strong decay, the original strange quark and antiquark survive into different kaons, while a gluon produces a light qqˉ​ pair:
ssˉ⟶(sqˉ​)(qsˉ),q=u,d.
(20)
This gives K−K+ or Kˉ0K0 and is allowed by the OZI rule. A pionic final state contains no strange valence constituents, so the initial ssˉ pair must annihilate. The strong annihilation of a colour-singlet 1−− state requires at least three gluons: one gluon cannot carry the singlet colour, and two have the wrong charge-conjugation quantum number. This disconnected flavour topology is suppressed by the OZI rule, although it need not vanish.
The leptonic mode proceeds through ssˉ→γ∗→ℓ+ℓ− and is suppressed by electromagnetic couplings; its width is proportional to α2 times the vector-meson overlap. Unlike a strong hadronic decay, it cannot create its final particles through quark rearrangement. Hence a branching fraction of order 10−4 is qualitatively consistent with electromagnetic annihilation competing with strong modes.
Kaon dominance is especially suggestive because phase space is limited: using the supplied neutral-kaon and phi masses,
pK​=4mϕ2​​−mK02​​≃110 MeV,mϕ​−2mK0​=24 MeV.​
(21)
The two spin-zero kaons have orbital angular momentum L=1 to match the vector parent, so their partial width has the additional near-threshold factor pK3​. Pionic modes have more phase space yet remain subdominant because of their annihilation topology. The observations therefore support, but do not establish exact, ideal vector-meson mixing: a small nonstrange admixture, electromagnetic amplitudes and nonperturbative strong dynamics can all contribute to the pionic rate.
