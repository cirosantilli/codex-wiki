Quark mass differences break flavour symmetry. Isospin exchanges u,d, so its strong breaking is controlled by the small difference md​−mu​. U-spin exchanges d,s, so its strong breaking involves the much larger ms​−md​. Relative to the characteristic strong-interaction scale, the latter is much larger; this explains why U-spin is a substantially poorer symmetry.
Flavour-dependent interactions are a second source. Electromagnetism distinguishes u from d because Qu​=2/3 and Qd​=−1/3, and therefore breaks isospin. However, d,s both have charge −1/3, so their electromagnetic couplings alone do not break U-spin in the equal-mass limit. If two breaking mechanisms for each symmetry are required, the second common mechanism is the weak interaction: the CKM matrix and weak flavour transitions distinguish the flavours and do not conserve either strong-interaction flavour symmetry. The dominant difference in the quality of the two approximate strong symmetries is still the mass splitting.
