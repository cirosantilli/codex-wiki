The Holstein–Primakoff transformation realizes a spin algebra using bosonic creation operators and bosonic annihilation operators. A boson counts a unit of spin deviation from the local positive quantization axis: Sz=S−n with n=a†a. The square root in S+ supplies the correct spin-ladder matrix elements. In particular,
S+∣n⟩=n(2S−n+1)​∣n−1⟩,S−∣n⟩=(n+1)(2S−n)​∣n+1⟩.
(14)
Their commutator is 2(S−n) on every physical number state, reproducing the Spin commutation relations. Although the oscillator Fock space is infinite, the physical subspace is only
n=0,1,…,2S.​
(15)
The order of the square root and oscillator operator matters in obtaining these coefficients.
When the spin deviations are dilute, expand the square root in n/(2S). The leading transverse operators are linear in the bosonic creation operators and bosonic annihilation operators and turn exchange interactions into a quadratic Hamiltonian, which can be diagonalized. Higher powers describe interactions between spin waves. The method therefore organizes corrections by inverse powers of S about an ordered classical configuration. Large S alone is insufficient: the average depletion must remain small compared with S. Possible problems include the occupation constraint after truncation, noncollinear order requiring site-dependent axis rotations, zero modes, and infrared fluctuations that destroy the assumed dilute expansion.

This is a birth-death master equation: qn​ is the transition rate n→n+1 and rn​ is the transition rate n→n−1. The first two terms are incoming probability flows from the adjacent states; the last removes probability at the total escape rate qn​+rn​. Rates must be nonnegative. For a population on the nonnegative integers, take r0​=0 and no incoming flux from negative states.
Summing over all states and shifting the two incoming indices gives
dtd​∑n​Pn​=∑n​rn​Pn​+∑n​qn​Pn​−∑n​(qn​+rn​)Pn​=0.
(29)
Thus an initially normalized probability distribution remains normalized. The cancellation assumes no boundary flux and legitimate summation; on an infinite state space this means the nonexplosive process or a boundary prescription that conserves total probability. All rates used in the later parts satisfy this requirement.

For longitudinal-vector annihilation through a scalar contact interaction, first use the supplied vertex 2ig2ημν​. Let s=(p1​+p2​)2 and work in the center-of-mass frame. The amplitude is M=2g2ϵ1​⋅ϵ2​, up to its overall phase. For two longitudinal incoming vectors,
ϵL​(p1​)⋅ϵL​(p2​)=mA2​p1​⋅p2​​=2mA2​s−2mA2​​,∣MLL​∣2=g4mA4​(s−2mA2​)2​.
(53)
This grows as s2/mA4​, whereas transverse amplitudes stay of order g2 and mixed longitudinal-transverse amplitudes vanish in this frame. Consequently the leading high-energy contribution is longitudinal.
For an unpolarized total cross section, average over the 3×3 incoming polarizations. The massive-vector polarization sum gives
∑i,j​∣ϵi​(p1​)⋅ϵj​(p2​)∣2=2+mA4​(p1​⋅p2​)2​.
(54)
The identical final scalars require a factor 1/2! in the two-body phase space. With βA​=1−4mA2​/s​ and βh​=1−4mh2​/s​, the angle-independent result is
σunpol​(s)=βA​βh​​288πsg4​[mA4​(s−2mA2​)2​+8]s≫m2∼​288πmA4​g4s​.​
(55)
For a prepared longitudinal pair there is no incoming polarization average, giving instead
σLL​(s)∼32πmA4​g4s​=9σunpol​(s).​
(56)
There is no extra initial-state factor 1/2: a cross section uses the incoming beam flux, while the final-state factor removes double counting of indistinguishable outgoing states.
Define the energy in the stated bound to be the total center-of-mass energy, E=s​. Equating the leading expressions to 4π/E2 gives
Eunpol​=(1152π2)1/4gmA​​≃2.58 TeV,ELL​=(128π2)1/4gmA​​≃1.49 TeV,​
(57)
for mA​=100 GeV and g=0.4. The second value shows that a longitudinal channel reaches the supplied perturbative bound earlier. If the source's unspecified E denotes the energy of one incoming particle and the bound is literally 4π/E2, substitute s=4E2 instead: the corresponding values are 1.83 TeV unpolarized and 1.05 TeV longitudinal. Stating the energy and polarization conventions is necessary for a numerical answer.
Interaction-normalization qualification: the written Lint​=g2Aμ​Aμh2 differentiates to 4ig2ημν​, because there are 2! vector assignments and 2! scalar assignments. For example, differentiating g2(A0)2h2 twice in each variable gives 4g2. The supplied 2ig2ημν​ would instead follow from Lint​=g2Aμ​Aμh2/2. Thus all cross sections above follow the explicitly requested rule. Using the literal written interaction multiplies them by four and divides every quoted threshold energy by 2​. The energy growth itself is the physical issue: a contact interaction of massive longitudinal vectors needs additional dynamics before its perturbative high-energy extrapolation becomes reliable.

Introduce three independent local parameters αj​(x). A consistent convention for the three Abelian gauge transformations is
Bμj​⟼Bμj​−g1​∂μ​αj​,ϕ1​⟼ei(α1​−qα2​)ϕ1​,ϕ2​⟼ei(α2​−qα3​)ϕ2​.​
(58)
Indeed, g(Bμk​−qBμk+1​) shifts by −∂μ​(αk​−qαk+1​), cancelling the derivative of the scalar phase. Thus Dμ​ϕk​ transforms by the same phase as ϕk​. The two kinetic terms, the absolute-value potentials and all Abelian field strengths are invariant.
In units where the gauge coupling is g, the charge vectors under the three factors are
Q(ϕ1​)=(1,−q,0),Q(ϕ2​)=(0,1,−q).​
(59)
Locally this is the gauge algebra of U(1)3. To interpret it globally as three compact circle groups, the charges must belong to a compatible charge lattice: in the stated unit-charge convention integer q is sufficient, while arbitrary irrational q is not a single-valued compact-group representation. The local transformations and mass calculation remain meaningful for general positive real q as a Lie-algebra calculation, or with noncompact Abelian factors.

For this three-site Abelian Higgs chain, the potential is minimized by ∣ϕ1​∣=∣ϕ2​∣=v. Choose their phases real in a local unitary gauge. The scalar kinetic terms then give
Lmass​=g2v2[(B1−qB2)2+(B2−qB3)2]=g2v2BTKB,
(60)
where Lorentz contractions are understood and
K=RTR=​1−q0​−q1+q2−q​0−qq2​​,R=(10​−q1​0−q​).
(61)
The nonzero eigenvalues of K equal those of
RRT=(1+q2−q​−q1+q2​),
(62)
namely 1+q2−q and 1+q2+q. The third eigenvalue is zero because R has rank two. Both nonzero eigenvalues are positive for q>0.
Mass-normalization correction: the canonical vector mass term is 21​BTM2B, so the Lagrangian actually gives M2=2g2v2K. Its physical masses are therefore
m1​=0,m2​=2​gvq2−q+1​,m3​=2​gvq2+q+1​.​
(63)
The printed dimensionless eigenvalue pattern is correct, but its common scale must be m=2​gv, not gv, for the written kinetic terms and vacuum value. For a concrete counterexample set q=1 and B=(A,0,−A)/2​. This is a canonically normalized eigenmode and its mass term is g2v2Aμ​Aμ, giving physical squared mass 2g2v2 rather than the printed g2v2. The printed scale gv would require a vacuum modulus v/2​ or a corresponding change in scalar normalization, neither of which is the specified potential minimum.
The unbroken transformations preserve both vacuum phases:
α1​−qα2​=0,α2​−qα3​=0,(α1​,α2​,α3​)=α(q2,q,1).
(64)
Thus the local breaking pattern is
U(1)3⟶U(1).​
(65)
Two scalar phases supply the longitudinal polarizations of the two massive vector bosons; one massless vector remains. This is the Higgs mechanism, with its mass matrix fixed by the scalar charge matrix and the vacuum normalization.[ref]

A massless eigenvector must lie in the null space of R, so its coefficients satisfy c1​=qc2​ and c2​=qc3​. Hence they are proportional to (q2,q,1). The original gauge kinetic terms have the Euclidean unit matrix in species space, so normalize this vector by its ordinary squared length. The massless field is
A1μ​=q4+q2+1​q2B1μ​+qB2μ​+B3μ​​.​
(66)
The remaining two eigenvectors can be chosen orthogonal to it, making the full transformation orthogonal and preserving canonical vector kinetic terms. At q=1, the formula is exactly (B1μ​+B2μ​+B3μ​)/3​ as required. This null direction and its normalization are unaffected by the common mass-scale correction in part b.
For q>1, the massless mode has geometrically unequal components; a matter field coupled to one end can therefore couple more weakly to this surviving vector than a field coupled to the other end. This follows directly from the eigenvector coefficients, without changing the equal microscopic gauge coupling g.
