Take a=a0​(T−Tc​) and hold b,c fixed. The heat capacity comes from the minimized free energy, C=−Td2Fmin​/dT2, with any smooth background added separately. For b>0 and T<Tc​,
Fmin​−Freg​=−4ba2​+O(a3),Csing,−​=2bTa02​​+O(Tc​−T).
(76)
Above Tc​, m=0 and the polynomial contributes zero. There is a finite jump, rather than a divergence: the conventional ordinary mean-field critical exponents are
α−​=α+​=0(b>0),ΔC=2bTc​a02​​.​
(77)
The upper branch has zero order-parameter contribution; a finite regular heat capacity has exponent zero.
Along the tuned tricritical path b=0, the ordered minimum gives
Fmin​−Freg​=−3c​(−a)3/2​,Csing,−​=4c​−a​Ta02​​=4c​Ta03/2​​(Tc​−T)−1/2,α−tri​=21​.​
(78)
Upper-side qualification: the vanishing disordered-side heat-capacity amplitude in Landau theory matters here: for b=0 and a>0, the minimizing solution is still exactly m=0, so this saddle-point polynomial gives Fmin​−Freg​=0 and Csing,+​=0. A zero function cannot determine a nonzero-amplitude power-law exponent. For example, adding the allowed smooth background Freg​=−C0​Tlog(T/T∗​), with C0​>0, leaves the entire phase diagram unchanged but makes the upper-side heat capacity exactly C0​, not a (T−Tc​)−1/2 divergence.
One convention assigns the tricritical scaling exponent α=1/2 to both sides, writing Csing,±​=A±​∣T−Tc​∣−1/2 with A+​=0 at this mean-field approximation saddle. If the source's literal C∼∣T−Tc​∣−α+​ is meant to have a nonzero leading coefficient, the given polynomial establishes no upper-side tricritical singular exponent; with a nonzero smooth background its leading upper-side exponent is zero. A nonzero upper-side singularity requires fluctuation information beyond this minimization. This distinction prevents claiming an upper-side divergence that the displayed Landau free energy does not produce.
