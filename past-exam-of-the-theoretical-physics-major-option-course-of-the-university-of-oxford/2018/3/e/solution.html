The massless two-body kinematics gives q1​⋅q2​=M2/2. The scalar scattering amplitude therefore has constant magnitude ∣Aaa​∣=∣λa​∣M2/2. Use ∫dΩ=4π and the identical-particle factor from part d:
Γaa​=2!1​64π2M4π​4∣λa​∣2M4​=128π∣λa​∣2M3​.​
(27)
For the photons, the two-photon vertex of a scalar field-strength interaction following from ΦFμν​Fμν is, up to an irrelevant overall phase,
Aγγ​=λγ​[(q1​⋅q2​)(ϵ1​⋅ϵ2​)−(q1​⋅ϵ2​)(q2​⋅ϵ1​)].
(28)
Vertex qualification: the source prints same-index polarization contractions in the second product, whereas the contractions must be crossed as above. The printed expression fails the Ward identity. For a concrete counterexample take q1​=(E,0,0,E), q2​=(E,0,0,−E) and ϵ2​=(1,1,0,−1). This is a transverse unit spacelike photon polarization vector, since q2​⋅ϵ2​=0 and ϵ22​=−1. Replacing ϵ1​ by q1​ in the printed expression leaves 4λγ​E3 instead of zero. The corrected crossed expression vanishes under either such replacement.
Choose physical spatial transverse photon polarizations along x and y in the parent rest frame. Then both crossed products vanish; the two equal-polarization combinations have ∣ϵ1​⋅ϵ2​∣2=1 and the two orthogonal combinations give zero. There is no initial-spin average for a spin-zero parent. Hence
∑pol​∣Aγγ​∣2=2∣λγ​∣2(2M2​)2=2∣λγ​∣2M4​,Γγγ​=64π∣λγ​∣2M3​.​
(29)
The calculation in this spatial transverse gauge also yields the intended result from the misprinted expression, but only the corrected vertex makes its gauge invariance explicit. For equal-magnitude couplings, the photon decay width is twice the two-scalar decay width because of its two nonzero polarization combinations.
