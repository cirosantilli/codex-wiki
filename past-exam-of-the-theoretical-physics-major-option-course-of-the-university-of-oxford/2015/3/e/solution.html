Specify the Fourier transform before performing the Gaussian integral:
mj​(q)=∫d3xe−iq⋅xmj​(x),mj​(x)=∫(2π)3d3q​eiq⋅xmj​(q).
(24)
At u=0 the functional is diagonal in both component and momentum, with inverse kernels q2+t+r, q2+t−r, and q2+t. The condition 0<r<t makes all three positive. The covariance of a Gaussian distribution with statistical weight e−F/(kB​T) is kB​T times the inverse kernel, hence
⟨m1​(q)m1​(p)⟩⟨m2​(q)m2​(p)⟩​=(2π)3δ(3)(q+p)q2+t+rkB​T​,=(2π)3δ(3)(q+p)q2+t−rkB​T​.​​
(25)
If F is already the dimensionless free energy divided by kB​T, the numerators are one instead. Translational invariance forces opposite momenta to pair. There is no complex conjugate on the second field, so the delta function is at p=−q, not at p=q. A correlation with mj​(p)∗ would have the latter convention. Rotational symmetry in real space leaves dependence on q2 alone once the momentum-conservation delta function is factored out. The correlation lengths are ξ1​=(t+r)−1/2 and ξ2​=(t−r)−1/2; the second component becomes soft as r↑t. The skipped letter in the source is preserved: this solution is part (e), with no inferred part (d).
