Keep the index convention of b: Aμ=(A0​,A), so Aμ​=(A0​,−A) and the spatial gauge covariant derivative is ∇+ieA. The physical three-current is the spatial part of Jμ:
j=ie(φ∗∇φ−φ∇φ∗)−2e2∣φ∣2A.​
(72)
Equivalently, writing φ=Reiθ gives j=−2eR2(∇θ+eA). Under the gauge transformation in a, θ↦θ+α and A↦A−∇α/e, so this expression is manifestly invariant. Switching to a convention with the opposite spatial gauge sign reverses the phase-gradient term, not the screening term proportional to e2.
For static fields the spatial Ampère-Maxwell equation is ∇×B=j, while ∇⋅B=0. Taking its curl yields the general magnetic-field equation
∇2B=−∇×j=−2ie∇φ∗×∇φ+2e2∇∣φ∣2×A+2e2∣φ∣2B.​
(73)
No uniform-condensate approximation has yet been used. For a varying scalar amplitude the gradient terms must be kept; simply replacing ∣φ∣2 by a constant before deriving the current would conceal their origin. The homogeneous-condensate limit supplies the London screening equation in d.
