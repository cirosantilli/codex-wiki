Use one common ultraviolet cutoff for all physical degrees of freedom, with positive sign for each bosonic degree of freedom and negative sign for each fermionic degree of freedom. For the mass-moment cancellation of vacuum energy divergences, define the weighted supertrace Strf(m)=∑B​f(mB​)−∑F​f(mF​). The large-momentum expansion is
∑B​p2+mB2​​−∑F​p2+mF2​​=pStr1+2pStrm2​−8p3Strm4​+O(p−5).
(10)
After multiplication by the radial measure p2dp, these three terms produce quartic, quadratic and logarithmic ultraviolet divergences. Therefore the necessary and sufficient cancellations for the displayed unsubtracted finite-species vacuum energy are
Str1=0,Strm2=0,Strm4=0.​
(11)
They respectively mean equal numbers of bosonic and fermionic physical degrees of freedom, equal sums of squared masses, and equal sums of fourth powers of masses. Once they hold, the remaining radial integrand falls as p−3 and is integrable at infinity. Nonnegative masses introduce no lower-end divergence.
Pairing every bosonic degree of freedom with a fermionic one of the same mass, as in an unbroken supersymmetric spectrum, is sufficient and cancels the entire expression. It is stronger than these three moment conditions. For a concrete non-paired example, take bosonic squared masses {0,3,3}m∗2​ and fermionic squared masses {1,1,4}m∗2​. Both have three degrees of freedom, squared-mass sum 6m∗2​ and fourth-power-mass sum 18m∗4​, but their spectra differ. If a vacuum energy counterterm is instead permitted, a renormalized finite answer need not satisfy them; that is a different prescription from finiteness of the source's bare sum.

Goldstone's theorem states that a spontaneously broken continuous internal global symmetry in field theory of a local relativistic quantum field theory produces massless Goldstone bosons. For a Lorentz invariance vacuum breaking G to its stabilizer H, there are dimG−dimH independent symmetry-required massless directions. Additional accidentally massless modes are possible. A gauge redundancy does not obey this physical-state counting, and nonrelativistic systems need not have one independent mode per broken generator.
For the classical proof, consider real scalar fields ϕi​ with a positive canonical kinetic term and an invariant smooth scalar potential V. An infinitesimal generator Ta satisfies
∂ϕi​∂V​(Taϕ)i​=0.
(12)
Let v be a stationary vacuum and differentiate this identity with respect to ϕj​. At v, the term proportional to ∂i​V vanishes, leaving
Mji2​(Tav)i​=0,Mji2​=∂ϕj​∂ϕi​∂2V​​v​.​
(13)
The Hessian matrix M2 is the scalar mass matrix. An unbroken generator has Tav=0; broken generators span the tangent space of the vacuum manifold G/H. Each independent nonzero tangent vector is a zero eigenvector of M2. The quadratic Euler-Lagrange equations in these directions are □πa=0, giving the massless dispersion relation ω2=p2. This proves the classical result and explains why the Goldstone bosons describe motion along the degenerate minima rather than against a restoring curvature.

Both the canonical kinetic term and V(ϕTϕ) are invariant under the orthogonal group O(n). The stated nonzero vacuum expectation value has length ∣v∣. Its stabilizer is the orthogonal group O(n−1) acting on the perpendicular subspace. Hence
NGB​=2n(n−1)​−2(n−1)(n−2)​=n−1.​
(14)

Explicitly, with s=ϕTϕ,
M2=2V′(v2)I+4V′′(v2)vvT.
(15)
stationary point at nonzero v requires V′(v2)=0. The n−1 transverse eigenvalues vanish, while the radial squared mass is 4v2V′′(v2). If this curvature also vanishes, there is an additional accidental massless radial mode; the number of symmetry-protected Goldstone bosons remains n−1. If v=0, the symmetry is unbroken and none is required.

The breaking of the internal global symmetry in field theory SU(n)×SU(n) to SU(n) removes 2(n2−1)−(n2−1) generators. The relativistic Goldstone theorem therefore requires
n2−1 massless Goldstone bosons.​
(19)
Other states fall into multiplets of the unbroken special unitary group, but their masses are not fixed by this counting. For the usual diagonal embedding SU(n)V​⊂SU(n)L​×SU(n)R​, the Goldstone bosons form its adjoint multiplet.
If small explicit symmetry-breaking terms are added, the broken-generator directions generally acquire small curvature, producing light pseudo-Goldstone bosons rather than exactly massless states. Their squared masses tend to zero with those breaking parameters. Small breaking can also split multiplet degeneracies. These claims concern a small perturbation of the broken phase; approximate symmetry alone does not establish which vacuum is selected.

The light states have the counting 3+4+1=8. This suggests approximate chiral symmetry breaking
SU(3)L​×SU(3)R​⟶SU(3)V​,2(32−1)−(32−1)=8,​
(20)
with a nonzero quark chiral condensate ⟨qˉ​Rj​qLi​⟩∝δij for the three light flavours u,d,s. The three pions, four kaons and predominantly octet eta meson are the resulting pseudo-Goldstone bosons. Their small nonzero masses reflect explicit chiral symmetry breaking by the light-quark masses; for example, mπ2​ is proportional to mu​+md​ at leading order. The larger strange-quark mass makes the kaons and octet eta meson heavier than the pions, while electromagnetic interactions help split charged and neutral partners.[ref]
Although the massless strong-interaction terms admit independent rotations of all six flavours, the charm, bottom and top masses are too large to treat six-flavour chiral symmetry as a small explicit breaking at the light-hadron scale. Full approximate SU(6)L​×SU(6)R​ breaking would suggest 35 light modes, rather than this octet. The classical axial U(1) is also broken by the chiral anomaly, so it does not supply a ninth equally light Goldstone boson.
Terminology qualification: these mesons have spin zero but negative parity: they are pseudoscalar mesons, with JP=0−, rather than positive-parity scalar particles with JP=0+. Calling the listed states “scalar” is acceptable only if it is being used loosely to mean spin zero.

The action is dimensionless and [d4x]=−4, so [L]=4. A canonical scalar kinetic term then requires 2+2[ϕ]=4, and the electromagnetic kinetic term requires 2[Fμν​]=4. Thus
[Φ]=[a]=[b]=1,[Fμν​]=2.​
(21)
The brackets here denote mass dimension. The Lagrangian has no explicit coordinate dependence, and all indices are contracted as Lorentz scalars. Its continuous spacetime symmetry is the Poincaré group. Noether's theorem gives conserved four-momentum from translations and conserved angular momentum, including the boost generators, from Lorentz transformations. The nonzero mass M and dimensionful couplings prevent a dilation symmetry.
Sign qualification: the printed electromagnetic kinetic term has the opposite healthy relative sign to the scalar kinetic terms. With the stated mostly-minus Minkowski metric, a physical Maxwell Lagrangian is −Fμν​Fμν/4. The physical decay widths below use the healthy Maxwell sign with the given interaction normalization. The dimensional and spacetime-symmetry conclusions themselves do not depend on this sign correction.

Every interaction contains an even number of b fields, so the exact discrete symmetry b↦−b forbids Φ→bbb to every perturbative order. There is no such prohibition for Φ→bb.
The latter decay channel is absent at tree level in the displayed interactions, but contracting two b legs at a Φb2(∂b)2 vertex produces a tadpole diagram with two external b legs. For example, contracting the undifferentiated pair leaves the local operator Φ(∂b)2 multiplied by an integral proportional to ∫d4ℓ/(ℓ2+i0). Contracting the differentiated pair instead gives a local Φb2 contribution. Both are allowed effective field theory operators. With a ultraviolet cutoff, these local terms generically carry quadratic or quartic cutoff dependence and require matching coefficients or counterterms.
There is a relevant qualification to a claim that the one-loop decay width must be nonzero. Since b is exactly massless here, the one-vertex tadpole integrals have no external scale and are scaleless integrals. They vanish in dimensional regularization. Thus the correct conclusion is allowed, and generically generated by quantum corrections or matching, not a regulator-independent nonzero one-loop prediction from λb​ alone. No exact symmetry protects the two-b channel; its numerical rate requires the renormalized coefficients of the allowed operators.

The nonzero residual entropy comes from an exponentially large ground-state degeneracy, generated by geometrical frustration. A triangle with antiferromagnetic energy J[st+(s+t)τ] has minimum energy −J: if s=t, the apex must have the opposite sign; if s=−t, either apex sign is permitted. The two all-equal configurations instead have energy 3J. Thus exactly one of a minimum-energy triangle's bonds is frustrated. The bridge can simultaneously minimize its energy at −J by requiring t=−s′, where s′ is the next cell's first base spin.
For fixed s and s′, the number of allowed apex choices is therefore two when s=s′ and one when s=−s′. The zero-temperature counting transfer matrix on successive first base spins is

Choose a coarse-grained magnetization m(x) as the order parameter: it vanishes in the paramagnetism phase and develops a spontaneous nonzero value in the ferromagnetism phase. In zero applied magnetic field, spin reversal sends m↦−m, so the local Landau free energy contains only even powers. A minimal spatially inhomogeneous free-energy functional is
F[m]=Freg​(T)+∫ddx[2a(T)​m2+4b​m4+6c​m6+2κ​∣∇m∣2−hm+⋯],κ>0.
(66)
The positive gradient coefficient penalizes rapid spatial variations. Near an ordinary transition take a(T)=a0​(T−T0​), a0​>0, and positive b; retain the sextic term when b can approach or cross zero. The source h is conjugate to the magnetization and explicitly breaks spin reversal.
Determine equilibrium by minimizing this free-energy functional, imposing the appropriate boundary conditions. Its Euler-Lagrange equation is
−κ∇2m+a(T)m+bm3+cm5−h=0.​
(67)
For a uniform system at h=0, compare the values of all stable stationary solutions, rather than accepting every solution of this equation. The selected global minimum gives the equilibrium magnetization; derivatives of its free energy give entropy, heat capacity and magnetic susceptibility. At an ordinary transition the two ordered minima emerge continuously from zero. Spatial dependence also permits domain walls and an estimate of the correlation length.
This is a mean-field approximation: it selects a minimizing profile and neglects statistical fluctuations around it. The symmetry constrains the expansion, but its numerical coefficients are phenomenological. Close enough to a transition, thermal fluctuations near a critical point can invalidate the resulting mean-field critical exponents.[ref]
