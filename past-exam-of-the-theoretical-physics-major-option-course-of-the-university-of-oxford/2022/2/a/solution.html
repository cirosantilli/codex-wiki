The continuous internal symmetry is a common rotation around z:
Sj±​⟼e±iθSj±​,Sjz​⟼Sjz​.
(10)
Both the transverse exchange and the Dzyaloshinskii-Moriya interaction contain one raising and one lowering spin operator, so the phases cancel. Equivalently, [H,∑j​Sjz​]=0 and the continuous group is U(1).
The discrete internal symmetry is time reversal, TSj​T−1=−Sj​, together with TiT−1=−i. Each term in H contains two spin operators, so it is invariant. The operation is antiunitary; treating the reversal of all three spin components as a unitary rotation would violate the spin commutation relations. It reverses the magnetization and interchanges the two easy-axis ordered orientations. A convenient equivalent representative, composed with a z rotation, is Rz​(π)T, which reverses Sz while preserving Sx,Sy as operators.
The statement about one continuous and one discrete internal symmetry describes generic parameters. At D=0, additional unitary π rotations about transverse axes are allowed; at D=u=0, the continuous internal symmetry enlarges to full spin rotation symmetry. Spatial translations of the uniform chain are separate symmetries of the sites.
