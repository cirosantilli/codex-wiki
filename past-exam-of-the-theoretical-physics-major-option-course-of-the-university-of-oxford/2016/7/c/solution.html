Write R2=∑i​xi2​ and impose tracelessness by a Lagrange multiplier ζ. Differentiating V−ζ∑i​xi​ gives
4bxi3​+(4aR2−2m2)xi​−ζ=0(i=1,…,5).​
(60)
For b=0, each eigenvalue is a real root of the same nonzero cubic polynomial, so at most three distinct values occur at any stationary point, in particular at any existing minimum. If the multiplicities are n1​,…,nk​, the unbroken subgroup is the block centralizer
S(U(n1​)×⋯×U(nk​)),k≤3,α∑​nα​=5.​
(61)
Its connected Lie algebra is ⨁α​su(nα​)⊕u(1)k−1. The partitions allowed by this upper bound are 5, 4+1, 3+2, 3+1+1, and 2+2+1. These are candidate patterns, not a proof that every one is a stable minimum for given couplings. For three distinct roots, the absent quadratic coefficient also implies that their unweighted sum is zero; tracelessness imposes an additional multiplicity-weighted relation.
The unrestricted printed claim has an exception. If b=0, a>0 and m>0, every traceless matrix with R2=m2/(2a) is a global minimum. For example
Φd​=20a​m​diag(−2,−1,0,1,2)
(62)
has five distinct eigenvalues and attains that minimum. Its unbroken subgroup is the maximal torus U(1)4. The cubic argument therefore requires a nonzero b, or another condition excluding the identically vanishing stationarity polynomial. Couplings for which the potential is unbounded below have no global minimum to classify.
