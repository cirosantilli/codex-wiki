Let ΔM​ be the free real-scalar Feynman propagator and Dm​(x−y)=⟨0∣Tφ(x)φ†(y)∣0⟩0​ the free complex-scalar one. Expanding the interaction exponential once and applying Wick's theorem gives the connected decay Green's function
⟨Tϕ(x)φ(y)φ†(z)⟩c​=−ig∫d4wΔM​(x−w)Dm​(y−w)Dm​(w−z)+O(g3).​
(65)
There is one allowed contraction of each external field with the appropriate vertex field, so no factorial remains. Fourier transformation gives the momentum-conserving delta factor times (−ig)ΔM​(p)Dm​(q1​)Dm​(q2​), and amputation of external propagators gives the decay vertex −ig. An on-shell physical decay additionally requires M>2m.
If one literally asks for the full three-point correlation function without a connected restriction or a tadpole condition, it also contains
−ig∫d4wΔM​(x−w)Dm​(0)Dm​(y−z),
(66)
the product of the induced heavy-field one-point function and the free light-field two-point function. This disconnected tadpole diagram does not describe a three-particle decay and drops out of its connected amputated amplitude.
For two equally charged daughters the corresponding Green's function vanishes to every perturbative order:
⟨Tϕ(x)φ†(y)φ†(z)⟩=0.​
(67)
Every interaction contains equal numbers of φ and φ†, so the two excess conjugate fields can never be paired. Equivalently the exact global U(1) symmetry φ↦eiαφ, φ†↦e−iαφ† leaves the vacuum and action invariant, whereas this insertion has charge −2. Charge conservation forbids the decay of a neutral particle into two particles of the same nonzero charge.
