Put ρ=φ†φ. The heavy-field equation of motion is
(□+M2)ϕ=−gρ,ϕ=−M2g​ρ+M4g​□ρ+O(M−6).
(70)
This solution describes the low-energy sector without an independent incoming heavy-particle wave. It is valid when all external momentum invariants and light masses squared are small compared with M2.
After an integration by parts, the terms involving the heavy field are −21​ϕ(□+M2)ϕ−gρϕ. Substituting the solution into both terms, rather than the source term alone, gives
ΔLeff​=2g2​ρ(□+M2)−1ρ=2M2g2​ρ2−2M4g2​ρ□ρ+O(M−6).
(71)
Hence, with the question's sign convention for the local quartic interaction,
λ=−2M2g2​,Leff​=∂μ​φ†∂μφ−m2ρ−λρ2+O(M−4).​
(72)
This is integrating out a field followed by a derivative expansion. The negative quartic means attraction. It also makes the truncated potential unbounded at large fields; minimizing the original potential over ϕ reveals the same negative quartic. Thus neither displayed model, without extra stabilizing interactions, supplies a globally stable nonperturbative vacuum. The matching describes perturbative low-energy scattering about the local vacuum. The simple equation-of-motion matching fixes the leading tree-level coefficient; precision quantum matching requires consistent loop renormalization and the additional effective operators at the requested accuracy.
