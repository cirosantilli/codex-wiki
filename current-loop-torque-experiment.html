Current-loop torque experiment

The PHYWE guide Magnetic moment in the magnetic field makes the magnetic dipole moment measurable. A circular test current loop sits between a pair of Helmholtz coils, with separate supplies and meters for the test electric current I and field-producing electric current IH​. The test current loop is mounted on a torsion dynamometer. The guide varies IH​, the number of turns, the test electric current, diameter, and the angle between the magnetic dipole moment and magnetic field. Its angular settings are spaced by 15°. The leads hang loosely and are twisted together to suppress extra torque; the instrument zero is checked frequently because moving leads can shift it. Its reported results include approximately linear dependence on each electric current and on winding count, and approximately quadratic dependence on diameter.[ref]
The readout is a balance of torques. According to the manufacturer's Torsion dynamometer 0.01 N operating instructions, a metallic band supplies the restoring torque. First set the indication to zero with the applied force absent and center the lever using the zero knob. Apply the load, then turn the indicating knob until the lever returns to its original zero position. The scale then reads a force equivalent, not a torque directly. The quoted scale division is 0.1mN. For a force applied perpendicular to the calibration lever arm at distance ℓ, the conversion is τ=Fℓ; a current loop applies a couple, so its torque must be compared with the calibrated restoring torque.[ref]
For a quantitative repeat, independently check that conversion with known forces at a measured perpendicular lever arm, and check the imposed magnetic field at the test current loop. These are proposed calibration checks, not additional measurements reported here. The ideal Helmholtz coil model predicts
B0​=(54​)3/2RH​μ0​NH​IH​​,∣τ∣=NIπR2B0​∣sinθ∣,
(88)
where NH​ and RH​ are the winding count and radius of each field-producing Helmholtz coil, distinct from N and R of the test current loop. Derive B0​ by adding the on-axis magnetic field of a circular current loop at distances RH​/2 from both coils. Independently calibrating B0​ and the restoring torque lets a fitted torque amplitude determine m as amplitude divided by B0​, to compare with NIπR2.
Useful additional controls are to reverse only I, reverse only IH​, and reverse both: the signed torque should reverse in either single reversal and be unchanged in the double reversal. Record baselines with each electric current separately zero, and subtract a nearby zero reading to reduce torsion balance zero drift. Repeat angular settings in both directions to reveal lead-dependent offsets. These are suggested controls. Limits include finite magnetic field uniformity, uncertainty in effective winding area and angular zero, meter calibration, Joule heating, and residual lead torque. An apparently sinusoidal trace alone does not establish the absolute magnetic dipole moment. No raw laboratory data or new measured values are claimed here.
