McShane extension theorem (F(x)=infa∈A​{f(a)+Ld(x,a)})

 Home Mathematics Area of mathematics Analysis Real analysis Lipschitz continuity
OurBigBook.com Words: 320
Let A be a nonempty subset of a metric space X, and let f:A→R satisfy a Lipschitz bound L. Then
F(x)=infa∈A​{f(a)+Ld(x,a)},G(x)=supa∈A​{f(a)−Ld(x,a)}
(48)
are real-valued Lipschitz functions on all of X, agree with f on A, and obey the same bound L. Every other extension H satisfying that bound lies between them, G≤H≤F. Despite the infimum in its formula, F is the largest such extension: every given sample supplies an upper constraint H(x)≤f(a)+Ld(x,a).
Take three real samples f(0)=0, f(1)=1/2, f(2)=0, and allow the bound L=1. Their least Lipschitz constant is only 1/2, so this is deliberately a looser permitted bound. The two extensions are
F(x)=min{∣x∣,21​+∣x−1∣,∣x−2∣},G(x)=max{−∣x∣,21​−∣x−1∣,−∣x−2∣}.
(49)
At x=1/2, the allowable interval is 0=G(1/2)≤H(1/2)≤F(1/2)=1/2. The interpolant obtained by linear interpolation is a piecewise linear function and has value 1/4 there. Extending it constantly outside [0,2] obeys the same bound on the entire real line. At x=5/2, the allowable interval is [−1/2,1/2]. Thus the data and the bound control possible values without selecting a unique curve.
The construction is useful when data are known only on a subset: it extends them without increasing an allowed Lipschitz bound. A short justification explains why the envelopes work. Fix a0​∈A. The triangle inequality and the bound on f imply
f(a0​)−Ld(x,a0​)≤f(a)+Ld(x,a)(a∈A),
(50)
so the infimum is finite below; the term a=a0​ makes it finite above. At a sample x=a0​, all upper terms are at least f(a0​), while that term gives equality. Finally, d(x,a)≤d(y,a)+d(x,y) gives F(x)≤F(y)+Ld(x,y) after taking an infimum; exchanging x,y proves the bound. The lower envelope follows by applying the same argument to −f.
E. J. McShane's paper Extension of range of functions appeared in the Bulletin of the American Mathematical Society, volume 40, in 1934, pp. 837–842. [ref] [ref]
