Positive feedback

 Home Biology Signal transduction Signaling feedback
OurBigBook.com Words: 2k Articles: 16
Positive feedback closes a causal loop whose net effect reinforces an initiating change. A loop with two inhibitory links can therefore have positive feedback. In signal transduction, a product that increases the activity of the enzyme making it illustrates reinforcement; the consequences depend on the full mathematical model, not just the loop sign. See signaling feedback.
A biological example is cell-cycle activation feedback. Cyclin B binds Cdc2, whose activity promotes activation of Cdc25; Cdc25 removes inhibitory phosphorylation from Cdc2, permitting further activation. Cdc2 also inhibits Wee1, an enzyme that inhibits Cdc2 through phosphorylation. That second return contains two inhibitions and therefore also has positive feedback. These are regulatory paths, not a claim that every connection is a single direct molecular encounter. [ref]
Reinforcement can preserve a decision after its initiating stimulus weakens: returning from the high state may require a lower input than entering it. The cell-cycle hysteresis experiment below tests this history dependence at matched cyclin B concentrations. A steep response alone is insufficient evidence of bistability, and finite observation times can make slow relaxation look like memory. A proposed switch must specify its input, readout and observation interval.
