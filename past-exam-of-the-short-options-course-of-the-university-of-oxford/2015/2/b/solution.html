For the given group homomorphism φ:H→Aut(N), the external semidirect product has underlying Cartesian product N×H and operation
(n,h)(m,k)=(nφ(h)(m),hk).​
(17)
The notation Aut(N) denotes the automorphism group of N. Closure follows because φ(h) is an automorphism of N. To check associativity, calculate both bracketings:
((n,h)(m,k))(r,l)(n,h)((m,k)(r,l))​=(nφ(h)(m)φ(hk)(r),hkl),=(nφ(h)(mφ(k)(r)),hkl)=(nφ(h)(m)φ(h)φ(k)(r),hkl).​
(18)
They agree because φ(hk)=φ(h)φ(k). The identity is (eN​,eH​), using φ(eH​)=idN​, and the two-sided inverse is
(n,h)−1=(φ(h−1)(n−1),h−1).
(19)
Indeed multiplication in either order gives the identity, using φ(h)φ(h−1)=idN​. This verifies the group axioms.
For the specified group presentation, take N=C9​=⟨X⟩ and H=C3​=⟨Y⟩. The rule α(X)=X4 defines an automorphism because 4 is coprime to 9. Furthermore 43≡1(mod9), so Y↦α defines the required group homomorphism C3​→Aut(C9​). In the resulting semidirect product S, the canonical generators satisfy
X9=Y3=e,YXY−1=X4.
(20)
Consequently the group presentation defines a surjective group homomorphism G→S, sending x to X and y to Y. The underlying Cartesian product gives ∣S∣=27, whereas part (a) gives ∣G∣≤27. Surjectivity forces equality, so the map is an isomorphism:
G≅C9​⋊α​C3​,α(X)=X4,∣G∣=27.​
(21)
This also proves that the 27 normal forms in part (a) are distinct and that x has element order exactly 9.
