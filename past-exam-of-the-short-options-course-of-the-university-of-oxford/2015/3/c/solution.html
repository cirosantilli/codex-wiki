OurBigBook.com Words: 104
Choose three distinct projective points A,B,C. Their images under the injective map τ are distinct. Sharp triple transitivity gives a projective transformation S agreeing with τ on these three points. The map σ=S−1∘τ fixes A,B,C and preserves their cross-ratios.
For any other projective point P, invariance gives
(AB:Cσ(P))=(AB:CP).
(31)
Let T normalize (A,B,C) to (∞,0,1). By part (b), the left side is T(σ(P)) and the right side is T(P). Since T is a bijection, σ(P)=P. It fixes the original three points too, so σ is the identity map everywhere. Therefore
τ=S is a projective transformation.​
(32)
No continuity or prior surjectivity assumption was needed. This is the rigidity assertion that cross-ratio preserving maps are projective.
