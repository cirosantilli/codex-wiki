For the specified functional,
Fy′​=y1+y′2​y′​,y′Fy′​−F=−y1+y′2​1​.
(69)
Since y>0, the Beltrami identity gives y1+y′2​=R for some R>0. This suggests a circular arc, but it is worth deriving its equation without dividing by y′: such division would lose the point where the arc is horizontal.
Substitute the displayed partial derivative and Fy​=−1+y′2​/y2 in the full Euler-Lagrange equation. Combining terms over the common denominator gives
dxd​Fy′​−Fy​=y2(1+y′2)3/2yy′′+1+y′2​=0.
(70)
Thus (yy′)′=−1, so yy′=h−x and a second integration gives
(x−h)2+y2=R2.
(71)
This establishes the semicircular extremals of the hyperbolic length functional and excludes constant y, which would satisfy the first-order Beltrami identity for a suitable R but not the full Euler-Lagrange equation.
The two prescribed endpoints give
(−1−h)2+4c2=R2=(1−h)2+4d2.
(72)
Subtracting yields 4h=4(d2−c2). Therefore
h=d2−c2,R2=(1+h)2+4c2,y(x)=R2−(x−h)2​.​
(73)
In particular the centre is (d2−c2,0)​. Admissibility requires c,d>0, as the prescribed heights must lie in y>0. Under that condition the radicand is positive throughout [−1,1]: it is a concave quadratic polynomial whose two endpoint values are 4c2 and 4d2. The positive root consequently gives one smooth admissible arc, uniquely determined by the endpoints.
