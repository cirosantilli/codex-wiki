The feasible set is closed and contained in the rectangle
0≤x≤p1/p,0≤y≤q1/q.
(47)
It is therefore compact, and the continuous function xy attains a global maximum there by the extreme value theorem. At the two boundary endpoints where a coordinate is zero, the product is zero. Meanwhile (1,1) is feasible because the conjugate exponents satisfy 1/p+1/q=1, and its product is one. Hence every maximizer has x,y>0.
On that positive region the constraint is C1 with nonzero gradient. The Lagrange multiplier equations are
y=λxp−1,x=λyq−1,pxp​+qyq​=1.
(48)
The first two identities imply xy=λxp=λyq. Since x,y>0, the multiplier is nonzero, so xp=yq=r. The constraint now gives
r(p1​+q1​)=1,r=1.
(49)
Thus the sole interior candidate is (1,1). Existence of a maximum and the boundary comparison prove the unique global result
maxxy=1,attained exactly at x=y=1.​
(50)
This conjugate-power constrained product maximum is valid for all real p,q>1 with 1/p+1/q=1; no integrality of the exponents is needed.
