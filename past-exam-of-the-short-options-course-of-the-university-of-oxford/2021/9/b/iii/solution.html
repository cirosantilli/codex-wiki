For r>s, a>0. When 0<a≤1/4, the roots of μ2−μ+a are real and lie strictly between zero and one. When a>1/4, they are a complex-conjugate pair of modulus a​. Thus the positive steady state is linearly asymptotically stable exactly when 0<a<1, and unstable when a>1. Since a=2s−2s2/r increases strictly with r, the crossing occurs at
a=1,rc​=2s−12s2​=2δ+12(δ+1)2​.​
(111)
At the crossing,
μ±​=21±i3​​=e±iπ/3,nt​=Acos(πt/3)+Bsin(πt/3).​
(112)
Every nonzero real linearized perturbation has period six. This establishes the intended linear stability threshold and linearized period six.
The nonlinear period-six claim in the printed paper does not follow, and is false as a local bifurcating-branch claim for this map. One can check this directly rather than infer a nonlinear period from the eigenvalues. At r=rc​ normalize Nt​=N∗(1+u), Nt−1​=N∗(1+v). The exact planar map is
F(u,v)=((1+u)[2s−1+(1+v)22s2​−(s−1)]−1, u).
(113)
Its cubic Taylor expansion is
F(u,v)=(u−v−uv+2s2−s​v2+2s2−s​uv2+s2s−1​v3, u)+O(∥(u,v)∥4).
(114)
Here is a concrete check of the sixfold return. Put z=(u,v)T, Q=u2−uv+v2, and
K=(12​−2−1​),K2=−3I,α=−s23s2−3s+1​,β=−2s25s2−6s+2​.
(115)
Composing the displayed cubic expansion six times cancels all quadratic terms and gives
F6(z)−z=Q(αI+βK)z+O(∥z∥4).
(116)
In particular
Q(F6(z))−Q(z)=−s22(3s2−3s+1)​Q(z)2+O(∥z∥5).​
(117)
The quartic coefficient is strictly negative. Hence, sufficiently near the equilibrium, every nonzero point has smaller Q after six iterations: the critical nonlinear map has no nearby nontrivial period-six orbit, and the equilibrium itself is locally attracting despite its neutral linearization.
One can also rule out a branch of small period-six orbits on either side of the crossing. Normalize about the positive equilibrium at each nearby r, and put η=a−1. The derivative of the sixth power of (11​−a0​) at a=1 is 3I+K. Smooth dependence on r therefore gives
Fη6​(z)−z=[(3η+αQ)I+(η+βQ)K]z+o((∣η∣+∥z∥2)∥z∥).
(118)
The leading matrix has determinant (3η+αQ)2+3(η+βQ)2. Both terms could vanish with Q>0 only if α=3β, but
α−3β=2s2(3s−2)2​>0.
(119)
Thus its norm on nonzero z is bounded below by a positive constant times (∣η∣+∥z∥2)∥z∥, and the smaller remainder cannot cancel it. There is no nearby nonzero solution of Fη6​(z)=z. The eigenvalues satisfy the nonresonance conditions through order four, their moduli cross outward with increasing r, and the cubic radial coefficient is negative. The standard Neimark–Sacker bifurcation theorem therefore gives a supercritical invariant closed curve. Its rotation is near the linear resonance 1/6; it is not a local branch of exact period-six orbits.
Finally, the discrete population map does not preserve nonnegativity for arbitrary large lagged populations: r/(1+bNt−12​)−δ can be negative. The positive-equilibrium stability conclusions above are local and do not assume a global positivity property that the formula lacks.
