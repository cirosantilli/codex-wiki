Let V>0 be the observer's speed, so its laboratory velocity is −Vex​. The particle's laboratory speed along +y follows from the energy–momentum relation:
u=c1−E2m2c4​​,q=cu​∈(0,1).
(97)
Here E>mc2 because the massive particle is moving. Boosting to the observer's frame with velocity −V gives, by the relativistic velocity-addition formula,
ux′​=V,uy′​=γu​,γ=(1−V2/c2)−1/2.
(98)
Equivalently its four-momentum has px′​=γVE/c2, py′​=py​. Thus tanϕ=u/(γV). Solving this equation, rather than using the Galilean transverse velocity, yields
V=tan2ϕ+u2/c2​u​=sin2ϕ+q2cos2ϕ​cqcosϕ​.​
(99)
For a strictly moving observer, 0<ϕ<π/2. At ϕ=π/2 the limiting observer speed is zero; as ϕ↓0 it tends to c. The direction of the observer's velocity remains negative x; the displayed V is its positive magnitude.
