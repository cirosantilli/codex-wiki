With H>0, set
s=sin(ϕ/2),y−1/2+λ=sH​,c=H2>0,b=Hλ​.
(59)
Then the first integral gives 1/1+y′2​=s. On the descending and ascending halves of the physical track choose respectively 0<ϕ<π and π<ϕ<2π, so that y′=cot(ϕ/2). Solving for y gives
y(ϕ)=c(1−bsin(ϕ/2))2sin2(ϕ/2)​.​
(60)
The unsquared equation requires 1−bs>0. At s=1 this says b<1; it is not permissible to retain other values merely because the displayed formula for y has a squared denominator.
For completeness, the Euler-Lagrange equation also gives y′′=(A′(y)/A(y))(1+y′2)<0. Thus y′ decreases through zero exactly once, so the chosen parameter runs through the whole single arch without an extra branch. Differentiating the formula for y and dividing by y′=cot(ϕ/2) gives, continuously also at ϕ=π,
dϕdx​=c(1−bsin(ϕ/2))3sin2(ϕ/2)​.
(61)
Using x(0)=0 yields
x(ϕ)=c1​∫0ϕ​(1−bsin(u/2))3sin2(u/2)​du.​
(62)
Since 1+y′2​=1/sin(ϕ/2), the arc length is obtained by cancelling one factor of sin(ϕ/2) in dx/dϕ. The conditions x(2π)=1 and S=L become
cL​=∫02π​(1−bsin(u/2))3sin2(u/2)​du,=∫02π​(1−bsin(u/2))3sin2(u/2)​du∫02π​(1−bsin(u/2))3sin(u/2)​du​.​​
(63)
These determine a unique parameter pair for each L>1. To see uniqueness, regard L as the expected value of 1/s under a probability density function proportional to s2/(1−bs)3 on 0<u<2π. Differentiation under the integral sign gives the covariance identity
dbdL​=3Cov(s1​,1−bss​)<0.
(64)
The first argument decreases with s and the second increases. The strict sign follows by applying (f(s)−f(t))(g(s)−g(t))<0 to two independent unequal values and averaging; the integrable endpoint singularity of 1/s causes no problem. As b↑1, the weight concentrates at s=1, so L↓1. As b=−k→−∞, endpoint estimates give numerator ∼2/k2 and denominator ∼4logk/k3, so L∼k/(2logk)→∞. Continuity then covers every L>1. At b=0 one recovers the cycloid and L=4/π.
Smoothness qualification. Near ϕ=0,
x∼12cϕ3​,y∼4cϕ2​,y′→+∞,
(65)
and the other endpoint has y′→−∞. These are smooth graphs on 0<x<1, not C1 graphs on the closed interval. Indeed a finite endpoint slope would make the conserved H diverge as y→0. The travel time remains finite because
T=2gc​1​∫02π​(1−bsin(ϕ/2))2dϕ​<∞(b<1).
(66)
Thus the printed phrase “smooth solution” must be understood on the interior, with improper endpoint limits. The variational calculation supplies necessary stationary arches; it does not by itself prove global minimality in every larger class of tracks.
