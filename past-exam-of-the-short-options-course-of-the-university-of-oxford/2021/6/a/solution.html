Let g>0 be the gravitational acceleration, and let y denote positive downward depth. For a frictionless particle released from rest, conservation of energy gives v2=2gy. The arc length element of (x,−y(x)) is ds=1+y′2​dx, so the travel time and length are
T[y]=2g​1​∫01​y​1+y′2​​dx,S[y]=∫01​1+y′2​dx.
(55)
For a finite-time single arch, require y>0 in 0<x<1, y(0)=y(1)=0, and interpret the endpoint integrals improperly. At a regular constrained minimizer with S[y]=L, the Lagrange multiplier rule for an isoperimetric constraint makes 2g​T+λ(S−L) stationary. Dropping the constant −λL gives
I[y]=∫01​(y−1/2+λ)1+y′2​dx,y(0)=y(1)=0.​
(56)
For L>1 the constraint is regular under interior variations: if its first variation vanished for every compactly supported variation, the Euler-Lagrange equation for S would force y′ constant, hence y≡0 and S=1, a contradiction. Thus the multiplier is justified for a nondegenerate arch.
The printed case L=1 is degenerate. Since 1+y′2​≥1, equality of the length with the horizontal separation forces y′=0 and y=0. A particle initially at rest then has no tangential gravitational acceleration and cannot complete the trip. Its travel time is infinite. The finite-time calculation therefore concerns L>1, with endpoint regularity qualified below.
