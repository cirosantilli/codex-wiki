For real p>0, substitute t=px in the Laplace transform:

The Gamma function integral is finite because a>0. For complex p, absolute convergence holds exactly when Rep>0: the modulus of the integrand is xa−1e−(Rep)x. On this half-plane the same answer holds with the branch p−a=exp(−aLogp) whose logarithm is real on the positive axis, by holomorphic continuation of the two sides.
If “exists” permits conditionally convergent ordinary improper integrals, there are additional boundary points when 0<a<1: for p=iω=0, the Dirichlet test gives convergence, with value Γ(a)∣ω∣−ae−isgn(ω)πa/2. For a≥1 these boundary integrals do not converge in that sense. At p=0, and for Rep<0, the integral diverges. Thus Rep>0 is the region of absolute convergence of a Laplace transform, with the stated conditional boundary qualification.
