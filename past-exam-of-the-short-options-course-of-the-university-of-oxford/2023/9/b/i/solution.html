Abbreviate A=α12​, B=α21​. The coordinate factors immediately give the three boundary equilibria S1​,S2​,S3​. The Jacobian matrix is
J(u)=(1−2u1​−Au2​−ρBu2​​−Au1​ρ(γ−2u2​−Bu1​)​).
(55)
At S1​, the eigenvalues are 1,ργ>0, so it is a repelling equilibrium. At the other two points, triangularity gives
S2​:S3​:​−1, ρ(γ−B),1−Aγ, −ργ.​​
(56)
Therefore S2​ is asymptotically stable for γ<B and a saddle point for γ>B; S3​ is asymptotically stable for γ>1/A and a saddle point for γ<1/A. Equality gives a zero eigenvalue, so linearization alone is inconclusive there.
For later use, the coexistence point is physically positive exactly when B<γ<1/A if AB<1, or 1/A<γ<B if AB>1. Its stability is as supplied in the question. If AB=1 the displayed formula for S4​ does not apply: when also γ=B=1/A, the coincident non-axis nullclines give a whole segment of equilibria.
