Since γ=1−δ/r2​, increasing hunting effort moves leftward on these diagrams. The first stability threshold corresponds to
δlocal​=r2​(1−α21​)​.
(61)
Above this removal rate, a sufficiently small grey population can be eliminated while reds recover. Until the second threshold, however, the system is bistable: an initially large grey population can still win, so the history and initial abundances matter. The second threshold is
δglobal​=r2​(1−α12​1​)​.
(62)
Above it, any positive red population recovers and the grey population tends to zero, regardless of its initial abundance in this deterministic model. This threshold is smaller than r2​, so competition from surviving reds can make eradication possible with less removal than would be necessary to suppress grey growth without competitors. If δ>r2​, part (a)(iii) supplies the simpler competitor-independent extinction bound. These are model predictions about long-term dynamics; neither finite-time extinction nor uncertainty in ecological parameters is encoded in the equations.
