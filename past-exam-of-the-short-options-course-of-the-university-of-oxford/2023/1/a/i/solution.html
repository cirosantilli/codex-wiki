The multiplicative group Fp×​ is a cyclic group of order p−1, so choose a primitive root g. Every nonzero residue is uniquely gj, with j taken modulo p−1. The condition becomes (p−1)∣kj. Writing d=gcd(k,p−1), its solutions are j=0,(p−1)/d,…,(d−1)(p−1)/d. Zero is not a solution. Therefore the number is
gcd(k,p−1)​.
(1)
This includes p=2. The cyclic group argument gives the exact count, whereas the bound of k roots for a nonzero degree-k polynomial over a field would only give an upper bound.
