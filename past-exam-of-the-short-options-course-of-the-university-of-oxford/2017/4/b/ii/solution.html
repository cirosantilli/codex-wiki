Since A is invertible, the quadratic form gradient vanishes exactly at the origin. At every nonzero point of Cα​, the scalar derivative is surjective, so the regular level set theorem gives a smooth submanifold of dimension n−1.
If α=0, the origin is absent. Therefore the largest relatively open smooth subset is all of Cα​.
If α=0, the nontriviality assumption forces the form to be an indefinite quadratic form: an invertible definite form would have zero set consisting only of the origin. Every nonzero point is regular. To show that the origin really fails to be a manifold point, use Sylvester's law of inertia to reduce to ∣u∣2−∣w∣2=0. With positive and negative coordinate vectors ei​,fj​, the null vectors ei​+fj​ and ei​−fj​ span Rn. Each line in one of these directions lies in the cone.
If a relatively open manifold neighborhood of the origin existed, its tangent space would contain all these line directions, so would have dimension n. Such an embedded manifold would be open in Rn, impossible for the zero set of a nonzero quadratic form. Consequently
smooth locus={Cα​,C0​∖{0},​α=0,α=0.​​

This gives the smooth locus of a nondegenerate real quadratic level set, including maximality at the singular vertex.
