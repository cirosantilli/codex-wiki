Let d(u) be the weight of a shortest vu-path, and let p(u) be the predecessor of u on the chosen Pu​. Its prefix to p(u) must also be shortest: a cheaper prefix followed by the final edge would give a cheaper walk to u, and cycle erasure in minimum-weight walks would produce a cheaper path because every edge weight is positive. Therefore
d(u)=d(p(u))+w(eu​)>d(p(u)).
(65)
Following predecessor arrows strictly decreases d, so cannot revisit a vertex. Since each nonroot vertex has a predecessor, finiteness forces every predecessor chain to end at v. Thus T is connected.
The edges eu​ are distinct: if two vertices selected the same undirected edge, each would be the other's predecessor, contradicting strict decrease. Hence T has exactly ∣V(G)∣−1 edges. A finite connected graph with this edge count is a tree. This proves that positive-weight shortest-path predecessors form a tree, without requiring the initially chosen paths to be mutually consistent.
