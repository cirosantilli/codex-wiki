The Chinese remainder theorem says that for pairwise coprime positive moduli m1​,…,mr​ and arbitrary integers a1​,…,ar​, the simultaneous modular congruences
n≡ai​(modmi​)(1≤i≤r)
(11)
have a solution, unique modulo M=∏i​mi​.
Choose 2017 distinct prime numbers q1​,…,q2017​. Their prime powers qi2017​ are pairwise coprime. Apply the Chinese remainder theorem to
n≡−i(modqi2017​)(1≤i≤2017).​
(12)
For the resulting integer n, each n+i is divisible by qi2017​, the required power of an integer greater than one. Adding a sufficiently large multiple of ∏i​qi2017​ makes n positive without changing any modular congruence. Thus such a positive n exists, indeed infinitely many do. This is the general construction of consecutive integers divisible by prescribed prime powers.

Write the group order as ∣G∣=pam, where p is a prime number and p∤m. A Sylow p-subgroup is a subgroup of group order pa, the largest power of p dividing ∣G∣.
The three Sylow theorems assert:
  • Existence: a Sylow p-subgroup exists, and every subgroup of prime power order a power of p is contained in one.
  • Conjugacy: any two Sylow p-subgroups are conjugate in G.
  • Counting: their number np​ satisfies np​∣m and np​≡1(modp).
In particular, a unique Sylow p-subgroup is a normal subgroup, since all its conjugates must equal it.

Put N=HK=⟨r⟩, the normal subgroup of order 35. By Cauchy's theorem for finite groups, G contains an element t of order two. Such an element cannot belong to the odd-order subgroup N. Thus N∩⟨t⟩={1} and G=N⟨t⟩, giving the semidirect product
G≅C35​⋊C2​.​

Every automorphism of a finite cyclic group sends r to ra for a unit a modulo 35. The relation t2=1 forces the conjugation action of t to square to the identity:
trt−1=ra,a2≡1(mod35).
(20)
The Chinese remainder theorem identifies a congruence class modulo 35 with its pair of classes modulo 5 and 7. In each of these prime fields, the factorization (a−1)(a+1)=0 forces a=1 or a=−1. The choices are independent, giving exactly four homomorphisms from a finite cyclic group C2​→Aut(C35​). Explicitly,
a≡1,6,29,34(mod35).​
(21)
The factors C5​ and C7​ of N are preserved by every automorphism, since each is its unique subgroup of that order. The four conjugation actions therefore give:
  • a=1: the action is trivial on both factors, so G≅C35​×C2​≅C70​​, by cyclicity of a product of two finite cyclic groups.
  • a=34: the action inverts both factors, so ⟨r,t∣r35=t2=1, trt−1=r−1⟩ is the dihedral group D70​​.

The real projective plane is the set of one-dimensional linear subspaces of R3. Equivalently,
RP2=(R3∖{0})/∼,v∼λv (λ∈R∖{0}).​
(22)
A projective point is written in homogeneous coordinates [x:y:z]. A projective line is the set of projective points whose representatives belong to one fixed two-dimensional linear subspace of R3. Such a linear subspace is the kernel of a linear map of a nonzero linear functional, so its equation has the form αx+βy+γz=0 with (α,β,γ)=0.
Two distinct projective points have linearly independent representatives p,q. Their linear span has dimension two and hence defines a projective line containing both. Conversely, any two-dimensional linear subspace containing both representatives contains their entire linear span and therefore equals it. Thus exactly one projective line passes through two distinct projective points.

Two projective triangles are in perspectivity from a point O when their three corresponding-vertex projective lines PP′, QQ′, and RR′ all pass through O.
Desargues's theorem: perspectivity from a point implies perspectivity from a line. More explicitly, if PP′,QQ′,RR′ are concurrent, then the three corresponding-side intersections
X=PQ∩P′Q′,Y=QR∩Q′R′,Z=RP∩R′P′
(25)
are collinear. The usual statement assumes nondegenerate projective triangles and well-defined, distinct corresponding-side intersections. The projective line containing X,Y,Z is the axis of perspectivity from a line. All intersections are projective: parallel lines in an affine chart meet on its projective line at infinity.

The dual projective space is P((R3)∗). A projective line with coefficients [α:β:γ] becomes a projective point in this dual plane; a projective point [a:b:c] becomes the dual projective line aα+bβ+cγ=0.
The point-line duality reverses incidence:
P∈l⟺l∗∈P∗.​
(26)
Thus the dual statement is that the dual point l∗ lies on the dual line P∗. A join of projective points becomes an intersection of dual projective lines, and the converse holds too. In particular, collinear projective points correspond to concurrent dual projective lines.
The dual of Desargues's theorem is its converse: if the intersections of the three pairs of corresponding sides are collinear, then the three lines joining corresponding vertices are concurrent. In the same nondegenerate setting, perspectivity from a line therefore implies perspectivity from a point.

The map is differentiable at x if there is a linear map L:Rn→Rm such that
limh→0​∥h∥∥f(x+h)−f(x)−Lh∥​=0.

Since A is invertible, the quadratic form gradient vanishes exactly at the origin. At every nonzero point of Cα​, the scalar derivative is surjective, so the regular level set theorem gives a smooth submanifold of dimension n−1.
If α=0, the origin is absent. Therefore the largest relatively open smooth subset is all of Cα​.
If α=0, the nontriviality assumption forces the form to be an indefinite quadratic form: an invertible definite form would have zero set consisting only of the origin. Every nonzero point is regular. To show that the origin really fails to be a manifold point, use Sylvester's law of inertia to reduce to ∣u∣2−∣w∣2=0. smooth locus={Cα​,C0​∖{0},​α=0,α=0.​​

Writing the coordinates as (x,y,z), the level equation is x2+y2−z2=α. Therefore:
  • α=0: a circular double cone, x2+y2=z2. Its vertex at the origin is the sole singular point; every other point is a smooth surface point.
  • α=1: a one-sheeted hyperboloid, with horizontal radius 1+z2​. It is smooth everywhere.
  • α=−1: a two-sheeted hyperboloid, z=±1+x2+y2​. Both sheets are smooth everywhere, including their tips (0,0,±1).
The cone vertex is an instance of the double cone singularity. The tips of the two-sheeted hyperboloid are regular because the gradient (2x,2y,−2z) is nonzero there.

Under the usual walk convention allowing repeated edges, the stated hypothesis is insufficient. Take the connected graph consisting of one zero-weight edge xy. It has no simple cycles, so the cycle condition holds. Nevertheless Q=x,y,x,y is a minimum-weight xy-walk of weight zero and is not a path.
