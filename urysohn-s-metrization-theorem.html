Urysohn's metrization theorem (T1​+regular+second countable⟹metrisable)

 Home Mathematics Metrizable space
OurBigBook.com Words: 122
Every second-countable space that is also a T3 space is a metrizable space. First regularity of a topological space and the Lindelöf property yield a normal space: cover each of two disjoint closed sets by countably many open sets whose closures avoid the other, and subtract the first n opposite closures from the nth neighborhood. The resulting unions are disjoint open neighborhoods. Urysohn's lemma then gives, for each pair of basic sets Bn​⊆Bm​, a continuous function equal to one on the smaller closure and zero outside the larger set. There are countably many such pairs. The shrinking form of regularity of a topological space shows these functions generate the topology, and T1​ makes them separate points. Their coordinate map embeds the space into the countable cube, with compatible metric d(x,y)=∑j​2−j∣fj​(x)−fj​(y)∣. The bounded tails prove compatibility with the product topology. Thus a countable base converts separation axioms into a numerical distance.
