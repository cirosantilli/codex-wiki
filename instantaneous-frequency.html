Instantaneous frequency (fi​(t)=ϕ′(t)/(2π))

 Home Electrical engineering Frequency modulation
OurBigBook.com Words: 162
For a signal with a specified differentiable, continuously unwrapped wave phase ϕ(t), its instantaneous frequency is
fi​(t)=2π1​dtdϕ​.
(14)
Unwrapping means following successive turns instead of resetting the wave phase at 2π. Differentiating a reset phase would introduce artificial jumps. An arbitrary real signal does not by itself specify a unique amplitude-phase representation; this definition refers to the phase chosen in the model.
For the single-message-frequency FM model,
ϕ(t)=2πfc​t+βsin(2πfm​t),fi​(t)=fc​+βfm​cos(2πfm​t).
(15)
The proof is the chain rule: the derivative of the sine contributes 2πfm​, which cancels the 2π in the definition. In the 20Hz example, fi​(0)=25, fi​(1/4)=20, and fi​(1/2)=15Hz. Although the frequency offset vanishes at t=1/4, the wave phase offset is then 5 radians; the wave phase offset and its rate of change need not vanish together.
Between times t1​,t2​, the accumulated number of cycles is (ϕ(t2​)−ϕ(t1​))/(2π)=∫t1​t2​​fi​(t)dt. Thus a crossing-to-crossing measurement yields an average frequency over that interval. Its reciprocal time is only an approximation to the instantaneous frequency at one point when fi​ varies slowly over a cycle.
