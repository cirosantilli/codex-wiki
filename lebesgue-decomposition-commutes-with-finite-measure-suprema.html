Lebesgue decomposition commutes with finite measure suprema ((⋁i​μi​)a=⋁i​μia​,(⋁i​μi​)s=⋁i​μis​)

 ... Analysis Real analysis Measure theory Measure Positive measure Finite supremum of positive measures
OurBigBook.com Words: 101
Take Lebesgue decompositions relative to a fixed finite positive measure κ. The finite union S of null supports of their singular components is still κ-null. On S each original measure equals its singular component; on Sc it equals its absolutely continuous component. Restriction commutes with the partition formula defining the finite supremum of positive measures. Thus the supremum restricts to ⋁i​μis​ on S and to ⋁i​μia​ off S. The latter is absolutely continuous with respect to κ because it is bounded by the sum of the absolutely continuous components. Uniqueness of the Lebesgue decomposition proves the displayed identities, and the Radon-Nikodym derivative of the absolutely continuous component is the pointwise maximum of the individual derivatives.
