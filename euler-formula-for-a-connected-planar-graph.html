Euler formula for a connected planar graph (n−m+f=2)

 Home Mathematics Area of mathematics Foundations of mathematics Graph theory Planar graph
OurBigBook.com Words: 591 Articles: 7
For a finite connected graph with at least one vertex, embedded as a plane graph, let n count vertices, m count edges, and f count faces of a plane graph, including the outer face. Then
n−m+f=2.
(2)
The hypotheses refer to an actual embedding; regions in a crossing-filled graph drawing do not supply its f.[ref]
In the plane graph K4​ pictured above, three bounded faces of a plane graph and the outer face give 4−6+4=2. A square cycle graph gives 4−4+2=2, while the graph path A−B−C gives 3−2+1=2. Changing a crossing into a new vertex changes the graph: the square-and-diagonals drawing then has five vertices, eight edges, and five faces of a plane graph, giving 5−8+5=2.
A proof outline deletes edges on graph cycles. Each deletion preserves the connected graph and merges two faces of a plane graph, leaving n−m+f unchanged. Eventually a tree remains, with m=n−1 and f=1. Adding the point at infinity identifies the plane picture with a sphere; the value two agrees with its Euler characteristic and the cell-count formula for Euler characteristic.
