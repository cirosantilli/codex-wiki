Capacitor energy (U=Q2/(2C))

 Home Physics Branches of physics Electromagnetism Capacitor
OurBigBook.com Words: 415 Articles: 1
The capacitor energy is the electrostatic energy stored when electric charge is separated. For a linear capacitor of fixed positive capacitance C, plate electric charge magnitude Q, and terminal electric potential difference V=Q/C, it is
U=2CQ2​=21​CV2=21​QV.
(44)
The zero is the uncharged state. Thus the capacitor energy is positive for nonzero Q, even though the total electric charge of the two plates is zero. [ref].
For the parallel-plate capacitor example, capacitance C=8.854×10−11F and terminal electric potential difference V=10V give
U=21​(8.854×10−11)(10)2J=4.427×10−9J=4.427nJ.
(45)
The plate electric charge magnitude is Q=0.8854nC. Doubling terminal electric potential difference with capacitance unchanged doubles the plate electric charge magnitude and quadruples the capacitor energy.
The electric charge is a linear function of terminal electric potential difference, while capacitor energy is a quadratic function. The second curve in each plot uses twice the capacitance: at a given terminal electric potential difference, both plate electric charge magnitude and capacitor energy double. These relations let us calculate how much energy an electrical circuit can receive when a capacitor discharges.
A brief charging argument explains the factor 1/2. If the plate electric charge magnitude has reached q, the terminal electric potential difference is q/C. Transferring a further amount dq of electric charge quasistatically requires work dW=(q/C)dq. With the geometry and permittivity fixed, the integral is
U=∫0Q​Cq​dq=2CQ2​.
(46)
Using Q=CV gives the other two forms. This integral accounts for the gradually rising terminal electric potential difference; it is the work stored in the capacitor's electric field, rather than a claim that all energy drawn from a fixed-voltage battery during charging ends up stored.
The field picture gives the same result. In the vacuum gap of a parallel-plate capacitor, the electric field has energy density u=ϵ0​E2/2. Multiplying by the gap volume Ad gives the electrostatic energy
U≃21​ϵ0​E2Ad=21​dϵ0​A​V2=21​CV2.
(47)
For the worked example, u=4.427×10−4J/m3 and Ad=10−5m3 reproduce U=4.427nJ. The electrostatic energy resides in the electric field throughout the gap, despite overall charge neutrality.
When capacitance changes, specify the constraint. At fixed plate electric charge magnitude Q, increasing capacitance decreases capacitor energy Q2/(2C); doubling capacitance halves it. At fixed terminal electric potential difference V, increasing capacitance increases capacitor energy CV2/2; doubling capacitance doubles it. In the latter case a connected battery supplies additional electric charge and energy, and there may also be mechanical work. Use the fixed-voltage capacitor energy balance to account for all transfers before inferring a force from changing capacitor energy.
