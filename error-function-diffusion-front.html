Consider the one-dimensional diffusion equation on the whole real line,
∂t∂u​=D∂x2∂2u​,D>0,
(35)
with initial value one for x<0 and zero for x>0. This is an idealized Heaviside step function profile, not a measured material or an apparatus specification. The heat kernel gives a convolution with the initial profile:
u(x,t)=∫−∞0​4πDt​e−(x−y)2/(4Dt)​dy=21​erfc(2Dt​x​),t>0.
(36)
The change of variables s=(x−y)/(2Dt​) turns the convolution into a Gaussian integral tail. The resulting similarity variable η=x/(2Dt​) shows that the transition width grows like Dt​: multiplying elapsed time by four doubles every fixed-profile distance from the original interface.
At every positive time, u(0,t)=1/2, while u(x,t)→1 as x→−∞ and u(x,t)→0 as x→+∞. For every fixed x=0, the limit as t→0+ returns the specified initial value. The value assigned at the single initial discontinuity does not change this convolution. The error function therefore explains smoothing without pretending that a discontinuous initial profile remains sharp.
Direct differentiation checks the partial differential equation. Writing η=x/(2Dt​) gives
ut​=2π​tηe−η2​,Duxx​=2π​tηe−η2​.
(37)
The model assumes constant diffusion coefficient, no advection, and no finite-domain boundary effects. For an interpretation as heat conduction, the heat equation applies to a normalized temperature difference under its own material assumptions.
