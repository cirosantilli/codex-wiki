Gyroscope

 Home Physics Branches of physics Classical mechanics Momentum Angular momentum
OurBigBook.com Words: 1k Articles: 4
A gyroscope is a rotating instrument whose rapidly spinning, nearly symmetric rotor provides a direction reference through its angular momentum. In an inertial frame, negligible external torque approximately preserves that direction by conservation of angular momentum. A transverse torque turns the angular momentum instead: the surprising sideways motion of a supported spinning wheel is precession. An ideal freely falling test gyroscope in general relativity has its spin undergo parallel transport along its timelike geodesic. Comparing that direction with distant reference axes measures Lense-Thirring precession; the direction need not remain fixed in arbitrary rotating coordinates.
A gyroscope direction reference is useful precisely because its direction can persist while its housing turns. A spinning-wheel precession experiment deliberately supplies gravitational torque to reveal how that persistence fails. The gyroscope vector geometry explains the direction, and slow steady gyroscope precession gives a numerical prediction with a check on its assumptions.
