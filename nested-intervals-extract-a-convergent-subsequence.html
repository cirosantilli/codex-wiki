Nested intervals extract a convergent subsequence (∣Ij​∣=(b−a)/2j)

 ... Mathematics Area of mathematics Analysis Real analysis Sequence and series Bolzano-Weierstrass theorem
OurBigBook.com Words: 177
Begin with a closed interval I0​=[a,b] containing the entire bounded sequence. Split the interval at its midpoint. At least one of the two closed halves contains infinitely many indexed terms; choose such a half and repeat. This gives nested intervals Ij​ with lengths (b−a)/2j. The nested interval theorem, using completeness of the real numbers, supplies their unique common point ℓ.
Choose nj​>nj−1​ with xnj​​∈Ij​. Infinitely many indexed terms in Ij​ guarantee that the next index can be chosen. Both xnj​​ and ℓ belong to Ij​, so
∣xnj​​−ℓ∣≤2jb−a​⟶0.
(42)
This is a short existence proof, not necessarily a finite algorithm for an arbitrary input sequence: deciding which half contains infinitely many future terms may require mathematical knowledge about that sequence.
