Mycielskian chromatic increase (χ(μ(G))=χ(G)+1)

 ... Area of mathematics Foundations of mathematics Graph theory Graph coloring Chromatic number Mycielskian
OurBigBook.com Words: 129
For a nonempty finite simple graph, the upper bound copies an optimal proper vertex colouring to its Mycielski shadow vertices and gives the Mycielski apex a fresh colour. For the lower bound, suppose μ(G) uses k=χ(G) colours, with w coloured k. Shadows avoid k. Replace colour k at each original vi​ by ui​'s colour. Changed originals form an independent set; unchanged neighbours differ because ui​vj​ is an edge. This proper vertex colouring of G uses k−1 colours, contradicting its chromatic number.[ref]
The nonempty condition means V(G)=∅, not E(G)=∅. An edgeless n-vertex graph with n≥1 has chromatic number one; its Mycielskian is a star graph on w,u1​,…,un​ together with n isolated vertices, and needs two colours. If G has no vertices, μ(G) is a single vertex, so the same formula also holds with the convention χ(G)=0.
