The second law requires the specific entropy to increase along the fluid's passage through a nontrivial physical shock: s1​>s0​. For a perfect gas this selects a compressive shock, whose upstream shock-frame Mach number is greater than one and whose downstream shock-frame flow is subsonic.
Here the upstream Mach number is M0​=V/c0​. The quadratic gives V2−c02​=(γ+1)UV/2>0, so M0​>1​. Density and pressure both rise. With c12​=γp1​/ρ1​, one also has V−U<c1​. For example, put M=V/c0​ and use the normal-shock identity
c12​(V−U)2​=2γM2−(γ−1)(γ−1)M2+2​<1(M>1).
(33)
The characteristic geometry is therefore c0​<V<U+c1​. The plus-family characteristics from the gas ahead are overtaken by the shock, while those behind catch up with it; they converge into the discontinuity. The minus-family speeds are −c0​ ahead and U−c1​ behind. The piston is the line x=Ut and the shock is the steeper-in-x/t line x=Vt.
