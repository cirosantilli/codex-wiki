Scale x,y by L, z by δL, tangential velocities by U, normal velocity by δU, and time by L/U. Continuity balances all three derivatives. Tangential viscous forces scale as μU/(δ2L2), hence their pressure balance requires
p∼δ2LμU​,t∼L/U.​
(10)
The inertial/normal-viscous ratio is δ2ρLU/μ, so it is negligible under the given smallness condition. Tangential viscous derivatives are smaller by δ2; the normal pressure balance gives pz​=0 at leading order. In dimensional variables the remaining equations are continuity, μuzz​=px​, μvzz​=py​, pz​=0.
No-slip boundary condition at each plate imposes u=v=0, while their normal velocities are w(0)=0 and w(h)=h˙. Integrating the two tangential equations twice gives
(u,v)=2μz(z−h)​∇p,u=h1​∫0h​(u,v)dz=−12μh2​∇p.​
(11)
Integrate continuity through the gap. Since h is independent of in-plane position and the tangential velocity vanishes at the upper wall, the flux divergence is ∇⋅(hu) and the normal contribution is h˙. Therefore
h˙+∇⋅(hu)=0.​
(12)
