Let the bed be z=0 and the free surface z=h(x,t). In a long, shallow layer, vertical acceleration is smaller than gravity, so the vertical Euler equation gives hydrostatic pressure p=patm​+ρg(h−z). Incompressibility makes the vertical speed smaller than the horizontal speed by the aspect ratio; irrotationality then makes the leading horizontal velocity depth-independent. A passive jump cannot create mechanical energy, so the change is nonpositive. Since q>0, h+​>h−​​ for a genuine jump; equality describes no jump. The upstream flow is supercritical and the downstream flow subcritical, since Fr−2​=B(A+B)/(2A2)>1 and Fr+2​=A(A+B)/(2B2)<1.
The gas-dynamic analogue is a compressive normal shock: density and pressure increase and flow changes from supersonic to subsonic. Entropy increases; directed mechanical energy is irreversibly converted into internal energy. Total gas energy, including internal energy, is conserved across an adiabatic shock. This distinguishes that total-energy balance from the reduced shallow-water mechanical-energy loss.
