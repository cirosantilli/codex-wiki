For a material volume moving with the fluid, the Reynolds transport theorem gives, componentwise,
dtd​∫V(t)​qdV=∫V(t)​(∂t​q+∇⋅(qu))dV.
(1)
For a scalar q this is also ∫V(t)​(Dq/Dt+q∇⋅u)dV. Apply the component formula to momentum density q=ρui​. Constant density and incompressibility make the integrand ρDui​/Dt.
The traction t(n) is the force per unit area exerted by the material outside a surface on the material inside, with outward unit normal n. Reversing the normal exchanges the two sides of the same internal surface. Thus t(−n)=−t(n) expresses equal and opposite internal contact forces, the local form of Newton's third law.
With no body force, Newton's second law equates the rate of change of the material volume's total momentum to its surface force. Combining this balance with the transport theorem gives
∫V(t)​ρDtDu​dV=∫∂V(t)​t(n)dS.​
(2)
This is a vector identity, since the component argument applies to each direction.

An attached similarity profile must approach f′→A as η→∞. Linearizing about f∼Aη+constant gives, for the small velocity correction g=f′−A,
g′′+2αA​ηg′−2αAg≃0.
(25)

Thus the attached exponential similarity construction requires
αA>0.​
(26)
Equivalently the outer speed increases along the direction of motion, giving a favorable pressure gradient. In the intended flow away from the leading edge along positive x, this means A>0 and α>0. The sign-reversed choice A<0,α<0 describes locally favorable flow in the opposite direction; it is not a layer developing downstream from the stated leading edge.
The asymptotic approximation also requires a geometrically thin layer, away from the leading-edge region. Comparing its thickness with the streamwise variation length 1/∣α∣ gives the local condition Re∣A∣eαx/∣α∣≫1. An exponentially decaying outer speed eventually violates this condition on an infinite downstream interval. These scale and orientation requirements distinguish formal similarity reduction from a uniformly valid attached boundary layer.
