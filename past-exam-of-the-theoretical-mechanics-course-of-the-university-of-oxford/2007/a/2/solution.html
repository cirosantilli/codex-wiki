Let the thin transverse length be Re−1/2 times the longitudinal scale and write Y=Re1/2y, V=Re1/2v. Continuity then remains ux​+VY​=0. Transverse momentum gives pY​=0 to leading order; the quiescent exterior has constant pressure, hence px​=0. Axial viscous diffusion in x is smaller by Re−1, leaving
uux​+VuY​=uYY​,ux​+VY​=0.​
(6)
Symmetry about the jet centreline requires V=0 and uY​=0 at Y=0. At large positive Y, require u→0 and uY​→0. The transverse velocity can approach a finite nonzero scaled entrainment velocity; imposing V→0 as well would rule out the jet. The physical transverse velocity remains smaller by Re−1/2 and matches an entraining outer flow.
Using continuity, the momentum equation is ∂x​(u2)+∂Y​(uV−uY​)=0. Integrating across the jet, the boundary terms vanish because u,uY​→0 while V is finite. Thus
M=∫−∞∞​u2dY=constant.​
(7)
Apart from the dimensional density and velocity/length factors, this is the conserved axial momentum flux supplied by the nozzle. Entrainment increases volume flux without adding axial momentum from the resting exterior.
For u=xαf′(η) with η=Y/xβ, use the stream function ψ=xα+βf(η) so that u=ψY​ and V=−ψx​. Momentum conservation requires 2α+β=0. Inertia and diffusion scale as x2α−1 and xα−2β, so α+2β=1. Therefore α=−1/3,β=2/3​. In particular
V=x−2/3(2ηf′−f)/3.
(8)
Substitution reduces momentum to 3f(3)+ff′′+(f′)2=0, with f(0)=f′′(0)=0 and f′(∞)=0. Integrating first gives 3f′′+ff′=0, the integration constant vanishing at the centreline. A second integration gives 3f′+f2/2=C. Parameterize its positive constant as C=18A2 and solve the resulting Riccati equation with f(0)=0:
f=6Atanh(Aη),u=6A2x−1/3sech2(AYx−2/3).​
(9)
The momentum integral fixes its scale, since ∫−∞∞​sech4zdz=4/3:
M=48A3,A=(M/48)1/3.​
(10)
The upper-half entrainment is V→−2Ax−2/3; the negative side has the opposite sign, drawing ambient fluid inward. This is the Bickley jet, valid in the slender far-downstream laminar regime, rather than in the immediate nozzle region.
