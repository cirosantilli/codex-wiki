Let t(n) denote traction: the force per unit area exerted across an oriented internal surface with unit normal n. Define σij​ as component i of the traction on a coordinate plane whose normal is ej​. Balance of linear momentum on a shrinking tetrahedron makes the surface-force terms, of order area, dominate its inertia and body forces, of order volume. Resolving its oblique face into the three coordinate projected areas gives Cauchy's traction formula
t(n)=σn,ti​(n)=σij​nj​.​
(1)
Opposite faces have opposite tractions, as follows independently from a shrinking pillbox. This fixes both the index and normal conventions.
For a material volume V(t), the Reynolds transport theorem says d∫V(t)​fdV/dt=∫V(t)​[ft​+∇⋅(fu)]dV. Apply it componentwise to f=ρui​ and use mass conservation. With no body force in this calculation, balance of linear momentum and the divergence theorem give
∫V(t)​ρDtDui​​dV=∫∂V(t)​σij​nj​dS=∫V(t)​∂j​σij​dV.
(2)
Arbitrariness of the material volume yields ρDui​/Dt=∂j​σij​​, with D/Dt=∂t​+uj​∂j​.
For constant dynamic viscosity and incompressible velocity, ∂j​uj​=0, so
∂j​σij​=−∂i​p+μ∂j​∂j​ui​+μ∂i​(∂j​uj​)=−∂i​p+μ∇2ui​.
(3)
Thus ρ(ut​+u⋅∇u)=−∇p+μ∇2u​.
In fully developed steady pipe flow u=w(x,y)ez​, the convective acceleration vanishes. The transverse equations give px​=py​=0 and the axial equation gives pz​=μ(wxx​+wyy​). One side depends only on z and the other only on (x,y), so both equal a constant. Hence
wxx​+wyy​=pz​/μ,pz​=constant.​
(4)
If n is outward from the fluid cross-section, the wall-on-fluid axial shear per length is μ∮∂n​wds=μ∫A​ΔwdA=Apz​. The fluid-on-pipe force is its negative:
drag per unit length=−Apz​.​
(5)
For forward pressure-driven flow pz​<0, this force points along the flow. Its value depends on cross-sectional area and pressure drop, even though the detailed velocity profile depends on the shape.
