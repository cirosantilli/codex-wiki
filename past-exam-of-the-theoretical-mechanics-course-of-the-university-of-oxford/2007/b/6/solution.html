For a vector a described in axes rotating at constant angular velocity Ω, the rotating-axes theorem is (da/dt)inertial​=(da/dt)rotating​+Ω×a. Inertial particle velocity is u+Ω×x. Applying the theorem once more gives inertial acceleration Du/Dt+2Ω×u+Ω×(Ω×x). Thus the rotating momentum equation is
DtDu​+2Ω×u+Ω×(Ω×x)=−ρ1​∇p+g.​
(22)
If g=−∇Φ and density is constant, the centrifugal term can be absorbed into the reduced pressure in a rotating fluid:
P=p+ρΦ−2ρ​∣Ω×x∣2,ut​+(u⋅∇)u+2Ω×u=−∇P/ρ.​
(23)
For small disturbances with Ω=Ωez​, linearize to ut​−2Ωv=−Px​/ρ, vt​+2Ωu=−Py​/ρ, wt​=−Pz​/ρ, together with incompressibility. Put ζ=vx​−uy​. Taking the vertical curl gives ζt​=2Ωwz​. Taking divergence gives ∇2P=2ρΩζ. Applying the Laplacian to the vertical momentum equation therefore gives ∂t​∇2w=−2Ωζz​. Another time derivative yields
∂t2​∇2w+4Ω2wzz​=0.​
(24)
All three velocity components are retained, even for fields independent of y; rotation produces the transverse component.
Set m=π/h and seek w=ae−iωtW(x)sinmz. The rigid-wall conditions are then automatic and the scalar equation gives
W′′+m2(ω24Ω2​−1)W=0,W(0)=1.
(25)
For 0<∣ω∣<2∣Ω∣, define q=m4Ω2/ω2−1​. The two possible oscillatory factors are distinguished by energy propagation. With horizontal wavenumber k, the dispersion relation is ω2=4Ω2m2/(k2+m2) and cg,x​=−ωk/(k2+m2). An outgoing wave in x>0 must have ωk<0, giving
w=ae−iωt−isgn(ω)qxsin(πz/h).​
(26)
For ∣ω∣>2∣Ω∣, define κ=m1−4Ω2/ω2​. Reject the exponentially growing branch, leaving the evanescent response
w=ae−iωt−κxsin(πz/h).​
(27)
The real part is the physical velocity. The radiation or bounded-decay condition is needed in addition to the wavemaker boundary value to pick a unique half-channel response. Steady forcing (ω=0) is a singular limit: with nonzero rotation a steady geostrophic balance and impermeable top/bottom require w=0, incompatible with a nonzero imposed sine profile. At the cutoff equality, the bounded outgoing polarization also degenerates. These exceptional cases cannot be obtained by blindly substituting into the two strict-inequality formulas. The propagating sign is the radiation sign for an inertial wave.
