For a steady unidirectional field u=wk, incompressibility gives ∂z​w=0. Thus w=w(x,y) and the convective acceleration vanishes. The transverse momentum equations give px​=py​=0, so p=p(z). The axial equation is
μΔ⊥​w=p′(z).
(8)
Its left side is independent of z and its right side independent of x,y, so both equal a constant G. No slip against the translating wall gives w=U on the boundary. Thus the asserted Poisson problem follows.
For the circular pipe, the axisymmetric solution is
w(r)=U+4μG​(r2−R2).​
(9)
It satisfies the boundary value and Δ⊥​r2=4. Integrating over the cross-section gives
Q=2π∫0R​w(r)rdr=πR2U−8μπR4G​.
(10)
Therefore the laboratory-frame volume flux vanishes precisely when
U=8μR2​dzdp​.​
(11)
For a pressure decrease in the positive axial direction, this requires upstream wall translation.

Y=Re1/2y,V=Re1/2v.​

uux​+VuY​=Us​Us′​+uYY​,ux​+VY​=0.​
(13)

ΨY​ΨxY​−Ψx​ΨYY​=Us​Us′​+ΨYYY​.​

At these parameters the equation becomes g′′+g2−4=0, where g=f′. Multiply by g′ and integrate:
21​(g′)2+31​g3−4g=C.
(18)
Outer matching gives g→−2 and g′→0, so C=16/3. No slip gives g(0)=0. At that point the first integral fixes g′(0)=±42/3​, so there are exactly two smooth initial-value solutions of the second-order equation satisfying the matching condition. Put η0​=arctanh2/3​. Translation invariance of the supplied profile gives them explicitly:
g−​(η)=4−6tanh2(η+η0​),g+​(η)=4−6tanh2(η−η0​).​
(19)
The first decreases monotonically from zero to −2. The second first rises to 4 at η0​, crosses zero again at 2η0​, and then tends to −2. Integrating either profile from zero recovers f with f(0)=0.
One would not expect both branches to be equally stable. The rising branch contains flow opposite to its negative outer stream and interior inflection points, typical ingredients for shear instability. The monotone branch has g′′=4−g2>0 at finite positive η and no interior inflection point, so the classical inviscid inflection-point mechanism is absent there. This motivates expecting the reversed-flow branch to be unstable and the monotone branch to be the stable candidate. A definitive viscous temporal-stability result requires perturbing the time-dependent equations; the steady first integral alone is not a stability proof.

The material derivative D/Dt=∂t​+u⋅∇​ measures change while following a moving gas parcel. The four equations respectively express mass conservation, momentum conservation, internal-energy balance, and the ideal gas law. The first accounts for density change under expansion or compression. The second is Newton's law with pressure as the only force. In the third the specific internal energy is cv​T and pressure work −p∇⋅u heats compression and cools expansion. The fourth relates thermodynamic pressure, density and temperature; R is the gas constant per unit mass.

Use ϕ=F(r)Θ(θ)Z(z)e−iωt. Periodicity gives Θ=cosmθ or sinmθ, m≥0, with only the cosine branch for m=0. The end-wall conditions give Z=cos(ℓπz/h), ℓ≥0. With radial separation constant κ2, the remaining equation is
F′′+r1​F′+(κ2−r2m2​)F=0,ω2=c02​(κ2+h2ℓ2π2​).
(36)
For κ>0, boundedness on the axis selects F=Jm​(κr). The cylindrical wall then requires Jm′​(κa)=0. Thus the modes are real and imaginary parts of
Jm​(km,n​r/a){cosmθ,sinmθ}cos(ℓπz/h)e−iωt,ω2=c02​(a2km,n2​​+h2ℓ2π2​).​
(37)
Here take the positive derivative zeros and include the separate constant radial mode m=0, k=0. For zero radial separation constant, regularity gives rm when m>0, which fails the wall condition, and a constant for m=0. This avoids counting a formal derivative zero at the origin that would make Jm​(0r) identically zero. The completely constant mode has zero frequency and no velocity; the remaining modes are acoustic oscillations. The self-adjoint Neumann Laplacian has these separated modes as a complete system, so no additional nonzero natural frequencies occur.

In the rarefaction fan, right-running characteristics bring the unchanged invariant Q+​=2c0​. The left-running family originates at the removed dam and has speed u−c=x/t. Solving these two relations gives
u=32​(c0​+x/t),c=31​(2c0​−x/t).

c(x,t)={c0​,(2c0​−x/t)/3,​−a<x<−c0​t,−c0​t≤x<2c0​t,​​
