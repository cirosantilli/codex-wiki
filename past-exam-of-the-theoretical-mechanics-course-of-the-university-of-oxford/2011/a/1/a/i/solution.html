For a material volume moving with the fluid, the Reynolds transport theorem gives, componentwise,
dtd​∫V(t)​qdV=∫V(t)​(∂t​q+∇⋅(qu))dV.
(1)
For a scalar q this is also ∫V(t)​(Dq/Dt+q∇⋅u)dV. Apply the component formula to momentum density q=ρui​. Constant density and incompressibility make the integrand ρDui​/Dt.
The traction t(n) is the force per unit area exerted by the material outside a surface on the material inside, with outward unit normal n. Reversing the normal exchanges the two sides of the same internal surface. Thus t(−n)=−t(n) expresses equal and opposite internal contact forces, the local form of Newton's third law.
With no body force, Newton's second law equates the rate of change of the material volume's total momentum to its surface force. Combining this balance with the transport theorem gives
∫V(t)​ρDtDu​dV=∫∂V(t)​t(n)dS.​
(2)
This is a vector identity, since the component argument applies to each direction.
