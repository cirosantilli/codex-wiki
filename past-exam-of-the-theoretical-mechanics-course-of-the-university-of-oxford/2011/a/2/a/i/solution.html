Take the tangential length and velocity scales as order one. In the boundary layer, continuity reads ux​+(δ2​/δ1​)VY​=0, so a nontrivial balance requires δ2​∼δ1​. Tangential inertia is order one, and the normal viscous term has size Re−1δ1−2​. Balancing them gives
δ1​=δ2​=Re−1/2​

up to constants absorbable in the scaled variables. Streamwise diffusion is then smaller by Re−1. The normal momentum equation has a pressure term of size δ1−1​pY​ and inertial/viscous terms of size δ1​, hence pY​=0 at leading order. The remaining Prandtl boundary-layer equations are
uux​+VuY​=−px​+uYY​,pY​=0,ux​+VY​=0.​
(14)
The boundary conditions are no slip and no penetration at the plate, and matching to the outer tangential velocity:
u(x,0)=0,V(x,0)=0,u(x,Y)⟶Us​(x)(Y→∞).​
(15)
The outer inviscid tangential momentum equation supplies px​=−Us​Us′​; pressure is shared between the boundary layer and outer flow because it has no leading normal variation.
