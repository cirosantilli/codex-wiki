Two-toss sample space (Ω={HH,HT,TH,TT})

 ... Mathematics Area of mathematics Probability and statistics Probability theory Probability space Sample space
OurBigBook.com Words: 482 Articles: 1
Model two fair independent coin tosses, recording their order. Here H denotes heads and T tails, and
Ω={HH,HT,TH,TT},F=P(Ω),P({ω})=41​(ω∈Ω).
(2)
The power set P(Ω) includes every question expressible as a subset of these four outcomes. The mass 1/4=(1/2)(1/2) comes from the fairness and independence assumptions. These are exact model probabilities, rather than measured toss frequencies.
Let A be the event that the first toss is heads and B the event that the second toss is heads. Then
A={HH,HT},B={HH,TH},A∪B={HH,HT,TH},A∩B={HH}.
(3)
The union means “at least one head,” while the intersection means “both heads.” Their probabilities are 3/4 and 1/4. Counting the union through the inclusion-exclusion principle gives 1/2+1/2−1/4=3/4; alternatively its complement of an event is {TT}, of probability 1/4.
On the same sample space, assigning masses 3/8,1/8,1/8,3/8 to HH,HT,TH,TT would still make each toss fair: P(A)=P(B)=1/2. But P(A∩B)=3/8=1/4, so the tosses would be dependent. The event of at least one head would have probability 5/8. Changing the probability measure changes the answer even when the sample space and the single-toss probabilities stay the same.
