Reciprocal lattice change in a structural phase transition

 ... Quantum mechanics Quantum scattering Scattering amplitude Crystal scattering Bravais lattice Reciprocal lattice
OurBigBook.com Words: 118
The reciprocal lattice of a body-centered cubic lattice is face-centred cubic, and that of a face-centered cubic lattice is body-centred cubic. In both cases its conventional cubic side is 4π/a if the direct conventional side is a. Thus a direct BCC-to-FCC phase transition reverses the reciprocal lattice types. The reciprocal primitive cell volume is (2π)3/Ω, so thermal expansion contracts reciprocal spacings, while a discontinuous change in direct primitive unit cell volume gives an inverse change in reciprocal cell volume.
A first-order structural transition need not continuously deform one perfect reciprocal array into another. Phase coexistence, new orientation variants, domains and loss of single-crystal alignment can produce two sets of diffraction peaks or many differently oriented sets. The two endpoint lattice types alone do not specify the transformation path or orientation relationship.
