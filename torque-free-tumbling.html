Torque-free tumbling (c2​=e^2​⋅L^)

A useful readout of a tumble is the alignment of the intermediate principal axis e^2​ with the conserved angular momentum direction L^. In the principal body frame, write Li​=Ii​ωi​ and L=∣L∣. Then
c2​(t)=e^2​(t)⋅L^=LL2​(t)​.
(11)
A change from c2​≃+1 to c2​≃−1 means that the directed body axis has turned nearly 180∘ relative to L. It does not mean that L reversed in the fixed space frame. The unsigned line through that principal axis hides this distinction, so a marked end matters in an actual demonstration.
For the normalized intermediate-axis model with ω(0)=(0.01,1,0), the rotational kinetic energy and squared angular momentum are E=1.00005 and L2=4.0001. Hence L2−2EI2​=−0.0001: this initial condition lies close to, but off, the intermediate-axis separatrix. An independent calculation evolves L˙body​=Lbody​×I−1Lbody​ and a rotation matrix taking body components to space components. The intermediate principal axis crosses c2​=0 at normalized times approximately 10.3773 and 31.1319, alternating long nearly aligned intervals with quicker reversals.
Both calculations use step size 0.005 without rescaling either invariant. Over 0≤t≤50, the tumbling calculation changes rotational kinetic energy and squared angular momentum by less than 4×10−13 relative to their initial values. Reconstructing L in the fixed space frame gives relative error below 3×10−12. Halving the step changes the body components and rotation matrix entries by less than 3×10−10. These checks distinguish physical tumbling from numerical drift. On the exact intermediate-axis separatrix, approach to an intermediate-axis rotation takes infinite time; the recurrent off-separatrix example here is different.
