A deck transformation of a covering p:X→X is a homeomorphism d:X→X with p∘d=p. A connected covering is a regular covering map if its deck transformation group acts transitively on each fiber; for a path-connected base, transitivity on the fiber over the chosen basepoint is equivalent.
Write H=p∗​π1​(X,b) and G=π1​(X,b). A based loop represents an element of H exactly when its lift starting at b is closed. Indeed a closed lift itself projects to a representative of an image element; conversely the image of a closed path lifts to that path, and the closed-endpoint property is unchanged by based homotopy.
Suppose the covering is regular, let h∈H, and let g∈G. Lift a representative of g from b to an endpoint v. By regularity some deck transformation sends b to v. Applying it to the closed lift of h gives a closed lift of h at v, by uniqueness in the path lifting theorem. The concatenation of the lift of g, that closed lift of h, and the reverse lift of g is therefore a closed lift of ghg−1. Thus gHg−1⊆H; applying the same argument to g−1 gives equality. Hence a regular covering has a normal image subgroup.

Choose the spanning tree consisting of the first five x edges from B to U. The tree paths to B,V,L,T,C,U have labels 1,x,x2,x3,x4,x5. For every directed non-tree edge vz​w, the path from B along the tree to v, then along the edge, then back along the tree from w, has label tv​ztw−1​. Collapsing a spanning tree turns a connected graph into a wedge of circles, so these paths form a free basis of its fundamental group. There are seven non-tree edges, since 12−6+1=7. For a connected covering of a connected, locally path-connected, semilocally simply connected space, the classification of connected covering spaces says that the image subgroup is normal if and only if the covering is regular. The two-circle wedge and this connected finite graph satisfy those hypotheses. Since regularity fails, H is not normal in the base group π1​(X,b)≅F(x,y). All normality comparisons here are in that base group.
