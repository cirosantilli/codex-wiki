Use a topological graph consisting of the ray [0,∞) with vertices vn​=n and one oriented graph loop ℓn​ attached at each vn​, for n=0,1,2,…. Set the base point to b=v0​. This is a connected, locally finite graph; it is deliberately infinite.
Define continuous maps f,g:X→X by
f(t)g(t)​=t+1=max(t−1,0)​​on the ray,on the ray,​f(ℓn​(s))g(ℓn​(s))​=ℓn+1​(s),={b,ℓn−1​(s),​n=0,n≥1.​​
(50)
Here each graph loop is parametrized by s∈[0,1] with its endpoints at the attaching vertex. The formulas agree at every vertex, and their restrictions to every closed edge are continuous. The weak topology of a CW complex therefore makes f,g continuous maps. Directly on the ray and on each graph loop,
gf=idX​.​
(51)
We check the two fundamental group maps at exactly the specified base points. The ray is a spanning tree. At b=v0​ a free basis of a group consists of an​, obtained by going along the ray to vn​, following ℓn​, and returning. At f(b)=v1​, use the corresponding free basis of a group cn​ with connecting paths starting at v1​; in particular c0​ travels backwards along the first edge before following ℓ0​. The standard spanning tree description applies to this infinite topological graph as well: every loop and every homotopy between based loops has compact image, so lies in a finite subcomplex.
The induced group homomorphisms are
f∗​(an​)=cn+1​,g∗​(c0​)=1,g∗​(cn​)=an−1​ (n≥1).​
(52)
Thus f∗​ misses c0​: words in c1​,c2​,… cannot equal c0​ by the normal form for free groups and free product of groups. Consequently f∗​ is not surjective. Meanwhile c0​ is a nontrivial free group element killed by g∗​, so g∗​ is not injective. Neither induced map is a group isomorphism, while functoriality of the fundamental group correctly gives g∗​f∗​=id.
