Assume p has a common finite degree of a covering map d. Declare the lifted vertices to be p−1(V(X)), so there are exactly dv(X) of them. Each open edge is homeomorphic to (0,1), a simply connected interval. The restriction of the covering map above it is a disjoint union of d copies of that open edge.
These copies acquire endpoints as follows. Lift the parametrizing path [0,1]→X of each oriented edge, starting at each of the d points over its initial vertex. The path lifting theorem gives a unique lift all the way to 1. Its endpoint lies over the terminal vertex, and the interiors of these lifted paths are exactly the d open edges already described. This also handles a graph loop, whose two endpoints may coincide downstairs or upstairs. Local covering sheets above a small star at a vertex show that the lifted incidence relations have precisely the original topology. Thus the graph structure lifted through a covering map is a finite topological graph with
v(X)=dv(X),e(X)=de(X).
(47)
Subtracting gives the Euler characteristic under a finite covering formula
χ(X)=dχ(X).​
(48)
The formula holds even when X is disconnected. If a disconnected base is allowed to have degrees dC​ that vary with its connected components C, the precise replacement is
χ(X)=∑C​dC​χ(C).
(49)
The printed formula with a single d uses the constant-degree convention explained in part (a)(ii).
