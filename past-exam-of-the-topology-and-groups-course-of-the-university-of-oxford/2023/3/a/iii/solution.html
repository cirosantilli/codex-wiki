With local path connectedness, the intended conclusion holds. Choose a base point x0​ and the standard covering map
p:R⟶S1,p(t)=e2πit.
(38)
The fundamental group of the circle is Z. Since the image of a finite group under a group homomorphism is finite and Z has no nontrivial finite subgroups,
f∗​:π1​(X,x0​)⟶π1​(S1,f(x0​))≅Z
(39)
is trivial. If X is also locally path-connected, the lifting criterion for a covering space supplies a continuous map f​:X→R with pf​=f, because p∗​π1​(R)=0. The homotopy
H(x,s)=p((1−s)f​(x)+sf​(x0​))​
(40)
runs from f to the constant function f(x0​). This proves the claimed null-homotopy under that additional hypothesis, in particular for CW complexes and the topological graphs considered later in the paper.
For arbitrary path-connected spaces, the printed assertion is false. Here is an explicit Warsaw circle counterexample, including the obstruction that the usual lifting argument misses. In the Euclidean plane take
G={(x,sin(π/x)):0<x≤1},L={0}×[−1,1].
(41)
Join P=(1,0) to Q=(0,−1) by the polygonal arc A passing successively through (2,0),(2,−2),(0,−2), and put W=G∪L∪A with its subspace topology. The arc meets G∪L only at P,Q. The topologist's sine curve G∪L has two path components, but the added arc joins them, making W path-connected.
Every loop in W is contained in
Tδ​=L∪A∪{(x,sin(π/x)):δ≤x≤1}
(42)
for some δ>0. To justify the assertion, a path cannot reach L along the oscillating tail: the intermediate value theorem for its x-coordinate would force its y-coordinate to attain both 1 and −1 arbitrarily close to the supposed arrival time, contradicting continuity. The only alternative route from the tail to L uses P and the joining arc. If a loop visited tail points with x→0, a convergent subsequence of their parameter values would approach a time at which the loop lies in L. On a sufficiently small parameter interval around that time its image avoids P. That would give a forbidden path from L into the tail without passing through P. Thus those tail coordinates have a positive lower bound. The space Tδ​ is a finite tree, in fact a single arc, so every such loop contracts. Consequently
π1​(W)=1.
(43)
Now define a continuous map h:W→S1 to be 1 on G∪L, and to make one full turn on A: for a parametrization a:[0,1]→A from P to Q, set h(a(t))=e2πit. The pieces G∪L and A are closed in W, and the formulas agree at P,Q, so the pasting lemma for closed subspaces proves continuity.
There is no continuous lift h:W→R. Any such lift is integer-valued on G∪L, so it is constant on each of the connected spaces G,L. Since G accumulates on L, continuity forces those two constants to be the same. But along A a lift of e2πit has the form t+k, giving values at Q and P differing by 1, a contradiction. Finally, if h had a null-homotopy, the homotopy lifting theorem for a covering map, starting with a lift of the final constant function and reversing time, would produce a continuous lift of h. This theorem needs no local path connectedness of the parameter space. Hence h is not null-homotopic, although W has finite, indeed trivial, fundamental group.
The native PDF explicitly assumes only path connectedness here. The missing local path connectedness cannot be inferred from the finiteness of the fundamental group.
