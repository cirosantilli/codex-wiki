Past exam of the topology and groups course of the University of Oxford / 2023 / 2 / b / ii / Solution

OurBigBook.com Words: 220
Let n=(0,0,1) and use the open cover
U=Y∖{n},V={(x,y,z)∈S2:z>1/2}.
(33)
The cap V is open in Y: it equals Y∩{z>1/2} because the attached chord has z=0. The cap is homeomorphic to an open disk, and U∩V=V∖{n} is a punctured open disk, hence homotopy equivalent to a circle. Both sets and their intersection are path-connected. Choose the base point b in that intersection.
We first calculate π1​(U,b). A stereographic projection identifies S2∖{n} with R2 and sends the two chord endpoints to (−1,0),(1,0). Thus U becomes the Euclidean plane with an extra closed interval attached at those two points. This description is a homeomorphism: gluing the two closed pieces along their endpoints gives exactly the original subspace topology.
The Euclidean plane has a deformation retraction onto L=[−1,1]×{0} while fixing its endpoints. An explicit homotopy is
D((u,v),t)=((1−t)u+tc(u),(1−t)v),c(u)=max(−1,min(1,u)).
(34)
Extend it by the identity map on the extra chord. The pasting lemma for closed subspaces gives a deformation retraction of U onto two distinct closed intervals sharing just their endpoints, a circle. Consequently π1​(U,b)≅Z, with the routine base point transport if the retraction moves b.
The inclusion-induced group homomorphism π1​(U∩V,b)→π1​(U,b) is trivial: it factors through S2∖{n}≅R2, which is contractible. Also π1​(V,b)=1. All hypotheses of the Seifert-van Kampen theorem are now checked, and it gives the fundamental group of a sphere with an attached chord:
π1​(Y,b)≅Z∗Z​1≅Z,​
(35)
where both maps from the amalgamating infinite cyclic group are trivial. A group generator follows the chord from one endpoint to the other and returns along any path on the sphere, with a connecting path from the chosen base point.
