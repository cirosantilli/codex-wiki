Sky projection of the Galactic-centre orbit model (θ=(x/D,y/D))

 ... Astrophysics Astrophysical black hole Supermassive black hole Sagittarius A* Stellar orbits at the Galactic centre Galactic-centre point-mass orbit model
OurBigBook.com Words: 171
Let z increase away from the observer, with x,y spanning the sky plane. A rotation R=Rz​(Ω)Rx​(i)Rz​(ω) sends the orbital-plane coordinates to r=Rr′. Here i is orbital inclination, Ω the longitude of ascending node, and ω the argument of periapsis. For distance D≫a, the small-angle model gives angular positions θx​=x/D, θy​=y/D in radians. Doppler spectroscopy instead supplies the line-of-sight velocity vlos​=z˙+vsys​, with a fitted systemic offset vsys​.
Define α=a/D, the angular scale of the deprojected fitted orbit. It is not generally the semi-major axis of the apparent ellipse on the sky. Projection also need not send a true focus to a focus of the apparent ellipse. Fitting the rotated orbit preserves that distinction; measuring a bounding ellipse alone loses it.
