Sagnac effect (Δt≃4Ω⋅A/c2)

 Home Physics Branches of physics Special relativity
OurBigBook.com Words: 1k Articles: 5
The Sagnac effect is a difference in return times, or an associated interference fringe displacement, for light sent in opposite directions around a closed optical path in a rotating apparatus. The light leaves one beam splitter, travels around the same loop in opposite senses, and returns to the rotating beam splitter and readout. For a slowly rotating apparatus in flat spacetime, the leading laboratory arrival-time difference is
Δt=t+​−t−​≃c24Ω⋅A​,A=21​∮C​r×dr.
(41)
Here c is the vacuum speed of light, Ω is the apparatus's angular velocity relative to an inertial frame, and A is the vector area oriented by the chosen positive direction around the closed curve C. With positive rotation about the upward normal vector, the counterclockwise beam is labelled + and arrives later. Reversing rotation, or reversing the orientation convention alone, changes the sign of Δt.
The surprise is that a small loop can reveal its rotation by comparing two beams locally, even though each local vacuum speed of light measurement gives c. A rotating reference frame is not a single inertial frame: a beam must catch the moving receiver after a full circuit. The Sagnac area law makes this difference a practical way to measure angular velocity, while the Relativistic Sagnac delay on a circular ring distinguishes laboratory coordinate time from the receiver's proper time.
