In x,y>0, the characteristic equation of a partial differential equation gives dx/x​=±y​dy. Integration yields the characteristic coordinates
ξ=x​+3y3/2​,η=x​−3y3/2​.
(19)
Their Jacobian determinant is −y​/(2x​)=0, so they are valid independent coordinates. To see cancellation of the lower-order terms explicitly, first put r=x​, s=y3/2/3 and u(x,y)=U(r,s). The chain rule gives
ux​=2x​Ur​​,uxx​=4xUrr​​−4x3/2Ur​​,uy​=2y​​Us​,uyy​=4y​Uss​+4y​Us​​.
(20)
Substitution cancels the first-derivative terms, leaving Uss​−Urr​=0. With ξ=r+s, η=r−s and U=V(ξ,η), the characteristic normal form is Vξη​=0. Integrating once in each variable gives
u(x,y)=g(x​+3y3/2​)+h(x​−3y3/2​),​
(21)
where g,h are arbitrary twice continuously differentiable functions on the relevant coordinate intervals. This is a curvilinear reduction to the one-dimensional wave equation.
