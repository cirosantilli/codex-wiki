One suitable autonomous planar system is
x˙=y2−1,y˙​=x2−1.​
(57)
Its equilibrium points are exactly the four combinations (x,y)=(±1,±1). Its Jacobian matrix is
J(x,y)=(02x​2y0​),λ2=4xy.
(58)
Thus (−1,−1) and (1,1) have eigenvalues ±2 and are saddle equilibria. At the other two points the eigenvalues are ±2i. To establish the nonlinear centre equilibrium classifications, use the first integral
H(x,y)=3x3​−x−3y3​+y,H˙=(x2−1)(y2−1)−(y2−1)(x2−1)=0.
(59)
Its Hessian matrix is diag(2x,−2y), a negative-definite matrix at (−1,1) and a positive-definite matrix at (1,−1). Thus each is a strict local extremum, and nearby regular level sets are closed curves supporting periodic orbits, by the planar first integral criterion for a centre.
Immediately to the right of (−1,1), the vector field points down because x2−1<0, so that centre is clockwise. Immediately to the right of (1,−1) it points up because x2−1>0, so that centre is counterclockwise. This verifies both the nonlinear classifications and the prescribed orientations, with exactly four equilibrium points.
