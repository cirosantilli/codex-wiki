For the Weierstrass M-test proof of the Picard-Lindelöf theorem, the required bound is
Mh≤k,with no additional restriction on Lh.​
(18)
To see this, use Picard iteration starting with y0​(x)=b and
yn+1​(x)=b+∫ax​f(s,yn​(s))ds.
(19)
If yn​ takes its values in [b−k,b+k], then ∣yn+1​−b∣≤M∣x−a∣≤Mh≤k. Thus every Picard iterate remains in the rectangle where the hypotheses hold. Successive applications of the Lipschitz condition and integration give the factorial estimate for Picard iteration
∣yn+1​(x)−yn​(x)∣≤(n+1)!MLn∣x−a∣n+1​.
(20)
For example, the n=0 estimate is M∣x−a∣, and integrating the nth estimate multiplied by L proves the next one. The sum of the uniform bounds is
∑n=0∞​(n+1)!MLnhn+1​<∞
(21)
for every finite Lh, by the exponential series. Hence the telescoping series of Picard iterates converges uniformly by the Weierstrass M-test. The uniform limit stays in the closed rectangle, and the Lipschitz condition permits passage to the limit in the integral equation. The fundamental theorem of calculus then gives the desired solution of a differential equation on [a−h,a+h].
