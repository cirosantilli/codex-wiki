At an equilibrium point, x=−y2 and x2=a2y, so y(y3−a2)=0. With d=∣a∣2/3>0, the two equilibrium points and their Jacobian matrix are
(0,0),(−d2,d),​J(x,y)=(2x1​−a22y​).
(23)
At (−d2,d), detJ=−3a2<0. The trace-determinant stability criterion therefore gives an unstable saddle equilibrium. At the origin, the eigenvalues are λ=±i∣a∣: its linearization is a center equilibrium. The linearization stability theorem does not classify this nonhyperbolic equilibrium of the nonlinear system.
Nonlinear classification. In fact the answer depends on ∣a∣:
0<∣a∣<1∣a∣=1∣a∣>1​unstable weak focus at the originstable center at the originasymptotically stable weak focus at the origin.​​
(24)
Here is an explicit derivation, rather than an inference from the imaginary eigenvalues. When a2=1, set L=1−x+y. Near the origin L>0, and the first integral
H(x,y)=L1+xy​+logL
(25)
has
Hx​=L2x+y2​,Hy​=L2y−x2​,dtdH​=0,H=1+2x2+y2​+O((x2+y2)3/2).
(26)
Its Hessian matrix at the origin is the identity. Small level curves are closed ovals on which the vector field is nonzero, proving the center equilibrium classification and Lyapunov stability.
For a2=1, a higher-order Lyapunov function calculation settles the issue. Put k=∣a∣, U=x, V=ky, τ=kt, p=k−1 and q=k−2. Then
Uτ​=−V+pU2,Vτ​=U+qV2.
(27)
Write R2=U2+V2 and introduce the differential operators
L=−V∂U​+U∂V​,N=pU2∂U​+qV2∂V​.
(28)
Successively cancel the terms of degrees three, four and five in the derivative of R2+H3​+H4​+H5​, using these homogeneous polynomials:
H3​H4​H5​​=−2pU2V−34​pV3+34​qU3+2qUV2,=2p2U2V2−4pq(U3V+UV3)+(p2−q2)V4,=−154q​(p2+8q2)U5+316q​(p2−q2)U3V2−34p​(p2−q2)U2V3+4q(p2−q2)UV4+154p​(11q2−2p2)V5.​
(29)
Direct differentiation verifies LH3​=−NR2, LH4​=−NH3​ and LH5​=−NH4​. The remaining degree-six term has circular average
c=2π1​∫02π​(NH5​)(cosθ,sinθ)dθ=4pq(q2−p2)​=4k71−k2​.
(30)
For example, the only nonzero averages of degree-six monomials needed here are ⟨U6⟩=⟨V6⟩=5/16 and ⟨U4V2⟩=⟨U2V4⟩=1/16. Since L=∂θ​ in polar coordinates, the zero-average part can be cancelled by a degree-six homogeneous polynomial H6​ satisfying
LH6​=cR6−NH5​.
(31)
Indeed its restriction to the unit circle is obtained by integrating a zero-average trigonometric polynomial; its Fourier modes have orders 2,4,6, so multiplication by R6 gives a polynomial. Consequently the positive Lyapunov function W=R2+H3​+H4​+H5​+H6​ satisfies
dτdW​=cR6+O(R7).
(32)
For k>1 this is strictly negative near the origin, giving asymptotic stability. For k<1 it is strictly positive, and Wτ​≥CW3 in a sufficiently small neighbourhood; every nonzero starting point must eventually leave that neighbourhood. Finally θτ​=1+O(R)>0, so the nearby trajectories rotate: these are weak foci, with the stability stated above. Merely calling the nonlinear origin a center for every a=0 would miss this distinction.
