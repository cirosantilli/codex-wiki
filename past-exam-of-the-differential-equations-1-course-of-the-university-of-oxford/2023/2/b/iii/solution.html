The condition s<1 is essential: at s=1 there is finite-time blowup, so the opposite branch cannot be reached from the data. From r=(1−ssinθ)−1 and y=rsinθ,
s=yr−1​.

The ratio inside the cube root is positive precisely on the two data-determined regions

On D+​ each point traces to the upper semicircle, and on D−​ to the lower semicircle, with s<1. To check the parameter ranges explicitly, for η=sinθ>0 they are −∞<s<1, whereas for η<0 they are 1/η<s<1. At the latter lower endpoint the planar projection escapes to infinity. The inverse function theorem and uniqueness for the characteristic ordinary differential equations give a unique smooth solution on each region. Equivalently, each point has the unique foot (x/r,y/r) and parameter (r−1)/y.
A direct check is particularly short. For s=(r−1)/y, let D=xy∂x​+y2∂y​. Then Dr=yr, Dy=y2, and
Ds=y2(yr)y−(r−1)y2​=1.
(42)
Since dz/ds=−z4/3, the displayed formula solves the partial differential equation, and r=1 gives z=−1 on both open semicircles. The finite boundary where r=1+y, away from the axis, is the parabola y=(x2−1)/2 and corresponds to blowup. Points on y=0 cannot be added to a classical continuation of these regions: where they are approached from a data-determined region, the height behaves like a nonzero multiple of ∣y∣1/3, or has incompatible limits at the endpoints. The formula is therefore the complete classical answer for the compatible arcs, while the full-circle problem as printed has no classical solution.
