Runge's phenomenon

 ... Mathematics Area of mathematics Analysis Numerical analysis Interpolation Polynomial interpolation
OurBigBook.com Words: 295
The Runge phenomenon is the failure of increasing-degree polynomial interpolation at equally spaced interpolation nodes to converge to some smooth target functions, with large oscillations near the ends of the interval. It is a property of the approximation problem, and can occur even with exact arithmetic. A numerically stable evaluation method cannot remove it. Endpoint clustering, as in Chebyshev-Lobatto nodes, changes the approximation problem and can greatly improve it.[ref]
Take f(x)=1/(1+25x2) on [−1,1] and polynomial degree at most 10. Use either xk​=−1+k/5 or xk​=cos(kπ/10) for k=0,…,10. Each interpolation polynomial passes through all eleven of its own samples, yet the first has severe endpoint oscillations and the second stays much closer to the target function. Evaluating both by the barycentric interpolation formula on 20,001 equally spaced points, including the endpoints, gives sampled maximum absolute errors of approximately 1.915659 and 0.132197, respectively: about a 14.49-fold reduction. These are maxima over the stated grid, not certified maxima over the continuous interval. The generator separately checks exact reproduction of all stored nodal values.
The polynomial interpolation error formula explains why interpolation nodes enter the analysis. If f has n+1 continuous derivatives on an interval containing x and all the interpolation nodes, then for a suitable ξx​ in that interval,
f(x)−pn​(x)=(n+1)!f(n+1)(ξx​)​∏i=0n​(x−xi​).
(143)
At an interpolation node the error is zero directly. Away from the interpolation nodes, both the product and the high-order derivative matter. A short proof applies Rolle's theorem repeatedly to f(t)−pn​(t)−[f(x)−pn​(x)]∏i​(t−xi​)/∏i​(x−xi​), which vanishes at n+2 distinct points.[ref]
The successful comparison here does not assert that Chebyshev-Lobatto nodes solve every approximation problem for every function. The derivatives, the polynomial degree, and measurement errors still matter. The Lebesgue constant of interpolation measures worst-case amplification of nodal errors, while the polynomial reproduction error bound relates interpolation to the best uniform approximation available at that polynomial degree.
