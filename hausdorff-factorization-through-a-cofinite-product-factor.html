Hausdorff factorization through a cofinite product factor (F:Z×T→Y continuous ⟹ F=g∘πT​)

 ... Mathematics Area of mathematics Geometry and topology Topology Cofinite topology Continuous maps from an infinite cofinite space to a Hausdorff space are constant
OurBigBook.com Words: 103
Let Z be a nonempty infinite set with the cofinite topology, let T be any topological space, and let Y be Hausdorff. For a continuous map F:Z×T→Y, every slice z↦F(z,t) is constant. Thus F(z,t)=g(t), where g(t)=F(z0​,t) for any fixed z0​. The map g is continuous, since t↦(z0​,t) is continuous into the product topology. It is unique because the coordinate projection πT​ is a surjection. If F is a surjection, so is g.
For T=[0,1] and Y homeomorphic to the circle, a surjective g cannot be injective: otherwise the compact-to-Hausdorff continuous bijection theorem would contradict the fact that the circle and interval are not homeomorphic. Hence distinct a,b∈[0,1] satisfy F(z,a)=F(z,b) simultaneously for all z∈Z.
