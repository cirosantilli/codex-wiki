Iterated collinear boosts with equal rapidity (un​=ctanh(nα))

 ... Physics Branches of physics Special relativity Lorentz transformation Lorentz boost Rapidity
OurBigBook.com Words: 246
Starting from rest, compose n positive collinear Lorentz boosts, each with the same finite rapidity α>0. Each step has relative velocity w=ctanhα, measured in the preceding rest inertial frame. The relativistic velocity-addition formula gives
u0​=0,un+1​=1+un​w/c2un​+w​,θn​=nα,un​=ctanh(nα).
(23)
These are equal increments of rapidity, not equal increments of velocity measured in the original inertial frame. They can describe a chain of relatively moving inertial frames or successive idealized changes of a massive particle's motion; no particular acceleration schedule is assumed. [ref].
For a relative velocity w=c/2 at each step,
α=artanh(1/2)=21​ln3,cun​​=3n+13n−1​.
(24)
The first four velocities are u0​=0, u1​=c/2, u2​=4c/5, and u3​=13c/14. At step eight, u8​/c=3280/3281≈0.99969521. The rapidities grow by the same amount at each step, while the successive velocity increments become smaller.
A short derivation applies the hyperbolic-function identity for tanh(a+b) to the recurrence, giving θn+1​=θn​+α. Substituting tanhx=(e2x−1)/(e2x+1) and e2α=3 gives the displayed closed expression. In particular,
1−cun​​=3n+12​>0
(25)
for every finite nonnegative integer n. The limit is un​→c as n→∞, but no finite sequence reaches the speed of light. This illustrates the beauty of rapidity: unbounded additive parameters coexist with bounded physical velocities. Replacing every step by a negative rapidity gives the corresponding approach to −c.
