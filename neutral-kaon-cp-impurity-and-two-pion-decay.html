Neutral-kaon CP impurity and two-pion decay (∣KL​⟩=1+∣ϵ∣2​∣K2​⟩+ϵ∣K1​⟩​)

Use the kaon CP eigenstates convention CP∣K0⟩=−∣Kˉ0⟩, matching kaon CP mixing parameter. Then ∣K1​⟩=(∣K0⟩−∣Kˉ0⟩)/2​ is CP even, while ∣K2​⟩=(∣K0⟩+∣Kˉ0⟩)/2​ is CP odd. With CPT symmetry and a common small mixing parameter, the physical states can be written
∣KS​⟩=1+∣ϵ∣2​∣K1​⟩+ϵ∣K2​⟩​,∣KL​⟩=1+∣ϵ∣2​∣K2​⟩+ϵ∣K1​⟩​.
(13)
Here S,L mean short and long mean lifetimes; the kaon CP mixing parameter ϵ depends on the flavor-state phase convention. A positive flavor-exchange convention instead makes the symmetric flavor combination CP even, as explained in exchange-positive CP convention for neutral kaon mixing. Mixing these conventions would reverse the interpretation of the written sums.
Why does the two-pion final state test CP symmetry? A spin-zero kaon decaying into two spin-zero pions has relative orbital angular momentum ℓ=0 by angular momentum conservation. Two negative intrinsic parities multiply to plus, and (−1)ℓ=1. Charge conjugation exchanges π+ and π−; the ℓ=0 state is even under this exchange. Hence this two-pion state has CP eigenvalue +1. If a decay transition operator T preserves CP symmetry, inserting CP on both sides of ⟨ππ∣T∣K2​⟩ changes its sign without changing its value: that decay amplitude must vanish.
Assume for illustration that CP violation occurs only through the state admixture and the decay transition preserves CP symmetry. Put A1​=⟨ππ∣T∣K1​⟩. Then
AL​=1+∣ϵ∣2​ϵA1​​,AS​=1+∣ϵ∣2​A1​​,ηππ​=AS​AL​​=ϵ.
(14)
Neglecting the tiny difference in two-body Lorentz-invariant phase space between the physical masses gives ΓLππ​/ΓSππ​≃∣ϵ∣2. A branching fraction, however, divides a partial decay width by the total decay width. With ℏ=1, τi​=1/Γi​, so
BSππ​BLππ​​≃∣ϵ∣2ΓL​ΓS​​=∣ϵ∣2τS​τL​​.
(15)
For chosen values ∣ϵ∣=0.01 and τL​/τS​=100, the branching fraction ratio is 0.01, whereas the partial decay width ratio is 0.0001. This is original arithmetic, not a historical fit. The longer observation time amplifies the integrated fraction relative to the instantaneous partial decay rate.
