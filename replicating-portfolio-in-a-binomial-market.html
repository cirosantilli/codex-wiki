Replicating portfolio in a binomial market (Δ=(Vu​−Vd​)/(Su​−Sd​),c0​=(Vd​−ΔSd​)/R)

 ... Mathematics Area of mathematics Mathematical optimization Mathematical finance Binomial options pricing model Discrete-time binomial market
OurBigBook.com Words: 287 Articles: 2
Let c0​ be the initial monetary balance in the bank account, negative for borrowing. Matching successor financial payoffs gives the linear system
ΔSu​+Rc0​=Vu​,ΔSd​+Rc0​=Vd​.
(16)
Subtracting determines the stock holding Δ in the header; substitution determines c0​. The initial cost is V0​=ΔS0​+c0​. For the European call option with Su​=120, Sd​=80, strike price 100 and payoffs 20 and zero,
Δ=120−8020−0​=21​,c0​=21/200−(1/2)80​=−21800​,V0​=50−21800​=21250​.
(17)
The debt becomes 40 at maturity: half a share minus 40 yields 60−40=20 in the up state and 40−40=0 in the down state. Holding these positions until maturity is self-financing. Repeating this two-equation construction at earlier nodes gives backward option pricing and the unique price in the complete market.
The discrete replication method was developed in Cox, Ross and Rubinstein's 1979 paper, whose Sections 2–3 explain borrowing, statewise matching and why physical probability drops out of relative option valuation. Their symbols for physical and pricing probabilities differ from the p,q convention here. [ref]
