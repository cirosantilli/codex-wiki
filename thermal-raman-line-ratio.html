Thermal Raman line ratio (IAS​/IS​)

 Home Physics Branches of physics Optics Scattering of light Raman scattering
OurBigBook.com Words: 360 Articles: 1
Assume one harmonic normal mode at thermal equilibrium, weak spontaneous excitation far from electronic resonances, the same collection solid angle and wave polarization, and matched response after instrumental correction. The Boltzmann distribution gives
r=exp(−kB​Thνv​​),Pv​=(1−r)rv,v=1−rr​.
(41)
Here kB​ is the Boltzmann constant, T>0 is temperature, and Pv​ is the probability of vibrational quantum number v. Thus P1​/P0​=r. Linear coupling to Q has squared oscillator matrix elements proportional to v+1 for Stokes Raman scattering and v for Anti-Stokes Raman scattering. Summing over initial levels gives strengths proportional to v+1 and v respectively; their ratio is r, even when levels above v=1 are occupied.
Let IS​ and IAS​ denote line-integrated radiant intensities, meaning power per collection solid angle. The off-resonant electric dipole radiation law adds the fourth power of emitted frequency:
IS​IAS​​=(ν0​−νv​ν0​+νv​​)4exp(−kB​Thνv​​).
(42)
The photon rate divides each line's power by its own hνs​. Consequently, the ideal response-corrected anti-Stokes/Stokes count-rate ratio has the third power of the same frequency ratio. Peak heights alone do not give line-integrated radiant intensities if linewidths differ. Uncorrected detector counts can also include unequal detector response and unequal optical filter transmission. Resonant enhancement, overlapping spectral lines, nonthermal populations and local optical heating can invalidate a temperature inferred from this simple formula.
