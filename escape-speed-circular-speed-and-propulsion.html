Escape speed, circular speed, and propulsion

 ... Physics Branches of physics Classical mechanics Energy Potential energy Escape velocity
OurBigBook.com Words: 213
For a circular orbit, gravity supplies centripetal acceleration, giving vc2​/r=μ/r2. Thus
vc​=rμ​​,vesc​=2​vc​,εc​=−2rμ​.
(25)
A body in a circular orbit is bound despite its continuous free fall. At the same r, escape needs twice its kinetic energy per unit mass. An instantaneous forward tangential velocity increment from the circular orbit requires Δv=(2​−1)vc​, rather than the full escape speed from rest. In the synthetic example this is exactly 2(2​−1)m/s≈0.828427m/s.[ref]
Escape speed is a coasting threshold, not a required continuously maintained speed or a measure of rocket propellant. A powered vehicle can start at rest and acquire energy gradually. The rocket equation relates an ideal velocity increment to rocket exhaust speed and rocket mass ratio; rocket thrust, drag, and the timing and direction of propulsion affect a real ascent. Rotation also changes the relation between surface-relative velocity and the inertial frame used in the calculation.
Reaching another body is a finite-destination spacecraft trajectory problem. In the synthetic radial example, a launch at 2m/s reaches r=4m while remaining bound. A spacecraft can similarly encounter the Moon on a bound geocentric transfer; actual planning must include the Moon's gravity, target motion, arrival velocity, and any propulsion needed for capture. Escaping Earth alone does not imply escaping the Sun, because the relevant reference frame and attracting mass have changed. The isolated formula describes one gravitational well; a full mission requires more than its scalar threshold.
