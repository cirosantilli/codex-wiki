Rayleigh scattering by a small sphere (σ=38π​k4a6(m2+2m2−1​)2)

 Home Physics Branches of physics Electromagnetism Electromagnetic radiation Rayleigh scattering
OurBigBook.com Words: 228
Consider a fixed, homogeneous, nonabsorbing, nonmagnetic sphere of radius a in vacuum. Let m be its relative refractive index, a fixed real number; in vacuum this equals the sphere's refractive index. Treat m as independent of wavelength over the range being compared, neglecting optical dispersion. Air can be approximated by vacuum here because its refractive index is close to one. These assumptions describe a toy particle, rather than a literal model of an individual air molecule.
The Rayleigh scattering cross-section σ is an effective area: the total scattered power is Psca​=Iinc​σ, where Iinc​ is the incident light intensity. For ka≪1 its leading approximation is
σ=38π​k4a6(m2+2m2−1​)2,k=λ2π​.
(25)
The units are area, since k4a6 has units m−4m6=m2. This Rayleigh scattering cross-section measures scattering, not conversion of the incident electromagnetic radiation into heat. The definition follows [ref].
To see the mechanism, the nearly uniform applied electric field produces an electric dipole moment with peak magnitude
p0​=4πϵ0​a3m2+2m2−1​E0​,
(26)
where E0​ is the incident electric field peak wave amplitude and ϵ0​ is vacuum permittivity. This is the sphere's response in electrostatics, with its permittivity divided by vacuum permittivity equal to m2 under the nonmagnetic assumption. [ref]. The averaged electric dipole radiation power is Psca​=ω4p02​/(12πϵ0​c3), with angular frequency ω=ck and speed of light c. Dividing by Iinc​=ϵ0​cE02​/2 gives the displayed Rayleigh scattering cross-section. Thus doubling radius multiplies the electric dipole moment by eight and the scattered power by 64, provided the larger sphere still satisfies ka≪1. [ref].
