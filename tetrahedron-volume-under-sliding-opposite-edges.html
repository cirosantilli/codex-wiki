Tetrahedron volume under sliding opposite edges (V=61​∣Δ⋅(a×c)∣∣δλδμ∣)

 ... Area of mathematics Geometry and topology Euclidean geometry Polyhedron Tetrahedron Opposite edges of a tetrahedron
OurBigBook.com Words: 124
Place one pair of opposite tetrahedron vertices on the line b+λa and the other pair on d+μc, with fixed nonzero direction vectors a,c. Let Δ=d−b and let δλ,δμ be the parameter differences within the two pairs. The scalar triple product of the three edge vectors from one vertex gives the displayed volume. Terms parallel to a or c vanish in the triple product, leaving only Δ⋅(a×c). Sliding either pair along its own line while preserving its separation leaves this volume unchanged. When the two lines are skew lines, it can also be written V=d⊥​ℓ1​ℓ2​∣sinα∣/6, where d⊥​ is their shortest distance, ℓ1​,ℓ2​ the two edge lengths and α their direction angle. Intersecting or parallel lines give zero volume because all four vertices are coplanar.
