Period average (⟨f⟩=T1​∫t0​t0​+T​f(t)dt)

The period average extracts the reference level of an integrable function that repeats. For a real- or complex-valued periodic function f with positive period of a function T, assume f is Lebesgue integrable on every bounded interval. Its average is
⟨f⟩=T1​∫aa+T​f(t)dt.​
(7)
The answer is independent of the starting point a. Splitting a shifted interval at the old endpoint and using f(t+T)=f(t) moves its extra piece back into the missing piece. The interval length must be a period; an arbitrary window can give a different average.
For g(t)=2+sin(2πt)+21​cos(4πt), every length-one window has average 2: both oscillating integrals vanish over that window. A half-unit window instead has average 2+(2/π)cos(2πa), which depends on its start. Thus a short sample of an oscillation can bias an estimate of its reference level even when the full-cycle average is known exactly.
In the complex Fourier series convention with basis e2πint/T, the zero-frequency Fourier coefficient is c0​=⟨f⟩. This assertion defines the coefficient even when an additional convergence theorem is needed to reconstruct f from its series. Strang's §4.1 explicitly identifies that constant coefficient with the average.[ref]
Averaging a square measures something different from averaging its original function. For the illustrated g, orthogonality of the trigonometric terms gives ⟨g2⟩=4+1/2+1/8=37/8, whereas ⟨g⟩2=4. Its root mean square is therefore 37/8​. Similarly, the period average of sin2 is 1/2. A zero mean need not imply a zero mean square.
