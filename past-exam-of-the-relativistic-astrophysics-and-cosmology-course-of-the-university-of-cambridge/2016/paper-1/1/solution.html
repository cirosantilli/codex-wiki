An active galactic nucleus is a compact, exceptionally luminous central source powered chiefly by black-hole accretion rather than ordinary stellar fusion. Typical signatures include a broad spectral-energy distribution extending from radio to X-rays, rapid variability implying a small emitting region, strong optical or ultraviolet emission lines and, in some objects, radio jets and lobes. Broad permitted lines indicate rapidly moving dense gas; narrower forbidden lines can arise farther out. Obscuration and viewing orientation can hide the broad-line region, and many active nuclei are radio quiet, so no one signature is required in every object.
For the Schwarzschild circular-ring energy shift, set rg​=GM/c2 and let E0​ be the photon energy in the orbiting gas frame. A local static observer in Schwarzschild spacetime measures Estat​. The factors describe gravitational redshift, transverse time dilation and the longitudinal Doppler shift. Here the emitter is on the maximally receding azimuth of a circular disc ring. A general azimuth has a smaller projected velocity, and a fully ray-traced image must use the photon direction at emission rather than equating that local direction with the distant inclination. The supplied expression is the direct-projection transfer model, not a complete light-bending calculation.
The innermost stable circular orbit is the marginal stable timelike circular geodesic separating stable circular motion from radial instability. In Schwarzschild spacetime,

Emission from a plunging region, occultation and returning rays are outside this ISCO-emitting model.

For accretion output relative to galactic binding energy, distinguish accreted rest mass from the mass retained by the black hole. At constant radiative efficiency, building a hole from a negligible seed to MBH​=αM emits

For an approximately virialized galaxy with negligible boundary term, K=21​Mσv2​ and W=−2K=−Mσv2​. The ratio to the magnitude of its gravitational potential energy is therefore

If “binding energy” means the net energy needed to unbind the virialized system, ∣K+W∣=Mσv2​/2, the ratio is twice as large. The widely used order-of-magnitude estimate drops the retained-mass correction and writes ηα(c/σv​)2; it should not be confused with a precisely fixed structural coefficient.
For the numerical parameters given,
α(c/σv​)2≃998.6,∣W∣Erad​​≃998.61−ηη​.
(10)
No efficiency is specified, so this function is the numerical result. For an explicitly chosen illustration η=0.1, it is about 111, or 222 relative to the net virial binding energy. The non-spinning zero-torque efficiency gives about 60.6 relative to ∣W∣; the ideal retrograde and prograde limits give about 39.2 and 731, respectively. Spin affects the ratio through the adopted efficiency and orbital orientation, not through a universal rule that a larger unsigned spin always means a larger efficiency.
Such large energy ratios make even a modest coupling of the output to galactic gas dynamically important. Radiation and winds can heat or eject gas and reduce star formation or later black-hole feeding. For the η=0.1 illustration a percent-level coupling is already comparable to ∣W∣. Energy available is not energy necessarily deposited: cooling, escape directions, gas geometry, photon opacity and the duration of activity determine the actual feedback. It does not follow that the entire stellar galaxy must be disrupted.
For the swept-shell calculation, the problem now explicitly redefines σv​ as a one-dimensional line-of-sight dispersion. The specified isothermal profile has
Mgal​(R)=G2σv2​R​,Msh​=fMgal​,Mint​=(1−f)Mgal​.
(11)

Fg​=R2GMint​Msh​​=G4f(1−f)σv4​​.
(12)
In a single-scattering momentum-driven model with the shell intercepting the central luminosity, Frad​=L/c. At the hydrogen Eddington luminosity this becomes 4πGMBH​mp​/σT​. Sustained outward acceleration against that weight requires
σT​4πGMBH​mp​​>G4f(1−f)σv4​​,
(13)
and hence
MBH​>πmp​G2(1−f)fσv4​σT​​.​
(14)
The radius cancels because the swept isothermal mass is proportional to R. This is the isothermal-galaxy momentum-balance shell threshold, with its fourth-power dispersion scaling.
The coefficient identifies the approximations. Self-gravity in a thin spherical shell expansion adds the shell's inward pressure-equivalent force GMsh2​/(2R2) and replaces f(1−f) by f(1−f/2). A shell intercepting only a fraction of the photon momentum needs a larger luminosity; Thomson-thin material does not automatically receive L/c. Conversely, multiple scattering or an energy-driven hot wind changes the force model. A shell already carrying sufficient outward kinetic energy need not satisfy this instantaneous acceleration condition everywhere to escape. The printed inequality is the stated quasistatic, fully coupled momentum-balance estimate, not an assumption-free lower bound for every possible shell trajectory.
