This recession law concerns proper distances on common cosmic-time slices. It is not a local special-relativistic relative velocity measurement; a large recession rate is not a signal exceeding the local speed of light.
For a comoving volume V=V0​R3, total fluid energy is E=ρc2V. Adiabatic energy conservation gives dE+PdV=0. Expanding,
c2Vρ˙​+(ρc2+P)V˙=0,V˙=3HV,
(18)
hence
ρ˙​=−3H(ρ+P/c2).​
(19)
Here ρ is mass-equivalent energy density. Radiation loses energy both by dilution and by pressure work; pressureless matter only dilutes in this description.
In the time-unit convention for the cosmological constant, it represents a uniform vacuum contribution with pressure equal to minus its energy density. There is a units convention in the printed field equations: their term is Λ/3, so the symbol Λ there has units of inverse time squared. If the geometrical constant is instead given in inverse length squared, this symbol equals c2Λgeom​. The curvature parameter k labels constant-curvature spatial slices; with a conventional scale-factor normalization it is +1,0,−1 for positively curved, flat and negatively curved slices. It fixes local curvature sign, not every possible global spatial topology.
For a flat fluid-only model without a cosmological constant and with P=wρc2, the two field equations give
q=38πG​ρ34πG​(ρ+3P/c2)​=21+3w​.
(20)
The two printed cases and their scale-factor laws are worked under the literal inline labels below.
Inflation is accelerated expansion, R¨>0, equivalently a decreasing comoving Hubble radius (RH)−1 on an expanding branch. An approximately constant positive vacuum-like energy can give nearly exponential expansion. Enough inflation can put regions now widely separated inside a common earlier causal patch, suppress ∣k∣/(RH)2 and dilute unwanted relics such as monopoles. These are the horizon, flatness and relic-abundance motivations. Reheating is needed to populate the subsequent hot universe. Quantum fluctuations can seed primordial perturbations, but inflation alone is not a universal solution to the initial singularity or a proof that all initial conditions inflate.
Now adopt the printed natural units c=ℏ=1. For the Homogeneous massive-scalar equation in an expanding universe,
ρ˙​=ϕ˙​ϕ¨​+m2ϕϕ˙​,ρ+P=ϕ˙​2.
(21)
The continuity equation gives
ϕ˙​(ϕ¨​+3Hϕ˙​+m2ϕ)=0.
(22)
Where ϕ˙​=0, this implies the expanding-universe Klein-Gordon equation
ϕ¨​+3Hϕ˙​+m2ϕ=0.​
(23)
A regular canonical-field solution extends it through isolated turning points by continuity. Purely dividing the conservation equation does not determine a trajectory on an interval where ϕ˙​ is identically zero; canonical field dynamics, or variation of its action, supplies the equation there. In particular a constant nonzero massive field is not an exact freely evolving solution.
With Λ=k=0,
RR¨​=−34πG​(ρ+3P)=−34πG​(2ϕ˙​2−m2ϕ2).
(24)
Therefore
R¨>0⟺ϕ˙​2<21​m2ϕ2.​
(25)
This inflation condition for a homogeneous massive scalar means a potential-dominated field has sufficiently negative pressure to accelerate expansion. In quadratic chaotic inflation, a large initial field with V=m2ϕ2/2 can enter slow roll, with 3Hϕ˙​≃−m2ϕ and H2≃4πGm2ϕ2/3. The potential slow-roll parameter is ϵV​=1/(4πGϕ2), so a large field relative to the gravitational scale can support many e-folds. The field eventually rolls far enough that this regime ends and oscillation and reheating become relevant. Large potential energy with arbitrary initial kinetic energy does not by itself guarantee an inflationary interval.
Differentiating the flat Friedmann equation and then using the scalar equation,
2HH˙=38πG​ϕ˙​(ϕ¨​+m2ϕ)=−8πGHϕ˙​2.
(26)

H˙=−4πGϕ˙​2≤0.​
(27)
This Nonincreasing Hubble rate for a canonical inflaton decreases strictly where the field rolls and can be momentarily stationary at a turning point; the statement “always decreasing” means nonincreasing. This does not conflict with acceleration, because R¨/R=H2+H˙ remains positive when ϵH​=−H˙/H2<1.
At horizon exit, larger comoving wavelengths leave earlier, when H is larger. For a canonical field in slow roll, with reduced Planck mass MPl​=(8πG)−1/2,
PT​≃π2MPl2​2H2​,nT​≃−2ϵH​≤0.
(28)
The decreasing expansion rate gives a red tensor tilt at this order. The scalar curvature spectrum also contains the conversion from field fluctuations to curvature:
PR​≃8π2ϵH​MPl2​H2​,ns​−1≃−2ϵH​−dNdlnϵH​​.​
(29)
Here N=lnR counts elapsed e-folds. Thus the Decreasing Hubble rate does not alone fix scalar tilt. Evolution of ϵH​ matters. For the quadratic slow-roll model, ηV​=ϵV​ and ns​−1≃−6ϵV​+2ηV​=−4ϵV​<0. If Nrem​ e-folds remain until the end, leading estimates give ns​≃1−2/Nrem​ and tensor-to-scalar ratio rTS​≃8/Nrem​, with small end-of-inflation corrections.
The relevant contemporary comparison is a nearly scale-invariant but red scalar spectrum. The February2015 Planck analysis reported ns​=0.968±0.006 for its temperature, low-polarization and lensing combination, and an upper limit r0.002​<0.11 at95percent confidence. The scalar tilt is qualitatively consistent with a slowly evolving inflaton, but that result does not establish the quadratic potential; its relatively large tensor prediction was already disfavored against lower-tensor alternatives in that analysis.[ref]
