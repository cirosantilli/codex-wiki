In a close binary star, the rotating effective gravitational potential defines two Roche lobes meeting at the inner Lagrange point. Stellar expansion or orbital shrinkage can bring the donor into Roche-lobe overflow. Gas then flows through the inner Lagrange point; its angular momentum can form an accretion disk around the companion. wind mass transfer in a binary star is also possible without Roche-lobe filling. For a circular, synchronized binary, the Eggleton Roche-lobe radius approximation is

Since M˙d​<0, transfer from the more massive donor initially shrinks the orbit; after binary mass ratio reversal it expands. Stability depends on both stars and their changing lobes. Let ζ∗​=dlnRd​/dlnMd​ and ζL​=dlnRL​/dlnMd​. A small mass loss reduces the relative overfill if ζ∗​>ζL​. Use the adiabatic donor response for dynamical stability, and its thermal response for slower adjustment; an equilibrium mass-radius law alone cannot decide both.
Stable transfer can proceed on nuclear or thermal timescales, while dynamically unstable overflow may create a common envelope, orbital spiral-in and either envelope ejection or a merger. Escaping gas, gravitational radiation and magnetic braking make evolution nonconservative by changing orbital angular momentum. accretion onto a compact stellar object releases power of order GMa​M˙/Ra​, producing bright X-ray binaries; radiation-driven outflows can limit retained mass. Eclipses, radial velocities and disk emission constrain the geometry and masses, but inclination, irradiation and assumptions about mass or angular-momentum loss affect the inference. The essential feedback is the donor's radius relative to its evolving Roche lobe.
