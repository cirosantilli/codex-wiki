A galaxy cluster deflects background light through its projected gravitational field. In the thin-lens approximation for gravitational lensing, β=θ−α(θ) maps image to source angle. Its surface mass density enters through

Here μ is signed lensing magnification; observed brightness uses ∣μ∣. The D's are angular diameter distances to lens, source and between them; γs​ denotes lensing shear, not a Lorentz factor. strong gravitational lensing creates arcs and multiple images near critical curves. For an axisymmetric lens, a tangential Einstein ring satisfies mean enclosed convergence one, giving
Mproj​(<Dl​θE​)=π(Dl​θE​)2Σcrit​​.

This is projected enclosed mass, not a general three-dimensional mass profile inferred from a single arc.
weak gravitational lensing uses coherent distortions of many background galaxies. Their intrinsic orientations average approximately to zero, allowing an estimate of reduced shear g=γs​/(1−κ) and a projected mass map. The method traces all gravitating material without assuming hydrostatic equilibrium, which is valuable in merging clusters. Accurate work requires correction of the point spread function, calibrated shape measurement, source redshifts, foreground-member rejection and selection effects. Intrinsic shape noise, unrelated line-of-sight structures and lens-model degeneracies remain. The mass-sheet degeneracy leaves reduced shear unchanged under κ↦λκ+(1−λ) and γs​↦λγs​, so absolute mass normalization needs additional information.
In the Bullet Cluster, weak gravitational lensing places the main projected mass peaks near the collisionless galaxies, displaced from the X-ray-emitting gas that contains most observed baryon mass. Comparing the lensing, optical and X-ray maps supports a dominant weakly collisional dark component under the usual gravity model; lensing alone does not identify its particle species. Cluster lensing measures projected gravitational mass independently of gas temperature or dynamical equilibrium.[ref]
