mass from motion. A resolved Keplerian orbit gives M=4π2a3/(GPorb2​) when the orbiting object has negligible mass. At the Galactic centre, repeated infrared astrometry and spectroscopy measure sky motion, acceleration and line-of-sight velocity of stars around Sagittarius A*. Resolving a compact enclosed mass and fitting complete orbits avoids reliance on a single projected velocity. Source confusion, reference-frame stability, distance, inclination and possible extended mass must be included in the fit.[ref]
For an unresolved stellar-mass binary in a circular orbit, the donor's velocity semiamplitude K2​ and orbital period give the binary mass function
f(MBH​)=2πGPorb​K23​​=(MBH​+M2​)2MBH3​sin3i​.
(37)
It provides a lower mass bound; donor mass and orbital inclination are needed for an actual mass estimate. For an eccentric orbit, multiply the observational combination Porb​K23​/(2πG) by (1−e2)3/2 when evaluating the binary mass function. Resolved gas or maser rotation and stellar-dynamical models also measure masses, provided pressure support, noncircular motion and line-of-sight projection are modeled. Resolving the black-hole sphere of influence improves separation of central mass from the host galaxy.
For active nuclei, reverberation mapping measures a lag τ between variable continuum and broad-line response. With a line velocity scale Δv,
MBH​=fgeom​GcτΔv2​.
(38)
The factor fgeom​ accounts for geometry and kinematics and is not known simply from the lag. Cadence, line responsivity, inclination and winds require control. Rapid variability demonstrates compactness but is not by itself a unique mass measurement.
black-hole spin from strong-field emission. Define a∗​=cJ/(GM2). Large-radius Keplerian motion is principally a mass probe and usually insensitive to black-hole spin. In the Kerr model, the radius and binding energy of the innermost stable circular orbit vary with a∗​ and orbital orientation, making inner-disk radiation useful.
black-hole continuum fitting infers an inner emitting radius from the disk's thermal spectrum and flux normalization, then identifies it with the innermost stable circular orbit in a relativistic thin-disk model. Independent mass, distance and inclination, together with atmospheric spectral hardening, absorption and contamination, are needed. Spectra should be selected in a disk-dominated regime where truncation and strong advection are limited. A 2011 Cygnus X-1 analysis combined such spectra with independently constrained binary parameters and reported a near-extreme black-hole spin conditional on black-hole spin–orbit alignment and its disk model.[ref]
black-hole reflection spectroscopy instead models a relativistically broadened iron-line and reflection continuum: orbital Doppler shifts and beaming broaden the profile, while gravitational redshift contributes to the extended red wing. Its innermost emitting radius can constrain black-hole spin if reflection extends to the stable inner edge. Ionization, abundance, absorption, coronal illumination, disk thickness and possible truncation are competing parameters; a broad line is not a direct black-hole spin reading independent of these assumptions. Timing or precession models can add information but introduce their own dynamical identification.
mass is principally inferred from gravitating dynamics; black-hole spin is inferred from strong-field effects with a specified spacetime and accretion model. Agreement between independent methods is especially valuable because disk inner radius, inclination and radiation-transfer assumptions can otherwise mimic a change of black-hole spin.
