Fermionic atom antibunching

OurBigBook.com Words: 195 Articles: 4
The 2007 paper Comparison of the Hanbury Brown-Twiss effect for bosons and fermions by T. Jeltes and others compares polarized helium-3 fermions with helium-4 bosons. The chemical atoms occupy a metastable atomic level. Switching off magnetic trapping of neutral atoms releases them onto a microchannel plate detector with a delay-line anode, 63 cm below. Position and arrival-time readouts give three-dimensional pair separations by time-of-flight atom detection. About 1000 clouds per isotope supply histograms; normalization removes the broad cloud shape.
The pair detection correlation dips below one for helium-3 and rises above one for helium-4. The same apparatus, matched temperatures near 0.5μK, nearly identical trapping potentials, negligible interactions, and magnetic field gradient checks of spin polarization support an exchange-statistics interpretation. Finite detector resolution suppresses contrast; trap switch-off and resolution uncertainties limit quantitative agreement. A diverging laser lens deepens the dip, but its aberrations preclude a precise lens-model test. This detects exchange correlations, rather than measuring occupation of one selected spin orbital.[ref]
