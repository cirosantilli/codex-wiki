A group homomorphism from an additive cyclic group Zn​ is determined by the image a of 1, subject exactly to an=e in multiplicative notation, or na=0 in an additive target.
For Z18​→Z15​, the relation is 18a≡0(mod15), equivalently 3a≡0(mod15). The possibilities are a=0,5,10, and each defines a homomorphism between cyclic groups. Thus there are three such maps, an instance of the number of homomorphisms between finite cyclic groups.
For Z9​→S4​, the image of 1 must be a permutation whose order of a group element divides 9. A permutation on four symbols can have order 1,2,3 or 4, so the allowed images are the identity and the three-cycles. There are (34​)(3−1)!=8 three-cycles: choose their three symbols and one of the two cyclic orientations. Hence there are nine maps.
Finally, each transposition in S5​ has order 2, so its image in Z5​ must have order dividing both 2 and 5, and is therefore the identity. Every permutation is a product of transpositions, so every element must map to the identity. There is one map. In the order asked, the counts are
3,9,1.​
