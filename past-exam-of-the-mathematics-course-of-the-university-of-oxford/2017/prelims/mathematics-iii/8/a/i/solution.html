Treat the explanatory variables as fixed and put Sxx​=∑i​xi2​, Szz​=∑i​zi2​. To identify both slopes, assume Sxx​>0 and Szz​>0. The given centring and orthogonality conditions make the three columns 1,x,z pairwise orthogonal.
The log-likelihood function in the normal linear model, up to a constant independent of the coefficients, is
ℓ(β0​,β1​,β2​)=−2σ21​∑i​(Yi​−β0​−β1​xi​−β2​zi​)2.
(67)
Thus maximum likelihood estimation minimizes the residual sum of squares. Setting its partial derivatives to zero gives
∑i​(Yi​−β0​−β1​xi​−β2​zi​)=0,∑i​xi​(Yi​−β0​−β1​xi​−β2​zi​)=0,∑i​zi​(Yi​−β0​−β1​xi​−β2​zi​)=0.
(68)
Using ∑xi​=∑zi​=∑xi​zi​=0 decouples these equations:
β​0​=Y,β​1​=Sxx​∑i​xi​Yi​​,β​2​=Szz​∑i​zi​Yi​​.​
(69)
The Hessian matrix of the residual sum of squares is 2diag(n,Sxx​,Szz​), which is positive definite. These are therefore the unique minimizers. If either squared sum is zero, the associated explanatory variable vanishes identically and its coefficient cannot be identified.
