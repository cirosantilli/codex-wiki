Write xi​∈Rp for row i of the data matrix. The k-means clustering objective is the within-cluster sum of squared Euclidean distances:
J(C1​,…,CK​;m1​,…,mK​)=k=1∑K​i∈Ck​∑​∥xi​−mk​∥2.​
(84)
The nonempty Ck​ form a partition of a set of observation indices. For fixed clusters, part (a) shows that the best mk​ is their centroid.
Initialize distinct centres, for example by choosing K≤n distinct observations. Then repeat the following steps:
  • Assign each observation to a nearest centre in Euclidean distance. Keep its current assignment on an exact tie; for a previously unassigned observation use a fixed ordering to resolve ties.
  • If a cluster is empty, choose an observation from a cluster with at least two members and make it the empty cluster's sole member, placing its centre at that observation. A definite rule is to choose an eligible observation farthest from its currently assigned centre, breaking ties by observation index. Repeat until all clusters are nonempty.
  • Replace each centre by the arithmetic mean of its assigned observations.
  • After updating, check whether every observation is still assigned to a nearest updated centre, retaining its assignment on ties. If so, stop; otherwise repeat.
The empty-cluster repair is possible for K≤n and cannot increase J: the transferred observation now has squared distance zero, while all other squared distances stay unchanged before the centre update.
Assignment cannot increase J, and any changed assignment strictly decreases it under the tie rule. Updating centres cannot increase J by the centroid sum-of-squares identity. After an update, the centres are determined by the partition of a set. There are only finitely many possible assignments. Unless this is the initial round, any nonterminal round starts with an assignment that strictly reduces J; repairing empty clusters and updating cannot undo that decrease. Thus a partition cannot recur, proving finite termination of K-means clustering. The final result need not be a global minimum; different initial centres can lead to different final clusterings.
