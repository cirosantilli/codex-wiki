Write h=x−x0​, k=y−y0​, and let a=fxx​, b=fxy​ and c=fyy​ be evaluated at the critical point. Assuming f is twice continuously differentiable nearby, the Taylor expansion is
f(x0​+h,y0​+k)−f(x0​,y0​)=21​(ah2+2bhk+ck2)+o(h2+k2).
(17)
The linear terms vanish because the gradient is zero. For a>0, completing the square gives
21​(ah2+2bhk+ck2)=2a1​((ah+bk)2+(ac−b2)k2).
(18)
Consequently the requested constant is the Hessian determinant
D=fxx​fyy​−fxy2​.​
(19)
The displayed identity in the question is an identity for the quadratic part; for a general f, its right-hand side needs the remainder o(h2+k2).
If D>0, the quadratic form is positive for every nonzero (h,k). On the unit circle it has a positive minimum, so it dominates the remainder sufficiently near the critical point. Hence the point is a strict local minimum.
If D<0, along k=0 the quadratic term is ah2/2>0, whereas along h=−bk/a it is Dk2/(2a)<0. Along each line the remainder is of smaller order. Thus values both above and below f(x0​,y0​) occur arbitrarily nearby, giving a saddle point.
