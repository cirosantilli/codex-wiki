For α=3, the divergence vanishes at every point with r>0, so its ordinary volume integral, ignoring the undefined single point, is zero. Nevertheless, the outward flux integral through the unit sphere is still 4π:
∭0<r≤1​∇⋅udV=0,∬r=1​u⋅ndS=4π​.

Let S be a piecewise smooth oriented surface with piecewise smooth boundary ∂S, and let F be a continuously differentiable vector field on a neighbourhood of S. The Stokes theorem states
∮∂S​F⋅dr=∬S​(∇×F)⋅ndS​.
(21)
The boundary orientation is induced by the chosen unit normal vector n through the right-hand rule: with the right thumb along n, the curled fingers indicate positive boundary traversal. Equivalently, when walking along the boundary with one's head pointing along n, the surface lies to the left. An upward oriented disk therefore has counterclockwise boundary traversal when viewed from above. Reversing the orientation reverses both the line integral and the surface integral.

Let the uniform string have constant tension τ>0 and linear mass density μ>0. Assume small transverse motion with ∣ux​∣≪1, negligible longitudinal motion, and no external transverse force. If the string makes angle β with the horizontal, then tanβ=ux​, and the small-angle approximation gives sinβ≃ux​ and ds≃dx to first order.
The net upward force on the segment from x to x+Δx is therefore
τ(ux​(x+Δx,t)−ux​(x,t)).
(45)
By Newton's second law, its mass μΔx times its transverse acceleration equals this force, to the order retained in the small displacement model. Divide by Δx and let Δx→0 to obtain
μutt​=τuxx​.
(46)
Thus the wave equation on a string is
utt​=c2uxx​,c=μτ​​​,
(47)
where c is the wave speed. The horizontal force balance makes the horizontal component of tension constant; to first order it equals τ.

The printed arguments x−t and x+t assume unit wave speed. For the stated wave equation with general c>0, the correct arguments are x−ct and x+ct, as in the paper's subsequent formula. Equivalently, use rescaled time t=ct to obtain the printed unit-speed form.
Introduce coordinates ξ=x−ct, η=x+ct along the characteristic lines for the one-dimensional wave equation, and write u(x,t)=U(ξ,η). The chain rule gives
∂x​=∂ξ​+∂η​,∂t​=−c∂ξ​+c∂η​,
(48)
so the wave equation becomes
utt​−c2uxx​=−4c2Uξη​=0.
(49)
Thus Uξ​ is independent of η, and integration gives the countertravelling decomposition of the one-dimensional wave equation
u(x,t)=F(x−ct)+G(x+ct)​.
(50)
Conversely, differentiating this expression twice verifies the wave equation for any sufficiently differentiable F,G.
The two initial conditions give
F(x)+G(x)=f(x),−cF′(x)+cG′(x)=g(x).
(51)

For a nonzero separable solution of the Laplace equation in polar coordinates, write T(r,θ)=R(r)Θ(θ) with r>0. Multiplying the partial differential equation by r2 and dividing where RΘ=0 gives
Rr2R′′+rR′​=−ΘΘ′′​=λ,
(57)
where λ is a real separation constant. The resulting ordinary differential equations are
r2R′′+rR′−λR=0,Θ′′+λΘ=0.
(58)
The equations continue across zeros of the factors by continuity. The radial equation is a Cauchy-Euler equation; putting s=logr changes it to d2R/ds2−λR=0.
For positive separation constant, write λ=k2 with k>0. The radial factors are powers, while the angular factors are sine and cosine:
T=(Ark+Br−k)(Ccos(kθ)+Dsin(kθ))​.
(59)
For zero separation constant, each transformed equation has zero second derivative, giving
T=(A+Blogr)(C+Dθ)​.
(60)
For negative separation constant, write λ=−k2 with k>0. The radial factors oscillate in logr, and the angular factors are exponential functions:
T=(Acos(klogr)+Bsin(klogr))(Cekθ+De−kθ)​.
(61)
These exhaust the nonzero real separated solutions before imposing angular periodicity or regularity at the origin. The zero solution is also allowed. Since Laplace's equation is linear, sums of these separated solutions are solutions wherever the sums and required derivatives converge appropriately.

A temperature on the full unit disk must be single valued, so its angular factor and angular derivative are 2π-periodic. For positive separation constant λ=k2, this requires k=n to be a positive integer. For λ=0, periodicity removes Dθ.
