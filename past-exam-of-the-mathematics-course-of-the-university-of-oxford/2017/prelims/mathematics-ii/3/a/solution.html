A real or complex sequence (an​) is a Cauchy sequence if
∀ε>0 ∃N∈N ∀m,n≥N:∣am​−an​∣<ε.
(17)
For zn​=xn​+iyn​, the complex modulus gives
∣xm​−xn​∣≤∣zm​−zn​∣,∣ym​−yn​∣≤∣zm​−zn​∣.
(18)
Thus the same N works for both coordinate Cauchy sequences. Conversely, if both real coordinate Cauchy sequences converge to x and y, then ∣zn​−(x+iy)∣≤∣xn​−x∣+∣yn​−y∣→0. This proves the needed completeness for complex numbers from the assumed completeness of the real numbers.
Now suppose ∑n≥0​∣zn​∣ converges. Its real partial sums form a Cauchy sequence. Given ε>0, sufficiently late tails therefore satisfy ∑k=n+1m​∣zk​∣<ε. For sm​=∑k=0m​zk​, the triangle inequality yields
∣sm​−sn​∣≤∑k=n+1m​∣zk​∣<ε(m>n≥N).
(19)
The complex partial sums are a Cauchy sequence, hence converge by the coordinate argument. Therefore absolute convergence implies convergence for a complex series.
