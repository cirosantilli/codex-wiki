Define the radius of convergence of the power series by
R=sup{∣w∣:w∈C, ∑n=0∞​an​wn converges}∈[0,∞],
(20)
with R=∞ when this set is unbounded. It is nonempty because the power series converges at zero.
If ∣z∣<R, the definition of supremum supplies a point w of convergence with ∣z∣<∣w∣. The terms an​wn of a convergent series tend to zero and are bounded, say ∣an​wn∣≤M. Therefore
∣an​zn∣≤M(∣w∣∣z∣​)n.
(21)
The right-hand side has a convergent geometric series, since ∣z∣/∣w∣<1. The comparison test proves absolute convergence at z.
If ∣z∣>R and the power series converged at z, then ∣z∣ would belong to the set whose supremum is R, an immediate contradiction. Thus
∣z∣<R⇒absolute convergence,∣z∣>R⇒divergence.​
(22)
No assertion about the boundary ∣z∣=R follows from this argument. If R=0 the first assertion is vacuous; if R=∞ the second is vacuous.
