For completeness, continuity at zero follows directly from a finite-head and tail argument. Given ε>0, choose N with ∑k>N​1/k2<ε. Choose δ>0 below every positive rk​ with k≤N. For 0≤t<δ, the first N indicator functions have exactly their values at zero; the one with rk​=0, if present, is always zero. Thus ∣f(t)−f(0)∣≤∑k>N​1/k2<ε. This confirms that the endpoint exception is real. Replacing the printed enumeration of [0,1) by one of (0,1) would remove it.
Finally, the density of the rational numbers supplies a positive rational rj​ in every nonempty open subinterval of [0,1]. Thus a Riemann integrable function can have a discontinuity in every open subinterval: dense jumps do not prevent Riemann integrability.
