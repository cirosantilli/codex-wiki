The infimum of A is its greatest lower bound: α=infA means that α≤x for every x∈A, and that every lower bound l of A satisfies l≤α. The supremum is its least upper bound: β=supA means that x≤β for every x∈A, and every upper bound u satisfies β≤u. The completeness of the real numbers ensures that both exist for a nonempty bounded set of real numbers.

If f is a continuous function, the intermediate value theorem ensures that every value between f(a) and f(b) is attained. Monotonicity excludes values outside that range, so f([a,b])=[f(a),f(b)].
Conversely, suppose this equality of sets holds. At an interior point, if f−(x0​)<f(x0​), any y strictly between these two numbers lies in [f(a),f(b)]. Yet inputs below x0​ have values at most f−(x0​) and inputs at or above x0​ have values at least f(x0​), so no input attains y. This contradicts surjectivity. The same argument excludes f(x0​)<f+(x0​), so part (b) gives continuity at every interior point.
At a, a strict gap f(a)<f+(a) likewise leaves every value between them unattained; hence f+(a)=f(a), giving right continuity. At b, the corresponding argument gives f−(b)=f(b) and left continuity. These are the appropriate endpoint notions on [a,b]. Hence
f continuous on [a,b]⟺f([a,b])=[f(a),f(b)].​
(31)
