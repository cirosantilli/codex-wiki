OurBigBook.com Words: 161
For any integer n≥1, let R=R2π/n​ in the stabilizer subgroup from i. Since Rθ​Rϕ​=Rθ+ϕ​ and Rθ​=I exactly when θ∈2πZ, this rotation matrix has order exactly n. Therefore
⟨R2π/n​⟩≅Cn​​
(65)
is a subgroup of G of order n, including the trivial case n=1.
For infinitely many non-Abelian examples, let n≥3, retain R=R2π/n​, and take the half-turn
J=diag(−1,1,−1)∈G.
(66)
Direct multiplication gives J2=I and JRJ=R−1. Every word in R,J therefore reduces to Rk or RkJ, 0≤k<n. The Rk are distinct, as are the RkJ, and no element from the first family equals one from the second: they send N to N and −N, respectively. Hence ⟨R,J⟩ has exactly 2n elements and is a dihedral group.
It is non-Abelian because R=R−1 for n≥3, so JR=RJ. Different values of n give different group orders, and therefore different subgroups. Thus G has infinitely many non-Abelian subgroups, already among its finite subgroups.
