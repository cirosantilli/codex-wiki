Suppose T(1) and T(2) are two sufficiently regular steady state solutions with the same prescribed temperature on the boundary. Their difference w=T(1)−T(2) satisfies
∇⋅(k∇w)=0in R,w=0on ∂R.
(13)
The identical PDE source terms cancel. Apply the product rule and the divergence theorem to wk∇w; this is the weighted form of Green's first identity:
0​=∭R​w∇⋅(k∇w)dV=∬∂R​wk∇w⋅ndA−∭R​k∥∇w∥2dV=−∭R​k∥∇w∥2dV.​
(14)
Since k>0, the nonnegative integrand must vanish, so ∇w=0. Hence w is constant on each connected component of R, and its zero Dirichlet boundary condition makes each constant zero. Therefore T(1)=T(2)​: the solution, if it exists, is unique. This proves Dirichlet uniqueness for variable-conductivity heat conduction; it does not require a constant thermal conductivity.
