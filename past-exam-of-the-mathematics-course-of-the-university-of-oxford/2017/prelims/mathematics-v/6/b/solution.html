Subtract the constant steady state profile: set v=T−T0​. Then
vt​=κvxx​,v(0,t)=0,vx​(L,t)=0.
(57)
The last equation follows from Fourier's law and zero heat flux at the insulated end. For a nonzero separated solution v=X(x)H(t), separation of variables gives
κHH′​=XX′′​=−μ,−X′′=μX,X(0)=0,X′(L)=0.
(58)
To see that μ>0 for a nonzero eigenfunction, multiply the spatial equation by X and use integration by parts:
μ∫0L​X2dx=∫0L​(X′)2dx.
(59)
Both boundary terms vanish. If the right side were zero, X would be constant and X(0)=0 would make it zero. Thus write μ=λ2 with λ>0. Solving gives X=Asin(λx), and X′(L)=0 requires cos(λL)=0. The mixed Dirichlet-Neumann modes on an interval therefore have
λn​=2L(2n−1)π​,Xn​(x)=sin(λn​x),Hn​(t)=e−κλn2​t,n=1,2,….
(60)
The eigenvalues of −d2/dx2 are μn​=λn2​; λn​ are their positive square roots. By superposition, with coefficients for which the resulting series and its required derivatives converge at positive times,
T(x,t)=T0​+n=1∑∞​bn​sin(2L(2n−1)πx​)exp[−4L2κ(2n−1)2π2t​].​
(61)
The eigenfunctions are orthogonal on [0,L] and have squared norm L/2. Expanding the initial excess temperature v(x,0)=T0​x/L gives
bn​​=L2​∫0L​LT0​x​sin(λn​x)dx=L22T0​​[−λn​xcos(λn​x)​+λn2​sin(λn​x)​]0L​=L2λn2​2T0​(−1)n−1​=(2n−1)2π28T0​(−1)n−1​​.​
(62)
Thus the requested temperature is
T(x,t)=T0​+π28T0​​n=1∑∞​(2n−1)2(−1)n−1​sin(2L(2n−1)πx​)e−κ(2n−1)2π2t/(4L2).​
(63)
At the insulated endpoint sin(λn​L)=(−1)n−1 cancels the alternating coefficient sign, leaving
T(L,t)=T0​+π28T0​​n=1∑∞​(2n−1)2e−κ(2n−1)2π2t/(4L2)​>T0​for every finite t≥0.​
(64)
Every summand is positive. The endpoint temperature never drops below T0​; it decreases towards T0​ as t→∞ and starts at T(L,0)=2T0​.
For completeness, the Sturm-Liouville eigenfunction expansion gives the initial profile in the L2 sense. Here the coefficients are absolutely summable, so the Weierstrass M-test also gives uniform convergence on [0,L]×[0,∞), and the initial values are recovered everywhere. For positive times the exponential factors justify all required termwise derivatives, giving a classical solution. There is, however, an initial incompatibility with a Neumann boundary condition at (L,0): the initial slope is T0​/L, while the insulated Neumann boundary condition requires zero slope for t>0. The temperature is continuous to the initial edge, but Tx​ cannot extend continuously to that corner. One must not differentiate the initial series term by term at x=L.
