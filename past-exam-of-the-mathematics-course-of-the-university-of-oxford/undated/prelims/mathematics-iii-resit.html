P(X∈S)=1,S countable.​

At fixed centroids, assigning each point to a nearest centroid minimizes its squared Euclidean distance. At fixed assignments, the centroid sum-of-squares identity shows that the sample mean uniquely minimizes each group's contribution. These alternating steps cannot increase W.
For finite termination of K-means clustering, more than a lower bound on W is needed. Keep an assignment unchanged when nearest centroids tie, and stop when assignments no longer change. Empty clusters in cluster analysis require a convention, such as moving a point from a nonsingleton cluster in cluster analysis to become its own centroid, which cannot increase W.
