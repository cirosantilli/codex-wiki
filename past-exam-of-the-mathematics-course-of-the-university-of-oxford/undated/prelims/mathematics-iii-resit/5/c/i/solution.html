An expected value of a signed random variable is finite precisely when its absolute value is integrable. With the standard normal distribution probability density function,
E∣ZeαZ2∣=2π​2​∫0∞​zexp[−(1/2−α)z2]dz.
(42)
For α<1/2, this integral equals 1/[2π​(1/2−α)]<∞. For α≥1/2, its integrand does not decay sufficiently and the integral diverges. Therefore
E[ZeαZ2] exists exactly for α<21​,and then equals 0.​
(43)
The value is zero by the odd function and the symmetric distribution. For the other α, both positive and negative parts have infinite integrals; formal cancellation would give only a Cauchy principal value, not an expected value. This distinction is the integrability threshold for exponential powers of a Gaussian.
