For an outcome ω=(i,j), the two random variables are the coordinate maps
X(i,j)=i,Y(i,j)=j.​
(26)
Their marginal probability mass functions are pX​(i)=pY​(j)=1/6 on {1,…,6}, and their joint probability mass function satisfies
pX,Y​(i,j)=361​=pX​(i)pY​(j).
(27)
Off that support, the same factorization holds with zeros. Hence X and Y are independent random variables, by the joint probability mass function criterion for independence.
