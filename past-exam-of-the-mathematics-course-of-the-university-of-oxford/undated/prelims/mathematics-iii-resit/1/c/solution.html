Use the change of variables u=x+y. Then u′=1+y′=1+cosu=2cos2(u/2), a separable differential equation. Where u is not an odd multiple of π, separation of variables gives
∫1+cosudu​=∫dx,tan2x+y​=x+C.​
(6)
Indeed the derivative of tan(u/2) with respect to u is 1/(1+cosu). The nonconstant branches can also be written y=2arctan(x+C)+2kπ−x, k∈Z. Dividing by 1+cosu excluded its zeros: the missing equilibrium solutions for u give the additional solutions of a differential equation
y=(2k+1)π−x,k∈Z.​
(7)
They satisfy y′=−1=cos(x+y) directly. The transformed right side is continuously differentiable, so the uniqueness theorem for ordinary differential equations prevents a nonconstant branch from crossing an equilibrium solution at finite x.
