Let w be the difference of two solutions. It is a harmonic function in the open rectangle, continuous on its closure, with
w(x,0)=0,wy​(x,π)=0,wx​(0,y)=wx​(π,y)=0.
(48)
Interpret these Neumann boundary conditions classically on the open edges, with the corresponding first derivatives extending there. We use the strong maximum principle for harmonic functions together with even reflection across a homogeneous Neumann boundary. This avoids imposing differentiability at the two bottom corners, where the original data fail a corner compatibility condition for mixed boundary data.
By continuity on the compact rectangle, w attains a maximum M. Suppose M>0. It cannot occur on the bottom edge, where w=0. If it occurs in the interior, the strong maximum principle for harmonic functions makes w constant, contradicting its bottom value. If it occurs on an open vertical or top edge, the zero normal derivative allows an even reflection across that edge. The reflected harmonic function then has an interior maximum, giving the same contradiction.
The only remaining possibilities are the two top corners. Reflect evenly across both meeting edges. This gives a bounded harmonic function on a punctured neighbourhood of the corner. The removable singularity for a bounded harmonic function fills the centre with its continuous value. The corner is now an interior maximum, again impossible. Therefore w≤0. Applying the identical argument to −w gives w≥0.
Thus w=0​, and the solution is unique among the continuous classical solutions with the stated edge conditions.
