On every strip y≥ε>0, the Fourier coefficients have additional exponential decay, allowing differentiation and verifying Laplace's equation and all three Neumann boundary conditions.

Subtracting 1 from the hyperbolic tangent leaves an absolutely convergent series. For the remaining part, the power series −log(1−z)=∑m≥1​zm/m gives, by taking real parts at z=re2ix and then r↑1,
∑m=1∞​mcos(2mx)​=−log(2sinx),0<x<π.

The same formula follows as the limit of the differentiated interior solution, since its leading multiplier is e−2my. This establishes the boundary normal derivative used in b. It also explains why the failure of the corner compatibility condition for mixed boundary data affects first derivatives at the bottom corners without spoiling continuity of u itself.
