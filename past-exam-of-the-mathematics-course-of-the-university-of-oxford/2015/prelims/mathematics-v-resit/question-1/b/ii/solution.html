Here a potential of a conservative vector field means a single-valued scalar field ψ with ∇ψ=F. By the fundamental theorem for line integrals, its line integral around every closed curve must vanish. Here the angular circulation obstruction to a cylindrical potential lets us test this using a circle about the axis.
On V1​, the circle from i lies entirely in the region and has line integral 42​. Therefore there is no potential of a conservative vector field on V1​.
On V2​, choose the continuous angular branch −π<θ<π. By a, the equation for a potential of a conservative vector field reduces to ψr​=ψz​=0 and ψθ​=2​cos(θ/2). Thus
ψ=22​sin(θ/2)+C=r(r+x)​2y​+C,r=x2+y2​,on V2​.​
(7)
The denominator is positive throughout V2​, including when y=0 and x>0, so this is a well-defined differentiable potential of a conservative vector field on the entire region.
For V3​, choose the particular circle x=2cosθ, y=2sinθ, z=0. Its only point on the removed half-plane's supporting cut is (−2,0,0), where (x+2)2+z2=0<1; this point is retained by V3​. Thus the whole circle lies in V3​ and again has line integral 42​. Consequently there is no potential of a conservative vector field on V3​. The opening in the cut allows a closed curve to go around the excluded axis.
Finally V4​⊂V2​. Restricting the potential of a conservative vector field already constructed on V2​ gives
ψ=r(r+x)​2y​+Con V4​.​
(8)
The extra excluded cylinder does not invalidate an existing potential of a conservative vector field. Thus being simply connected is a sufficient condition in the usual curl-free criterion, not a necessary condition for a particular vector field to have a potential of a conservative vector field.
