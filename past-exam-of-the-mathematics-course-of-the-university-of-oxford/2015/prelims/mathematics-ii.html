Call k a peak of a sequence when ak​≥am​ for every m>k. If there are infinitely many peaks of a sequence, list their indices in increasing order, k1​<k2​<⋯. Since kr+1​>kr​,
ak1​​≥ak2​​≥⋯.​
(1)
This is a decreasing subsequence; throughout this argument, a decreasing monotone sequence may have equal successive terms.
If there are only finitely many peaks of a sequence, choose N beyond all their indices; take N=1 if there are none. Every n≥N fails to be a peak of a sequence, so some m>n satisfies am​>an​. Starting with n1​=N, choose such an nr+1​>nr​ recursively. This gives
an1​​<an2​​<⋯.​
(2)
The two cases prove the monotone subsequence theorem without any boundedness hypothesis.

The Bolzano-Weierstrass theorem states: every bounded sequence of real numbers has a convergent subsequence.
By the monotone subsequence theorem proved in the preceding part, a bounded sequence (an​) has a monotone subsequence (br​). It inherits the bounds of (an​). For completeness, the bounded monotone sequence theorem follows directly from completeness of the real numbers. If (br​) is increasing, let L=sup{br​:r≥1}. For every ε>0, the definition of supremum supplies r0​ with L−ε<br0​​≤L. Then
r≥r0​⟹L−ε<br0​​≤br​≤L,
(3)
so br​→L. If (br​) is decreasing, apply the increasing case to (−br​), or use its infimum. Thus in either case
some n1​<n2​<⋯ and L∈R satisfy anr​​⟶L.​
(4)

If an​→L, every subsequence also has limit of a sequence L: its increasing indices eventually exceed any prescribed threshold. This applies in particular to every monotone subsequence.
Conversely, suppose (an​) is a bounded sequence and all its monotone subsequences converge to the same L. At least one exists by the monotone subsequence theorem. If an​ did not tend to L, there would be ε0​>0 and a subsequence (anj​​) such that
∣anj​​−L∣≥ε0​(j≥1).
(7)
Apply the monotone subsequence theorem to (anj​​). The resulting further subsequence is also a monotone subsequence of (an​), so it tends to L, contradicting the displayed bound. Therefore
an​→L ⟺ every monotone subsequence tends to L.​
(8)
The bounded monotone sequence theorem ensures that these subsequences have finite limits of sequences; the hypothesis requires those values to agree.

A function f:E→R is continuous on E if it is continuous at every p∈E, meaning
∀p∈E ∀ε>0 ∃δ>0 ∀x∈E:∣x−p∣<δ ⟹ ∣f(x)−f(p)∣<ε.​
(42)
The function has uniform continuity on E when
∀ε>0 ∃δ>0 ∀x,y∈E:∣x−y∣<δ ⟹ ∣f(x)−f(y)∣<ε.​
(43)
For continuity, δ may depend on both p and ε. For uniform continuity, one δ must work for every pair of points in E.
