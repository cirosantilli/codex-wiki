OurBigBook.com Words: 79
Lagrange's theorem states that if H is a subgroup of a finite group G, then
∣G∣=[G:H]∣H∣,∣H∣∣∣G∣,​
(46)
where the index of a subgroup [G:H] is the number of distinct left cosets.
Every g∈G lies in gH, since g=ge. By i, distinct left cosets are disjoint, so these sets form a partition of a set G. Each has cardinality ∣H∣: the function h↦gh from H to gH is a bijection, with inverse gh↦h, by cancellation in a group. Counting the elements in this finite partition of a set gives ∣G∣=[G:H]∣H∣, proving the theorem.
