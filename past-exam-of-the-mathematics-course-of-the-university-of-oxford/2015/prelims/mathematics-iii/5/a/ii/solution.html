Under the extra hypothesis that G(s) exists for every real s, its power series has infinite radius of convergence. Termwise differentiation of a power series is therefore valid to every finite order. For k≥1,
G(k)(s)=∑j=k∞​j(j−1)⋯(j−k+1)p(j)sj−k.
(41)
At s=1 this is the expected value of the falling factorial of X:
G(k)(1)=E[(X)k​]=E[X(X−1)⋯(X−k+1)].​
(42)
In particular G′(1)=E[X]​. The terms with j<k vanish because their falling factorials contain a zero factor. These factorial moments are finite under the stated hypothesis.
