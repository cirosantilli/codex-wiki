For the thin spherical charge layer limit, smoothness of h gives
δh(1+δ)−h(1)​⟶h′(1).
(76)
Thus the shell's total electric charge tends to 4πρ0​a3h′(1) while its thickness tends to zero. At each fixed r>a, the observation point eventually lies outside the shell, whereas at each fixed r<a the interior electric field is unchanged. The limiting electric field away from the sphere is therefore
E0​(r)=⎩⎨⎧​3ϵ0​ρ0​​r,ϵ0​r2ρ0​a3​[31​+h′(1)]er​,​r<a,r>a.​
(77)
Its outward-minus-inward jump is
E0​(a+)−E0​(a−)=ϵ0​ρ0​ah′(1)​er​.​
(78)
Physically, the limit is a uniformly charged ball of charge density ρ0​, together with an infinitesimally thin spherical shell of uniform surface charge density
σ=ρ0​ah′(1).​
(79)
Indeed its electric charge is 4πa2σ, and the standard normal electric field jump is σ/ϵ0​. The surface charge density can have either sign, or vanish, according to h′(1).
The order of limits matters. For every finite δ>0, taking the two one-sided limits at r=a first gives zero jump. The displayed nonzero jump is obtained by first taking δ→0 at fixed points on either side, then approaching a. The value at the surface itself need not coincide with either trace of the limiting electric field.
