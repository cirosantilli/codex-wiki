By the assumed spherical symmetry of the electric potential, the electric field is radial: E=Er​(r)er​ for r>0. On a sphere of radius r, Gauss's law therefore reads
4πr2Er​(r)=ϵ0​Q(r)​,Q(r)=4π∫0r​ρ(s)s2ds.
(71)
Put R=a(1+δ). For r<a, the enclosed electric charge is 4πρ0​r3/3. In the spherical shell, the factor s2 in the volume element cancels s−2 in the charge density, so
Q(r)​=34πρ0​a3​+δ4πρ0​a2​∫ar​h′(s/a)ds=4πρ0​a3[31​+δh(r/a)−h(1)​].​
(72)
It follows that
E(r)=⎩⎨⎧​3ϵ0​ρ0​​r,ϵ0​r2ρ0​a3​[31​+δh(r/a)−h(1)​]er​,ϵ0​r2ρ0​a3​[31​+δh(1+δ)−h(1)​]er​,​0≤r<a,a≤r<R,r≥R.​​
(73)
At the origin the first formula gives E=0 without needing a radial unit vector. At both shell interfaces the adjacent formulas agree: for every fixed δ>0, there is no surface charge concentrated on either sphere, and the electric field is continuous there. No sign restriction on h′ has been assumed, so the shell charge density and the radial electric field can be negative.
