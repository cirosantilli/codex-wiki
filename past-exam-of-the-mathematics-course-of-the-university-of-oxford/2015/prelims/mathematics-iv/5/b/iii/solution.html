After removing the two redundant inequalities, the feasible region is
y1​≥0,y2​≥max{0, y1​−1, 3−43​y1​, 6−3y1​}.
(68)
Its lower boundary consists of three line segments or rays: y2​=6−3y1​ for 0≤y1​≤4/3, then y2​=3−3y1​/4 for 4/3≤y1​≤16/7, then y2​=y1​−1 for y1​≥16/7. The feasible region is everything on or above this boundary in the first quadrant.
The boundary extreme points are
(0,6),(4/3,2),(16/7,9/7).
(69)
Along its three successive pieces, the objective function 2y1​+y2​ has respective slopes −1, 5/4 and 3 as y1​ increases. Thus it decreases to the middle extreme point and increases thereafter. For fixed y1​, moving above the boundary only increases the objective function. Hence
y∗=(4/3,2),min(2y1​+y2​)=314​.​
(70)
As an algebraic check of the graphical answer, the active inequalities give
2y1​+y2​=91​(3y1​+4y2​)+95​(3y1​+y2​)≥912​+930​=314​,
(71)
with equality at the stated extreme point.
