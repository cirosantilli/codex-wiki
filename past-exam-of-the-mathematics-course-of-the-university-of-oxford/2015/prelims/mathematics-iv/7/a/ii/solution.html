 ... 2015 Prelims Mathematics IV 7 a ii
OurBigBook.com Words: 96
Set H(x)=g(x)−x. This function is continuous on [a,b]. Since g takes values in [a,b],
H(a)=g(a)−a≥0,H(b)=g(b)−b≤0.
(89)
The intermediate value theorem therefore supplies ξ∈[a,b] with H(ξ)=0, equivalently g(ξ)=ξ. This includes the possibility that one endpoint is already a fixed point.
For uniqueness, the mean value theorem gives, for any x,y∈[a,b],
∣g(x)−g(y)∣≤γ∣x−y∣.
(90)
If both ξ and η are fixed points, then
∣ξ−η∣=∣g(ξ)−g(η)∣≤γ∣ξ−η∣.
(91)
Since γ<1, this forces ∣ξ−η∣=0. Thus there exists exactly one fixed point. When a=b, the conclusion follows immediately from g(a)=a; otherwise the argument above applies. The contraction mapping estimate also proves convergence of the associated fixed-point iteration, as used next.
