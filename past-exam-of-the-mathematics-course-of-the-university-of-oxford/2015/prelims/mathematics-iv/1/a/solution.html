Let
r0​=∣a∣2a∧b​,N=b+a∧y.
(1)
The vector triple product identity (a∧b)∧a=∣a∣2b−(a⋅b)a shows that r0​∧a=b. Moreover, (r−r0​)∧a=0 exactly when r−r0​ is a scalar multiple of a. Thus the given line is
L={r0​+ta:t∈R}.
(2)
Also a∧r0​=−b, so N=a∧(y−r0​). The assumption N=0 says precisely that y is outside L. Consequently L and y determine a unique plane, with normal vector N.
To identify its equation, use cyclic invariance of the scalar triple product:
r⋅(a∧y)=y⋅(r∧a).
(3)
For r∈L, this equals y⋅b, while r⋅b=r⋅(r∧a)=0. Hence r⋅N=y⋅b throughout L. The point y also satisfies that equation because y⋅(a∧y)=0. Since N=0, the required plane is therefore exactly
r⋅(b+a∧y)=y⋅b.​
(4)
This also proves the reusable plane through a point and a line given by a cross product construction.
