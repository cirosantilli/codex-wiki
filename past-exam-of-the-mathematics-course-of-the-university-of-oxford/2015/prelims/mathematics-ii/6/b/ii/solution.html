Choose hn​=min{1/n,(b−c)/2}>0. Then hn​→0 and [c,c+hn​]⊂(a,b). The mean value theorem states that a continuous function on a closed interval, differentiable in its interior, has an interior derivative equal to its endpoint difference quotient. It gives xn​∈(c,c+hn​) with
f′(xn​)=hn​f(c+hn​)−f(c)​.
(66)
Here xn​=c, xn​→c, and the right-hand side tends to f′(c) by differentiability at c. If L=limx→c​f′(x) exists, the same values f′(xn​) must tend to L. Uniqueness of the limit of a sequence gives
x→clim​f′(x)=f′(c).​
(67)
Thus a derivative cannot have a removable discontinuity at a point where the original function is differentiable. The proof also excludes an infinite limit of a function there, since the displayed difference quotients have a finite limit of a sequence.
