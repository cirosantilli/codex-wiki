For an m×n matrix, the corresponding elementary matrices are m×m, because the operation is implemented by left matrix multiplication. Apply the desired elementary row operation to the identity matrix Im​. Writing Eij​ for the matrix unit with its only nonzero entry, 1, in position (i,j) gives:
Pij​Di​(c)Lij​(c)​=Im​−Eii​−Ejj​+Eij​+Eji​,=Im​+(c−1)Eii​,=Im​+cEij​,​Pij−1​Di​(c)−1Lij​(c)−1​=Pij​,=Di​(c−1)(c=0),=Lij​(−c)(i=j).​
(1)
Indeed, these matrix products interchange rows i,j, scale row i, and add c times row j to row i, respectively. The displayed inverses undo precisely those operations. Algebraically, Eij2​=0 when i=j, so (Im​+cEij​)(Im​−cEij​)=Im​; the other two inverse identities follow immediately from their row actions. Thus every elementary matrix is an invertible matrix.
