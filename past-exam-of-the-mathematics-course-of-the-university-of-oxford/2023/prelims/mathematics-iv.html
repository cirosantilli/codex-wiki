Write × for the paper's ∧. In a right-handed Cartesian coordinate system, the cross product is
a×b=(a2​b3​−a3​b2​, a3​b1​−a1​b3​, a1​b2​−a2​b1​).​
(1)
It is perpendicular to both vectors, has norm ∣a∣∣b∣sinϕ, where ϕ is their angle, and has direction given by the right-hand rule when nonzero. Its norm is the area of the parallelogram spanned by the two vectors.
If b×c=0, use that parallelogram as the base of the parallelepiped. Its perpendicular height is ∣a⋅n∣, where n=(b×c)/∣b×c∣. Thus its volume is
V=∣b×c∣∣a⋅n∣=∣a⋅(b×c)∣.​
(2)
If b×c=0, both the volume and this scalar triple product vanish. If the cross product is nonzero, the scalar triple product vanishes exactly when a lies in the plane spanned by b,c. If it is zero, b,c are linearly dependent, and adjoining a still spans at most a plane. Consequently the three vectors are coplanar if and only if a⋅(b×c)=0, including cases involving a zero vector.

The quadric surface x2+y2+z2=1 is the unit sphere centered at the origin. Each section z=c, for ∣c∣<1, is a circle of radius 1−c2​.
The quadric surface z=x2+y2 is a circular elliptic paraboloid, opening in the positive z direction with vertex at the origin. Its horizontal sections z=c>0 are circles of radius c​, and its vertical sections through the z axis are parabolas.
For ax2+y2=0, z is unrestricted. If a>0, both nonnegative terms must vanish, so the locus is the z axis, a straight line. If a=0, it is the plane y=0, represented by the repeated factor y2. If a<0, factorization gives
(y−−a​x)(y+−a​x)=0,
(12)
so the locus is two distinct planes, y=±−a​x, meeting along the z axis. These are degenerate quadrics, not ordinary curved cylinders.

Choose the rotated Cartesian coordinates
X=2​x+y​,Y=2​y−x​,Z=z.
(13)
Their inverse is x=(X−Y)/2​, y=(X+Y)/2​. Consequently x2+y2=X2+Y2 and xy=(X2−Y2)/2, giving the canonical form of a quadric
(1+2a​)X2+(1−2a​)Y2+Z2=1.​
(14)
The orthogonal diagonalization identifies the eigenvalues 1+a/2,1−a/2,1 of the symmetric matrix of the quadratic form.
For ∣a∣<2, all three coefficients are positive: the surface is an ellipsoid with semiaxes (1+a/2)−1/2,(1−a/2)−1/2,1. At a=0 it is a sphere.
For a=2, the equation is 2X2+Z2=1, an elliptic cylinder parallel to the Y axis. For a=−2, it is 2Y2+Z2=1, an elliptic cylinder parallel to the X axis. The absent coordinate is unrestricted; its disappearance does not turn the surface into an ellipse.
For a>2, the negative coefficient is that of Y2: this is a one-sheet hyperboloid with axis Y. For a<−2, the negative coefficient is that of X2: it is a one-sheet hyperboloid with axis X. This gives the signature transition from an ellipsoid to an elliptic cylinder and a one-sheet hyperboloid, including both transition values.

Write N=(n1​,n2​,n3​). Restriction to the plane z=0 gives
M11​x2+(M12​+M21​)xy+M22​y2+n1​x+n2​y+k=0.
(15)
This is an equation of degree at most two in two Cartesian coordinates, the general algebraic form of a conic, with possible degenerate conics and an empty set of real points. No determinant hypothesis is needed for this substitution.
For an arbitrary plane, choose a point x0​ and an orthonormal basis e,f of its direction linear subspace. Put B=(e f) and x=x0​+Bξ, where ξ=(s,t)T. With S=(M+MT)/2, the symmetric part of a matrix, the restricted equation is
ξTBTSBξ+(2x0T​SB+NB)ξ+x0T​Sx0​+Nx0​+k=0.​
(16)
This proves the plane-section result whenever “conic” allows the appropriate degeneracies. A rigid motion taking the chosen plane to z=0 gives the same argument.
The source's assumption detM=0 does not guarantee a nondegenerate conic. Even with M=I and f=x2+y2+z2−1, the section z=1 is a single point, and z=2 has no real points. With the nonsingular symmetric matrix giving f=2xz+y2, the section z=0 is the repeated straight line y=0.
There is a further literal omission: the source does not assume M is symmetric. For
M=​0−11​10−1​110​​,N=0,k=0,
(17)
one has detM=2 but f=2xz. Thus the section z=0 is the entire plane, rather than a conic. Only S controls the quadratic form; invertibility of an arbitrary M cannot remove this exception. If the intended hypothesis is that M is symmetric and nonsingular, the quadratic restriction to a plane cannot vanish identically: otherwise BTMB=0, so M would map a two-dimensional linear subspace injectively into its one-dimensional orthogonal complement, which is impossible. Degenerate and empty sections still remain possible.
