These modes have X(−x)=X(x) and move the mass. In dimensionless form their equation is cotz=αz, where α=m/(2ρℓ)>0. This is the spectral splitting for a string with a central point mass.
For the antisymmetric family, the graph of sinz crosses zero at every positive multiple of π. For the symmetric family, on each interval (nπ,nπ+π/2), n=0,1,…, the graph of cotz decreases continuously from +∞ to zero while αz increases. By the intermediate value theorem and strict monotonicity, there is exactly one intersection in each interval. On the remaining half-intervals cotz<0<αz, so there are no further positive roots. This proves graphically and analytically that both families contain infinitely many normal modes. The figure uses α=1 for illustration.
The algebraic case k=0 is not a mode: a zero-frequency profile would be linear on each half, continuous and zero at the endpoints; the zero-acceleration mass equation also makes its slopes agree, forcing the entire profile to be zero.
