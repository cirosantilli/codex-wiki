Use the ideal transverse-motion model of the question: constant tension, small slopes, no bending stiffness, and no transverse body-force term. On a short segment away from x=0, the vertical components of the endpoint tensions are, to first order in yx​, Tyx​(x+Δx,t) and −Tyx​(x,t). Its mass is ρΔx, since ρ is linear mass density. Newton's second law gives
ρΔxytt​=T[yx​(x+Δx,t)−yx​(x,t)].
(59)
Dividing by Δx and taking its limit proves the wave equation on a string on each side of the mass:
ytt​=c2yxx​,c2=ρT​,−ℓ<x<0 or 0<x<ℓ.​
(60)
The string on the right pulls the point mass with vertical component Tyx​(0+,t), while the string on the left contributes −Tyx​(0−,t). Hence
mY¨(t)=T[yx​(0+,t)−yx​(0−,t)].​
(61)
The fixed supports and attachment impose the boundary conditions
y(−ℓ,t)=y(ℓ,t)=0,y(0−,t)=y(0+,t)=Y(t).​
(62)
Together with the mass equation, these are the endpoint and matching conditions. The slope can jump at x=0: the jump supplies the mass's vertical acceleration. Requiring the slope to be continuous would incorrectly remove its dynamics.
