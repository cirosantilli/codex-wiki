Let a be an arbitrary constant vector. By the cyclic identity for the scalar triple product,
a⋅∮∂Σ​dr×F=∮∂Σ​(F×a)⋅dr.
(27)
Apply Stokes theorem to F×a. Since a is constant, the curl product identity gives
∇×(F×a)=(a⋅∇)F−a(∇⋅F).
(28)
Consequently
a⋅∮∂Σ​dr×F​=∬Σ​[n⋅(a⋅∇)F−(n⋅a)(∇⋅F)]dS=a⋅∬Σ​(n×∇)×FdS.​
(29)
For precision, the operator in the last line has components
[(n×∇)×F]i​=∑j=13​nj​∂i​Fj​−ni​∑j=13​∂j​Fj​.
(30)
The partial derivatives act on F, with n multiplying their values; they do not differentiate n. Since the dot products agree for every constant a, the cross-product form of Stokes theorem follows:
∮∂Σ​dr×F=∬Σ​(n×∇)×FdS.​
(31)
Here ∧ in the paper denotes the cross product, and the printed surface integral has its area element dS understood.
