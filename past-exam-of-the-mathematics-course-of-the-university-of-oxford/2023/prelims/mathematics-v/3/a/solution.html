Let Σ be a piecewise smooth oriented surface, and let F be continuously differentiable on an open neighbourhood of it. Stokes theorem states that
∮∂Σ​F⋅dr=∬Σ​(∇×F)⋅ndS.​
(17)
The boundary orientation is induced by the chosen unit normal vector through the right-hand rule. Smoothness on a neighbourhood makes the spatial partial derivatives in the curl well defined.
Choose the outward orientation of a surface on the lower half of the ellipsoid. At its lowest point the normal points down, so its boundary ellipse is traversed clockwise when viewed from above. Use
r(t)=(2cost,−3sint,0),0≤t≤2π.
(18)
The line integral is
∮∂Σ​F⋅dr​=∫02π​[(−6sint)(−2sint)+(6cost)(−3cost)]dt=12π−18π=−6π​.​
(19)
For an independent surface integral calculation, first compute ∇×F=(2y,−2x,1). Parametrize the lower half by
R(u,v)=(2sinucosv,3sinusinv,−33​cosu),0≤u≤2π​,0≤v≤2π.
(20)
The outward vector area element is
ndS=(Rv​×Ru​)dudv=(93​sin2ucosv,63​sin2usinv,−6sinucosu)dudv.
(21)
Thus
∬Σ​(∇×F)⋅ndS​=∫0π/2​∫02π​(303​sin3usinvcosv−6sinucosu)dvdu=−12π∫0π/2​sinucosudu=−6π​.​
(22)
The two sides agree. Choosing the inward unit normal vector and its corresponding counterclockwise boundary orientation changes both answers to 6π.
