The Fourier cosine series converges to the continuous even 2π-periodic extension. If r∈[−π,π] represents x modulo 2π, its value is
Fe​(x)=min(∣r∣,π/2).
(41)
It has value zero at x=2jπ, rises linearly with distance from these points until height π/2, and then stays flat until the next descending ramp. Thus the sketch on [−3π,3π] has minima at −2π,0,2π, and plateaux centred at the odd multiples of π. Its large partial sums round the corners and closely follow the whole graph.
The Fourier sine series converges, away from the odd multiples of π, to the odd 2π-periodic extension
Fo​(x)=sgn(r)min(∣r∣,π/2)(−π<r<π).
(42)
At every odd multiple of π its value is zero, the midpoint of the jump from π/2 to −π/2. At even multiples of π it passes continuously through zero. Thus on every interval ((2j−1)π,(2j+1)π) it has a negative plateau, a rising linear segment through 2jπ, and a positive plateau. The jump size is π.
The blue curves are the partial sums through n=60. The open endpoint circles and filled zero dots in the lower panel distinguish the one-sided limits from the value of the Fourier sine series at each jump. Near these jumps the partial sums exhibit the Gibbs phenomenon: the oscillations become concentrated in narrower regions as the number of terms grows, while their limiting overshoot remains about 0.08949 times the jump, here about 0.281.
