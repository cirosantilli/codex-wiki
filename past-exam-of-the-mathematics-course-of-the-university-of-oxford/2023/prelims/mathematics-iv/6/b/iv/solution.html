For a fixed attachment ratio δ>0, making the disc much heavier than the rod gives
ω2⟶ℓg​δ2+α2/2δ​=d2+a2/2gd​.
(63)
If the disc is also small relative to the attachment distance, α/δ=a/d→0, its moment of inertia about its own center is negligible compared with Md2. Thus
ω2⟶dg​,T⟶2πgd​​.​
(64)
This is a simple pendulum with a point mass at the disc's center and effective length d; it is length ℓ only if d=ℓ. Even before the small-angle approximation, its equation tends to θ¨=−(g/d)sinθ.
If δ is allowed to tend to zero together with the other parameters, the bare conditions α≪1 and μ≫1 do not alone justify that reduction. The needed dominance conditions are μδ≫1, μδ2≫1, and α/δ≪1, so that the rod contributes negligible gravitational torque and moment of inertia, and the disc behaves as a point mass. They follow automatically in the combined limit with fixed δ>0.
