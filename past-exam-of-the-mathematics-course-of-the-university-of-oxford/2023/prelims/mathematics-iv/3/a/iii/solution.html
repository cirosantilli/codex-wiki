For the cardioid, r′=−asinθ. The expression under the square root simplifies to
r2+(r′)2=a2(2+2cosθ)=4a2cos2(θ/2).
(21)
Since 0≤θ≤θ2​≤π, the cosine is nonnegative. Therefore
s(θ2​)=∫0θ2​​2acos(θ/2)dθ=4asin(θ2​/2).​
(22)
The upper half of the cardioid starts at (2a,0), passes through (0,a), bends to negative x, and ends at the cusp (0,0) when θ2​=π. Its full upper-half arc length is 4a; reflecting gives 8a for the full cardioid.
The fundamental theorem of calculus gives
dθ2​ds​=2acos(θ2​/2).
(23)
This decreases from 2a to zero, so the arc length increases fastest at θ2​=0, interpreted as the right-hand rate at the initial endpoint. If only positive endpoints are allowed, the rate has supremum 2a as θ2​↓0, rather than an attained interior maximum.
