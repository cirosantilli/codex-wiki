Use the dimensionless variables
A=4R2a2​,x=4R2r2​,β=2gR​v​.
(50)
The stated R>a/2 gives 0<A<1. Since f(r)=4Rx(1−x), the inequality from b factorizes as
D(r)=4R3(x−A)[4x2−4(1−A)x+β2]≥0.​
(51)
The two additional possible turning points are
x±​=21−A±(1−A)2−β2​​.
(52)
They are real exactly when β+A≤1. This exposes the intended critical value
(2gR​v​+4R2a2​)crit​=1,​
(53)
but the ordering of the initial radius A relative to these roots must also be checked.
First suppose 0<A<1/3. Then A<(1−A)/2. If β<1−A, the allowed branch starting at A is trapped either between x−​ and A or between A and x−​, according to its initial direction. At the coincident-root value β2=4A(1−2A) it is exactly a circular orbit. For β=0, the inner radial bound is the axis rather than a nonzero turning point.
At β=1−A, the additional roots coalesce at x∗​=(1−A)/2>A. The particle starts outward and approaches x∗​, or
r∗​=R2(1−A)​,
(54)
without reaching it in finite time. Indeed D is proportional to (x−A)(x−x∗​)2 and the radial energy equation gives dt/dx proportional to 1/∣x−x∗​∣ near x∗​. Its integral diverges. This is a bounded separatrix approaching an unstable circular orbit. For β>1−A, there is no outer root and the particle escapes. Thus for 0<A<1/3, boundedness holds exactly when β+A≤1, with the equality case included. At A=1/3, the same inequality characterizes boundedness, but equality is the exact circular orbit at the starting radius.
The source's weaker assumption A<1 is insufficient to make this single threshold a general criterion. For 1/3<A<1/2, the initial radius lies beyond the coalescing-root position. The transition is instead the unstable circular orbit at
βcirc​=2A(1−2A)​<1−A.
(55)
Below this speed the initial radial acceleration is inward and the particle remains in the inner allowed interval. At equality it stays exactly circular; above it the initial acceleration is outward and the accessible branch extends to infinity, even if β+A<1. For A>1/2, the surface slopes downward outward and every such launch escapes. At A=1/2, only β=0 remains at rest on the summit circle.
Consequently the complete classification for the intended azimuthal launch, including zero launch speed and exact equilibria, is
0<A≤1/3:1/3<A≤1/2:1/2<A<1:​bounded iff 0≤β≤1−A,bounded iff 0≤β≤2A(1−2A)​,unbounded for every β≥0.​​
(56)
For a concrete counterexample to the unqualified proposed criterion, take A=3/4 and β=1/10. Then β+A=17/20<1, but f′(a)<0 and the outward motion has no accessible outer barrier. This satisfies the printed radius assumption and escapes. A sufficient additional radius restriction for the intended threshold is a≤2R/3​, with the equality and circular orbit qualifications above.
