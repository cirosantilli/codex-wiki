Let D be a bounded planar region with a piecewise smooth, simple closed boundary C, oriented counterclockwise, and let P,Q have continuous first partial derivatives on an open neighbourhood of its closure. Green's theorem states that
∮C​(Pdx+Qdy)=∬D​(∂x∂Q​−∂y∂P​)dxdy.​
(1)
For a region with holes, orient every boundary component with D on its left: the outer component is counterclockwise and the inner components are clockwise.
Choose P=−y/2 and Q=x/2. Their indicated partial derivatives differ by one, so the area is
∣D∣=∬D​1dxdy=21​∮C​(xdy−ydx).​
(2)
This is signed area as a boundary integral; reversing the direction of C changes the sign of the integral.

For an oriented piecewise smooth parametric curve r:[a,b]→R3 and a continuous vector field F along it, the vector-field line integral is
∫C​F⋅dr=∫ab​F(r(t))⋅r′(t)dt.​
(5)
For a piecewise smooth curve, sum these integrals over its smooth pieces. The dot product selects the component of the vector field in the direction of motion.
If F=∇ϕ for a smooth function ϕ, the multivariable chain rule gives
F(r(t))⋅r′(t)=dtd​ϕ(r(t)).
(6)
The fundamental theorem of calculus, with cancellation at intermediate endpoints, therefore proves the fundamental theorem for line integrals:
∫C​∇ϕ⋅dr=ϕ(r(b))−ϕ(r(a)).
(7)
On a closed curve r(a)=r(b), so ∮C​∇ϕ⋅dr=0​.

An orientation-preserving reparametrization has the form γ​(s)=γ(h(s)), where h:[α,β]→[a,b] is an increasing, piecewise smooth change of parameter with h(α)=a, h(β)=b. ∮∂Σ​F⋅dr=∬Σ​(∇×F)⋅ndS.​

The sufficiency claim requires a domain qualification. For a continuously differentiable vector field on all of R3, or more generally on a simply connected open domain, the equivalence is valid. On an arbitrary domain, vanishing curl is necessary but may fail to be sufficient.
Necessity. Suppose every closed-curve line integral vanishes. Fix a point p in the open domain and a unit vector a. A sufficiently small planar disk Dε​ of radius ε, centred at p and normal to a, lies in the domain. Stokes theorem gives
0=∮∂Dε​​F⋅dr=∬Dε​​(∇×F)⋅adS.
(23)
Divide by πε2 and let ε↓0. By continuity, the average tends to (∇×F)(p)⋅a. Taking the three coordinate directions proves ∇×F=0​ everywhere.
Sufficiency on R3. A direct proof constructs a potential of a conservative vector field. Put
ϕ(x)=∫01​F(tx)⋅xdt.
(24)
Write Fj​ for the components of F. Vanishing curl says ∂i​Fj​=∂j​Fi​. Differentiation under the integral, followed by the chain rule, gives
∂i​ϕ(x)​=∫01​[Fi​(tx)+tj=1∑3​xj​∂i​Fj​(tx)]dt=∫01​[Fi​(tx)+tdtd​Fi​(tx)]dt=[tFi​(tx)]01​=Fi​(x).​
(25)
This is the radial integral potential for a curl-free field. The fundamental theorem for line integrals now gives zero on every closed curve. On a simply connected open domain the analogous argument uses path integrals: every loop can be contracted within the domain, and applying Stokes theorem to a piecewise smooth contraction makes its circulation zero. Equivalently, whenever a particular loop bounds an oriented spanning surface entirely in the domain, Stokes theorem immediately gives zero for that loop.
Why the domain matters. On U=R3∖{(0,0,z):z∈R}, the smooth vector field
F=(−x2+y2y​,x2+y2x​,0)
(26)
has zero curl, but its line integral around the unit circle in z=0 is 2π, as in ii. No spanning surface for this loop can lie entirely in U. The literal claim without a condition on the domain therefore has this counterexample.

It has value zero at x=2jπ, rises linearly with distance from these points until height π/2, and then stays flat until the next descending ramp. ρΔxytt​=T[yx​(x+Δx,t)−yx​(x,t)].
(59)
Dividing by Δx and taking its limit proves the wave equation on a string on each side of the mass:
ytt​=c2yxx​,c2=ρT​,−ℓ<x<0 or 0<x<ℓ.​
(60)
The string on the right pulls the point mass with vertical component Tyx​(0+,t), while the string on the left contributes −Tyx​(0−,t). Hence
mY¨(t)=T[yx​(0+,t)−yx​(0−,t)].​
