OurBigBook.com Words: 116
A point is in the closure E exactly when every neighbourhood meets E. This is equivalent to belonging to E or being a limit point of E.
If p∈/E, choose ε>0 with (p−ε,p+ε)∩E=∅. Every q with ∣q−p∣<ε/2 has a smaller neighbourhood contained in this interval and disjoint from E. Thus (p−ε/2,p+ε/2) misses E, and the complement of E is open. Hence E is closed.
Every closed set F containing E contains all limit points of E: a limit point outside F would have a neighbourhood missing F, and hence missing E. Therefore E⊆F. Since E is itself one of these closed sets,
E=⋂{F:E⊆F, F closed in R}.​
