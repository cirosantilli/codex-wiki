The endpoint radii are 5 and 13, but substituting those two numbers in the finite conical frustum formula is invalid. A rotation about the z-axis preserves the horizontal angle between the endpoint position vectors; here (3,4) and (12,5) are not parallel. The radius along the generating segment is not linear in height.
Parametrize the actual segment by
(x,y,z)=(3+9t,4+t,1+6t),0≤t≤1.
(27)
Its rotated circle at height 1+6t has radius
ρ(t)=(3+9t)2+(4+t)2​=25+62t+82t2​,ρ′(t)=ρ(t)31+82t​.
(28)
Thus the rotated image is the surface of revolution R(t,θ)=(ρ(t)cosθ,ρ(t)sinθ,1+6t). Applying the conical frustum formula to infinitesimal pieces of this radial profile, or directly using the cross product as in ii, gives the surface area of a surface of revolution
A​=2π∫01​ρ(t)ρ′(t)2+36​dt=2π∫01​9676t2+7316t+1861​dt.​
(29)
To evaluate it explicitly, put u=82t+31. Then 9676t2+7316t+1861=(59u2+19602)/41. Define
G(u)=u59u2+19602​+59​19602​log(59​u+59u2+19602​).
(30)
The chain rule gives G′(u)=259u2+19602​, so the answer is
A=8241​π​(G(113)−G(31))≃561.8721076764.​
