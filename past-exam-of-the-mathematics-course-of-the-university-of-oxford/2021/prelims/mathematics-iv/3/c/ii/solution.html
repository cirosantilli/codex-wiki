Let B=ATA. It is a symmetric matrix, and vTBv=∥Av∥2≥0. By the spectral theorem for real symmetric matrices, choose an orthonormal basis v1​,v2​,v3​ of eigenvectors of B, with nonnegative eigenvalues λ1​,λ2​,λ3​.
For each i=j, take the unit parallelogram parametrized by svi​+tvj​, 0≤s,t≤1. Its surface area is 1. Moreover,
Avi​⋅Avj​=viT​Bvj​=0,∥Avi​∥2=λi​.
(34)
The image parallelogram consequently has area λi​λj​​. By the assumption this equals 1 for all three pairs, giving
λ1​λ2​=λ1​λ3​=λ2​λ3​=1.
(35)
No eigenvalue can be zero. Dividing the equations and using positivity yields λ1​=λ2​=λ3​=1. Hence
ATA=I,so A is orthogonal.​
(36)
Thus testing all surfaces is stronger than necessary: the three unit parallelograms in these principal directions already determine the result. This is the three-dimensional characterization of linear surface-area isometries.
