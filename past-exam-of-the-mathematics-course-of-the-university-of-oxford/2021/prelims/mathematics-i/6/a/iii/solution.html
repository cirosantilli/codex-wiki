Let the finite set G/H contain r distinct left cosets. By i, they form a disjoint partition of a set G, and by ii each has ∣H∣ elements. If H is also finite, counting this finite disjoint set union gives
∣G∣=W∈G/H∑​∣W∣=r∣H∣=∣G/H∣∣H∣.​
(55)
Thus G is a finite group. This is the counting argument behind Lagrange's theorem.
