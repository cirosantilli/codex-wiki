By the spectral theorem for real symmetric matrices, there is an orthogonal matrix Q such that
QTAQ=diag(λ1​,…,λn​).
(40)
For clarity, the diagonalization can also be justified recursively. The characteristic polynomial has a complex root of a polynomial by the fundamental theorem of algebra. By ii its eigenvalue is real; taking a nonzero real or imaginary part of an associated eigenvector in Cn gives a real eigenvector, which can be normalized. Its orthogonal complement is invariant, because ⟨Ax,z⟩=⟨x,Az⟩=λ⟨x,z⟩=0. Restriction to this linear subspace is again symmetric, and induction on dimension gives an orthonormal basis of eigenvectors.
All λi​≥0 by ii. The permitted invariance of determinant and matrix trace under change of basis, applied to the diagonal matrix, yields
detA=i=1∏n​λi​,trA=i=1∑n​λi​,λi​≥0.​
