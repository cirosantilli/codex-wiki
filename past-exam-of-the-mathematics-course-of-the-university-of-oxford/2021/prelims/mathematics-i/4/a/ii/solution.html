The two-vector Gram matrix is
A=(∥v1​∥2⟨v1​,v2​⟩​⟨v1​,v2​⟩∥v2​∥2​),detA=∥v1​∥2∥v2​∥2−⟨v1​,v2​⟩2.
(34)
The upper bound follows because the subtracted square is nonnegative. If either vector is zero, the determinant is zero. Otherwise apply the non-strict version of the calculation in i: ∥v1​−tv2​∥2≥0 with t=⟨v1​,v2​⟩/∥v2​∥2 gives ⟨v1​,v2​⟩2≤∥v1​∥2∥v2​∥2. Therefore
0≤detA≤∥v1​∥2∥v2​∥2.​
(35)
Equality on the left holds exactly when the two vectors are linearly dependent; equality on the right holds exactly when they are orthogonal. The Gram determinant measures the squared area of their parallelogram: in the nonzero case, detA=∥v2​∥2∥v1​−tv2​∥2, the square of base times perpendicular height.
