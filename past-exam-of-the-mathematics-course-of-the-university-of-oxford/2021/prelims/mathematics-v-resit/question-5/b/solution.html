Put u(r,θ)=R(r)Θ(θ) in Laplace's equation and multiply by r2/(RΘ) on intervals where the factors are nonzero. Separation of variables gives

The angular Dirichlet boundary conditions are Θ(0)=Θ(π/2)=0. Multiplication of the angular equation by Θ, followed by integration by parts, gives
λ∫0π/2​Θ2dθ=∫0π/2​(Θ′)2dθ.

For a nonzero angular eigenfunction this forces λ>0: equality would make Θ constant and then zero. Write λ=μ2. The first angular condition removes the cosine term, and the second requires sin(μπ/2)=0. Thus
μ=2n,λn​=4n2,Θn​(θ)=sin(2nθ),n=1,2,….
(57)
The radial Cauchy-Euler equation has the two solutions r2n and r−2n. By superposition, the general separated series is therefore
u(r,θ)=n=1∑∞​(An​r2n+Bn​r−2n)sin(2nθ)​.
(58)
The coefficients are arbitrary subject to convergence with enough partial derivatives on compact subsets to give a harmonic function and to the prescribed zero values on the two open radial edges. Completeness of the sine eigenfunctions gives the usual series representation for solutions smooth up to those edges on closed radial subintervals. There is no constant or logarithmic zero mode because its angular factor would be linear in θ and would vanish at both ends only if it were zero.
The negative powers must be retained: the stated domain omits r=0, and no boundedness at that vertex is prescribed. For example, r−2sin(2θ) is a harmonic function satisfying both edge conditions. An additional boundedness condition as r→0 would remove all Bn​; an additional value on the circular arc would then determine the remaining An​. With only the conditions in the paper, neither restriction is available and the solution is not unique.
