OurBigBook.com Words: 81
For n=1, choose x=0, y=1. For n≥2, define the continuous function
g(t)=f(t+1/n)−f(t),0≤t≤1−1/n.
(33)
Its values at the grid points have the telescoping sum
∑k=0n−1​g(k/n)=∑k=0n−1​(f((k+1)/n)−f(k/n))=f(1)−f(0)=0.
(34)
If one summand is zero, we already have a zero of g. Otherwise the summands cannot all have the same sign, so two of these values have opposite signs. The intermediate value theorem between their arguments gives t∈[0,1−1/n] with g(t)=0. Set x=t, y=t+1/n. Then
∣x−y∣=1/n,f(x)=f(y).​
(35)
This is the horizontal chord of reciprocal-integer length property; the telescoping sum explains why reciprocal integers appear.
