 ... 2021 Prelims Mathematics II 6 d ii
OurBigBook.com Words: 155
A maximum or minimum can occur at an endpoint. The rule that a derivative vanishes at an extremum requires an interior extremum and differentiability there. The hypotheses give no two-sided derivative at the endpoints, and even an existing one-sided derivative need not vanish there.
For example, f(x)=x on [0,1] has m=0 at xm​=0 and M=1 at xM​=1, but f′(x)=1 at every interior point; its endpoint one-sided derivatives are also one. Its graph meets both horizontal bounding lines without being tangent to either. Thus the claimed conclusion about two zeros of the derivative fails.
There is a second logical issue: the argument never establishes two distinct extremizing points. For a constant function, the maximum and minimum coincide and may be represented by the same point. The constant function does have many stationary points, but that needs its own argument; it does not follow from counting the same selected point twice. In Rolle's theorem, equal endpoint values ensure an interior extremum when the function is nonconstant, which is the missing mechanism here.
