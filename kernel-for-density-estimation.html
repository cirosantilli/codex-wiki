Kernel for density estimation (K,Kh​(u)=h−1K(u/h))

 ... Mathematics Area of mathematics Probability and statistics Statistical inference Nonparametric statistics Density estimation
OurBigBook.com Words: 2k Articles: 34
A kernel for density estimation is an integrable function K:R→R with unit integral. For an ordinary kernel density estimator that is itself a probability density function, take
K(u)≥0,∫R​K(u)du=1,Kh​(u)=h1​K(hu​),h>0.
(1)
Thus K specifies the bump's shape and the smoothing bandwidth h specifies its width. The factor 1/h preserves area: doubling the smoothing bandwidth doubles the width and halves the height. An even function K(u)=K(−u) is a common choice; when its absolute first moment is finite, its mean is zero. Symmetry is useful but is not required for normalization.
The Gaussian density kernel is the standard normal density, K(u)=(2π)−1/2e−u2/2. Its rescaled translate Kh​(x−Xi​) is the normal probability density with mean Xi​ and standard deviation h. It is positive everywhere, whereas an Epanechnikov kernel has compact support. With the Gaussian density kernel, the smoothing bandwidth is a standard deviation, not a cutoff beyond which observations have no influence.
The broader convention also allows signed kernels of order ell, such as the flat-top kernel for density estimation, to cancel moments and reduce the bias of an estimator. Their unit integral still normalizes total area, but a resulting kernel density estimator can take negative values and need not be a probability density function. Nonnegativity is therefore an additional condition, not a consequence of unit integral.
