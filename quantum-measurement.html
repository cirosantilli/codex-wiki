Quantum measurement (pm​=Tr(Em​ρ))

 Home Physics Branches of physics Quantum theory
OurBigBook.com Words: 6k Articles: 66
A quantum measurement turns a quantum state preparation into a classical outcome, such as a detector label. Two questions must be distinguished: how likely is each outcome, and what quantum state remains conditional on that outcome? A positive operator-valued measure answers the first; a quantum instrument answers both. This distinction is useful whenever a measured system participates in a later experiment, including quantum state tomography and measurement-based quantum feedback.
For a density operator ρ, the Born rule gives pm​=Tr(Em​ρ), where the Em​ are positive operators satisfying ∑m​Em​=I. Here I is the identity operator, and Tr denotes the trace. Positivity makes every pm​ nonnegative; completeness makes their sum one. For a pure state, ρ=∣ψ⟩⟨ψ∣ and pm​=⟨ψ∣Em​∣ψ⟩. A projective measurement is the special case in which the Em​ are orthogonal projections onto mutually orthogonal subspaces.
For a qubit, choose the computational basis and write a normalized pure state, up to global phase, as
∣ψ(θ,ϕ)⟩=cos(θ/2)∣0⟩+eiϕsin(θ/2)∣1⟩,0≤θ≤π,0≤ϕ<2π.
(1)
The angle ϕ is a relative quantum phase; at the poles it does not distinguish states. Measuring the Pauli matrix Z reports +1 for label zero and −1 for label one, with
p0​=cos2(θ/2),p1​=sin2(θ/2).
(2)
These probabilities describe repeated identical preparations. They do not assign a hidden, pre-existing value to every possible measurement basis.
The Born rule grew out of Max Born's 1926 work on quantum scattering: squared probability amplitudes determine the relative frequencies of scattering outcomes. Born explained this statistical interpretation again in his 1954 Nobel lecture. This probability law does not by itself select a post-measurement state. [ref]; [ref].

Sequential qubit measurement correlation (En​(U)=21​Tr(NUNU†))

Words: 79
Quantum measurement OurBigBook.com
Prepare I/2, measure a Pauli observable N=n⋅σ, apply a fixed unitary operator U, then measure N again. If the first and second eigenvalue labels are j,k∈{+1,−1}, their joint probability is 21​∣⟨nk​∣U∣nj​⟩∣2. Consequently
En​(U)=E[jk]=21​Tr(NUNU†).
(3)
The first outcome has probability 1/2 and its conditional state is ∣nj​⟩ by the Lüders rule. Summing jk times the joint probabilities and using N=∑j​j∣nj​⟩⟨nj​∣ proves the trace formula. This temporal correlation differs from the spatial correlation ⟨ψ∣N⊗N∣ψ⟩ of two simultaneously measured qubits.

State-selective fluorescence detection

A readout in which one logical quantum state is coupled to an optical cycling transition and scatters many photons, while another is dark. An ideal bright/dark record implements a projective measurement in the logical basis. Neglecting that record removes their relative quantum coherence. Missed photons, off-resonant excitation and pumping out of the logical subspace qualify the ideal model.

After a computational-basis projective measurement, an observer who lacks the result assigns a diagonal density matrix. If the observer's preparation prior has mean q=E∣α∣2, the assignment is the displayed matrix. No outcome record and no stated prior do not logically force q=1/2; that value follows from a symmetric or maximum-entropy ignorance convention. Quantum Zeno effect

Words: 358 Articles: 3
Quantum measurement OurBigBook.com
Inhibition of a transition by increasingly frequent ideal projective measurements that check whether a quantum system remains in its initial quantum state. For a normalized vector ψ, a fixed Hamiltonian operator H and the orthogonal projection P=∣ψ⟩⟨ψ∣, the probability of passing every check at N equally spaced times up to fixed time T is
PN​(T)=​⟨ψ,e−iHT/(Nℏ)ψ⟩​2N.
(4)
For a finite-dimensional Hilbert space, the quadratic short-time quantum survival probability implies PN​(T)→1. This is an ideal limit with instantaneous measurements and unitary time evolution between them.

Beryllium-ion quantum Zeno experiment of 1990

Words: 127
Quantum Zeno effect OurBigBook.com
In 1990, Wayne M. Itano, Daniel J. Heinzen, J. J. Bollinger and David J. Wineland reported inhibited transitions in about 5,000 9Be+ ions held in a cylindrical Penning trap. Trapped magnesium ions, illuminated at 280 nm, kept the beryllium ions cold. A 320.7 MHz radio frequency field coupled two ground-state hyperfine structure sublevels during a 256 ms pulse adjusted for complete transfer. Up to 64 intervening 313 nm pulses addressed an optical transition from level 1: scattering distinguished it from level 2. Afterward, calibrated fluorescence counts over 100 ms measured the final quantum state population. This ensemble readout did not select ions that remained in the initial quantum state at every check; departure followed by return also contributes. The apparatus made inhibition by repeated quantum measurements experimentally accessible.[ref]

Nonselective two-level Zeno dynamics (Pb​(T)=21​[1−cosn(ΩT/n)])

Quantum Zeno effect OurBigBook.com
Start in a, drive resonantly with Hamiltonian operator ℏΩX/2, and perform n ideal basis measurements separated by T/n. An interval changes either basis state with probability p=sin2(ΩT/(2n)). Ignoring measurement records leaves a two-state Markov chain whose population difference is multiplied by 1−2p=cos(ΩT/n) each step. This proves the displayed final-population formula.
For a pi pulse, ΩT=π, it is π2/(4n)+O(n−2). The repeated projective-measurement survival probability instead counts records in which every result is a, giving Palla​=cos2n(π/(2n)). Final return after an earlier departure separates the two finite-n events.

Measurement-guided Zeno transfer (PN​=cos2N(π/(2N))→1)

Words: 72
Quantum Zeno effect OurBigBook.com
Successively measure in bases whose first vector rotates from ∣0⟩ to ∣1⟩ in N equal angular steps. The probability to remain on that branch throughout is the displayed product of squared overlaps. Without postselection, the final target probability is [1+cosN(π/N)]/2. Both approach one for an initial ∣0⟩, but a general input loses its relative phase between the two measurement branches; the process does not implement a coherent Pauli X gate on unknown inputs.

The coherent interaction that correlates a system observable with detector records before selecting an outcome. An ideal coupling maps ∣sj​⟩∣d0​⟩ to ∣sj​⟩∣dj​⟩. Linearity then maps a superposition to ∑j​cj​∣sj​dj​⟩. The full state can remain pure and entangled, whereas a nonselective projective measurement removes coherence between different record sectors. Elitzur-Vaidman bomb tester

Words: 323 Articles: 2
Quantum measurement OurBigBook.com
A photon in a quantum superposition of a safe path and an absorbing path can sometimes certify a live absorber without being absorbed. Recombination is chosen so that one output is impossible if the absorber is absent. A detection at that output is then an unambiguous successful test. The measurement back-action of a surviving live-absorber trial changes the interference pattern.
In 1993, Avshalom Elitzur and Lev Vaidman asked how to identify a working bomb whose sensor would be triggered by even one photon. Their theoretical proposal places the sensor in one arm of a Mach-Zehnder interferometer, adjusted so that destructive interference keeps one detector dark when both arms are unobstructed. A click there can identify a working bomb while leaving it unexploded. The motivation is to obtain information about a fragile object without destroying it; the bomb makes that operational claim explicit without committing to an interpretation of quantum mechanics.[ref]

Balanced Hadamard interaction-free measurement (Psuccess​=1/4,Pabsorb​=1/2,Pinconclusive​=1/4)

Words: 80
Elitzur-Vaidman bomb tester OurBigBook.com
With two Hadamard gates as mixers, an unobstructed zero-phase arm gives only port 0 for upper-port incidence. An ideal absorber in arm 1 acts on the intermediate superposition (∣0⟩+∣1⟩)/2​. It absorbs with probability 1/2; its no-absorption branch is the unnormalized state ∣0⟩/2​. Recombination gives amplitude 1/2 at each surviving output, so the dark-port success probability is 1/4. The result assumes a calibrated transparent alternative and an ideal absorber; a dark-port event alone does not establish those apparatus assumptions.

Repeated weak-rotation bomb-test efficiency (PN​=1+qq​(1−q2N),q=1−p)

Words: 84
Elitzur-Vaidman bomb tester OurBigBook.com
For an Elitzur-Vaidman bomb tester using a rotation and its inverse, let p be the probability of entering the absorbing path and q=1−p. Conditional on a live absorber, one trial has explosion probability p, successful safe identification probability pq, and inconclusive probability q2. The inconclusive projective measurement resets the input, so summing a geometric series gives the displayed success probability after at most N trials. Its limit is q/(1+q), approaching one half as p tends to zero through positive values. Exactly zero coupling produces no information.

Measurement backaction (ρ↦Km​ρKm†​/Tr(Km​ρKm†​))

Words: 223
Quantum measurement OurBigBook.com
Measurement backaction is the effect of a quantum measurement on later predictions for the measured system. A quantum instrument with one Kraus operator Km​ per recorded outcome has
pm​=Tr(Km​ρKm†​),ρm​=pm​Km​ρKm†​​,∑m​Km†​Km​=I.
(5)
The conditional density operator ρm​ exists only when pm​>0. Its nonlinear normalization expresses conditioning; the unnormalized map ρ↦Km​ρKm†​ is linear. More generally, an unresolved outcome can contain several Kraus operators: replace the numerator by ∑a​Kma​ρKma†​ and take its trace for pm​. [ref].
Keeping the outcome gives a selective quantum measurement. Discarding it gives a nonselective quantum measurement with quantum channel ρ↦∑m​pm​ρm​. The latter describes the whole measured population without its labels, whereas each ρm​ describes a selected subpopulation. Neither description requires the measured quantity to have possessed its eventual value before the interaction.
For a concrete test, prepare the Pauli X eigenstate ∣+⟩=(∣0⟩+∣1⟩)/2​. Its X=+1 probability is one. An ideal Z projective measurement gives zero or one with equal probability, leaving ∣0⟩ or ∣1⟩ under the Lüders rule. Discarding the result leaves the maximally mixed state I/2. A subsequent X measurement now gives either sign with probability one half, even if the Z result was kept. This observable change in statistics is backaction. Measuring X on the original ∣+⟩ instead leaves it unchanged: disturbance depends on both the preparation and the instrument. Measurement-based quantum feedback uses the recorded outcome to choose a subsequent control operation, so both parts of the instrument matter.

Perfect discrimination of pure states requires orthogonality

Words: 65
Quantum measurement OurBigBook.com
If a positive operator-valued measure distinguishes normalized pure states ∣u⟩,∣v⟩ with certainty, the effect E for the first answer satisfies ⟨u∣E∣u⟩=1 and ⟨v∣E∣v⟩=0. Positivity of E and I−E implies E∣v⟩=0 and E∣u⟩=∣u⟩, because a positive operator with zero expectation annihilates the vector. Consequently ⟨u∣v⟩=⟨u∣E∣v⟩=0. Conversely, orthogonal states can be measured with their rank-one linear projections. A common unitary operator preserves this inner product and cannot remove the obstruction.

Quantum nondemolition measurement

Words: 1k Articles: 6
Quantum measurement OurBigBook.com
An ideal measurement preserving the measured eigenspaces. In the projective setting, the selective instrument ρ↦Pj​ρPj​ preserves every vector within outcome j, including coherence inside a degenerate eigenspace. Repeated measurements agree in the absence of intervening dynamics changing the measured quantity. Measuring a finer observable can destroy degeneracy coherence and need not realize the same Lüders rule instrument.
A physical measurement interaction must correlate the system with a meter; in Haroche cavity photon nondemolition experiment that meter is a chemical atom. For a measured observable A, a useful sufficient ideal condition is [A,Hint​]=0 together with [A,H0​]=0, where Hint​ is the interaction Hamiltonian operator and H0​ the free Hamiltonian operator. These conditions protect its eigenvalues during probing and between probes. They do not protect every other observable, nor prevent conditional quantum state changes. In particular, preserving photon number can disturb the field's quantum phase.

Haroche cavity photon nondemolition experiment

Words: 967 Articles: 2
Quantum nondemolition measurement OurBigBook.com
In the 2007 cavity quantum electrodynamics experiment of Christine Guerlin and collaborators, including Sébastien Gleyzes, Michel Brune, Jean-Michel Raimond and Serge Haroche, an injected coherent state occupied a 51.1 GHz microwave cavity with niobium-coated mirrors exhibiting superconductivity. Velocity-selected rubidium chemical atoms were prepared as circular Rydberg atoms in levels with principal quantum numbers 50 and 51. Repeated atomic readouts progressively narrowed the inferred photon number to a Fock state; later records revealed jumps. The cavity damping time was Tc​=0.130s. Empty-cavity Ramsey interferometry scans fixed the analysis-phase origin; weak injected fields refined the response calibration. Finite fringe contrast required calibrated likelihoods; photon losses could interrupt convergence.[ref]
The functional sequence is preparation, first Ramsey interferometry zone R1​, dispersive cavity C, second zone R2​, and state-selective detection. The first π/2 microwave pulse prepares an atomic quantum superposition; dispersive atom-cavity coupling adds a photon number-dependent relative quantum phase while suppressing energy exchange. The second pulse converts that quantum phase into atomic populations; state-selective field ionization reads e or g. Successive chemical atoms carry the meter records away while the same field remains available.[ref]
This arrangement makes quantum nondemolition measurement useful for following a field repeatedly and preparing a Fock state by conditioning. The photon number is inferred from a sequence of physical records; it is not a directly visible label attached to a photon. Each atomic probe makes a weak, partial quantum measurement of photon number, and the accumulated records can approach a resolved count. A single binary meter need not identify one among many possible Fock states, as perfect discrimination of pure states requires orthogonality.

Sequential Bayesian photon counting (pk​(n)=pk−1​(n)Lsk​​(n;ϕk​)/Zk​)

Words: 453
Haroche cavity photon nondemolition experiment OurBigBook.com
Consider an original balanced-readout model, rather than a fit to the experiment. Let n∈{0,…,Nmax​}, with a normalized prior probability p0​(n)≥0 and ∑n​p0​(n)=1. A detected chemical atom has outcome s∈{e,g}. Define the conditional probabilities
Le​(n;ϕ)=P(e∣n,ϕ)=21+Ccos(ϕ+nχ)​,Lg​(n;ϕ)=1−Le​(n;ϕ),0≤C≤1.
(6)
Here χ is the accumulated quantum phase per photon, ϕ the controlled analysis quantum phase, and C the fringe contrast. The two probabilities sum to one for every n. Actual calibration may also require an offset and asymmetric detection errors; C alone does not specify a quantum instrument.
Assume no photon number change during the record and fresh independently prepared atomic meters. For observed outcomes s1​,…,sk​ at recorded settings ϕ1​,…,ϕk​, the Born rule and Bayes' theorem give
Zk​=∑m=0Nmax​​pk−1​(m)Lsk​​(m;ϕk​),pk​(n)=Zk​pk−1​(n)Lsk​​(n;ϕk​)​=∑m=0Nmax​​p0​(m)∏j=1k​Lsj​​(m;ϕj​)p0​(n)∏j=1k​Lsj​​(n;ϕj​)​.
(7)
The posterior probability is defined only for Zk​>0. Summing its numerator over n proves normalization. This is also the update of diagonal elements of a field density operator under a quantum measurement; it does not assume that an initial quantum superposition was an unknown pre-existing integer. The observed settings are part of the record, especially when selected adaptively.
For an exact finite example take p0​=(1,1,1,1)/4, C=4/5, and χ=π/2. An e outcome at ϕ=0 has likelihood (9,5,1,5)/10, giving p1​=(9,5,1,5)/20. Another e at ϕ=−π/2 gives p2​=(9,9,1,1)/20. A subsequent g at ϕ=0 gives
p3​=681​(9,45,9,5),P(s1​=e,s2​=e,s3​=g)=20017​.
(8)
Thus three atomic records raise the posterior probability of n=1 from 1/4 to 45/68, while leaving finite uncertainty. No one of these outcomes identifies n with certainty.
The periodic likelihood function imposes an identifiability limit. If χ=2π/q for an integer q>0, then Ls​(n+q;ϕ)=Ls​(n;ϕ) for every setting: the posterior odds between n and n+q never change. Varying ϕ can resolve a cosine reflection ambiguity within a period, but cannot remove this exact period. Changing χ can supply additional information. A finite cutoff is therefore a physical assumption to justify, not evidence that excluded high photon numbers were absent. At C=0 no outcome changes the prior probability; at 0<C<1 neither binary outcome has an exactly zero likelihood.
The no-loss assumption also has a time limit. In an original zero-temperature loss model, let κ=1/Tc​. A Fock state ∣n⟩ loses its first photon at rate nκ, so its survival probability is exp(−nκt) and its mean lifetime is Tc​/n for n>0. Several records must be acquired within that lifetime to identify a plateau. When losses matter, first predict pk−​(n)=∑m​Tnm​(Δt)pk−1​(m) using a normalized transition matrix, then apply the atomic Bayesian inference update to pk−​. Thermal excitation requires upward transitions too. A delayed or broadened inference after a jump need not indicate that an atom caused the jump.

Photon-number measurement backaction (ρnm​↦ρnm​cos((n−m)χ/2))

Words: 238
Haroche cavity photon nondemolition experiment OurBigBook.com
An explicit original ideal meter model explains how photon number survives while field quantum coherence changes. Let N^ be the number operator and prepare an atomic qubit in ∣+⟩=(∣e⟩+∣g⟩)/2​. The dispersive unitary operator
U=exp(−iχN^⊗σz​/2)
(9)
commutes with N^⊗I. For a field quantum superposition ∑n​cn​∣n⟩, it produces ∑n​cn​∣n⟩∣an​⟩, where ∣an​⟩=(e−inχ/2∣e⟩+einχ/2∣g⟩)/2​. The atomic meter states encode n through relative quantum phase and can become entangled with the field. The second Ramsey interferometry pulse and detector turn those correlations into the outcome probabilities above when C=1.
Tracing out the atom multiplies a field density matrix entry ρnm​ by ⟨am​∣an​⟩=cos((n−m)χ/2). Hence the nonselective quantum measurement preserves every photon number population ρnn​ while changing off-diagonal quantum coherence. For χ=π/2, coherence between ∣0⟩ and ∣1⟩ is multiplied by 1/2​ per fresh meter, even with no photons absorbed. Field quantum phase information can therefore be disturbed by an energy-preserving probe. No universal self-adjoint phase operator or naive canonical number–phase commutator is needed for this conclusion.
One possible finite-contrast quantum instrument makes the distinction sharper. Define diagonal Kraus operators Ke​=cos((ϕ+χN^)/2) and Kg​=sin((ϕ+χN^)/2). After their ideal readout, flip the reported label with probability r=(1−C)/2. The unnormalized selective maps are
Je​(ρ)=(1−r)Ke​ρKe†​+rKg​ρKg†​,Jg​(ρ)=rKe​ρKe†​+(1−r)Kg​ρKg†​.
(10)
Their traces reproduce Le​,Lg​ on a Fock state, and their sum reproduces the displayed dephasing quantum channel, independently of r. At C=0 the recorded labels carry no information although the physical meter interaction can still cause measurement backaction. This is an illustrative instrument, not a reconstruction of all laboratory imperfections; equal likelihoods alone do not determine the disturbance.

Entanglement-assisted statistical singlet verification (Ω=Ps​+(I−Ps​)/3)

Words: 63
Quantum nondemolition measurement OurBigBook.com
Choose a shared random axis uniformly from x,y,z, perform its entanglement-assisted nondemolition parity measurement with one Bell pair, and accept the anticorrelated parity. The spin singlet state always passes unchanged. The averaged acceptance operator is Ω=∑j​(I−σj​⊗σj​)/6=Ps​+(I−Ps​)/3, so an orthogonal triplet state passes with probability one third. This is a statistical verifier with a nonzero acceptance gap, rather than an exact single-shot singlet-versus-triplet decision. The Bell-pair cost of exact nondemolition singlet verification concerns the stronger zero-error task.

Bell-pair cost of exact nondemolition singlet verification (R≥4)

Words: 82
Quantum nondemolition measurement OurBigBook.com
Exact singlet verification accepts the spin singlet state with certainty, rejects every orthogonal state, and preserves the singlet on acceptance. Each nonzero accepting Kraus operator is then a scalar multiple of the singlet projector, whose operator Schmidt rank is four. The local Kraus rank bound from an entangled resource excludes a single shared Bell pair, which has Schmidt rank two. Two shared Bell pairs suffice by a Bell-state nondemolition measurement followed by reporting whether its label is the singlet. This protocol may disturb triplet coherence. The rank bound concerns the resource Schmidt rank, and is not an entropy lower bound of two bits for arbitrary resource states.

Entanglement-assisted nondemolition parity measurement (Kuv​=2−1/2Pu⊕v​)

Share a Bell state meter pair, apply local system-to-meter CNOT gates, and measure each meter in the computational basis. The parity of the local meter records measures system ZA​ZB​ while preserving all coherence within either parity sector. The complete tuple of local records has Kraus operator Pu⊕v​/2​. Each local record is uniform, and the nonlocal result requires local operations and classical communication to compare records. A local Hadamard gate conjugation supplies the corresponding XA​XB​ measurement.

Antidistinguishable quantum states

A finite family of quantum states is antidistinguishable if a quantum measurement has labelled outcomes such that each outcome has zero probability for its corresponding state, while the outcomes form a complete quantum measurement. It can exclude a state without identifying which state was prepared. The PBR exclusion measurement for zero and plus supplies a four-state projective measurement example.

Postselection

Words: 219 Articles: 3
Quantum measurement OurBigBook.com
Postselection conditions a quantum measurement on a specified outcome. If its branch acts by a linear operator K on a normalized input ∣b⟩, its probability is ∥K∣b⟩∥2, and its normalized output is K∣b⟩/∥K∣b⟩∥. This conditional state is defined only when the probability is nonzero. Physically unsuccessful trials are discarded; postselection is not a deterministic implementation of the nonlinear normalization map.

Aharonov-Bergmann-Lebowitz rule (pj​=∣⟨b∣Pj​∣a⟩∣2/∑k​∣⟨b∣Pk​∣a⟩∣2)

Words: 161 Articles: 2
Postselection OurBigBook.com
For an ideal projective measurement with the Lüders rule, condition on both an initial quantum state preparation and a final successful postselection. Its outcome probabilities are proportional to ∣⟨b∣Pj​∣a⟩∣2, where a is the forward-evolved initial state and b the backward-evolved final state. The normalization must be nonzero. The expression is symmetric in these two boundary states, although its derivation uses the ordinary Born rule and conditional state update.

Context dependence of pre- and post-selected measurements

Words: 99 Articles: 1
Aharonov-Bergmann-Lebowitz rule OurBigBook.com
In the ABL rule, probabilities depend on the full intermediate quantum measurement instrument. A binary Lüders rule quantum measurement preserves coherence within its unresolved complement, whereas a fully resolved projective measurement removes it. Summing fine-grained probabilities after quantum measurement does not generally reproduce the coarse-grained experiment. Different postselection success rates are part of the distinction.

Quantum state tomography

Quantum state tomography estimates a density operator from measurement frequencies on many independently and identically prepared systems. One fixed projective measurement reveals only its basis populations, so further measurement settings are generally needed. For a qubit, write
ρ=21​(I+rx​X+ry​Y+rz​Z),rj​=Tr(ρσj​)=pj,+​−pj,−​.
(19)
The three Pauli matrices thus turn measured expectation values into the Bloch vector. This reconstruction uses the Bloch ball representation of arbitrary qubit density operators. [ref].
Divide the prepared ensemble into groups measured in the X, Y and Z bases. For example, positive-outcome frequencies 0.8,0.5,0.9 give the estimate r=(0.6,0,0.8) and

(20)

Multiple bases are needed in this projective protocol; a single fixed informationally complete POVM, such as the tetrahedral qubit POVM, is an alternative. It still requires an ensemble of trials.
