Measurement in quantum mechanics (pm​=Tr(Em​ρ))

 Home Physics Branches of physics Quantum theory
OurBigBook.com Words: 3k Articles: 44
A measurement in quantum mechanics turns a quantum state preparation into a classical outcome, such as a detector label. Two questions must be distinguished: how likely is each outcome, and what quantum state remains conditional on that outcome? A positive operator-valued measure answers the first; a quantum instrument answers both. This distinction is useful whenever a measured system participates in a later experiment, including quantum state tomography and measurement-based quantum feedback.
For a density operator ρ, the Born rule gives pm​=Tr(Em​ρ), where the Em​ are positive operators satisfying ∑m​Em​=I. Here I is the identity operator, and Tr denotes the trace. Positivity makes every pm​ nonnegative; completeness makes their sum one. For a pure state, ρ=∣ψ⟩⟨ψ∣ and pm​=⟨ψ∣Em​∣ψ⟩. A projective measurement is the special case in which the Em​ are orthogonal projections onto mutually orthogonal subspaces.
For a qubit, choose the computational basis and write a normalized pure state, up to global phase, as
∣ψ(θ,ϕ)⟩=cos(θ/2)∣0⟩+eiϕsin(θ/2)∣1⟩,0≤θ≤π,0≤ϕ<2π.
(1)
The angle ϕ is a relative quantum phase; at the poles it does not distinguish states. Measuring the Pauli matrix Z reports +1 for label zero and −1 for label one, with
p0​=cos2(θ/2),p1​=sin2(θ/2).
(2)
These probabilities describe repeated identical preparations. They do not assign a hidden, pre-existing value to every possible measurement basis.
The Born rule grew out of Max Born's 1926 work on quantum scattering: squared probability amplitudes determine the relative frequencies of scattering outcomes. Born explained this statistical interpretation again in his 1954 Nobel lecture. This probability law does not by itself select a post-measurement state. [ref]; [ref].

A photon in a quantum superposition of a safe path and an absorbing path can sometimes certify a live absorber without being absorbed. Repeated weak-rotation bomb-test efficiency (PN​=1+qq​(1−q2N),q=1−p)

Words: 84
Elitzur-Vaidman bomb tester OurBigBook.com
For an Elitzur-Vaidman bomb tester using a rotation and its inverse, let p be the probability of entering the absorbing path and q=1−p. Conditional on a live absorber, one trial has explosion probability p, successful safe identification probability pq, and inconclusive probability q2. The inconclusive projective measurement resets the input, so summing a geometric series gives the displayed success probability after at most N trials. Its limit is q/(1+q), approaching one half as p tends to zero through positive values. Exactly zero coupling produces no information.

Measurement backaction (ρ↦Km​ρKm†​/Tr(Km​ρKm†​))

Words: 223
Measurement in quantum mechanics OurBigBook.com
Measurement backaction is the effect of a measurement in quantum mechanics on later predictions for the measured system. A quantum instrument with one Kraus operator Km​ per recorded outcome has
pm​=Tr(Km​ρKm†​),ρm​=pm​Km​ρKm†​​,∑m​Km†​Km​=I.
(3)
The conditional density operator ρm​ exists only when pm​>0. Its nonlinear normalization expresses conditioning; the unnormalized map ρ↦Km​ρKm†​ is linear. More generally, an unresolved outcome can contain several Kraus operators: replace the numerator by ∑a​Kma​ρKma†​ and take its trace for pm​. [ref].
Keeping the outcome gives a selective quantum measurement. Discarding it gives a nonselective quantum measurement with quantum channel ρ↦∑m​pm​ρm​. The latter describes the whole measured population without its labels, whereas each ρm​ describes a selected subpopulation. Neither description requires the measured quantity to have possessed its eventual value before the interaction.
For a concrete test, prepare the Pauli X eigenstate ∣+⟩=(∣0⟩+∣1⟩)/2​. Its X=+1 probability is one. An ideal Z projective measurement gives zero or one with equal probability, leaving ∣0⟩ or ∣1⟩ under the Lüders rule. Discarding the result leaves the maximally mixed state I/2. A subsequent X measurement now gives either sign with probability one half, even if the Z result was kept. This observable change in statistics is backaction. Measuring X on the original ∣+⟩ instead leaves it unchanged: disturbance depends on both the preparation and the instrument. Measurement-based quantum feedback uses the recorded outcome to choose a subsequent control operation, so both parts of the instrument matter.

Perfect discrimination of pure states requires orthogonality

Words: 65
Measurement in quantum mechanics OurBigBook.com
If a positive operator-valued measure distinguishes normalized pure states ∣u⟩,∣v⟩ with certainty, the effect E for the first answer satisfies ⟨u∣E∣u⟩=1 and ⟨v∣E∣v⟩=0. Positivity of E and I−E implies E∣v⟩=0 and E∣u⟩=∣u⟩, because a positive operator with zero expectation annihilates the vector. Consequently ⟨u∣v⟩=⟨u∣E∣v⟩=0. Conversely, orthogonal states can be measured with their rank-one linear projections. A common unitary operator preserves this inner product and cannot remove the obstruction.

Entanglement-assisted statistical singlet verification (Ω=Ps​+(I−Ps​)/3)

Choose a shared random axis uniformly from x,y,z, perform its entanglement-assisted nondemolition parity measurement with one Bell pair, and accept the anticorrelated parity. The spin singlet state always passes unchanged. The averaged acceptance operator is Ω=∑j​(I−σj​⊗σj​)/6=Ps​+(I−Ps​)/3, so an orthogonal triplet state passes with probability one third. Bell-pair cost of exact nondemolition singlet verification (R≥4)

Words: 82
Quantum nondemolition measurement OurBigBook.com
Exact singlet verification accepts the spin singlet state with certainty, rejects every orthogonal state, and preserves the singlet on acceptance. Each nonzero accepting Kraus operator is then a scalar multiple of the singlet projector, whose operator Schmidt rank is four. The local Kraus rank bound from an entangled resource excludes a single shared Bell pair, which has Schmidt rank two. Two shared Bell pairs suffice by a Bell-state nondemolition measurement followed by reporting whether its label is the singlet. This protocol may disturb triplet coherence. The rank bound concerns the resource Schmidt rank, and is not an entropy lower bound of two bits for arbitrary resource states.

Postselection conditions a measurement in quantum mechanics on a specified outcome. If its branch acts by a linear operator K on a normalized input ∣b⟩, its probability is ∥K∣b⟩∥2, and its normalized output is K∣b⟩/∥K∣b⟩∥. Positive operator-valued measure (POVM)

Words: 479 Articles: 6
Generalized measurement postulate OurBigBook.com
A positive operator-valued measure is a family of positive operators Ei​ satisfying ∑i​Ei​=I. Each Ei​ is called an effect and determines a probability pi​=Tr(Ei​ρ) for input density operator ρ. The effects need not be orthogonal projections or commute. This permits measurements with more outcomes than the dimension of the Hilbert space, such as the trine qubit POVM. Coupling to a quantum ancilla and measuring the larger system supplies a physical route to generalized measurements. [ref].
For an elementary unsharp Z example on a qubit, take
E±​=21​(I±ηZ),0≤η≤1.
(4)
On the eigenstate ∣0⟩, p±​=(1±η)/2; at η=1/2 they are 3/4 and 1/4. At η=0 the outcome is a fair coin independent of the input, and at η=1 these effects specify a sharp Z projective measurement. Choosing K±​=E±​​ specifies one quantum instrument, but U±​E±​​ for outcome-dependent unitary operators has the same effects and different state updates. Thus POVM does not determine the post-measurement state.

POVM–ensemble duality for the maximally mixed state (Ei​=dpi​ρi​)

A POVM {Ei​} in dimension d corresponds to a quantum state ensemble with pi​=Tr(Ei​)/d and ρi​=Ei​/Tr(Ei​), omitting zero effects. Its average is the maximally mixed state I/d. Conversely any ensemble with that average defines a POVM by Ei​=dpi​ρi​. These transformations are inverse. A pure POVM corresponds exactly to a pure-state ensemble decomposition of I/d.

Trine qubit POVM (Ej​=32​∣ϕj​⟩⟨ϕj​∣)

Words: 85
Positive operator-valued measure OurBigBook.com
Choose three pure qubit states whose unit Bloch vectors are coplanar, sum to zero and have pairwise angle 120 degrees. The three effects Ej​=32​∣ϕj​⟩⟨ϕj​∣ sum to the identity, giving a POVM. If an input is the state orthogonal to ϕ1​ or to ϕ2​, outcome one excludes the first input and outcome two excludes the second. Each conclusive outcome has probability one half on its corresponding input; the third outcome is inconclusive with probability one half on either input. This gives unambiguous quantum state discrimination, rather than identification on every trial.

Informationally complete POVM

A POVM is informationally complete if its outcome probabilities determine every input density operator uniquely. Its effects must span the real space of Hermitian operators. For a qubit, normalization leaves at most m−1 independent probabilities from m outcomes; an arbitrary Bloch vector has three real parameters, so one fixed informationally complete measurement requires at least four outcomes. Using several differently oriented binary measurements is a different experimental setting and can also perform quantum state tomography.

Projective measurement (Pi​Pj​=δij​Pi​,∑i​Pi​=I)

Words: 828 Articles: 10
Measurement in quantum mechanics OurBigBook.com
A projective measurement has self-adjoint orthogonal projections Pi​ satisfying Pi​Pj​=δij​Pi​ and ∑i​Pi​=I. Measuring an observable with discrete spectral decomposition A=∑i​ai​Pi​ reports eigenvalue ai​ with Born rule probability pi​=Tr(Pi​ρ). A projector can have rank greater than one: the outcome then identifies an eigenspace, rather than a particular eigenvector inside it. Projectors specify the outcome statistics; the usual ideal conditional update is the additional Lüders rule.
In the qubit example from measurement in quantum mechanics, Z=∣0⟩⟨0∣−∣1⟩⟨1∣. At θ=π/3, the zero and one probabilities are 3/4 and 1/4, regardless of the relative quantum phase ϕ. Under the Lüders rule, obtaining zero prepares ∣0⟩; obtaining one prepares ∣1⟩, up to global phase. Repeating the same measurement returns the same label with certainty, provided no intervening evolution or operation changes the measured sector. Repeatability concerns the conditional output, and does not mean the first outcome was predetermined.
Change to X and its Pauli X eigenstates ∣±⟩=(∣0⟩±∣1⟩)/2​. Adding the two probability amplitudes before squaring gives
p±​=∣⟨±∣ψ⟩∣2=21​​cos(θ/2)±eiϕsin(θ/2)​2=21±sinθcosϕ​.
(5)
At θ=π/3 and ϕ=0, p+​=(2+3​)/4≃0.9330 and p−​≃0.0670. At ϕ=π/2, both are 1/2; at ϕ=π, the first pair is exchanged. The Z statistics remain 3/4,1/4 throughout. This is the same addition-and-cancellation principle as wave interference: relative quantum phase changes how amplitudes reinforce or cancel in another basis.
The beauty of this basis dependence is that information invisible to one measurement becomes accessible to another. A Z measurement of an ensemble estimates its quantum state populations, while X probes one component of its quantum coherence. These are experiments on separate identically prepared copies. Sequentially measuring Z and then X on one copy instead probes the disturbed post-measurement state. Quantum state tomography combines suitable ensemble statistics to reconstruct the original state.

Lüders rule (ρi​=Pi​ρPi​/Tr(Pi​ρ))

Words: 415 Articles: 6
Projective measurement OurBigBook.com
The Lüders rule specifies the ideal quantum instrument for a projective measurement. Conditional on an outcome with nonzero probability, it gives
ρi​=Tr(Pi​ρ)Pi​ρPi​​,∣ψi​⟩=⟨ψ∣Pi​∣ψ⟩​Pi​∣ψ⟩​
(7)
for a density operator and a pure state, respectively. The second formula applies only to a pure state input. If the result is discarded, the output is the nonselective projective measurement ∑i​Pi​ρPi​.
The crucial feature for a degenerate eigenvalue is preservation of quantum coherence within its eigenspace. For a qutrit with orthonormal basis ∣0⟩,∣1⟩,∣2⟩, consider
A=0P0​+1P1​,P0​=∣0⟩⟨0∣+∣1⟩⟨1∣,P1​=∣2⟩⟨2∣.
(8)
On ∣χ⟩=(∣0⟩+∣1⟩)/2​, outcome zero has probability one and the Lüders rule leaves ∣χ⟩ unchanged. Measuring all three rank-one projectors and then forgetting whether the label was zero or one instead gives
ρ​=21​∣0⟩⟨0∣+21​∣1⟩⟨1∣.
(9)
Both procedures always report the coarse value zero, but a subsequent test with projector ∣χ⟩⟨χ∣ succeeds with probability one for the first and one half for the second. Resolving extra information destroyed coherence. This explains why a quantum nondemolition measurement of a degenerate quantity must specify what information the apparatus actually records.
Gerhart Lüders proposed this state-change rule in 1951, explicitly discussing degenerate quantities and distinguishing selected subensembles from the aggregate measured ensemble. Kirkpatrick's translation was first posted in 2004; equations (7) and (8) give the unnormalized selective update and the nonselective update. [ref].
Repeatability follows briefly from Pj​Pi​=δij​Pi​: after outcome i, Tr(Pj​ρi​)=δij​. This assumes a second application of the same ideal instrument without intervening dynamics. An instrument with the same effects can rotate or reset the output and need not satisfy this conclusion; see POVM does not determine the post-measurement state.

Quantum state tomography

Words: 407 Articles: 2
Measurement in quantum mechanics OurBigBook.com
Quantum state tomography estimates a density operator from measurement frequencies on many independently and identically prepared systems. One fixed projective measurement reveals only its basis populations, so further measurement settings are generally needed. For a qubit, write
ρ=21​(I+rx​X+ry​Y+rz​Z),rj​=Tr(ρσj​)=pj,+​−pj,−​.
(11)
The three Pauli matrices thus turn measured expectation values into the Bloch vector. This reconstruction uses the Bloch ball representation of arbitrary qubit density operators. [ref].
Divide the prepared ensemble into groups measured in the X, Y and Z bases. For example, positive-outcome frequencies 0.8,0.5,0.9 give the estimate r=(0.6,0,0.8) and
ρ=(0.90.3​0.30.1​).
(12)
Here ∣r∣=1, so the reconstructed state is the pure state (3∣0⟩+∣1⟩)/10​. Its Z frequencies alone also fit the diagonal mixed state with populations 0.9,0.1; the X data distinguish them. For the angular pure state in measurement in quantum mechanics, rx​=sinθcosϕ, ry​=sinθsinϕ and rz​=cosθ, so Y resolves the sign of the phase that X alone cannot.
Finite samples introduce statistical uncertainty: with N independent binary trials in one basis, the frequency has variance p(1−p)/N. At p=1/2 and N=100, its standard deviation is 0.05. Fluctuations can place a naive Bloch vector estimate outside the Bloch ball; a physical reconstruction must enforce positivity of the density operator. Repeated measurements on one unprepared specimen sample its changing conditional state, rather than independent copies of its original state. Multiple bases are needed in this projective protocol; a single fixed informationally complete POVM, such as the tetrahedral qubit POVM, is an alternative. It still requires an ensemble of trials.

Single-qubit process tomography (Rij​=21​Tr(σi​Vσj​V†))

Quantum state tomography OurBigBook.com
To identify the physical channel ρ↦VρV† of a single-qubit unitary gate, prepare each of the three positive Pauli matrix eigenstates and estimate each output Pauli expectation. Their nine means are
Rij​=21​Tr(σi​Vσj​V†),i,j∈{x,y,z}.
(13)
They determine the Bloch vector rotation r↦Rr, hence the channel on all states. If two gates have the same channel, their relative unitary commutes with all three Pauli matrices: commuting with Z forces it diagonal and commuting with X forces equal diagonal entries. Thus they differ by a scalar global phase, proving uniqueness up to that phase. This procedure needs no controlled gate or eigenstate of the unknown gate. Estimating its bounded measurement means to error ϵ with fixed confidence uses order ϵ−2 repetitions. It determines the relative eigenphases but cannot determine an overall eigenvalue phase from uncontrolled gate access alone.

POVM does not determine the post-measurement state

Words: 61
Quantum instrument OurBigBook.com
A POVM fixes outcome probabilities, but a quantum instrument additionally fixes the conditional output state. For computational-basis effects Ey​=∣y⟩⟨y∣, the operators My​=∣y⟩⟨y∣ leave the measured eigenstate as output, whereas Ny​=∣0⟩⟨y∣ reset every outcome to ∣0⟩. Both have My†​My​=Ny†​Ny​=Ey​. Thus a specification of effects alone cannot select a unique joint state that retains the measured quantum system.

Local quantum operation

Words: 81
Quantum instrument OurBigBook.com
A local quantum operation on one tensor-factor subsystem acts as ΦA​⊗idB​, with ΦA​ completely positive. An individual outcome branch can decrease trace, while the sum over outcomes of a normalized instrument preserves trace. The unselected operation consequently leaves the remote reduced state unchanged. In a local field algebra, Kraus operators inside one region and commuting with a distant observable give an analogous no-signalling statement. Locality of the operation must be established; an arbitrary globally defined projection is not automatically a physically local measurement.
