An observation can increase entropy

Before the report, X=0 is strongly favored: its information entropy is H(X)=h2​(1/10)≈0.468996 bits, where h2​ is binary entropy. A report Y=1 makes the two states equally likely, so H(X∣Y=1)=1 bit, strictly larger than before. This report occurs with probability 1/5. The more common report Y=0 determines X=0, giving H(X∣Y=0)=0. Therefore the averaged conditional entropy is
H(X∣Y)=54​⋅0+51​⋅1=51​<h2​(1/10)=H(X).

The resulting mutual information is I(X;Y)=h2​(1/10)−1/5≈0.268996 bits. The rare report increases uncertainty about the state; that increase does not make the report useless. Together the reports remove uncertainty on average. These are stipulated probabilities in an exact model, not measured frequencies.
