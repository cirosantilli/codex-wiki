Particle detection infers a particle's passage from a physical change in matter. A cloud chamber makes the spatial pattern of ionization visible; an ionization chamber instead collects electric charge from ionization. Choosing the readout determines what can be inferred from a single event.
The Richter-Ting independent J/psi discovery experiments show how different readouts can establish the same particle resonance: reconstruct daughter invariant masses after a beam hits a stationary target, or tune the initial center-of-mass energy of two colliding beams. Both require an ensemble of events and calibrated particle detectors.

Collision pileup

Words: 40
Particle detection OurBigBook.com
Additional collisions overlapping an event in the same beam crossing or detector response window. Extra tracks and calorimeter deposits affect particle-jet reconstruction, isolation and missing transverse momentum. Vertex association, subtraction and response calibration reduce the effect but leave uncertainties that depend on collision conditions.

Active detector veto

Words: 43
Particle detection OurBigBook.com
An additional active detector region or system whose correlated signal identifies events to reject from a signal sample. Its efficiency, time window, dead time and accidental coincidences must be measured. It can reject some backgrounds without proving that every unvetoed event is signal.

Signal region

Words: 39
Particle detection OurBigBook.com
A pre-specified region of reconstructed event space selected for sensitivity to a hypothesized signal. Observed events are compared with a background prediction constrained by particle-physics control regions and other measurements. A blind analysis can hide this region while choices and uncertainty estimates are finalized.

Particle-physics control region

Words: 115 Articles: 1
Particle detection OurBigBook.com
An event sample designed to constrain a background contribution to a signal region, while having little expected signal contamination. Changed particle-identification, kinematic or tagging requirements can isolate a process. The model must account for its relation to the signal sample, shared uncertainties and any signal leakage. A separate validation sample can test extrapolation without tuning the discovery selection.

Control-region transfer factor (bSR​=TbCR​)

Words: 58
Particle-physics control region OurBigBook.com
A ratio translating a background yield in a particle-physics control region into its yield in a signal region. It may be estimated with simulation and calibration measurements. Its uncertainty must include acceptance, response and physics differences between the regions; contaminating backgrounds are subtracted or fitted jointly. A profile likelihood can constrain the control and signal counts together rather than treating the transferred estimate as exact.

Detector acceptance

Words: 44
Particle detection OurBigBook.com
The fraction of produced events falling within a detector's geometrical and kinematic selection. Reconstruction and trigger efficiencies are additional factors; conventions must state whether they are folded into the quoted acceptance. A changed decay spectrum can alter acceptance even at the same parent mass.

Displaced decay vertex

Words: 38
Particle detection OurBigBook.com
A reconstructed decay position measurably separated from the primary collision point. Its distribution constrains a particle's flight distance and lifetime, after correcting for boost, geometry, resolution and selection. It is one possible signature of a long-lived new particle.

Fiducial volume

Words: 31
Particle detection OurBigBook.com
A selected inner detector volume in which the response and background rejection meet specified criteria. Position cuts can suppress surface backgrounds, but the selected target mass and position-reconstruction uncertainty must be calibrated.

Electron recoil

Words: 35
Particle detection OurBigBook.com
An energy deposition in a target's electronic degrees of freedom, as opposed to a nuclear recoil. gamma rays and beta decay can produce backgrounds in this channel. Detector discrimination is statistical because the measured light and charge distributions have finite widths.

Time projection chamber (TPC)

Words: 175 Articles: 5
Particle detection OurBigBook.com
A detector that reconstructs one coordinate from the time required for ionization electrons to drift in an electric field, with the other coordinates measured by the charge or light pattern. A two-phase xenon implementation obtains depth from the delay between prompt particle scintillation and gas-phase charge readout.

Dual-phase xenon detector

Words: 133 Articles: 4
Time projection chamber OurBigBook.com
A liquid target with a gaseous charge-readout layer. An interaction produces prompt particle scintillation, conventionally S1, and ionization electrons that drift to the liquid surface. Extracted electrons produce gas electroluminescence, conventionally S2. Timing and light-pattern information give three-dimensional position; the S2/S1 distribution helps distinguish electron recoils from nuclear recoils. It does not remove neutron-induced single recoils, and the discrimination requires calibration.

Fiducial self-shielding in a xenon detector

Words: 41
Dual-phase xenon detector OurBigBook.com
Selecting an inner fiducial volume reduces externally produced radiation because outer xenon attenuates it before it reaches the selected target. This trades target mass for reduced background. It does not remove uniformly distributed internal radioactivity or every neutron recoil, so purification, veto information and calibration remain important.

Decay vertex

Particle identification

Words: 58
Particle detection OurBigBook.com
Particle identification assigns a species hypothesis to a detector signal or reconstructed track. Curvature in a magnetic field supplies magnetic rigidity; additional responses, such as those of a Cherenkov detector or a particle shower, help distinguish an electron from a hadron. The identity hypothesis enters the energy–momentum relation and therefore the reconstructed invariant mass. An apparatus can observe a peak before it can identify every decay channel, as the SPEAR psi energy-scan experiment illustrates.

The mean energy lost by a charged particle per unit path length through a material, with the particle species, material and energy specified. It is distinct from the particle range obtained by following the particle until it stops.

Inverse-energy stopping-power model (S=k/E,R=E02​/(2k))

Electronic stopping (Se​=−(dE/dx)e​)

Stopping power OurBigBook.com
Energy loss of a moving particle to electronic excitation and ionization. It is distinct from direct momentum transfer to target nuclei; electronic energy deposition can also couple to atomic motion in a material-dependent way.

High-speed charge and isotope scaling of electronic stopping (δE∝Z2/v2≃Z2M/(2E))

Words: 135
Stopping power OurBigBook.com
For a fast ion traversing a fixed material thickness with only a small fractional energy loss, the dipole excitation probability per atomic encounter scales as Z2/v2 at fixed impact parameter. Summing transferred excitation energies and integrating the encounter measure 2πbdb gives the leading electronic stopping power scaling, with slower logarithmic dependence from the allowed excitation energies and impact-parameter range.
At fixed ion kinetic energy E=Mv2/2, a heavier isotope is slower, so the leading loss scales as Z2M/E. At fixed velocity, isotopes of the same element instead have approximately the same electronic loss; plotting against energy per nucleon removes the leading isotope mass dependence. The charge should be the effective projectile charge if its bound electrons screen the nucleus. At lower speeds, excitation thresholds, slow-passage suppression, charge exchange and nuclear energy transfer invalidate a simple extrapolation of the high-speed law.

A multiwire proportional chamber is a proportional counter: parallel anode wires between cathodes in a gas. Charged particle ionization frees electrons; an electric field drives them toward wires, triggering an electron avalanche read by electronic amplifiers.[ref]

Multiwire proportional chamber experiment (1968)

Words: 99
Multiwire proportional chamber OurBigBook.com
The original apparatus used 40μm stainless-steel wires between meshes 7.5mm away, flowing argon with organic vapor, negative mesh voltage and individual electronic amplifiers. A 5.9keV X-ray source calibrated pulse height while voltage was varied.[ref]
A coincidence circuit defined a narrow proton or pion beam. Scanning it across wires tested localization; the response edge matched the beam width, limiting the inference. Distant counts were attributed to accidental coincidences. Separate electron scans measured pulse delay versus position, comparing gas mixtures and pulse-height-dependent electronics.[ref]
Thresholds rejected small pulses, reducing detection efficiency. Wire-independence tests did not establish unlimited separation of overlapping tracks.[ref]

Wire-address position model (σx​=p/12​)

Words: 281
Multiwire proportional chamber OurBigBook.com
Consider an original idealized track reconstruction model. Let equally spaced wires have positions xk​=kp and assign each event to the nearest wire, reporting x=xk​. Assume exactly one correct wire address, perfect detection efficiency, exact wire positions and a uniform distribution of true positions within each cell. These assumptions explicitly exclude missed pulses, false addresses and several unresolved tracks.
Writing u=x−xk​, the measurement error is e=x−x=−u, with u uniform on [−p/2,p/2]. Symmetry gives zero expected value. Its variance and standard deviation follow by integration:
E[e]=0,σx2​=E[e2]=p1​∫−p/2p/2​u2du=12p2​,σx​=12​p​.
(1)
For chosen wire pitches p=2mm and 1mm this gives 0.577mm and 0.289mm respectively. These are model standard deviations, not measured chamber spatial resolutions. The largest possible absolute error in this model is p/2, which is a different quantity.
A useful subtlety is that the formula depends on the illumination. For an unresolved pencil beam fixed at x=xk​+0.4p, every reconstructed position is xk​. The repeated estimates have zero variance, but every error is −0.4p. Small repeat-to-repeat spread can coexist with a biased position. The uniform-cell result therefore cannot be transferred to an arbitrary beam profile or detector selection.
The wire address supplies one transverse coordinate in this model. Combining several separated measurements supports track reconstruction; a single address does not specify a complete trajectory. If two charged particles enter the same cell during one accepted interval, an address-only record can be identical to that from one particle. No reconstruction of that record can recover the lost multiplicity without additional information.

Calibrated two-wire charge centroid (x=pqR​/(qL​+qR​))

Words: 354
Multiwire proportional chamber OurBigBook.com
Can two readout values locate a hit between wires? Only with a specified response model. Place two readout wires at 0 and p, put u=x/p∈[0,1], and stipulate calibrated, noiseless electric charges
qL​=Q(1−u),qR​=Qu,Q>0.
(2)
These are assumptions for a toy sensor response, not the natural response law of adjacent anode wires. Under these assumptions the centroid
x=qL​+qR​0qL​+pqR​​=pu=x
(3)
recovers position exactly. The apparent gain in precision comes from adding calibrated analogue information. Taking the mean of two fired wire addresses without their electric charges would instead give the fixed midpoint p/2.
For a chosen noise model, replace the two electric charges by qL​+nL​ and qR​+nR​, with independent random variables nL​,nR​ of zero expected value and common standard deviation s. Assume s/Q≪1 and no threshold censoring. First-order expansion of the ratio gives
δx≃Qp​[(1−u)nR​−unL​],σlin​(u)=Qps​(1−u)2+u2​.
(4)
For stipulated p=2mm and s/Q=0.02, this approximation gives 0.0283mm at the midpoint and 0.0400mm at either endpoint. The endpoint values describe uncensored signed channel readouts, including small negative values after baseline subtraction. They do not assume that both channels would pass a positive threshold. A ratio with a fluctuating denominator also need not have exactly zero bias; this is a local small-noise approximation.
Three original counterexamples show why calibration and selection matter:
  • If the right channel retains a relative gain g=1.10, then x/p=gu/(1−u+gu). At u=1/2, the bias is p/42=0.0476mm for the chosen pitch, larger than the midpoint noise estimate above.
  • Suppose symmetric electronic leakage transforms the normalized right signal to v=ϵ+(1−2ϵ)u. Treating v as u gives a position bias pϵ(1−2u). A known ϵ=1/2 permits inversion; ϵ=1/2 destroys position information entirely. This is a stipulated crosstalk model, not a measurement.
  • Requiring both noiseless channels to exceed 0.1Q accepts only 0.1<u<0.9: 80% of uniformly distributed hits. Computing a position spread after that cut describes a selected sample. It does not establish improved spatial resolution for all incident hits.
Likewise, two equal hits at u=1/4 and u=3/4 have exactly the same total two-channel response as one hit at u=1/2 with twice the electric charge. A centroid can hide multiplicity even when each charge measurement is exact.

Lederman-Schwartz-Steinberger two-neutrino beam experiment (1962)

Words: 703 Articles: 1
Particle detector OurBigBook.com
In 1962, Leon Max Lederman, Melvin Schwartz, Jack Steinberger and collaborators distinguished muon neutrinos from electron neutrinos using a neutrino beam.[ref]
The Alternating Gradient Synchrotron delivered 15GeV protons onto beryllium. Particle decay of pions and kaons supplied neutrinos; a 13.5m iron shield preceded a ten-ton aluminium spark chamber.[ref]
Production and detection are separate. In modern neutrino flavour notation,
π+⟶μ++νμ​.
(5)
Shielding suppresses the daughter muon; a newly produced muon inside the spark chamber's aluminium marks an interaction.[ref]
Beam-timed coincidence circuits and veto counters rejected entering tracks. Machine-off runs measured cosmic ray contamination; shield-thickness changes tested neutrons. Placing lead near the target shortened the meson decay path and reduced events. Cosmotron electron calibration established particle shower recognition.[ref]
The paper compared 34 single-muon candidates, including 5 estimated cosmic ray events, with six shower candidates unlike calibrated electron showers. Kaon decays could supply genuine electron neutrinos. The deficit favored distinct neutrino flavours under lepton universality.[ref]
The inference concerns production and detection channels. It does not measure neutrino mass or establish permanently fixed neutrino flavour. Later neutrino oscillation experiments address changes during propagation.[ref]

Neutrino event classification model (K∼Bin(N,q))

Words: 508
Lederman-Schwartz-Steinberger two-neutrino beam experiment OurBigBook.com
How can the relative abundance of tracks and showers distinguish hypotheses? Consider an original teaching model, with chosen inputs throughout. Condition on a sample of N selected events, each assigned exactly one of two labels: track or shower. Multitrack events and unclassified events lie outside this model. A shower label need not identify an electron; background can imitate it. Conversely, a true electron interaction need not receive a shower label.
Let b be the background fraction among selected events. Among the selected signal events, let f be the fraction whose associated charged lepton is an electron, with the remainder associated with a muon. These are fractions after event selection, rather than fractions of the incident neutrino particle flux. Define the conditional probabilities
a=Pr(shower∣e,signal),r=Pr(shower∣μ,signal),qb​=Pr(shower∣background).
(6)
Here a and r describe classification within the selected sample. They are not measured historical detection efficiencies. The law of total probability gives the overall shower probability
q(f,b)=(1−b)[af+r(1−f)]+bqb​.
(7)
This formula is the bridge between an interaction hypothesis and a visible label. It keeps beam contamination, background and mistaken classification conceptually distinct: genuine electron neutrino interactions belong in f, while non-signal events belong in b.
Assume independent random variables for the event labels, all with this same q. If K counts shower labels, every specified pattern with k showers has probability qk(1−q)N−k. Counting those patterns with a binomial coefficient yields the binomial distribution
Pr(K=k)=(kN​)qk(1−q)N−k,E[K]=Nq,Var(K)=Nq(1−q).
(8)
The track count is N−K, so its expected value is N(1−q). At fixed N, the two counts cannot fluctuate independently. The variance above measures sampling fluctuations under the stipulated model, not uncertainty in a, r, b or qb​.
Choose N=40, a=0.90, r=0.05, b=0.10 and qb​=0.40. Compare a signal with only muon-associated interactions (f=0) against one with equal selected electron- and muon-associated interactions (f=1/2). These give q=0.085 and q=0.4675, respectively. Their expected shower counts are 3.4 and 18.7; their expected track counts are 36.6 and 21.3. A background-only model with the specified qb​ gives 16 showers and 24 tracks on average. These are expectations, so they need not be integers.
For a hypothetical observation K=4, the likelihood ratio of the two signal models is
L(f=1/2)L(f=0)​=(0.46750.085​)4(0.53250.915​)36≃3.18×105.
(9)
The binomial coefficient cancels because the same hypothetical sample is used in both likelihoods. This ratio is not the probability that either hypothesis is true. A separate original check gives Pr(K≤5)≃0.880, 4.57×10−6 and 1.44×10−4 for the muon-associated, equal-mixture and specified background-only models, respectively.
Why are the controls essential? If the background-only model may choose any qb​, taking qb​=K/N can fit the observed shower fraction. Counts alone then fail to distinguish it from a signal model. Independent background measurements and calibrated particle identification constrain that freedom. Changing the decay path additionally tests where the candidate-producing particles originate. The power of the experiment comes from combining apparatus interventions with event classification; the small binomial distribution model isolates only the latter part of that reasoning.

Super-Kamiokande atmospheric-neutrino experiment (1998)
Words: 467 Articles: 1
Super-Kamiokande OurBigBook.com
Super-Kamiokande used 535 days (33.0 kiloton-years) and 22.5 fiducial kilotons of water. Inner photomultiplier times and charges reconstructed interactions; the outer anticoincidence detector vetoed entering particles and marked exits. Contained single-ring events used likelihood classification: electron particle showers blur rings; muons give sharper edges. The 1.33GeV visible-energy sample division is not a universal detection threshold. Simulation estimated classification efficiency above about 98%, distinct from total detection efficiency; stopping muons and decay electrons checked classification.[ref]
Downward neutrinos travel about 15km; upward ones about 13000km. The multi-GeV muon-like up/down asymmetry was −0.296±0.048±0.010; electron-like events lacked a comparable effect. Calibration constrained directional bias. This was disappearance evidence; tau neutrino appearance was untagged and sterile conversion remained possible.[ref]
Writing U and D for the selected upward and downward populations, the asymmetry is A=(U−D)/(U+D): a negative value means fewer upward events. An absolute source-normalization error alone rescales both populations and leaves this ratio unchanged. A direction-dependent response error need not cancel.
Two-flavour vacuum survival model (Pμμ​)
Words: 303

Wrong combinations create background, and several combinations from one event are statistically correlated.

A simple isolated particle resonance model starts with a survival amplitude proportional to
a(t)=e−iE0​t/ℏe−Γt/(2ℏ),t≥0.
(48)
Its survival probability is ∣a(t)∣2=e−Γt/ℏ, giving mean rest-frame lifetime τ=ℏ/Γ. The Fourier transform of this one-sided amplitude is proportional to (Γ/2−i(E−E0​))−1; its squared modulus gives a Lorentzian line shape. Write its normalized density as

Gσ​(x−E)=2π​σe−(x−E)2/(2σ2)​,O(x)=∫−∞∞​L(E)Gσ​(x−E)dE.

For a pair spectrometer the response summarizes invariant-mass resolution; for an idealized collider scan it summarizes incoming center-of-mass energy spread. These are different experimental sources of uncertainty even when the same mathematical response is useful.
Choose an arbitrary scale E∗​, intrinsic decay width Γ=0.20E∗​ and response standard deviations 0.40E∗​ and 1.20E∗​.
