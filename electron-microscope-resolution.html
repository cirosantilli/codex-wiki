Electron microscope resolution

 Home Physics Branches of physics Optics Electron optics Electron microscope
OurBigBook.com Words: 386
Electron microscope resolution concerns distinguishable specimen detail under a specified contrast criterion. It depends on diffraction, lens defects, electron energy spread, specimen motion and detection. A smaller de Broglie wavelength permits finer detail in principle, but neither wavelength nor magnification is the instrument's measured resolution.
For an electron accelerated from negligible initial kinetic energy through a positive electric potential difference U, combine K=eU, the relativistic energy-momentum relation and the de Broglie wavelength:
p2c2=K(K+2me​c2),λ(U)=2me​eU(1+2me​c2eU​)​h​.
(9)
Here h is the Planck constant, me​ the electron mass, e the elementary charge, and c the speed of light. This includes the relativistic correction omitted by the low-voltage electron accelerating-voltage wavelength approximation. With me​≃9.1093837×10−31kg and SI defining values of h,e,c, calculated wavelengths are approximately 6.979, 3.701 and 1.969pm at 30, 100 and 300kV respectively. The rounded mass agrees with the recommended value.[ref]
For a circular aperture admitting a small semi-angle α, the Rayleigh criterion suggests a diffraction-limited resolution scale dD​=0.61λ/α. At 100kV and α=10mrad, this gives 0.226nm, already much larger than the 0.00370nm wavelength. This is an ideal aperture estimate. Transmission electron microscopy with coherent illumination and a particular specimen requires its own image-contrast analysis; this scale alone is not a universal two-feature resolution test.
An original illustrative blur model adds competing effects:
b(α)2=(α0.61λ​)2+(Cs​α3)2+(Cc​αKΔK​)2+b02​.
(10)
Define Cs​α3 as a characteristic transverse blur from the electron-lens analogue of spherical aberration, and Cc​αΔK/K as one from energy-dependent focusing, the analogue of chromatic aberration. The coefficients Cs​,Cc​ have units of length, ΔK is a chosen characteristic kinetic energy spread, and b0​ is a fixed residual blur. Adding their squares is a declared heuristic, not a measured point spread function or a rigorous resolution theorem. The model omits contrast transfer, specimen scattering and signal statistics, so its minimum is only an estimate within its assumptions.
Choose Cs​=Cc​=1mm, ΔK=0.7eV and b0​=0.10nm at K=100keV. At 5mrad, the four contributions are about 0.452, 0.125, 0.035 and 0.100nm, giving b≃0.480nm. At 10mrad, the diffraction contribution falls to 0.226nm but the assumed spherical aberration contribution grows to 1.00nm. Thus opening the aperture indefinitely worsens this model's image. All these values are calculations with chosen parameters, not observations of any microscope.
