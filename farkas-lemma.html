Farkas' lemma (Az=b, z≥0orATy≥0, bTy<0)

 Home Mathematics Area of mathematics Mathematical optimization Convex optimization Conic optimization
OurBigBook.com Words: 1k Articles: 3
For a real matrix A∈Rm×n and a vector b∈Rm, exactly one of the following systems has a solution:
​Az=b,z∈Rn,z≥0;ATy≥0,y∈Rm,bTy<0.​
(11)
The linear inequalities here are componentwise. In the first system z is nonnegative; in the second system y is unrestricted in sign. The second system is a certificate that the first has no feasible point. This nonnegative-equations alternative is developed in Boyd and Vandenberghe, Convex Optimization slides, §5.34.
Build the target, or separate it. Write the columns of A as a1​,…,an​. Then Az=∑j​zj​aj​ is a conic combination: its coefficients must be nonnegative, but need not sum to one. The obtainable targets form the finitely generated cone
K={Az:z≥0}=cone{a1​,…,an​}.
(12)
A solution z explains how to build b from those columns. A certificate y instead defines a linear functional v↦yTv. The condition ATy≥0 says that its dot product with every generating column is nonnegative, and hence with every point of K. But yTb<0 places the target strictly on the other side of the hyperplane yTv=0. Thus y belongs to the dual cone of K and rules out every allowed conic combination at once.
A certificate that can be checked by eye. Take the identity matrix
A=I2​=(10​01​),b=(−11​).
(13)
Here K is the nonnegative orthant. The equation Az=b forces z=(−1,1)T, violating z≥0. The certificate y=(1,0)T gives
ATy=(10​)≥0,bTy=−1<0.
(14)
The linear functional simply reads the first coordinate: all allowed column combinations have a nonnegative first coordinate, while b has first coordinate −1. For comparison, the target (1,1)T is obtained with z=(1,1)T.
Why this is useful and striking. A successful search can exhibit one feasible point; an unsuccessful search alone cannot explain why every candidate fails. Farkas' lemma guarantees an equally concrete witness of impossibility for this finite linear problem. Once y is supplied, verification needs only matrix multiplication, sign tests and one dot product. The same idea gives a Farkas certificate for linear inequalities and underlies linear programming duality.
Brief geometric proof. The two systems cannot both hold, because they would imply
0>bTy=(Az)Ty=zT(ATy)≥0.
(15)
For existence of a certificate when b∈/K, closedness of finitely generated cones makes K a closed convex cone, so separation from a closed convex cone supplies yTb<0 and yTv≥0 for every v∈K. Testing each column gives ATy≥0. Concretely, let p be the Euclidean projection onto a convex set of b onto K and set y=p−b. The projection inequality gives yT(v−p)≥0 for v∈K. Testing v=0 and v=2p gives yTp=0, whence yTb=−∥p−b∥22​<0. In the example, p=(0,1)T and y=(1,0)T. Alternatively, the Fenchel-Moreau theorem applied to the cone's indicator functional proves the same separation through its polar cone.
