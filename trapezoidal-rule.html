Trapezoidal rule (T(f)=(b−a)(f(a)+f(b))/2)

 ... Area of mathematics Analysis Numerical analysis One-step method Runge-Kutta method Implicit Runge-Kutta method
OurBigBook.com Words: 740 Articles: 6
As a quadrature rule, the trapezoidal rule replaces an integrand by the linear interpolation joining its two endpoint values:
I(f)=∫ab​f(x)dx≈T(f)=2b−a​(f(a)+f(b)),a<b.
(15)
For nonnegative endpoint values, this is the area of a trapezoid; in general it is a signed area. The quadrature nodes are a,b, each with quadrature weight (b−a)/2. Unlike the midpoint quadrature rule, it uses endpoint samples, which can be reused between adjacent panels.
Every affine function f(x)=αx+β agrees with its linear interpolation, so the trapezoidal rule integrates it exactly. On [0,2], f(x)=2x+1 gives T(f)=(2/2)(1+5)=6=I(f). On [0,1], the polynomial f(x)=x2 instead gives T(f)=1/2, exceeding I(f)=1/3 by 1/6. Its degree of precision of a quadrature rule is exactly one.
If f∈C2[a,b], its signed approximation error satisfies
T(f)−I(f)=12(b−a)3​f′′(ξT​),a<ξT​<b.
(16)
A convex function lies below its endpoint chord, so the trapezoidal rule overestimates its integral; a concave function gives the reverse inequality. The error formula assumes that the second derivative exists and is a continuous function throughout the closed interval.[ref] A short derivation is the identity
T(f)−I(f)=21​∫ab​(t−a)(b−t)f′′(t)dt,
(17)
obtained by two integrations by parts. The nonnegative Peano kernel has integral (b−a)3/12, and the weighted mean value theorem for integrals gives the formula.
The trapezoidal rule for an ordinary differential equation averages vector fields at the old and new states and is A-stable.
This time-stepping use applies the same trapezoidal rule to the integral of the derivative in an ordinary differential equation. That connection explains its place under implicit Runge-Kutta methods; its quadrature behavior and the nonlinear issue in trapezoidal rule fails B-stability concern different uses of the same endpoint averaging.
