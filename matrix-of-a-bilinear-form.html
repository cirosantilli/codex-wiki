Matrix of a bilinear form (Aij​=B(ei​,ej​),B(v,w)=vTAw)

 Home Mathematics Area of mathematics Algebra Linear algebra Bilinear form
OurBigBook.com Words: 173
Choose an ordered basis (e1​,…,en​) of a finite-dimensional vector space. The matrix of a bilinear form records all pairings of basis vectors, with Aij​=B(ei​,ej​). Writing v=∑i​vi​ei​ and w=∑j​wj​ej​, separate linearity gives
B(v,w)=∑i,j​vi​wj​B(ei​,ej​)=∑i,j​vi​Aij​wj​=vTAw,
(2)
where the last expression uses the coordinate vectors of v,w. Conversely, every matrix A defines a bilinear form by this formula.[ref]
For a concrete bilinear form on R2, take
A=(10​21​),B(v,w)=v1​w1​+2v1​w2​+v2​w2​.
(3)
Then B((1,0),(0,1))=2 but B((0,1),(1,0))=0: a bilinear form need not be a symmetric bilinear form. For v=(2,1)T and w=(−1,3)T, first compute Aw=(5,3)T, then vTAw=2⋅5+1⋅3=13. Reversing the inputs gives Av=(4,1)T and wTAv=−4+3=−1.
Under change of basis, let the columns of an invertible matrix P be the new basis vectors in old coordinates. Thus v=Pv′ and w=Pw′, so
B(v,w)=(Pv′)TA(Pw′)=(v′)T(PTAP)w′,A′=PTAP.
(4)
This is matrix congruence, rather than the matrix similarity P−1AP for an endomorphism. Both inputs have changed coordinates, which explains the two factors of P. For the displayed A and P=(10​11​), direct multiplication gives A′=(11​34​); its (1,2) entry is B(e1​,e1​+e2​)=3.[ref]
