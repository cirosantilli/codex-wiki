Put a=p(x)q(x,y) and b=p(y)q(y,x). For a+b>0, the off-diagonal probability flux of the proposed rule is
p(x)q(x,y)β(x,y)=aa+bb​=a+bab​=ba+ba​=p(y)q(y,x)β(y,x).
(4)
If a=b=0, define both acceptance probabilities to be zero; both fluxes are zero. The diagonal detailed balance equations hold automatically, including the probability of rejected proposals. Summing the detailed balance equations over x gives stationarity:
∑x​p(x)P(x,y)=∑x​p(y)P(y,x)=p(y).
(5)
The alternative rule therefore preserves p. The unknown normalizing constant cancels after replacing p by r. This Barker acceptance probability is no larger than the Metropolis–Hastings acceptance probability for the same proposed move: if t=b/a>0, then t/(1+t)≤min(1,t). Preservation of p still requires separate Markov chain irreducibility and convergence checks before it guarantees useful sampling.
