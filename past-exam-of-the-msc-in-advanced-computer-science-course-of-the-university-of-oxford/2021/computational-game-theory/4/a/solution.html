If the core is nonempty, it is exactly the singleton allocation pj​=v({j}) for every j. Take any p in the core of a cooperative game and write p(C)=∑j∈C​pj​. Core membership gives efficiency of the imputation, p(N)=v(N), and the coalition inequalities
p(C)≥v(C),p(N∖C)≥v(N∖C).
(17)
Using the given complementary-coalition equality in this constant-sum cooperative game, the second inequality yields
p(C)=v(N)−p(N∖C)≤v(N)−v(N∖C)=v(C).
(18)
Combining upper and lower bounds gives p(C)=v(C) for every coalition C. In particular,
pj​=p({j})=v({j})for every j∈N.​
(19)
Since this holds for any core member and at least one exists by hypothesis, the displayed allocation is in the core and is its only member. The argument also shows why nonemptiness is restrictive: it forces v(C)=∑j∈C​v({j}) for every coalition, so the characteristic function of a coalitional game must be additive. No superadditive coalitional game or monotonicity assumption is needed.
