ℓ2​≻ℓ1​. The distinction is small but strict: their normalized expected utilities are 18/25 and 7/10, respectively.
By the Von Neumann-Morgenstern utility theorem, use a representing utility function and apply a positive affine function to set u(ω1​)=1 and u(ω4​)=0. This is possible because the given strict preference implies u(ω1​)>u(ω4​). The two specified indifference relations then give
u(ω3​)=53​,u(ω2​)=43​+41​53​=109​.
(1)
Taking the expected utility of each lottery yields
U(ℓ1​)=52​+51​109​+51​53​=107​,U(ℓ2​)=52​109​+53​53​=2518​.
(2)
Their difference is 1/50>0, establishing the stated strict preference. These numbers measure utility, not monetary reward or an assumed ordering of the subscripts.
Here is the underlying argument directly in terms of the axioms. The independence axiom for lottery preferences permits substitution of indifferent lotteries inside a common mixture. Replacing ω3​ by its given mixture of ω1​,ω4​, and then replacing ω2​, followed by reduction of compound lotteries, gives
ω2​∼109​ω1​+101​ω4​,
(3)
and consequently
ℓ1​∼107​ω1​+103​ω4​,ℓ2​∼2518​ω1​+257​ω4​.
(4)
The transitive relation axiom combines the successive substitutions. Finally, mixture monotonicity says that among mixtures of a strictly better and a strictly worse outcome, the greater probability of the better outcome is strictly preferred; this follows from the independence axiom for lottery preferences and transitivity. Since 18/25>7/10 and ω1​≻ω4​, the comparison follows. Complete lottery preferences and continuity of lottery preferences are part of the representation theorem's hypotheses; the direct substitution calculation uses the already supplied indifferences and does not need to invoke continuity of lottery preferences again.[ref]
