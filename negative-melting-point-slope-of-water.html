Negative melting-point slope of water

For a local illustrative ice–water melting calculation, stipulate Tm​=273.15K, latent heat of fusion L=334kJkg−1, solid mass density ρs​=917kgm−3, and liquid mass density ρl​=1000kgm−3. These are explicitly stipulated rounded inputs, not measurements made here. The specific volume change on melting is
Δv=ρl​1​−ρs​1​=−9.0513×10−5m3kg−1.
(25)
The common-pressure melting-point slope therefore gives
dpdTm​​=LTm​Δv​=−7.4022×10−8KPa−1≃−0.07402KMPa−1.
(26)
A small 1MPa increase in the common pressure thus lowers the melting temperature by about 0.074K in this local model. Positive latent heat does not force a positive phase coexistence curve slope: ice contracts on conversion to liquid water. Increasing pressure favors the smaller specific volume. This calculation compares equilibrium states of the same two thermodynamic phases; extending its tangent to large pressures would require updated mass densities, latent heat of fusion, and the relevant ice phase diagram.
