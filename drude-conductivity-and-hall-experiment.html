Drude conductivity and Hall experiment

OurBigBook.com Words: 443
A motivating laboratory realization is The Hall Effect and Conductivity in Germanium by Rex Tayloe. The handout specifies a germanium sample holder with temperature and magnetic field control, a low-voltage supply, digital meters, and a current-reversing switch. Measure the sample dimensions, map the contacts, and establish the directions of electric current, magnetic field, and transverse voltage. At fixed temperature and zero magnetic field, sweep positive and negative electric currents and record longitudinal electric potential differences. A straight voltage-versus-electric current relation tests Ohm's law; sample geometry converts its slope to electrical resistivity. For the Hall effect, minimize the zero-field transverse offset and measure transverse voltage at all four combinations of ±I and ±B, repeating for several electric currents. The readouts test longitudinal proportionality, transverse polarity, and transverse proportionality to electric current. These are protocol instructions, not reported measurement outcomes.[ref]
For interpreting such measurements, an ideal uniform bar of wire cross-sectional area A with longitudinal sensing contacts separated by L gives σ=LI/(AVL​). Four-terminal sensing suppresses voltage drops in current contacts when the voltage meter draws negligible electric current; it does not fix uncertain dimensions or a nonuniform current density. Reverse electric current and use [VL​(+I)−VL​(−I)]/2 to reject a constant voltage offset. Keep temperature stable, allow the current density to settle, and reduce electric current to test for Joule heating. Those are controls for interpreting the material coefficient, rather than reasons to assume every measured curve is linear.
For the transverse measurement define J=Jx​x, B=Bz​z, width w along y, and thickness d along z. Define VH​=V(y=−w/2)−V(y=+w/2)=Ey​w, so Jx​=I/(wd) gives
RH​=Jx​Bz​Ey​​=IBz​dVH​​.
(88)
Extract the part odd in both electric current and magnetic field using
VHodd​=4V(+I,+B)−V(−I,+B)−V(+I,−B)+V(−I,−B)​.
(89)
This combination rejects a constant offset and longitudinal pickup even in magnetic field. It cannot reject another signal with the same two reversal symmetries. Check field polarity and magnitude independently; a sign error in the contact map changes the inferred Hall coefficient.
For one Drude model species in transverse open circuit, the steady Lorentz force balance is 0=q(E+vd​×B)−mvd​/τ with vd,y​=0. Its y component gives Ey​=vd,x​Bz​. Consequently
RH​=nq1​,RH​=−ne1​for electrons,n=e∣RH​∣1​,μ=σ∣RH​∣=meτ​,τ=ne2mσ​.
(90)
Longitudinal electrical conductivity and the Hall coefficient therefore separate number density from charge carrier mobility in this single-species model. Inferring a momentum relaxation time additionally requires an appropriate mass. A constant-field longitudinal measurement alone cannot supply all three.
A Hall coefficient is not universally the reciprocal of signed number density. For example, adding independent Electron and electron hole contributions with densities n,p and positive charge carrier mobilities μe​,μh​ in the weak-magnetic field limit gives
σ=e(nμe​+pμh​),RH​=e(nμe​+pμh​)2pμh2​−nμe2​​.
(91)
The Hall coefficient can vanish even while electrical conductivity is positive, and its sign weights charge carrier mobility as well as number density. The germanium handout explicitly asks students to track possible transverse sign changes with temperature; it does not establish a universal single-carrier interpretation. Real electronic band structure and unequal scattering also limit that interpretation.[ref]
