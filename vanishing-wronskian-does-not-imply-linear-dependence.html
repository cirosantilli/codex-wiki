Vanishing Wronskian does not imply linear dependence

 ... Analysis Differential equation Ordinary differential equation Linear ordinary differential equation Second-order linear differential equation Wronskian
OurBigBook.com Words: 94
Two differentiable functions can be linearly independent on a connected interval while their Wronskian vanishes everywhere. For example, let f(x)=(max{−x,0})2 and g(x)=(max{x,0})2. Both have a continuous first derivative, and their values and first derivatives vanish at zero. On either side of zero one of the functions is identically zero, so fg′−f′g=0 everywhere. Evaluating a constant linear combination Af+Bg=0 at −1 and 1 forces A=B=0.
The obstruction is that the two halves supply independent information without simultaneous nonzero value-and-derivative data at any point. For solutions of a common regular second-order linear differential equation, the uniqueness theorem for ordinary differential equations removes this possibility; Abel's identity gives the zero-or-nowhere-zero alternative for their Wronskian.
