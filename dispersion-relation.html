Dispersion relation (ω(k))

 Home Mathematics Area of mathematics Analysis Partial differential equation Wave equation
OurBigBook.com Words: 6k Articles: 69
A dispersion relation specifies which angular frequencies and wavevectors a linear wave model admits. Fix the plane wave convention
u(x,t)=Re{aei(k⋅x−ωt)},D(ω,k)=0.
(1)
Here a is a constant complex amplitude, k is the wavevector, and ω is the angular frequency. Substitution into a constant-coefficient linear partial differential equation replaces ∂t​ by −iω and ∇ by ik, turning the wave problem into an algebraic condition. A system can have several roots; ω=ω(k) means one chosen branch, rather than a promise that the full dispersion relation is single-valued. For real k, Imω>0 means temporal growth in this convention, while Imω<0 means damping. The real-branch discussion of phase velocity and group velocity below assumes negligible loss.
For the one-dimensional wave equation utt​=c2uxx​, the dispersion relation is ω2=c2k2. The branch ω=ck carries right-moving travelling waves for c>0; both its phase velocity and group velocity are c. One may instead use the positive-angular frequency branch ω=c∣k∣, with right-moving components at k>0 and left-moving components at k<0. A real wave pairs each (k,ω) with (−k,−ω); that conjugate pair represents the same travelling pattern. Branch and sign choices therefore matter when interpreting a dispersion diagram.
A particularly tangible dispersive example is a small-amplitude surface gravity wave on still water of uniform depth h. Neglect surface tension and viscosity, assume incompressible flow and irrotational flow, and impose a flat impermeable bottom. With k>0 and positive angular frequency,
ω2=gktanh(kh),g>0.
(2)
Here g is gravitational acceleration and tanh is the hyperbolic tangent. Deep water means kh≫1: ω≃gk​ and longer wavelengths have larger phase speed. Shallow water means kh≪1: ω≃gh​k, an approximately nondispersive wave. Thus depth relative to wavelength, rather than depth alone, determines whether crests and wave packets travel together.[ref]
The dispersion diagram below also includes the ideal deep-water capillary wave limit ω=γ/ρ​k3/2, where γ is surface tension and ρ is mass density. This limit requires the surface tension restoring term to dominate gravity; it reverses the trend of the surface gravity wave, giving larger phase speed at shorter wavelength. The combined gravity-capillary wave has ω2=gk+(γ/ρ)k3.[ref]
The curves use dimensionless normalizations chosen separately for each model; they are analytical diagrams, not experimental data. Their beauty is that a single dispersion relation encodes several observable behaviours: its value gives the angular frequency, its origin-to-point slope gives the one-dimensional phase velocity, its tangent slope gives the group velocity, and variation of that tangent slope predicts distortion of a wave packet. A straight line through the origin describes a nondispersive wave; an affine branch ω=ck+b with b=0 has constant group velocity but phase velocity c+b/k, so those properties must be distinguished.

Constant phase-group velocity product for cutoff dispersion (vP​vG​=c2)

Dispersion relation OurBigBook.com
On a real positive-frequency branch of ω2=a2+c2k2, phase velocity is vP​=ω/k and group velocity is vG​=dω/dk=c2k/ω. Their product is c2. Real propagation requires ω2>a2 when solving for a nonzero real k; the inequality ω>a alone assumes a is nonnegative. The phase-group product does not, without further assumptions on the medium, prove a general statement about signal-front propagation.

Static mode lost by cancelling frequency

Words: 65
Dispersion relation OurBigBook.com
When a dispersion relation factors as ωP(ω,k)=0, dividing by ω discards a possible time-independent Fourier mode. This can remove a necessary stationary solution from an initial value problem. For instance, a constant-coefficient temporal equation T(3)+iaT′′+bT′=0 has a constant solution as well as the two exponential solutions from its quadratic factor. Three initial conditions generally require all three modes. Cancellation is valid only when a nonzero frequency has already been required.

Dispersive wave

A wave whose phase speed depends on frequency. In a one-dimensional branch, phase velocity is ω/k and the narrow-packet group velocity is dω/dk. Confinement can create dispersion even when the underlying unbounded material has a constant wave speed.

Spatiotemporal wave-packet stability

Words: 847 Articles: 15
Dispersion relation OurBigBook.com
The response to a localized perturbation must be followed in space as well as time. Growth at a fixed position is absolute wave-packet instability; growth transported away while the original position decays is convective wave-packet instability. The Briggs-Bers criterion evaluates the relevant complex spatial branches of a dispersion relation, while a real-wavenumber temporal mode test alone does not generally distinguish those behaviors.

Briggs-Bers criterion

Words: 437 Articles: 6
Spatiotemporal wave-packet stability OurBigBook.com
A criterion for absolute wave-packet instability based on analytic continuation of the spatial roots of a dispersion relation. Start the temporal inversion contour above the spectrum, then lower it while deforming the spatial contour. A spatial pinch point occurs when branches originating in opposite spatial half-planes obstruct that deformation. A double root D=Dk​=0 is only an algebraic candidate. The opposite-half-plane condition is stated in the primary study doi.org/10.1017/jfm.2016.195, section 4.2.

Wave dispersion

Words: 155 Articles: 3
Dispersion relation OurBigBook.com
Wave dispersion means that phase velocity depends on wavenumber or, equivalently, wavelength. Wave packets generally travel with group velocity, which need not equal phase velocity. The linear rotating shallow-water dispersion relation ω2=f2+c2k2 has faster long-wave phase motion but slower long-wave packet motion when f=0.

Angular dispersion (dθ/dλ)

The change of an optical propagation angle with wavelength. A diffraction grating at normal incidence has dsinθ=mλ, so differentiating gives dθ/dλ=m/(dcosθ). This converts a small spectral-line separation into angular separation, but resolving the two images also requires sufficient aperture and a narrow source image.

Intermodal dispersion

Words: 37
Wave dispersion OurBigBook.com
Intermodal dispersion is the temporal separation of components in different propagating normal modes because their group velocities differ. A pulse exciting several modes can therefore broaden even when its individual modal components have negligible bandwidth. It is distinct from group velocity dispersion within a single mode.

Nondispersive wave

Words: 43
Wave dispersion OurBigBook.com
A nondispersive wave has constant phase velocity on a propagation branch. For ω=ck, its group velocity is also c. A frequency relation with a nonzero offset, such as ω=ck+b, has constant group velocity but a phase velocity c+b/k depending on wavenumber; constant group velocity alone is therefore not sufficient for nondispersion.

Three velocities should consequently be kept separate. The group velocity predicts narrow-band wave envelope motion. An energy-transport velocity is energy flux divided by energy density, using the appropriate time average; it agrees with group velocity in standard lossless linear wave models when all stored energy is included.[ref] A signal-front velocity concerns the earliest response to a new disturbance and requires a causal model over its full spectrum. A strictly band-limited wave packet has no sharply bounded onset, and a mathematical two-tone envelope cannot test front arrival. In strongly absorbing or broadband propagation, a single group velocity need not describe the observed peak, energy transport, or first detectable response. A complex ω or k also prevents interpreting its derivative as an ordinary real transport velocity without further analysis.

The practical value of group velocity is precisely this translation between a local slope on a dispersion diagram and a measurable packet transit, with its assumptions made explicit.

At finite spectral width, a peak's measured speed need not equal the group velocity at the central wavenumber. Neither a peak nor a fixed fraction of its height generally identifies front velocity.
