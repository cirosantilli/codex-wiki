Divergence of normalized iid partial sums without a first absolute moment (E∣X1​∣=∞⟹limsupn​∣Xn​∣/n=limsupn​∣Sn​∣/n=∞ a.s.)

For independent identically distributed random variables, the tail-sum formula applied to ∣X1​∣/λ shows that ∑n​P(∣Xn​∣≥λn)=∞ for every λ>0 when the first absolute moment is infinite. The Borel-Cantelli second lemma, followed by a countable intersection over positive integer λ, gives the first limsup assertion.
If ∣Sn​∣/n had finite limsup along such a sample path, it would eventually be bounded by some finite constant C. Then Xn​=Sn​−Sn−1​ would imply ∣Xn​∣/n≤2C eventually, a contradiction. This is a necessary-integrability counterpart to the strong law of large numbers; without a finite first absolute moment, the normalized sums cannot converge to any finite value.
