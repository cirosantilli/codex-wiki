Expectation of an absolutely summable random series (E[∑i​Xi​]=∑i​E[Xi​]if ∑i​E∣Xi​∣<∞)

 ... Mathematics Area of mathematics Probability and statistics Probability theory Expected value Linearity of expectation
OurBigBook.com Words: 81
If random variables Xi​ satisfy ∑i​E∣Xi​∣<∞, then ∑i​Xi​ converges absolutely almost surely, is integrable, and its expected value equals ∑i​E[Xi​]. Indeed, the monotone convergence theorem gives E[∑i​∣Xi​∣]=∑i​E∣Xi​∣<∞, so that nonnegative sum is finite almost surely. It dominates every finite partial sum in absolute value; the dominated convergence theorem then justifies passing the limit through the expected value. No independence is required.
For example, if ∣Xi​∣≤Mqi for a fixed M and 0<q<1, the geometric series bound proves the hypothesis directly. This is useful when repeated observations are multiplied by geometrically shrinking distances or weights.
