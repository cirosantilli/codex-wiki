Conditioning reduces entropy (H(X∣Y)≤H(X))

 Home Mathematics Area of mathematics Probability and statistics Information theory Conditional entropy
OurBigBook.com Words: 813 Articles: 4
For finite discrete random variables X,Y, knowing Y reduces the information entropy of X on average:
H(X∣Y)=∑y:pY​(y)>0​pY​(y)H(X∣Y=y)≤H(X).
(6)
Equality holds exactly when X and Y are independent random variables. The average over observations is essential: H(X∣Y=y) can exceed H(X) for a particular y. All logarithms here have base two, giving bits, and zero-probability terms contribute zero.[ref]
For Bayesian inference, this compares a prior distribution with the posterior distributions produced by possible observations. An unexpected report can make previously unlikely alternatives plausible, while useful reports reduce uncertainty overall. For lossless source coding, an observation shared by encoder and decoder lets them choose descriptions for the appropriate conditional distribution; sending that observation also has a cost. The example below makes both the saving and that cost explicit.
In A Mathematical Theory of Communication by Claude Shannon (1948), the definition of conditional entropy is already an average over observations, followed by the inequality and its independence condition in section 6, property 6. The phrase “knowledge reduces uncertainty” must be read with that averaging convention.[ref]
