Cantor-Schröder-Bernstein theorem (∣X∣≤∣Y∣, ∣Y∣≤∣X∣⇒∣X∣=∣Y∣)

 Home Mathematics Area of mathematics Foundations of mathematics Set theory Cardinal number
OurBigBook.com Words: 803 Articles: 2
If there are injections X→Y and Y→X, then there is a bijection X≅Y.
For arbitrary sets X and Y, the precise equivalence is
(∃ injection f:X→Y) ∧ (∃ injection g:Y→X)⟺∃ bijection h:X→Y.
(19)
The reverse direction uses the bijection h and its inverse function. Neither set is assumed to be a subset of the other: the hypothesis concerns functions, rather than containments. The forward direction holds in ZF without the axiom of choice.[ref]
This makes cardinality comparison an antisymmetric relation: if each set is no larger than the other in the sense of an injection, they have equal cardinality. It also gives a practical strategy: build two convenient injections and let the theorem supply the bijection. The Injection-chain proof of the Cantor-Schröder-Bernstein theorem explains how to combine them without losing an output or pairing two inputs with the same output.
