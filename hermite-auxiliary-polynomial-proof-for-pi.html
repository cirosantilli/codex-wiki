Hermite auxiliary-polynomial proof for pi

Under an assumed algebraicity of iπ, form the product ∏j​(1+eαj​) over its conjugates; its one known vanishing factor makes the whole product zero and regroup the subset sums as k+∑bj​eβj​=0, with nonzero exponents and Galois-invariant integral weights. After clearing their denominators, use fp​(z)=(cz)p−1∏j​(cz−cβj​)p/(p−1)!. The sum of its derivatives at each βj​ is divisible by p in the algebraic integers, whereas at zero its first nonzero derivative is a fixed nonzero residue for all but finitely many primes. Summing with the weights gives a nonzero rational integer. Integrating the derivative of e−z∑fp(j)​(z) expresses that integer as a quantity bounded by Cp/(p−1)!, which tends to zero. This is the contradiction.
