Fixed-target antiproton production threshold (Kthr​=6mp​c2)

 ... Quantum field theory Relativistic quantum field Dirac field Mode expansion of a Dirac field Antiparticle Antiproton
OurBigBook.com Words: 213
The fixed-target production threshold concerns the incident accelerator beam, whereas antiproton mass from momentum and velocity concerns an outgoing secondary beam. To see the distinction, consider a free proton hitting a stationary free proton, producing
p+p⟶p+p+p+pˉ​.
(7)
The final electric charge is +2e and final baryon number is 3−1=2, as initially. Approximate all four final rest masses by the same proton rest mass m. With metric signature (+−−−), define the squared centre-of-mass energy by W2=c2(P1​+P2​)2. The incident proton has total energy E=mc2+K, and the target four-momentum is (mc,0). Four-momentum conservation and the mass shell relation give
W2=2m2c4+2mc2E=4m2c4+2mc2K.
(8)
At threshold the four final particles have zero relative momentum in their centre-of-momentum frame, so Wthr​=4mc2. Hence
16m2c4=4m2c4+2mc2Kthr​,Kthr​=6mc2≃5.63GeV.
(9)
Only 2mc2 of new rest energy is created. The larger incident kinetic energy is required because the final system must also carry the initial laboratory momentum. The incident total energy at threshold is 7mc2; neither this nor Kthr​ is the selected secondary momentum.
The 1955 authors explicitly qualified the stationary-nucleon threshold: target nucleon motion inside an atomic nucleus can lower the required incident energy.[ref]
The kinematic reason is visible for two on-shell incoming nucleons:
W2=2m2c4+2(Eb​Et​−c2pb​⋅pt​).
(10)
A target momentum directed against the beam increases W2. Applying this illustration to a bound nucleon also requires binding energy and residual-nucleus recoil; the free stationary-target 6mc2 result is therefore not a universal sharp threshold for a copper target. A low observed secondary momentum is entirely compatible with a much larger accelerator kinetic energy.
