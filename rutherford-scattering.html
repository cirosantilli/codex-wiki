Rutherford scattering

 Home Physics Atomic physics
OurBigBook.com Tags: Atomic nucleus Words: 973 Articles: 6
Rutherford scattering is elastic scattering by a Coulomb potential. In the repulsive classical case, a positively charged projectile bends around a positively charged atomic nucleus. A close approach can turn it backwards without a contact collision. The calculation connects an invisible impact parameter with a measurable outgoing angle, then with a differential scattering cross-section and a detector count.
Assume a projectile of mass m and electric charge ze, a stationary point atomic nucleus of electric charge Ze, and positive z,Z. Here e is the elementary charge. Neglect nuclear recoil, electron screening effect, energy loss, and the nuclear force. With vacuum permittivity ε0​, define
V(r)=rκ​,κ=4πε0​zZe2​>0,E=21​mv2,L=mvb.
(64)
Here E and v are the incident kinetic energy and speed far from the atomic nucleus, b≥0 is the impact parameter, and L is the magnitude of angular momentum. The nonrelativistic limit requires v≪c, with c the speed of light. The fixed-target approximation requires the target mass to be much greater than m. In SI, κ has units Jm, E has units J, and b has units m.
