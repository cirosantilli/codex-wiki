Entropy of mixing (Δmix​S=−nR∑i​xi​lnxi​)

 Home Chemistry Mixture Solution (chemistry) Ideal solution
OurBigBook.com Words: 203 Articles: 1
The entropy of mixing is the entropy change between initially separate components and their final mixture, with initial and final temperatures, pressures and component amount of substance values specified. For an ideal solution made from pure liquids at the same temperature and pressure, let n=∑i​ni​. Then
Δmix​S=−R∑i​ni​lnxi​=−nR∑i​xi​lnxi​.
(17)
The right-hand expression is the ideal solution result; the general definition does not require ideality. Each interior mole fraction has a negative logarithm, so mixing distinct components gives positive entropy of mixing. At a pure endpoint, use the limit xlnx→0 as x→0+.
For one mole of the chosen A/B ideal solution with xA​=0.4,
−0.4ln0.4−0.6ln0.6≈0.673011667,Δmix​S≈5.59573JK−1.
(18)
For the same total amount of substance, equal mole fractions maximize this binary entropy of mixing: the composition function s(x)=−xlnx−(1−x)ln(1−x) has
s′(x)=lnx1−x​,s′′(x)=−x1​−1−x1​<0,
(19)
so its unique maximum is s(1/2)=ln2. This is a comparison between different prepared compositions, not a prediction that a closed sample changes its component inventory to become equimolar.
