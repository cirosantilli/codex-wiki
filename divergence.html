Divergence (∇⋅)

 ... Area of mathematics Analysis Real analysis Calculus Multivariable calculus Vector calculus
OurBigBook.com Words: 665 Articles: 12
For F=(F1​,F2​,F3​) in Cartesian coordinates,
∇⋅F=∂i​Fi​=∂x∂F1​​+∂y∂F2​​+∂z∂F3​​.
(32)
For a continuously differentiable vector field, the divergence is the limiting outward flux per unit volume through a small closed surface. Opposite faces of a small rectangular box almost cancel; the remaining differences in the three components give the sum of partial derivatives above. The divergence theorem extends this local balance to a whole region.
For F=(x,y,0), the divergence is 1+1+0=2. In a planar unit square, the right edge contributes outward flux 1 and the top contributes 1, while the left and bottom contribute zero. The total 2 equals the integral of the planar divergence over area 1.
If F is a velocity field, positive divergence means local volume expansion, not automatically creation of mass. The continuity equation gives Dρ/Dt=−ρ∇⋅F, where D/Dt is the material derivative. For this field, a uniform initial density evolves as ρ(t)=ρ(0)e−2t while material volumes grow as e2t. Their product stays constant.
