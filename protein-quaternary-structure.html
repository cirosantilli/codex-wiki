Protein quaternary structure

 Home Biology Protein Protein structure
OurBigBook.com Words: 61
The protein quaternary structure is the arrangement of multiple polypeptide chains in a functional assembly. A single-chain protein has protein tertiary structure without requiring protein quaternary structure. For an exact bookkeeping example, two intact model chains of twelve amino acid residues each contain 2×12=24 residues and 2×11=22 peptide bonds. Assembly alone adds no peptide bond joining their ends. Stable functional assembly should also be distinguished from protein aggregation. See Alberts et al.: proteins as assemblies of polypeptide subunits.
