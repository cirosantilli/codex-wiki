Periodic distribution (⟨u,ϕ(⋅+T)⟩=⟨u,ϕ⟩)

 Home Mathematics Area of mathematics Analysis Distribution theory Distribution (mathematical analysis)
OurBigBook.com Words: 88
A distribution has period T>0 if the displayed equality holds for every test function ϕ. This agrees with f(x+T)=f(x) almost everywhere for a regular distribution. If u is a tempered distribution, density of test functions in the Schwartz space extends the identity to all Schwartz functions.
For a test function Ψ with ∑k​Ψ(x+kT)=1, one has
⟨u,ϕ⟩=⟨u,Ψ(x)∑k​ϕ(x+kT)⟩.
(1)
Indeed, expand ϕ=∑k​Ψ(x+kT)ϕ(x) in the Schwartz space and move each translation to ϕ by periodicity. All Schwartz seminorms of the localized terms decay faster than any power of ∣k∣, justifying the sum of pairings. This localization permits rigorous Fourier coefficients without multiplying a distribution by a discontinuous window.
