Finite element solution of the one-dimensional Poisson equation (−u′′=1, u(0)=u(1)=0)

 Home Mathematics Area of mathematics Analysis Numerical analysis Finite element method
OurBigBook.com Words: 531
Consider the Poisson equation on an interval with homogeneous Dirichlet boundary conditions:
−u′′(x)=1(0<x<1),u(0)=u(1)=0.
(113)
Two integrations and the endpoint conditions give the exact solution u(x)=x(1−x)/2. To approximate it, multiply by a test function v that vanishes at both endpoints. Integration by parts gives
∫01​(−u′′)vdx=[−u′v]01​+∫01​u′v′dx=∫01​vdx.
(114)
The boundary term is zero. Thus the weak formulation is to find u∈V=H01​(0,1) such that
a(u,v):=∫01​u′v′dx=ℓ(v):=∫01​vdx(v∈V).
(115)
The zero-trace Sobolev space H01​(0,1) consists of square-integrable functions with square-integrable weak derivatives and zero endpoint values in their continuous representatives; it does not require a classical second derivative. This is why continuous piecewise linear functions are admissible despite corners. The Poincaré inequality makes a a coercive bilinear form on V. Strang derives this one-dimensional weak formulation, the piecewise-linear hat functions, and their load and derivative-product integrals in the cited lecture; its endpoint example uses a free left endpoint, while both endpoints here are fixed.[ref]
Take the uniform finite element mesh xj​=jh, j=0,…,n, with h=1/n. The continuous P1​ trial space is
Vh​={vh​∈C([0,1]):vh​(0)=vh​(1)=0,vh​∣[xj​,xj+1​]​ is affine for every j}.
(116)
Here P1​ means polynomials of degree at most one on each interval. This is a conforming finite element space in V, with n−1 interior piecewise-linear hat functions ϕi​ as a basis. Their endpoint values enforce the Dirichlet boundary conditions automatically. Set uh​=∑i=1n−1​ci​ϕi​ and test the weak formulation against each ϕi​:
Kij​=∫01​ϕj′​ϕi′​dx,Fi​=∫01​ϕi​dx=h,Kc=F.
(117)
Each interior hat has two slopes 1/h and −1/h, each over a length h. Its squared derivative therefore integrates to 2/h. Adjacent hats have opposite slopes on their one shared interval, giving −1/h. All other derivative products vanish. The stiffness matrix is the tridiagonal matrix
Kii​=h2​,Ki,i+1​=Ki+1,i​=−h1​,Kij​=0(∣i−j∣>1).
(118)
The load Fi​=h is the area of a triangle of base 2h and height one, including hats next to the boundary. Boundary hats are omitted because the boundary values are prescribed. The piecewise-linear hat function article explains how the same entries arise by adding local two-node contributions.
With four mesh intervals, h=1/4, there are three unknowns:
K=​8−40​−48−4​0−48​​,F=​1/41/41/4​​,c=​3/321/83/32​​.
(119)
For example, the first row gives 8(3/32)−4(1/8)=1/4 and the middle gives −4(3/32)+8(1/8)−4(3/32)=1/4. The coefficients are the nodal values uh​(xi​)=ci​. At x=3/8, linear interpolation gives uh​(3/8)=7/64=0.109375, whereas u(3/8)=15/128=0.1171875: exactness at the nodes still leaves a visible error between them.
In this model the Galerkin method solution equals the nodal approximation Ih​u obtained by linear interpolation on any finite element mesh. Indeed, on each interval [a,b], (Ih​u)′ is the average of u′ and vh′​ is constant. Therefore ∫ab​(u′−(Ih​u)′)vh′​dx=0. Summing proves Galerkin orthogonality for Ih​u, and uniqueness gives uh​=Ih​u. This argument relies on the constant diffusion coefficient, conformity and exact integration; nodal exactness is not a general promise for finite element methods with other operators or integration rules.
On a uniform mesh, direct subtraction of the chord from the parabola gives, on every element [a,b] of length h,
e(x):=u(x)−uh​(x)=2(x−a)(b−x)​,e′(x)=2a+b​−x.
(120)
The largest value error is at an element midpoint. Its size and the energy norm error are exactly
∥e∥∞​=8h2​,∥e∥a2​=∫01​∣e′∣2dx=n∫−h/2h/2​t2dt=12h2​,∥e∥a​=12​h​.
(121)
Thus h=1/4 gives ∥e∥∞​=1/128=0.0078125 and ∥e∥a​=1/(412​)≈0.0721688. With eight elements, h=1/8, these become 1/512=0.001953125 and 1/(812​)≈0.0360844. Halving the mesh spacing quarters the maximum value error but only halves the energy norm error: values and slopes converge at different rates.
