Convex combination (∑i=1n​λi​xi​,λi​≥0,∑i​λi​=1)

 Home Mathematics Area of mathematics Mathematical optimization Convex set
OurBigBook.com Words: 325 Articles: 2
A convex combination is a finite weighted average of points in a real vector space. For n≥1, it has the form
∑i=1n​λi​xi​,λi​≥0,∑i=1n​λi​=1.
(4)
The nonnegative coefficients forbid subtracting a component, and their unit sum keeps the result on the same affine scale as the inputs. With two points it parametrizes their line segment; equal coefficients give their centroid. A convex set contains every finite convex combination of its points. Conversely, the two-point case already gives the defining property of a convex set.[ref]
The distinction from a linear combination is concrete. Combining (0,0) and (2,0) with coefficients −1 and 2 gives (4,0), outside their line segment, despite the unit coefficient sum. Using two coefficients both equal to one gives (2,0) here, but their sum is two, so it is not a convex combination with those coefficients. A numerical output may happen to coincide with a point admitting a different convex representation; the definition applies to the specified coefficients.
