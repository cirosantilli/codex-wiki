Multigrid method

 Home Mathematics Area of mathematics Analysis Numerical analysis
OurBigBook.com Words: 2k Articles: 6
A multigrid method solves a linear system by combining local relaxation with coarse-grid correction on a hierarchy of meshes. The key is to divide the work by scale: a suitable relaxation removes rapidly oscillating approximation error, while coarse-grid correction addresses slowly varying approximation error that relaxation barely changes. A broad error hump occupies many fine-grid points but fewer coarse-grid points; it becomes easier to resolve by relaxation after enough coarsening. This complementary action is why multigrid methods are useful for large finite difference method and finite element method systems.
For a concrete model, take the one-dimensional Poisson equation with homogeneous Dirichlet boundary conditions:
−u′′(x)=f(x),0<x<1,u(0)=u(1)=0.
(1)
Use n interior points xj​=jh, h=1/(n+1), and the centered finite difference system
(Ah​uh​)j​=h22uh,j​−uh,j−1​−uh,j+1​​=bh,j​,bh,j​=f(xj​),uh,0​=uh,n+1​=0.
(2)
Thus Ah​=h−2tridiag(−1,2,−1) is a symmetric matrix and a positive-definite matrix. The minus sign in −u′′ fixes this positive convention; changing both Ah​ and bh​ to their negatives leaves the relaxation update unchanged. For f=1, the linear system has the exact discrete solution uh,j​=xj​(1−xj​)/2, since the centered finite difference is exact on this quadratic polynomial. In general, the exact discrete solution need not equal the sampled continuous solution: solving the linear system removes algebraic approximation error, not the global discretization error.
Let uh∗​ solve Ah​uh∗​=bh​, let vh​ be the current approximation, and define the signed approximation error eh​=uh∗​−vh​. The linear-system residual is rh​=bh​−Ah​vh​, so Ah​eh​=rh​. In particular, a linear-system residual is available without knowing uh∗​; it supplies the right-hand side for a correction equation. With D=2h−2I, a weighted Jacobi method sweep is
vh​←vh​+ωD−1rh​,eh​←Sω​eh​,Sω​=I−ωD−1Ah​.
(3)
The sine eigenvectors shared by Ah​ and the iteration matrix Sω​ are wj(k)​=sin(kπj/(n+1)), 1≤k≤n. Their eigenvalues are
λk​(Ah​)=4h−2sin22(n+1)kπ​,μk​(Sω​)=1−2ωsin22(n+1)kπ​.
(4)
For n=63, one weighted Jacobi method sweep with ω=2/3 multiplies the lowest Fourier frequency mode k=1 by about 0.999197, but the highest Fourier frequency mode k=63 by about −0.332530. Four sweeps retain about 99.68% of the first amplitude and 1.22% of the second. The sign reversal of the second amplitude is harmless when its magnitude shrinks. However, the plain Jacobi method, ω=1, has factor cos(kπ/(n+1)): at k=63 it is about −0.998795. A nearly alternating mode near the grid's Nyquist limit therefore persists almost as strongly as the smoothest mode. Plain Jacobi method convergence on a fixed Dirichlet boundary condition grid does not imply effective smoothing.[ref] The high-frequency smoothing factor of weighted Jacobi is uniformly at most 1/3 for ω=2/3 and k≥(n+1)/2; the exact finite-grid high-frequency Jacobi smoothing optimum distinguishes this convenient uniform choice from the finite-grid optimum.[ref]
One two-grid cycle applies a few weighted Jacobi method sweeps, computes the linear-system residual, performs coarse-grid correction, and applies a few more sweeps. The first relaxation makes the remaining approximation error easier to represent on a coarse mesh; the last relaxation damps oscillatory components left after the transferred correction. In a geometric multigrid V-cycle, replace the expensive exact coarse solve by one recursive cycle on the coarse correction equation, starting its correction from zero. Repeat until a tiny coarsest linear system can be solved directly, then return through the levels by adding each interpolated correction and post-smoothing. The descent and return give the cycle its V shape.[ref]
For this one-dimensional model, fixed sweep counts and bounded local finite difference and transfer work give a geometric multigrid V-cycle cost proportional to n+n/2+n/4+⋯<2n, hence O(n) in Big O notation. A fixed error reduction also needs a contraction factor bounded below one independently of h; per-cycle work alone does not establish that. Starting from arbitrary approximation error and demanding a relative reduction ε generally adds a factor proportional to log(1/ε) in the cycle count. Full multigrid uses coarse solutions as initial guesses on successively finer meshes to target the global discretization error efficiently. The two-grid Poisson factor with weighted Jacobi illustrates favorable convergence for this model. Other partial differential equations, strong anisotropy, or poorly chosen coarse spaces and relaxation can defeat these choices; a linear-work guarantee for arbitrary linear systems does not follow.
A historical example of the multilevel viewpoint is Achi Brandt's April 1977 paper, which analyzes adaptive meshes and reports numerical experiments on linear and nonlinear boundary value problems. This is a documented development of the approach, not a claim that the paper was its first invention.[ref] The figure above is generated from the displayed eigenvalues, with lines joining discrete nodes; it contains no experimental measurements.

Full multigrid

Words: 79
Multigrid method OurBigBook.com
Full multigrid solves first on a coarse mesh, interpolates that approximation to the next finer mesh, and applies a fixed number of geometric multigrid V-cycles before proceeding to the next level. If interpolation introduces only the expected discretization-scale error and the cycle count reduces the inherited error sufficiently at every level, the final algebraic error matches the fine-grid discretization error. The geometric sum of level costs is then O(N), unlike reducing an arbitrary fine-grid initial error by an arbitrarily small tolerance.

Two-grid Poisson factor with weighted Jacobi (ρ(E)=1/9)

Words: 81
Multigrid method OurBigBook.com
For the one-dimensional Poisson equation, use linear interpolation, full-weighting restriction, an exact coarse solve, and one pre- and one post-sweep of weighted Jacobi with weight 2/3. On a pair of harmonics θ,θ+π, put s=sin2(θ/2) and c=1−s. The coarse correction is C=(s−s​−cc​), and the smoother is S=diag(1−4s/3,1−4c/3). The rank-one error operator SCS has nonzero eigenvalue s(1−4s/3)2+c(1−4c/3)2=1/9. This exact harmonic calculation illustrates mesh-independent two-grid contraction for the constant-coefficient model; it is not an unconditional result for arbitrary operators or transfer choices.

Geometric multigrid V-cycle

Words: 77
Multigrid method OurBigBook.com
A geometric multigrid V-cycle pre-smooths an approximate solution, restricts its residual, recursively solves the coarse error equation once from zero, prolongs and adds that error correction, and post-smooths. The recursion ends in a direct solve on a small coarsest grid. With fixed sweep counts and bounded stencil work, coarsening in d dimensions gives total work O(N)∑j≥0​2−dj=O(N) per cycle. A mesh-independent convergence factor also requires compatible coarse approximation and effective smoothing; linear work alone does not prove convergence.

Full-weighting restriction (R=2−dPT)

Words: 62
Multigrid method OurBigBook.com
Full-weighting restriction transfers a fine-grid residual to a coarser grid by local averaging. In one dimension its weights are (1,2,1)/4; in two dimensions they are the tensor-product stencil (121​242​121​)/16. For linear or bilinear interpolation P and coarsening by two in every direction, it equals 2−dPT in unweighted coordinates. This scaling makes restriction the adjoint of interpolation for mesh-weighted inner products.

Coarse-grid correction (uh​←uh​+PAH−1​R(bh​−Ah​uh​))

Words: 670 Articles: 1
Multigrid method OurBigBook.com
A coarse-grid correction improves a fine-grid approximation by solving a smaller equation for the part of its approximation error representable on a coarse mesh. Use the signed convention eh​=uh∗​−vh​ and the linear-system residual rh​=bh​−Ah​vh​=Ah​eh​. A prolongation linear map P:Rm→Rn interpolates coarse corrections into fine-grid corrections, and a restriction linear map R:Rn→Rm transfers the linear-system residual. Solve AH​zH​=Rrh​ exactly or approximately and update vh​←vh​+PzH​. The plus sign follows from defining the approximation error as exact minus approximate. It is the correction equation's right-hand side Rrh​, rather than merely restricted solution values, that determines the update.
For the multigrid method model Ah​=h−2tridiag(−1,2,−1), take n=2m+1, so H=2h and the coarse nodes coincide with every second fine node. Use linear interpolation with zero boundary corrections z0​=zm+1​=0:
(Pz)2i​=zi​(1≤i≤m),(Pz)2i+1​=2zi​+zi+1​​(0≤i≤m).
(5)
Use full-weighting restriction for the interior linear-system residual:
(Rr)i​=4r2i−1​+2r2i​+r2i+1​​(1≤i≤m),R=21​PT.
(6)
Here PT is the matrix transpose in ordinary unweighted coordinates. With these specific transfers, the Galerkin coarse-grid operator is
AH​=RAh​P=H−2tridiag(−1,2,−1).
(7)
Thus it equals the rediscretized Poisson equation operator on this constant-coefficient one-dimensional mesh. The equality is special to the stated model and transfers. In particular, dropping h−2 from the fine matrix while using H−2 on the coarse mesh changes the correction unless the right-hand sides and transfers are rescaled consistently.
Take seven interior points, h=1/8, three coarse unknowns, and the deliberately constructed approximation error
z=(1,2,1)T,eh​=Pz=(1/2,1,3/2,2,3/2,1,1/2)T.
(8)
For example, choose bh​=0, so uh∗​=0 and vh​=−eh​. Direct matrix multiplication gives
rh​=Ah​eh​=(0,0,0,64,0,0,0)T,Rrh​=(0,32,0)T,AH​=16​2−10​−12−1​0−12​​.
(9)
The exact coarse solve returns zH​=z, so adding PzH​ removes the entire chosen approximation error. A broad triangular error can have a sharply localized linear-system residual: the latter measures discrete curvature, not error height. This is why the two should not be confused. A smooth sine mode instead has linear-system residual λk​(Ah​)eh​, so interpreting its magnitude also requires the eigenvalue and the equation's scaling.
The algebra explains the exact cancellation beyond this example. Assume P has linearly independent columns and choose an invertible Galerkin coarse-grid operator AH​=RAh​P. With an exact coarse solve, the iteration matrix for the remaining approximation error is
ehnew​=Ceh​,C=I−PAH−1​RAh​,CP=0,C2=C,RAh​C=0.
(10)
The short proof is to substitute RAh​P=AH​. Thus every vector in the column space of P is eliminated, and the corrected linear-system residual restricts to zero. These are exact projection identities, not a statement that every visually smooth vector belongs to that column space. If Ah​ is a symmetric matrix and a positive-definite matrix, and R=cPT with c>0, then AH​ is also a positive-definite matrix. Moreover PTAh​Ceh​=0: C is an orthogonal projection onto the complement of the column space of P in the energy norm ∥q∥Ah​​=qTAh​q​. Equivalently, the added correction is the best approximation to eh​ from that column space in this energy norm. Without these symmetry and transfer assumptions, the projection need not be orthogonal.[ref]
For an approximate coarse solve represented by a fixed linear map BH​, replace AH−1​ by BH​. Then
CB​=I−PBH​RAh​,CB​P=P(I−BH​AH​).
(11)
There is no exact cancellation of coarse-representable approximation error unless BH​AH​=I on the relevant directions. For the triangular example, deliberately returning half the exact correction, BH​=21​AH−1​, leaves eh​/2, as the figure shows. This controlled mathematical comparison illustrates an incomplete coarse solve; it is not a measured performance result or a prescription for a geometric multigrid V-cycle. More generally, if zH∗​=AH−1​Rrh​, then an approximate zH​ leaves Ceh​+P(zH∗​−zH​), separating the nonrepresentable remainder from the coarse-solve defect.
With pre- and post-relaxation iteration matrices Spre​ and Spost​, a two-grid error step has iteration matrix E=Spost​CB​Spre​. A geometric multigrid V-cycle supplies BH​ by one recursive coarse cycle from zero, with a direct solve only at the bottom. Effective smoothing, a coarse column space that approximates the surviving approximation error, and a sufficiently accurate coarse correction must work together. Coarsening alone supplies no convergence guarantee, and correcting an arbitrary fine-grid vector exactly would require a coarse column space large enough to contain it.

Galerkin coarse-grid operator (AH​=RAh​P)

Words: 70
Coarse-grid correction OurBigBook.com
A Galerkin coarse-grid operator is obtained by composing interpolation, the fine operator and residual restriction. With R=cPT, c>0, and positive-definite Ah​, the exact coarse-grid correction I−P(RAh​P)−1RAh​ is the energy-orthogonal projection onto the complement of the coarse space. Indeed it annihilates every vector Pz, and its corrected error e′ satisfies PTAh​e′=0. These identities explain why compatible transfers remove representable smooth error rather than merely averaging an approximate solution.
