Embedded Markov chain at level exits

 ... Mathematics Area of mathematics Probability and statistics Probability theory Markov process Markov chain
OurBigBook.com Words: 176
Suppose motion within each level ends on hitting one of two boundaries, and each boundary transition resets the process to a fixed entry state of an adjacent level. Observe only the successive level exits. The resulting level process is a Markov chain, with transition probabilities equal to the within-level boundary-hitting probabilities. The fixed-entry condition matters: without it, retaining only the level may discard information needed for the next transition law.
For a simple symmetric random walk on local rooms 0,…,r, starting each excursion at room 1, the gambler's ruin probability of reaching r first is 1/r. Thus the level chain moves up with probability 1/r and down with probability (r−1)/r. Independent future decisions, or more generally the Strong Markov property, justify restarting this calculation after each exit.
For two active levels between absorbing levels 0 and 3, starting on level 1, put p=1/r and q=1−p. The success probabilities satisfy u=pv and v=p+qu, hence
