In the straight-trajectory dipole model, retain the Coulomb-coupling convention of V(t)=Ze2(bx+vty)/(b2+v2t2)3/2. In SI units the coupling denoted e2 here is e2/(4πϵ0​). First-order time-dependent perturbation theory expresses a transition through the Fourier transform of this pulse at the transition angular frequency.
For positive ω put β=ωb/v. The two Cartesian pulse transforms are
Ix​=∫−∞∞​(b2+v2t2)3/2beiωt​dt=bv2βK1​(β)​,Iy​=∫−∞∞​(b2+v2t2)3/2vteiωt​dt=bv2iβK0​(β)​,
(23)
where Kν​ is a Modified Bessel function of the second kind. One may derive these from the Fourier transform of (1+s2)−1/2, differentiating in its scale and integrating its spatial derivative by parts. For β≪1, βK1​(β)→1 while βK0​(β)→0, so the transverse impulse gives the probability in the title. If the x matrix element vanishes, the smaller longitudinal term can be the leading nonzero contribution.
The conditions include b large compared with the atomic extent for the electric-dipole approximation, a small total transition probability for first-order theory, and a collision time b/v short compared with 1/∣ω∣. Large β instead gives exponentially suppressed excitation in this model through the large-argument behavior of Kν​.
