Relativity of simultaneity (Δt′=−γvΔx/c2(Δt=0))

 Home Physics Branches of physics Special relativity Lorentz transformation
OurBigBook.com Words: 599 Articles: 7
Spatially separated simultaneous events in one inertial frame are generally not simultaneous in a relatively moving frame. The Lorentz transformation explicitly mixes position into time. This is essential when comparing distances between moving light pulses: one must compare their positions at the same time in the measuring frame.
At v=0.6c, the rod's simultaneous laboratory endpoint measurements in length contraction have (ct,x)=(0,0) and (0,8m). They become (ct′,x′)=(0,0) and (−6m,10m). In contrast, simultaneous rod-frame measurements at t′=0 use (ct,x)=(0,0) and (7.5m,12.5m). The latter front-end event occurs later in the laboratory, after the front has moved. The sloping t′=0 line in the figure is a set of simultaneous spacetime events, not a signal's trajectory. [ref].
