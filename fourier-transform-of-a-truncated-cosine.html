Fourier transform of a truncated cosine (f​(k)=sin[(k−1)a]/(k−1)+sin[(k+1)a]/(k+1))

 Home Mathematics Area of mathematics Analysis Fourier transform
OurBigBook.com Words: 75
For f(x)=cosx on [−a,a] and zero outside, the angular-frequency Fourier transform follows by writing the cosine as two complex exponentials. Combining fractions gives [2kcosasin(ak)−2sinacos(ak)]/(k2−1). The apparent singularities at k=±1 are removable, with value a+sin(2a)/2. If cosa=0, jumps at the endpoints give an oscillatory O(∣k∣−1) envelope. If cosa=0, the function is continuous at the endpoints but its first derivative jumps, giving an O(∣k∣−2) envelope. This is an explicit instance of Fourier decay from a derivative jump.
