Divisor function (τ(n))

 Home Mathematics Area of mathematics Number theory Arithmetic function
OurBigBook.com Words: 293 Articles: 2
The divisor function τ(n)=∑d∣n​1 counts the positive integer divisors of a positive integer n. In particular τ(1)=1, because 1 has just one positive integer divisor. More generally the notation σk​(n)=∑d∣n​dk gives τ=σ0​ and the sum-of-divisors function σ1​. Thus the divisor function counts the positive integer divisors, while σ1​ adds their sizes. [ref]
If the prime factorization is n=∏k=1r​pkak​​ with distinct prime numbers pk​ and positive exponents ak​, then
τ(n)=∏k=1r​(ak​+1).
(72)
The short proof is a count: each positive integer divisor has a unique form ∏k=1r​pkbk​​, where 0≤bk​≤ak​. There are ak​+1 choices at each prime number, and choosing them gives every positive integer divisor exactly once. For n=1 there are no prime factors and the product is 1.
For 12=22⋅3, choose i∈{0,1,2} and j∈{0,1} to obtain 2i3j. The six values are 1,2,4,3,6,12; sorted, the positive integer divisors are 1,2,3,4,6,12. Therefore τ(12)=(2+1)(1+1)=6. The diagram makes the independent exponent choices visible.
This count is useful because a known prime factorization gives τ(n) without testing every smaller positive integer for divisibility. It also proves the divisor function is a multiplicative function: coprime integers have disjoint prime factors, so their exponent choices combine. For example τ(6)=4=τ(2)τ(3), but τ(4)=3=4=τ(2)2, so it is not a completely multiplicative function. Equivalently, τ=1∗1 under Dirichlet convolution, counting ordered pairs of positive integers whose product is n.
