Principal ideal property of quotients of a Dedekind domain (I=0⟹R/I is a principal ideal ring)

 Home Mathematics Area of mathematics Algebra Commutative algebra Dedekind domain
OurBigBook.com Words: 123
For a Dedekind domain R and a nonzero proper ideal I, the quotient ring R/I is a principal ideal ring. First, the correspondence theorem for ideals and unique factorization of ideals in a Dedekind domain show that the ideals of R/Pn are exactly Pi/Pn, 0≤i≤n. Consecutive powers are distinct by cancellation of invertible fractional ideals. Choose π∈Pi∖Pi+1. There is no intermediate ideal between these consecutive powers, so Pi=(π)+Pi+1. Multiplication by Pr and substitution show inductively that Pi=(π)+Pi+r for every r≥1. Taking r=n−i gives a single generator modulo Pn. Now factor I into pairwise comaximal ideals Pjnj​​. The Chinese remainder theorem writes R/I as a finite direct product of rings of the preceding type, which is a principal ideal ring. It need not be an integral domain, as Z/4Z demonstrates. Applying the result to (a) for any 0=a∈I makes I/(a) cyclic, yielding two generators for an ideal in a Dedekind domain.
