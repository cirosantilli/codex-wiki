Primary decomposition theorem for linear maps (V=⨁i​kerpi​(T)ai​)

 ... Area of mathematics Algebra Linear algebra Operator theory Minimal polynomial Kernel decomposition for coprime polynomials
OurBigBook.com Words: 145
For a linear map T:V→V on a finite-dimensional vector space over a field F, factor its minimal polynomial as mT​=∏i​piai​​ with distinct monic polynomials pi​ that are irreducible polynomials. Then
V=i⨁​Vi​,Vi​=kerpi​(T)ai​,mT∣Vi​​​=piai​​​.
(45)
Each Vi​ is an invariant subspace, since T commutes with every polynomial in T. These are canonical vector subspaces, determined by T, without requiring any eigenvalues in F.
For a proof, put mi​=piai​​ and Mi​=mT​/mi​. The coprime polynomials mi​,Mi​ admit a Bézout's identity for polynomials bi​Mi​+ci​mi​=1. Set Ei​=bi​(T)Mi​(T). Its image is contained in Vi​ because mi​(T)Ei​=0. The polynomial ∑i​bi​Mi​−1 is divisible by every mi​, hence by their product, so ∑i​Ei​=I. On Vi​, Ei​=I; on Vj​ with j=i, Ei​=0 because mj​ divides Mi​. These facts prove the direct sum and identify Ei​ as its projection operators. Finally, if the minimal polynomial of any restriction had a smaller exponent, mT​/pi​ would annihilate every summand and hence all of V, a contradiction.
