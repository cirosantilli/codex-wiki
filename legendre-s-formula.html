Legendre's formula (vp​(n!)=∑j≥1​⌊n/pj⌋)

 Home Mathematics Area of mathematics Number theory P-adic valuation
OurBigBook.com Words: 299
For a nonnegative integer n and a prime number p, the Legendre formula computes the prime exponent in a factorial without forming that huge product:
vp​(n!)=∑j≥1​⌊pjn​⌋.
(48)
Here ⌊x⌋ is the floor function. The sum is finite: every term with pj>n is zero. When n=0, all terms vanish, agreeing with 0!=1 and vp​(1)=0. The prime number hypothesis matters: a composite number does not give the same P-adic valuation or additive prime exponent. For example, the floor sum with n=6,p=4 is one, whereas 42 divides 6!=720.
Why count multiples repeatedly? Each multiple of p in 1,…,n contributes one factor of p; each multiple of p2 contributes another; and so on. A factor m with P-adic valuation a occurs in exactly the first a counts. Adding those contributions proves the formula.[ref]
For the original example n=50,p=3,
v3​(50!)=⌊350​⌋+⌊950​⌋+⌊2750​⌋=16+5+1=22.
(49)
The five multiples 9,18,27,36,45 each contribute a second factor of three. The factor 27 contributes a third, so it appears in all three rows. Equivalently, among 1,…,50 there are eleven factors of P-adic valuation one, four of valuation two, and one of valuation three: 11+2⋅4+3⋅1=22. Thus 322 divides 50!, while 323 does not.
Repeated Euclidean division provides a practical evaluation: replace q by ⌊q/p⌋, starting from q=n, and add each new quotient until it is zero. This uses only the successive quotients 16,5,1,0 for the example. The same calculation handles divisibility of binomial coefficients, where the P-adic valuations of the denominator factorials are subtracted.
