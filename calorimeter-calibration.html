Calorimeter calibration (Ceff​=mw​cw​+Ccal​)

 ... Physics Branches of physics Statistical physics Thermodynamics Calorimetry Calorimeter
OurBigBook.com Words: 317
Calorimeter calibration establishes the response of the actual measurement assembly to a known energy input. It determines a participating heat capacity, not just the empty vessel's mass. For an electrical calibration with an immersed resistor, measure voltage V(t) across that resistor and electric current I(t) through it. If that electrical work is deposited inside the chosen boundary, its input is
Eel​=∫t0​t1​​V(t)I(t)dt=Ceff​(T1​−T0​)+Qloss,out​,Ceff​=mw​cw​+Ccal​.
(5)
For a covered receiver at constant pressure, Ceff​ describes enthalpy storage; for a rigid fixed-volume assembly, use the matching heat capacity at constant volume. Negligible endpoint mechanical storage and no escaping material are assumed. Thus Eel​/(T1​−T0​) overestimates Ceff​ if outward loss is ignored. Account for lead losses outside the boundary and maintain the same water charge and immersed hardware in calibration and sample runs.
An alternative calibration mixes known hot and cold water portions. With the vessel initially at the cold portion's temperature Tc​, the mixing calorimetry balance gives
Ccal​=Tf​−Tc​mh​cw​(Th​−Tf​)−Qloss,out​​−mc​cw​.
(6)
The previous synthetic water example returns 100JK−1. The sign matters: attributing room losses to the vessel makes this estimate too large. With both Ccal​ and the loss unknown, one final temperature supplies only one equation; separate cooling information or controlled independent runs are needed.
A calorimeter heat-loss correction can make the electrical balance quantitative. For a second synthetic example, stipulate 0.100kg of water with cw​=4180Jkg−1K−1 and immersed hardware with Ccal​=82JK−1, so Ceff​=500JK−1. Supply 10W for 300s, starting at a room temperature of 20∘C, with loss coefficient G=1WK−1. The model below gives T1​≈24.512∘C, storage 2255.942J and outward loss 744.058J. The uncorrected ratio gives about 665JK−1; subtracting the loss recovers 500JK−1.
