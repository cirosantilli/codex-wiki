Data processing inequality (I(X;Z)≤min{I(X;Y),I(Y;Z)})

For finite discrete random variables, suppose X→Y→Z is a Markov chain: the final channel uses only Y, so X and Z have conditional independence given Y. In terms of the joint probability mass function,
p(x,y,z)=pX​(x)p(y∣x)p(z∣y),
(34)
and hence I(X;Z∣Y)=0. Then the two data processing inequalities are
I(X;Z)≤I(X;Y),I(X;Z)≤I(Y;Z).
(35)
All mutual informations here use base-two logarithms. Thus deterministic or randomized processing cannot increase the information shared with an unprocessed variable.[ref]
For a concrete cascade, take a fair bit X and Bernoulli random variables Nq​,Nr​ with probabilities q=0.1, r=0.2 of being one. Assume X,Nq​,Nr​ are jointly independent random variables, and pass through two binary symmetric channels:
Y=X⊕Nq​,Z=Y⊕Nr​.
(36)
The second noise is independent of both X and Y, so this is a Markov chain. Both Y and Z are fair bits. The net exclusive or error is one exactly when one noise flips the bit and the other does not:
s=P(Nq​⊕Nr​=1)=q(1−r)+(1−q)r=q+r−2qr=0.26.
(37)
Using the binary entropy h2​(t)=−tlog2​t−(1−t)log2​(1−t), the three mutual informations are fully determined:
I(X;Y)I(Y;Z)I(X;Z)​=1−h2​(0.1)≈1−0.4689955936≈0.5310044064,=1−h2​(0.2)≈1−0.7219280949≈0.2780719051,=1−h2​(0.26)≈1−0.8267463725≈0.1732536275.​
(38)
All numbers are in bits. The two losses are I(X;Y∣Z)≈0.3577507789 and I(Y;Z∣X)≈0.1048182776. Thus the cascade retains less information about the original source than either individual channel shares between its own input and output.
With q=0.1 fixed and 0≤r≤1/2, the effective error is s=0.1+0.8r. At r=0, Z=Y and I(X;Z)=I(X;Y); at r=1/2, Z is independent of the previous variables and I(X;Z)=I(Y;Z)=0. The diagram plots these endpoints and the computed r=0.2 example.
The data processing inequality is useful for testing what a proposed decoder or data summary could possibly reveal: if its only input is Y, even randomized processing cannot provide more mutual information about X than Y already contains. The Markov chain hypothesis matters. In the exclusive or example from conditional mutual information, allowing a processor to use both Y and Z reveals X exactly; that processor has additional input, so the conclusion for processing Y alone does not apply.
The chain rule for mutual information proves both inequalities and identifies their exact gaps. First expand the information about X in the two observation orders:
I(X;Y,Z)​=I(X;Y)+0I(X;Z∣Y)​​=I(X;Z)+I(X;Y∣Z).​
(39)
Therefore I(X;Y)−I(X;Z)=I(X;Y∣Z)≥0. Next use Z as the variable being learned:
I(Z;X,Y)​=I(Z;Y)+0I(Z;X∣Y)​​=I(Z;X)+I(Z;Y∣X).​
(40)
Symmetry of mutual information gives I(Y;Z)−I(X;Z)=I(Y;Z∣X)≥0. Both signs follow from the nonnegativity of conditional mutual information; the first gap also gives equality in data processing inequality.
