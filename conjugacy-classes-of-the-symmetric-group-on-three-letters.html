Conjugacy classes of the symmetric group on three letters (S3​: 1+3+2=6)

 ... Area of mathematics Algebra Group theory Group action Conjugation action Conjugacy class
OurBigBook.com Words: 173
The symmetric group S3​ has exactly six permutations, written using permutation cycles:
S3​={e,(12),(13),(23),(123),(132)}.
(20)
Here e is the identity element; (12), (13) and (23) are transpositions; and (123) and (132) are permutation cycles of length three. With t=(12) and r=(123), rightmost-first function composition gives r2=(132) and r−1=r2. The conjugacy classes and representative centralizers are
xetr​ClS3​​(x){e}{(12),(13),(23)}{(123),(132)}​CS3​​(x)S3​{e,t}{e,r,r2}​∣ClS3​​(x)∣6/6=16/2=36/3=2​​
(21)
An explicit check of the conjugation gtg−1 with g=r=(123) sends 1↦3↦3↦1, 2↦1↦2↦3, and 3↦2↦1↦2, where each chain applies g−1, then t, then g. Thus 1 is fixed and 2,3 are exchanged: gtg−1=(23). Relabelling also produces (13), so the three transpositions share one conjugacy class. A transposition reverses the length-three permutation cycle, since trt−1=(213)=(132)=r−1, putting both length-three permutation cycles in one conjugacy class.
Checking each of the six possible permutations against gx=xg gives the displayed centralizers. Only e has a one-element conjugacy class, so the center of a group here is Z(S3​)={e}. The class equation is 6=1+3+2. The second diagram in centralizer and normalizer uses the same r and t to show how preserving a cyclic subgroup differs from fixing its group generator.
