Delayed cursor feedback experiment

 Home Mathematics Area of mathematics Control theory Closed-loop control Feedback delay
OurBigBook.com Words: 212
Can feedback delay change correction timing even when the physical task stays the same? Susilaradeya and colleagues tested this in a human tracking experiment. Participants pressed with both index fingers against fixed force sensors; the two forces controlled a screen cursor along two diagonal axes. A target moved in slow circular motion, usually at 0.2 rotations per second. The researchers sampled the forces at 50 samples per second and inserted an additional delay of 0, 100, 200, 300, or 400 milliseconds before displaying the cursor. Each trial lasted 20 seconds; the 70 trials included 14 per delay, in pseudorandom order. [ref]
The readout was fluctuation in cursor angular velocity, analyzed by Fourier transforms over approximately 10 seconds from the middle of each trial. The principal peak was around 2 hertz without added delay, around 1.4 hertz with 100 milliseconds, and around 1 hertz with 200 milliseconds. Thus changing observation timing changed the timing of corrective motion. [ref] This is an empirical motivation for taking stale observations seriously. Human feedback control includes prediction and other dynamics; these observations do not measure k in the ideal integrator model or test its kτ=π/2 boundary.
