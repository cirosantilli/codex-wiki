Limit superior (limsup)

 ... Mathematics Area of mathematics Analysis Real analysis Sequence and series Sequence
OurBigBook.com Words: 297 Articles: 1
The limit superior of a real sequence is
limsupn→∞​an​=infN​supn≥N​an​,
(68)
allowing a value in the extended real number line. For a finite value U, every ε>0 gives an eventual upper bound an​<U+ε, while arbitrarily late terms still satisfy an​>U−ε. The second condition concerns recurrence at arbitrarily large indices, not the fraction of indices where a large value occurs.
In limit inferior and limit superior, an​=(−1)n(1+1/n) has limit superior one because its positive branch approaches one from above and all later values eventually stay below 1+ε. Changing any finite number of terms leaves the limit superior unchanged: once the tail of a sequence begins beyond those positions, all its suprema are the same as before. The sparse peaks can determine a limit superior example shows that even a set of peak indices with zero natural density can decide the upper long-run edge.
