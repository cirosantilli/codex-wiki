Bose condensation in a three-dimensional harmonic trap (kB​Tc​=ℏωˉ[N/ζ(3)]1/3)

OurBigBook.com Words: 537 Articles: 5
Consider N noninteracting bosons in one internal quantum state, at thermal equilibrium in the three-dimensional harmonic oscillator potential
V(r)=2m​(ωx2​x2+ωy2​y2+ωz2​z2),ωˉ=(ωx​ωy​ωz​)1/3.
(34)
Here m is the mass, the positive ωi​ are angular frequencies, and ωˉ is their geometric mean. Measure the one-particle energy ϵ and chemical potential μ from the ground-state energy. The quantum harmonic oscillator levels then have ϵ=ℏ(ωx​nx​+ωy​ny​+ωz​nz​) with nonnegative integer ni​ and reduced Planck constant ℏ=h/(2π).
When ϵ≫ℏωi​, counting lattice points can be approximated by the volume of a tetrahedron in the positive octant of (nx​,ny​,nz​):
N(ϵ)≃6ℏ3ωx​ωy​ωz​ϵ3​,g(ϵ)=dϵdN​≃2(ℏωˉ)3ϵ2​.
(35)
This is a density of states, with units of inverse energy. The factor 1/6 is the unit tetrahedron volume; differentiating produces the factor 1/2. Treat the single ground state separately: its macroscopic population would be lost by replacing every level with a smooth density of states.
For kB​T≫ℏωi​, integrate the Bose-Einstein distribution over excited energy eigenstates. As μ approaches zero from below, their maximum population is
Nexsat​(T)=∫0∞​eϵ/(kB​T)−1g(ϵ)dϵ​=ζ(3)(ℏωˉkB​T​)3.
(36)
Here kB​ is the Boltzmann constant and ζ the Riemann zeta function. A short proof uses 1/(ex−1)=∑ℓ≥1​e−ℓx and ∫0∞​x2e−ℓxdx=2/ℓ3. The nonnegative terms permit exchanging summation and integration by Tonelli's theorem, giving ∫0∞​x2/(ex−1)dx=2ζ(3). Near zero the integrand is proportional to x, so its integral converges. That finite excited-state capacity is the mechanism of Bose-Einstein condensation.
Set Nexsat​(Tc​)=N. Particle conservation gives, to this continuum accuracy,
kB​Tc​=ℏωˉ(ζ(3)N​)1/3,NN0​​=1−(Tc​T​)3(0≤T≤Tc​).
(37)
Above Tc​, the macroscopic condensate fraction is zero and the chemical potential adjusts below zero. This cubic power belongs to a three-dimensional harmonic trap; it is not the homogeneous three-dimensional ideal gas exponent. For N=105, ωˉ/(2π)=100Hz and ζ(3)≃1.202056903, the model gives Tc​≃209.5nK. At T=0.8Tc​≃167.6nK, N0​/N=0.488, or approximately 48800 condensed bosons. These are chosen parameters and calculated values.
Finite N gives a smooth crossover rather than a sharp phase transition. The exact ideal gas sum over excited quantum harmonic oscillator levels differs from the continuum integral, especially when kB​T is not large relative to every ℏωi​. A sharp trapped-gas limit takes N→∞, ωˉ→0 with Nωˉ3 fixed and fixed frequency ratios. Interactions also alter the critical temperature, condensate fraction and release profile; a dilute gas can have appreciable interaction energy compared with the trap level spacing. The cubic law is a useful baseline, not a fit guaranteed for either 1995 experiment.
