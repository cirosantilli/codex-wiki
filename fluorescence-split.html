Fluorescence

 Home Physics Branches of physics Optics Physical optics
OurBigBook.com Words: 4k Articles: 52
Fluorescence is photon emission following photon absorption by matter: an excited state releases some of its energy as light. A fluorophore can therefore mark where something is, while its fluorescence emission spectrum and fluorescence lifetime report different aspects of its environment. Fluorescein is one example; FRET exploits a competing route for excitation energy rather than requiring a second emitted photon.
The usual molecular sequence is excitation, relaxation, then emission, as in the Jablonski diagram. The emitted photon commonly has lower energy and longer wavelength, giving a Stokes shift; the exceptions are discussed below.[ref]
