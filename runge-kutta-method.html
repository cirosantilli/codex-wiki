Runge-Kutta method

 Home Mathematics Area of mathematics Analysis Numerical analysis
OurBigBook.com Words: 3k Articles: 40
A Runge-Kutta method advances an initial value problem y′=f(t,y), y(t0​)=y0​, by sampling its vector field at several trial states within one time step. These samples are Runge-Kutta stages; a weighted combination of their slopes produces the next state. The construction improves on the single tangent line used by the Euler method while requiring evaluations of f, rather than explicit higher derivatives of the solution. It applies to scalar equations and systems of ordinary differential equations alike. The coefficient choices distinguish methods such as Heun's method, the classical fourth-order Runge-Kutta method and implicit Runge-Kutta methods.
For s stages, write A=(aij​) for the stage matrix, ci​ for relative time nodes and bi​ for output weights. A time step of length h computes
Yi​=yn​+h∑j=1s​aij​kj​,ki​=f(tn​+ci​h,Yi​),yn+1​=yn​+h∑i=1s​bi​ki​.
(1)
Here ki​ is a slope, with units of state per time; hki​ is a state increment. Another common convention writes Ki​=hki​, so its stage states are Yi​=yn​+∑j​aij​Kj​ and its output is yn​+∑i​bi​Ki​. These are equivalent conventions: including h both inside Ki​ and again in the output would count the time step twice. The textbook formulation uses the increment convention.[ref]
Usually the Butcher tableau obeys ci​=∑j​aij​, called internal consistency. Stage i then combines slopes over an effective time ci​h. The second-order conditions with independent Runge-Kutta abscissae explain what changes when time nodes are specified independently. A Runge-Kutta stage is generally an auxiliary approximation, not a separate accepted solution value at its node. Several stages can even have the same time node but different state arguments, as in the classical fourth-order Runge-Kutta method.
The useful idea is to approximate the integral y(tn​+h)−y(tn​)=∫tn​tn​+h​f(t,y(t))dt without knowing the intermediate exact trajectory. Trial states provide places to sample f, and Butcher order conditions make the resulting combination agree with the exact Taylor expansion. More stages alone do not guarantee a higher order of a numerical method: their coefficients must satisfy the appropriate conditions. Embedded Runge-Kutta pairs reuse these same samples for a second output and a practical local error estimator.

Runge-Kutta stage (ki​=f(tn​+ci​h,Yi​))

Words: 70
Runge-Kutta method OurBigBook.com
A Runge-Kutta stage consists of a trial state Yi​ at the time node tn​+ci​h and the corresponding vector field evaluation ki​. In the slope convention, Yi​=yn​+h∑j​aij​kj​. Stages belong to the construction of one Runge-Kutta method step; they need not have the same accuracy as its final output. Stage states can leave the physically allowed region even when the endpoint lies inside it, so f must be defined at every stage used.

Explicit Runge-Kutta method

Words: 259 Articles: 1
Runge-Kutta method OurBigBook.com
An explicit Runge-Kutta method has aij​=0 whenever j≥i. Its stage matrix is strictly lower triangular: compute each Runge-Kutta stage using already known slopes, starting with Y1​=yn​. In contrast, an implicit Runge-Kutta method requires solving coupled stage equations, whose existence is addressed by stage solvability of an implicit Runge-Kutta method.

Heun's method (yn+1​=yn​+h(k1​+k2​)/2)

Words: 219
Explicit Runge-Kutta method OurBigBook.com
Here Heun's method means the explicit endpoint-averaging Runge-Kutta method: take an Euler predictor Y2​=yn​+hf(tn​,yn​), sample k2​=f(tn​+h,Y2​) there, and average it with k1​=f(tn​,yn​) for the output. The second Runge-Kutta stage is evaluated at the predictor, not at the corrected endpoint. In particular it requires no implicit solve as the ODE trapezoidal rule does. Naming conventions vary between books; the formulas specify the method unambiguously.[ref]
For smooth f, a Taylor expansion about (tn​,yn​) gives k2​=f+h(ft​+fy​f)+O(h2), where fy​ is the state Jacobian matrix. Consequently the output is yn​+hf+h2(ft​+fy​f)/2+O(h3), matching the exact solution through degree two by the chain rule. This gives local truncation error O(h3) and, with the usual bounded smoothness and Lipschitz continuity hypotheses on a fixed finite interval, global error O(h2). The Euler-Heun embedded pair compares this output with its Euler predictor.
For y′=y, yn​=1 and h=0.2, the sampled slopes are 1 and 1.2. Transporting the second slope to the starting point would end at 1.24; the average slope 1.1 instead ends at 1.22. The exact exponential function reaches e0.2≈1.22140275816. The magnified endpoint comparison separates the pair difference 0.02 from the actual Heun local defect 0.00140275816.

Maximal-order Runge-Kutta methods are A-stable (p=2s⟹A-stability)

Words: 78
Runge-Kutta method OurBigBook.com
An s-stage Runge-Kutta method has stability function R(z)=1+zbT(I−zA)−11, a rational function with numerator and denominator degrees at most s. Order 2s forces R(z)−ez=O(z2s+1), so uniqueness of the diagonal Padé approximant identifies R with [s/s]ez​. That approximant has no poles in the closed left half-plane and modulus at most one there. Hence every such maximal-order method is A-stable. Since the Padé approximant numerator and denominator are coprime and both have degree s, the original stage determinant cannot have hidden canceled poles in that half-plane.

Theta method (yn+1​−yn​=h[(1−a)f(yn​)+af(yn+1​)])

Words: 155 Articles: 2
Runge-Kutta method OurBigBook.com
This one-step family interpolates between the Forward Euler method at a=0, the trapezoidal rule at a=1/2, and the Backward Euler method at a=1. Its stability function is R(z)=[1+(1−a)z]/[1−az]. It is first order except at one half, where it is second order. Applying it to spatial diffusion gives the theta diffusion stability criterion.

Theta diffusion stability criterion (G(s)=[1−(1−a)s]/[1+as],s≥0)

Words: 112 Articles: 1
Theta method OurBigBook.com
On an orthogonal diffusion eigenmode, the theta method has the displayed amplification with s=−Δtλ. The identity (1+as)2−[1−(1−a)s]2=2s+(2a−1)s2 proves contraction at all nonnegative s exactly when a≥1/2. For a<1/2, contraction holds up to s≤2/(1−2a), with a positive denominator in that range. Rough-data damping distinction for the theta method (G(s)→−(1−a)/a(s→∞, a>0))

Words: 58
Theta diffusion stability criterion OurBigBook.com
At a=1/2, arbitrarily stiff heat modes approach amplification −1: the Crank-Nicolson method is contractive but does not rapidly damp unresolved rough components. The Backward Euler method instead has limit zero. Thus unconditional linear stability and strong stiff decay are different requirements. High smooth-data consistency order alone does not imply a uniform high-order estimate down to the initial instant for arbitrary L2 initial conditions.

Strong stability preserving Runge-Kutta method

Words: 113
Runge-Kutta method OurBigBook.com
A strong stability preserving Runge-Kutta method writes its stages as convex combinations of previously computed states and suitable Forward Euler method steps. It transfers any convex-functional nonincrease property of the forward step, such as a norm bound, under a proportionally scaled time-step restriction.
For example, if Ek​(U)=U+kF(U) is nonexpansive in a norm, then Y=Ek​(U) and Unew​=(U+Ek​(Y))/2 is also nonexpansive: the triangle inequality bounds the new difference by one half of the initial difference plus one half of the twice-advanced difference. Both are at most the initial difference. A Taylor expansion yields Unew​=U+kF(U)+k2F′(U)F(U)/2+O(k3), so this is a second-order Runge-Kutta method. The forward-step hypothesis must hold on the stage states; preservation of one chosen convex bound is not the same as unconditional B-stability.

Butcher tableau

Words: 98
Runge-Kutta method OurBigBook.com
A Butcher tableau records a Runge-Kutta method's stage matrix A, time nodes c, and output weights b in the block array
c​AbT​​.
(3)
Read row i above the line as the recipe for Runge-Kutta stage i: the left entry gives tn​+ci​h, and the right entries give the coefficients in Yi​=yn​+h∑j​aij​kj​. Below the line, bi​ weights slope ki​ in the final update. Zeros on and above the diagonal identify an Explicit Runge-Kutta method. For the internally consistent convention, row sums of A equal c; first-order consistency of a numerical method also requires ∑i​bi​=1. An embedded Runge-Kutta pair adds another weight row while retaining A,c.

Butcher order condition

Words: 139 Articles: 2
Runge-Kutta method OurBigBook.com
A Butcher order condition equates the coefficient assigned by a Runge-Kutta method to a rooted tree with the corresponding exact Taylor coefficient. Satisfying every condition through tree order p is equivalent to Runge–Kutta order at least p.

Stage solvability of an implicit Runge-Kutta method

Words: 64
Implicit Runge-Kutta method OurBigBook.com
For a vector field uniformly Lipschitz continuous in the state with constant L, the stage fixed-point map of an implicit Runge-Kutta method is a contraction in the maximum stage norm if hLmaxi​∑j​∣aij​∣<1. The contraction mapping theorem then gives unique stages. This is a sufficient small-step condition; a dissipative vector field may permit solvability for larger steps. An algebraic stability or B-stability estimate compares existing stage solutions and should not be mistaken for an unqualified stage-existence theorem.

Contractivity of split Crank-Nicolson diffusion (∥CA​(h)CB​(h)∥2​≤1)

Words: 72
Crank-Nicolson method OurBigBook.com
For symmetric negative semidefinite matrices A,B, each factor CA​(h)=(I−hA/2)−1(I+hA/2) has norm at most one, since its eigenvalues are (1+hλ/2)/(1−hλ/2) with λ≤0. The same holds for B, so their ordered product is a contraction for every h≥0, whether or not A,B commute. This proves unconditional discrete L2 norm stability of the split Crank-Nicolson method for directional diffusion. Its splitting error remains first order in general, because the product's quadratic defect is h2[A,B]/2.

Collocation order theorem (p=s+k)

Words: 191 Articles: 1
Collocation Runge-Kutta method OurBigBook.com
For s distinct collocation nodes, let πs​(t)=∏i=1s​(t−ci​). If ∫01​tjπs​(t)dt=0 for 0≤j<k, and the next moment is nonzero, the associated interpolatory quadrature has order s+k, and the collocation Runge-Kutta method has the same classical order for sufficiently smooth differential equations. Here 0≤k≤s. Equivalently, if the quadrature is exact through degree p−1 but not degree p, the method has order p. The generic order is at least s, Gauss collocation methods reach 2s, and Radau IIA methods reach 2s−1.
Collocation endpoint superconvergence by quadrature orthogonality (p=q)
Words: 111
Collocation order theorem OurBigBook.com
For s collocation nodes, the residual of the collocation polynomial vanishes at each node and contains the factor ω(τ)=∏i​(τ−ci​). Quadrature exactness through degree q−1 implies ∫01​ω(τ)p(τ)dτ=0 for every polynomial of degree at most q−s−1. Expand the smooth vector-field interpolation error and the derivative of the exact flow in powers of the step. Every endpoint-error term of degree below q has this vanishing weighted moment, so the endpoint local error is O(hq+1), although the interior stage accuracy is generally only s. A step Lipschitz estimate and discrete Gronwall inequality then give global order q. Gaussian nodes attain q=2s, while the first nonexact quadrature moment bounds the order from above.

Padé identification of a Radau stability function (R(z)=[s−1/s]ez​)
Words: 109
Radau IIA method OurBigBook.com
Suppose an s-stage Runge-Kutta method has order 2s−1, an invertible matrix A, and bTA−11=1. Its stability function is R(z)=1+zbT(I−zA)−11. The adjugate matrix formula gives a rational function with denominator degree s and numerator degree at most s; its zero limit at infinity lowers the latter degree to at most s−1. The order of a numerical method gives R(z)−ez=O(z2s), identifying the [s−1/s] Padé approximant. Uniqueness follows because the cross-multiplied difference of two candidates has degree at most 2s−1 but a zero of order at least 2s, so is identically zero. The A-stability of near-diagonal exponential Padé approximants then proves A-stability, and the zero limit proves L-stability. These conditions identify the stability function; they do not alone claim uniqueness of all entries of the Runge-Kutta method tableau.

Runge-Kutta conservation of quadratic invariants (bi​aij​+bj​aji​=bi​bj​)

For stage states Yi​=yn​+h∑j​aij​kj​ and a vector field with YiT​ki​=0, expansion of the new squared norm gives ∥yn+1​∥2−∥yn​∥2=−h2∑i,j​Mij​kiT​kj​, where Mij​=bi​aij​+bj​aji​−bi​bj​. Butcher contractivity theorem
Words: 77
Algebraic stability of a Runge-Kutta method OurBigBook.com
An algebraically stable Runge-Kutta method is B-stable: for a dissipative vector field, a well-defined step cannot increase the distance between two starting values. This assumes that the compared stage equations have solutions, as required by stage solvability of an implicit Runge-Kutta method. The Runge-Kutta contractivity identity expresses the distance change as a nonpositive weighted term coming from the dissipative vector field minus a nonnegative quadratic form involving the matrix defining algebraic stability. Positive weights also rule out stage poles in the open left half-plane on the scalar test equation, giving A-stability there; zero-weight redundant stages need separate solvability attention.
Runge-Kutta contractivity identity
Words: 75
Algebraic stability of a Runge-Kutta method OurBigBook.com
For differences Di​ between corresponding stages and differences Fi​ between their vector fields, a Runge--Kutta step satisfies
∥dn+1​∥2=∥dn​∥2+2h∑i​bi​Re⟨Di​,Fi​⟩−h2∑i,j​mij​Re⟨Fi​,Fj​⟩.
(10)
To derive the identity, expand the squared norm of dn+1​=dn​+h∑i​bi​Fi​ and substitute dn​=Di​−h∑j​aij​Fj​ in the linear term. Symmetrizing the double sum gives the displayed coefficients mij​. For a dissipative vector field, the first sum is nonpositive when bi​≥0, and the second sum is nonnegative when the matrix (mij​) is a positive semidefinite matrix. Thus algebraic stability implies B-stability.

Trapezoidal rule (T(f)=(b−a)(f(a)+f(b))/2)

Words: 468 Articles: 2
Implicit Runge-Kutta method OurBigBook.com
As a quadrature rule, the trapezoidal rule replaces an integrand by the linear interpolation joining its two endpoint values:
I(f)=∫ab​f(x)dx≈T(f)=2b−a​(f(a)+f(b)),a<b.
(11)
For nonnegative endpoint values, this is the area of a trapezoid; in general it is a signed area. The quadrature nodes are a,b, each with quadrature weight (b−a)/2. Unlike the midpoint quadrature rule, it uses endpoint samples, which can be reused between adjacent panels.
Every affine function f(x)=αx+β agrees with its linear interpolation, so the trapezoidal rule integrates it exactly. On [0,2], f(x)=2x+1 gives T(f)=(2/2)(1+5)=6=I(f). On [0,1], the polynomial f(x)=x2 instead gives T(f)=1/2, exceeding I(f)=1/3 by 1/6. Its degree of precision of a quadrature rule is exactly one.
If f∈C2[a,b], its signed approximation error satisfies
T(f)−I(f)=12(b−a)3​f′′(ξT​),a<ξT​<b.
(12)
A convex function lies below its endpoint chord, so the trapezoidal rule overestimates its integral; a concave function gives the reverse inequality. The error formula assumes that the second derivative exists and is a continuous function throughout the closed interval.[ref] A short derivation is the identity
T(f)−I(f)=21​∫ab​(t−a)(b−t)f′′(t)dt,
(13)
obtained by two integrations by parts. The nonnegative Peano kernel has integral (b−a)3/12, and the weighted mean value theorem for integrals gives the formula.
The trapezoidal rule for an ordinary differential equation averages vector fields at the old and new states and is A-stable.
This time-stepping use applies the same trapezoidal rule to the integral of the derivative in an ordinary differential equation. That connection explains its place under implicit Runge-Kutta methods; its quadrature behavior and the nonlinear issue in trapezoidal rule fails B-stability concern different uses of the same endpoint averaging.

On an equal-panel partition of an interval, with h=(b−a)/n, sum the trapezoidal rule over adjacent panels:
Tn​(f)=h[2f(a)​+∑j=1n−1​f(a+jh)+2f(b)​].
(14)
There are n+1 distinct quadrature nodes. Each interior sample belongs to two panels and therefore has quadrature weight h, while each endpoint has quadrature weight h/2. Equivalently, the composite trapezoidal rule integrates the piecewise linear function obtained by linear interpolation exactly.[ref]

for some ξ∈(a,b). Thus panel quadrature errors of order O(h3) accumulate to order O(h2), using big O notation.[ref] On a nonuniform partition of an interval with widths hj​ and largest width H, the bound is K∑j​hj3​/12≤K(b−a)H2/12.

Trapezoidal rule fails B-stability

Words: 53
Trapezoidal rule OurBigBook.com
The trapezoidal rule is A-stable but is not B-stable. For the dissipative vector field f(y)=−y3 at step size one, its uniquely defined step map T obeys T+T3/2=y−y3/2. At y=2​, T=0 and implicit differentiation gives T′=−2, so nearby states expand rather than contract. This is a concrete nonlinear obstruction, rather than merely failure of the sufficient algebraic stability criterion.

A one-step numerical method with step map Φh​ is time symmetric when Φ−h​=Φh−1​. Its leading local-error power is odd, so every nontrivial consistent time-symmetric method has even order.

Third-order conditions for an explicit Runge-Kutta method

Order of a Runge-Kutta method OurBigBook.com
For an explicit Runge-Kutta method with coefficients A,b, put c=A1 and let c2 mean componentwise squaring. The conditions bT1=1, bTc=1/2, bTc2=1/3 and bTAc=1/6 match the exact flow expansion through degree three: the two cubic differentials are f′′[f,f] and (f′)2f. For sufficiently smooth vector fields these give local error O(h4) and, under the usual finite-interval Lipschitz continuity assumptions, global error O(h3).
