Quantronium Ramsey interference experiment

The 2002 experiment described in Manipulating the Quantum State of an Electrical Circuit demonstrates Ramsey interferometry in a circuit using superconductivity. An aluminum island between two small Josephson junctions and a larger readout Josephson junction formed the loop; a dilution refrigerator cooled it. Manipulation used the charge/flux bias point least sensitive to first-order fluctuations. Microwave pulse amplitude and duration were calibrated using Rabi oscillations. Two phase-coherent π/2 pulses then enclosed a variable free interval. A current pulse switched the large Josephson junction to finite voltage with state-dependent probability; a preamplifier and threshold discriminator counted repeated trials.[ref]
As a readout control, switching was tested against pulse height at thermal equilibrium. The Ramsey interferometry oscillation period agreed with inverse frequency detuning, supporting relative quantum phase accumulation against the external oscillator. The decaying envelope indicated finite quantum coherence, while reduced contrast was attributed provisionally to population relaxation during readout. This supports controlled coherent evolution over a finite interval, without establishing perfect isolation or identifying one exclusive noise mechanism.[ref]
