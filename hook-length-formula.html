Hook-length formula (fλ=n!/∏(i,j)∈Y(λ)​hi,j​(λ))

 ... Representation theory of the symmetric group Partition of an integer Young tableau Tabloid Polytabloid Specht module
OurBigBook.com Words: 610 Articles: 5
For any partition of an integer λ⊢n, the number fλ of standard Young tableaux of shape λ is
fλ=∣SYT(λ)∣=(i,j)∈Y(λ)∏​hi,j​(λ)n!​.
(48)
The numerator is a factorial, and the denominator is the hook product of a partition, using every cell's hook length once. The empty shape has one empty standard Young tableau, and the formula still holds with 0!=1 and an empty product equal to one. By the standard polytabloid basis, fλ is also the dimension of the Specht module Sλ over the complex numbers, or equivalently the degree χλ(1) of its irreducible character. For a teaching derivation of this dimension formula, see Etingof et al., Introduction to Representation Theory, §5.17, Theorem 5.17.1.
In the Young diagram of (3,2), the hook length array [[4,3,1],[2,1]] gives
H(3,2)​=4⋅3⋅1⋅2⋅1=24,f(3,2)=245!​=24120​=5=dimC​S(3,2).
(49)
This agrees with the five explicit fillings in standard Young tableau. Its conjugate partition (2,2,1) has the same hook lengths after transposition, hence also gives dimension five. At the extremes, for n≥1, a single row (n) and a single column (1n) each have hook product n! and exactly one standard Young tableau; their Specht modules over the complex numbers are the trivial representation and sign representation, respectively.
Why the formula is beautiful: global compatibility of all the row and column inequalities reduces to a product of local hook lengths. It is useful when enumeration is already expensive: computing one hook length per cell replaces testing up to n! numbered Young tableaux. For (3,2), the proportion of the 120 bijective fillings that are standard Young tableaux is 5/120=1/24, the reciprocal of the hook product. This product does not justify treating overlapping hook conditions as independent; Grinberg explains that tempting but invalid argument in §1.3 of his teaching slides.
Historically, the modern hook-product statement was published by J. S. Frame, G. de B. Robinson and R. M. Thrall in 1954: [ref]. Their manuscript was received on 1 September 1953, and their proof reorganizes an earlier irreducible character degree formula into the product of the hook lengths. The theorem unifies the counting and representation viewpoints developed earlier by Young. For proof routes within this wiki, see trace computation of Specht module dimension, determinant formula for Specht module dimension, and Greene–Nijenhuis–Wilf hook walk.
