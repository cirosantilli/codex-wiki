Incircle of a triangle (r=Δ/s)

 Home Mathematics Area of mathematics Geometry and topology Euclidean geometry Triangle
OurBigBook.com Words: 93
The incircle of a triangle is the circle inside the triangle tangent to all three sides. Its center is the intersection of two internal angle bisectors; its radius is r=Δ/s, where Δ is the area and s the semiperimeter. To prove it is the largest contained circle, let any candidate have center P, radius ρ and distances di​≥ρ from the three side lines. Splitting the triangle into three triangles with vertex P gives Δ=21​∑i​ℓi​di​≥sρ. The tangent circle attains equality, proving maximality. For a right triangle with legs a,b and hypotenuse c, r=(a+b−c)/2, since r=ab/(a+b+c) and a2+b2=c2.
