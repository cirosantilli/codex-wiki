Inverse derivative of an exponential coordinate map (f(x,y)=(ex+ey,ex+e−y))

 ... Real analysis Calculus Multivariable calculus Differentiable map Inverse function theorem Derivative of a local inverse map
OurBigBook.com Words: 82
This map is a diffeomorphism from R2 onto V={(s,t):s>0,t>0,st>1}. Writing q=ex, the inverse problem becomes (s−q)(t−q)=1 with 0<q<min(s,t). The left side decreases strictly from st to zero, proving existence and uniqueness exactly on V. Its Jacobian determinant is −ex(ey+e−y)=0, so the inverse function theorem supplies a differentiable inverse. Along (x,y)=(c,c), applying the inverse Jacobian to (1/2,1/2) gives (e−c/2,0). Establishing the actual image matters before using a global inverse, even when a local inverse already exists at the point of interest.
