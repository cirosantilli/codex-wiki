Local quadratic convergence of Newton iteration (∣er+1​∣≤2mM​∣er​∣2)

 Home Mathematics Area of mathematics Analysis Numerical analysis Newton root-finding iteration
OurBigBook.com Words: 126
Let F be twice continuously differentiable on an interval around a zero of a function t∗​, with ∣F′(t)∣≥m>0 and ∣F′′(t)∣≤M there. Choose a closed interval [t∗​−ρ,t∗​+ρ] inside that interval with Mρ/(2m)<1. Starting inside it makes the Newton root-finding iteration well defined, keeps it inside, and gives the displayed quadratic convergence bound for er​=tr​−t∗​. The nonzero derivative and sufficiently close start are essential hypotheses. The scalar estimate follows from the Taylor remainder argument in MIT's root-finding notes.[ref]
For a short proof outline, apply Taylor's theorem between tr​ and t∗​; subtract the defining tangent line equation. The linear error cancels, leaving er+1​=F′′(ξr​)er2​/(2F′(tr​)). The displayed bound shrinks the error and preserves the chosen interval. If F′′(t∗​)=0, the error ratio tends to ∣F′′(t∗​)/(2F′(t∗​))∣; if it vanishes, convergence can be faster.
