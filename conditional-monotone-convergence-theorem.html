Conditional monotone convergence theorem (Yn​↑Y⟹E[Yn​∣G]↑E[Y∣G])

 ... Mathematics Area of mathematics Analysis Real analysis Measure theory Conditional expectation
OurBigBook.com Words: 114
For nonnegative random variables Yn​↑Y, the corresponding nonnegative conditional expectations increase almost surely to E[Y∣G], allowing the extended value +∞. When Y is an integrable random variable, all conditional expectations can be taken in their ordinary finite sense.
To prove this integrable case, choose versions simultaneously ordered outside a single null set, and put V=limn​E[Yn​∣G]. This limit is G-measurable. For every A∈G, the ordinary monotone convergence theorem twice gives
E[1A​V]=limn​E[1A​E[Yn​∣G]]=limn​E[1A​Yn​]=E[1A​Y].
(24)
In particular EV=EY<∞, and this is exactly the defining property of conditional expectation. For a general nonnegative Y, define its extended conditional expectation by increasing bounded truncations; the same nonnegative integral argument proves the statement.
