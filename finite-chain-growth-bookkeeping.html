Finite chain-growth bookkeeping (Xn​=S/C)

 Home Chemistry Polymer Polymerization Chain-growth polymerization
OurBigBook.com Words: 124
Stipulate a feed of exactly M0​=40 monomer molecules, four successfully initiated linear chains, one polymerization-active site per chain, no chain transfer in chain polymerization, no loss of incorporated units, and no further chain-growth initiation. Count only initiated chains in C; exclude unreacted monomers. Assume each consumed monomer molecule contributes exactly one monomeric unit. With incorporated feed fraction q=S/M0​, the number-average degree of polymerization is the counting identity
Xn​=C∑i=1C​ji​​=CS​=CqM0​​.
(13)
This is conditional conservation bookkeeping, not a universal empirical law. Immediately after chain-growth initiation, the four lengths are (1,1,1,1), so (C,A,S)=(4,4,4) and q=0.1. Stipulate twelve subsequent chain-growth propagation events, three on each chain: the lengths become (4,4,4,4), giving (C,A,S)=(4,4,16), q=0.4 and Xn​=4. Sixteen units have been incorporated, while twenty-four free monomer molecules remain. The chain-length assignment is deliberately chosen, not a prediction of equal growth in an experiment.
