For an original illustrative readout model, take detected photon count K in a fixed exposure to have a Poisson distribution, with stipulated means λB​=12 for a bright state and λD​=0.2 for a dark state. Classify K≥3 as bright. Direct summation gives

With equally likely preparations, the average misclassification probability is 0.0008354. These numbers describe only the chosen count model. Actual fluorescence includes short-time photon correlations, detector effects and possible state changes; it need not follow a fixed-rate Poisson process. Longer exposure can improve separation of count distributions while increasing unwanted optical pumping, metastable decay or recoil.
If the prepared bright population is p, let α and β denote the two conditional errors above. The observed bright-label probability is q=(1−α)p+β(1−p), hence p=(q−β)/(1−α−β) when the denominator is nonzero. Known-state controls must estimate α,β in the actual apparatus; the formula cannot correct unknown drift. For M independent preparations, the raw bright fraction has variance q(1−q)/M. Counting uncertainty, preparation error, pulse error and state-detection bias therefore enter different parts of the inference. Repeating a biased measurement reduces its sampling uncertainty without removing its bias.
