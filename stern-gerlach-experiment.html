Stern-Gerlach experiment

 Home Physics Branches of physics Quantum mechanics Angular momentum operator Spin
OurBigBook.com Words: 2k Articles: 5
A Stern-Gerlach experiment separates atomic beams according to a component of their magnetic moment in a nonuniform magnetic field. For an appropriate two-state spin system, calibrated outputs distinguish the two spin eigenvalues; Stern-Gerlach expectation estimation explains how repeated fresh preparations turn channel counts into an expectation value.
The decisive contrast is between two separated components of an atomic beam and the continuous spread predicted for randomly oriented classical magnetic moments. Neither component need be an infinitely narrow spot: velocity spread and the resolving power of the scientific instrument affect the spatial image. The apparatus converts an internal magnetic moment into a displacement large enough to inspect, connecting angular momentum eigenstates to macroscopic evidence.
