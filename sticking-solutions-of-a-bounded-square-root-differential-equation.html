Sticking solutions of a bounded square-root differential equation (v′=1−v2​,∣v∣≤1)

OurBigBook.com Words: 126
For the autonomous differential equation v′=1−v2​, the real solutions are nondecreasing and lie in [−1,1]. On each nonconstant interval, separation of variables gives arcsinv=t−C, so the solution crosses from −1 to 1 in exactly time π. Every nonconstant solution on the entire real time axis is consequently
v(t)=⎩⎨⎧​−1,−cos(t−a),1,​t≤a,a<t<a+π,t≥a+π,​
(34)
for some real a. The two constant solutions v≡±1 complete the list; solutions on shorter intervals are restrictions of these. At the joins both the derivative and the right-hand side are zero, so these are continuously differentiable functions. The right-hand side is continuous but fails Lipschitz continuity at both endpoints, allowing arbitrary waiting time at −1 before departure. Once v reaches 1, monotonicity forces it to stay there. The sine representation without the nonnegative-cosine restriction would incorrectly introduce decreasing pieces.
