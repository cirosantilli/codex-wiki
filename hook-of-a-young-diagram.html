Hook of a Young diagram (Hi,j​(λ))

 ... Area of mathematics Algebra Representation theory Representation theory of the symmetric group Partition of an integer Young diagram
OurBigBook.com Words: 643 Articles: 9
In the English convention of Young diagram, the hook at a cell (i,j) consists of that cell itself, all cells to its right in the same row, and all cells below it in the same column:
Hi,j​(λ)={(i,j)}∪{(i,k):j<k≤λi​}∪{(k,j):i<k≤λj′​}.
(16)
Here λ′ is the conjugate partition. The cells to the right form the arm of a Young-diagram cell, and those below form the leg of a Young-diagram cell. Both extend all the way to the edge of the Young diagram, rather than containing just the immediate neighbors. The three pieces are disjoint, so their total cardinality is one plus the two lengths.
For (3,2), the highlighted Young-diagram hook is H1,2​={(1,2),(1,3),(2,2)}, of hook length three. The hook at (1,1) is {(1,1),(1,2),(1,3),(2,1)}, of hook length four: the southeast cell (2,2) is excluded because it is in neither the anchor's row nor its column. These local shapes are useful because their hook lengths give the denominator of the hook-length formula.
