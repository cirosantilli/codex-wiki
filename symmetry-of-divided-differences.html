Symmetry of divided differences (f[xσ(0)​,…,xσ(k)​]=f[x0​,…,xk​])

 ... Area of mathematics Analysis Numerical analysis Interpolation Polynomial interpolation Divided difference
OurBigBook.com Words: 146
A divided difference depends on the chosen interpolation nodes and their data, but is unchanged by any permutation σ of those same interpolation nodes. The explicit sum in divided difference makes this visible: a permutation merely rearranges its summands.[ref]
There is also a short explanation using Lagrange cardinal polynomials. The coefficient of xk in the jth Lagrange cardinal polynomial is 1/∏i=j​(xj​−xi​). Taking that coefficient in their weighted sum gives exactly the explicit divided difference formula. Since polynomial interpolation has a unique answer for a fixed set of distinct interpolation nodes, this coefficient cannot depend on their ordering. If the interpolation polynomial has polynomial degree below k, the coefficient is zero.
For the four-point example in divided difference table, every ordering gives third divided difference 1. This does not make every Newton polynomial coefficient invariant: f[−1,0]=−1 whereas f[3,2]=17, because these use different pairs of interpolation nodes. Even at second order, f[−1,0,2]=1 and f[3,2,0]=5 use different triples. The node order in Newton interpolation determines which subsets occur along the top row.
