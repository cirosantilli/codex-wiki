Conditional mutual information (I(X;Y∣Z))

 Home Mathematics Area of mathematics Probability and statistics Information theory Mutual information
OurBigBook.com Words: 575 Articles: 2
For finite discrete random variables, conditional mutual information measures the average reduction in uncertainty about X from observing Y, when Z is already known. With base-two logarithms, it is measured in bits:
I(X;Y∣Z)=H(X∣Z)−H(X∣Y,Z)=H(X∣Z)+H(Y∣Z)−H(X,Y∣Z).
(25)
The second expression follows from the chain rule for conditional entropy and makes the symmetry in X,Y visible. Equivalently, compute the mutual information separately in each conditional probability distribution and then average:
I(X;Y∣Z)​=z:pZ​(z)>0∑​pZ​(z)I(X;Y∣Z=z)=z:pZ​(z)>0∑​pZ​(z)D(pX,Y∣z​​pX∣z​pY∣z​)=x,y,z:p(x,y,z)>0∑​p(x,y,z)log2​p(x∣z)p(y∣z)p(x,y∣z)​.​
(26)
Here D is the Kullback-Leibler divergence, also computed with base-two logarithms; zero-probability terms contribute zero. The Gibbs inequality gives I(X;Y∣Z)≥0, with equality exactly when X and Y have conditional independence given Z: the conditional joint probability mass function factors for every z of positive probability.[ref]
For a fully computed example, take independent random variables X,Y that are fair bits, and let Z=X⊕Y, where ⊕ is exclusive or. The complete joint probability distribution has only these four positive entries:

Every pair (x,y) has probability 1/4=pX​(x)pY​(y), so I(X;Y)=4(1/4)log2​1=0. But pZ​(0)=pZ​(1)=1/2. Given Z=0, the pairs (0,0),(1,1) each have conditional probability 1/2, while both conditional marginals are fair. Thus
I(X;Y∣Z=0)=21​log2​(1/2)(1/2)1/2​+21​log2​(1/2)(1/2)1/2​=1.
(27)
Given Z=1, the pairs (0,1),(1,0) give the same calculation. Therefore
I(X;Y∣Z)=21​⋅1+21​⋅1=1.
(28)
The conditional entropy calculation tells the same story: H(X∣Z)=1 because either value of Z leaves X fair, whereas H(X∣Y,Z)=0 because X=Y⊕Z. Learning the exclusive or does not reveal either bit alone; it reveals whether they agree, so learning one then determines the other.
This example distinguishes conditional independence from ordinary independence. It also disproves the claim that conditioning always reduces mutual information, despite conditioning reduces entropy. There is no general ordering between I(X;Y∣Z) and I(X;Y): the example above increases it from zero to one, while taking Y=X and Z=X for a fair bit decreases it from one to zero. The chain rule for mutual information explains why both are possible.[ref]
Conditional mutual information is useful for asking whether an additional observation tells us anything after information already available has been accounted for. Its zero case expresses conditional independence exactly; in a Markov chain, this is the step that turns the chain rule for mutual information into the data processing inequality.
