Angular sound-horizon degeneracy (θ∗​=rs​(z∗​)/DM​(z∗​),ℓA​=π/θ∗​)

 Home Physics Branches of physics Cosmology Physical cosmology
OurBigBook.com Words: 254
The angular sound horizon compares two lengths using the same distance convention. If DA​ is the angular diameter distance to last scattering at redshift z∗​, define the transverse comoving distance DM​=(1+z∗​)DA​. A comoving sound horizon rs​ has proper length rs​/(1+z∗​) at emission, so its angular size is θ∗​=rs​/DM​. Mixing a comoving ruler with DA​ would introduce an erroneous factor 1+z∗​. In a curved FLRW metric, DM​ is generally different from the radial comoving distance.
For a stipulated example, rs​=144Mpc and DM​=14000Mpc give θ∗​=0.010286 radians, or 0.589∘, and an acoustic scale ℓA​=305.4. Multiplying both lengths by 1.1 gives 158.4Mpc and 15400Mpc with exactly the same θ∗​ and ℓA​. This example establishes a ratio degeneracy, without claiming that any arbitrary rescaling corresponds to an allowed cosmological model. Equivalently,
δlnθ∗​=δlnrs​−δlnDM​.
(3)
Other observables are needed to separate changes that preserve the ratio.
The approximate adiabatic acoustic-peak spacing is ℓA​. The first observed temperature peak is shifted by gravitational driving and angular projection of density and Doppler acoustic sources; it need not occur at ℓA​. In particular, neither the illustrative 305.4 nor the BOOMERANG acoustic-peak measurement of 197±6 should be substituted for the other.
The cosmological baryon density enters through the baryon loading parameter and cosmological recombination, changing relative acoustic amplitudes as well as the sound horizon. The baryon-to-photon ratio counts net baryon number per photon, whereas θ∗​ is an angle. Constraining cosmological baryon density requires the pattern of relative peak heights within a model, with other parameters fitted jointly. Big Bang nucleosynthesis provides a separate abundance test. Baryon-density consistency between nucleosynthesis and the microwave background is consequently a test of one common cosmic history, rather than two direct measurements of the same angle. A first peak by itself cannot determine every component of the cosmological density parameter or uniquely fix spatial curvature of an FLRW universe.
