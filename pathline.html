Pathline (x˙=u(x,t))

 Home Physics Branches of physics Fluid mechanics
OurBigBook.com Words: 421
A pathline records the motion of one identified fluid element: keep its starting position a and starting time t0​ fixed, and let physical time t advance. In a velocity field u(x,t) its Lagrangian trajectory X(t;t0​,a) solves the initial value problem
dtdX​=u(X(t),t),X(t0​;t0​,a)=a.
(12)
A streamline instead freezes the time argument of the velocity field; a streakline fixes the observation time and varies the release time of many fluid elements. A pathline is useful for answering where a particular parcel goes and how long it takes, rather than merely which way the fluid is moving now. [ref]
For a concrete analytical model, take spatial coordinates x,y in metres, time t≥0 in seconds, and the spatially uniform but time-dependent velocity field
u(x,y,t)=(U,at),U=1 ms−1,a=1 ms−2.
(13)
Its numerical components in these units are (1,t). A fluid element starting at the origin at t0​=0 has the pathline
x(t)=Ut,y(t)=2at2​,y=2U2a​x2.
(14)
At t=1 s it is at (1,0.5) m; at t=2 s it is at (2,2) m. The parabola records earlier, shallower velocity directions as well as the final direction. Differentiating gives (x˙,y˙​)=(U,at) and acceleration (0,a), so this curved pathline needs no spatial variation of the velocity field. More generally the chain rule along a pathline gives df(X(t),t)/dt=∂t​f+u⋅∇f, the material derivative of a transported scalar field.
A motivating tracer demonstration appears in S. J. Kline's film notes: water passes through a contracting channel, and hydrogen bubbles mark a small element at a wire at time t0​. The illustrated element is shown again 4.7 s later, with its accumulated pathline superposed. Following that same marked element across successive film frames records a history; the entire bubble pattern in one frame has a different meaning. [ref]
For a modern version of the readout, use sparse small markers in a transparent water channel, illuminate the viewed plane, and keep an optical camera fixed with a ruler in that plane. Record timestamps and track the same isolated marker between frames; successive positions estimate its pathline and [X(t+Δt)−X(t)]/Δt estimates its velocity. A long exposure records a pathline segment but loses the timing within it, so its total length divided by exposure time gives only mean speed. These are proposed measurement steps based on Jean Hertzberg's teaching guidance, rather than additional measurements from Kline's film. Markers leaving the viewed plane, overlapping tracks and perspective can break particle identification; repeat with a thinner viewed slice and a lower marker concentration. [ref]
