Ceiling count for fixed-size batches (b=⌈N/k⌉)

 ... Area of mathematics Analysis Real analysis Calculus Floor and ceiling functions Ceiling function
OurBigBook.com Words: 174
Suppose N is a nonnegative integer count of items and each batch holds at most k items, where k is a positive integer. The smallest sufficient number of batches is
b=⌈kN​⌉.
(9)
Indeed, bk≥N exactly when the integer b is at least N/k. If N=0, no batches are needed. If N is a multiple of k, no partly filled batch is needed.
For 102 items with capacity 20 per batch, b=⌈5.1⌉=6. Five batches can be full and the sixth holds two items, leaving 6⋅20−102=18 unused places. This is a different question from the five complete groups counted by ⌊102/20⌋.
Writing the Euclidean division N=kq+r, 0≤r<k, gives the equivalent rule b=q when r=0, and b=q+1 when r>0. This explains the ceiling function's jump: one extra item beyond a complete multiple demands a whole additional batch. For exact integer arithmetic, the identity b=⌊(N+k−1)/k⌋ avoids a fractional intermediate value; an implementation still needs an integer representation large enough to hold N+k−1.
