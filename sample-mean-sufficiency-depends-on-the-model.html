For the normal sample sum with known variance, assume independent and identically distributed Xi​∼N(μ,σ2) with fixed known variance σ2>0. The joint probability density factors as

The Fisher-Neyman factorization theorem proves that the sum, and hence the sample mean, is a sufficient statistic for μ. Its remaining deviations Xi​−X have a joint Gaussian law independent of μ and are independent of X. If both μ and σ2 are unknown, the sample mean alone loses information about variance; the pair (∑i​Xi​,∑i​Xi2​) is sufficient for the full statistical parameter.
A discrete counterexample shows why a sample mean is not automatically sufficient for arbitrary statistical models. Let X1​,X2​ be independent and identically distributed on {0,1,2} with
Pθ​(Xi​=0)=Pθ​(Xi​=2)=θ/2,Pθ​(Xi​=1)=1−θ,0<θ<1.
(69)
Each observation has mean one. The arrangements (0,2),(1,1),(2,0) all give sample mean one, yet
Pθ​((X1​,X2​)=(1,1)∣X=1)=(1−θ)2+θ2/2(1−θ)2​.
(70)
This conditional probability is 18/19 at θ=1/4 and 2/11 at θ=3/4. The conditional distribution therefore still depends on the unknown statistical parameter: this sample mean is not a sufficient statistic. The useful reduction depends on the family of full-data laws, even when its mean stays fixed.
