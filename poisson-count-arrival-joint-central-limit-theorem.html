Poisson count-arrival joint central limit theorem (ρ=−min{λ​,1/λ​})

 Home Mathematics Area of mathematics Probability and statistics Probability theory Poisson process
OurBigBook.com Words: 294
For a rate-one Poisson process with Poisson arrival times Tn​, suppose tn​→∞ and tn​/n→λ∈[0,∞]. Then
(tn​​Ntn​​−tn​​,n​Tn​−n​)d​BN(ρ),ρ={−λ​,−1/λ​,​0≤λ≤1,1≤λ≤∞,​
(3)
where 1/∞​=0 and BN(ρ) denotes the bivariate normal distribution with standard normal marginal distributions, including the singular cases. In particular, equal scales give ρ=−1 and separated scales give ρ=0.
To prove this, fix real x,y and put sn​=n+yn​>0 for large n. The event (Tn​−n)/n​≤y is exactly Nsn​​≥n. Counts at the two deterministic times tn​,sn​ are sums of a shared count on [0,min(tn​,sn​)] and an independent count on the remaining interval. The central limit theorem for a centered Poisson random variable of diverging parameter follows by writing a count at a real time u as its count at ⌊u⌋, a sum of independent unit increments, plus an independent remainder with expected value at most one. Markov's inequality makes that remainder negligible after division by u​.
Apply this central limit theorem to the two independent count blocks whenever their lengths diverge. A block with bounded length, or length negligible compared with the scale of its coordinate, contributes a term tending to zero in probability. The standardized pair of counts therefore has a joint normal limit whose correlation coefficient is
limn→∞​tn​sn​​min(tn​,sn​)​={λ​,1/λ​,​0≤λ≤1,1≤λ≤∞.​
(4)
This includes λ=1: the difference block then has negligible variance after normalization. In the arrival event the standardized count at sn​ must be at least (n−sn​)/sn​​→−y. Reversing this inequality changes the sign of the second limiting normal random variable, giving the asserted negative correlation coefficient. Continuity of the marginal distributions permits the moving thresholds and the weak inequalities, including at the singular limits.
Every ρ∈[−1,0) is realized by tn​=λn with λ=ρ−2≥1, and zero by tn​=n4. Conversely, even when tn​/n has no limit, it has a subsequence converging in the extended interval [0,∞]. If the original pair has a joint bivariate normal distribution limit, that subsequence has the same limit, forcing its correlation coefficient into [−1,0].
