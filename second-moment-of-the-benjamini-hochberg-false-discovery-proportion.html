Second moment of the Benjamini-Hochberg false discovery proportion (E(FDP2))

For independent p-values, with m0​ true-null values independent and uniform on [0,1], let R(i) denote the Benjamini-Hochberg procedure rejection count after replacing one true-null value by zero. The Benjamini-Hochberg leave-one-out identity gives
E(R∨1)21{i rejected}​=mα​ER(i)1​.
(84)
For distinct true-null indices, the Benjamini-Hochberg leave-two-out identity gives
E(R∨1)21{i,j rejected}​=m2α2​.
(85)
Indeed, conditional on the remaining values and R(ij)=r, the two independent uniform values both meet their threshold with probability (αr/m)2, cancelling the denominator r2. Expanding the square of the false rejection count into its diagonal terms and ordered distinct pairs, and using symmetry among the true-null values, proves
E(FDP2)=mαm0​​ER(i)1​+m2α2m0​(m0​−1)​.
(86)
If no null is true, both sides are zero; if exactly one is true, the pair contribution is absent. This formula complements the exact false discovery rate αm0​/m by describing second-moment variability.
