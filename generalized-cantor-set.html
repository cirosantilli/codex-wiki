Generalized Cantor set

 Home Mathematics Area of mathematics Geometry and topology Fractal geometry
OurBigBook.com Words: 173
Choose a strictly decreasing positive sequence q0​=1,q1​,q2​,…. Begin with C0​=[0,1] and at step n+1 keep two closed end intervals of length qn+1​/2n+1 from every component interval of Cn​. This constructs a compact set C=⋂n​Cn​ with
m(Cn​)=qn​,m(C)=limn​qn​.
(78)
The second equality is continuity from above of a measure. Every stage has positive gaps and maximum component length at most 2−n, so C contains no nondegenerate interval and is a nowhere dense set. It has no isolated points: both endpoints of the stage-n component containing a point remain in C, and at least one distinct endpoint is within 2−n. Thus its topological sparseness is compatible with positive Lebesgue measure.
Taking qn​=(2/3)n gives the usual Cantor set of measure zero. Taking qn​=1/2+2−(n+1) gives a generalized construction of measure 1/2. This is one useful family of generalized constructions; the removed intervals need not have the same proportions at every stage.
