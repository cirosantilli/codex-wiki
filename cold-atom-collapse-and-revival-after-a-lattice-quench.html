Cold-atom collapse and revival after a lattice quench

 Home Physics Branches of physics Quantum mechanics Sudden approximation Quantum quench
OurBigBook.com Words: 216
A concrete quantum quench experiment loaded a Bose-Einstein condensate of 87Rb chemical atoms from a magnetic field trap into an optical lattice: three orthogonal 838nm laser standing waves, with approximately 125μm beam waists. An 80ms loading ramp preceded a 50μs increase in lattice depth. This suppressed quantum tunnelling while retaining each well's vibrational ground state. The switch preserved atom-number populations rather than freezing every motional degree of freedom.[ref]
After variable hold times, releasing the traps and taking photon absorption images after 16ms of flight revealed disappearance and return of interference peaks. Background-subtracted peak counts supplied a coherent fraction. Varying final depth tested the predicted revival period h/U; varying initial depth tested the collapse's sensitivity to atom-number statistics. Peak broadening outside counting windows and spatial variation of U limited later signals.[ref]
Here U is the on-site interaction energy. The approximate isolated-well Hamiltonian operator H=Un^(n^−1)/2, with number operator n^, gives different quantum phases to its number states, which later rephase. This is a many-level realization of the distinction between fixed populations and changing quantum phases.[ref]
