Differential magnetic susceptibility (χdiff​=(∂M/∂Hint​)T​)

 Home Physics Branches of physics Statistical physics Magnetic susceptibility
OurBigBook.com Words: 107
Differential magnetic susceptibility is the local slope at a specified bias magnetic field intensity, holding temperature and other stated constraints fixed. It equals volume magnetic susceptibility only for a linear response through the origin. For example, M=Ms​tanh(H/Hs​) has M/H=(Ms​/Hs​)tanh(x)/x, but dM/dH=(Ms​/Hs​)/cosh2(x), where x=H/Hs​. At x=1 these are about 0.762 and 0.420 times their common zero-field slope. A finite sweep chord is another quantity, not automatically this derivative.
If the differentiated field is instead the internal magnetic flux density, the longitudinal chain rule applied to Bint​=μ0​(Hint​+M) gives
(∂Bint​∂M​)T​=μ0​(1+χdiff​)χdiff​​.
(64)
This differs from the vacuum/applied-B0​ conversion in volume magnetic susceptibility. It assumes a differentiable single branch with locally invertible Bint​(Hint​).
