Wu experiment

 ... Physics Branches of physics Quantum field theory Standard Model Weak interaction Parity violation
OurBigBook.com Words: 287
The Wu experiment was performed by Chien-Shiung Wu, Ernest Ambler, Raymond Webster Hayward, Dale D. Hoppes, and Ralph Percy Hudson; its 1957 report was Experimental Test of Parity Conservation in Beta Decay. Their experiment supplied the evidence that the Lee-Yang proposal for a parity test had called for.[ref]
A cobalt-60 surface layer on a cerium magnesium nitrate crystal was cooled by adiabatic demagnetization; a solenoid's magnetic field polarized its atomic nuclei. An internal anthracene counter detected electrons by particle scintillation; a light pipe carried flashes to a photomultiplier. Polar and equatorial gamma ray counters monitored nuclear polarization and effective temperature; bulk thermometry could miss surface heating.[ref]
Reversing nuclear polarization reversed the electron excess; warming erased it with gamma ray anisotropy. Warm rates were field-independent. Cooling-field reversal checked remanence; poorly polarized sources checked magnetic deflection. Electrons favored the direction opposite spin.[ref]
The gamma ray anisotropy diagnoses the preparation; the electron asymmetry diagnoses parity violation. These are different jobs. A directionally uneven gamma ray signal alone need not violate parity conservation: a distribution even in the cosine of the angle can distinguish an axis from its perpendicular without distinguishing its two ends. The spin-momentum correlation instead distinguishes those ends.
The inference requires control of changes in detection efficiency, electron transport and nuclear polarization. A fixed detector under spin reversal avoids physically exchanging detector positions, but still requires checks for magnetic field effects. The result establishes parity violation in this weak interaction process. A precise microscopic coupling requires more information than the existence or sign of a counting asymmetry; the following model makes that distinction explicit.
