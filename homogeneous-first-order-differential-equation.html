Homogeneous first-order differential equation (y′=F(y/x))

 Home Mathematics Area of mathematics Analysis Differential equation Ordinary differential equation
OurBigBook.com Words: 306 Articles: 2
A homogeneous first-order differential equation has the form y′=F(y/x) on a domain where x=0. The substitution v=y/x, so y′=v+xv′, reduces it to
xv′=F(v)−v.
(13)
Where F(v)−v=0, this is a separable differential equation, with ∫dv/[F(v)−v]=ln∣x∣+C. Values v0​ satisfying F(v0​)=v0​ give the additional straight-line solutions y=v0​x, which must be checked before division. This scaling-based use of “homogeneous” is different from a homogeneous linear differential equation.
For example, y′=y/x+x/y gives F(v)=v+1/v, so vv′=1/x and y2=x2(2ln∣x∣+C). Real explicit branches are y=±x2ln∣x∣+C​ on intervals where the expression under the square root is positive. Zeros of a denominator and endpoints of a branch must still be checked in the original ordinary differential equation.
