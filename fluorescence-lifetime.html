Fluorescence lifetime (τ)

 Home Physics Branches of physics Optics Physical optics Fluorescence
OurBigBook.com Words: 598 Articles: 3
For the constant-rate model in fluorescence quantum yield, fluorescence lifetime is the mean residence time in the emitting excited state and the 1/e timescale of its exponential decay. The detected fluorescent photon rate after an ideal instantaneous pulse is I(t)=ηkr​N0​e−t/τ for t≥0. Its amplitude and its decay timescale are separate quantities.
For a stipulated example, rates (kr​,knr​)=(0.05,0.20)ns−1 give τ=4ns and Φ=0.20. Rates (0.20,0.05)ns−1 give the same τ but Φ=0.80. Conversely, doubling N0​ doubles the ideal amplitude without changing τ. Equal fluorescence lifetimes need not imply equal brightness or equal fluorescence quantum yields.
Different environments can supply several populations, giving I(t)=∑j​Aj​e−t/τj​ rather than one exponential decay. A fitted single timescale then depends on the selected model and detection band. FRET donor lifetime uses the change in a donor's decay when transfer introduces an additional loss route; the FRET branch develops that interpretation.
