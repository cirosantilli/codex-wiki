Gumbel distribution (Λ)

 Home Mathematics Area of mathematics Probability and statistics Probability theory Extreme value theory
OurBigBook.com Words: 520 Articles: 4
The standard Gumbel distribution for maxima has distribution function Λ(z)=e−e−z for every real z. Its probability density function is the derivative of its distribution function:
Λ(z)=exp(−e−z),g(z)=e−zexp(−e−z),z∈R.
(11)
This article uses the maxima convention throughout. Reflecting a standard Gumbel distribution random variable Z to −Z gives the minima convention, with distribution function 1−exp(−ez). The two conventions have opposite directions of asymmetry, so their formulas must not be interchanged. [ref].
For a location μ∈R and positive scale b>0, let Y=μ+bZ. This location-scale family has cumulative distribution function and probability density function
FY​(x)=exp[−exp(−bx−μ​)],fY​(x)=b1​exp(−bx−μ​)exp[−exp(−bx−μ​)].
(12)
Solving Λ(z)=p gives the standard quantile function q(p)=−log(−logp), 0<p<1, and hence the standard median
m=−log(log2)≈0.366513.
(13)
The median of Y is therefore μ−blog(log2). Location μ is not the median: at x=μ the cumulative distribution function is e−1≈0.367879.
To derive the Gumbel distribution as an extreme-value limit, take the maximum of independent exponential random variables with rate λ>0 and set
Zn​=λMn​−logn.
(14)
The exact distribution function is
P(Zn​≤z)=⎩⎨⎧​(1−ne−z​)n,0,​z≥−logn,z<−logn.​
(15)
The finite-n lower boundary is required because Mn​≥0. For each fixed real z, eventually z>−logn, and the natural logarithm of the first branch satisfies
nlog(1−ne−z​)⟶−e−z.
(16)
Exponentiating proves convergence in distribution to the standard Gumbel distribution. It is the familiar limit (1−u/n)n→e−u with u=e−z: a simple calculation reveals a whole limiting shape. The name honors Emil Gumbel; Siegrist gives this exponential-maxima derivation and the standard Gumbel distribution formulas. [ref].
For the toy delay model λ=1s−1, n=100, the limiting Gumbel distribution approximation to M100​ has μ=(log100)s and b=1s. Its predicted median is
m100approx​=(log100−log(log2))s≈4.971683s,
(17)
compared with the exact median −log(1−2−1/100)s≈4.975147s. The approximation is about 0.003464 seconds lower in this example. It is not an exact finite-sample probability distribution: the approximating Gumbel distribution assigns positive probability to negative delays, while the exact sample maximum cannot be negative.
The maximum domain of attraction of the Gumbel distribution is characterized by an exponential tail ratio on the scale of a Gumbel auxiliary function. This allows many original probability distributions to share the limiting shape, but it does not make the centering logn appropriate to all of them.
