Expected value of a capped nonnegative random variable (E[min(Y,a)]=∫0a​P(Y>t)dt)

For a nonnegative random variable Y and a fixed cap a≥0, the tail-sum formula gives
E[min(Y,a)]=∫0a​P(Y>t)dt,E[(Y−a)+​]=∫a∞​P(Y>t)dt.
(12)
Below the cap, the events {min(Y,a)>t} and {Y>t} coincide; at and above the cap, the former event is empty. For the second identity, the positive part (Y−a)+​ exceeds t≥0 precisely when Y>a+t. Thus the two integrals divide the area under the survival function, just as Y=min(Y,a)+(Y−a)+​ divides each outcome. The capped expected value is always finite, while the excess expected value may be infinite.
For the uniform distribution on the interval [0,4] and cap a=2, the capped expected value is ∫02​(1−t/4)dt=3/2 and the excess expected value is ∫24​(1−t/4)dt=1/2. Directly averaging the capped values also gives ∫02​y(1/4)dy+2P(Y>2)=1/2+1=3/2. Capping therefore measures a different average from the uncapped expected value two, even when both are finite.
