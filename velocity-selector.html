Velocity selector (v0​=E/Bs​)

 ... Branches of physics Electromagnetism Relativistic electromagnetism Electromagnetic four-potential Electromagnetic field tensor Lorentz force
OurBigBook.com Words: 243
A velocity selector uses perpendicular electric field and magnetic field to transmit a narrow range of speeds along a chosen beam direction. Assume uniform static electric field E=Ey​ and magnetic field Bs​=Bs​z, with E,Bs​>0, and an incident velocity v=vx. The cross product gives v×Bs​=−vBs​y​, so the transverse Lorentz force is
Fy​=q(E−vBs​),Fy​=0 ⟹ v0​=Bs​E​.
(22)
For an electron, the electric force points downward and the magnetic force upward. A faster electron initially bends upward; a slower one initially bends downward. At the selected speed, both forces cancel along the whole straight trajectory. The cancellation is independent of mass and nonzero electric charge; it selects velocity, not a particular charge-to-mass ratio. A component of velocity parallel to the magnetic field is not constrained by the cancellation, so the incident beam must also have its direction restricted.
A real exit slit accepts a finite range of speeds and incident directions. A zero displacement at one screen alone does not establish zero force throughout the path: a curved trajectory could return to that screen position. The ideal velocity selector interpretation requires a straight aligned beam, known overlapping electric field and magnetic field, and sufficiently narrow entrance and exit slits. Its useful role in the Thomson charge-to-mass experiment teaching model is to supply speed independently of the subsequent magnetic bending.
