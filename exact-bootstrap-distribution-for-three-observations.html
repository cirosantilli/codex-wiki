Exact bootstrap distribution for three observations (P∗​(X∗=7/3)=2/9)

OurBigBook.com Words: 231
Take the original observations (1,2,4), with sample mean x=7/3. All 33=27 ordered bootstrap samples have probability 1/27. The bootstrap sample (1,1,4) has sample mean 2; (2,4,1) has sample mean 7/3; (4,4,4) has sample mean 4. These are illustrative possibilities, not three simulated measurements used to estimate the distribution.
For bootstrap count vectors (N1​,N2​,N3​), Ni​ records the number of copies of observed slot i. Their multinomial distribution is
P∗​((N1​,N2​,N3​)=(a,b,c))=a!b!c!3!​271​,a+b+c=3,
(56)
and X∗=(N1​+2N2​+4N3​)/3. Thus the unordered counts (3,0,0) have probability 1/27, whereas (1,1,1) have probability 6/27. Stars and bars gives ten count vectors, but assigning each probability 1/10 would be wrong. Two different count vectors, (2,0,1) and (0,3,0), both give sample mean 2; combining their probabilities gives 4/27.
The conditional bootstrap variance of a sample mean gives a compact check on the enumeration. Each resampled draw has expected value 7/3 and variance
31​[(1−37​)2+(2−37​)2+(4−37​)2]=914​.
(57)
Averaging three independent draws divides that variance by three:
E∗​[X∗]=37​,Var∗​(X∗)=2714​.​
(58)
The exact conditional bootstrap standard error is 14/27​≃0.720. The original sample variance with denominator n−1 is 7/3, so its usual estimated standard error for the sample mean is 7/9​≃0.882. Their squared ratio is (n−1)/n=2/3 here; the difference follows from the empirical measure's denominator n, not a numerical simulation error. Neither number is the known true sampling standard error of the unknown source probability distribution.
