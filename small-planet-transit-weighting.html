Small-planet transit weighting (δ≃k2I(μp​)/I)

 ... Branches of physics Optics Radiometry Radiance Spectral radiance Limb darkening
OurBigBook.com Words: 346
A dark, opaque circular planet of radius Rp​ blocks specific intensity over its overlap with the projected star. With k=Rp​/R⋆​ and no transit dilution, the instantaneous exoplanet transit depth is exactly
δ=πR⋆2​I∫overlap​IdAproj​​.
(24)
When k≪1, the entire planetary disk is inside the stellar disk, and specific intensity varies little across that disk, small-planet transit weighting reduces to
δ≃k2II(μp​)​=k21−u/31−u+uμp​​,μp​=1−z2​,z<1−k.
(25)
Here z is the projected centre separation divided by R⋆​. The numerator samples the local specific intensity; the denominator normalizes by the whole star's mean specific intensity. The small-size argument and the distinction between full overlap and ingress are explicit in the original analytic treatment. [ref]
For a short approximately straight crossing at constant transverse velocity v⊥​, write x=v⊥​t/R⋆​ with t=0 at mid-transit, and let b be the transit impact parameter. Then z2=x2+b2 and μp​=1−b2−x2​. With stipulated u=0.6 and k=0.05, a central crossing has mid-transit exoplanet transit depth approximately 0.003125, whereas b=0.8 gives 0.002375. A uniform disk would give k2=0.0025 for either fully overlapping crossing. The brighter central chord produces a deeper dip and the off-centre chord a shorter crossing at the same v⊥​.
At fixed z<1 away from the edge, a two-dimensional Taylor's theorem expansion over the symmetric planetary disk cancels the first derivatives; the first correction to the blocked radiant flux is of order k4 if the second derivatives remain bounded. This error estimate is not uniform at the edge, where the radial derivative of the linear limb-darkening law diverges. Ingress, egress, a grazing exoplanet transit, and a crossing of a starspot require the overlap integral or a suitable more complete model. Inferring k=δ​ without the local-to-mean specific intensity ratio can bias the estimated planet radius.
