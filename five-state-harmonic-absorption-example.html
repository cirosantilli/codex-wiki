Five-state harmonic absorption example (hi​=i/4,ti​=i(4−i))

 ... Area of mathematics Probability and statistics Probability theory Markov process Markov chain Harmonic function for a Markov chain
OurBigBook.com Words: 572
Take a birth-death chain on {0,1,2,3,4}. At each interior state, move up one state with probability p=1/2 and down one state with probability q=1/2. The endpoints are absorbing states: P00​=P44​=1. This is a small gambler's ruin model in which the state can represent capital, and reaching 4 is success.
Let Ta​=inf{n≥0:Xn​=a} be the hitting time of endpoint a, and let T=min(T0​,T4​) be the first endpoint hitting time. The desired hitting probability is hi​=Pi​(T4​<T0​). First-step analysis and the Markov property give
h0​=0,h4​=1,hi​=21​hi−1​+21​hi+1​(i=1,2,3).
(6)
The interior equation says adjacent differences are equal. Four equal increments must bridge the boundary values 0 and 1, so
hi​=4i​,(h0​,h1​,h2​,h3​,h4​)=(0,41​,21​,43​,1).
(7)
For example, from state 2 the hitting probability is 1/2, and its one-step average is 21​(1/4)+21​(3/4)=1/2. Individual sample paths can wander back and forth, but the same averaging rule already accounts for all of them.
Now ask how long it takes to reach either endpoint. The expected hitting time ti​=Ei​T counts steps, including the transition that first reaches an endpoint. First-step analysis gives
t0​=t4​=0,ti​=1+21​ti−1​+21​ti+1​(i=1,2,3).
(8)
This is a discrete Poisson equation, (I−P)t=1 in the interior. The extra 1 counts the step just taken. The quadratic values
ti​=i(4−i),(t0​,t1​,t2​,t3​,t4​)=(0,3,4,3,0)
(9)
satisfy the equations; from state 2, t2​=1+21​(3)+21​(3)=4 steps. The hitting probability is harmonic, whereas the expected hitting time has a nonzero source term. These are special cases of expected duration of symmetric gambler's ruin; see Hao Wu's MIT lecture 16, gambler's ruin, slide 9.
For a biased random walk with p=0.6 upward and q=0.4 downward, keep the same absorbing states. The interior harmonic equation becomes hi​=phi+1​+qhi−1​. Using biased gambler's ruin probability with these boundary values gives
r=pq​=32​,hi​=1−r41−ri​,(h0​,h1​,h2​,h3​,h4​)=(0,6527​,139​,6557​,1).
(10)
The three interior hitting probabilities are approximately 0.415385, 0.692308, and 0.876923. Thus starting at 2 now succeeds about 69.23% of the time. A direct check is 0.6(57/65)+0.4(27/65)=9/13. The curved profile reflects the unequal transition weights: its successive increments shrink by the factor q/p=2/3. The formula, with the endpoint denoted T there, is verified in Karger and Lynch's MIT lecture 26, sections 2.3-2.4; the five-state numbers here are direct substitutions.
Why does harmonic averaging produce the correct hitting probability? With the natural filtration Fn​=σ(X0​,…,Xn​), the Markov property gives the conditional expectation identity
E[h(Xn+1​)∣Fn​]=(Ph)(Xn​).
(11)
For a function harmonic everywhere, this makes h(Xn​) a martingale whenever it is integrable. For a function harmonic only in the interior, Mn​=h(Xn∧T​) is a stopped martingale. In either five-state example, 0≤Mn​≤1. Also, from any interior state at most three consecutive upward steps reach 4, with probability at least p3>0. Repeated three-step blocks give Pi​(T>3k)≤(1−p3)k, so T is almost surely finite and has finite expected value; compare finite-interval exit times have geometric tails. Applying optional stopping at n∧T and then the bounded convergence theorem gives
hi​=Ei​[h(XT​)]=Pi​(XT​=4).
(12)
The boundedness and finite hitting time justify this passage to the limit. See Steven Lalley's notes, harmonic functions and stopped expectations, theorem 1, and stopped martingale in discrete time for this bounded version of optional stopping.
Uniqueness has a short maximum principle explanation. On a finite state space, the difference of two harmonic extensions with the same boundary conditions is zero on the boundary. If its positive maximum occurs inside, the weighted average forces every successor with positive transition probability to have the same maximum. A path to the boundary propagates that maximum to zero, a contradiction. Applying the argument to the negative of the difference proves uniqueness. Reachability of the boundary is essential: an interior closed communicating class can retain an arbitrary constant.
