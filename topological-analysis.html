Brouwer fixed-point theorem

Words: 308 Articles: 7
Topological analysis OurBigBook.com
Every continuous self-map of a closed disc has a fixed point.

Positive matrix eigenvector from simplex normalization

Words: 58
Brouwer fixed-point theorem OurBigBook.com
For a strictly positive real matrix A, the map x↦Ax/∑i​(Ax)i​ is a continuous self-map of the probability simplex. A Brouwer fixed-point theorem fixed point satisfies Ax=λx with λ>0. The image lies in the interior of the simplex, so every entry of this eigenvector is strictly positive. Nonnegative entries and nonzero columns alone do not imply this strict conclusion.

Surjectivity of a continuous bounded-displacement map

Words: 36
Brouwer fixed-point theorem OurBigBook.com
If a continuous map g:Rn→Rn has ∥g(x)−x∥≤K, then it is onto. For a target y, choose R≥K+∥y∥, R>0, and map the unit ball to itself by f(x)=x−(g(Rx)−y)/R. A fixed point gives g(Rx)=y.

Brouwer inward-pointing zero lemma

Words: 47
Brouwer fixed-point theorem OurBigBook.com
Let V be a finite-dimensional real inner-product space and let F:V→V be continuous. If ⟨F(x),x⟩<0 on the sphere ∥x∥=R, then F has a zero in the open ball. Otherwise the radial projection of F gives a map of the ball that contradicts the Brouwer fixed-point theorem.

Sperner's lemma

Words: 77
Brouwer fixed-point theorem OurBigBook.com
Triangulate a triangle and label every vertex by 1, 2, or 3, forbidding label i on the side opposite vertex i. Then the number of small triangles carrying all three labels is odd, and in particular at least one such triangle exists. Counting edges whose endpoint labels are 1 and 2 proves the parity statement: boundary incidences are odd, while a small triangle has odd incidence exactly when it has all three labels.

Distortion (mathematics)

Words: 52
Metric space OurBigBook.com
Distortion measures how a mapping changes distances or geometric quantities relative to their original values. For an injective map between metric spaces, one multiplicative measure is the product of its Lipschitz constant and the Lipschitz constant of its inverse on the image. Subgroup distortion instead compares intrinsic and ambient word metrics through a growth function.

Ball (mathematics)

Words: 42
Metric space OurBigBook.com
In a metric space, an open ball B(a,r) consists of points whose distance from a is less than r. The closed ball uses a nonstrict inequality. A Euclidean ball uses Euclidean distance, while a unit ball in a normed space uses its norm and radius one.

Maximum product metric (D((x,x′),(y,y′))=max{d(x,y),d′(x′,y′)})

The displayed metric on a product of two metric spaces satisfies its triangle inequality because max{a+c,b+d}≤max{a,b}+max{c,d} for nonnegative coordinates. Its open balls are products of equal-radius balls, so it induces the product topology.

Closed diagonal of a metric space (Δ={(x,x):x∈X})

Words: 58
Maximum product metric OurBigBook.com
For a metric space with the same metric on both factors, the diagonal is a closed subset of its product. At an off-diagonal point (x,y) let δ=d(x,y)>0. A maximum-metric ball of radius δ/3 misses the diagonal, since a common coordinate z would give d(x,y)≤d(x,z)+d(z,y)<2δ/3. Equality of underlying sets without equality or compatibility of the metrics is a different assertion.

Metric epsilon-net (ε-net)

Words: 56
Metric space OurBigBook.com
An epsilon-net in a metric space is a set within distance ε of every point. A maximal ε-separated set is an epsilon-net: any uncovered point could be added. On the unit sphere of Rk, disjoint Euclidean balls of radius ε/2 around separated points lie in the ball of radius 1+ε/2. Comparing Lebesgue measures gives a net of cardinality at most (1+2/ε)k.

Metric space OurBigBook.com
The supremum of pointwise distances defines a metric on maps when it is finite, for example if the target metric space is bounded. Convergence in this metric is uniform convergence. If the target is complete, Cauchy sequences have pointwise limits and the Cauchy estimate makes convergence uniform.

Completeness of the continuous-map space in the uniform metric (C(X,Y) is complete when Y is complete and bounded)

Words: 54
Uniform metric OurBigBook.com
A Cauchy sequence in the uniform metric has a pointwise limit in the complete target, and the common Cauchy bound makes convergence uniform. The triangle inequality with one fixed continuous approximant shows that the limit is continuous. Thus the space of all continuous maps into a bounded complete metric space is complete, with no compactness assumption on the domain.

A subset is r-separated if distinct points have distance at least r. Such a subset is a packing by points; small balls around them have disjoint interiors at suitable radius. A large separated set supplies many distinguishable alternatives in statistical lower-bound arguments.

An inclusion-maximal separated set is one to which no further point of the ambient metric space can be added while keeping the same separation. It need not have the largest possible cardinality. In a finite space it can be obtained by repeatedly adding an admissible point.
Maximal separated sets give covers

If an inclusion-maximal separated set has separation at least r, every point of the ambient metric space is at distance strictly less than r from a selected point. Otherwise that point could be added. Thus upper bounds on the sizes or measures of radius-r balls give lower bounds on the size of the separated set.

In a metric space (M,d), the closed ball centred at x of radius r≥0 is {y∈M:d(x,y)≤r}. It is a closed set, since the distance to x is a continuous function. Its boundary need not be the whole corresponding distance sphere in an arbitrary metric space.

Unit sphere net from ball covering (∣Nδ​∣≤(2A/δ)k)

Words: 65
Metric net OurBigBook.com
A cover of the unit ball by balls of radius δ/2 gives a radius-δ metric net on the unit sphere: discard balls missing the sphere and choose a sphere point in each remaining ball. Every sphere point lies within δ of the selected point in its ball. The argument keeps the number of balls and ensures that the selected points have unit length, regardless of the initial covering centers.

Quadratic form net bound (∥H∥op​≤(1−2η)−1maxu∈U​∣u⊤Hu∣)

Words: 55

For a symmetric matrix H and an η-metric net U of the unit sphere, with 0<η<1/2, approximate a maximizing unit vector x by u∈U. Expanding x⊤Hx−u⊤Hu=(x−u)⊤Hx+u⊤H(x−u) bounds its absolute value by 2η∥H∥op​. Rearrangement proves the bound. The volumetric bound for Euclidean metric nets limits the number of needed directions, allowing a union bound to control a random matrix.

Volumetric bound for Euclidean metric nets (∣U∣≤(1+2R/ε)n)

Words: 49
Metric net OurBigBook.com
A subset of the radius-R Euclidean ball admits an ε-metric net of size at most (1+2R/ε)n. Take a maximal separated set: its disjoint radius-ε/2 Euclidean balls lie in the radius-(R+ε/2) ball. Comparing Lebesgue measures gives the estimate. In particular, the unit sphere admits a net of radius one with at most 3n points.

Metric covering number (N(ε,T,d))

Metric space OurBigBook.com
For a metric space or pseudometric space (T,d), this is the minimum number of balls of radius ε>0, with centers in T, needed to cover T. The displayed grid bound uses closed balls. For open balls, replacing the radius by a fixed constant factor gives the same entropy estimates. For T=[0,1] and d(s,t)=c∣s−t∣H, a uniform grid gives N(ε,T,d)≤1+⌈(c/ε)1/H⌉. These polynomial growth bounds make the Dudley entropy integral finite.

Metric entropy (H(ε,E)=logN(ε,E))

Words: 285 Articles: 2
Metric covering number OurBigBook.com
For a subset E of a metric space, its metric entropy at resolution ε is the logarithm of the least number of radius-ε balls covering E, with value infinity when no finite cover exists. It quantifies how many choices are needed to approximate an element at that resolution. If points are pairwise more than 2ε apart, each covering ball contains at most one, giving a lower bound by their logarithmic cardinality. The logarithm base changes only a constant factor. This approximation entropy differs from Kolmogorov-Sinai entropy of a dynamical system.
Entropy of Lipschitz compositions (H(ε,F)≤H(ε/2,B)+mH(ε/(2L),E))
Words: 91
Metric entropy OurBigBook.com
Suppose outer functions in a family B obey ∣f(x)−f(y)∣≤Lmaxj​∣xj​−yj​∣, and compose them with m inner functions drawn from E. Approximate the outer function within ε/2 and each inner function within ε/(2L). The triangle inequality bounds the composed error by ε. Multiplying the numbers of possible net choices and taking logarithms gives the stated inequality. A uniform bound on first partial derivatives supplies L=m for the Cp unit ball, so the exponent of a common entropy bound for the outer and inner classes is retained under function composition.
Entropy of a smooth periodic function ball (cε−n/p≤H≤Cε−n/plog(1/ε))
Words: 105
Metric entropy OurBigBook.com
For the unit ball of Cp(Tn) measured in the uniform metric, cε−n/p≤H(ε)≤Cε−n/plog(1/ε). The lower bound uses disjoint bump functions of spatial scale h, height proportional to hp and independently chosen zero/one coefficients: there are exp(ch−n) functions separated at scale hp. For the upper bound, Multivariable Jackson approximation gives a trigonometric polynomial of degree O(ε−1/p). Its O(ε−n/p) coefficients are bounded and can be rounded with coefficient accuracy proportional to ε divided by the coefficient count. Counting those rounded coefficient arrays supplies the logarithmic factor. The continuous-function unit ball without derivative bounds has infinite entropy for ε<1/2, by an infinite sequence of disjoint height-one bumps.

Geodesic metric space

Words: 181 Articles: 2
Metric space OurBigBook.com
A metric space in which every two points are joined by a metric geodesic segment. Equivalently, they admit a continuous path whose metric path length equals their distance. In this general setting no Riemannian metric or covariant derivative is required.

Metric geodesic (d(γ(s),γ(t))=∣s−t∣)

Words: 154 Articles: 1
Geodesic metric space OurBigBook.com
A unit-speed metric geodesic in a metric space is an isometric embedding γ:I→X of a real interval. Every restricted segment realizes the distance between its endpoints. A one-point interval permits a constant segment. In a unit-edge graph, shortest edge paths parametrized by metric path length are metric geodesics; no smooth structure is required. This is stronger than the differential definition of a geodesic in a Riemannian manifold, which need only minimize locally: on a unit circle, an arc of angle 3π/2 is a Riemannian geodesic but its endpoint distance is only π/2.
Metric geodesic triangle ([p,q]∪[q,r]∪[r,p])
Words: 70
Metric geodesic OurBigBook.com
A metric geodesic triangle consists of three points of a geodesic metric space and a chosen metric geodesic segment between each pair. Multiple choices are allowed, and coincident vertices give degenerate triangles. A Gromov-hyperbolic metric space has a uniform bound on the distance from each side to the other two sides for every such choice. This definition applies to Cayley graphs and trees without tangent vectors or curvature assumptions; it does not invoke the surface angle formula for a geodesic triangle.

Metric (d)

Words: 283 Articles: 7
Metric space OurBigBook.com
A metric on a set X is a function d:X×X→[0,∞) that is symmetric, satisfies the triangle inequality, and obeys d(x,y)=0 exactly when x=y.
A metric equips its underlying set with a metric space structure; the distance function and the space carrying it are distinct concepts.

Bounded metric transform (db​(x,y)=d(x,y)/(1+d(x,y)))

Words: 50
Metric OurBigBook.com
The increasing function ϕ(s)=s/(1+s) is subadditive on nonnegative arguments: each summand b/(1+b) and c/(1+c) is at least its numerator divided by 1+b+c. Thus ϕ∘d is a metric whenever d is. It is bounded and induces the same topology because ϕ and its inverse near zero preserve convergence to zero.

Compatible metric

Words: 44
Metric OurBigBook.com
A metric is compatible with a given topology when its open balls generate exactly that topology. Compatible metrics on a compact topological space are uniformly equivalent: the identity maps between the resulting compact metric spaces are uniformly continuous. Consequently they define the same proximality and the same asymptotic-pair relation for a fixed continuous map.
