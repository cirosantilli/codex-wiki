Isothermal expansion

 ... Statistical physics Thermodynamics Second law of thermodynamics Carnot's theorem Carnot cycle Isothermal process
OurBigBook.com Words: 195
An isothermal expansion increases the volume of a thermodynamic system at fixed temperature. For a reversible ideal gas path, the gas delivers positive work and absorbs positive heat, with Q=Wby,rev​=nRTln(Vf​/Vi​) and ΔU=0. The heat bath supplies the expansion energy; without sufficient heat transfer, doing work instead lowers the temperature. This transfer is the hot step of a Carnot cycle, and it is why an expanding gas need not cool when its surroundings continuously replenish its internal energy.
An insulated free expansion into an evacuated compartment is different. In the idealization with no heat exchange, no external work and no container energy change, Q=Wby​=0. Since internal energy of an ideal gas depends only on temperature, its final equilibrium temperature equals its initial value. The transient need not have a uniform temperature or pressure, so equal endpoint temperatures do not make it a reversible isotherm. Do not apply the logarithmic isothermal ideal-gas work formula to this irreversible process. Its entropy still increases by ΔS=nRln(Vf​/Vi​); entropy change in free expansion evaluates this state function difference using a hypothetical reversible path.[ref]
For a real gas, even equal-temperature equilibrium endpoints need not have equal internal energy. An insulated free expansion can therefore change the temperature, while a controlled isothermal expansion must provide the heat needed for both its internal energy change and its delivered work. The internal energy at constant temperature article gives the Van der Waals equation example.[ref]
