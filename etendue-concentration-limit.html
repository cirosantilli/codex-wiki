Etendue concentration limit (C≤nout2​/(nin2​sin2θin​))

 ... Physics Branches of physics Optics Geometrical optics Etendue Optical concentrator
OurBigBook.com Words: 339
Suppose an extended source fills every position of the entrance aperture with a cone of half-angle θin​, and all these light rays must reach a one-sided planar receiver. If the receiver accepts at most a cone of half-angle θout​, its available etendue bounds the admitted etendue:
nin2​Ain​πsin2θin​≤nout2​Aout​πsin2θout​.
(13)
Consequently
C≤nin2​sin2θin​nout2​sin2θout​​≤nin2​sin2θin​nout2​​.
(14)
This original derivation combines conservation of etendue with the exact cone integral; the second inequality allows the complete output hemisphere. It bounds capacity and does not prove that a chosen shape attains the bound.
For the 30∘ input cone with nin​=nout​=1, C≤4: a 10mm entrance cannot deliver the entire filled bundle to a circular receiver smaller than 5mm in diameter. If the output half-angle is restricted to 60∘, C≤3 and the receiver diameter must be at least 10/3​≈5.7735mm. For a receiver medium with model refractive index nout​=1.5, the full-hemisphere capacity bound instead gives C≤9; that larger value also depends on transmitting the bundle into that medium.
Attempting C=10 in the air-to-air example forces rejection of some input. For the uniform radiance already specified, even filling the entire output hemisphere gives
η=Φin​Φout​​≤Gin​Gout,capacity​​=104​=0.4.
(15)
The maximum mean irradiance gain remains ηC≤4. With nonuniform radiance, rejecting a given fraction of geometric etendue need not reject the same fraction of radiant flux.
These bounds use a filled extended-source bundle in passive reversible geometrical optics, comparing the same wavelength and the same radiation channels. An ideal optical point source does not fill that four-dimensional set. Diffraction, optical coherence, or coupling a Gaussian beam into an optical fiber require a physical optics calculation; the cone bound is not a universal guarantee for a single wave mode. Likewise, optical gain or fluorescence changes the propagation assumptions.
