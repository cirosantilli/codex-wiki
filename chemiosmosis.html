Chemiosmosis

 Home Chemistry Biochemistry Energy metabolism Oxidative phosphorylation
OurBigBook.com Words: 241 Articles: 1
Chemiosmosis couples transmembrane ion movement down an electrochemical gradient to a chemical transformation. For mitochondrial ATP synthesis, protons return from the intermembrane space to the mitochondrial matrix through ATP synthase.[ref]
An original thermodynamic example separates the electrical and chemical contributions. At fixed temperature T, use ideal dilute thermodynamic activities a and the electric potential ψ on each side. The molar electrochemical potential difference for inward transfer of a singly charged proton is
Δμ​in​=RTln(aoutside​amatrix​​)+F(ψmatrix​−ψoutside​).
(16)
Here R is the molar gas constant, F the Faraday constant, and ln the natural logarithm. The sign convention is destination minus origin: a negative difference makes inward transfer favourable. Both lower proton activity and a more negative electric potential inside favour inward movement.
Stipulate T=300K, amatrix​/aoutside​=0.1, ψmatrix​−ψoutside​=−0.12V, R=8.314Jmol−1K−1 and F=96485Cmol−1. These illustrative inputs give approximately −5.743 and −11.578kJmol−1 for the chemical and electrical terms, hence −17.321kJmol−1 per transferred proton. For a hypothetical device coupling three inward protons per ATP to an ATP-formation cost of 45kJmol−1,
ΔGcoupled​=45+3Δμ​in​≈−6.964kJmol−1.
(17)
The sign follows by adding changes in the state function Gibbs free energy. It establishes thermodynamic feasibility for this chosen model, not its reaction rate. Three is a stipulated coupling count, not a universal ATP synthase stoichiometry; transport costs and leaks can also consume the available free energy.
