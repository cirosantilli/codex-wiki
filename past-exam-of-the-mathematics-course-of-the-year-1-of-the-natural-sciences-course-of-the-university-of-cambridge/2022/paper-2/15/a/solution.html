On a nonconstant branch with siny=0, regard x as a function of y. The equation becomes the linear ordinary differential equation
dydx​+xcoty=2cosy.
(40)
An integrating factor is siny, giving
dyd​(xsiny)=2sinycosy.
(41)
Taking an antiderivative yields the implicit family
xsiny−sin2y=C​.
(42)
On a regular branch this can also be written x=siny+C/siny. The latter form loses the constant solutions y=nπ, which must be restored separately.
The implicit relation is a first integral of an ordinary differential equation: along any differentiable solution of the original equation,
dxd​(xsiny−sin2y)=siny−(2siny−x)cosyy′=0.
(43)
Conversely, a differentiable graph satisfying this relation solves the original equation wherever cosy=0. The original equation is undefined when cosy=0; points at which 2siny−x=0 also need separate attention before solving explicitly for y′. This is why dividing immediately by tany can lose valid solutions and obscure nonuniqueness.
