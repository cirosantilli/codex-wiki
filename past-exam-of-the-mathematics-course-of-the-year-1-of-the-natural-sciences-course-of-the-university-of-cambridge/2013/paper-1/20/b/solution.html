The triangular roof has height (w/2)tanθ and two slopes of length (w/2)secθ. Hence the area constraint and cost are
A=wh+4w2​tanθ,C=2αh+βwsecθ.
(90)
The wall height is fixed, so its cost is constant. Assume the physical case A,h,β>0. Minimize C subject to the area constraint by Lagrange multipliers:
βsecθ=λ(h+2w​tanθ),βwsecθtanθ=λ4w2​sec2θ.
(91)
Eliminating the positive common factors gives
wsec2θ=4htanθ+2wtan2θ,w(1−tan2θ)=4htanθ.
(92)
Thus the optimum has 0<θ<π/4 and
2tan2θ=hw​.​
(93)
Set D=A−wh>0, so the area constraint gives tanθ=4D/w2. Substituting into the preceding equation and multiplying by w3 yields w4−16D2=16hwD. Therefore
w4=16D(D+wh)=16A(A−wh).​
(94)
For a global check, the square of the total roof length is
Q(w)=w2+w216(A−wh)2​,0<w<A/h.
(95)
Its derivative has the sign of P(w)=w4+16Ahw−16A2. Since P′(w)>0, P(0)<0 and P(A/h)>0, it has one root in this interval, with Q decreasing before that root and increasing afterwards. This proves the unique global minimum, rather than merely a stationary design. The minimum roof length at fixed wall height and cross-sectional area is independent of α and of the positive factor β. If β=0, every feasible shape has equal cost; the displayed conditions then describe a possible minimizer but are not necessary.
