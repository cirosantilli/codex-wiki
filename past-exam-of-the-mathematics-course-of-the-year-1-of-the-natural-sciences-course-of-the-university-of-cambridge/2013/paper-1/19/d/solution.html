For p≤0, the terms k−p do not tend to zero, so the series diverges. For p>0, group the positive terms into the dyadic blocks 2j≤k<2j+1. Each block contains 2j terms, and monotonicity gives

When p>1, the upper bounds form a convergent geometric series with ratio 21−p<1, proving convergence. When 0<p≤1, the lower bounds do not tend to zero, so infinitely many blocks each make a nonvanishing contribution and the sum diverges. Thus the p-series criterion is

This dyadic comparison is the mechanism of the Cauchy condensation test. In particular p=1 is the divergent harmonic series.
