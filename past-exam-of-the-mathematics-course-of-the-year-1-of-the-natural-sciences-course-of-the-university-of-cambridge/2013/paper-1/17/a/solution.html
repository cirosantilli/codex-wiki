With the standard real normalization, the Fourier series of a period-2L function is
f(x)∼2a0​​+∑n=1∞​[an​cosLnπx​+bn​sinLnπx​],

where
an​=L1​∫−LL​f(x)cosLnπx​dx(n≥0),bn​=L1​∫−LL​f(x)sinLnπx​dx(n≥1).​
(68)
For the partial sum SN​, Fourier orthogonality eliminates all cross terms. The constant term contributes 2L(a0​/2)2, while every squared sine or cosine integrates to L, giving
∫−LL​SN​(x)2dx=L2a02​​+L∑n=1N​(an2​+bn2​).
(69)
For a square-integrable function, completeness of the trigonometric orthogonal basis in the Hilbert space L2[−L,L] makes SN​→f in norm. Passing to the limit proves Parseval's identity:
∫−LL​f(x)2dx=L[2a02​​+n=1∑∞​(an2​+bn2​)].​
(70)
Square integrability is the needed hypothesis here; periodicity alone is not an integrability or convergence condition.
