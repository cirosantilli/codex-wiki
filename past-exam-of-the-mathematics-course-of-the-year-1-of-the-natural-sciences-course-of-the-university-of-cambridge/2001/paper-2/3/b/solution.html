On the circular cylinder, ρ=R is constant, so the given line element reduces to ds2=R2dϕ2+dz2. Unroll the circular cylinder with u=Rϕ. The line element is then ds2=du2+dz2, so the usual Euclidean geometry formula measures arc length in the (u,z) plane.

If δ is a minimizing angular increment, a shortest geodesic is parametrized by (ρ,ϕ,z)=(R,δt,z1​t), 0≤t≤1. This is a helix when both increments are nonzero, and a circular arc or vertical generator in the limiting cases. A nonminimal lift also gives a geodesic, but it is only shortest within its fixed winding class.
