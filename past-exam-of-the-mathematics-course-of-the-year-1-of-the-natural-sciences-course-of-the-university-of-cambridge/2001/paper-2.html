At an ordinary point, an order-n linear ordinary differential equation has n arbitrary constants in its general solution. Determining them uniquely requires n independent, consistent scalar conditions. Prescribing y,y′,…,y(n−1) at one ordinary point gives uniqueness under the standard continuous-coefficient assumptions. Conditions at different endpoints need an additional nonsingularity check: for example, y′′+y=0, y(0)=y(π)=0 leaves every multiple of sinx possible. Thus counting boundary conditions alone does not guarantee uniqueness for a boundary value problem.
