For f=xlnx, the derivatives are f′=1+lnx and f′′=1/x>0. Its unique stationary point is the global minimum (e−1,−e−1). For g=lnx/x, g′=(1−lnx)/x2 changes from positive to negative at x=e, giving the global maximum (e,e−1). Their endpoint limits are
f(x)g(x)​x→0+0 from below−∞​x→∞+∞0 from above​​​
(19)
An intersection requires (x2−1)lnx=0, and on x>0 both factors vanish only at x=1. There f=g=0 and f′=g′=1, proving a common tangent line y=x−1. In fact,
f−g=x(x2−1)lnx​>0(x>0, x=1),
(20)
because the two numerator factors always have the same sign. Thus the curves touch at (1,0) without exchanging their order.
