Away from zeros of sinx, an integrating factor for a differential one-form is μ(x)=1/sinx. Multiplication converts the form to
(cotx+y)dx+xdy=d[ln∣sinx∣+xy].
(69)
Therefore the implicit solution family is xy+ln∣sinx∣=C. On each graph interval between consecutive multiples of π, this becomes
y(x)=xC−ln∣sinx∣​,kπ<x<(k+1)π,k∈Z.​
(70)
No such open interval contains zero. At x=kπ, the undivided graph equation would read cos(kπ)=0, which is impossible for a finite derivative; hence no differentiable graph extends across one of these points.
If solutions are instead understood as arbitrary parametrized integral curves of the differential form, the vertical lines x=kπ are additional solutions: along them dx=0 and xsinxdy=0. They were lost on division by sinx and are not graphs y(x) over an interval.
