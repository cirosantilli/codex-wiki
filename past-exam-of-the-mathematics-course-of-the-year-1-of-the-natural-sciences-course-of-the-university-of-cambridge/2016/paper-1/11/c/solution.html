For real λ>0, squaring the two nonnegative moduli gives ∣z−i∣2=λ2∣z+i∣2. In Cartesian coordinates this is
(1−λ2)(x2+y2+1)−2(1+λ2)y=0.
(19)
Assume λ=1 and put h=(1+λ2)/(1−λ2). Dividing by 1−λ2 yields x2+y2−2hy+1=0. Since zz∗=x2+y2 and bz∗+b∗z=2Re(bz∗), the requested complex equation has
b=−i1−λ21+λ2​,c=1.​
(20)
Indeed these choices make the mixed term −2hy. Completing the square gives
x2+(y−h)2=h2−1=(1−λ2)24λ2​.
(21)
The Circles of Apollonius therefore have
centre zc​=i1−λ21+λ2​,radius ∣1−λ2∣2λ​.​
(22)
The radius is positive for the stated range. No spurious point at z=−i has been introduced: at that point the undivided equation would require 4=0. Positive λ<1 gives a circle above the real axis, while λ>1 gives one below it.
