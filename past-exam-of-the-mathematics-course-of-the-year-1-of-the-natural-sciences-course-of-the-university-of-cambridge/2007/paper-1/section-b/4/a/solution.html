Let M and F count the two labels. Conditional on N=M+F=k+l, independent labeling gives a binomial distribution, so the joint probability mass function is
P(M=k,F=l)​=P(N=k+l)(kk+l​)pk(1−p)l=e−λk!(λp)k​l![λ(1−p)]l​=[e−λpk!(λp)k​][e−λ(1−p)l![λ(1−p)]l​].​
(33)
Each bracket is a normalized Poisson distribution. Summing over l≥0 therefore gives
P(M=k)=e−λpk!(λp)k​,M∼Poisson(λp).​
(34)
The factorization also proves that M and F are independent random variables. This is Poisson thinning; the phrase about ignoring the other count refers to taking a marginal distribution, rather than fixing that count.
