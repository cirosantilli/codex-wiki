Put S=θ1​+θ2​ and D=θ1​−θ2​. The gradient has components 3sinS+sinD and 3sinS−sinD, so a stationary point requires sinS=sinD=0. In the closed square, S∈[0,2π] and D∈[−π,π], and the compatible possibilities are precisely the four corners and (π/2,π/2).
The Hessian matrix is
(3cosS+cosD3cosS−cosD​3cosS−cosD3cosS+cosD​).
(43)
Its eigenvectors (1,1) and (1,−1) have eigenvalues 6cosS and 2cosD. The second-derivative test gives the following classification:
Point || U || Hessian eigenvalues || Classification(0,0),(π,π) | −4 | 6,2 | Minima(π,0),(0,π) | 4 | −6,−2 | Maxima(π/2,π/2) | 2 | −6,2 | Saddle point of a scalar function
The corner classifications also hold for the smooth extension beyond the square, and hence for the restricted region. The global bounds −4≤U≤4 show that the stated minima and maxima are global within it. The central point has opposite curvature along the two diagonal directions.
