For the final row, first calculate ii=e−π/2 and only then raise this positive real number to the power i. Treating nested powers as ordinary real powers would ignore the branching of complex exponentiation.
If all branches are requested, i​ also has the negative of its principal value, and the complex logarithm of −e is 1+i(π+2πk). Allowing nonprincipal logarithms of the positive base 2 gives e−2πkeiln2. Finally, all values of (ii)i are −ie−2πm, for integers k,m as appropriate. These alternatives do not alter the principal-value table.
