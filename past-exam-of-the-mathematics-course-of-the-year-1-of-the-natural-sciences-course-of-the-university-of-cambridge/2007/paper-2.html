Normalization of a probability distribution and the geometric series give 1=A/(1−ρ), so A=1−ρ. For a nonnegative integer n, the strict tail starts at n+1:
P(N>n)=j=n+1∑∞​(1−ρ)ρj=ρn+1.​
(19)
For nonnegative integers n>m, the event {N>n} is contained in {N>m}. Therefore
P(N>n∣N>m)=ρm+1ρn+1​=ρn−m.​
(20)
This is the memorylessness of the geometric distribution with the zero-based counting convention. Keeping strict versus non-strict inequalities explicit is essential: N−m−1, conditional on N>m, has the original distribution of N.

Set g(x,y)=y−(x−2)2. The gradient of g never vanishes, so the Lagrange multiplier equations ∇f=λ∇g apply at a constrained stationary point. They are 4x=−2λ(x−2), 2y=λ, and y=(x−2)2. Eliminating λ,y gives
x+(x−2)3=0.
(48)
With t=x−2, this becomes t3+t+2=(t+1)(t2−t+2)=0. The quadratic factor is (t−1/2)2+7/4>0, so the only real possibility is t=−1. Therefore
(x,y)=(1,1),f=3,λ=2.​
(49)
The level curve through this point is the ellipse 2x2+y2=3, meeting the coordinate axes at (±3/2​,0) and (0,±3​). Its tangent line at (1,1) has slope −2x/y=−2, equal to the derivative 2(x−2) of the constraint parabola there. The two curves are tangent.

Substitute u(y,t)=Y(y)T(t) into the diffusion equation and divide on a region where both factors are nonzero. This gives Y′′/Y=T′/(νT). The two sides depend on different variables, so they equal a separation constant a:
Y′′=aY,T′=νaT.​
(51)
These identities then extend through the zeros of the separated factors. The zero Dirichlet boundary conditions require Y(0)=Y(1)=0. For a nontrivial solution, a must be negative: a=0 gives only a vanishing linear function and a>0 gives only a vanishing hyperbolic-sine solution. Writing a=−k2 gives Y=Asin(ky), and k=nπ, n=1,2,…. Consequently the separated modes of free viscous decay between parallel plates are sin(nπy)e−νn2π2t.
The first initial condition selects the lowest mode:
u(y,t)=sin(πy)e−νπ2t.​
(52)
The superposition principle for a homogeneous linear differential equation states that any linear combination of solutions is again a solution, with the same combination of homogeneous boundary conditions and initial data. It therefore gives
u(y,t)=sin(πy)e−νπ2t+101​sin(3πy)e−9νπ2t.​
(53)
The higher spatial mode decays nine times as fast, so the velocity profile approaches a multiple of the first sine mode before vanishing altogether.
