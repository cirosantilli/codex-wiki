Write Δ=d−b. At an intersection, Δ=λa−μc, so its scalar triple product with a,c vanishes. The expression in the question is exactly
a⋅(c×d)+c⋅(a×b)=Δ⋅(a×c)=0.
(16)
Under a shift of origin by h, the direction vectors a,c stay fixed, while b,d both change by −h. Thus Δ and the condition are invariant. For nonparallel lines this is also sufficient for intersection; for parallel lines it only asserts coplanarity and need not imply intersection.
Put δλ=λ2​−λ1​ and δμ=μ2​−μ1​. Then x2​−x1​=δλa and x4​−x1​=(x3​−x1​)+δμc. Bilinearity of the cross product gives
[(x3​−x1​)×(x4​−x1​)]⋅(x2​−x1​)​=δμδλ[(Δ+μ1​c−λ1​a)×c]⋅a=−δμδλΔ⋅(a×c)=Δ⋅[(x4​−x3​)×(x2​−x1​)].​
(17)
The tetrahedron volume is one sixth of the absolute value of this triple product. Sliding either vertex pair along its line preserves ∣δλ∣ or ∣δμ∣, because its separation is fixed and the direction vectors are fixed. Thus the volume is unchanged, including the zero-volume coplanar case. This is tetrahedron volume under sliding opposite edges.
