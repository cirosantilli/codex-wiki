For c>0 and l>0, the zero-endpoint Dirichlet boundary conditions select the Fourier sine basis functions sin(nπx/l). Separation of variables gives temporal factors with angular frequencies ωn​=nπc/l. Zero initial displacement removes the temporal cosine terms, giving
y(x,t)=∑n=1∞​an​sin(nπx/l)sin(nπct/l).
(60)
Differentiating at t=0 and using Fourier orthogonality gives
an​=nπc2​∫0l​v(x)sin(nπx/l)dx.​
(61)
The evaluation property of the given initial velocity means v(x)=δ(x−l/2) in the sense of a Dirac delta, an idealized impulsively struck fixed-end string. Thus an​=2sin(nπ/2)/(nπc): every even mode vanishes, while the lowest two excited modes have n=1,3. Their ordinary frequencies and amplitude ratio are
f1​=2lc​,f3​=2l3c​,∣a1​∣:∣a3​∣=3:1.​
(62)
The signed coefficient ratio is a3​/a1​=−1/3. The impulse solution is interpreted as a distributional weak solution, rather than one with a smooth initial velocity.
