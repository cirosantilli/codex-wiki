Write Δ=d−b. At an intersection, Δ=λa−μc, so its scalar triple product with a,c vanishes. The expression in the question is exactly

Put δλ=λ2​−λ1​ and δμ=μ2​−μ1​. Then x2​−x1​=δλa and x4​−x1​=(x3​−x1​)+δμc. Bilinearity of the cross product gives

For a separated harmonic function V=F(x)G(y), Laplace's equation gives F′′/F=−G′′/G=−k2. Bounded periodic dependence in x has F=Acos(kx)+Bsin(kx); decay on each half-plane selects G=e−k∣y∣ for k>0, when the potential is matched continuously at the sheet. These are the separated decaying modes. General decaying solutions can be superpositions of them; decay alone does not force a single value of k.
The prescribed cosine density selects k=1 and V=Ccosxe−∣y∣. Its normal derivative jump is −2Ccosx. Matching the stated jump 2πΣ0​cosx gives
V(x,y)=−πΣ0​cosxe−∣y∣.​
(63)
This is the gravitational potential of a sinusoidal sheet in the normalization of the question. It is continuous at the sheet and decays on both sides. Since Σ0​cosx changes sign, it can physically describe a Fourier component of a density perturbation rather than an everywhere positive isolated mass sheet.
