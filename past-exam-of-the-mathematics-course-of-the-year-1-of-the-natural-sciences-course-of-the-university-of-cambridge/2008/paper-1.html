Since n⋅n=1, the vector Kr is perpendicular to n:
n⋅Kr=n⋅r−(n⋅r)(n⋅n)=0.
(14)
It follows that K2r=Kr. If r=tn, then Kr=0; if n⋅r=0, then Kr=r. Thus K=I−nnT is the orthogonal projection matrix onto n⊥: it removes exactly the component normal to the plane.
For the equally inclined plane choose n=(1,1,1)/3​. Reversing its sign gives the same plane; other sign choices give equivalent reflections of the coordinate axes. One suitable orthonormal basis of this plane is
e1​=6​(1,1,−2)​,e2​=2​(−1,1,0)​.
(15)
Their dot products with n vanish, their lengths are one, and their mutual dot product is zero. The equal first two components of e1​ give its equal inclinations to i,j. Because ej​ is already in the projection plane, ej​⋅Kr=ej​⋅r. The coordinates of the projected point are therefore
u=6​x+y−2z​,v=2​−x+y​.​

For a circle of radius R centered at the origin in z=0, its equation x2+y2=R2 becomes 3u2+v2=R2. This is an ellipse with semiaxes R/3​ and R. Translating the original center only translates its orthogonal projection, so the result applies to every circle in that plane. Consequently

The general reason is the orthogonal projection of a circle between planes: the direction common to the planes retains its length, while the perpendicular direction is shortened by the cosine of their angle.
