Near a nonzero stationary point p, Taylor expansion of the gradient gives ∇h(p+v)=H(p)v+O(∣v∣2). Since the Hessian matrix is positive definite, v⋅∇h(p+v)>0 for sufficiently small nonzero v. Thus the gradient arrows point away from each of the four local minima, with different strengths in the two eigenvector directions.
At the origin, on the x-axis the gradient is (6x5,0) and on the y-axis it is (0,6y5), so arrows point outward. On y=x the gradient is 6x3(x2−8)(1,1), and on y=−x it is 6x3(x2−8)(1,−1), so arrows point toward the origin for small ∣x∣. Away from the axes the leading terms are (−48xy2,−48x2y), pointing inward in both coordinate signs. The narrow outward sectors close to the axes are visible in the exact vector field.
