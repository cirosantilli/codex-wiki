Three vectors in real three-dimensional space have zero determinant exactly when they are linearly dependent. Their linear span then has dimension at most two, so they lie in a common plane through the origin. Conversely vectors in such a plane span at most two dimensions, so their scalar triple product vanishes. This includes parallel nonzero vectors: they need not determine a unique plane, but they lie in at least one. Even zero vectors satisfy the determinant characterization, though the question excludes them.
