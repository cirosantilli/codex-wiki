An antiderivative of the initial velocity is −2ce−s2/2. Thus D'Alembert's formula gives
ψ(x,t)=2c1​[−2ce−s2/2]x−ctx+ct​=e−(x−ct)2/2−e−(x+ct)2/2​.
(118)
This is a positive Gaussian function moving right and a negative Gaussian function moving left. Their cancellation at t=0 gives the zero initial displacement, while direct differentiation gives ψt​(x,0)=2cxe−x2/2.
For every t>0, the profile is an odd function of x, vanishes at x=0, is positive for x>0 and negative for x<0, and tends to zero at both spatial infinities. The signs follow from (x+ct)2−(x−ct)2=4xct. The positive and negative lobes move farther apart as t increases; for large separation they approach the individual unit-height Gaussian functions centred at x=±ct. At finite separation the actual extrema lie slightly beyond these centres: writing a=ct, the positive global maximum occurs at the unique x>a satisfying (x−a)/(x+a)=e−2ax.
