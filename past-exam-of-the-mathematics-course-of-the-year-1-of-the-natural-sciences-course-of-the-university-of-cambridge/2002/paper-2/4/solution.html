The number R of successes has a binomial distribution. Each arrangement of r successes and n−r failures has probability prqn−r by independence, and the binomial coefficient counts the arrangements. Therefore
pr​=(rn​)prqn−r,0≤r≤n;r=0∑n​pr​=(p+q)n=1.​
(14)
The final equality is the binomial theorem. Writing R as a sum of independent Bernoulli random variables gives its expectation and variance: ER=np, Var(R)=npq. Thus the matching normal distribution has
μ=np,σ=npq​.​
(15)
The normal approximation is appropriate when both expected counts np and nq are sufficiently large; large n alone is insufficient if p is extremely close to zero or one.
