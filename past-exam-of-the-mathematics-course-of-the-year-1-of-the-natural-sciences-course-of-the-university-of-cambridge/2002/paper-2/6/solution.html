Compute the curl component by component from the given vector field:
B=∇×A=(2xz, 2yz, 2(1−z2)).​
(22)
For example, Bx​=∂y​Az​−∂z​Ay​=0−(−2xz) and Bz​=∂x​Ay​−∂y​Ax​=(1−z2)−(z2−1). Its divergence is
∇⋅B=2z+2z−4z=0,​
(23)
as expected because the divergence of a curl is zero.
Take C counterclockwise when viewed from positive z, the disc D upward oriented, and the hemisphere H outward oriented. These choices give both surfaces the same positive boundary orientation. Parametrize C by r(t)=(cost,sint,0), 0≤t≤2π. On it, A=(−sint,cost,0) and dr=(−sint,cost,0)dt, so the line integral is
∮C​A⋅dr=∫02π​1dt=2π.​
(24)
On D, use polar coordinates (x,y)=(rcosϕ,rsinϕ) and dS=(0,0,1)rdrdϕ. Since Bz​=2 there, the surface flux is
∫D​B⋅dS=∫02π​∫01​2rdrdϕ=2π.​
(25)
On the unit hemisphere, the outward unit normal vector is n=(x,y,z). Since x2+y2=1−z2, we have B⋅n=4z(1−z2). In spherical polar coordinates, z=cosθ and dS=sinθdθdϕ, giving the explicit surface integral
∫H​B⋅dS=∫02π​∫0π/2​4cosθsin3θdθdϕ=2π.​
(26)
Indeed the inner integral is [sin4θ]0π/2​=1.
These equalities illustrate Stokes theorem twice: B=∇×A and either upward D or outward H spans the oriented circle C, so each surface flux equals the same line integral. For the divergence theorem, close the upper half-ball by adjoining the downward oriented base, namely −D. Its outward surface flux is
∫H∪(−D)​B⋅dS=2π−2π=0=∫V​∇⋅BdV.
(27)
The minus sign on the base is required because its outward normal vector points down. Reversing a specified orientation reverses that integral's sign.
