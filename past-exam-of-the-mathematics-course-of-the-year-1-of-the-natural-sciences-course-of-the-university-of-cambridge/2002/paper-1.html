For two independent smooth constraints g=h=0, the tangent direction is orthogonal to both gradients. At a constrained stationary point, the gradient of the objective is also orthogonal to that direction, hence lies in their span: ∇f=λ∇g+μ∇h. This is the two-constraint Lagrange multiplier condition. Alternatively, distance extrema on a central plane section of an ellipsoid follow from a two-dimensional eigenvalue problem.
Minimizing distance is equivalent to minimizing f=x2+y2+z2. Put g=x−y+z and h=x2+(y2+z2)/4−1. The Lagrange multiplier equations are
2(1−μ)x=λ,(2−μ/2)y=−λ,(2−μ/2)z=λ.
(13)
Adding the latter two gives (2−μ/2)(y+z)=0. If μ=4, those equations give λ=x=0; the constraints then give y=z=±2​ and f=4. Otherwise z=−y and x=2y. The ellipsoidal constraint gives y=±2​/3, and hence
(x,y,z)=±32​​(2,1,−1),f=34​.
(14)
The intersection is compact. The two constraint gradients are independent there: if ∇h=t∇g, then (x,y,z)=(t/2,−2t,2t), and g=0 forces t=0, contradicting h=0. Thus every global extremum is among these candidates. Taking square roots gives
dmin​=3​2​,dmax​=2.​
(15)

Put τ=t+b and q=x+a. For θ=f(t)e−q2/(4τ), partial differentiation gives
θt​=(f′+4τ2q2f​)e−q2/(4τ),θxx​=(−2τf​+4τ2q2f​)e−q2/(4τ).
(39)
The heat equation therefore requires f′=−f/(2τ), giving f=C/∣t+b∣​ on an interval not containing −b. A decaying spatial Gaussian function uses t+b>0, so f=C/t+b​ there.
The initial profile is a difference of two translated Gaussian functions. Set b=1/4, a=−2 and a=2, with respective constants C=1/2 and C=−1/2. The superposition principle gives
θ(x,t)=1+4t​e−(x−2)2/(1+4t)−e−(x+2)2/(1+4t)​,t≥0.​
(40)
This is an odd pair of Gaussian profiles under diffusion, obtained from Gaussian initial data for the heat equation. It is odd in x, vanishes at zero, is positive for x>0 and negative for x<0. The two lobes broaden and decrease in height, and θ(x,t)→0 at every fixed x as t→∞. Their extrema move; they do not remain exactly at ±2.

Expand the constant right-edge value in a Fourier sine series. Its coefficients are
bn​=π2​∫0π​sin(ny)dy=nπ2[1−(−1)n]​.
(49)
The superposition principle combines the normalized modes, giving

(50)

The stated open-edge condition avoids that incompatibility, and isolated corner assignments do not change the bounded interior Dirichlet problem.
