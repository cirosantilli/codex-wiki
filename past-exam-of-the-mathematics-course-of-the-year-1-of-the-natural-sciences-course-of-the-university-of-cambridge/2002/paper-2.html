Three vectors in three-dimensional Euclidean space are non-coplanar precisely when their scalar triple product is nonzero. Put Δ=a⋅(b×c)=0. In particular, a=0. The identity for cross products with a common vector gives
(a×b)×(a×c)=[a⋅(b×c)]a=Δa.
(1)
For completeness, this follows from the vector triple product identity: writing p=a×b gives p×(a×c)=a(p⋅c)−c(p⋅a), with p⋅c=Δ and p⋅a=0. Therefore the new scalar triple product is
a⋅[(a×b)×(a×c)]=∣a∣2Δ=0.​
(2)
Thus a,a×b,a×c are non-coplanar, or equivalently linearly independent.

The comparison test for series states that if 0≤an​≤bn​ for all sufficiently large n, then convergence of ∑bn​ implies convergence of ∑an​; conversely, divergence of ∑an​ implies divergence of ∑bn​. A summable bound on ∣an​∣ proves absolute convergence of ∑an​.
For the ratio test, suppose the terms are eventually nonzero and L=limn→∞​∣an+1​/an​∣ exists. If L<1, the series converges absolutely. If L>1, including L=∞, the terms fail to tend to zero and the series diverges. If L=1, the test is inconclusive. An eventually zero series converges separately. Convergence means that the sequence of partial sums has a finite limit; the terms tending to zero is only a necessary condition for a convergent series.

Near a nondegenerate stationary point, the quadratic part of the Taylor expansion determines the local contour plot. For a strict local maximum, the model f=f0​−X2−Y2 gives nested closed level sets, with values decreasing as one moves outward. For a strict local minimum, the model f=f0​+X2+Y2 gives the same nested shape with values increasing outward. Unequal nonzero quadratic coefficients stretch the circles into ellipses after a rotation to the Hessian matrix's eigenvectors.
For a nondegenerate saddle point of a scalar function, the model f=f0​+X2−Y2 gives hyperbolas. Levels above f0​ open along the positive-curvature direction; levels below f0​ open along the negative-curvature direction. The critical level set is the two crossing lines Y=±X. Degenerate stationary points can have different local geometry, so these are the standard nondegenerate sketches.

2 local maxima, 2 local minima, and 5 saddle points.​
(59)
All nine stationary points are nondegenerate, so no higher-order test is needed. Neither type of local extremum is global: f(x,0)=x4−x2→+∞ as ∣x∣→∞, whereas f(0,y)=−y4+4y2→−∞ as ∣y∣→∞.
To organize the contour plot, complete the squares:
f(x,y)=415​+(x2−21​)2−(y2−2)2.
(60)
The level set through all four outer saddle points of a scalar function factors exactly:
f−415​=(x2−y2+23​)(x2+y2−25​).
(61)
It is therefore the union of the circle x2+y2=5/2 and the hyperbola y2−x2=3/2, which cross at those four saddle points of a scalar function. Near the origin, f=−x2+4y2+O((x2+y2)2), so the zero contours have tangents y=±x/2. These two exact structural features, together with the nested contours around the four local extrema, determine the requested sketch. They are an example of a separable quartic landscape with nine stationary points.
