Use the substitution v=y/x for a homogeneous first-order differential equation. Since y′=v+xv′, it gives
xv′=tanv.
(44)
Where sinv=0, this separable differential equation integrates to ln∣sinv∣=ln∣x∣+C, hence sinv=C1​x. One can obtain the same relation without division by sinv: the equation gives (sinv)′=(sinv)/x, so sinv=C1​x, including sinv≡0.
All real branches of the inverse sine are captured by
y(x)=x[kπ+(−1)karcsin(Cx)],C∈R,k∈Z.​
(45)
Each branch is used on an interval with x=0 and ∣Cx∣<1, with fixed k. The strict inequality excludes zeros of cos(y/x), where the original tangent is undefined. When C=0, the formula includes all straight-line solutions y=kπx. Keeping both families of inverse sine branches is essential for the two different initial conditions below.
