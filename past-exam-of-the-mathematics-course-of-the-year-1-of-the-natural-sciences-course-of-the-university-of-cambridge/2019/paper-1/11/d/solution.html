Take r>0 and select a complex argument θ for z. To specify the complex exponentiation, use z1+i=exp((1+i)(lnr+iθ)). Expanding its exponent gives
z1+i=elnr−θei(θ+lnr).
(26)
Its complex logarithm therefore has values
ℜf=lnr−θ,ℑf=θ+lnr+2πk,k∈Z.​
(27)
The continuous logarithm obtained by retaining the displayed exponent uses k=0. If the outer logarithm is the principal logarithm, choose k to put the imaginary part in (−π,π] instead; the real part is unchanged. In particular one cannot generally assert Log(ew)=w without checking its imaginary part.
The condition on the real part gives lnr=θ, so the required logarithmic spiral has polar coordinates
r=eθ,z(θ)=eθ(cosθ+isinθ).​
(28)
As θ increases, it runs from the negative real axis near radius e−π through −ie−π/2, 1 and ieπ/2 to the negative real axis at radius eπ. The angle increases by one complete turn. Using the conventional principal argument −π<θ≤π, the inner negative-axis endpoint is excluded and the outer endpoint is included. The convention −π≤θ<π reverses those endpoint choices. If both limiting arguments are admitted by the paper's wording, both limiting endpoints are drawn; the interior curve is the same. The origin is never included because the complex logarithm is undefined there.
