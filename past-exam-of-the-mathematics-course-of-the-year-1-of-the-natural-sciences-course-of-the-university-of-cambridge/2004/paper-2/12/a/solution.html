The base costs ab, the glass front costs 2ac, the wooden rear costs ac, and the other two walls cost 2bc. Thus minimize C=ab+3ac+2bc subject to abc=V>0. With the Lagrange multiplier convention ∇C=λ∇(abc), the equations are
b+3c=λbc,a+2c=λac,3a+2b=λab.
(57)
Multiply these by a,b,c, respectively. Comparing their equal right sides gives ab=3ac=2bc. Since all dimensions are positive, a=2c and b=3c. The volume condition then yields
(a,b,c)=(2,3,1)(6V​)1/3.​
(58)
This stationary shape is the unique global minimum, not merely a candidate: the arithmetic-geometric mean inequality applied to the three cost terms gives
C≥3[(ab)(3ac)(2bc)]1/3=3(6V2)1/3,
(59)
with equality exactly at those ratios. Therefore the least cost, in pounds in the specified units, is
Cmin​=361/3V2/3=18(6V​)2/3.​
(60)
This is a weighted open-box minimization, with each pair of opposite walls combined into its appropriate positive face weight.
