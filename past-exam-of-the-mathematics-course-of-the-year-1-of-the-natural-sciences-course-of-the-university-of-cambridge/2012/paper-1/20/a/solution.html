Take positive dimensions measured in metres. The volume is V=lwh and the external surface area constraint is S=lw+2lh+2wh=12. There is one base and four walls, with no lid. The Lagrange multiplier equations for V−λ(S−12) are
wh=λ(w+2h),lh=λ(l+2h),lw=2λ(l+w).
(81)
The first equation implies λ>0. Multiply the first by l and the second by w and subtract; this gives 2λh(l−w)=0, hence l=w=s. The last equation gives λ=s/4. Substituting into the first yields sh=(s/4)(s+2h) and therefore s=2h.
The constraint now reads s2+4sh=3s2=12. Thus
l=w=2 m,h=1 m,Vmax​=4 m3.​
(82)
For a global check, apply the arithmetic-geometric mean inequality to lw, 2lh and 2wh, whose sum is 12. Their product is 4V2≤43=64, so no feasible box has V>4. Equality holds exactly at the dimensions found above. This is the maximum-volume open rectangular box at fixed surface area.
