Minimize the squared distance F=x2+y2, which has the same minimizers as the distance. The Lagrange multiplier equations for the constraint G=x2+xy−1=0 are
2x=λ(2x+y),2y=λx,x2+xy=1.
(83)
The constraint gradient cannot vanish on this branch, since its second component is x>0. Eliminate λ=2y/x to obtain x2=2xy+y2. Put t=y/x; then t2+2t−1=0, so t=−1±2​. But the constraint says x2(1+t)=1, requiring 1+t>0 and selecting t=2​−1. It follows that
(x,y)=(2−1/4,(2​−1)2−1/4),dmin​=2(2​−1)​.​
(84)
To prove a global minimum on the whole x>0 branch, substitute y=1/x−x into F:
F=2x2+x−2−2≥22​−2.
(85)
The arithmetic-geometric mean inequality proves this bound, with equality only when 2x2=x−2, namely x=2−1/4. Thus the stationary point is the unique closest point on the required branch. Numerically it is approximately (0.840896,0.348311) and its distance is 0.910180 in coordinate units.
