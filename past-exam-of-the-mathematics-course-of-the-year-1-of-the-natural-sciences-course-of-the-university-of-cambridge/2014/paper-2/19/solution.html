For a short string element of length Δx, the small-slope approximation makes the upward components of tension at its two ends Tyx​(x+Δx,t) and −Tyx​(x,t). Its mass is ρΔx. Newton's second law gives
ρΔxytt​=T[yx​(x+Δx,t)−yx​(x,t)].
(75)
Divide by Δx and take the limit to obtain the wave equation on a string,
ytt​=c2yxx​,c=ρT​​​.
(76)
Here ρ is linear mass density, and the derivation assumes small transverse slopes, uniform tension, and a flexible string.
At the right endpoint the string pulls the ring toward the interior. Its vertical force is therefore −Tyx​(L,t) to first order; the frictionless wire supplies no vertical force. The ring obeys
Mytt​(L,t)=−Tyx​(L,t)​.
(77)
This is the dynamic endpoint law for a string with an endpoint mass.
For a separated oscillatory solution, write y=X(x)q(t) with q′′=−ω2q and X′′+(ω/c)2X=0. The fixed left end removes the spatial cosine, so the nonzero normal modes have
yn​(x,t)=[An​cos(ωn​t)+Bn​sin(ωn​t)]sincωn​x​.
(78)
The endpoint law gives Mωn2​sin(ωn​L/c)=T(ωn​/c)cos(ωn​L/c). Since sin(ωn​L/c)=0 would also force the cosine to vanish, it can be divided out for a nonzero mode. Using T=ρc2 gives
cotcωn​L​=ρcMωn​​​.
(79)
For physical M≥0, these give all positive modal frequencies. There is no nonzero static linear mode: the left value and right slope conditions would force it to vanish. Positivity also follows by integration by parts from
ω2(ρ∫0L​X2dx+MX(L)2)=T∫0L​(X′)2dx.
(80)
For M=0, the right endpoint is free, with the Neumann boundary condition yx​(L,t)=0. At L=π, the allowed wave numbers are kn​=n+1/2, n≥0, and ωn​=ckn​. Zero initial displacement removes every cosine-in-time coefficient. The initial velocity contains only the modes k=1/2 and k=5/2, each with coefficient one. Dividing those velocity coefficients by their angular frequencies gives
y(x,t)=c2​sin2ct​sin2x​+5c2​sin25ct​sin25x​​.
(81)
At x=0 each term vanishes, at x=π each spatial derivative vanishes, and at t=0 the displacement and its time derivative reproduce the two initial conditions.
