OurBigBook.com Words: 187
Assume positive corridor widths a,b. Write the line through the two wall-contact endpoints in intercept form. Requiring it to pass through the inner corner gives
a+Xa​+b+Yb​=1⟺XY=ab,X,Y>0.
(91)
Its squared length is F=(a+X)2+(b+Y)2. Apply Lagrange multipliers to this function subject to G=XY−ab=0:
2(a+X)=λY,2(b+Y)=λX.
(92)
Multiplying by X and Y respectively eliminates the multiplier, giving X(a+X)=Y(b+Y). Substitute Y=ab/X and multiply by X2:
X3(a+X)=ab2(a+X),(X3−ab2)(X+a)=0.​
(93)
The branch X=−a is geometrically inadmissible. Thus X=a1/3b2/3 and Y=a2/3b1/3. If A=a1/3 and B=b1/3, the two intercepts become A(A2+B2) and B(A2+B2), so
Fmin​=(A2+B2)3,L=(a2/3+b2/3)3/2.​
(94)
Along XY=ab in the positive quadrant, F→∞ as X→0+ or X→∞, and this is its only stationary point. It is therefore the minimum of the longest sticks that fit at each orientation. A stick being turned must pass through every intermediate orientation, so this narrowest clearance is precisely the maximum carryable length, rather than a maximum of F. This is the ladder around a right-angled corner problem, with a zero-width stick and wall contact allowed as in the ideal geometry.
