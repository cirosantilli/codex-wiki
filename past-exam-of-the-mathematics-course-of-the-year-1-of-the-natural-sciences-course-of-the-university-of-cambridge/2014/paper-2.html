Use the usual model of independent games, each with Sheila-win probability θ. After m+n games, an outcome with m wins and n losses has (mm+n​) possible orders. Since both totals are below eleven, none of those sequences has already ended the match. Thus
P(m wins,n losses)=(mm+n​)θm(1−θ)n​.

For a completed Sheila win with n losses, the last game must be her eleventh win. The preceding 10+n games contain ten wins and n losses, so their probability is (1010+n​)θ10(1−θ)n, followed by one further factor θ. The allowed loss counts are 0≤n≤10, and these terminal events are disjoint. Hence
