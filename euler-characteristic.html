Euler characteristic (χ)

 ... Mathematics Area of mathematics Geometry and topology Algebraic topology Homology (mathematics) Simplicial homology
OurBigBook.com Words: 2k Articles: 19
The Euler characteristic compresses a finite CW complex's homology into one integer: count its topological cells with alternating signs, or count its Betti numbers with the same signs. A filled triangle has Euler characteristic one; its cycle graph boundary has Euler characteristic zero. The missing face changes the answer even though the vertices and edges are identical.
For a finite CW complex X, including the geometric realization of a finite simplicial complex,
χ(X)=∑i​(−1)idimQ​Hi​(X;Q).
(22)
Here bi​=dimQ​Hi​(X;Q) is the rational Betti number. More generally this ordinary homology definition makes sense whenever every rational homology group is finite-dimensional and only finitely many are nonzero. An arbitrary infinite CW complex need not satisfy these assumptions; its formal infinite alternating cell count is not a definition of this invariant.
The usefulness is that a finite description can reveal something about a space without computing every homology group. By the cell-count formula for Euler characteristic, count pieces rather than solve all the boundary operators. If two spaces give different answers, topological invariance of Euler characteristic rules out a homeomorphism, and even a homotopy equivalence. Equal answers are less decisive: the Euler characteristic of an annulus and the Euler characteristic of a torus are both zero, while their homology groups differ. This is a compact diagnostic, with a clearly defined limit on what it can tell us.
