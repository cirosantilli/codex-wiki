Group of order six

 Home Mathematics Area of mathematics Algebra Group theory Finite group theory
OurBigBook.com Words: 159
Every group of order six is isomorphic to the cyclic group C6​ or the symmetric group on three points S3​. Lagrange's theorem restricts element orders to 1,2,3,6. If there is an element of order six, it generates the group. If every nonidentity element had order two, the group of exponent two is abelian result would produce a subgroup {e,a,b,ab} of order four, contradicting Lagrange's theorem. Thus the noncyclic case has an element x of order three.
There cannot be two distinct subgroups of order three: their intersection would be trivial and their nine pairwise products would be distinct elements of a six-element group. Hence exactly two elements have order three, and the other three nonidentity elements have order two. The subgroup ⟨x⟩ has index two and is normal. Choose an involution h outside it. Conjugation sends x to x or x−1. The first case would make xh have order six, so hxh−1=x−1. The generators therefore obey x3=h2=e, hxh−1=x−1, the relations realized by (123) and (12) in S3​.
