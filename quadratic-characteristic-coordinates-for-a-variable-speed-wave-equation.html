On x>0, the linear partial differential equation uxx​−x2uyy​−ux​/x=0 has characteristic curves y±x2/2=constant. The change of variables ξ=x2−2y, η=x2+2y has Jacobian determinant 8x=0. The chain rule gives
uxx​−x2uyy​−ux​/x=16x2Uξη​.
(21)
Thus its canonical form of a second-order partial differential equation is Uξη​=0, and its general twice differentiable solution is u=f(x2−2y)+g(x2+2y). The first derivative term exactly cancels the derivatives of the coordinate coefficients.
More generally, for positive differentiable a(x), choose s(x) with s′(x)=a(x). Then the same cancellation gives
uxx​−a(x)2uyy​−a(x)a′(x)​ux​=a(x)2(Uss​−Uyy​).
(22)
The characteristic coordinates are s±y. The quadratic example is a(x)=x, s=x2/2, with both coordinates multiplied by two.
