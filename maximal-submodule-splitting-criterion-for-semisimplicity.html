Maximal-submodule splitting criterion for semisimplicity (∀M maximal in V,V=M⊕C⟹V semisimple)

 ... Mathematics Area of mathematics Algebra Commutative algebra Module theory Semisimple module
OurBigBook.com Words: 129
For a finite-dimensional module, it suffices that every maximal proper submodule have a submodule complement in order that every submodule have one. Let W be the sum of all simple modules contained in the given module. If W is proper, choose a maximal submodule M containing it. A complement C is isomorphic to the simple quotient V/M and hence is itself simple. This forces C⊆W⊆M, contradicting the complement. Thus W=V. Greedily adding simple summands not already in the sum gives a direct sum in finitely many steps. Finally, given any U, choose a maximal sum of these simple summands disjoint from U. A missing simple summand has zero intersection with their sum plus U, contradicting maximality unless their sum complements U. This proves the semisimple module criterion, including the zero module.
