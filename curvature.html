Curvature (κ)

 Home Mathematics Area of mathematics Geometry and topology Differential geometry
OurBigBook.com Words: 686 Articles: 3
For a twice continuously differentiable regular curve r(t) in Euclidean space, its unsigned curvature measures how quickly its unit tangent vector changes per unit arc length. Write
v(t)=∥r′(t)∥>0,dtds​=v(t),T(t)=v(t)r′(t)​.
(6)
The curvature is
κ=​dsdT​​=v(t)1​​dtdT​​≥0,
(7)
where the norm is the Euclidean norm. Differentiating with respect to arc length removes the effect of traversal speed; differentiating T with respect to t alone generally does not. See Ghomi's university lecture notes, section 1.7.
For a circle of radius R>0, use r(t)=(Rcost,Rsint). Then v=R, T=(−sint,cost) and ∥dT/dt∥=1, giving κ=1/R. A straight line r(t)=p+ta with a=0 has constant unit tangent vector, so κ=0.
The same circle traversed as q(u)=(Rcos(3u),Rsin(3u)) has speed 3R and ∥dT/du∥=3: its curvature remains 1/R. By contrast, enlarging the circle to radius 2R changes its geometry and gives curvature 1/(2R). More generally, a dilation r↦ar with a>0 divides curvature by a, because it multiplies arc length by a while leaving unit tangent vectors unchanged. Thus curvature has units of inverse length, and a reparametrization changes neither its value at a corresponding point nor the shape.
Differentiating T⋅T=1, using the dot product, shows T⋅(dT/ds)=0: the change is perpendicular to the unit tangent vector. Wherever κ>0, the principal normal vector Np​=(dT/ds)/κ points toward the bend. For a plane curve, the osculating circle at such a point has radius of curvature ρ=1/κ and center r+ρNp​. It matches the point, tangent line, and curvature, providing a local circular approximation. At κ=0 there is no finite osculating circle defined by this formula. See Ghomi's university lecture notes, section 1.13.
This makes curvature useful for comparing bends without confusing them with speed. If t denotes time, differentiating r′=vT gives acceleration r′′=v′T+v2(dT/ds), whose component perpendicular to the unit tangent vector has magnitude v2κ. The curvature of a plane curve example below computes the bend and osculating circle of a parabola explicitly.
For smooth surfaces, bending has more than one direction. The principal curvatures k1​,k2​ instead define Gaussian curvature K=k1​k2​ and mean curvature H=(k1​+k2​)/2. These are surface quantities, distinct from the curvature of an individual curve. In particular, the circle value 1/R must not be confused with the Gaussian curvature 1/R2 of a sphere of radius R. See Ritter's Oxford lecture notes, section 12.2.
