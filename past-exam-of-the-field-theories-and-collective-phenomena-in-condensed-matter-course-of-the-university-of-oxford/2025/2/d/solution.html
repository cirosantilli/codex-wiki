Put r=tanhK, so r→1− as the temperature tends to zero. For even N, the finite antiferromagnetic Ising-ring correlation becomes
CN​(n)=(−1)n1+rNrn+rN−n​⟹βJ→∞lim​CN​(n)=(−1)n(N even).​
(33)
The two perfectly alternating ground states give exactly the same product S1​Sn+1​=(−1)n; averaging them preserves its magnitude even though the one-point correlation function remains zero.
For odd N the signs instead give
CN​(n)=(−1)n1−rNrn−rN−n​.
(34)
Both numerator and denominator vanish. Set r=1−δ and expand at fixed N,n:
rn−rN−n=(N−2n)δ+O(δ2),1−rN=Nδ+O(δ2).
(35)
Hence
βJ→∞lim​CN​(n)=(−1)n(1−N2n​)(N odd).​
(36)
There is a direct ground state interpretation. The mandatory parallel bond of an odd antiferromagnetic Ising ring is uniformly distributed among its N bonds in the limiting canonical ensemble. Along the directed n-bond arc from site 1 to site n+1, the product is (−1)n if the defect lies outside that arc, and −(−1)n if it lies inside. The respective probabilities are 1−n/N and n/N, giving the displayed two-point correlation function. Global spin inversion symmetry doubles the number of ground states but leaves each product unchanged.
The reduction on an odd ring survives at separations comparable with N, despite the vanishing defect density 1/N. For fixed n, however, taking N→∞ after the zero-temperature limit recovers (−1)n for either parity, consistently with the diverging correlation length. The linear envelope at finite odd N comes from one freely located frustration defect; it is distinct from the exponential decay caused by thermal defects in the thermodynamic limit.
