Secondary steady and Hopf thresholds of a resonant pure mode (rS​=−2Re(βd)∣d∣2​,rH​=−βR​dR​​)

OurBigBook.com Words: 102
In the symmetric resonant cubic amplitude equation, put A=r​eiΩt, B=CeiΩt, with dR​>0, βR​<0. The transverse equation is C˙=−(d+βr)C−βrC. Its real matrix has trace −2(dR​+βR​r) and determinant ∣d∣2+2rQ, where Q=Re(βd). A positive steady threshold requires Q<0; a simple zero eigenvalue also requires Q=Q∗​=βR​∣d∣2/(2dR​). A Hopf bifurcation requires Q>Q∗​, giving a positive determinant at rH​. For Q<Q∗​ the first instability is steady, producing reflection-related mixed phase-locked periodic branches. For Q>Q∗​ it is Hopf, producing modulated oscillations, generically quasiperiodic in the original variables. Equality is a double-zero degeneracy.
