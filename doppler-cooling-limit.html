Doppler cooling limit (TD​=ℏΓ/(2kB​))

OurBigBook.com Words: 213
Damping removes random kinetic energy, but the discrete photon kicks cause diffusion in momentum. Their balance gives a nonzero temperature. Define the momentum diffusion coefficient Dp​ by the noise contribution d⟨px2​⟩/dt=2Dp​. With atom mass m and linear damping coefficient α,
dtd⟨px2​⟩​=−m2α​⟨px2​⟩+2Dp​,kB​T=m⟨px2​⟩​=αDp​​
(49)
at stationary balance, where kB​ is the Boltzmann constant and mean momentum is zero.
For ideal three-dimensional optical molasses with three identical independent beam pairs and isotropic spontaneous emission, the x-pair absorption rate at rest is Rx​=Γs/(1+4d2). Absorption from that pair contributes (ℏk)2Rx​ to the noise growth of ⟨px2​⟩. Emission from all three pairs contributes the same amount: its total rate is 3Rx​ and each emitted photon has mean squared x-momentum (ℏk)2/3. Hence Dp​=(ℏk)2Rx​. Using the Doppler cooling force coefficient gives
T(d)=8kB​ℏΓ​−d1+4d2​,d<0.
(50)
Put a=−d>0. Then (1+4d2)/(−d)=a−1+4a≥4, with equality at a=1/2. The ideal weak-field Doppler cooling limit is therefore TD​=ℏΓ/(2kB​) at δ=−Γ/2. It is the minimum of this specified diffusion-and-damping model, rather than a universal lower bound for laser cooling. The diffusion approximation also assumes recoil energy much smaller than ℏΓ. In a one-pair geometry, emission patterns and transverse recoil change the diffusion bookkeeping. [ref].
