Karatsuba carry handling

 Home Computer science Algorithm Karatsuba multiplication
OurBigBook.com Words: 180
The blocks in the Karatsuba three-product identity are integers, not single digits: p0​ can need 2m positions and h can exceed q−1. Concatenating the displayed blocks therefore fails. Accumulate their shifted values with ordinary addition, normalizing each digit position by Euclidean division: a coefficient v becomes the digit vmodB, with carry ⌊v/B⌋ passed to the next position. Subtraction likewise propagates borrows. Although h≥0 for these nonnegative integers, intermediate digitwise subtraction can still borrow.
In the four-digit Karatsuba worked multiplication, grouping by base q=100 gives unnormalized coefficients [672,2840,2652], written from high to low. Normalize from the right: 2652=26⋅100+52; 2840+26=2866=28⋅100+66; 672+28=700=7⋅100+0. The final groups are [7,00,66,52], yielding 7,006,652. The zero group must retain its two positions.
Since a,b,c,d<Bm, their sums are less than 2Bm≤Bm+1 and can need m+1 digits. Allocate that extra digit before the third recursive product; discarding it can change the answer. The GMP manual describes this sum growth and uses a difference-based form to avoid it.[ref]
