Minkowski inequality for error aggregation (∥f−a∥p​≤∥f−b∥p​+∥b−a∥p​)

 ... Mathematics Area of mathematics Analysis Real analysis Lp norm Minkowski inequality
OurBigBook.com Words: 210
Suppose an approximation a is compared with a target f through an intermediate approximation b, with both error functions in the same Lp space. The identity f−a=(f−b)+(b−a) and the Minkowski inequality combine two error budgets without any independence assumption. The L1 norm controls aggregate absolute error, the L2 norm controls the square root of aggregate quadratic loss, and the L-infinity norm controls the largest error almost everywhere. For equally weighted samples with the probability measure 1/n on each coordinate, the L2 norm is the root mean square error; the inequality still applies when this normalization is used consistently.
For example, two signed error vectors e=(3,0) and r=(0,4) have Euclidean norms 3 and 4, while ∥e+r∥2​=5≤7. Reinforcing errors e=(3,0) and r=(4,0) attain the bound 7; opposing errors e=(3,0) and r=(−3,0) cancel completely. The Minkowski inequality always allows reinforcement. A smaller combined budget requires additional information about the errors, not just their separate norms.
Iteration gives ∥∑k=1N​ek​∥p​≤∑k=1N​∥ek​∥p​ for every finite sum. For an infinite series, if all ek​ belong to one fixed Lp space with 1≤p≤∞ and ∑k=1∞​∥ek​∥p​<∞, its partial sums form a Cauchy sequence in the full Lp norm. Completeness of this Banach space then gives norm convergence to an element of the same Lp space. A finite sum bound alone does not establish convergence, and completeness is an additional ingredient beyond the Minkowski inequality. [ref]
