Weighted arithmetic mean (xˉw​)

 ... Mathematics Area of mathematics Arithmetic Addition Summation Sum
OurBigBook.com Words: 1k Articles: 5
The weighted arithmetic mean combines values according to how much each contributes to the quantity being summarized. For a finite list of real numbers xi​ and nonnegative weights wi​ with W=∑i​wi​>0, define
xˉw​=W∑i​wi​xi​​.​
(13)
Zero weights ignore an entry; at least one weight must be positive. Multiplying every weight by the same positive number leaves the result unchanged. Writing pi​=wi​/W makes the weighted mean a convex combination, since pi​≥0 and ∑i​pi​=1. It therefore lies between the smallest and largest input values. [ref]
For example, give the values 90 and 60 weights 2 and 8:
xˉw​=2+82⋅90+8⋅60​=66​.
(14)
The larger weight pulls the result closer to 60. The balancing identity
∑i​wi​(xi​−xˉw​)=0
(15)
explains a geometric interpretation: if the weights were masses placed at positions xi​, the weighted mean would be their center of mass. Here 8(66−60)=2(90−66)=48, so the two sides balance even though their distances from the mean differ.
Weights need an interpretation. They can represent numbers of observations, masses, or probabilities in a finite probability distribution. These meanings lead to the same formula, while answering different questions. For instance, combining means of groups uses group sizes as weights to recover the mean over individual observations. For a function f and nonnegative weight function w, the analogous integral is ∫wf/∫w, provided wf is integrable and the total weight ∫w is positive and finite.
