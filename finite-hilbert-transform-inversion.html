For G∈C1([0,1]), the displayed expression solves Tf=G, where T=H(0,1)​/i and H(0,1)​ is the finite Hilbert transform with denominator t−x. To derive it, put S(z)=z(z−1)​∼z at infinity, take h to be the Cauchy transform of iρG, and set w=h/S. The Sokhotski–Plemelj formula gives w+​+w−​=G and the displayed jump f=w+​−w−​. The endpoint behavior is at most inverse square root, and w=O(z−2) at infinity. Cauchy reconstruction from a jump across a finite interval then gives w=C[f] and hence Tf=G. Adding any constant multiple of 1/ρ gives another solution in the integrable endpoint class.
Multiplying an auxiliary sign-changing factor by a growing analytic factor can invalidate this reconstruction. For example, using S instead of 1/S with G=1 produces the constant function w=1/2 and zero jump. The boundary sum alone is not enough: the entire remainder in reconstructing w from its jump must also be controlled.
