Angle-resolved photoemission spectroscopy

 Home Chemistry Physical chemistry Photoemission spectroscopy
OurBigBook.com Words: 246 Articles: 2
Angle-resolved photoemission spectroscopy measures the kinetic energy and emission angle of electrons removed from a solid by light. With photon energy hν, sample work function Φ, and binding energy EB​ measured downwards from the Fermi level, Ekin​=hν−Φ−EB​. The measured parallel momentum is k∥​=2me​Ekin​​sinθ/ℏ, modulo a surface reciprocal-lattice vector. Bright ridges reveal occupied electronic band structure; their slopes give the corresponding group velocity. Extracting a Fermi energy relative to a Dirac point requires a calibrated Fermi level, not merely an unlabelled image boundary.
