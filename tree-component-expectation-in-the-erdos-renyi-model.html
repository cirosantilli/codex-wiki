For Xj​ the number of tree components of order j in a binomial random graph, choose their vertices, choose one of jj−2 labelled trees by the Cayley formula, require its j−1 edges, and exclude both the remaining internal edges and all crossing edges. For j=1 interpret jj−2=1, recovering the isolated vertex count. If p=λ/n for fixed positive λ and j=O(logn), the Stirling formula gives EXj​∼n(λe1−λ)j/(λ2π​j5/2). Distinct overlapping vertex sets cannot both be graph components; for disjoint sets their joint occurrence gains the factor (1−p)−j2 relative to the product, because their between-set edges must be absent only once.
