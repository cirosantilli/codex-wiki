Minimal polynomial of a normal operator (mT​=∏i​fi​(TT∗=T∗T))

 ... Mathematics Area of mathematics Algebra Linear algebra Operator theory Minimal polynomial
OurBigBook.com Words: 92
On a real or complex inner product space of finite dimension, a normal operator has a square-free polynomial as its minimal polynomial. To prove this without the spectral theorem, suppose an irreducible polynomial f occurs at least twice in mT​. Put h=mT​/f2 and N=f(T). Since polynomials of a normal operator are normal, N is a normal operator. For every v,
N2h(T)v=mT​(T)v=0.
(42)
By kernel stabilization for a normal operator, Nh(T)v=0. Hence the smaller-degree monic polynomial mT​/f annihilates T, contradicting minimality.
Over the complex numbers, all irreducible polynomials are linear, so the primary decomposition theorem for linear maps now gives a basis of eigenvectors. Over the real numbers, irreducible quadratics can remain: a nontrivial planar rotation need not have any real eigenvectors, although its minimal polynomial is square-free.
