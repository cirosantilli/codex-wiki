Time-independent Schrödinger equation (Hψ=Eψ)

 Home Physics Mathematical physics Schrödinger equation
OurBigBook.com Words: 635 Articles: 8
For a time-independent Hamiltonian operator H, an energy eigenstate satisfies
Hψ=Eψ.
(90)
For a one-dimensional particle of mass m in a real potential energy U(x),
−2mℏ2​ψ′′+U(x)ψ=Eψ.
(91)
Its full time-dependent wavefunction is Ψ(x,t)=ψ(x)e−iEt/ℏ: substitution in the Schrödinger equation checks both the minus sign and the factor ℏ. Thus an energy eigenstate has stationary position probability density, even though its quantum phase evolves. The boundary conditions and operator domain are part of this eigenvalue problem, selecting physically allowed solutions; the infinite square well and Radial Schrodinger equation for the hydrogen atom give complementary confined examples.
For a complete discrete orthonormal basis of energy eigenstates, linearity gives Ψ(x,t)=∑n​cn​ψn​(x)e−iEn​t/ℏ, with ∑n​∣cn​∣2=1. Cross terms in ∣Ψ∣2 oscillate at angular frequencies (En​−Em​)/ℏ. A continuous spectrum requires the corresponding spectral integrals; for a free particle on the whole line, individual plane waves are generalized eigenfunctions, and a normalized Gaussian wave packet is their integral quantum superposition. Adding a spatially constant real C to U shifts every energy eigenvalue by C and multiplies the full state by e−iCt/ℏ, a global phase; energy differences, position probabilities and probability current are unchanged.
