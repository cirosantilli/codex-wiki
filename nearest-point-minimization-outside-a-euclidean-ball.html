Nearest-point minimization outside a Euclidean ball (argmin∥x∥≥R​∥x−a∥2=Ra/∥a∥(0<∥a∥<R))

Suppose 0<∥a∥<R in a Euclidean space. Among points outside or on the radius-R ball centred at zero, the unique closest point to a is x∗​=Ra/∥a∥. Its squared distance is (R−∥a∥)2.
Indeed, the reverse triangle inequality gives ∥x−a∥≥∥x∥−∥a∥≥R−∥a∥. Equality requires ∥x∥=R and x pointing in the same direction as a, giving the stated unique point. If a=0, every point on the sphere is a minimizer. If ∥a∥≥R, the point a itself is feasible and the minimum distance is zero.
The exterior is not a convex set, so a direct geometric argument is useful: it certifies the global answer without treating every candidate satisfying the Karush-Kuhn-Tucker conditions as a minimum.
