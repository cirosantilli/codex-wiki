Finite trigonometric sum

Taking the real and imaginary parts of the finite geometric series ∑m=1N​eimθ yields
∑m=1N​cos(mθ)=sin(θ/2)sin(Nθ/2)cos((N+1)θ/2)​,∑m=1N​sin(mθ)=sin(θ/2)sin(Nθ/2)sin((N+1)θ/2)​.
(26)
These formulas apply when θ∈/2πZ. At multiples of 2π, the sums are directly N and 0. Complex exponentials turn two real trigonometric summations into one finite geometric series.
