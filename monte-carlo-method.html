Quantum Monte Carlo

Words: 31
Monte Carlo method OurBigBook.com
Stochastic methods for quantum many-particle expectation values or ground-state projection. Variational and diffusion approaches can include correlations beyond a single Slater determinant; fermion signs or nodal constraints and finite-system extrapolation require explicit control.

Pseudorandom number generator

Words: 315 Articles: 5
Monte Carlo method OurBigBook.com
A pseudorandom number generator is a deterministic state-update algorithm producing numbers intended to imitate an independent sequence from a prescribed probability distribution. For Monte Carlo integration, desirable features include good one-dimensional and multidimensional uniformity, a period far longer than the calculation, negligible detectable dependence, reproducible seeds, sufficient numerical precision and fast generation. Uniform marginal distributions alone do not provide independent draws.

Random seed

Words: 26
Pseudorandom number generator OurBigBook.com
A value initializing a pseudorandom generator. Repeating a seed need not reproduce a parallel stochastic calculation unless the generator, stream assignment and implementation are also controlled.

Lagged Fibonacci generator

Words: 43
Pseudorandom number generator OurBigBook.com
A pseudorandom number generator whose state sequence combines earlier values at two fixed lags, for example Sn​=(Sn−j​+Sn−k​)modm with 0<j<k. A seed supplies the first k values. A circular buffer of k entries suffices for generation because each update uses only the retained lag window.

Pseudorandom number (Ui​)

Words: 48
Pseudorandom number generator OurBigBook.com
A pseudorandom number is an output of a deterministic algorithm chosen to imitate samples from a specified probability law. For example, a linear congruential generator returns fractions Si​/M on a finite grid in [0,1). Their intended uniform distribution is an approximation, and their serial dependence must be assessed separately from the generator's period.

Linear congruential generator (Sn+1​=(aSn​+c)modM)

Words: 143 Articles: 1
Pseudorandom number generator OurBigBook.com
Choose integers M>1, a, c and a seed S0​. Iterate Sn+1​=(aSn​+c)modM and return Un​=Sn​/M. This gives a deterministic finite-state pseudorandom number generator with output in [0,1). A nonzero increment gives a mixed generator; c=0 gives a multiplicative generator. Its period is at most M, but even a full period does not guarantee good multidimensional uniformity or statistical independence.

Full-period criterion for a mixed congruential generator (period=M)

Words: 81
Linear congruential generator OurBigBook.com
A linear congruential generator has period M for every seed if and only if gcd(c,M)=1, every prime dividing M divides a−1, and 4∣M implies 4∣a−1. The coprimality of c prevents the orbit from being trapped in a residue class; the multiplier conditions permit full-period lifting through each prime power. Combining the prime-power periods by the Chinese remainder theorem gives period M. For M=12k=22k3k, the multiplier criterion reduces to a≡1(mod12), together with gcd(c,6)=1. A positive increment alone is insufficient.

Monte Carlo integration (IS​=S−1∑s=1S​h(Xs​))

Words: 283 Articles: 4
Monte Carlo method OurBigBook.com
To calculate I=∫h(x)p(x)dx, generate independent Xs​ with density p and average h(Xs​). Integrability gives convergence by the strong law of large numbers; finite variance gives an unbiased estimate with variance Var(h(X))/S. Applying this to indicators estimates probabilities. Correlated Markov chain Monte Carlo samples can also approximate the integral, but their error is determined by the chain's dependence rather than the independent-sample variance formula.

Antithetic variates

Words: 225 Articles: 3
Monte Carlo integration OurBigBook.com
Antithetic variates deliberately couple unbiased estimators of the same expectation with negative covariance. Their average can have smaller variance than an average using independent draws. For example, evaluating a monotone integrand at U and 1−U, where U has a uniform distribution, gives opposite fluctuations while preserving both marginal distributions.

Antithetic Cauchy-tail integration on a finite interval (p​=21​−2rk​∑j=1r​[f(kUj​)+f(k(1−Uj​))])

Words: 48
Antithetic variates OurBigBook.com
For standard Cauchy distribution probability density function f and k>0, this estimates its upper-tail probability without sampling a heavy tail. The function f(ku) decreases on [0,1], while f(k(1−u)) increases, so their covariance is nonpositive. The paired estimator is unbiased and has no larger variance than an estimator using the same number 2r of independent probability density function evaluations.

Minimum-variance combination of unbiased sample means (w∗​=(VY​−C)/(VX​+VY​−2C))

Words: 134 Articles: 1
Antithetic variates OurBigBook.com
For two unbiased estimators A,B of the same parameter with variances VX​,VY​ and covariance C, the fixed-weight estimator wA+(1−w)B is unbiased and has variance w2VX​+(1−w)2VY​+2w(1−w)C. Completing the square gives w∗​=(VY​−C)/(VX​+VY​−2C) and minimum variance (VX​VY​−C2)/(VX​+VY​−2C) when the denominator is positive. These are optimal among fixed linear unbiased combinations. Substituting weights estimated from the same observations need not preserve unbiasedness.
Cross-fitted combination of unbiased sample means
Words: 80
Minimum-variance combination of unbiased sample means OurBigBook.com
Split independent observational blocks into two folds. Use one fold to estimate the combination weight for the two sample means in the other fold, and conversely. Each held-out combination is an unbiased estimator conditionally on its training fold because its two held-out sample means have the same expectation. A deterministic average of the two combinations remains unbiased. Under consistency of the moment estimates and balanced fold sizes, the weights approach those of the minimum-variance combination of unbiased sample means. Paired observations must stay together when splitting or bootstrapping so their covariance is preserved.

Conditional Monte Carlo

Words: 50
Monte Carlo method OurBigBook.com
Replacing a simulated quantity H by E[H∣G] preserves its expectation and reduces variance by the law of total variance. This is Rao-Blackwellization. For a random sum, conditioning on the count and all but one summand replaces an event indicator by a cumulative distribution function evaluation. The resulting estimator needs that conditional expectation to be computable.

Box-Muller transform

Independent uniform distribution draws U1​,U2​ on (0,1) produce independent standard normal distribution draws X1​=−2logU1​​cos(2πU2​) and X2​=−2logU1​​sin(2πU2​). The proof transforms an independent uniform angle and radial density re−r2/2 through the polar-coordinate Jacobian.

Nested Monte Carlo

Words: 45
Monte Carlo method OurBigBook.com
Nested Monte Carlo approximates an outer expected value whose integrand contains another expected value. A nonlinear transformation, such as a logarithm, of an inner sample average usually introduces finite-sample bias. Increasing both sample sizes requires suitable integrability controls to establish consistency; an unbiased estimator of the inner quantity does not make its logarithm unbiased.

A Monte Carlo estimator is a random approximation obtained from simulated samples, commonly a sample average or a weighted sample average.

Summing before taking a mean changes Monte Carlo normalization

Words: 54
Monte Carlo estimator OurBigBook.com
For n observations, taking the mean of their scalar sum returns that sum, not their sample mean. If a−bH is unbiased for a target, replacing the mean by a sum gives expected value a−bnEH and multiplies its variance by n2. A mean-zero control variate changes variance but cannot correct this missing normalization factor.

For zero mean, the bounded-weight inequality gives importance sampling no larger asymptotic variance. Empty rejected samples and per-proposal computational cost are separate considerations.

Variance reversal under additive shifts of an importance-sampling integrand

Words: 62
Proposal-budget variance comparison of importance and rejection sampling OurBigBook.com
An additive constant leaves the target variance and accepted-sample mean variance unchanged, but changes the variance of unnormalized importance sampling because its random weights do not sum to one. With g uniform on (0,1), f(x)=2x and M=2, phi(x)=x gives importance variance 16/45 versus rejection proposal-budget variance 1/9. Replacing phi by x-1 gives importance variance 1/45 with the same rejection variance, reversing preference.

Control variates

Words: 250 Articles: 3
Monte Carlo estimator OurBigBook.com
A control variate is a random variable with known expected value whose centered value is subtracted from a Monte Carlo estimator to reduce its variance. For a mean-zero vector G, put S=Cov(G) and c=Cov(G,H). When S is invertible, the optimal estimator averages H−cTS−1G and has variance (Var(H)−cTS−1c)/N for N independent draws. Improvement is strict exactly when c=0.

Multivariate control variates (β∗​=Σ−1Cov(C,Y))

Words: 82
Control variates OurBigBook.com
If Y is an unbiased estimator and a vector C has known mean, then Y−βT(C−EC) remains unbiased for a deterministic coefficient vector. Its variance is VarY−2βTc+βTΣβ, where c=Cov(C,Y) and Σ=Cov(C). For invertible Σ, completing the square gives the displayed optimum and minimum variance VarY−cTΣ−1c. With singular Σ, use a generalized inverse on its range. Estimating coefficients from the same simulation can introduce finite-sample bias; fixed pilot coefficients or independent training preserve the elementary unbiasedness assertion.

Cauchy tail control variate on a bounded interval (β∗​=Cov(g(Y),H(Y))/Var(H(Y)))

Words: 62
Control variates OurBigBook.com
For Y uniform on (0,2), put g(y)=1/(π(1+y2)) and H(y)=y2−4/3. Then EH=0, and 1/2−2g−βH​ estimates the standard Cauchy distribution probability above two. Its variance is 4Var(g−βH)/n, minimized by the displayed coefficient. Here Var(H)=64/45 and Cov(g,H)=1/π−7arctan(2)/(6π), so the optimal coefficient is negative. A coefficient estimated using a separate pilot can be fixed for a fresh unbiased production sample.

Posterior score control variate (g(β)=∇logp(β∣Y))

Words: 53
Control variates OurBigBook.com
If a smooth posterior density has vanishing boundary terms, its log-posterior gradient satisfies E[g]=0 and
E[g(β)h(β)]=−E[∇h(β)].
(1)
This is a posterior integration-by-parts identity; it concerns differentiation in the random parameter, distinct from the usual mean-zero score identity for sampling distributions. It makes g a vector control variate. Finite moments and known coefficients preserve unbiasedness of the Monte Carlo estimator.

Monte Carlo error

Words: 104 Articles: 1
Monte Carlo method OurBigBook.com
Monte Carlo error is the random difference between a simulation estimate and the exact quantity it approximates. For an average of independent finite-variance draws, its standard deviation is proportional to the inverse square root of the number of draws.

Monte Carlo standard error (MCSE(hˉ)≃σh2​/Neff​​)

Words: 65
Monte Carlo error OurBigBook.com
The Monte Carlo standard error estimates the standard deviation of a simulation-based estimate across repeated simulation runs. For an average of N independent random variables with finite variance σh2​, it is σh​/N​. Under a suitable central limit theorem for Markov chain Monte Carlo, correlated draws instead give the approximation σh​/Neff​​, where Neff​ is the effective sample size of a Markov chain for the particular estimated function h. This measures numerical uncertainty in an estimate, not uncertainty in the unknown parameter itself.

Rejection sampling

Words: 1k Articles: 17
Monte Carlo method OurBigBook.com
Suppose f is a target probability density and h is a proposal density with f(x)≤Mh(x). Draw X∼h and an independent U∼Uniform[0,1], and accept X when
U≤Mh(X)f(X)​.
(2)
The accepted value has density f, the acceptance probability is 1/M, and the expected number of proposals is M.

Acceptance mass for an unnormalized rejection envelope (pacc​=C/M)

Words: 48
Rejection sampling OurBigBook.com
If f≥0, C=∫f∈(0,∞) and f(x)≤Mg(x) for a proposal probability density function g, accept a proposal Y when an independent uniform distribution value is at most f(Y)/(Mg(Y)). Integrating the joint acceptance probability density function gives pacc​=C/M and conditional accepted probability density function f/C. For n independent proposals the accepted count has binomial distribution with parameters n,C/M.

Truncated gamma rejection sampling with a Pareto envelope (x∗​=(α+b)/β)

Words: 57
Rejection sampling OurBigBook.com
For target density proportional to xα−1e−βx on x>a>0, use the Pareto distribution proposal g(x)=babx−b−1 with b>0. Its density ratio is proportional to xα+be−βx, maximized at (α+b)/β if that point exceeds a. The tight envelope yields the highest acceptance probability for this fixed proposal. An inverse draw is aU−1/b with U uniformly distributed on (0,1).

Uniform-envelope rejection sampling for a beta distribution (r(x)=xa−1(1−x)b−1,a,b≥1)

Words: 60
Rejection sampling OurBigBook.com
For a,b>1, the maximum of r on [0,1] occurs at (a−1)/(a+b−2). If one parameter is one, the maximum is attained at the corresponding endpoint; if both are one, r is constant. Let M=supr. Accept an independent uniform distribution proposal X when another uniform draw is at most r(X)/M. The accepted probability density function is the beta distribution, and acceptance probability is B(a,b)/M.

Bayesian rejection sampling for a segregating-site count

Words: 139 Articles: 1
Rejection sampling OurBigBook.com
With proper prior π(θ) independent of the neutral Kingman's coalescent times, propose (θ,T) from their joint prior and accept with probability e−θL/2(θL/2)k/k!. The accepted density is proportional to the prior times the observed-count likelihood, hence is the posterior given S=k. The acceptance rate is the prior predictive probability P(S=k). For k>0, divide the acceptance function by its maximum Mk​=e−kkk/k!, achieved at θL/2=k, to improve the rate to P(S=k)/Mk​ without altering the accepted distribution. For k=0, M0​=1.

Joint posterior of mutation rate and coalescent times

Words: 54

For a proper prior distribution π(θ) independent of coalescent times T, let q(T)=∏j=2n​λj​e−λj​Tj​ with λj​=(2j​) and L=∑jTj​. Observing S=k under an infinite sites mutation model gives posterior density π(θ)q(T)e−θL/2(θL/2)k/(k!mk​), where mk​ is the prior predictive probability of S=k. Multiplication by the conditional Poisson mass and normalization is an application of Bayes' theorem.

Match-biased reshuffling preserves uniform rank marginals

Words: 69
Rejection sampling OurBigBook.com
Accepting a fresh uniform deck permutation with a probability depending only on whether its endpoint ranks match preserves uniform individual rank marginals by symmetry. It changes their joint law. For four cards of each of thirteen ranks and rejection probability p on a mismatch, the accepted matching probability is 1/(17−16p). The two accepted ranks are independent precisely at p=1/4; equality of marginals alone does not imply independence.

Exponential-envelope rejection sampling for a chi-squared variable (χν2​, ν≥2)

Words: 60
Rejection sampling OurBigBook.com
Write a=ν/2. Against an exponential distribution proposal of mean ν, the chi-squared distribution density has envelope constant M=aae1−a/Γ(a). The density ratio divided by M is exp((a−1)(log(y/ν)−y/ν+1)). Thus generate Y=−νlogU and accept when logV≤(a−1)(log(Y/ν)−Y/ν+1) for independent uniform distributions. The inequality logt≤t−1 validates the acceptance probability. The accepted probability density function is the target because rejection sampling weights the proposal by its target-to-envelope ratio.

Ratio-of-uniforms method (X=V/U,0<U≤h(V/U)​)

Words: 282 Articles: 3
Rejection sampling OurBigBook.com
For a nonnegative integrable function h with integral Z, its ratio-of-uniforms region has area Z/2. A uniform point in the region transforms under v=ux with Jacobian determinant u; integrating 2u/Z over 0<u≤h(x)​ gives density h(x)/Z. A rectangle containing the region gives a practical rejection sampling algorithm using only uniform random variables, with acceptance Z/(2area(B)).

Laplace ratio-of-uniforms envelope (a=1,b1​=−4e−5/4,b2​=4e−3/4)

Words: 79
Ratio-of-uniforms method OurBigBook.com
For the unnormalized Laplace distribution probability density function h(x)=e−∣x−1∣/2, the ratio-of-uniforms method uses 0<u≤h(v/u)​. The maximum of h is one. On x≤0, the maximum of x2h(x) occurs at x=−4 and equals 16e−5/2. On x≥0, it occurs at x=4 and equals 16e−3/2. Thus the displayed rectangle contains the region. Its area is 4(e−3/4+e−5/4), while the target region has area 21​∫h=2. Uniform proposals in the rectangle, accepted when u2≤h(v/u), therefore return the correct Laplace distribution with acceptance probability [2(e−3/4+e−5/4)]−1.

Normal ratio-of-uniforms envelope (0<u<1,∣v∣≤2σu−logu​)

Words: 52
Ratio-of-uniforms method OurBigBook.com
For the unnormalized normal density h(x)=e−x2/(2σ2), the smallest axis-aligned envelope is 0<u<1 and ∣v∣≤σ2/e​. The boundary reaches that width at u=e−1/2. Region area is σπ/2​, so the acceptance rate is πe​/4, independently of σ. Multiplying h by a positive constant rescales both coordinates and their areas consistently.

Finite-area envelope for a ratio-of-uniforms region

Words: 96
Ratio-of-uniforms method OurBigBook.com
A finite bounding rectangle exists when h(x)​ and xh(x)​ are bounded. This is not automatic for every probability density function: tails proportional to (1+∣x∣)−3/2 give an unbounded second coordinate. In principle any finite-area measurable region has an open envelope of finite area, decomposable up to null boundaries into disjoint dyadic rectangles. Select a rectangle with probability proportional to its area, then sample its two coordinates uniformly and reject outside the target region. The accepted point is uniform on the region, with acceptance equal to the ratio of their areas. Constructing an effective envelope remains a density-specific computational step.

Normal rejection sampling with an exponential envelope (M(λ)=2/π​eλ2/2/λ)

Words: 72
Rejection sampling OurBigBook.com
For the half-normal distribution, the density ratio to Exp(λ) is maximized at y=λ. Completing the square gives the envelope constant above and acceptance probability e−(y−λ)2/2. Its logarithmic derivative is λ−1/λ, so the optimal rate is λ=1. Give an accepted nonnegative value an independent fair sign to obtain the standard normal distribution. The expected number of proposals is M, while the probability of rejecting one proposal is 1−1/M; these are different quantities.

Marsaglia polar method (Z=V−2logS/S​)

Words: 43
Rejection sampling OurBigBook.com
Reject pairs of independent uniform coordinates from the square until their squared radius S lies in (0,1). Multiply each coordinate by −2logS/S​ to obtain two independent variables with the standard normal distribution. Squaring either gives a chi-squared variable with one degree of freedom.

Binomial count and iid values in fixed-budget rejection sampling (N∼Bin(n,1/M))

Words: 100 Articles: 1
Rejection sampling OurBigBook.com
For n independent proposals and an envelope constant M, each rejection decision succeeds with probability 1/M. The accepted count is Bin(n,1/M). Conditional on any acceptance pattern, the accepted values have independent target density f; summing over patterns preserves that product law conditional on the count. The empty-output probability is (1−1/M)n.

Random-count central limit theorem for accepted rejection samples (n​(θrej​−θ)⇒N(0,MVarf​ϕ))

Words: 47
Binomial count and iid values in fixed-budget rejection sampling OurBigBook.com
Conditional iid accepted values have the ordinary target-sample CLT at scale N​. Since N/n→1/M, the same estimator has asymptotic variance MVarf​ϕ at scale n​. The centered count has limiting variance (1/M)(1−1/M). Any fixed convention on an empty output is asymptotically negligible.

Rejected-proposal distribution (r(z)=M−1Mg(z)−f(z)​)

Words: 50
Rejection sampling OurBigBook.com
With normalized densities f,g and envelope f≤Mg, M>1, conditioning a proposal on rejection gives density r=(Mg−f)/(M−1). It can be used as an importance sampling proposal only if it covers the relevant target support. If Mg=f on a set of positive target mass, rejected draws never visit that set.

Adaptive rejection sampling

Words: 55
Rejection sampling OurBigBook.com
Adaptive rejection sampling builds a piecewise linear upper envelope for a differentiable log-concave probability density, samples from its piecewise exponential form, and accepts with the target-to-envelope ratio. Adding evaluated points improves the envelope without changing the target distribution. Initial tangent slopes must yield integrable tails. The accepted draws are exact samples, unlike a finite run of Markov chain Monte Carlo.

Importance sampling

To estimate Ef​[g(X)], sample Y1​,…,Ym​ independently from a reference density h whose support covers that of gf, and use
m1​∑i=1m​g(Yi​)h(Yi​)f(Yi​)​.
(3)

Importance sampling of a Cauchy tail (Y=2/(1−U),W=Y2/[2π(1+Y2)])

Words: 78
Importance sampling OurBigBook.com
For U uniform on (0,1), Y has density q(y)=2y−2 on y≥2. The importance sampling weight for the standard Cauchy distribution tail is W=f(Y)/q(Y), and its expectation is the target tail probability. Writing A=arctan(1/2) gives EW=A/π and EW2=(A+2/5)/(4π2) by integrating y2/(1+y2)2. The weight varies only between 2/(5π) and 1/(2π), making its variance much smaller than that of a rare-event indicator. This is a comparison of sampling variances, not a deterministic ordering of every pair of empirical variance estimates.

Rao-Blackwell identity for a fixed-denominator rejection estimator (E[nM​∑i​Ii​ϕ(Xi​)​X]=θIS​)

For rejection indicators with conditional acceptance f(Xi​)/(Mg(Xi​)), the conditional expectation of (M/n)∑i​Ii​ϕ(Xi​) given all proposals is the unnormalized importance estimator. The Rao-Blackwell theorem reduces variance relative to that fixed-denominator estimator. It gives no such automatic comparison with the accepted-sample mean, whose denominator is the random count.

Minimum-variance importance distribution (q∗​(x)=∣h(x)∣f(x)/∫∣h∣f)

For μ=∫hf and 0<A=∫∣h∣f<∞, the proposal q∗​=∣h∣f/A minimizes the variance of the ordinary unbiased importance sampling estimator. The Cauchy-Schwarz inequality gives ∫h2f2/q≥A2, so the minimum variance for n independent samples is (A2−μ2)/n. The proposal need only cover the nonzero integrand.

Support condition for importance sampling

Words: 53
Importance sampling OurBigBook.com
An importance sampling proposal q must be positive wherever the target-weighted integrand can contribute. The usual sufficient condition is that the target probability is absolutely continuous with respect to q. Otherwise multiplying observed draws by f/q cannot recover a missing part of the integral. A strict rejection envelope supplies this condition for the rejected-proposal distribution.

For target density f and proposal density h, the importance weight is the density ratio w(x)=f(x)/h(x) wherever the target is supported.
