Let θ:Rd→(0,Θ] be a Borel measurable function bounded away from zero on every compact set. If B is standard d-dimensional Brownian motion, the displayed clock is finite on compact intervals, continuous, strictly increasing, and unbounded. Its inverse τt​=A−1(t) is a Brownian stopping time, with τt​≤2Θt. The optional sampling theorem and the change of variables dτt​=2θ(Bτt​​)dt show that Xt​=Bτt​​ solves the martingale problem for L=θΔ on test functions Cc∞​(Rd). The filtration is Fτt​B​. These are true martingales: on a finite new-time horizon the sampling times have a deterministic bound. Continuous strictly positive θ satisfies the compact-set lower bound. Pointwise positivity alone does not; pointwise positivity does not ensure weak existence gives a counterexample.
