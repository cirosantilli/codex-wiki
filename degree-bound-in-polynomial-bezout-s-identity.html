Degree bound in polynomial Bézout's identity (up+vq=1,degu,degv<n)

 ... Mathematics Area of mathematics Algebra Polynomial Coprime polynomials Bézout's identity for polynomials
OurBigBook.com Words: 101
For two polynomials over the complex numbers p,q of equal positive degree of a polynomial n with no common root of a polynomial, there are unique polynomials u,v of degree of a polynomial less than n such that up+vq=1. To prove the bound, let Vm​ be the vector space of polynomials of degree of a polynomial at most m, including the zero polynomial. The linear map (a,b)↦ap+bq from Vn−1​×Vn−1​ to V2n−1​ has zero kernel of a linear map: if ap=−bq, each root of a polynomial of p, counted with its multiplicity of a root, must occur in b because it does not occur in q, forcing b=0 by the degree of a polynomial bound, and then a=0. Both vector spaces have dimension 2n, so the rank-nullity theorem makes the linear map a bijection, and 1 has a unique preimage.
