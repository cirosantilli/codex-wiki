Van der Waals liquid-gas coexistence

 ... Branches of physics Statistical physics Thermodynamics Pressure Equation of state Van der Waals equation
OurBigBook.com Words: 315
Below Tc​, the equilibrium liquid and gas have the same temperature, pressure p∗​, and per-particle chemical potential. For macroscopic bulk thermodynamic phases, neglect interface contributions. The Maxwell equal-area construction determines vl​ and vg​ by
p(vl​,T)=p(vg​,T)=p∗​,∫vl​vg​​[p(v,T)−p∗​]dv=0.
(162)
It expresses equality of chemical potentials, since dμ=vdp at fixed temperature. Equivalently, the equilibrium Helmholtz free energy per particle follows a common tangent line between the two endpoint states, lying below the homogeneous continuation between them. The construction uses that mathematical continuation to compare endpoints; it is not a physical compression path through negative isothermal compressibility states.[ref]
Here is a reproducible calculated example at t=0.9. With q∗​=p∗​/pc​, the homogeneous intersections solve the cubic equation
3q∗​x3−(q∗​+8t)x2+9x−3=0.
(163)
The Maxwell construction gives
q∗​≃0.646998352,(xl​,xm​,xg​)≃(0.603401903, 1.090526633, 2.348842376).
(164)
All three roots of a polynomial satisfy x>1/3. Only the smallest and largest are the phase coexistence endpoints; the middle one has negative isothermal compressibility. Equal pressures at three admissible values of volume therefore do not imply three stable thermodynamic phases. An analytic area check is
38t​ln3xl​−13xg​−1​+3(xg​1​−xl​1​)−q∗​(xg​−xl​)=0.
(165)
At full numerical precision, the residual is below 10−11 in reduced units; independent numerical integration gives the same cancellation. At t=0.9, the spinodal points are instead x≃0.718597189 and 1.528504964.
The equilibrium plateau changes the phase fraction rather than the endpoint densities. For a gas particle fraction fg​=Ng​/N and mean particle volume vˉ=V/N, the lever rule reads
vˉ=(1−fg​)vl​+fg​vg​,fg​=vg​−vl​vˉ−vl​​.
(166)
Thus vˉ=vc​ at t=0.9 gives fg​≃0.227219: this is a particle fraction, not the volume fraction occupied by gas. Differentiating equality of chemical potentials along this model's phase coexistence curve yields the same Clapeyron equation explained below, dp∗​/dT=ℓ/[T(vg​−vl​)], with latent heat ℓ per particle. Both endpoint differences vanish as the Van der Waals critical point is approached; the equation's critical limit must be taken as a ratio.
