Repeated-measurement latent signal model (X=T+ε1​,Y=T+ε2​)

 Home Mathematics Area of mathematics Probability and statistics Regression toward the mean
OurBigBook.com Words: 640 Articles: 2
Imagine an instrument measuring the same fixed quantity twice on each unit. Across units the persistent signal T is a latent variable; the two measurement errors ε1​,ε2​ are fresh, mutually independent random variables, also independent of T. Assume they have mean zero and common finite variance v, while T has mean μ and variance s. Then
X=T+ε1​,Y=T+ε2​,E[X]=E[Y]=μ,Var(X)=Var(Y)=s+v,Cov(X,Y)=s.
(18)
Thus the Pearson correlation coefficient is ρ=s/(s+v) when s+v>0. Both measurements retain the same marginal mean and variance. Repetition does not make the entire population collapse toward μ; it changes what we expect after selecting on X.
For s,v>0, further assuming normal distributions for T and both measurement errors gives the conditional expectation
E[Y∣X=x]=μ+ρ(x−μ).
(19)
Since 0<ρ<1, this predicted reading is closer to μ than x is. Independence and zero mean errors alone do not justify this linear conditional expectation: without the normal distribution assumption the same expression is the linear least-squares projection, and the actual conditional expectation can be nonlinear. Correlated errors, a changing signal, or different error variances require their own model; a noisy reading by itself supplies no universal shrinkage factor.
