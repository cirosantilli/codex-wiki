Astrophysics

 Home Physics Branches of physics
OurBigBook.com Words: 112k Articles: 2k
Astrophysics uses physics to explain stars, planets, galaxies, and their electromagnetic radiation; cosmology extends the questions to the universe as a whole. Much of its evidence arrives as light. The challenge is to infer the source's physical properties from what reaches an observer: a bright star might be nearby, unusually luminous, or both. Stellar parallax and observed radiative flux make this distinction quantitative.
This is an inverse problem. A forward problem predicts observations from a source model and the intervening radiative transfer; the inverse problem works backwards from observations to possible models. A spectral line can constrain chemical elements, temperature, and motion through the Doppler effect, but its strength also depends on the stellar atmosphere. Combining different measurements is therefore more informative than interpreting brightness alone. The attraction is that a tiny change in the direction of light can supply a distance, turning received radiative flux into intrinsic luminosity. [ref].
