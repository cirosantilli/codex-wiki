Förster resonance energy transfer

 Home Biology Biophysical measurement
OurBigBook.com Words: 2k Articles: 13
Förster resonance energy transfer is a nonradiative transfer of energy from an excited state of a FRET donor to a nearby FRET acceptor, through coupling of their electric dipoles. The transfer step does not emit a photon that the FRET acceptor subsequently absorbs. It competes with the FRET donor's own fluorescence and other routes out of its excited state. A fluorescent FRET acceptor can then emit fluorescence; an absorbing but nonfluorescent FRET acceptor can still receive the transferred energy.[ref]
The striking use of FRET is to turn a change in molecular proximity into an optical readout. Proteins carrying a FRET donor and a FRET acceptor may change the readout when they associate, as in the chemotaxis FRET assay; two labels on one protein may report a change in its shape. Interpretation depends on the FRET spectral overlap, FRET dipole orientation factor and labeling geometry, as well as separation. A change in FRET is therefore evidence about proximity under specified conditions, rather than a direct measurement of an arbitrary distance.[ref]
