Preconditioned steepest descent (Mzk​=rk​,αk​=rkT​zk​/(zkT​Azk​))

 ... Mathematics Area of mathematics Analysis Numerical analysis Numerical linear algebra Matrix preconditioning
OurBigBook.com Words: 447 Articles: 1
For fixed symmetric positive-definite matrices A and M, preconditioned steepest descent minimizes F(x)=xTAx/2−bTx along the direction supplied by the auxiliary solve. Given xk​, form the linear-system residual rk​=b−Axk​. If rk​=0, stop: xk​=x∗​. Otherwise solve Mzk​=rk​ and use
αk​=zkT​Azk​rkT​zk​​,xk+1​=xk​+αk​zk​,rk+1​=rk​−αk​Azk​.
(16)
This is exact line search for a positive-definite quadratic along zk​. Indeed,
F(xk​+αzk​)=F(xk​)−αrkT​zk​+21​α2zkT​Azk​,
(17)
whose derivative vanishes at the displayed positive step size. Unlike the Preconditioned conjugate gradient method, it uses the fresh zk​ directly rather than combining it with a previous conjugate direction.
For diagonal scaling for a two-dimensional linear system, start at x0​=0. Without matrix preconditioning, take M=I: z0​=(1,1)T, α0​=10/507, and
x1​=5071​(1010​),r1​=169165​(1−1​).
(18)
With the Jacobi preconditioner M=diag(1,100), z0​=(1,1/100)T, α0​=505/507, and
x1​=(505/507101/10140​),r1​=(33/16900−33/169​).
(19)
The original linear-system residual becomes much smaller after this correction. Quantify progress in the same energy norm in both cases: Ek​=F(xk​)−F(x∗​) and E0​=2515/4998. Exact substitution gives
E0​E1​​=qI​=8500781675​≈0.960803228,E0​E1(M)​​=qM​=85007003267​≈0.000384321291.
(20)
In this particular example the entire histories are Ek​/E0​=qIk​ and Ek(M)​/E0​=qMk​. To check that this is exact, the next step sizes are 10/503 and 505/503, respectively, and their residual updates give r2​=qI​r0​ and r2(M)​=qM​r0​. Multiplying a linear-system residual by a nonzero scalar leaves the next step size unchanged; every pair of steps repeats the same directions at a smaller scale. The two first-step energy ratios then establish these formulas for all k.
The curves come from exact rational updates of the specified linear system, converted to decimal values for plotting. The left panel counts iterations. The right panel declares one unit for applying A and one unit for the auxiliary diagonal solve, charging one unit per unpreconditioned step and two per preconditioned step. It excludes setup and vector operations, so it compares a declared cost model rather than measured runtime. Scaling is advantageous even with that extra charge.
These curves compare steepest descent method iterations. On this two-dimensional linear system, both the conjugate gradient method and the Preconditioned conjugate gradient method terminate within two steps in exact arithmetic. The plot therefore establishes no CG iteration-count advantage for this example; the benefit of scaling for larger problems depends on their eigenvalues and auxiliary-solve costs.
