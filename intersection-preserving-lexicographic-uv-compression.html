Intersection-preserving lexicographic UV-compression

 ... Combinatorics Extremal set theory Set family shadow Lower shadow Kruskal-Katona theorem UV-compression
OurBigBook.com Words: 159
Let U,V be disjoint, equally sized, nonempty sets, with minU<minV. Suppose an intersecting family is unchanged by all such UV-compressions of smaller size. Then its (U,V)-UV-compression is still an intersecting family. Indeed, a newly moved member A′=(A∖V)∪U can only be disjoint from a retained old member B, since two newly moved members share U. Here B∩U=∅ and V′=B∩V=∅. If V′=V, retaining B forces (B∖V)∪U to have been present, contradicting the old intersection property with A. Otherwise choose U′⊂U with ∣U′∣=∣V′∣ and minU∈U′. Stability under the smaller (U′,V′)-UV-compression forces (A∖V′)∪U′ to be present; this member is disjoint from B, again a contradiction. Repeatedly applying a changing UV-compression of smallest size terminates, since the sum of lexicographic order positions decreases. A terminal set family is a lexicographic initial segment: a missing earlier member X and present later member Y would be moved by U=X∖Y, V=Y∖X.
