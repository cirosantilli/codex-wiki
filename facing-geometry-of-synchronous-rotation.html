Facing geometry of synchronous rotation (θ−f=constant)

 ... Branches of physics Astrophysics Planetary science Planetary system dynamics Tidal locking Synchronous rotation
OurBigBook.com Words: 146
View a circular orbit along its perpendicular spin axis. Let f be the orbital angle measured from a fixed direction, choosing an arbitrary zero for the circular limit of true anomaly, and let θ be the direction of an outward arrow painted on the body, measured from the same fixed direction. The direction from the body to its companion is f+π. Thus an inward-facing arrow satisfies
θ=f+π(mod2π),θ˙=f˙​=n,n=T2π​.
(8)
More generally synchronous rotation keeps θ−f constant; its particular value selects which surface direction faces the companion. Over one orbital period T, both f and the unwrapped θ increase by 2π. This is the short geometric explanation for one turn per orbit. For a nonspinning body, θ˙=0 instead, and the companion direction sweeps through all surface longitudes. On an eccentric orbit, mean 1:1 spin does not force θ−f to stay exactly constant: true anomaly advances nonuniformly, producing the longitudinal component of libration.
