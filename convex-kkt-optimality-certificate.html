Convex KKT optimality certificate (f(x∗​)≤f(z)(z feasible))

 Home Mathematics Area of mathematics Mathematical optimization Karush-Kuhn-Tucker conditions
OurBigBook.com Words: 196
For differentiable functions f,gi​ that are convex functions on an open convex set and affine functions hj​, the KKT conditions certify a global minimum. Once Lagrange multipliers satisfy them, no extra Slater condition is needed.[ref]
The certificate is a short chain of inequalities. Nonnegative Lagrange multipliers and affine equalities make L(⋅,λ,μ) a convex function. Its zero gradient gives a global minimum at x∗​ by the first-order optimality condition. For every feasible point z,
f(x∗​)=L(x∗​,λ,μ)≤L(z,λ,μ)≤f(z).
(72)
The first equality uses feasibility and complementary slackness; the last inequality uses gi​(z)≤0 and λi​≥0. Equivalently, the Lagrange dual function attains the same value as the primal objective function, proving strong duality. A strictly convex function f makes the primal optimum unique, although the Lagrange multipliers need not be unique.
Affine equality optimization constraints matter: an arbitrary signed multiple of a nonlinear equality can destroy convexity of the optimization Lagrangian. For example, minimizing (x−2)2 subject to the affine equality x=b gives x∗​=b and μ=2(2−b) for L=(x−2)2+μ(x−b). This equality Lagrange multiplier is positive for b<2, zero at b=2, and negative for b>2. Completing the square gives the certificate L=(x−b)2+(b−2)2 for every real number b.
Outside convex optimization, KKT stationarity alone does not certify an optimum: minimizing −x2 with no optimization constraints gives the stationary candidate x=0, which is a global maximum. The Lagrangian sufficiency theorem remains applicable whenever a global minimum of the optimization Lagrangian can be proved directly.
