Equal-or-disjoint translates characterize cosets (gB∩hB=∅⟹gB=hB)

 Home Mathematics Area of mathematics Algebra Group theory Coset
OurBigBook.com Words: 144
A nonempty subset B of any group G is a left coset of a subgroup if and only if any two left translates gB,hB are equal or disjoint. No finiteness assumption is needed.
For the forward direction, write B=bH with H a subgroup. Then gB=gbH, and distinct left cosets of H are disjoint. For the converse, first assume e∈B. For every b∈B, b∈B∩bB, so bB=B. This proves closure under multiplication. Since e∈B=bB, there is c∈B with bc=e, showing b−1∈B. Thus B passes the subgroup test.
For a general nonempty B, choose b∈B and set H=b−1B. It contains e, and all its left translates have the assumed property because gH=(gb−1)B. The previous argument makes H a subgroup, and B=bH is a left coset. This gives a recognition criterion for cosets from their translate intersections alone.
