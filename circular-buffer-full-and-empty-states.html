Circular buffer full and empty states

 Home Computer science Data structure Circular buffer
OurBigBook.com Words: 214
With front-plus-count storage, empty means n=0 and full means n=C; every allocated slot is usable. With only the two wrapped array indices h,w, the equality h=w describes both possibilities. Indeed w−h≡n(modC), and the two allowed counts 0,C have the same remainder. Recovering the count as (w−h)modC therefore loses the full state. A count, a separate full flag, or a different index representation must resolve that ambiguity.
Another convention reserves one slot: for a physical array of length C≥2, empty means w=h and full means (w+1)modC=h. Its usable capacity is C−1, and (w−h)modC now recovers its count because 0≤n<C. A five-slot array then stores at most four items.
This is the convention of the kernel's documented CIRC_* helpers, which additionally require power-of-two array lengths. It is not a universal property of circular buffers; our capacity-five count-based example uses all five slots and does not use those helpers.[ref]
The smallest count-based case, C=1, makes the issue vivid: both h and w are always zero, but n still distinguishes one live item from none. Capacity zero is excluded from our formulas because remainder modulo zero is undefined.
