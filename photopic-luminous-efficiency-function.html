Photopic luminous efficiency function (V(λ))

 Home Physics Branches of physics Optics Photometry (optics)
OurBigBook.com Words: 139
The photopic luminous efficiency function V(λ) is dimensionless and describes standardized relative sensitivity for photopic vision. It is normalized to V(555nm)=1 and tabulated over 360–830nm. The wavelength coordinate is in standard air, rather than vacuum; changing coordinates requires converting the spectral radiant flux density as well. The official prescription uses linear interpolation between the specified 1 nm entries.[ref]
Write λa​ for the standard-air wavelength at 540THz. With spectral radiant flux Φe,λ​ in Wnm−1 and dλ in nanometres, the luminous flux is
Φv​=Km​∫360nm830nm​V(λ)Φe,λ​(λ)dλ,Km​=V(λa​)Kcd​​,Kcd​=683lmW−1.

The integral has units of watts, and Km​ converts them to lumens. For radiation at a single wavelength λ0​ with total radiant flux P, this reduces to Φv​=Km​V(λ0​)P. For a sufficiently narrow finite band, this is an approximation requiring nearly constant V across the band.[ref]
