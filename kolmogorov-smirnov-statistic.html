Kolmogorov-Smirnov statistic (Dn​=supx​∣Fn​(x)−F(x)∣)

For an empirical distribution function Fn​ and a specified cumulative distribution function F0​, the one-sample Kolmogorov-Smirnov statistic is their Kolmogorov distance:
Fn​(x)=n1​∑j=1n​1{Xj​≤x}​,Dn​=supx∈R​∣Fn​(x)−F0​(x)∣.
(8)
The displayed sum uses an indicator function. With this convention, Fn​ is right-continuous. For distinct observations, its value and its left one-sided limit at the ith order statistic are Fn​(X(i)​)=i/n and Fn​(X(i)−​)=(i−1)/n.
For continuous F0​, both jump sides matter. Define the two directional discrepancies by
Dn+​=supx​(Fn​(x)−F0​(x))=max1≤i≤n​{ni​−F0​(X(i)​)},
(9)
Dn−​=supx​(F0​(x)−Fn​(x))=max1≤i≤n​{F0​(X(i)​)−ni−1​},Dn​=max(Dn+​,Dn−​).
(10)
Thus Dn+​ uses the empirical distribution function immediately after a jump, and Dn−​ uses its left one-sided limit.[ref] A supremum need not be attained.
For n=4, F0​(x)=x on [0,1], and the stipulated order statistics (1/10,2/5,4/5,9/10), the complete calculation is
i1234​X(i)​1/102/54/59/10​F4​(X(i)−​)01/41/23/4​F4​(X(i)​)1/41/23/41​i/4−F0​(X(i)​)3/201/10−1/201/10​F0​(X(i)​)−(i−1)/41/103/203/103/20​​
(11)
The last two columns give the signed directional gaps: D4+​=3/20, D4−​=3/10, and D4​=3/10. In contrast, checking only ∣F4​(X(i)​)−F0​(X(i)​)∣ gives 3/20 and misses the largest gap approached as x↑4/5. At x=4/5 itself, the gap has already fallen to 1/20.
The finite formula follows from the behavior between jumps: the empirical distribution function Fn​ is constant and the cumulative distribution function F0​ is monotone, so the largest directional gaps occur at the two interval ends, possibly as one-sided limits. The two tails introduce no larger gap. Checking every jump side therefore gives the exact supremum.
A common normalized Kolmogorov-Smirnov statistic is n​Dn​, which equals 3/5 in this example. Its large-sample reference law comes from the Kolmogorov-Smirnov theorem. The unscaled Dn​ remains the largest difference of cumulative probabilities, not a p-value.
There is a useful exact exception to the usual warning about fitting statistical parameters. Suppose n≥2 independent and identically distributed observations have the uniform distribution on (0,θ) with unknown θ>0. Let M=maxi​Xi​ and let J be the label of its almost surely unique maximizing observation. Conditional on M=m and J=j, the surviving labelled observations (Xi​)i=j​ are independent with the uniform distribution on (0,m); after division by m they are independent and identically distributed uniforms on (0,1). These are conditional distributions defined by probability density functions, since the event M=m has probability zero.
To check this, the joint probability density function of M and J=j is mn−1/θn for 0<m<θ. Dividing the original joint probability density function θ−n by this factor gives the conditional survivor probability density function
m−(n−1)∏i=j​1{0<xi​<m}​.
(12)
This factorization proves the claimed conditional independence; scaling removes m. Keep the surviving labels in a fixed order, or apply an independent random permutation. The sorted survivors instead have the joint law of uniform order statistics and exhibit statistical dependence; sorting does not affect their empirical distribution function. Delete the maximum itself, which would otherwise become a forced value 1, and compute the Kolmogorov-Smirnov statistic using sample size n−1 and jump heights i/(n−1). This gives an exact conditional Kolmogorov-Smirnov test under the uniform-endpoint statistical model. It does not justify fitting an arbitrary endpoint or other statistical parameters and using the ordinary n-sample calibration.
