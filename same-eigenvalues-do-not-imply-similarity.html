Same eigenvalues do not imply similarity

 Home Mathematics Area of mathematics Algebra Linear algebra Matrix similarity
OurBigBook.com Words: 93
Consider the diagonal matrix D=2I2​ and a Jordan block
J=(20​12​).
(85)
Both have characteristic polynomial (t−2)2, trace 4, determinant 4, and eigenvalue 2 with algebraic multiplicity two. But ker(D−2I) is the whole plane, whereas (J−2I)(a,b)T=(b,0)T vanishes only when b=0. Their eigenspaces have different dimensions, so eigenvectors under similarity rules out matrix similarity. There is an even shorter proof: S−1(2I)S=2I for every invertible matrix S.
The missing information is how the linear operator acts beyond its eigenvectors. Here D−2I=0, while J−2I=0 but (J−2I)2=0. Their minimal polynomials are respectively t−2 and (t−2)2. Over a field where the characteristic polynomial splits, the full sizes of the Jordan blocks, encoded by the Jordan normal form, determine the matrix similarity class.
