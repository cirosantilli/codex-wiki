Random evaluation test for a polynomial identity (P(P(a1​,…,an​)=0)≤d/∣S∣)

 Home Computer science Algorithm Randomized algorithm Polynomial identity testing
OurBigBook.com Words: 261
Let P be a nonzero multivariate polynomial over a field K, with total degree at most d≥0. Choose a nonempty finite set S⊆K. For independent random variables a1​,…,an​, each with a uniform distribution on a finite set on S, the Schwartz-Zippel lemma gives
P(P(a1​,…,an​)=0)≤min{1,d/∣S∣}.
(64)
Nonzeroness is essential; the zero polynomial always evaluates to zero. The total degree sums exponents within each monomial, rather than taking their maximum: x2y3+y has total degree five. The field need not be finite, but S must be finite. The estimate reduces error only when d/∣S∣<1.[ref]
The proof begins with the root bound for a polynomial. For several variables, write P=∑j=0m​Qj​(x1​,…,xn−1​)xnj​, with Qm​=0. The total degree of Qm​ is at most d−m. By induction, Qm​ vanishes with probability at most (d−m)/∣S∣. Otherwise the remaining univariate polynomial has at most m polynomial roots. Adding the two contributions gives d/∣S∣.[ref]
The algorithm samples a point, performs exact polynomial evaluation, rejects the identity if the value is nonzero, and otherwise accepts. The lemma bounds false acceptance for every fixed nonzero input. For the polynomial identity testing over a finite field example, G has d=2 and ∣S∣=5, so its exact 2/5 error attains the bound. Numerical near-zero values from floating-point arithmetic do not satisfy this exact algebraic contract. A polynomial-time algorithm claim additionally needs efficient sampling and evaluation, including the bit cost of representing and operating on field elements. Counting field operations alone does not establish that cost.
