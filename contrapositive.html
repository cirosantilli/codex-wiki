Contrapositive (¬B→¬A)

 ... Foundations of mathematics Mathematical logic Propositional logic Proposition Logical connective Logical implication
OurBigBook.com Words: 99
The contrapositive of a logical implication A→B reverses the direction and applies logical negation to both sides: ¬B→¬A. In classical propositional logic they are equivalent, so either can be proved in place of the other. The contrapositive of “divisibility by four implies being an even number” says that a number that is not an even number cannot be divisible by four.
In Intuitionistic propositional logic, A→B still implies its contrapositive: assumptions A and ¬B would yield B and hence a contradiction. The reverse direction is not generally valid. From ¬B→¬A and A, one obtains only ¬¬B; obtaining B additionally requires double-negation elimination.
