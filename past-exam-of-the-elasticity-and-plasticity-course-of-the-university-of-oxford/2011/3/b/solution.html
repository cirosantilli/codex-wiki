Put P=−F>0. With G=0, the Euler elastica equation is EIθ′′+Psinθ=0. Linearization about the straight configuration gives EIθ′′+Pθ=0. Under the boundary conditions explicitly stated, θ(0)=θ(L)=0, a nonzero solution requires
Pn​=EI(Lnπ​)2,θn​(s)=CsinLnπs​,n=1,2,….​
(31)
Thus with only the stated boundary conditions, the first critical compressive force is Fc​=−π2EI/L2​. The corresponding linear transverse displacement field, with y(0)=0, is
yn​(s)=nπCL​(1−cosLnπs​),
(32)
and x(s)=s to first order. These are the successive small-amplitude buckling modes. The nonlinear branches retain the simple pendulum equation; if the angular amplitude is θm​ and m=sin(θm​/2), fitting n half-periods into the rod gives P=4EIn2K(m)2/L2, which tends to Pn​ as m↓0. Here K is the complete elliptic integral of the first kind, with m its modulus, not its squared parameter.
Endpoint position compatibility for the Euler elastica adds a condition when fully fixed clamps have equal transverse height. If the word “clamped” also imposes y(L)=y(0), then ∫0L​θn​ds=0. With G=0, this retains only even n=2j, yielding
Pj​=4j2π2EI/L2,Pfirst​=4π2EI/L2.​
(33)
The same exclusion is exact on the nonlinear branches: integrating the elastica equation gives P∫sinθds=−EI[θ′(L)−θ′(0)], and an odd number of half-periods reverses the nonzero endpoint slope. Hence odd n changes the endpoint height. The conventional fixed-clamp first Euler buckling load is the second boxed value, whereas the source's explicitly listed boundary conditions alone give the first. Additional clamped modes with a nonzero transverse reaction require releasing the imposed G=0. Load-controlled buckling also permits axial shortening; an exactly inextensible rod whose endpoint separation is fixed equal to its full length has no room to buckle.
