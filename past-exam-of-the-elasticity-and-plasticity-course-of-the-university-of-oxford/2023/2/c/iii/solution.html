Let the contact interval be [−s,s], where w=−H. Its slope and curvature vanish. Continuity of the displacement field, slope and bending moment at x=s gives
w(s)=−H,w′(s)=0,w′′(s)=0.
(36)
On the free interval s<x<L, the cubic polynomial satisfying these three conditions is w=−H+C(x−s)3. Put d=L−s. The end boundary conditions become
Cd3=H,3Cd2=α.
(37)
Consequently
d=α3H​,s=L−α3H​,C=d3H​.​
(38)
This requires α≥3H/L, with s=0 at the transition. The full displacement field is
w(x)=⎩⎨⎧​−H,−H+H(L−s∣x∣−s​)3,​∣x∣≤s,s≤∣x∣≤L.​​
(39)
It satisfies nonpenetration. In the open contact interval the distributed normal contact force is R=Bw′′′′=0, since the obstacle is flat and p=T=0. The normal contact force is concentrated at the two endpoints. At x=s, w′′′ jumps from zero to 6H/d3; at x=−s, it jumps from −6H/d3 to zero. Both upward point forces are therefore
R−s​=Rs​=d36BH​>0.​
(40)
At s=0 they merge into the single midpoint point force of the preceding regime. These formulae belong to the small-slope model; interpreting arbitrarily large α as a finite physical rotation would exceed its assumptions.
