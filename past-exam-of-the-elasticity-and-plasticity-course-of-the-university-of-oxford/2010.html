The displacement follows a material point from its reference position to its current position:
u(X,t)=x(X,t)−X.​
(1)
The deformation gradient is the Jacobian matrix F=∂x/∂X=I+∇X​u, and the volume Jacobian is
J=detF,dV=JdV0​.​
(2)
For an orientation-preserving physical deformation, J>0 measures the local volume expansion factor. Incompressibility requires J=1; conservation of mass gives ρ(x,t)J=ρ0​(X). For small displacement gradients, J=1+∇X​⋅u+O(∣∇X​u∣2). If a spatial representation is desired, the same displacement is u(x,t)=x−X(x,t).

A Cartesian Cartesian second-rank tensor has components transforming under a constant orthogonal change of axes as
Tij′​=pia​pjb​Tab​,T′=PTPT.​
(3)
Here X′=PX and u′=Pu. By the chain rule and orthogonality,
∂Xj′​∂ui′​​=pia​∂Xb​∂ua​​∂Xj′​∂Xb​​=pia​pjb​∂Xb​∂ua​​.
(4)
Thus the displacement gradient is a Cartesian second-rank tensor. Its symmetric part transforms in the same way:
eij′​=21​pia​pjb​(∂Xb​∂ua​​+∂Xa​∂ub​​)=pia​pjb​eab​.
(5)
Hence the infinitesimal strain tensor is also second-rank. This is a passive coordinate rotation, under which both reference and spatial coordinates rotate; it does not assert finite-deformation frame indifference of the linearized strain formula.

Apply the wave ansatz to the oscillatory displacement about a static body-force balance, or equivalently to the zero-body-force homogeneous equation. The parallel and perpendicular polarizations have speeds
cp​=ρλ+2μ​​,cs​=ρμ​​.​
(13)
The first is longitudinal and the second transverse. A wave family is dispersive when its phase speed ω/∣k∣ varies with wavenumber. In each of these homogeneous bulk families, ω=c∣k∣, so both are nondispersive and group and phase speeds coincide.
The positivity assumptions imply λ>−2μ/3, hence λ+μ>μ/3>0. Therefore cp2​−cs2​=(λ+μ)/ρ>0: longitudinal waves travel faster.

fi′′​+(csi2​ω2​−k2)fi​=0.​
(14)
The oscillatory seam solution requires m2=ω2/cs12​−k2, giving
ω2=cs12​(k2+m2),cL​=∣k∣ω​=cs1​1+m2/k2​.​
(15)
For an exponentially decaying exterior, f2′′​=ℓ2f2​ with ℓ>0, so the same differential equation instead requires
ℓ2=k2−ω2/cs22​,ω2=cs22​(k2−ℓ2).​
(16)
The printed exterior plus sign is inconsistent with its decaying ansatz and must be a sign error. The guided mode has cs1​<cL​<cs2​, so a slower shear-wave seam than its cladding is necessary.
For an even seam mode f1​=Acos(my), upper-interface displacement and traction continuity give
Acos(mh)=A2​e−ℓh,−μ1​Amsin(mh)=−μ2​ℓA2​e−ℓh.
(17)
The lower interface gives the same equations when B2​=A2​. Eliminating the amplitudes yields
μ1​mtan(mh)=μ2​ℓ.​
(18)
For an odd seam mode f1​=Bsin(my), the equations are
Bsin(mh)=A2​e−ℓh,μ1​Bmcos(mh)=−μ2​ℓA2​e−ℓh.
(19)
With B2​=−A2​ the lower condition agrees, and elimination gives
μ1​mcot(mh)=−μ2​ℓ.​
(20)
These interface conditions together with the two corrected dispersion identities determine the guided-wave branches.

In the formal stiff-cladding limit with a nonvanishing exterior decay rate, the interface displacement tends to zero. The even seam mode then has cos(mh)=0, whereas the odd mode has sin(mh)=0. The positive wavenumbers are
meven,n​=h(n+1/2)π​, n=0,1,…,modd,n​=hnπ​, n=1,2,….​
(21)
The zero odd index would give an identically zero mode and is excluded. The smallest even transverse wavenumber is π/(2h), so its lowest branch satisfies
ω2≥cs12​(k2+4h2π2​).​
(22)
This is the symmetric-mode cutoff: the actual limiting branches have the discrete listed values of m. If the exterior shear speed remains finite, the separate decay requirement ω2<cs22​k2 still applies; the cutoff alone does not replace that guided-wave condition.

Use the same first variation on the half-plane, with no restrictions on the edge traces of η. Write M=wxx​+νwyy​. Two x integrations of the Mηxx​ term produce the boundary term −Mηx​+Mx​η at x=0. The mixed term, after one integration in x and one along the edge in y, contributes 2(1−ν)wxyy​η. All other boundary terms vanish at infinity. Hence
δU=D∬x>0​η∇4wdxdy−D∫−∞∞​{ηx​(wxx​+νwyy​)−η[wxxx​+(2−ν)wxyy​]}x=0​dy.
(30)
The bulk equation makes its area term zero. Stationarity therefore gives exactly
∫−∞∞​{ηx​(wxx​+νwyy​)−η[wxxx​+(2−ν)wxyy​]}x=0​dy=0.​
(31)
The two edge traces can be prescribed independently: use η(x,y)=χ(x)[f(y)+xg(y)] with χ=1 near zero and compactly supported smooth f,g. The fundamental lemma on the edge then gives
wxx​+νwyy​=0,wxxx​+(2−ν)wxyy​=0(x=0).​
(32)
They are zero bending moment and zero effective transverse shear at a free straight edge. The second includes the edge derivative of the twisting moment; omitting the mixed contribution would give the wrong coefficient.
