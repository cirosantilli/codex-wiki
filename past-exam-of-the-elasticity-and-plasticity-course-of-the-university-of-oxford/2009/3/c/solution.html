Insert the two constitutive stresses into the radial component of the Cauchy momentum equation. After collecting terms, (λ+2μ)(ur′′​+ur′​/r−ur​/r2)=0, so ur​=Ar+B/r. Its stresses are
τrr​=2(λ+μ)A−2μB/r2,τθθ​=2(λ+μ)A+2μB/r2.
(21)
The far pressure gives A=−p∞​/[2(λ+μ)]. At the tunnel wall, compressive bracing imposes τrr​(a)=−P, so B=(P−p∞​)a2/(2μ). Hence
τrr​=−p∞​−r2a2​(P−p∞​),τθθ​=−p∞​+r2a2​(P−p∞​),​
(22)
ur​=−2(λ+μ)p∞​r​+2μr(P−p∞​)a2​.​
(23)
With no shear and τθθ​=kτrr​, divide the yield identity by the nonzero τrr2​ to obtain 4k=(1+k)2cos2ϕ. Solving its quadratic gives
k+​=1−sinϕ1+sinϕ​,k−​=1+sinϕ1−sinϕ​.​
(24)
To locate first yield, the stress-circle center is the constant −p∞​, while its radius is ∣P−p∞​∣a2/r2. The radius is maximal at r=a, so that is where it first meets the friction envelope as the bracing departs from the hydrostatic value. At the wall τrr​=−P and τθθ​=P−2p∞​; hence k=(2p∞​−P)/P and
P=1+k2p∞​​.​
(25)
For lowering bracing from P=p∞​, the hoop stress becomes more compressive, so k=k+​>1 and Pcrit​=p∞​(1−sinϕ)​. Increasing bracing instead selects k−​ and reaches the other threshold p∞​(1+sinϕ). Stating the loading direction removes any ambiguity between the two reciprocal stress ratios. These conclusions describe first yield of the elastic field, not the later plastic-zone solution.
