For a surface with unit normal n, traction is ti​=τij​nj​. Thus τij​ is the ith force-per-area component on a plane whose positive normal is ej​. Diagonal entries are normal stresses and off-diagonal entries are shear stresses, with tensile normal stress positive in this convention.
The symmetric strain has six independent components and so does the symmetric stress. A general linear map between these two six-dimensional spaces therefore has
6×6=36 independent constitutive coefficients.​
(2)
Equivalently choose the minor symmetries Cijkl​=Cjikl​=Cijlk​. Components antisymmetric in the strain indices have no observable effect and are omitted from this representative.
A further distinction is important: if a strain energy density exists, Cijkl​=∂2W/(∂eij​∂ekl​) has the major symmetry Cijkl​=Cklij​. Then the six-by-six map is symmetric and has 6⋅7/2=21 independent coefficients. Thus 36 follows from the two stated tensor symmetries alone; 21 additionally uses the energy/reciprocity assumption, as in the variational setting later in the paper. These are the minor and major symmetries of linear elasticity.
