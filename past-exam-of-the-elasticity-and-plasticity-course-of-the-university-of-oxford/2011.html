a OurBigBook.com
The deformation gradient is the derivative of the deformation map with respect to the reference configuration coordinates, at fixed time:
FiA​(X,t)=∂XA​∂xi​​(X,t),dx=FdX.​
(1)
Its determinant J=detF is the local ratio of current to reference volume: dv=JdV for an orientation-preserving deformation in continuum mechanics. Thus elastic incompressibility is the pointwise constraint J=1​, rather than a requirement that each individual principal stretch be one.

Solution

Words: 79
b OurBigBook.com
Choose the outward unit normal n to the boundary of the chosen current material subbody. The traction t(x,n) is the contact force exerted on that subbody per unit current area at the boundary point. Reversing the subbody side reverses n and t.
By Cauchy's stress theorem, the dependence on n is linear. With the usual column-vector convention the Cauchy stress tensor satisfies
t(x,n)=τ(x)n,ti​=τij​nj​.​
(2)
The same point has different traction vectors on differently oriented planes, but a single Cauchy stress tensor encodes all of them. In the absence of body couples, conservation of angular momentum makes τ symmetric.

c OurBigBook.com
In the current configuration, balance of linear momentum is the Cauchy momentum equation
divx​τ+ρb=ρ(∂t​v+(v⋅∇x​)v).​
(3)
Here τ is the Cauchy stress tensor, ρ is current mass density, b is body force per unit mass, and v(x,t) is the velocity of the material particle currently at x. The expression in parentheses is the material acceleration. The divergence is taken in current spatial coordinates, with (divτ)i​=∂j​τij​.
Equivalently the right side is ρ∂t2​x(X,t) evaluated at the particle, because this is the same material acceleration in Lagrangian coordinates. For the subsequent static inflation with negligible body force, the equation reduces to divτ=0.

d OurBigBook.com
Write B=A+H for the reference outer radius and b=a+h for the current outer radius. The inner material normal is −er​. A fluid pressure P applies traction −Pn=Per​ there, so the radial Cauchy stress tensor component must be negative:
tr​(a)=−P,tr​(b)=0.​
(4)
The second condition uses zero external gauge pressure. Both faces also have zero shear traction; the axisymmetric diagonal stress field satisfies those conditions automatically. The axial constraint is plane strain, so supports may supply an axial reaction; it does not imply zero axial stress.

For the axisymmetric deformation map, take θ=Θ, z=Z, and r=r(R). A reference cylindrical shell of unit axial length has volume 2πRdR, and the corresponding current shell has volume 2πrdr. Elastic incompressibility therefore gives rdr=RdR.
Integrating from the inner surface, where r(A)=a, gives r2−a2=R2−A2. The positive radius is consequently
r(R)=R2+a2−A2​,A≤R≤B.​
(5)
The outer surface is not an additional independent unknown: b=B2+a2−A2​​, and hence h=b−a.

f OurBigBook.com
Use orthonormal cylindrical bases in the reference configuration and current configuration. The deformation gradient maps a radial increment dR to r′dR, an azimuthal arc RdΘ to rdΘ, and an axial increment dZ to itself. Therefore
F=r′er​⊗ER​+Rr​eθ​⊗EΘ​+ez​⊗EZ​.​
(6)
Differentiating the radius relation gives r′=R/r. Its three principal stretches are
λr​=rR​,λθ​=Rr​,λz​=1.​
(7)
Their product is one, as required by elastic incompressibility. The symbols called “strains” in the source are these stretch ratios; engineering strain would instead subtract one. The axial stretch is one because this is plane strain.

The multiplier enforcing elastic incompressibility contributes an arbitrary hydrostatic stress. Denote it by q(r) to distinguish it from the applied pressure P. For the neo-Hookean solid, the Cauchy stress tensor is τ=−qI+μFFT. Thus its principal components are
tr​=−q+μλr2​=−q+μr2R2​,tθ​=−q+μλθ2​=−q+μR2r2​.​
(8)
The axial component is tz​=−q+μ, despite the unit axial stretch. The quantity q is a Lagrange multiplier determined together with equilibrium and the traction conditions; it is not obtained by differentiating the reduced stretch energy while ignoring its constraint.
In particular the stress difference eliminates this multiplier:
tθ​−tr​=μ(R2r2​−r2R2​).​
(9)

The radial equilibrium equation gives dtr​/dr=(tθ​−tr​)/r. Set C=a2−A2, so that R2=r2−C. Substitution of the neo-Hookean solid stress difference eliminates both q and tθ​:
drdtr​​=rμ​[r2−Cr2​−r2r2−C​]=r3(r2−C)μC(2r2−C)​,a<r<b.​
(10)
This is a first-order ordinary differential equation for the radial stress, parametrized by the unknown inner radius. For inflation C>0, its right side is positive, so tr​ increases from the negative inner pressure to zero at the outer surface. The denominator stays positive because r2−C=R2≥A2>0.

i OurBigBook.com
Integrate the radial equilibrium equation across the thickness, retaining the integral rather than evaluating its antiderivative. The traction conditions give tr​(b)−tr​(a)=P. Hence a must satisfy
P=μ∫a(A+H)2+a2−A2​​r1​[r2−a2+A2r2​−r2r2−a2+A2​]dr.​
(11)
All endpoints and coefficients here depend only on a and the specified material and reference geometry. This is the required implicit pressure–radius relation for inflation and extension of an incompressible tube with unit axial stretch. In particular a=A gives zero integrand and P=0. Once an admissible root is selected, the radial stress follows by integrating from the outer face, tr​(r)=−∫rb​(dtr​/ds)ds, and the remaining stress components follow from the constitutive equation.

a OurBigBook.com
Substituting the sinusoidal plane wave into the Navier-Cauchy equation gives Δu=−u, utt​=−c2u, and ∇divu=−k(k⋅a)sin(k⋅X−ct) because ∣k∣=1. Equality of the amplitudes is therefore
ρc2a=μa+(λ+μ)k(k⋅a).​
(12)
This is an eigenvalue problem for the acoustic tensor μI+(λ+μ)k⊗k. Choosing a nonzero amplitude parallel to k gives ρc2=λ+2μ; choosing any nonzero amplitude perpendicular to k gives ρc2=μ. For positive mass density and stable Lamé parameters, both squared speeds are positive. These choices construct nonzero solutions explicitly, proving existence.

b

Words: 105 Articles: 1
2 OurBigBook.com

Solution

Words: 105
b OurBigBook.com
Resolve each amplitude by the orthogonal projections
aL​=(a⋅k)k,aT​=a−(a⋅k)k.
(13)
The acoustic tensor acts by λ+2μ on the longitudinal line and by μ on its perpendicular plane. Thus
cL​=ρλ+2μ​​,cT​=ρμ​​.​
(14)
The longitudinal component is a longitudinal wave, while the two independent transverse polarizations are transverse waves. Each projection obeys its own wave equation, and superposition adds the corresponding solutions.
A general planar disturbance is therefore a sum of the two types. For a single sinusoid with a common speed, both projections cannot be nonzero unless the two speeds coincide: projecting the eigenvalue equation gives (ρc2−λ−2μ)aL​=0 and (ρc2−μ)aT​=0. This distinguishes superposition of waves with different speeds from a mixed polarization traveling rigidly at one speed.

c OurBigBook.com
Divide the Navier-Cauchy equation by mass density. Since μ/ρ=cT2​ and (λ+μ)/ρ=cL2​−cT2​, it becomes
utt​=cT2​Δu+(cL2​−cT2​)∇divu.​
(15)
Equivalently, using the vector calculus identity Δu=∇divu−∇×(∇×u),
utt​=cL2​∇divu−cT2​∇×(∇×u).​
(16)
The divergence and curl consequently propagate at cL​ and cT​, respectively.

d OurBigBook.com
Use the complex amplitude and take its real part at the end; the equations are linear with real coefficients. Let M=λ+2μ, ω=kc, and v=(A,B)T. The differential operators on this exponential function are ∂X​=ik, ∂Y​=−b, and ∂t​=−iω. Substitution gives
(μb2−Mk2+ρk2c2−i(λ+μ)kb​−i(λ+μ)kbMb2−μk2+ρk2c2​)(BA​)=0.
(17)
The determinant factors as
μM[b2−k2+Mρk2c2​][b2−k2+μρk2c2​].
(18)
Consequently choose the positive decay roots
b1​=k1−c2/cL2​​,b2​=k1−c2/cT2​​.​
(19)
The Rayleigh wave decay exponents must both give strict decay into the half-space. A genuine nonzero Rayleigh wave combines both, as the free-surface equations below also show. Thus b1​,b2​>0 requires 0<c<min(cT​,cL​)​. In a stable ordinary isotropic solid cT​<cL​, so it is slower even than the transverse wave. The roots depend on the candidate speed as well as the material constants and wavenumber; the speed is fixed by the boundary condition.

For the b1​ root the elastic wave polarization is parallel to the complex derivative vector (ik,−b1​), giving
B1​=kib1​​A1​.​
(20)
For the b2​ root the field has zero divergence, ikA2​−b2​B2​=0, giving
B2​=b2​ik​A2​.​
(21)
These relations also follow by inserting each decay root into either row of the matrix. The complete decaying displacement field has complex components
U(Y)=A1​e−b1​Y+A2​e−b2​Y,V(Y)=kib1​​A1​e−b1​Y+b2​ik​A2​e−b2​Y,
(22)
multiplied by eik(X−ct), followed by the real part. The first constituent is irrotational and the second is divergence-free; both are needed to cancel the two surface tractions.

The material occupies Y≥0, so its outward unit normal at the surface is n=−eY​. A free surface has zero traction, τn=0. Therefore
τXY​(X,0,t)=0,τYY​(X,0,t)=0,τZY​(X,0,t)=0.​
(23)
The third condition is automatic for the two-dimensional displacement field with zero third component and no dependence on the third coordinate. The first two impose zero shear and normal stress at the free surface.

Write the in-plane displacement field as (u,v). The infinitesimal strain tensor gives 2eXY​=uY​+vX​, eYY​=vY​, and tre=uX​+vY​. The two nontrivial zero-traction conditions become
uY​+vX​=0,λuX​+(λ+2μ)vY​=0(Y=0).​

Equivalently for the complex amplitudes they are U′(0)+ikV(0)=0 and ikλU(0)+(λ+2μ)V′(0)=0. Because the constitutive equation is real and linear, vanishing of these complex amplitudes enforces the conditions at every position and time.

h OurBigBook.com
Substituting both profiles into the shear traction condition gives
2b1​A1​+b2​b22​+k2​A2​=0.
(25)
For the normal traction, use Mb12​=Mk2−ρk2c2 and b22​+k2=k2(2−c2/cT2​). Removing the common nonzero factors yields the second equation. Thus
(2b1​(b22​+k2)/k​(b22​+k2)/b2​2k​)(A2​A1​​)=0.​
(26)
A nonzero displacement field requires the determinant to vanish:
(b22​+k2)2=4k2b1​b2​.​
(27)
Let s=c2/cT2​ and η=cT2​/cL2​. Cancelling k4 gives the Rayleigh secular equation
(2−s)2=41−s​1−ηs​,0<s<1.​
(28)
Squaring and excluding the zero root gives s3−8s2+(24−16η)s−16(1−η)=0; any candidate must still satisfy the unsquared equation and the decay inequalities. For stable isotropic elasticity 0<η<3/4, this cubic has one root in (0,1): its values at zero and one have opposite signs, and its strictly negative second derivative on this interval prevents a second crossing back to a positive final value. Both sides of the unsquared equation are positive there, so this root is valid.
The root s=0 of the squared equation is not a propagating surface wave: at c=0, the two roots coincide, and the null combination A2​=−A1​ in this representation produces zero displacement field. As a numerical example, λ=μ gives η=1/3 and c≃0.9194cT​. The surface-wave speed is independent of k in this homogeneous half-space model.

Let s be arc length, so inextensibility makes r′=d3​ a unit tangent vector. The curvature is κ=θ′, hence m′=EIθ′′ez​ for uniform bending stiffness. Balance of linear momentum in static equilibrium gives a constant resultant n=Fex​+Gey​.
Taking the cross product directly,
d3​×n=(cosθex​+sinθey​)×(Fex​+Gey​)=(Gcosθ−Fsinθ)ez​.
(29)
The scalar component of conservation of angular momentum therefore becomes
EIθ′′+Gcosθ−Fsinθ=0.​
(30)
Together with x′=cosθ and y′=sinθ, this Euler elastica equation determines the centerline once the appropriate boundary conditions are supplied.

b OurBigBook.com
Put P=−F>0. With G=0, the Euler elastica equation is EIθ′′+Psinθ=0. Linearization about the straight configuration gives EIθ′′+Pθ=0. Under the boundary conditions explicitly stated, θ(0)=θ(L)=0, a nonzero solution requires
Pn​=EI(Lnπ​)2,θn​(s)=CsinLnπs​,n=1,2,….​
(31)
Thus with only the stated boundary conditions, the first critical compressive force is Fc​=−π2EI/L2​. The corresponding linear transverse displacement field, with y(0)=0, is
yn​(s)=nπCL​(1−cosLnπs​),
(32)
and x(s)=s to first order. These are the successive small-amplitude buckling modes. The nonlinear branches retain the simple pendulum equation; if the angular amplitude is θm​ and m=sin(θm​/2), fitting n half-periods into the rod gives P=4EIn2K(m)2/L2, which tends to Pn​ as m↓0. Here K is the complete elliptic integral of the first kind, with m its modulus, not its squared parameter.
Endpoint position compatibility for the Euler elastica adds a condition when fully fixed clamps have equal transverse height. If the word “clamped” also imposes y(L)=y(0), then ∫0L​θn​ds=0. With G=0, this retains only even n=2j, yielding
Pj​=4j2π2EI/L2,Pfirst​=4π2EI/L2.​
(33)
The same exclusion is exact on the nonlinear branches: integrating the elastica equation gives P∫sinθds=−EI[θ′(L)−θ′(0)], and an odd number of half-periods reverses the nonzero endpoint slope. Hence odd n changes the endpoint height. The conventional fixed-clamp first Euler buckling load is the second boxed value, whereas the source's explicitly listed boundary conditions alone give the first. Additional clamped modes with a nonzero transverse reaction require releasing the imposed G=0. Load-controlled buckling also permits axial shortening; an exactly inextensible rod whose endpoint separation is fixed equal to its full length has no room to buckle.

c OurBigBook.com
Set ℓ=EI/F​. The Euler elastica equation becomes θ′′=ℓ−2sinθ. Multiplying by θ′ gives the first integral
2EI​(θ′)2+Fcosθ=C.
(34)
Straight asymptotic ends require θ′→0 and a horizontal tangent, so C=F. This is the separatrix of a simple pendulum with its angle shifted by π. A nonconstant solution moves from θ=0 to 2π, with the same physical tangent at both ends, taking infinite arc length to approach either limit. One explicit representative is
θ(s)=4arctanes/ℓ.​
(35)
Using cosθ=1−2sech2(s/ℓ) and sinθ=−2sech(s/ℓ)tanh(s/ℓ) and integrating r′=(cosθ,sinθ) gives the tensile elastica loop
x(s)=s−2ℓtanh(s/ℓ),y(s)=2ℓsech(s/ℓ).​
(36)
This solution is nontrivial, tends to horizontal straight tails, and has finite excess bending energy. It is also necessarily self-intersecting. Indeed y is even and x is odd. For z>0, let f(z)=z−2tanhz. Near zero f(z)<0, while f(2)>0, so there is a root z∗​∈(0,2). At the two distinct parameters s=±ℓz∗​, both points have x=0 and the same y>0. Numerically z∗​≃1.91501.
Any nonconstant smooth solution with the same straight asymptotic conditions has this first integral. Between consecutive horizontal orientations, separating variables gives the displayed solution up to translation, reflection, and reversal. It cannot pass through a straight equilibrium at a finite parameter, by uniqueness of the ordinary differential equation. Thus all such nontrivial branches have the same loop crossing. This is an ideal zero-thickness rod solution; a physical rod with self-contact excluded cannot realize the crossing without adding contact forces.

d OurBigBook.com
Take the clamp at s=0 with x(0)=y(0)=θ(0)=0. An end force without an applied end moment gives the free-end condition θ′(1)=0. With F=0, the Euler elastica equation is EIθ′′+Gcosθ=0. For ∣G∣/(EI)≪1, θ is small, so the leading equation is
EIθ′′=−G.
(37)
Integrating with the two boundary conditions gives
θ(s)=EIG​(s−2s2​).​
(38)
To first order, x′=cosθ=1+O(G2) and y′=sinθ=θ+O(G3). Hence
x(s)=s+O(G2),y(s)=6EIG​s2(3−s)+O(G3),0≤s≤1.​
(39)
Equivalently y(x)=Gx2(3−x)/(6EI) at leading order, the familiar cantilever response. Because G<0, the deflection is downward. The free-end displacement field and slope are y(1)=G/(3EI) and θ(1)=G/(2EI). The remainder notation holds with fixed positive EI and nondimensional unit rod length.
