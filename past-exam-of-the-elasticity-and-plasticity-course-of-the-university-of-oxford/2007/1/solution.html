The in-plane equilibrium equations are τxx,x​+τxy,y​=0 and τxy,x​+τyy,y​=0. On a simply connected patch, each divergence-free row has a streamfunction: write τxx​=ϕy​, τxy​=−ϕx​=−ψy​, τyy​=ψx​. Symmetry gives ϕx​=ψy​, so another potential exists with Ay​=ϕ, Ax​=ψ. Hence
τxx​=Ayy​,τyy​=Axx​,τxy​=−Axy​.​
(1)
If two such Airy stress functions give the same stress, their difference has zero Hessian and is therefore an arbitrary affine function a+bx+cy on a connected domain. This is the full nonuniqueness for a fixed stress. The existence argument is local, or global on a simply connected domain; domains that are multiply connected regions require the associated force and moment period conditions for a single-valued global potential. Equilibrium alone should not silently erase that topological qualification.
For plane strain put s=exx​+eyy​. The constitutive equation gives τxx​+τyy​=2(λ+μ)s. Consequently
exx​=2μτxx​−λs​,eyy​=2μτyy​−λs​,exy​=2μτxy​​,s=2(λ+μ)ΔA​.
(2)
The displacement Saint-Venant compatibility equations exx,yy​+eyy,xx​−2exy,xy​=0 then becomes
0=2μ(exx,yy​+eyy,xx​−2exy,xy​)=2(λ+μ)λ+2μ​Δ2A.
(3)
For stable elastic moduli the coefficient is nonzero, so Δ2A=0​. If D is bounded and smooth and A=∂n​A=0 on its boundary, integrating twice by parts gives 0=∫D​AΔ2AdV=∫D​(ΔA)2dV. Thus A is harmonic with zero boundary value and A=0​ by uniqueness of the Dirichlet problem. The normal-derivative condition is what removes the extra fourth-order boundary terms.
For the pressurized annulus, substituting the radial constitutive equations into equilibrium gives u′′+u′/r−u/r2=0, so u=Cr+D/r. The stresses are
τrr​=2(λ+μ)C−r22μD​,τθθ​=2(λ+μ)C+r22μD​.
(4)
Using gauge stresses relative to ambient, the boundaries are τrr​(a)=−P, τrr​(b)=0. Therefore
2(λ+μ)C=b2−a2Pa2​,2μD=b2−a2Pa2b2​,
(5)
and
∣τrr​−τθθ​∣=(b2−a2)r22Pa2b2​,a≤r≤bmax​∣τrr​−τθθ​∣=b2−a22Pb2​.​
(6)
The maximum occurs at the bore. Once the chosen yield criterion is reached, Hooke's law cannot describe the total strain alone: write total strain as elastic plus plastic strain and supplement the elastic stress law with a yield condition, a plastic flow rule and any required work hardening law. A plastic region starts near the bore and must be matched to the remaining elastic region. The exact conversion between a principal-stress difference and the quoted yield stress depends on whether the material criterion is Tresca or von Mises. This is the elastic stress concentration in a pressurized thick cylinder.
