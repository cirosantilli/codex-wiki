The infinitesimal strain is eij​=(ui,j​+uj,i​)/2. A constant translation has zero gradient. For the remaining term, ui​=ϵikl​ωk​xl​ has ui,j​=ϵikj​ωk​, a skew-symmetric matrix. Therefore
eij​=0.​
(1)
Physically a is a translation and ω×x is an infinitesimal rigid rotation, with axis along ω and rotation angle ∣ω∣. It is the linearized rotation displacement, not the exact displacement of a general finite rotation. This distinction matters because the infinitesimal strain omits quadratic displacement-gradient terms.

b OurBigBook.com
Choose zero energy in the unstrained state. The required strain energy density is
W(e)=2λ​(tre)2+μe:e.​
(2)
Its variation is δW=(λtr(e)I+2μe):δe, giving the prescribed stress as the tensor gradient. This variational notation handles the symmetric off-diagonal components without a spurious factor of two.
Decompose e=edev+(tre)I/3 into orthogonal deviatoric and spherical parts. Then
W=μedev:edev+63λ+2μ​(tre)2.​
(3)
The two squares vanish together only for e=0. Thus μ>0 and 3λ+2μ>0 are sufficient for positive definiteness. They are necessary by testing a nonzero trace-free strain and a pure dilation separately. Equivalently both shear modulus μ and bulk modulus K=λ+2μ/3 must be positive.

Multiply equilibrium by the displacement and integrate. Integration by parts and the traction condition give
0=∫B​u⋅(∇⋅T+ρg)dV=∫∂B​u⋅σdS−∫B​T:∇udV+∫B​ρu⋅gdV.
(4)
The stress is symmetric, so it contracts only with the symmetric part of ∇u, namely e. Hooke's law then gives T:e=λ(tre)2+2μe:e=2W. Hence
∫B​ρu⋅gdV=∫B​2WdV−∫∂B​u⋅σdS.​
(5)
The identity is the linear-elastic work balance: twice the stored quadratic energy equals the body-force and boundary-traction work paired with the final displacement.

Solution

Words: 92
d OurBigBook.com
Apply energy uniqueness for a linear elastic traction problem. Subtract two solutions with the same loading, writing their displacement difference as v. Linearity makes its body force and boundary traction zero. The preceding energy identity therefore yields ∫B​2W(e(v))dV=0. Positive definiteness and continuity imply e(v)=0 everywhere. By the permitted zero-strain characterization,
v=a+ω×x.​
(6)
Thus the solution is unique modulo infinitesimal rigid translations and rotations, on each connected body. Conversely adding such a displacement changes neither strain nor stress, so these are exactly the unconstrained modes. This is conditional uniqueness; existence of a pure-traction solution additionally requires balanced resultant force and torque.

Use the torsion stress function for a simply connected bar. The only nonzero strain components are exz​=(wx​−Ωy)/2 and eyz​=(wy​+Ωx)/2. Thus
τxz​=μ(wx​−Ωy),τyz​=μ(wy​+Ωx).
(7)
The axial equilibrium equation is ∂x​τxz​+∂y​τyz​=0. Since the section is simply connected, the planar divergence-free stress field has a stream function:
τxz​=ϕy​,τyz​=−ϕx​,wx​=Ωy+ϕy​/μ,wy​=−Ωx−ϕx​/μ.​
(8)
Equality of the mixed warping derivatives gives Δϕ=−2μΩ​. On the lateral boundary the axial traction is ϕy​nx​−ϕx​ny​, the tangential derivative of ϕ. Traction-free walls therefore make ϕ constant on the connected boundary, and its arbitrary additive constant can be chosen so ϕ=0 on ∂D​.
The axial torque is
M=∫D​(xτyz​−yτxz​)dA=−∫D​(xϕx​+yϕy​)dA=2∫D​ϕdA​.
(9)
The divergence theorem removes the boundary term because ϕ=0.
Tresca's yield criterion requires the maximum shear stress, half the largest principal-stress difference, not to exceed the material's shear yield stress τY​. The stress eigenvalues here are 0,±τxz2​+τyz2​​, so the maximum shear is ∣∇ϕ∣ and
∣∇ϕ∣≤τY​.​
(10)
If yield is instead quoted as a uniaxial tensile stress, that parameter is 2τY​; the present convention is the source's shear parameter.
For the ellipse, the proposed quadratic vanishes on its boundary. Its Laplacian fixes
C=a2+b2μΩa2b2​,ϕ=C(1−x2/a2−y2/b2).​
(11)
The corresponding warping is w=Ω(b2−a2)xy/(a2+b2)+constant. Elliptic polar coordinates x=arcosϑ, y=brsinϑ give ∫D​ϕ=Cπab/2, hence
M=a2+b2πμΩa3b3​,R=M/Ω=a2+b2πμa3b3​.​
(12)
To locate first yield, maximize ∣∇ϕ∣=2∣C∣x2/a4+y2/b4​. It increases radially to the boundary. For a>b, the maximum there occurs at (0,±b) and equals 2∣C∣/b. Therefore
∣Ω∣Y​=2a2bμ(a2+b2)τY​​.​
(13)
The positive-twist value is the requested expression. The ends of the minor axis yield first, not the ends of the longer axis. This is elliptical-bar torsion and first yield.

a OurBigBook.com
For an admissible variation η with η(0)=η(L)=0,
δU=∫0L​(EIθ′η′−Psinθη)ds=−∫0L​(EIθ′′+Psinθ)ηds.
(14)
Stationarity for every variation therefore gives the Euler-Lagrange equation
EIθ′′+Psinθ=0.​
(15)
The first energy term is bending energy: the curvature of an inextensible planar beam is θs​. The shortening relative to the straight reference is ∫(1−cosθ)ds. A prescribed compressive dead load does positive work on that shortening, so its potential-energy contribution is minus P times that integral. A minimizer must satisfy the stationary equation, but a stationary solution need not be a minimizer.

Solution

c OurBigBook.com
Linearization gives θ′′+π2λθ=0. The two homogeneous Dirichlet conditions select λ=n2,θ=Asin(nπξ),n=1,2,…​. For any small trial amplitude in this mode, ∫01​sin2(nπξ)=∫01​cos2(nπξ)=1/2, so
U[Asin(nπξ)]=4Lπ2EIA2​(n2−λ)+O(A4).​
(17)
For λ<1 every nonzero quadratic variation is positive by the sharp Poincaré inequality. For λ>1, the first mode gives negative energy at sufficiently small amplitude, so zero cannot be a local minimum.
The printed strict “only if” conclusion omits the critical case. At λ=1 the quadratic test is degenerate, not negative. In fact zero is still the unique minimum of the full displayed functional: use 1−cosθ≤θ2/2 and ∫θ′2≥π2∫θ2 to get U≥0, with strict inequality for a nonzero continuous function because 1−cost<t2/2 when t=0. In particular on the critical first mode,
U[Asinπξ]=64Lπ2EI​A4+O(A6)>0for small A=0.​
(18)
Thus strict positivity of the second variation holds for λ<1, while local minimality of the full energy holds also at λ=1. This is the distinction captured by critical-load minimality of the angular Euler strut.

Near the first eigenvalue, load detuning supplies a term ϵλ1​θ, while the first nonlinearity in sinθ=θ−θ3/6+⋯ is cubic. Balancing them gives θ2=O(ϵ), so θ=O(ϵ​)​.
Put θ=ϵ​[A0​sinπξ+ϵΘ1​+⋯]. At order ϵ3/2,
(∂ξ2​+π2)Θ1​=−π2λ1​A0​sinπξ+6π2A03​​sin3πξ.
(19)
The operator has kernel sinπξ, so Fredholm solvability requires its forcing to be orthogonal to that function. Since ∫sin2πξ=1/2 and ∫sin4πξ=3/8,
−λ1​A0​/2+A03​/16=0,A0​(A02​−8λ1​)=0.​
(20)
When this holds the nonresonant forcing is −π2A03​sin3πξ/24; for instance Θ1​=A03​sin3πξ/192 is a solution after absorbing its arbitrary first-mode component into the amplitude convention.
The branches are A0​=0 for all λ1​,A0​=±8λ1​​ for λ1​≥0​. This is a supercritical pitchfork bifurcation. The leading reduced energy is proportional to A04​−16λ1​A02​: the nonzero branches are minima for λ1​>0, while zero loses minimality on that side. At the origin the quartic term still stabilizes the critical state.

Solution

a OurBigBook.com
Take z positive upwards, so the source's sign convention makes p a downward applied load. The small-slope tension contribution on a short interval is Tw′′. Let r≥0 be the upward contact reaction. Force balance gives Tw′′−p+r=0. Nonpenetration is w≥f, and reaction is absent where w>f. Therefore
w−f≥0,Tw′′−p=−r≤0,(w−f)(Tw′′−p)=0.​
(21)
The contact boundary must have continuous w to avoid a broken string, and continuous slope in this smooth-contact model with no concentrated point force. Integrating force balance over a vanishing interval around a contact endpoint gives T[w′]=0 when the load and reaction are locally bounded. This is the one-dimensional obstacle problem, with its reaction acting as a nonnegative constraint force.

b OurBigBook.com
For any admissible v, put q=v−w. At endpoints q=0. On the contact set, w=f and v≥f imply q≥0, while away from contact the reaction r=p−Tw′′ is zero. Hence rq≥0 everywhere. Integration by parts gives
T∫−11​w′(v′−w′)dx+∫−11​p(v−w)dx​=∫−11​(p−Tw′′)(v−w)dx=∫−11​r(v−w)dx≥0.​
(22)
Rearranging proves the requested variational inequality. Piecewise integration is legitimate because the slope is continuous at the contact boundary, so there are no omitted point-force terms.

Solution

c OurBigBook.com
The small-slope excess length is ∫(w′)2/2, so the first term is the work needed against uniform tension. The second is the potential energy of the downward distributed load. Their sum is total potential energy subject to the obstacle constraint.
Expand the difference exactly:
U[v]−U[w]=T∫w′(v′−w′)dx+∫p(v−w)dx+2T​∫(v′−w′)2dx.
(23)
The first two integrals together are nonnegative by part (b), and the square is nonnegative. Thus
U[w]≤U[v]for every admissible v.​
(24)
For T>0 this also proves uniqueness: equality for two minimizers forces identical derivatives, and the fixed endpoints remove their additive constant. The convex admissible set and strictly convex energy make the variational inequality a global minimization condition.

d OurBigBook.com
With no applied load the free string segments are straight, while in contact w=f=h−x2. By symmetry use contact [−s,s] and a right-hand line through (1,0) tangent at s. The tangency conditions are w(s)=h−s2 and w′(s)=−2s, giving w=2s(1−x) on [s,1] and h−s2=2s(1−s). Therefore
s=1−1−h​,w(x)={h−x2,2s(1−∣x∣),​∣x∣≤s,s≤∣x∣≤1.​​
(25)
The other algebraic root 1+1−h​ is outside the admissible contact interval except at the coincident endpoint h=1.
Verification makes the construction complete: the values and slopes match at ±s, the endpoints are zero, and outside contact w−f=(∣x∣−s)2≥0. Inside contact Tw′′=−2T≤0; outside it w′′=0, so complementarity holds. The minimization argument proves this verified solution is the unique equilibrium. At h=0 the contact set is the single point {0} and w=0; at h=1 contact fills the whole interval. This is the parabolic obstacle under a taut string.
