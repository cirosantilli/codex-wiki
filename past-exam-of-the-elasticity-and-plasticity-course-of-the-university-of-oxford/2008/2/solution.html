Use the torsion stress function for a simply connected bar. The only nonzero strain components are exz​=(wx​−Ωy)/2 and eyz​=(wy​+Ωx)/2. Thus
τxz​=μ(wx​−Ωy),τyz​=μ(wy​+Ωx).
(7)
The axial equilibrium equation is ∂x​τxz​+∂y​τyz​=0. Since the section is simply connected, the planar divergence-free stress field has a stream function:
τxz​=ϕy​,τyz​=−ϕx​,wx​=Ωy+ϕy​/μ,wy​=−Ωx−ϕx​/μ.​
(8)
Equality of the mixed warping derivatives gives Δϕ=−2μΩ​. On the lateral boundary the axial traction is ϕy​nx​−ϕx​ny​, the tangential derivative of ϕ. Traction-free walls therefore make ϕ constant on the connected boundary, and its arbitrary additive constant can be chosen so ϕ=0 on ∂D​.
The axial torque is
M=∫D​(xτyz​−yτxz​)dA=−∫D​(xϕx​+yϕy​)dA=2∫D​ϕdA​.
(9)
The divergence theorem removes the boundary term because ϕ=0.
Tresca's yield criterion requires the maximum shear stress, half the largest principal-stress difference, not to exceed the material's shear yield stress τY​. The stress eigenvalues here are 0,±τxz2​+τyz2​​, so the maximum shear is ∣∇ϕ∣ and
∣∇ϕ∣≤τY​.​
(10)
If yield is instead quoted as a uniaxial tensile stress, that parameter is 2τY​; the present convention is the source's shear parameter.
For the ellipse, the proposed quadratic vanishes on its boundary. Its Laplacian fixes
C=a2+b2μΩa2b2​,ϕ=C(1−x2/a2−y2/b2).​
(11)
The corresponding warping is w=Ω(b2−a2)xy/(a2+b2)+constant. Elliptic polar coordinates x=arcosϑ, y=brsinϑ give ∫D​ϕ=Cπab/2, hence
M=a2+b2πμΩa3b3​,R=M/Ω=a2+b2πμa3b3​.​
(12)
To locate first yield, maximize ∣∇ϕ∣=2∣C∣x2/a4+y2/b4​. It increases radially to the boundary. For a>b, the maximum there occurs at (0,±b) and equals 2∣C∣/b. Therefore
∣Ω∣Y​=2a2bμ(a2+b2)τY​​.​
(13)
The positive-twist value is the requested expression. The ends of the minor axis yield first, not the ends of the longer axis. This is elliptical-bar torsion and first yield.
