Apply energy uniqueness for a linear elastic traction problem. Subtract two solutions with the same loading, writing their displacement difference as v. Linearity makes its body force and boundary traction zero. The preceding energy identity therefore yields ∫B​2W(e(v))dV=0. Positive definiteness and continuity imply e(v)=0 everywhere. By the permitted zero-strain characterization,
v=a+ω×x.​
(6)
Thus the solution is unique modulo infinitesimal rigid translations and rotations, on each connected body. Conversely adding such a displacement changes neither strain nor stress, so these are exactly the unconstrained modes. This is conditional uniqueness; existence of a pure-traction solution additionally requires balanced resultant force and torque.
