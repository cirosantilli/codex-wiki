The in-plane equilibrium equations are τxx,x​+τxy,y​=0 and τxy,x​+τyy,y​=0. On a simply connected patch, each divergence-free row has a streamfunction: write τxx​=ϕy​, τxy​=−ϕx​=−ψy​, τyy​=ψx​. Symmetry gives ϕx​=ψy​, so another potential exists with Ay​=ϕ, Ax​=ψ. Hence
τxx​=Ayy​,τyy​=Axx​,τxy​=−Axy​.​
(1)
If two such Airy stress functions give the same stress, their difference has zero Hessian and is therefore an arbitrary affine function a+bx+cy on a connected domain. This is the full nonuniqueness for a fixed stress. The existence argument is local, or global on a simply connected domain; domains that are multiply connected regions require the associated force and moment period conditions for a single-valued global potential. Equilibrium alone should not silently erase that topological qualification.
For plane strain put s=exx​+eyy​. The constitutive equation gives τxx​+τyy​=2(λ+μ)s. Consequently
exx​=2μτxx​−λs​,eyy​=2μτyy​−λs​,exy​=2μτxy​​,s=2(λ+μ)ΔA​.
(2)
The displacement Saint-Venant compatibility equations exx,yy​+eyy,xx​−2exy,xy​=0 then becomes
0=2μ(exx,yy​+eyy,xx​−2exy,xy​)=2(λ+μ)λ+2μ​Δ2A.
(3)
For stable elastic moduli the coefficient is nonzero, so Δ2A=0​. If D is bounded and smooth and A=∂n​A=0 on its boundary, integrating twice by parts gives 0=∫D​AΔ2AdV=∫D​(ΔA)2dV. Thus A is harmonic with zero boundary value and A=0​ by uniqueness of the Dirichlet problem. The normal-derivative condition is what removes the extra fourth-order boundary terms.
For the pressurized annulus, substituting the radial constitutive equations into equilibrium gives u′′+u′/r−u/r2=0, so u=Cr+D/r. The stresses are
τrr​=2(λ+μ)C−r22μD​,τθθ​=2(λ+μ)C+r22μD​.
(4)
Using gauge stresses relative to ambient, the boundaries are τrr​(a)=−P, τrr​(b)=0. Therefore
2(λ+μ)C=b2−a2Pa2​,2μD=b2−a2Pa2b2​,
(5)
and
∣τrr​−τθθ​∣=(b2−a2)r22Pa2b2​,a≤r≤bmax​∣τrr​−τθθ​∣=b2−a22Pb2​.​
(6)
The maximum occurs at the bore. Once the chosen yield criterion is reached, Hooke's law cannot describe the total strain alone: write total strain as elastic plus plastic strain and supplement the elastic stress law with a yield condition, a plastic flow rule and any required work hardening law. A plastic region starts near the bore and must be matched to the remaining elastic region. The exact conversion between a principal-stress difference and the quoted yield stress depends on whether the material criterion is Tresca or von Mises. This is the elastic stress concentration in a pressurized thick cylinder.

Decomposing a plane displacement into components parallel and perpendicular to its wavevector gives compressional and shear speeds
cp​=(λ+2μ)/ρ​,cs​=μ/ρ​,kp​=ω/cp​,ks​=ω/cs​.​
(7)
The incident polarization is longitudinal. Its reflected longitudinal wave reverses the vertical wavevector component while retaining its horizontal component. The converted shear wave has wavevector ks​(sinβ,−cosβ) perpendicular to polarization (cosβ,sinβ).
Every term must have the same phase along the rigid boundary. Thus
ks​sinβ=kp​cosα,sinβ=cp​cs​​cosα.​
(8)
Choose cosβ>0 so the reflected shear wave propagates downward into y<0. At y=0, both displacement components must vanish for every boundary phase:
(1+R1​)cosα+R2​cosβ=0,(1−R1​)sinα+R2​sinβ=0.
(9)
Solving this two-by-two system yields
R1​=sin(α+β)sin(α−β)​,R2​=−sin(α+β)sin2α​.​
(10)
For a stable isotropic material 0<cs​/cp​<1. An incident wave directed toward the boundary has 0<α<π; choosing β∈(−π/2,π/2) by the sine relation gives real reflected waves throughout this range and no zero denominator. In the usual convention restricting incident horizontal propagation to positive x, this is 0<α≤π/2. Grazing endpoints have no incoming normal energy flux and are limiting cases. There is no critical angle for incident compressional-to-shear conversion here, because the converted wave is slower; the opposite incident-wave type has different critical-angle behaviour. At normal incidence α=π/2, β=0, R1​=1 and R2​=0, cancelling the incident displacement with the reversed longitudinal polarization. This is compressional-wave reflection from a rigid elastic boundary.

The tension term Twxx​ is the transverse restoring force from a nearly straight, tensioned rod. The bending term −EIwxxxx​ is the force from Euler-Bernoulli flexure, where E is Young's modulus and I the second moment of area. The right side is transverse inertia. In the displayed one-dimensional equation ρ denotes linear mass density; if it denotes volumetric density instead, the inertial coefficient must include cross-sectional area.
For a Fourier mode, wxx​=−k2w, wxxxx​=k4w and wtt​=−ω2w, giving
ρω2=Tk2+EIk4.​
(11)
For k>0 the phase speed is cp​=ω/k=T/ρ+EIk2/ρ​, strictly increasing with k when EI>0 and T≥0. Thus shorter wavelengths propagate faster in phase. The group velocity cg​=(T+2EIk2)/ρ(T+EIk2)​ is also increasing, so the statement is consistent with wave-packet transport.
At a simple support the displacement and bending moment vanish: w=0 and EIwxx​=0 at x=0,L. The eigenmodes are sin(nπx/L) for n=1,2,…. Setting T=0 in the dispersion relation therefore yields
ωn​=ρEI​​L2n2π2​.​
(12)
The integer is positive for a nonzero mode; n=0 gives the identically zero sine function, and negative integers repeat the same modes. Bending stiffness makes the frequencies quadratic in mode number, unlike a purely tensioned string.

For antiplane displacement u=(0,0,w) the only nonzero strain components are exz​=wx​/2 and eyz​=wy​/2. Commutation of mixed partial derivatives gives the necessary local compatibility condition
∂y​exz​−∂x​eyz​=0.​
(13)
The supplied stress is symmetric, independent of z and trace-free. Its first two equilibrium equations are automatic; its third gives
∂x​(−x2+y2μy​)+∂y​(x2+y2μx​)=0
(14)
away from the origin. Antiplane shear has zero trace, so isotropic Hooke's law reduces to e=τ/(2μ)​. Hence
wx​=−x2+y2y​,wy​=x2+y2x​.​
(15)
Locally this integrates to w=arg(x+iy)+C, represented on a suitable branch by arctan(y/x)+C. The strain condition is satisfied at every point of the punctured plane, but the displacement cannot be made globally single-valued there:
∮r=R​(wx​dx+wy​dy)=2π.​
(16)
A gradient of a globally single-valued smooth displacement would instead integrate to zero around any closed curve. Local compatibility is therefore not sufficient on this multiply connected region.
A physical construction is a Volterra screw dislocation: cut the cylinder along a radial half-plane ending on its axis, shift one cut face relative to the other parallel to the axis, and rejoin. The axial mismatch is the Burgers vector. In the given normalization its magnitude is 2π; with a dimensional mismatch b one uses w=barg(x+iy)/(2π). An intact pristine solid cannot reach this state by a smooth single-valued elastic deformation alone. The axis is a dislocation core where the continuum elastic field is singular and must be regularized by microscopic structure or plastic deformation. On the punctured domain the differential condition holds locally; globally its nonzero circulation detects the defect. In a distributional description, that incompatibility is concentrated on the axis. This is local strain compatibility versus screw-dislocation circulation.

Write exx​=ux,x​, eyy​=uy,y​ and 2exy​=ux,y​+uy,x​. Differentiating twice and cancelling the mixed partial derivatives gives
exx,yy​−2exy,xy​+eyy,xx​=0.​
(17)
A convenient set of six three-dimensional Saint-Venant compatibility equations is
e11,22​+e22,11​−2e12,12​e22,33​+e33,22​−2e23,23​e33,11​+e11,33​−2e13,13​e11,23​+e23,11​−e12,13​−e13,12​e22,13​+e13,22​−e12,23​−e23,12​e33,12​+e12,33​−e13,23​−e23,13​​=0,=0,=0,=0,=0,=0.​​
(18)
They follow by inserting eij​=(ui,j​+uj,i​)/2 and commuting derivatives. They form the six symmetric components of the incompatibility tensor Cij​=ϵikℓ​ϵjmn​eℓn,km​, up to the indicated signs and permutation of components.
They obey the three differential identities ∂i​Cij​=0, because a symmetric pair of derivatives contracts with an antisymmetric Levi-Civita symbol. Thus there are three independent differential constraints, represented symmetrically by six component equations; they are not six unrelated constraints on the six strain functions. For example, at a nonzero wavevector directed along the third axis, compatibility imposes e11​=e22​=e12​=0 while e13​,e23​,e33​ can arise from the three displacement components. This confirms rank three. One cannot simply discard the same arbitrary three component equations in every direction; their independence is differential, with the stated identities. On a simply connected domain these are the local Saint-Venant compatibility equations for strain to be a symmetric displacement gradient, while domains that are multiply connected regions require global closure conditions as in the screw example. This is Saint-Venant strain compatibility and differential identities.

Take the trace of the constitutive relation at zero stress. It gives 0=(2μ+3λ)ekk​−3cα(T−T0​). With the observed stress-free volume strain this requires
c=K=λ+32μ​.​
(19)
This is the bulk modulus. A stable solid resists uniform compression, so K>0​; equivalently the elastic energy of a nonzero pure dilation is positive. The thermal strain is isotropic, eijth​=α(T−T0​)δij​/3, because α here is the volumetric, not linear, thermal expansion coefficient.
Let β=Kα. Using eij​=(ui,j​+uj,i​)/2, stress divergence is
∂j​τij​=μΔui​+(λ+μ)∂i​(∇⋅u)−β∂i​T.
(20)
Zero body force and equilibrium therefore give
μΔu+(λ+μ)∇(∇⋅u)=β∇T,β=α(λ+2μ/3).​
(21)
For an applied body force per unit volume b, equilibrium is ∇⋅τ+b=0, so its right-hand side becomes β∇T−b. Consequently
b=+β∇T​
(22)
cancels the effective thermal forcing in the displacement equation. Per unit mass the force is β∇T/ρ. This sign follows from the divergence of the thermal stress −β(T−T0​)I, which is −β∇T. Cancelling that divergence is distinct from removing the stress tensor itself: boundary tractions and thermal compatibility still matter.
For spatially uniform heating with the entire boundary fixed, u=0 is the unique equilibrium displacement in a stable elastic solid, and
τij​=−Kα(T−T0​)δij​.​
(23)
The stress is compressive hydrostatic stress for a temperature rise. The fixed boundary supplies the tractions that prevent thermal expansion. If T varies spatially and no compensating body force is imposed, fixing the boundary alone does not generally force the interior displacement to vanish: one must solve the displayed forced Navier-Cauchy equation with u=0 on the boundary, then recover the stress. The simple uniform hydrostatic expression should not be asserted for arbitrary temperature fields. This is uniform thermal stress in a fully constrained solid.
