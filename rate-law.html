Rate law

 Home Chemistry Chemical reaction Chemical kinetics Reaction rate
OurBigBook.com Words: 2k Articles: 9
A rate law specifies how a reaction rate depends on concentrations under stated conditions. A common form is
v=k[A]α[B]β,
(38)
where k is a rate constant and the exponents are partial orders of reaction. Determine them experimentally, or derive them from a justified reaction mechanism. Do not infer α=a and β=b from the net chemical equation: its coefficients describe consumption ratios, whereas the exponents describe sensitivity to concentration. See IUPAC Gold Book: rate law and Flowers et al., Chemistry 2e, §12.3: rate laws.
A rate law becomes a prediction of time evolution when combined with stoichiometry in a rate equation, for example d[A]/dt=−av. Agreement with a measured rate law is useful evidence for a reaction mechanism, but several routes can give the same dependence.
