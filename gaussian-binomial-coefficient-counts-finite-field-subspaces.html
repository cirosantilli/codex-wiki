Gaussian binomial coefficient counts finite-field subspaces (#Gr(k,Fqn​)=Gn,k​(q))

 Home Mathematics Area of mathematics Combinatorics Binomial coefficient Gaussian binomial coefficient
OurBigBook.com Words: 203
For a prime power q and 0≤k≤n, count ordered lists of k linearly independent vectors in Fqn​. The first has qn−1 choices. After i independent choices, their linear span contains qi vectors, so the next has qn−qi choices. Every k-dimensional vector subspace has exactly ∏i=0k−1​(qk−qi) ordered bases. Dividing the list count by this common multiplicity gives
#Gr(k,Fqn​)=∏i=0k−1​(qk−qi)∏i=0k−1​(qn−qi)​=Gn,k​(q).
(61)
The notation Gr refers to the Grassmannian, here considered simply as the finite collection of these vector subspaces.[ref]
For n=4,k=2,q=2, there are 15⋅14=210 ordered independent pairs. Each two-dimensional vector subspace has 3⋅2=6 ordered bases, leaving 210/6=35 vector subspaces. There are only (24​)=6 coordinate-selected vector subspaces; most of the 35 also contain vectors combining coordinates.
A unique reduced row echelon form gives another count. Its six possible pivot-column patterns have 4,3,2,2,1,0 freely chosen entries. Each free entry lies in F2​, giving 16,8,4,4,2,1 vector subspaces, respectively. The diagram fixes the zero entries required to make each pivot the first nonzero entry in its row and the only nonzero entry in its column. Different choices describe different row spaces.
