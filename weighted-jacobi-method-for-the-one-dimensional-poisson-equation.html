Weighted Jacobi method for the one-dimensional Poisson equation

For the centered second-difference matrix with diagonal −2 and off-diagonals 1, the weighted-Jacobi eigenvalues are
λk​(ω)=1−ω+ωcosn+1kπ​,1≤k≤n.
(127)
The method converges for every grid size exactly when 0<ω≤1.
Multiplying both A and b by −1 leaves D−1A and the weighted Jacobi method unchanged. Thus the same formula applies to the positive second-difference matrix Tn​ with diagonal 2 and neighboring entries −1, the finite difference form of the one-dimensional Poisson equation with Dirichlet boundary conditions. Its eigenvectors have components sin(jkπ/(n+1)), and D−1Tn​ has eigenvalues 1−cos(kπ/(n+1)). At ω=2/3, upper-half modes have factors between −1/3 and 1/3, while low-frequency factors remain close to one on fine grids. This explains why the weighted Jacobi method helps a multigrid method by reducing oscillatory errors, even when it is a slow solver on its own.[ref]
