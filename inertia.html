Inertia

 Home Physics Branches of physics Classical mechanics
OurBigBook.com Words: 119
Inertia is a body's persistence in its state of rest or constant velocity when the net external force vanishes in an inertial frame. It is not an extra force that pushes a moving body forward. Newton's first law describes this persistence; for a point mass, Newton's second law quantifies how a nonzero net external force changes its momentum. At constant mass, Fnet​=ma, so a larger mass gives a smaller acceleration for the same net force.
A moving cart does not need a forward net force to maintain its velocity. Ordinary carts slow because friction and drag force produce an opposing net external force; reducing those interactions reveals Newton's first law more clearly. The air-track test of inertial motion separates this persistence from the initial push. In overdamped particle dynamics, the inertial acceleration term is small compared with drag force over the time scales considered; that approximation does not mean that inertia has disappeared.
