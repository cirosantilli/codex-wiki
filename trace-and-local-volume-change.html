Trace and local volume change (det(I+tA)=1+ttrA+O(t2))

 Home Mathematics Area of mathematics Algebra Linear algebra Matrix trace
OurBigBook.com Words: 413
For any fixed real n-by-n matrix A, as the real parameter t tends to zero,
det(I+tA)=1+ttrA+O(t2).
(96)
The big O notation means the remainder is bounded in absolute value by Ct2 for some constant C near zero. The determinant stays positive for sufficiently small t, so its oriented volume factor is also its ordinary volume factor. Thus the trace is the first-order fractional volume change, measured from the identity matrix.
A proof follows directly from multilinearity of the determinant. Each column of I+tA is ej​+tAej​. The constant term chooses all the standard basis columns and equals one. A term containing exactly one factor t replaces just column j by Aej​; expanding that vector in the standard basis leaves only its ej​ component, ajj​, because every other choice repeats a column. Summing gives trA. Terms with two or more replacements have order t2 or higher. This also proves the derivative of the determinant at the identity matrix.
This coefficient has a direct visual meaning. At the point ej​, the linear map x↦Ax has component ajj​ along direction ej​. In a small step x↦x+tAx, these are the first-order changes of the square's or box's sides in their own directions. Summing them gives its fractional area or volume change; the off-diagonal entries initially change shape rather than contributing to this first-order total.
For the constant-coefficient linear system of differential equations x′=Ax, the exact linear flow is x(t)=etAx(0), using the matrix exponential. Its volume factor is
V(t)=det(etA)=ettrA,V(0)=1,V′(t)=(trA)V(t).
(97)
This is the matrix exponential determinant identity. One can derive it without diagonalization: set E(t)=etA, whose matrix inverse is E(−t), and use E′=AE in Jacobi's formula to get V′=detEtr(E−1AE)=(trA)V. The scalar differential equation with initial value one gives the displayed exponential function. Thus positive trace causes volume expansion, negative trace causes contraction, and zero trace preserves volume, even when individual directions behave differently.

The first and third diagonal matrices expand or contract both directions. The second expands horizontally and contracts vertically by reciprocal factors, making a rectangle of unchanged area. For the last nilpotent matrix, A2=0, so its matrix exponential is exactly I+tA; it sends (x,y) to (x+ty,y), a shear mapping. Zero trace allows substantial shape change and does not mean the flow map is stationary.
The first-order statement and the exact flow map should be distinguished. For A=diag(1,−1), det(I+tA)=1−t2, whereas det(etA)=1 for all real t. Zero trace eliminates the first-order change in the approximate step, and it preserves volume exactly in the matrix exponential flow. The examples use an abstract parameter t and coordinate vectors; no physical units are assumed.
