Radial zero-energy rigidity (W(Dy)=0⟹y(x)=vx)

 ... Physics Branches of physics Continuum mechanics Elasticity (physics) Finite elasticity Radial deformation of an elastic solid
OurBigBook.com Words: 141
Consider an orientation-preserving classical radial deformation of an elastic solid on a connected spherical annulus. If its nonnegative energy is zero everywhere and the material is isotropic with strict Baker-Ericksen inequalities, every gradient is a natural state, so r′=r/R. Thus (r/R)′=0 and y=vx.
A separate rigidity argument applies when the zero wells are SO(3)∪⋃i=1N​SO(3)Ui​, with finitely many positive definite symmetric Ui​. The radial gradient F is positive symmetric; if F=QUi​, then F2=Ui2​, so uniqueness of the positive square root gives F=Ui​. The rotation well similarly gives F=I. Hence the continuous ratio r(R)/R takes values in a finite set of eigenvalues and must be constant on the connected radial interval. Differentiating r=vR finishes the proof. This version needs neither material isotropy nor Baker-Ericksen inequalities and does not require an assumed continuity of r′ beyond the radial-map regularity used for the ratio.
