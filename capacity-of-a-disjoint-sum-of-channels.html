Capacity of a disjoint sum of channels (C=log2​∑i​2Ci​)

 Home Mathematics Area of mathematics Probability and statistics Information theory Channel capacity
OurBigBook.com Words: 85
Combine finitely many discrete memoryless channels with disjoint input alphabets and disjoint output alphabets into a block-diagonal channel. The block label B is determined by both input and output. If it has law α, the mutual information is at most H(α)+∑i​αi​Ci​, attained by using each block's capacity-achieving conditional input law. Put S=∑i​2Ci​ and ri​=2Ci​/S. The expression equals log2​S−d(α,r), so Gibbs inequality proves the displayed capacity, attained for block probabilities αi​=ri​. Even blocks of capacity zero contribute distinguishable block choices; three disjoint zero-capacity blocks give capacity log2​3.
