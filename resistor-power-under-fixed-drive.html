Resistor power under fixed drive (PI​=I02​R,PV​=V02​/R)

OurBigBook.com Words: 182
Whether increasing electrical resistance increases Joule heating depends on what the supply holds fixed. Consider separate ideal resistor experiments with R=5Ω and R=10Ω, each maintained at its specified temperature. At fixed electric current I0​=1A, doubling R changes the required electric potential difference from 5 to 10V and doubles power from 5 to 10W. At fixed electric potential difference V0​=5V, doubling R halves electric current from 1 to 0.5A and halves power from 5 to 2.5W. These are stipulated model values, not measured data.
The comparison also predicts feedback when a particular resistor's electrical resistance rises with temperature: a supply maintaining electric current increases its heating power, whereas one maintaining terminal electric potential difference reduces it. Predicting the actual temperature still needs the material response R(T) and its heat transfer to the surroundings; a nominal supply setting does not establish which drive is actually constant at the resistor.
