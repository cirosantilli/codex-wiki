Bragg crystal reflection experiment of 1913

OurBigBook.com Words: 192
William Lawrence Bragg introduced the plane interpretation; his father William Henry Bragg built the X-ray spectrometer. Their joint 1913 paper, The Reflection of X-rays by Crystals, reports the experiment. [ref] [ref]
An X-ray tube supplied radiation through lead slits to a crystal mounted with its cleavage face through the rotation axis. A rotating ionization chamber replaced a spectrometer telescope; electric current measured the scattered signal, and a circular scale supplied angular readings. They first scanned the chamber at fixed crystal orientation, then swept crystal and chamber together to follow the reflection. [ref]
Changing chamber gas checked detector dependence; readings on opposite sides checked face-setting errors. Repeated peaks with sines of half-deflections in integer ratios, together with matching absorption coefficients, distinguished repeated diffraction orders of the same wavelength from different radiation components. Comparing crystal faces constrained interplanar spacings. Their limits included slit broadening, face alignment and incomplete interception at glancing incidence; assigning absolute wavelengths still required a structural choice for d. [ref]
