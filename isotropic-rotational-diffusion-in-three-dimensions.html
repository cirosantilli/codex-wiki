Isotropic rotational diffusion in three dimensions (⟨u(t)⋅u(0)⟩=e−2Dr​t)

 ... Physics Branches of physics Statistical physics Thermodynamics Diffusion Rotational diffusion
OurBigBook.com Words: 140
For isotropic turning in three dimensions, the tip of a marked unit vector u wanders over the unit sphere. Its probability density function f obeys the orientational Fokker-Planck equation
∂t∂f​=Dr​∇u2​f,C1​(t)=⟨u(t)⋅u(0)⟩=e−2Dr​t,τ1​=2Dr​1​.
(57)
Here ∇u2​ is the Laplace-Beltrami operator on the unit sphere. The factor two follows from the sphere surface derivative identities: ∇u2​ui​=−2ui​, so each mean vector component decays at rate 2Dr​. This is a different orientation space from the circle of planar rotational diffusion, whose corresponding rate is Dr​. For the same illustrative Dr​=0.25s−1 and t=2s, this three-dimensional autocorrelation is e−1≃0.368.
One unit vector records a pointing direction, not every aspect of a rigid body's rigid body orientation: rotation about that vector remains unspecified. Also, C1​ requires an identifiable arrow. An unmarked ellipsoid's long axis alone does not supply one, and projecting a three-dimensional trajectory onto a camera plane does not automatically produce the planar model.
