Sample median (Xn​)

 ... Mathematics Area of mathematics Probability and statistics Probability theory Probability distribution Median
OurBigBook.com Words: 228 Articles: 1
The sample median is a central value of an ordered statistical sample. The usual numerical convention selects the middle order statistic for odd sample size and the arithmetic mean of the two central order statistics for even sample size:
Xn​={X(m+1)​,(X(m)​+X(m+1)​)/2,​n=2m+1,n=2m.​
(61)
NIST uses this convention. [ref]. For (0.7,0.1,0.9,0.4,0.2), the sample median is 0.4; for (0.1,0.2,0.4,0.7), it is (0.2+0.4)/2=0.3, which need not be an observed value.
More generally, every value between the two central order statistics in an even statistical sample is a median of its empirical distribution function. The generalized-inverse sample quantile at p=1/2 selects the lower central order statistic in that case, so it can differ from the midpoint convention. Ties are allowed in all these definitions.
The sample median measures the center using sorted positions. Changing the largest observation from 0.9 to 9 in the five-value example leaves the sample median at 0.4, while the sample mean rises from 0.46 to 2.08. Its sampling distribution still depends on the population probability distribution: the mean 1/2 found for five observations from a uniform distribution is a special calculation, and a sample median's expected value need not equal the population median in general.
For a concrete counterexample, three independent and identically distributed random variables with rate-one exponential distribution have sample median X(2)​ with expected value 1/3+1/2=5/6, by independent exponential order-statistic spacings. The population median instead solves 1−e−m=1/2, giving m=log2≈0.693147. Thus even the middle order statistic can have mean different from the population median.
