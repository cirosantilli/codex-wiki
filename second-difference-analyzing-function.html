The Haar wavelet provides an explicit one-vanishing moment comparison:
h(u)=⎩⎨⎧​1−10​0≤u<1/2,1/2≤u<1,otherwise,​∫h=0,∫uh(u)du=−41​,∥h∥2​=1.
(34)
It cancels a constant polynomial, but not a nonconstant affine function. To obtain two vanishing moments by an equally transparent construction, take three adjacent unit intervals with heights proportional to 1,−2,1:
η(u)=6​1​⎩⎨⎧​1−210​0≤u<1,1≤u<2,2≤u<3,otherwise.​
(35)
The L2 norm normalization follows from 12+(−2)2+12=6. Direct integration gives
∫η=6​1−2+1​=0,∫uη(u)du=6​1/2−2(3/2)+5/2​=0,∫u2η(u)du=6​2​=0.
(36)
Thus η has exactly two vanishing moments. In fact its coefficient is a finite difference of adjacent local integrals:
cη​(f;a,b)=6​a​​(A0​−2A1​+A2​),Aj​=∫jj+1​f(b+au)du.
(37)
The weights are those of the second-order central difference. The first moment balances the centres of the three intervals; it is not enough merely to balance their signed areas.
This η is a normalized analyzing function, with no orthonormal basis assertion. Indeed, its unit translate has nonzero L2 inner product:
∫R​η(u)η(u−1)du=6(−2)(1)+(1)(−2)​=−32​.
(38)
Hence these translates already fail orthogonality. The Daubechies wavelet construction imposes additional refinement and orthogonality requirements; two vanishing moments and unit L2 norm by themselves do not supply those requirements.
