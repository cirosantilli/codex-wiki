Twin paradox with finite acceleration

 ... Physics Branches of physics Special relativity Lorentz factor Time dilation Twin paradox
OurBigBook.com Words: 395 Articles: 1
An instantaneous reversal is an idealization. A real traveller changes velocity over a finite time, follows a smooth world line, and has finite proper acceleration. Special relativity still calculates the elapsed proper time using the home inertial frame and the same integral; general relativity is not needed merely because the traveller accelerates in otherwise flat spacetime. The clock hypothesis identifies an ideal clock's reading with this geometric proper time.
One explicit smooth trip between the same O and R starts and ends at rest in the home inertial frame. Choose

Its velocity reverses continuously at T/2, and its proper acceleration is bounded because vmax​<c and dv/dt is finite. With vmax​=0.6c and T=10yr, numerical integration gives
ΔτT​=∫010yr​1−0.36sin2(10yr2πt​)​dt≃9.028yr.

This comparison fixes the reunion spacetime events and maximum speed, not the turnaround distance: the smooth trip reaches vmax​T/π≃1.910 light-years, whereas the ideal trip reaches 3 light-years. The smooth traveller spends less time at high speed and therefore accumulates more proper time than the ideal traveller, while still accumulating less than the home clock.
For a sufficiently short smooth reversal replacing the corner, the proper time approaches the piecewise inertial result. A reversal interval of home duration δt contributes between zero and δt to the clock reading; shrinking that interval cannot create a finite extra loss concentrated at the corner. The finite difference survives in the long coasting legs. Keeping the same reversal manoeuvre while lengthening those legs changes the age difference. Conversely, two travellers following mirrored paths with the same speed history accumulate equal proper times, even though both accelerate. Thus acceleration distinguishes paths and enables reunion here, but an amount of acceleration alone does not determine the age difference.
The changing simultaneity assignment also becomes continuous. At a traveller spacetime event (t,x(t)) with instantaneous velocity v(t), the momentarily comoving inertial frame assigns home time tH​=t−v(t)x(t)/c2. Differentiating gives
dtdtH​​=1−c2v(t)2​−c2x(t)​dtdv​.
(16)
During the reversal at positive x, the last term advances the assigned home time rapidly. It describes the traveller's changing relativity of simultaneity, not a locally measured alteration of the home clock's ticking.
