Inverse transform sampling

 ... Mathematics Area of mathematics Probability and statistics Probability theory Probability distribution Quantile function
OurBigBook.com Words: 180 Articles: 1
If U has a uniform distribution on (0,1) and Q is the quantile function of a cumulative distribution function F, then Q(U) has the desired probability distribution. Indeed, right continuity gives
Q(p)≤x⟺p≤F(x),0<p<1,
(147)
so P(Q(U)≤x)=P(U≤F(x))=F(x). This short argument works for both jumps and smooth cumulative distribution functions. MIT's continuous-probability notes introduce the inverse-transformation construction. [ref].
For the three-value example, send U≤0.2 to 0, 0.2<U≤0.7 to 1, and U>0.7 to 3. These intervals have exactly the desired widths 0.2, 0.5, 0.3. For the density example, output U​: for 0≤y≤1, P(U​≤y)=P(U≤y2)=y2. Independent uniform draws give independent random variables after this deterministic transformation. This direction of construction works even when F has jumps; applying the probability integral transform in the other direction requires its own continuity hypotheses.
