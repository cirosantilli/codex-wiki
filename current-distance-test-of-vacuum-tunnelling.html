Current-distance test of vacuum tunnelling

 Home Physics Branches of physics Quantum mechanics Potential barrier Quantum tunnelling
OurBigBook.com Words: 717 Articles: 10
Tunneling through a controllable vacuum gap by Gerd Binnig and others made barrier width experimentally adjustable. Their apparatus used a pointed electrical conductor opposite a conducting plate, with fine positioning by piezoelectricity. Rubber-tube support and magnetic levitation above a cooled bowl exhibiting superconductivity suppressed vibrations; the junction itself remained near room temperature.[ref]
The readout was electrical resistance versus displacement at a fixed electric potential difference of 60 mV of either polarity. They observed an exponential function dependence spanning four orders of magnitude. Cleaning and better vacuum increased the inferred barrier from approximately 0.6-0.7 eV to 3.2 eV. Reproducible curves and the cleaning response argued against deforming contamination layers; an appropriate slope also helped exclude an ordinary conducting contact. The paper reports residual contamination, electronics-limited range and a jump attributed to release of thermal stress; its curve origins generally do not determine absolute gap widths.[ref]
The ideal tunnel junction model explains the slope. At fixed small electric potential difference, fixed tip geometry and approximately unchanged density of states, take an effective barrier energy Φ above the relevant electron states and approximate the current magnitude by
I(s)≃Cexp(−2κs),κ=ℏ2me​Φ​​,−dsdlnI​≃2κ.
(80)
The coefficient C includes the applied electric potential difference, available occupied and empty quantum states, and tip geometry. A single transmission probability is not an electric current: a junction combines contributions from many electron states in both directions, with the electric potential difference producing a net imbalance. Its tilted, material-dependent barrier is only approximated by the equal-exterior rectangular potential barrier; the exact single-particle formula above is not a complete junction model.
For the chosen model value Φ=4 electronvolts, κ≃10.25nm−1. Increasing separation by 0.1nm multiplies I by about 0.129, a reduction by about 7.8. The following diagram chooses I(0.4nm)=1nA solely for illustration; it contains no measured data and is not a fit to the 1982 curves.
Distance calibration is essential. In Scanning Tunneling Microscopy: From Birth to Adolescence by Gerd Binnig and others, the authors describe an initial inferred barrier near 1 eV. Recalibration found only half the quoted piezo displacement sensitivity and raised the inferred barrier above 4 eV. Thus an exponential trace alone was insufficient evidence for tunnelling through vacuum. The model makes the sensitivity explicit: the inferred Φ=ℏ2(dlnI/ds)2/(8me​) depends quadratically on the distance scale.[ref]
For scanning tunnelling microscopy, feedback control adjusts the vertical position to hold electric current fixed during a lateral scan; the recorded height follows separation only when the surface is electronically homogeneous. Changes in the local density of states can also change apparent height. This limitation is already explicit in the authors' account of constant-current imaging.[ref]
