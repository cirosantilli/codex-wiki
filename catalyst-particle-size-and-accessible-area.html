Catalyst particle size and accessible area (S=3Vs​/r)

 Home Chemistry Chemical reaction Chemical kinetics Catalysis Heterogeneous catalysis
OurBigBook.com Words: 300
Consider an original geometric model of N separate nonporous solid spheres, each with radius r. Hold their total volume Vs​ fixed. The sphere formulas give
Vs​=N34πr3​,S=N4πr2=r3Vs​​.
(28)
At fixed mass density, fixed Vs​ also means fixed total mass. Halving r requires eight times as many particles and doubles their total external surface area S; this follows by eliminating N, without any assumption about chemical kinetics.
For example, take one sphere of radius 1.00mm, so Vs​=4π/3mm3. Eight spheres of radius 0.50mm have the same Vs​. Their total surface areas are respectively 4πmm2 and 8πmm2, approximately 12.57 and 25.13mm2. Sixty-four particles of radius 0.25mm would instead give 16πmm2, four times the original area.
To obtain a kinetic prediction, add assumptions explicitly. Let f be the accessible fraction of external surface area, σ the amount of substance of catalytic surface sites per unit accessible area, and ν the mean number of completed conversions per site per unit time under the chosen conditions. Then the model's reaction product formation rate is
dtdnB​​=νσfS=r3νσfVs​​.
(29)
Here ν has units s−1 and σ has units molm−2, giving mols−1 overall. Halving r doubles this predicted rate only if f, σ and ν remain unchanged. Particle contact can hide area; pores can add area; diffusion, altered surface structure and catalyst deactivation can change the other factors. Consequently, twice the calculated external area is not a universal promise of twice the measured reaction rate. The geometric argument changes contact, not the net Gibbs free energy or the equilibrium constant of the same overall chemical reaction.
