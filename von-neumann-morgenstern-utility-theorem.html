Von Neumann-Morgenstern utility theorem (U(L)=∑ω​L(ω)u(ω))

 ... Mathematics Area of mathematics Mathematical optimization Mathematical finance Utility function Expected utility hypothesis
OurBigBook.com Words: 113
For a finite outcome space, complete lottery preferences, a transitive relation, continuity of lottery preferences and the independence axiom for lottery preferences are equivalent to an expected utility representation of the displayed form. The representing outcome utility function is unique up to a positive affine function. The theorem is associated with John von Neumann and Oskar Morgenstern.
A useful proof construction selects a best outcome a and a worst outcome b. If all outcomes are indifferent, a constant utility function suffices. Otherwise assign u(a)=1, u(b)=0. Continuity of lottery preferences makes each outcome indifferent to a mixture u(ω)a+(1−u(ω))b, and mixture monotonicity makes the coefficient unique. Substitution of indifferent lotteries and reduction of compound lotteries turn any lottery into a best–worst mixture with best-outcome probability ∑ω​L(ω)u(ω). Mixture monotonicity then ranks all such mixtures. The reverse direction follows by taking expected values; positive affine functions preserve every comparison.[ref]
