The numbered features in the original colour-magnitude diagram have the following interpretation.
  • 1: the main sequence, where core hydrogen fusion supplies the luminosity; stars span a range of masses and effective temperatures.
  • 2: the subgiant region, following core hydrogen exhaustion, with a contracting helium-rich core and increasing hydrogen-shell burning.
  • 3: the red giant branch, with an extended cool envelope and a compact helium core fed by hydrogen-shell burning.
  • 4: the roughly horizontal core-helium-burning branch, or its red-clump part, reached after helium ignition; hydrogen-shell burning can continue outside the core.
  • 5: the white-dwarf cooling sequence, consisting of compact remnants supported mainly by electron degeneracy pressure and radiating stored heat.
Continuous star formation explains the coexistence of newly formed massive main-sequence stars with evolved low-mass remnants; interpreting a single sharp turnoff as the age of a coeval cluster would be inappropriate here.
