A pumped-storage plant moves water uphill using electrical power, then returns it through a turbine and generator when electricity is needed. The upper and lower reservoirs set the usable volume and head; pumps, penstocks, valves and reversible pump-turbines set the power and flow. The plant stores energy rather than generating net energy over a cycle.
For constant head, the gravitational energy is Eg​=ρVgh. With the stated generation efficiency,
Eusable​=0.89(1000)(1.4×107)(9.81)(400)≃4.89×1013J=13.6GWh.​
(49)
At 3 GW, P=ηρgQh gives
Q≃8.59×102m3s−1,tfull​=Eusable​/P≃4.53h.​
(50)
Head variation, dead volume and hydraulic losses beyond the stipulated efficiency have been neglected.
To deliver 9 kWh with the same head and efficiency needs V≃9.3m3 of water, roughly a 2.1 m cube, together with a 400 m elevation difference and a lower reservoir. At a 10 m head it would need about 370m3. This contrasts with the small fuel volume: the useful energy density of lifted water depends on its external head, not its chemical composition.
Pumped storage offers long service life, fast dispatch, substantial power, and separate choices of storage volume and turbine rating. Its drawbacks include geographic constraints, construction cost and time, ecological impacts, evaporation and water requirements, plus round-trip losses. The quoted generation efficiency alone is not the round-trip efficiency because pumping also consumes more than the stored potential energy.
The rock cylinder has mass ρr​πr2(2r) and rises through r. Equating its useful energy to the reservoir result cancels the common efficiency and gravity:
2πρr​r4=ρw​Vh,r=[2π(3000)1000(1.4×107)(400)​]1/4≃131m.​
(51)
Its height is about 262 m and its lift about 131 m, ignoring buoyancy as instructed. Stored energy scales as r4, excavated volume roughly as r3, and lined area as r2. If excavation dominates cost, cost per unit stored energy decreases approximately as r−1. Seals, increasing pressure, geology, leakage, supporting structure and power equipment can change that scaling; a volume law is not a complete cost model.
