At room temperature, electrons require quantum mechanics, including their occupied orbitals or bands and Fermi statistics. Heavy nuclei can often be treated as distinguishable particles near classical equilibrium lattice sites, because their exchange wavelengths are short. Their coupled displacements form phonons, however, and the occupation of a vibrational mode is classical only if kB​T≫ℏω for that mode. Even at room temperature some high-frequency modes remain quantum mechanical.
At 1 K, quantum lattice vibrations, zero-point motion and the scarcity of thermally excited phonons are essential. Many heavy nuclei still do not have overlapping exchange wavefunctions; quantum vibration and quantum exchange are distinct criteria. For the same spacing, the nuclear overlap threshold from the rough estimate is TN​≃211/A K, illustrating why the nuclear mass cannot be omitted from a blanket low-temperature claim.
