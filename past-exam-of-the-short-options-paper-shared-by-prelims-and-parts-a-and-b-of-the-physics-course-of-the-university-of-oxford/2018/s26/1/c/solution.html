The radiation pressure at a photospheric temperature near 5800 K is only about 0.3 Pa. For an illustrative photospheric gas density 3×10−4kgm−3 and mean molecular weight near 1.3, the ideal gas pressure is about 104 Pa. Thus gas pressure dominates those outer layers by many orders of magnitude. Both the temperature and density are needed: low temperature alone is not a complete comparison.
For homologous stars, hydrostatic and ideal-gas estimates give T∼μmH​GM/(kB​R) and ρ∼M/R3. Therefore
Pgas​Prad​​=3ρkB​aμmH​T3​∝μ4M2​,
(158)
with a structural coefficient independent of mass only under the homology assumption. As mass rises, radiation becomes increasingly important. A rough solar interior normalization of 10−3-10−2 suggests equality after a mass increase by tens, while realistic radiation-dominated structure and composition shift the scale; stars of order 102M⊙​ are a useful estimate for strong radiation support. The very small photospheric ratio must not be used as a universal normalization for their whole interiors.
Radiation also transfers outward momentum. Comparing radiative acceleration κL/(4πr2c) with gravity gives the Eddington luminosity
LEdd​=κ4πcGM​.​
(159)
Near this scale, radiatively driven winds and envelope instabilities can remove matter or impede further accretion. Opacity, composition, rotation, mass loss and formation history affect the limiting stellar mass. The Eddington condition is a luminosity-to-mass constraint, not a unique composition-independent maximum stellar mass.
