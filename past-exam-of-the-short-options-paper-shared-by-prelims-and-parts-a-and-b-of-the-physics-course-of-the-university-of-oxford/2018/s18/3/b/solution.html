In two spatial dimensions there are only two kinetic matrices and one mass matrix. The three Pauli matrices square to identity and anticommute pairwise, so
H2+1​=cσx​px​+cσy​py​+mc2σz​​
(128)
is a two-component Dirac Hamiltonian with H2+12​=c2(px2​+py2​)+m2c4. It therefore reproduces the stated first-order equation and its Klein-Gordon square. Requiring a fourth anticommuting matrix in three spatial dimensions is precisely what prevented this two-component construction above.
Printed angular-momentum qualification. Define the dimensionless spin si​=−iεijk​αj​αk​/4 and the physical orbital angular momentum ℓi​=εijk​xj​pk​, with [xi​,pj​]=iℏδij​. Then
[si​,sj​]=iεijk​sk​,[ℓi​,ℓj​]=iℏεijk​ℓk​,[ℓi​,sj​]=0.
(129)
The source prints Li​=iℓi​ instead. Taken literally, it is anti-Hermitian, has incompatible units with the dimensionless spin when they are added, and gives
[Li​,Lj​]=−ℏεijk​Lk​,[Ji​,Jj​]=εijk​(−ℏLk​+isk​).​
(130)
It cannot yield the requested usual angular-momentum algebra or conservation law. For a concrete counterexample set ℏ=1 and take a wave with momentum along z. Since
[HD​,ℓi​]=−ic(α×p)i​,[HD​,si​]=ic(α×p)i​,
(131)
the printed Ji​=iℓi​+si​ obeys [HD​,Jx​]=(1+i)cαy​pz​=0.
The physically consistent operator is Jphys​=ℓ+ℏs. It satisfies
[Jphys,i​,Jphys,j​]=iℏεijk​Jphys,k​,[HD​,Jphys,i​]=0.​
(132)
The cancellation follows directly from the two mixed commutators above. In a spherical basis the free spinor can be labelled by energy, j=1/2,3/2,…, and mj​=−j,…,j, together with a parity or equivalent Dirac angular label distinguishing the two orbital couplings ℓ=j±1/2. Alternatively one can choose a momentum and helicity basis. One cannot simultaneously assign all components of momentum and all angular labels as independent commuting observables.
