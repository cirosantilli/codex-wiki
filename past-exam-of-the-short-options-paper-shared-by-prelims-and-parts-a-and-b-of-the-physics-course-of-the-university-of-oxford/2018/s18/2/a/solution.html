Let x<​=min(x,x′) and x>​=max(x,x′). A homogeneous solution vanishing at the left wall is u(x)=sin[k(x+a/2)]; one vanishing at the right wall is v(x)=sin[k(a/2−x)]. Their Wronskian is uv′−u′v=−ksinka. Continuity and the source derivative jump G+′​−G−′​=−2m/ℏ2 therefore give
G(x,x′)=kℏ2sinka2m​sin[k(x<​+a/2)]sin[k(a/2−x>​)].​
(112)
This is exactly the printed form because x<​=(x+x′−∣x−x′∣)/2 and x>​=(x+x′+∣x−x′∣)/2, with a minus sign from reversing the argument of the second sine.
The true poles occur at kn​=nπ/a, n=1,2,…, corresponding to
En​=2ma2ℏ2π2n2​.​
(113)
The apparent zero at k=0 is removable: the numerator and ksinka both vanish quadratically. Negative values of kn​ duplicate the same energies, rather than describing extra levels. The boundary conditions and source jump uniquely determine the inverse whenever E is not an eigenvalue.
