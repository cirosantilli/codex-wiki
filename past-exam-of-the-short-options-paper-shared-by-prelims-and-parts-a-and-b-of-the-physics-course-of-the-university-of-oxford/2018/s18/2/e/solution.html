Multiply the expression for ψ by F∗(x) and integrate. Cancelling the nonzero C gives
1=κℏ2αm​∫∫F∗(x)F(y)e−κ∣x−y∣dxdy.
(120)
The kernel is real and symmetric, so relabelling the integration variables puts the expression in the printed order:
κ=ℏ2αm​∫∫F(x)F∗(y)e−κ∣x−y∣dxdy,E=−2mℏ2κ2​,κ>0.​
(121)
This is the scalar consistency condition for the rank-one potential, rather than an independent normalization equation. The double integral is real and nonnegative because the Fourier transform of e−κ∣x∣ is 2κ/(q2+κ2)>0. Thus attractive α>0 is needed for a nontrivial solution. Under the usual integrability assumptions the corresponding free resolvent expectation decreases with κ, giving at most one bound state for this rank-one attraction.
