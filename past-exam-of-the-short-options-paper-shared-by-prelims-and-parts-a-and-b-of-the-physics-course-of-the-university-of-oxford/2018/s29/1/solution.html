Hydrostatic equilibrium means that the local pressure gradient balances gravity, with no large acceleration or strength-supported departure from that balance. In a spherical planet,

Limitations include uncertain seismic coverage, temperature and composition tradeoffs, nonadiabatic boundary layers, phase changes, lateral heterogeneity, rotation and finite material strength. Small asteroids and comet nuclei commonly retain irregular shapes because their self-gravity is too weak to overcome strength and impact history; a few larger bodies, such as Ceres, are much closer to global hydrostatic figures. A spherical shape alone is not a complete proof of present hydrostatic equilibrium.
Two complementary remote constraints are precise spacecraft tracking, which measures gravitational harmonics and constrains mass distribution when combined with shape and rotation; and tidal or rotational measurements, such as Love numbers, librations and spin precession, which constrain deformability, moment of inertia and possible fluid layers. Magnetic induction measurements in a time-varying external field can provide an additional conductivity constraint, but are not a unique density inversion.
