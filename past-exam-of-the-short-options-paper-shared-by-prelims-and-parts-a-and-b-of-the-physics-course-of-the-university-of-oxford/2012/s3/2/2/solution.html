A classical orbiting electron accelerates and therefore radiates according to the Larmor formula. Its mechanical energy becomes more negative, its radius decreases, and the orbit spirals inward rather than remaining a stable atom. The Bohr model replaces this prediction with postulated nonradiating stationary states and quantized orbital angular momentum, together with photon emission or absorption only between states, hν=∣En​−Em​∣.
For a heavy nucleus, me​vr=nℏ. Since the de Broglie wavelength is h/(me​v), this implies

The electronic wave fits an integer number of wavelengths around the orbit. This is a standing-wave consistency interpretation of the quantization rule, not a classical mechanism that prevents an accelerating charge from radiating.
