Let t=0 now denote the year 2000 and C00​=336.95ppmv. When emissions stop, the model gives
C(t)=C0​+(C00​−C0​)e−t/(200yr).​
(207)
The concentration peaks at the switch, then returns exponentially to 279ppmv; in 2500 it is about 283.76ppmv. Before 2000 both scenarios share the exponential-emissions trajectory.
Using the specified logarithmic equilibrium warming relative to C0​,
ΔTeq​(C)=3log2​(C/C0​)K,
(208)
gives the fast-adjustment peak estimate
ΔTpeak​≈3log2​(336.95/279)≃0.82K.​
(209)
An unspecified finite thermal response time below twenty years delays and reduces that peak relative to the instantaneous-equilibrium value; its exact amount cannot be fixed without a response law and a chosen thermal time. The carbon-removal time is much longer, so the warming subsequently declines gradually in this model. Taking the 1800 concentration 280 as the temperature reference instead changes the quoted estimate by only 0.016K.
