Use the optical-depth weighting stipulated in the paper. A jump in the Planck function at τ1​ gives dB/dτ=(B1​−B0​)δ(τ−τ1​), so
I0​=π[B0​+(B1​−B0​)e−τ1​]=πB0​(1−e−τ1​)+πB1​e−τ1​.​
(184)
The upper isothermal layer emits πB0​(1−e−τ1​) and transmits the lower region's blackbody spectral irradiance with factor e−τ1​. The lower layer and black surface have the same B1​, so their combined radiation entering the upper layer is πB1​, independent of how its emission is divided between the lower gas and surface.
More generally, integration by parts rewrites the supplied expression as
I0​=πB(τs​)e−τs​+π∫0τs​​B(τ)e−τdτ.
(185)
The terms are attenuated surface emission and the atmosphere's own emission. In the original boundary-plus-gradient form, πB0​ is the isothermal reference flux and the integral corrects for temperature variation below the top. Optical depths of order unity contribute most strongly. These formulas follow the paper's specified exponential weighting; a full angular radiative transfer calculation uses ray-angle-dependent optical paths rather than identifying every ray with a vertical path.
