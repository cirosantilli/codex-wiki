The series is predictable in a statistical sense beyond linear return autocorrelation. For example, the observed past feature Zt​=Xt−1​Xt−2​ satisfies E[Xt​Zt​]=1 and EZt2​=4, so its least-squares predictor within the family bZt​ is Xt​=Zt​/4​. This is a predictor based on observed increments, not an assumption that the hidden driving signs are directly visible; it need not equal the full conditional expectation.
A finite bound on ordinary peak-to-trough financial drawdown does not follow. Choose a long alternating sequence of driving signs, at​=(−1)t. It produces Xt​=−1+(−1)t, alternating −2 and 0. The price never recovers during that episode and its cumulative loss grows without bound as the episode length grows. Every finite such driving sequence has positive probability. Thus no finite worst drawdown magnitude or duration exists without a horizon bound.​
More precisely, over an N-month window and over admissible starting histories, the maximum possible drawdown is
Dmax​(N)=2⌊N/2⌋+2(N≥1).​
(96)
One derivation keeps the last two signs as a four-state dynamic program. Let dn​(a,b) be the smallest cumulative increment after n steps ending with signs (a,b), with d0​(a,b)=0 for each permitted initial pair. Then dn+1​(b,c)=mina=±1​[dn​(a,b)+ab+c]. Induction gives the negative of the displayed minimum. The bound is attained by increments (−2,0)k,−2 for N=2k+1, or (−2,0)k−1,−2,−2 for N=2k, so it is also an attained peak-to-trough loss over N months, not only an algebraic net displacement bound.
For comparison, the index with increments at​=±1 has Dmax​(N)=N, attained by N successive negative signs; it too has no finite horizon-free bound. If “drawdown” were intended to mean a strictly decreasing consecutive run, the first model permits at most two successive −2 increments, for a loss of four over two months, whereas the simple walk permits arbitrarily long such runs. That different run statistic must not be substituted for the usual drawdown definition: zero increments can separate continuing losses without any recovery.
