Let S1​(t) be the probability that an ATP-waiting state has not yet bound a substrate molecule. A constant bimolecular binding coefficient k1​ at fixed ATP concentration c gives a pseudo-first-order hazard λ=k1​c. Thus S˙1​=−λS1​, S1​(0)=1, and the waiting-time distribution is
P1​(t)=−S˙1​(t)=k1​ce−k1​ct,t≥0,⟨t1​⟩=k1​c1​.​
(107)
The probability density function starts at k1​c and decreases exponentially. Increasing c shortens the waiting time in inverse proportion. This supports one rate-limiting ATP-binding event before the associated motion, rather than a concentration-independent chemical delay.
For the second delay, consider sequential independent first-order transitions A⟶ka​B⟶kb​C. Their dwell times have exponential distributions. The total waiting time is their sum, so convolution gives
P2​(t)=∫0t​ka​e−ka​skb​e−kb​(t−s)ds=ka​−kb​ka​kb​​(e−kb​t−e−ka​t),t≥0.​
(108)
It is normalized, symmetric under exchange of ka​,kb​, and has
⟨t2​⟩=ka​1​+kb​1​,Var(t2​)=ka2​1​+kb2​1​.​
(109)
Unlike one exponential delay, it vanishes at t=0, rises initially as ka​kb​t, reaches a maximum at tmode​=log(ka​/kb​)/(ka​−kb​) and eventually decays at the slower rate. For equal rates the continuous limit is P2​(t)=k2te−kt, an Erlang distribution with mode 1/k. If one rate is much faster than the other, the observed long-time behavior approaches one exponential. The sketch in the preceding section displays these shapes using arbitrary specified rates.
The simplest catalytic cycle compatible with the fits therefore contains an ATP-dependent binding delay and at least two resolvable ATP-concentration-independent kinetic events. Hydrolysis, product release and conformational changes are possible microscopic assignments, but the two-rate fit alone does not uniquely identify them or the order of ka​ and kb​. If each complete 120∘ cycle contains these three waits and step durations are negligible, its mean duration is (k1​c)−1+ka−1​+kb−1​ and the mean revolution rate is the inverse of three times this duration. Multiple catalytic sites can cooperate during that cycle.
