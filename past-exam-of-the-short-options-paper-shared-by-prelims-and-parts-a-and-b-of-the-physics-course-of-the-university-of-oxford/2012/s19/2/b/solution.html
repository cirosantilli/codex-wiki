Without replenishment, the electron's momentum decreases. The reference dipoles then bend it more tightly, producing a displaced orbit. In linear beam optics, x=Dx​δ and y=Dy​δ, where δ=Δp/p≃−U0​/E≃−1.11×10−3 per uncompensated turn. In an ideal planar ring the horizontal dispersion is nonzero and the orbit shifts horizontally, inward where Dx​>0. There is no corresponding vertical closed-orbit shift when Dy​=0. Real coupling or vertical dispersion can change that idealization.
With current I=0.500 A, the number of circulating particles is N=ITrev​/e, so the radiated power is
P=Trev​NU0​​=eIU0​​≃2.76MW.​
(177)
The 2μs revolution time cancels when using beam current. Radio-frequency cavities restore the lost energy longitudinally, with synchronous gain eVRF​sinφs​=U0​ in the sine-phase convention. Their RF power system must supply at least this beam power, in addition to its own losses. Radiation damping and quantum excitation determine beam emittances; they should not be confused with the dispersive closed-orbit shift from a momentum change.
