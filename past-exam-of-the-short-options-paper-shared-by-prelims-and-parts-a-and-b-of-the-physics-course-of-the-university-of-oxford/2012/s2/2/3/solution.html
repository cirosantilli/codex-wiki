A Hertzsprung-Russell diagram plots stellar luminosity upward and effective temperature increasing toward the left. The main sequence runs from hot luminous stars at upper left to cool faint stars at lower right, with the Sun near Teff​≃5800K, L=L⊙​.
In the evolution of a solar-mass star, core hydrogen fusion initially supplies the luminosity. As core hydrogen is exhausted, hydrogen-shell fusion around a contracting helium core carries the star through the subgiant and red giant phases toward higher luminosity and lower surface temperature. Helium ignition is followed by core helium fusion with a hydrogen-burning shell, placing a solar-mass star in the red clump. The later Asymptotic giant branch has hydrogen- and helium-burning shells around a carbon-oxygen core. Envelope ejection exposes a hot central star of a planetary nebula; residual shell burning ends and the resulting white dwarf radiates stored thermal energy as it cools.
