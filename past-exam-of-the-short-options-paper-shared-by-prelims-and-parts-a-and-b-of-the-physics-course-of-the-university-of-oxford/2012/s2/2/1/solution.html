Build the star by adding spherical shells. With m(r) the enclosed mass, its gravitational binding energy is
Ω=−∫0M⊙​​r(m)Gm​dm.
(15)
For a nondegenerate extended star, r(m)<R⊙​ on the interior, so
∣Ω∣>R⊙​G​∫0M⊙​​mdm=2R⊙​GM⊙2​​.​
(16)
This is a gravitational binding-energy lower bound for a spherical star. The Kelvin-Helmholtz cooling time is of order
τKH​∼R⊙​L⊙​GM⊙2​​≃3.1×107yr.​
(17)
The displayed energy bound gives ∣Ω∣/L⊙​>1.6×107 years; it supplies a lower bound on that reservoir-to-luminosity ratio. A stellar structure model sets its coefficient. For an ideal monatomic, gas-supported star the virial theorem puts about half the released gravitational energy into heat, leaving half available for radiation; a uniform-density model would give about 9×106 years. These contraction estimates are all of order 107 years, much shorter than the nuclear lifetime.
