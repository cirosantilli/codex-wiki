Compared with the supplied composition curves, this is inconsistent with a bare iron-rich or ordinary rock-only planet. A volatile-rich interior and/or an extended low-density gas envelope is needed. A water-rich body with an atmosphere and a rocky core with a modest H/He envelope are different plausible families; mass and radius do not uniquely determine the composition.[ref]

A spectral feature spanning N scale heights changes the blocked stellar fraction by the atmospheric annulus area:
Δδ≃R∗2​2Rp​NH​≃3.38×10−4N.​
(187)
Thus one scale height is about 338 parts per million in transit depth; a clear strong feature spanning roughly five scale heights is of order 1.7×10−3. The number of scale heights requires the actual opacity ratio; N≃ln(κline​/κcont​) is a useful isothermal estimate, not a supplied measured value. The true terminator temperature need not equal this redistribution estimate.
A nearly flat spectrum can result from a high-mean-molecular-mass atmosphere with a much smaller scale height, or from optically thick clouds/hazes that hide the deeper absorption features. These are the two qualitative possibilities; detailed precision data can discriminate them. In particular, the pre-2015 HST study favored clouds over the clear high-molecular-mass models it tested, so listing both physical mechanisms is not a claim that all such models fit those data equally well.[ref]
