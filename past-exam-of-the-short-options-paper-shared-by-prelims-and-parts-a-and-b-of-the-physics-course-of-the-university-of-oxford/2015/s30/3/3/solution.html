The circumstellar habitable zone is the range of stellar irradiation at which a suitable rocky planet could maintain liquid water at its surface under specified atmospheric conditions. It is not a guarantee of life or even of liquid water on every planet at that distance. Its boundaries depend on greenhouse gases, clouds, humidity, pressure, albedo, rotation and the stellar spectrum; stellar evolution, ultraviolet activity, atmospheric loss and tidal locking can complicate applications to cool stars.

For reflected light the reflected-light planetary phase curve gives the flux ratio ϵ=Ag​Φ(α)(R⊕​/aE​)2, where Ag​ is geometric albedo and Φ(0)=1. Numerically,
ϵfull​≃5.66×10−7Ag​.​

A unit-geometric-albedo reference gives about 6×10−7; an Earth-like chosen Ag​=0.3 gives 1.7×10−7. Albedo and phase function were not specified, so an exact contrast cannot be inferred just from the radius. Full-phase brightness occurs close to zero projected separation for an edge-on orbit; at the best-resolved quadrature, a Lambert sphere has Φ(π/2)=1/π, reducing the Earth-albedo example to 5.4×10−8. Thus the best angular separation and maximum reflected contrast are not generally achieved at the same phase.
