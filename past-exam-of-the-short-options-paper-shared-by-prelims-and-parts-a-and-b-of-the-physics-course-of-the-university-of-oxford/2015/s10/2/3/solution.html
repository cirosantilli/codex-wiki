Use the linear-quadratic radiobiology model, independent fractions with complete inter-fraction repair, and no tumour repopulation. For N fractions of dose d, the logarithmic cell kill is N(αd+βd2). Dividing by α defines the biologically effective dose,
BED=Nd(1+α/βd​).
(62)
For the tumour, the original schedule gives 26(2)(1+2/10)=62.4Gy10​​. A 3Gy fraction contributes 3(1+3/10)=3.9Gy10​, so precisely 62.4/3.9=16​ such fractions give the same tumour biologically effective dose. The physical total dose changes from 52 to 48Gy.
For the specified normal-tissue α/β=3Gy, the original and altered schedules give
BEDold​=52(1+32​)=86.7Gy3​,BEDnew​=48(1+33​)=96.0Gy3​​.
(63)
Thus the normal-tissue model predicts an increase of 9.33Gy3​, or about 10.8%, despite the smaller total physical dose. The smaller normal-tissue α/β makes increasing fraction size relatively more costly. These are comparisons within the source's hypothetical radiobiological model and equal-dose geometry, not individualized treatment prescriptions.[ref]
