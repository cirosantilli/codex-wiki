The quadratic coefficient must have units Wm−2K−2; the printed K−1 makes its term dimensionally incompatible with a radiative flux. Interpret its numerical value as λ2​=0.05Wm−2K−2, with λ1​=1.5Wm−2K−1. The total imbalance is
N=Fext​−λ1​ΔT+λ2​ΔT2.
(143)
A quadrupling gives Fext​=2(3.75)=7.5Wm−2. The two equilibrium roots are
ΔT±​=2λ2​λ1​±λ12​−4λ2​Fext​​​,ΔT−​=6.34K,ΔT+​=23.66K.​
(144)
For positive heat capacity, stability requires ∂N/∂T=−λ1​+2λ2​ΔT<0. Thus only the lower root is stable. A saddle-node bifurcation occurs at
Fcrit​=4λ2​λ12​​=11.25Wm−2,ΔTcrit​=2λ2​λ1​​=15K.​
(145)
The logarithmic forcing relation gives Ccrit​=280211.25/3.75=2240ppm. Strict linear stability holds below this concentration on the lower branch; the equality is marginal and is the supremum, not another strictly stable equilibrium. Sustained forcing above it causes warming without a balancing equilibrium in this quadratic model. At such large changes its local feedback expansion can fail, and other physics can intervene; it does not prove unlimited real-planet warming or locate a measured Earth runaway threshold.
