Take the logarithmic forcing law with its normalization fixed by the given doubling value. Let x be the positive magnitude of cooling and f the positive magnitude of the earlier negative forcing:
f=ln23.75​lnC280​,x=1.0K.
(138)
At C=212ppm, f0​=1.5051Wm−2. Equilibrium then yields
λ0​=f0​/x0​=1.505Wm−2K−1,ΔT2×,0​=3.75f0​x0​​=2.492K.​
(139)
The concentration endpoints 185 and 244ppm imply forcing magnitudes 2.242 and 0.7445Wm−2, approximately f0​±0.75. Define normalized variables u=f/f0​ and v=x/x0​. Independent approximately Gaussian uncertainties give the one-standard-deviation covariance ellipse
0.502(u−1)2​+0.502(v−1)2​≃1.​
(140)
Its centre is (1,1) and its principal directions are the two coordinate axes. Warming is 2.492(v/u)K, so rays from the origin have constant inferred warming. The bounding rays tangent to this ellipse have slopes m satisfying (1−m)2=41​(1+m2), or
m−​≃0.4514,m+​≃2.2153.
(141)
This gives the useful asymmetric one-parameter profile estimate
ΔT2×​≃2.5K,approximate 1σ interval 1.1–5.5K.​
(142)
The concentration data supply only marginal intervals, not a full likelihood. This result explicitly approximates their logarithmic forcing uncertainty as Gaussian. Linear error propagation would instead give fractional uncertainty 0.52+0.52​=0.707, or roughly 2.49±1.76K, and misses the large reciprocal asymmetry. A unit-radius two-dimensional covariance ellipse itself contains about 39% of a bivariate Gaussian, not 68%; the quoted interval uses the one-parameter Δχ2=1 profile/ratio construction, rather than claiming that ellipse is a 68% joint region.
