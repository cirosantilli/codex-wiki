Assume a well-mixed ideal gas with constant g,cp​, no latent heat, and sufficiently strong surface heating to maintain dry convection near the surface. An adiabatically displaced unit mass has specific volume V=1/ρ, so the stated relation and hydrostatic equilibrium give
cp​dT=Vdp=−gdz,Γd​=−dzdT​=cp​g​≃9.8Kkm−1.​
(131)
This is the dry neutral lapse rate; infrared absorption alone does not guarantee that a radiative profile is steep enough to trigger convection. Combining the same equations with the ideal gas law yields T∝pRa​/cp​ along that adiabat.
High above the main infrared-emitting region, optical depth to space becomes small. A transparent-to-sunlight atmosphere has no substantial direct solar heating there; a thin grey layer absorbs the upwelling planetary infrared flux Q=(1−α)S0​/4 and emits from both faces. In the optically thin radiative-equilibrium limit,
σTskin4​≃Q/2,Tskin​≃[8σ(1−α)S0​​]1/4.​
(132)
The factor four in Q is projected-area versus whole-sphere averaging; the additional factor two counts emission upward and downward by the tenuous layer. For Earth-like S0​≃1360Wm−2 and α≃0.30, this is about 214K. Thus temperature approaches a height-independent skin value as τ→0, rather than continuing the dry adiabat to zero. This is an asymptotic grey-atmosphere approximation, not a proof that an entire finite stratosphere is exactly isothermal.
