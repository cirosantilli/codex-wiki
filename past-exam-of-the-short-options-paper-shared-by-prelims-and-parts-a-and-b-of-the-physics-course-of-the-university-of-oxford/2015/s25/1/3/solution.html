Let q be the mass mixing ratio of the uniformly mixed infrared absorbers, and let k be their constant mass absorption coefficient. Hydrostatic integration gives downward optical depth τ=kqp/g. The useful fixed-tropopause-optical-depth approximation requires a sufficiently optically thick lower column, unchanged absorbed sunlight and upper radiative profile in optical-depth coordinates, and the same convective lapse law after the perturbation. Then the matching to the radiative region occurs at the same τt​ and temperature Tt​. Doubling all those absorbers gives
pt′​≃pt​/2.​
(133)
Assume also that the surface pressure is unchanged. A dry adiabat satisfies Tt​/Ts​=(pt​/ps​)κ with κ=Ra​/cp​=0.287. Consequently
Ts​Ts′​​=20.287=1.2201,ΔTs​≃0.2201Ts​≃63K​
(134)
for an initial Earth-like surface temperature 288K. Its first-order logarithmic estimate, ΔTs​≃κTs​ln2, is about 57K; the finite-doubling formula is preferable because the perturbation is large. This estimate is conditional on fixed τt​,Tt​ and dry convective adjustment, not on carbon dioxide's measured spectral behavior.
There is also a consistency limitation to the most literal opacity model. A self-contained plane-parallel grey atmosphere with constant mass opacity has T4∝τ+2/3 and τ∝p. Its radiative logarithmic gradient is
∇rad​=dlnpdlnT​=4(τ+2/3)τ​<41​<κ.
(135)
It is therefore subadiabatic for these dry heat capacities, so it does not by itself establish the assumed deep dry convective column. The preceding warming is the intended fixed-matching-level, convectively prescribed estimate. A fully self-consistent model needs opacity varying with pressure, other heating structure or another closure to supply that convection; the two statements should not be silently identified.
