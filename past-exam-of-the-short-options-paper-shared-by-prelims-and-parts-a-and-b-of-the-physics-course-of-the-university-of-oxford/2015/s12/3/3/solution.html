Use the initial equal-concentration, zero-potential reference, or equivalently quote component changes relative to the initial electrochemical equilibrium. At n=n0​/2=5000, with C=10−16F,
ΔGelec​=C5000e2​=1.28×10−18J=8.01eV.​
(76)
At room temperature T=300K, the chemical component is
ΔGchem​=kB​Tln2=2.87×10−21J=0.0179eV.​
(77)
The electrical cost dominates by about 447. The associated change in membrane potential is −ne/C=−8.01V. Such a large voltage is a consequence of the ideal fixed-capacitance, perfectly impermeable model; an actual lipid membrane would not generally remain intact and nonconducting over this entire extrapolation.
The electrical component is a straight line through zero, ne2/C. The chemical component, kB​Tln[n0​/(n0​−n)], starts at zero, increases convexly, and diverges as n→n0−​ because the remaining internal concentration vanishes. Their sum lies above both: nearly electrical over most of the stated range, but eventually diverging with the chemical term. For a nonzero initial equilibrium potential, the separate absolute electrical and chemical components have opposite constant offsets; the total curve and the quoted changes remain the same. The final few ions require a discrete-particle treatment rather than a literal continuum divergence.
