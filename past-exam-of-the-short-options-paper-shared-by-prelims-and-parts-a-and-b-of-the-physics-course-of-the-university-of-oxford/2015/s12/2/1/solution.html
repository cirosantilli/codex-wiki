Let the interface be y=0, with glass at y<0 and water at y>0, and choose x parallel to the incident beam's tangential component. Define k=ω/c as the vacuum wavenumber, with glass and water refractive indices ng​ and nw​. Continuity of phase along the interface fixes kx​=kng​sinθ. In water, the dispersion relation is kx2​+ky2​=k2nw2​, so above the critical angle,
ky2​=−k2(ng2​sin2θ−nw2​),ky​=iκ,κ=kng2​sin2θ−nw2​​>0.
(68)
Choose the positive imaginary part because a field growing exponentially into the water would be unphysical. The evanescent field in total internal reflection is therefore
E(x,y,t)=E0​exp[i(kng​sinθx−ωt)]exp[−kng2​sin2θ−nw2​​y].​
(69)
It propagates in phase parallel to the interface but decays normally. Here E0​ is the transmitted field amplitude at the interface; relating it to the incident amplitude requires the appropriate polarization-dependent Fresnel equations. If the symbol k instead denotes the incident glass wavenumber, replace every vacuum k in the formula by k/ng​; equivalently kx​=ksinθ and κ=ksin2θ−(nw​/ng​)2​. This states the wavenumber convention left implicit in the source.
