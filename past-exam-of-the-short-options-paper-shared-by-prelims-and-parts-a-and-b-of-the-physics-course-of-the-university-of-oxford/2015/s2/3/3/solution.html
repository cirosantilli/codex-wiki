Nonbaryonic dark matter can provide the additional gravitating mass. Gravitational lensing independently maps cluster mass, while the cosmic microwave background and Big Bang nucleosynthesis constrain the cosmic matter and baryon budgets. The separation of lensing mass from strongly interacting X-ray gas in merging clusters is evidence that much of the gravitating component is not that hot gas.
Uncounted baryonic matter, including the observed hot gas, faint stars and compact remnants, certainly adds to the stellar estimate. X-ray emission directly establishes a large gas reservoir. However, baryon-budget constraints and microlensing constraints on compact objects prevent arbitrarily filling the entire discrepancy with ordinary hidden objects.
Modified gravity could change the inference from a measured acceleration to a Newtonian mass. Such a theory must also reproduce the observed lensing and cosmological evidence; explaining some rotation curves alone is insufficient, and cluster mass discrepancies remain a demanding test. Departures from equilibrium and projection can bias individual mass estimates, but would have to explain the independent observations systematically rather than merely modify one virial prefactor.
