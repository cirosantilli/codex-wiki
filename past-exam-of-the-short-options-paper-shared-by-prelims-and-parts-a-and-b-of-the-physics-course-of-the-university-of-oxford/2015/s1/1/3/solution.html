Take g(z)=L(z)3/z3 on the same logarithm branch. At zero, L(z)3=−z3+O(z4), so the apparent singularity is removable and the closed contour integral is zero. The bank difference is
(ℓ−iπ)3−(ℓ+iπ)3=−6iπℓ2+2iπ3.
(4)
The circular contributions vanish by the previous estimates with the third power of the logarithm. Consequently
0=−6iπ∫1∞​x3log2(x−1)​dx+2iπ3∫1∞​x3dx​.
(5)
The last integral is 1/2, giving
∫1∞​x3log2(x−1)​dx=6π2​.​
(6)
Increasing the logarithm power has converted the desired real integral into a jump across a branch cut, while removing the pole inside the contour.
