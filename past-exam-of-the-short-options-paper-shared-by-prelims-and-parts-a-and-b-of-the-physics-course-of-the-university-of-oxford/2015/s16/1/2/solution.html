Let N=nH​+np​ be the conserved number density of hydrogen nuclei, whether ionized or bound, and x=np​/N=ne​/N the ionization fraction. The ideal gas pressure counts neutral atoms, free protons and free electrons:
P=NkB​T(1+x),1−xx2​=NS(T)​,S(T)=h3(2πme​kB​T)3/2​e−R/(kB​T).
(81)
At low temperature x≃0 and P≃NkB​T. Heating through the ionization region increases the particle count as well as their thermal energy, giving a steeper rise. At high temperature x≃1, so P≃2NkB​T and the asymptotic slope is twice the low-temperature slope. There is a smooth ionization crossover, not a pressure discontinuity.
The held graph is legible. Representative readings are P(5000K)≃6.8Pa and P(15000K)≃41.4Pa, with readout uncertainties of roughly 0.3–0.5Pa and about 100K. They give N≃P/(kB​T) on the neutral branch and N≃P/(2kB​T) on the ionized branch, respectively. Both are consistent with
N≃(1.0±0.05)×1020m−3.​
(82)
The error is a conservative graph-reading estimate, not a fitted experimental confidence interval.
For that density, the Saha equation gives x≃0.50 at 9000K, x≃0.81 at 10000K, and x≃0.9992 at 15000K. Differentiating the ionization relation gives
x′=2−xx(1−x)​(2T3​+kB​T2R​),P′=NkB​(1+x+Tx′).
(83)
The calculated maximum slope occurs near 9.25×103K, consistent with the source's visibly steepest region at roughly (9.3±0.4)×103K. This checks the actual slope rather than identifying it without justification with exactly half ionization.
Taking the neutral ground-state gas as the zero of internal binding energy, the matter energy density is translational kinetic energy plus ionization energy:
u=23​NkB​T(1+x)+NxR.
(84)
At 15000K, with R=13.6eV, these terms are about 62 and 218Jm−3, giving
u≃2.8×102Jm−3.​
(85)
Rest energies are excluded. A separate equilibrium radiation field, if the gas were in a blackbody cavity, would add its own radiation energy density; it is not part of this gas-only estimate.
