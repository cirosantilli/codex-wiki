An apparent collapse is the transition from predicting several possible recorded outcomes to describing a particular recorded result. Whether that transition is a physical discontinuity, a conditional state update or an effective description depends on what the wave function is taken to represent. The practical success of collapse-based calculation does not by itself settle that interpretive question.
The difficulty is exposed by unitary time evolution. An ideal apparatus that reliably measures a basis has
∣0⟩∣Aready​⟩⟼∣0⟩∣A0​⟩,∣1⟩∣Aready​⟩⟼∣1⟩∣A1​⟩.
(162)
Linearity then makes a superposed input evolve as
(a∣0⟩+b∣1⟩)∣Aready​⟩⟼a∣0⟩∣A0​⟩+b∣1⟩∣A1​⟩.
(163)
This is an entangled state, not automatically one pointer packet or the other. A separate projection rule would instead select one recorded outcome, with Born rule weights, and assign its corresponding conditional state.
Quantum decoherence explains much of the practical appearance of a classical record. Interaction with an environment correlates the packets with nearly orthogonal environment states. The subsystem's reduced density matrix then has very small interference terms between the pointer alternatives. Local experiments on that subsystem approximately agree with a diagonal mixture. But this is an improper mixture: the total state can remain a pure superposition. Taking a partial trace has not selected one term of the total state. Suppression of observable interference and the occurrence of a unique absolute outcome are distinct claims.
There is a measurement problem if one simultaneously insists that the wave function is the complete physical description, that its evolution is universally unitary, and that a measurement has exactly one absolute outcome of the ordinary recorded kind. The ideal model shows why simply applying the same unitary law to a larger apparatus does not make that combination work. Nor does calling collapse a change in knowledge solve the problem for someone who has already taken the wave function to be a complete ontic state.
Different interpretations revise different parts of this package. Bohmian mechanics adds an actual configuration and obtains effective subsystem collapse while the total wave evolves unitarily. The many-worlds interpretation retains the total superposition and treats each record as definite relative to its decohering branch. A stochastic collapse theory instead modifies the physical dynamics. Everett's original proposal explicitly separates his relative-state account from an additional collapse postulate.[ref]
Thus collapse is not problematic merely because conditional probabilities change when a record is learned. The problem is the status of that record and of the dynamical rule used to describe it. A defensible interpretation must explain when its updating rule is applicable, what makes a record definite in its own ontology, and why the ordinary measurement statistics follow. No appeal to human consciousness is required by the displayed unitary measurement model.
