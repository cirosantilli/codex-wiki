The age difference is the difference between proper times along two worldlines joining the same departure and reunion events. In the inertial Earth frame a traveler who spends total coordinate time T moving at speed magnitude v, with negligible-duration turnaround, accumulates T1−v2/c2​ while the stay-at-home clock accumulates T. These are invariant readings at reunion. Acceleration permits the change of traveling inertial frame, but the result is determined by the whole path, not a universal rule that acceleration itself slows clocks.
Relativity of simultaneity is important when explaining the same result using the traveler's successive inertial coordinates. At the turnaround, the traveler changes which distant Earth event is assigned the same time. The simultaneity slice changes, producing a jump in the coordinate time attributed to Earth in the ideal instantaneous limit. On each long inertial leg the traveler can assign a slow rate to the distant Earth clock without contradicting Earth's larger total elapsed time, because those assignments do not cover the reunion comparison with a single simultaneity convention.
The key distinction is between explaining a coordinate-based apparent symmetry and deriving an invariant result. One can calculate both proper-time integrals in one inertial frame without ever using the traveler's simultaneity jump. Conversely, claiming that each twin must simply see the other younger confuses two reciprocal comparisons between different sets of simultaneous distant events. Radar signals and Doppler shifts provide an operational account with finite signal travel times and the same reunion readings.
Thus relativity of simultaneity resolves a specific misleading inference in the paradox, but is not an extra physical mechanism that changes a clock at a distance. The worldline asymmetry and the invariant proper-time calculation suffice for the prediction; simultaneity explains why reciprocal inertial time-dilation statements cannot be pasted together indiscriminately.[ref]
The geometrical explanation uses dτ2=dt2−dx2/c2 along each timelike path. In flat spacetime an inertial path between fixed timelike endpoints locally maximizes proper time, unlike the familiar shortest-path property of Euclidean distance. This is a physical metric property tested by clocks, not an appeal to a diagram alone. The explanation is satisfactory insofar as it connects that metric, specified worldlines and actual clock behavior; it does not require an absolute flow of time or a special clock-damaging action at the turnaround.
