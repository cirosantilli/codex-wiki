A tokamak confines a hot, approximately toroidal plasma using a strong toroidal magnetic field and a poloidal field associated with plasma current and external coils. Helical field lines and suitable magnetic surfaces reduce rapid transverse escape. D-T fusion releases 17.6 MeV, mostly in an uncharged neutron that escapes magnetic confinement and transfers energy to a surrounding blanket. A heat exchanger and thermal power cycle convert some of that energy into electricity.
Essential systems include the vacuum vessel, toroidal and poloidal magnets, current-drive/transformer and auxiliary heating equipment, fueling and exhaust systems, divertor for concentrated heat and particle removal, neutron-absorbing/breeding blanket and shielding. Alpha particles retained in the plasma contribute self-heating. Confinement and controlled exhaust are as important as reaching a high temperature; a magnetic field does not confine the neutrons or make material heat loads vanish.
