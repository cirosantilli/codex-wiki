The 21.1 cm line is neutral hydrogen's hyperfine spin-flip transition, observed with radio telescopes and interferometers in the cool/warm neutral gas disk, often beyond the bright stellar disk. The mean wavelength gives z=22.0/21.1−1≃0.0427 and, using the stated low-redshift Hubble approximation, d≃180 Mpc. A 16-arcsecond angular radius gives R≃14 kpc. For a thin circular disk, cosi=8/32, so i≃75.5∘ from face-on.
The original spectrum reaches roughly 21.98 and 22.02 cm: vlos,max​≃c(0.02/22.0)≃273 km s−1. Deprojecting gives vmax​≃282 kms−1​. A spherical dynamical estimate M(<R)≃v2R/G gives M∼2.6×1011M⊙​​. A thin massive disk is not spherical: its exact rotation-to-total-mass coefficient depends on radial mass profile. The no-exterior-matter assumption does not uniquely fix that coefficient, so the quoted result is the conventional order-of-magnitude dynamical estimate. Cosmological luminosity and angular-diameter distances differ at this redshift; the source's simple Hubble-distance convention is being used consistently.
