Degenerate electrons conduct heat efficiently, making most of the interior nearly isothermal while an outer nondegenerate insulating envelope sustains a large temperature gradient. Nuclei have much larger masses and much smaller thermal de Broglie wavelengths than electrons, so their quantum degeneracy is weak at these temperatures/densities, although Coulomb coupling and crystallization can matter.
