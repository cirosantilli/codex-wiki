For local dichotomic outcomes define A(a,λ),B(b,λ)=±1 and a setting-independent normalized distribution ρ(λ). At each λ,
A(a)[B(b)+B(b′)]+A(a′)[B(b)−B(b′)]=±2,
(42)
because exactly one bracket vanishes and the other is ±2. Integrating and using the triangle inequality yields
∣E(a,b)+E(a,b′)+E(a′,b)−E(a′,b′)∣≤2.​
(43)
For stochastic local response functions the same bound follows by replacing outcomes with conditional means in [−1,1], or by including local randomness in λ. The important locality condition is factorization of joint conditional outcome probabilities, together with independence of the setting distribution and λ.
The spin singlet instead predicts E(a,b)=−a⋅b. Two orthogonal directions at one wing and directions halfway between them at the other give ∣S∣=22​. Therefore reproducing the quantum predictions requires giving up at least one of the stated premises. In the perfect-anticorrelation version, B(a,λ)=−A(a,λ) gives Bell's three-setting form ∣E(a,b)−E(a,c)∣≤1+E(b,c) by the same algebra.
This does not establish that all deterministic or hidden-variable theories are impossible: nonlocal guidance is a counterexample to that reading. Nor does it equate Bell locality with no-signalling; averaging quantum outcomes can preserve setting-independent local marginals despite nonfactorizing correlations. The theorem is significant because it turns a local-completeness dispute into incompatible quantitative correlation bounds, not because it refutes every possible meaning of physical reality.[ref]
