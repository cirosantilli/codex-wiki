Albedo reduction increases absorbed short-wave flux by S0​(a1​T+a2​T2)/4. Define A=λP​−1.5−S0​a1​/4 and B=S0​a2​/4. The perturbation energy balance is N(T)=F−AT+BT2, where F=3.7 W m−2 for doubling. With the preceding constants, A≃1.220 W m−2 K−1 and B=0.068 W m−2 K−2. The two roots are
Tstable​≃3.86 K,Tunstable​≃14.1 K.​
(73)
Stability follows from N′(T)=−A+2BT<0 at the lower root. They merge at T=A/(2B)≃8.97 K and Fmax​=A2/(4B)≃5.47 W m−2; at equality the equilibrium is marginal, not linearly stable.
Assuming the usual logarithmic forcing extension F=3.7log(C/280)/log2 gives a stable branch only for C<Ccrit​≃7.8×102 ppm​. This “maximum” is the supremum of stable concentrations in the stated quadratic, linearized model. It is not a physical prediction of a real-world runaway threshold; extrapolating its feedback laws far beyond their small-change regime is an additional assumption.
