A luminosity does not determine a stellar mass without a mass-luminosity calibration. Using the usual approximate main-sequence law L/L⊙​=(Ms​/M⊙​)α gives Ms​=31/αM⊙​. With α=3.5, the numerical results are
Ms​≃1.37M⊙​,Mp​≃0.39MJ​,a≃0.101 AU,ϑ≃2.46×10−4 arcsec.​
(12)
The angular radius follows directly from a(AU)/d(pc). Taking α=4 changes these estimates by only a few percent, but the calibration must be stated. Hubble's 2.4 m aperture has visible-light diffraction scale 1.22λ/D≃0.05 arcsec at 500 nm, hundreds of times larger. The separation is therefore unresolved even before the severe star/planet contrast is considered.
