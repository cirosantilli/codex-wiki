Nonexplosion of a continuous-time Markov chain (#{jumps in [0,t]}<∞ almost surely)

Words: 83
Continuous-time Markov chain OurBigBook.com
A continuous-time Markov chain is nonexplosive if it makes only finitely many jumps on every bounded time interval. A uniformly bounded total jump intensity permits domination by a Poisson process. More generally, if a population process stays in a finite random set of states on each finite interval and every individual state has finite exit rate, localizing to fixed finite sets gives the same conclusion. In an open migration process, external Poisson immigration bounds the total population while internal moves preserve it and exits reduce it, giving this localization.

A Markov jump process is a nonexplosive, piecewise-constant continuous-time Markov process. Transition rates q(x,y) give exponential holding time of rate ∑y=x​q(x,y) and next-state probabilities proportional to these rates. Being Markov relative to a specified filtration requires the conditional future law given that filtration to depend only on the present state.

A continuous-time Markov chain with finite holding rates is nonexplosive when its jump times have no finite accumulation almost surely. It then remains in its specified state space at every finite time, rather than being lost to a cemetery state after infinitely many jumps. Absorption-or-escape dichotomy for a chain with killing (P(absorption or Xt​→∞)=1)

Words: 94
Nonexplosive continuous-time Markov chain OurBigBook.com
For a nonexplosive continuous-time Markov chain on the nonnegative integers, suppose zero is absorbing and each positive state has positive rate to zero. On every finite positive set the conditional chance of absorption at the next jump has a positive minimum. By the Strong Markov property, the chance of surviving r departures from that set is bounded by (1−δ)r. Without absorption only finitely many such departures occur, and finite positive holding rates prohibit staying forever at a positive state. Thus eventually the path stays outside each finite set: it escapes to infinity. This conclusion needs no upward skip-free assumption.
