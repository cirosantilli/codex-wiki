Falling bodies and lunar motion as tests of gravity

 ... Physics Branches of physics Classical mechanics Energy Potential energy Inverse-square potential
OurBigBook.com Words: 140
One motivating laboratory demonstration compares two balls initially at the same height. A switch releases one vertically when the other leaves a horizontal track; arranging their paths to meet gives a collision as the readout of equal downward displacement over equal time. Equal initial height and synchronized release control the comparison. This tests the local independence of horizontal motion and free fall, under nearly constant gravitational acceleration; it does not measure an inverse-square law.[ref]
A complementary astronomical comparison uses the Moon's distance and orbital period to infer inward centripetal acceleration, a=4π2r/T2, and compares it with surface gravitational acceleration scaled by (R/r)2. The approximately sixtyfold distance ratio gives the approximately 1/3600 prediction. The inference links terrestrial free fall to a lunar orbit. Distance calibration matters; the circular orbit approximation and the Sun's tidal force limit this elementary comparison.[ref]
