Smooth function with a pointwise vanishing derivative

 ... Area of mathematics Analysis Topological analysis Metric space Complete metric space Baire category theorem
OurBigBook.com Words: 209
Let f∈C∞(R) and suppose that for every x there is an n=n(x)≥0 such that f(n)(x)=0. Then f is a polynomial.
Set Sn​={x:f(n)(x)=0} and En​=intSn​. The sets Sn​ are closed, En​⊂En+1​, and Baire's theorem on each interval shows that Ω=⋃n​En​ is dense. On every connected component of Ω, compactness and the increasing cover by the En​ show that one derivative vanishes locally with a uniform finite order, so f agrees there with one polynomial.
If F=R∖Ω were nonempty, it would be closed and have no isolated points: polynomial pieces on both sides of an isolated point would have matching derivatives of every order and hence join into one polynomial piece. Applying Baire's theorem to the cover F=⋃n​(F∩Sn​) gives an interval U and an index k for which F∩U is nonempty and contained in Sk​. Because F has no isolated points, difference quotients show that every derivative of order at least k vanishes on F∩U. Each polynomial component of Ω meeting U has an endpoint in F∩U, so its degree is less than k. Thus f(k)=0 throughout U, contradicting F∩U=∅. Hence Ω=R, and its sole connected component carries one polynomial.
