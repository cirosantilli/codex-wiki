Real and imaginary parts of a symmetric unitary matrix (U=A+iB,AB=BA,A2+B2=I)

 ... Mathematics Area of mathematics Algebra Linear algebra Operator theory Unitary matrix
OurBigBook.com Words: 99
Suppose a unitary matrix U is symmetric under ordinary transpose, and write its entrywise real and imaginary parts as A and B. They are real symmetric matrices. Expanding U†U=I gives A2+B2+i(AB−BA)=I, proving the displayed identities. Commuting real symmetric matrices admit simultaneous diagonalization: diagonalize A, then diagonalize the restriction of B to each invariant eigenspace of A. A common real orthonormal eigenbasis gives
U=Qdiag(eiθj​)QT,A=Qdiag(cosθj​)QT,B=Qdiag(sinθj​)QT.
(70)
If A has simple eigenvalues, every eigenvector of A is already an eigenvector of B, because its one-dimensional eigenspace is invariant. The eigenvalues of A and B are paired as the real and imaginary parts of the same eigenvalue of U.
