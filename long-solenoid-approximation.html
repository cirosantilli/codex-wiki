Long-solenoid approximation (Bin​=μ0​nIz,Bout​=0)

 Home Physics Branches of physics Electromagnetism Magnetic field Solenoid
OurBigBook.com Words: 369
In magnetostatics, an ideal infinitely long solenoid is a cylindrical sheet of circumferential surface current density K=nI. It has negligible winding advance, no magnetic core, and no imposed external magnetic field. Choose positive electric current to produce magnetic field along +z. In this ideal model the interior magnetic field is uniform and axial, B=μ0​nI, and the exterior magnetic field is zero; μ0​ is the vacuum permeability. For a finite, closely wound air-core coil, the same result is an approximation well inside the coil, several radii from either end. Air is treated as having the magnetic permeability of vacuum. [ref].
For a short proof, the translational and rotational symmetries of the ideal cylindrical current sheet, together with the Biot-Savart law, give an axial magnetic field. Ampère's law on rectangles wholly outside makes that axial exterior value independent of radius; the condition of no imposed magnetic field makes it zero at infinity and hence everywhere outside. Rectangles wholly inside similarly show that the interior value is independent of radius. Now choose an Amperian loop with axial length s, one long side inside and the other outside. The transverse sides are perpendicular to the magnetic field, so their contributions to the line integral vanish. The loop crosses ns turns of the winding, giving
∮Γ​B⋅dl=Bs=μ0​(ns)I,B=μ0​nI.
(6)
Here s is the integration-path length, not the whole coil length ℓ. An Amperian loop surrounding both sides of the winding encloses equal and opposite electric currents; zero net circulation alone would not prove zero magnetic field at every exterior point.
For the 1000-turn, 0.50m coil with electric current I=0.50A, the long-solenoid approximation gives
B≈(1.256637×10−6)(2000)(0.50)T=1.256637×10−3T≈1.257mT.
(7)
This illustrative calculation uses vacuum permeability μ0​≈1.256637×10−6H/m, a rounded value, not an exact constant. Since the 2019 redefinition of SI, vacuum permeability is determined experimentally.[ref] Doubling I doubles the ideal magnetic field; doubling N at fixed ℓ does the same. Increasing ℓ while keeping N fixed decreases n and hence the ideal magnetic field.
