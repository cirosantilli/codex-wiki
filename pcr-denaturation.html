PCR denaturation

 Home Biology Genetics Molecular genetics Polymerase chain reaction PCR thermal cycle
OurBigBook.com Words: 151
PCR denaturation is the high-temperature stage that separates a DNA duplex into single-stranded templates for the next copying step. It disrupts the pairing and stacking arrangement of nucleobases; it does not require cutting the phosphodiester bonds along either strand. The distinction matters: breaking the backbone would damage the ordered nucleotide sequence that is meant to serve as a template.
For an original eight-base pair duplex, complete PCR denaturation exposes two eight-nucleotide strands. It leaves sixteen nucleotides altogether: separation has made the templates accessible without yet making more DNA. PCR extension, rather than heating alone, adds the new material. Already copied duplexes also need to separate at the beginning of subsequent cycles.
Separation is a condition-dependent process. Template composition and the buffer solution affect the required temperature and duration; heating longer is not automatically better because prolonged heating can reduce DNA polymerase activity. Incomplete separation leaves fewer accessible templates even when the PCR primers themselves are well chosen.[ref]
