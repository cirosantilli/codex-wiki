Karush-Kuhn-Tucker conditions for the Lasso (XT(Y−Xb)/n∈λ∂∥b∥1​)

Words: 33
Lasso OurBigBook.com
For the Lasso criterion ∥Y−Xb∥22​/(2n)+λ∥b∥1​, the displayed subgradient optimality condition is necessary and sufficient. A nonzero coefficient has residual score λsign(bk​), and a zero coefficient has residual score in [−λ,λ]. The subdifferential of the L1 norm supplies both cases.

Lasso regularization path (β​(t))

Words: 226 Articles: 2
Lasso OurBigBook.com
The Lasso regularization path follows its coefficients as the penalty or constraint radius changes. Under the usual least-angle construction, the coefficient path is piecewise linear between knots, and variables can enter or leave the active set. Reading support at a selected cross-validation fraction requires checking which coefficients are actually zero at that fraction, rather than retaining only visibly large traces.

KKT-consistent resolution of tied Lasso knots (v=argmin{21​vTXTXv−sET​vE​: vEc​=0, sj​vj​≥0 (j∈E∖N)})

Words: 101
Lasso regularization path OurBigBook.com
At a positive penalty level, let E be the residual-score equality set, sj​ its signs and N the nonzero regression coefficient support. With full column rank, the displayed strictly convex quadratic program selects the direction for a small decrease of the penalty. Existing nonzero regression coefficients have unrestricted tangent components; tied zero regression coefficients may move only with their residual signs. Stationarity gives XjT​Xv=sj​ for j∈N and for entering zero coordinates, and sj​XjT​Xv≥1 for tied coordinates that remain zero. These are exactly the first-order Karush-Kuhn-Tucker conditions for the Lasso along the next segment. Normalize Xv to obtain the corresponding least-angle direction. This resolves simultaneous entries or dropouts without adding a generic-position assumption.

Lasso dropout knot (γdrop​=minj∈A:−βj​/dj​>0​(−βj​/dj​))

Words: 67
Lasso regularization path OurBigBook.com
On a least-angle segment β(γ)=β+γd, a nonzero active regression coefficient reaches zero at the displayed first positive root. Continue only as far as the first dropout, inactive-entry event or zero-correlation endpoint. At a dropout remove the vanishing regression coefficient from the active direction and recompute. This preserves agreement between nonzero regression coefficient signs and residual-score signs, which the Karush-Kuhn-Tucker conditions for the Lasso require. A removed regression coefficient may re-enter later; the active set need not grow monotonically.

Gaussian score event for the Lasso (Ω={∥XTε∥∞​/n≤cλ})

Words: 48
Lasso OurBigBook.com
Let 0<c<1, A>0, σ>0 and p≥2. For design matrix columns of norm n​ and ε∼Nn​(0,σ2I), the event ∥XTε∥∞​/n≤cλ has probability at least 1−p1−A2c2/2 when λ=Aσlog(p)/n​. Apply the sharp two-sided Gaussian tail bound to each standardized score and then the union bound; independence of the scores is not required.

Sparse maximum eigenvalue (κm2​)

Words: 141 Articles: 1
Lasso OurBigBook.com
For a design matrix X with p columns, define κm2​=max∣M∣=m​λmax​(XMT​XM​/n) for 1≤m≤p. Equivalently this bounds ∥Xv∥22​/(n∥v∥22​) for vectors supported on at most m coordinates. These quantities are nondecreasing: extend the support of a maximizing vector by zero coordinates and use the variational characterization of the largest eigenvalue. Values at m>p, including infinity, require a separate convention.

Lasso support bound from sparse eigenvalues (∣S∣≤64κp2​s/ϕ2)

For λ>0 and ϕ>0, use the Lasso objective ∥Y−Xβ∥22​/(2n)+λ∥β∥1​. Suppose the noise score has maximum norm at most λ/2, the true support has size s, and ∥X(β​−β0)∥22​/n≤16λ2s/ϕ2. For any nonempty B⊆S, the Karush-Kuhn-Tucker conditions imply
λ∣B∣/2≤n−1sgn(β​B​)TXBT​X(β0−β​)≤4λκ∣B∣​∣B∣s​/ϕ.
(1)

Group Lasso

Words: 150 Articles: 2
Lasso OurBigBook.com
The group Lasso partitions a coefficient vector into groups G1​,…,Gq​ and replaces the coordinatewise ℓ1 penalty with a weighted sum of group norms,
P(β)=∑j=1q​mj​∥βGj​​∥2​.
(2)
It tends to retain or discard an entire group at once. Its dual norm is
P∗(v)=maxj​mj−1​∥vGj​​∥2​,
(3)
so the corresponding Holder inequality is ∣uTv∣≤P(u)P∗(v).

Paired group Lasso for Gaussian graph estimation (∑j<k​Θjk2​+Θkj2​​)

Words: 100 Articles: 1
Group Lasso OurBigBook.com
Joint squared-error nodewise regression with this penalty groups the two coefficients of each unordered edge. Its norm is nonsmooth only when the whole pair is zero, encouraging edge-wise sparsity. A fitted edge is present when its pair norm is positive. The penalty does not require equal directional coefficients, two nonzero entries in every selected group, or a symmetric positive definite precision matrix.

Paired group Lasso optimality conditions (g=0: ∥c∥2​≤λ;g=0: c=λg/∥g∥2​)

Words: 41
Paired group Lasso for Gaussian graph estimation OurBigBook.com
For each unordered pair, g=(Θjk​,Θkj​) and c=(XjT​rk​/n,XkT​rj​/n) with centred columns and regression residuals. The subdifferential of the Euclidean norm is the unit ball at zero and g/∥g∥2​ otherwise. These KKT conditions are necessary and sufficient for the convex paired objective and explain joint edge deletion.

Gaussian identity design for debiased Lasso

Words: 191
Debiased-Lasso asymptotic normality OurBigBook.com
In the normal linear model, assume ε∼Nn​(0,σ2I) is independent of X. Independent rows of X with multivariate normal distribution Np​(0,Ip​) give a simple random design for Debiased Lasso. Let p→∞, logp=o(n) and slogp=o(n​), with fixed positive noise scale. Choose the Lasso penalty and all Nodewise Lasso penalties as sufficiently large constant multiples of logp/n​, including the noise scale in the former. Uniform sample cross-correlations are bounded by this scale, so zero is the nodewise solution with probability tending to one. Column squared norms divided by n converge uniformly to one. To check the Compatibility condition for the Lasso directly, put Σ=XTX/n and η=maxj,k​∣Σjk​−1{j=k}​∣. On the Lasso cone condition, ∥δ∥1​≤4∥δS​∥1​, and the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality gives
δTΣδ≥∥δ∥22​−η∥δ∥12​≥(1/s−16η)∥δS​∥12​.
(8)
For s≥1, entrywise concentration of a Gaussian Gram matrix gives η≤D0​logp/n​ with probability tending to one for sufficiently large fixed D0​. The stated sparsity condition implies sη→0 on this event, so one can take ϕ2≥1/2 eventually. The conditional Gaussian tail bound for the noise score and the Basic inequality for the Lasso then give ∥β​−β0∥1​≤Cslogp/n​ with probability tending to one. Consequently the debiased-Lasso remainder bound is at most Aslogp/n​ with probability tending to one, for a fixed constant A. These assumptions allow both p and s to grow, for example p=n2, s=⌊n1/4⌋.

Elastic net regularization

Lasso OurBigBook.com
Elastic-net regression minimizes squared error plus both an ℓ1 coefficient penalty and a squared ℓ2 penalty. It combines exact sparsity with strict convexity when the quadratic penalty is positive.

Elastic net regularization in Hilbert sequence space (J(u)=∥u∥1​+β∥u∥22​,β>0)

Words: 193 Articles: 2
Elastic net regularization OurBigBook.com
Extend J by infinity outside ℓ1. For a bounded linear operator K:ℓ2→ℓ2, the objective ∥Ku−f∥22​/2+αJ(u) is coercive, weakly lower semicontinuous and strictly convex for α>0, so it has a unique minimizer. The quadratic penalty ensures existence and uniqueness even if K has a null space. Its limiting exact solution minimizes J in the data fiber, which need not be the minimum-norm least-squares solution.

Nonlinearity of elastic net reconstruction

Words: 67
Elastic net regularization in Hilbert sequence space OurBigBook.com
For fixed α>0 and nonzero bounded K, the reconstruction vanishes when ∥K∗f∥∞​≤α, which includes a neighborhood of zero. If it were linear it would vanish everywhere. Choose a coordinate ej​ with Kej​=0 and data f=tKej​ with t∥Kej​∥2>α; the optimality inequality at zero fails, so its minimizer is nonzero. Thus the map is nonlinear. The qualification K=0 is essential: K=0 gives the zero linear map.

Norm convergence of elastic net regularization

Words: 67
Elastic net regularization in Hilbert sequence space OurBigBook.com
Assume an exact solution of finite penalty exists and select the unique J-minimizing exact solution u†. Minimality gives ∥Kuαδ​−fδ∥2/2+αJ(uαδ​)≤δ2/2+αJ(u†). If α→0 and δ2/α→0, boundedness, weak sequential compactness of bounded sequences in a reflexive Banach space and lower semicontinuity identify every weak limit with u†. The separate lower semicontinuity of ∥u∥1​ and ∥u∥22​, together with the upper bound on J, forces the Hilbert norms to converge. The Radon-Riesz theorem then gives norm convergence.

Grouping effect of the elastic net

Words: 30
Elastic net regularization OurBigBook.com
The quadratic penalty encourages strongly correlated predictors to receive similar coefficients, so an elastic net tends to retain or discard correlated predictors as a group more readily than the Lasso.
