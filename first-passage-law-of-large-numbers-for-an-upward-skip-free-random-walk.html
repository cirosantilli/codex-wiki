First-passage law of large numbers for an upward skip-free random walk (Tj​/ja.s.​1/μ)

 ... Probability and statistics Probability theory Markov process Markov chain Random walk Upward skip-free random walk
OurBigBook.com Words: 85
Suppose an integer-valued random walk starts at S0​=0, has upward increments at most one, and satisfies Sn​/n→μ>0 almost surely. Write Tj​ for its first-passage time to j≥1. On the event of almost sure convergence, the walk tends to +∞ and cannot jump over any positive integer, so every Tj​ is finite. The bound Sn​≤n gives Tj​≥j→∞. Along this subsequence, j/Tj​=STj​​/Tj​→μ on each such sample path, proving the displayed reciprocal limit. No independence between the hitting times and the path is required. The strong law of large numbers supplies the hypothesis for integrable independent and identically distributed random variables as steps with positive mean.
