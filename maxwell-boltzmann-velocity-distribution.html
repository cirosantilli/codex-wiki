Maxwell-Boltzmann velocity distribution (f(v))

OurBigBook.com Words: 795 Articles: 5
In the gas rest frame, the Maxwell-Boltzmann velocity distribution is the probability density function per unit volume in velocity space:
f(v)=(2πkB​Tm​)3/2exp(−2kB​Tm∣v∣2​)=(2πs2)3/2e−∣v∣2/(2s2)​,∫R3​f(v)d3v=1.
(55)
Thus f(v)d3v gives the probability of a small three-dimensional velocity region. For a gas with bulk mean velocity V, replace v by v−V; the Maxwell speed distribution then describes the magnitude relative to that bulk motion.
For any one Cartesian velocity component vx​, integrating out the other two gives
q(vx​)=2π​s1​e−vx2​/(2s2),⟨vx​⟩=0,⟨vx2​⟩=s2=mkB​T​.
(56)
The factorization f(v)=q(vx​)q(vy​)q(vz​) establishes independence: all three components are centered normal random variables, with variance s2. Both the component density q and the vector density f have their largest values at zero. This concerns densities for small equal-sized regions; any single exact velocity still has probability zero. [ref]
The standard deviation s measures thermal motion even though the mean velocity is zero. Positive and negative components cancel in the mean, but their squares add. This is why thermal Doppler broadening measures a nonzero line width in a gas with no bulk motion.
