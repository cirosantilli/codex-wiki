Quantum optics

 Home Physics Branches of physics Quantum theory
OurBigBook.com Words: 4k Articles: 44
Quantum optics studies light using quantum states of the electromagnetic field and their interaction with matter. Photon statistics asks how detections fluctuate; the quantum theory of optical coherence relates those detections to field correlations. A coherent state, thermal light, and a Fock state can have the same mean photon number yet different pair counts. This distinction makes photon counting useful for testing sources intended to emit one photon at a time.

Field-squared quadrature (K1​=(a†2+a2)/2,K2​=(a†2−a2)/(2i))

Hermitian combinations of pair creation and annihilation operators. With K3​=(N+1/2)/2, their commutators are [K1​,K2​]=−4iK3​, [K2​,K3​]=iK1​ and [K3​,K1​]=iK2​. A coherent state has both variances ⟨N⟩+1/2, saturating the corresponding uncertainty product.

A field-squared quadrature variance below the equal-variance coherent-state reference in this normalization. Its conjugate variance must remain compatible with the state-dependent commutator bound. This is not the same observable as ordinary field-amplitude squeezing or a reduction of mean intensity.

Nonclassical state of light

Words: 103 Articles: 1
Quantum optics OurBigBook.com
A state of light is nonclassical when it is not a classical mixture of coherent states. The Glauber-Sudarshan representation states this criterion in phase space. Examples include nonvacuum number states and quadrature squeezing states. A coherent state is on the classical boundary despite its vacuum fluctuations; thermal light is a classical mixture despite its non-Poisson fluctuations. None of these nonclassicality witnesses is a necessary condition for nonclassicality.

Nonclassicality witnesses

Words: 50
Nonclassical state of light OurBigBook.com
Sufficient single-mode witnesses include a Fano factor Varn/⟨n⟩<1, an optical quadrature variance below 1/4 for [X,P]=i/2, or photon antibunching with g(2)(0)<1. Classical mixtures obey Varn≥⟨n⟩, and g(2)(0)=E(∣α∣4)/E(∣α∣2)2≥1 by nonnegative variance. The tests are sufficient rather than necessary and exclude the vacuum case when dividing by zero mean occupation.

Photon subtraction (∣ψ−​⟩=a∣ψ⟩/⟨N⟩​)

Words: 89 Articles: 1
Quantum optics OurBigBook.com
Ideal conditional application of an annihilation operator, defined only for positive initial mean photon number. Number-dependent weighting means that the conditional mean need not fall. Vacuum input gives the zero vector and therefore no successful conditional state.

Conditional photon-number bias (nˉ−​=μ−1+VarN/μ)

Words: 54
Photon subtraction OurBigBook.com
A subtraction event weights initial occupation n by n before removing one photon. Thus its conditional output mean is ⟨N(N−1)⟩/μ, giving the displayed identity for μ>0. If the initial variance exceeds its mean, this biased selection can offset the removed photon. The unconditional ensemble includes unsuccessful events and has a different energy balance.

Photon addition (∣ψ+​⟩=a†∣ψ⟩/⟨N⟩+1​)

Words: 41
Quantum optics OurBigBook.com
Ideal conditional application of a creation operator, followed by normalization. It reweights the input number distribution as well as raising each occupied number by one. A heralded physical implementation has a success probability; the algebraic creation operator alone is not a trace-preserving deterministic quantum operation.

Schrödinger cat state

Words: 213 Articles: 4
Quantum optics OurBigBook.com
A coherent superposition of distinct optical coherent amplitudes, for example N(∣α⟩+eiϕ∣−α⟩). It differs from the corresponding incoherent mixture through cross terms in observables and its phase-space interference. For large ∣α∣, branch distinguishability is large while ordinary quadrature squeezing can become exponentially small.

Loss of coherence in an optical cat state (senv​=e−2(1−η)∣α∣2)

Schrödinger cat state OurBigBook.com
A pure-loss channel of transmissivity η maps the two branches to system amplitudes ±η​α and environment amplitudes ±1−η​α. Tracing over the environment multiplies their cross terms by its branch overlap, the factor displayed. Large cat states can therefore lose interference rapidly even while their mean intensities change only moderately.

Phase-dependent cat-state quadrature variance (VarX2​=41​−(1+scosϕ)2α2s(cosϕ+s)​)

Schrödinger cat state OurBigBook.com
For real α, put s=e−2α2 in N(∣α⟩+eiϕ∣−α⟩). Its mean second quadrature is −αssinϕ/(1+scosϕ), which is generally nonzero. Subtracting its square from the second moment gives the displayed variance and squeezing precisely when cosϕ>−s, for nonzero finite amplitude. Odd coherent-state superposition

Words: 34
Schrödinger cat state OurBigBook.com
The difference N−​(∣α⟩−∣−α⟩) contains only odd photon numbers and tends to a one-photon state as α→0. Exactly at zero amplitude the unnormalized difference vanishes; the normalized limiting state is obtained only by taking a limit.

Even coherent-state superposition

Words: 38
Schrödinger cat state OurBigBook.com
The normalized sum N+​(∣α⟩+∣−α⟩) contains only even photon numbers, since the two coherent-state expansions cancel every odd term. It tends to vacuum at small amplitude. With real α, its second quadrature can be squeezed below the vacuum variance.

Balanced homodyne detection

Words: 167 Articles: 1
Optical homodyne detection OurBigBook.com
Mix a signal mode a and an independent coherent reference mode b on a balanced beam splitter, detect both outputs, and subtract the currents. For c=(a+ib)/2​ and d=(ia+b)/2​, the difference is I=c†c−d†d=i(a†b−ab†). A reference β=∣β∣eiϕ gives mean 2∣β∣⟨Xϑ​⟩ with ϑ=ϕ+π/2. Other beam-splitter conventions shift this phase calibration. The exact finite-reference balanced homodyne variance quantifies the strong-reference approximation.
Matched subtraction cancels the shared total-power term. It therefore suppresses common reference intensity noise, while imbalance and phase fluctuations reduce this advantage. Repeated samples measure a distribution, not just its mean; scanning reference phase supports optical homodyne tomography.

Finite-reference balanced homodyne variance (VarI=4∣β∣2VarXϑ​+⟨a†a⟩)

Words: 72
Balanced homodyne detection OurBigBook.com
For the convention of balanced homodyne detection, assume independent signal and coherent reference states and ideal detectors. Write b=β+δb, with δb in vacuum. The current difference splits into 2∣β∣Xϑ​ and i(a†δb−aδb†). Cross terms have zero expectation, and the second term has variance ⟨a†a⟩. Consequently the exact variance is 4∣β∣2VarXϑ​+⟨a†a⟩. Division by 4∣β∣2 approaches the signal optical quadrature variance when the photon-number correction is negligible.

Optical quadrature (Xϑ​=(ae−iϑ+a†eiϑ)/2)

Words: 307 Articles: 5
Quantum optics OurBigBook.com
A phase-selected Hermitian field observable. With annihilation operator a and [a,a†]=1, define X1​=(a+a†)/2 and X2​=(a−a†)/(2i). Then [X1​,X2​]=i/2 and the Fock vacuum has variance 1/4 in either quadrature. This dimensionless convention gives Xϑ​=X1​cosϑ+X2​sinϑ. Coherent-state electric-field variance (VarE=E02​)

Words: 54
Optical quadrature OurBigBook.com
For one optical mode, E=E0​(a+a†) and [a,a†]=1. A coherent state obeys a∣α⟩=α∣α⟩, so normal ordering gives ⟨E2⟩=E02​[(α+α∗)2+1]. Subtracting the squared mean leaves E02​, independent of the coherent displacement. The extra one is the canonical commutation relation, not noise from a random coherent amplitude. A rotated optical quadrature has the same coherent-state variance with the same amplitude normalization.

Quadrature noise ellipse

Words: 67
Optical quadrature OurBigBook.com
For a single-mode Gaussian state with no quadrature covariance, the covariance contour has semiaxes VarX​ and VarP​. A coherent state has both equal to 1/2 in the convention [X,P]=i/2. A pure squeezed vacuum state has semiaxes e−r/2 and er/2; the uncertainty area remains fixed while one noise width decreases. An ellipse of means alone does not specify a general non-Gaussian state.[ref]

Quadrature squeezing (VarXϑ​<1/4)

Words: 90 Articles: 1
Optical quadrature OurBigBook.com
With Xϑ​=(ae−iϑ+a†eiϑ)/2, the coherent-state and vacuum variances are 1/4. A state is squeezed in the chosen quadrature when its variance about its own mean is smaller. A second moment about zero is not the variance when that mean is nonzero. balanced homodyne detection measures a phase-selected quadrature distribution.

Quadrature squeezing OurBigBook.com
Mixing a mode with independent vacuum on a beam splitter of intensity transmission η gives the displayed optical quadrature variance when vacuum variance is 1/4. It approaches vacuum noise as transmission falls. Phase noise instead mixes squeezed and anti-squeezed axes, and cannot in general be represented by the same efficiency alone.

Quantum theory of optical coherence

Words: 338 Articles: 1
Quantum optics OurBigBook.com
Fix a detected position and polarization of waves. Write the electric field operator as E(t)=E(+)(t)+E(−)(t), where the positive-frequency part contains annihilation operators and E(−)=(E(+))†. An ideal absorbing photodetector responds to
G(1)(t,t′)=Tr[ρE(−)(t)E(+)(t′)],
(2)
with density matrix ρ. The diagonal gives the single-count rate up to the detector coupling. Multiple absorptions require higher, normal-ordered field products. Roy J. Glauber used these correlations to develop the quantum account of optical coherence.[ref]
The ordering follows from a detection amplitude. If absorption takes an initial pure state ∣i⟩ to an unobserved final state ∣f⟩, its field factor is ⟨f∣E(+)∣i⟩. Squaring its complex modulus and summing over a complete orthonormal basis of final states gives ⟨i∣E(−)E(+)∣i⟩. Averaging over initial preparations replaces this expression by a trace. Thus a detector that absorbs cannot count the Fock vacuum, although the vacuum has quantum fluctuations.
For one selected normal mode, E(+)(t)=Eu(t)a. All Fock states with nonzero occupation, coherent states, and thermal light in this same mode have
G(1)(t,t)G(1)(t′,t′)​G(1)(t,t′)​=∣u(t)∣∣u(t′)∣u∗(t)u(t′)​
(3)
where both denominators are nonzero. Its magnitude is one even when the expected value of the field vanishes. Perfect first-order optical coherence therefore does not identify a coherent state. Pair detection supplies additional information.

Second-order optical coherence (g(2)(τ))

Words: 146
Quantum theory of optical coherence OurBigBook.com
For a source whose counting correlations are stationary, define the dimensionless correlation
g(2)(τ)=⟨E(−)(t)E(+)(t)⟩⟨E(−)(t+τ)E(+)(t+τ)⟩⟨E(−)(t)E(−)(t+τ)E(+)(t+τ)E(+)(t)⟩​.
(4)
Assume finite moments and nonzero denominators. The numerator is in normal ordering: two detections remove two photons. The denominator is the product of the two single-count factors, so independent counts have reference value one. This is a diagonal restriction of the four-point second-order field correlation, rather than its complete definition.[ref]
For one selected mode with number operator N=a†a and μ=⟨N⟩>0, evaluate both detections at the same time. The canonical commutation relation aa†=a†a+1 implies N2=(a†)2a2+N, hence
g(2)(0)=μ2⟨N(N−1)⟩​=1+μ2VarN−μ​.
(5)
The factorial moment counts ordered pairs of distinct photons. Using ⟨N2⟩ would include pairing each photon with itself and would give the wrong coincidence prediction. At μ=0 this normalized expression is undefined, rather than zero.

Photon statistics is the probability distribution of the number operator in specified field modes, or the distribution of registered counts in a specified gate. Those distributions agree only for an appropriate ideal measurement. Mode selection, losses, and detector dead time matter. For a one-mode density matrix, pn​=⟨n∣ρ∣n⟩ and
μ=∑n=0∞​npn​,⟨N(N−1)⟩=∑n=0∞​n(n−1)pn​.
(6)
These two sums already distinguish preparations with the same expected value.

Coherent-state photon statistics (pn​=e−μμn/n!,g(2)(0)=1)

Words: 125
Photon statistics OurBigBook.com
For a coherent state, write a∣α⟩=α∣α⟩ and ∣α⟩=∑n​cn​∣n⟩ in number states. The annihilation operator gives cn+1​n+1​=αcn​, so cn​=c0​αn/n!​. Normalization yields ∣c0​∣2=e−∣α∣2. Thus photon statistics follows a Poisson distribution with μ=∣α∣2, and the normal ordering of every moment gives ⟨(a†)kak⟩=μk. This factorization is the defining virtue of coherent states for optical calculations.[ref]
As an original comparison, choose μ=2. Then p0​=e−2, p1​=p2​=2e−2, VarN=2, and ⟨N(N−1)⟩=4. A two-photon Fock state also has mean two but has VarN=0 and pair moment two. Consequently
gcoherent(2)​(0)=1,g∣n⟩(2)​(0)=1−n1​(n≥1).
(7)
In particular, ∣1⟩ cannot supply a pair. Nevertheless, photon statistics alone does not determine a quantum state: averaging ∣α⟩⟨α∣ over a uniform quantum phase removes its off-diagonal entries while retaining exactly the same pn​ and these equal-time moments.

Thermal light

Words: 165
Photon statistics OurBigBook.com
For a single mode in thermal equilibrium, its quantum harmonic oscillator energy ladder gives a geometric distribution
pn​=(1−q)qn,q=e−ℏω/(kB​T),μ=1−qq​,0<q<1.
(8)
Its probability generating function is F(z)=(1−q)/(1−qz). Differentiating twice gives F′′(1)=2q2/(1−q)2=2μ2, hence g(2)(0)=2. At the chosen mean μ=2, q=2/3, p0​=1/3, p1​=2/9, p2​=4/27, and VarN=6. Rare high occupations increase the pair count even though the mean matches the other preparations. The geometric weights also arise from a Gaussian mixture of coherent states.[ref]
Single-mode selection is essential to this factor of two. If M independent equally populated thermal modes are collected together, let N=∑j=1M​Nj​ with ⟨Nj​⟩=μ/M. Adding their variances gives VarN=μ+μ2/M, hence g(2)(0)=1+1/M. Averaging over many unresolved modes can make thermal pair counts approach the independent-count reference.

Photon bunching

Words: 75
Photon statistics OurBigBook.com
Photon bunching is enhanced near-coincident detection relative to an appropriate independent-count reference. It need not require nonclassical light. In a classical intensity model with nonnegative instantaneous light intensity I, ideal detection with a conditional Poisson distribution gives
g(2)(0)=⟨I⟩2⟨I2⟩​=1+⟨I⟩2VarI​≥1.
(9)
A fluctuating intensity therefore bunches counts. For a stationary classical intensity with finite second moment, the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality further gives ⟨I(t)I(t+τ)⟩≤⟨I2⟩. A zero-delay correlation smaller than one at a later delay cannot be explained by this model.

Photon antibunching

Words: 72
Photon statistics OurBigBook.com
Photon antibunching means suppression of close pairs compared with pairs at some later delay: g(2)(0)<g(2)(τ) for some τ. If correlations decay to the independent-count reference, g(2)(0)<1 is a sufficient signature. It violates the classical intensity bounds in photon bunching. For the specified single-mode number measurement, second-order optical coherence also shows that g(2)(0)<1 is equivalent to VarN<μ. This variance criterion concerns that mode or gate; it must not be applied unchanged to arbitrarily long integrated counting intervals.

Photon counting

Words: 553 Articles: 3
Quantum optics OurBigBook.com
Photon counting registers discrete detector events and their times. A beam splitter sends the selected light to two photodetectors; a coincidence circuit or time-recording system compares the outputs. With a vacuum input at the unused optical port, splitter fractions R,T and independent quantum efficiencies η1​,η2​, a selected input photon reaches the two recorded channels with probabilities p1​=Rη1​ and p2​=Tη2​. Conditional on input occupation N, each pair of distinct photons contributes p1​p2​ to the cross-channel pair moment. Therefore
⟨K1​⟩=p1​μ,⟨K2​⟩=p2​μ,⟨K1​K2​⟩=p1​p2​⟨N(N−1)⟩.
(10)
Their normalized ratio recovers g(2)(0). This assumes number-resolving counts, or sufficiently dilute gates for threshold detectors to approximate them. One photon cannot create both channel counts in this ideal model.

Coincidence counting

Words: 47
Photon counting OurBigBook.com
Count detections in two channels within a specified time window to estimate pair correlations. Delayed windows or independent singles rates estimate accidental coincidences. Finite efficiency, timing jitter, dark counts and multiple pairs must be included before interpreting a coincidence fringe as a property of the prepared state.

Finite-bin photon correlations (g​j(2)​=Cj​/(r1​r2​Tj​Δτ))

Words: 223
Photon counting OurBigBook.com
For stationary continuous detection, let Cj​ count all cross-channel pairs with t2​−t1​ in a bin of full width Δτ, and let r1​,r2​ be observed single-count rates. Let Tj​ be their joint live observation time at that delay, approximately the common duration Tobs​ when delays are short. Independent streams have expected pair count r1​r2​Tj​Δτ, giving the displayed estimator. It estimates a bin average of second-order optical coherence, further blurred by detector timing response. For a finite uninterrupted run the exact overlap weighting is Tobs​−∣τ∣ inside the delay integral, rather than a constant at long delays.
For an original numerical example, choose r1​=2000s−1, r2​=3000s−1, Tj​=100s, and Δτ=10ns. The independent reference is six pairs per bin. Chosen counts Cj​=3,6,12 give estimates 0.5,1,2; they are teaching inputs with large counting fluctuations, not historical measurements. For pulsed sources, compare properly normalized same-pulse and separate-pulse coincidences instead of assuming this continuous-source baseline.
Detector dead time suppresses nearby same-detector counts and can manufacture an apparent dip in a single-detector record. Two independent detector channels avoid that particular same-channel exclusion, but shared electronics can still reject pairs. Timing spread and wide bins wash out narrow features. A device recording only the first stop after each start measures a waiting-time distribution; interpreting it as all-pair correlations requires a sufficiently low stop probability over the relevant delay range.

Photon correlation loss and background model (gobs(2)​=1+ρ1​ρ2​(gsig(2)​−1))

Words: 138
Photon counting OurBigBook.com
Independent loss alone leaves normalized factorial moments invariant: if every input photon is retained with probability η, binomial thinning conditional on a total gives ⟨K⟩=ημ and ⟨K(K−1)⟩=η2⟨N(N−1)⟩. Both powers cancel from g(2)(0). Fewer coincidences still mean poorer statistical precision.
Now let each channel contain a signal rate si​ and an independent Poisson process background rate bi​, independent also of the signal. Put ρi​=si​/(si​+bi​). The pair density is s1​s2​gsig(2)​+s1​b2​+b1​s2​+b1​b2​. Dividing by (s1​+b1​)(s2​+b2​) proves the displayed formula, for the same bin and timing response. For chosen equal signal fractions ρ1​=ρ2​=4/5, ideal signal values 0,1,2 become 9/25,1,41/25. Background pulls both dips and peaks toward one.
This calculation assumes additive, mutually independent background counts. Scattered coherent light that interferes with the signal, correlated electronic noise, or recovery effects require a different model. Correcting a dip without independently estimating the backgrounds cannot establish photon antibunching.

Hanbury Brown and Twiss effect

Words: 1k Articles: 6
Quantum optics OurBigBook.com
Robert Hanbury Brown and Richard Q. Twiss sought angular sizes through correlations of detected light intensities. Their method multiplies detector outputs after detection, whereas a Michelson interferometer combines fields before detection.[ref]
A monochromatic discharge source illuminated a half-silvered beam splitter and two photodetectors. The averaged current product showed an excess near coincidence. Moving one detector introduced a delay that removed it, supplying a control. Slow response weakened the peak. This photon bunching is compatible with fluctuating classical light intensity.[ref]

Stellar intensity interferometry

Words: 1k Articles: 5
Hanbury Brown and Twiss effect OurBigBook.com
Stellar intensity interferometry measures a star's angular diameter by comparing fluctuations of detected light intensity at separated collectors. Each collector has its own photodetector; an analog multiplier or a digital correlator compares the electrical outputs. Changing the interferometric baseline changes the sampled spatial optical coherence. An unresolved star produces correlated fluctuations; a resolved extended source supplies different mixtures of emission directions, reducing their correlation.
This makes a useful telescope out of a surprising observable: the fluctuations discarded by an ordinary average-brightness measurement. Optical beams need not travel to a common combiner. A relative wave phase perturbation E2​↦eiϕE2​ leaves ∣E2​∣2 unchanged, so pure phase errors do not directly change the local light intensity. Electronic delay alignment remains necessary; intensity changes from atmospheric scintillation, collection losses, and instrumental noise still require controls.
Let b join the collectors and let s0​ be the unit line of sight toward the star. The relevant interferometric baseline is its projection onto the plane of the sky:
b⊥​=b−(b⋅s0​)s0​,B=∣b⊥​∣.
(11)
The reference arrival-time difference has magnitude ∣b⋅s0​∣/c, with speed of light c. Correct that delay, with a sign fixed by channel order, before averaging the residual correlation at τ=0. The physical collector separation equals B only when it is perpendicular to s0​; tracking a star can change the projection even for fixed collectors.

Sirius intensity interferometry experiment

Words: 142
Stellar intensity interferometry OurBigBook.com
In their 1956 Sirius report, Robert Hanbury Brown and Richard Q. Twiss used two 1.56-m searchlight reflectors feeding photomultipliers. The photocurrent fluctuations passed through 5–45-MHz amplifiers; an adjustable delay aligned arrival times. A linear mixer multiplied the outputs; an integrating motor accumulated their product. A second motor monitored its root-mean-square fluctuation to control gain changes. Separate lamps illuminated capped photomultipliers before and after observations to test electronic coupling and drift; blank-sky runs tested correlated sky background. They fitted a uniformly bright disk and an unknown zero-baseline scale to relative correlations at different interferometric baselines. Broadband response and possible baseline-dependent systematic errors limited the diameter inference.[ref]
This astronomical test extends the preceding laboratory Hanbury Brown and Twiss effect to two independently collected samples of a star. The historical motor reading was an instrument-specific correlation statistic. It should not be identified numerically with the normalized g(2) below without the corresponding measurement calibration.

Siegert relation (g12(2)​(τ)=1+∣g12(1)​(τ)∣2)

Words: 254
Stellar intensity interferometry OurBigBook.com
For one selected polarization of waves, write the detected complex fields as E1​(t),E2​(t+τ) and their light intensities as Ij​=∣Ej​∣2, absorbing a common conversion constant. With stationary ensemble averages and nonzero Iˉj​=⟨Ij​⟩, define
g12(1)​(B,τ)=Iˉ1​Iˉ2​​⟨E1∗​(t)E2​(t+τ)⟩​,g12(2)​(B,τ)=Iˉ1​Iˉ2​⟨I1​(t)I2​(t+τ)⟩​.
(12)
Here second-order optical coherence concerns distinct detector channels, with the corresponding normal ordering in the quantum formulation. Independent detection has g(2)=1. Subtracting the means gives the normalized covariance
Iˉ1​Iˉ2​⟨δI1​(t)δI2​(t+τ)⟩​=g12(2)​(B,τ)−1.
(13)
Thus multiplying raw photocurrents contains a large mean-product contribution; multiplying their fluctuations isolates the small excess. Independent detector shot noise does not supply a mean cross-channel excess, although it increases measurement uncertainty.
The displayed Siegert relation requires a zero-mean, jointly circular complex Gaussian random field, the usual chaotic thermal light model. Circular means invariance under a common arbitrary complex phase rotation; in particular ⟨Ei​Ej​⟩=0. Apply the Gaussian moment pairing theorem to the real and imaginary parts:
⟨E1∗​E1​E2∗​E2​⟩=⟨∣E1​∣2⟩⟨∣E2​∣2⟩+∣⟨E1∗​E2​⟩∣2.
(14)
The remaining pairing vanishes by circularity. Division proves the Siegert relation. Gaussianity is an assumption about fourth moments, not a consequence of merely measuring first-order optical coherence; a coherent state or photon antibunching cannot be interpreted by this thermal formula.
For equal-intensity beams combined in a common optical mode with matched wave polarizations, interferometric visibility is V=∣g12(1)​∣. Consequently ideal thermal stellar intensity interferometry measures g(2)−1=V2, while the full g(2) includes the independent-count reference one. Unpolarized thermal light with two independent equally populated wave polarizations has half this excess: adding their covariances doubles the numerator while multiplying total means quadruples the denominator. Finite detector response further suppresses the peak and must be calibrated.[ref]

Resonance fluorescence antibunching experiment

Words: 160
Quantum optics OurBigBook.com
In 1977, H. Jeff Kimble, Mario Dagenais, and Leonard Mandel excited a dilute sodium atomic beam with a resonant dye laser. Apertures restricted the observed atoms; a microscope objective collected fluorescence perpendicular to both beams. A beam splitter sent the collected light to two photomultipliers. Shaped pulses drove the start and stop inputs of a time-to-digital converter; its delay histogram rose away from zero delay, indicating photon antibunching. Turning the atomic beam off measured scattered-laser backgrounds. More than one contributing atom and scattered light limited the comparison with an ideal isolated two-level system; the paper explicitly treated corrections.[ref]
A simple reason for suppressed immediate re-emission is visible in an ideal two-level system. After spontaneous emission, the atom is in ∣g⟩. Its emission operator σ−​=∣g⟩⟨e∣ obeys σ−2​=0, so two instantaneous emissions are impossible. Excitation must rebuild the excited-state population before another emission. This argument explains the zero-delay idealization; the actual photon counting record additionally depends on collection, background, timing, and the number of contributing atoms.
