Entropy bound for a random-length binary string (H(M)≤H(L)+EL)

 Home Mathematics Area of mathematics Probability and statistics Information theory Information entropy
OurBigBook.com Words: 98
For a random finite binary string M of length L, conditioning on L=ℓ leaves at most 2ℓ possible strings. Thus its conditional entropy is at most ℓ. Since the length is a function of the string, the chain rule for information entropy gives
H(M)=H(L)+H(M∣L)≤H(L)+EL.
(2)
The term H(L) cannot generally be removed. For concatenations of m words from a fixed finite decipherable code with maximum length d, it is at most log2​(md+1). Decipherability preserves the Shannon entropy of the source block. For an independent source block, dividing the resulting inequality by m and taking m→∞ proves the expected codeword length is at least the one-symbol Shannon entropy.
