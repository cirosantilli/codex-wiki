Quotient topology

 Home Mathematics Area of mathematics Geometry and topology Topology
OurBigBook.com Words: 2k Articles: 16
The quotient topology turns an instruction to glue points together into a precise topological space. Start with a topological space (X,τX​) and an equivalence relation ∼. The quotient set X/∼ consists of equivalence classes, and its canonical projection of a quotient set is
q:X⟶X/∼,q(x)=[x].
(14)
The quotient topology is defined by the rule using preimages of open sets:
τq​={U⊆X/∼:q−1(U)∈τX​}.
(15)
Thus a subset of the quotient space is open if and only if its entire preimage is open in X. Each fiber q−1({[x]}) is the whole equivalence class [x], so the preimage includes every point glued to any of its members.[ref]
This is the finest topology making q a continuous map: every topology with that property can contain only subsets whose preimages are open. It really is a topology, because preimages preserve the empty set, the whole set, arbitrary unions, and finite intersections. The gluing example below shows why checking a complete preimage matters, especially at the seam.
The quotient topology also makes continuous maps out of a glued space easy to construct. A continuous map f:X→Z descends to a unique continuous map fˉ​:X/∼→Z exactly when it is constant on every fiber of q, meaning x∼y⇒f(x)=f(y). Define fˉ​([x])=f(x); the equivalence class representative cannot affect the answer. For every open set V⊆Z,
q−1(fˉ​−1(V))=f−1(V).
(16)
The right side is open, so the quotient topology makes fˉ​−1(V) open. Conversely, if f=fˉ​∘q for a continuous map fˉ​, then composition of continuous functions makes f continuous, and equal equivalence classes force equal values. This is the universal property of the quotient topology. [ref]
A surjective continuous map is not automatically a quotient map. On the same two-point set D={a,b}, take the identity map from the discrete topology to the indiscrete topology. It is continuous and surjective, but {a} is not open in the target even though its preimage is open in the domain. The quotient topology for this identity map would be the discrete topology. Thus continuity supplies only one direction of the rule using preimages of open sets; a quotient map requires both.
