Sequence ((xn​)n=1∞​)

 Home Mathematics Area of mathematics Analysis Real analysis Sequence and series
OurBigBook.com Words: 4k Articles: 64
A sequence assigns a value to each position in an ordered index list. For an infinite sequence in a set X, one standard convention is a function x:N>0​→X, written (xn​)n=1∞​ with xn​=x(n). Some applications start the index at zero, and a finite sequence has only finitely many positions. Values may repeat; both order and repetition matter. [ref].
For example, an​=(−1)n begins −1,1,−1,1,…, whereas bn​=(−1)n+1 begins 1,−1,1,−1,…. Their sets of values are both {−1,1}, but these are different sequences. A subsequence keeps selected positions in increasing order; the subsequence of an​ at its even integers is the constant sequence 1,1,1,…. A tail of a sequence retains all positions from one index onward.
Sequences express successive approximations, repeated observations, and the iterates of a recurrence relation. A limit of a sequence describes where its terms eventually concentrate. Finite observations alone cannot establish that behaviour: for any positive integer K, the all-zero sequence and the sequence with un​=0 for n≤K and un​=1 for n>K agree through index K, yet have different limits of sequences. A formula or bound controlling the entire future, such as the tail diameter of a sequence in the Cauchy sequence condition, supplies information that a finite list cannot.
