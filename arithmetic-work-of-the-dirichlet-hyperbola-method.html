Arithmetic work of the Dirichlet hyperbola method

 ... Mathematics Area of mathematics Number theory Arithmetic function Dirichlet convolution Dirichlet hyperbola method
OurBigBook.com Words: 186
The direct divisor summatory function formula evaluates N floor divisions. Its balanced Dirichlet hyperbola method formula evaluates r=⌊N​⌋ floor divisions and reuses their sum for the other strip, followed by doubling and square-overlap subtraction. Thus it uses O(N​) arithmetic operations, with big O notation, in a model that counts each division, addition and multiplication as one operation. At N=106, the division counts for these two formulas are 106 and 103.
For a general Dirichlet convolution, the asymmetric formula has ⌊A⌋+⌊B⌋ outer terms; evaluating F and G has an additional cost. Under AB=N, equal thresholds minimize A+B, but an asymmetric split can be useful if the two partial sums have different evaluation costs. The balanced divisor summatory function gains the further reuse because its two arithmetic functions are identical.
These are arithmetic operation counts, rather than counts of elementary operations on binary digits: the integers grow with N, and division of longer integers costs more in that model of computational complexity theory. Computing r also requires an exact integer square root, or checking and correcting a numerical square root until r2≤N<(r+1)2 holds.
