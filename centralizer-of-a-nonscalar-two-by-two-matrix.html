Centralizer of a nonscalar two by two matrix (AB=BA⟺B=αI+βA)

 ... Algebra Linear algebra Vector space Linear map Linear operator Commuting operators
OurBigBook.com Words: 108
For any real nonscalar 2×2 matrix A, its commuting matrices are exactly B=αI+βA with α,β∈R. There is a vector v for which v,Av form a basis: otherwise every nonzero vector would be an eigenvector, and applying this to two independent vectors and their sum would force A to be scalar. Given a commuting B, uniquely write Bv=αv+βAv. Then BAv=ABv=(αI+βA)Av, so B and αI+βA agree on a basis and are equal. Conversely every polynomial in A commutes with it. This generalizes the centralizer of a planar quarter-turn; the scalar-matrix exception is essential, because every matrix commutes with a scalar matrix.
