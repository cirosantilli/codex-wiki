Dominance from a nonzero column antisymmetrizer

OurBigBook.com Words: 97
Let t be a Young tableau of shape λ, and let u be a Young tableau of shape μ, both on n entries. If the Column antisymmetrizer of a Young tableau bt​ satisfies bt​{u}=0 over any field, then λ dominates μ in the dominance order on partitions.
If two entries from one column of t lie in the same row of u, their transposition fixes the tabloid {u}. Pairing terms of bt​ by this transposition gives bt​{u}=0, including in characteristic two. Otherwise the first r rows of u contain at most min(r,λj′​) entries from column j of t. Thus
∑i=1r​μi​≤∑j​min(r,λj′​)=∑i=1r​λi​
(34)
for every r, proving dominance.
