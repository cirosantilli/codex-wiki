Arithmetic series (Sn​=∑k=1n​ak​)

 Home Mathematics Area of mathematics Arithmetic Arithmetic progression
OurBigBook.com Words: 414 Articles: 2
An arithmetic series adds the sequence terms of an arithmetic progression. Its n-th partial sum is Sn​=∑k=1n​ak​, with the empty sum convention S0​=0. For n≥1,
Sn​=2n(a1​+an​)​=na1​+2n(n−1)​d.​
(38)
Thus the total is the number of sequence terms multiplied by the arithmetic mean of the two endpoints. The formula also covers decreasing and constant arithmetic progressions.[ref]
For the eight-entry example, the sum is 8(7+28)/2=140, and the arithmetic mean of all eight entries is 17.5. A decreasing example 11,9,7,5,3,1 gives 6(11+1)/2=36. Negative values cause no algebraic difficulty: 3,1,−1,−3,−5 has sum 5(3−5)/2=−5.
The triangular number Tn​=1+2+⋯+n is the special case a1​=d=1, giving n(n+1)/2. More generally, an equal increase in each successive allocation makes the total grow as a quadratic function of the number of allocations. The eight-entry example has
ak​=3k+4,Sn​=23n2+11n​.
(39)
Its first four entries add to 46, its first eight to 140, and its first twelve to 282. The sequence terms lie on a straight line when plotted against their sequence indices, while the partial sums follow a quadratic function.
