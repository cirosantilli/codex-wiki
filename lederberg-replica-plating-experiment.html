Lederberg replica plating experiment

 Home Biology Genetics Molecular genetics Replica plating
OurBigBook.com Words: 167
Joshua Lederberg and Esther Lederberg reported replica plating in 1952. They autoclave-sterilized velveteen and secured it pile-up on a wood or cork cylinder with a metal hoop. Inverted agar plates touched the fabric lightly. Pencil marks or pin indentations fixed orientation. Complete-medium and minimal-medium replicas compared growth of biological colonies.[ref]
Escherichia coli W-1 grew on plain agar; serial T-1 bacteriophage replicas showed resistant biological colonies at matching sites. Corresponding unexposed master regions seeded repeated broth-and-plain-agar enrichment, yielding resistant isolates. Stray-bacteriophage checks were negative; resistance persisted without challenge. This supported preexisting heritable resistance. The authors noted variable transfer and moisture-dependent resolution.[ref]
The Luria-Delbrück experiment uses variation between independently grown cell cultures to investigate the timing of resistant mutations. The Lederberg replica plating experiment adds a spatial route to a recoverable source: the tested samples identify an unexposed lineage. These are complementary arguments about mutation and natural selection. Growth readouts establish a tested phenotype; identifying the responsible nucleotide sequence requires other evidence, such as DNA sequencing.
