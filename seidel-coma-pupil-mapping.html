Seidel coma pupil mapping (w=hρ3cosθ)

 ... Branches of physics Optics Geometrical optics Optical aberration Seidel aberration Coma (optics)
OurBigBook.com Words: 537
Consider an original, explicitly normalized model of primary coma, rather than a prescription for a particular optical lens. Let (u,v) be Cartesian coordinates in a circular optical pupil divided by its radius a, with u2+v2≤1. Let h=α/α0​ be a signed small field angle divided by an arbitrary positive reference angle. With polar coordinates u=ρcosθ, v=ρsinθ, prescribe the wavefront error
W(u,v)=W0​w(u,v),w(u,v)=hρ3cosθ=hu(u2+v2).
(30)
Here W0​ is an arbitrary positive optical path difference scale, not a measured error. The cubic optical pupil dependence and linear field dependence are the classical Seidel form.[ref]
In the paraxial approximation, light ray direction errors follow the transverse gradient of wavefront error. For a reference sphere a distance L from the exit optical pupil in a uniform image medium of refractive index n, the corresponding image-error scale is s=LW0​/(na). Choose image-error coordinate signs so the normalized displacement is ∇w; reversing the convention reverses the plotted orientation. Image coordinates below are errors relative to the ideal image of this particular object point, rather than positions of different objects:
X=∂u∂w​=h(3u2+v2),Y=∂v∂w​=2huv.
(31)
The partial derivatives explain the asymmetry: opposite optical pupil points (u,v) and (−u,−v) land together, rather than on opposite sides of the ideal image. For h>0, every X is nonnegative, while Y can have either sign. The figures stipulate this single wavefront error, omit other optical aberrations and diffraction, and assign no instrument dimensions.
For a ring of fixed optical pupil radius ρ, the double-angle formulas for trigonometric functions give
X=hρ2(2+cos2θ),Y=hρ2sin2θ,(X−2hρ2)2+Y2=h2ρ4.
(32)
Thus each ring maps into a circle of radius ∣h∣ρ2 whose centre is displaced by 2hρ2. Doubling ρ quadruples both sizes; for h=1, the edge ring has centre (2,0) and radius 1, while the half-radius ring has centre (1/2,0) and radius 1/4. Superposing these displaced circles produces the comet-like envelope.
For uniform irradiance over the circular optical pupil, equal area samples give an equal-weight geometrical image. Its centroid is (h,0) because ⟨u2⟩=⟨v2⟩=1/4 and ⟨uv⟩=0. Moving the coordinate origin to that centroid translates the image; it does not remove its asymmetric shape. Adding a linear term to w likewise translates all light rays equally. These origin choices must be distinguished from changing the optical aberration itself.
The model's full transverse extent is 3∣h∣ along X and 2∣h∣ along Y. Halving the field angle halves the blur, and reversing it reverses the pattern about the ideal image. This isolates why coma matters for an optical camera's usable field of view: collecting more light does not ensure that off-axis points remain compact. Near diffraction-limited resolution, a physical optics calculation of the point spread function is needed instead of interpreting the geometrical tip as an infinitely sharp image.[ref]
