Cesàro mean (n1​∑k=1n​ak​)

 ... Mathematics Area of mathematics Analysis Real analysis Sequence and series Convergent sequence
OurBigBook.com Words: 345 Articles: 3
The Cesàro mean of the first n≥1 terms of a sequence is their arithmetic mean. It is a cumulative average: increasing n includes the whole initial segment, rather than a fixed-length sliding window. For ak​=(−1)k, the even-length averages are zero and the odd-length averages are −1/n, so they tend to zero even though the sequence does not converge.
For a periodic sequence with period p≥1 and one-cycle arithmetic mean M, split an initial segment into full cycles and a remainder of fewer than p terms. The full cycles contribute exactly M per term; the remainder contributes a fixed bounded error divided by n. Thus the Cesàro means tend to M. The cycle 0,2,1 in the bounded partial sums need not be Cesàro summable illustration has arithmetic mean one. Treating it as a sequence of partial sums gives a series with first-order Cesàro sum one; averaging a sequence and summing its terms remain different operations.
