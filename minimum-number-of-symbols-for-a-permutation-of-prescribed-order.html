Minimum number of symbols for a permutation of prescribed order (μ(m)=∑pa∥m​pa(m>1))

 Home Mathematics Area of mathematics Combinatorics Permutation Order of a permutation
OurBigBook.com Words: 149
Write the prime factorization of m>1 as m=q1​⋯qt​, with each qj​ a positive power of a distinct prime number. The least n such that Sn​ contains a permutation of order m is q1​+⋯+qt​.
For existence, take disjoint permutation cycles of lengths qj​. Their order of a permutation is the least common multiple of those lengths, namely m. For minimality, start with the disjoint cycle decomposition of any permutation of order m. Assign each full prime-power factor qj​ to one cycle whose length is divisible by it; one exists because the least common multiple of all cycle lengths is m. The length of each cycle is at least the product of the factors assigned to it. For integers a,b≥2, ab≥a+b, so a product of at least two assigned factors is at least their sum; the assertion is immediate for one factor. Summing over cycles gives at least ∑j​qj​ symbols. In particular an element of order 60=4⋅3⋅5 requires at least twelve symbols.
