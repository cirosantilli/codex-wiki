Retarded delay differential equation (y′(t)=f(t,y(t),y(t−τ)))

 Home Mathematics Area of mathematics Analysis Differential equation Delay differential equation
OurBigBook.com Words: 713 Articles: 2
For a single constant delay τ>0, a retarded delay differential equation has the displayed form: delayed values of the unknown function enter, but delayed first derivatives do not. The unknown can also be a vector.[ref]
Here t denotes time, and the rule applies for t>0, with a right derivative at 0. On 0≤t≤τ, the argument t−τ lies in the prescribed past. Once this interval is known, it supplies the delayed values for τ≤t≤2τ. This is the method of steps for a delay differential equation: solve successive ordinary differential equations with a known delayed input, keeping each new endpoint as the next initial condition. For a continuous function f locally Lipschitz continuous in its two state arguments, locally uniformly in t, this gives local existence and uniqueness for each continuous history function of a delay differential equation, while the solution of a differential equation remains in the domain of f. A nonlinear rule can still produce finite-time blowup.
