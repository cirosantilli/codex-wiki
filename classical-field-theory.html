Global symmetry in field theory

Words: 117 Articles: 1
Classical field theory OurBigBook.com
A global symmetry has transformation parameters constant across spacetime and preserves the action. It can relate physically distinct states. Continuous global symmetries give Noether currents and conserved charges when boundary fluxes vanish; discrete symmetries do not receive currents from the same infinitesimal theorem. A gauge redundancy instead uses local parameters to relate descriptions of the same physical state, except that nontrivial boundary transformations may carry physical charges. The global phase symmetry of a complex scalar field is an important internal example.

Scattering charge selection rule from global gauge symmetry ([Q,S]=0 ⟹ (Qf​−Qi​)⟨f∣S∣i⟩=0)

Words: 48
Global symmetry in field theory OurBigBook.com
For a conserved charge Q and invariant vacuum, its global symmetry commutes with the S-matrix. Matrix elements between charge eigenstates give the displayed selection rule. Constant electromagnetic phases cancel at every QED vertex, and nonzero physical amplitudes conserve total charge. Local gauge invariance strengthens this global rule to the Ward-Takahashi identity.

Gauge theory

Words: 334 Articles: 7
Classical field theory OurBigBook.com
A classical field theory is a gauge theory when its description has a local redundancy represented by gauge transformations. A gauge field defines the covariant derivative that compares matter fields at different points, and its gauge field strength measures the curvature of that comparison. Electromagnetism is the familiar Abelian example; Yang-Mills theory generalizes it to a non-Abelian internal group. Both classical equations and their quantum field theory can have this structure. Physically equivalent gauge descriptions must give the same observables, even though the gauge potential changes.

Chiral gauge theory

Gauge theory OurBigBook.com
A gauge theory whose left- and right-chiral fermions transform differently under its gauge group. The weak charged current gives a physical example. A chiral representation assignment must satisfy the relevant gauge anomaly cancellation conditions to define a consistent quantum theory.

Lattice gauge theory

Words: 64
Gauge theory OurBigBook.com
A formulation of a gauge theory with discretized spacetime. Matter fields are assigned to sites and gauge-group elements to links between nearby sites. Products of link variables around closed paths provide gauge-invariant quantities. This structure preserves the local gauge symmetry while making nonperturbative numerical calculations possible. Physical predictions require control of finite spacing, finite volume and statistical errors rather than treating one finite grid as the continuum theory.

Noether gauging procedure (δS0​=∫jμ∂μ​ϵ)

Words: 65
Gauge theory OurBigBook.com
Localizing a parameter of a global symmetry produces its Noether current times a derivative of that parameter. Introduce a gauge field with an inhomogeneous transformation and couple it to the Noether current to cancel this variation. Recompute the remaining variation and add the required interaction and transformation terms. Closure of the local symmetry algebra constrains the completion. For supersymmetry, its supercurrent couples to the gravitino, and the translation in the supersymmetry algebra requires a vierbein.

Internal symmetry of a classical field theory

Words: 214 Articles: 2
Classical field theory OurBigBook.com
An internal symmetry changes field values while leaving spacetime coordinates fixed, preserving the action. A constant phase rotation of a complex scalar field is an example. This distinguishes internal charges from the spacetime translation charges in the canonical stress-energy tensor. Localizing the parameter generally requires gauge fields; a global internal symmetry is not automatically a gauge symmetry.

O(4) symmetry of a complex scalar doublet

Words: 78
Internal symmetry of a classical field theory OurBigBook.com
Two canonically normalized complex scalar fields with potential V=m2Φ†Φ+λ(Φ†Φ)2 are four real fields with a radial potential. Consequently the isolated action is invariant under the orthogonal group O(4), not only under complex-linear U(2) transformations. Writing ϕ1​=(q1​+iq2​)/2​ and ϕ2​=(q3​+iq4​)/2​ makes the kinetic term ∑A​(∂qA​)2/2 and the potential a function of ∑A​qA2​. The six real-plane rotations give currents jABμ​=qB​∂μqA​−qA​∂μqB​. Gauge or Yukawa couplings can break this larger symmetry, so it should not be asserted for an arbitrary interacting doublet.

Variational symmetry of a Lagrangian density

Words: 54
Classical field theory OurBigBook.com
A differentiable infinitesimal transformation is a variational symmetry when its change of the Lagrangian density is a divergence, δL=ε∂μ​Kμ. Under suitable boundary conditions the action is invariant, so exact pointwise invariance is unnecessary. The off-shell variation identity gives the Noether current for a first-derivative scalar field. This definition is for a constant transformation parameter; a spacetime-dependent parameter is a different, gauge-symmetry problem.

Scalar field

Words: 699 Articles: 16
Classical field theory OurBigBook.com
A scalar field assigns one scalar to each point of a specified space, such as Euclidean space or spacetime. For example, F(x,y)=x2+2y2 is a real-valued function on a plane, and its change along a moving observation is given by the derivative along a curve. Under a change of coordinates, a scalar field's value at the same physical point is unchanged.

Zero-dimensional quartic perturbation series (I(λ)/I(0)=1−λ/(8m4)+35λ2/(384m8)+⋯)

Words: 121
Zero-dimensional scalar field theory OurBigBook.com
For m2>0, the zero-dimensional scalar field theory with Euclidean action m2x2/2+λx4/4! has a Gaussian integral normalization I(0)=2π/m2​. Its Feynman rules assign 1/m2 to each propagator, −λ to each quartic vertex, and the reciprocal Feynman-diagram symmetry factor to each vacuum diagram. The integral itself includes disconnected vacuum diagrams; its logarithm selects connected ones. At second order the three possible numbers of edges joining the two vertices are zero, two and four, with Feynman-diagram symmetry factors 128,16,48 respectively. Their weights add to 35λ2/(384m8).
The Wick contractions give the general coefficient I(0)(−1)v(4v−1)!!/[v!(4!)vm4v]. Successive absolute coefficients have ratio (4v+3)(4v+1)/[24(v+1)m4], which tends to infinity. Thus this is an asymptotic expansion as λ↓0, rather than a convergent power series. For every fixed truncation the Taylor theorem remainder estimate for e−u, u≥0, can be integrated against the Gaussian distribution to justify the corresponding remainder bound.

Flat direction of a scalar potential

Words: 82
Scalar potential OurBigBook.com
In vacuum analysis, an exact flat direction of a scalar potential is a nonconstant continuous family of degenerate stationary vacua. The scalar potential stays constant along this family and its gradient vanishes there. An ordinary level set does not suffice: V(x,y)=x2+y2 is constant on every circle, but its vacuum at the origin has no flat direction. A vanishing eigenvalue of the quadratic Hessian matrix also does not suffice: V(x)=x4 has zero quadratic mass at zero but no family of nearby vacua. Exact flat directions can be lifted by corrections absent from the classical scalar potential.
