QR decomposition (A=QR)

 ... Mathematics Area of mathematics Algebra Linear algebra Inner product Gram-Schmidt process
OurBigBook.com Words: 576 Articles: 5
A reduced QR decomposition of an m×k matrix over the real numbers with full column rank, m≥k, writes A=QR, where Q is m×k with orthonormal columns and R is a k×k upper triangular matrix with positive diagonal. Applying the Gram-Schmidt process to the columns vj​ of A constructs Q=(q1​ ⋯ qk​) and records the coefficients
rij​=⎩⎨⎧​⟨vj​,qi​⟩,∥uj​∥,0,​i<j,i=j,i>j.​vj​=∑i≤j​rij​qi​.
(37)
This last column identity is exactly the matrix multiplication A=QR. The upper triangular matrix records that the jth input uses only the first j new directions, while its positive diagonal records the norms of the nonzero orthogonal projection residuals. Since QTQ=I, one also has R=QTA; QT denotes the transpose and I the identity matrix. Over the complex numbers, use the conjugate transpose Q∗ in these identities.
For the three-dimensional Gram-Schmidt process example, the exact matrices are
A=​110​101​011​​,Q=​1/2​1/2​0​1/6​−1/6​2/6​​−1/3​1/3​1/3​​​,
(38)
R=​2​00​1/2​3/2​0​1/2​1/6​2/3​​​.
(39)
For example, the last column reconstructs as
2​q1​​+6​q2​​+3​2q3​​=21​(1,1,0)T+61​(1,−1,2)T+32​(−1,1,1)T=(0,1,1)T.
(40)
The diagonal entries are approximately 1.414214,1.224745,1.154701. Each is a norm of the new direction before it was scaled to length one.
For a linear least-squares problem, the orthogonal projection of b onto the column space of A is QQTb. Hence the minimizing coefficients solve the linear system Rx=QTb by back substitution. Let A2​=(v1​ v2​) consist of the first two columns of the example; its reduced factors are Q2​=(q1​ q2​) and the leading 2×2 block R2​ of R. For b=(0,0,1)T, the linear system R2​x=Q2T​b is
(2​0​1/2​3/2​​)(x1​x2​​)=(02/6​​),x2​=32​,x1​=−31​.
(41)
Thus A2​x=(1/3,−1/3,2/3)T, agreeing with the orthogonal projection computed above, and the minimum Euclidean norm of A2​x−b is 1/3​. The QR decomposition separates building an orthonormal basis from recovering coefficients in the original basis.[ref]
