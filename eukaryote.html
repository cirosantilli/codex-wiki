Eukaryote

 Home Biology Organism
OurBigBook.com Words: 2k Articles: 19
A eukaryote is an organism belonging to the lineage characterized by a membrane-bounded cell nucleus and extensive cellular compartmentalization. A nuclear envelope surrounds the nuclear genome; nuclear chromosomes are typically linear. Ribosomes perform biological translation outside the cell nucleus. See OpenStax Microbiology, 3.4: nuclear organization and protein synthesis. Specialized biological cells can lose their cell nucleus, as mature mammalian red blood cells do, while remaining parts of eukaryotic organisms; see OpenStax Biology 2e, 40.2: mature red blood cells.
Yeasts, plants and animals are all eukaryotes. Typical cells in these groups have mitochondria, but chloroplasts belong to photosynthetic plant and algae lineages, not to every eukaryote. A cell wall occurs in typical plant and fungus cells but is absent from animal cells. These are comparisons of representative cells, not a checklist every specialized cell must satisfy. See OpenStax Biology 2e, 4.3: nucleus, organelles and representative cells.
The interesting feature is controlled separation: cell membranes and organelle membranes let different cellular processes occupy distinct compartments while membrane transport connects them. The evolution of eukaryotes also involved partnership: mitochondria descend from a bacterial endosymbiosis. Modern phylogenetics supports an archaeal ancestry for the nuclear lineage, alongside this bacterial contribution; see two-domain system. Thus the history of a whole eukaryote cannot be read as the history of just one of its genes.
