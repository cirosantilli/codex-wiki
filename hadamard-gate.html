Hadamard gate (H)

 Home Physics Branches of physics Quantum theory Hadamard transform
OurBigBook.com Words: 367 Articles: 2
The Hadamard gate is the unitary operator
H=2​1​(11​1−1​),H2=I,
(2)
where I is the identity operator. It maps the computational basis to the Hadamard basis:
∣0⟩↦2​∣0⟩+∣1⟩​,∣1⟩↦2​∣0⟩−∣1⟩​.
(3)
These outputs have equal computational-basis measurement probabilities, but their relative quantum phase differs. Applying another Hadamard gate sends them to different definite outputs. Calling this gate a classical coin toss would discard precisely the information that makes this possible. [ref].
Starting from ∣0⟩, compare two quantum circuits, reading matrix products from right to left:
HH∣0⟩=∣0⟩,HZH∣0⟩=∣1⟩.
(4)
Here Z is the Pauli Z gate. After the first Hadamard gate, the probability amplitudes are (1/2​,1/2​). Inserting Z changes them to (1/2​,−1/2​) without changing their squared magnitudes. At the final gate, the contributions to output zero cancel and those to output one add. Without Z, cancellation occurs at output one instead. This is controlled interference, with the quantum phase determining which answer emerges.
The same calculation works for any phase gate Pϕ​=diag(1,eiϕ):
HPϕ​H∣0⟩=21+eiϕ​∣0⟩+21−eiϕ​∣1⟩,P(1)=sin2(ϕ/2),P(0)=cos2(ϕ/2).
(5)
For example, ϕ=π/3 gives P(1)=1/4, while ϕ=π/2 gives P(1)=1/2. The output-one amplitude at ϕ=π/2 is (1−i)/2: its two complex contributions add before the Born rule is applied. A phase gate alone leaves the populations unchanged; recombination converts its relative quantum phase into observable probabilities.
If quantum decoherence completely removes the computational basis coherence between the gates, the intermediate density matrix becomes I/2. Both the phase gate and final Hadamard gate leave I/2 unchanged, so P(1)=1/2 for every ϕ. The disappearing fringe explains why preserving quantum coherence matters: it enables the gate sequence to carry phase information into the final measurement.
