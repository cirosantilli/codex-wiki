Local error estimator (en+1​)

 Home Mathematics Area of mathematics Analysis Numerical analysis Error control for ODE solvers
OurBigBook.com Words: 1k Articles: 5
A local error estimator approximates a numerical step's defect from the exact solution starting at the same input. Differences between formulas estimate error only after their asymptotic orders and constants are accounted for. Step-doubling error estimation, an embedded Runge-Kutta pair and predictor-corrector error estimation are common constructions. They do not by themselves bound accumulated global error.
Let Φh​(tn​,yn​) be the exact endpoint of the ordinary differential equation started at the numerical value yn​, and let Yn+1​ be the candidate numerical endpoint. The signed local defect being estimated is ℓn+1​=Φh​(tn​,yn​)−Yn+1​. In contrast, actual global error compares Yn+1​ with the solution of the original initial value problem, including all previous steps. The local truncation error convention inserts original exact data instead. These starting-value distinctions matter even when the same formula is used for every step. A practical local error estimator also relies on a smooth Taylor expansion; a small difference can reflect accidental cancellation rather than a rigorous upper bound.
