Turán's theorem (ex(n,Kr+1​)=tr​(n))

 Home Mathematics Area of mathematics Foundations of mathematics Graph theory Extremal graph theory
OurBigBook.com Words: 958 Articles: 9
How many edges can we retain while excluding a prescribed clique? For integers r≥1 and n≥0, every simple graph on n vertices containing no complete subgraph Kr+1​ satisfies
e(G)≤tr​(n):=e(Tr​(n)).
(7)
Equality holds exactly for the Turán graph Tr​(n), up to graph isomorphism. Thus the extremal number is ex(n,Kr+1​)=tr​(n).[ref]
For example, T3​(8) has independent sets of sizes 3,3,2 and 21 edges, as counted in Turán graph. It contains triangles in a graph, but no four-vertex clique. Consequently any simple graph on eight vertices with 22 edges contains K4​, while 21 edges alone do not force one. Excluding larger cliques permits denser graphs; Mantel theorem is precisely the first nontrivial case.
A short proof outline uses Zykov symmetrization. If an extremizer is not a complete multipartite graph, it has vertices x,y,z with xy,xz absent and yz present. If either y or z has larger vertex degree than x, clone that vertex in place of x. Otherwise replace both y,z by nonadjacent clones of x, gaining 2d(x)−d(y)−d(z)+1>0 edges. Both contradict extremality; balancing the resulting at most r parts gives Tr​(n).[ref]
The vertex cloning in a graph step preserves clique exclusion because any clique using a clone can substitute its original vertex; nonadjacent clones cannot occur together in a clique. At most r parts can be nonempty, since selecting one vertex from each gives a clique. If fewer than r parts are nonempty and one has at least two vertices, splitting it creates more edges, so it cannot be extremal. For r=1 the bound is zero. For r≥n, the Turán graph is Kn​, and the bound is the total (2n​) possible edges.
