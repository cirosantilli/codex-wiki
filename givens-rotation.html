Givens rotation (Gij​(c,s))

 Home Mathematics Area of mathematics Analysis Numerical analysis
OurBigBook.com Words: 1k Articles: 4
A Givens rotation eliminates one component of a vector by rotating just two coordinates, preserving its Euclidean norm. For example,
(3/5−4/5​4/53/5​)(34​)=(50​).
(111)
The length remains five: elimination moves the component into the retained coordinate rather than discarding it. Applied to a matrix, the same operation mixes two rows; QR decomposition by Givens rotations uses these local operations to build an upper triangular matrix.
For indices i<j, the matrix Gij​(c,s) is the identity matrix except for its block on coordinates i,j:
G​i,j​=(c−s​sc​),c2+s2=1.
(112)
This article uses left matrix multiplication on column vectors. If c=cosθ and s=sinθ, the block is a rotation matrix through angle −θ: the minus sign belongs in the lower left. Its transpose reverses the rotation. Direct multiplication gives GTG=I and determinant one, so it is an orthogonal matrix; consequently ∥Gx∥22​=xTGTGx=∥x∥22​. Only two coordinates change, but this Euclidean norm identity holds for the entire vector.
