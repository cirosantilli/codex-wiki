Quantum state (ρ)

 Home Physics Branches of physics Quantum mechanics Quantum system
OurBigBook.com Words: 12k Articles: 111
A quantum state describes a preparation through its predicted measurement in quantum mechanics probabilities. In the Hilbert space formulation, a pure state is a quantum state ray: a normalized vector representative ∣ψ⟩ satisfies ⟨ψ∣ψ⟩=1, and representatives differing by a global phase describe the same quantum state. [ref]
Both pure states and mixed states are represented by a density operator on the chosen complex Hilbert space:
ρ=ρ†,⟨v∣ρ∣v⟩≥0 for every ∣v⟩,Trρ=1.
(1)
Thus ρ is Hermitian, positive semidefinite and of unit trace; in infinite dimension it must also be a trace-class operator. For a normalized pure state, ρ=∣ψ⟩⟨ψ∣ is a rank-one orthogonal projection. A mixed state has no single such vector representative. Its density matrix is the matrix of ρ in a chosen orthonormal basis. [ref] For a discrete POVM with effects Ea​≥0 and ∑a​Ea​=I, the Born rule reads
P(a)=Tr(ρEa​),P(a)=⟨ψ∣Ea​∣ψ⟩ for a pure state.
(2)
These constraints and Born rule probabilities describe arbitrary finite-dimensional preparations. [ref]
Randomly choosing normalized pure states with probabilities wj​ and forgetting the label gives
ρ=∑j​wj​∣ψj​⟩⟨ψj​∣,wj​≥0,∑j​wj​=1.
(3)
This quantum state ensemble averages probabilities; a quantum superposition adds probability amplitudes. Several ensembles can give the same density operator. Quantum entanglement can make a reduced density matrix mixed while the joint quantum state is pure. [ref]
The preparation, the choice of basis, and the measurement are separate ingredients. A wavefunction ψ(x)=⟨x∣ψ⟩ is the position representation of a pure state; a general mixed state requires a density operator. For a closed quantum system, a unitary operator evolves it as ρ↦UρU†, preserving its eigenvalues and purity of a density operator. [ref] The Bloch sphere and Bloch ball give the qubit geometry; quantum state tomography connects repeated preparations to an estimated density matrix; projective measurement treats the conditional state after an outcome.
