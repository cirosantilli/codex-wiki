Slow steady gyroscope precession (Ωslow​≃Mgd/(I3​ω3​))

 ... Physics Branches of physics Classical mechanics Momentum Angular momentum Gyroscope
OurBigBook.com Words: 358 Articles: 1
Model the assembly as a heavy symmetric top with a fixed frictionless support, a uniform gravitational field, and centre of mass on its symmetry axis. Assume constant spin, negligible damping, and initial conditions prepared for uniform precession of a heavy symmetric top. Large spin means the transverse angular momentum from precession is small: I1​∣Ω∣sinθ≪∣I3​ω3​∣. Also require ∣Ω∣≪∣ω3​∣. A large spin rate alone does not select steady motion after an arbitrary release.
For an original illustrative calculation, stipulate
M=2.0kg,d=0.15m,g=9.81m/s2,I3​=0.080kgm2,I1​=I2​=0.090kgm2,ω3​=100rad/s,θ=60∘.
(17)
The transverse principal moments of inertia I1​,I2​ are about the pivot, including the parallel axis theorem contribution. These inputs give
Ls​=8.0kgm2/s,K=Mgd=2.943Nm,∣τO​∣=Ksin60∘=2.5487Nm,
(18)
Ω0​=K/Ls​=0.367875rad/s,T0​=2π/Ω0​≃17.080s.
(19)
The ratio Ω0​/ω3​=0.00368 is small; the more direct angular momentum ratio I1​Ω0​sin60∘/Ls​=0.00358 is small too. These are model calculations, not experimental results.
Check the approximation against the existing exact uniform precession of a heavy symmetric top condition. Its total angular momentum is
LO​=I1​Ω(z−cosθe)+Ls​e.
(20)
At constant θ,Ω,Ls​, differentiating this expression and equating it to the gravitational torque gives
I1​cosθΩ2−Ls​Ω+K=0.
(21)
For Ls​>0 the slow solution can be evaluated without subtracting two nearly equal numbers:
Ωslow​=Ls​+Ls2​−4I1​Kcosθ​2K​≃0.368639rad/s.
(22)
It exceeds Ω0​ by about 0.208%. The other solution is about 177.409rad/s and violates the assumed slow precession; the quadratic's two roots must not be conflated. Its discriminant must be nonnegative for these steady solutions to exist.
The original curves hold I1​,I3​,θ fixed and vary spin and the imposed torque coefficient K in τO​=Kz×e. For gravity alone, K=Mgd; the other curves are hypothetical changes in the transverse torque, not observations of this wheel. Solid curves use the slow quadratic root; dashed curves use K/(I3​ω3​). The second panel displays their relative difference. Increasing spin improves this approximation for the parameters shown. It does not remove nutation caused by inappropriate initial conditions.
