Cavity in a conductor

 Home Physics Branches of physics Electromagnetism Electrostatics Conductor in electrostatic equilibrium
OurBigBook.com Words: 145 Articles: 1
A cavity in a conductor is a void surrounded by an electrical conductor; completely enclosed is the essential qualification in exact electrostatic shielding arguments. At electrostatic equilibrium, the surrounding connected metal is an equipotential. An empty closed cavity in a conductor has constant electric potential and zero electric field, even if the metal is isolated and charged. An occupied closed cavity in a conductor generally has a nonzero electric field; its inner-wall total electric charge cancels the total electric charge of the internal sources. These are distinct statements, illustrated by Faraday's ice pail experiment.[ref]
