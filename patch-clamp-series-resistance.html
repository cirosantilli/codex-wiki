Patch-clamp series resistance (Rs​)

 Home Biology Neuroscience Patch clamp Whole-cell recording
OurBigBook.com Words: 195
Patch-clamp series resistance, also called patch-clamp access resistance, is the effective electrical resistance between the pipette's sensed voltage and the cell interior. It includes tip access and any remaining obstruction after break-in. For injected electric current I into the biological cell, an ideal resistive access path gives
Vp​−V=IRs​.
(8)
Thus 1nA through 20MΩ produces a 20mV difference: a substantial possible voltage clamp error. The electric current in this equation is the access-path current, excluding any bypass seal leak current. In current clamp, the same access drop contaminates the pipette voltage during injection. Bridge compensation subtracts an estimated IRs​; an estimate that changes during recording leaves an error.
An independent passive voltage clamp calculation makes both speed and error visible. Write v=V−EL​ and vp​=Vp​−EL​, with fixed leak reversal potential EL​, constant membrane electrical resistance Rm​ and membrane capacitance Cm​. Assume an ideal command at the pipette node and omit seal leak current and pipette capacitance. Electric charge conservation gives
Cm​dtdv​=Rs​vp​−v​−Rm​v​.
(9)
For a step vp​=ΔV from v(0)=0, the ordinary differential equation gives
v(t)=ΔVRs​+Rm​Rm​​(1−e−t/τvc​),τvc​=Cm​Rs​+Rm​Rs​Rm​​.
(10)
With the figure's parameters and ΔV=10mV, the time constant is 0.909ms, the final membrane rise is 9.091mV, and access-path electric current falls from 500 to 45.45pA. Only when Rm​≫Rs​ does the time constant approach Rs​Cm​ and the final voltage approach the command. Actual voltage clamp also depends on amplifier feedback control and the space clamp problem.
