Cell cycle

 Home Biology Biological cell
OurBigBook.com Words: 3k Articles: 60
The cell cycle is the sequence of events by which a biological cell prepares for and completes division, including DNA replication and distribution of chromosomes. In Saccharomyces cerevisiae, changing bud appearance provides an accessible readout; see the Burgess, Powers and Mell account and Hartwell, Culotti and Reid's primary study cited there.
For a typical cycling diploid eukaryotic biological cell, the order is G1 phase, S phase, G2 phase, then M phase. DNA replication doubles nuclear DNA amount during S phase; mitosis distributes the copies, and cytokinesis separates the daughters. The useful distinction is between copying a library and allocating its copies: DNA replication changes how much material exists, whereas division changes which biological cell contains it.
Gene expression continues to supply RNA and proteins as the biological cell grows; it is different from duplicating the genome. In the yeast cross-species complementation experiment, restored growth requires successful repeated cell cycles, but growth alone does not locate a defect within a phase. Bud appearance in Saccharomyces cerevisiae provides another readout, while a flow cytometric DNA-content assay asks how much nuclear DNA is present and DNA pulse labeling asks whether new DNA was synthesized during an exposure.

Mitosis

Words: 970 Articles: 12
M phase OurBigBook.com
Mitosis allocates copied chromosomes to daughter cell nuclei. The spindle apparatus aligns sister chromatids before separation in anaphase.
The copying has already occurred during S phase, before mitosis. Nuclear chromosome segregation and cytokinesis, the partition of the cytoplasm, are distinct processes: two daughter cell nuclei can form inside one undivided biological cell.[ref]
The original cartoon follows a hypothetical diploid animal biological cell with 2n=4. Its two homologous chromosome pairs are A,a and B,b; upper- and lower-case letters identify homologues, not dominant and recessive alleles. In prophase and metaphase, each replicated chromosome contains two sister chromatids: four replicated chromosomes contain eight chromatids, rather than eight replicated chromosomes. The X shape depicts two associated copies, not the shape of every chromosome throughout the cell cycle.
This open mitosis cartoon shows nuclear envelope breakdown and reassembly. Closed mitosis in some yeasts retains the nuclear envelope while daughter chromosomes separate inside it; the cartoon is therefore not a universal cellular architecture.[ref]

Metaphase

In metaphase, replicated chromosomes align near the spindle apparatus equator, with their two sister chromatids connected through kinetochores toward opposite poles.

Anaphase

Words: 94
Mitosis OurBigBook.com
In mitotic anaphase, sister chromatids separate into daughter chromosomes moving toward opposite poles.
Release of cohesin-mediated association permits the sister centromere regions to separate. Each former chromatid is then counted as an independent daughter chromosome. This changes the counting convention without making additional DNA copies. For the 2n=4 example, the whole undivided biological cell transiently contains eight daughter chromosomes, four moving toward each pole. Movement toward poles and separation of the poles both contribute to partition in the illustrated animal setting.[ref]

In open mitosis, telophase includes reassembly of nuclear envelopes around the separated chromosome groups and relaxation of their condensation.[ref]

Mitotic daughter chromosome accounting (2n=4)

Words: 292
Mitosis OurBigBook.com
Count the same hypothetical diploid animal biological cell in three ways: chromosomes, double-stranded DNA molecules, and nuclear DNA amount. Assume one continuous double-stranded DNA molecule per unreplicated chromosome or replicated chromatid, complete S phase, and no losses or mutations. Exclude mitochondrion DNA. Here n=2 is the size of one haploid set, and C is its unreplicated nuclear DNA amount, as defined in cell-cycle DNA content. A double-stranded DNA molecule counts as one, despite containing two strands.
Stage and compartmentChromosomesDouble-stranded DNA moleculesNuclear DNA amount
One G1 phase parent4 unreplicated42C
One prophase or metaphase biological cell4 replicated8 in 4 pairs of sister chromatids4C
Whole undivided anaphase biological cell8 separated daughter chromosomes84C total
One anaphase pole's allocation4 daughter chromosomes42C allocated
Each daughter after mitosis and cytokinesis4 unreplicated42C each
The allocation can be checked copy by copy. DNA replication turns A into A1​,A2​, a into a1​,a2​, and similarly for B,b. One daughter receives A1​,a1​,B1​,b1​ and the other A2​,a2​,B2​,b2​. Each therefore inherits the same four chromosome identities and the same gene and allele content as the unreplicated parent, under the stated assumptions. Across both daughters there are 4+4=8 DNA molecules and 2C+2C=4C: partition conserves the material present after S phase.
The transient whole-cell count of eight does not mean that each daughter's ploidy has doubled. Each completed daughter is diploid, containing both homologues of each homologous chromosome pair. This differs from separating homologous chromosomes during meiosis I. Faithful mitosis preserves a nuclear genome through growth and renewal; equal inherited genes need not imply equal gene expression or equal cell fate. Mutations or chromosome segregation errors can break the ideal inheritance pattern.

Kinetochore ablation experiment
Words: 187
Spindle checkpoint OurBigBook.com
In 1995, Conly L. Rieder, Richard W. Cole, Alexey Khodjakov and Greenfield Sluder tested whether an unattached kinetochore supplies the inhibitory signal delaying anaphase. Living PtK1 biological cells on glass coverslips were mounted in Rose chambers at 35–37∘C. An inverted microscope with a 60× objective recorded differential interference contrast microscopy video. A pulsed 532nm laser focused through that objective destroyed selected chromosome regions. The readout was chromosome movement and anaphase onset before and after surgery.[ref]
Destroying the last unattached kinetochore permitted anaphase without that chromosome aligning. Cutting chromosome arms served as a damage control: progression still awaited attachment of the last naturally one-pole-attached chromosome. Correlative electron microscopy checked the absence of the targeted kinetochore. Together these results located an inhibitory activity in or near the unattached kinetochore, rather than establishing a requirement for alignment itself.[ref]
This intervention localized control; it did not identify every signaling molecule or guarantee faithful inheritance after damage. The cultured PtK1 preparation differs from the hypothetical 2n=4 cartoon. Neither the cartoon nor this experiment establishes identical control in every species.

Cytokinesis

Words: 643 Articles: 6
M phase OurBigBook.com
Cytokinesis partitions the cytoplasm. Without cytokinesis, two cell nuclei can share one biological cell. [ref]
Cytokinesis makes physically separate daughter biological cells by partitioning their cytoplasm and completing their cell membrane boundaries. Mitosis divides nuclear material. These processes can overlap during M phase, but completion of one does not imply completion of the other.[ref]
For an original compartment-counting example, consider a hypothetical diploid biological cell after two daughter cell nuclei have formed. Let n=5 be the number of chromosomes in one haploid set, and let C be that set's unreplicated nuclear DNA amount, as in cell-cycle DNA content. Assume faithful mitosis, no nuclear DNA replication during the partition, and no loss of nuclear DNA; exclude mitochondrion DNA.
Counting boundaryBefore cytokinesisAfter cytokinesis
Biological cells12
Cell nuclei in each biological cell21
Each cell nucleus2n=10 chromosomes, 2C2n=10 chromosomes, 2C
Nuclear DNA per biological cell4C2C
Nuclear DNA across all descendants4C4C
The check is to follow the two existing cell nuclei: initially one boundary encloses both; finally each daughter boundary encloses one. The total remains two cell nuclei, twenty chromosomes and 2C+2C=4C of nuclear DNA. Thus the amount per biological cell halves because the counting boundary changes, without any new DNA replication or change of nuclear ploidy. Doubling the number of biological cells need not double their combined material.
Coordinating chromosome segregation with cytoplasm partition lets descendants inherit nuclear information inside separate working compartments. Equal nuclear DNA allocations do not require equal daughter sizes or equal allocations of every organelle. The geometry of cytokinesis connects an internal allocation to the creation of two independently bounded biological cells.

Actomyosin ring

Words: 65
Cytokinesis OurBigBook.com
An actomyosin ring is a contractile assembly containing actin filaments and myosin II. In typical animal cytokinesis, it forms in the cell cortex beneath the cell membrane. Its contraction draws the boundary inward to produce a cleavage furrow; an inward groove is not yet complete separation of the daughters.[ref]

Cleavage furrow

Words: 190 Articles: 1
Cytokinesis OurBigBook.com
A cleavage furrow is the inward indentation of the cell membrane during cytokinesis. It is the visible deformation; the actomyosin ring is machinery beneath that boundary.[ref]
Toroidal sand dollar egg experiment
Words: 155
Cleavage furrow OurBigBook.com
In 1961, Raymond Rappaport gently removed membrane and jelly from sand dollar ova with a constricted pipette. After fertilization, he used an inverted optical microscope and rack-and-pinion manipulator to press a needle-mounted glass sphere through an egg onto the chamber floor, producing a torus-shaped biological cell. Visible spindle apparatus positions and cleavage furrow progression were the readout.[ref]
First cytokinesis opened one side into a horseshoe containing two cell nuclei. During the next division, daughters separated from its free ends; an additional cleavage furrow crossed between mitotic asters of different spindle apparatuses. Control eggs supplied the timing comparison. This region lacked intervening chromosomes or a previous nearby spindle apparatus. This supported mitotic aster-associated positioning, without identifying the signal or establishing universal necessity.[ref]

Cell plate

Words: 60
Cytokinesis OurBigBook.com
A cell plate is an internal partition assembled during plant cytokinesis by fusion of transport vesicles. It grows outward to join the parental cell membrane, providing daughter boundaries and material for a new plant cell wall.[ref]

G0 phase (G0​)

Words: 30
Cell cycle OurBigBook.com
G0 phase describes withdrawal from the division cycle. Some biological cells re-enter G1 phase; others remain nondividing. Metabolism and gene expression can continue. [ref] In the ordinary diploid case, 2C alone cannot distinguish G0 phase from G1 phase.

Cell-cycle DNA content ((2n,2C)→(2n,4C)→2(2n,2C))

Words: 259
Cell cycle OurBigBook.com
Let n count chromosomes in one complete set, and let C denote the nuclear DNA amount in one unreplicated haploid set of this organism. Ploidy counts sets, whereas C units count material; C is not a universal mass shared by all species. Here we count a paired replicated chromosome as one until its sister chromatids separate. A copied diploid genome is therefore still diploid through S phase: 2n persists even as 2C becomes 4C.
An original bookkeeping example uses a hypothetical diploid biological cell with n=3. Name its three homologous chromosome pairs A,a, B,b and D,d. Assume ordinary nuclear DNA replication, no chromosome loss, and one continuous double-stranded DNA molecule per unreplicated chromosome or replicated chromatid. Count nuclear material only, excluding mitochondrion DNA.
Stage and counting compartmentChromosomesDouble-stranded DNA moleculesNuclear DNA amount
One G1 phase biological cell6 unreplicated62C
One G2 phase biological cell6 replicated12 in 6 pairs of sister chromatids4C
Whole undivided anaphase biological cell12 separated daughter chromosomes124C total
Each completed daughter cell nucleus6 unreplicated62C each
For example, A becomes the paired copies A1​,A2​ during S phase. They remain one replicated chromosome until separation. Each completed daughter receives one A copy and one a copy, and similarly for the other two pairs. Across both daughters, 2×6=12 double-stranded DNA molecules and 2(2C)=4C: division allocates the copied material without another round of DNA replication.
The whole undivided anaphase biological cell transiently contains 4n separately counted daughter chromosomes. This counting change does not establish a four-set ploidy in a completed daughter genome: each pole receives 2n, and each completed daughter cell nucleus is diploid. Always state whether a number refers to one cell nucleus, one pole, or the entire undivided biological cell. Compare meiotic DNA content: after meiosis I, a haploid cell nucleus can contain 2C, so amount alone cannot determine ploidy.

Cell cycle analysis

Words: 260 Articles: 3
Cell cycle OurBigBook.com
Cell cycle analysis assigns evidence to stages of the cell cycle. A flow cytometric DNA-content assay measures accumulated nuclear DNA, whereas DNA pulse labeling detects synthesis over an exposure window. Agreement between these readouts is useful because an amount and a synthesis event answer different questions.

DNA pulse labeling

Words: 225 Articles: 1
Cell cycle analysis OurBigBook.com
DNA pulse labeling exposes biological cells to a detectable precursor for a bounded interval. After removal, later sampling can follow the labeled material and descendants. A short pulse asks about synthesis during that interval; overnight labeling instead accumulates evidence across many hours. Detectable synthesis does not by itself prove completion of a cell cycle, and DNA repair can also incorporate precursors.

DNA synthesis labeling with EdU

Words: 172
DNA pulse labeling OurBigBook.com
EdU labeling uses a modified nucleoside, 5-ethynyl-2'-deoxyuridine, incorporated into newly synthesized DNA, then chemically couples it to a fluorescent reporter.
A 2008 experiment injected adult mice with 100μg of this label and inspected intestinal sections 96 hours later. Fixed sections on slides received 10μM Alexa568-azide for 10 minutes. Imaging with a fluorescence microscope combined the reporter image with a Hoechst DNA counterstain and a transmitted-light view. Labeled cell nuclei marked descendants of biological cells synthesizing DNA during exposure, rather than identifying current S phase at collection. [ref]
The same study's cultured-cell controls compared overnight 10μM label, no label, and label with 10mM hydroxyurea. No-label samples had little reporter background; hydroxyurea strongly suppressed incorporation. The camera detected residual signal with a 3-second exposure versus 40 milliseconds under otherwise identical settings, demonstrating why acquisition settings matter. These controls support synthesis-dependent labeling, without proving that every labeled biological cell completes division. [ref]
