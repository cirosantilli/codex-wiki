Particle accelerator

 Home Physics Experimental apparatus
OurBigBook.com Words: 4k Articles: 33
A particle accelerator increases the kinetic energy of charged particles through the work of an electric field. A single electric potential difference V gives an energy gain of magnitude ∣q∣V to a particle of electric charge q crossing in the accelerating direction. Reusing a smaller electric potential difference many times can give a much larger final kinetic energy without requiring the whole apparatus to withstand the equivalent single voltage. A magnetic field can return the particles to the accelerating region: magnetic forces do no work, but they make repeated energy gains possible. The cyclotron turns this separation between bending and acceleration into a particularly elegant machine.

Cyclotron

Words: 1k Articles: 6
Particle accelerator OurBigBook.com
A cyclotron repeatedly passes ions through an alternating electric field between two hollow electrodes called cyclotron dees. A nearly uniform magnetic field perpendicular to their plane bends the charged particles back toward the gap. Inside each cyclotron dee, ideal shielding suppresses the accelerating electric field, so each segment is a constant-speed circular motion. The next gap crossing increases the speed and the radius of curvature. The complete path consists of connected, progressively larger arcs, rather than one circle or a smooth spiral with continuous acceleration everywhere.
The striking feature is a timing cancellation: the longer arc traveled by a faster particle takes the same time. This cyclotron resonance condition lets one fixed radio frequency accelerate many successive gap crossings. The following construction and cyclotron energy and orbit radius example describe an ideal model; the Lawrence-Livingston cyclotron experiment explains what the builders actually measured.

Cyclotron dee

Words: 74
Cyclotron OurBigBook.com
A hollow D-shaped electrode that shields a circulating ion from the accelerating electric field during one half-turn. Two opposed cyclotron dees provide two shielded regions separated by a gap; their electric potential difference alternates. The vacuum reduces collisions with residual gas. An ion source supplies particles near the center, and an extraction system directs an outer orbit toward a particle detector or target. Useful electric current depends on retaining particles near the median plane and delivering them to the outlet, as well as on increasing their kinetic energy.

Cyclotron resonance condition (ωRF​=ωc​=∣q∣B/m)

Words: 273 Articles: 1
Cyclotron OurBigBook.com
Take an ion of mass m and electric charge q=0, with velocity perpendicular to a uniform magnetic field of magnitude B>0. In the classical mechanics limit, the Lorentz force provides the centripetal acceleration:
∣q∣vB=rmv2​,ωc​=rv​=m∣q∣B​,r=ωc​v​=∣q∣Bmv​.
(1)
Here r is the local radius of curvature and ωc​ is the cyclotron frequency, expressed as an angular frequency. A half-turn takes
τ1/2​=vπr​=ωc​π​=∣q∣Bπm​.
(2)
Increasing speed increases the path length in exactly the same proportion. Drive the gap at angular frequency ωRF​=ωc​, or ordinary frequency fRF​=∣q∣B/(2πm). In each half-turn the gap electric field reverses once, just as the particle reverses its direction of passage through the gap. The particle therefore receives two accelerating kicks per complete turn, using one oscillation per complete turn. This is the fundamental resonance; transit time through the gap and departures from a uniform magnetic field have been neglected.

Cyclotron gap energy gain (ΔK=qΔV)

Words: 149
Cyclotron resonance condition OurBigBook.com
Define ΔV=Φentry​−Φexit​ as the electric potential drop along the particle's passage. If the electric field changes negligibly during that short crossing, the work gives
ΔK=qΔV.
(3)
The sign matters: a positive ion gains kinetic energy while going to lower electric potential; a negative ion needs the opposite drop. A fixed-sign field would accelerate on one crossing and decelerate on the next. The alternating field must reverse before the particle comes back.
For a fixed electric charge sign and circulation direction, absorb this alternating crossing direction into the definition of the arrival phase ϕn​. If V0​ is the peak electric potential difference across the whole gap, rather than the amplitude of either electrode relative to ground, a thin-gap model is
Kn+1​=Kn​+∣q∣V0​cosϕn​.
(4)
Arrival at ϕn​=0 gives the largest accelerating kick; ∣ϕn​∣<π/2 gives a positive kick. Arrival at ϕn​=π gives deceleration. For an ideal classical particle initially on crest, the cyclotron resonance condition keeps successive arrival phases equal. It does not automatically bring an initially mistimed particle onto crest.

Cyclotron energy and orbit radius (K=∣q∣2B2r2/(2m))

Words: 164
Cyclotron OurBigBook.com
Combining r=mv/(∣q∣B) with the classical kinetic energy gives
E≡K=21​mv2=2m∣q∣2B2r2​.
(5)
Here E denotes kinetic energy, not relativistic total energy. At fixed magnetic field, twice the final radius allows four times the classical kinetic energy. At fixed final kinetic energy, a larger accelerating gap voltage reduces the number of crossings needed, but does not change the required orbit radius.
For an illustrative proton with B=1.00T and peak gap voltage V0​=10.0kV, take negligible injection kinetic energy, on-crest kicks, a zero-width gap, and no losses. Each crossing adds 10.0keV, so 100 crossings give 1.00MeV, using electronvolts. With e=1.602176634×10−19C and mp​=1.67262192595×10−27kg,
fRF​=15.2452MHz,τ1/2​=32.7972ns,r(1.00MeV)=0.144497m.
(6)
The 100th kick arrives after 99 intervening half-turns, about 3.247μs after the first kick in this idealization. The final two curvature radii differ by r(1MeV)[1−99/100​]≃0.724mm. That difference is a curvature-radius increment, not a general formula for separation of real extracted turns: successive arc centers shift, and extraction also depends on focusing and the gap geometry. These chosen settings are a calculation, not historical measurements.

Lawrence-Livingston cyclotron experiment

Words: 235
Cyclotron OurBigBook.com
Ernest Orlando Lawrence and Milton Stanley Livingston reported repeated ion acceleration in 1932. Their evacuated brass chamber used one driven hollow cyclotron dee; the grounded box and slotted divider supplied the other electrode. Filament electrons ionized hydrogen near the gap. A perpendicular magnetic field bent the ions; a high-frequency oscillator drove the gap. Deflecting plates and slits selected particles for a Faraday cup, read by an electrometer.[ref]
Scanning the magnetic field produced narrow resonance peaks. The optimum deflecting voltage tracked the kinetic energy predicted from field and orbit radius, supporting the reported 1.22MeV protons. Source width and slit geometry limited this check; it was not a direct energy measurement of every particle.[ref]
This combination distinguishes producing a circulating electric current from establishing its kinetic energy. The electrometer measures collected electric charge per unit time; it cannot by itself tell whether the collected particles are fast. The separate deflection selection supplies a speed-dependent test. Agreement of two consequences of the model, the cyclotron resonance condition and the cyclotron energy and orbit radius relation, makes the inference stronger than either a current signal or a predicted orbit alone.
Milton Stanley Livingston built the early cyclotrons with Ernest Orlando Lawrence; the 1939 Nobel Prize in Physics went to Ernest Orlando Lawrence. His lecture, The evolution of the cyclotron, was delivered on 11 December 1951.[ref][ref]

Relativistic detuning of a cyclotron (ω(K)=∣q∣B/(γm))

Words: 324
Cyclotron OurBigBook.com
At larger kinetic energy, special relativity changes the relation between momentum and velocity. With m the invariant mass, the Lorentz factor is γ=1+K/(mc2), and a relativistic cyclotron orbit in a fixed uniform magnetic field satisfies
p=γmv=∣q∣Br,ω(K)=γm∣q∣B​,r(K)=∣q∣BcK(K+2mc2)​​.
(7)
The cyclotron frequency decreases as the kinetic energy increases. If the drive stays at the classical value ω0​=∣q∣B/m, the half-turn time is πγ/ω0​. During it the drive advances through πγ rather than π radians. Its excess phase per half-turn is therefore
δϕ=ω0​τ1/2​−π=π(γ−1)=mc2πK​,ω0​ω(K)−ω0​​=−mc2+KK​.
(8)
Even a small fractional frequency mismatch accumulates across many turns. A particle eventually reaches a weak or decelerating gap phase, so the classical fixed-field, fixed-frequency design has a practical kinetic energy limit. The limit depends on the kick size and acceptable accumulated phase error, rather than one universal cutoff energy. Changing drive frequency or shaping the magnetic field can restore synchronism; the argument does not forbid relativistic circular accelerators.
For the illustrative proton at 1.00MeV, γ−1≃0.00106579: the frequency is lower by about 0.1065%, and the next half-turn adds about 0.19184∘ of drive phase excess. To see cumulative sensitivity, prescribe an ideal ramp Kj​=jϵ with ϵ=10keV and sum the errors of the first N coasts after kicks:
ΦN​=∑j=1N​mc2πKj​​=2mc2πϵN(N+1)​.
(9)
This gives Φ100​≃9.688∘ after the coast following kick 100; the arrival at kick 100 instead has Φ99​≃9.496∘. The prescribed ramp diagnoses detuning but does not feed the resulting phase error back into the kicks. A self-consistent thin-gap model uses the cyclotron gap energy gain with cosϕn​, then advances the phase with the new kinetic energy during the following coast.

Electrostatic accelerator

Words: 2k Articles: 6
Particle accelerator OurBigBook.com
An electrostatic accelerator gives a charged particle kinetic energy by taking it through a sustained electric potential difference. Its ion source, accelerating tube and target form a particularly direct chain: prepare a charge, let an electric field do work, and measure what arrives or what the target emits. A vacuum reduces collisions with gas before the beam reaches its target. The Cockcroft-Walton generator supplies the potential by accumulating electric charge on capacitors.

Electrostatic acceleration energy gain (Kf​−Ki​=q(Φi​−Φf​))

Words: 147
Electrostatic accelerator OurBigBook.com
For a fixed electric charge q, a time-independent electric potential Φ and negligible collisions or radiation losses, conservation of energy gives
Kf​−Ki​=−Δ(qΦ)=q(Φi​−Φf​)=qV.
(10)
Here V is the signed potential drop along the trajectory. A proton starting nearly at rest at +V and arriving at ground gains eV: a chosen 500kV gives 500keV, by the definition of the electronvolt. Several intermediate electrodes can distribute that drop along a tube, but the gains telescope to the same endpoint difference. Passing through more electrodes does not multiply the available kinetic energy beyond qV.
This is the distinction from a cyclotron. In a static electric field, a closed trip gives zero net work. A cyclotron changes the gap electric field between successive visits, so correctly timed particles repeatedly gain kinetic energy. Its limit involves the cyclotron resonance condition and relativistic detuning of a cyclotron; a single-pass electrostatic accelerator instead requires the full accelerating electric potential difference to be sustained at once. Neither machine's beam energy by itself specifies the energy released by a nuclear reaction.

Cockcroft-Walton generator

Words: 540 Articles: 1
Electrostatic accelerator OurBigBook.com
A voltage multiplier made of rectifiers and capacitors that transfers electric charge from an alternating supply into a series stack of stored electric potential differences. The rectifiers allow charge transfer during selected portions of the cycle; the capacitors retain charge between transfers. The supply continually replaces charge removed by the load. Multiplication of voltage does not multiply energy: the input must supply the output power and losses.
In his 1951 lecture The Artificial Production of Fast Particles, Ernest Thomas Sinton Walton describes the fourfold multiplier's series storage capacitors and alternating transfer capacitor in Fig. 2, p. 190. This is a mechanism for producing a nearly steady accelerating potential from an alternating input.[ref]

Cockcroft-Walton multiplier model (Videal​=2NVp​)

Words: 359
Cockcroft-Walton generator OurBigBook.com
Define a stage as one storage increment of 2Vp​, with Vp​ the alternating input's peak electric potential difference relative to its reference, not its peak-to-peak value. Assume ideal rectifiers, no load, no leakage or stray capacitance, a stiff alternating source and enough cycles to reach equilibrium. For N such stages,
Vk​=2kVp​,k=0,1,…,N,Videal​=2NVp​.
(11)
The factor two follows from adding the input's next opposite excursion to an already stored peak. The subsequent series increments add. This ideal argument concerns equilibrium voltage; it supplies neither the charge-up time nor the useful load electric current.
To isolate the effect of drawing charge, consider the following original reservoir model. Each storage stage has equal capacitance C; a constant output electric current I removes Δq=I/f between replenishment events of frequency f. Every series reservoir loses this charge, hence its voltage falls by a=I/(fC) per interval. Suppose replenishment removes a fixed fraction 1−λ of each reservoir's accumulated deficit, with 0≤λ<1. This is an explicit phenomenological pump rule, not a solution of a particular multiplier's rectifier network. If dj​ is the deficit immediately after event j, then
dj+1​=λ(dj​+a),d+​=1−λλa​,d−​=1−λa​.
(12)
Here d+​ and d−​ are the equilibrium deficits just after and just before replenishment. Linear discharge between events gives the average output and peak-to-peak variation
Vout​=2NVp​−NfCI​2(1−λ)1+λ​,Vpp​=NfCI​.
(13)
For chosen dimensionless settings N=3, a/Vp​=0.020 and λ=1/2, the output varies between 5.88Vp​ and 5.94Vp​, averaging 5.91Vp​, instead of the unloaded 6Vp​. Starting with zero deficit, the recurrence approaches equilibrium geometrically with factor λ. Taking I→0 recovers the ideal ladder; increasing C or f reduces droop in this model. As λ→1, effective replenishment fails and the small-deficit assumption fails too.
Real multiplier stages have unequal charge-transfer burdens; their mean deficits need not be equal and depend on topology, input impedance and parasitic capacitance. Thus this reservoir model explains droop and voltage variation without claiming their exact dependence on stage count for a real Cockcroft-Walton generator. In particular, adding more stages cannot be judged solely by the unloaded 2NVp​. For a beam sampling a slowly varying output, the electrostatic acceleration energy gain follows that instantaneous potential drop, so voltage variation also produces a spread of beam kinetic energies.

Cockcroft-Walton lithium disintegration experiment

Words: 773 Articles: 2
Electrostatic accelerator OurBigBook.com
In 1932 John Douglas Cockcroft and Ernest Thomas Sinton Walton accelerated hydrogen ions from a discharge source through an evacuated tube onto lithium at 45∘. A side-facing zinc sulfide screen, viewed by microscope, showed particle scintillations. Mica stopped scattered protons; switching off or blocking the beam removed the flashes. An external screen allowed absorption-range measurements.[ref]
Magnetic deflection checked voltage calibration. Opposite screens and independent observers found excess coincidences for a thin lithium target, supporting paired alpha particle emission; geometry and screen efficiency limited detection.[ref]
John Douglas Cockcroft's 1951 lecture recalls detection at 125kV and an absorption range about 8.4cm, then refined alpha particle energies about 8.6MeV each. He places the work under Ernest Rutherford's direction. The inferred nuclear reaction was lithium-7 plus a proton producing two alpha particles.[ref]
The logic has several independent links. Beam blocking tests whether the flashes depend on bombardment. An absorber tests whether scattered beam particles could reach the screen. A particle range tests product identity and kinetic energy, while coincidences test whether two products belong to one event. These tests support a reaction interpretation jointly; counting flashes alone is not an isotope identification, and a range-to-energy conversion needs calibration. The lithium proton reaction energy below is a modern calculation, separate from those historical measurements.

Lithium proton reaction energy (7Li+p⟶2α)

Words: 525 Articles: 1
Cockcroft-Walton lithium disintegration experiment OurBigBook.com
The nuclear reaction
37​Li+11​H⟶24​He+24​He
(14)
conserves electric charge and total nucleon count: 3+1=2+2 and 7+1=4+4. Its nuclear reaction Q-value is the initial minus final rest energy. It is additional to the incoming beam kinetic energy; the accelerator triggers a rearrangement whose products can carry much more kinetic energy than the incident proton.
Use one consistent set of neutral-atom isotope masses, in unified atomic mass units, from the National Institute of Standards and Technology:
M(7Li)=7.0160034366u,M(1H)=1.00782503223u,M(4He)=4.00260325413u.
(15)
These are isotope masses, not element-averaged atomic weights.[ref] With uc2=931.49410372MeV,[ref]
ΔM=0.01862196057u,Qatom​=[M(7Li)+M(1H)−2M(4He)]c2=17.34625MeV≃17.3MeV.
(16)
The four electron rest masses cancel between sides. Differences of total electronic binding energies do not cancel exactly: this atomic-mass result approximates the bare-nucleus nuclear reaction Q-value at the displayed 0.001MeV scale, not at arbitrary precision. No mixture of rounded nuclear binding energies is needed. At chosen incident kinetic energy T=0.500MeV on a stationary target, the two outgoing alpha particles together carry approximately Q+T=17.846MeV before losses in the target and detector window.
Lithium reaction laboratory recoil
Words: 249
Lithium proton reaction energy OurBigBook.com
Equal product masses imply equal kinetic energies and opposite momenta in the center of mass frame. The laboratory frame has nonzero initial momentum because the proton is moving. Conservation of momentum therefore forbids the two laboratory momenta from always being equal and opposite. The products are equal in laboratory kinetic energy only for particular emission directions, not for every event.
An exact two-body calculation makes this distinction quantitative. Write a=mp​c2, b=mLi​c2, h=mα​c2, and T for incident kinetic energy. Express every quantity below in the same energy units; P=pc is momentum times the speed of light. For a stationary target,
P=T(T+2a)​,W=(a+b)2+2bT​,β=a+b+TP​,γ=Wa+b+T​.
(17)
Here W is the center-of-mass energy, and β is the center-of-mass speed divided by the speed of light. Each outgoing alpha particle has center-of-mass total energy E∗​=W/2 and momentum-times-c magnitude P∗​=E∗2​−h2​. A Lorentz transformation gives the paired laboratory kinetic energies, with θ∗​ measured from the incident beam in the center of mass frame:
K1​=γ(E∗​+βP∗​cosθ∗​)−h,K2​=γ(E∗​−βP∗​cosθ∗​)−h.
(18)
Their sum is independent of θ∗​ and equals Q+T. Their difference is 2γβP∗​cosθ∗​; the symmetry belongs to the product interchange and the center of mass frame.
For the chosen T=0.500MeV model, subtract Z electron rest masses from each neutral-atom mass, neglect electronic binding energies, and use me​c2=0.51099895069MeV.[ref] At θ∗​=0, the paired laboratory kinetic energies are approximately 9.979 and 7.867MeV; at θ∗​=90∘ both are approximately 8.923MeV. In the latter case both products have a forward laboratory momentum component, so even equal laboratory kinetic energies do not imply back-to-back laboratory trajectories. A real thick target and finite detector acceptance would broaden or select these ideal kinematic possibilities.

Super Proton-Antiproton Synchrotron (Sppˉ​S)

Words: 111
Particle accelerator OurBigBook.com
The Super Proton-Antiproton Synchrotron used the converted SPS ring for counterrotating proton and antiproton beams. In the discovery running, two 270GeV beams provided s​=540GeV, unlike a stationary-target collision at the same incident energy. Antiproton production, accumulation and stochastic cooling supplied the collisions used in the W and Z discovery experiments.[ref]
Why can opposite electric charges share the bending magnets? Reversing both q and velocity v leaves the Lorentz force qv×B unchanged at a given point. Both circulating species can therefore bend toward the same ring center in the same magnetic field, with equal magnetic rigidity magnitudes. This argument explains the bending geometry; actual injection, focusing, timed acceleration and collisions still require a complete particle accelerator system.

Beam cooling

Words: 562 Articles: 3
Particle accelerator OurBigBook.com
Beam cooling reduces random deviations around a beam's reference motion, such as its momentum spread and transverse spread. A highly energetic beam can be cool in this sense: its large common directed kinetic energy is distinct from the spread. Concentrating the occupied phase space helps accumulated particles fit the next machine's acceptance. Stochastic cooling achieves this through measured beam fluctuations and feedback control.

Stochastic cooling

Words: 510 Articles: 2
Beam cooling OurBigBook.com
Simon van der Meer's stochastic cooling uses measured beam fluctuations, a pick-up, amplifier and timed kicker. Finite samples supply the signal; other particles and electronic noise contribute unwanted kicks. Delay and oscillation phase must make the correction oppose the sampled deviation.[ref]
This active system dissipates energy; it is no one-particle Maxwell's demon individually tracking antiprotons. The stochastic cooling variance model isolates the tradeoff.

Beam mixing

Words: 42
Stochastic cooling OurBigBook.com
Beam mixing changes sample membership between turns through revolution-time differences. Repeatedly correlated samples and thermal noise limit stochastic cooling.[ref]
Fresh samples help across turns; losing pick-up–kicker correlation hurts. Useful feedback gain therefore depends on bandwidth, delay, beam dynamics and signal-to-noise ratio.

Stochastic cooling variance model (Vn+1​=(1−g)2Vn​+g2N)

Words: 341
Stochastic cooling OurBigBook.com
Let xn​ be a centered scalar beam-deviation coordinate in a deliberately controlled toy model. Let the readout be sn​=xn​+ηn​, where the centered measurement noise ηn​ has variance N and is independent of xn​ and of earlier noise. Stipulate a correctly timed correction of strength g: xn+1​=xn​−gsn​. This scalar observation is a teaching surrogate, not a claim that real cooling measures each particle. From variance additivity for independent random variables,
xn+1​=(1−g)xn​−gηn​,Vn+1​=(1−g)2Vn​+g2N,Vn​=Var(xn​).
(19)
For fixed current V>0 and N≥0, differentiate the next-step variance with respect to feedback gain:
dgdVnext​​=−2V+2g(V+N),dg2d2Vnext​​=2(V+N)>0.
(20)
The unique one-step minimizing feedback gain and resulting variance are
g∗​=V+NV​,Vnext,min​=V+NVN​.
(21)
The noisier the readout relative to the current spread, the smaller the useful correction. The exact one-step cooling condition follows by subtraction:
Vnext​−V=g[g(V+N)−2V]<0⟺0<g<V+N2V​=2g∗​.
(22)
Thus excessive positive feedback gain heats this model. The threshold depends on the current variance: a gain that initially cools can later heat if the beam has become narrower or the noise has grown.
For chosen dimensionless V0​=1 and N=1/4, g∗​=0.8 gives V1​=0.20. Taking g=1.8 instead gives V1​=1.45, larger than the initial spread. Holding g=0.8 fixed gives
Vn​=61​+65​(0.04)n,
(23)
so the variance approaches the nonzero noise floor 1/6. More generally, for 0<g<2 the fixed-gain limit is V∞​=gN/(2−g). This long-run stability condition differs from the condition for cooling the current beam on the very next step.
If the toy feedback gain is recomputed after every step and N>0, the reciprocal recurrence is even simpler:
Vn+1​1​=Vn​1​+N1​,Vn​=N+nV0​V0​N​.
(24)
With the chosen inputs, V5​=1/21≃0.047619. This ideal model has no additional diffusion, finite observation time or lost correlation; its indefinitely falling spread is not a prediction for an operating storage ring.
To expose the missing physics mathematically, replace s=x+η by an arbitrary centered sample signal s. Defining C=Cov(x,s) and S=Var(s)>0 gives
Var(x−gs)=V−2gC+g2S,g∗​=C/S.
(25)
Unwanted contributions from other particles or electronics can enlarge S without increasing useful C; poor timing or lost correlation can reduce C or even reverse its sign. This is why real stochastic cooling needs measured beam fluctuations, appropriate beam mixing and correctly phased feedback control, rather than an imagined catalogue of individual particle positions.
