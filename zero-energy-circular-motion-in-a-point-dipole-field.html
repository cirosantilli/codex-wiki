Zero-energy circular motion in a point-dipole field (d2r2/dt2=4E/m)

 ... Branches of physics Electromagnetism Electrostatics Electric dipole moment Electric dipole Electric dipole field
OurBigBook.com Words: 82
For potential energy homogeneous of degree −2, Newton's second law gives d2r2/dt2=4E/m. Indeed, Euler's identity implies r⋅F=2U, so the second derivative equals 2v2+4U/m=4(K+U)/m. Thus a trajectory with zero total energy and initially zero radial velocity remains on a sphere of constant radius. A positive electric charge released from rest on the equator of a fixed ideal electric dipole has these properties; in its meridional plane it oscillates on the semicircle on the negative-pole side. A finite two-charge electric dipole does not have exactly homogeneous potential energy and perturbs this result.
