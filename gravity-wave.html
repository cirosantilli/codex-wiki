Gravity wave

 Home Physics Branches of physics Fluid mechanics
OurBigBook.com Words: 5k Articles: 98
A gravity wave is a fluid disturbance restored by gravity or buoyancy. A surface gravity wave displaces a free surface, while an internal gravity wave displaces density surfaces within a stratified fluid. Rotation can additionally contribute to restoration, giving an inertia-gravity wave.

Internal-wave response of a sharp buoyancy interface (T/Wi​=2im/[k(1−S)+im])

Words: 76
Internal wave OurBigBook.com
For a lower uniformly stratified half-space and an upper well-mixed half-space, the vertical velocity amplitude is continuous and its derivative jumps by [W′]=−k2ΔbW/ω2. A wave incident upwards with vertical phase factor e−imz therefore has upper evanescent amplitude T/Wi​=2im/[k(1−S)+im], where m=kN2/ω2−1​ and S=Δbk/ω2. Its magnitude peaks at S=1, with amplitude twice the incident amplitude. Lossless reflection carries all incident energy back down; the evanescent upper field carries no mean outgoing vertical energy flux.

Pressure projection in a displaced stratified blob (ω2=N2kh2​/(kh2​+m2))

Words: 68
Internal wave OurBigBook.com
Incompressibility makes pressure adjust throughout the surrounding fluid. Projecting the linear Boussinesq equations perpendicular to a Fourier wavevector gives wt​=[kh2​/(kh2​+m2)]b and bt​=−N2w. A localized spherical disturbance contains many wavevector directions and therefore many internal gravity wave frequencies, rather than one oscillator at N. Its shape evolves and energy radiates into the surroundings; a rigid-sphere added mass analogy illustrates extra inertia but does not give an exact coherent frequency for a free material blob.

Finite-amplitude stationary channel internal waves (∇2ψ′=−N2ψ′/U2,σ′=N2ψ′/U)

Words: 78
Internal wave OurBigBook.com
In a nonrotating ideal Boussinesq layer with uniform current U>0, constant buoyancy frequency N and rigid walls, any stationary superposition of channel wave modes with kn2​+(nπ/H)2=N2/U2 obeys the displayed relations. With (u,w)=(ψz​,−ψx​), the total streamfunction is Ψ=Uz+ψ′ and total buoyancy is σ=(N2/U)Ψ. Its material derivative vanishes. The perturbation vorticity is proportional to ψ′, so its nonlinear self-advection vanishes as well. The modes are therefore exact finite-amplitude solutions, although large amplitudes may violate physical approximation assumptions or become unstable.

Internal-wave momentum deposition (u=(k/ω)E)

Nonlinear disruption of an internal gravity wave, for example when large displacement gradients overturn the stable density arrangement or strong shear becomes unstable. The resulting turbulence dissipates wave energy and mixes the stratification, limiting ideal geometric focusing.

Monochromatic internal-wave overturning criterion (∣mAζ​∣>1)

Words: 57
Plane internal gravity wave OurBigBook.com
For a plane internal gravity wave with vertical displacement ζ, linear density advection gives ρ′=−ρˉ​z​ζ. Stratified internal-wave guide

Words: 35
Internal wave OurBigBook.com
A stratified layer between unstratified regions can trap internal gravity waves, because the exterior disturbance is an evanescent wave. Its discrete normal modes obey Robin boundary conditions representing the exterior layers. Periodic boundary forcing at a trapped normal mode frequency produces resonance in the ideal undamped model.

Slowly modulated internal-wave boundary forcing (AT​+cgz​AZ​=0)

Two-beam radiation from a localized oscillating boundary (Z±​=z±αx,α=ω/N2−ω2​)

Words: 89
Boundary-forced internal gravity wave OurBigBook.com
For a nonrotating uniform-stratification fluid under the Boussinesq approximation driven at 0<ω<N by a localized vertical-wall velocity, positive vertical wavenumbers radiate down and right and negative ones up and right. The real velocity separates into functions of Z+​ and Z−​. Its in-phase component is half the imposed boundary profile on each beam; the quadrature component is its Hilbert transform partner. Thus compact localization at maximum boundary speed does not imply compact localization of all components at every phase. The density gradient is the gradient of a spatial mass density field. With an upward vertical coordinate in the Boussinesq approximation, the squared buoyancy frequency is N2=−(g/ρ∗​)∂z​ρ0​. A negative vertical density gradient therefore gives stable density stratification.

Halocline

Words: 114 Articles: 1

A layer with a pronounced vertical change in salinity is a halocline. When relatively fresh surface water lies over saltier water, its contribution to density stratification inhibits vertical exchange. A cold Arctic halocline can separate sea ice from warmer Atlantic water; increased heat at depth does not imply an equally increased ocean heat flux at the ice unless the intervening exchange also changes.
Cold halocline

Halocline OurBigBook.com
The Arctic cold halocline is a near-freezing layer in which salinity increases downward and produces stable density stratification, helping isolate sea ice from warmer water below. It can be maintained through shelf-water input, cooling and limited winter convection of inflowing waters, followed by freshwater capping and advection. Its formation cannot be inferred from shelf area alone: water-mass volumes, salt budgets and residence times matter.

Ocean mixed layer

Density stratification OurBigBook.com
The ocean mixed layer is a near-surface layer with relatively weak vertical variations of temperature and mass density, maintained by mixing. Beneath it, stable density stratification can isolate colder water from recent surface heating. The mixed-layer depth is not necessarily the full local water depth.

Stable density stratification

Density stratification OurBigBook.com
A fluid has stable density stratification when a small vertical displacement produces a restoring buoyancy force. In a gravitational field this normally means that mass density increases downward.

Equating an inertial eddy turnover time to the inverse buoyancy frequency gives the scale separating buoyancy-affected motions from a smaller approximately isotropic cascade. Buoyancy frequency (N)

The buoyancy frequency is the natural angular frequency of small vertical oscillations in a stably stratified fluid.

Radial buoyancy frequency (Nr2​)

Words: 94 Articles: 1
Buoyancy frequency OurBigBook.com
The radial buoyancy frequency describes restoration of a radially displaced parcel in a stratified astrophysical disk. With a dimensionless specific entropy S, its square is proportional to −ρ−1(dP/dr)(dS/dr). Negative Nr2​ means an adverse entropy stratification, but epicyclic motion can still stabilize the parcel. For smooth thin disk profiles, ∣Nr2​∣ is usually of order (H/r)2Ω2.

Words: 44

For ideal axisymmetric radial displacements in a Keplerian shearing sheet, the hydrodynamic squared oscillation frequency is Nr2​+κ2, with radial epicyclic frequency κ=Ω. Exponential instability occurs when Nr2​+κ2<0. This is the radial special case of rotating-fluid convective stability, rather than the complete set of three-dimensional Solberg–Høiland criteria.

Dry parcel buoyancy in a homogeneous atmosphere (N2=(g/T)(dT/dz+g/Cp​))

Words: 54
Buoyancy frequency OurBigBook.com
For a dry, homogeneous ideal gas in hydrostatic equilibrium, a displaced parcel undergoing an adiabatic process follows dT/dz=−g/Cp​, where Cp​ is specific heat capacity at constant pressure. At a common pressure its buoyancy depends on its temperature difference from the environment, giving N2=(g/T)(dT/dz+g/Cp​). Negative N2 means growing displacements; zero N2 means neutral stability. This is the homogeneous dry limit of the Schwarzschild criterion.
