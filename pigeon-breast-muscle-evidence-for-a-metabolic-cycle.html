Pigeon breast muscle evidence for a metabolic cycle

 Home Chemistry Biochemistry Metabolic pathway Citric acid cycle
OurBigBook.com Words: 252
Hans Adolf Krebs described the experimental reasoning in his 1953 Nobel lecture. Pigeon breast skeletal muscle retained strong oxidative activity after preparation in a Latapie mincing mill and suspension in aqueous medium. Small fumarate additions increased oxygen uptake by more than complete oxidation of the added fumarate could explain; added four-carbon acids could still be detected afterward. His Table 1 compares the increase in uptake with that calculated from complete consumption of the addition. These comparisons challenge the interpretation that the addition merely supplied extra fuel.[ref]
The lecture identifies the preparation apparatus but does not describe the gas-measuring instrument behind Table 1. The result supports regeneration, not a unique complete reaction mechanism. Its later discussion combines citrate formation from oxaloacetate with breakdown back toward the four-carbon intermediates to close the cycle. Competitive inhibition supplies another comparison: malonate inhibits succinate dehydrogenase, and succinate accumulates. Such evidence links a blocked step to the surrounding metabolic pathway; damaged tissue and restricted inhibitor access can complicate interpretation.[ref]
An increase above the added fuel's own oxygen demand is a particularly revealing control on the explanation: it compares the proposed cause with the largest effect that simple consumption could produce. The modern mitochondrial oxygen electrode assay asks a different question about a prepared mitochondrion, testing respiratory control through ADP additions. Neither an oxygen uptake signal nor an accumulating intermediate is by itself a direct measurement of ATP production.
