Finite-energy radial cavitation versus homogeneous dilation (Ecav​(t)∼8πt2,Ehom​(t)∼(4π/3)t6)

For the unit ball and energy W(F)=∣F∣2+(detF−1)2, boundary dilation y=tx admits a singular incompressible radial competitor r(R)=(R3+t3−1)1/3. Its radial stretch is R2/r2 and its double tangential stretch is r/R. Although the latter diverges at zero, the three-dimensional volume element makes its squared gradient integrable. Its energy is 4π∫01​[R6/r4+2r2],dR∼8πt2.
The smooth homogeneous equilibrium y=tx has constant stress and energy (4π/3)[3t2+(t3−1)2], of sixth-order growth. It is therefore beaten for large t in the finite-energy Sobolev space class admitting the cavity. This conclusion depends on that class: the singular map is not continuous at the center and cannot simply be substituted as a smooth cavity-free competitor.
