Removing electron–electron repulsion lowers the entire curve and exaggerates the bonding stabilization. In the same fixed-orbital notation and electronic-energy convention,
Eno ee​(c2​)=2a+4bc2​1−c22​​.​
(61)
Its minima and maxima occur at c2​=+1/2​ and −1/2​, respectively; all three localized points c2​=0,±1 have energy 2a. The physical curve lies above it by the positive Coulomb expectation Jψψ​.
For the one-electron problem, the bonding energy is E(H+⋯H)=a+b. Two noninteracting opposite-spin electrons may both occupy that orbital, so the lower curve's bonding minimum is
Eno ee,min​=2(a+b)=2E(H+⋯H).​
(62)
This is the third requested energy label in the common graph. If total molecular energies include nuclear repulsion, the corresponding identity is Eno ee,total​=2Eone electron,total​−VNN​, because the two-electron molecule contains only one nuclear pair. An effective one-electron potential re-fitted after removing repulsion would be a different comparison; here the same kinetic and nuclear-attraction operators are retained.
