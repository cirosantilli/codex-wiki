Use Wade's rules with the metal included as a skeletal vertex. Each BH vertex supplies two skeletal electrons, each CH supplies three, and the two additional hydrogen atoms supply two more. The Ru(CO)3​ fragment supplies 8+3(2)−12=2 skeletal electrons after the metal's non-skeletal orbitals have been accounted for. Thus
Nskeletal​=3(2)+2(3)+2+2=16,SEP=8=6+2.
(1)
The six-vertex cluster is therefore nido, with a pentagonal-pyramidal skeleton: Ru is the apex, bonded to the five vertices of the planar B3​C2​ base, and the three carbonyl ligands project away from that base. This is the six-vertex fragment obtained by removing an equatorial vertex from a seven-vertex pentagonal bipyramid. The five ring atoms carry their terminal B–H or C–H bonds; the two additional hydrogens belong to the hydrogen-rich open-face bonding framework. The skeletal electron count does not, by itself, locate every bridging hydride, so a unique detailed hydrogen arrangement should not be inferred from it.
