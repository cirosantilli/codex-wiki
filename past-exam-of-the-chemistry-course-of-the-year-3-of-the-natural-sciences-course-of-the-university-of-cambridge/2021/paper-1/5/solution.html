Disconnect the tertiary alcohol at its bond to the alkenyl group. The required carbonyl partner is MeCOCH(Me)Et; the other partner is an E-configured PhCH2​CH=CH organometallic reagent. This disconnection fixes the alkene geometry before the alcohol is made.
First form the ethyl-side enolate of butan-2-one under equilibrating conditions, then trap it with ethyl iodide and isolate the required monoalkylated ketone:
MeCOCH2​Me⟶MeCOCH(Me)Et.
(5)
The incoming group is Et: methylation would give a carbon skeleton one carbon too short. Because an unsymmetrical ketone can form two enolates and undergo repeated alkylation, regioisomer separation and controlled conversion are part of this step.
Independently perform a halo-Wittig reaction on phenylacetaldehyde using Ph3​P=CHBr and isolate E-PhCH2​CH=CHBr from any geometrical mixture. magnesium insertion in dry ether gives the corresponding vinylmagnesium halide; add the isolated ketone at low temperature and protonate the resulting alkoxide. Addition to C=O preserves the pre-existing C=C geometry:
(E)-PhCH2​CH=CHMgBr+MeCOCH(Me)Et⟶(E)-PhCH2​CH=CHC(OH)(Me)CH(Me)Et.
(6)
The E alkene and the complete fifteen-carbon skeleton are obtained. No absolute configuration is specified at the newly formed alcohol centre, so an unselected stereoisomeric mixture there is acceptable. An alternative carbon–carbon disconnection is acetylide addition to the same ketone followed by a controlled trans-selective alkyne reduction; the vinyl-reagent route avoids relying on reduction of a tertiary propargylic alcohol.
