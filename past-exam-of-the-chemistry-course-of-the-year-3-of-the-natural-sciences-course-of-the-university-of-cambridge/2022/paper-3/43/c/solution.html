At night, nitrate radical addition and ozone attack are relevant oxidations of isoprene; direct daytime photochemistry is absent, though small OH sources can still exist. The nitrate-containing products identify the NO3​-addition route. Add NO3​ to the terminal carbon next to the methyl-substituted alkene carbon, producing a nitrooxy substituent and an allylic radical. Resonance moves the radical to the far terminal carbon while shifting the double bond. Oxygen trapping gives a peroxy radical with the carbon skeleton
O2​NOCH2​−C(CH3​)=CH−CH2​OO∙.
(18)
A nitrate-forming termination branch, for example the organic-nitrate branch of reaction with NO, replaces the terminal peroxyl group by a nitrate ester, giving O2​NOCH2​−C(CH3​)=CH−CH2​ONO2​. An alternative branch forms an alkoxy radical, such as peroxy-radical reaction with NO3​ producing RO∙, NO2​ and O2​. Reaction of the terminal CH2​O∙ group with O2​ then gives an aldehyde plus HO2​, producing O2​NOCH2​−C(CH3​)=CH−CHO. These are covalent O–NO2​ ester groups, not C–NO2​ nitro groups. The delocalized radical can be trapped after conformational change, so this mechanism does not require one universal alkene geometry for both product channels. Product structures alone do not fix a unique branching ratio.
