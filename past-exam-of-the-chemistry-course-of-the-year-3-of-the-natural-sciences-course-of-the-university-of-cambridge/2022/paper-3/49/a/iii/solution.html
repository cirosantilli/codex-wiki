The spin isomers of hydrogen illustrate how nuclear spin statistics constrain the product of electronic, vibrational, rotational and nuclear-spin exchange symmetries. In the ground electronic and vibrational states of these homonuclear molecules, the first two factors are symmetric. Exchanging the nuclei reverses the internuclear direction, so a rotational state has parity (−1)J.
For 1H2​, the protons are spin-one-half fermions and the total nuclear-exchange wavefunction must be antisymmetric. The antisymmetric spin singlet pairs with even J (para-hydrogen, one spin state); the symmetric spin triplet pairs with odd J (ortho-hydrogen, three spin states). For 2H2​, spin-one deuterons are bosons, so the total must be symmetric. Nuclear spins I=0,2 supply six symmetric states paired with even J (ortho-deuterium); I=1 supplies three antisymmetric states paired with odd J (para-deuterium). The high-temperature ortho/para statistical ratios are 3:1 for hydrogen and 2:1 for deuterium, while low-temperature equilibrium populations also depend on rotational energies and whether spin conversion can occur.
