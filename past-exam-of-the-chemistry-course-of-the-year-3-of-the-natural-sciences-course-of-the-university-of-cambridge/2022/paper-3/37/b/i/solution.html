A is the N-bound linkage isomer, [(NH3​)5​Co(NCS)]2+: five ammines plus NCS account for CoCH15​N6​S.
The natural route is conjugate-base substitution. Hydroxide reversibly removes an ammine proton:
[(NH3​)5​CoX]2++OH−⇌[(NH3​)4​(NH2​)CoX]++H2​O.
(1)
The amido donor promotes loss of X−, producing a short-lived five-coordinate [(NH3​)4​(NH2​)Co]2+ intermediate. That leaving-group-sensitive loss is the slower stage. Subsequent trapping/proton transfers are fast and no longer depend on which halide left.
Water capture followed by proton transfer gives the hydroxo complex. Competing SCN− capture can occur at either end of this ambidentate ligand, producing Co-SCN and Co-NCS. Any initially captured S-bound material can undergo the base-assisted linkage rearrangement considered next. Water is overwhelmingly abundant, so the stable N-bound product can remain a minor overall product. The slow X-dependent and fast X-independent stages fit loss of X followed by chemistry of a common intermediate; a single rate-limiting direct attack by thiocyanate would not explain that separation naturally.
