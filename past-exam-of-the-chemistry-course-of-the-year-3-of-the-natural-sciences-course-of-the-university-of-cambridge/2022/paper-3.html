i OurBigBook.com
Ni(II) is d8. A square-planar complex can keep its strongly antibonding in-plane orbital empty and has a paired, S=0 configuration. A tetrahedral d8 arrangement has two unpaired electrons, S=1.
The important change here is the substituent on the imine nitrogen: Me, Et, then tert-butyl. The phenolic ortho-tert-butyl substituent is common to all three ligands. The small N-Me groups permit coplanar N2​O2​ coordination in Ni(La​)2​. N-tert-butyl crowds that arrangement and favors twisting the two chelate planes toward a distorted tetrahedron in Ni(Lc​)2​. N-Et gives a closer energetic competition, so Ni(Lb​)2​ accesses both structures. The observed equilibrium reflects ligand-field stabilization, pairing, steric strain and temperature-dependent entropy; it is not a change in nickel oxidation state.

ii OurBigBook.com
The negative activation volume is consistent with an associative substitution pathway: an incoming donor of the tetradentate ligand approaches square-planar Ni, giving a five-coordinate transition structure/intermediate before a donor of an original chelate leaves. Successive donor substitution and chelate closure give the tetradentate N2​O2​ complex. Proton transfers convert the incoming phenolic groups to coordinated phenoxides while releasing the original ligands.
With acid/base forms written explicitly, the net ligand exchange is Ni(La​)2​+H2​Ld​→Ni(Ld​)+2HLa​. The connected four-donor ligand benefits from the chelate effect.
The bulky N-tert-butyl groups in Ni(Lc​)2​ hinder precisely the close donor approach needed for that associative route, and the starting tetrahedral geometry must reorganize to accept the planar tetradentate ligand. Consequently exchange can be too slow to observe under the stated conditions. Failure to observe a new product is a kinetic observation; it does not establish that all tetrahedral nickel complexes are inert or that the tetradentate product is thermodynamically impossible.

A is the N-bound linkage isomer, [(NH3​)5​Co(NCS)]2+: five ammines plus NCS account for CoCH15​N6​S.
The natural route is conjugate-base substitution. Hydroxide reversibly removes an ammine proton:
[(NH3​)5​CoX]2++OH−⇌[(NH3​)4​(NH2​)CoX]++H2​O.
(1)
The amido donor promotes loss of X−, producing a short-lived five-coordinate [(NH3​)4​(NH2​)Co]2+ intermediate. That leaving-group-sensitive loss is the slower stage. Subsequent trapping/proton transfers are fast and no longer depend on which halide left.
Water capture followed by proton transfer gives the hydroxo complex. Competing SCN− capture can occur at either end of this ambidentate ligand, producing Co-SCN and Co-NCS. Any initially captured S-bound material can undergo the base-assisted linkage rearrangement considered next. Water is overwhelmingly abundant, so the stable N-bound product can remain a minor overall product. The slow X-dependent and fast X-independent stages fit loss of X followed by chemistry of a common intermediate; a single rate-limiting direct attack by thiocyanate would not explain that separation naturally.

A solvent-caged pair of five-coordinate cobalt amido species and the departing thiocyanate can reorient and recombine through nitrogen before the ligand diffuses into bulk solution. These are inner-sphere electron transfer pathways distinguished by where Cr(II) attacks the coordinated thiocyanate. Cr(II) is substitution-labile and can replace an aqua ligand to form a bridged Co(III)/Cr(II) precursor.
  • Remote attack at nitrogen: form CoIII−S−C−N−CrII. Electron transfer through the bridge gives Co(II)/Cr(III). Cleavage on the now labile Co(II) side leaves the N-bound product [Cr(NCS)(OH2​)5​]2+. This is the faster 2.0×105M−1s−1 channel.
  • Adjacent attack at sulfur: Cr binds the same sulfur that is attached to cobalt, giving a shared-S bridge, CoIII−S(CN)−CrII. Electron transfer and Co-S cleavage leave [Cr(SCN)(OH2​)5​]2+, the 0.8×105 channel.
In both cases the product Co(II) rapidly loses its ammines to give the hexaaqua ion, whereas substitution-inert Cr(III) retains the bridge-end information. The two chromium linkage isomers are therefore a record of two precursor geometries, rather than evidence that one outer-sphere electron-transfer event somehow selects two arbitrary new ligands afterwards.

The free end of N-bound Co−NCS is sulfur. Cr(II) binds there, giving the remote bridge CoIII−N−C−S−CrII. Electron transfer and cleavage of the Co-N bond leave S-bound Cr(III), [Cr(SCN)(OH2​)5​]2+; labile Co(II) becomes hexaaquacobalt(II).

i OurBigBook.com
Write L for the imidazolylidene N-heterocyclic carbene bearing the two mesityl groups shown. The proposed active unit is LNi(H)(SiPh3​), with H and SiPh3​ both bonded to nickel, rather than a nickel-free hydrosilane or a bare NHC-Ni fragment.
Displacement of coordinated styrene and formal oxidative addition of Si-H to Ni(0) give a low-coordinate nickel(II) hydride-silyl unit; substrate binding fills a vacant site. The productive cycle is alkene coordination, migratory insertion into Ni-H, then beta-hydride elimination from the other beta carbon, releasing the shifted alkene and regenerating Ni-H.
The strongly donating NHC stabilizes a reactive low-coordinate metal and assists Si-H activation. Its steric bulk discourages unproductive saturated bis-alkene species and promotes product departure, but excessive crowding can hinder substrate binding/insertion. The silyl ligand also tunes the metal electronically: changing its aryl substituents changes donation and the barriers for hydride transfer/elimination. Product release and reversible insertion determine whether further double-bond migration occurs. Bulky ligands can suppress competing alkyl-silyl reductive elimination, which would instead consume substrate by hydrosilylation.
The hydride-silyl formulation and insertion/elimination interpretation are the experimentally proposed mechanism; the individual catalyst resting states need not all be uniquely fixed by the activation recipe.[ref]

Label the starting exomethylene carbon a, the aryl-bearing carbon b, the ethyl CH2​/CD2​ carbon c, and the terminal methyl d:
aCH2​=Cb​(Ar)−Cc​L2​−Cd​H3​.
(3)
Insertion into Ni-H places H on a and Ni on b. Elimination of a beta H or D from c gives CH3​−C(Ar)=C(L)−CH3​ and Ni-H or Ni-D. Thus the labeled substrate can retain one D on the new vinylic carbon while exporting the other into the catalyst hydride pool.
Ni-D can then add to the exomethylene of either aryl substrate, introducing D into the methyl group derived from a. Reversible insertion/elimination and substrate exchange also introduce D into the vinylic position of the originally unlabeled compound. This explains both the approximately comparable methyl labeling of the two products and the larger residual vinylic labeling of the initially CD2​-bearing substrate.
The decisive observation is intermolecular crossover of deuterium into the unsubstituted-phenyl product. It rules out a purely intramolecular, identity-preserving 1,3-H/D shift. The unequal percentages retain kinetic and substrate-history information; they are not a requirement for completely statistical H/D equilibration and cannot determine all elementary rate constants by themselves.

A Fischer carbene complex typically has a low-oxidation-state, relatively electron-rich middle/late transition metal with strong pi-acceptor ligands such as CO. The carbene is treated as a neutral two-electron donor; metal-to-carbene back-donation contributes to its bond, but its carbon remains electrophilic. An OR or NR2​ substituent stabilizes that carbon by heteroatom donation. Here the metal is Cr(0), d6, in an 18-electron pentacarbonyl complex.
A Schrock carbene complex usually contains an early metal in a high oxidation state, often d0, with electron-donating ligands rather than a shell of pi-accepting carbonyls. Its alkylidene has substantial dianionic character in ionic counting, and its carbon is correspondingly nucleophilic. Alkyl substituents are common; heteroatom stabilization of an electrophilic carbon is not the defining feature. These are bonding/electronic limiting descriptions rather than a rule based only on the drawn metal-carbon double bond.

Solution
Words: 177
ii OurBigBook.com
CO first dissociates from the 18-electron Fischer carbene. Pyridyl nitrogen of C then coordinates at the vacant chromium site, positioning an ortho C-D bond of its phenyl group beside the carbene unit.
The appropriate pathway is oxidative hydrogen migration: chromium assists C-D cleavage while D transfers to the electrophilic carbene carbon. The resulting cyclometalated intermediate has both an aryl-Cr bond and an alkyl-Cr bond, the latter to C(D)(OEt)(o−tolyl). Formally Cr(0) becomes Cr(II). Reductive elimination between those two carbon ligands forms the new aryl–C(D)(OEt)(o−tolyl) bond and returns chromium to low oxidation state.
This accounts for all three controls. Extra CO suppresses the coordinative vacancy. Biphenyl E lacks the pyridyl-N directing group and cannot organize the necessary C-H activation geometry. The ortho deuterium moves onto the former carbene carbon rather than being lost to solvent: four D remain on the original phenyl ring and the fifth becomes the benzylic C-D bond.
This is not adequately represented as electrophilic aromatic substitution followed by proton loss, which would discard the diagnostic D. The primary mechanistic study distinguishes oxidative hydrogen migration/reductive elimination from that classical alternative.[ref]

Hydroxide/boronic-acid association forms a Rh-O-B linkage; transfer of the aryl group from boron to rhodium, with Rh-O bond cleavage, gives the Rh-aryl initiator. Coordinate one diphenylacetylene and insert it into Rh-Ar. Syn migration puts the new Rh-C and Ar-C bonds on the same face of the former alkyne. Repeat alkyne coordination/insertion into the resulting Rh-vinyl bond. The chain after two insertions is
(COD)(L)Rh−C1​(Ph)=C2​(Ph)−C3​(Ph)=C4​(Ph)−Ar.
(5)
Numbering runs from the metal toward the initiating aryl group. Each insertion is syn: Rh and the continuing chain lie on the same side of the newly made double bond, giving the displayed cis relationship of the two Ph substituents at each inserted unit. The second alkyne inserts at the Rh end; it is not appended by coupling two free vinyl radicals.
To obtain F, promote PPh3​ dissociation, allowing G's dienyl group to fold and form the new C1​-C4​ bond. A four-pi-electron electrocyclic ring closure gives a cyclobutenyl ligand, stabilized by eta-three coordination of its allylic C1​-C2​-C3​ fragment to Rh. The substituted C4​ is the saturated ring carbon bearing Ar and Ph.
The thermal four-pi closure is conrotatory. Ring closure plus eta-three binding restores a 16-electron Rh(I) centre after phosphine loss; no net oxidation-state change is needed. F is an eta-three cyclobutenyl complex, not an eta-four cyclobutadiene with five substituents squeezed onto four unsaturated carbons.

a OurBigBook.com
A warmer troposphere does not imply a warmer stratosphere: increased carbon dioxide can cool the middle and upper stratosphere by enhancing infrared emission. One direct kinetic effect is slower temperature-dependent destruction in the Chapman cycle, tending to increase upper-stratospheric ozone. Two indirect chemical effects can have the opposite sign. If the entry atmospheric cold trap warms, it can admit more water; reaction with an electronically excited oxygen atom then supplies more of the HOx family, enhancing catalytic ozone loss. In cold polar air, changes in water and temperature can favour polar stratospheric clouds, whose surfaces activate halogen reservoirs and can enhance ozone destruction when sunlight returns. These are conditional pathways, not a universal sign for total ozone: cold-trap temperature and circulation, halogen abundance and altitude all matter. Methane oxidation can also supply stratospheric water.[ref]

For one specified channel, the photolysis rate coefficient is
J(z)=∫Fλ​(z)σλ​(T)Φλ​(T)dλ.
(7)
It depends on spectral actinic flux, absorption cross section, and channel quantum yield. Solar zenith angle, season, overhead absorbers, clouds and aerosol scattering affect the photon field; branching and collisional quenching affect the chemical yield. Absorption alone does not guarantee the desired dissociation.
Curve A is oxygen photodissociation; curve B is ozone photodissociation. Molecular oxygen needs relatively short-wavelength ultraviolet photons, which are strongly screened at lower altitudes, so its rate increases steeply with height. Ozone can dissociate using longer wavelengths that penetrate much further, producing the much larger, less height-sensitive rate shown by B. The plotted quantity is a first-order coefficient with units s−1, rather than a bimolecular rate constant.

For a real nonmagnetic T1​ electronic triplet, the symmetric square of a group representation decomposes as
Sym2T1​=A1​+E+T2​.
(41)
The A1​ coordinate shifts all components equally and does not remove the degeneracy. The linear Jahn-Teller selection rule therefore permits E and T2​ vibrational modes to couple and split the triplet. Both methane T2​ vibrational triplets are symmetry-eligible. A nonzero allowed coupling generally produces a symmetry-lowering instability, although symmetry alone does not determine its strength or the preferred minimum.

The difficulty is competing chemistry and isolation selectivity. Alkaline treatment can promote dehydrohalogenation at chlorinated backbone units while hydrolysing the acetate side groups. Loss of HCl produces unsaturated segments, potentially followed by further degradation or cross-linking; hydroxyl formation also changes solvent compatibility and intermolecular association. The isolated material can therefore differ from the ideal undegraded C in part (e). Competing dehydrochlorination during copolymer saponification explains why selective conditions are needed.
This is not an absolute prohibition on hydroxyl-containing chloride copolymers. Analytical work on these copolymers used staged alcoholysis/saponification and polymer precipitation to restrain dehydrochlorination. The sensible conclusion is that clean, exhaustive conversion and isolation are difficult under unselective conditions, rather than that no suitable treatment can ever preserve chloride units.[ref]
