A=K[B5​H8​] is nido; B=K[B6​H11​] and C=B6​H12​ are arachno. The initial hydride removes an acidic bridging hydrogen as a proton, releasing H2​ and retaining the potassium counterion. Addition of one BH3​ unit expands the boron framework; final protonation supplies the neutral hexaborane and KCl. These assignments conserve all the atoms in the displayed sequence, without inserting an unshown dehydrogenation step.
For Bn​Hmq−​, take one terminal B–H bond per vertex and remove its 2n electrons from the valence total. The skeletal electron count is 3n+m+q−2n=n+m+q. Thus

Wade's rules assign n+1, n+2 and n+3 pairs to closo, nido and arachno frameworks respectively. A derives from a six-vertex closo octahedron by removal of one vertex, giving a square-pyramidal boron skeleton. B and C have six-vertex open frameworks formally derived from an eight-vertex closo polyhedron by removing two vertices. Their additional hydrogens are distributed over bridging and other open-face positions; counting every hydrogen as an independent terminal B–H vertex would give the wrong classification.
The arachno assignment of [B6​H11​]− has specific theoretical and NMR support. It should not be replaced by a permanently localized, unrearranged [B5​H8​]−⋅BH3​ picture: the corresponding symmetric adduct participates in fluxional rearrangement rather than being the lowest-energy structure.[ref]
