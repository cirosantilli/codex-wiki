Write L=[C2​B9​H11​]2− for the open-face dicarbollide ligand. The structure consistent with the stated attachment is [LMo(CO)3​→Mo(CO)5​]2−, with a direct Mo–Mo connection, three carbonyls on the cage-capping Mo and five on the outside Mo. The first Mo has an η5 interaction with the five-vertex face and completes a 12-vertex metallacarborane cage; the second is exo to that cage. The two tetramethylammonium ions are counterions.
The unsaturated Mo(CO)5​ fragment is a 16-electron Lewis acceptor; [LMo(CO)3​]2− is an 18-electron metal-base fragment in ionic counting, d6+6e(L)+6e(3CO). Donation from a filled Mo-based orbital supplies the external fragment with a pair. This donor–acceptor representation avoids the mistaken claim that both initially saturated 18-electron fragments simply add a new conventional bond without changing anything.
An alternative formal oxidation-state allocation for a single covalent Mo–Mo bond is cage Mo(I) and external Mo(−I): the local counts are d5+6+6+1=18 and d7+10+1=18. These are electron-bookkeeping alternatives to the metal-base/metal-acid picture, not a measurement of integral site charges. With terminal carbonyls, the external Mo(CO)5​ group can be approximately octahedral when the Mo–Mo direction occupies its sixth coordination direction. The cage-containing unit must accommodate an exo metal interaction in addition to its facial cage/CO environment; its geometry need not be a perfectly undistorted isolated half-sandwich.
