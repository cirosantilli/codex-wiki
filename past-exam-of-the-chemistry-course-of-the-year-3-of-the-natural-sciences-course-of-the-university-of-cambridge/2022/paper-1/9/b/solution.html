React pentaborane with an alkyne under relatively mild conditions to obtain a nido C2​B4​H8​ framework, conventionally nido-2,3-dicarbahexaborane(8) for acetylene. The two entering carbon atoms initially retain their adjacency. A specific mild example uses 2-butyne with triethylamine-assisted B5​H9​ at or below room temperature to give nido-Me2​C2​B4​H6​. This is a practical route to an explicitly nido carborane, rather than an assumption that an alkyne supplies a closo cage under every condition.[ref]
More energetic gas-phase reaction or pyrolysis promotes hydrogen loss, boron-fragment loss and cage rearrangement, giving closo carboranes, for example 1,5-C2​B3​H5​ and 1,6-C2​B4​H6​, with other cage sizes possible in the mixture. Thermolysis of a previously formed nido carborane is an alternative route to this stage. The point is not a clean same-composition nido-to-closo isomerization: the change of hydrogen content and sometimes cage size must be retained in the formulas.
Milder conditions favour kinetically trapped adjacent-carbon products; higher temperatures permit migration to carbon-separated arrangements, often increasing favourable B–C contacts relative to adjacent C–C/B–B contacts. Conditions and Lewis bases also influence which separated-carbon cage is isolated; temperature alone does not prescribe a single quantitative isomer distribution. Primary thermolysis studies and the small-carborane literature document both cage rearrangement and reagent-dependent product mixtures.[ref]
