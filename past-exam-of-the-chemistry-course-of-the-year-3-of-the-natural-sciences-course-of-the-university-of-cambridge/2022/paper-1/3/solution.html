Solid MoCl2​ contains octahedral Mo6​ clusters, while α-MoCl3​ has paired molybdenum centres in a layered chloride lattice. The first material is better represented by Mo6​Cl12​ than by isolated bent or linear MoCl2​ molecules. Eight inner chlorides cap the triangular faces of an octahedral metal core, giving [Mo6​(μ3​−Cl)8​]4+. Outer chloride coordination completes the solid: one useful connectivity notation is Mo6​Cl8​Cl2​Cl4/2​, with two terminal outer chlorides and four intercluster-shared outer chlorides per octahedron. The empirical total is 8+2+4/2=12 chlorides for six metals.
Each Mo(II) is formally d4, giving 24 metal-based electrons. These support extensive Mo–Mo bonding along the 12 octahedral edges; they are not localized as four free spins on each molybdenum. In α-MoCl3​, Mo(III) is d3 in edge-sharing MoCl6​ octahedral layers. The metal honeycomb distorts into short Mo–Mo pairs. A localized dimer picture uses the six d electrons to fill one sigma and two pi bonding combinations, producing a formal triple bond and pairing the spins. The bulk crystal remains an extended chloride lattice, rather than a collection of unconnected Mo2​Cl6​ molecules.
By comparison, CrCl2​ has predominantly localized high-spin Cr(II), d4, in Jahn–Teller-distorted chloride octahedra; CrCl3​ has layered Cr(III), d3, with three locally unpaired electrons. Their magnetic exchange is not evidence for the same strong short multiple bonds as in the molybdenum solids. The greater radial extent of Mo 4d orbitals enables larger metal–metal overlap than the more contracted Cr 3d orbitals. Real structure and magnetism still depend on ligand geometry and phase, so the orbital comparison is a trend rather than a prohibition of chromium–chromium bonds in other compounds. Diffraction and magnetic/spectroscopic studies directly distinguish the dimerized α-MoCl3​ lattice from CrCl3​.[ref]
