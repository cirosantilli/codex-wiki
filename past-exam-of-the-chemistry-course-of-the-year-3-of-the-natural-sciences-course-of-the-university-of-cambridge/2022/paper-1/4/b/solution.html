Radiation changes the solution's chemistry by radiolysis; it does not turn Am(VI) nuclei into Am(V) nuclei. An emitted alpha particle deposits energy into many water and solute molecules, generating ionized/excited species, hydrated electrons, hydrogen atoms and molecular products including hydrogen peroxide. Reducing products can supply the electron needed for
AmO22+​+e−⟶AmO2+​.
(3)
For example, hydrogen peroxide can be oxidized while reducing two americyl(VI) ions:
2AmO22+​+H2​O2​⟶2AmO2+​+O2​+2H+.
(4)
The 0.3 mM loss of Am(VI) equals the 0.3 mM appearance of Am(V), preserving the measured dissolved americium total while transferring 0.3mmolL−1 of electron equivalents. Oxidizing radiolysis products are also present, and the net reduction depends on acid, dissolved gases and scavengers; not every radiolytic event is reducing. In nitric acid, primary kinetic work identifies hydrogen peroxide and radiolytically produced nitrous acid as important reductants, with a rate controlled by total americium activity rather than necessarily by the instantaneous Am(VI) concentration.[ref]
