All three marked points are at strongly oxidizing potentials, where X is depleted at the electrode: cX​(0)≃0. Assuming only X initially in bulk, cX+​(∞)=0 and cX​(∞)=cb​. Oxidation produces X+ at the interface.
  • A: the largest steady limiting current corresponds to the fastest rotation and thinnest diffusion boundary layer. X rises steeply from zero to cb​; X+ falls steeply from its surface value to zero.
  • B: slower rotation gives a thicker layer and shallower gradients, hence a smaller limiting current.
  • C: the nonrotating electrode gives the peaked, subsequently decaying current. Its diffusion layer continues to broaden with time, approximately as Dt​, giving the broadest and shallowest profiles at the indicated late-scan point.
For equal diffusion coefficients, the two concentration profiles are complementary, cX​+cX+​=cb​. More generally equality of their interfacial molar fluxes requires DX​cX′​(0)=−DX+​cX+′​(0), so the product surface concentration need not equal cb​. A and B are approximately steady hydrodynamic profiles; C is a time-dependent diffusion profile.
