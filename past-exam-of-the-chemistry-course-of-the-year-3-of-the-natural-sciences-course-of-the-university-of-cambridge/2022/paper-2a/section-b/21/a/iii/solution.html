A spectral component shifts by the difference between its upper- and lower-level Stark shifts. With ΔJ=+1, ΔK=0 and ΔM=q, the supplied energy formula gives
δν=hcμEfield​​K[(J+1)(J+2)M+q​−J(J+1)M​].
(21)
For radiation polarized parallel to the static field, q=0, so
δν=−hcJ(J+1)(J+2)2μEfield​KM​.​
(22)
Thus the assigned K=0 feature has no first-order splitting, whereas the K=0 features acquire symmetrically arranged magnetic-sublevel components with spacings proportional to ∣K∣. With perpendicular radiation, q=±1 gives the corresponding different allowed pattern. Recording field-dependent spectra with specified polarization would test both the J values and the K sequence; using an individual level's shift alone would not predict the observed line positions. Higher-order shifts of the nominally unsplit feature are outside this first-order test.
