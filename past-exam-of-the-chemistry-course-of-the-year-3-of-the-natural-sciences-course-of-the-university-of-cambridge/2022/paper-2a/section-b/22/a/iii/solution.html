Past exam of the chemistry course of the year 3 of the natural sciences course of the University of Cambridge / 2022 / Paper 2A / Section B / 22 / a / iii / Solution

Successive differences remove the unknown absolute origin and the unknown initial vibrational quantum number. The five spacings are 329.3, 296.0, 261.7, 226.7 and 190.7 cm−1. Plot them at relative mid-indices x=n+1/2. A straight-line fit gives
Δν(x)≃347.505−34.650xcm−1,x∗​≃10.029.
(32)
The curve reaches zero spacing at x∗​. Since a quadratic Morse progression has a spacing equal to its derivative at the interval midpoint, integrating the fitted line from the first observed relative level n=0 to x∗​ estimates the energy still needed to reach the continuous limit:
νlim​≃56298.9+∫0x∗​​(347.505−34.650x)dx=56298.9+2(34.650)347.5052​≃58041.5 cm−1.
(33)
Thus the appropriate graphical conclusion is νlim​≈5.80×104 cm−1, not a many-digit threshold. Directly fitting a quadratic to the six measured transition positions puts its vertex at about 58042.8 cm−1, which checks the arithmetic but not the physical extrapolation law. The slight variation of second differences shows that extrapolating a constant anharmonicity beyond the observed range is approximate. No assignment of the arbitrary index n to an absolute upper-state v is needed.
