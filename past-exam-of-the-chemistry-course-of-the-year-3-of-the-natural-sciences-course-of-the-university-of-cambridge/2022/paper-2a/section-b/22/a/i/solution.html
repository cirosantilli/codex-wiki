The lower ground-state vibrational wavefunction is localized near its equilibrium bond length. If the two equilibrium lengths are similar, it overlaps strongly with the upper v′=0 wavefunction, but its overlap with highly excited upper vibrational states is usually small or suffers oscillatory cancellation. If the equilibrium lengths differ enough that a vertical excitation reaches the outer portion of the upper well, high-v′ transitions can be strong while the 0→0 overlap is small. Thus the two ends of a long progression are not normally both intense. This is an overlap argument, not a ban on either set of transitions; anharmonicity, a varying electronic transition moment and unusual wavefunctions can modify the intensity envelope.
