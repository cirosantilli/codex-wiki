For two electrons, n+1+D<4 whenever 0<n<1: both sides of D<3−n are positive and squaring reduces the inequality to n<1. Since β<0, the trapezium then has a higher total electronic energy than the square. The two-electron model favours the square. For four electrons, D>2, so 2βD<4β: the four-electron model favours the distortion toward a trapezium. The lower split middle orbital is occupied while the upper one is empty.
The distinction is especially clear near the square. Write δ=1−n>0; then D=2+δ2/4+O(δ4) and

In particular the four-electron lowering in this specified one-edge model is quadratic near the square; a first-order lowering of the entire occupied sum should not be asserted merely from the middle-level splitting. This Hückel square-to-trapezium distortion is an electronic comparison under the stated fixed-α, fixed-short-edge-β assumptions. Nuclear repulsion, other bonding contributions and the structural cost of distortion are omitted, so an actual equilibrium geometry or optimum n is not determined by this calculation alone.
