Stable construction of a Givens rotation (r=hypot(a,b))

 Home Mathematics Area of mathematics Analysis Numerical analysis Givens rotation
OurBigBook.com Words: 293
For finite real numbers a,b, a Givens rotation that annihilates b is defined mathematically by
r=hypot(a,b)=a2+b2​,(c,s)={(a/r,b/r),(1,0),​r>0,a=b=0.​
(210)
Here hypot means an evaluation of the Euclidean norm of (a,b)T designed to avoid unnecessary range failures in floating-point arithmetic. The zero case selects the identity matrix, avoiding division by zero. If a=0,b=0, then c=0,s=sign(b); if b=0,a=0, then c=sign(a),s=0. Thus the retained component is nonnegative, including when a<0. In exact arithmetic, ca+sb=r and −sa+cb=0.
Computing the square root of a*a + b*b directly can fail even when the final norm is representable. In binary64 floating-point arithmetic, a=b=10308 makes the squares overflow, whereas r≈1.4142×10308 is finite. With a=b=10−300 the squares round to zero, although r≈1.4142×10−300 is representable. A range-safe hypot avoids these intermediate failures.
The simple divisions a/r,b/r still need care when r is a subnormal number: rounding r can cause large relative errors. For the smallest positive binary64 subnormal number η=2−1074, taking a=b=η rounds 2​η to η; dividing by that rounded value gives c=s=1, which is not an orthogonal matrix. For nonzero finite inputs, compute the coefficients before rescaling the norm:
t=max(∣a∣,∣b∣),α=a/t,β=b/t,h=hypot(α,β),c=α/h,s=β/h,r=th.
(211)
At least one scaled component has absolute value one, and 1≤h≤2​. This construction keeps the coefficients meaningful even when rounding r is severe. It cannot make an unrepresentably large r finite, preserve an arbitrarily tiny s against underflow, or repair inputs already rounded to zero. The row updates themselves can also overflow if their exact results exceed the available range.
LAPACK's DLARTG uses scaling for numerical stability but a different sign convention: for a=0 it chooses r=sign(a)a2+b2​ and c≥0; for b=0 it returns c=1,s=0,r=a. Its block still has the lower-left minus sign. Do not combine its returned coefficients with an assumed nonnegative r.[ref]
