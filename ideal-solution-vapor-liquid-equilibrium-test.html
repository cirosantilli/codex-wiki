The Mixture measurements and the Gibbs-Duhem constraint article describes the inspected acetone–chloroform preparation, apparatus, paired readouts, controls and sampling limits.[ref]
That existing apparatus account supplies the experimental controls and limitations. Its refractive index calibration yields mass fractions, which must be converted to mole fractions. For a measured component-A mass fraction wA​ and component molar masses MA​,MB​,
xA​=wA​/MA​+(1−wA​)/MB​wA​/MA​​.
(25)
Apply this conversion separately to the flask sample and to completely condensed vapor to obtain xA​ and yA​. Comparing yi​P with xi​pi∗​(T) tests Raoult's law at the measured temperature; using the changing temperature at each composition is essential. The chosen A/B values above are unrelated to the protocol's substances.
The more general diagnostic, retaining an ideal gas vapor and negligible pure-liquid pressure correction, is
yi​P=xi​γi​pi∗​(T),γi​≃xi​pi∗​(T)yi​P​.
(26)
This follows by replacing lnxi​ with ln(xi​γi​) in the chemical potential subtraction used for Raoult's law. A reproducible discrepancy from γi​=1 can expose failure of the ideal solution approximation, but interpreting it requires checking measurement uncertainty, representative paired sampling and the ideal gas approximation. Apparent agreement within uncertainty supports an approximation over the tested conditions; it cannot establish exact ideality at every composition.
Positive or negative deviations of component partial pressures require activity coefficients different from one under these assumptions. An azeotrope is also absent from the chosen unequal-vapor pressure model: the explicit inequality in ideal binary vapor composition gives yA​>xA​ throughout 0<xA​<1. An interior equality would require pA∗​=pB∗​; in that degenerate case every composition has yA​=xA​, rather than an isolated enrichment reversal. Neither an azeotrope nor liquid phase separation should be inferred from the ideal curves.
