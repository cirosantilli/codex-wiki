Bragg path difference (Δℓ=2dsinθ)

 ... Quantum mechanics Quantum scattering Scattering amplitude Crystal scattering Elastic Bragg scattering condition Bragg's law
OurBigBook.com Words: 197
Compare two rays scattered into parallel outgoing directions from neighboring lattice planes. In the diagram, A lies on the upper plane and B on the lower plane, directly beneath A with AB=d. Draw through A the incoming and outgoing equal-wave phase fronts, perpendicular to their respective rays. They meet the lower ray at C and D. Each right triangle gives CB=BD=dsinθ, so the lower ray travels the extra distance
Δℓ=CB+BD=2dsinθ.
(28)
The resulting phase difference is Δφ=2πΔℓ/λ. Constructive interference requires Δφ=2πn, which proves Bragg's law in this ray construction. Comparing equal-wave phase fronts, rather than arbitrary ray endpoints, is what makes the distance comparison meaningful.
The same geometry connects to the reciprocal lattice. With wavenumber k=2π/λ, the elastic Bragg scattering condition gives a transfer magnitude ∣q∣=2ksinθ. A reciprocal vector normal to these lattice planes has ∣q∣=2πn/d. Equating them again yields Bragg's law. The ray and reciprocal lattice descriptions therefore express the same wave phase matching.
