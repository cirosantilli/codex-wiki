Draining tank with constant cross-sectional area (h(t)=H(1−t/T)2)

 Home Physics Branches of physics Fluid mechanics Torricelli's law
OurBigBook.com Words: 430
Take a reservoir with constant horizontal cross-sectional area A, one small orifice of area a, no inflow, and constant stipulated discharge coefficient Cd​>0. Let the initial head be H>0. For constant mass density ρ, the stored mass above the outlet level is ρAh and outgoing mass flow rate is ρQ. Mass conservation cancels ρ and gives the initial value problem
Adtdh​=−Q,dtdh​=−kh​,h(0)=H,k=ACd​a​2g​.
(40)
The downward speed of the free surface is Q/A, smaller than the ideal jet speed by the factor Cd​a/A. Thus a small opening relative to the reservoir supports the negligible-surface-velocity assumption.
For h>0, separation of variables or the chain rule gives dh​/dt=−k/2. Integrating yields
T=k2H​​=Cd​aA​g2H​​,h(t)={H(1−t/T)2,0,​0≤t≤T,t≥T,​
(41)
Q(t)={Q0​(1−t/T),0,​0≤t≤T,t≥T,​Q0​=Cd​a2gH​.
(42)
The model reaches zero head in finite time even though volumetric flow rate tends to zero. Extending the squared expression beyond T would make head grow again and violate the original ordinary differential equation. The zero-head continuation enforces no further outflow. For an elevated side opening, zero head means the surface has reached that opening, leaving fluid below it; T is not the time to remove every drop from such a tank.
For an original synthetic example, stipulate
A=0.100 m2,a=1.00×10−4 m2,Cd​=0.620,H=0.250 m,g=9.81 ms−2.
(43)
The initial volume above outlet level is AH=0.0250 m3=25.0 L. The initial ideal jet speed is 2.215 ms−1, the initial volumetric flow rate is Q0​=0.1373 Ls−1, and the predicted zero-head time is T=364.1 s. These are consequences of stipulated parameters, not experimental measurements. At t=T/2=182.1 s, head is H/4=0.0625 m, stored volume is 6.25 L, and volumetric flow rate is Q0​/2=0.06866 Ls−1. Half the initial volume has already left at t=T(1−1/2​)=106.7 s.
An independent mass conservation check integrates the outgoing volumetric flow rate:
∫0t​Q(s)ds=Q0​(t−2Tt2​)=A[H−h(t)](0≤t≤T),∫0T​Q(s)ds=2Q0​T​=AH.
(44)
Multiply both sides by mass density to obtain discharged mass. A dimensional analysis check gives [k]=m1/2s−1, [Q]=m3s−1 and [T]=s. The triangular area under Q(t) explains why the initial-rate estimate AH/Q0​ is only half the actual model time. Doubling A doubles T; doubling a or Cd​ halves T, within the same assumptions.
This finite-time result is an idealized prediction. The constant Cd​ and quasi-steady approximation need not survive very small head or the initial startup. Appreciable viscosity in a narrow outlet tube requires a different pressure balance, as in Poiseuille flow; appreciable surface tension can prevent zero head altogether.
