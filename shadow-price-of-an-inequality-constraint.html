Shadow price of an inequality constraint (∂V/∂bi​=−λi​)

A shadow price measures the marginal improvement from relaxing an inequality optimization constraint. Use L=f+∑i​λi​(gi​−bi​)+∑j​μj​hj​ for gi​(x)≤bi​ in a minimization problem. With strong duality and a differentiable optimal value function, ∂V/∂bi​=−λi​.[ref]
In the quadratic minimization with a moving upper bound, direct differentiation gives
V′(b)={−2(2−b),0,​b<2,b≥2,​V′(b)=−λ∗​(b).
(78)
The derivative at b=2 is zero from both sides, although x∗​(b) has different one-sided derivatives there. The value of additional room has faded continuously to zero just as the active constraint stops restricting the optimizer.
At b=1, x∗​=1, V=1 and λ∗​=2. Raising the bound to 1.1 lowers the value to 0.81: the first-order prediction is a change of −2(0.1)=−0.2, while the exact change is −0.19. At b=3, the same relaxation leaves x∗​=2 and V=0. At b=2, tightening by 0.1 costs 0.01, a second-order effect despite the zero Lagrange multiplier.
There is also a global statement in this example. Keeping the Lagrange multiplier at the value associated with b0​, the Lagrange dual function changes by −λ∗​(b0​)(b−b0​). Weak duality therefore gives
V(b)≥V(b0​)−λ∗​(b0​)(b−b0​).
(79)
At b0​=1 this is V(b)≥3−2b, the supporting tangent shown in the diagram. This bound remains valid for large changes, whereas the derivative predicts only the first-order effect. If the value function is not differentiable, an optimal Lagrange multiplier can instead supply a supporting subgradient, rather than a unique marginal rate. This is the same idea used in sensitivity analysis in convex perturbation duality.
