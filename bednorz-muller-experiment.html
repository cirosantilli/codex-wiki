Bednorz-Müller experiment

 Home Physics Branches of physics Statistical physics Superconductivity High-temperature superconductivity
OurBigBook.com Words: 160
In 1986, Johannes Georg Bednorz and Karl Alexander Müller precipitated mixed oxalates from aqueous barium, lanthanum and copper nitrates using oxalic acid. Heating at 900∘C for five hours preceded pressing at 4kbar and reheating for sintering. X-ray diffraction resolved three phases. Rectangular pieces carried gold electrodes and indium wires; four-terminal sensing in a continuous-flow cryostat measured electrical resistance versus temperature. Annealing atmosphere and measuring electric current density were varied; larger electric current densities suppressed the drop. An onset near 35K differed from the much lower temperature where the drop approached three orders of magnitude.[ref]
This was evidence for possible granular superconductivity, with phase identification unfinished. The paper deferred Meissner effect and heat capacity tests; its proof note corrected bulk lanthanum/barium ratios. A resistive onset did not establish a uniform bulk superconducting transition temperature.[ref]
Their 1987 authored lecture describes later magnetometer checks on lead, followed by magnetic susceptibility measurements: samples with a resistive drop developed diamagnetism at somewhat lower temperatures. Combining magnetic, transport and X-ray diffraction results identified barium-substituted La2​CuO4​ as the superconducting phase. These later results strengthened the case beyond the initial mixed-phase transport evidence.[ref]
