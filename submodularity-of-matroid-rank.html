Submodularity of matroid rank (r(A)+r(B)≥r(A∩B)+r(A∪B))

 Home Mathematics Area of mathematics Combinatorics Matroid Rank of a matroid
OurBigBook.com Words: 139
The rank of a matroid is a normalised set function, a monotone set function, and a submodular set function. Adding one element increases it by at most one.
Proof of submodularity. Choose a maximal matroid independent set I0​ in A∩B, extend it to a maximal matroid independent set IA​ in A, then extend it to one IU​ in A∪B. The matroid independence axioms make their sizes r(A∩B), r(A) and r(A∪B). Every element of IU​∖IA​ lies outside A, since otherwise it could be added to IA​ inside A. Hence I0​∪(IU​∖IA​) is an independent subset of B, so its size r(A∩B)+r(A∪B)−r(A) is at most r(B).
The remaining assertions follow from the definition of the rank of a matroid. The empty set has maximum independent size zero, increasing the available subset cannot reduce that maximum, and deleting a newly added element from an independent subset decreases its size by at most one.
