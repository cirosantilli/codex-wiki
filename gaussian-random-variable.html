Gaussian random variable (X∼N(μ,σ2))

 ... Mathematics Area of mathematics Probability and statistics Probability theory Probability distribution Normal distribution
OurBigBook.com Words: 338 Articles: 4
A Gaussian random variable is a random variable whose probability distribution is a normal distribution. For σ>0, write X=μ+σZ with a standard normal random variable Z; conversely, standardization of a normal random variable recovers Z=(X−μ)/σ. For example, X∼N(100,152) has mean 100, standard deviation 15, and variance 225. The outcome x=115 corresponds to standard score z=1, one standard deviation above the mean.
Some definitions also allow a Gaussian random variable with σ=0. This means X=μ almost surely, with variance zero and probability distribution equal to the Dirac measure δμ​. It has no probability density function with respect to Lebesgue measure: P(X=μ)=1. The positive-scale density formula and division by σ apply only when σ>0. MIT's constructive definition explicitly includes the constant case. [ref].
