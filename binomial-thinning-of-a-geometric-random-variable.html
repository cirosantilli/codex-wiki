Binomial thinning of a geometric random variable (p′=p+r(1−p)p​)

 ... Area of mathematics Probability and statistics Probability theory Random variable Random sum Binomial thinning
OurBigBook.com Words: 50
Use the zero-based geometric distribution, with P(N=k)=p(1−p)k for k≥0 and 0<p<1. After binomial thinning with retention probability 0≤r≤1, the retained count is again zero-based geometrically distributed, with the displayed success probability. Indeed, writing q=1−p, its probability generating function is
GY​(s)=1−q(1−r+rs)p​=1−(1−p′)sp′​.
(3)
For r=0 this includes the degenerate success parameter p′=1 and retained count zero.
