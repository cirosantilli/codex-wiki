Density of a composite system (ρtotal​=(mA​+mB​)/(VA​+VB​))

OurBigBook.com Words: 198
Consider two hypothetical parcels at the same temperature 300K, kept separated with negligible partition mass and excluded container volume. Parcel A has mass 2kg and volume 0.002m3; parcel B has mass 3kg and volume 0.006m3. They may be different materials. Their individual mass densities and the composite mass density are
ρA​=1000kgm−3,ρB​=500kgm−3,ρtotal​=0.008m35kg​=625kgm−3.
(82)
The composite mass density is total material mass divided by total occupied volume; it need not be the local mass density anywhere. Equal temperature does not make these parcels replicas. In particular, the unweighted average 750kgm−3 is wrong. The volume fractions are 1/4 and 3/4, giving
ρtotal​=41​ρA​+43​ρB​=625kgm−3.
(83)
With mass fractions 2/5 and 3/5, the equivalent rule is a weighted harmonic mean:
ρtotal​1​=ρA​2/5​+ρB​3/5​=0.0016m3kg−1.
(84)
This follows directly by writing Vi​=mi​/ρi​ and dividing total volume by total mass; it is also the mass-weighted average of specific volumes. An unweighted average of mass densities is valid when the parcel volume values are equal, not merely when their temperatures are equal.
If a partition is removed and different substances mix, the final volume need not equal the sum of initial volume values. A new temperature, thermodynamic phase or composition may also change the local mass density. Use the actual final volume in ρ=m/V; the pre-mixing weighted identity cannot supply it. Even unchanged temperature alone does not guarantee unchanged volume.
