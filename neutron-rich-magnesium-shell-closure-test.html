Neutron-rich magnesium shell-closure test

 Home Physics Nuclear physics Atomic nucleus Nuclear shell model Empirical evidence for nuclear shells
OurBigBook.com Words: 72
A 2013 RIKEN experiment used a fast calcium beam on beryllium, selected radioactive aluminium and silicon fragments, then struck carbon to produce magnesium isotopes with N=22,24,26. Emitted gamma rays supplied excitation energies; comparison across these isotopes supported deformation extending beyond the N=20 region toward 28. This tests the limits of traditional magic numbers, not merely their arithmetic. The inferred shape depends on nuclear-structure interpretation of the energy levels, rather than direct imaging.[ref]
