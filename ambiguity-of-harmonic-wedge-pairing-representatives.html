Ambiguity of harmonic wedge-pairing representatives (Ann(Hp)=imd⊕imδ)

 ... Geometry and topology Differential form Hodge star operator Harmonic differential form Hodge decomposition theorem Representing functionals on harmonic forms by wedge pairing
OurBigBook.com Words: 117
On a compact oriented Riemannian manifold without boundary, a smooth (n−p)-form χ annihilates all harmonic p-forms under wedge integration exactly when its harmonic projection vanishes. Indeed ∫M​φ∧χ=⟨∗φ,χ⟩L2​, and the Hodge star maps Hp isomorphically onto Hn−p. By the Hodge decomposition theorem, this annihilator is
dΩn−p−1⊕δΩn−p+1.
(22)
Thus both exact and coexact additions are allowed; a harmonic representative is unique. The potentials of these additions need not be unique, and out-of-range degree spaces are zero. For a concrete coexact ambiguity, take the torus (R/2πZ)2 with metric dx2+dy2. The form χ=δ(sinydx∧dy)=cosydx annihilates every harmonic one-form by orthogonality, yet dχ=sinydx∧dy is nonzero. Thus an annihilating representative need not even be closed, let alone exact.
