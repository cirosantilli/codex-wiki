Nearest-neighbour model of graphene (E±​(k)=±t∣f(k)∣)

Take a planar honeycomb lattice with two sites A,B per primitive cell and one localized atomic orbital per site. In this explicitly idealized tight-binding model, all three nearest-neighbour hopping matrix elements are −t, with t>0; there is no on-site offset or further-neighbour hopping. Ignore electron interactions and treat physical spin as a separate degeneracy. Let the bond length be b, and choose the three vectors from an A site to its neighbours as
δ1​=b(0,1),δ2​=b(3​/2,−1/2),δ3​=b(−3​/2,−1/2).
(20)

Its characteristic equation is E2=t2∣f∣2, giving the two displayed energy bands. The two-component structure comes from the two sites, rather than two values of physical spin. This is a particularly small matrix model in which a crystal's geometry creates an unexpected low-energy law.
Historically, Philip Russell Wallace used a tight-binding model to investigate the electronic band structure of graphite in 1947. The material in that paper was layered graphite; the single-sheet model here isolates one simpler part of the problem.[ref]
