Radial disintegration of an orthogonally invariant measure (∫fdμ=∫[0,∞)​∫Sd−1​f(rθ)dσ(θ)d(∣⋅∣#​μ)(r))

 ... Area of mathematics Analysis Real analysis Measure theory Measure Pushforward measure
OurBigBook.com Words: 111
An orthogonally invariant random vector has a probability measure determined by its radial pushforward measure. Here σ is normalized surface measure on a sphere, and the formula holds for bounded measurable f and for nonnegative measurable f. It includes an atom of a measure at zero: the inner integral there is simply f(0).
To prove the formula first for continuous bounded f, average f(Qx) over normalized Haar measure of the compact orthogonal group. Right invariance makes this average depend only on ∣x∣; averaging also over surface measure on a sphere identifies it with the spherical average. Invariance of μ and Fubini's theorem then give the identity. Uniqueness of finite Borel measures and measurable approximation extend it to the stated classes of f. No extra sphere-area factor belongs in an average against normalized surface measure on a sphere.
