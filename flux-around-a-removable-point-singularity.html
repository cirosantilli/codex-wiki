Flux around a removable point singularity (​∫∂Bε​​F⋅ndS​=O(εd−1+α))

 ... Analysis Real analysis Calculus Multivariable calculus Vector calculus Divergence theorem
OurBigBook.com Words: 102
A continuous extension of a vector field across one point need not be differentiable, so it does not automatically meet the classical hypotheses of the divergence theorem. Instead apply the divergence theorem after deleting a ball Bε​ around that point. If ∥F∥≤Cεα on its boundary in d dimensions, the magnitude of the inner flux is at most a constant times εd−1+α. When d−1+α>0 this tends to zero. If the divergence is also integrable, passing to the limit recovers the outer flux from the volume integral. For example, a planar vector field with ∥F∥≤r has an inner flux of order ε2, even when its extension is not differentiable at the origin.
