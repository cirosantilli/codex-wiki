Linear regression (Y=Xβ+ε)

 ... Mathematics Area of mathematics Probability and statistics Statistical model Statistical modelling Normal linear model
OurBigBook.com Words: 5k Articles: 58
Linear regression models a response's conditional expectation as a linear combination of specified predictors. In matrix form,
Y=Xβ+ε,E(ε∣X)=0,
(1)
where the design matrix X describes the observations, β contains the regression coefficients, and ε contains the errors. The fitted values Xβ​ estimate the response means. The model is linear in its regression coefficients; its columns may include powers, interactions, or other transformed predictors, so polynomial regression can fit curved relationships using the same machinery.
For a concrete example, take five constructed observations
(xi​,yi​)=(0,1),(1,2),(2,2),(3,4),(4,5).
(2)
An ordinary least squares fit with an intercept gives the line y​=0.8+x. Its regression residuals are (0.2,0.2,−0.8,0.2,0.2) and its residual sum of squares is 0.8. A one-unit increase in the predictor corresponds to a one-unit increase in the fitted response. This is a description of this fitted association; interpreting a change as a causal effect requires the assumptions of a causal inference problem.
The geometric reason for the fit is simple. The possible mean vectors Xβ form the column space of X. Ordinary least squares chooses the orthogonal projection of the data vector onto that vector subspace, so the remaining regression residual is orthogonal to every permitted fitted direction. The least-squares normal equations express this orthogonality as XT(Y−Xβ​)=0. The data's explained component and unexplained component are therefore separated by the same geometry used in a Hilbert space.
The optimization definition makes sense for any observed data. Quantifying the uncertainty of the regression coefficients needs additional assumptions about the sampling and errors. With fixed X of full column rank and mean-zero errors with covariance matrix σ2I, ordinary least squares is unbiased and has covariance σ2(XTX)−1; the Gauss-Markov theorem compares it with other linear unbiased estimators. A normal linear model additionally makes Student t-distribution intervals and tests exact in finite samples. Model adequacy and predictive assessment remain relevant even when these calculations are available.
Two historical settings help explain the subject's appeal. Adrien-Marie Legendre's Nouvelles méthodes pour la détermination des orbites des comètes (1805) includes an appendix on the method of least squares: observations give more equations than unknowns, and squared errors provide a criterion for reconciling them. Its astronomical setting shows the motivation for combining imperfect measurements. [ref] Francis Galton's Regression Towards Mediocrity in Hereditary Stature (1886) examines the association between parental and offspring heights and the tendency of group averages toward the population average. This is a historical use of the regression name; the modern linear model is a general statistical formulation. [ref]

Log-linear exponential-decay fit (y=ln(R/R∗​),m=−1/τ,c=ln(R0​/R∗​))

Words: 162
Linear regression OurBigBook.com
An exponential decay rate R=R0​e−t/τ becomes a straight line in x=t and y=ln(R/R∗​), where R∗​ is a fixed reference rate. Constant small fractional rate errors become approximately constant vertical errors s≃σR​/R by propagation of uncertainty. With independent Gaussian log-rate errors, linear least squares gives m=Sxy​/Sxx​ and c=yˉ​−mxˉ.
The covariance matrix is obtained by applying the variance rules to these linear estimators:
Varm=Sxx​s2​,Varc=s2(n1​+Sxx​xˉ2​),Cov(m,c)=−Sxx​s2xˉ​.
(3)
Transforming back gives τ=−1/m, στ​≃σm​/m2, R0​=R∗​ec and σR0​​≃R0​σc​. If a candidate lifetime fixes the slope ma​, the intercept must be refitted as ca​=yˉ​−ma​xˉ. The profiled objective obeys
χ2(ma​,ca​)=χmin2​+s2Sxx​​(ma​−m)2.
(4)
This follows by separating the centred slope residual from the unrestricted regression residual, whose inner product with the design columns is zero by the normal equations for linear least squares. Known supplied measurement errors are not rescaled by the observed reduced chi-squared. This log-error model is an approximation for proportional errors, not an exact replacement for every raw-count likelihood.

Multiple linear regression models a scalar response by an intercept and several predictors. It is linear in the regression coefficients, even when predictors include transformed variables or interactions. In an observational study, adjustment can address measured confounding, but does not automatically identify a causal effect. Normal equations for linear least squares (XTXθ=XTy)

Words: 148
Linear regression OurBigBook.com
Minimizing ∥y−Xθ∥2 gives the displayed equations by differentiation. Equivalently, the regression residual r=y−Xθ satisfies XTr=0: it is orthogonal to every column of the design matrix. If X has full column rank, the minimizer is unique and the fitted values are the orthogonal projection of y onto its column space. A rank-deficient design matrix can give multiple coefficient vectors, while the projected fitted vector is still unique.
For an intercept and one predictor, put Sxx​=∑i​(xi​−xˉ)2 and Sxy​=∑i​(xi​−xˉ)(yi​−yˉ​). The equations give β​=Sxy​/Sxx​ and α=yˉ​−β​xˉ, provided Sxx​>0. In the five-point example in linear regression, xˉ=2, yˉ​=2.8, Sxx​=10 and Sxy​=10, giving β​=1 and α=0.8.
The normal equations describe the mathematical optimum, but explicitly forming and inverting XTX can amplify numerical problems. A QR decomposition computes the same least-squares solution through orthogonal coordinates; a singular value decomposition also exposes nearly dependent columns. This connects statistical fitting to numerical linear algebra and the choice of a stable algorithm. [ref]

Omitted-variable bias

Words: 65
Linear regression OurBigBook.com
If the true conditional mean is β0​+β1​X+β2​Z, the population slope from regressing on X alone is β1​+β2​Cov(X,Z)/Var(X), assuming a mean-zero error uncorrelated with the predictors. The omitted predictor can steepen, flatten, or reverse the pooled association. The same identity holds for fitted coefficients and empirical covariances when comparing nested ordinary least-squares fits. Adjustment changes the comparison being described; a causal interpretation requires additional assumptions.

Predictor centering (z=x−c)

Words: 50
Linear regression OurBigBook.com
Replacing a numerical predictor x by x−c preserves the fitted mean space and slope in a linear regression containing an intercept. The intercept becomes the fitted mean at x=c. In simple linear regression, centering at the sample mean makes intercept and slope estimates uncorrelated under a normal linear model. This changes coefficient interpretation, not predictions.

Regression diagnostics

Regression diagnostics check assumptions and influential observations in a fitted regression function. Residual-versus-fitted plots can reveal omitted mean structure or nonconstant variance; a quantile-quantile plot checks a specified error distribution. Influential observation

Words: 53
Regression diagnostics OurBigBook.com
An influential observation substantially changes a fitted model or its predictions when removed or perturbed. In a linear regression, influence depends jointly on residual size and regression leverage. Large predictor leverage with a small residual and a large residual at ordinary leverage can have different consequences. Regression diagnostics examine this sensitivity; influence is not synonymous with an invalid observation.

Regression outlier

Words: 56
Regression diagnostics OurBigBook.com
A regression outlier has a response unusually far from its fitted conditional mean. A standardized regression residual adjusts for regression leverage; an externally studentized residual also estimates noise variance with the observation deleted. Selecting the largest residual among many observations changes its reference distribution. An outlier need not be an influential observation, and unusual valid data should not be discarded solely for being unusual.

Least angle regression

Words: 263 Articles: 4
Linear regression OurBigBook.com
A piecewise linear regression-path algorithm which starts at zero, activates a predictor with maximal absolute residual score, and moves in a direction that decreases all active absolute scores equally until another predictor ties them. With G=XTX/n and active correlation signs sA​, the coefficient direction is GAA−1​sA​. Ordinary LAR does not drop a predictor when its coefficient crosses zero; sign compatibility of LAR and Lasso characterizes when its path also obeys the Lasso KKT conditions.

Equiangular direction in least angle regression (u=XAs​w,w=αGA−1​1,α=(1TGA−1​1)−1/2)

Words: 53

With signed active covariate columns XAs​=(sj​xj​)j∈A​ and their positive-definite Gram matrix GA​=(XAs​)TXAs​, the displayed direction has unit norm and (XAs​)Tu=α1. Thus it makes equal angles with unit-length signed active covariates, and movement along it reduces their signed residual correlations at the common rate α. The regression coefficient direction is dA​=diag(sA​)w, with inactive components zero.

Sign compatibility of LAR and Lasso

Words: 52
Least angle regression OurBigBook.com
If every nonzero active coefficient has the sign of its residual score along the least angle regression path, the LAR active correlation invariant and inactive bounds give the Karush-Kuhn-Tucker conditions for the Lasso. A unique Lasso solution then equals the LAR path. Zero coefficients use a subgradient interval; literal sign equality with sign(0)=0 is not appropriate for a newly entering coefficient at a positive knot.

LAR active correlation invariant (XAT​(Y−Xβ​(λ))/n=λsA​)

Words: 42
Least angle regression OurBigBook.com
Each least angle regression segment starts with all active scores at magnitude t. Moving with direction dA​=GAA−1​sA​ changes the score vector to tsA​−(t−λ)GAA​dA​=λsA​. The first-hit rule bounds every inactive score in magnitude by λ. This is the key Lasso optimality link.

Entry knot in least angle regression (λhit)

Words: 47
Least angle regression OurBigBook.com
On a segment starting at residual-score level t, an inactive score evolves as cj​−haj​ while the active level is t−h. Solving cj​−haj​=±(t−h) gives distances (t−cj​)/(1−aj​) and (t+cj​)/(1+aj​). The smallest positive admissible distance determines the next entry; reaching level zero ends the algorithm.

Student t test for a simple-regression intercept (T=(α−α0​)/(s1/n+xˉ2/Sxx​​))

The residual variance estimate s2=SSE/(n−2) is independent of the coefficient estimators and (n−2)s2/σ2 has a chi-squared distribution with n−2 degrees of freedom. Independent normal and inverse-gamma regression priors

Words: 48
Polynomial regression OurBigBook.com
With independent prior distributions β∼N(μ,Σ) and s∼IG(a,b) in a normal distribution regression model, the conditional posterior distribution of β has covariance matrix V=(XTX/s+Σ−1)−1 and mean V(XTy/s+Σ−1μ). The conditional posterior distribution of s is inverse-gamma distribution IG(a+n/2,b+∥y−Xβ∥2/2). The shape contains no half-dimension contribution from β, since its prior distribution is independent of s. These conditionals give a two-block Gibbs sampler.

Coefficient of determination (R2)

Words: 275 Articles: 1
Linear regression OurBigBook.com
In an ordinary least squares regression with an intercept and a nonconstant response, R2=1−RSS/∑i​(Yi​−Y)2. It measures the fitted proportion of sample variation relative to an intercept-only baseline. It increases when regressors are added, even if those regressors are unhelpful for prediction. A high value does not establish the assumptions of a normal linear model or rule out a systematic regression residual pattern.
For the five-point example in linear regression, the total centered sum of squares is 10.8 and the residual sum of squares is 0.8, so R2=25/27≈0.926. This summarizes the fitted reduction in squared error. It does not record the order or shape of the regression residuals, which are worth examining in a residual-versus-fitted plot.
Here is a fully constructed illustration. Use x=(−2,−1,0,1,2) and the same fitted line 3+x for both datasets. Add respectively the residual vectors
rcurve​=0.2(2,−1,−2,−1,2),ralt​=0.257​​(1,−2,0,2,−1).
(5)
Both sum to zero, both have zero inner product with x, and both have squared norm 0.56. Their ordinary least squares fit is therefore exactly the same line, with the same R2=10/(10+0.56)=125/132≈0.947. But the first residual is 0.2(x2−2), a systematic quadratic shape, while the second changes direction across the predictor values. These examples show directly which information the scalar R2 discards; neither constructed pattern proves a random-error model.

Adjusted coefficient of determination (R2)

Words: 67
Coefficient of determination OurBigBook.com
In a normal linear model with an intercept, n observations and p independent mean coefficients, adjusted R2 is
R2=1−SST/(n−1)RSS/(n−p)​.
(6)
It compares the residual variance estimate with the unmodelled sample variance, adjusting the raw coefficient of determination for fitted dimension. It can be negative and is not guaranteed to increase when another regressor is added. In penalized regression a related convention uses the fit's effective degrees of freedom instead of p; the precise smoothing-software variance convention should be specified.

Residual-versus-fitted plot

Words: 172 Articles: 1
Linear regression OurBigBook.com
A residual-versus-fitted plot displays residuals vertically against predicted responses horizontally. Curvature suggests an incorrect conditional-mean model; a fan-shaped spread suggests heteroscedasticity, while isolated large residuals may indicate unusual observations. It does not alone measure leverage or influence: regression leverage records unusual predictor combinations, and Cook's distance combines leverage and residual size.
The figure in coefficient of determination shows why the plot is useful: subtracting the fitted linear trend makes a small curved component easy to see. A visible pattern supplies a reason to investigate the model, while the absence of a pattern in a small sample does not establish that all assumptions hold. Other views address other questions: regression residuals against observation order can reveal temporal dependence, and a normal probability plot examines distributional shape. NIST's statistical handbook develops this use of graphical residual analysis for model validation. [ref]

Multivariate analysis of variance

Words: 216 Articles: 2
Analysis of variance OurBigBook.com
Multivariate analysis of variance compares mean vectors of several groups while accounting for correlations between their response variables. In the one-way normal model, independent observations Yri​∼Np​(μr​,Σ) share a positive-definite covariance matrix. Put E=∑r,i​(Yri​−Yˉr​)(Yri​−Yˉr​)T and H=∑r​nr​(Yˉr​−Yˉ)(Yˉr​−Yˉ)T. Under equal means, these matrices have independent Wishart distributions with degrees of freedom N−g and g−1, respectively. This follows by separating the Gaussian data into orthogonal within-group and between-group projections. The Wilks lambda statistic tests equality of the means using both matrices, rather than independently testing each coordinate.

Within-group and between-group scatter decomposition (T=W+B)

Words: 59
Multivariate analysis of variance OurBigBook.com
For groups with sizes nr​, means xˉr​, and overall mean xˉ, define W=∑r,i​(xri​−xˉr​)(xri​−xˉr​)T and B=∑r​nr​(xˉr​−xˉ)(xˉr​−xˉ)T. Expanding xri​−xˉ=(xri​−xˉr​)+(xˉr​−xˉ) gives total scatter T=W+B because each within-group residual sum vanishes. Both matrices are positive semidefinite, and rankB≤min(p,g−1). They separate within-group noise from between-group mean separation.

Wilks lambda statistic (Λ=∣E∣/∣E+H∣)

Words: 71
Multivariate analysis of variance OurBigBook.com
For nonsingular within-group scatter E and between-group scatter H, the statistic is ∏j​(1+θj​)−1, where θj​ solve the generalized eigenvalue problem Hv=θEv. It is small when the group means differ strongly relative to within-group variation. Maximizing a common-covariance normal likelihood under equal and unrestricted means gives the likelihood ratio ΛN/2. Its determinant factors cancel under any nonsingular change of response coordinates. For one response it is equivalent to the analysis of variance F-test.

Two-way analysis of variance (Yij​=α+ri​+gj​+εij​)

Words: 310 Articles: 3
Analysis of variance OurBigBook.com
An additive two-factor Gaussian model separates row and column effects. With one observation in each of rc cells, constraints fixing one row and column effect leave r+c−1 mean parameters and (r−1)(c−1) residual degrees of freedom. Balanced least squares gives fitted values equal to row mean plus column mean minus grand mean. With no cell replication, unrestricted interactions are confounded with residual error; the additive interpretation requires an assumption that those interactions are absent or negligible.

Missing cells can destroy factor orthogonality in two-way ANOVA (CT(I−P1​)J=0)

Words: 63
Two-way analysis of variance OurBigBook.com
In a fully balanced crossed design, centered indicator columns for the two factors are orthogonal because each pair of levels occurs equally often. Missing cells can change the level-pair counts so that the displayed cross-product becomes nonzero. Then sequential sums of squares depend on term order, although the final additive fit is unchanged. Testing either factor conditional on the other requires its extra sum of squares from the appropriate reduced model.

Identifiability of an incomplete two-factor additive design (rankX=r+c−k)

Words: 94
Two-way analysis of variance OurBigBook.com
For observed cells with means ai​+bj​, form a bipartite graph joining row i to column j when that cell is observed. If all levels occur, the design rank is r+c−k, where k is the number of connected components. Indeed a coefficient perturbation in the kernel satisfies ai​=−bj​ on every observed edge, and hence has exactly one freely chosen constant on each component. A connected design has rank r+c−1 and is identifiable after a single reference constraint. Missing cells destroy the balanced-design orthogonality of row and column effects, so sequential sums of squares can depend on order.

Saturation of an unreplicated two-factor regression (rank(X)=number of observed cells)

Words: 77
Two-way analysis of variance OurBigBook.com
A two-way analysis of variance with the full interaction and one observation in each observed factor cell has one independently varying fitted cell mean per observation. A cell-indicator design matrix has full row matrix rank, even when some combinations are missing, so the model is a saturated statistical model. Its residual sum of squares is zero and its residual degrees of freedom are zero. Missing combinations make some coefficients nonidentifiable; they do not restore a pure-error estimate. Standard errors and an ordinary residual-based F-test for interactions therefore cannot be recovered merely by fitting this larger model.

Sequential sum of squares (SSj​=RSSj−1​−RSSj​)

Words: 87
Analysis of variance OurBigBook.com
For an ordered sequence of nested linear regression models M0​⊂M1​⊂⋯, the sequential sum of squares for the jth added term is RSS(Mj−1​)−RSS(Mj​). These are also called type-I sums of squares. They depend on term order when the design is not orthogonal. Dividing by the number of newly added coefficients and the full model's residual mean square gives the sequential F-test. A covariate added first is tested before adjustment for later terms, whereas a treatment added after the covariate is tested with that covariate already included.

The within-block ANOVA stratum contains response vectors whose coordinates sum to zero separately in every block. For b blocks of size k its dimension is b(k−1). A shared additive block effect cancels in every vector in this subspace.

Balanced factorial orthogonality

Words: 57

In a complete two-factor design with equal cell replication, centred factor indicator columns for different factors have zero cross-products. Consequently the two main effects in an additive normal linear model are orthogonal, and their extra sums of squares do not depend on the order in which these main effects are entered. An omitted interaction term can still require a separate adequacy check.

Simple linear regression

Words: 187 Articles: 1
Linear regression OurBigBook.com
Simple linear regression uses one predictor and an intercept, Yi​=α+βxi​+εi​, with E(εi​∣xi​)=0. The regression coefficients describe a straight conditional-mean line: α is its value at zero and β its rate of change per unit of the predictor. When zero is outside the observed range, predictor centering can make the intercept refer to a more representative predictor value.
For fixed predictors and uncorrelated mean-zero errors of common variance σ2, with Sxx​>0,
Var(β​)=Sxx​σ2​,Var(α)=σ2(n1​+Sxx​xˉ2​).
(7)
A larger predictor spread improves slope precision under this model. In the worked example Sxx​=10 and n=5; the estimated error variance is s2=RSS/(n−2)=0.8/3. A normal linear model gives the slope pivot (β​−β)/(s/Sxx​​) a Student t-distribution with n−2 degrees of freedom. The distinction between a fitted line and a reliable uncertainty statement is precisely the role of the error assumptions.

Centred simple linear regression (α=Y,β​=Sxy​/Sxx​)

Words: 60
Simple linear regression OurBigBook.com
In simple linear regression written as Yi​=α+β(xi​−xˉ)+εi​, the constant and centred predictor columns are orthogonal. Differentiating the sum of squared residuals gives the displayed ordinary least squares estimators, provided Sxx​=∑i​(xi​−xˉ)2>0. For independent mean-zero errors of common variance σ2, both estimators are unbiased, their variances are σ2/n and σ2/Sxx​, and their covariance is zero. The uncentred intercept is α−β​xˉ.

Residual sum of squares (RSS)

Words: 191
Linear regression OurBigBook.com
The residual sum of squares measures how far the observed responses lie from their fitted values. If ri​=Yi​−Yi​ is the ith regression residual, then
RSS=∑i=1n​(Yi​−Yi​)2=∥Y−Y∥22​.
(8)
It is the squared Euclidean norm of the regression residual vector. Squaring prevents positive and negative deviations from cancelling and gives large deviations more weight. In the constructed five-point example in linear regression, the regression residuals are (1/5,1/5,−4/5,1/5,1/5), so the residual sum of squares is 4/5.
Ordinary least squares minimizes the residual sum of squares over the chosen linear regression family; ridge regression minimizes the residual sum of squares plus a penalty. Consequently the ridge regression fit can have a larger residual sum of squares than the ordinary least squares fit on exactly the same design matrix. For the one-predictor construction in ridge regression, the residual sum of squares rises from 4/5 to 33/10 when the penalty changes from 0 to 10; that increase is the cost of shrinking the fitted slope.
Dividing the residual sum of squares by n gives the training error for squared-error loss, not an estimate based on fresh responses. Its units are squared response units, and it adds across observations, so raw totals need not be comparable across different response scales or sample sizes. Comparing fits to the same responses is useful, but choosing a ridge regression penalty by minimum training residual sum of squares favors the unpenalized fit. Assess prediction error using a validation set or cross-validation instead.[ref]

Ridge regression (RSS+λ∥β∥22​)

Words: 1k Articles: 9
Linear regression OurBigBook.com
Ridge regression fits a linear regression model while penalizing the size of its slope regression coefficients. For a fixed n×p design matrix X containing the slope predictors, observed responses Y, and an unpenalized regression intercept α, the objective is
(αλ​,β​λ​)=α∈R,β∈Rpargmin​{∥Y−α1−Xβ∥22​+λ∥β∥22​},λ≥0.
(9)
Here 1 is the all-ones vector, and ∥β∥22​=∑j​βj2​ is the squared Euclidean norm of the slopes alone. The first term is the residual sum of squares; the second implements regularization. No random-error assumption is needed to define this fit. The convention is a summed squared-error loss: writing the objective as n−1RSS+λavg​∥β∥22​ gives the same fit when λ=nλavg​.[ref]
For an exact example, reuse the constructed pairs (xi​,Yi​)=(0,1),(1,2),(2,2),(3,4),(4,5) from linear regression. Their sample means are xˉ=2 and Yˉ=14/5, and the centered cross-products are ∑i​(xi​−2)2=10 and ∑i​(xi​−2)(Yi​−14/5)=10. Thus
β​λ​=10+λ10​,αλ​=514​−2β​λ​,Yi​=514​+β​λ​(xi​−2).
(10)
The ordinary least squares line has slope 1 and residual sum of squares 4/5. At λ=10, ridge regression has slope 1/2, regression intercept 9/5, and residual sum of squares 33/10; at λ=30 these become 1/4, 23/10, and 257/40. Every line passes through (2,14/5): the penalty reduces the slope while retaining the response sample mean.
The computational benefit is visible after predictor centering. Write Xc​ for the design matrix with each column's sample mean subtracted, and Yc​=Y−Yˉ1. The unpenalized intercept in ridge regression gives αλ​=Yˉ−xˉTβ​λ​, and the closed-form ridge regression estimator solves
(XcT​Xc​+λIp​)β​λ​=XcT​Yc​.
(11)
For any nonzero vector v and λ>0, vT(XcT​Xc​+λIp​)v=∥Xc​v∥22​+λ∥v∥22​>0. This positive-definite matrix is therefore invertible even when Xc​ lacks full column rank, including repeated columns or p≥n. The unique penalized slopes resolve the ambiguity of rank-deficient ordinary least squares; they do not supply evidence about slope directions absent from the data. This makes ridge regression useful for multicollinearity.[ref]
Predictor centering and scaling serve different purposes. Centering handles the free regression intercept. Scaling determines what counts as a large slope: replacing xj​ by cxj​ changes its coefficient to βj​/c for the same predictions, changing its contribution to an unweighted penalty. A common convention forms standard scores zij​=(xij​−xˉj​)/sj​ using each nonconstant predictor's sample standard deviation sj​, then penalizes slopes θj​ in those coordinates. Since θj​=sj​βj​, this is the weighted penalty λ∑j​sj2​βj2​ in the original units. Constant columns have sj​=0 and must be removed before this scaling. Scaling is a modelling choice; equal penalties in original units and in standardized units define different fits.[ref]
The singular value decomposition explains the shrinkage. If Xc​=UDVT is the compact decomposition using the r positive singular values dj​, with columns uj​ of U, then
Yλ​=Yˉ1+∑j=1r​dj2​+λdj2​​uj​ujT​Yc​.
(12)
The principal-component shrinkage by ridge regression factors are between 0 and 1: directions with small singular values are damped most. As λ→∞, the fitted values approach Yˉ1; the vanishing-penalty ridge limit recovers the ordinary least squares fitted vector as λ↓0, even if the slope estimates at zero penalty are nonunique. The covariance and bias of a ridge regression estimator describes the resulting bias-variance tradeoff. A penalty can reduce variance of an estimator while introducing bias of an estimator; the unbounded directional risk of fixed ridge shrinkage rules out a uniform improvement for a fixed positive penalty over all possible signals.[ref]
For a second exact construction, choose a centered orthonormal set of three vectors,
u1​=21​(1,−1,1,−1)T,u2​=21​(1,1,−1,−1)T,u3​=21​(1,−1,−1,1)T,
(13)
and set X=(3u1​,u2​) and Y=21+3u1​+u2​+21​u3​. The squared singular values are 9 and 1. At λ=1, the fitted coordinates along u1​,u2​ are (27/10,1/2), retaining 9/10 and 1/2 of their ordinary least squares values (3,1). At λ=9 they become (3/2,1/10), retaining 1/2 and 1/10. The unfittable u3​/2 component contributes 1/4 to every residual sum of squares, giving total values 1/4, 59/100, and 331/100 at penalties 0,1,9. These are constructed fits, not measured prediction results.
The penalty λ is a hyperparameter, so fitting slopes at a fixed value and selecting that value are separate tasks. Use a validation set or K-fold cross-validation to compare candidate penalties by held-out squared-error loss. In each split, compute predictor sample means and scales from that split's training data, apply the same transformation to its validation set, and refit the slopes; using held-out information in preprocessing introduces data leakage. Minimum training residual sum of squares cannot select useful shrinkage: ordinary least squares already attains the smallest possible value.[ref] After model selection, refit on all training data with the selected penalty. A separate test set or nested cross-validation assesses the entire selection procedure; a low selected validation score alone is no performance guarantee.[ref]

Fitted values (Y)

Words: 331 Articles: 2
Linear regression OurBigBook.com
Fitted values are the predictions at the predictor values used to fit a regression model. In linear regression with an explicit regression intercept,
Y=α1+Xβ​,r=Y−Y.
(15)
Here X is the slope design matrix, and r is the regression residual vector. The fitted values estimate the regression function at the observed predictors; they need not equal the observed responses, which may also contain random errors. Prediction at a new predictor vector x∗​ uses α+x∗T​β​ with the same centering and scaling used in fitting. A prediction interval addresses uncertainty in a new response rather than just displaying this point prediction.
For the five constructed observations in linear regression, ordinary least squares gives fitted values (4/5,9/5,14/5,19/5,24/5). The ridge regression fit at λ=10 instead gives (9/5,23/10,14/5,33/10,19/5). Both fitted vectors have sample mean 14/5 because the regression intercept is unpenalized; their regression residuals differ and their residual sums of squares are 4/5 and 33/10, respectively.
For ordinary least squares, the fitted values are the orthogonal projection of Y onto the column space of the complete design matrix, including its regression intercept column. They remain unique even in rank-deficient ordinary least squares, although the regression coefficients can be nonunique. The regression residuals are then orthogonal to every column.[ref] With centered slopes, ridge regression instead satisfies XcT​r=λβ​λ​, so the regression residuals need not be orthogonal to the slope columns. Their sum is still zero because the regression intercept remains free.
For fixed predictors and scales and a fixed positive penalty, ridge regression gives a linear smoother,
Yλ​=Hλ​Y,Hλ​=n11T​+Xc​(XcT​Xc​+λIp​)−1XcT​.
(16)
For λ>0 with at least one nonzero slope direction, this matrix is not an orthogonal projection matrix: applying it twice shrinks that direction again. Its effective degrees of freedom are 1+∑j=1r​dj2​/(dj2​+λ), where dj​ are the positive singular values of Xc​; the extra 1 counts the free regression intercept. If the penalty is selected using Y, the resulting fitting rule need not be a linear smoother, since its chosen Hλ​ also depends on Y.
