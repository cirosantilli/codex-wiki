Adjacent nodes of a wavefunction are separated by roughly a/n, so a locally sinusoidal wavefunction has typical wavevector k∼πn/a. The kinetic-energy estimate from wavefunction nodes is therefore
⟨K⟩∼2ma2ℏ2π2n2​(n≫1).​
(3)
Equivalently, ⟨K⟩=ℏ2∫∣ψ′∣2dx/(2m∫∣ψ∣2dx). The robust scaling is ℏ2n2/(ma2); the precise coefficient depends on how the extent is defined and how the oscillations vary near classical turning points. For few nodes, use an order-one offset to n: the nodeless ground state still has confinement energy bound from position support of order ℏ2/(ma2), rather than zero.

Centre the infinite square well at zero. Its ground state wavefunction is ψ(x)=2/L​cos(πx/L) inside the well and zero outside. The momentum representation in quantum mechanics gives the wavevector amplitude
ϕ(k)=2π​1​∫ψ(x)e−ikxdx=2πL​π2−(kL)2cos(kL/2)​.
(4)
Consequently the unnormalised wavevector distribution of an infinite-well ground state is
p(k)∝[π2−(kL)2]2cos2(kL/2)​.​
(5)
The apparent singularities at k=±π/L are removable. The normalized probability density is 4πL times the boxed expression and has limit L/(4π) there. Translating the well changes only the Fourier transform's phase. The wavevector is continuously distributed; a box energy eigenstate is not a free momentum eigenstate.

For identical particles, the particle exchange operator exchanges both spatial and spin labels. The symmetrization postulate requires a symmetric full state for bosons and an antisymmetric full state for fermions. A product Ψ=ϕχ must therefore have spatial and spin exchange eigenvalues whose product is +1 or −1, respectively; a general state may be a sum of such products.
For orthonormal spatial orbitals u,v, the combinations ϕ±​=[u(1)v(2)±v(1)u(2)]/2​ have opposite exchange parities. Two spin-one-half fermions combine ϕ+​ with the antisymmetric spin-one-half singlet state (∣↑↓⟩−∣↓↑⟩)/2​, or ϕ−​ with one of the three symmetric spin-one-half triplet states. Two particles in the same spatial orbital must be a spin singlet. This is the Pauli exclusion principle applied to complete spin orbitals, not a ban on sharing a spatial orbital with opposite spins.
A Slater determinant enforces fermionic antisymmetry for many occupied spin orbitals. Bosonic symmetrization instead uses a permanent; repeated orbital occupations are allowed. These exchange requirements apply even when particles do not interact.

A single-mode coherent state is an annihilation operator eigenstate:
∣α⟩=e−∣α∣2/2∑n=0∞​n!​αn​∣n⟩,a∣α⟩=α∣α⟩.
(6)
Its photon statistics are Poisson distribution: ⟨n⟩=Varn=∣α∣2. For optical quadratures X=(a+a†)/2, P=(a−a†)/(2i), one has [X,P]=i/2 and VarX=VarP=1/4. Its field mean oscillates classically while retaining vacuum fluctuations.[ref][ref]
A classical mixture of coherent states has a nonnegative Glauber-Sudarshan representation. A nonclassical state of light cannot be represented that way. Examples are a squeezed vacuum state, with quadrature variances e−2r/4 and e2r/4, and a nonvacuum number state, with zero photon number variance. Noise below the vacuum level in one quadrature, sub-Poisson number noise, or photon antibunching can witness nonclassicality. For ∣n⟩, g(2)(0)=1−1/n<1; coherent light has g(2)(0)=1. Thermal light has excess noise but is a classical mixture: noncoherent does not by itself mean nonclassical.

The Stark effect changes atomic energy levels in a static electric field. For E=Ez^, the electric-dipole interaction is H′=−dz​E=eEz for an electron, with e>0. A nondegenerate state of definite parity has ⟨z⟩=0, so its leading quadratic Stark effect is
ΔEn(2)​=e2E2∑r=n​En​−Er​∣⟨r∣z∣n⟩∣2​.
(7)
The ground state shift is negative, conventionally −αE2/2 with positive electric polarizability.[ref]
Degenerate opposite-parity states require degenerate perturbation theory. In ideal Coulomb hydrogen, the 2s,2p0​ subspace has an off-diagonal matrix element of magnitude 3eaB​E, where aB​ is the Bohr radius. Its mixed states shift by
ΔE=±3eaB​E,ΔE2p±1​​=0 to first order.​
(8)
This Linear Stark effect in the first excited hydrogen eigenspace assumes fine-structure and Lamb splittings are negligible compared with the mixing. In an isolated parity eigenstate with such degeneracies resolved, sufficiently weak fields restore quadratic behaviour. Strong fields require further mixing and can ionize the atom.[ref]

Define ℏω0​=E2​−E1​ and use radiation energy density u(ω) per unit angular frequency, excluding the zero-point contribution. The Angular-frequency Einstein coefficients give rates per atom: B12​u(ω0​) for absorption, B21​u(ω0​) for stimulated emission, and A21​ for spontaneous emission. Stimulated emission adds a photon to the stimulating mode; spontaneous emission persists with no incident photons.
In thermal equilibrium the Boltzmann distribution gives N2​/N1​=e−ℏω0​/(kB​T). Balancing up and down rates yields
N1​B12​u=N2​(A21​+B21​u),u=B12​eℏω0​/(kB​T)−B21​A21​​.
(15)
Matching the Planck law for every temperature fixes
B12​=B21​,B21​A21​​=π2c3ℏω03​​.​
(16)
No degeneracy factor is needed for these nondegenerate levels.[ref]
For plates of large area A and spacing d, the surviving low-frequency transverse electromagnetic mode has one polarization, E∥z^, and in-plane wavevector. Periodic in-plane counting gives
dN=(2π)2A​2πkdk,Ad1​dωdN​=2πc2dω​.

Multiplying this single-polarization two-dimensional photon density of states by ℏω and the Bose-Einstein distribution produces the thermal radiation between parallel conducting plates:
uplates​(ω)=2πc2dℏω2​eℏω/(kB​T)−11​.​
(18)
The same rate balance therefore gives
(BA​)plates​=2πc2dℏω02​​,(A/B)free​(A/B)plates​​=2ω0​dπc​≫1.​
(19)
These B coefficients refer to the radiation polarization in their respective environments: isotropic free-space B must not automatically be equated to the coefficient for a specified z-polarized field.
For hydrogen, take the quantization axis along z, neglect spin-orbit coupling, and assume perfect plates, a wide in-plane region and an unmodified atomic wavefunction: aB​≪d≪c/ω0​, with negligible surface-induced mixing or shifts. The electric-dipole selection rules for hydrogen require Δl=±1; the allowed z field has Δmℓ​=0. Hence the polarization-selective spontaneous emission between conducting plates gives
mℓ​=0:Aplates​∼Afree​ω0​dc​,τplates​∼τfree​cω0​d​.​

The thin-cavity enhancement of a normal electric dipole applies: the 2p0​→1s dipole points along z and couples to the enhanced mode density. For mℓ​=±1, the transition dipoles lie in the x–y plane and couple to none of these low-frequency modes: the electric-dipole decay rate is zero in the ideal model, so this channel's lifetime is infinite. Real finite, imperfect plates and additional decay or mixing channels limit the lifetime. The order-one orientation factor for the allowed transition does not change the requested lifetime scaling.
