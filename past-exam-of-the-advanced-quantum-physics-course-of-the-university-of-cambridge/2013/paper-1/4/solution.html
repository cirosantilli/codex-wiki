Define ℏω0​=E2​−E1​ and use radiation energy density u(ω) per unit angular frequency, excluding the zero-point contribution. The Angular-frequency Einstein coefficients give rates per atom: B12​u(ω0​) for absorption, B21​u(ω0​) for stimulated emission, and A21​ for spontaneous emission. Stimulated emission adds a photon to the stimulating mode; spontaneous emission persists with no incident photons.
In thermal equilibrium the Boltzmann distribution gives N2​/N1​=e−ℏω0​/(kB​T). Balancing up and down rates yields
N1​B12​u=N2​(A21​+B21​u),u=B12​eℏω0​/(kB​T)−B21​A21​​.
(15)
Matching the Planck law for every temperature fixes
B12​=B21​,B21​A21​​=π2c3ℏω03​​.​
(16)

These B coefficients refer to the radiation polarization in their respective environments: isotropic free-space B must not automatically be equated to the coefficient for a specified z-polarized field.
For hydrogen, take the quantization axis along z, neglect spin-orbit coupling, and assume perfect plates, a wide in-plane region and an unmodified atomic wavefunction: aB​≪d≪c/ω0​, with negligible surface-induced mixing or shifts. The electric-dipole selection rules for hydrogen require Δl=±1; the allowed z field has Δmℓ​=0. Hence the polarization-selective spontaneous emission between conducting plates gives
mℓ​=0:Aplates​∼Afree​ω0​dc​,τplates​∼τfree​cω0​d​.​
(20)
The thin-cavity enhancement of a normal electric dipole applies: the 2p0​→1s dipole points along z and couples to the enhanced mode density. For mℓ​=±1, the transition dipoles lie in the x–y plane and couple to none of these low-frequency modes: the electric-dipole decay rate is zero in the ideal model, so this channel's lifetime is infinite. Real finite, imperfect plates and additional decay or mixing channels limit the lifetime. The order-one orientation factor for the allowed transition does not change the requested lifetime scaling.
