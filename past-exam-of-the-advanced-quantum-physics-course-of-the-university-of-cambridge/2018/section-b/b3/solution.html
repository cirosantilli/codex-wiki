Take a normalized spin one-half state in the Sz​ eigenbasis. Since S=(ℏ/2)σ, direct multiplication of the Pauli matrices gives
⟨Sx​⟩=2ℏ​(α∗β+β∗α),⟨Sy​⟩=2ℏ​(−iα∗β+iβ∗α),⟨Sz​⟩=2ℏ​(∣α∣2−∣β∣2).
(21)
Substitution of the half-angle parametrization gives
⟨S⟩=2ℏ​(sinθcosϕ,sinθsinϕ,cosθ).​
(22)
Thus θ and ϕ are precisely the polar and azimuthal angles of the mean spin. Two complex coefficients contain four real parameters; normalization removes one and an unobservable common quantum phase removes another. The resulting pure states form the Bloch sphere. One may choose α real and nonnegative away from the patch where α=0. At the poles the azimuth is redundant. The length ∣⟨S⟩∣=ℏ/2 should not be confused with ⟨S2⟩​=3​ℏ/2.
For a spatial plane wave, −iℏ∇ becomes ℏk, so the Hamiltonian operator reduces to the Weyl Hamiltonian matrix
H(k)=ℏvσ⋅k.
(23)
Define n=(sinθcosϕ,sinθsinϕ,cosθ) and χ+​=(cos(θ/2),eiϕsin(θ/2))T. The matrix
σ⋅n=(cosθeiϕsinθ​e−iϕsinθ−cosθ​)
(24)
satisfies (σ⋅n)χ+​=χ+​ by the angle-addition identities. Consequently the displayed spinor is an energy eigenstate for either k=+kn or k=−kn, with respective energies +ℏvk and −ℏvk, for k≥0. Equivalently, at each fixed nonzero wavevector the two branches are
E±​(k)=±ℏv∣k∣(v>0).​
(25)
If v<0, the two energy labels exchange; the ordered eigenvalues are ±ℏ∣v∣∣k∣. Taking v>0 sets the convention in the sketches. An orthogonal spinor χ−​=(−e−iϕsin(θ/2),cos(θ/2))T obeys (σ⋅n)χ−​=−χ−​ and supplies the other branch at +kn.
The Pauli matrix multiplication law independently gives H(k)2=(ℏv)2∣k∣2I. The dispersion relation is therefore a pair of cones touching at zero energy and momentum; a signed straight-line cut has upper and lower branches ±ℏv∣kx​∣. In the kz​=0 plane, θ=π/2, and the upper-branch mean spin points radially outwards, the lower-branch spin radially inwards. This is spin-momentum locking, not a tangential spin texture. At the touching point the two states are degenerate and the spin direction is not selected by the Hamiltonian operator.
Now let a spatially constant perturbation have matrix V=(uw∗​wz​), with u,z real. The Hermitian two-by-two matrix in the Pauli basis decomposition follows explicitly from
(uw∗​wz​)=dI+aσx​+bσy​+cσz​=(d+ca+ib​a−ibd−c​),
(26)
where d=(u+z)/2, c=(u−z)/2, a=Rew, and b=−Imw are real. Equivalently, d=trV/2 and (a,b,c)i​=tr(Vσi​)/2. The four matrices are linearly independent under the trace inner product and span the four-dimensional real vector space of Hermitian two-by-two matrices.
Spatial constancy means the perturbation does not mix different wavevectors, so each momentum sector can still be diagonalized independently. Write b=(a,b,c). The full matrix and its exact eigenvalues are
H′(k)=dI+(ℏvk+b)⋅σ,E±​(k)=d±(ℏvkx​+a)2+(ℏvky​+b)2+(ℏvkz​+c)2​.​
(27)
This constant matrix perturbation of a Weyl Hamiltonian translates the cone to
k∗​=−ℏv(a,b,c)​,E∗​=d.​
(28)
In three-dimensional momentum space the energy bands still touch; the perturbation produces no full spectral gap. The new branch spin directions are parallel and antiparallel to ℏvk+b, so their radial texture is centered on the shifted point.
A restricted cut must be distinguished from the full dispersion relation. In the fixed plane kz​=0, the energies are d±(ℏvkx​+a)2+(ℏvky​+b)2+c2​, with minimum branch separation 2∣c∣. This plane misses the crossing if c=0. Along any line through k∗​ the dispersion relation still crosses linearly. Thus a sketch of a gapped fixed-plane cut alone would not describe the gapless three-dimensional Hamiltonian operator. The cone after a general perturbation is also no longer a function of ∣k∣ about the old origin alone.
