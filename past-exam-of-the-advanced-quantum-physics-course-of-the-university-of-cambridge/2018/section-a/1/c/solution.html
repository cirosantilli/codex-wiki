Neglect hyperfine structure and use the electronic weak-field Zeeman splitting regime, where μB​B is small compared with the spin-orbit interaction splitting. The sodium levels are 3s2S1/2​, 3p2P1/2​ and 3p2P3/2​. The Landé g-factor formula gives
g(3s2S1/2​)=2,g(3p2P1/2​)=32​,g(3p2P3/2​)=34​.​
(7)
For example, inserting L=1,S=1/2,J=1/2 gives 3/2+(3/4−2)/(2⋅3/4)=2/3. Each sublevel with magnetic quantum number mJ​ shifts by gJ​mJ​μB​B. Thus the lower level has shifts ±μB​B, the upper J=1/2 level has shifts ±μB​B/3, and the upper J=3/2 level has shifts ±2μB​B and ±2μB​B/3.
The electric-dipole selection rule allows ΔJ=0,±1 except 0↔0, and mu​−ml​=0,±1. Both fine structure branches are allowed. The emitted photon energy is
Eγ​=Eu(0)​−El(0)​+μB​B(gu​mu​−2ml​).
(8)
All the allowed components of the sodium D-line Zeeman pattern are listed below; the last column is the photon shift in units of μB​B:
upper term2P1/2​2P3/2​​mu​−1/2−1/2+1/2+1/2−3/2−1/2−1/2+1/2+1/2+3/2​ml​−1/2+1/2−1/2+1/2−1/2−1/2+1/2−1/2+1/2+1/2​δEγ​/(μB​B)+2/3−4/3+4/3−2/3−1+1/3−5/3+5/3−1/3+1​​
(9)
These are four components of D1 and six of D2. Their strengths need not be equal; the table and arrows specify allowed transitions, not intensities.
The 3p2P3/2​ level lies above 3p2P1/2​, so D2 has the larger zero-field photon energy. [ref] In the stated weak-field regime its most positive shift is 5μB​B/3. Since λ=hc/Eγ​, the shortest wavelength is therefore produced by
3p2P3/2​, mJ​=+21​ ⟶ 3s2S1/2​, mJ​=−21​.​
(10)
The tempting mu​=3/2→ml​=−1/2 pair has a larger difference of level shifts but is forbidden because ∣mu​−ml​∣=2.
