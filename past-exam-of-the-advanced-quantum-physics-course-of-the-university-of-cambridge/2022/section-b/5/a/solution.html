The unperturbed infinite square well is invariant under the parity operator. Its one-dimensional energy eigenvalues are nondegenerate, so each energy eigenstate has definite parity. Its position expectation value therefore vanishes: ∣ϕn​(x)∣2 is even, while x is odd. The first-order nondegenerate perturbation theory correction is consequently
δϵn(1)​=eE⟨ϕn​∣x∣ϕn​⟩=0.
(23)
The electric field does mix opposite-parity quantum states, and second-order nondegenerate perturbation theory contains two factors of eEx. Its leading contribution is thus proportional to E2: this is a quadratic Stark effect. More generally, applying the parity operator changes H(E) into H(−E), so their corresponding energy eigenvalues agree. An analytic expansion about zero electric field contains only even powers of E. We assume e∣E∣a is small compared with the unperturbed excitation scale.
