In the normalized plane wave basis, the only nonzero potential matrix elements are
Vmn​=−2λ​(δm,n+1​+δm,n−1​).
(16)
Thus a useful small parameter is ∣λ∣/EL​≪1, which suppresses admixture of well-separated energy levels. First-order nondegenerate perturbation theory further requires ∣λ∣/2≪∣En​−En±1​∣ for the coupled states. This latter condition fails near a half-integer α even for very small λ; there one must use degenerate perturbation theory in the nearly degenerate pair. Smallness is measured against the gaps to the other states, not the vanishing gap inside this pair.

For positive λ, the lower quantum state at the crossing is the symmetric combination of n=0,1, and the upper state is the antisymmetric combination. A negative λ exchanges these combinations but leaves the ordered energy eigenvalues unchanged.
This cosine-potential gap in a quantum ring appears in the middle panel above: each lowest-branch crossing opens into an avoided crossing of gap ∣λ∣ to first order. Away from these crossings, the diagonal matrix elements vanish, so the leading shifts are second order. At integer α, the two first-excited plane waves differ by two units of n and therefore have no first-order coupling; their smaller splitting begins at second order. The numerical plot includes that splitting.
