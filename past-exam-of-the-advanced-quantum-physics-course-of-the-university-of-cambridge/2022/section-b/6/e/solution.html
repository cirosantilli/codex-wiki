The thermal oscillator occupation is the Bose-Einstein distribution. With the rate notation Γησ​ for a transition from σ to η, the master equation for the up population is
dtdp↑​​=Γ↑↓​p↓​−Γ↓↑​p↑​=Γ0​[nΩ​p↓​−(nΩ​+1)p↑​].
(46)
At thermal equilibrium, assuming Γ0​>0, vanishing time derivative is detailed balance between the two spin states. Hence
p↓​p↑​​=nΩ​+1nΩ​​=eβℏΩ/(eβℏΩ−1)1/(eβℏΩ−1)​=e−βℏΩ,
(47)
or
p↑​=e−βℏΩp↓​.​
(48)
Together with normalization, this gives p↑​=1/(1+eβℏΩ) and p↓​=eβℏΩ/(1+eβℏΩ), the Boltzmann distribution for the energy eigenvalues ±ℏΩ/2. At zero temperature, absorption vanishes and emission relaxes the spin into its down ground state.
The nonzero-rate condition is necessary: if λ=0 or g(Ω)=0, both rates vanish, so every initial pair of populations is stationary within this approximation. In that case stationarity alone cannot imply a thermal distribution. The equilibrium conclusion therefore presumes coupling to resonant oscillator modes.
