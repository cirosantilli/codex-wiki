The Pauli exclusion principle allows each one-particle magnetic substate only once, since the fermions share the other quantum numbers. To maximize the total angular momentum projection, occupy mj​=5/2,3/2,1/2,−1/2, giving
Mmax​=25​+23​+21​−21​=4.
(1)
The corresponding Slater determinant is a highest-weight vector: every attempted raising operation either tries to exceed j or duplicates an occupied substate and vanishes. Therefore it belongs to a multiplet with Jmax​=4​, not the distinguishable-particle value 4j=10. Equivalently, four particles in a six-state shell have two holes whose maximum angular momentum projection is 5/2+3/2=4. The general maximum total angular momentum of equivalent fermions is Jmax​=Nj−N(N−1)/2 for N≤2j+1.

At incident wave number k, low kinetic energy means ka≪1: the de Broglie wavelength is much larger than the scattering region. A generic finite-range scatterer can then be described to leading order without resolving its angular structure. In partial-wave scattering, this is leading S-wave scattering. The ℓ=0 spherical harmonic is constant, whereas the centrifugal term ℏ2ℓ(ℓ+1)/(2mr2) impedes penetration to the potential for ℓ>0.
For a central potential with regular threshold behaviour, the finite-range partial-wave threshold law gives δℓ​=O(k2ℓ+1). Thus
f(θ)=k1​∑ℓ=0∞​(2ℓ+1)eiδℓ​sinδℓ​Pℓ​(cosθ)≃keiδ0​sinδ0​​,
(2)
which is independent of scattering angle. For finite nonzero scattering length as​, f→−as​ and dσ/dΩ→as2​ is isotropic. The potential range a and scattering length as​ are different quantities. The claim describes the generic leading S-wave scattering limit; tuned vanishing S-wave scattering amplitude or exceptional threshold resonances require separate analysis rather than a universal angular conclusion.

Closed 1s2 and 2s2 subshells have L=S=0. In the open 2p4 subshell, three electrons can have parallel spins in mℓ​=−1,0,1, and the fourth must have the opposite spin, giving maximum S=1. Treating the shell as two p holes gives the same allowed terms as p2; at this spin, antisymmetry permits L=1, rather than L=2. One can also see ML​=1 by placing the fourth electron in mℓ​=1.
Hund's rules therefore select the 3P term. Since four electrons exceed half filling of the six-state p subshell, its lowest atomic fine-structure level has the largest J=L+S=2:
1s22s22p43P2​,S=1, L=1, J=2.​
(3)
Its zero-field magnetic degeneracy is 2J+1=5. The experimentally tabulated neutral-oxygen ground level has precisely this term and J.[ref]

The quantum variational principle turns the ground-state problem into minimization of a Rayleigh quotient:
E[ψ]=⟨ψ∣ψ⟩⟨ψ∣H∣ψ⟩​≥E0​.
(4)
The Hamiltonian must be a self-adjoint operator bounded below, and the nonzero trial state must have finite form energy. Expanding a normalized trial state in exact energy eigenstates gives E[ψ]=∑n​∣cn​∣2En​, a weighted average no smaller than E0​. Equality requires support only in the ground-state eigenspace. The spectral version also accommodates continuous spectrum; a lowest normalizable eigenstate is not required merely to bound the spectral infimum.
The variational method chooses a trial family with the correct boundary conditions, fermionic exchange symmetry and other required quantum numbers, evaluates its energy and minimizes over its parameters. It gives an upper bound on ground-state energy. Enlarging a nested trial family can only improve or preserve its optimal bound. A low energy does not by itself establish a highly accurate wavefunction, especially near degeneracy; energy errors are second order in small admixtures orthogonal to a nondegenerate exact ground state.
For example, a normalized Gaussian trial for a one-dimensional quantum harmonic oscillator is ψβ​=(β/π)1/4e−βx2/2, with β>0. Its energy is
E(β)=4mℏ2β​+4βmω2​,βopt​=mω/ℏ,Emin​=ℏω/2.​
(5)
The two terms balance kinetic localization cost against the potential cost of spreading out. This Gaussian variational energy for the harmonic oscillator happens to attain the exact answer because the family contains the exact ground state.
A trial expansion ψ=∑j​cj​χj​ in a finite, possibly nonorthogonal basis gives the generalized eigenvalue problem Hc=ESc, with Hij​=⟨χi​∣H∣χj​⟩ and Sij​=⟨χi​∣χj​⟩. Its lowest Ritz value minimizes the quotient in that subspace. For excited states, the excited-state variational constraint imposes exact orthogonality to the lower eigenstates to obtain a corresponding upper bound; the Courant–Fischer min-max principle yields upper bounds for ordered Ritz eigenvalues from a common subspace. orthogonality only to an independently approximate ground state does not automatically provide an excited-state bound: residual true-ground-state contamination can lower the trial energy below the desired excited energy. Symmetry can impose exact orthogonality when the relevant states have different symmetry sectors.

For a particle of charge q and mass m in a uniform magnetic field B=Bz^, minimal coupling replaces momentum by p−qA. Choose Landau gauge A=(0,Bx,0) and first restrict motion to the xy plane. Since py​=ℏky​ is conserved,
H=2mpx2​​+2m(ℏky​−qBx)2​=2mpx2​​+21​mωc2​(x−xc​)2,
(6)
ωc​=m∣qB∣​,xc​=qBℏky​​,En​=ℏωc​(n+21​),n=0,1,2,….​
(7)
These Landau levels quantize the classical cyclotron motion. The wavefunctions are harmonic-oscillator functions centred at xc​, multiplied by eiky​y. Changing ky​ shifts the guiding centre without changing the energy. Gauge-dependent labels such as ky​ are not themselves observables; the level spacing and degeneracy are gauge independent. The magnetic length ℓB​=ℏ/∣qB∣​ is the transverse orbital scale.
For a large rectangle of sides Lx​,Ly​, periodicity along y gives Δky​=2π/Ly​. Requiring centres inside the sample gives, for each orbital level and each specified spin state,
D=2πℓB2​Lx​Ly​​=h∣qB∣A​=h/∣q∣∣Φ∣​.​
(8)
Thus each magnetic flux quantum supplies one guiding-centre state. The continuum bulk count neglects boundary corrections; magnetic periodic conditions on a torus require integer flux count. A finite confining edge lifts the simple flat guiding-centre spectrum near that edge.
spin adds a Zeeman term. For electrons with gyromagnetic factor g, its contribution is gμB​Bms​, with ms​=±1/2 and μB​=eℏ/(2me​). If g=2 and the orbital mass is me​, the branches become ℏωc​n and ℏωc​(n+1): the lowest combined level has one spin branch, while positive levels coincide in pairs. In a material, effective mass and effective g need not give that coincidence.
In three dimensions, free motion parallel to B adds ℏ2kz2​/(2m), so each orbital index labels a one-dimensional continuum rather than an isolated three-dimensional energy. Resolved magnetic quantization requires the spacing to exceed substantial thermal and disorder broadening. The changing occupation of these levels underlies magnetic quantum oscillations; in two dimensions their degeneracy and filling are central to the integer quantum Hall effect. These statements assume nonrelativistic, approximately independent particles, rather than arbitrary interacting or disordered spectra.

A helium atom has two electrons bound to nuclear charge +2e. In the nonrelativistic fixed-nucleus approximation, using atomic units,
H=−21​∇12​−21​∇22​−r1​2​−r2​2​+r12​1​.
(9)
The last term couples the electrons, so a product of independent hydrogenic orbitals is not an exact solution. The dominant ground configuration is 1s2. Its spatial wavefunction is symmetric, so Fermi antisymmetry requires the spin singlet
χ00​=2​∣↑↓⟩−∣↓↑⟩​,helium ground level 1s21S0​.​
(10)
A triplet cannot put both electrons in the same 1s spatial orbital. Correlation changes the exact spatial function, including its dependence on r12​, while preserving this ground-state symmetry.
A useful helium variational effective charge takes normalized orbitals ϕζ​(r)=(ζ3/π)1/2e−ζr for both electrons and multiplies their product by χ00​. The energy in Hartree units is
E(ζ)=ζ2−4ζ+85​ζ,ζopt​=1627​,Evar​=−256729​Eh​≃−77.5eV.​
(11)
The reduced effective charge expresses screening. Neglecting repulsion gives −4Eh​, while its expectation in the unscreened ζ=2 product gives −2.75Eh​; optimizing the orbital improves that trial estimate. The exact ground energy is lower than the optimized product energy, since the product omits explicit electron correlation. This calculation keeps nuclear charge two in H; only the trial orbital parameter is varied.
For a singly excited helium state with distinct orthonormal orbitals a=1s and b=nℓ, the spatial combinations are
Φ±​=2​a(1)b(2)±b(1)a(2)​.
(12)
The symmetric Φ+​ pairs with the antisymmetric spin singlet, while antisymmetric Φ−​ pairs with the symmetric spin triplet. With the same orbitals, their leading energies are
E±​=ϵa​+ϵb​+Jab​±Kab​,Esinglet​−Etriplet​=2Kab​.​
(13)
Here Jab​ is the direct Coulomb integral and Kab​ is the exchange integral. The latter is positive for the Coulomb interaction, so the triplet is lower for this fixed-orbital comparison. singlet-triplet exchange splitting in helium arises from antisymmetry and spatial correlation in the Coulomb interaction, not from inserting a separate classical spin-spin force. Spin-singlet helium states are parahelium; spin-triplet helium states are orthohelium. The two spectra approximately form separate families because the leading electric dipole operator does not change total spin.[ref]
The lowest 1s2s terms are 3S1​ and 1S0​, with the triplet lower. The 1s2p configuration gives 3P0,1,2o​ and 1P1o​, where the odd parity allows singlet P to connect to the even-parity ground singlet by an electric dipole transition. Both 1s2s levels lack an allowed direct electric dipole decay to 1s2: the triplet is spin forbidden, and singlet S→S is forbidden by orbital angular momentum and parity. This explains helium metastability without implying an infinite lifetime once weaker radiative processes or collisions are allowed.
The observed first 1s2s excitation energies are approximately 19.82 and 20.62eV for the triplet and singlet, respectively. Higher singly excited Rydberg series approach the He II ground-state ionization threshold; doubly excited states above that threshold can autoionize and are not all stable bound excited levels.[ref]

Introduce a bookkeeping parameter λ in H=H0​+λH1​ and expand ∣ϕn​⟩=∣ψn​⟩+λ∣ηn​⟩+O(λ2) and En​=En​+λΔEn​+O(λ2). Fix phase and normalization at this order by ⟨ψn​∣ηn​⟩=0. The first-order Schrödinger equation is
(H0​−En​)∣ηn​⟩=−(H1​−ΔEn​)∣ψn​⟩.
(14)
angular momentum projection onto ⟨ψn​∣ gives ΔEn​=⟨ψn​∣H1​∣ψn​⟩. angular momentum projection onto a different eigenstate gives
⟨ψj​∣ηn​⟩=En​−Ej​⟨ψj​∣H1​∣ψn​⟩​,j=n.
(15)
Completeness therefore yields the first-order nondegenerate perturbation theory result
∣ϕn​⟩=∣ψn​⟩+j=n∑​En​−Ej​⟨ψj​∣H1​∣ψn​⟩​∣ψj​⟩+O(H12​).​
(16)
Nonzero energy denominators are essential. The displayed state has unit norm through first order; exact normalization would change terms of second order. A continuum, if present, contributes its corresponding integral as well as any discrete sum.
For the oscillator in a static electric field, electrostatic potential energy is H1​=−qϵx and ω=k/m​. parity gives ⟨0∣x∣0⟩=0, and the supplied Gaussian wavefunctions give
xzp​≡⟨1∣x∣0⟩=2α​∫x2∣ψ0​(x)∣2dx=2α​1​=2mωℏ​​.
(17)
Since E0​−E1​=−ℏω, the two-state first-order ground state is
∣ϕ0​⟩=∣0⟩+η∣1⟩+O(ϵ2),η=ℏωqϵxzp​​.
(18)
Thus ⟨x⟩=2ηxzp​+O(ϵ2), and the induced electric dipole is
d=q⟨x⟩=mω2q2ϵ​=kq2​ϵto first order.​
(19)
It is aligned with the field for either sign of q. The harmonic-oscillator electric polarizability is q2/k. The position operator connects ∣0⟩ only to ∣1⟩, so the requested two-state calculation contains the complete first-order dipole response, rather than dropping other first-order terms.
Complete the square to find the exact electric-field displacement of a quantum harmonic oscillator:
H=2mp2​+21​k(x−x0​)2−2kq2ϵ2​,x0​=kqϵ​.
(20)
The exact normalized ground wavefunction is ψ0​(x−x0​), with energy ℏω/2−q2ϵ2/(2k). Its mean displacement is x0​, so
dexact​=qx0​=kq2ϵ​.​
(21)
The first-order dipole equals the exact dipole, because the restoring force is exactly linear and translating a harmonic well does not change its width or frequency. This agreement of one expectation value does not make the truncated wavefunction exact. The Hellmann–Feynman theorem provides an independent check: d=−∂E0​/∂ϵ.
For perturbation theory to approximate the state, ∣η∣≪1, or ∣x0​∣≪2xzp​. The exact translated ground state has the original-oscillator expansion
∣0;x0​⟩=e−η2/2∑n=0∞​n!​ηn​∣n⟩.
(22)
It is a coherent state with mean occupation η2. Large fields require many oscillator levels, whereas the first-order state retains only two. The normalized two-state truncation of a displaced oscillator instead gives
∣ϕ01​⟩=1+η2​∣0⟩+η∣1⟩​,⟨x⟩01​=1+η2x0​​,
(23)
which incorrectly tends towards zero for large ∣η∣ instead of following the displaced well. In position space the truncated polynomial times Gaussian also develops a node absent from the exact ground state. Taking a linearized formula outside its small-state-correction regime is therefore invalid even though its dipole coefficient happens to be exact.

Insert the interaction-picture expansion into the Schrödinger equation. The phases multiplying the basis states already solve the unperturbed evolution, leaving
iℏc˙r​=∑s​Hrs′​cos(ωt)ei(ωr​−ωs​)tcs​.
(24)
Hermiticity requires H21′​=ℏ(ω′)∗, not ℏω′ unless the coupling is real. Since the diagonal matrix elements vanish and ω0​=ω2​−ω1​, expanding the cosine gives
ic˙2​=2(ω′)∗​c1​[ei(ω+ω0​)t+e−i(ω−ω0​)t],​
(25)
ic˙1​=2ω′​c2​[ei(ω−ω0​)t+e−i(ω+ω0​)t].​
(26)
The conjugate pair of couplings conserves ∣c1​∣2+∣c2​∣2. Near resonance, the difference frequency is slow while the sum frequency is approximately 2ω0​. Rapid terms alternate sign before an appreciable slow transfer accumulates. The rotating-wave approximation retains the slow terms when ∣ω′∣,∣ω−ω0​∣≪ω+ω0​; resonance alone is insufficient for a strong drive. Counter-rotating corrections include small shifts of the resonance and need not remain negligible at arbitrary strength or arbitrarily long times.
For the spin preparation, use H=−μ⋅B=−γs⋅B and s=ℏσ/2. Choose B>0 and γ>0 to fix helicity, with ∣↑⟩=(1,0)T, ∣↓⟩=(0,1)T and ω0​=γB. The initial ∣↑⟩ is the lower state. For negative γ, reverse the RF rotation sense; pulse areas use ∣γ∣, and signed Larmor precession determines the corresponding phase adjustment.
A circularly rotating magnetic drive uses two transverse fields in quadrature during 0<t<τ:
B(t)=(B1​cos(ω0​t+φ),−B1​sin(ω0​t+φ),B),Ω=γB1​>0.
(27)
With U0​(t)=e+iω0​tσz​/2, their interaction Hamiltonian is exactly constant,
HI​=−2ℏΩ​(cosφσx​−sinφσy​).
(28)
The minus sign in the second RF component selects co-rotation for the specified positive gyromagnetic ratio. Exponentiating this Pauli matrix gives
∣ψI​(t)⟩=cos2Ωt​∣↑⟩+ie−iφsin2Ωt​∣↓⟩.
(29)
The laboratory phase of a resonant spin pulse includes free precession. Returning to the laboratory frame and removing a common phase,
∣ψ(t)⟩∼cos2Ωt​∣↑⟩+ie−i(φ+ω0​t)sin2Ωt​∣↓⟩.​
(30)
Unlike a linearly polarized RF field, this exactly co-rotating field has no counter-rotating component. A single linearly polarized field Bx,peak​cos(ω0​t+φ) gives the same slow rotation in the weak-drive rotating-wave approximation with Ω=∣γ∣Bx,peak​/2. For the circular field above, Ω=∣γ∣B1​. Keeping these amplitude conventions distinct avoids a factor-of-two pulse-duration error.
For a single harmonic pulse detuned by Δ=ω0​−ω, the effective rotating-frame field tilts out of the transverse plane. Its rotation rate becomes Ω2+Δ2​, and its maximum population transfer is Ω2/(Ω2+Δ2). Exact inversion to −z is impossible with one fixed nonzero-detuning pulse. Half transfer, needed for a transverse eigenstate, gives the equatorial reachability of a detuned spin pulse criterion ∣Δ∣≤Ω. With that condition, changing the duration and RF phase can still prepare +x or +y at the chosen laboratory stopping time. The mere statement ω≈ω0​ does not ensure this condition, because the drive can be much weaker than either frequency. If ∣Δ∣>Ω, neither transverse target is reached by that single pulse. These restrictions concern fixed-frequency, fixed-amplitude single-pulse control; retuning, chirping or phase-controlled composite pulses change the available controls and can remove the restriction. No dissipative spin relaxation is included in this coherent two-state model.

The +x eigenstate is ∣+x⟩=(∣↑⟩+∣↓⟩)/2​, since σx​(1,1)T=(1,1)T. Use a resonant quarter-turn pulse, τπ/2​=π/(2Ω), and choose its phase so that
φ+ω0​τπ/2​=2π​(mod2π).
(31)
The laboratory-frame relative amplitude then is ie−iπ/2=1, giving
∣ψ(τπ/2​)⟩∼∣+x⟩,⟨sx​⟩=ℏ/2.​
(32)
In rotating axes the equivalent target is obtained with φ=π/2; the extra laboratory phase above compensates Larmor precession during the pulse. After switching the RF field off, the spin continues to precess in the static field, so this statement specifies the preparation instant rather than a state fixed along laboratory x forever.
