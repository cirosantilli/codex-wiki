The closed shells have L=S=0, so only the two equivalent p electrons matter. Their addition of angular momentum allows L=0,1,2 and S=0,1, subject to the Pauli exclusion principle. Interchanging two l=1 orbital states has sign (−1)2−L=(−1)L: even L is spatially symmetric and needs the antisymmetric spin singlet; odd L is spatially antisymmetric and needs the symmetric spin triplet. Thus the allowed LS terms of a p-squared configuration are 1S, 1D and 3P.
In LS coupling, J=∣L−S∣,…,L+S. The complete list of term symbols is therefore
1S0​,1D2​,3P0​, 3P1​, 3P2​.​
(1)
They contain 1+5+(1+3+5)=15=(26​) magnetic substates, exhausting the antisymmetric two-electron space. All have even spatial parity, since each p orbital is odd.

For the linear rigid rotor, Eℓ​=ℏ2ℓ(ℓ+1)/(2I) and the electric electric dipole moment interaction is V=−Ed^z​. The isotropic ℓ=0 state has no first-order shift. Its only coupled rotational state is ℓ=1,mℓ​=0, at gap Δrot​=ℏ2/I, giving the quadratic Stark shift of a polar linear rotor
ΔEg​=−Δrot​E2(d2/3)​=−3ℏ2Id2E2​.​
(2)
Using 1kVcm−1=105Vm−1 and the supplied I and d gives
ΔEg​≃−8.05×10−27J,ΔEg​/h≃−12.2MHz.​
(3)
The negative sign is the expected second-order Stark effect of a nondegenerate ground state. The mixing parameter dE/(3​Δrot​)≃0.074 is small, supporting the weak-field estimate. The constants h and ℏ=h/(2π) use their defined SI values; no molecular input beyond the supplied data is needed.

The coherent state satisfies ⟨a⟩=α, ⟨a†⟩=α∗ and ⟨a†a⟩=∣α∣2. The canonical commutation relation adds the vacuum term ⟨aa†⟩=∣α∣2+1. Therefore
⟨E^⟩=E0​(α+α∗),⟨E^2⟩=E02​[α2+(α∗)2+2∣α∣2+1],
(4)
and the coherent-state electric-field variance is
(ΔE)2=⟨E^2⟩−⟨E^⟩2=E02​,ΔE=E0​.​
(5)
Here E0​ is taken positive as the field-amplitude scale. The coherent state displaces the mean field without changing this vacuum optical quadrature noise.

The WKB approximation, also called JWKB, describes a short local de Broglie wavelength in a slowly varying potential energy. For p(x)=2m[E−V(x)]​ in a classically allowed region, its two local waves are
ψ(x)≃p(x)​1​[Ae(i/ℏ)∫xp(s)ds+Be−(i/ℏ)∫xp(s)ds].​
(6)
The phase follows the classical momentum; its amplitude varies to conserve probability current. In a forbidden region, putting κ(x)=2m[V(x)−E]​ gives ψ≃κ−1/2[Ce∫xκ/ℏ+De−∫xκ/ℏ], with boundary conditions selecting the physical combination.
The WKB validity condition is ℏ∣p′∣/p2≪1, with the analogous condition on κ. It fails at a turning point where E=V. For a simple turning point, locally linearizing V yields an Airy function; matching fixes the relative amplitudes and a π/4 phase. Two such turning points give the two-turning-point WKB quantization condition
∫x1​x2​​p(x)dx=(n+21​)πℏ,n=0,1,….​
(7)
For an opaque barrier, the WKB tunnelling exponent instead gives
T∼exp[−ℏ2​∫x1​x2​​κ(x)dx].​
(8)
The factor two distinguishes probability from amplitude. Matching determines the prefactor; the exponential alone is not accurate near the barrier top. These formulas connect bound state energy eigenvalues and quantum tunnelling to classical actions. [ref]

For a point, stationary proton, the leading hydrogen fine structure comes from three order-1/c2 contributions to the nonrelativistic electron Hamiltonian:
Hfs​=−8me3​c2p4​+2me2​c2r1​drdV​L⋅S+8me2​c2ℏ2​∇2V,V=−4πϵ0​re2​.​
(9)
These are the relativistic kinetic-energy correction, spin-orbit interaction and Darwin term. The spin-orbit coefficient includes the Thomas precession factor of one half; ⟨L⋅S⟩=ℏ2[j(j+1)−l(l+1)−3/4]/2. The Coulomb Darwin contribution is a positive contact term, relevant for S states. These corrections distinguish total angular momentum j=l±1/2, while leaving magnetic substates degenerate without an external field.
Writing αfs​=e2/(4πϵ0​ℏc), the combined hydrogen fine-structure correction is
ΔEnj​=−2n3me​c2αfs4​​[j+1/21​−4n3​].​
(10)
Thus the splitting is of order αfs2​ times the principal binding energy. In the n=2 shell, 2S1/2​ and 2P1/2​ remain degenerate in this approximation, while 2P3/2​ is higher by me​c2αfs4​/32. The Lamb shift lifts the remaining S–P degeneracy; hyperfine structure is a separate nuclear-spin effect. Neither is part of the displayed correction. Nuclear recoil and finite nuclear size have also been neglected. [ref]

A three-level laser uses a pump transition from ground level1 to a higher level3, rapid relaxation 3→2, and a long-lived upper lasing level2. The laser transition is 2→1, so its lower level is the ground state. Fast feeding and slow upper-state decay allow excitation to accumulate, unlike steady resonant pumping of a simple two-level transition.
Stimulated emission adds photons to the stimulating optical mode, but ground-state photon absorption competes with it. The population inversion condition is N2​/g2​>N1​/g1​ when the relevant transition strengths obey the usual Einstein relations. For equal degeneracies and negligible N3​, it reduces to N2​>N1​, requiring more than half the active population in level2.
For a simple pump rate R per ground-state emitter, fast 3→2 relaxation and negligible laser intensity, the three-level laser rate equation reduces to
N˙2​=RN1​−τ21​N2​​,N1​+N2​≃Ntot​,N1​N2​​=Rτ21​,Rτ21​>1 for inversion.​
(11)
This three-level ground-state inversion threshold is only transparency; positive gain must also overcome optical losses. For an illustrative uniformly filled cavity of length ℓ, equal absorption/emission cross-section σ, internal intensity-loss coefficient αi​ and mirror power reflectivities R1​,R2​, the threshold condition is
g=σ(n2​−n1​)≥αi​+2ℓ1​lnR1​R2​1​.​
(12)
Here ni​ are number densities, unlike the total populations Ni​. The optical resonator supplies feedback and selects modes; a partially transmitting mirror couples out light. Spontaneous emission seeds the field, and stimulated transitions deplete the inversion until saturated gain balances loss. A ruby laser is the standard example. Real level degeneracies, branching losses and pump saturation modify the simple equations. [ref]

For a spinless particle, minimal coupling gives H=(p−qA)2/(2m)+qϕ. Choose the vector potential A=(0,αx2/2,0) and ϕ=0. Its curl is B=αxz^ and [py​,Ay​]=0, so
H=2m1​[px2​+(py​−2qαx2​)2].​
(13)
The translation separation in a magnetic field gradient follows from [H,py​]=0: a simultaneous energy eigenstate can be written Ψ=Ly−1/2​eikyΦk​(x). The periodic boundary condition requires eikLy​=1, hence k=2πn/Ly​, n∈Z. The remaining Schrödinger equation is
[−2mℏ2​dx2d2​+Vk​(x)]Φk​=Ek​Φk​,Vk​(x)=2m1​(ℏk−2gx2​)2,g=qα.​
(14)
This magnetic-gradient quartic confinement has a positive quartic coefficient for g=0. If k/g<0, it is a single well at zero, with Vk​(0)=ℏ2k2/(2m). Its harmonic curvature gives
ωs​=m−ℏgk​​,Φ0​∝e−x2/(2as2​),Φ1​∝xe−x2/(2as2​),as​=mωs​ℏ​​.
(15)
Thus the two lowest states are respectively even and odd, with one central peak or two opposite-sign lobes, and approximately Ej​=Vk​(0)+(j+1/2)ℏωs​. They are not a nearly degenerate pair.
If k/g>0, the two minima are x=±x0​, x0​=2ℏk/g​, with zero potential there and a central barrier ℏ2k2/(2m). For well-separated minima the lowest parity states of a symmetric double well are
Φ0​≃2(1+s)​ϕL​+ϕR​​,Φ1​≃2(1−s)​ϕL​−ϕR​​,s=⟨ϕL​∣ϕR​⟩≪1.​
(16)
Here ϕL​,ϕR​ are the localized ground-state Gaussians. Their even combination is the lower state; the odd combination has a node at the origin. Weak quantum tunnelling gives an exponentially small double-well tunneling splitting.
To obtain their common leading energy, put x=x0​+δx. Since ℏk=gx02​/2,
Vk​(x0​+δx)=2mg2x02​​(δx)2+O((δx)3),ωd​=m∣g∣x0​​=m2ℏgk​​.
(17)
The double-well oscillator scale in a linear magnetic field therefore gives
Ek,0​≃Ek,1​≃2ℏωd​​=m1​2ℏ3qαk​​.​
(18)
The asymptotic condition is x0​≫ℏ/(mωd​)​, so the barrier separates narrow local oscillator states. It is this scale comparison that makes the source's “large” ratio meaningful.
The signed group velocity of a magnetic-gradient doublet follows by differentiating the band energy with respect to the conserved wavevector:
vy​=ℏ1​dkdEk​​≃2ℏkEk​​=2m2ℏqαk​ℏqα​.​
(19)
For qα>0 the double-well branch has k>0 and moves along +y; reversing both signs reverses its velocity. To this order the two parity states have the same velocity. Corrections include local anharmonicity and the exponentially small splitting. The spinless Hamiltonian specified here has no extra Zeeman effect term.

Insert the expansion in the time-dependent Schrödinger equation, cancel the unperturbed evolution, and project onto ⟨ψn​∣. With Vnm​(t)=⟨ψn​∣V(t)∣ψm​⟩,
iℏc˙n​(t)=∑m​ei(ωn​−ωm​)tVnm​(t)cm​(t).
(20)
In first-order time-dependent perturbation theory, put cm​(t)=δm0​ on the right and use cn​(−∞)=0 for n=0. Integration gives
cn​(t)=iℏ1​∫−∞t​ei(ωn​−ω0​)t′Vn0​(t′)dt′+O(V2).​
(21)
The initial state must be the specified unperturbed eigenstate, the integral must exist under the switching prescription, and total transition probability must stay small. The diagonal term produces a first-order phase; setting c0​=1 in the off-diagonal integral changes those amplitudes only at second order. A weak peak perturbation alone is insufficient if its accumulated action invalidates this expansion.
For the infinite square well, retain the source's zero-based labels:
ψn​(x)=w2​​sinw(n+1)πx​,En​=2me​w2π2ℏ2(n+1)2​,n=0,1,….
(22)
An electron in a field along +x has perturbation V(t)=+eE0​xe−t/τ for t≥0. About the well centre, x−w/2 is odd, whereas ψ0​ and ψ2​ are both even. The constant w/2 has zero off-diagonal matrix element, so the first-order parity selection in an infinite well gives ⟨ψ2​∣x∣ψ0​⟩=0. Excitation to that state is forbidden at first order, not necessarily at higher orders.
For the first excited state, direct integration or the supplied integral gives
x10​=w2​∫0w​xsinw2πx​sinwπx​dx=−9π216w​,ω10​=2me​w23π2ℏ​.
(23)
The Fourier amplitude of the exponentially decaying electric-field pulse is ∫0∞​e−t/τ+iωtdt=τ/(1−iωτ). Thus
c1​(∞)=iℏ(1−iω10​τ)eE0​x10​τ​,P0→1​=81π4ℏ2[1+(3π2ℏτ/(2me​w2))2]256e2E02​w2τ2​.​
(24)
This is the leading, quadratic-in-field transition probability; the total probability of all excitations must remain small.
Starting instead in ψ1​, which is odd about the centre, the pulse can transfer amplitude to the ground state and to even-labelled excited states ψ2​,ψ4​,…. Other odd-labelled states have zero first-order dipole amplitude. Both downward and upward transitions are possible; their relative probabilities depend on dipole matrix elements and the pulse's spectral weight at the corresponding energy gaps. Most population remains in ψ1​, with a small depletion equal to the summed leading transition probabilities. The common diagonal dipole phase in an infinite well is −eE0​wτ/(2ℏ) and has no population effect. After the pulse, the remaining coherent superposition evolves under the unperturbed Hamiltonian; no spontaneous relaxation is implied by this closed-system model.
