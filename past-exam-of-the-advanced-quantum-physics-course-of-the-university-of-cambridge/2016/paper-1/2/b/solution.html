Stimulated emission adds a photon to an already occupied radiation mode; spontaneous emission can occur when that mode initially contains no photons. In the electric-dipole approximation the atom-field interaction is Hint​=−d⋅E. Quantizing the radiation field makes the emission matrix element into a mode with occupation n proportional to
⟨n+1∣a†∣n⟩=n+1​.
(12)
This photon occupation enhancement of emission makes the emission rate into that mode proportional to n+1: the part proportional to n is stimulated and the remaining part is spontaneous. Absorption is proportional to ∣⟨n−1∣a∣n⟩∣2=n. The photon energy is the atomic energy difference, up to any specified recoil or line broadening.
The stimulated contribution preferentially populates the stimulating mode, including its propagation direction and polarization; it provides optical gain. Spontaneous photons populate the available modes according to the transition's dipole matrix elements and radiation environment. They are not universally isotropic. An excited bare atom with the field vacuum is not an eigenstate of the fully coupled Hamiltonian, which resolves the apparent paradox of a nominally stationary excited state radiating.[ref]
