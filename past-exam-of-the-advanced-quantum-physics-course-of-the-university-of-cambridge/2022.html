By addition of angular momentum, combining the first two spin one-half particles gives 0⊕1. Adding the third gives the three spin-one-half angular-momentum decomposition

ES,M​=ϵt​S(S+1)+ϵz​M.​

Write the trap angular frequency as ω and the single-particle oscillator length as ℓ=ℏ/(mω)​. The center of mass coordinate X=(x1​+x2​)/2 and relative coordinate r=x1​−x2​ give independent quantum harmonic oscillators, both of frequency ω, with masses 2m and reduced mass m/2. Label their excitation numbers by N and n. The unperturbed energy eigenvalue is ℏω(N+n+1).
Particle exchange leaves X unchanged and sends r to −r. The exchange symmetry of two identical spin-one-half fermions therefore requires even n to accompany the spin-one-half singlet state, and odd n to accompany a spin-one-half triplet state. For the contact shifts of two spin-one-half fermions in a harmonic trap, the delta potential depends only on r. For odd n, the relative wavefunction vanishes at r=0, so its contact shift is zero. Within the first-excited manifold, both perturbations are diagonal in the labels (N,n,S,M): this is a perturbation diagonal within a degenerate eigenspace. For n=0, the relative oscillator length is 2​ℓ, giving the first-order nondegenerate perturbation theory shift
U=λ∣φrel,0​(0)∣2=2π​ℓλ​.
(7)
The Zeeman splitting is 2μB​Bz​M for total spin projection ℏM. Thus the five lowest states, counted with multiplicity, have
(N,n)(0,0)(0,1)(1,0)​S010​M0−1,0,10​E to first orderℏω+U2ℏω+2μB​Bz​M2ℏω+U​​​
(8)
under the assumed conditions ∣U∣,2μB​∣Bz​∣≪ℏω. The three triplet states have slopes −2μB​,0,2μB​, while both singlet states are field independent at this order. The three triplet state branches coincide at zero magnetic field. Their crossing with the excited singlet state is allowed because the total-spin sectors do not mix. The odd relative wavefunctions are in fact unaffected by this delta potential at any coupling; the displayed singlet state shifts retain only first order.

The unperturbed infinite square well is invariant under the parity operator. Its one-dimensional energy eigenvalues are nondegenerate, so each energy eigenstate has definite parity. Its position expectation value therefore vanishes: ∣ϕn​(x)∣2 is even, while x is odd. The first-order nondegenerate perturbation theory correction is consequently
δϵn(1)​=eE⟨ϕn​∣x∣ϕn​⟩=0.
(23)
The electric field does mix opposite-parity quantum states, and second-order nondegenerate perturbation theory contains two factors of eEx. Its leading contribution is thus proportional to E2: this is a quadratic Stark effect. More generally, applying the parity operator changes H(E) into H(−E), so their corresponding energy eigenvalues agree. An analytic expansion about zero electric field contains only even powers of E. We assume e∣E∣a is small compared with the unperturbed excitation scale.

The electron's electric dipole moment is d=−e⟨x⟩x^. It vanishes in the unperturbed ground energy eigenstate. The first correction to the wavefunction is linear in the electric field and has the opposite parity; its interference with the unperturbed state therefore gives a nonzero position expectation value of first order.
In detail, the normalized ground quantum state, to the order needed, is
∣g⟩=∣ϕ0​⟩+eE∑k≥1​ϵ0(0)​−ϵk(0)​xk0​​∣ϕk​⟩+O(E2).

⟨x⟩=2eE∑k≥1​ϵ0(0)​−ϵk(0)​∣xk0​∣2​+O(E3).
(29)

The Pauli matrix σx​ flips the spin with unit matrix element. A nonzero oscillator matrix element changes exactly one occupation number, through a creation operator or an annihilation operator:
⟨nj​+1∣aj†​∣nj​⟩=nj​+1​,⟨nj​−1∣aj​∣nj​⟩=nj​​.
(41)
For a spin flip, the spin energy change divided by ℏ is −sσ​Ω. Thus creation and annihilation in mode j have total frequency changes ωj​−sσ​Ω and −ωj​−sσ​Ω, respectively. Distinct modes or different occupation changes lead to orthogonal final quantum states, so their probabilities add. Fermi's golden rule consequently gives
∣cησ​(t)∣2=ℏ22πλ2t​∑j​[(nj​+1)δ(ωj​−sσ​Ω)+nj​δ(ωj​+sσ​Ω)],η=σ.
(42)
The Dirac delta function is even, accounting for the sign in the annihilation term. Since all ωj​,Ω>0, an initially up spin can only emit an oscillator quantum, while an initially down spin can only absorb one. Writing the oscillator density of states per unit angular frequency as g(ω)=∑j​δ(ω−ωj​), the continuum limit gives
∣cησ​(t)∣2=ℏ22πλ2t​{g(Ω)(nΩ​+1),g(Ω)nΩ​,​σ=↑,σ=↓,​η=σ.​
(43)
The +1 permits emission even from the oscillator vacuum; absorption requires a quantum already present. The notation nΩ​ assumes a common or smoothly specified occupation at the resonant frequency. If several resonant modes have different occupations, it denotes their density of states weighted mean, so that g(Ω)nΩ​=∑j​nj​δ(Ω−ωj​) after continuum smoothing. This is the golden-rule relaxation of a spin coupled to an oscillator bath.

The thermal oscillator occupation is the Bose-Einstein distribution. With the rate notation Γησ​ for a transition from σ to η, the master equation for the up population is
dtdp↑​​=Γ↑↓​p↓​−Γ↓↑​p↑​=Γ0​[nΩ​p↓​−(nΩ​+1)p↑​].
(46)
At thermal equilibrium, assuming Γ0​>0, vanishing time derivative is detailed balance between the two spin states. Hence
p↓​p↑​​=nΩ​+1nΩ​​=eβℏΩ/(eβℏΩ−1)1/(eβℏΩ−1)​=e−βℏΩ,
(47)
or
p↑​=e−βℏΩp↓​.​
(48)
Together with normalization, this gives p↑​=1/(1+eβℏΩ) and p↓​=eβℏΩ/(1+eβℏΩ), the Boltzmann distribution for the energy eigenvalues ±ℏΩ/2. At zero temperature, absorption vanishes and emission relaxes the spin into its down ground state.
The nonzero-rate condition is necessary: if λ=0 or g(Ω)=0, both rates vanish, so every initial pair of populations is stationary within this approximation. In that case stationarity alone cannot imply a thermal distribution. The equilibrium conclusion therefore presumes coupling to resonant oscillator modes.
