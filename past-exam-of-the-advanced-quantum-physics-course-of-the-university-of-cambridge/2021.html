The initial quantum state is a coherent state. Since a∣α⟩=α∣α⟩ and the state is normalized, its mean occupation number is N=⟨a†a⟩=∣α∣2. Each quantum harmonic oscillator energy eigenstate acquires the phase e−iω(n+1/2)t, so
∣α(t)⟩=e−iωt/2∣αe−iωt⟩,⟨a⟩t​=αe−iωt.
(1)
The prefactor is a global phase and does not change an expectation value. Inverting the definitions of the annihilation operator and creation operator gives x=ℏ/(2mω)​(a+a†). If α=N​eiφ, then
⟨x⟩t​=mω2ℏN​​cos(ωt−φ).​
(2)
For a classical harmonic oscillator with amplitude A, the conserved energy is E=mω2A2/2. Setting E=ℏωN gives exactly the same amplitude, A=2ℏN/(mω)​. Thus these coherent-state oscillator expectation values follow the classical orbit.
The full quantum mean energy is nevertheless ⟨H⟩=ℏω(N+1/2): the extra zero-point energy belongs to the nonzero position and momentum uncertainties required by the Heisenberg uncertainty relation, rather than to the motion of the packet centre. In particular, N=0 gives zero mean displacement but a finite ground-state width. A coherent state follows a classical mean trajectory while retaining finite position and momentum uncertainties.

Use the uncoupled angular momentum eigenstates ∣mL​,↑⟩ and ∣mL​,↓⟩, with mL​=−1,0,1. For the Hamiltonian operator actually written in the paper, these are the energy eigenstates, with
E=μB​Bz​(mL​+2mS​),mS​=±21​.
(3)
The initial J=3/2 states are not all energy eigenstates of this operator. To find their energy populations, first perform the Clebsch-Gordan decomposition. The extremal states are ∣3/2,3/2⟩=∣1,↑⟩ and ∣3/2,−3/2⟩=∣−1,↓⟩. Applying the total angular momentum lowering operator to the first gives
3​∣3/2,1/2⟩=2​∣0,↑⟩+∣1,↓⟩.
(4)
Similarly,
∣3/2,−1/2⟩=32​​∣0,↓⟩+31​​∣−1,↑⟩.
(5)
The initial preparation is an equal incoherent mixture of the four coupled states. Each extremal state contributes population 1/4 to its one energy eigenvalue, while each nonextremal state contributes 2/3 and 1/3 of its own 1/4 population to the two displayed components. The resulting Zeeman splitting is therefore
E/(μB​Bz​)fraction of atomsenergy multiplicity​−21/41​−11/61​01/62​11/61​21/41​​​
(6)
for nonzero Bz​. The relative populations are 3:2:2:2:3​ in this order; the two zero-energy states each have population 1/12. These Zeeman populations from an unpolarized angular-momentum multiplet remain constant under subsequent evolution by the stated static Hamiltonian operator, although off-diagonal quantum coherence can evolve.
There is a distinct weak-field model in which a retained spin-orbit interaction keeps J=3/2 isolated and one projects the magnetic perturbation into that quartet. The Landé g-factor is then 4/3, giving four shifts μB​Bz​(±2,±2/3) with equal populations. That calculation requires the additional projection or dominant spin-orbit interaction; it is not diagonalization of the full magnetic Hamiltonian operator specified here, which also mixes the J=1/2 sector.

A confined ground state requires k>0; take α>0 so the trial wavefunction is normalizable. Its normalized form is
fα​(x)=(πα​)1/4e−αx2/2.
(7)
The Gaussian moment recurrence gives ⟨x2⟩=1/(2α) and ⟨x4⟩=3/(4α2). Also, integration by parts gives ⟨p2⟩=ℏ2∫∣fα′​∣2dx=ℏ2α/2. The Rayleigh quotient for the quartic oscillator is consequently
E(α)=4mℏ2α​+4α23k​.
(8)
Differentiation yields E′(α)=ℏ2/(4m)−3k/(2α3), so the unique minimum is at α∗​=(6mk/ℏ2)1/3. Since the quotient diverges at both endpoints 0 and ∞, this is the global minimum in the trial family. The Rayleigh-Ritz variational principle gives
E0​≤E(α∗​)=8m3ℏ2​(ℏ26mk​)1/3.​
(9)
For the first excited energy eigenstate, choose the odd trial family
gβ​(x)=(π4β3​)1/4xe−βx2/2,β>0.​
(10)
The even confining potential has an even, nodeless ground state, by the nodeless theorem for a one-dimensional ground state. By the Sturm oscillation theorem, its first excited state is odd with one node. The proposed family has the same parity operator eigenvalue and one node, and is orthogonal to the exact ground state, not merely to its Gaussian trial approximation. Minimization in the odd linear subspace therefore bounds the first excited energy from above. For completeness, its Rayleigh quotient is 3ℏ2β/(4m)+15k/(4β2), minimized at β3=10mk/ℏ2, giving E1​≤(9ℏ2/8m)(10mk/ℏ2)1/3. These are Gaussian variational bounds for a quartic oscillator.
If k=0, there is no normalizable free particle ground state on the whole line, though the spectral infimum is zero. If k<0, the Hamiltonian operator is not bounded below, so a ground-energy bound of the requested kind does not exist.

Write ωz​=γBz​ and use the ordered spin basis (∣↑⟩,∣↓⟩). The free Hamiltonian operator is H0​=−ℏωz​σz​/2, so
VI​(t)=−2γℏBx​​cos(ωt)(0eiωz​t​e−iωz​t0​).
(13)

cos(ωt)e−iωz​t=21​[ei(ω−ωz​)t+e−i(ω+ωz​)t],
(14)
with the complex conjugate in the lower entry. Choose the field orientation with ωz​>0, as implicit in the printed near-resonance condition, and put δ=ω−ωz​. The rotating-wave approximation discards the terms at ω+ωz​, retaining the slow terms at δ. The resulting interaction picture equations are
dtd​(c1​c2​​)=4iγBx​​(0e−iδt​eiδt0​)(c1​c2​​).​
(15)
The factor 1/4 contains 1/2 from Sx​=ℏσx​/2 and 1/2 from the cosine decomposition. This is a Driven two-level Rabi model, with signed on-resonance coupling Ω=γBx​/2 and generalized Rabi angular frequency ωR​=δ2+Ω2​. Here the Rabi detuning uses the paper's convention, drive minus free frequency.
Small detuning alone is insufficient to derive the printed equations. One also requires ∣γBx​∣,∣δ∣≪ω+ωz​, and an evolution time over which neglected terms stay small. At resonance this is the weak transverse-field regime ∣Bx​∣≪∣Bz​∣. The supplied solution is thus an approximate solution for the linearly oscillating field, not an exact one at arbitrary drive strength. If γBz​<0, the other exponential is resonant and the physical positive resonance frequency is ∣γBz​∣.

At resonance, retain the signed coupling Ω=γBx​/2. In the interaction picture, the given solution reduces to c1​=cos(Ωt/2) and c2​=isin(Ωt/2). Returning to the Schrödinger picture gives
∣ψ(t)⟩=eiωz​t/2cos(Ωt/2)∣↑⟩+ie−iωz​t/2sin(Ωt/2)∣↓⟩.
(19)
For a normalized spinor (a,b)T, the Pauli matrices give ⟨σ⟩=(2Rea∗b,2Ima∗b,∣a∣2−∣b∣2). Therefore the spin expectation value within the rotating-wave approximation is
⟨S⟩t​=2ℏ​​sin(Ωt)sin(ωz​t)sin(Ωt)cos(ωz​t)cos(Ωt)​​.​
(20)
Its magnitude is ℏ/2: the Bloch vector stays on the unit sphere. In a frame rotating about z at angular velocity −ωz​, it makes a uniform rotation in the yz plane. For ∣Bx​∣≪∣Bz​∣, this gives slow Rabi oscillations between the poles, accompanied by rapid laboratory Larmor precession about z.
If the same formula is formally used for ∣Bz​∣≪∣Bx​∣, it predicts fast meridional rotations whose plane turns slowly about z. However, the original PDF really does ask for this opposite limit, and it lies outside the assumption used in part (b). That formal description is not a controlled prediction for the stated linearly driven Hamiltonian operator.
A valid strong-drive description starts from the exact equation for the Bloch vector s=2⟨S⟩/ℏ:
dtds​=γs×(Bx​cos(ωt),0,Bz​).
(21)
For times ∣γBz​∣t≪1, neglecting the weak bias gives a controlled leading approximation, since the time integral of the omitted Hamiltonian operator has small norm. The remaining field has a fixed x axis, so its generators commute at different times and
s(t)≃(0,sinΘ(t),cosΘ(t)),Θ(t)=ωγBx​​sin(ωt).
(22)
At the nominal resonance ω=γBz​ and on the early spin-rotation scale t∼1/∣γBx​∣, this becomes Θ≃γBx​t. The initial rotation rate is consequently γBx​, twice the rate predicted by extrapolating the rotating-wave approximation. For longer times the exact differential equation must be retained. The following original calculation illustrates both regimes rather than asserting an exact strong-drive Rabi formula.

The components of the momentum operator do not generally commute with a position-dependent magnetic vector potential. Expanding minimal coupling with the ordering intact gives
H=2mp2​+V+2me​(p⋅A+A⋅p)+2me2​A2.
(23)
Acting on a wavefunction shows that p⋅A−A⋅p=−iℏ∇⋅A. In the Coulomb gauge the divergence vanishes, so the two cross terms agree. Discarding the quadratic term in the weak radiation amplitude gives

(24)
The equality of the two operator orderings follows from the gauge condition, not from treating p and A as commuting numbers component by component.

For the requested oscillator response, treat y as the fixed transverse position of a one-dimensional quantum harmonic oscillator along z, and put θ=ky. The supplied radiation is transverse and already satisfies the Coulomb gauge. Its perturbation is
H1​(t)=meA0​​pz​(eiθe−iωt+e−iθeiωt),t>0.
(25)
The annihilation operator and creation operator give
pz​=i2mℏω0​​​(a†−a),⟨n∣pz​∣0⟩=i2mℏω0​​​δn1​.
(26)
Thus the only first-order transition out of the ground state is to ∣1⟩. More generally, the oscillator electric-dipole selection rule is Δn=±1. This is a rule for matrix elements; exact energy conservation is replaced by a finite-time resonance profile during a switched finite-duration interaction.
To display both the resonant and nonresonant terms without spurious singularities, define
Fν​(t)=∫0t​eiνt′dt′={(eiνt−1)/(iν),t,​ν=0,ν=0,​C=eA0​2mℏω0​​​.
(27)
The interaction picture coefficient in the given expansion is then
b1​(t)=C[eiθFω0​−ω​(t)+e−iθFω0​+ω​(t)],bn​(t)=0 (n≥2).​
(28)
The full Schrödinger picture coefficient of ∣1⟩ is e−3iω0​t/2b1​(t), while that of ∣0⟩ is e−iω0​t/2 at this order. The normalization changes only at order A02​. In particular, at ω=ω0​ the first term is Ceiθt, finite for every finite time, and the perturbative result requires ∣b1​∣≪1.
The fixed-y interpretation is needed for the displayed target formulae. If p2 were instead a three-dimensional kinetic operator with no transverse confinement, that model would have no normalizable transverse ground state, and e±iky would transfer transverse momentum ±ℏk. A treatment of that different model needs specified transverse states and recoil energies; they are absent from the question. The following parts use the intended localized-oscillator response.

Inside the infinite square well, the orbital and spin operators act on different factors of the tensor product. Write S=S1​+S2​. The identity
S1​⋅S2​=21​(S2−S12​−S22​)=21​(S2−23​ℏ2)
(36)
shows that the exchange interaction commutes with S2 and Sz​. The sum of the one-particle kinetic energies also commutes with them and with particle exchange. Thus one may use orbital quantum numbers together with the total-spin sector S=0,1 and projection M=−S,…,S, with physical Sz​=ℏM.
For identical spin one-half particles the Pauli exclusion principle further restricts the states. The spin-one-half singlet state is antisymmetric in spin and must multiply a symmetric spatial wavefunction; a spin-one-half triplet state must multiply an antisymmetric spatial wavefunction. Thus n1​,n2​ label unordered orbital occupations in symmetry-adapted states, not arbitrary ordered product states. This gives the labels ∣n1​,n2​,S,M⟩, with the paper's last label expressed in units of ℏ.

For distinguishable particles, the ordered spatial products are independent states, and neither singlet states nor triplet states are excluded from any orbital occupation. The unperturbed energy eigenvalues are
Ers,0​=(r2+s2)ε+3J/4,Ers,1​=(r2+s2)ε−J/4.

energy2ε−J/42ε+3J/45ε−J/4​multiplicity316​normalized eigenfunctionsu1​(x1​)u1​(x2​)χ1M​u1​(x1​)u1​(x2​)χ00​u1​(x1​)u2​(x2​)χ1M​, u2​(x1​)u1​(x2​)χ1M​​​​
(44)
where M=−1,0,1. The first two levels do have zero first-order correction, since their spatial expectation value factorizes as ⟨1∣x∣1⟩2=0.
The last instruction in the original PDF is false for the third level. Although every diagonal product-state matrix element vanishes, the two orbital states have degenerate energy levels. Degenerate perturbation theory requires diagonalizing the perturbation within their span. For each M, its matrix in the ordered basis (u1​u2​,u2​u1​) is
W=α(0D​D0​),D=⟨1∣x∣2⟩⟨2∣x∣1⟩>0.
(45)
The eigenfunctions are Φ12±​χ1M​, with
δE±(1)​=±αD=±81π4256αa2​,multiplicity three for each branch.​
(46)
Thus the sixfold unperturbed level splits into two triplets. Its average shift is zero, and the diagonal expectation in an individual ordered product state is zero, but neither fact makes all its energy eigenvalue corrections zero. This is the degenerate orbital exchange splitting from a product perturbation; distinguishability allows both combinations rather than removing their coupling.
For negative J, the lowest two rows exchange order and the third unperturbed level is instead the twofold singlet orbital pair at 5ε+3J/4. It splits into Φ12±​χ00​ with the same shifts ±αD, each nondegenerate. At J=0, the three lowest distinct unperturbed energies are 2ε,5ε,8ε, with multiplicities 4,8,4. The first and third have zero first-order shift; the middle splits by ±αD, each fourfold. The printed zero-shift assertion therefore fails also in the zero-exchange limit.
