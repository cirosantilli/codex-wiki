Use ∣m1​,m2​⟩ for a product of the two individual spin-one spin eigenstates, with magnetic quantum numbers mi​=−1,0,1. Total Sz​=0 requires m1​+m2​=0, leaving precisely
∣1,−1⟩,∣0,0⟩,∣−1,1⟩.​
(1)
Here these are product labels, not labels of the total angular momentum. Write their coefficients in this order as α,β,γ. With physical spin ladder operators S±​=S1±​+S2±​, each nonzero individual step has amplitude 2​ℏ, so
S+​∣ψ⟩=2​ℏ[(α+β)∣1,0⟩+(β+γ)∣0,1⟩],
(2)
S−​∣ψ⟩=2​ℏ[(α+β)∣0,−1⟩+(β+γ)∣−1,0⟩].
(3)
Both must vanish for a singlet state. The independent product states therefore require α=γ=−β. Wavefunction normalization gives the spin-one singlet state
∣S=0,M=0⟩=3​∣1,−1⟩−∣0,0⟩+∣−1,1⟩​,​
(4)
up to an overall quantum phase. Conversely, Sz​∣ψ⟩=S+​∣ψ⟩=0 implies S2∣ψ⟩=0 through S2=S−​S+​+Sz2​+ℏSz​, so these conditions really select total spin zero. Distinguishability imposes no additional exchange restriction. The ladder hint uses dimensionless angular momentum units; restoring its common factor ℏ does not change the coefficient ratios.

For a bound energy eigenstate with a nondegenerate energy eigenvalue of an even one-dimensional potential, the wavefunction can be chosen with definite parity. A uniform electric field E gives the electric-dipole interaction H′=−qEx, which is odd under reflection. Hence
ΔEn(1)​=−qE⟨n∣x∣n⟩=0,
(5)
since x∣ψn​(x)∣2 is odd. Second-order nondegenerate perturbation theory then gives the leading allowed Stark effect shift:
ΔEn​=q2E2r=n∑​En(0)​−Er(0)​∣⟨r∣x∣n⟩∣2​+O(E4).​
(6)
Only opposite-parity intermediate states have nonzero position matrix elements. More generally, reflection maps H(E) to H(−E), so an analytic nondegenerate energy level is an even function of the field. This explains the absence of all odd orders and the usual quadratic Stark effect.
A level separation changes by the difference of the two quadratic coefficients. Symmetry alone does not guarantee a nonzero differential shift: in a displaced quantum harmonic oscillator every level moves by the same −q2E2/(2mω2), leaving every spacing unchanged. Degenerate states would instead require degenerate perturbation theory and can split linearly; the nondegeneracy of one-dimensional bound states applies to ordinary isolated well levels.

Neglect hyperfine structure and use the electronic weak-field Zeeman splitting regime, where μB​B is small compared with the spin-orbit interaction splitting. The sodium levels are 3s2S1/2​, 3p2P1/2​ and 3p2P3/2​. The Landé g-factor formula gives
g(3s2S1/2​)=2,g(3p2P1/2​)=32​,g(3p2P3/2​)=34​.​
(7)
For example, inserting L=1,S=1/2,J=1/2 gives 3/2+(3/4−2)/(2⋅3/4)=2/3. Each sublevel with magnetic quantum number mJ​ shifts by gJ​mJ​μB​B. Thus the lower level has shifts ±μB​B, the upper J=1/2 level has shifts ±μB​B/3, and the upper J=3/2 level has shifts ±2μB​B and ±2μB​B/3.
The electric-dipole selection rule allows ΔJ=0,±1 except 0↔0, and mu​−ml​=0,±1. Both fine structure branches are allowed. The emitted photon energy is
Eγ​=Eu(0)​−El(0)​+μB​B(gu​mu​−2ml​).
(8)
All the allowed components of the sodium D-line Zeeman pattern are listed below; the last column is the photon shift in units of μB​B:
upper term2P1/2​2P3/2​​mu​−1/2−1/2+1/2+1/2−3/2−1/2−1/2+1/2+1/2+3/2​ml​−1/2+1/2−1/2+1/2−1/2−1/2+1/2−1/2+1/2+1/2​δEγ​/(μB​B)+2/3−4/3+4/3−2/3−1+1/3−5/3+5/3−1/3+1​​
(9)
These are four components of D1 and six of D2. Their strengths need not be equal; the table and arrows specify allowed transitions, not intensities.
The 3p2P3/2​ level lies above 3p2P1/2​, so D2 has the larger zero-field photon energy. [ref] In the stated weak-field regime its most positive shift is 5μB​B/3. Since λ=hc/Eγ​, the shortest wavelength is therefore produced by
3p2P3/2​, mJ​=+21​ ⟶ 3s2S1/2​, mJ​=−21​.​
(10)
The tempting mu​=3/2→ml​=−1/2 pair has a larger difference of level shifts but is forbidden because ∣mu​−ml​∣=2.

For the quantum harmonic oscillator, the canonical commutation relation [x,p]=iℏ gives [a,a†]=1 and H0​=ℏω0​(a†a+1/2). Inverting the definition of the annihilation operator gives
x=2mω0​ℏ​​(a+a†).
(11)
With normalized energy eigenstates and consistent phases, a∣0⟩=0 and a†∣0⟩=∣1⟩. Their orthogonality therefore gives
∣⟨1∣x∣0⟩∣=2mω0​ℏ​​≡x01​.​
(12)
More generally, ⟨n∣x∣0⟩=x01​δn1​, the oscillator electric-dipole selection rule out of the ground state.
To derive the transition amplitude, insert the expansion in stationary energy eigenstates into the Schrödinger equation with H=H0​+V1​(t). The derivatives of the explicit phases cancel the H0​ terms. Projection onto ⟨n∣ leaves the exact coefficient equations
iℏc˙n​(t)=∑r​ei(En​−Er​)t/ℏ⟨n∣V1​(t)∣r⟩cr​(t).
(13)
The initial conditions are c0​(−∞)=1 and cn=0​(−∞)=0. To first order in the perturbation, replace the amplitudes on the right by these initial values. Since En​−E0​=nℏω0​, integration gives time-dependent perturbation theory in the form
cn(1)​(t)=iℏ1​∫−∞t​einω0​t′⟨n∣V1​(t′)∣0⟩dt′(n=0).​
(14)
This neglects depletion and higher-order transitions; it is justified when the total transition probability is small. Here ⟨0∣x∣0⟩=0, so there is also no first-order diagonal amplitude change from this particular perturbation.
For the exponential electric field, the electric-dipole interaction is V1​=−qExet/τ, with τ>0. Only n=1 couples directly to the initial state, and
c1(1)​(0)=ℏiqEx01​​∫−∞0​e(1/τ+iω0​)t′dt′.
(15)
The Born rule gives
p1​(0)≃2ℏmω0​q2E2​​∫−∞0​eiω0​t′et′/τdt′​2.​
(16)
The real part 1/τ>0 makes the lower-limit contribution vanish. Thus
∫−∞0​e(1/τ+iω0​)t′dt′=1/τ+iω0​1​,p1​(0)≃2ℏmω0​[1+ω02​τ2]q2E2τ2​.​
(17)
Set u=ω0​τ and η=qEx01​/(ℏω0​). The dimensionless form is p1​/η2=u2/(1+u2), increasing monotonically from zero to one, with half the asymptotic value at u=1. A sufficient weak-field condition for the entire curve is ∣η∣≪1.
For ω0​τ≪1, the force has acted for too little time to appreciably change the state at the instant of measurement. The short-time amplitude is approximately iqEx01​τ/ℏ, so
p1​(0)≃2ℏmω0​q2E2τ2​(ω0​τ≪1).​
(18)
For ω0​τ≫1, instead,
p1​(0)⟶2ℏmω03​q2E2​=η2.​
(19)
The nonzero slow-switch limit is a probability in the original H0​ basis. It does not contradict the adiabatic theorem. At t=0 the instantaneous oscillator is displaced by d=qE/(mω02​):
H(0)=2mp2​+21​mω02​(x−d)2−2mω02​q2E2​.
(20)
Its ground state is ∣0E​⟩=∣0⟩+η∣1⟩+O(η2) in the original basis. Adiabatic following therefore predicts exactly the finite original-basis limit found above.
The exponential switch-on of a driven quantum oscillator also permits an exact check. A linear force preserves a coherent state, with original-basis displacement α(0)=iηu/(1+iu). Thus the exact original-basis probability is ∣α∣2e−∣α∣2, whose weak-field limit is the expression above. In the instantaneous displaced basis the mean excitation is instead ∣α−η∣2=η2/(1+u2) and does tend to zero for slow switching. The two bases measure different notions of excitation.

Take a normalized spin one-half state in the Sz​ eigenbasis. Since S=(ℏ/2)σ, direct multiplication of the Pauli matrices gives
⟨Sx​⟩=2ℏ​(α∗β+β∗α),⟨Sy​⟩=2ℏ​(−iα∗β+iβ∗α),⟨Sz​⟩=2ℏ​(∣α∣2−∣β∣2).
(21)
Substitution of the half-angle parametrization gives
⟨S⟩=2ℏ​(sinθcosϕ,sinθsinϕ,cosθ).​
(22)
Thus θ and ϕ are precisely the polar and azimuthal angles of the mean spin. Two complex coefficients contain four real parameters; normalization removes one and an unobservable common quantum phase removes another. The resulting pure states form the Bloch sphere. One may choose α real and nonnegative away from the patch where α=0. At the poles the azimuth is redundant. The length ∣⟨S⟩∣=ℏ/2 should not be confused with ⟨S2⟩​=3​ℏ/2.
For a spatial plane wave, −iℏ∇ becomes ℏk, so the Hamiltonian operator reduces to the Weyl Hamiltonian matrix
H(k)=ℏvσ⋅k.
(23)
Define n=(sinθcosϕ,sinθsinϕ,cosθ) and χ+​=(cos(θ/2),eiϕsin(θ/2))T. The matrix
σ⋅n=(cosθeiϕsinθ​e−iϕsinθ−cosθ​)
(24)
satisfies (σ⋅n)χ+​=χ+​ by the angle-addition identities. Consequently the displayed spinor is an energy eigenstate for either k=+kn or k=−kn, with respective energies +ℏvk and −ℏvk, for k≥0. Equivalently, at each fixed nonzero wavevector the two branches are
E±​(k)=±ℏv∣k∣(v>0).​
(25)
If v<0, the two energy labels exchange; the ordered eigenvalues are ±ℏ∣v∣∣k∣. Taking v>0 sets the convention in the sketches. An orthogonal spinor χ−​=(−e−iϕsin(θ/2),cos(θ/2))T obeys (σ⋅n)χ−​=−χ−​ and supplies the other branch at +kn.
The Pauli matrix multiplication law independently gives H(k)2=(ℏv)2∣k∣2I. The dispersion relation is therefore a pair of cones touching at zero energy and momentum; a signed straight-line cut has upper and lower branches ±ℏv∣kx​∣. In the kz​=0 plane, θ=π/2, and the upper-branch mean spin points radially outwards, the lower-branch spin radially inwards. This is spin-momentum locking, not a tangential spin texture. At the touching point the two states are degenerate and the spin direction is not selected by the Hamiltonian operator.
Now let a spatially constant perturbation have matrix V=(uw∗​wz​), with u,z real. The Hermitian two-by-two matrix in the Pauli basis decomposition follows explicitly from
(uw∗​wz​)=dI+aσx​+bσy​+cσz​=(d+ca+ib​a−ibd−c​),
(26)
where d=(u+z)/2, c=(u−z)/2, a=Rew, and b=−Imw are real. Equivalently, d=trV/2 and (a,b,c)i​=tr(Vσi​)/2. The four matrices are linearly independent under the trace inner product and span the four-dimensional real vector space of Hermitian two-by-two matrices.
Spatial constancy means the perturbation does not mix different wavevectors, so each momentum sector can still be diagonalized independently. Write b=(a,b,c). The full matrix and its exact eigenvalues are
H′(k)=dI+(ℏvk+b)⋅σ,E±​(k)=d±(ℏvkx​+a)2+(ℏvky​+b)2+(ℏvkz​+c)2​.​
(27)
This constant matrix perturbation of a Weyl Hamiltonian translates the cone to
k∗​=−ℏv(a,b,c)​,E∗​=d.​
(28)
In three-dimensional momentum space the energy bands still touch; the perturbation produces no full spectral gap. The new branch spin directions are parallel and antiparallel to ℏvk+b, so their radial texture is centered on the shifted point.
A restricted cut must be distinguished from the full dispersion relation. In the fixed plane kz​=0, the energies are d±(ℏvkx​+a)2+(ℏvky​+b)2+c2​, with minimum branch separation 2∣c∣. This plane misses the crossing if c=0. Along any line through k∗​ the dispersion relation still crosses linearly. Thus a sketch of a gapped fixed-plane cut alone would not describe the gapless three-dimensional Hamiltonian operator. The cone after a general perturbation is also no longer a function of ∣k∣ about the old origin alone.

For physical spin ladder operators, Sj±​=Sjx​±iSjy​. Operators at distinct sites commute. Expanding the two products cancels their cross terms and gives
21​(Sj+​Sj+1−​+Sj−​Sj+1+​)=Sjx​Sj+1x​+Sjy​Sj+1y​.
(29)
Adding the remaining component proves
Sj​⋅Sj+1​=21​(Sj+​Sj+1−​+Sj−​Sj+1+​)+Sjz​Sj+1z​.​
(30)
For spin one-half, S+∣↓⟩=ℏ∣↑⟩, S−∣↑⟩=ℏ∣↓⟩, and raising an up spin or lowering a down spin gives zero. These formulas restore the physical ℏ factors implicit in the dimensionless ladder hint.
Let ∣n⟩ abbreviate the state with the only down spin at site n, keeping ∣ψ0​⟩ for the fully up state. If n lies on neither end of bond (j,j+1), that bond contains two up spins. The ladder terms vanish, while Sjz​Sj+1z​ gives ℏ2/4. Thus
Sj​⋅Sj+1​∣n⟩=4ℏ2​∣n⟩(n=j,j+1).
(31)
For a down spin at j, the z product is instead −ℏ2/4, and Sj+​Sj+1−​ moves it to j+1 with amplitude ℏ2. For a down spin at j+1, the other ladder product moves it back. Therefore
Sj​⋅Sj+1​∣j⟩=4ℏ2​(2∣j+1⟩−∣j⟩),Sj​⋅Sj+1​∣j+1⟩=4ℏ2​(2∣j⟩−∣j+1⟩).​
(32)
Every bond in ∣ψ0​⟩ is aligned, so summing its N bond energies gives
H∣ψ0​⟩=E0​∣ψ0​⟩,E0​=−4NJ​.​
(33)
This is indeed a ground-state energy: a pair of spin-one-half particles has Sj​⋅Sj+1​ eigenvalues ℏ2/4 and −3ℏ2/4, so each ferromagnetic bond is bounded below by −J/4, simultaneously attained by the fully aligned state. The isotropic Heisenberg model has a whole maximum-total-spin ground multiplet, not a unique all-up ground state.
In the single-excitation subspace, only the two bonds adjacent to the down spin differ from the aligned background. Their diagonal energy increase is J, and each neighboring hop has amplitude −J/2. Hence, with periodic site indices,
H∣n⟩=(E0​+J)∣n⟩−2J​(∣n−1⟩+∣n+1⟩).​
(34)
This is an exact finite-dimensional hopping problem. Choose the active one-site translation convention T∣n⟩=∣n+1⟩. A periodic plane-wave coefficient pattern requires eiq(n+N)a=eiqna, so
q=Na2πℓ​,ℓ∈Z,q identified modulo a2π​.​
(35)
There are N independent such wavevectors, for example one set of N consecutive integers ℓ in a Brillouin zone. The normalized one-magnon Bloch states on a ring are ∣q⟩=N−1/2∑n=1N​eiqna∣n⟩. Reindexing with the periodic coefficient condition gives T∣q⟩=e−iqa∣q⟩ and hence TN∣q⟩=∣q⟩.
There is a logical qualification to the condition stated in the paper. On an N-site ring TN=I already holds for every state. In particular, the finite sum written with an arbitrary real q also returns to itself after N translations; that condition alone does not quantize q. The displayed allowed values are the ones for which the ansatz is a single one-site translation eigenstate, equivalently a consistently periodic plane wave. This is the additional condition needed for the subsequent energy-eigenstate argument. Other q values generally describe superpositions of the allowed momentum modes.
Apply the hopping Hamiltonian operator and shift the summation index using periodic boundary condition. The coefficients from ∣n−1⟩ and ∣n+1⟩ are respectively eiqa and e−iqa times the original coefficient. Thus
H∣q⟩=(E0​+J−2J​eiqa−2J​e−iqa)∣q⟩,
(36)
and the nearest-neighbour ferromagnetic magnon dispersion is
E(q)=E0​+J[1−cos(qa)]=E0​+2Jsin22qa​.​
(37)
The unnormalized state in the question has the same energy. Distinct allowed momenta are orthogonal, since N−1∑n​ei(q−q′)na=δqq′​ over one set of representatives.
For large N the sampled wavevectors become dense, and the requested sketch is the periodic curve [E(q)−E0​]/J=1−cos(qa) over −2π<qa<2π. It has a zero at q=0 and tends to the equivalent zero at either open endpoint; maxima 2J occur at q=±π/a. Near any equivalent zero,
E(q)−E0​=2Ja2q2​+O(q4),​
(38)
using q measured from that zero. The low-momentum ferromagnetic magnon is quadratic, with group velocity ℏ−1dE/dq=(Ja/ℏ)sin(qa). The q=0 state is proportional to the total spin lowering operator acting on ∣ψ0​⟩, so it belongs to the same ground multiplet and has exactly zero excitation energy. Extending the plot beyond one reciprocal period repeats states rather than adding new independent modes. The lattice spacing a is the nearest-neighbor chord length specified in the paper; Na is the translation length in this lattice description, not necessarily the continuous circumference of a circular arrangement.
