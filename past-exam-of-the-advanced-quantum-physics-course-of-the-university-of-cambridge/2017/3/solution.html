The Zeeman effect comes from −μ⋅B. For an electron, the orbital and spin magnetic moments are approximately −μB​L/ℏ and −2μB​S/ℏ, respectively. This explains both the sign and the relative factor two in the term linear in B. The second term is the spin-orbit interaction: the electron's motion through the atomic electric field produces a magnetic interaction in its rest frame. The relativistic correction, including Thomas precession, and the radial expectation value are incorporated into K. This effective Hamiltonian retains the linear Zeeman splitting and spin-orbit coupling, while omitting diamagnetic and hyperfine structure corrections.
Define the angular momentum ladder operators by L±​=Lx​±iLy​ and similarly for S±​. Direct expansion gives
21​(L+​S−​+L−​S+​)=Lx​Sx​+Ly​Sy​,
(28)
so adding Lz​Sz​ gives the required scalar product. The off-diagonal terms change mℓ​ and ms​ in opposite directions. Consequently Jz​=Lz​+Sz​ commutes with H, and mj​ remains a good quantum number at every magnetic field.
In the given product eigenbasis, the diagonal matrix elements are
⟨mℓ​,ms​∣H∣mℓ​,ms​⟩=μB​B(mℓ​+2ms​)+2Kmℓ​ms​.
(29)
For ℓ=1 and s=1/2, the only off-diagonal couplings are between (1,−1/2) and (0,1/2), and between (0,−1/2) and (−1,1/2). Each relevant orbital ladder factor is ℏ2​ and each spin ladder factor is ℏ. Multiplying by K/ℏ2 gives 2​K. Therefore
a=μB​B,b=K,c=2​K​.
(30)
All other off-diagonal entries vanish either by the ladder endpoints or by conservation of mj​. This derives every entry of the displayed matrix.
The stretched states have no mixing and yield
Emj​=3/2​=K+2a,Emj​=−3/2​=K−2a​.
(31)
The mj​=1/2 block is (−K2​K​2​Ka​), while the mj​=−1/2 block is (−a2​K​2​K−K​). Taking the trace and determinant of each 2×2 block gives the remaining four energy eigenvalues:
E+(±)​=2a−K​±21​(a+K)2+8K2​​,
(32)
E−(±)​=2−a−K​±21​(a−K)2+8K2​​.
(33)
The subscripts indicate mj​=±1/2 and the superscripts indicate the upper or lower root. These formulas are the Zeeman spectrum for orbital angular momentum one and remain valid for either sign of K.
For the usual ordering K>0, including sodium, the zero-field states follow from
L⋅S=21​(J2−L2−S2),Ej​=K[j(j+1)−ℓ(ℓ+1)−s(s+1)].
(34)
The j=3/2 quartet lies at K and the j=1/2 doublet at −2K, separated by 3K. In a weak magnetic field, the Landé g-factors give
E3/2,mj​​=K+34​amj​+O(a2/K),E1/2,mj​​=−2K+32​amj​+O(a2/K).
(35)
These label the curves near B=0. In a strong magnetic field, the Paschen-Back effect makes (mℓ​,ms​) the appropriate approximate labels:
Emℓ​,ms​​=a(mℓ​+2ms​)+2Kmℓ​ms​+O(K2/a)
(36)
for the mixed states, with the stretched-state formulas already exact. The branch connections for B>0 are
zero-field label (j,mj​)(3/2,3/2)(3/2,1/2)(1/2,1/2)(3/2,−1/2)(1/2,−1/2)(3/2,−3/2)​strong-field label (mℓ​,ms​)(1,1/2)(0,1/2)(1,−1/2)(−1,1/2)(0,−1/2)(−1,−1/2)​​
(37)
Within a fixed mj​, the nonzero coupling prevents a crossing; this is an avoided crossing. Curves with different mj​ may cross because the Hamiltonian does not couple them.
For the sodium estimate, the zero-field frequency separation fixes
3K=hδf,K=1.14×10−22 J.
(38)
The lower 3s doublet has shifts ±a. The Sodium D-line Zeeman pattern gives photon-frequency shifts a/h times {±2/3,±4/3} for D1​ and {±1/3,±1,±5/3} for D2​. Thus the highest-frequency D1​ component and lowest-frequency D2​ component approach each other by 3a/h. Extrapolating the weak-field Zeeman splitting gives
Bcross​∼3μB​hδf​=12.3 T​,
(39)
so the crossover occurs at a field of order 10 T, where a∼K and the weak-field approximation is becoming marginal.
The exact eigenvalues refine the estimate within this Hamiltonian. The relevant photon energies, with a common zero-field reference omitted, are E+(−)​+a and E−(+)​−a. The corresponding electric dipole transitions have Δmj​=+1 and −1, respectively. Equating them gives
6a=(a+K)2+8K2​+(a−K)2+8K2​.
(40)
Its positive solution is a/K=10​/3, hence
Bcross​=9μB​10​hδf​≃13.0 T​
(41)
in the retained model. The crossing is between optical transition frequencies; the participating upper states themselves need not be degenerate.
