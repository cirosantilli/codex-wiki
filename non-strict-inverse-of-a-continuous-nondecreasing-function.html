Non-strict inverse of a continuous nondecreasing function (h(a)=inf{t:f(t)≥a}=G(a−))

 ... Mathematics Area of mathematics Analysis Real analysis Calculus Monotonic function
OurBigBook.com Words: 91
For a continuous unbounded nondecreasing function f on the nonnegative half-line and a>f(0), the non-strict inverse h is left-continuous in its level argument. It equals the left limit of the strict generalized inverse of a nondecreasing function G(a)=inf{t:f(t)>a}. Indeed G(b)≤h(a) for b<a, while for every t<h(a), f(t)<a and choosing b∈(f(t),a) gives G(b)≥t. Similarly h(a+)=G(a). A flat part of f at level a gives a discrepancy between the two inverses: h(a) selects the beginning of the flat part and G(a) its end. For a Brownian running maximum, these are the non-strict and strict passage times.
