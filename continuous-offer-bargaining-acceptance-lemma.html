Continuous-offer bargaining acceptance lemma

 ... Mathematics Area of mathematics Mathematical optimization Game theory Extensive-form game Finite-horizon alternating-offer bargaining
OurBigBook.com Words: 163
Let a proposer divide a pie of size M>0 by continuously offering its responder q∈[0,M]. Rejection gives fixed continuation payoffs b to the proposer and c to the responder, with 0≤c<M and b<M−c. Then a subgame perfect equilibrium must have the responder accept q=c with probability one. The proposer offers exactly c, retaining M−c.
Sequential rationality forces rejection for q<c and acceptance for q>c. If acceptance at c had probability a<1, that offer would give the proposer a(M−c)+(1−a)b<M−c. Every larger offer gives less than M−c and can be improved by a smaller offer still above c; every smaller offer gives b<M−c. The supremum M−c would therefore be unattained. Randomizing offers cannot attain it either: the nonnegative gap from M−c would be strictly positive almost surely and hence have positive expectation. No best response would exist, contradicting equilibrium. Acceptance at c removes this gap and makes q=c the unique optimal proposal. The assumptions c<M and b<M−c exclude degenerate indifference of the proposer.
