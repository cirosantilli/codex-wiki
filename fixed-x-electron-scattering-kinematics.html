Fixed-x electron scattering kinematics (E′=E−Q2/(2Mx))

 ... Branches of physics Quantum field theory Standard Model Quantum chromodynamics Deep inelastic scattering SLAC-MIT deep inelastic electron scattering experiment
OurBigBook.com Words: 182
Work in natural units with c=1, neglect the electron mass, and put the initial proton at rest with mass M. Let k and k′ be the initial and final electron four-momenta, q=k−k′, and Q2=−q2>0. Define the laboratory energy transfer νlab​=E−E′=P⋅q/M. This νlab​ has units of energy; the invariant P⋅q has units of energy squared. The Bjorken scaling variable and the final hadron invariant mass obey
Q2=4EE′sin2(θ/2),x=2Mνlab​Q2​,W2=(P+q)2=M2+Q2(x1​−1).
(19)
These follow by squaring k−k′ and P+q, using k2=k′2=0 and P=(M,0). Solving the Bjorken x definition gives the outgoing energy and then the scattering angle:
E′=E−2MxQ2​,sin22θ​=4E(E−Q2/(2Mx))Q2​.
(20)
Alternatively, substituting Q2=4EE′sin2(θ/2) before solving gives E′=E/[1+2Esin2(θ/2)/(Mx)]. A chosen x,E,Q2 must satisfy E′>0 and 0≤sin2(θ/2)≤1. In addition, W must exceed the lightest allowed inelastic hadron threshold; positive W2 alone is insufficient.
For an original numerical example, take M=0.938GeV, x=0.3, E=15GeV and Q2=3GeV2. Then νlab​≃5.330GeV, E′≃9.670GeV, θ≃8.25∘, and W≃2.807GeV. Varying 2≤Q2/GeV2≤5 and 10≤E/GeV≤20 at this same Bjorken x keeps every setting physical and gives W2>5.54GeV2, above the proton-pion threshold and the stricter chosen continuum selection W>2GeV. These are calculated settings, not historical measurements.
