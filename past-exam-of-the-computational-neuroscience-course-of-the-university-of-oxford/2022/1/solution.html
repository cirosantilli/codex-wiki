A two-layer heteroassociative memory can retrieve one activity pattern from a different associated cue through learned feedforward synapses. Let xμ∈Rn represent a cue and tμ∈Rm its associate. An input population projects to an output population through W, with retrieval y=ϕ(Wx−θ). The output activation function can be linear for an analysis or thresholded with synaptic competition for a sparse categorical response. This is pattern association: the output need not reproduce the input, unlike an autoassociative memory.
During storage, the cue and its desired associate must be coactive. A local Hebbian learning rule, ΔWij​=ηti​xj​, strengthens the connection from an active cue neuron to an active target neuron. With normalized inputs, the idealized batch weights are
W=∑μ=1P​tμ(xμ)T.

Presenting cue xν gives
Wxν=tν∥xν∥2+∑μ=ν​tμ((xμ)Txν).
(2)
The first term is the desired association; the second is interference from overlapping cues. Thus retrieval follows exactly for orthonormal cue patterns, while sparse coding, centring, synaptic normalization and a competitive output can reduce interference for realistic overlapping inputs. These devices have distinct roles: synaptic normalization prevents unlimited weight growth, and synaptic competition separates competing associates. Signed, centred model weights should not be identified literally with negative excitatory synapses; biological inhibition and baseline subtraction need an explicit circuit implementation.

A cue (0.8,0.1)T produces (0.8,0.8,0.1)T, so threshold 0.5 retrieves the first associate. These are chosen model values, not recorded firing rates. If two genuinely different associates share an identical cue, a deterministic feedforward map cannot choose between them without additional context; adding capacity alone does not solve that ambiguity. A cue-to-associate map also need not provide the reverse association unless the reverse pathway is separately learned.
The hippocampus provides a plausible anatomical setting. In a hippocampal episodic memory model, a retrieved hippocampal CA3 event pattern can project through the hippocampal CA1 output pathway to reinstate associated cortical content. The feedforward CA3-to-CA1 and hippocampal-to-cortical links can provide heteroassociation, while recurrent CA3 connectivity provides autoassociation. The entorhinal cortex supplies converging information and routes cortical communication. This is a computational assignment to pathways; anatomical connectivity alone does not prove that their weights implement the displayed rule.
An especially direct physiological test comes from trained visual pairs. Monkeys learned arbitrary associations between computer-generated pictures and then selected the associated picture after a delay. Extracellular single-unit recording in anterior temporal cortex found both neurons responding to the two members of a learned pair and neurons whose delay activity anticipated the appropriate associate. The preparation therefore links an experimentally controlled cue–associate relation to neural responses, rather than merely showing visual selectivity. However, the recordings do not identify the individual modified synapses, establish one-way connectivity, or exclude recurrent retrieval. They support an associative representation in temporal cortex, not a unique proof of a two-layer implementation.[ref]
The rapid-learning requirement is relevant to the hippocampal implementation: CA3-restricted NMDA receptor deletion impaired learning of a newly located hidden platform in a delayed matching-to-place task while sparing performance with previously experienced locations. This supports rapid hippocampal storage, but does not by itself identify a specific feedforward association matrix.[ref]
A decisive model test would measure cue and associate representations before learning, after learning, and during partial-cue retrieval, while selectively disrupting the proposed connecting pathway. Correct associate decoding with unrelated-cue and familiar-visual-response controls would be stronger evidence than activity during successful recall alone. The architecture is a plausible component of temporal and hippocampal memory circuits; its local learning rule, interference properties and pathway-specific causal predictions make it testable.
