Neutral buoyancy (ρbody​=ρf​)

 Home Physics Branches of physics Fluid mechanics Buoyancy
OurBigBook.com Words: 123
Neutral buoyancy means a fully immersed body's upward buoyancy equals its downward force from gravity. For a body of fixed volume in a constant-mass density fluid under a uniform gravitational field, this occurs when its mean mass density equals the surrounding mass density. A 0.10 m cube of mass density 1000 kgm−3 in the stipulated water has mass 1.000 kg, so both forces are 9.81 N.
Ideally it can remain at rest at any depth for which it stays fully immersed and clear of boundaries. Raising or lowering it changes the absolute pressure, but neither its displaced volume nor the difference between the vertical pressure forces. There is therefore no vertical restoring force toward a preferred depth in this model. Neutral buoyancy is a vertical force balance, not a claim about resistance to tilting or about a compressible body whose volume changes with pressure.
