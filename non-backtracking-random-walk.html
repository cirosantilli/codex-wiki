Non-backtracking random walk

 ... Probability and statistics Probability theory Markov process Markov chain Random walk Random walk on a graph
OurBigBook.com Words: 62
A non-backtracking random walk remembers its preceding vertex and excludes that neighbor from the next choice. At vertices of degree at least two, it is a Markov chain on directed edges: (a,b) moves to (b,c) with probability 1/(degb−1) for each neighbor c=a. Dead ends require an additional convention. An initial vertex without a preceding edge uses the ordinary first-step neighbor distribution.
