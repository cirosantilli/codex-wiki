Arithmetic

 Home Mathematics Area of mathematics
OurBigBook.com Words: 19k Articles: 332
Arithmetic studies operations on numbers: combining quantities by addition, finding differences by subtraction, scaling through multiplication, and reversing a nonzero scale through division. Its usefulness goes beyond producing an answer. A calculation can express how resources are shared, how a measurement changes with scale, or why two different descriptions of a quantity agree.
The allowed numbers matter. Start with the natural numbers 0,1,2,…. Counting collections gives 2+3=5, but removing three from two calls for the integer −1. Sharing three units equally between two recipients gives the rational number 3/2. These operations motivate larger number systems: integers supply additive inverses, and rational numbers supply division by nonzero integers. A real number can also express lengths such as a diagonal of Euclidean norm 2​, which is not a rational number.
The beauty is that bookkeeping, geometry, and algebra can check one another. The same product 3(2+4)=18 counts three groups of six, measures the area of a rectangle, and splits as 3⋅2+3⋅4 by distributivity. A number line makes negative integers visible as positions; a fraction records an exact share even when its decimal representation does not terminate. An arithmetic rule becomes easier to remember when these interpretations fit together.
