Stationary inflection point (f′(a)=0)

 ... Mathematics Area of mathematics Geometry and topology Topology Curve Inflection point
OurBigBook.com Words: 85
An inflection point of the graph of a differentiable real function at which its derivative is zero. It has a horizontal tangent and a change of concavity, but need not be a local maximum or minimum. For a three-times continuously differentiable function, f′(a)=f′′(a)=0 and f′′′(a)=0 suffice: the expansion f′′(a+h)=f′′′(a)h+o(h) gives opposite signs on the two sides. The elementary example is f(x)=x3 at zero. The word “saddle” is sometimes used for this one-dimensional situation in elementary examination papers, but the usual saddle point of a scalar function has a multivariable meaning; do not merge the two concepts.
