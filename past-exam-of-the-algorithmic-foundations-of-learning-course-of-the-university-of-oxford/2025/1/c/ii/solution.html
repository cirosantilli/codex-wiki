We give a finite chaining argument, keeping the constants explicit. If δ=0, the metric space has just the point t0​ and both sides vanish. Suppose δ>0, and write
εk​=2−kδ,Nk​=N(εk​;T,d),k≥1.
(12)
Choose a smallest metric net Tk​⊆T of radius εk​, and maps πk​:T→Tk​ satisfying d(t,πk​(t))≤εk​. Set T0​={t0​} and π0​(t)=t0​. Since T is finite, choose K with εK​ smaller than the minimum distance between distinct points; then TK​=T and πK​(t)=t.
For each k, retain only the pairs actually occurring along these approximation paths:
Ek​={(πk​(t),πk−1​(t)):t∈T},mk​=∣Ek​∣.
(13)
The triangle inequality gives d(u,v)≤εk​+εk−1​=3εk​ for (u,v)∈Ek​; for k=1 the same argument uses d(t,t0​)≤δ=ε0​. Also
m1​≤N1​,mk​≤Nk​Nk−1​≤Nk2​ (k≥2),logmk​≤2logNk​.
(14)
Here only monotonicity in the radius for the same space T is used. No monotonicity under subsets is assumed.
For every pair in Ek​, the increment has the Sub-Gamma random variable in the right tail bound with common parameters 9νεk2​ and 3cεk​: for 0<s<(3cεk​)−1,
logEes(Xu​−Xv​)≤2(1−cd(u,v)s)νd(u,v)2s2​≤2(1−3cεk​s)9νεk2​s2​.
(15)
Identical pairs give the zero increment, which obeys the same bound. Both orientations have finite exponential moments, so all increments are integrable random variables. Differentiating their two upper bounds at zero also gives E(Xu​−Xv​)=0. Applying the maximum bound from part (b), including its mk​=1 case, gives
E(u,v)∈Ek​max​(Xu​−Xv​)​≤3εk​2νlogmk​​+3cεk​logmk​≤6ν​εk​logNk​​+6cεk​logNk​.​
(16)
The telescoping sum along each path is exact:
Xt​−Xt0​​=∑k=1K​(Xπk​(t)​−Xπk−1​(t)​).
(17)
Take the supremum, bound it by the sum of the maxima, and take expected values. This proves
Esupt∈T​(Xt​−Xt0​​)≤6ν​∑k=1K​εk​logNk​​+6c∑k=1K​εk​logNk​.
(18)
For either f(u)=logN(u;T,d)​ or f(u)=logN(u;T,d), the function is nonnegative and nonincreasing. Thus
∫εk+1​εk​​f(u)du≥2εk​​f(εk​),∑k=1K​εk​f(εk​)≤2∫0δ/2​f(u)du.
(19)
Combining the estimates yields the required metric entropy bound:
E[t∈Tsup​Xt​−Xt0​​]≤12ν​∫0δ/2​logN(u;T,d)​du+12c∫0δ/2​logN(u;T,d)du.​
(20)
In particular, C=12 and C′=12 work. The square-root and linear metric entropy terms arise respectively from the two terms in the sub-Gamma maximum bound. Because the chain ends exactly at T, no limiting sample-path regularity assumption is needed.
