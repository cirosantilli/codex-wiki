Heat flux density (q)

 Home Physics Branches of physics Statistical physics Thermodynamics Heat
OurBigBook.com Words: 88
Heat flux density is a vector q describing the rate of heat transfer per unit area, measured in Wm−2. Through a planar face of area A with uniform normal component q, the signed rate is Q˙​=Aq in W; positive q means heat travels in the chosen positive normal direction. Fourier's law determines the conductive contribution. In a bar with a constant cross-sectional area, steady thermal conduction without internal sources or side losses makes both q and Q˙​ constant along the bar. Constant Q˙​ need not mean constant q when the cross-sectional area varies.
