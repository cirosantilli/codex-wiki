Powers of the identity plus a square-zero matrix ((I+N)k=I+kN,N2=0,k∈Z)

 ... Area of mathematics Algebra Linear algebra Operator theory Nilpotent linear map Nilpotent matrix
OurBigBook.com Words: 65
The binomial theorem for the commuting matrices I,N proves the formula for nonnegative integers: every term containing N2 vanishes. Since (I+N)(I−N)=I, the matrix inverse is I−N; applying the same argument to its powers gives the negative integers as well. The same identity shows that I+N is invertible even when the nilpotent matrix N is nonzero. In dimension two, the example N=(00​p0​) describes repeated shear mappings, whose displacement parameters add under composition.
