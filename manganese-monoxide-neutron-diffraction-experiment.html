Manganese monoxide neutron diffraction experiment

 ... Physics Branches of physics Electromagnetism Magnetism Magnetic order Magnetic neutron diffraction
OurBigBook.com Words: 368
In 1949, Clifford Shull and J. Samuel Smart compared powdered manganese(II) oxide at room temperature and 80K. Powder preparation supplies many crystal orientations. Their Fig. 1 records scattered-neutron intensity versus counter angle. Nuclear reflections remained, while the cold pattern gained strong reflections forbidden by the chemical unit cell. They indexed these with a magnetic unit cell having twice the chemical cell edge. The warm pattern already contained diffuse magnetic scattering: loss of sharp magnetic peaks does not mean loss of individual magnetic moments.[ref]
The source was Oak Ridge's graphite-moderated reactor. In Early Development of Neutron Scattering (1994), Clifford Shull describes how the early two-axis instruments used a monochromating crystal to select the incident-neutron wavelength; a shielded neutron counter rotated around the specimen to record angular intensity. This specifies the source–monochromator–sample–counter path, without identifying which instrument revision produced the MnO trace.[ref]
The control was the same compound above and below magnetic ordering, with retained nuclear peaks identifying the underlying chemical periodicity. The original paper labels an aluminium impurity peak and defers a complete magnetic-structure account. It supplies neither a chemical synthesis recipe nor detailed cold-stage construction. The inference is periodic magnetic order, rather than a claim that every possible structural change was excluded at arbitrary resolution.[ref]

The actual MnO indexing is three-dimensional; the toy row does not assert its reflection positions or relative peak strengths.
Louis Néel supplied the ordering theory; the 1949 diffraction work was a later experimental test. His 1970 Nobel Prize in Physics recognized antiferromagnetism and ferrimagnetism. Clifford Shull's neutron-diffraction award followed in 1994.[ref]
