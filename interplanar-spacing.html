Interplanar spacing (d)

 Home Chemistry Physical chemistry Crystal structure Lattice plane
OurBigBook.com Words: 202
The interplanar spacing d is the perpendicular distance between parallel planes in a specified family. Measuring along an oblique line gives a larger distance, so the direction of measurement matters.
There is a short reciprocal lattice derivation. With the angular convention ai​⋅bj​=2πδij​, put G=hb1​+kb2​+lb3​. Then G⋅r=2πm is the same plane equation as hx+ky+lz=m. Thus G is a normal vector. Moving between consecutive sheets along this normal changes the dot product by 2π, giving
∣G∣d=2π,d=∣G∣2π​.
(7)
For perpendicular cubic axes of length a, this becomes
G=a2π​(h,k,l),dhkl​=h2+k2+l2​a​.
(8)
For example, choose a primitive cubic lattice with a=0.600nm. Its (210) lattice planes have d210​=0.600/5​≃0.268328nm. This is a chosen geometry, not an experimental value. The distance from a point to a plane gives the same result: the planes 2X+Y=0 and 2X+Y=a in ordinary Cartesian length coordinates are separated by a/5​.
Specify whether dhkl​ denotes spacing between successive occupied lattice planes or the formal spacing assigned to a reflection label. In a primitive cubic lattice, (200) has formal spacing a/2 but every other sheet contains no lattice points; its reflection is the second diffraction order of (100). In a face-centered cubic lattice with conventional cubic axes, the (200) sheets at half-integer X/a do contain lattice points. Here a/2 is the actual spacing of the complete family perpendicular to that cube edge. The Primitive cubic Bragg peak positions example explains how to avoid counting a reflection twice.
