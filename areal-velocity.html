Areal velocity (A˙)

 Home Physics Branches of physics Classical mechanics Momentum Angular momentum
OurBigBook.com Words: 1k Articles: 7
Areal velocity measures how fast a position vector from a chosen origin sweeps signed swept area. It connects a geometric picture of motion to angular momentum: a long position vector with slow transverse motion can sweep the same area per unit time as a short one with fast transverse motion. Constancy requires a dynamical condition, given by the torque criterion for constant areal velocity.
Choose a fixed origin O in an inertial frame and a fixed plane through O, with constant unit vector n normal to it. For a differentiable trajectory in that plane, let r(t) be position relative to O and v=r˙ its velocity. Positive swept area follows the right-hand rule about n.
