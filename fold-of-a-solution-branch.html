Fold of a solution branch (F=0,Fx​=0,Fλ​=0,Fxx​=0)

 Home Mathematics Area of mathematics Analysis Numerical analysis Continuation method
OurBigBook.com Words: 152
A nondegenerate fold of a solution branch in a smooth scalar equation satisfies the displayed conditions. The implicit function theorem still gives λ=λ(x) because Fλ​=0. Implicit differentiation gives λ′(x∗​)=0 and λ′′(x∗​)=−Fxx​/Fλ​=0: the parameter has a genuine local extremum on the curve.
In the cubic polynomial, Fx​=0 gives x∗​=±1/3​, and substituting into F=0 gives λ∗​=2x∗​/3. Furthermore, Fxx​=6x∗​=0. Yet ∇F=(3x2−1,1) never vanishes, so the full solution branch remains a regular level set everywhere. The obstruction belongs to the projection onto λ, not to the curve itself.
For systems, the cited continuation account uses a one-dimensional kernel of Fu​ and Fλ​ outside its image of a linear map to define a simple fold (its Definition 2.2). These conditions make [Fu​ Fλ​] have full rank and support a transverse correction. A nonzero second-order term along the kernel is additionally needed to describe a nondegenerate quadratic turning point; in the scalar example this is Fxx​=0.[ref]
