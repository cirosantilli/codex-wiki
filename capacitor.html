Capacitor

 Home Physics Branches of physics Electromagnetism
OurBigBook.com Words: 3k Articles: 27
A capacitor stores electrostatic energy by separating electric charge between two electrical conductors. In the ideal two-terminal model their electric charges are +Q and −Q, with a vacuum or insulating gap preventing direct transfer between them. Write V=ϕ+​−ϕ−​ for the terminal electric potential difference, where ϕ+​ and ϕ−​ are the electric potentials of the two electrical conductors. A linear capacitor obeys
Q=CV,
(3)
where its capacitance C depends on the geometry and permittivity, and is independent of the applied electric potential difference while these stay fixed. [ref].
For example, the parallel-plate capacitor below has capacitance C=88.54pF. At terminal electric potential difference V=10V, the electric charge magnitude on either plate is
Q=CV=(8.854×10−11)(10)C=8.854×10−10C=0.8854nC.
(4)
The total electric charge of the device is Q+(−Q)=0: charge neutrality does not imply zero capacitor energy. This capacitor stores 4.427nJ in its electric field. The schematic in parallel-plate capacitor shows the separation of electric charge; the plots in capacitor energy show how storage grows with terminal electric potential difference.
The useful distinction is between storing net electric charge and storing separated electric charge. A capacitor can release electrostatic energy into an electrical circuit; in an ideal RLC circuit, energy moves between the capacitor's electric field and the magnetic field associated with inductance. An ideal disconnected capacitor retains its separated electric charges and capacitor energy because there is no path for discharge.
