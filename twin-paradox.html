Twin paradox

 Home Physics Branches of physics Special relativity Lorentz factor Time dilation
OurBigBook.com Words: 2k Articles: 9
The twin paradox compares proper time along the different world lines of a travelling observer and a stay-at-home observer who reunite. Reciprocal inertial time dilation produces an apparent contradiction only if the traveller's change of inertial frame is ignored.
The decisive experiment is local: put two clocks together at departure, separate them, then compare their readings together at reunion. Everyone agrees on those two spacetime events and on the final difference. The twin paradox asks why two clocks can accumulate different proper times between the same spacetime events, despite reciprocal time dilation during uniform motion. Its answer is the geometry of their complete world lines, together with the relativity of simultaneity when comparing distant clocks.
