Thermal buoyancy support of topography (ΔH≃(ρs​/ρref​)αΔTL)

 Home Physics Branches of physics Geophysics Isostasy
OurBigBook.com Words: 154
Suppose a compensated vertical column contains thickness L of material warmed by ΔT. For a small anomaly, the volumetric thermal expansion coefficient gives δρ≃−ρs​αΔT. Its column mass change is δM≃−ρs​αΔTL. Define ρref​ΔH to be the additional column mass associated with the permitted uplift and interface motion in the chosen isostasy model. The mass balance ρref​ΔH+δM=0 proves the displayed scaling.
The model must specify ρref​: translating an upper column of fixed internal thickness above mantle compensation gives a mantle-density scale, while fixing an internal interface and changing only surface-layer thickness gives that surface layer's density scale. If their ratio is near one, L=7 km, ΔT=350 K and α=10−5K−1 imply only about 25 m of relief. Extending this to a whole lithosphere requires its depth-dependent temperature anomaly, not only the mean temperature anomaly of the crust. Dynamic topography and flexural stresses lie outside a simple local-column calculation.
