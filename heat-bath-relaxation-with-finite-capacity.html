Heat bath relaxation with finite capacity (τ=C1​C2​/[G(C1​+C2​)])

 ... Branches of physics Statistical physics Thermodynamics Thermal equilibrium Heat bath Finite heat bath
OurBigBook.com Words: 301
The equilibrium calculation determines where the two temperatures end, without determining how long they take. Stipulate a constant thermal conductance G>0 and the linear heat transfer law underlying Newton's law of cooling. With Q positive from body 1 to bath 2, the ordinary differential equations are
Q˙​=G(T1​−T2​),C1​T˙1​=−G(T1​−T2​),C2​T˙2​=G(T1​−T2​).
(14)
The dot denotes a derivative with respect to time. This model assumes internal equilibration is fast compared with exchange between the bodies. Adding the two energy rates gives zero; subtracting the temperature rates gives
dtd​(T1​−T2​)=−G(C1​1​+C2​1​)(T1​−T2​),τ=G(C1​+C2​)C1​C2​​.
(15)
The temperature difference has exponential decay with time constant τ. For the finite heat bath example and the stipulated G=2WK−1=2Js−1K−1, τ=45s, and solving the ordinary differential equations gives
T1​(t)=296K+54Ke−t/(45s),T2​(t)=296K−6Ke−t/(45s),
(16)
Q(t)=5400J(1−e−t/(45s)).
(17)
At one time constant, T1​≃315.865K, T2​≃293.793K and Q≃3413.451J. Equal temperatures are approached as a limit at infinite time. The finite bath causes the temperature difference to shrink faster than the fixed-bath model, because both bodies change temperature.
For an ideal heat bath with the same G, set T2​=Tb​=290K rather than evolving its temperature. Newton's law of cooling then yields
T1​(t)=290K+60Ke−t/(50s),τ∞​=C1​/G=50s.
(18)
Changing G changes these time constants, while the final temperature is fixed by the capacities and initial conditions. The dynamical model independently checks the Second law of thermodynamics:
S˙tot​=T1​−Q˙​​+T2​Q˙​​=T1​T2​G(T1​−T2​)2​≥0.
(19)
Its integral over the relaxation reproduces the endpoint entropy change. The useful separation is between capacity, which governs how much the bath temperature shifts, and thermal conductance, which governs the exchange rate. Neither alone establishes a reliable heat bath.
