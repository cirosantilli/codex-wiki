Radial flux through a truncated unit sphere (Φh​(s)=2π∫−1s​h(z)dz+πs(1−s2)h(s))

Let −1<s<1 and let Ss​ be the outward-oriented boundary of x2+y2+z2≤1, z≤s. For a continuous function h:[−1,s]→R, the vector field F=h(z)(x,y,z)T has outward flux
Φh​(s)=2π∫−1s​h(z)dz+πs(1−s2)h(s).
(68)
On the curved unit sphere, the outward unit normal is (x,y,z)T, so the normal component is h(z). A horizontal spherical band has area 2πdz, producing the first term. On the top disk, the outward unit normal is (0,0,1)T, the normal component is sh(s), and the area is π(1−s2), producing the second term.
For h=f′ with a continuous first derivative, the fundamental theorem of calculus gives
Φf′​(s)=2π(f(s)−f(−1))+πs(1−s2)f′(s).
(69)
This direct surface integral proof needs no second derivative of f. For h=1, it reduces to π(2+3s−s3); the divergence theorem identifies this with three times the enclosed volume.
