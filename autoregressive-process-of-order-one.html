Autoregressive process of order one (AR(1))

 ... Mathematics Area of mathematics Probability and statistics Time series Autoregressive moving-average model Autoregressive model
OurBigBook.com Words: 829 Articles: 11
An autoregressive process of order one obeys
Xt​−μ=ϕ(Xt−1​−μ)+εt​,
(14)
where ϕ is real and εt​ is centered white noise of variance σ2>0. The zero-mean version is Xt​=ϕXt−1​+εt​; in the affine form Xt​=c+ϕXt−1​+εt​, the stationary mean satisfies c=(1−ϕ)μ.
For ∣ϕ∣<1, its unique weakly stationary process solution on all integer times is the causal time-series representation
Xt​=μ+∑j=0∞​ϕjεt−j​.
(15)
The sum converges in the sense of mean-square convergence because ∑j≥0​ϕ2j=1/(1−ϕ2)<∞. It is an infinite moving-average representation using only present and past driving noise. Weak white noise suffices for this second-order conclusion; strong white noise also gives strict stationarity. The autocovariance of an AR(1) process is σ2ϕ∣h∣/(1−ϕ2), so its variance is σ2/(1−ϕ2) and its ACF is ϕ∣h∣. See Bartlett, lecture 5: AR(1) representation and causality.
The appeal of an AR(1) is that one coefficient expresses how a deviation persists. For 0<ϕ<1, disturbances propagate with weights 1,ϕ,ϕ2,…. For −1<ϕ<0, these weights alternate sign. With μ=10, ϕ=0.7, and Xt​=12, the recursive forecasts of an autoregressive process give 11.4 and 10.98 one and two steps ahead. If σ2=1, the stationary variance is 100/51≃1.9608, while the one-step forecast error variance is only 1. Under weak white noise these are best linear forecasts; independent driving noise also makes them conditional expectations given the observed past.
The condition ∣ϕ∣<1 describes a stationary causal solution, not every finite recursion with those coefficients. Starting from a prescribed value gives the transient in finite initialization of an AR(1) process. Nor is it a condition for all possible two-sided stationary solutions: when ∣ϕ∣>1, noncausal stationary autoregression uses future driving noise. See stationary versus causal solution of a two-sided AR(1) equation for the boundary cases.
