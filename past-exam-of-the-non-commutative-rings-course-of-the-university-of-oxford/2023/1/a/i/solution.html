A left-primitive ideal is a two-sided ideal of the form
P=AnnR​(V)={r∈R:rV=0}
(1)
for a nonzero simple module V on the left. Equivalently, the quotient ring R/P has a faithful module that is a simple module, and is therefore a primitive ring.
To prove that P is a prime ideal of a noncommutative ring, let A,B be two-sided ideals with AB⊆P. Since B is two-sided, BV is a submodule of V: for r∈R, b∈B and v∈V, one has r(bv)=(rb)v∈BV. If B⊆P, then BV=0, so simplicity gives BV=V. Consequently
AV=A(BV)=(AB)V=0,
(2)
and A⊆P. Thus AB⊆P implies A⊆P or B⊆P. Also P=R, since 1 acts as the identity element on the nonzero module V. These are precisely the required prime ideal of a noncommutative ring conditions.
