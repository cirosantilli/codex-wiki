For the submodule N⊆M, define its subspace filtration and the quotient filtration on the quotient module Q=M/N by
Nj​=N∩Mj​,Qj​=(Mj​+N)/N.
(35)
They are compatible filtrations of a module, since multiplication by Fi​R sends Nj​ into Ni+j​ and Qj​ into Qi+j​.
In each degree, inclusion and the quotient map induce
ιj​:Nj​/Nj−1​⟶Mj​/Mj−1​,πj​:Mj​/Mj−1​⟶Qj​/Qj−1​.
(36)
The first map is injective: if n∈Nj​ belongs to Mj−1​, then n∈N∩Mj−1​=Nj−1​. The second map is surjective by the definition of Qj​.
For exactness in the middle, a class m+Mj−1​ lies in kerπj​ precisely when m∈Mj​ and m+N∈Qj−1​. This means m=m′+n for m′∈Mj−1​ and n∈N. Since m,m′∈Mj​, one also has n∈Nj​, and
m+Mj−1​=n+Mj−1​∈imιj​.
(37)
Conversely, every class from Nj​ maps to zero in Qj​/Qj−1​. Taking the direct sum over all degrees gives the short exact sequence of graded modules
0⟶grN⟶grM⟶gr(M/N)⟶0.
(38)
The maps respect the grR-action because they come from module homomorphisms compatible with the filtrations of a module. Consequently
grM/grN≅gr(M/N)​
(39)
as grR-modules. This proves exactness of associated graded modules for induced filtrations.
Furthermore A=grR is a Noetherian ring, and grM is a finitely generated module. Its submodule grN and its quotient module gr(M/N) are therefore finitely generated modules. Hence the induced subspace filtration and quotient filtration are both good filtrations for the order filtration.
