Lippmann colour-recording experiment

 Home Physics Branches of physics Optics Lippmann plate
OurBigBook.com Words: 246
In his 14 December 1908 Nobel lecture, Gabriel Lippmann described a photographic plate bearing a transparent, even, extremely fine sensitive layer. A holder put Mercury against that layer as a mirror during exposure. Incident and reflected light formed a standing wave within the photographic emulsion; after photographic processing and drying, white light revealed colour by selective reflection. The reflector served during recording; the developed structure supplied the later colour selection.[ref]
The lecture's control was to project a spectrum photograph, wet it, and project it again. Its colours disappeared as the gelatin swelled, then returned during drying, red before the shorter wavelengths. This tested a spacing-dependent explanation. Lippmann also reported roughly one-minute sunlight exposures and identified portrait exposure time as a limitation.[ref]
A reversible colour change supports a structural explanation, but does not independently measure every fringe spacing or the refractive index. For a conceptual comparison, record a narrow band and compare reflected optical spectra at fixed illumination and viewing angles; an unexposed processed plate tests background. A second comparison changes only viewing geometry. A third compares the dry state before and after a reversible spacing change. These are proposed discriminating controls, not additional experiments attributed to Lippmann. Background surface reflection must be separated from the selective signal. A photograph's appearance also depends on the illuminating optical spectrum; viewing under an arbitrary lamp is not a calibrated spectral measurement.
