Short-rate market price of risk (θ=(m−rV)/s)

 ... Mathematical optimization Mathematical finance Risk-neutral measure Martingale deflator Local martingale deflator Market price of risk
OurBigBook.com Words: 78
In a one-Brownian-factor arbitrage-free market, every traded derivative with nonzero diffusion exposure s has the same excess-drift/exposure ratio. Otherwise holdings s2​ in the first and −s1​ in the second, financed in the bank account, cancel all diffusion risk while leaving a nonzero excess drift. At zero exposure, the correct statement is m−rV=θs=0, not division by zero. The short rate itself need not be a traded asset, so its physical drift does not alone fix this risk price.
