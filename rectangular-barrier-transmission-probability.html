Rectangular barrier transmission probability (T(E,a))

 ... Physics Branches of physics Quantum mechanics Potential barrier Quantum tunnelling Finite rectangular barrier matching
OurBigBook.com Words: 394 Articles: 2
For equal exterior potential energies and constant mass, the exact transmission probability is
T(E,a)=⎩⎨⎧​[1+4E(V0​−E)V02​​sinh2(κa)]−1,[1+2ℏ2mV0​a2​]−1,[1+4E(E−V0​)V02​​sin2(qa)]−1,​0<E<V0​,E=V0​,E>V0​,​q=ℏ2m(E−V0​)​​.
(75)
Below the barrier, the hyperbolic sine is positive for a>0, so 0<T<1. At E=V0​, the interior wavefunction is linear in x; either neighbouring formula has the displayed finite limit, since sinhz/z and sinz/z tend to one. Above the barrier, the interior wavefunction oscillates: the quantum reflection probability generally remains nonzero, but qa=nπ for a positive integer n gives perfect transmission probability T=1 through constructive wave interference. These are scattering resonances, not below-barrier quantum tunnelling.
At half height, E=V0​/2, we have k=κ and T=sech2(κa), consistent with finite square barrier transmission at half barrier height after replacing this width a by that example's width 2a. In the illustration, the width is four times ℓ=ℏ/2mV0​​ and E/V0​=1/2, giving T≃0.01388. The plotted wavefunction comes from solving the four interface equations; the transmission probability curve uses the closed formula.
