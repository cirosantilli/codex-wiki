Theory of infinitely many singleton and doubleton equivalence classes

 ... Mathematics Area of mathematics Foundations of mathematics Mathematical logic First-order logic First-order theory
OurBigBook.com Words: 140
In the language containing one binary equivalence relation E, require all classes to have size at most two and infinitely many classes of each of the two sizes. This is a complete theory: every countable model has countably many singleton classes and countably many doubleton classes, giving a class-by-class isomorphism; Downward Lowenheim-Skolem theorem then rules out a sentence separating arbitrary models. It is also an aleph-zero-categorical theory.
It is not finitely axiomatizable. Any finite fragment of the explicit infinitude schemes has a finite model with sufficiently many classes of each size; compactness theorem would make such a finite fragment suffice if any finite axiomatization existed. It is not model-complete: adjoining a new singleton preserves the theory, and subsequently giving that new point a partner also preserves the theory, while changing the existential partner formula at that point. Naming the singleton-class predicate removes this particular obstruction to elementary embeddings.
