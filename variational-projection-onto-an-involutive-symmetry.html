Variational projection onto an involutive symmetry (E±​=(a±c)/(1±b))

 Home Mathematics Area of mathematics Analysis Calculus of variations Variational principle
OurBigBook.com Words: 146
Let H be a lower-bounded self-adjoint Hamiltonian operator and I a commuting self-adjoint operator with I2=1. For a normalized trial vector ϕ, define a=⟨H⟩ϕ​, b=⟨I⟩ϕ​, c=⟨HI⟩ϕ​. The Rayleigh quotient on ϕ+tIϕ is [(1+t2)a+2tc]/[1+t2+2tb], with derivative 2(c−ab)(1−t2)/(1+t2+2tb)2. Unless c=ab, its extrema are the normalized nonzero vectors ϕ±Iϕ, with energies (a±c)/(1±b). Their difference is 2(c−ab)/(1−b2), identifying the maximum and minimum.
The orthogonal projections (1±I)/2 split any trial energy into a weighted mean of the two sector energies, so one nonzero sector projection never raises the trial energy. If ∣b∣=1, one projected vector is zero and its quotient is undefined; the remaining family has constant energy. Exchange symmetry makes these sectors bosonic and fermionic spatial wavefunctions. Stationarity within this two-dimensional trial space does not generally make a vector an exact eigenvector of H.
