Single-phonon neutron scattering model (ε(q)=ε∗​∣sin(qa/2)∣)

Consider an original ideal one-dimensional monatomic harmonic oscillator chain with neighbor separation a, mass M and nearest-neighbor restoring coefficient K>0. Its displacement equation is
Mu¨r​=K(ur+1​+ur−1​−2ur​).
(9)
Substitute the normal mode ur​=u0​ei(qra−ωt). Since eiqa+e−iqa−2=−4sin2(qa/2),
ω(q)=2K/M​∣sin(qa/2)∣,ε(q)=ℏω(q)=ε∗​∣sin(qa/2)∣.
(10)
Choose a=4 ångströms and ε∗​=10meV. This is one acoustic phonon branch, distinct from the two-atom elastic example. It has ε(π/(2a))=52​meV and ε(π/a)=10meV.
For coherent creation of one phonon in a crystal, the excitation's wave vector q and the measured transfer obey Q=G+q, with G a reciprocal lattice vector. To connect the dispersion relation to an illustrative intensity, take zero temperature, longitudinal displacements and the first interval Q=q∈(0,π/a], choosing G=0. Expand the scattering phase to first order, eiQ(ra+ur​)≃eiQra(1+iQur​). The constant term supplies elastic scattering; the linear term creates one phonon. Its displacement matrix element is proportional to 1/ω​, so the squared amplitude has weight Q2/ε(Q), apart from common factors. Normalize this weight to one at Q=π/a:
x=πQa​,w(x)=sin(πx/2)x2​,0<x≤1.
(11)
The limit w(0)=0 follows from sin(πx/2)∼πx/2. Set σ=0.4meV and replace the ideal sharp line by a normalized Gaussian function:
J(Q,ΔE)=2π​σw(x)​exp[−2σ2(ΔE−ε(Q))2​].
(12)
This chosen broadening illustrates readout width; it is not a measured resolution or a full spectrometer resolution function. Integrating over all ΔE gives w(x). The small negative-energy tail is a broadening artifact, not a zero-temperature phonon-annihilation signal.
These panels separate three kinds of information: allowed reciprocal lattice positions, cancellation within a crystal basis structure factor, and an excitation's energy as a function of wave vector. The chain has no magnetic degrees of freedom, optical branches, damping or multi-phonon processes. It illustrates how one excitation could be read; it proves nothing about the completeness of a real material's excitation spectrum.
