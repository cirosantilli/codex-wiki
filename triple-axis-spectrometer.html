Triple-axis spectrometer

 ... Hadron Baryon Nucleon Neutron Neutron scattering Inelastic neutron scattering
OurBigBook.com Words: 173
A triple-axis spectrometer uses Bragg's law at an initial crystal monochromator to select λi​ and at a second analyzing crystal to select λf​. The sample sits between these selectors. The three axes refer to the monochromator, sample and analyzer rotations. The final counter measures accepted counts; the analyzer's wavelength selection makes those counts sensitive to outgoing energy even when the counter does not measure each neutron's energy directly.
For a constant-Q scan, change incident or outgoing energy while coordinating sample and analyzer settings so that ki​−kf​ stays fixed. Counts versus Ei​−Ef​ then probe excitations at one momentum transfer. Changing only the analyzer angle generally changes both transferred quantities. Incident-beam monitoring, analyzer response and the instrumental resolution must enter quantitative interpretation; the diagram's arrows specify information flow, not an absolute intensity calibration.
