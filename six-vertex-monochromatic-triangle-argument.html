Six-vertex monochromatic triangle argument (R(3,3)≤6)

 ... Combinatorics Ramsey theory Ramsey's theorem Finite Ramsey theorem Ramsey number Ramsey number for triangles
OurBigBook.com Words: 161
Choose any vertex v of a red-blue colored complete graph K6​. Its five incident edges go to the other five vertices. By the pigeonhole principle, at least three have the same color. Call their other endpoints a,b,c and, after swapping color names if needed, suppose va,vb,vc are red.
If any of ab,ac,bc is red, it completes a red triangle in a graph with v. If none is red, all three are blue, so a,b,c themselves form a blue triangle in a graph. Both possibilities force a monochromatic triangle in a graph. This proves the upper bound for the Ramsey number for triangles using only five incident edges and the three pairs among the selected neighbors; the colors of the other edges cannot defeat the argument.
Both panels show all 15 edges of a complete graph on six vertices. Faint colored edges are example choices outside the decisive argument; bold edges exhibit the forced triangle in a graph. The selected neighbor color could instead be blue, giving the same argument with the color names exchanged.
