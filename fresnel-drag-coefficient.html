Fresnel drag coefficient (f=1−n−2)

 ... Physics Branches of physics Special relativity Lorentz transformation Velocity-addition formula Fizeau experiment
OurBigBook.com Words: 200
The Fresnel drag coefficient is the factor f=1−1/n2 multiplying a transparent medium's velocity in the first-order change of collinear light phase velocity, when optical dispersion is neglected. Here n is the medium's refractive index in its rest frame. The name recalls Augustin-Jean Fresnel's partial-drag law; special relativity obtains the same coefficient from the velocity-addition formula without introducing a luminiferous aether.
Let the water move at signed velocity u along the positive laboratory axis, with ∣u∣≪c/n, where c is the vacuum speed of light. Assume a transparent medium with constant n>1, uniform flow and negligible optical dispersion. In the water's local rest frame, a collinear constant-phase surface travels at v′=c/n. Dividing the Lorentz transformation differentials along that surface gives the same longitudinal velocity-addition formula as for a particle:
vlab​=1+u/(nc)c/n+u​=(nc​+u)(1−ncu​+O(c2u2​))=nc​+u(1−n21​)+O(cu2​).
(37)
The correction comes from the transformed time denominator. Simply adding u to c/n would omit it. This argument concerns collinear phase velocity; in a medium with optical dispersion, group velocity is a different quantity.[ref]
For n=4/3, the Fresnel drag coefficient is f=7/16. A chosen flow speed of 6ms−1 therefore changes the forward phase velocity by approximately 2.625ms−1, even though the stationary-medium phase velocity is about 2.25×108ms−1. These are model values. The invariant vacuum speed of light remains c; c/n describes propagation through matter in its own rest frame.
