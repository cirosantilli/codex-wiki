Spin-triplet p-wave pairing (S=1, L=1)

 Home Physics Branches of physics Statistical physics Superfluidity Helium-3 superfluidity
OurBigBook.com Words: 415 Articles: 3
Spin-triplet p-wave pairing has pair spin S=1 and orbital angular momentum L=1, allowing multiple order parameters beyond conventional BCS theory with zero orbital angular momentum. [ref]
Specifying this symmetry does not determine its microscopic attraction mechanism. A short exchange-symmetry check explains why these quantum numbers fit fermions. The spin-one-half triplet states ∣↑↑⟩, (∣↑↓⟩+∣↓↑⟩)/2​ and ∣↓↓⟩ are symmetric under exchange. A stipulated orbital factor proportional to relative momentum kx​ changes sign under k↦−k. Multiplying any symmetric spin function by that odd orbital factor gives an antisymmetric pair wave function, as required by Fermi-Dirac statistics. By comparison, an even s-wave orbital factor needs the antisymmetric spin singlet state. This checks compatibility with exchange statistics; it neither selects a stable phase nor derives its transition temperature.
