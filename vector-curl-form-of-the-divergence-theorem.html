Vector curl form of the divergence theorem (∫∂V​v×ndS=−∫V​∇×vdV)

For a continuously differentiable vector field v on a bounded sufficiently regular region V, apply the divergence theorem to a×v for an arbitrary constant vector a. The vector calculus identity ∇⋅(a×v)=−a⋅(∇×v) and the cyclic scalar triple product identity show that the dot products of both sides of the displayed formula with a agree. Since a is arbitrary, the vector integrals agree. Here n is the outward unit normal vector. Reversing the order on the boundary gives the equivalent positive-sign formula ∫n×vdS=∫∇×vdV.
