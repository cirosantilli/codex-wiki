Variation of parameters (yp​=−y1​∫y2​r/W+y2​∫y1​r/W)

Variation of parameters replaces the constants in a complementary solution by functions to construct a particular solution.
For the inhomogeneous linear differential equation y′′+py′+qy=r, assume p,q,r are continuous functions on an interval I, and y1​,y2​ are linearly independent homogeneous solutions there. With W=W(y1​,y2​) and x0​∈I, variation of parameters constructs the particular solution
yp​(x)=−y1​(x)∫x0​x​W(s)y2​(s)r(s)​ds+y2​(x)∫x0​x​W(s)y1​(s)r(s)​ds.
(21)
The Wronskian has no zeros on I, so these quotients are defined. For Ay′′+By′+Cy=F, normalize first: r=F/A, not F.[ref]
To derive the formula, write yp​=u1​y1​+u2​y2​ and impose u1′​y1​+u2′​y2​=0. This auxiliary choice removes extra terms from the first derivative. Substitution into the inhomogeneous linear differential equation leaves u1′​y1′​+u2′​y2′​=r. The two linear equations for u1′​,u2′​ have determinant W, and Cramer's rule gives
u1′​=−Wy2​r​,u2′​=Wy1​r​.
(22)
Integrating from x0​ makes yp​(x0​)=yp′​(x0​)=0. Arbitrary integration constants contribute only homogeneous solutions; adding c1​y1​+c2​y2​ supplies the general solution.
For a worked example, use the same homogeneous solutions e−x,e2x and the forcing term r=6e3x in y′′−y′−2y=6e3x. With x0​=0 and W=3ex,
u1′​=−2e4x,u2′​=2ex,u1​=−21​(e4x−1),u2​=2(ex−1).
(23)
Consequently the particular solution selected by zero initial conditions is
yp​(x)=23​e3x+21​e−x−2e2x.
(24)
Its value and first derivative vanish at zero. Direct substitution confirms the forcing term: the differential operator L[y]=y′′−y′−2y annihilates the last two terms and maps e3x to 4e3x. The two integrals therefore build a response from known homogeneous solutions, even when the forcing term has no convenient elementary guess.
