Normal mean confidence interval with known variance (Xˉ±z1−α/2​σ/n​)

 Home Mathematics Area of mathematics Probability and statistics Statistical inference Confidence interval
OurBigBook.com Words: 587 Articles: 1
Suppose X1​,…,Xn​ are independent and identically distributed with normal distribution N(μ,σ2), where the mean μ is unknown and the standard deviation σ>0 is known. The sample mean Xˉ=n−1∑i​Xi​ has normal distribution N(μ,σ2/n) and standard error σ/n​. With zq​ the standard normal quantile at q, the displayed confidence interval has exact coverage probability 1−α for every μ and every positive integer n. No central limit theorem approximation is needed.
Take σ=2, n=16 and nominal confidence 95%. The standard error is 2/16​=0.5, and z0.975​=1.9599639845…. Therefore
C(X)=[Xˉ−0.9799819923…, Xˉ+0.9799819923…].
(37)
Rounded for reporting, this is xˉ±0.98. If xˉ=1.2, the realized confidence interval is approximately [0.22,2.18]. Exact 95% calibration refers to the unrounded standard normal quantile; using 1.96 literally gives coverage probability 0.9500042097…, almost but not exactly 0.95.
The confidence interval's width scales as n−1/2: increasing sample size from 16 to 64 halves its half-width to 0.4899909961…. If σ is unknown, merely substituting the square root of a sample variance loses the displayed exact calibration; for the same normal distribution model, use a Student t confidence interval.
The figure holds the true mean at μ=0 and independently generates 40 samples of 16 observations from the same normal distribution. NumPy's PCG64 generator with seed 42 gives 39 covering confidence intervals and one miss, at sample 32: observed coverage is 39/40=97.5%. Every bar uses the unrounded standard normal quantile. This finite collection illustrates the moving endpoints; it does not establish the analytic coverage probability or require exactly two misses.
The beauty of the construction is that a familiar standard normal distribution calibrates every possible unknown mean at once. For a short proof, the pivotal quantity
Z=σ/n​Xˉ−μ​∼N(0,1)
(38)
has a probability distribution independent of μ. The event ∣Z∣≤z1−α/2​ has probability 1−α; rearranging it gives μ∈C(X). The right panel shows the equivalent event ∣Xˉ∣≤0.9799819923… when μ=0.
