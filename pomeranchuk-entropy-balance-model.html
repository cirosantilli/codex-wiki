Pomeranchuk entropy-balance model (T(x)=T0​−(1−x0​)(T0​−Ti​)/(1−x))

 ... Physics Branches of physics Statistical physics Superfluidity Helium-3 superfluidity Pomeranchuk cooling
OurBigBook.com Words: 272
An original deliberately local model makes the cooling quantitative. Fix a total amount n of material, let x be the mole fraction of solid, and stipulate molar entropies
Sl​(T)=aT,Ss​(T)=b,a,b>0,nStotal​​=(1−x)aT+xb.
(25)
The constants a and b have units of molar entropy per temperature and molar entropy, respectively. Ignore their pressure dependence and the cell's own heat capacity. This is a local thermodynamics model, not an extrapolation to zero temperature. A constant positive b cannot remain valid once the solid's spins order.
For a reversible thermodynamic process with no heat exchange, total entropy is constant. Differentiating gives
(1−x)adT+(b−aT)dx=0,dxdT​=−a(1−x)b−aT​<0
(26)
when b>aT and x<1. Define T0​=b/a; solving between two states gives
T(x)=ab​−a(1−x)(1−x0​)(b−aTi​)​.
(27)
In particular, stipulate x0​=0, Ti​=T0​/4 and x=1/8. Then
T0​T​=71​,nbStotal​​=41​,bT0​Ql→s​​=T0​T​(1−T0​T​)=496​.
(28)
The last expression is the local reversible heat requirement per mole frozen at the final state, divided by bT0​; it is not the heat exchanged during the isolated compression. That compression exchanges no heat. This distinction separates the latent-heat sign from the actual boundary heat budget.
The utility of the model is its explicit condition: Ss​>Sl​ makes dT/dx<0, while positive total heat capacity supplies a stable thermal response. More generally, for molar entropies depending only on temperature, let Cl​,Cs​ be the molar heat capacities. Then dT/dx=−T(Ss​−Sl​)/[(1−x)Cl​+xCs​]. Incoming heat or entropy production adds terms to the balance and can overwhelm the ideal cooling. Neither the numerical ratios above nor the straight entropy lines are measurements of helium-3.
