The Maxwell equations are the field equations of classical electromagnetism. In SI units, in vacuum with prescribed charge density ρ and current density J, they are
∇⋅E∇×E​=ρ/ϵ0​,=−∂t∂B​,​∇⋅B∇×B​=0,=μ0​J+μ0​ϵ0​∂t∂E​.​
(1)
Here E is the electric field in volts per metre, B the magnetic field in teslas, ρ in coulombs per cubic metre, and J in amperes per square metre. The constants are vacuum permittivity ϵ0​ and the vacuum value μ0​ of magnetic permeability. “Vacuum” specifies the field laws without a material response; “source-free” additionally means ρ=0 and J=0. Matter described by averaged fields instead requires Maxwell equations in matter and constitutive relations.
The first equation, Gauss's law, makes electric charge a source of electric flux. Gauss's law for magnetism sets the net magnetic flux through a closed surface to zero. The two curl equations, Faraday's law and the Ampère-Maxwell equation, couple changes of the electric field and magnetic field. Appropriate initial conditions and boundary conditions select a physical solution; the two divergence equations constrain the initial fields. [ref].

Quasistatic approximation (electromagnetism, ωL/c≪1)

Words: 74
Maxwell equations OurBigBook.com
An approximation that keeps slow electromagnetic time variation while treating propagation across characteristic size L as rapid compared with the variation time 1/ω. The dimensionless parameter ωL/c measures that separation. Which terms of the Maxwell equations can be dropped still depends on the physical scaling; small propagation delay alone does not justify deleting both displacement current and electromagnetic induction. For a slowly driven parallel-plate capacitor, a nearly uniform gap electric field can coexist with the magnetic field generated by its changing electric flux.

Magnetic Galilean limit of the pre-Maxwell equations

Words: 114
Maxwell equations OurBigBook.com
The magnetic nonrelativistic limit neglects displacement current and uses ∂t​B=−∇×E, ∇×B=μ0​J, ∇⋅B=0. Under a Galilean transformation x′=x−vt, t′=t, its consistent field transformations are B′=B, J′=J and E′=E+v×B. Indeed ∂t′​=∂t​+v⋅∇ and ∇×(v×B)=−(v⋅∇)B, giving covariance of the induction equation. The full charge-current transformation has J′=J−ρe​v; neglecting its convective correction belongs to the magnetic quasineutral approximation, not exact relativistic electrodynamics. With u′=u−v, the combination E+u×B is invariant. Vanishing electric field in the fluid rest frame therefore gives the ideal magnetohydrodynamic induction equation.

Birefringence (Δn=ne​−no​)

Words: 2k Articles: 28
Linear anisotropic dielectric OurBigBook.com
Birefringence is the existence of different optical propagation constants for different eigenpolarizations in an anisotropic material. It provides the propagation-mode separation needed for adiabatic optical following in a twisted nematic.
Two different refractive indices can produce two displaced images, as in the calcite double-image experiment, or a phase retardation between overlapping components, as in a waveplate. Spatial separation concerns where energy flux travels; phase retardation concerns the relative wave phase. A specimen need not show two visibly separated images to have birefringence.

Calcite double-image experiment
Words: 227
Birefringence OurBigBook.com
Place a clear calcite specimen on a transparency bearing printed words on an overhead projector. The projected readout is two copies of the lettering. Put a rotatable linear polarizer after the specimen: suitable orientations suppress one copy or the other. These are the actual apparatus and observations in the University of Iowa demonstration.[ref]
For a useful control, remove the specimen while keeping the transparency, projector and screen fixed: the doubling should disappear. Replace it, hold it stationary and rotate only the polarizer; complementary brightness changes distinguish two linear polarizations from an accidental duplicate transparency. These are suggested controls, rather than additional reported measurements. Ideally the two copies obey complementary Malus's law curves, Io​(α)=Io,max​cos2α and Ie​(α)=Ie,max​sin2α, with angle α measured from the exiting ordinary wave's linear polarization. Unequal maxima do not invalidate the test. Finite polarizer extinction, poor focus and specimen defects limit visibility; image separation alone does not determine either refractive index without the specimen geometry.

Birefringent walk-off (ρ=ϕ−θ)

The extraordinary wave's Poynting vector can differ in direction from its wavevector. The latter specifies wave phase propagation, while the former specifies energy flux. This angular separation is walk-off.[ref]
In the lossless, nondispersive model above, take the gradient of ω=ckx2​/ne2​+kz2​/no2​​ to obtain the group velocity, parallel to the Poynting vector. Waveplate
Words: 1k Articles: 11
Birefringence OurBigBook.com
A waveplate uses birefringence to change polarization of an electromagnetic wave through phase retardation. Its fast axis has the smaller refractive index, hence the larger phase velocity; its slow axis has the larger index. Consider normal incidence with the optic axis in the plate face, so both components follow the same straight path. A linear polarization at 45∘ to these axes excites equal wave amplitudes.
A complementary experiment uses two sheet polarizers with a cellophane sheet between them. First cross the polarizers and remove the sheet: the field is dark. Insert and rotate the sheet about the beam: light returns at intermediate orientations, but extinction recurs at two perpendicular orientations. The sheet has changed the polarization of an electromagnetic wave, without requiring a resolved double image. With white light, different wavelengths can produce different transmitted colors.[ref]. Brightness alone does not establish circular polarization: an ideal rotating linear polarizer also gives orientation-independent light intensity for unpolarized light.

Double-pass quarter-wave plate

Quarter-wave plate OurBigBook.com
For a reciprocal ideal quarter-wave plate followed by a mirror at normal incidence, use the same fixed transverse laboratory axes for the forward and returning Jones vectors. If the axes coincide with the plate's fast and slow axes, each pass has Jones matrix J=diag(1,i) up to a common phase factor. A polarization-independent mirror has scalar reflection coefficient rm​, so the returned Jones vector is rm​J2e=rm​diag(1,−1)e. The plate acts twice as a half-wave plate, rather than undoing its phase retardation.
For input linear polarization at angle α to the fast axis, the returned line is at −α modulo π. At α=45∘ it is perpendicular to the original line. An axis-aligned input instead returns along the same line. General elliptical polarization requires its full input Jones vector; no unique returned state follows from the word “polarized” alone. The propagation direction reverses, so comparing descriptions of circular polarization must also specify the viewing convention.

Lenz's law expresses the minus sign in Faraday's law of induction: the induced electromotive force drives a passive resistive loop's electric current in the direction whose own magnetic field opposes the imposed change in magnetic flux. It opposes the change of flux, not invariably the external magnetic field. Choose an oriented surface and its positive boundary direction first, find the sign of dΦext​/dt, and then use the right-hand rule to translate the signed electric current into a physical direction. [ref].

With counterclockwise positive, I=−0.0030A is clockwise, producing an in-page magnetic field through the loop. Reverse the ramp to B(t)=0.40T−(0.30T/s)t on the same one-second interval: now E=+0.0060V and I=+0.0030A. The counterclockwise electric current produces an out-of-page magnetic field, supporting the decreasing external magnetic flux. For the increasing-area example in motional electromotive force, clockwise electric current runs down the right-hand rod. Its magnetic force on a current-carrying wire points left, opposing the imposed motion to the right. Writing Imag​=Bℓv/R for the current magnitude, the power required to maintain constant speed is
Pdrive​=(Imag​ℓB)v=R(Bℓv)2​=Imag2​R=PJoule​.

The distinction matters in the lossless limit of magnetic loop braking. The resistive toy examples use L=0 as an approximation, not a general consequence of Lenz's law.

Words: 1k Articles: 13
Maxwell equations OurBigBook.com
Gauss's law relates the electric field on a closed surface to the net electric charge inside it. In SI units, with outward unit normal n,
∮∂V​E⋅ndS=ε0​Qenclosed​​,Qenclosed​=∫V​ρdV.​
(22)
Here ρ describes a volume charge density, and ε0​ is the vacuum permittivity; for ideal point or surface charges, use the enclosed electric charge directly rather than an ordinary volume-density integral. The left side is electric flux: the surface integral of the outward normal component of the electric field. Field directed outward contributes positively; field directed inward contributes negatively. The equality uses net signed electric charge, so positive and negative enclosed charges can cancel. The Gaussian surface is an imagined accounting boundary, not necessarily a material object. See OpenStax, explaining Gauss's law.
Where the electric field and charge density are smooth, the local statement is ∇⋅E=ρ/ε0​. The divergence theorem connects this divergence to the surface integral. This is the satisfying connection between local sources and global accounting: a surface can register how much net electric charge it encloses without revealing where that charge is located within it. A point charge requires care with the singular field at its position; the integral formulation remains useful with a boundary that avoids the charge.
The law fixes total electric flux, rather than every value of the electric field. In an uncharged region, a uniform electric field E=(E0​,0,0) crosses a cube of side ℓ. Its left and right faces contribute −E0​ℓ2 and +E0​ℓ2; the other four faces contribute zero. Thus the net electric flux is zero while the electric field is nonzero everywhere. Charges outside a Gaussian surface can change the field on it without changing its net electric flux. Conversely, fields cannot generally be reconstructed from enclosed electric charge alone. Electrostatics also requires the electric field to be the negative gradient of an electric potential, with appropriate boundary information. This distinction is emphasized in Feynman lectures II.5, application of Gauss' law.
Spherical symmetry supplies the extra information needed for a particularly simple calculation. An isolated spherically symmetric charge distribution in vacuum produces a radial electric field, constant in magnitude on each concentric sphere. Its electric flux is therefore 4πr2Er​(r), giving
Er​(r)=4πε0​r2Qenclosed​(r)​.
(23)
Outside the distribution, doubling r quadruples the surface area and divides the radial electric field by four, while the electric flux stays Q/ε0​. The inverse-square dependence is an area-dilution result. The electric field of a uniformly charged ball and electric field of a uniformly charged spherical shell show that the same total charge can give different interior fields.
