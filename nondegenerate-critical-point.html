Nondegenerate critical point

 ... Area of mathematics Analysis Real analysis Calculus Multivariable calculus Hessian matrix
OurBigBook.com Words: 61
A critical point of a real function with continuous second partial derivatives is nondegenerate when its Hessian matrix is an invertible matrix there, equivalently when none of its eigenvalues is zero. A positive-definite matrix gives a strict local minimum, a negative definite Hessian gives a strict local maximum, and opposite-sign eigenvalues give a saddle point of a scalar function. A nondegenerate critical point need not be an extremum: x2−y2 has Hessian matrix diag(2,−2), which is an invertible matrix.
Such critical points are the local building blocks of Morse theory.
