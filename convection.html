Convection cell

Words: 45
Convection OurBigBook.com
An organized region of buoyant rising fluid and compensating descending flow that transports heat or composition. Convection rolls are a simple planar example; solar granules are evolving compressible examples. A cellular pattern does not require every material trajectory to form a closed streamline, especially near a radiating free surface.

Convection zone

Words: 57
Convection OurBigBook.com
A stellar convection zone is a region where buoyant fluid motion transports a significant part of the stellar heat flux. Rising and sinking parcels carry enthalpy, so heat transport is not entirely radiative. The Sun's outer convection zone extends from near 0.7R⊙​ to the surface and overlies its radiative interior. The tachocline lies near this transition, and the convection helps power the solar dynamo.

Compositional convection

Words: 104 Articles: 1
Convection OurBigBook.com
Compositional convection is buoyancy-driven motion caused by composition-dependent density variations. In a planetary core, freezing can expel light elements into the surrounding liquid, making that liquid buoyant and supplying mechanical power for a planetary dynamo. It can operate alongside thermally driven convection and release of latent heat.

Thermal and compositional interfacial buoyancy criterion (T−​/T+​>M−​/M+​)

Words: 60
Compositional convection OurBigBook.com
For two ideal gas layers at a common interfacial pressure, the lower layer has smaller density precisely when M−​/T−​<M+​/T+​, where M is mean molar mass. That ordering is unstable to an infinitesimal interchange. A heavy condensable constituent can therefore inhibit thermal convection in a light background gas even when the lower layer is hotter. This local criterion assumes no phase change during the initial displacement.

A reduced long-wave convection model can combine linear damping, destabilizing second derivatives, stabilizing fourth derivatives and gradient nonlinearities. In the displayed normalization the quadratic coefficient s breaks Boussinesq symmetry, while the cubic gradient term dissipates the mean-square temperature. Thermal convection

Words: 1k Articles: 25
Convection OurBigBook.com
Thermal convection is fluid motion driven by temperature-dependent buoyancy. It transports heat by bulk motion in addition to thermal conduction.

Horizontal convection

Words: 181 Articles: 2
Thermal convection OurBigBook.com
Horizontal convection is buoyancy-driven circulation forced by lateral variations of temperature along one horizontal boundary. Hot and cold surface regions drive a circulation even though all thermal forcing is applied at the same height. An insulated bottom and sides require zero net heat exchange through the remaining surface in steady state, but permit heat to enter its warmer region and leave its colder region.

Forced convection

Words: 37
Thermal convection OurBigBook.com
Heat-carrying fluid motion dominated by an externally imposed flow or shear. Buoyancy may still influence it; forced and free contributions can coexist. In surface-layer scaling, z≪∣ℓO​∣ is predominantly shear-driven, while z≫∣ℓO​∣ is predominantly buoyancy-driven for lower-boundary heating.

Natural convection

Words: 66 Articles: 1
Thermal convection OurBigBook.com
Convection driven primarily by buoyancy generated by heating or cooling, rather than an externally imposed mean flow. In a constant-flux unstable surface layer, the local speed scales as (B0​z)1/3.

Free-convection surface-layer scaling (wc​∼(B0​z)1/3,ϵ∼B0​)

Words: 36
Natural convection OurBigBook.com
For a positive approximately constant vertical turbulent buoyancy flux, dimensional turnover scaling gives Kb​∼B01/3​z4/3 and N2∼−B02/3​z−4/3. The negative squared frequency describes unstable overturning, not a real restoring oscillation. The leading dissipation is height-independent, unlike neutral wall turbulence.

Hexagonal convection amplitude equations (AT​=μ2​KA+γKBˉCˉ−3K2A∑∣Aj​∣2)

Words: 318 Articles: 4
Thermal convection OurBigBook.com
A finite-wavenumber convection instability with a resonant three-wave triad can yield three coupled amplitude equations. For the gradient nonlinearity ∇⋅(∣∇Θ∣2∇Θ) and weak quadratic asymmetry −ϵγ∇⋅(Θ∇Θ), projection onto a triad of length squared K gives the stated equation and its cyclic copies. The quadratic coefficient is +γK, while both self and cross cubic coefficients are −3K2 for angles of 120 degrees. The finite critical mode requires positive linear damping in the underlying long-wave equation.

Rotational hexagon amplitude equations

Words: 177 Articles: 2
Hexagonal convection amplitude equations OurBigBook.com
For a resonant three-wave triad with rotational and translational symmetry but no spatial reflection, the cubic amplitude equations have the cyclic form A˙=μA+BC−ν1​∣A∣2A−(ν2​+δ)∣B∣2A−(ν2​−δ)∣C∣2A. A half-turn induces complex conjugation, so all coefficients are real. The coefficient δ distinguishes the two cyclic cross-couplings that a reflection would interchange.
Roll and hexagon spectra without reflection symmetry
Words: 70
Rotational hexagon amplitude equations OurBigBook.com
For the rotational hexagon amplitude equations, put d=ν2​−ν1​ and N=ν1​+2ν2​. A roll with r2=μ/ν1​ has transverse eigenvalues −dr2±δ2r4+r2​, each twice, radial eigenvalue −2μ, and one neutral translation. A hexagon with μ=Nh2−h has real-amplitude eigenvalues h−2Nh2 and −2h+2dh2±2i3​δh2; its imaginary-amplitude eigenvalues are −3h,0,0. These follow by diagonalizing the cyclic Jacobian matrix. Stability is understood modulo translations. If ν2​>ν1​>0 and δ=0, the upper positive hexagon branch reaches a Hopf bifurcation at h=1/d, μ=(2ν1​+ν2​)/d2.
Axial isotropy of rolls and hexagons
Words: 64
Rotational hexagon amplitude equations OurBigBook.com
Write the translation group action as (A,B,C)↦(eiθ1​A,eiθ2​B,e−i(θ1​+θ2​)C). Modulo lattice translations, the stabilizer subgroup of (h,h,h), with nonzero real h, is the sixfold rotation group. Its fixed-point subspace of a group action is the real line (a,a,a). The stabilizer subgroup of (r,0,0), with nonzero real r, consists of the circle θ1​=0 together with a half-turn; its fixed-point subspace of a group action is the real line (a,0,0). The equivariant branching lemma therefore supplies generic branches of both axial types.

Roll instability from broken up-down symmetry (λ⊥​=±∣γ∣K∣A∣)

Words: 69
Hexagonal convection amplitude equations OurBigBook.com
In the hexagonal convection amplitude equations, a pure convection roll has ∣A∣2=μ2​/(3K) and B=C=0. The transverse diagonal linear growth cancels because self and cross cubic coefficients coincide. Broken up-down symmetry leaves the off-diagonal coupling γKA, giving eigenvalues ±∣γ∣K∣A∣ and an unstable orientation perturbation whenever γ=0. With zero quadratic coupling these directions are neutral at the retained order, so that degenerate case cannot be declared strictly unstable by this calculation.

Convection roll

Words: 67
Thermal convection OurBigBook.com
A convection roll is a circulating cell pattern whose ideal velocity and temperature fields are periodic across a chosen horizontal direction and independent of the coordinate along the convection roll axis. In a two-dimensional stream function representation, its velocity field is (−ψz​,0,ψx​). Rayleigh-Bénard convection can select stationary or oscillatory convection rolls; a two-dimensional convection roll may be unstable to three-dimensional disturbances, a different convection roll orientation, or slow amplitude and phase modulations. Existence of a saturated Landau amplitude equation branch does not alone establish its stability.

Oscillatory convection

Words: 105 Articles: 1
Thermal convection OurBigBook.com
oscillatory convection is time-dependent convection emerging through nonzero-frequency overstability or a Hopf bifurcation. At onset a complex-conjugate growth-rate pair crosses the imaginary axis. Travelling and standing convection rolls are distinct nonlinear possibilities, and their stability cannot be decided from the linear angular frequency alone.

Travelling and standing convection rolls

Words: 69
Oscillatory convection OurBigBook.com
At fixed orientation, opposite travelling amplitudes can obey Z˙±​=(r+iω0​)Z±​−(g∣Z±​∣2+h∣Z∓​∣2)Z±​. For r>0 and gr​=Reg>0, a travelling branch has one nonzero intensity r/gr​ and is stable to its counter-propagating amplitude when hr​>gr​. A standing branch has both intensities r/(gr​+hr​) and is stable within the pair when gr​>hr​ and gr​+hr​>0. These follow by linearizing the real intensity equations. Spatial modulation and other orientations remain separate stability tests.

Stationary convection

Words: 35
Thermal convection OurBigBook.com
stationary convection has a time-independent velocity and temperature field, in the chosen fixed frame. Its onset from a conduction state corresponds to a real growth rate passing through zero. Stability of a resulting steady convection roll is a separate nonlinear question.

Convective stability (N2>0)

Words: 33
Thermal convection OurBigBook.com
A stable stratification gives a restoring buoyancy force and positive squared buoyancy frequency for an adiabatically displaced parcel. An adverse specific entropy gradient can instead drive convection; other forces such as rotation can modify the full stability criterion.

Convective overshoot

Words: 44
Thermal convection OurBigBook.com
Convective overshoot carries material from an unstable convecting region into an adjacent stably stratified region because its momentum does not vanish at the stability boundary. In stars this mixes material beyond the boundary determined by a local stellar convective stability criterion, changing stellar core masses and nuclear lifetimes.

Double-diffusive convection

Words: 412 Articles: 7
Thermal convection OurBigBook.com
Double-diffusive convection occurs when two properties, such as temperature and composition, affect buoyancy but diffuse at different rates. A stabilizing composition gradient can oppose a destabilizing thermal gradient and change the form of heat transport.

Thermosolutal convection

Words: 349 Articles: 5
Double-diffusive convection OurBigBook.com
Thermosolutal convection is double-diffusive convection driven by temperature and concentration gradients. Their buoyancy contributions can oppose one another, while unequal thermal diffusivity and solutal diffusivity allow steady, oscillatory and finite-amplitude states. For a layer heated from below with a stabilizing concentration gradient, thermal buoyancy is destabilizing and solutal buoyancy restoring.
Double-diffusive overflow reservoir (θs​=C(1−θs​)10/3/(1−ηs​)2)

Thermosolutal convection OurBigBook.com
A steady well-mixed hot salty reservoir receives volume flux Q and loses the same flux over a weir. Heat and salt balances are Q(Ti​−T)=AFT​ and Q(Si​−S)=AFS​. If βFS​/(αFT​)=RF​ and αFT​=b(αΔT)4/3/Rρ2​, define R0​=β(Si​−S∞​)/[α(Ti​−T∞​)]. Then ηs​=(RF​/R0​)θs​ and C=Ab[α(Ti​−T∞​)]1/3/(QR02​). For R0​>1 and 0≤RF​<1 the interface is statically stable while preferential heat transfer can drive double-diffusive convection.

Lorenz model of thermosolutal convection (a,b,c,d,e)
Words: 197 Articles: 2
Thermosolutal convection OurBigBook.com
A five-mode Galerkin method model retains the roll velocity a, its correlated temperature and concentration modes b,d, and the mean-profile distortions c,e. Its parameters include the Prandtl number σ, diffusivity ratio τ, thermal forcing r, stabilizing solutal forcing rs​, and a positive geometric decay ratio ϖ. The velocity equation is a˙=σ(−a+rb−rs​d), while the scalar equations are b˙=−b+a(1−c), c˙=ϖ(−c+ab), d˙=−τd+a(1−e) and e˙=ϖ(−τe+ad). The solutal forcing is normalized using the thermal diffusion scale, so its steady stabilizing effect is rs​/τ.
Oscillatory threshold of a thermosolutal Lorenz model (rH​=1+τ(σ+1+τ)/σ+rs​(σ+τ)/(σ+1))

Linearization of the Lorenz model of thermosolutal convection at conduction yields (λ+σ)(λ+1)(λ+τ)−σr(λ+τ)+σrs​(λ+1)=0. A Hopf bifurcation requires the cubic coefficient identity a3​=a1​a2​ and positive frequency squared. Its threshold is the stated rH​, with ω2=σrs​(1−τ)/(σ+1)−τ2>0. If rs​=β2τ and δ=σ/(1+σ), then rH​=1+τ/δ+β2τδ+O(τ2). Comparing with the steady fold of a thermosolutal Lorenz model gives a leading nonnegative difference τ(βδ−1)2/δ for β≥0.
Steady fold of a thermosolutal Lorenz model (rmin​=(rs​τ​+1−τ2​)2)
Words: 51
Lorenz model of thermosolutal convection OurBigBook.com
A nonzero steady Lorenz model of thermosolutal convection branch obeys r(x)=(1+x)+rs​τ(1+x)/(τ2+x), with x=a2. For 0<τ<1, it has an interior saddle-node bifurcation precisely when rs​>τ3/(1−τ2). Differentiating gives x∗​=−τ2+rs​τ(1−τ2)​ and the stated minimum. The weaker-versus-stronger threshold distinction matters: a sufficient bound need not be the sharp subcriticality condition. Steady-branch existence alone does not decide oscillatory stability.

Layered convection

Words: 30
Double-diffusive convection OurBigBook.com
Layered convection consists of mixed convecting layers separated by more weakly transporting interfaces. In a giant planet, composition gradients can support such interfaces and reduce heat loss, contributing to delayed cooling of an inflated giant planet.
