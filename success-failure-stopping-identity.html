Success-failure stopping identity (P(Tn​<Um​)=∑k=0m−1​(kn−1+k​)pnqk)

In independent Bernoulli trials with success probability p and failure probability q=1−p, let Tn​ be the trial of success n and Um​ the trial of failure m, for positive integers n,m. If success n occurs after k<m failures, its final trial is a success and the preceding trials have n−1 successes and k failures. The negative binomial stopping argument therefore gives the title sum.
Put F=n+m. By trial F−1, at least one target must have been reached, so the same event is that a binomial distribution with F−1 trials has at least n successes. Conditioning instead on the final trial of a block of F trials gives
∑k=0m−1​(kn−1+k​)pnqk=(nF−1​)pnqF−n+∑k=n+1F​(kF​)pkqF−k.
(39)
Indeed, more than n successes in the full block guarantee the success target won; with exactly n successes it won only when the final trial was a failure. These disjoint cases explain the extra boundary term. A zero success target wins immediately; a zero failure target has already been reached. To include n=0 in the displayed finite sum requires the explicit generalized convention (0−1​)=1; an upper summation limit of −1 denotes an empty sum.
