Truth table

 ... Mathematics Area of mathematics Foundations of mathematics Mathematical logic Propositional logic Boolean valuation
OurBigBook.com Words: 147
A truth table lists a propositional formula's value for every Boolean valuation of its distinct primitive propositions. With k primitive propositions, there are 2k rows: each independently receives T or F. Evaluate compound columns from their subexpressions using the classical rules for logical connectives. This is exhaustive checking, so a formula true on every row is valid in classical propositional logic.
For a computed example over the natural numbers, let A(n) mean that n is an even number and B(n) mean n>2. The choices n=4,2,3,1 realize respectively (A,B)=(T,T),(T,F),(F,T),(F,F): all four possible inputs.
The logical implication A→B and its contrapositive agree on every row; the converse implication B→A differs on two rows. A truth table therefore checks equivalence or supplies a counterexample. Classical truth conditions are given in forall x: Calgary, section 31.2. These two-valued truth tables do not characterize validity in Intuitionistic propositional logic.
