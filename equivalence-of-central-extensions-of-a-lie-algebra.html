Equivalence of central extensions of a Lie algebra

 ... Area of mathematics Algebra Diagonal dominance Lie theory Lie algebra Central extension of a Lie algebra
OurBigBook.com Words: 152
Two central extensions of a Lie algebra 0→z→E→g→0 and 0→z→E′→g→0 are equivalent if a Lie algebra isomorphism E→E′ commutes with both maps in the short exact sequences, inducing the identity on the specified z and g. This is finer than asking only that E and E′ be isomorphic as Lie algebras.
For z=kz, choose linear sections and identify the two vector spaces with g⊕kz. A linear map compatible with both ends necessarily has the form (x,uz)↦(x,(u+t(x))z) for a linear functional t. If the two Lie algebra two-cocycles are α,α′, preservation of the Lie bracket is exactly α′(x,y)=α(x,y)+t([x,y]). Hence equivalence means that their difference is a Lie algebra two-coboundary. If changes of the identifications at the ends are allowed, an automorphism of a Lie algebra at either end can identify further classes. For a two-dimensional abelian Lie algebra and a one-dimensional central line, the finer classes are parametrized by k, whereas the middle Lie algebras have only the zero and nonzero classes, represented by the abelian Lie algebra k3 and the Heisenberg Lie algebra.
