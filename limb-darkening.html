Limb darkening

 ... Physics Branches of physics Optics Radiometry Radiance Spectral radiance
OurBigBook.com Words: 1k Articles: 5
Limb darkening is the decrease of emergent specific intensity toward the apparent edge of a stellar disc. A transit depth then depends on the intensity sampled by the planet as well as its projected area; the uniform-disc area ratio is only an approximation.
For a spherical star of radius R⋆​, let r be distance from the centre of its projected disk and let θ be the angle between the outward unit normal and the line of sight. The angular coordinate is
μ=cosθ=1−(r/R⋆​)2​.
(18)
Disk centre has μ=1; the apparent edge approaches μ=0. A resolved image measures specific intensity in each direction. Limb darkening means that this specific intensity itself falls toward the edge; the projected area factor used when collecting radiant flux is a separate geometric effect.
A simple explanation assumes a horizontally uniform plane-parallel atmosphere, a sufficiently deep optically thick medium, negligible scattering of light, and thermal absorption and emission in local thermodynamic equilibrium. Let τν​ be inward vertical optical depth at fixed frequency ν. An oblique light ray has line of sight optical depth τν​/μ, so it encounters the same attenuation at a smaller vertical optical depth. The formal solution of the radiative transfer equation gives
Iν​(0,μ)=∫0∞​Sν​(τν​)e−τν​/μμdτν​​,0<μ≤1.
(19)
Here Sν​ is the source function; under these assumptions it is the Planck function Bν​[T(τν​)]. If temperature increases inward, an oblique view preferentially samples cooler layers and hence lower specific intensity. [ref]
The weight e−τν​/μ/μ is an exponential distribution in vertical optical depth, with expected value μ and standard deviation μ. Thus “seeing down to optical depth one” refers to the line of sight, and represents a broad sampling of layers rather than a sharp emitting surface. For a source function that is linear throughout the atmosphere, the Eddington-Barbier relation is exact:
Sν​(τν​)=aν​+bν​τν​⟹Iν​(0,μ)=aν​+bν​μ=Sν​(μ).
(20)
This follows by integrating the constant and linear terms separately. A constant source function instead gives constant specific intensity, as in the blackbody limit of an isothermal atmosphere. An outward-rising temperature or important scattering of light changes the argument. Close enough to the actual edge, a plane-parallel atmosphere also loses validity because of curvature. Neither one limb-darkening coefficient nor a universal dependence on wavelength follows from this simple model.
