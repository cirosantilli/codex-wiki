Logarithm and square-root continuation along different paths

 ... Area of mathematics Analysis Complex analysis Holomorphic function Analytic continuation Monodromy theorem
OurBigBook.com Words: 472
Start at a=1 with complex logarithm germ of a holomorphic function L(1)=0 and holomorphic square root germ of a holomorphic function S(1)=1. On the punctured plane D=C∖{0}, both admit analytic continuation along every path. To construct it locally, for any c=0 choose a small open disc avoiding 0, select a holomorphic logarithm there, and select its additive multiple of 2πi to agree with the arriving germ of a holomorphic function; exponentiating half this logarithm supplies the arriving holomorphic square root. A finite chain of these open discs follows any given path.
Compare two paths from 1 to i, parametrized by 0≤t≤1:
γA​(t)=eiπt/2,γB​(t)=e−3iπt/2.
(24)
The first follows a counterclockwise quarter-circle; the second follows a clockwise three-quarter-circle. Following γA​ and then path reversal of γB​ makes one positive turn about 0. Its winding number is 1, so the two paths cannot be homotopic with endpoints fixed in the punctured plane.
The continued values along the chosen paths are
LA​(t)=2πit​,LB​(t)=−23πit​,SA​(t)=eiπt/4,SB​(t)=e−3iπt/4.
(25)
At their common endpoint,
LA​(1)=2πi​,LB​(1)=−23πi​,LA​(1)−LB​(1)=2πi,SA​(1)=2​1+i​,SB​(1)=−2​1+i​.
(26)
Both complex logarithm values exponentiate to i, and both holomorphic square root values square to i. These give different terminal germ of a holomorphic function representatives, consistently obtained from the same starting germ of a holomorphic function. The monodromy theorem applies, but does not equate these two non-homotopic paths.
The closed path λ(t)=e2πit instead returns to 1, with continued values Lλ​(t)=2πit and Sλ​(t)=eπit. One counterclockwise turn changes the complex logarithm by 2πi and flips the holomorphic square root from 1 to −1. Hence neither initial germ of a holomorphic function can come from a global holomorphic logarithm or a global holomorphic square root of z on the punctured plane. Repeating a loop of winding number n gives the changes 2πin and (−1)n. [ref]
Now restrict to the slit domain Dslit​=C∖(−∞,0]. The principal logarithm and exp(Logz/2) realize the same initial germ of a holomorphic function for each expression globally. To see that this is a simply connected domain, polar form identifies this domain with (0,∞)×(−π,π), a convex set; every loop contracts there. The simply connected corollary of the monodromy theorem now forces equality along every pair of paths with matching endpoints. In particular, γA​ and the segment σ(t)=1−t+it both give L(i)=πi/2 and S(i)=(1+i)/2​. The explicit homotopy H(s,t)=(1−s)γA​(t)+sσ(t) stays in the closed first quadrant away from 0, hence in Dslit​.
The other route γB​ crosses the removed ray at t=2/3 and is no longer admissible. In the punctured plane, that crossing is locally regular: analytic continuation passes through −1 smoothly, although evaluating the principal logarithm would reset its argument. The branch cut changes the allowed domain; it does not create additional branch points or poles. That distinction is the beauty of the obstruction: perfectly compatible local holomorphic functions can fail to make a global choice solely because of the allowed based loops.
