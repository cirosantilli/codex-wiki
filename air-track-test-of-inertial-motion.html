Air-track test of inertial motion

 ... Physics Branches of physics Classical mechanics Force Newton's laws of motion Newton's first law
OurBigBook.com Words: 344
An actual teaching experiment in Arbor Scientific's The Level Air Track uses one glider, an air track, two photogates, and a ruler. Turn on the blower and place the glider near the middle. Adjust the feet until it does not drift when released. Put the photogates at least half a track length apart and align them with the glider's flag. Measure the length L that interrupts the beams. Give a gentle push, let go, and record each beam-blocking time Δti​. The readout is the pair of speeds si​=L/Δti​. The worksheet asks how those speeds compare, which forces remain after the push, and which interaction the air track minimizes.[ref]
The motivating contrast is between a brief push and the subsequent glide. The air cushion reduces contact friction; it does not remove the downward gravitational weight or the upward support force. Similar successive speeds after release are consistent with a small horizontal net external force, rather than with a continuing push. The experiment approximates the zero net external force model over a finite track and observation interval.
To strengthen the documented procedure, use independent measurement calibration: check the flag length and each timer against a known length and time reference; measuring L sets the length scale but does not calibrate the timer. Repeat launches in both directions without contact during the timed passage, and check for drift at several track locations. These are additional controls, not reported steps or measured results in the worksheet. A residual inclined plane produces a downhill component of gravitational weight with the same laboratory direction on both runs; friction and drag force oppose the direction of travel. Reversing the launch can therefore help distinguish these effects.
A photogate directly measures a beam-blocking time; L/Δti​ is an average speed during that passage, not an exact instantaneous velocity. Assign the direction separately. Equal readings at two gates do not prove zero acceleration everywhere between them: opposing changes could cancel. Measurement uncertainty from timer resolution, an incorrectly measured flag length, residual slope, airflow disturbances, and remaining friction or drag force limit the inference. No measured glider speeds are assumed here.
