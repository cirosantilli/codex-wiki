Take σ(ζ)=ζ[(5+α)ζ2−(4+8α)ζ+11−5α]/6. The root condition for a multistep method holds exactly for −1<α<1. Since ρ′(1)=σ(1)=2(1−α), these and only these parameters give a convergent method. Its exponential-symbol order criterion for a multistep method has defect −(α+5)z4/12+O(z5), giving formal order three except at α=−5, where the first defect is z5/10. None of the convergent family is A-stable: for a parasitic root r=α+i1−α2​, perturbation of ρ(r(z))−zσ(r(z))=0 gives Re[r′(0)/r]=(α−1)/12<0. A small negative real z therefore moves it outside the unit disk. Simpson multistep method (Yn+2​−Yn​=3h​(fn+2​+4fn+1​+fn​))

Words: 65
Linear multistep method OurBigBook.com
Integrating a quadratic interpolant of the derivative over two steps gives this linear multistep method, which has order four. Its local truncation error is −h5y(5)(tn+1​)/90+O(h7) and its zero-stability polynomial is ρ(ζ)=ζ2−1, with simple roots 1,−1. The Dahlquist equivalence theorem therefore gives fourth-order convergence with sufficiently accurate starts. This attains the even two-step first Dahlquist barrier. Its undamped parasitic zero-step mode need not have favorable long-time absolute stability; finite-time convergence and stiff damping are separate requirements.

First Dahlquist barrier

Words: 271 Articles: 3
Linear multistep method OurBigBook.com
For a consistent zero-stable real linear k-step method with constant coefficients and only first-derivative evaluations, the attainable order is at most k+1 for odd k and k+2 for even k. For an explicit method the bound is k. These are order barriers under the root condition for a multistep method, not bounds on multiderivative methods or on arbitrary variable-step formulations. The Second Dahlquist barrier adds the stronger bound of order two for irreducible A-stable methods.

Using Newton-Cotes closed quadrature for the integrated derivative yields the displayed linear multistep method. Its first characteristic polynomial is ρ(z)=zs−1, whose unit-circle roots are all simple, proving zero-stability. Quadrature exactness gives order s+1 for odd s and at least s+2 for even s. The first Dahlquist barrier gives equality, while the Dahlquist equivalence theorem gives convergence with consistent starts. These are finite-time convergence statements, not claims of A-stability.

Cayley coefficient proof of the first Dahlquist barrier (p≤s+1 (s odd),p≤s+2 (s even))

Words: 146 Articles: 1
First Dahlquist barrier OurBigBook.com
For a real consistent zero-stable linear multistep method, transform its first characteristic polynomial by z=(1+x)/(1−x) to (1−x)sρ(z)=xP(x). The root condition for a multistep method puts all finite roots of P in the closed left half-plane; real linear factors and conjugate quadratic factors consequently give nonnegative coefficients, after choosing P(0)>0. The order criterion requires a polynomial of degree at most s to agree through degree p−1 with P(x)x/(2arctanhx). The latter quotient has strictly negative nonconstant even coefficients. Its first even coefficient beyond degree s includes a strictly negative multiple of P(0) and cannot vanish. This gives the stated bound.

Canceling a common factor of the characteristic polynomials changes the allowed starting relations and can remove a parasitic mode rather than prove stability of the original recurrence. For example ρ=(ζ−1)2 and σ=(ζ2−1)/2 fail zero-stability. On y′=0, the original recurrence admits yn​=y0​+n(y1​−y0​); starting perturbations y1​−y0​=h​ vanish but give divergent error at fixed positive time. The canceled one-step recurrence excludes these data, so its stability cannot be assigned to the original scheme.

The zero-stability roots are 1 and α−1, so it is convergent exactly for 0≤α<2. Multiderivative multistep method

Words: 577 Articles: 7
Linear multistep method OurBigBook.com
A multiderivative multistep method uses higher time derivatives as well as values and first derivatives from several time levels. For an autonomous ordinary differential equation y′=f(y), the second time derivative is y′′=f′(y)f(y) by the chain rule. Its local truncation error must include the higher-derivative terms. Consequently the usual second-order barrier for A-stable linear multistep methods, which only use first derivatives, does not apply.

Bounded-root stability set of the symmetric two-derivative formula (z=iy,y4−45y2+225>0)

Words: 96
Symmetric two-step two-derivative formula OurBigBook.com
For the symmetric two-step two-derivative formula, the closed-disk root condition for a multistep method fails off the imaginary axis. Positive real part makes the product of root moduli exceed one; negative real part fails the complex quadratic Schur criterion, as in the empty strict decay domain of the symmetric two-derivative formula proof. On z=iy, write D=15−y2−7iy=∣D∣eiδ and set ξ=ie−iδη. The amplification polynomial becomes η2−16yη/∣D∣+1=0. Its two roots are distinct and on the unit circle exactly when ∣16y/∣D∣∣<2, or y4−45y2+225>0. Thus the bounded-root set consists of the imaginary intervals ∣y∣<(45−155​)/2​ and ∣y∣>(45+155​)/2​, including y=0 and excluding the double-root endpoints. In particular, the set is unbounded. Unit-modulus oscillations are bounded but do not tend to zero.

For a fixed-step multiderivative multistep method, the ordinary zero-stability root condition still controls propagation of the starting errors when all derivative evaluation maps are uniformly Lipschitz continuous on the relevant bounded region. A local defect O(hp+1) then yields global error O(hp) over a fixed time interval, provided the starting errors are O(hp) and the implicit updates use the nearby solution branch.
For example, writing the numerical error equation as ρ(E)en​=hFh​(en+s​)+dn​, with Fh​ uniformly Lipschitz continuous, a bounded impulse response for the root condition for a multistep method gives

Absorbing the last current-step term for small h and applying the discrete Gronwall inequality gives the stated order. The higher derivatives enter through Fh​, whose bound stays uniform as h→0.

Parasitic amplification root

Words: 147 Articles: 1
Root condition for a multistep method OurBigBook.com
An amplification root of a multistep method is parasitic when it represents an additional numerical mode rather than the principal mode approximating the exact differential-equation flow. For a consistent method the principal root starts at one as the step tends to zero, while other roots can start elsewhere. A simple parasitic root on the unit circle is compatible with zero-stability, but its motion outside the circle for a negative test parameter can destroy absolute stability. Fixed-time convergence and all-time damping must be distinguished.
Centered two-step discretization of exponential decay (ρ±​=−Kh±1+K2h2​)
Words: 67
Parasitic amplification root OurBigBook.com
Replacing the derivative in x′=−Kx by a central finite difference gives xn+1​+2Khxn​−xn−1​=0. Its principal root is ρ+​=e−arsinh(Kh), while the parasitic amplification root is ρ−​=−earsinh(Kh). On a fixed time interval, Ah​ρ+n​+Bh​ρ−n​ converges uniformly to Ce−Kt exactly when Ah​→C and Bh​→0. A fixed nonzero parasitic amplitude alternates between two incompatible limits, but a vanishing starting error can converge despite the root's modulus exceeding one. This distinguishes fixed-time numerical convergence from long-time absolute stability.

Necessity of the root condition for multistep convergence

Words: 75
Dahlquist equivalence theorem OurBigBook.com
For a linear multistep method, convergence for every set of consistent starting values requires the root condition for a multistep method. Apply the recurrence to y′=0 over N steps. A root ξ with ∣ξ∣>1 gives the error mode en​=∣ξ∣−Nξn: its fixed-index starting errors vanish as N→∞, but ∣eN​∣=1. A unit-modulus root of multiplicity m≥2 instead gives en​=N−(m−1)nm−1ξn, with the same failure. Repeated roots inside the unit disk are harmless because their polynomial growth is dominated by exponential decay.

Amplification root (ξ(z))

Words: 61
Amplification polynomial of a multistep method OurBigBook.com
An amplification root is a root of a polynomial governing a geometric mode yn​=ξn of a numerical recurrence on the Dahlquist test equation. Its modulus measures damping or growth per step. A repeated root produces additional modes such as nξn; therefore a unit-circle root can be permitted by a bounded-root condition only when it is simple. The principal root approximates ez near zero, while additional roots can describe parasitic amplification roots.

Exterior roots of the derivative polynomial bound multistep stability (σ(w∗​)=0, ∣w∗​∣>1)

Words: 71
Amplification polynomial of a multistep method OurBigBook.com
If the derivative characteristic polynomial σ of a linear multistep method has a simple root outside the unit disk and keeps its degree in the limit, then its absolute stability region is bounded. Otherwise a stable sequence with ∣z∣→∞ would give amplification polynomials σ−ρ/z tending coefficientwise to σ. A root near the exterior root would persist by continuity, contradicting the root condition for a multistep method. This argument excludes stability at every sufficiently large complex z, not only along the negative real axis.
