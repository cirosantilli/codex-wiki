Blasius boundary layer

 Home Physics Branches of physics Continuum mechanics Boundary layer
OurBigBook.com Words: 934 Articles: 1
A Blasius boundary layer is the steady flow, two-dimensional laminar flow along a smooth stationary flat plate with uniform outer velocity U>0. Let x>0 measure distance from its leading edge and y≥0 distance normal to its upper face. For a Newtonian fluid with constant mass density ρ and dynamic viscosity μ, write kinematic viscosity ν=μ/ρ. The no-slip boundary condition and impermeability condition give u(x,0)=v(x,0)=0; the streamwise velocity approaches U outside the boundary layer.
For example, choose U=10ms−1 and ν=1.5×10−5m2s−1. At x=0.30m, the local Reynolds number is Rex​=Ux/ν=2.0×105 and the boundary layer thickness is about 3.29mm. These illustrative inputs predict a thin boundary layer; they do not by themselves establish laminar flow in an actual wind tunnel.
The boundary-layer scaling follows by comparing advection, of size U2/x, with normal viscous transport, of size νU/δ2:
δ∼Uνx​​,xδ​∼Rex−1/2​,Rex​=νUx​.
(14)
Equivalently, the available travel time x/U equals the viscous diffusion time δ2/ν in order of magnitude. Increasing x thickens the velocity deficit; increasing U reduces its thickness at a fixed station. This boundary-layer scaling requires Rex​≫1. It cannot resolve the immediate leading-edge region where x is comparable to ν/U.[ref]
The boundary-layer equation assumes a thin, attached laminar flow with streamwise changes over distances much larger than δ, negligible spanwise changes, incompressible flow, and constant material properties. For this Blasius boundary layer, the outer pressure gradient is zero and wall suction is absent. Mass conservation gives v/U=O(δ/x), but vuy​ is still of order U2/x, just like uux​. Thus the Prandtl boundary-layer equation retains both advection terms:
ux​+vy​=0,uux​+vuy​=νuyy​,py​≃0.
(15)
The discarded streamwise viscous term νuxx​ is smaller than νuyy​ by O((δ/x)2). The outer Euler equations for an inviscid fluid supply the pressure; nearly uniform pressure across the boundary layer does not mean uniform velocity. Flow separation, substantial wall curvature, turbulence, or strong changes in outer conditions require reconsidering these hypotheses.
Introduce the stream function and a dimensionless normal coordinate:
η=yνxU​​,ψ=νUx​f(η),u=ψy​=Uf′(η),v=−ψx​=21​xνU​​[ηf′(η)−f(η)].
(16)
Substitution gives the Blasius equation f′′′+ff′′/2=0, with f(0)=f′(0)=0 and f′(∞)=1. Its numerical boundary value problem gives f′′(0)≃0.332057. All streamwise velocity profiles therefore collapse onto the same f′(η) after rescaling y by νx/U​. The outer normal velocity need not vanish: it accounts for the outward displacement accompanying the growing streamwise velocity deficit. The MIT Similarity Solution uses the alternative coordinate ηMIT​=η/2​ and obtains fMIT′′​(0)≃0.46960; its factor of 2​ must be converted before comparing wall shear stress.
The historical Motion of fluids with very little viscosity by L. Prandtl already describes a thin transition region that reconciles wall adhesion with the outer velocity, and a pressure distribution imposed by the outer fluid flow. Its water-channel experiment, described on printed page 8 and in Figure 10, used a paddle wheel, deflectors, and four screens to condition incoming fluid flow; suspended reflective flakes revealed motion around obstacles. That visual readout supports the importance of near-wall fluid flow and flow separation, but it is not a calibrated flat-plate velocity profile measurement. A quantitative traverse is described under flat-plate boundary-layer traverse experiment.
