Upper-bound search (p=min({i:a(i)>x}∪{N}))

 Home Computer science Algorithm Binary search
OurBigBook.com Words: 98
For a sorted array a[0..N), find the unique boundary p∈[0,N] with a[0..p)≤x<a[p..N). A binary search maintains 0≤l≤r≤N, an excluded prefix a[0..l)≤x, and an excluded suffix x<a[r..N). At an interior midpoint m, replace l by m+1 when a(m)≤x, and otherwise replace r by m. Sortedness preserves the loop invariant; the width r−l is a decreasing loop variant. Termination at l=r gives the boundary, including p=0 and p=N. The time complexity is O(log(N+1)) with constant-cost comparisons and indexed access. Unlike searching for x+1, this formulation does not risk integer overflow at the largest representable key.
