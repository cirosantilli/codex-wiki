In-line holography

 Home Physics Branches of physics Optics Physical optics Holography
OurBigBook.com Words: 254 Articles: 1
In-line holography uses nearly the same direction for the holographic reference wave and holographic object wave. In a transmission geometry, the undiffracted illumination supplies R while the object supplies the scattered component O. This makes small optical path differences possible, but leaves the desired image, its twin image in holography and the bright background sharing an axis.
Dennis Gabor's electron-microscopy motivation led to optical tests: one line of a high-pressure mercury-vapor lamp illuminated a 3 μm pinhole, then approximately 1 mm microphotographs, recording approximately 1 cm holograms on sensitive photographic emulsions with minute-scale exposures. The roughly 0.1 mm optical coherence length favoured an in-line arrangement. Reillumination reconstructed images; focusing separated their depths incompletely. Distorted letters and random optical disturbances limited the results. These were optical demonstrations, not an operating electron microscope.[ref]
The recorded microphotograph and its reconstructed lettering provide an object-to-image comparison in that account. The blocked-reference control described above is an explanatory control for the model; it is not a claim about an additional historical measurement.
