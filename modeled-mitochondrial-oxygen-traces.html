Modeled mitochondrial oxygen traces (q=−VdcO2​​/dt)

The following original model chooses piecewise constant oxygen consumption rates in a closed, well-mixed 2mL suspension. It assumes no gas exchange, negligible addition volume, a corrected sensor and sufficient oxygen throughout. The curves are invented teaching examples, not measured traces, fitted data or a reconstruction of Figure 6.
Start at c(0)=220μmolL−1. On successive one-minute intervals, choose positive slope magnitudes 5,25,5,25,6,32,1μmolL−1min−1. Integration gives the endpoint concentrations 220,215,190,185,160,154,122,121μmolL−1. This is just the recurrence ci+1​=ci​−ri​Δt, with Δt=1min; the step plot shows ri​=−dc/dt. The trace remains above zero by construction.
During the second ADP interval, the total consumption rate is
q=−VdtdcO2​​​=(0.002L)(25μmolL−1min−1)=50nmolmin−1.
(5)
With a stipulated mitochondrial protein mass of 0.20mg, that is 250nmolmin−1mg−1. Using a denominator twice as large would halve this reported rate without changing the raw oxygen trace. Comparisons therefore need both loading and the chosen denominator.
For a second bookkeeping check, the modeled post-antimycin A slope of 1μmolL−1min−1 corresponds to 2nmolmin−1 background. If that background is additive and unchanged in earlier windows, subtraction gives 48, 10 and 62nmolmin−1 for excess ADP, oligomycin and FCCP, respectively. The oligomycin-sensitive difference is 48−10=38nmolmin−1. It is a difference in oxygen consumption under the chosen interventions, not an ATP production rate. Converting it into one requires additional coupling assumptions or an independent ATP measurement; the FCCP interval shows why slope alone cannot supply that conversion.
Across the whole seven-minute example, consumption is V(c(0)−c(7))=198nmol, also the sum of the seven interval consumptions. This equality checks the rate-to-amount accounting independently of how the intervals are named. Actual records require attention to calibration, mixing transients, background stability and decreasing oxygen availability; a line that steepens or flattens alone does not identify the cause.
