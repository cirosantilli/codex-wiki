Finite population correction (f=n−1n−r​​)

 ... Area of mathematics Probability and statistics Probability theory Probability distribution Discrete probability distribution Hypergeometric distribution
OurBigBook.com Words: 189
For uniform sampling without replacement from a population of n>1 objects, the finite population correction accounts for the reduced spread caused by drawing distinct objects. For the marked-object count K in a hypergeometric distribution, put p=c/n. Then
EK=rp,Var(K)=rp(1−p)n−1n−r​.
(29)
Relative to the corresponding binomial distribution, the variance is multiplied by f2=(n−r)/(n−1) and the standard deviation by f. The square-root convention for the correction is used in Philip Stark's discussion of sample sums.[ref]
The factor has a direct interpretation. For r=1, replacement makes no difference and f=1. For r=n, the entire population is observed and the marked count is exactly c, so f=0. In the ten-object, five-draw example, f2=5/9 and (6/5)(5/9)=2/3. The exceptional population size n=1 is deterministic and should be handled directly rather than inserting it into a zero denominator.
A short explanation uses an indicator random variable Ij​ for a marked object at draw j. Each has expected value p and variance p(1−p). For distinct draw positions i,j,
E[Ii​Ij​]=n(n−1)c(c−1)​,Cov(Ii​,Ij​)=−n−1p(1−p)​.
(30)
Since K=∑j=1r​Ij​, the variance of a sum includes r individual terms and r(r−1) ordered covariance terms, giving the displayed correction. Linearity of expectation still gives rp despite this dependence.
