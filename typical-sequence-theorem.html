Typical sequence theorem (P(Tε(n)​)→1,∣Tε(n)​∣≤2n(H+ε))

 ... Mathematics Area of mathematics Probability and statistics Information theory Asymptotic equipartition property Typical set
OurBigBook.com Words: 129
For a memoryless classical information source, the typical set consists of positive-probability words with ∣−(1/n)log2​p(un)−H(U)∣≤ε. The weak law of large numbers applied to −log2​p(Uj​) proves its probability tends to one. Every typical word has probability between 2−n(H+ε) and 2−n(H−ε). Summing the lower bound gives the displayed cardinality upper bound; if the set has probability at least 1−δ, summing the upper bound gives cardinality at least (1−δ)2n(H−ε).
To compress at any rate R>H(U), choose 0<ε<R−H(U), assign one fixed-length binary index to each typical word, and reserve one additional index for all atypical words. At most ⌈nR⌉ bits suffice for all large n. Decoding inverts the typical-word indices and makes an arbitrary output for the reserved index. The reconstruction error is at most the probability of the atypical set and tends to zero.
