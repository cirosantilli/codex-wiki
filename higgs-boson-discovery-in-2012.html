Higgs boson discovery in 2012

 ... Branches of physics Quantum field theory Standard Model Electroweak interaction Higgs field Higgs boson
OurBigBook.com Words: 563 Articles: 1
The ATLAS experiment and CMS experiment searched proton-proton collisions at the Large Hadron Collider at collision energies of 7 and 8TeV. The ATLAS experiment used silicon tracking, a 2T solenoid, liquid-argon electromagnetic calorimeters and an external muon spectrometer. Its July 2012 paper reported a combined local statistical significance of 6.0 standard deviations near 126.5GeV; this paper result is distinct from the initial 4 July announcement.[ref]
The CMS experiment used silicon tracking in a 3.8T solenoid, lead-tungstate electromagnetic calorimeters and external muon chambers. Electrons combine tracks and particle showers; muons combine tracking and chamber hits; photons supply shower energies and directions. Selected H→γγ and H→ZZ∗→4ℓ candidates, with ℓ=e,μ, yielded invariant mass peaks. It checked lepton efficiencies with Z boson decays, estimated fake-lepton backgrounds from control samples with relaxed identification, and fitted the smooth diphoton background from data. Its combined local statistical significance was 5.0 standard deviations near 125GeV.[ref]
The ATLAS experiment compared signal-plus-background and background-only hypotheses with a profile likelihood, accounting for systematic uncertainties and correlations between decay channels. Diphoton and four-lepton candidates gave precise invariant masses; WW∗ decays supplied additional evidence with poorer mass reconstruction because of escaping neutrinos. A local p-value concerns one specified mass; scanning masses requires a look-elsewhere effect correction.[ref]
Two-photon decay excludes spin one under the usual assumptions, but does not prove spin zero. A mass peak does not determine every Higgs boson coupling. Production rates, branching fractions and angular correlations require additional tests.[ref]
