Incoming boundary condition in dense bubble clearing (q′(α)=Vs​(1−2α)<0(α>1/2))

 Home Physics Branches of physics Fluid mechanics Void fraction Bubble clearing in a cylindrical vessel
OurBigBook.com Words: 149
For αt​+[Vs​α(1−α)]x​=0 in a finite upward pipe, bubbles move upward at Vs​(1−α), but dense-mixture phase-fraction characteristics move downward. Hence an open upper end may be an incoming boundary for the scalar conservation law even though the bubbles themselves leave through it. Atmospheric pressure alone does not prescribe that incoming phase-fraction trace.
The bottom-clearing shock from zero to a uniform initial α0​ has speed Vs​(1−α0​). Continuing that constant right state to the outlet gives clearance time L/[Vs​(1−α0​)], but at α0​>1/2 it requires a compatible maintained incoming trace. For α0​=3/4, the shock is x=Vs​t/4, and a right-state characteristic traced backward from it has initial position 3Vs​t/4. It reaches the upper boundary at time 4L/(3Vs​), before the formal clearance time 4L/Vs​. Later evolution therefore depends on the top closure. This boundary issue is distinct from the validity of the local slip and conservation derivation.
