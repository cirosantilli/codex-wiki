Radiation pressure of an isotropic photon gas (prad​=u/3)

 ... Physics Branches of physics Statistical physics Thermodynamics Pressure Radiation pressure
OurBigBook.com Words: 300
For radiation in vacuum with isotropy, energy is distributed equally per solid angle: write uΩ​=u/(4π) for energy density per unit solid angle. Let θ be the angle between a ray's path and the wall normal. Its normal velocity ccosθ determines the arrival rate, while normal momentum per energy is cosθ/c. Their product is cos2θ. For a perfectly reflecting cavity wall,
prad​=2∫incoming hemisphere​uΩ​cos2θdΩ=4π2u​∫02π​dφ∫0π/2​cos2θsinθdθ=3u​.
(4)
The leading two is the normal momentum reversal; the incoming hemisphere contains half of the field's directional energy density. This is also the normal component of the bulk pressure tensor, Pnn​=∫4π​uΩ​cos2θdΩ=u/3. Isotropy makes all three diagonal components equal, while their sum is u because the squared direction components sum to one. The factor one third therefore depends on three-dimensional angular geometry, not on the shape of the blackbody radiation spectrum.
A perfectly absorbing wall exposed to this incoming hemisphere receives only u/6 from absorption. If it is in thermal equilibrium with the isotropic radiation, its outgoing emission supplies another u/6 of recoil, recovering u/3. A cold absorber before equilibrium is a different boundary condition. Similarly, the one-way incident energy flux is cu/4, whereas the net energy flux across a plane in the undisturbed isotropic field is zero. Dividing cu/4 by c misses the extra normal-momentum projection and does not give the cavity pressure.
For a stipulated isotropic radiation energy density u=6μJm−3, the bulk pressure is 2μPa regardless of its spectral shape. In thermal equilibrium, blackbody radiation supplies u=arad​T4 with radiation constant arad​=4σ/c, so prad​=arad​T4/3; Stefan–Boltzmann law defines σ. The equilibrium photon gas derives this same equation of state from its free energy.[ref]
