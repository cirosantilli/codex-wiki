Gauge-invariant scalar-vector-tensor perturbations of Minkowski spacetime (Φ=A−B˙+E¨,Ψ=C−ΔE/3,Vi​=Bi​−E˙i​)

Words: 164 Articles: 1
Linearized gravity OurBigBook.com
For signature (+−−−), decompose a metric perturbation into h00​=2A, h0i​=∂i​B+Bi​ and hij​=2Cδij​+2(∂i​∂j​−δij​Δ/3)E+∂i​Ej​+∂j​Ei​+hijTT​, where the spatial vectors are divergence-free and hTT is a transverse-traceless tensor. Under a passive coordinate shift ξ0=τ, ξi=∂i​L+Li​, with ∂i​Li​=0, the changes are δA=−τ˙, δB=L˙−τ, δC=ΔL/3, δE=L, δBi​=L˙i​, and δEi​=Li​. The displayed combinations and hTT are therefore gauge invariant. The decomposition is unique after appropriate spatial decay or mode restrictions; homogeneous and boundary modes require separate conventions.

Vacuum constraints for gauge-invariant Minkowski perturbations (ΔΨ=0,ΔVi​=0,ΔΦ=0,□hijTT​=0)

Words: 73
Gauge-invariant scalar-vector-tensor perturbations of Minkowski spacetime OurBigBook.com
With the reversed convention in curvature-sign convention in Killing derivative identities, choose the representative h00​=2Φ, h0i​=Vi​, hij​=2Ψδij​+hijTT​. The linearized Einstein components are G00(1)​=−2ΔΨ, G0i(1)​=−2∂i​Ψ˙−ΔVi​/2 and Gij(1)​=(∂i​∂j​−δij​Δ)(Φ−Ψ)−2δij​Ψ¨−(∂i​V˙j​+∂j​V˙i​)/2+□hijTT​/2. On all space with regular decaying fields, or for nonzero spatial Fourier modes, the vacuum constraints remove the scalar and vector parts, leaving two radiative gravitational wave polarizations. Singular Coulomb fields, spatial boundaries and homogeneous modes are not covered by that removal argument.

Static scalar perturbation of Minkowski spacetime (ds2=(1+2A)dt2−(1+2C)δij​dxidxj)

Words: 95
Linearized gravity OurBigBook.com
For static scalar perturbations with no mixed time-space or spatial shear terms, the displayed metric tensor uses signature (+−−−). With the reversed convention in curvature-sign convention in Killing derivative identities, its first-order Einstein tensor is δG00​=2ΔC, δG0i​=0, and δGij​=∂i​∂j​(A+C)−δij​Δ(A+C). These follow by substituting the metric into the first-order Christoffel symbols and Ricci tensor, discarding products of perturbations. A static source with the dust stress-energy tensor obeys ΔC=−4πGρ and the decaying spatial equation makes A=−C. For a point mass the Newtonian potential of a point mass gives A=−GM/r, C=GM/r. The spatial potential is necessary to determine light propagation; a time potential alone is insufficient.

Linearized Schwarzschild metric (ds2=(1−rS​/r)dt2−(1+rS​/r)dr2−r2dΩ2)

Linearized gravity OurBigBook.com
For a static spherical point source, the weak-field solution in isotropic radius ρ has gtt​=1−2GM/ρ and spatial metric −(1+2GM/ρ)(dρ2+ρ2dΩ2). The isotropic-to-areal weak-field radius change gives the displayed metric with rS​=2GM. Its validity requires rS​/r≪1. Weak-field photon-orbit validity (u=rS​/r≪1)

Words: 59
Linearized Schwarzschild metric OurBigBook.com
The first-order null-orbit equation (u′)2=rS2​E2/h2−u2(1−u) formally has a circular solution at u=2/3, with ∣h/E∣=33​rS​/2. Its second-order equation gives a perturbation δu′′=δu, hence instability. However, u=2/3 is outside the weak-field expansion. The exact Schwarzschild spacetime has a corresponding unstable photon sphere, but that physical result needs the exact geometry; extrapolating the linearized metric is not a controlled proof.

Isotropic-to-areal weak-field radius change (r=ρ+GM+O(G2M2/ρ))

Linearized Schwarzschild metric OurBigBook.com
The area of a symmetry sphere in the weak-field isotropic metric is 4πρ2(1+2GM/ρ). Equating it to 4πr2 defines the areal radius and gives the displayed change. To first order dr=dρ, and replacing 1/ρ by 1/r changes the perturbation only at second order. Quadratic gravitational effective stress-energy tensor (tμν​=−Gμν(2)​[h,h]/(8π))

Words: 73
Linearized gravity OurBigBook.com
Expand the Einstein tensor about the Minkowski metric. The quadratic term in the metric perturbation defines tμν​=−Gμν(2)​[h,h]/(8π), a symmetric tensor relative to the background. For a first-order vacuum solution, the expanded contracted Bianchi identity gives ∂μtμν​=0. Equivalently, a second-order correction j obeys G(1)[j]=8πt, whose flat divergence vanishes identically. This conservation is an on-shell statement. The local t depends on perturbative gauge and the background split; conservation alone does not make it a gauge-independent local gravitational stress-energy tensor.

Linearized coordinate gauge transformation (hμν​↦hμν​−∂μ​ξν​−∂ν​ξμ​)

Words: 156 Articles: 1
Linearized gravity OurBigBook.com
An infinitesimal coordinate change generated by ξ changes a metric perturbation about Minkowski spacetime by hμν​↦hμν​−∂μ​ξν​−∂ν​ξμ​, in the passive sign convention. For a passive coordinate change xμ↦xμ+ξμ, the first-order change is minus the Lie derivative of a tensor field of the background metric, equivalently the pullback by the inverse flow. The gauge invariance of the linearized Riemann tensor follows because the added terms have commuting third derivatives, which cancel in the curvature antisymmetrizations. Gauge choices alter the metric components while preserving linearized tidal effects.

Affine transverse trace in a diagonal linearized plane wave is pure gauge

Words: 83
Linearized coordinate gauge transformation OurBigBook.com
For a perturbation hxx​=2a(t−z), hyy​=2b(t−z) with all other components zero, the linearized Vacuum Einstein equations give (a+b)′′=0. Put s=a+b and choose ξx​=sx/2, ξy​=sy/2, ξt​=−s′(x2+y2)/4, ξz​=s′(x2+y2)/4. Under the linearized coordinate gauge transformation, the mixed time/transverse and longitudinal/transverse components cancel, while the other time/longitudinal changes are proportional to s′′=0. The remaining components are hxx​=a−b, hyy​=−(a−b), hxy​=0, a transverse-traceless gauge perturbation of plus polarization. This explicitly removes a nonzero affine trace without assuming it vanishes by boundary conditions. If (a−b)′′=0 too, all linearized curvature vanishes.

Flat linearized metric perturbations are locally pure gauge (R(1)[h]=0 ⟹ hab​=2∂(a​ξb)​)

Words: 70
Gauge invariance of the linearized Riemann tensor OurBigBook.com
On a contractible Cartesian patch of a flat background, zero linearized Riemann curvature operator says that the connection 1-forms Γ(1)abc​dxc are closed. The Poincaré lemma writes them as derivatives of a matrix-valued potential. Symmetry of the two lower affine connection indices then supplies a second potential, giving Γ(1)abc​=∂b​∂c​ξa. Linearized metric compatibility implies that hab​−2∂(a​ξb)​ is constant. Its symmetric constant part is absorbed by an affine vector field. Thus the metric perturbation is locally a coordinate change. Global topology and imposed boundary conditions can obstruct the corresponding global gauge choice.
