Equality in Minkowski inequality (∥f+g∥p​=∥f∥p​+∥g∥p​)

 ... Mathematics Area of mathematics Analysis Real analysis Lp norm Minkowski inequality
OurBigBook.com Words: 233
For 1<p<∞ and nonzero elements f,g of the same real or complex Lp space, equality in the Minkowski inequality holds if and only if
∥f∥p​f​=∥g∥p​g​almost everywhere.
(66)
Equivalently, g=cf almost everywhere for a single positive real number c. The multiplier must be constant across X; matching signs at each input alone is insufficient. For complex numbers, an arbitrary complex scalar multiple is also insufficient: g=if gives ∥f+g∥p​=2​∥f∥p​<2∥f∥p​ for nonzero f. If either element is zero in Lp space, equality holds automatically, even if a representative has nonzero values on a null set.
The reason is strictly convex normed space geometry. Normalize f,g to unit Lp norm and use weights ∥f∥p​/(∥f∥p​+∥g∥p​) and ∥g∥p​/(∥f∥p​+∥g∥p​). Distinct normalized functions give a convex combination of Lp norm strictly below one. This follows by integrating the strict convexity of z↦∣z∣p on the real numbers or complex numbers; equality therefore forces the normalized functions to agree almost everywhere.
At p=1, equality instead means ∣f+g∣=∣f∣+∣g∣ almost everywhere: the nonnegative difference has zero integral. For real functions, this is fg≥0 almost everywhere. For complex functions, their nonzero values must have the same argument wherever both are nonzero, but their magnitude ratio may vary. The disjoint-coordinate vectors (1,0) and (0,1) illustrate equality without constant proportionality.
At p=∞, no such constant proportionality condition is necessary: (1,0) and (1,1) have L-infinity norms one and a sum of L-infinity norm two. Nor must an essential supremum be attained: under Lebesgue measure on (0,1), f(x)=g(x)=x give equality 2=1+1, although neither function takes the value one. The endpoint Lp spaces need not be strictly convex normed spaces.
