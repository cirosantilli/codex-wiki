Lambertian reflectance

 Home Physics Branches of physics Optics
OurBigBook.com Words: 181 Articles: 1
Lambertian reflectance is the ideal diffuse-reflection model with direction-independent outgoing radiance, the frequency-integrated specific intensity. If a fraction 0≤ρ≤1 of incident radiant flux is reflected, a surface receiving irradiance E has reflected radiance L=ρE/π. The reflected outward power per area is πL=ρE by the same hemispherical integral used for radiant exitance. Its radiant intensity still acquires the projected area factor in Lambert's cosine law.
For E=100Wm−2 and ρ=0.80, the reflected radiance is 80/π≈25.4648Wm−2sr−1. A patch of area 0.020m2 reflects 1.6W in total, and its radiant intensities at 0∘ and 60∘ are respectively 1.6/π and 0.8/πWsr−1. The radiance stays fixed as the view changes, provided the illumination stays fixed. Changing the incident angle can change irradiance and therefore the reflected radiance.
