First work in the receiver's rest frame. If a source recedes at speed βc, two successive emissions separated by source proper time T0​ occur γT0​ apart in this frame. During that interval the source retreats by βcγT0​, so the arrival interval is TR​=γT0​(1+β). The longitudinal relativistic Doppler effect is consequently
ν0​νR​​=1+β1−β​​,λ0​λR​​=1−β1+β​​.​
(13)
A negative β describes approach and gives a blueshift.
Interpret 633nm as the laser's rest-frame wavelength and the stated detector velocity as measured in the initial laboratory frame. Choose +x from source to sensor, with detector velocity −0.2c. The required relative recession speed follows from
q=(1000/633)2,βrec​=q+1q−1​=0.4278687….
(14)
By relativistic velocity addition, the source velocity βS​c in the laboratory obeys (βS​+0.2)/(1+0.2βS​)=−βrec​. Hence
βS​=−1+0.2βrec​βrec​+0.2​=−0.578375….​
(15)
The laser must move away from the sensor at about 0.5784c in that laboratory frame. Although the sensor moves toward the source's initial location, the retreating source produces the required net redshift. The frame qualification matters: a velocity permanently fixed relative to the moving source would specify the Doppler factor already, rather than leave it adjustable.
