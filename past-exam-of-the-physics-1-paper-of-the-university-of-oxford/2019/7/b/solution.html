The escaping sand initially has the bucket's velocity, so it produces no relative-velocity thrust. This distinction is essential in a leaking Atwood machine. With upward velocity v for the leaky side, the two bucket equations are
Mv˙=T−Mg,M0​v˙=M0​g−T.
(19)
Their sum gives v˙=g(M0​−M)/(M+M0​). Since M˙=−α, it follows that
dMdv​=αg​M+M0​M−M0​​.
(20)
Integrate from M0​, where the velocity is zero:
v(M)=αg​[M−M0​−2M0​log(2M0​M+M0​​)].
(21)
At emptying, M=(1−ϵ)M0​, giving
vempty​=αgM0​​[−ϵ−2log(1−ϵ/2)].​
(22)
This is upward and positive for 0<ϵ<1. For no initial sand, ϵ=0, the emptying time is zero and the speed is zero. As the dry bucket mass tends to zero,
ϵ→1−lim​vempty​=αgM0​​(2log2−1).​
(23)
This finite limit refers to the idealized model; the endpoint itself would describe a massless empty bucket. For small ϵ, vempty​∼gM0​ϵ2/(4α), consistent with initially zero acceleration that grows as mass is lost.
