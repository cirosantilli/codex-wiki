Choose the emitted beam direction as positive x. In the original laboratory frame the sensor moves with velocity −0.2c; let the laser recede from it with velocity −βc. The collinear relativistic Doppler shift from source to laboratory and then to sensor gives
ν0​νsensor​​=0.81.2​​1+β1−β​​.
(10)
At the detection threshold this ratio is 633/1000. Hence
q=1.5(633/1000)2​=0.267126,β=1+q1−q​≃0.578375.​
(11)
The minimum collinear speed is therefore about 0.578c, away from the approaching sensor, while the laser is still aimed towards it. The source must recede fast enough to overcome the sensor's approach-induced blueshift. A larger recession speed gives a longer received wavelength, if the detector has no additional upper cutoff.
As a check, the required relative recession speed is (1−0.6332)c/(1+0.6332); relativistic velocity addition gives this same value from (β−0.2)c/(1−0.2β).
