For the frictional conversion of spin to rolling, consider the horizontal slipping stage on the rough surface, whose contact radius is now a, and neglect extra vertical impact or height-change energies at the transition. A friction impulse Jf​ changes Mv by Jf​ and clockwise spin Iω by −aJf​. Thus Iω+Mav is conserved. At the end vf​=aωf​, giving
vf​=2v0​+aω0​​,ωf​=2ω0​+v0​/a​.​
(16)
Because I=Ma2, the final total kinetic energy is Kf​=Mvf2​. Subtraction yields
ΔK=−4M​(v0​−aω0​)2=−16M(R−r)2ω02​​.​
(17)
The result is independent of the friction coefficient, provided sufficient contact time allows rolling without slipping to be regained. Initially v0​=bω0​<aω0​, so the contact point slips backwards. Friction acts forwards and can increase translational kinetic energy, while decreasing rotational kinetic energy by more. A finite vertical drop or inelastic landing would require additional geometric information; the boxed expression describes the horizontal rolling-conversion loss.
