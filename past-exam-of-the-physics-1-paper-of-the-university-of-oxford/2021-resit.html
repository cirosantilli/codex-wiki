Interpret h as the vertical drop of the cylinder's centre of mass, and initially take the suspended rod to be vertical. The moment of inertia of a uniform solid cylinder is I=mr2/2. Before impact, rolling without slipping gives ω=u/r, so conservation of mechanical energy gives
mgh=21​mu2+21​Ir2u2​=43​mu2,u=34gh​​.
(1)
Use the usual instantaneous smooth elastic collision model: the horizontal impact impulse passes through the cylinder's centre of mass, and the floor supplies negligible tangential impulse during impact. The cylinder's angular velocity is then unchanged during impact; its rotational kinetic energy does not transfer to the bob. The rod supplies no horizontal impulse when vertical. If v and V are the signed cylinder and bob velocities immediately afterwards, conservation of momentum and the unit coefficient of restitution give
mu=mv+2mV,V−v=u,v=−3u​,V=32u​.
(2)
During the subsequent simple pendulum swing, conservation of mechanical energy gives
21​(2m)V2=2mgh(1−cosθmax​),cosθmax​=2719​,θmax​=cos−12719​≃45.28∘.​
(3)
The cylinder generally slips immediately after this impact, which is consistent with imposing rolling without slipping only on the descent.
Impact convention matters. The floor-impulse dependence of a rolling-cylinder collision gives a different answer if a different constraint is imposed. If instead the floor enforces rolling without slipping throughout the impulsive collision, it supplies a nonzero horizontal impulse. The cylinder's effective translational mass is then m+I/r2=3m/2. Writing J for the impulse delivered to the bob gives (3m/2)(v−u)=−J, 2mV=J and V−v=u. Hence V=6u/7 and cosθmax​=25/49, giving 59.32∘. The original wording specifies the descent constraint but does not specify an impulsive floor-friction law; the first boxed value uses the smooth-impact convention.

Choose the heavier particle's outgoing velocity above the initial direction. Let its speed be V, and let the lighter particle have components (vcosθ,−vsinθ). Thus θ and ϕ are nonnegative deflections on opposite sides. Conservation of momentum gives
mu=mvcosθ+MVcosϕ,mvsinθ=MVsinϕ.
(4)
The elastic collision conserves kinetic energy:
mu2=mv2+MV2.
(5)
Taking the squared magnitude of the vector momentum equation yields
m2v2=m2u2+M2V2−2mMuVcosϕ.
(6)
Eliminate v2 with the kinetic energy equation. For a nontrivial collision, V=0, so
(M+m)V=2mucosϕ.
(7)
Substituting back into the two momentum components gives
vcosθ=m+Mu(m−Mcos2ϕ)​,vsinθ=m+MuMsin2ϕ​.
(8)
Therefore
tanθ=m/M−cos2ϕsin2ϕ​.​
(9)
The ratio alone does not determine the quadrant: for M>m, the lighter particle can move backwards. The two component equations select the correct 0≤θ≤π. At m/M=cos2ϕ, the outgoing direction is perpendicular to the initial direction; the displayed tangent has a pole there.

Let t=0 be the start of deposition and I0​=MR2/2 be the original moment of inertia of a uniform disc. Assume that all deposited sand sticks and remains at radius r, with negligible thickness and no losses. The sand's incoming angular momentum about the vertical axis is zero. Neither the bearing nor gravity supplies an axial torque, so conservation of angular momentum gives
I(t)=I0​+αr2t,I(t)ω(t)=I0​ω0​.
(10)
Consequently the angular velocity and angular acceleration are
ω(t)=I0​+αr2tI0​ω0​​=MR2+2αr2tMR2ω0​​,​ω˙(t)=−(I0​+αr2t)2αr2I0​ω0​​.​
(11)
The rotational kinetic energy is
K(t)=21​I(t)ω(t)2=2(I0​+αr2t)I02​ω02​​=K(0)I0​+αr2tI0​​.​
(12)
Thus, for αr2>0,
t1/2​=αr2I0​​=2αr2MR2​.​
(13)
This is mass-loaded rotation: the sticking process dissipates mechanical energy even though angular momentum is conserved. Indeed K˙=−αr2ω2/2. If r=0 or α=0, no additional axial moment of inertia is deposited and the nonzero initial rotational kinetic energy never halves.

In the inertial centre of mass frame, the Lagrangian mechanics description starts from kinetic energy minus potential energy:
L=21​m1​∣r˙1​∣2+21​m2​∣r˙2​∣2−V(∣r1​−r2​∣).
(14)
Define the two-particle relative coordinate r=r1​−r2​. The centre of mass condition gives
m1​r1​+m2​r2​=0,r1​=m1​+m2​m2​​r,r2​=−m1​+m2​m1​​r.
(15)
Substitution therefore gives
L=21​μ∣r˙∣2−V(r),μ=m1​+m2​m1​m2​​,r=∣r∣.​
(16)
The Euler-Lagrange equation is simply μr¨=−∇V(r). Thus center-of-mass reduction turns a two-body problem into a one-particle central force problem with reduced mass μ, reducing six position components to three relative components. In an arbitrary inertial frame one adds the independent centre of mass term 21​(m1​+m2​)∣R˙∣2; that free motion does not affect the relative solution. Since V depends only on separation, conservation of angular momentum further confines a nonradial relative trajectory to a plane.

Take positive velocity towards B, and interpret 100MHz as the emitted frequency in the probe's rest frame. The relativistic velocity-addition formula gives the probe's station-frame velocity:
uP​=1+(0.5)(0.5)0.5c+0.5c​=0.8c.
(20)
For reception at the station, photons propagate backwards while the probe recedes. The longitudinal relativistic Doppler effect gives
fstation​=1001+0.81−0.8​​MHz=3100​MHz≃33.33MHz.​
(21)
Before the probe overtakes B, their separation decreases. The probe's velocity in B's inertial frame is
uP∣B​=1−(0.8)(0.4)0.8c−0.4c​=1710​c.
(22)
The approaching-source relativistic Doppler effect therefore gives
fB​=1001−10/171+10/17​​MHz=100727​​MHz≃196.40MHz.​
(23)
An independent frame calculation gives the same result: forward photons have station-frame frequency 300MHz, and a receiver moving at 0.4c measures 3000.6/1.4​MHz. If reception occurs after the probe passes B, backwards photons instead give fB​=1007/27​MHz≃50.92MHz. The direction of propagation and the reception geometry are essential to choosing the Doppler factor.

Powered ascent. Use upward signed velocity, constant gravitational acceleration g=9.81ms−2, constant relative rocket exhaust speed u=2500ms−1, vertical flight from rest, no drag force, and no impulse at stage separation. The constant-g approximation is an explicit modelling assumption; a varying Newtonian gravitational field would require integrating a different ascent equation. Put m0​=540000kg and q=2500kgs−1. During ascent m(t)=m0​−qt, and momentum balance gives the rocket equation with gravity:
mv˙=uq−mg,v(t)=ulnm0​−qtm0​​−gt.
(24)
The launch rocket thrust uq=6.25×106N exceeds m0​g, so immediate lift-off is consistent. At ascent burnout the entire attached vehicle still has mass mb​=140000kg, not 40000kg; the latter is the separated first stage. The burn duration and endpoint speed are
tb​=2500400000​=160s,vb​=ulnmb​m0​​−gtb​=1805.22ms−1.​
(25)
Coasting height. Integrating the ascent velocity from zero height gives
z(t)=u[t−qm0​−qt​lnm0​−qtm0​​]−21​gt2.
(26)
For example this follows by substituting m=m0​−qt in ∫0t​ln[m0​/(m0​−qs)]ds. At separation,
zb​=u[tb​−qmb​​lnmb​m0​​]−21​gtb2​=85442.26m.
(27)
The separated stage then undergoes free fall in a constant gravitational field with initial upward speed vb​. Conservation of mechanical energy during this coast gives
H=zb​+2gvb2​​=251538.47m≃251.54km.​
(28)
Its smaller mass after separation does not change that coasting height.
Reserve burn. On descending to zi​=100000m, conservation of mechanical energy gives downward speed
wi​=2g(H−zi​)​=1724.29255ms−1.
(29)
Reset time to s=0 at reserve ignition. Now mi​=40000kg, mf​=14000kg and qr​=26000/92kgs−1. Downward ejection supplies upward rocket thrust. The upward signed velocity is
vr​(s)=−wi​+ulnmi​−qr​smi​​−gs.
(30)
Thus the formal free-flight burnout value is
vr​(92)=−wi​+uln1400040000​−92g=−2.25724ms−1,​
(31)
corresponding to a formal downward speed of 2.26ms−1. Keeping g=9.8ms−2 throughout instead gives 3.82ms−1 downward; premature rounding is especially damaging because three large velocity terms almost cancel.
The free-flight endpoint is below ground. Integrating the same reserve-burn velocity gives
zr​(s)=zi​−wi​s+u[s−qr​mi​−qr​s​lnmi​−qr​smi​​]−21​gs2.
(32)
At the stated endpoint this gives zr​(92)=−167.27m for g=9.81ms−2. The surface is crossed at approximately s=89.15s, before reserve burnout. The stage has already hit the surface, so a physical post-impact burnout speed cannot be inferred without a ground-contact model. The boxed velocity is the intended ideal continuation, not an achievable free-flight landing result under these exact assumptions. In particular, the given numbers do not describe an exact soft landing. The constant-rate retro-rocket landing must satisfy both its velocity and height conditions.

Axis theorems. The parallel axis theorem states
IO​=IG​+md2,
(33)
where IG​ is the moment of inertia about an axis through the centre of mass, IO​ is about a parallel axis, m is the body's total mass, and d is the perpendicular separation of the axes. Expanding ∫[(x−d)2+y2]dm proves the result because ∫xdm=0 at the centre of mass. The perpendicular axis theorem applies to a planar lamina in the xy plane:
Iz​=Ix​+Iy​.
(34)
All three axes meet at the same point, x,y lie in the lamina and z is normal to it; the identity follows from x2+y2 being the squared distance to the z axis. It is not a theorem for an arbitrary three-dimensional body.
Cylinder integrals. Put the cylinder's midpoint at z=0 and its symmetry axis along z. Its uniform mass density is ρ=m/(πr2l). The transverse moment of inertia of a uniform solid cylinder about the x axis is
I⊥,G​=ρ∫−l/2l/2​dz∫0r​sds∫02π​dφ(z2+s2sin2φ)=12ml2​+4mr2​.
(35)
Equivalently each thin circular slice has diametral moment of inertia r2dm/4, by the perpendicular axis theorem and symmetry, and shifting it by z adds z2dm. The parallel axis theorem then gives the transverse end-axis moment of inertia:
I⊥,G​=12m​(l2+3r2),I⊥,end​=12m​(4l2+3r2).​
(36)
The axial moment of inertia of a uniform solid cylinder is I∥​=mr2/2. Equating it to I⊥,G​ gives
l2=3r2,l=3​r.​
(37)
Here the source's principal axis means the cylinder's symmetry axis; its transverse centre axes are also principal axes. This equality describes a short cylinder rather than the thin-rod limit.
Hammer dynamics and forces. Treat the compact head as a point mass and neglect its intrinsic moment of inertia. Let θ be the displacement from downward vertical, and let er​ point along the rod from pivot to head, with eθ​ in the direction of increasing θ. Define
mT​=M+m,A=Ml+2ml​,d=mT​A​,I=Ml2+m(3l2​+4r2​).
(38)
The two external weights Mg and mg act at distances l and l/2 from the pivot. The frictionless pivot supplies a reaction R but no torque about its axis. The total restoring torque and rotational equation of motion are
τ=−gAsinθ,Iθ¨+gAsinθ=0.​
(39)
For completeness, Newton's second law for the centre of mass gives both components of the pivot force:
Rr​=−mT​dθ˙2−mT​gcosθ,Rθ​=mT​dθ¨+mT​gsinθ.​
(40)
The negative radial component points towards the pivot. These follow from the centre of mass acceleration −dθ˙2er​+dθ¨eθ​ and the total weight mT​g(cosθer​−sinθeθ​). They specify the external forces even when the oscillation is not small.
With the small-angle approximation, sinθ≃θ, this is simple harmonic motion. The physical pendulum has small-amplitude oscillation period
T0​=2πgAI​​=2πgl(M+m/2)Ml2+m(l2/3+r2/4)​​.​
(41)
For release from a finite amplitude 0<θ0​<π, conservation of mechanical energy instead gives 21​Iθ˙2=gA(cosθ−cosθ0​). Integrating a quarter-cycle gives the exact oscillation period
T(θ0​)=4gAI​​K(sin2θ0​​),K(k)=∫0π/2​1−k2sin2ψ​dψ​,
(42)
where K is the complete elliptic integral of the first kind; K(0)=π/2 recovers T0​.
For m=M/10 and r=l/20, compare with the simple pendulum value Ts​=2πl/g​:
Ts​T0​​=1+201​1+101​(31​+16001​)​​=5040049603​​≃0.992062.​
(43)
For small oscillations, the hammer's oscillation period is about 0.794% shorter. The extra rod weight adds restoring torque proportionately faster than it adds pivot moment of inertia.

Assume the station's mass remains fixed through an instantaneous kick, with subsequent motion governed only by the Earth's Newtonian gravitational field. Put κ=GME​, and work per unit station mass. The initial circular orbit has specific angular momentum j and specific orbital energy ε0​ given by
v02​=ro​κ​,j2=κro​,ε0​=−2ro​κ​.
(44)
Let δ denote the fractional increase of kinetic energy. The position and potential energy are unchanged during the kick, so
ε=ε0​+δ2ro​κ​=−2ro​κ(1−δ)​.
(45)
Unchanged angular momentum at unchanged position fixes the tangential velocity; the added kinetic energy must be radial. Thus this is a radial kick, not a purely tangential speed increase. The new orbital eccentricity follows from
e2=1+κ22εj2​=δ.
(46)
At either radial turning point, the Kepler radial energy equation gives ε=j2/(2r2)−κ/r. Its two roots, equivalently the periapsis and apoapsis of the elliptic orbit, are
rp​=1+δ​ro​​,ra​=1−δ​ro​​.​
(47)
For δ=0.01, e=0.1 and
rp​=1110​ro​,ra​=910​ro​.​
(48)
An inward or outward kick gives the same pair of radial turning points, although it places the station at a different phase of the orbit. These two finite positive radii require 0≤δ<1.

Let D be the separation of the Earth and Moon, and let x be the spacecraft's distance from the Earth's centre towards the Moon. On 0<x<D, the two gravitational forces oppose each other. Their equality, by Newton's law of universal gravitation, is
x2GME​​=(D−x)2GMM​​.
(52)
Taking the positive square root gives
x=ME​​+MM​​DME​​​=1+MM​/ME​​D​.​
(53)
The centre of mass of the two bodies is at xG​=DMM​/(ME​+MM​) from the Earth's centre. Thus the gravitational field null between two point masses lies a distance
x−xG​=D[1+MM​/ME​​1​−ME​+MM​MM​​]​
(54)
from their centre of mass, on the Moon's side. With ME​/MM​=81, x=9D/10 and xG​=D/82, so
x=3.456×108m,x−xG​=205182​D≃3.40917×108m.​
(55)
The point is 3.84×107m from the Moon. It is an instantaneous zero of the physical gravitational field, not a co-rotating Lagrange point: a spacecraft remaining fixed relative to the Earth-Moon line would need a nonzero centripetal acceleration. With zero net force it cannot follow that circular co-rotation.

Invariant and collision. For a particle of rest mass m, special relativity gives relativistic energy and momentum
E=γmc2,p=γmv,γ=1−v2/c2​1​.
(56)
Therefore the relativistic energy-momentum relation is
E2−p2c2=γ2m2c4(1−v2/c2)=m2c4.​
(57)
It is the squared Minkowski norm of the four-momentum, multiplied by c2, and hence is preserved by every Lorentz transformation. Explicitly a boost with speed U along x gives E′=Γ(E−Upx​), px′​=Γ(px​−UE/c2), with py′​=py​, pz′​=pz​. Expanding E′2−c2px′2​ gives E2−c2px2​ because Γ2(1−U2/c2)=1. Spatial rotations preserve p2, completing the invariance argument for all inertial frames.
For the incoming moving particle, v=3c/5 and γ=5/4. The total laboratory energy and momentum before the perfectly inelastic collision are
Ei​=(45​+2)m0​c2=413​m0​c2,pi​=45​m0​53c​=43​m0​c.
(58)
Four-momentum conservation identifies these with the final particle's energy and momentum. Its rest mass is therefore
m′=c2Ei2​−pi2​c2​​=4m0​​132−32​=10​m0​.​
(59)
The result exceeds the sum 3m0​ of the incoming rest masses; some initial kinetic energy has become internal rest energy. Since pc2/E=v for a massive particle,
