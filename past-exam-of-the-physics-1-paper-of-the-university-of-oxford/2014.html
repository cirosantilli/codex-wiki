A straight diameter at nonzero angle χ to the rotation axis moves the body toward that axis during the inward half of its trip. With no external axial torque, the total axial angular momentum of the Earth and body is conserved. The body's contribution to the moment of inertia decreases toward the centre, so the Earth spins slightly faster; the reverse happens on the outward half, restoring the original rotation rate at the far surface.
For a body of mass m constrained to follow a tunnel fixed in the Earth, an illustrative angular-momentum balance is
Ω(x)=Ω0​IE​+mx2sin2χIE​+mr02​sin2χ​.​
(7)
Here IE​ is the Earth's axial moment of inertia, treated as constant, and x is the signed distance along the diameter. A frictionless guide can still exert normal forces: these supply the transverse acceleration and exchange axial angular momentum with the rotating Earth. Simply allowing an unconstrained body to fall would not keep it inside a narrow nonpolar straight tunnel. The effect vanishes for χ=0 and is largest for an equatorial diameter.

The number of degrees of freedom is the number of independent coordinates needed to specify an allowed configuration locally. For N unconstrained point particles in three dimensions it is 3N; k independent regular holonomic constraints reduce it to 3N−k. A purely radial launch is a degenerate zero-angular-momentum trajectory through the central body and requires separate treatment of the collision.
