For a launch angle θ, the projectile motion equations give a flight time 2usinθ/g, a projectile range R=u2sin(2θ)/g, and an apex height u2sin2θ/(2g). Thus the ceiling requires sin2θ≤2gH/u2.
Set z=sin2θ. Then R2=4u4z(1−z)/g2, which increases up to z=1/2. The optimum is therefore z=min(1/2,2gH/u2), giving the projectile range under a ceiling
Rmax​={8H(u2/g−2H)​,u2/g,​0≤H≤u2/(4g),H≥u2/(4g).​​
(1)
The printed expression is valid in the first regime. A higher ceiling leaves the usual 45∘ launch admissible, so it cannot reduce the maximum range. The angle in the ceiling-limited regime is arcsin2gH/u2​. Equality at the roof is allowed in this ideal geometrical model; if touching the roof is forbidden, the same value is a supremum approached from below.

Let w=v1​−v2​ be the relative velocity and V=(M1​v1​+M2​v2​)/(M1​+M2​) the centre-of-mass velocity. Inverting gives
v1​=V+M1​+M2​M2​​w,v2​=V−M1​+M2​M1​​w.
(2)
Substitute into the sum of the two kinetic energies. The cross terms cancel, while the coefficient of ∣w∣2/2 is the reduced mass μ=M1​M2​/(M1​+M2​). Hence
T=21​(M1​+M2​)∣V∣2+21​μ∣w∣2.
(3)
The first term is translation of the centre of mass; the second is the kinetic energy measured in the centre-of-momentum frame. For an isolated perfectly inelastic collision, the bodies finish with a common velocity, while their total momentum and thus V are unchanged. The kinetic energy converted to internal forms is therefore
Ti​−Tf​=21​μv2,​
(4)
where v is their initial relative speed. Total energy remains conserved; it is the translational-plus-relative kinetic energy that decreases.

Measure position x along the ideal thin rod from its centre of mass. Its perpendicular distance from the inclined axis is ∣x∣sinα, and its linear mass density is M/L. The moment of inertia is therefore
I=LM​∫−L/2L/2​x2sin2αdx=12ML2​sin2α.​

Write the incoming particle speed as u>0, its final signed speed as v, and the rod's final centre-of-mass velocity along the impact direction as V. Let b be the signed impact distance from the centre, and choose the sign of the angular velocity Ω so that the contact point's velocity component is V+bΩ. The rod's central moment of inertia is I=ML2/12.
The impulse delivered to the rod is MV. Conservation of linear and angular momentum gives
u=v+V,IΩ=MbV.
(7)
Using these in conservation of kinetic energy,
u2=v2+V2+MI​Ω2⟹V=2+Mb2/I2u​,v=2+Mb2/I(Mb2/I)u​.
(8)
This is the equal-mass instance of the effective contact mass calculation. For u>0, both final speeds are nonnegative, so equal speeds require v=V=u/2. Therefore Mb2/I=2 and
∣b∣=6​L​.​
(9)
Both symmetric impact points lie on the rod, since L/6​<L/2.
More generally, the relative contact velocity immediately afterwards is
v−(V+bΩ)=u−(2+Mb2/I)V=−u.
(10)
Thus its magnitude equals the incoming relative speed u and its direction reverses. At the specified impact point, V+bΩ=3u/2 and v=u/2, which makes the reversal particularly explicit.

With metric convention (+−−−), the invariant interval is c2Δτ2=c2Δt2−∣Δr∣2 along an inertially moving clock. For constant speed v in the ground frame, ∣Δr∣=vΔt, so
Δt=γΔτ,γ=(1−v2/c2)−1/2.​
(11)
The aircraft records proper time Δτ=1000s. The ground duration is measured by a network of clocks at rest in the ideal ground frame, with Einstein synchronisation. Its excess is
Δt−Δτ=1000(γ−1)s≃10002c23002​s≃5.0×10−10s.
(12)
Thus the ground-frame duration is longer by about 0.50ns. This is the special-relativistic time dilation contribution under the stated constant-speed assumptions; gravitational potential differences and Earth's rotation are outside this model.

First work in the receiver's rest frame. If a source recedes at speed βc, two successive emissions separated by source proper time T0​ occur γT0​ apart in this frame. During that interval the source retreats by βcγT0​, so the arrival interval is TR​=γT0​(1+β). The longitudinal relativistic Doppler effect is consequently
ν0​νR​​=1+β1−β​​,λ0​λR​​=1−β1+β​​.​
(13)
A negative β describes approach and gives a blueshift.
Interpret 633nm as the laser's rest-frame wavelength and the stated detector velocity as measured in the initial laboratory frame. Choose +x from source to sensor, with detector velocity −0.2c. The required relative recession speed follows from
q=(1000/633)2,βrec​=q+1q−1​=0.4278687….
(14)
By relativistic velocity addition, the source velocity βS​c in the laboratory obeys (βS​+0.2)/(1+0.2βS​)=−βrec​. Hence
βS​=−1+0.2βrec​βrec​+0.2​=−0.578375….​
(15)
The laser must move away from the sensor at about 0.5784c in that laboratory frame. Although the sensor moves toward the source's initial location, the retreating source produces the required net redshift. The frame qualification matters: a velocity permanently fixed relative to the moving source would specify the Doppler factor already, rather than leave it adjustable.

During dt, the remaining rocket has mass M+dM and velocity v+dv, while the expelled positive mass −dM has velocity v−u to first order. Subtract the initial momentum Mv from their final momentum to obtain Mdv+udM. Equating this to the external impulse gives the rocket equation
F=Mv˙+uM˙.
(16)
With F=−Mg−kMv and M˙/M=−b, this reduces to v˙+kv=ub−g. Therefore, for initial velocity v0​ and k>0,
v(t)=kub−g​+(v0​−kub−g​)e−kt.
(17)
For a launch from rest,
v(t)=kub−g​(1−e−kt),b>g/u for upward launch.​
(18)
The inequality makes the initial upward acceleration positive. Equality gives no upward launch from rest. In the zero-drag limit, v=(ub−g)t. These expressions apply while the prescribed burn law and constant-g approximation remain valid.

The escaping sand initially has the bucket's velocity, so it produces no relative-velocity thrust. This distinction is essential in a leaking Atwood machine. With upward velocity v for the leaky side, the two bucket equations are
Mv˙=T−Mg,M0​v˙=M0​g−T.
(19)
Their sum gives v˙=g(M0​−M)/(M+M0​). Since M˙=−α, it follows that
dMdv​=αg​M+M0​M−M0​​.
(20)
Integrate from M0​, where the velocity is zero:
v(M)=αg​[M−M0​−2M0​log(2M0​M+M0​​)].
(21)
At emptying, M=(1−ϵ)M0​, giving
vempty​=αgM0​​[−ϵ−2log(1−ϵ/2)].​
(22)
This is upward and positive for 0<ϵ<1. For no initial sand, ϵ=0, the emptying time is zero and the speed is zero. As the dry bucket mass tends to zero,
ϵ→1−lim​vempty​=αgM0​​(2log2−1).​
(23)
This finite limit refers to the idealized model; the endpoint itself would describe a massless empty bucket. For small ϵ, vempty​∼gM0​ϵ2/(4α), consistent with initially zero acceleration that grows as mass is lost.

a OurBigBook.com
A central force exerts zero torque about its centre, so angular momentum is conserved and the motion is planar. In plane polar coordinates, J=mr2θ˙ and
T=21​m(r˙2+r2θ˙2),E=21​mr˙2+2mr2J2​+U(r).​
(24)
Use F=−U′ and either the radial Euler-Lagrange equation or differentiation of the energy away from turning points to get
mr¨=F(r)+mr3J2​.​
(25)
The radial acceleration is r¨−rθ˙2, so this is exactly m(r¨−rθ˙2)=F(r), not an additional physical outward force in an inertial frame. The variational derivation remains valid at turning points where dividing an energy derivative by r˙ would be illegitimate.

b OurBigBook.com
The effective potential includes the angular part of the kinetic energy. Taking U(0)=0 gives U=kr4/4, hence
Ueff​(r)=2mr2J2​+4kr4​.
(26)
For J=0, this diverges at both zero and infinity and has a unique minimum. Finite-energy motion therefore remains between two radial turning points, or at the minimum itself. For J=0, the centrifugal barrier is absent, but U→∞ still bounds every finite-energy trajectory; radial motion can pass through the force centre.
A circular orbit requires Ueff′​(rc​)=0, so
rc​=(mkJ2​)1/6,Ec​=43​krc4​=43​k1/3(mJ2​)2/3.​
(27)
It is stable because Ueff′′​(rc​)=6krc2​>0. These are nonzero-radius orbits for J=0; J=0 also permits the stationary particle at the origin. Boundedness does not mean that every noncircular orbit is closed.

The diagram places the force centre on the circumference, not at the centre of the circle. Choose the polar axis through the circle's geometric centre. Its equation is r=2acosθ, so u=secθ/(2a) and
u′′+u=asec3θ​=8a2u3.
(28)
Substitute into Binet's equation:
F(1/u)=−mJ2u2​(u′′+u)=−m8J2a2​u5.
(29)
Consequently a circular orbit through the force centre is generated by
n=−5,C=m8J2a2​,F(r)=−mr58J2a2​.​
(30)
The result applies to the regular arc where r>0 and J=0. The force is singular at the point where the geometrical circle meets the origin, so the curve is not a regular periodic orbit continued through that point. Indeed, U=−C/(4r4) and this trajectory has zero total energy, with kinetic and negative potential terms diverging as the centre is approached.

A holonomic constraint can be expressed as f(q1​,…,qn​,t)=0, rather than as a genuinely nonintegrable relation among velocities. A fixed rod length is an example. Rolling along a single straight incline also gives an integrable relation s+Rθ=constant with the sign convention used below; arbitrary rolling in more dimensions need not be holonomic.

Take s positive uphill, X positive to the right, and the cylinder's rotation positive anticlockwise. Contact with the fixed-angle surface determines its normal position; no slipping gives θ˙=−s˙/R. These two constraints leave X,s as the two independent coordinates of the cylinder rolling on a freely translating wedge.
The cylinder centre is at (X+scosα−Rsinα, ssinα+Rcosα). Since the angle is constant, its squared speed is X˙2+2X˙s˙cosα+s˙2. Including the block's kinetic energy and Ic​=Mc​R2/2, and dropping a constant from the potential, gives
L=21​(Mb​+Mc​)X˙2+Mc​cosαX˙s˙+43​Mc​s˙2−Mc​gssinα.​
(32)
The cyclic coordinate is X. Its conserved conjugate momentum is pX​=(Mb​+Mc​)X˙+Mc​cosαs˙, the total horizontal momentum. The two Euler-Lagrange equations are
(Mb​+Mc​)X¨+Mc​cosαs¨=0,cosαX¨+23​s¨=−gsinα.
(33)
Eliminating the accelerations, with D=3(Mb​+Mc​)−2Mc​cos2α>0, gives
s¨=−D2(Mb​+Mc​)gsinα​,X¨=D2Mc​gsinαcosα​.​
(34)
Thus the cylinder rolls downhill and the wedge accelerates rightward. For Mb​≫Mc​, X¨→0 and s¨→−(2/3)gsinα, the ordinary fixed-incline result.

With the wedge fixed, let r be the signed gap between the cylinder bottom and the surface, positive outwards. The centre height is ssinα+(R+r)cosα. Retaining all three coordinates before imposing constraints gives
L=21​Mc​(r˙2+s˙2)+21​Ic​θ˙2−Mc​g[ssinα+(R+r)cosα].
(35)
Use the holonomic constraints f1​=r=0 and f2​=s+Rθ−s0​=0. With the convention d(∂L/∂q˙​)/dt−∂L/∂q=∑j​λj​∂fj​/∂q, the Lagrange multiplier equations are
Mc​r¨+Mc​gcosα=λ1​,Mc​s¨+Mc​gsinα=λ2​,Ic​θ¨=Rλ2​.
(36)
The constraint gradients identify λ1​=N, the normal reaction, and λ2​=f, the uphill static friction force. The latter supplies the rolling torque. Setting r¨=0 and θ¨=−s¨/R yields
N=Mc​gcosα,s¨=−32​gsinα,f=31​Mc​gsinα.​
(37)
The static friction condition ∣f∣≤μs​N therefore becomes
μs​≥31​tanα,​
(38)
for 0≤α<π/2. Static friction is required to create rotation, but does not dissipate energy here because the contact point is instantaneously at rest on the fixed plane.

For a boost along +x at speed v, the energy-momentum four-vector transforms as
E′=γ(E−vpx​),px′​=γ(px​−vE/c2),py′​=py​,pz′​=pz​.​
(39)
The total four-vector is the sum over the particles. Write E=∑i​Ei​ and P=∑i​pi​. A centre-of-momentum rest frame exists when this total is timelike, with VCM​=c2P/E. Boost along P and use P=EVCM​/c2:
ECM​=γCM​(E−VCM​P)=E/γCM​.
(40)
Therefore
γCM​=ECM​∑i​Ei​​=1−VCM2​/c2​1​.​
(41)
The timelike qualification is needed: exactly parallel photons alone have null total four-momentum and no such rest frame.

W2=(mp​c2+Eγ​)2−Eγ2​=mp2​c4+2mp​c2Eγ​.

At threshold, the pion is at rest in the final centre-of-momentum frame, which moves in the laboratory with
β=mp​c2+Eγth​Eγth​​=0.1336592…,γ=(mp​+mπ​)c2mp​c2+Eγth​​=1.0090539….

E±​=2mπ​c2​γ(1±β).
