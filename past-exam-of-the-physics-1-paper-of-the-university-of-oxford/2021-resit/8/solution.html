Axis theorems. The parallel axis theorem states
IO​=IG​+md2,
(33)
where IG​ is the moment of inertia about an axis through the centre of mass, IO​ is about a parallel axis, m is the body's total mass, and d is the perpendicular separation of the axes. Expanding ∫[(x−d)2+y2]dm proves the result because ∫xdm=0 at the centre of mass. The perpendicular axis theorem applies to a planar lamina in the xy plane:
Iz​=Ix​+Iy​.
(34)
All three axes meet at the same point, x,y lie in the lamina and z is normal to it; the identity follows from x2+y2 being the squared distance to the z axis. It is not a theorem for an arbitrary three-dimensional body.
Cylinder integrals. Put the cylinder's midpoint at z=0 and its symmetry axis along z. Its uniform mass density is ρ=m/(πr2l). The transverse moment of inertia of a uniform solid cylinder about the x axis is
I⊥,G​=ρ∫−l/2l/2​dz∫0r​sds∫02π​dφ(z2+s2sin2φ)=12ml2​+4mr2​.
(35)
Equivalently each thin circular slice has diametral moment of inertia r2dm/4, by the perpendicular axis theorem and symmetry, and shifting it by z adds z2dm. The parallel axis theorem then gives the transverse end-axis moment of inertia:
I⊥,G​=12m​(l2+3r2),I⊥,end​=12m​(4l2+3r2).​
(36)
The axial moment of inertia of a uniform solid cylinder is I∥​=mr2/2. Equating it to I⊥,G​ gives
l2=3r2,l=3​r.​
(37)
Here the source's principal axis means the cylinder's symmetry axis; its transverse centre axes are also principal axes. This equality describes a short cylinder rather than the thin-rod limit.
Hammer dynamics and forces. Treat the compact head as a point mass and neglect its intrinsic moment of inertia. Let θ be the displacement from downward vertical, and let er​ point along the rod from pivot to head, with eθ​ in the direction of increasing θ. Define
mT​=M+m,A=Ml+2ml​,d=mT​A​,I=Ml2+m(3l2​+4r2​).
(38)
The two external weights Mg and mg act at distances l and l/2 from the pivot. The frictionless pivot supplies a reaction R but no torque about its axis. The total restoring torque and rotational equation of motion are
τ=−gAsinθ,Iθ¨+gAsinθ=0.​
(39)
For completeness, Newton's second law for the centre of mass gives both components of the pivot force:
Rr​=−mT​dθ˙2−mT​gcosθ,Rθ​=mT​dθ¨+mT​gsinθ.​
(40)
The negative radial component points towards the pivot. These follow from the centre of mass acceleration −dθ˙2er​+dθ¨eθ​ and the total weight mT​g(cosθer​−sinθeθ​). They specify the external forces even when the oscillation is not small.
With the small-angle approximation, sinθ≃θ, this is simple harmonic motion. The physical pendulum has small-amplitude oscillation period
T0​=2πgAI​​=2πgl(M+m/2)Ml2+m(l2/3+r2/4)​​.​
(41)
For release from a finite amplitude 0<θ0​<π, conservation of mechanical energy instead gives 21​Iθ˙2=gA(cosθ−cosθ0​). Integrating a quarter-cycle gives the exact oscillation period
T(θ0​)=4gAI​​K(sin2θ0​​),K(k)=∫0π/2​1−k2sin2ψ​dψ​,
(42)
where K is the complete elliptic integral of the first kind; K(0)=π/2 recovers T0​.
For m=M/10 and r=l/20, compare with the simple pendulum value Ts​=2πl/g​:
Ts​T0​​=1+201​1+101​(31​+16001​)​​=5040049603​​≃0.992062.​
