The disc carries mass Md​=2M/3 and the thin rim carries Mr​=M/3. About the wheel's central normal axis, add the moment of inertia of a uniform disc and the thin rim's moment of inertia:
Iz​=21​Md​R2+Mr​R2=21​32M​R2+3M​R2=32​MR2.​
(2)
Treat the wheel as a thin planar lamina, as implied by the disc-and-thin-rim model. Interpret the requested perpendicular axis as perpendicular to the wheel's original axis and tangent to the rim: if the wheel lies in the xy plane, choose the tangent line x=R,z=0, parallel to the y diameter. By rotational symmetry, the two diametral moments of inertia are equal. The perpendicular axis theorem therefore gives
Ix​=Iy​=2Iz​​=31​MR2.
(3)
The parallel axis theorem then gives the tangent-axis inertia of an axisymmetric lamina:
Itangent,in plane​=Iy​+MR2=34​MR2.​
(4)
The axis direction matters. If “perpendicular” were intended to mean perpendicular to the wheel's plane, an axis through a rim point would instead be parallel to the original wheel axis, giving Irim,normal​=Iz​+MR2=5MR2/3. The 4MR2/3 answer uses a tangent axis perpendicular to the original axis; the 5MR2/3 value belongs to the different, explicitly identified normal axis. Finite wheel thickness would require additional information for the in-plane moment of inertia.
