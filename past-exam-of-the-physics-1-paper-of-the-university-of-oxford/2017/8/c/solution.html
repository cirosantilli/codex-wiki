Choose positive horizontal velocity to the right and positive spin angular velocity anticlockwise, as in the source drawing. At the bottom of the sphere, the rotational velocity is then to the right: the initial tangential contact-point velocity is
w1​=v1​+aΩ1​.
(29)
For the illustrated positive v1​,Ω1​, the contact slips to the right, so the tangential contact force acts leftward. The upward normal force acts through the sphere centre and produces no spin torque. The tangential contact force acts at lever arm a and produces a clockwise torque, reducing the positive spin. The weight acts vertically downward through the centre of mass.
During the short impact the contact impulses dominate the weight's impulse. In an ideal elastic rough contact, the tangential deformation can store and return energy; its contact force need not be a dissipative sliding-friction law throughout the collision. For other signed incoming states, reverse the initial tangential force if w1​<0; if w1​=0, no tangential impulse is required.
