Write the sphere's mass as M and use I=2Ma2/5 from b. Let Kn​ and Kt​ be the upward and rightward contact impulses. The initial vertical velocity is −u1​ and the outgoing vertical velocity is u2​. Perfect normal elasticity gives
u2​=u1​,Kn​=M(u2​+u1​)=2Mu1​.
(30)
The tangential and angular impulse equations are
M(v2​−v1​)=Kt​,I(Ω2​−Ω1​)=aKt​.
(31)
For the ideal elastic rough-sphere bounce, the tangential translational plus rotational kinetic energy is restored on separation. Substituting these two equations into that requirement gives
0=Kt​(v1​+aΩ1​)+2Kt2​​(M1​+Ia2​).
(32)
The interacting rough-contact branch, rather than the smooth branch Kt​=0, therefore has
Kt​=−1/M+a2/I2(v1​+aΩ1​)​=−74​M(v1​+aΩ1​).
(33)
Consequently, with all tangential quantities signed in the convention of c,
u2​=u1​,v2​=73v1​−4aΩ1​​,Ω2​=−7a10v1​+3aΩ1​​.​
(34)
In particular, v2​+aΩ2​=−(v1​+aΩ1​): the tangential contact-point velocity reverses. The outgoing v2​ may be negative; the sphere rebounds backward when aΩ1​>3v1​/4. Substitution also checks both total kinetic energy and angular momentum about the contact point, IΩ−Mav.
This result uses the conventional perfectly elastic, fully rough contact idealization. The word rough alone, with an unspecified finite coefficient of friction, would not determine the outgoing tangential state. For example, imposing sticking at separation would instead give Kt​=−2M(v1​+aΩ1​)/7 and dissipate M(v1​+aΩ1​)2/7, contradicting total elasticity unless w1​=0. The displayed elastic branch requires enough tangential impulse; a Coulomb bound ∣Kt​∣≤μKn​ would at least require μ≥2∣v1​+aΩ1​∣/(7u1​).
