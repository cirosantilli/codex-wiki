Take the upward velocity as positive during ascent. Newton's second law with linear drag gives
v˙+kv=−g,v(0)=v0​.
(1)
Multiplication by the integrating factor ekt and integration give
v(t)=(v0​+kg​)e−kt−kg​.
(2)
The maximum height occurs at the first zero of this velocity, hence
ttop​=k1​log(1+gkv0​​).​
(3)
Here k has units of inverse time. The limit k→0 gives v0​/g, and positive drag shortens the ascent relative to motion in a vacuum. The expression applies only up to the turning point; the drag force opposes the subsequent downward motion.

Let M˙=500kgs−1 and V=10ms−1. Incoming material has zero horizontal momentum, while incorporation requires horizontal momentum VdM. Thus the engine supplies force F=VM˙, and its power is
F=5000N,P=FV=M˙V2=50kW.​
(4)
No duration or loading distance is given, so a single total work cannot be assigned. Over an interval t in seconds,
W=50,000tJ,ΔKhorizontal​=21​M˙V2t=25,000tJ.​
(5)
The remaining 25,000tJ becomes internal energy in the inelastic collision that brings the falling material to the wagon's horizontal velocity. Vertical falling kinetic energy, if any, is a separate contribution to dissipation; it does not alter this horizontal work balance. The source alternates between stone and coal; the calculation depends only on the given loading rate.

Let a,b be the outgoing velocities. In the nonrelativistic elastic collision, equal masses give
v=a+b,v2=a2+b2
(10)
by conservation of momentum and conservation of energy. Squaring the first relation and comparing with the second yields
a⋅b=0.​
(11)
If both particles move, their paths are perpendicular. If either velocity vanishes, its direction is undefined and the other particle carries all the incident momentum and kinetic energy. This conclusion depends on the stated v≪c approximation.

Treat the carriage as translating along the circular track, neglecting wheel rotational kinetic energy and its finite size. At the top, gravity and the inward normal force supply the centripetal acceleration:
mg+N=rmvtop2​​.
(14)
For unilateral contact, N≥0, so the limiting speed is vtop2​=gr. Conservation of energy from rest at height h then gives
mgh=2mgr+21​mgr,hmin​=25​r.​
(15)
This top contact condition is the most restrictive: at angle ϕ measured from the bottom, N/(mg)=2h/r−2+3cosϕ, whose minimum occurs at the top. A mechanically captive carriage able to sustain an outward rail reaction has a different constraint; the result here uses the usual requirement of positive normal contact.

Choose parallel axes along z, one through the centre of mass and one displaced transversely by a. Write the perpendicular position as r⊥​=r⊥′​+a. Direct expansion of the moment of inertia gives
I=∫∣r⊥′​+a∣2dm=IG​+2a⋅∫r⊥′​dm+ma2.
(16)
The integral in the cross term is zero by the definition of the centre of mass. Therefore the parallel axis theorem is
I=IG​+ma2.​
(17)
The axes must be parallel and the reference axis must pass through the centre of mass; the formula is not generally valid between arbitrary parallel axes using either axis as the reference.

About a chosen origin O, a particle has angular momentum L=r×p. A body has L=∑i​ri​×pi​, or the corresponding integral for a continuous mass distribution. For a fixed origin in an inertial frame,
dtdL​=τexternal​;τexternal​=0 ⟹ L=constant.​

ii OurBigBook.com
At a fixed launch radius the escape threshold is zero total mechanical energy. It depends on the initial speed alone:
vesc​=2GM/R​independent of outward launch direction in the nonrotating model.​
(32)
A direction aimed into the planet can of course cause impact before escape. If the planet rotates, the ground-relative launch velocity must be added to Ω×R to obtain the inertial velocity. The condition becomes ∣u+Ω×R∣2≥2GM/R: prograde launch needs less ground-relative speed than retrograde launch. For a spherical planet the threshold expressed as inertial speed at the same radius remains independent of direction.

Let H be the common drop height in the small-gap, small-ball approximation and put u=2gH​. Use upward positive velocity. Just before ground impact both balls have velocity −u. The massive fixed ground reverses the lower ball's velocity elastically, so immediately before the ball-ball encounter
u1​=−u,u2​=+u.
(33)
One-dimensional conservation of momentum and reversal of relative velocity in an elastic collision give
v1​=m1​+m2​3m2​−m1​​u,v2​=m1​+m2​m2​−3m1​​u.​
(34)
The sequence is shown for the optimal ratio in b. At that ratio the lower ball stops and the upper ball departs upward, so no immediate third encounter needs to be included. Finite gaps introduce unequal contact times and gravitational corrections; no gap or ball radii are supplied to calculate those corrections.

Write r=m1​/m2​>0. The upper ball's fraction of the available kinetic energy after the separated elastic collisions is
f(r)=21​(m1​+m2​)u221​m1​v12​​=(1+r)3r(3−r)2​,1−f(r)=(1+r)3(1−3r)2​≥0.
