For the eccentric circular-hole disk inertia, let D=πρR2 be the mass of the complete disc before removing material, h=πρro2​ the missing mass, and W=D−h the wheel's remaining mass. Denote the complete disc's centre by C and the axle/hole centre by H; CH=ro​. Assume the hole fits inside the disc, 0<ro​<R/2. The parallel axis theorem gives the full disc's moment of inertia about H as 21​DR2+Dro2​. Subtract the absent little disc about its own centre H:
IH​=21​DR2+Dro2​−21​hro2​=2πρ​(R4+2R2ro2​−ro4​).​
(29)
The axle is a support, not part of the rotating wheel's stated mass. Its frictionless bearing does not alter this moment of inertia. For fixed-axis angular velocity ω,
TH​=21​IH​ω2=4πρω2​(R4+2R2ro2​−ro4​).​
(30)
The missing material must be subtracted with its actual displacement relative to the axis; treating the wheel as a concentric annulus would give the wrong result.
