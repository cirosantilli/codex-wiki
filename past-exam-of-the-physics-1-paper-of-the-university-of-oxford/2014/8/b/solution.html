Let q be the transferred fuel mass. Before any engine reversal, rocket A has mass a=mA​+M−q and velocity
vA​(q)=u0​lnmA​+M−qmA​+M​.
(22)
For exhaust collection by a second rocket, under the stipulated eventual capture of all exhaust, final conservation of momentum for the complete initially stationary system gives mA​vAf​+(mB​+M)vBf​=0. Thus
vAf​=u0​lnmA​mA​+M​,vBf​=−mB​+MmA​​vAf​,vrel​=u0​mB​+MmA​+mB​+M​lnmA​mA​+M​.​
(23)
Capture is inelastic: no kinetic energy conservation for B is assumed. Finite exhaust flight time changes the intermediate sharing of momentum but not this endpoint balance if the stated complete capture actually occurs.
The ballistic exhaust interception condition is a physical restriction on that idealization. For purely ballistic exhaust to reach a receding collector to the rear, its laboratory velocity must be more negative than B's while interception occurs. In the negligible-flight-time approximation this requires vA​−u0​<vB​. Very large fuel ratios need not meet that condition throughout a complete burn; the boxed endpoint formula is conditional on the prompt's complete-collection stipulation, not an unconditional prediction for every ballistic two-rocket geometry.
