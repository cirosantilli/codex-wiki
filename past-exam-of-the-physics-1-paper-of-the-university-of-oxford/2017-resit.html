During the short collision, the pivot's impulse has zero torque about the pivot, so angular momentum conservation about that point gives
mvL=IΩ,I=3ML2​.
(1)
The bullet stops rather than becoming embedded: immediately after impact only the rod contributes to the kinetic energy. Because this is an elastic collision,
21​mv2=21​IΩ2.
(2)
Eliminating Ω yields mL2=I: the projectile mass equals the effective impact mass of a pivoted body, I/L2. Hence the bullet has one third of the rod's mass, and
m=3M​,Ω=Lv​.​
(3)
Ordinary momentum conservation for the bullet and rod together would be incorrect: the fixed pivot supplies an external impulse. The short-impact approximation neglects the change in gravitational potential energy during impact.

The source's tangential arrows a,c respectively point along and against the original motion; b,d point radially outward and inward. Write r0​ for the initial orbital radius, vc​=GM/r0​​ for the circular orbital speed, and δ>0 for the small added speed. The resulting trajectories follow the velocity kick on a circular Kepler orbit formulas. Here orbital energy includes kinetic energy and gravitational potential energy; angular momentum refers to the central object.

Adding tangential velocity along the existing motion increases both orbital energy and angular momentum:
ΔE=mvc​δ+21​mδ2>0,ΔL=mr0​δ>0.
(4)
For a sufficiently small kick the Kepler orbit remains an ellipse. At the kick point its radial velocity vanishes and its tangential speed exceeds the circular orbital speed, so that point becomes the pericentre. The ellipse extends farther outward than the original circle.

b OurBigBook.com
A radial outward kick has no angular impulse about the centre. Therefore
ΔL=0,ΔE=21​mδ2>0.​
(5)
The initial position is on the outward-moving portion of a bound elliptic Kepler orbit, between pericentre and apocentre, rather than at either turning point. The eccentricity is e=δ/vc​ and the semilatus rectum remains r0​. The orbital energy change is second order: it vanishes to first order in the impulse but is positive for any nonzero kick.

For planets with negligible mass compared with the Sun, Kepler's third law gives T2∝a3, where a is the semi-major axis. Relative to Earth,
a⊕​aMercury​​=0.242/3≃0.386,a⊕​aJupiter​​=11.92/3≃5.21.​

In the pion rest frame, momentum conservation makes the muon and neutrino momenta opposite, with common magnitude p. For a massless neutrino, Eν​=pc; energy conservation and the relativistic energy-momentum relation give
Eμ​+pc=mπ​c2,Eμ2​−p2c2=mμ2​c4.
(8)
Substitute pc=mπ​c2−Eμ​ into the second equation. The Eμ2​ terms cancel, leaving
Eμ​=2mπ​mπ2​+mμ2​​c2.​
(9)
For completeness, the complementary relativistic two-body decay energy is Eν​=(mπ2​−mμ2​)c2/(2mπ​). The result is a total energy, including the muon rest energy, rather than its kinetic energy.

Treat the ladder as a uniform slender rigid body. While both contacts remain imposed, its centre of mass is (lsinθ,lcosθ), with speed l∣θ˙∣. Its central moment of inertia is m(2l)2/12=ml2/3. Adding translational and rotational kinetic energy gives
K=21​ml2θ˙2+21​3ml2​θ˙2=32​ml2θ˙2.
(10)
Taking the floor as zero gravitational potential energy, the Lagrangian, conjugate momentum and Euler-Lagrange equation are
L=32​ml2θ˙2−mglcosθ,pθ​=34​ml2θ˙,θ¨=4l3g​sinθ.​
(11)
The positive sign is appropriate: gravity increases the angle from the vertical. These are the constrained equations of a frictionless sliding ladder. Real wall contact is unilateral: a wall cannot pull the ladder. Once the computed normal force would become negative, the two-contact model ceases to apply; the question explicitly assumes that contact persists.

Write ω for the imposed constant angular velocity. In inertial polar coordinates, the bead has v2=r˙2+ω2r2. Its constrained Lagrangian is
L=2m​(r˙2+ω2r2),r¨=ω2r.
(12)
The reduced Lagrangian has no explicit time dependence, so its Lagrangian energy function is conserved:
J=r˙∂r˙∂L​−L=2m​(r˙2−ω2r2).​
(13)
This is the Jacobi integral J=E−ωLz​, since the bead's actual inertial energy and axial angular momentum are
E=2m​(r˙2+ω2r2),Lz​=mωr2.
(14)
The motor maintaining the stick's angular velocity supplies torque and does work. Indeed E˙=2mω2rr˙=ωL˙z​, generally nonzero. A time-independent reduced Lagrangian therefore need not imply conservation of the bead's inertial kinetic energy when its imposed constraint is moving.

Choose upward velocity positive during ascent. Gravity and linear drag both point downward, giving the linear differential equation
mv˙=−mg−αv,v(t)=(v0​+αmg​)e−αt/m−αmg​.
(15)
At the highest point v=0, so
tmax​=αm​log(1+mgαv0​​).​
(16)
The vertical ascent under linear drag time is smaller than v0​/g for α>0, because log(1+x)<x. Its zero-drag limit is v0​/g, as required by constant-gravity projectile motion.

Choose downward velocity v positive. The remaining rocket mass is m(t)=m0​−αt, and the upward rocket thrust has magnitude αu, where u>0 is the relative rocket exhaust speed. The rocket equation including gravity is
mv˙=mg−αu,v˙=g−m0​−αtαu​.​

This follows by applying momentum conservation to the rocket and its just-expelled rocket propellant, with gravity supplying the external impulse. Applying d(mv)/dt=mg to the rocket alone would omit the exhaust momentum flux.

For two particles, let M=m1​+m2​ and Vcm​=(m1​v1​+m2​v2​)/M. The centre-of-mass frame velocities are vi∗​=vi​−Vcm​, so m1​v1∗​+m2​v2∗​=0. Thus before and after an isolated collision the two momentum vectors are equal and opposite. For an elastic collision their common magnitude is also unchanged: only their direction rotates.
The kinetic energy decomposition about the center of mass separates unchanged bulk translation from relative motion:
K=21​MVcm2​+21​μ∣v1​−v2​∣2,μ=Mm1​m2​​.
(24)
The reduced mass μ reduces the calculation to one relative velocity. After solving the geometry in the centre-of-mass frame, add Vcm​ to each velocity to return to the laboratory. The four panels below illustrate this for the subsequent example.

b OurBigBook.com
For equal masses m, the reduced mass is m/2. The kinetic energy of their centre of mass cannot be removed by an isolated collision, since total momentum is fixed. Therefore the maximum energy available for conversion to internal forms is the initial relative kinetic energy,
Kavailable​=4m​∣v1​−v2​∣2.​
(25)
It is attained by a perfectly inelastic collision in which the final relative velocity is zero. This concerns the convertible initial kinetic energy, not an arbitrary pre-existing internal energy reservoir.

Einstein's postulates state that the laws of physics have the same form in every inertial frame, and that light in vacuum has the same speed of light c in every inertial frame, independently of the motion of its source. For a primed frame moving with velocity vex​ relative to an unprimed frame, with coincident origins at t=t′=0, the Lorentz transformations are
x′=γ(x−vt),t′=γ(t−vx/c2),y′=y,z′=z,γ=(1−v2/c2)−1/2.​
(30)
The following worldlines use Earth-frame time in years and distance in light years, so numerically c=1. The instantaneous turnaround is the idealized limit of a short acceleration stage; the elapsed proper time is calculated along each inertial segment.

a OurBigBook.com
For a planar lamina in the xy-plane and three mutually perpendicular axes through one common point, the perpendicular axis theorem is Iz​=Ix​+Iy​. Indeed Iz​=∫(x2+y2)dm, while Ix​=∫y2dm and Iy​=∫x2dm. Planarity and the common axis intersection are essential.
For two parallel axes, one through the centre of mass G and one a perpendicular distance d away, the parallel axis theorem is
I=IG​+Md2,​
(35)
where M is the total mass. Expanding the squared distance in the defining moment of inertia integral leaves a cross term proportional to ∫rG​dm=0. The second axis need not pass through the body, and uniform density is not required, but the reference axis for IG​ must pass through its centre of mass.
