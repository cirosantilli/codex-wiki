Neglecting the planets' masses compared with the Sun's mass, Newton's law of universal gravitation supplies the centripetal acceleration of each circular orbit:
r2GM⊙​​=T24π2r​.
(1)
Thus Kepler's third law, calibrated by the Earth's orbital period of one year and orbital radius of one astronomical unit, gives
1yrT​=(1AUr​)3/2,TJ​=5.23/2=11.86yr,TS​=9.53/2=29.28yr.​
(2)
Both orbits are taken to have the same direction. Their relative angular speed is 2π(1/TJ​−1/TS​), so the Sun-centred interval between successive conjunctions is the synodic period
Tsyn​=1/TJ​−1/TS​1​=19.93yr.​
(3)
Using the supplied rounded orbital radii explains the small difference from the commonly quoted approximately twenty-year interval.
From the Earth, the two lines of sight are displaced differently because Jupiter and Saturn are at different distances. The Earth's annual motion along its orbit therefore shifts the dates of apparent conjunctions by amounts of order months. The broad recurrence remains about twenty years, but successive apparent conjunctions need not be equally spaced: apparent retrograde motion can produce a group of three conjunctions separated by months during one encounter. The Earth's daily rotation adds a much smaller change of viewpoint. This is a geometrical comparison in the stipulated coplanar model; daylight and the horizon can prevent a particular conjunction from being visible at a chosen surface location.
