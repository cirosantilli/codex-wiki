Take +y upwards in the source diagram. Write w for B's incoming speed, so the initial velocities are
uA​=(−u,0),uB​=(23​​w,21​w).
(45)
A contact-direction assumption is needed for a unique answer. The diagram specifies the incoming velocities but does not specify the contact normal or an outgoing angle. First adopt the usual intended model: the impulse is horizontal, so neither particle changes its y component of velocity. This is the smooth oblique collision model with a horizontal line of centres at impact. Bringing A to rest in the x direction then brings it entirely to rest.
Let b be B's outgoing x component of velocity. Conservation of momentum and conservation of kinetic energy give
−3u+23​​w=b,3u2+w2=b2+41​w2.
(46)
Put q=(3​/2)w. The second equation becomes 3u2+q2=(q−3u)2, so q=u. Therefore
w=3​2u​,vA​=(0,0),vB​=(−2u,3​u​).​
(47)
The outgoing kinetic energies are
KA​=0,KB​=21​m(4u2+3u2​)=613​mu2.​
(48)
The outgoing speed of B is u13/3​, directed above the negative x axis at angle tan−1[1/(23​)]≃16.1∘. The initial kinetic energy is also (3/2+2/3)mu2=13mu2/6, as required for an elastic collision.
For the perfectly inelastic collision with these same incoming velocities, both particles share a common outgoing velocity. Conservation of momentum gives
V=4m3muA​+muB​​=(−2u​,43​u​),∣V∣=43​13​​u.​
(49)
Its direction is again 16.1∘ above the negative x axis. The separate translational kinetic energies of the constituents and their total are
KA​=3213​mu2,KB​=9613​mu2,Kfinal​=2413​mu2.​
(50)
The loss of translational kinetic energy is
Kinitial​−Kfinal​=813​mu2.​
(51)
This becomes internal energy, for example deformation and heating; total energy is conserved. In the elastic collision no kinetic energy is lost and the particles separate with different velocities. In the perfectly inelastic collision relative motion disappears and the common velocity is the original centre of mass velocity.
The limitation of the printed data can be made explicit. Without the horizontal-impulse assumption, write the final velocity of A as (0,h). Then conservation of momentum determines
vB​=(23​​w−3u,21​w−3h),
(52)
and conservation of kinetic energy gives only
w(3​u+h)=2u2+4h2.​
(53)
There is a family of possibilities. For a concrete second solution, h=u/3​ gives
w=653​​u,vA​=(0,3​u​),vB​=(−47​u,−1273​​u).
(54)
These velocities conserve both components of momentum and conserve kinetic energy, and they satisfy the specified vanishing x component for A, while requiring a different incoming speed. They can arise from a smooth contact normal proportional to (1,1/3​). Thus the horizontal-impulse result is conditional, rather than uniquely implied by the printed statement. For any member of this family the corresponding sticking result remains well determined once w is chosen:
V=41​(−3u+23​​w,21​w),Kfinal​=2m∣V∣2,ΔK=83m​([u+23​​w]2+4w2​).
(55)
The last expression is the lost relative kinetic energy in a perfectly inelastic collision of these two point masses.
