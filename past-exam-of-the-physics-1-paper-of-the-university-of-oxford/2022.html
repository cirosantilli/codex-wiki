For motion in a central potential, conservation of angular momentum gives L=mr2θ˙. In polar coordinates, the conserved mechanical energy is
E=2m​r˙2+2m​r2θ˙2−G(r)=2m​r˙2+2mr2L2​−G(r).

r˙=±m2​[E+G(r)]−m2r2L2​​,dθdr​=Lmr2​r˙=±Lr2​2m[E+G(r)]−r2L2​​.​
(2)
The sign in r˙ distinguishes inward and outward motion. Choosing the orientation so L>0, it is also the sign of dr/dθ. The expression under the square root must be nonnegative. If L=0, the motion is radial and θ is constant, so r cannot be parametrized by θ this way.

Take β>0. At a radial turning point, r˙=0, and the effective potential gives
E=2mr2L2​−rβ​,Ermin2​+βrmin​−2mL2​=0.​
(3)
For L=0, the smaller positive radial turning point is conveniently written without division by E:
rmin​=mβ+m2β2+2mEL2​L2​.​
(4)
This includes E=0, where rmin​=L2/(2mβ). For E<0 there is also an outer radial turning point; the two coincide for the circular orbit at E=−mβ2/(2L2). Smaller energies are inaccessible at that L. For E>0 there is just one positive radial turning point. If L=0, there is no centrifugal barrier and an inward trajectory can reach the singular centre; its limiting closest distance is zero.
The requested omission of β is not possible. For example, at E=0 and fixed nonzero L,m, doubling β halves rmin​. Thus E,L,m alone cannot determine closest approach.

A constant displacement proportional to (1,−3) leaves the velocities unchanged and changes 3x+y by zero. It is therefore a continuous symmetry of the Lagrangian. The generalized momentum components are
px​=∂x˙∂L​=m(6x˙+y˙​),py​=∂y˙​∂L​=m(x˙+4y˙​).
(9)
Noether's theorem gives the conserved quantity
P=px​−3py​=m(3x˙−11y˙​)=constant.​
(10)
A direct check using the Euler-Lagrange equations is
6x¨+y¨​=−3g,x¨+4y¨​=−g⟹3x¨−11y¨​=0.
(11)
The normalization of a symmetry generator is arbitrary. One can take the transformation parameter to have length units and (Kx​,Ky​)=(1,−3) dimensionless, giving P the units of momentum. Alternatively, for the paper's convention with dimensionless parameter and length-valued Ki​, use (Kx​,Ky​)=(ℓ,−3ℓ); the conserved quantity is then ℓP. The conserved content is identical.

Write the incident and scattered photon energies as ε=hc/λ and ε′=hc/λ′. Conservation of relativistic energy gives the recoiling electron energy Ee​=mc2+ε−ε′. By momentum conservation, its momentum is the difference of the two photon momenta; hence
pe2​c2=ε2+ε′2−2εε′cosθ.
(19)
Insert these into the relativistic energy-momentum relation for the electron:
(mc2+ε−ε′)2=m2c4+ε2+ε′2−2εε′cosθ.
(20)
Expanding and cancelling terms gives
mc2(ε−ε′)=εε′(1−cosθ),ε′1​−ε1​=mc21−cosθ​.
(21)
Since 1/ε=λ/(hc), the Compton scattering formula follows:
λ′=λ+mch​(1−cosθ).​
(22)
The scale h/(mc) is the electron Compton wavelength. At θ=0, λ′=λ: there is no energy or momentum transfer in this ideal forward limit, so the electron remains at rest. At θ=π, the wavelength shift is maximal:
Δλmax​=mc2h​,εmin′​=1+2ε/(mc2)ε​.​
(23)
The electron gains its largest kinetic energy, ε−εmin′​, and recoils forward while the photon reverses direction.

For the power law central potential V=βrk, differentiate the effective potential:
Veff′​(r)=βkrk−1−mr3J2​.
(27)
The circular orbit condition is mβkr0k+2​=J2. Thus, when J=0, βk>0 and k=−2,
r0​=(mβkJ2​)1/(k+2).​
(28)
The sign condition βk>0 expresses attraction. At k=0 the force vanishes and there is no nontrivial circular orbit. At k=−2, attraction requires β<0 and the condition reduces to J2=−2mβ. In that exceptional case the effective potential is identically zero and every r0​>0 is a possible circular radius for the tuned J; there is no isolated preferred radius. A small radial velocity then gives r¨=0, rather than an oscillation.

Put r=r0​+η, where ∣η∣≪r0​. The Taylor expansion at a circular orbit is
Veff​(r0​+η)=Veff​(r0​)+21​Veff′′​(r0​)η2+O(η3).

Choose the orientation J>0. To leading order in the radial disturbance, the azimuthal angular frequency is ωθ​=J/(mr02​), so
ωθ​ωr​​=k+2​.​
(32)
For either orientation the positive frequency ratio is ωr​/∣ωθ​∣. At k=−1, the ratio is 1: there is one radial oscillation per revolution. The bound Kepler orbit is an ellipse with the force centre at a focus, with one closest and one furthest approach per revolution. Its exact polar coordinates equation is
r(θ)=1+ecosθp​,p=m∣β∣J2​,0<e≪1.
(33)
At k=2, the ratio is 2: there are two radial oscillations per revolution. The isotropic harmonic oscillator trajectory is an ellipse centred on the force centre. An exact parametrization is
x(t)=acosΩt,y(t)=bsinΩt,Ω=m2β​​,a≃b.
(34)
Here r2=a2cos2Ωt+b2sin2Ωt repeats twice during one full circuit. The two different placements of the force centre distinguish these ellipses. These are the two all-bound-orbit cases of Bertrand's theorem.

(m+dm)(v+dv)+(−dm)(v−w)−mv=−mgdt.
(42)
Discarding the second-order product dmdv gives the vertical rocket equation
mdtdv​=−wdtdm​−mg.​
(43)
With m˙=−α and m=m0​−αt, integration for free flight starting from rest at t=0 gives
v(t)=wlnm0​−αtm0​​−gt,0≤t≤T.​

The logarithmic term is the ideal Tsiolkovsky rocket equation contribution; gt is the gravity loss. Immediate upward departure requires positive initial acceleration, or
αw>m0​g,wthreshold​=αm0​g​.​
(45)

For an equal-speed elastic impact on a free rod, let h be the signed distance of the impact point from the rod's center of mass, I=ml2/12, and J the horizontal impulse delivered to the rod. For a smooth impact the impulse is perpendicular to the rod. If the ball's signed final velocity is u, the rod's center of mass velocity is V and its angular velocity is ω, momentum balance and angular impulse give
m(v0​−u)=J=mV,Iω=Jh.
(48)
Both objects have positive final translational velocities for a nonzero impact away from the centre. More explicitly, restitution in an elastic collision gives V+ωh−u=v0​, and hence
V=2+mh2/I2v0​​,u=2+mh2/I(mh2/I)v0​​≥0.
(49)
Equal final speeds thus require u=V=v0​/2. One can also see that the alternative u=−V would violate u+V=v0​.
By conservation of kinetic energy in the elastic collision,
21​mv02​=21​m(2v0​​)2+21​m(2v0​​)2+21​Iω2.
(50)
Thus Iω2=mv02​/2. Since Iω=mVh=mv0​h/2, elimination of ω gives
h2=m2I​=6l2​,∣h∣=6​l​.​
(51)
Either side of the center of mass works; the nearer end is a distance l(1/2−1/6​) away. The final angular speed is ∣ω∣=6​v0​/l. These values also satisfy the restitution condition because the struck point's velocity is V+ωh=3v0​/2, reversing the initial relative contact velocity.
