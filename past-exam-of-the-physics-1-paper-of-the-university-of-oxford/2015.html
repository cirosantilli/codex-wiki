Direct fall towards a point Sun. If the Sun is treated as a point target, the projectile must lose its heliocentric angular momentum. The cheapest change is retrograde, cancelling Earth's tangential orbital velocity. It then starts its radial fall with zero heliocentric speed, so w=vE​ and
uradial fall​=ve,E2​+vE2​​≃31.81kms−1.​

Minimum for impact on a finite Sun. A literal collision with the solar surface does not require zero angular momentum. The limiting solar impact by a retrograde launch instead has apocentre a at Earth's orbit and pericentre R⊙​ at the solar surface. va​=vE​a+R⊙​2R⊙​​​.

Let the projectile's initial laboratory velocity be ux^. The centre-of-momentum frame moves at
V=M+mMu​.
(18)
In that frame, the projectile's initial speed is w=u−V=mu/(M+m). An elastic collision preserves that speed, but can change its direction. Transforming the outgoing projectile velocity back by Galilean velocity addition gives
uout​=Vx^+wn,∣n∣=1.
(19)
This is the velocity-circle construction for elastic scattering: the outgoing tip lies on a circle of radius w, centred a distance V from the origin in the velocity plane. (20)

In particular, a heavier projectile cannot reverse its laboratory direction in an isolated elastic collision with an initially stationary lighter target.

The two positive powers below require β>0; the negative power requires β<0, so that the force is attractive. To leading order, r≃r0​[1+ϵcos(k+2​ϕ+δ)]. The azimuthal advance from one radial maximum to the next is
Δϕ=k+2​2π​.
(34)
For k=2, ωr​/Ω=2: there are two radial oscillations per revolution. The isotropic harmonic oscillator has orbits that are exact ellipses centred on the force centre, and the perturbed circle is such an ellipse.
For k=7, ωr​/Ω=3: the leading perturbation has three radial lobes per revolution, with consecutive radial maxima separated by 2π/3. Its small-amplitude sketch is a rounded three-lobed orbit.
For k=−7/4, ωr​/Ω=1/2: the radius completes one oscillation while the body makes two revolutions. The leading curve closes after Δϕ=4π, with a double-wound, generally self-crossing shape; the nearest and furthest points occur in the same spatial direction one revolution apart.
The rational ratios for k=7 and k=−7/4 describe closure of the linearized near-circular curves. They do not imply that every finite-amplitude orbit closes exactly: higher-order changes in the radial-to-orbital frequency ratio generally produce apsidal precession. Bertrand's theorem identifies the harmonic potential k=2 and the Kepler potential k=−1 as the exceptional attractive central potentials with all nearby bounded orbits closed.

The lines of centres from either lower cylinder to the upper cylinder make 60∘ with the horizontal. Let NL​,NR​ be the upward normal forces on the top cylinder from the left and right cylinders, and let Q be the lower cylinders' mutual normal force. While all three contacts remain closed, the triangular configuration translates horizontally with common acceleration a; frictionless normals exert no torques about the cylinder centres.

2NL​−NR​​=ma,23​​(NL​+NR​)=mg.

33​g​≤a≤3​g​.​
(37)

Use the tunnel frame S, with the entrance at x=0, the far end at x=L, and t=0 at both runner entry and photon emission. The runner has xR​=vt, while the approaching photon has xγ​=L−ct. At detection,
tD​=c+vL​,xD​=c+vvL​,f=LxD​​=c+vv​=1+ββ​,β=cv​.​
(58)
For 0<v<c, 0<f<1/2: the photon meets the runner before the runner reaches the tunnel midpoint.

Let S′ be the runner's inertial frame, with origin at the entry event. The Lorentz transformation is
x′=γ(x−vt),t′=γ(t−c2vx​),γ=(1−β2)−1/2.
(59)
The emission event E=(L,0) has coordinates xE′​=γL, tE′​=−γvL/c2. It is earlier than entry in S′: entry and far-end emission are simultaneous only in the tunnel frame. This is relativity of simultaneity, not an extra delay of the light source.

The entrance clock is at x=0. Let G be the event on that clock simultaneous with emission in the runner's frame. Since tG′​=γtG​=tE′​, its reading is
tG​=−c2vL​.​
(60)
This is the tunnel clock's reading, not the runner-frame coordinate time tE′​=−γvL/c2. It is also not a calculation of what a photograph of that distant clock would show, which would require an additional light-travel condition.

ii OurBigBook.com
At that simultaneous entrance-clock event, xG​=0 and tG​=−vL/c2. Its runner-frame position is
xG′​=γ(0−vtG​)=γc2v2L​=γβ2L.​
(61)
The runner is at x′=0, so the entrance is still ahead by distance γβ2L at the runner-frame emission time. The simultaneously measured distance between the two tunnel ends is xE′​−xG′​=γL(1−β2)=L/γ, consistent with length contraction.

The photon starts at xE′​=γL and travels left to the runner at the fixed position x′=0. Thus
dγ′​=γL,Δtγ′​=cγL​.​
(62)
This distance is not the simultaneous contracted tunnel length L/γ, because the runner is not at the entrance when the photon is emitted in S′. Adding the entrance-to-runner gap from part (ii) to L/γ indeed gives γL.

During the photon's runner-frame flight, the tunnel entrance moves left by vΔtγ′​=γβL. It began ahead of the runner by γβ2L, so at detection it lies behind by
din′​=γβL−γβ2L=γβL(1−β).
(63)
Divide this distance into the tunnel by the simultaneously measured tunnel length L/γ:
f′=L/γdin′​​=γ2β(1−β)=1+ββ​=f.​
(64)
The runner is stationary in S′, but their location relative to the moving tunnel still has this fraction. Omitting the initial entrance gap would incorrectly count the runner's progress from an emission event at which they had not yet entered in this frame. These simultaneous positions give the tunnel photon timing in two inertial frames.

The photon propagates along −x in the tunnel frame and has energy Eγ​=hc/λ0​ and momentum px​=−Eγ​/c. A Lorentz transformation into the approaching runner's frame gives
Eγ′​=γ(Eγ​−vpx​)=γ(1+β)Eγ​.
(65)
Since Eγ′​=hc/λ′, the approaching-source relativistic Doppler effect is
λ′=γ(1+β)λ0​​=λ0​1+β1−β​​.​
(66)
It is a blueshift. With q=λ′/λ0​=500.0/589.1, solving q2=(1−β)/(1+β) gives
β=1+q21−q2​=589.12+500.02589.12−500.02​≃0.16253,v≃4.873×107ms−1.​
