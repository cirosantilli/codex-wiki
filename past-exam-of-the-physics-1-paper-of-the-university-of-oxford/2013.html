The parallel axis theorem states I=IG​+Md2 for parallel axes separated by d, with the reference axis through the centre of mass. The perpendicular axis theorem states Iz​=Ix​+Iy​ for a planar lamina in the xy plane, about mutually perpendicular axes through one point. It does not apply directly to a three-dimensional solid.
For the transverse moment of inertia of a uniform solid cylinder, divide the cylinder into thin disks at axial positions z∈[−h/2,h/2]. Symmetry and the perpendicular axis theorem give the diametral moment of inertia r2dM/4 of each disk; the parallel axis theorem adds z2dM. Hence
I⊥​=hM​∫−h/2h/2​(4r2​+z2)dz,I⊥​=12M​(3r2+h2).​
(1)
The thin-disk limit is Mr2/4, while the slender-rod limit is Mh2/12.

A cyclic coordinate qj​ is absent explicitly from the Lagrangian: ∂L/∂qj​=0, even though q˙​j​ may occur. The Euler-Lagrange equation then gives
dtd​(∂q˙​j​∂L​)=0,pj​=∂q˙​j​∂L​=constant.​
(2)
Thus a continuous coordinate-translation symmetry gives a conserved conjugate momentum. The conserved quantity need not be ordinary linear momentum: an absent azimuthal angle can give conserved angular momentum. The derivative is taken holding the other coordinates and velocities fixed.

For the relativistic head-on elastic collision with a stationary target, use c=1 as stipulated, so masses are measured in energy units. The incoming momentum is p=E2−M2​ and the invariant squared total four-momentum is
s=(E+m)2−p2=M2+m2+2mE.
(5)
The centre-of-mass frame moves at β∗​=p/(E+m), with γ∗​=(E+m)/s​. In this frame the incident particle has
EM∗​=s​M2+mE​,pM∗​=γ∗​(p−β∗​E)=s​mp​.
(6)
A nontrivial head-on elastic collision reverses pM∗​ and preserves EM∗​. Boosting back to the laboratory frame gives
E′=γ∗​(EM∗​−β∗​pM∗​)=s(E+m)(M2+mE)−m(E2−M2)​,
(7)
E′=2Em+m2+M22mM2+E(m2+M2)​.​
(8)
The no-interaction forward branch instead leaves E′=E; the requested formula describes the actual one-dimensional encounter and recoil.

If S′ moves at +v relative to S, the Lorentz transformations are
x′=γ(x−vt),t′=γ(t−vx/c2),γ=(1−v2/c2)−1/2.​
(13)
A rod at rest in S has length L. To measure it in S′, select endpoint events simultaneous there: Δt′=0, so Δt=vL/c2 and Δx′=γ(L−vΔt)=L/γ. This is length contraction.
Now let a clock at rest in S′ cross the two rod endpoints. These are different events, with Δx=L, Δt=L/v and Δx′=0. Their transformed time interval is
Δt′=γ(vL​−c2vL​)=γvL​=vL′​.
(14)
Thus
L′=L/γ,Δt=γΔt′.​
(15)
The moving clock measures the proper time Δt′. The rod's contracted length divided by its speed in that clock's frame gives exactly the same crossing time; time dilation and length contraction are consistent consequences of the same transformation. Measuring a length and measuring a traversal interval require different simultaneity conditions.

The alpha particle has electric charge 2e and acquires kinetic energy K=2e(20kV)=40keV. For Coulomb head-on closest approach with target recoil, the initially stationary beryllium nucleus is free to recoil. Approximating the nuclear masses by 4u and 9u, the initial relative-motion kinetic energy is
K∗​=mα​+mBe​mBe​​K=139​(40keV)=27.6923keV.
(17)
At closest separation the relative radial velocity vanishes, although the centre of mass continues moving. Conservation of energy for the relative motion gives
4πε0​rmin​(2e)(4e)​=K∗​,rmin​=4πε0​K∗​8e2​≃4.16×10−13m.​
(18)
Using e2/(4πε0​)=1.43996MeVfm gives rmin​≃416fm. If the nucleus were externally held fixed, the relevant energy would be K, yielding 288fm instead. Initially stationary is not the same as constrained stationary; the given proton and neutron counts supply the recoil correction. Nuclear sizes and strong interactions are negligible at this separation.

For the uniform unloaded space-elevator cable, let the constant linear mass density be λ and the outer endpoint be b=η′R. In the co-rotating frame the outward effective force per unit length is λ(ω2r−GME​/r2). The cable is just clear of the surface, with neither a ground load nor an endpoint counterweight, so both ends have zero applied tension. The integrated effective force must vanish:
0=λ∫Rb​(ω2r−r2GME​​)dr=λ[2ω2​(b2−R2)+GME​(b1​−R1​)].
(21)
Using GME​=ω2η3R3, this becomes
0=(η′−1)[2η′+1​−η′η3​].
(22)
The root η′=1 is a zero-length cable. The physical nonzero cable has
η′=21+8η3​−1​≃23.6.​
(23)
It extends well beyond the geostationary orbit so its outer centrifugal force balances the inner segment's weight. If a cable is anchored with a nonzero endpoint load or has a counterweight, that load changes the integrated balance; its length is not fixed by the zero-end-load formula.

In the rim-mass pulley coupled to a hanging mass, the printed figure shows the smaller pulley fixed overhead, and the mechanical string leaves the right-hand tangent of the larger pulley. Let θ increase counterclockwise from the downward vertical to the radius carrying M. Then the hanging mass m moves downward by Rθ. The string is taken to grip the rim without slipping. Its tension must be solved dynamically, rather than set equal to mg during motion.
The kinetic energy and potential energy are

Interpret the one-light-day separation D as measured in the station frame at capsule launch, and retain the ship's initial inertial rest frame until reception at the station. The phrase does not explicitly name a frame; an alternative is given below. The ship-frame simultaneous separation is D′=D/γs​. The capsule moves at 0.9c towards a station approaching at 0.85c, so their separation decreases at 1.75c in this frame. This is a separation rate, not either object's speed. Hence the elapsed ship-frame coordinate time is
Δts​=γs​(0.9+0.85)cD​≃0.301018day≃7.2244h.​
(39)
It is the interval assigned using synchronized clocks in the ship frame to the launch and remote arrival. A single onboard clock does not directly mark the remote arrival event; observing its arrival signal would add a light-travel delay. If the stated one-light-day separation were instead measured in the ship frame, the answer would be Δts​=(1/1.75)day=0.571429day.
