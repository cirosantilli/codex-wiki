Use coordinates s down the inclined plane and n along its upward normal, with the launch point as origin. Gravity has components gsinϕ and −gcosϕ. For launch angle θ above the downhill direction,
s(t)=v0​cosθt+21​gsinϕt2,n(t)=v0​sinθt−21​gcosϕt2.
(20)
The nonzero root of n(t)=0 is tf​=2v0​sinθ/(gcosϕ). Substituting into s(t) and combining the two trigonometric terms gives the distance along the slope:
Rslope​=gcos2ϕ2v02​sinθcos(θ−ϕ)​.​
(21)
This assumes 0≤ϕ<π/2, sinθ>0, and a downhill landing with cos(θ−ϕ)>0, as well as the neglected air resistance. It reduces to the horizontal projectile range v02​sin(2θ)/g at ϕ=0. The angle relative to the horizontal is θ−ϕ, which explains the shifted cosine. The original diagram uses stipulated illustrative angles; no examination figure is being reconstructed.
