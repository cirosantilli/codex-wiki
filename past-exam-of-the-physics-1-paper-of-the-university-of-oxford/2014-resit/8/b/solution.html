Allowing azimuthal motion makes the endpoint speed satisfy v2=l2(θ˙2+sin2θφ˙​2). The light-rod spherical pendulum therefore has
L=21​ml2(θ˙2+sin2θφ˙​2)+mglcosθ.​
(26)
The azimuth φ is a cyclic coordinate, since the Lagrangian is independent of it. Its conjugate canonical momentum is the conserved vertical angular momentum:
pφ​=ml2sin2θφ˙​=Jz​=constant.​
(27)
For a finite uniform rod, replace ml2 by I and mgl by A from i. The same azimuthal symmetry then gives pφ​=Isin2θφ˙​.
The Hamiltonian is the Legendre transform of this Lagrangian. With pθ​=Iθ˙,
H=2Ipθ2​​+2Isin2θpφ2​​−Acosθ=2I​(θ˙2+sin2θφ˙​2)−Acosθ.
(28)
There is no explicit time dependence, so dH/dt=∂H/∂t=0. Here H also equals the total mechanical energy: the constraint is stationary, the kinetic energy is quadratic in generalized velocities, and gravity is conservative. The apparent singularity of the momentum expression at sinθ=0 is a spherical-coordinate singularity; trajectories reaching a pole must be described consistently, and nonzero Jz​ prevents reaching it at finite energy.
