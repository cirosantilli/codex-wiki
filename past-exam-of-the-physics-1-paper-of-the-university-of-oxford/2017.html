Measure θ from the downward vertical. The falling mass loses gravitational potential energy 3mgl(1−cosθ). The fixed pivot does no work, so conservation of energy gives its incident speed u:
3mgl(1−cosθ)=21​(3m)u2,u=2gl(1−cosθ)​.
(1)
At the bottom, the rod is vertical and the impact is horizontal. Its impulsive reaction therefore has no horizontal component. Let U and w be the subsequent horizontal velocities of the heavy and light balls. Horizontal momentum conservation and unit coefficient of restitution give
3mu=3mU+mw,w−U=u.
(2)
Thus U=u/2, and the light ball's outgoing speed is
w=23​2gl(1−cosθ)​=3gl​​sin2θ​​.​
(3)
The calculation neglects the sizes of the balls, treats the rod as massless, and uses an elastic collision. The expression vanishes for release at the bottom; a ball placed exactly at the unstable upright equilibrium requires a perturbation before it can fall.

Let μ be the instantaneous total mass of the rocket, including its remaining rocket propellant. During a short ejection it changes by dμ<0. The expelled mass −dμ has velocity v−v0​ to first order in a fixed inertial frame. With no external force, momentum conservation reads
μv=(μ+dμ)(v+dv)+(−dμ)(v−v0​).
(8)
Discarding the second-order product gives μdv=−v0​dμ. Integrate from initial total mass m0​+M to rocket dry mass m0​:
vf​=−v0​∫m0​+Mm0​​μdμ​,vf​=v0​ln(1+m0​M​).​
(9)

These are Newtonian velocities; a very large rocket mass ratio would require a relativistic treatment even if the exhaust is slow in the instantaneous rocket frame.

L=T−V=21​mx˙2−21​kx2.

The parallel axis theorem relates the moment of inertia I about an axis to ICM​ about a parallel axis through the centre of mass, separated by perpendicular distance d:
I=ICM​+Mtot​d2.​
(15)
In the defining mass integral, expanding the squared distance produces a cross term whose first mass moment about the centre of mass is zero.

Iz​=Ix​+Iy​.​
(16)
Indeed, Ix​=∫y2dm, Iy​=∫x2dm, and Iz​=∫(x2+y2)dm. The planar condition is essential; this is not an identity for an arbitrary three-dimensional rigid body.

Choose positive horizontal velocity to the right and positive spin angular velocity anticlockwise, as in the source drawing. At the bottom of the sphere, the rotational velocity is then to the right: the initial tangential contact-point velocity is
w1​=v1​+aΩ1​.

For independent generalized coordinates qi​, their generalized velocities q˙​i​ and a Lagrangian L(q,q˙​,t), the Euler-Lagrange equations are
dtd​(∂q˙​i​∂L​)−∂qi​∂L​=0.​
(51)
Here the partial derivatives treat qi​,q˙​i​,t as independent arguments, whereas d/dt follows the motion. For a conservative mechanical system, choose coordinates satisfying the mechanical constraints, express the kinetic energy T and potential energy V in those coordinates, set L=T−V, and evaluate these equations to obtain the equations of motion. If nonconservative generalized forces Qi​ are present, they appear on the right-hand side.
A cyclic coordinate is absent explicitly from L: ∂L/∂qi​=0, even though L may depend on q˙​i​. Its canonical momentum is consequently conserved,
pi​=∂q˙​i​∂L​,p˙​i​=0.​
(52)
This does not require q˙​i​=0 or even that q˙​i​ be constant; the relation between canonical momentum and generalized velocity depends on the Lagrangian.
