Take the launch point as (x,y)=(0,0) and ignore air drag. In projectile motion with constant gravitational acceleration g, a launch at angle θ above the horizontal gives
x=v0​tcosθ,y=v0​tsinθ−21​gt2.
(1)
At the wall x=l, the height is
y(l)=ltanθ−2v02​gl2​sec2θ=lq−2v02​gl2​(1+q2),q=tanθ.
(2)
This is a concave quadratic polynomial in q, so its unique maximum satisfies l−gl2q/v02​=0. Hence
θmax​=arctanglv02​​,ymax​(l)=2gv02​​−2v02​gl2​.​
(3)
For l=v02​/g, the best trajectory has θ=45∘ and reaches the wall at launch height. For l>v02​/g, the mathematical maximum height is negative. If the ball must stay above level ground, it lands before reaching the wall: v02​/g is the maximum same-level projectile range. If there is no intervening ground and the wall extends below the launch height, the same optimum still applies, but its impact point is below that height.
