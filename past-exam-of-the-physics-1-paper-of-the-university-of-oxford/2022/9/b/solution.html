Take upward velocity as positive, and let w>0 be the rearward rocket exhaust speed. During dt, the ejected parcel has mass −dm>0 and laboratory velocity v−w to first order. The momentum change of the rocket and this parcel equals the gravitational impulse:
(m+dm)(v+dv)+(−dm)(v−w)−mv=−mgdt.
(42)
Discarding the second-order product dmdv gives the vertical rocket equation
mdtdv​=−wdtdm​−mg.​
(43)
With m˙=−α and m=m0​−αt, integration for free flight starting from rest at t=0 gives
v(t)=wlnm0​−αtm0​​−gt,0≤t≤T.​
(44)
The logarithmic term is the ideal Tsiolkovsky rocket equation contribution; gt is the gravity loss. Immediate upward departure requires positive initial acceleration, or
αw>m0​g,wthreshold​=αm0​g​.​
(45)
At exact equality the initial acceleration is zero, but further burning reduces the weight and permits departure immediately thereafter. If departure is allowed to be delayed while fuel burns on the pad, the condition is instead αw>m(t)g at some time before burnout. With final rocket dry mass mf​, any powered departure before burnout requires w>gmf​/α; the free-flight formula then starts at the actual departure time tℓ​ with m(tℓ​), giving v=wln[m(tℓ​)/m(t)]−g(t−tℓ​).
For the specified data, the initial mass includes the payload. The rocket fuel fraction gives
mf​=(1−f)m0​=2500kg,T=αfm0​​=15s.
(46)
The 100kg payload is already part of the 2500kg final mass; it is not the entire rocket dry mass and must not be added a second time. Here m0​g/α=196m/s, well below the stated rocket exhaust speed, so departure is immediate. The burnout speed is
v(T)=2500ln4−9.8(15)≃3319m/s.​
(47)
This is the speed at burnout. Subsequent motion under the assumed uniform gravity has v(t)=v(T)−g(t−T).
