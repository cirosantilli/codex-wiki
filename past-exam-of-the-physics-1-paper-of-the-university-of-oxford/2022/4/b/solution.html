For L=0, the smaller positive radial turning point is conveniently written without division by E:

This includes E=0, where rmin​=L2/(2mβ). For E<0 there is also an outer radial turning point; the two coincide for the circular orbit at E=−mβ2/(2L2). Smaller energies are inaccessible at that L. For E>0 there is just one positive radial turning point. If L=0, there is no centrifugal barrier and an inward trajectory can reach the singular centre; its limiting closest distance is zero.
The requested omission of β is not possible. For example, at E=0 and fixed nonzero L,m, doubling β halves rmin​. Thus E,L,m alone cannot determine closest approach.
