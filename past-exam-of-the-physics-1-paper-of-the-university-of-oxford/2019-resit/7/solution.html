Let L0​=100m be the train's proper length, D=90m the tunnel's length in the track reference frame, and v=0.99c. The Lorentz factor is γ=7.0888. At one track-frame time, length contraction makes the train
L=γL0​​=14.107m<D.
(18)
Yes, the whole train fits inside at a single track-frame time. Set t=0 when the rear enters the near portal. Until the front reaches the far portal, the rear and front are at vt and vt+L, respectively. Complete containment lasts
0≤t≤vD−L​,
(19)
a track-frame interval of approximately 256ns, counting boundary contact.
In the train's reference frame, its length is 100m, while the moving tunnel has simultaneous length D/γ=12.696m. No single train-frame time has the whole train between the portals. The two answers refer to different simultaneity slices.
For an explicit check, denote the rear-entry event by A and the front-exit event by B. In track coordinates,
Δt=vD−L​>0,Δx=D.
(20)
The Lorentz transformation gives
Δt′=γ(Δt−c2vD​)=vD/γ−L0​​<0.​
(21)
Thus the front leaves before the rear enters in train coordinates. These spatially separated events have a frame-dependent order, as allowed by relativity of simultaneity; neither answer asserts a different local event at a portal. No collision with closed doors has been stipulated.
