Keep the signs of part i. The tension has horizontal component Tcosθ but still has counterclockwise torque Tr, since it is tangent to the axle. Thus
Ma=Tcosθ+f,Iα=Tr+Rf,a=−Rα.
(67)
Solving these equations gives the critical pulling angle of a rolling spool law
a=I+MR2TR(Rcosθ−r)​,f=−TI+MR2Icosθ+MrR​.​
(68)
The zero-acceleration angle is therefore
θc​=cos−1(Rr​).​
(69)
At this angle the line of action of the pull passes through the ground contact, so its torque about that point vanishes. A spool initially at rest stays at rest while the contact constraints hold; one already rolling has zero acceleration, rather than being brought to rest.
For equilibrium the vertical normal force is N=Mg−Tsinθc​ and ∣f∣=Tcosθc​. The static friction inequality ∣f∣≤μN becomes
T(cosθc​+μsinθc​)≤μMg.
(70)
Hence, for 0<r<R,
Tmax​=cosθc​+μsinθc​μMg​=r+μR2−r2​μMgR​.​
(71)
At this limit the normal force is still nonnegative; for finite μ and positive r it is positive. For θc​<θ≤π/2, the numerator of a is negative. Released from rest, the spool rolls leftward, away from the pulling hand, with counterclockwise rotation, provided the assumed rolling without slipping and ground contact remain possible. The nonzero acceleration is not obtained by assuming that friction simply opposes the centre's motion.
