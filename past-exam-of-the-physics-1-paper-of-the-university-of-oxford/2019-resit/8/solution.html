In the laboratory frame, the initial target is at rest and the outgoing directions are directly related to detector positions. The centre-of-momentum frame has zero total momentum, so the two outgoing momenta are equal and opposite; it cleanly separates bulk motion from relative kinetic energy. Transforming back is needed to predict laboratory angles and speeds. Neither reference frame removes the need for an appropriate collision law.
Take the incident direction as x and put S=M1​+M2​. Momentum conservation fixes the centre-of-momentum frame velocity, and a Galilean transformation gives the initial velocities in that frame:
V=SM1​u1​​x,w1​=SM2​u1​​x,w2​=−SM1​u1​​x.​
(22)
After the collision, M1​w1′​+M2​w2′​=0. Hence both particles have the same final-to-initial speed ratio,
q=∣w1​∣∣w1′​∣​=∣w2​∣∣w2′​∣​=u1​∣v1′​−v2′​∣​.
(23)
The relative kinetic energy is multiplied by q2. This common ratio exists independently of how the outgoing direction is selected.
The meaning of restitution matters here. The usual coefficient of restitution en​ is a ratio of normal separation and approach speeds, not total speeds. In a smooth oblique collision, the tangential relative velocity is unchanged. If the incoming relative velocity makes angle χ to the contact normal, then
q2=en2​cos2χ+sin2χ.​
(24)
For example, en​=1/2 and χ=π/4 give q=5/8​=1/2. Therefore the requested identification is not generally true with the standard normal definition. The printed equation is valid if its e denotes the relative speed retention in an oblique collision, namely e=q, or if an additional collision model supplies that equality. The elastic cases have q=en​=1 and are unaffected by this qualification.
To derive the scattering formula under that convention, write v1′​=V+w1′​. Its components give the laboratory scattering circle
(v1​cosθ1​−V)2+(v1​sinθ1​)2=q2(SM2​u1​​)2.
(25)
Solving the resulting quadratic for the nonnegative speed yields
u1​v1​​=SM1​​​cosθ1​±cos2θ1​−1+q2(M1​M2​​)2​​.​
(26)
With q=e, this is the requested formula. A real square root and v1​≥0 are both necessary: a negative algebraic root is not a second physical speed, and v1​=0 does not define a scattering angle. The subsequent limits are taken using this laboratory scattering circle.
