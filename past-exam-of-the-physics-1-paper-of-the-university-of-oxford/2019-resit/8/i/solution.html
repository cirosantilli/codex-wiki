For equal masses and an elastic collision, q=1 and
u1​v1​​=21​(cosθ1​±∣cosθ1​∣).
(27)
Thus the nonzero outgoing branch is
v1​=u1​cosθ1​,0≤θ1​<2π​,​
(28)
and the other nonnegative algebraic root is v1​=0. The latter is the head-on transfer of the incoming momentum and kinetic energy to particle 2: v2​=u1​. A stationary particle has no defined outgoing direction, so this root is not a separate physical outcome for every chosen θ1​.
On the nonzero branch, momentum conservation gives
v2′​=u1​x−v1′​,v2​=u1​sinθ1​.
(29)
The two nonzero outgoing velocities are perpendicular: squaring momentum conservation and comparing with kinetic energy conservation gives v1′​⋅v2′​=0. For cosθ1​<0, the nonzero root is negative and must be rejected; backward scattering of a moving incident particle is impossible in this equal-mass, initially stationary-target case.
