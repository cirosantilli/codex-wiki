Mössbauer effect

 Home Physics Nuclear physics
OurBigBook.com Words: 1k Articles: 4
A nucleus bound in a solid can emit or absorb a photon without exciting lattice vibrations, transferring momentum to the solid as a whole. The resulting recoil energy is negligible compared with the isolated-nucleus recoil, making very narrow resonant emission and absorption lines observable. The Pound-Rebka experiment uses this effect to resolve the small frequency shift from gravitational redshift.
The striking feature is the combination of a large photon energy and a tiny natural linewidth: an atomic nucleus can act as an extraordinarily selective energy filter. The Mössbauer effect supplies a narrow shared emission line and absorption line even when nuclear recoil would separate the corresponding free-nucleus lines. A slow mechanical motion can then tune their overlap through the Doppler effect. The Pound-Rebka experiment is an application to general relativity; the discovery experiment below concerns the recoil-free process itself.

Recoil-free nuclear transition

Words: 683 Articles: 1
Mössbauer effect OurBigBook.com
A recoil-free nuclear transition emits or absorbs a gamma ray while leaving the internal vibrational state of the surrounding solid unchanged. It belongs to the elastic part of the process: no phonon is created or destroyed. The photon still carries momentum p=Eγ​/c, where c is the speed of light. The emitting solid takes momentum −p; the absorbing solid takes +p. Conservation of momentum holds for each event.
Why does an isolated atomic nucleus pose a problem? Start with an excited state at rest and let E0​ be its transition energy, with nuclear mass M and E0​≪Mc2. Conservation of momentum gives a recoil momentum of magnitude p; its nonrelativistic kinetic energy is therefore
ER​=2Mp2​=2Mc2Eγ2​​≃2Mc2E02​​.
(1)
By conservation of energy, emission spends part of E0​ on nuclear recoil, whereas absorption must supply both E0​ and the final kinetic energy. To leading order,
Eem​≃E0​−ER​,Eabs​≃E0​+ER​,Eabs​−Eem​≃2ER​.
(2)
Thus the relevant comparison is 2ER​ against the overlapping spectral line widths, rather than ER​/E0​ alone. If 2ER​ greatly exceeds the widths, the isolated-nucleus lines have negligible overlap. Finite Lorentzian line shape tails mean that this is suppression, rather than an absolute prohibition on absorption.
For a bound atomic nucleus, the recoil-free channel transfers momentum to the center of mass of the emitting or absorbing solid, with negligible kinetic energy p2/(2Msolid​). Other channels exchange phonons and carry substantial energy into or out of the internal vibrations. A large Msolid​ makes the elastic channel's energy cost small; the vibrational quantum state determines its probability. Merely replacing M by a large mass in the free-particle formula does not calculate that probability.
Here is a deliberately stipulated teaching system, independent of historical isotope data: E0​=20keV, Mc2=1011eV, and a single-line natural linewidth ΓE​=10−7eV measured as an energy full width at half maximum. These give ER​=0.002eV and 2ER​/ΓE​=40,000. A solid of 1012 such nuclear masses has elastic recoil energy 2×10−15eV, far below ΓE​. In this article ΓE​ is an energy width; the corresponding lifetime is τ=ℏ/ΓE​, with ℏ the Planck constant divided by 2π.

Lamb-Mössbauer factor (f)

Words: 395
Recoil-free nuclear transition OurBigBook.com
The Lamb-Mössbauer factor f is the fraction in the coherent elastic line. In the harmonic-lattice approximation it is a Debye–Waller factor. Write the displacement of the emitting or absorbing atomic nucleus relative to its equilibrium site as u, put u∥​=k⋅u, and define the angular wavenumber k=Eγ​/(ℏc). Choose the equilibrium site so that ⟨u∥​⟩=0. Its directional displacement variance is σ∥2​=⟨u∥2​⟩. A harmonic solid in thermal equilibrium has a Gaussian distribution for this displacement, whose characteristic function gives the elastic amplitude
⟨eiku∥​⟩=exp(−2k2σ∥2​​).
(3)
Taking the squared magnitude gives the intensity fraction
f=​⟨eiku∥​⟩​2=exp(−k2⟨u∥2​⟩).
(4)
The factor 1/2 belongs to the amplitude exponent; it disappears when the amplitude is squared. For an isotropic solid, ⟨u∥2​⟩=⟨∣u∣2⟩/3, so an equivalent expression is f=exp[−k2⟨∣u∣2⟩/3]. Directional and three-dimensional mean squared displacements must not be substituted for one another without this factor.
An Einstein solid approximation replaces the vibrational spectrum by one positive angular frequency ω. For the directional quantum harmonic oscillator, with the same effective mass M as in ER​,
⟨u∥2​⟩=2Mωℏ​(2nˉ+1),nˉ=eℏω/(kB​T)−11​.
(5)
Here kB​ is the Boltzmann constant and T is temperature. This follows by taking the expected value of u∥2​ in each oscillator energy eigenstate, ℏ(n+1/2)/(Mω), and averaging with the Boltzmann distribution. Substitution gives
f(T)=exp[−ℏωER​​coth(2kB​Tℏω​)],f(0)=exp(−ℏωER​​)<1.
(6)
The zero-temperature limit retains zero-point energy and a nonzero displacement variance. Cooling therefore increases the recoil-free fraction towards a limit below one for finite ER​/(ℏω)>0. At high temperature, the exponent tends to −2ER​kB​T/(ℏω)2. This is a single-frequency approximation to a harmonic lattice's coherent elastic weight, not a complete description of all zero-net-energy processes of an isolated oscillator. Real vibrational spectra, directional binding and anharmonic motion can change its predictions.
Extend the stipulated teaching system by choosing ℏω=0.020eV, so η=ER​/(ℏω)=0.1. Then f(0)=0.90484, while f=0.80542 at kB​T=ℏω. If source and absorber are independent, the fraction of events eligible for recoil-free emission followed by recoil-free absorption contains the product fs​fa​; this gives 0.81873 for two zero-temperature copies. It is not the total probability of absorption, which also depends on the number of resonant atomic nuclei, geometry and spectral line overlap.

Mössbauer velocity scan

Words: 362
Mössbauer effect OurBigBook.com
Let v be the source velocity towards the absorber, positive for approach along the photon direction. A first-order Lorentz transformation of the photon energy gives E′=E+vp; hence, for ∣v∣≪c,
δE≃Eγ​cv​.
(7)
Moving the source shifts its emission line through the stationary absorption line. A minimum in transmitted photon counts marks their strongest overlap. The absorber is the narrow energy discriminator; the downstream particle detector only needs to count the transmitted gamma rays.
For a thin absorber with unsplit Lorentzian line shapes, the overlap is a convolution of the source and absorber profiles. The combined full width at half maximum is W=Γs​+Γa​. A simple expected transmission model is
I0​I(v)​=1−1+[2Eγ​(v−v0​)/(cW)]2A​,0<A<1.
(8)
Here I0​ is the off-resonance intensity, v0​ encodes a relative shift of the two transition energies, and A includes the resonant absorber strength and fs​fa​. The convolution result can be checked by multiplying the Fourier transforms of two Cauchy distributions: their exponential decay constants add. The width of the absorption dip in velocity is cW/Eγ​, rather than the width of one individual spectral line converted to velocity.
For the teaching system with Γs​=Γa​=ΓE​ and v0​=0, the dip's full width at half maximum is 2.9979mms−1. Stipulate A=0.25 at zero temperature and 10,000 expected off-resonance counts per equal-duration bin: the central bin then has 7,500 expected counts, and the half-depth bins have 8,750. These are model expectations, without sampled counting noise. Holding all other parameters fixed while heating both lattices to kB​T=2ℏω multiplies A by [f(T)/f(0)]2. This isolates how a falling recoil-free fraction weakens the dip.
The thin-absorber model is deliberately limited. A thick absorber can saturate and broaden the dip; unresolved hyperfine structure can create multiple components. Differences in chemical surroundings can shift the centers, and uncontrolled motion can smear them. These effects must be modeled before interpreting an observed width as a natural linewidth. Their sensitivity also makes the Mössbauer effect useful: a small displacement of a narrow dip can reveal a small change in the local nuclear environment.

Mössbauer discovery experiment

Words: 129
Mössbauer effect OurBigBook.com
Rudolf Ludwig Mössbauer found the effect during his Heidelberg work in 1957.[ref] His 1961 lecture Recoilless nuclear resonance absorption of gamma radiation describes crystalline preparations using the 129keV transition of the iridium isotope 191Ir. Separate cryostats, lead collimation and a particle scintillation detector measured transmission. Liquid-air cooling of both preparations enhanced absorption; cooling only the source against a room-temperature absorber slightly reduced it. A rotating source cryostat supplied nearly constant projected velocities over selected arcs. Centimetres per second suppressed absorption relative to zero velocity; a nonresonant absorber supplied the comparison. This tested a narrow common emission/absorption energy beyond the detector's resolution. Temperature data alone had not established its width. The lecture cites the initial and direct-verification publications from 1958.[ref]
