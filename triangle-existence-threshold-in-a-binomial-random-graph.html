Triangle-existence threshold in a binomial random graph (p≍n−1)

 ... Area of mathematics Foundations of mathematics Graph theory Binomial random graph Triangle count in a binomial random graph Triangle variance in a binomial random graph
OurBigBook.com Words: 55
If np→0, the triangle count in a binomial random graph has expectation at most (np)3/6→0, so the Markov inequality shows there are no triangles in a graph with probability tending to one. If np→∞, the triangle variance in a binomial random graph divided by the squared mean is O((np)−3)+O((n2p)−1)→0. The second moment method then gives at least one triangle in a graph with probability tending to one. At the intermediate scale np→c∈(0,∞), a nontrivial Poisson limit for triangles in a binomial random graph describes the count.
