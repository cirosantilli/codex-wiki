Colour-focusing proof of Van der Waerden theorem

 Home Mathematics Area of mathematics Combinatorics Ramsey theory Van der Waerden theorem
OurBigBook.com Words: 255
For positive r,k, let W(r,k) bound the interval needed to force a monochromatic length-k arithmetic progression in every finite colouring using at most r colours. The colour-focusing proof of Van der Waerden theorem inducts on k, simultaneously for every number of colours, and then creates progressions of distinct colours with a common next point.
Assume W(r,k) exists for every r. For fixed r, construct Ft​ such that a finite colouring of [Ft​] using at most r colours contains either a monochromatic length-(k+1) arithmetic progression, or t length-k arithmetic progressions of pairwise distinct colours of the form f−hdi​, 1≤h≤k, with a common focus f∈[Ft​]. Take F1​=2W(r,k): a length-k arithmetic progression in its first half has its next point inside the whole interval. For k=1, give a singleton step 1.
Given N=Ft​, put M=W(rN,k) and Ft+1​=2NM. Regard the first M length-N blocks as coloured by their full colour profiles. Some block indices j0​,j0​+D,…,j0​+(k−1)D share a profile. If there is no monochromatic length-(k+1) arithmetic progression, this profile supplies t focused progressions with focus position f, steps di​, and distinct colours qi​. The colour q of position f differs from every qi​. In the later block j∗​=j0​+kD≤2M, put F=N(j∗​−1)+f. For each i, the points F−h(ND+di​) have colour qi​: they lie in the selected block j∗​−hD at position f−hdi​. The points F−hND have colour q. Thus t+1 distinct colours focus at F. For t=r, its own colour completes one of the r focused progressions. The base case W(r,1)=1 completes the mathematical induction.
