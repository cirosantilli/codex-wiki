Stefan–Boltzmann law (F=ϵσT4, Mb​=σT4)

 Home Physics Branches of physics Statistical physics Thermodynamics Heat
OurBigBook.com Words: 2k Articles: 12
The Stefan–Boltzmann law gives the total hemispherical radiant exitance of an ideal blackbody at absolute temperature T:
Mb​(T)=σT4,[Mb​]=Wm−2.
(30)
Here σ is the Stefan-Boltzmann constant. Total means integrated over all wavelengths; hemispherical means integrated over all outward directions with the projected area weighting. Thus Mb​ is emitted power per actual emitting area, rather than directional radiance or spectral radiance. Absolute temperature must be in kelvin; raising a Celsius reading to the fourth power gives the wrong result. A uniform surface of area A emits radiant flux Pemit​=AMb​. A real surface instead has M=ϵ(T)σT4, where ϵ(T) is its total hemispherical emissivity. The older symbol F in the formula above denotes this emitted radiant exitance.[ref]
For an ideal blackbody, doubling absolute temperature multiplies radiant exitance by 16. With the approximate Stefan-Boltzmann constant 5.670374419×10−8Wm−2K−4, the radiant exitances at 300K and 600K are approximately 459.300 and 7348.805Wm−2. This steep response explains why thermal radiation becomes so consequential at high temperature. It also enables heat transfer through a vacuum, where thermal conduction through a gas and convection are absent.
A short derivation integrates Planck's law in its frequency form. For a blackbody, direction-independent spectral radiance is Bν​(T)=2hν3/[c2(ehν/(kB​T)−1)], where h is the Planck constant, kB​ the Boltzmann constant and c the speed of light. The outward hemisphere contributes ∫cosθdΩ=π, so
Mb​=π∫0∞​Bν​(T)dν=h3c22πkB4​T4​∫0∞​ex−1x3​dx,x=kB​Thν​.
(31)
Expanding (ex−1)−1=∑n=1∞​e−nx, nonnegative terms allow termwise integration. Repeated integration by parts gives ∫0∞​x3e−nxdx=6/n4; the Riemann zeta function value ζ(4)=π4/90 gives the remaining integral as π4/15. Hence Mb​=σT4 with the coefficient in Stefan-Boltzmann constant. The angular integral is essential: omitting it confuses radiance with radiant exitance.
