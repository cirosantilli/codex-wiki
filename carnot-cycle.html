Carnot cycle (W=(Th​−Tc​)ΔS)

 ... Physics Branches of physics Statistical physics Thermodynamics Second law of thermodynamics Carnot's theorem
OurBigBook.com Words: 3k Articles: 14
A Carnot cycle realizes the reversible bound in Carnot's theorem. Its four steps for an ideal gas working substance are:
  • Reversible isothermal expansion at Th​, absorbing Qh​ and increasing entropy by ΔS.
  • Reversible adiabatic expansion, doing work while the temperature falls from Th​ to Tc​ and entropy stays fixed.
  • Reversible isothermal compression at Tc​, rejecting Qc​ and decreasing entropy by ΔS.
  • Reversible adiabatic compression, requiring work to restore Th​ at fixed entropy.
The temperature versus entropy plot in the Carnot's theorem figure is therefore a rectangle traversed clockwise. Because δQrev​=TdS, its width is ΔS=Qh​/Th​. For the illustrated engine, ΔS=5/3JK−1: the upper step absorbs 600×5/3=1000J, the lower step rejects 300×5/3=500J, and the enclosed area is the 500J net work. Geometry displays the thermal efficiency without solving the gas motion. The two vertical steps are isentropic processes, not constant-temperature steps. [ref] [ref]
Reversible heat transfer is a limit of vanishing temperature differences; ordinary finite-rate transfer introduces entropy production. The Carnot cycle is a performance benchmark rather than a guarantee of useful power. Running it backward gives the ideal operation of a refrigerator or heat pump, with work supplied to move heat toward the hotter thermal reservoir. [ref]
