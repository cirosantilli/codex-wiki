Bragg angle (θ)

OurBigBook.com Words: 140
The Bragg angle is the glancing angle from the incident beam to the selected lattice planes, with 0<θ≤90∘. If α is instead the angle from the beam to the plane normal, α=90∘−θ and Bragg's law becomes nλ=2dcosα. Defining the angle explicitly prevents a sine-versus-cosine error as well as a factor-of-two error in the detector deflection.
Choose λ=0.154nm and d=0.200nm as an illustrative model, not historical measurements. For the first diffraction order,
sinθ=2(0.200)0.154​=0.385,θ≃22.643740∘,2θ≃45.287481∘.
(26)
The second diffraction order has sinθ=0.770 and 2θ≃100.707778∘; the third would require sinθ=1.155 and is impossible. These are different diffraction orders of the same interplanar spacing at the same wavelength.
Conversely, a first-order reflection at a measured deflection 2θ gives the interplanar spacing
d=2sinθλ​.
(27)
Using 2θ≃45.287481∘ recovers d≃0.200nm. This is the useful reversal: a known wavelength turns an angular measurement into an atomic length. An unassigned diffraction order or an unknown wavelength prevents that single measurement from determining d uniquely.
