Sinc fourth-power integral (∫0∞​(sinx/x)4dx=π/3)

The triangular function f(x)=(1−∣x∣) on ∣x∣<1, zero elsewhere, has Fourier transform f​(k)=[sin(k/2)/(k/2)]2 in the angular-frequency convention. Its squared L2 norm is 2/3. Parseval's identity therefore gives
32​=2π1​∫−∞∞​(k/2sin(k/2)​)4dk=π2​∫0∞​(usinu​)4du.
(2)
This proves the displayed value. The removable value at zero is one, and the fourth-power decay bounds the tail. The sinc-squared integral follows similarly from a rectangular function rather than a triangle.
