Every allowed member has ratio strictly below four. For any proposed constant C<4, a sufficiently large a<1/2 has ratio greater than C. Combined with Hardy's integral inequality, this proves that four is the smallest possible squared L2 norm constant.[ref]
The endpoint is excluded: at a=1/2, ∫01​∣fa​∣2dx=∫01​x−1dx=∞, and the inner portion of ∥Hfa​∥22​ also diverges. Its tail still has finite integral four. There is no finite L2 norm ratio at this endpoint, hence no equality example obtained by substituting it. The limit four is unattained by this family. Multiplying each fa​ by 1−2a​ makes its L2 norm one without changing the ratio.
