Tsui-Störmer-Gossard fractional Hall experiment

 ... Electromagnetic four-potential Electromagnetic field tensor Lorentz force Hall effect Quantum Hall effect Fractional quantum Hall effect
OurBigBook.com Words: 210
Daniel Tsui, Horst Störmer and Arthur Gossard reported the effect in 1982. They grew gallium arsenide–aluminium gallium arsenide layers by molecular beam epitaxy, separating the two-dimensional electron gas from its semiconductor doping. They cut Hall bars and made indium contacts. Low-field measurements established number density and charge carrier mobility. A perpendicular magnetic field sweep measured transverse and longitudinal sheet resistivities at several temperatures. Cooling sharpened the plateau near 3h/e2 and the longitudinal minimum at filling factor 1/3; integer plateaus provided an internal comparison. The longitudinal minimum remained finite at the lowest temperature: zero-temperature vanishing was unresolved. This established anomalous transport, without directly measuring excitation electric charge.[ref]
The two readouts test different statements. A single intersection with RH​=3RK​ would also occur on the smooth classical relation RH​=B/(ns​e) at ns​h/(eB)=1/3. A quantum Hall plateau instead persists over a magnetic field interval. Comparing transverse and longitudinal signals helps distinguish that plateau from a contact-placement artefact; the quantum Hall conductivity tensor inversion quantifies the contamination when longitudinal sheet resistivity is nonzero.
Later, 1997 shot noise measurements associated with quantum tunnelling at filling factor 1/3 tested the excitation electric charge separately, finding e/3. That is a different observable from a Hall plateau.[ref]
