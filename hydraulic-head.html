Hydraulic head (h=z+p/(ρg))

 Home Physics Branches of physics Fluid mechanics Porous-media flow Darcy law
OurBigBook.com Words: 150
Hydraulic head is the sum of elevation and pressure head, measured in metres relative to a chosen height datum. For constant mass density ρ and gravitational acceleration magnitude g, the pressure head p/(ρg) is the height of a column of the same fluid supported by fluid pressure p. A water-filled open measuring tube records hydraulic head as the elevation of its water level when p is gauge fluid pressure. Changing the common pressure reference or height datum adds a constant to hydraulic head and leaves its driving gradient unchanged.
For water with ρ=1000kgm−3 and g=9.81ms−2, a point at z=2m with gauge fluid pressure p=9810Pa has h=3m. A second point at z=0 with gauge fluid pressure 29430Pa also has h=3m: these values are compatible with a stationary column under hydrostatic pressure. It is a difference in hydraulic head, rather than a difference in fluid pressure alone, that drives saturated Darcy flow. This form uses constant mass density; one cannot generally pull spatially varying ρ through the gradient.[ref]
