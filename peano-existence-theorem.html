Peano existence theorem (y′=f(t,y),y(t0​)=y0​)

Let U⊂R×Rd be an open set, d<∞, and f:U→Rd a continuous function. Through every (t0​,y0​)∈U there is a local solution of a differential equation: a continuously differentiable function y:I→Rd on an open interval containing t0​, satisfying the displayed initial value problem pointwise. No Lipschitz condition is assumed.[ref]
Continuity of a proposed rate law therefore guarantees at least one trajectory for a short time. For a physical model, this is a first consistency check: can the stated rule and initial condition actually be followed? Prediction also needs uniqueness. The delayed solutions of a square-root differential equation below show how the same rule and initial condition can leave a departure time undetermined. The Picard-Lindelöf theorem supplies a sufficient additional hypothesis for uniqueness; failure of that hypothesis alone does not prove nonuniqueness.
For the initial value problem y′=2∣y∣​, y(0)=0, a forward solution of a differential equation can stay zero until any c≥0, then follow y=(t−c)2. The delayed solutions of a square-root differential equation verify this freedom exactly.
Local existence gives neither a global solution of a differential equation nor a bound on all future values. The continuous function f(t,y)=y2 already permits finite-time blowup; the finite-dimensional continuation criterion explains the additional issue of extending a trajectory within its domain.
Proof outline: choose a compact set K=[t0​−a,t0​+a]×Br​(y0​)⊂U. By the extreme value theorem, M=maxK​∥f∥<∞. Take 0<h≤a with Mh≤r. Forward Euler method polygons remain in the closed ball and satisfy ∥yn​(t)−yn​(s)∥≤M∣t−s∣. The Arzelà-Ascoli theorem yields a convergent subsequence with uniform convergence. Uniform continuity of f on K makes their integral equation defect tend uniformly to zero, so the limit satisfies
y(t)=y0​+∫t0​t​f(s,y(s))ds.
(30)
The fundamental theorem of calculus gives a continuously differentiable function. Repeat backward and join at t0​, where both derivatives equal f(t0​,y0​).[ref]
The compact set here uses a finite-dimensional vector space; bounded closed balls in an arbitrary Banach space need not be compact. When M=0, the constant solution of a differential equation y=y0​ works on the entire time interval in K. In general the proof selects a subsequence, without asserting that all polygons converge to one unique trajectory.
