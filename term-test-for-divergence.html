Term test for divergence

 ... Mathematics Area of mathematics Analysis Real analysis Sequence and series Series (mathematics)
OurBigBook.com Words: 86
The term test for divergence says that a series cannot converge if its terms fail to tend to zero. Indeed, if its partial sums satisfy Sn​→S, then an​=Sn​−Sn−1​→S−S=0. This is a necessary condition for a convergent series.
The converse fails: 1/n→0, but the harmonic series diverges. For a quick successful test, ∑n=1∞​n/(n+1) diverges because its terms tend to one. The test gives no conclusion for ∑1/n or ∑1/[n(n+1)]: both have terms tending to zero, yet only the second is a convergent series. The telescoping series calculation and the harmonic series block estimate resolve those cases.
