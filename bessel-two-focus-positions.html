Bessel two focus positions (x±​=(L±L2−4Lf​)/2)

Place a converging thin lens at coordinate x between the object at 0 and the screen at L. In air and the paraxial approximation, the required object and image distances are u=x and v=L−x. The thin lens equation gives a quadratic equation:
f1​=x1​+L−x1​=x(L−x)L​,x2−Lx+Lf=0.
(21)
Its discriminant is L2−4Lf. For L>4f>0, the two roots and their separation are
x±​=2L±d​,d=x+​−x−​=L2−4Lf​,x−​+x+​=L.
(22)
Both roots lie inside (0,L), and both object and image distances exceed f. The two roots therefore describe finite real images on the screen. Exchanging the roots exchanges u,v; from transverse magnification m=−v/u,
m−​=−L−dL+d​,m+​=−L+dL−d​,m−​m+​=1.
(23)
The image-height ratio between the enlarged and reduced settings is ∣m−​∣/∣m+​∣=∣m−​∣2, rather than ∣m−​∣.
At L=4f, the roots coincide: d=0, x=L/2=2f, u=v=2f and m=−1. There is one setting, not two separately measurable settings. For 0<L<4f the discriminant is negative, so no position between the fixed object and screen gives a sharp real image there. This does not forbid a real image elsewhere if the screen is moved. Another short proof observes that x(L−x) has maximum L2/4 at x=L/2; the focusing requirement x(L−x)=Lf is possible exactly when L≥4f.
