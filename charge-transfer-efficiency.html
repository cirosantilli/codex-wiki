Charge-transfer efficiency (ηtr​)

 ... Physics Branches of physics Optics Optical instrument Photodetector Charge-coupled device
OurBigBook.com Words: 224
The charge-transfer efficiency is the fraction of a packet delivered into its intended next site or time slot in one transfer, under stated conditions. It is distinct from quantum efficiency, which concerns conversion of incident photons into collected charge carriers. Define the complementary inefficiency as ϵ=1−ηtr​.
In an original model with constant 0≤ηtr​≤1, let Nj​ denote the mean number of original packet electrons still delivered on time after j transfers. Assuming no reinjection into that packet,
Nj+1​=ηtr​Nj​,Nout​=Nin​ηtrn​.
(47)
Repeated substitution proves the result; for different efficiencies the fraction is ∏j=1n​ηj​. For ϵ≪1, a Taylor expansion of ln(1−ϵ) gives ηtrn​≃e−nϵ, while the simpler 1−nϵ requires nϵ≪1.
Choose Nin​=10000 and n=2000. For ηtr​=0.9999, the expected on-time output is 8187.23 electrons; for ηtr​=0.99999, it is 9801.99. These are model means, so fractional electron counts do not describe an individual packet. Even a small per-transfer imperfection can imprint distance-dependent image errors.
If every original electron independently stays on time with probability p=ηtrn​, a fixed initial count gives a binomial distribution with variance Nin​p(1−p). An initial Poisson distribution with mean μ instead gives an on-time Poisson distribution with mean and variance μp, by Poisson thinning. A deterministic attenuation formula alone does not specify noise. Real trapping can correlate packets and depends on timing, illumination and temperature; the independent constant-efficiency model then fails.
