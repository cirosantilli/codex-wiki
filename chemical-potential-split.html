Chemical potential (μ)

 Home Physics Branches of physics Statistical physics Thermodynamics
OurBigBook.com Words: 3k Articles: 46
The chemical potential measures how a thermodynamic potential changes when matter is added under specified constraints. It plays for matter exchange the role that temperature plays for heat exchange: a difference supplies a direction, and equality characterizes thermodynamic equilibrium when the corresponding exchange is allowed. This connects diffusion, phase coexistence and chemical equilibrium through one quantity.
For a single component, the particle convention is
μparticle​=(∂N∂U​)S,V​,
(1)
where U is internal energy, N counts particles, S is entropy and V is volume. This partial derivative holds entropy and volume fixed; it measures an incremental change rather than the average energy per particle. See David Tong, Statistical Physics, section 1.4.
In chemistry the molar convention instead uses amount of substance n, measured in moles:
μm​=(∂n∂G​)T,p​,n=NA​N​,μm​=NA​μparticle​.
(2)
Here G is Gibbs free energy, T is temperature, p is pressure and NA​ is the Avogadro constant. The units are J per particle versus Jmol−1. For several chemical species, define each chemical potential by varying only its own particle count or amount of substance, with all other components' counts or amounts fixed. Many texts use μ for either convention, so the units and the variable being differentiated must identify it.
For a simple compressible system with variable component inventories, the fundamental differentials are
dU=TdS−pdV+∑i​μparticle,i​dNi​,dG=−SdT+Vdp+∑i​μm,i​dni​.
(3)
They describe changes between states in thermodynamic equilibrium with only pressure-volume work. If every component inventory is fixed, the sums vanish. The Gibbs free energy form follows by differentiating G=U−TS+pV and using dNi​=NA​dni​; this is why the two definitions describe the same chemical potential in different units.
A chemical potential can be negative. Holding entropy fixed while adding matter need not increase internal energy; Tong explains this explicitly for a classical ideal gas in Statistical Physics, section 2.2.4. Its absolute sign also depends on the reference energy: replacing U by U+ϵ0​N shifts μparticle​ by ϵ0​. A common reference shift cancels from differences for the same chemical species, which are what determine exchange. Thus “energy cost” means a signed increment, not an always positive amount that must be supplied.
