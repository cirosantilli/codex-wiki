Eddington experiment

 Home Physics Branches of physics General relativity Schwarzschild spacetime Schwarzschild light deflection
OurBigBook.com Words: 96
The 1919 solar eclipse expeditions organized by Frank Watson Dyson and Arthur Eddington compared background-star positions near the obscured Sun with reference photographs. The optical train used a slowly rotating flat mirror to send the light into a horizontal photographic telescope. A smaller telescope at Sobral provided a separate set of plates. Focus and plate-scale changes had to be distinguished from the radial pattern predicted by Schwarzschild light deflection. The experiment therefore tested a spatial pattern and its amplitude, not merely whether a star image had moved.[ref] [ref]
