Delta-shell Born amplitude (fB​(θ)=−gRsinc[2kRsin(θ/2)])

OurBigBook.com Words: 82
For a real shell potential V(r)=V0​δ(r−R), define g=2mV0​R/ℏ2 and sincu=sinu/u, with value one at zero. Angular integration of its three-dimensional Fourier transform gives 4πV0​R2sinc(qR), where the momentum transfer has wave-number magnitude q=2ksin(θ/2). Substitution in the first Born approximation proves the displayed scattering amplitude. It is real. Its squared modulus is a nonzero order-V02​ differential scattering cross-section, whose optical theorem counterpart requires the order-V02​ imaginary part of the forward amplitude from the next Born term. Thus squaring the first-order amplitude does not supply a consistently unitary second-order amplitude.
