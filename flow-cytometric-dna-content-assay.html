Flow cytometric DNA-content assay

 Home Biology Flow cytometry
OurBigBook.com Words: 300
Under controlled staining and detector settings, integrated fluorescence can be approximately proportional to nuclear DNA amount. A histogram of accepted single-cell events then separates a 2C region, an intermediate region and a 4C region. These are ordinarily assigned to G0 phase/G1 phase, S phase, and G2 phase/M phase respectively in the stated diploid model.
A Cornell protocol fixes a single-cell suspension by adding cold 70% ethanol dropwise while mixing, reducing clumping. After washing, its staining solution contains propidium iodide at 50μg/ml; ribonuclease A removes interfering RNA. It specifies excitation at 488nm and emission around 600nm. The resulting readout is fluorescence per event, not a direct count of chromosomes. [ref]
Useful controls include an unstained sample for background and a reference population with known nuclear DNA amount, processed with identical settings. Two attached 2C biological cells can mimic one 4C event. Comparing signal integral with pulse height or width helps exclude such doublets; a gate selecting intact single particles also limits debris. These are proposed assay checks, not measurements underlying the illustration.
A 4C peak cannot distinguish G2 phase from mitosis; a 2C peak cannot distinguish G1 phase from G0 phase. Mixed ploidy, uneven staining and aggregates can invalidate phase assignments. Peak areas describe the accepted sample at collection, and do not by themselves establish phase durations, division rates or a particular arrest mechanism. A treatment comparison needs matched handling and another readout appropriate to the claimed mechanism.
