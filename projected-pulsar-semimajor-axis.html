Projected pulsar semimajor axis (x=a1​sini/c)

 ... Astrophysics Stellar astrophysics Stellar evolution Neutron star Pulsar Binary pulsar
OurBigBook.com Words: 267 Articles: 1
The semimajor axis a1​ of the pulsar's orbit about the binary barycenter, projected along the line of sight and divided by the speed of light. The orbital inclination i is zero for a face-on orbit. Thus x is a time, often quoted as the numerical number of light-seconds in a1​sini, not the true binary separation. The relative-orbit semimajor axis is instead a=a1​+a2​; conservation of momentum gives a1​=am2​/(m1​+m2​).
Combining Kepler's third law with that barycenter relation gives the timing form of the binary mass function, also valid for eccentric Kepler orbits:
f=GPb2​4π2(cx)3​=(m1​+m2​)2(m2​sini)3​.
(25)
Choose x=2s and Pb​=8h for an original example: a1​sini=599,585km and f≃0.0773M⊙​, where M⊙​ is a solar mass. For a specified pulsar mass m1​=1.4M⊙​, the smallest allowed companion mass occurs at sini=1 and is approximately 0.698M⊙​. A different assumed m1​ changes this bound. One measured f does not determine both masses and orbital inclination.
