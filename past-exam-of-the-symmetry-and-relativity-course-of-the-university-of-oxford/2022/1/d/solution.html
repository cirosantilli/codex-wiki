The four-potential can be chosen to transform as a four-vector. To make the argument rigorous, choose the retarded potential with no freely added homogeneous solution. If □Gret​(x)=δ(4)(x), this choice is
Aμ(x)=−μ0​∫Gret​(x−y)Jμ(y)d4y.
(11)
The retarded Green function is a Lorentz scalar under proper orthochronous Lorentz transformations, and d4y is invariant. The four-vector rule for Jμ therefore proves A′μ(x′)=Λμν​Aν(x). More generally, transform any homogeneous initial data by this same rule. Both the wave equation and the Lorenz gauge condition are then preserved.
Qualification. The wave equation by itself cannot prove the transformation rule for every independently chosen gauge potential: the d'Alembert operator has a nontrivial kernel of a linear map. For a concrete counterexample, zero sources and zero fields admit Aμ=0 in one inertial frame and A′μ=(0,C,0,0) in another. Both satisfy the wave equation and the Lorenz gauge condition, but the latter is not the Lorentz transformation of zero. It is a residual Lorenz gauge symmetry, generated by χ=Cx′. Thus the usual assertion is a covariant choice of four-potential, with a consistent gauge transformation and homogeneous data, rather than a uniqueness result from the displayed equation.
