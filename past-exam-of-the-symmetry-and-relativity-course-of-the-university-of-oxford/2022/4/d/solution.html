Both the muon and electron have the same electric charge magnitude. At equal kinetic energy K per particle, their Lorentz factors are γ=1+K/(mc2), so the heavier muon has the smaller Lorentz factor. At equal total relativistic energy the same ordering follows from γ=E/(mc2), provided that energy is large enough to create either particle.
If the equal-acceleration comparison of c is retained, Pcircular​=Cq​a2γ4≤Cq​a2γ6=Plinear​. Thus the four-way instantaneous comparison selects
a muon in a circular accelerator, at fixed energy and coordinate acceleration.​
(43)
For positive K, its smaller Lorentz factor reduces the power relative to an electron, and transverse acceleration reduces the power relative to longitudinal acceleration of the same magnitude. This conclusion follows from the constraint actually stated in c.
For the customary engineering comparison at equal applied force, the result changes. From c,
Plinear​=6πϵ0​m2c3q2F2​,Pcircular​=γ2Plinear​.
(44)
Under that comparison the choice is
a muon in a linear accelerator, at fixed energy and applied force.​
(45)
Its longitudinal radiative power is smaller than an electron's by (me​/mμ​)2, while avoiding bending removes the extra γ2 factor. At a fixed bend radius R, the circular loss is Cq​γ4v4/R2, giving the familiar m−4 suppression at fixed ultrarelativistic energy and R.
An energy budget alone does not specify a unique optimization. The force, acceleration, bend radius, acceleration time, and length are otherwise unrestricted. For example, in a linear accelerator a constant small force F gives radiative power proportional to F2, and reaching a specified final three-momentum takes time proportional to F−1; the total radiated energy therefore tends to zero as F→0. One cannot infer a unique accelerator choice without an additional constraint. The standard practical preference is the second boxed answer, with its stated comparison. Muon decay introduces a separate lifetime constraint if slow acceleration is contemplated, but it was not part of the specified radiative-loss calculation.
