For a massive particle on a timelike world line, proper time satisfies dτ=dt1−v2/c2​=dt/γ. Its four-velocity and four-acceleration are U=dX/dτ and A=dU/dτ. Differentiating U=γ(c,v) gives
Aμ=(γ4cv⋅a​,γ2a+γ4c2v⋅a​v).​
(1)
The invariant Minkowski inner products are
U2=c2,P2=m2c2,P=mU.​
(2)
For the explicitly requested contraction,
U⋅A=γ5v⋅a−γ3v⋅a−γ5c2v2​v⋅a=γ3v⋅a[γ2(1−v2/c2)−1]=0.
(3)
Thus four-acceleration is orthogonal to four-velocity. Equivalently this follows by differentiating the constant Lorentz scalar U2=c2 with respect to proper time.

The event separation is timelike precisely when
(D−B)2=c2(td​−tb​)2−∣xd​−xb​∣2>0.​
(4)
A simultaneous pair in an inertial frame would instead have (D−B)2=−∣Δx′∣2≤0. Since a Lorentz transformation preserves this interval, no such simultaneous frame exists. A timelike pair can be brought to the same spatial position: the required velocity is u=Δx/Δt, whose magnitude is less than c.
The separate condition D⋅B=0 says that the two event-position four-vectors, measured from the chosen spacetime origin, are orthogonal in the Minkowski inner product. It is invariant under Lorentz transformations about that origin but not under arbitrary translations. It is not a general criterion for their causal separation.
For a concrete example, put B=(cT,0,0,0) and D=(0,R,0,0). Their inner product vanishes, but their separation has squared interval c2T2−R2, which can be positive, zero or negative. If B is timelike, a rest frame for B makes D0=0; this means that D and the chosen origin are simultaneous in that frame, not that D and B are simultaneous.

The proper acceleration is α=−A2​, the magnitude of the instantaneous rest-frame three-acceleration. A pure force is a four-force preserving rest mass, or equivalently satisfying F⋅U=0. Indeed F=d(mU)/dτ gives F⋅U=c2dm/dτ.
For the right-hand branch of the hyperbola, use c=1, x>0 and x=L2+t2​, taking L>0. Direct differentiation gives
v=dtdx​=xt​,∣v∣=1−x2L2​​,γ=Lx​,a=dtdv​=x3L2​.
(5)

aγ3=L1​=α.​
(6)

For a Lorentz boost of speed v along x, the velocity-addition formula formulas are
ux′​=1−ux​v/c2ux​−v​,uy′​=γv​(1−ux​v/c2)uy​​.
(12)
Substitute ux​=ucosθ, uy​=usinθ and take the ratio. The common time-transformation denominator cancels, yielding
tanθ′=γv​(ucosθ−v)usinθ​.​
(13)
To specify the direction rather than just its tangent, use θ′=atan2(usinθ/γv​,ucosθ−v). This keeps the correct quadrant when the boost reverses the sign of the longitudinal velocity. For physical subluminal speeds the canceled denominator is positive. At ux​=v and nonzero uy​, the transformed direction is perpendicular to the boost; if the particle is exactly comoving, its transformed velocity vanishes and the angle is undefined.

Each photon has energy ε=ℏω. Their total four-momentum is
P=cε​(2,1+cosθ,sinθ,0).
(18)

Let ε=ℏω be the incident laser-photon energy and γ=W/(mc2). The maximum in the usual soft-laser geometry occurs for a head-on encounter followed by a forward outgoing photon along the electron beam. Boost into the initial electron rest frame: the incident energy becomes ε∗​=γ(1+β)ε. Backscattering there gives the Compton scattering recoil relation ε∗,s​=ε∗​/[1+2ε∗​/(mc2)]. Boosting this outgoing photon forward to the lab gives
εmax​=1+2γ(1+β)ε/(mc2)γ2(1+β)2ε​.​
(21)
The familiar 4γ2ε limit additionally requires γ≫1 and ε∗​≪mc2. The denominator is the recoil correction, not an optional factor at arbitrary incident energy.
For completeness, if the incidence direction is prescribed rather than optimized, let its unit vector be n and the outgoing direction ns​. Squaring the residual electron four-momentum on its mass shell gives the more general kinematic relation
εs​=W−cp⋅ns​+ε(1−n⋅ns​)ε(W−cp⋅n)​.
(22)
Thus an unspecified crossing angle must not silently be identified with head-on incidence; the boxed result is the maximum for the stated head-on soft-photon arrangement.
Numerically W=2GeV gives γ=3913.89, and a 1cm photon has energy 1.23984×10−4eV. The maximum scattered energy is 7.5970keV, giving
λscattered​=1.6320×10−10m=0.16320nm.​
(23)
The rest-frame incident photon is only about one electronvolt, so the small recoil correction is consistent with the amplification estimate.

a OurBigBook.com
Three useful observational phenomena are relativistic Doppler effect in spectral lines from approaching and receding ejecta, relativistic beaming through jet brightness asymmetry, and apparent superluminal motion inferred from successive radio images of moving knots. The first compares spectral frequencies with rest-frame lines; the second compares the two sides after accounting for intrinsic emission and absorption; the third tests the light-travel-time relation βapp​=βsinϑ/(1−βcosϑ). NRAO's time sequence of Galactic microquasar ejecta gives a concrete example of the third measurement.[ref]
These are tests of relativistic radiation kinematics when source geometry and environmental effects are controlled, rather than claims that any one redshift or brightness contrast by itself uniquely proves special relativity.
For the frequency transformation, a photon has wave four-vector K=(ω/c,k) with ∣k∣=ω/c in vacuum. The temporal component of a Lorentz boost is
cω′​=γv​(cω​−cv⋅k​),ω′=γv​ω(1−cv​cosθ).​
(24)
This is the relativistic Doppler effect: frequency changes both because of relative motion along the ray and because clocks in relative motion are time dilated. The angle here is measured in S. A frame chasing the photon redshifts it, ω′/ω=(1−β)/(1+β)​; a head-on frame blueshifts it. Transverse angles defined in different frames must not be interchanged.

The phase velocity is the speed of a constant-phase surface, vph​=ω/k. The group velocity is the envelope speed of a narrow packet, vgr​=dω/dk, requiring a local dispersion relation rather than only a single monochromatic wave. In an isotropic transparent medium with k=n(ω)ω/c,
vph​=n(ω)c​,vgr​=n(ω)+ωdn/dωc​.​
(25)
For a vacuum wave, n≡1 over the packet bandwidth, so both speeds are c and vph​vgr​=c2​. No superluminal signalling is implied by a phase speed in a dispersive material.
Strictly, the value n(ω0​)=1 at one frequency alone does not prove the product identity. For example n(ω)=1+q(ω−ω0​)/ω0​ gives vph​(ω0​)=c but vgr​(ω0​)=c/(1+q). The vacuum interpretation, or an equivalent nondispersive assumption, is necessary.

Keep the unequal masses: equal and opposite velocities are not equal and opposite momenta. Write the rest energies r=me​c2, R=Mp​c2, S=r+R, and let the incoming Lorentz factor of each particle be γ. Then
Etot​=γS,cpz​=γβ(r−R),Etot2​−c2pz2​=(R−r)2+4rRγ2.
(26)
The transverse photon has energy ε=hc/λ and no z momentum. If the final composite has rest energy Q, squaring its four-momentum gives the exact constraint
Q2=(R−r)2+4rRγ2−2εγS.​
(27)
The mixed term contains no longitudinal momentum contribution because the photon is transverse. For a specified final rest energy, the physical positive root is
γ=4rRεS+ε2S2+4rR[Q2−(R−r)2]​​,
(28)
provided it is real and at least one.
The source does not specify the composite rest mass or its binding energy. With the usual approximation that the lowest final rest energy is Q=S, the minimum incoming energy occurs at that lowest mass and is
Etot,min​=S[χ+1+χ2​],χ=4rRεS​=4μc2λhc​,μ=me​+Mp​me​Mp​​.​
(29)
If a hydrogen bound state is intended at precision sensitive to its binding, replace Q by its actual rest energy in the preceding equation; the mass cannot be inferred exactly from the phrase “single particle.” Higher composite excitation raises the required incoming energy on this branch. Setting initial total momentum to zero would give a different answer and contradict the stipulated equal-speed electron and proton.

For a forward wave, the Faraday's law of induction fixes the relative amplitudes and signs:
B0​=ω1​k×E0​,B0​=−ωk​E0​=−cn​E0​.
(30)
Thus the two scalar amplitudes in the source cannot both be positive when the stated propagation direction and axes are used. The supplied product magnitude fixes only an overall amplitude in the chosen field units.
One convenient potential representative is
φ=0,A=−y^​ωE0​​sin(ωt−kz),Kμ=(ω/c,0,0,nω/c).​
(31)
Indeed −∂t​A gives the electric field and ∇×A gives the signed magnetic field above. Moreover ∇⋅A+c−2∂t​φ=0, the Lorenz gauge condition. The potentials are not unique because residual gauge transformations may preserve this condition.
Since n>1, the electromagnetic electromagnetic field invariants satisfy E⋅B=0 and B2−E2/c2=(n2−1)E2/c2>0 away from field nodes. A boost along positive z with speed u=c/n gives
Ey′​=γu​(Ey​+uBx​)=0,Bx′​=−cE0​​n2−1​cos(ω′t′−k′z′).
(32)
Here ω′=γu​(ω−uk)=0 and k′=(ω/c)n2−1​. The wave becomes a static spatially varying magnetic pattern in this frame. This is possible because its medium wave four-vector is spacelike, K2=(1−n2)ω2/c2<0; it is not a vacuum-photon rest frame. The medium moves in the new frame, so its original rest-frame constitutive relation must not be reused there unchanged.
No inertial frame makes the magnetic field vanish throughout a nontrivial wave: that would turn the positive invariant B2−E2/c2 into a nonpositive one. Both fields vanish at isolated nodes already, which is distinct from removing a field everywhere.

Take Aμ=(φ/c,A), ∂μ​=(c−1∂t​,∇) and Jμ=(cρ,j). Then
E=−∇φ−∂t​A,B=∇×A,F0i=−Ei​/c,Fij=−ϵijkBk​.
(33)
Expanding the cyclic derivative of Fab=∂aAb−∂bAa produces six second derivatives, which cancel in pairs because partial derivatives commute. Hence
∂cFab+∂aFbc+∂bFca=0.​
(34)
This Bianchi identity contains ∇⋅B=0 and ∇×E=−∂t​B. The inhomogeneous Maxwell equations are
∂μ​Fμν=μ0​Jν,∂μFμν​=μ0​Jν​.​
(35)
The temporal component is ∇⋅E=ρ/ϵ0​, since ϵ0​μ0​c2=1; the spatial components give ∇×B−c−2∂t​E=μ0​j. The source suppresses the coupling factor, as in units with μ0​=1 or a rescaled current.
For the physical Lorentz force density on matter, the consistent tensor formula is
fμ=FμνJν​=(cj⋅E​,ρE+j×B),fμ​=Fμν​Jν.​
(36)
Its temporal component is power transferred to matter per unit volume divided by c. The printed expression JνFνμ​ has the opposite sign for the stated definition of F in this convention. A stationary positive charge in a positive x electric field is a concrete check: the physical upper spatial force is +ρEx​, whereas the printed expression, raised to an upper index, gives −ρEx​. It can represent the opposite momentum transfer to the electromagnetic field, but must not be identified with the force on the charge without changing a convention.

In the massive particle's rest frame, P=(m0​c,0) and the physical Pauli-Lubanski pseudovector is W=(0,m0​csrest​), up to a common overall sign. Their Minkowski inner product is therefore zero. Since it is a Lorentz scalar,
W⋅P=0,UW​⋅P=0​
(37)
in every inertial frame, for any nonzero scalar normalization of W. This proves the requested orthogonality without a boosted component calculation.
The printed assertion that its particular normalization is always a unit vector needs qualification. For the displayed components W=(s⋅p,Es/c),
W2=−m02​c2∣s∣2−∣s×p∣2.
(38)
Thus dividing by m0​c∣s∣ is unit spacelike only when s×p=0, or when s in that denominator is explicitly replaced by the proper rest-spin magnitude with correctly boosted components of W. As a counterexample to the literal displayed normalization, choose s⊥p with nonzero momentum: it gives UW2​=−γ2, not −1. Orthogonality remains true, but it does not establish normalization.

An isolated free electron in vacuum cannot spontaneously emit a nonzero real photon. If P=Pf​+Q, where Q is the emitted photon four-momentum, P2=Pf2​=m2c2 and Q2=0, conservation would require Pf​⋅Q=0. A future-pointing timelike four-vector has strictly positive inner product with every nonzero future-pointing null four-vector, so this is impossible.
The same conclusion follows from the classical Cherenkov radiation condition v>c/n: in vacuum n=1, while the electron with γ=100 has β=1−10−4​<1. Hence there is no vacuum emission angle for the specified free particle. Acceleration by an external field or scattering from another body would change the physical problem and can permit radiation.

cosθC​=nβ1​.​

Here nβ=21−10−4​>1, so emission is allowed and

The final unnumbered force question has this inferred third subpart. For either wire's exterior field,
E⋅B=0,E2−c2B2=E2(1−v2/c2)>0.​
(43)
This electromagnetic field invariants is positive in every inertial frame. A purely magnetic nonzero field would instead have E′2−c2B′2<0. Thus no physical boost makes the interaction purely magnetic. There is a comoving frame with a purely electric interaction, which is consistent with the invariant's sign.
The convected charge current gives the same conclusion. Its effective line-current invariant is λ2−I2/c2=λ02​>0, so no inertial frame can neutralize the charge while leaving only a current. In particular for a longitudinal boost of speed u, λ′=γu​λ(1−uv/c2) cannot vanish when ∣u∣,∣v∣<c.

LF​=λE−IB=2πϵ0​dλ2​(1−v2/c2)=2πϵ0​dλ02​​>0.​
