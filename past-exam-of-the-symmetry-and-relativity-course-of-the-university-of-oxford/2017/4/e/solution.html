Let ε=Eph​ and γ=W/(M0​c2). For a head-on photon and particle collision, boost to the particle's initial rest frame. The incident photon energy is ε∗​=γ(1+β)ε. On the inverse-Compton branch ε<γβM0​c2, the maximal outgoing lab energy corresponds to rest-frame backscattering into the particle's forward direction. The exact Compton scattering recoil formula gives ε∗′​=ε∗​/[1+2ε∗​/(M0​c2)]. Boosting the backscattered photon back to the lab gives
εmax′​=1+2γ(1+β)ε/(M0​c2)γ2(1+β)2ε​.​
(46)
For γ≫1 this becomes
εmax′​≃1+4γε/(M0​c2)4γ2ε​.​
(47)
Only in the additional Thomson scattering limit 4γε≪M0​c2 may the denominator be neglected, yielding the printed estimate 4[W/(M0​c2)]2Eph​. Ultrarelativistic motion alone does not justify it. For example γ=1000 and ε=M0​c2 would give 4×106M0​c2 from that estimate, exceeding the available total energy; the recoil-corrected maximum is about 999.75M0​c2.
For an arbitrarily hard incident photon, maximizing over direction needs a further qualification: if ε≥γβM0​c2, the largest outgoing photon energy is the unchanged ε, rather than this amplified backscattering branch. The exact photon energy gain is Δεmax​=εmax′​−ε. Calling the outgoing energy the gain is accurate only when amplification is large. These distinctions prevent the inverse Compton scattering estimate from violating conservation of energy outside its low-rest-frame-photon-energy regime.
