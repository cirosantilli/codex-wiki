For the magnetic-dominated crossed-field orbit, choose V=E/B along x, which is subluminal when ∣E∣<c∣B∣. Then Ey′​=0 and Bz′​=B/Γ. The initial velocity in this frame is (−V,0,0), and the constant particle Lorentz factor is γ′=Γ because a magnetic field does no work.
Define the signed relativistic cyclotron frequency Ω=qBz′​/(Γme​), retaining q=−e. With t′ as parameter, the exact primed trajectory is
x′=−ΩV​sin(Ωt′),y′=ΩV​[1−cos(Ωt′)],z′=0.​
(18)
It is a circle of radius ∣V/Ω∣ with centre (0,V/Ω); its initial velocity is (−V,0,0) as required. Transforming back gives
x=ΓV[t′−Ωsin(Ωt′)​],y=ΩV​[1−cos(Ωt′)],t=Γ[t′−c2ΩV2​sin(Ωt′)].​
(19)
In the laboratory this is a stretched cycloidal trajectory with cusps where the electron is instantaneously at rest. Its average drift is ⟨vx​⟩=V=E/B, along E×B for the positive field orientation. Its energy oscillates as the electric field exchanges energy with it; it does not accelerate without bound. If E=0, the initially stationary electron remains stationary. At ∣E∣=c∣B∣, neither of these finite boosts exists; that separate null-field limit is not one of the two strict cases requested.
