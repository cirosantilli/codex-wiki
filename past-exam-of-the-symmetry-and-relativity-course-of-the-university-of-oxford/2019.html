f OurBigBook.com
For causal reachability with periodic time, let Pt​=2πT. The lower-right panel of part c identifies its top and bottom edges. The vertical curve at fixed x is a closed timelike curve: after a positive proper duration Pt​, it returns to the same spacetime event. The quotient is a mathematically well-defined flat Lorentzian manifold, but it violates chronology and admits no usual global causal ordering or ordinary global initial-value evolution.
These are physical causality problems, rather than an algebraic contradiction in the metric. Fields and particle histories must satisfy periodic consistency conditions; an arbitrary initial state followed by irreversible evolution will generally fail to return to itself after a temporal lap. The fact that the causal past and causal future overlap everywhere, established below, explains why the ordinary three-way causal partition degenerates.

An invariant has the same value when the same physical event or state is described in different inertial frames. A conserved quantity has the same value as the physical system evolves, subject to its equations and isolation conditions. These are different comparisons. The invariant mass of a heated object can change with time, while an isolated system's conserved energy has different numerical values in boosted frames.
Under a Lorentz transformation x′μ=Λμν​xν, a Lorentz four-vector transforms as
V′μ(x′)=Λμν​Vν(x).​
(43)
A covector transforms by the inverse matrix, Wμ′​=(Λ−1)νμ​Wν​. A general tensor has one corresponding factor for each index; for example,
T′μνρ​(x′)=Λμα​Λνβ​(Λ−1)γρ​Tαβγ​(x).​
(44)
Contraction with the Minkowski metric produces Lorentz scalars; it does not by itself make those scalars conserved.

To prove the vanishing component theorem for a Lorentz four-vector, first suppose Y0=0 in every inertial frame. An infinitesimal boost along spatial direction i gives Y′0=Y0−βYi+O(β2). It too vanishes for every β, so each Yi=0. If instead a specified spatial component vanishes in every frame, spatial rotations first imply that all three spatial components vanish in a chosen frame, and a boost in the specified direction then forces Y0=0. Therefore
one specified component zero in every Lorentz frame ⟹ Yμ=0.​
(45)
A physical consequence is that conservation of energy in every inertial frame implies full four-momentum conservation: apply the result to the difference between outgoing and incoming total four-momenta.
The statement fails for general tensors. A nonzero antisymmetric rank-two tensor has F00=0 in every frame, because antisymmetry is preserved. For an off-diagonal component, the nonzero Minkowski metric is another counterexample: η01=0 in all orthonormal Lorentz frames, while η is certainly not zero. The transformation law can preserve structural zero components without forcing the entire tensor to vanish.

A pion mass gives a Yukawa potential range of order a femtometre, so meson exchange can describe the short range of the residual nuclear force. The nuclear suppression relative to a massless potential of the same coupling is only about a factor of two at one femtometre. At atomic separations both massive exchange potentials are exponentially negligible. Molecular binding needs an interaction that survives at atomic distances; actual chemical bonds is governed principally by electromagnetism and quantum mechanics, with a massless photon rather than a scalar mediator. c OurBigBook.com
A relativistic two-component spinor is an element of a two-dimensional complex representation of the special linear group SL(2,C), the double cover of the proper orthochronous Lorentz group. A Weyl spinor transforms in either (1/2,0) or (0,1/2); parity interchanges the two chiralities. It describes a chiral spin-1/2 degree of freedom. A free massless Weyl field has one particle helicity and the opposite antiparticle helicity.
The number of components alone does not prove zero mass. A massive Dirac field has two coupled two-component chiral fields, and a nonrelativistic Pauli spinor describes the two spin states of a massive spin-1/2 particle. A Majorana mass term can also be written using a two-component field and its complex conjugate. Thus a single independent free massless Weyl equation is a dynamical assumption, not part of the bare algebraic definition of a two-component spinor.
