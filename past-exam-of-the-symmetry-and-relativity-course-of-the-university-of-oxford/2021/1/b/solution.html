Write Γ=(1−v2/c2)−1/2 for the Lorentz factor of the inertial frame S′, distinguishing it from the particles' changing Lorentz factors. Apply the Lorentz transformation
x′=Γ(x−vt),t′=Γ(t−vx/c2)
(3)
to each world line in a. Both particles initially have velocity −v in S′. The switching events have coordinates
(te′​,xe′​)=(0,0),(tp′​,xp′​)=(−Γvd/c2,Γd).
(4)
Thus the Positron starts accelerating earlier in S′. Its velocity first becomes less negative, vanishes when its laboratory velocity reaches v, and then becomes positive. The electron continues at −v until t′=0, then accelerates toward −c. The relativistic velocity-addition formula makes this precise:
ue′​=1−vue​/c2ue​−v​,up′​=1−vup​/c2up​−v​.
(5)
The electric field parallel to the Lorentz boost has unchanged magnitude, but its switching surface is t′+vx′/c2=0, rather than t′=0 everywhere.
In the laboratory, the total three-momentum is zero for all t: the two Lorentz forces cancel at equal laboratory times. In S′, each active force is still qE, since this is one-dimensional motion with a parallel electric field. Define tp′​=−Γvd/c2. For v>0 the total three-momentum, evaluated at equal t′, is
Px′​(t′)=⎩⎨⎧​−2Γmv,−2Γmv+F(t′−tp′​),−2Γmv+ΓFvd/c2,​t′<tp′​,tp′​≤t′<0,t′≥0.​​
(6)
This follows by integrating dpp′​/dt′=F from its switching event and dpe′​/dt′=−F from its later switching event. During the interval between them only the Positron feels the prescribed electric field, so momentum conservation for the particle pair fails. Once both forces act, the total three-momentum is constant again.
The equal-time momentum of an externally driven particle pair illustrates a distinction. The sum of particle four-momenta at a fixed pair of events is always a four-vector. However, a fresh sum at equal time in each inertial frame generally uses different events. Here transforming an equal-t laboratory sum would give −2Γvm2c4+F2c2t2​/c2, which varies with t; this differs from the equal-t′ answer above. For example, with c=m=F=d=1 and v=0.6, the late-time equal-t′ sum is −0.75, whereas transforming the equal-t=0 sum gives −1.5. This is a concrete failure of treating the pair's equal-time sums as components of a single frame-independent four-vector.
A closed system's total four-momentum, including the electromagnetic field and the apparatus supplying it, is conserved and independent of the choice of spacelike integration surface under the usual isolated-system boundary conditions. The particles alone exchange energy and momentum with an external system, so neither that independence nor universal particle-only momentum conservation is guaranteed.
