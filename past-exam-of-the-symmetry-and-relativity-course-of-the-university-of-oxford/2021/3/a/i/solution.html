Assume E,B>0 for the directions and parametrizations; the boost formulas retain their signed meaning for reversed fields. A Lorentz boost with velocity wz^ along E×B transforms the transverse electric field and magnetic field as
Ex′​=γw​(E−wB),By′​=γw​(B−wE/c2).
(21)
In the electric-dominated case choose
w=E2c2E×B​=Ec2B​z^,B′=0,Ex′​=E1−c2B2/E2​.​
(22)
The inequality ensures ∣w∣<c, so this is an inertial frame. The field-removing boosts for perpendicular electric and magnetic fields are governed by the electromagnetic field invariants E⋅B=0 and E2−c2B2>0 explain why a purely electric inertial frame exists and a purely magnetic one does not.
The electron is not initially at rest in this convenient inertial frame: its initial velocity is −wz^ and its initial three-momentum is pz′​=−γw​mw. Thereafter px′​=−eE′t′, while pz′​ stays constant. The electric field accelerates it toward −x; as its relativistic energy increases, its negative transverse velocity decreases in magnitude. Defining a=eE′/m>0 and s=aτ/c gives a useful parametric world line:
t′=aγw​c​sinhs,x′=−aγw​c2​(coshs−1),z′=−aγw​wc​s.
(23)
Here τ is the proper time. These formulas follow by integrating U′0=γw​ccoshs, U′x=−γw​csinhs, and constant U′z=−γw​w. Its projection onto the t′,x′ plane is a hyperbola, but the spatial trajectory includes the initial transverse motion and is not a straight line.
Transforming back with z=γw​(z′+wt′), t=γw​(t′+wz′/c2) gives
x=x′,z=aγw2​wc​(sinhs−s),t=aγw2​c​(sinhs−w2s/c2).
(24)
The laboratory trajectory starts from rest toward −x, then bends toward +z. It is unbounded in −x and ultimately has uz​→w=c2B/E and ux​→−c/γw​. Thus the late velocity has magnitude c as a limit, consistent with the relativistic energy-momentum relation, rather than undergoing a closed cyclotron orbit.
