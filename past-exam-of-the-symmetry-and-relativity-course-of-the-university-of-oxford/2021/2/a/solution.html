Let zμ(τ) be the point electric charge's world line, with proper time τ and four-velocity Uμ=dzμ/dτ=γ(c,u). For the field event xμ, choose the retarded source event: Rμ=xμ−zμ(τret​) is future-directed and null. Write Rμ=(R,Rn), where R=∣x−z(tret​)∣=c(t−tret​). In the instantaneous rest inertial frame of that source event,
U′μ=(c,0),−Rμ′​U′μ=cR′,A′μ=(4πϵ0​cR′q​,0).
(10)
Consequently the covariant expression matching this Coulomb potential is
Aμ(x)=4πϵ0​q​−Rν​UνUμ/c​​ret​.​
(11)
The denominator is a Lorentz scalar, so the right-hand side is a four-vector with the required instantaneous-rest components. This describes the retarded potential in Lorenz gauge, with no added free electromagnetic wave. Lorentz covariance alone does not establish the boundary conditions or exclude additional terms depending on four-acceleration. The physical input is the retarded point-source solution of Maxwell equations: the potential at the retarded event depends on the instantaneous four-current, while its derivatives generate the acceleration field. Equivalently, evaluating the retarded source integral gives a Jacobian 1/(1−n⋅β) from the light-cone constraint; this fixes that input for arbitrary smooth subluminal motion.[ref]
In any inertial frame, put β=u/c and κ=1−n⋅β. Then −R⋅U=γcRκ, and the Liénard–Wiechert potential is
ϕ=4πϵ0​Rκq​,A=c2u​ϕ.
(12)
Differentiate using E=−∇ϕ−∂t​A and B=∇×A, including the position and time dependence of the retarded time. For example, ∂tret​/∂t=1/κ and ∇tret​=−n/(cκ). The resulting Liénard-Wiechert field is
E=4πϵ0​q​[κ3R2(1−β2)(n−β)​+cκ3Rn×[(n−β)×β˙​]​]ret​,B=c1​[n×E]ret​.​
(13)
Here the dot denotes a derivative with respect to source coordinate time. The first term is the velocity or non-radiative field: it falls as R−2 and exists even without acceleration. The second is the acceleration or radiation field, falling as R−1 and transverse to n. Its Poynting flux falls as R−2, so its outward power integrated over a large sphere stays finite as its radius tends to infinity. The first term's squared-field flux instead vanishes in that limit. This large-distance distinction defines radiation; a moving near field can carry local energy without constituting radiation escaping to infinity.
