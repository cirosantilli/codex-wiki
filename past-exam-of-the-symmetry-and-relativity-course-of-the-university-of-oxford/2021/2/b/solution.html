euB=rpu​,p=eBr,γ=1+(me​ceBr​)2​.

The magnetic rigidity relation gives pc=0.299792458B[T]r[m]GeV=2.99792458GeV. Using me​c2=0.511MeV,
γ≃5.867×103,E=γme​c2≃2.998GeV.​
(15)
The coordinate centripetal acceleration is a=u2/r. For transverse acceleration, the magnitude of the four-acceleration, or proper acceleration, is a0​=γ2a. The supplied Larmor formula, in its proper acceleration form, therefore gives
P=6πϵ0​c3r2e2γ4u4​,T=u2πr​,ΔE=PT=3ϵ0​re2​γ4β3,β=u/c.
(16)
The emitted power here is also the laboratory total emitted power: in the instantaneous rest inertial frame the dipole radiation pattern has zero net radiated three-momentum, so both emitted energy and elapsed coordinate time gain the same Lorentz factor on transformation. This does not assert that a directional detector flux is invariant.
Numerically T≃1.048×10−5s, P≃2.18×10−10W per electron, and
ΔE≃2.29×10−15J≃1.43×104eV=14.3keVper revolution.​
(17)
The synchrotron's radiofrequency accelerating system must replace this lost energy to keep r and magnetic rigidity fixed over many revolutions. The fraction lost per turn is about 4.77×10−6, so a fixed-orbit calculation over one turn is self-consistent.[ref]
