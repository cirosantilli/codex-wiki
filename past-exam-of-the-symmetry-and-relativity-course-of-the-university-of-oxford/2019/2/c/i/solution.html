The following plots adopt a compact, optically thin cloud, weak excitation, isotropic incoherent spontaneous emission in the atomic rest frame, and negligible velocity dispersion. They show an intensity envelope as the observable amplitude; its rms electric-field amplitude is proportional to the square root of this intensity. An incoherent fluorescence signal has no fixed optical phase. The four rows include a nonrelativistic speed, both critical speeds, and an ultra-relativistic speed. All rows share one intensity normalization, so off-resonant signals remain visibly weak.

The population is an exponential memory of earlier absorption events, not an instantaneous resonance indicator. For a detector of fixed small area at the origin, its received bolometric intensity scales as
IR​(tR​(t))∝r(t)2Dout​(t)4​τN∗​(t)​.​
(39)
Two powers of Dout​ come from aberration of light, one from photon energy, and one from arrival-rate compression. Finite lifetime is dilated to γτ in the source frame; over an interval with slowly changing geometry its observed decay time is τ/Dout​. Thus approach can compress and amplify a pulse, while recession stretches and redshifts it. Illumination and collection each supply an inverse-square geometric factor in the prompt weak-excitation limit. Strong forward beaming can even make a detuned approaching precursor brighter than the later line-centre feature, as in the ultra-relativistic row; the intensity maximum need not occur at exact resonance.
Absolute amplitude and a unique detailed spectral line profile cannot be inferred from f,τ,v alone: cloud size, atom number, source power, optical depth, angular emission pattern and saturation were not specified. Finite cloud size convolves these point-centroid curves with additional delays. The plots describe incoherent, frequency-redistributed emission near f; a coherent Rayleigh scattering component would instead retain the driving frequency in the atom frame and add a different spectral component. These qualifications do not change the kinematic resonance thresholds or return-time relations.
