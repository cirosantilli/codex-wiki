Decreasing q=f0​/f increases the blueshift required for excitation. Both characteristic speeds move toward c:
βmin​=1+q21−q2​,β⊥​=1−q2​.
(40)
If q is near one, modest approach speeds can excite the line. If q≪1, only strongly relativistic motion reaches the line centre. At a fixed qualifying speed, decreasing q moves the resonance toward earlier approach, since a larger Din​ is required. It can also move the event outside the illumination window set by te​≥−T.
At fixed q, increasing β moves the resonance from the far approaching tail, through closest approach at β⊥​, and eventually to the receding side. The received pulse frequency cannot be estimated simply by applying the same blueshift twice: the inward and outward Doppler factors are different, and decay may occur well after excitation. The critical-speed rows of part i make these changes explicit.
