A relativistic two-component spinor is an element of a two-dimensional complex representation of the special linear group SL(2,C), the double cover of the proper orthochronous Lorentz group. A Weyl spinor transforms in either (1/2,0) or (0,1/2); parity interchanges the two chiralities. It describes a chiral spin-1/2 degree of freedom. A free massless Weyl field has one particle helicity and the opposite antiparticle helicity.
The number of components alone does not prove zero mass. A massive Dirac field has two coupled two-component chiral fields, and a nonrelativistic Pauli spinor describes the two spin states of a massive spin-1/2 particle. A Majorana mass term can also be written using a two-component field and its complex conjugate. Thus a single independent free massless Weyl equation is a dynamical assumption, not part of the bare algebraic definition of a two-component spinor.
