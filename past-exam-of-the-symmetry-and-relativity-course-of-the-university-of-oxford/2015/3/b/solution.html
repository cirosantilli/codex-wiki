is invariant. The subset need not comprise the entire plasma, and its particles need not move together. This expression includes cross terms between different particles; it is not the sum of their individual rest-mass squares in a.
The comparison must use the same subset and the transformed four-momenta at the same chosen events. Selecting “the lowest-energy half” anew in each frame need not do that. For example, take four photons along ±x, with energies E0​,2E0​ in each direction. The two lowest in the original frame are an opposite pair and have invariant energy squared 4E02​. After a boost at c/2 along +x, both forward photons are redshifted enough to become the lowest pair; that new collinear pair has zero invariant mass. This does not contradict covariance, because the subset changed.
If energies are resampled on a different simultaneity slice in an interacting plasma, they can also refer to different events. A physical conserved subsystem total must then include exchanged field energy and any boundary flux as appropriate. Such operational conservation requirements are distinct from the exact algebraic covariance of a sum of fixed four-vectors.
