Choose the source motion along +x, write β=v/c, and let primes denote its instantaneous rest frame. A photon with rest-frame energy E′ and direction θ′ has px′​=E′cosθ′/c and transverse momentum E′sinθ′/c. A Lorentz boost to the laboratory gives
E=γE′(1+βcosθ′),px​=γcE′​(cosθ′+β).
(1)
Since ∣p∣=E/c, the relativistic aberration relation and its inverse are
cosθ=1+βcosθ′cosθ′+β​,cosθ′=1−βcosθcosθ−β​.​
(2)
The mapping is monotone. The boundary of the rest-frame forward hemisphere, cosθ′=0, therefore maps to cosθ=β. An isotropic photon count assigns exactly half the photons to that hemisphere, proving
θ1/2​=cos−1β=sin−1(1/γ).​
(3)
The photon count inside a relativistic beaming cone concerns an emitted ensemble and counts photons, not their total energy or the arrival-time-weighted power at a detector. The laboratory photon energies depend on direction, so “half the photons” need not mean half the radiated energy. At large γ the angular scale is θ1/2​≃1/γ.

A fixed distant observer receives appreciable radiation while the velocity points within an angle of order 1/γ of the line of sight. The corresponding emission interval is Δtem​∼R/(γv). The source moves toward the detector during that interval, shortening the light-travel distance. In the far-field limit,
dtem​dtarr​​=1−βcosϕ,
(11)
where ϕ is the angle between velocity and the detector direction. Near the tangent, 1−βcosϕ∼γ−2, so
Δtarr​∼cγ3R​=9.25×10−18 s.​
(12)
The emission interval itself is of order 3.2×10−10 s. Omitting the arrival-time compression would therefore miss many orders of magnitude.
For a definite angular window −1/γ≤ϕ≤1/γ, integrating the arrival-time relation gives
Δtarr​=vR​[γ2​−2βsin(1/γ)]≃3cγ34R​=1.23×10−17 s.
(13)
The order-one coefficient depends on how the beam window or pulse width is defined. These are intrinsic single-electron radiation-flash scales. For bunch-length broadening of synchrotron radiation, a finite bunch moving through the same tangent point contributes an arrival-time spread of order its length divided by v; the longitudinal bunch profile and instrumental response must be convolved with the intrinsic flash. “Short bunch” alone does not specify an experimental bunch pulse duration at the attosecond scale, because no bunch length has been supplied.[ref]

A shorter radiation flash has a broader Fourier transform. From iii, its characteristic angular-frequency bandwidth is
Δω∼Δtarr−1​∼Rc​γ3.​
(14)
At fixed bending radius this scales as γ3 as v→c. The standard synchrotron critical angular frequency makes the same scaling explicit:
ωc​=2R3c​γ3.​
(15)
A periodically circulating electron produces harmonics of its revolution frequency. The very large harmonic range, of order γ3, forms the familiar effectively continuous broad synchrotron spectrum at ordinary detector resolution. The result concerns the intrinsic spectrum of the radiation, not merely the slow envelope imposed by a long incoherent bunch. Ordinary frequency is ν=ω/(2π); mixing cycles per second with radians per second would introduce a spurious factor of 2π into photon-energy estimates.[ref]

Regard f as a scalar field evaluated on a specified worldline xλ(τ). The ordinary multivariable chain rule gives
dτd​f(x(τ))=dτdxλ​∂xλ∂f​=uλ∂λ​f.​
(17)
This is the directional derivative along the four-velocity. With x0=ct, it reads df/dτ=γ(∂t​f+v⋅∇f). The spatial term is present because the worldline moves through the field; it would be lost by identifying a total derivative along the particle with a partial derivative at a fixed spatial position. For a scalar the contracted expression has the same value in all coordinate systems. For a tensor field the analogous covariant derivative also transports its indices.

Use uλuλ​=−c2 and a constant Minkowski metric. Vary the velocity-dependent square root before imposing the proper-time normalization. Equivalently, vary a reparametrization-invariant worldline action first and then choose proper time. Its canonical covector momentum is
∂ua∂L​=mc−u2​ua​​+qAa​ ⟶ mua​+qAa​.
(18)
The coordinate derivative at fixed velocity is ∂L/∂xa=quλ∂a​Aλ​. The Euler-Lagrange equation and the chain rule from a therefore give
mu˙a​+quλ∂λ​Aa​=quλ∂a​Aλ​.
(19)
Define the antisymmetric field tensor by
Fab​=∂a​Ab​−∂b​Aa​,Fab=∂aAb−∂bAa.​
(20)
The particle equation becomes
mdτdua​​=qFaλ​uλ.​
(21)
Antisymmetry ensures uaFaλ​uλ=0, so the force is orthogonal to the four-velocity and preserves u2=−c2. The worldline variation before proper-time normalization is essential: substituting −u2​=c into the Lagrangian before taking its velocity derivative would turn the kinetic term into a constant and incorrectly remove the inertial momentum. The normalization is a property of the proper-time solution, not permission to discard the variational dependence.

a OurBigBook.com
Each on-shell particle satisfies Ei2​−c2∣pi​∣2=mi2​c4. Thus for a selected subset A,
i∈A∑​(Ei2​−c2pi2​)=c4i∈A∑​mi2​.​
(31)
It is Lorentz invariant when the same particle labels and physical states are compared, with each energy and momentum transformed as that particle's four-momentum. No requirement that the particles share a velocity is needed. Their energies can change under interactions while their individual fixed rest masses remain the same.
A frame-dependent rule for choosing “half” is more delicate: different subsets can have different sums of rest-mass squares. If every particle has the same rest mass and the number selected is fixed, however, the displayed sum is unchanged even if the selected identities change. In a massless plasma it is identically zero for every subset. The precise condition is constancy of the selected scalar sum, not a blanket claim that every frame-dependent selection must change it.

b OurBigBook.com
The covariance of a selected particle momentum sum follows because, for a fixed set of particle four-momenta, their sum PAμ​=∑i∈A​piμ​ is a four-vector by linearity of the Lorentz transformation. Therefore
(i∈A∑​Ei​)2−c2​i∈A∑​pi​​2=−c2PAμ​PAμ​​
(32)
is invariant. The subset need not comprise the entire plasma, and its particles need not move together. This expression includes cross terms between different particles; it is not the sum of their individual rest-mass squares in a.
The comparison must use the same subset and the transformed four-momenta at the same chosen events. Selecting “the lowest-energy half” anew in each frame need not do that. For example, take four photons along ±x, with energies E0​,2E0​ in each direction. The two lowest in the original frame are an opposite pair and have invariant energy squared 4E02​. After a boost at c/2 along +x, both forward photons are redshifted enough to become the lowest pair; that new collinear pair has zero invariant mass. This does not contradict covariance, because the subset changed.
If energies are resampled on a different simultaneity slice in an interacting plasma, they can also refer to different events. A physical conserved subsystem total must then include exchanged field energy and any boundary flux as appropriate. Such operational conservation requirements are distinct from the exact algebraic covariance of a sum of fixed four-vectors.

t1​=22.1710 ns,t2​=84.5978 ns,r1​=(0,4.89455) m,r2​=(21.59875,−9.78910) m.​

a OurBigBook.com
Maintaining a fixed separation in one inertial laboratory frame is not the same as maintaining a fixed separation measured locally with the accelerating rockets. The attached string is prevented from undergoing the laboratory contraction that an unstrained comoving string would have. It is therefore stretched and develops tensile stress: this is Bell's spaceship paradox, not a failure of Lorentz contraction.
For sufficiently prolonged acceleration and a finite elastic breaking strain, the string breaks. If an acceleration history produces only a small bounded increase in speed, whether it actually breaks depends on its material strength; the kinematic conclusion is stretching, not an unconditional assertion that every arbitrarily weak acceleration must snap every string. Stress also propagates through a real string at a finite speed. The construction enforcing Born rigidity in the following parts is a different set of trajectories chosen to keep the comoving separation fixed.

Let A=(ctA​,f(tA​)) on the rear rocket and B=(ctB​,h(tB​)) on the front rocket. The instantaneous inertial rest frame of the rear rocket at A moves at v=f′(tA​) relative to S. Transform the time difference of these two events:
Δt′=γ[(tB​−tA​)−c2v​(h(tB​)−f(tA​))].
(42)
Setting Δt′=0 gives
c2(tB​−tA​)=v[h(tB​)−f(tA​)].​
(43)
The velocity belongs to the rear event A. It is not to be evaluated at laboratory time tB​, nor does the simultaneity condition mean tA​=tB​ in S. At nonzero velocity the front event on the same instantaneous-rest slice is at a different laboratory time.
