Single-beam gradient trapping experiment

 Home Physics Branches of physics Optics Optical tweezers
OurBigBook.com Words: 165
In 1986, Arthur Ashkin, Joseph Martin Dziedzic, John Ernst Bjorkholm and Steven Chu used spatially filtered 514.5nm argon laser light, a water-immersion objective of numerical aperture 1.25, and a water-filled glass cell. An auxiliary beam loaded large glass beads; dispersed small particles entered by Brownian motion. A side microscope and 90∘ scattering of light detector provided readout. Their Fig. 1 shows a 10μm bead below the focus with about 100mW. On p. 290, silica size was inferred by scattering against a latex standard; translating the cell imposed fluid drag. These observations support single-beam confinement, while inferred size depends on the scattering model.[ref]
A loading beam can help bring a bead into the capture region without supplying its subsequent confinement. Distinguish loading, holding and sensing when interpreting an apparatus drawing. The following optical trap position readout is a proposed calibration arrangement; its detector layout is not a reconstruction of this historical experiment.
