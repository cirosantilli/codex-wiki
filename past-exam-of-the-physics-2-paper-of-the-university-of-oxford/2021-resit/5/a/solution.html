In SI units, the Maxwell equations in vacuum with prescribed charge density ρ and current density J are
∇⋅E=ϵ0​ρ​,∇⋅B=0,∇×E=−∂t​B,∇×B=μ0​J+μ0​ϵ0​∂t​E.
(17)
When ρ=0 and J=0, take the curl of the third equation and substitute the fourth. Using the curl of the curl identity and ∇⋅E=0 gives the electromagnetic wave equation:
∇×(∇×E)=−∇2E=−μ0​ϵ0​∂t2​E,∇2E−c21​∂t2​E=0,c=(μ0​ϵ0​)−1/2.​
(18)
For example, E=E0​xcos(kz−ωt), B=(E0​/c)y​cos(kz−ωt) is a plane electromagnetic wave provided ω=ck. The electric field, magnetic field and propagation direction are mutually perpendicular.
The Maxwell equations are linear, so the superposition principle applies to fields. However, the Poynting vector is quadratic:
⟨S⟩=μ0​1​⟨E1​×B1​+E2​×B2​+E1​×B2​+E2​×B1​⟩.
(19)
One may add the average wave intensities only when the cross terms vanish under the actual detector average. For co-propagating plane electromagnetic waves of parallel linear polarization, averaging over optical cycles but potentially resolving the difference frequency gives
I(t)=I1​+I2​+2I1​I2​​cos[(ω1​−ω2​)t+ϕ].
(20)
If the detector integrates for T, the last term acquires sinc[(ω1​−ω2​)T/2], with sinc function convention sincx=sinx/x, evaluated at the midpoint phase. Thus T≫1/∣f1​−f2​∣ suppresses beats, and the infinite-time average adds the wave intensities exactly for distinct fixed frequencies. Random mutually uncorrelated wave phases also remove the cross terms in an ensemble average; orthogonal linear polarizations remove them for co-propagating waves in polarization-insensitive detection. Merely having different frequencies does not remove a resolved beat signal: two nearly equal frequencies with fixed relative wave phase are a concrete counterexample. For different propagation directions, the relevant addition is of average Poynting vectors, or their components through the chosen detector surface.
