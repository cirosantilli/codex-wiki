Let the original cylinder axis be r=0, and let the hole axis be at transverse vector a, with ∣a∣=a. The charge density is reproduced by a full cylinder of radius R1​ and charge density +ρ, superposed with a cylinder of radius R2​ centred at a and charge density −ρ. This uses the superposition principle for fixed insulating electric charges; the hole is not a hole in an electrical conductor.
For a complete uniformly charged infinite cylinder of radius R, a coaxial Gaussian surface of length ℓ gives, by Gauss's law,
2πsℓE(s)={ρπs2ℓ/ϵ0​,ρπR2ℓ/ϵ0​,​s<R,s≥R.​
(35)
Consequently define
FR​(s)={s,R2s/∣s∣2,​∣s∣≤R,∣s∣>R.​
(36)
The complete electric field, including the exterior if desired, is
E(r)=2ϵ0​ρ​[FR1​​(r)−FR2​​(r−a)].​
(37)
In the insulating material, ∣r∣<R1​ and s=∣r−a∣>R2​, so
Ematerial​(r)=2ϵ0​ρ​[r−∣r−a∣2R22​(r−a)​].​
(38)
Inside the hole both contributions use their interior formulas, and all dependence on r cancels:
Ehole​=2ϵ0​ρ​[r−(r−a)]=2ϵ0​ρ​a.​
(39)
The electric field in the hole is uniform, points from the original axis towards the hole axis for ρ>0, and vanishes for a concentric hole. The two expressions agree at the hole wall, as expected with no imposed surface charge density there. Its uniformity is the useful uniform electric field in an off-axis cylindrical cavity result.
