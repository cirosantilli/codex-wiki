The phrase “image” does not specify whether a real image or a virtual image is required. The later mention of an image plane makes a projected real image a natural interpretation. Let u>0 be object distance and v>0 real-image distance from a converging thin lens. A transverse magnification of magnitude 15 gives v=15u, and the object-to-image separation gives u+v=200mm. Hence
u=12.5mm,v=187.5mm,f=u+vuv​=11.71875mm.​
(24)
Place the optical lens 12.5mm beyond the object along the illuminating beam. The real image is inverted and has transverse magnification −15.
For the alternative single-optical lens magnifying glass interpretation, the virtual image is upright and lies on the object's side, so signed v=−15u. Its separation from the object is ∣v∣−u=14u=200mm. The thin lens equation then gives
u=7100​mm=14.286mm,v=−71500​mm,f=49750​mm=15.306mm.​
(25)
The source does not explicitly select between these two geometries; they should not be conflated with a 200mm lens-to-image distance.
