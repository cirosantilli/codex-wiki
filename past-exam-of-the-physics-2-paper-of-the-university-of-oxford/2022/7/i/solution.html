Take coordinate y across the slit and observation angle θ from its normal vector. Under Fraunhofer diffraction, the relative optical path difference from y is ysinθ. Uniform coherent illumination makes the complex amplitude proportional to the aperture integral:
E(θ)=K∫−w/2w/2​e−ikysinθdy=Kwβsinβ​,β=λπwsinθ​,k=λ2π​.
(27)
The light intensity is proportional to ∣E∣2, so
I(θ)=I(0)[πwsinθ/λsin(πwsinθ/λ)​]2.​
(28)
The central value is the continuous limit of the sinc function. The first minima satisfy sinθ=±λ/w, if λ≤w. Infinite slit height removes spreading in the other transverse direction; this expression describes the slit-width direction.
Strictly, distance D≫w alone does not ensure Fraunhofer diffraction: one also needs w2/(λD)≪1, so that quadratic aperture-dependent wave phase is negligible, or observation in a suitable focal plane. For example, w=1mm, λ=500nm and D=1m have D/w=1000 but w2/(λD)=2; the Fresnel diffraction correction is then substantial. The displayed result uses the intended far-field regime of the question and the usual scalar aperture approximation.
