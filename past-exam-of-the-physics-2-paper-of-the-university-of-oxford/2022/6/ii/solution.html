Past exam of the physics 2 paper of the University of Oxford / 2022 / 6 / ii / Solution

OurBigBook.com Words: 141
Approximate the atmosphere by concentric spherical layers of refractive index. At every boundary, Snell's law conserves nrsinψ, where ψ is the angle between the light ray and the radius. Straight propagation between boundaries conserves the same quantity because rsinψ is the distance of that line from the origin. In the smooth limit this is the spherical ray invariant
p=n(r)rsinψ.
(25)
At the surface, a light ray apparently on the horizon is tangent, so p=ns​R. In vacuum far from the Earth, n=1, and the straight incident line has distance p from the centre. Removing the atmosphere while retaining that incident line gives minimum altitude
H=p−R=(ns​−1)R=0.0003(6400km)=1.92km.​
(26)
The detailed variation of refractive index with altitude changes the local curvature of the light ray, but not the conserved p. This conclusion assumes a spherically stratified atmosphere and a light ray which actually connects the surface to space; nonspherical structure or a trapping layer would require separate analysis.
