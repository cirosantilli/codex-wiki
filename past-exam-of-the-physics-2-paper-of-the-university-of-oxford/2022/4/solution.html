Choose zero electrostatic potential at infinity. By Coulomb's law and the superposition principle, the two point charges give
Φ(r,θ)=4πϵ0​1​[r2+r12​−2rr1​cosθ​q1​​+r2+r22​−2rr2​cosθ​q2​​].​
(13)
Here ϵ0​ is the vacuum permittivity. For a nondegenerate zero-equipotential curve, the electric charges must have opposite signs. Let d1​,d2​ be the two distances. The equation Φ=0 becomes q1​d2​=−q2​d1​, and hence
q12​(r2+r22​−2rr2​cosθ)=q22​(r2+r12​−2rr1​cosθ).
(14)
For a circle r=ρ>0 centred at the origin, its cosθ coefficient must vanish: q12​r2​=q22​r1​. If r1​,r2​>0 and r1​=r2​, this gives
q2​=−q1​r2​/r1​​.​
(15)
Substitute q22​/q12​=r2​/r1​ in the constant term:
r1​ρ2+r1​r22​=r2​ρ2+r2​r12​,(r1​−r2​)(ρ2−r1​r2​)=0.
(16)
Therefore
ρ=r1​r2​​.​
(17)
This is an Apollonius circle: the distance ratio d2​/d1​ is constant. The distinct-position assumption matters. For example, r1​=r2​=1, q2​=−q1​ gives zero electrostatic potential everywhere away from the coincident point charges, so there is no distinguished zero circle. If only one position is at the origin and the other is not, no nontrivial positive-radius circle centred at the origin is possible.
