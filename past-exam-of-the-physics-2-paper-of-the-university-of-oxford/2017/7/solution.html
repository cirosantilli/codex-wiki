The original circuit has R1​ and C in series between the input and the inverting node, with R2​ providing feedback and the noninverting node grounded. An ideal operational amplifier in stable linear negative feedback draws no input electric current and holds the inverting node at virtual ground. For phasors proportional to eiωt, the input complex impedance is Z1​=R1​+1/(iωC). Kirchhoff's current law gives Vin​/Z1​=−Vout​/R2​, hence
H(ω)=Vin​Vout​​=−1+iωR1​CiωR2​C​,∣H∣=1+ω2R12​C2​ωR2​C​.​
(14)
This is an inverting high-pass filter. Its phase can be chosen continuously as
argH=−2π​−tan−1(ωR1​C).​
(15)
Thus Vout​(t)=V0​∣H∣cos(ωt+argH). The ratio above is a phasor ratio, not an instantaneous ratio at input zero crossings. Setting R1​=R2​ does not remove the capacitor's phase: the output lags by 90∘+tan−1(ωR1​C), between 90∘ and 180∘. At ωR1​C=1 the lag is 135∘; only for ωR1​C≫1 does it approach 180∘. Being within the amplifier's own bandwidth does not specify the external RC corner frequency.
