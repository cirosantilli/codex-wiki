Take the conducting plane as y=0, with the real point charges Q at (0,b) and −2Q at (a,b). The method of images replaces the plane by image charges −Q at (0,−b) and +2Q at (a,−b). This makes the plane an equipotential; the physical field above it is the sum of the real- and image-charge fields. No factor of 1/2 enters the force on a real charge.
Set ke​=1/(4πϵ0​) and d=a2+4b2​, and use positive x from Q toward −2Q, positive y upward. The horizontal forces combine the mutual attraction and the force from the other charge's image:
FQ,x​=2ke​Q2(a21​−d3a​),F−2Q,x​=−FQ,x​.​
(1)
For the vertical components, the own-image attractions act downward; each cross-image term acts upward. Thus
FQ,y​=ke​Q2(−4b21​+d34b​),F−2Q,y​=ke​Q2(−b21​+d34b​).​
(2)
These are the electrostatic forces; the external forces holding the charges fixed are their negatives. The smaller positive charge's vertical force need not always point downward, because the other charge's induced surface charge also contributes. The construction assumes the usual ideal large-plane limit with no imposed background field.

Let the wire radius be a, and take its electric current along +z. Cylindrical symmetry makes the magnetic flux density azimuthal. For a circular Amperian loop of radius r, Ampère's law gives 2πrB=μ0​Ienclosed​. Uniform current density means Ienclosed​=Ir2/a2 inside and I outside. Consequently
B(r)=⎩⎨⎧​2πa2μ0​Ir​ϕ^​,2πrμ0​I​ϕ^​,​0≤r≤a,r≥a.​​
(6)
Its direction follows the right-hand rule. The magnitude rises linearly from zero to μ0​I/(2πa) at the surface, is continuous there, and then falls as 1/r.

For the intended force-balanced straight-line motion, the Lorentz force vanishes:
E+v×B=0.​
(7)
For a nonzero uniform magnetic field, this implies E⋅B=0 and
v⊥​=B2E×B​,
(8)
while a component parallel to B is unrestricted. A straight trajectory by itself states only that the force has no transverse component, v×(E+v×B)=0. For example, parallel electric field and motion can give straight but accelerated motion. The boxed cancellation is the usual constant-velocity interpretation of the source.

After the electric field is removed, the uniform magnetic field preserves the speed. Decompose the instantaneous initial velocity as v0​=v∥​+v⊥​, with b^=B/B and signed Ω=qB/m. Then
v(t)=v∥​+v⊥​cosΩt+(v⊥​×b^)sinΩt,
(9)
and, integrating,
r(t)=r0​+v∥​t+Ωv⊥​​sinΩt+Ωv⊥​×b^​(1−cosΩt).​
(10)
This is a helical motion in a uniform magnetic field of radius m∣v⊥​∣/(∣q∣B) and angular frequency ∣q∣B/m. It is a circle if v∥​=0, and a straight line if v⊥​=0. The formula assumes a uniform, nonzero field and nonrelativistic motion.

Huygens' principle treats each point of a wavefront as the source of a secondary wavelet. The later wavefront is the common forward envelope of those wavelets. Consider a plane front reaching a flat interface between media with speeds v1​ and v2​. In the same interval Δt, the incident front advances v1​Δt while the first transmitted wavelet grows to radius v2​Δt.
The two right triangles sharing the interface segment of length d give dsini=v1​Δt and dsinr=v2​Δt, where i and r are ray angles to the normal. Hence
sinrsini​=v2​v1​​=n1​n2​​,n1​sini=n2​sinr.​
(14)
This is Snell's law. The frequency is unchanged at a stationary interface, while wavelength changes with the propagation speed.

Let Is​ be the intensity that either narrow slit would produce alone, and let y measure screen displacement from the perpendicular bisector. The optical path difference is approximately δ=bsinθ≃by/D. Equal coherent field amplitudes then give
I=Is​+Is​+2Is​cos(λ2πδ​)=4Is​cos2(λDπby​)​.
(15)

Δy=bλD​=0.532mm.​
(16)
The expression uses the paraxial approximation and neglects the slowly varying single-slit diffraction envelope because the slit widths are unspecified.

The slide adds an optical path length Δ=(n−1)t=50μm in the covered path. If the covered slit is at positive transverse position, its geometric path to y>0 is shorter, giving total path difference Δ−by/D. The zero-difference fringe therefore moves toward that slit:

The band has mean wavelength λˉ≃530nm and width Δλ=20nm. At order m, the positions of the extreme-wavelength maxima differ by about ∣m∣DΔλ/b. Comparing this spread with a fringe spacing Dλˉ/b gives
∣m∣∼Δλλˉ​≃26.5.
(18)
Equivalently, the coherence length scale is ℓc​∼λˉ2/Δλ≃14μm. Thus the usual bandwidth estimate is
about 26 orders on either side, or order 50 fringes in total.​
(19)
This is a washout scale, not a sharp definition of “clearly”. For a spectrum uniform in inverse wavelength σ=1/λ, the interference visibility is ∣sinc(πΔσδ)∣, with sincu=sinu/u; its first zero occurs near order 26.5. Fringes well inside that limit have stronger contrast. A half-fringe-smear criterion would instead retain about 13 orders each side. The precise visible number needs the spectral distribution and a contrast threshold, neither supplied. If the slide remains in place and dispersion is neglected, the envelope is centred on the shifted zero-path-difference position and this order-count scale is unchanged.

At (x,0) the distances from the two centres are equal:
r=x2+a2/4​,x1​=23​​a,x2​=215​​a.

By the electrostatic shell theorem, a uniformly charged spherical shell gives zero field inside and its point-charge field outside. For x<x1​ the point is inside both shells. For x1​<x<x2​ only the small, negatively charged shell contributes. For x>x2​ both contribute, so
E(x)=⎩⎨⎧​0,r3ke​q​(−xx^+2a​y^​),r3ke​qa​y^​,​0≤x<x1​,x1​<x<x2​,x>x2​,​ke​=4πϵ0​1​.

The source diagram has 2R in series with two parallel branches: one containing R then L, and the other containing 2R. Let their common voltage be u(t), measured from the upper node to the lower return. Kirchhoff's current law and the branch laws give
I0​=I1​+I2​,u=2RI2​=RI1​+LdtdI1​​,V=2RI0​+u.
(26)

The switch was open for a long time, so I1​(0−)=0. Inductor current continuity gives I1​(0+)=0 for a finite applied voltage.

Maxwell's equations in matter, with free charge and current densities, are
∇⋅D=ρfree​,∇⋅B=0,∇×E=−∂t∂B​,∇×H=Jfree​+∂t∂D​.​

2 OurBigBook.com
For a charging parallel-plate capacitor of area A, neglect fringing. The field in the gap is E=Q/(ϵ0​A), so the displacement current through the gap is
ID​=ϵ0​dtd​∫E⋅dS=ϵ0​AdtdE​=dtdQ​=I.​
(32)
An Amperian loop around the feed wire can bound a surface cutting the wire, which has conduction current I, or a bulging surface passing through the dielectric gap, which has no conduction current. Including displacement current makes both surfaces give the same magnetic circulation. It is also required by charge conservation: the divergence of the Ampère-Maxwell equation and Gauss's law yields ∇⋅J+∂ρ/∂t=0, the continuity equation.

In charge- and current-free vacuum, ∇⋅E=∇⋅B=0. Taking the curl of Faraday's law and using the Ampère-Maxwell equation gives
∇×(∇×E)=−μ0​ϵ0​∂t2∂2E​.
(33)
Since ∇×(∇×E)=∇(∇⋅E)−∇2E, it follows that
∇2E=μ0​ϵ0​∂t2∂2E​,∇2B=μ0​ϵ0​∂t2∂2B​.​
(34)
Substituting a plane wave with phase k⋅r−ωt gives k2=μ0​ϵ0​ω2 and therefore
c=kω​=μ0​ϵ0​​1​≃3.00×108ms−1.​
(35)
The divergence equations make the waves transverse, and the curl equations fix B=k^×E/c.
