Let C denote the capacitance omitted after the word “capacitance” in the source sentence. For the ideal series RC charging circuit, Kirchhoff's voltage law gives RI+Q/C=V, with I=dQ/dt and Q(0)=0. Therefore
Q(t)=CV(1−e−t/τ),I(t)=RV​e−t/τ,τ=RC.​
(12)
The electric current falls from V/R to zero, while the electric charge rises from zero to CV. Their initial slopes are −V/(Rτ) and V/R, respectively.
Reducing RC shortens the approach to the relevant final values. Which heights change depends on the component changed: reducing R at fixed C increases I(0) but leaves Q(∞) unchanged; reducing C at fixed R leaves I(0) unchanged but reduces Q(∞). A shorter time constant alone does not specify both endpoint amplitudes.

For a proton, the fundamental cyclotron resonance condition is
ω=mp​eB​.​
(15)
A half-orbit takes πmp​/(eB), during which the applied voltage reverses sign once. The proton then crosses the gap in the opposite direction and receives another accelerating impulse. The electric force in the gap, rather than the magnetic bending inside a dee, provides the kinetic energy. The injection phase must be chosen so the first crossing accelerates the proton.

Fermat's principle states that the actual ray makes the travel time stationary under small variations of its path with endpoints fixed. Equivalently, for a stationary medium it makes the optical path length ∫nds stationary. “Stationary” allows minima, maxima, or other stationary paths; it does not require a strict minimum in every optical system.

In wave optics, a finite lens aperture produces diffraction. Contributions interfere constructively near the geometric focus but form a finite point spread function, rather than a point of infinite intensity. For a uniformly illuminated circular aperture of diameter D, the paraxial focal pattern is an Airy disk, with first dark-ring radius approximately 1.22λf/D. Aberrations can broaden or distort it further; even an ideal lens retains the finite diffraction scale.
