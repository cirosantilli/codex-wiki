The original diagram has an ideal 5V electric battery feeding series resistors of 8Ω above the tapping point and 2Ω below it. The long battery bar is positive, so terminal A is above terminal B in electric potential. With an open circuit at the output, the internal electric current is 5/(8+2)=0.5A. The voltage divider therefore gives
E0​=VA​−VB​=(0.5A)(2Ω)=1.0V.​
(16)
For the Thévenin resistance, suppress the ideal voltage source by replacing it with a short circuit. The two resistors are then in parallel as seen from A,B:
RTh​=8+28×2​Ω=1.6Ω.​
(17)
By Thévenin's theorem, the terminal voltage under an outward load electric current I is VA​−VB​=E0​−IRTh​. Consequently
Isc​=RTh​E0​​=0.625A,​
(18)
flowing through an external short circuit from A to B. Directly shorting the original terminals bypasses the 2Ω resistor and leaves 5V across 8Ω, independently confirming the result.
