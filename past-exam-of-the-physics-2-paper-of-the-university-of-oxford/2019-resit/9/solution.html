For the subsequent charging operation, take the initial outer shell to be earthed, or interpret the stated V as the inner-to-outer voltage with the outer reference zero. Isolating the inner shell fixes its electric charge at q=CV. Removing the outer shell leaves an isolated conducting sphere. Its electric field and electric potential, now with zero at infinity, are

These changes of electric potential are compatible with constant electric charge. Without the initial reference convention the absolute stated V does not determine q=CV: for example, if the outer shell were initially isolated and neutral, the absolute inner electric potential would instead be q/(4πϵ0​a).
Finally take the connected spheres to be sufficiently far apart that the long wire's capacitance and their mutual influence can be neglected. Their common electric potential requires
aqa​​=2aq2a​​,qa​+q2a​=q,
(42)
so qa​=q/3 and q2a​=2q/3. The magnitudes of the surface electric fields are
Ea​=12πϵ0​a2q​,E2a​=24πϵ0​a2q​,Ea​:E2a​=2:1.​

This is the usual widely separated-sphere approximation. A wire enforces an equipotential, but alone does not make this ratio exact for arbitrary arrangements. As a concrete counterexample, if the second shell enclosed the first concentrically and they were connected, all net electric charge would reside on the exterior and the inner sphere's surface electric field would be zero.
