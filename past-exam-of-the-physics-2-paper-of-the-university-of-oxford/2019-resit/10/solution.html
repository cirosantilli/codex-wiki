In the Huygens-Fresnel principle construction, after a time Δt the incident disturbance advances v1​Δt while the secondary disturbance in the second medium advances v2​Δt. The two right triangles share the segment along the interface. Hence
sinθ2​sinθ1​​=v2​v1​​=n1​n2​​,θ2​=arcsin(n2​n1​​sinθ1​).​
(44)
Here both angles are measured from the normal vector, and vj​=c/nj​ are phase velocities. This is Snell's law. For n1​>n2​, no real transmitted angle exists when
θ1​>θc​=arcsin(n2​/n1​).​
(45)
This is total internal reflection; equality is the limiting grazing geometry. The statement concerns an ideal transparent interface, not material absorption or losses at a nearby second boundary.
Let β be the internal angle to the optical axis in the straight step-index optical fiber. At its flat entrance, Snell's law gives sinθi​=n1​sinβ. At the side wall a meridional ray has angle of incidence π/2−β. The limiting total internal reflection condition is cosβ=n2​/n1​. Thus the optical fiber acceptance angle satisfies
sinθi,max​=n12​−n22​​≡NA,θi,max​=arcsinn12​−n22​​.​
(46)
The numerical aperture expression assumes the square root is at most one for air; otherwise all forward launch angles are admitted by this ideal ray test. Strictly confined meridional rays lie inside the limiting cone.
For n1​=1.48 and n2​=1.46, NA=0.242487 and θi,max​=14.0334∘. A light ray at internal angle β travels distance L/cosβ along a guide of axial length L. In the nondispersive approximation its travel time is t=n1​L/(ccosβ). Therefore
tmax​thalf​​=cn2​n12​L​=5.00438μs,=c1−sin2(θi,max​/2)/n12​​n1​L​=4.95365μs,​Δt≃50.7ns.​
(47)
Here L=1000m and c=299792458ms−1; using 3.00×108ms−1 gives 5.001μs and 4.950μs. The halving applies to the external launch angle, not to β. For actual short pulses the group refractive index ng​ replaces the prefactor n1​ in t=ng​L/(ccosβ); the source supplies no optical dispersion data. The difference calculated here is geometrical intermodal dispersion in a nondispersive geometrical optics model.
Bending lowers the outer-wall angle of incidence, allowing a previously guided light ray to escape into the cladding. To calculate a definite meridional ray bend threshold, continue the on-axis launch assumption and take a meridional ray in the plane of the circular bend. Its initial internal angle to the centreline tangent obeys sinβ=sin10∘/n1​. Its straight path has distance bray​=Rcosβ from the bend centre. At the outer wall of radius R+r, elementary right-triangle geometry gives
siniout​=R+rRcosβ​.
(48)
At the loss threshold, this equals n2​/n1​. Solving for the centreline radius gives
Rc​=n1​cosβ−n2​n2​r​=n12​−sin210∘​−n2​n2​r​=14.93mm.​
(49)
For this launch, R<Rc​ violates the total internal reflection condition at the outer wall; R=Rc​ is the limiting case. A real optical fiber has gradual bend loss governed by its electromagnetic wave modes, rather than a universal abrupt cutoff.
Launch dependence. The final sentence does not separately specify the entry position or orientation of the bend relative to the incident light ray. For a planar launch displaced by u from the centreline toward the outer wall, replace the numerator of siniout​ by (R+u)cosβ. For example, a launch at the inner edge, u=−r, instead gives
Rc,inner​=rn1​cosβ−n2​n1​cosβ+n2​​=29.96mm.
(50)
Thus 14.93mm is the result for the stated on-axis continuation; it is not an entry-independent threshold. No missing figure is needed for these explicitly specified ray models.
