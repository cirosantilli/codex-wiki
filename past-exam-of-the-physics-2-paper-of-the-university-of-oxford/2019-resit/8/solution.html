Faraday's law of induction states that the oriented electromotive force around a closed material circuit is minus the rate of change of its linked magnetic flux:
E=∮C(t)​(E+u×B)⋅dℓ=−dtd​∫S(t)​B⋅dS.
(24)
The surface orientation and circuit direction obey the right-hand rule. The minus sign expresses Lenz's law: the induced electric current, when allowed to flow, produces a magnetic field opposing the change of linked magnetic flux. For a stationary circuit this reduces to ∮E⋅dℓ=−dΦB​/dt.
The source's “Lorenz force” is the Lorentz force, q(E+v×B). A wire element of cross-section Ac​ contains nAc​dℓ carriers of charge q with drift velocity vd​. Adding their magnetic forces and using I=nqAc​vd​ gives
dF=Idℓ×B,F=IL×B​
(25)
for a straight wire in a uniform magnetic field. The length vector follows conventional electric current, irrespective of the carrier sign.
For the unlabelled upward-motion request, define t^=(cosϕ,0,−sinϕ) down the slope, b^=(0,1,0) across the rails and n^=(sinϕ,0,cosϕ) out of their plane. Choose the vertical magnetic field as B=Bz^. An electric current I across the bar in direction −b^ gives a horizontal magnetic force −IBsx^. The other forces are weight −mgz^ and the rail normal force Nn^. Uniform upward velocity requires
mgsinϕ−IBscosϕ=0,N−mgcosϕ−IBssinϕ=0.
(26)
Consequently
I=Bsmgtanϕ​,FB​=cosϕmgsinϕ​,N=cosϕmg​.​
(27)
Each symmetric rail supplies half of N. The electric current must make IL×B point horizontally toward the uphill side; for the coordinates just defined it flows along −b^. Reversing the vertical magnetic field reverses this current direction. The external supply must maintain that electric current against the motional electromotive force during ascent. For cosϕ=0, no finite horizontal magnetic force can support uniform motion along the vertical rails.
