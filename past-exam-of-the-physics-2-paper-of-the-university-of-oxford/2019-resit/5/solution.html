Let vC​ denote the capacitor voltage with the same polarity as the applied voltage. By Kirchhoff's voltage law, Ohm's law and I=CdvC​/dt,
RCdtdvC​​+vC​=V,vC​(0)=0.
(14)
Solving this first-order differential equation gives the RC low-pass step response
vC​(t)=V(1−e−t/τ),τ=RC=0.050s.​
(15)
The capacitor voltage is continuous at switching because an instantaneous finite jump would require an impulsive electric current.
For the square-wave response of an RC low-pass filter, take equal positive and negative half-periods. Let A=10V, T=1/f, h=T/2 and α=e−h/τ. After the transient, symmetry makes the capacitor voltage alternate between −vp​ and +vp​. Over the positive half-period,
vC​(t)=A−(A+vp​)e−t/τ,0≤t<h.
(16)
Requiring vC​(h)=vp​ gives
vp​=A1+α1−α​=Atanh(4τT​).​
(17)
The next half-period is the sign-reversed copy. These exact expressions explain the requested sketches:
  • At 1kHz, h/τ=0.01. The voltage barely changes in each half-period, so the output is nearly triangular about zero, with extrema about ±0.0500V. Its small-signal slope is approximately ±A/τ=±200Vs−1.
  • At 10Hz, h=τ. Clearly curved exponential rises and falls reach only ±4.621V; the output never settles to either input level.
  • At 0.1Hz, h=100τ. The output closely follows the input plateaux at ±10V, joined by continuous exponential transitions with time constant 0.050s.
