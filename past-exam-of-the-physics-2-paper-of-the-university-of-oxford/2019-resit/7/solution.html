For refraction at a spherical surface, take incident light travelling rightwards from refractive index n0​ to ng​, object distance u>0 to the left of the vertex, image distance v positive to the right, and signed curvature Rs​ positive when the centre is inside the rod. In the paraxial approximation, applying Snell's law to a light ray at height y gives
n0​(uy​+Rs​y​)=ng​(Rs​y​−vy​).
(20)
After cancelling y, the image position and transverse magnification are
un0​​+vng​​=Rs​ng​−n0​​,v=(ng​−n0​)u−n0​Rs​ng​uRs​​,M=−ng​un0​v​.​
(21)
The magnification follows by considering a paraxial light ray through the vertex: Snell's law there gives n0​(−yo​/u)=ng​(yi​/v).
For a convex entrance, Rs​=+20mm and u=60mm. In air, the denominator (ng​−n0​)/Rs​−n0​/u is 1/75mm−1, giving
vair​=120mm,Mair​=−1.25.​
(22)
This is a real, inverted image within a sufficiently long glass rod, before any subsequent surface.
For water with refractive index 1.33, the denominator is −13/1500mm−1, and
vwater​=−132400​mm≃−184.6mm,Mwater​=52133​≃+2.558.​
(23)
The emerging light rays diverge as though from an upright virtual image on the object side. The change of sign is consistent with v diverging when n0​=ng​u/(u+Rs​)=1.20. A reversed curvature or a second exit surface changes these answers; the unavailable diagram prevents a definitive answer for the full illustrated arrangement.
