Use phasors with time dependence eiωt and electrical impedance Z=V/I. The element laws V=IR, I=CdV/dt and V=LdI/dt give
ZR​=R,ZC​=iωC1​=−ωCi​,ZL​=iωL.​

A complex number records both amplitude and phase, turning the linear differential equations of a sinusoidally driven electrical circuit into algebraic equations. A resistor has in-phase voltage and electric current and dissipates average power. For an ideal capacitor, the electric current leads the voltage by 90∘; for an ideal inductor, it lags by 90∘. Their imaginary electrical impedances express reversible energy storage and return, with zero net average power over a period. Using e−iωt would conjugate the displayed reactive signs without changing predictions.
In the illustrated Maxwell bridge, the left and right vertices are the supply terminals and the upper and lower vertices are detector terminals. Insert a sensitive AC null detector between the latter and drive the former with a sinusoidal source. The upper-left arm is Zx​=Rx​+iωLx​, the upper-right arm is R3​, the lower-left arm is R2​, and the lower-right arm is the parallel combination
Zp​=(R1​1​+iωC)−1.
(52)
At a null no detector electric current flows, so the two voltage dividers give
Zx​+R3​R3​​=R2​+Zp​Zp​​,Zx​Zp​=R2​R3​.
(53)
Multiplying by the parallel-arm electrical admittance yields
Rx​+iωLx​=R2​R3​(R1​1​+iωC).
(54)
Equating real and imaginary parts therefore gives
Rx​=R1​R2​R3​​,Lx​=CR2​R3​.​
(55)
Two independent adjustments are needed to annul the in-phase and quadrature detector signals. For example, change R2​ to set Lx​ and R1​ to set Rx​, iterating until the detector reads zero. Alternatively, with fixed R2​,R3​, independently vary R1​ and C.
The Maxwell bridge compares an unknown lossy inductor with a stable standard capacitor, avoiding the need for an accurately known reference inductor. The ideal balance equations contain no frequency, and a null measurement need not rely on absolute detector calibration. In practice choose a suitable frequency below the coil's self-resonance, where the series Rx​,Lx​ model and the standard capacitor remain accurate.
If the initial setting is far from balance, the bridge as drawn needs adjustable components and a detector, not merely a more precise readout. Fit resistance boxes or switched ranges for R1​ and a ratio arm, and a selectable standard C. Choose ranges around
R2​R3​=CLx​​,R1​=CRx​Lx​​.
(56)
Then approach the null with coarse adjustments followed by finer ones. A large required R1​ can be brought into range by increasing C and decreasing R2​R3​ proportionally. Start with reduced excitation or reduced detector sensitivity to avoid overload, then increase sensitivity near the null. Changing frequency alone cannot repair an impossible component range, since the ideal balance conditions are frequency-independent. Stray capacitance and lead electrical resistance set practical accuracy limits and may require shielding and short connections.
