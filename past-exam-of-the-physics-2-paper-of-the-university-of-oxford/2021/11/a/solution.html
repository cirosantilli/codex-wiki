Faraday's law of induction states that the induced electromotive force around an oriented closed circuit equals the negative rate of change of linked magnetic flux. For a stationary circuit,
E=∮E⋅dℓ=−dtdΦB​​,ΦB​=∫A​B⋅dA.​
(50)
Here E is electric field, B is magnetic flux density, and the oriented surface A has the circuit as its boundary. For N identical series turns, replace ΦB​ by the total flux linkage NΦB​. Lenz's law supplies the sign: an induced electric current opposes the change of linked magnetic flux, in agreement with conservation of energy.
If the wire moves with local velocity u, the more general electromotive force includes the magnetic Lorentz force per unit electric charge:
E=∮(E+u×B)⋅dℓ=−dtd​∫A(t)​B⋅dA.
(51)
The boundary and surface orientations in both expressions are related by the right-hand rule.
