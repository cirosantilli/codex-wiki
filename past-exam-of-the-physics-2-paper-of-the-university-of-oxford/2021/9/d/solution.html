The two optical frequencies have no persistent mutual optical coherence over the detector's averaging time, so their light intensities add. Assuming equal strengths and using s=1.0mm, let Is​ be the one-slit light intensity at either wavelength. Then
I(θ)=4Is​[cos2(λ1​πssinθ​)+cos2(λ2​πssinθ​)],λ1​=520nm,λ2​=540nm.​
(40)
The identity cosA+cosB=2cos[(A+B)/2]cos[(A−B)/2] rewrites this as
I(θ)=4Is​[1+cos(πssinθ(λ1​1​+λ2​1​))cos(πssinθ(λ1​1​−λ2​1​))].
(41)
The slowly varying interferometric visibility is approximately V=∣cos(πssinθ[1/λ1​−1/λ2​])∣. Its first zero occurs at
ssinθzero​=2(λ2​−λ1​)λ1​λ2​​=7.02μm,θzero​=0.402∘.
(42)
The rapid fringes have effective wavelength λh​=2λ1​λ2​/(λ1​+λ2​)=529.81nm, so the first contrast minimum is at order 7.02μm/λh​=13.25. Thus the conventional estimate is about 13 fringes on each side, before their first washout, plus the central maximum. “Clearly” is not a specified visibility threshold: the last of those fringes already has low contrast.
The fringes do not disappear permanently beyond that point. Their contrast revives; the first exact common maximum satisfies 27λ1​=26λ2​=14.04μm, at ∣θ∣=0.804∘. This is a concrete counterexample to interpreting 13 as a global bound on visible fringes. Unequal spectral strengths would leave nonzero residual contrast at the nominal washout; the plotted equal-strength case is the natural interpretation in the absence of specified strengths.
