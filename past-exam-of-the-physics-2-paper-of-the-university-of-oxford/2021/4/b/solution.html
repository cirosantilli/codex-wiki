The condenser lens should image the point source onto the small projection lens. Then every illuminated LCD point directs its central light ray into the projection lens, rather than wasting light elsewhere. In the paraxial idealization this gives approximately uniform useful illumination.
Put the LCD at z=0, the source at z=−100mm, the projection lens at z=u=29.5567mm, and the condenser lens at z=−h. The cone from the condenser lens's rim, of radius 15mm, to the projection lens has radius
rLCD​=15u+hu​mm
(18)
at the LCD. Maximum approximately uniform illumination without unnecessary overfilling makes this equal the half-diagonal 7.5mm. Thus h=u: place the condenser lens 29.56mm before the LCD, or 70.44mm after the source. Its object and image distances are 100−h and u+h, so
fc​=100+u(100−h)(u+h)​=32.14mm.​
(19)
The illuminated disk just contains the rectangular LCD. Exact spatially uniform irradiance is not guaranteed by a single spherical lens and a point source; the result uses paraxial geometrical optics, negligible optical aberration, and an approximately uniform incident cone.
