The magnetic Lorentz force is
F=qv×B.​
(1)
Since the given velocity is perpendicular to the magnetic flux density, its magnitude is ∣q∣vB. Its direction follows the vector product for positive charge and reverses for negative charge.

Place charges +Q and −Q on the inner and outer conductors. In the vacuum gap, Gauss's law gives E(r)=Q/(4πϵ0​r2). Their electric potential difference is
Va​−Vb​=∫ab​E(r)dr=4πϵ0​Q​(a1​−b1​).

In the linear inverting amplifier, the non-inverting terminal is grounded and negative feedback connects the output to the inverting terminal. For large open-loop gain A, Vo​=A(V+​−V−​); a finite unsaturated output therefore requires V−​≃V+​=0. Thus
V−​≃0under stable negative feedback.​
(8)
This is a virtual ground: it has approximately ground potential, but it is not a conducting connection to ground. The ideal operational amplifier draws no input current, so input current flows through the feedback resistor. Saturation, loss of feedback, or insufficient bandwidth invalidates the approximation.

Assume a converging thin lens with f>0, a real object, and a real image on the detector. For positive object and image distances u,v, the thin lens equation gives uv=f(u+v). Put S=u+v. Then
(u−v)2=S2−4uv=S(S−4f)≥0.
(10)
Since S>0, S≥4f, with equality at u=v=2f. Therefore
Smin​=4f,u=v=2f.​
(11)
Below this separation no real conjugate pair exists for one ideal thin lens; a diverging lens alone cannot form a real image of a real object on the detector.

Faraday's law states
E=∮E⋅dℓ=−dtdΦB​​​
(12)
for a stationary loop, with ΦB​=∫B⋅dS and consistent contour and area orientations. The minus sign expresses Lenz's law: the induced response opposes the change of magnetic flux. An emf can exist across an open loop even when no conduction current flows.

Choose the loop normal along +z. Rotation about x gives Bz​=Bcosωt, so the linked magnetic flux is ΦB​=Ba2cosωt. Therefore
E(t)=Ba2ωsinωt.​
(13)
The sign uses the contour orientation associated with +z. For the small open gap, this is the corresponding open-circuit terminal emf, with the stated terminal ordering. The tangential induced electric field is nonconservative, so its circulation is not the integral of a single-valued electrostatic potential gradient.

If R closes the break, a conduction current flows and electrical energy is dissipated. When loop self-inductance is negligible,
I(t)=RBa2ω​sinωt.​

Use positive current in the charging direction and passive voltage drops across R,L,C. The five ideal elements obey
vbattery​=V0​,vR​=RI,vL​=LdtdI​,I=CdtdvC​​.​
(15)
The battery maintains its emf independently of current. An ideal open switch has I=0 with its voltage unconstrained; an ideal closed switch has vswitch​=0 with its current unconstrained. These statements are the switch's piecewise current-voltage relation. Inductor current continuity and capacitor-voltage continuity hold when no impulse voltage or current is imposed.

α=2LR​,ωn​=LC​1​,ωd​=ωn2​−α2​.

I(t)=Lωd​V0​​e−αtsinωd​t.​

Put q1​ at x=−L, q2​ at x=+L, and q3​ at x=fL, with −1<f<1. For a nonzero test charge, the force vanishes when the electric field vanishes:
(1+f)2q1​​=(1−f)2q2​​.

With q2​=−q1​, both field contributions between the charges point the same way. Their signed force on q3​ is
Fx​(f)=L2ke​q1​q3​​[(1+f)21​+(1−f)21​]=L22ke​q1​q3​​(1−f2)21+f2​,ke​=4πϵ0​1​.

In the Fraunhofer approximation, contributions from positions ξ across the slit have relative phase kξsinθ. With uniform illumination, the field relative to its axial value is
E(0)E(θ)​=w1​∫−w/2w/2​eikξsinθdξ=βsinβ​,β=λπwsinθ​.
(24)
Thus the single-slit diffraction intensity is
I(θ)=I(0)(βsinβ​)2.​
(25)
On the detector sinθ=y/d2+y2​; near the axis, β≃πwy/(λd). The central maximum takes the continuous limit sinβ/β→1, and the minima satisfy wsinθ=mλ for nonzero integers m. The stated far-field condition justifies adding the aperture contributions with a linear phase.

Solution

ii OurBigBook.com
The first single-slit minimum is at sinθ=λ/w, so the characteristic angular resolution of an aperture of width D is of order λ/D. With D=1 m, λ=550 nm, and distance D=108 m, this slit-based estimate is
Δθ∼5.5×10−7rad,Δx∼55m.​
(26)
For the actual circular lens, the Rayleigh criterion uses the first zero of the Airy disk, Δθ≃1.22λ/D. It gives 6.71×10−7 rad and a resolved separation of about 67 m. The factor 1.22 belongs to the circular aperture, not to the one-dimensional slit derivation.

For normal incidence, adjacent grating lines have optical path difference ssinθ. Constructive interference at order m requires ssinθm​=mλ. Differentiating at fixed m yields the angular dispersion
dλdθm​​=scosθm​m​.​

Let At​=πd2/4 be the tube cross-section. A long uniformly wound solenoid with n1​ total turns has field BA​=μ0​n1​I/s, approximately uniform inside and negligible outside. Therefore the flux through one cross-section, or one turn of either coil, is
Φ=sμ0​n1​IAt​​=4sμ0​n1​Iπd2​.​
(28)
The linked flux of coil A is n1​Φ; that of coil B is n2​Φ. Distinguishing flux from flux linkage prevents an extra factor of the turn count.

For chosen positive terminal currents, define self-inductance by the coil's own flux linkage Λj​=Lj​Ij​ and mutual inductance by Λ2​=MI1​ when only coil A carries current. With matching winding directions and terminal references,
LA​=sμ0​n12​At​​,LB​=sμ0​n22​At​​,M=sμ0​n1​n2​At​​.​

The reciprocal flux-linkage relation gives the same M from B to A, and M2=LA​LB​ in this ideal fully coupled long-solenoid limit. The induced emf is −dΛ/dt; passive terminal voltage drops use the opposite sign, vA​=LA​I˙A​+MI˙B​ and similarly for B.

Reversing one winding reverses its signed coupling to the other, while its self-inductance is unchanged. With the same north-to-south current references,
M⟶−M,LA​,LB​ unchanged.​

Equal currents then produce opposing magnetic fields: the signed axial field is proportional to (n1​−n2​)I, rather than (n1​+n2​)I. Which direction is positive must be fixed by the right-hand rule and the actual winding orientation.

Let BE​>0 be the northward component of the ambient magnetic field. Define η=+1 if positive north-to-south current in A produces a northward field, and η=−1 otherwise. With opposite windings and the same signed current in both,
BN​=BE​+ηsμ0​(n1​−n2​)​I.
(31)
A compass reverses only when this northward component becomes negative. For current chosen to make the coil field southward,
∣I∣>Icrit​=μ0​∣n1​−n2​∣BE​s​.​
(32)
At equality the ideal axial field is zero and the needle has no preferred axial direction. If n1​=n2​, the opposing coil fields cancel and no finite current reverses it in this model. A numerical threshold cannot be given without BE​ and the coil parameters.

Use north-minus-south terminal voltages for both oppositely wound coils, with coil A measured at high input impedance so its AC current is zero. Then vB​=LB​I˙B​ and vA​=MI˙B​, with M<0. Hence
vA​(t)=LB​M​vB​(t)=−n2​n1​​V0​sinωt.​

Use sinusoidal phasors, v(t)=Re(Veiωt). Differentiation becomes multiplication by iω, so the time-dependent element laws reduce to algebraic complex impedances:
ZR​=R,ZL​=iωL,ZC​=iωC1​.​
(34)
Then V=ZI encodes both amplitude ratio ∣Z∣ and voltage-current phase difference argZ. Series impedances add, while parallel admittances add. This applies to linear sinusoidal steady state; switching transients are separate homogeneous solutions.

The source figure has a series capacitor followed by R in parallel with L. Its complex impedance is
Z=−ωCi​+R+iωLiωLR​=R2+ω2L2Rω2L2​+i[R2+ω2L2ωLR2​−ωC1​].
(35)
The imaginary part vanishes at a finite positive frequency only if R2C>L, in which case
