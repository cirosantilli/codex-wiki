For a fixed number N of identical bulbs, first approximate each as a resistor of electrical resistance R connected to a fixed ideal battery electric potential difference V. A series circuit has electrical resistance NR, while a parallel circuit has electrical resistance R/N. Thus
Pseries​=NRV2​,Pparallel​=RNV2​,Pseries​Pparallel​​=N2​.
(5)
Parallel connection therefore maximizes dissipation in this model: every bulb receives the full source electric potential difference instead of sharing it. For N=1 the two arrangements coincide.
An actual incandescent light bulb changes electrical resistance as its filament heats, so a single constant R need not describe both operating points. The same qualitative conclusion holds for identical stable bulbs with increasing current-voltage function i(U): parallel operation gives Pp​=NVi(V), whereas symmetric series operation gives Ps​=Vi(V/N). The former is larger for N>1. This comparison assumes a fixed collection and an ideal source as specified; an unlimited number of parallel bulbs on a zero-internal-resistance ideal battery would have no finite total-power maximum.
