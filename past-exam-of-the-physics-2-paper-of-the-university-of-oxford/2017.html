Use a cross-section normal to the sheets' line of intersection: the positive sheet is x=0, and the negative sheet is y=0. A Gaussian pillbox gives a single infinite sheet's electric field magnitude σ/(2ϵ0​) on either side. Superposition consequently gives, away from the sheets,

Place eight identical cubes around the charged corner to make one larger cube, with the point charge at its centre. Each large face has electric flux q/(6ϵ0​) and consists of four equivalent small faces. Each of the original cube's three faces opposite the charged corner therefore has
Φopposite​=24ϵ0​q​.​
(7)

Φincident​=0,Φoriginal cube​=8ϵ0​q​.​

The requested source-image relation is the thin lens equation, using positive real-object distance u and signed image distance v:
f1​=u1​+v1​.​
(12)
Here f>0 for a converging lens, and v<0 denotes a virtual image. This is usually distinguished from Lensmaker's equation, 1/f=(n−1)(1/R1​−1/R2​) for a thin lens in air with the usual signed surface radii. The latter relates focal length to the lens material and curvature, rather than to object and image positions.[ref]
For a magnifying glass held close to the eye, compare the angle h/u with the unaided near-point angle h/Dn​, where Dn​=25cm. Maximum usable angular magnification occurs with the virtual image at the near point, so v=−Dn​. Thus

The original circuit has R1​ and C in series between the input and the inverting node, with R2​ providing feedback and the noninverting node grounded. An ideal operational amplifier in stable linear negative feedback draws no input electric current and holds the inverting node at virtual ground. For phasors proportional to eiωt, the input complex impedance is Z1​=R1​+1/(iωC). Kirchhoff's current law gives Vin​/Z1​=−Vout​/R2​, hence
H(ω)=Vin​Vout​​=−1+iωR1​CiωR2​C​,∣H∣=1+ω2R12​C2​ωR2​C​.​
(14)
This is an inverting high-pass filter. Its phase can be chosen continuously as
argH=−2π​−tan−1(ωR1​C).​
(15)
Thus Vout​(t)=V0​∣H∣cos(ωt+argH). The ratio above is a phasor ratio, not an instantaneous ratio at input zero crossings. Setting R1​=R2​ does not remove the capacitor's phase: the output lags by 90∘+tan−1(ωR1​C), between 90∘ and 180∘. At ωR1​C=1 the lag is 135∘; only for ωR1​C≫1 does it approach 180∘. Being within the amplifier's own bandwidth does not specify the external RC corner frequency.

a OurBigBook.com
In Fraunhofer diffraction, the source and observation geometry make the incident and outgoing wavefronts effectively planar across the aperture. For direct propagation over distance z, the quadratic variation in propagation phase across a slit of width w must be negligible, approximately w2/(λz)≪1. The far-field angular amplitude is then the Fourier transform of the aperture's complex illumination. A converging lens also produces this angular distribution in its rear focal plane for collimated illumination, without requiring a physically enormous observation distance. The approximation concerns the aperture-dependent phase, not the absence of diffraction.

The Rayleigh criterion declares two equal incoherent point images just resolved when the central maximum of one lies at the first minimum of the other's point spread function. For a single rectangular slit, the first minimum occurs at wsinθ1​=λ. Thus
ΔθR​=sin−1(λ/w)≃λ/w.​
(36)
This is a resolution in the direction across the slit width, and the last approximation requires λ≪w. The central maximum's full width between its two first minima is approximately 2λ/w; it is not the Rayleigh criterion's separation. Two coherent sources can interfere with one another, so a resolution statement for them also needs their relative phase.
