Translational and reflection symmetry parallel to the slabs imply E=Ex​(x)x^. The differential Gauss's law is dEx​/dx=ρ/ϵ0​. Starting at the left boundary with Ex​(−a)=0 gives
Ex​(x)=−ϵ0​aC​(x+a),−a≤x≤0.
(5)
There is no singular surface charge at x=0, so the normal electric field is continuous there: Ex​(0)=−C/ϵ0​. Integrating through the positive slab gives
E(x)=⎩⎨⎧​−ϵ0​aC(x+a)​x^,−ϵ0​bC(b−x)​x^,0,​−a≤x≤0,0≤x≤b,x<−a or x>b.​​
(6)
The second expression indeed returns to zero at x=b, because the two total charges per unit area are −C and +C. For C>0, the electric field throughout the interior points from the positive slab towards the negative slab, along −x; reversing C reverses the directions. The piecewise linear profile is a useful consequence of the uniform volume charge density.
