The largest field in each gap lies at that gap's smaller radius. Thus the quantity to minimize is
Emax​(V2​)=max{a(c−a)c∣V1​−V2​∣​,c(b−c)b∣V2​∣​}.
(33)
For positive V1​, projecting any V2​ outside [0,V1​] back to that interval reduces both absolute potential drops, so the optimum lies inside it. Within the interval, one candidate maximum decreases linearly with V2​ and the other increases linearly. Their maximum is minimized at their intersection, not by making the potential drops equal.
With a=0.1 m, c=0.2 m, b=0.4 m and V1​=1000 V, the two maxima are 20(1000−V2​) and 10V2​ in Vm−1. Equating them gives
V2​=32000​ V≃666.7 V,Emax​=320000​ Vm−1≃6667 Vm−1​.
(34)
The maximum is halved relative to the two-sphere system and is attained at both a+ and c+. At c− and b− the optimized field is only about 1667 Vm−1; at c the charged middle shell produces a jump.
For plotting, the two-sphere electric potential is V(2)(r)=V1​(1/r−1/b)/(1/a−1/b). In the three-sphere system it is
V(3)(r)=⎩⎨⎧​V2​+(V1​−V2​)1/a−1/c1/r−1/c​,V2​1/c−1/b1/r−1/b​,​a≤r≤c,c≤r≤b.​
(35)
The electric potential remains continuous at c, while its radial derivative changes there. The graphs show both the continuous potentials and the one-sided gap fields; the marked endpoints identify all maxima.
