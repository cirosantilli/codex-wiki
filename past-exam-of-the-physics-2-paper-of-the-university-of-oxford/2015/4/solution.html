Choose the entry point as (0,0), the initial velocity as vx^, and the slit as (d,a), with a,d>0. A magnetic field perpendicular to the page changes the direction of the velocity, while the Lorentz force does no work. For positive q, an upward initial force requires the magnetic flux density to point into the page. The resulting circular motion has centre (0,r), since its initial tangent is horizontal.
The slit must lie on the circle, giving d2+(a−r)2=r2 and hence r=(d2+a2)/(2a). Equating the centripetal force to the magnetic part of the Lorentz force, mv2/r=∣q∣vB, gives

A negative electric charge requires the opposite direction of magnetic flux density. These expressions assume nonrelativistic motion and negligible energy loss through electromagnetic radiation.
For an opaque screen, reaching the slit must be the first encounter with x=d. The velocity there has vx​/v=(d2−a2)/(d2+a2), so passage from left to right requires a<d, as in the drawn arrangement. At a=d the trajectory merely touches the screen. If a>d, the same circle first meets the screen at height d2/a<a, where the particle is blocked; its later passage through (d,a) does not solve the opaque-screen problem. This qualification distinguishes a geometric circle through two points from an accessible slit trajectory.
