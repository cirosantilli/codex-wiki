Write the wire diameter as dw​ to distinguish it from other geometric labels. Its cross-sectional area is Aw​=πdw2​/4, and the total wire length is 4a. From the electrical resistivity, its electrical resistance is
R=Aw​4aρc​​=πdw2​16ρc​a​.
(31)
While the loop straddles the boundary, let h be the height of each vertical side remaining in the magnetic field. The linked magnetic flux has magnitude Bah, with h˙=−v. Faraday's law therefore gives ∣E∣=Bav. Neglecting the loop's self-inductance, Ohm's law gives
I=RBav​=16ρc​πdw2​Bv​.​
(32)
The into-page magnetic flux is decreasing, so Lenz's law requires a clockwise electric current as viewed in the diagram. If the loop is wholly inside a uniform magnetic field, or wholly outside it, its magnetic flux is constant and this induced electromotive force is zero. During a rapid transient one should instead solve LI˙+RI=Bav; at the assumed steady speed the same boxed relation holds.
