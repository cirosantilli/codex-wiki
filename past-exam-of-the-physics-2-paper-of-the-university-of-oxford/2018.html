Ez​(0+)=−4πϵ0​2​∑j​hj2​Qj​​=4πϵ0​60​(500021​−800021​).

The continuity equation for electric charge is ∇⋅J=−∂t​ρ. Taking the divergence of the magnetostatic Ampère's law would instead require ∇⋅J=0. The displacement current repairs this conflict: using Gauss's law, ∇⋅(ϵ0​∂t​E)=∂t​ρ, so the Ampère-Maxwell equation is
∇×B=μ0​(J+ϵ0​∂t​E).
(6)

A grating spectrometer places an illuminated narrow slit at the front focal plane of a collimator. The resulting parallel beam reaches the diffraction grating at known incidence, here taken normal. A rotatable telescope focuses each outgoing diffracted parallel beam onto its crosshair or detector. Align the slit with the grating rulings, focus the collimator at infinity, and illuminate many grooves across a suitable aperture. A narrow slit supplies sufficient spatial coherence and prevents slit width from blurring the angular separation; the telescope must also resolve the two images.
The groove spacing is d=(2000×100)−1 m=5.00 μm. The grating equation at normal incidence is dsinθm​=mλ. For diffraction order m=2,
θ579​=arcsin(0.2316)≃13.3913∘,θ577​=arcsin(0.2308)≃13.3442∘.
(10)
Therefore
Δθ≃8.22×10−4 rad=0.0471∘=2.83 arcmin.​
(11)
The ideal resolving power of a diffraction grating is ∣m∣N, so the Rayleigh criterion for this doublet requires about N≥145 illuminated grooves, spanning roughly 0.73 mm. This is an ideal lower bound; finite slit width, aberrations and detector resolution can require a larger usable aperture.

Use an ideal inverting summing amplifier, grounding the non-inverting input and connecting the two sources through resistances R and R/2. A feedback resistance Rf​=2R gives the desired weights. Operational-amplifier negative feedback makes the inverting node a virtual ground, and negligible input current lets Kirchhoff's current law give
RVa​​+R/2Vb​​=−2RVout​​,Vout​=−2(Va​+2Vb​).​
(12)
Replacing the feedback resistor by a capacitor C makes a weighted op amp integrator:
dtdVout​​=−RCVa​+2Vb​​,Vout​(t)=Vout​(t0​)−RC1​∫t0​t​[Va​(u)+2Vb​(u)]du.​
(13)
The initial capacitor charge determines the integration constant. A nonzero DC weighted input produces a ramp; a real operational amplifier eventually reaches its output-voltage limit. A parallel feedback resistance can limit drift while preserving integration over an appropriate frequency range.

i OurBigBook.com
Initially the isolated sphere has Q=4πϵ0​RV. After insertion without contact, its charge remains Q. In the long-cylinder approximation, with the sphere far from the openings, neglect electric flux escaping through the ends. A Gauss surface running through the shielding metal and closed by end sections of negligible flux then encloses zero net charge. Its cavity wall therefore carries −Q. Since that cylinder is isolated and initially neutral, its outside wall carries +Q. The grounded outer cylinder supplies charge −Q on its inside wall, terminating the annular electric field lines.
For positive V, the cavity electric field lines run from the sphere to the inner wall, while annular electric field lines run from the outside of the inner conductor to the grounded outer conductor. Negative V reverses their directions. The transverse picture is schematic: exact cavity fields depend on the sphere's position and the ends. The following annular formulas neglect end effects and place the sphere well inside the long cylindrical assembly.

The Biot-Savart law is
B(r)=4πμ0​I​∫∣r−r′∣3dℓ′×(r−r′)​.
(19)
Here r′ is the current-element position, dℓ′ follows the electric current, and r is the observation point. For a circular current loop, transverse components cancel on the axis. Each element contributes axial factor a/a2+x2​ in addition to the inverse-square distance. Integrating around length 2πa gives the on-axis magnetic field of a circular current loop
Baxis​(x)=2(a2+x2)3/2μ0​Ia2​,​
(20)
with axial direction set by the right-hand rule.
For a finite straight wire parametrized by z∈[−b,b], at perpendicular distance a, the Biot-Savart law gives
B=4πμ0​I​∫−bb​(a2+z2)3/2adz​=2πaa2+b2​μ0​Ib​.​
(21)
The direction is azimuthal about the current axis by the right-hand rule. In the hairpin, the semicircle contributes μ0​I/(4a) and each semi-infinite straight segment contributes μ0​I/(4πa). For the indicated current, all three contributions point out of the page. Thus the magnetic field at the center of a hairpin wire is
B(P)=4πaμ0​I​(π+2),out of the page.​

The Huygens-Fresnel principle constructs a later wavefront as the envelope of secondary wavelets emitted from the earlier one. At a planar interface, take a time interval during which one point on the incoming front travels distance v1​Δt before reaching the interface, while the point that reached it first generates a wavelet of radius v2​Δt. The two right triangles share the interface segment, giving sini/sinr=v1​/v2​. Since the refractive index is n=c/v, this yields Snell's law:
n1​sini=n2​sinr.​
(34)
The angles are measured from the interface normal. This geometric construction assumes homogeneous media and uses the propagation speeds at the given frequency.

Let r=arcsin(sinθ/n) be the angle inside the prism relative to the inward normal of face A. For the entry position shown, below the midpoint of A, and 0<θ<90∘, the transmitted ray first reaches face B. Its incidence angle there is iB​=∣α−r∣. If nsiniB​<1, a transmitted ray leaves B according to Snell's law. If nsiniB​>1, it undergoes total internal reflection at B and then reaches C. At the equality, transmission is grazing.
On the reflected route through this equilateral prism, the incidence angle at C is r, so nsinr=sinθ<1 permits emergence from C. The one-reflection path through an equilateral prism is drawn alongside the direct-transmission route.
Real interfaces also produce partial Fresnel reflection below the critical angle; these additional weaker rays are omitted from the principal-path sketch. Which face a ray first reaches also depends on its entrance position, so the route statement uses the entrance geometry illustrated in the source.

Take the positive horizontal direction along B. The initial direction is (α−90∘)−θ. Reflection at the horizontal face B reverses the vertical direction. At C, symmetry of the equilateral geometry gives internal incidence r, so Snell's law makes the emerging angle to C's normal equal to θ. Its direction is therefore (90∘−α)+θ. Subtracting the incoming direction gives
δ=180∘−2α+2θ=60∘+2θ.​
(37)
This is the smaller angle between the incident and emergent propagation directions in the requested reflection interval. The cancellation of n occurs because the entry and exit refraction angles have equal magnitudes; refractive index still determines whether the reflected route exists and where it exits.

iv OurBigBook.com
Assume normal dispersion, so shorter visible wavelengths have larger refractive indices. At fixed external angle θ, the relevant criticality parameter is
nsiniB​=sinαn2−sin2θ​−cosαsinθ,
(38)
which increases with n on this route. Thus shorter-wavelength blue light is totally reflected at B and reaches C, while longer-wavelength red light can transmit through B; green lies at the critical threshold. The prism separates the routes by wavelength near the green cutoff.
The totally reflected rays emerging from C have the same external deviation 180∘−2α+2θ despite their different refractive indices; their exit positions can differ. Red light also has a partially reflected component by the Fresnel equations, so C is not a mathematically perfect colour cutoff. The observation concerns total versus partial reflection, with the transmitted B rays spread by wavelength. Anomalous dispersion, if present, would require the actual n(λ) instead of the normal ordering assumed here.
