Set the red image at coordinate zero, the blue image at δ=0.010m, and the screen at D=1.000m. Let the correcting thin lens be at x, with δ<x<D. Both original images are then real objects for the correcting thin lens, with positive object distances ur​=x, ub​=x−δ, and common positive image distance v=D−x. The thin lens equation gives
fr​=Dx(D−x)​,fb​=D−δ(x−δ)(D−x)​.​
(17)
The refractive indices and Lensmaker's equation require
fr​fb​​=nb​−1nr​−1​=1110​=ρ.
(18)
Substitute the two focal lengths and cancel D−x:
D(x−δ)=ρx(D−δ),x=D−ρ(D−δ)Dδ​=0.1000m.
(19)
Thus the correcting thin lens is 100mm beyond the red image and 900mm before the screen. The required focal lengths and surface radius are
fr​=90.0mm,fb​=81.818…mm,R=2(nr​−1)fr​=72.0mm.​
(20)
The blue focal length is shorter because the blue refractive index is larger; these two differing object distances are mapped to the same screen, removing the specified longitudinal chromatic aberration.
