The passive RC low-pass filter is simple, inexpensive, requires no power supply, and has no operational amplifier saturation or finite bandwidth limitation. It offers no voltage gain; its output impedance is finite, and both source electrical resistance and loading can change the observed frequency response. Its resistor also produces thermal noise.
The active inverting low-pass amplifier provides a low-frequency voltage gain of magnitude five and, within the usable bandwidth, a small output impedance, so it can drive a load with much less change in frequency response. The input electrical resistance is approximately R1​=1kΩ and may itself load the source. It needs a power supply and adds electrical noise and offset; finite operational amplifier gain, bandwidth, slew rate, available output swing, and output electric current limit the ideal formula. Its inversion must also suit the application. The best choice depends on required voltage gain, source and load electrical impedances, and the signal's frequency and amplitude.
