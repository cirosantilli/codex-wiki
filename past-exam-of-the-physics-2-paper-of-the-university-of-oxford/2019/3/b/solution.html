Electrical contact equalizes electric potential and preserves total electric charge. The usual isolated-sphere approximation assigns the two conducting spheres the capacitances 4πϵ0​R and 8πϵ0​R. It then gives
RQR​​=2RQ2R​​,QR​+Q2R​=Q,QR​≃Q/3,Q2R​≃2Q/3.​
(7)
These are accurate when the conducting spheres are far apart and joined by a thin wire. Literal touching changes the answer: each conducting sphere polarizes the other, so its electric potential is not its isolated value. The source does not specify an approximation which would remove this effect.
For completeness, the exact result for ideal externally tangent conducting spheres can be obtained by the method of images. Let their radii be a,b, their centre separation D=a+b, and their common electric potential V. Begin with central image charges 4πϵ0​Va and 4πϵ0​Vb. Reflect each external image charge q′ at centre-distance s into the other conducting sphere of radius a: the new image charge is −aq′/s at centre-distance a2/s toward the original. Repeat, alternately, in the two conducting spheres. Pairing successive opposite-sign terms gives the convergent net electric charges
Qa​Qb​​=4πϵ0​Vabn=0∑∞​(b+nD1​−(n+1)D1​),=4πϵ0​Vabn=0∑∞​(a+nD1​−(n+1)D1​).​
(8)
Each reflection cancels a nonconstant surface electric potential; the remaining central term fixes V. The total internal image charge equals the exterior electric flux divided by 1/ϵ0​, so these sums give the physical net electric charges.
For a=R,b=2R, multiply both dimensionless sums by the common factor 4πϵ0​V(2R/3). Their values follow by writing reciprocals as integrals and setting t=u3:
Sa​Sb​​=n=0∑∞​(n+2/31​−n+11​)=3∫01​1+u+u2udu​=23​ln3−23​π​,=n=0∑∞​(n+1/31​−n+11​)=3∫01​1+u+u2(1+u)du​=23​ln3+23​π​.​
(9)
Dividing by Sa​+Sb​ enforces conservation of electric charge and yields
QR​=Q(21​−63​ln3π​)≃0.22483Q,Q2R​=Q(21​+63​ln3π​)≃0.77517Q.​
(10)
This concrete touching-sphere result disproves applying the isolated 1:2 sharing rule to literal contact.
