The force is downwards on the equator and upwards on the negative axial side. A free positive electric charge released from rest at the left-hand point initially accelerates downwards. As it moves into x<0,y<0, the force also acquires a positive x component. Its path bends toward the right; the force continually changes as its position changes. An initial velocity was not specified, so the following more precise trajectory assumes release from rest and neglects radiation reaction.
There is a useful exact result within the point-electric dipole approximation. Put A=ke​pQ>0; the potential energy is U=Ay/r3, a homogeneous function of degree −2. Thus r⋅F=2U. For a particle of mass m, Newton's second law and conservation of energy imply

The electric charge gains speed until the bottom point, where the force is inward and the speed is 2A/(md2)​. It then slows, reaches (d,0) at rest, and reverses. Thus, under these assumptions,
the charge oscillates along the lower semicircle x2+y2=d2,y≤0.​

Its trajectory is not an electric field line: the force determines acceleration, rather than velocity direction. For the finite two-charge electric dipole, corrections of order a2/d2 perturb the circular trajectory; an arbitrary nonzero initial velocity also changes it.
