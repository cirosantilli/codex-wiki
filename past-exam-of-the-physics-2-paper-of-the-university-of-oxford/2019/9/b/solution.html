The electric dipole moment is p=qay^​. Write θ for the angle measured from +y, with θ^ pointing toward increasing θ. The exact electric potential is
ϕ(r)=ke​q(∣r−(a/2)y^​∣1​−∣r+(a/2)y^​∣1​).
(29)
For r≫a, expand the reciprocals to first order in a/r. Their common 1/r terms cancel, giving the electric dipole approximation
ϕ(r,θ)=r2ke​pcosθ​,E=r3ke​p​(2cosθr^+sinθθ^).​
(30)
The radial electric field follows from Er​=−∂ϕ/∂r, and the tangential electric field from Eθ​=−(1/r)∂ϕ/∂θ. The omitted terms are of relative order a2/r2 away from zeros of a component. Multiplying by the test electric charge gives the force components
Fr​=r32ke​pQcosθ​,Fθ​=r3ke​pQsinθ​.​
(31)
The electric field lines emerge from the positive electric charge and terminate on the negative electric charge. They are mirror-symmetric about the electric dipole axis and bow out across its equatorial plane. In the point-electric dipole limit their meridional curves obey dr/(rdθ)=Er​/Eθ​=2cotθ, hence r=Csin2θ.
