Circle of intersection of two spheres (∣p−q∣<∣b−a∣<p+q)

 Home Mathematics Area of mathematics Geometry and topology Euclidean geometry Sphere
OurBigBook.com Words: 75
Two spheres with centres a,b and positive radii p,q intersect in a nondegenerate circle exactly under the displayed strict triangle inequalities. Put d=∣b−a∣. Subtracting ∣r−a∣2=p2 and ∣r−b∣2=q2 gives its plane,
2(b−a)⋅r=p2−q2+∣b∣2−∣a∣2.
(13)
The circle centre is a+ℓ(b−a)/d, where ℓ=(p2−q2+d2)/(2d), and its squared radius is p2−ℓ2. This is positive exactly under the strict inequalities. Equality gives internal or external tangency; coincident equal spheres have an entire sphere in common rather than a unique intersection circle.
