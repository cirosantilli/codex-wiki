Critical damping (ζ=1,bcrit​=2mk​)

 ... Mathematics Area of mathematics Analysis Partial differential equation Wave equation Damping
OurBigBook.com Words: 259
Critical damping is the repeated-root case of the viscous damping model: its two characteristic roots coincide at r=−ω0​. The solution with arbitrary initial conditions is
x(t)=[x0​+(v0​+ω0​x0​)t]e−ω0​t.
(20)
The extra factor t is essential: one exponential function alone cannot fit arbitrary initial displacement and velocity when the characteristic equation has a double polynomial root. Substitution, or taking the limit of the underdamped formula as its angular frequency tends to zero, gives the displayed expression.
The precise fastest-return statement is this: hold m, k, x0​>0 and v0​=0 fixed, and compare all ζ≥1. Every response decreases positively toward equilibrium; the critically damped response
xc​(t)=x0​(1+ω0​t)e−ω0​t
(21)
is smaller than every overdamped response at each t>0. It therefore reaches and stays within any specified band ∣x∣≤ϵx0​, 0<ϵ<1, sooner than those responses. Exact arrival at rest takes infinite time. This is a comparison among nonoscillatory regimes for these particular initial conditions, not a universal minimum of every possible settling-time criterion.
One short comparison argument uses q=∂X/∂ζ. Its initial conditions are zero and q′′+2ζq′+q=−2X′. For release from rest, X′<0 at τ>0; the causal Green function of a damped oscillator is positive at positive time for ζ≥1. Its convolution with −2X′ gives q>0: increasing damping beyond the critical value increases the remaining displacement.
There is no repeated oscillation at critical damping, but there can be overshoot once. For x0​>0, the factor in square brackets crosses zero precisely when v0​<−ω0​x0​, at
t∗​=−v0​+ω0​x0​x0​​>0.
(22)
For example, v0​=−2ω0​x0​ gives x=x0​(1−ω0​t)e−ω0​t, crossing equilibrium at t=1/ω0​ as shown above. An overdamped response can also cross once for suitable initial conditions. Conversely, specially chosen initial conditions can remove its slow exponential decay entirely. Thus the generic slow-root argument must not be applied to a response whose slow-root coefficient is zero.
