Galvanometer null detection

 ... Physics Branches of physics Electromagnetism Electric charge Electric current Galvanometer
OurBigBook.com Words: 193
In a Wheatstone bridge, the galvanometer connects the two comparison junctions. Its purpose is to locate a null measurement, rather than to measure the branch electric current through the unknown resistor. Using Wheatstone bridge detector loading and Thévenin's theorem, a settled moving-coil galvanometer with winding electrical resistance Rg​ obeys
Ig​=Rth​+Rg​Voc​​,θ=SI​Rth​+Rg​Voc​​.
(19)
Here Voc​ is the bridge's open-circuit voltage and Rth​ its Thévenin resistance. For a functioning passive detector with finite positive electrical resistances, exact Ig​=0 implies Voc​=0, preserving Wheatstone bridge balance. Away from balance, the detector loads the junctions; its electrical resistance cannot be ignored.
For the illustrative Rg​=100Ω, SI​=3000rad/A and a chosen Rth​=1900Ω, a 1.00mV bridge open-circuit voltage produces 0.500μA and 0.0859∘. That lies inside the stipulated 0.10∘ zero tolerance. The corresponding resolvable open-circuit voltage scale is about 1.16mV, not arbitrarily small. Actual limits also depend on the zero stability and calibration.
Use the detector checks in the slide-wire resistance bridge experiment: perturb the comparison to either side of the proposed balance and require opposite signs, while confirming the supply is on. A broken connection, a jammed pointer or an inactive supply can also give a zero reading. Conversely, a healthy bridge detector reads zero while the bridge arms still carry electric current. A zero deflection alone proves neither a broken conducting path nor infinite sensitivity.
