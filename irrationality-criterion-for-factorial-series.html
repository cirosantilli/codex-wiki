Irrationality criterion for factorial series (∑an​/n!∈/Q)

 ... Area of mathematics Analysis Real analysis Sequence and series Series (mathematics) Factorial series
OurBigBook.com Words: 87
A factorial series with integral digits 0≤an​<n is an irrational number exactly when positive digits occur infinitely often and digits below their maximum occur infinitely often. Under these two conditions every scaled tail N!∑n>N​an​/n! lies strictly between zero and one. If the sum were a rational number A/B, choose N≥B; then N!A/B and the scaled finite head are integers, contradicting the strict tail bound. Conversely, an eventually zero digit sequence gives a finite rational number sum, and an eventually maximal sequence gives a finite head plus 1/N!, again a rational number.
