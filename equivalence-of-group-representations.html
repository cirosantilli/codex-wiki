Equivalence of group representations (E(g)=S−1D(g)Sfor every g∈G)

 Home Mathematics Area of mathematics Algebra Representation theory Group representation
OurBigBook.com Words: 79
Two finite-dimensional group representations D,E over the same field are equivalent if there is one invertible linear map S intertwining all their group actions, equivalently E(g)=S−1D(g)S for every g. The same change of basis must work simultaneously for all group elements. The cyclic property of the trace implies that equivalent representations have identical characters: tr(S−1D(g)S)=trD(g). Consequently a difference at a single group element proves inequivalence. This implication does not need the group to be finite, and the reverse implication is not asserted for arbitrary groups or fields.
