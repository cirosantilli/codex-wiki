Bessel focal length uncertainty (σf2​≃A2σL2​+B2σd2​+2ABCov(L,d))

For small measurement errors, write f=L/4−d2/(4L). Its partial derivatives and first-order propagation of uncertainty are
A=∂L∂f​=41​(1+L2d2​),B=∂d∂f​=−2Ld​,
(25)
σf2​≃A2σL2​+B2σd2​+2ABCov(L,d).
(26)
Here the σ values are standard deviations used to describe uncertainty, and Cov(L,d) is the covariance. Independent carrier-reading errors give σd2​=σr−​2​+σr+​2​; correlated readings require the term −2Cov(r−​,r+​). Averaging repeated independent pairs reduces the random component of the uncertainty of the mean, but does not remove calibration or model errors.
As a stipulated uncertainty calculation, let L=45cm, d=15cm, σL​=0.10cm and σr−​​=σr+​​=0.05cm, with all three input errors independent. Then σd​=0.0707cm, A=5/18, B=−1/6 and σf​≃0.0302cm. The result would be f=10.000cm with this standard uncertainty in the stipulated thin lens model; these numbers are not a measured uncertainty budget. A small neglected principal-plane separation gives an additional first-order bias fthin​−f≃Ah, distinct from that random budget.
The two kinds of sensitivity must be distinguished. At fixed focal length,
∂L∂d​=dL−2f​,∂L∂x±​​=21​(1±dL−2f​).
(27)
These derivatives diverge as L approaches 4f from above: a small change in object-screen spacing strongly changes the separation of the two settings. Yet the inferred focal length's displacement sensitivity ∣B∣=d/(2L) tends to zero. That does not make coalescing settings easy to measure. For fixed L, define the axial mismatch between the predicted real image and the screen as g(x)=fx/(x−f)−(L−x), for x>f. At a focused setting,
g′(x)=1−(x−f)2f2​=1−m2.
(28)
Near u=v=2f, moving the optical lens changes this mismatch only weakly, making visual discrimination of the two settings difficult. At the example's settings g′(15cm)=−3 and g′(30cm)=3/4, so equal carrier motion produces different axial defocus responses. This is an axial mismatch calculation, not a full prediction of perceived image sharpness.
Choose L sufficiently above 4f to separate the two accepted focusing ranges, while retaining both real images on the available screen and rail. There is no universal best L: focusing uncertainty itself depends on aperture, transverse magnification, optical aberrations and the target. Close to d=0, the nonnegative measured separation and the quadratic term in d also make first-order propagation of uncertainty unreliable if its error is comparable to its value. Repeatability, controls and the principal plane correction determine whether the elegant two-distance formula is an adequate measurement model.
