Polarized beta-decay angular distribution (f(θ)=1+APβcosθ)

 ... Physics Branches of physics Quantum field theory Standard Model Weak interaction Parity violation
OurBigBook.com Words: 858 Articles: 2
Consider a parent atomic nucleus at rest. Write its signed nuclear polarization along a fixed axis z as P=⟨Jz​⟩/(Iℏ), where Iℏ is its maximal spin component. Let θ be the angle of the emitted electron momentum to that axis and let β=v/c. Use P for the parity operator, so it is not confused with P.
For an axially symmetric leading-dipole model, retain a constant and the term linear in the polarization vector P=Pz. Rotational symmetry makes the directional term proportional to P⋅p​. Define its dimensionless coefficient as Aβ. At fixed electron energy the relative intensity and normalized probability density function per solid angle are therefore
f(θ)=1+acosθ,a=APβ,dΩdPr​=4π1+acosθ​,∣a∣≤1.
(1)
The factor β is the conventional separation of the speed dependence; rotational symmetry alone does not determine that dependence or A. The coefficient A contains the decay dynamics. This leading model omits higher angular terms and detailed recoil and radiative effects. A partially oriented higher-spin ensemble can also have alignment that is not specified by its vector nuclear polarization alone.
The unit-integral condition follows without any model of a detector:
∫dΩ=4π,∫cosθdΩ=2π∫−11​udu=0.
(2)
Thus f has unit angular average, while the probability density function in the polar angle alone contains the Jacobian determinant factor:
dθdPr​=21​(1+acosθ)sinθ,dudPr​=21​(1+au),u=cosθ.
(3)
In particular, f itself is neither a normalized probability density function in θ nor a detector count. Positivity on −1≤u≤1 gives ∣a∣≤1.
Under full spatial reflection, p↦−p but J↦J. The latter follows already for orbital angular momentum from r×p↦(−r)×(−p)=r×p; spin has the same axial vector transformation. Hence P⋅p​ is a pseudoscalar, and cosθ↦−cosθ. If parity conservation held for this model, equal probabilities for the inverted process would force a=0. Conversely, a=0 in an unpolarized sample proves nothing about A.
Integrating over the two hemispheres gives a useful original check:
Pr(u>0)=21​+4a​,Pr(u<0)=21​−4a​,Ahemispheres​=2a​.
(4)
Choose A=−0.8, P=0.5, and β=0.75, so a=−0.3. Then the forward and backward hemisphere probabilities are 0.425 and 0.575, and their counting asymmetry is −0.15. These are chosen teaching values, not measured values from the Wu experiment. At a single energy, the angular shape determines the product APβ; it cannot separately determine A without a polarization calibration.
