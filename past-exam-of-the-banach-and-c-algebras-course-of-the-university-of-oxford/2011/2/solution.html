An element a is a Normal element of a C-star algebra if a∗a=aa∗. It is a Unitary element of a C-star algebra if a∗a=aa∗=1. Its Banach algebra spectrum is
σA​(a)={z∈C:a−z1∈/A×},​
(5)
where A× is the group of invertible elements of a Banach algebra.
For normal a, the continuous functional calculus is the unique unital isometric star-isomorphism
Φa​:C(σA​(a))⟶C∗(1,a)⊆A,f⟼f(a),
(6)
which sends the coordinate function z↦z to a. It preserves sums, products, complex scalars and the involution: Φa​(f​)=Φa​(f)∗. In particular ∥f(a)∥=maxz∈σA​(a)​∣f(z)∣. The spectral mapping theorem in this setting states
σA​(f(a))=f(σA​(a))(f∈C(σA​(a))).​
(7)
The common unit and spectral permanence for C-star subalgebras permit computing this spectrum in A itself.
For unitary u, the C-star identity gives ∥u∥=∥u∗∥=1. If ∣z∣>1, u−z1=−z(1−u/z) has a multiplicative inverse by the Neumann series. If ∣z∣<1, write u−z1=u(1−zu∗) and apply the same series. Therefore σA​(u) lies on the unit circle.
For ∣t∣=1, the denominator 1+λt is nonzero because ∣λ∣<1. The function is consequently continuous on the spectrum, and
∣t+λ∣2=1+∣λ∣2+λt+λt=∣1+λt∣2.
(8)
Thus ∣f(t)∣=1 throughout σA​(u), so f​f=ff​=1 there. Applying the star-homomorphism Φu​ proves
f(u)∗f(u)=f(u)f(u)∗=1.​
(9)
Hence f(u) is unitary. Explicitly, the same calculus gives
f(u)=(u+λ1)(1+λu)−1,​
(10)
with the denominator's inverse also supplied by a norm-convergent Neumann series. This is the Möbius transform of a unitary element. The conjugation on λ in the denominator is essential to the unit-modulus identity.
