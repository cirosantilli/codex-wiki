Finite-window lock-in response (FT​(ν)=eiνT/2sinc(νT/2))

An observation interval of finite duration is not an infinitely sharp frequency selector. Combine the lock-in quadratures into
ZT​=T2​∫0T​s(t)e−iωtdt.
(28)
For unwanted electric potential difference s(t)=Bcos((ω+δ)t+ψ), where δ is an angular frequency offset and ω+δ>0, expansion into complex exponentials gives the exact response
ZT​FT​(ν)sinc(x)​=BeiψFT​(δ)+Be−iψFT​(−(2ω+δ)),=T1​∫0T​eiνtdt=eiνT/2sinc(νT/2),={sinx/x,1,​x=0,x=0.​​

The sinc function formula follows by integrating the exponential function and factoring eiνT−1=2ieiνT/2sin(νT/2); its value at zero follows from a limit. The first term changes slowly after multiplication; the second oscillates at the sum frequency. It vanishes at exact matching when T is an integer number of reference cycles, each lasting one period of a function, but cannot generally be discarded in a short-window calculation.
For ∣δ∣≪ω and many reference cycles, the first term usually dominates: its modulus is B∣sinc(δT/2)∣ and its complex argument includes δT/2. Thus ∣δ∣T≪1 gives almost no rejection. A longer observation separates closer frequencies, with the first zeros of this term at ∣δ∣=2π/T. Those zeros are not a brick-wall cutoff: the sinc function has nonzero lobes beyond them. At δ=0 a synchronized contribution remains however large T becomes.
For a numerical check, use f0​=1Hz, T=4s, ψ=0 and an unwanted frequency f=1.125Hz. Then (f−f0​)T=1/2 and the exact expression gives
B∣ZT​∣​=π2​−17π2​=17π32​≈0.599172,
(30)
whereas the difference-frequency term alone gives 2/π≈0.636620. An interference amplitude B=0.9V therefore contributes 0.539254V to ∣ZT​∣ in this constructed example. A nearby frequency can survive strongly even though the three-times-frequency component in lock-in harmonic rejection cancels exactly.
A practical lock-in amplifier's low-pass filter weights past products differently from this rectangular observation interval. Its time constant and roll-off determine near-reference rejection and settling time; narrowing detection slows the response.[ref]
