Weak interaction

 Home Physics Branches of physics Quantum field theory Standard Model
OurBigBook.com Words: 4k Articles: 35
The weak interaction changes particle species through the charged current and also acts through the neutral current. Familiar examples include beta-minus decay and neutrino scattering. It is short-range and chiral: its W boson and Z boson couplings distinguish chirality. Above the electroweak scale it belongs to the unbroken SU(2)L​×U(1)Y​ electroweak interaction. At much lower energies, W boson exchange is approximated by the Fermi interaction.
A particularly direct window into the weak interaction is the Wu experiment: a sample with oriented nuclear spins emits electrons preferentially toward one end of its spin axis. The comparison tests parity conservation, because spin is an axial vector and momentum is a polar vector. The polarized beta-decay angular distribution translates that geometric distinction into a measurable counting asymmetry.
