Raman shift (Δν=ν0​−νs​)

 Home Physics Branches of physics Optics Scattering of light Raman scattering
OurBigBook.com Words: 112
The signed Raman shift subtracts scattered from incident spectroscopic wavenumber:
Δν=cν0​−νs​​=λ0​1​−λs​1​=hcEf​−Ei​​.
(39)
Here c is the speed of light, and both wavelengths are in vacuum. Positive shifts denote Stokes Raman scattering; negative shifts denote Anti-Stokes Raman scattering. Some plots reverse the horizontal direction or report separate unsigned magnitudes, so check the convention. Spectroscopic wavenumber has no 2π factor, unlike the common angular wavenumber k=2π/λ. A shift of 1000cm−1 means 100000m−1, not a frequency of 1000Hz.
For a one-quantum change of molecular vibration with ordinary frequency νv​, the magnitude is νv​=νv​/c. This makes Raman spectroscopy attractive: the same molecular energy spacing can be read using different visible excitation wavelengths. Equal and opposite Raman shifts are not equal and opposite changes measured in nanometres, because wavelength is the reciprocal of spectroscopic wavenumber.
