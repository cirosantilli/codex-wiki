Neutral current

 Home Physics Branches of physics Quantum field theory Standard Model Weak interaction
OurBigBook.com Words: 1k Articles: 6
The weak neutral current couples the Z boson to a fermion without changing its electric charge. Its vector and axial-vector coefficients depend on weak isospin, electric charge, and the weak mixing angle.
The neutral current transfers no electric charge at a fermion vertex; “neutral” does not mean that every participating particle is uncharged. For example, νμ​e−→νμ​e− transfers energy and momentum to an electron through Z boson exchange. By contrast, a charged current can convert a muon neutrino into a muon, as in νμ​n→μ−p. Both processes conserve total electric charge. A photon also carries no electric charge, but an active neutrino has no electromagnetic charge coupling: its neutral current is a distinct weak interaction.
The experimentally useful contrast on a nucleon target is
νμ​+N⟶μ−+X(charged current),νμ​+N⟶νμ​+X(neutral current),
(14)
where N is a nucleon and X is an allowed final state of hadrons. The charged current makes a charged lepton available for identification; the outgoing neutrino in a neutral current leaves no bubble-chamber track. A sample of hadronic events without an identified muon therefore poses an experimental question: how many could instead be neutron interactions, or charged current events whose muon was missed? The answer requires a detector-response and background analysis, not merely an inspection for an absent line.
