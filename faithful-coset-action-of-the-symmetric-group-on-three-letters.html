Faithful coset action of the symmetric group on three letters (CoreS3​​(⟨(12)⟩)={e})

 ... Mathematics Area of mathematics Algebra Group theory Group action Coset action
OurBigBook.com Words: 222
Take the symmetric group G=S3​ and the subgroup H=⟨(12)⟩={e,(12)} of group order two. With the rightmost permutation applied first, its three left cosets are
C3​=H={e,(12)},C2​=(23)H={(23),(132)},C1​=(13)H={(13),(123)}.
(55)
The subscripts record the image of 3: every member of Ci​ sends 3 to i. Because H is the stabilizer subgroup of 3 in the usual group action, aH↦a(3) is a bijection onto {1,2,3}. It is an equivariant map, since (ga)(3)=g(a(3)). The coset action therefore becomes the familiar group action on three letters. In particular,
(12):C1​↔C2​, C3​↦C3​,(123):C1​↦C2​↦C3​↦C1​.
(56)
The three stabilizer subgroups are
GC1​​={e,(23)},GC2​​={e,(13)},GC3​​={e,(12)}.
(57)
Their intersection is {e}, so the subgroup core and the group kernel are trivial. This proves that the coset action is a faithful group action even though every point has a nontrivial stabilizer subgroup. The orbit-stabilizer theorem reads 6=3⋅2, while the induced group homomorphism gives six distinct permutations of the three cosets. For example, (12) and e agree at C3​, but disagree at C1​; inspecting one label would miss their difference.
The subgroup need not be a normal subgroup for the coset action to exist: (123)H(123)−1={e,(23)}=H. Here the group action is informative because the three conjugate stabilizer subgroups have no common nonidentity element. For the normal subgroup A3​ instead, nonfaithful coset action of the symmetric group on three letters shows that only the sign of a permutation survives.
