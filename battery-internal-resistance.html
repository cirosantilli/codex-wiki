Battery internal resistance (VL​=E−Ir)

 Home Physics Branches of physics Electromagnetism Electrical circuit Electric battery
OurBigBook.com Words: 185
A useful short-interval approximation replaces a battery by an ideal electromotive force E in series with a positive internal electrical resistance r. This is the Thévenin terminal response with open-circuit voltage E and Thévenin resistance r. The model describes the terminals without asserting that there is a separate physical resistor hidden inside the cell. Both E and r can change with composition, temperature, electric current and history.[ref]
For a resistive electrical load R>0, take I>0 to mean discharge: conventional current leaves the positive terminal. Kirchhoff's voltage law and Ohm's law give
E−Ir−IR=0,I=R+rE​,VL​=IR=E−Ir.
(46)
For the stipulated E=3V, r=1Ω, R=4Ω,
I=0.6A,Ir=0.6V,VL​=2.4V.
(47)
An open-circuit voltage of 3V therefore does not promise 3V at this electrical load. Increasing the load electrical resistance reduces electric current and brings VL​ closer to E, as in Thévenin load response.
The signed relation also explains charging within this model. If an external source drives electric current into the positive terminal, I<0 and VL​=E+∣I∣r. The terminal electric potential difference then exceeds E. This algebra is not evidence that every battery is rechargeable.[ref]
