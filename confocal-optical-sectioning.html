Confocal optical sectioning

 ... Branches of physics Optics Physical optics Fluorescence Fluorescence microscope Confocal microscopy
OurBigBook.com Words: 262
At one depth, scan the illumination spot across x and y and assign the detector reading during each dwell interval to a pixel. Move the specimen or objective optical lens axially to acquire the next depth. A stack of these optical sections samples three-dimensional structure. With beam scanning, the returning fluorescence commonly retraces the scanning optics: this descanning holds the image of the currently illuminated point at the detection pinhole. With stage scanning, the fixed spot and pinhole remain aligned while the specimen moves. [ref]
An optical section is a depth-weighted image of an intact specimen, with a finite axial response and tails. A physical cut actually separates a slice of material; its thickness and cutting damage are properties of the preparation. Changing optical focus selects another optical section without removing a slice, although illumination can still damage a specimen. Neither a thin physical slice nor a sharply displayed optical section makes the imaging point spread function infinitely narrow. [ref]
Point response and section thickness answer different questions. For a spatially uniform, very thin fluorescent sheet, its response versus axial displacement is proportional to the lateral integral of the three-dimensional confocal response,

A smaller axial sampling step gives more samples of the same response, rather than automatically improving diffraction-limited resolution. [ref]
