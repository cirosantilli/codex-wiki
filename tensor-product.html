Tensor product (⊗)

 Home Mathematics Area of mathematics Algebra Linear algebra Multilinear algebra
OurBigBook.com Words: 2k Articles: 19
A tensor product turns a bilinear map into a linear map. For vector spaces V,W over the same field F, its elements are finite sums of pure tensors v⊗w, subject to bilinearity:
(v+v′)⊗wv⊗(w+w′)(av)⊗w​=v⊗w+v′⊗w,=v⊗w+v⊗w′,=a(v⊗w)=v⊗(aw).​
(1)
The scalar a belongs to F; writing V⊗F​W makes this choice explicit. These rules preserve separate linearity while allowing arbitrary linear combinations of products.[ref]
For a concrete example, take V=W=R2, with basis vectors e1​,e2​ in the first copy and f1​,f2​ in the second. The four tensor-product basis elements are
e1​⊗f1​,e1​⊗f2​,e2​⊗f1​,e2​⊗f2​.
(2)
The pure tensor made from v=2e1​+e2​ and w=−f1​+3f2​ expands to
v⊗w=−2e1​⊗f1​+6e1​⊗f2​−e2​⊗f1​+3e2​⊗f2​.
(3)
Each basis vector in one factor can interact with each basis vector in the other. This is why dimensions multiply; the tensor-product coefficient matrix displays these four interactions as a two-by-two grid.
The tensor product is different from the Cartesian product V×W. With componentwise vector addition and scalar multiplication, the latter contains pairs and has dimension dimV+dimW. The map τ(v,w)=v⊗w is bilinear, generally neither linear on that Cartesian product nor injective nor surjective. In our example, τ(2v,2w)=4τ(v,w), and τ(2v,w/2)=τ(v,w); the separability of a tensor explains why some outputs cannot be reached at all. Both spaces happen to have dimension four when V=W=R2, but this coincidence does not identify their structures.
