Point transitivity ({fn(x):n≥0}​=X)

 Home Physics Branches of physics Dynamical systems Topological dynamics
OurBigBook.com Words: 106
A continuous map on a nonempty topological space is point-transitive if it has a dense forward orbit. This is one convention for topological transitivity, but on spaces with isolated points it must be distinguished from the open-set intersection definition. On a nonempty compact metric space, the positive-time open-set intersection property implies point transitivity: for each member Uj​ of a countable basis of a topology, ⋃n≥1​f−n(Uj​) is open and dense, and the Baire category theorem supplies a point in their intersection. Conversely, a surjective point-transitive continuous map has every tail of its dense orbit dense, since fk(X)=X and continuity sends its orbit closure into the closure of that tail. The map f(1/j)=1/(j+1), f(0)=0 on {0}∪{1/j:j≥1} is point-transitive but fails the open-set intersection property.
