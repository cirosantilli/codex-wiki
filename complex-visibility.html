Complex visibility (V(u,v))

 ... Branches of physics Astrophysics Observational astronomy Radio astronomy Aperture synthesis Radio interferometry
OurBigBook.com Words: 570 Articles: 6
A complex visibility is a calibrated complex correlation of the signals received at a particular interferometric baseline. In a narrow field, with scalar radiation, the far-field approximation for an outgoing source, a narrow frequency channel, negligible noncoplanar phase curvature and a steady sky of mutually incoherent emitters,
V(u,v)=∬A(l,m)I(l,m)e−2πi(ul+vm)dldm.
(57)
Here l,m are small dimensionless sky-direction offsets, I is brightness in the chosen channel, and A is the primary beam. This is the Fourier transform of beam-weighted brightness. The two-dimensional formula requires those approximations: a wide field generally needs the third baseline coordinate and curved sky geometry.
For an explicit one-dimensional derivation, set the primary beam to one over the small field, and place independent emitters at angular offsets θj​. Choose a wave phase convention in which, after removing the phase centre's delay, their received complex amplitudes are
E1​(t)=∑j​aj​(t),E2​(t)=∑j​aj​(t)e2πiuθj​,⟨aj​ak∗​⟩=Ij​δjk​.
(58)
Here δjk​ is the Kronecker delta and angle brackets denote an expected value estimated by time averaging a steady signal. The last equality states mutual incoherence: different emitters have zero averaged cross term, although each emitter's two received copies remain correlated. Multiplying and averaging gives
V(u)=⟨E1​E2∗​⟩=∑j,k​⟨aj​ak∗​⟩e−2πiuθk​=∑j​Ij​e−2πiuθj​.
(59)
For continuous brightness this becomes V(u)=∫I(θ)e−2πiuθdθ. Real brightness implies the Hermitian symmetry V(−u)=V(u)∗; it does not require brightness to be even. In particular, an asymmetric real source usually has a nonzero imaginary complex visibility. At zero baseline, V(0)=∑j​Ij​ is the total beam-weighted source strength. Ordinary separated-element correlations need not measure that zero-baseline value.
