Set x=B2/S and q=(S/B)α, so W=qV(t,x). Direct differentiation gives
Wt​=qVt​,SWS​=q(αV−xVx​),
(67)
S2WSS​=q[α(α−1)V+2(1−α)xVx​+x2Vxx​].
(68)
Substitute into the Black-Scholes equation and divide by q:
qLW​=Vt​+21​σ2x2Vxx​+[σ2(1−α)−r]xVx​+[21​σ2α(α−1)+rα−r]V.
(69)
To recover the same operator on V, its first-derivative coefficient must be r. This gives
α=1−σ22r​.​
(70)
For this choice the last coefficient is −r, since 21​σ2(α−1)+r=0. Hence LW=qLV=0, proving the required invariance. The transformation is the stock-inversion reflection for the Black-Scholes equation.
