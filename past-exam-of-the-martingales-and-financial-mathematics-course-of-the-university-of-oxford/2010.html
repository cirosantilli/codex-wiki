A measurable space is a set E together with a sigma-algebra E of its subsets: it contains E and is closed under complements relative to E and countable unions. A measure is a map μ:E→[0,∞] with μ(∅)=0 and countable additivity on disjoint measurable sets:
μ(⋃j​Aj​)=∑j​μ(Aj​).
(1)
A finite measure additionally satisfies μ(E)<∞.

Solution

ii OurBigBook.com
For ε>0, define the measurable decreasing tail events
An​(ε)=⋃m≥n​{∣fm​−f∣>ε}={supm≥n​∣fm​−f∣>ε}.
(2)
If fn​→f almost everywhere, every point outside its null exceptional set eventually leaves each such tail event, so μ(⋂n​An​(ε))=0. Continuity from above of a measure applies because μ(E)<∞, giving μ(An​(ε))→0.
Conversely, assume these measures tend to zero. For each positive integer k, the intersection ⋂n​An​(1/k) has measure zero. Outside the union of these countably many null sets, every k has an index after which ∣fm​−f∣≤1/k for all later m. Hence fm​→f there. This proves the desired equivalence. The argument uses all positive thresholds; replacing the intersection of tail events by a single strict limsup inequality at the same threshold would mishandle equality cases.

The triangle inequality gives the event inclusion

The Cauchy assumption lets us choose nk​ recursively, increasing strictly, so that μ(∣fn​−fm​∣>2−k)<2−k for all m,n≥nk​. In particular the measurable events
Ek​={∣fnk+1​​−fnk​​∣>2−k}
(4)
have summable measures. The measure of ⋃k≥K​Ek​ is at most ∑k≥K​2−k→0, so limsupEk​ is a null set. Outside it, the successive differences are eventually bounded by a summable geometric series. Hence fnk​​ is pointwise Cauchy and has a finite real limit.
Define f to be that limit wherever the sequence converges, and zero elsewhere. The convergence set is measurable by the countable Cauchy criterion, and the limit is measurable as a limit of measurable functions on that set. Thus there is no need to assume the measure is complete. Part (a)(ii), or the corresponding finite-measure implication, gives fnk​​→f in measure.
For the full sequence use
μ(∣fn​−f∣>ε)≤μ(∣fn​−fnk​​∣>ε/2)+μ(∣fnk​​−f∣>ε/2).
(5)
Choose a sufficiently late subsequence index so the second term is small; the Cauchy property then bounds the first uniformly for all large n. Therefore fn​→f in measure. This proves completeness of convergence in measure on a finite measure space.

Kolmogorov zero-one law states that every event in the tail sigma-algebra of an independent sequence has probability zero or one. Removing any fixed number of summands changes Sn​/n by a finite random constant divided by n, so does not change its extended-real limsup. Therefore X is measurable with respect to the tail sigma-algebra. Each event {X≤q} for rational q has probability zero or one. Monotonicity of these events forces the distribution to be concentrated at one extended-real value, allowing either infinity.
The strong law of large numbers says that for independent identically distributed variables with E∣X1​∣<∞,
Sn​/n⟶EX1​almost surely.​

The printed update needs a one-step index correction to match its filtration: the draw for the transition from n to n+1 must be the fresh variable Un+1​. Also the trivial initial sigma-algebra is {∅,Ω}, rather than the printed {∅,F}. With literal Un​, the next increment is already known at time n, so E[Xn+1​∣Fn​]=Xn+1​; the nontrivial update differs from Xn​. Thus the stated martingale claim is false with that indexing. The following uses the standard Pólya urn described in words.
Put m=B0​+R0​, so the total count is m+n. Conditional on Fn​, the next blue indicator In+1​ has expectation Xn​. Hence
E[Xn+1​∣Fn​]=m+n+1Bn​+E[In+1​∣Fn​]​=m+n+1Bn​+Bn​/(m+n)​=Xn​.
(12)
Adaptedness and integrability follow from the recursive construction and 0≤Xn​≤1. Thus Xn​ is a bounded martingale. The martingale convergence theorem gives an almost-sure limit X∞​∈[0,1]. Boundedness and the dominated convergence theorem then give E∣Xn​−X∞​∣2→0, establishing convergence in L2.

The risk-neutral probability must make the expected stock multiplier equal the bank multiplier:
1.2q+1(1−q)=1.1,q=21​.​
(17)
Both up and down paths have positive probability under this measure. A self-financing portfolio's discounted value is then a Q-martingale, since the discounted bank asset is constant and the discounted stock is a martingale. An arbitrage with zero initial wealth would have nonnegative terminal value and positive value in some possible state, giving a strictly positive Q-expectation and contradicting its zero discounted initial value. Thus the market with er=1.1 is arbitrage free.

The commodity's total gain from holding one unit is dS−δdt, because the storage cost is a fixed cash rate, not a proportional dividend yield. Form a locally self-financing delta hedge long the derivative and short Δ=VS​ units of the commodity. Itô's formula eliminates Brownian risk and leaves drift
Vt​+21​σ2S2VSS​+δVS​.
(30)
Its value is V−SVS​, so absence of arbitrage makes that drift equal to r(V−SVS​). Therefore the Black-Scholes equation with constant cash storage costs is
Vt​+(rS+δ)VS​+21​σ2S2VSS​=rV,V(T,S)=Ψ(S).​
(31)
The cash cost enters as δVS​, rather than as δSVS​, because one unit costs δ per unit time regardless of its current price.

At expiry, the difference of a European call and put with matching strike is (ST​−K)+−(K−ST​)+=ST​−K. Linearity of pricing therefore makes their difference equal the forward-contract value from part (c):
C(t,S)−P(t,S)=S+rδ​(1−e−r(T−t))−Ke−r(T−t).​
