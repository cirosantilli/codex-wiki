A filtration is an increasing sequence of sub-sigma-algebras Fn​⊆F: if m≤n, then Fm​⊆Fn​. It represents information available through each time.

A Borel measurable function has f−1(B)∈B for every Borel set B. Thus the upper-ray condition is necessary.
For sufficiency, note that the rays generate the stipulated sigma-algebra: (a,b]=(a,∞)∖(b,∞), and (y,∞)=⋃m>y​(y,m], with integer m. The family
H={B⊆R:f−1(B)∈B}
(26)
is a sigma-algebra because inverse images preserve complements and countable unions. If it contains all upper rays, it contains their generated Borel sigma-algebra. Therefore upper-ray preimages suffice to prove Borel measurability.

Every open interval is Borel, since (a,b)=⋃m≥1​(a,b−1/m], omitting empty pieces. Open subsets of the line are countable unions of open intervals and hence Borel. Conversely, each half-open interval is obtained from open sets by sigma-algebra operations, so this is the usual Borel sigma-algebra.
A continuous function has open, and therefore Borel, inverse images of open sets. Since open sets generate the target Borel sigma-algebra, it is a Borel measurable function. A discontinuous example is 1[0,∞)​, whose only level sets are built from the Borel half-lines; it jumps at zero.

At maturity a shout has payoff S2​−K, while no shout gives (S2​−K)+. If S2​<K, shouting creates a negative payoff and is strictly worse. If S2​≥K, the two choices give exactly the same payoff, so shouting is optional and gives no improvement.
Thus do not shout when out of the money; be indifferent when at or in the money. The terminal stock values are 121, 99, and 81, so only the up-up node permits an indifferent shout with the given strike.

If the first move is up, S1​=110 and S2​ can be 121 or 99. A shout now gives max(S2​,110)−101, hence payoffs 20 and 9. Waiting until maturity or never shouting gives 20 and 0. The first policy is no worse on either branch and strictly better on the down branch, so shout after the first up move.
If the first move is down, S1​=90 and the terminal prices are 99 or 81. A shout would give −2 or −11, while waiting gives zero. Thus do not shout after the first down move. These comparisons use terminal payoffs directly, so no intermediate discounting can change their ordering.
