The optional stopping theorem applies to M at the bounded stopping time n∧Tk​. As M0​=0, it yields the valid stopped second-moment identity for a simple symmetric random walk
ESn∧Tk​2​=E(n∧Tk​).​
(27)
The printed bound on ETk​ is false. Before hitting the positive level k, the walk can travel arbitrarily far downwards, so ∣Sn∧Tk​​∣≤k is unavailable.
Here is a valid proof of finiteness of Tk​. For a positive integer L, stop instead at τ=inf{n:Sn​∈{−L,k}}. The same identity and the bound ∣Sn∧τ​∣≤max(L,k) give
E(n∧τ)≤max(L,k)2.
(28)
The monotone convergence theorem makes Eτ<∞, so τ<∞ almost surely. Applying optional stopping to S at n∧τ and then using dominated convergence gives ESτ​=0. Since Sτ​∈{−L,k}, this means
P(Sτ​=k)=L+kL​.
(29)
Consequently P(Tk​<∞)≥L/(L+k) for every L, and letting L→∞ proves Tk​<∞ almost surely.
In fact this is the infinite mean first passage of a simple symmetric random walk. If ETk​<∞, the first displayed identity would bound the second moments of Sn∧Tk​​ uniformly. Their uniform integrability and their almost sure convergence to k would imply ESn∧Tk​​→k. But the optional stopping theorem applied to S at every bounded n∧Tk​ makes each expectation zero, a contradiction. Hence the correct conclusion is
Tk​<∞ almost surely,ETk​=∞.​
(30)
