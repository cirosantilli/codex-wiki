At T1​, with underlying spot s, the call option on a put option pays
H(s)=[p(T1​,s;K2​,T2​)−K1​]+.
(45)
Let D=K2​e−r(T2​−T1​). The underlying put option value is strictly decreasing and convex in s>0, with limits D at zero and zero at infinity. If 0<K1​<D, there is a unique s∗​ at which that put value equals K1​. Hence the compound option payoff is a decreasing convex curve from D−K1​ to zero on [0,s∗​], followed by a horizontal zero segment. There is an upward kink at s∗​. If K1​≥D, the payoff is identically zero.
For K1​=0, the holder obtains the underlying put at no cost, so the payoff is its full value at T1​. The discounted put price is a martingale under the risk-neutral measure. Using the tower property in the risk-neutral pricing,
V(t,s)=p(t,s;K2​,T2​),t<T1​.​
(46)
Explicitly, this is K2​e−r(T2​−t)Φ(−d2​)−sΦ(−d1​) with d1​,d2​ evaluated over T2​−t. The intermediate choice time disappears when its exercise cost is zero.
