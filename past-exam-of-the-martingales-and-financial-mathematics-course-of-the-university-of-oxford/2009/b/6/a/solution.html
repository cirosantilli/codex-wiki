If d1​>d2​, buy 1/100 of stock 1 and sell 1/100 of stock 2. Initial cost is zero; the one-period payoff is u1​−u2​>0 in the up state and d1​−d2​>0 in the down state. This is an arbitrage. If terminal rather than time-one arbitrage is required, reinvest the positive receipts in either positive-price stock.
For d1​<d2​, define the one-period Arrow state prices
πu​=u1​d2​−u2​d1​d2​−d1​​,πd​=u1​d2​−u2​d1​u1​−u2​​.
(49)
The denominator is positive because u1​/u2​>1>d1​/d2​. Both state prices are strictly positive and satisfy πu​ui​+πd​di​=1 for i=1,2. Thus every portfolio's present value equals the positive state-price-weighted value of its two future payoffs.
Use the same strictly positive pricing rule at every node of the two-period tree. A nonnegative terminal payoff that is positive in at least one state has strictly positive price by backward induction, so a zero-cost self-financing strategy cannot generate an arbitrage. Therefore the market is arbitrage free when d1​<d2​, despite having no initially supplied bond. This is a two-stock binomial market without a supplied bond.
