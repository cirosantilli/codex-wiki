The pi-systems criterion says that if I1​,I2​ are pi-systems and P(A∩B)=P(A)P(B) for all A∈I1​, B∈I2​, then their generated sigma-algebras are independent. Necessity is immediate. We may add Ω to either pi-system, since it does not alter the generated sigma-algebra or the product identities.
For sufficiency, fix A∈I1​ and define
DA​={B∈G2​:P(A∩B)=P(A)P(B)}.
(29)
It contains Ω, is closed under complements by subtraction from P(A), and is closed under countable disjoint unions by countable additivity. Thus it is a lambda-system. It contains I2​, so the pi-lambda theorem gives DA​=G2​.
Now fix any B∈G2​ and apply the same lambda-system argument to the sets A∈G1​ satisfying the identity. The first step shows that this lambda-system contains I1​, so it contains all G1​. Thus the product identity holds on both entire sigma-algebras, proving their independence.

Let C be the event that the sample averages have a limit in the extended real number line. It is measurable: the bounded sequence arctanSn​ converges exactly when it is Cauchy, and the Cauchy condition is a countable combination of measurable inequalities. Limits at ±π/2 correspond to extended limits ±∞.
For each fixed m, remove the first m−1 terms and set
Sn(m)​=n1​∑k=mn​Xk​(n≥m).
(33)
The difference Sn​−Sn(m)​=n−1∑k<m​Xk​ tends to zero for every outcome, since the finitely many variables are real-valued. Thus these two sequences have an extended limit simultaneously. Equivalently their arctangents differ by a quantity tending to zero, because arctangent is Lipschitz. The event C is therefore expressible using Xm​,Xm+1​,… alone. Since this holds for every m, C∈T.
Kolmogorov's law gives P(C)=0 or one. In the latter case the measurable extended variable limsupSn​, redefined on a null set if needed, is the almost sure convergence. In the former there is almost surely no extended-real limit. Hence extended convergence occurs almost surely, or divergence occurs almost surely. The law need not make the value of a possible limit finite.

A down-and-out option with no rebate pays (ST​−K)+​ only if the stock has stayed above the lower barrier B up to maturity; if the barrier is hit, it expires worthless. For an unknocked-out contract currently at S>B, apply part (a) to the ordinary European call option price:
CDO(t,S)=CE(t,S;K)−(BS​)1−2r/σ2CE(t,B2/S;K),K≥B.​
(71)
Both terms solve the PDE. At S=B they cancel, enforcing the absorbing boundary. At maturity and S>B, the reflected stock value is B2/S<B≤K, so the reflected call option is zero and the required terminal contingent claim payoff remains. Standard growth and uniqueness conditions for the killed pricing problem identify this solution as the option value.
If the contract has already hit its barrier, its value is zero regardless of a later hypothetical stock recovery. Thus the formula is conditional on survival to the current time; the barrier history is part of the contract state.
