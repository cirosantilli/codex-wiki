Sparse peaks can determine a limit superior (an​=1{n=2k for some k≥0}​)

Define a sequence by an​=1 when n is a power of two, including 1=20, and an​=0 otherwise. Every tail of a sequence contains both a later power of two and a later nonpower. Therefore liminfan​=0 and limsupan​=1, and the sequence does not converge. Its peak indices become increasingly sparse, yet never stop appearing.
Through index N≥1, the number of peaks is 1+⌊log2​N⌋, so their fraction is
N1+⌊log2​N⌋​⟶0.
(21)
The set of powers of two has natural density zero. The same fraction is the Cesàro mean of the first N terms, which tends to zero even though the limit superior stays one. At N=1024, eleven peaks give mean 11/1024≈0.0107422. An average and a long-run upper edge answer different questions about the same sequence.
