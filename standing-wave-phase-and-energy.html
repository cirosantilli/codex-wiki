Standing wave phase and energy (⟨P⟩t​=0)

 Home Physics Mathematical physics Standing wave
OurBigBook.com Words: 201
A stationary mode shape does not imply absence of energy. Write A=2a and y=Asin(kx)cos(ωt). For the ideal wave equation on a string, the kinetic energy and elastic potential energy per unit reference length add to
e=2μ​yt2​+2τ​yx2​,P=−τyx​yt​.
(104)
Here P is signed power crossing a position on the string, positive to the right; e is an energy density per length, not per volume. Differentiation and μytt​=τyxx​ give the local conservation of energy identity ∂t​e+∂x​P=0. Substitution into P gives
P(x,t)=τA2kωsin(kx)cos(kx)sin(ωt)cos(ωt).
(105)
It can be nonzero away from a node of a standing wave or an antinode, but its time average over an oscillation period is zero. Energy flows locally back and forth even though this pure standing wave transports no net energy across a section over a complete cycle. At a node of a standing wave, yt​=0, so kinetic energy density is zero, but yx​ need not vanish: elastic potential energy density can be positive there.
For a fixed-end normal mode, integration over 0≤x≤L gives
K(t)=4μA2ω2L​sin2(ωt),U(t)=4τA2k2L​cos2(ωt),E=K+U=4μA2ω2L​.
(106)
The last equality uses τk2=μω2. At a straight-string snapshot, U=0 while K=E; at an extreme snapshot, K=0 while U=E. This is normal-mode energy exchange. A driven real string with damping requires continuing input power to replace lost energy, so a visually stationary pattern does not establish zero average supplied power.
