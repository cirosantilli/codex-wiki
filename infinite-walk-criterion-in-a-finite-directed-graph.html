Infinite walk criterion in a finite directed graph

 ... Mathematics Area of mathematics Foundations of mathematics Graph theory Directed graph Strongly connected component
OurBigBook.com Words: 116
A finite directed graph admits an infinite edge-following walk from s exactly when a directed cycle is reachable from s. An infinite walk repeats a vertex and therefore contains a reachable cycle; conversely, reach a cycle and repeat it. In a strongly connected component, a cycle exists precisely when the component has at least two vertices or is a singleton with a self-loop. Reachability followed by the two-pass strongly connected component algorithm decides the criterion in O(∣V∣+∣E∣) time.
The nonempty set of vertices visited infinitely often by a single infinite walk lies within one strongly connected component. For two such vertices, arbitrarily late visits supply directed walks in both directions. This set need not be the whole component: the walk can keep choosing one cycle and omit other vertices.
