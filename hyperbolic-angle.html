Hyperbolic angle (t=2A)

 ... Area of mathematics Analysis Real analysis Calculus Exponential function Hyperbolic function
OurBigBook.com Words: 120
A hyperbolic angle t is twice the signed area A enclosed by the two rays from the origin to (1,0) and (cosht,sinht) and the intervening arc of the unit hyperbola. Counterclockwise traversal is positive: t>0 gives an ordinary area t/2 above the horizontal axis, and t<0 gives signed area t/2 below it. This parallels the unit circle, whose sector has area θ/2 for an angle θ measured in radians.
To check the area interpretation, write x(u)=coshu and y(u)=sinhu. Green's theorem expresses the signed area as a line integral; the straight radial edges contribute zero, leaving
A(t)=21​∫0t​(x(u)y′(u)−y(u)x′(u))du=21​∫0t​(cosh2u−sinh2u)du=2t​.
(54)
Thus t is an area parameter, rather than the ordinary angle of the ray. For t>0, that ordinary angle is arctan(tanht); it stays below π/4 even as the hyperbolic angle grows without bound.
