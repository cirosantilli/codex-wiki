Renormalization of spontaneously broken gauge theories

 Home Physics Branches of physics Quantum field theory Perturbative quantum field theory Renormalization
OurBigBook.com Words: 975 Articles: 3
Renormalization of spontaneously broken gauge theories means that the Higgs mechanism can generate gauge boson masses while a fixed finite set of renormalized couplings, masses and field normalizations still suffices to absorb ultraviolet divergences. The symmetry constrains the counterterms even when the chosen vacuum expectation value hides it. This makes radiative corrections to the electroweak interaction calculable after inputs have been fixed by renormalization conditions.
A useful distinction is between choosing variables that display the physical particles and choosing variables that display good power counting. Unitary gauge removes the would-be Goldstone bosons from the explicit fields. R-xi gauge keeps them and the required Faddeev-Popov ghosts in intermediate Feynman diagrams, exposing simpler large-momentum behavior. The physical scattering amplitude must agree after all contributions and counterterms have been included consistently.
In Renormalizable Lagrangians for massive Yang-Mills fields (1971), Gerard 't Hooft constructed spontaneously broken gauge theories with massive gauge bosons. His worked SU(2) gauge theory with an adjoint Higgs field leaves a massless photon; it is a demonstration model, not the full chiral Standard Model. The gauge fixing, Faddeev-Popov ghosts and Ward identities connect renormalizability with physical unitarity.[ref]
Regularization and renormalization of gauge fields (1972), by Gerard 't Hooft and Martinus J. G. Veltman, developed dimensional regularization with recursive subtraction of ultraviolet subdivergences. It continued Feynman integrals in the dimension, keeping the relevant Ward identities in non-anomalous cases; its final section examined quantum anomalies. The procedure does not make an inconsistent chiral gauge theory consistent: gauge anomaly cancellation remains essential.[ref]
The organizing proof outline is to use good large-momentum propagators, subtract every divergent subgraph, and constrain the remaining local divergences by the Ward identities. A finite family of allowed counterterms must close under this operation at every loop order. The same identities must remove unphysical intermediate contributions from physical amplitudes. Power counting alone establishes neither closure nor these cancellations. Modern BRST symmetry packages these restrictions; the scalar subtraction below illustrates only locality.
