X-ray astronomy

 Home Physics Branches of physics Astrophysics Observational astronomy
OurBigBook.com Words: 1k Articles: 7
X-ray astronomy infers the properties of celestial sources from their X-rays. It requires instruments above the strongly absorbing layers of Earth's terrestrial atmosphere. Riccardo Giacconi's 2002 Nobel lecture connects the early rocket detections to later grazing-incidence X-ray optics, which collect photons over a large entrance area onto a small detector.[ref]
The measurement is an inverse problem: a count rate mixes incident photon arrival rate, detector response, and background. A point-like astronomical source produces a direction-dependent excess as the instrument scans; diffuse X-ray background contributes over many directions. Neither a count excess nor a narrow feature by itself determines the emission mechanism. The useful progression is from detecting an excess, to locating it, to measuring its spectrum and light curve.
