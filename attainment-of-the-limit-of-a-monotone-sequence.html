Attainment of the limit of a monotone sequence

 ... Analysis Real analysis Sequence and series Sequence Monotone sequence Bounded monotone sequence theorem
OurBigBook.com Words: 106
The supremum of an increasing bounded sequence need not be a maximum of its set of terms. For an​=1−1/n, every term is strictly less than one; the supremum and limit of a sequence are one, but there is no maximum. Every candidate term is beaten by the next. The decreasing sequence 1+1/n similarly has infimum one without a minimum.
If an increasing sequence with limit of a sequence L attains ak​=L, it must remain there: L=ak​≤an​≤L for n≥k. The decreasing case is identical with inequalities reversed. Thus attainment of the limit of a sequence is equivalent to an eventually constant tail of a sequence for a convergent monotone sequence. The increasing sequence 0,1/2,1,1,… has both supremum and maximum one. [ref]
