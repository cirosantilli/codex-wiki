Exact variational solution in a two-level system (E(θ)=2sin2θ)

 Home Physics Branches of physics Quantum theory Variational method
OurBigBook.com Words: 187 Articles: 1
In a two-level system, let the Hamiltonian operator have matrix H=diag(0,2) in the orthonormal basis ∣0⟩,∣1⟩. The normalized real trial quantum states and their Rayleigh quotient are
∣ψθ​⟩=cosθ∣0⟩+sinθ∣1⟩,E(θ)=⟨ψθ​∣H∣ψθ​⟩=2sin2θ.
(104)
The excited-state probability amplitude is sinθ, so its probability is sin2θ. Thus E(θ)≥0=E0​, with equality if and only if the excited-state probability amplitude vanishes: θ∈πZ. The remaining quantum state is ∣0⟩ up to a global phase. At θ=π/4 the trial energy is 1; at θ=π/2 it is 2. These are upper bounds on the ground-state energy, even though neither is a minimizing trial.
The real trial family contains the ground state, so it attains the exact minimum despite not covering every complex quantum state. More generally, a family covering all normalized vectors in a two-dimensional Hilbert space gives the exact lowest eigenvalue of any Hermitian matrix by minimizing its Rayleigh quotient. For a complex trial c0​∣0⟩+c1​∣1⟩ with ∣c0​∣2+∣c1​∣2=1, the same calculation gives E=2∣c1​∣2: a relative quantum phase cannot change this diagonal Hamiltonian energy. This finite example is the weighted mean proof of the variational method with just two energy eigenstates.
