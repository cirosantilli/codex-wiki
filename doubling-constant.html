Doubling constant (∣A+A∣/∣A∣)

 Home Mathematics Area of mathematics Combinatorics Additive combinatorics
OurBigBook.com Words: 869 Articles: 15
The doubling constant of a nonempty finite set S⊆G in an abelian group G is
K(S)=∣S∣∣S+S∣​.
(20)
It measures the cardinality of the sumset relative to the original set. For the integer examples, K(A)=7/4 and K(B)=10/4=5/2. A nonconstant arithmetic progression with n members has doubling constant (2n−1)/n, which stays below two as n grows. This is the sense in which its sumset has small growth.
In any abelian group, K(S)≥1: fixing s0​∈S puts a translated copy s0​+S inside S+S. The stronger integer bound gives K(S)≥2−1/∣S∣ only in that setting. A finite subgroup H has K(H)=1, as does every coset x+H, since its sumset is 2x+H. In particular H={0,2,4}⊆Z/6Z has doubling constant one.
Small doubling constant is useful because it constrains repeated growth, as in the Plünnecke-Ruzsa inequality. It signals structure such as arithmetic progressions and subgroups; precise descriptions depend on the ambient abelian group and are the subject of the Freiman-Ruzsa theorem. The additive energy bound below gives a simpler immediate consequence: E(S)≥∣S∣3/K(S).
