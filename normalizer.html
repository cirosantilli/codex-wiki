Normalizer (NG​(H))

 ... Area of mathematics Algebra Group theory Group action Conjugation action Centralizer and normalizer
OurBigBook.com Words: 473 Articles: 1
For a subgroup H of a group G, the normalizer is
NG​(H)={g∈G:gHg−1=H}.
(29)
It is the stabilizer subgroup of H under the conjugation action on subgroups. Its elements can permute members of H; they need not commute with them. For r=(123) in the symmetric group S3​, the cyclic subgroup H=⟨r⟩ has normalizer S3​, although the centralizer of r has only three elements, as illustrated in centralizer and normalizer.
Every element of H preserves H under conjugation, so H⊆NG​(H). Moreover, H is a normal subgroup of NG​(H), and this is the largest subgroup of G with that property. Indeed, any subgroup in which H is a normal subgroup consists entirely of elements preserving H under conjugation. In particular, NG​(H)=G exactly when H is a normal subgroup of G.[ref]
The orbit-stabilizer theorem counts distinct conjugate subsets of a subgroup by
#{gHg−1:g∈G}=[G:NG​(H)].
(30)
For H=⟨(123)⟩ in S3​, there is just one, since [S3​:NS3​​(H)]=6/6=1. For the subgroup K=⟨(12)⟩={e,(12)}, preserving K forces its unique nonidentity element to stay fixed. Hence NS3​​(K)=CS3​​((12))=K, and its three distinct conjugate subsets are {e,(12)}, {e,(13)} and {e,(23)}. This is the subgroup counterpart of counting a conjugacy class with a centralizer: the object being stabilized determines which denominator belongs in the count.[ref]
The equality in the definition is essential. For a subgroup H that is a finite group, an inclusion gHg−1⊆H already gives equality by equal cardinalities. For an infinite subgroup, that inference can fail: conjugation might carry it onto a proper subgroup. The normal subgroup criterion is therefore, with the explicit hypothesis H≤G,
H⊴G⟺NG​(H)=G.
(31)
No finiteness or commutativity assumption is needed. A normal subgroup is preserved as a set; its elements need not lie in the center of a group.
There is a second useful count, distinct from counting conjugate subgroups. The normalizer acts on H itself by group automorphisms, giving the group homomorphism
θ:NG​(H)⟶Aut(H),θ(g)(h)=ghg−1.
(32)
Its group kernel is CG​(H), so the centralizer of a subset is a normal subgroup of NG​(H) and the first isomorphism theorem gives
NG​(H)/CG​(H)≅imθ≤Aut(H).
(33)
This quotient group records the different rearrangements of H induced by the surrounding group. It gives a faithful group action on H, but need not be the entire automorphism group of H. For example, take the ambient group G=H=⟨(123)⟩. Since G is an abelian group, NG​(H)=CG​(H)=G, so the quotient group is trivial; nevertheless r↦r−1 is a nonidentity group automorphism of H. Returning to the ambient symmetric group S3​ makes that group automorphism available through a reflection, and NS3​​(H)/CS3​​(H)≅C2​. Its two cosets are the three rotations and the three reflections.
The subgroup H itself need not be contained in CG​(H): their intersection is the center of a group Z(H). It is contained precisely when H is an abelian group. Thus NG​(H)/H, which exists because H is a normal subgroup of its normalizer, and NG​(H)/CG​(H) answer different questions and should not be substituted for one another.
