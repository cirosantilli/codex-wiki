Six-vertex triangulation of the real projective plane (V=6,E=15,F=10,χ=1)

 ... Area of mathematics Geometry and topology Differential geometry Grassmannian Grassmannian as projection matrices Real projective plane
OurBigBook.com Words: 88
On vertices 1,…,6, take the simplicial complex generated by 123,124,135,146,156,236,245,256,345,346. Every edge occurs in two facets. The vertex links, listed cyclically, are 23564, 13654, 12645, 12536, 13426, and 14325, respectively. Thus each vertex link is a cycle graph, and the realization is a compact connected surface without boundary. Its Euler characteristic is 6−15+10=1, so the classification theorem for surfaces identifies it with the real projective plane. All fifteen possible edges occur, and the listed triangles are distinct three-element sets, so this is an honest abstract simplicial complex, not merely a polygon presentation.
