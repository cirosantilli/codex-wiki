Connected space

 Home Mathematics Area of mathematics Geometry and topology
OurBigBook.com Words: 3k Articles: 31
A topological space X is connected when it has no separation of a topological space: no partition of a set into two nonempty open subsets U,V satisfying
X=U∪V,U∩V=∅.
(30)
Here the union covers all of X, and the intersection is the empty set, so no point belongs to both pieces. For a subset X of a larger topological space, "open" means open in the subspace topology: each piece is the intersection of X with an ambient open set. Both pieces must be open; merely dividing a drawing into two colours does not establish a separation of a topological space. [ref]
For example, the closed interval I=[0,1] with its usual subspace topology from the real line is connected. The proposed split [0,1/2)∪[1/2,1] fails: the second subset is not open in I, since every neighbourhood of 1/2 in I contains points less than 1/2. More generally, a connected subset of the real line is precisely a real interval. In contrast, for the union
D=[−2,−1]∪[1,2],U=D∩(−3,0)=[−2,−1],V=D∩(0,3)=[1,2],
(31)
the open intervals (−3,0) and (0,3) exhibit U,V as nonempty open subsets in the subspace topology of D. Their intersection is empty and their union is D, so D is not connected. Although U,V are closed intervals in the real line, they are also open within D.
Why is connectedness useful? A continuous image of a connected space is connected: a proposed separation of a topological space in the image would pull back through the continuous map to a forbidden separation of a topological space in its source. Thus a continuous map from a real interval to the real line cannot skip values between two attained values. This is the intermediate value theorem. For a computed example, the continuous function f(x)=x2 on [0,1] has image [0,1]; the value 1/2 occurs at x=1/2​≈0.707107. Connectedness guarantees an input attaining that value even when we have no formula for it.
Path connectedness is a stronger condition: it supplies a continuous path between every pair of points. The topologist's sine curve below shows that a connected space need not be a path-connected space.
