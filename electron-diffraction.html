Electron diffraction

 ... Physics Branches of physics Quantum mechanics Quantum scattering Scattering amplitude Diffraction
OurBigBook.com Words: 873 Articles: 4
Electron diffraction is interference between scattered electron probability amplitudes. It connects the de Broglie wavelength of the incident beam to the arrangement of the scattering material. In elastic scattering from a fixed crystal, ∣kout​∣=∣kin​∣=2π/λ, and constructive crystal scattering selects wavevector transfers Q=kout​−kin​ in the reciprocal lattice. The physical momentum transfer to the electron is ℏQ in this convention.
Bragg's law expresses the same condition as 2dsinθB​=nλ, where d is the spacing of the chosen lattice planes, n the diffraction order, and θB​ the angle to those planes. The angle between incoming and outgoing propagation directions is χ=2θB​ for that reflection geometry. In normal-incidence backscattering, a detector's angle ϕ from the outward surface normal instead gives χ=π−ϕ; neither detector colatitude nor angle to the surface normal should silently replace θB​. A finite-row electron diffraction model uses a different spacing, the spacing between sites along a surface row, and makes that distinction explicit.
