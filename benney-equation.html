Benney equation (ht​+∂x​q[h]=0)

 Home Physics Branches of physics Fluid mechanics Viscous fluid flow Falling film flow
OurBigBook.com Words: 83
The Benney equation is a long-wave approximation for a gravity-driven thin film that expresses its volume flux per unit width locally in terms of height and its derivatives. For surface-velocity scaling on a plane inclined by α, one normalization is
q[h]=32​h3+ε[−32​h3hx​cotα+Sh3hxxx​+158​Reh6hx​].
(7)
Here ε is the film aspect ratio, Re the thickness-based Reynolds number, and S the capillary parameter for a falling film. The leading velocity is parabolic. Its first inertial correction uses the leading mass conservation equation to eliminate the time derivative of the height. This local closure requires the correction to remain small; it does not describe arbitrary large-amplitude short-wave dynamics.
