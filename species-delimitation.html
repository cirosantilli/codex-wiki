Species delimitation

 Home Biology Taxonomy (biology) Species
OurBigBook.com Words: 233
Species delimitation infers the boundaries and number of species from evidence. No single arbitrary percentage of nucleotide sequence difference is a universal boundary. Divergence within an old, widespread species can exceed divergence between recently separated species; geography and multiple kinds of evidence matter. [ref]
An original counterexample to a universal percentage rule: an assay could examine a strongly conserved gene in one comparison and a rapidly changing nucleotide sequence in another. Equal percentages then answer different historical questions. Even the denominator needs specifying: all sites, aligned sites, or only a filtered region? A threshold is an operational rule for a stated assay and group of organisms, whose errors need evaluating. It is not a definition that automatically transfers to every species.
The diagram's hypothetical pair B has similar morphology but reduced mating and distinct ancestry evidence. A morphological species concept could miss that distinction. Pair C instead has different morphology but compatible mating and ancestry that fails to separate the samples. That combination calls for checking variation in phenotype within a species. Pair A has only a structural difference observed: the other criteria remain untested. These examples show why missing evidence, disagreeing evidence and agreeing evidence require different interpretations.
