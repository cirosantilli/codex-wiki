Two-state ion-channel gating (X˙=αX​(V)(1−X)−βX​(V)X)

OurBigBook.com Words: 884 Articles: 2
Two-state ion-channel gating models an individual gate as a random switch between closed and open states. Write p(t) for its open probability; this is the X in the header equation. An individual gate's state is always closed or open, rather than partly open. A population of identical gates can have a fraction open near p(t), and averaging repeated identically prepared trials estimates the same probability. A finite population's observed fraction fluctuates. See Purves and colleagues, Neuroscience, 2nd edition (2001): Ion Channels Underlying Action Potentials for single-channel steps and their population averages.
At fixed membrane potential V, let constant rates α,β>0 govern closed-to-open and open-to-closed transitions respectively, in s−1. This is a two-state continuous-time Markov chain. Over a short interval Δt, an initially closed gate opens with probability αΔt+o(Δt), and an initially open gate closes with probability βΔt+o(Δt). Gain minus loss gives the deterministic ordinary differential equation for the probability:
dtdp​=α(1−p)−βp=τp∞​−p​,p∞​=α+βα​,τ=α+β1​.
(13)
Here p∞​ is the fixed-voltage limiting open probability, and τ is a time constant. The ratio of the rates fixes the limit, while their sum fixes the speed. With initial probability p(0)=p0​∈[0,1],
p(t)=p∞​+(p0​−p∞​)e−t/τ.
(14)
For a short derivation, subtract p∞​: the deviation obeys d(p−p∞​)/dt=−(p−p∞​)/τ, yielding the exponential function. After one time constant, 1/e of the initial deviation remains. The solution stays between p0​ and p∞​, so it remains a valid probability. The rate and time constant formulations are described in Gerstner and colleagues, Neuronal Dynamics, §2.2.2: transition rates and channel kinetics.
For a worked toy condition A, choose α=β=50s−1 and p0​=0.1. Then p∞​=0.5, τ=0.01s=10ms, and
p(10ms)=0.5−0.4e−1≈0.352848.
(15)
In a separately prepared condition B, choose α=200s−1, β=50s−1 and the same p0​=0.1. Now p∞​=0.8, τ=4ms, and p(10ms)=0.8−0.7e−2.5≈0.742540. Condition B approaches a higher limit more quickly. These are two stipulated constant-rate conditions with V held fixed in each experiment, not an empirical function relating rates to voltage or a sequential switch between conditions. The initial p0​=0.1 need not be the limiting probability at the newly imposed voltage.
In the Hodgkin-Huxley model, the rates depend on membrane potential: αX​(V) and βX​(V). Its X∞​(V)=αX​(V)/(αX​(V)+βX​(V)) is the limit for voltage held at that V. During an action potential, changing V changes the rates and gates feed back onto V through ionic electric currents; substituting the present voltage into the constant-rate solution does not solve that coupled system. Gating describes availability of a conducting pathway. Even an open ion channel carries zero net ionic electric current at its reversal potential.
The useful bridge is from abrupt random molecular events to a smooth population prediction. A channel requiring several independent permissive gates in an ion channel has an open probability equal to their product; this can make channel availability much smaller than each gate's availability. The Hodgkin-Huxley model uses gˉ​Na​m3h and gˉ​K​n4 for sodium and potassium electrical conductances, respectively.
