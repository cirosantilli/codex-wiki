Cauchy sequence (∀ε>0∃N∀m,n≥N: d(xm​,xn​)<ε)

 Home Mathematics Area of mathematics Analysis Real analysis Sequence and series
OurBigBook.com Words: 1k Articles: 7
A Cauchy sequence is a sequence whose sufficiently late terms are all close to one another. In a metric space (X,d), the precise condition is
∀ε>0∃N∈N>0​∀m,n≥N: d(xm​,xn​)<ε.
(93)
For real numbers with their usual metric, d(xm​,xn​)=∣xm​−xn​∣. The useful feature is that the definition compares computed terms to other computed terms: it does not require an already known limit of a sequence. The one index N must control every pair of later indices, not merely successive terms. [ref].
For an explicit example, take qn​=1−2−n for n≥1. Its tail of a sequence beginning at N lies in [1−2−N,1), and for every m,n≥N,
∣qm​−qn​∣=∣2−n−2−m∣<2−N.
(94)
Choose N with 2−N<ε to certify the entire future sequence. For ε=0.1, N=4 works because 2−4=0.0625. The limit of a sequence is one, but the pairwise bound establishes the Cauchy sequence property without first needing that answer. By contrast, an​=(−1)n is not a Cauchy sequence: arbitrarily late even integers and odd integers give values separated by two. [ref].
Tiny successive changes do not provide this guarantee. For the harmonic numbers Hn​=∑j=1n​1/j, the successive difference Hn+1​−Hn​=1/(n+1) tends to zero, but
H2n​−Hn​=∑j=n+12n​j1​≥n2n1​=21​.
(95)
Thus, beyond every proposed N, choosing n≥N and m=2n defeats the fixed tolerance ε=1/2. Even every fixed positive lag h passes the weaker test: 0<Hn+h​−Hn​≤h/(n+1)→0. The failing lag grows with n, as explained more generally by convergence from arbitrary variable-lag increments. [ref].
Every convergent sequence in a metric space is a Cauchy sequence: apply the triangle inequality to two late terms and their common limit of a sequence. The reverse implication requires completeness. It holds in the real numbers by completeness of the real numbers, but can fail in the rational numbers: the rational endpoints from the bisection method approaching 2​ are a Cauchy sequence with no rational limit of a sequence. The existing construction in completeness of the real numbers shows how this happens. A Cauchy sequence is always a bounded sequence; in R, boundedness gives a convergent subsequence by the Bolzano-Weierstrass theorem, and the pairwise condition forces the whole sequence to share its limit. [ref].
