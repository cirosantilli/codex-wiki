Isothermal process

 ... Branches of physics Statistical physics Thermodynamics Second law of thermodynamics Carnot's theorem Carnot cycle
OurBigBook.com Words: 2k Articles: 12
An isothermal process changes a thermodynamic system while keeping its temperature constant. It need not keep its pressure, volume or internal energy constant, and it does not generally have zero heat transfer. An adiabatic process specifies zero heat transfer instead. For example, a slowly expanding ideal gas can absorb heat from a heat bath and deliver the same amount of work while its temperature stays fixed.
For a fixed amount of substance and composition, internal energy of an ideal gas depends only on temperature. With Q>0 for heat entering and Wby​>0 for work delivered to the surroundings, the first law of thermodynamics therefore gives
ΔU=Q−Wby​=0,Q=Wby​.
(7)
This equality does not determine either transfer until the path is specified. Isothermal ideal-gas work gives the reversible value; an insulated free expansion has a different value even when its equilibrium endpoints have the same temperature. The closed system convention here excludes changes in bulk kinetic energy and external potential energy.
A conducting cylinder in a large, stirred heat bath approximates an isothermal process when the piston moves slowly enough for thermal conduction to keep up. A quasistatic process keeps the gas close to internal thermodynamic equilibrium; a reversible thermodynamic process additionally requires negligible friction and vanishing driving differences in pressure and temperature. Exact equality of the gas and heat bath temperatures gives no finite heat transfer rate through a finite thermal resistance. The reversible description is a limit of arbitrarily small differences and arbitrarily slow motion, illustrated by the finite-rate isothermal piston model.[ref]
For a real gas, internal energy at constant temperature can change with volume because molecular interaction potential energy changes. In one smooth thermodynamic phase of a fixed-composition simple compressible system,
(∂V∂U​)T,n​=T(∂T∂p​)V,n​−p.
(8)
Thus an isothermal process generally has Q=ΔU+Wby​, with no reason to set ΔU=0. The right-hand side vanishes for the ideal gas law, but not generally for another equation of state.[ref]
