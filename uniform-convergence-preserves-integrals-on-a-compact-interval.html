Uniform convergence preserves integrals on a compact interval (​∫ab​(fn​−f)​≤(b−a)∥fn​−f∥∞​)

 ... Mathematics Area of mathematics Analysis Real analysis Sequence and series Uniform convergence
OurBigBook.com Words: 217
If Riemann integrable functions fn​:[a,b]→R converge by uniform convergence to f, then f is Riemann integrable and
​∫ab​fn​(x)dx−∫ab​f(x)dx​≤(b−a)∥fn​−f∥∞​⟶0.
(76)
This concerns one fixed compact interval, with finite length.[ref]
Here is a proof outline that also establishes Riemann integrability of the uniform limit. Assume a<b, and put ℓ=b−a and EN​=∥fN​−f∥∞​. One bounded fN​ and finite EN​ show that f is a bounded function. For any partition of an interval P, its upper Darboux sum and lower Darboux sum satisfy
U(f,P)−L(f,P)≤U(fN​,P)−L(fN​,P)+2ℓEN​.
(77)
Given η>0, choose N with EN​<η/(4ℓ), then a partition of an interval with U(fN​,P)−L(fN​,P)<η/2. The displayed gap is below η, proving Riemann integrability. Finally, ∣fn​−f∣≤En​ and monotonicity of the Riemann integral give the stated error estimate. When a=b, every integral is zero.
For the polynomial functions fn​(x)=xn on [0,0.8], direct integration and the uniform bound give
∫00.8​xndx=n+10.8n+1​≤0.8⋅0.8n⟶0.
(78)
At n=21, the exact integral is approximately 0.0003353953, while the general error bound is approximately 0.0073786976. The bound certifies the result without an exact antiderivative. On [0,1], these same integrals still tend to zero, although there is no uniform convergence: sufficient conditions need not be necessary. On an unbounded interval, even uniform convergence can fail to control total area, as uniformly vanishing functions can retain a nonzero integral shows.
Uniform convergence alone also fails to preserve derivatives: fn​(x)=sin(n2x)/n converges uniformly to zero on [−1,1], while fn′​(0)=n. The small amplitude does not control the slopes of the sine oscillations.
