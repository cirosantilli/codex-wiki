Central contribution to kurtosis (Ch​=E[Z41{∣Z∣≤h}​])

For a standard score Z and h>0, split kurtosis into the contribution Ch​ from within h standard deviations of the mean and the contribution Th​ from outside:
κ=Ch​+Th​,Ch​=E[Z41{∣Z∣≤h}​],0≤Ch​≤h4.
(6)
The indicator function selects the central outcomes. The bound follows directly from Z4≤h4 there. Thus Ch​/κ≤h4/κ: when kurtosis is large, any fixed central range contributes a small fraction. This is a precise reason to emphasize extreme standard scores rather than peak height.[ref]
For the rare-outlier three-point distribution with q=1/100, C1​=0 and T1​=100. At the other endpoint q=1, all probability mass lies at Z=±1, so C1​=κ=1: central dominance is possible when kurtosis is small. More generally, the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality gives κ=E[Z4]≥(E[Z2])2=1; the equally weighted outcomes ±1 attain this minimum.
