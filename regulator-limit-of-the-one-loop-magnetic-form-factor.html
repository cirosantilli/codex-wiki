Regulator limit of the one-loop magnetic form factor (limr→0+​I(r)=1)

In Feynman gauge, use the auxiliary photon propagator −igμν​/(k2−λ2+i0) and r=λ/m>0. The projected one-loop Feynman parameter integral is
F2(1)​(0;λ)=2πα​I(r),I(r)=∫01​(1−x)2+xr22x(1−x)2​dx.
(34)
Here x is the source's photon parameter z.[ref]
To check the reduction, use N2​=4z(1−z)m2 and Δ′=(1−z)2m2+zλ2. The three-parameter expression has prefactor e2/(16π2) multiplying N2​/Δ′. At fixed z, ∫01−z​du=1−z, giving e2/(4π2)∫01​z(1−z)2/[(1−z)2+zr2]dz. Substituting e2=4πα gives exactly the displayed integral. This starts from the projected QED numerator; it is not a derivation of the whole QED calculation from an arbitrarily chosen integral.
The regulator can then be removed by a short independent argument. Write its integrand as fr​(x). For 0≤x<1, fr​(x)→2x, and
0≤fr​(x)=2x(1−x)2+xr2(1−x)2​≤2x,∫01​2xdx=1.
(35)
The dominated convergence theorem therefore gives I(r)→1 and F2(1)​(0)=α/(2π). At x=1 the regulated integrand is zero, whereas the limiting function can be assigned value 2; one point has zero measure and changes no integral. For every r>0, 0<I(r)<1, and decreasing r increases the integral. The visible suppression near x=1 narrows as the regulator shrinks; it is not a uniform pointwise approximation at that endpoint.
This auxiliary mass regulates an infrared divergence, not an ultraviolet divergence; the finite-mass prescription alone is not fully gauge-invariant.[ref]
The ultraviolet problem belongs to the full QED amplitude. Electron self-energy, wave-function renormalization and the charge vertex require consistent counterterms and renormalization conditions. The Ward identity for QED renormalization constants ties Z1​ to Z2​, rather than allowing the vertex and external electron normalization to be fitted independently. Minimal renormalizable QED has no independent Pauli term to adjust this finite one-loop F2​(0): its dimension-five coefficient is predicted from the mass and electric charge inputs. Adding a free Pauli term would define a different theory with an extra parameter. Finiteness of the reduced integral alone proves neither all-order renormalizability nor finiteness of every QED observable.
