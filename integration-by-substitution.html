Integration by substitution (∫f(g(x))g′(x)dx=F(g(x))+C)

 ... Mathematics Area of mathematics Analysis Real analysis Calculus Integral
OurBigBook.com Words: 167 Articles: 1
If F′=f and g is differentiable, the chain rule gives (F∘g)′(x)=f(g(x))g′(x). Taking antiderivatives gives the substitution rule
∫f(g(x))g′(x)dx=F(g(x))+C.​
(4)
Writing u=g(x) and du=g′(x)dx records this chain rule in reverse. For a continuous function f and continuously differentiable g, the fundamental theorem of calculus gives the corresponding definite integral identity
∫ab​f(g(x))g′(x)dx=∫g(a)g(b)​f(u)du.
(5)
The order of the transformed endpoints determines the sign. For example, ∫01​2xcos(x2)dx=∫01​cosudu=sin1. If the substitution is used in the opposite direction by solving x as a function of u, one must restrict to a suitable inverse branch; the tangent half-angle substitution illustrates why separate branches can be necessary even when the original integrand is smooth. The general change of variables formula extends substitution to higher dimensions.
