Leonhard Euler's Elementa doctrinae solidorum, or Elements of the doctrine of solids, appeared in 1758 in volume 4 of Novi Commentarii academiae scientiarum Petropolitanae, pp. 109–140. The repository record dates its composition to 1750. Composition and publication are distinct dates here.[ref]
The modern cell-count formula for Euler characteristic puts polyhedral counting into an arbitrary-dimensional CW complex. It also identifies what is being counted: the Euler characteristic of a cube boundary and its interior distinguishes a polyhedral surface from the filled solid using the missing three-dimensional term. The modern homology formulation explains why this alternating arithmetic persists across different decompositions.[ref]
