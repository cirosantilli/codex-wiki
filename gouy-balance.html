Gouy balance

 Home Physics Branches of physics Statistical physics Magnetic susceptibility
OurBigBook.com Words: 341
A Gouy balance detects the force exerted by a nonuniform magnetic field on a sample of uniform cross-section extending between high- and low-field regions. TeachSpin's implementation reverses the usual arrangement: a rare-earth permanent-magnet assembly sits on a one-pan electronic balance, while a separate holder brings the sample into the field. The magnet's apparent mass change measures the opposite of the sample's vertical force, by Newton's third law. The described pole faces have diameter 25mm and separation 15mm; samples occupy cuvettes of cross-section (10mm)2 and height about 32mm. [ref]
The same balance calibrates magnetic flux density by measuring the reaction force on the magnet when a wire of known horizontal length L carries known electric current i perpendicular to the field: ∣Fz​∣=iLB0​. The manufacturer describes water being repelled by the high-field region, identifying negative magnetic susceptibility. This observed sign supplies a qualitative test, not a measured Curie's law curve. [ref]
For weak reversible linear response, a uniform sample displacing a weakly magnetic surrounding medium has, to leading order, downward force
F↓​≃2μ0​A(χv​−χmedium​)​(B0,bottom2​−B0,top2​).
(68)
This follows by integrating the induced-dipole force density (χv​−χmedium​)∂z​(B02​)/(2μ0​) along a downward coordinate. A is the cross-sectional area, and the two B0​ values are applied magnetic flux densities. The force depends on the endpoint fields; the entire field-gradient profile need not be measured under these assumptions. In the reversed apparatus, F↓​=−gΔmmagnet​ after subtracting the holder background, where g is gravitational acceleration. This sign must be reversed before inferring volume magnetic susceptibility.
Useful controls are an empty-holder reading at the same position, a distant-sample baseline, repeated wire-current reversals during field calibration, and stable temperature. They separate holder response, balance drift, force offsets, and thermal changes; these are proposed checks for the stated inference. Finite endpoint fields, nonuniform cross-section, uneven powder packing, stray forces and a demagnetizing field limit the simple formula. If magnetization is nonlinear, the weak-response force involves A∫B0,top​B0,bottom​​M(B0​)dB0​ instead of a constant-χv​ endpoint square. A balance reading then measures an integrated response, not directly the local differential magnetic susceptibility.
