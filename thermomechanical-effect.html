Thermomechanical effect (dP=ρsdT)

 ... Branches of physics Statistical physics Superfluidity Superfluid Normal component of a superfluid Two-fluid model of superfluidity
OurBigBook.com Words: 230 Articles: 1
The thermomechanical effect couples a temperature difference to a pressure difference across a superleak. For the closed-chamber model in the apparatus figure, consider neighboring equilibrium states with small velocities, negligible height difference and pure helium-4. Let μm​ be chemical potential per unit mass, in Jkg−1. The Gibbs free energy differential per unit mass gives
dμm​=−sdT+ρ1​dP.
(17)
The superleak allows the superfluid to redistribute until the two sides have equal μm​. Setting dμm​=0 therefore gives
dTdP​​μm​​=ρs>0,Ph​−Pc​=∫Tc​Th​​ρ(T,P(T))s(T,P(T))dT.
(18)
The sign matters: heating one side at initially equal pressure lowers its μm​; inflow raises its pressure until chemical balance is restored. The hotter side has the higher pressure. The integral follows the constant-μm​ path; it need not be evaluated at fixed pressure when material properties vary appreciably.
For an original local example, stipulate constant ρ=150kgm−3, s=100Jkg−1K−1 and Th​−Tc​=0.020K. The predicted pressure difference is 300Pa. These are teaching assumptions, not helium measurements. This static thermodynamics relation predicts neither the required heater power nor the filling time: those require heat leakage, channel conductance and chamber heat capacity. An outlet would introduce kinetic energy and a flowing balance, so the static 300Pa is not a predicted fountain height.
