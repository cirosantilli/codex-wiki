Chebyshev polynomial

 Home Mathematics Area of mathematics Analysis Numerical analysis
OurBigBook.com Words: 804 Articles: 10
The first-kind Chebyshev polynomial Tn​ converts a multiple-angle cosine into a polynomial:
Tn​(cosθ)=cos(nθ),0≤θ≤π,n≥0.
(196)
The cosine addition formula gives the recurrence relation
T0​(x)=1,T1​(x)=x,Tn+1​(x)=2xTn​(x)−Tn−1​(x)(n≥1).
(197)
This constructs polynomials valid for every real x, including outside [−1,1], where the real-angle description is unavailable. For example,
T2​(x)=2x2−1,T3​(x)=4x3−3x,T4​(x)=8x4−8x2+1.
(198)
For n≥1, Tn​ has polynomial degree n and leading coefficient 2n−1. On [−1,1] its uniform norm is one, even as its oscillations become more numerous.[ref][ref]
The polynomial roots of Tn​ are especially simple:
xk​=cos(2n(2k−1)π​),k=1,…,n.
(199)
They correspond to equally spaced angles, but their locations in x cluster near −1 and 1. For T3​, the polynomial roots are 3​/2,0,−3​/2. For T8​, the positive polynomial roots are approximately 0.980785,0.831470,0.555570,0.195090, with their negatives completing the set. The distance from the right endpoint to the nearest polynomial root is 1−cos(π/(2n))∼π2/(8n2); central neighboring polynomial roots are separated on the larger scale 1/n. The small change of cosine near angles zero and π explains the clustering.
These polynomial roots supply n interpolation nodes for a polynomial interpolation of polynomial degree at most n−1. They differ from the n+1 Chebyshev-Lobatto nodes cos(jπ/n), which include both endpoints. This choice of polynomial roots has nodal polynomial
∏k=1n​(x−xk​)=21−nTn​(x).
(200)
The monic Chebyshev extremal polynomial minimizes the uniform norm of this product among monic polynomials of polynomial degree n on [−1,1]. Consequently, if f has n continuous derivatives, the usual polynomial interpolation error gives the useful bound
∥f−p∥∞​≤n!21−n​max−1≤x≤1​∣f(n)(x)∣,degp≤n−1.
(201)
This optimizes the nodal factor in that bound, not the actual error for every function. Endpoint clustering helps control the large endpoint oscillations of equally spaced interpolation associated with Runge's phenomenon. The two node conventions and their geometric construction are discussed in Trefethen's authored text.[ref]
The same cosine substitution for polynomial approximation explains the weighted orthogonality:
∫−11​1−x2​Tm​(x)Tn​(x)​dx=∫0π​cos(mθ)cos(nθ)dθ=⎩⎨⎧​0,π,π/2,​m=n,m=n=0,m=n≥1.​
(202)
The endpoint singularities of the weight are integrable. Thus the Chebyshev polynomials are orthogonal polynomials for this weight, making coefficients in a least-squares polynomial in an orthogonal-polynomial basis separate cleanly. Their polynomial roots also give Chebyshev–Gauss quadrature, connecting polynomial interpolation to Gaussian quadrature.[ref]
Accuracy still depends on the target function. The absolute value function ∣x∣ has a corner at zero, so the hypothesis requiring continuous derivatives in the displayed polynomial interpolation error bound fails. Endpoint clustering cannot remove that corner. A jump poses a stronger obstruction: polynomials are continuous functions, so they cannot converge in the uniform norm to a discontinuous function on the whole interval. Chebfun's documented examples show persistent overshoot for such jumps; these are a limitation of global polynomial interpolation, not a universal promise of rapid accuracy from Chebyshev polynomials.[ref]
