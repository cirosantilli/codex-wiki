Arc length (L(γ))

The arc length of a piecewise smooth curve γ:[a,b]→M on a Riemannian manifold is the integral of its speed:

An odometer measures this accumulated travel rather than displacement between the endpoints. In Euclidean space, the Riemannian metric is the ordinary inner product, so the formula becomes ∫ab​∑i​γ˙​i​(t)2​dt.
For a circle of radius R traversed through an angle α, use γ(t)=(Rcos(αt),Rsin(αt)), 0≤t≤1. Its speed is R∣α∣, hence its arc length is R∣α∣. Traversing a full circle twice gives 4πR, even though the starting and ending points coincide. The same formula measures the two great circle arcs pictured in geodesic.
The curve parameter controls how fast the journey happens, not its arc length. A regular increasing or decreasing reparametrization preserves the integral by change of variables; reversing direction leaves the speed norm unchanged. Retracing part of a route does add length, because it changes the actual journey.
