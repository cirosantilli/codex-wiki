Local influence versus replacement breakdown

 ... Mathematics Area of mathematics Probability and statistics Statistical inference Robust statistics Influence function
OurBigBook.com Words: 244
The influence function describes an infinitesimal change at a specified reference probability distribution. The replacement breakdown point asks how many observations in a particular finite sample can be replaced before an estimator becomes unbounded. The finite-sample maximum bias records the intervening changes. These are different questions: a bounded influence function at one baseline is not a general bound over all epsilon-contamination neighborhoods, nor a proof of a positive replacement breakdown point.[ref]
The mean illustrates agreement between local and global fragility: its gross-error sensitivity is infinite and one replacement can send the sample mean arbitrarily far. The median has finite gross-error sensitivity under the stated positive-density assumptions, while its sample median withstands fewer than about half the observations being replaced under ordinary tie conventions. Its population contamination bias above still diverges as ε↑1/2 when the atom remains beyond the shifted median. Here x must also move outward; taking that limit with a fixed finite atom would invalidate the displayed quantile formula.
For scalar location with a fixed positive scale, the Huber score has a direct global argument: if strictly more than half of the sample stays fixed, then beyond every retained observation by more than ks, their saturated scores outweigh all possible replaced scores. Every root therefore stays in a bounded interval. This is a separate finite-replacement argument; estimating scale changes its premises. A smooth bounded influence function is valuable local protection, but global robustness also depends on the full estimating rule, scale choice, and root selection.
