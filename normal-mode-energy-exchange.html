Normal-mode energy exchange (KE=Esin2(ωt),PE=Ecos2(ωt))

For the undamped first normal mode of a fixed string, start at maximum displacement with u(x,t)=Asin(πx/L)cos(ω1​t). In the small-slope wave equation on a string, its kinetic energy and excess potential energy are
KE(t)=2μ​∫0L​ut2​dx,PE(t)=2τ​∫0L​ux2​dx.
(55)
The potential energy comes from the extra length under tension: 1+ux2​​−1≃ux2​/2. It depends on slope, so a node of a standing wave need not have zero local potential energy density. These are physical energies, with the mass and tension factors restored to the normalized wave energy. [ref]
Integrating sin2(πx/L) and cos2(πx/L) over the string gives L/2 in both cases. Using μω12​=τπ2/L2 yields
E=4LτA2π2​,KE(t)=Esin2(ω1​t),PE(t)=Ecos2(ω1​t),KE+PE=E.
(56)
For the model μ=1kgm−1, τ=1N, L=1m, and A=0.010m, the angular frequency is ω1​=πrads−1, the frequency is 0.5Hz, and E≃2.4674×10−4J. The maximum slope Aπ/L≃0.0314 is small. At t=0 all the energy is potential energy; at t=T1​/4=0.5s the string is straight and all the energy is kinetic energy. At t=T1​/2=1s the displacement is reversed and all the energy is again potential energy. The energy curves repeat twice during one displacement cycle, while conservation of energy keeps their sum constant.
