Stability of a supremum under uniform perturbations (∣supX​f−supX​g∣≤∥f−g∥∞​)

 ... Mathematics Area of mathematics Analysis Real analysis Infimum and supremum Supremum
OurBigBook.com Words: 361
Suppose f,g:X→R are bounded functions on the same nonempty set, and the supremum norm of their difference is at most δ. Then their optimal values differ by at most the same amount:
​x∈Xsup​f(x)−x∈Xsup​g(x)​≤∥f−g∥∞​≤δ.​
(14)
Indeed, f(x)≤g(x)+δ≤supX​g+δ for every x, so supX​f≤supX​g+δ; exchange f and g for the other direction. Adding a constant shifts a supremum by that constant, a basic compatibility between upper bounds and addition.[ref]
For a chosen example on [−1,1], take f(x)=−x2 and g(x)=−x2+0.2x. Their supremum error is 0.2, since ∣g(x)−f(x)∣=0.2∣x∣. Completing the square gives
g(x)=−(x−0.1)2+0.01.
(15)
Thus f has maximum zero at x=0, while g has maximum 0.01 at x=0.1. The change in optimal value, 0.01, is smaller than the certified bound 0.2. For the vertical translation h(x)=f(x)+0.2, the bound is exact: its supremum error and optimal-value change are both 0.2.
A stable optimal value does not guarantee a stable maximizing input. On [−1,1], the two linear functions fε​(x)=εx and gε​(x)=−εx satisfy
∥fε​−gε​∥∞​=2ε⟶0,supfε​=supgε​=ε.
(16)
For every positive ε, their unique maximizing inputs remain two units apart, at 1 and −1. Both functions approach the flat zero function, which has many maximizing inputs. Consequently an accurate objective function value is not by itself a certificate that the optimizing decision is accurate.
The estimate also gives a convenient consequence of uniform convergence. If fn​ converges uniformly to a bounded function f on a nonempty X, then supX​fn​→supX​f for the bounded tail of the sequence. Uniform control handles the changing input that defines the best value. Pointwise convergence alone supplies no such control; the existing examples under uniform convergence illustrate that distinction.
