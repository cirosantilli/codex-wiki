Information entropy dominates min-entropy

 ... Mathematics Area of mathematics Probability and statistics Information theory Information entropy Min-entropy
OurBigBook.com Words: 389
For a discrete random variable X with probability mass function px​, using base-two logarithms for both Shannon entropy and min-entropy,
H(X)≥H∞​(X)≥0.
(31)
The first inequality says that average information content cannot be below the least information content of a possible outcome. For a finite alphabet, equality H(X)=H∞​(X) holds exactly when the probability mass function is uniform on its positive-probability outcomes. Impossible outcomes do not need to share that positive probability. In particular, (1/2,1/2,0) has both quantities equal to 1 bit, while (1,0,0) has both zero. The same equality characterization holds for a countable alphabet: equality forces only finitely many positive-probability outcomes, because all must have the same positive mass. The uniform maximum of Shannon entropy is discussed in Shannon's original treatment.[ref]
For the proof, 0<px​≤pmax​ gives −log2​px​≥−log2​pmax​; taking the expected value proves information entropy dominates min-entropy. More precisely, the nonnegative gap is
H(X)−H∞​(X)=∑x:px​>0​px​log2​px​pmax​​≥0.
(32)
It vanishes precisely when every positive term vanishes, namely px​=pmax​ for every possible outcome. With m such outcomes, normalization forces pmax​=1/m. If Shannon entropy is infinite, the inequality still holds and is strict, because min-entropy of a countable probability mass function is finite. No subtraction of two infinite quantities is needed.
An analytical family makes the gap visible. Give A probability q and each of B,C,D,E probability (1−q)/4, with 1/5≤q≤1. Then A is a most likely outcome, so its probability is also the best single-guess success probability, and
H∞​(q)=−log2​q,H(q)=h2​(q)+2(1−q),
(33)
where h2​ is binary entropy. The entropy bound with one prescribed probability explains the second expression: it separates whether A occurred from which of four equally likely alternatives occurred. At q=1/5, all five outcomes are uniform and both quantities are log2​5≈2.3219 bits. At q=1/2, Shannon entropy is 2 bits while min-entropy is 1 bit, reproducing the biased example in min-entropy. At q=1, both are zero; the four impossible alternatives contribute nothing. For every 1/5<q<1, the probability mass function is nonuniform on its possible outcomes and the gap is strictly positive.
For finite Shannon entropy, exponentiating the inequality gives the useful but potentially loose bound pguess​(X)≥2−H(X). Equality holds exactly for a discrete uniform distribution on the possible outcomes. Thus Shannon entropy alone supplies a lower bound on optimal success, not the upper bound needed to promise that every guess is unlikely. Shannon's source coding theorem concerns average compression under its hypotheses, while min-entropy answers the single-guess question directly. The two quantities serve different tasks even when both are expressed in bits.
