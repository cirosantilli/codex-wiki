Riemannian metric (g)

 Home Mathematics Area of mathematics Geometry and topology Differential geometry
OurBigBook.com Words: 2k Articles: 30
A Riemannian metric is a smoothly varying positive-definite inner product gp​ on each tangent space Tp​M of a smooth manifold. It measures tangent vectors before measuring entire journeys:
∥v∥g​=gp​(v,v)​,cosθ=∥u∥g​∥v∥g​gp​(u,v)​(u,v=0).
(1)
Integrating this speed along a curve gives arc length; taking the infimum over routes gives Riemannian distance. A smooth manifold together with this measuring rule is a Riemannian manifold.
In a coordinate chart (x1,…,xn), the rule is represented by a smooth positive-definite symmetric matrix G=(gij​):
ds2=∑i,j​gij​(x)dxidxj,g(v,v)=∑i,j​gij​(x)vivj.
(2)
The notation ds2 describes the squared infinitesimal line element, rather than an ordinary distance between distinct points. Changing coordinates changes the matrix entries; it preserves the measured norm of the same tangent vector.
For the plane with ds2=4dx2+dy2, the vector (1,0) has norm 2 and (0,1) has norm 1. The coordinate change X=2x,Y=y turns this into ds2=dX2+dY2: coefficients that look different can describe the same geometry. On a circular cylinder the induced metric R2dθ2+dz2 similarly becomes Euclidean when unrolled. On a round sphere, polar coordinates give R2dϕ2+R2sin2ϕdθ2, away from the poles; the apparent degeneracy at a pole belongs to this coordinate choice, not to the Riemannian metric.
The point of the construction is that measurements make sense intrinsically, even without a chosen surrounding Euclidean space. It gives a common starting point for geodesics, curvature encoded by the Riemann curvature tensor, and the Laplace-Beltrami operator. [ref].
