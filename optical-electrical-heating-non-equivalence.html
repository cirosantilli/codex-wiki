Optical-electrical heating non-equivalence

 Home Physics Branches of physics Optics Radiometry Electrical substitution radiometer
OurBigBook.com Words: 138
Optical-electrical heating non-equivalence means that equal absorbed optical and electrical powers do not necessarily give identical thermometer responses. Light and the heater may deposit heat in different places, producing different temperature gradients and different routes of thermal conduction to the support. Holding one thermometer reading fixed need not hold every part of the cavity at the same temperature. This differs from incomplete radiometric absorptance: one concerns where absorbed heat goes, the other how much incident light becomes absorbed heat.
A useful accounting convention is
Pin​=aΔPh​+ΔQout​−ΔPpar​+δne​​,
(33)
where the signed correction δne​ is determined by characterization of the instrument and represents the residual departure from the lumped electrical substitution heat balance. It is not a universal extra source of energy. Varying the beam location or heater location, comparing equivalent temperature excursions, and checking repeatable dark returns can test the assumptions; no single small correction proves that every other correction vanishes.
