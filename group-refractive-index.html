The group refractive index is ng​=c/vg​ for a transparent isotropic propagation branch with forward group velocity vg​=dω/dk>0. Differentiating the local wavenumber k=n(ω)ω/c gives
ng​=n+ω∂ω∂n​=n−λ0​∂λ0​∂n​,τg​=∂ω∂ϕprop​​=c1​∫γ​ng​(r,ω)ds.

The last formula gives the propagation group delay along a path whose geometry is fixed as frequency varies; additional apparatus wave phases require their own frequency derivatives. For a narrow wave packet with little loss and negligible reshaping, it approximates the transit of its wave envelope. In the nondispersive case ∂ω​n=0, it reduces to OPL/c. An optical path length at one frequency contains no information about ∂ω​n, so in a medium with optical dispersion it cannot by itself determine the group delay. [ref]
For an original local model near ω∗​, choose two equal geometric paths of length L, with nA​(ω)=3/2 and nB​(ω)=3/2+(ω−ω∗​)/(10ω∗​). At ω∗​ both optical path lengths equal 3L/2, and matched launch and interface references give equal carrier wave phases. But ng,A​=3/2 and ng,B​=8/5, so their group delays differ by L/(10c). This local stipulated model is a counterexample to identifying equal wave phase with equal pulse arrival; it asserts no material response outside the narrow band considered. Strong loss or pulse reshaping requires a fuller propagation model, and group delay need not time the earliest causal response.
