Streakline

 Home Physics Branches of physics Fluid mechanics
OurBigBook.com Words: 682
A streakline at observation time T is the set of present positions of all fluid elements that passed through one fixed release point a during a specified history of injection. For continuous release over τ0​≤τ≤T, solve a different initial value problem for each release time τ:
∂t∂X​(t;τ,a)=u(X(t;τ,a),t),X(τ;τ,a)=a,ST​={X(T;τ,a):τ0​≤τ≤T}.
(141)
Thus a pathline varies t for one particle; a streakline varies τ while holding T fixed. Interrupted release gives only the corresponding subsets of the streakline. A moving nozzle requires a time-dependent release position instead, so the standard fixed-point definition should not silently be applied to it. Continuous release makes streaklines easy to observe and useful for showing where material from one source has reached. [ref]
In the analytical velocity field (U,at) of pathline, a particle released at the origin at time τ follows
x(t;τ)=U(t−τ),y(t;τ)=2a​(t2−τ2),t≥τ.
(142)
Observe all particles together at time T, after continuous release from τ=0:
x=U(T−τ),y=2a​(T2−τ2),y=UaT​x−2U2a​x2,0≤x≤UT.
(143)
For U=1 ms−1, a=1 ms−2 and T=2 s, the streakline is the downward-curving parabola y=2x−x2/2 in metre coordinates. Releases at τ=0,1,2 s occupy (2,2), (1,1.5) and (0,0) m, respectively. At x=1 m, the streakline lies above the pathline y=x2/2 and below the origin's streamline y=2x. All particles nevertheless have the same present velocity (1,2) ms−1. The streakline slope dy/dx=aτ/U generally differs from the present velocity slope aT/U; they agree at the nozzle. The oldest particle is the downstream endpoint, which explains why the pathline and streakline meet there without being the same curve.
Each panel fixes one observation time. Coloured dots are the present positions of particles with the indicated release times; dashed pathlines show those particles' earlier histories. The black streakline connects present positions of the continuously released family, including release times between the sampled dots. After T=1 s, particles already present keep moving and new ones enter at the origin, so the later streakline is not a frozen record of the earlier one. Injection beginning only at τ=1 s would show just 0≤x≤1 m of the T=2 s curve. Both panels are diffusion-free analytical models, not experimental photographs.
Kline's contracting-channel demonstration switches from marking an element to continuous bubble production at a marking wire: the resulting trace is a streakline. The apparatus therefore changes the curve being displayed by changing the marking history, even with the same channel. A wire produces a family of traces; conceptually each fixed emitting location supplies its own streakline. Individual bubbles are markers rather than perfect fluid elements: Jean Hertzberg's teaching notes describe hydrogen production by electrolysis at a thin cathode wire and warn that larger bubbles have more buoyancy. A dye version uses a small fixed injection port upstream in a transparent water channel, a dilute premix of dye with the channel water, illumination and a fixed optical camera. Start continuous injection at a recorded time τ0​ and take a short exposure at T: the visible filament approximates a streakline, not the accumulated pathline of one particle. Repeat with the same port and imaging geometry after changing the flow with time; compare the filament against local directions estimated from short displacements of sparse markers at T. Agreement in a steady flow and possible disagreement when the flow changes are the predictions being tested; no measured curves are claimed here. The protocol follows Hertzberg's advice to use small ports, little injected volume and a dilute premix with matched density and viscosity. Check for nozzle disturbance by reducing injection rate, for buoyancy by watching the premix in still water, and for diffusion by comparing filament widths at different ages. Keep exposure short enough to suppress motion blur. Dye concentration approximates a passive scalar only when it has negligible feedback on the velocity field; finite port width, diffusion and out-of-plane motion limit how literally a visible filament can be read as a geometrical streakline.
