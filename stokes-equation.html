Stokes equation (−∇p+μ∇2u+f=0)

 Home Physics Branches of physics Fluid mechanics Viscous fluid flow Stokes flow
OurBigBook.com Words: 248 Articles: 1
For a steady incompressible flow of a Newtonian fluid with constant density ρ and constant dynamic viscosity μ>0, neglecting inertia in the Navier-Stokes equation gives
−∇p+μ∇2u+f=0,∇⋅u=0.
(9)
The first equation is local force balance; the second is mass conservation. The term μ∇2u is the divergence of the viscous part of the Newtonian fluid stress tensor. The body force f has units of force per unit volume, Nm−3, and equals ρg for uniform gravity. Appropriate boundary data for velocity or traction, together with suitable distant conditions for an exterior flow, complete the problem; a rigid surface normally imposes the no-slip boundary condition.
With uniform gravity, write p=ph​+p′ where the hydrostatic pressure satisfies ∇ph​=ρg. Then the disturbance equation is −∇p′+μ∇2u=0. This removes gravity from the fluid disturbance equation, while the hydrostatic pressure still supplies buoyancy on an immersed body. Omitting it from the total force balance would give the wrong Stokes settling velocity.
