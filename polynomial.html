Additive polynomial (L(X+Y)=L(X)+L(Y))

Words: 253 Articles: 3
Polynomial OurBigBook.com
Over a field of characteristic p, additive polynomials are sums of p-power monomials, L(X)=∑i​ai​Xpi. They act linearly over the prime field. The identity follows from the Frobenius endomorphism; the converse follows by comparing mixed binomial coefficients. Their zero sets are additive groups, and a nonzero linear coefficient makes all roots simple.

Linearized polynomial over a finite field (L(X)=∑i=0d​ai​Xqi)

Words: 201 Articles: 2
Additive polynomial OurBigBook.com
Over a field containing Fq​, a sum of q-power monomials is Fq​-linear. If its leading coefficient and linear coefficient are nonzero, its root space has exactly qd elements and dimension d over Fq​. Automorphisms of its splitting field act faithfully and linearly on that root space, embedding the Galois group in GL(d,q).

Projective transitivity of a trinomial linearized polynomial (L(X)=Xqd+Xq+TX)

Words: 76

For d≥2, nonzero roots define projective-point coordinates s=Xq−1, satisfying sN+s+T=0, where N=(qd−1)/(q−1). This polynomial is irreducible over Fq​(T). After fixing one root s, the remaining coordinates satisfy R(Y,s)=(YN−sN)/(Y−s)+1. Substitute z=Y/s and h=1/s: the equation becomes hN−1+(zN−1)/(z−1)=0. At a nontrivial Nth root of unity the second term has a simple zero, so the polynomial is Eisenstein over the algebraic closure's rational function field. This proves irreducibility of R and hence two-transitivity on projective points.

Fixed-degree polynomial limit

Words: 72
Polynomial OurBigBook.com
If polynomials of degree at most d converge at d+1 distinct points, their coefficients converge, by invertibility of the Vandermonde matrix. Their limit is a polynomial of degree at most d, and convergence is uniform on every compact subset of C. Under convergence on compact sets, Rouché's theorem further locates a unique nearby zero for each simple root of the limit, for all sufficiently large indices. Finite initial polynomials need not have any root.

Polynomial pencil with four square members

Let u,v∈C[t] be coprime polynomials. If αu+βv is a square for four distinct (α:β), then u,v are constant. For independent u,v, the four square members Lj​=sj2​ are pairwise coprime polynomials. Descartes' rule of signs (N+​(p)≤V(p))

Words: 139
Polynomial OurBigBook.com
Let V(p) be the number of sign changes in the list of nonzero coefficients of a real polynomial, arranged by increasing or decreasing degree. The positive roots of a polynomial, counted with multiplicity of a root, satisfy
N+​(p)≤V(p),V(p)−N+​(p) is even.
(1)
The bound follows by induction on the nonzero terms after removing the lowest power of x. Differentiation removes the constant coefficient. If it and the next coefficient have opposite signs, the Rolle root count with multiplicities loses at most one root and the sign-change count loses one. If their signs agree, the derivative must have an extra root before the first positive root, so neither count needs that extra allowance. The parity statement follows by comparing the signs near zero and at positive infinity: roots of odd multiplicity reverse the sign, and roots of even multiplicity preserve it. Apply the same rule to p(−x) to bound negative roots.

Polynomial SOS

A sum of squares polynomial is a real polynomial expressible as p=∑i​qi2​ for real polynomials qi​. In the homogeneous degree-2m case the summands may be taken homogeneous of degree m. Every such polynomial is nonnegative on real inputs; the converse fails for multivariate polynomials in general.

Homogeneous sum of squares representation

If a degree-2d homogeneous polynomial is a sum of squares polynomial, it has a representation as squares of degree-d homogeneous polynomials. In any sum of squares, the highest-degree terms cannot cancel, since their homogeneous part is itself a sum of squares. Likewise the lowest nonzero degree cannot cancel. Thus every nonzero summand has only degree d.

Monic polynomial quotient is finite free (A[T]/(F)≅AdegF)

Monic irreducible integer-polynomial quotient is a domain (Z[X]/(p))
Words: 82
