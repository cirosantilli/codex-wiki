Photoelectric contact-potential correction (Vs​=Vapp,stop​+Vc​)

OurBigBook.com Words: 176
Define Vc​ as positive when the contact field adds to the retarding barrier, and let Vapp​ be the signed externally imposed retarding voltage. In this chosen sign convention the barrier is e(Vapp​+Vc​), hence
Vapp,stop​=eh​ν−eΦ​−Vc​,Vs​=Vapp,stop​+Vc​.
(50)
This convention specifies a model of the gap; an instrument's polarity labels must be translated into it. With a fixed Vc​, subtraction at two frequencies removes both the work function and the offset:
ν2​−ν1​Vapp,stop​(ν2​)−Vapp,stop​(ν1​)​=eh​.
(51)
For an original example with the same Φ=2.30eV and an additional Vc​=+0.200V, choose ν1​=6.00×1014Hz and ν2​=1.00×1015Hz. The predicted terminal endpoints are −0.018599 and 1.635668V. The negative first endpoint calls for a small externally accelerating voltage because the contact field already over-retards the fastest electrons. Their difference is 1.654267V, giving h/e≃4.135668×10−15Vs. Ignoring Vc​ in the intercept would falsely infer 2.50eV for the work function. The terminal line's zero would then be mistaken for a material threshold. If Vc​ drifts between the two readings, even the slope is biased. These are calculated examples, not Millikan's measurements.
