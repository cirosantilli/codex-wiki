Chemical potential of an ideal gas (μm​=μm∘​+RTln(p/p∘))

 Home Physics Branches of physics Statistical physics Thermodynamics Chemical potential
OurBigBook.com Words: 202
For a pure ideal gas at temperature T and pressure p,
μm​(T,p)=μm∘​(T)+RTlnp∘p​,
(10)
where R is the molar gas constant and p∘ specifies the reference pressure of the standard state. The ratio inside the natural logarithm is dimensionless. The chemical potential difference therefore depends on a ratio of pressures, not an absolute difference. See MIT 5.60, Lecture 14, page 4.
For a chemical species in an ideal gas mixture, replace p by its partial pressure. More generally write μm,i​=μm,i∘​+RTlnai​ using its thermodynamic activity ai​ and a consistent standard state. For an ideal dilute chemical solution, ai​=ci​/c∘ with reference concentration c∘; the thermodynamic activity section describes departures from this idealization.
As a computed comparison, take identical chemical species at T=300K, with pA​=2p∘ and pB​=p∘. Stipulate R=8.314462618Jmol−1K−1. Then
μm,B​−μm,A​=RTlnpA​pB​​=−RTln2≃−1728.944Jmol−1.
(11)
Halving the pressure gives the same decrement whether starting from 2p∘ or 20p∘, provided the ideal gas approximation remains valid. This comparison uses different pressures; it is not the common-pressure reservoir setup of chemical potential equality for particle exchange. The fixed-volume example in chemical potential and Helmholtz free energy supplies an exchange experiment with unequal initial pressures.
