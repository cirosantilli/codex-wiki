For identical bosons the full wave function is symmetric under the particle exchange operator:
ψ(x2​,x1​)=ψ(x1​,x2​).​
(1)
In the given position-only model this is the spatial parity under the particle exchange operator. If additional spin variables are present, the symmetry applies to the complete state including them.

For identical fermions the full wave function is antisymmetric:
ψ(x2​,x1​)=−ψ(x1​,x2​).​
(2)
In particular it vanishes when the two complete one-particle arguments coincide. Applying this to the position-only model assumes that no independent spin symmetry changes the required spatial parity.

The orthogonal coordinate change preserves the kinetic energy and takes the particle exchange operator to (x+​,x−​)↦(x+​,−x−​). Thus the spatial wave function is even in x−​ for bosons and odd for fermions.
The total potential energy and Hamiltonian separate as
V=2mω2​x+2​+2mω2(1+2κ)​x−2​,H=H+​(ω)+H−​(Ω),Ω=ω1+2κ​.
(3)
Assume 1+2κ>0 so the relative oscillator is confining. Its spectrum is En+​,n−​​=ℏω(n+​+1/2)+ℏΩ(n−​+1/2), and its parity in the two-particle relative coordinate is (−1)n−​.
The lowest boson state uses n+​=n−​=0, whereas the lowest fermion state uses n+​=0, n−​=1. Therefore
EB​=21​ℏω[1+1+2κ​],EF​=21​ℏω[1+31+2κ​].​
(4)
For an oscillator of frequency Ω, ⟨x−2​⟩n​=(n+1/2)ℏ/(mΩ). Since (x1​−x2​)2=2x−2​,
⟨(x1​−x2​)2⟩B​=mΩℏ​,⟨(x1​−x2​)2⟩F​=mΩ3ℏ​.​
(5)
The factor three is the extra relative-coordinate excitation required by antisymmetry. The supplied oscillator wave functions correspond to kinetic energy P2/(2m); the extra factor 1/2 printed before that term in the hint is inconsistent with those functions and the stated energies. The calculation uses the conventional kinetic energy dictated by particle mass m.

Use the normalized Rayleigh quotient R(ψ)=⟨ψ,Hψ⟩/⟨ψ,ψ⟩. Its real directional derivative in an arbitrary complex direction η is
δR=⟨ψ,ψ⟩2Re⟨η,(H−R)ψ⟩​.
(6)
It vanishes for every direction precisely when (H−R)ψ=0, since testing η=(H−R)ψ gives its squared norm. Conversely an eigenvector makes every derivative zero. Stationarity over all states is stronger than stationarity in a restricted trial family.
Normalize the Gaussian as ψα​=(α/π)1/4e−αx2/2, with α>0. Its moments are ⟨X2⟩=1/(2α), ⟨X4⟩=3/(4α2) and ⟨P2⟩=ℏ2α/2. The printed potential energy consequently gives
E(α)=4mℏ2α​+4αmω2​+256ℏα215m2ω3​.​
(7)
Put a=αℏ/(mω). Then E/(ℏω)=a/4+1/(4a)+15/(256a2), whose second derivative is 1/(2a3)+45/(128a4)>0. It diverges at both positive endpoints, so its sole stationary point is the unique global minimum. Its equation is
a3−a−3215​=0,a∗​≃1.18179610.​
(8)
The variational principle therefore yields the optimized upper bound
E0​≤E(α∗​)≃0.548944745ℏω,α∗​≃1.18179610ℏmω​.​
(9)
The printed minimizer is inconsistent with the printed quartic coefficient. At a=3/2 the derivative is 5/48>0, so this is not the stationary Gaussian for that potential energy. It is nevertheless a legitimate trial vector and gives the simple, weaker bound E0​≤109ℏω/192​. If the quartic coefficient were instead 5m2ω3/(16ℏ), four times the printed value, then a=3/2 would indeed minimize the family and its energy would be 31ℏω/48. This possible coefficient correction is identified explicitly rather than silently used. The reusable calculation is Gaussian trial for a quadratic-plus-quartic oscillator.

Let Ψ=0 and take the reduced positive operator ρ1​=Tr2​∣Ψ⟩⟨Ψ∣. Choose orthonormal eigenvectors ξj​ for its positive eigenvalues λj​. Define
vj​=(⟨ξj​∣⊗I)Ψ,cj​=λj​​,ηj​=vj​/cj​.
(10)
The defining identity of the partial trace gives ⟨vi​,vj​⟩=λj​δij​, so the ηj​ are orthonormal. Expansion in a full eigenbasis of ρ1​ yields Ψ=∑j​ξj​⊗vj​; zero-eigenvalue terms have zero squared norm and contribute nothing. Thus
Ψ=j∑​cj​∣ξj​⟩∣ηj​⟩,cj​>0.​
(11)
The zero vector has the empty decomposition. Unit normalization would give ∑j​cj2​=1, but no normalization is needed for the theorem itself.
For the given vector, the coefficient matrix is M=(101​011​). Its second reduced operator is M∗M=(21​12​), whose eigenvalues are 3,1 and eigenvectors η+​=(E1​+E2​)/2​, η−​=(E1​−E2​)/2​. The corresponding normalized first-factor vectors are
ξ+​=6​e1​+e2​+2e3​​,ξ−​=2​e1​−e2​​.
(12)
Their norms are one and their inner product is zero. Hence
ψ=3​∣ξ+​⟩∣η+​⟩+∣ξ−​⟩∣η−​⟩.​
(13)
Expanding the two terms returns the original three contributions. The vector has squared norm 4; if a normalized state is wanted, divide by two and the Schmidt coefficients become 3​/2 and 1/2.

The no-cloning theorem states that no fixed deterministic quantum operation can perfectly duplicate every unknown pure state. For a unitary copier with a fixed blank, suppose it cloned two normalized vectors ∣a⟩,∣b⟩. Preservation of inner products would give ⟨a∣b⟩=⟨a∣b⟩2, impossible for a nonorthogonal distinct pair with overlap strictly between zero and one in modulus. Including an environment does not help: its final overlap has modulus at most one, so one would require ∣⟨a∣b⟩∣≤∣⟨a∣b⟩∣2. Thus a universal perfect copier is impossible. Orthogonal known alternatives do not contradict this restriction.
Label the three qubits as input S, Alice's entangled qubit A and Bob's qubit B. Initially their joint state is
2​1​[α0​(∣000⟩+∣011⟩)+α1​(∣100⟩+∣111⟩)].
(14)
The controlled-NOT gate from S to A changes this to
2​1​[α0​(∣000⟩+∣011⟩)+α1​(∣110⟩+∣101⟩)].
(15)
Apply H∣0⟩=(∣0⟩+∣1⟩)/2​ and H∣1⟩=(∣0⟩−∣1⟩)/2​ to the first qubit and group by Alice's two computational-basis outcomes. The result is
21​[∣00⟩∣ψ⟩+∣01⟩X∣ψ⟩+∣10⟩Z∣ψ⟩+∣11⟩XZ∣ψ⟩].​
(16)
In expanded form the last two Bob factors are α0​∣0⟩−α1​∣1⟩ and α0​∣1⟩−α1​∣0⟩. Their Alice labels must be 10 and 11; the repeated 00 labels in the source are printing errors.
Alice measures her two qubits and sends the two classical bits (a,b) to Bob. Each outcome has probability 1/4 and Bob's conditional state is XbZa∣ψ⟩. He applies ZaXb​ to recover the input, up to physically irrelevant global phase. Explicitly the corrections for 00,01,10,11 are I,X,Z,ZX. This is quantum teleportation: Alice's input is consumed by measurement, so no second surviving copy is created. Before her classical message, Bob's reduced density operator is I/2, independent of the input, so the protocol also supplies no faster-than-light communication.

Use metric signature (+−−−) and the curvature sign fixed by the stated Newtonian limit. In flat spacetime the source-free Maxwell equations are ∂a​Fab=0 and ∂[a​Fbc]​=0. The stress-energy tensor in mixed form is Tab​=FacFcb​+δba​Fcd​Fcd/4. Its divergence is
∂a​Tab​=(∂a​Fac)Fcb​+Fac∂a​Fcb​+41​∂b​(Fcd​Fcd).
(17)
Contracting the cyclic Maxwell equation with Fac gives Fac∂a​Fcb​=−∂b​(Fcd​Fcd)/4. The first term also vanishes, proving ∂a​Tab=0​. The component b=0 expresses local electromagnetic energy conservation, and the spatial components express momentum conservation. Integrating converts them into the usual energy and momentum flux balances.
In curved spacetime the tensor equations are
∇a​Fab=0,∇[a​Fbc]​=0.​
(18)
They agree with the flat equations in coordinates of a local inertial frame at a point, where the connection vanishes, and covariance then extends them to arbitrary coordinates. Antisymmetry makes the homogeneous equation equivalent to dF=0. Metric compatibility permits the same contraction proof, now giving ∇a​Tab=0​. In general,
∂a​Tab=−Γaac​Tcb−Γbac​Tac,
(19)
which need not vanish. Covariant local balance remains valid; ordinary coordinate components do not provide a universal conserved global energy-momentum vector in a curved geometry. Additional spacetime symmetries can furnish conserved currents. A locally inertial frame removes the connection at a point, not the curvature or the global obstruction.
The alternating array with fixed component ϵ0123​=1 is appropriate in oriented inertial special-relativistic coordinates, whose proper Lorentz transformations have determinant one. It is not itself a covariant tensor under general coordinate changes. For an oriented Lorentzian metric, replace it by the metric metric volume tensor εabcd​=−detg​[abcd]. The genuine dual is
(∗F)ab​=21​εabcd​Fcd.​
(20)
This is the Hodge star; the determinant factor, orientation and raising conventions are essential. Even in flat spacetime arbitrary curvilinear coordinates require the metric volume tensor, so the distinction is about coordinate covariance rather than curvature alone.
The isolated equation for R00​ singles out a coordinate component and cannot be a generally covariant law. Replacing it by a proportionality Rab​∝Tab​ still fails: the contracted Bianchi identity gives ∇aRab​=∇b​R/2, whereas stress-energy conservation requires ∇aTab​=0. Such a law would force R constant and hence impose an unphysical restriction on a varying relativistic dust density. Missing units or a coupling constant in the proposed proportionality would not repair this structural problem.
The Einstein tensor Gab​=Rab​−Rgab​/2 is divergence-free. Therefore
Gab​=−8πGTab​​
(21)
is consistent with covariant stress-energy conservation in the source's curvature convention. Its trace gives R=8πGT and hence Rab​=−8πG(Tab​−Tgab​/2). For slowly moving relativistic dust with VaVa​=1, T00​≃ρ, T≃ρ, g00​≃1, so R00​≃−4πGρ, exactly matching −Δϕ and Poisson's equation.
Finally, electromagnetic stress-energy is trace-free: Taa​=FacFca​+Fcd​Fcd=0 by antisymmetry. The traced field equation then gives R=0​ for a purely electromagnetic source. This does not say Rab​=0 or that spacetime is flat; its trace-free Ricci curvature can still be nonzero.

Under a smooth coordinate change x↦x′, a contravariant vector obeys
T′a=∂xb∂x′a​Tb.​
(22)
The scalar-contraction hypothesis gives Ua′​(∂x′a/∂xb)Tb=Ub​Tb for every choice of Tb. Equality of all coefficients and multiplication by the inverse Jacobian matrix give Ua′​=(∂xb/∂x′a)Ub​​, the covector law.
Let Va=dxa/ds be a timelike geodesic tangent. Metric compatibility and the geodesic equation give
dsd​(Ta​Va)=VaVb∇b​Ta​+Ta​DsDVa​=VaVb∇(a​Tb)​.
(23)
A Killing vector therefore gives a conserved contraction. Conversely, if that contraction is conserved along every timelike geodesic, then at any point its symmetric tensor Sab​=∇(a​Tb)​ satisfies Sab​VaVb=0 for every unit timelike initial tangent. Homogeneity extends this to the entire timelike cone, an open set of tangent vectors. A quadratic polynomial vanishing on an open set has every coefficient zero, so ∇(a​Tb)​=0​. Considering a single selected geodesic would not suffice. This is the Converse geodesic characterization of a Killing vector.
For the given exterior transformation, differentiating at fixed r gives
T=∂t​=−4mU​∂U​+4mV​∂V​.​
(24)
The transformation also gives UV=(2m−r)er/(2m). This determines r smoothly for r>0 throughout UV<2m. The flow of the displayed vector field rescales U↦e−λ/(4m)U, V↦eλ/(4m)V. It leaves UV, r, dUdV and the angular metric unchanged. Thus it preserves the full metric everywhere in that region and extends as a global Killing field, not merely by a formal exterior coordinate substitution.
Its squared norm is
g(T,T)=−rUV​e−r/(2m)=1−r2m​.​
(25)
It is timelike for UV<0 (r>2m), spacelike for 0<UV<2m (0<r<2m), and a nonzero null vector on either horizon UV=0 away from U=V=0. At U=V=0 it is the zero vector, on the whole bifurcation surface of angular points. Zero must be distinguished from a nonzero null vector. This is the Schwarzschild time-translation Killing field in Kruskal coordinates.

The coordinate derivatives of the smooth two-parameter map commute. With a torsion-free Levi-Civita connection,
DsDYa​=∂s∂τ∂2xa​+Γabc​VbYc=DτDVa​,
(26)
because Γabc​ is symmetric in its lower indices. Each member of the family is a geodesic, so DV/Ds=0. Consequently
Ds2D2Yd​=DsD​DτD​Vd=(DsD​DτD​−DτD​DsD​)Vd.
(27)
The commutator of covariant derivatives is the action of the Riemann curvature tensor. With the source's index and sign convention this gives
Ds2D2Yd​=Rabc​dVaYbVc.​
(28)
This is geodesic deviation: free fall relative acceleration measures curvature, even where an individual observer can eliminate the connection at one point.
For Earth's exterior Newtonian field, ϕ=−GM/r and acceleration is ai​=−∂i​ϕ. Nearby freely falling particles have relative acceleration δai​=−ϕ,ij​Yj. At the observation point align z radially upward and x,y horizontally. Since GM/RE2​=g,
−ϕ,ij​=RE​g​diag(−1,−1,2).
(29)
The weak-field geodesic-deviation equation therefore identifies
R010​1≃−RE​g​,R020​2≃−RE​g​,R030​3≃RE​2g​.​
(30)
In these units time is measured in seconds as in the stated Newtonian comparison; if the zeroth coordinate is ct instead, the curvature components acquire the corresponding c−2 factor. A small freely falling cloud is squeezed in the two horizontal directions and stretched radially. These are tidal effects, not the uniform acceleration removed by a freely falling frame.
Their characteristic inverse-square time is g/RE​, so ttide​=RE​/g​≃8.1×102 s≃13.4 min​. One concrete thought experiment is an ideal satellite moving in a circular orbit just above a spherical Earth, ignoring atmosphere and terrain. Its orbital angular frequency is GM/RE3​​=g/RE​​ and its period is 2πttide​≃84 minutes. Equivalently, initially nearby free fall trajectories develop order-one tidal changes on this characteristic scale. This is the surface tidal time scale of a spherical gravitating body.

Write f(r)=1−2m/r and restore c for the numerical estimate. A stationary clock at radius r has dτ=f(r)​dt. Let radii r1​ and r2​=r1​−h denote the upper and lower computers. The metric is stationary and the same two fixed spatial points exchange both signals, so each signal has the same coordinate propagation time. Therefore the interval between reception events equals the interval between emission events in coordinate time: the common delay cancels.
For upper proper running time τ1​, the lower computer has run for
τ2​=f(r1​)f(r2​)​​τ1​.​
(31)
In the weak gravitational field near Earth's surface, m=GM/c2 and
f(r2​)/f(r1​)​≃1−c2gh​.
(32)
The lower clock runs more slowly, so its remaining work is
Nleft​≃(1012 s−1)(604800 s)(2.99792458×108 ms−1)2(9.81 ms−2)(10 m)​≃6.6×102.​
(33)
Thus about 660 micro-operations remain; the proper-time shortfall is only about 6.6×10−10 seconds.
Successful comparison tests the gravitational dependence of stationary proper-clock rates, specifically the radial variation of gtt​ and its weak-field gravitational redshift prediction, assuming the computers execute equally per unit proper time. It also checks that this clock effect is independent of the chosen physical clock mechanism. It does not separately verify the Schwarzschild radial metric coefficient: although light propagation uses the null metric, the identical start and finish propagation delays cancel from this particular observable. This is Gravitational redshift measured by a computation counter.

Put f=1−2m/r, use proper time s, and denote its derivatives by dots. The timelike geodesic equations, away from the Schwarzschild coordinate singularity, are
t¨+ff′​r˙t˙r¨+2ff′​t˙2−2ff′​r˙2−rf(θ˙2+sin2θϕ˙​2)θ¨+r2r˙​θ˙−sinθcosθϕ˙​2ϕ¨​+r2r˙​ϕ˙​+2cotθθ˙ϕ˙​​=0,=0,=0,=0,​​
(34)
with normalization ft˙2−f−1r˙2−r2θ˙2−r2sin2θϕ˙​2=1. Spherical symmetry permits an equatorial plane, θ=π/2, and the cyclic coordinates give E=ft˙,J=r2ϕ˙​​. Normalization then gives r˙2=E2−f(1+J2/r2).
For J=0 and u=m/r, du/dϕ=−mr˙/J. Thus
(u′)2=J2(E2−1)m2​+J22m2​u−u2+2u3.​
(35)
At E=1, J=4m, the right side factors as 2u(u−1/4)2. Set q=2u​, so u=q2/4 and (q′)2=(q2−1)2/8. Integrating the regions 0<q<1 and q>1 gives, up to angular shifts and time reversal,
u=41​tanh2(22​ϕ−ϕ0​​),u=41​coth2(22​ϕ−ϕ0​​).​
(36)
The constant u=1/4, or r=4m, is the corresponding circular timelike orbit. The hyperbolic-tangent branch lies at r>4m and approaches that orbit from outside as ∣ϕ∣→∞; the hyperbolic-cotangent branch lies at 0<r<4m and approaches it from inside. Near the limit u−1/4 decays exponentially as e−∣ϕ∣/2​. Since ϕ˙​ tends to J/(4m)2=1/(4m), the limiting approach also takes infinite proper time. Neither nonconstant branch reaches and joins the circular orbit at a finite angular parameter.
Differentiating the orbit equation gives u′′=m2/J2−u+3u2. A perturbation about u=1/4 obeys δu′′=δu/2, showing that the circular orbit is unstable. The two explicit curves are its separatrix branches, not ordinary small stable oscillations. For E=1 the energy is marginally bound, so the outer critical branch is the limiting orbit between scattering and Schwarzschild marginally bound capture from infinity. Time-reversed inner and outer branches describe the corresponding approach or departure from that unstable orbit. These are the Explicit separatrices of the marginally bound Schwarzschild orbit.
