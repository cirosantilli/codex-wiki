Oil drop charge readout and calibration

Robert Andrews Millikan's 1911 protocol alternated electrically driven ascent with timed descent after short-circuiting the plates; a changed ascent speed signalled ion capture.[ref]
His 1913 refinements checked voltage before and after runs, calibrated optical distance and the clock, compared independent air-dynamic viscosity measurements, and varied pressure to test slip.[ref] His 1924 lecture describes suppressing convection, switching the electric field, neutralizing drops, and reproducing smallest observed electrical speed increments.[ref]
Harvey Fletcher's posthumous account recalls building the first oil apparatus and measuring with Robert Andrews Millikan; priority for suggesting oil remains uncertain.[ref]
To interpret the readout, keep one drop and one electric field polarity. Let ui​ be its signed upward terminal velocity in charge state i, and let vf​>0 be its field-off falling speed. Use the linear drag coefficient k already defined in elementary charge, with any required gas slip correction to Stokes drag included. Provided the drop's radius, surrounding air and electric field remain fixed, its electrical speed increment is
Si​=ui​+vf​,∣qi​∣=Ek​Si​,∣qj​∣∣qi​∣​=Sj​Si​​,Δ∣q∣=Ek​(ui+1​−ui​).
(6)
These relations apply while the electric force points upward in every state. A drop may still descend, with ui​<0, when that force is too weak to support it. A held drop has ui​=0 and therefore Si​=vf​, rather than zero electrical response. A neutral drop has Si​=0: its signed terminal velocity agrees with field-off descent. Thus neither raw upward speed nor the ability to hold a drop is itself a count of electrons.
The ratios cancel a fixed k/E, even if its value is poorly known. This is why relative charge quantization and absolute measurement calibration are different questions. A changed field-off vf​ warns that evaporation, air motion or another change has spoiled comparison of charge states. A stable vf​ is useful evidence but cannot exclude every compensating change. Repeat field-off checks around a proposed jump, allow the terminal velocity under linear drag to settle after switching, and propagate measurement uncertainty in both the field-on and field-off readings. Reusing a single estimate of vf​ gives the different Si​ a shared covariance.
