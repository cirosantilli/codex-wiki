Hierarchical leading-zero counter

 ... Theoretical computer science Computational complexity theory Circuit complexity Boolean circuit Combinational logic Count leading zeros
OurBigBook.com Words: 121
For a block of 2k bits, encode its count leading zeros result as a zero flag z followed by k low count bits r. An all-zero block has (z,r)=(1,0); otherwise z=0. Two half-block encoders supply flags zH​,zL​ and low counts h,ℓ. Combine them using z=zH​∧zL​, rk−1​=zH​∧¬zL​, and ri​=mux(zH​,hi​,ℓi​) for i<k−1. A one-bit leaf has z=¬x and no low count bits. Checking whether the upper half is nonzero, only the lower half is nonzero, or both halves are zero proves the formula.
All count bits are selected in parallel, so D(n)=D(n/2)+O(1) for circuit depth. The circuit size satisfies S(n)=2S(n/2)+O(logn); summing the work over levels gives S(n)=O(n). Sharing the all-zero flags avoids a serial adder along the critical path.
