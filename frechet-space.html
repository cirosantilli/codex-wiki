Fréchet space

 ... Mathematics Area of mathematics Analysis Functional analysis Topological vector space Locally convex space
OurBigBook.com Words: 622 Articles: 2
A Fréchet space is a Hausdorff locally convex space whose topology is induced by a complete translation-invariant metric. Translation invariance means d(x+z,y+z)=d(x,y); completeness means every Cauchy sequence converges in the space. Use a translation-invariant metric for this condition: an arbitrary compatible metric can change which sequences are Cauchy sequences. [ref].
Equivalently, the topology comes from a countable separating family of seminorms (pk​)k≥0​ and is complete in their common sense: if pk​(xn​−xm​)→0 as n,m→∞ for every fixed k, then some x∈E satisfies pk​(xn​−x)→0 for every k. A useful compatible translation-invariant metric is
d(x,y)=∑k=0∞​2−(k+1)min{1,pk​(x−y)}.
(9)
It is finite because the weights sum to one. The separating condition makes it a metric; without that condition the formula gives only a pseudometric. Its convergence in a metric space and Cauchy sequence conditions are exactly the conditions for all the pk​ separately: control finitely many terms, then bound the remaining tail by 2−(K+1). Replacing pk​ by max0≤j≤k​pj​ gives an increasing family with the same topology.
Every Banach space is a Fréchet space, using its norm as the single measurement. The space of smooth functions on a compact interval is a Fréchet space with derivative seminorms but with no single norm inducing its smooth topology. This is why Fréchet spaces are useful in differential equations: one can require accuracy in every order of derivative, while each particular estimate uses only finitely many orders.
The name honors Maurice Fréchet. His doctoral thesis was published as [ref]; an [ref] is available. This identifies a historical source without attributing the modern locally convex definition to that paper.
