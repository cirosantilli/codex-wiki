Light-emitting diode

 Home Physics Branches of physics Electromagnetism Electrical circuit Diode
OurBigBook.com Words: 2k Articles: 9
A light-emitting diode is a semiconductor diode producing electroluminescence under electric current. Forward bias injects electrons and electron holes; radiative electron-hole recombination emits photons. A double heterostructure uses band offsets to retain both charge carriers in an active layer. A sufficiently thin layer is a quantum well.[ref]
The optical and electrical questions are different: photon energy sets a color scale, while light-emitting diode efficiency determines how much supplied energy escapes as light. The blue light-emitting diode made compact white light-emitting diodes and full-color displays practical. A modulated light-emitting diode can also supply the optical signal in a chopped-light lock-in experiment.
