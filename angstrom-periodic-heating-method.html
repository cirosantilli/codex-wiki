Ångström periodic heating method

 ... Branches of physics Statistical physics Thermodynamics Heat Thermal conduction Thermal diffusivity
OurBigBook.com Words: 2k Articles: 6
The Ångström periodic heating method measures thermal diffusivity by periodically heating one end of a rod and comparing the resulting temperature oscillations at two separated thermometers. The farther signal has a smaller amplitude and a later wave phase. Their combination measures how quickly the material redistributes heat, without requiring the absolute heater power. The method bears the name of Anders Jonas Ångström, the physicist who became a professor at Uppsala in 1858.[ref]
An actual teaching apparatus is PASCO's TD-8513: four metal bars share a heater/cooler using the thermoelectric effect, and each has two embedded thermistors about 3cm apart. Foam covers reduce environmental heat transfer. Its manual instructs students to measure sensor spacing and connect the thermistors through a temperature interface to a computer. For the aluminium bar, a 0–8V triangular drive is applied with a 30s period. Recording begins after the amplitudes stabilize; two cycles provide amplitudes and the delay between corresponding peaks. Repeated runs at 40 and 50s test consistency. The manual converts the inferred thermal diffusivity to thermal conductivity using mass density and specific heat capacity.[ref]
For a general rod experiment, attach small thermocouples, thermistors or other thermometers at measured positions x1​<x2​, record both against the same clock, and establish a steady periodic thermal state. A sinusoid is especially convenient. For a switched or triangular drive, isolate the same Fourier series component at both sensors: unrelated peaks in distorted waveforms need not measure that component's phase difference. Subtract each sensor's mean temperature; a steady spatial background does not belong in the oscillating amplitude ratio. Small excursions help keep thermal conductivity, mass density and specific heat capacity constant.
The appeal is that the rod supplies two complementary clocks: decreasing amplitude measures spatial damping, while increasing wave phase lag measures delay. Unlike laser flash analysis, which follows a transient after one pulse, this method repeatedly samples a steady periodic thermal state. The timing determines thermal diffusivity α in m2s−1. To infer thermal conductivity k in Wm−1K−1, also measure or otherwise justify the mass density ρ and specific heat capacity at constant pressure cp​:
k=ρcp​α.
