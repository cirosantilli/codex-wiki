Guided wavelength (λg​=2π/β)

The guided wavelength is the distance for a 2π change of axial wave phase in a propagating normal mode. For the vacuum-filled rectangular electromagnetic waveguide,
λg​=1−(fc​/f)2​λ0​​,λg2​1​=λ02​1​−(2a)21​

for TE10​, with λ0​=c/f and f>fc​. It exceeds the vacuum wavelength at the same frequency. This longer axial spacing is consistent with vph​>c: multiplying λg​ by f gives the phase velocity, not the group velocity.
Two oppositely traveling fields at the same frequency form a standing wave. For a perfect electric short at z=0, the total electric field is proportional to sin(βz)sin(πx/a), so successive axial electric nodes are separated by
d=βπ​=2λg​​.
(13)
Thus neighboring minima in a slotted line scan give half a guided wavelength. In the original a=30mm, f=1.25fc​ example, d=40mm implies λg​=80mm, not 40mm. Combined with the chosen f, the inferred broad dimension is a=λ0​/[21−(λ0​/λg​)2​]=30mm. Below cutoff, β is imaginary and 2π/β is not a physical periodic spacing.
