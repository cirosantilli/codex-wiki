Spectroscopic test of the Twin Quasar lens

 Home Physics Branches of physics General relativity Gravitational lensing Strong gravitational lensing
OurBigBook.com Words: 201
The two resolved quasar images of 0957+561 provide an observational question: are they separate sources, or different light paths from one source? In May 1979, spectrophotometry used the McDonald Observatory 2.7 m reflecting telescope, the UVITS spectrograph, and an intensified dissector scanner at the f/9 focus of the Cassegrain reflector. A 600-groove-per-millimetre diffraction grating produced 4100-7100 ångström scans sampled every 2 ångströms, with about 7 ångström resolution over most of that range. Two spectrograph slits, about 52 arcseconds apart, recorded object and sky background simultaneously for subtraction. Interleaving observations of the two images reduced instrumental and atmospheric gain changes.[ref]
The readout was received light versus wavelength for each image. Closely matching absorption line redshifts supported a common source and absorber; differences in continua and line strengths motivated differential interstellar extinction. Spectral agreement supplied evidence for gravitational lensing, rather than proving the point-mass gravitational lens model. The authors discussed a physical double and discrepant optical and radio light ratios. Their note added in proof attributed the redder spectrum to foreground galaxy contamination and emphasized an extended mass distribution.[ref]
