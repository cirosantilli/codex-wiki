Two-length machine compliance separation (E=A0​(q2​−q1​)L2​−L1​​)

 ... Continuum mechanics Elasticity (physics) Linear elasticity Young's modulus Tensile modulus experiment Machine compliance
OurBigBook.com Words: 432 Articles: 1
Prepare two uniform specimens with the same material, direction and A0​, but different deforming lengths L1​,L2​. Keep grip engagement, adapters, loading window and measurement configuration equivalent. Determine qi​=dxi​/dF separately by linear regression with its own displacement intercept. Subtracting the two series equations eliminates the common machine compliance:
q2​−q1​=EA0​L2​−L1​​,E=A0​(q2​−q1​)L2​−L1​​,Cm​=L2​−L1​L2​q1​−L1​q2​​.
(16)
This is an inverse problem: one length supplies only one equation for two unknowns, while two distinct lengths separate the length-dependent specimen contribution from the constant contribution. In a graph of compliance against length, the slope is 1/(EA0​) and the vertical intercept is Cm​. It is the compliance, not kobs​, that is linear in L.
The dimensional analysis is revealing: [q2​−q1​]=m/N, so [(L2​−L1​)/(A0​(q2​−q1​))]=N/m2. For L2​>L1​, the ideal model requires q2​>q1​. A negative fitted Cm​ challenges the common-intercept assumptions or the measurements. Two lengths determine two parameters exactly, so they cannot test the assumed straight line; three or more lengths allow regression residuals to reveal departures. Nearly equal lengths amplify slope errors through the small difference q2​−q1​. If the actual apparatus compliances differ by ΔCm​, the inferred length slope becomes 1/(EA0​)+ΔCm​/(L2​−L1​).
