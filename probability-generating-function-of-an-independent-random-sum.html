Probability generating function of an independent random sum (GS​(s)=GN​(GY​(s)))

 ... Mathematics Area of mathematics Probability and statistics Probability theory Probability distribution Probability generating function
OurBigBook.com Words: 334
Let N be a nonnegative integer-valued random variable, and let Y1​,Y2​,… be nonnegative integer-valued independent and identically distributed random variables, with common probability generating function GY​. Require the whole sequence to be independent of N, so N,Y1​,Y2​,… are jointly independent. Set the random sum
S=∑j=1N​Yj​,
(47)
with the empty sum equal to zero. Given N=n, the probability generating function of an independent sum yields the conditional expectation E[sS∣N=n]=GY​(s)n. By the law of total expectation,
GS​(s)=E[GY​(s)N]=GN​(GY​(s)),∣s∣≤1.
(48)
The composition is valid because ∣GY​(s)∣≤1. Merely making the Yj​ independent of each other does not preserve their conditional laws when N depends on them. This is the probability generating function version of transform composition for an independent random sum.
For a Poisson distribution count with mean λ, substitution into GN​(u)=exp(λ(u−1)) gives a compound Poisson distribution with
GS​(s)=exp(λ(GY​(s)−1)).
(49)
If the jumps have finite second moment, conditioning also gives mean E[S]=λE[Y] and variance Var(S)=λE[Y2]. The extra spread comes from randomness in the count as well as randomness in the jumps.[ref]
For an original example, choose N∼Poisson(2) and jumps taking values one and two with equal probability. Keep the independence assumptions above. Then
GY​(s)=2s+s2​,GS​(s)=exp(2[2s+s2​−1])=exp(s+s2−2).
(50)
Here E[Y]=3/2, Var(Y)=1/4, and E[Y2]=5/2. The law of total expectation and law of total variance give
E[S]=2⋅23​=3,Var(S)=2⋅41​+2(23​)2=5.
(51)
Thus the aggregate is not itself a Poisson distribution, whose mean and variance would coincide.
To recover the probability mass function pn​=P(S=n) without repeated differentiation, use GS′​(s)=(1+2s)GS​(s) and compare coefficients:
p0​=e−2,npn​=pn−1​+2pn−2​(n≥1),pn​=0(n<0).
(52)
For example, (p0​,p1​,p2​,p3​)=e−2(1,1,3/2,7/6). As an independent check, expanding es+s2−2=e−2eses2 gives
pn​=e−2∑b=0⌊n/2⌋​(n−2b)!b!1​.
(53)
The plotted probability masses stop at 20, although the support of a probability distribution is unbounded. The omitted tail probability is
P(S>20)=1−e−2∑n=020​∑b=0⌊n/2⌋​(n−2b)!b!1​≈1.95613×10−7.
(54)
This model has bounded jumps and an unbounded count. Its entire probability generating function does give a finite moment-generating function at every real argument, but the general random sum composition only guarantees the stated ∣s∣≤1 domain.
