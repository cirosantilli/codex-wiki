Damped torsional oscillation (Iθ¨+bθ˙+κθ=0)

OurBigBook.com Words: 207
A damped torsional oscillation is rotation about a fiber's fixed reference orientation with a restoring torque and dissipative torque. In the ideal viscous model, damping contributes −bθ˙ with constant b≥0. This is a rotational damped harmonic oscillator. For known constant moment of inertia I and torsion constant κ, define γ=b/(2I). In the oscillatory regime γ2<κ/I,
θ(t)=Ae−γtcos(ωd​t+φ),ωd2​=Iκ​−γ2,Td​=ωd​2π​.
(14)
A and φ are set by the initial state. Successive positive peaks measured from the same equilibrium have magnitudes pn​ and pn+1​; their ratio gives δ=ln(pn​/pn+1​)=γTd​. Consequently a measured damped oscillation period and decay together recover
κ=I[(Td​2π​)2+γ2]=Td2​I​(4π2+δ2).​
(15)
Using only 4π2I/Td2​ underestimates the torsion constant in this model. The toy curve in torsion constant sets I=2.0×10−6kgm2, κ=1.0×10−8Nm/rad, and γ=0.020s−1. It gives T0​≃88.86s, Td​≃92.64s, and δ≃1.853. Ignoring the decay recovers 9.2×10−9Nm/rad, an 8% underestimate; including it recovers the stipulated torsion constant.
These equations assume a single rotational mode, a stationary zero, linear elasticity, and viscous damping. Internal fiber losses need not behave as a constant viscous coefficient: Fleischer and colleagues model their precision apparatus using a loss angle. Their response is evidence for specifying a damping model, not for applying the viscous correction universally. [ref]
