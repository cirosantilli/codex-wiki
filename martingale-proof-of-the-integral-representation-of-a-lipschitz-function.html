Martingale proof of the integral representation of a Lipschitz function (f(x)−f(0)=∫0x​g(u)du,∣g∣≤L)

 ... Mathematics Area of mathematics Probability and statistics Probability theory Martingale Dyadic slope martingale
OurBigBook.com Words: 89
For an L-Lipschitz function f on [0,1], its slopes on the dyadic partition, evaluated at a uniform distribution input, form a bounded martingale for the dyadic filtration. The uniformly integrable martingale convergence theorem yields a bounded terminal random variable g(X), with g chosen as a Borel measurable function by the Doob-Dynkin lemma. The terminal conditional expectation identity says that each slope is the average of g over the corresponding dyadic interval. Integrating over adjacent dyadic intervals and taking a telescoping sum proves the displayed representation first at dyadic endpoints. The Lipschitz condition for f and the bound ∣g∣≤L extend it to every endpoint by continuity. This proves absolute continuity of a function without presupposing a derivative.
