Chirped pulse amplification

 Home Physics Branches of physics Optics Laser
OurBigBook.com Words: 1k Articles: 4
Chirped pulse amplification increases an optical pulse's energy while keeping its peak power lower during amplification: stretch the pulse, amplify it, then compress it. A chirp is a changing instantaneous frequency. Stretching separates the arrival times of different frequencies; pulse compression brings their contributions back together. The gain medium supplies energy, whereas the compressor concentrates the surviving energy into a shorter time.
Donna Strickland and Gérard Mourou demonstrated chirped pulse amplification at Rochester in 1985. Their experiment used an optical fiber, a regenerative amplifier and a diffraction grating compressor.[ref]
The useful idea is to arrange where the large peak power occurs. Lower light intensity in the amplifier reduces unwanted self-focusing and self-phase modulation; the compressed output can still drive strong nonlinear optics. Stretching does not remove every damage mechanism, and compression does not manufacture energy.
