Normals through a point on a ring torus (P=(a,R/2,0),Q=(a,0,0))

 ... Mathematics Area of mathematics Geometry and topology Topology Topological surface Torus
OurBigBook.com Words: 109
For the surface of revolution of the circle with center (a,R) and radius r<R in the upper half-plane, use X(u,v)=(a+rcosu,(R+rsinu)cosv,(R+rsinu)sinv) and unit normal n=(cosu,sinucosv,sinusinv). Exactly four surface normals pass through the displayed P. The equation P−X=tn forces (t+r)cosu=0. The case t=−r would put P at radial distance R, so cosu=0; its nonzero radial coordinate then forces v=0 or π. All four combinations work. By contrast, infinitely many normals pass through Q: both complete circles with u=π/2 or 3π/2 have radial normals through that point. This is a concrete illustration of degenerate distance-critical sets versus isolated critical points.
