Lorentz transformation (Λ)

 Home Physics Branches of physics Special relativity
OurBigBook.com Words: 6k Articles: 81
A Lorentz transformation preserves the Minkowski metric. For a Lorentz boost, let S′ move at constant velocity +v along the x-axis of S, with parallel spatial axes and coincident origins at t=t′=0. With β=v/c and the Lorentz factor γ, the coordinates of the same spacetime event obey
ct′=γ(ct−βx),x′=γ(x−βct),y′=y,z′=z.
(21)
The inverse Lorentz transformation replaces v by −v. In particular, x′=0 traces the moving origin x=vt, fixing the sign convention. The time formula is t′=γ(t−vx/c2); dropping its position term would lose the relativity of simultaneity. [ref].
For the moving-clock example in time dilation, the second tick has (t,x)=(5μs,3cμs). At v=0.6c, substitution gives t′=1.25(5−0.6×3)μs=4μs and x′=1.25(3−0.6×5)cμs=0. The clock stays at its own origin, and its coordinate time there equals its proper time.
