Circle and interval are not homeomorphic

 Home Mathematics Area of mathematics Geometry and topology Topology Homeomorphism
OurBigBook.com Words: 120
The circle S1 and the closed interval [0,1] are both compact and connected, so those two properties alone do not distinguish them. Instead examine connectedness after removing a point. Every punctured circle is connected: after rotating the missing point to (1,0), the continuous map t↦(cost,sint) from the connected open interval (0,2π) maps onto it. Continuous maps preserve connectedness. [ref]
If a homeomorphism h:S1→[0,1] existed, choose p=h−1(1/2). Its restriction would give a homeomorphism between S1∖{p} and [0,1]∖{1/2}. But the latter splits into two nonempty disjoint open sets in its subspace topology, [0,1/2) and (1/2,1], contradicting connectedness of the punctured circle. Removing an endpoint of [0,1] leaves a connected space; the proof deliberately chooses the preimage of an interior point. This illustrates how a simple connectedness test can rule out every possible homeomorphism.
