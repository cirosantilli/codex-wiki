Bulk electric-dipole selection rule (χ(2)=0)

 Home Physics Branches of physics Optics Nonlinear optics Nonlinear optical susceptibility
OurBigBook.com Words: 142
Suppose a homogeneous bulk material is unchanged when all spatial coordinates are reversed. Both electric polarization and electric field reverse under this inversion, so a local electric dipole response must obey P(−E)=−P(E). Substituting the nonlinear optical susceptibility expansion gives
P(−E)+P(E)=2ϵ0​(χ(2)E2+χ(4)E4+⋯)=0.
(60)
For every sufficiently small E, all even coefficients vanish. In the full tensor relation, two reversed input electric fields leave their product unchanged while the output electric polarization changes sign, forcing every bulk electric-dipole component of χ(2) to vanish.
The qualification matters: the argument assumes inversion symmetry, a local bulk electric dipole response, and no applied bias that breaks that symmetry. A surface breaks bulk inversion symmetry; spatially varying or magnetic responses also lie outside this local electric-dipole model. Thus detecting second-harmonic generation at a surface or through a weaker magnetic contribution does not contradict the bulk selection rule. Conversely, absence of inversion symmetry permits some quadratic components but does not guarantee a nonzero component in every beam geometry.
