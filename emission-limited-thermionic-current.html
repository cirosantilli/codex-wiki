Emission-limited thermionic current

 Home Physics Subatomic particle Electron Thermionic emission
OurBigBook.com Words: 126
An emission-limited thermionic current is set by the rate at which the heated surface supplies electrons, with nearly complete collection. At fixed temperature, increasing the attracting electric potential difference then produces an approximate collection plateau. That plateau is the useful regime for fitting the Richardson-Dushman law, provided the electric field has not significantly lowered the surface barrier.
The emitting surface still matters in this regime. Adsorption changes the work function; different crystal faces can have different barriers. Heating may desorb contaminants or change a coating, so repeated measurements at the same temperature need not agree unless the surface preparation is controlled. For fixed A,T, a barrier change ΔW multiplies J by e−ΔW/(kB​T). In the chosen model at 1000K, raising the barrier by 0.10eV reduces J to about 0.313 of its previous value.
