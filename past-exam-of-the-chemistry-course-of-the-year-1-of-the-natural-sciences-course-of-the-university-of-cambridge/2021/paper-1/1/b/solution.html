Carbon-13 NMR spectra: 1 = C, 2 = A, 3 = B. Approximate shifts, read from the printed axes, are shown in the annotated structures.
  • C: HC≡C−CH(OCH2​CH3​)2​ has five carbon environments: acetal CH near 91; substituted/terminal alkyne carbons near 79 and 74; OCH2​ near 61; and methyl near 15ppm. One alkyne signal is close to the CDCl3​ solvent region, which must not be counted as a new molecular environment.
  • A: EtOCO−CH=C(CH3​)2​ has ester carbonyl near 167, substituted alkene C near 157, vinyl CH near 116, OCH2​ near 60, two nonequivalent alkene methyls near 28 and 21, and ethoxy methyl near 14ppm.
  • B: CH2​=CH−CH2​−OCO−CH2​−CH2​−CH3​ has carbonyl near 173, vinyl CH near 133, terminal vinyl CH2​ near 118, OCH2​ near 65, acyl-alpha CH2​ near 36, the next CH2​ near 19, and terminal methyl near 14ppm.
The two methyls in A are chemically distinct because one is cis and the other trans to each substituent at the other alkene carbon. Their two measured shifts are secure; assigning an individual methyl to a particular side of the drawn double bond requires an additional stereospecific shift argument or experiment, so their spatial interchange is left explicit.
