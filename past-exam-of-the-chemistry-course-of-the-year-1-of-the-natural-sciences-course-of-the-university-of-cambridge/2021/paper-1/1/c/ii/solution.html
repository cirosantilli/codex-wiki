A, spectrum 4: ethoxy CH3​ at about 1.3 is a NMR triplet, and OCH2​ at about 4.15 is a NMR quartet. The two alkene methyls at about 1.9 and 2.2 each integrate to three protons; they are apparent NMR singlets on the overview but have small allylic NMR doublet splitting. The vinyl CH at about 5.7 is correspondingly a narrow NMR multiplet, formally a quartet of quartets when its two methyl couplings are unequal, or approximately a NMR septet if they coincide. A broadened overview must not be used to declare all those small couplings exactly zero.
C, spectrum 5: the acetal CH at about 5.25 and terminal C≡CH at about 2.55 each integrate to one proton and show their small mutual long-range NMR doublet splitting. The ethoxy methyls give a six-proton NMR triplet near 1.25. The OCH2​ region has two sets near 3.6 and 3.75, each integrating to two protons. These are an ABX3 spin system: geminal coupling and coupling to CH3​ produce approximately one doublet of quartets for each proton type, with second-order distortion in the expanded trace. Treating the whole region as one ideal four-proton NMR quartet misses the printed structure.
B, spectrum 6: the internal alkene CH near 5.95 is a doublet of doublets of triplets from trans/cis coupling to CH2​ and vicinal coupling to OCH2​. The two terminal alkene protons near 5.2–5.35 are distinct doublet of doublets-type signals, with possible additional small allylic splitting. OCH2​ near 4.6 is principally a NMR doublet. The acyl-alpha CH2​ near 2.3 is a NMR triplet, the middle CH2​ near 1.65 an approximate NMR sextet or overlapping NMR triplet of quartets, and the terminal methyl near 0.95 a NMR triplet.
