The most acidic sites are: acetic-acid O–H, pKa​≈4.8; phenol O–H, ≈10; ethanal alpha-CH3​, ≈17–20; propyne terminal ≡C–H, ≈25. These are ordinary approximate organic-chemistry estimates; exact acidities depend on solvent and reference convention.
Acetic acid gives a carboxylate in which negative charge is delocalized over two oxygens through equivalent resonance structures. Phenol gives a phenoxide with delocalization into the aromatic system, but its important contributors are less equivalent and do not stabilize the charge as effectively as a carboxylate.
For ethanal, removal of a methyl hydrogen gives the enolate −CH2​−CH=O↔CH2​=CH−O−. The aldehydic hydrogen is not the preferred acidic site: removing it does not give this alpha-carbon/oxygen delocalization. For propyne, loss of the terminal hydrogen gives an acetylide with a lone pair in an sp orbital. Its greater s character stabilizes negative charge relative to an ordinary sp2 or sp3 carbanion; the methyl hydrogens are much less acidic.
