The ordering SCN−<CNS−≪NSC− reflects different connectivities, bond strengths and charge distributions, rather than different elemental composition. Relative to thiocyanate, the supplied formation enthalpies are higher by 154.8 and 574.0kJmol−1, respectively.
For S−C−N, carbon can make strong C–N multiple bonding with good 2p–2p overlap while keeping its octet. The contributors −S−C≡N and S=C=N− avoid a compulsory positive central atom and place the excess negative charge on terminal heteroatoms.
For C−N−S, an octet description with four bond-order units forces the central nitrogen to formal charge +1. A representative contributor is −C≡N+−S−, with both charge separation and negative charge on less electronegative carbon. The alternative C2−=N+=S does not remove that disadvantage. Connectivity prevents simply transferring the favourable thiocyanate charge pattern to this isomer.
For N−S−C, a central-sulfur octet with four bond-order units gives sulfur formal charge +2. One illustrative contributor is −N=S2+=C2−, which has severe charge separation. Attempts to avoid it by drawing strong expanded-octet multiple bonding at sulfur do not supply the strong, well-matched second-row pi overlap available in C–N bonds. Sulfur 3p functions are larger and less well matched to 2p functions, and high-energy 3d orbitals are not a remedy for this deficiency.
These arguments rationalize the large differences but do not quantitatively predict the supplied enthalpies from electronegativity alone. Real charge delocalization and geometry relaxation matter; formal charges are bookkeeping, not measured atomic charges.
