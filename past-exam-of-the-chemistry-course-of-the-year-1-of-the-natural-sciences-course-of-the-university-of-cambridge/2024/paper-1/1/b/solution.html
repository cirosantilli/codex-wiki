The carbon spectra are A → 5, B → 8, C → 6, and D → 7. Number each acyclic acid or ester chain from its carbonyl carbon. The assignments below account for every carbon-13 NMR environment.
For A, both ketone carbonyl carbons have δ=208.3 ppm and all four methylene carbons have δ=36.5 ppm. Molecular symmetry and rapid conformational averaging explain why six carbons give only two resonances.
For B, written HOOC−CH=CH−CH=CH−CH3​, assign δ(C1​)=172.8, δ(C2​)=118.1, δ(C3​)=147.4, δ(C4​)=129.7, δ(C5​)=140.8, and δ(C6​)=18.1 ppm. The alternation among vinylic chemical shifts reflects polarization of the conjugated chain, especially the deshielded carbon beta to the carboxyl group.
For C, written HOOC−C≡C−CH2​−CH2​−CH3​, assign the carbonyl at 158.5, the adjacent sp carbon at 72.9, the other sp carbon at 92.6, and the terminal methyl at 13.4 ppm. The two methylene resonances are 20.7 and 21.0 ppm. A plausible assignment is C4​=20.7 and C5​=21.0 ppm, but their ordering is tentative from the supplied shift list alone; a correlation experiment would resolve it.
For D, written CH3​CH=CHC(=O)OCH=CH2​, the carbonyl is at 163.2 ppm. The crotonyl beta-CH, alpha-CH and methyl carbons are at 148.0, 121.7 and 18.2 ppm, respectively. The O-bound vinyl CH is at 141.3 ppm and its terminal CH2​ is at 97.4 ppm. The two sides of the ester oxygen therefore produce quite different vinylic environments.
