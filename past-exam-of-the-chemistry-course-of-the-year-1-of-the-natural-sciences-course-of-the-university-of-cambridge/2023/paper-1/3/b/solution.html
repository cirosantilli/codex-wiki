The first product is BrCH=C(CH3​)CH2​CH2​OCH3​, with the original alkene geometry retained. methoxide attacks the primary saturated carbon bearing bromine from the backside. Its oxygen lone pair forms the new C–O bond as the C–Br bonding pair leaves with bromide. The other bromine is vinylic, directly bonded to an sp2 carbon: ordinary SN2 reaction is unfavourable there, and ionization would require an unstable vinyl cation. Thus the primary alkyl bromide is replaced selectively, rather than replacing the vinylic halide or moving the double bond. There is no observable inversion label at the attacked carbon because it is a methylene.
The second product is sodium 4-propoxybutane-1-sulfonate, Na+−O3​S(CH2​)4​OCH2​CH2​CH3​. sodium hydride first removes the alcohol proton, giving propoxide and hydrogen. The propoxide oxygen then attacks the primary ring carbon bonded to the ring oxygen; simultaneous cleavage of that carbon–oxygen bond opens the sultone. The departing ring oxygen remains attached to sulfur as part of the resonance-stabilized sulfonate anion. The carbon attached directly to sulfur is not the substitution site, because cleavage there would not expel the same stable sulfonate leaving group. This is carbon-centred ring opening of a cyclic sulfonate ester, not addition across an S=O bond.
