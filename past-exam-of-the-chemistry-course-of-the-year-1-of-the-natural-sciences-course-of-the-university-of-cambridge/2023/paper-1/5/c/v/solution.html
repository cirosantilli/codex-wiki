Under the steady-state approximation in chemical kinetics, initiation and termination have equal event rates, so their enthalpy contributions cancel. The two propagation steps also have equal flux, and their combined enthalpy is 5−189=−184kJmol−1​ per overall reaction producing two moles of HCl. Step 3 dominates the heat release; the small endothermic contribution of step 2 only slightly offsets it.
An insulated vessel cannot remove this released energy as heat, so its temperature rises as reaction proceeds. In a rigid vessel the precise energy balance uses reaction internal energy and constant-volume heat capacities, whereas a constant-pressure adiabatic balance uses enthalpy; the strong exothermic conclusion is the same. Warming changes the rate constants, and activated initiation and propagation can accelerate, producing positive thermal feedback and potentially a rapid temperature rise. A numerical final temperature or a claim of an inevitable explosion is not justified without composition, heat capacities and heat-loss information. The initially assumed 298-K rate constants cannot remain fixed throughout an appreciable adiabatic temperature rise.
