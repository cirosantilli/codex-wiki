The mechanism is nucleophilic addition followed by elimination of methoxide. An ethoxide oxygen lone pair attacks the carbonyl carbon, while the C=O pi pair moves to oxygen:
MeC(=O)OMe+EtO−⇌MeC(O−)(OMe)(OEt).
(8)
The tetrahedral intermediate collapses when its anionic oxygen reforms the carbonyl and the C–OMe bond breaks with its electron pair going to the methoxy oxygen:
MeC(O−)(OMe)(OEt)⇌MeC(=O)OEt+MeO−.
(9)
lithium is a coordinating counterion; it does not supply an electron pair to the acyl carbon. proton exchange with ethanol connects the methoxide produced to the ethoxide catalyst pool and gives methanol, so the net solvent-mediated exchange is methyl acetate plus ethanol to ethyl acetate plus methanol. The reversibility of the elementary acyl steps is important: changing an alkoxy group is not automatically an irreversible reaction.
