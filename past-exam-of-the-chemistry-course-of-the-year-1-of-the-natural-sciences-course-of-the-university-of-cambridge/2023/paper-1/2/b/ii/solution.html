Two electrons can occupy a fully bonding orbital delocalized equally over all three hydrogen atoms. Three 1s atomic orbitals on an equilateral triangle form one totally symmetric a1′​ combination and a degenerate pair e′ of higher orbitals. In an orthonormal equal-interaction model with diagonal energy α and off-diagonal β<0,
E(a1′​)=α+2β,E(e′)=α−β.
(13)
The lower orbital is proportional to 1sA​+1sB​+1sC​, with constructive overlap along every edge. Its two electrons give a three-centre two-electron bond and equivalent interactions between every pair of nuclei. At the equilibrium distance, this bonding competes with nuclear Coulomb force to favour the symmetric triangular arrangement. The occupied shell is nondegenerate and closed, so there is no first-order Jahn-Teller incentive to distinguish the three edges. A straight-line or localized single-bond description would miss that equally shared bonding pattern.
