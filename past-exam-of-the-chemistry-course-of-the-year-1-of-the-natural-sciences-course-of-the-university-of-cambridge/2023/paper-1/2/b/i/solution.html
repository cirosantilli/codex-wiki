Both dimers have two electrons in a valence sigma-bonding orbital and a formal valence bond order of one. For hydrogen the minimal-basis configuration is (1σg​)2, with the 1s-derived 1σg​ as highest occupied molecular orbital and 1σu∗​ as LUMO. The bonding combination has no internuclear node; the antibonding combination does.
For lithium, include the filled core pair of levels as well as the valence levels:
Li2​: (1σg​)2(1σu​)2(2σg​)2.
(12)
The first two orbitals are predominantly core 1s combinations whose bonding and antibonding occupations cancel in a simple bond-order count. The highest occupied molecular orbital is predominantly σg​(2s), denoted 2σg​; the LUMO is 2σu​, predominantly the antibonding 2s combination but modified by mixing with compatible 2p contributions. The nearby empty 2p-derived sigma and pi levels must also be shown. Only orbitals of matching symmetry mix, so this does not mean that every 2s combination mixes with every 2p combination.
On a common qualitative energy reference, hydrogen 1s is much lower than lithium's valence 2s, while lithium's core 1s levels lie much deeper. The Li valence bonding–antibonding separation is smaller, consistent with its longer and weaker bond. The figure uses a compressed core region and schematic gaps, not measured orbital energies. The role of symmetry-compatible s–p orbital mixing in the first-row diatomics is illustrated in institutional molecular-orbital notes.[ref]
