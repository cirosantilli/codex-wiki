Amine nitrogen binds the carbon end of CO2​; Mg2+ binds through oxygen. The polarized C=O bonds make carbon electrophilic and oxygen electron rich. An amine nitrogen lone pair therefore donates toward carbon, potentially giving a carbamate-like adduct after proton transfer. The hard Lewis acid Mg2+ accepts an oxygen lone pair, either from coordinated CO2​ or from an oxygen of a captured carbamate species. Simply reversing these assignments because nitrogen and oxygen both have lone pairs would put two electron-pair donors together without accounting for the electrophilic site.
The local binding pattern does not by itself determine the full proton-transfer state or whether cooperative chains form; those depend on the framework and amine arrangement. A documented diamine-appended framework shows cooperative carbon-dioxide insertion and ammonium carbamate chain formation, illustrating one possible implementation.[ref]
To regenerate the material, lower the CO2​ chemical potential using reduced pressure or a CO2​-free purge, and, where binding is exothermic, heat the framework to favour desorption. The temperature must remain compatible with framework stability. This reverses adsorption or reversible chemisorption; it does not require permanently destroying the metal–organic framework.
