The two terminal protons are diastereotopic: one is cis and the other trans to c, so they have different shifts and different vicinal couplings. Each terminal proton also couples through geminal coupling to the other and through long-range scalar coupling to the two b protons. When those small couplings coincide, an underlying doublet of doublets of NMR triplets collapses to the observed NMR doublet of apparent NMR quartets. The O-bound protons are enantiotopic and have chemical equivalence in NMR in this achiral environment; they are split by c and by the two terminal protons. The integrated terminal envelope contains two protons, not two equivalent protons at one shift.
