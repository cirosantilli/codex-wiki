Take the bond axis as z. The heavy-atom 2s and 2pz​ atomic orbitals can mix with hydrogen 1s to make three sigma-symmetry molecular orbitals. In the qualitative valence ordering, these are a low predominantly heavy-atom 2s level, a bonding σ level and a higher σ∗ level. The perpendicular 2px​,2py​ orbitals form a degenerate approximately nonbonding π pair because hydrogen has no valence orbitals of the matching sideways symmetry. The filled heavy-atom 1s core is omitted from the diagram.
Counting valence electrons gives
CH:(σs​)2(σb​)2(πx​,πy​)1,NH:(σs​)2(σb​)2(πx​)1(πy​)1.
(9)
Thus both exhibit paramagnetism: CH has one unpaired electron and NH has two in its ground state. For NH, Hund's rules favour separate parallel-spin occupation of the two degenerate π orbitals. The corresponding observed ground terms are X2Π and X3Σ−; the total bond order is approximately one in this simple sigma-bond/nonbonding-pi description.[ref]
The nitrogen-derived orbital energies lie lower than the corresponding carbon-derived ones on an approximate common energy scale, while the isolated hydrogen 1s reference is unchanged. The bonding orbitals consequently have different polarizations and mixing coefficients; drawing identical energies for C and N would miss the screening trend. The figure specifies ordering and occupancy, not measured gaps or a numerical prediction from (a).
