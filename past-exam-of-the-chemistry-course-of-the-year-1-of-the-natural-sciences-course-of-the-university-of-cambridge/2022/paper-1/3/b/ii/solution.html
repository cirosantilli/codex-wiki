 ... Past exam of the chemistry course of the year 1 of the natural sciences course of the University of Cambridge 2022 Paper 1 3 b ii
OurBigBook.com Words: 193
The oxygen substituent changes position through a halohydrin–epoxide sequence. First, hydroxide attacks the acetate carbonyl of BrCH2​C(Me)2​OAc, giving a tetrahedral intermediate. Collapse expels the substrate alkoxide, and proton transfer gives BrCH2​C(Me)2​OH plus acetate. This is saponification, breaking the acyl–oxygen bond rather than replacing bromide directly with hydroxide.
A small equilibrium population of BrCH2​C(Me)2​O− undergoes intramolecular SN2 reaction at the adjacent primary carbon: the O lone pair attacks CH2​Br and its C–Br pair leaves with bromide. The result is 2,2-Dimethyloxirane. Addition of HBr protonates the ring oxygen. bromide then attacks preferentially at the more substituted carbon, where the protonated ring has greater carbocation character, breaking that C–O bond and leaving oxygen attached to the former primary carbon:
BrCH2​C(Me)2​OH ⟶ (CH2​)O(CMe2​) HBr​ HOCH2​C(Br)(Me)2​.
(15)
The middle formula denotes a three-membered epoxide, not an open-chain ether. Acidic opening of this highly unsymmetrical epoxide has the opposite regioselectivity to a simple basic nucleophilic opening at its less substituted carbon.
Finally, the primary alcohol oxygen attacks acetyl chloride. The tetrahedral intermediate collapses with chloride departure, and triethylamine removes the O–H proton and captures HCl. The product is AcOCH2​C(Br)(Me)2​. oxygen is carried through the epoxide to the other carbon; it is not necessary to invent a methyl migration or a carbon-skeleton rearrangement.
