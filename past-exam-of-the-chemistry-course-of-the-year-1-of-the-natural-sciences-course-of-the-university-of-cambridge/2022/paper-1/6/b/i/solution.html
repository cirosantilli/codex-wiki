OurBigBook.com Words: 214
The four terminal methyl groups form ordinary Al–C sigma bonds, with formal bond order one. Each of the two bridging methyl carbons connects the two aluminium centres through a three-centre two-electron bond. One occupied bonding combination extends over Al–C–Al; the other combinations are unoccupied in the simple electron-deficient bridge model. A single shared pair therefore supplies one bond in total across the two contacts, giving formal Al–C bridge bond order 1/2 per contact, compared with one for terminal Al–C.
Each aluminium has approximately tetrahedral, sp3-like orbital directions. A terminal methyl carbon uses sp3-like orbitals for its three C–H bonds and its Al–C bond. A bridging methyl carbon still uses three directed orbitals for C–H bonding, while its remaining approximately sp3 hybrid overlaps with orbitals on both aluminium atoms. This shared bridge avoids placing five independent ordinary two-electron bonds around carbon. The hybrid labels are idealized and the bridge geometry is distorted; the four occupied C–H/bridge orbital directions are not equivalent to five localized carbon bonds.
The total valence count provides a useful check: 2(3)+6(4)+18(1)=48 electrons. Eighteen C–H bonds use 36, four terminal Al–C bonds use eight, and two three-centre bridges use four. A description assigning a full two-electron bond to each of the four bridging contacts would require too many electrons.
