The vinyl ether has an important resonance contributor
CH2​=CH−O−R ↔ −CH2​−CH=O+−R,R=CH2​CH2​Cl.
(6)
Donation from oxygen into the conjugated system increases electron density at the terminal unsaturated carbon, helping explain its unusually shielded δC​=87.4 ppm and its terminal protons near 4.1–4.2 ppm. The directly oxygen-bound unsaturated carbon is strongly deshielded at 151.1 ppm; its proton at 6.480 ppm also experiences the oxygen substituent and alkene magnetic anisotropy. These trends are consistent with the whole delocalized electronic structure; a chemical shift is not simply a meter for an atom's partial atomic charge.
For the saturated part, the stronger local deshielding by oxygen places OCH2​ at δC​=67.8, δH​≃3.948, while CH2​Cl lies at 41.9 and 3.708 ppm. The two terminal vinylic protons have different geometrical relationships to the oxygen substituent and therefore different chemical shifts as well as different vicinal couplings.
