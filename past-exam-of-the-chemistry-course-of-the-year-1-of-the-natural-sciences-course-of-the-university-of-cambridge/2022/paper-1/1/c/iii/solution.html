For the a doublet of doublets, pairing corresponding lines gives Jab​≃300[(6.516−6.468)+(6.492−6.445)]/2=14.25 Hz and Jac​≃300[(6.516−6.492)+(6.468−6.445)]/2=7.05 Hz. The larger value identifies trans vinylic coupling; the smaller identifies cis vinylic coupling. The b resonance confirms the large splitting, approximately 14.25 Hz, and has a small geminal coupling to c.
The small spacings are not perfectly reciprocal if treated as exact First-order NMR multiplets: the b lines imply ∣Jbc​∣≃2.55 Hz, whereas the c lines imply ∣Jbc​∣≃2.10 Hz and Jac​≃6.60 Hz. Thus report Jab​≃14.3 Hz, Jac​≃7 Hz and ∣Jbc​∣≃2–3 Hz, rather than asserting incompatible exact constants. Printed precision and departures from a simple first-order pattern limit this line-picking estimate; a full spin-system fit would be needed for refined values. Splittings alone do not establish the signs of these couplings.
The two methylene groups split one another into approximate NMR triplets, with Jde​≃300(3.727−3.708)=5.7 Hz; the oxygen-bound triplet gives approximately the same value from its rounded line labels. The three vinylic protons plus two methylenes give the required seven-proton integral. The terminal vinylic protons are diastereotopic, because replacing one or the other creates different alkene geometries.
