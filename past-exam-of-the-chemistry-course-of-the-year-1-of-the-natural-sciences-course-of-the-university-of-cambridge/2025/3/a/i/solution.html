The products are hexanenitrile, CH3​(CH2​)4​C≡N, and ethyl triphenylmethyl ether, Ph3​C−O−CH2​CH3​. The nitrile carbon comes from cyanide, so replacing chloride in the five-carbon chain gives a six-carbon product.
For the primary chloride, the carbon-end lone pair of CN− attacks the carbon carrying Cl from the back side while the C–Cl bond pair moves onto Cl. The single concerted nucleophilic substitution step forms the C–C bond and releases Cl−. Attack through the carbon end gives the nitrile, not an isocyanide.
For the triphenylmethyl chloride, the C–Cl bond first undergoes heterolysis to Ph3​C+ and Cl−. An ethanol oxygen lone pair then attacks the carbocation, giving Ph3​C−O+(H)Et. Finally pyridine accepts that O–H proton, with the O–H bond pair returning to oxygen, giving the neutral ether and pyridinium chloride.
