For the outer ns,np valence electrons, Slater's rules predict a modest initial rise down group 15, followed by approximately constant values. Write the effective nuclear charge as Zeff​=Z−S, where S is the shielding constant. Each atom has ns2np3, so the other four electrons in the outer group contribute 4(0.35)=1.40. Electrons in shell n−1 contribute 0.85 each, and those deeper contribute 1.00 each.[ref]

The increasing nuclear electric charge is largely offset by additional inner-electron shielding; once the preceding shell contains 18 electrons, these simple weights give a plateau. The n−1 shell includes its d electrons. This trend concerns outer-shell values, not every orbital: a 1s electron is shielded only by its partner, so Zeff​(1s)=Z−0.30 rises strongly down the group. The larger principal shell also means nearly constant outer Zeff​ does not imply nearly constant atomic size or binding energy.
