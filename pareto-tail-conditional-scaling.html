Pareto tail conditional scaling (X∣X≥u∼Pareto(α,u),u≥β)

 ... Area of mathematics Probability and statistics Probability theory Probability distribution Continuous probability distribution Pareto distribution
OurBigBook.com Words: 186
Conditioning a Pareto distribution on exceeding a threshold changes its minimum while preserving its shape parameter. For u≥β and x≥u, the definition of conditional probability gives
P(X>x∣X≥u)=(β/u)α(β/x)α​=(xu​)α.
(24)
Equivalently, for r≥1,
P(X/u>r∣X≥u)=r−α.
(25)
The ratio to the threshold has the same Pareto distribution at every admissible threshold.[ref] For the chosen α=2, β=10, only 1/4 of outcomes exceed 20, but among those outcomes the probability of exceeding 40 is again 1/4. When α>1, the same identity gives conditional expectation E[X∣X≥u]=αu/(α−1).
This multiplicative scaling differs from the additive memoryless property of an exponential distribution. Indeed Y=log(X/β) has exponential distribution of rate α, because P(Y>y)=e−αy for y≥0. Thus logarithms turn multiplication into addition and explain the scaling property. Conversely, if U has uniform distribution on (0,1), inverse transform sampling gives X=β(1−U)−1/α.
The excess Z=X−u conditional on X≥u has survival function (1+z/u)−α for z≥0. In the generalized Pareto distribution convention P(Z>z)=(1+ξz/σ)−1/ξ, this means ξ=1/α and σ=u/α. Here the excess starts at zero; the original Pareto distribution starts at β>0.
