Mixing length (l)

 Home Physics Branches of physics Fluid mechanics Turbulence
OurBigBook.com Words: 130 Articles: 1
A mixing length is the characteristic transverse displacement over which a turbulent parcel retains its streamwise momentum before mixing with its surroundings. Taylor expansion of a mean velocity profile gives a fluctuation of magnitude l∣Uy​∣. Combining comparable transverse fluctuations with down-gradient Reynolds stress transport gives the closure u′v′=−l2∣Uy​∣Uy​. This is a phenomenological approximation, not a consequence of the Navier-Stokes equation. In a free jet, a prescribed local mixing length must also be compatible with centreline regularity and vanishing outer momentum flux.
