Parabola

 Home Mathematics Area of mathematics Geometry and topology Euclidean geometry Conic section
OurBigBook.com Words: 113 Articles: 1
A parabola is a plane curve whose points are equally distant from a focus and a directrix. In suitable coordinates it has equation y=ax2, with a=0. Its quadratic geometry can also describe a contour deformation through a saddle point.
For example, take a focus of a conic at (1,0) and a directrix of a conic x=−1. Equality of squared Euclidean distances gives (x−1)2+y2=(x+1)2, hence y2=4x. The point (1,2) has distance 2 to both the focus and the directrix. Its eccentricity of a conic is one. Unlike an ellipse, a parabola is unbounded and has only one matched focus-directrix description.
