Spin expectation value (⟨Sz​⟩=ℏcosθ/2)

 ... Branches of physics Quantum mechanics Quantum system Quantum state Observable Expectation value
OurBigBook.com Words: 195
For spin one-half, choose the normalized spin eigenstates ∣+z⟩,∣−z⟩ with Sz​∣±z⟩=±(ℏ/2)∣±z⟩. The spin one-half along an axis pure state in the xz plane is
∣ψθ​⟩=cos(θ/2)∣+z⟩+sin(θ/2)∣−z⟩,0≤θ≤π.
(31)
Its norm is one because the two eigenstates are orthogonal and cos2(θ/2)+sin2(θ/2)=1. Applying the Born rule to a z projective measurement gives
p+​=cos2(θ/2)=21+cosθ​,p−​=sin2(θ/2)=21−cosθ​.
(32)
Consequently the expectation value, second moment, and variance are
⟨Sz​⟩=2ℏ​(p+​−p−​)=2ℏ​cosθ,⟨Sz2​⟩=4ℏ2​,(ΔSz​)2=4ℏ2​sin2θ.
(33)
At θ=π/3, the outcome probabilities are 3/4 and 1/4, the mean is ℏ/4, and the variance is 3ℏ2/16. Every individual outcome remains ±ℏ/2. At θ=π/2, the mean is zero and the variance is maximal, ℏ2/4. At either pole, one outcome has probability one and the variance vanishes.
The diagonal mixed state ρdiag​=p+​∣+z⟩⟨+z∣+p−​∣−z⟩⟨−z∣ has the same Sz​ probabilities, expectation value, and variance as ∣ψθ​⟩. It is nevertheless a different quantum state for 0<θ<π: the displayed pure state has ⟨Sx​⟩=ℏsinθ/2, while ρdiag​ has ⟨Sx​⟩=0. A single component's mean does not reveal all relative quantum phases or establish purity of a density operator.
