Independent increments

 Home Mathematics Area of mathematics Probability and statistics Probability theory Stochastic process
OurBigBook.com Words: 76
A stochastic process has independent increments when, for every t0​<t1​<⋯<tk​, the random variables Xt1​​−Xt0​​,…,Xtk​​−Xtk−1​​ are independent. Adjacent intervals are allowed: each increment represents the change after its left endpoint up to its right endpoint. A Poisson process, Brownian motion, and a random walk with independent steps have this property. Their values at different times need not be independent: for a fair simple random walk on the integer line, Cov(Sm​,Sn​)=min(m,n) because both positions contain the same first min(m,n) increments. Independent increments need not be stationary increments; summing independent steps with time-dependent variances gives a counterexample.
