Klein-Gordon packet velocities (vp​vg​=c2)

 ... Area of mathematics Analysis Partial differential equation Wave equation Dispersion relation Phase velocity and group velocity
OurBigBook.com Words: 325
A useful stipulated dispersive medium obeys the Klein-Gordon equation utt​−c2uxx​+ω02​u=0, with c>0 and ω0​>0. Its positive-angular frequency dispersion relation is
ω(k)=ω02​+c2k2​,vp​=kω(k)​,vg​=ω′(k)=ω(k)c2k​.
(8)
Here ω0​ is the gap at zero wavenumber, not the central angular frequency ωc​=ω(k0​) of a wave packet; the parameter in the preceding formula is A=ω0​/c. For k>0, the phase velocity satisfies vp​>c, the group velocity satisfies 0<vg​<c, and vp​vg​=c2. In this stipulated medium c is the limiting propagation speed of the model; it need not mean the vacuum speed of light. The same square-root dispersion relation appears in waveguide phase and group velocities, with a waveguide cutoff frequency playing the role of ω0​.[ref]
For the original dimensionless choice c=ω0​=k0​=1, a carrier wave has phase velocity vp​=2​, while a narrow wave packet has leading group velocity vg​=1/2​. After time t=8, the carrier's zero-wave phase reference has moved to x=82​≃11.31, whereas the leading wave envelope centre is at x=8/2​≃5.66. A carrier wave crest therefore moves through the wave envelope; it does not carry an identifiable piece of the envelope with it.
The origin-to-curve secant on the dispersion diagram gives phase velocity, while its local tangent gives group velocity. As k→0+, vp​→∞ and vg​→0; at k=0 the phase velocity itself is undefined. As k→∞, both approach c, from opposite sides. On the same positive-angular frequency branch with k<0, both signed velocities are negative and their product remains c2. These conventions distinguish direction from phase speed. Reversing both k and ω represents the same real plane wave; changing just one sign changes its direction.
For an original finite-bandwidth check, take equal-amplitude plane waves at k1​=0.8 and k2​=1.2, still with c=ω0​=1. The exact two-tone wave envelope speed is
vbeat​=1.2−0.81+1.22​−1+0.82​​≃0.703563,
(9)
while its carrier wave has phase velocity (1+0.82​+1+1.22​)/2≃1.421337. Their product is exactly 1: rationalizing the difference gives vbeat​=2/(ω1​+ω2​). Nevertheless vbeat​=ω′(1); the derivative describes the limit of vanishing separation, not an arbitrary pair of tones. The two-tone wave envelope repeats indefinitely, so it has no isolated first arrival and cannot measure front velocity.
