Finite-generator criterion for a natural uniform algebra (⋂j​fj−1​(0)=∅⟹∑j​fj​gj​=1)

OurBigBook.com Words: 117
A uniform algebra is natural exactly when functions with no common zero generate the unit ideal. If a generated ideal is proper, it is contained in a maximal ideal. That maximal ideal is closed: the closure of a proper ideal stays proper, since an element within distance less than one of the identity is invertible by the Neumann series. The Gelfand-Mazur theorem identifies the maximal-ideal quotient with the complex numbers, yielding a character of an algebra. Naturality would make that character a common-zero evaluation, a contradiction. Conversely, the asserted unit-ideal property makes the zero sets of every finite collection in a character's kernel intersect. Compactness gives a point common to all these zero sets; applying this to f−φ(f)1 proves φ(f)=f(x). For C(K), one can directly take gj​=fj​​/∑k​∣fk​∣2.
