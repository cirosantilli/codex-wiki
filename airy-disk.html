Airy disk (θ1​≃1.22λ/D)

 Home Physics Branches of physics Optics Physical optics Point spread function
OurBigBook.com Words: 998 Articles: 3
The Airy disk is the central bright region, up to the first dark ring, in the scalar diffraction pattern of a uniformly illuminated, unobscured circular aperture. The whole pattern includes the weaker surrounding rings; its radius is distinct from its full width at half maximum.
For a circular optical pupil of diameter D under normal, uniform monochromatic plane wave illumination, Fraunhofer diffraction gives
I0​I(θ)​=P(u)=[u2J1​(u)​]2,u=λπDsinθ​,P(0)=1.
(3)
Here I0​ is the on-axis light intensity, θ is the angle from the optical axis, λ is the wavelength in the propagation medium, and J1​ is the order-one Bessel function of the first kind. The paraxial approximation replaces sinθ by θ, measured in radians. The first positive zero j1,1​=3.831705970… gives
sinθ1​=πj1,1​​Dλ​,θ1​≃1.219669891Dλ​≃1.22Dλ​.
(4)
This is the angular radius of the Airy disk; the angular diameter is twice as large. The Airy disk derivation explains the origin of the Bessel function of the first kind and of the numerical coefficient.[ref]
The image and both radial curves show the same calculated light intensity, divided by its peak. The image's logarithmic scale makes the weak rings visible; its colour is a display choice. The linear curve shows how small the ring peaks are compared with the centre. A dark ring marks a zero of wave amplitude, produced by cancellation across the optical pupil; it is not the edge of all transmitted light. The first Airy disk contains about 83.78% of the total radiant flux, as calculated in Airy disk normalization and encircled energy.
For an original design example, take an ideal optical lens in air with focal length f=100mm, circular optical pupil diameter D=5.00mm and wavelength λ=550nm. In the focal plane, the radial coordinate r≃fθ gives
θ1​≃1.34164×10−4rad,r1​≃1.219669891Dλf​=13.4164μm.
(5)
The first-zero diameter is 26.8327μm. Solving P(u1/2​)=1/2 gives u1/2​=1.616339948…, so the diameter measured by full width at half maximum is only
w1/2​=π2u1/2​​Dλf​≃1.028993970Dλf​=11.3189μm.
(6)
Thus a quoted “spot diameter” is incomplete without its definition. Doubling D halves both widths at fixed wavelength and focal length; increasing magnification alone changes the image scale without improving object-space discrimination.
The Airy disk supplies a calculable reference for a point spread function, allowing diffraction to be distinguished from defocus and optical aberrations. Its beauty is that one circular boundary determines an entire sequence of bright and dark rings through a Fourier transform. The ideal formula assumes an unobscured pupil, uniform illumination, ideal optical focus, a single wavelength, and a scalar description with small output angles. A blocked centre, nonuniform pupil transmission or a significant pupil wave phase error changes the response. High numerical aperture may require polarization of an electromagnetic wave and a vector field description. The circular-aperture diffraction laboratory experiment tests the far-field pattern directly; the Rayleigh criterion uses its first zero to define a two-source convention.
