Glucagon

 Home Biology Signal transduction Hormone
OurBigBook.com Words: 371 Articles: 1
Glucagon is a peptide hormone made by alpha cells in the pancreatic islets. Its mature polypeptide has 29 amino acid residues. Alpha cells store glucagon in secretory granules and release it by exocytosis. Glucagon is a message about fuel availability; glucose is the sugar whose supply it helps regulate. A falling blood glucose concentration favors glucagon release, whereas a rise usually suppresses it. Amino acids can also stimulate release, so a meal containing protein need not produce the same hormone pattern as a meal dominated by carbohydrates. [ref]
The principal blood glucose-raising target is the liver. In a hepatocyte, the glucagon receptor is a G protein-coupled receptor: activation of a stimulatory G protein drives adenylyl cyclase, increasing the second messenger cAMP and activating protein kinase A. Downstream phosphorylation, including the phosphorylase kinase relay to glycogen phosphorylase, favors glycogenolysis. Glucagon also supports gluconeogenesis. These are different supplies: glycogenolysis draws on a finite glycogen reserve, whereas gluconeogenesis makes glucose from other substrates and depends on their availability. Longer fasting depletes the glycogen reserve; a stronger hormone signal cannot release material that is no longer there. [ref] [ref]
Do not copy the liver pathway into every target of insulin. Skeletal muscle uses its own glycogen reserve locally; its glucose uptake and the liver's glucose output are different flows. Nor is direct glucagon-stimulated lipolysis in human adipose tissue as firmly established as its action in the liver. Glucagon action depends on the tissue, other hormones, and available substrates. [ref]
There is a useful surprise inside the pancreatic islets: local glucagon can support insulin release from neighboring beta cells. The islet perifusion experiment distinguishes that interaction from glucagon's action on the liver. Thus the two hormones can oppose each other's effects on blood glucose while cooperating locally. Delta cells supply another local brake through somatostatin. [ref] [ref]
