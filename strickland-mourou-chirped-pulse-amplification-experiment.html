Strickland-Mourou chirped pulse amplification experiment

 Home Physics Branches of physics Optics Laser Chirped pulse amplification
OurBigBook.com Words: 291
The 1985 experiment launched 150ps pulses from a mode-locked laser into 1.4km of optical fiber. Dispersion and self-phase modulation produced a broader spectrum and approximately 300ps pulses. A Pockels cell selected a pulse for repeated amplification in neodymium glass, giving approximately 2mJ before the double-pass diffraction grating compressor. Its transmission was 50 percent. The amplified output was reported as 1mJ with a 2ps width limited by the streak camera response. The compressor was adjusted on fiber-output pulses using intensity autocorrelation; that measurement gave 1.5ps. Steve Williamson was acknowledged for help developing the concept, and Philippe Bado and Marcel Bouvier for technical support.[ref]
The crucial comparison is compression before and after amplification. It asks whether enough usable spectral phase structure survives the amplifier, rather than merely whether its output is bright. A response-limited trace cannot establish the true width below the detector's resolution or prove that every higher-order phase coefficient vanished. An intensity autocorrelation is also not a direct unique measurement of the complex electric field. These are limits on the inference, not extra controls attributed to the experiment.
Donna Strickland's 2018 lecture describes setting the compressor with the high-repetition oscillator pulses and making the amplified-pulse measurement with Steve Williamson's streak camera. It explicitly notes imperfect chirp matching.[ref] The first apparatus therefore illustrates the strategy without establishing a universal lossless or exactly phase-matched amplifier.
Donna Strickland leads an ultrafast laser group at Waterloo investigating intense laser interactions and nonlinear optics.[ref] Gérard Mourou's later work includes coherent combination of laser beams in the XCAN project at École polytechnique.[ref] Their complementary research settings connect the original short-pulse experiment with the continuing problems of producing useful energy, peak power and average power together.
