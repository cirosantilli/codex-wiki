Polymer

Words: 299 Articles: 5
Chemistry OurBigBook.com
A polymer is a large molecular chain or network built from many chemically linked repeating units. Statistical chain models idealize its microscopic structure by segment vectors and describe its conformation through distributions, correlations and scattering. A freely jointed chain fixes each segment length while allowing independent orientations; a Gaussian chain replaces the segment or coarse-grained displacement distribution by a Gaussian distribution. These ideal models neglect excluded-volume and other segment interactions.

Flory approximation (F/(kB​T)∼R2/(Nb2)+vN2/Rd)

Words: 66
Polymer OurBigBook.com
The Flory approximation balances the entropic cost of expanding an ideal chain against a mean-field excluded volume interaction. For N segments of length b in d spatial dimensions, minimizing R2/(Nb2)+vN2/Rd gives Rd+2∼vN3b2. This yields the scaling exponents 3/5 in three dimensions and 3/4 in two dimensions. It is a scaling approximation, not an exact numerical free energy or proof of critical exponents; confinement replaces Rd by the accessible coil volume.

Polymerization

Words: 32
Polymer OurBigBook.com
Polymerization joins molecular units into a polymer. For a filament end exchanging units with a solution, addition and removal rates determine growth, while steric constraints and an applied force can modify those rates.

Monomer

A monomer is a molecular unit that can join other units through polymerization. A statistical link in a coarse-grained chain model need not coincide with one chemical monomer.

Deoxyribonucleic acid

Polymer OurBigBook.com
A deoxyribonucleic acid molecule is a nucleotide polymer that stores genetic information. Its double-stranded form is often modeled mechanically as a worm-like chain, with elasticity and effective excluded volume depending on solvent conditions.

Radius of gyration (Rg2​=2N21​∑m,n​⟨∣Rm​−Rn​∣2⟩)

Words: 82
Polymer OurBigBook.com
For N equally weighted positions, the squared radius of gyration is the average squared distance from the center of mass: Rg2​=N−1∑n​⟨∣Rn​−Rcm​∣2⟩, where Rcm​=N−1∑n​Rn​. Expanding pair differences gives ∑m,n​∣Rm​−Rn​∣2=2N∑n​∣Rn​−Rcm​∣2, and therefore
Rg2​=2N21​∑m,n​⟨∣Rm​−Rn​∣2⟩.
(1)
For a Gaussian chain or freely jointed chain with these N positions and mean squared link length b2, each pair variance is b2∣m−n∣. Summing separations yields Rg2​=b2(N2−1)/(6N)∼Nb2/6. Counting all endpoints of N links instead gives N+1 positions and the corresponding finite-size formula.

Counterion

Words: 37
Ion OurBigBook.com
A mobile ion whose charge has the opposite sign to a charged macromolecule, surface or other fixed species. Its local concentration can increase close to that fixed charge; counterion condensation distinguishes a bound fraction that remains localized in an infinite-dilution limit.

Electrochemistry

Words: 57 Articles: 1
Chemistry OurBigBook.com
Chemical systems whose reactions, transport or equilibrium couple to electric charges and electric potential. An electrolyte carries mobile ions; a Poisson-Boltzmann equation can approximate their equilibrium distributions.

Electrolyte

Words: 36
Electrochemistry OurBigBook.com
A medium with mobile ions capable of carrying electric charge. Dissolved salts typically supply both positive and negative species; a counterion-only model keeps only the mobile species required to oppose a fixed charge. Poisson-Boltzmann equations use ideal Boltzmann distributions for those species.

Thermal dissociation

Words: 49
Chemical reaction OurBigBook.com
Thermal dissociation is temperature-driven breaking of a molecule into simpler fragments. The chemical equilibrium abundance depends on pressure as well as temperature: reactions increasing particle number are generally favored at low pressure. In an exoplanet atmosphere, reduced water on a very hot dayside does not by itself establish a low planetary oxygen abundance.

Stoichiometry

Words: 42
Chemical reaction OurBigBook.com
Stoichiometry records reactant and product multiplicities in a chemical reaction or a chemical reaction network. The change caused by one reaction is its stoichiometric vector; multiplying by the reaction rate gives its contribution to the concentration equations. For two identical reactants the rate-constant convention absorbs any combinatorial factor.

Chemical element OurBigBook.com
Beryllium is the chemical element with atomic number four. Its isotope beryllium-7 provides an important route to primordial lithium-7.
Beryllium-7 (7Be)
Words: 47
Beryllium OurBigBook.com
Beryllium-7 contains four protons and three neutrons. It forms in reactions including 3He+4He→7Be+γ and becomes lithium-7 through electron capture. In the early universe its ionization state affects when capture occurs, so the final primordial lithium inventory includes the earlier beryllium inventory rather than just lithium present when nuclear burning ends.

Lithium (Li)

Words: 86 Articles: 1
Chemical element OurBigBook.com
Lithium is the chemical element with atomic number three. Its stable isotopes include lithium-6 and lithium-7. Lithium observed in old stellar atmospheres is an important but processing-sensitive test of Big Bang nucleosynthesis.
Lithium-7 (7Li)
Words: 58
Lithium OurBigBook.com
Lithium-7 has three protons and four neutrons. In Big Bang nucleosynthesis it can form directly or through beryllium-7, which later undergoes electron capture. Competition between direct lithium production, lithium destruction and beryllium production produces a valley in the primordial lithium abundance versus baryon-to-photon ratio. Its measured stellar surface abundance need not equal its birth abundance because stellar transport and nuclear burning can remove lithium.

Nickel-56 decay chain (56Ni⟶56Co⟶56Fe)
Words: 46
Nickel OurBigBook.com
The radioactive chain converts nickel-56 through cobalt-56 into iron-56, releasing energy that can power supernova light curves. Electron capture and, where allowed, beta-plus decay convert protons into neutrons; gamma rays and charged decay products deposit some of the released energy. Ionization and escape of decay radiation affect the observed heating.

Helium-3 (3He)
Words: 46
Helium OurBigBook.com
This stable helium isotope contains two protons and one neutron. In solar proton-proton burning it is an intermediate: two such nuclei can form helium-4 and return two protons, or one can capture helium-4 to enter the second chain branch. The balance between production and destruction determines the helium-3 equilibrium abundance and its helium-3 relaxation time.
Helium-4 (4He)
Words: 30
Helium OurBigBook.com
Helium-4 is the stable helium isotope with two protons and two neutrons; its nucleus is an alpha particle. Its strong binding makes it the principal neutron-containing product of primordial Big Bang nucleosynthesis.

Metallic hydrogen is a high-pressure conducting state of hydrogen. Its fluid form in the deep interiors of gas giants supplies a conducting medium for a planetary dynamo, unlike the iron-alloy conducting cores of terrestrial planets.
Deuterium (2H)
Words: 39
Hydrogen OurBigBook.com
This stable hydrogen isotope has one proton and one neutron. In the Proton–proton chain it is produced by the slow weak proton-pair reaction and is rapidly consumed by proton capture to make helium-3. Its short nuclear lifetime inside a stellar core allows a deuterium quasi-equilibrium in proton-proton burning approximation.

Snowflake
Words: 76 Articles: 1
Ice OurBigBook.com
A snowflake is an ice crystal or an aggregate of ice crystals grown in atmospheric conditions. Its morphology depends on vapour supersaturation, transport, temperature and crystal anisotropy. Not every snowflake is dendritic.
Dendritic growth of a snowflake
Words: 47
Snowflake OurBigBook.com
An exposed tip receives vapour more efficiently than a sheltered surface and can amplify its lead, producing crystal dendrites and side branches. The planar thermal solidification mechanism is an analogy, not a complete vapour-growth model. The hexagonal ice crystal anisotropy selects six preferred directions; an isotropic linear instability alone does not select six arms.

Steady state (chemistry)

Words: 40
Chemistry OurBigBook.com
A steady state has time-independent concentrations because formation and removal rates balance for the species considered. Nonzero reaction fluxes can continue, so a steady state need not be chemical equilibrium. A quasi-steady-state approximation imposes this balance approximately for a fast intermediate while slower species evolve.
