Two-population molar mass example

 Home Chemistry Molar mass
OurBigBook.com Words: 98
Suppose a synthetic polymer population contains three chains of molar mass 10000gmol−1 for every one chain of molar mass 40000gmol−1. Multiplying both chain counts by any common factor leaves the averages unchanged. Substitution into the existing number-average molar mass and weight-average molar mass definitions gives
Mn​=43(10000)+40000​gmol−1=17500gmol−1,Mw​=3(10000)+400003(10000)2+(40000)2​gmol−1≈27143gmol−1.
(15)
The larger chains are only one quarter of the chain count but supply 40000/70000=4/7 of the mass. Thus a weighted mean that emphasizes mass gives a larger result than one that weights each molecule equally. Total mass divided by total chain amount of substance gives Mn​, while Mw​/NA​ is not the mean mass of a randomly chosen chain. Inferring chain counts from size-exclusion chromatography still requires the existing SEC detector response and SEC molar mass calibration assumptions.
