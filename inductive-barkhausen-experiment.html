Inductive Barkhausen experiment

 ... Branches of physics Electromagnetism Bound charge and bound current Magnetization Ferromagnetism Barkhausen effect
OurBigBook.com Words: 459
Heinrich Barkhausen's 1919 report used iron, a 300-turn pickup coil, moving magnet, amplified telephone readout and galvanometer. Removing iron corrected drive pickup. [ref]
A modern primary experiment by Gianfranco Durin and Stefano Zapperi used annealed iron–silicon strips and amorphous alloys. A 60 cm solenoid swept a triangular magnetic field intensity; a 50-turn pickup coil, wound within 1 mm, sensed the specimen. A preamplifier with a 3–20 kHz low-pass filter preceded sampling. Measurements selected the central hysteresis region dominated by magnetic domain wall motion. Above-background threshold crossings defined event durations; integrating between crossings defined event sizes. These are reported procedures, rather than data generated for the diagrams.[ref]
For this stationary implementation, secure a thin ferromagnet and its pickup coil inside a longer drive solenoid, keeping their relative positions fixed. Drive the solenoid with a slow triangular electric current, monitor that electric current, and record the amplified, filtered induced electromotive force with a digitizer. A speaker may make suitable frequencies audible, but the voltage record is needed for quantitative analysis. Slow variation of the drive does not require smooth magnetization. A triangular drive supplies an approximately constant applied-field sweep rate along each ramp; it does not guarantee constant instantaneous dM/dt.
A proposed control sequence makes the inference testable. First record the electronics background with no drive. Repeat the same sweep with the specimen removed, retaining coil geometry: mutual induction from the drive can itself induce a voltage. A matching empty pickup coil wired in opposition can suppress common drive pickup, after checking its matching. Compensation of air flux by an opposed coil is an established method.[ref] Then restore the specimen, repeat both sweep directions, reduce sweep rate, and compare the same hysteresis region with fixed amplifier gain, filter and threshold. Monitor drive irregularities and mechanical vibration; either can generate spurious pulses. These controls are recommendations for the schematic, rather than undocumented controls attributed to the modern paper.
A genuine material contribution should differ from the blank-coil record and change reproducibly with the magnetic preparation and sweep region. Quieting the drive tests drive-correlated pickup, but a held field need not eliminate all delayed material motion. Reversing the sweep is a consistency check on signed changes, not a demand that individual events retrace. A changing pulse count under a different filter or threshold cannot by itself establish changed microscopic dynamics. The useful inference is intermittent collective magnetization change within the instrument's signal bandwidth, with spatial detail left unresolved.
