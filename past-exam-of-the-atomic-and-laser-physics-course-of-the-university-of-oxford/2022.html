In LS coupling, first add the individual orbital angular momenta and spins separately, then add their totals:
L=∑i​li​,S=∑i​si​,J=L+S.
(1)
The Coulomb interaction between electrons must separate different atomic terms more strongly than the spin-orbit interaction mixes them. Then L,S,J,MJ​ are useful quantum numbers, with J=∣L−S∣,…,L+S. This approximation is usually better for lighter chemical atoms and well-separated atomic terms; strong configuration interaction or strong spin-orbit coupling can spoil it. The term symbol is 2S+1LJ​.
The general electric-dipole selection rules, for isolated chemical atoms with definite parity and total electronic angular momentum, are a change of parity, ΔJ=0,±1 excluding J=0↔0, and ΔMJ​=0,±1, with the accessible magnetic sublevels determined by the light's polarization. In pure LS coupling, the additional selection rules are ΔS=0 and ΔL=0,±1, excluding L=0↔0. In a one-electron description the electric dipole operator also requires Δl=±1 for the active electron. These latter statements can fail as exact labels when atomic terms mix; the general parity and total-angular momentum restrictions still apply. Unresolved hyperfine structure is neglected here.
For strontium, the closed core contributes L=S=0. The Pauli exclusion principle makes the two equivalent 5s electrons a singlet state. Different radial orbitals permit both a singlet state and a triplet state. Thus the complete lists of atomic fine-structure levels are
configuration5s25s5p5s6s​terms1S1P,3P1S,3S​levels1S0​1P1​,3P0​,3P1​,3P2​1S0​,3S1​​​​
(2)
The 5s5p electron configuration has odd parity; the other two electron configurations have even parity.

Measure each energy level by the wavenumber E=(E−Eg​)/(hc) in m−1. An upward electronic transition of vacuum wavelength λ adds 1/λ. Treat the quoted wavelengths as vacuum values to their limited accuracy; air corrections would be smaller than the precision justified here.
The strong ground-state absorption must be 5s21S0​→5s5p1P1​ at 461nm. The weak ground-state electronic transition at 689nm must reach 5s5p3P1​: the electric-dipole selection rule on J excludes the other two triplet state energy levels, while weak spin-orbit coupling admixes singlet state character and relaxes the approximate spin selection rule.

The three nearby strong spectral lines then join these energy levels to a common 5s6s3S1​ energy level. The closest components above differ by Δνmin​=μB​B/(2h)≃7.0GHzT−1B. At B=0.1T, their separation is about 0.70GHz. A visible spectral line near 500nm would consequently require spectral resolving power of order ν/Δνmin​≃9×105. An ordinary low-resolution spectrometer would merge the components.
A suitable arrangement places an excited low-pressure strontium discharge or a transversely observed atomic beam between wide electromagnet pole pieces. Collect the emission perpendicular to the magnetic field, select the desired spectral line with a prefilter or monochromator, and send it through a high-finesse scanning Fabry–Pérot interferometer to a detector. A rotatable linear polarizer separates the two π components from the four σ components. Calibrate both the interferometer's free spectral range and the local magnetic field; take zero-field and several field-strength scans, and fit component positions against B.
The magnetic field should be uniform across the viewed volume, with its associated Zeeman splitting larger than the combined natural linewidth, Doppler broadening, pressure broadening and instrumental width, but much smaller than the separations of the relevant atomic fine-structure levels. A collimated transverse atomic beam, low collision rate and restricted collection angle reduce unwanted spectral line broadening. Avoid appreciable power broadening, resolve or model isotope and hyperfine structure, and check for self-absorption. A zero-field scan and the predicted linear scaling help distinguish actual Zeeman splitting components from neighbouring spectral lines.

Let the dimensionful angular momentum operators obey F=I+J and take the interaction Hamiltonian as H′=AI⋅J. Since
I⋅J=21​(F2−I2−J2),
(10)
the energy eigenvalues at fixed I,J are
EF​=2a​{F(F+1)−I(I+1)−J(J+1)},a=Aℏ2,
(11)
where F=∣I−J∣,…,I+J. Subtraction gives the Landé interval rule
EF​−EF−1​=aF.​
(12)
Many atomic conventions use dimensionless angular momentum operators and call the energy-valued constant itself A; then the right-hand side is AF. Each energy level has 2F+1 magnetic sublevels with the same energy in zero field.
For fine structure, the effective interaction is aLS​L⋅S with dimensionless angular momentum operators. It originates in the spin-orbit interaction, including the magnetic interaction of electron spin with orbital motion and its relativistic correction. Within an isolated LS coupling atomic term, the labels are the electron configuration, L,S,J,MJ​, and the interval between adjacent J energy levels is aLS​J. This is smaller than the electrostatic separation of atomic terms.
For magnetic-dipole hyperfine structure, the nuclear magnetic dipole moment interacts with the magnetic field generated by the electrons. Contributions include the Fermi contact interaction of penetrating s electrons, orbital magnetic fields, and the dipolar field of electron spin. Projected onto one electronic J energy level, their rotationally invariant magnetic-dipole part has the form aIJ​I⋅J. The labels are I,J,F,MF​, together with the approximate electronic labels such as L,S. This splitting is generally smaller than fine structure, reflecting the much smaller nuclear magnetic dipole moment. An electric nuclear quadrupole moment can add a different interaction and violate the simple Landé interval rule.
At the larger electrostatic energy scale, an effective two-spin exchange interaction can also be written as​s1​⋅s2​ within fixed orbitals. Its origin is the Coulomb interaction combined with the Pauli exclusion principle, rather than a large direct magnetic interaction. It separates singlet state and triplet state atomic terms; before spin-orbit coupling, useful labels include the electron configuration, L,ML​,S,MS​. General electrostatic atomic term separations need not obey a single Landé interval rule as L varies.

Rabi oscillations require a coherent drive with a stable phase over several population cycles and an isolated, appreciably coupled pair of quantum states. For large-amplitude oscillations, the Rabi detuning must be comparable to or smaller than the Rabi angular frequency. The population lifetime, optical coherence time, transit time and drive coherence time should all exceed 2π/ΩR​ by enough to resolve the cycles. An ensemble also needs sufficiently uniform Rabi frequencies and Rabi detunings that phase averaging does not wash the oscillations out. The usual sinusoidal formula additionally assumes the rotating-wave approximation.
The Einstein coefficients describe incoherent transition rates, rather than the evolving optical probability amplitudes. Their simplest use is appropriate for broadband, randomly phased radiation, with field correlation time short relative to population evolution, or for rapidly dephased chemical atoms whose optical coherences can be eliminated. The radiation energy density per unit frequency must be smooth over the relevant spectral line. Stimulated upward and downward transitions and spontaneous emission then enter rate equations, without predicting coherent population return. A short observation time can give a small excitation probability even for a coherent field; that condition alone does not justify an Einstein coefficients rate description. Coherent laser irradiation can also admit steady rate equations after optical coherence has relaxed, with the appropriate saturation response.

The resonant absorption cross section is of optical-wavelength-squared size, much larger than a typical atomic geometric collision area. Pressure broadening cross sections involve phase-changing interatomic encounters and need not equal hard-sphere collision areas; their numerical value depends on collision partners and speed. No precise ratio follows from the supplied data. A useful distinction is that resonant absorption involves coherent response on the optical scale, so its cross section need not resemble an chemical atom's geometric area.
For homogeneous spectral broadening, all participating chemical atoms must share the same resonance and single-atom response width. In a dilute sample dominated by natural linewidth, the weak-beam requirement is
ΔνD​=λ1​mNa​8ln2kB​T​​≪2πτ1​≃9.95MHz.
(46)
Using the sodium atomic mass, equality occurs near T=0.017K, so T≪17mK is a sufficient thermal condition. Spatial Zeeman energy shifts, light shifts and transit effects should also be small, the laser should address the same selected component, and the interaction should last long enough for steady populations. Uniform impact pressure broadening can also be homogeneous, but would change the width and effective cross section; it is not included in the lifetime-only numerical model.
Real sodium also has hyperfine structure and optical pumping among magnetic sublevels. The inferred σa​ is the effective cross section of the stated gJ​=2J+1 two-level system model, assuming statistical sublevel populations and no loss into unaddressed states. A measurement on a resolved, polarized closed hyperfine structure component requires its actual transition dipole moment and selection rules; a universal polarization-independent sodium cross section cannot be inferred from this simplified model.
