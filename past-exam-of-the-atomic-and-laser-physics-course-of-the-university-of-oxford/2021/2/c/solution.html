The ground electron configuration of caesium has a single 6s electron, with J=1/2. Addition of angular momentum giving F=3,4 fixes the nuclear spin to I=7/2. With dimensionless angular momentum operators, write the Fermi contact interaction as
Hhf​=AI⋅J,EF​=2A​[F(F+1)−I(I+1)−J(J+1)].
(17)
The two hyperfine levels therefore differ by 4A. In the convention μI​=gI​μB​I and gs​≃2,
A=32μ0​​gs​gI​μB2​∣ψ6s​(0)∣2,ω0​=ℏ4A​.​
(18)
This exposes a missing ingredient in a prediction from the supplied data: Z and gI​ alone do not specify the valence electron contact density. Screening effect and penetration of the closed core strongly affect it. If gI​ instead denotes the total nuclear moment in units of μB​, it must first be divided by I in the displayed convention.
An explicitly crude unscreened hydrogen-like ion model uses ∣ψ6s​(0)∣2=Z3/(π63a03​) and gives ν0​≃5.76×1011Hz, or ω0​≃3.62×1012s−1. It describes neither the shielding nor the radial function of neutral caesium. Using a pure unit-charge 6s hydrogen orbital instead gives only 3.46MHz, illustrating why a unique useful estimate cannot follow from an unspecified effective charge.
For physical neutral 133Cs, the independently known zero-magnetic field clock transition is
ν0​=9192631770Hz,ω0​=2πν0​≃5.776×1010s−1.​
(19)
This measured frequency, used to define the second, is supplementary physical information, not a consequence of Z=55 and the rounded gI​ alone.[ref] The direct electronic transition is in the microwave range and is a magnetic dipole transition, rather than a direct optical electric dipole transition. An optical implementation needs a two-field Raman transition.
Using the corrected weak-pulse Ramsey spectrum of part (b), the peak at δ=0 has height (Ωτp​)2. The quantum interference fringes have spacing 2π/T in angular frequency, with zeros at (2k+1)π/T, and the sinc function envelope has its first zeros at δ=±2π/τp​. For T≫τp​, the central peak has approximately full width at half maximum π/T in angular frequency. The clock transition between the two MF​=0 states has no first-order Zeeman energy, although a quadratic one remains.
