For two decays from a common upper energy level, their photon wavenumber difference equals the separation of the two lower energy levels. Treating both quoted wavelengths as vacuum values initially gives
ν1​=193.09107​=51789.32cm−1,ν2​=247.86107​=40345.36cm−1,
(41)
whose difference 11443.96cm−1 is close to 21648−10192=11456cm−1. The only matching lower pair among the supplied energy levels is 1D2​,1S0​, with the shorter-wavelength photon ending on 1D2​. The two independent upper energy estimates are
Eu​=10192+51789.32=61981.32cm−1,Eu​=21648+40345.36=61993.36cm−1.
(42)
Thus
Eu​≃6.20×104cm−1≃7.69eV,1s22s22p3s1P1o​.​
(43)
The electric-dipole selection rules require odd parity, S=0, L=1 and J=1 to connect to both singlet lower energy levels. The apparent discrepancy has a concrete convention explanation. The NIST carbon line table lists 193.0906nm under vacuum wavelengths and 247.8561nm under air wavelengths, reproducing the source's rounded numbers.[ref] The second number must therefore be converted to a vacuum wavelength before using E=hc/λ. For the tabulated upper 61981.82cm−1 and lower 21648.02cm−1 energy levels, its vacuum wavelength is 247.931nm, corresponding to an air refractive index about 1.00030. This removes the spurious 12cm−1 discrepancy. The first calculation alone, which correctly uses a vacuum wavelength, already gives the intended upper energy level to the accuracy of the rounded data. The independent upper value is 61981.82cm−1.[ref]
