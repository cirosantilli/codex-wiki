In the idealized isolated-carbon chemical atom interpretation, both beams carry approximately 2.68eV per incident particle, close to the 1S0​ threshold. However equal incident energies do not imply equal excitation channels.
A single optical photon cannot strongly excite any of the listed even-parity 2p2 energy levels from the even-parity 3P0​ ground state through an electric dipole transition. In particular 3P0​→1S0​ also violates ΔS=0 and the J=0↔0 restriction. The lowest strong opposite-parity excitations lie well above 2.68eV. Thus one expects no strong resonant absorption or corresponding strong atomic fluorescence from these ground-state chemical atoms at this optical wavelength; weak higher-order processes and scattering are separate effects.
Incident electrons can undergo inelastic scattering and collisional excitation. Their orbital angular momentum and exchange with bound electrons allow transfers excluded for a single electric dipole photon. The 1D2​ energy level at 1.264eV and the low fine structure excitations are energetically accessible. One therefore expects scattered electrons with discrete energy losses, possible weak forbidden spectral lines, and collisional or surface relaxation. For example 1D2​→3P2​ has wavelength approximately 107/(10192−43.4)=985nm; spin mixing supplies the weak spin-forbidden radiative channel.
There is a numerical threshold caveat. Literally,
E(1S0​)=21648hccm−1≃2.68401eV,Eγ​(462.6nm)≃2.68016eV,Ee​=2.68000eV.
(44)
Neither quoted beam reaches 1S0​ from the exact ground state if these are taken as exact monoenergetic values. The electron value rounded to two decimal places could represent an actual energy above threshold; if so, 1S0​ is collisionally accessible and its decay to 1D2​ produces the weak electric quadrupole transition at 107/11456≃873nm, followed by lower-level relaxation. These emissions require populations and sufficiently low collisional quenching of a metastable line.
An actual solid carbon foil has bands, collective excitations and surface losses, so it need not reproduce the isolated-chemical atom spectrum or selection rules. Its optical and electron-induced emission cannot be predicted quantitatively from the five atomic energy levels alone. This is the limitation of the foil wording in the source.
