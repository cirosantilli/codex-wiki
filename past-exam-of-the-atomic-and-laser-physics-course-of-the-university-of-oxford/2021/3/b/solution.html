Use the energy-density convention of part (a), a normalized common spectral line profile ∫ϕ(ν)dν=1, and isotropic spontaneous emission. For a narrow spectral line centered at ν0​,
jν​=4πhν0​​n2​A21​ϕ(ν),κν​=chν0​​(n1​B12​−n2​B21​)ϕ(ν).
(26)
The factor 1/c follows from uν​=4πJν​/c: angle-integrated absorption removes cκν​uν​ of energy per volume and time. The emission coefficient is per solid angle, whereas the absorption coefficient has units of inverse length.
The Boltzmann distribution in thermal equilibrium gives n2​/n1​=(g2​/g1​)e−hν0​/(kB​T). Dividing the two expressions and using the relations between Einstein coefficients,
Sν​​=4πc​n1​B12​−n2​B21​n2​A21​​=ehν0​/(kB​T)−12hν03​/c2​=Bν0​​(T).​
(27)
Thus the source function equals the Planck function at the transition frequency, and in the usual narrow-line approximation
Sν​=Bν​(T).​
(28)
With an exactly common finite-width ϕ and constant line-center Einstein coefficients, their ratio is constant across the profile; exact equality to a varying Bν​ at every frequency is only a narrow-line approximation. Exact thermal radiative transfer requires frequency-resolved detailed balance, which supplies the corresponding small profile corrections. This distinction does not alter the intended equilibrium result.
