Specify the radiation normalization first. Let uν​ be radiation energy density per ordinary frequency, with SI units Jm−3Hz−1. Then A21​ is the spontaneous emission rate per upper-state chemical atom, while B12​uν​ and B21​uν​ are the induced absorption and stimulated emission rates, evaluated at a sharp transition frequency. For a finite spectral line profile, replace uν​ by its profile-weighted average.
The Einstein coefficients obey
g1​B12​=g2​B21​,B21​A21​​=c38πhν03​​.​
(24)
Here gi​ are the statistical weights of the two energy levels, and the field is unpolarized and isotropic. The units and physical dimensions are
[A]=s−1,[A]=T−1;[B]=m3J−1s−2,[B]=LM−1.
(25)
The apparently time-independent dimension of B follows because a per-hertz density includes an extra unit of time. If instead B multiplies the mean intensity Jν​, use B(J)=(4π/c)B and A/B(J)=2hν03​/c2. These distinct normalizations must not be mixed in the radiative transfer equation.
