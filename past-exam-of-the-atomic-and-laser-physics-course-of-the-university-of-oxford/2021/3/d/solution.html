In local thermodynamic equilibrium, write the one-ray radiative transfer equation as
Iν​+κν−1​dzdIν​​=Bν​[T(z)].
(31)
Large total optical depth removes the boundary contribution, but an additional local condition is essential: the temperature and opacity vary little over an absorption length κν−1​. Iterating once in that small gradient gives
Iν​=Bν​−κν​1​∂T∂Bν​​dzdT​+higher gradients.
(32)
The integrated Planck function and its derivative are
∫0∞​Bν​dν=πσT4​,∫0∞​∂T∂Bν​​dν=π4σT3​.
(33)
Using the specified Rosseland mean opacity, with κR​ measured here in inverse length, the source's definition Fray​=∫Iν​dν therefore gives
Fray​(z)=πσT4​−πκR​4σT3​dzdT​+higher gradients.​
(34)
The printed leading term σT4 lacks a factor 1/π under that definition. An isothermal optically thick medium is a concrete counterexample: Iν​=Bν​ gives Fray​=σT4/π exactly, while the printed formula gives σT4.
A physical radiative flux requires an angular integral. For direction cosine μ, the same gradient expansion gives Iν​(μ)=Bν​−μκν−1​∂z​Bν​. Integration over all directions gives the net radiative diffusion flux
Fnet​=2π∫−11​μdμ∫0∞​Iν​(μ)dν=−3κR​16σT3​dzdT​.​
(35)
The isotropic equilibrium contribution cancels between opposite directions. For comparison the outward-hemisphere flux alone is σT4−8σT3(dT/dz)/(3κR​) at this order. None of these consistent definitions yields the printed mixture of coefficients.
