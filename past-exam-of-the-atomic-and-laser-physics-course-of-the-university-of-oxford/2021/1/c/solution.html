Write k=e2/(4πϵ0​) and retain the nuclear Coulomb potential. In spherical coordinates, the total potential energy is
V=−rk​+r2[α+(β−α)cos2θ]=−rk​+2αr2​[1−2P2​(cosθ)]
(7)
when β=−α/2; P2​(u)=(3u2−1)/2 is a Legendre polynomial. Treat the added term using quadratic anisotropic perturbation of hydrogen and degenerate perturbation theory in the n=2 hydrogen energy eigenbasis. It preserves parity and m, so it cannot mix 2s with 2p, or distinct 2p magnetic sublevels.
For a hydrogen atom, the hydrogen radial second moment gives
⟨r2⟩nl​=2a02​n2​[5n2+1−3l(l+1)],⟨r2⟩1s​=3a02​,⟨r2⟩2s​=42a02​,⟨r2⟩2p​=30a02​.
(8)
The angular expectation values of cos2θ are 1/3 for an s orbital, 3/5 for p0​, and 1/5 for p±1​, obtained by integrating the squared spherical harmonics. Therefore the five lowest spatial quantum states, counting statistical weight, have first-order energy levels
state1s2p0​2s, 2p+1​, 2p−1​​E to first order−Ry+23​αa02​−Ry/4+3αa02​−Ry/4+21αa02​​spatial multiplicity113​​​
(9)
Each spatial quantum state has two electron spin states when spin-orbit coupling and the magnetic field are omitted. Thus these are five spatial states but only three distinct first-order energies; five distinct energies would be an incorrect reading. The accidental 2s,2p±1​ equality can be lifted at higher order. Quantitatively the added shifts must also be small compared with neighboring principal-shell gaps; α≪k/a03​ is an asymptotic condition, not a guarantee for arbitrary large n.
For general β, the 1s expectation value is
ΔE1s(1)​=(2α+β)a02​,β=−2α cancels the first-order shift.​
(10)
It does not cancel every order. At β=−2α, Vext​=−2αr2P2​(cosθ) couples 1s to d orbitals. For example its angular matrix element with Y20​ is 1/5​, and the 3d radial matrix element is nonzero. The second-order nondegenerate perturbation theory sum has negative denominators, so this is a concrete nonzero higher-order contribution.
There is also a global limitation: for either negative β above, V(0,0,z)=−k/∣z∣+βz2→−∞. A localized wavepacket translated far along z has fixed kinetic energy but arbitrarily negative potential energy, proving there is no true lowest energy level. The results describe local perturbative resonance poles, or a potential completed to a confining form far from the atomic nucleus. No globally exact ground-state cancellation exists for the potential as printed.
