The printed magnetic dipole moment uses dimensionless angular momentum operators: their physical counterparts are ℏL and ℏS. Choose the magnetic field along z. Then the Zeeman energy is HZ​=μB​B(Lz​+gs​Sz​), whose characteristic size is μB​B. In LS coupling, treating this as a time-independent perturbation theory requires it to be much smaller than the relevant fine structure separation, rather than merely smaller than the Coulomb interaction energy.
For a concrete hydrogen atom estimate take the 2p1/2​,2p3/2​ pair. The hydrogen fine-structure correction is
Enj​=−n2Ry​−n3Ryαfs2​​(j+1/21​−4n3​),
(1)
where αfs​ is the fine-structure constant, distinguished from the later potential coefficient. Thus
ΔE2p​=16Ryαfs2​​≃4.53×10−5eV,μB​ΔE2p​​≃0.78T.
(2)
Consequently a suitable weak-magnetic field condition for this pair is
B≪0.8T;B∼10−2T is comfortably perturbative.​
(3)
There is no single threshold for every hydrogen energy level: the relevant fine structure separations decrease with n. The 1s energy level has no same-n spin-orbit interaction partner, so it cannot itself define this comparison. This calculation neglects hyperfine structure; resolving that smaller scale requires an additional weak-magnetic field condition.

Within a fixed L,S,J angular momentum multiplet, the component of L along J follows from 2L⋅J=J2+L2−S2. The corresponding result for S follows by interchanging L and S. For the quantum state ∣LSJMJ​⟩,
⟨Lz​⟩⟨Sz​⟩​=MJ​2J(J+1)J(J+1)+L(L+1)−S(S+1)​,=MJ​2J(J+1)J(J+1)+S(S+1)−L(L+1)​.​
(4)
These are dimensionless expectation values in the convention of part (a). Substituting into the first-order time-independent perturbation theory formula ΔE=⟨HZ​⟩ gives
ΔE=gJ​μB​BMJ​,gJ​=1+(gs​−1)2J(J+1)J(J+1)+S(S+1)−L(L+1)​.​
(5)
For gs​=2, the Landé g-factor becomes
gJ​=1+2J(J+1)J(J+1)+S(S+1)−L(L+1)​.​
(6)
The magnetic sublevels must first be diagonalized within a degenerate multiplet; choosing MJ​ along the magnetic field already does this. For J=0 the displayed fraction is undefined, but all vector expectation values vanish and there is no first-order Zeeman splitting. A higher-order shift may remain.

Write k=e2/(4πϵ0​) and retain the nuclear Coulomb potential. In spherical coordinates, the total potential energy is
V=−rk​+r2[α+(β−α)cos2θ]=−rk​+2αr2​[1−2P2​(cosθ)]
(7)
when β=−α/2; P2​(u)=(3u2−1)/2 is a Legendre polynomial. Treat the added term using quadratic anisotropic perturbation of hydrogen and degenerate perturbation theory in the n=2 hydrogen energy eigenbasis. It preserves parity and m, so it cannot mix 2s with 2p, or distinct 2p magnetic sublevels.
For a hydrogen atom, the hydrogen radial second moment gives
⟨r2⟩nl​=2a02​n2​[5n2+1−3l(l+1)],⟨r2⟩1s​=3a02​,⟨r2⟩2s​=42a02​,⟨r2⟩2p​=30a02​.
(8)
The angular expectation values of cos2θ are 1/3 for an s orbital, 3/5 for p0​, and 1/5 for p±1​, obtained by integrating the squared spherical harmonics. Therefore the five lowest spatial quantum states, counting statistical weight, have first-order energy levels
state1s2p0​2s, 2p+1​, 2p−1​​E to first order−Ry+23​αa02​−Ry/4+3αa02​−Ry/4+21αa02​​spatial multiplicity113​​​
(9)
Each spatial quantum state has two electron spin states when spin-orbit coupling and the magnetic field are omitted. Thus these are five spatial states but only three distinct first-order energies; five distinct energies would be an incorrect reading. The accidental 2s,2p±1​ equality can be lifted at higher order. Quantitatively the added shifts must also be small compared with neighboring principal-shell gaps; α≪k/a03​ is an asymptotic condition, not a guarantee for arbitrary large n.
For general β, the 1s expectation value is
ΔE1s(1)​=(2α+β)a02​,β=−2α cancels the first-order shift.​
(10)
It does not cancel every order. At β=−2α, Vext​=−2αr2P2​(cosθ) couples 1s to d orbitals. For example its angular matrix element with Y20​ is 1/5​, and the 3d radial matrix element is nonzero. The second-order nondegenerate perturbation theory sum has negative denominators, so this is a concrete nonzero higher-order contribution.
There is also a global limitation: for either negative β above, V(0,0,z)=−k/∣z∣+βz2→−∞. A localized wavepacket translated far along z has fixed kinetic energy but arbitrarily negative potential energy, proving there is no true lowest energy level. The results describe local perturbative resonance poles, or a potential completed to a confining form far from the atomic nucleus. No globally exact ground-state cancellation exists for the potential as printed.

Neglect hyperfine structure, spin-orbit coupling and the diamagnetic term, and set b=μB​B, ε=αa02​. The Zeeman energy is b(LB​+2SB​). The electron spin is free, so every spatial result below is duplicated with an added ±b.
For a magnetic field parallel to z, the old magnetic quantum number m remains good and the first-order energy levels are
E1s,±​E2p0​,±​E2s,±​E2pm​,±​​=−Ry+23​ε±b,=−Ry/4+3ε±b,=−Ry/4+21ε±b,=−Ry/4+21ε+mb±b,m=±1.​​
(11)
The two s orbitals have zero orbital angular momentum, so only their electron spin contributes at this order.
For a magnetic field parallel to x, the expectation of Lx​ in each individual pm​ orbital vanishes. One must still check the degenerate m=±1 subspace: its projected Lx​ is zero as well. Thus there is no first-order orbital splitting in a sufficiently weak transverse magnetic field. Spin still contributes ±b.
The transverse orbital Zeeman mixing can be seen without assuming its size. Put E0​=−Ry/4+3ε, Et​=−Ry/4+21ε, and Δ=Et​−E0​=18ε. In the real px​,py​,pz​ orbital basis, px​ is unaffected while py​,pz​ have off-diagonal matrix elements of modulus b. Diagonalizing this two-dimensional Hamiltonian gives the spatial energy eigenvalues
Et​,2Et​+E0​​±(2Δ​)2+b2​.​
(12)
When b≪Δ, their shifts relative to the earlier levels are 0,+b2/Δ,−b2/Δ, with ±b added for electron spin. The displayed square-root expression also handles b comparable to Δ, provided coupling to other principal shells stays negligible. The 2s orbital retains zero orbital correction.
These quadratic orbital estimates keep the enhanced contribution from mixing the split 2p levels. Minimal magnetic minimal coupling also produces e2B2r⊥2​/(8me​); its expectation value is positive and must be added for a full order-B2 calculation. For example it gives e2B2a02​/(4me​) for 1s. It is parametrically smaller than the 2p mixing term when ε≪Ry.

Expand the wavefunction in unperturbed energy eigenstates,
∣ψ(t)⟩=c1​(t)e−iE1​t/ℏ∣1⟩+c2​(t)e−iE2​t/ℏ∣2⟩.
(13)
The cj​ are interaction picture probability amplitudes, so ∣cj​∣2 are level populations and ∣c1​∣2+∣c2​∣2=1 in this closed two-level system. A classical field E0​cosωt supplies the electric-dipole interaction −d⋅E0​cosωt. Choosing the relative phase of the energy eigenstates makes Ω=−⟨1∣d⋅E0​∣2⟩/ℏ real. This is the resonant Rabi angular frequency.
Combining the field's two exponentials with the unperturbed quantum phases produces terms oscillating at ω−ω0​=δ and at ω+ω0​. The rotating-wave approximation drops the latter, leaving exactly the supplied equations with coupling Ω/2. It requires ∣Ω∣,∣δ∣≪ω0​ and a field envelope slow enough on the carrier timescale. Spontaneous emission and dephasing must be negligible during the sequence. For a ground-state hyperfine level transition, the analogous direct drive is a magnetic dipole transition; the same equations hold with the magnetic matrix element replacing the electric one.

c2​​=−2iΩ​(∫0τp​​e−iδtdt+∫TT+τp​​e−iδtdt)+O(Ω3)=−iΩτp​e−iδ(T+τp​)/2sinc(δτp​/2)cos(δT/2)+O(Ω3),​
(14)
where the sinc function means sincu=sinu/u, with value one at zero. Squaring gives
P2​=(Ωτp​)2sinc2(δτp​/2)cos2(δT/2)+O((Ωτp​)4).​
(15)
The printed prefactor is a factor of four too small for the supplied equations and pulse durations. A direct counterexample is δ=0: the exact resonant Rabi oscillation gives P2​=sin2(Ωτp​) for two pulses of duration τp​, because the intervening resonant rotating-frame evolution is trivial. Its small-area limit is (Ωτp​)2, rather than (Ωτp​/2)2. The latter is the single-pulse result before coherent addition.
The condition ωτp​≪1 printed in the source also does not justify the derivation from a physical carrier field. A consistent rotating-wave approximation pulse regime is
ω0−1​≪τp​≪∣Ω∣−1,∣δ∣≪ω0​.​

If the supplied equations are treated as the mathematical model regardless, their weak-excitation integral remains valid for any τp​; subcycle pulses of the original real field require the discarded counter-rotating term and depend on carrier phase. The ground electron configuration of caesium has a single 6s electron, with J=1/2. Addition of angular momentum giving F=3,4 fixes the nuclear spin to I=7/2. With dimensionless angular momentum operators, write the Fermi contact interaction as
Hhf​=AI⋅J,EF​=2A​[F(F+1)−I(I+1)−J(J+1)].
(17)
The two hyperfine levels therefore differ by 4A. In the convention μI​=gI​μB​I and gs​≃2,
A=32μ0​​gs​gI​μB2​∣ψ6s​(0)∣2,ω0​=ℏ4A​.​
(18)
This exposes a missing ingredient in a prediction from the supplied data: Z and gI​ alone do not specify the valence electron contact density. Screening effect and penetration of the closed core strongly affect it. If gI​ instead denotes the total nuclear moment in units of μB​, it must first be divided by I in the displayed convention.
An explicitly crude unscreened hydrogen-like ion model uses ∣ψ6s​(0)∣2=Z3/(π63a03​) and gives ν0​≃5.76×1011Hz, or ω0​≃3.62×1012s−1. It describes neither the shielding nor the radial function of neutral caesium. Using a pure unit-charge 6s hydrogen orbital instead gives only 3.46MHz, illustrating why a unique useful estimate cannot follow from an unspecified effective charge.
For physical neutral 133Cs, the independently known zero-magnetic field clock transition is
ν0​=9192631770Hz,ω0​=2πν0​≃5.776×1010s−1.​
(19)
This measured frequency, used to define the second, is supplementary physical information, not a consequence of Z=55 and the rounded gI​ alone.[ref] The direct electronic transition is in the microwave range and is a magnetic dipole transition, rather than a direct optical electric dipole transition. An optical implementation needs a two-field Raman transition.
Using the corrected weak-pulse Ramsey spectrum of part (b), the peak at δ=0 has height (Ωτp​)2. The quantum interference fringes have spacing 2π/T in angular frequency, with zeros at (2k+1)π/T, and the sinc function envelope has its first zeros at δ=±2π/τp​. For T≫τp​, the central peak has approximately full width at half maximum π/T in angular frequency. The clock transition between the two MF​=0 states has no first-order Zeeman energy, although a quadratic one remains.

The first-order excitation amplitude is the Fourier transform of the temporal pulse envelope evaluated at the Rabi detuning. Two rectangular time windows therefore give exactly the mathematical structure of double-slit interference: each window gives a sinc function amplitude, and their separation gives a relative quantum phase δT. The transition probability is a sinc function squared envelope multiplied by cos2(δT/2).
Pulse duration corresponds to slit width, pulse-start separation to slit-center separation, and Rabi detuning to the transverse wavevector sampled on the screen. The narrow fringes measure the long separation, while the broad envelope measures the short interaction duration. As in spatial interference, adding probabilities instead of coherent probability amplitudes would lose the fringes. Randomizing the relative pulse phase averages the cosine squared to 1/2.

idtd​(c1​c2​​)=21​(δΩ​Ω−δ​)(c1​c2​​).

λ±​=±21​δ2+Ω2​.

Specify the radiation normalization first. Let uν​ be radiation energy density per ordinary frequency, with SI units Jm−3Hz−1. Then A21​ is the spontaneous emission rate per upper-state chemical atom, while B12​uν​ and B21​uν​ are the induced absorption and stimulated emission rates, evaluated at a sharp transition frequency. For a finite spectral line profile, replace uν​ by its profile-weighted average.
The Einstein coefficients obey
g1​B12​=g2​B21​,B21​A21​​=c38πhν03​​.​
(24)
Here gi​ are the statistical weights of the two energy levels, and the field is unpolarized and isotropic. The units and physical dimensions are
[A]=s−1,[A]=T−1;[B]=m3J−1s−2,[B]=LM−1.
(25)
The apparently time-independent dimension of B follows because a per-hertz density includes an extra unit of time. If instead B multiplies the mean intensity Jν​, use B(J)=(4π/c)B and A/B(J)=2hν03​/c2. These distinct normalizations must not be mixed in the radiative transfer equation.

Use the energy-density convention of part (a), a normalized common spectral line profile ∫ϕ(ν)dν=1, and isotropic spontaneous emission. For a narrow spectral line centered at ν0​,
jν​=4πhν0​​n2​A21​ϕ(ν),κν​=chν0​​(n1​B12​−n2​B21​)ϕ(ν).
(26)
The factor 1/c follows from uν​=4πJν​/c: angle-integrated absorption removes cκν​uν​ of energy per volume and time. The emission coefficient is per solid angle, whereas the absorption coefficient has units of inverse length.
The Boltzmann distribution in thermal equilibrium gives n2​/n1​=(g2​/g1​)e−hν0​/(kB​T). Dividing the two expressions and using the relations between Einstein coefficients,
Sν​​=4πc​n1​B12​−n2​B21​n2​A21​​=ehν0​/(kB​T)−12hν03​/c2​=Bν0​​(T).​

Iν​+κν−1​dzdIν​​=Bν​[T(z)].

To derive fermionic Einstein-coefficient relations, first consider an idealized emitter that can exchange one ultrarelativistic fermion with the bath, with zero chemical potential. Let Dν​=d4πν2/c3 be its density of states per volume and ordinary frequency, with d populated helicity states; for one neutrino helicity d=1, whereas the corresponding photon count is two. The Fermi-Dirac distribution gives
fν​=ehν/(kB​T)+11​,uν​=hνDν​fν​.
(36)
Pauli blocking changes the final-state factor in downward emission from the bosonic 1+f to 1−f. Define positive coefficients B12​,C21​ and A21​=C21​hνDν​. The upward rate per lower emitter is B12​uν​, and the downward rate per upper emitter is
A21​(1−fν​)=A21​−C21​uν​.
(37)
Thermal detailed balance, n1​B12​uν​=n2​(A21​−C21​uν​) with n2​/n1​=(g2​/g1​)e−hν/(kB​T), yields
g1​B12​=g2​C21​,C21​A21​​=dc34πhν3​.​
(38)
For example, substituting ehν/(kB​T)=x reduces the balance equation to B12​=(g2​/g1​)C21​ because xfν​=1−fν​.
If one insists on the original additive notation A21​+B21​uν​, its formal coefficient is negative:
B21​=−C21​,g1​B12​=−g2​B21​,A21​/B21​=−d4πhν3/c3.​
(39)
A negative correction means inhibited emission into occupied modes, not a negative total decay rate; 0≤fν​≤1 ensures positivity. The dimensions are those of part (a), and the factor of two relative to the photon density is a helicity count, independent of the statistical sign.
There is a physical qualification: ordinary electronic energy levels of the same chemical atom have the same fermion parity, and cannot interconvert by emission or absorption of a single neutrino. A realizable single-fermion emitter must change the relevant particle-number and parity sector, with matching conservation laws. For ordinary atomic levels, neutrino-pair processes would require a different calculation. A bath with nonzero chemical potential would also require the corresponding chemical-equilibrium population ratio. The calculation above is the intended single-mode statistical analogy under explicit assumptions.

In the idealized isolated-carbon chemical atom interpretation, both beams carry approximately 2.68eV per incident particle, close to the 1S0​ threshold. However equal incident energies do not imply equal excitation channels.
A single optical photon cannot strongly excite any of the listed even-parity 2p2 energy levels from the even-parity 3P0​ ground state through an electric dipole transition. In particular 3P0​→1S0​ also violates ΔS=0 and the J=0↔0 restriction. The lowest strong opposite-parity excitations lie well above 2.68eV. Thus one expects no strong resonant absorption or corresponding strong atomic fluorescence from these ground-state chemical atoms at this optical wavelength; weak higher-order processes and scattering are separate effects.
Incident electrons can undergo inelastic scattering and collisional excitation. Their orbital angular momentum and exchange with bound electrons allow transfers excluded for a single electric dipole photon. The 1D2​ energy level at 1.264eV and the low fine structure excitations are energetically accessible. One therefore expects scattered electrons with discrete energy losses, possible weak forbidden spectral lines, and collisional or surface relaxation. For example 1D2​→3P2​ has wavelength approximately 107/(10192−43.4)=985nm; spin mixing supplies the weak spin-forbidden radiative channel.
There is a numerical threshold caveat. Literally,
E(1S0​)=21648hccm−1≃2.68401eV,Eγ​(462.6nm)≃2.68016eV,Ee​=2.68000eV.
(44)
Neither quoted beam reaches 1S0​ from the exact ground state if these are taken as exact monoenergetic values. The electron value rounded to two decimal places could represent an actual energy above threshold; if so, 1S0​ is collisionally accessible and its decay to 1D2​ produces the weak electric quadrupole transition at 107/11456≃873nm, followed by lower-level relaxation. These emissions require populations and sufficiently low collisional quenching of a metastable line.
An actual solid carbon foil has bands, collective excitations and surface losses, so it need not reproduce the isolated-chemical atom spectrum or selection rules. Its optical and electron-induced emission cannot be predicted quantitatively from the five atomic energy levels alone. This is the limitation of the foil wording in the source.

The finite nuclear size correction perturbation is unchanged for the same lead atomic nucleus. Its leading effect on one occupied 1s orbital is still proportional to that orbital's contact density,
ΔE1s​≃52π​kZ​R2∣ψ1s​(0)∣2.​
(55)
Other electrons screen the nuclear attraction and alter the self-consistent 1s wavefunction, usually reducing its contact density and hence its positive shift relative to the bare hydrogen-like ion. The reduction is modest for a deep 1s orbital: it penetrates well inside most other shells and still samples nearly the full Z=82 attraction. Replacing Z by the neutral atom's asymptotic charge one would be inappropriate for this core orbital.
A neutral lead atom has two 1s electrons. Their contributions add in a frozen-orbital estimate of total atomic energy, roughly twice the per-electron shift, with relaxation and correlation corrections. An experimentally observed transition frequency again depends on the difference between the finite-size shifts of both participating many-electron quantum states. A quantitative neutral-lead result requires a screened relativistic many-electron calculation, not the one-electron formula with a single universally valid effective charge.
