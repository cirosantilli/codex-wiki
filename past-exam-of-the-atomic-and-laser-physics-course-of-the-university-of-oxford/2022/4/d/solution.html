The resonant absorption cross section is of optical-wavelength-squared size, much larger than a typical atomic geometric collision area. Pressure broadening cross sections involve phase-changing interatomic encounters and need not equal hard-sphere collision areas; their numerical value depends on collision partners and speed. No precise ratio follows from the supplied data. A useful distinction is that resonant absorption involves coherent response on the optical scale, so its cross section need not resemble an chemical atom's geometric area.
For homogeneous spectral broadening, all participating chemical atoms must share the same resonance and single-atom response width. In a dilute sample dominated by natural linewidth, the weak-beam requirement is
ΔνD​=λ1​mNa​8ln2kB​T​​≪2πτ1​≃9.95MHz.

Using the sodium atomic mass, equality occurs near T=0.017K, so T≪17mK is a sufficient thermal condition. Spatial Zeeman energy shifts, light shifts and transit effects should also be small, the laser should address the same selected component, and the interaction should last long enough for steady populations. Uniform impact pressure broadening can also be homogeneous, but would change the width and effective cross section; it is not included in the lifetime-only numerical model.
Real sodium also has hyperfine structure and optical pumping among magnetic sublevels. The inferred σa​ is the effective cross section of the stated gJ​=2J+1 two-level system model, assuming statistical sublevel populations and no loss into unaddressed states. A measurement on a resolved, polarized closed hyperfine structure component requires its actual transition dipole moment and selection rules; a universal polarization-independent sodium cross section cannot be inferred from this simplified model.
