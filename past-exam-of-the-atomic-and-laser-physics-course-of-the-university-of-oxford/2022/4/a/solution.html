Define r=g2​/g1​ and let σ=σ21​(ω) be the stimulated-emission cross section, averaged over upper magnetic sublevels. The Einstein coefficients relation g1​B12​=g2​B21​ gives the absorption cross section σ12​=rσ. Assume equally populated magnetic sublevels within each energy level, a steady pump, and a monochromatic beam. The photon flux is I/(ℏω); define W=σI/(ℏω) and the effective population inversion D=N2​−rN1​. Then the rate equations are
N˙2​=R2​−A21​N2​−WD,N˙1​=A21​N2​+WD−τ1​N1​​.​
(33)
The WD term includes both stimulated emission and absorption. In steady state, adding the two rate equations gives N1​=R2​τ1​. The first equation then yields
N2​=A21​+WR2​+WrR2​τ1​​,D=A21​+WR2​(1−rA21​τ1​)​.
(34)
The directed beam gains energy at rate ℏωWD per volume, giving dI/dz=σDI and the optical gain coefficient α=σD. At line centre, write σ0​=σ21​(ω21​). Therefore
α=1+I/Is​α0​​,α0​=σ0​R2​(A21​1​−rτ1​),Is​=σ0​ℏω21​A21​​.​
(35)
Here α0​ is the small-signal optical gain coefficient and Is​ is the saturation intensity, at which the local optical gain is half its small-signal value. Positive optical gain requires rA21​τ1​<1: the lower energy level must empty sufficiently quickly.
The cancellation of τ1​ from Is​ is specific to this model's steady fixed pump and the fact that every upper spontaneous emission feeds the lower energy level. Adding an extra lower-state accumulation factor to Is​ would contradict these rate equations. Alternative conventions using the absorption cross section must replace σ0​ by σ12​/r throughout. Homogeneous spectral broadening permits the whole selected ensemble to share this local saturation response.
