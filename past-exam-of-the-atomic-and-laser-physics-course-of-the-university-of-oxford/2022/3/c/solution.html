Let the upper-state population lifetime be T1​=1/Γ, and take the lower energy level to be stable. Spontaneous emission gives survival probability e−Γt, hence a field or excited-state probability amplitude proportional to e−Γt/2e−iω0​t for t≥0. Its Fourier transform at angular frequency ω is proportional to
∫0∞​e−Γt/2ei(ω−ω0​)tdt=Γ/2−i(ω−ω0​)1​.
(25)
The squared modulus therefore has the Lorentzian line shape
L(ω)=π1​(ω−ω0​)2+(Γ/2)2Γ/2​,ΔωFWHM​=Γ=T1​1​.​
(26)
Here L is normalized over the full mathematical frequency axis. The full width at half maximum in ordinary frequency is 1/(2πT1​). If both energy levels decay, their population decay rates add in the natural linewidth; pure dephasing adds to the amplitude-decay rate instead.
Impact-regime pressure broadening provides another example. Independent phase-randomizing collisions with rate γc​ give optical correlation proportional to e−γc​∣t∣, whose Fourier transform is a Lorentzian line shape of full width at half maximum 2γc​. This assumes short, uncorrelated impacts: a general static distribution of perturbing fields need not give a Lorentzian line shape.
