Equivalently, a complete set of six hyperfine levels gives 2I+1=6 when I<J. This inference assumes that every upper hyperfine level contributes an observed position and the negligible lower hyperfine structure lets coincident transitions be grouped by their upper energy. A line count without these completeness assumptions would not alone fix nuclear spin.
The supplied positions obey the Landé interval rule only approximately. For example, νe​−νi​=4.97GHz, whereas 7(νa​−νb​)/4=4.935GHz. Even allowing each printed position a rounding error of 0.005GHz does not fully reconcile these two relations. A least-squares dipole-only fit has a largest position residual of about 0.017GHz. Small additional hyperfine interactions, measurement uncertainty beyond the displayed precision, or an error in the tabulated data could account for this; it is inappropriate to claim an exact identity. The grouping and nuclear spin are nevertheless robust. Reversing the convention for the printed frequency axis reverses the inferred signs of both constants, without changing the following ratio.
