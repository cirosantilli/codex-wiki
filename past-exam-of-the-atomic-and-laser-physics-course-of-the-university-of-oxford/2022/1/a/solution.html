In LS coupling, first add the individual orbital angular momenta and spins separately, then add their totals:
L=∑i​li​,S=∑i​si​,J=L+S.
(1)
The Coulomb interaction between electrons must separate different atomic terms more strongly than the spin-orbit interaction mixes them. Then L,S,J,MJ​ are useful quantum numbers, with J=∣L−S∣,…,L+S. This approximation is usually better for lighter chemical atoms and well-separated atomic terms; strong configuration interaction or strong spin-orbit coupling can spoil it. The term symbol is 2S+1LJ​.
The general electric-dipole selection rules, for isolated chemical atoms with definite parity and total electronic angular momentum, are a change of parity, ΔJ=0,±1 excluding J=0↔0, and ΔMJ​=0,±1, with the accessible magnetic sublevels determined by the light's polarization. In pure LS coupling, the additional selection rules are ΔS=0 and ΔL=0,±1, excluding L=0↔0. In a one-electron description the electric dipole operator also requires Δl=±1 for the active electron. These latter statements can fail as exact labels when atomic terms mix; the general parity and total-angular momentum restrictions still apply. Unresolved hyperfine structure is neglected here.
For strontium, the closed core contributes L=S=0. The Pauli exclusion principle makes the two equivalent 5s electrons a singlet state. Different radial orbitals permit both a singlet state and a triplet state. Thus the complete lists of atomic fine-structure levels are
