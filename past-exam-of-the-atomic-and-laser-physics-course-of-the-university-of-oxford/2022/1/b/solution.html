Measure each energy level by the wavenumber E=(E−Eg​)/(hc) in m−1. An upward electronic transition of vacuum wavelength λ adds 1/λ. Treat the quoted wavelengths as vacuum values to their limited accuracy; air corrections would be smaller than the precision justified here.
The strong ground-state absorption must be 5s21S0​→5s5p1P1​ at 461nm. The weak ground-state electronic transition at 689nm must reach 5s5p3P1​: the electric-dipole selection rule on J excludes the other two triplet state energy levels, while weak spin-orbit coupling admixes singlet state character and relaxes the approximate spin selection rule.
Assume the usual ordering E(3P0​)<E(3P1​)<E(3P2​), as expected from a positive effective spin-orbit coupling constant. The three nearby strong spectral lines then join these energy levels to a common 5s6s3S1​ energy level. Larger lower-state energy means a smaller gap and longer wavelength, so 679,688,707nm correspond to J=0,1,2 respectively. The remaining 1.12μm spectral line joins 5s5p1P1​ and 5s6s1S0​. Assume the latter is the upper energy level. These assignments and orderings agree with independently tabulated strontium energy levels.[ref]
Starting with the two ground-state gaps, the construction is

Both 461nm and 1.12μm exhibit the normal Zeeman effect. Each connects two singlet state energy levels, with Landé g-factor gJ​=1 for the J=1 energy level and no first-order shift for J=0. Their frequency shifts are 0,±μB​B/h. The weak 689nm spectral line also has three components, but g(3P1​)=3/2 gives shifts 0,±3μB​B/(2h); a three-component pattern alone does not establish the normal Zeeman effect. The 679nm spectral line likewise has three components with g(3S1​)=2.
