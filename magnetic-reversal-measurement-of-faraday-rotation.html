Magnetic reversal measurement of Faraday rotation (θodd​(B)=[θobs​(B)−θobs​(−B)]/2)

Consider an original model at fixed vacuum wavelength 600nm and temperature 20∘C. Stipulate a transparent sample with Verdet constant V=20radT−1m−1, length L=0.050m, uniform adjustable Bz​, and a field-independent analyzer-zero offset θ0​=0.020rad. These are chosen values, not measured glass properties. On a continuously tracked small-angle branch,
θobs​(B)=θ0​+VLB,θobs​(+0.10T)=0.120rad,θobs​(−0.10T)=−0.080rad.
(28)
Taking half the difference gives θodd​=0.100rad; half the sum recovers θ0​=0.020rad. Hence V=θodd​/(LB)=20radT−1m−1. A field-reversal difference of 0.200rad is twice the single-field rotation, not that rotation itself. More generally this subtraction removes every contribution that is even under B↦−B, provided the sample and apparatus remain otherwise matched.
An ideal polarimeter analyzer at laboratory angle a reads normalized light intensity I(a,B)/I0​=cos2[a−θobs​(B)]. Here I0​ is the light intensity immediately before the analyzer, separately calibrated for each condition. A scan locates the maximum at a=θobs​ modulo π. At a=π/4, expansion about zero gives I/I0​=1/2+θobs​+O(θobs3​), with angles in radians. At crossed extinction, the response instead begins as θobs2​ and loses the sign near zero. The favorable 45∘ slope follows directly by differentiating Malus's law; its sign reverses if the angular convention is reversed.
