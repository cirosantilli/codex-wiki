L=−mc21−v2/c2​−eϕ(x,t)+eA(x,t)⋅v.
(1)

∂vi​∂L​=mγvi​+eAi​,∂xi​∂L​=−e∂i​ϕ+evj​∂i​Aj​,γ=(1−v2/c2)−1/2.

(3)

For a uniform constant electric field and zero magnetic field, the mechanical momentum changes linearly even though the velocity does not:
p(t)=p0​+eEt,p0​=mγ0​v0​,γ0​=(1−v02​/c2)−1/2.
(6)
Invert p=mγv using γ=1+p2/(m2c2)​. The result for arbitrary initial direction is
v(t)=1+∣γ0​v0​+(eE/m)t∣2/c2​γ0​v0​+(eE/m)t​.​
(7)
Equivalently, v=cp/m2c2+p2​. This gives v(0)=v0​ and guarantees ∣v∣<c at every finite momentum. The transverse momentum stays constant, but its velocity component generally decreases because the Lorentz factor increases. Thus an initial transverse velocity must not be treated as constant during the electric acceleration; this is the relativistic motion in a constant electric field with transverse momentum effect.

If L′=L+dF(q,t)/dt, the action changes by an endpoint term:
S′[q]−S[q]=F(q(tf​),tf​)−F(q(ti​),ti​).
(28)
In the principle of stationary action with fixed endpoints, δq(ti​)=δq(tf​)=0 and the endpoint times are fixed. Therefore δ(S′−S)=0, and the two actions give the same bulk Euler-Lagrange equations.
For a Lagrangian density, adding ∂μ​Kμ changes the action by ∫∂Ω​Kμnμ​dΣ, by the divergence theorem. Its variation vanishes for compactly supported field variations, or when the appropriate boundary data are held fixed. These total divergences in a variational principle can therefore be discarded when deriving bulk equations under those conditions.
The qualification about boundaries matters: if Kμ depends on field derivatives, their variations on the boundary must also be controlled. A total derivative can change the canonical momenta or the chosen representative of a Noether current, even while leaving the bulk dynamics unchanged.

Let Pμ=∂L/∂(∂μ​h) and Qμν=∂L/∂(∂μ​∂ν​h). Define the second coefficient with ordered repeated indices and Qμν=Qνμ, so that
δL=Lh​δh+Pμ∂μ​δh+Qμν∂μ​∂ν​δh.
(29)
This convention avoids double-counting a mixed derivative such as h,12​=h,21​. The first derivative term needs one integration by parts. The second derivative term needs two:
∫Ω​Qμν∂μ​∂ν​δh=boundary terms+∫Ω​(∂μ​∂ν​Qμν)δh.
(30)
Combining the terms gives
δS=∫Ω​[Lh​−∂μ​Pμ+∂μ​∂ν​Qμν]δh+∫∂Ω​nμ​[(Pμ−∂ν​Qμν)δh+Qμν∂ν​δh]dΣ.
(31)
For compactly supported variations, or fixed δh and its first derivatives at the boundary, the last integral vanishes. The fundamental lemma of the calculus of variations then yields the Euler-Lagrange field equation with second derivatives:
∂h∂L​−∂μ​[∂(∂μ​h)∂L​]+∂μ​∂ν​[∂(∂μ​∂ν​h)∂L​]=0.​
(32)
The plus sign of the final term comes from the two integrations by parts. In general this gives fourth-order equations, although the order in a particular coordinate depends on which second derivatives occur in the density.

With constant ν, the bulk action is invariant under time translations, translations in the two spatial coordinates, and rotations or reflections within the substrate plane. The Laplacian has no preferred direction, giving the planar O(2) symmetry. It also has scalar-field shift symmetry h↦h+a, expressing the arbitrary origin of height, and the discrete symmetry h↦−h because its residual is squared.
There is an additional exact tilt symmetry h↦h+b⋅x: the added slope is time independent and has zero Laplacian. More generally, adding any function f satisfying ft​=ν∇2f leaves the residual unchanged. Such bulk transformations need not preserve prescribed boundary conditions or a restricted physical interpretation of the height.
In two spatial dimensions the action also has the anisotropic scale invariance
x↦bx,t↦b2t,h↦h(b>0).​
(37)
The spacetime measure scales as b4 and the squared residual as b−4. The time and length scales are therefore related diffusively, rather than by a Lorentz transformation.
Ordinary time reversal needs a boundary qualification. The identity
(ht​−ν∇2h)2=ht2​+ν2(∇2h)2+ν∂t​∣∇h∣2−2ν∇⋅(ht​∇h)
(38)
shows that the density is time-reversal invariant up to a total divergence. Thus the bulk stationary equation htt​−ν2∇4h=0 is invariant under t↦−t, although the printed density is not pointwise unchanged. The time reversal of the squared diffusion-residual action exchanges the growing and decaying branches; the selected first-order diffusion equation alone is not time-reversal invariant at fixed positive ν.

Use xμ=(t,x) and Minkowski metric ημν=diag(1,−1), with the wave speed equal to one. Regarding ϕ as a scalar field, the symmetric canonical stress-energy tensor is
Tμν=∂μϕ∂νϕ−ημνL=(Ep​pE​),E=21​(ϕt2​+ϕx2​),p=−ϕt​ϕx​.
(40)
It obeys ∂μ​Tμν=0 on the wave equation. For a scalar field the intrinsic spin current vanishes. The total orbital Lorentz current and its integrated angular-momentum tensor are
Jλμν=xμTλν−xνTλμ,Jμν(t)=∫R​J0μνdx.​
(41)
Because there is only one spatial dimension, its only independent possibly nonzero component is
J01=∫R​(tp−xE)dx=tP−∫R​xEdx,J10=−J01,​
(42)
where P=∫pdx. The corresponding flux is J101=tE−xp. Symmetry and conservation of T give
∂λ​Jλμν=Tμν−Tνμ=0.
(43)
Thus J01 is conserved provided the boundary flux vanishes and the integrals exist. For example, localized wave packets on an infinite line have this property. One can also check directly that d∫xEdx/dt=P, so the two terms in J01 have cancelling derivatives.
The significance is the boost charge of a one-dimensional scalar field. There is no independent spatial rotation or ordinary spatial angular-momentum component in one dimension. Instead, the action has the mathematical Lorentz invariance of a massless wave field with invariant speed cs​=1, and J01 generates its boost. Its conservation says that the center of energy moves uniformly. This is a symmetry of the ideal wave-field model, rather than an assertion of fundamental vacuum Lorentz symmetry for a material rod. Fixed boundaries can break it, and a general solution with nonvanishing boundary flux need not conserve the integrated charge.
