Use metric signature (+,−,−,−) and units with propagation speed one. The scalar Euler-Lagrange equation gives
∂ϕ∂LKG​​=−m2ϕ,∂(∂μ​ϕ)∂LKG​​=∂μϕ,(∂t2​−∇2+m2)ϕ=0.​
(25)
With the stated spatial Fourier convention, −∇2 becomes k2. Each transformed mode obeys
ϕ~​¨​+ωk2​ϕ~​=0,ωk​=k2+m2​,ϕ~​(k,t)=F(k)cosωk​t+ωk​V(k)​sinωk​t.​
(26)
Here F and V are the initial transformed field and velocity. Reality requires F(−k)=F(k)∗ and the same for V; the Fourier convention places no extra (2π)3 in these evolution formulas.
Including the interaction gives the two coupled real Klein-Gordon fields,
(□+m2)ϕ1​−gϕ2​=0,(□+m2)ϕ2​−gϕ1​=0.​
(27)
Adding and subtracting diagonalizes them:
(□+m2−g)ϕ+​=0,(□+m2+g)ϕ−​=0,ω±​(k)=k2+m2∓g​.
(28)
Both squared masses are positive for 0<g<m2. For arbitrary initial data Fi​=ϕ~​i​(0) and Vi​=ϕ~​˙​i​(0), define
U±​(k,t)=(F1​±F2​)cosω±​t+ω±​V1​±V2​​sinω±​t.
(29)
Then the general solution is ϕ~​1​=(U+​+U−​)/2, ϕ~​2​=(U+​−U−​)/2, with the corresponding reality conditions on initial data.
For the specified sinusoidal initial displacement, put Ω±​=q2+m2∓g​. Both normal fields initially have amplitude Asin(q⋅x) and zero time derivative. Consequently
ϕ1​=2A​sin(q⋅x)[cosΩ+​t+cosΩ−​t],ϕ2​=2A​sin(q⋅x)[cosΩ+​t−cosΩ−​t].​
(30)
Writing Ωˉ=(Ω−​+Ω+​)/2 and δΩ=(Ω−​−Ω+​)/2 gives ϕ1​=Asin(q⋅x)cosΩˉtcosδΩt and ϕ2​=Asin(q⋅x)sinΩˉtsinδΩt. This is field-amplitude beating of coupled Klein-Gordon modes. The conserved energy includes the interaction term −gϕ1​ϕ2​; separate uncoupled-field energies need not be conserved, and an envelope zero is not by itself exact complete energy transfer.
