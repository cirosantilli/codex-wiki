Noether's theorem associates a continuous one-parameter differentiable symmetry of the action with an on-shell conserved current. If a first-derivative Lagrangian density varies by δL=∂μ​Bμ, the current is Jμ=L∂μ​ϕ​δϕ−Bμ. The integral of J0 is conserved when the spatial boundary flux vanishes. This connects symmetries to conservation laws; time translation, spatial translation and internal phase symmetry furnish familiar examples.
For the strict fixed-coordinate invariance in the question, Bμ=0. In one space dimension, integration by parts at the density level gives
δL=[Lϕ​−∂t​Lϕ˙​​−∂x​Lϕ′​]δϕ+∂t​(Lϕ˙​​δϕ)+∂x​(Lϕ′​δϕ).
(31)
The bracket vanishes by the field equation. Thus δL=0 yields
∂t​ρ+∂x​Jx​=0,ρ=Lϕ˙​​δϕ,Jx​=Lϕ′​δϕ.​
(32)
In four spacetime dimensions the same identity gives ∂μ​Jμ=0 with Jμ=L∂μ​ϕ​δϕ. In particular, for the usual scalar kinetic term the spatial current has the minus sign inherited from ∂i=−∂i​.
For the quartic density in n dimensions,
Lϕ​=−4λϕ3,L∂μ​ϕ​=∂μϕ,□ϕ+4λϕ3=0.​
(33)
To evaluate the displayed dilation current, abbreviate S=(∂μ​ϕ)(∂μϕ) and D=xν∂ν​. Product differentiation gives
∂μ​Jμ​=2S+(D∂μ​ϕ)∂μϕ+(ϕ+Dϕ)□ϕ−nL−DL=2S−4λϕ4−nL+(ϕ+Dϕ)(□ϕ+4λϕ3)=(4−n)L+(ϕ+Dϕ)(□ϕ+4λϕ3).​
(34)
Here D(S/2)=(D∂μ​ϕ)∂μϕ makes the differentiated kinetic terms cancel, and D(λϕ4)=4λϕ3Dϕ. On shell, the on-shell divergence of a quartic scalar dilation current reduces to
∂μ​Jμ=(4−n)L.​
(35)
Hence this current is conserved for generic solutions in four spacetime dimensions. Special field configurations with vanishing density do not alter the generic dimensional statement.
The subtraction −xμL has a symmetry explanation: for δϕ=ϕ+Dϕ, direct variation gives δL=DL+4L=∂μ​(xμL)+(4−n)L. At n=4 the action is invariant up to a boundary term, so Bμ=xμL. Strict invariance of the density is not required. This is a classical result; no claim about quantum scale invariance is needed.
