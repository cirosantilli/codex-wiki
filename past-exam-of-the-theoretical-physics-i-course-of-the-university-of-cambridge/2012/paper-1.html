The time-independent Lagrangian conserves E=T+V. Substitute x˙=−m2​lϕ˙​cosϕ/M into the kinetic energy to obtain
E=2Mm2​l2​(m1​+m2​sin2ϕ)ϕ˙​2−m2​glcosϕ.​
(6)
This is the angle-dependent effective inertia of a pendulum with a freely sliding support. Solving for the speed gives
∣ϕ˙​∣=m2​l2(m1​+m2​sin2ϕ)2M(E+m2​glcosϕ)​​.
(7)
On a monotone increasing segment, the travel time is therefore
t=l2Mm2​​​∫ϕ1​ϕ2​​E+m2​glcosϕm1​+m2​sin2ϕ​​dϕ.​
(8)
The printed oriented integral assumes that increasing-angle branch. For decreasing motion use ∣dϕ∣ or interchange the limits; if a turning point lies between the two events, split the trajectory into monotone segments and add their positive times. The numerator remains positive for positive masses. At an ordinary turning point the inverse-square-root singularity is integrable; reaching the unstable upright separatrix takes infinite time.

A one-degree-of-freedom canonical transformation requires the Poisson bracket {x,p}X,P​=1. To first order,
{x,p}X,P​=(1+2α1​X+2α2​P)(1+2β2​X+2β3​P)+O(αβ)=1+2(α1​+β2​)X+2(α2​+β3​)P+O(2).
(9)
The product of the off-diagonal derivatives contributes only at second order. Consequently
β2​=−α1​,β3​=−α2​,​
(10)
with α3​ and β1​ unconstrained at this order. The self-brackets vanish identically. This is perturbative preservation of the symplectic form, not exact canonicity of the truncated polynomial map.

Treat all transformation coefficients as O(λ), so products of them with λ are second order. Substitution into the Hamiltonian gives its harmonic part plus
(mω2α1​+λ)X3+(2mω2α2​+β1​/m)X2P+(mω2α3​+2β2​/m)XP2+(β3​/m)P3.

Insert the harmonic variables into the forward map and reduce the squared trigonometric functions. This gives
x(t)=Acosϑ−2mω23λA2​+2mω2λA2​cos2ϑ+O(λ2),​
(15)
p(t)=−mωAsinϑ−ωλA2​sin2ϑ+O(λ2).​
(16)
Indeed p=mx˙ at this order. The second harmonic and mean displacement of a weakly cubic oscillator show asymmetry of the motion: for positive λ the mean shifts towards negative x. There is no first-order frequency shift, although the waveform is no longer purely sinusoidal.
The small parameter is ∣λ∣A/(mω2)≪1. A purely cubic perturbation is unbounded in one direction, so these formulas describe small local oscillations around the stable origin, not globally confined motion at arbitrary energy. Amplitude A labels the transformed orbit and must be inferred using the inverse map if physical initial position and momentum are supplied.

In Cartesian components, ∂L/∂vi​=mvi​+eAi​ and ∂L/∂xi​=evj​∂i​Aj​−e∂i​ϕ. The Euler-Lagrange equation therefore gives
mv˙i​=e[−∂i​ϕ−∂t​Ai​+vj​(∂i​Aj​−∂j​Ai​)]=e[Ei​+(v×B)i​].
(17)
Thus the minimally coupled particle Lagrangian yields the Lorentz force.
For an arbitrary smooth gauge function χ, take A→A+∇χ and ϕ→ϕ−∂t​χ. Both fields are unchanged, while
L→L+e(∂t​χ+v⋅∇χ)=L+edtdχ​.
(18)
The action changes only by an endpoint term, so fixed-endpoint variation gives the same motion. Canonical momentum shifts by e∇χ, whereas kinetic momentum mv and the Lorentz force are gauge invariant. This is the relevant gauge invariance of the classical particle dynamics.

For constant r=R>0, the radial equation requires θ˙(θ˙+2eμ/m)=0. Select the stipulated nonzero angular velocity and define the positive cyclotron frequency Ωc​=2eμ/m. Then
θ(t)=θ0​−Ωc​t,z(t)=Zcos(Ωz​t+δ),Ωz​=2eλ/m​.​

This is a circle in the transverse plane combined with an independent axial oscillation, as in uniform magnetic field with axial harmonic confinement. Viewed from positive z, positive charge rotates clockwise. Its three-dimensional path lies on the cylinder r=R and reverses its vertical motion; it is generally not a constant-pitch helix. For Z=0 it is a horizontal circle. This orbit has pθ​=−eμR2.

For the specified parameter relation,
Ωz2​=m2eλ​=(m2eμ​)2n2,Ωz​=∣n∣Ωc​.​
(24)
Thus there are ∣n∣ axial oscillations per transverse revolution, and the nondegenerate three-dimensional trajectory closes after 2π/Ωc​. Since λ>0, the integer n=0 is excluded; changing its sign changes nothing. More generally commensurate cyclotron and axial oscillations close for any rational frequency ratio, whereas irrational ratios give quasiperiodic motion when both components are present. An integer ratio is sufficient, not the only closure condition. The same frequency argument also applies to transverse cyclotron circles centred away from the chosen cylindrical origin.

Use metric signature (+,−,−,−) and units with propagation speed one. The scalar Euler-Lagrange equation gives
∂ϕ∂LKG​​=−m2ϕ,∂(∂μ​ϕ)∂LKG​​=∂μϕ,(∂t2​−∇2+m2)ϕ=0.​
(25)
With the stated spatial Fourier convention, −∇2 becomes k2. Each transformed mode obeys
ϕ~​¨​+ωk2​ϕ~​=0,ωk​=k2+m2​,ϕ~​(k,t)=F(k)cosωk​t+ωk​V(k)​sinωk​t.​
(26)
Here F and V are the initial transformed field and velocity. Reality requires F(−k)=F(k)∗ and the same for V; the Fourier convention places no extra (2π)3 in these evolution formulas.
Including the interaction gives the two coupled real Klein-Gordon fields,
(□+m2)ϕ1​−gϕ2​=0,(□+m2)ϕ2​−gϕ1​=0.​
(27)
Adding and subtracting diagonalizes them:
(□+m2−g)ϕ+​=0,(□+m2+g)ϕ−​=0,ω±​(k)=k2+m2∓g​.
(28)
Both squared masses are positive for 0<g<m2. For arbitrary initial data Fi​=ϕ~​i​(0) and Vi​=ϕ~​˙​i​(0), define
U±​(k,t)=(F1​±F2​)cosω±​t+ω±​V1​±V2​​sinω±​t.
(29)
Then the general solution is ϕ~​1​=(U+​+U−​)/2, ϕ~​2​=(U+​−U−​)/2, with the corresponding reality conditions on initial data.
For the specified sinusoidal initial displacement, put Ω±​=q2+m2∓g​. Both normal fields initially have amplitude Asin(q⋅x) and zero time derivative. Consequently
ϕ1​=2A​sin(q⋅x)[cosΩ+​t+cosΩ−​t],ϕ2​=2A​sin(q⋅x)[cosΩ+​t−cosΩ−​t].​
(30)
Writing Ωˉ=(Ω−​+Ω+​)/2 and δΩ=(Ω−​−Ω+​)/2 gives ϕ1​=Asin(q⋅x)cosΩˉtcosδΩt and ϕ2​=Asin(q⋅x)sinΩˉtsinδΩt. This is field-amplitude beating of coupled Klein-Gordon modes. The conserved energy includes the interaction term −gϕ1​ϕ2​; separate uncoupled-field energies need not be conserved, and an envelope zero is not by itself exact complete energy transfer.

Noether's theorem associates a continuous one-parameter differentiable symmetry of the action with an on-shell conserved current. If a first-derivative Lagrangian density varies by δL=∂μ​Bμ, the current is Jμ=L∂μ​ϕ​δϕ−Bμ. The integral of J0 is conserved when the spatial boundary flux vanishes. This connects symmetries to conservation laws; time translation, spatial translation and internal phase symmetry furnish familiar examples.
For the strict fixed-coordinate invariance in the question, Bμ=0. In one space dimension, integration by parts at the density level gives
δL=[Lϕ​−∂t​Lϕ˙​​−∂x​Lϕ′​]δϕ+∂t​(Lϕ˙​​δϕ)+∂x​(Lϕ′​δϕ).
(31)
The bracket vanishes by the field equation. Thus δL=0 yields
∂t​ρ+∂x​Jx​=0,ρ=Lϕ˙​​δϕ,Jx​=Lϕ′​δϕ.​
(32)
In four spacetime dimensions the same identity gives ∂μ​Jμ=0 with Jμ=L∂μ​ϕ​δϕ. In particular, for the usual scalar kinetic term the spatial current has the minus sign inherited from ∂i=−∂i​.
For the quartic density in n dimensions,
Lϕ​=−4λϕ3,L∂μ​ϕ​=∂μϕ,□ϕ+4λϕ3=0.​
(33)
To evaluate the displayed dilation current, abbreviate S=(∂μ​ϕ)(∂μϕ) and D=xν∂ν​. Product differentiation gives
∂μ​Jμ​=2S+(D∂μ​ϕ)∂μϕ+(ϕ+Dϕ)□ϕ−nL−DL=2S−4λϕ4−nL+(ϕ+Dϕ)(□ϕ+4λϕ3)=(4−n)L+(ϕ+Dϕ)(□ϕ+4λϕ3).​
(34)
Here D(S/2)=(D∂μ​ϕ)∂μϕ makes the differentiated kinetic terms cancel, and D(λϕ4)=4λϕ3Dϕ. On shell, the on-shell divergence of a quartic scalar dilation current reduces to
∂μ​Jμ=(4−n)L.​
(35)
Hence this current is conserved for generic solutions in four spacetime dimensions. Special field configurations with vanishing density do not alter the generic dimensional statement.
The subtraction −xμL has a symmetry explanation: for δϕ=ϕ+Dϕ, direct variation gives δL=DL+4L=∂μ​(xμL)+(4−n)L. At n=4 the action is invariant up to a boundary term, so Bμ=xμL. Strict invariance of the density is not required. This is a classical result; no claim about quantum scale invariance is needed.

Gk​(τ)=Θ(τ)dk​c2​e−γτsinh(dk​τ)=Θ(τ)α2−k2/c2​e−αc2τ​sinh(α2c4−k2c2​τ).​
(37)
For an explicit time-Fourier route, use Gk​(τ)=∫e−iωτGk​(ω)dω/(2π). Then
Gk​(ω)=k2−2iαω−ω2/c21​,ω±​=−iγ±c2k2−γ2​.
(38)
For k=0, both poles lie below the real axis. Closing above for τ<0 gives zero; closing below for τ>0 is clockwise, giving −i times the residue sum. Since Gk​=−c2/[(ω−ω+​)(ω−ω−​)], this is
Gk​(τ)=ic2ω+​−ω−​e−iω+​τ−e−iω−​τ​(τ>0),

which reduces to the same damped sine or hyperbolic sine above. The k=0 pole on the real axis is interpreted by the retarded limiting prescription, or directly by the initial-value matching above.
For ∣k∣<αc the response is overdamped and nonoscillatory. For ∣k∣>αc, put Ωk​=c2k2−γ2​; analytic continuation gives Gk​=Θ(τ)c2e−γτsin(Ωk​τ)/Ωk​, a real damped oscillation. At equality the finite limit is Θ(τ)c2τe−γτ.
All nonzero Fourier modes decay. The uniform mode instead has G0​=(1−e−2γτ)/(2α) for positive time, leaving a constant temperature increment. At small wavenumber the slow decay exponent is −k2/(2α)+O(k4), so the diffusion limit of the damped heat-transmission equation has diffusivity 1/(2α). The full hyperbolic equation has finite propagation speed c, unlike the limiting parabolic heat equation.

The spatial transform of cos(px) is π[δ(k−p)+δ(k+p)]. Convolution with the impulsive source and the inverse transform therefore give simply T(x,t)=cos(px)Gp​(t−t0​). Take p≥0 without loss of generality and set τ=t−t0​.
For α>p/c, let dp​=α2c4−p2c2​. Then
T(x,t)=Θ(τ)cos(px)dp​c2​e−αc2τsinh(dp​τ).​
(40)
For α<p/c, let Ωp​=p2c2−α2c4​. Then
T(x,t)=Θ(τ)cos(px)Ωp​c2​e−αc2τsin(Ωp​τ).​
(41)
Both satisfy T(t0+​)=0 and ∂t​T(t0+​)=c2cos(px), so the impulse changes the time derivative rather than discontinuously changing temperature. The first case rises and then relaxes without sign oscillations for a nonzero mode; the second oscillates as its amplitude decays. The limiting equality gives T=Θ(τ)c2τe−αc2τcos(px), and p=0 gives the nondecaying uniform increment discussed above.
A cosine source has both positive and negative regions, so this linear solution can be understood as a temperature perturbation about a background; its negative values are not assertions of negative absolute temperature. The temporal oscillations arise from the second-time-derivative term. Dropping that term changes the initial-jump structure as well as removing the underdamped regime.
