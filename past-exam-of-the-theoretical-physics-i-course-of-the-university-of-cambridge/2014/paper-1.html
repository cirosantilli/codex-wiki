Let ψ be the small hoop's absolute spin angle with the same angular sign convention. Equating the two contact-point velocities cancels the large hoop centre's translation. The remaining tangential equality is
aϕ˙​+aψ˙​=2aθ˙,ψ˙​=2θ˙−ϕ˙​.​

dH=∑i​q˙​i​dpi​−∑i​∂qi​∂L​dqi​−∂t∂L​dt.

The assumed relation makes the old and new actions differ by the endpoint term G(tf​)−G(ti​). Fixed endpoint values of both coordinate sets make its variation zero. Therefore stationarity of the old action is equivalent to stationarity of
∫(∑i​Pi​Q˙​i​−H(Q,P,t))dt.

Varying independent Qi​,Pi​ and integrating Pi​δQ˙​i​ by parts gives

The local gauge symmetry contains the constant-phase subgroup. For constant α, δAμ​=0, and Noether's theorem applied to the matter fields gives
αjNμ​​=(Dˉμϕ∗)(−iqϕ)+(Dμϕ)(iqϕ∗)=Jμ.
(38)
Thus ∂μ​Jμ=0 on the matter field equations. More explicitly, allowing α(x) in the matter variation while holding A fixed gives δL=Jμ∂μ​α. On shell it also equals ∂μ​(αJμ), so equality for arbitrary α implies conservation. In the full gauge transformation, varying A cancels the Jμ∂μ​α term; this is the related Noether second-theorem identity, not a new independent charge for every gauge function.

First fix the source at the spatial origin. Fourier transform replaces ∂t​ by −iω and ∇ by ik in the free term. Multiplication by a static background becomes a momentum convolution; the derivative in A⋅∂G acts on the Green function, so it carries the internal momentum k′. Consequently
(k2+m2−ω2)G(k;ω)+2e∫(2π)3d3k′​[ωA0(k−k′)−A(k−k′)⋅k′]G(k′;ω)=1.​
(41)
The transformed point source supplies the right-hand side one. The signs follow from Aμ∂μ​=A0∂t​+A⋅∇ in these conventions.
Source-position qualification. For a nonuniform A(r), the general Green function is not translation invariant. In the printed basis eik⋅(r−r′), its coefficient must also depend on r′, and the convolution contains an extra factor ei(k−k′)⋅r′. The displayed source equation is valid for r′=0, or with the background itself expressed in coordinates centred on that source. Equivalently, a general spatial Fourier Green function has two momentum arguments and a momentum-delta-function source. Staticity alone permits the time-difference transform, not a general position-difference-only transform.

G(k;ω)=−ω2+2ibω+K21​,ω±​=ib±K2−b2​.​

Let τ=t−t′ and Ω=K2−b2​>0. Because ∣e−i(u+iv)τ∣=evτ, close below for τ>0 and above for τ<0. The lower closure is clockwise. The two residues sum to iebτsin(Ωτ)/Ω. Hence, for nonzero b,

a OurBigBook.com
At mean-field level, positive gradient stiffness favours a spatially uniform order parameter. Writing r=∣ϕ∣, minimization of f=ar2+r4/2 gives
ϕ=0(a≥0),ϕ=−a​eiδ(a<0),​
(44)
with arbitrary constant δ. The minimized density is zero above the transition and −a2/2 below it. The order parameter vanishes continuously as (−a)1/2; the first derivative of the minimized free energy is continuous while the second changes. This is the continuous mean-field transition.
The broken symmetry is global U(1) symmetry, not merely the Ising sign symmetry. Selecting one ordered phase breaks this continuous symmetry and leaves a soft phase direction. These are minima of the supplied Landau free energy, not an exact finite-temperature long-range-order claim in one dimension: phase fluctuations invalidate that inference for a short-range one-dimensional system.[ref]

Take the ordered phase selected by a small positive real field, so ϕ is real and positive before sending B→0+. Stationarity gives 2aϕ+2ϕ3=B. Differentiation yields χ=(2a+6ϕ2)−1. Therefore
χ=2a1​(a>0),χ∥​=4∣a∣1​(a<0).​
(45)
Both have mean-field critical exponent one, with a factor-of-two amplitude difference. The below-transition result is the longitudinal response of a selected pure phase. The transverse phase direction is soft, so a transverse zero-field response is singular; an unselected phase average is not the same susceptibility. At a=0, ϕ=(B/2)1/3 and the linear susceptibility diverges.

Set r=ϕ0​ and Ak​=a+ck2+k4. The trial density is Ak​r2+r4/2. At fixed k, its minimum is r=0 if Ak​≥0 and r2=−Ak​ otherwise, with density −Ak2​/2. Minimizing Ak​ uses
dkdAk​​=2k(c+2k2)=0.
(46)
For c≥0, k=0 minimizes the quadratic coefficient. For c<0, the minima are at k=±−c/2​ and have Ak​=a−c2/4. Thus
ordered regionc≥0, a<0c<0, a<c2/4​k0±−c/2​​ϕ02​−ac2/4−a​​​
(47)
Everywhere else the minimum is ϕ=0; at the transition boundary the ordered amplitude tends to zero. The zero-field minimum is respectively −a2/2 or −(a−c2/4)2/2 in the two ordered regions. For c<0 the helical solution is lower than a uniform ordered extremum whenever the latter exists, and lower than the disordered state whenever its amplitude is nonzero. The assumed positive-amplitude ansatz must allow its zero-amplitude boundary to describe disorder.
The phase δ remains arbitrary because the functional has global U(1) symmetry. At nonzero k, a translation shifts that phase; separate translation and phase operations retain a combined unbroken subgroup. The two signs of k also select opposite handedness. The meeting point of the uniform, modulated and disordered mean-field regions is the Lifshitz point (a,c)=(0,0).
