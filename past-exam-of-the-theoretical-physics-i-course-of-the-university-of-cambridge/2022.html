The two continuous, time-independent symmetries are time translation and a common rotation of both angles. Their Noether charges are respectively the total energy and vertical angular momentum:
E=21​I1​θ˙2+21​I2​φ˙​2+2k​[a2+A2+h2−2aAcos(θ−φ)],​
(4)
J=I1​θ˙+I2​φ˙​.​
(5)
For the second symmetry, θ↦θ+ϵ and φ↦φ+ϵ, so the Noether theorem gives the sum of their conjugate momenta. Independent rotations are not symmetries of the spring coupling.
There are also discrete symmetries: simultaneous reflection (θ,φ)↦(−θ,−φ), and time reversal in classical mechanics, which reverses both angular velocities. These leave the dynamics invariant but provide no additional infinitesimal Noether charges. Combined with common rotations, the reflections give the full planar orthogonal symmetry of the angular geometry. If I1​=I2​, interchange of the two angular coordinates is an additional discrete symmetry of the reduced dynamics.
There is a further useful time-dependent symmetry, up to a total derivative, on a continuous lift of the angles: adding the same constant angular velocity, (θ,φ)↦(θ+vt,φ+vt). It changes L by
dtd​[v(I1​θ+I2​φ)+21​(I1​+I2​)v2t].
(6)
Its conserved quantity is I1​θ+I2​φ−tJ​. It is the integration constant for free common rotation, rather than a new globally single-valued angle charge on the configuration torus. The globally defined continuous charges are E and J above.

Interpret ϕ as the small transverse displacement of the elastic beam. The first term is the positive kinetic energy density ρϕt2​/2, with ρ the mass per unit length. The Lagrangian density is kinetic minus potential energy, so the potential density encoded by the other two terms is
V=−2α​ϕx2​+2β​ϕxx2​.​
(18)
For a compressed rod, transverse deflection releases compression energy: the small-slope geometric change in length is proportional to ϕx2​/2, so the applied compressive load lowers the potential by αϕx2​/2. Bending costs elastic energy. At small slopes the curvature is ϕxx​, so positive bending stiffness β gives potential βϕxx2​/2, and hence a negative term in the Lagrangian. For a uniform slender beam β is its flexural rigidity. The competition between destabilizing compression and stabilizing bending is responsible for Euler buckling of an elastic filament.

The dispersion relation shows that an infinite rod has unstable long-wave modes. A finite rod excludes sufficiently long wavelengths, so stability depends on its length and the precise end conditions. The fourth spatial derivative requires two conditions at each end; fixing transverse positions alone does not specify whether slopes are constrained.
First take displacement-fixed ends with free rotation. Setting ϕ(0)=ϕ(l)=0 leaves slope variations free; the bending boundary term in the first variation then gives the corresponding natural boundary conditions on the slope, ϕxx​(0)=ϕxx​(l)=0. The spatial normal modes are sin(nπx/l), and

l<παβ​​: stable oscillatory modes;l>παβ​​: unstable straight rod.​
(25)

The mean-field approximation replaces the fluctuating environment of each microscopic variable by a uniform average field. For spins, write Si​=S+δSi​ and drop products δSi​δSj​ between different sites. The resulting single-site problem can be solved independently, and its average spin must reproduce the assumed order parameter S: this is the self-consistency equation.
It is reliable when correlated thermal fluctuations about the mean are small, for example with many interacting neighbours, long-range interactions or high spatial dimension. Near a thermodynamic critical point the quantitative test is the Ginzburg criterion. For short-range scalar ordering the upper critical dimension is four, with logarithmic qualifications at four; a two-dimensional nearest-neighbour lattice is below this and its asymptotic critical region of a phase transition is not described accurately by simple mean-field theory.[ref]

The coarse-grained order parameter is a real scalar field because the microscopic spin has one component. At B=0, spin inversion symmetry takes m↦−m, so an analytic local Landau free energy contains only even powers. Near a continuous phase transition, the quadratic coefficient changes sign linearly, represented by a(T−Tc​) with a>0. At equality, the repeated temporal root gives (A+Bt)e−t/(2τ)​ for the first field and (C+Dt)et/(2τ)​ for the second. The full two-field system is generically unstable on both sides because of the amplified sector. This is compatible with its autonomous Lagrangian: the total conserved energy is an indefinite quadratic form, rather than a positive energy forcing every component to remain bounded.
