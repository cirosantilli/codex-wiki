For this neutral Coulomb pair in a uniform magnetic field, the reduced Lagrangian gives the canonical momenta
P=MR˙−eB×r,p=μr˙−e∗B×r.
(18)
Invert these relations and take the Legendre transform. The velocity-linear terms cancel against their momentum contributions, giving
H=2M∣P+eB×r∣2​+2μ∣p+e∗B×r∣2​−re2​.​
(19)
There is no explicit time dependence, so dH/dt=0. There is no dependence on R, so P˙=0. The distinction between kinetic momentum and magnetic pseudomomentum matters: the conserved vector is P, not generally MR˙. The mechanical centre-of-mass momentum MR˙=P+eB×r generally varies as the separation changes. A neutral pair can therefore have accelerated centre-of-mass motion in a magnetic field.

For a real scalar triplet, with metric signature (+−−−), differentiating the Lagrangian density gives ∂L/∂ϕa​=−λϕa​ and ∂L/∂(∂μ​ϕa​)=∂μϕa​. Hence
(□+λ)ϕa​=0,∂t2​ϕa​−∇2ϕa​+λϕa​=0.​
(33)
For a constant internal rotation parameter, the variation is
δL=θϵabc​nb​[(∂μ​ϕa​)(∂μϕc​)−λϕa​ϕc​]=0.
(34)
Each bracketed tensor is symmetric in a,c, so its contraction with the antisymmetric symbol vanishes. This rotates the three field components, not the space coordinates; both the kinetic term and common mass term are invariant.
For a first-principles Noether current derivation, temporarily let θ depend on spacetime. The terms proportional to θ still cancel, leaving
δL=jμ(n)∂μ​θ,jμ(n)=∂μϕa​ϵabc​nb​ϕc​.
(35)
On a solution of the field equations, the action variation for a compactly supported θ vanishes after integration by parts. Thus ∂μ​jμ=0. To match the charges printed in the question, use the equivalent overall sign convention for the SO(3) Noether current of a real scalar triplet
Jaμ​=ϵabc​(∂μϕb​)ϕc​,jμ(n)=−na​Jaμ​.​
(36)
Arbitrariness of n gives three continuity equations. Their charges are exactly
Qa​=∫d3xJa0​=∫d3xϵabc​ϕ˙​b​ϕc​.​
(37)
An overall reversal of all currents is immaterial; the minus sign above keeps the generator convention distinct from the supplied charge convention.
Direct verification is also short:
Q˙​a​=∫d3xϵabc​[(∇2ϕb​−λϕb​)ϕc​+ϕ˙​b​ϕ˙​c​]=∫∂V​dSϵabc​(∂n​ϕb​)ϕc​.
(38)
The mass term and the products of equal-type derivatives cancel by antisymmetry; integration by parts produces the boundary term. The charges are conserved when these integrals exist and the net boundary flux vanishes: sufficiently decaying fields on all space, periodic boundary conditions, or suitable symmetry-preserving no-flux boundaries suffice. Arbitrary open boundaries do not. A common constant λ is necessary for this internal symmetry; its positivity is a stability issue, not needed for the algebraic conservation proof.

The expansion is a local phenomenological description in a small scalar order parameter near a transition. Its coefficients summarize microscopic physics and generally depend smoothly on temperature. At zero field, spin-reversal Z2 symmetry requires even powers of magnetization; the conjugate field supplies the term −hm. The additive F0​ does not affect minimization with respect to m.
For the ordinary continuous-transition quartic model, b>0 ensures stability, and the quadratic coefficient changes sign, a=a0​t+O(t2) with a0​>0. If b vanishes or is negative, stabilizing higher powers are required and the phase structure can change. Treating one uniform magnetization neglects spatial gradients, fluctuations and domains; the resulting exponents are predictions of mean-field theory.

Assume b>0 and temperature-independent d for the local model. The zero-field stationary points obey
m(a+dm+bm2)=0,m±​=2b−d±d2−4ab​​.
(47)
For d=0, the favoured ordered sign is opposite to d. The nonzero extrema first appear at a=d2/(4b); their existence is not yet a coexistence condition. To find the equilibrium transition, require both F′(m∗​)=0 and F(m∗​)=F(0). Substituting a=−dm∗​−bm∗2​ into the free energy gives
F(m∗​)−F0​=−6d​m∗3​−4b​m∗4​.
(48)
Hence
m∗​=−3b2d​,a∗​=9b2d2​>0.​
(49)
At coexistence the entire potential factorizes:
F−F0​=4b​m2(m+3b2d​)2.
(50)
It has equal minima at zero and m∗​ separated by a barrier. Every fixed nonzero cubic coefficient produces a first-order transition in this quartic model, with a finite magnetization jump before the quadratic coefficient reaches zero.
For d2/(4b)>a>a∗​, the ordered minimum is metastable and zero is globally stable. For a∗​>a>0, the ordered state is globally stable and zero is metastable. At a=0 the zero minimum loses local stability. Thus the spinodals are a=d2/(4b) and a=0, distinct from a∗​. For a<0, the opposite-sign nonzero state can also be a local minimum, but the cubic term favours the state of sign −d.
If a=a0​(T−Tc​)/Tc​, the transition is shifted to T∗​=Tc​[1+2d2/(9ba0​)]. With b,d constant and a′(T)>0, the entropy jump from the disordered to ordered phase is −a′(T∗​)m∗2​/2, giving a latent heat of magnitude T∗​a′(T∗​)m∗2​/2. As d→0, the jump and shift vanish and the original continuous transition is recovered.
A genuine zero-field uniaxial ferromagnet with exact spin-reversal Z2 symmetry forbids this cubic term. The modified model therefore presumes that symmetry has been changed; it must not be presented as a symmetry-allowed correction for the original ferromagnet.
