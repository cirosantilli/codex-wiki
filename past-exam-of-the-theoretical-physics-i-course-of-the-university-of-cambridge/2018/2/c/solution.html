The support displacement and pendulum angle oscillate in phase. Inserting this relation into either Euler-Lagrange equation yields
ω2(1+ω12​ω02​​)=ω02​,

ω=ω02​+ω12​​ω0​ω1​​=mg+kℓgk​​,ω21​=gℓ​+km​.​

The negative frequency describes the same real oscillation after a phase change. For a general phase, replace the sine in both coordinates by Acos(ωt)+Bsin(ωt).
There is no missing second finite branch. The quadratic Lagrangian is
L2​=21​(η˙​+θ˙)2−21​ω12​η2−21​ω02​θ2,
(26)
whose kinetic matrix is (11​11​) and has rank one. Its characteristic determinant is ω02​ω12​−(ω02​+ω12​)ω2, not a quadratic polynomial in ω2. This singular kinetic matrix in small oscillations results from taking the support mass to be exactly zero. Adding a small support mass restores a second mode that runs to infinite frequency as that mass vanishes.
For a stiff spring,
k→∞:η→0,ω→g/ℓ​.​
(27)
The hinge becomes fixed, recovering the simple pendulum frequency, as expected for the pendulum with a massless spring-mounted support. At finite k, horizontal support motion makes the frequency smaller.
