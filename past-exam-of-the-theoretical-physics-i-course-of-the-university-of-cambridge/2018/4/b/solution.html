Use xμ=(t,x) and Minkowski metric ημν=diag(1,−1), with the wave speed equal to one. Regarding ϕ as a scalar field, the symmetric canonical stress-energy tensor is
Tμν=∂μϕ∂νϕ−ημνL=(Ep​pE​),E=21​(ϕt2​+ϕx2​),p=−ϕt​ϕx​.
(40)
It obeys ∂μ​Tμν=0 on the wave equation. For a scalar field the intrinsic spin current vanishes. The total orbital Lorentz current and its integrated angular-momentum tensor are
Jλμν=xμTλν−xνTλμ,Jμν(t)=∫R​J0μνdx.​
(41)
Because there is only one spatial dimension, its only independent possibly nonzero component is
J01=∫R​(tp−xE)dx=tP−∫R​xEdx,J10=−J01,​
(42)
where P=∫pdx. The corresponding flux is J101=tE−xp. Symmetry and conservation of T give
∂λ​Jλμν=Tμν−Tνμ=0.
(43)
Thus J01 is conserved provided the boundary flux vanishes and the integrals exist. For example, localized wave packets on an infinite line have this property. One can also check directly that d∫xEdx/dt=P, so the two terms in J01 have cancelling derivatives.
The significance is the boost charge of a one-dimensional scalar field. There is no independent spatial rotation or ordinary spatial angular-momentum component in one dimension. Instead, the action has the mathematical Lorentz invariance of a massless wave field with invariant speed cs​=1, and J01 generates its boost. Its conservation says that the center of energy moves uniformly. This is a symmetry of the ideal wave-field model, rather than an assertion of fundamental vacuum Lorentz symmetry for a material rod. Fixed boundaries can break it, and a general solution with nonvanishing boundary flux need not conserve the integrated charge.
