Write ρ for cylindrical radius in this question, not the rod's mass density. A charged relativistic particle in a time-independent magnetic field has the charged-particle electromagnetic Lagrangian
L=−mc21−v2/c2​+qA⋅v,
(18)
with zero electrostatic potential. The cylindrical speed is v2=ρ˙​2+ρ2θ˙2+z˙2. Inside a uniform axial field, choose the symmetric magnetic vector potential Aθ​=Bρ/2, Aρ​=Az​=0. Its cylindrical curl gives Bz​=ρ−1∂ρ​(ρAθ​)=B. Hence

The piecewise Lagrangian is its hard-edge limit and retains fringe impulses through the jump in Aθ​. Those impulses are essential to the focusing calculation.

Causal support and contour conditions
Words: 140
Solution OurBigBook.com
A retarded Green function obeys G(τ)=0 for τ<0 and
(∂τ2​+1−ε∂τ3​)G(τ)=δ(τ).
(49)
For a bounded or suitably tempered response represented by the real-axis Fourier integral, analyticity in the upper frequency half-plane is required, with suitable behaviour at infinity. For negative τ, e−iωτ decays on a large upper semicircle. If that half-plane has no singularities, contour closure gives zero response before the source. Lower-half-plane poles give factors decaying for positive time. Real poles are approached from below for a retarded prescription; at ε=0, for example, GR​(τ)=Θ(τ)sinτ.
The causality versus stability of a Green function distinction matters: an unstable initial-value problem can still have a strictly causal response that grows exponentially. Such a response has no ordinary real-axis Fourier transform. Its inverse Laplace transform must run on a frequency contour above its growth poles. Thus “upper poles violate causality” is correct only with the stable/tempered real-axis Fourier assumptions, not as an unconditional theorem about differential equations.

Substitute ω=α3​/ε+⋯ into the cubic denominator. Its leading terms are

For physical radiative sign ε>0, the fast pole lies high in the upper half-plane. Its homogeneous mode is e−iω3​t≃et/ε, a runaway. There are two distinct boundary choices. A strictly causal initial-value Green function exists and includes the growing mode for ε>0; it must be represented by a Laplace/Bromwich contour above that pole rather than the stated ordinary Fourier integral.
