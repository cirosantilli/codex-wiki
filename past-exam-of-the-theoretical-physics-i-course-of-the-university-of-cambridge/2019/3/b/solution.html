Treat ψ,ψ∗ independently in the Legendre transform in mechanics. Their canonical momenta are
πψ​=2iℏ​ψ∗,πψ∗​=−2iℏ​ψ.
(33)
The time-derivative terms cancel in πψ​ψ˙​+πψ∗​ψ˙​∗−L, giving the Hamiltonian density
H=2Mℏ2​∣∇ψ∣2−μ∣ψ∣2+2g​∣ψ∣4.​

The first-order time dependence makes the momenta constrained; this direct calculation still gives the canonical density. In physical terms this is the grand-canonical Hamiltonian of a Bose field, K=H−μN. The physical energy density is obtained by adding μ∣ψ∣2 back. A stationary field in this chemical-potential frame has the usual factor e−iμt/ℏ in the physical frame.

For uniform or periodic boundary conditions, or in the homogeneous bulk, both nonconstant terms are nonnegative and vanish simultaneously at
ψ0​=gμ​​eiθ0​,Hmin​=−2gμ2​.​

The ground states form a circle. A particular nonzero ψ0​ is changed by every nontrivial phase rotation, although the full family is invariant. Selecting one such mean-field theory state therefore exhibits spontaneous symmetry breaking of U(1). This describes phase selection in the thermodynamic limit; an exact finite-system particle-number eigenstate need not itself have a nonzero field expectation.
