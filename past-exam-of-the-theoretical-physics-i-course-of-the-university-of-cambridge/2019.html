Use Minkowski metric gμν​=diag(−1,1,1,1) and units with the speed of light equal to one. Thus uμuμ​=−1, and hμν​=gμν​+uμ​uν​ is the spatial projector of a timelike congruence. Keep the medium velocity fixed when varying the electromagnetic four-potential. Since the electromagnetic field tensor is Fμν​=∂μ​Aν​−∂ν​Aμ​, its variation gives
δA​S=∫d4x21​(Mμν−Fμν)(∂μ​δAν​−∂ν​δAμ​)=∫d4x(Mμν−Fμν)∂μ​δAν​.
(1)

δA​S=∫d4x∂μ​(Fμν−Mμν)δAν​.
(2)
The Euler-Lagrange field equation is ∂μ​(Fμν−Mμν)=0. Renaming the free index and using antisymmetry gives
∂ν​Fμν=∂ν​Mμν.​
(3)
These are the inhomogeneous Maxwell equations with the bound charge and bound current encoded by the polarization-magnetization tensor.

There is a sign inconsistency in the original PDF: varying its action gives a plus sign for the spatially projected magnetic term, whereas its displayed target equation has a minus sign. The following Lagrange multiplier for antisymmetry calculation derives the result of the action as printed.
Temporarily vary all sixteen entries Mμν independently and add
Lλ​=21​λμν​(Mμν+Mνμ),λμν​=λνμ​.
(4)
Variation of the symmetric Lagrange multiplier imposes Mμν+Mνμ=0. Define
wμ​=uρ​Mρμ​,Cμν​=hμρ​hνσ​Mρσ.
(5)
On this constraint surface, Cνμ​=−Cμν​. Differentiating the original contractions before imposing the constraint gives
∂Mμν∂​(uα​uβ​MλαMβλ​)​M=−MT​=uν​wμ​−uμ​wν​,
(6)
and
∂Mμν∂​(hαρ​hβσ​MαβMσρ)​M=−MT​=−2Cμν​.
(7)
In particular, the reversed order σρ in the last factor of the action matters. The unrestricted Euler-Lagrange field equation, evaluated on the antisymmetry constraint, is
0=21​Fμν​−2κ1​(uν​wμ​−uμ​wν​)+2χ1​Cμν​+λμν​.
(8)
Subtract its index-transposed equation to eliminate the symmetric multiplier:
0=Fμν​−κ1​(uν​uρ​Mρμ​−uμ​uρ​Mρν​)+χ1​hμρ​hνσ​Mρσ.​
(9)
A direct check is to restrict the action to a rest-frame magnetic component M12=m, M21=−m: its quadratic contribution is +m2/(2χ), whose derivative has a positive sign. To obtain the PDF's target equation instead, one must reverse the sign of that quadratic term in the action, or replace its last Mσρ by Mρσ. These are alternative repairs; changing conventions cannot make the printed action and printed equation agree.

The constants specify isotropic, nondispersive linear response: κ controls electric polarization, and χ controls magnetization. Their numerical relation to conventional electric susceptibility and magnetic susceptibility depends on the signs and field normalization in the action. In the rationalized units used here, the printed action gives the constitutive relations
D=E+P=(1−κ)E,H=B−M=(1+χ)B.
(16)
Thus its relative permittivity and magnetic permeability are
εr​=1−κ,μr​=1+χ1​,χe​=−κ,χm​=−1+χχ​.​

Constraint and example. A holonomic constraint can be written fα​(q1​,…,qn​,t)=0, with no irreducible dependence on velocities. A velocity relation is holonomic if it integrates to such an equation. For example, a particle constrained to a stationary circle satisfies x2+y2=ℓ2, reducing its two Cartesian degrees of freedom to one angle. A nonholonomic constraint cannot be integrated in this way.
Kinematics and kinetic energy. Set d=R−r>0. In the plane of the original diagram, choose the orbital angle θ positive counterclockwise from vertically downward. The marked radius of the inner cylinder then has absolute orientation α=θ−ϕ measured from the same downward direction. Its signed spin angular velocity is Ω=θ˙−ϕ˙​, while
XG​=d(sinθ,−cosθ),∣X˙G​∣=d∣θ˙∣.
(18)
The moment of inertia of a uniform solid cylinder is I=mr2/2. Applying the kinetic energy decomposition about the center of mass gives
T=21​md2θ˙2+21​IΩ2=2m​(R−r)2θ˙2+4m​r2(ϕ˙​−θ˙)2.​
(19)
No slip. With tangential unit vector eθ​=(cosθ,sinθ), the inner contact point has velocity (dθ˙+rΩ)eθ​. The fixed wall requires this to vanish. Hence
dθ˙+r(θ˙−ϕ˙​)=0,Rθ˙−rϕ˙​=0.
(20)
Because r,R are constant, integration gives
rϕ−Rθ=0,Ω=−rR−r​θ˙,​
(21)
where the integration constant is zero with the marked-dot reference in the diagram and continuously unwrapped angles. This is a holonomic constraint, even though it was first obtained from a condition on velocities.
Oscillations. The gravitational potential energy above the lowest position is V=mgd(1−cosθ). Eliminating ϕ gives the reduced Lagrangian
L=43​md2θ˙2−mgd(1−cosθ).
(22)
The Euler-Lagrange equation becomes
23​md2θ¨+mgdsinθ=0.
(23)
Thus the bottom is a stable equilibrium, and for ∣θ∣≪1 there is simple harmonic motion, with
ω0​=3(R−r)2g​​,f0​=2π1​3(R−r)2g​​.​
(24)
The extra rotational kinetic energy reduces the frequency compared with a particle sliding without friction along the same circular path. The small oscillations inside a circular cylinder formula applies with inertia ratio γ=1/2.
Friction and energy. Let Ft​ be the signed tangential contact force in the eθ​ direction. Applying Newton's second law to translation and the torque equation to rotation gives
mdθ¨=−mgsinθ+Ft​,IΩ˙=rFt​.
(25)
Using the rolling without slipping condition yields
Ft​=31​mgsinθ.​
(26)
Except at special instants, static friction is needed to produce the spin acceleration. There is no sliding at the contact point, whose instantaneous velocity is zero; therefore this force does no total work. Its translational and rotational powers cancel: Ft​dθ˙+Ft​rΩ=0. The stationary normal reaction likewise does no work. With a rigid wall, adequate static friction, and no rolling resistance, conservation of energy gives
E=43​md2θ˙2+mgd(1−cosθ)=constant.​
(27)
Angular momentum. The cylinder's spin angular momentum IΩ is generally not conserved because the contact friction exerts a torque about its center of mass. About the fixed outer-cylinder center O, the angular momentum decomposition about the centre of mass gives
LO​=md2θ˙+IΩ=md(d−2r​)θ˙.
(28)
Gravity and friction exert the external torque
L˙O​=−mgdsinθ+RFt​=mg(r−32R​)sinθ.
(29)
Thus the rolling cylinder does not generally conserve angular momentum, although it conserves mechanical energy. There is a special geometric cancellation at R=3r/2: then LO​ and its torque vanish identically for this planar motion. Angular momentum of a larger isolated system including the wall and Earth is a separate conservation statement.
Scope of the holonomic reduction. The integrated constraint is exact at every angle for planar rolling without slipping with sustained contact; it is not restricted to small oscillations. Completing a full turn requires tracking angle winding rather than reducing each angle separately modulo 2π. This global bookkeeping does not make the local velocity relation nonintegrable.
However, arbitrary initial conditions need not sustain this ideal rolling motion. The inward normal reaction and the required friction are
N=m(gcosθ+dθ˙2),∣Ft​∣=31​mg∣sinθ∣.
(30)
Contact requires N≥0, and a finite coefficient μs​ of static friction requires ∣Ft​∣≤μs​N. If either fails, the cylinder detaches or slips and the one-coordinate rolling Lagrangian no longer applies. Slipping contact allows θ and ϕ to be independent; detachment removes the fixed center-distance constraint. These changes alone do not establish a nonholonomic constraint. Allowing general three-dimensional reorientation can introduce nonintegrable rolling constraints, but that motion is outside the diagram's two-angle planar model. Thus the stated planar rolling constraint is holonomic throughout its regime of validity; it cannot be imposed on every physically possible motion.

Under ψ↦eiαψ with constant real α, the field and its complex conjugate acquire opposite phases. These cancel in ∣ψ∣2, ∣ψ∣4, ∇ψ∗⋅∇ψ, and ψ∗ψ˙​−ψψ˙​∗. The Lagrangian density therefore has the particle-number phase symmetry, a global circle group U(1).
For a spacetime-dependent real α(r,t),
ψ˙​′=eiα(ψ˙​+iα˙ψ),∇ψ′=eiα(∇ψ+iψ∇α).
(31)
Writing n=∣ψ∣2, the change in the Lagrangian density is
ΔL=−ℏnα˙−2Mℏ2​[i(ψ∇ψ∗−ψ∗∇ψ)⋅∇α+n∣∇α∣2].
(32)
This is not generally zero or a boundary term. Thus the symmetry is global, with no arbitrary local phase symmetry. A compensating gauge field and gauge-covariant derivative of a charged scalar field would be required to promote it to a local redundancy.

Treat ψ,ψ∗ independently in the Legendre transform in mechanics. Their canonical momenta are
πψ​=2iℏ​ψ∗,πψ∗​=−2iℏ​ψ.
(33)
The time-derivative terms cancel in πψ​ψ˙​+πψ∗​ψ˙​∗−L, giving the Hamiltonian density
H=2Mℏ2​∣∇ψ∣2−μ∣ψ∣2+2g​∣ψ∣4.​
(34)
The first-order time dependence makes the momenta constrained; this direct calculation still gives the canonical density. In physical terms this is the grand-canonical Hamiltonian of a Bose field, K=H−μN. The physical energy density is obtained by adding μ∣ψ∣2 back. A stationary field in this chemical-potential frame has the usual factor e−iμt/ℏ in the physical frame.
Complete the square:
H=2Mℏ2​∣∇ψ∣2+2g​(∣ψ∣2−gμ​)2−2gμ2​.
(35)
For uniform or periodic boundary conditions, or in the homogeneous bulk, both nonconstant terms are nonnegative and vanish simultaneously at
ψ0​=gμ​​eiθ0​,Hmin​=−2gμ2​.​
(36)
The ground states form a circle. A particular nonzero ψ0​ is changed by every nontrivial phase rotation, although the full family is invariant. Selecting one such mean-field theory state therefore exhibits spontaneous symmetry breaking of U(1). This describes phase selection in the thermodynamic limit; an exact finite-system particle-number eigenstate need not itself have a nonzero field expectation.

A Landau free energy is a coarse-grained thermodynamic potential expressed as a function of a proposed macroscopic order parameter. Its coefficients include microscopic and thermal effects already averaged over. At fixed external thermodynamic conditions, equilibrium selects its global minimum; other local minima can represent metastable states. It is not the internal energy of a single microscopic magnetic configuration. Here the real scalar m represents a selected component of magnetization: zero describes the disordered state, while nonzero values distinguish two oppositely ordered states.
Every power is even, so the internal discrete symmetry is
m⟼−m,Z2​.​
(49)
For a magnetic order parameter, this represents reversal of the magnetization, as under time reversal in zero applied magnetic field. A nonzero field would generally add an odd term and break this symmetry explicitly. This scalar model has no continuous internal rotation symmetry; physical rotational symmetry of a vector magnet is not implied by treating one real component. The condition c>0 makes the polynomial bounded below at large ∣m∣.
For the requested ordered phase on cooling, take the usual implicit assumption a>0, and write r=a(T−Tc​). If a<0 the temperature directions reverse; if a=0 this expansion has no temperature-driven transition. The source explicitly states only c>0.

f′(m)=2m(r+bm2+cm4)=0.

For b<0 at T=Tc​, the quartic term lowers the free energy near m=0, while the positive sextic term stabilizes it at larger ∣m∣. The nonzero stationary points have
m2=−cb​,f(m)=6c2b3​<0.​
