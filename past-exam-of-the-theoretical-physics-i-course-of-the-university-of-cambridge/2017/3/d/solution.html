Write ϕ=x+iy. The added term is −(x2−y2), so the full potential simplifies to
V(x,y)=−2x2+eλ(x2+y2).
(43)
The phase-rotation subgroup is reduced from U(1) to the two-element symmetry ϕ↦−ϕ. More fully, the kinetic term and potential are invariant under independent reflections x↦−x and y↦−y, a Z2​×Z2​ internal symmetry generated by ϕ↦−ϕ and complex conjugation. There is no continuous internal rotation symmetry for the explicit twofold anisotropy of a complex scalar field.
For fixed x, positive λ makes the exponential grow with y2, so every global minimum has y=0. On the real axis,
∂x∂V​=2x[−2+λeλx2].
(44)
Thus
{ϕ=±v,v2=ln(2/λ)/λ,ϕ=0,​0<λ<2,λ≥2.​​
(45)
For 0<λ<2, the nonzero stationary points are the two global minima; the origin is unstable along the real direction. For λ≥2 the origin is the unique minimum, with zero real-direction quadratic curvature at the critical value 2. Selecting +v breaks the sign reversal that exchanges the two vacua, while complex conjugation remains unbroken.
Set ϕ=v+χ, χ=u+iw. Since eλv2=2/λ, expansion gives
V=Vmin​+4λv2u2+2w2+O(χ3)​,Vmin​=λ2​[1−lnλ2​].
(46)
Equivalently, in canonical fields h=2​u, π=2​w,
V=Vmin​+21​mh2​h2+21​mπ2​π2+⋯,mh2​=4λv2=4ln(2/λ)>0,mπ2​=2>0​.
(47)
Both masses are positive throughout the broken phase 0<λ<2. A broken discrete symmetry does not demand a massless Goldstone boson. At λ=2 the radial mass tends to zero at a phase boundary, so a claim of strictly positive mass there would be incorrect. For completeness, expanding around the symmetric origin gives squared masses λ−2 and λ for the canonical real and imaginary fields.
