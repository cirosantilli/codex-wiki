Write ρ for cylindrical radius in this question, not the rod's mass density. A charged relativistic particle in a time-independent magnetic field has the charged-particle electromagnetic Lagrangian
L=−mc21−v2/c2​+qA⋅v,
(18)
with zero electrostatic potential. The cylindrical speed is v2=ρ˙​2+ρ2θ˙2+z˙2. Inside a uniform axial field, choose the symmetric magnetic vector potential Aθ​=Bρ/2, Aρ​=Az​=0. Its cylindrical curl gives Bz​=ρ−1∂ρ​(ρAθ​)=B. Hence
L=−mc21−c2z˙2+ρ˙​2+ρ2θ˙2​​+2qBρ2θ˙​​.
(19)
In a field-free region far from either end one can use Aθ​=0, recovering the requested outside expression. The question's V=−qBρ2θ˙/2 is a velocity-dependent generalized potential, not electrostatic potential energy.
There is an important end-field qualification. A finite axial field that jumps to zero with no radial component would violate Gauss's law for magnetism. The consistent near-axis idealisation is the solenoid fringe field from an axisymmetric vector potential, Aθ​=ρB(z)/2, giving Bρ​=−ρB′(z)/2 and Bz​=B(z). The piecewise Lagrangian is its hard-edge limit and retains fringe impulses through the jump in Aθ​. Those impulses are essential to the focusing calculation.
