A retarded Green function obeys G(τ)=0 for τ<0 and
(∂τ2​+1−ε∂τ3​)G(τ)=δ(τ).
(49)
For a bounded or suitably tempered response represented by the real-axis Fourier integral, analyticity in the upper frequency half-plane is required, with suitable behaviour at infinity. For negative τ, e−iωτ decays on a large upper semicircle. If that half-plane has no singularities, contour closure gives zero response before the source. Lower-half-plane poles give factors decaying for positive time. Real poles are approached from below for a retarded prescription; at ε=0, for example, GR​(τ)=Θ(τ)sinτ.
The causality versus stability of a Green function distinction matters: an unstable initial-value problem can still have a strictly causal response that grows exponentially. Such a response has no ordinary real-axis Fourier transform. Its inverse Laplace transform must run on a frequency contour above its growth poles. Thus “upper poles violate causality” is correct only with the stable/tempered real-axis Fourier assumptions, not as an unconditional theorem about differential equations.
