Zero flavour triality of a light-quark hadron (a+2b≡0(mod3))

 ... Physics Branches of physics Quantum field theory Standard Model Quantum chromodynamics Flavor symmetry
OurBigBook.com Words: 92
For a colour singlet hadron built entirely from up quarks, down quarks, strange quarks, antiquarks and gluons, the net number of quarks is a multiple of three. The central SU(3) colour element multiplies the state by ωnq​−nqˉ​​, while a singlet must be unchanged. The central flavour element supplies the same phase because all three light flavours form the fundamental representation. Every resulting flavour multiplet therefore has zero SU(3) triality. Heavy quarks are singlets of light-flavour SU(3), so hadrons containing them do not obey this argument. The zero-triality restriction allows higher light-quark multiplets as well as the familiar singlet, octet and decuplet.
