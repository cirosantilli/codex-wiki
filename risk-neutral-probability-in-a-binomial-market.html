Risk-neutral probability in a binomial market (q=(R−d)/(u−d))

 ... Mathematics Area of mathematics Mathematical optimization Mathematical finance Binomial options pricing model Discrete-time binomial market
OurBigBook.com Words: 69
The martingale condition for the discounted stock is quS0​+(1−q)dS0​=RS0​. Therefore the up risk-neutral probability is
q=u−dR−d​,1−q=u−du−R​.
(15)
With down factor d=1+a, up factor u=1+b and R=1+r, this is equivalently (r−a)/(b−a). For d=0.8, u=1.2, R=1.05, it gives q=0.25/0.4=0.625. Under the risk-neutral measure, the terminal stock price has expected value (5/8)120+(3/8)80=105, whose discounted value is 100. The physical probability p need only satisfy 0<p<1; it does not enter this calculation.
