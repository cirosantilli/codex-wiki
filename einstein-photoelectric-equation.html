Einstein photoelectric equation (Kmax​=hν−Φ)

 ... Physics Branches of physics Quantum mechanics Photon Photon absorption Photoelectric effect
OurBigBook.com Words: 193 Articles: 1
For the ideal single-photon metal model, conservation of energy gives
hν=Φ+Kmax​,Kmax​=hν−Φ,ν≥ν0​=hΦ​.
(47)
Here h is the Planck constant, ν the incident frequency, Φ the work function, and Kmax​ the largest emitted kinetic energy relative to the nearby vacuum level. An electron at the highest occupied energy can reach this edge if it escapes without an additional loss. An electron starting lower or losing energy within the metal emerges below it. The material takes recoil momentum; its recoil energy is neglected in this model.
For an original chosen example, take Φ=2.30eV and ν=8.00×1014Hz. Using h=6.62607015×10−34Js and the elementary charge e=1.602176634×10−19C gives hν=3.308534eV and Kmax​=1.008534eV. The ideal stopping potential is therefore 1.008534V. At 4.00×1014Hz the same model supplies only 1.654267eV per photon, so this single-photon channel is closed. A negative result from hν−Φ means no emission through that channel, not an emitted electron with negative kinetic energy.
