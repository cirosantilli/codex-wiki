Integration on an oriented manifold (∫M​α)

Words: 68
Differential form OurBigBook.com
A compactly supported top-degree differential form on an oriented smooth manifold is integrated using orientation-preserving coordinates and a partition of unity. The coordinate change of variables formula makes the result independent of the choices. More generally, a form is integrated over a lower-dimensional oriented submanifold by its pullback. The Generalized Stokes theorem states ∫M​dβ=∫∂M​β with the induced boundary orientation. Integrating a closed differential form over a closed cycle depends only on its homology class, since its integral over a boundary vanishes.

Comass (∥φx​∥∗​=supξ oriented unit simple p-vector​∣φx​(ξ)∣)

Words: 46
Differential form OurBigBook.com
The comass is the operator norm of a differential form restricted to simple unit tangent multivectors. Reversing orientation gives the equivalent two-sided bound. It differs from the Euclidean norm of all the form's components. The calibration bound uses comass because tangent planes are represented by simple multivectors.

Integral pairing of complex top forms (in2θ∧θ≥0)

Words: 52
Differential form OurBigBook.com
For a smooth form θ=gdz1​∧⋯∧dzn​ of type (n,0), the complex orientation gives in2θ∧θ=2n∣g∣2dx1​∧dy1​∧⋯∧dxn​∧dyn​. Consequently its integral on a compact complex manifold is positive unless θ vanishes identically. Applying Stokes theorem to d(ψ∧dψ​) proves that a holomorphic differential form of degree n−1 on a compact complex n-manifold is closed, without requiring a Kähler metric.

Differential three-form (α∈Ω3(M))

Differential form OurBigBook.com
A differential three-form is a smooth, totally antisymmetric covariant tensor of rank three. For a Killing-Yano two-form, its covariant derivative is such a tensor, because it is antisymmetric in the first two and last two slots.

Metric volume form (ωg​=detg​dx1∧⋯∧dxn)

Words: 74
Volume form OurBigBook.com
On an oriented Riemannian manifold, the metric volume form is the volume form taking value one on positively oriented orthonormal frames. In an oriented manifold chart it has the displayed expression. Under an orientation-preserving coordinate change with Jacobian A, detg acquires the factor (detA)2, precisely matching the transformation of the top exterior product. Thus the expressions agree on chart overlaps. Orientation is needed to make a globally positive form, though the volume density exists without it.

Metric volume tensor (ϵa1​…ad​​)

Words: 56
Volume form OurBigBook.com
On an oriented d-dimensional pseudo-Riemannian manifold, the metric volume tensor is the totally antisymmetric tensor with ϵ1…d​=∣detg∣​ in positively oriented coordinates. It supplies the components of the volume form. It differs from the coordinate Levi-Civita symbol, whose entries are just 0,1,−1. Boundary obstruction for commuting volume-preserving vector fields

Words: 88
Volume-preserving vector field OurBigBook.com
Suppose a nonempty compact (n+1)-manifold with a volume form has n pointwise independent volume-preserving vector fields that commute and are tangent to its boundary. If restriction HdR1​(M)→HdR1​(∂M) is injective, the boundary must have at least two connected components.
Indeed η=ιX1​​⋯ιXn​​ω is nowhere zero. Cartan's magic formula and commutation show dη=0, while boundary tangency gives j∗η=0. Injectivity in de Rham cohomology makes η=df. On a connected boundary f is constant; a maximum or minimum differing from that boundary value would be an interior critical point, contradicting df=0. Empty boundary is also impossible.

Pointwise representation of one-forms by vector-field functionals (θ(ω)(X)=ω(X))

Words: 69
One-form OurBigBook.com
Let W consist of real-linear maps α from smooth vector fields to smooth functions such that Xp​=0 implies α(X)(p)=0. These are exactly evaluations by smooth differential one-forms. Define ωp​(v)=α(X)(p) using any global field with Xp​=v; the vanishing condition makes it well-defined. Bump-extended local frame vectors make its coefficients smooth. Uniqueness of the exterior derivative from its axioms (dα=∑I,j​∂j​aI​dxj∧dxI)

Words: 45
Exterior derivative OurBigBook.com
An R-linear degree-one graded derivation of the exterior algebra of smooth differential forms that equals the differential on functions and squares to zero is unique. A smooth bump function and the graded Leibniz rule first establish locality. Then d(dxi)=0, and applying the graded Leibniz rule to α=∑I​aI​dxI gives the displayed coordinate formula. The chain rule makes that formula coordinate-independent.

Generalized Stokes theorem

Words: 59
Exterior derivative OurBigBook.com
If X is an oriented n-manifold with boundary, F:∂X↪X is the inclusion, and ω is a compactly supported (n−1)-form, then
∫X​dω=∫∂X​F∗ω,
(3)
where the boundary has the outward-normal-first boundary orientation. A subordinate partition of unity reduces the theorem to the fundamental theorem of calculus in oriented coordinate half-spaces; local finiteness and compact support ensure that only finitely many terms contribute.

Closed and exact differential forms

Words: 1k Articles: 28
Exterior derivative OurBigBook.com
A closed differential form satisfies dα=0; an exact differential form has α=dβ. The identity d2=0 makes every exact form closed. Global failure of the converse is measured by de Rham cohomology; the Poincaré lemma gives the local converse in positive degrees.

On the punctured plane, this one-form is closed and locally the differential of the polar angle. Its line integral around a closed curve is 2π times the winding number. Closed differential one-form (η∈Ω1(M),dη=0)

Closed differential form OurBigBook.com
A differential one-form is closed when its exterior derivative vanishes. In coordinates, η=ηi​dxi is closed exactly when ∂i​ηj​=∂j​ηi​. The Poincaré lemma makes it locally an exact differential form, but global exactness can fail through nonzero integrals around closed curves.
Closed one-forms on the two-sphere are exact (HdR1​(S2)=0)
Words: 55
Closed differential one-form OurBigBook.com
Remove the north pole and south pole separately. Each resulting chart is diffeomorphic to the plane, so the Poincaré lemma supplies local primitives of a closed one-form. Their difference has zero derivative on the connected overlap and is constant. Adjust one primitive by that constant and glue. This proves exactness without assuming a prior computation of de Rham cohomology.
Averaging closed one-forms on a surface unit tangent bundle (ζ=π∗ξ+aω+dh,dξ=aKΩa​)
Words: 93
Closed differential one-form OurBigBook.com
Average a closed one-form over fibre rotations. The average differs from the original by an exact form because each rotation is isotopic to the identity; the Cartan formula for the Lie derivative supplies an explicit homotopy integral. Its vertical value a is constant, since closedness and rotational invariance give da=0. Subtracting aω leaves a basic form π∗ξ, and closedness gives dξ=aKΩa​. For a zero-flux magnetic field, the Liouville mean of ζ(X+fV) is zero: the directional mean of ξ(v) vanishes, the vertical mean is 2πa∫fΩa​, and the coboundary has zero mean by volume preservation.
Poincaré lemma (dα=0⟹α=dβ locally,p>0)
Words: 443 Articles: 6
Closed differential form OurBigBook.com
Every smooth closed differential form of positive degree is locally an exact differential form. On a coordinate ball centered at zero, put Ft​(x)=tx and let R=xi∂i​ be the radial vector field. Define the degree-lowering operator
Kα=∫01​t−1Ft∗​(ιR​α)dt.
(4)
For a p-form with p>0, the integrand has a factor tp−1 in its coordinate coefficients and is smooth at zero. Cartan's magic formula and differentiation of the pullback of a differential form give ∂t​(Ft∗​α)=t−1Ft∗​(dιR​α+ιR​dα). Integrating from zero to one gives dKα+Kdα=α, since F0∗​α=0 in positive degree. If dα=0, take β=Kα. The local potential of a closed differential one-form gives the explicit degree-one construction. Global exactness can fail through nonzero periods, measured by de Rham cohomology.
Relative Poincaré lemma
Words: 58
Poincaré lemma OurBigBook.com
Let a neighborhood of a submanifold retract onto it by fiberwise radial contraction in a tubular neighborhood. If a closed differential form β pulls back to zero on the submanifold, the radial homotopy formula gives β=dα, with α vanishing along the zero section. Applying the homotopy operator to β gives the primitive directly. This is the relative ingredient in the relative Moser theorem.
Radial homotopy operator (Hα=∫01​ρt∗​(ιR​α)dt/t)
Words: 173 Articles: 2
Poincaré lemma OurBigBook.com
On a star-shaped domain, let ρt​(x)=tx and R=∑xj​∂xj​​. On positive-degree differential forms, Hα=∫01​ρt∗​(ιR​α)dt/t satisfies dH+Hd=id−ρ0∗​. Degree-zero correction to radial homotopy (dh+hd=id−F0∗​)

For radial contraction on a star-shaped domain, the homotopy formula is the identity in positive form degrees because pullback by the constant map vanishes there. On functions it is h1​df=f−f(0). Green-theorem proof of exactness on a disc
Words: 54
Poincaré lemma OurBigBook.com
On a star-shaped planar domain containing zero, let η be a smooth closed differential one-form and set f(x)=∫[0,x]​η. Green theorem on the triangle with vertices 0,x,x+h gives f(x+h)−f(x)=∫[x,x+h]​η. Dividing by a displacement and taking its limit proves df=η. Degree-zero de Rham cohomology (HdR0​(M)={f∈C∞(M):df=0})
Words: 60
de Rham cohomology OurBigBook.com
There are no exact forms in degree zero. A smooth function has zero exterior derivative precisely when it is locally constant, so HdR0​(M) is the space of locally constant functions. On a connected nonempty smooth manifold, it is R. An overlapping cover by two connected open subsets therefore does not give the direct-sum formula for de Rham cohomology of a finite disjoint union: restriction sends a constant to equal constants on both pieces.
de Rham cohomology of a finite disjoint union (HdRp​(⨆j=1k​Mj​)≅⨁j=1k​HdRp​(Mj​))
Words: 50
de Rham cohomology OurBigBook.com
Restriction of differential forms to the finitely many open and closed components gives an isomorphism of cochain complexes Ω∗(⨆Mj​)≅⨁j​Ω∗(Mj​). Piecewise forms glue because there are no overlaps, and the exterior derivative acts componentwise. Taking the quotient of closed differential forms by exact differential forms proves the displayed de Rham cohomology isomorphism. For an infinite disjoint union, forms and cohomology give products instead.

The complex of smooth differential forms resolves the constant complex sheaf by the Poincaré lemma and consists of fine sheaves. Its global cohomology therefore agrees with sheaf cohomology of that constant sheaf. A good-cover Čech-de Rham double complex gives an explicit comparison, used in Čech-de Rham curvature descent.
Top de Rham cohomology of a compact connected oriented manifold (HdRn​(M)≅R)
Words: 63
de Rham cohomology OurBigBook.com
For a compact connected oriented boundaryless n-dimensional smooth manifold, choose a Riemannian metric. Harmonic functions are constant because ⟨f,Δf⟩=∥df∥2, and Hodge star commutes with the Hodge Laplacian. Thus the Hodge star operator identifies the constant functions with the harmonic differential forms of top degree, exactly Rωg​. The Hodge decomposition theorem identifies these with the top de Rham cohomology. The metric volume form has nonzero class by Stokes theorem. The harmonic-representative theorem is stated in Denis Auroux's Hodge theory lecture.
First de Rham cohomology of the two-sphere (HdR1​(S2)=0)
Words: 44
de Rham cohomology OurBigBook.com
A closed differential form of degree one on the two-sphere has primitives on its north-punctured and south-punctured charts by the Poincaré lemma. The overlap is connected, so their difference is constant. Adjust one constant and glue the primitives. Thus every closed one-form is an exact differential form, proving the first de Rham cohomology vanishes.
de Rham cohomology of a finite quotient (HdRp​(M/G)≅HdRp​(M)G)
Words: 67
de Rham cohomology OurBigBook.com
For a free smooth action of a finite group G on a smooth manifold M, the quotient projection induces
HdRp​(M/G)≅HdRp​(M)G.
(6)
Invariant differential forms descend uniquely through local inverse branches of the covering map. Averaging by ∣G∣−1∑g∈G​g∗ commutes with the exterior derivative, produces invariant representatives of invariant classes, and produces an invariant primitive for every invariant exact form. Hence taking invariant forms and taking invariant cohomology give the same result over the real numbers.
Top-degree de Rham cohomology (HdRn​(M))
Words: 118 Articles: 1
de Rham cohomology OurBigBook.com
On a compact connected oriented n-dimensional smooth manifold without boundary, integration is a linear isomorphism HdRn​(M)→R. Thus two top-degree differential forms have the same de Rham cohomology class exactly when their integrals agree.
Top-degree de Rham cohomology of a compact Lie group (HdRn​(G;R)≅Rπ0​(G))
Words: 93
Top-degree de Rham cohomology OurBigBook.com
A compact Lie group has finitely many connected components, each an oriented compact manifold of dimension n. Componentwise integration identifies its top-degree de Rham cohomology with one real coordinate per component. For a connected group, if top-degree classes are represented by invariant forms, the invariant volume form on a Lie group spans the cohomology: invariant top forms are one-dimensional, and its positive integral proves that its class is nonzero by the Generalized Stokes theorem. A disconnected group cannot have an invariant representative of every top-degree class, since left translations permute its components. For example, S1×Z/2 has two independent degree-one classes but only one independent invariant top form.
Averaging differential forms over the circle
Words: 102 Articles: 1
de Rham cohomology OurBigBook.com
Let S1 act on M×S1 by rotation of the second factor. Averaging a differential form over this action is a cochain map. Every rotation is homotopic to the identity through smooth rotations, and integrating Cartan's magic formula along them gives a cochain homotopy between the averaging map and the identity. Consequently every de Rham cohomology class has a rotation-invariant representative, and an invariant exact form has an invariant primitive obtained by averaging any primitive.
