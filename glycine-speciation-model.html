Glycine speciation model

In an ideal aqueous chemical solution, set h=[H3​O+]/c∘=10−pH, with c∘=1molL−1, and approximate activity coefficients by one. Put K1​=10−2.35 and K2​=10−9.78 for the stipulated dimensionless acid dissociation constants. The Henderson-Hasselbalch equation gives the adjacent-state ratios
[G+][G0]​=hK1​​,[G0][G−]​=hK2​​.
(191)
With analytical concentration CT​=[G+]+[G0]+[G−], normalization gives the three fractions and mean electric charge in units of the elementary charge:
D=h2+K1​h+K1​K2​,α+​=Dh2​,α0​=DK1​h​,α−​=DK1​K2​​,zˉ=α+​−α−​.
(192)
All three fractions are nonnegative and sum to one. At pH 7, the model gives about 99.832% zwitterion, 0.00224% cation and 0.1657% anion; zˉ≈−0.00163. Predominantly zwitterionic therefore need not mean exactly at the isoelectric point.
Setting zˉ=0 requires h2=K1​K2​. Taking the logarithm yields
pI=2pKa1​+pKa2​​=22.35+9.78​=6.065.
(193)
At this model isoelectric point, α+​=α−​≈0.0001927 and α0​≈0.9996146: about 0.0193% of each charged form remains. At either pKa, the relevant adjacent fractions are equal, but the third fraction is not mathematically zero. This three-state model neglects the minor uncharged form H2​N−CH2​−COOH; macroscopic pKa values alone do not determine its fraction separately from the zwitterion.
