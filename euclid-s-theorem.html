There are infinitely many prime numbers. Indeed, for any finite list of prime numbers p1​,…,pk​, the integer N=p1​⋯pk​+1>1 has a prime factor, by the fundamental theorem of arithmetic. None of the listed prime numbers divides N, since each divides N−1 and a common divisor would divide 1. Thus no finite list contains all prime numbers. The constructed N need not itself be a prime number.
For example, the first six prime numbers give
2⋅3⋅5⋅7⋅11⋅13+1=30031=59⋅509.
(3)
Both prime factors lie outside the original list, although 30031 is a composite number. The construction defeats a proposed complete list; it is not a formula guaranteed to produce a prime number. Book IX, Proposition 20 of Euclid's Elements explicitly considers both possibilities, prime and composite. [ref].
