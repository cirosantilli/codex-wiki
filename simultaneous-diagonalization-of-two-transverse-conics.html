Simultaneous diagonalization of two transverse conics (PTAP, PTBP diagonal)

 Home Mathematics Area of mathematics Geometry and topology Euclidean geometry Conic section
OurBigBook.com Words: 102
Two complex plane conics meeting in four distinct points can be diagonalized by the same invertible change of homogeneous coordinates. Their intersection points form a projective frame: three on a line would force that line to be a common component by Bézout theorem, contradicting a finite intersection. Move the four points to the coordinate points and [1:1:1]. Each equation becomes ayz+bzx+cxy=0 with a+b+c=0. Substituting x=v+w,y=w+u,z=u+v gives au2+bv2+cw2; the substitution matrix has determinant two. Composing this with the frame change yields the displayed simultaneous congruence of the symmetric coefficient matrices. This is congruence of quadratic forms, not a claim of simultaneous similarity of arbitrary matrices.
