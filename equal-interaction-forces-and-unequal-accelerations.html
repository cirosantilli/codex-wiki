Equal interaction forces and unequal accelerations (mA​aA​=−mB​aB​)

 ... Physics Branches of physics Classical mechanics Force Newton's laws of motion Newton's third law
OurBigBook.com Words: 243
Choose right as positive and let two carts have total moving masses mA​=1kg and mB​=2kg, including any attached apparatus. At an instant when their repulsive interaction has magnitude 4N, assume no other horizontal forces. Then Newton's third law gives FB on A​=−4N and FA on B​=+4N, while Newton's second law gives
aA​=1kg−4N​=−4m/s2,aB​=2kg+4N​=+2m/s2.
(16)
The smaller mass has the larger acceleration magnitude. For a nonzero interaction and zero other horizontal forces, aA​/aB​=−mB​/mA​. Meanwhile d(pA​+pB​)/dt=mA​aA​+mB​aB​=−4+4=0 in force units. The two carts' velocities change while total momentum stays constant.
To distinguish an instantaneous force from its accumulated impulse, prescribe an original triangular interaction pulse lasting T=1s:
f(t)=⎩⎨⎧​8N/st,8N/s(T−t),0,​0≤t≤T/2,T/2≤t≤T,otherwise,​FB on A​(t)=−f(t),FA on B​(t)=f(t).
(17)
This chosen pulse reaches 4N at t=0.5s; it is a constructed model, not an experimental trace or a claim about a particular bumper's material response. Its positive area is 21​(1s)(4N)=2Ns. The impulse-momentum theorem therefore gives
JA​=−2Ns,JB​=+2Ns,ΔvA​=−2m/s,ΔvB​=+1m/s.
(18)
Opposite impulses give opposite momentum changes, while the velocity changes differ because of the unequal masses. The cumulative positive impulse is I(t)=4N/st2 during the first half and I(t)=2Ns−4N/s(T−t)2 during the second half. Thus JA​(t)=−I(t) and JB​(t)=I(t) cancel throughout the pulse, not merely at its endpoint.
