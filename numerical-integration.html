Numerical integration (I(f)=∫ab​f(x)dx)

 Home Mathematics Area of mathematics Analysis Numerical analysis
OurBigBook.com Words: 3k Articles: 24
Numerical integration approximates a definite integral using finitely many sampled function values. For an integrand f on a finite interval [a,b], a<b, a quadrature rule has the form
I(f)=∫ab​f(x)dx,Q(f)=∑j=1m​wj​f(xj​)≈I(f).
(1)
The quadrature nodes xj​ tell us where to evaluate f; the quadrature weights wj​ turn those values into an approximation to the accumulated signed area. The exact definite integral depends on the integrand throughout the interval, whereas the quadrature rule sees only these samples. Integrating a chosen interpolant exactly still generally approximates the original integrand's integral.[ref]
The fundamental theorem of calculus evaluates I(f) as F(b)−F(a) when an antiderivative F is available. Numerical integration is useful when no convenient antiderivative is known, or each evaluation of the integrand requires an expensive computation. The task then becomes choosing informative quadrature nodes and controlling the quadrature error with as few evaluations as possible. Fixed data also permit a quadrature rule, but refinement cannot supply new information between observations.[ref]
For an exact comparison, take the polynomial f(x)=x2 on [0,1]. Its antiderivative x3/3 gives I(f)=1/3. On a partition of an interval into n≥1 equal panels, the composite midpoint quadrature rule uses n midpoint samples, each with quadrature weight 1/n; the composite trapezoidal rule uses n+1 endpoint samples, with outer quadrature weights 1/(2n) and interior quadrature weights 1/n. The existing derivations under these two quadrature rules give
Mn​(f)=31​−12n21​,Tn​(f)=31​+6n21​.
(2)
In particular, M1​=1/4 and T1​=1/2 already bracket 1/3; two panels give M2​=5/16 and T2​=3/8. The convex function x2 explains the opposite signs. A midpoint rectangle can cross the curve within its panel, so its total signed area, rather than pointwise ordering, determines the underestimate.
Define the signed approximation error as En​=Qn​(f)−I(f). In this example the absolute errors are 1/(12n2) and 1/(6n2): doubling n reduces each by exactly four. A plot with both axes on a logarithmic scale therefore has slope −2, expressing order two with respect to panel refinement. This is an exact calculation for this polynomial, including n=1, rather than an observed rate extrapolated from a few computed answers.
For a general integrand, the composite midpoint quadrature rule and composite trapezoidal rule give second-order quadrature error bounds when f has two continuous derivatives on [a,b] and a known bound on ∣f′′∣. These hypotheses describe behavior between samples. A formal error formula containing an unknown derivative is not a numerical accuracy certificate or a justified sample count. Finite samples do not certify quadrature accuracy makes the missing information explicit. Errors in the evaluations and roundoff error must also be considered; noise sensitivity of a quadrature rule treats the former.
More elaborate choices exploit other information: Gaussian quadrature obtains high polynomial exactness from carefully chosen quadrature nodes, and Simpson's rule uses a quadratic interpolant. Adaptive numerical integration compares approximations and concentrates samples where they disagree, often saving evaluations. Such a difference is an error estimate, not a certified bound without further assumptions: both approximations can miss the same feature.[ref]

Balancing quadrature truncation and round-off error (Cn−p≈δ ⟹ n≈(C/δ)1/p)

Words: 121
Numerical integration OurBigBook.com
If a quadrature error bound is Cn−p and the relevant numerical resolution is δ, equating these scales gives an estimate of the strip count beyond which that truncation guarantee reaches the rounding scale. For the composite Simpson's rule, p=4. This estimate is useful for choosing a sensible order of magnitude, but a worst-case bound need not equal the actual quadrature error.
For a normal floating-point number in base two, the spacing depends on the exponent of the result; half the spacing is a natural final-storage rounding scale. Machine epsilon instead measures the gap above one. Function-evaluation errors, accumulation of summation error, cancellation, and the arithmetic used for the nodes can impose an earlier practical accuracy limit. Thus final storage precision alone cannot give a universal optimal strip count.

Separable integrand (∫∏j​Ij​​∏j​gj​(xj​)dx=∏j​∫Ij​​gj​(t)dt)

Words: 110
Numerical integration OurBigBook.com
An integrand is separable across coordinates when it factors as g(x1​,…,xd​)=∏j=1d​gj​(xj​). On a product domain, the Fubini theorem reduces its integral to a product of one-dimensional integrals, provided the factors are integrable; for nonnegative factors the Tonelli theorem gives the extended-value version.
A finite sum of separable terms can likewise be integrated as a finite sum of products. For example,
∫[0,1]d​∑i=1m​e∥x−ai​∥2dx=∑i=1m​∏j=1d​∫01​e(t−aij​)2dt.
(3)
For fixed centres, the factors are smooth on compact intervals. Repeated one-dimensional composite Simpson's rule refinements have fourth-order discretization error, avoiding the exponential tensor-grid cost of a generic d-dimensional rule. The numerical constants and possible floating-point overflow still depend on the scale of the factors; separability removes a dimension-dependent combinatorial cost, not all conditioning difficulties.

Tensor-product quadrature (Q(f)=∑i1​,…,id​​wi1​​⋯wid​​f(xi1​​,…,xid​​))

Words: 83
Numerical integration OurBigBook.com
On a product domain, apply a one-dimensional quadrature rule independently in each coordinate. The resulting quadrature nodes form a product grid and the quadrature weights multiply. With m subdivisions per direction in fixed dimension d, the evaluation count is N=O(md). If the one-dimensional rule has error O(m−p), uniformly for the required coordinate slices, and its weight sums are bounded, telescoping the d integration operators against their quadrature operators gives total error O(m−p)=O(N−p/d). Thus composite Simpson's rule has p=4 for sufficiently smooth integrands, but loses efficiency as d increases.

Finite samples do not certify quadrature accuracy (p(x)=∏j=1m​(x−xj​)2)

Words: 206
Numerical integration OurBigBook.com
For any finite set of quadrature nodes x1​,…,xm​ in [a,b], a<b, consider the polynomial
p(x)=∏j=1m​(x−xj​)2,A=∫ab​p(x)dx>0.
(4)
Every sample is zero, so every quadrature rule at these quadrature nodes gives Q(p)=0. Yet p is nonnegative and strictly positive away from its finitely many polynomial roots; its continuity makes the integral positive. Consequently the smooth functions 0 and g(x)=p(x)/A have identical samples but definite integrals 0 and 1. Multiplying g by any positive constant makes the difference arbitrarily large. This is an information limit, even with exact arithmetic; merely assuming smoothness without quantitative bounds does not remove it. The related degree ceiling for quadrature exactness uses the same squared nodal polynomial to limit polynomial exactness.
For example, the endpoint quadrature nodes 0,1 give p(x)=x2(1−x)2, A=1/30, and g(x)=30x2(1−x)2. Both the zero function and g produce zero under the one-panel trapezoidal rule, although I(g)=1. More generally, any estimate based only on their shared samples has absolute error at least 1/2 for one of these two integrands, by the triangle inequality.
The obstruction also applies to a deterministic adaptive procedure that terminates after finitely many evaluations on the zero function: form p using every quadrature node it visited. On g, the same zero responses lead to the same choices and answer. Certified quadrature error control therefore needs additional information, such as a known bound on relevant derivatives, rather than agreement among sampled approximations alone.

Boole's rule (∫−11​p=(7p(−1)+32p(−1/2)+12p(0)+32p(1/2)+7p(1))/45)

Words: 50
Numerical integration OurBigBook.com
Integrate the five Lagrange interpolation polynomials at the equally spaced nodes to obtain the displayed rule. It is exact for polynomials through degree four, and symmetry also makes it exact in degree five. Symmetric endpoint, inner-node and midpoint weights a,b,c satisfy 2a+2b+c=2, 2a+b/2=2/3, 2a+b/8=2/5, giving a=7/45, b=32/45, c=12/45.

Quadrature rule (Q(f)=∑i=1m​wi​f(xi​))

Words: 2k Articles: 18
Numerical integration OurBigBook.com
A quadrature rule approximates an integral by a finite weighted sum of sampled function values:
Iρ​(f)=∫ab​ρ(x)f(x)dx≈Q(f)=∑i=1m​wi​f(xi​).
(5)
The quadrature nodes xi​ specify where to sample, and the quadrature weights wi​ specify how much each sample contributes. The density ρ(x) specifies the target integral, whereas the discrete quadrature weights belong to the approximation. Ordinary numerical integration has ρ=1. Gaussian quadrature and the midpoint quadrature rule choose interior quadrature nodes; the trapezoidal rule includes endpoints.[ref]
For ordinary numerical integration, exactness on the constant function 1 requires ∑i​wi​=b−a; exactness on x additionally requires ∑i​wi​xi​=(b2−a2)/2. These two checks make the quadrature rule exact for every affine function. For example, both the midpoint quadrature rule and trapezoidal rule give 6 for ∫02​(2x+1)dx. Testing progressively higher polynomial degrees defines the degree of precision of a quadrature rule. Exactness describes a class of integrands, rather than promising exact results for every function.
Composite midpoint quadrature rules and composite trapezoidal rules apply a simple quadrature rule on every panel of a partition of an interval. This buys accuracy by shrinking the panels while keeping the elementary formula fixed. For continuous functions sampled exactly, their quadrature errors tend to zero as the largest panel width tends to zero. The familiar second-order bounds require stronger assumptions about derivatives. The signed approximation error used here is Q(f)−Iρ​(f); some sources define quadrature error with the opposite sign, so the convention must accompany any formula.

Interpolatory quadrature (Q(f)=∫ab​pn​(x)dx)

Words: 76 Articles: 1
Quadrature rule OurBigBook.com
An interpolatory quadrature approximates a definite integral by exactly integrating the interpolation polynomial through prescribed sampled values. For distinct quadrature nodes x0​,…,xn​, its quadrature weights are Wj​=∫ab​ℓj​(x)dx, where ℓj​ are the Lagrange cardinal polynomials. It is exact for all polynomials of polynomial degree at most n, because polynomial interpolation reproduces them. Newton-Cotes quadrature adds equal spacing of the quadrature nodes.

Newton-Cotes quadrature (Q(f)=∫ab​pn​(x)dx)

Words: 33
Interpolatory quadrature OurBigBook.com
A Newton-Cotes quadrature is an interpolatory quadrature rule whose quadrature nodes are equally spaced. Integrating the Lagrange interpolation polynomial gives quadrature weights Wj​=∫ab​ℓj​(x)dx. A Newton-Cotes closed quadrature includes both endpoints; an open rule uses interior quadrature nodes. Simpson's rule is the closed rule with three quadrature nodes. Its quadrature weights are (b−a)(1,4,1)/6.

Quadrature node (xi​)

Words: 36
Quadrature rule OurBigBook.com
A quadrature node is a point at which the integrand is sampled by a quadrature rule. Under the affine map x=(a+b)/2+(b−a)t/2 from [−1,1] to [a,b], a reference quadrature node ti​ becomes xi​=(a+b)/2+(b−a)ti​/2; for ordinary numerical integration, the corresponding quadrature weight is multiplied by (b−a)/2.

Quadrature weight (wi​)

Words: 41
Quadrature rule OurBigBook.com
A quadrature weight multiplies a sampled integrand value in a quadrature rule. For ordinary numerical integration, its units match those of the integration variable. Positive quadrature weights that sum to b−a make Q(f)/(b−a) a weighted average of the samples; signed quadrature weights can magnify sample perturbations, as quantified by noise sensitivity of a quadrature rule.

Degree of precision of a quadrature rule (d)

Words: 39
Quadrature rule OurBigBook.com
The degree of precision of a quadrature rule is the largest integer d such that Q(p)=Iρ​(p) for every polynomial p of polynomial degree at most d. By linearity, checking the monomials 1,x,…,xd suffices. Exactness for one higher-degree polynomial alone does not raise this degree: every lower polynomial degree must also be covered.

Noise sensitivity of a quadrature rule (∣ΔQ∣≤δ∑i​∣wi​∣)

Words: 138
Quadrature rule OurBigBook.com
If the observed samples are yi​=f(xi​)+εi​, the resulting quadrature rule changes by ΔQ=∑i​wi​εi​. Bounded sample perturbations ∣εi​∣≤δ therefore satisfy
∣ΔQ∣≤δ∑i​∣wi​∣.
(6)
For ordinary numerical integration with nonnegative quadrature weights exact on constant functions, the bound is (b−a)δ, independent of panel refinement. A common bias εi​=δ attains it: the quadrature error due to the function approximation may vanish while this sample error persists. Interpolating a fixed data set onto a finer grid likewise does not create new observations or remove its uncertainty.
With new samples whose errors are independent random variables of zero expectation and common variance σ2, a different model gives
Var(ΔQ)=σ2∑i​wi2​.
(7)
For equal panels this is σ2(b−a)2/n for the composite midpoint quadrature rule and σ2h2(n−1/2) for the composite trapezoidal rule. These variance reductions require the stated independence and unbiasedness assumptions; a common bias or correlated sample errors need not shrink. The two displayed perturbation formulas follow directly from the triangle inequality and the variance additivity for independent random variables, respectively.

Newton-Cotes closed quadrature (wj​=∫0s​Lj​(t)dt)

Words: 57
Quadrature rule OurBigBook.com
Integrate the Lagrange polynomial through values at all equally spaced nodes 0,…,s, including both endpoints. The resulting quadrature rule is exact through degree s. For even s, the node polynomial ∏j=0s​(t−j) is odd about s/2, so its integral vanishes and exactness extends through degree s+1. The weights need not remain positive as the number of nodes increases.

Simpson's rule (∫aa+2h​f≈3h​[f(a)+4f(a+h)+f(a+2h)])

Words: 898 Articles: 4
Quadrature rule OurBigBook.com
Simpson's rule approximates a definite integral by integrating the quadratic polynomial obtained by polynomial interpolation at two endpoints and their midpoint. For an interval [a,b] with a<b, write m=(a+b)/2 and h=(b−a)/2. The three quadrature nodes are a,m,b, and the quadrature rule is
S(f)=3h​[f(a)+4f(m)+f(b)].
(8)
Thus its quadrature weights are h/3,4h/3,h/3. Their sum is 2h=b−a, as required for exactness on constant functions. This is the three-node Newton-Cotes closed quadrature; its degree of precision of a quadrature rule is three, even though the interpolating polynomial has polynomial degree at most two.[ref]
For an original worked example, take the cubic polynomial f(x)=x3−2x+4 on the interval [−1,2]. Here m=1/2 and h=3/2, so the function evaluations give
f(−1)=5,f(1/2)=825​=3.125,f(2)=8.
(9)
Simpson's rule gives
S(f)=21​(5+4⋅825​+8)=451​=12.75.
(10)
An antiderivative supplies an independent exact definite integral:
∫−12​(x3−2x+4)dx=[4x4​−x2+4x]−12​=451​.
(11)
The quadratic polynomial through the same quadrature nodes is p2​(x)=23​x2−21​x+3. It differs from f between the quadrature nodes, but its definite integral agrees exactly. The picture shows why: the signed polynomial interpolation error has two lobes with opposite integrals.
Three function evaluations therefore capture more information about an integral than about the whole function. When an antiderivative is unavailable, composite Simpson's rule repeats this inexpensive local construction over a partition of an interval. For a sufficiently smooth function, halving the node spacing reduces its truncation error by approximately a factor of sixteen.

Cubic exactness of Simpson's rule (S(p)=∫ab​p(x)dx(degp≤3))

Words: 142
Simpson's rule OurBigBook.com
Polynomial interpolation makes Simpson's rule exact for every polynomial of polynomial degree at most two. To understand the extra polynomial degree, centre the coordinate at the midpoint: y=x−m, with endpoints −h,h. For any cubic polynomial p, let p2​ be its interpolating quadratic polynomial. The polynomial p−p2​ vanishes at all three quadrature nodes, so
p(x)−p2​(x)=A(x−a)(x−m)(x−b)=Ay(y2−h2),
(12)
where A is the coefficient of x3 in p. As a function of y, this is an odd function, whose integral over the symmetric interval [−h,h] is zero. Hence integrating p2​ also integrates p exactly. This is the symmetry mechanism in the nodal-polynomial criterion for quadrature exactness.
In the worked example, the polynomial difference factors as
f(x)−p2​(x)=(x+1)(x−1/2)(x−2)=y3−49​y.
(13)
Its definite integrals on [−1,1/2] and [1/2,2] are respectively 81/64 and −81/64. Their cancellation concerns signed integrals, rather than pointwise accuracy of polynomial interpolation.
The degree of precision of a quadrature rule stops at three: on the same interval, the monomial x4 gives S(x4)=69/8, whereas ∫−12​x4dx=33/5. Their difference, exact integral minus approximation, is −81/40.

Composite Simpson's rule (Sn​)

Words: 544 Articles: 1
Simpson's rule OurBigBook.com
Composite Simpson's rule applies Simpson's rule to consecutive pairs of equal subintervals. Choose a positive even integer n≥2, put h=(b−a)/n, and take quadrature nodes xj​=a+jh for j=0,…,n. There are n subintervals, n/2 two-subinterval panels, and n+1 quadrature nodes. Then
Sn​=3h​[f(x0​)+4∑j=1n/2​f(x2j−1​)+2∑j=1n/2−1​f(x2j​)+f(xn​)].
(14)
The second sum is empty when n=2, recovering one panel. The quadrature weights, before multiplication by h/3, are 1,4,2,4,…,2,4,1. Every interior even-indexed quadrature node is an endpoint of two panels, so its two endpoint contributions add to two; every odd-indexed quadrature node is a midpoint with weight four. An odd n cannot be covered by these pairs. The displayed formula requires equal spacing.[ref]
For the exponential function on the interval [0,1], the exact integral is I=e−1. With n=2, the formula gives S2​≈1.718861151876593, an overestimate of about 5.793234175×10−4. With n=128, it gives S128​≈1.718281828494607, an overestimate of about 3.556140064×10−11. The figure shows the intermediate refinements and compares every computed absolute error with the error bound for composite Simpson's rule.
For this exponential function, a geometric series also gives a compact independent formula. With q=eh and h=1/n, summing the n/2 panel contributions yields
Sn​=(e−1)3(q2−1)h(1+4q+q2)​.
(15)
A Taylor series at h=0 gives
Sn​−I=(e−1)(180h4​−1512h6​+O(h8)).
(16)
The Big O notation describes the remaining terms as h tends to zero. Consequently (Sn​−I)/(S2n​−I) tends to sixteen: the computed ratios increase from about 15.65169 to 15.99965. Fourth-order truncation error is also obtained by Richardson extrapolation of the composite trapezoidal rule.[ref]
Error bound for composite Simpson's rule (∣I−Sn​∣≤M(b−a)h4/180)
Words: 273
Composite Simpson's rule OurBigBook.com
Suppose f has a fourth derivative that is a continuous function on the interval [a,b], and ∣f(4)(x)∣≤M there. With I=∫ab​f(x)dx, the signed quadrature error here is I−Sn​. Then, for some point ξ∈(a,b),
I−Sn​=−180b−a​h4f(4)(ξ),∣I−Sn​∣≤180M(b−a)​h4.
(17)
In particular, a strictly positive fourth derivative makes I−Sn​<0: composite Simpson's rule overestimates the integral.[ref]
The bound's scale follows by adding the panel errors. A panel of width 2h has signed quadrature error −h5f(4)(ξj​)/90 and absolute error at most Mh5/90. There are (b−a)/(2h) panels, so the triangle inequality gives M(b−a)h4/180. The intermediate value theorem for the continuous fourth derivative replaces the average of the panel values f(4)(ξj​) by a single value f(4)(ξ), giving the signed formula.
The underlying single-panel calculation can be seen through the Peano kernel theorem. On the interval [0,2], define the raw Peano kernel K(t)=L[(x−t)+3​], where L is integral minus Simpson's rule and (x−t)+​=max(x−t,0) is the positive part. Then K(t)=−t3(4−3t)/12 for 0≤t≤1 and K(t)=−(2−t)3(3t−2)/12 for 1≤t≤2. It is strictly negative inside the interval and has integral −1/15. Thus
L(f)=3!1​∫02​K(t)f(4)(t)dt,∣L(f)∣≤90∥f(4)∥∞​​.
(18)
The uniform norm ∥f(4)∥∞​ bounds the fourth derivative. The polynomial f(x)=x4/24 attains equality. Scaling to endpoint spacing h multiplies the coefficient by h5, giving the sharp single-panel bound h5∥f(4)∥∞​/90. The normalized Peano kernel is K/3!, so the external factorial must be omitted when using that normalization.
For the exponential function on [0,1], f(4)(x)=ex, so M=e and the bound is e/(180n4). Its leading actual absolute error is (e−1)/(180n4), smaller than the bound because a uniform upper bound on the fourth derivative does not use its variation across the interval. The theorem bounds truncation error with exact function evaluations; sample errors and roundoff error are additional contributions, as described by noise sensitivity of a quadrature rule.

Convergence of positive quadrature on continuous functions

Words: 32
Quadrature rule OurBigBook.com
On a compact interval, positive quadrature rule weights whose sum is the interval length define uniformly bounded functionals on continuous functions. If the rules eventually integrate every fixed polynomial exactly, the Weierstrass approximation theorem proves convergence for every continuous function.

Nodal-polynomial criterion for quadrature exactness

Words: 30
Quadrature rule OurBigBook.com
An (n+1)-node quadrature rule already exact through degree n is exact through degree n+1+k exactly when its nodal polynomial
Qn+1​(x)=∏i=0n​(x−xi​)
(19)
is orthogonal to every polynomial of degree at most k.

Degree ceiling for quadrature exactness

Words: 32
Quadrature rule OurBigBook.com
No (n+1)-node quadrature rule for a positive interval weight can be exact through degree 2n+2, because it evaluates the nonnegative nodal square Qn+12​ as zero although its integral is positive.

Positivity of quadrature weights from degree 2n exactness

Words: 39
Quadrature rule OurBigBook.com
If an (n+1)-node quadrature rule for a positive interval weight is exact through degree 2n, every weight is positive. Indeed, applying the rule to the square of the corresponding degree-n Lagrange cardinal polynomial isolates that weight.

Convergence of positive quadrature rules

Words: 41
Quadrature rule OurBigBook.com
If each (n+1)-node quadrature rule is exact through degree n and has positive weights, then it converges on every continuous function. The Weierstrass approximation theorem reduces the error to the uniform polynomial-approximation error, while exactness on constants controls the sum of the weights.

Moment matching

A quadrature rule is exact through degree d precisely when its node weights reproduce the first d+1 moments of the integration measure.
