First suppose E=E∗. For u∈U=imE, choose v with Ev=u. For w∈W=kerE,
⟨u,w⟩=⟨Ev,w⟩=⟨v,Ew⟩=0.
(9)
Thus U⊆W⊥. The direct sum of vector spaces gives dimU=dimV−dimW, while the orthogonal complement has dimW⊥=dimV−dimW. Equal finite dimensions imply U=W⊥.
Conversely, suppose U=W⊥. Decompose arbitrary vectors as v=u+w and v′=u′+w′, with u,u′∈U and w,w′∈W. Since E is the indicated projection, Ev=u and Ev′=u′. Orthogonality makes the cross terms vanish, so
⟨Ev,v′⟩=⟨u,u′⟩=⟨v,Ev′⟩.
(10)
Therefore E satisfies the defining identity of its own adjoint operator. By uniqueness from part (a), E=E∗. Hence
E=E∗⟺U=W⊥.​
(11)
This is the characterization of an orthogonal projection among projections along a complementary subspace.
