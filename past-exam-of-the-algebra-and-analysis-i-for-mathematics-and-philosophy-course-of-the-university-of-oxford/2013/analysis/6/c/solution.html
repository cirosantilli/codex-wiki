The complex sine has simple zeros exactly at z=kπ, k∈Z, because sin(kπ)=0 and cos(kπ)=(−1)k=0. Hence the denominator has a zero of order three at each of these points and nowhere else.
At 0, the numerator has order two, so the quotient has a simple pole. Indeed its leading term is −π4/z. At π, the numerator has order one, so there is a pole of order two. At −π, the numerator has order three, cancelling the denominator's order-three zero, so the singularity is removable. Its extension value is
