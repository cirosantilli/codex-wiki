Linear reversibility and yield in tensile tests

 ... Branches of physics Continuum mechanics Elasticity (physics) Linear elasticity Young's modulus Tensile modulus experiment
OurBigBook.com Words: 156
Linearity and reversibility answer different questions. The proportional limit ends the interval in which axial stress is proportional to axial strain. Elastic deformation means that deformation is recovered after unloading; a nonlinear response can still be elastic. Conversely, a short straight portion of a loading curve does not by itself demonstrate recovery.
To check recovery, unload and examine the local extensometer strain relative to its initial value, allowing for instrument resolution, drift and any time dependence. A residual extension can indicate plasticity in continuum mechanics, but grip slip can produce a residual in crosshead displacement without the same local residual. Compare the channels before assigning the effect to the specimen. Yield point concerns the onset of irreversible deformation; a specified offset proof stress is an operational convention rather than Young's modulus. For example, a 0.2% offset proof stress is the intersection of the loading curve with σ=E(ε−0.002), using the correctly referenced strain. Report the fitted stress/strain window and the unloading evidence alongside the modulus, so that apparatus seating, departure from linearity and onset of plasticity in continuum mechanics remain distinguishable.
