Finite residue-field theorem for Gaussian integer orders

 ... Algebra Commutative algebra Integral domain Principal ideal domain Euclidean domain Gaussian integer
OurBigBook.com Words: 86
If α=a+bi is a Gaussian integer and P is a nonzero prime ideal of R=Z[α], then R/P is a finite field. Complex conjugation preserves R, so a nonzero β∈P gives the positive integer ββˉ​∈P. Primality supplies a rational prime p∈P. Since α satisfies the monic polynomial t2−2at+a2+b2, the ring R/P is a quotient of Fp​[t]/(Fˉ) and has at most p2 elements. It is a finite integral domain, hence a field. The proof does not require R to be a principal ideal domain.
