Graphene exfoliation and field-effect experiments (2004–2005)

 Home Chemistry Chemical substance Chemical element Carbon Graphene
OurBigBook.com Words: 129
Andre Geim, Konstantin Novoselov and collaborators mechanically cleaved graphite, identified thin flakes, and fabricated multi-terminal Hall bars on oxidized silicon. A substrate gate changed the carrier density; longitudinal electrical resistance and the Hall coefficient supplied different checks. The 2004 report included one-, two- and three-layer devices and described a few-layer semimetal interpretation. Layer identification and electrical control were separate experimental achievements.[ref]
The 2005 monolayer study used an optical microscope, electron microscopy and atomic force microscopy to select monolayers. It then measured Shubnikov-de Haas oscillations as temperature, gate voltage and magnetic field varied. Their damping inferred a density-dependent cyclotron effective mass; comparison with oscillation frequency tested a linear dispersion relation. A comparison with two-layer devices also distinguished their quantum Hall effect sequences. These are complementary tests of the monolayer model, rather than consequences of finding a thin-looking flake alone.[ref]
