Stretch factor (t(f)=supx=y​dX​(x,y)/dY​(f(x),f(y)))

For an injective function that does not increase distances, one stretch-factor convention measures the largest compression that must be undone:
t(f)=supx=y​dY​(f(x),f(y))dX​(x,y)​=Lip(f−1).
(6)
If the sharp Lipschitz constant of f is one, this equals bi-Lipschitz distortion. Fixing the scale matters: uniform compression f(x)=x/2 has stretch factor two in this convention, but bi-Lipschitz distortion one. In a geometric spanner, the corresponding stretch factor is the largest graph-route distance divided by direct Euclidean distance; the graph-to-Euclidean identification does not increase distances.
