Nirenberg and Matthaei experiment

 ... Biology Genetics Molecular genetics Gene expression Translation (biology) Genetic code
OurBigBook.com Words: 134
In 1961, Marshall Warren Nirenberg and J. Heinrich Matthaei ground washed Escherichia coli and used centrifugation to prepare a cell-free extract. Reactions at 35∘C combined ribosomes, soluble components, ATP regeneration and phenylalanine carrying a 14C isotope label, with or without polyuridylic acid, an RNA containing only U. After incubation, trichloroacetic acid precipitation, washing and counting radioactive decay detected label in insoluble material. Added polyuridylic acid strongly increased incorporation; removing ribosomes or ATP regeneration suppressed it. Product characterization supported a phenylalanine polypeptide.[ref]
The inference connects an imposed RNA template to a specific amino acid product. Yet repeated U alone cannot distinguish one-, two- or three-letter coding units: every possible group still contains only U. Neither triplet length nor the complete genetic code follows.[ref]
