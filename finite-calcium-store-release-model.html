Finite calcium-store release model

 Home Biology Signal transduction Second messenger Inositol trisphosphate IP3-sensitive calcium store
OurBigBook.com Words: 327
For an original mathematical model, imagine a sustained, imposed release pathway, with no store refill during the plotted interval. Let S be releasable store amount of substance, X excess freely dissolved cytosolic calcium amount of substance, and Q the cumulative amount removed from X into a separate sink. Choose S(0)=1amol, X(0)=Q(0)=0, cytosolic volume Vc​=1pL, release rate constant k=0.4s−1 and clearance rate constant h=0.8s−1. These are stipulated values, not physiological measurements or a model of IP3 receptor gating.
Assume fixed volume, uniform mixing, no binding, no reverse transfer, no outside entry and first-order loss from each donating compartment. Then
dtdS​=−kS,dtdX​=kS−hX,dtdQ​=hX,S+X+Q=S(0).
(1)
The last equality is conservation of mass for the tracked calcium across the store, mobile excess and sink together; it does not assert conservation inside a biological cell if the sink represents export. A constant background cb​=0.1μM is held separate from this excess bookkeeping. Since 1amol/1pL=1μM, the response is c=cb​+X/Vc​.
Solving these ordinary differential equations gives
S=S(0)e−kt,X=h−kkS(0)​(e−kt−e−ht),Q=S(0)−S−X.
(2)
Release kS declines from the start, yet X initially rises because delivery exceeds clearance. Its peak occurs when kS=hX, at t∗​=log(h/k)/(h−k)=log(2)/0.4≈1.73s. There X=0.25amol and c=0.35μM. Ultimately S and X tend to zero, Q tends to 1amol and c returns to cb​. The delayed peak is a consequence of material balance, even though this model has no delayed channel activation.
This distinction guides interpretation: a large released amount need not mean a large simultaneous free cytosolic concentration. Clearance and volume intervene. Real biological cells add binding, store refilling, IP3 turnover and IP3 receptor regulation; the two plotted curves isolate one finite-store effect rather than explaining every calcium response.
