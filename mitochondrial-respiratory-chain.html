Mitochondrial respiratory chain

 Home Chemistry Biochemistry Energy metabolism Electron transport chain
OurBigBook.com Words: 190 Articles: 4
NADH supplies two electrons to respiratory complex I, then ubiquinone, respiratory complex III, cytochrome c and cytochrome c oxidase; terminal oxygen reduction forms water. Succinate dehydrogenase, or respiratory complex II, supplies another entry to ubiquinone. Respiratory complex I, respiratory complex III and cytochrome c oxidase move protons outward; respiratory complex II does not.[ref]
For an original electron balance, supply exactly 6μmol of NADH to an ideal preparation with no other electron donor or competing electron acceptor. The donor supplies 12μmol of electrons. Complete reduction of one oxygen molecule needs four electrons, so the corresponding oxygen consumption is 3μmol. Adding the two formal half-reactions makes the result explicit:

The cancelling electrons determine the donor-to-oxygen ratio. These chemical equations do not count transmembrane protons or determine an ATP yield. Chemical protons consumed in water formation and transported protons have different roles in the ledger.
