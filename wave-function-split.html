Wave function (ψ(x,t))

 Home Physics Branches of physics Quantum mechanics
OurBigBook.com Words: 5k Articles: 41
A wavefunction is the position representation of a pure state. In the one-dimensional model used here, ψ(x,t) takes values in the complex numbers, and the Born rule gives
ρ(x,t)=∣ψ(x,t)∣2,Pr(a<x<b)=∫ab​ρ(x,t)dx.
(1)
The probability density ρ has units of inverse length; the wavefunction has units of inverse square root of length. A value of ρ at a point is not itself a probability: probabilities come from an integral over a region. For the infinite square well ground state, ρ(L/2)=2/L, whereas the probability of the exact single point L/2 is zero. The interesting question is how much probability a finite interval contains.
Two wavefunctions differing only by a global phase represent the same pure state. Relative quantum phases between components of a quantum superposition affect matter-wave interference, so retaining the full complex number amplitude is essential. This scalar description covers a particle without active spin degrees of freedom; a general electron wavefunction can have two spin components, with the position probability density obtained by adding the squared modulus of each component.
