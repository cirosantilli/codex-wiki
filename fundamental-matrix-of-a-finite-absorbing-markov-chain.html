Fundamental matrix of a finite absorbing Markov chain (N=(I−Q)−1)

 ... Area of mathematics Probability and statistics Probability theory Markov process Markov chain Absorbing state
OurBigBook.com Words: 171
For a finite Markov chain in which an absorbing state is accessible from every state, order the nonabsorbing states first and write
P=(Q0​RI​),N=(I−Q)−1=∑n≥0​Qn.
(26)
Here I is the identity matrix and N is the matrix inverse of I−Q. With T the first hitting time of the absorbing set,
Nij​=Ei​∑n=0T−1​1{Xn​=j}​,B=NR,t=N1.
(27)
Thus N gives the expected occupation count before absorption, including the initial visit; Bia​ is the absorption probability of state a; and ti​ is the expected hitting time of the absorbing set. The vector 1 has every entry one. See Grinstead and Snell, section 11.2, theorems 11.4-11.6.
For the toy routine in absorbing state, direct matrix multiplication gives
N=(12/54/5​4/58/5​),B=(3/51/5​2/54/5​),t=(16/512/5​).
(28)
The first row of N says that starting at u gives 2.4 visits to u and 0.8 to v before termination. Their sum is the mean duration 3.2. The identities (I−Q)N=I, B=QB+R, and t=1+Qt independently verify the inverse, outcome, and duration calculations. The absorbing set must be reachable from every state for these finite formulas: applying them to all nonabsorbing states in the closed-cycle example would leave a persistent cycle in Q and make I−Q singular.
