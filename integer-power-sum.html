Integer power sum (Sp​(N)=∑k=0N−1​kp)

OurBigBook.com Words: 450 Articles: 1
An integer power sum adds a fixed nonnegative integer power of consecutive integers. Let N be a nonnegative integer and p a nonnegative integer. For p=0, use the constant polynomial x0=1, including at zero, so S0​(N)=N. Summing the Bernoulli polynomial finite difference identity makes the intermediate terms cancel and gives
Sp​(N)=∑k=0N−1​kp=p+1Bp+1​(N)−Bp+1​(0)​=p+11​∑j=0p​(jp+1​)Bj​Np+1−j.
(14)
The sum is empty when N=0. For the endpoints 1,…,N, the formula valid also at p=0 is instead
∑k=1N​kp=p+1Bp+1​(N+1)−Bp+1​(1)​.
(15)
For p≥1, Bp+1​(1)=Bp+1​(0), and this becomes the standard positive-endpoint formula. The exception at p=0 is essential: B1​(1)−B1​(0)=1.[ref]
For p≥1, adding Np to Sp​(N) changes the B1​ contribution from −Np/2 to +Np/2. Equivalently,
∑k=1N​kp=p+11​∑j=0p​(jp+1​)Bj+​Np+1−j(p≥1),
(16)
where only B1+​ differs from our Bernoulli numbers. A quick endpoint check is p=1: the sums over 0,…,N−1 and 1,…,N are N(N−1)/2 and N(N+1)/2, respectively. The latter is a triangular number.
