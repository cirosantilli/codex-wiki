Resistance pressure gauge (R=R(p,T,history))

 ... Statistical physics Thermodynamics Pressure Pressure measurement High-pressure experimental metrology Pressure calibration
OurBigBook.com Words: 312
A resistance pressure gauge is a pressure sensor whose electrical resistance is calibrated against pressure. Even a simple local response needs both pressure and temperature. For an original illustrative model define
y=R0​R​−1=αp+β(T−T0​)+h,p=αy−β(T−T0​)−h​,
(32)
where R0​ is the reference electrical resistance at p=0, T=T0​, and zero history offset. The derivatives α and β are local sensitivities with units Pa−1 and K−1, respectively; h is a dimensionless history offset. Constant sensitivities are an approximation over a chosen range, not material constants valid at every pressure.
Choose synthetic coefficients α=0.020GPa−1 and β=2.0×10−4K−1, with T−T0​=10K and h=0. A measured ratio R/R0​=1.022 then gives p=(0.022−0.002)/0.020=1.00GPa. Ignoring the temperature change gives 1.10GPa, a 0.10GPa error. This model is deliberately stipulated; these numbers are not measured manganin coefficients or Bridgman's data.
If two histories differ by Δh=0.0004, their outputs at the same pressure differ by 0.0004R0​; interpreting both with a single history-free curve gives an apparent pressure separation Δh/α=0.020GPa. The half separation is 0.010GPa. Reversing the drive can additionally cause mechanical friction hysteresis, independently of this sensor model. Record both the electrical calibration history and the loading direction.
For independent small standard measurement uncertainties uy​,uT​,uh​ and exact stipulated α,β, first-order propagation gives
up2​=α2uy2​+β2uT2​+uh2​​.
(33)
For uy​=10−4, uT​=0.5K, and uh​=10−4, this is up​=0.00866GPa. Real calibration also includes coefficient measurement uncertainties, correlations, reference-point measurement uncertainty, and any unresolved nonhydrostatic loading. A sensor calibrates its local environment; it cannot by itself certify the absence of deviatoric stress throughout the sample.
