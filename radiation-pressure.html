Radiation pressure (prad​)

 Home Physics Branches of physics Statistical physics Thermodynamics Pressure
OurBigBook.com Words: 1k Articles: 4
Radiation pressure is the normal momentum flux transferred by electromagnetic radiation to a surface. A photon travelling in vacuum carries momentum pγ​=Eγ​/c=hν/c, where Eγ​ is its energy, ν its frequency, h the Planck constant and c the speed of light. Photon absorption transfers that momentum; specular reflection reverses its normal component. This lets light exert a force even when almost no energy becomes heat in the receiver.
Two useful formulas describe different preparations. A directed beam normally incident on a stationary perfect absorber gives pressure I/c, and on a stationary perfect mirror gives 2I/c. Here I is light intensity, the incident power per beam cross-sectional area perpendicular to propagation, in Wm−2. An isotropic photon gas has pressure u/3, where u=U/V is its radiation internal energy per volume, in Jm−3. Stored energy density and travelling energy flux must be distinguished: an isotropic field can have zero net energy flux and positive radiation pressure.
The physical appeal is that the same momentum conservation connects a single photon collision, a mirror's measurable force, and a radiation-filled cavity's equation of state.
