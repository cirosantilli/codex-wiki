Manganese dioxide peroxide contact demonstration

 Home Chemistry Chemical reaction Chemical kinetics Catalysis Heterogeneous catalysis
OurBigBook.com Words: 184
The University of Washington's Decomposition of H₂O₂ with MnO₂ describes a supervised demonstration using solid manganese dioxide and hydrogen peroxide listed as 30%. Its cylinder version uses a spatula to introduce the solid; the stated readout is immediate molecular oxygen bubbles and heat production. An alternate bottle version replaces the contents of a tea bag with the solid and suspends it above the liquid. Releasing the bag brings it into contact with the liquid. The bag-above-liquid stage supplies a before-contact comparison; the page gives no timed gas collection, replicated blank trials or quantitative reaction rates.[ref]
The balanced net chemical equation is 2H2​O2​⟶2H2​O+O2​. Appearance of gas after contact supports a contact-dependent acceleration of decomposition under these conditions. It does not distinguish surface conversion from a contribution of dissolved material, establish regeneration, or measure an activation energy. The simultaneous heat release also means that temperature can change after contact. A matched no-solid comparison and an empty-bag comparison would test background decomposition and the bag's contribution; these are proposed controls, not reported trials. The catalysed peroxide comparison supplies a complementary inspected teaching experiment with separate single-addition and paired-addition comparisons.
