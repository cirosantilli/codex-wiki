Conjugate base

 Home Chemistry Acid-base chemistry Base (chemistry)
OurBigBook.com Words: 398 Articles: 3
A conjugate base is what remains after a chemical acid donates one proton. In the conjugate acid-base pair HA/A−, HA is the conjugate acid of A−; accepting a proton reverses the conversion. For example, acetic acid CH3​COOH loses a proton to give its conjugate base CH3​COO−. Partners differ by one proton and one unit of electric charge; they need not always be neutral and negative. Hydronium/water and water/hydroxide are also conjugate acid-base pairs.
At a prescribed pH, the ideal dilute acid dissociation constant determines the partition of a monoprotic acid inventory between its chemical species:
αA−​=Ka​+10−pHKa​​,αHA​=Ka​+10−pH10−pH​.
(163)
These dimensionless fractions sum to one. They are the weak acid dissociation fraction and its complement when the only acid-bearing chemical species are HA and A−. Equal fractions occur at pH=pKa​ in this ideal approximation. For nonideal chemical solutions, equality of thermodynamic activities need not mean equality of concentrations.
For stipulated pKa 5, the conjugate base fractions at pH 4, 5 and 6 are 1/11, 1/2 and 10/11. Here an external reservoir sets pH; its ions must supply the additional electric charge balance.
