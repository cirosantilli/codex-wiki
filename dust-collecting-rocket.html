Dust-collecting rocket (mv˙=αu−βv2)

 Home Physics Branches of physics Classical mechanics Momentum Rocket equation
OurBigBook.com Words: 249 Articles: 2
A classical rocket ejecting fuel at rate α>0 and relative exhaust speed u>0, while collecting stationary dust of mass βdx per traveled distance, obeys
m˙=βv−α,mv˙=αu−βv2.
(8)
These are mass conservation and momentum conservation for the rocket, incoming dust and outgoing gas. The dust-loading term supplies the momentum needed to accelerate incoming stationary material. Put λ=α/(βu)​ and V=λu. While the engines operate and 0≤v<V, an initially stationary rocket satisfies
m0​m​=(VV−v​)(λ−1)/2(VV+v​)−(λ+1)/2.
(9)
For λ>1, continuing to V would drive total mass to zero. For 0<λ<1, total mass instead grows without bound and V is approached only after infinite time under indefinitely maintained thrust. In both cases a finite fuel supply runs out before that limiting speed. Total stored mass must not be confused with remaining fuel.
