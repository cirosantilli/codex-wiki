Fieller's theorem ((C−rE)2≤q2(vC​−2rvCE​+r2vE​))

 Home Mathematics Area of mathematics Probability and statistics Statistical inference Confidence interval
OurBigBook.com Words: 157
For jointly approximately normal estimates (C,E) with estimated variances vC​,vE​ and covariance vCE​, a confidence interval for the ratio C/E, where E=0, can be obtained by inverting tests of C−rE=0. For each proposed ratio r, the contrast has estimate C−rE and variance vC​−2rvCE​+r2vE​. Retain r when its absolute standardized contrast is at most the chosen critical value q. This gives the displayed quadratic inequality, with coefficients A=E2−q2vE​, B=−2(CE−q2vCE​), D=C2−q2vC​. When A>0 and the discriminant is positive, retain the interval between the roots. When A<0, a confidence set can instead be two unbounded rays or the entire real line. Degenerate linear cases are interpreted directly from the inequality. A bounded interval must not be forced when the denominator is poorly separated from zero. Known covariance matrix and a joint normal distribution give exact normal critical values; estimated covariance usually gives approximate coverage, unless an appropriate exact common-scale pivot exists.
