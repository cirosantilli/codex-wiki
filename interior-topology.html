Interior (topology, A∘=intX​A)

The interior of a subset A of a topological space X is the union of all open sets contained in A, equivalently the largest open subset of A. A point lies in A∘=intX​A exactly when it has an open neighbourhood contained in A. In a metric space, this says that some open ball B(x,r), with r>0, is contained in A. The interior therefore identifies the points with room to move locally while staying in A. [ref]
For the half-open interval A=[0,1) in the real line, A∘=(0,1). Every point strictly between the endpoints has a sufficiently small open ball inside A, but every open ball about zero also contains negative points. Although 0∈A, it is not in the interior; it is a boundary point instead. Together, the interior and closure bracket the original subset: A∘⊆A⊆A.
The ambient topological space matters: for X=[0,1] with its subspace topology, intX​X=X, whereas intR​X=(0,1). The diagram accompanying boundary of a set illustrates the relative open ball at zero. A subset is open exactly when A=A∘, and taking the interior twice has the same effect as taking it once. The complement identity intX​A=X∖clX​(X∖A) links interior directly to closure.
