For an infinite circular cylinder of radius R, assume the electrostatic potential is independent of the axial coordinate. An angular Fourier mode sn​cosnθ, or sn​sinnθ, in the prescribed jump ∂r​Φ∣R+​−∂r​Φ∣R−​=−σ(θ) produces the same angular mode in the potential, multiplied by
2nRsn​​{(r/R)n,(R/r)n,​r<R,r>R.​
(17)
Here r<​=min(r,R) and r>​=max(r,R) in the abbreviated header formula. Separation of variables selects rn inside by regularity and r−n outside by decay. Continuity equates their boundary values, and the derivative jump is minus 2n/R times that value. A nonzero angular mean of the surface charge density introduces a logarithmic term, so zero mean is necessary for a potential decaying at infinity in this two-dimensional reduction.
