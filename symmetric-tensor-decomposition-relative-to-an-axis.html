Symmetric tensor decomposition relative to an axis (T=αI+βAAT+BAT+ABT+C)

 ... Algebra Linear algebra Multilinear algebra Tensor Cartesian tensor Cartesian second-rank tensor
OurBigBook.com Words: 106
For a real symmetric Cartesian second-rank tensor T and a nonzero vector A, require B⋅A=0 and C symmetric and traceless with CA=0. Put q=ATA, s=ATTA, t=trT, and P=I−AAT/q. The unique decomposition is
α=2t−s/q​,β=2q3s/q−t​,B=qTA​−q2sA​,C=PTP−αP.
(30)
The orthogonal projection P isolates the plane perpendicular to A. Splitting I=P+AAT/q on both sides of T gives its longitudinal, mixed and transverse blocks, proving reconstruction. The transverse trace fixes α, the longitudinal component fixes β, and the mixed block fixes B, proving uniqueness. The remaining transverse block C has two degrees of freedom. Together α,β,B,C carry 1+1+2+2=6 components, the number in a symmetric three-by-three matrix.
