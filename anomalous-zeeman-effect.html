Anomalous Zeeman effect

 Home Physics Atomic physics Zeeman splitting
OurBigBook.com Words: 417 Articles: 3
The anomalous Zeeman effect is Zeeman splitting whose pattern is not the simple orbital triplet. Spin and spin-orbit interaction make the upper and lower Landé g-factors differ. The same weak-field formula still applies when its assumptions hold: “anomalous” does not mean nonlinear in the magnetic field.
For example, take an electronic transition with Lu​=1,Su​=1/2,Ju​=1/2 and Ll​=0,Sl​=1/2,Jl​=1/2, neglecting hyperfine structure. With gs​=2, the formula above gives gu​=2/3 and gl​=2. The two allowed q=0 transitions have dimensionless shifts gu​mu​−gl​ml​=±2/3, while the q=±1 transitions give ±4/3. Thus there are four frequencies, even though each level contains only two magnetic quantum numbers. This is an independent worked deduction; it should not be substituted for the unresolved widths in the 1896 experiment.
