Topological space ((X,τ))

 Home Mathematics Area of mathematics Geometry and topology Topology
OurBigBook.com Words: 3k Articles: 73
A topological space is a set X together with a topology τ⊆P(X), a family of subsets satisfying
∅,X∈τ,⋃i∈I​Ui​∈τ(Ui​∈τ),⋂j=1n​Uj​∈τ(Uj​∈τ).
(3)
Here P(X) is the power set; the union may have arbitrarily many members, while the intersection must be finite. The members of τ are the open sets. These are axioms about subsets, with no assumed metric or surrounding Euclidean space. [ref]
For X={a,b}, the discrete topology is τd​={∅,{a},{b},X}, while the indiscrete topology is τi​={∅,X}. Both satisfy the axioms. They describe different topological spaces on the same set: the first is Hausdorff and disconnected, while the second is connected and not Hausdorff. Each is compact because X is a finite set.
The identity function i:(X,τd​)→(X,τi​) is a continuous map: the only target open sets have inverse images ∅ and X. Its inverse is not a continuous map, since the target open set {a} has inverse image {a}, which is not an open set in τi​. Thus even a continuous bijection need not be a homeomorphism.
