Floquet growth rate (γ=P−1logρ(N))

The Floquet growth rate measures the exponential trend of a periodic linear differential equation over many coefficient periods. A Floquet multiplier m measures the factor per period T; the corresponding rate per unit time is
γm​=Tlog∣m∣​=Reρm​,eTρm​=m,

where ρm​ is a Floquet exponent. Taking the real part removes the ambiguity in the complex logarithm.
For the scalar example under periodic solution, m=e2πa and the Floquet growth rate is a, independently of b. Indeed,

The instantaneous logarithmic derivative keeps oscillating even though its average over complete periods is exactly a. Sampling at the same phase in each period removes the modulation altogether: y(nT)=mny(0).
For the general finite-dimensional homogeneous linear differential equation x′=A(t)x, assume that A is continuous on the real line and T-periodic, with T>0. Normalize its fundamental matrix by Φ(0)=I and set M=Φ(T). The maximal asymptotic exponential rate, using any induced matrix norm, is
γ=limt→∞​tlog∥Φ(t)∥​=Tlogρ(M)​,ρ(M)=maxj​∣mj​∣.
(62)
Here ρ(M) is the spectral radius, mj​ are the Floquet multipliers, and invertibility gives ρ(M)>0. This concerns growth of the solution operator; it does not say that every initial condition attains the maximum.[ref]
To prove the formula, write t=nT+s with 0≤s<T. Periodicity and uniqueness give
Φ(t)=Φ(s)Mn.
(63)
Both Φ(s) and Φ(s)−1 have uniformly bounded matrix norms on a period. Consequently ∥Φ(t)∥ differs from ∥Mn∥ by at most fixed multiplicative factors. The spectral radius formula gives ∥Mn∥1/n→ρ(M), proving the claimed limit without choosing a matrix logarithm.
For a particular nonzero initial condition v, decompose v into the generalized eigenspaces of M over the complex numbers. The rate of Φ(t)v is the largest T−1log∣mj​∣ among components that are nonzero. Generic data have a component in the dominant generalized eigenspaces and attain γ; data contained in the sum of the subdominant generalized eigenspaces have a smaller rate. For example, with A=diag(0.2,−0.3), the maximal rate is 0.2, while v=(0,1) decays at rate −0.3. These invariant subspaces describe initial data at phase zero. Their transported versions E(t)=Φ(t)E(0) can vary with time and satisfy E(t+T)=E(t).

Its exponential rate is still −1: the Jordan block contributes a polynomial factor, whose logarithm divided by t tends to zero. At a unit-modulus Floquet multiplier, such polynomial growth can even be unbounded with zero exponential rate; A=(00​10​) gives etAv=(t,1). Thus zero Floquet growth rate alone proves neither boundedness nor a periodic solution.
