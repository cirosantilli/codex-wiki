Graph Laplacian quadratic form (xTLx=∑{i,j}∈E​(xi​−xj​)2)

 Home Mathematics Area of mathematics Foundations of mathematics Graph theory Laplacian matrix
OurBigBook.com Words: 146
Assign a real value xi​ to each vertex. The Graph Laplacian quadratic form measures disagreement across edges:
xTLx=∑{i,j}∈E​(xi​−xj​)2≥0.
(48)
This makes the Graph Laplacian a positive semidefinite matrix. The energy is small when neighbours have similar values, even if distant vertices have very different values; it depends on connections rather than where the graph is drawn. In the three-vertex path graph, take x=(2,0,−1)T. Then
Lx=(2,−1,−1)T,xTLx=2⋅2+0⋅(−1)+(−1)⋅(−1)=5,
(49)
and the two edges contribute (2−0)2=4 and (0−(−1))2=1.
For a short proof, expand xT(D−A)x. Each edge contributes xi2​+xj2​ through the degree matrix and −2xi​xj​ through the adjacency matrix, yielding (xi​−xj​)2. The sum over unordered edges has no factor 1/2; a sum over all ordered pairs does:
xTLx=21​∑i,j​Aij​(xi​−xj​)2.
(50)
Equivalently, let B be an oriented incidence matrix, with one row per edge. Then Bx records endpoint differences and L=BTB, independently of the chosen orientations. This translates a local comparison into a global quadratic form and makes its nonnegativity visible.
