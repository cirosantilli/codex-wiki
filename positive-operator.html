Positive operator (T≥0)

 ... Analysis Functional analysis Hilbert space Riesz representation theorem Adjoint operator Hermitian operator
OurBigBook.com Words: 611 Articles: 7
A bounded Hermitian operator T on a Hilbert space is positive, also called a positive semidefinite operator, when
qT​(x)=⟨Tx,x⟩≥0for every x∈H.
(33)
The value must be real as well as nonnegative. This permits zero values on nonzero vectors: the zero linear operator is positive. For a bounded Hermitian operator, positivity is equivalent to its spectrum being contained in [0,∞); in finite dimension, this is the condition that all eigenvalues are nonnegative.[ref]
The Hermitian operator diagram separates three cases. D=diag(1,−1) is self-adjoint but not positive, because qD​((0,1)T)=−1. The orthogonal projection P=diag(1,0) is positive semidefinite, with qP​(x)=∣x1​∣2, but is not strictly positive on every nonzero vector. The complex Hermitian matrix H=(2−i​i2​) is positive definite because qH​(ae1​+be3​)=∣a∣2+3∣b∣2>0 whenever (a,b)=(0,0). For real symmetric matrices, MIT's lecture 27 relates these tests to the geometry of quadratic forms and their minima.[ref]
A fundamental source of positive operators is T∗T for a bounded linear operator T:H→K:
(T∗T)∗=T∗T,⟨T∗Tx,x⟩=⟨Tx,Tx⟩=∥Tx∥2≥0.
(34)
For the complex matrix T=(12​i1−i​) used above,
T∗T=(52+i​2−i3​),qT∗T​(x)=∣x1​+ix2​∣2+∣2x1​+(1−i)x2​∣2.
(35)
Its eigenvalues are 4−6​ and 4+6​, both positive. Positivity organizes the squared error minimized by a least-squares solution: the same T∗T occurs in the normal equation for a linear inverse problem. The positive square root of an operator gives the converse squared-length description, qT​(x)=∥T1/2x∥2 for every bounded positive operator T.
On a real Hilbert space, nonnegativity of ⟨Tx,x⟩ alone does not imply self-adjointness, so the Hermitian operator hypothesis is part of the definition. On a complex Hilbert space, real-valuedness of all these expressions already forces self-adjointness by the polarization identity.
Strict positivity and a uniform positive lower bound also differ in infinite dimension. On the l2 sequence space, the bounded Hermitian operator (Tx)n​=xn​/n satisfies
qT​(x)=∑n=1∞​n∣xn​∣2​>0(x=0),qT​(en​)=n1​⟶0.
(36)
Consequently no γ>0 satisfies qT​(x)≥γ∥x∥2 for every x. Compare the positive definite symmetric operator and uniformly positive definite symmetric operator conditions.
