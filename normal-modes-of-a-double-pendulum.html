Normal modes of a double pendulum (ω±2​=(g/ℓ)(2±2​))

For equal lengths and mass ratio μ=m/M, the squared angular frequencies are ω02​(1+μ±μ(1+μ)​), where M is the upper mass, m the lower mass, and ω02​=g/ℓ. The equal-mass case μ=1 admits particularly simple mode shapes.
Use the ideal model of the double pendulum, with both angles measured from the downward vertical. Around θ1​=θ2​=0, the Taylor expansions sinθi​=θi​+O(θi3​) and cosθi​=1−θi2​/2+O(θi4​) give the quadratic Lagrangian
L2​=21​θ˙TMθ˙−21​θTKθ,θ=(θ1​θ2​​),M=mℓ2(21​11​),K=mgℓ(20​01​).
(35)
Here both angles and their angular velocities are counted as small. Consequently the correction to cos(θ1​−θ2​)θ˙1​θ˙2​ is fourth order and does not enter L2​. The Euler-Lagrange equations give Mθ¨+Kθ=0. The mass matrix is not diagonal: an angular motion of the upper rod carries the lower mass too.[ref]
For a normal mode, put θ(t)=acos(ωt+φ) with fixed amplitude vector a. The resulting generalized eigenvalue problem for small oscillations is Ka=ω2Ma. With λ=ω2ℓ/g, its characteristic equation is
0=det(2−2λ−λ​−λ1−λ​)=λ2−4λ+2.
(36)
Solving this quadratic and using the second row, a2​/a1​=λ/(1−λ), gives
ω−2​ω+2​​=ℓg​(2−2​),=ℓg​(2+2​),​a−​a+​​=(12​​),=(1−2​​).​
(37)
The lower-frequency normal mode has the rods in phase, with the lower angle 2​ times the upper one. The higher-frequency normal mode has opposite signs, corresponding to an oscillation phase difference of π. These are ratios of absolute angles. The horizontal displacements are instead x1​≃ℓθ1​ and x2​≃ℓ(θ1​+θ2​); their ratios are 1+2​ and 1−2​ respectively. Thus the lower mass moves horizontally opposite to the upper mass in the faster normal mode.
Both squared angular frequencies are positive. Their ratio satisfies ω+​/ω−​=1+2​, independently of m, g and ℓ. For a constructed model with ℓ=1m and g=9.81ms−2, the angular frequencies are approximately 2.3972 and 5.7874s−1, and the oscillation periods 2π/ω±​ are 2.6211 and 1.0857s. Doubling both masses leaves these unchanged; multiplying both rod lengths by four doubles both oscillation periods.
The eigenvectors are not orthogonal in the ordinary dot product: a−T​a+​=−1. They satisfy mass-matrix orthogonality of normal modes, since
a−T​Ma+​=0,a−T​Ma−​=mℓ2(4+22​),a+T​Ma+​=mℓ2(4−22​).
(38)
Writing θ=a−​q−​+a+​q+​ therefore removes the cross terms from both quadratic energies. The two normal coordinates satisfy q¨​±​+ω±2​q±​=0. This is why the normal modes are useful: one coupled system becomes two independently evolving harmonic oscillators, and any small release can be decomposed into them. At larger angles, the discarded terms in the double pendulum equations of motion matter and this exact linear superposition no longer applies.
