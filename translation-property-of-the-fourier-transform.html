Translation property of the Fourier transform (f(⋅−a)​(k)=e−ikaf​(k))

 Home Mathematics Area of mathematics Analysis Fourier analysis Fourier inversion theorem
OurBigBook.com Words: 72
For an integrable function and the angular-frequency convention,
F[f(x−a)](k)=e−ikaf​(k),
(53)
so a translation multiplies each frequency component by a factor of modulus one. The amplitude of every component stays the same, while its wave phase changes by −ka. For example, delaying cos(2πνt) by a produces cos(2πνt−2πνa): faster oscillations accumulate a larger wave phase shift for the same delay. Substituting y=x−a in the defining Fourier transform is the entire proof. Conversely, this multiplier in the Fourier transform produces a translation after Fourier inversion.
