Work (W=∫C​F⋅dr)

 Home Physics Branches of physics Classical mechanics Energy
OurBigBook.com Words: 799 Articles: 5
Work measures the energy transferred by a force as its point of application moves. For a point mass following a path C, add the contribution of the force along each small displacement:
W=∫C​F⋅dr=∫ti​tf​​F(t)⋅v(t)dt.
(103)
This line integral uses the actual path, and the dot product selects the component of force along the motion. A large force perpendicular to every small displacement does zero work; holding something still also does zero mechanical work on it, even if holding it feels tiring. The SI unit is J=Nm. [ref]
The usefulness of work is that it connects a description in terms of forces and distances to one in terms of energy. The work-energy theorem gives the change in kinetic energy from the total net work, often without solving for the entire motion. The work of one selected force equals that change only when the other forces contribute zero total work.
