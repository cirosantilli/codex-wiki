Dobiński's formula (Bn​=e−1∑j=0∞​jn/j!)

 ... Mathematics Area of mathematics Combinatorics Set partition Stirling numbers of the second kind Bell number
OurBigBook.com Words: 88
For X with Poisson distribution of mean one, every factorial moment satisfies E[(X)k​]=1. The polynomial identity
xn=∑k=0n​S(n,k)(x)k​
(36)
expresses powers in terms of falling factorials, where S(n,k) are Stirling numbers of the second kind. Substituting X and using linearity of expectation proves
E[Xn]=∑k=0n​S(n,k)=Bn​.
(37)
On the other hand, the probability mass function gives E[Xn]=e−1∑j=0∞​jn/j!. This proves Dobiński's formula and explains why a count of set partitions is a moment of a Poisson distribution. For example, E[X3]=E[(X)3​+3(X)2​+X]=1+3+1=5=B3​. At n=0, take j0=1 also for j=0, obtaining B0​=1. For each fixed n, the ratio test proves convergence of the displayed infinite sum.
