Suppose a three-dimensional Debye model is normalized to 3Nm​ vibrational modes in volume V. Its low-temperature heat-capacity coefficient is the one displayed, and its spherical wavevector cutoff obeys kD3​=6π2Nm​/V. The Debye temperature is ΘD​=ℏvD​kD​/kB​. At fixed measured β and volume, changing the counting convention by Nm​↦cNm​ raises both ΘD​ and kD​ by c1/3; the inferred average sound speed is unchanged.
For a mole of formula units containing r atoms each, a conventional full atomic-mode count uses Nm​=rNA​ and tends to lattice heat capacity 3rR at high temperature. A reduced convention using Nm​=NA​ instead has three effective modes per formula unit. These are different continuum normalizations, not a claim that the real crystal has only three modes per formula unit. A real multi-atom primitive cell has three acoustic branches and additional optical branches; a Debye sphere preserving the full atomic count is an effective representation of that spectrum, rather than its literal first Brillouin zone.
