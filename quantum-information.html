Quantum information

 Home Physics Branches of physics Quantum theory Quantum information theory
OurBigBook.com Words: 548 Articles: 1
Quantum information is information encoded in quantum states and processed through quantum channels or quantum measurements. A qubit supplies a two-dimensional state space; quantum phase and entanglement affect which operations and outcome statistics are possible. The Born rule connects the state description with the probabilities of classical measurement records.
The continuously varying probability amplitudes of a qubit do not supply a readable list of arbitrarily many classical bits. A measurement produces a classical record with state-dependent probabilities. Quantum state tomography estimates the state from many identically prepared systems. For a classical label encoded into one transmitted qubit, with no additional shared resource, Holevo's theorem bounds the mutual information available from a measurement by one bit. This bound concerns information about the label, rather than the number of possible outcome labels a measuring device can print. [ref]
