Rare-outlier three-point distribution (P(X=0)=1−q,P(X=±1)=q/2)

 ... Mathematics Area of mathematics Probability and statistics Probability theory Probability distribution Kurtosis
OurBigBook.com Words: 206
For 0<q≤1, put probability mass 1−q at zero and q/2 at each of −1 and 1. This symmetric probability distribution has mean zero. Its second and fourth moments are both q, because the nonzero outcomes have square and fourth power equal to one:
Var(X)=q,E[X4]=q,κ(X)=q2q​=q1​.
(123)
For q=1/4 the kurtosis is 4; for q=1/100 it is 100. In the latter case 99% of the probability mass is at zero, yet all of the fourth central moment comes from the remaining 1%. The standard score Z=X/q​ takes values 0 and ±q−1/2, with variance one. Each nonzero outcome contributes
2q​(q−1/2)4=2q1​
(124)
to kurtosis. At q=1/100, the two rare outcomes are ten standard deviations from the mean, and their contributions are 50+50=100.
Every member has finite support of a probability distribution, so all its moments are finite. Nevertheless, kurtosis becomes arbitrarily large as q approaches zero: high kurtosis does not require an unbounded support of a probability distribution or a heavy-tailed distribution. At q=0 the variance vanishes and kurtosis is undefined. At q=1/3 this discrete probability distribution even has the normal distribution value κ=3 and excess kurtosis zero, so those values do not establish a normal distribution.
