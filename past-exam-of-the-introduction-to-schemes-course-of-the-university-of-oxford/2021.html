Let X=SpecR be an affine scheme, and let (Uλ​)λ∈Λ​ be an open cover. Write each complement as a Zariski closed subset X∖Uλ​=V(Iλ​) for an ideal Iλ​⊆R. The intersection rule for Zariski closed subsets gives
∅=⋂λ∈Λ​V(Iλ​)=V(∑λ∈Λ​Iλ​).
(1)
A proper ideal lies in a maximal ideal, which is a prime ideal; consequently V(J)=∅ forces J=R. Thus 1∈∑λ​Iλ​. By the definition of a sum of ideals, this membership is witnessed by finitely many elements:
1=a1​+⋯+ar​,aj​∈Iλj​​.
(2)
Hence ∑j=1r​Iλj​​=R, so ⋂j=1r​V(Iλj​​)=∅, and therefore X=⋃j=1r​Uλj​​. If R is the zero ring, X is empty and the empty subcover suffices. This proves
Every affine scheme is quasi-compact.​
(3)
The proof uses only complements, the intersection rule for Zariski closed subsets, and the finite expression of an element in a sum of ideals.

The quotient. Evaluation Z[t]→Z[i], t↦i, is a surjective ring homomorphism whose kernel of a ring homomorphism is (t2+1). Indeed, division by this monic polynomial leaves a remainder a+bt, and a+bi=0 for a,b∈Z forces a=b=0. Taking the further quotient ring modulo p gives the explicit isomorphism
Fp​[t]/(t2+1) ∼ ​Z[i]/(p),t⟼i.​
(4)
All fibers, including the generic fiber. For a point s∈SpecZ with residue field κ(s), its scheme-theoretic fiber is
Xs​=Spec(Z[i]⊗Z​κ(s)).
(5)
At the generic point s=(0), the residue field is Q, and t2+1 is an irreducible polynomial over Q, so X(0)​=SpecQ(i). For a closed point (p) the residue field is Fp​. The permitted criterion for a square root of −1 gives three cases. If p≡1(mod4), choose a∈Fp​ with a2=−1; the two roots a,−a are distinct, so the Chinese remainder theorem gives Fp​[t]/(t2+1)≅Fp​×Fp​. If p≡3(mod4), the quadratic has no root and is an irreducible polynomial, giving the finite field Fp2​. Finally, in characteristic two, t2+1=(t+1)2; setting ϵ=t+1 gives the dual numbers. Thus
Xs​≅⎩⎨⎧​SpecQ(i),SpecFp​ ⨿ SpecFp​,SpecFp2​,Spec(F2​[ϵ]/(ϵ2)),​s=(0),s=(p), p≡1(mod4),s=(p), p≡3(mod4),s=(2).​​
(6)
The split scheme-theoretic fiber has two closed points, represented by (p,i−a) and (p,i+a). The inert scheme-theoretic fiber has one closed point, represented by (p). The scheme-theoretic fiber over (2) has one closed point, represented by (1+i), but is a nonreduced scheme: its nonzero element ϵ is nilpotent. These descriptions retain the residue fields and the nilpotent structure, which a count of points alone would lose.
The topology. The printed PDF says that the nonempty proper Zariski closed subsets are finite subsets of Z[i]∖{0}. Its points are prime ideals, so the intended set is SpecZ[i]∖{(0)}.
Put R=Z[i]. Since R is a principal ideal domain, every nonzero prime ideal is a maximal ideal. Each such prime ideal contains a nonzero Gaussian integer z, and therefore contains the positive integer zz. Its contraction to Z is consequently a nonzero prime ideal (p), so the scheme-theoretic fibers just computed list all the closed points of X. The sole remaining point is the generic point (0).
Every proper Zariski closed subset is V((a)) with a=0: if its defining ideal were zero, it would be all of X. A nonzero nonunit a has a finite unique factorization
a=uπ1e1​​⋯πrer​​,V((a))={(π1​),…,(πr​)}.
(7)
Here the πj​ are pairwise nonassociate prime elements. Conversely, any finite set of nonzero prime ideals {(π1​),…,(πr​)} equals V((π1​⋯πr​)); the empty set is V((1)). Therefore
X={(0)} ∪ {nonzero prime ideals of Z[i]},proper closed subsets of X=finite subsets of its closed points.​​
(8)
In particular, the closure of the generic point is X, and every nonempty open subset contains that generic point and all but finitely many closed points.
A nonidentity automorphism. Complex conjugation
c:R⟶R,a+bi⟼a−bi,
(9)
is a ring automorphism fixing Z. It induces the automorphism σ=Spec(c) over SpecZ. Since c(i)=−i=i, this is not the identity morphism of schemes. On the generic fiber it is the nontrivial field automorphism of Q(i)/Q. On a split scheme-theoretic fiber it swaps the two closed points, since i−a maps to −(i+a). On an inert scheme-theoretic fiber it fixes the only point but induces the nonidentity finite-field Frobenius automorphism z↦zp on its residue field, because ip=−i. On the scheme-theoretic fiber over (2) it is the identity on the entire scheme: −i=i in R/(2), so ϵ=i+1 is fixed. Thus
σ:generic conjugation; split points exchanged; inert Frobenius; identity over 2.​
(10)

The immersions and gluing. For p>0, the prime ideals of the localization Z(p)​ are (0) and (p)Z(p)​. Inverting p gives Z(p)​[1/p]=Q, so
ψp​:SpecQ ∼ ​D(p)↪Up​​
(11)
is an open immersion sending the generic point to the generic point. For p=0, Z(0)​=Q, and take ψ0​ to be the identity. Identify each pair of these open subschemes using the identity of SpecQ. The identities compose to the identity on every triple overlap, so gluing of schemes along open subschemes applies. No finiteness of the family is required.
The resulting scheme Y has one common generic point η and a distinct closed point yp​ for every positive prime number p. Its affine open subschemes Up​={η,yp​}, for p>0, intersect pairwise in U0​={η}. A subset W⊆Y is open precisely when its intersection with every Up​ is open. In the two-point topological space Up​, the open subsets are ∅, {η} and Up​. Consequently
The nonempty opens of Y are exactly WS​={η}∪{yp​:p∈S},​
(12)
where S is any subset of the positive prime numbers, including the empty subset.
The canonical morphism. The ring homomorphisms Z→Z(p)​ give morphisms of schemes Up​→SpecZ agreeing on the overlaps, hence a morphism of schemes f:Y→SpecZ. By uniqueness of the structure morphism, this is the required map. Contraction of prime ideals gives
f(η)=(0),f(yp​)=(p).​
(13)
These are all points of SpecZ, each exactly once, so f is a bijection. It is continuous because its restrictions to the open cover (Up​) are continuous; explicitly, every nonempty open subset of SpecZ is the complement of a finite collection of closed points, whose inverse image is one of the WS​.
Sections on every open. For any S, the sheaf gluing axiom on the cover of WS​ by U0​ and the Up​ with p∈S identifies
Γ(WS​,OY​)=p∈S⋂​Z(p)​ ⊆Q,​
(14)
where the intersection over the empty set is Q. Indeed, a section on Up​ is an element of Z(p)​; restrictions to every overlap are its inclusion into Q. Compatibility means that all these elements are the same rational number. In lowest terms a/b, this rational number lies in the intersection exactly when no prime number in S divides b. Equivalently, this is the integer localization at a set of primes obtained by inverting all positive prime numbers outside S.
Now let V⊆SpecZ be nonempty and open. Its finite complement consists of (p1​),…,(pr​); put N=p1​⋯pr​, with N=1 for an empty complement. Then V=D(N) and
Γ(V,OSpecZ​)=Z[1/N]=⋂p∤N​Z(p)​=Γ(f−1V,OY​).
(15)
The equality follows by the denominator criterion above. The map f# is this same equality of subrings of Q, so it is a ring isomorphism. Over the empty open subset both rings of sections are the zero ring. These identifications respect every restriction map. Thus the precise comparison of structure sheaves is
OSpecZ​ ∼ ​f∗​OY​.​
(16)
Here “over every open set” refers to an open subset V of the target and its inverse image. The preceding intersection formula additionally describes the sections on every open subset of Y, including ones whose images are not open in the target.
Topology and finiteness. The singleton {η}=U0​ is open in Y, but {(0)} is not open in SpecZ: a nonempty open subset there contains all but finitely many of the infinitely many closed points. Therefore f is not a homeomorphism, despite being a continuous bijection and giving the indicated isomorphism of structure sheaves after direct image.
The open cover (Up​)p>0​ of Y has no finite subcover, because yq​ belongs only to Uq​. Thus Y is not a quasi-compact scheme, and part (a) rules out an affine scheme. Each Z(p)​ is a Noetherian ring, being a localization of the Noetherian ring Z. Hence the Up​ form an affine open cover by spectra of Noetherian rings, proving that Y is a locally Noetherian scheme. A Noetherian scheme is a quasi-compact scheme, so
Y is locally Noetherian, not quasi-compact, not affine, and not Noetherian.​
(17)

Constructing the subsheaf. On an integral scheme, every nonempty open subset is an irreducible topological space and hence a connected space. A locally constant function from such an open subset to F is constant. Thus the constant sheaf F​ has sections F on every nonempty open subset, and the structure sheaf embeds in it by restriction to the generic point. Empty members of the given open cover may be discarded.
For every open subset W⊆X, define
L(W)={s∈F​(W): fi​s∣W∩Ui​​∈OX​(W∩Ui​) for every i}.
(18)
This is a subsheaf of the sheaf of modules F​: its condition is local, and compatible sections glue in F​, with the regularity conditions gluing in OX​. On Ui​∩Uj​, the hypothesis says that fj​/fi​ is a section of the sheaf of units of the structure sheaf. Therefore multiplication by it preserves OX​, and
fi−1​OX​∣Ui​∩Uj​​=fj−1​OX​∣Ui​∩Uj​​.
(19)
It follows that L∣Ui​​=fi−1​OUi​​: the condition with index i implies every other condition on an overlap, and the converse is part of the definition. Multiplication by 1/fi​ is an isomorphism OUi​​→L∣Ui​​, with inverse multiplication by fi​. Hence L is an invertible sheaf, or equivalently a line bundle, and
L(Ui​)=OX​(Ui​)⋅fi​1​⊆F.​
(20)
These local descriptions also give uniqueness as a subsheaf of F​. This construction is the divisor line bundle for the local rational equations fi​.
The tensor product. Refine the two open covers to Wij​=Ui​∩Vj​. The ratios
fi′​gj′​fi​gj​​=fi′​fi​​gj′​gj​​
(21)
are sections of the sheaf of units of the structure sheaf on the corresponding overlaps, so the products give a line bundle M⊆F​ by the construction just proved. Multiplication in F defines a sheaf morphism
L⊗OX​​L′⟶M.
(22)
On each Wij​ it sends the local generator (1/fi​)⊗(1/gj​) to the local generator 1/(fi​gj​). It is thus an isomorphism on an open cover, and hence an isomorphism globally. In particular,
(Ui​∩Vj​,fi​gj​) represents L⊗OX​​L′.​
(23)
The sign for projective twists. Write homogeneous coordinates [x0​:⋯:xn​] on projective space PZn​, and let Ui​=D+​(xi​) be its standard affine open cover. For any integer m, take the nonzero elements of the function field
fi​=(xi​x0​​)m∈Q(x1​/x0​,…,xn​/x0​)∗,0≤i≤n.​
(24)
Their ratios are fi​/fj​=(xj​/xi​)m, which are sections of the sheaf of units of the structure sheaf on Ui​∩Uj​, including when m<0. The twisting sheaf on projective space O(m) has local generator xim​ on Ui​. Divide a rational section of O(m) by x0m​ to embed it in the constant sheaf of the function field; the local generator becomes (xi​/x0​)m=1/fi​. This identifies the constructed line bundle with O(m) and fixes the sign explicitly.
For n≥1, Pic(PZn​)≅Z, with generator [O(1)]; the displayed data gives all its powers, with O(m)⊗O(r)≅O(m+r) by the product rule above. If n=0, projective space is SpecZ, its Picard group is zero, and the same formula gives only the trivial line bundle.

Discard empty members of the open cover: every Čech cochain group factor supported on an empty intersection is the zero abelian group, so this changes no Čech cohomology. Every remaining member is the one-point space X; the index set is nonempty even if the same open subset is listed repeatedly. Choose a well-order with least index α. Use the ordered Čech cochain complex permitted in the question:
Cq=∏i0​<⋯<iq​​A,(δs)i0​⋯iq+1​​=∑r=0q+1​(−1)rsi0​⋯ir​​⋯iq+1​​.
(25)
All restriction maps are identities. For q≥1 define a cochain homotopy hq​:Cq→Cq−1 by
(hq​s)i0​⋯iq−1​​={0,sαi0​⋯iq−1​​,​i0​=α,i0​>α.​
(26)
We verify δhq​+hq+1​δ=idCq​ for every q≥1. Fix I=(i0​<⋯<iq​). If i0​=α, then (hq+1​δs)I​=0. In (δhq​s)I​, the term deleting i0​ is sI​ and every other term is zero because its retained first index is α. Thus the identity holds on I.
If i0​>α, then
(δhq​s)I​=∑r=0q​(−1)rsα,I∖ir​​,(hq+1​δs)I​=(δs)α,I​=sI​−∑r=0q​(−1)rsα,I∖ir​​.
(27)
Adding proves the identity in this case also. Each formula uses only finitely many terms on each component, so it remains valid for an infinite open cover and arbitrary abelian group A.
If s∈Cq is a Čech cocycle, then δs=0, and the identity gives s=δ(hq​s): every Čech cocycle in positive degree is a Čech coboundary. Therefore, using the permitted comparison with ordered Čech cohomology,
HˇUq​(X,A​)=0(q≥1).​
(28)
The identity is asserted in positive degrees; degree zero has H0≅A, so an unaugmented contraction to zero in every degree would be incorrect.

In an irreducible topological space, any two nonempty open subsets intersect. Let W⊆X be a nonempty open subset. Two nonempty subsets open in W are also open in X, and hence intersect; thus W is itself an irreducible topological space. If W were disconnected, a separation into two disjoint nonempty open subsets would contradict this intersection property. Hence W is a connected space. The empty open subset is connected as well under the usual convention.
Discard empty members of U as in (b). Every finite intersection Ui0​​∩⋯∩Uiq​​ is nonempty by repeated use of the intersection property and is an open subset, hence a connected space. A locally constant function on this intersection is constant. It follows that the constant sheaf has
A​(Ui0​​∩⋯∩Uiq​​)≅A,
(29)
and every restriction map between these nonempty intersections is the identity on A. The whole Čech cochain complex is therefore the same as for the indexed open cover of a point having the same index set. The cochain homotopy constructed in (b) applies component by component and proves
Every open subset of X is connected,HˇUq​(X,A​)=0(q≥1).​
(30)
The argument concerns the constant sheaf of locally constant functions; arbitrary coefficient sheaves on an irreducible topological space need not have the same Čech cohomology.

Choose an affine open subscheme V=SpecA⊆Y containing y. Since f is an affine morphism, f−1V=SpecB; by hypothesis B is a finitely generated module over A. If m⊂A corresponds to the closed point y, then κ(y)=A/m. The scheme-theoretic fiber is
Xy​=X×Y​Specκ(y)≅Spec(B⊗A​κ(y))=Spec(B/mB).
(31)
If b1​,…,br​ generate the A-module B, their images span the algebra B/mB as a vector space over κ(y). It is therefore finite-dimensional. By the allowed fact about the spectrum of a finite-dimensional commutative algebra, it has only finitely many prime ideals. These are exactly the points of Xy​, so
∣f−1(y)∣=∣Spec(B/mB)∣<∞.​
(32)
The points of the scheme-theoretic fiber correspond to the prime ideals of B contracting to m, which explains the equality with the underlying set-theoretic fiber. An empty fiber is allowed. In fact, replacing A/m by κ(y) gives the same finite-dimensional argument for every point, without requiring it to be a closed point.

Failure in general. Let f:Ak1​=Speck[t]→Speck be the structure morphism and take the trivial line bundle L=OAk1​​. The polynomial ring k[t] is a Noetherian ring, so this is a coherent sheaf. Its direct image on the one-point target has sections
Γ(Speck,f∗​L)=k[t].
(33)
A coherent sheaf on Speck corresponds to a finite-dimensional vector space over k, whereas 1,t,t2,… are linearly independent. Thus
f∗​OAk1​​ is not coherent.​
(34)
Quasi-coherence for the stated morphism. Now let f satisfy (a), and let F be a quasi-coherent sheaf on X. Over any affine open subscheme V=SpecA of Y, write f−1V=SpecB and F∣f−1V​=M for a B-module M. Regard M as an A-module through the ring homomorphism A→B. For a∈A,
Γ(D(a),f∗​F)=Γ(D(φ(a)),M)=Mφ(a)​=Ma​.
(35)
The last two localizations are identical: both invert the action of the same element on M. These identifications commute with restrictions, so on the basis of principal open subsets they identify (f∗​F)∣V​ with the affine module sheaf A​M​. Hence this direct image is a quasi-coherent sheaf on each V, and therefore on Y. This part uses only that f is an affine morphism.
Coherence under the Noetherian hypotheses. If X,Y are Noetherian schemes and F is a coherent sheaf, then M is a finitely generated module over B. Choose generators m1​,…,ms​ of M over B, and generators b1​,…,br​ of B over A, supplied by (a). Then the finite family
{bj​mℓ​:1≤j≤r, 1≤ℓ≤s}
(36)
generates M as an A-module: expand each coefficient of an expression m=∑ℓ​cℓ​mℓ​ in the bj​. Since A is a Noetherian ring, the affine module sheaf A​M​ is a coherent sheaf. This holds on an affine open cover of Y, proving
f∗​:QCoh(X)→QCoh(Y),f∗​:Coh(X)→Coh(Y) if X,Y are Noetherian.​

The exact sequence and associated modules. Put A=k[x] and m=(x). Evaluation at zero gives the short exact sequence of A-modules
0⟶(x) j ​A ev0​ ​A/(x)=k⟶0.
(38)
The affine module sheaf associated to (x) is the ideal sheaf J of the closed point p, and the one associated to A is the structure sheaf OAk1​​. To identify the third term, on a principal open subset D(g),
Γ(D(g),k)=kg​={k,0,​g(0)=0,g(0)=0,​
(39)
which equals Γ(i−1D(g),Op​). The restrictions also agree, so k≅i∗​Op​. Exactness of localization proves that applying the affine module sheaf construction to the module sequence remains exact: on every stalk the sequence is its localization. Thus
0⟶J⟶OAk1​​⟶i∗​Op​⟶0.​
(40)
Each term is an affine module sheaf, hence a quasi-coherent sheaf. In this example each is also a coherent sheaf, since its defining module is finitely generated over the Noetherian ring k[x].
The failure of left exactness. For an affine module sheaf M, the pullback of a sheaf of modules along i corresponds to the tensor product of modules k⊗A​M. In particular,
i∗J ↔ (x)⊗A​A/(x)≅(x)/(x)2≅k,i∗OAk1​​ ↔ k.
(41)
The first vector space is nonzero, with basis the class of x. But i∗j sends this class to the class of x in A/(x), which is zero. Thus i∗j is the zero map k→k, and is not injective. More explicitly, the pulled-back sequence is
0⟶k 0 ​k id ​k⟶0,
(42)
where exactness fails at its first copy of k. Thus i∗ is not a left-exact functor:
i∗ is not a left-exact functor on sheaves of OAk1​​-modules.​
(43)
Although (x) is a free module over A with one generator, its inclusion into A becomes multiplication by x after choosing its generator; the tensor product of modules with A/(x) kills that map. This makes explicit why local freeness of J does not preserve this injection under the given pullback.
