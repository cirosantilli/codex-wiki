i OurBigBook.com
Write hT​(S)=Hom(S,T) for the functor of points of a scheme T. A subfunctor H⊆F is an open subfunctor if every natural transformation hT​→F has a pullback represented by an open subscheme U⊆T:
hT​×F​H≅hU​,U↪T an open immersion.​
(1)
The displayed natural isomorphism must identify the projection to hT​ with the natural transformation induced by U↪T. Equivalently, for each ξ∈F(T), there is an open subscheme U⊆T such that a morphism of schemes a:S→T factors through U exactly when F(a)(ξ)∈H(S). The pullback is taken in the functor category, so this tests arbitrary families, rather than just underlying points.

A Zariski cover of a scheme T is a family of open immersions (ji​:Ui​↪T)i∈I​ whose images cover its underlying topological space:
T=i∈I⋃​ji​(Ui​).​
(2)
There is no finiteness requirement. Intersections used in the sheaf on a site condition are the pullbacks Ui​×T​Uj​, identified with the corresponding open subschemes Ui​∩Uj​.

A presheaf F:Schop→Set is a Zariski sheaf when every Zariski cover (Ui​→T) gives an equalizer diagram
F(T)⟶i∏​F(Ui​)⇉i,j∏​F(Ui​×T​Uj​).​
(3)
Thus compatible local elements glue to a unique element of F(T). Compatibility means that the two restrictions agree on every intersection. This includes the empty Zariski cover of the empty scheme, so F(∅) is a singleton.

The open affine representability criterion says that F is a functor represented by a scheme exactly when F is a Zariski sheaf and admits a family of open subfunctors Fi​⊆F, each represented by an affine scheme, which covers F in the following sense: for every scheme T and every ξ∈F(T), the open subschemes representing hT​×F​Fi​ cover T. In particular, the covering condition does not demand that every ξ lie in one Fi​(T).
In symbols, for affine schemes Xi​,
Fi​≅hXi​​,Fi​⊆F open,i⋃​(T×F​Fi​)=T,F a Zariski sheaf.​
(4)
The family is indexed by a set. For necessity, take an affine open cover of a representing scheme; morphisms of schemes glue over a Zariski cover. For sufficiency, Fi​×F​Fj​ is represented by an open subscheme of each Xi​,Xj​. The two descriptions identify these overlaps and satisfy the compatibility on triple overlaps because all maps come from the inclusions into F. Apply gluing of schemes along open subschemes to obtain X. Local identification gives hX​→F, and the Zariski sheaf condition makes this a natural isomorphism: its elements can be constructed and compared on the pulled-back open cover.

A morphism of schemes f:X→Y defines a natural transformation gf​:gX​→gY​ by u↦f∘u. We construct the inverse correspondence.
Let α:gX​→gY​ be a natural transformation, and choose an affine open cover (ji​:Ui​↪X). Regard ji​ as an element of gX​(Ui​) and set
fi​=αUi​​(ji​):Ui​⟶Y.
(5)
Here and below the equivalence of categories in the question lets us write the restricted functor of points on affine schemes, contravariantly, rather than on commutative rings, covariantly. If W⊆Ui​∩Uj​ is an affine open subscheme, naturality for its two inclusions gives
fi​∣W​=αW​(W↪X)=fj​∣W​.
(6)
Such W cover the overlap even when that overlap is not an affine scheme. Consequently the fi​ agree and glue to a morphism of schemes f:X→Y.
It remains to check all elements of the restricted functor of points, not only the chosen inclusions. Given an affine scheme T and u:T→X, cover each u−1(Ui​) by affine open subschemes V. On such a V, factor u∣V​=ji​∘v. Applying naturality first to v:V→Ui​ and then to V↪T gives
αT​(u)∣V​=αV​(u∣V​)=αUi​​(ji​)∘v=f∘u∣V​.
(7)
These local equalities prove αT​(u)=f∘u. The glued f is unique, since its restrictions to all Ui​ are prescribed. Hence
HomSch​(X,Y)≅Nat(gX​,gY​),​
(8)
so the assignment is fully faithful.
If gX​≅gY​, the natural isomorphism and its inverse correspond to morphisms of schemes f:X→Y and h:Y→X. Their compositions induce the identity natural transformations, and faithfulness implies hf=idX​, fh=idY​. Conversely, an isomorphism of schemes induces a natural isomorphism. Thus
X≅Y⟺gX​≅gY​.​
(9)

For an idempotent e∈R, define the principal open subset D(e)⊆SpecR. Since e(1−e)=0, a prime ideal contains at least one of e,1−e, and it cannot contain both. Therefore
D(e)=V(1−e),SpecR=D(e)⊔D(1−e).
(10)
Thus D(e) is a clopen set.
Conversely, let C be a clopen set in T=SpecR. The structure sheaf glues the section 1 on C and the section 0 on its complement to a unique global section e∈Γ(T,OT​)=R. Checking on these two open subsets gives e2=e, and D(e)=C. Conversely, the restriction of an idempotent e to D(e) is 1 because e is a unit there, and its restriction to D(1−e) is 0. Thus the two constructions are inverse, including for the zero ring:
{e∈R:e2=e} ⟷ {C⊆SpecR:C clopen},e⟼D(e).​
(11)

Starting from (r,e), the construction in (iii) preserves the composite to the affine line and sends ZA​ to the A zero section and ZB​ to the B zero section. Part (ii) therefore recovers (r,e).
Conversely, start with u:T→X and construct (r,e). The only points of X outside A lie on B0​, and the only points outside B lie on A0​. Consequently u−1(A)=T∖ZB​=TA​ and u−1(B)=T∖ZA​=TB​. For every valuation ring D with fraction field K, consider compatible morphisms of schemes
u:SpecK→X,v:SpecD→S,fu=v∣SpecK​.
(19)
An extension is a morphism of schemes ℓ:SpecD→X satisfying ℓ∣SpecK​=u and fℓ=v. The valuative criterion for universal closedness states
f universally closed⟺f quasi-compact and every such pair admits an extension.​
(20)
Equivalently, among quasi-compact morphisms, the existence condition characterizes universal closedness. A universally closed morphism is automatically a quasi-compact morphism; the converse direction of the criterion needs this compactness condition. All valuation rings are allowed, with arbitrary fraction fields; there is no Noetherian ring hypothesis and one cannot replace them by discrete valuation rings without additional assumptions.[ref][ref]

Use the compatible pair u,v and the notion of extension from (i). The valuative criterion for separatedness is
f separated⟺f quasi-separated and every such pair has at most one extension.​
(21)
A quasi-separated morphism means one whose diagonal morphism is a quasi-compact morphism. A separated morphism automatically has this property because its diagonal morphism is a closed immersion. The quasi-separated morphism hypothesis is needed in the converse valuative test for arbitrary schemes. Existence is not demanded.[ref]

With the same compatible pair u,v, the valuative criterion for properness is
f proper⟺f is of finite type and quasi-separated, and every pair has exactly one extension.​
(22)
Equivalently, one can replace finite type by being a quasi-compact morphism and locally of finite type. Necessity follows from the definition of a proper morphism and the existence and uniqueness criteria in (i) and (ii). Conversely, finite type supplies the compactness needed in (i), while being a quasi-separated morphism supplies the compactness of the diagonal morphism needed in (ii). Thus the extension condition gives both universal closedness and separatedness, and these together with finite type give a proper morphism. These hypotheses are part of the criteria, even though the question is phrased for unrestricted morphisms of schemes.[ref]

Let S′→S be a morphism of schemes and set X′=X×S​S′, with projection f′:X′→S′. Given compatible morphisms of schemes u′:SpecK→X′ and v′:SpecD→S′, compose them with the projections to obtain a valuative problem for f. An extension ℓ:SpecD→X for that problem and the prescribed v′ determine a unique morphism of schemes
ℓ′=(ℓ,v′):SpecD⟶X×S​S′.
(23)
The universal property of a fibre product shows that ℓ′ extends u′ and lies over v′. Conversely, every extension ℓ′ has such a first component ℓ. Thus the sets of extensions in the two problems are in bijection.
If f is a universally closed morphism, it is a quasi-compact morphism and satisfies existence. The allowed stability of quasi-compact morphisms under base change and (a)(i) prove that f′ is a universally closed morphism. If f is a separated morphism, it is a quasi-separated morphism and satisfies uniqueness. Stability of quasi-separated morphisms under base change and (a)(ii) prove that f′ is a separated morphism.
If f is a proper morphism, the same bijection supplies existence and uniqueness. The permitted stability under base change of quasi-compact morphisms, quasi-separated morphisms and locally of finite type supplies all remaining hypotheses of (a)(iii). Hence
f universally closedf separatedf proper​⟹f′ universally closed,⟹f′ separated,⟹f′ proper.​​
(24)

Let D be a valuation ring, K its fraction field, and let u:SpecK→X, v:SpecD→S be a compatible pair. Since D is a local ring, every open subset of SpecD containing its unique closed point is the whole space: a principal open subset containing the maximal ideal is defined by a unit. Choose an affine open subscheme V=SpecA⊆S containing the image under v of that closed point. The entire v factors through V.
Because f is a finite morphism, f−1(V)=SpecB with B a finite A-module. The valuative problem is the compatible ring homomorphisms
A⟶D⊆K,ψ:B⟶K.
(29)
Every b∈B is an integral element over A. Mapping its monic polynomial equation to K proves that ψ(b) is an integral element over D. Part (c) gives ψ(b)∈D. Thus ψ factors uniquely as a ring homomorphism B→D↪K, producing the unique extension SpecD→X.
For completeness, a finite morphism is a quasi-compact morphism and of finite type: over V its inverse image is an affine scheme, and finite A-module generators also generate it as an A-algebra. It is also a separated morphism. Affine-locally its diagonal morphism corresponds to the surjective multiplication ring homomorphism
B⊗A​B⟶B,b⊗c⟼bc,
(30)
and hence is a closed immersion. In particular it is a quasi-separated morphism. The valuative criterion for properness now applies, giving
f finite⟹f proper.​
(31)

For a quasi-compact scheme X, the Serre criterion for affineness gives the equivalences
X affine​⟺H1(X,F)=0 for every quasi-coherent OX​-module F⟺Hq(X,F)=0 for every such F and every q>0.​​
(36)
Indeed, an affine scheme has vanishing of quasi-coherent cohomology on an affine scheme. Conversely, vanishing of the first sheaf cohomology for every quasi-coherent sheaf includes every quasi-coherent sheaf of ideals, which suffices for the criterion. No separated scheme hypothesis is needed in this formulation. Nor may one test only the structure sheaf.[ref]

Every affine scheme X=SpecA is a separated scheme: its diagonal morphism over SpecZ is induced by the surjective ring homomorphism A⊗Z​A→A, a⊗b↦ab, so it is a closed immersion. This contradicts the assumption that X is not a separated scheme. Therefore

Suppose X=U1​∪⋯∪Un​ with each Ui​ an affine open subscheme. Every affine scheme is a quasi-compact topological space: a cover by principal open subsets D(fλ​) means that the fλ​ generate the unit ideal; an expression 1=∑j=1r​aj​fλj​​ gives a finite subcover. Every open cover has a refinement by principal open subsets, so this proves the general covering property.
Restrict an arbitrary open cover of X to each Ui​ and choose a finite subcover there. The union of these finitely many finite families covers X. Thus X is a quasi-compact scheme.
Now assume X is a separated scheme. By (c), every finite intersection of the Ui​ is an affine scheme, and hence has vanishing positive sheaf cohomology for the structure sheaf. This affine open cover therefore computes sheaf cohomology using its Čech cochain complex. Its degree p term is
Cˇp=∏1≤i0​<⋯<ip​≤n​Γ(Ui0​​∩⋯∩Uip​​,OX​).
(41)
There are no strictly increasing (p+1)-tuples when p≥n, so those Čech cochain groups vanish. Consequently
Hm(X,OX​)=0(m≥n).​
(42)
The same argument works for every quasi-coherent sheaf. The relevant comparison requires the acyclicity of the finite intersections; the bare assertion that arbitrary Čech cohomology equals sheaf cohomology would be too broad.

A Noetherian scheme is a quasi-compact scheme, so choose a finite affine open cover X=⋃i=1s​SpecAi​ with all Ai​ Noetherian rings. The nilradical sheaf restricts on this chart to the quasi-coherent sheaf associated to Nil(Ai​): the nilradical commutes with localization.
By the allowed fact, choose di​≥1 such that Nil(Ai​)di​=0 and put m=maxi​di​. On every chart, the ideal sheaf Nm is zero, and hence it is zero globally. Higher powers are also zero. Thus
Nd=0for every d≥m.​

If X is empty, take m=1. The finite affine open cover is essential for choosing one bound valid everywhere.

Let i:Xred​↪X be the reduced induced subscheme, with structure sheaf OXred​​=OX​/N. Its underlying map of topological spaces is a homeomorphism, since every prime ideal contains the nilradical.
Put G=F/NF. Multiplication by N annihilates G, so its action by OX​ factors through OX​/N. Thus G is naturally a sheaf of modules over OXred​​. On an affine open subscheme SpecA, with F=M, it corresponds to M/Nil(A)M over A/Nil(A), so it remains a quasi-coherent sheaf.
The sheaf cohomology of a sheaf of modules is the sheaf cohomology of its underlying sheaf of abelian groups. Under the above homeomorphism, that underlying sheaf is identical on the two spaces: the global sections functor and its flasque resolutions are identical. Equivalently, use sheaf cohomology under a closed inclusion with i∗​G. Therefore there are canonical isomorphisms, in every degree,
Hq(X,F/NF)≅Hq(Xred​,F/NF)(q≥0).​
(49)
In particular, this gives the requested equality in degree one. Equality of the underlying topological spaces alone would not compare arbitrary coefficient sheaves; the factorization of the action explains why the indicated coefficients are the same.

Let F be any quasi-coherent sheaf on X, and choose m from (i). Filter it by the powers of the nilradical sheaf:
F⊇NF⊇⋯⊇NmF=0.
(50)
Each subquotient
Qj​=NjF/Nj+1F,0≤j<m,
(51)
is a quasi-coherent sheaf annihilated by N. Apply (ii) to the quasi-coherent sheaf NjF. Since Xred​ is an affine scheme, vanishing of quasi-coherent cohomology on an affine scheme gives
H1(X,Qj​)≅H1(Xred​,Qj​)=0.
(52)
The short exact sequences
0⟶Nj+1F⟶NjF⟶Qj​⟶0
(53)
yield the segments of the long exact sequence in sheaf cohomology
H1(X,Nj+1F)⟶H1(X,NjF)⟶H1(X,Qj​)=0.
(54)
Starting from NmF=0, descending mathematical induction makes every middle group zero, finally giving H1(X,F)=0.
This holds for every quasi-coherent sheaf F. Since a Noetherian scheme is a quasi-compact scheme, the Serre criterion for affineness in (a) applies and proves
Xred​ affine⟹X affine.​
(55)
This is an instance of the general fact that affineness is invariant under nilpotent thickening.
