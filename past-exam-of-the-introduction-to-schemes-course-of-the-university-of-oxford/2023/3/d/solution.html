Suppose X=U1​∪⋯∪Un​ with each Ui​ an affine open subscheme. Every affine scheme is a quasi-compact topological space: a cover by principal open subsets D(fλ​) means that the fλ​ generate the unit ideal; an expression 1=∑j=1r​aj​fλj​​ gives a finite subcover. Every open cover has a refinement by principal open subsets, so this proves the general covering property.
Restrict an arbitrary open cover of X to each Ui​ and choose a finite subcover there. The union of these finitely many finite families covers X. Thus X is a quasi-compact scheme.
Now assume X is a separated scheme. By (c), every finite intersection of the Ui​ is an affine scheme, and hence has vanishing positive sheaf cohomology for the structure sheaf. This affine open cover therefore computes sheaf cohomology using its Čech cochain complex. Its degree p term is
Cˇp=∏1≤i0​<⋯<ip​≤n​Γ(Ui0​​∩⋯∩Uip​​,OX​).
(41)
There are no strictly increasing (p+1)-tuples when p≥n, so those Čech cochain groups vanish. Consequently
Hm(X,OX​)=0(m≥n).​
(42)
The same argument works for every quasi-coherent sheaf. The relevant comparison requires the acyclicity of the finite intersections; the bare assertion that arbitrary Čech cohomology equals sheaf cohomology would be too broad.
