In an irreducible topological space, any two nonempty open subsets intersect. Let W⊆X be a nonempty open subset. Two nonempty subsets open in W are also open in X, and hence intersect; thus W is itself an irreducible topological space. If W were disconnected, a separation into two disjoint nonempty open subsets would contradict this intersection property. Hence W is a connected space. The empty open subset is connected as well under the usual convention.
