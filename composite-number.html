Composite number

 Home Mathematics Area of mathematics Number theory
OurBigBook.com Words: 122 Articles: 1
A composite number is an integer greater than one that is not a prime number. Equivalently, it has a factorization n=ab with integers a,b>1. Thus 4=2⋅2, 9=3⋅3, and 12=3⋅4 are composite numbers; one is neither a composite number nor a prime number.
Every composite number has a prime factor at most its square root. In 91=7⋅13, seven is the smaller factor and lies below 91​; in 49=72, equality occurs. This square-root bound makes trial division finite and lets the sieve of Eratosthenes stop marking with prime numbers beyond N​ when listing prime numbers up to N.
