SIR model equations (s˙=−βsi,i˙=βsi−γi,r˙=γi)

For frequency-dependent transmission, the closed SIR model is a system of ordinary differential equations for the fractions s,i,r:
dtds​=−βsi,dtdi​=βsi−γi,dtdr​=γi,s+i+r=1.
(38)
Here β>0 is the coefficient of frequency-dependent transmission, and γ>0 is the recovery rate, interpreted more generally as the removal rate. Both have units of inverse time. With a constant recovery rate, the associated individual time in the infectious compartment has an exponential distribution with mean 1/γ; the SIR model does not require every person to spend exactly that long there. The fractions themselves have no units.[ref]
For counts S=Ns, I=Ni, R=Nr, the same SIR model equations read
dtdS​=−βNSI​,dtdI​=βNSI​−γI,dtdR​=γI,S+I+R=N.
(39)
The factor 1/N keeps the same β in both descriptions. Dropping it while keeping these units would fail dimensional analysis. Some existing SIR model variants use the unnormalized mass-action infection convention instead, including the SIR susceptible-recovered identity; their coefficient is b=β/N in the notation used here.
Adding the SIR model equations gives d(s+i+r)/dt=0, a short proof of conservation of population. Nonnegative starting fractions stay nonnegative: the outflow from either the susceptible compartment or the infectious compartment vanishes when that group is empty, while dr/dt≥0. Thus the susceptible compartment can only shrink and the removed compartment can only grow.
For a concrete initial value problem, stipulate
β=0.3day−1,γ=0.1day−1,(s0​,i0​,r0​)=(0.99,0.01,0).
(40)
At the start, the flow into the infectious compartment is 0.3×0.99×0.01=0.00297 of the total per day, and the flow into the removed compartment is 0.1×0.01=0.00100 of the total per day. Hence di/dt=0.00197 per day. If N=1000, these correspond to model rates of 2.97 and 1.00 people per day, and net growth 1.97 people per day. A rate need not be an integer count of events on any particular day.
For this SIR model, the basic reproduction number is
R0​=γβ​=3.
(41)
It describes one introduced member of the infectious compartment in an otherwise wholly susceptible compartment. The instantaneous reproduction number at the actual starting state is R0​s0​=2.97, obtained by freezing the current conditions and the current susceptible compartment's fraction. More generally it is R0​s(t) in this SIR model. The basic reproduction number is a ratio of two rates, so it has no units; r0​ is a different quantity, the initial removed compartment's fraction.
While i>0, its instantaneous relative growth is
i1​dtdi​=βs−γ.
(42)
Initially this equals 0.3×0.99−0.1=0.197day−1. Freezing s≈s0​ gives the early exponential growth approximation i(t)≈0.01e0.197t with t measured in days. It must fail as the susceptible compartment shrinks. An epidemic invasion threshold occurs at R0​s0​=1; if i0​=0, the deterministic SIR model stays at i=0 even above that threshold.
