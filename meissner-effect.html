Meissner effect

 Home Physics Branches of physics Electromagnetism Magnetic field London equations
OurBigBook.com Words: 2k Articles: 7
The Meissner effect is the equilibrium expulsion of a sufficiently weak, static magnetic field from the bulk of a material exhibiting superconductivity. Expulsion includes a magnetic field that was already present before cooling: zero electrical resistance alone does not predict this. The Field-cooled Meissner experiment tests that distinction. A finite London penetration depth means that the magnetic field and screening electric current occupy a surface layer, rather than disappearing at a mathematical boundary.[ref]
This makes superconductivity useful for shielding weak magnetic fields, and makes its magnetic response an independent test of the phase. The striking result is that the same screened equilibrium can be reached by two different preparation histories. The assumptions matter: Finite-thickness Meissner screening can leave an appreciable magnetic field throughout a small sample, and the Limits of Meissner screening distinguish quantum vortex penetration and retained magnetic flux from this equilibrium state.
