Catalysed peroxide comparison

 Home Chemistry Chemical reaction Chemical kinetics Catalysis Heterogeneous catalysis
OurBigBook.com Words: 414
A simple motivating comparison is visible gas evolution from hydrogen peroxide, whose net chemical equation is
2H2​O2​⟶2H2​O+O2​.
(127)
The products are water and oxygen. The Royal Society of Chemistry's teaching resource uses small test tubes and droppers, hydrogen peroxide labelled 5–10 volumes, solutions of metal salts including copper(II) sulfate, and approximately 1molL−1 sodium hydroxide. Its qualitative procedure tests additions singly and in pairs to tubes containing about 1cm depth of hydrogen peroxide. It reports the expected classroom result that the additions individually are not effective under those conditions, whereas the solids produced by mixing metal-salt solution with sodium hydroxide give effective heterogeneous catalysis. This is the resource's expected observation, not a newly measured ranking.[ref]
A focused adaptation compares four matched tubes: no addition, copper(II) sulfate alone, sodium hydroxide alone, and the pair. Use equal initial hydrogen peroxide amounts, identical tubes and the same starting temperature. Record stock concentrations and added volumes; add water to the other tubes to match the total added liquid. Additions can first be checked without hydrogen peroxide to distinguish precipitation from gas evolution. Start a timer at contact, mix consistently, and observe gas evolution over the same interval. These matching controls are proposed improvements to the qualitative resource, not claimed details of its original trials. Follow its supervised chemical-handling requirements; the stated strengths describe its teaching protocol rather than an unrestricted recipe.
The immediate readout is relative bubbling, together with whether a solid appears. More vigorous bubbling in the paired tube supports faster conversion under the comparison conditions; it does not identify the solid's exact composition or establish a unique reaction mechanism. Bubble counts are not a calibrated reaction rate: bubble sizes, gas retention and warming can differ. For a quantitative extension, use a freely moving gas syringe to record collected volume V(t) and compare early slopes dV/dt at matched temperature and pressure; check for leaks and repeat runs. This extension is a measurement proposal, not source-reported gas traces.
The paired treatment also changes pH and produces a surface, so the four tubes alone cannot assign the whole effect to one microscopic catalytic step. A filtered-liquid comparison and a separated, washed-solid comparison would help distinguish dissolved and solid contributions, although washing itself can change the active material. More gas at one time establishes neither a changed equilibrium constant nor indefinite catalyst reuse. The open gas-evolution experiment is especially unsuitable as a demonstration that two closed mixtures reach the same chemical equilibrium: the escaping oxygen changes the constraints. The reversible model above supplies that separate explanation. No numerical experimental rate constants, barrier heights or gas volumes are asserted here.
