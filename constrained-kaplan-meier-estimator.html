Constrained Kaplan–Meier estimator (∏sj​≤t​(1−qj​)=p)

 Home Mathematics Area of mathematics Probability and statistics Survival analysis Kaplan–Meier estimator
OurBigBook.com Words: 122
The Kaplan–Meier estimator constrained to have survivor function value p∈(0,1) at t maximizes the right-censoring log-likelihood ∑j​[dj​logqj​+(nj​−dj​)log(1−qj​)] subject to the displayed constraint. Here qj​ is the discrete hazard, dj​ the event count and nj​ the risk set size. In an interior event-time solution, a Lagrange multiplier gives qj​=dj​/(nj​+λ) before the constrained time, with qj​=dj​/nj​ afterwards. The multiplier is chosen to satisfy the product constraint. It is essential to allow a zero-event jump at the constrained time or another relevant cell boundary: a constraint can require mass outside observed event times. A general implementation instead maximizes the concave probability-mass log-likelihood with total mass one and mass above t equal to p, allowing all relevant cells and a terminal tail. This also handles unobserved tails and boundary solutions without imposing unjustified event-only support.
