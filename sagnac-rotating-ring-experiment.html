Sagnac rotating-ring experiment

 Home Physics Branches of physics Special relativity Sagnac effect Sagnac interferometer
OurBigBook.com Words: 194
In 1913 Georges Sagnac mounted a lamp, collimator, beam splitter, four mirrors and photographic plate on a horizontal rotating platform. The mirrors defined a closed polygon; the two beams traversed it in opposite senses. A slight beam splitter adjustment produced visible interference fringes. A motor brought the platform to steady rotation, and switching the lamp exposed the plate. Comparing opposite senses of rotation revealed a reversing displacement. Changing the beam splitter adjustment supplied a further check against optical deformation. The report compared measured and calculated displacements. Georges Sagnac interpreted this as evidence for a luminiferous aether; the relativistic derivation below reproduces the delay without that hypothesis.[ref]
The readout measures a relative wave phase, not two stopwatch readings separated by an optical transit time. Reversal isolates a contribution odd in angular velocity: if a signed interference fringe position is q(Ω)=q0​+kΩ to leading order, then [q(Ω)−q(−Ω)]/2=kΩ cancels the fixed offset q0​. Changes of alignment or temperature correlated with reversal can survive this subtraction. Thus the experimental inference concerns a reproducible rotating-loop phase difference, with controls for disturbances; it is not by itself a unique test of an underlying propagation hypothesis.
