Independent permissive gates in an ion channel (Popen​=pq)

 Home Biology Biological cell Membrane potential Voltage-gated ion channel Two-state ion-channel gating
OurBigBook.com Words: 322
A permissive gate allows conduction when it is open. Suppose an ion channel conducts only when all q gates are open, where q is a positive integer. If the gate-open events are mutually independent events and each has the same open probability p at the time considered, then
Popen​=∏j=1q​p=pq.
(16)
This follows from the multiplication rule for independent events. Identical transition rates alone do not guarantee independence: the initial gate states must also be independently prepared, and the model must not couple their transitions. Gates sharing a fluctuating membrane potential can be correlated; the simple product requires an appropriate independence assumption, for example independent gates at an externally fixed voltage.
At p=0.5, channels requiring q=1,2,4 permissive gates have open probabilities 0.5, 0.25 and 0.0625. For four independent gates there are 24=16 possible open/closed configurations, equally probable at p=0.5; only the all-open configuration conducts. Thus 0.5 is the gate-open probability, while 0.0625 is the whole-channel open probability. Neither is a fractional state of one observed channel. Among 160 independently prepared identical four-gate channels, the expected value of the open count is 160/16=10, although an actual count fluctuates.
For unequal independent gate-open probabilities pj​, use Popen​=∏j​pj​ rather than pq. This is the distinction behind the Hodgkin-Huxley model's m3h: m and h are different activation and inactivation-availability variables, rather than one common p. Its n4 uses four identical activation factors. Independence and the all-open requirement are model assumptions; correlated gates or alternative conducting configurations require a different calculation. The simple product is useful because a modest gate-open probability can produce sharply suppressed whole-channel availability without any additional gate interaction.
