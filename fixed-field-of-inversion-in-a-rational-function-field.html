Fixed field of inversion in a rational function field (k(t)t↦1/t=k(t+1/t))

 ... Area of mathematics Algebra Field extension Algebraic independence Algebraically independent elements Rational function field
OurBigBook.com Words: 144
Let t be transcendental over a field k and let τ be the field automorphism sending t to 1/t. The rational function u=t+1/t is fixed, and t satisfies X2−uX+1=0. Thus the extension over k(u) has degree at most two. It is not one because the nontrivial automorphism τ fixes k(u). The fixed field of τ also has index two by the fixed-field theorem, so it equals k(u).
No nonconstant polynomial in k[t] is fixed by inversion. For a polynomial of positive degree, substitution of 1/t creates negative powers, whereas the original has positive powers. Uniqueness of the coefficients of a Laurent polynomial rules out equality. Consequently this fixed field cannot have a generator that is a polynomial in the original variable, even though it has the simple rational-function generator above. The statement is valid in characteristic two as well: t and 1/t remain distinct transcendental rational functions.
