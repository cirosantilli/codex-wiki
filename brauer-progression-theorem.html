Brauer progression theorem

 Home Mathematics Area of mathematics Combinatorics Ramsey theory Van der Waerden theorem
OurBigBook.com Words: 110
For positive integers k,s,ℓ, some N ensures that every k-color finite coloring of [N] contains a monochromatic set
{sd,a,a+d,…,a+(ℓ−1)d},a,d>0.
(6)
It follows from the Van der Waerden theorem by mathematical induction on the number of colors. If M is a bound for k−1 colors, take a long one-color arithmetic progression of length (ℓ−1)M+1 and step d. Either one of sd,2sd,…,Msd has its color, giving the result immediately, or these multiples use only k−1 colors. Pull back their finite coloring to [M] and use mathematical induction, then multiply the resulting configuration by sd. Enlarge the ambient finite integer interval to include all these multiples. The cases k=1 and ℓ=1 are immediate. The parameter s makes this useful for positive monochromatic solutions of a partition regular equation with arbitrary integer coefficients.
