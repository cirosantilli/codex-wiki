Square wave (sgn(sinθ)=π4​∑n≥1, n odd​nsinnθ​)

Words: 54
Fourier series OurBigBook.com
A square wave alternates between two constant values for equal halves of a period. For the 2π-periodic function equal to 1 on (0,π) and −1 on (π,2π), the Fourier coefficients are a0​=an​=0 and bn​=2[1−(−1)n]/(πn). Its Fourier series contains only odd sine modes. At a jump discontinuity the Fourier series converges to the midpoint 0, irrespective of the assigned point value.

Uniqueness of a trigonometric series outside a finite set

Words: 95
Fourier series OurBigBook.com
If the symmetric partial sums of ∑r∈Z​ar​eirt tend to zero at every point outside a finite set, then all coefficients vanish. Almost-everywhere convergence first gives a±n​→0. The twice-integrated series F(t)=a0​t2/2−∑r=0​ar​eirt/r2 is continuous. Riemann summation of a convergent trigonometric series makes its symmetric second derivative zero away from the exceptional points, so it is affine on each intervening interval. Sinc-squared summation of a sequence tending to zero makes its symmetric first-order quotient zero everywhere, removing all possible affine corners. The globally affine function differs from a periodic function by a0​t2/2, forcing a0​=0; periodicity then makes it constant, and integration against each nonzero frequency gives ar​=0.

Kahane-Katznelson divergence theorem

Words: 57
Fourier series OurBigBook.com
Every null subset of the circle lies in the divergence set of the Fourier series of some continuous complex-valued function. One can arrange unbounded Fourier partial sums there. The compact-set Fourier amplification lemma, compact batching of a small open set and frequency-separated Fourier block series construct the function by a summable series of small blocks. The result covers nonclosed and dense null sets; it does not require the divergence set to equal the originally specified set.

For an integer m, direct integration gives zero unless m=0, in which case it gives one. Products of this identity on the unit cube turn integrals of finite exponential sums into counts of integer solutions. This is the elementary orthogonality used in the Vinogradov mean value.

Dirichlet-Jordan convergence theorem

Words: 61
Fourier series OurBigBook.com
The Fourier series of a periodic function of bounded variation converges at each point to the mean of its two one-sided limits. In particular, it converges to the function at continuity points. Periodizing a compact interval introduces jumps at its integer endpoints; their half-values explain the half-weight convention in the Van der Corput sum-integral lemma. The conclusion is pointwise convergence, not a claim of absolute convergence of every such Fourier series.

Pointwise convergence of a piecewise smooth Fourier series

Words: 57
Fourier series OurBigBook.com
For a periodic function that is piecewise continuously differentiable with finitely many pieces, its Fourier series converges at each point to the average of the two one-sided limits. At a point of continuity this is the function value. This includes the identified interval endpoints, whose two limits belong to the periodic extension. Absolute summability of the Fourier coefficients additionally gives uniform convergence.

Generic unbounded Fourier sums at a fixed point

Words: 98
Fourier partial sum OurBigBook.com
For each integer m≥1, the set of continuous functions satisfying ∣SN​(f,0)∣≤m for every N is closed in the uniform norm. If it contained a ball of radius r, subtracting two members of that ball would bound every partial-sum functional norm by 4m/r. The Dirichlet kernel harmonic lower bound excludes this, so these sets are nowhere dense. By the Baire category theorem, their complement is a dense Gδ​ set. Every function in that complement has unbounded Fourier partial sums at zero. Translation gives the same conclusion at any prescribed point. This is a direct Uniform boundedness principle argument on the real Banach space of continuous functions on the circle.

Bounded nonconvergent Fourier partial sums

Words: 70
Fourier partial sum OurBigBook.com
A continuous complex-valued function can have uniformly bounded Fourier partial sums which fail to converge at one point. Normalize harmonic sine polynomials by their harmonic coefficient sums, modulate them into disjoint high-frequency intervals and choose their degrees so that the complete norms are summable. At zero, complete blocks vanish while their midpoint prefixes have a common nonzero value. The frequency-separated Fourier block series gives a uniform bound on every partial sum as well as two distinct subsequential values.

If trigonometric polynomials have spectra in increasing disjoint positive-frequency intervals, the displayed summability gives a continuous uniform sum. Fixed-size excursions occurring in arbitrarily late blocks contradict the Cauchy criterion, even when the complete blocks vanish at the point under consideration.
