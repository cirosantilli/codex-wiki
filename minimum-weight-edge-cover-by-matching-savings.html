Minimum-weight edge cover by matching savings (OPT=∑v​cv​−maxM​∑uv∈M​(cu​+cv​−w(uv)))

For a finite graph without an isolated vertex and with nonnegative edge weights, put cv​=mine∋v​w(e) and let ev​ attain this minimum. Define savings p(uv)=cu​+cv​−w(uv) and baseline B=∑v​cv​. A maximum-weight matching M with weights p, allowing arbitrary cardinality, gives a minimum-weight edge cover
FM​=M∪{ev​:v is unmatched by M}.
(11)
It replaces separate cheapest coverage of two endpoints by a shared edge when doing so saves enough weight. Savings can be negative even though original costs are nonnegative; such edges need not be selected.
Proof. The construction covers all vertices. Duplicate selected edges are counted once, so nonnegative weights give w(FM​)≤B−p(M). Take an optimal edge cover and delete redundant edges to make it inclusion-minimal without increasing cost. Its components are star graphs, by the argument for the edge cover and matching identity. Select one edge from each component to obtain a matching in a graph N. A component with center a, selected leaf b and other leaves L contributes w(ab)+∑v∈L​cv​ to B−p(N), at most its total edge weight since cv​≤w(av). Thus B−p(N)≤OPT. Maximizing p gives
OPT≤w(FM​)≤B−p(M)≤B−p(N)≤OPT,
(12)
which proves both the construction and the displayed identity, including zero-weight cases. One call to a general-graph maximum-weight matching polynomial-time algorithm suffices, together with linear-time preprocessing. Choosing a maximum cardinality matching under these modified weights would impose an unnecessary constraint and can spoil the reduction.[ref]
