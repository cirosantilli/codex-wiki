Burgers characteristic breaking (x=ξ+tu0​(ξ),ux​=1+tu0′​(ξ)u0′​(ξ)​)

 ... Area of mathematics Analysis Partial differential equation Transport equation Scalar conservation law Inviscid Burgers equation
OurBigBook.com Words: 210
Before characteristic crossing, the method of characteristics for the Inviscid Burgers equation transports each initial value without changing it:
dtdx​=u,dtdu​=0,x=ξ+tu0​(ξ),u(x,t)=u0​(ξ).
(16)
Here ξ labels the starting position. Suppose u0​ is a bounded continuously differentiable function with bounded derivative. As long as 1+tu0′​(ξ)>0 for every label, the characteristic flow map is invertible and differentiating it gives the displayed formula for ux​.[ref]
If the negative minimum m=minξ​u0′​(ξ) is attained, the first breaking time is t∗​=−1/m. Two labels ξ1​<ξ2​ with u0​(ξ1​)>u0​(ξ2​) have formal intersection time
t=u0​(ξ1​)−u0​(ξ2​)ξ2​−ξ1​​>0.
(17)
Their neighboring-label limit explains why the most negative initial slope breaks first. As t↑t∗​, the amplitude remains bounded but ux​ diverges: this is gradient blow-up. Continuing intersecting lines would assign incompatible values at one position; the post-breaking entropy solution instead uses shock waves. Nondecreasing data have no forward characteristic crossing by this mechanism.
For a concrete calculation in dimensionless quantities on the infinite line, choose u0​(ξ)=1−tanhξ, with limiting states 2 and 0. Then u0′​(ξ)=−(1−tanh2ξ) has minimum −1 at ξ=0, so t∗​=1 and the breaking point is (x,t)=(1,1). Along the central characteristic curve x=t, u=1 and ux​=−1/(1−t) before breaking. The labels ξ=±1 give intersecting formal lines at t=1/tanh1≈1.313, after the classical solution has already broken.
