Friedgut-Kalai sharp threshold theorem (q−p≥Clog((1−ε)/ε)/logn)

Words: 68
Analysis of Boolean functions OurBigBook.com
There is an absolute C>0 such that any nontrivial transitive increasing event on n≥2 independent bits satisfies μq​(A)>1−ε if μp​(A)>ε, 0<ε<1/2, and the displayed bound holds with p<q<1. A uniform weighted Kahn-Kalai-Linial theorem gives one pivotal probability at least cμr​(A)(1−μr​(A))log(n)/n. Transitivity makes the total influence at least cμr​(A)(1−μr​(A))logn. The Margulis–Russo formula identifies this with the derivative of μr​(A). Integration of the derivative of its log odds proves the threshold bound.

Kahn-Kalai-Linial theorem (maxi​Ii​(f)≥cVar(f)log(n)/n)

Words: 179 Articles: 2
Analysis of Boolean functions OurBigBook.com
For a Boolean function f:{0,1}n→{0,1} under the uniform probability measure, some coordinate has influence of a variable at least cVar(f)log(n)/n, where c>0 is an absolute constant and Ii​(f) is the probability that flipping coordinate i changes f. For variance bounded below, this is order log(n)/n. The tribes function shows that this order cannot be improved for general balanced Boolean functions.

Simplicial section compression (Cisimp​)

Words: 146 Articles: 1
Vertex-isoperimetric inequality in the discrete cube OurBigBook.com
Split a set family in the hypercube graph according to whether coordinate i is present, and delete that coordinate from the present section. Replace both sections by initial segments of the simplicial order on the discrete cube with the same respective sizes. Induction and nesting of initial-segment closed graph neighbourhoods show that this operation cannot enlarge the original closed graph neighbourhood. Repeated nontrivial compressions terminate because the sum of simplicial positions strictly decreases.
Terminal families for simplicial section compression
Words: 90
Simplicial section compression OurBigBook.com
A family fixed by every simplicial section compression is either an initial segment of the simplicial order on the discrete cube or that segment with its last vertex exchanged for the next vertex, where the exchanged vertices are complementary. Indeed, an earlier absent vertex and a later present vertex must differ in every coordinate, and cannot have another vertex between them. Complementary consecutive vertices occur only across the two central ranks in odd dimension, or at the transition from middle-rank sets containing coordinate one to those avoiding it in even dimension. Direct closed graph neighbourhood comparison resolves these exceptions in the proof of Harper theorem.

Complementation and reversal of coordinates turn this into colexicographic order minimizing the lower shadow, giving the Kruskal-Katona theorem.

Entropy proof of cube edge-isoperimetry
Words: 162 Articles: 1

For a vertex set of size m in a hypercube graph, split one coordinate into sections of sizes a,b. Induction bounds the internal edges within the sections by (alog2​a+blog2​b)/2; at most min(a,b) edges cross between them. Put t=min(a,b)/(a+b). The chord bound for the binary entropy function gives H2​(t)≥2t, hence alog2​a+blog2​b+2min(a,b)≤mlog2​m. Equality cases of entropy cube edge-isoperimetry (e(A)=21​∣A∣log2​∣A∣⟺A is a coordinate subcube)

For a nonempty subset of a hypercube graph, split it into sections of sizes a,b. The proof of the entropy edge bound uses H2​(t)≥2t, t=min(a,b)/(a+b). Strict concavity makes equality possible only at t=0 or t=1/2. In the first case one section is empty; in the second the sections coincide, since all possible crossing edges must be present. Functional completeness

Words: 163 Articles: 2
Boolean function OurBigBook.com
A set of Boolean functions is functionally complete if function composition using those operations can express every positive-arity Boolean function. The NAND gate alone suffices: tie its inputs to obtain negation, negate its output to obtain logical conjunction, and apply De Morgan's laws to obtain logical disjunction; then use disjunctive normal form.
Negation with one binary connective (8 adequate binary truth functions)
Words: 59
Functional completeness OurBigBook.com
Among sixteen binary Boolean truth functions, eight become functionally complete together with negation. The eight affine functions cannot express conjunction because composition preserves affinity. Each of the other eight truth tables has exactly one or three true rows. Negating the output if needed and selected inputs transforms its unique true row to the conjunction table. Thus conjunction and negation become definable and disjunctive normal form expresses every Boolean function.
Singleton truth-preservation obstruction to expressive completeness
Words: 67
Functional completeness OurBigBook.com
Suppose a first-order language has logical implication as its only connective and has either or both of the quantifiers, but no primitive falsity. In a singleton structure with every atomic predicate true, all atomic equalities are also true, and structural induction makes every formula true. No formula of this language can therefore express a negated equality. Quantifiers cannot repair this failure of expressive completeness, even though they add syntactic operations beyond propositional connectives.

Consensus theorem (xy+xˉz+yz=xy+xˉz)

Words: 76
Boolean function OurBigBook.com
In Boolean algebra, the product yz is the consensus of xy and xˉz. If yz=1, either x=1 and xy=1, or x=0 and xˉz=1, so adding the consensus does not change the Boolean function. It can change a circuit's transient behavior: while x switches, the two original product terms may briefly both be zero because of unequal propagation delays, but yz remains one. Keeping the logically redundant consensus product can therefore remove a static-one logic hazard.

An input combination at which a Boolean function's specification imposes no required output value. Karnaugh map minimization may assign either value independently to simplify the implemented Boolean circuit. The resulting circuit still assigns a definite output to every input. An unused finite-state machine encoding is therefore an optimization opportunity, but its eventual transitions must be checked if recovery from that encoding matters.

Karnaugh map

Words: 58
Boolean function OurBigBook.com
A truth table arranged so neighbouring cells differ in one Boolean variable, using Gray code order along each multi-bit axis. Opposite edges are also adjacent. A valid group contains all 2k combinations of k varying variables while every other variable stays fixed; it eliminates those k variables and gives an implicant; covering the one-cells yields a disjunctive normal form. The method is associated with Maurice Karnaugh.

Every term in a disjunctive normal form is an implicant of the represented Boolean function. To rule out a one-literal term, it suffices to find a false Boolean valuation of f where that literal is true.

Balanced Boolean function (∣f−1(0)∣=∣f−1(1)∣)

Words: 55
Boolean function OurBigBook.com
A Boolean function on a finite nonempty domain is balanced when it is zero on exactly half the inputs and one on the other half. For a one-bit domain, the balanced functions are f(x)=x and f(x)=1⊕x, while the remaining two functions are constant functions. The Deutsch algorithm distinguishes these two classes with one Boolean quantum oracle query in the absence of noise.

Under uniform independent input bits, a zero-one Boolean function is quite fair when its mean lies between one quarter and three quarters. This convention excludes functions whose output is nearly constant; it does not require exact balance or equal coordinate influences.

Algebraic normal form (f(z)=⨁S⊆[d]​cS​∏i∈S​zi​)

Words: 85
Boolean function OurBigBook.com
The algebraic normal form is the unique multilinear polynomial representation of a Boolean function over the field with two elements, using exclusive or for addition and ordinary bit multiplication for products. The empty product is one. Existence and uniqueness follow by induction from f(z′,zd​)=f(z′,0)⊕zd​(f(z′,0)⊕f(z′,1)): the first polynomial supplies all coefficients not containing zd​, and the difference supplies exactly those that do. For split inputs (x,y), each monomial factors into one locally computed product of Alice's bits and one of Bob's bits, yielding a separated Boolean decomposition.

Separated Boolean decomposition (f(x,y)=⨁ξ∈{0,1}m​1{x=ξ}​f(ξ,y))

Words: 67
Boolean function OurBigBook.com
Every Boolean function of distributed inputs is an exclusive or of products of locally computable bits. The displayed identity follows because exactly one indicator is one. It uses at most 2m terms, each a function of x times a function of y; exchanging the two input roles gives at most 2n terms. This elementary truth-table factorization is useful for distributed computation with PR boxes and does not depend on a probabilistic approximation.

Tribes function

Words: 243 Articles: 2
Boolean function OurBigBook.com
A tribes function is an OR of AND blocks: partition mw coordinates into m blocks of size w, and let f=1 when at least one block consists entirely of ones. For independent random variables with each bit uniform, P(f=0)=(1−2−w)m. The influence of a variable is 2−(w−1)(1−2−w)m−1, because every other bit in its block must be one and every other block must fail. Taking m approximately (log2)2w keeps both output probabilities bounded away from zero and gives influences of order log(mw)/(mw). This shows the order in the Kahn-Kalai-Linial theorem is sharp.
