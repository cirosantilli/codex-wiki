Picard-Lindelöf theorem (y′=f(t,y),y(t0​)=y0​)

 Home Mathematics Area of mathematics Analysis Differential equation Ordinary differential equation
OurBigBook.com Words: 1k Articles: 3
Let U⊂R×Rd be an open set and f:U→Rd a continuous function. Suppose f is locally Lipschitz in the state y, with a Lipschitz constant uniform on a sufficiently small time-state neighborhood of each point. Then each (t0​,y0​)∈U determines a unique local solution of a differential equation to the displayed initial value problem, on an interval around t0​. This is the local theorem in Ordinary Differential Equations and Dynamical Systems by Gerald Teschl.
For example, y′=y, y(0)=1, has the solution of a differential equation y(t)=et: its derivative is itself and its starting value is 1. The Lipschitz constant is L=1, since ∣u−v∣=∣f(t,u)−f(t,v)∣. Thus uniqueness follows even before computing y(1)=e≈2.71828183. Picard iteration below constructs this exponential function one polynomial at a time.
The Lipschitz condition has real content. For the continuous function involving a square root,
f(y)=2max(y,0)​,y′=f(y),y(0)=0,
(45)
each waiting time c≥0 gives a different forward solution of a differential equation:
yc​(t)={0,(t−c)2,​0≤t≤c,t>c.​
(46)
The constant solution of a differential equation y(t)=0 is another choice. At t=2, delays c=0,1,2 give y=4,1,0 respectively. Before the join, both sides of the ordinary differential equation are zero; afterwards, yc′​=2(t−c)=2yc​​. Both one-sided derivatives at c are zero, so the join is valid. All these trajectories can also be extended by zero to t<0. The failed Lipschitz condition is visible in the quotient
∣ε−0∣∣f(ε)−f(0)∣​=ε​2​⟶∞(ε↓0).
(47)
For ε=10−2,10−4,10−6 this quotient is 20,200,2000. Continuity still allows local existence by the Peano existence theorem; it cannot choose a waiting time. MIT's 18.034 Lecture 2, Example 2.3 develops the same waiting-time mechanism for a different ordinary differential equation, y′=3y2/3.
The useful promise is that an initial condition and a sufficiently regular local rule select one trajectory. It justifies comparing models and following trajectories without an arbitrary choice at each instant. A locally Lipschitz continuous rule need not keep that trajectory bounded: y′=1+y2 meets the theorem's hypotheses everywhere but has finite-time blowup. Longer existence is governed by the finite-dimensional continuation criterion.
