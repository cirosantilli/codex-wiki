Envelope detector

 Home Electrical engineering Amplitude modulation
OurBigBook.com Words: 280
An envelope detector extracts the slowly varying nonnegative wave envelope of a rapid carrier wave. In a simple electrical version a diode charges a capacitor near positive carrier peaks and an attached resistor discharges it between peaks. When 0≤m<1, ideal envelope extraction gives E(t)=A[1+mcos(Ωt)]; removing the constant A leaves Amcos(Ωt). This permits recovery without a synchronized carrier reference. Carrier injection thus trades transmitted power for simplicity.[ref]

Differentiating the ratio locates its maximum at cosθ=−m. This original slope check explains why operation near m=1 is demanding even before overmodulation: the upper bound tends to zero while the need to smooth carrier cycles persists. It is an approximate circuit criterion, not a guarantee in the presence of diode drops, loading or noise. At m=0 there is no varying message to track; at m=1 ideal envelope extraction remains algebraically possible, but the simple detector has no positive trough margin.
For m>1, even an ideal noiseless envelope detector returns A∣q(t)∣. Removing its mean or its original offset cannot restore the discarded sign. A more elaborate receiver can use the retained wave phase through coherent demodulation.
