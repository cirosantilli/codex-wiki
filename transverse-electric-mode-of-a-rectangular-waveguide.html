Transverse electric mode of a rectangular waveguide (TEmn​)

 Home Physics Branches of physics Electromagnetism Electromagnetic wave Rectangular electromagnetic waveguide
OurBigBook.com Words: 233 Articles: 1
A transverse electric mode of a rectangular waveguide has Ez​=0 while its axial magnetic field is generally nonzero. For time dependence e−iωt and axial dependence eiβz, set Δ=(ω/c)2−β2. The Maxwell equations reconstruct the transverse components from Bz​:
Bx​=Δiβ​∂x​Bz​,By​=Δiβ​∂y​Bz​,Ex​=Δiω​∂y​Bz​,Ey​=−Δiω​∂x​Bz​.
(10)
The axial complex amplitude satisfies a transverse Helmholtz equation, (∂x2​+∂y2​+Δ)Bz​=0. Zero tangential electric field at each perfect conductor wall becomes a Neumann boundary condition on Bz​. Separation of variables gives
Bz​=B∗​cos(mπx/a)cos(nπy/b)eiβz,Δ=κmn2​.
(11)
The index m counts half-periods across a, and n across b. Either index can vanish, allowing TE10​; both cannot vanish. Indeed a spatially constant axial oscillating magnetic field would give nonzero changing magnetic flux through the cross-section, whereas the line integral of tangential electric field around its metal boundary is zero. The integral form of Faraday's law excludes this TE00​ candidate. Dividing the reconstruction formulas by Δ=0 would be invalid and cannot produce the separate transverse electromagnetic mode.
For m=1,n=0, choose B∗​=−i(π/a)E0​/ω. The formulas recover exactly the Ey​, Bx​ and Bz​ in rectangular electromagnetic waveguide. In particular, the transverse magnetic field accompanies Bz​ and ensures zero divergence; an oscillating longitudinal magnetic field on its own is not the guided solution.
