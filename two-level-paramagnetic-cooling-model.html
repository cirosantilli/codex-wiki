Two-level paramagnetic cooling model (x=μB/(kB​T))

Use the independent spin-one-half two-level system with fixed N distinguishable sites and Hamiltonian levels E±​=±μB, where μ>0 is the magnitude of the field-axis magnetic moment and B>0 is a uniform externally controlled magnetic field. Assume positive temperature, no interactions between sites, and no appreciable lattice or load entropy. The two levels differ by 2μB. With inverse temperature β, the canonical ensemble gives the one-site and total partition functions
β=kB​T1​,x=βμB,z=ex+e−x=2coshx,Z=zN,p−​=2coshxex​,p+​=2coshxe−x​.
(6)
Here the minus subscript denotes the lower energy −μB. Factorization of the partition function requires additive energy and independent sites; there is no particle-exchange factor N! for these fixed distinguishable sites.
Differentiate the partition function, holding B fixed when differentiating with respect to β. The internal energy, Helmholtz free energy, total field-axis magnetic moment m, and magnetic entropy are
Umag​=−∂β​lnZ=−NμBtanhx,F=−NkB​Tln(2coshx),

(8)
The extensive moment m is distinct from magnetization, the moment per unit volume. Direct substitution of p±​ into the Gibbs entropy gives the same s(x) independently of the free-energy derivative.
At fixed B, the magnetic heat capacity is
CB,mag​=(∂T∂Umag​​)B​=NkB​x2sech2x≥0,dxds​=−xsech2x<0(x>0).
(9)
Strict decrease makes s invertible on x>0. Constant magnetic entropy therefore fixes x, not B or T separately:
TB​=constant,Ti​Tf​​=Bi​Bf​​(Bi​,Bf​,Ti​,Tf​>0).
(10)
The partition function may be used to label these equilibrium states even though the isolated cooling leg has no external heat bath. Actual thermalization of the lattice and load additionally requires a sufficiently fast spin-lattice relaxation; perfectly uncoupled spins by themselves cannot refrigerate an attached object.
The limiting cases are instructive. For x≪1, s(x)=ln2−x2/2+O(x4), m≃Nμ2B/(kB​T), and CB,mag​≃NkB​x2. The moment response reproduces Curie's law. For x≫1, s(x)≃(1+2x)e−2x and the upper-state occupation is exponentially small. Taking B→0 at fixed T>0 gives s→ln2; taking T→0 at fixed B>0 gives s→0. The origin has a path-dependent limit. Extrapolating a line of constant x to B=T=0 does not construct a usable zero-temperature state or prove that a real material retains independent degenerate spins there.
