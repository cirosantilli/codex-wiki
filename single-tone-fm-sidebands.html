Single-tone FM sidebands (fk​=fc​+kfm​)

 Home Electrical engineering Frequency modulation
OurBigBook.com Words: 430
A single-tone FM signal has components displaced from the carrier wave frequency by integer multiples of the message frequency. These are temporal sidebands; the spatial sidebands used to perturb a spatial pattern are a related but different setting. To find the temporal components, apply the Jacobi-Anger expansion to the complex representation:

Here Jk​ is a Bessel function of the first kind. The identity follows by setting the generating variable to eiθ in its generating function.[ref]
Taking the real part gives the exact real signal expansion
s(t)=A∑k=−∞∞​Jk​(β)cos(2π(fc​+kfm​)t).
(17)
The complex representation has a Fourier coefficient AJk​(β) at fc​+kfm​; the normalized magnitude is ∣Jk​(β)∣. The identity J−k​(β)=(−1)kJk​(β) makes upper and lower complex sideband magnitudes equal, but their signs matter when reconstructing the wave phase. A negative coefficient reverses a component's wave phase by π; an amplitude display alone hides that information.
For β=5, original evaluation gives J0​(5)≈−0.17760, J1​(5)≈−0.32758, J5​(5)≈0.26114 and J6​(5)≈0.13105. Thus there are appreciable components at 14 and 26Hz, outside the 15–25Hz range of the instantaneous frequency. There is no contradiction: instantaneous frequency describes the local rate of the combined wave phase, while the frequency spectrum describes a global superposition of constant-frequency components.
For the real signal, negative frequencies must also be accounted for: a cosine at −f equals one at f. When fc​/fm​=20, the normalized positive-frequency cosine coefficient at nfm​, n>0, is exactly Jn−20​(β)+J−n−20​(β). The second term is extremely small over the plotted range, but the figure includes it. The chosen complex representation need not have a frequency spectrum supported only at positive frequencies, even when its specified instantaneous frequency is everywhere positive.
For a nonzero FM modulation index, the ideal frequency spectrum has infinitely many sidebands. Many become tiny, but drawing only visible lines does not make the exact signal bandwidth finite. A useful engineering width must state a threshold or a retained-power fraction. For the complex representation, Parseval's identity gives a simple check:
2π1​∫02π​​eiβsinθ​2dθ=∑k=−∞∞​∣Jk​(β)∣2=1.

At β=5, the sum through ∣k∣≤6 is approximately 0.99356. Retaining those complex components captures over 99% of their squared magnitudes and spans 12Hz from 14 to 26Hz. This is a stated truncation criterion, not an exact cutoff or a universal bandwidth rule. It also explains why a diminished carrier component need not imply a diminished envelope: its contribution has been redistributed among sidebands.
