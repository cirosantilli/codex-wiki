Local Joule heating rate (pvol​=J⋅E)

 ... Electric charge Electric current Electrical resistance and conductance Electrical resistance Ohm's law Joule heating
OurBigBook.com Words: 226
The electric field does work on matter at local power per volume
pvol​=J⋅E,[pvol​]=m2A​mV​=Wm−3.
(93)
Here J is current density and the dot product includes the relative direction. Positive J⋅E means energy passes from the field to matter; negative means matter supplies energy to the field. In general this is a work rate, not automatically an irreversible heating rate.[ref]
For a stationary isotropic Ohmic conductor with real scalar electrical conductivity σ>0, local Ohm's law gives J=σE. Thus the local Joule heating rate is
pvol​=σ∣E∣2=σ∣J∣2​=ρel​∣J∣2≥0,ρel​=σ1​.
(94)
The electrical resistivity ρel​ has units Ωm, and electrical conductivity has units (Ωm)−1. These formulas use the material's rest frame and dissipative conduction current density. Moving media, complex conductivity, and additional storage or transport processes require their appropriate energy balance rather than this scalar substitution.
For uniform current in a straight homogeneous wire of length L, wire cross-sectional area A and constant electrical conductivity, E=V/L, J=I/A and R=L/(σA). The volume integral recovers terminal power:
P=∫wire​J⋅Ed3x=AI​LV​(AL)=IV=I2R.
(95)
As a numerical check, the wire already used in electrical resistance has L=1m, A=10−6m2, σ=5×105(Ωm)−1 and V=5V. It gives J=2.5×106A/m2, pvol​=1.25×107W/m3 and P=12.5W, agreeing with I=2.5A and R=2Ω. Concentrating the same electric current in a smaller wire cross-sectional area therefore raises the local heating rate if electrical resistivity stays fixed.
