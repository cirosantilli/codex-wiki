Photogate measurement of acceleration down an incline (a=dv/dt)

 Home Physics Branches of physics Classical mechanics Inclined plane
OurBigBook.com Words: 334
A modern teaching experiment slows gravity-driven motion enough to resolve it with a photogate. PASCO scientific's Inclined Plane Dynamics Set, ME-6966, Instruction Manual 012-10874A describes “Acceleration Down an Incline” on printed pages 15–16. Its apparatus is a GOcar on a PAStrack with an adjustable incline support, a bracket-mounted photogate, a cart-mounted picket fence, and an interface with recording software. For the interface method, the fence's 1cm pattern is aligned to interrupt the beam. Recording starts before release from rest; the cart is caught at the bottom bumper. The procedure specifies three runs per angle, starting at 14∘ and reducing by 2∘, with the release position fixed. The readout is velocity against time; its slope estimates acceleration. Mean accelerations are plotted against sinθ, testing the ideal slope g. An added-mass comparison is also requested. These are protocol settings, not observed results.[ref]
The cart undergoes rolling without slipping at its wheels, whereas the worked block undergoes contact slip. Even ideal wheel moment of inertia changes the cart prediction. For total cart mass M, wheel radii ri​ and axial moments of inertia Ii​, rigid wheels with fixed axles and no losses give
a=1+i∑​Ii​/(Mri2​)gsinθ​.
(8)
To derive it, combine translational kinetic energy Mv2/2 with wheel rotational kinetic energy ∑i​Ii​(v/ri​)2/2, then equate their rate of change to the release of gravitational potential energy Mgsinθv. This is the multiwheel counterpart of rolling acceleration with rotational inertia. Neglecting wheel moment of inertia yields a≃gsinθ; treating a cart as a solid rolling cylinder would give a different correction.
Useful additional controls are repeated releases without a push, checking the fence alignment and spacing, and checking that the recorded velocity trace is straight over the fitted interval. A gently moving cart on a level track can expose losses or unintended tilt. These checks help distinguish an angle or timing error from neglected wheel moment of inertia, bearing friction, or drag force. The three-run spread measures repeatability but cannot by itself reveal a shared calibration error. The inspected protocol makes the gravity component measurable; it does not measure the sliding block's coefficient of kinetic friction.
