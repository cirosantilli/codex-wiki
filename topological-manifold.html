Topological manifold (M)

 Home Mathematics Area of mathematics Geometry and topology Topology
OurBigBook.com Words: 7k Articles: 110
A topological n-manifold without boundary is a topological space M satisfying three conditions: it is Hausdorff, it is second countable, and every point p∈M lies in an open set U⊆M with a homeomorphism φ:U→V onto an open set V⊆Rn. Here n≥0 is a fixed dimension of a manifold, and Rn has the usual topology of Euclidean space. The pair (U,φ) is a coordinate chart. Unless explicitly qualified, “topological manifold” here means without boundary.[ref]
The Hausdorff condition requires disjoint open neighbourhoods for distinct points. Second countability requires a countable basis of a topology. Neither follows merely from the local homeomorphisms: non-Hausdorff manifolds fail the first condition, while an uncountable disjoint union of copies of the real line fails the second. Indeed, a basis of a topology must supply a different nonempty member inside each copy, so it cannot be countable.
The circle and the real line are both one-dimensional topological manifolds, with the same local model. Their global topologies differ: the circle is compact, while the real line is not. By contrast, the junction of a topological tripod already fails the local test. Thus local coordinate charts specify what kind of small pieces are allowed; global topological invariants distinguish how those pieces fit together.[ref]
The usefulness of a topological manifold is that local questions about continuity can be transported into Euclidean space, while compactness, connectedness, and the fundamental group still record global information. For calculus, further structure is needed: a smooth atlas requires smooth transition maps, letting the chain rule reconcile derivatives computed in different coordinate charts. A topological atlas by itself only guarantees homeomorphisms between coordinates.[ref]

Orientation of a topological manifold

Words: 110
Topological manifold OurBigBook.com
An orientation of a topological manifold chooses a generator of every local homology group Hn​(M,M∖{x};Z)≅Z, compatibly on coordinate neighbourhoods. Each choice is a local orientation of a manifold. Compatibility means the generators are locally restrictions of the same relative orientation class. It can be checked by choosing oriented coordinate balls and requiring agreement wherever their local classes overlap.
For a connected closed manifold, an orientation gives a unique fundamental class in Hn​(M;Z) whose restriction at each point is the chosen local generator. In an oriented triangulation this class is the sum of compatibly signed top simplices: interior faces cancel in opposite pairs, while the local coefficient in every top simplex is one. Reversing every local generator negates the fundamental class.

Topological atlas (A)

Words: 90 Articles: 1
Topological manifold OurBigBook.com
A topological atlas is a family A={(Ui​,φi​)} of coordinate charts whose domains form an open cover of a topological manifold. Each point must belong to at least one chart domain; no individual domain needs to cover the entire topological manifold. On overlaps, topological transition maps describe the same points in two coordinate systems.

Topological transition map (gji​=φj​∘φi−1​)

Words: 45
Topological atlas OurBigBook.com
For two coordinate charts in a topological atlas, the topological transition map is the homeomorphism
gji​:φi​(Ui​∩Uj​)⟶φj​(Ui​∩Uj​),gji​=φj​∘φi−1​.
(1)
Both domains are open sets in Euclidean space. Function composition gives gij​=gji−1​ and gki​=gkj​∘gji​ wherever all three coordinate charts overlap. These identities express consistency of the coordinate descriptions; imposing smooth functions as the coordinate changes gives a smooth atlas.

Two stereographic charts on the circle (S1)

Words: 354
Topological manifold OurBigBook.com
Take the circle S1={(x,y)∈R2:x2+y2=1} with its subspace topology. Write W=(−1,0) for its west point and E=(1,0) for its east point. Two coordinate charts obtained by stereographic projection are
U=S1∖{W},φ(x,y)=t=1+xy​,V=S1∖{E},ψ(x,y)=u=1−xy​.
(2)
Both take values in the entire real line. Geometrically, join W to (x,y) and intersect the resulting line with x=0: the height is t. Projecting from E instead gives height u. Each projection omits its own starting point, but the other coordinate chart includes that point. Hence U∪V=S1.[ref]
To verify the homeomorphisms, solve the displayed equations for (x,y). The inverse functions are
φ−1(t)=(1+t21−t2​,1+t22t​),ψ−1(u)=(1+u2u2−1​,1+u22u​).
(3)
Each formula lies on the circle, avoids its excluded point, and is continuous for every real argument. Substitution into the projection formulas returns t or u; substitution in the other direction returns (x,y). Thus these are continuous bijections with continuous inverse functions, as required for coordinate charts. Since the Euclidean plane is Hausdorff and second countable, the subset S1 with its subspace topology is too. This checks all three conditions for a topological manifold, rather than just drawing a smooth-looking curve.
On the overlap U∩V, both x=±1 are excluded, so y=0. Using y2=1−x2, the topological transition map is
u=1−xy​=y1+x​=t1​,t∈R∖{0}.
(4)
For example, (x,y)=(3/5,4/5) has t=1/2 and u=2. The two connected components of the overlap, the upper and lower arcs, correspond to the positive and negative parts of the punctured real line. The values t=0 and u=0 represent E and W, respectively, and belong to only one chart each. The reciprocal coordinate change and its inverse function are smooth functions away from zero, so this topological atlas also supplies a smooth atlas.
Why can neither chart be enlarged to a single coordinate chart on the whole circle? The circle is a closed set and a bounded set in the Euclidean plane, hence compact by the Heine-Borel theorem. A global coordinate chart would give a homeomorphism to a nonempty open set O⊆R. Its image would be compact and therefore closed in the real line. By connectedness of the real line, this forces O=R, which is not compact. This is the one-dimensional instance of no compact manifold has a single Euclidean chart. Locally the circle is indistinguishable from the real line; globally compactness obstructs a single chart.

Topological manifold with boundary (Hn={x∈Rn:xn​≥0})

Words: 219 Articles: 1
Topological manifold OurBigBook.com
For n≥1, a topological n-manifold with boundary is a Hausdorff second-countable space locally homeomorphic to relatively open sets in the half-space Hn. The openness here is in the subspace topology of Hn, not necessarily in Rn. Its intrinsic boundary ∂M consists of points sent to xn​=0; the remaining points have ordinary coordinate charts with images open in Euclidean space. The intrinsic boundary is independent of the chosen chart.[ref]
The closed interval [0,1] illustrates the distinction: its interior points have open interval charts, while 0 and 1 have charts modeled on [0,ε)⊆H1, using x and 1−x. Its intrinsic boundary is {0,1}. The circle has empty intrinsic boundary even though its boundary of a set as a subset of the Euclidean plane is the whole circle. A topological manifold without boundary is therefore a special case with ∂M=∅, and a smooth atlas on the half-space charts gives a manifold with boundary in the smooth sense.

Manifold boundary (∂M)

Words: 64
Topological manifold with boundary OurBigBook.com
The manifold boundary of a topological manifold with boundary consists of the points which, in a half-space coordinate chart, have last coordinate zero. These are precisely the points without a neighbourhood homeomorphic to an open set in the full Euclidean space of the same dimension. It is an intrinsic property, independent of the coordinate chart. It differs from the boundary of a set in an ambient topological space: a circle has empty manifold boundary, but its boundary of a set in the Euclidean plane is the entire circle.

Category of topological manifolds

Words: 94
Topological manifold OurBigBook.com
The category of topological manifolds has topological manifolds without boundary as its objects of a category and all continuous maps between them as its categorical morphisms. Allowing every dimension of a manifold lets maps connect spaces of different dimensions. Identity functions are continuous, and function composition preserves continuity, so the category laws are inherited from functions. Its isomorphisms are exactly homeomorphisms.
In coordinate charts (U,φ) on M and (V,ψ) on N, a continuous map f:M→N is represented locally by ψ∘f∘φ−1 on φ(U∩f−1(V)). Changing coordinate charts changes this expression through topological transition maps, while f itself stays fixed. This viewpoint separates the underlying continuous map from the coordinates used to describe it; restricting to smooth manifolds and smooth maps gives the corresponding smooth category.

Gluing three-balls along a boundary disk

Words: 52
Piecewise linear ball OurBigBook.com
Two piecewise linear balls of dimension three glued along a common boundary disk form another piecewise linear ball. The Schoenflies theorem identifies each boundary pair with a sphere and a hemisphere. Coning the resulting boundary homeomorphisms extends them over the balls, which then become the two halves of a standard ball. The same argument shows that removing a ball attached along one boundary disk leaves a ball.

Fiber sum of four-manifolds (X#C​Y)

Words: 110 Articles: 1
Smooth four-manifold OurBigBook.com
Remove tubular neighborhoods of diffeomorphic embedded oriented surfaces with opposite normal Euler numbers, and glue their boundary circle bundles by a fiber-reversing identification. The resulting smooth manifold is a fiber sum. The square-zero case uses trivial normal bundles. Additional symplectic hypotheses give a symplectic fiber sum along a square-zero surface.
Smooth fiber sum along unknotted null-homologous spheres (X#S2​Y≅X#Y#(S1×S3))
Words: 70

For standard unknotted spheres contained in four-balls in X and Y, the standard fiber-reversing gluing gives the displayed connected sum. The complement of an unknotted S2×D2 in S4 is S1×D3, and its double is S1×S3. The portions of X and Y outside the four-balls remain as connected summands. Taking both ambient manifolds to be the Complex projective plane gives a smooth fiber sum with no symplectic form, by the symplectic connected-sum obstruction.

Double of a manifold (D(W)=W∪∂W​W)

Words: 54
Topological manifold OurBigBook.com
Glue two copies of a compact manifold along their boundaries to form its double. A collar gives the double a smooth structure when W is smooth. Folding the copies onto W gives a retraction; in particular, inclusion of either copy is injective on homology. For an oriented manifold, use the opposite orientation on the second copy.

Topological tripod

Words: 388
Topological manifold OurBigBook.com
A topological tripod is three copies of the closed interval [0,1] with their zero endpoints identified to a single junction o. Equivalently, embed three line segments with distinct directions in the Euclidean plane and use the subspace topology. It is a compact, connected metric space, and is Hausdorff, second countable, and paracompact. Every interior arm point has a coordinate chart to an open interval, and each outer endpoint has a half-interval chart as in a topological manifold with boundary. The junction has neither kind of chart.
Here is a precise local obstruction. Let S be a small open neighbourhood of o consisting of initial segments of the three arms, excluding their outer endpoints. Every connected open neighbourhood N of o contained in S contains an initial segment on every arm, because N is open at o. Its intersection with each arm has no gaps: if a point between o and a point of N were missing, cutting there would separate the part beyond it from the rest of N, contradicting connectedness. Thus N∖{o} has exactly three connected components, one interval per arm.
Suppose a coordinate chart h to the real line existed at o. Choose a small open interval I about h(o) lying inside h(S∩domain(h)), and restrict the chart to N=h−1(I). This is a connected open neighbourhood of o contained in S. Removing o leaves three connected components in N, while removing h(o) leaves two in I. A homeomorphism preserves connected components, so this is impossible. This restriction step is essential: the count is proved for any sufficiently small connected chart neighborhood, not merely one chosen drawing.
A higher dimension of a manifold cannot repair the junction. For n≥2, repeat the argument with an open ball about h(o): a punctured open ball in Rn is path-connected, whereas N∖{o} still has three connected components. A zero-dimensional chart would make o an isolated point, which it is not. A chart at a boundary point of a topological manifold with boundary similarly gives a punctured relative half-ball: one connected component when n=1, and a path-connected space when n≥2. Consequently the topological tripod fails the local definition even when intrinsic boundary is allowed.
The circle provides a useful warning about scale: deleting a point from the whole circle leaves a connected space, but deleting it from a sufficiently small connected arc leaves two connected components. Local chart tests use the latter. The circle and interval are not homeomorphic article uses deletion from the whole spaces for a different, global obstruction.

Invariance of domain

Words: 43
Topological manifold OurBigBook.com
Invariance of domain says that a continuous map that is an injective function between topological manifolds without boundary of the same dimension is an open map. It is useful in the Penrose singularity theorem: projecting a compact achronal boundary along timelike curves onto a Cauchy hypersurface gives a nonempty subset that is both open and closed, forcing that connected Cauchy hypersurface to be compact.

Lorentzian manifold ((−,+,…,+))

Topological manifold OurBigBook.com
A smooth manifold carrying a nondegenerate bilinear form on each tangent space, with one negative and all remaining positive eigenvalues, or the overall reversed-sign convention. Its nonzero null vectors have vanishing metric quadratic form.

Metric signature ((−,+,+,+))

Words: 47
Lorentzian manifold OurBigBook.com
The metric signature records the signs of the diagonalized nondegenerate metric tensor, equivalently its numbers of negative and positive directions. For a four-dimensional Lorentzian manifold, both (−,+,+,+) and its overall negative convention are common. The numerical signs of contractions and component Hodge star operator identities must be consistent with that choice and the orientation.

Spacelike submanifold

Words: 47
Lorentzian manifold OurBigBook.com
In metric signature (−,+,…,+), a spacelike submanifold of a Lorentzian manifold has an induced metric tensor defining a positive-definite quadratic form. With the overall reversed metric signature, its induced metric tensor instead has only negative eigenvalues. The definition applies to any dimension: enclosing charge-integration surfaces in four dimensions are two-dimensional examples, whereas a spacelike Cauchy hypersurface in four dimensions is three-dimensional.

A handle decomposition builds an n-dimensional manifold by successively adjoining Dh×Dn−h along Sh−1×Dn−h. The attaching data comprise an attaching sphere and its framing of an embedded sphere. Critical level handle embedding

Words: 59
Handle decomposition OurBigBook.com
In a critical level handle embedding, the handles occur at distinct heights in an ambient product with the real line. Between consecutive heights, the boundary of the already attached handles travels through a product collar neighbourhood. At a handle height the horizontal slice contains the handle, attached to the incoming boundary along its attaching region. This separates the local topology changes from the intervening product regions.

Zero-framed Hopf-link handle decomposition of the sphere product (S2×S2=h0∪h2∪h2∪h4)

Words: 82
Handle decomposition OurBigBook.com
Write each sphere as two disks, S2=D−2​∪D+2​. In their product, D−2​×D−2​ is a zero-handle; D+2​×D−2​ and D−2​×D+2​ are two-handles. Their attaching circles are the core circles of the complementary solid tori in ∂(D2×D2), hence a Hopf link. Constant normal directions from the disk factors give both components the zero Seifert framing. The remaining D+2​×D+2​ is a four-handle. This product construction establishes the actual diffeomorphism type, rather than relying on its intersection form alone. The resulting intersection matrix is (01​10​).

Commutator handlebody of the torus (T2×D2)

Words: 98
Handle decomposition OurBigBook.com
Attach two one-handles to a four-dimensional zero-handle, then a two-handle along the commutator of their generators with the product zero framing. The result is T2×D2. Its spine is the usual cell structure of the torus: one vertex, two one-cells, and a two-cell attached by xyx−1y−1. An embedded representative of its H2​ generator is obtained by taking a once-punctured torus carried by the two one-handles and capping its boundary by the core disk of the two-handle. It meets the relative co-core once and has self-intersection zero. The boundary is T3, whose integral homology has ranks 1,3,3,1.

Handle slide

A handle slide replaces the framed attaching sphere of one handle by a band sum with a parallel copy of another handle of the same index. It changes the attaching data while preserving the diffeomorphism type of the resulting manifold. On the handle incidence matrix it performs an elementary row operation, or a column operation when applied to the dual decomposition.
Handle-slide isolation of a cancelling pair ((δ0​0D−cb/δ​))
Words: 83
Handle slide OurBigBook.com
In the intermediate-index range, if an attaching sphere A1​ meets a belt sphere B1​ at exactly one transverse point, handle slides can remove the intersections of every other attaching sphere with B1​. The dual slides then remove the intersections of A1​ with the other belt spheres. Each move uses a band to the unique pivot sheet and a local isotopy removing the old and new intersections. If mji​=Aj​⋅Bi​ and δ=m11​=±1, the remaining incidence block is mji​−mj1​m1i​/δ, the Schur complement of the pivot. Extreme-index cases need separate hypotheses.

Normal surface

3-manifold OurBigBook.com
Relative to a triangulation, a normal surface meets every tetrahedron in disjoint normal disks: four types of triangles cutting off vertices and three types of quadrilaterals separating opposite pairs of edges. A normal disk meets any edge at most once; its face arcs join distinct edges. Two different quadrilateral types cannot coexist in a tetrahedron.

Normal surface coordinates (x∈Z≥07t​)

For t tetrahedra, count the seven disk types in each tetrahedron. Across each glued face the counts of each of its three normal arc types must agree. These are homogeneous linear equations with integer coefficients. An admissible solution also has at most one nonzero quadrilateral type in each tetrahedron. Matching the arcs in their boundary order reconstructs the normal surface up to an isotopy preserving the tetrahedra.

Generalized Heegaard diagram
Words: 37
Heegaard diagram OurBigBook.com
A generalized Heegaard diagram permits compression bodies and a nonempty negative boundary. Equivalently, a suitable handlebody with two-handles attached along only part of a compressing system can retain torus boundary components. Disk crossings supply generators and attaching-curve words supply relators for its fundamental group.

Homology above the dimension of a manifold

Topological manifold OurBigBook.com
An n-manifold has zero singular homology in every degree greater than n. One proof uses a countable exhaustion by subsets that retract onto n-dimensional CW complexes and the compact support of every singular cycle.
