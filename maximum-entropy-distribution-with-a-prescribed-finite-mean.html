For a finite list of real values yi​, maximize information entropy H=−∑i​pi​logpi​ with pi​>0, ∑i​pi​=1, and ∑i​pi​yi​=Y. Lagrange multipliers give
pi​=Z(b)e−byi​​,Z(b)=∑j​e−byj​,Y=−dbd​logZ(b).

The constrained second variation is −∑i​(δpi​)2/pi​<0 for any nonzero tangent perturbation, proving uniqueness of the entropy maximum whenever a feasible interior solution exists. The mean has derivative −Varb​(y), so for nonconstant yi​ it decreases strictly with b and ranges between the largest and smallest yi​ as b ranges over the real line. Thus a finite b exists uniquely exactly when mini​yi​<Y<maxi​yi​.
At an extreme mean, strictly positive probabilities are impossible unless all yi​ coincide. If all yi​=y0​, the mean constraint is redundant when Y=y0​, the maximum is uniform, and b is not identifiable. This finite exponential family is an exponential tilting of the uniform distribution.
