U-tube differential manometer (Δp=pL​−pR​=ρg(zR​−zL​))

 Home Physics Branches of physics Fluid mechanics Manometer
OurBigBook.com Words: 990 Articles: 2
A U-tube differential manometer contains a connected column of liquid with one gas connection above each liquid meniscus. Write pL​,pR​ for the left and right pressures, and zL​,zR​ for the meniscus heights on an upward vertical coordinate. Assume constant mass density ρ and downward gravitational acceleration of magnitude g; neglect the gas column's mass density and differences in capillary pressure. The signed vertical length difference Δh=zR​−zL​ then gives
Δp=pL​−pR​=ρgΔh.
(3)
Larger left pressure means a lower left liquid meniscus: pL​>pR​ implies zL​<zR​. Equal pressures give equal meniscus heights.
The balance follows directly from hydrostatic pressure. At a common height z0​ below both surfaces, the two points in the connected homogeneous liquid have equal pressure:
pL​+ρg(zL​−z0​)=pR​+ρg(zR​−z0​).
(4)
Subtracting cancels the common bottom depth and leaves pL​−pR​=ρg(zR​−zL​). The same-height rule follows from hydrostatic equilibrium, ∇p=−ρgez​: a horizontal displacement changes no pressure, and for constant ρ the quantity p+ρgz is constant throughout the connected liquid. Equal height alone is insufficient for unrelated vessels or for fluid flow with significant acceleration.
For an original constructed example, choose water of stipulated mass density ρ=1000kgm−3, gravitational acceleration g=9.81ms−2, and vertical length difference Δh=0.060m. Then
Δp=1000×9.81×0.060=588.6Pa.
(5)
The SI pressure unit is the pascal: the product has units kgm−3×ms−2×m=kgm−1s−2=Pa. Swapping the two connections gives −588.6Pa and reverses which liquid meniscus is lower. In equal-bore arms, volume conservation makes each surface move 30mm from its initial equal-height position; using only one surface's displacement would halve the inferred pressure. With unequal arm areas AL​,AR​, a left fall a and right rise b instead obey AL​a=AR​b, while the vertical length difference is still a+b.
If the right port is open to the local atmosphere, pL​−pR​ is the left gauge pressure. Only with that reference and known atmospheric pressure can one obtain absolute pressure from
pL,abs​=patmosphere​+pL,gauge​.
(6)
For an explicitly stipulated atmospheric pressure of 100000Pa, the example gives 100588.6Pa absolute pressure. If the right port is connected to another vessel or a pressure tapping, the same reading is a differential pressure and supplies no absolute pressure without knowing that reference.[ref]
