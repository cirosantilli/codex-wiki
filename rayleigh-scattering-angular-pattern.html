Rayleigh scattering angular pattern (F(θ)=(1+cos2θ)/2)

 Home Physics Branches of physics Electromagnetism Electromagnetic radiation Rayleigh scattering
OurBigBook.com Words: 220
For incident unpolarized light and the isotropic electric dipole response of Rayleigh scattering by a small sphere, let θ be the angle between the incident beam's propagation direction and the outgoing direction. The differential scattering cross-section and its relative angular pattern are
dΩdσ​=16π3σ​(1+cos2θ),F(θ)=21+cos2θ​,
(52)
where dΩ is an element of solid angle. Averaging over the two transverse directions of incident polarization of an electromagnetic wave gives this pattern. Forward scattering at 0∘ and backward scattering at 180∘ are equally strong: F=1. Sideways scattering at 90∘ is weakest, but nonzero: F=1/2. [ref].
The normalization is consistent because
∫(1+cos2θ)dΩ=2π∫0π​(1+cos2θ)sinθdθ=316π​.
(53)
Thus integrating dσ/dΩ gives the total Rayleigh scattering cross-section σ. The plotted F has maximum one; it is not itself normalized to unit solid angle integral.
For a single electric dipole driven by incident linear polarization, the angular distribution of electric dipole radiation instead has a sin2α factor, where α is measured from the electric dipole moment direction, parallel to the incident electric field. That direction is perpendicular to the incident beam. Confusing α with the beam angle θ would incorrectly predict zero forward scattering. Averaging the dipole patterns over incident unpolarized light yields the displayed F(θ).
