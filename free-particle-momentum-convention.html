Free-particle momentum convention (ψ=Aei(k⋅r−ωt),p=ℏk)

 ... Physics Branches of physics Quantum mechanics Wave function Matter wave de Broglie wavelength
OurBigBook.com Words: 166
For a free particle of mass m>0 in nonrelativistic quantum mechanics, choose the potential energy zero and write
ψ(r,t)=Aexp[ℏi​(p⋅r−Et)],E=2m∣p∣2​,ω=ℏE​.
(92)
Applying the momentum operator −iℏ∇ returns pψ; this fixes the sign convention. The angular frequency is measured relative to this chosen energy zero. Adding a constant energy changes only a time-dependent global phase, leaving the spatial de Broglie wavelength unchanged. No rest-energy term is included in this nonrelativistic quantum mechanics convention.
The plane wave has ∣ψ∣2=∣A∣2 and probability current j=(p/m)∣A∣2. Its real part can be drawn as a sine or cosine, but that graph is a component of a probability amplitude, not a transverse displacement. On infinite space this is a generalized momentum eigenstate, not a state satisfying wavefunction normalization. A narrow wave packet instead moves with group velocity ∇k​ω=p/m; the phase velocity ω/∣k∣=∣p∣/(2m) is not its transport speed.
The equality of canonical momentum and kinetic momentum here assumes a field-free region with the magnetic vector potential chosen zero. For a charged particle in a magnetic field, the kinetic momentum is Π=p−qA. An arbitrary canonical phase gradient must not then be substituted into mv; the free particle formula needs its stated assumptions.
