Expectation-maximization algorithm

 ... Mathematics Area of mathematics Probability and statistics Statistical model Statistical modelling Latent variable
OurBigBook.com Words: 1k Articles: 10
The expectation-maximization algorithm fits a latent-variable model by alternating a conditional expectation over the latent variables with a maximization over the statistical parameters. Its target is the observed-data likelihood function, although its updates use the simpler complete-data log-likelihood.[ref]
Write y for the observations, z for the latent variables, and pθ​(y,z) for their joint probability density function or probability mass function. The observed-data log-likelihood is
ℓ(θ)=logpθ​(y),pθ​(y)=∫pθ​(y,z)dz,
(2)
where the integral becomes a sum for discrete z. Directly maximizing a logarithm of this sum can be awkward. The E-step instead computes a conditional distribution at the current statistical parameters θ(t); the M-step uses it to construct a new fit. The attractive idea is to retain uncertainty about the hidden labels while recovering simple weighted means and other complete-data updates. The Known-variance Gaussian mixture EM example shows the mechanism without estimating a variance or mixture weight.
Arthur Pentland Dempster, Nan M. Laird, and Donald B. Rubin gave the general framework its name in their 1977 paper, which also discusses earlier special-case algorithms.[ref]
