Compactly supported linear trial for hydrogen (E(β)=5/β2−5/(2β),E(4)=−5/16)

OurBigBook.com Words: 101
In atomic units, use the radial trial wavefunction ψ(r)=max(1−r/β,0) with β>0. Its radial norm integral is ∫r2ψ2dr=β3/30, its gradient contribution is ∫r2ψ′2dr=β/3, and its Coulomb contribution is −2∫rψ2dr=−β2/6. In the hydrogen radial Rayleigh quotient, this gives the displayed energy, minimized at β=4. It is above the exact hydrogen ground-state energy −1/2, as required by the quantum variational principle. The derivative jumps at r=β, but the function has a square-integrable weak first derivative and is an admissible trial for the energy quadratic form. One must use that form rather than assume that a classical second derivative exists at the join.
