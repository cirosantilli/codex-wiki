Rutherford differential cross-section (dΩdσ​=16E2κ2​csc42θ​)

 Home Physics Atomic physics Rutherford scattering
OurBigBook.com Words: 208 Articles: 1
For an incident beam uniform across its relevant impact parameters, an incident annulus has area dσ=2πb∣db∣. Rotational symmetry maps it to outgoing solid angle dΩ=2πsinθdθ. The central-potential classical differential cross-section and the Rutherford impact-parameter relation therefore give
​dθdb​​=2a​csc22θ​,dΩdσ​=sinθb​​dθdb​​=4a2​csc42θ​=16E2κ2​csc42θ​​.
(68)
This is a differential scattering cross-section, with units of area per steradian, conventionally m2sr−1, not a detector count and not an area per degree. It applies for 0<θ<π, with a finite limiting value at π. Doubling incident kinetic energy divides it by four at fixed angle; doubling either electric charge number multiplies it by four.
A useful independent check integrates over all azimuths between two positive angles. For 0<θ1​<θ2​≤π,
σ[θ1​,θ2​]​=∫θ1​θ2​​dΩdσ​2πsinθdθ=πa2(cot22θ1​​−cot22θ2​​).
(69)
This equals the incident annulus area π(b12​−b22​) directly. A detector covering only a small patch at one angle does not collect this full azimuthal band.
