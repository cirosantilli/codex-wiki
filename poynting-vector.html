Poynting vector (S=E×B/μ0​)

 Home Physics Branches of physics Electromagnetism Electromagnetic wave
OurBigBook.com Words: 1k Articles: 15
In vacuum the Poynting vector
S=μ0​1​E×B
(42)
is the local energy flux, measured in watts per square metre. Its surface integral P=∫A​S⋅ndA gives signed power crossing a surface with unit normal vector n. Positive flux follows the chosen normal; for a closed surface the usual convention is outward. Power depends on the normal component and area, rather than just the field magnitude.
The Poynting vector applies to steady fields as well as electromagnetic waves. In a driven resistive electrical circuit, surrounding fields can carry energy into the conductor, where electrical work becomes Joule heating. This turns a circuit-level power balance into a spatial description of energy delivery. [ref].
