OurBigBook.com Words: 144
Let T=Th(C) in the finite language of rings: it is the set of all first-order sentences true in the field of complex numbers. It is satisfiable, with first-order model C, so the countable-language Löwenheim-Skolem theorem gives an at most countable first-order model K of T.
All field axioms belong to T, hence K is a field. For every positive integer n, the first-order sentence saying that there are at least n distinct elements is also true in C and belongs to T. Thus K cannot be finite, and is countably infinite.
Finally, for each first-order sentence σ, either σ∈T or ¬σ∈T. Since K satisfies all of T, it satisfies σ exactly when C does. Therefore
∣K∣=ℵ0​,K≡C.​
(25)
Elementary equivalence concerns first-order first-order sentences and does not imply an isomorphism; the two fields have different cardinalities. The stronger downward form may even choose K as a countable elementary subfield of C.
