By the natural-number recursion theorem, let α0​=0 and αn+1​=F(αn​). These ordinals form a nondecreasing sequence by the preceding bound. If αn+1​=αn​ for some n, it is already a fixed point.
Otherwise the sequence is strictly increasing. Its supremum λ=⋃n<ω​αn​ is a limit ordinal, and the sequence is cofinal in it. Continuity and monotonicity give
F(λ)=supβ<λ​F(β)=supn<ω​F(αn​)=supn<ω​αn+1​=λ.
(24)
For the middle equality, every β<λ is below some αn​, while every αn​<λ. Therefore F has a fixed point. The sequence is a set by Axiom schema of replacement, since F is a definable set-theoretic class function.
