For this calculus the deduction theorem for propositional logic is
Γ∪{α}⊢β⟺Γ⊢α→β.​
(12)
For the reverse logical implication, retain the logical derivation from Γ, add the assumption α, and use modus ponens rule.
For the forward logical implication, induct on a logical derivation from Γ∪{α}. For each line δ we construct a logical derivation of α→δ from Γ. If δ=α, use (ii). If δ is an axiom instance or belongs to Γ, it is already derivable from Γ; L1 gives δ→(α→δ), and modus ponens rule finishes that case.
If δ comes from η and η→δ by modus ponens rule, the induction hypothesis supplies
Γ⊢α→η,Γ⊢α→(η→δ).
(13)
Part (iii) supplies Γ⊢α→δ.
If δ comes by the elimination rule E from η→δ and ¬η→δ, the induction hypothesis supplies
Γ⊢α→(η→δ),Γ⊢α→(¬η→δ).
(14)
The L2 instance with (α,β,γ)=(α,η,δ) combines these by two applications of modus ponens rule. Thus the rule E also survives the discharge construction. These are all possible lines, proving the theorem for the final line β.
