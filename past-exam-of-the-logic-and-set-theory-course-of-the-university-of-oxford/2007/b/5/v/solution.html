Fix m and induct on n. The initial equality m+0=0+m follows from the defining zero clause and (ii). Assuming m+n=n+m, the successor clause and (iii) give
m+n+=(m+n)+=(n+m)+=n+m+=n++m.
(39)
This proves
m+n=n+m(m,n∈ω).​
(40)
The extension to arbitrary ordinals is false. By the limit clause for ordinal addition,
1+ω=supn<ω​(1+n)=ω,
(41)
whereas ω+1 is the successor ordinal of ω and is strictly greater than it. Therefore
1+ω=ω+1.​
(42)
Finite addition is commutative; ordinal addition records the order of concatenated well-orders and need not be commutative.
