Irrationality of 2​ makes the expression q+r2​ unique for rational q,r: equality of two such expressions with different r would express 2​ as a rational number. Therefore the rule selecting the coefficient r defines a function on A. Its domain is a vector subspace over Q, and direct coefficient addition and rational scaling show it is Q-linear, hence weakly linear. In particular
f(1)=0,f(2​)=1.
(62)
Use Zorn's lemma on the nonempty family of weakly linear extensions of this particular function. Chain unions remain extensions by (a); hence there is a maximal extension h. Any further extension in F would also contain the original function, so this h is maximal in F. Part (b) therefore gives domh=R.
A real-linear map R→R is multiplication by its value at 1. Were h real-linear, it would satisfy
h(2​)=2​h(1)=0,
(63)
contradicting the inherited value h(2​)=1. Thus a weakly linear function on all of R need not be real-linear. Its rational homogeneity of an additive function is insufficient for real homogeneity. This particular extension is also discontinuous: rational numbers approaching 2​ have value zero, while the value at their limit is one.
