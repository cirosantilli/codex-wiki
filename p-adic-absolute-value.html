p-adic absolute value (∣⋅∣p​)

 Home Mathematics Area of mathematics Arithmetic Non-Archimedean analysis P-adic number
OurBigBook.com Words: 549 Articles: 6
Fix a prime number p. Write a nonzero rational number as x=pka/b, where k∈Z and the nonzero integers a,b are not divisible by p. The exponent k is uniquely determined. Its P-adic valuation is this signed exponent vp​(x)=k: equivalently, for any nonzero integer numerator A and denominator B,
vp​(A/B)=vp​(A)−vp​(B),∣x∣p​=p−vp​(x),∣0∣p​=0.
(18)
Factors of p in the numerator decrease the p-adic absolute value; factors in the denominator increase it. For p=2 the computations are:

This is an absolute value on a field measuring divisibility, rather than the magnitude on a real number number line. It satisfies
∣xy∣p​=∣x∣p​∣y∣p​,∣x+y∣p​≤max{∣x∣p​,∣y∣p​}.
(19)
The proof comes directly from P-adic valuations: exponents add in a product of numbers, and extracting a common power of p from a sum gives vp​(x+y)≥min{vp​(x),vp​(y)}. If the two P-adic valuations differ, the remaining term with smaller exponent cannot cancel modulo p, so equality holds. Thus the p-adic absolute value is a Non-Archimedean absolute value. [ref]
The metric dp​(x,y)=∣x−y∣p​ makes two rational numbers close when their difference contains a high power of p. This packages successive modular congruences into a numerical error estimate, allowing convergence in a metric space and convergent series to encode exact arithmetic information.
