Switch on a constant electric field E0​ at t=0. For prescribed initial current density J(0), the exact Drude model solution is the displayed formula. For Electrons, the corresponding drift velocity is
vd​(t)=−meτ​E0​+[vd​(0)+meτ​E0​]e−t/τ.
(83)
Starting from zero drift velocity, the current density reaches 1−e−1≈0.632 of its limiting value after one momentum relaxation time. Switching the electric field off at T instead gives J(t)=J(T)e−(t−T)/τ for t≥T. A finite step of electric field changes the acceleration immediately but leaves drift velocity and current density continuous.
More generally, multiplying the current density equation by an integrating factor gives
J(t)=e−(t−t0​)/τJ(t0​)+mnq2​∫t0​t​e−(t−s)/τE(s)ds.
(84)
This is the causal response of a first-order relaxation equation: only earlier electric fields enter the integral. It also shows why instantaneous Ohm's law is an approximation when the electric field changes appreciably during a momentum relaxation time.
