Hemocytometer

 Home Biology Biological cell Cell line Cell counting
OurBigBook.com Words: 239
A hemocytometer has a calibrated grid and depth. ATCC describes 1mm2 squares 0.1mm deep, each sampling 10−4mL.[ref]
An original constructed hemocytometer count uses four such squares after mixing the biological cell preparation with the same volume of stain. The dye-excluding counts are 36,44,40,40; the dye-stained counts are 4,6,5,5. The dilution factor is D=2. With v the sampled volume per square and nˉ the arithmetic mean count per square,
v=(1mm2)(0.1mm)=10−4mL,c=Dvnˉ​.
(4)
Hence the undiluted preparation has dye-excluding number density 2(40)/10−4=800000mL−1 and total number density 2(45)/10−4=900000mL−1. Its dye-excluding fraction is 160/180=8/9≈88.9%. If the well-mixed preparation contains 3mL, it contains an estimated 2400000 dye-excluding biological cells. The chamber sample itself contains only 160 counted dye-excluding biological cells.
The relation c=Dnˉ/v follows from counting material in a known volume, then undoing dilution. It assumes a representative mixed sample and correctly identified single biological cells. Clumps, inconsistent decisions at grid edges, wrong chamber depth and nonrepresentative sampling introduce measurement error. A fixed convention such as including top and left edges while excluding bottom and right edges assigns shared-edge centers to just one square. Adding ten times as much suspension to a correctly filled chamber does not make each square's calibrated volume ten times larger.
