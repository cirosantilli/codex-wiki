Periodic Poisson equation on a four-point cycle (2xn​−xn−1​−xn+1​=bn​)

OurBigBook.com Words: 184
Consider the discrete linear system Lx=b with
L=​2−10−1​−12−10​0−12−1​−10−12​​,b=(1,0,−1,0)T.
(82)
This is a finite periodic analogue of the Poisson equation, using the negative second finite difference with unit grid spacing. It is also a Graph Laplacian equation on a cycle graph; no continuum approximation is being claimed here.
Summing the four equations gives zero on the left, since each value occurs twice positively and twice negatively. A necessary condition is therefore ∑n​bn​=0. The Fourier diagonalization of a circulant matrix shows that this is also sufficient: the only zero eigenvalue of L is the constant mode k=0, and b0​=∑n​bn​.
For the displayed forcing, the discrete Fourier transform gives
b=(0,2,0,2)T,(λ0​,λ1​,λ2​,λ3​)=(0,2,4,2).
(83)
Divide only at the three nonzero eigenvalues, and choose x0​=0 to impose zero arithmetic mean:
x=(0,1,0,1)T,x=(1/2,0,−1/2,0)T.​
(84)
Direct substitution gives Lx=(1,0,−1,0)T. All other vectors satisfying the linear system are x+a(1,1,1,1)T, because the kernel is the space of constant vectors. Thus balanced forcing determines the differences between values, while a reference level is an extra choice. This small model explains why a periodic Poisson equation needs both a compatibility condition and a normalization.
