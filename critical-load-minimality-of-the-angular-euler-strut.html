Critical-load minimality of the angular Euler strut (λ=1:U[θ]>U[0] for θ=0,δ2U[0] is degenerate)

For the angular endpoint conditions θ(0)=θ(1)=0, take U=(EI/L)∫01​[θ′2/2−π2λ(1−cosθ)]dξ. The Poincaré inequality and 1−cost≤t2/2 give U≥0 for 0≤λ≤1. At λ=1 a nonzero continuous angle still has strictly positive energy because 1−cost<t2/2 for t=0. Thus the straight state remains the unique global minimum at the critical parameter even though the quadratic variation vanishes on the first sine mode.
Along that mode the energy is π2EIA4/(64L)+O(A6) at criticality. For λ>1 a sufficiently small first-mode variation lowers energy, so the straight state loses local minimality. Near the first threshold, expansion of the Euler strut equation gives A0​(A02​−8λ1​)=0 when λ=1+ϵλ1​ and θ∼ϵ​A0​sinπξ. The resulting supercritical pitchfork bifurcation is stabilized by the same positive quartic term. A zero second variation is therefore an inconclusive quadratic test, not proof of failure of minimization.
