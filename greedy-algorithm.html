Greedy algorithm

 Home Computer science Algorithm
OurBigBook.com Words: 41
A greedy algorithm commits to a locally preferred next choice without revisiting earlier choices. Its rule needs a justification to guarantee a globally optimal result: the largest-individual-value rule in the 0-1 knapsack problem example fails, whereas Dijkstra's algorithm correctly settles the smallest tentative distance under its nonnegative-edge assumptions.
