Mixed state

 Home Physics Branches of physics Quantum theory Density matrix
OurBigBook.com Words: 210
A mixed state is a density operator that is not a rank-one orthogonal projection. In finite dimension it is characterized by Tr(ρ2)<1, so its purity of a density operator distinguishes it from a pure state.
Compare a pure state quantum superposition with an equal classical choice between two computational-basis states of a qubit:
∣+⟩=2​∣0⟩+∣1⟩​,ρ+​=∣+⟩⟨+∣=21​(11​11​),ρmix​=21​∣0⟩⟨0∣+21​∣1⟩⟨1∣=2I​.
(5)
A Z projective measurement in the computational basis gives P(0)=P(1)=1/2 for both density operators. An X projective measurement in the Hadamard basis ∣±⟩=(∣0⟩±∣1⟩)/2​ distinguishes them: the Born rule gives P(+)=1 for ρ+​ and P(+)=1/2 for ρmix​. These comparisons use separate copies of each preparation; measuring Z first changes the state tested by a subsequent X measurement.
The off-diagonal entries of ρ+​ encode quantum coherence in the computational basis. Such quantum coherence depends on the basis: ρ+​ is diagonal in the Hadamard basis but remains a pure state. Mixedness is independent of the basis, because a unitary change of basis preserves the eigenvalues and the purity of a density operator.
Even the quantum state ensemble is not unique:
2I​=21​∣0⟩⟨0∣+21​∣1⟩⟨1∣=21​∣+⟩⟨+∣+21​∣−⟩⟨−∣.
(6)
The off-diagonal entries of the two Hadamard basis projectors cancel. Thus the density operator retains precisely what measurements on the system can reveal, while an accessible preparation label carries additional information. A mixed state can also be the reduced density matrix of an entangled state, even when the whole system is in a pure state; classical ignorance about a selected component is not its only physical origin.
