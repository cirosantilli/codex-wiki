For a random sample Xi​∣θ∼N(θ,σ2) with known σ>0 and prior distribution θ∼N(0,τ2) with τ>0, compare H0​:θ<0 to H1​:θ≥0. The prior odds are one by symmetry. Normal-normal conjugacy gives a normal posterior distribution with
μn​=σ2+nτ2nτ2X​,vn​=σ2+nτ2σ2τ2​.
(39)
Its negative and nonnegative masses are Φ(−z) and Φ(z), where
z=vn​​μn​​=σσ2+nτ2​nτX​.
(40)
Since the Bayes factor is the posterior odds divided by the prior odds,
B01​=Φ(z)Φ(−z)​.​
(41)
Here Φ is the cumulative distribution function of the standard normal distribution. The ratio decreases with the sample mean. For any threshold b>0, the inequality B01​<b is equivalent to Φ(z)>1/(1+b) and hence to
X>nτσσ2+nτ2​​Φ−1(1+b1​).
(42)
For b=1/10, the quantile is Φ−1(10/11)≈1.33518, and the equivalent posterior probability threshold for H0​ is 1/11. The symmetry and proper normalization of the two hypothesis-conditional priors are essential to replacing the Bayes factor by the posterior odds directly.
