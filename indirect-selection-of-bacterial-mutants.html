Indirect selection of bacterial mutants

 Home Biology Genetics Molecular genetics Replica plating
OurBigBook.com Words: 399
Indirect selection of bacterial mutants recovers a source using a related sample's tested phenotype, rather than directly challenging the retained lineage. A positive example uses a surviving replica biological colony to locate its unexposed source. A negative example uses failure to grow in one condition to locate a source that does grow elsewhere. The second is particularly useful for finding auxotrophy: dependence on an externally supplied nutrient.
Construct a four-by-four teaching layout with rows A–D and columns 1–4. The master has 13 biological colonies; A4, D1 and D4 are empty. A growth-supporting control replica grows at every occupied master site except B2 and C3. A replica lacking an assumed nutrient X grows at those same control sites except A2 and C1. All other requirements are stipulated equal. These are invented readouts, not observations from the Lederberg replica plating experiment.
Let M, C and S be the finite sets of sites with growth on the master, control and X-omission replica respectively. In this construction S⊆C⊆M, with cardinalities
∣M∣=13,∣C∣=11,∣S∣=9.
(5)
The set difference
C∖S={A2,C1}
(6)
contains the two condition-dependent candidates. Pick from A2 and C1 on the master, then compare fresh samples of each recovered source with and without X. Restoration of growth when X is supplied supports an X-requirement phenotype, subject to the scientific controls; growth alone does not identify a particular gene or mutation.
By contrast,
M∖C={B2,C3}
(7)
is unresolved sampling or viability in this first comparison. Neither site should be counted as an X-requirement candidate. The three sites outside M have no master source to recover at all. The short logical argument is membership testing: a candidate must belong to C, establishing control growth, and not belong to S, establishing the contrasting readout. Being outside S alone supplies only the second condition. Among the seven empty X-omission sites, two are candidates, two are unresolved and three have no master source. The beauty of the method is that a shared location turns this logical distinction into a practical retrieval route.
