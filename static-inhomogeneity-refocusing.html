Use the rotating frame and positive gyromagnetic ratio convention of free induction decay. Let Δ=ω0​−ω be each component's fixed detuning, and normalize its magnetization by the equilibrium value. An instantaneous resonant pulse along +u rotates through −π/2 about u, preparing m(0+)=(0,1,0). A second pulse along +v rotates through −π about v. These ideal pulses require a drive much stronger than the relevant detunings and negligible relaxation during either pulse. The corresponding rotation matrices, acting on column vectors, are

Here FΔ​(t) is free Larmor precession in the rotating frame, before including transverse relaxation. Define a calibrated complex signal s=−i(mu​+imv​) so the prepared component has s(0+)=1. The pulse P sends mu​+imv​ to its negative complex conjugate, hence sends s to s∗. Direct multiplication gives
sΔ​(t)={e−t/T2​e−iΔt,e−t/T2​e−iΔ(t−2τ),​0≤t<τ,t>τ.​
(12)
The phase accumulated before the pulse is reflected; the angular velocity during subsequent free evolution keeps its sign. At 2τ every fixed detuning has zero residual phase. Equivalently, PFΔ​(t)P=FΔ​(−t), so FΔ​(τ)PFΔ​(τ)=P. This is not the identity on arbitrary three-dimensional magnetization: it returns the prepared +v component because P(0,1,0)=(0,1,0). The two transverse pulse axes specified here also fix the receiver sign of the echo.
For an original ensemble example, let the static detunings have a Gaussian distribution with mean zero and standard deviation σ, independent of a common homogeneous T2​. Averaging sΔ​ uses the characteristic function of that Gaussian distribution:

Choose σ/(2π)=25Hz, τ=40ms and T2​=160ms. Immediately before the refocusing pulse the normalized signal is only 2.08×10−9, yet S(80ms)=e−1/2=0.60653. A one-pulse free induction decay at 80 ms would instead be about 3.11×10−35. These chosen numbers describe a calculation, not a measured trace. Increasing σ narrows the echo without changing S(2τ) in this ideal model. Because this Gaussian distribution produces a Gaussian factor rather than an exponential decay, its inhomogeneous decay is not characterized by a unique exponential T2∗​.
The exact phase-refocusing time is 2τ. A slowly falling transverse relaxation envelope moves the maximum of this particular echo shape slightly earlier: differentiating logS after the pulse gives tmax​=2τ−1/(σ2T2​), or 79.747ms here. Measuring consistently at 2τ avoids confusing this small envelope effect with a failure of refocusing. A series of such measurements gives logS(2τ)=−2τ/T2​ only when the assumed homogeneous exponential decay, pulse quality and negligible additional attenuation are appropriate.
