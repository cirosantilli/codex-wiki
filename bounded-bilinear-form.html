Bounded bilinear form (∣B(u,v)∣≤C∥u∥∥v∥)

 Home Mathematics Area of mathematics Algebra Linear algebra Bilinear form
OurBigBook.com Words: 619 Articles: 5
A bilinear form B:X×X→R on a real normed vector space is bounded when one constant 0≤C<∞ satisfies
∣B(u,v)∣≤C∥u∥∥v∥(u,v∈X).
(17)
This is an upper bound on the magnitude of every pairing. It says nothing about the sign of B(v,v) and does not require a symmetric bilinear form. The chosen norm is part of the hypothesis. Boundedness is equivalent to joint continuity, as shown by the boundedness criterion for a bilinear form.[ref]
For example, on R2 with the Euclidean norm, the bilinear forms
B−​(u,v)=u1​v1​−u2​v2​,B0​(u,v)=u1​v1​
(18)
both satisfy the upper bound with C=1, by the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality. Yet their quadratic forms are B−​(v,v)=x2−y2 and B0​(v,v)=x2: the former takes negative values and the latter vanishes at the nonzero vector (0,1). Thus neither is a coercive bilinear form. The latter is also a degenerate bilinear form, whereas B−​ is nondegenerate. Boundedness controls size, not positivity or loss of directions.
Every bilinear form on a real or complex finite-dimensional vector space equipped with any norm is bounded. In coordinates, write B(u,v)=uTAv using its matrix of a bilinear form. The Cauchy-Schwarz inequality gives ∣uTAv∣≤∥A∥2​∥u∥2​∥v∥2​, with ∥A∥2​ the spectral norm; finite-dimensional equivalence of norms transfers this to the chosen norm. The conclusion concerns each fixed norm and fixed bilinear form: it does not supply a single constant for an arbitrary family of matrices or changing dimensions.
On an infinite-dimensional vector space, the upper bound is an additional condition. Equip the finitely supported sequences c00​ with the norm inherited from the l2 sequence space and set
B(u,v)=∑n≥1​nun​vn​.
(19)
Each pairing is a finite sum, so this is a well-defined bilinear form. For the basis vector en​, however, ∥en​∥2​=1 and B(en​,en​)=n; no finite C works. It is even a coercive bilinear form with α=1, since ∑nvn2​≥∑vn2​. Consequently coercivity alone does not imply boundedness on a normed vector space. This example uses c00​, which is not a Hilbert space; it does not define this pairing on all of the l2 sequence space.
A bounded bilinear form defines a bounded linear operator T:X→X′ by (Tu)(v)=B(u,v), where X′ is the continuous dual space. Its operator norm is the smallest admissible C:
∥T∥=supu=0,v=0​∥u∥∥v∥∣B(u,v)∣​.
(20)
This converts an upper bound for pairings into an upper bound for a linear map taking an unknown vector to a linear functional.
