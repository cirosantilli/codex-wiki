Greedy perfect-heuristic descent (h∗(sk+1​)<h∗(sk​))

 ... Foundations of mathematics Graph theory Graph Graph search Best-first search Greedy best-first search
OurBigBook.com Words: 109
In a finite reachable search problem with strictly positive step costs and a reachable goal, greedy best-first search using a perfect heuristic selects one parent–child chain until it reaches a goal state. At a selected nonterminal state s, an optimal first transition has h∗(s′)=h∗(s)−c(s,s′)<h∗(s). Every older search frontier entry has estimate at least h∗(s), so the next selected entry must be a newly generated child with smaller estimate. Repeating proves the chain property. In an infinite finitely branching problem, a positive uniform lower bound on costs also bounds the chain length by the initial remaining cost divided by that lower bound. Zero-cost ties can break the chain property; the lemma does not establish least accumulated path cost.
