Atomic nucleus (A=Z+N)

 Home Physics Nuclear physics
OurBigBook.com Words: 7k Articles: 74
The central system of protons and neutrons in an atom. Its mass number A is its baryon number in ordinary nuclear states; Z is the proton number and N the neutron number.
The nucleus contains almost all of an atom's mass and carries positive electric charge Ze, where e is the elementary charge. The atomic number Z identifies the chemical element; changing N at fixed Z gives another isotope. The mass number A=Z+N counts nucleons, rather than specifying the exact nuclear mass: nuclear binding energy lowers the mass of a bound system relative to its separated constituents. The simplest nucleus is a single proton; other atomic nuclei can contain many protons and neutrons bound by the nuclear force.
A nuclear quantum state has properties such as spin and a spatial nuclear charge distribution. These are different questions from merely counting nucleons. A small-angle elastic scattering measurement can reveal a nuclear charge radius, while a wider range of momentum transfer tests the distribution's shape. The Hofstadter nuclear electron scattering experiment made this connection between scattering patterns and nuclear structure experimentally concrete.

Nuclear matrix element (M=⟨f∣O∣i⟩)

Words: 47
Atomic nucleus OurBigBook.com
A transition-operator matrix element between specified nuclear states. It combines their many-nucleon wavefunctions with the relevant interaction operator. In double beta decay, different assumed mechanisms and nuclear-structure calculations give different matrix elements, so converting a half-life bound to a neutrino-mass bound requires that input. A measured energy endpoint alone does not determine it.

Nuclear radius scaling (R≃r0​A1/3)

Words: 62
Atomic nucleus OurBigBook.com
The approximate mass-number dependence of a sharp equivalent nuclear radius. Near-constant interior nucleon number density gives A≃(4π/3)n0​R3, hence R∝A1/3. A conventional matter-radius scale is r0​≃1.2fm, with isotope and definition dependence. A charge-weighted root-mean-square radius is a different observable: for a uniform sphere it is 3/5​R. Diffuse surfaces and neutron skins prevent interpreting the scaling as a universal hard boundary.

Nuclear excited state

Words: 94 Articles: 1
Atomic nucleus OurBigBook.com
A nuclear excited state lies above the same nucleus's ground state in internal energy. spin, parity and approximate isospin help classify it. Single-particle rearrangements, broken pairs and collective deformation/vibration give different excitation patterns and can mix. Below particle-emission thresholds, gamma decay and internal conversion are important de-excitation routes; an excitation energy is not necessarily the energy of every emitted gamma ray in a cascade.

Nuclear isomer

Words: 46
Nuclear excited state OurBigBook.com
A nuclear isomer is a relatively long-lived metastable nuclear state. Gamma de-excitation can be hindered by a large angular-momentum change, high electromagnetic multipolarity or small overlap with lower states. Its lifetime and decay channels depend on the actual level structure; metastability is not an absolute prohibition on decay.

Odd-odd nucleus

Words: 24
Atomic nucleus OurBigBook.com
An atomic nucleus with odd proton number and odd neutron number. Both unpaired species can contribute to the total nuclear spin; addition of angular momentum gives possible couplings, while the nuclear residual interaction determines their ordering.

Nuclear isobars are different nuclides with the same mass number A and different atomic numbers Z. They differ from isotopes, which have fixed Z. Beta decay connects neighboring nuclear isobars.

Nuclear level scheme

Words: 107 Articles: 1
Nuclear structure OurBigBook.com
A nuclear level scheme represents nuclear energy levels and transitions among them. Gamma ray energies constrain pairwise differences; emission counts and coincidence measurements help resolve different schemes having the same difference multiset.

Nuclear level population balance (Fi​=∑j<i​Ci→j​−∑k>i​Ck→i​)

Words: 83
Nuclear level scheme OurBigBook.com
For a complete ensemble of downward nuclear de-excitation cascades, let Fi​ be the direct population of excited level i and Ci→j​ the total number of transitions from i to j. Conservation of population gives the displayed outward-minus-inward formula. A negative Fi​ rules out a proposed scheme under the assumed complete transition inventory. Gamma ray counts equal transition counts only when unreported internal conversion and other competing paths can be neglected or corrected. Summing over all excited levels cancels internal transitions and leaves the transitions into the ground state.

Nuclear de-excitation

Words: 476 Articles: 8
Nuclear structure OurBigBook.com
Nuclear de-excitation is a transition from a higher to a lower nuclear energy level. It can transfer energy to an emitted gamma ray or to an atomic electron through internal conversion, among other channels when thresholds permit.

Internal conversion

Words: 33
Nuclear de-excitation OurBigBook.com
Internal conversion transfers nuclear excitation energy directly to a bound atomic electron, which is emitted, rather than to a real gamma ray. Photon counts alone then underestimate the number of nuclear transitions unless the conversion contribution is included.

Gamma decay (Eγ​≃Ei​−Ef​)

Words: 414 Articles: 6
Nuclear de-excitation OurBigBook.com
Gamma decay is electromagnetic nuclear de-excitation by photon emission. It preserves mass number and atomic number, while changing the nuclear state. Angular momentum and parity constrain the allowed radiation multipoles. The gamma ray topic is broader than this nuclear process.
Weisskopf single-particle estimate
Words: 69
Gamma decay OurBigBook.com
Named for Victor Weisskopf, a Weisskopf estimate is a benchmark electromagnetic nuclear transition strength based on a simple single-particle size model. It supplies a scale against which enhancement or hindrance is measured, rather than a universal rate. The partial rate contains both the photon-energy power Eγ2L+1​ and the actual reduced nuclear strength. Electric and magnetic multipoles at different energies cannot be universally ranked by order alone.[ref]
Gamma-cascade intensity balance (∑Ifeed​=∑Ideexcite​)
Words: 122 Articles: 1
Gamma decay OurBigBook.com
For a prompt nuclear level fed in a steady decay cascade, incoming and outgoing transition populations balance. Quoted photon intensities must represent efficiency-corrected emission counts; internal conversion, alternative particle decays and unobserved feeds must either be included or explicitly excluded. Equal photon counts suggest a serial cascade, while a feed intensity equal to the sum of two branches suggests splitting. intensities alone do not determine the chronological order of equal-count lines.
Nuclear level reconstruction from energy differences (Eγ,i​=Eui​​−Eli​​)
Words: 59
Gamma-cascade intensity balance OurBigBook.com
Reconstructing a nuclear gamma scheme amounts to assigning measured photon energies to differences between ordered level energies. Cascade energy sums constrain the graph, while population-flow balances constrain its orientation and feeding. Experimental central energies need not close exactly; a weighted least-squares energy-network fit uses their uncertainties consistently. Multiple graphs can satisfy the same spectrum when coincidence or lifetime information is absent.
Nuclear gamma-ray multipole selection rules (∣Ji​−Jf​∣≤L≤Ji​+Jf​,L≥1)
Words: 196 Articles: 2
Gamma decay OurBigBook.com
A real photon emitted in gamma decay carries a radiation multipole of integer angular momentum L≥1. The triangle rule gives the displayed range. An electric multipole EL requires πf​/πi​=(−1)L, while a magnetic multipole ML requires πf​/πi​=(−1)L+1. There is no real single-photon E0 decay; an electric monopole transition can instead proceed through internal conversion or, above threshold, pair creation. In the long-wavelength regime kR≪1, increasing L usually strongly suppresses the rate, but nuclear transition matrix elements and collective enhancement can make an allowed lower multipole weaker than a higher one.
Missing gamma lines and matrix-element hindrance
Words: 110 Articles: 1
Nuclear gamma-ray multipole selection rules OurBigBook.com
An absent otherwise-fast gamma transition can support a spin assignment that forbids it. It is not an absolute selection rule: a nuclear reduced matrix element may be small even when angular momentum and parity allow the mode. Meaningful limits require detection thresholds, branching normalization and competing-channel information. Assuming ordinary unhindered strengths can yield a useful conventional assignment, but that assumption must be distinguished from strict observed-line constraints.
Spin-reflection ambiguity in a gamma cascade
Words: 51
Missing gamma lines and matrix-element hindrance OurBigBook.com
If a cascade determines only absolute spin differences relative to a known reference spin, reflection of all inferred spins around that reference can preserve the same multipole pattern. Nonnegative-spin constraints, independently known spins, angular correlations or polarization measurements may break the ambiguity. A set of line energies and lowest-multipole labels need not do so.

Nuclear recoil

Words: 278 Articles: 3
Atomic nucleus OurBigBook.com
Movement of an atomic nucleus after it receives momentum, for example in a collision. A finite nuclear mass requires sharing kinetic energy as well as momentum; the fixed-target approximation neglects this motion.

Nuclear-recoil quenching factor (Q(E)=Ynuclear​(E)/Yelectron​(E))

Words: 61
Nuclear recoil OurBigBook.com
The relative light or charge yield of a nuclear recoil compared with an electron recoil at the same deposited energy in a specified material and field configuration. It connects an electron-equivalent calibration scale with a recoil-energy scale. It can depend on energy and measurement conditions; assuming a constant or extrapolating below calibration changes light-WIMP interpretations. It does not mean energy conservation fails: energy can enter channels not measured by that readout.

Nuclear-recoil kinematics of WIMP scattering (ER,max​=2μχN2​v2/mN​)

Words: 69
Nuclear recoil OurBigBook.com
For nonrelativistic elastic scattering of a WIMP of mass mχ​ from a stationary nucleus of mass mN​, the reduced mass is μχN​=mχ​mN​/(mχ​+mN​). In the centre-of-momentum description, a scattering angle ϑ gives momentum transfer squared q2=2μχN2​v2(1−cosϑ). The target kinetic energy is ER​=q2/(2mN​), giving the displayed maximum at backscattering. In SI units, using masses in kilograms and velocity in metres per second gives joules. Low recoil energy and a falling recoil spectrum make threshold and response calibration important for dark-matter direct detection.

Nuclear elastic electron scattering kinematics (E′=E/[1+2Esin2(θ/2)/(Mc2)])

Words: 121
Nuclear recoil OurBigBook.com
An elastic scattering event leaves the nuclear internal quantum state unchanged while transferring energy to its motion. Let a target of mass M initially be at rest. Neglect the electron rest mass, but retain relativistic target recoil. Conservation of momentum and conservation of relativistic energy give
ℏ2c2q2=E2+E′2−2EE′cosθ,(Mc2+E−E′)2=M2c4+ℏ2c2q2.
(1)
Eliminating q yields Mc2(E−E′)=EE′(1−cosθ) and hence
E′=1+Mc22E​sin2(θ/2)E​,TR​=E−E′.
(2)
The static relation q=2Esin(θ/2)/(ℏc) follows only when E/(Mc2) is negligible. For small recoil, TR​≃ℏ2q2/(2M); this last approximation is nonrelativistic for the recoiling nucleus, not for the incident electron. The invariant spacelike transfer, expressed in energy-squared units, is
Q2=ℏ2c2q2−(E−E′)2=4EE′sin2(θ/2),
(3)
so Q/(ℏc) and the spatial inverse length q coincide only in the static limit. Recoil kinematics alone does not correct the static differential scattering cross-section: a finite-mass target also changes its kinematic prefactor and the appropriate current matrix elements.

The bound atomic nucleus containing one proton and one neutron. Its ground-state spin is one and its isospin is zero. The appropriately quantized toroidal two-Skyrmion models these quantum numbers.

Deuteron D-state admixture (PD​=∫0∞​∣uD​(r)∣2dr)

Deuteron OurBigBook.com
The deuteron has a dominant 3S1​ component and a smaller 3D1​ component coupled by the nuclear tensor force. The electric quadrupole moment rules out a purely isotropic S wave spatial state. A D-state probability depends on the chosen nuclear Hamiltonian and current approximation; it is not an independently model-free observable.

Nuclear binding energy (Ebind​=(Zmp​+Nmn​−MA​)c2)

Words: 420 Articles: 8
Atomic nucleus OurBigBook.com
The positive energy needed to separate a bound atomic nucleus into its constituent protons and neutrons. It is the mass defect times c2. In a Skyrmion model of a nucleus, both separated nucleon masses and the multi-soliton state require a consistent quantization and calibration.

Liquid-drop model of the nucleus

Words: 43
Nuclear binding energy OurBigBook.com
A collective nuclear model that treats the nucleus as a nearly uniform fluid drop with short-range cohesive binding, surface energy and electrostatic self-energy. The semi-empirical mass formula incorporates these contributions, together with asymmetry and pairing terms, to describe broad binding trends. Discrete nuclear shell structure requires additional physics.

Mass defect (Δm)

Words: 42
Nuclear binding energy OurBigBook.com
A difference between the mass of a bound system and an appropriate sum of reference constituent masses. For a nuclear transformation, the initial-minus-final mass difference gives the available Q value after multiplication by c2, with atomic electrons counted consistently when using neutral-atom masses.

Binding energy per nucleon (B/A)

Words: 36
Nuclear binding energy OurBigBook.com
Binding energy per nucleon divides conventional positive nuclear binding energy by mass number. Typical well-bound nuclei have values of order 8MeV, and the broad maximum lies near iron-group nuclei. A mass correction added to free-nucleon masses instead has the opposite sign, Ecorr​/A=−B/A.

Semi-empirical mass formula

Words: 261 Articles: 4
Nuclear binding energy OurBigBook.com
The semi-empirical mass formula approximates nuclear binding energy using volume, surface, electrostatic, neutron-proton asymmetry, and nuclear pairing contributions. At fixed mass number, its quadratic dependence on atomic number gives a nuclear mass parabola. It describes broad trends and does not resolve individual nuclear shell effects.

Semi-empirical mass errors can change beta-decay thresholds

Words: 66
Semi-empirical mass formula OurBigBook.com
Beta decay Q values depend on small differences between much larger atomic masses. A smooth semi-empirical mass formula can therefore approximate absolute masses reasonably while predicting the wrong sign for a MeV-scale decay threshold. Nuclear pairing explains offset even-even and odd-odd mass branches, but does not guarantee that every specific even-even neighbor lies below the intervening odd-odd nucleus. Measured masses or additional nuclear shell corrections are needed for quantitative threshold tests near a model's residual error scale.

Nuclear Coulomb energy (EC​≃aC​Z2/A1/3)

Words: 100 Articles: 1
Semi-empirical mass formula OurBigBook.com
Nuclear Coulomb energy is the positive electrostatic mass-energy contribution from proton repulsion in a nucleus. A uniform-charge model gives scaling Z2/R, and approximately constant nuclear density gives R∝A1/3. It reduces conventional positive nuclear binding energy.
Uniform-sphere Coulomb displacement energy (ΔEC​=3αℏcΔ(Z2)/(5R))
Words: 67
Nuclear Coulomb energy OurBigBook.com
The electrostatic self-energy of a uniformly charged sphere of sharp radius R is 3Z2αℏc/(5R). For mirror nuclei, subtract the changed neutron/proton rest-mass contribution from their nuclear mass difference before identifying the residual with a nuclear Coulomb energy difference. The inferred R is a sharp-sphere radius; the corresponding nuclear charge radius defined as an rms radius is 3/5​R. Replacing Z2 by Z(Z−1) removes a proton self-interaction approximation and shifts the estimate.

Nuclear mass parabola (M(A,Z)≃a(A)Z2+b(A)Z+c(A))

Words: 65
Semi-empirical mass formula OurBigBook.com
At fixed mass number A, the smooth semi-empirical mass formula makes the atomic mass approximately quadratic and convex in atomic number Z. Neighboring nuclear isobars undergo energetically allowed beta decay toward a discrete local minimum, subject to the electron-mass threshold for beta-plus decay. For odd A there is one smooth branch; for even A, nuclear pairing splits the even-even and odd-odd branches. A local minimum against a single change of Z need not be stable against double beta decay.

Nuclear charge distribution (ρch​(r))

Words: 1k Articles: 6
Atomic nucleus OurBigBook.com
A spatial charge density describing the electric charge inside an atomic nucleus. In a static description centered on the nucleus, normalize it by
∫R3​ρch​(r)d3r=Ze,
(4)
where Z is the atomic number and e>0 the elementary charge. A nuclear charge distribution is an average over a nuclear quantum state, rather than a photograph of immobile protons. Even when the average has spherical symmetry, individual nucleons need not sit on spherical shells. The proton charge radius and the internal charge density of the neutron also contribute: a distribution of pointlike proton centers is an approximation to a nuclear charge distribution, not its definition.
The experimental attraction is that an electron probes electric charge through the electromagnetic interaction, without itself feeling the nuclear force. Varying its momentum transfer changes the spatial scale tested. Long wavelengths mainly constrain a nuclear charge radius; shorter wavelengths can distinguish a diffuse edge from a sharper one through diffraction. Recovering the entire nuclear charge distribution from a finite range of noisy scattering data remains an inverse problem.

Two-parameter Fermi nuclear charge distribution (ρ(r)=ρ0​/[1+e(r−c)/a])

Words: 76
Nuclear charge distribution OurBigBook.com
A common spherical model of a heavy nuclear charge distribution uses a half-density radius c and surface diffuseness a>0. The normalization ρ0​ is fixed by 4π∫0∞​r2ρ(r)dr=Ze. The 90%-to-10% surface thickness is t=4aln3: solve ρ/ρ0​=0.9 and 0.1 and subtract the two radii. Typical fits have an approximately constant surface thickness and a radius scaling roughly as the cube root of mass number. This is a spatial density model, not an assertion of a thermal Fermi-Dirac distribution for nuclear positions.

Hofstadter nuclear electron scattering experiment

Words: 201
Nuclear charge distribution OurBigBook.com
Robert Hofstadter's Stanford installation selected electrons up to 190MeV. Magnetic spectrometer scans counted shielded Lucite Cherenkov radiation pulses per monitor charge. Carbon-12 at 187MeV and 80∘ resolved 185.1MeV elastic scattering and 180.7MeV inelastic scattering (4.43MeV excitation). Calibration used forward-beam energy, hydrogen nuclear recoil and carbon-12 energy levels. Closed entrance slits checked background.[ref]
The key distinction is between an electron counter and an energy measurement. Here the counter registers particles admitted by the magnetic spectrometer; changing its magnetic field selects another outgoing momentum. A peak is therefore reconstructed from a scan, rather than read as one pulse whose height equals the scattered electron energy. Dividing counts by monitored incident electric charge compensates for changes in beam intensity. A peak's height alone is insufficient: its area and the instrument's accepted momentum interval determine the selected yield.
An inelastic scattering peak corresponds to a different final nuclear quantum state, so including it in the elastic scattering yield would answer a different structural question. Conversely, finite energy resolution can hide sufficiently nearby excitations beneath an apparent elastic scattering peak. Background subtraction, target-loss corrections, angular acceptance and counter efficiency all enter before counts become a differential scattering cross-section. A dip in uncorrected counts is therefore not automatically a zero of a nuclear charge form factor.

Nuclear charge radius (rch​=⟨r2⟩ch​​)

The root mean square radius of a nuclear charge distribution, referred to its center. Define its charge-weighted second moment by
⟨r2⟩ch​=Ze1​∫r2ρch​(r)d3r.
(5)
For a spherical charge density with finite fourth moment, insert the Taylor series sinu/u=1−u2/6+O(u4) into the nuclear charge form factor:
F(q)=1−6q2​⟨r2⟩ch​+O(q4),⟨r2⟩ch​=−6d(q2)dF​​q=0​.
(6)
Thus a radius is a low-momentum transfer slope, not necessarily the position of a sharp edge. In this real static model,

Nuclear charge form factor (F(q))

Words: 612 Articles: 2
Nuclear charge distribution OurBigBook.com
In the static charge density model, define the normalized Fourier transform
F(q)=Ze1​∫ρch​(r)eiq⋅rd3r,F(0)=1.
(8)
Here q=(p−p′)/ℏ is the spatial momentum transfer divided by the reduced Planck constant: q=∣q∣ has units of inverse length. Physical momentum transfer is ℏq. We keep ℏ and the speed of light c explicit; compute q=∣p−p′∣c/(ℏc), expressing the numerator in MeV and ℏc in MeVfm, to obtain q in inverse femtometres. The spatial q is distinct from the invariant four-momentum transfer used in relativistic scattering cross-sections.
For spherical symmetry, take the polar axis along q and use spherical coordinates. With μ the cosine of the polar angle,
∫−11​eiqrμdμ=2qrsin(qr)​,F(q)=Ze4π​∫0∞​r2ρch​(r)qrsin(qr)​dr.
(9)
Every radial shell contributes an amplitude. At q=0 all shells add with the same sign; at larger q their contributions can cancel. This cancellation is the origin of a diffraction minimum in the nuclear charge form factor, rather than an absence of electric charge in the nucleus.
The static Fourier transform interpretation is appropriate for the nuclear model and kinematic regime specified here. A fully relativistic composite system requires frame and current conventions; its electric form factor is not universally the transform of a frame-independent three-dimensional charge density.

Uniform-sphere nuclear charge form factor (F(q)=3[sin(qR)−qRcos(qR)]/(qR)3)

Nuclear charge form factor OurBigBook.com
Consider an ideal nuclear charge distribution that is constant inside a ball of radius R>0 and zero outside:
ρch​(r)={3Ze/(4πR3),0,​0≤r≤R,r>R.​
(10)
This is a deliberately sharp-edged model. Substitution into the spherical nuclear charge form factor gives, for q=0,
F(q)=R33​∫0R​r2qrsin(qr)​dr=qR33​[−qrcos(qr)​+q2sin(qr)​]0R​=3(qR)3sin(qR)−qRcos(qR)​.
(11)
At q=0 take the continuous limit F(0)=1. Direct radial integration gives ⟨r2⟩ch​=3R2/5, and the Taylor series provides an independent check:
F(q)=1−10(qR)2​+280(qR)4​+O((qR)6).
(12)
The first positive zero solves sinx−xcosx=0, equivalently tanx=x, with x=qR≃4.49340946. Consequently the first diffraction minimum moves toward smaller momentum transfer as R increases.
For comparison, a smooth Gaussian function charge density with the same nuclear charge radius is

(13)

Both models have the same leading low-q curvature, but they disagree at higher momentum transfer: at qR=2, the sphere gives F≃0.653097 and the Gaussian function gives FG​≃0.670320. Measuring only a nuclear charge radius cannot distinguish these two nuclear charge distributions.
As a chosen scale illustration, take R=3.00fm. The predicted first zero is q=1.49780fm−1, or ℏcq≃295.557MeV using ℏc≃197.327MeVfm. With a 200MeV ultrarelativistic electron beam and negligible nuclear recoil, it occurs at θ≃95.27∘. These are consequences of chosen model parameters, not measured properties of carbon or a claim that all atomic nuclei are uniform balls.

Nuclear elastic electron scattering cross-section (dσ/dΩ≃(dσ/dΩ)point​∣F(q)∣2)

Words: 183
Nuclear charge form factor OurBigBook.com
For an ultrarelativistic electron scattered elastically by a static, spherical, spin-zero atomic nucleus, a plane-wave Born approximation retains a single electromagnetic interaction. The Fourier transform of its Coulomb potential is proportional to ZeF(q)/q2. Relative to the same point charge, the scattering amplitude is therefore multiplied by F(q) and the differential scattering cross-section by ∣F(q)∣2:
dΩdσ​≃(2EZαℏc​)2sin4(θ/2)cos2(θ/2)​∣F(q)∣2,q≃ℏc2E​sin2θ​.
(14)
Here E≫me​c2 is the incident total electron energy, θ the scattering angle, dΩ a solid angle element, and α the fine-structure constant. The cosine factor already accounts for the projectile's spin; a spinless projectile would give a different point-charge factor. The formula neglects nuclear recoil, electron rest-mass corrections, radiative corrections and Coulomb distortion in electron scattering. The latter can be substantial at large Zα: electron waves then require treatment in the target's Coulomb potential, rather than undistorted plane waves.
Nonzero target spin can introduce magnetic and additional electric contributions, so division by this point-charge expression does not generally isolate one charge form factor for a proton or deuteron. Even for spin zero, finite energy resolution, recoil and Coulomb distortion in electron scattering can change a measured minimum. A zero of the uniform-sphere model is a clear illustration of amplitude cancellation, not a universal prediction of a zero experimental counting rate.

Nuclear shell model

Words: 2k Articles: 19
Atomic nucleus OurBigBook.com
Why should adding one neutron be easy for one atomic nucleus and much less favorable for its neighbor? In the nuclear shell model, nucleons occupy discrete energy levels in a nuclear mean field. Filling all available states below a sufficiently large gap produces a nuclear shell closure. This connects nuclear separation energies with the counting of quantum states, and connects the spin of an odd atomic nucleus with its last unpaired nucleon.
The starting point is an approximate Hamiltonian operator
H=∑i=1A​[2mi​pi2​​+Ui​(ri​)−ai​(ri​)ℏ2Li​⋅Si​​]+Vres​,
(15)
where Ui​ is a confining potential energy, ai​ specifies nuclear spin-orbit splitting, and Vres​ contains interactions left out of the nuclear mean field. This is an effective organization of the nuclear many-body problem, rather than a derivation of the nuclear force. In a spherical approximation, addition of angular momentum combines the orbital angular momentum l with spin s=1/2 to give j=l±1/2; for l=0, only j=1/2 exists.
A level labelled nlj has 2j+1 magnetic substates mj​=−j,−j+1,…,j. The Pauli exclusion principle permits one neutron in each such state and, separately, one proton. Thus a j=7/2 level holds eight neutrons, or eight protons; it does not hold sixteen identical neutrons. Here n=1,2,… counts radial levels of the same l, starting with the lowest; it is not the hydrogenic principal quantum number. The letters s,p,d,f,g,h,i mean l=0,1,2,3,4,5,6.

Magic number

Words: 308 Articles: 1
Nuclear shell model OurBigBook.com
A proton number Z or neutron number N associated with a pronounced nuclear shell closure. The traditional sequence is
2,8,20,28,50,82,126.
(18)

For example, in the chosen scheme the 20-particle core plus the eight substates of 1f7/2​ gives 28. The next group contributes 4+6+2+10=22, giving 50. The following two groups contribute 8+6+4+2+12=32 and 10+8+6+4+2+14=44, giving 82 and 126. Counting verifies the capacities once the grouping has been chosen; it does not establish the physical energy gaps that justify that grouping.

Nuclear shell closure

Words: 158
Magic number OurBigBook.com
Filling the accessible one-species nuclear shell model states below a substantial energy gap. A closed spherical j level has all mj​ values occupied, so their sum is zero. In the independent-particle picture, a closed core therefore contributes zero total angular momentum; a single particle outside it, or a single missing particle inside it, supplies the leading spin assignment. Residual interactions can change this simple picture.
The three-dimensional isotropic harmonic oscillator alone has one-species shell capacities (q+1)(q+2) for oscillator index q=0,1,…, including spin. Summing gives closures 2,8,20,40,70,112,…, rather than the complete traditional magic number sequence. Strong nuclear spin-orbit splitting can separate a high-j level from its former neighbors. Realistic radial potential energies and residual interactions also matter: a chosen oscillator plus an arbitrary single constant is not a demonstrated explanation of every real nuclear gap.
Traditional nuclear shell closures can weaken along isotope chains far from stability, as in neutron-rich magnesium.[ref]
The relevant test is the actual energy gap and the correlated nuclear quantum states, rather than membership in an immutable numerical list.

Nuclear pairing

Words: 62
Nuclear shell model OurBigBook.com
Correlations that favor pairs of like nucleons coupled to zero total angular momentum in the simplest spherical picture. They help explain why an unpaired nucleon often dominates an odd nucleus's spin, and why neighboring nuclear separation energies show an odd-even staggering. Nuclear pairing belongs to the residual-interaction physics beyond merely filling independent energy levels. Mixing different occupations and deformation can also be necessary; the closed-core picture is most useful near suitable nuclear shell closures.

Empirical evidence for nuclear shells

Words: 278 Articles: 1
Nuclear shell model OurBigBook.com
Isotope abundance, nuclear separation energies, spin and nuclear magnetic moments supply complementary tests: abundance concerns which atomic nuclei survive and accumulate, removal energy concerns binding, and magnetic properties test the angular momentum assignments. Agreement across these observables is more informative than fitting one list of numbers.
In her 1963 lecture, Maria Goeppert Mayer recalled excess abundances at N=50 and 82. She contrasted the strong binding of the last neutron in oxygen isotope 16O with the weaker extra neutron in oxygen isotope 17O, and displayed beta decay energies near N=20 and 50. These are compiled empirical patterns, rather than readings from an apparatus she built.[ref]
Her 1949 letter compared observed spins and nuclear magnetic moments with orbital assignments. For example, lead isotope 207Pb was assigned a p1/2​ neutron hole near N=126, whereas bismuth's odd proton began the next shell as h9/2​. The moments tested orbital character as well as spin.[ref]
A useful comparison holds Z fixed when comparing neighboring isotopes, and examines odd-even staggering separately from a shell discontinuity. Abundance additionally depends on production and decay history. Neither the abundance pattern nor a successful spin assignment directly images orbiting particles; neither fixes a unique nuclear mean field. The experimental techniques themselves include mass spectrometry and nuclear magnetic resonance, whose preparation and readout differ. The Hofstadter nuclear electron scattering experiment separately constrains nuclear size and nuclear charge distributions, rather than measuring these occupancies.

Emitted gamma rays supplied excitation energies; comparison across these isotopes supported deformation extending beyond the N=20 region toward 28. Goeppert Mayer and Jensen nuclear shell model history

Words: 112
Nuclear shell model OurBigBook.com
The 1948 empirical synthesis preceded the 1949 strong spin-orbit interaction proposals. Collective nuclear model

Words: 741 Articles: 4
Atomic nucleus OurBigBook.com
A description of an atomic nucleus in terms of coordinated motion of many nucleons, especially changes in shape and orientation. The nuclear shell model organizes individual nucleon states in a nuclear mean field; the collective nuclear model organizes low-lying energy levels by nuclear deformation, nuclear rotational bands and nuclear quadrupole vibrations. These are complementary organizations of the same nuclear many-body problem. A deformed nuclear mean field can support individual-particle states while its orientation supplies a slow collective coordinate.
In his 1975 lecture, Ben Roy Mottelson described coupling individual-particle motion to nuclear shape coordinates, and distinguished observed rotational spectra from vibrations about spherical equilibrium. This connects the two descriptions without treating the collective coordinates as extra constituent particles.[ref]
James Rainwater's 1975 account identifies unexpectedly large electric quadrupole moments as a motivation for a deformable nuclear mean field. The challenge concerned their magnitude, even where spherical-shell trends had the correct sign.[ref]
A simple original potential energy example makes the coupling tangible. Let q be a dimensionless signed shape coordinate for a chosen intrinsic configuration, C>0 its restoring stiffness, and f a configuration-dependent coupling, both with units of energy. Suppose
V(q)=21​Cq2−fq=2C​(q−Cf​)2−2Cf2​.
(22)
The minimum is q∗​=f/C, with energy gain f2/(2C). For chosen values C=20u and f=4u, where u is an arbitrary positive energy unit, q∗​=0.20 and the gain is 0.40u. This illustrates how a configuration can favor deformation when the core can respond. It is not a fitted nuclear surface or a claim that every configuration chooses the same shape; competing configurations and nuclear pairing can favor a spherical minimum.
The predictive attraction is economy: one collective scale relates several energy levels, while electric quadrupole transitions independently test their common structure. Agreement in both is much more restrictive than assigning a separate free parameter to every level. Nuclear magnetic moments test magnetic structure and angular momentum assignments; they are distinct observables from the electric quadrupole moments used to infer charge deformation.

Nuclear quadrupole vibration (En​−E0​=nℏω)

Words: 217 Articles: 1
Collective nuclear model OurBigBook.com
A collective oscillation of the quadrupole shape coordinates of an atomic nucleus. Near spherical equilibrium, the ideal harmonic description has five components, corresponding to the five magnetic substates of angular momentum two. Each quantum carries Iπ=2+; it is an excitation of the many-nucleon system rather than an extra constituent. Its frequency is ω, and its energy spacing ℏω follows the quantum harmonic oscillator.
One quantum produces a 2+ level at ℏω. Two identical quadrupole quanta can couple to I=0,2,4 in a symmetric state, giving a degenerate 0+,2+,4+ multiplet at 2ℏω in the ideal model. Odd total I would be antisymmetric under their exchange. Thus the first 4+ to first 2+ excitation ratio is 2, rather than the nuclear rotational band value 10/3. Real nuclear quadrupole vibrations can be anharmonic and their multiplets split: the ratio is a diagnostic of a limiting model, not a universal classification rule.
There is a useful state-counting check. Two identical quanta distributed among five substates span 5(5+1)/2=15 symmetric states. The proposed multiplet contains (2⋅0+1)+(2⋅2+1)+(2⋅4+1)=1+5+9=15. This verifies the angular-state bookkeeping independently of the chosen energy scale.
