Riemann sphere (C=C∪{∞})

 Home Mathematics Area of mathematics Analysis Complex analysis Riemann surfaces
OurBigBook.com Words: 1k Articles: 13
The Riemann sphere is C∪{∞} with two charts related by reciprocal coordinates.
The extra point completes the complex plane into a compact Riemann surface. It is one point shared by every direction of escape: n, −n and in all tend to ∞ as n→∞. A basis of neighbourhoods at ∞ is
UR​={∞}∪{z∈C:∣z∣>R},R>0.
(21)
This is the one-point compactification of the complex plane; convergence to ∞ means that the modulus tends to infinity.[ref]
For a concrete picture, use the unit sphere
S2={(X,Y,Z)∈R3:X2+Y2+Z2=1}
(22)
and identify z=x+iy with (x,y,0) in its equatorial plane. The north pole N=(0,0,1) represents ∞ and the south pole S=(0,0,−1) represents 0. The stereographic projection below gives the identification. This convention fixes the scale: the equator corresponds to ∣z∣=1.[ref]
Why add this point? The Möbius transformation z↦1/z then exchanges 0 and ∞ and becomes a holomorphic map everywhere on the Riemann sphere. Its apparent blow-up at zero disappears when its output is expressed in the reciprocal chart at infinity: w=1/(1/z)=z. This is a concrete instance of a meromorphic function as a holomorphic map to the Riemann sphere. The additional point makes the complex plane compact, while its second manifold chart lets us do ordinary complex analysis near infinity.
