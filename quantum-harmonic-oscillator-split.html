Quantum harmonic oscillator (H=p2/(2m)+mω2x2/2)

 Home Physics Branches of physics Quantum mechanics
OurBigBook.com Words: 3k Articles: 63
The quantum harmonic oscillator describes a particle of mass m>0 in a quadratic potential energy with angular frequency ω>0. In the position representation its Quantum Hamiltonian is
H=−2mℏ2​dx2d2​+2mω2x2​.
(1)
The model is useful far beyond a literal spring: a Taylor series about a stable equilibrium with positive potential curvature has this quadratic term as its leading nonconstant approximation. The pure quartic oscillator has zero curvature at its minimum, so that particular approximation does not apply. [ref].
In units with ℏ=1, creation and annihilation operators give
H=ω(N+21​),N=A†A.
(2)
Here N is the number operator. Restoring the reduced Planck constant, the energy eigenvalues are En​=ℏω(n+1/2) for n=0,1,2,…: neighboring levels are separated by ℏω, and the ground-state energy is the zero-point energy ℏω/2.
With length scale ℓ=ℏ/(mω)​ and dimensionless coordinate u=x/ℓ, the ground state is the normalized Gaussian wave packet
ϕ0​(x)=π1/4ℓ​1​e−u2/2,⟨x2⟩0​=2ℓ2​.
(3)
For ℏ=m=ω=1, this is precisely the a=1 trial wavefunction in the quantum variational principle. The balance of kinetic energy and potential energy therefore recovers the exact ground-state energy using only Gaussian integrals.
