Cotangent contour summation (Resz=n​[πcot(πz)h(z)]=h(n))

 Home Mathematics Area of mathematics Analysis Residue theorem
OurBigBook.com Words: 125
The cotangent kernel πcot(πz) has a simple pole of residue one at every integer. If a meromorphic h is regular at the integers, these residues sample its integer values. Integrating around squares of half-side N+1/2 and applying the residue theorem converts a finite sum of samples into residues at the other poles. When the contour integral tends to zero and h has only finitely many other poles, the limiting sum is the negative sum of those off-integer residues of πcot(πz)h(z). A uniform cotangent bound and sufficiently rapid decay of h justify the vanishing contour term. At an integer where h itself has a pole, use the full Laurent series to find the combined residue instead of substituting a divergent sample; the Basel problem calculation with h(z)=z−2 illustrates this exception.
