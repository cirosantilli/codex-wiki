Repeated integration by parts gives the Gamma integral
n!=∫0∞​tne−tdt.
(14)
The logarithm of its integrand is nlogt−t, whose unique maximum is at t=n. Write t=n+n​s. For fixed s and large n, a Taylor expansion gives
nlog(1+n​s​)−sn​=−2s2​+3n​s3​+O(n−1).
(15)
The Big O notation here is for a fixed s, or uniformly for s in a fixed bounded interval. It does not claim a uniform approximation over the entire growing integration domain. Consequently the peak, divided by its height, approaches e−s2/2.
The exact substitution gives
n!=nne−nn​∫−n​∞​exp(nlog(1+n​s​)−sn​)ds.
(16)
Replacing the integral by the Gaussian integral ∫R​e−s2/2ds=2π​ produces Sn​. Thus the estimate is peak height nne−n times width n​ times the area of a standard Gaussian peak. This is Laplace's method in a concrete form; controlling the integral's tails justifies the replacement.
