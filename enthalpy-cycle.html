Enthalpy cycle (∑cycle​ΔH=0)

 Home Chemistry Thermochemistry Hess's law
OurBigBook.com Words: 204
An enthalpy cycle is a collection of compatible thermodynamic processes that returns the entire material inventory to its initial thermodynamic state. Its signed enthalpy changes sum to zero. This supplies a consistency check and lets a missing change be recovered from the others.
Consider three abstract thermodynamic states A,B,C of the same conserved material inventory at common temperature and pressure. Choose their molar enthalpies, on the same one-mole basis, as
hA​=0,hB​=18kJmol−1,hC​=−7kJmol−1.
(20)
These are constructed values, not material measurements. Endpoint subtraction gives
ΔhAB​ΔhBC​ΔhAC​​=hB​−hA​=+18kJmol−1,=hC​−hB​=−25kJmol−1,=hC​−hA​=−7kJmol−1.​
(21)
Thus +18−25=−7: the two-step route and the direct route have the same molar enthalpy change. Closing the route with C→A gives +18−25+7=0. Shifting every h by the same arbitrary reference constant leaves all these differences unchanged.
The short proof is cancellation:
(HB​−HA​)+(HC​−HB​)=HC​−HA​.
(22)
Adding any finite number of consecutive endpoint differences works the same way. For a closed enthalpy cycle the final HA​ cancels the initial HA​. A nonzero experimental residual can therefore expose mismatched states or measurement error; it does not identify which individual input is responsible.
