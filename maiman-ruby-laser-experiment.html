Maiman ruby laser experiment

 ... Physics Branches of physics Optics Laser Three-level laser Ruby laser
OurBigBook.com Words: 270
Theodore Harold Maiman and Irnee J. D'Haenens obtained laser action in May 1960. Their arrangement put a ruby rod inside a helical xenon flashlamp. Increasing excitation produced a marked intensity threshold, with subsequent measurements showing narrowing of the ruby emission line.[ref] The 1960 letter describes silvered parallel crystal faces and compares low- and high-excitation spectra.[ref]
The controls answer different questions. Increased brightness establishes neither optical feedback nor narrow-band emission by itself: ordinary fluorescence can also brighten with pumping. Comparing spectra at lower and higher excitation, retaining the same optical collection geometry, tests a change in the emission process. Spectrometer resolution, response saturation and heating can alter an apparent line profile; a bright spot alone cannot distinguish those effects. These are interpretation requirements, rather than additional measurements attributed to the short original letter.
Several experimental models were made. The Smithsonian's surviving head has uncertain priority, and its installed crystal came from a later experiment.[ref] The functional diagram therefore describes the optical mechanism rather than claiming to reconstruct the first surviving unit.
