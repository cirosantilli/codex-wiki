Population inversion (N2​>N1​)

 Home Physics Branches of physics Optics Laser Stimulated emission
OurBigBook.com Words: 261
A population inversion puts more emitters in the upper than in the lower quantum state of a transition in the nondegenerate two-level model. Let N2​,N1​ be their number densities. Equal stimulated-transition strengths make the emission rate proportional to N2​ and the competing photon absorption rate proportional to N1​, so net optical gain is proportional to N2​−N1​. For N2​=6×1020m−3 and N1​=4×1020m−3, the difference is positive; swapping them makes the medium absorb. Equal populations give zero net stimulated contribution. With degenerate levels, the relevant comparison uses populations per statistical weight of an atomic level, rather than simply N2​>N1​.
Why pump through extra levels? Ordinary steady resonant pumping of a nondegenerate two-level system drives both photon absorption and stimulated emission. With equal induced rate W and upper-state decay rate Γ, its elementary population equation is
N˙2​=W(N1​−N2​)−ΓN2​,N1​N2​​=W+ΓW​≤1
(39)
at a stationary population. Increasing this pump saturates towards N2​=N1​; it cannot sustain population inversion in this model. This is a steady rate-equation conclusion, not a universal prohibition on all driven two-level dynamics or all possible laser gain mechanisms. [ref].
In a familiar three-level path, pumping excites a higher level which relaxes into the upper lasing level; the lower lasing level is the ground state, so substantial ground-state depletion is needed. A four-level path also provides rapid emptying of the lower lasing level into a separate ground state. Suitable lifetimes then allow accumulation above and depletion below the lasing transition. Pumping still supplies the energy: extra levels provide a route to population inversion, not free energy. [ref].
