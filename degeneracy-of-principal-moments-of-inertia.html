Degeneracy of principal moments of inertia

 ... Branches of physics Classical mechanics Rigid body dynamics Moment of inertia Inertia tensor Principal moment of inertia
OurBigBook.com Words: 159
If two principal moments of inertia coincide, for example I1​=I2​=I3​, every direction in their two-dimensional eigenspace is a principal axis. The perpendicular axis is determined, but there is no privileged orthogonal pair within that plane. Rotating the pair in the plane leaves the diagonal inertia tensor unchanged. If all three principal moments of inertia coincide, IO​=I0​1 and every axis through O is principal. This follows from the eigenvalue multiplicity and eigenspace, rather than from a failure of diagonalization. [ref]
Equal principal moments of inertia do not prove that a body's outline has continuous rotational symmetry. For example, six equal point masses at (±a,0,0), (0,±a,0) and (0,0,±a) have IO​=4ma21. This discrete layout has the same axial moment of inertia in every direction, although its actual mass distribution changes under most rotations. Conversely, even when all three principal moments of inertia are distinct, principal axes are unoriented lines: reversing an eigenvector's sign gives the same axis. Near repeated eigenvalues, small measurement errors can rotate fitted principal axes substantially within the nearly degenerate eigenspace, so quoted directions need an uncertainty assessment as well as quoted principal moments of inertia.
