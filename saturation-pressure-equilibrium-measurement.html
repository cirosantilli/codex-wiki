Saturation-pressure equilibrium measurement

OurBigBook.com Words: 211
A concrete experiment turns the phase coexistence curve into pairs of temperature and saturation pressure. A 1969 paper reports water measurements made in 1942 and 1947–1949 using a boiler and precision mercury manometer. Platinum resistance thermometers read the boiler temperature through a resistance bridge. The pressure-transfer line passed through an ice-water condenser and a dry-ice trap; helium filled connecting sections. Corrected mercury column height gave the pressure at the thermometer coils, including hydrostatic pressure contributions from water vapor, helium, mercury, and mercury vapor.[ref]
Controls included normal/reversed-lead readings, readings at two thermometer electric currents extrapolated to zero current to remove self-heating, repeated bridge calibration, and daily triple point thermometer calibration checks. Cellar temperature measurements corrected fluid-column and gauge-block thermal expansion. One measurement series was excluded because a trapped gas bubble was suspected of corrupting the mercury head. The paper adjusted measurements to round temperatures using previously tabulated slopes; those adjusted values therefore are not independent measurements of those slopes.[ref]
The thermodynamic inference requires liquid and gas in phase coexistence, a well-defined common temperature, and a pressure corrected to that same location. Leaks, contamination, or a temperature gradient can spoil that interpretation. A separate calorimetry measurement of latent heat, together with the two specific volumes, allows a test of the full Clapeyron equation. Converting a measured phase coexistence curve slope directly into latent heat instead assumes that equation; it is not an independent test.
