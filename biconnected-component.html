Biconnected component

 Home Mathematics Area of mathematics Foundations of mathematics Graph theory Cut vertex
OurBigBook.com Words: 104
A biconnected component is a maximal connected subgraph with no cut vertex of its own. We use the block convention that also includes each bridge in a graph with its two endpoint vertices, and each isolated vertex, as a biconnected component. A biconnected component with at least three vertices stays connected after any single vertex deletion. Distinct biconnected components share at most one vertex; the vertices belonging to more than one biconnected component are exactly the cut vertices. The shared-A example has two biconnected components, its two triangles in a graph. The joined-CD example has three: ABC, the bridge in a graph CD with its endpoints, and DEF. The edges partition into these pieces, while the shared cut vertices occur in several pieces. [ref]
