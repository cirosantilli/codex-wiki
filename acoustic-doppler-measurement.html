Acoustic Doppler measurement (v=c(1−fs​/fo​))

 Home Physics Wave Doppler effect
OurBigBook.com Words: 213
UNSW School of Physics documents a Doppler effect experiment using a buzzer based on piezoelectricity, powered by a taped-on 9V battery, and a computer's built-in microphone. Audacity records the acoustic wave. First record the stationary source to establish its baseline frequency; then attach it to a bicycle passing the stationary microphone. The published spectrogram shows a rapid decrease in frequency during passage.[ref]
For numerical readout, select an approaching or receding interval, display its frequency spectrum using Plot Spectrum, and read the interpolated Peak frequency. The protocol recommends repeating with slightly different selections to test stability, and working outdoors to reduce reflections and standing waves. Longer windows refine frequency spacing while averaging over more motion. The baseline controls for the source's actual tone; repeated selections expose analysis-window sensitivity.[ref]
Under nearly collinear constant-speed approach, infer v=c(1−fs​/fo​) from the classical Doppler effect for sound. For recession the positive speed is v=c(fs​/fo​−1). These inversions need a specified speed of sound. An offset path changes the projection, as in Doppler frequency along a trajectory; neither inversion measures the full speed near closest passage. The protocol's controls cannot remove arbitrary acceleration, background measurement noise or unresolved reflected paths. No new measurements are claimed here.
