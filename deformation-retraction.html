Deformation retraction (H:X×[0,1]→X)

 ... Mathematics Area of mathematics Geometry and topology Algebraic topology Homotopy Retract
OurBigBook.com Words: 191
A deformation retraction of X onto a subspace A is a homotopy H:X×[0,1]→X such that H(x,0)=x, H(x,1)=r(x)∈A, and H(a,t)=a for every a∈A and every t. Thus the retract is fixed pointwise throughout. With inclusion i:A↪X, the equations r∘i=idA​ and i∘r≃idX​ exhibit a homotopy equivalence. See Hatcher, Algebraic Topology, Chapter 0, pp. 2–3.
Take the closed annulus
A={x∈R2:1≤∥x∥≤2}.
(3)
An exact deformation retraction onto its inner circle is
H(x,t)=((1−t)+∥x∥t​)x,r(x)=∥x∥x​.
(4)
The denominator is nonzero everywhere on the annulus. If the starting radius is ρ=∥x∥, its radius at time t is (1−t)ρ+t. This lies between 1 and ρ≤2, verifying that the entire homotopy stays in the original annulus. When ρ=1, the point is stationary, as required.
For x=(0,2), times 0,1/2,1 give (0,2),(0,3/2),(0,1). A radius-3/2 loop similarly becomes a radius-5/4 loop halfway through, then the unit circle. Its angle and winding number are preserved. This is useful because computing the fundamental group of the annulus reduces to computing that of the circle; the explicit homotopy equivalence establishes why the answer transfers.
