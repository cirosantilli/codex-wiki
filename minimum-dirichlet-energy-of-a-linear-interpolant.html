Minimum Dirichlet energy of a linear interpolant (∫ab​∣u′∣2=∫ab​∣s′∣2+∫ab​∣u′−s′∣2)

 ... Mathematics Area of mathematics Analysis Uniform approximation Spline (mathematics) Linear spline

For nodes a=x0​<⋯<xn​=b and prescribed real node values, let s be the linear spline joining them. Among all absolutely continuous functions u with the same node values and square-integrable derivative, s uniquely minimizes the Dirichlet energy ∫ab​∣u′∣2.
On each node interval, s′ is constant and u−s vanishes at both endpoints. The fundamental theorem of calculus therefore gives ∫s′(u′−s′)=s′[(u−s)(xi+1​)−(u−s)(xi​)]=0. Expanding the square and summing proves the displayed energy decomposition. Its final term is nonnegative; equality forces u′=s′ almost everywhere and the endpoint values fix the additive constant, proving uniqueness. In particular, the minimum is ∑i​(u(xi+1​)−u(xi​))2/(xi+1​−xi​). If all interior node values are zero and there is at least one interior node, only the two endpoint intervals contribute to this minimum.
