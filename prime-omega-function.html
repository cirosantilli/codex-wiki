Prime omega function (ω(n))

 Home Mathematics Area of mathematics Number theory Arithmetic function
OurBigBook.com Words: 314 Articles: 3
The prime omega function ω(n) counts the distinct prime factors of a positive integer n, so it is the cardinality of the prime support of an integer P(n). For a prime factorization with distinct prime numbers p1​,…,pr​ and positive exponents,
n=p1a1​​⋯prar​​⟹ω(n)=r=∑p∣n​1.
(92)
At n=1 there are no prime factors, so the empty sum gives ω(1)=0. The total number of prime factors instead gives Ω(n)=∑i​ai​. [ref]
For 72=23⋅32, the prime support of an integer is {2,3} and ω(72)=2. The comparison examples give ω(30)=3, ω(32)=1 and ω(36)=2. Raising an existing prime factor to a higher power does not affect the prime omega function: ω(pk)=1 for a prime number p and k≥1. Thus the prime omega function is a strongly additive arithmetic function, with
ω(ab)=ω(a)+ω(b)if gcd(a,b)=1.
(93)
The reason is that coprime integers have disjoint prime supports of an integer. For arbitrary positive integers, their prime support of an integer combines by set union, and the shared prime factors are exactly those of the greatest common divisor. Counting this set union gives the more general identity
ω(ab)=ω(a)+ω(b)−ω(gcd(a,b)).
(94)
For a=12, b=18, both prime supports of an integer are {2,3} and gcd(a,b)=6, so ω(216)=2+2−2=2. In particular ω(4)=1=2=ω(2)+ω(2): the prime omega function is not a completely additive arithmetic function.
The prime omega function is useful for counting choices involving distinct prime factors. Every squarefree divisor of n chooses a subset of its prime support of an integer, so there are 2ω(n) squarefree divisors. For 72, this gives 22=4, namely 1,2,3,6. Also ω(rad(n))=ω(n), because the radical of an integer keeps exactly that prime support of an integer. The total number of prime factors records what this count discards, and the two counts agree exactly on squarefree integers.
