Concentration of a beta prior near zero (P(Θ>t)=(1−t)β)

 ... Mathematics Area of mathematics Probability and statistics Probability theory Probability distribution Beta distribution
OurBigBook.com Words: 82
For Θ∼Beta(1,β), direct integration of its probability density function gives the displayed tail for 0<t<1. Increasing β strictly decreases every such tail probability, and Θ converges in probability to zero as β→∞. Its mean is 1/(β+1) and its variance is β/[(β+1)2(β+2)]; both decrease for β>1. For the variance, logarithmic differentiation gives 1/β−2/(β+1)−1/(β+2)<0 in that range.
After S successes in N conditionally independent Bernoulli trials, Beta-binomial conjugacy gives posterior mean (S+1)/(N+β+1). Its derivative with respect to β is negative, while the weight (β+1)/(N+β+1) assigned to the prior mean is increasing for N>0.
