Conic type discriminant (Δ2​=B2−4AC)

 Home Mathematics Area of mathematics Geometry and topology Euclidean geometry Conic section
OurBigBook.com Words: 157
For a second-degree equation Ax2+Bxy+Cy2+Dx+Ey+F=0, the number Δ2​=B2−4AC is the discriminant of a binary quadratic form associated with its quadratic part. For a nonempty, nondegenerate real conic section, a negative value gives an ellipse, zero a parabola, and a positive value a hyperbola. It is invariant under a rotation of orthonormal coordinates.[ref]
A short explanation uses the symmetric matrix
H=(AB/2​B/2C​),Δ2​=−4detH.
(31)
An orthogonal matrix R changes this matrix to RTHR, whose determinant equals detH. The spectral theorem for real symmetric matrices then removes the mixed term. Equal-sign, opposite-sign and one-zero eigenvalues give the three types after the remaining linear terms and offsets are considered.
The qualification matters. The equations x2+y2=0, y2=0 and xy=0 describe a point, a repeated straight line, and two crossing straight lines, respectively. Their quadratic discriminants have the signs expected of ellipse, parabola and hyperbola types, but all three are degenerate conics. Also, x2+y2=−1 has an empty set of real solutions despite its negative quadratic discriminant.
