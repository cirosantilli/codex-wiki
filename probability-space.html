Probability space ((Ω,F,P))

 Home Mathematics Area of mathematics Probability and statistics Probability theory
OurBigBook.com Words: 2k Articles: 19
A probability space is a measure space (Ω,F,P) whose total measure is one. The sample space Ω contains the elementary outcomes; the sigma-algebra F contains the events to which the model assigns probabilities; the probability measure P supplies those values. The sigma-algebra lets us combine observable questions using complements and countable unions while remaining within the model.
For two independent uniform dice, take Ω={1,…,6}2, F=P(Ω), and give each ordered pair mass 1/36. A random variable might record the sum, the larger result, or whether both results are even integers. These are different functions on the same sample space, rather than different elementary experiments. A probability distribution records the mass of the values of one such random variable and can forget information retained by the full probability space.
