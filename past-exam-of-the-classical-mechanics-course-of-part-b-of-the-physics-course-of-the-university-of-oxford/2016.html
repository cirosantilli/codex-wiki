In a frame rotating with angular velocity Ω, a particle of relative velocity v experiences the Coriolis force
FC​=−2mΩ×v.​
(1)
For nearly horizontal atmospheric motion, the relevant vertical rotation component is Ωsinℓ, with latitude ℓ. In the northern hemisphere the horizontal deflection is to the right of the motion. A low-pressure center produces an inward pressure-gradient force; an anticlockwise circulation has an outward Coriolis force to oppose it. A high-pressure center instead supports clockwise circulation, with inward Coriolis force opposing the outward pressure-gradient force.
The sense reverses in the southern hemisphere. There, anticlockwise circulation is anticyclonic and corresponds to high atmospheric pressure. Hence
Earth northern hemisphere: low anticlockwise, high clockwise; Jupiter’s Great Red Spot: high pressure.​
(2)
This is the large-scale rotating-fluid interpretation; friction, centrifugal acceleration and departures from ideal balance modify actual wind profiles. NASA's description independently identifies the southern, counterclockwise Great Red Spot as a high-pressure system.[ref]

Let R(α) denote the planar rotation matrix, and choose the initial cup center on the positive x axis, as in the requested equations. Its trajectory is C(t)=R(cosΩt,sinΩt). The handle rotates relative to the radial line at signed rate ϕ, so its absolute angular velocity is ν=Ω+ϕ. A positive recurrence time is 2π/∣ϕ∣; the sign records rotation direction.
Writing q=(x1​,y1​), the relation is
r=C+R(νt)q,​
(3)
or explicitly
x=RcosΩt+x1​cosνt−y1​sinνt,y=RsinΩt+x1​sinνt+y1​cosνt.
(4)
One obtains the same result by rotating cup coordinates through ϕt into the radially aligned axes, then rotating through Ωt into the inertial axes.
At ϕ=−Ω, ν=0, and
x=RcosΩt+x1​,y=RsinΩt+y1​.​
(5)
The axes maintain a fixed orientation in inertial space while their origin accelerates around a circle. This is translation without rotation of the cup frame; it is not an inertial frame because the origin accelerates. In particular its inertial correction is a spatially uniform fictitious force, rather than a Coriolis force from rotation of these axes.

Treat the marble as a frictionless point particle; no size, rolling constraint or friction law is supplied. Neglect its vertical kinetic energy as requested, but retain the gravitational potential energy mgz=mω2(x12​+y12​)/2.
For completeness keep the general constant cup rotation rate ν=Ω+ϕ. In cup components the center's velocity is w=RΩ(sinϕt,cosϕt). With J(x,y)=(−y,x), the horizontal velocity norm is ∣q˙​+νJq+w∣. Thus
L=2m​[(x˙1​−νy1​+RΩsinϕt)2+(y˙​1​+νx1​+RΩcosϕt)2]−2mω2​(x12​+y12​).​
(6)
In the nonrotating translating case ϕ=−Ω, this reduces to
L=2m​[(x˙1​−RΩsinΩt)2+(y˙​1​+RΩcosΩt)2]−2mω2​(x12​+y12​).​
(7)

A constant of motion is a function I(q,p,t) whose total derivative vanishes along every physical trajectory. It may depend explicitly on time; arbitrary functions of conserved quantities are also conserved, so a count normally concerns a specified independent set or symmetry generators.
For a fixed-time infinitesimal variation δqi​=ϵξi​(q,t), suppose δL=ϵdB/dt. On a solution of the Euler-Lagrange equations,
ϵδL​=∑i​(Lqi​​ξi​+pi​ξ˙​i​)=dtd​∑i​pi​ξi​.
(15)
Therefore Noether theorem gives
I=i∑​pi​ξi​−B,dtdI​=0.​
(16)
For a transformation also changing time by δt=ϵτ, the action variation includes Ld(δt)/dt, and the transformed velocity varies by ϵ(ξ˙​i​−q˙​i​τ˙). The same integration by parts gives the general point-symmetry charge
I=i∑​pi​ξi​−Hτ−B.​
(17)
Thus a time-translation symmetry gives the energy, a spatial translation gives the corresponding total momentum, and a rotation gives its angular-momentum generator. The required symmetry is a symmetry of the action, allowing a boundary term; demanding that the numerical value of the Lagrangian remain strictly unchanged is unnecessarily restrictive. Conversely, explicit time dependence of a Lagrangian generally prevents conservation of its energy even while other charges survive.[ref]

Assume positive, pairwise distinct masses and generic nonzero ω,λ. Let pxi​​=mi​x˙i​, and similarly for y,z. The three uncoupled x oscillators each conserve
Exi​​=2mi​pxi​2​​+21​mi​ω2xi2​(i=1,2,3).​
(18)
Uniform translation of all yi​ conserves Py​=∑i​pyi​​. The z1​,z2​ interaction permits their common translation, while z3​ is entirely free, giving two independent momenta Pz,12​=pz1​​+pz2​​ and pz3​​. The interacting z1​,z2​ subsystem is autonomous and has its own conserved energy. A useful basic collection is therefore
Py​,Pz,12​,pz3​​,Ez,12​=2m1​pz1​2​​+2m2​pz2​2​​+V(z1​−z2​),Ex1​​,Ex2​​,Ex3​​.​
(19)
The total energy is generally not conserved. With κ=λ(m1​−m2​), its derivative is H˙=−κsin(κt)U12​(y1​−y2​), which is generically nonzero.
Unequal masses do not remove every rotation charge in the oscillator sector. Put Xi​=mi​​xi​, Pi​=pxi​​/mi​​. This is a canonical transformation and makes that sector an isotropic harmonic oscillator. Its rotations conserve
Jij​=Xi​Pj​−Xj​Pi​=mj​mi​​​xi​pxj​​−mi​mj​​​xj​pxi​​.​
(20)
These mass-weighted oscillator symmetries exist already at distinct masses. The energies and three rotation charges are not all functionally independent; the three-oscillator sector has at most five independent time-independent integrals on regular trajectories.
There are further simple explicitly time-dependent constants, such as xi​cosωt−(x˙i​/ω)sinωt and xi​sinωt+(x˙i​/ω)cosωt, and center-of-mass boost constants. For example MY−tPy​ is constant for M=∑i​mi​ and Y=∑i​mi​yi​/M. These illustrate why an unrestricted raw number of conserved functions is not meaningful.

At m1​=m2​, the explicitly time-dependent coefficient becomes one. This restores time-translation symmetry, and the total energy becomes conserved. No further analogous restoration occurs merely from setting m3​ equal to them: the three arbitrary interaction functions still distinguish their arguments and particle labels.
If the question counts the extra ordinary unweighted rotations of the x coordinate labels, the conventional generator count is as follows. For m1​=m2​=m3​, rotations of (x1​,x2​) preserve both the kinetic and oscillator terms, so one ordinarily displayed rotation charge joins the restored energy: two displayed generators beyond the elementary list. For three equal masses, the full ordinary rotation group of (x1​,x2​,x3​) has three generators; together with energy this gives four, or two more than in the preceding equal-pair case.
There is a necessary qualification to interpreting those numbers as genuinely new independent conservation laws. The weighted charges in solution deform continuously to those same ordinary rotation charges at equality. For example J12​ is conserved for every positive m1​,m2​ and becomes x1​px2​​−x2​px1​​ when the masses agree. Thus
Restricted ordinary-coordinate generator count: 2 and 4.New generic energy conservation: 1 at m1​=m2​;0 further at m3​=m1​.​​
(21)
The restriction must be stated if using the first count. Equal mass does not create three previously absent independent oscillator integrals. If λ=0, energy conservation was already present; special choices of the interaction functions can add further symmetries and are excluded by the generic assumption.

For an action functional S[q]=∫ti​tf​​L(q,q˙​,t)dt, vary a path while fixing its endpoint coordinates. Integration by parts gives
δS=[∑i​∂q˙​i​∂L​δqi​]ti​tf​​+∫ti​tf​​∑i​[∂qi​∂L​−dtd​∂q˙​i​∂L​]δqi​dt.
(23)
The endpoint term vanishes. Since the interior variations are arbitrary, stationarity gives
dtd​∂q˙​i​∂L​−∂qi​∂L​=0.​
(24)
Define canonical momentum pi​=∂L/∂q˙​i​. For a regular velocity Hessian, these relations can be inverted for q˙​(q,p,t), and the Legendre transform defines
H(q,p,t)=i∑​pi​q˙​i​−L(q,q˙​,t).​
(25)
Its differential is dH=∑i​(q˙​i​dpi​−Lqi​​dqi​)−Lt​dt. Inserting the Euler-Lagrange equations already suggests the canonical equations.
To derive them directly from an principle of stationary action, vary the first-order action I[q,p]=∫(∑i​pi​q˙​i​−H)dt with independent q,p and fixed endpoint q. Integrating pi​δq˙​i​ by parts gives
δI=∫∑i​[(q˙​i​−Hpi​​)δpi​+(−p˙​i​−Hqi​​)δqi​]dt.
(26)
Therefore
q˙​i​=∂pi​∂H​,p˙​i​=−∂qi​∂H​.​
(27)
The endpoint momenta need not be fixed. A singular velocity Hessian instead requires a constrained Hamiltonian formulation; the ordinary Legendre inversion is an assumption, not an identity valid for every Lagrangian.

For n degrees of freedom, phase space is the 2n-dimensional space of canonical coordinates (q1​,…,qn​,p1​,…,pn​). Initial data at one point determine a trajectory under the Hamilton equations.
Liouville's theorem in Hamiltonian mechanics states that this flow preserves the canonical volume element dΓ=∏i​dqi​dpi​. Its divergence is
∇q,p​⋅(q˙​,p˙​)=∑i​(∂qi​∂pi​∂2H​−∂pi​∂qi​∂2H​)=0.
(28)
If J(t) is the Jacobian determinant of the flow from initial data, the determinant evolution formula gives J˙=J∇⋅(q˙​,p˙​)=0. Since J(0)=1, it remains one, proving preservation of every advected phase volume. The proof also applies to smooth explicitly time-dependent Hamiltonians.
For an ensemble density ρ(q,p,t), probability conservation and the same vanishing divergence give
∂t​ρ+i∑​(q˙​i​∂qi​​ρ+p˙​i​∂pi​​ρ)=0,or∂t​ρ+{ρ,H}=0.​
(29)
Thus the fine-grained density is constant along the Hamiltonian trajectories, although the ensemble may shear, stretch and develop fine structure. This is not a claim that coordinate-space volume or every lower-dimensional projection is conserved.

For a truly isolated Hamiltonian bunch, Liouville's theorem preserves the fine-grained volume in its full 6N-dimensional phase space. Hamiltonian manipulation cannot increase its full phase-space density by compressing all canonical position and momentum directions simultaneously. Merely focusing the beam to a smaller transverse spot generally increases angular or momentum divergence; it does not by itself reduce beam emittance.
The distinction between full volume and projected spreads matters. Coupling transverse and longitudinal degrees of freedom can exchange emittance or move spread into other coordinates and correlations, so a projected transverse quantity may decrease without reducing the complete phase volume. That is redistribution, not cooling of every degree of freedom. In linear uncoupled optics, preservation of each transverse canonical area makes the spot-size/divergence tradeoff particularly direct.
Actual cooling allows the beam to exchange energy and entropy with other systems. In electron cooling, a colder comoving electron population receives the protons' random kinetic energy and is subsequently renewed or cooled. In stochastic cooling, a pickup measures deviations and a downstream kicker applies feedback corrections; the electronics, measurement noise and environment belong to the enlarged system. CERN's description identifies exactly this pickup-and-kicker arrangement.[ref]
Thus these methods relax the idealization that the proton bunch alone is isolated. Including the cooling apparatus does not require the complete closed dynamics to violate Liouville's theorem. For protons, direct radiative damping is much weaker than for electrons, so stochastic or electron cooling is a more relevant practical suggestion than relying on strong synchrotron damping.

Fix an initial event (qi​,ti​). For each reachable final event (qf​,tf​), evaluate the action on a classical path satisfying those endpoints. On a chosen local branch this produces Hamilton's principal function S(qf​,tf​), rather than a functional of every possible path. Multiple classical paths may make this function multivalued, so the derivation is local to a branch.
Allow the final event to vary. The integrated interior variation vanishes by the Euler-Lagrange equations. The endpoint variation must distinguish the total coordinate change from the change at fixed time: δq(tf​)=δqf​−q˙​f​δtf​. Consequently
δS=∑i​pi,f​(δqi,f​−q˙​i,f​δtf​)+Lf​δtf​=∑i​pi,f​δqi,f​−Hf​δtf​.
(32)
Hence pi​=∂S/∂qi​ and ∂S/∂t=−H. Eliminating the momenta yields the Hamilton-Jacobi equation
∂t∂S​+H(q,∂q∂S​,t)=0.​
(33)
A complete integral of the Hamilton-Jacobi equation depends on n suitable independent constants for n coordinates; differentiating it with respect to those constants supplies the conjugate integration constants needed to reconstruct trajectories.

An autonomous Hamiltonian system with n degrees of freedom is Liouville integrable if it has n conserved functions I1​,…,In​, including the Hamiltonian, whose differentials are independent on a regular open set and which are in involution:
dI1​∧⋯∧dIn​=0,{Ii​,Ij​}=0.​
(40)
The equations can then be reduced locally to quadratures. The statement about action-angle coordinates on invariant tori additionally requires compact connected regular common level sets; integrable unbounded motion need not be periodic or lie on a torus.

For the uniform-gravity Hamiltonian, take the three integrals px​,py​,H. Since the potential does not depend on x,y,
{px​,py​}=0,{px​,H}=−∂x​H=0,{py​,H}=0.
(41)
For g=0, the mgdz term in dH makes these three differentials independent. Thus the system is Liouville integrable, as the explicit separated solution already demonstrates.
Replacing mgz by a sufficiently smooth arbitrary function f(z) preserves the same commuting integrals. They are independent wherever pz​ and f′(z) do not vanish simultaneously. The vertical motion reduces to
2mpz2​​+f(z)=Ez​,t−t0​=±2m​​∫z0​z​Ez​−f(ζ)​dζ​.​
(42)
The horizontal motion remains uniform. Thus an elementary antiderivative is unnecessary: the system is still integrable by a one-dimensional quadrature on regular branches, joined at turning points. Critical equilibria may be singular common level sets without destroying generic integrability. If f is constant, the three momenta themselves give an everywhere independent commuting set, avoiding the rank loss of px​,py​,H at pz​=0.
