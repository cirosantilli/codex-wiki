Sufficient statistic (Lθ​(X∣T)=K(T,⋅))

 Home Mathematics Area of mathematics Probability and statistics
OurBigBook.com Words: 2k Articles: 15
A statistic T=T(X) is sufficient for a statistical model {Pθ​:θ∈Θ} when the conditional distribution of the full data X given T has a version independent of the unknown statistical parameter θ. Once T is recorded, the remaining uncertainty about the data's arrangement can be generated without knowing θ. [ref]
For data and statistic taking values in standard Borel spaces, express this precisely with one Markov kernel K, common to every statistical parameter value:
Pθ​(X∈B∣T)=K(T,B)Pθ​-almost surely,θ∈Θ,
(61)
for each measurable event B. Thus K(t,⋅) is a regular conditional distribution of X given T=t. Equality is only required outside a null set for the distribution of T under each θ; the choice of version matters on impossible conditioning events. For a continuous probability distribution, the event T=t may have zero probability even at meaningful observations, so the definition uses a regular conditional distribution, rather than division by Pθ​(T=t).
A sufficient statistic is a property of a specified statistical model, rather than of a formula for summarizing data in isolation. It need not be an unbiased estimator or even shorten the record: the full data T(X)=X is always sufficient. An estimator instead targets a quantity such as a mean. An ancillary statistic has a parameter-independent marginal probability distribution, whereas a sufficient statistic has a parameter-independent conditional full-data law. A minimal sufficient statistic permits the greatest reduction among sufficient statistics. A complete statistic satisfies a separate condition about zero expectations; a complete sufficient statistic must satisfy both conditions.
