Runge-Kutta method

 Home Mathematics Area of mathematics Analysis Numerical analysis One-step method
OurBigBook.com Words: 3k Articles: 46
A Runge-Kutta method advances an initial value problem y′=f(t,y), y(t0​)=y0​, by sampling its vector field at several trial states within one time step. These samples are Runge-Kutta stages; a weighted combination of their slopes produces the next state. The construction improves on the single tangent line used by the Euler method while requiring evaluations of f, rather than explicit higher derivatives of the solution. It applies to scalar equations and systems of ordinary differential equations alike. The coefficient choices distinguish methods such as Heun's method, the classical fourth-order Runge-Kutta method and implicit Runge-Kutta methods.
For s stages, write A=(aij​) for the stage matrix, ci​ for relative time nodes and bi​ for output weights. A time step of length h computes
Yi​=yn​+h∑j=1s​aij​kj​,ki​=f(tn​+ci​h,Yi​),yn+1​=yn​+h∑i=1s​bi​ki​.
(1)
Here ki​ is a slope, with units of state per time; hki​ is a state increment. Another common convention writes Ki​=hki​, so its stage states are Yi​=yn​+∑j​aij​Kj​ and its output is yn​+∑i​bi​Ki​. These are equivalent conventions: including h both inside Ki​ and again in the output would count the time step twice. The textbook formulation uses the increment convention.[ref]
Usually the Butcher tableau obeys ci​=∑j​aij​, called internal consistency. Stage i then combines slopes over an effective time ci​h. The second-order conditions with independent Runge-Kutta abscissae explain what changes when time nodes are specified independently. A Runge-Kutta stage is generally an auxiliary approximation, not a separate accepted solution value at its node. Several stages can even have the same time node but different state arguments, as in the classical fourth-order Runge-Kutta method.
The useful idea is to approximate the integral y(tn​+h)−y(tn​)=∫tn​tn​+h​f(t,y(t))dt without knowing the intermediate exact trajectory. Trial states provide places to sample f, and Butcher order conditions make the resulting combination agree with the exact Taylor expansion. More stages alone do not guarantee a higher order of a numerical method: their coefficients must satisfy the appropriate conditions. Embedded Runge-Kutta pairs reuse these same samples for a second output and a practical local error estimator.
