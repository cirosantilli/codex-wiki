Nuclear force

 Home Physics Nuclear physics
OurBigBook.com Words: 1k Articles: 11
The residual strong interaction between nucleons. Its long-distance part involves pion exchange and depends on spin and isospin. It is distinct from directly exchanging gluons between color-singlet nucleons.
Why do protons and neutrons bind into an atomic nucleus, even though protons repel electrically? An attractive nuclear force can lower the total energy below that of separated nucleons, producing nuclear binding energy. The deuteron, containing one proton and one neutron, is the simplest such bound state.
In Quantum chromodynamics, nucleons are composite colour singlets built from quarks and gluons. Their residual nuclear force has a longest-range strong contribution from one-pion exchange, with additional interactions at shorter distances.[ref]

Nuclear matter saturation

Words: 38
Nuclear force OurBigBook.com
The approximately bounded binding energy per nucleon and approximately constant interior density of well-bound medium and heavy nuclei. The short effective range and many-body character of the nuclear force prevent each nucleon from binding independently to every other nucleon with an unsuppressed long-range attraction.

Neutron-proton radiative capture (np→dγ)

Words: 29
Nuclear force OurBigBook.com
Formation of a deuteron from a neutron and a proton with photon emission. For an initial pair at rest, two-body energy and momentum conservation gives Eγ​=B−B2/[2(mp​+mn​)] in natural units, where B=mp​+mn​−md​. The missing binding energy becomes nuclear recoil.

Diproton

Words: 29
Nuclear force OurBigBook.com
A two-proton system. Its low-energy spin-singlet strong channel does not give a vacuum bound state, and electrostatic repulsion further opposes binding. This is distinct from proton pairing correlations within an atomic nucleus.

Dineutron

Words: 32
Nuclear force OurBigBook.com
A two-neutron system. At low orbital angular momentum its identical-fermion exchange symmetry forces the spin-singlet, isospin-one channel; there is no bound dineutron in vacuum at physical parameters. This does not forbid correlated neutron pairs inside larger nuclei.

Charge independence of nuclear forces

Words: 41
Nuclear force OurBigBook.com
The approximate invariance of the strong nuclear force under isospin rotations. It compares channels with the same spin, orbital and total-isospin quantum numbers; it does not identify the spin-triplet isospin-zero deuteron channel with the spin-singlet isospin-one neutron-neutron channel. Electromagnetic effects and quark mass differences break the approximation.

Yukawa meson theory of nuclear forces

Words: 752 Articles: 2
Nuclear force OurBigBook.com
In 1935, Hideki Yukawa proposed a new massive exchange quantum to explain nucleon binding. His paper began with the inadequacy of an electron–neutrino exchange description of the binding strength. It then required a potential energy falling faster with distance than a Coulomb potential, introduced an exponentially decaying field, and related its inverse range to the exchange particle's rest mass. This was a theoretical proposal, not a detection with an apparatus of his own.[ref]
The crucial idea is that a finite rest mass supplies a length scale even in empty space. In a scalar model, the static limit of a Klein-Gordon equation produces the modified Helmholtz equation. Its decaying Green's function gives the Yukawa potential. The derivation below isolates this range mechanism; one-pion exchange also carries spin and isospin structure.
The nuclear emulsion track experiment used developed photographic emulsions exposed to cosmic rays and inspected microscopically. Stopping pions gave daughter muon tracks with closely grouped ranges. Comparing penetrating muons with pion-induced nuclear breakup distinguished their interactions; this was not a direct static force measurement. The muon is a lepton, not Yukawa's strongly interacting meson.[ref]

Static Yukawa Green function (Gκ​(r)=e−κr/(4πr))

Words: 275
Yukawa meson theory of nuclear forces OurBigBook.com
Let κ>0 be an inverse length. In three spatial dimensions, seek the Green's function that decays at infinity and satisfies
(−∇2+κ2)Gκ​(r)=δ(3)(r),r=∣r∣,
(1)
where ∇2 is the Laplacian and δ(3) the three-dimensional Dirac delta function. The point source and the differential operator have spherical symmetry. For r>0, the radial Laplacian gives
Gκ′′​+r2​Gκ′​−κ2Gκ​=0.
(2)
Writing u(r)=rGκ​(r) reduces this to u′′−κ2u=0. Decay at infinity removes the growing exponential function, leaving Gκ​(r)=Ce−κr/r.
The Dirac delta function fixes the coefficient. Integrating over a ball Bϵ​ and using the divergence theorem gives
−4πϵ2Gκ′​(ϵ)+κ2∫Bϵ​​Gκ​d3r=1.
(3)
The first term tends to 4πC, while the second is O(ϵ2). Hence C=1/(4π) and
Gκ​(r)=4πre−κr​.
(4)
This solves the source equation in the sense of distributions, including at the origin. Merely verifying the equation for r>0 would leave the normalization undetermined. The limit κ→0 at fixed r>0 is 1/(4πr), the Green function of the Laplacian for the convention −∇2.
For an attractive scalar toy interaction, absorb constants into A>0 and set
V(r)=−Are−κr​,Fr​=−drdV​=−Ar2e−κr​(1+κr).
(5)
Here A has units of energy times length, and negative Fr​ points inward. At equal A, comparison with the unscreened model V0​=−A/r gives
∣V0​∣∣V∣​=e−x,∣F0r​∣∣Fr​∣​=(1+x)e−x,x=κr.
(6)
Thus the potential energy and force have the same exponential scale but different suppression factors. At x=1, these ratios are approximately 0.368 and 0.736; at x=3, they are 0.0498 and 0.199. Finite range means rapid decay, not an abrupt cutoff.

Mass and range of a nuclear exchange field (λ=ℏ/(mc))

Words: 291
Yukawa meson theory of nuclear forces OurBigBook.com
A free scalar Klein-Gordon equation with rest mass m is
(c21​∂t2∂2​−∇2+ℏ2m2c2​)ϕ=0.
(7)
Here c is the speed of light and ℏ the reduced Planck constant. In the static limit, comparison with the static Yukawa Green function identifies
κ=ℏmc​,λ=κ−1=mcℏ​=mc2ℏc​.
(8)
This λ is the reduced Compton wavelength, rather than h/(mc); the two differ by 2π. It is an exponential decay length of this exchange field, not the geometric size of a nucleon or an exact radius of a bound state. This article uses λ for a length; the 1935 paper used that letter for its inverse.
For a deliberately rounded worked example, stipulate ℏc=197.3MeVfm and mc2=140MeV. Then
λ=140197.3​fm=1.4092857…fm≈1.41fm,κ≈0.70958fm−1.
(9)
A femtometre is 10−15m. The chosen rest energy is a convenient pion-scale input; neither it nor the resulting Yukawa potential is asserted to be an exact measured nuclear interaction. Holding ℏc fixed, chosen rest energies 70, 140 and 280MeV give lengths 2.819, 1.409 and 0.7046fm. Doubling the exchange rest mass halves the decay length.
Range alone does not guarantee binding: the potential energy must overcome the kinetic energy cost of localizing a wavefunction. The existing exponential variational bound for the Yukawa potential makes this precise. For the same toy V, with reduced mass M, choosing a normalized exponential wavefunction proportional to e−αr with α=κ/2 yields
⟨H⟩=8Mℏ2κ2​−8Aκ​.
(10)
For additional stipulated inputs Mc2=470MeV and A=100MeVfm, this gives ⟨H⟩≈−3.66MeV. A negative value proves a bound state in this scalar model by the quantum variational principle; it is an upper bound on its ground-state energy, not a prediction for the deuteron. A nonnegative trial value would not prove the absence of binding.

One-pion exchange

Words: 129 Articles: 2
Nuclear force OurBigBook.com
Although the pion has spin zero, one-pion exchange depends on nucleon spin and isospin and includes a nuclear tensor force. Shorter-range and multiple-nucleon interactions also matter. The scalar Yukawa potential isolates a range mechanism rather than specifying the complete nuclear force.[ref]

Nuclear tensor force (S12​=3(σ1​⋅r^)(σ2​⋅r^)−σ1​⋅σ2​)

Words: 83 Articles: 1
One-pion exchange OurBigBook.com
The linear operator VT​(r)S12​ couples nucleon spin orientations to their separation direction r^. Here σi​ are Pauli matrices. A scalar radial potential energy alone omits this directional dependence.

Deuteron quadrupole evidence for a tensor force

Words: 60
Nuclear tensor force OurBigBook.com
An isotropic pure L=0 spatial state has zero electric quadrupole moment. The nonzero deuteron quadrupole moment consequently requires anisotropic structure. With ground-state JP=1+, a nuclear tensor force can mix 3S1​ and 3D1​ waves while preserving total angular momentum and parity. A purely central spin-independent potential cannot produce this mixing. The inference of an orientation-dependent force was already explicit in 1939.[ref]
