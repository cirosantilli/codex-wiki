The ideal operational-amplifier rules are zero input currents and, when stable negative feedback maintains unsaturated linear operation, equal input voltages. Infinite open-loop gain motivates the latter; it must not be assumed to guarantee asymptotic stability for every external network.

The formal amplitude falls from 5 to zero, with high-frequency slope −40 dB per decade. Its unwrapped phase rises from zero through +90∘ at ω=104s−1 to +180∘; the corresponding characteristic frequency is 1.59 kHz. At that frequency the amplitude is 2.5.
These are formal sinusoidal particular-solution curves, not a stable settled filter response. For the formal sinusoidal response of an unstable linear system, replacing iω by the Laplace variable s gives G(s)=5/(1−sτ)2, with a repeated pole at s=+1/τ. A homogeneous output contains (A+Bt)et/τ and grows, so real perturbations lead to supply-limited nonlinear behaviour. With equal positive resistors and capacitors in the frequency-setting network, the general denominator is 1+(3−K)sτ+s2τ2: the Sallen-Key equal-component stability bound is K<3. For example K=3−2​ gives a Butterworth filter response. Thus the Sallen-Key low-pass filter architecture is useful for active filtering with suitable gain and damping, but the stated K=5 component set is unsuitable for linear filtering. The component values are retained in this diagnosis.[ref]
