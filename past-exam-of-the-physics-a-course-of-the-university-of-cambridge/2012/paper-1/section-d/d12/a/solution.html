A chi-squared test compares discrepancies with their expected random size. For independent Gaussian observations with known standard deviations, a fitted model gives
χ2=∑i=1n​σi2​[yi​−f(xi​;θ)]2​.
(27)
Under a correct regular model with p fitted parameters, its reference statistical degrees of freedom are usually ν=n−p. Correlated errors instead require χ2=rTC−1r with the correct covariance matrix. The reduced statistic has mean about one and standard deviation 2/ν​, so it should not be forced to equal exactly one.
For binned counts, Pearson's statistic is ∑i​(Oi​−Ei​)2/Ei​. With a fixed total and k populated categories, fitting p distribution parameters generally gives ν=k−1−p. The approximation needs adequately populated expected bins and the appropriate dependence assumptions. Sparse data may require a Poisson likelihood, exact calculation or simulated reference distribution rather than a casual chi-squared approximation.
The upper-tail probability pvalue​=P(χν2​≥χobs2​) measures how unusual the discrepancy would be under the null model; it is not the probability that the model is true. A large statistic can indicate a wrong model, underestimated noise or unmodelled systematic effects; an unusually small one can indicate overestimated errors or correlations. Residual plots and checks of the noise model are needed alongside the numerical test.
