A compatible basis has J2=ℏ2j(j+1) and Jz​=mℏ, where m=−j,−j+1,…,j. ladder operators change m by one, terminate at ±j, and give 2j+1 magnetic substates. orbital angular momentum is L=r×p=−iℏr×∇. Its angular eigenfunctions are spherical harmonics, with integer ℓ arising from their spatial rotation properties and single-valuedness. spin is intrinsic angular momentum, not literal rotation of a finite charged sphere. It may be half-integer; spin 1/2 is represented by S=ℏσ/2. For several contributions, J=L+S obeys the same algebra. Coupling angular momenta j1​,j2​ permits J=∣j1​−j2​∣,…,j1​+j2​, with Clebsch-Gordan coefficients relating product and coupled bases. spin-orbit coupling can distinguish these total-angular-momentum sectors.
The Stern-Gerlach apparatus sends a collimated neutral atomic beam through an inhomogeneous magnetic field and records its deflection. A magnetic moment experiences Fz​≃μz​∂Bz​/∂z; field uniformity would produce precession but no analogous net separating force. A classically continuous distribution of moment orientations would broaden the beam continuously. Gerlach and Stern instead observed separated silver-beam components. Reversing or removing the field and comparing the undeflected profile controls the magnetic origin; collimation, velocity spread and finite resolution affect the spots. The original experiment demonstrated space quantization; its modern silver interpretation uses the unpaired electron spin with nearly zero orbital angular momentum, rather than a direct measurement of a free electron's trajectory.[ref]

Their total S2 values are 2ℏ2 and zero, while S1​⋅S2​ has values ℏ2/4 and −3ℏ2/4. The singlet is rotationally invariant and gives anticorrelated spin measurements along a common axis. For identical fermions the complete state is antisymmetric: a symmetric spatial state pairs with a singlet spin state, whereas an antisymmetric spatial state pairs with triplet spin. Orbital, spin and coupled angular momenta share an algebra, but their physical origins and allowed representations differ.
