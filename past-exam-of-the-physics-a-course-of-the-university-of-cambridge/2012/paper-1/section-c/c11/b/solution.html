The Time-dependent Schrödinger equation iℏ∂t​∣ψ⟩=H(t)∣ψ⟩ governs unitary evolution of a closed system. If H is independent of time, expand in its energy eigenstates:

An individual energy eigenstate changes only by a phase, so its probability density and time-independent-observable expectations are stationary. Superpositions can have interference terms oscillating at (En​−Em​)/ℏ; fixed energy populations do not imply that every observable is constant.
For general H(t), U(t,t0​)=Texp[−(i/ℏ)∫H(t′)dt′] requires time ordering unless Quantum Hamiltonians at different times commute. cm(1)​(t)=−ℏi​∫t0​t​⟨m∣V(t′)∣n⟩ei(Em​−En​)t′/ℏdt′.

Its squared modulus gives the leading transition probability while depletion remains small. A nearly monochromatic perturbation emphasizes energy differences near its quantum of frequency; a continuum and suitable long-time limit yield Fermi's golden rule. These approximations have stated weak-coupling and time-resolution limits; they do not replace exact unitary dynamics in every regime.
