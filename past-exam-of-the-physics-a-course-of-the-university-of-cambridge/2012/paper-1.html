The Stern-Gerlach apparatus sends a collimated neutral atomic beam through an inhomogeneous magnetic field and records its deflection. In a finite potential barrier with E<V, the stationary Schrödinger equation has exponentially decaying and growing solutions, with local decay constant κ=2m(V−E)​/ℏ. Matching the wavefunction and its derivative at finite steps produces a transmitted probability current even though a classical particle at that energy could not cross. energy remains conserved. For a smooth thick barrier, the leading WKB transmission factor is
T∼exp[−2∫x1​x2​​ℏ2m[V(x)−E]​​dx]​.
(23)
For a rectangular barrier of height V0​, width d and 0<E<V0​, exact matching gives T=[1+V02​sinh2(κd)/(4E(V0​−E))]−1. Thickness and energy therefore affect transmission exponentially in the opaque limit; prefactors and near-turning-point corrections require more than the leading exponent.
alpha decay illustrates escape from a nuclear well through a Coulomb barrier. Bias, tip electronic structure and local electronic density of states also affect the current, so constant-current height is not purely geometric topography. For a photon scattering through angle θ from a free electron initially at rest, conserve energy and momentum. Eliminating the final electron momentum by its relativistic energy relation gives
mc2(E−E′)=EE′(1−cosθ),λ′−λ=mch​(1−cosθ)​,

χ2=∑i=1n​σi2​[yi​−f(xi​;θ)]2​.

For binned counts, Pearson's statistic is ∑i​(Oi​−Ei​)2/Ei​. With a fixed total and k populated categories, fitting p distribution parameters generally gives ν=k−1−p. For an experimental-noise interpretation of Nyquist's theorem, a resistor R in thermal equilibrium at temperature T has one-sided open-circuit voltage-noise spectral density
SV​(f)=4kB​TR​,⟨vn2​⟩=4kB​TRΔf​
(28)
in the classical range hf≪kB​T, over an approximately rectangular bandwidth Δf. Across a matched load, half the open-circuit voltage appears, giving available noise power kB​TΔf. This is thermal agitation, present without a mean current, and is distinct from shot noise. A real readout weights the spectrum with its transfer function: ⟨vout2​⟩=∫0∞​SV​(f)∣H(f)∣2df. Cooling, reduced bandwidth and suitable impedance/noise matching reduce measured noise; the resistor's noise does not simply disappear in an ideal amplifier model.[ref]
The bare name also has a sampling interpretation. The Nyquist–Shannon sampling theorem permits exact reconstruction of a bandlimited signal from sufficiently fast uniform samples. The practical condition is fs​>2fmax​, with an anti-aliasing filter before sampling; otherwise distinct continuous frequencies produce identical samples. Ideal reconstruction uses shifted sinc functions. Critical-rate equality needs an appropriate signal class and can fail for unrestricted edge-frequency sinusoids with unknown phase. Sampling fast enough prevents aliasing, but does not remove thermal noise. This distinction covers the source's unspecified use of “Nyquist's theorem”.[ref]

A closed conducting enclosure redistributes surface charge so that an empty interior is protected from an external static electric field. Grounding fixes a reference and drains deposited charge, but an ideal isolated closed conductor also provides inward electrostatic shielding. Real seams, apertures and cables need careful bonding, shielding and feedthrough filtering. For time-varying fields the relevant wavelength, conductivity, geometry and penetration depth matter; a static argument alone is insufficient. Differential wiring and small pickup-loop areas can complement shielding, while careless multiple ground connections create unwanted current loops.
Ordinary copper or aluminium does not generally exclude a static magnetic field. High-permeability shells instead guide magnetic flux around the protected volume. Their effectiveness depends on shape, continuity, openings and saturation; magnetic history and mechanical stress can worsen performance. Nested shields and degaussing can reduce residual fields. Superconducting shielding can exploit the Meissner effect, but trapped flux, openings and critical-field limits require attention.
At nonzero frequency, eddy currents in conducting shields oppose changing magnetic flux. The skin depth is
δ=μσω2​​​
(29)

High-permeability and conducting layers can therefore complement each other. Active compensation coils are an additional method, not passive shielding. Test performance by applying known fields along relevant axes and measuring the residual field or induced signal over the apparatus bandwidth; room-temperature shielding materials and electronics can themselves add noise.
