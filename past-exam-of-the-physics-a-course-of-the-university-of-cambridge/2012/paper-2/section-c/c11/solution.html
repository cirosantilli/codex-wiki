With periodic boundary conditions, each k state occupies volume (2π)3/V and has two spin states. Filling a sphere gives n=kF3​/(3π2) and
EF​=2me​ℏ2​(3π2n)2/3​,D(E)=2π21​(ℏ22me​​)3/2E​,D(EF​)=2EF​3n​​.
(23)
Here D is density of states per volume and per energy; the extensive g(E) is VD(E). Both spin states are already included.
For divalent magnesium, n=8.6×1028 m−3, giving
EF​=7.10 eV,D(EF​)=1.13×1047 J−1m−3=1.82×1028 eV−1m−3​.
(24)
At T≪TF​, only electrons within about kB​T of the Fermi surface change occupancy. The Sommerfeld heat capacity is
VCe​​=3π2​kB2​D(EF​)T=71.1T Jm−3K−1​.
(25)
Equivalently Ce,m​=0.995T mJmol−1K−1, with T in kelvin, so at room temperature it is about 0.299 Jmol−1K−1. A temperature was not specified for the estimate; the linear coefficient is the general result.
At sufficiently low temperature, an ordinary nonsuperconducting metal has C≃γT+βT3: electrons dominate at the lowest temperatures, acoustic phonons produce the cubic contribution, and the lattice approaches 3R per mole as temperature exceeds its characteristic Debye scale. Anharmonic expansion can distinguish measured Cp​ from CV​. In the room-temperature regime, T/TF​≃0.0036 since TF​≃8.24×104 K, so electron excitation is strongly Pauli-limited. The free-electron estimate is only about 1.2% of the classical 3R lattice scale. Calling it negligible means small relative to the lattice contribution, not identically zero or negligible as T→0. Real band structure and interactions can alter the coefficient.
