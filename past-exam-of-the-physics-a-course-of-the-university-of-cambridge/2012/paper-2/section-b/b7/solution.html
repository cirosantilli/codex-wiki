The angle in the source ray sketch is inside the gap. Two extra reflections add a full round trip. Comparing adjacent emerging rays on a common outgoing phase plane subtracts the lateral-path contribution, so the optical path difference is 2μdcosθ, rather than 2μd/cosθ. Thus
δ=2k0​μdcosθ​,δ=2πm

at transmission maxima. The two internal reflection phases add to an integer full cycle in the stated ideal symmetric-coating model. Snell's law, μsinθ=μ0​sinθout​, converts to the external direction. A fixed internal angle defines a cone and hence an equal-inclination ring in a focal plane.
For identical lossless mirrors the first transmitted amplitude has magnitude (1−R) times the incident amplitude. Each additional round trip multiplies it by Reiδ, because R is an intensity reflection coefficient for one mirror but the product of the two amplitude reflection magnitudes. Summing the geometric series gives

The maximum is one in this symmetric lossless limit; absorption, unequal mirrors or additional incoherent reflections change it. At fixed angle and negligible material dispersion, mλ=2μdcosθ and adjacent orders differ by one. For m≫1,
FSRλ​≃2μdcosθλ2​​,δλ≃FFSRλ​​.
(10)
order-safe angular scanning of a Fabry–Pérot spectrum should cover the approximately 38 nm spectral span within one order while making the instrumental width smaller than 1 nm. Near normal incidence, requiring the smallest approximate FSR to exceed the span gives μd≲5.3 μm, and F should exceed roughly 40. These are design bounds, not a unique optimum. One suitable choice is
μd=5.0 μm,R=0.95,F≃61.2​.
(11)
The physical spacing is d=5.0 μm/μ; an air gap gives d≃5 μm. Approximate FSRs near normal incidence are 40.1 nm at 633 nm and 44.9 nm at 670 nm, with widths about 0.65 and 0.73 nm. For a concrete angular scan choose order m=14. The 670 nm pair lies near internal θ=20.3∘ and the 633 nm line near 27.6∘; their widths within this scan remain below 1 nm. Restricting to this calibrated order window avoids the other-order 633 nm ring near 18.3∘. Index dispersion and unknown extra source lines would need calibration or order-sorting filters.
The resolving power is approximately mF: many coherent cavity round trips create a much longer effective optical delay than one traversal. A Michelson instrument with the same available physical delay has less resolving power unless its mirror travel provides a correspondingly long maximum optical path difference. Mirror separation errors spread the round-trip phase; keeping them below a linewidth requires roughly
∣Δd∣≪2μFcosθλ​​.
(12)
For this design the scale is about 6 nm in an air gap. Both surface flatness and parallelism over the illuminated area, as well as angular collimation and coating uniformity, must support the intended finesse.
