The string speed is c=T/ρ​. The characteristic impedance is the ratio of the forward-wave transverse force to transverse velocity, Z=T/c=Tρ​. With peak displacement amplitude A, the mean power is
⟨P⟩=21​Zω2A2​.

Displacement and transverse force are continuous at a massless junction. Since Z2​=2Z1​, the displacement amplitudes are r=(Z1​−Z2​)/(Z1​+Z2​)=−1/3 and t=2Z1​/(Z1​+Z2​)=2/3. Taking the incident amplitude real, the light-string motion is
y=A[cos(kx−ωt)−31​cos(kx+ωt)]=32A​cos(kx−ωt)+32A​sinkxsinωt.

In this standing-and-travelling decomposition of a reflected string wave, the first term travels and the second stands; the decomposition is not a separation into independent incident-power fractions because these fields interfere. The reflected wave has a phase reversal. power coefficients and standing-wave ratio are
R=∣r∣2=91​,T=Z1​Z2​​∣t∣2=98​,SWR=1−∣r∣1+∣r∣​=2​.

For a uniform inserted matching section, unequal exterior impedances require a quarter-wave impedance transformer: ZM​=Z1​Z2​​ and kM​a=(2j+1)π/2. At equal tension this means middle density 2ρ0​ and an odd-quarter-wavelength length in that section. Reflections at its two ends then cancel at the design frequency. An arbitrary density or an arbitrary half-wavelength segment cannot perfectly match these unequal exterior strings; the ideal match is frequency-selective.
amplitude scaling along a slowly tapered string instead follows from matching over an adiabatic frequency range. The source fixes frequency, while conserved power and the final characteristic impedance determine amplitude. In the heavy string,
f2​=f1​,A2​=A1​/2​,λ2​=λ1​/2​.
