The Hamiltonian operator is
H=2mpx2​​+V(x)=−2mℏ2​dx2d2​+V(x).​
(44)
Since multiplication by x commutes with multiplication by V(x), part (b) gives [x,H]=iℏpx​/m. Similarly [px​,H]=−iℏV′(x). Substituting into part (c) gives Ehrenfest theorem:
dtd⟨x⟩​=m⟨px​⟩​,dtd⟨px​⟩​=−⟨V′(x)⟩.​
(45)
Thus the mean momentum changes according to the mean force, and the derivative of mean position is mean velocity. These exact quantum equations resemble Hamilton's classical equations, but ⟨V′(x)⟩ generally differs from V′(⟨x⟩). The classical point-particle equation for the center is exact for potentials at most quadratic and is an approximation for a localized packet when the force varies little across it. A spatially broad packet in a nonlinear potential need not follow a classical trajectory. Even where its center follows classical motion, its width and measurement uncertainties remain quantum properties.
