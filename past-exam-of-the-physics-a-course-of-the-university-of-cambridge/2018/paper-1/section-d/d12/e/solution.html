 ... Past exam of the physics A course of the University of Cambridge 2018 Paper 1 Section D D12 e
OurBigBook.com Words: 158
Introduce an additive background rate B, from ambient radiation, detector dark counts or another weakly attenuated contribution. If S0​ denotes the unattenuated signal alone, the attenuation count model with an additive background is
N(n)=S0​e−bn+B.​
(64)
It approaches a nonzero constant as the transmitted signal becomes small. The source-off background should ideally be measured in a separate long exposure and its uncertainty included in the fit; otherwise estimate B≥0 jointly using a Poisson count likelihood.
There is a normalization detail: the given N0​ was itself obtained from an n=0 count measurement. If that measurement included the same background, then it measures S0​+B, and the attenuation fit normalized to a total reference rate is
N(n)=(N0​−B)e−bn+B.​
(65)
Writing N0​e−bn+B instead treats N0​ as a background-subtracted signal calibration. The difference is small here because the background is a few counts per second while N0​ is about two thousand, but the distinction prevents double-counting background. The last three observations alone do not give an exact value of B, because residual transmitted signal and sizeable counting fluctuations remain.
