Use first the conservative signal-dominated range n=1,…,4, where the count approximation is reliable and background is a small correction. Using the estimated errors from part (c), the weighted residual statistic is
Qobs​(b)=∑n=14​yn​(yn​−μn​)2​.

It gives Qobs​(1)=4.45,Qobs​(1.1)=12.74​. Since both b candidates are fixed and N0​ was calibrated independently, neither was fitted to these four counts: the approximate reference has ν=4, not three. The upper-tail probabilities are approximately 0.349 and 0.0126, respectively. Thus b=1 is compatible with the scatter, whereas b=1.1 is disfavored at the usual 5% level in this approximate test.
A Pearson chi-squared statistic, using model variances instead of estimated observed variances, gives the same preference:
QP​(1)=4.04,QP​(1.1)=15.62,p≃0.401, 0.00357.
(62)
Including the fifth point also leaves the decision unchanged: the estimated-error statistics are 8.37 and 12.74 on five degrees of freedom, with approximate upper-tail probabilities 0.137 and 0.0259; the Pearson statistics are 6.34 and 15.63. Hence
b=1 provides the better exponential fit.​
(63)
The small uncertainty in N0​ contributes correlated prediction uncertainty, as in chi-squared comparison of a calibrated attenuation model, but is negligible compared with the one-second counting errors here. Choosing the test range after inspecting the observations limits a strict interpretation of its nominal significance; the numerical comparison and its stated range are the relevant evidence.
If all eight observations are inserted into the estimated-error formula, the totals are 14.99 and 22.48. Their nominal eight-degree-of-freedom calibration is unreliable because the final expected counts are much smaller than one for some predictions. In fact the full-range Pearson statistics are 43.34 and 118.25, exposing the late excess much more strongly. The full Poisson deviance values are 24.09 and 47.03, again favoring b=1 but signaling that a background-free exponential is inadequate over the complete range. These sparse-count comparisons should not be used to claim that either unmodified model explains all eight measurements.
