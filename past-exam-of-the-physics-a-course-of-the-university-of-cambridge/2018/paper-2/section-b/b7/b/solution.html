Let the aperture occupy the z=0 plane, with coordinates (x,y) and opening Σ. Write ψΣ​(x,y) for the incident scalar complex amplitude and h(x,y) for its dimensionless amplitude transmission, including any imposed phase. The observation point has transverse coordinates (X,Y), corresponding to the PDF's (x0​,y0​), and axial distance R. Also k=2π/λ is the wavenumber and i2=−1.
The Huygens-Fresnel principle expresses the propagated field, up to common prefactors and an approximately constant obliquity factor, as
ψP​∝∬Σ​ψΣ​hreikr​dxdy,r=R2+(X−x)2+(Y−y)2​.
(25)
In the paraxial approximation, expand the path as
r≃R+2RX2+Y2​−RXx+Yy​+2Rx2+y2​.
(26)
The last term is negligible in the Fraunhofer diffraction limit, while the first two terms and 1/r≃1/R are common across the aperture. Factoring them out leaves
ψP​∝∬Σ​ψΣ​(x,y)h(x,y)e−ik(Xx+Yy)/Rdxdy​.
(27)
It is the two-dimensional Fourier transform of the transmitted aperture field, evaluated at transverse wavevector (kX/R,kY/R). For appreciable observation angles the corresponding exact directional components are used rather than X/R,Y/R.
