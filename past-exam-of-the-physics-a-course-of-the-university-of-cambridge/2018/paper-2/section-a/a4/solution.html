The monatomic-chain dispersion relation has one acoustic phonon branch, with ω≃dK/m​∣k∣ near the origin. The first Brillouin zone ends at k=±π/d​, where ω=2K/m​ and the group velocity vanishes.
For alternating masses m,M, choose a two-atom cell of length 2d. The coupled normal modes give the diatomic-chain dispersion relation with equal springs,
ω±2​=K(m1​+M1​)±K(m1​+M1​)2−mM4​sin2(kd)​​.

The first Brillouin zone now ends at k=±π/(2d)​. The minus branch is the acoustic phonon branch; its two atoms move together at long wavelength. The plus branch is the optical phonon branch; at k=0 the atoms move oppositely with a stationary centre of mass and ω+2​=2K(1/m+1/M). For M>m the edge frequencies are 2K/M​ and 2K/m​, with a gap between them.
If M=m, the two branches reduce to 2K/m​∣sin(kd/2)∣ and 2K/m​∣cos(kd/2)∣, touching at 2K/m​. This is zone folding in an equal-mass diatomic chain: identical sites actually have a primitive unit cell of length d. A doubled cell folds the monatomic branch but opens no gap. Equal masses alone do not specify all possible spring models; the sketches assume equal nearest-neighbour spring constants.
