Assume sodium supplies one conduction electron per atom. The supplied Fermi energy gives
TF​=1.380649×10−233.24(1.602176634×10−19)​=3.760×104K,T/TF​=7.98×10−3.
(62)
The gas is strongly degenerate at room temperature. For one mole, N=NA​ and NA​kB​=R=8.31446Jmol−1K−1. Using the requested estimate,
CV,mel​≃29​RTF​300​=0.299Jmol−1K−1​.
(63)
The supplied mass density determines the answer per unit volume: N/V=(970/0.023)NA​=2.54×1028m−3. Therefore
cVel​≃1.26×104Jm−3K−1​,cV,massel​≃13.0Jkg−1K−1​.
(64)
The density is not needed for the molar heat capacity once EF​ is supplied. Using the precise Sommerfeld expansion coefficient instead gives 0.327Jmol−1K−1.
Either electronic estimate is only about 1% of the stated measured total. Most of the total heat capacity comes from lattice phonons, whose high-temperature Dulong-Petit law limit is 3R=24.94Jmol−1K−1. The remaining difference from 28Jmol−1K−1 need not be electronic: anharmonic lattice effects and the difference between heat capacity at constant pressure and heat capacity at constant volume also contribute. The paper does not specify the measurement constraint, so the two totals should not be equated exactly.
