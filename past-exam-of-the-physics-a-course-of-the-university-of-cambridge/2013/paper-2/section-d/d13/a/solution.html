For a circular structure, cylindrical symmetry reduces the scalar Fresnel-Kirchhoff diffraction integral to concentric annuli. With plane-wave illumination, radius a and distance z, the relevant Fresnel number is NF​=a2/(λz). Successive Fresnel half-period zones have radii rj2​≃jλz: their areas are approximately equal while their phases alternate by π, causing neighbouring-zone cancellation.
For Fresnel diffraction by a circular aperture, the on-axis field follows directly from the radial integral,
Ufree​Uap​​=iλz1​∫0a​eiπr2/(λz)2πrdr=1−eiπNF​,Iap​/Ifree​=4sin2(πNF​/2)​.
(41)
Odd zone counts reinforce on axis; even counts cancel in this ideal scalar model. An annular opening gives the difference of the corresponding two edge exponentials. A zone plate alternately blocks or phase-shifts zones so that otherwise cancelling contributions reinforce at a focus, with rj2​≃jλf for its principal focal distance.
Babinet's principle gives Udisk​=Ufree​−Uap​=Ufree​eiπNF​ for Fresnel diffraction by an opaque disk. Thus its ideal on-axis intensity equals the unobstructed intensity, the Arago spot, even within the geometric shadow. This concerns a narrow axial feature, not the whole shadow. Finite source size, imperfect circularity, bandwidth and alignment reduce its visibility. When NF​≪1, the circular-aperture far-field pattern becomes the Airy disk intensity pattern [2J1​(u)/u]2, with u=kasinθ and first minimum near θ=1.22λ/(2a).
