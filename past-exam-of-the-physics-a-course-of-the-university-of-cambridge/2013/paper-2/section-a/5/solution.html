In the Hall effect, the Lorentz force deflects drifting charge carriers until a transverse electric field balances it: EH​=−vd​×B. Choose current along x, field along z and define RH​=Ey​/(jx​Bz​). Since jx​=nqvx​,
RH​=nq1​,n=e∣RH​∣1​for electron carriers​.
(4)
The sign is negative for electrons with this convention. If the sample's mass density is ρm​ and its molar mass is M, the atomic number density is nat​=ρm​NA​/M, so
zfree​=nat​n​=e∣RH​∣ρm​NA​M​​.
(5)
Measure the transverse voltage, specimen thickness, current and magnetic field; reversing field and current helps remove longitudinal-voltage pickup and offsets. This free-electron count from a Hall coefficient assumes one approximately free carrier band. Multiple bands, unequal mobilities or a complicated Fermi surface can make a Hall coefficient differ from −1/(ne).
