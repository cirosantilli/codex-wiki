For the squared discrepancy of a digit frequency, write dN2​=(f0​−0.1)2. The plotted ordinates are 10−2, 4.00×10−4, 1.69×10−4, 4.90×10−5, 3.60×10−5 and 9.00×10−6 in increasing N. Under an independent, equiprobable-digit model, the zero count is binomial and
E[dN2​]=Var(f0​)=N0.1(0.9)​=N0.09​​.
(4)
Thus the expected log-log slope is −1. If “relative” means division by the expected fraction before squaring, every ordinate is multiplied by 100 and the prediction becomes 9/N; the slope is unchanged. These are nested prefixes of one digit sequence, so the points are correlated, rounded observations, not independent variance estimates. Their rough trend supports a reciprocal-power scale but does not prove independence or that π is a normal number.
