The remaining cyclic commutators are [Sy​,Sz​]=iℏSx​ and [Sz​,Sx​]=iℏSy​. In a spin-s irreducible representation,
Sz​=ℏms​,ms​=−s,−s+1,…,s;S2=ℏ2s(s+1)​.
(13)
For spin one-half, Si​=ℏσi​/2, appropriate to an electron's spin. Each Si2​=ℏ2I/4, so this Hamiltonian is H=Aℏ2I/4. The orthonormal vectors (1,0)T and (0,1)T are eigenstates with the same energy Aℏ2/4; every normalized superposition is also an eigenstate. The B term vanishes identically because spin-one-half quadratic anisotropy is constant.
For the supplied three-dimensional basis one consistent choice is
Sy​=iℏ​00−1​000​100​​,Sz​=iℏ​0−10​100​000​​.
(14)
These Hermitian matrices obey the cyclic commutators with the given Sx​, and S2=2ℏ2I, representing spin one. Their squares give
H=ℏ2diag(A−B,A+B,0).
(15)
Thus the three normalized Cartesian basis vectors e1​,e2​,e3​ have energies ℏ2(A−B), ℏ2(A+B) and 0, respectively; degeneracies permit arbitrary orthonormal choices in the corresponding eigenspaces.
The parallel and antiparallel Sx​ states are ∣+x⟩=(0,1,−i)T/2​ and ∣−x⟩=(0,1,i)T/2​. Evolution from the first gives
∣ψ(t)⟩=2​1​​0e−i(A+B)ℏt−i​​,P−x​(t)=sin2(2(A+B)ℏt​).
(16)
This spin-one reversal under quadratic anisotropy is complete when
t=∣A+B∣ℏ(2j+1)π​,j=0,1,…​
(17)
provided A+B=0. If A+B=0, its initial state is stationary up to phase and complete reversal never occurs. Assume real A,B for a Hermitian Hamiltonian.
