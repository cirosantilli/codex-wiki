A mass M on a spring of stiffness K, subject to linear viscous damping and a sinusoidal drive, obeys the forced harmonic oscillator equation
Mx¨+2Mγx˙+Kx=F0​cosΩt,ω0​=K/M​,γ>0.
(63)
Here x is displacement, 2Mγ has units kgs−1, and F0​ is a force in newtons. A series RLC circuit is an electrical analogue, with M↔L, 2Mγ↔R, K↔1/C and x↔q.
For free motion set F0​=0 and solve the characteristic equation s2+2γs+ω02​=0. The roots s=−γ±γ2−ω02​​ distinguish three cases:
  • For underdamping, γ<ω0​, the motion is x=Ae−γtcos(ωd​t−ϕ), where ωd​=ω02​−γ2​. The amplitude decays on time 1/γ and the frequency is reduced from its undamped value.
  • At critical damping, γ=ω0​, the repeated root gives x=(A+Bt)e−ω0​t. This marks the boundary between oscillatory and nonoscillatory relaxation; it is the usual fastest settling without oscillation for a release from rest.
  • For overdamping, γ>ω0​, x=Aes+​t+Bes−​t with both roots real and negative. At large damping the slow root approaches −ω02​/(2γ), so increasing the resistance to motion can slow equilibration.
The constants are set by initial position and velocity. The mechanical energy and its rate of change in free motion are
E=21​Mx˙2+21​Kx2,dtdE​=−2Mγx˙2≤0.
(64)
For weak damping, the cycle-averaged energy decays approximately as e−2γt. The quality factor is Q=ω0​/(2γ); for Q≫1 it equals 2π times stored energy divided by the loss per cycle. Logarithmic decrement of successive free-decay peaks determines γ experimentally.
For driven motion the complete answer is the decaying free transient plus a steady response at the driving angular frequency:
xss​=X(Ω)cos(Ωt−δ),X(Ω)=(ω02​−Ω2)2+4γ2Ω2​F0​/M​,δ=atan2(2γΩ,ω02​−Ω2).​
(65)
At low frequency, X→F0​/K and the displacement is nearly in phase with the drive. At Ω=ω0​, the lag is π/2 and X=F0​/(2Mγω0​). At high frequency, X≃F0​/(MΩ2) and the lag tends to π. The displacement resonance maximum lies at
Ωr​=ω02​−2γ2​
(66)
only when γ<ω0​/2​. Thus an underdamped oscillator need not have a nonzero-frequency displacement resonance peak.
In the steady state the mean input power equals the mean dissipated power:
P=MγΩ2X2=(ω02​−Ω2)2+4γ2Ω2F02​γΩ2/M​.​
(67)
This absorption peaks exactly at Ω=ω0​, rather than at Ωr​. Its half-power points have bandwidth ΔΩ=2γ, so Q=ω0​/ΔΩ for this linear model. For weak damping, a high quality factor therefore means a narrow strong resonance and a long free-decay time. Exactly zero damping at resonant driving has no finite steady response: a secular amplitude grows with time.
