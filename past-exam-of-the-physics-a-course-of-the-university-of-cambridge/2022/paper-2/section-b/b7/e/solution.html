The required resolving power of a diffraction grating is
Rreq​=0.6nm589.3nm​=982.2.
(41)
For N=260 illuminated slits, R=260∣m∣. In orders 1,2,3,4, the ideal resolvable wavelength differences are respectively 2.267, 1.133, 0.756 and 0.567nm. Thus
The doublet is unresolved in orders 1–3; order 4 is the first potentially resolving order.​
(42)
Fourth order gives R=1040>982.2, only a modest margin. It must also exist: for normal incidence the grating equation requires D≥4λ≃2.36μm, with D>4λ needed to avoid an exactly grazing order. The relevant order must transmit appreciable light intensity rather than lie at a single-slit diffraction zero, and the source and detector widths must preserve this resolution.
The original question gives only the slit count, not the line spacing, slit width, usable diffraction order or instrumental broadening. It therefore supports the conditional conclusion “yes in fourth or higher usable order”, but not an unconditional historical claim that this particular instrument resolved the sodium absorption line doublet in its observed configuration.
