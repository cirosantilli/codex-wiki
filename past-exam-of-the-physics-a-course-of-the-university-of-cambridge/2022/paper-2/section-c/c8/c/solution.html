Let d=1,2,3 and take a specimen of length, area or volume Ld. One monovalent atom per unit cell supplies
Ne​=(L/a)d

electrons. At zero temperature, the Pauli exclusion principle fills the lowest energy states, with two spin states per spatial wavevector. The occupied region is an interval, disk or ball of radius kF​. Using the k-space density of states gives, in one dimension,
aL​=22πL​(2kF​),kF(1)​=2aπ​,EF(1)​=8ma2ℏ2π2​.​
(51)
In two dimensions,
a2L2​=2(2πL​)2πkF2​,kF(2)​=a2π​​,EF(2)​=ma2πℏ2​.​
(52)
In three dimensions,
a3L3​=2(2πL​)334πkF3​​,kF(3)​=a(3π2)1/3​,EF(3)​=2ma2ℏ2(3π2)2/3​.​
(53)
Each Fermi energy follows from the free electron model dispersion relation EF​=ℏ2kF2​/(2m). The Fermi wavevectors have units m−1 and the Fermi energies have units joules for a in metres and m in kilograms. No numerical lattice spacing is supplied, so numerical energies in electronvolts cannot be uniquely calculated. The spin factor of two is necessary here for actual monovalent crystals; the spinless auxiliary count of part (a) was not an instruction to remove electron spin from these crystals.
