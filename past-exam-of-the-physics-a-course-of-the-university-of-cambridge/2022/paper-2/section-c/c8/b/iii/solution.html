In three dimensions use the k-space density of states (L/2π)3 per spin species and the spherical shell volume 4πk2dk:

Thus the three-dimensional free-electron density of states grows as ϵ1/2. For physical electrons, including both spin states gives
g3​(ϵ)=2π2L3​(ℏ22m​)3/2ϵ1/2.
(49)
All three total density of states functions have units J−1 when energy is in joules; they count states per energy interval. The paper explicitly suppresses spin in (a) but does not repeat that convention in (b), so giving both conventions avoids a hidden factor-of-two ambiguity. Parts (c)--(f) below use the physical two-spin-state electron gas.
