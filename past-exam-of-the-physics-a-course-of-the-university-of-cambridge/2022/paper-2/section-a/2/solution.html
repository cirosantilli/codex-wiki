The components are in phase quadrature when the axes of the ellipse coincide with the coordinate axes. Since the x wave amplitude is one and the y wave amplitude is two, the Jones vector must have A=±2i.
A handedness convention is essential. Adopt the optical convention in which right-handed elliptical polarization rotates clockwise when viewed toward the source, from the positive-z side. With the paper's e+iωt phase convention for complex amplitudes, this gives
A=+2i,Ψ(t)=(cosωt,−2sinωt).​
(5)
The tip follows Ψx2​+Ψy2​/4=1. In particular,
Ψ(0)=(1,0),Ψ(π/(2ω))=(0,−2).​
(6)
The figure states the viewing direction and shows increasing time. Under the alternative convention that calls rotation from +x toward +y right-handed when looking along propagation, the equally consistent answer is A=−2i and the second vector is (0,+2). The original paper does not state a handedness convention, so its wording alone cannot select between these labels; the real components and viewing direction remove the ambiguity.
