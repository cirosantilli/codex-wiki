In an ordinary three-dimensional crystalline electrical insulator, heat is carried mainly by phonons. Kinetic theory gives the estimate
κ≃31​cV​vs​ℓ,
(11)
where cV​ is heat capacity per unit volume, vs​ is a typical sound speed, and ℓ is the phonon mean free path. The thermal conductivity κ has units Wm−1K−1.
At low temperature, T≪ΘD​, the Debye model gives cV​∝T3. With phonon-phonon collisions frozen out, diffuse scattering at the sample boundaries makes ℓ approximately constant, and vs​ is nearly constant. Hence
κ∝T3at low T with boundary-limited scattering.​
(12)
At high temperature, of order or above the Debye temperature, the Dulong-Petit law makes cV​ approximately constant. Anharmonic phonon scattering, especially resistive Umklapp scattering, has a collision rate approximately proportional to T. Thus ℓ∝T−1 and
κ∝T−1at high T with Umklapp-limited scattering.​
(13)
These two regimes usually give an intermediate maximum of thermal conductivity. The assumptions matter: glassy materials, strong defects or specular boundary reflection can change the low-temperature law; these are not universal laws for every electrical insulator.
