A non-ideal amplifier is not specified by infinite input impedance alone: its open-loop gain and output impedance must also be modeled. Take its internal controlled output to be u=−A(s)v, where v is the inverting-input potential, and place a resistance ro​ between u and the actual output y=Vout​. The non-inverting input remains grounded; assume no external load. This gives an explicit finite-gain operational-amplifier integrator model.
Writing Kirchhoff's current law at the input and output nodes gives
RVin​−v​=sC(v−y),ro​u−y​=sC(y−v),u=−A(s)v.
(68)
The second equation gives y=(sCro​−A)v/(1+sCro​). Substitution in the first gives the general transfer function
H(s)=Vin​Vout​​=1+sC{ro​+R[1+A(s)]}sCro​−A(s)​.​
(69)
For sinusoidal phasors, set s=iω. The numerator and denominator are complex, so the magnitude gives the amplitude ratio and their arguments give the relative phase.
The simplest model takes ro​=0 and a constant finite gain A0​>0. It gives
H(iω)=−1+iωRC(1+A0​)A0​​,∣H∣=1+ω2R2C2(1+A0​)2​A0​​,argH=π−tan−1[ωRC(1+A0​)].
(70)
Thus below ωl​=[(1+A0​)RC]−1 the amplifier has gain magnitude A0​ and inversion. Above that frequency it approaches an integrator with magnitude A0​/[(1+A0​)ωRC]≃1/(ωRC) and phase +90∘. Constant finite gain by itself predicts no further high-frequency turnover.
For a more realistic sketch, use the standard dominant-pole open-loop gain A(s)=A0​/(1+s/ωa​), still with ro​=0. Then
H(s)=1+bs+cs2−A0​​,b=ωa−1​+(1+A0​)RC,c=RC/ωa​.​
(71)
The exact frequency response is
∣H(iω)∣=(1−cω2)2+b2ω2​A0​​,ϕ(ω)=π−atan2(bω,1−cω2).
(72)
Assuming A0​≫1 and A0​ωa​RC≫1, the turnover frequencies are well separated: ωl​≃1/(A0​RC) and ωh​≃A0​ωa​, the approximate unity-gain angular frequency of the amplifier. The regimes are
frequencyω≪ωl​ωl​≪ω≪ωh​ω≫ωh​​∣H∣A0​(ωRC)−1A0​ωa​/(ω2RC)​phase180∘90∘0∘​​​
(73)
with magnitude slopes 0,−20,−40dB per decade and smooth phase transitions. This is the solid curve in the figure; 180∘ and −180∘ denote the same phase.
If finite ro​ is retained and A(iω)→0, the general expression instead approaches ro​/(R+ro​) at very high frequency: the capacitor couples the input to the output through a passive voltage divider. The dashed curve illustrates that eventual feedthrough. Other amplifier poles or an external load would alter the response again. No particular values of A0​,ωa​,ro​ are given in the paper, so the numerical sketch is illustrative and the general expression states the model dependence explicitly.
