The orbital angular momentum of a particle is the operator L=r×p, with p=−iℏ∇ in the position representation. Its angular momentum commutation relations are
[Li​,Lj​]=iℏϵijk​Lk​,[L2,Li​]=0.
(55)
Thus one may specify L2 and one component, conventionally Lz​, simultaneously. One generally cannot assign sharp values to all three components, because they do not commute. The common angular eigenfunctions are the spherical harmonics:
L2Ylm​=ℏ2l(l+1)Ylm​,Lz​Ylm​=ℏmYlm​,l=0,1,2,…,m=−l,…,l.​
(56)
For a scalar orbital wavefunction, single-valuedness in the azimuthal angle gives integer m, and regularity on the sphere and termination of the ladder give integer l≥∣m∣. Half-integer angular momenta describe intrinsic spin rather than scalar orbital motion.
The ladder operators L±​=Lx​±iLy​ obey [Lz​,L±​]=±ℏL±​. Their normalized action is
L±​∣l,m⟩=ℏl(l+1)−m(m±1)​∣l,m±1⟩.
(57)
The coefficient vanishes at the top or bottom of the multiplet. Its squared magnitude follows from L∓​L±​=L2−Lz2​∓ℏLz​, and positivity of the resulting norm constrains the allowed m values.
For a central potential, the Hamiltonian commutes with L2 and every component of L. Stationary states can be separated as Rnl​(r)Ylm​(θ,ϕ), and rotational symmetry makes the energy independent of m, giving a 2l+1 quantum degeneracy at fixed radial label and l. Additional degeneracy between distinct l values requires additional structure, as in the Coulomb potential. The parity of Ylm​ is (−1)l. These results organize atomic orbitals and their selection rules. They describe quantized rotation and symmetry, without assigning the electron a classical orbit or a simultaneously definite three-component angular momentum vector.
