The nuclear beta decay is fast compared with the electronic orbital timescale. In the sudden approximation, the electronic wavefunction does not have time to change during the increase from Zi​=1 to Zf​=2. The subsequent probability of a particular daughter energy eigenstate is therefore its squared overlap with the initial state.
For the final 1s state, write βi​=1/a0​ and βf​=2/a0​. The sudden hydrogenic charge change gives the amplitude

The final 2p states have spherical harmonics Y1m​, whereas the initial state is proportional to Y00​. Their angular overlap ∫Y1m∗​Y00​dΩ vanishes by orthogonality. Accordingly P2p​=0​ in the ideal spherically symmetric sudden-change model. Nuclear recoil or interactions with the emitted particles can produce small corrections. This is an overlap selection rule for a sudden change, not a photon-induced electric dipole transition.
