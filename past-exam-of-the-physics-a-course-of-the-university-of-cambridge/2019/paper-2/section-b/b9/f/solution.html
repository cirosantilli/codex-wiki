Resistance to bending adds a restoring contribution that grows more rapidly with spatial curvature than the tension contribution. Short-wavelength modes are therefore stiffened especially strongly, raising their frequencies and changing the large-wavenumber behaviour.
For example, a thin plate of flexural rigidity Db​ has σztt​=T∇2z−Db​∇4z. When its edge conditions admit the same sine transverse mode, bending stiffness in a membrane waveguide gives
ωn2​=σT​(k2+qn2​)+σDb​​(k2+qn2​)2.
(45)
Thus the cutoffs increase, and bending-dominated propagation has ω∝k2 at large ∣k∣, rather than approaching cm​∣k∣. Its phase velocity and group velocity then grow with ∣k∣. If “rigidly fixed” means a genuinely clamped plate edge, the slope must also vanish and the mode shapes change; the qualitative stiffening and stronger wave dispersion remain, but simple qn​=nπ/a need not remain exact.
