With length measured in units of a=1, plot the real function
f(k)=cosk+10ksink​,f(0)=11.
(55)
Only the portions with ∣f(k)∣≤1 can equal cosϕ, so the strip between the horizontal lines +1 and −1 identifies allowed energy bands. The band edges of a repulsive delta-comb potential have the following approximate first intervals:
2.62775.30738.067110.9087​≤k≤π,≤k≤2π,≤k≤3π,≤k≤4π.​​
(56)
Each interval corresponds to energy E=ℏ2k2/(2m); the intervening intervals are forbidden. The exact upper endpoints follow from f(jπ)=(−1)j. The lower endpoints solve f(k)=(−1)j−1 inside ((j−1)π,jπ). The divergence suggested by sink/k at the origin is removable, and f(0)=11>1, so there is no zero-energy state in the repulsive model.
The periodic barriers produce separated energy bands and band gaps, unlike the continuous free-particle spectrum. A finite N-atom loop has discrete states within these bands. Whether the material conducts still depends on band filling: one electron per primitive cell half-fills a spin-degenerate band, while two electrons per cell can fill the first band and place the Fermi energy in a gap. Band gaps alone do not establish that every filling is insulating. At high k, the perturbing term 10sink/k becomes relatively small and the spectrum approaches free-electron behaviour in relative terms.
