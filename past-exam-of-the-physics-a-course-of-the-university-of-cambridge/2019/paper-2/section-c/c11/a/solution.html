The quantum free electron model treats conduction electrons as independent particles moving in an approximately constant potential, with parabolic dispersion relation E=ℏ2k2/(2m). Fermi-Dirac statistics successfully explains important features such as a Fermi energy and a small electronic heat capacity, but a constant potential gives no band gaps and cannot by itself explain why different band fillings produce metals, semiconductors and band insulators. It also misses lattice-dependent effective masses, detailed directional responses and the topology of real Fermi surfaces. Scattering and finite electrical resistivity require additional physics.
The nearly-free electron model introduces a weak periodic lattice potential. Bloch's theorem replaces individual free plane waves by Bloch states, and Fourier components of the potential mix plane waves whose wavevectors differ by a reciprocal lattice vector. Near degeneracy at a Brillouin zone boundary, this coupling opens a band gap; for a two-wave crossing its size is 2∣VG​∣. Away from the crossing, energies remain close to free-particle values. Band curvature produces band effective masses, and band filling determines whether the Fermi energy lies inside an allowed band or a gap.
The improvement is the inclusion of lattice periodicity, rather than a switch from classical to quantum statistics: the quantum free electron model already uses Fermi-Dirac statistics. The strong delta barriers in the later numerical example are treated exactly by the Kronig-Penney model, rather than by assuming that their strength is perturbatively small.
