Each electromagnetic normal mode is a quantum harmonic oscillator with En​=(n+1/2)hν. For x=hν/(kB​T) its canonical partition function and occupation probabilities are
Zν​=1−e−xe−x/2​,Pn​=(1−e−x)e−nx,⟨n⟩=ex−11​.
(18)
The geometric distribution is normalized and gives thermal mode energy hν/(ex−1). The zero-point term hν/2 is not part of the thermally emitted black-body intensity. The equilibrium photon chemical potential is zero because photon number is not conserved.
Counting two transverse polarizations gives 8πν2/c3 modes per volume per frequency interval. Thus the spectral energy density is uν​=8πhν3/[c3(ex−1)]. In an isotropic radiation field, spectral radiance is Bν​=cuν​/(4π). Transforming the spectral measure, Bλ​=Bν​∣dν/dλ∣, gives
Bλ​(T)=λ52hc2​ehc/(λkB​T)−11​.​
(19)
Here intensity means radiance per wavelength and solid angle; the hemispheric spectral exitance of a black surface is Mλ​=πBλ​. The extra Jacobian is essential: the wavelength peak is not found by simply converting the frequency peak using λ=c/ν.
At short wavelength the exponential suppresses radiation. At long wavelength the Rayleigh-Jeans limit is Bλ​≃2ckB​T/λ4; using classical equipartition at every frequency instead would produce the ultraviolet catastrophe. The wavelength maximum solves 5(1−e−x)=x, with nonzero root x=4.9651, yielding Wien's displacement law. For the specified temperature,
λmax​=2.7K2.898×10−3mK​≃1.07mm.​
(20)
Integrating the hemispheric flux gives the Stefan-Boltzmann law, M=σT4≃3.01×10−6Wm−2. The plot uses the specified ideal temperature, rather than substituting a later measured value. The physical black-body description of the cosmic microwave background is tested by comparing the sky spectrum with an absolute reference blackbody and separating instrumental and Galactic contributions.[ref]
