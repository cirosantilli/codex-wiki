The ground configuration therefore has two electrons in n=1 and seven in n=2, giving Eg​=−C(2+7/4)=−15C/4. The least energy-increasing promotion is from n=2 to n=3:

For the proposed angular factor, cosθ∝Y10​, so l=1 and magnetic quantum number zero. Direct differentiation gives ∇2(re−αrcosθ)=(α2−4α/r)ψ. The 1/r term in the Schrödinger equation vanishes when 2ℏ2α/m=C. With Bohr radius a0​=ℏ2/(mC),

B8 OurBigBook.com
The angular momentum commutation relations are [Lx​,Ly​]=iℏLz​ and cyclic permutations. The product rule yields [Lz​,Lx2​]=iℏ(Ly​Lx​+Lx​Ly​) and [Lz​,Ly2​]=−iℏ(Lx​Ly​+Ly​Lx​), so [Lz​,L2]=0. Simultaneous eigenstates have eigenvalues ℏ2l(l+1) and ℏm; orbital angular momentum has integer l≥0 and m=−l,…,l.
By cyclic symmetry, L2 commutes with every component and hence every squared component. Therefore [H,L2]=0: an energy eigenbasis can be chosen with definite l. For unequal transverse coefficients, Lz​ need not commute with H, so definite m is not generally available.
Expanding the ladder operators gives L+2​+L−2​=2(Lx2​−Ly2​) and L2−Lz2​=Lx2​+Ly2​. Comparing coefficients gives
α=4ax​−ay​​,β=2ax​+ay​​,γ=az​.​
(11)
For l=1,m=0, both squared ladder operators vanish, since two raising or lowering steps would leave the multiplet. Thus E0​=2βℏ2=ℏ2(ax​+ay​). In the remaining basis {∣1,1⟩,∣1,−1⟩} the Hamiltonian is
ℏ2(β+γ2α​2αβ+γ​).
(12)
Its symmetric and antisymmetric combinations have energies ℏ2(ax​+az​) and ℏ2(ay​+az​), respectively. Consequently
{El=1​}=ℏ2{ax​+ay​, ay​+az​, az​+ax​}.​
(13)
The energies are related by permutation of the principal axes, not generally equal. Two coincide when the corresponding coefficients coincide; all three become 2aℏ2 in the isotropic limit.

H=2mQ†Q​=2mp2+f2−ℏf′​,V(x)=2mf(x)2−ℏf′(x)​.​
(14)
The energy form is ⟨ψ,Hψ⟩=∥Qψ∥2/(2m)≥0, so every normalizable energy eigenstate has E≥0. If a normalizable zero-energy state exists, its nonnegative norm must vanish, Qψ0​=0. Hence
ℏψ0′​+fψ0​=0,ψ0​(x)=Nexp[−ℏ1​∫0x​f(t)dt].​
(15)
Zero energy is an additional existence assumption, not a consequence of factorization alone.
For ψ1​=Q†ψ0​, the commutator [Q,Q†]=2ℏf′ gives
Hψ1​=mℏ​Q†(f′ψ0​)=−miℏ2​f′′ψ0​+mℏ​f′ψ1​.
(16)
Thus α(x)=−iℏ2f′′(x)/m and β(x)=ℏf′(x)/m in the requested relation. Generally these are multiplication functions, not constant coefficients, and ψ1​ is not automatically an energy eigenstate.
If f(x)=cxn+⋯, its integral has leading term cxn+1/(n+1). Decay at both infinities on the full real line requires odd n and positive leading coefficient c. Odd degree alone is not sufficient. For f=kx take k=mω>0. Then H=HSHO​−ℏω/2, α=0 and β=ℏω. After normalization and removal of an irrelevant phase,

(17)
The corresponding factorized-Hamiltonian energies are zero and ℏω; the constant shift must be restored for the stated oscillator Hamiltonian.

C10 OurBigBook.com
Each electromagnetic normal mode is a quantum harmonic oscillator with En​=(n+1/2)hν. For x=hν/(kB​T) its canonical partition function and occupation probabilities are
Zν​=1−e−xe−x/2​,Pn​=(1−e−x)e−nx,⟨n⟩=ex−11​.
(18)
The geometric distribution is normalized and gives thermal mode energy hν/(ex−1). The zero-point term hν/2 is not part of the thermally emitted black-body intensity. The equilibrium photon chemical potential is zero because photon number is not conserved.
Counting two transverse polarizations gives 8πν2/c3 modes per volume per frequency interval. Thus the spectral energy density is uν​=8πhν3/[c3(ex−1)]. In an isotropic radiation field, spectral radiance is Bν​=cuν​/(4π). Transforming the spectral measure, Bλ​=Bν​∣dν/dλ∣, gives
Bλ​(T)=λ52hc2​ehc/(λkB​T)−11​.​
(19)
Here intensity means radiance per wavelength and solid angle; the hemispheric spectral exitance of a black surface is Mλ​=πBλ​. The extra Jacobian is essential: the wavelength peak is not found by simply converting the frequency peak using λ=c/ν.
At short wavelength the exponential suppresses radiation. At long wavelength the Rayleigh-Jeans limit is Bλ​≃2ckB​T/λ4; using classical equipartition at every frequency instead would produce the ultraviolet catastrophe. The wavelength maximum solves 5(1−e−x)=x, with nonzero root x=4.9651, yielding Wien's displacement law. For the specified temperature,
λmax​=2.7K2.898×10−3mK​≃1.07mm.​
(20)
Integrating the hemispheric flux gives the Stefan-Boltzmann law, M=σT4≃3.01×10−6Wm−2. The plot uses the specified ideal temperature, rather than substituting a later measured value. The physical black-body description of the cosmic microwave background is tested by comparing the sky spectrum with an absolute reference blackbody and separating instrumental and Galactic contributions.[ref]

a OurBigBook.com
For a normalized top-hat wavefunction of width L centred at zero, ψ(x)=L−1/2 inside the interval and zero outside. Its position distribution has ⟨x⟩=0 and Δx=L/12​. With Fourier convention (2πℏ)−1/2, the momentum amplitude is
ψ​(p)=2πℏL​​sinc(2ℏpL​),sincz=zsinz​.
(21)
The momentum density has long oscillatory 1/p2 tails. Consequently ∫p2∣ψ​∣2dp diverges: the ideal discontinuous top hat has infinite r.m.s. momentum spread. Its symmetric momentum density has formal principal-value mean zero, but no finite absolute first moment either. The finite-variance form ΔxΔp≥ℏ/2 is therefore not saturated and cannot be evaluated using the width of the central diffraction lobe as Δp.
The first momentum zeros occur at p=±2πℏ/L, showing the inverse-width scale. Smoothing the edges sufficiently gives a finite momentum variance and restores the ordinary finite-variance uncertainty comparison; sharper edges require larger high-momentum components. It is the ideal jump, not a violation of the uncertainty principle, that causes the divergence.

b OurBigBook.com
In a classically forbidden region the local wavevector becomes imaginary. For a slowly varying barrier, the WKB transmission probability has leading exponential
T∼e−2G,G=ℏ1​∫r1​r2​​2μ[V(r)−Q]​dr,
(22)
where r1​,r2​ are turning points and μ is the reduced mass. This is a probability exponent; the wave amplitude has only half that exponent. Near a turning point one uses matching formulas rather than the unmodified local WKB expression.
For alpha decay, an attractive nuclear interior supports a quasibound state behind the repulsive Coulomb barrier of the daughter nucleus. For zero orbital angular momentum, write the exterior potential as C/r, C=2Zd​e2/(4πϵ0​), with nuclear matching radius R and outer turning point b=C/Q>R. The exterior action can be integrated:
G=ℏ2μQ​b​[arccosR/b​−(R/b)(1−R/b)​].
(23)
The decay rate is approximately λ=νPpre​e−2G and the half-life is ln2/λ. Here ν is the interior assault-frequency scale and Ppre​ is the alpha preformation probability. They are model-dependent; barrier penetration alone does not determine them. Nonzero orbital angular momentum adds a centrifugal barrier.
For R/b≪1, the leading dependence is G∝Zd​/Q​. Thus a modest increase in decay energy can cause an enormous increase in decay rate, giving the Geiger-Nuttall law relating logarithmic half-life to inverse square-root energy along a suitable isotope sequence. Tunnelling conserves the total energy: the escaping alpha particle does not acquire energy from the barrier.

c OurBigBook.com
electron spin is intrinsic angular momentum, independent of an orbital wavefunction's r×p. It has s=1/2, so
S2=43​ℏ2,Sz​=±2ℏ​,Si​=2ℏ​σi​,[Si​,Sj​]=iℏϵijk​Sk​.​
(24)
A spatial orbital therefore carries a two-component spinor degree of freedom. Noncommuting components do not describe an ordinary vector with simultaneously definite projections; measurements along one axis give only the two allowed values. Even a spherically symmetric l=0 orbital retains electron spin.
The associated magnetic moment is μ=−ge​eS/(2me​) with e>0 and ge​≃2. A spinor changes sign under a 2π rotation and returns under 4π; that sign is observable only relative to another coherent amplitude, not as an isolated global phase. A literal classically rotating charged sphere does not account for this fixed half-integer representation, component algebra and spinor rotation law. Classical angular momentum provides useful analogies for torque and precession, but no ordinary classical orientation reproduces the full intrinsic quantum spin structure.

In an operational-amplifier negative feedback loop, an operational amplifier has large open-loop gain A, with Vo​=A(V+​−V−​). Feeding a fraction βVo​ back to the inverting input opposes an output increase. For a non-inverting signal input,
Vi​Vo​​=1+AβA​≃β1​when ∣Aβ∣≫1.​
(29)
Its sensitivity to open-loop gain changes is reduced by dlnAcl​/dlnA=1/(1+Aβ). Thus resistor ratios can set a reproducible gain despite device variation; feedback also suppresses internal distortion and reduces output impedance. Gain is traded for usable bandwidth, subject to the amplifier's frequency response.
For the non-inverting connection, place Rf​ between output and inverting input and Rg​ between that input and ground. Then β=Rg​/(Rf​+Rg​), giving gain 1+Rf​/Rg​. For an inverting connection, ground the non-inverting input, inject the signal through Rin​ and return Rf​ from output to the inverting node. Small differential input and negligible input current then give gain −Rf​/Rin​. The near-zero node is a virtual ground, not a physical ground connection.
The feedback must remain stable at the frequencies where loop gain matters. Extra phase lag can turn the intended opposition into oscillation; adequate phase margin, supply headroom, input common-mode range, output-current capability and slew rate are required. Feedback neither prevents clipping nor removes all input noise.[ref]

A lock-in amplifier multiplies the measured signal by a synchronized reference and low-pass filters the product. For s(t)=Acos(ωt+ϕ)+n(t) and reference 2cosωt, the signal product is Acosϕ+Acos(2ωt+ϕ). Averaging away the second harmonic gives X=Acosϕ in this peak-amplitude convention. A second reference −2sinωt gives Y=Asinϕ, so
A=X2+Y2​,ϕ=atan2(Y,X).​
(30)
A single quadrature could read zero for a substantial signal 90∘ from its reference. Two quadratures avoid that blind spot; the phase is undefined at zero amplitude. Instrument RMS calibrations can include a different constant normalization.
The reference shifts the wanted frequency to zero, where a narrow low-pass bandwidth can select it. Uncorrelated broadband noise averages down, whereas a simple amplifier would enlarge signal and noise together. Longer averaging improves narrow-band sensitivity but slows the response to changing amplitude. The signal must remain phase coherent with the reference; frequency mismatch creates a beat instead of a constant output. interference at the same reference frequency survives, so shielding and independent background measurements remain necessary.[ref]

c OurBigBook.com
Sampling at tn​=n/fs​ makes a frequency f indistinguishable from f+kfs​, since the extra phase is 2πkn. For a real sinusoid, reflection to ∣kfs​−f∣ also gives an alias, up to phase. For example, a 50Hz cosine sampled at 80Hz has exactly the samples of a 30Hz cosine. A measured low-frequency feature can therefore originate above the instrument's Nyquist frequency.
To reconstruct an arbitrary baseband signal limited to bandwidth B, sample strictly above 2B and use an analogue anti-aliasing filter before the sampler. A practical filter needs a transition band, so merely choosing a nominal rate 2B is insufficient. frequency components already folded together cannot in general be separated by later digital filtering. aliasing from insufficient sampling is distinct from spectral leakage caused by a finite observation window.
