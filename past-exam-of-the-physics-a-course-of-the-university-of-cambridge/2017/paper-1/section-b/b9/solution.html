Distinguishable noninteracting particles. Interpret V as the same external one-particle potential for each particle; the total potential is V(r1​)+V(r2​). The Time-dependent Schrödinger equation is
iℏ∂t∂Ψ​=[−2mℏ2​(∇12​+∇22​)+V(r1​)+V(r2​)]Ψ.​
(47)
Let hϕa​=ϵa​ϕa​ and hϕb​=ϵb​ϕb​, where h=−ℏ2∇2/(2m)+V. Applying h1​+h2​ to the product multiplies it by ϵa​+ϵb​. The time derivative of e−iEt/ℏ multiplies it by E. Thus the proposed stationary product is a solution provided
E=ϵa​+ϵb​.​
(48)
The tensor product separates the two independent one-particle dynamics.
Identical spin-one-half particles. Identical fermions require antisymmetry under exchange of the complete position-and-spin labels. First take a,b to be distinct orthonormal spatial orbitals, and define
Φ±​(1,2)=2​ϕa​(r1​)ϕb​(r2​)±ϕb​(r1​)ϕa​(r2​)​.
(49)
The symmetric spatial state must multiply the antisymmetric spin-one-half singlet state; the antisymmetric spatial state must multiply one of the three symmetric spin-one-half triplet states. The four normalized possibilities, each with the common time factor e−iEt/ℏ, are
spatial factorΦ+​Φ−​Φ−​Φ−​​spin factor2​∣↑↓⟩−∣↓↑⟩​∣↑↑⟩2​∣↑↓⟩+∣↓↑⟩​∣↓↓⟩​(S,mS​)(0,0)(1,1)(1,0)(1,−1)​​
(50)
These are the required product states under fermionic exchange symmetry. If a=b, the antisymmetric spatial combination vanishes. Only the product ϕa​(r1​)ϕa​(r2​) times the singlet remains, as required by the Pauli exclusion principle. A nonorthogonal pair would require different normalization factors; the displayed 1/2​ assumes an orthonormal stationary-state basis.
Weak repulsive Coulomb interaction. For fixed orbitals a,b, first-order perturbation theory gives spatial energy shifts J±K. Here J is the direct interaction energy, while the exchange integral for repulsion strength g>0 is
K=g∬∣r−r′∣ϕa∗​(r)ϕb​(r)ϕb∗​(r′)ϕa​(r′)​d3rd3r′≥0.
(51)
The positivity follows from the positive Fourier transform of the Coulomb kernel. Consequently Coulomb exchange splitting of two orbitals gives
Etriplet​=ϵa​+ϵb​+J−K≤Esinglet​=ϵa​+ϵb​+J+K.​
(52)
All three triplet spin components remain degenerate for a spin-independent Coulomb interaction. Their antisymmetric spatial factor vanishes at coincidence and reduces close encounters. The inequality is strict when K>0; for negligible orbital overlap, the splitting may vanish. This comparison fixes one particle in each of the specified orbitals and does not rank singlets with a different orbital occupation.
Spin-zero bosons. For one particle in each of the same distinct orthonormal orbitals, the spin factor is symmetric and bosonic exchange symmetry requires Φ+​. At coincident positions,
∣Φ+​(r,r)∣2=2∣ϕa​(r)∣2∣ϕb​(r)∣2,∣ΨD​(r,r)∣2=∣ϕa​(r)∣2∣ϕb​(r)∣2.
(53)
Thus the bosonic coincidence enhancement in two orthogonal orbitals is
PD​(r,r)PB​(r,r)​=2.​
(54)
These are joint probability densities, or small-detector-volume limits; an exactly specified point has zero probability in a continuous distribution. If both particles occupy the same orbital, the properly normalized product is already symmetric and the corresponding enhancement relative to distinguishable particles in that same orbital is one. The paper's usual factor-two answer presumes distinct orthogonal input orbitals.
