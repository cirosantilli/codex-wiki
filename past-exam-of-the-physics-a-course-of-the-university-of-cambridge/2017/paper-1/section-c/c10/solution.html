Operators, quantization and rotations. Angular momentum is the generator of spatial rotations. In quantum mechanics, its components are Hermitian operators on their appropriate domains and obey
[Ji​,Jj​]=iℏϵijk​Jk​,[J2,Ji​]=0.
(55)
These angular momentum commutation relations prevent all three components from having simultaneous sharp values. For example, the Robertson uncertainty principle gives ΔJx​ΔJy​≥ℏ∣⟨Jz​⟩∣/2. One conventionally diagonalizes J2 and a selected component Jz​:
J2∣j,m⟩=ℏ2j(j+1)∣j,m⟩,Jz​∣j,m⟩=ℏm∣j,m⟩.​
(56)
The allowed labels are j=0,21​,1,23​,… and m=−j,−j+1,…,j. An angular-momentum multiplet has 2j+1 states. The magnitude ℏj(j+1)​ differs from the largest possible component ℏj; the remaining components fluctuate even in a state with maximal m.
The angular momentum lowering operator and its raising counterpart, J±​=Jx​±iJy​, satisfy
J±​∣j,m⟩=ℏj(j+1)−m(m±1)​∣j,m±1⟩.
(57)
The ladder terminates at m=±j. A rotation through angle θ about unit vector n acts through the unitary operator exp[−iθn⋅J/ℏ]. Thus quantized components and the continuous rotation of a state are compatible.
Orbital angular momentum. The orbital contribution is associated with motion in space:
L=r×p​=−iℏr×∇.
(58)
For a spinless spatial wavefunction, the simultaneous angular eigenfunctions are the spherical harmonics Yℓm​(θ,ϕ), with ℓ=0,1,2,… and m=−ℓ,…,ℓ. Single-valued ordinary scalar spatial wavefunctions give integer orbital labels. In a central potential energy, [H,L2]=[H,Lz​]=0, so stationary states may be labelled by these angular quantum numbers. The angular kinetic contribution appears in the radial equation as ℓ(ℓ+1)ℏ2/(2mr2), the centrifugal potential. Noncentral interactions need not conserve these orbital labels.
Intrinsic spin and addition. Spin angular momentum is an intrinsic internal degree of freedom with the same commutation algebra. It is not the angular momentum of a small classical charged sphere literally rotating about its centre. For an electron, s=1/2 and ms​=±1/2, so Si​=(ℏ/2)σi​ in terms of the Pauli matrices. Its squared spin is 3ℏ2/4. Other particles can have integer or half-integer spin, including spin zero.
For one particle with orbital and spin degrees of freedom, J=L+S; the two component sets commute with each other. Addition of angular momentum permits j=∣ℓ−s∣,∣ℓ−s∣+1,…,ℓ+s. The coupled states ∣j,mj​⟩ are linear combinations of the uncoupled ∣ℓ,mℓ​⟩∣s,ms​⟩ with mj​=mℓ​+ms​, determined by Clebsch-Gordan coefficients. A rotationally invariant spin-orbit interaction can conserve J2,Jz​ while mixing the separate component labels.
Angular momentum also carries a magnetic moment. For an electron's orbital motion and intrinsic spin,
μL​=−2me​e​L,μS​=−gs​2me​e​S,gs​≃2.
(59)
The minus signs reflect negative electric charge. With the Bohr magneton μB​=eℏ/(2me​), a spin projection ms​ has μz​=−gs​μB​ms​. A magnetic field can therefore distinguish angular-momentum states through their interaction energy −μ⋅B.
Stern–Gerlach measurement. In the Stern-Gerlach experiment, atoms emerge from a heated source, pass through collimating apertures, traverse a strongly inhomogeneous magnetic field, and strike a position-sensitive screen. The beam is neutral, so its separation is interpreted through the atomic magnetic moment rather than the ordinary charged-particle Lorentz force. Near a chosen quantization direction, the interaction gives
U=−μz​Bz​,Fz​≃μz​∂z∂Bz​​.
(60)
A uniform field can exert a torque but would not separate the beam by this translational force; the gradient is essential. Different allowed magnetic-moment components become correlated with different trajectories. A classical ensemble with continuously varying orientations would yield a continuous spread, whereas discrete quantum components yield separated channels.
In the modern electronic description of ground-state silver, filled shells cancel their electronic angular momenta and the unpaired 5s electron has L=0, S=1/2, J=1/2. The effective electronic measurement therefore has two components, approximately μz​=±μB​, rather than an orbital ℓ=1/2, which is not allowed. For positive field gradient the electronic mJ​=+1/2 component has negative μz​ and deflects downward in this coordinate convention. Nuclear spin and hyperfine structure are neglected in this simplified two-channel account. Historically the original splitting preceded the electron-spin interpretation, so the modern explanation should not be confused with the original investigators' interpretation.
More generally a prepared spin state a∣+z⟩+b∣−z⟩ gives the two outcomes with probabilities ∣a∣2,∣b∣2. Selecting one channel prepares a state for further measurements. An ideal second analyser along the same axis then gives a definite result, whereas one along a perpendicular axis gives two equally likely outcomes for a spin-one-half eigenstate. This connects the apparatus directly to incompatible spin components and the Born rule. Angular momentum is quantized through its operator algebra; the Stern–Gerlach gradient turns that discrete internal information into spatially distinguishable outcomes.
