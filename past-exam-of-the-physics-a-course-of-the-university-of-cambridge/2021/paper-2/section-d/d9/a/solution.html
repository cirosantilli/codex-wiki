In a metal, a partially filled energy band or overlapping energy bands supplies electrons with nearby unoccupied states. At thermal equilibrium, oppositely directed group velocities are equally populated and the net electric current vanishes. An applied electric field distorts the occupation distribution, producing a small mean drift velocity. A filled energy band alone has no such low-field intraband response because its velocity contributions cancel over the entire Brillouin zone.
In the Drude model, let n be the mobile electron number density, let e>0 be the elementary charge, and let τ be the momentum relaxation time. The mean motion obeys
mdtdvd​​=−eE−τmvd​​.
(55)
The steady state is vd​=−eτE/m. The current density is opposite to the electron drift velocity:
J=−nevd​=σE,σ=mne2τ​,ρel​=ne2τm​.​
(56)
This gives microscopic Ohm's law, with electrical conductivity σ in Sm−1 and electrical resistivity ρel​ in Ωm. For a uniform wire of length ℓ and cross-sectional area A, its electrical resistance is R=ρel​ℓ/A. The dissipated power per volume is J⋅E=σE2, the Joule heating supplied by the external source.
The free electron model with Fermi-Dirac statistics improves the microscopic picture. The Pauli exclusion principle fills states up to the Fermi energy at low temperature. For a spherical, parabolic energy band with two spin states,
kF​=(3π2n)1/3,EF​=2mℏ2kF2​​,vF​=mℏkF​​.
(57)
Only states near the Fermi surface change their occupation in a weak-field response; the large equilibrium Fermi velocity is not the small drift velocity. The same ne2τ/m formula results for this isotropic parabolic model when τ is evaluated for transport near the Fermi surface. In a crystal, the energy band group velocity, effective mass and possibly direction-dependent scattering replace the simplest bare-particle picture. The mean free path is approximately ℓmfp​=vF​τ.
A perfectly periodic static lattice is already included in Bloch states and does not by itself give a finite electrical resistivity. Momentum relaxation comes from defects, impurities, boundaries, and electron-phonon scattering. Independent scattering mechanisms approximately add their rates, giving Matthiessen's rule,
τ1​≃τimp​1​+τph​1​+⋯,ρel​(T)≃ρ0​+ρph​(T).
(58)
The approximately temperature-independent ρ0​ is the residual resistivity. In ordinary simple metals, the phonon contribution is approximately proportional to temperature at high temperature; a three-dimensional acoustic-phonon transport model gives a T5 contribution well below the relevant Debye temperature. Other mechanisms can dominate in particular materials or regimes, so neither power law is universal.
The classical Drude model incorrectly treats the equilibrium carrier statistics. Fermi-Dirac statistics explains the small electronic heat capacity, while electrons carrying both charge and heat give the approximate Wiedemann-Franz law κe​/(σT)=π2kB2​/(3e2) for degenerate carriers with appropriate nearly elastic scattering. Both the simple electrical conductivity formula and this thermal relation depend on the assumptions about energy bands and scattering, rather than being exact for all metals.
