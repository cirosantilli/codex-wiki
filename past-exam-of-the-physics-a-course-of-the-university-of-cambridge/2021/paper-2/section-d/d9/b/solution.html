The Debye model approximates a three-dimensional crystal as an elastic continuum supporting quantized normal modes, or phonons. At long wavelengths, the acoustic phonon branches have approximately linear dispersion relations ωs​(k)=vs​k: one longitudinal branch and two transverse branches. Replace their complicated actual spectra by these linear forms, then introduce a cutoff chosen to preserve the total 3N vibrational degrees of freedom for N atoms. This is an approximation to the spectrum, not a claim that a real crystal has no non-acoustic branches.
For periodic boundary conditions in volume V, each allowed wavevector occupies (2π)3/V in the space of wavevectors. Counting a spherical shell for each branch gives the density of states per unit angular frequency
g(ω)=2π2Vω2​(vl3​1​+vt3​2​).
(59)
An effective speed of sound vD​ can be defined by 3/vD3​=1/vl3​+2/vt3​. In the single-speed Debye model, choose ωD​=vD​kD​ so that
∫0ωD​​g(ω)dω=3N.
(60)
Thus the Debye wavevector, Debye frequency and Debye temperature satisfy
kD​=(V6π2N​)1/3,ωD​=vD​kD​,ΘD​=kB​ℏωD​​,g(ω)=ωD3​9Nω2​(0<ω<ωD​).​
(61)
Here g has units of seconds when angular frequency is measured in s−1; its integral is the dimensionless number of normal modes. The spherical cutoff replaces the real Brillouin zone by an equal-count continuum construction.
Each phonon mode is a quantum harmonic oscillator. Since phonon number is not conserved at thermal equilibrium, the chemical potential is zero and its Bose-Einstein distribution gives mean thermal energy ℏω/[eℏω/(kB​T)−1]. The zero-point energy is independent of temperature in the harmonic, fixed-volume approximation and does not contribute to the heat capacity at constant volume. Excluding that constant term,
U(T)=∫0ωD​​eℏω/(kB​T)−1g(ω)ℏω​dω=9NkB​T(ΘD​T​)3∫0ΘD​/T​ex−1x3​dx.
(62)
Differentiating the first integral at fixed volume avoids losing the temperature-dependent upper limit in the second one. The resulting Debye heat capacity is
CV​=9NkB​(ΘD​T​)3∫0ΘD​/T​(ex−1)2x4ex​dx.​
(63)
For T≪ΘD​, extend the upper limit to infinity. Expanding 1/(ex−1)=∑r≥1​e−rx and integrating term by term gives ∫0∞​x3/(ex−1)dx=6ζ(4)=π4/15. Therefore
U≃53π4​NkB​ΘD3​T4​,CV​≃512π4​NkB​(ΘD​T​)3.​
(64)
This T3 law follows from the three-dimensional ω2 density of states and the availability only of low-frequency modes at low temperature. It is a central success of the Debye model.
For T≫ΘD​, the Bose-Einstein distribution reduces to the classical equipartition theorem: every mode contributes thermal energy kB​T. Consequently
U≃3NkB​T,CV​⟶3NkB​,​
(65)
the Dulong-Petit law, or 3R per mole of atoms. The simple model omits detailed phonon dispersion relation, distinct optical phonon branches, and anharmonic effects such as thermal expansion. In a metal, the low-temperature total heat capacity additionally contains a term approximately proportional to T from electrons, so the lattice T3 term should not be mistaken for the whole measurement.
