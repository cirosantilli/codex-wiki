Let the source remain at (0,0,−p) and let the observation point be (X,Y,q). The paraxial approximation to the source-to-aperture-to-observer optical path length is
p+q+2px2+y2​+2q(X−x)2+(Y−y)2​.
(38)
Completing the squares gives
p+q+2(p+q)X2+Y2​+2Reff​(x−x0​)2+(y−y0​)2​,x0​=p+qpX​,y0​=p+qpY​.
(39)
The point (x0​,y0​) is where the straight source-observer line intersects the aperture. Define the four dimensionless edge positions
ux,±​=λReff​2​​(±2d​−x0​),uy,±​=λReff​2​​(±2d​−y0​).
(40)
On the Cornu spiral F(u)=C(u)+iS(u), draw the directed chord from F(ux,−​) to F(ux,+​), and a second directed chord from F(uy,−​) to F(uy,+​). Denote their complex differences by Dx​ and Dy​. The complex amplitude is proportional to their product: multiply their lengths and add their arguments, including the common propagation phase factor.
More explicitly, normalize by the unobscured field ψ0​(X,Y) at this same observation point. The constant wave phase from completing the square is then already included in ψ0​, and
ψ0​(X,Y)ψ(X,Y)​=2iDx​Dy​​,I0​(X,Y)I(X,Y)​=4∣Dx​∣2∣Dy​∣2​.​
(41)
Each directed chord is a one-dimensional Fresnel diffraction factor. One must multiply the two factors, rather than add the chords or use just one chord length for the square aperture. Setting X=Y=0 recovers Dx​=Dy​=2F(w) and the on-axis answer.
