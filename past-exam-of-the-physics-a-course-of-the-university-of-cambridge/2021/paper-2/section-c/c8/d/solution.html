Both have units of mass; their magnitudes are much smaller than m in this asymptotic regime. The upper energy band has a local minimum and positive effective mass, while the lower energy band has a local maximum and negative effective mass. The exact lower-branch sign changes at U=2EB​ in the two-wave formula; the intended weak-coupling limit lies below that threshold.
The physical meaning follows from the semiclassical equations for an electron wave packet in one energy band:
v=ℏ1​dkdE​,ℏk˙=F,v˙=m∗F​.
(54)
An upper-band electron near its minimum accelerates in the direction of the applied force, whereas a lower-band electron near its maximum accelerates opposite to it. At the extremum itself the group velocity is zero. A nearly filled lower energy band is conveniently described by electron holes with positive electric charge +e and positive effective mass mh∗​=−m−∗​. Negative curvature is a response of a crystal energy eigenstate, not a negative bare electron mass. These statements assume slow forcing that does not induce transitions across the band gap.
