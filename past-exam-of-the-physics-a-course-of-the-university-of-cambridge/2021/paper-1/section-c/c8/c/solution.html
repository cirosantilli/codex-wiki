Assume independent approximately Gaussian log-rate errors with known common standard deviation s=0.15. The weighted least squares objective is
χ2(m,c)=s21​∑i=16​(yi​−mxi​−c)2.
(33)
Since all weights agree, the normal equations for linear least squares reduce to
m=Sxx​Sxy​​,c=yˉ​−mxˉ,Sxx​=∑i​(xi​−xˉ)2,Sxy​=∑i​(xi​−xˉ)(yi​−yˉ​).
(34)
For these data, xˉ=1.5ps, yˉ​=4.1596533, Sxx​=4.375ps2 and Sxy​=−10.3782409ps. Hence m=−2.3721693ps−1 and c=7.7179073.
The covariance matrix of the fitted slope and intercept follows from the inverse weighted design matrix product, or directly by writing both estimates as linear combinations of the independent yi​:
Var(m)=Sxx​s2​,Var(c)=s2(61​+Sxx​xˉ2​),Cov(m,c)=−Sxx​s2xˉ​.
(35)
This gives
m=(−2.372±0.072)ps−1,c=7.718±0.124.​
(36)
The parameter conversion and first-order propagation of uncertainty are
τ=−m1​,στ​=m2σm​​,R0​=R∗​ec,σR0​​=R0​σc​.
(37)
Consequently,
τ=(0.4216±0.0127)ps,R0​=(2.25±0.28)×103countsday−1.​
(38)
These are one-standard deviation errors. The estimates are correlated, since Cov(m,c)=−0.0077143ps−1. The minimum is χmin2​=4.6537 for four residual degrees of freedom. Because s is supplied as a known measurement uncertainty, it should not be rescaled by the observed reduced χ2. This is a log-linear exponential-decay fit.
