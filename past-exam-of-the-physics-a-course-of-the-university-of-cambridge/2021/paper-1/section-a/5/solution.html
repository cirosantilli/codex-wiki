For a real signal with highest frequency fmax​=40MHz, the Nyquist–Shannon sampling theorem gives the nominal Nyquist sampling rate
fs,Nyquist​=2fmax​=80MHz=80×106 samples per second.​
(9)
To cover an arbitrary component exactly at the band edge, choose fs​>80MHz: a 40MHz sine sampled at exactly 80MHz can have every sample equal to zero for an unfortunate phase. Practical aliasing suppression also calls for a margin above the nominal limiting rate.
With rounding to the nearest digitizer level, quantization with step q=0.1mV has the uniform-quantization error bound
∣Vdigitized​−Vsample​∣≤q/2=0.05mV.​
(10)
Thus the stated digitizer meets the requested nominal amplitude precision per sample, assuming no clipping or other errors. The bandwidth sets the nominal minimum sampling rate; the level spacing sets the rounding precision. Strictly, the sampling theorem reconstructs exactly from exact samples. A uniform 0.05mV error bound on the entire reconstructed continuous waveform is not guaranteed by the level spacing and sampling rate alone; that stronger requirement needs a specified reconstruction and noise model.
