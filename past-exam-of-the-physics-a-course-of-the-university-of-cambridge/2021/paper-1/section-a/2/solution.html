Inside the rectangular potential barrier, the electron's wavefunction is evanescent, with decay constant
κ=ℏ2me​(V0​−E)​​=ℏ2me​(2eV)​​≃7.25nm−1.
(3)
For width a=1nm, the squared exponential suppression in the quantum tunnelling probability is
T∼e−2κa=e−14.49≃5.1×10−7.​
(4)
This is the usual leading-exponential estimate. The rectangular-barrier tunnelling probability, retaining the interface-matching prefactor for equal potentials on the two sides, is
T=[1+4E(V0​−E)V02​sinh2(κa)​]−1≃V02​16E(V0​−E)​e−2κa.
(5)
Here that prefactor is 16(8)(2)/102=2.56, so the more accurate result is T≃1.30×10−6​. Both estimates show that transmission is of order one part in a million. A classical particle with this kinetic energy cannot cross the barrier at all.
