The normalized Sx​ eigenvectors are (∣↑⟩±∣↓⟩)/2​, and the Sy​ eigenvectors are (∣↑⟩±i∣↓⟩)/2​, with eigenvalues ±ℏ/2 in each case.
For N=∣a∣2+∣b∣2>0, allowing for a possibly unnormalized input pair of coefficients,
⟨S^z​⟩=2ℏ​N∣a∣2−∣b∣2​,⟨S^2⟩=43ℏ2​.​
(24)
For a normalized state N=1. The second result follows because each squared Spin operator matrix equals ℏ2I^/4.
The tilted component is S^z′​=(ℏ/2)(cosθsinθ​sinθ−cosθ​). Its eigenvalues remain ±ℏ/2, with one real phase convention for the normalized eigenvectors
∣+z′⟩=cos(θ/2)∣↑⟩+sin(θ/2)∣↓⟩,∣−z′⟩=−sin(θ/2)∣↑⟩+cos(θ/2)∣↓⟩.​
(25)
Projecting the initial ∣↑⟩ onto them gives P+​=cos2(θ/2) and P−​=sin2(θ/2). Thus
⟨S^z′​⟩=2ℏ​(P+​−P−​)=2ℏ​cosθ.​
(26)
A smaller expectation does not imply a smaller individual spin eigenvalue; it describes the probabilities of the same two measurement outcomes.
