Take the electron's signed charge to be e=−q, where q>0 is the elementary-charge magnitude. Conventional electric current flows along +x, while the mean electron drift velocity is negative. With B=Bz​z^, the magnetic Lorentz force separates charge across the width until a transverse electric field balances it. The accumulated edge charge then sustains a Hall voltage without a transverse steady current. In a one-carrier model, the Hall coefficient identifies the sign and number density of carriers; real multiband materials require a more careful interpretation.
The acceleration term contains the effective mass describing the local band response. The relaxation term removes drift momentum on the mean momentum-relaxation time τ. The remaining two terms are the electric and magnetic Lorentz force on signed charge e; the magnetic force is perpendicular to velocity and does no instantaneous work. The relaxation-time approximation replaces individual scattering events by an averaged drift equation.

A measured voltage ratio additionally needs probe polarities, which the geometric bar diagram does not label. To reproduce the positive ratio specified for electrons, define the longitudinal voltage drop Vx​=φ(0)−φ(l)=Ex​l and the transverse rise Vy​=φ(w)−φ(0)=−Ey​w. Then

A monovalent copper model assigns roughly one delocalized conduction electron per atom to a broad, approximately free-electron-like band. Using me​ is therefore a sensible first estimate for its transport scale. Monovalence alone does not prove that every band curvature or orbit has exactly the free mass: periodic-potential mixing, the nonspherical Fermi surface and direction-dependent scattering limit the model. The calculated τ is conditional on the stipulated effective-mass approximation.
Experimentally, reverse both current and magnetic field and extract the part of the transverse voltage odd in each. This rejects fixed contact offsets and much longitudinal pickup; use four-terminal longitudinal sensing, measure dimensions independently and check heating before assigning material parameters. These controls are implemented in a reusable Hall/conductivity teaching experiment, although that experiment uses germanium rather than the copper sample here.[ref]
