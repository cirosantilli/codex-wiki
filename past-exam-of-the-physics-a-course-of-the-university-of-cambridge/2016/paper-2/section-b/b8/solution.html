The Fourier expression is the scalar Fraunhofer diffraction approximation for coherent, effectively monochromatic illumination with a uniform incident plane wave over the aperture. At a distant screen the aperture-dependent quadratic propagation phase must be negligible, roughly D2/(λz)≪1 for transverse size D. Alternatively, a thin lens forms the angular Fourier transform in its back focal plane. That focal-plane construction is the appropriate one here; the aperture-to-lens distance need not itself be an enormous free-propagation distance. aperture features should be large compared with wavelength for this simple scalar transmission-mask model.
If complementary transmissions are h and 1−h, linearity gives Uh​+U1−h​=Ufree​. Away from the undiffracted forward beam, the uniform plane-wave contribution Ufree​ vanishes in angular space, so U1−h​=−Uh​ and their intensities agree. This is Babinet's principle. It equates the off-forward diffracted intensities, not the transmitted power or the on-axis intensity including the direct beam.
Let X,Y be screen coordinates conjugate to aperture coordinates x,y, so p≃kX/f, q≃kY/f. With width a=0.2 mm along x and height b=0.5 mm along y, define sincu=sinu/u. The complementary rectangle has transform
H(p,q)=absinc(pa/2)sinc(qb/2).
(25)
Thus the obstruction's off-forward scattered component has
Isc​(X,Y)∝sinc2(λfπaX​)sinc2(λfπbY​).
(26)
Its dark lines occur at
X=m(0.500 mm),Y=n(0.200 mm),m,n=±1,±2,….​
(27)
The central-lobe zero-to-zero size is 1.00 mm by 0.400 mm; it is wider in the direction conjugate to the narrower obstruction. The central forward image of the illuminating beam must be distinguished from this scattered envelope in a real screen observation.
The source figure gives centre-to-centre separation D=1.0 mm. Choose the three rectangle centres at x=−D,0,D; an overall translation changes only the transform phase. Their scattered amplitude is proportional to
H3​(p,q)=absinc(pa/2)sinc(qb/2)[1+2cos(pD)].
(28)
Therefore
Isc​(X,Y)∝sinc2(λfπaX​)sinc2(λfπbY​)[1+2cos(λf2πDX​)]2.​
(29)
Along the screen's x direction, the three-element interference factor has principal maxima separated by λf/D=0.100 mm, under the single-rectangle envelope. Its zeros lie at X=(m+1/3)(0.100 mm) and (m+2/3)(0.100 mm). It also has weaker secondary maxima between principal orders. Every fifth principal order coincides with a single-rectangle zero and is missing; multiplication by the envelope slightly shifts maxima away from the nominal interference-factor positions.
