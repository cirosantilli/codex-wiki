Let the first transmitted complex amplitude be TEin​eikd: two amplitude transmission factors each of magnitude T​ give magnitude T. Each extra internal round trip adds two amplitude reflections, with combined magnitude R, and phase δ. With the mirror phase convention already incorporated in the stipulated δ,
Eout​=TEin​eikd∑j=0∞​(Reiδ)j=1−ReiδTEin​eikd​.

Taking the squared modulus gives
B=1+R2−2RcosδT2​.​
(31)
It is R, not R2, that multiplies the field after a round trip; R2 would be the corresponding round-trip intensity fraction.
At resonance δ=2mπ, Bmax​=T2/(1−R)2. If h is the positive phase displacement to half maximum,
sin(h/2)=2R​1−R​,h=2arcsin2R​1−R​≃R​1−R​.

For R=0.80, the stipulated high-reflectivity approximation gives h=0.2236 rad, while the exact value is 0.2241 rad. The full width is twice this value. With T=0.20, Bmax​=1, Bmin​=T2/(1+R)2=1/81 and the pattern repeats every 2π. Its ideal finesse is approximately πR​/(1−R)=14.05.
A wavelength component resonates at d=mλ/2. Within the scan interval, the individual resonances are
λ (nm)1000.01000.2​m10001000​d (mm)0.5000000.500100​​
(33)
No neighboring orders of these lines fall within the stated interval. The observed signal is the sum of their two Airy transmissions, since different spectral components add intensities. Individual resonance positions identify the two peaks to the plotted precision; the tails cause very small shifts of the maxima of the sum.
The gap-coordinate full width is
ΔdFWHM​=2πλh​≃0.0357 μm.
(34)
The separation is 0.100 μm, about 2.8 full widths, so the two components can be resolved by the ideal etalon. The equivalent wavelength resolution near order 1000 is about λ/(mF)≃0.071 nm, smaller than their 0.2 nm separation.
The added wavelength has an order-999 resonance at
d=2999(1001.1 nm)​=0.50004945 mm.​
(35)
It lies between the preceding two peaks; its adjacent orders are outside the scan. Its input strength was not specified, so the third peak's height cannot be uniquely assigned. The original plot uses equal strength only as a declared illustration.
This is an order-identification problem: the free spectral range near d=0.5 mm is ΔλFSR​≃λ2/(2d)≃1 nm. A line more than one free spectral range away can appear among peaks of another order. A lower-resolution prefilter or additional dispersive spectrometer is needed to identify the order; a narrow peak alone does not identify an absolute wavelength. mirror parallelism, beam collimation and detector angular acceptance must also be controlled before attributing a measured width entirely to reflectivity.[ref]
