For independent Gaussian measurements with correctly specified standard deviations, model residuals give the chi-squared test statistic
X2=∑i=1n​σi2​[yi​−fi​(θ)]2​.
(30)
For a linear fit of p identifiable parameters under the null model, it has a chi-squared distribution with ν=n−p degrees of freedom. The upper-tail p-value P(χν2​≥Xobs2​) tests unusually large residuals; a nonlinear fit generally uses this distribution only approximately. Correlated Gaussian errors instead require X2=rTΣ−1r.
For counts, Pearson's chi-squared test uses X2=∑j​(Oj​−Ej​)2/Ej​, with Ej​ calculated under the null hypothesis. For k multinomial categories and p fitted parameters the usual asymptotic degrees of freedom are k−1−p; the subtraction of one reflects the fixed total count. Sparse expected counts can invalidate this approximation, requiring a suitable exact or simulation-based test. Contingency-table independence gives (r−1)(c−1) degrees of freedom.
A small p-value indicates tension with the specified model and error assumptions; it is not the probability that the null is true. Excessive X2 may reflect a wrong model, underestimated errors, correlations or outliers. An implausibly small value may suggest overestimated errors or hidden constraints. For an adequate fitted model X2/ν is typically near one, with fluctuations, rather than required to equal one.
