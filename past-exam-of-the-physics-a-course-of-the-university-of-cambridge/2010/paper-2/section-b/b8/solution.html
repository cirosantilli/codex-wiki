The angular-momentum commutators give
[L+​,Lz​]=−ℏL+​,[L−​,Lz​]=+ℏL−​,[L+​,L−​]=2ℏLz​​.
(14)
For example [Lx​+iLy​,Lz​]=−iℏLy​+i(iℏLx​)=−ℏL+​. In the displayed basis ∣1,1⟩,∣1,0⟩,∣1,−1⟩, the only nonzero upper entries of L+​ connect adjacent magnetic quantum numbers. Subtracting their column and row Lz​ eigenvalues multiplies each by −ℏ, proving the first matrix commutator; the lower matrix similarly gives the second. Direct multiplication gives
L+​L−​=2ℏ2diag(1,1,0),L−​L+​=2ℏ2diag(0,1,1),
(15)
whose difference is 2ℏLz​. Also L2=Lz2​+(L+​L−​+L−​L+​)/2=2ℏ2I, confirming ℓ=1.
A rotation changes the quantization axis without changing the spectrum, so Ln​ has eigenvalues +ℏ,0,−ℏ. Its dimensionless matrix is
ℏLn​​=​cosθsinθ/2​0​sinθ/2​0sinθ/2​​0sinθ/2​−cosθ​​.
(16)
Solving (Ln​−ℏI)v=0 and normalizing gives the spin-one maximum-projection eigenvector
v+​(θ)=​cos2(θ/2)sinθ/2​sin2(θ/2)​​​.
(17)
For instance the first equation is (cosθ−1)cos2(θ/2)+sin2θ/2=0, and the squared components sum to one. An overall phase is immaterial.
At θ=π/3, v+​=(3/4,6​/4,1/4)T. The Born rule therefore predicts, in the requested order,
P(Lz​=−ℏ)=161​,P(Lz​=0)=83​,P(Lz​=+ℏ)=169​​.
(18)
This treats the Stern-Gerlach experiment as the stipulated ideal measurement of orbital projection. In an actual atom, other magnetic moments and spin-orbit coupling can change which angular momentum the apparatus resolves.
