quantum tunnelling is transmission through a finite region in which the classical kinetic energy E−V is negative. The wavefunction need not vanish there: the Schrödinger equation permits evanescent solutions. The transmitted particle retains its conserved energy; it does not borrow energy to surmount the barrier.
For a rectangular barrier of height Vb​ on 0<x<d, with 0<E<Vb​ and zero exterior potential energy, define k=2mE​/ℏ and κ=2m(Vb​−E)​/ℏ. A left-incident scattering solution has the three pieces
ψL​=eikx+re−ikx,ψB​=beκx+ce−κx,ψR​=teikx.
(27)
Continuity of ψ,ψ′ at both boundaries imposes
1+r=b+c,ik(1−r)=κ(b−c),beκd+ce−κd=teikd,κ(beκd−ce−κd)=ikteikd.
(28)
Eliminating b,c,r gives
t=coshκd+i2kκκ2−k2​sinhκde−ikd​,T=∣t∣2=[1+4E(Vb​−E)Vb2​sinh2(κd)​]−1​.
(29)
Equal incident and exit velocities justify T=∣t∣2; different exterior potential energies require the velocity ratio. Probability-current conservation gives R+T=1. Although either real exponential alone has zero current, their complex-coefficient combination can carry the current crossing the barrier.
For κd≫1, T≃16E(Vb​−E)Vb−2​e−2κd. A slowly varying barrier generalizes this to the WKB approximation T∼exp[−2∫x1​x2​​2m(V−E)​dx/ℏ], with matching prefactors and turning-point limitations. This exponential sensitivity explains why very small geometric or energy changes can produce large changes in a tunnelling rate.
In alpha decay, a quasibound alpha particle penetrates the nuclear Coulomb barrier; penetration probability, attempt frequency and nuclear preformation determine the rate. In a scanning tunnelling microscope, a bias between a conducting tip and sample gives current approximately proportional to e−2κd. Piezoelectric positioning and constant-current feedback probe surfaces, while vibration isolation and electronic-state dependence limit a purely geometric interpretation. Atomic-scale steps and surface reconstruction were resolved in the original surface-imaging study.[ref] In a Josephson junction, coherent tunnelling of Cooper pairs permits I=Ic​sinφ, a phase-dependent supercurrent through a weak link. Thus the same barrier physics explains natural decay and underpins sensitive surface and superconducting devices.
