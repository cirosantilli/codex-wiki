For a classical ideal monatomic gas, CV,He​=3NkB​/2 and Cp,He​=5NkB​/2. Treat copper as having one conduction electron per atom. Its degenerate-electron contribution is
Ce,Cu​=2π2​NkB​EF​kB​T​.
(7)
Consequently the electron-only estimates at5K are
Ce,Cu​CV,He​​=4.94×103,Ce,Cu​Cp,He​​=8.23×103.​
(8)
The constant-pressure gas versus electronic metal heat capacity convention makes the second numerator appropriate to a constant-pressure gas measurement. For the solid, the low-temperature difference between Cp​ and CV​ is usually small, but its phonon contribution is not fixed by EF​. In a Debye model,
CV,Cu​Cp,He​​≃(π2/2)kB​T/EF​+(12π4/5)(T/ΘD​)35/2​.
(9)
The supplied EF​ alone therefore does not determine the ratio to copper's total heat capacity; the electronic-only result is conditional.[ref] An estimate using additional material data is possible: the specific-heat fit in Material Properties: OFHC Copper by National Institute of Standards and Technology gives cp,Cu​(5K)≃0.155Jkg−1K−1. Using copper's rounded molar mass 0.0635kgmol−1 gives Cp,Cu​≃9.85×10−3Jmol−1K−1, and hence
Cp,Cu​Cp,He​​≃9.85×10−3Jmol−1K−15R/2​≃2.1×103.​
(10)
This supplementary estimate includes copper's lattice contribution and retains the ideal-gas approximation for helium. The NIST fit reports a 10% deviation from its source data below 15 K; neither that fit nor the stated pressure alone supplies helium nonideality corrections.[ref]
