The transient is the homogeneous part of a damped harmonic oscillator, mx¨+bx˙+kx=0. Define α=b/(2m) and ω0​=k/m​. Its characteristic roots are −α±α2−ω02​​.
  • For α<ω0​, the underdamped response is x=e−αt(Acosωd​t+Bsinωd​t), with ωd​=ω02​−α2​. The displacement oscillates with a decaying envelope.
  • At critical damping, x=(A+Bt)e−ω0​t. It has a repeated root, so two identical exponentials are not an independent pair of solutions.
  • For α>ω0​, two real negative exponentials produce an overdamped return without sustained oscillation. In the strong-damping limit the slow decay rate is approximately k/b, not b/m.
Initial position and velocity determine A,B. For an imposed force or support motion, the full solution is a particular response plus this transient, which decays for b>0 and k>0. The mechanical energy obeys d(K+U)/dt=−bx˙2. In light damping its cycle-averaged envelope decays as e−2αt, with Q≃ω0​/(2α). Initial conditions can still produce an overshoot in a nonoscillatory regime; the damping category describes the roots rather than every possible trajectory.
