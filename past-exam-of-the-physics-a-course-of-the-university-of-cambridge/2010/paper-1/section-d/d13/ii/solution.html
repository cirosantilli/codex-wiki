Lattice energy is transported by phonons; long-wavelength acoustic modes usually dominate low-temperature conduction. Their speed is set by elastic wave propagation. In the Debye model their volumetric heat capacity grows as T3 at low temperature and approaches the classical lattice limit at high temperature. With boundary-limited mean free path, this gives a low-temperature T3 conductivity; increasing phonon scattering can instead produce a peak and a declining high-temperature trend.
In metals, electrons near the Fermi surface carry both heat and charge. Their heat capacity is linear in T and their typical transport speed is the Fermi velocity, not a classical gas thermal speed. Degeneracy restricts the active thermal fraction but the large speed and long mean free path can make electronic heat conduction dominant. Insulators have few mobile electrons, so phonon conduction usually dominates. The approximate parallel-channel sum is κ=κe​+κph​; which channel dominates is material- and temperature-dependent.
