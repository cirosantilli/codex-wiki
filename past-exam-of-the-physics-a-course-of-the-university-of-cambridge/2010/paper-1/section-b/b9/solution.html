Fresnel diffraction retains the quadratic transverse propagation phase; Fraunhofer diffraction neglects it and leaves an aperture Fourier transform. Expanding the propagation distance gives z+y2/(2z)+⋯. The aperture phase variation is of order πρ2/(λz), so the crossover scale is z∼ρ2/λ, with far-field propagation requiring a much smaller Fresnel number than1.
Here λ≃0.0100m and the full gap width gives ρ2/λ=25m, comparable with the16m range. More decisively, measured from the grazing line of sight to the far gap edge, the quadratic phase reaches π(0.5)2/(0.01×16)=4.91rad. Fresnel diffraction is required; conventions using a half-width change the quoted Fresnel number but not this phase test.

For the largest contiguous Fresnel aperture, a freely chosen single contiguous opening must maximize the chord between two points of the Cornu spiral. Its longest chord joins F(−w∗​) to F(w∗​), where the first maximum radius satisfies C(w∗​)cos(πw∗2​/2)+S(w∗​)sin(πw∗2​/2)=0. This gives w∗​=1.2094 and
−0.342m<x<+0.342m,Pmax​/Popen​≃1.80.​

Here x is signed displacement along the fence from the grazing optical axis. The pointwise Fresnel gain versus integrated transmitted power distinction explains why an aperture can concentrate more power at one receiver than the fully open field through constructive interference; this is not an increase of integrated transmitted power.
The existing-opening constraint in Fresnel aperture optimization matters literally: the original gap already occupies [0,0.5]m. The symmetric optimum would require filling its far end. If only further removal is permitted and the final opening must be connected, maximize the chord subject to w−​≤0 and w+​≥1.768. The constrained optimum is approximately
w−​=−1.2158,w+​=2.3405,−0.344m<x<0.662m,P/Popen​=1.60.​
