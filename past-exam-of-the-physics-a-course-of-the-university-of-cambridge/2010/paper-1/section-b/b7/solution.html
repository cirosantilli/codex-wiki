A dispersive wave has phase speed depending on frequency. Its phase velocity is vp​=ω/k, whereas the group velocity vg​=dω/dk describes a narrow wave packet in a lossless single branch. Examples are deep-water gravity waves, ω2=gk, and electromagnetic waves in a cold plasma, ω2=ωp2​+c2k2.
The fixed-edge membrane waveguide has transverse profiles sin(nπy/b) with
ky​=nπ/b,n=1,2,…,ωn2​=v2[kx2​+(nπ/b)2].​
(14)
There is no nonzero n=0 displacement satisfying both fixed-edge conditions. Even though the unconfined membrane is nondispersive, transverse confinement makes propagation along x dispersive. The membrane-guide phase and energy speeds are, for a propagating mode,
vp,x​=kx​ω​,vg,x​=ωv2kx​​,vp,x​vg,x​=v2,fc,n​=2bnv​.​
(15)
For positive kx​, the phase speed exceeds v while group speed is below v. At cutoff kx​=0 the formal phase speed diverges, but group speed vanishes; no finite longitudinal energy flux is implied.
With the supplied width and speed, fc,n​=10nkHz. At15kHz only n=1 propagates:
kx​=(30π)2−(20π)2​=10π5​=70.2m−1.​
(16)
