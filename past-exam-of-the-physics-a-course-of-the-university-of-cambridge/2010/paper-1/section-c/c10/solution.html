Write the lattice reciprocal vector as G=2π/a in one dimension. The nearly-free electron model starts from εk​=ℏ2k2/(2me​) and couples plane waves separated by G. Near a zone boundary, the two-wave Hamiltonian is
(εk​VG∗​​VG​εk−G​​),E±​=2εk​+εk−G​​±(2εk​−εk−G​​)2+∣VG​∣2​.
(26)
At k=G/2 the free waves are degenerate and the periodic potential opens a gap 2∣VG​∣. A reduced-zone drawing folds k modulo G into [−G/2,G/2], displaying multiple bands at each reduced wavevector. The original plot shows free folded parabolas, avoided crossings and an allowed vertical optical transition. [ref]
Including two spin states, filling a free Fermi sphere gives n=2(4πkF3​/3)/(2π)3=kF3​/(3π2). A monovalent BCC cell contributes two electrons, so
kF​=(3π2n)1/3=a(6π2)1/3​,2​π/akF​​=0.877<1.​
(27)
The sphere therefore lies inside every first-zone face, since the supplied distance is the nearest boundary.
For the shortest BCC reciprocal vector, G=22​π/a. In the zero-potential folded-band limit, the energy of a vertical transition from k along G to the neighbouring free branch is ℏ2(G2−2Gk)/(2me​). It decreases over the occupied interval and is smallest at k=kF​. Thus the monovalent BCC direct optical threshold is
EF​E0​​=(kF​G​)2−2kF​G​=0.6376≃0.64.​
(28)
The final state is empty because G−kF​>kF​. Finite weak VG​ changes the energies and makes optical matrix elements nonzero; an exactly free electron gas by itself has no interband absorption. The expression is a leading weak-potential threshold, not a claim that every peak equals the onset.
Using the supplied lattice constants and bare-electron free bands gives EF​=(3.24,2.12,1.85)eV and thresholds (2.06,1.35,1.18)eV for Na,K,Rb. The source plot's broad higher-frequency features shift toward lower energy in the same order as a increases, qualitatively consistent with the predicted a−2 scale. Their peak shapes, heights and exact positions need matrix elements, band corrections and scattering; low-frequency intraband conductivity is a separate contribution. No measured trace is reproduced.
