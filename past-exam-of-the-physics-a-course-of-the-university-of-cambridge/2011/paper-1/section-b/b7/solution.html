A wave packet is a localized superposition of nearby wavenumbers. Its rapid carrier crests have phase velocity vp​=ω/k; the slowly varying envelope translates at the group velocity. Dispersion means that ω(k) is not simply a constant times k, so different carrier components have different phase velocities. Expand around k0​:
u(x,t)=Re∫A(k)ei[kx−ω(k)t]dk≃Re{ei(k0​x−ω0​t)∫A(k0​+κ)eiκ[x−ω′(k0​)t]dκ}.
(11)
The integral is an envelope depending on x−ω′(k0​)t, so vg​=dω/dk at the central frequency. Neglected curvature causes packet spreading. For a deep-water gravity wave, ω=gk​, hence vp​=g/k​ and
vg​=21​g/k​=vp​/2=4πgT​.​
(12)
For crest crossings through a dispersive wave packet, an instantaneous group containing six crests has length approximately L=6λ. A stationary ship takes L/vg​ to encounter the group; its crest-arrival frequency is vp​/λ. Consequently
N=λvp​​vg​L​=12.​
(13)
In the envelope frame the ship moves backwards at vg​ while crests move forwards at vp​−vg​=vg​. Their relative speed is 2vg​, explaining why counting a snapshot and counting crossings differ.
To estimate deep-water swell distance from period-dependent arrival times, assume a localized storm emits both periods at the same time and place, propagation is in deep water, and currents and refraction can be neglected. If its distance is D, the arrival lag obeys
86400=D(vg​(15s)1​−vg​(20s)1​)=g4πD​(151​−201​).
(14)
With g=9.81ms−2, D=15g(86400)/π≃4.05×106m, about 4050 km. The corresponding travel times are three and four days. A moving or long-lived storm with frequency-dependent emission times would invalidate the unique distance inference.
