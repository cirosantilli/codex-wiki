The wave impedance is the travelling-wave field ratio Z=E/H; for the stated nonmagnetic media it is Z0​/n. A reflection coefficient is a reflected-to-incident complex amplitude ratio with an explicitly chosen field orientation. With this reflection-amplitude sign convention, using the question's formula for incidence inside silicon gives
r=Z0​/3.5+Z0​Z0​/3.5−Z0​​=−95​​,R=∣r∣2=8125​.

With a common tangential electric-field direction, the usual electric-field Fresnel equations instead give rE​=(Z2​−Z1​)/(Z2​+Z1​)=+5/9 for this incidence. The printed formula corresponds to the opposite reflected-field sign convention, for example the magnetic amplitude convention. It gives the same reflectance and the same round-trip factor r2.
Each extra internal round trip multiplies the transmitted amplitude by r2eiδ, with δ=4πnd/λ. A geometric series therefore gives

Identical highly reflecting coatings increase the finesse: transmission maxima become narrower and the intensity between them falls. Ideal symmetric lossless coatings retain Tmax​=1; absorption or unequal mirrors reduce it. The illustrated maxima stay fixed only because additional reflection wave phases are held unchanged. Real coating wave phases can also shift the resonances.
