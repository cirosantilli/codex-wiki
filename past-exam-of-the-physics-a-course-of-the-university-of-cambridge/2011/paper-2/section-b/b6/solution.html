For each mode in Cartesian coordinates, [ai​,aj†​]=δij​ and ai†​ai​=Hi​/(ℏω)−1/2. Consequently the two-dimensional isotropic harmonic oscillator has
H=ℏω(Nx​+Ny​+1)​,Ni​=ai†​ai​,Ekl​=ℏω(k+l+1).
(6)
The normalized actions of the creation and annihilation operators are
ax​∣k,l⟩=k​∣k−1,l⟩,ay†​∣k,l⟩=l+1​∣k,l+1⟩,ax​∣0,l⟩=0.
(7)
Invert their definitions as x=ℏ/(2mω)​(ax​+ax†​) and px​=imℏω/2​(ax†​−ax​), and similarly for y. linear operators belonging to different coordinates commute, so substitution into Lz​=xpy​−ypx​ gives
Lz​=2iℏ​[(ax​+ax†​)(ay†​−ay​)−(ay​+ay†​)(ax†​−ax​)]=−iℏ(ax†​ay​−ay†​ax​)​.
(8)
Each term transfers one excitation between the two modes: [Nx​+Ny​,ax†​ay​]=(1−1)ax†​ay​=0, and likewise for its adjoint. Thus [H,Lz​]=0 and, with no explicit time dependence, dLz​/dt=(i/ℏ)[H,Lz​]=0. This is conservation of angular momentum under planar rotations.
Finally Lz​∣1,0⟩=iℏ∣0,1⟩ and Lz​∣0,1⟩=−iℏ∣1,0⟩. Hence
Lz​(∣1,0⟩+i∣0,1⟩)=ℏ(∣1,0⟩+i∣0,1⟩)​.
(9)
The angular momentum is +ℏ; division by 2​ performs wavefunction normalization on this state without changing its eigenvalue. It is one excitation of the positive circular mode.
