The spherical harmonics form an orthonormal angular basis. The integer restriction is specific to orbital wavefunctions; half-integer spin is an intrinsic degree of freedom, not the angular dependence of a scalar spatial wavefunction.
The angular momentum ladder operators obey
L±​Yℓm​=ℏℓ(ℓ+1)−m(m±1)​Yℓ,m±1​.
(40)
The ladder ends at m=±ℓ, producing 2ℓ+1 states. A state with specified m has a sharp projection ℏm, but the angular momentum magnitude is ℏℓ(ℓ+1)​, not ℏℓ. No classical picture of a vector with simultaneously fixed Cartesian components reproduces all these measurement properties.
In a central potential, rotational symmetry gives [H,Li​]=0. separation of variables yields ψ(r)=Rℓ​(r)Yℓm​(θ,ϕ). The radial Schrödinger equation includes a centrifugal potential ℏ2ℓ(ℓ+1)/(2mr2), which suppresses small-radius amplitude for nonzero ℓ. The energy is independent of m when rotational symmetry is intact, but need not be independent of ℓ. A magnetic field can break that degeneracy. Spatial inversion gives Yℓm​(−r)=(−1)ℓYℓm​(r), connecting orbital angular momentum to parity and to the even/odd rotational restriction for the hydrogen spin isomers.
