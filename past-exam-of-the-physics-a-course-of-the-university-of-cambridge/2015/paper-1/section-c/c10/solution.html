A density operator describes both a quantum state prepared in one pure state and a statistical preparation whose members occupy different pure states. If normalized vectors ∣ψi​⟩ occur with probabilities pi​, define
ρ=∑i​pi​∣ψi​⟩⟨ψi​∣,pi​≥0,∑i​pi​=1.
(32)
The resulting operator is Hermitian, positive semidefinite and has trace one. Positivity follows because ⟨v∣ρ∣v⟩=∑i​pi​∣⟨ψi​∣v⟩∣2≥0. Conversely, the spectral theorem gives such a representation of any finite-dimensional operator with these properties, using its eigenvectors and eigenvalues. The matrix representing ρ depends on the chosen basis; the operator and its predictions do not.
One operator gives every measurement probability. For a projective measurement with orthogonal projections Πa​ and outcomes a,
P(a)=Tr(ρΠa​),⟨A⟩=Tr(ρA),(ΔA)2=Tr(ρA2)−⟨A⟩2.
(33)
For example, the first formula follows by averaging ⟨ψi​∣Πa​∣ψi​⟩ over the preparation. The trace expression is basis independent. A general measurement replaces Πa​ by positive measurement effects that sum to the identity, without changing the probability formula. Thus ρ specifies the statistics accessible to measurements on that system.
The distinction between pure and mixed states is especially transparent in the eigenvalues. A pure state has ρ=∣ψ⟩⟨ψ∣, ρ2=ρ, and Trρ2=1. A mixed state has at least two positive eigenvalues and Trρ2<1. For a qubit,
ρ=21​(I+r⋅σ),∣r∣≤1,Trρ2=21​(1+∣r∣2).
(34)
pure states lie on the Bloch sphere; mixed states lie inside the Bloch ball. The maximally mixed qubit ρ=I/2 can be prepared by choosing equally between the two z spin eigenstates, or equally between the two x spin eigenstates. These ensembles are different preparation procedures but give the same density operator. No measurement on the resulting qubit alone can identify which ensemble was used.
quantum coherence must be distinguished from merely having two populated levels. In the z basis, the state ∣+x⟩ has
ρ+x​=21​(11​11​),ρmix​=21​(10​01​).
(35)
Both give equal probabilities for the two z outcomes. A measurement along x gives the positive result with probability one for the first state and one half for the second. The off-diagonal entries encode the relative phase information responsible for this difference. Whether a matrix entry is off diagonal is basis dependent; purity is not.
For an isolated system, each component evolves with the same unitary time-evolution operator. Therefore ρ(t)=U(t)ρ(0)U†(t) and
iℏρ˙​=[H,ρ]​.
(36)
Unitary evolution preserves the eigenvalues and purity of ρ. A density operator commuting with a time-independent Hamiltonian operator is stationary. A thermal equilibrium example is the canonical ensemble ρβ​=e−βH/Z, where Z=Tr(e−βH) and β=1/(kB​T). expectation values then reproduce quantum statistical mechanics without assigning the system a definite energy eigenstate on every run.
A mixed state can also arise without classical randomness in the joint preparation. For a bipartite quantum system, the reduced density operator ρA​=TrB​ρAB​ gives every measurement probability accessible to subsystem A. For the Bell state (∣00⟩+∣11⟩)/2​, the joint state is pure, whereas ρA​=I/2. Its local mixedness reflects entanglement with the inaccessible system; the partial trace discards correlations that matter for joint measurements. More generally, interaction with an environment can suppress off-diagonal entries in a selected basis, producing decoherence while the combined system still evolves unitarily.
Finally, state evolution conditional on a measurement outcome differs from isolated evolution. An ideal projective measurement yielding a updates the state by the Lüders rule ρ↦Πa​ρΠa​/P(a). Discarding the outcome gives ∑a​Πa​ρΠa​, which generally removes coherence between different eigenspaces. The Von Neumann entropy −Tr(ρlnρ) quantifies mixedness: it vanishes for pure states and reaches lnd for I/d. Multiplying by kB​ gives thermodynamic entropy units. These uses make the density operator a common language for preparation uncertainty, measurement, thermal states and entangled subsystems, while retaining their distinct physical origins.
