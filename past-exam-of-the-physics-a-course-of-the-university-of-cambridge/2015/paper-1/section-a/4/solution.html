For a fixed magnitude zero point, photometric magnitude uncertainty follows from propagation of uncertainty
dfdm​=−fln102.5​,σm​=ln102.5​fσf​​=0.1086 mag​.
(5)
This is the absolute standard uncertainty of the apparent magnitude. The requested fractional uncertainty is instead
∣m∣σm​​=∣m∣0.1086​=∣ln(f/f0​)∣0.1​​.
(6)
This is the requested fractional standard uncertainty as a function of m; there is no single numerical fractional error independent of the magnitude. At m=0 it is undefined. Moreover, its value changes when the magnitude zero point changes; the absolute magnitude uncertainty is the useful invariant of such a zero-point shift. Treating the stated percentage as a finite perturbation rather than a standard uncertainty gives changes −0.1035 mag for f↦1.1f and +0.1144 mag for f↦0.9f, consistent with the linear estimate.
