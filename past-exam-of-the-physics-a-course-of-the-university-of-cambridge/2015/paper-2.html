Compare a directly transmitted ray emerging at A with a ray that makes one extra round trip inside the Fabry–Pérot interferometer and emerges at C. Both emerge parallel at angle θ to the plate normal. The extra internal distance is 2d/cosθ, and their emergence points differ laterally by AC=2dtanθ. A Michelson interferometer divides a source beam at a partially reflecting beam splitter. The two perpendicular arms terminate in mirrors whose reflected beams return to the splitter and overlap at the detector. The recombined fields must have overlapping wavefronts and compatible polarization of waves. Moving one mirror by ΔL changes the round-trip optical path difference by 2ΔL, so N passing fringes determine ΔL=Nλ/2. Conversely a known mirror displacement measures wavelength. Scanning the delay can determine temporal optical coherence and, by Fourier transform spectroscopy, spectral structure. A compensating plate may equalize glass traversal, particularly for broadband illumination.
For equal real peak field amplitudes, the superposition is
E(t)=acosωt+acos(ωt+ϕ)=2acos(ϕ/2)cos(ωt+ϕ/2).
(25)
Time averaging the square over many optical cycles gives ⟨E2⟩=2a2cos2(ϕ/2)=a2(1+cosϕ). With ϕ=kx after absorbing a constant port-dependent phase into the zero of x,
I(x)=I0​[1+cos(kx)].​
(26)
Here I0​ is the sum of the two separate-arm intensities; maximum intensity is 2I0​ and the ideal minimum is zero. Equal amplitudes give unit interference visibility.
For a stationary source with a flat intensity spectrum, different spectral components add in time-averaged intensity. Keeping their individual two-arm interference terms and defining sincz=sinz/z gives
I(x)=C∫k0​−Δk/2k0​+Δk/2​[1+cos(k′x)]dk′=CΔk[1+cos(k0​x)sinc(2xΔk​)].
(27)
One must not coherently add unrelated source frequencies as if they had a fixed common phase. The sinc envelope is the Fourier transform of the rectangular spectral intensity.
Numerically, ν0​=5.00×1014Hz and Δν=1.00×1013Hz. The annotated interferogram uses
CΔkI(x)​=1+cos(c2πν0​x​)sinc(cπΔνx​).​
(28)
It is even in x, has central value 2 and background 1, and its carrier-fringe period in optical path difference is c/ν0​=0.600μm. The first envelope zeros are at x=±c/Δν=±30.0μm, corresponding to mirror shifts of ±15.0μm from equal paths. Side lobes persist beyond the zeros; a negative sinc reverses fringe phase. The intensity remains between 1−∣sinc∣ and 1+∣sinc∣, not between the signed sinc curves with an assumed fixed ordering.

The free electron model replaces the microscopic ionic potential and interacting electronic motion by independent mobile electrons in a uniform neutralizing background, with approximately parabolic dispersion ϵ=ℏ2k2/(2m∗). Its quantum version fills states according to Fermi-Dirac statistics and the Pauli exclusion principle. Scattering is represented by a momentum relaxation time τ, rather than derived from the model's ideal free trajectories. This model explains a Fermi energy much larger than room-temperature thermal energy, an electronic heat capacity proportional to temperature at low temperature, and a Drude electrical conductivity. With appropriate scattering assumptions it also yields the Wiedemann-Franz law. It cannot by itself explain band gaps, detailed band anisotropy, strong correlations, or the microscopic temperature dependence of τ.
The inertial term is mass times the rate of change of mean drift velocity. The electric force is eE, taking the printed e as the signed electron charge, and −γ⟨v⟩=−m∗⟨v⟩/τ describes loss of directed momentum to scattering. electrons retain large microscopic velocities; this damping is of their mean directed motion. In a steady DC field,
⟨v⟩=m∗eτ​E,j=ne⟨v⟩,σ0​=m∗ne2τ​.​
(29)
Both charge factors are negative for electrons, so the conductivity is positive. If e instead denotes a positive charge magnitude, the force and current equations each require a minus sign and give the same result.
For AC response choose physical fields as real parts of phasors proportional to e−iωt. Substitution gives
(−iω+1/τ)v=m∗e​E,σ(ω)=1−iωτσ0​​.​
(30)
Thus
Reσ=1+(ωτ)2σ0​​,Imσ=1+(ωτ)2σ0​ωτ​.​
(31)
The real part decreases from σ0​ to zero. The imaginary part starts at zero, reaches σ0​/2 at ωτ=1, and falls as 1/ω. The real part falls as 1/ω2 at high frequency. With an e+iωt convention, only the sign of the imaginary part changes. Positive Reσ gives positive cycle-averaged dissipation ⟨jE⟩=Reσ∣E∣2/2.
The supplied DC conductivity determines the transport time:
τ=ne2me​σ0​​=2.49×10−14s.

A completely filled valence band carries no net current: its occupied crystal-momentum states have velocities that cancel. Near a valence-band maximum, an almost full band is conveniently described by the few missing electrons, or electron holes. Removing an electron of charge −e0​, with e0​>0, changes the charge relative to the filled band by +e0​. This is a quasiparticle description of electronic motion, not a mobile positive ion or a Positron.
For an isotropic parabolic maximum, ϵv​(k)=Ev​−ℏ2k2/(2mh∗​). Assign the missing-electron state momentum kh​=−ke​ and hole energy
ϵh​(kh​)=Ev​−ϵv​(−kh​)=2mh∗​ℏ2kh2​​.
(41)
It has positive effective mass mh∗​. Its velocity equals the velocity of the missing electron state, and its positive-charge current replaces the negative of that state's electronic current. Semiclassical motion is thus ℏk˙h​=+e0​(E+vh​×B). With momentum relaxation time τh​, the low-field mobility is μh​=e0​τh​/mh∗​ and
σh​=e0​pμh​=mh∗​pe02​τh​​.​
(42)
If electrons also contribute, σ=e0​(pμh​+nμe​): the two species' longitudinal conductivities add.
A Hall bar separates carrier concentration from mobility. Apply current density jx​, magnetic field Bz​, and measure transverse electric field Ey​. For one isotropic hole population, transverse force balance gives Ey​=vx​Bz​, so
RH​=jx​Bz​Ey​​=e0​p1​>0,p=e0​RH​1​,μh​=σRH​.​
(43)
For thickness t, the Hall-voltage magnitude is ∣VH​∣=IB/(e0​pt). A longitudinal four-terminal resistance measurement supplies σ independently. Use ohmic contacts, known conducting thickness, controlled temperature and low enough current to avoid self-heating. Reversing magnetic field removes even-in-field longitudinal offsets from contact misalignment; reversing current also helps reject thermoelectric offsets. These are central controls, rather than merely taking one transverse voltage reading.[ref]
The simple inference assumes one carrier type and a known Hall scattering factor. More generally RH​=rH​/(e0​p), so σRH​ measures Hall mobility rather than automatically the drift mobility. With two carrier populations at weak field,
RH​=e0​(pμh​+nμe​)2pμh2​−nμe2​​.
(44)
Its sign alone need not identify the more numerous carrier. A Hall experiment also cannot determine mh∗​ and τh​ separately: their ratio sets mobility. cyclotron resonance supplies an independent mass measurement by sweeping frequency or magnetic field through ω=e0​B/mc​ and detecting resonant absorption. The measured cyclotron effective mass can depend on band anisotropy and need not equal every curvature mass; the isotropic parabolic model identifies them. Its linewidth provides a scattering-time estimate only with a specified damping model.
semiconductor doping generates holes by adding acceptor impurities. In silicon, a substitutional group-III atom such as boron provides one fewer valence electron than silicon. A shallow acceptor level lies slightly above the valence-band edge: accepting a valence electron makes the dopant negatively ionized and leaves a mobile hole in the valence band. The impurity stays fixed in the lattice. charge neutrality and the nondegenerate equilibrium mass-action law give
p+ND+​=n+NA−​,np=ni2​.
(45)
For fully ionized, uncompensated acceptors in the extrinsic regime, p≃NA​ and n≃ni2​/NA​; with fully ionized donor compensation, p≃NA​−ND​ while holes remain dominant. At low temperature, semiconductor carrier freeze-out suppresses mobile electron holes; at high temperature intrinsic electron-hole generation can dominate. Increasing dopant density also changes scattering, so a conductivity increase alone cannot distinguish increased carrier density from changed mobility. Temperature-dependent Hall and conductivity measurements separate these effects, subject to the carrier and scattering qualifications above.

For a small longitudinal disturbance, let the equilibrium pressure and mass density be p0​,ρ0​, their perturbations be p′,ρ′, and the fluid velocity be v. Linearizing conservation of mass and the inviscid momentum equation in one dimension gives
∂t​ρ′=−ρ0​∂x​v,ρ0​∂t​v=−∂x​p′.
(46)

∂t2​ρ′=cs2​∂x2​ρ′,cs​=γp0​/ρ0​​=γRT/Mmol​​​
(47)
for an ideal gas with heat-capacity ratio γ=Cp​/Cv​ and molar mass Mmol​. Compression raises temperature as well as density, explaining the factor γ compared with the isothermal value p0​/ρ0​​.
A rightgoing plane sound wave has pressure and particle velocity in phase, p′=ρ0​cs​v, and mean intensity ⟨p′v⟩=p​2/(2ρ0​cs​)=ρ0​cs​v2/2 for peak amplitudes. Particle displacement is a quarter-cycle out of phase with particle velocity: particles oscillate locally while the compression pattern transports energy. This continuum linear description requires wavelength much larger than the mean free path, small pressure perturbations and negligible viscous attenuation. Adiabatic propagation requires heat exchange across the compressed region to be slow on the oscillation time; strong thermal coupling would instead favour an isothermal response.

For propagation along z, a transverse wave has two perpendicular displacement components. With ϕ=kz−ωt, write ux​=Acosϕ, uy​=Bcos(ϕ+δ). Their trajectory at a fixed point obeys
(Aux​​)2+(Buy​​)2−2ABux​uy​​cosδ=sin2δ.​
(51)
The general nondegenerate trajectory is an ellipse: elliptical polarization. For δ=0 or π, the components remain in phase or antiphase and the trajectory collapses to a line. A single nonzero component is also linearly polarized. Equal amplitudes A=B with δ=±π/2 give circular polarization. Unequal quadrature amplitudes give an ellipse aligned with the chosen axes, while general phase differences rotate its axes.
For the displayed convention and δ=+π/2, at z=0 the vector starts at +x and moves towards +y as time increases. Reversing δ reverses its rotation. Right- and left-handed labels require a stated viewing and phase convention, so rotation is defined explicitly here. A fixed ellipse is a coherent relationship between transverse components; unpolarized light instead has fluctuating orientation and phase relationships on the measurement timescale.
A linear polarizer transmits a linearly polarized beam with intensity proportional to cos2θ, allowing its direction to be measured by an analyser. circular polarization transmits equal intensity through any linear analyser, but that test alone does not distinguish it from unpolarized light. A quarter-wave plate converts a suitable coherent circular state to linear polarization, allowing the combination to discriminate them. For light the electric field defines the usual polarization; the magnetic field remains perpendicular to it and to propagation. Longitudinal sound in a homogeneous gas lacks the two transverse degrees of freedom needed for these polarization states.
