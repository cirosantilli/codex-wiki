Take the aperture in z=0 and the observation point P=(0,0,b), with b>0. Under scalar diffraction theory, the Huygens-Fresnel principle sums complex amplitudes from area elements, rather than summing their intensities. Apart from a common normalization,
ψ(P)∝∬h(x,y)K(θ)ReikR​dxdy,R=b2+x2+y2​,k=2π/λ.
(18)
Here h is the complex aperture transmission, b is the axial distance, and θ is the angle of the secondary ray to the aperture normal. The obliquity factor K describes the direction dependence of the secondary contribution; one Kirchhoff convention is K=(1+cosθ)/2 for normal illumination. Other scalar boundary approximations have slightly different angular weights, all tending to unity near the axis.
Put r2=x2+y2. The paraxial approximation gives R≃b+r2/(2b) in the phase and 1/R≃1/b in the amplitude. Removing common eikb/b yields
ψ(P)∝∬h(x,y)K(θ)exp[λbiπ(x2+y2)​]dxdy.​
(19)
Assumptions are uniform normally incident plane-wave illumination, coherent monochromatic propagation, aperture dimensions large enough for the scalar description, r/b≪1, and phase accuracy kr4/(8b3)≪1 over the relevant aperture. The last condition justifies dropping the quartic distance term; geometric smallness alone does not guarantee phase accuracy.
For a circular aperture, h=1 for r≤a and zero elsewhere. Rotational symmetry and s=r2 give dxdy=rdrdφ, rdr=ds/2, and
ψ∝2π∫0a​K(θ)eiπr2/(λb)rdr=π∫0a2​K(θ)eiπs/(λb)ds.
(20)
The constant π can be absorbed into the proportionality factor, producing the required one-dimensional integral.
At a focus a distance f away, half-period-zone boundaries obey
f2+rn2​​−f=2nλ​,rn2​=nλf+4n2λ2​≃nλf.
(21)
With slowly varying obliquity and illumination, each zone has almost the same area and its integrated phasor has the opposite sign to its neighbour. A zone plate blocks alternate zones, so the remaining phasors add instead of nearly cancelling. Choosing an open central zone, the first three open regions have outer boundaries n=1,3,5:
r1​=0.100mm,r3​=0.173mm,r5​=0.224mm.​
(22)
These are outer radii, not zone widths; the second open region is the annulus between r2​ and r3​.
For the specified outer radius, R2/(λf)=49: there are approximately 49 half-period zones, of which 25 odd zones are open. Normalize to the field ψ0​ of the unobstructed plane wave. The normalized Fresnel aperture integral is
ψ0​ψ​=iλf1​∫heiπr2/(λf)dA.
(23)
For any odd open annulus this gives 2, so the whole plate gives ψ/ψ0​≃50 and
Ifocus​/Iunobstructed​≃4(25)2=2.50×103.​
(24)
The axial intensity gain of an amplitude zone plate grows as the square of the number of open zones. Treating the number of zones continuously gives 492≃2.40×103, the same accuracy as an estimate. This large local intensity gain is consistent with conservation of energy because light is concentrated into a small focal spot. It is not a power efficiency; the ideal first-order power efficiency of a binary amplitude zone plate is only 1/π2.[ref]
“Without the zone plate” has here meant removing the finite plate and its outer stop, giving the unobstructed incident wave. If instead a clear circular aperture of radius R is retained, its axial field is ψclear​/ψ0​=1−ei49π=2, so the ratio to that finite-aperture reference is 2500/4=625. The two reference experiments differ; neither ratio should be confused with total transmitted power.
