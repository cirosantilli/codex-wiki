A completely filled valence band carries no net current: its occupied crystal-momentum states have velocities that cancel. Near a valence-band maximum, an almost full band is conveniently described by the few missing electrons, or electron holes. Removing an electron of charge −e0​, with e0​>0, changes the charge relative to the filled band by +e0​. This is a quasiparticle description of electronic motion, not a mobile positive ion or a Positron.
For an isotropic parabolic maximum, ϵv​(k)=Ev​−ℏ2k2/(2mh∗​). Assign the missing-electron state momentum kh​=−ke​ and hole energy
ϵh​(kh​)=Ev​−ϵv​(−kh​)=2mh∗​ℏ2kh2​​.
(41)
It has positive effective mass mh∗​. Its velocity equals the velocity of the missing electron state, and its positive-charge current replaces the negative of that state's electronic current. Semiclassical motion is thus ℏk˙h​=+e0​(E+vh​×B). With momentum relaxation time τh​, the low-field mobility is μh​=e0​τh​/mh∗​ and
σh​=e0​pμh​=mh∗​pe02​τh​​.​
(42)
If electrons also contribute, σ=e0​(pμh​+nμe​): the two species' longitudinal conductivities add.
A Hall bar separates carrier concentration from mobility. Apply current density jx​, magnetic field Bz​, and measure transverse electric field Ey​. For one isotropic hole population, transverse force balance gives Ey​=vx​Bz​, so
RH​=jx​Bz​Ey​​=e0​p1​>0,p=e0​RH​1​,μh​=σRH​.​
(43)
For thickness t, the Hall-voltage magnitude is ∣VH​∣=IB/(e0​pt). A longitudinal four-terminal resistance measurement supplies σ independently. Use ohmic contacts, known conducting thickness, controlled temperature and low enough current to avoid self-heating. Reversing magnetic field removes even-in-field longitudinal offsets from contact misalignment; reversing current also helps reject thermoelectric offsets. These are central controls, rather than merely taking one transverse voltage reading.[ref]
The simple inference assumes one carrier type and a known Hall scattering factor. More generally RH​=rH​/(e0​p), so σRH​ measures Hall mobility rather than automatically the drift mobility. With two carrier populations at weak field,
RH​=e0​(pμh​+nμe​)2pμh2​−nμe2​​.
(44)
Its sign alone need not identify the more numerous carrier. A Hall experiment also cannot determine mh∗​ and τh​ separately: their ratio sets mobility. cyclotron resonance supplies an independent mass measurement by sweeping frequency or magnetic field through ω=e0​B/mc​ and detecting resonant absorption. The measured cyclotron effective mass can depend on band anisotropy and need not equal every curvature mass; the isotropic parabolic model identifies them. Its linewidth provides a scattering-time estimate only with a specified damping model.
semiconductor doping generates holes by adding acceptor impurities. In silicon, a substitutional group-III atom such as boron provides one fewer valence electron than silicon. A shallow acceptor level lies slightly above the valence-band edge: accepting a valence electron makes the dopant negatively ionized and leaves a mobile hole in the valence band. The impurity stays fixed in the lattice. charge neutrality and the nondegenerate equilibrium mass-action law give
p+ND+​=n+NA−​,np=ni2​.
(45)
For fully ionized, uncompensated acceptors in the extrinsic regime, p≃NA​ and n≃ni2​/NA​; with fully ionized donor compensation, p≃NA​−ND​ while holes remain dominant. The Fermi level moves towards the valence band. At low temperature, semiconductor carrier freeze-out suppresses mobile electron holes; at high temperature intrinsic electron-hole generation can dominate. Increasing dopant density also changes scattering, so a conductivity increase alone cannot distinguish increased carrier density from changed mobility. Temperature-dependent Hall and conductivity measurements separate these effects, subject to the carrier and scattering qualifications above.
