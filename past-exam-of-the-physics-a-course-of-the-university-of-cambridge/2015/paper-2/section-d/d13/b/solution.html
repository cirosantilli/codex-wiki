The Q factor measures stored energy relative to loss: Q=2πE/(energy lost per cycle), or Q=ω0​E/Ploss​ in the weak-loss limit. For viscous damping mx¨+bx˙+kx=0, write γ=b/(2m), ω0​=k/m​ and ωd​=ω02​−γ2​. The conventional oscillator parameter is
Q=bmω0​​=2γω0​​.​
(48)
In free ringdown, fit the amplitude envelope A(t)=A0​e−γt. Its decay time is τA​=1/γ, whereas the cycle-averaged energy envelope decays on τE​=1/(2γ); thus Q=ω0​τA​/2=ω0​τE​. Equivalently the logarithmic decrement between same-sign peaks is δ=ln(An​/An+1​)=2πγ/ωd​. It gives Q≃π/δ for weak damping, or the exact parameter relation Q=1/4+(π/δ)2​. Fit multiple peaks above the noise floor, measure the damped period, and verify an exponential envelope rather than assuming all amplitude loss is viscous.
For a driven measurement, allow transients to settle and scan frequency at fixed force amplitude. Near resonance and at high Q, the response is Lorentzian in displacement power, with half-power points at displacement amplitudes 1/2​ of the maximum. Their angular-frequency separation gives Q≃ω0​/Δω. Halving the amplitude is not a half-power criterion.
The absorbed-power bandwidth of a viscously damped oscillator can be found exactly. Since
P(Ω)=2[(k−mΩ2)2+b2Ω2]bF02​Ω2​,
(49)
its maximum is at Ω=ω0​, and the half-power frequencies satisfy ∣k−mΩ2∣=bΩ. Hence
Ω±​=2mb2+4mk​±b​,Ω+​−Ω−​=b/m,Q=ω0​/(Ω+​−Ω−​).​
(50)
For broad resonances, displacement and absorbed-power peaks differ, so the measured observable must be specified. Frequency-dependent drive, backgrounds or several overlapping modes can otherwise masquerade as a changed Q factor.
