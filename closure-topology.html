Closure (topology, A)

 Home Mathematics Area of mathematics Geometry and topology Topology
OurBigBook.com Words: 230 Articles: 1
The closure of a subset A of a topological space X is the smallest closed set containing A. Equivalently, it is the intersection of all closed sets containing A, or the set of points whose every neighbourhood in X meets A. The notation clX​A makes the ambient topological space explicit. This definition works without a metric or any appeal to sequences. [ref]
For A=[0,1) in the usual real line, A=[0,1]: every neighbourhood of 1 meets A, while a point outside [0,1] has an open ball disjoint from A. Passing to the closure adds the missing endpoint and retains all original points, including isolated points when present. In general, the closure is A together with its limit points; its individual points are the adherent points of A.
The closure turns an arbitrary subset into a closed set with no unnecessary added points. It satisfies A⊆A and A=A; a subset is closed exactly when A=A. In a metric space, x∈A exactly when some sequence of points of A has limit of a sequence x: choose its nth point in A∩B(x,1/n). For a general topological space, keep the neighbourhood definition; sequences need not detect every point of the closure.
