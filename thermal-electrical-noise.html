Thermal electrical noise (vn,rms​=4kB​TRB​)

 Home Electrical engineering Signal processing Signal Noise (signal processing)
OurBigBook.com Words: 1k Articles: 6
Thermal electrical noise is the fluctuating electric potential difference or electric current produced by thermal fluctuations in a dissipative electrical circuit, even without an applied voltage. A resistor at thermal equilibrium is both a source of noise and a sink for electrical energy. Its electrical resistance describes the dissipation, and its absolute temperature sets the scale of the fluctuations. This connects a practical limit on signal-to-noise ratio to statistical mechanics: a warm, unbiased resistor is not electrically silent.
John Bertrand Johnson measured the effect using a vacuum-tube amplifier and thermocouple; his 1928 paper reported agreement with the Boltzmann constant. Harry Nyquist's companion paper calculated the effect from thermodynamics and statistical mechanics.[ref][ref]
