Bragg structure inference from measured intensities

 Home Chemistry Physical chemistry Crystal structure Crystallography X-ray crystallographic structure inference
OurBigBook.com Words: 170
William Lawrence Bragg's 1913 structure paper connects a slit-selected beam, an oriented crystal and an ionization chamber to strength-versus-angle curves. Repeated sine ratios and matching absorption coefficients identify common radiation. Narrower slits resolve blended lines. Absolute wavelengths remain undetermined there; intercrystal comparisons first give relative scales. The Bragg crystal reflection experiment of 1913 covers the alignment controls.[ref]
William Henry Bragg's 1915 Bakerian lecture, X-rays and Crystal Structure, separates the geometric and intensity arguments. Its Figure 9 compares reflection positions and strengths from three sodium chloride orientations; odd and even diffraction orders differ in strength along one orientation. The interpretation uses alternating scattering layers. Later, the calcite example identifies an internal oxygen coordinate that symmetry alone cannot fix. Improved wave intensity measurements revise a previously rough estimate. Thus a lattice assignment can precede determination of positions inside the unit cell; intensity interpretation also has experimental and theoretical limits. This is the father's 1915 lecture, distinct from the joint 1913 reflection paper.[ref]
