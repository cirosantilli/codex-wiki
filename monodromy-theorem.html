Monodromy theorem

 ... Mathematics Area of mathematics Analysis Complex analysis Holomorphic function Analytic continuation
OurBigBook.com Words: 1k Articles: 7
The monodromy theorem says that, when analytic continuation exists throughout a domain, its ambiguity depends on homotopy. Let D⊂C be a domain, a∈D, and ga​ a germ of a holomorphic function at a. Assume ga​ admits analytic continuation along every continuous path in D starting at a. Two such paths ending at b give the same terminal germ of a holomorphic function if they are homotopic with endpoints fixed in D. [ref]
Here a homotopy with endpoints fixed is a continuous map H:[0,1]2→D satisfying
H(0,t)=γ0​(t),H(1,t)=γ1​(t),H(s,0)=a,H(s,1)=b.
(23)
Every intermediate path must stay in D. Equality means agreement on a neighbourhood of b, rather than merely equal values at b. [ref]
The identity theorem provides local rigidity; the monodromy theorem shows precisely how topology controls the remaining global freedom. Deforming a route without crossing a forbidden point cannot change the resulting germ of a holomorphic function. Thus a complicated analytic continuation calculation can be replaced by one on a simpler homotopic route. Distinct homotopy classes can still give the same continuation: a rational function on its domain already gives a single-valued answer along every route, even around a pole.
